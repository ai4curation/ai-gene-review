    Time-split annotation benchmarks

    ai-gene-review benchmark builds benchmarks of protein function annotations that no predictor could have seen. A target is an annotation that is in a later GO release and absent from a baseline release on or before a cutoff date. The targets are then checked, mostly by code, for leakage, near-duplication and balance. The schema is src/ai_gene_review/schema/benchmark.yaml (LinkML). The skill that drives it is .claude/skills/benchmark-builder/. The worked example is benchmarks/human-goa-2025-06/.

    Purpose

    The benchmark expands the analysis in the fable-sequence-to-function preprint (ai4curation/fable-sequence-to-function). That preprint asked small, hand-built questions of a model that sees the sequence alone. This benchmark asks the same kind of question on many more proteins, with time-split, leakage-checked targets. It also compares conditions item by item:

    condition context what the predictor sees
    blind BLIND the sequence only (peptalk ask --context blind, the fs2f protocol), optionally with a shuffled-sequence control
    organism ORGANISM the sequence plus organism and taxon id
    full FULL the sequence plus the UniProt-style header: accession, protein name, gene
    agent-review AGENT_REVIEW combined reasoning: an agent reads the sequence and reasons over knowledge and context, as in an ai-gene-review review seeded with baseline-release annotations only

    The difference between blind and agent-review on the same item separates reading the sequence from recalling or retrieving knowledge about the named protein. Decoy terms and shuffled sequences measure how often a predictor says yes regardless.

    Workflow

    just benchmark-build  benchmarks/<id>/<id>-spec.yaml    # draw items -> <id>.yaml
just benchmark-check  benchmarks/<id>/<id>.yaml         # -> <id>-check-report.{yaml,md}
just benchmark-export benchmarks/<id>/<id>.yaml         # -> run-inputs/ (FASTA, claims, baseline GOA)
peptalk ask run-inputs/<id>.fasta --claims run-inputs/<id>-claims.tsv --context blind --shuffle-control > runs/blind.jsonl
just benchmark-score  benchmarks/<id>/<id>.yaml --peptalk runs/blind.jsonl --reviews-dir runs/agent-review/genes
just validate-benchmarks

    GO release files and UniProt responses are cached in .cache/benchmark/ (git-ignored). run-inputs/ and runs/ are git-ignored too: export regenerates the inputs, and run outputs belong with whoever ran them.

    The spec

    A BenchmarkSpec states the request: taxon, cutoff date, baseline and current GO releases, aspects, optional GO branches, evidence codes (default: CAFA's EXP, IDA, IPI, IMP, IGI, IEP, TAS, IC), excluded terms (default: roots, binding, protein binding), whether NOT annotations count as negative targets, size, seed, feature quotas (for example PSEUDOENZYME, with a UniProt query), the number of decoys per item, and the evaluation conditions. Each condition records its model and, where known, the model's training cutoff.

    min_reference_date keeps only targets whose supporting PubMed references were all published on or after a date. A target that also rests on an older paper is dropped whole, and the earliest supporting paper becomes the target's reference. A target with any non-PubMed supporting reference (GO_REF, Reactome) has no checkable date, so it is dropped when the floor is set. This matters more for language models than it did for CAFA. A GO time split only shows that the GO record is new. A model may have read the paper. A feature quota can set its own min_reference_date when the feature is too rare to fill from the main window.

    Modes

    • GOA_TIME_SPLIT: targets are current-release rows that pass the filters and the novelty test. Proteins are sampled with the seed, feature quotas first. A reference that adds new annotations to more than max_proteins_per_reference proteins is treated as high-throughput and its rows are dropped.
    • REVIEW_DERIVED: targets are the ACCEPT and NEW annotations of ai-gene-review reviews (--reviews ...), kept only when they too are absent from the baseline release. Run agentically, the skill first reviews N candidate genes with the normal review workflow, then builds from those reviews.

    Both modes pin each item's UniProt sequence and sequence version, record PubMed publication dates, attach the review action when the gene has a review, grade every target, and draw decoys.

    Deterministic checks

    benchmark check reruns everything against the pinned sources. A hand-edited or review-derived benchmark is held to the same tests as a drawn one.

    check what it tests
    schema the Benchmark validates (closed LinkML/JSON Schema)
    novelty no baseline experimental row for the term, a descendant, or an alt_id or obsolete id the current ontology maps onto them; for NOT targets, no baseline NOT row to the term or an ancestor (FAIL)
    present_in_current the row is in the current release (FAIL for GOA targets)
    date_window the GAF date is on or after the cutoff (WARN only; see below)
    prior_prediction the term or a descendant was predicted on the protein at baseline (IBA/PAN-GO, IEA, ISS, ...)
    homolog_leakage the term or a descendant was experimentally annotated at baseline on a protein in the same PANTHER subfamily (INFO when the subfamily has no other member in the species: not checked)
    near_duplicate the closest baseline experimental term in the same aspect has Lin similarity at or above the threshold (IC from baseline annotation frequencies; INFO when there is no baseline term to compare)
    uniprot_leakage the current UniProt entry carries the target in a DR GO line (INFO) or its label in comment text (WARN)
    header_leakage the protein name shown in FULL conditions shares most of the target label's words
    reference_recency the earliest supporting paper was published after each condition's model training cutoff
    decoy decoys neither entail nor are entailed by any current annotation of the protein
    feature_label a query-derived feature (pseudoenzyme) is supported by the UniProt protein name, not only by comment text
    review_agreement an ai-gene-review review of the gene keeps (PASS) or contests (WARN) the target
    balance requested size, aspects and feature quotas met; no single reference supplies more than 25% of targets or targets to more than 10% of items
    difficulty a recorded difficulty agrees with the grading checks

    Difficulty follows from three of these checks. A target is EASY if prior_prediction or homolog_leakage fires, MEDIUM if near_duplicate fires, and HARD otherwise. An item takes the hardest grade among its targets. The knowledge category follows CAFA, judged on baseline experimental rows outside the excluded terms: NO_KNOWLEDGE, LIMITED_KNOWLEDGE (experimental annotation only in another aspect) and PARTIAL_KNOWLEDGE.

    Agentic parts

    • Agent evaluation (<id>-agent-evaluation.yaml, class AgentEvaluation): an agent reads the finished benchmark and its check report and records ambiguous, wrong, trivial or leaked targets and imbalance. Each finding should name a deterministic check that would confirm it. These are an agent's judgements, not expert review. Say who the evaluator is and whether it is independent of the builder.
    • Review-derived build: see Modes.

    Things the example taught

    • GAF dates are not creation dates. GO re-stamps the date column. An IPI from a 2021 paper carries 20260725 in the 2026-08-05 release. Novelty is decided by absence from the baseline release, never by date. date_window is informational.
    • Evidence codes churn between releases. Between 2025-06-01 and 2026-08-05 the human GAF lost about 15,000 IDA rows and gained about 15,000 EXP rows. Novelty is therefore keyed on term and qualifier, not evidence code.
    • New to GO is not new knowledge. In the example window, 10,771 rows on 4,420 human proteins passed the novelty test (after the high-throughput filter). Only 393 rows on 182 proteins rest wholly on papers published since 2025-06-01, and 10 rows on 6 proteins on papers since 2026-06-01.
    • GO moved its per-species files to annotations/gaf/<CODE>-uniprot.gaf.gz in 2026. The loader tries both layouts.
    • Feature labels from UniProt queries are not literature checks. In draws of the example, SMARCA1 matched a pseudoenzyme query only through isoform-2 text, KLK11 (an active protease) only through the names of its cleavage chains ("inactive chain 1"), and WHR1 (formerly STK19) only through an AltName. All are recorded with literature_checked: false. feature_label reads only the protein's own names and flags the first two. This is the lesson of fs2f's NAMED_INACTIVE_LABELS.md.

    Related practice

    • CAFA (Critical Assessment of Functional Annotation). Prediction is followed by a waiting period, and new experimental annotations become the targets. Proteins are split into no-knowledge and limited-knowledge targets, and in CAFA3 partial knowledge. A target is excluded when a descendant was already known. evaluation/cafa.py holds the repository's PAN-GO-style scorer, and the benchmark reuses its excluded-term defaults.
    • fable-sequence-to-function. Its test sets are committed exactly as drawn, because the build scripts query live services. It has a decoy control (substitutions at non-catalytic positions) and shuffled-sequence controls. It stratifies by prior homology (no close earlier PDB match). Its "inactive" labels were checked against the full text, and two of three checked were contradicted. The benchmark follows each of these: it commits the drawn benchmark with source checksums, draws decoy terms and supports shuffled controls, grades difficulty by prior and homolog knowledge, and does not treat UniProt-name labels as literature.
    • "A Society of AI Scientists" (alphaXiv 2611.society-ai-scientists, 21 Nov 2026; only the abstract page loaded, the PDF viewer rendered no text). It is an observational study of 936 agents publishing to a shared preprint server, not a benchmark paper, so its relevance is low. Three points carry over. Text-only agent referees barely separated flawed from corrected papers (11/16 vs 10/16). Objections run against the uploaded data overturned conclusions far more often. LLM ratings are not expert review. The agent evaluation here therefore asks each finding to name a deterministic check, and the benchmark, its sources and its check report are released together.

    Limits

    • The homolog check uses same-species PANTHER subfamilies only, and most human subfamilies have one human member, so it seldom applies: in the example it checked 3 of 51 targets and reports the rest as INFO. Cross-species ortholog knowledge enters through IBA, ISS and ISO rows (prior_prediction).
    • Decoy aspects cycle through the item's sorted target aspects, so an item whose targets are mostly one aspect draws its decoys from that aspect.
    • PANTHER subfamilies, UniProt entries, sequences and feature queries come from current UniProt (release recorded), not from the baseline date.
    • A decoy is a term the protein is not annotated to, not one it is known to lack, so a yes on a decoy is not necessarily wrong.
    • min_reference_date and reference_recency use the PubMed record date. A preprint, accepted manuscript or author copy may have been readable, and in a model's training data, a year earlier. A preprint-date lookup would tighten both.
    • Decoys are scored only where a predictor is asked about them. A review is never asked about decoys, so the agent-review condition records them as missing, and its decoy rate is not comparable with the peptalk conditions. Compare conditions on targets, and on decoys only among the peptalk runs.
    • Review verdicts count ACCEPT, KEEP_AS_NON_CORE, NEW, TOO_GENERAL and MODIFY rows (including MODIFY's replacement terms) and every core-function term slot as assertions. REMOVE and MARK_AS_OVER_ANNOTATED count as "no".
    • Existing ai-gene-review reviews were written with current GOA in view. Scoring them with --reviews-dir genes measures agreement, not an AGENT_REVIEW run. A real run seeds each review with run-inputs/baseline-goa/<ACC>-goa.tsv and keeps the agent away from post-cutoff sources.
