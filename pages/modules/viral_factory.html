Viral factory (virus-induced replication microcompartment)

A viral factory (VF) is a virus-induced intracellular microcompartment that concentrates the viral genome, the replication and transcription machinery, and the structural proteins of the progeny virion into one place, raising the local concentration of every reactant in the replication cycle while screening replication intermediates from host nucleic-acid sensors and nucleases. The module is deliberately mechanism-neutral at its core and taxon-spanning: what makes a VF a VF is the compartment boundary plus the co-localised replication, transcription, and assembly programme, not any one way of drawing the boundary. Three boundary mechanisms are modelled as variants along a single axis - a host-membrane-derived boundary (spherules, tubes, double-membrane vesicles, convoluted ER), a membraneless biomolecular condensate formed by liquid-liquid phase separation, and a self-assembled proteinaceous shell - because the same downstream programme runs inside all three. Around that core the module places the recruitment of host systems the factory depends on (cytoskeleton, mitochondria, donor membranes, host lipid kinases, in some lineages host transcription machinery), the shielding of replication intermediates from host defences, non-lytic egress, and the antagonists that act on the factory itself.

Scope decisions. (1) The module is ABSTRACT: the boundary it draws is the factory itself, which recurs across RNA and DNA viruses of eukaryotes and across jumbo phages of bacteria, so most leaf roles are selectors with orienting representative members rather than a species-specific member list. Representative members are examples only; where a strain-specific reference entry exists it is cited, and representatives within one family descriptor may come from different reference strains. (2) Phage nuclei are included as a boundary variant, with a caveat. The source review is explicit that the chimallin-shelled compartments of Chimalliviridae are "not considered true VFs", and GO has no term for them. They are modelled here anyway because they satisfy the module's functional definition exactly - a boundary that concentrates replication and transcription, excludes translation, and blocks host nucleases - and because treating the boundary mechanism as the variant axis is the whole point of the module. The caveat is recorded on the variant node itself, not hidden here. (3) Translation is deliberately outside the module. In every system where it has been localised - mimivirus, and the phage nucleus - translation happens outside the factory, and that exclusion is a property worth stating rather than a part to include. (4) Host restriction factors and virophages are grouped as one optional "antagonists" part rather than scattered as negative regulation on individual steps, because both act on the assembled factory as a unit. (5) Provenance caution on the GO groundings. The GO viral-factory branch used throughout this module is not independent of ViralZone: GO:0039713 carries the dbxref VZ:1951, and the definitions of viral factory, spherule viral factory, double membrane vesicle viral factory, tube viral factory, virogenic stroma and peristromal region viral factory are all close paraphrases of that single ViralZone page (GO does drop its claim that filoviruses produce viroplasms). Citing VZ:1951 alongside those GO terms would therefore count one source twice rather than corroborate, so it is recorded here and not as an evidence item. The primary literature cited on each boundary variant is the independent support.

Biomolecular condensate boundary (liquid-liquid phase separation)Cellular Componentvf_condensate_boundary

The boundary is a phase boundary rather than a membrane: viral scaffold proteins with intrinsically disordered regions, in concert with the viral genome, demix from the cytoplasm or nucleoplasm into a liquid condensate. Because the boundary is permeable to small molecules and to diffusing macromolecules, no pore is needed; concentration and selective partitioning do the work instead. The recurring architecture across Mononegavirales is a nucleoprotein-phosphoprotein pair, at least one of which contributes an essential disordered region.

The boundary is a single-layer protein lattice with ~2 nm pores, built de novo by the virus rather than borrowed from the host. In Chimalliviridae this shell is preceded by a transient membrane-bound compartment, making the two boundary mechanisms sequential within one infection.

Caveat carried from the source review: these phage compartments are conventionally described as analogous to, rather than instances of, viral factories, and GO has no term for them.
