Human cytosolic translation elongation and termination — eEF1A aminoacyl-tRNA delivery recycled by the eEF1B exchange complex, eEF2 translocation (restrained by EEF2K), hypusinated eIF5A at stalling motifs, eRF1-eRF3 peptide release and ABCE1 ribosome recycling

Once initiation has placed an 80S ribosome on the start codon with initiator tRNA in the P site, the cytosolic ribosome extends the polypeptide by a repeated three-step cycle and then releases it at a stop codon. (1) Decoding: the GTP-bound elongation factor eEF1A (EEF1A1 in most tissues, its paralog EEF1A2 in neurons, heart and skeletal muscle) carries aminoacyl-tRNA to the A site as a ternary complex; codon-anticodon pairing activates GTP hydrolysis and eEF1A-GDP leaves the ribosome. The eEF1B complex, a heterotrimer of eEF1Balpha (EEF1B2), the second exchange subunit encoded by EEF1D and the scaffold eEF1Bgamma (EEF1G), exchanges GDP for GTP on eEF1A so that it can bind the next aminoacyl-tRNA. (2) Peptidyl transfer is catalysed by the large-subunit rRNA of the ribosome itself. (3) Translocation: the GTPase eEF2 moves the tRNA-mRNA module from the hybrid pre-translocation state to the post-translocation state, vacating the A site for the next codon. eEF2 is inhibited when the calcium/calmodulin-dependent alpha-kinase EEF2K phosphorylates it, which couples elongation rate to energy and stress signals. At sequences that the ribosome forms peptide bonds on slowly, such as runs of prolines and some other tripeptide motifs, the hypusine-modified factor eIF5A binds between the E and P sites and stimulates peptide-bond formation. When a stop codon reaches the A site, the class I release factor eRF1 (ETF1) recognises it and, through its GGQ motif, induces hydrolysis of peptidyl-tRNA; the translational GTPase eRF3 (eRF3a, GSPT1, or eRF3b, GSPT2) delivers eRF1 as a GTP-bound complex and its GTP hydrolysis accelerates release. Finally the ABC-family ATPase ABCE1 binds the post-termination ribosome together with eRF1 and splits it into 60S and 40S subunits, which return to initiation.

Curator-authored functions without an experimental human GOA row: the guanyl-nucleotide exchange factor activity of the eEF1B complex and of its EEF1B2 and EEF1D units (IBA rows only; literature PMID:26587907, PMID:35971611), and translation elongation factor activity for EEF1A2 and EIF5A, which are the core functions of their gene reviews. The structure of the termination part follows modules/dmel_erf1_erf3_termination_complex.yaml (structure only; no Drosophila evidence is used). Boundary and modelling decisions. (1) The ribosome is not modelled. Peptidyl transfer and decoding are performed by the ribosomal RNAs and proteins of the 80S ribosome, which are modelled once in the sibling module cytoplasmic_ribosome; no ribosomal protein is listed here. Initiation and the 60S joining by eIF5B belong to the sibling translation_initiation; the 40S subunit released by ABCE1, still bound to mRNA and tRNA, is recycled by initiation factors, which belong to that sibling. (2) The translational GTPases eEF1A and eEF2 carry translation elongation factor activity, the function of their experimental rows, of the GO-CAM activity for EEF1A1 and of the EEF1A2 review; their GTPase activity is recorded in the role text as part of the factor cycle rather than as a second annoton, so the GTP-hydrolysis participants are grounded only where GTPase activity is the annoton function (eRF3), as in the GO-CAM termination model and the Drosophila analogue. (3) eEF1A paralogs. EEF1A1 and EEF1A2 have the same translation activity and reciprocal tissue expression; they are two annotons in one node rather than a variant set because each is a complete implementation of the same step and the axis (tissue) is stated in the node description. Their moonlighting functions (actin bundling, PI4KB activation by EEF1A2, nuclear export, apoptosis) are outside the module. (4) eEF1B. GO has no term for the exchange complex alone; GO:0005853 eukaryotic translation elongation factor 1 complex is defined with all four subunits (eEF1A with eEF1B), so it is the concept of the parent node that holds the carrier and the exchanger, and the exchange complex is a protein-complex descriptor without a GO id. Subunit names are confusing: EEF1B2 encodes eEF1Balpha (UniProt "Elongation factor 1-beta"), EEF1D encodes the second exchange subunit (UniProt "Elongation factor 1-delta", called eEF1Bbeta in PMID:35971611) and EEF1G encodes the scaffold eEF1Bgamma. Both exchange subunits carry the GEF function because each was assayed as an isolated protein; EEF1G has none. EEF1E1 (AIMP3), which shares the EEF1 name, is a component of the multi-tRNA synthetase complex and is excluded. GO:0005085 is used for the exchange activity although its definition says "signaling GTPase"; GO cross-references the Reactome eEF1B exchange reaction from the same term, and a definition change is recorded in proposed_ontology_changes. (5) EEF2K is included as an optional regulatory part with a NEGATIVELY_REGULATES edge to eEF2, because eEF2 is its only established substrate and it acts directly on an elongation factor. The upstream signals (calcium-calmodulin, AMPK, mTORC1-S6K, RSK) are not modelled; other regulators of elongation are likewise left out. (6) eIF5A is included. Despite its name, the human EIF5A review gives translation elongation factor activity as its core function, its experimental GOA row is translational elongation (IMP, PMID:29712776), and in human cells it is required for translation of ATG3, whose polyproline motif stalls the ribosome (PMID:29712776). Hypusine synthesis by DHPS and DOHH is a required maturation of eIF5A, not a step of elongation, and is left to a hypusine module (a Drosophila analogue, dmel_hypusine_biosynthesis, exists; there is no human module yet). EIF5A2 is not modelled (protein-binding rows only). eIF5A's reported roles in termination and in mRNA export are not modelled. (7) Rescue of stalled ribosomes is excluded. RGD annotates PELO and HBS1L to PW:0000582, and its text describes them as the stalled-ribosome counterparts of eRF1 and eRF3 in no-go and nonstop decay; both have human gene reviews and GO-CAM models (gomodel:63e5937500001279). They are rescue factors acting on stalled, not terminating, ribosomes, and the split plan leaves no-go and nonstop decay to a later ribosome-quality-control module; they are named here by symbol only. ABCE1 splits ribosomes in both settings; only its post-termination role is pinned to GO-CAM activities here, and its activities in the rescue and RQC models (gomodel:63e5937500001279, gomodel:63f809ec00000512, gomodel:63f809ec00000560, gomodel:65f3ae5c00000257) are left for that module. (8) Termination is the canonical eRF1-eRF3 route. Stop-codon readthrough, selenocysteine insertion (see selenocysteine_biosynthesis_incorporation) and nonsense-mediated decay (sibling nonsense_mediated_mrna_decay, PW:0000548, where eRF1-eRF3 at premature stop codons interact with UPF1) are outside. The eRF1 glutamine methyltransferase HEMK2-TRMT112 modifies eRF1 and is not a participant. (9) Aminoacyl-tRNA biosynthesis (PW:0001159, a diagram link of PW:0000101) supplies the substrates of eEF1A and is out of scope; aminoacyl-tRNAs are inputs, and the cytosolic aminoacyl-tRNA synthetases and the multi-synthetase complex are not modelled here. Mitochondrial elongation and termination (TUFM, TSFM, GFM1 and others) are in mitochondrial_translation; co-translational targeting of the nascent chain is in er_cotranslational_translocation. (10) Substrate-versus-participant check: the module lists only gene products that perform a step (carrying, exchanging, translocating, releasing, splitting) or directly regulate one (EEF2K). The aminoacyl-tRNAs, mRNA and nascent chain are acted on and are not participants. Ubiquitin ligases that degrade stalled eEF1A or eRF1 (GCN1, RNF14, RNF25) act on these factors in ribosome-collision responses and are excluded. GO-CAM links (gocams/index.tsv). gomodel:63e5937500001319 (Translation termination by the eRF1-eRF3 complex, followed by ribosome dissociation by ABCE1, Human; the seed's best_gocam) has GSPT1 GTPase activity directly positively regulating ETF1, which directly positively regulates ABCE1; all three activities are pinned, and the two edges are the eRF3-eRF1 connection and the termination-to-recycling connection. Its ETF1 activity uses the parent term translation termination factor activity (GO:0008079), and its ABCE1 activity uses ribonucleoside triphosphate phosphatase activity (GO:0017111), the parent of the ATP hydrolysis activity used here; both are consistent with the module, which uses the more specific terms supported by the experimental rows and the ABCE1 review. The ABCE1 activity in gomodel:65f3ae5c00000170 (60S recycling after termination) is also pinned. EEF1A1 in gomodel:63d320cd00001688 and ETF1 in gomodel:63d320cd00001733 (ribosome-stalling responses leading to their degradation) are pinned to their annotons, whose functions match; the ligases in those models are not modelled.
