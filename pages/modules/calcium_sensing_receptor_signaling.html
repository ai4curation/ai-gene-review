Calcium-sensing receptor signaling — extracellular calcium activation of CaSR, Gq/11-phospholipase C and Gi/o-adenylyl cyclase coupling, AP2-dependent endocytic signalling, PKC feedback, and GRK/beta-arrestin desensitization

The calcium-sensing receptor (CaSR) is a class C G protein-coupled receptor that detects the concentration of extracellular calcium and sets the body's calcium set point. It is most abundant in the parathyroid glands, where its activation suppresses parathyroid hormone secretion, and in the kidney, where it regulates calcium reabsorption. CaSR is an obligate disulfide-linked homodimer; calcium ions bind cooperatively in the Venus flytrap domains of its large extracellular region, closing them and driving an asymmetric rearrangement of the seven-transmembrane domains. The active receptor couples to G alpha q/11, which activates phospholipase C beta to produce inositol trisphosphate (release of stored calcium) and diacylglycerol (protein kinase C), and to G alpha i/o, which inhibits adenylyl cyclase and lowers cAMP. Loss-of-function mutations in CASR, in GNA11 (G alpha 11) or in AP2S1 (the sigma 2 subunit of the clathrin adaptor AP-2, which mediates CaSR endocytosis and sustained signalling) each cause familial hypocalciuric hypercalcaemia, and gain-of-function CASR and GNA11 mutations cause autosomal dominant hypocalcaemia. Protein kinase C phosphorylation of the receptor tail feeds back to blunt calcium mobilization, and G protein-coupled receptor kinases and beta-arrestins desensitize the receptor.

Downstream PKA, IP3 receptor calcium release and PTH secretion are outside the module. Added genes. GNA11, GNAQ, GNAI1, GNAI2 and AP2S1 are not on RGD's curated list for PW:0001141 (RGD represents the G protein steps as links to PW:0000229 and PW:0000228) but the coupling steps cannot be modelled without the G alpha subunits, and GNA11 and AP2S1 are the FHH2 and FHH3 genes that regulate CaSR signalling directly (PMID:23802516, PMID:23222959). AP2S1 is modelled as a regulator that sustains signalling: FHH3 mutations reduce CaSR endocytosis and decrease cell sensitivity to extracellular calcium, and loss of the CaSR dileucine motif that AP2 sigma 2 recognises also reduces intracellular signalling, so AP2-mediated endocytosis supports rather than ends the response here. Termination and feedback. GRK4 and GRK2 desensitize CaSR and beta-arrestin2 matters in vivo (PMID:15637145); GRK3, also on the RGD list, has no specific CaSR evidence in the cached literature and is mentioned only. PKC phosphorylation of Thr888 blunts receptor-evoked calcium release (PMID:9694886). The RGS proteins on the RGD list (RGS2, RGS4, RGS5, RGS12) act on the G alpha subunits generally and are not modelled as parts; the RGD record notes that several inhibit CaSR signalling, but no cached paper was found to ground a specific one. Filamin A (FLNA) and RHOA, the G12/13-Rho route, Gs coupling in breast cancer cells, and the ERK/MAPK outputs (RGD links PW:0000230, PW:0000105, PW:0000102) are out of scope; FLNA binds the receptor tail and scaffolds Rho signalling (RGD record text) and belongs with a Rho module. GO-CAM grounding. CASR is linked to its two human models: the Gq model gomodel:67c10cc400004380 (CASR, GNAQ, PLCB3) and the Gi model gomodel:67c10cc400004342 (CASR, GNAI1, ADCY6). PLCB3 and ADCY6 are in the referenced generic modules. GNA11 is not in either CASR model, which uses GNAQ. The GRK2 and arrestin annotons are linked, as in pth_receptor_signaling, to the desensitization model gomodel:67f85f2b00004426 built for C5AR1 (the seed's best_gocam lead): gene and function match but not the receptor. No cached GO-CAM covers AP2S1 with CaSR. GRK4, ARRB1 and ARRB2 have no gene review yet in this repository's genes/human tree, which is curation follow-up for the project plan.
