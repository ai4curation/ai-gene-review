Homologous recombination repair of DNA double-strand breaks — MRN-CtIP and EXO1/BLM-DNA2 end resection, RPA coating, BRCA1-PALB2-BRCA2 and RAD51-paralog loading of RAD51, strand invasion with RAD54, D-loop extension, and BTR dissolution versus SLX4-MUS81/GEN1 resolution; MRE11/RAD50/NBN/RBBP8/EXO1/BLM/DNA2/RPA/BRCA1/PALB2/BRCA2/RAD51B/RAD51C/RAD51D/XRCC2/XRCC3/RAD51/RAD54L/RAD54B

Homologous recombination (HR) repairs a DNA double-strand break (DSB) by copying the missing information from an intact homologous duplex, normally the sister chromatid, and is therefore restricted to the S and G2 phases of the cell cycle. Once the upstream ATM signalling cascade has marked the break and BRCA1 has displaced 53BP1-RIF1, the DNA ends are resected to expose 3' single-stranded tails. Resection begins with short-range processing by the MRE11-RAD50-NBN (MRN) complex, whose MRE11 endonuclease, stimulated by phosphorylated CtIP (RBBP8), nicks the 5'-terminated strand at a distance from the end and MRE11 3'-5' exonuclease then degrades back toward the break. Long-range resection extends the tail over kilobases through either of two machineries: the 5'-3' exonuclease EXO1, or the RecQ helicase BLM (or WRN) unwinding the duplex while the DNA2 nuclease degrades the 5' strand. The heterotrimeric single-stranded DNA binding protein RPA coats the exposed tails, removing secondary structure and protecting them. BRCA1 binds PALB2, which in turn binds BRCA2; BRCA2 delivers RAD51 to the RPA-coated DNA and stabilises nascent RAD51 filaments, and the RAD51 paralog complexes BCDX2 (RAD51B-RAD51C-RAD51D-XRCC2) and CX3 (RAD51C-XRCC3) assist RAD51 nucleation and filament stability. The RAD51-ssDNA presynaptic filament searches for homology and invades the donor duplex, forming a displacement loop (D-loop); the Swi2/Snf2-family translocases RAD54 (RAD54L) and RAD54B promote homologous pairing and remodel the D-loop. The invading 3' end is extended by DNA polymerase delta, with polymerase eta able to initiate synthesis on D-loops. The D-loop can then be resolved by synthesis-dependent strand annealing, by break-induced replication, or by second-end capture to form a double Holliday junction. Double Holliday junctions are either dissolved into non-crossover products by the BLM-TOP3A-RMI1-RMI2 (BTR) dissolvasome, or cleaved by the structure-specific endonucleases SLX1-SLX4, MUS81-EME1 and GEN1, which can give crossovers.

Part 8: processing of joint molecules
Dissolution or nucleolytic resolution of double Holliday junctionsReactionjunction_processing

After second-end capture, the double Holliday junction is either dissolved by the BTR complex, giving only non-crossovers, or cut by structure-specific endonucleases, giving crossovers or non-crossovers. SDSA and BIR avoid double Holliday junctions and are not modelled as separate parts (see notes).

Variant set: BTR dissolution versus SLX4-MUS81/GEN1 resolution by double Holliday junction processing route [Route] (One Or More)

Dissolution is the primary route in S/G2; junctions that escape it are resolved in late G2/M by SLX1-SLX4-MUS81-EME1 and in mitosis by GEN1, so all may act in one cell cycle.

Recombinant GEN1 and Yen1 resolve Holliday junctions by the introduction of symmetrically related cuts across the junction point, to produce nicked duplex products in which the nicks can be readily ligated.
