cAMP/PKA signal transduction — adenylyl cyclase cAMP production, PKA holoenzyme activation, AKAP anchoring and PDE termination; ADCY1-10, PRKAR1A/1B/2A/2B, PRKACA/B/G, AKAP1/5/6/7/10/12, EZR, PDE1-4/7/8/10/11

The cAMP/PKA cassette is the intracellular core shared by every Gs-coupled receptor and by bicarbonate/calcium sensing through soluble adenylyl cyclase. Cyclic AMP is made from ATP by ten mammalian adenylyl cyclases. Nine (ADCY1-9) are polytopic plasma-membrane enzymes whose two cytoplasmic catalytic domains form the active site; they are stimulated by GTP-bound G alpha s and are grouped by how other inputs regulate them: calcium/calmodulin stimulates ADCY1, ADCY3 and ADCY8; G beta gamma conditionally stimulates ADCY2, ADCY4 and ADCY7; G alpha i and calcium inhibit ADCY5 and ADCY6; ADCY9 is insensitive to forskolin. The tenth, ADCY10 (soluble adenylyl cyclase), has no membrane spans, is not regulated by heterotrimeric G proteins and is activated by bicarbonate and calcium. cAMP acts mainly through cAMP-dependent protein kinase (PKA). The inactive PKA holoenzyme is a tetramer of a regulatory-subunit dimer (type I, RIalpha/RIbeta, PRKAR1A/PRKAR1B; or type II, RIIalpha/RIIbeta, PRKAR2A/PRKAR2B) holding two catalytic subunits (Calpha, Cbeta, Cgamma; PRKACA, PRKACB, PRKACG) with a pseudosubstrate or substrate-like inhibitory segment in each active site. Each regulatory subunit has two cAMP-binding domains, and type I binds cAMP with higher affinity than type II. Binding of four cAMP molecules releases the catalytic subunits, which phosphorylate serine and threonine residues in R-R-X-S/T motifs of cytoplasmic and nuclear substrates. Where in the cell this happens is set by A-kinase anchoring proteins (AKAPs), which bind the docking/dimerisation domain of the regulatory dimer through an amphipathic helix, mostly that of type II PKA, and assemble PKA with substrates, phosphatases, phosphodiesterases and sometimes adenylyl cyclases at the plasma membrane, mitochondria, the perinuclear membrane and other sites. The signal is ended by cyclic-nucleotide phosphodiesterases (PDEs), the only enzymes that hydrolyse cAMP to 5'-AMP. PDE4, PDE7 and PDE8 are cAMP-specific, whereas PDE1, PDE2, PDE3, PDE10 and PDE11 hydrolyse both cAMP and cGMP and are regulated by calcium/calmodulin (PDE1) or by cGMP (stimulation of PDE2, inhibition of PDE3). PDEs tethered to AKAP complexes shape local cAMP microdomains, and PKA phosphorylation of some PDE4 isoforms provides negative feedback.

Derived from the RGD Pathway Ontology diagram PW:0000543 (protein kinase A (PKA) signaling pathway) through the RGD_PATHWAY_ONTOLOGY project's diagram->module route. The PW record cites GO:0010737 protein kinase A signaling as its GO cross-reference; that term is obsolete (go-basic 2026-07-26) with consider pointers to GO:0007189 and GO:0141156. The project's curated SSSOM mapping uses GO:0141156 cAMP/PKA signal transduction (skos:closeMatch), which is the module concept here. The related obsolete GO:0019933 cAMP-mediated signaling points to the same two terms. RGD curates 50 human genes to PW:0000543: ADCY1-10; AKAP1, AKAP3-14 and PALM2AKAP2; EZR; CRHR2 and GLP1R; PDE1A/B/C, PDE2A, PDE3A/B, PDE4A-D, PDE7A/B, PDE8A/B, PDE10A, PDE11A; PRKACA/B/G; PRKAR1A/1B/2A/2B. Forty-four of them are modelled as participants or representative members below; the rest are discussed here. Boundary decisions. (1) This is the intracellular cassette, not a receptor pathway. modules/gpcr_camp_pka_signaling.yaml already models a compact five-gene receptor -> Gs -> ADCY5 -> PRKACA -> CREB1 chain (GO:0007188). It is not edited and not duplicated. Its receptor/Gs node is included here as an optional upstream part by module_ref (gpcr_camp_pka_signaling#gs_gpcr_activation). It is optional because ADCY10 makes cAMP without any receptor or G protein, and because Gi, Gq-calcium and G beta gamma inputs also tune the transmembrane cyclases. This module is the detailed expansion of that module's adenylyl cyclase and PKA nodes. CRHR2 and GLP1R, which RGD lists, are Gs-coupled receptors that feed the cassette. They are upstream context, belong to receptor-specific modules (as the dopamine, vasopressin and acetylcholine modules do for their receptors) and are not modelled. RGD's subpathway PW:0000227 (Galphas signalling) is mentioned only here and is not an evidence source_id. CREB and the other PKA substrates drawn by RGD as outcomes (gene expression, metabolism, ion channels, exocytosis) are out of scope. Under the CLAUDE.md substrate-vs-participant rule, a PKA substrate does none of the work of the cassette, even though GO:0141156's definition ends at effector activation and CREB1, ATF1 and CREM carry the term in GOA. (2) cAMP production is a variant set on the axis of AC regulatory class, selection ONE_OR_MORE because most tissues express several isoforms (RGD: "Most tissues express more than one AC"). The grouping (Ca2+/CaM-stimulated AC1/3/8; G beta gamma-conditional AC2/4/7; Gi/Ca2+- inhibited AC5/6; forskolin-insensitive AC9; soluble AC10) is the standard classification. Of these, the calcium/calmodulin activation of AC1/AC8 and the calcium inhibition of AC5/AC6 are quoted from PMID:11264454. The G beta gamma class is stated from general knowledge, without a cached quote. All variants use adenylate cyclase activity GO:0004016, which is experimental (EXP/IDA/IMP) for ADCY2, ADCY5, ADCY6, ADCY7, ADCY9 and ADCY10. ADCY1, ADCY3, ADCY4 and ADCY8 carry it only by IBA/ISS/TAS/IEA. The calcium/calmodulin class could use the child GO:0008294 calcium- and calmodulin-responsive adenylate cyclase activity, but it is only ISS/NAS/TAS on ADCY1 and ADCY8 and was not used. (3) GO:0141156 placement and the comparator check. In human GOA (QuickGO, 2026-10), GO:0141156 is carried by 16 gene products. Most are effectors (CREB1, ATF1, CREM, CREBBP) or receptor/ligand context (EDN1, EDNRA, GHRHR). The anchors EZR (IMP), AKAP12 (IEA) and AKAP6 (ISS) also carry it. No adenylyl cyclase, PKA subunit or PDE does. GO:0141156 is a recent term, and its definition names adenylate cyclase cAMP production and PKA activation as the cassette's steps. The cyclases, the regulatory subunits and the catalytic subunits each do one of those steps, so the absence reads as term recency rather than a convention against annotating the enzymes. The term is therefore placed on the AC, PKA-R, PKA-C and AKAP annotons, matching the AKAP12 gene review, whose core anchoring and scaffolding functions are directly involved in GO:0141156. PDEs end the signal and are given cAMP catabolic process GO:0006198, the process they catalyse, not GO:0141156. The regulation term GO:0141162 negative regulation of cAMP/PKA signal transduction would also be defensible for PDEs, but no PDE carries it and it is not asserted. (4) PKA holoenzyme. The regulatory subunits are a variant set on the axis of holoenzyme type (type I RIalpha/RIbeta; type II RIIalpha/RIIbeta). All four use cAMP-dependent protein kinase regulator activity GO:0008603 (IDA on all four; also GO:0004862 inhibitor activity IDA), and cAMP binding is in the role text. The catalytic subunits are a separate variant set (selection ONE_OR_MORE). PRKACA and PRKACB use cAMP-dependent protein kinase activity GO:0004691 (IDA). PRKACG, a testis-expressed retrogene, has GO:0004691 only by IBA/IEA/TAS; its only experimental MF row is potassium channel inhibitor activity (EXP). The holoenzyme is not drawn as a single complex annoton because the active, released catalytic subunit is a monomer outside GO:0005952; the complex term is used for context in the regulatory annotons' role text instead. (5) AKAP anchoring is an optional part (PKA is catalytically competent without anchoring, and type I PKA is largely cytosolic). It is a variant set on the axis of anchoring compartment, selection ZERO_OR_MORE, naming the AKAPs whose anchoring role is supported by cached evidence: AKAP5 (AKAP79/150) and AKAP12 (gravin) and AKAP7 (AKAP18) at the plasma membrane; AKAP1 (D-AKAP1) and AKAP10 (D-AKAP2) at mitochondria; AKAP6 (mAKAP) at the perinuclear membrane; and EZR at membrane rafts. EZR is included. It is an ERM-family actin-membrane linker, not an AKAP by family, but it has a documented AKAP activity: it is the AKAP that targets type I PKA to T-cell lipid rafts (PMID:17911601), and GOA gives it IPI RI- and RII-binding rows and an IMP row for GO:0141156. MF on AKAP annotons is protein kinase A regulatory subunit binding GO:0034237, the AKAP7/AKAP10/AKAP12 gene-review core function and an experimental row for AKAP1, AKAP5, AKAP9 and AKAP14. AKAP6, AKAP7 and AKAP10 have only the parent GO:0051018 protein kinase A binding (IPI/IDA) in GOA, and EZR has GO:0034237 by IPI. AKAP3 and AKAP4 (sperm fibrous sheath), AKAP8 (AKAP95, nuclear matrix), AKAP9 (yotiao/AKAP450, centrosome/Golgi and KCNQ1), AKAP11 (AKAP220), AKAP13 (AKAP-Lbc, a Rho GEF), AKAP14 (AKAP28, cilia) and PALM2AKAP2 (AKAP2/AKAP-KL) are family members on the RGD list that are not named. Adenylyl cyclase production of cAMPReactioncamp_production

Adenylyl cyclases convert ATP to cAMP and pyrophosphate. The nine transmembrane isoforms are stimulated by G alpha s and differ in how calcium, G beta gamma and G alpha i regulate them. The soluble isoform responds to bicarbonate and calcium.

cAMP-induced dissociation of the PKA holoenzymeRegulatory Steppka_holoenzyme_activation

The R2C2 holoenzyme (cAMP-dependent protein kinase complex, GO:0005952) is held inactive by the regulatory dimer. Binding of four cAMP molecules, two to each regulatory subunit, changes the regulatory subunits' conformation and releases the two catalytic monomers.

Variant set: PKA holoenzyme type by regulatory subunit class (type I RI or type II RII) (One Or More)
file:projects/RGD_PATHWAY_ONTOLOGY/pathways/PW_0000543.yaml
RGD states two type I and two type II regulatory subunits in humans and the higher cAMP affinity of type I.
Each regulatory subunit has two cAMP binding sites; type I has a higher affinity for cAMP than type II.

Type I binds cAMP with higher affinity than type II; type II is the main AKAP-anchored form, type I is largely cytosolic but can be anchored by dual-specificity AKAPs and by ezrin.

Cyclic-nucleotide phosphodiesterases hydrolyse cAMP to 5'-AMP, setting the amplitude, duration and spatial extent of cAMP and so of PKA activity. Anchored PDEs (PDE4D with gravin or mAKAP) shape local microdomains.

Variant set: Phosphodiesterase substrate selectivity by substrate selectivity of the PDE family (cAMP-specific or dual cAMP/cGMP) (One Or More)

RGD describes PDEs as the only cyclic-nucleotide-degrading enzymes and groups the 11 families by selectivity.
cAMP is hydrolyzed by phosphodiesterases (PDE), the only enzymes degrading cycling nucleotides.

cGMP-specific PDE5, PDE6 and PDE9 are not part of this cassette and are not modelled.
