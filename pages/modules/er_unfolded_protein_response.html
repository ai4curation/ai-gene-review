Endoplasmic reticulum unfolded protein response

The endoplasmic reticulum unfolded protein response (UPR-ER) matches the folding capacity of the ER to the load of client proteins entering it. In animals ER stress is sensed by three ER-membrane transducers. The kinase-endoribonuclease IRE1 removes a small intron from the XBP1 mRNA, and the spliced mRNA encodes the active bZIP transcription factor XBP1s, which induces chaperone, folding and ER-associated degradation genes, including the BiP gene. The eIF2alpha kinase PERK phosphorylates eIF2alpha to attenuate general translation and so reduce client load; reduced ternary complex favours translation of the uORF-containing ATF4 mRNA, and ATF4 then activates cytoprotective genes. ATF6 is an ER-membrane-anchored bZIP factor that is released by intramembrane proteolysis and enters the nucleus. The ER HSP70 BiP is both a luminal folding chaperone and a UPR target, and its binding to the luminal domains of the transducers keeps them inactive until misfolded proteins compete it away. The IRE1 arm is the ancestral eukaryotic core: budding yeast has only Ire1 and its spliced-mRNA target Hac1, an unrelated bZIP factor that plays the XBP1 role, while the PERK and ATF6 arms are animal additions. The arms are partly redundant; in C. elegans, loss of the IRE1-XBP1 arm together with PERK or ATF6 causes larval arrest, and neuronal XBP1s can activate the UPR-ER in distant tissues.

Boundary. This module covers the three UPR-ER transducer arms (IRE1/XBP1, PERK/eIF2alpha/ATF4 and ATF6) and the ER HSP70 BiP, which is both a regulator of the transducers and a transcriptional output. It has four parts. It replaces the earlier C. elegans-only module MODULE:c_elegans_er_unfolded_protein_response; all worm members and their evidence are kept as representative members. Taxon scope. The module context is Metazoa (NCBITaxon:33208), the clade over which the three-arm boundary holds. The IRE1 arm is older: PAINT node PTN000359335 places IRE1-mediated UPR at an ancestral eukaryotic IRE1 node (donors include plant, fungal, fly and mouse genes). Its output varies by lineage and is modeled as the variant set ire1_output_transcription_factor (XBP1 in animals, the unrelated bZIP Hac1 in budding yeast). The yeast variant documents the ancestral single-arm core and lies outside the module's metazoan context. Yeast Ire1 also binds unfolded proteins directly, whereas mammalian IRE1alpha activation depends mainly on BiP release (PMID:19538957). The tRNA ligase that completes splicing (yeast Trl1; RtcB in animals) is not modeled. Representatives. Each role is a FAMILY selector with the worm protein and one human exemplar (plus yeast Ire1 and Hac1 for the IRE1 arm). All accessions follow their gene reviews (worm ire-1 Q09499, xbp-1 G5EE07, pek-1 Q19192, eif-2alpha Q9BKU3, atf-4 Q22156, atf-6 Q20435, hsp-4 P20163, hsp-3 P27420; human ERN1 O75460, XBP1 P17861, EIF2AK3 Q9NZJ5, ATF4 P18848, ATF6 P18850, HSPA5 P11021; yeast IRE1 P32361, HAC1 P41546). The XBP1 and ATF6 annotons use DNA-binding transcription activator activity (GO:0001228) as chosen by the human XBP1 and ATF6 reviews; the worm xbp-1 and atf-6 reviews use the parent term GO:0000981, and the yeast HAC1 review also uses GO:0000981. Human XBP1 acts as the spliced isoform XBP1s (P17861-2 in GO-CAM gomodel:689e7a5d00000567). eIF2alpha. eIF2alpha (worm EIF-2alpha, Q9BKU3) is modeled as a participant of the PERK arm, following its gene review, which places it in the PERK-mediated UPR on the grounds that phosphorylated eIF2alpha does the attenuation work: it converts eIF2 into an inhibitor of its own recycling by eIF2B. The function term is its ordinary translation initiation factor activity (GO:0003743), negatively regulated by PERK. GO has no molecular-function term for the inhibitory activity of phosphorylated eIF2 toward eIF2B, so the annoton records the participant correctly but not the attenuating activity itself; this tension is noted on the annoton and is an ontology question, not a knowledge gap. Human EIF2S1 has no gene review and is not listed. Integrated stress response. GCN2 is a second eIF2alpha kinase activated by amino-acid limitation and, in worms, by mitochondrial stress (PMID:22719267, PMID:23692540), not by ER stress. It belongs with the integrated stress response, of which the PERK branch is the ER-specific input. ATF4 is included as the output of the PERK branch; in mammals PERK dependence of ATF4 translation is shown (PMID:11106749), but in C. elegans the dependence of ATF-4 induction on PEK-1 itself has not been tested (see knowledge_gaps). Its process term is integrated stress response signaling, following the atf-4 review. Left out. The C. elegans ABU/PQN "activated in blocked UPR" genes (PMID:12186849) are a compensatory response in xbp-1 mutants. The neuronal signal for cell-non-autonomous UPR-ER activation in worms (small clear vesicle release, PMID:23791175) is inter-tissue regulation and not modeled as a part. ERAD and ER-phagy are downstream proteostasis outputs. Related modules. The mitochondrial UPR is MODULE:mitochondrial_upr and the HSF1 cytosolic heat shock response is MODULE:heat_shock_response. Under ER stress IRE1 oligomerizes and trans-autophosphorylates, and its cytosolic endoribonuclease removes a small intron from the mRNA of its bZIP target (XBP1 in animals, HAC1 in budding yeast). The spliced mRNA encodes the active transcription factor, which binds UPR-element promoters, including the BiP locus, and drives most inducible UPR genes.

Part 2: PERK/eIF2alpha translational arm with ATF4 output
PERK eIF2alpha phosphorylation and ATF4Signaling Pathwayperk_arm

PERK phosphorylates eIF2alpha on ER stress (Ser51 in mammals, Ser49 in C. elegans). Phosphorylated eIF2 inhibits its own recycling by eIF2B, attenuating global translation and reducing client load on the ER. Reduced ternary complex favours translation of ATF4, whose 5' UTR uORFs make it a translational-control reporter; ATF4 then activates chaperone, amino-acid and cytoprotective genes. In worms PEK-1 is required for about a quarter of inducible UPR genes but not for constitutive UPR genes.

Part 3: ATF6 membrane-anchored transcription factor arm
ATF6 armSignaling Pathwayatf6_arm

ATF6 is a type II ER-membrane bZIP factor. In mammals ER stress moves it to the Golgi, where Site-1 and Site-2 proteases release its cytosolic bZIP domain, which enters the nucleus and induces chaperone genes, including BiP, and XBP1. In worms ATF-6 regulates few inducible but many constitutive UPR genes, binds promoters directly under ER stress, and controls ER calcium buffering through calreticulin.

Part 4: ER HSP70 (BiP) chaperone as transducer repressor and UPR output
BiP ER HSP70 chaperonesBiological Processbip_chaperones

BiP (human HSPA5; worm paralogs HSP-4 and HSP-3) is the ER-luminal HSP70. It folds client proteins in an ATP-dependent cycle, and in unstressed mammalian cells it binds the luminal domains of IRE1, PERK and ATF6 and keeps them inactive until misfolded clients titrate it away. BiP is also a major UPR target. In C. elegans hsp-4 is strongly stress-inducible and is the standard UPR-ER reporter, while hsp-3 is expressed at higher basal levels; direct binding of either paralog to the worm transducers has not been shown.
