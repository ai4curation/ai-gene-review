Bacterial sulfoquinovose catabolism (sulfoglycolysis and sulfo-SMO)

A reusable bacterial module for breakdown of sulfoquinovose (SQ; 6-deoxy-6-sulfo-D-glucose), the polar head group of the plant and cyanobacterial sulfolipid sulfoquinovosyl diacylglycerol. An optional glycoside hydrolase first releases free SQ from sulfoquinovosyl glycerides. SQ is then consumed by one of several alternative, biochemically characterized routes. Three C-C bond cleavage (sulfoglycolytic) routes split the C6 sulfosugar into a non-sulfonated C3 unit used for growth and the C3 organosulfonate 3-sulfolactaldehyde (SLA), which is then reduced to 2,3-dihydroxypropane-1-sulfonate (DHPS) or oxidized to 3-sulfolactate (SL) for excretion: the sulfo-EMP route (SQ isomerase, sulfofructose kinase, sulfofructose-1-phosphate aldolase), the sulfo-ED route (SQ dehydrogenase, sulfogluconolactonase, sulfogluconate dehydratase, KDSG aldolase), and the sulfo-SFT route (SQ isomerase and sulfofructose transaldolase). In the oxidative sulfo-SMO route a two-component flavin-dependent SQ monooxygenase cleaves the C-S bond, releasing sulfite and 6-dehydroglucose, which is reduced to glucose so that all six carbons are used. Uptake, regulation, and downstream mineralization of DHPS, SL or sulfite by other enzymes or community members are outside the module boundary.

Boundary decision. The routes share one input (SQ, released by an optional sulfoquinovosidase) and one ecological role (use of the sulfosugar as carbon and energy source), but no route-defining enzyme is shared across all of them; SQ isomerase is shared only by sulfo-EMP and sulfo-SFT, and SLA dehydrogenase only by sulfo-ED and sulfo-SFT. They are therefore modeled as alternative implementations in a single route variant_set (selection ONE_OR_MORE, because some bacteria carry more than one route) rather than as four standalone modules, with each variant carrying its own ordered parts and connections. The terminal SLA reduction or oxidation is modeled inside each route with the enzyme documented for that route's exemplar organism; the same SLA-redox alternatives recur across routes (for example, Arthrobacter sulfo-EMP clusters use an SLA dehydrogenase instead of YihU, and anaerobic sulfo-SFT clusters use an SLA reductase instead of SftD/SlaB), as summarized in PMID:41676062 and PMID:32919372. Not modeled: the sulfo-ASDO route (Fe(II)/2-oxoglutarate-dependent SQ dioxygenase SqoD) and the sulfo-TK route (SF transketolase SqwGH), which have fewer characterized reference proteins in UniProtKB; the sulfo-EMP2 variant (SqvD/SqiK/SqiA isozymes); the SQ mutarotases YihR/SqvB; SQ and sulfoquinovosyl glyceride uptake systems (YihO, SmoEFGH); the transcriptional repressor CsqR; and downstream desulfonation of DHPS, SL, isethionate or 3-sulfopropionate (HpsGH, SuyAB, IseG, HpfG and related enzymes), which is a distinct community-level module. Reaction chaining across the non-CoA sulfosugar intermediates was checked manually against the cited Rhea equations. Ontology gaps noted for GO editors: GO has specific molecular-function terms only for the sulfo-EMP reactions (GO:0061593, GO:0061594, GO:0061595, GO:0061596) and for sulfoquinovosidase (GO:1990929); the sulfo-ED (EC 1.1.1.390, 3.1.1.99, 4.2.1.162, 4.1.2.58), sulfo-SFT (EC 2.2.1.14), SQ monooxygenase (EC 1.14.14.181) and 6-dehydroglucose reductase (EC 1.1.1.432) reactions have Rhea entries but no rhea2go/ec2go mapping, so their annotons use the nearest existing parent GO term with the exact Rhea reaction as evidence. GO:0061722 sulphoglycolysis is defined by DHAP and pyruvate formation, which covers the sulfo-EMP and sulfo-ED routes but not the F6P-forming sulfo-SFT route or the sulfite-releasing sulfo-SMO route; the root concept therefore uses the broader GO:1902777.

Defines the sulfo-SMO route in A. tumefaciens, in which all six SQ carbons are used and sulfite is released.
A sulfoquinovosidase hydrolyzes the SQ glycoside and the liberated SQ is acted on by a flavin mononucleotide-dependent sulfoquinovose monooxygenase, in concert with an NADH-dependent flavin reductase, to release sulfite and 6-oxo-glucose. An NAD(P)H-dependent oxidoreductase reduces the 6-oxo-glucose to glucose, enabling entry into primary metabolic pathways.
