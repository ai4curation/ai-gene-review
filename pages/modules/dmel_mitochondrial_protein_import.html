Mitochondrial protein import machinery in Drosophila melanogaster

Most mitochondrial proteins are made on cytosolic ribosomes and imported post-translationally. Nearly all enter through the TOM complex of the outer membrane, whose channel is formed by Tom40. Beta-barrel outer membrane proteins are then handed by the small TIM chaperones of the intermembrane space to the SAM complex for insertion. Carrier-type inner membrane proteins are inserted by the TIM22 complex, and presequence-bearing precursors are translocated by the TIM23 complex, driven into the matrix by the presequence translocase-associated motor (PAM) built around mitochondrial Hsp70 (Hsc70-5). Matrix presequences are removed by the mitochondrial processing peptidase, and some intermembrane space-sorted precursors are further cleaved by the inner membrane peptidase. Cysteine-rich intermembrane space proteins are imported and oxidatively folded by the Mia40-Alr disulfide relay. Drosophila has testis-specific paralogs of several subunits (for example tomboy40, tomboy20 and several Tim17 and Tim50 paralogs).
