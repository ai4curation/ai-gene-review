Diadenosine tetraphosphate (Ap4A) turnover

A reusable metabolic module for the production and removal of diadenosine 5',5'''-P1,P4-tetraphosphate (Ap4A), a dinucleoside polyphosphate that accumulates under stress and has been proposed to act as an intracellular signal. Ap4A is made as a side reaction of aminoacyl-tRNA synthetases, either by transfer of the aminoacyl-adenylate AMP to ATP (as in lysyl-tRNA synthetase) or by direct condensation of two ATP molecules (as in glycyl-tRNA synthetase). It is removed by one of three unrelated enzyme chemistries: asymmetrical Nudix hydrolases that yield ATP + AMP (animals and other eukaryotes), symmetrical ApaH-type hydrolases that yield two ADP (proteobacteria), and Ap4A phosphorylases that yield ADP + ATP by phosphorolysis (fungi). Downstream Ap4A effectors (for example HINT1 or transcription factors) and the related Ap3A/FHIT system are outside the module boundary.

The term is ancestral to the family (see the PTN000482012 node assessment). SF7 is left out of the applicable set because none of its members has been assayed. Diadenosine tetraphosphate (Ap4A) turnoverMetabolic Pathwaydiadenosine_tetraphosphate_turnover
diadenosine polyphosphate metabolic processGO:0015959

The module boundary is synthesis plus removal of Ap4A. Effectors that bind Ap4A (HINT1, MITF/USF2 signalling, Ap4A-binding proteins in bacteria) and the Ap3A-specific FHIT branch are downstream or parallel biology and are left out. Np4A/Ap4A can also be incorporated as non-canonical 5' RNA caps, and the ApaH, YqeK and NUDT2 removal enzymes also decap such RNAs; that epitranscriptomic role is a candidate for a separate, connected module. Whether Ap4A is a regulated signal or a constitutively cleared by-product appears to differ between organisms and is not resolved by this module. The asymmetrical Nudix variant is grounded on four completed gene reviews and the PTHR21340 family review; the ApaH, APA1 and synthetase exemplars (including YqeK) have no gene review in this repository yet. The KARS1 and GARS1 subfamily assignments were confirmed in the PANTHER 19.0 human classification file, which lists UniProtKB=Q15046 under PTHR42918:SF9 and UniProtKB=P41250 under PTHR10745:SF0. PANTHER families are not asserted for ApaH or APA1/APA2 because their PANTHER placements (ApaH in PTHR40942:SF4, APA1 and APA2 in different families) were not checked against the family trees. PTN000482012 is the PTHR21340 root node in the local PAINT data and also carries AMP and ATP biosynthetic process IBDs that the reviews judge to be over-annotations; only its GO:0004081 assertion is used here.

Variant set: Ap4A synthesis mechanism by enzyme mechanism (One Or More)

Both routes have the same net stoichiometry (2 ATP = Ap4A + diphosphate), which is what GO:0141192 describes. Several class I and class II synthetases can make Ap4A in vitro; lysyl- and glycyl-tRNA synthetases are used as exemplars because their Ap4A production is best characterised in cells.

Aminoacyl-adenylate route (LysRS type)Reactionaminoacyl_adenylate_route

The aminoacyl-AMP intermediate of tRNA aminoacylation is attacked by ATP instead of tRNA, releasing the amino acid and Ap4A. Ap4A output therefore depends on the cognate amino acid. In stimulated mammalian mast cells, phosphorylated LysRS leaves the multi-tRNA synthetase complex and produces Ap4A in the nucleus.

The four implementations are evolutionarily unrelated and use three chemistries with different products. A given organism usually relies on one of them, but some carry more than one Ap4A-degrading activity, so the selection is ONE_OR_MORE.

Asymmetrical Nudix hydrolase (NUDT2 type)Reactionasymmetrical_nudix_hydrolase

A small Nudix-fold hydrolase cleaves Ap4A between the gamma and delta phosphates to give ATP + AMP. This is the animal implementation; the same subfamily occurs in other eukaryotes, archaea and many bacteria.
