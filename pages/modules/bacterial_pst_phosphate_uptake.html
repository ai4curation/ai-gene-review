Bacterial Pst high-affinity phosphate uptake

A reusable bacterial ABC-transport module in which periplasmic PstS captures inorganic phosphate, the PstA/PstC membrane pair forms the translocation pathway, and PstB supplies ATP-dependent energy coupling. PhoU-mediated phosphate-homeostasis regulation and the PhoR/PhoB starvation response are adjacent regulatory systems rather than transport steps.

The module is species-neutral and uses Pseudomonas putida KT2440 proteins as concrete exemplars. PstB1 and PstB2 are represented as alternative PstB-like energy-coupling exemplars, not two simultaneously required ATPase steps. The broad PANTHER family containing PstB2 has a misleading iron-export subfamily label; the exact UniProt record and gene review provide the phosphate-transporter grounding. Whether PstB2 exchanges freely with the PP_2656-PP_2659 PstSACB1 assembly remains a species-level question.
