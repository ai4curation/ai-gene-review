L-threonine biosynthesis from L-aspartate

De novo biosynthesis of L-threonine from L-aspartate in five reactions, as found in bacteria, fungi and plants; animals lack the pathway. The first three reactions form the aspartate-family trunk that produces L-homoserine: aspartate kinase phosphorylates L-aspartate to 4-phospho-L-aspartate, aspartate-semialdehyde dehydrogenase reduces it with NADPH to L-aspartate 4-semialdehyde, and homoserine dehydrogenase reduces the semialdehyde with NAD(P)H to L-homoserine. L-homoserine is a branch point: homoserine kinase commits it to threonine as O-phospho-L-homoserine, and threonine synthase, a pyridoxal-phosphate enzyme, converts O-phospho-L-homoserine to L-threonine. The chemistry is conserved but the enzyme architecture varies. Aspartate kinase and homoserine dehydrogenase are separate monofunctional proteins in fungi, whereas bacteria such as Escherichia coli and plants also carry bifunctional aspartate kinase-homoserine dehydrogenase fusion proteins. The threonine synthases of plants are allosterically activated by S-adenosylmethionine, whereas the bacterial and fungal enzymes are not. In plants O-phospho-L-homoserine is also the substrate of cystathionine gamma-synthase, so the methionine branch diverges after the kinase. In bacteria and plants L-aspartate 4-semialdehyde also feeds the diaminopimelate lysine pathway; fungi make lysine by the unrelated alpha-aminoadipate pathway, so in fungi the trunk serves only threonine, isoleucine and methionine. Threonine feedback-inhibits the trunk, chiefly at aspartate kinase.

L-threonine biosynthetic processGO:0009088

Boundary: L-aspartate to L-threonine. Aspartate supply (pyruvate carboxylase and aspartate aminotransferase; YeastCyc PYC1, PYC2, AAT1, AAT2) belongs to aspartate_asparagine_metabolism. The methionine branch from L-homoserine is methionine_biosynthesis. Isoleucine synthesis from threonine and threonine catabolism (for example threonine aldolase) are downstream and outside the module. The module covers bacteria, fungi and plants, so no compartment is asserted: the fungal enzymes are cytosolic in both GO-CAMs, whereas the plant enzymes are chloroplastic (PMID:8204822, PMID:9636232). YeastCyc assessment: the five reaction assignments (HOM3, HOM2, HOM6, THR1, THR4) are correct and no S. cerevisiae enzyme is missing. Generalisation caveats: (1) in fungi aspartate semialdehyde does not feed lysine synthesis, which uses the alpha-aminoadipate pathway (lysine_biosynthesis_aminoadipate), whereas in bacteria and plants it also feeds the diaminopimelate pathway; (2) in plants O-phospho-L-homoserine, not L-homoserine, is the threonine and methionine branch point (PMID:9636232), so homoserine kinase is shared there; (3) aspartate kinase and homoserine dehydrogenase are modelled with monofunctional and bifunctional AK-HSDH variants. ASADH is one annoton because the two PANTHER families are divergent homologues catalysing the same reaction, not different architectures. GO-CAM disagreements: (a) none of the YeastPathways models has causal edges between the trunk and branch activities; (b) THRESYN-PWY, PWY3O-7 and P4-PWY-1 include AAT1 in the cytosol, although Aat1 is the mitochondrial isozyme; (c) P4-PWY-1 puts every activity under GO:0006566 L-threonine metabolic process rather than the specific biosynthetic processes, and its causal edges run MET6 -> MET17 -> MET2 -> THR1, the reverse of the biosynthetic order and linking the competing MET2 and THR1 branches; (d) PWY3O-7 mixes GO:0009090 (HOM3, HOM2) and GO:0009088 (HOM6) part_of assertions for the trunk. The PomBase model 678073a900003175 agrees with the module for the five steps and adds threonine aldolase, transport and tRNA ligation activities that are outside it. Regulation is not modelled: threonine inhibits yeast aspartate kinase and homoserine kinase (PMID:1847111, PMID:6821505), and HOM3, HOM2 and THR1 are under general amino acid control (PMID:1789001).
