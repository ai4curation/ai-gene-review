Autophagosome biogenesis by the core Atg machinery in Drosophila melanogaster

Macroautophagy begins with formation of a cup-shaped phagophore that expands and closes to form the double-membrane autophagosome. The core Atg proteins act in conserved functional units. The Atg1 kinase complex (Atg1, Atg13, Atg17/FIP200 and Atg101) initiates phagophore formation in response to nutrient starvation and TOR inhibition. The class III phosphatidylinositol 3-kinase complex (Vps34/Pi3K59F, Vps15, Atg6/Beclin 1 and Atg14) produces phosphatidylinositol 3-phosphate at the phagophore. Atg9 vesicles supply membrane and Atg9 has phospholipid scramblase activity, while Atg2, recruited with the PI3P-binding Atg18 proteins, transfers lipids to the expanding phagophore. Two ubiquitin-like conjugation systems act downstream: Atg7 and Atg10 conjugate Atg12 to Atg5, and the Atg12-Atg5-Atg16 complex promotes lipidation of Atg8 proteins with phosphatidylethanolamine by Atg7 and Atg3 after processing of Atg8 by the Atg4 proteases. In Drosophila, the fat body of starved larvae is the classic setting for studying this pathway.

Pi3K59F and Vps15 enter through the FlyBase autophagy-specific PI3 kinase complex group; they also act in endosomal PI3K complexes. Autophagosome-lysosome fusion (HOPS, SNAREs), cargo receptors and upstream TOR signalling are outside the module. The connections give the conventional order of action; parts also act partly in parallel. FlyBase groups realized by this module: ATG (FBgg0000076), ATG-CPX (FBgg0000216).
