Tryptophan catabolism to ommochrome pigments in Drosophila melanogaster

The brown ommochrome eye pigment xanthommatin is made from tryptophan through the kynurenine pathway, which in insects is also the main route of tryptophan catabolism. Tryptophan 2,3-dioxygenase (vermilion) opens the indole ring to give N-formylkynurenine, kynurenine formamidase (Kfase) releases kynurenine, and kynurenine 3-monooxygenase (cinnabar) hydroxylates it to 3-hydroxykynurenine. 3-Hydroxykynurenine is imported into pigment granules of the eye and other tissues by the White-Scarlet ABC transporter, where it is condensed to xanthommatin; this last step requires the heme peroxidase Cardinal. Mutations in v, cn, w, st and cd give the classic eye-colour phenotypes that lack or reduce brown pigment.

White also pairs with Brown to import pteridine (drosopterin) precursors; that heterodimer belongs to pteridine pigment synthesis and is not modelled here. No molecular function is given to the White-Scarlet transporter, since the exact transported substrate has been inferred from genetics rather than transport assays, or to Cardinal, which has peroxidase activity by homology but whose precise chemistry in xanthommatin synthesis is not settled. Kynurenic acid and other kynurenine side branches are not part of this module. FlyBase groups realized by this module: TRYCAT (FBgg0002189), OMMOBI (FBgg0002233).
