Carotenoid biosynthesis I — carotene backbone (GGPP -> phytoene -> lycopene -> cyclic carotenes)

The carotene backbone segment of carotenoid biosynthesis converts two molecules of the C20 isoprenoid geranylgeranyl diphosphate (GGPP) into the C40 cyclic carotenes that seed every downstream carotenoid class. Phytoene synthase performs the head-to-head condensation that commits GGPP to carotenoids, giving 15-cis-phytoene. Phytoene is then desaturated to all-trans-lycopene by one of two mutually exclusive routes: a single FAD-dependent CrtI-type desaturase in most bacteria and fungi, or the four-enzyme poly-cis route of cyanobacteria and plants (PDS, Z-ISO, ZDS, CRTISO) in which two plastoquinone-dependent desaturases each introduce two double bonds and two isomerases convert the poly-cis intermediates to the all-trans product. Lycopene cyclases then form beta rings (giving gamma- and beta-carotene) or, in plants, one epsilon ring (giving delta- and alpha-carotene). Supply of GGPP, xanthophyll formation by ring hydroxylation and epoxidation, ketolation, and oxidative cleavage to apocarotenoids (retinal, abscisic acid, strigolactones, neurosporaxanthin) are outside this module.

OpenScientist carotene backbone biosynthesis module research
Module-level research supports the three-operation boundary (phytoene synthesis, four desaturations with isomerization, cyclization), the mutually exclusive but functionally interchangeable CrtI-type versus poly-cis desaturation routes (bacterial CrtI substitutes for the whole PDS/Z-ISO/ZDS/CRTISO set in planta), and at least four non-homologous cyclase families (CrtY, CrtL-type LCYB/LCYE, the fungal bifunctional synthase/cyclase, CruA/CruP). Manual assessment keeps GGPP supply, the ORANGE chaperone of PSY, the plastid terminal oxidase that re-oxidizes plastoquinone for the plant desaturases, xanthophyll formation and apocarotenoid cleavage outside the reusable core; identifiers in this module were verified against UniProt, PANTHER, Rhea, GO and the cached GO-CAM, not taken from the report.

This reusable module is taxon-neutral at the root and records lineage differences in variant sets: the desaturation route (CrtI-type versus poly-cis) and the cyclase implementation (LCYB/CrtL-type, CrtY-type, or the fungal bifunctional phytoene synthase/lycopene cyclase). Representative members orient each family without restricting the module to those organisms. Grounding: Arabidopsis PSY1 (P37271), PDS (Q07356), Z-ISO (Q9SAC0), ZDS (Q38893), CRTISO (Q9M9Y8), LCYB (Q38933) and LCYE (Q38932); cyanobacterial pds/crtP (P26294), crtQ (P74306) and crtL (Q55276); Pantoea ananatis CrtB (P21683), CrtI (P21685) and CrtY (P21687); and Neurospora crassa al-2 (P37295) and al-1 (P21334), whose activities are also captured in the production GO-CAM 62f58d8800000065 (Carotenoid biosynthesis, N. crassa). PAINT ancestral nodes are cited only where a local PTHR42923 IBD slice exists (PDS and ZDS). GGPP synthesis is covered by the isoprenoid_diphosphate_biosynthesis module; xanthophyll and apocarotenoid branches are deliberately excluded and are candidates for sibling modules.

Part 2: Desaturation and isomerization of phytoene to all-trans-lycopene
Phytoene -> all-trans-lycopene (four desaturations)Metabolic Pathwayphytoene_desaturation_to_lycopene

Four double bonds are introduced into 15-cis-phytoene to give all-trans-lycopene. Bacteria and fungi use one FAD-dependent CrtI-type desaturase that also isomerizes the 15-cis bond; plants and cyanobacteria use two plastoquinone-dependent desaturases (PDS, ZDS) that each introduce two cis double bonds, and two isomerases (Z-ISO, CRTISO) that convert the poly-cis intermediates to the all-trans configuration.

lycopene biosynthetic processGO:1901177
Variant set: Phytoene desaturation route by desaturase enzyme system (Exactly One)

The two routes are mutually exclusive within a lineage: CrtI-type desaturases are found in most non-photosynthetic bacteria and in fungi, while the poly-cis route is found in cyanobacteria, algae and plants. Both are recognized by KEGG M00097.

CrtI-type single-enzyme desaturationReactioncrti_type_desaturation

A single FAD-dependent desaturase of the amine-oxidase fold carries out all four desaturations and the 15-cis to trans isomerization, converting 15-cis-phytoene to all-trans-lycopene via phytofluene, zeta-carotene and neurosporene. Fungal orthologs (al-1 type) add a fifth desaturation to 3,4-didehydrolycopene, feeding the torulene branch, which is outside this module.

Poly-cis desaturation/isomerization route (PDS, Z-ISO, ZDS, CRTISO)Metabolic Pathwaypoly_cis_desaturation_isomerization

Two plastoquinone-dependent desaturases each introduce two cis double bonds, and two isomerases restore the all-trans configuration: PDS converts 15-cis-phytoene to 9,15,9'-tri-cis-zeta-carotene; Z-ISO isomerizes the 15-cis bond to give 9,9'-di-cis-zeta-carotene; ZDS forms 7,7',9,9'-tetra-cis-lycopene (prolycopene); CRTISO isomerizes prolycopene to all-trans-lycopene. Light-driven photoisomerization can partially substitute for the two isomerases in illuminated tissue, but the enzymes are required in the dark and in non-photosynthetic tissues.

Part 3: Cyclization of lycopene to cyclic carotenes
Lycopene cyclization (lycopene -> beta-carotene or alpha-carotene)Metabolic Pathwaylycopene_cyclization

Lycopene cyclases fold the psi end groups of all-trans-lycopene into six-membered rings. Two beta-ring cyclizations give gamma-carotene then beta-carotene. In plants an epsilon cyclase forms one epsilon ring (delta-carotene), and a beta cyclase then forms the other ring to give alpha-carotene; the beta and epsilon branches are the branch point between the beta,beta- and beta,epsilon-xanthophyll series.
