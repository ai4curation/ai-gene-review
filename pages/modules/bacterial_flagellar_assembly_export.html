Bacterial flagellar assembly and type III export

Gammaproteobacterial flagellar assembly and export mechanisms connecting the MS/C-ring platform, protein export, rod/hook construction and length control, late transcription, and filament completion. Pseudomonas putida, Escherichia coli and Salmonella exemplars ground the assembly/export roles; P. putida MotAB/MotCD and Vibrio alginolyticus PomAB resolve conditional motor function and coupling-ion differences.

The species-aware report finds all six architectural parts represented, identifies omitted export, switch, rod, and junction subunits, and flags KEGG name-collision and regulatory-boundary issues.

The conserved assembly/export architecture is represented within Gammaproteobacteria, with experimentally studied Pseudomonas, enterobacterial and Vibrio realizations. This is not a claim that all members of the taxon possess flagella or this regulatory/stator configuration. The selected components do not establish de novo whole-flagellum sufficiency: FliE, compatible envelope/LP-ring assembly, substrate supply, chaperones, membrane insertion and energy conditions remain explicit external prerequisites. Gram-positive absence of the LP ring and outgroup motor structures limit extrapolation rather than expand the declared taxon automatically. Chemotactic sensing is handled by its separate module; the switch is the motor output. Assembly/export, normal export efficiency, torque production and condition-specific swimming are distinct endpoints. The FliA/FlgM regulatory part is conditional on the source of late proteins, and FlgM is optional as a negative regulator. Stator pairs are optional for assembly and required for powered rotation. The official FliI PANTHER label contains ATP SYNTHASE but does not imply that reaction. FlgD retains a named family descriptor with exact members because available verified identifiers describe domains or an N-terminal region, not a justified whole-protein family. No PANTHER ID or ancestral placement is guessed.
