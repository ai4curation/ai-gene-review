iRhom-ADAM17 ectodomain shedding module

Regulated release of the soluble form of a membrane-anchored signalling protein by the sheddase ADAM17. Many cytokines, growth factors and receptors are made as transmembrane precursors, so for them the appearance of a soluble ligand is a proteolytic event rather than a transcriptional one. The module covers the whole machine, not just the protease: an iRhom pseudoprotease (iRhom2 or iRhom1) binds ADAM17 in the endoplasmic reticulum and escorts it out, furin removes the inhibitory prodomain in the trans-Golgi network, FRMD8/iTAP holds the mature complex at the cell surface, phosphorylation of the cytoplasmic tails licenses cleavage on stimulation, TIMP3 inhibits it, and the catalytic step cuts the substrate's juxtamembrane stalk. Without the iRhom the protease never matures, which is why a module centred on ADAM17 alone would miss where the control lies.

Built for projects/PRODUCTION_TERMS.md, where the curated classification makes this the mechanism behind the largest group of production terms after the interferons: 7 production terms and 17 regulation terms (TNF, RANKL, amphiregulin, fractalkine, CXCL16 and the TNF superfamily parent). Each of those terms names an outcome; this module names the machine that produces it. Boundary: it starts with ADAM17 biosynthesis and ends with the released ectodomain. What the released ligand then does belongs to the receptor's own module, and the transcription of the substrate gene is upstream of this module rather than part of it. ADAM10 is deliberately not modelled as a variant of the protease step: it shares substrates such as fractalkine and CXCL16 but is regulated by tetraspanins rather than by iRhoms, so it is a different machine and belongs in its own module.

Part 1: sheddase maturation and delivery
ADAM17 maturation and delivery to the cell surfaceTransport Stepadam17_maturation

Pro-ADAM17 cannot leave the endoplasmic reticulum on its own. An iRhom binds it there and escorts it to the trans-Golgi network, where furin removes the prodomain that keeps the catalytic site blocked, and on to the plasma membrane. This is the step that makes the iRhoms, rather than ADAM17 itself, the point of control.
