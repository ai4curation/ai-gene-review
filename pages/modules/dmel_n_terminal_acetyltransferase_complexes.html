N-terminal acetyltransferase complexes in Drosophila melanogaster

N-terminal acetyltransferases (NATs) transfer an acetyl group from acetyl-CoA to the alpha-amino group of protein N termini, mostly co-translationally at the ribosome. Each complex pairs a catalytic subunit with one or more auxiliary subunits that anchor it to the ribosome and shape substrate choice. NatA (catalytic Naa10, vnc in flies, with auxiliary Naa15-16) acetylates N termini exposed after removal of the initiator methionine; Naa50 (san) associates with NatA. NatB (Naa20 with Naa25, psidin) acetylates Met-Asp/Glu/Asn/Gln N termini. NatC (Naa30 with Naa35 and Naa38) acetylates Met followed by a hydrophobic residue. Drosophila has a testis-specific NatC catalytic paralog, Naa30B.
