Phagosome maturation

Phagosome maturation turns the nascent phagosome, which has no microbicidal or degradative activity, into a phagolysosome. It does so through an ordered series of fusion and fission events with endocytic and lysosomal compartments. The sealed phagosome first recruits active Rab5, and the class III PI3K VPS34 makes PI(3)P on its membrane. PIKfyve converts part of this PI(3)P to PI(3,5)P2. The Mon1-Ccz1 complex, a Rab5 effector and Rab7 GEF, then hands the vacuole over to Rab7. Rab7 and the HOPS tethering complex drive fusion with late endosomes and lysosomes, and the V-ATPase delivered by these fusions acidifies the lumen. This core machinery is shared by eukaryotes and works the same way in amoebae that eat bacteria, in nematode and fly cells that clear apoptotic corpses, and in mammalian macrophages. Animals add lineage-specific parts: the Rab7 effector RILP and LAMP membrane proteins. In vertebrate professional phagocytes, the NOX2 NADPH oxidase also assembles on the phagosomal membrane and produces superoxide into the lumen.

Part 2: Rab5-to-Rab7 conversion
Mon1-Ccz1-mediated Rab5-to-Rab7 conversionRegulatory Steprab5_to_rab7_conversion

The Mon1-Ccz1 complex binds GTP-Rab5 and activates Rab7. This hands the phagosome over from the Rab5 to the Rab7 stage. In sand-1 or ccz-1 mutant worms, engulfed corpses stay in RAB-5-positive phagosomes. PIKfyve, which makes PI(3,5)P2 from PI(3)P, is also needed for phagosomes to acquire Rab7: in Dictyostelium PIKfyve knockouts Rab5 dynamics are normal but little Rab7 or V-ATPase arrives.
