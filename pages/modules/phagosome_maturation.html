Phagosome maturation

Phagosome maturation turns the nascent phagosome, which has no microbicidal or degradative activity, into a phagolysosome. It does so through an ordered series of fusion and fission events with endocytic and lysosomal compartments. The sealed phagosome first recruits active Rab5, and the class III PI3K VPS34 makes PI(3)P on its membrane. The Mon1-Ccz1 complex, a Rab5 effector and Rab7 GEF, then hands the vacuole over to Rab7. Rab7 and its effectors, together with LAMP proteins and the HOPS tethering complex, drive microtubule-dependent movement and fusion with late endosomes and lysosomes. The V-ATPase acidifies the lumen as it is delivered. In professional phagocytes the NOX2 NADPH oxidase also assembles on the phagosomal membrane and produces superoxide into the lumen. The same machinery degrades internalized microbes and engulfed apoptotic cells, and its core is conserved between C. elegans and mammals.

Part 1: early phagosome (Rab5/PI(3)P stage)
Early phagosome Rab5 and PI(3)P stageBiological Processearly_phagosome_stage

Part 3: phagosome-lysosome fusion
Rab7-dependent phagosome-lysosome fusionBiological Processphagosome_lysosome_fusion

Active Rab7 recruits RILP and the dynein-dynactin motor. These move the phagosome toward the cell center and extend tubules that fuse with late endosomes and lysosomes. LAMP-1/LAMP-2 are needed for Rab7 recruitment and fusion. The HOPS tethering complex acts downstream of RAB-7 recruitment.
