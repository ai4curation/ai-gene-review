cGMP-dependent sensory transduction in Caenorhabditis elegans ciliated neurons

Many Caenorhabditis elegans sensory neurons turn an environmental stimulus into an electrical signal through the second messenger cGMP. A guanylyl cyclase in or near the sensory cilium makes cGMP. The cGMP then opens a cyclic nucleotide-gated (CNG) cation channel made of the pore-forming alpha subunit TAX-4 and the modulatory beta subunit TAX-2. The resulting cation and Ca2+ influx changes the activity of the neuron. The channel is the same in different neurons, but the cyclase differs with the stimulus. In the AWC olfactory neurons, the transmembrane guanylyl cyclases ODR-1 and DAF-11 act downstream of odorant receptors and are required for responses to AWC-sensed odors. In the AFD thermosensory neurons, the receptor guanylyl cyclases GCY-8, GCY-18 and GCY-23 together are required for temperature-evoked currents, and they set the temperature range of isothermal tracking. In the URX and BAG gas-sensing neurons, heme-containing soluble guanylyl cyclases act as oxygen sensors: GCY-35/GCY-36 respond to rises in O2 and GCY-31/GCY-33 to falls. tax-4 and tax-2 are expressed in the thermosensory, gustatory and olfactory neurons. tax-4 mutants fail to respond to temperature and to soluble or volatile attractants, and they are defective in oxygen responses. A separate set of C. elegans sensory neurons, including AWA and ASH, uses OSM-9/OCR-2 TRPV channels instead of this cGMP-CNG pathway.

Boundary. This is a concrete C. elegans module for the primary transduction step in ciliated sensory neurons that use cGMP. A guanylyl cyclase makes cGMP, and cGMP gates the TAX-4/TAX-2 CNG channel. The cyclase step is a variant set along the neuron and stimulus axis (AWC odors, AFD temperature, URX/BAG oxygen). In this concrete scope these variants coexist in one animal and are not alternatives, so the selection is ONE_OR_MORE. The CNG channel is a shared required part. Left out on purpose. (1) The OSM-9/OCR-2 TRPV branch, used in AWA olfaction and ASH nociception. It does not use cGMP and should get its own module. OCR-2 has a gene review but is therefore not a member here. OSM-9 does act in AWC, but as an adaptation factor and not as the transduction channel (PMID:12160748). (2) GPA-12. Its review places it in the epidermal antifungal G12/13-PKC-p38 pathway, not in sensory transduction. (3) Upstream receptors and G proteins: AWC odorant GPCRs such as STR-2, the Galpha ODR-3, and the photoreceptor LITE-1 in ASJ. None has a gene review here. They are described in text and not given annotons. (4) Downstream modulators: EGL-4/PKG adaptation, TAX-6 calcineurin, the CNG-3 channel subunit and cGMP phosphodiesterases. (5) The ASE salt-sensing receptor guanylyl cyclases (GCY-22, GCY-5 and others). TAX-4/TAX-2 also act in ASE, but the cached primary evidence for ASE rGCs is abstract-only and indirect, so no ASE cyclase variant is modeled. (6) ASJ phototransduction, which also uses DAF-11, ODR-1 and TAX-2/TAX-4 (PMID:20436480). It is recorded here as context, not as a separate variant. Grounding. ODR-1, TAX-2 and TAX-4 have completed gene reviews, and their annotons use those reviews' core molecular functions. DAF-11, GCY-8, GCY-18, GCY-23, GCY-35, GCY-36, GCY-31 and GCY-33 have no gene reviews here. Their accessions are Swiss-Prot entries from UniProt (gene_exact query, organism 6239). Their annotons use guanylate cyclase activity (GO:0004383), which matches their EC 4.6.1.2 assignment and the cited genetic evidence. The rGCs are written as parallel annotons in each variant. Whether they act as obligate heterodimers in vivo is not established for AWC or AFD. The direction of the stimulus-evoked cGMP change differs between neurons and is not modeled. Relation to MODULE:nitric_oxide_cgmp_signaling. That module is the vertebrate-style NO-sGC-cGMP-PKG pathway. The worm GCY-35/GCY-36 and GCY-31/GCY-33 enzymes are atypical soluble guanylyl cyclases with heme H-NOX domains that sense O2. Their output here is the CNG channel, not PKG. The GO term nitric oxide-cGMP-mediated signaling (GO:0038060) is therefore not used. GO currently has no non-obsolete general "cGMP-mediated signaling" process term (GO:0019934 is obsolete), so the root concept uses the stimulus-detection process.

Part 2: cGMP-gated TAX-4/TAX-2 CNG channel
TAX-4/TAX-2 heteromeric cyclic nucleotide-gated channelProtein Complextax4_tax2_cng

TAX-4 is the CNGA-type pore-forming subunit and forms a cGMP-activated channel on its own. TAX-2 is a CNGB-type subunit that cannot form a channel alone. It co-assembles with TAX-4, lowers cGMP sensitivity about 25-fold, keeps the channel selective for cGMP over cAMP, and changes block by divalent cations. Native channels are probably mostly TAX-4/TAX-2 heteromers. In the cryo-EM structure of cGMP-bound TAX-4, a C-linker gating ring couples cyclic-nucleotide binding to pore opening. Both subunits go to sensory cilia, and they depend on each other for this in a cell-specific way.
