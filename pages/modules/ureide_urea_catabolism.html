Ureide pathway - nitrogen recovery from allantoin via urea to ammonia

The ureide pathway recovers the four nitrogen atoms of the purine ring as ammonium. In organisms that keep urate oxidase, urate made by purine catabolism is oxidised to 5-hydroxyisourate, which 5-hydroxyisourate hydrolase and 2-oxo-4-hydroxy-4-carboxy-5-ureidoimidazoline (OHCU) decarboxylase convert to (S)-allantoin. Organisms without these enzymes, such as budding yeasts, can import allantoin from outside and enter the pathway at this point. Allantoinase opens the hydantoin ring to give allantoate. The allantoate is then split along one of two chemical routes. In the first, allantoicase releases urea and (S)-ureidoglycolate, and ureidoglycolate lyase releases a second urea and glyoxylate. In the second, used by plants and many bacteria, allantoate amidohydrolase and ureidoglycine aminohydrolase release ammonium directly, and ureidoglycolate is either split by the lyase or fully hydrolysed by ureidoglycolate amidohydrolase without making urea. The urea is then broken down in one of two ways. Most fungi, plants and many bacteria use a nickel-dependent urease, which hydrolyses urea in one step. Saccharomycetes yeasts instead use urea amidolyase, a biotin-dependent enzyme with two domains: urea carboxylase makes allophanate from urea, bicarbonate and ATP, and allophanate hydrolase converts allophanate to ammonium and CO2. The glyoxylate carbon goes to the glyoxylate cycle. In fungi and animals the enzymes are soluble, and in some organisms the urate oxidase steps are peroxisomal; in plants the steps after allantoin take place in the endoplasmic reticulum.

YeastCyc assessment: all five S. cerevisiae reactions and genes (DAL1, DAL2, DAL3, DUR1,2) are kept, all cytosolic. DAL3 was historically called "ureidoglycolate hydrolase" (PMID:3915539, PMID:1776360). The reaction YeastCyc, UniProt and the GO-CAMs assign it is the urea-releasing lyase (EC 4.3.2.3, GO:0050385), not the plant amidohydrolase (GO:0004848). S. cerevisiae lacks the urate oxidase entry part and urease (PMID:21447149; no member of PTHR42874, PTHR10395, PTHR43466 or PTHR33569 in the PANTHER 19.0 yeast proteome). Generalisation: the crosswalk asked for an entry variant (urate oxidase route present or absent) and a urea-cleavage variant (urease or urea amidolyase). The entry is an optional part rather than a variant set, because allantoin import is a transport step, not an alternative enzyme. Two more lineage variant sets were added from the literature: plant and E. coli allantoate amidohydrolase plus ureidoglycine aminohydrolase in place of allantoicase, and plant ureidoglycolate amidohydrolase in place of the lyase (PMID:19935661, PMID:21482173). (4) The model has a separate urease activator complex activity (GO:0150006) that positively regulates ure2. The S. pombe accessory proteins are ure4 (UreD-like, P87125), ure6 (UreF-like, O14016) and ure7 (UreG-like, Q96WV0); they belong to the urease biogenesis module and are not exemplars here.
