Cytotoxic granule exocytosis module

Delivery of the killing machinery of a cytotoxic lymphocyte onto one target cell. Perforin, the granzymes and granulysin are not secreted when they are made: they are loaded into a secretory lysosome, held there, and released only when the cell meets a target, at the immunological synapse and nowhere else. The granule is carried to the synapse by RAB27A, docked and primed by Munc13-4 (UNC13D), and fused with the plasma membrane by syntaxin-11 with its partner Munc18-2 (STXBP2). Perforin then forms pores in the target membrane through which the granzymes reach its cytosol. Human loss-of-function in each step causes the same disease, familial hemophagocytic lymphohistiocytosis, which is how the parts list was established.

Built for projects/PRODUCTION_TERMS.md. The curated classification marks the perforin, granzyme A, granzyme B and granulysin production terms (4 production, 6 regulation) as resting on dedicated machinery, and this is it. These four products share one storage and release machine, which is the argument against giving each of them its own production term: nothing in the route is specific to any one of them. Boundary: starts with a loaded granule and ends with pore formation in the target membrane. Granule biogenesis from the endolysosomal system, and the synapse formation that positions the cell, are upstream and are not modelled here; granzyme action on target substrates is downstream, and cached Reactome events such as R-HSA-139893 cover it. LYST and MYO5A are deliberately excluded. LYST mutations (Chediak-Higashi) change granule size rather than the release steps modelled here, and MYO5A is the control that shows the pathway's boundary: it causes the same pigmentary disease as RAB27A but not the immune one, and MYO5A-defective T cells kill normally. No cached GO-CAM has an activity on any of these proteins in this role, so there are no pins.
