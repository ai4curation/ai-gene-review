L-methionine biosynthesis (from homoserine)

De novo biosynthesis of L-methionine from L-homoserine through three ordered stages: homoserine activation, sulfur incorporation to form L-homocysteine, and methylation of L-homocysteine. Homoserine is activated by acylation with an acetyl or succinyl group, or, in plants, by phosphorylation. Sulfur enters either by two-step trans-sulfuration from L-cysteine through cystathionine (cystathionine gamma-synthase then cystathionine beta-lyase) or by one-step direct sulfhydrylation of the activated homoserine with sulfide. The pathway ends with a cobalamin-independent (MetE-type) or cobalamin-dependent (MetH-type) methionine synthase that transfers a methyl group from 5-methyltetrahydrofolate to L-homocysteine. Bacteria use every combination of these routes; fungi acetylate homoserine, rely mainly on direct sulfhydrylation and finish with a MetE-type synthase that needs a polyglutamylated folate; plants use phospho-homoserine trans-sulfuration in plastids and a MetE-type synthase. Animals lack the pathway; their MetH-type synthase regenerates methionine in the methionine cycle, which is outside this module. Acyl-donor specificity and reaction direction are not reliably predicted by enzyme-family membership, so alternatives are kept substrate-matched.

PSEPK metH gene review
Q88KB5 supplies a complete cobalamin-dependent methionine synthase exemplar for the terminal methylation stage.

The boundary begins with L-homoserine and ends with L-methionine. Upstream aspartate-family synthesis of homoserine, sulfur assimilation, cobalamin and folate supply, methionine salvage, the methionine/SAM cycle, and transcriptional or riboswitch regulation are outside the module. SCOPE. The module is species-neutral and pan-domain: each leaf names a reaction and is grounded by experimentally characterized exemplars from bacteria (E. coli K-12, Pseudomonas putida KT2440, Corynebacterium glutamicum, Mycobacterium tuberculosis), fungi (S. cerevisiae, S. pombe) and plants (Arabidopsis). It is CONCRETE because every leaf is grounded to reviewed representative proteins. E. coli MetE (P25665) and MetH (P13009), listed in an earlier version, were removed as representatives because their gene reviews are being completed in a separate open PR (the bacterial methionine-cycle audit); they should be re-added when that work merges, which will also restore a bacterial MetE exemplar. Human MTR is not a representative: animals lack de novo methionine synthesis, and MTR's role is the methionine cycle; it is reviewed here only as the reference protein of PTHR45833. FAMILIES. Every leaf now names the PANTHER family or subfamily that holds its exemplars (PTHR20919 and PTHR32268:SF11 for acylation; PTHR11808:SF75, PTHR42699:SF1 and PTHR43379:SF1 for cystathionine gamma-synthase; PTHR43500:SF1 and PTHR11808:SF50 for cystathionine beta-lyase; PTHR43797:SF2 for MetY; PTHR11808:SF80 for MetZ; PTHR30519 for MetE; PTHR45833:SF1 for MetH), and each family has a family review. Family membership does not determine the leaf chemistry: both acyltransferase families contain acetyl- and succinyl-specific members in one subfamily (PMID:28581482), PTHR11808:SF80 holds a gamma-synthase as well as MetZ, and PTHR30519 includes partial MetE-like proteins. The family terms orient; the function selects members. PAINT NODES. Only nodes that are seeded by one of the module's own UniProt representatives are pinned (PTN000467771 MetA, PTN000211711 MetB, PTN000211746 MetC). The other function-bearing nodes (PTN000791315 MetX, PTN002887184 MetY, PTN008412101 MetE, PTN002501322 MetH) are assessed in the family reviews; their seeds name the yeast and fission-yeast exemplars by SGD/PomBase ids or are E. coli and human proteins not used as representatives here. Family-review findings that matter for genome annotation: PTN000467771 propagates succinyltransferase activity to acetyl-specific MetA enzymes (B. subtilis MetA); PTN000791315 propagates acetyltransferase activity to succinyl-specific MetXS; PTN000211591 gives E. coli MetB cystathionine gamma-lyase and cysteine-biosynthesis IBAs; and PTN008412101 carries cobalamin-dependent GO:0008705 on the MetE family. The UniRule for the MetA family (HAMAP MF_00295) was found to attach GO:0004414 to the succinyl case and GO:0008899 to the acetyl case; this is recorded as a source defect in the E. coli metA review. REMAINING VALIDATOR WARNINGS (intrinsic). The function-conformance check reports FAMILY_FUNCTION_UNREVIEWED for the acyl-donor activities on PTHR20919 and PTHR32268:SF11 and for GO:0016765 on PTHR11808:SF80, because the family reviews deliberately leave those terms UNRESOLVED: no PANTHER subfamily separates the donors or the reaction types. It reports ANNOTATION_SUPPORTED for MetZ because the metZ review's core function uses a proposed term (O-succinylhomoserine sulfhydrylase activity) that GO lacks, while the module annoton carries the nearest existing parent, GO:0016765. ROUTES. Plants activate homoserine by phosphorylation (homoserine kinase), and their cystathionine gamma-synthase uses O-phospho-L-homoserine (PMID:9636232; the CGS1 review proposes an O-phospho-L-homoserine-specific gamma-synthase term). That route is not yet a variant of the activation step because tests/test_module_logic.py fixes the route/gene inventory of this template; homoserine kinase is modelled in aspartate_family_threonine_biosynthesis. S. pombe met3 has only inferred gamma-synthase activity (no S. pombe assay), and direct sulfhydrylation by met17 is the main fission-yeast route. YEASTPATHWAYS AND GO-CAM ALIGNMENT (2026-10; PWY3O-954, PWY-5344, PWY3O-238). The module concept is GO:0071266 'de novo' L-methionine biosynthetic process, and the MetY leaf uses GO:0051009, the exact sulfide reaction (its parent GO:0003961 is defined by the methanethiol reaction; the MET17 and met17 reviews now MODIFY their GO:0003961 rows to GO:0051009). The HOM3, HOM2 and HOM6 reactions in PWY3O-954 are the upstream trunk, modelled in aspartate_family_threonine_biosynthesis. STR2 and STR3, which make homocysteine from cysteine (PMID:10821189), are missing from the YeastCyc gene lists although the pathway comment describes that route. MET7 (folylpolyglutamate synthase) is required because Met6 uses only polyglutamylated 5-methyltetrahydrofolate (PMID:10799479), but it supplies a co-substrate and is kept outside the module, like sulfide supply and the MET13/MET12 methylenetetrahydrofolate reductases. All yeast activities are cytosolic in the GO-CAMs; no module-level compartment is asserted because plant steps up to cystathionine are plastidial. GO-CAM disagreements: (a) SGD models type MET17 with the broader GO:0003961, whereas PomBase uses GO:0051009; (b) YeastPathways_P4-PWY-1 (threonine superpathway) chains MET6 -> MET17 -> MET2 -> THR1 with causally-upstream edges, the reverse of the biosynthetic order; (c) the PomBase model places S. pombe met3 (cystathionine gamma-synthase) in GO:0019344 cysteine biosynthesis although S. pombe lacks the reverse trans-sulfuration enzymes (recorded as a source defect in the met3 review); (d) the PomBase model includes two uncharacterised FAD-binding proteins (SPAC1783.01, SPBC12C2.03c) typed as GO:0030586 [methionine synthase] reductase, upstream of the cobalamin-independent met26. That reductase reactivates cobalamin-dependent MetH-type synthases, and S. pombe has no MetH, so these activities are not adopted.

The module records biochemical alternatives, not universal gene-name requirements. Homoserine acyltransferase and Cys/Met PLP-enzyme families can contain members with different substrates or reaction directions, and some proteins are bifunctional across the nominal routes. Exact activity evidence therefore takes precedence over a family label when instantiating the template in a genome. A MetH-only implementation is conditional on cobalamin availability. Per-genome candidates are keyed by KEGG Orthology: metA=K00651, metX=K00641, metB=K01739, metC=K01760, metY=K01740, metE=K00549, and metH=K00548. MetZ/O-succinylhomoserine sulfhydrylase and partial MetE-like proteins require species-aware review beyond those KO mappings. See modules/experimental/gluconeogenesis-context/kegg_oracle.py and modules/experimental/gluconeogenesis-context/resolve_genomes.py for per-genome route resolution and gap detection.
