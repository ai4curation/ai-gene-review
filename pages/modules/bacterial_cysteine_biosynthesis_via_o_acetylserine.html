Bacterial L-cysteine biosynthesis via O-acetyl-L-serine

A reusable two-step bacterial pathway in which serine O-acetyltransferase (CysE) converts L-serine and acetyl-CoA to O-acetyl-L-serine, followed by a pyridoxal-phosphate-dependent O-acetylserine sulfhydrylase that incorporates sulfide to produce L-cysteine. OAS-active CysK or CysM proteins can implement the terminal reaction. The route is distributed across bacterial lineages, but neither the gene names nor broad family membership establish substrate choice. Thiosulfate incorporation produces S-sulfocysteine and requires a further reduction; it is adjacent chemistry rather than the direct sulfide route. A regulatory cysteine synthase complex forms in some lineages but is not a required pathway assembly. Upstream sulfide generation and distinct O-phosphoserine/sulfur-carrier pathways lie outside this module.

The taxon context denotes a reusable bacterial route, not universal possession or dependence. Sulfate uptake and reduction supply sulfide upstream. Thiosulfate incorporation into OAS produces S-sulfocysteine and requires downstream reduction, so it cannot silently replace the stated sulfide chemistry. CysK and CysM names describe diverse proteins: mycobacterial CysM prefers O-phosphoserine and CysO thiocarboxylate, whereas Campylobacter CysM uses OAS and strongly favors sulfide. Minor in-vitro side reactions do not automatically define a physiological module implementation. The cysteine synthase complex is regulatory context: CysE-tail occupation inhibits the CysK active site, and the enterobacterial interaction cannot be assumed in Pseudomonas or Bacillus. PTHR42811 grounds serine acetyltransferase; heterogeneous PTHR10314 contains both relevant OAS enzymes and proteins with other substrates/functions. PAINT node membership must therefore be combined with a gene review that establishes the route-specific core activity. The CysK node PTN000034535 includes bacterial CysK exemplars, but CysM node PTN008497439 also contains M. tuberculosis P9WP53; it cannot alone qualify a native OAS-route implementation. Absence of a representative from the cached tree is not evidence for absence of family membership. Campylobacter Cj0912c is retained as an explicitly unplaced OAS-active alternative: its CysM symbol and CysK-like signature do not establish either existing PAINT node. Fifteen positive member exemplars span E. coli, Pseudomonas, Bacillus, Campylobacter and Mycobacterium; a sixteenth gene review assesses Mtb CysM as a mechanistically distinct comparator. Mtb regulatory-complex formation has direct support in PMID:38311199, but exact full-length construct and assembly details remain unresolved; its CysE activity is reduced, so regulatory direction is not universal. Bacillus instead has directly demonstrated CymR-CysK regulation, while tested CysE-CysK association is negative. Quoted biochemical results, genetic evidence, family inference and construct/strain limits are distinguished. An intact enzyme panel describes supported capacity, not measured native flux or whole-genome completeness.
