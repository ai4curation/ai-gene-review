ATM signaling at DNA double-strand breaks — MRN sensing and ATM activation, gammaH2AX-MDC1 amplification, the RNF8-UBE2N-RNF168 ubiquitin cascade, 53BP1-RIF1 versus BRCA1-A recruitment, and phosphatase/deubiquitinase/HECT-ligase attenuation; ATM/MRE11/RAD50/NBN/KAT5/H2AX/MDC1/RNF8/UBE2N/RNF168/TP53BP1/RIF1/BRCA1

A DNA double-strand break (DSB) in chromatin is detected by the MRE11-RAD50-NBN (MRN) complex, which binds and tethers the broken DNA ends and recruits the serine/threonine kinase ATM through a conserved C-terminal motif of NBN. Inactive ATM dimers dissociate into active monomers that autophosphorylate on Ser1981, and acetylation of ATM by the histone acetyltransferase KAT5 (TIP60), which is held in a constitutive complex with ATM, is needed for full kinase activity. Active ATM phosphorylates hundreds of substrates. At the break it phosphorylates the histone variant H2AX on Ser139 over megabase domains of flanking chromatin, producing gammaH2AX. The tandem BRCT domain of MDC1 binds the phosphorylated H2AX C-terminus, and MDC1 in turn binds ATM and NBN, concentrating more MRN-ATM on the flanking chromatin and so spreading H2AX phosphorylation in a positive feedback loop. ATM-phosphorylated MDC1 recruits the RING E3 ubiquitin ligase RNF8 through its FHA domain. RNF8, working with the E2 enzyme UBE2N (UBC13), initiates K63-linked ubiquitin chains on chromatin proteins, which recruit a second RING ligase, RNF168. RNF168 monoubiquitylates nucleosomal H2A and H2AX on Lys13/Lys15 and extends K63-linked chains, amplifying the ubiquitin signal. These marks recruit two antagonistic readers. 53BP1 (TP53BP1) binds nucleosomes that carry both H4K20me2 and H2AK15ub; the H4K20me2 sites are first unmasked by VCP/p97-driven extraction of L3MBTL1 and by RNF8/RNF168-dependent degradation of KDM4A. ATM-phosphorylated 53BP1 recruits RIF1, and together they block DNA end resection and favour non-homologous end joining, chiefly in G1. The BRCA1-A complex, recruited by the RAP80 (UIMC1) ubiquitin-interaction motifs to K63 chains, carries BRCA1-BARD1 and the K63-specific deubiquitinase BRCC3 (BRCC36); BRCA1 and its partner CtIP displace 53BP1-RIF1 from S phase onward, and KAT5 acetylation of H2AK15, which blocks its ubiquitylation, further limits 53BP1 binding, so that resection and homologous recombination are favoured. SUMO conjugation by PIAS1 and PIAS4 is needed for productive accumulation of 53BP1, BRCA1 and RNF168, and the SUMO-targeted ubiquitin ligase RNF4 turns over SUMOylated MDC1. In parallel, ATM-phosphorylated RNF20-RNF40 monoubiquitylates H2B to relax chromatin for repair. The phosphorylation state of H2AX Tyr142, set by the WSTF (BAZ1B) tyrosine kinase and removed by EYA phosphatases, gates MDC1 binding and the choice between repair and apoptosis. The signal is confined and switched off by phosphatases that dephosphorylate ATM and gammaH2AX (PP2A, PP4, PP6 and WIP1/PPM1D), by deubiquitinases that remove the chromatin ubiquitin marks (USP3, USP16, USP44) or inhibit UBE2N without catalysis (OTUB1), and by the HECT ligases TRIP12 and UBR5, which keep RNF168 levels limiting. ATM output then drives checkpoint arrest through CHK2 and p53, apoptosis or senescence, and the repair reactions themselves; those downstream programs are outside this module.
