Mitochondrial calcium uniporter and NCLX efflux — MCU-EMRE pore with MICU1-MICU2/MICU3 gatekeepers and the dominant-negative MCUB subunit for calcium uptake into the matrix, and SLC8B1 (NCLX) sodium/lithium-calcium exchange for efflux (human)

Mitochondria take up calcium from the cytosol across the inner membrane through the mitochondrial calcium uniporter, a highly calcium-selective channel driven by the negative-inside membrane potential set up by the respiratory chain. In humans the uniporter holocomplex (uniplex) contains four MCU pore subunits, the small single-pass subunit EMRE (SMDT1), which is essential for MCU opening in animals and links the pore to the gatekeepers, and a disulfide-linked heterodimer of EF-hand gatekeepers in the intermembrane space: MICU1 with MICU2 in most tissues, or MICU1 with MICU3 in brain. At resting cytosolic calcium the calcium-free gatekeepers occlude the pore, so little calcium enters; when local calcium rises, for example near IP3 receptors at ER-mitochondria contacts, calcium binding to the EF-hands opens the channel, giving a sigmoidal response that prevents overload while letting mitochondria respond to calcium signals. MCUB, a paralog of MCU, enters the pore tetramer as a dominant-negative subunit that lowers uniporter activity and gatekeeper association, and is induced after cardiac stress. Matrix calcium activates dehydrogenases of the citric acid cycle and so couples calcium signals to ATP production; it also shapes cytosolic calcium transients and, in excess, triggers the permeability transition and cell death. Calcium leaves the matrix mainly through the inner-membrane sodium/calcium exchanger NCLX (SLC8B1), which also accepts lithium, so that uptake and efflux together set matrix calcium.

Part 1: calcium-conducting pore of the uniporter
MCU-EMRE poreProtein Complexmcu_emre_pore

Four MCU subunits form the calcium-selective pore in the inner membrane, each bound by an EMRE subunit whose transmembrane helix holds MCU open and whose matrix and intermembrane-space regions link the pore to the MICU gatekeepers. Both are required: MCU without EMRE does not conduct calcium in human cells.
