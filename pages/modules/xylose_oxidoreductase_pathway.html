D-xylose catabolism to D-xylulose 5-phosphate (oxidoreductase and isomerase routes)

D-xylose, the main pentose of plant hemicellulose, is channelled into the pentose phosphate pathway as D-xylulose 5-phosphate. The isomerisation of xylose to xylulose is achieved by one of two routes. In xylose-utilising yeasts and filamentous fungi, an aldo-keto reductase family xylose reductase reduces D-xylose to xylitol with NADPH (or NADH), and an NAD+-dependent medium-chain dehydrogenase, xylitol dehydrogenase (D-xylulose reductase), oxidises xylitol to D-xylulose. Because the two steps use different cofactors, the route can create a redox imbalance and xylitol accumulation under oxygen limitation. Bacteria, and some anaerobic fungi, instead use a single cofactor-free xylose isomerase. In both routes an FGGY-family xylulokinase then phosphorylates D-xylulose with ATP to D-xylulose 5-phosphate, which enters the non-oxidative pentose phosphate pathway. Xylose uptake and downstream pentose phosphate metabolism are outside this module.

D-xylose catabolic processGO:0042843

NEW module (YeastPathways crosswalk, 2026-10) generalising PWY3O-8 (xylose metabolism) and PWY3O-5 (xylulose degradation). The crosswalk slug names the fungal oxidoreductase route, but the module also includes the bacterial and anaerobic-fungal xylose isomerase as an alternative entry variant, as the crosswalk scope asks; the xylulokinase step is common to both. Boundary: xylose transport (hexose transporters in S. cerevisiae) and the pentose phosphate pathway are outside. S. cerevisiae: all three enzymes are present, but wild-type cells grow on xylulose and not on xylose and convert xylose mainly to xylitol (PMID:18555359, PMID:15184173), because GRE3 and XYL2 activities are low, the NADPH-preferring reductase and NAD+-dependent dehydrogenase unbalance cofactors, and XKS1 expression is low (PMID:10981687). Other S. cerevisiae aldose reductases (for example YPR1) and sorbitol dehydrogenases SOR1/SOR2 can contribute to the two oxidoreductase steps (SOR1 is xylose-induced, PMID:15184173); they were not added as exemplars because their in vivo contribution is not established. YeastCyc corrections: the GRE3 reaction RXN-8773 is written in the xylitol-oxidation direction with EC 1.1.1.21; the module models the physiological reduction of D-xylose. GO-CAM disagreements: the YeastPathways PWY3O-8 model types GRE3 with the general aldose reductase (NADPH) term GO:0004032 and uses the broad process D-xylose metabolic process (GO:0042732); there is no GO-CAM for the XKS1 step. No PomBase GO-CAM is linked and S. pombe is not used as an exemplar.
