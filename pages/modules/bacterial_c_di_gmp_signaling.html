Bacterial cyclic di-GMP second-messenger signalling

A reusable bacterial second-messenger motif in which the intracellular level of cyclic di-GMP (c-di-GMP) is set by the opposing activities of synthesising and degrading enzymes and read out by dedicated receptor proteins. GGDEF-domain diguanylate cyclases condense two GTP molecules into c-di-GMP, usually as dimers and often under non-competitive product inhibition at an allosteric I-site in the GGDEF domain. Two unrelated phosphodiesterase families turn the signal over: EAL-domain enzymes hydrolyse c-di-GMP to linear pGpG, and HD-GYP-domain enzymes hydrolyse it via pGpG towards GMP. Receptors convert c-di-GMP binding into a change in the activity of a downstream target; well-characterised protein receptor classes are PilZ-domain proteins and proteins carrying catalytically degenerate GGDEF or EAL domains that retain c-di-GMP binding. The cyclases and phosphodiesterases typically carry N-terminal sensory domains, so the motif integrates many environmental inputs, and many receptors act on motility, adhesion, exopolysaccharide synthesis and virulence outputs that are modelled as separate modules.

Boundary. The module is the second-messenger motif itself: synthesis, degradation and the receptor step that converts c-di-GMP binding into a change in a target protein. The physiological outputs are deliberately left to their own modules; c-di-GMP receptor subunits already modelled elsewhere include the BcsA PilZ switch in MODULE:bacterial_cellulose_biosynthesis and the Alg44 PilZ subunit in MODULE:alginate_polymerization_export. c-di-GMP riboswitches and c-di-GMP-binding transcription factors (e.g. FleQ, VpsT, Clp) are real receptor classes but are outside this boundary: riboswitches are not protein annotons, and the transcription-factor receptors are better represented with the regulons they control. Sensory input domains (PAS, GAF, CHASE, Cache, CZB, globin) are described on the enzymes rather than as separate parts because they differ from one cyclase or phosphodiesterase to the next. Representative members. PleD (Caulobacter crescentus) and DgcZ (Escherichia coli) are the cyclase exemplars with direct biochemistry; BdcA is retained as the Bordetella bronchiseptica exemplar because its cyclase activity was shown in vivo and it illustrates scaffold-dependent activation by the degenerate GGDEF/EAL protein DdpA (BB2109), but the GGAAF catalytic mutant could not be shown to be stable, so BdcA rests on weaker biochemistry than PleD or DgcZ. The Xanthomonas campestris HD-GYP protein RpfG is not used as the HD-GYP exemplar because the PNAS 2006 paper reporting its c-di-GMP hydrolysis (PMID:16611728) is retracted; Pseudomonas aeruginosa PA4781 is used instead, on direct enzyme kinetics (PMID:24066157). In Bordetella the single PilZ protein YcgR does not control flagellar motility (PMID:41452322), so the E. coli YcgR flagellar-brake function is not assumed to transfer to all PilZ proteins; the receptor annotons assert c-di-GMP binding and leave the downstream target to each receptor. Ontology. GO:0061939 (c-di-GMP signaling) has the right label but its definition describes information transfer "from one cell to another" and it sits under cell-cell signaling (GO:0007267), whereas bacterial c-di-GMP is an intracellular second messenger. It is therefore not used as the module concept; the definition should be raised with GO. The I-site feedback inhibition and the degenerate-domain receptors share structural domains with the enzymes, so domain presence alone does not predict which role a protein plays; catalytic-motif conservation (GGDEF/GGEEF; EAL with the DDFGTGYSS loop; HD and GYP) has to be checked before assigning an enzyme role.

Part 1: c-di-GMP synthesis by GGDEF diguanylate cyclases
Diguanylate cyclase synthesis of c-di-GMPReactionc_di_gmp_synthesis

Two GGDEF domains, each binding one GTP, align in an antiparallel dimer and condense the two GTPs into c-di-GMP with release of pyrophosphate. Catalysis requires divalent metal (Mg2+, and in many enzymes Mn2+) coordinated by the GG(D/E)EF motif. Many cyclases are inhibited non-competitively by c-di-GMP binding to an allosteric I-site (RxxD motif) within the GGDEF domain itself, and N-terminal sensory domains (receiver, CZB, globin, Cache and others) gate the activity in response to the cue each enzyme senses.

PMID:42429561
GGDEF dimerisation, antiparallel GTP alignment and Mg2+/Mn2+ dependence of the cyclase reaction.
Dimerization is crucial for c-di-GMP synthesis, as each GGDEF monomer binds one GTP molecule.
PMID:16923812
Allosteric control of cyclic di-GMP signaling.
Defines the I-site as an allosteric c-di-GMP binding site within the GGDEF domain mediating non-competitive product inhibition, with an RXXD core motif conserved in most GGDEF proteins.

Part 2: c-di-GMP degradation by EAL or HD-GYP phosphodiesterases
Phosphodiesterase turnover of c-di-GMPReactionc_di_gmp_degradation

Two structurally unrelated phosphodiesterase families hydrolyse c-di-GMP. Both require divalent metal, and both are represented in most genomes that use the messenger, so the step is modelled as a variant set over the enzyme family rather than as two separate parts.

PMID:41452322
Distinguishes the products of the two phosphodiesterase families.
EAL-domain PDEs hydrolyze c-di-GMP to linear pGpG (5’-phosphoguanylyl-(3’,5’)-guanosine), whereas HD-GYP domain PDEs hydrolyze c-di-GMP into two GMP molecules
PMID:42429561
Metal requirements of the EAL and HD-GYP catalytic domains, including the Ca2+ inhibition that first linked metals to c-di-GMP turnover.
Although most characterized HD-GYPs utilize Fe²⁺, Mn²⁺, or Mg²⁺ for activity, other metals can influence the function of HD-GYPs
Variant set: c-di-GMP phosphodiesterase family by enzyme family (One Or More)

EAL and HD-GYP domains are unrelated folds that catalyse the same first hydrolytic step; which family (or both) a species uses is a genomic property, not a mechanistic alternative within one enzyme.

EAL-domain phosphodiesteraseReactioneal_phosphodiesterase

The EAL domain opens one phosphodiester bond of c-di-GMP to give the linear dinucleotide pGpG. Catalysis uses two divalent metals coordinated by the EAL motif and the conserved DDFG(T/A)GYSS loop, is faster with Mn2+ than Mg2+, and is inhibited by Ca2+. Onward hydrolysis of pGpG by EAL domains is very slow, so pGpG clearance is a separate activity.

Part 3: c-di-GMP recognition by protein receptors
Protein receptors of c-di-GMPRegulatory Stepc_di_gmp_reception

Receptors bind c-di-GMP and convert the occupancy of that site into a conformational change that alters a downstream target. Two protein classes with structural and binding evidence are modelled: PilZ-domain proteins, the canonical standalone c-di-GMP binding module, and proteins whose GGDEF or EAL domain has lost catalysis but retained ligand binding. Because the downstream targets differ from one receptor to the next, the annotons assert the binding activity and name each receptor's target in prose rather than claiming a shared output process.
