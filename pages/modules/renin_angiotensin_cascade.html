Renin-angiotensin cascade — renin (with the (pro)renin receptor) cleavage of angiotensinogen, ACE or chymase/cathepsin G formation of angiotensin II, the ACE2/neprilysin/PRCP angiotensin-(1-7) arm acting on MAS1, and the ENPEP/ANPEP aminopeptidase arm to angiotensin III and IV

The renin-angiotensin cascade is the extracellular proteolytic pathway that generates the family of angiotensin peptide hormones from a single precursor, angiotensinogen (AGT), a liver-secreted serpin-fold glycoprotein that does none of the cleaving itself. Renin (REN), an aspartic endopeptidase released by the juxtaglomerular cells of the kidney, cleaves the N-terminal Leu-Val bond of angiotensinogen and releases the inactive decapeptide angiotensin I; this is the rate-limiting step. On tissue surfaces renin and its zymogen prorenin bind the (pro)renin receptor ATP6AP2, which raises renin's catalytic efficiency and lets prorenin act without proteolytic activation. The zinc peptidyl-dipeptidase angiotensin-converting enzyme (ACE), anchored on endothelial cells and also shed into plasma, removes the C-terminal His-Leu dipeptide of angiotensin I to give the octapeptide angiotensin II, the principal effector, which acts on the AT1 and AT2 receptors. In tissues, angiotensin II is also formed independently of ACE by the mast-cell serine protease chymase (CMA1) and by neutrophil cathepsin G (CTSG). A counter-regulatory arm converts the pressor peptides into angiotensin-(1-7): the monocarboxypeptidase ACE2 removes the C-terminal phenylalanine of angiotensin II (its preferred substrate) and the C-terminal leucine of angiotensin I to give angiotensin-(1-9); neprilysin (MME) cleaves angiotensin I and angiotensin-(1-9) to angiotensin-(1-7), as ACE also does for angiotensin-(1-9); and the lysosomal prolylcarboxypeptidase PRCP releases the C-terminal phenylalanine of angiotensin II and III. Angiotensin-(1-7) is the agonist of the G protein-coupled receptor MAS1 and opposes angiotensin II with vasodilator and antiproliferative actions. A third arm trims angiotensin II from the N terminus: glutamyl aminopeptidase (aminopeptidase A, ENPEP) removes Asp1 to give angiotensin III, which still acts on the AT1 and AT2 receptors and dominates in the brain renin-angiotensin system, and alanyl aminopeptidase (aminopeptidase N, ANPEP) removes Arg1 of angiotensin III to give angiotensin IV.

Conversion of angiotensin I to angiotensin II by ACE, or by chymase or cathepsin G in tissuesReactionras_angiotensin_ii_formation

Angiotensin I is cleaved at its Phe8-His9 bond to the octapeptide angiotensin II (AGT 25-32). ACE, a dipeptidyl carboxypeptidase that removes His-Leu, is the canonical and ACE-inhibitor-sensitive route; the mast-cell chymase CMA1 and neutrophil cathepsin G (CTSG) are serine endopeptidases that make the same product at the same bond in tissues, independently of ACE.

Variant set: Angiotensin II-forming proteases by protease and tissue context (One Or More)

ACE is the systemic enzyme, at the endothelial surface and shed into plasma. Chymase and cathepsin G are released from mast-cell and neutrophil granules and form angiotensin II locally, notably in the human heart and vessel wall, which is why ACE inhibitors do not abolish tissue angiotensin II. Each variant alone satisfies the step.

Part 4: angiotensin-(1-7) formation (counter-regulatory arm)
Formation of angiotensin-(1-7) and angiotensin-(1-9) by ACE2, neprilysin, ACE and prolylcarboxypeptidaseReactionras_angiotensin_1_7_formation

The counter-regulatory arm converts the pressor peptides into angiotensin-(1-7) (AGT 25-31). ACE2 removes the C-terminal Phe of angiotensin II (its best substrate) and, much less efficiently, the C-terminal Leu of angiotensin I to give angiotensin-(1-9) (AGT 25-33). Neprilysin cleaves angiotensin I and angiotensin-(1-9) directly to angiotensin-(1-7), and ACE also converts angiotensin-(1-9) to angiotensin-(1-7). The lysosomal prolylcarboxypeptidase PRCP releases the C-terminal Phe of angiotensin II and III. Which enzyme dominates depends on the tissue and on their relative levels.

Variant set: Angiotensin-(1-7)-forming peptidases by peptidase and substrate route (One Or More)

Three routes: from angiotensin II by ACE2 (dominant; kcat/Km 2.2e6 M-1 s-1 in PMID:15283675) or PRCP; from angiotensin I directly by neprilysin; and from angiotensin I via angiotensin-(1-9) by ACE2 followed by neprilysin or ACE. Each route alone yields angiotensin-(1-7).
