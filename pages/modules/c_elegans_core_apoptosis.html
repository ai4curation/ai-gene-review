Core programmed cell death machinery in Caenorhabditis elegans

Most somatic and germline apoptotic deaths in Caenorhabditis elegans are initiated by four proteins acting in a linear pathway. In living cells the BCL2-family protein CED-9 sits on mitochondria and binds an asymmetric dimer of the Apaf-1-like adaptor CED-4, holding it in an inactive state. A death signal raises the level of the BH3-only protein EGL-1, whose BH3 helix binds a hydrophobic groove on CED-9 and remodels it so that the CED-4 dimer is released. Free CED-4 dimers assemble into an ATP-bound octameric apoptosome that relocates to the nuclear periphery, recruits two molecules of the CED-3 caspase zymogen into its central hutch and drives their autocatalytic maturation. Active CED-3 then cleaves execution substrates such as DCR-1 and CED-8, which promote chromosome fragmentation and phosphatidylserine exposure for corpse engulfment. In the adult germline, DNA damage feeds into the same machinery through the p53-family transcription factor CEP-1, which induces transcription of egl-1 and of a second BH3-only gene, ced-13. Unlike the mammalian intrinsic pathway, the worm pathway has no known requirement for mitochondrial outer membrane permeabilization or cytochrome c release; CED-9 inhibits by direct sequestration of CED-4 rather than by controlling a pore.

Boundary. This is a concrete C. elegans module for the decision and activation steps of apoptosis: BH3-only trigger, BCL2-family restraint, adaptor oligomerization and caspase activation, plus caspase cleavage of execution substrates as the module output. Corpse recognition and engulfment are a separate module (MODULE:phagocytic_engulfment); CED-3 cleavage of CED-8 is the hand-off point, since acCED-8 promotes phosphatidylserine exposure that engulfing cells recognize. CEP-1 is modeled as an optional upstream part because it is needed only for DNA damage-induced germline death. Developmental transcriptional control of egl-1 (for example TRA-1 in the HSN neurons, or CES-1/CES-2 in the NSM sister cells) is cell-type specific upstream regulation and is not modeled here. Relation to the mammalian modules. MODULE:bcl2_family_momp_control and MODULE:intrinsic_apoptotic_signaling describe BAX/BAK-mediated outer membrane permeabilization, cytochrome c release and the CYCS/APAF1/CASP9 apoptosome. None of those steps is part of this module. C. elegans has no BAX/BAK-type effector in the core pathway, and CED-4 oligomerizes without cytochrome c once CED-9 releases it. The ced-9 gene review removed BCL2-family PAINT transfers for cytochrome c release and channel activity on these grounds. Mitochondria matter here as the site where CED-9 holds CED-4, not as a source of released apoptogenic factors. CED-3 combines the initiator role of CASP9 and the executioner role of CASP3/CASP7 in the mammalian MODULE:apoptotic_execution_phase. Members and terms. All five members have completed gene reviews. EGL-1 has no fitting GO molecular function: GO:0051434 BH3 domain binding describes the groove-side receptor (CED-9), not the BH3 ligand. The EGL-1 annoton therefore uses the free-text proposed term "pro-survival BCL2 family protein inhibitor activity" from the egl-1 review, as in MODULE:bcl2_family_momp_control for the mammalian BH3-only sensitizers. CED-9 carries two activities, CED-4 sequestration (GO:0140311) and EGL-1 BH3 binding (GO:0051434), matching its two core functions. CED-13 is a second CEP-1-induced BH3-only protein that binds CED-9; it has no gene review yet and is described in the CEP-1 part rather than modeled as a member. Non-core branches recorded in the gene reviews (local EGL-1/CED-9/CED-4/CED-3 activity in synapse pruning, CED-9 roles in mitochondrial dynamics, CED-3 cleavage of heterochronic regulators) are outside this boundary. No production GO-CAM in gocams/index.tsv contains these proteins.

Part 3: BCL2-family restraint of CED-4 on mitochondria
CED-9 sequestration of CED-4Molecular Functionced9_sequestration

One CED-9 molecule binds an asymmetric CED-4 dimer on the mitochondrial outer membrane and blocks CED-4 from activating CED-3. The CED-4-binding surface of CED-9 is distinct from its EGL-1-binding groove, so EGL-1 releases CED-4 by an allosteric change rather than by direct competition.

Part 4: CED-4 apoptosome assembly and CED-3 zymogen activation
CED-4 apoptosomeMolecular Functionced4_apoptosome

Released CED-4 dimers further oligomerize into a funnel-shaped octamer (a tetramer of asymmetric dimers) through the NB-ARC AAA+ ATPase module. The octamer binds two CED-3 zymogen molecules through the CED-3 L2' loop inside its hutch, forcing their dimerization and autocatalytic maturation, and it also stimulates the activity of mature CED-3. CED-4 moves from mitochondria to the nuclear periphery in dying cells; this does not need ced-3.

Part 5: caspase activation and cleavage of execution substrates
CED-3 caspase activation and executionMolecular Functionced3_caspase

CED-3 is made as an inactive zymogen and is matured by apoptosome-stimulated autoprocessing. Active CED-3 is an Asp-directed cysteine protease with substrate preferences resembling mammalian CASP3. Its substrates include DCR-1, whose C-terminal fragment becomes a deoxyribonuclease that nicks chromosomes, and CED-8, whose cleavage product promotes phosphatidylserine exposure on the dying cell.
