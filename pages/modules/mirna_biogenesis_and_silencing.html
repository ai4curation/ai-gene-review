Human canonical microRNA pathway — Microprocessor (DROSHA-DGCR8) pri-miRNA cleavage, XPO5-RAN-GTP pre-miRNA export, DICER1-TARBP2/PRKRA dicing, AGO1-4 RISC loading, and TNRC6-directed CCR4-NOT/PAN2-PAN3 deadenylation, DCP2 decapping and XRN1 decay

MicroRNAs (miRNAs) are ~22-nucleotide RNAs that guide Argonaute proteins to partially complementary sites, mostly in the 3' untranslated regions of messenger RNAs, and so repress translation and promote decay of hundreds of transcripts. Most human miRNAs are transcribed by RNA polymerase II as long primary transcripts (pri-miRNAs) that fold into stem-loop hairpins. In the nucleus the Microprocessor, made of the RNase III enzyme DROSHA and its double-stranded RNA-binding partner DGCR8, cuts the hairpin out of the pri-miRNA to release a ~60-70-nucleotide precursor (pre-miRNA) with a two-nucleotide 3' overhang. Exportin-5 (XPO5) binds the pre-miRNA together with GTP-bound RAN and carries it through the nuclear pore; GTP hydrolysis in the cytoplasm releases the cargo. The cytoplasmic RNase III enzyme DICER1, partnered by the dsRNA-binding protein TARBP2 (TRBP) or PRKRA (PACT), removes the terminal loop and leaves a ~22-base-pair miRNA duplex. The duplex is loaded into one of the four human Argonautes (AGO1-AGO4); one strand, the guide, is kept and the other is discarded, forming the miRNA-induced silencing complex (miRISC). AGO2 is the only efficient slicer and can cut fully complementary targets, but most animal miRNA targets pair only through the seed (guide nucleotides 2-7) and are silenced without cleavage. For those, AGO binds a GW182-family protein (TNRC6A, TNRC6B or TNRC6C), whose tryptophan-containing motifs recruit the CCR4-NOT deadenylase complex (through CNOT1 and CNOT9) and the PAN2-PAN3 deadenylase (through PAN3, which also binds the poly(A)-binding protein PABPC1). Deadenylation is followed by removal of the 5' cap by the DCP2-DCP1 decapping complex with its activators DDX6, PATL1, EDC3 and EDC4, and by 5'-to-3' degradation by XRN1. The same CCR4-NOT-DDX6 axis represses translation of the target. A few miRNAs bypass parts of this route: mirtrons are excised by the spliceosome instead of DROSHA, and miR-451 is matured by AGO2 slicing instead of DICER1.

Boundary decisions. (1) Canonical route only as parts. Mirtrons (short introns released by the spliceosome and debranched, bypassing DROSHA; PMID:17964270) and the DICER1-independent miR-451 route (pre-miR-451 cut by AGO2 slicer activity, PMID:20448148) are recorded in prose and on the AGO2 slicer annoton, not as parts. Neither adds a protein to the module (mirtrons use the spliceosome and lariat-debranching enzyme, outside this boundary; miR-451 uses AGO2, already a participant), and both converge on the same export, loading and silencing steps. (2) Argonautes are a ONE_OR_MORE variant set on the slicer axis: AGO2 has two annotons (miRNA binding and GO:0090624 slicing); AGO1 (NOT|enables RNA endonuclease activity, IDA), AGO3 (slicing only with particular guides and targets) and AGO4 carry miRNA binding only. AGO3's restricted slicing is recorded on its annoton but not asserted as its role here. (3) TARBP2 and PRKRA are a ONE_OR_MORE variant set of DICER1 partners; neither is required for the cleavage chemistry, so neither is given a ribonuclease activator activity. (4) RNA editing and uridylation are context, not parts. ADAR and ADARB1 deaminate adenosines in some pri- and pre-miRNAs, which can block DROSHA or DICER1 processing or retarget a miRNA, and TUT4 and TUT7 uridylate pre-miRNAs (monouridylation of group II pre-let-7 promotes dicing; LIN28-directed oligouridylation blocks it). These are substrate- specific regulatory layers acting on the pathway's RNA intermediates, not steps every miRNA passes through; they are what RGD draws as side reactions. Such roles fit GO's regulation-of-miRNA-processing terms rather than miRNA processing itself, and modelling them as parts would make the canonical route look conditional on editing or uridylation. Their gene symbols are recorded here for the triage, and they are the obvious first members of a future "regulation of miRNA biogenesis" module together with LIN28A/B. (5) The RAN cycle (RCC1, RANGAP1, RANBP1) is general nuclear-transport machinery and is left out; RAN itself is a part because the XPO5-pre-miRNA complex does not form without RAN-GTP. (6) Deadenylation, decapping and XRN1 decay are parts, not context, because miRISC recruits the deadenylases directly through TNRC6 (PMID:21981923, PMID:21984185) and RGD draws all three as outputs of the pathway. XRN1 is optional (decision in part 8). (7) Translational repression is recorded as a process term on the TNRC6 and DDX6/PATL1 annotons and not as a separate part, because its mechanism in human cells (CCR4-NOT-DDX6 and 4E-T/4EHP, eIF4A displacement) is not settled and RGD itself calls it poorly understood. (8) The "substrate is not a participant" rule was applied to PABPC1: it is included because it does part of the work (stimulating PAN2-PAN3 and binding the TNRC6 PAM2 motif), not because it sits on the poly(A) tail being removed. Target mRNAs and the miRNAs themselves are not participants. GO-CAM and GOA observations for upstream review. Few production GO-CAMs contain these proteins (gocams/index.tsv, checked November 2026). Part 2: RAN-GTP-dependent nuclear export of the pre-miRNA by exportin-5
XPO5-RAN-GTP export of pre-miRNA to the cytoplasmTransport Steppre_mirna_export

Exportin-5 is a karyopherin that binds the pre-miRNA stem and its 3' overhang only when it also binds RAN-GTP. Part 3: cytoplasmic cleavage of the pre-miRNA loop by DICER1 with a dsRBD cofactor
DICER1 cleavage of pre-miRNA to the miRNA duplex, with TARBP2 or PRKRAProtein Complexdicer_cleavage

DICER1 measures from the 2-nt 3' overhang left by DROSHA (PAZ domain) and its two RNase III domains cut both strands near the terminal loop, releasing a ~22-bp miRNA duplex. DICER1 works with one of two paralogous dsRBD proteins, TARBP2 (TRBP) or PRKRA (PACT), which bind its helicase domain. Neither is needed for the cleavage itself; they tune substrate choice and cleavage site (and so isomiR length) and bridge DICER1 to AGO2 in the RISC-loading complex.

Part 4: loading of the miRNA duplex into an Argonaute and guide-strand selection (miRISC assembly)
Argonaute (AGO1-AGO4) loading and miRISC formationProtein Complexago_loading

DICER1-TARBP2 hands the duplex to an Argonaute in the RISC-loading complex. The Argonaute keeps the strand whose 5' end is less stably paired (the guide), anchoring its 5' phosphate in the MID domain and its 3' end in the PAZ domain, and discards the passenger strand. The four human Argonautes bind miRNAs without sequence preference and are largely redundant for seed-mediated repression; they differ in slicing. AGO2 cleaves perfectly paired targets and also cuts the passenger strand of perfectly paired duplexes; AGO1 and AGO4 lack efficient slicer activity, and AGO3 slices only with particular guides and targets. The loaded Argonaute is the core of miRISC and the hand-off point to the TNRC6 effectors.

Part 5: recruitment of a GW182-family scaffold (TNRC6A/B/C) by the miRNA-loaded Argonaute
TNRC6 (GW182) binding to AGO and recruitment of deadenylasesRegulatory Steptnrc6_recruitment

The N-terminal GW-repeat region of TNRC6 proteins contains several tryptophan-containing "AGO hooks" that dock into pockets on the Argonaute PIWI domain.
