Efferocytosis-induced macrophage priming

Engulfing an apoptotic cell changes how a macrophage responds to later challenges. In Drosophila embryos, each engulfed corpse triggers a brief rise in cytosolic calcium in the macrophage, which activates JNK. JNK signalling raises expression of the engulfment receptor Draper. Macrophages primed in this way migrate to wounds and take up bacteria; naive macrophages that have never met a corpse do neither. Beads of the same size are engulfed without causing the calcium rise or priming, so the trigger is the corpse itself. Priming is a switch rather than a dial: extra JNK or Draper does not make primed macrophages respond more strongly.

Boundary: this module starts after an apoptotic corpse has been engulfed and ends with a macrophage that can respond to wounds and microbes. Engulfment of the corpse belongs to MODULE:phagocytic_engulfment, where Draper also acts as an engulfment receptor. The outputs (migration to wounds, uptake of bacteria) are downstream processes, not parts of this module. GO has no term for this programme. The closest are GO:0042116 macrophage activation (used as the module concept) and GO:0090714 innate immunity memory response, which covers re-exposure to the same stimulus and so does not fit here. "efferocytosis-induced macrophage priming" (is_a GO:0042116, preceded_by GO:0043277) has been proposed to the GO editors in go-ontology#32748. Taxon scope: shown only in Drosophila. Draper belongs to the same receptor family as C. elegans CED-1, and JNK is conserved across animals, but whether corpse engulfment primes phagocytes this way in other animals is not known. In mammals, efferocytosis is usually anti-inflammatory (see MODULE:efferocytosis_induced_anti_inflammatory_programme).
