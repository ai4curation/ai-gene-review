L-histidine biosynthesis

De novo biosynthesis of L-histidine from PRPP and ATP through the conserved ten-reaction His pathway found in bacteria, archaea, fungi and plants. The module begins at the first committed ATP phosphoribosyltransferase reaction; PRPP production is shared upstream metabolism and is intentionally outside the module boundary. The pathway returns the ATP-derived purine ring to nucleotide metabolism as AICAR during the imidazole-glycerol-phosphate synthase reaction, which couples histidine and purine biosynthesis. The reaction chain is invariant, but its enzyme architecture varies widely across lineages: ATP phosphoribosyltransferase exists as a short-form catalytic subunit with a separate HisZ regulatory subunit or as a long-form single chain; the phosphoribosyl-ATP diphosphatase and phosphoribosyl-AMP cyclohydrolase can be separate proteins, a bifunctional HisIE protein, or domains of a trifunctional protein that also carries histidinol dehydrogenase (as in budding yeast); the glutaminase and cyclase halves of imidazole-glycerol-phosphate synthase can be separate HisH and HisF subunits or a single fused HisHF chain (as in fungi); and histidinol-phosphate phosphatase activity has arisen in several unrelated families (IMPase-like, HAD and PHP). The terminal histidinol dehydrogenase performs two successive NAD+-dependent oxidations through histidinal to produce L-histidine.

ALIGN with YeastPathways (2026-10): the ten YeastCyc reactions map one-to-one onto the module (the two histidinol dehydrogenase half-reactions HISTOLDEHYD-RXN and HISTALDEHYD-RXN are one GO:0004399 annoton), and the seven YeastCyc catalysts (HIS1, HIS4, HIS6, HIS7, HIS3, HIS5, HIS2) are all correct; no enzymes are missing. The former separate diphosphatase and cyclohydrolase parts were merged into one part (hisie_steps) so that the standalone, bifunctional HisIE (S. pombe his7) and trifunctional HIS4 architectures could be explicit variants; histidinol dehydrogenase gained a matching monofunctional/trifunctional variant set, and IGP synthase gained a heteromeric HisH+HisF versus fused HisHF (S. cerevisiae HIS7, S. pombe his4) variant set. A PHP-family HisJ variant (S. cerevisiae HIS2, S. pombe SPCC1672.01, B. subtilis HisJ) was added for the phosphatase step. The HisC family anchor moved from PANTHER PTHR43643:SF3 to InterPro IPR005861 because the fungal enzymes sit in PANTHER PTHR42885:SF2. In both GO-CAMs every activity occurs in the cytosol; the module does not assert a compartment because the scope includes plants. GO-CAM disagreements - YeastPathways_HISTSYN-PWY types HIS7 (GLUTAMIDOTRANS-RXN) and both HIS4 histidinol dehydrogenase half-reactions only with GO:0003674 rather than GO:0000107 and GO:0004399, and has no causal edges between activities; YeastCyc gives no EC number for the two dehydrogenase half-reactions. The PomBase model (6690711d00002904) agrees with the module and additionally includes a mitochondrial histidyl-tRNA ligase (hrs1) and L-histidine transport, which are outside this module. In KT2440 the OpenScientist taxon report supports the boundary, but step 7 remains unresolved at the candidate level; its recommendation to exclude PP_5147 is not adopted because the available evidence is insufficient either to establish or to rule out that protein's histidinol-phosphatase role.

Part 2: phosphoribosyl-ATP diphosphatase and phosphoribosyl-AMP cyclohydrolase
Phosphoribosyl-ATP to ProFAR (HisE and HisI reactions)Metabolic Pathwayhisie_steps

Two consecutive hydrolytic reactions: phosphoribosyl-ATP diphosphatase (HisE) releases diphosphate to give phosphoribosyl-AMP, and phosphoribosyl-AMP cyclohydrolase (HisI) opens the adenine ring to give ProFAR. The two activities are always both required, but their protein architecture varies by lineage, so the pair is modelled as one part with an explicit architecture variant set. Within every variant the diphosphatase reaction precedes the cyclohydrolase reaction.

Variant set: HisE/HisI enzyme architecture variants by enzyme architecture (Exactly One)

The HisE (phosphoribosyl-ATP pyrophosphohydrolase, InterPro IPR008179) and HisI (phosphoribosyl-AMP cyclohydrolase, Pfam PF01502) catalytic domains are the same in all three variants; only their fusion state differs. PANTHER does not separate the architectures (PSEPK standalone HisI and S. pombe bifunctional his7 are both PTHR42945:SF1), so no PANTHER family is asserted.

Part 4: imidazole-glycerol-phosphate synthase (glutamine amidotransferase)
PRFAR to imidazole-glycerol-phosphate + AICARReactionigp_synthase_step

Imidazole-glycerol-phosphate (IGP) synthase is a glutamine amidotransferase made of a cyclase (HisF) and a glutaminase / amidotransferase (HisH) unit. Glutamine hydrolysed by HisH supplies ammonia through an internal channel to the HisF active site, which cleaves PRFAR into IGP and AICAR. AICAR re-enters purine biosynthesis, linking histidine and nucleotide metabolism. The two units are separate subunits in most bacteria and archaea and a single fused chain in fungi.

Part 8: terminal bifunctional dehydrogenase
L-histidinol to L-histidineReactionhisD_step

Histidinol dehydrogenase is a bifunctional NAD+-dependent enzyme that oxidises L-histidinol to L-histidine in two steps via an L-histidinal intermediate, consuming two equivalents of NAD+. The catalytic domain is a standalone protein in most lineages, including fission yeast, but is the C-terminal domain of the trifunctional HIS4 protein in budding yeast.
