Alcoholic fermentation of pyruvate to ethanol

Alcoholic fermentation is the two-step, NAD+-regenerating branch that follows glycolysis in yeasts and other fungi, in plants under hypoxia, and in a few bacteria such as Zymomonas mobilis. A thiamine-diphosphate-dependent pyruvate decarboxylase removes CO2 from pyruvate to give acetaldehyde. An NADH-dependent alcohol dehydrogenase then reduces acetaldehyde to ethanol. That second step reoxidises the NADH formed at the glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate dehydrogenase step of glycolysis, so glycolysis can continue without respiration and the overall yield is two ATP per hexose. Two unrelated alcohol dehydrogenase families can carry out the reduction: zinc-dependent medium-chain dehydrogenases (fungal and plant ADH1) and the group III ("iron-containing") dehydrogenase family (Zymomonas ADH II and fungal ADH4-type enzymes), whose members differ in metal activation. Pyruvate decarboxylase also has a minor carboligase activity that joins acetaldehyde to a second acetaldehyde or to pyruvate, making acetoin. Acetoin can then be reduced to 2,3-butanediol. That side branch is modelled here as optional, together with the bacterial acetolactate route to the same product.

cytosolGO:0005829

Boundary. The module starts at pyruvate and ends at ethanol (plus the optional acetoin/butanediol branch). Glycolysis (modules/emp_glycolysis.yaml) supplies the pyruvate and NADH. The YeastPathways superpathway GLUCFERMEN-PWY is that module plus this one. Acetaldehyde oxidation to acetate and acetyl-CoA synthesis are the opposite, oxidative fate of the same acetaldehyde and are in modules/pdh_bypass_acetyl_coa_synthesis.yaml. Glycerol formation (the other cytosolic NADH sink) and the Ehrlich pathway (fusel alcohols via the same PDC/ADH enzymes acting on other 2-oxo acids) are separate modules. YeastCyc corrections. In GLUCFERMEN-PWY the alcohol dehydrogenase reaction lists ADH1-ADH5. Only ADH1 is the fermentative enzyme. ADH2 is the glucose-repressed ethanol-oxidising isozyme, ADH3 a mitochondrial-matrix redox-shuttle enzyme, and ADH4 (iron family, mitochondrial) and ADH5 are minor, so they are not members of the core annoton. ADH4 is kept as an orienting member of the iron-ADH variant. PDC6 is an active decarboxylase but is a sulfur-limitation and ethanol-phase isozyme that cannot support fermentation on its own. ARO10 and THI3 are not pyruvate decarboxylases. For the acetolactate-derived acetoin in PWY3O-335, the ILV2/ILV6 acetohydroxyacid synthase is the mitochondrial branched-chain amino acid enzyme (see modules/branched_chain_amino_acid_biosynthesis.yaml). Diacetyl arises from non-enzymatic oxidative decarboxylation of leaked acetolactate. That route is therefore not a yeast fermentation member, and only the dedicated bacterial acetolactate route is modelled. GO-CAM disagreements. (1) Every activity in YeastPathways_GLUCFERMEN-PWY is part_of GO:0019658, whose current label is "bifid shunt". That is wrong for yeast glucose fermentation; the appropriate process is GO:0019655 pyruvate fermentation to ethanol (plus glycolysis). (2) The same model places the mitochondrial ALD4 and ALD5 in the cytosol and includes FBP1. (3) YeastPathways_PWY3O-335 places the ILV2/ILV6 complex (CPX-3034) in the cytosol, but the enzyme is mitochondrial. (4) PWY3O-981 uses the broad GO:1902652 secondary alcohol metabolic process, and it types BDH1's diacetyl reduction (ACETOINDEHYDROG-RXN) as GO:0019152 acetoin dehydrogenase (NAD+) activity, a reaction written in the oxidative direction. (5) The PomBase fermentation model 678073a900000393 contains the four pdc genes, adh1, and out-of-boundary activities: atd1 (acetaldehyde dehydrogenase, used here in the PDH-bypass module), pyk1 (glycolysis), tnr3 (thiamine diphosphokinase, cofactor supply) and the Phx1 transcription factor. It does not include adh4.
