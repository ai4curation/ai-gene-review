Alcoholic fermentation of pyruvate to ethanol

Alcoholic fermentation converts pyruvate to acetaldehyde and carbon dioxide, then reduces acetaldehyde to ethanol while oxidizing NADH to NAD+. The terminal branch regenerates reducing-cofactor balance without itself producing ATP; upstream ATP yield depends on the glycolytic route. Canonical thiamine-dependent pyruvate decarboxylases operate in fungi, plants and selected bacteria, while Carassius fish possess a separately evolved mitochondrial E1-derived implementation. Zinc-dependent medium-chain and group III alcohol dehydrogenases provide distinct reduction catalysts, with metal activation and physiological deployment varying among members. An optional acetoin/butanediol branch diverts carbon through PDC carboligation or the bacterial catabolic acetolactate route.

YeastPathways:PWY3O-335
acetoin biosynthesis
Acetolactate-derived acetoin: ILV2/ILV6 acetohydroxyacid synthase makes (S)-2-acetolactate, which decomposes non-enzymatically by oxidative decarboxylation to diacetyl, and BDH1 reduces diacetyl to acetoin. In yeast the acetolactate synthase is the mitochondrial branched-chain amino acid enzyme and diacetyl formation is a spill-over. It is therefore recorded in notes and not counted as a member. The bacterial catabolic acetolactate route (with an acetolactate decarboxylase) is modelled as a variant instead.

The root is an evidence envelope, not a claim of ancestral presence in every descendant. Each variant constrains its physiological and taxonomic use. No universal compartment is asserted.

Boundary: direct pyruvate decarboxylation followed by acetaldehyde reduction, plus the existing optional acetoin/R,R-butanediol branch. Upstream glycolysis, cofactor synthesis, pyruvate-formate lyase/AdhE routes, PDH bypass, glycerol formation and Ehrlich chemistry remain separate. The terminal ethanol branch produces no ATP; EMP-fed yeast and ED-fed Zymomonas have different upstream yields. Taxa are an evidence envelope, not a distribution or universal ancestral-presence claim. Representative physiology is conditional. PDC6 can support partial glucose fermentation after promoter activation. Group III ADH does not imply iron activation in every member. Carassius subunit-level decarboxylase assignment is inferred and its beta2 accession is an incomplete submitted sequence, not evidence of native truncation. Plant ADH1 does not explain all ethanol production in mutants. The optional branch models a defined R,R endpoint, not all stereoisomer outputs in Klebsiella, Serratia or Bacillus. In yeast, leaked biosynthetic acetolactate can form diacetyl nonenzymatically and Bdh1 can reduce it; the mitochondrial ILV2/ILV6 precursor pathway is not a native catabolic AlsS substitute. The current leaf carboligation activity lacks a specific GO term and is kept as explicit chemistry. Production GO-CAM associations are organism-specific. PomBase model 678073a900000393 supplies the four PDC activities and adh1; its regulator, cofactor-supply, glycolytic and acetate-producing nodes lie outside this module. Broad secondary-alcohol process annotations and GO:0019152 for Bdh1 diacetyl reduction are not intrinsically incorrect merely because a reaction can be written in the opposite direction. Additional imported-model discrepancies are documented in audit notes after source verification.

The irreversible decarboxylation step that diverts pyruvate away from the pyruvate dehydrogenase complex. In yeasts the same acetaldehyde also feeds the PDH bypass (modules/pdh_bypass_acetyl_coa_synthesis.yaml). PDC-null mutants fail to grow in defined medium with glucose as sole carbon source, while complex glucose medium permits slower growth (PMID:8904337); the acetyl-CoA supply role must be distinguished from fermentative NADH recycling. The yeast isozymes also decarboxylate branched-chain 2-oxo acids (Ehrlich pathway), which is outside this module. ARO10 and the TPP-sensing regulator THI3 are PDC-family members that do not decarboxylate pyruvate and are not members here. Localization is supported for fungal and plant exemplars, but no direct native ZM4 localization assay is asserted. Fission-yeast isozyme genetics and total PDC activity do not constitute purified kinetic characterization of each paralog.
