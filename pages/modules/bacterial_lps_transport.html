Bacterial lipopolysaccharide transport

ATP-driven delivery of lipopolysaccharide or lipooligosaccharide from the outer leaflet of the inner membrane to the bacterial cell surface. Coupled LptBFG extraction, LptC-mediated coupling, periplasmic LptA-family transfer and LptD-mediated outer-membrane insertion form a trans-envelope bridge. The canonical LptDE terminal assembly and the meningococcal LptD-dependent surface-delivery profile differ in their requirement for LptE. Accessory LptM and LptY support terminal transport in characterized enterobacterial systems without defining universal components.

Boundary: mature LPS/LOS in the outer IM leaflet to exposure in the outer OM leaflet; synthesis, MsbA flipping and O-antigen elaboration are upstream. Bacteria is the shared taxon envelope, not a universal capacity assertion. LPS substrate, a diderm envelope and a compatible extraction/bridge/insertion system are necessary scope conditions. Lpt-like sequences in LPS-free diderms do not establish this substrate-specific process. E. coli and P. putida provide complete seven-component reviewed panels; the three Neisseria representatives establish a distinct terminal-role boundary and carrier evidence, not a complete reviewed extractor panel. Native Neisseria assays are H44/76 or HB-1; MC58 LptE/LptH are exact protein-sequence matches, not native MC58 phenotype assays. Meningococcal viability after LPS loss does not establish transport: LptC/H/F/G mutants fail surface accessibility, whereas LptE deletion preserves detectable surface LPS. Selected E. coli LptF suppressors bypass LptC; canonical missing-C failures do not imply impossibility in every genetic background. Cross-species interfaces require positive support: Pa LptC complementation can depend on elevated LptB expression. The 2026 E. coli fusion bridge expressed in an LPS-null Acinetobacter host is neither a native Acinetobacter panel nor proof of universal one-copy LptA stoichiometry. Permease ATP-coupling, carrier transfer, terminal insertion and accessory stabilization are distinct roles; no autonomous transport is assigned to isolated accessory proteins. The LptD chaperone LptE is not directly involved in lipopolysaccharide transport in Neisseria meningitidis.
LptE deletion preserves surface-accessible LPS in the tested meningococcal backgrounds while reducing LptD abundance, supporting a chaperone/biogenesis role. LptE dispensability for that endpoint differs from absence of all possible contributions. LptH/C/F/G mutants fail cell-surface accessibility assays. LptC/B/H conditional depletion reduces cellular LPS, restored by expression. Viability without LPS is not normal LPS transport; no native LptC-independent pathway is established. Meningococcal carrier LptH is distinct from the lipid A phosphoethanolamine transferase also called LptA. Q7DDQ9 is the carrier, even though its UniProt gene field uses lptA.
