Cardiolipin biosynthesis and remodelling in Drosophila melanogaster

Cardiolipin is the signature phospholipid of the inner mitochondrial membrane, where it supports respiratory chain complexes and cristae structure. It is made in mitochondria from CDP-diacylglycerol: Pgs1 forms phosphatidylglycerol phosphate, the phosphatase Ptpmt1 dephosphorylates it to phosphatidylglycerol, and cardiolipin synthase (Cls) joins phosphatidylglycerol with a second CDP-diacylglycerol to make nascent cardiolipin. Nascent cardiolipin is then remodelled: a lipase removes an acyl chain to form monolysocardiolipin, and the transacylase tafazzin (Taz) transfers an acyl chain from another phospholipid to monolysocardiolipin, giving cardiolipin its mature acyl composition. ABHD18 deacylates cardiolipin in flies and mice, and the calcium-independent phospholipase A2 iPLA2-VIA has also been implicated in cardiolipin deacylation. Loss of tafazzin causes monolysocardiolipin to accumulate and models Barth syndrome in flies.
