Bacterial NADPH-dependent GS–GOGAT ammonia assimilation

Glutamine synthetase and a two-chain NADPH-dependent glutamate synthase couple ammonium incorporation to net L-glutamate synthesis. GS uses ATP to amidate L-glutamate; GOGAT transfers the glutamine amide nitrogen to 2-oxoglutarate using NADPH, yielding two glutamates. One glutamate replenishes the GS substrate pool, giving one net glutamate per turn. This chemistry occurs in Pseudomonas putida, Escherichia coli, Bacillus subtilis and Azospirillum brasilense, with differences in enzyme families, regulation, assembly and physiological dependence.

The review supplies an explicit cyclic synthesis and network-boundary interpretation.
Net glutamate production from 2-oxoglutarate can hereby be achieved not only by GDH alone but also by GS-GOGAT coupled together. The latter process expends one ATP.

Direct GDH assimilation is an alternative in E. coli and some other bacteria, not a third required step or a universal property of every GDH. Native B. subtilis RocG/GudB physiology is primarily catabolic under the studied conditions, while C. glutamicum GOGAT is dispensable in tested regimes. High-affinity language and numeric kinetic values are not generalized from conserved chemistry. Ferredoxin- and NADH-linked GOGAT, transport, nitrogen fixation/reduction, PII/Ntr signaling, GlnA adenylylation and GlnR/TnrA regulation are outside the minimal catalytic requirement; their actual gene functions remain in the member reviews. The broader nitrogen_cycle module is a coarse neighboring view. No ancestral PTN is asserted: the old broad nodes are unnecessary for exact-product requirements and do not establish NADPH specificity or universal localization.
