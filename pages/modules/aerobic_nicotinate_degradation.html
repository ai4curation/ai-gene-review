Aerobic nicotinate degradation through 2,5-dihydroxypyridine

A six-reaction aerobic nicotinate degradation route in Pseudomonadota, converting nicotinate to fumarate through 6-hydroxynicotinate, 2,5-dihydroxypyridine, N-formylmaleamate, maleamate and maleate. The characterized two-component NicAB enzyme initiates hydroxylation, followed by NicC oxidative decarboxylation, NicX ring cleavage, NicD deformylation, NicF deamidation and MaiA isomerization. Related bacteria share downstream chemistry while differing in pathway distribution and entry-enzyme architecture.

Bordetella provides an additional native catabolic context but does not establish that every lineage or every entry architecture implements the same protein complex.

The complete native route is anchored in Pseudomonas putida KT2440. The taxon is an evidence-supported envelope, not a universal trait or a claim of evolutionary origin. Bordetella bronchiseptica NicC and NicX extend reaction-level sampling to Burkholderiales, but the full Bordetella entry complex is not asserted to have the two-component KT2440 architecture. BB1776 and BB1783 are separately reviewed architectural comparators; their NicB1/NicB2 roles remain predicted. All six reactions are required at this boundary. Nicotinate uptake, cofactor assembly, regulation by NicR/BpsR or NicS, terminal cytochrome oxidation and downstream fumarate metabolism remain context. Nicotine is a different starting substrate: S16 Iso/MaiA is a directly assayed terminal-reaction comparator, not a native complete-route exemplar. PANTHER names are copied verbatim and used only with reaction-specific constraints; no substrate specificity or PAINT ancestral placement is inferred from a family label. GO lacks exact terms for the NicAB cytochrome reaction and the two amide hydrolases; these leaves use explicit activity names and Rhea chemistry instead of overbroad molecular functions. Gene reviews propose matching new terms. Evidence coverage remains uneven: the founding paper assigns KT2440 MaiA by homology rather than a target enzyme assay, and the reported Bordetella NicX product analysis is a meeting abstract. These are limits of available evidence, not established absence of biochemical knowledge.
