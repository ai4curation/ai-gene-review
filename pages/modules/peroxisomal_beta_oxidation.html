Peroxisomal fatty-acid beta-oxidation

Peroxisomal beta-oxidation chain-shortens acyl-CoAs two carbons per turn inside the peroxisomal matrix. In fungi such as budding yeast and in plants it is the only fatty-acid beta-oxidation system of the cell, whereas in animals it handles the substrates mitochondria cannot: very-long-chain fatty acids, 2-methyl-branched fatty acids (pristanic acid), the C27 bile-acid intermediates and dicarboxylic acids. Long-chain substrates enter as acyl-CoA through an ABC-D half-transporter dimer, which in yeast hydrolyses the thioester during transport so that the fatty acid is re-activated on the matrix side by an acyl-CoA synthetase; medium-chain fatty acids enter independently and are activated inside. Each turn uses four reactions with peroxisome-specific enzymes: an FAD acyl-CoA oxidase that passes electrons directly to O2 (making H2O2, degraded by catalase); hydration and NAD+-dependent dehydrogenation by multifunctional proteins (the D-specific, (3R)-hydroxyacyl-CoA-forming multifunctional enzyme type 2 in fungi and as the main animal enzyme, plus an L-specific bifunctional protein in animals); and thiolytic cleavage by peroxisomal 3-ketoacyl-CoA thiolases. Because the peroxisomal membrane is impermeable to NAD(H), NADP(H) and acetyl-CoA, the pathway also depends on matrix redox shuttles (a peroxisomal malate dehydrogenase reoxidising NADH) and on carnitine or glyoxylate-cycle routes that export the acetyl units. Unsaturated fatty acids additionally need auxiliary enzymes: a delta3-delta2-enoyl-CoA isomerase and an NADPH-dependent 2,4-dienoyl-CoA reductase, whose NADPH is regenerated inside the peroxisome by NADP-dependent isocitrate dehydrogenase. In humans, single-enzyme defects (ACOX1, HSD17B4, EHHADH, SCP2, ABCD1) and peroxisome biogenesis disorders cause accumulation of very-long-chain and branched fatty acids and bile-acid intermediates with severe neurological disease.
