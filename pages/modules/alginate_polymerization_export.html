Alginate polymerization and export

A bacterial synthase-dependent alginate-production module: Alg8 polymerizes mannuronate with the c-di-GMP-responsive Alg44 partner, periplasmic AlgG and AlgKX support polymer handling, and the AlgE outer-membrane channel mediates export. AlgG epimerization changes polymer composition, while AlgL removes misplaced periplasmic polymer in a background-dependent homeostasis role. AlgX acetyltransferase chemistry, precursor synthesis, extracellular epimerases and upstream regulation are separate processes.

The abstract module distinguishes demonstrated activities from homolog inference. Families with substrate-diverse PANTHER assignments are described by verified members and relevant domain/function constraints rather than asserted as substrate-specific subfamilies. Alg44 is not assigned intrinsic polymerase catalysis. AlgG catalytic activity can be dispensable while its polymer-protection role remains required in the characterized P. aeruginosa background. AlgL is an optional homeostasis branch, not a required catalytic component of the synthase. AlgKX polymer handling is distinct from AlgX acetylation. Exact AlgG chemistry lacks a specific GO MF term and is represented with the valid broader GO term plus RHEA:45572; this ontology limitation is not a biological knowledge gap. Step order is presentational; the complete dynamic export mechanism has not been reconstructed. Scope is ABSTRACT across synthase-dependent alginate producers of Pseudomonas and Azotobacter. P. aeruginosa supplies most direct experiments, P. putida KT2440 is kept as one exemplar (with direct AlgKX structure and binding data), and A. vinelandii adds native genetics and recombinant enzymology. Other genera are not added without reviewed members. PANTHER families were reviewed for every member that carries one (PTHR22913 for Alg8, PTHR30386 for Alg44, PTHR11102 for AlgK). Each is functionally heterogeneous. SF12 mixes Alg8 with HasA hyaluronan synthases and NodC; Alg44 sits in the EmrA-related efflux-adaptor SF19; AlgK shares SF160 with Hrd3 and unrelated Sel1-repeat proteins. The root hyaluronan IBD and the SF19 bile-acid/xenobiotic IBDs are assessed as too deep or needing pruning for these members. No PANTHER or PTN id is therefore asserted as a module selector, and the roles are grounded by InterPro entries and reviewed representative members.
