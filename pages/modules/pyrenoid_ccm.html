Pyrenoid-based CO2-concentrating mechanism

The pyrenoid-based CO2-concentrating mechanism (PCCM) of green algae, modeled on Chlamydomonas reinhardtii. Rubisco is condensed by a multivalent linker protein into a liquid-like matrix (the pyrenoid) inside the chloroplast stroma. Thylakoid membrane tubules traverse the matrix. Bicarbonate accumulated in the stroma enters the thylakoid lumen through bestrophin-like channels and is dehydrated to CO2 by a lumenal carbonic anhydrase within the tubules, releasing CO2 into the Rubisco matrix. A starch sheath around the matrix limits CO2 back-diffusion, and a stromal LCIB/LCIC carbonic anhydrase complex at the pyrenoid periphery recaptures escaping CO2 as bicarbonate. The parts follow the six-step minimal-PCCM decomposition used in modeling and in plans to engineer a pyrenoid into C3 plant chloroplasts. Bicarbonate uptake across the plasma membrane and chloroplast envelope, and transcriptional induction under low CO2, are included as optional upstream parts.

Part 2: Thylakoid tubule association with the condensate
Pyrenoid tubule biogenesis and matrix tetheringCellular Componenttubule_matrix_tethering

Thylakoid membranes enter the matrix as pyrenoid tubules, built in three stages. SAGA1 initiates tubules where thylakoids meet the matrix; its homolog MITH1, recruited by SAGA1, extends them through the matrix; and RBMP2 converts the central tubules into a narrow reticulated region that hosts CAH3. Proteins carrying the Rubisco-binding motif (RBM) were proposed to hold the tubules and matrix together, but neither tubule RBM membrane protein tested so far behaves as a tether: BST4 (also called RBMP1) is not needed for tubule-matrix contact, and RBMP2 still builds the reticulated region without its RBMs. SAGA1 remains the best candidate for the membrane-matrix bond.

Part 3: Lumenal carbonic anhydrase in the pyrenoid tubules
Lumenal bicarbonate dehydration (CAH3)Reactionlumenal_co2_release

In the acidic thylakoid lumen of the tubules, CAH3 dehydrates bicarbonate to CO2, which diffuses into the surrounding Rubisco matrix. Under limiting CO2, CAH3 is phosphorylated and activated, and its share in the pyrenoid tubules is reported to rise (from about 19% to 37% of the total); a 2026 preprint instead finds it concentrated in the central tubules at both high and low CO2.

CAH3 is a lumenal alpha-type carbonic anhydrase; complementation of the cia-3 mutant with cah3 restores growth at air CO2.
PMID:23139834
Phosphorylation controls the localization and activation of the lumenal carbonic anhydrase in Chlamydomonas reinhardtii.

Part 4: Bicarbonate channels in the thylakoid membrane
Thylakoid bicarbonate entry (bestrophin-like channels)Transport Stepthylakoid_bicarbonate_entry

Three bestrophin-like paralogs (BST1-3), induced at low CO2 under CIA5 control and located on the thylakoid membrane, are proposed to move stromal bicarbonate into the lumen. No transport has been measured for BST1-3: the evidence is localization, CIA5-dependent induction, knockdown physiology and homology to bestrophin anion channels. GO:0160133 bicarbonate channel activity would be the precise term once conductance is shown.

Starch sheath around the matrixCellular Componentstarch_sheath_barrier

Plates of starch around the matrix are proposed to slow CO2 leakage. SAGA1 and its homolog SAGA2, each with starch-binding CBM20 domains and Rubisco-binding motifs, link the matrix to the sheath; the saga1;saga2 double mutant has no starch sheath at all. SAGA1 also controls sheath morphology and pyrenoid number. Modeling predicts that some diffusion barrier is needed for an efficient PCCM. Whether starch itself is the main barrier is not settled.

Part 6: Stromal CO2 recapture by carbonic anhydrase
LCIB/LCIC CO2 recapture at the pyrenoid peripheryReactionstromal_co2_recapture

The LCIB/LCIC complex, members of a distinct beta-carbonic anhydrase group, gathers around the pyrenoid under limiting CO2. Purified Chlamydomonas LCIB, LCIC and the LCIB/LCIC complex were inactive in vitro; CA activity of LCIB rests on rescue of CA-deficient yeast and Arabidopsis mutants and on its beta-CA zinc site. It is proposed to hydrate CO2 leaking from the matrix, and CO2 entering the chloroplast, to bicarbonate that is then trapped in the stroma.

Part 7: Bicarbonate uptake into the chloroplast (upstream supply) (optional)
Plasma membrane and chloroplast envelope bicarbonate uptakeTransport Stepci_uptake

At very low CO2, HLA3 at the plasma membrane and LCIA at the chloroplast envelope raise bicarbonate supply to the stroma. This is part of the full algal CCM but outside the minimal PCCM proposed for plants, where stromal bicarbonate is supplied by native stromal CA.
