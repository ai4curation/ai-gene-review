Human histone acetyltransferase complexes outside TIP60 — the GCN5/PCAF (KAT2A/KAT2B) HAT modules of SAGA and ATAC, the MYST acetyltransferases KAT6A/KAT6B (MOZ/MORF-BRPF-ING5-MEAF6), KAT7 (HBO1 with JADE or BRPF scaffolds) and KAT8 (MOF in the NSL and MSL complexes), and the stand-alone CBP/p300 acetyltransferases (CREBBP, EP300)

Histone acetyltransferases (HATs) transfer the acetyl group of acetyl-CoA to the epsilon-amino group of lysine residues in histone tails, neutralising their charge and creating binding sites for bromodomain and YEATS readers; acetylation of promoters and enhancers accompanies and promotes transcription. Apart from the TIP60-p400 complex, the human nuclear HATs fall into three groups. The GNAT-family paralogues GCN5 (KAT2A) and PCAF (KAT2B) are the catalytic subunits of two coactivator complexes, SAGA and ATAC, which share the ADA3 and SGF29 subunits but use different ADA2 paralogues (ADA2B in SAGA, ADA2A in ATAC); both acetylate nucleosomal histone H3, chiefly at K9 and K14. The MYST-family enzymes work in tetrameric assemblies on EPc-domain scaffolds with an ING reader and MEAF6: MOZ (KAT6A) and MORF (KAT6B) with a BRPF scaffold acetylate histone H3 (K14 and K23); HBO1 (KAT7) acetylates histone H4 at K5, K8 and K12 when bound to a JADE scaffold, but H3K14 when bound to a BRPF scaffold, so the scaffold, not the enzyme, chooses the histone tail; MOF (KAT8) acetylates H4K5 and H4K8 in the nine-subunit NSL complex at housekeeping promoters and deposits most H4K16 acetylation in the MSL complex. The paralogous coactivators CBP (CREBBP) and p300 (EP300) are themselves HATs recruited by many transcription factors without a dedicated stable complex, and are responsible for most H3K18 and H3K27 acetylation. Reader subunits (SGF29 for H3K4me3, ING4 and ING5 for H3K4me3, BRPF PHD fingers for unmodified H3, YEATS2 for acylated H3K27, ZZZ3 for the H3 N-terminus, MSL3 for H4K20me1) target the enzymes to chromatin.

Human SAGA acetylates histone H3, not H4, in free histones and nucleosomes, and does so more efficiently than recombinant GCN5. In cells GCN5/PCAF are required chiefly for H3K9 acetylation, and SAGA and ATAC together for H3K9 and H3K14 acetylation. KAT2A and KAT2B carry the catalytic activity: the histone H3K9 acetyltransferase rows on both (IDA, PMID:21131905) come from a study of GCN5/PCAF double-knockout mouse cells; KAT2A also carries histone H3 acetyltransferase (IDA, PMID:18838386, human SAGA and ATAC) and histone acetyltransferase rows (EXP and IDA), and KAT2B histone acetyltransferase rows (EXP, PMID:8945521, and others). SGF29 carries its own reader function (IDA, PMID:26421618). TADA2B and TADA3 have no experimental human molecular function beyond coactivator rows from assays of the whole STAGA complex, so they contribute to the complex activity only. GCN5 and PCAF also acetylate non-histone proteins and catalyse other acyl transfers (succinyl, glutaryl), which are outside this module.

Purified human ATAC acetylates histone H3 (H3K14 in vitro; H3K9 and H3K14 in cells together with SAGA) and does not acetylate histone H4. The catalytic subunit is GCN5 or PCAF. KAT14 (ATAC2) has a GNAT domain and a GOA histone acetyltransferase row (IDA, PMID:19103755) for weak H4-directed activity of the recombinant protein, but the purified human complex does not acetylate H4 (PMID:18838386) and KAT14 depletion disassembles the complex, so in this module KAT14 is a structural unit without a catalytic function; the weak activity is treated as in vitro activity of unknown relevance. Reader and adaptor units carry their experimental human GOA functions: YEATS2 histone reader activity (IDA, PMID:27103431; the paper shows selective reading of crotonylated H3K27), ZZZ3 histone binding (IDA, PMID:30217978; the ZZ domain reads the H3 N-terminus), DR1 TBP-class protein binding (IDA, PMID:18838386) and SGF29 H3K4me3 reader activity. TADA2A carries a contributes_to histone acetyltransferase row (PMID:9674425) and no function of its own; TADA3, MBIP and WDR5 have no experimental molecular function relevant to ATAC (MBIP's protein kinase inhibitor rows concern MAP3K12; WDR5's reader rows are from COMPASS studies). ATAC also stably carries the kinase MAP3K7 (TAK1) and the POLE3-POLE4 histone-fold dimer; these are named in the notes and not modelled.
