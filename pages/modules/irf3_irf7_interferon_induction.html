IRF3/IRF7 interferon-induction module

The shared signalling arm that turns innate-immune sensor activation into transcription of the type I interferon genes. Two routes phosphorylate the latent cytosolic transcription factors IRF3 and IRF7. In the adaptor-licensed route, the adaptors STING, MAVS and TRIF (downstream of cGAS, RIG-I/MDA5 and TLR3/TLR4) recruit the IKK-related kinase TBK1 or IKBKE, are phosphorylated on a conserved pLxIS motif, and use it to present IRF3 to the kinase. In plasmacytoid dendritic cells, TLR7 and TLR9 signal through MYD88, IRAK4, TRAF6, IRAK1 and IKK-alpha to phosphorylate IRF7. Phosphorylated IRFs dimerize and enter the nucleus. There they bind CBP/p300 and assemble with NF-kappaB and ATF-2/c-Jun on the interferon-beta enhancer, or drive the interferon-alpha genes. The sensors and receptors are modelled in their own modules; this module starts at the adaptor or the receptor-proximal adaptor and ends at interferon gene transcription.

First module of projects/PRODUCTION_TERMS.md. It exists because 21 production GO-CAMs file every step of this arm under GO:0032481 positive regulation of type I interferon production, a regulation term named for the output, and because the same TBK1 and IRF3 activities are filed under five or more different processes from model to model. The module gives the steps one home, names the interferon genes as the transcription factor's targets (17 of those 21 models name no interferon gene), and proposes the missing process term. Boundary: it starts at the pLxIS adaptor or the MyD88 complex and ends at interferon gene transcription. The sensors and receptors upstream are in modules/cgas_sting_signaling.yaml, modules/rig_i_signaling.yaml and modules/tlr4_md2_lps_signaling.yaml, which each carry their own copy of the TBK1-to-IRF3 step as the tail of their pathway; this module is the shared arm those tails instantiate, and the overlap is deliberate rather than a duplication to resolve by deleting either side. Signalling by the interferon that results is modules/type_i_interferon_signaling.yaml. NF-kappaB activation, which the same adaptors and kinases also drive, is modules/nfkb_canonical_signaling.yaml: the enhanceosome needs both, so neither module is complete on its own. IRF5 is left out; it is activated by the same kinases but drives inflammatory cytokines rather than interferon.

Adaptor-licensed TBK1/IKBKE routeRegulatory Stepadaptor_licensed_tbk1_route

The adaptor recruits TBK1 or IKBKE and is phosphorylated by it on a pLxIS motif; the phosphorylated motif binds IRF3 and positions it for phosphorylation by the same kinase. Scaffold proteins of the TANK family hold the kinase, and for MAVS and TRIF, TRAF3 self-ubiquitination is needed to bring it in.

Part 4: kinase scaffold (optional)

Part 2: IRF dimerization and nuclear import
IRF dimerization and nuclear importTransport Stepirf_dimerization_import

Phosphorylation of the C-terminal serine cluster releases the autoinhibited conformation, the IRF dimerizes, and the dimer accumulates in the nucleus. This is the step that GO-CAM models of this pathway compress into a single causal edge from a cytosolic kinase to a nuclear IRF transcription factor activity (see projects/GOCAM_COMPARTMENTS.md). Dimerization is a change of state of the IRFs themselves; import uses other gene products, included here by reference. IRF3 is exported from resting nuclei, so the switch is a change in the balance of import and export, not the appearance of a new signal.

Part 3: interferon gene transcription
Interferon gene transcriptionRegulatory Stepinterferon_gene_transcription

In the nucleus the IRF dimer binds the interferon gene regulatory elements and recruits CBP or p300. At the interferon-beta enhancer the IRF dimer occupies PRDIII-I alongside NF-kappaB and ATF-2/c-Jun in the enhanceosome, which is why this module ends at transcription and does not claim to be the only input to the gene. The interferon genes are named here as the targets of the transcription factor activity: the output is a gene product, not a free-standing "production" process.
