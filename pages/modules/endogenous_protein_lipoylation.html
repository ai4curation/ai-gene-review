Endogenous protein lipoylation

Endogenous protein lipoylation builds a lipoyl cofactor directly on conserved lysine residues in lipoyl domains. All characterized routes transfer an octanoyl group from octanoyl-acyl carrier protein to a protein carrier and use a radical-SAM lipoate synthase to insert sulfur atoms at C6 and C8. The topology varies by lineage: some bacteria (for example Escherichia coli and Pseudomonas putida) modify client lipoyl domains directly, whereas firmicutes such as Bacillus subtilis and eukaryotic mitochondria (human, yeast) first octanoylate the glycine cleavage H protein GcvH and use an amidotransferase to relay the acyl group from GcvH to the client E2 subunits. This module models the direct route, the Bacillus LipM-LipL relay and the mitochondrial LIPT2-LIAS-LIPT1 relay as alternatives. Exogenous lipoate salvage by ATP-dependent lipoate-protein ligases and the downstream lipoate-dependent enzyme complexes are outside the boundary.

protein lipoylationGO:0009249

These variants are separately characterized implementations of conserved endogenous lipoylation chemistry, not universal taxonomic rules. The module is species-neutral: P. putida KT2440 LipB/LipA is one exemplar of the direct route alongside E. coli; B. subtilis exemplifies the firmicute LipM-LipL relay; human and yeast exemplify the mitochondrial GCSH relay. The ONE_OR_MORE selection deliberately avoids asserting strict exclusivity but does not claim coexistence. Molecular functions occur only on leaf annotons, no generic module-level location is asserted, and salvage ligases plus downstream client complexes remain outside the boundary. Archaeal routes with split LipS1/LipS2 lipoyl synthases are not modeled; they would need their own variant with independently grounded families. Family grounding: PTHR10993 (LipB/LIPT2) and PTHR10949 (LipA/LIAS) each span direct and relay topologies, so family membership identifies the step, not the route. The LipM and LipL annotons use InterPro IPR024898 and IPR024897 because both proteins share PANTHER PTHR43679:SF2. The LIPT1 annoton carries no family term because PTHR12561:SF3 also contains ATP-dependent salvage ligases. Family reviews for all four PANTHER families are in interpro/panther. Intrinsic validator warnings: the LipL and LIPT1 functions have no GO id because no term in the configured GO release covers them. They are distinct reactions, recorded as distinct proposed terms in the gene reviews: LipL relays octanoyl from GcvH (RHEA:20213, EC 2.3.1.204) and LIPT1 relays lipoyl from GCSH (RHEA:16413, EC 2.3.1.200). Newer GO has GO:7770091 lipoyl-GcvH:protein N-lipoyltransferase activity, defined by the LIPT1 lipoyl-donor reaction; swap it in for LIPT1 once the configured release contains it, and decide then whether LipL fits it (its xrefs cite EC 2.3.1.204) or needs a separate octanoyl-donor term. The seed-overlap warning on the eukaryotic LIPT2 node PTN000863675 is also intrinsic: its IBD seeds are MOD and TAIR identifiers with no UniProtKB accession to compare with the representatives. PANTHER grounding PR #5076 proposed adding poplar LIP1 (B9H5L9) to the Bacillus LipA annoton and P. putida LipB (Q88DM4) to the human LIPT2 annoton; those placements put members in the wrong route and were not adopted. Yeast Lip3 (AIM22, P47051) is not used as a LIPT1-step exemplar because its amidotransfer activity has not been reconstituted.

Variant set: Endogenous protein-lipoylation topologies by route topology and lineage (One Or More)

The variants preserve the same chemistry but differ in whether the octanoyl or lipoyl group is relayed through GcvH before reaching client E2 lipoyl domains. ONE_OR_MORE is relaxed modeling, not evidence that the routes coexist in one organism. The cited work generally describes lineage-associated alternatives; coexistence and strict exclusivity remain untested and must be assessed for each concrete realization.

Direct LipB-LipA routeMetabolic Pathwaydirect_lipb_lipa_route

LipB transfers octanoyl from octanoyl-ACP directly to a client lipoyl domain, and LipA inserts both sulfur atoms on that protein-bound intermediate. This is the characterized route in Escherichia coli; other organisms require independent evidence for this topology.

Bacillus GcvH octanoyl-relay routeMetabolic Pathwaybacillus_gcvh_octanoyl_relay_route

In the characterized Bacillus subtilis route, LipM octanoylates GcvH, LipL transfers the acyl group from GcvH to client E2 lipoyl domains, and LipA inserts sulfur. LipL transfers octanoyl from octanoyl-GcvH in vitro, which is the order modeled here, but LipA can also act on octanoyl-GcvH: lipoyl-GcvH accumulates in cells lacking LipL and the ligase LplJ. The modeled order is therefore one supported realization, not a claim that sulfur insertion always follows relay.

PMID:21338420
A novel two-gene requirement for the octanoyltransfer reaction of Bacillus subtilis lipoic acid biosynthesis.
LipM and LipL catalyse sequential reactions; lipoyl-GcvH accumulates when LipL and LplJ are absent, so LipA can act on GcvH-bound octanoyl in vivo.

Mitochondrial GCSH lipoyl-relay routeMetabolic Pathwaymitochondrial_gcsh_lipoyl_relay_route

In mitochondria, a LipB-family octanoyltransferase (human LIPT2, yeast Lip2) transfers octanoyl from mitochondrial acyl carrier protein to the glycine cleavage H protein (GCSH, yeast Gcv3), the LipA-family lipoyl synthase (LIAS, yeast Lip5) inserts sulfur on GCSH-bound octanoyl, and a lipoate-protein-ligase-fold amidotransferase (human LIPT1) transfers the mature lipoyl group from GCSH to client E2 lipoyl domains. The order is biochemically defined in human; in yeast, Lip2, Lip5 and Gcv3 are genetically required for E2 lipoylation, and the Lip3 relay enzyme is required in vivo but its amidotransfer activity has not been reconstituted.

PMID:19570983
Lipoic acid synthesis and attachment in yeast mitochondria.
LIP2 and LIP5 are required for lipoylation of Gcv3, Lat1 and Kgd2, and lipoylated Gcv3 is required for Lat1 and Kgd2 lipoylation.
