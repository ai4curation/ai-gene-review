Siroheme biosynthesis from uroporphyrinogen III

Biosynthesis of siroheme, the iron-containing isobacteriochlorin prosthetic group of assimilatory sulfite and nitrite reductases, from the common tetrapyrrole precursor uroporphyrinogen III. Three reactions are required: S-adenosyl-L-methionine-dependent bis-methylation of uroporphyrinogen III at C-2 and C-7 to precorrin-2, NAD(P)+-dependent dehydrogenation of precorrin-2 to sirohydrochlorin, and insertion of ferrous iron into sirohydrochlorin to give siroheme. The chemistry is conserved in bacteria, fungi and plants, but the enzyme architecture differs. In enterobacteria a single multifunctional siroheme synthase (CysG) carries all three activities, with the methylase in its C-terminal region and the dehydrogenase and chelatase sharing an N-terminal region. Fungi split the pathway between a monofunctional uroporphyrinogen-III methyltransferase (Met1-type) and a bifunctional dehydrogenase-ferrochelatase (Met8-type) that uses a single active site for both reactions. Bacillus and plants use separate enzymes for every step, including a small SirB-type sirohydrochlorin ferrochelatase that in plants is located in the plastid. Uroporphyrinogen III synthesis is shared with heme, chlorophyll and cobalamin biosynthesis and lies upstream of this module; incorporation of siroheme into sulfite reductase lies downstream.

siroheme biosynthetic processGO:0019354

NEW module from YeastPathways PWY-5194-1 (2026-10). Boundary: starts at uroporphyrinogen III (shared with heme, chlorophyll and cobalamin; its synthesis by the HEM enzymes is upstream) and ends with free siroheme. Insertion of siroheme into sulfite reductase belongs to aps_dependent_assimilatory_sulfate_reduction, whose sulfite-reduction step depends on this module (in S. cerevisiae met1 and met8 mutants lack sulfite reductase activity, PMID:1479340); cobaltochelation of sirohydrochlorin for anaerobic cobalamin synthesis (CbiK/CbiX) is a separate branch. YeastCyc assessment - the three reactions and the MET1 and MET8 assignments are correct; YeastCyc's comment speaks of "four enzymatic steps" because the bis-methylation is two methyl transfers in one reaction (RXN-13403). Compartment: the yeast GO-CAM places all three activities in the cytosol, whereas plant SUMT and SirB are plastid enzymes, so no compartment is asserted at module level. Architecture is modelled as an explicit variant set for the two terminal reactions (bifunctional Met8, the N-terminal region of trifunctional CysG, or separate SirC + SirB); the methylase step is a single family role whose exemplars include the CysG C-terminal region. S. pombe orthologs (SPCC1739.06c SUMT and met8) exist but no PomBase GO-CAM is linked to this module, so they are not used as exemplars. GO-CAM comparison - YeastPathways_PWY-5194-1 agrees with the module on genes, functions and compartment, but has no causal edges between the three activities. In GOA, MET8 also carries an IDA for GO:0004325 protoporphyrin ferrochelatase activity (PMID:11980703); the physiologically relevant substrate is sirohydrochlorin and only GO:0051266 is used here.
