Trehalose synthesis via trehalose 6-phosphate and trehalase hydrolysis

Trehalose (alpha-D-glucopyranosyl alpha-D-glucopyranoside) is a non-reducing glucose disaccharide used as a reserve carbohydrate and stress protectant by fungi, bacteria, invertebrates and plants. This module covers its synthesis through the phosphorylated intermediate and its hydrolysis. A retaining glycosyltransferase of GT-20 family, trehalose-6-phosphate synthase (TPS, OtsA), transfers glucose from UDP-glucose to glucose 6-phosphate to form alpha,alpha-trehalose 6-phosphate (T6P). A HAD-superfamily trehalose-6-phosphate phosphatase (TPP, OtsB) then removes the phosphate to release free trehalose. The phosphatase is either a standalone OtsB-type enzyme (bacteria, plants) or the C-terminal phosphatase domain of a larger TPS2-type protein that also carries an inactive GT-20-like domain (fungi). In yeasts the two catalytic subunits form a cytosolic trehalose synthase complex together with large TSL1/TPS3-type regulatory subunits, which also carry GT-20-like domains and are needed for full activity and stability of the complex. Trehalose is hydrolysed to two glucose molecules by trehalases. A cytosolic glycoside hydrolase family 37 neutral trehalase mobilises intracellular trehalose, and an extracellular or periplasmic trehalase (GH65 acid trehalase in budding yeast, GH37 TreA in enterobacteria) hydrolyses external trehalose. T6P also acts as a signalling metabolite (for example as a hexokinase inhibitor in budding yeast), which is a separate role of the pathway intermediate. The other trehalose routes (TreY/TreZ and TreS from alpha-glucans, trehalose phosphorylases), the supply of UDP-glucose and glucose 6-phosphate, and trehalose transport are outside the boundary.

NEW module (YeastPathways crosswalk, 2026-10) generalising TRESYN-PWY and TREDEG-YEAST-PWY into the OtsA/OtsB-type (TPS/TPP) trehalose synthesis plus trehalase hydrolysis module found in fungi, bacteria, invertebrates and plants. Corrections and additions to YeastCyc: (1) TSL1 and TPS3 are described in the TRESYN-PWY comment but not attached to a reaction; they are modelled as an optional regulatory-subunit part of the trehalose synthase complex (GO:0005946). Their function is regulatory, not catalytic, so they get GO:0030234 enzyme regulator activity, matching the PomBase GO-CAM. (2) NTH2 is added as a second cytosolic trehalase (PMID:18065618). Its physiological contribution is minor compared with NTH1. (3) ATH1 is placed in the periplasmic space rather than the vacuole: more than 90% of Ath1 activity is extracellular (PMID:15128531). The YeastCyc comment agrees. Both catalytic yeast subunits are cytosolic, as in the YeastPathways GO-CAM. Architecture: the TPP step has a fungal TPS2-type variant and a standalone OtsB variant. In S. pombe both tps2 (SPAC3G6.09c) and tpp1 (SPAC19G12.15c) are TPS2-type proteins; tpp1 is the main phosphatase by genetics (PMID:11004189), and the GO-CAM types both as TPP. S. pombe SPAC22F8.05 is a regulator in the GO-CAM only on phylogenetic grounds and falls in a different PANTHER subfamily (SF119) from TSL1/TPS3 (SF15); its role is not experimentally established. GO-CAM disagreements: the YeastPathways TRESYN-PWY model omits TSL1/TPS3 and has no causal edge between TPS and TPP; there is no YeastPathways GO-CAM for TREDEG-YEAST-PWY. The PomBase model includes pgm1, uge1 and gal10 sugar-phosphate supply activities (Leloir pathway) that are outside this module. Out of scope: TreY/TreZ and TreS routes (modules trey_trez_trehalose_biosynthesis and bacterial_trehalose_synthesis_from_alpha_glucans), trehalose phosphorylases, trehalose transport (AGT1/MAL11), the PKA/Bmh control of NTH1, and T6P signalling to hexokinase (PMID:8809751). In plants most TPS-like and TPP-like genes are regulatory or signalling proteins; plant exemplars are not included.

Variant set: Intracellular versus extracellular trehalases by compartment and enzyme family (One Or More)

The same reaction (EC 3.2.1.28) is carried out by unrelated families in different compartments. Budding yeast has both variants: cytosolic GH37 NTH1/NTH2 mobilise endogenous trehalose and the GH65 acid trehalase ATH1 hydrolyses external trehalose. E. coli likewise has a cytoplasmic (TreF) and a periplasmic (TreA) GH37 trehalase.
