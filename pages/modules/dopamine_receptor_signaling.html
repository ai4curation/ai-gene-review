Dopamine receptor signaling — D1-like Golf/Gs and D2-like Gi/o control of adenylyl cyclase, the cAMP/PKA/DARPP-32/PP1 switch, and GRK/arrestin/RGS termination in striatal neurons

Dopamine signals through five G protein-coupled receptors split into two classes with opposite effects on adenylyl cyclase. The D1-like receptors (DRD1 and DRD5) couple through the stimulatory G alpha subunits — in the striatum chiefly the Golf variant GNAL rather than ubiquitous Gs — to activate adenylyl cyclase type 5 (ADCY5), raising cAMP and activating cAMP-dependent protein kinase (PKA). The D2-like receptors (DRD2, DRD3 and DRD4) couple through the inhibitory G alpha subunits GNAI2 and GNAO1 to inhibit ADCY5 and lower cAMP. The signal converges on a single bistable integrator, the phosphoprotein DARPP-32 (PPP1R1B). When PKA phosphorylates DARPP-32 at threonine 34 it becomes a potent inhibitor of protein phosphatase 1 (PP1, the PPP1CA/PPP1CB/PPP1CC catalytic subunits), amplifying phosphorylation of PP1 substrates downstream of D1 signalling. The switch is governed by a network of kinases and phosphatases acting on distinct residues: CDK5 phosphorylates threonine 75, converting DARPP-32 into a competitive inhibitor of PKA; a PKA-activated PP2A holoenzyme with the B56-delta regulatory subunit (PPP2R5D) and a Ca2+-activated PP2A holoenzyme with the PR72 subunit (PPP2R3A) dephosphorylate threonine 75; the Ca2+/calmodulin phosphatase calcineurin (PPP3CA/PPP3CB/PPP3CC) dephosphorylates threonine 34; and casein kinase 1 (CSNK1A1) phosphorylates serine 137 to protect phospho-threonine 34 from calcineurin. Receptor output is terminated by G protein-coupled receptor kinases (GRK2, GRK3, GRK5, GRK6), which phosphorylate agonist-occupied receptors so that beta-arrestins (ARRB1, ARRB2) uncouple them from G proteins and drive internalisation, and by regulators of G protein signalling, most prominently the striatal RGS9-2/G-beta-5/R7BP GAP complex (RGS9, GNB5, RGS7BP) that accelerates GTP hydrolysis on the D2-coupled G alpha subunits. Dopamine signalling also reaches the ERK cascade through DARPP-32-dependent control of the striatal tyrosine phosphatase STEP (PTPN5), and D2 receptors engage a G protein-independent arrestin arm in which ARRB2 scaffolds PP2A onto AKT.

The module core is the canonical cascade: D1-like receptors (DRD1 GO:0001588 IDA, DRD5 GO:0001588 IDA) -> Golf/Gs (GNAL GO:0003925) -> ADCY5 (GO:0004016 IDA/EXP) -> cAMP -> PKA catalytic subunits (PRKACA/PRKACB/PRKACG GO:0004691 IDA/IBA) -> DARPP-32 phospho-Thr34 (PPP1R1B GO:0004864 protein phosphatase inhibitor activity, IDA PMID:6087160) inhibiting PP1 (PPP1CA/PPP1CB/PPP1CC GO:0004722), and the opposing D2-like arm (DRD2/DRD3/DRD4 GO:0001591 IDA) -> Gi/o (GNAI2, GNAO1 GO:0003925) inhibiting ADCY5. Everything else is regulation: the DARPP-32 phospho-switch, GRK/arrestin desensitisation, the RGS GAP step, the STEP/ERK branch and the non-canonical arrestin-AKT arm. These are modelled as separate REGULATORY_STEP parts and linked with POSITIVELY_REGULATES/NEGATIVELY_REGULATES connections rather than folded into the core reaction chain. DARPP-32 as a participant, not a mere substrate. DARPP-32 is a PKA/CDK5 substrate, but it does the work of the step it is annotated to: phospho-Thr34-DARPP-32 is itself the active inhibitor of PP1 (GO:0004864, IDA PMID:6087160) and phospho-Thr75-DARPP-32 is itself the active inhibitor of PKA (PMID:10604473), so it is a legitimate functional participant and the hub of the switch, not a passive input. PP2A and calcineurin are modelled as holoenzyme complexes, because specificity for DARPP-32 is set by the regulatory subunit. B56-delta/PPP2R5D (GO:0019888, IDA) directs the cAMP/PKA-driven Thr75 dephosphorylation (PMID:17301223) and PR72/PPP2R3A (GO:0019888) directs the Ca2+-driven Thr75 dephosphorylation (PMID:17535922); the catalytic subunits PPP2CA/PPP2CB carry protein serine/threonine phosphatase activity (GO:0004722). Calcineurin (catalytic PPP3CA/PPP3CB/PPP3CC, GO:0033192 IDA) dephosphorylates Thr34. The regulatory subunits that are not in the RGD gene list (PP2A scaffold PPP2R1A, calcineurin B PPP3R1) are omitted; their absence is a deliberate scope choice, not a claim they are uninvolved. CSNK1A1/CK1 and Ser137. The RGD diagram lists CSNK1A1 among the DARPP-32 kinases. The Ser137 mechanism (phospho-Ser137 blocks Thr34 dephosphorylation by calcineurin) is shown in PMID:7708705 for casein kinase I, which did not resolve which CK1 gene product is responsible; the CSNK1A1-level assignment therefore rests on the RGD curation plus CSNK1A1's experimental protein serine/threonine kinase activity (GO:0004674 EXP/IDA), and the isoform caveat is flagged in a knowledge gap. RGS9-2/G-beta-5/R7BP. The striatal GAP for the D2-coupled G alpha subunits is the obligate RGS9-2 (RGS9 GO:0005096 GTPase activator activity, IMP/IBA) / G-beta-5 (GNB5 GO:0005096, IDA, the atypical beta subunit) / R7BP (RGS7BP, the palmitoylated membrane anchor) trimer (PMID:23857581, PMID:19332565). RGS7BP carries no GO molecular function (it is a scaffold/anchor), so it is a functionless active unit, kept visible because it sets the complex's localisation and stability. RGS2, RGS4 and RGS7 (GO:0005096) are the additional GAPs the RGD text names under "RGS in DA signaling" and are modelled as an alternative variant within the same termination part. STEP (PTPN5) and SRC. PTPN5/STEP is retained because it has a defined, directly supported role: it dephosphorylates and inactivates ERK (GO:0004725 protein tyrosine phosphatase activity, IBA/TAS; PMID:12483215, PMID:15608059), and DARPP-32/PP1 controls its phosphorylation state, so it is the mechanistic link from the cAMP/PP1 core to the ERK branch drawn on the RGD diagram. SRC is on RGD's wider human gene list but is not drawn on the diagram and has only a broad, indirect role (a ubiquitous tyrosine kinase implicated in D1/NMDA potentiation); it is excluded as peripheral rather than asserted with a dopamine-specific function it does not have in GOA. Non-canonical arrestin-AKT arm. The RGD record text states that Arrb2 in complex with Drd2 and PP2 inactivates PI3K/Akt and activates GSK3. This G protein-independent arm (PMID:16051150, GO:0160213 beta-arrestin-dependent dopamine receptor signaling pathway) is modelled as a regulatory part: ARRB2 (GO:0030674 protein-macromolecule adaptor activity, IDA) scaffolds PP2A (GO:0004722) onto AKT1, lowering AKT activity and disinhibiting GSK3B. AKT1 and GSK3B are not on the RGD curated gene-link list but are named in the RGD description and have completed gene reviews, so they are included as the targets of this arm.
