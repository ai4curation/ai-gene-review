Human COMPASS-family histone H3K4 methyltransferase complexes — the shared WRAD core (WDR5, RBBP5, ASH2L, DPY30) stimulating the SET-domain methyltransferase of three branches, SET1A/SET1B (with CFP1, WDR82 and HCF-1), MLL1/MLL2 (with menin and HCF-1) and MLL3/MLL4 (with PTIP, PA1, NCOA6 and UTX)

Methylation of histone H3 lysine 4 (H3K4), a mark of active promoters and enhancers, is deposited in human cells chiefly by six COMPASS-family complexes, each built around one SET-domain lysine methyltransferase of the KMT2 family: SETD1A, SETD1B, KMT2A (MLL1), KMT2B (MLL2), KMT2C (MLL3) or KMT2D (MLL4). All six share a four-subunit core, WRAD (WDR5, RBBP5, ASH2L and DPY30). The isolated SET domains are slow, mostly monomethylating enzymes; WDR5 binds the methyltransferase and bridges it to the RBBP5-ASH2L heterodimer, which binds the SET domain and stimulates its activity by orders of magnitude, while DPY30 binds ASH2L. The complexes fall into three branches by their branch-specific subunits. The SET1A and SET1B complexes carry CFP1 (CXXC1), which binds unmethylated CpG dinucleotides, and WDR82, which binds the Ser5-phosphorylated C-terminal domain of RNA polymerase II; they are the most active H3K4 trimethylases in vitro and are responsible for most H3K4 trimethylation near transcription start sites. The MLL1 and MLL2 complexes carry menin, which links them to the chromatin anchor LEDGF and to transcription factors and is required for H3K4 methylation of the HOX loci; HCF-1 is found in both the SET1 and MLL1 complexes. The MLL3 and MLL4 complexes carry PTIP, PA1, NCOA6 and the H3K27 demethylase UTX; MLL3 and MLL4 are the major H3K4 monomethylases of enhancers and are required for enhancer activation during differentiation.

(1) WRAD modelled once. The shared core is part 1, a single complex annoton, and the three branches are variants of part 2; one POSITIVELY_REGULATES connection from WRAD to the catalytic part stands for stimulation of whichever methyltransferase is present. Writing the four WRAD units into each of six complexes would repeat them six times; the branch complex terms (Set1C/COMPASS, MLL1/2 complex, MLL3/4 complex) are placed on the branch nodes and describe whole complexes including WRAD. (2) Catalysis is on the KMT2 subunit. Each methyltransferase is a gene product annoton with its own activity, as all six SET domains monomethylate H3K4 alone and all carry experimental methyltransferase rows. WRAD subunits get no catalytic function; the WRAD complex carries the curator-authored regulator term methyltransferase regulator activity, because WRAD was assayed as an added subcomplex that binds and stimulates the isolated SET domain. The reported intrinsic methyltransferase activity of the non-SET subunits (PMID:19556245) is not adopted. (3) Paralogues as nested variant sets. Within each branch the two catalytic paralogues (SETD1A/SETD1B, KMT2A/KMT2B, KMT2C/KMT2D) are an EXACTLY_ONE variant set, so the module encodes the six complexes; the branch-specific subunits are one complex annoton per branch, shared by both paralogue complexes of that branch. HCF-1 is written in both the SET1 and the MLL1/2 branch annotons. (4) Molecular function terms. SETD1A, SETD1B, KMT2A and KMT2B carry histone H3K4 methyltransferase activity (up to three methyl groups). WRAD carries no catalytic function in this module. Its subunits were added as a subcomplex (or as the RBBP5-ASH2L heterodimer plus WDR5) to isolated KMT2 SET domains, which they bound and stimulated about 600-fold for MLL1 (PMID:19556245) and for every family member (PMID:25561738, PMID:26886794), so the complex-level function is the regulator term methyltransferase regulator activity. This term is curator-authored: no human GOA row asserts it for any WRAD subunit; their experimental molecular-function rows are WDR5 histone reader rows (PMID:18840606, PMID:16600877), RBBP5 histone binding, ASH2L cis-regulatory region binding and DPY30 self-association. Those are not used as unit functions: the WDR5 pocket that binds the H3 tail is the same pocket that binds the KMT2 WIN motif (PMID:18840606), so its reader rows do not describe its role in the complex. Patel et al. (PMID:19556245) reported a weak methyltransferase activity of the non-SET components; it is not adopted, since later reconstitutions explain the dimethylation by stimulation of the SET domain (PMID:26886794).
