Arginine catabolism via arginase and ornithine aminotransferase

The arginase pathway degrades L-arginine to L-glutamate (or, without oxygen, to L-proline) and lets eukaryotes use arginine as a nitrogen source. Manganese-dependent arginase hydrolyzes L-arginine to L-ornithine and urea. Pyridoxal-phosphate-dependent ornithine aminotransferase then transfers the delta-amino group of ornithine to 2-oxoglutarate, forming L-glutamate and L-glutamate 5-semialdehyde, which cyclizes non-enzymatically to delta-1-pyrroline-5-carboxylate (P5C). How P5C reaches glutamate depends on where the enzymes sit. In fungi, arginase and ornithine aminotransferase are cytosolic: P5C is first reduced to proline by P5C reductase, and proline is then oxidized back to P5C and on to glutamate by mitochondrial proline dehydrogenase and P5C dehydrogenase. Proline oxidation needs the respiratory chain, so under anaerobic conditions the pathway stops at proline. Where ornithine aminotransferase is mitochondrial (animals, plants), P5C dehydrogenase can oxidize the semialdehyde directly. Urea goes on to urea-degrading enzymes and is outside the module.

NEW (YeastPathways project, 2026-10), merging ARGDEG-YEAST-PWY (aerobic) and ARG-PRO-PWY (anaerobic, ending at proline). The core is two enzymes, arginase and ornithine aminotransferase. The fate of P5C is an ONE_OR_MORE variant set: (1) the fungal proline-shuttle route (P5C reductase in the cytosol, then mitochondrial proline dehydrogenase and P5C dehydrogenase; PMID:6997271, PMID:7009582); (2) a proline end-point used anaerobically, when proline oxidation is blocked (PMID:12620851); (3) direct oxidation by P5C dehydrogenase where ornithine aminotransferase is mitochondrial (animals, plants; PMID:18419821). The P5C reductase, proline dehydrogenase and P5C dehydrogenase roles are the same as in proline_metabolism and use the same representatives. P5C/semialdehyde interconversion is non-enzymatic and is not modelled as a step. YeastCyc corrections: PUT2 (P07275) added as catalyst of RXN-14116, which has no gene in YeastCyc; PUT1 and PUT2 are mitochondrial, not cytosolic. S. pombe has two arginases (car1 and aru1, both PANTHER PTHR43782:SF3), and both are needed for arginine conversion (PMID:26075619). Plant arginases (for example Arabidopsis ARGAH2, Q9ZPF5) fall in the separate arginase/agmatinase family PTHR11358, so they are not representatives of the PTHR43782 selector. Hepatic ARG1 acts in the urea cycle (modules/urea_cycle.yaml); ARG2 is given as the mitochondrial, non-ureagenic animal exemplar. Excluded from the module: urea degradation (DUR1,2 urea amidolyase in fungi, urease in plants), arginine and proline uptake, nitrogen catabolite repression and ArgR/Mcm1 induction, and the arginase-OTCase "epiarginase" complex (GO:1903269). That complex is a regulatory link in which arginase bound to substrate inhibits ARG3 ornithine carbamoyltransferase, preventing futile re-synthesis of arginine during catabolism (PMID:12679340). It is recorded here and in the ARG3/CAR1 reviews rather than as a module step. GO-CAM disagreements: both YeastPathways models put every activity in cytosol, including mitochondrial PUT1 and PUT2. ARG-PRO-PWY also has no P5C dehydrogenase, consistent with its anaerobic end-point. The PomBase model 678073a900003902, linked here for the S. pombe arginases and car2, places car1 and aru1 arginase activities in GO:0000050 urea cycle. S. pombe has no ornithine carbamoyltransferase-based ureagenic cycle, so L-arginine catabolic process is the better process term.
