RNA exosome and nuclear exosome targeting complex in Drosophila melanogaster

The RNA exosome is a 3'-5' exoribonuclease complex that processes and degrades many classes of RNA in the nucleus and cytoplasm. A catalytically inactive nine-subunit barrel, made of a six-subunit ring (including Ski6, Mtr3, Rrp42, Rrp45 and Rrp46) capped by Csl4, Rrp4 and Rrp40, channels RNA to the processive exonuclease and endonuclease Dis3 (Rrp44). The nuclear exosome also contains the distributive exonuclease Rrp6. Nuclear cofactors deliver substrates: the nuclear exosome targeting (NEXT) complex, formed by the RNA helicase Mtr4, a zinc-knuckle protein (CG4622, ZCCHC8 homolog) and an RRM protein (CG11454, RBM7 homolog), targets short-lived non-polyadenylated transcripts for decay.
