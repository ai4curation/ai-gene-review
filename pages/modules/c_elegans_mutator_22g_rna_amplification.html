Mutator foci 22G-RNA amplification in Caenorhabditis elegans

In the Caenorhabditis elegans germline, silencing guided by primary small RNAs is weak, and is made effective by a second round of small RNA synthesis. Transcripts recognized by a primary Argonaute are cut, and the 5' fragment is given a poly(UG) tail. RNA-dependent RNA polymerases then use the tailed RNA as a template to synthesize abundant 22-nucleotide, 5'-guanosine secondary siRNAs (22G-RNAs). Most of this amplification takes place in Mutator foci. These are perinuclear, phase-separated condensates that lie next to P granules at nuclear pores. The intrinsically disordered Q/N-rich protein MUT-16 is the scaffold of the foci. Distinct regions of MUT-16 recruit the RdRP RRF-1 and, through the adaptor RDE-2 (MUT-8), the 3'-5' exoribonuclease MUT-7. The poly(UG) polymerase RDE-3 (MUT-2) also localizes to the foci. The 22G-RNAs made in this way are loaded into worm-specific Argonautes (WAGOs) that lack slicer residues, such as WAGO-1, and silence transposons, pseudogenes and other germline-repressed genes. A second germline 22G-RNA system, made by the RdRP EGO-1 and bound by the catalytic Argonaute CSR-1, targets most germline-expressed genes. It does not need the Mutator machinery, and it protects its targets from WAGO-mediated silencing instead of silencing them.

Part 2: MUT-7 exoribonuclease subcomplex
RDE-2/MUT-8 bridging of the MUT-7 exoribonuclease to MUT-16Protein Complexmut7_rde2_subcomplex

RDE-2 (MUT-8) has no catalytic activity. Its N-terminal region binds MUT-16, and its C-terminal region binds a Caenorhabditis-specific insertion in the MUT7-C domain of MUT-7. This tethers the 3'-5' exoribonuclease to Mutator foci. The interaction is needed for efficient RNAi. MUT-7 is required for transposon silencing, RNAi and germline small RNA production, but its RNA substrate in the amplification cycle is not defined.
