Mitochondrial sulfide oxidation in Drosophila melanogaster

Hydrogen sulfide produced by cysteine catabolism and gut microbes is detoxified in the mitochondrial matrix. Sulfide:quinone oxidoreductase (Sqor) oxidises sulfide to a protein-bound persulfide, passing the electrons to ubiquinone in the respiratory chain. The persulfide sulfur is then oxidised to sulfite by the persulfide dioxygenase ETHE1 (CG30022 in flies) or transferred to sulfite to form thiosulfate. Sulfite oxidase (shop) oxidises sulfite to sulfate, and the mitochondrial dicarboxylate carrier (Dic1) can export thiosulfate and sulfate in exchange for other anions.

Boundary. The sulfurtransferase (rhodanese) step that makes thiosulfate is not in the FlyBase group and is not modelled. CG30022 is the fly ortholog of ETHE1 (UniProt names it persulfide dioxygenase ETHE1); its activity and those of Sqor follow orthology and have not been assayed in flies. Dic1 transport of thiosulfate, sulfate and dicarboxylates has been shown in reconstituted proteoliposomes; Dic1 also transports malate, succinate and phosphate, so its role here is one of several. Sulfite oxidase also handles sulfite from cysteine catabolism. FlyBase groups realized by this module: SULOXID (FBgg0002230).
