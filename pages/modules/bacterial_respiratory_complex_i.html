Bacterial respiratory complex I (proton-translocating NADH:quinone oxidoreductase)

Reusable bacterial module for the conserved 14-subunit respiratory complex I, also called NDH-1 or NADH:quinone oxidoreductase. The peripheral arm oxidizes NADH, transfers electrons through FMN and iron-sulfur centers, and reduces the quinone pool. The membrane arm couples this redox chemistry to proton translocation. The module separates the NADH-input, quinone-reduction, membrane-interface, and antiporter-like proton-pumping parts so that fused subunits such as NuoC/D can be represented without collapsing the enzyme to a single step. It excludes the non-homologous, non-proton-pumping type-II NADH dehydrogenase (Ndh/NDH-2).

PMID:34308841
Structure of Escherichia coli respiratory complex I reconstituted into lipid nanodiscs reveals an uncoupled conformation.
Cryo-EM of the E. coli enzyme in lipid nanodiscs resolves the homologues of the 14 conserved core subunits as an L-shaped peripheral and membrane arm, with the antiporter-like NuoL, NuoM and NuoN chain and a quinone cavity formed by NuoH with NuoB and NuoD near cluster N2, grounding the E. coli exemplar set.

The 14-subunit NDH-1 core is conserved across proteobacteria and other bacterial phyla, so the module is written at the family level and grounded with two experimentally anchored gammaproteobacterial exemplar sets: the Escherichia coli K-12 NuoA-N complex, the best-characterized bacterial complex I genetically and biochemically, and the single-copy Pseudomonas putida KT2440 PP_4119-PP_4131 nuo locus. In both exemplars NuoC and NuoD are fused into one polypeptide (E. coli P33599, P. putida Q88FH5), so 13 proteins supply the 14 conserved subunit roles; in lineages with separate C and D subunits (for example Thermus thermophilus Nqo5 and Nqo4) the fused annoton is satisfied by the two polypeptides together. The fused-annoton grounding, PANTHER:PTHR11993, is the NuoD/49-kDa family; a separate NuoC/30-kDa polypeptide belongs to a different family that is not grounded here. Thermus Nqo subunits, which provided the first complete complex I structures, were not added as a third exemplar set because they would add 14 more members without adding a module role. PANTHER groundings were adopted from the module-wide grounding pass and checked against the PANTHER 19.0 member index: each exemplar falls in the cited family, except that PANTHER's own classification file places E. coli NuoF in PTHR11780:SF10 (with mitochondrial NDUFV1) while UniProt and InterPro place E. coli and P. putida NuoF in PTHR43578:SF3. The tree placement is adopted for E. coli NuoF (recorded in interpro/panther/panther-members-overrides.tsv), while P. putida NuoF, which PANTHER's files do not classify, stays in PTHR43578 by its UniProt cross-reference; the NuoF annoton is therefore grounded to both families, and the PTHR43578 and PTHR11780 reviews describe the split. NuoG is grounded at subfamily PTHR43105:SF10 rather than the family, because PTHR43105 is dominated by molybdopterin oxidoreductase catalytic subunits (nitrate reductases, formate dehydrogenases) for which a family-wide electron-transfer-subunit claim does not hold. Mitochondrial complex I, with its eukaryotic supernumerary NDUF subunits, is modelled separately (mitochondrial_complex_i_core); absence of those subunits here is not a pathway hole. GO:0050136 denotes non-electrogenic NADH:quinone activity and is incompatible with the defining proton-translocating chemistry of NDH-1; its automated propagation to Nuo subunits is removed in the exemplar gene reviews rather than used to model this module. The two terms are siblings, not parent and child: in GO, GO:0008137 NADH dehydrogenase (ubiquinone) activity (RHEA:29091, EC 7.1.1.2, with 4 H+ translocated per NADH) and GO:0050136 (RHEA:46160, EC 1.6.5.9) are each is_a GO:0003954 NADH dehydrogenase activity, and GO:0008137 is not an is_a descendant of GO:0050136 (checked against the GO release in the local OAK database, 2026-11-02).

Family review supports GO:0003954 FAMILY_WIDE. NADH oxidation at the FMN of the 51 kDa-type flavin module is shared by the complex I NuoF members (experimentally in E. coli NuoF) and by the NAD+-reducing hydrogenase and formate-dehydrogenase flavoprotein subunits, which catalyse the same NADH/NAD+ exchange (GO:0003954 is defined without direction-specific acceptor). PANTHER:PTHR11780

Family review supports GO:0003954 FAMILY_WIDE. The subunit's own chemistry, with the acceptor left unspecified: NADH docks on the glycine-rich loop (UniProt binding site 87-96 of P49821, GRGGAGFPTG, conserved in bacteria, plants, fungi and animals) and transfers a hydride to the bound FMN. Family review supports GO:0009055 FAMILY_WIDE. The subunit's own molecular function: redox exchange through its [2Fe-2S] cluster N1a with the reduced FMN of the 51 kDa subunit. The four cysteine ligands and the cluster domain (NCBIfam TIGR01958, PIRSF000216, PROSITE PS01099) are conserved across the reviewed members, and rapid NADH-driven reduction of N1a is measured in E. coli complex I. Family 		electron transfer activity

Family review supports GO:0009055 for PANTHER:PTHR43105:SF10. For the complex I 75 kDa subunits electron transfer through their iron-sulfur clusters is the subunit's own function (E. coli NuoG clusters N1b, N4, N5 on the main path). nuob_terminal_fes NuoB/Nqo6/NDUFS7 family
PANTHER:PTHR11995
Family 		electron transfer activity
GO:0009055 		Family Supported
Family review supports GO:0009055 FAMILY_WIDE. Every characterised member is the terminal iron-sulfur subunit of a complex I-type enzyme (bacterial NDH-1 NuoB, mitochondrial NDUFS7, chloroplast NDH NdhK) and carries the conserved four-cysteine motif (Pfam Oxidored_q6, PROSITE COMPLEX1_20K) that ligates the [4Fe-4S] cluster N2, which passes electrons from the iron-sulfur chain to the bound quinone. Family review supports GO:0009055 FAMILY_WIDE. Every member has two cysteine-ligated 4Fe-4S ferredoxin-type domains and is the two-cluster relay segment of a complex-I-like electron path.
