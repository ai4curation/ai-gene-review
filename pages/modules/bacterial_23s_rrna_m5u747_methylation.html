Bacterial 23S rRNA U747/U1939 C5 methylation

A paired rRNA modification program producing 5-methyluridine at positions corresponding to Escherichia coli 23S rRNA U747 and U1939. In E. coli, RlmC and RlmD divide these two activities; in the characterized Bacillus subtilis and Streptococcus pneumoniae implementations, RlmCD modifies both sites. The reactions share SAM-dependent C5 chemistry but act on distinct RNA regions. Their coexistence does not establish a required order or dependence between the two reactions. This comparison covers the named bacterial examples rather than universal bacterial conservation.

PMID:32290235
Surveyed mollicutes lack U747 modification and delimit the paired-site scope.
Consistent with this, MS analyses of the G725-C772 in domain II of the mollicutes 23S rRNAs confirmed that there was no modification at U747, or at any other nucleotide in this region (not shown).

The existing U747 module identifier is retained while its one-reaction boundary is expanded to the experimentally linked U747/U1939 specificity program. Each native variant contains two substantive activities. No PRECEDES or regulatory edge is asserted because the sources do not establish an obligatory ordering between these sites. RlmAII-dependent G748 methylation, Erm(B)-dependent antibiotic phenotypes, tRNA TrmA activity and U1939-only RlmFO implementations are outside this boundary. Broad family identifiers ground membership; they do not assert that all family members or all bacteria execute these reactions.
