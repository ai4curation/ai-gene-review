Stomatal lineage progression and spacing (EPF-ERECTA-YODA-MAPK and SPCH/MUTE/FAMA-SCRM)

In the Arabidopsis leaf epidermis, stomata are produced by a dedicated cell lineage whose successive transitions are driven by three closely related group Ia bHLH transcription factors, each acting as a heterodimer with the broadly expressed group IIIb bHLHs SCREAM/ICE1 (SCRM) and SCRM2. SPEECHLESS (SPCH) initiates the lineage by driving the asymmetric entry division of a meristemoid mother cell, MUTE terminates the self-renewing asymmetric divisions of the meristemoid and converts it into a guard mother cell (GMC), and FAMA drives the single symmetric GMC division and guard cell differentiation, then locks in guard cell fate with RETINOBLASTOMA-RELATED. Lineage entry and spacing are restrained by cell-cell signalling: the secreted cysteine-rich peptide EPF2, produced by early lineage cells, binds the leucine-rich-repeat receptor kinase ERECTA in a complex with the non-catalytic co-receptor TOO MANY MOUTHS (TMM); ligand binding recruits SERK co-receptor kinases and activates the MAPKKK YODA, which phosphorylates MKK4/MKK5, which in turn activate MPK3/MPK6. MPK3/MPK6, docked onto SCRM through its KiDoK motif and recruited with YODA to the cortex by the polarity scaffold BASL, phosphorylate SPCH and SCRM and promote their degradation, limiting lineage entry. Because SPCH-SCRM directly activates EPF2 and TMM, the circuit forms a negative feedback loop that self-organizes stomatal density and the one-cell spacing rule. The related peptide EPF1, perceived mainly by ERL1, acts later on spacing divisions, and the positive peptide stomagen (EPFL9) competes with EPF2 for ERECTA-TMM. Brassinosteroid (via BIN2 inhibition of YODA), CO2 (via CRSP cleavage of EPF2) and other environmental inputs converge on this module.
