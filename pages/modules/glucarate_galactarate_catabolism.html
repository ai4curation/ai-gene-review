Bacterial D-glucarate and D-galactarate catabolism

A bacterial aldarate catabolic pathway in which substrate-specific dehydratases convert D-glucarate (glucarate dehydratase, GudD) or D-galactarate (galactarate dehydratase, GarD) to the common intermediate 5-dehydro-4-deoxy-D-glucarate (5-keto-4-deoxy-D-glucarate, KDG). Two alternative downstream routes then dispose of KDG. In the oxidative route of Pseudomonas, Acinetobacter and other Proteobacteria and Actinobacteria, KDG dehydratase (KDGDH) forms 2,5-dioxopentanoate (alpha-ketoglutaric semialdehyde), which alpha-ketoglutaric semialdehyde dehydrogenase (KGSADH) oxidizes to 2-oxoglutarate. In the aldolase route of Escherichia coli and other Enterobacteriaceae, KDG aldolase (GarL) cleaves KDG to pyruvate and tartronate semialdehyde. Tartronate semialdehyde reductase (GarR) then forms D-glycerate, and glycerate 2-kinase (GarK) phosphorylates it to 2-phosphoglycerate. Organisms can encode either or both substrate-entry reactions.

Boundary: from the substrate-specific aldarate dehydratases to the first central-metabolism product, 2-oxoglutarate (oxidative route) or 2-phosphoglycerate (aldolase route; the pyruvate released by GarL also enters central metabolism). Aldarate uptake (GudP/GarP), upstream uronate oxidation (Udh), regulation (SdaR, CdaR) and downstream central metabolism are outside the module. The two entry reactions are ONE_OR_MORE alternatives because a genome may support either substrate or both. The two KDG-disposal routes are ROUTE alternatives with different chemistry; a genome normally encodes one. The module was generalized from a P. putida KT2440 draft. KT2440 remains an exemplar of the oxidative route, alongside the biochemically characterized A. baylyi ADP1 enzymes, and E. coli K-12 is the exemplar of the aldolase route. Representative members are limited to proteins with experimental evidence or reviewed family-rule support in those organisms. The uncharacterized Arthrobacter KDGDH used earlier was dropped. KT2440 encodes three KGSADH paralogs in PANTHER PTHR43353:SF3 (Q88GW5/PP_3602, Q88NF5/PP_1256, Q88JR4/PP_2585); only the aldarate-locus PP_3602 is a representative, and its NAD+ versus NADP+ preference is unmeasured. ADP1 KGSADH Q6FFQ0 has the NADP+-linked reaction recorded experimentally. Family-review notes: the PTHR21599 root PAINT node for glycerate 2-kinase is truthfully UNRESOLVED (product specificity is untested outside the Enterobacteriaceae), and the PTHR12128 DapA-activity scope is UNRESOLVED because SF66 is mixed. Neither affects the subfamily-level assessments that ground these annotons. Module QC may report FAMILY_FUNCTION_UNREVIEWED warnings arising from these truthful UNRESOLVED verdicts. Two function-conformance warnings are expected. (1) gudd_activity reports FAMILY_SCOPE_RESTRICTED for GO:0016836 (hydro-lyase activity), an ancestor of the annoton function GO:0008872. The PTHR48080 review scopes that parent SUBFAMILY_ONLY to a list that includes SF4, and scopes GO:0008872 itself SUBFAMILY_ONLY to SF4, which the check reports as FAMILY_SUPPORTED. The warning arises because the validator treats a SUBFAMILY_ONLY assessment of an ancestor term as restricting even when the grounded subfamily is inside its allowed list; it does not indicate a scope problem. (2) The KGSADH annoton reports a FUNCTION_UNREVIEWED coverage gap for PP_3602. Like its paralog PP_1256, PP_3602's core function and its MODIFY replacements use the proposed cofactor-neutral term "2,5-dioxovalerate dehydrogenase [NAD(P)+] activity", which has no GO id yet, so nothing with a GO id carries GO:0004030 for comparison. The proposed term's parent is GO:0004030, and the annoton is also grounded by the characterized ADP1 ortholog ACIAD0131, which reports CORE_SUPPORTED. Molecular functions are confined to the reaction annotons; no generic cytoplasmic localization is asserted.
