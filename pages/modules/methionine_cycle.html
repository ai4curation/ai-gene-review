Methionine (S-adenosylmethionine) cycle module

A taxon-neutral decomposition of the methionine / S-adenosylmethionine (SAM) cycle, the core methyl-group-cycling pathway that interconverts methionine, S-adenosyl-L-methionine, S-adenosyl-L-homocysteine, and homocysteine, with exits to transsulfuration (via cystathionine beta-synthase) and remethylation inputs from folate (via methionine synthase) and choline/betaine (via BHMT). This module is intentionally structured in two layers. The CATALYTIC layer (enzymatic steps) grounds each step to a GO molecular-function term, the same EC <-> GO MF alignment used by the gluconeogenesis module. The REGULATORY layer is captured as connections of type POSITIVELY_REGULATES / NEGATIVELY_REGULATES whose predicate is grounded to a Systems Biology Ontology (SBO) term that distinguishes the MECHANISM (competitive vs allosteric), with the effector represented as a ChEBI-grounded metabolite-pool node. This regulatory wiring (small-molecule effector -> enzyme, with mechanism and sign) is information GO cannot express: GO annotations attach to gene products, the effectors here are metabolites, and GO has no competitive-vs-allosteric distinction. The regulatory structure is transcribed from the biosustain Maud kinetic model `data/methionine/methionine_cycle.toml`. A notable showcase is METAT, where two isozyme forms of methionine adenosyltransferase catalyse the SAME reaction but are OPPOSITELY regulated by SAM: MAT-I is competitively product-inhibited by SAM, whereas MAT-III is allosterically activated by SAM. Isozyme-specific regulation of this kind is the central thing GO flattens away. The core chemistry (SAM synthesis, transmethylation, SAH hydrolysis, homocysteine remethylation) is universal; the lineage-specific pieces are modelled as optional parts or variants. Fungi such as budding and fission yeast run the cycle with single-chain SAM synthetases (two paralogues in S. cerevisiae), a single adenosylhomocysteinase, and a cobalamin-independent (MetE-type) methionine synthase that uses 5-methyltetrahydropteroyl triglutamate; they have no glycine N-methyltransferase or betaine-homocysteine methyltransferase, whereas mammals use the cobalamin-dependent MetH-type enzyme plus hepatic BHMT and GNMT. The 5'-methylthioadenosine (MTA) salvage route back to methionine is a separate module (methionine_salvage_mta_cycle).

Layering: catalytic steps ground to GO molecular-function terms (GO:0004478, GO:0008168, GO:0017174, GO:0004013, GO:0008705, GO:0047150, GO:0004122, GO:0004489); the pathway grounds to GO:0033353. The regulatory layer is deliberately NOT expressed in GO. Each regulatory edge is a connection typed POSITIVELY/NEGATIVELY_REGULATES whose predicate is grounded to SBO so the competitive-vs-allosteric MECHANISM is explicit (SBO:0000206 competitive inhibitor, SBO:0000636 allosteric activator, SBO:0000639 allosteric inhibitor), with the effector grounded to ChEBI. This is the information that falls outside GO scope: a metabolite cannot bear a GO annotation, and GO has no representation of inhibition mechanism. The MAT-I vs MAT-III split (same GO MF GO:0004478, opposite SAM regulation) is the canonical illustration. Quantitative kinetics (K_m, k_cat, K_i, Hill/MWC parameters) from the Maud model are intentionally out of scope for this module and belong with the model / SABIO-RK-style resources. YeastPathways alignment (PWY-5041, SAM-PWY): the SGD frames supply the fungal exemplars SAM1/SAM2 (single-chain MAT variant), SAH1 (adenosylhomocysteinase), and MET6 (cobalamin-independent methionine synthase). MET6 catalyses GO:0003871 with the triglutamate folate donor, not GO:0008705 (the monoglutamate MetH-type reaction), so it is modelled as a separate remethylation variant rather than added under ms_route. The single-reaction SAM-PWY frame is folded into the SAM-forming step. MET13 / MET12 (MTHFR) and CYS4 (CBS) are added as yeast exemplars of existing parts although the PWY-5041 frame does not list them. GNMT and BHMT are vertebrate-specific; the GNMT part is optional and the BHMT route is one of several ONE_OR_MORE remethylation/exit variants. The YeastPathways GO-CAM (gomodel:YeastPathways_PWY-5041) agrees with this module for all four activities (MF terms GO:0004478, GO:0004013, GO:0003871; cytosol); it has no node for the generic methylation reaction, which YeastCyc leaves without a catalyst. The crosswalk also listed PomBase model gomodel:69c59f8a00000584 for this module. That model is titled "L-methionine cycle (GO:0033353)" but its activities are the 5'-methylthioadenosine salvage enzymes (mta1 MTA phosphorylase, mri1, mde1/mta3, utr4, adi1), none of the SAM/SAH/homocysteine interconversions in the GO:0033353 definition. It is therefore associated with the methionine_salvage_mta_cycle module (process GO:0071267 L-methionine salvage) instead, and is not linked here. Fission yeast exemplars here (sam1, sah1, met26, met9, met11) are taken from UniProt and PAINT IBD donor lists rather than from a PomBase GO-CAM.

Part 1: SAM-forming step (methionine activation)
Methionine -> S-adenosyl-L-methionineReactionmetat_step

Methionine adenosyltransferase (EC 2.5.1.6) condenses methionine and ATP to S-adenosyl-L-methionine (SAM), the universal methyl donor. The isozyme forms differ in their feedback logic, so they are modelled as variants to carry isozyme-specific regulation.
