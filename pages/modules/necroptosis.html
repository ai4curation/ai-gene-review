Necroptosis (RHIM-dependent RIPK3-MLKL lytic cell death) module

A decomposition of the necroptotic process (GO:0070266): the caspase-8-restrained, RIPK3- and MLKL-dependent form of programmed necrosis. The module follows GO's own partition of the process into a signaling phase (GO:0097527 necroptotic signaling pathway), the assembly of the RIPK1/RIPK3 RHIM platform that licenses the effector kinase (GO:1901026 ripoptosome assembly involved in necroptotic process; complex GO:0097342 ripoptosome), and an execution phase (GO:0097528) carried out by MLKL at the plasma membrane. Design intent: the committing chemistry is a single linear hand-off - an upstream RHIM-bearing sensor or adaptor nucleates a RHIM amyloid core with RIPK3, activated RIPK3 phosphorylates MLKL, and phosphorylated MLKL destabilizes the membrane - so the module is one ordered chain of parts rather than the redundant-suppressor topology used for ferroptosis. The branch point that makes necroptosis a distinct program sits upstream: three alternative initiators converge on the same RIPK3 core and are modelled as a variant set along the axis of which receptor supplies the RHIM (RIPK1 downstream of TNFR1 and other death receptors; TICAM1/TRIF downstream of TLR3 and endosomal TLR4; ZBP1 downstream of Z-form nucleic acid sensing). The caspase-8/FADD/cFLIP brake is modelled as an optional regulatory part that negatively regulates the chain, not as a core step: its proteolysis is what normally keeps the module off, and the same complex is the apoptotic alternative.

file:genes/human/RIPK1/RIPK1-ai-review.yaml
Human RIPK1 AI gene review
The reviewed RIPK1 core functions distinguish a kinase-dependent pro-necroptotic role (GO:0004674 in GO:0097527/GO:0070266/GO:1901026) from a kinase-independent scaffold role that restrains necroptosis; only the former is a core part of this module.

Boundary decisions. (1) Scope is the necroptotic process itself (GO:0070266) and its three GO parts. Receptor-proximal biology that merely feeds it is out of scope and is reached by reference, not duplicated: TNFR1 ligation, complex I assembly, cIAP/LUBAC ubiquitination and the NF-kappaB survival output belong to modules/tnf_signaling.yaml; LPS recognition and the TRAM-TRIF endosomal branch belong to modules/tlr4_md2_lps_signaling.yaml; FAS/FADD/DISC apoptotic initiation belongs to modules/death_receptor_apoptotic_signaling.yaml; effector-caspase execution belongs to modules/apoptotic_execution_phase.yaml. The upstream initiator variants therefore model only the RHIM-bearing adaptor that nucleates RIPK3, and the receptor that activates it is named in prose rather than given an annoton. (2) RIPK1 appears only in its kinase-dependent, necrosome-nucleating role. Its kinase-independent scaffold role is an inhibitor of this module (reviewed core function in GO:0060546), and the TRIF and ZBP1 routes can reach RIPK3 without it, which is why RIPK1 is one variant rather than an obligate part. (3) MLKL is placed in the execution phase and RIPK1/RIPK3 in the signaling phase, following the per-gene reviews. MLKL's core activity is taken as GO:0140912 membrane destabilizing activity; the reviews reject GO:0140911 pore-forming activity for MLKL, and this module does not assert it. (4) Caspase-8/FADD/cFLIP are the brake, not the core. They are an optional regulatory part with NEGATIVELY_REGULATES edges, because necroptosis is the output that appears when this proteolytic restraint fails; the same platform is also the apoptotic alternative, modelled in the death-receptor and apoptotic-execution modules. (5) Ripoptosome/necrosome naming. The literature calls the RIPK1-RIPK3 RHIM core the "necrosome" and the RIPK1-FADD-caspase-8-cFLIP platform the "ripoptosome". GO has one term, GO:0097342 ripoptosome, whose definition centres on RIPK1/RIPK3 as core components while carrying "necrosome" as an exact-in-spirit (RELATED) synonym; GO:1901026 is correspondingly named "ripoptosome assembly involved in necroptotic process". This module uses those GO terms as they stand and does not invent a separate necrosome term; the naming mismatch is recorded as an ontology knowledge gap rather than resolved by curator fiat. (6) Parthanatos is excluded. PARP1/AIF-dependent parthanatotic death does not require RIPK3 or MLKL and is not a form of necroptosis, so it is outside this module even though GO:0070266 carries "parthanatos" and "PARP-dependent cell death" as RELATED synonyms. Those synonyms are legacy aggregation, not a scope statement, and nothing in this module should be read as covering parthanatos. (7) No module-level deep research exists for necroptosis: no provider run was available when this module was curated, so there is no necroptosis-deep-research-<provider>.md file. All structural claims are grounded in the per-gene reviews under genes/human/, cached publications, local PAINT rows and the cached GO-CAM named above.

Part 1: necroptotic signaling - a RHIM-bearing sensor or adaptor is activated and engages RIPK3
Necroptotic signaling (upstream RHIM-dependent initiation)Signaling Pathwaynecroptotic_signaling

The signaling phase of necroptosis. An upstream RHIM-containing protein is activated by its own receptor system and presents its RHIM to RIPK3. The three known routes - RIPK1 downstream of ligated death receptors, TICAM1/TRIF downstream of TLR3 and endosomal TLR4, and ZBP1 downstream of Z-form nucleic acid - differ in sensor and in whether RIPK1 is required, but converge on the same RIPK3 core. Receptor ligation itself is modelled in the TNF, TLR4 and death-receptor modules and is not duplicated here.

Part 2: RHIM amyloid assembly of the necrosome/ripoptosome core, which activates RIPK3
Necrosome (ripoptosome) RHIM assemblyProtein Complexnecrosome_rhim_assembly

The RHIM of RIPK3 polymerizes with the RHIM of the upstream initiator (RIPK1, TRIF or ZBP1) into hetero-amyloid filaments that form the core of the necrosome. This assembly is not merely an interaction: RHIM mutation abolishes cluster formation, RIPK3 kinase activation and necroptosis, so assembly is the step that switches the effector kinase on.

RIPK3 phosphorylation of MLKLRegulatory Stepripk3_mlkl_licensing

Within the assembled necrosome, RIPK3 phosphorylates MLKL on T357/S358 in the pseudokinase activation loop. This is the committing step of the pathway: it is required for MLKL oligomerization and membrane translocation, and activating mutations in the MLKL pseudoactive site cause RIPK3-independent necroptosis.

Part 5: caspase-8/FADD/cFLIP brake - proteolytic restraint whose failure permits the chain above (optional)
Caspase-8/FADD/cFLIP brake on necroptosisRegulatory Stepcasp8_fadd_cflip_brake

In a cell with intact caspase-8 activity, FADD-recruited caspase-8 (heterodimerized with cFLIP(L)) cleaves RIPK1 and other substrates and keeps the RHIM core from forming; necroptosis is what appears when this restraint is lost, which is why caspase-8 deficiency is embryonic-lethal in a RIPK3-dependent way. The same RIPK1/FADD/caspase-8/cFLIP platform is the apoptotic alternative and is modelled as such in modules/death_receptor_apoptotic_signaling.yaml and modules/apoptotic_execution_phase.yaml; it is included here only as regulation, not as a core necroptotic step.
