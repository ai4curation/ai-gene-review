High-voltage-activated calcium channels — CaV1 (L-type; CACNA1S, CACNA1C, CACNA1D, CACNA1F) and CaV2 (P/Q-, N-, R-type; CACNA1A, CACNA1B, CACNA1E) pore-forming alpha1 subunits assembled with auxiliary CaVbeta (CACNB1-4) and alpha2delta (CACNA2D1-4) subunits for depolarisation-gated calcium entry across the plasma membrane (human)

High-voltage-activated (HVA) calcium channels open when the plasma membrane is strongly depolarised and let calcium flow down its steep electrochemical gradient into the cytosol, converting electrical activity into calcium signals. Each channel is a complex built around one pore-forming alpha1 subunit, a protein of four homologous repeats that each contain a voltage-sensing domain and a pore domain, so that the alpha1 subunit alone carries both the voltage sensor and the calcium-selective pore. Seven human genes encode HVA alpha1 subunits in two subfamilies. The CaV1 (L-type, dihydropyridine-sensitive) channels are CaV1.1 (CACNA1S) in skeletal muscle, CaV1.2 (CACNA1C) in heart, smooth muscle and neurons, CaV1.3 (CACNA1D) in sinoatrial node, cochlear hair cells, neurons and endocrine cells, and CaV1.4 (CACNA1F) in retinal photoreceptors and bipolar cells; they initiate contraction, secretion, gene expression and transmitter release at ribbon synapses. The CaV2 channels are P/Q-type CaV2.1 (CACNA1A), N-type CaV2.2 (CACNA1B) and R-type CaV2.3 (CACNA1E), which trigger fast transmitter release at presynaptic terminals. The alpha1 subunit is assembled with a cytosolic CaVbeta subunit (CACNB1-4), a membrane-associated guanylate kinase-like protein that binds the alpha-interaction domain in the I-II linker, promotes surface expression and tunes activation and inactivation, and with an extracellular, GPI-anchored alpha2delta subunit (CACNA2D1-4), whose disulfide-linked alpha2 and delta chains dock on the pore domain and raise channel number and current density. In skeletal muscle a gamma1 subunit (CACNG1) is a further component. Which alpha1, beta and alpha2delta paralogues combine is tissue- dependent, and the auxiliary subunits do not conduct calcium themselves.
