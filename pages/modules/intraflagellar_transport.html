Intraflagellar transport

Intraflagellar transport (IFT) is the bidirectional, motor-driven movement of large protein trains along the ciliary axoneme that builds and maintains cilia. It is ancestral in ciliated eukaryotes and has been lost along with cilia in lineages such as most fungi, flowering plants and Dictyostelium. IFT trains are polymers of two multisubunit complexes. IFT-B (including IFT88, IFT52, IFT57, IFT80, IFT70, IFT38, IFT46, IFT54, IFT56, IFT172, IFT20 and the IFT74/IFT81 tubulin-binding module) is needed to extend the axoneme and carries tubulin and other axonemal precursors. IFT-A (IFT140, IFT122, IFT144, IFT139, IFT121 and IFT43) is needed for retrograde transport and for ciliary import of membrane proteins. Heterotrimeric kinesin-II (two kinesin-2 motor subunits and a non-motor KAP subunit) carries trains from the ciliary base through the transition zone toward the tip. Some lineages add a homodimeric kinesin-2 accessory motor. In Caenorhabditis elegans sensory cilia, OSM-3 takes trains over from kinesin-II in a handover zone and alone builds the distal singlet segment, and the IFT-B subunit DYF-1 is required for OSM-3 to dock onto trains. At the tip trains turn around and are returned to the base by IFT-dynein (cytoplasmic dynein-2), whose heavy chain is DYNC2H1 (worm CHE-3). Ciliary kinases of the MAK/CILK1 family (worm DYF-5, human CILK1) regulate motor behaviour, train turnaround and cilium length; DYF-5 phosphorylates the tubulin-binding module of IFT-74 to promote tubulin unloading, and CILK1 phosphorylates the kinesin-II subunit KIF3A. The BBSome couples IFT-A and IFT-B and regulates IFT assembly at the base and turnaround at the tip. Loss of IFT-B, IFT-A, kinesin-II or dynein-2 function causes absent, truncated or malformed cilia; in worms this prevents dye uptake by sensory neurons, and in humans IFT gene mutations cause skeletal and renal ciliopathies.

Boundary. This module covers the IFT machine itself: the IFT-B and IFT-A train complexes, the anterograde kinesin-2 motors, the retrograde IFT-dynein, and the MAK/CILK1-family kinases that regulate motor behaviour and cargo release. The ciliary transition zone (MODULE:ciliary_transition_zone) is a separate module; IFT trains pass through it, and IFT and transition-zone proteins act at different steps of ciliogenesis. Transcriptional control of ciliary genes (RFX factors such as worm DAF-19) is regulatory context, not a part. Taxon scope. The module is set at Eukaryota because the IFT-A, IFT-B, kinesin-II and dynein-2 families were present in the last eukaryotic common ancestor and the IFT cycle has been characterised in green algae, nematodes and vertebrates. It does not apply to lineages that lost cilia, and some ciliated lineages build flagella without IFT (for example, Plasmodium gametes assemble their axonemes in the cytoplasm). Representatives here are from C. elegans and human only. No Chlamydomonas IFT protein has a gene review in this repository, so Chlamydomonas is used as evidence, not as a representative. Lineage-variable motor. The homodimeric kinesin-2 accessory motor is modelled as an optional part with a variant set. Only the nematode OSM-3 variant is grounded. Vertebrate KIF17 is the OSM-3 homolog and acts as an accessory or cell-type-specific motor (for example, in photoreceptor outer segments), but it has no gene review here, so it is mentioned in these notes and not modelled as a variant. Chlamydomonas flagella are built by heterotrimeric kinesin-II (FLA10/FLA8/KAP) without a homodimeric kinesin-2. Relation to MODULE:bbsome. The BBSome is modelled separately. In worms BBS-7 and BBS-8 hold IFT-A and IFT-B together so that both motors move the same train (in bbs-7/bbs-8 mutants IFT-A is moved by kinesin-II and IFT-B by OSM-3 separately; PMID:16049494), and the BBSome binds anterograde trains in a DYF-2- and BBS-1-dependent manner and is needed for turnaround at the tip (PMID:22922713). Those interactions are recorded as an interface in the IFT-A part and the turnaround connection, not as BBSome membership. Members and molecular functions. Each subunit or motor role is an ortholog family, with the worm protein and, where a gene review exists, a human representative (IFT88, IFT140, KIF3A, DYNC2H1, CILK1). Human orthologs of the other worm subunits (for example KIF3B, KIFAP3, DYNC2LI1, IFT122, WDR19, WDR35) have no gene reviews here and are named in text only. Molecular functions follow the gene reviews' core functions. Several worm reviews (OSM-6, CHE-13, CHE-11, DAF-10, DYF-2) give structural molecule activity for scaffold subunits; this module does not use that term, and those subunits, like the ones whose reviews assert no MF (CHE-2, DYF-3, DYF-6, OSM-1, IFT-20, IFT-139, IFT-43, IFTA-1, human IFT88 and IFT140), carry only a role description. IFT20 has a Golgi-associated role in mammalian cells that is not seen in worms; this module covers only its ciliary IFT-B role. No PANTHER family ids are asserted for these roles. Kinase regulation. DYF-5 and CILK1 are modelled as regulators of IFT, not as transport machinery. Worm has a single MAK/CILK1-family ciliary kinase (DYF-5); mammals have both CILK1 and MAK, and only CILK1 is reviewed here. Ciliary segments. Worm amphid channel cilia have a transition zone, a middle segment of doublet microtubules and a distal segment of singlet microtubules. Segment-level anatomy is described in prose because GO has no terms for ciliary middle or distal segments.

Part 1: anterograde IFT train complex B
IFT-B complexProtein Complexift_b_complex

IFT-B is the larger of the two IFT train subcomplexes. It is needed to build the axoneme and carries tubulin through the IFT74/IFT81 tubulin-binding module. Mammalian interaction mapping divides it into a core (IFT-B1) and a peripheral (IFT-B2) subcomplex. In worms IFT-B is the subcomplex that OSM-3 moves when trains are split in bbs mutants; worm IFT-B subunits concentrate at the ciliary base and move bidirectionally along the axoneme, and mutants lacking them have short or absent axonemes and fail to take up dyes.
