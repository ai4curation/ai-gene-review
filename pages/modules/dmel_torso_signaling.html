Torso receptor tyrosine kinase signaling in Drosophila melanogaster

Torso is a receptor tyrosine kinase that is uniformly distributed in the early embryo membrane but activated only at the anterior and posterior poles, where it specifies the terminal structures. Local activation depends on Torso-like, which is deposited at the poles by follicle cells and acts together with eggshell components (Nasrat/fs(1)N, Polehole/fs(1)M3 and Closca) to allow activation of Torso by its ligand Trunk. Torso signals through Drk, Sos and Ras to the Raf-MEK (Dsor1)-ERK (Rolled) cascade. ERK phosphorylation relieves repression by Capicua, which with the corepressor Groucho represses the terminal gap genes tailless and huckebein outside the poles. In the larval prothoracic gland, Torso is activated by prothoracicotropic hormone (PTTH) to trigger ecdysone production and the onset of metamorphosis.
