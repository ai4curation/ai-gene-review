ER/SR calcium store cycling — calcium release through IP3 receptors (ITPR1/2/3) and ryanodine receptors (RYR1/2/3, with CACNA1S-coupled RYR1 release in skeletal muscle and calcium-induced calcium release by RYR2 in heart) and re-uptake by SERCA pumps (ATP2A1/2/3), with phospholamban/sarcolipin inhibition of SERCA and calsequestrin-2 luminal buffering

The endoplasmic reticulum (ER), and its muscle specialisation the sarcoplasmic reticulum (SR), is the main intracellular calcium store. Calcium leaves the store through two families of large tetrameric release channels in the ER/SR membrane and returns through P-type ATPase pumps. Inositol 1,4,5-trisphosphate (IP3) receptors (ITPR1, ITPR2, ITPR3) open when IP3, produced by phospholipase C downstream of surface receptors, binds together with cytosolic calcium; the three paralogs form homo- and heterotetramers with overlapping tissue distributions. Ryanodine receptors (RYR1, RYR2, RYR3) are activated by cytosolic calcium itself (calcium-induced calcium release). In skeletal muscle RYR1 in the junctional SR is opened by direct conformational coupling to the voltage sensor of the T-tubule L-type channel CACNA1S (Cav1.1), so depolarisation releases SR calcium without needing calcium entry; in cardiac muscle calcium entering through the L-type channel triggers RYR2 by calcium-induced calcium release; RYR3 is a low-sensitivity release channel expressed in brain and other tissues. Released calcium is pumped back into the store by the sarco/endoplasmic reticulum calcium ATPases (SERCA1-3, ATP2A1-3), which move two calcium ions into the lumen per ATP hydrolysed. In muscle the small transmembrane regulins phospholamban (PLN, mainly heart) and sarcolipin (SLN, mainly skeletal and atrial muscle) bind SERCA and lower its calcium affinity, and the luminal protein calsequestrin-2 buffers SR calcium near RYR2 and regulates the release complex. Release and re-uptake together set ER/SR calcium content and shape cytosolic calcium signals, contraction and relaxation.
