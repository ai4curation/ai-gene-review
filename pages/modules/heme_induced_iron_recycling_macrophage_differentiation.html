Heme-induced iron-recycling macrophage differentiation

Macrophages that engulf aged or damaged red blood cells digest them in the phagolysosome and release heme. The heme transporter HRG1 carries heme out of the phagolysosome into the cytosol. Heme then switches on a differentiation programme. The transcriptional repressor BACH1 normally keeps the gene for the transcription factor SPI-C silent. Heme binds BACH1 through its cysteine-proline motifs and triggers its proteasomal degradation, which de-represses Spic. SPI-C drives monocytes to become iron-recycling macrophages: splenic red pulp macrophages and VCAM1-positive bone marrow macrophages. The same heme signal also de-represses the iron-recycling enzymes and transporters these cells use, heme oxygenase 1 and ferroportin.

Boundary: this module starts with heme in the phagolysosome of a macrophage that has engulfed an erythrocyte, and ends with a differentiated iron-recycling macrophage that exports iron. Engulfment of the erythrocyte and its digestion belong to the phagocytosis modules. The programme is defined by heme, not by phagocytosis. Heme released by haemolysis induces Spic in the same way. Engulfed erythrocytes are the main physiological source of heme for these cells. GO has no term for red pulp macrophage differentiation. The module concept is GO:0030225 macrophage differentiation, and "splenic red pulp macrophage differentiation" (is_a GO:0030225, cell type CL:0000874) has been proposed to the GO editors in go-ontology#32748. The two arms are supported by separate papers in different cells. HRG1 work is in bone-marrow-derived macrophages and spleen and liver macrophages without red pulp markers; the BACH1/SPIC work is in bone-marrow-derived macrophages, monocytes and chimeric mice. The link between them, phagolysosomal heme reaching BACH1, is inferred. Taxon scope: the programme is shown only in mouse. An OpenScientist phylogenetic profile (file:human/SPIC/SPIC-hypotheses/spic-bach1-cooccurrence/openscientist.md) found SPIC orthologs from cartilaginous fish through all bony vertebrates but not in cyclostomes or invertebrates, and found the BACH1 C-terminal cysteine-proline motif conserved in every curated bony-vertebrate BACH1 checked. No lineage has SPIC without BACH1. The parts of the switch therefore co-occur across jawed vertebrates, but whether the switch operates outside mammals is untested. HRG1 is conserved more widely across animals (C. elegans hrg-1 imports heme), so the heme-export step is broader than the differentiation programme. A second profile (file:human/SLC48A1/SLC48A1-hypotheses/hrg1-protist-distribution/openscientist.md) found the HRG1 family almost only in animals and choanoflagellates, with one ortholog in Acanthamoeba and none in Dictyostelium.

Part 1: heme export from the phagolysosome
HRG1 exports heme from the phagolysosomeTransport Stepphagolysosomal_heme_export

Haemoglobin is digested in the phagolysosome and its heme is released there. Heme oxygenase faces the cytosol, so heme must cross the phagolysosomal membrane. HRG1 is recruited to that membrane during erythrophagocytosis and moves heme into the cytosol.

Heme also de-represses, through BACH1, the genes for heme oxygenase 1 and ferroportin. HMOX1 breaks heme down to release iron, and ferroportin exports the iron. These are the work the differentiated macrophage does, and the same derepression step controls them.
