Bacterial trans-4-hydroxy-L-proline catabolism to 2-oxoglutarate

A reusable bacterial four-reaction route that converts trans-4-hydroxy-L-proline, the major imino acid of host and plant collagen and cell-wall protein, to 2-oxoglutarate. A PLP-independent epimerase first forms cis-4-hydroxy-D-proline. An FAD-dependent oxidoreductase generates 4-hydroxy-1-pyrroline-2-carboxylate, a deaminase converts that cyclic imine to 2,5-dioxopentanoate and ammonium, and an aldehyde dehydrogenase-family enzyme completes oxidation to 2-oxoglutarate. The route is distributed across proteobacteria, firmicutes and actinobacteria, and is unrelated to the mammalian hydroxyproline pathway, which proceeds through hydroxyproline dehydrogenase and the HOGA1 aldolase to glyoxylate and pyruvate.

The boundary starts with the stereochemical entry reaction and ends at 2-oxoglutarate. Hydroxyproline transport (the LhpPMNO-type ABC systems), LhpR-mediated induction, the separate trans-3-hydroxy-L-proline branch, and downstream use of 2-oxoglutarate are outside the module. No generic cellular location is asserted: the enzymes are soluble bacterial proteins, but only PP_1255 has a locus-level cytoplasmic call, and that is an electronic inference. Species scope. The module is written species-neutrally. KT2440 supplies the only organism with all four steps grounded, and is one exemplar rather than the subject. P. aeruginosa PAO1 grounds the epimerase step and the second oxidase architecture; A. baylyi ADP1 grounds the terminal reaction, which it performs in the D-glucarate pathway that converges on the same 2,5-dioxopentanoate intermediate. Enzyme-family grounding. Both PANTHER families whose members carry these steps are functionally mixed, so the module grounds those nodes on exemplars and InterPro signatures instead of PANTHER terms. PTHR33442 mixes 4-hydroxyproline epimerases with trans-3-hydroxy-L-proline dehydratases in both of its subfamilies, and the epimerase activity arose more than once in that superfamily, so IPR008794 (the fold) is asserted and the activity is established per protein. PTHR13847:SF289 mixes the KT2440 oxidase with ThiO glycine oxidases and HcnC. PTHR43353:SF3 does contain both grounded terminal dehydrogenases, but it also holds aldehyde dehydrogenases with other substrates, so the narrower IPR044151 (ALDH_KGSADH) is used. Only the deaminase step carries a PANTHER subfamily term, PTHR12128:SF72, verified against panther.obo and the member index; that assertion comes from open maintainer PR #5076 and is retained here. All four families now have FamilyReview documents recording this divergence. The oxidase step is a variant set rather than a single enzyme, because the two characterized enzymes are of independent origin: a single-chain FAD protein in P. putida and a multi-subunit FAD/FMN/[2Fe-2S] enzyme in P. aeruginosa. The subunit accessions of the P. aeruginosa enzyme are not asserted: the cached abstract names the operon as lhpABFE without mapping subunits to locus tags, and the full text was not available, so that variant is deliberately left ungrounded rather than filled with a guess. This produces a leaf-grounding warning, which is a real curation gap and not a modelling choice. The oxidase step has no exact GO molecular-function term, so GO:0016645 is used as the closest donor-class parent while the substrate-specific activity stays explicit in the descriptor and in the proposed term in the PP_1255 review. One validator warning is expected and accepted. The function-conformance check reports FAMILY_FUNCTION_UNREVIEWED for the deaminase, because the PTHR12128 review leaves the subfamily-level scope of GO:0047425 UNRESOLVED: the activity is established for PP_1257 itself, but SF72 has no other assayed member, so claiming it subfamily-wide would overstate the evidence. The warning therefore records a true gap in the family evidence, not a defect in this module. Page QC additionally lists the multi-subunit oxidase variant as a leaf without representative members, for the reason given above. The NCBITaxon label for the module's taxonomic context is reported as unvalidated wherever OLS cannot be reached, as it is for every module that asserts a taxon.
