HSF-driven cytosolic heat shock response

The heat shock response couples proteotoxic stress to transcriptional induction of the cytosolic chaperone network. Its driver is heat shock factor (HSF), a winged helix-turn-helix transcription factor found across eukaryotes. HSF binds heat shock elements (HSEs), inverted nGAAn pentamer repeats, as a homotrimer and activates RNA polymerase II transcription of chaperone genes. How HSF is held in check differs between lineages. In metazoans, exemplified by human HSF1, HSF is kept as an inert monomer in complexes with HSP90 and HSP70. Proteotoxic stress titrates these chaperones away, and HSF1 then trimerizes, binds HSEs and concentrates in nuclear stress granules. The induced chaperones feed back to switch HSF1 off. In budding yeast, Hsf1 is constitutively trimeric and nuclear. Its essential job is to maintain basal expression of the Hsp70 and Hsp90 genes, and stress raises its activity further. The chaperone output falls into three activities. Hsp70 family foldases refold non-native clients by ATP-driven cycles. J-domain co-chaperones deliver clients and stimulate the Hsp70 ATPase, and HSP110- and BAG-family nucleotide-exchange factors reset the cycle. HSP90 is an ATP-dependent chaperone for metastable signaling and structural clients and is also the main repressor of metazoan HSF1. Small heat shock proteins are ATP-independent holdases that keep heat-denatured proteins soluble and refolding-competent for the ATP-dependent chaperones. Nematodes add an HSF-1/DAF-16-co-regulated branch of atypical 12-kDa small HSPs that contributes to longevity rather than to the acute response. In C. elegans the capacity of the response also falls sharply at the onset of reproduction, when repressive chromatin at stress loci blocks HSF-1 binding.

History. This module was first curated as a C. elegans-only module (MODULE:c_elegans_heat_shock_response) and was generalized to the eukaryotic HSF-driven response. The worm evidence is kept; human and yeast exemplars were added from their gene reviews. C. elegans members remain among the representative members of every role. Boundary. The module covers HSF activation and HSE binding (part 1) and the cytosolic chaperone output it induces (parts 2 to 4), with an optional nematode-specific atypical sHSP branch (part 5). The context taxon is Eukaryota because HSF, Hsp70, HSP90 and the BAG and HSP110 exchange factors have PAINT nodes at or above Eukaryota (see ancestral_nodes). The representative members are opisthokont only (human, yeast, worm). The small HSP holdase node (PTN000897708) is placed at Eumetazoa, so yeast Hsp26/Hsp42 are not used as representatives. Excluded: heat-shock-independent HSF programs, such as the worm developmental program with EFL-1/DPL-1 (PMID:27688402), linker cell death through the ubiquitin-proteasome system (PMID:26952214) and autophagy induction after hormetic heat (PMID:28198373). HSF1 roles in mitosis, DNA repair and cancer are also excluded, as are the ER unfolded protein response and the mitochondrial UPR. HSF also induces the chaperonin CCT/TRiC, which is modeled separately in MODULE:cct_chaperonin_folding and is not repeated here. Activation mode as a variant set. Part 1 uses a variant set with the axis taxon / HSF activation mode. The metazoan variant is a latent monomer that trimerizes under stress and is repressed by HSP90 and HSP70 (human HSF1). Worm HSF-1 is placed in this variant by family membership and by its restraint at an early activation step by DDL-1/DDL-2. It differs from human HSF1 in two ways: it is constitutively nuclear (PMID:23107491), and a stress-triggered monomer-to-trimer switch has not been shown directly in the worm. The yeast variant is constitutively trimeric and nuclear, and its essential output is basal Hsp70/Hsp90 expression. The family-level MF is GO:0001228, as in the human and yeast reviews. The worm hsf-1 review asserts its parent GO:0003700. HSP90 feedback on HSF. HSP90 represses human HSF1 (PMID:9727490). This is modeled as a NEGATIVELY_REGULATES connection from the HSP90 part to HSF activation, with metazoan context. The human HSF1 review also lists HSP70/DNAJB1 complexes as repressors, and yeast Hsf1 is under Hsp70/Hsp90 feedback (yeast HSF1 review). These are recorded in prose only, because this module cites no verbatim quote for the HSP70 arm. No cached worm study tests whether HSP-90 binds or represses HSF-1, so the connection is not asserted for nematodes (see knowledge_gaps). Regulators kept as context. Worm DDL-1/DDL-2 hold HSF-1 inactive under insulin/IGF-1-like signaling (PMID:22265419). PQM-1 contributes to heat induction of worm hsp-90 (PMID:29949773). JMJD-3.1 and H3K27me3 set the reproductive decline of the worm response (PMID:26212459). None of these has a gene review, so they are not modeled as members. HSF1 post-translational modifications (phosphorylation, SUMOylation, acetylation) are likewise left to the HSF1 review. Hsp70 cofactors. The J-domain role is grounded by human DNAJB1 only. No worm Hsp40 (DNJ) gene has a review, and no cached paper names the HSF-1-induced worm Hsp40 partner. DNJ-13 (PMID:25053410) and DNJ-19 (PMID:19165329) are mentioned in text only. Human HSPH1 (HSP110) has no review and is not used, so worm HSP-110 is the only HSP110 representative. The HSPA1A review gives its core MF as the parent GO:0044183, while its description calls HSPA1A an ATP-dependent foldase. The family-level MF here is GO:0140662, as in the worm hsp-1, hsp-70 and F44E5.4 reviews. Small HSP holdases. GO has no current holdase term, because GO:0051082 was obsoleted. The worm hsp-16 reviews propose a holdase chaperone activity NTR, and the HSPB1 review uses the parent GO:0044183. The family-level function is therefore GO:0044183, and the holdase sense is described in prose. Atypical small HSPs. The 12-kDa sHSPs are a nematode-specific optional part that follows their gene reviews. HSP-12.3 forms tetramers and HSP-12.6 is monomeric; neither shows in vitro chaperone activity. Because hsp-12.6 is not stress-inducible, no heat-response process is asserted for it. Their link to HSF-1 runs through HSF-1/DAF-16 co-regulation of sHSP genes in reduced insulin signaling and longevity (PMID:12750521), not through acute heat induction. Relation to other modules. MODULE:bacterial_dnak_dnaj_refolding_cycle models the bacterial DnaK/DnaJ/GrpE Hsp70 cycle. Part 2 here is the eukaryotic cytosolic counterpart, with HSP110- and BAG-family NEFs in place of GrpE, inside a stress-induced transcriptional program, so the two modules do not duplicate each other. Part 1: stress-regulated heat shock factor binding HSEs and inducing chaperone genes
HSF activation and HSE-dependent transcriptionRegulatory Stephsf_activation

HSF binds HSEs (continuous inverted nGAAn pentamers) as a trimer and activates RNA polymerase II transcription of chaperone genes. The lineages differ in how HSF is kept inactive and how stress activates it (variant set). In metazoans, HSF1 is a latent monomer that trimerizes when stress titrates away its HSP90/HSP70 repressors. In budding yeast, Hsf1 is constitutively trimeric and nuclear and drives essential basal chaperone expression. Heat shock concentrates HSF in nuclear stress granules in both human and worm cells.

Variant set: HSF activation mode by taxon / HSF activation mode (Exactly One)

Metazoan latent-monomer HSF1 (human; worm HSF-1 by family and pathway context) versus constitutively trimeric, nuclear fungal Hsf1 (budding yeast). Both are HSF family members (PTHR10015) that descend from the eukaryotic PAINT node PTN007416549.

Metazoan latent-monomer HSF1, repressed by HSP90/HSP70Regulatory Stepmetazoan_latent_monomer_hsf

In unstressed cells, HSF1 is an inert monomer, held by intramolecular leucine-zipper contacts and by HSP90- and HSP70-containing complexes. Proteotoxic stress releases it. HSF1 then homotrimerizes, gains HSE binding, accumulates in nuclear stress granules and activates HSP70, HSP90 and small HSP genes. The induced chaperones attenuate it again. Worm HSF-1 belongs here by family and by an early activation step that DDL-1/DDL-2 restrain under insulin/IGF-1-like signaling. Unlike human HSF1, worm HSF-1 is constitutively nuclear, and its trimerization switch is inferred rather than shown.

Part 2: heat-inducible ATP-dependent HSP90 chaperone and metazoan HSF1 repressor
Cytosolic HSP90 ATP-dependent chaperoneMolecular Functionhsp90_chaperone

Cytosolic HSP90 is an ATP-dependent clamp chaperone. Co-chaperones regulate its cycle, which completes the maturation and stabilization of metastable clients such as kinases, steroid receptors and transcription factors. HSF induces it through HSEs: worm HSP-90 (historical mutant name DAF-21), the only cytosolic HSP90 in C. elegans, is induced through distal HSEs bound by HSF-1, with a PQM-1 contribution. In human cells, HSP90 is also the main repressor of HSF1, which closes the negative feedback loop of the response.

Part 2: ATP-independent small HSP holdase output
Small HSP holdasesMolecular Functionshsp_holdase

Canonical small heat shock proteins are alpha-crystallin-domain proteins that form dynamic oligomers. Without ATP, they bind heat-denatured proteins and keep them soluble and refolding-competent; the ATP-dependent chaperones later refold these clients. They do not refold clients themselves. The four worm hsp-16 genes (hsp-16.1, hsp-16.2, hsp-16.41, hsp-16.48) are arranged as divergently transcribed pairs sharing intergenic HSEs, are silent without stress and strictly HSF-1-dependent for heat induction. Human HSPB1 (Hsp27) is the vertebrate exemplar. GO has no current holdase term (see notes).
