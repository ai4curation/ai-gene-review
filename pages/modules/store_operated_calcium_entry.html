Store-operated calcium entry — STIM1/STIM2 sensing of ER calcium depletion, STIM translocation to ER-plasma membrane junctions, gating of ORAI1/2/3 CRAC channels, with STIMATE support and SARAF-mediated slow calcium-dependent inactivation

Store-operated calcium entry (SOCE) is the calcium influx across the plasma membrane that is triggered by depletion of calcium from the endoplasmic reticulum (ER). In resting cells the single-pass ER membrane proteins STIM1 and STIM2 bind calcium through a luminal EF-hand-SAM region. When ER calcium falls, typically after IP3 receptor-mediated release, calcium dissociates, the STIM luminal domains unfold and oligomerize, and the cytosolic STIM-ORAI activating region (SOAR/CAD) is exposed. The STIM oligomers accumulate as puncta in the cortical ER at ER-plasma membrane junctions, where they bind and open ORAI channels, the hexameric pore-forming subunits of the highly calcium-selective calcium release-activated calcium (CRAC) channel. Humans have three ORAI paralogs that form homo- and heteromeric channels; ORAI1 dominates the classical CRAC current while ORAI2 and ORAI3 tune its calcium sensitivity and inactivation. STIM1 responds to substantial store depletion and STIM2 to small decreases, which lets STIM2 stabilize basal cytosolic and ER calcium. The ER multi-pass protein STIMATE (TMEM110) promotes the STIM1 conformational switch and maintains the junctions, and the ER protein SARAF associates with STIM to drive slow calcium-dependent inactivation of the entry, limiting store overfilling. The calcium entering through ORAI channels refills the ER through SERCA pumps and drives calcium-dependent signalling such as calcineurin-NFAT activation in lymphocytes.

Part 1: sensing of ER calcium store depletion
STIM1 and STIM2 luminal EF-hand sensing of ER calciumModulesoce_store_depletion_sensing

The luminal EF-hand-SAM region of STIM1 and STIM2 binds calcium in the ER lumen. Loss of bound calcium on store depletion is the trigger of the pathway. STIM2 has a lower calcium affinity and responds to smaller decreases in ER calcium than STIM1, so the two sensors cover basal and receptor-evoked depletion.

Variant set: STIM ER calcium sensors by paralog and sensitivity to store depletion [Realizer] (One Or More)

Both sensors are widely co-expressed and can co-cluster. STIM1 drives receptor-evoked entry; STIM2 activates on small decreases of ER calcium and sets basal cytosolic and ER calcium.

Part 5: slow calcium-dependent inactivation of store-operated entry
SARAF-mediated slow calcium-dependent inactivationRegulatory Stepsoce_saraf_inactivation

After entry has refilled the store, the ER membrane protein SARAF associates with STIM and promotes slow calcium-dependent inactivation of the CRAC current, which prevents store overfilling. Fast calcium-dependent inactivation, which depends on calcium binding near the ORAI pore and on calmodulin, is a channel property not modelled as a separate step. SARAF is not on RGD's PW:0001139 list and is added on direct human evidence.
