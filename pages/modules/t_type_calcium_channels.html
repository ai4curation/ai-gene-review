T-type (low-voltage-activated) calcium channels — CaV3.1 (CACNA1G), CaV3.2 (CACNA1H) and CaV3.3 (CACNA1I) pore-forming alpha1 subunits mediating calcium entry across the plasma membrane near the resting potential (human)

T-type calcium channels are the low-voltage-activated (LVA) members of the voltage-gated calcium channel family. They open with small depolarisations from near the resting membrane potential, inactivate rapidly and deactivate slowly, and have a small single- channel conductance, so their currents are transient and tiny. Each channel is a single alpha1 protein of four homologous repeats that carries both the voltage sensors and the calcium-selective pore; unlike the high-voltage-activated CaV1 and CaV2 channels, the CaV3 channels form functional channels without obligatory beta or alpha2delta subunits. Humans have three paralogues: CaV3.1 (CACNA1G), highly expressed in brain, notably thalamus and cerebellum, and in cardiac nodal tissue; CaV3.2 (CACNA1H), in brain, heart, adrenal gland, kidney and sensory neurons; and CaV3.3 (CACNA1I), mainly in brain, with slower kinetics. Calcium entering through T-type channels depolarises cells toward threshold and supports pacemaking, low-threshold calcium spikes and rhythmic or burst firing, and it also feeds calcium signalling in secretory and smooth muscle cells. Overlap of their activation and inactivation ranges gives a steady window current near rest.
