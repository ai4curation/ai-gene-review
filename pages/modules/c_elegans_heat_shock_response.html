HSF-1 cytosolic heat shock response in Caenorhabditis elegans

In Caenorhabditis elegans the single heat shock factor HSF-1 couples proteotoxic stress to induction of the cytosolic chaperone network. HSF-1 is a nuclear, sequence-specific DNA-binding transcription factor that recognizes heat shock elements (HSEs) made of continuous inverted nGAAn pentamer repeats. On heat shock it is redistributed into transcriptionally active nuclear stress granules and shifts its genomic occupancy to inducible HSE sites. HSF-1 is required for heat-induced transcription of the hsp-16 small heat shock protein genes, which are silent without stress, and it drives heat induction of the abundant Hsc70 HSP-1 and of the single cytosolic HSP90, HSP-90, through distal tandem HSEs. The chaperone output falls into three activities. HSP-1 is an ATP-dependent Hsp70 foldase whose nucleotide cycle is tuned by nucleotide-exchange factors, the HSP110-family HSP-110 and the BAG-domain protein BAG-1. HSP-90 is an ATP-dependent chaperone for signaling and structural client proteins. HSP-16.2 is an ATP-independent alpha-crystallin-domain holdase that keeps heat-denatured proteins soluble. Insulin/IGF-1-like signaling restrains an early step of HSF-1 activation through the DDL-1/DDL-2 complex. Reduced insulin signaling lets HSF-1 act together with DAF-16/FOXO on small heat shock protein genes, including the atypical 12-kDa sHSPs HSP-12.3 and HSP-12.6, which lack chaperone activity in vitro. The capacity of the response falls sharply at the onset of reproduction, when H3K27 trimethylation at stress loci blocks HSF-1 binding.

Boundary. This is a concrete C. elegans module for the HSF-1-driven cytosolic heat shock response: HSF-1 activation and HSE binding (part 1) and the cytosolic chaperone output it induces (parts 2 to 5). HSF-1's heat-shock-independent developmental program with EFL-1/DPL-1 (larval development, PMID:27688402), its role in linker cell death through the ubiquitin-proteasome system (PMID:26952214), autophagy induction after hormetic heat (PMID:28198373) and pathogen resistance are outside the boundary; the hsf-1 review keeps these as non-core. The ER unfolded protein response (HSP-4/BiP) and the mitochondrial UPR belong to the CAEEL_UPR_STRESS project, not here. Chaperonin (CCT/TRiC) induction is a known HSF-1 output but no CCT subunit has a worm review yet, so it is not modeled. Relation to other modules. MODULE:bacterial_dnak_dnaj_refolding_cycle models the bacterial DnaK/DnaJ/GrpE Hsp70 cycle. Part 2 here is the eukaryotic cytosolic counterpart (Hsc70 HSP-1 with HSP110 and BAG-family nucleotide-exchange factors instead of GrpE), within a stress-induced transcriptional program, so the two are not duplicates. No eukaryotic Hsp70/Hsp110 or Hsp90 cycle module exists yet. Regulators kept as context. DDL-1/DDL-2 form the DHIC complex that holds HSF-1 inactive under insulin/IGF-1-like signaling (PMID:22265419); PQM-1 contributes to heat induction of hsp-90 and to transcellular chaperone signaling (PMID:29949773); JMJD-3.1 and H3K27me3 set the reproductive decline of the response (PMID:26212459). None of these has a worm review, so they are recorded here rather than modeled as members. HSP-90-mediated negative feedback on HSF-1, a well-known feature of the response in yeast and mammals, has no cached worm evidence and is not modeled (see knowledge_gaps). Members deliberately excluded. Hsp40/J-domain co-chaperones are part of the worm Hsc70 cofactor network (DNJ-13 antagonizes UNC-23 in muscle, PMID:25053410; DNJ-19 limits SOD1 aggregation, PMID:19165329), but no worm DNJ gene has a review, and no cached paper assigns a specific DNJ protein as the HSF-1-induced Hsp40 partner. They are therefore named in text only. The strictly heat-inducible HSP70s (hsp-70/C12C8.1, F44E5.4/5) and the other hsp-16 genes (hsp-16.1, hsp-16.41, hsp-16.48) are direct HSF-1 outputs but have no worm review; HSP-1 and HSP-16.2 stand in for their families. Atypical small HSPs. The 12-kDa sHSPs are modeled as a separate output part, following their gene reviews. HSP-12.3 forms tetramers and HSP-12.6 is monomeric; neither shows in vitro chaperone activity. The hsp-12.6 review marks "response to heat" as an over-annotation because hsp-12.6 is not stress-inducible, so no heat-response process is asserted for it. The link of these genes to HSF-1 is through HSF-1/DAF-16 co-regulation of sHSP genes in reduced insulin signaling and longevity (PMID:12750521), not through acute heat induction. Their part is therefore not required for the acute response.

Part 1: stress-activated heat shock factor binding HSEs and inducing chaperone genes
HSF-1 activation and HSE-dependent transcriptionRegulatory Stephsf1_activation

HSF-1 is constitutively nuclear. Heat shock causes its rapid, reversible redistribution into nuclear stress granules that colocalize with markers of active transcription; granule formation needs HSF-1 DNA binding. HSF-1 binds continuous inverted nGAAn pentamer HSEs (worm HSF-1, unlike other HSFs tested, does not bind discontinuous nGAAn units) and on heat shock occupies inducible distal HSEs at chaperone genes. HSF-1 self-associates into dimers and trimers (hsf-1 review); trimerization as the activation switch is inferred from HSF family biology rather than shown directly in the worm. Insulin/IGF-1-like signaling inhibits an early activation step through DDL-1/DDL-2, and repressive chromatin at stress loci blocks HSF-1 binding from early adulthood.

Part 2: ATP-dependent Hsp70 refolding of heat-denatured clients with nucleotide-exchange factors
HSP-1 Hsc70 folding cycle with HSP-110 and BAG-1Biological Processhsp70_folding_cycle

HSP-1 is the abundant cytosolic Hsc70. Its mRNA is high without stress and rises two- to six-fold on heat shock, driven by distal HSEs that HSF-1 occupies during heat shock. HSP-1 binds non-native clients and refolds them through ATP hydrolysis-coupled cycles. Nucleotide exchange resets the cycle: HSP-110 (HSP110/HSPH family) and BAG-1 (BAG domain) both act as nucleotide-exchange factors for HSP-1, and BAG-1 stimulates the Hsc70 ATPase more strongly than its paralog UNC-23. J-domain (Hsp40) partners that target clients to HSP-1 are part of this cycle but are not modeled (see notes).

HSP-90 ATP-dependent chaperoneMolecular Functionhsp90_chaperone

HSP-90 (historical mutant name DAF-21) is the only cytosolic HSP90 in C. elegans. It is heat-inducible through distal HSEs bound by HSF-1, with an additional PQM-1 contribution, and it maintains signaling and structural clients; loss of HSP-90 causes MYO-3 myosin aggregation in muscle.

Part 2: ATP-independent small HSP holdase output
HSP-16.2 small HSP holdaseMolecular Functionshsp_holdase

The four hsp-16 genes are arranged as divergently transcribed pairs sharing intergenic HSEs. They are silent without stress and strictly heat-inducible, and their induction is HSF-1-dependent. HSP-16.2 is an alpha-crystallin-domain holdase that binds heat-denatured proteins without ATP and keeps them soluble and refolding-competent for Hsp70-dependent refolding. GO has no current term for holdase activity (GO:0051082 was obsoleted), so the function is given by name only.

Part 3: HSF-1/DAF-16-co-regulated atypical 12-kDa sHSPs (longevity branch, not required for the acute response)
Atypical 12-kDa small HSPs HSP-12.3 and HSP-12.6Biological Processatypical_shsp_output

The four 12-kDa sHSPs of C. elegans are essentially bare alpha-crystallin domains with very short N-terminal regions and no C-terminal tail. HSP-12.3 forms tetramers (with HSP-12.2) and HSP-12.6 is monomeric; neither has in vitro chaperone activity, and hsp-12.6 is not stress-inducible. Their connection to HSF-1 is via HSF-1 and DAF-16 jointly activating sHSP genes when insulin/IGF-1 signaling is reduced, a branch that contributes to longevity and delays polyglutamine aggregation. No holdase or heat-response function is asserted for HSP-12.6.
