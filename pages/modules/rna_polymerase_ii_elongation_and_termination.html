Human RNA polymerase II elongation and termination — DSIF/NELF promoter-proximal pausing, P-TEFb and super elongation complex pause release, PAF1C, SPT6, FACT and TFIIS in productive elongation, and XRN2 torpedo termination

After initiation, human RNA polymerase II (Pol II) transcribes some 20 to 60 nucleotides and then pauses in the promoter-proximal region. The pause is stabilised by DSIF (SUPT4H1 and SUPT5H) and by the four-subunit negative elongation factor NELF (NELFA, NELFB, NELFCD, NELFE), which binds the polymerase funnel, restrains the mobile modules needed for pause release and excludes TFIIS. Release into productive elongation requires the kinase P-TEFb, CDK9 paired with cyclin T1 or cyclin T2, which phosphorylates the Pol II C-terminal domain and its linker, NELF and SUPT5H; NELF then leaves and DSIF becomes a positive elongation factor travelling with the polymerase. P-TEFb is delivered to many genes within the super elongation complex (SEC), in which an AF4/FMR2 scaffold (AFF4 or AFF1) binds P-TEFb, an ELL elongation factor with its EAF partner, and the YEATS protein ENL or AF9. In the activated elongation complex the PAF1 complex (PAF1, CTR9, LEO1, CDC73, RTF1, SKIC8) displaces NELF from the polymerase funnel, SPT6 binds the phosphorylated CTD linker, and the histone chaperone FACT (SUPT16H and SSRP1) removes and redeposits an H2A-H2B dimer so that Pol II can pass through nucleosomes. TFIIS (TCEA1) rescues backtracked polymerases by stimulating cleavage of the extruded transcript in the Pol II active site. At the end of protein-coding genes, cleavage of the nascent RNA at the poly(A) site by CPSF73 leaves an uncapped 5' end on the downstream RNA that is still attached to the polymerase; the nuclear 5'-3' exoribonuclease XRN2 degrades it co-transcriptionally and, on catching the polymerase, promotes its release from the template (the torpedo model).
