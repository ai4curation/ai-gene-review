Part 2: mavs k63-polyubiquitination amplification (optional)
PMID:42704283
ATG13 promotes K63-linked polyubiquitination of MAVS via ASB1 to restrict influenza A virus replication
Lys348 of MAVS is the acceptor residue whose mutation abolishes both the ATG13-driven ubiquitination and the ATG13-driven increase in interferon output.
indicating that K348 is the critical ubiquitin modification site for the regulation of MAVS function by ATG13

Added in response to issue #3922. The step is marked optional because it tunes an already-assembled MAVS signalosome rather than gating it: RIG-I signalling proceeds in its absence, with reduced interferon output. It rests on a single 2026 study from one group, all of it in human cell lines, and none of it is yet in GOA for either ATG13 or ASB1; it should be revisited when independent replication appears. The same paper reports an ATG13-TRAF3 interaction that was not followed up mechanistically, so TRAF3 is deliberately left out of this step.

ATG13-ASB1 amplification of MAVS K63-polyubiquitinationRegulatory Stepmavs_k63_polyubiquitination_amplification

A non-autophagic amplification step acting on assembled MAVS rather than a step the signal has to pass through. ATG13 binds the MAVS 180-360 region through its N-terminal HORMA domain and recruits ASB1, the substrate-recognition subunit of the Cullin5-RING ligase complex (CRL5); CRL5-ASB1 then builds a K63-linked polyubiquitin chain on MAVS Lys348, increasing MAVS oligomerization and IFN-beta/ISRE output. ATG13 carries no ubiquitin-ligase activity of its own, so it acts here as the substrate-presenting adaptor and ASB1 as the ligase substrate receptor. Reporter epistasis places both proteins upstream of TBK1, which is why the step is modelled on MAVS signalosome assembly and not as a fourth stage of the cascade.
