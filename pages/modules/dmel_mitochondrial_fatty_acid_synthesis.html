Mitochondrial type II fatty acid synthesis in Drosophila melanogaster

Mitochondria carry their own type II fatty acid synthesis (mtFAS) system, in which separate enzymes act on acyl chains attached to the mitochondrial acyl carrier protein (ND-ACP). Malonyl groups are loaded onto ACP by the malonyl transacylase (beg), condensed by a beta-ketoacyl-ACP synthase (CG12170), reduced by a 3-ketoacyl-ACP reductase, dehydrated and then reduced by the enoyl-ACP reductase Mecr. The main product, octanoyl-ACP, is the precursor of lipoic acid; acylated ACP also binds and supports assembly of respiratory complex I and the iron-sulfur cluster assembly machinery.

Boundary and caveats. The 3-hydroxyacyl-ACP dehydratase step (step 4; HTD2 in mammals) is not in the FlyBase group and is not modelled, so the connection from ketoacyl reduction to enoyl reduction spans it. Acc is a FlyBase member but is best known as the cytosolic acetyl-CoA carboxylase; whether a fly Acc isoform supplies malonyl-CoA inside mitochondria is not established (mammals use ACSF3 for intramitochondrial malonyl-CoA), so it is modelled as the malonyl-CoA supply step without a location claim. CG3603 is assigned to the ketoreductase step by its membership in the FlyBase group; in mammals this step is performed by an HSD17B8-CBR4 heterotetramer and the fly assignment is only by sequence similarity, so no molecular function is given. ND-ACP is also a subunit of respiratory complex I and part of the iron-sulfur cluster assembly complex (module dmel_iron_sulfur_cluster_assembly). Other molecular functions follow orthology and have not been assayed in flies. FlyBase groups realized by this module: MTFABIO (FBgg0002247).
