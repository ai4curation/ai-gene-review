Glutathione-dependent formaldehyde detoxification

A detoxification module found in bacteria and eukaryotes in which formaldehyde is captured by glutathione as S-(hydroxymethyl)glutathione, the hemithioacetal is oxidized to S-formylglutathione by a class-III alcohol dehydrogenase, and the thioester is hydrolyzed to formate while regenerating glutathione. Chemical capture, oxidation, and hydrolysis are represented as separate steps; formate can then be oxidized to CO2 by an NAD-dependent formate dehydrogenase.

The boundary begins with glutathione capture of formaldehyde and ends at formate plus regenerated glutathione. Formate oxidation to carbon dioxide, formaldehyde efflux, DNA repair, protein-quality control, and nitrosothiol reduction are distinct downstream or parallel functions. Organisms can carry additional formaldehyde oxidation routes, so this module represents the glutathione-dependent branch rather than the entire cellular response. In KT2440, PP_3970 has genetic evidence for a contribution to formaldehyde mineralization, but that does not place it in the defined FrmA-FrmC reaction sequence. The literature uses conflicting names and glutathione-dependence assignments for PP_3970, while UniProtKB:Q88FV8 currently assigns only a generic zinc-dependent, NAD(P)-binding oxidoreductase function. Its precise chemistry therefore remains unresolved. The exact contribution of PP_1616/FrmA and PP_1617/FrmC to this branch remains based on homology and should be tested directly. ALIGN (YeastPathways project, 2026-10): the bacterial module was widened to bacteria and eukaryotes. S. cerevisiae SFA1 (P32771) shares PANTHER PTHR43880:SF12 with E. coli FrmA, and YJL068C (P40363) and human ESD share PTHR10061 with FrmB/YeiG. GENERALIZATION (2026-11): the pathway is conserved across bacteria and eukaryotes with the same three-step architecture, so the module is species-neutral and P. putida KT2440 FrmA/FrmC are kept as one homology-based exemplar alongside experimentally characterized E. coli FrmA (P25437), FrmB (P51025) and YeiG (P33018), yeast SFA1 and YJL068C, and human ADH5 (P11766) and ESD (P10768). PANTHER places class III ADH in four subfamilies of PTHR43880 (SF12 bacteria and fungi, SF4 mammals, SF21 fish, SF58 plants), so the dehydrogenase selector lists all four rather than SF12 alone, which had excluded animals and plants; the ethanol-active subfamilies of PTHR43880 are not class III and are excluded. No plant or fish representative is listed because none has a gene review here; the PTHR43880 family review records the plant member. The hydrolase step uses the whole PTHR10061 family; members fall in SF0 (E. coli FrmB, yeast YJL068C, human ESD) and SF1 (E. coli YeiG, KT2440 FrmC), both of which keep S-formylglutathione hydrolase activity. Gfa, the Paracoccus glutathione-dependent formaldehyde-activating enzyme, was not added to step 1: its reported acceleration of S-(hydroxymethyl)glutathione formation was not reproduced by time-course assays (PMID:26675168), so the step stays without a gene participant and the question is recorded as a knowledge gap. SFA1 is also the yeast S-nitrosoglutathione reductase; that activity is outside this module. The YeastCyc formate dehydrogenase step (FDH1) was added as an optional part 4, because formate oxidation is not required for formaldehyde detoxification. FDH2 is a pseudogene split into YPL275W/YPL276W in S288C. GO-CAM disagreement: the SGD model attaches SFA1 only to the root molecular_function term in cytoplasm, while GO:0051903 is directly supported. No PomBase model is linked for this group. Part 1: Glutathione capture of formaldehyde
S-(hydroxymethyl)glutathione formationReactionformaldehyde_glutathione_capture

Formaldehyde reacts with glutathione to form the hemithioacetal S-(hydroxymethyl)glutathione, which feeds the subsequent dehydrogenase step. This capture is spontaneous and has no gene participant. A catalytic role for the Paracoccus Gfa protein was proposed but is not supported by later kinetic work.

Part 2: Hemithioacetal oxidation
S-(hydroxymethyl)glutathione oxidationReactionfrma_hemithioacetal_oxidation

A class-3 alcohol dehydrogenase oxidizes S-(hydroxymethyl)glutathione to S-formylglutathione. The GO term permits NAD(P)+, whereas characterized family members are NAD+-biased.
