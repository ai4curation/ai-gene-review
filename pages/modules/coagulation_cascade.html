Coagulation cascade — tissue-factor initiation, FXI/FIX amplification, prothrombinase, fibrin formation and cross-linking, with the plasma anticoagulant brakes

The coagulation cascade converts a local vascular injury into an insoluble, cross-linked fibrin clot through a chain of limited proteolytic activations of circulating zymogens and their non-enzymatic protein cofactors. Initiation is extrinsic: tissue factor (F3), an integral membrane protein exposed at the injury site, binds factor VII/VIIa (F7) and the resulting extrinsic tenase complex cleaves both factor X (F10) and factor IX (F9). The small amount of thrombin this generates feeds back to activate the cofactors factor V (F5) and factor VIII (F8) and the zymogen factor XI (F11), switching the system into its amplification mode: factor XIa activates factor IX, and factor IXa together with factor VIIIa forms the intrinsic tenase that sustains factor Xa production. Factor VIII circulates bound to von Willebrand factor (VWF), which carries it and protects it from clearance until thrombin releases it. The pathways converge on the prothrombinase complex, factor Xa with its cofactor factor Va on a procoagulant phospholipid surface, which raises the rate of prothrombin (F2) activation by several orders of magnitude. Thrombin then cleaves fibrinopeptides from fibrinogen (FGA, FGB, FGG); the fibrin monomers polymerise into a branched network by their own end-to-middle and lateral associations, and thrombin-activated factor XIII (the catalytic A subunit F13A1 with its carrier B subunit F13B) covalently cross-links the gamma chains, stabilising the clot and making it resistant to lysis. The same proteases are held in check by plasma anticoagulants: antithrombin (SERPINC1) traps thrombin, factor Xa and factor IXa; tissue factor pathway inhibitor (TFPI) shuts down the extrinsic tenase and factor Xa; the protein C pathway, in which thrombin bound to endothelial thrombomodulin (THBD) activates protein C (PROC) and activated protein C with its cofactor protein S (PROS1) destroys factors Va and VIIIa, removes the cofactors the cascade depends on; and protein Z-dependent protease inhibitor (SERPINA10) with protein Z (PROZ) inactivates factor Xa at the membrane surface. Most of the proteases and protein C, S and Z are vitamin K-dependent, their Gla domains anchoring them to the anionic phospholipid surface.

Four cofactor roles have no supporting molecular function in GOA and are asserted here as curator judgment, flagged rather than hidden. Tissue factor (F3), factor Va (F5) and factor VIIIa (F8) are given endopeptidase activator activity (GO:0061133): each is a non-enzymatic protein cofactor whose only job is to raise the catalytic efficiency of a partner serine protease by orders of magnitude (PMID:7350159, PMID:6782101), and F3 already carries contributes_to serine-type endopeptidase activity (GO:0004252, IDA PMID:24998411) for the same complex. Protein S (PROS1) is given peptidase activator activity (GO:0016504) because it accelerates APC-catalysed factor Va inactivation (PMID:6892911); its only GOA molecular function is protein binding, which this project does not use. Protein Z (PROZ) is left without a molecular function: it is the membrane cofactor in the factor Xa-PZ-ZPI complex (PMID:9689066) and no GO term describes that role, while its IBA GO:0004252 is implausible for a protein with a degenerate catalytic triad; this is recorded as a doubt rather than corrected here. Factor XIII B subunit (F13B) is likewise functionless, carrying only the fibrin-clot-formation process it has by IDA, because its role is to carry and stabilise the A subunit. Thrombomodulin keeps the GO:0038023 signaling receptor activity that its IDA annotation and all four cached protein C GO-CAMs use, although its role in this module is to redirect thrombin's specificity rather than to transduce a signal. Fibrinogen follows the substrate-versus-participant rule: it is thrombin's substrate, but the polymerisation and network formation are its own work, so FGA, FGB and FGG are modelled with structural molecule activity and the fibrin-clot-formation and protein-polymerization processes they carry by IDA, not with any protease term. Deliberately outside this module. Contact activation (F12, KLKB1, KNG1) belongs to modules/kallikrein_kinin_cascade.yaml; it is drawn on the RGD diagram and is retained here only as the upstream connection into FXI activation (F12a activating F11), which the revised model of coagulation shows is also, and in vivo mainly, performed by thrombin (PMID:1652157). Fibrinolysis (PLG, PLAT and partners) belongs to modules/fibrinolysis.yaml, which takes cross-linked fibrin from this module as its input. The vitamin K cycle (GGCX, VKORC1) is cofactor supply for the Gla domains of F2, F7, F9, F10, PROC, PROS1 and PROZ and is a separate module; platelet activation and the protease-activated receptors (F2R, F2RL1, F2RL2, F2RL3) are downstream signalling outputs, not steps of fibrin formation. SERPING1 (C1 inhibitor) is on the RGD gene list but its coagulation targets are FXIIa, FXIa and kallikrein — the contact system — so it is placed with modules/kallikrein_kinin_cascade.yaml; its action on FXIa partly overlaps this module and is noted there. APOH (beta-2-glycoprotein I) appears in RGD's wider gene list for this pathway but is an anionic-phospholipid-binding plasma protein best known as the antiphospholipid-antibody antigen, not a step in the cascade, and is excluded. Gene reviews exist only for VWF (and APOH); the remaining twenty members are unreviewed, which is a curation gap recorded in the project plan rather than a knowledge gap here.
