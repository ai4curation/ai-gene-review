ESCRT machinery in Drosophila melanogaster

The endosomal sorting complexes required for transport (ESCRTs) sort ubiquitylated membrane proteins into intralumenal vesicles of multivesicular bodies and drive membrane scission that buds away from the cytoplasm. ESCRT-0 (Hrs, Stam) binds ubiquitylated cargo on endosomes; ESCRT-I and ESCRT-II bind cargo and membrane and nucleate ESCRT-III, whose subunits polymerize into spirals that constrict the bud neck. The AAA ATPase Vps4, with its cofactor Vta1, disassembles ESCRT-III and recycles it. The same ESCRT-III and Vps4 machinery acts in cytokinetic abscission, nuclear envelope sealing and membrane repair. In Drosophila, loss of ESCRT function causes accumulation of signalling receptors such as Notch on endosomes and neoplastic overgrowth in epithelia.
