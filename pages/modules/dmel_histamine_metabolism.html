Histamine synthesis and recycling in Drosophila melanogaster

Histamine is the neurotransmitter of Drosophila photoreceptors and some central neurons. Histidine decarboxylase (Hdc) makes histamine from histidine, and LOVIT, a putative vesicular histamine transporter, maintains histamine in photoreceptor synaptic terminals. After release, histamine is taken up by surrounding epithelial glia, in part by the histamine-specific transporter HisT. In glia, Ebony conjugates histamine with beta-alanine to form carcinine (beta-alanyl-histamine), which returns to photoreceptors through the carcinine transporter CarT. Tan then hydrolyses carcinine, releasing histamine for reuse. This glia-photoreceptor recycling loop sustains visual transmission when de novo synthesis is limiting.
