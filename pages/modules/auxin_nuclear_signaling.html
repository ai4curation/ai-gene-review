Canonical nuclear auxin signaling in land plants

Canonical nuclear auxin signaling couples TIR1/AFB–Aux/IAA co-receptor assembly to regulated transcription by ARF proteins. Auxin enhances receptor–degron association, enabling SCF-dependent Aux/IAA turnover and reducing corepression of class-A ARFs. This circuit is represented by native Arabidopsis and Marchantia implementations within land plants; older algal ARF DNA-binding systems alone do not establish the complete circuit. Arabidopsis TIR1 also produces cAMP, and separation-of-function experiments show that substrate degradation alone does not guarantee transcriptional activation. The downstream cAMP effector remains unidentified, and MpTIR1 AC activity is untested. Marchantia class-B MpARF2 provides an additional active TOPLESS-recruiting repression and DNA-competition branch. Paralog combinations, expression domains and promoter/chromatin contexts determine output; neither all ARFs nor all Aux/IAAs share one regulatory behavior. Auxin synthesis/transport, rapid cytoplasmic or cell-surface pathways, noncanonical receptor routes and the downstream developmental programs are outside this boundary.
