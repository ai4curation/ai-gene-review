Nuclear auxin signaling (TIR1/AFB-Aux/IAA-TOPLESS-ARF module)

Nuclear auxin signaling converts intracellular auxin (indole-3-acetic acid, IAA) levels into changes in gene transcription through a short, derepression-based pathway. At low auxin, AUXIN RESPONSE FACTOR (ARF) transcription factors sit on auxin-response elements (AuxREs) in target promoters through their B3 DNA-binding domains, but activator ARFs (class A, e.g. ARF5/MONOPTEROS, ARF7/NPH4 and ARF19) are kept inactive by Aux/IAA repressors. Aux/IAAs bind the ARF C-terminal PB1 domain through their own PB1 domain and recruit the Groucho/Tup1-type co-repressor TOPLESS (TPL) and its TPL-related paralogs through an EAR (LxLxL) motif in domain I; TPL represses transcription by engaging the Mediator complex and histone deacetylases. Auxin is perceived by a co-receptor formed by an LRR F-box protein of the TIR1/AFB family and the domain II degron of an Aux/IAA: auxin binds the bottom of the TIR1/AFB pocket and acts as molecular glue that stabilizes Aux/IAA binding. Because the TIR1/AFB protein is the substrate-recognition subunit of an SCF (ASK1-CUL1-RBX1) E3 ligase, co-receptor assembly leads to Aux/IAA polyubiquitination and rapid proteasomal degradation, which removes the Aux/IAA-TPL repressor from ARFs and allows ARF-dependent activation of early auxin-responsive genes, including Aux/IAA genes themselves (negative feedback). Specific responses arise from combinations of co-expressed pairs, such as BODENLOS/IAA12 with MONOPTEROS/ARF5 in embryonic root initiation, and IAA14/IAA18/IAA19 with ARF7 and ARF19 in lateral root formation and tropic growth. Auxin biosynthesis, polar auxin transport (a separate module), non-transcriptional auxin responses and the downstream developmental programs are outside this module's boundary.
