Bacterial medium-chain-length PHA synthesis and intracellular mobilization

Intracellular storage-polyester synthesis and turnover begin with a supplied pool of medium-chain (R)-3-hydroxyacyl-CoA. Substrate-compatible PhaC enzymes polymerize hydroxyacyl units, and intracellular PhaZ enzymes mobilize the resulting storage polymer. PhaG-dependent transfer from fatty-acid-synthesis intermediates provides an optional upstream supply route. Polymer composition, synthase substrate acceptance and depolymerase access depend on the enzyme, substrate pool and native granule context.

Make it or break it: A review on PHA synthase and depolymerase proteins
Class II synthases are usual mcl-PHA anchors, but class labels are not absolute substrate exclusion rules; the review discusses C. necator class I acceptance of C8/C12 precursors in heterologous E. coli. Intracellular native-granule and extracellular denatured-polymer depolymerases are distinct roles. Publisher calls this OriginalPaper, but its content is a literature review.
PMID:38845815
Current trends in medium-chain-length polyhydroxyalkanoates: Microbial production, purification, and characterization.
The mcl range is C6–C14, distinct from C3–C5 scl-PHA. Wild-type production reports extend beyond Pseudomonas, but a polymer-production report does not identify the matching synthase/depolymerase pair or license family-only role transfer. Composition depends on feedstock; C8/C10 are frequent, not exclusive.
PMID:28425176
Polyhydroxyalkanoate-associated phasins as phylogenetically heterogeneous, multipurpose proteins.
Phasins organize granules and influence accumulation, segregation and enzyme access. These are adjacent granule functions, not additional obligatory chemical reactions in the bounded synthase-plus-intracellular-depolymerase profile; PHB-specific phasin dependencies cannot automatically be transferred to mcl systems.
PMID:35338129
Metabolic engineering strategies to produce medium-chain oleochemicals via acyl-ACP:CoA transacylase activity.
KT2440 PhaG expressed in E. coli supports forward ACP-to-CoA routing in genetic product-sink comparisons: the product does not depend on FadD reactivation. The paper explicitly lacks direct forward acyl-ACP in-vitro kinetics. Endogenous thioesterases offer an alternative explanation for free hydroxyacids in older experiments. The main subsequent enzyme optimization uses P. koreensis PhaG and is not KT2440 native physiology.
PMID:12200450
Biochemical characterization of the Pseudomonas putida 3-hydroxyacyl ACP:CoA transacylase, which diverts intermediates of fatty acid de novo biosynthesis.
Purified/refolded P. putida PhaG was measured transferring hydroxyacyl groups from CoA to ACP: direct reverse-reaction evidence, not a direct forward-ACP assay. Abstract also reports cytosolic localization and substrate-dependent dimerization. Exact target strain/construct requires fuller source bridge.
PMID:22101037
Development of a new strategy for production of medium-chain-length polyhydroxyalkanoates by recombinant Escherichia coli via inexpensive non-fatty acid feedstocks.
This study inferred PhaG thioesterase chemistry from heterologous free hydroxyacid accumulation and increased PHA when PP0763 was coexpressed. It did not isolate PhaG hydrolysis from endogenous CoA-thioester hydrolysis. Preserve the observed phenotype while reassessing that interpretation against the 2022 controls; do not make PP0763 universally required for PhaG routing. Native KT2440 polymer composition is measured separately from recombinant E. coli output.
PMID:1989978
Metabolism of poly(3-hydroxyalkanoates) (PHAs) by Pseudomonas oleovorans. Identification and sequences of genes and function of the encoded proteins in the synthesis and degradation of PHA.
The original GPo1 locus study selected synthase/degradation complementation and sequenced the two synthases and intervening depolymerase. M58445 CDS translations exactly match P26494/P26495/P26496. It does not establish a mandatory sequential C1/C2 division, and the abstract reports no clear specificity difference.
PMID:11133449
Polyhydroxyalkanoate degradation is associated with nucleotide accumulation and enhances stress resistance and survival of Pseudomonas oleovorans in natural water microcosms.
Native GPo1 and GPo500 phaZ were compared for polymer mobilization, with genetic complementation. The rescuing construct contains phaC1 plus phaZ and their promoter, so it is not purified single-protein reconstitution. The mutant is leaky, not absolute zero turnover. ATP/ppGpp and survival are consequences, not additional PhaZ catalytic functions. The source describes primarily C8/C6 polymer.
PMID:10713430
The Pseudomonas aeruginosa phaG gene product is involved in the synthesis of polyhydroxyalkanoic acid consisting of medium-chain-length constituents from non-related carbon sources.
PAO1 PhaG supports complementation of a P. putida phaG mutant; the resulting polymer contains C6/C8/C10/C12. Native P. aeruginosa phaG disruption reduces gluconate-supported PHA to about 40% of wild type while fatty-acid-supported accumulation remains at wild-type level. This establishes a useful, nonobligatory substrate-routing role, not direct purified target chemistry. AF209711 exactly matches Q51553.
PMID:10952003
In vitro synthesis of poly(3-hydroxydecanoate): purification and enzymatic characterization of type II polyhydroxyalkanoate synthases PhaC1 and PhaC2 from Pseudomonas aeruginosa.
Purified His-tagged P. aeruginosa class II PhaC1/PhaC2 synthesize poly(3-hydroxydecanoate) in vitro. Exact modern PAO1 PhaC1 construct identity remains unresolved: historical X66592 C1 is 557 aa versus current 559 aa. C2 and depolymerase archive sequences match modern entries, but adding a full PAO1 panel is unnecessary for the present mechanism comparison.
PMID:41055782
Revealing the essential role of the lid in mclPHA intracellular depolymerase from Pseudomonas putida KT2440.
Despite KT2440 in its title, the assayed pPAZ3/template derives from KT2442 and protein is expressed in E. coli. GPo1 supplies radiolabeled polymer, not the enzyme. WT PhaZ acts on mcl polymer and pNP ester substrates; commercial polymer is mainly C8 with C6. Do not transfer native KT2440 localization, whole-strain phenotypes or variant kinetics from this paper without identity/context qualification. Extracellular GK13 comparator is a distinct compartmental role.
PMID:10612741
Analysis of in vivo substrate specificity of the PHA synthase from Ralstonia eutropha: formation of novel copolyesters in recombinant Escherichia coli.
The Ralstonia eutropha class I PhaC produces 3HB/3HO and 3HB/3HO/3HDD copolyesters in engineered E. coli. This supports medium-chain comonomer acceptance, not pure or predominantly medium-chain polymer or complete native intracellular turnover. The primary abstract distinguishes this single-synthase result from the separate two-synthase blend control; exact assay-construct sequence equality remains unverified.
PMID:35341893
High amounts of medium-chain-length polyhydroxyalkanoates subunits can be accumulated in recombinant Cupriavidus necator with wild-type synthase.
Cupriavidus necator H16 with its unmodified native synthase accumulates SCL–MCL copolymer after engineered precursor routing. Class I membership therefore cannot exclude medium-chain incorporation. This engineered substrate-pool result does not establish complete native MCL synthesis and mobilization or a matched depolymerase.

The core boundary starts at externally supplied hydroxyacyl-CoA and includes polymerization plus intracellular mobilization; requiring PhaZ for this combined profile does not make it necessary for synthesis alone. KT2440 and exact GPo1 references anchor the core; PAO1 PhaG is an optional-route comparator, not a complete separately reviewed PAO1 panel. GPo1 taxonomy/nomenclature aliases denote one strain. The unverified Q9KJH8 sequence-only PhaG representative is not retained as an equally direct functional exemplar. Class II PhaCs are positive anchors rather than a universal exclusion of class I medium-chain acceptance. PhaZ family placements 43433 and 43798 remain distinct with source-qualified substrate/function evidence. A KT2440 historical direct-localization proposal is retired in place solely for unsupported target-direct provenance, while qualified core localization is retained. Provider artifacts remain immutable discovery inputs; current primary evidence determines the mechanism. Full validation, capacity closure and independent review remain pending. CUPNH/phaC P23608 is reviewed as a class I substrate-range comparator. Its supported SCL–MCL copolymer incorporation is distinct from the matched synthase/depolymerase core panels.
