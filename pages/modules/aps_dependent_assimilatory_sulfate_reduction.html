Assimilatory sulfate reduction to sulfide (APS and PAPS routes)

Assimilatory sulfate reduction supplies sulfide for sulfur-containing metabolites in characterized bacterial, fungal and plant systems. ATP sulfurylase first forms APS. Activated sulfate is then reduced either directly by an APS reductase or after APS kinase forms PAPS. The plant APS reductase exemplar uses glutathione through a fused redox domain; bacterial APS and fungal PAPS reductases have distinct external redox-partner requirements. Siroheme/iron-sulfur sulfite reductases complete the six-electron reduction, using characterized NADPH-linked complexes or ferredoxin-linked enzymes; the immediate carrier in the Pseudomonas Fpr-linked system remains unresolved. Sulfate import, cofactor biosynthesis, reductant regeneration and downstream sulfide incorporation are boundary processes.
