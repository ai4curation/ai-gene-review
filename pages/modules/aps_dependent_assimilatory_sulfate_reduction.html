Assimilatory sulfate reduction to sulfide (APS and PAPS routes)

A reusable pathway that reduces inorganic sulfate to sulfide for incorporation into sulfur amino acids. Sulfate is first activated by ATP sulfurylase to adenosine 5'-phosphosulfate (APS), because the sulfate/sulfite couple is too electronegative for reduction by physiological donors. The activated sulfate is then reduced to sulfite by one of two routes: directly, by a thioredoxin-dependent APS reductase (many bacteria, and plants), or after phosphorylation of APS by APS kinase to 3'-phosphoadenosine 5'-phosphosulfate (PAPS), by a thioredoxin-dependent PAPS reductase that releases sulfite and adenosine 3',5'-bisphosphate (enterobacteria and fungi). Finally a siroheme- and iron-sulfur-containing sulfite reductase performs the six-electron reduction of sulfite to sulfide, receiving electrons either from NADPH through a flavoprotein subunit (enterobacterial CysJ/CysI and the fungal Met10/Met5 alpha2beta2 enzyme) or from reduced ferredoxin. ATP sulfurylase itself is either a heterodimeric GTPase-coupled CysD/CysN enzyme (proteobacteria) or a homo-oligomeric enzyme (fungi, plants and many bacteria). Sulfate import is upstream; siroheme synthesis, thioredoxin regeneration, recycling of adenosine 3',5'-bisphosphate, and incorporation of sulfide into homocysteine or cysteine are supporting or downstream biology outside the pathway boundary.

ALIGN with YeastPathways (2026-10): the module, which previously represented only the direct APS-reductase route, now models the APS-kinase/PAPS-reductase route as an explicit variant of the APS-to-sulfite step instead of a separate module, and the slug is kept for continuity even though the title now names both routes. Fungal implementations were added at every step: a homo-oligomeric Sat/Met3-type ATP sulfurylase variant (MET3, S. pombe sua1) beside CysD/CysN; the PAPS route with APS kinase (MET14, met14, E. coli CysC), PAPS reductase (MET16, met16, E. coli CysH) and its thioredoxin reductant (TRX1, TRX2, S. pombe trx1); and a fungal Met10/Met5 NADPH sulfite-reductase variant beside the CysJ/CysI and ferredoxin/Fpr variants. Pseudomonas aeruginosa CysH (biochemically an APS-specific reductase) was added as an exemplar of the direct route. YeastCyc assessment (SO4ASSIM-PWY, PWY-781, PWY-5340) - all catalyst assignments (MET3, MET14, MET16, MET10 + MET5) are correct; YeastCyc omits the thioredoxins TRX1/TRX2, whose combined loss causes methionine auxotrophy (PMID:2026619), and the siroheme enzymes MET1 and MET8, which are required for sulfite reductase activity but are modelled in siroheme_biosynthesis. Adenosine 3',5'-bisphosphate released by MET16 is recycled by a separate phosphatase (MET22), and thioredoxin is regenerated by NADPH-thioredoxin reductase; both are supporting biology outside the boundary. PWY-5340 (sulfate activation for sulfonation) is the MET3 + MET14 prefix of the PAPS route and is not a separate module; the animal bifunctional PAPS synthase plus Golgi import is modelled in paps_sulfate_activation. Compartment: every S. cerevisiae and S. pombe activity is cytosolic in the GO-CAMs, but plant enzymes are plastid localized, so no module-level compartment is asserted. GO-CAM comparison - the YeastPathways models agree on genes and functions but have no causal edges and omit thioredoxin; the PomBase model includes trx1 (typed GO:0009055 electron transfer activity rather than the disulfide-reductase activity used here) and represents the sulfite reductase as two separate GO:0004783 activities enabled by sir1 and met10 rather than one activity of the heteromeric complex. Retained from the previous version: exact UniProt exemplars delimit the CysD/CysN, ferredoxin/Fpr, and CysJ/CysI implementations without restricting the module taxonomically; the Fpr-linked variant records electron-supply architecture while leaving the immediate donor as a concrete-system knowledge gap; no molecular function is duplicated at module level; and PTN001249481 is asserted only from the local PTHR43196 PAINT IBD record.

Fungal Met10/Met5 NADPH sulfite reductaseReactionmet10_met5_nadph_route

Fungal alpha2beta2 hemoflavoprotein: the Met10 flavoprotein (alpha) transfers electrons from NADPH to the Met5 siroheme/[4Fe-4S] hemoprotein (beta). Met10 is related to CysJ but has an N-terminal pyruvate-flavodoxin oxidoreductase-like region and lacks obvious FMN-binding motifs (PMID:7928966); instead the flavodoxin-like (FMN) domain sits on the Met5 beta subunit, the reverse of enterobacterial CysJ8I4, so the fungal enzyme is kept as a separate variant. The activity belongs to the complex: both subunits contribute to GO:0004783.
