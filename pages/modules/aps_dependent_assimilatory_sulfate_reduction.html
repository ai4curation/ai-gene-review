APS-dependent assimilatory sulfate reduction

A reusable pathway that converts sulfate to sulfide through adenosine 5'-phosphosulfate (APS) and sulfite. The module contains sulfate activation by ATP sulfurylase, thioredoxin-dependent APS reduction, and assimilatory sulfite reduction. It represents the direct APS branch rather than the alternative APS-kinase/PAPS-reductase route. Sulfate import is upstream, whereas siroheme synthesis and incorporation of sulfide into cysteine are supporting or downstream biology outside the pathway boundary.

sulfate assimilationGO:0000103

This module deliberately represents the direct APS-reduction route. An APS-kinase/PAPS-reductase branch should be modeled as a separate route variant rather than adding a PAPS step to every instance. Exact UniProt exemplars delimit the CysD/CysN, ferredoxin/Fpr, and CysJ/CysI implementations without restricting the module taxonomically. The Fpr-linked variant records electron-supply architecture while leaving the immediate donor as a concrete-system knowledge gap. No molecular function or location is duplicated at module level, and PTN001249481 is asserted only from the local PTHR43196 PAINT IBD record.

Part 3: sulfite reduction
Assimilatory reduction of sulfite to sulfideReactionsulfite_reduction_step
Variant set: Sulfite-reductase electron-donor architecture by electron-supply architecture (Exactly One)

The catalytic siroheme/iron-sulfur hemoprotein is conserved, but lineages differ in whether electrons arrive through a separate CysJ flavoprotein or an Fpr-linked system. In the latter route, the immediate donor may be reduced ferredoxin or Fpr itself and must be established for the concrete organism.

Ferredoxin/Fpr-fed CysI routeReactionferredoxin_fpr_cysi_route

A siroheme sulfite reductase receives reducing equivalents through an Fpr-linked system, either from reduced ferredoxin or possibly directly from a type-1 Fpr enzyme. Physiological evidence can establish the electron-supply route without resolving the immediate donor.
