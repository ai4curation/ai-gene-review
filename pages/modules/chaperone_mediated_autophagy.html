Chaperone-mediated autophagy (HSPA8 substrate delivery and LAMP-2A translocation)

Chaperone-mediated autophagy (CMA) is a selective lysosomal degradation pathway for soluble cytosolic proteins that carry a KFERQ-like pentapeptide motif. The constitutive Hsp70 chaperone HSPA8/Hsc70 recognizes the motif in the cytosol and delivers the substrate to the cytosolic side of the lysosomal membrane, where it binds monomeric LAMP-2A, the CMA-specific splice isoform of LAMP2. Substrate binding drives LAMP-2A multimerization into a high-molecular-weight translocation complex, the unfolded substrate crosses the membrane, and it is degraded by lumenal hydrolases; HSPA8 then disassembles the LAMP-2A complex, returning LAMP-2A to its monomeric, substrate-binding state. The module covers substrate recognition and delivery, translocation through the LAMP-2A complex, and complex recycling. Regulation of CMA (for example lysosomal mTORC2/PHLPP1/AKT signalling or cathepsin A-mediated LAMP-2A turnover), the cochaperones that assist HSPA8 at the membrane, and the downstream consequences of degrading particular substrates (NLRP3, BBC3/PUMA, GPX4) are outside the boundary.
