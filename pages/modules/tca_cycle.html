Tricarboxylic acid cycle

A taxon-neutral module for the oxidative tricarboxylic acid (TCA) cycle, covering acetyl-CoA entry through citrate synthase, citrate/isocitrate interconversion by aconitase, oxidative decarboxylation of isocitrate to 2-oxoglutarate and of 2-oxoglutarate to succinyl-CoA, substrate-level phosphorylation by succinyl-CoA ligase, succinate oxidation by respiratory complex II, fumarate hydration, and malate oxidation to regenerate oxaloacetate. The chemistry is conserved between bacteria, where the cycle runs in the cytoplasm and complex II sits in the plasma membrane, and eukaryotes, where it runs in the mitochondrial matrix and complex II sits in the inner mitochondrial membrane. The enzyme implementations differ at several steps: citrate synthase is a hexameric type II enzyme in many Gram-negative bacteria and a dimeric type I enzyme in mitochondria; the isocitrate step uses NADP-dependent isocitrate dehydrogenases in many bacteria but an allosterically regulated NAD-dependent hetero-oligomeric isocitrate dehydrogenase in eukaryotic mitochondria; the eukaryotic 2-oxoglutarate dehydrogenase complex carries an extra adaptor subunit that recruits the E3 component; and malate can be oxidized by NAD-dependent malate dehydrogenase or by membrane-associated malate:quinone oxidoreductases. Paralogous isozymes in other compartments (peroxisomal or cytosolic citrate synthase, malate dehydrogenase and NADP-isocitrate dehydrogenase) serve the glyoxylate cycle, NADPH supply or redox shuttles rather than the oxidative cycle.

file:projects/P_PUTIDA/deep-research/PSEPK__tca_cycle__ppu00020-deep-research-openscientist.md
OpenScientist PSEPK/ppu00020 TCA-cycle research
The P. putida KT2440 pathway report concludes that the oxidative TCA cycle is satisfiable, with quinone-linked MQO as the primary malate oxidation branch and with scpC/prpC treated as methylcitrate or CoA-transferase boundary cases rather than core SCS/citrate synthase replacements.

Reusable boundary: this module describes the oxidative TCA cycle and its immediate bacterial and eukaryotic alternatives, not every KEGG ppu00020 or YeastCyc neighbor. Pyruvate dehydrogenase supplies acetyl-CoA upstream and pyruvate carboxylase supplies an external anaplerotic oxaloacetate input (both in pyruvate_metabolism); the isocitrate lyase/malate synthase bypass is glyoxylate_cycle; methylcitrate/propionyl-CoA CoA-transferase proteins are adjacent propionate-metabolism context. The PSEPK instantiation is satisfiable with gltA/Q88FA4, acnB/Q88KF1 plus acnA-I/Q88L24, icd/Q88FS2 plus idh/Q88FS1, sucA/Q88FA9-sucB/Q88FB0 with lpdG/Q88FB1 or related E3 components, sucD/Q88FB3-sucC/Q88FB2, sdhABCD/Q88FA7-Q88FA8-Q88FA5-Q88FA6, fumarase paralogs including the class-I enzyme PP_0897/Q88PF3 and the class-II enzymes fumC/Q88M20 and fumC-I/Q88PA6, and malate oxidation by mqo1/Q88PU7, mqo2/Q88NF9, mqo3/Q88IS4, and/or mdh/Q88Q44. ALIGN (YeastPathways project, 2026-10): S. cerevisiae and S. pombe mitochondrial exemplars were added at every step, with two lineage variant sets (type II bacterial versus type I eukaryotic citrate synthase, which fall in different PANTHER families PTHR42871 and PTHR11739; NADP-IDH of bacteria versus the NAD-IDH IDH1/IDH2 hetero-octamer of mitochondria), an optional eukaryotic Kgd4 adaptor part in the 2-oxoglutarate dehydrogenase complex, and both plasma-membrane (bacterial) and inner-mitochondrial-membrane (eukaryotic) locations for complex II. PANTHER family ids were added only where every representative falls in the same family (verified against the UniProt PANTHER cross-references and interpro/panther/panther.obo); the aconitase, fumarase, SdhD/Sdh4 and malate dehydrogenase descriptors stay without an id because their bacterial and eukaryotic representatives fall in different families. YeastCyc corrections: ACO2 is removed from the aconitase step because Aco2p is a homocitrate dehydratase used only in lysine biosynthesis (PMID:23106124; consistent with lysine_biosynthesis_aminoadipate); CIT2 (peroxisomal), MDH2 (cytosolic) and MDH3 (peroxisomal) belong to the glyoxylate cycle and gluconeogenesis rather than the mitochondrial cycle; KGD4 was added as the E3-recruiting subunit of the 2-oxoglutarate dehydrogenase complex; the poorly characterized SDH1 paralog YJL045W (SDH1b/SDH9) and the SDH3/SDH4 paralogs SHH3 and SHH4 were not added as exemplars. The mitochondrial NADP-IDH IDP1 is not a TCA-cycle member: it does not support TCA-based respiration (PMID:8099357), and the major isocitrate flux on glucose is through NAD-IDH, while cytosolic IDP2 carries it on nonfermentable carbon (PMID:15574419). GO-CAM disagreements: the YeastPathways TCA-EUK-PWY conversion enables MALATE-DEH-RXN by MAE1 (malic enzyme) and MDH1 rather than by MDH1 alone, places both aconitase activities in the cytosol, enables CITSYN-RXN by CIT2 as well as CIT1/CIT3, includes pyruvate carboxylase, and types every activity as part of the generic aerobic respiration process; the PWY3O-94 superpathway conversion places all activities, including complex II, in the cytosol or plasma membrane. The PomBase model 665912ed00000015 types aco2 (Q9P7D4, which UniProt annotates as a homocitrate dehydratase-mitoribosomal bL21m fusion) as a TCA aconitase, places dld1 in pyruvate decarboxylation rather than the 2-oxoglutarate dehydrogenase step, types sdh1 with succinate dehydrogenase (FAD) activity (GO:0160308) rather than the quinone-reducing complex activity, and includes NADP-IDH idp1, isocitrate lyase (SPBC1683.11c), glyoxylate reductase, fumarate reductase, oxaloacetate tautomerase, lipoylation and thiamine-pyrophosphate transport activities that lie outside this module.
