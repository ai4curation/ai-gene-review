L-arginine biosynthesis via acetylated ornithine (microbes and fungi)

De novo L-arginine biosynthesis from L-glutamate through N-acetylated intermediates and L-ornithine, as found in bacteria, archaea, fungi and plants. The module includes the linear route, in which ArgA initiates acetylation and ArgE hydrolyses N-acetyl-L-ornithine, and the cyclic (acetyl-cycle) route, in which bifunctional ArgJ (ornithine acetyltransferase) recycles the acetyl group from N-acetyl-L-ornithine to L-glutamate and can also initiate the pathway; the cyclic route is the one used by fungi, where a structurally distinct fungal-type N-acetylglutamate synthase supplies anaplerotic N-acetylglutamate. After ornithine formation, ornithine carbamoyltransferase, argininosuccinate synthase and argininosuccinate lyase convert it through L-citrulline and argininosuccinate to L-arginine. In fungi the pathway is split between compartments: the five ornithine-forming reactions take place in the mitochondrial matrix, where acetylglutamate kinase and acetylglutamyl-phosphate reductase are made as a single precursor polyprotein that is cleaved into two enzymes after import, and the argininosuccinate reactions are cytosolic. Ornithine carbamoyltransferase is cytosolic in budding yeast, which exports ornithine from mitochondria, but mitochondrial in fission yeast and Neurospora, which export citrulline. Fungi also have a carbamoyl-phosphate synthetase dedicated to arginine biosynthesis (CPS-A), distinct from the pyrimidine-pathway enzyme, and it is modelled as an optional part; where carbamoyl phosphate comes from a synthetase shared with pyrimidine biosynthesis, as in many bacteria, that supply lies outside the module boundary. Succinylated-intermediate, N-acetylcitrulline, and LysW-dependent arginine pathways are distinct implementations not expanded in this module.

L-arginine biosynthetic processGO:0006526

Molecular functions are attached only to leaf annotons. The module-level concept is the biological process, and no module-wide location is asserted because the scope spans bacteria and compartmentalised eukaryotes; the only asserted locations are on the two OTC compartment variants. Exact PSEPK UniProt accessions ground each family selector without making the reusable reaction architecture species-specific. PAINT nodes are attached only where a local IBD row names a reviewed molecular-function exemplar; they do not establish species-specific pathway usage. Shared carbamoyl-phosphate production and unrelated arginine catabolism are outside this module. ALIGN with YeastPathways (2026-10; ARGSYNBSUB-PWY and CITRUL-BIO2-PWY, the latter folded in): every YeastCyc reaction maps onto the module. The YeastCyc gene assignments for the arginine enzymes are correct (ARG2, ARG7, ARG5,6, ARG8, ARG3, ARG1, ARG4, CPA1, CPA2); URA2 is listed by YeastCyc for CARBPSYN-RXN but is the pyrimidine CPSase-ATCase and is not adopted. Changes made - a fungal-type NAGS variant (ARG2, arg6; PANTHER PTHR23342:SF4) was added because fungal NAGS is unrelated to bacterial ArgA; the ArgJ, ArgB, type 1 ArgC, ArgD, ASS and ASL selectors already contain the fungal enzymes, which were added as representatives (ARG5,6 and arg11 under both ArgB and ArgC, as the precursor polyprotein is cleaved into two separate enzymes, so no fused-architecture variant was needed); an optional pathway-dedicated CPS-A part (CPA1/CPA2, arg5/arg4) was added, keeping the original boundary that excludes shared CarAB-type supply; and the OTC step gained a cytosol versus mitochondrial-matrix variant set reflecting ornithine export (S. cerevisiae, ORT1/ARG11) versus citrulline export (S. pombe, SPBC365.16; Neurospora). Fungi lack ArgE, so the ArgJ transacetylation variant is their only ornithine-release implementation. Mitochondrial carriers (ORT1, SPBC365.16) are transport steps between compartments and are kept outside the enzymatic core. In fungi the ornithine-forming steps (NAGS, ARG5,6, ARG8, ARG7) are mitochondrial-matrix enzymes (PMID:205532). GO-CAM disagreements - YeastPathways_ARGSYNBSUB-PWY places all activities in cytosol, including the mitochondrial ARG2, ARG5,6, ARG8 and ARG7 activities; it types the acetyl-CoA-dependent glutamate acetylation by ARG2 and ARG7 as GO:0103045 (L-methionine N-acyltransferase activity) instead of GO:0004042; and has a single causal edge. The PomBase model (6690711d00000916) agrees with the module's compartment split but omits the arginine-specific CPSase (arg5/arg4) and the aga1 acetyl-CoA-dependent activity, and also includes arginyl-tRNA ligase (msr1) and arginine import, which are outside this module.

Part 6: pathway-dedicated carbamoyl-phosphate synthetase (optional)

Optional because many bacteria make carbamoyl phosphate with a single CarA/CarB synthetase shared with pyrimidine biosynthesis, which is kept outside this module. Fungi have a separate arginine-specific CPS-A (S. cerevisiae CPA1/CPA2, S. pombe arg5/arg4) in addition to the pyrimidine-pathway CPSase-ATCase (S. cerevisiae URA2, S. pombe ura1); only the dedicated enzyme is a module part. YeastPathways also lists URA2 for this reaction, but URA2 is the pyrimidine paralog and is not adopted here.

L-glutamine + hydrogencarbonate + 2 ATP to carbamoyl phosphateProtein Complexcarbamoyl_phosphate_synthetase_a_step

Heterodimeric glutamine-dependent carbamoyl-phosphate synthetase in which a small subunit hydrolyses glutamine and a large subunit uses the released ammonia, bicarbonate and two ATP to make carbamoyl phosphate; together the subunits provide carbamoyl-phosphate synthase (glutamine-hydrolyzing) activity. The enzyme is cytosolic in S. cerevisiae but mitochondrial in S. pombe and Neurospora, matching the compartment of ornithine carbamoyltransferase in each species.
