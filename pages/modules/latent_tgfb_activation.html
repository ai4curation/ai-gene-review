Latent TGF-beta activation module

Release of active TGF-beta from the latent complex in which it is stored. TGF-beta is unusual among cytokines: the cell secretes it already folded around its own prodomain, which holds it inert, and the controlled step is not transcription or secretion but mechanical release from that complex, often long afterwards and somewhere else. Furin cuts the proprotein into the latency-associated peptide and the mature growth factor, which stay non-covalently associated; a latent TGF-beta-binding protein (LTBP1, LTBP3 or LTBP4) ties the small latent complex into the extracellular matrix, or GARP (LRRC32) tethers it to the surface of a regulatory T cell or platelet. An alpha-V integrin then grips the RGD motif in the prodomain and pulls: force, not proteolysis, unfastens the prodomain's "straitjacket" and frees the growth factor to reach its receptor. The receptor signalling that follows is a separate module.

Built for projects/PRODUCTION_TERMS.md. The curated classification there marks the four TGF-beta production terms (4 production, 12 regulation) as resting on dedicated machinery, and this module is that machinery. The terms are also among the six cytokine production terms GO never flagged do-not-annotate, so unlike most of the branch they are still open for direct annotation, which makes the missing activation term more consequential. Boundary: starts at the secreted proprotein and ends with free, receptor-competent TGF-beta. Receptor binding and SMAD signalling are modules/tgfb_smad_signaling.yaml. Transcription of the TGF-beta genes is upstream and is not modelled here; that is the part the "production" terms name. Not modelled as variants of the activation step: thrombospondin-1, plasmin, MMP2 and MMP9, and low pH are all reported to activate latent TGF-beta, but they are a heterogeneous set whose physiological weight is contested, and treating them as alternative realizers would assert a parity with integrin-mediated activation that the evidence does not support. THBS1 is noted in the knowledge gap instead. No cached GO-CAM has an activity on any of these proteins in this role, so there are no pins.

Anchoring of the small latent complexRegulatory Steplatent_complex_anchoring

Part 3: force-dependent release
Integrin- and force-dependent release of active TGF-betaRegulatory Stepintegrin_force_release

An alpha-V integrin binds the RGD motif in the prodomain and transmits cytoskeletal force to it. Binding alone does nothing: the prodomain only lets go when force unfastens it, which is why this step needs a cell pulling on an anchored complex rather than an enzyme.
