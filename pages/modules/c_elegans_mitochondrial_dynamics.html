Mitochondrial fission and fusion in Caenorhabditis elegans

Mitochondrial shape in Caenorhabditis elegans is set by a balance between fission and fusion carried out by three dynamin-family GTPases. Fission of the outer membrane is driven by DRP-1, the DNM1L/Drp1 ortholog, which is recruited from the cytosol to constriction sites on the outer membrane. There it assembles and uses GTP hydrolysis to sever the membrane, while inner-membrane division can proceed without it. The tail-anchored outer-membrane proteins MFF-1 and MFF-2, orthologs of mitochondrial fission factor, act redundantly to promote DRP-1-dependent fission of mitochondria and peroxisomes. The two FIS1 homologs, FIS-1 and FIS-2, are not required for fission in worms. Fusion happens in two membrane-specific steps. The mitofusin FZO-1 fuses outer membranes, and EAT-3, the OPA1 ortholog, fuses inner membranes and maintains cristae. Loss of fzo-1 or eat-3 fragments the mitochondrial network, and this fragmentation depends on drp-1; loss of drp-1 gives interconnected networks in which matrix compartments are joined by outer-membrane tubules. Fission and fusion are not needed for programmed cell death in this species. They are used in stress responses, in recovery of the network after acute heat stress, and in segregating damaged mitochondria for mitophagy.

This is a concrete C. elegans module for the membrane-remodeling reactions that divide and join mitochondria: DRP-1-dependent outer-membrane fission with its MFF adaptors, FZO-1 outer-membrane fusion, and EAT-3 inner-membrane fusion. Selective removal of mitochondria is MODULE:c_elegans_mitophagy, which uses MFF/DRP-1 fission upstream of the FIS-1/FIS-2-dependent disposal step in muscle. FIS-1 and FIS-2. The project page lists the FIS1 homologs as fission factors, but in worms neither single nor double mutants have a detectable fission or morphology defect (PMID:24196833, PMID:18722182). Both gene reviews give them mitophagy and disposal roles, not a role in DRP-1 recruitment. They are therefore members of the mitophagy module, not of the fission part here. FIS-1 overexpression can still fragment mitochondria, even in the Mff double mutant, so it can promote fission when overexpressed. That is not evidence for an endogenous role. MFF-2. MFF-2 (P91344, F55F8.6; unreviewed UniProt entry, no gene review in this repository) is included alongside MFF-1 because the strong fission defect needs both genes. Its accession is from UniProt and matches the F55F8 locus named in PMID:21248201. MiD49/MiD51-type recruiters are vertebrate-specific and absent from worms. BCL-2-family regulation. CED-9 binds FZO-1 and, when bound to EGL-1, binds and recruits DRP-1 (PMID:21949250). Another group found that loss or gain of egl-1 or ced-9 had no effect on mitochondrial connectivity or size (PMID:19327994). CED-9 and EGL-1 are therefore not modeled as members. The conflict is recorded as a knowledge gap. Members left out. MIRO-1 (Q94263) is an outer-membrane Rho GTPase that, in other animals, links mitochondria to microtubule motors. Its worm review supports GTPase, calcium-binding and kinesin-binding activities, but no cached primary paper shows a worm transport or distribution defect: the miro-1 mutant has about half the normal amount of mitochondria and a long lifespan (PMID:27064409). Transport is also a different process from fission and fusion, so MIRO-1 is noted here and not modeled. SPG-7 (Q9N3T5) is the worm AFG3L2-type m-AAA protease subunit of the inner membrane. Its review places it in mitochondrial protein processing, respiratory-chain assembly and the UPRmt, not in the dynamics machinery. No worm evidence links it to EAT-3 processing, so it is left out. MOMA-1 affects cristae morphology and is also left out.

EAT-3 mitochondrial inner membrane fusionBiological Processeat3_imm_fusion

EAT-3 is the OPA1 ortholog, a dynamin-family GTPase of the inner membrane. In eat-3 mutants, matrix compartments are split by inner-membrane septa and cristae are disrupted. The animals are small, slow-growing, have small broods and are hypersensitive to paraquat. These phenotypes are stronger than those of the other fission and fusion mutants.
