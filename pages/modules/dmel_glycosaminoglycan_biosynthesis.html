Heparan sulfate and chondroitin sulfate proteoglycan biosynthesis in Drosophila melanogaster

Heparan sulfate and chondroitin sulfate chains are built in the endoplasmic reticulum and Golgi on serine residues of core proteins such as the glypicans Dally and Dally-like, Syndecan and Perlecan. Both start on a shared tetrasaccharide linker (xylose-galactose-galactose-glucuronic acid) made by Oxt, beta4GalT7, beta3GalTII and GlcAT-I. Addition of N-acetylglucosamine by Brother of tout-velu (Botv) commits the chain to heparan sulfate; the Tout-velu (Ttv) and Sister of tout-velu (Sotv) exostosin complex then polymerizes alternating glucuronic acid and N-acetylglucosamine. The chain is modified by the N-deacetylase/N-sulfotransferase Sulfateless (Sfl), the C5-epimerase Hsepi and the 2-O-, 6-O- and 3-O-sulfotransferases (Hs2st, Hs6st, Hs3st-A, Hs3st-B). Addition of N-acetylgalactosamine by Csgalnact instead starts chondroitin chains, which are polymerized by Chsy and Chpf and sulfated by C4st. Heparan sulfate made by this pathway is required for Wingless, Hedgehog, Dpp and FGF signaling in Drosophila.
