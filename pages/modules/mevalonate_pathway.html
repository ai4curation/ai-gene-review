Mevalonate pathway (acetyl-CoA -> isopentenyl diphosphate)

The eukaryotic (classical) mevalonate pathway converts acetyl-CoA into the universal five-carbon isoprenoid building block isopentenyl diphosphate (IPP), the precursor of sterols (cholesterol in animals, ergosterol in fungi), dolichol, ubiquinone (CoQ), heme A and the farnesyl/geranylgeranyl groups used for protein prenylation. A cytosolic acetoacetyl-CoA thiolase condenses two acetyl-CoA to acetoacetyl-CoA; HMG-CoA synthase adds a third acetyl-CoA to give 3-hydroxy-3-methylglutaryl-CoA (HMG-CoA); HMG-CoA reductase, the rate-limiting, sterol-feedback-regulated enzyme and target of the statin drugs, reduces HMG-CoA to mevalonate with two NADPH on the endoplasmic-reticulum membrane; mevalonate kinase and phosphomevalonate kinase then doubly phosphorylate mevalonate to mevalonate 5-diphosphate; and diphosphomevalonate decarboxylase decarboxylates it to IPP in an ATP-dependent reaction. The reaction chain is conserved across animals, fungi and plants, but the phosphomevalonate kinase step is carried out by two unrelated protein families: a GHMP-kinase-type enzyme (ERG8 type) in fungi, plants and bacteria, and a small P-loop kinase (PMVK type) that displaced it early in animal evolution. Fungi may carry two HMG-CoA reductase isozymes (budding yeast HMG1 and HMG2). Mevalonate kinase is a second, metabolite-level control point, competitively feedback-inhibited by downstream prenyl diphosphates. In humans, inherited defects cause mevalonate kinase deficiency (MVK; hyper-IgD/periodic fever syndrome to mevalonic aciduria) and porokeratosis (MVD and MVK); HMGCR and HMGCS1 are pharmacologic (statin), autoimmune and rare-myopathy loci rather than classic enzymopathies.

Context
cytosolGO:0005829

Generalised from a human-only module to the eukaryotic (classical) mevalonate pathway. Changes: (1) the cytosolic acetoacetyl-CoA thiolase step was added as part 1, because GO:0019287 is defined from acetyl-CoA, YeastCyc IPPSYN-PWY and both yeast GO-CAMs include it, and the ERG10 review places ERG10 directly in GO:0019287; the human representative is the cytosolic ACAT2 (UniProtKB:Q9BWD1), not the mitochondrial ACAT1 of ketone-body metabolism. (2) S. cerevisiae (ERG10, ERG13, HMG1, HMG2, ERG12, ERG8, MVD1) and S. pombe (erg10, hcs1, hmg1, erg12, erg8, mvd1) representatives were added; all share the human enzymes' PANTHER families except phosphomevalonate kinase. (3) The phosphomevalonate kinase step became a variant set: ERG8-type GHMP kinases (PTHR31814; fungi, plants, eubacteria) and the animal P-loop PMVK (PTHR13101) are nonorthologous (PMID:11243736), so one family selector cannot cover both. (4) Connections were retyped from PROVIDES_INPUT_FOR to PRECEDES. IDI1, which both YeastCyc frames list, is modelled in isoprenoid_diphosphate_biosynthesis (IPP -> DMAPP), because the GO process term ends at IPP. Archaeal variants of the lower mevalonate pathway are outside this module. Regulation. HMG-CoA reductase is the sterol-feedback, rate-limiting step (SREBP/Insig in animals; the PomBase model also records the ppe1 phosphatase and its inhibitor sds23 acting upstream of S. pombe hmg1). Mevalonate kinase is a second, metabolite-level control point: downstream isoprenoid pyrophosphates feedback-inhibit it, and the five known inhibitors (IPP, DMAPP, GPP, FPP, GGPP) compete for one shared site despite spanning C5-C20 (PMID:42754161, which reports inhibition kinetics and apo plus five inhibitor-bound X-ray structures for the S. cerevisiae enzyme; per-inhibitor Ki values are not in the abstract and are not asserted). FPP sensitivity of yeast ERG12 was already reported in PMID:1645230. GO-CAM disagreements: both SGD conversions place every activity, including the ER-membrane HMG1/HMG2, in cytosol, give HMG1 and HMG2 one shared activity IRI, and use either the broad GO:0008299 or the obsolete GO:0010142 as the process; the PomBase model uses GO:0010142 and GO:0045337 rather than GO:0019287 as process terms. Human disorders: MVK -> mevalonate kinase deficiency; MVD (and MVK) -> porokeratosis; HMGCR -> statin target and autoantibody-mediated necrotizing myopathy; HMGCS1 -> a rare rigid-spine myopathy.
