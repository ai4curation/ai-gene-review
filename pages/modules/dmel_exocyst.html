Exocyst complex in Drosophila melanogaster

The exocyst is an octameric tethering complex that captures secretory and recycling vesicles at sites of exocytosis on the plasma membrane before SNARE-mediated fusion. Its eight subunits (Sec3, Sec5, Sec6, Sec8, Sec10, Sec15, Exo70 and Exo84) are long helical-bundle proteins; Sec3 and Exo70 bind plasma membrane phosphoinositides and small GTPases, while Sec15 binds Rab11 on the vesicle. Structural studies divide the complex into two four-subunit modules, Sec3-Sec5-Sec6-Sec8 and Sec10-Sec15-Exo70-Exo84. In Drosophila the exocyst is required for polarized membrane delivery, for example to the rhabdomere of photoreceptors, to growing neurites and for epithelial junction protein trafficking.
