Bacterial Mla intermembrane phospholipid transport

Phospholipid transfer across the two-membrane envelope of Mla-containing diderm bacteria couples an outer-membrane MlaA interface, a soluble periplasmic MlaC carrier, and an ATP-coupled inner-membrane MlaF/E/D assembly. Characterized enterobacterial and pseudomonad systems include the MlaB accessory subunit, whereas a Bordetella pertussis realization lacks an identifiable MlaB homolog. MlaA-associated porins, carrier cargo and operon organization vary by lineage. Reconstituted Escherichia coli assays favor ATP-driven retrograde transfer from the outer-membrane interface toward the inner membrane; passive reverse exchange and contested anterograde interpretations do not establish a universal alternative transport direction.

Comparative literature, member and family review completed; full-corpus validation, publication and independent PR review remain tracked separately in the project. The Bacteria taxon is an occurrence envelope for this multi-interface mechanism, not a claim of universal bacterial presence. A complete realization requires matched MlaA, MlaC and MlaF/E/D components, suitable phospholipid cargo, ATP and compatible membrane/partner context. The MlaB-containing and MlaB-independent architectures are alternatives with positive evidence requirements; dropping B from an arbitrary incomplete panel does not establish the latter. E. coli OmpC/OmpF are characterized partners, not universally required named porins. Existing source-model and probe-identity errors are corrected in the evidence statements without rewriting cached papers. PSEPK PP_0140-PP_0142 and PP_1737 remain unassigned comparators rather than interchangeable Mla components. Chloroplast TGD and mycobacterial Mce/YrbE systems are distinct biological mechanisms despite overlapping subfamilies. PR #5076 overlaps module labels and MlaB grounding; its SF1 assertion is now supported for the sampled MlaB members by current official classification, superseding the older no-ID explanation without rewriting source caches. See the frozen preflight and baseline records for source access and strain boundaries. Historical OpenScientist reports remain archived as discovery provenance. Their assertions are now grounded in primary literature, official classifications and the individual gene assessments; generated research is not independent biological evidence.
