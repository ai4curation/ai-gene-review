Bacterial Mla intermembrane phospholipid transport

A species-neutral diderm-bacterial module for phospholipid exchange across the cell envelope through an outer-membrane MlaA/VacJ interface, a soluble periplasmic MlaC carrier, and an ATP-coupled inner-membrane MlaFEDB complex. The module represents the conserved transport architecture without forcing a universal retrograde or anterograde net direction.

Pseudomonas putida KT2440 names the MlaC- and MlaB-equivalent operon products Ttg2D and Ttg2E. The module uses their conserved family roles while retaining the exact local gene names. Transport direction is deliberately unresolved: older annotations describe retrograde removal of outer-membrane phospholipids, whereas biochemical and structural literature has supported multiple direction models. The species-aware report confirms that legacy Ttg2D and Ttg2E names correspond to MlaC and MlaB roles by domain, KEGG orthology, and contiguous operon context. Shared MlaF/E/D-like domains or KOs alone are insufficient because PP_0140-PP_0142 encode a separate Mce-like system. OmpC/OmpF names describe characterized canonical porin partners rather than a universally required named porin in every lineage.
