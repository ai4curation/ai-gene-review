Bacterial choline-O-sulfate uptake and desulfation

A bacterial uptake-and-hydrolysis mechanism in which intact choline-O-sulfate enters the cell and is cleaved by BetC to choline, sulfate and a proton. Import implementations and molecular assignments vary among lineages. Uptake for osmoprotection without hydrolysis is a distinct outcome; downstream choline oxidation and sulfur assimilation lie outside this boundary.

Bacteria is the reusable scope, not a universal distribution claim. OpuC supplies a resolved transport exemplar from Bacillus, whose tested strains do not perform the complete hydrolytic pathway. Pseudomonas, Sinorhizobium and Ruegeria provide physiological or biochemical hydrolysis evidence, while their COS importer assignments remain open. BetD/BetE and BetT candidates are not mandatory core components. BetC uses the specific TIGR03417/IPR017785 lineage rather than the heterogeneous PTHR45953 family. OpuC subunit family assignments are classification evidence, not cargo predictions; related OpuB proteins cannot substitute merely because a family label matches. Whole-cell observed uptake and gene-based capacity are separate evidence modes. No native compatibility of components taken from different taxa is implied. Ruegeria is a hydrolysis/utilization comparator with unresolved compartment; its growth phenotype does not count as directly observed intact COS uptake. The four OpuC components are distinct subunit types, not a four-polypeptide stoichiometry. For ontology maintenance, no exact COS-transporter GO term was identified. Generic transmembrane transport with structured COS cargo represents the measured complex activity; subunit contributions remain distinct from independently enabled transport. A term request would improve terminology, without constituting an unresolved biological activity.
