Cuticle tanning and pigmentation in Drosophila melanogaster

After each molt and at eclosion, the insect cuticle is hardened (sclerotized) and pigmented using dopamine made by tyrosine hydroxylase (Pale) and DOPA decarboxylase. Dopamine is used in three branches. Arylalkylamine N-acetyltransferase (Speck, Aanat1) acetylates it to N-acetyldopamine (NADA), the precursor of colourless sclerotin. Ebony conjugates dopamine with beta-alanine to N-beta-alanyldopamine (NBAD), the precursor of yellow-tan sclerotin, and Tan hydrolyses NBAD back to dopamine, so the Ebony-Tan balance sets cuticle colour. Oxidation of dopamine and DOPA by phenoloxidases yields quinones and chromes that polymerize into black and brown melanins; the Yellow family proteins Yellow-f and Yellow-f2 convert dopachrome and Yellow-h converts dopaminechrome, and Yellow itself is needed for black melanin. Beta-alanine for NBAD comes from decarboxylation of aspartate by Black (Adc) and from uracil degradation through dihydropyrimidine dehydrogenase, dihydropyrimidinase and beta-ureidopropionase.
