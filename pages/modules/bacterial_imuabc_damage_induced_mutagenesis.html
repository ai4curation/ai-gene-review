Bacterial ImuABC damage-induced mutagenesis

A bacterial ImuA-type accessory protein, ImuB adaptor and DnaE2/ImuC polymerase cooperate in damage-tolerant DNA synthesis. This family-grounded realization spans experimentally characterized bacterial lineages without asserting that every bacterium carries the cassette. ImuB couples the accessory protein and polymerase to the replication clamp; DnaE2 supplies DNA synthesis. RecA signaling and LexA-family repression are optional, regulon-specific induction context. Mutational output depends on the organism, damage and growth state: induced mutagenesis and stationary-phase antimutator effects need not conflict. Nucleotide excision repair, homologous recombination and bulk chromosome replication are outside the module boundary, although translesion synthesis itself participates in the broader GO process DNA replication.

Scope. CONCRETE denotes this specific, family-grounded bacterial realization, not a single species. Bacteria is an envelope for a patchy distribution. Complete cassette function is supported in Caulobacter, pseudomonads and mycobacteria; individual Myxococcus accessory-protein assays provide additional mechanistic comparison. Co-occurrence, a polymerase fold or a predicted complex does not establish a complete active cassette in an arbitrary genome. Accessory proteins. ImuA and actinobacterial ImuA′ are modeled as distinct family realizers of one accessory role. Their divergent sequence classifications do not establish non-homology of every structural component. The earlier universal no-activity claim is contradicted by PMID:38591662: purified Myxococcus ImuA binds DNA and hydrolyzes ATP. This does not grant those activities to Caulobacter/Pseudomonas ImuA or mycobacterial ImuA′. The 2021 Myxococcus study did not detect DNA binding; the 2024 study discusses the assay dependence of that result. Its ImuB experiments used a truncated protein, and its proposed trimeric mutasome and ATP-dependent assembly cycle remain models. Assay organisms. PMID:20615954 combines M. tuberculosis deletion genetics and yeast two-hybrid assays of its proteins with M. smegmatis individual accessory deletion, catalytic and clamp-dependent functional tests. PMID:37530405 and PMID:39706264 add M. smegmatis imaging and purified-complex evidence. These cannot be relabeled as native M. tuberculosis dynamics or stoichiometry. Process scope. GO:0019985 translesion synthesis encompasses condition-dependent mutational output. P. putida DnaE2 suppresses stationary-phase base substitutions in PMID:17331811, while PMID:28118378 finds ImuC-dependent induced mutagenesis. Neither observation cancels the other. QuickGO is_a/part_of closure places translesion synthesis under GO:0006260 DNA replication; absence of a housekeeping replicase role does not make that broad process false. Capacity boundary. Effector-cassette checks assume compatible host clamp/replication machinery and active proteins. Optional regulatory nodes do not establish native expression, RecA/LexA-independent induction, or autonomous purified lesion bypass. Regulation. RecA and LexA are optional because induction and paralog assignment vary. KT2440 LexA2 regulates a local damage-response cassette; a narrower regulon alone does not exclude SOS participation. No universal cassette-specific LexA paralog or directly measured cleavage reaction is inferred from family membership.
