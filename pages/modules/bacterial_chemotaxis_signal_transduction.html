Bacterial chemotaxis signal transduction to flagellar motor output

Reusable bacterial chemotaxis module linking methyl-accepting chemoreceptor input and CheR/CheB methylation-dependent adaptation through CheW-coupled CheA autophosphorylation and phosphotransfer to the response regulator CheY, CheZ-dependent CheY-P dephosphorylation, CheY-P action on the FliG/FliM/FliN C-ring switch, and ion-conducting MotA/MotB-family stators that power flagellar rotation. Escherichia coli K-12 proteins ground the paradigm relay and Pseudomonas putida KT2440 proteins ground a second, polar-flagellate realization with two stator systems; receptor ligand specificity is not made universal.

Reviews pathway satisfiability, receptor diversity, adaptation proteins, the C-ring output, and the dual-stator realization in KT2440.

The module captures the conserved signal flow rather than any species' complete receptor repertoire. Tar (E. coli) and McpS and PcaY (KT2440) are receptor exemplars, not a claim of shared ligand specificity; enterobacterial-type receptors (PANTHER PTHR43531) and Pseudomonas-type receptors (PTHR32089:SF120) are separate PANTHER families and are modelled as alternative realizers. Lineage differences are real and are recorded here rather than averaged away. (1) Pseudomonas encodes several chemosensory systems (flagellar Che, Che2, the c-di-GMP-linked Wsp and pilus-linked Chp systems); only the flagellar cluster is modelled, its components (CheA2/PP_4338, CheR2, CheB1, CheW PP_4332) are not assumed interchangeable with paralogs of the other systems, and the adjacent CheW-domain protein PP_4333 is not given a separate role. (2) KT2440 CheA also carries a C-terminal CheW-like domain, as all CheA do; free CheW is still needed to couple receptors to CheA. (3) The CheY-P reset uses CheZ in beta- and gammaproteobacteria; Bacillus-type systems instead use CheC, CheX and the FliY CheC-like domain at the switch, together with CheD and CheV, and in B. subtilis attractants activate rather than inhibit CheA. Those alternatives are not grounded here. (4) E. coli and Salmonella drive the motor with a single proton-type MotAB stator; KT2440 and P. aeruginosa have a second MotCD stator, and Vibrio uses sodium-type PomAB stators; the stator step is therefore a realizer variant set. MotA and MotC are grounded at the disjoint PTHR30433 subfamilies SF4 and SF3. PANTHER PTHR30329 is HETEROGENEOUS: it is an OmpA-like peptidoglycan-binding domain family in which only the MotB, PomB, LafU and MotD members are stator anchors (OmpA, Pal and OprF are outer-membrane proteins). MotD is grounded at SF20; MotB stays at family level because its subfamily SF21 mixes MotB with OmpA-type proteins. (5) The CheA-to-CheY His-Asp transfer follows the two-component phosphorelay motif, but CheA is controlled by separate receptors rather than sensing a signal itself, and the module does not declare conformance to the two-component relay template. Flagellar construction and export are modelled separately (bacterial_flagellar_assembly_export).
