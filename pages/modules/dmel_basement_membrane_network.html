Basement membrane laminin and collagen network in Drosophila melanogaster

Basement membranes are sheet-like extracellular matrices beneath epithelia and around muscles and organs. Their two self-assembling networks are made of laminin and type IV collagen. Drosophila has two laminin trimers, each made of one alpha chain (LanA or Wing blister) with the shared beta (LanB1) and gamma (LanB2) chains; laminins polymerise at the cell surface and initiate basement membrane assembly. Type IV collagen trimers of Col4a1 and Viking are made mainly by the fat body and hemocytes and then incorporated into basement membranes, where they form a cross-linked network that gives strength and shapes organs. Multiplexin (the collagen XV/XVIII homolog) and Pericardin (a collagen IV-like protein of the cardiac extracellular matrix) are additional collagens grouped with them.

Boundary. The module joins the FlyBase collagen and laminin groups. Other core basement membrane components, perlecan (trol), nidogen (Ndg) and SPARC, are not in these groups and are not added; the collagen IV cross-linking peroxidasin (Pxn) and the collagen-processing enzymes are also outside the module. The laminin alpha-chain composition is shown as two variants that share LanB1 and LanB2. Pericardin is grouped by FlyBase as a collagen and is found mainly in the cardiac and pericardial matrix rather than in all basement membranes. The connection from laminin to collagen IV summarises the assembly order shown for Drosophila embryos and is not a metabolic step.
