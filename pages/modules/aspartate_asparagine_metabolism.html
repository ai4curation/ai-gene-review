L-aspartate, L-asparagine and L-alanine formation and turnover

Reversible aspartate and alanine transamination connects oxaloacetate and pyruvate to amino-acid metabolism in the sampled fungi, animals and plants. Glutamine-dependent asparagine synthetase amidates aspartate using ATP and glutamine amide nitrogen, releasing glutamate, AMP and diphosphate. Asparaginases instead hydrolyse asparagine to aspartate and ammonium without a glutamate cosubstrate. Cytosolic, mitochondrial and plant plastid aminotransferases provide different implementations; their net flux depends on substrates, compartment and physiological conditions. Plant plastids include both conventional AspAT and a distinct bifunctional aspartate/prephenate aminotransferase. Asparagine hydrolysis is optional at this formation/turnover boundary and uses structurally distinct asparaginase families in the demonstrated yeast and plant examples. Secreted yeast Asp3 is strain dependent. Alanine transamination is a parallel reaction, not an asserted chemical link in the aspartate/asparagine chain. The taxonomic envelope does not imply universal retention, identical compartments or simultaneous flux through every branch.

Boundary: aspartate/asparagine formation and turnover plus parallel alanine transamination. Oxaloacetate/pyruvate supply, membrane transport, ammonium assimilation, the complete malate-aspartate shuttle and downstream aromatic/aspartate-family amino-acid pathways remain adjacent processes. The core requires an AspAT implementation and glutamine-dependent ASNS; hydrolysis and alanine turnover are optional branches. No common compartment is imposed. Plant plastid PAT is included only for AspAT chemistry; ASP5 is not assigned PAT activity. E. coli ansA/ansB are independently reviewed biochemical comparators for Class 1 asparaginases, not eukaryotic participant exemplars: ansB occupies an outer-membrane-bounded periplasm, not the fungal cell-wall-bounded compartment. The three cached YeastPathways imports place AAT1 and, in the degradation model, ASP3 in cytosol; target evidence governs their compartments here. Pyruvate carboxylase is upstream substrate supply at this chosen boundary; its broader GO process annotation is not declared erroneous for that reason. PomBase maa1 and caa1 retain reversible AspAT chemistry, but their prevalent physiological directions differ in PMID:39502420. Alt1 matrix localization is a curated inference, not disproved by transit-peptide length alone. Aat2 ribosome/Gcn2 regulation and peroxisomal targeting on oleate remain documented gene-level context outside the cytosolic implementation. ASP3 evolution is qualified by the later synteny study, and four identical S288C copies are not four independent biochemical experiments.
