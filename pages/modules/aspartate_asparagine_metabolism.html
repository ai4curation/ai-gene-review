L-aspartate, L-asparagine and L-alanine formation and turnover

Formation and turnover of the amino acids that lie one step from the central-carbon 2-oxoacids oxaloacetate and pyruvate, in eukaryotes. Aspartate aminotransferase reversibly transaminates oxaloacetate with L-glutamate to give L-aspartate and 2-oxoglutarate. Eukaryotes carry a cytosolic and a mitochondrial isozyme; in budding yeast the cytosolic enzyme supplies most cellular aspartate, whereas the mitochondrial one acts in the matrix. L-aspartate is then amidated by glutamine-dependent asparagine synthetase, which uses ATP and the glutamine amide nitrogen to form L-asparagine and releases glutamate, AMP and diphosphate. Asparaginases hydrolyse L-asparagine back to L-aspartate and ammonium, which lets asparagine serve as a nitrogen source; fungi can have an intracellular asparaginase and, in some lineages, a cell-wall asparaginase regulated by nitrogen availability. The aspartate made by transamination feeds the aspartate family (threonine, methionine, isoleucine), arginine and pyrimidine synthesis, and the malate-aspartate shuttle. In the same way, alanine aminotransferase reversibly transaminates pyruvate with L-glutamate to give L-alanine and 2-oxoglutarate, and so serves both alanine synthesis and alanine use as a nitrogen source. All of these reactions use L-glutamate as amino donor or acceptor and so connect the ammonium-assimilation hub to carbon metabolism.

Boundary and scope decision: the crosswalk folded six single-reaction YeastCyc frames into this module. Aspartate transamination, asparagine synthesis and asparaginase form one connected aspartate/asparagine unit. Alanine aminotransferase (ALANINE-SYN2-PWY, ALANINE-DEG3-PWY) is not chemically connected to it. It is kept here as a parallel part, as in KEGG's grouping of alanine with aspartate metabolism, because it is the equivalent glutamate-linked transamination of the other central-carbon 2-oxoacid (pyruvate). It could be split out if an alanine module is ever made. The title therefore names alanine, while the slug stays as assigned. Oxaloacetate supply by pyruvate carboxylase (ASPBIO-PWY: PYC1 major, PYC2 minor) is upstream and outside the module; note that fungal pyruvate carboxylases are cytosolic, whereas pyruvate_metabolism models the mitochondrial animal enzyme. Aspartate use by the threonine/methionine trunk is aspartate_family_threonine_biosynthesis; the mitochondrial isozyme's shuttle role is malate_aspartate_shuttle; the ammonium and glutamate/glutamine reactions are nitrogen_assimilation_glutamate_glutamine. The Aat2 non-catalytic ribosome/Gcn2 regulatory role (PMID:35621265) and its peroxisomal localisation on oleate (PMID:9288922) are not modelled. YeastCyc assessment: AAT1, AAT2, ASN1, ASN2, ASP1, ASP3-1 to ASP3-4 and ALT1 assignments are correct, and no S. cerevisiae catalyst is missing. YeastCyc correctly omits the inactive, cytosolic ALT2 (PMID:23049841). Compartments YeastCyc does not give are added: AAT1 and ALT1 act in the mitochondrial matrix, AAT2, ASN1/ASN2 and ASP1 in the cytosol, and the secreted ASP3 enzymes in the cell wall-bounded periplasmic space. The ASP3 cluster is strain-specific (PMID:22776361), so the cell-wall asparaginase is a lineage variant, not a general fungal step. Generalisation caveats: bacteria also use an ammonia-dependent asparagine synthetase (AsnA), which is not modelled; the human ASNS sits in a different PANTHER subfamily (PTHR11772:SF36) from the fungal and E. coli AsnB enzymes (SF2), so the family-level id is used. GO-CAM disagreements: (a) all three YeastPathways models put AAT1 in the cytosol, although Aat1 is a mitochondrial-matrix enzyme, and ASPARAGINE-DEG2-PWY puts the cell-wall ASP3 enzymes in the cytosol; (b) ASPBIO-PWY makes PYC1/PYC2 part_of GO:0006532, which is supply rather than aspartate synthesis; (c) none of the YeastPathways models has causal edges; (d) the alanine frames have no YeastPathways GO-CAM, so the alanine part is grounded only in the PomBase model 67c10cc400000148; (e) PomBase 67c10cc400005826 agrees with the module for caa1, maa1 and asn1, and its shuttle carriers, malate dehydrogenases and tRNA ligases are outside it.
