Human DNA mismatch repair — MutSalpha/MutSbeta mismatch recognition, RFC-PCNA-directed MutLalpha incision, EXO1 excision with RPA protection, and POLD resynthesis with LIG1 ligation; MSH2/MSH6/MSH3/MLH1/PMS2/RFC1-5/PCNA/EXO1/HMGB1/RPA1-3/POLD1-4/LIG1

Post-replicative DNA mismatch repair (MMR) removes base-base mismatches and small insertion/deletion loops that escape the proofreading of replicative DNA polymerases. In human cells a mismatch is recognised by one of two MutS homologue heterodimers that share MSH2: MutSalpha (MSH2-MSH6) binds base-base mismatches and one- or two-nucleotide insertion/deletion loops, and MutSbeta (MSH2-MSH3) binds larger insertion/deletion loops and has only a limited role in base-base mismatch repair. MutSalpha is pre-enriched on chromatin through binding of the MSH6 PWWP domain to H3K36me3. In an ATP-dependent step the mismatch-bound MutS complex recruits MutLalpha (MLH1-PMS2). MutLalpha is a latent endonuclease whose active site lies in PMS2; it is activated by the mismatch, MutSalpha, ATP and the PCNA sliding clamp that replication factor C (RFC1-5) loads at the strand break or primer terminus. Because RFC loads PCNA in a fixed orientation, the activated MutLalpha nicks the discontinuous (newly synthesised) strand, which is how the daughter strand is identified. Exonuclease 1 (EXO1), activated by MutSalpha, removes the error-containing tract 5' to 3' from a nick, and excision stops after the mispair is removed through the combined action of MutLalpha and RPA, the heterotrimeric single-stranded DNA-binding protein (RPA1-RPA3) that coats and protects the gap. The chromatin protein HMGB1 stimulates MutSalpha-activated excision. The gap is filled by DNA polymerase delta (POLD1-POLD4) working with PCNA and RFC, and DNA ligase 1 (LIG1) seals the remaining nick. Loss of MSH2, MSH6, MLH1 or PMS2 causes microsatellite instability and Lynch syndrome. Mismatch avoidance by polymerase proofreading, MMR-dependent signalling of DNA damage, meiotic crossover resolution and the specialised mutagenic MMR of antibody genes are outside this module.

Gene reviews exist only for HMGB1 and POLD2. Boundary decisions. (1) MutLbeta (MLH1-PMS1) is context, not a part. It forms in human cells (PMID:10748105), but no repair activity has been shown for it, PMS1 is not needed in the reconstituted reaction, and PMS1 germline variants are not established Lynch syndrome alleles. (2) MutLgamma (MLH1-MLH3) is context, not a part. MLH3 carries the conserved endonuclease motif, and a dominant-negative MLH3 causes microsatellite instability (PMID:10615123), but its established role is meiotic crossover formation, and any somatic MMR role is minor or a backup for MutLalpha; it would be a variant of the incision step only if purified human MutLgamma were shown to support repair. MLH3, PMS1 and PMS2 compete for the same MLH1 region (PMID:11292842). (3) RPA4 is excluded. It is an RPA2 paralogue that forms an alternative RPA complex that does not support DNA replication, and there is no evidence that it acts in MMR; the canonical RPA1-RPA3 heterotrimer is the RPA of the reconstituted reaction (PMID:9430682). (4) HMGB1 is included as an accessory annoton in the excision step, with positive regulation of mismatch repair as its process (accepted in the HMGB1 gene review), because it stimulates MutSalpha-activated excision but is not an essential catalytic participant. (5) EXO1-independent excision is context. Excision from a MutLalpha nick without EXO1, by POLD strand displacement or another nuclease, is described in the literature (PMID:33510387 notes unidentified nucleases) but has no defined human participant set, so it is not modelled as an alternative route and is recorded as a knowledge gap. (6) PCNA and RFC are modelled as one strand-discrimination step that feeds both incision and resynthesis; PCNA is not duplicated in the resynthesis step. (7) Out of scope: polymerase proofreading (DNA replication proofreading), MMR-dependent DNA damage signalling and apoptosis in response to alkylating agents, meiotic mismatch repair and crossover resolution, somatic hypermutation and class-switch recombination (which use MutSalpha, EXO1 and polymerase eta mutagenically), and the MCM9 helicase and WRN, which modulate MMR but are not RGD members. MSH4 and MSH5 are meiotic MutS homologues and are not MMR participants. Related modules. Part 4: excision of the error-containing tract
EXO1 5'-to-3' excision from the nick, stimulated by HMGB1Reactionexo1_excision

EXO1, activated and made processive by MutSalpha, hydrolyses the incised strand 5' to 3' from a nick and past the mismatch. Excision is terminated by MutLalpha and by RPA after the mispair is removed. HMGB1 stimulates MutSalpha-activated excision. EXO1-independent excision, which explains the weak mutator phenotype of EXO1 loss, is recorded in notes as context and not modelled as a route.
