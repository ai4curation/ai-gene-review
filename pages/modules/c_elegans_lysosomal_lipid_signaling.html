Lysosome-to-nucleus lipid signaling in Caenorhabditis elegans

In the Caenorhabditis elegans intestine, lysosomes send lipid signals to the nucleus that adjust fat metabolism and extend lifespan. The TFEB ortholog HLH-30 is a basic helix-loop-helix transcription factor that moves into the nucleus during fasting, after germline loss or when TOR signalling falls. It turns on autophagy and lysosome genes, and with the Max-like factor MXL-3 it links lysosomal lipolysis to nutrient supply. The lysosomal acid lipase LIPL-4, a homolog of human LIPA, is induced in these conditions and hydrolyses lipids at acidic pH inside the lysosome. Its activity raises the levels of omega-6 polyunsaturated fatty acids, which promote autophagy, and of the N-acylethanolamine oleoylethanolamide (OEA). The intestinal fatty acid-binding protein LBP-8 sits on lysosomes and binds OEA and oleic acid with high affinity. When LIPL-4 activity is high, LBP-8 enters the nucleus through an N-terminal nuclear localization signal. There, OEA binds the HNF4-family nuclear receptor NHR-80 directly. NHR-80 works with its partner NHR-49, which does not bind OEA, to switch on genes for fatty acid desaturation (fat-5, fat-6, fat-7) and mitochondrial beta-oxidation (acs-2), as well as lbp-8 itself. The pathway is needed for the long life of animals that overexpress LIPL-4 or LBP-8. It is independent of dietary restriction.

Boundary. This is a concrete C. elegans signalling module with four parts in order: (1) HLH-30/TFEB transcriptional control of the lysosomal and autophagy program, an upstream input that induces lipl-4; (2) LIPL-4 lipolysis in the lysosomal lumen; (3) LBP-8 lipid transfer from lysosome to nucleus; (4) the NHR-80/NHR-49 nuclear receptor pair that sets transcription of desaturase and beta-oxidation genes. Downstream target enzymes (FAT-5/6/7 delta-9 desaturases, ACS-2) are listed as targets but not modelled as annotons, because they are not reviewed and they are a metabolic output rather than part of the signal. The later mitochondrial step (ETC complex II, mtROS, JUN-1; PMID:30713071) is also outside the boundary. Excluded members. NHR-47 was considered and excluded: its gene review finds no published link to lysosomal lipid signalling, OEA or NHR-49/80. NHR-66 (NHR-49 partner for sphingolipid and remodelling genes) and NHR-13 are not part of the OEA axis. DAF-16 and PHA-4 regulate lipl-4 and autophagy genes in germline-less animals, but they belong to germline longevity signalling, not this module. MXL-3 and the NAPE-PLDs NAPE-1/2 appear in the text but have no reviews. Relation to MODULE:metazoan_autophagosome_biogenesis. HLH-30 induces autophagy genes (lgg-1, lgg-2, sqst-1, atg-18), and the omega-6 PUFAs that build up with LIPL-4 activity promote autophagy (PMID:23392608). Autophagy is in turn needed to keep lipase activity high in germline-less animals (PMID:21906946). The autophagy machinery itself is modelled in the autophagosome module. Here it is only the context that HLH-30 and the PUFA branch feed into. OEA source caveat. LIPL-4 is a triacylglycerol lipase, but OEA is made by NAPE-specific phospholipase D, and nape-1 loss only halves the longevity of LIPL-4 or LBP-8 overexpression. The LIPL-4 annoton therefore asserts only lipase activity, and the link from LIPL-4 to OEA is a PROVIDES_INPUT_FOR connection, not a reaction product of LIPL-4.

Part 1: TFEB-family transcriptional control of the lysosomal and autophagy program

Upstream input. HLH-30 induces lipl-4 together with autophagy and lysosome genes. It is not needed for the LBP-8 to NHR-80 relay once LIPL-4 activity has been raised.

HLH-30/TFEB lysosome and autophagy transcriptionRegulatory Stephlh30_lysosomal_program

HLH-30 stays in the cytoplasm in fed animals and moves to the nucleus during starvation, germline loss, low TOR activity and other longevity states. In the nucleus it activates autophagy genes, lysosome genes and lipases. Nuclear entry in germline-less animals needs the Mondo complex MML-1/MXL-2 acting through TOR.

Part 2: lysosomal lipolysis that generates lipid signals
LIPL-4 lysosomal acid lipolysisReactionlipl4_lysosomal_lipolysis

LIPL-4 is targeted to intestinal lysosomes by its signal peptide and hydrolyses lipids at pH 4.5. Overexpressing it in the intestine extends mean lifespan by 55%, but only if it reaches the lysosome. Higher LIPL-4 activity raises OEA and omega-6 C20 fatty acids (arachidonic acid, DGLA).

Part 3: lysosome-to-nucleus lipid transfer
LBP-8 lipid chaperone shuttleTransport Steplbp8_lipid_shuttle

LBP-8 is the only one of nine worm FABPs that is induced by LIPL-4 overexpression. It is mainly on intestinal lysosomes, and more of it moves to the nucleus when LIPL-4 activity is high. LBP-8 binds OEA with about three times the affinity it has for C20 fatty acids. Its 1.3 angstrom structure shows a conserved NLS and the residues that bind ligand. LBP-8 without the NLS stays out of the nucleus and does not extend lifespan.

Part 4: OEA-activated nuclear receptor transcription of lipid metabolism genes
NHR-80/NHR-49 nuclear receptor outputRegulatory Stepnhr_reception

NHR-80 binds OEA directly (Kd about 7.8 uM). NHR-49, its partner, does not bind OEA and is thought to act as a co-factor. Both are needed for longevity driven by LIPL-4 or LBP-8 and for acs-2 induction. The NHR-49/NHR-80 pair controls the delta-9 desaturase genes, NHR-49 promotes beta-oxidation genes, and NHR-80 is specifically needed for germline-loss longevity.
