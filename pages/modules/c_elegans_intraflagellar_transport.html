Intraflagellar transport in Caenorhabditis elegans sensory cilia

Intraflagellar transport (IFT) is the bidirectional, motor-driven movement of large protein trains along the ciliary axoneme that builds and maintains cilia. In Caenorhabditis elegans it operates in the non-motile sensory cilia of about 60 ciliated neurons, chiefly the amphid and phasmid chemosensory neurons. IFT trains are built from two multisubunit complexes: IFT-B (OSM-5/IFT88, OSM-6/IFT52, CHE-13/IFT57, CHE-2/IFT80, DYF-1/IFT70, DYF-3/IFT38, DYF-6/IFT46, DYF-11/IFT54 and DYF-13/IFT56) and IFT-A (CHE-11/IFT140, DAF-10/IFT122 and DYF-2/IFT144). Two kinesin-2 motors drive anterograde transport. Heterotrimeric kinesin-II (KLP-11, KLP-20 and the non-motor subunit KAP-1) imports trains through the ciliary base and transition zone and moves them along the doublet microtubules of the middle segment. The homodimeric kinesin-2 OSM-3 takes over in a handover zone on the proximal axoneme and is the only motor that moves trains along the singlet microtubules of the distal segment to the ciliary tip. The IFT-B subunit DYF-1 is required for OSM-3 to dock onto and move IFT particles. At the tip trains turn around and are returned to the base by IFT-dynein (cytoplasmic dynein-2), whose heavy chain is CHE-3 and light intermediate chain XBX-1. The ciliary MAK/RCK-family kinase DYF-5 regulates motor docking and undocking and phosphorylates the tubulin-binding module of IFT-74, promoting tubulin unloading and limiting cilium length. The BBSome couples IFT-A and IFT-B and regulates IFT assembly at the base and turnaround at the tip. Loss of IFT-B, IFT-A, kinesin-2 or dynein-2 function causes truncated or malformed sensory cilia, failure to take up lipophilic dyes and chemosensory defects.

Boundary. This is a concrete C. elegans module for the IFT machine itself: the IFT-B and IFT-A train complexes, the two anterograde kinesin-2 motors with their middle/distal segment division of labour, the retrograde IFT-dynein, and the DYF-5 kinase that regulates motor behaviour. The ciliary transition zone (MODULE:c_elegans_ciliary_transition_zone) is a separate module; IFT trains pass through it, and IFT and transition-zone proteins act at different steps of ciliogenesis. The DAF-19 RFX transcription factor that drives expression of most of these genes is regulatory context, not a part. Relation to MODULE:bbsome. The BBSome is modelled separately. In worms BBS-7 and BBS-8 hold IFT-A and IFT-B together so that both motors move the same train (in bbs-7/bbs-8 mutants IFT-A is moved by kinesin-II and IFT-B by OSM-3 separately; PMID:16049494), and the BBSome binds anterograde trains in a DYF-2- and BBS-1-dependent manner and is needed for turnaround at the tip (PMID:22922713). Those interactions are recorded as an interface in the IFT-A part and the turnaround connection, not as BBSome membership. Members. All members with a UniProt accession have a completed gene review under genes/worm/. Molecular functions follow those reviews' core functions. Where a review asserts no MF (CHE-2, DYF-3, DYF-6), only a preferred_term is given. KAP-1, the non-motor kinesin-II subunit, has no gene review and two unreviewed UniProt entries (H2KZP1, G5EEE6) for the same gene, so it is named in text without an accession. Further worm IFT subunits without gene reviews (OSM-1/IFT172, IFT-20, IFT-74, IFT-81, IFT-139, IFT-43, IFTA-1) are not modelled as members. The IFT-74/81 tubulin module is mentioned only as the DYF-5 substrate. Ciliary segments. Worm amphid channel cilia have a transition zone, a middle segment of doublet microtubules and a distal segment of singlet microtubules. Segment-level anatomy is described in prose because GO has no terms for ciliary middle or distal segments.

Part 3: anterograde transport by two sequential kinesin-2 motors
Anterograde IFT by kinesin-II and OSM-3Transport Stepanterograde_kinesin2_transport

Two kinesin-2 motors move the same IFT trains toward the ciliary tip. Kinesin-II imports trains through the ciliary base and transition zone and hands them over to OSM-3 on the proximal axoneme. In the middle segment the two motors act together and partly redundantly, and OSM-3 alone moves trains along the distal singlets. The BBSome (BBS-7, BBS-8) is needed to keep both motors on the same train.

Part 1: kinesin-II import and middle-segment transport
Kinesin-II import of IFT trains to the handover zoneTransport Stepkinesin_ii_import

Heterotrimeric kinesin-II (KLP-11, KLP-20, KAP-1) is the slower, less processive motor. It carries trains through the base and transition zone into a handover zone on the proximal axoneme and then turns around and recycles to the base. KLP-11 is unprocessive as a homodimer and gains processivity by heterodimerizing with KLP-20; heterodimerization is also needed to bind KAP-1, the link to cargo.

Cytoplasmic dynein-2 returns IFT trains, kinesin-II and their cargo from the tip to the base. In che-3 mutants retrograde IFT in cilia is blocked while anterograde IFT continues, and IFT proteins accumulate in bulbous cilia. Dynein-2 is itself carried into the cilium, and its ciliary entry needs its tail, the light intermediate chain XBX-1 and IFT-B.

Part 5: kinase regulation of motor docking and cargo unloading
DYF-5 kinase regulation of IFT motorsRegulatory Stepdyf5_regulation

DYF-5 is a ciliary MAK/RCK-family serine/threonine kinase. In dyf-5 loss-of-function mutants cilia are elongated, IFT proteins accumulate distally, kinesin-II is no longer confined to the middle segment and OSM-3 moves slowly without attached IFT particles. DYF-5 phosphorylates the tubulin-binding module of IFT-74, lowering IFT-74/81 affinity for tubulin and promoting tubulin unloading at the tip.
