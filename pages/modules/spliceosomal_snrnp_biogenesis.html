Human Sm-class spliceosomal snRNP biogenesis — PRMT5-WDR77 methylosome symmetric dimethylation and pICln chaperoning of Sm proteins, SMN-Gemin complex loading of the Sm ring onto snRNA, TGS1 cap hypermethylation, snurportin-importin beta nuclear import, and Cajal body maturation

The U1, U2, U4 and U5 small nuclear ribonucleoproteins (snRNPs) of the major spliceosome, and U11, U12 and U4atac of the minor spliceosome, each contain a ring of seven Sm proteins (SmB/B', SmD1, SmD2, SmD3, SmE, SmF and SmG) bound to the Sm site of the small nuclear RNA (snRNA). In vertebrate cells this ring is not allowed to form spontaneously. After transcription by RNA polymerase II, the capped snRNA precursor is exported to the cytoplasm. There, newly made Sm proteins are first taken up by the PRMT5 complex (methylosome): PRMT5, with its obligate WD-repeat partner WDR77 (MEP50), symmetrically dimethylates arginines in the RG-rich C-terminal tails of SmB/B', SmD1 and SmD3, and the adaptor pICln (CLNS1A) holds Sm proteins as a 6S ring-like intermediate that cannot bind RNA. The SMN complex (SMN, Gemin2 to Gemin8 and STRAP/unrip) then takes over the Sm proteins, releases pICln, and, with Gemin5 selecting the snRNA, closes the Sm ring on the Sm site in an ATP-dependent reaction that ensures Sm cores form only on correct RNAs. The trimethylguanosine synthase TGS1, recruited by SMN, then converts the snRNA 7-methylguanosine cap to a 2,2,7-trimethylguanosine (m3G) cap with two methyl groups from S-adenosyl-L-methionine, and the 3' end is trimmed. The m3G cap and the Sm core together form the nuclear import signal: snurportin-1 binds the m3G cap and, through its importin-beta-binding domain, docks importin beta, which carries the snRNP core, still bound to the SMN complex, through the nuclear pore. In the nucleus the new snRNPs pass through Cajal bodies, where small Cajal body-specific RNPs guide 2'-O-methylation and pseudouridylation of the snRNA and particle-specific proteins join before the snRNPs enter splicing.

Context, not a catalytic step. Coilin carries IDA rows for Cajal body localisation (PMID:16687569 and others) but no experimental molecular function other than identical protein binding, and no experimental process row for snRNP assembly, so it is a located participant without a function or process. Coilin depletion prevents U snRNPs from condensing into Cajal bodies, and loss of SMN, TGS1 or PHAX disperses Cajal bodies, so the body depends on ongoing snRNP biogenesis; whether it is required for snRNP maturation is not established here, which is why the part is optional.
