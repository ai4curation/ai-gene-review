NLRP3 inflammasome activation and pyroptotic execution module

A decomposition of the canonical NLRP3 inflammasome: the cytosolic sensor-adaptor-protease platform that converts a diffuse cellular perturbation into active caspase-1, and the lytic execution arm that caspase-1 then triggers. The chain is linear and each hand-off is a change of molecular currency rather than of chemistry: NLRP3 senses a perturbation indirectly and presents a pyrin-domain filament (GO:0044546), that filament nucleates ASC polymerisation into the one-per-cell speck (GO:0061703), the clustered ASC CARDs recruit procaspase-1 and permit proximity-induced autoprocessing (GO:0061702), the autoprocessed protease cleaves gasdermin D and the interleukin-1-family precursors, the released GSDMD N-terminal fragment oligomerises into wide plasma-membrane pores (GO:0141201), and NINJ1 finally ruptures the membrane. Four of the five steps contribute no catalysis at all - NLRP3, ASC and NEK7 all supply geometry - so the module is best read as a geometry cascade with a single proteolytic step in the middle and two independent membrane-acting effectors at the end. Design intent: one ordered chain of parts, like necroptosis and unlike the redundant-suppressor topology of ferroptosis, because there is no second route from the speck to the pore. The two places where the biology is genuinely alternative are handled differently from each other. NEK7 licensing is an optional regulatory part rather than a core step, because human myeloid cells can activate NLRP3 without it when IKKbeta has recruited NLRP3 to PI4P (PMID:36384135) and because raising NLRP3 abundance makes potassium-efflux-driven activation independent of both NEK7 and priming (PMID:42378282). Interleukin-1 beta and interleukin-18 are recorded as caspase-1 substrates inside the cleavage step and are deliberately given no annoton of their own: they are what the protease acts on, they perform none of the work of their own maturation, and their cytokine activity belongs to the receptor pathways downstream of this module.

Boundary decisions. (1) Scope is the canonical NLRP3 inflammasome and the pyroptotic execution it triggers: sensor activation, adaptor polymerisation, caspase-1 autoactivation, caspase-1 substrate cleavage, GSDMD pore formation, NINJ1 membrane rupture. Signal 1 (priming) is explicitly out of the core. NF-kappaB-driven transcriptional upregulation of NLRP3 and IL1B is reached by reference to modules/nfkb_canonical_signaling.yaml, and the receptor biology that generates that signal belongs to modules/tlr4_md2_lps_signaling.yaml and modules/tnf_signaling.yaml. Priming does real mechanistic work - it raises NLRP3 abundance and, through IKKbeta, moves NLRP3 into a PI4P-bound primed state - so it is modelled as context connected to those modules, not as a part here, and PMID:42378282 is cited for abundance substituting for it. (2) Other inflammasomes and other routes to a gasdermin pore are out of scope by the same logic. The non-canonical CASP4/CASP5 route to GSDMD (reviewed in genes/human/CASP4) reaches the same execution arm without NLRP3, ASC or caspase-1; NLRC4, NLRP1, AIM2, CARD8 and NLRP6 are sibling sensors on the same ASC/caspase-1 core; granzyme-driven GSDME cleavage is a third route into GO:0141201. None of them is modelled here. Those shared downstream parts (caspase-1 autoactivation onwards) are the natural factoring point if this module is later generalised to a canonical-inflammasome module with a sensor variant set; modules/nlr_signaling.yaml is the existing, much coarser sketch of the sensor layer and is not superseded by this module. (3) NEK7 is an optional licensing part, not an obligate core step, and this is the module's main judgement call. The case for including it is strong: NEK7 acts downstream of potassium efflux, is a stoichiometric subunit of the active disc, bridges adjacent NLRP3 LRRs, and is required in mouse macrophages in vivo. The case against making it obligatory is specific and human: when IKKbeta recruits NLRP3 to PI4P, NEK7 is redundant for inflammasome formation, and that IKKbeta-mediated route is reported as the predominant priming mechanism in human myeloid cells (PMID:36384135); raising NLRP3 abundance likewise renders potassium-efflux-driven activation independent of both NEK7 and priming (PMID:42378282). A part whose loss can be fully compensated in the organism the module is grounded in is regulation, so NEK7 carries optional: true and a POSITIVELY_REGULATES edge onto the sensor step rather than sitting in the chain. Its activity is asserted as GO:0140677 molecular function activator activity, not as a kinase activity: kinase-dead NEK7 rescues inflammasome activation. The local PAINT row for NEK7 in PTHR44329 propagates GO:0004674 protein serine/threonine kinase activity, which is the mitotic function and not this one, so it is deliberately not cited as support for the licensing role. (4) IL-1 beta and IL-18 are substrates, not participants. They are named in the substrates list of the caspase-1 cleavage annoton and nowhere else: the proteolysis is performed entirely by caspase-1, the precursors contribute no residue, no positioning and no chemistry to their own cleavage, and being required for the output is not the same as doing any of its work. Their cytokine activity (GO:0005125 in the IL-1 and IL-18 receptor pathways, as modelled in the cached GO-CAM) is downstream biology outside this boundary. The one thing the module does assert about them is a property of the pore, not of the cytokines: the GSDMD conduit favours the mature forms over their precursors (PMID:33883744), which is why cytokine release is placed on the pore annoton. Neither gene has a review under genes/human/, which is a further reason not to assert activities for them here. (5) Two membrane-acting effectors, two different acts. GSDMD-NT forms a defined wide pore and is asserted with GO:0022829, following the gene review; NINJ1 is asserted with GO:0140912 membrane destabilizing activity, the same term the necroptosis module uses for MLKL, and its description is phrased to match - both are lytic effectors that destabilise the bilayer rather than forming a gated channel. Note that GSDMD pore formation and NINJ1 rupture are not alternatives: the pore comes first and is survivable, rupture is terminal. (6) No module-level deep research exists for this module. No provider run was available when it was curated (the Falcon endpoint was unavailable), so there is deliberately no nlrp3_inflammasome-deep-research-<provider>.md file. Every structural claim is grounded in the six per-gene reviews under genes/human/, cached publications under publications/, local PAINT rows under interpro/panther/, and the two cached production GO-CAMs named in evidence. (7) Taxon scope is mammalian, represented with human gene products, although the GO terms used carry an in-taxon Vertebrata constraint and the architecture is broader: the cached zebrafish GO-CAM gomodel:60418ffa00001019 models an NLRP3 inflammasome driving pyroptosis through caspase-a/caspase-b and gasdermin Eb, i.e. through non-orthologous caspases and gasdermins. The module is therefore not asserted for vertebrates as a whole, since NEK7 licensing, the GSDMD step and NINJ1 are all characterised in mammals.

NLRP3 sensor activation and platform formationRegulatory Stepnlrp3_sensor_activation

NLRP3 responds to a wide range of stimuli without binding any of them. A polybasic region binds PI4P on the dispersed trans-Golgi network, the dispersed network and the pericentriolar material concentrate NLRP3 into condensates, and the NACHT domain converts from an ADP-bound autoinhibited oligomer to an ATP-bound active disc whose N-terminal pyrin domains stack into a filament. The output of this part is that filament; nothing here is catalytic except the ATPase that drives the conformational switch.

Part 2: NEK7 licensing of NLRP3 - non-catalytic, compensable regulation of sensor activation rather than a step in the chain (optional)
NEK7 licensing of NLRP3 (optional)Regulatory Stepnek7_licensing

Acting downstream of potassium efflux and at the microtubule organizing centre, NEK7 binds the NLRP3 leucine-rich repeat, opens the autoinhibited cage and bridges adjacent NLRP3 subunits in the assembled disc, of which it is a stoichiometric subunit. The role is kinase-independent. It is modelled as optional regulation because human myeloid cells can dispense with it: IKKbeta-driven recruitment of NLRP3 to PI4P makes NEK7 redundant and is reported as the predominant human priming route, and raising NLRP3 abundance makes activation independent of both NEK7 and priming.
