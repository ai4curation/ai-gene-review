Vertebrate Wnt/planar cell polarity (PCP) signalling - Wnt5a/Wnt11, ROR2/PTK7, core PCP complexes and Rho/Rac effector arms

Planar cell polarity (PCP) orients cells within the plane of a tissue. It is needed for convergent extension during gastrulation, neural tube closure, orientation of inner-ear hair-cell bundles and hair follicles, and limb elongation. In vertebrates the system is set up by Wnt ligands, chiefly Wnt5a and Wnt11, which do not stabilize beta-catenin in most contexts. They act through Frizzled receptors with the ROR2 and PTK7 co-receptors and induce dose-dependent phosphorylation of the four-pass membrane protein VANGL2. The core PCP proteins form two mutually exclusive membrane complexes at opposite sides of each cell. One is Frizzled with Dishevelled, recruited through its DEP domain to the Frizzled tail. The other is VANGL with Prickle, which binds Dishevelled and antagonizes its recruitment by Frizzled. The atypical cadherin CELSR1 forms homophilic bridges between neighbouring cells and recruits both sides to the junction. Dishevelled drives two effector arms: the formin DAAM1 links it to RhoA and Rho kinase for actomyosin contractility, and Rac1 activates JNK. Together they turn polarity cues into oriented cytoskeletal behaviour and cell movement. The pathway works independently of the beta-catenin destruction complex and TCF/LEF transcription.

Part 5: asymmetric core PCP complexes
Core PCP asymmetric membrane complexesBiological Processpcp_core_complexes

The core PCP proteins sort into two mutually exclusive membrane complexes at opposite sides of each cell, held across cell junctions by CELSR homodimers. Frizzled with Dishevelled (with Diego/ANKRD6, not modelled) occupies one side. VANGL with Prickle occupies the other, and Prickle antagonizes Frizzled-dependent recruitment of Dishevelled, which sharpens the asymmetry.

Dishevelled activates RhoA and Rac1 in parallel and independently. RhoA activation through the formin DAAM1 drives Rho kinase and actomyosin contraction. Rac1 activation drives JNK. Both arms are needed for vertebrate gastrulation movements. Neither affects beta-catenin signalling.
