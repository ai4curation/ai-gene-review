Nascent polypeptide-associated complex in Drosophila melanogaster

The nascent polypeptide-associated complex (NAC) is a heterodimer of an alpha and a beta subunit that binds near the exit tunnel of translating ribosomes and contacts emerging nascent chains. It acts as a ribosome-associated chaperone and sorting factor that shields nascent polypeptides and regulates their access to the signal recognition particle and to other targeting pathways. Drosophila has two alpha-subunit genes (Nacalpha and gNacalpha) and an expanded family of beta-subunit (BTF3-like) genes: bicaudal (bic), uncNacbeta and a cluster of betaNACtes genes, the latter expressed in the male germline.
