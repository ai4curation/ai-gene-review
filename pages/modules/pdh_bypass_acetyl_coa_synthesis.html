Pyruvate dehydrogenase bypass and acetate-dependent acetyl-CoA synthesis

The pyruvate dehydrogenase (PDH) bypass makes acetyl-CoA in the cytosol without the mitochondrial PDH complex. Acetaldehyde is oxidised by an aldehyde dehydrogenase to acetate, and acetate is ligated to coenzyme A by an AMP-forming acetyl-CoA synthetase. The acetaldehyde comes either from pyruvate decarboxylase during growth on sugars or from alcohol dehydrogenase oxidising ethanol during growth on ethanol. Acetyl-CoA cannot cross organelle membranes, and some fungi, including budding yeast, lack ATP-citrate lyase. In those organisms this route is the main or only source of nucleocytosolic acetyl-CoA for fatty acid and sterol synthesis, lysine synthesis and histone acetylation, and it is essential. The same three reactions carry ethanol and acetate into central metabolism (via the glyoxylate cycle) after the diauxic shift. The acetate-activation step is the conserved acetyl-CoA synthetase found from bacteria to mammals.

cytosolGO:0005829

Boundary. The module covers acetaldehyde formation (from pyruvate or ethanol), its oxidation to acetate, and acetate activation. The mitochondrial PDH complex (modules/pyruvate_metabolism.yaml), ATP-citrate lyase (animals, plants, oleaginous yeasts; absent from budding yeast), the bacterial acetate kinase / phosphotransacetylase route and the carnitine shuttle are other acetyl-CoA routes, outside this module. Alcoholic fermentation (modules/alcoholic_fermentation.yaml) shares the pyruvate decarboxylase step but reduces, rather than oxidises, the acetaldehyde. YeastCyc corrections. (1) PWY3O-4300 lists ADH1-ADH5 for ethanol oxidation; only ADH2 is the ethanol-oxidising isozyme. ADH1 is fermentative and ADH3 is a mitochondrial shuttle enzyme. (2) The PWY3O-4300 acetaldehyde dehydrogenase reaction (RXN66-3) has no gene in the frame. The GO-CAM assigns it to ALD2 with GO:0008774 acetaldehyde dehydrogenase (acetylating, NAD+) activity. That is wrong: yeast ALDs are non-acylating, ALD2/ALD3 are stress-induced enzymes with no role in acetate formation (PMID:15256563), and Ald6 plus Ald4 are needed for growth on ethanol (PMID:9675847). (3) In GLUCFERMEN-PWY and its GO-CAM, ALD4 and ALD5 are placed in the cytosol, but both are mitochondrial isozymes. Not grounded in fission yeast. No PomBase GO-CAM is linked to this group. The PomBase fermentation model types atd1 (SPAC9E9.09c) as acetaldehyde dehydrogenase (NAD+) in pyruvate fermentation to acetate; it is a candidate fission-yeast member, but its compartment and role in acetyl-CoA supply were not checked here.
