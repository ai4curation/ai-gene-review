Glycine biosynthesis in Drosophila melanogaster

Glycine can be made by two routes. From threonine, mitochondrial L-threonine 3-dehydrogenase (Tdh) oxidizes threonine to 2-amino-3-ketobutyrate, which 2-amino-3-ketobutyrate CoA ligase (Gcat) cleaves with coenzyme A into glycine and acetyl-CoA. From serine, serine hydroxymethyltransferase (Shmt) converts serine and tetrahydrofolate to glycine and 5,10-methylenetetrahydrofolate, linking glycine supply to one-carbon metabolism. Both reactions are reversible or bidirectional in context, so the enzymes also contribute to glycine and serine interconversion.
