L-carnitine biosynthesis in Drosophila melanogaster

L-carnitine carries long-chain fatty acids into mitochondria for beta-oxidation. In animals it is made from N6,N6,N6-trimethyllysine, released by degradation of proteins carrying methylated lysines, in four steps. Trimethyllysine dioxygenase (Tmlh) hydroxylates trimethyllysine to 3-hydroxy-trimethyllysine; an aldolase cleaves this to 4-trimethylaminobutyraldehyde and glycine; an aldehyde dehydrogenase oxidizes the aldehyde to gamma-butyrobetaine; and gamma-butyrobetaine dioxygenase hydroxylates gamma-butyrobetaine to carnitine. In Drosophila, CG10814 is the homolog of gamma-butyrobetaine dioxygenase, and the aldolase step is attributed to the threonine-aldolase-like protein CG10184 and to serine hydroxymethyltransferase (Shmt), which in mammals has hydroxytrimethyllysine aldolase activity.
