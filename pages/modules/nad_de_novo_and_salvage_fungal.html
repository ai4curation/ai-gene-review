Fungal NAD+ biosynthesis from tryptophan and pyridine salvage

NAD+ supply network of yeasts and other fungi, in which several precursor routes converge on nicotinate mononucleotide (NaMN) or nicotinamide mononucleotide (NMN) and then share the final adenylylation and amidation steps. De novo synthesis uses the kynurenine pathway: an indoleamine 2,3-dioxygenase-family enzyme opens L-tryptophan to N-formylkynurenine, a kynurenine formamidase releases kynurenine, kynurenine 3-monooxygenase and kynureninase give 3-hydroxyanthranilate, 3-hydroxyanthranilate 3,4-dioxygenase opens the ring to 2-amino-3-carboxymuconate semialdehyde, which cyclizes non-enzymatically to quinolinate, and quinolinate phosphoribosyltransferase makes NaMN. Fungi lack the aspartate-derived (NadA/NadB) de novo route. In the Preiss-Handler salvage route, nicotinamide released by NAD+-consuming enzymes is deamidated by a nicotinamidase and the resulting nicotinate is converted to NaMN by nicotinate phosphoribosyltransferase. Nicotinamide riboside and nicotinic acid riboside are either phosphorylated by nicotinamide riboside kinase (to NMN or NaMN) or cleaved to the free bases by nucleosidases and nucleoside phosphorylases, which then enter the Preiss-Handler route. NaMN is adenylylated to nicotinate adenine dinucleotide (NaAD) by NMN/NaMN adenylyltransferases and amidated to NAD+ by a glutamine-dependent NAD+ synthetase, whereas NMN is adenylylated directly to NAD+. The de novo route requires oxygen, so fermenting yeasts depend on salvage under anaerobic conditions. Precursor uptake, NAD+-consuming reactions such as sirtuin deacetylation, and NADP+ formation are outside the module.

Ten YeastCyc frames (PWY3O-4158, NADSYN-PWY, TRYPTOPHAN-DEGRADATION-1, PWY-5651, PWY-5653, PWY3O-4106, PWY3O-4107, PWY3O-96, PWY3O-224, PWY3O-236) describe one convergent network, so they are modeled as four precursor-route variants feeding shared NaMN adenylylation, NAD+ synthetase and NMN adenylylation steps. The human MODULE:kynurenine_nad_de_novo covers the same de novo chemistry with TDO2/IDO1 and an ACMSD branch, and MODULE:nadp_synthesis_and_nr_salvage covers human NR kinases; the aspartate de novo route of MODULE:nad_biosynthesis_salvage is absent from fungi. YeastCyc corrections: BNA3 is a kynurenine aminotransferase, not the formamidase (PMID:18205391), and is excluded; SIR2 is an NAD+ consumer that releases nicotinamide and is excluded; POF1 (NMN-specific adenylyltransferase, PMID:24759102) is added; MEU1 is noted but not modeled. Compartments: all YeastPathways and PomBase activities are placed in the cytosol; Npt1 and Nma2 are also nuclear (PMID:11884393) and UniProt places Bna4 in the mitochondrial outer membrane, so no module-level compartment is asserted. Precursor transporters (nicotinate and nicotinamide riboside permeases) and the NMN/IMP nucleotidases that regenerate nicotinamide riboside are outside the boundary. GO-CAM disagreements: YeastPathways types URH1 as uridine nucleosidase and PNP1 as purine-nucleoside phosphorylase for the riboside reactions, and assigns arylformamidase activity to BNA3; PomBase 698e557b00000821 types the S. pombe PNP (SPAC1805.16c) as a nicotinamide riboside hydrolase and includes transporters and a thiazole synthase outside this module. S. pombe has no kynurenine-route genes in the PomBase model and is represented only in the salvage variants.
