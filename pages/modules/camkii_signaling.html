CaMKII signaling — calcium/calmodulin activation of the CaMKII holoenzyme, Thr286 autophosphorylation and autonomy, GluN2B docking, CaMKIIN inhibition, and phosphatase termination

Calcium/calmodulin-dependent protein kinase II (CaMKII) is a dodecameric serine/threonine kinase assembled from four paralogous subunits (CaMKII alpha, beta, gamma and delta) that converts transient rises in cytosolic calcium into sustained phosphorylation of target proteins. Calcium-bound calmodulin binds the regulatory segment of each subunit and displaces the autoinhibitory region from the kinase domain. Neighbouring subunits in the holoenzyme then phosphorylate one another on the regulatory threonine (Thr286 in alpha, Thr287 in the other paralogs), which prevents the autoinhibitory region from rebinding and gives Ca2+/calmodulin-independent (autonomous) activity that outlasts the calcium signal. In neurons the activated kinase translocates to the postsynaptic density, where binding to the GluN2B subunit of the NMDA receptor anchors it and itself sustains autonomous activity; there CaMKII phosphorylates AMPA receptor subunits and the auxiliary subunit stargazin during long-term potentiation. In cardiac myocytes CaMKII delta phosphorylates L-type calcium channel subunits, the ryanodine receptor RyR2, phospholamban and the sodium channel Nav1.5, and in the nucleus the class IIa histone deacetylase HDAC4. Activity is limited by the endogenous inhibitor proteins CaMKIIN alpha and beta, which bind only the activated kinase, and is terminated by protein phosphatases (PP1, PP2A and the CaMK phosphatase PPM1F) that dephosphorylate Thr286.

The module uses GO:0004722 for it instead. Calmodulin. CALM1, CALM2 and CALM3 encode an identical protein and are one family participant. Calmodulin is the activator, not a substrate: Ca2+-calmodulin binding displaces the autoinhibitory segment. The upstream calcium sources (NMDA receptor and voltage-gated calcium channel influx, IP3 receptor and ryanodine receptor release; see modules/gpcr_plc_calcium_pkc_signaling.yaml calcium_mobilization) are context, not parts. Kinase variants. The four CaMKII genes are a ONE_OR_MORE variant set along the axis of paralog and tissue: alpha and beta dominate in brain (and form heteromers), delta in heart, gamma is broadly expressed. Thr286/287 autophosphorylation (GO:1990443, a CAMK2A review core function) is placed on every paralog because the regulatory segment and its three phosphosites are invariant (PW:0001185 record text). Inhibitory Thr305/306 phosphorylation and its role in long-term depression, and ROS-driven methionine oxidation as a second autonomy route (reversed by methionine sulfoxide reductases), are recorded in prose only. Substrates are targets, not participants. The RGD substrates (GRIA1, CACNG2 for the neuronal paralogs; CACNA1C, CACNB2, RYR2, SCN5A, PLN, HDAC4 and HDAC5 for CaMKII delta) are listed as targets of the kinase function, following the substrate-versus-participant rule: the kinase performs the step, and the channels and transcriptional repressors are what it acts on. Their own activities (channel conduction, deacetylation, MEF2 repression), long-term potentiation, excitation-contraction coupling and excitation-transcription coupling are downstream outputs and outside the module. PLN is on the RGD human gene list though not drawn; it is included as a target because the record text names it as a CaMKII substrate. Anchoring. GRIN2B is the one anchoring partner modelled, because RGD draws it and its binding both targets the kinase to the synapse and generates autonomous activity (PMID:11459059). Its annoton keeps its experimental molecular function, NMDA glutamate receptor activity, because no experimental GOA row records the CaMKII-binding activity itself; the docking is described in the role description and the connection, not asserted as a GO function. GRIN1, the obligatory NMDA receptor partner, and GRIN2A are on the RGD human list but not drawn, and are mentioned here only. The other RGD "Camk2 partners" (LRRC7/densin-180, DLG1, SYNGAP1) are left out: the record text says their functional role is less well established, and SYNGAP1 is itself a CaMKII substrate. Regulation. CAMK2N1 and CAMK2N2 inhibit only the activated kinase (PMID:9724800); they are an optional regulatory part. Termination is by phosphatases that remove the Thr286 phosphate: PSD PP1 (PMID:11502252; PPP1CC is the RGD gene), cytosolic PP2A (PMID:16516168), and the CaMK phosphatase PPM1F (PMID:11559703). PPM1E (CaMKP-N), also on the RGD list, acts mainly on nuclear CaMKI/CaMKIV and is mentioned only. Calcineurin acts only indirectly, through inhibitor-1 and PP1, and is not a member. GO-CAM grounding. The calmodulin activator and CAMK2A kinase annotons are linked to the human model gomodel:62b4ffe300004489, where CALM1 kinase activator activity directly positively regulates CAMK2A calcium/calmodulin-dependent protein kinase activity in an interferon-gamma context (the seed's best_gocam lead; the activities match even though the biological context differs). The GRIN2B annoton is linked to the NMDA receptor model gomodel:66c7d41500000672. No cached GO-CAM models CaMKII paralogs beta, gamma or delta, CaMKIIN, or CaMKII dephosphorylation. CAMK2B, CAMK2G, CAMK2D, CAMK2N1, CAMK2N2, GRIN2B, PPP1CC and PPM1F have no gene review yet; that is curation follow-up for the project plan.
