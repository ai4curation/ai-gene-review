Ubiquinone (coenzyme Q) biosynthesis in bacteria and mitochondria

De novo biosynthesis of ubiquinone (coenzyme Q) from 4-hydroxybenzoate and a long-chain all-trans-polyprenyl diphosphate in bacteria and in the mitochondria of eukaryotes. A dedicated trans-prenyltransferase sets the tail length (homomeric IspB or Coq1, or a heterotetrameric Dps1/Dlp1 or PDSS1/PDSS2 enzyme). The aromatic head group comes from chorismate via UbiC in bacteria, and from tyrosine in fungi, where aminotransferases form 4-hydroxyphenylpyruvate and an aldehyde dehydrogenase (Hfd1) oxidizes 4-hydroxybenzaldehyde to 4-hydroxybenzoate. UbiA-family prenyltransferases (UbiA in the bacterial membrane, Coq2 in the mitochondrial inner membrane) attach the tail. The ring then undergoes one decarboxylation, three hydroxylations, two O-methylations and one C-methylation, in lineage-specific order: bacteria decarboxylate first with prFMN-dependent UbiD (cofactor from UbiX) and use UbiI/UbiH/UbiF or Coq7 hydroxylases, whereas the mitochondrial CoQ synthome hydroxylates C5 with Coq6, O-methylates with Coq3, performs a C1 oxidative decarboxylation with Coq4, then C-methylates (Coq5), hydroxylates C6 (Coq7) and O-methylates again (Coq3). Accessory factors (UbiJ, UbiK and UbiB in bacteria; Coq8, Coq9 and the mitochondrial ferredoxin system in eukaryotes) organize the hydrophobic intermediates or supply electrons. Oxygen-independent UbiT/UbiU/UbiV implementations, C5-C20 prenyl diphosphate synthesis, and coenzyme Q transport and use are outside the module.

This module is intentionally narrower than KEGG map00130: it represents aerobic ubiquinone biosynthesis, not all terpenoid quinones. Homogentisate production, generic NAD(P)H:quinone oxidoreductases, acyl-CoA thioesterases, menaquinone synthesis, and downstream quinone use are outside the boundary. GENERALIZE with YeastPathways (2026-10): the module was broadened from a bacterial pathway to bacteria plus mitochondria rather than creating a separate eukaryotic sibling, because the tail synthase, the UbiA/Coq2 prenyltransferase, the UbiG/Coq3 and UbiE/Coq5 methyltransferases, the Coq6/UbiI-type flavin hydroxylase and the Coq7 hydroxylase are homologous across the two lineages; only the head-group supply, the decarboxylase and the reaction order differ. Those differences are modeled as exclusive lineage variants at the head-group, prenylation (compartment), ring-modification and accessory levels, so the bacterial content is kept intact inside the bacterial variants. The human-scoped MODULE:coenzyme_q10_biosynthesis remains a concrete human instance of the eukaryotic variant. The chain-length-determining polyprenyl diphosphate synthase (Coq1, IspB, Dps1/Dlp1, PDSS1/PDSS2) is now an optional part, because MODULE:isoprenoid_diphosphate_biosynthesis stops at GGPP and defers Coq1 here; ERG20 and BTS1 stay in that module. YeastCyc corrections: COQ4 fills the unassigned decarboxylation (RXN3O-73) and also performs the C1 hydroxylation that YeastCyc assigns to COQ6 (RXN3O-12) (PMID:38295803); CAT5 is COQ7; COQ8, COQ9 and the YAH1/ARH1 ferredoxin system are missing from the YeastCyc gene list; the coumarate route of PWY3O-1109 has no genes and is replaced by the Aro8/Aro9 and Hfd1 steps (PMID:27693056). All YeastPathways activities are placed in the cytosol, whereas the CoQ enzymes are mitochondrial. (PMID:38295803) assign both C1 reactions to COQ4; it also includes coq10 (CoQ binding after synthesis), coq11, coq12 and a ptc7 phosphatase that are not modeled as annotons here.

The bacterial UbiD/UbiX decarboxylase system is not used (PMID:38295803).
