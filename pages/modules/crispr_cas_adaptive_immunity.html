CRISPR-Cas adaptive immunity

A reusable prokaryotic module for RNA-guided adaptive immunity against mobile genetic elements. The module has three obligate stages: adaptation, in which a Cas1-Cas2 integrase captures a protospacer from invading DNA and integrates it as a new spacer at the leader-proximal end of a CRISPR array; crRNA biogenesis, in which the transcribed array is processed into mature guides; and interference, in which a crRNA-loaded effector recognises a complementary target and destroys it. Effector architecture is the principal axis of variation and is modelled as alternative implementations rather than as separate modules: multi-subunit class 1 effectors (Cascade-Cas3, Csm/Cmr-Cas10) and single-protein class 2 effectors (Cas9, Cas12a, Cas13a) occupy the same structural slot in the pathway. Phage-encoded suppression of this module is a separate counter-defence module (MODULE:anti_crispr_suppression); host restriction-modification, BREX, retron, and other innate defence systems are outside the boundary, as is the laboratory use of Cas effectors as genome editors.

Three design decisions are worth recording. First, the effector variants are variants rather than sibling modules because they are interchangeable realisations of one pathway slot; a genome may carry several systems, so selection is ONE_OR_MORE rather than EXACTLY_ONE. Second, primed acquisition is modelled as a connection from interference back to adaptation rather than as a fourth part, since it reuses the same Cas1-Cas2 machinery. Third, GO has no molecular-function term for crRNA-guided target recognition, so surveillance complexes carry nucleic-acid-binding terms and the gap is recorded in knowledge_gaps rather than papered over with an ill-fitting id. Exemplars are drawn from the two best-characterised concrete systems -- Escherichia coli K-12 type I-E (Cas1 Q46896 through Cas3 P38036) and Pseudomonas aeruginosa PA14 type I-F (the Csy complex) -- plus the reviewed class 2 and type III effectors; they orient the families and are not a claim that the module is confined to those organisms.

Part 2: crRNA biogenesis
crRNA biogenesisBiological Processcrrna_biogenesis

Conversion of the primary CRISPR array transcript into mature, repeat-derived guides that license the effector. The processing chemistry differs by system type, which is the variant axis here.
