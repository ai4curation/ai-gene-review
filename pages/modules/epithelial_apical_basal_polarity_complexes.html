Human epithelial apical-basal polarity complexes — the CDC42-activated PAR complex (PARD3, PARD6A/B/G, PRKCI/PRKCZ), the Crumbs complex (CRB3, PALS1, PATJ) and the basolateral Scribble module (SCRIB, DLG1, LLGL1/LLGL2), linked by mutual antagonism between aPKC and LGL

Epithelial cells divide their plasma membrane into an apical domain and a basolateral domain separated by the apical junctional complex. Three conserved groups of polarity proteins set up and maintain this boundary. The PAR complex consists of the multi-PDZ scaffold PAR3 (PARD3), a PAR6 adaptor (PARD6A, PARD6B or PARD6G) and an atypical protein kinase C (PRKCI or PRKCZ). PAR6 binds aPKC through its PB1 domain, PAR3 through its PDZ domain and GTP-bound CDC42 through its semi-CRIB motif, so CDC42-GTP recruits and activates the complex at the apical cortex and tight junction. The Crumbs complex is formed by the short transmembrane protein CRB3, the MAGUK PALS1 and the multi-PDZ protein PATJ; it marks the apical membrane and tight junction, and CRB3 and PALS1 bind PAR6 and so tie the two apical complexes together. The Scribble module comprises the LRR-PDZ scaffold SCRIB, the MAGUK DLG1 and the LGL proteins LLGL1 and LLGL2 at the basolateral membrane. The apical and basolateral groups antagonise each other: aPKC in the PAR6-aPKC complex phosphorylates LGL, which removes LGL from the apical cortex, and LGL in turn competes with PAR3 for PAR6-aPKC and suppresses the activity of the apical complex. This mutual exclusion positions the apical-basolateral boundary and the tight junction.

Several cited papers used mammalian non-human systems (MDCK cells, mouse brain); each evidence statement names the system, and human-specific evidence is cited where it exists (PAR6 and PAR3 homologues, CRB3, PATJ, SCRIB, DLG1 in Caco-2 cells, PRKCI). Boundary and modelling decisions. (1) Relation to protein_kinase_c_signaling. That module models aPKC activation (PDPK1 priming and the PAR3-PAR6 scaffold) as one isoform class of PKC signalling. This module models the polarity machinery that aPKC serves; the PAR complex annoton repeats the aPKC and PAR6 grounding with the same functions (GO:0004674 for aPKC, GO:0035591 for PAR6) so the two modules agree, and the PKC module's atypical variant can later point here by module_ref. (2) Planar cell polarity (wnt_planar_cell_polarity) is a separate axis and is not modelled; SCRIB, which RGD draws on the PCP diagram as a VANGL2 partner, appears here only in its basolateral role. (3) CDC42 is an activator, not a PAR complex subunit, so it is its own regulatory part with a POSITIVELY_REGULATES edge. Its G protein activity is the CDC42 gene-review core function. Other CDC42 effectors and its GEFs/GAPs are out of scope. (4) Catalytic activity sits only on aPKC. PARD6 gets signaling adaptor activity (gene-review core function for PARD6B and PARD6G; the human PAR6 papers PMID:11260256 and PMID:10934474 describe it as the adaptor linking CDC42 and aPKC to PAR3). PARD3 gets no function: it has no experimental human molecular function beyond protein binding and no review. (5) PARD3B is excluded from the PAR complex. Its gene review (PMID:33565714, PMID:12459187) records that PARD3B does not bind PAR6 or aPKC and localises to adherens junctions aPKC-independently; its core function is binding and restraining LKB1. It is a PAR3-family junctional polarity protein but not a PAR complex unit. (6) Crumbs complex: CRB3 is the epithelial Crumbs. CRB1 and CRB2 (retina and other tissues; both have gene reviews) form analogous complexes with PALS1 and PATJ but are not epithelial apical-basal determinants in the general sense and are left out. MPDZ (MUPP1), the PATJ paralogue, is named by the task as an alternative but has no cached human evidence for Crumbs-complex membership here and is not added. No molecular function is asserted for CRB3, PALS1 or PATJ: their experimental GOA rows are binding terms. GO has no cellular-component term for the Crumbs complex (GO:0035003 subapical complex is the invertebrate lateral membrane region, not the complex), so the complex descriptor has no term and a new term is requested in proposed_new_terms. LIN7 proteins, which bind PALS1, and AMOT family proteins are out of scope. (7) Scribble module: SCRIB, DLG1 and LGL are separate annotons because no stable tripartite complex is established. SCRIB carries signaling adaptor activity (IDA, PMID:27380321). DLG1 and the LGL proteins get no function: DLG1's experimental functions are channel or kinase binding in non-polarity contexts, and LGL antagonism of aPKC is competitive binding to PAR6 for which GO has no activity term (LLGL2 PDZ domain binding to PARD6B is the closest GOA row). (8) Connections. The two NEGATIVELY_REGULATES edges are the mutual antagonism the task asks for: aPKC phosphorylates LGL (PMID:12629547, PMID:12725730) and LGL suppresses the apical complex (PMID:16638806). The edges run between nodes, so the scribble_module edge is carried by LGL; no SCRIB-to-aPKC or DLG1-to-aPKC antagonism is asserted. The Crumbs-to-PAR edge (PAR6 recruitment, PMID:12545177, PMID:14718572) and the CDC42 edge are POSITIVELY_REGULATES. LGL is a substrate of aPKC and is not given a PAR-complex process role. (9) Out of scope: tight junction structural proteins (ZO-1, claudins, occludin), adherens junction cadherins, PAR1/MARK kinases and 14-3-3, LKB1, the Yurt/Coracle group, and downstream Hippo signalling. GO-CAM links. Gene reviews exist for CDC42, PARD6A, PARD6B, PARD6G and PARD3B (and CRB1, CRB2), and their core functions were used. PANTHER family ids and PTNs are deliberately not asserted.
