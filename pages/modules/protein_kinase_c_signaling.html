Protein kinase C signaling — chaperone-assisted maturation, PDPK1 and mTORC2 priming, isoform-class activation by DAG/Ca2+ or the PAR scaffold, RACK1/PICK1 anchoring and PHLPP termination; PRKCA/B/G/D/E/H/Q/Z/I, PDPK1, HSP90AA1/CDC37, PARD3/PARD6, RACK1, PICK1, PHLPP1/2

The protein kinase C (PKC) family comprises nine human AGC-group serine/threonine kinases, divided into three classes by the second messengers that activate them. All have an N-terminal autoinhibitory pseudosubstrate and a C-terminal kinase domain. Conventional PKCs (PRKCA, PRKCB, PRKCG) have a tandem diacylglycerol (DAG)-binding C1 domain and a calcium-binding C2 domain and need both DAG and Ca2+; novel PKCs (PRKCD, PRKCE, PRKCH, PRKCQ) have a C1 domain with higher DAG affinity and a C2 domain that does not bind calcium, so they need DAG alone; atypical PKCs (PRKCZ, PRKCI) have a single C1 domain that does not bind DAG and are controlled instead by protein-protein interactions, chiefly through the PB1 domain with PAR6 and with PAR3 in the PAR polarity complex. Newly synthesised conventional and novel PKCs are held by the HSP90-CDC37 kinase chaperone while they are processed by three ordered phosphorylations. PDPK1 (PDK1) phosphorylates the activation loop, and then the turn motif and hydrophobic motif in the C-terminal tail are phosphorylated in a step that requires mTORC2. The resulting mature, stable enzyme sits in the cytosol with its pseudosubstrate in the active site. When phospholipase C generates DAG and IP3-released calcium, conventional PKCs are recruited to the plasma membrane by the C2 domain and engage DAG through C1; novel PKCs go to DAG-containing membranes directly. Membrane binding releases the pseudosubstrate, and the active kinase is positioned at particular sites by anchoring proteins, including RACK1, which binds activated PKC, and the PDZ-BAR protein PICK1. The open, active conformation is also sensitive to phosphatases. PHLPP1 and PHLPP2 dephosphorylate the hydrophobic motif, which moves PKC to the detergent-insoluble fraction for degradation and so sets the cellular level of signalling-competent PKC. Atypical PKCs are phosphorylated by PDPK1 in an agonist-dependent manner and, within the PAR3-PAR6-aPKC complex, regulate cell polarity.

Part 1: chaperone-assisted maturation and priming phosphorylation of newly synthesised PKC
HSP90-CDC37 chaperoning, PDPK1 activation-loop and mTORC2-dependent C-tail phosphorylationRegulatory Steppkc_maturation

Newly made conventional and novel PKCs bind HSP90-CDC37 through a C-tail PXXP clamp. PDPK1 then phosphorylates the activation loop, and the turn and hydrophobic motifs are phosphorylated in an mTORC2-dependent step. Without these steps PKC is inactive and unstable. Atypical PKCs do not bind HSP90-CDC37, and their PDPK1 phosphorylation depends on agonist.
