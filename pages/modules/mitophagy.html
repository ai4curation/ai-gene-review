Mitophagy

Mitophagy is the selective macroautophagy of mitochondria. In animals, damaged or unwanted mitochondria are marked on their outer membrane and captured by phagophores decorated with Atg8-family proteins (LC3 and GABARAP in mammals, LGG-1 in nematodes). Two kinds of mark are used. In the ubiquitin-dependent route, the kinase PINK1 accumulates on the outer membrane of depolarized or import-defective mitochondria and phosphorylates ubiquitin and the ubiquitin-like domain of the RBR E3 ligase Parkin at Ser65. Activated Parkin builds ubiquitin chains on outer-membrane proteins, and in mammals these chains are read by soluble ubiquitin-binding receptors such as optineurin and NDP52, which also bind LC3/GABARAP and recruit the autophagy initiation machinery. In the receptor-mediated route, integral outer-membrane proteins bind Atg8-family proteins directly through LC3-interacting regions. BNIP3 and NIX (BNIP3L) act in hypoxia-induced and developmental mitophagy, such as mitochondrial clearance in maturing reticulocytes, and FUNDC1 acts in hypoxia-induced mitophagy. These receptors are conserved in nematodes, where the BNIP3/NIX ortholog DCT-1 acts in stress-induced somatic mitophagy together with PINK-1 and the Parkin ortholog PDR-1, and the FUNDC1 ortholog FNDC-1 acts in hypoxia-reoxygenation mitophagy. Nematodes also have lineage-specific components. Paternal mitochondria and membranous organelles in the C. elegans embryo are cleared by the nematode-specific receptor ALLO-1 (allophagy), and in worm muscle the FIS1-family proteins FIS-1 and FIS-2 act at a late step to complete orderly disposal of fission-derived mitochondrial fragments.

Boundary. This module covers the selective capture of mitochondria by the autophagy machinery in animals. It starts at the marking of a mitochondrion (PINK1/Parkin ubiquitin signalling, or an outer-membrane receptor) and ends at receptor engagement of Atg8-family proteins on the phagophore and, in nematodes, the FIS-1/FIS-2-dependent completion of disposal. Building the autophagosome (ULK1 initiation, PI3KC3-C1 PI3P synthesis, ATG9, ATG2/WIPI, and the ATG7/ATG3/ATG16L1 lipidation cascade) is the subject of MODULE:metazoan_autophagosome_biogenesis and is not re-modeled here; the Atg8-family proteins appear only in their role as the docking site that receptors bind. Autophagosome-lysosome fusion and degradation are also downstream and outside this boundary. The phagophore-ER contact that supplies lipid for phagophore growth is MODULE:phagophore_organelle_contact_site. The ER-mitochondria contact (MAM), where worm FIS-1 and human FUNDC1 also act, is a separate structure from the phagophore-ER contact. Taxon. The context is Eumetazoa. PAINT places the IBD statements for the PINK1 node (PTN001121970) and the BNIP3/NIX node (PTN001032684) at Eumetazoa, and the Parkin mitophagy node (PTN000188399) at Bilateria. The FUN14 and Atg8 family nodes are eukaryotic, so those two roles are older than the module. Mitophagy also occurs in fungi and plants, but there it uses different receptors (for example yeast Atg32) and no PINK1 or Parkin, so the boundary modeled here does not hold outside animals. Worm and human are the representatives. Drosophila Pink1 and parkin are classic genetic models of the ubiquitin route but have no gene review here, so they are not listed as representatives. Lineage differences. The ubiquitin-reading soluble receptors optineurin and NDP52 (CALCOCO2) are vertebrate proteins; no clear C. elegans ortholog of either has been identified, and which protein reads PDR-1-made ubiquitin in worms is unknown. ALLO-1 and the FIS-1/FIS-2 late disposal step are known only in C. elegans. In worms, PINK-1 and PDR-1 are placed in mitophagy by genetics (reporters, epistasis, LGG-1 colocalization) and by PDR-1 E3 ligase activity toward other substrates; ubiquitin Ser65 phosphorylation and phosphorylation-driven PDR-1 activation are carried over from the human and insect biochemistry. Fission. Stress-induced mitophagy in worm muscle uses MFF-dependent DRP-1 fission upstream of the FIS-1/FIS-2 step. The fission machinery itself is in MODULE:mitochondrial_dynamics. Fission is not required for every route: oxidative-stress-induced neuronal mitophagy in worms is drp-1-independent but partly pink-1-dependent (PMID:35916724). Fission is therefore recorded as context, not as a required part. Human FUNDC1 has a second, separate role as a DRP1 adaptor at ER-mitochondria contacts during hypoxia (PMID:27145933); that fission role is out of scope here. Members left out. SQSTM1/p62 (worm SQST-1) is excluded: the worm review keeps the mitophagy annotation as non-core with no worm evidence, its demonstrated worm role is aggrephagy, and in human cells it is not one of the primary PINK1/Parkin mitophagy receptors (PMID:26266977). TAX1BP1 and NBR1 are minor receptors in that assay and are not modeled. TBK1 (worm IKKE-1) phosphorylates optineurin, NDP52 and ALLO-1 and is described in the receptor annotons, not modeled as its own member. Human FIS1 has no gene review here and is not modeled; its role in mammalian mitophagy is recorded only in notes. BNIP3 and NIX pro-death (BH3-like) activity and their ER-phagy role are not part of this module. MIRO1 is a PINK1 and Parkin substrate but is not a mitophagy receptor. The E2 enzymes UBC-18 and UBC-16 that ubiquitinate paternal membranous organelles, and the transcriptional regulators SKN-1, ATFS-1 and DAF-16, are context, not machinery. ATFS-1 and the mitochondrial unfolded protein response are in MODULE:mitochondrial_upr. Accessions. LGG-1 is Q09490, the accession used by the lgg-1 review and UniProt. The project page gives a different accession. PANTHER. The PINK1 subfamily (PTHR22972:SF7), the BNIP3/NIX family (PTHR15186) and the Atg8 family (PTHR10969) contain both the worm and human representatives. No family term is given for Parkin, because the UniProt PANTHER cross-reference for human PRKN (PTHR10677) differs from the PAINT family that holds the Parkin node (PTHR11685), or for FUNDC1, because the FUN14 family (PTHR21346) also contains FUNDC2 and yeast Fun14, which are not mitophagy receptors.

MitophagyBiological Processmitophagy

Selective macroautophagy of mitochondria in animals. Damaged or unwanted mitochondria are marked by PINK1/Parkin ubiquitin signalling or by outer-membrane receptors (BNIP3/NIX, FUNDC1, and the nematode ALLO-1); ubiquitin-reading or membrane-integral receptors engage Atg8-family proteins on the phagophore; and, in nematodes, FIS-1/FIS-2 complete orderly disposal of fission-derived mitochondrial fragments.

Part 1: ubiquitin-dependent marking of damaged mitochondria
PINK1/Parkin ubiquitin signalling on damaged mitochondriaSignaling Pathwaypink1_parkin_marking

PINK1 is a mitochondrially targeted serine/threonine kinase. It is normally imported and degraded, but when import fails on depolarized mitochondria it accumulates on the outer membrane, phosphorylates ubiquitin and the Parkin ubiquitin-like domain at Ser65, and so recruits and activates Parkin. Parkin is an RBR E3 ubiquitin ligase that builds ubiquitin chains on outer-membrane proteins, which PINK1 phosphorylates in turn, making a feed-forward loop. In C. elegans, pink-1 is required for nascent LGG-1-positive mitophagosomes on muscle mitochondria, the Parkin ortholog PDR-1 joins the stalled mitophagy intermediates seen in fis-1 fis-2 mutants, and the receptor DCT-1 is ubiquitinated in a PINK-1-dependent manner under oxidative stress.

Part 2: receptor recognition of marked mitochondria
Mitophagy receptor recognitionBiological Processmitophagy_receptor_recognition

Receptors bridge the mitochondrion to Atg8-family proteins on the phagophore. Two receptor types exist. Soluble ubiquitin-binding receptors read the Parkin-made ubiquitin mark. Integral outer-membrane receptors carry their own LC3-interacting region and act largely without ubiquitin. The receptors are used in different contexts and lineages and are not interchangeable alternatives for one step; they are grouped as a variant set because each one supplies the mitochondrion-to-phagophore link.

Variant set: Mitophagy receptors by type and lineage by receptor type (ubiquitin-reading soluble receptor or integral outer-membrane receptor) and sequence lineage (One Or More)

Optineurin and NDP52 are the main PINK1/Parkin receptors in human cells and have no clear nematode ortholog. The BNIP3/NIX and FUNDC1 families are conserved between nematodes and mammals. BNIP3 and NIX act in hypoxia-induced and developmental mitophagy, and worm DCT-1 in adult somatic stress-induced mitophagy, genetically with PINK-1/PDR-1. FUNDC1 and FNDC-1 act in hypoxic mitophagy, and FNDC-1 also in the late, ubiquitin-independent phase of paternal mitochondrial elimination. ALLO-1 is nematode-specific and acts in the early, ubiquitin-dependent phase of paternal organelle clearance (allophagy), which also removes the sperm membranous organelles.

Optineurin and NDP52 ubiquitin-binding mitophagy receptors (vertebrate)Biological Processubiquitin_binding_receptors

Optineurin binds polyubiquitin through its UBAN domain and LC3/GABARAP through a LIR motif; NDP52 binds ubiquitin and Atg8-family proteins and its SKICH domain recruits the ULK1 complex. Once on mitochondria, both receptors recruit ULK1, DFCP1 and WIPI1 to start autophagosome formation next to the cargo. TBK1 phosphorylation of optineurin raises its ubiquitin and LC3 binding. These receptors are vertebrate proteins.

Part 3: receptor capture by Atg8-family proteins on the phagophore
Atg8-family docking of mitophagy receptors on the phagophoreBiological Processatg8_capture

Atg8-family proteins (LC3 and GABARAP subfamilies in mammals; LGG-1, the GABARAP-family ortholog, in worms) are conjugated to phosphatidylethanolamine on the phagophore membrane and serve as the binding site for LIR-containing receptors. In worm muscle, nascent mitophagosomes appear as LGG-1 spots on the mitochondrial surface; in the embryo, LGG-1 is recruited to paternal organelles. In human cells the GABARAP subfamily is the main contributor to PINK1/Parkin mitophagy, but Atg8-family proteins are dispensable for engulfment of mitochondria and essential for autophagosome-lysosome fusion, so receptor engagement of Atg8 proteins is not the only way cargo is captured. How the phagophore is built and Atg8 proteins are lipidated belongs to the autophagosome biogenesis module.

Established in C. elegans only. After stress-induced, MFF/DRP-1-dependent fission, the FIS1-family outer-membrane proteins FIS-1 and FIS-2 act redundantly at a late step of mitophagy, before fusion with lysosomes. Fis1 joins a DRP-1-containing complex at the ER-mitochondria interface but is not needed for DRP-1 recruitment or for fission.
