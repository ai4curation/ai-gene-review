All-trans-retinoic acid metabolism — RBP4/STRA6 retinol delivery, CRBP1 binding, RDH10/DHRS3 retinol-retinal interconversion, ALDH1A1-3 retinal oxidation, CRABP1/2 binding and CYP26A1/B1/C1 catabolism

All-trans-retinoic acid (atRA), the most potent vitamin A metabolite, is made on demand inside target cells from circulating retinol and is cleared by oxidative catabolism, so that its tissue level is set by the balance of local synthesis and local destruction. Retinol travels in plasma bound to retinol-binding protein 4 (RBP4), which circulates as a complex with the transthyretin (TTR) tetramer. The multi-pass membrane receptor STRA6 binds holo-RBP4 and moves retinol into the cell, where it is bound by cellular retinol-binding protein 1 (CRBP1, RBP1). The first and rate-limiting step of synthesis is the reversible, membrane-associated oxidation of all-trans-retinol to all-trans-retinal by the short-chain dehydrogenase/reductase RDH10. RDH10 works together with the retinaldehyde reductase DHRS3: the two proteins activate each other, and DHRS3 reduces retinal back to retinol, so the pair sets the flux of retinal toward acid. Cytosolic medium-chain alcohol dehydrogenases can also oxidise retinol. The second step, the oxidation of retinal to atRA, is irreversible and is catalysed by the NAD+-dependent retinaldehyde dehydrogenases ALDH1A1, ALDH1A2 and ALDH1A3, whose distinct tissue patterns define where atRA is produced in the embryo. Cellular retinoic acid-binding proteins bind atRA in the cytosol: CRABP2 delivers it to the nuclear retinoic acid receptors, and CRABP1 sequesters it and favours its catabolism. atRA is inactivated by the cytochrome P450 retinoic acid hydroxylases CYP26A1, CYP26B1 and CYP26C1, which form 4-hydroxy-, 4-oxo- and 18-hydroxy-atRA in the endoplasmic reticulum; CYP26A1 and DHRS3 are induced by atRA, giving negative feedback on its own level. Retinol not used for synthesis can be stored as retinyl esters, made by LRAT and hydrolysed back to retinol by retinyl ester hydrolases such as the carboxylesterase CES1. Loss of RDH10 or ALDH1A2 in mouse abolishes embryonic atRA synthesis, CYP26B1 deficiency causes craniosynostosis and skeletal anomalies, and STRA6 and RBP4 defects cause microphthalmia and coloboma.

RDH10-DHRS3 retinol dehydrogenase/retinaldehyde reductase pairReactionretinol_retinal_interconversion

RDH10 oxidises all-trans-retinol to all-trans-retinal at the ER and lipid-droplet membrane. DHRS3 reduces all-trans-retinal back to retinol. The two SDRs activate each other when co-expressed: DHRS3 reaches its full reductase activity only with RDH10 present, and RDH10 retinol dehydrogenase activity rises with DHRS3, with neither needing to be catalytically active to activate the other. Because DHRS3 is induced by atRA, the pair forms a feedback loop that limits retinal supply. No GO cellular component term exists for the RDH10-DHRS3 association, so it is modelled as two annotons in one node rather than as a named complex.

Part 4: irreversible oxidation of retinal to all-trans-retinoic acid
Retinaldehyde dehydrogenases ALDH1A1, ALDH1A2 and ALDH1A3Reactionretinal_oxidation

Cytosolic NAD+-dependent class 1 aldehyde dehydrogenases oxidise all-trans-retinal to atRA through a catalytic cysteine. The reaction is irreversible and commits retinal to atRA. The three paralogues catalyse the same reaction and differ in tissue and developmental expression (ALDH1A2 is the main embryonic and testicular enzyme, ALDH1A1 is abundant in liver and also oxidises other aldehydes, ALDH1A3 acts in the developing eye and nasal region), so they are modelled as variants.

Part 6: oxidative catabolism of atRA
CYP26 retinoic acid hydroxylases (CYP26A1, CYP26B1, CYP26C1)Reactionretinoic_acid_catabolism

ER cytochrome P450s hydroxylate atRA at C4 and C18, forming 4-OH-, 4-oxo- and 18-OH-atRA, which are far less active and are further oxidised and conjugated for elimination. The three enzymes share the chemistry and differ in tissue expression and kinetics: CYP26A1 has the highest capacity and is strongly atRA-inducible, CYP26B1 has the highest affinity and dominates in brain, and CYP26C1 also accepts 9-cis-RA.

Part 7: retinyl ester storage and mobilisation (buffer of the retinol pool) (optional)
Retinyl ester formation (LRAT) and hydrolysis (CES1)Metabolic Pathwayretinyl_ester_storage

Optional side branch. Cellular retinol not oxidised by RDH10 can be esterified by LRAT and stored as retinyl esters, mainly in hepatic stellate cells, and the esters are hydrolysed back to retinol when needed. It is optional because it buffers the retinol supply rather than being a step on the route from retinol to atRA, and atRA is made in tissues without a large ester store.
