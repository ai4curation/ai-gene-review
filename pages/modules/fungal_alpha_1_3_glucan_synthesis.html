Fungal cell wall alpha-1,3-glucan synthesis and remodeling

Alpha-1,3-glucan is a major cell wall polysaccharide in many ascomycetes and basidiomycetes. It is absent from Saccharomyces cerevisiae, but in Schizosaccharomyces pombe it is about a third of the wall glucan and is essential. It is made at the plasma membrane by Ags1-family synthases, very large multi-pass proteins with two probable catalytic regions. An intracellular GT5 (glycogen synthase-like) domain makes a short alpha-1,4-glucan primer. An extracellular GH13 (alpha-amylase-like) domain is proposed to join or remodel chains. The mature S. pombe polymer is two alpha-1,3-glucan chains of about 120 residues each, coupled through short alpha-1,4-linked segments. Separate GPI-anchored GH13 enzymes on the cell surface (Aah1 and Aah3 in S. pombe) are needed for normal chain assembly. By analogy to characterised Aspergillus homologs they are thought to be 4-alpha-glucanotransferases, not hydrolases. In S. pombe, Ags1 makes alpha-glucan at growing tips and in the primary septum, alongside the beta-glucan synthase Bgs1, while sporulation-specific paralogs (Mok12, Mok13, Mok14) make the spore-envelope alpha-glucans. UDP-glucose supply, the Rho2-Pck2 signalling that controls Ags1, synthase trafficking, and alpha-glucan degradation (e.g. Agn1 during cell separation) are outside the module.

NEW module (2026-11), built after reviewing S. pombe ags1 and aah3 and a consistency pass on aah1. It is the alpha-glucan counterpart of fungal_beta_1_3_glucan_synthesis. The two meet at the primary septum, where Ags1 and Bgs1 co-immunoprecipitate, and the PomBase septum GO-CAM 6918f23700003787 contains both. Boundary decisions: (1) Rho2-Pck2 controls Ags1 (PMID:11102532) but is a signalling input, not a holoenzyme subunit like Rho1 for the beta-glucan synthases, so it is outside. (2) Only Aah1/Aah3 are modelled for remodeling. aah2 and aah4 add nothing in vegetative growth, and the meiotic mde5 was not tested (PMID:40668835). (3) Ags1's own extracellular GH13 domain is described in the synthase annoton, not as a separate remodeling part, because its activity is unassayed. (4) The spore paralogs are a lightly grounded variant with Mok12 as exemplar. Mok13 and Mok14 are named but sit in different PANTHER subfamilies. Mok14 is smaller (1369 aa) and its exact role should be checked in a gene review.
