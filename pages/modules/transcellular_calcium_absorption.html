Transcellular calcium absorption — apical entry through the epithelial calcium channels TRPV6 (intestine) and TRPV5 (kidney distal convoluted and connecting tubule), cytosolic buffering by calbindin-D9k (S100G) and calbindin-D28k (CALB1), and basolateral extrusion by PMCA1 and NCX1

Active, transcellular calcium absorption moves calcium from the intestinal lumen or the tubular fluid across an epithelial cell into the blood, against the electrochemical gradient at the exit step. It complements passive paracellular flux and is the hormonally regulated route: in the duodenum it adapts dietary calcium uptake to need, and in the late distal convoluted tubule and connecting tubule (DCT2/CNT) of the kidney it fine-tunes how much filtered calcium is reabsorbed. The route has three steps. Calcium enters across the apical membrane through the highly calcium-selective epithelial channels TRPV6 (mainly intestine, also placenta and other epithelia) and TRPV5 (mainly kidney DCT2/CNT), which are constitutively open at low cytosolic calcium and inactivated by rising calcium. In the cytosol the vitamin D-induced EF-hand proteins calbindin-D9k (S100G; intestine) and calbindin-D28k (CALB1; kidney) bind the entering calcium, keeping free calcium low near the channel, preventing calcium- dependent channel inactivation and speeding diffusion to the basolateral side. There calcium is extruded to the interstitium by the plasma membrane calcium ATPase PMCA1b (ATP2B1) and the sodium/calcium exchanger NCX1 (SLC8A1), which carries most basolateral extrusion in DCT2/CNT. 1,25-dihydroxyvitamin D3 acting through the vitamin D receptor induces the channels, the calbindins and PMCA1b; parathyroid hormone and klotho stimulate renal reabsorption.

Part 1: apical calcium entry from the lumen into the epithelial cell
Apical calcium entry through the epithelial calcium channels TRPV6 and TRPV5Transport Stepapical_calcium_entry

Calcium crosses the apical membrane down its electrochemical gradient through the highly calcium-selective TRPV6 and TRPV5 channels. This is the rate-limiting, hormonally controlled step. TRPV6 predominates in the intestine (and placenta), TRPV5 in the kidney DCT2/CNT; the two can co-assemble, so both may contribute where they are co-expressed.

Part 2: cytosolic calcium buffering and diffusion from the apical to the basolateral side
Cytosolic calcium buffering and shuttling by calbindin-D9k and calbindin-D28kModulecytosolic_calcium_buffering

Vitamin D-induced EF-hand calbindins bind the calcium that enters through the apical channels. Buffering keeps free calcium low beneath the channel, which prevents calcium-dependent channel inactivation and protects the cell, and the mobile calcium-calbindin complex is proposed to speed diffusion of calcium to the basolateral membrane. Calbindin-D9k (S100G) is the intestinal protein, calbindin-D28k (CALB1) the renal one.

Variant set: Epithelial calbindins by protein and tissue (intestine versus kidney) [Realizer] (One Or More)

S100G and CALB1 are unrelated EF-hand proteins (an S100-family protein with two EF-hands and a hexa-EF-hand protein) that serve the same buffering role in different epithelia. Mouse knockouts of either, alone or with TRPV6, leave substantial active transport (PMID:18325990), so this part is supporting rather than strictly required (see notes).

Basolateral calcium extrusion by PMCA1 and NCX1Transport Stepbasolateral_calcium_extrusion

Calcium leaves the cell across the basolateral membrane against its gradient, through the plasma membrane calcium ATPase PMCA1 (the PMCA1b splice form in enterocytes and DCT2/CNT) and the sodium/calcium exchanger NCX1 (the NCX1.3 variant in DCT2/CNT, about 70% of renal basolateral extrusion).
