Anti-CRISPR suppression of host CRISPR-Cas immunity

A reusable counter-defence module for suppression of a host CRISPR-Cas system by proteins encoded by phages, prophages, and other mobile genetic elements. Anti-CRISPR (Acr) proteins are small, unrelated in sequence, and act by mechanism rather than by homology, so the module is decomposed by the step of the host pathway each class attacks: blockade of crRNA-guided target recognition, neutralisation of the effector nuclease, and enzymatic destruction of a CRISPR component or signal. A fourth, optional part covers the Aca autoregulatory switch that limits acr transcription once the host system is disabled. The host pathway itself is the separate module MODULE:crispr_cas_adaptive_immunity; general phage counter-defence against restriction-modification, and host-encoded self-regulators such as the Crn ring nucleases, are outside the boundary.

AcrF8 gene review
The curated review of the type I-F inhibitor AcrF8 (H9C181) supplies the target-recognition-blockade exemplar and its GO:0098672 assignment.

Boundary decisions. First, mechanism is the right decomposition axis because Acr families share no sequence relationship: AcrIF8, AcrIIA4, and AcrVA1 are unrelated proteins, and only their point of attack is comparable. Second, enzymatic Acrs are kept as a separate part from stoichiometric blockers because the distinction is functional, not cosmetic -- a blocker must be present in molar excess of its target, while an enzyme can act catalytically and sub-stoichiometrically. Third, the host-encoded Crn1/Crn2 ring nucleases (for example UniProtKB:Q97YD2 and UniProtKB:Q7LYJ6) are deliberately excluded: they degrade the same cyclic oligoadenylate signal as the viral AcrIII-1 nuclease does, but as self-regulation of the host's own response rather than as symbiont-mediated suppression, so they belong to the host module. Fourth, no connection is drawn to the host module's nodes because connections are document-scoped; each part names its host target in prose instead.

Part 1: Blockade of crRNA-guided target recognition
target-recognition blockadeBiological Processrecognition_blockade

The most common mechanism: the Acr binds the crRNA-loaded surveillance complex or single effector and prevents it from engaging target DNA, either by occluding the DNA-binding and PAM-reading surfaces or by contacting the crRNA itself. The effector's nuclease chemistry is left intact; it simply never reaches a substrate.

Variant set: recognition-blockade implementation by blocked surface and host effector class (One Or More)

A single phage genome commonly encodes several of these against one host system, so ONE_OR_MORE rather than EXACTLY_ONE.

Type I-F Csy complex blockadeBiological Processcsy_complex_blocker_variant

Acrs that bind the multi-subunit type I-F surveillance (Csy) complex. AcrIF8 is distinctive in contacting the crRNA as well as the Cas7f backbone, so the blockade does not depend on protein-surface recognition alone.

Part 4: Aca-mediated autoregulation of the acr locus (optional)
Aca autoregulatory switchRegulatory Stepaca_autoregulation

acr genes are typically co-transcribed with a small helix-turn-helix anti-CRISPR-associated (aca) repressor that binds the shared promoter and shuts the locus down. The result is a burst of Acr early in infection, when the host system must be neutralised, followed by repression -- avoiding the cost of sustained high-level expression. Because the repressor is transcribed from the very promoter it represses, this is genuine negative feedback on the acr locus rather than external regulation. This part is optional because not every characterised acr locus has an identified aca gene.
