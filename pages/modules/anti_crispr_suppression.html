Anti-CRISPR suppression of host CRISPR-Cas immunity

Proteins encoded by bacteriophages, archaeal viruses, prophages and other mobile genetic elements suppress host CRISPR-Cas immunity through alternative mechanisms. These include blocking effector assembly or target recognition, sequestering an effector subunit or disabling its nuclease, modifying Cas proteins, cleaving guide RNA, and degrading an immune second messenger. A mobile element may combine several inhibitors, but these are not compulsory sequential steps. Optional anti-CRISPR-associated regulators control inhibitor expression. The examples span bacterial and archaeal hosts; plasmid and conjugative-element inhibitors need not have a viral origin.

The participant taxon envelope includes viral proteins and proteins deposited under bacterial genomes, including prophages and nonviral mobile elements. Hosts are bacteria or archaea as specified for each experimental system; heterologous human-cell editing assays do not extend the native host range. Inhibitory occupancy depends on affinity, concentration, binding stoichiometry and timing: a stoichiometric blocker does not universally require molar excess. Catalytic turnover can permit substoichiometric inhibition in vitro without establishing effective native expression. Families are sampled by tested members; conserved membership alone does not establish all targets or native counterdefense. Host CRISPR functions are described separately in MODULE:crispr_cas_adaptive_immunity. Host self-regulatory ring nucleases and unrelated anti-defense mechanisms remain outside the functional boundary.
