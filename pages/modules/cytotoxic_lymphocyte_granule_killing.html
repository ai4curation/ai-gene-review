Cytotoxic lymphocyte granule-mediated target cell killing

The route by which a cytotoxic lymphocyte kills a cell it has recognised, using preformed secretory granules rather than death receptors. An activating receptor engages the target and triggers polarised exocytosis of cytolytic granules into the immunological synapse; perforin binds the target membrane in a calcium-dependent way, oligomerises and forms a large pore; granzyme B passes through that pore, or through a second perforin pore in the endosome the target internalises, into the target cytosol; and granzyme B then kills by limited activating proteolysis of executioner caspases and other substrates. Whether the target dies by non-inflammatory apoptosis or by lytic pyroptosis is set downstream, by whether it expresses a gasdermin that caspase-3 can cleave. A final, optional arm runs the other way: caspase-7 escaping through the same pores activates acid sphingomyelinase, whose ceramide supports membrane repair and so limits lysis. The module is perforin-dependent throughout - in perforin-null animals the lymphocytes are present in normal numbers and simply fail to lyse - which is what makes it a single mechanism rather than a collection of cytotoxic activities. Target recognition is included as the trigger but the receptor is interchangeable: CD16A grounds the antibody-dependent case here, while NKG2D, the T-cell receptor and other activating receptors substitute for it without changing anything downstream. Outside the boundary: granule biogenesis, the transcriptional arming of the lymphocyte, death-receptor killing, the cytokine arm of lymphocyte effector function, and the apoptotic and pyroptotic execution programmes themselves, which are separate modules reached from here.

Boundary decisions worth challenging. Target recognition is inside the module as an interchangeable trigger; a reviewer may prefer it as upstream context, which would make the module start at degranulation and leave CD16A out. Granule biogenesis and the lymphocyte's arming are outside. The apoptotic and pyroptotic execution programmes are named as the branch point only, since each is its own module and this repository already holds apoptotic_execution_phase and intrinsic_apoptotic_signaling. Known grounding gaps, stated rather than papered over. Part two has no annoton for the fusion machinery that actually executes exocytosis - STX11, UNC13D and STXBP2, the genes behind familial haemophagocytic lymphohistiocytosis types 3 to 5 - because none of them has a gene review in this repository. Arl8b grounds the trafficking that positions the granule, not the fusion. Granzyme B is the only granzyme grounded; granzyme A, granzyme H, granzyme K and granzyme M deliver different substrate programmes through the same pore and would each extend part four. Granulysin, which perforin also delivers and which kills intracellular bacteria, is absent for the same reason. Status is DRAFT, not COMPLETE, for three specific reasons rather than as a formality. No module deep research exists for this module, because no provider was reachable in the environment where it was written, so the derived QC panel's deep-research check will report none. Two of the eight grounded genes carry UNDECIDED rows in their gene reviews - CASP3 at 190 of 199 rows and CASP7 at 98 of 101 - so the gene-review completeness check does not come back wholly clean, although no row anywhere is left PENDING. And the gene reviews behind three parts are themselves not at COMPLETE status: GSDME and CORO1A sit at DRAFT and SMPD1 at INITIALIZED. That is deliberate and not a gap to close from here. All three are genes of the ClinGen Mendelian campaign, which records that a COMPLETE status does not certify assessment against its current evidence and curation rules and that campaign acceptance does not change a review's computed status; GSDME in particular was accepted as campaign gene 745 with its DRAFT status explicitly retained. Part 1: Target recognition by an activating receptor
Target recognition by an activating receptorRegulatory Steptarget_recognition

An activating receptor on the lymphocyte reads a determinant on the target and signals degranulation. CD16A grounds the antibody-dependent case, where the determinant is the Fc region of IgG bound to the target surface; it is the exemplar rather than the requirement, and NKG2D, the T-cell receptor and other activating receptors substitute without altering steps two to six. The receptor contributes recognition and signal transduction only - it has no catalytic activity, and in CD16A's case no signalling motif of its own, depending on CD247 or FcepsilonRI gamma for ITAMs.

Part 2: Granule polarisation and exocytosis at the synapse
Polarised delivery of cytolytic granules to the synapseTransport Stepgranule_delivery

Cytolytic granules, which are secretory lysosomes holding perforin and granzymes as soluble cargo, are moved to the synapse and fuse with the plasma membrane, releasing their contents into the cleft. Part 3: Perforin pore formation and cargo translocation
Calcium-dependent perforin pore formation in the target membraneTransport Stepperforin_pore

The conserved core of the module. Released perforin binds the target bilayer through its C-terminal C2 domain once calcium is bound, oligomerises into a ring through a membrane-bound pre-pore, and inserts amphipathic beta-hairpins to open a large aqueous channel. The channel is cargo-selective rather than an indiscriminate hole, favouring cationic molecules including granzyme B. Delivery is not confined to the plasma membrane: perforin also triggers endocytosis and then perforates the resulting enlarged endosome, and which route predominates in physiological killing is still disputed. Both membranes are therefore given as locations.

Part 4: Granzyme B proteolysis in the target cytosol
Granzyme B activating proteolysis of target substratesReactiongranzyme_proteolysis

Granzyme B, delivered through the pore, kills by limited activating cleavage rather than by degradation. It is a chymotrypsin-fold serine protease that cuts after aspartate, which is why it can substitute for caspases on their own substrates: it processes the accessible procaspase-3, which in turn permits caspase-7 activation, and it cleaves BID to the tBID fragment that drives mitochondrial outer-membrane permeabilisation. GO has no granzyme-specific molecular function term, so the generic endopeptidase term is as precise as the ontology allows.

Part 6: Target membrane repair limiting lysis (optional)
Caspase-7-driven sphingomyelinase repair of perforin poresRegulatory Stepmembrane_repair

A counter-regulatory arm that runs back against the module. Caspase-7, activated in the attacked cell, reaches acid sphingomyelinase by passing through the very pores that caused its activation, and activates it by proteolytic cleavage; the resulting ceramide supports endocytic removal of the pores and so limits lysis. Optional because it modulates the outcome rather than being required for killing, and because it has been established in a particular setting - hepatocytes under NK attack - rather than as a general feature of every target.
