Axonemal outer and inner dynein arms in Drosophila melanogaster

Axonemal dyneins are the motors that bend motile cilia and flagella. They are attached in rows along the doublet microtubules of the 9+2 axoneme as outer dynein arms, which mainly set beat frequency, and inner dynein arms, which mainly shape the waveform. Each arm contains one or more dynein heavy chains, the ATP-driven motor subunits, together with intermediate and light chains that anchor the arm and regulate it. In Drosophila, motile axonemes are found only in sperm flagella and in the cilia of chordotonal mechanosensory neurons, so axonemal dyneins act in male fertility and hearing. Several sperm-specific heavy chains are encoded by the Y-chromosome fertility factors kl-2, kl-3 and kl-5.

Heavy chains, intermediate chains and light chains are modelled per gene rather than as single complexes, because the composition of individual Drosophila arm species (which heavy chains pair with which intermediate chains, and which are sperm- versus chordotonal-specific) is not fully established. Heavy chains are given the general microtubule motor activity term. ODA-Dnal1 and Dnali1 are members of the FlyBase dynein light chain group (DYN-LC), which is assigned to dmel_cytoplasmic_dynein_dynactin; they are added here as non-member genes because they are axonemal arm light chains by homology. Outer-arm docking complex, dynein preassembly factors and the cytoplasmic dynein light chains are not parts.
