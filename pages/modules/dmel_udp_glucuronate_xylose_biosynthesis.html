UDP-glucuronate and UDP-xylose biosynthesis in Drosophila melanogaster

UDP-glucuronic acid and UDP-xylose are the sugar donors for proteoglycan glycosaminoglycan chains and for other glucuronidated and xylosylated molecules. UDP-glucose pyrophosphorylase (Ugp) makes UDP-glucose from glucose-1-phosphate and UTP, and UDP-glucose 6-dehydrogenase (Sugarless, sgl) oxidises it to UDP-glucuronic acid in the cytosol. The nucleotide-sugar transporter Fringe connection (frc) imports UDP-glucuronic acid and other UDP sugars into the ER and Golgi lumen, where UDP-glucuronic acid decarboxylase (Uxs) converts UDP-glucuronic acid to UDP-xylose. Sugarless and Fringe connection were identified genetically through their requirement for Wingless and other heparan sulfate proteoglycan-dependent signalling.

Boundary. The module ends with the nucleotide sugars; their use by glycosyltransferases (for example xylosyltransferase Oxt and the glucuronyltransferases) is in dmel_glycosaminoglycan_biosynthesis and other glycosylation modules. Ugp also supplies UDP-glucose for glycogen and trehalose synthesis. frc and sgl are in both FlyBase groups and appear once. Fringe connection transports several UDP sugars (UDP-glucuronic acid, UDP-GlcNAc, UDP-xylose and others); the annoton gives the UDP-glucuronate transport activity relevant here. Placing Uxs after import assumes a lumenal enzyme, as for its mammalian ortholog UXS1; the topology of fly Uxs has not been determined.
