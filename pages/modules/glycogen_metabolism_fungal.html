Fungal glycogen synthesis and mobilisation

Fungi store glucose as glycogen, a branched alpha-1,4/alpha-1,6 glucan, through a UDP-glucose-dependent pathway like that of animals and unlike the ADP-glucose pathway of bacteria. Glucose 1-phosphate is activated with UTP to UDP-glucose by UDP-glucose pyrophosphorylase. Synthesis starts on glycogenin, a self-glucosylating GT-8 glucosyltransferase that attaches glucose to its own tyrosine residue(s) and extends a short alpha-1,4 oligosaccharide primer. Glycogen synthase (GT-3) elongates the primer by adding glucose from UDP-glucose to non-reducing ends, and the GH13 branching enzyme moves segments of chain to alpha-1,6 positions to create branches. Mobilisation has two routes. In the cytosol, glycogen phosphorylase releases glucose 1-phosphate from chain ends until it stops near a branch, and the bifunctional debranching enzyme transfers the remaining short branch to another chain (4-alpha-glucanotransferase) and hydrolyses the single alpha-1,6-linked glucose (amylo-alpha-1,6-glucosidase). Alternatively, glycogen delivered to the vacuole by autophagy is hydrolysed to free glucose by a vacuolar GH15 glucoamylase. Phosphoglucomutase interconverts glucose 1-phosphate and glucose 6-phosphate, linking both arms to glycolysis. Both arms are induced by nutrient limitation and stress and are controlled post-translationally (for example by phosphorylation of glycogen synthase and phosphorylase); these regulatory inputs are outside the module.

fungiNCBITaxon:4751

NEW module (YeastPathways crosswalk, 2026-10) generalising PWY3O-4031 and GLYCOCAT-YEAST-PWY into one fungal glycogen module with a synthesis arm, a mobilisation arm (cytosolic phosphorolytic versus vacuolar hydrolytic routes) and the shared phosphoglucomutase link. The synthesis arm and the cytosolic route conform to the human glycogen_biosynthesis and glycogenolysis modules (same GO MFs and PANTHER families for UGP, glycogenin, glycogen synthase, branching enzyme, phosphorylase, debranching enzyme and PGM); those human modules are disease-oriented and were not edited. The bacterial ADP-glucose route is in glycogen_synthesis_and_mobilization, and the animal lysosomal acid alpha-glucosidase route (GAA, GH31) in lysosomal_glycogen_degradation is analogous to, but not homologous with, the fungal vacuolar GH15 glucoamylase route. Changes to YeastCyc: (1) glycogenin GLG1/GLG2 were expected to be missing from YeastCyc, but the current PWY3O-4031 frame does list them (GLYCOGENIN-GLUCOSYLTRANSFERASE-RXN and RXN-7667); they are retained as the priming step and the two YeastCyc glycogenin reactions are merged into one GO:0008466 activity. Priming is required in practice, although rare glg1 glg2 colonies can make glycogen (PMID:16004992). (2) YHL012W is listed by YeastCyc as a second UDP-glucose pyrophosphorylase; UniProt describes it only as probable and no characterisation was found, so it is not used as an exemplar. (3) The GDB1 reactions RXN3O-4038 (glycogen to maltotetraose) and AMYLOMALT-RXN are not adopted: they describe bacterial isoamylase/GlgX and amylomaltase/MalQ chemistry, whereas GDB1 is an AGL-type transferase/glucosidase (PMID:11094287). (4) SGA1 is placed in the vacuole (PMID:2493265, PMID:11486014), not the cytosol. GO-CAM disagreements: the YeastPathways models type GPH1 and the GDB1 transferase and glycogenin primer-extension activities only with GO:0003674, and place SGA1 in the cytosol. No PomBase GO-CAM is linked; S. pombe is not used as an exemplar. Regulation (PKA, Snf1, Pho85, Gac1/Glc7 phosphatase, Rim15) and the glycogen-granule proteins are outside the module.
