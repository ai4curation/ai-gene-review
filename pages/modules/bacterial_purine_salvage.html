PpnP-linked bacterial purine nucleoside and nucleobase salvage

A reusable PpnP-linked bacterial salvage realization in which purine nucleosides are phosphorolyzed to free bases and ribose 1-phosphate, after which adenine, hypoxanthine or guanine, and xanthine are returned to the nucleotide pool as AMP, IMP or GMP, and XMP. The three phosphoribosyltransferase branches use PRPP and release diphosphate.

PMID:40632566
A conserved editing mechanism for the fidelity of bacterial cell wall biosynthesis
Direct bacterial comparison found PgeF/YfiH PNP activity weak and unable to compensate for loss of physiological PNP activity in E. coli, so YfiH is not used to satisfy this salvage realization.

This module separates PpnP-linked bacterial salvage from de novo purine synthesis, nucleotide interconversion, uptake, and oxidative purine degradation. PpnP also acts on pyrimidine nucleosides, but only its purine substrates belong to this realization. A species may implement only a subset of the three nucleobase branches. DeoD-, XapA-, or nucleoside-kinase-dependent routes are alternative realizations rather than implicit PpnP equivalents. YfiH/PgeF is excluded: its weak in vitro PNP activity lacks demonstrated physiological relevance in bacteria, whereas peptidoglycan precursor editing is its established bacterial role.
