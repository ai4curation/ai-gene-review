Adrenal and interrenal steroidogenesis

Adrenal and interrenal steroidogenic cells convert cholesterol to pregnenolone and route it into corticosteroid or androgen branches. StAR-dependent cholesterol delivery and mitochondrial CYP11A cleavage supply the common entry. HSD3B and microsomal CYP17/CYP21 activities determine intermediate routes; mitochondrial CYP11 activities produce cortisol, corticosterone or aldosterone according to enzyme specificity and tissue expression. Human, mouse and zebrafish implementations share reactions but differ in paralog use, expression and terminal products.

Context
VertebrataNCBITaxon:7742

Vertebrata is a non-universal envelope for a repertoire of adrenal/interrenal reactions, not a claim that each vertebrate produces every endpoint. Native exemplars are human, mouse and zebrafish. Individual branches require the appropriate enzyme specificity and tissue expression.

The two entry steps are shared; optional downstream parts encode alternative branches in a species/tissue-specific realization, not dispensability of a reaction within a chosen branch. Display order is not a single linear chronology. HSD3B reactions are split by substrate and by dehydrogenase/isomerase activity so the graph does not imply an artificial CYP17-HSD3B cycle. CYP17A2 has hydroxylase activity without lyase; CYP11C1 is not an aldosterone synthase merely because it shares a named PANTHER subfamily. Redox partners (POR, ferredoxin reductase and ferredoxin, with cytochrome b5 modulation) are required biochemical context outside the present member boundary. Cortisol/cortisone interconversion, steroid sulfation, aromatization, backdoor and downstream 11-oxygenated androgen pathways are outside scope. The normal adult mouse/rat corticosterone pattern has a documented fetal mouse CYP17 exception. The two additional reviewed zebrafish paralogs cyp11a1.1 and cyp17a1 are comparative evidence, not asserted interrenal representatives.
