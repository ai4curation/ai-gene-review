Histone acetyltransferase B (Hat1-Hat2-Hif1) complex in Saccharomyces cerevisiae

Histone acetyltransferase B (HAT-B) is the type B histone acetyltransferase of budding yeast. It acetylates newly synthesized histone H4 on lysine 12, and to a lesser extent lysine 5, before the histone is deposited into chromatin. The catalytic subunit Hat1 is a GNAT-family acetyltransferase. Hat2, a WD40 protein of the RbAp46/RbAp48/Msi1 family, binds the histone tails, raises Hat1's affinity for histone H4 and is needed for Hat1 to enter the nucleus. The Hat1-Hat2 heterodimer is found in both the cytoplasm and the nucleus. In the nucleus it binds a third subunit, Hif1, a NASP-family histone H3-H4 chaperone that promotes histone deposition. HAT-B acts on soluble, non-chromatin-bound histone H4 and does not acetylate histone H4 packaged in nucleosomes. Loss of HAT-B subunits impairs telomeric silencing (when combined with histone H3 tail mutations) and DNA double-strand break repair, and Hat1 and Hif1 are recruited to chromatin next to a double-strand break.

Boundary. The module contains the three HAT-B subunits, Hat1 (catalytic), Hat2 (histone-binding WD40 subunit) and Hif1 (histone H3-H4 chaperone). Hat1 and Hat2 form the cytoplasmic heterodimer; Hif1 joins only in the nucleus, so its part is marked optional. Histone H3 and H4 are substrates, not parts. Downstream histone chaperones and deposition factors (Asf1, CAF-1, Rtt106) and the H3K56 acetyltransferase Rtt109-Vps75, which also act on newly synthesized histones, belong to separate modules. Ontology. GO has no term for the type B histone acetyltransferase complex, so the module concept carries no GO id and the term is requested under proposed_new_terms. GOA currently annotates all three subunits part_of GO:0000123 histone acetyltransferase complex (IDA and IPI). That class is part_of GO:0000785 chromatin, so these annotations entail that HAT-B is part of chromatin, which contradicts the defining property of a type B enzyme. The edge is questioned under proposed_ontology_changes. Because GO:0000123 is logically defined as any complex capable of histone acetyltransferase activity, a new HAT-B term placed anywhere would still be classified under it, and so under chromatin, until that edge is removed. Substrate site. The purified complex acetylates only H4 K12 on peptide substrates, while recombinant Hat1 and the complex in vivo also acetylate K5 (PMID:18373695). The catalytic annoton uses the K12 activity, the primary and best-supported site.
