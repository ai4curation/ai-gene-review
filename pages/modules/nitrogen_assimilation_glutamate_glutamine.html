Fungal ammonium assimilation into glutamate and glutamine, and glutamate deamination

Assimilation of ammonium into the amino group of L-glutamate and the amide group of L-glutamine, and the reverse release of ammonium from glutamate, as organised in fungi and other eukaryotic microbes. Glutamate and glutamine are the nitrogen donors for nearly all other nitrogenous compounds, so these reactions sit between nitrogen-source catabolism and biosynthesis. Glutamine synthetase ligates ammonium to L-glutamate with ATP to give L-glutamine; it is the only route to glutamine and is required in every growth condition. Ammonium reaches glutamate by two alternative routes. In the first, NADP-dependent glutamate dehydrogenase reductively aminates 2-oxoglutarate with NADPH; in budding yeast this is the main route when glucose is plentiful and is carried by two isozymes, one constitutive and one induced on non-fermentable carbon sources. In the second, the glutamine synthetase-glutamate synthase (GS-GOGAT) cycle, an NADH-dependent glutamate synthase transfers the glutamine amide nitrogen to 2-oxoglutarate, giving two molecules of L-glutamate, one of which replaces the glutamate consumed by glutamine synthetase. Fungal glutamate synthase is a single large polypeptide that combines the domains carried by the separate large and small subunits of bacterial NADPH-dependent glutamate synthase. The GS-GOGAT cycle has higher affinity for 2-oxoglutarate and uses NADH rather than NADPH, and it becomes required for glutamate synthesis when the NADP-dependent dehydrogenases are absent or when glutamine is the nitrogen source. Loss of both routes causes glutamate auxotrophy. An NAD-dependent glutamate dehydrogenase runs the reaction in the catabolic direction, oxidatively deaminating L-glutamate to 2-oxoglutarate and ammonium; it supplies the ammonium for glutamine synthesis when glutamate is the nitrogen source. 2-Oxoglutarate itself comes from isocitrate dehydrogenases of central carbon metabolism.

L-glutamate biosynthetic processGO:0097054 L-glutamine biosynthetic processGO:1901704

Boundary: ammonium to L-glutamate and L-glutamine, plus the NAD-GDH catabolic reaction that returns glutamate nitrogen to ammonium. 2-Oxoglutarate supply by isocitrate dehydrogenases is upstream central carbon metabolism and belongs to tca_cycle. YeastCyc lists only the NADP isozymes (ISOCITDEH-RXN: IDP1, IDP2, IDP3) and omits the mitochondrial NAD-dependent IDH1/IDH2, which carries the main flux and supplies most of the 2-oxoglutarate pool on glucose, while cytosolic IDP2 dominates on non-fermentable carbon (PMID:15574419). Any one isozyme suffices for glutamate-independent growth (PMID:8672488). IDP3 is peroxisomal and supplies NADPH for beta-oxidation of unsaturated fatty acids, not 2-oxoglutarate for glutamate synthesis. Glutamine-amide transfer to other acceptors, nitrogen-source catabolism and nitrogen catabolite repression (Gln3, Gat1, Ure2, Gcn4) are outside the module. Generalisation: the module is fungal-scope. bacterial_ammonia_assimilation models the bacterial GS-GOGAT cycle, which uses GSI-type GlnA and a two-subunit NADPH-dependent GltB/GltD glutamate synthase (GO:0004355) rather than the GSII-type glutamine synthetase and single-chain NADH-dependent GLT1 of fungi; the chemistry of the cycle conforms. glutamate_glutamine_ammonia_hub is the animal arrangement, which has no glutamate synthase, a dual-cofactor mitochondrial GDH (GO:0004353) and glutaminases; animal GS (GLUL) belongs to the same PANTHER family as fungal GLN1. YeastCyc assessment: gene assignments for GDH1, GDH3, GLN1, GLT1 and GDH2 are correct and no S. cerevisiae enzyme is missing. GO-CAM disagreements: (a) YeastPathways_PWY3O-13 asserts IDP1 (mitochondrial) and IDP3 (peroxisomal) in the cytosol and puts all three isocitrate dehydrogenases part_of GO:0097054; (b) GDH2 has no YeastPathways GO-CAM, because PWY-3322 was not converted; (c) PomBase model 67c10cc400000148 omits gdh1, the NADP-GDH that with GOGAT is required for growth on ammonium (PMID:7773104), and places glt1 and gdh2 in the mitochondrial matrix (see knowledge gap); (d) neither model has causal edges between glutamine synthetase and glutamate synthase. The PomBase model also contains alanine transaminase (alt1), which is modelled in aspartate_asparagine_metabolism, and arginine/lysine entry steps and tRNA ligases, which are outside both modules.
