N-glycan trimming and processing in Drosophila melanogaster

After oligosaccharyltransferase transfers Glc3Man9GlcNAc2 to asparagine residues of nascent proteins, the N-glycan is trimmed in the endoplasmic reticulum and then remodelled in the Golgi apparatus. In the ER, glucosidase I (Gcs1) removes the outer glucose and the glucosidase II heterodimer (Gcs2alpha catalytic and Gcs2beta regulatory subunits) removes the two inner glucoses; these steps also control entry into and exit from the calnexin-calreticulin folding cycle. Class I alpha-1,2-mannosidases trim mannoses in the ER and cis-Golgi. In the Golgi, GlcNAc transferase I (Mgat1) adds GlcNAc to the alpha-1,3 arm, which allows alpha-mannosidase II to remove two further mannoses. In insects most N-glycans are then converted to paucimannose glycans by the Golgi beta-N-acetylglucosaminidase Fused lobes (fdl), while a smaller fraction is extended to hybrid and complex glycans by GlcNAc transferase II, core fucosyltransferases, galactosyl-, GalNAc-, glucuronyl- and sialyltransferases.

Boundary. The module starts after transfer of the lipid-linked oligosaccharide to protein (oligosaccharyltransferase is outside the module) and covers ER trimming, Golgi trimming and Golgi diversification, the three FlyBase N-glycan processing groups plus the glucosidase II complex group. Core fucosylation by FutA and Fut6 needs the GlcNAc added by Mgat1 and can occur before or after mannosidase II trimming, so the order within the diversification part is shown only where it is clear. GlcAT-P and GlcAT-S are glucuronyltransferases that act on N-glycans and glycolipids (GlcAT-P is also in the O-GalNAc branch of dmel_protein_o_glycosylation); GalT1 is a beta-1,3-galactosyltransferase. No molecular function is assigned to the regulatory Gcs2beta subunit or to alpha-Man-IIb, whose in vitro activity differs from that of classical mannosidase II. Molecular functions of Gcs1, alpha-Man-Ib and Mgat1 follow orthology; the others are supported by assays of the fly enzymes in GO.
