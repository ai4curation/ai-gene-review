Small- and intermediate-conductance calcium-activated potassium channels — KCNN1, KCNN2 and KCNN3 (SK1-3, homo- or heterotetramers) and KCNN4 (IK/SK4, homotetramer), each pore subunit carrying a constitutively bound calmodulin (CALM1/2/3) whose Ca2+-binding N-lobe opens the gate, so that a rise in cytosolic Ca2+ drives K+ efflux and membrane hyperpolarization

Small-conductance (SK, KCa2) and intermediate-conductance (IK, KCa3.1) calcium-activated potassium channels convert a rise in cytosolic Ca2+ into an outward K+ current that hyperpolarizes the plasma membrane. Each channel is a tetramer of six-transmembrane pore subunits, KCNN1, KCNN2 or KCNN3 for SK channels and KCNN4 for IK channels. The pore subunits contain no Ca2+-binding site of their own: one calmodulin molecule is bound to the intracellular calmodulin-binding domain of every subunit, independently of Ca2+, so that four calmodulins are integral parts of each channel. The calmodulin C-lobe stays attached to the channel, while Ca2+ binding to the N-lobe makes it grip the S4-S5 linker, which pulls the S6 bundle open; SK and IK channels are therefore voltage-independent and respond to submicromolar Ca2+ with high cooperativity. SK1, SK2 and SK3 can assemble as homotetramers or as heterotetramers with each other, whereas IK channels are KCNN4 homotetramers with a larger unit conductance and a distinct pharmacology. In neurons SK channels generate the afterhyperpolarization that follows action potentials and shapes firing frequency and synaptic responses; SK2 and SK3 also contribute to atrial repolarization. IK channels act in erythrocytes (the Gardos channel), lymphocytes, epithelia and endothelium, where K+ efflux maintains the driving force for Ca2+ entry or salt and water secretion. The Ca2+ sensitivity of SK2 channels is further tuned by protein kinase CK2 and protein phosphatase 2A bound to the channel, which phosphorylate and dephosphorylate the channel-bound calmodulin.

(2) STRUCTURE AND MODULE CONCEPT. The module is a BIOLOGICAL_PROCESS with two parts: Ca2+ sensing by the channel-bound calmodulin (part 1), which POSITIVELY_REGULATES K+ conduction through the pore (part 2). Although sensor and pore form one complex, they are separate parts because their activities are distinct: calmodulin binds Ca2+ and the pore subunits conduct K+. The concept is GO:0071805 potassium ion transmembrane transport, the process every pore subunit carries by IDA in human GOA. The mapped GO:0019722 calcium-mediated signaling is kept as evidence only: the channels respond to Ca2+ signals but do not relay them to downstream targets. Other candidates were checked in go-basic 2026-07-26: GO has no afterhyperpolarization term. GO:0060081 membrane hyperpolarization covers it, since its definition uses the hyperpolarization after an action potential as its example. GO:0086009 membrane repolarization and GO:0060306 regulation of membrane repolarization are cardiac-framed and are not carried by these genes in experimental GOA. So no ontology request is needed. GO:0060081 is added as a second process on the SK channel annotons. This is curator-authored, from the afterhyperpolarization role (PMID:8781233, PMID:9774106): no experimental GOA row for KCNN1-3 asserts it. It is not added to the IK channel, whose cached evidence concerns conduction only; KCNN4 carries GO:0030322 stabilization of membrane potential by IDA, which is not used. (3) VARIANTS AND SELECTION. Part 2 is a REALIZER variant set on the pore-subunit paralogue with four variants (SK1, SK2, SK3, IK), selection ONE_OR_MORE. EXACTLY_ONE would be wrong because SK1-3 co-assemble: co-expressed human SK2 and SK3 form one population of heteromers (PMID:36502918), and SK1-SK2 heteromers are preferred, so one channel can realize two or three SK variants at once. Cells also often express more than one type. Each SK variant is therefore "an SK channel containing that paralogue", with pore-unit stoichiometry 1-4 per tetramer; the IK channel is four KCNN4 subunits. No heteromer of KCNN4 with SK1-3 is asserted. The SK and IK paralogues are variants of one step, not separate modules, because they share the calmodulin gating mechanism and differ mainly in unit conductance and pharmacology. Each pore subunit forms functional homotetrameric channels when expressed alone, and each has experimental human GOA rows (QuickGO, November 2026) for the specific GO term, so the function sits both on the pore unit and on the complex annoton. The complex term is GO:0034705 potassium channel complex, because GO has no SK- or IK-specific complex term. The calmodulin unit in each complex has no function there; its function is modelled once, on the part 1 annoton. Calmodulin's function is GO:0005509 calcium ion binding (IDA on CALM1, CALM2 and CALM3, for example PMID:23040497 and PMID:26969752), with process GO:0005513 detection of calcium ion. K+ cargo is grounded to CHEBI:29103 potassium(1+), and the gating Ca2+ (named in the calmodulin function description, not cargo) to CHEBI:29108 calcium(2+); both labels were checked in OLS (November 2026). (5) REGULATORY PARTNERS CK2 AND PP2A ARE NOT UNITS. RGD's text and the SK2 proteomic and functional work (PMID:17329434, and later neurotransmitter-modulation studies in rat neurons) describe protein kinase CK2 (CSNK2A1/CSNK2A2 with CSNK2B) and PP2A bound to SK2 and SK3 channels. In those studies, CK2 phosphorylates calmodulin Thr80 in closed channels and lowers Ca2+ sensitivity, and PP2A reverses this. The brief requires experimental human evidence that they are stable channel-complex units. None was found: the cached abstract of PMID:17329434 does not state the species of the clones; the original proteomics was on rodent brain; and no human GOA row links CK2 or PP2A subunits to KCNN2 or KCNN3. They are therefore recorded as node-level evidence on part 1 and named here, not modelled as units or parts. Their genes are not on the RGD PW:0001140 list. Other SK2 partners in GOA (FLNA, alpha-actinin ACTN2 and the L-type channel coupling of PMID:17110593) concern trafficking or local Ca2+ supply and are out of scope. (6) BOUNDARY. Upstream Ca2+ sources (voltage-gated Ca2+ channels, NMDA receptors, ER release) are modelled in the calcium transport modules (high_voltage_activated_calcium_channels, t_type_calcium_channels, er_calcium_store_cycling, store_operated_calcium_entry) and are context, not parts. Downstream physiology (firing-rate adaptation, synaptic plasticity, atrial repolarization, erythrocyte volume, T-cell activation) is not modelled. Large conductance BK channels (KCNMA1), which bind Ca2+ directly and are voltage-gated, are a different mechanism and are excluded. They are not on the RGD list.
