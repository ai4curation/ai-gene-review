Human mRNA nuclear export — TREX loading on spliced mRNA, NXF1-NXT1 handover, nuclear pore translocation and DDX19-GLE1 remodelling, with the CRM1 route for specific mRNAs; THOC1/THOC2/THOC3/THOC5/THOC6/THOC7/DDX39B/ALYREF/CHTOP/SARNP/NXF1/NXT1/MCM3AP/ENY2/PCID2/DDX19B/GLE1/NUP214/XPO1/RAN

A mature mRNA, capped, spliced and polyadenylated in the nucleus, must cross the nuclear pore complex (NPC) before it can be translated. In human cells bulk export is coupled to splicing and to the cap. The transcription-export complex TREX, built from the multi-subunit THO complex (THOC1, THOC2, THOC3, THOC5, THOC6, THOC7) with the DEAD-box ATPase UAP56 (DDX39B) and the export adaptors ALYREF, CHTOP and CIP29 (SARNP), is deposited near the 5' end of the spliced mRNA through an interaction between ALYREF and the cap-binding protein CBP80. UAP56 uses its ATPase cycle to load ALYREF and CHTOP onto the mRNA. The adaptors then recruit the export receptor, the NXF1-NXT1 (TAP-p15) heterodimer: ALYREF and THOC5 contact NXF1 and switch it from an autoinhibited state into one that exposes its arginine-rich RNA-binding domain, and the mRNA is handed over from ALYREF to NXF1. TREX-2 (GANP/MCM3AP with ENY2 and PCID2), anchored on the nuclear basket, helps deliver NXF1-bound mRNPs to the pore. NXF1-NXT1 carries the mRNP through the central channel by transient contacts between its NTF2-like and UBA domains and the phenylalanine-glycine (FG) repeats of the nucleoporins, on a scaffold whose outer rings are built from the Nup107-160 (Y) complex. At the cytoplasmic filaments the DEAD-box ATPase DDX19 (DDX19B), anchored on NUP214, is activated by GLE1 and its small-molecule cofactor inositol hexakisphosphate (IP6). DDX19 remodels the emerging mRNP, stripping export factors so that they recycle to the nucleus and making the passage directional. A minority of mRNAs leave by a second, Ran-dependent route through the karyopherin CRM1 (XPO1), which is recruited by mRNA-specific adaptors: LRPPRC with eIF4E for mRNAs that carry the eIF4E-sensitivity element, HuR (ELAVL1) with pp32 and APRIL (ANP32A, ANP32B) for AU-rich-element mRNAs, NXF3, and FMRP for N6-methyladenosine-marked mRNAs. RanGTP loads the cargo onto CRM1 in the nucleus, and GTP hydrolysis by Ran, stimulated by RanGAP1 with RanBP1 and RanBP2 at the cytoplasmic face, releases it.

Summarises the bulk route in mammalian cells: NXF1 binds the mRNP through adaptors and crosses the NPC by transient FG-nucleoporin contacts.
Bulk nuclear export of messenger ribonucleoproteins (mRNPs) through nuclear pore complexes (NPCs) is mediated by NXF1. It binds mRNPs through adaptor proteins such as ALY and SR splicing factors and mediates translocation through the central NPC transport channel via transient interactions with FG nucleoporins.

Derived from the RGD Pathway Ontology term PW:0001587 (mRNA nuclear export pathway) through the RGD_PATHWAY_ONTOLOGY project's diagram->module route. RGD curates 37 human genes to it. All 37 are modelled: the TREX subunits ALYREF, DDX39B, SARNP and THOC1/2/5/6/7; NXF1 and NXT1; the nucleoporins NUP62, NUP54, NUP58, NUP98, NUP153 and NUP214 and the Y-complex members NUP107, NUP133, NUP160, NUP85, NUP37, NUP43, SEC13 and SEH1L; DDX19B and GLE1; and the CRM1 branch XPO1, RAN, RANGAP1, RANBP1, RANBP2, LRPPRC, EIF4E, ELAVL1, ANP32A, ANP32B and NXF3. Added beyond the RGD list, each with its own evidence: THOC3 (the Tex1 subunit, present in the fly module and in TREX by GO's definition), CHTOP, POLDIP3 and ZC3H11A (TREX adaptors shown biochemically to join human TREX), the TREX-2 subunits MCM3AP (GANP), ENY2 and PCID2, and FMR1 (from the human GO-CAM). The mapped GO process GO:0006406 is the module concept. Of the diagram's sub-pathway links, the NXF1-NXT1 export pathway (PW:0001625) is fully modelled and listed as evidence; the CRM1 export pathway is modelled only for its mRNA branch, because CRM1 also exports proteins, snRNA and ribosomal subunits, so that RGD term is mentioned here but not claimed. The spliceosome, 5' capping and 3'-end processing sub-pathways are upstream inputs that produce the mature mRNA and are outside the boundary. Fly counterpart. modules/dmel_mrna_nuclear_export.yaml models the conserved core from the FlyBase TREX, THO-C, TREX-2 and NXF gene groups: TREX (Hel25E, Ref1, THO, tex), the Nxf1-Nxt1 receptor (sbr, Nxt1) and TREX-2 (xmas, PCID2, e(y)2), and puts the NPC and the EJC outside. This module keeps those three parts in the same order and with the same complex terms, so the two can be compared directly, and adds what the RGD pathway adds: the NPC translocation step, the DDX19-GLE1 remodelling step and the CRM1 route. Two small differences: TREX-2 is marked optional here (in human cells it behaves like a basket component and its depletion slows export rather than abolishing it, PMID:23591820), and the NXF1-NXT1 complex is grounded on GO:0042272 nuclear RNA export factor complex, whose exact synonyms are "NXF1-NXT1 complex" and "TAP-p15 complex". The fly module states that GO has no CC term for this heterodimer; GO:0042272 is that term and could be used there. The fly module is referenced in prose only, since a cross-species module_ref would assert an identity this module does not need. Nuclear pore. No human nuclear pore complex module exists in modules/ (only dmel_nuclear_pore_complex), so module_ref was not used and the pore is modelled inline as a required transport step with two complex annotons: the FG-nucleoporins the receptor contacts (NUP62, NUP54, NUP58, NUP98, NUP153) and the Y-complex scaffold. They are parts, not context, because RGD includes them in the pathway and the receptor-FG contact is the translocation mechanism, but the step is a shared transport organelle used by every karyopherin route, and if a human NPC module is written later this step should become a module_ref to it. The Y-complex annoton carries no mRNA-export process: it is the scaffold the FG layer sits on, and its contribution to export is structural. NUP96, which is produced from the same NUP98 precursor and belongs to the Y-complex, shares NUP98's accession and is represented only through it. TPR and NUP50 of the basket, NUP88 and NUP42 (hCG1, the GLE1 anchor) of the cytoplasmic face and the other nucleoporins are not listed; NUP42 is omitted because no cached human evidence for its GLE1 role was available. Boundary decisions. (1) TREX-2 is an optional part rather than context, following the fly module and the GANP evidence that it binds NXF1 and is needed for efficient NXF1 recruitment to pores. Its centrin (CETN2/CETN3) and DSS1 (SEM1) subunits, known from yeast and human biochemistry, are not listed. (2) The exon junction complex is context, not a part, as in the fly module. It is deposited by splicing and some of its components help recruit TREX, but its core function is in mRNA localisation, translation and nonsense-mediated decay, and RGD does not include it. The cap-binding complex (CBP80/NCBP1), which recruits TREX through ALYREF, and the SR-protein export adaptors (SRSF3, SRSF7) that also hand mRNA to NXF1 are likewise context, recorded in prose. (3) The CRM1 route is included as an optional part because it is half of the RGD pathway and is the route of the only production human mRNA export GO-CAM. Its adaptors form a ROUTE variant set, since they define different mRNA classes, not interchangeable carriers of one activity. RAN, RANGAP1, RANBP1 and RANBP2 are modelled inside the CRM1 part as the Ran cycle that drives cargo loading and release; the RanGDP import and RCC1 recharging steps of the Ran cycle are outside. NUP98's role in the CRM1 routes is noted on its unit rather than duplicated. (4) DDX39A (URH49), the paralog of UAP56, can substitute for it in TREX-like complexes, and DDX19A is a paralog of DDX19B; neither is in the RGD list and neither is modelled. The one human GO-CAM containing DDX39A (gomodel:66c7d41500002513) models it as a negative regulator of immune-related RNA export and is not linked here. (5) Inositol hexakisphosphate is named in prose and in GLE1's function (GO:0000822) rather than as a separate participant; its synthesis by IPMK and IPPK is outside the boundary. Part 4: translocation of the mRNP through the nuclear pore channel
Translocation through the nuclear pore complexTransport Stepnpc_translocation

NXF1-NXT1 carries the mRNP through the central channel by binding FG repeats of the channel, basket and cytoplasmic nucleoporins. The pore is modelled by two annotons: the FG-nucleoporins that the receptor contacts, and the Nup107-160 (Y) complex that forms the outer rings of the scaffold, whose importance for mRNA export RGD records as conserved. The NPC is a shared transport organelle used by all karyopherin routes; it is included here as the structural step mRNA export depends on, not as an mRNA-specific machine.

Part 5: mRNP remodelling and release at the cytoplasmic filaments
DDX19-GLE1-IP6 remodelling at the cytoplasmic faceBiological Processcytoplasmic_remodelling

As the mRNP emerges, the DEAD-box ATPase DDX19 (Dbp5), held on the cytoplasmic filaments by NUP214, is activated by GLE1 bound to inositol hexakisphosphate (IP6). GLE1-IP6 stimulates RNA-dependent ATP hydrolysis and RNA release, and NUP214 binding is mutually exclusive with RNA binding, so the cycle strips export factors (NXF1, ALYREF) from the mRNA and keeps export directional. The mechanism was worked out mainly in yeast (Dbp5, Gle1, Nup159) and with crystal structures of the human DDX19-NUP214 pair.

Part 6: CRM1 (XPO1)-dependent export of specific mRNA classes (optional)
CRM1 route for specific mRNAsBiological Processcrm1_mrna_export

A minority of mRNAs are exported by the karyopherin CRM1 (XPO1) instead of NXF1. Because mRNA carries no nuclear export signal, an NES-bearing RNA-binding adaptor links each mRNA class to CRM1. RanGTP is required for CRM1 to bind cargo in the nucleus, and on the cytoplasmic side RanGAP1, with RanBP1 and RanBP2, stimulates Ran GTP hydrolysis to release the cargo. The route is optional: it is not required for bulk export, and its adaptors define alternative cargo classes rather than successive steps.
