Insulin-like receptor signaling pathway in Drosophila melanogaster

Drosophila insulin-like peptides (Ilps), several of them secreted by the insulin-producing cells of the brain, bind the single insulin-like receptor InR, a receptor tyrosine kinase. Activated InR phosphorylates the adaptor Chico (IRS) and acts with the adaptor Lnk (SH2B), recruiting the class IA phosphatidylinositol 3-kinase made of the catalytic subunit Pi3K92E (Dp110) and the regulatory subunit Pi3K21B (p60). The resulting PIP3 recruits Pdk1 and Akt; Akt phosphorylates and excludes the transcription factor FOXO from the nucleus and promotes TORC1 activity through Rheb, leading to S6 kinase activation and inhibition of the translational repressor Thor (4E-BP). The pathway controls cell and organismal growth, carbohydrate and lipid metabolism, reproduction and lifespan.
