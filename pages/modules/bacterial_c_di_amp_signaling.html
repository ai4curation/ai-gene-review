Bacterial cyclic di-AMP second-messenger signalling

A reusable second-messenger motif, predominantly of Gram-positive bacteria, in which cyclic di-AMP (c-di-AMP) reports ionic and osmotic sufficiency. Diadenylate cyclases condense two ATP molecules into c-di-AMP; the membrane-anchored CdaA/DacA class is the only cyclase in many Firmicutes, while DisA and CdaS are additional cytoplasmic classes in spore-formers. Degradation is two-tiered: DHH/DHHA1 phosphodiesterases of the GdpP class, and the unrelated 7TMR-HD PgpH class, open c-di-AMP to the linear dinucleotide pApA, and a second cytoplasmic DHH/DHHA1 enzyme (Pde2/NrnA class) clears pApA to AMP, which matters because pApA feedback-inhibits GdpP. Receptors convert c-di-AMP binding into inhibition of potassium and compatible-solute uptake: RCK_C-domain gating subunits of Ktr/Trk potassium systems and of cation/proton antiporters, the USP domain of the KdpD sensor kinase, the tandem CBS domains of the OpuC ATPase, and the PII-like receptors DarA/PstA. Because every characterised receptor restrains an uptake system, high c-di-AMP corresponds to restrained turgor and a reinforced envelope, and both depletion and accumulation impair fitness.

Systematic affinity and DRaCALA screens identified KtrA, CpaA, KdpD and PstA as c-di-AMP receptors that are widely distributed beyond S. aureus, and mapped the binding activity of KtrA and CpaA to the RCK_C domain.

Boundary. The module is the messenger motif: synthesis, two-step degradation, and the receptor step at which c-di-AMP binding changes the activity of a target. The transport systems the receptors act on are their own modules; the Kdp system whose induction c-di-AMP suppresses through KdpD is modelled in MODULE:bacterial_kdp_potassium_homeostasis, and this module deliberately does not restate the KdpD/KdpE phosphorelay or the KdpFABC pump. Extracellular c-di-AMP sensing by the host (STING, inflammasome) is a host pathway and is out of scope; MODULE:cgas_sting_signaling covers the receiving side. Degradation is modelled as two sequential parts rather than one, because the two steps are catalysed by different enzymes, have different GO terms available (only the c-di-AMP step has one) and are coupled by pApA feedback inhibition of GdpP, which is the reason the second step exists. The second step has no GO molecular-function term: GO:0106409 covers only the c-di-AMP to pApA step and the former oligoribonucleotidase term GO:0034611 is obsolete, so the Pde2/NrnA annoton carries its activity without a GO id (substrate and product recorded with ChEBI) and a pApA hydrolase term is proposed on the knowledge gap about that enzyme class's in vivo substrates. Representatives. S. aureus DacA (UniProtKB:Q2FW92) is the exemplar cyclase but is unreviewed in UniProt and its GO:0106408 is IEA only; the experimentally grounded diadenylate-cyclase annotations sit on B. subtilis DisA (UniProtKB:P37573, EXP from PMID:18439896) and on CdaA/DacA of B. subtilis and L. monocytogenes, so both are carried as representatives. Likewise the GO:0106409 EXP annotations are on B. subtilis GdpP and L. monocytogenes PgpH, while the S. aureus orthologues carry IEA, so the Firmicute exemplars are used for the enzyme roles and the S. aureus proteins are named as the species instantiation. S. aureus Pde2 corresponds to SAUSA300_1650 (UniProtKB:A0A0H2XFX6, PANTHER PTHR47618:SF1, the NrnA subfamily), and its pApA-preferring activity is from PMID:27834680. Receptor-side caveats. PstA binds c-di-AMP with high affinity but its downstream target is unknown, so it is given the binding function and no process. The CpaA output direction (stimulated potassium export) is the review's reading of a predicted antiporter and is recorded as such. DarA is the B. subtilis PII-like receptor with structural evidence; GOA currently carries no c-di-AMP binding annotation for it (only cytoplasm), so GO:0180001 is asserted here from the structural literature rather than copied from GOA, and that annotation gap is worth reporting. GO:0180001 (cyclic-di-AMP binding) exists and is used; its only experimental annotation in GOA is on a coral STING, so the bacterial receptor side of this term is essentially uncurated.

Part 1: c-di-AMP synthesis by diadenylate cyclases
Diadenylate cyclase synthesis of c-di-AMPReactionc_di_amp_synthesis

A DAC domain condenses two ATP molecules into c-di-AMP with release of pyrophosphate. The reaction needs the domain to oligomerise: the isolated DacA catalytic domain is a dimer whose active sites face outward and only a higher-order head-to-head assembly is catalytic. Cyclase classes differ in their regulatory context rather than in the chemistry: membrane-anchored CdaA/DacA is coupled to cell-wall precursor metabolism through GlmM and to the membrane protein YbbR/CdaR, DisA is a DNA-scanning octamer suppressed by branched DNA, and CdaS acts in spore germination.

Part 2: c-di-AMP hydrolysis to pApA
Phosphodiesterase hydrolysis of c-di-AMP to pApAReactionc_di_amp_hydrolysis

Two unrelated phosphodiesterase classes open one phosphodiester bond of c-di-AMP to give the linear dinucleotide pApA. GdpP-class enzymes are membrane-anchored and carry a PAS sensory domain, a degenerate GGDEF domain and the DHH/DHHA1 catalytic pair; PgpH-class enzymes use an HD domain in a 7TMR-HD architecture. Both are inhibited by (p)ppGpp, linking c-di-AMP turnover to the stringent response, and GdpP is additionally inhibited by its own product pApA. Which classes are present is lineage-specific: staphylococci have GdpP but no PgpH.

Variant set: c-di-AMP phosphodiesterase class by enzyme family (One Or More)
GdpP-class DHH/DHHA1 phosphodiesteraseReactiongdpp_phosphodiesterase

Membrane-anchored PAS-GGDEF-DHH/DHHA1 protein; the DHH/DHHA1 domain hydrolyses c-di-AMP to pApA, the degenerate GGDEF domain has ATPase activity rather than cyclase activity and contributes to regulation, and the PAS domain binds heme, which inhibits hydrolysis. In S. aureus GdpP is the major c-di-AMP phosphodiesterase: loss-of-function raises c-di-AMP markedly and is selected under beta-lactam pressure.

Part 3: pApA clearance to AMP
Pde2/NrnA-class hydrolysis of pApA to AMPReactionpapa_clearance

A cytoplasmic DHH/DHHA1 enzyme of the NrnA class hydrolyses pApA to AMP. It can also hydrolyse c-di-AMP but prefers the linear dinucleotide, and its physiological point is to prevent pApA accumulation: pApA inhibits GdpP, so without this step the first degradation step stalls. The step therefore belongs to the module even though it does not act on the messenger itself.

Part 4: c-di-AMP recognition by receptor proteins
Protein receptors of c-di-AMPRegulatory Stepc_di_amp_reception

Receptors bind c-di-AMP through one of several unrelated ligand-binding modules and, in every characterised case, restrain an uptake system: RCK_C-domain gating subunits of Ktr/Trk potassium uptake systems and of cation/proton antiporters, the USP domain of the KdpD sensor kinase (suppressing induction of the high-affinity Kdp system), the tandem CBS domains of the OpuC ATPase (reducing compatible-solute import), and PII-like trimers. c-di-AMP therefore reads as a signal of ionic and osmotic sufficiency, and the receptor set, not a single effector, is what makes both depletion and accumulation of the messenger deleterious.

Variant set: c-di-AMP receptor module by ligand-binding module (One Or More)
RCK_C-domain receptorRegulatory Steprck_c_receptor

The RCK_C domain is the most widely distributed bacterial c-di-AMP binding module. In the Ktr/Trk potassium systems it is carried by the cytoplasmic gating subunit (KtrA in S. aureus, CabP in S. pneumoniae), where c-di-AMP binding locks a conformation that suppresses uptake through the membrane subunit; the same domain in the cation/proton antiporter CpaA binds c-di-AMP with the opposite consequence for potassium flux.
