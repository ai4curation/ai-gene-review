Dolichol / dolichyl-phosphate biosynthesis (cis-PT -> DHRSX -> SRD5A3 -> DHRSX -> DOLK)

Dolichyl phosphate (Dol-P) is the essential polyprenol lipid carrier on which the eukaryotic glycosylation machinery assembles and delivers glycans: it anchors the lipid-linked oligosaccharide for N-linked glycosylation, the mannose/glucose donors (Dol-P-Man, Dol-P-Glc) for N-glycan, O-mannose, GPI-anchor and C/O-mannosylation, and it is regenerated after each transfer cycle. Its biosynthesis begins where the isoprenoid-diphosphate trunk ends: the ER-membrane cis-prenyltransferase (cis-PT) complex — catalytic subunit DHDDS plus the non-catalytic partner NUS1 (NgBR) — elongates (2E,6E)-farnesyl diphosphate (FPP) by many cis (Z)-condensations of isopentenyl diphosphate (IPP) to the long-chain dehydrodolichyl diphosphate (dehydro-Dol-PP). This polyprenyl diphosphate is dephosphorylated to polyprenol by a phosphatase that has not been identified in humans. Polyprenol is then converted to dolichol by a three-step detour rather than by a single reduction. DHRSX oxidizes polyprenol to polyprenal (NAD+), SRD5A3 reduces the alpha-isoprene double bond of polyprenal to give dolichal (NADPH), and DHRSX reduces dolichal to dolichol (NADPH). Dolichol kinase DOLK then phosphorylates dolichol (CTP-dependent) to Dol-P, completing the carrier pool. The dolichyl-diphosphate phosphatase DOLPP1 regenerates Dol-P from the Dol-PP released after oligosaccharyltransferase moves the glycan onto protein. The cis-PT subunits, SRD5A3, DOLK and DOLPP1 are endoplasmic reticulum membrane proteins, whereas DHRSX has been localized to lipid droplets. Defects across this module cause congenital disorders of glycosylation (DHDDS-CDG/RP59, NUS1-CDG/epileptic encephalopathy, DHRSX-CDG, SRD5A3-CDG, DOLK-CDG) and inherited retinal dystrophy.

PMID:42360632
From Isoprene Units to Polyprenols and Dolichols: 70 Years of Polyisoprenoid Biosynthesis Research.
2026 review used to audit the module. It describes the 2024 revision of polyprenol-to-dolichol conversion from one SRD5A3 reduction to a three-step DHRSX/SRD5A3/DHRSX detour through polyprenal and dolichal. It also notes that polyprenols come from polyprenyl diphosphate dephosphorylation by a generic polyprenyl diphosphate phosphatase. The earlier module version modeled that step with DOLPP1 and omitted DHRSX.
In the first step, DHRSX functions as a dehydrogenase, oxidizing polyprenol alcohol to an aldehyde intermediate known as polyprenal. Subsequently, SRD5A3 acts as a polyprenal reductase, utilizing NADPH to reduce the double bond and form dolichal. In the third and final step, DHRSX acts again, this time as a NADPH-dependent reductase, converting the terminal aldehyde group of dolichal back into an alcohol to produce dolichol [23].
PMID:38821050
A pseudoautosomal glycosylation disorder prompts the revision of dolichol biosynthesis.
Primary evidence for the three-step detour. DHRSX (UniProtKB:Q8N5I4) performs the first (NAD+-dependent polyprenol dehydrogenase, GO:0160196) and third (NADPH-dependent dolichal reductase, GO:0160197) steps, and SRD5A3 performs only the second. DHRSX deficiency causes DHRSX-CDG.
Of note, DHRSX has a unique dual substrate and cofactor specificity, allowing it to act as a NAD+-dependent dehydrogenase and as a NADPH-dependent reductase in two non-consecutive steps.
PMID:42201967
The revised three-step detour pathway in dolichol biosynthesis is evolutionarily conserved in budding yeast.
Yeast TDA5 is a functional DHRSX ortholog: human DHRSX, but not DFG10 or SRD5A3, rescues the tda5 deletion. The detour is therefore conserved beyond mammals. Whether Env9 also takes part in yeast is still debated (PMID:42360632).
These findings show that Tda5 serves the same function as DHRSX in yeast, thereby demonstrating conservation of the three-step detour pathway in yeast and supporting a broader eukaryotic framework for dolichol biosynthesis.

Dolichol / dolichyl-phosphate biosynthesis (GO:0043048 + GO:0016094), grounded to five completed human gene reviews; it consumes FPP/IPP from the isoprenoid_diphosphate_biosynthesis module and supplies the Dol-P glycan carrier to N-glycosylation (dolichol-linked oligosaccharide, GO:0006488) and to the downstream DPM1/DPM2/DPM3 + MPDU1 utilization steps (separate, already reviewed). Steps: (1) ER cis-PT complex = DHDDS (Q86SQ9 PTHR10291, catalytic, GO:0045547) + NUS1/NgBR (Q96E22 PTHR21528, obligate non-catalytic partner, contributes_to GO:0045547 via its C-terminal -RXG motif; in_complex GO:1904423) elongates FPP with many cis-IPP units -> dehydrodolichyl-PP; this is dephosphorylated to polyprenol by an unassigned polyprenyl diphosphate phosphatase (knowledge gap); (2) DHRSX (Q8N5I4 PTHR24320:SF264, GO:0160196) oxidizes polyprenol -> polyprenal; (3) SRD5A3 (Q9H8P0 PTHR14624, GO:0160198 polyprenal reductase) saturates the alpha-isoprene unit -> dolichal; (4) DHRSX (GO:0160197) reduces dolichal -> dolichol; (5) DOLK (Q9UPQ8 PTHR13205, GO:0004168 dolichol kinase, CTP-dependent) -> Dol-P; (6) DOLPP1 (Q86YN1 PTHR11247, GO:0047874) dephosphorylates Dol-PP -> Dol-P + Pi in the recycling arm after OST glycan transfer. Revision (2026-10): an earlier version placed DOLPP1 on the de novo chain and had SRD5A3 convert polyprenol directly to dolichol. The 2024 identification of the DHRSX detour (PMID:38821050), its conservation in yeast (TDA5, PMID:42201967) and the 2026 review (PMID:42360632) replace that model. DHRSX has no gene review in this repository yet; its grounding is UniProtKB:Q8N5I4 and GOA EXP/IDA annotations to GO:0160196/GO:0160197 from PMID:38821050. Defects: DHDDS-CDG/RP59, NUS1-CDG/epileptic encephalopathy, DHRSX-CDG, SRD5A3-CDG, DOLK-CDG. GO term ids/labels verified with QuickGO.
