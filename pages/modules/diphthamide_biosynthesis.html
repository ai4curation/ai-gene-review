Diphthamide biosynthesis on translation elongation factor 2

Diphthamide is a post-translationally modified histidine residue found only on archaeal and eukaryotic translation elongation factor 2 (EF2), at the tip of domain IV. It is the target of diphtheria toxin and Pseudomonas exotoxin A, which ADP-ribosylate it and so inactivate EF2. The modification is built on the folded EF2 protein in the cytosol in four enzymatic steps. First, a [4Fe-4S]-cluster enzyme cleaves S-adenosyl-L-methionine at the methionine C(gamma)-S bond, unlike canonical radical-SAM enzymes, and transfers the 3-amino-3-carboxypropyl (ACP) group to C2 of the histidine imidazole ring. In archaea this enzyme is a Dph2 homodimer; in eukaryotes it is a Dph1-Dph2 heterodimer that also needs the CSL-zinc-finger protein Dph3, which reduces its iron-sulfur clusters, and the J-domain protein Dph4. Second, the SAM-dependent methyltransferase Dph5 trimethylates the amino group and methylates the carboxylate, giving diphthine methyl ester. Third, the WD40-repeat methylesterase Dph7 hydrolyses the ester to diphthine. Fourth, the ATP-binding diphthine-ammonia ligase Dph6 amidates the carboxylate with ammonium to form diphthamide. Diphthamide is not essential in yeast, but its loss reduces translational fidelity (more -1 ribosomal frameshifting) and confers resistance to diphtheria toxin and the antifungal sordarin.

Four ordered enzymatic modifications of a single histidine residue of EF2. Each step uses the product of the previous step, which stays attached to EF2 throughout, as its substrate.

Boundary: the module begins with the first ACP transfer onto EF2 and ends with diphthamide. Synthesis of SAM, ATP and the Fe-S clusters is outside it, and so are the toxin ADP-ribosylation of diphthamide and any role of diphthamide in translation. The EF2 substrate (yeast EFT1/EFT2) is the acceptor of every step and performs none of them, so it is not a module participant. KTI11/DPH3 also has a separate role with the Elongator tRNA modification complex, which is out of scope. The Dph7 methylesterase step is established for yeast and human. Archaeal EF2 also carries diphthamide, but the cached literature does not establish whether archaea use a Dph7-like esterase or a different chemistry, so this step is modelled from eukaryotic evidence. The S. pombe orthologs (dph1-dph5 are named in UniProt) are not listed because this group has no PomBase GO-CAM.
