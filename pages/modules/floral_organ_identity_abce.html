ABCE model of floral organ identity (MADS floral quartets)

In the eudicot flower, four concentric whorls of organs (sepals, petals, stamens and carpels) get their identities from overlapping homeotic gene activities, as described by the ABC model and its later ABCE extension. In Arabidopsis thaliana the A function is supplied by APETALA1 (AP1, a MIKC-type MADS-domain transcription factor) and APETALA2 (AP2, an AP2/ERF-domain transcriptional repressor). The B function is supplied by the obligate MADS heterodimer APETALA3/PISTILLATA (AP3/PI) and the C function by AGAMOUS (AG). The E function is supplied redundantly by the four SEPALLATA MADS proteins (SEP1-SEP4). A alone specifies sepals (whorl 1), A+B petals (whorl 2), B+C stamens (whorl 3) and C carpels (whorl 4). E activity is required for B and C function: sep1 sep2 sep3 flowers make only sepals, and sep1 sep2 sep3 sep4 flowers make only leaf-like organs. At the molecular level, the floral quartet model proposes that each organ identity is set by a distinct tetrameric MADS complex. Two MADS dimers bind two CArG boxes and loop the DNA between them, with SEP proteins (above all SEP3) acting as the tetramerization hub. Expressing A or C together with B and E genes is enough to turn leaves into petal-like or stamen-like organs. A and C are mutually antagonistic. AP2 confines AG to the inner two whorls by recruiting the TOPLESS/HDA19 co-repressor, while AG excludes AP1 from the inner whorls. The SEP3-AG tetramer also terminates the floral meristem after carpel initiation. Floral meristem identity (LEAFY, AP1 meristem function, CAULIFLOWER, FRUITFULL, TERMINAL FLOWER1) is a separate, upstream module. LEAFY is cited here only as an activator of the B and C genes. The D-class ovule identity genes (STK/SHP) and the downstream organ differentiation programs are also outside this boundary.
