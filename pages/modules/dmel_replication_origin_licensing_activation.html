Replication origin licensing and activation in Drosophila melanogaster

Eukaryotic DNA replication starts in two steps that are separated in the cell cycle. In G1, the origin recognition complex (ORC) binds replication origins and, with Cdc6 and Cdt1 (Double parked in Drosophila), loads the MCM2-7 helicase core as an inactive double hexamer around double-stranded DNA; this is origin licensing. In S phase, the Dbf4-dependent kinase (Cdc7 with its regulatory subunit Chiffon) and cyclin-dependent kinases trigger the recruitment of Cdc45 and the four-subunit GINS complex onto MCM2-7, forming the active CMG (Cdc45-MCM-GINS) helicase that unwinds DNA at the replication fork. Restricting licensing to G1 and activation to S phase ensures that each origin fires only once per cell cycle.

Boundary. The module covers origin recognition, MCM2-7 loading and helicase activation. The FlyBase groups CMG, GINS and MCM2-7 overlap; MCM2-7 subunits appear both in the loaded MCM2-7 part and in the CMG part, and GINS is represented inside the CMG part rather than as a separate part. Cdc6 and dup (Cdt1) are not FlyBase members of these groups and were added as textbook licensing factors. Other firing factors (for example Mcm10, Treslin/Sld3 and TopBP1/Dpb11 homologs) and the replicative polymerases (see dmel_dna_polymerase_complexes) are outside the module.
