Bacterial gallate catabolism by the GalA-D-B-C route

A reusable bacterial module for oxidative cleavage of gallate and conversion of the ring-opened product to pyruvate and oxaloacetate. An optional outer-membrane porin can facilitate substrate entry. GalA then cleaves the aromatic ring to the keto tautomer of 4-oxalomesaconate, GalD supplies the enol tautomer used by GalB, GalB hydrates that intermediate to 4-carboxy-4-hydroxy-2-oxoadipate, and GalC performs the terminal aldol cleavage. Regulation and downstream central metabolism are outside the module boundary.

Genetic and biochemical analysis establishes the GalA, GalD, GalB, and GalC sequence and connects the terminal products to central metabolism.

GalP is optional because its porin activity and outer-membrane location are supported, but direct gallate specificity and pathway essentiality are not. The module begins chemically at extracellular or periplasm-accessible gallate and ends at pyruvate plus oxaloacetate. GalR regulation, the GalT inner-membrane transporter candidate, and metabolism of the terminal products are adjacent context rather than additional core reactions.
