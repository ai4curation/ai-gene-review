Hair-cell stereocilium bundle organization — cytoplasmic actin core with espin/plastin-1 cross-linkers, MYO15A-whirlin-EPS8 and MYO3A/MYO3B-espin-like tip length regulation, transient USH2 ankle-link complex, and MYO6/TRIOBP/PTPRQ base and rootlet anchoring; ACTB/ACTG1/ESPN/PLS1/MYO15A/WHRN/EPS8/MYO3A/MYO3B/ESPNL/USH2A/ADGRV1/PDZD7/MYO6/TRIOBP/PTPRQ

The hair bundle of a cochlear or vestibular hair cell is a staircase of stereocilia, actin-based protrusions that grow from the apical surface in rows of graded height. Each stereocilium is supported by a paracrystalline core of parallel actin filaments with their barbed ends at the tip. The core is built from the two cytoplasmic actins, beta-actin (ACTB) and gamma-actin (ACTG1), and is held together by actin cross-linkers, chiefly espin (ESPN) and plastin-1 (PLS1). The height of each row is set at the tip, where actin filaments elongate. The unconventional motor myosin XVa (MYO15A) carries the PDZ scaffold whirlin (WHRN) and the actin regulator EPS8 to the tips of the stereocilia; this tip complex is needed for the stereocilia to elongate. A second pair of plus-end-directed motors, the kinase-myosins MYO3A and MYO3B, carry espin-1 and espin-like (ESPNL) to the tips and limit or tune stereocilia length. During bundle development, ankle links join the bases of neighbouring stereocilia; they are formed by the large transmembrane proteins usherin (USH2A) and ADGRV1 (VLGR1), anchored on the cytoplasmic side by the PDZ scaffolds whirlin and PDZD7, and disappear as the bundle matures. At the base, each stereocilium tapers and continues into the cuticular plate as a dense actin rootlet; the rootlet is bundled by TRIOBP, the minus-end-directed myosin VI (MYO6) is needed to anchor the apical membrane to the cuticular plate and keep stereocilia separate, and the phosphoinositide phosphatase PTPRQ forms the shaft connectors near the base and is needed for bundle maturation. Variants in most of these genes cause hereditary hearing loss: MYO15A (DFNB3), WHRN (DFNB31 and Usher syndrome type 2D), ESPN, MYO3A (DFNB30), ACTG1 (DFNA20/26), MYO6, TRIOBP (DFNB28), PTPRQ, PLS1, and USH2A, ADGRV1 and PDZD7 (Usher syndrome type 2 and its modifiers).

(1) Participant rule. Almost every hair-cell gene is needed for a normal bundle, so necessity was not enough. Each member was asked which step of building the bundle it does: supplying the core polymer (actins), cross-linking it (ESPN, PLS1, TRIOBP), moving cargo to the tip or setting tip polymerisation (MYO15A, WHRN, EPS8, MYO3A/MYO3B, ESPNL), forming a link between stereocilia (USH2A, ADGRV1, WHRN, PDZD7, PTPRQ), or anchoring the base (MYO6). Transcription factors, planar-polarity proteins, the kinocilium and hair-cell-fate genes are out of scope even though their loss disrupts the bundle. (2) PLS3 excluded. PLS3 is left out rather than modelled on the RGD description alone; a hair-bundle proteomics check could bring it back. FSCN2 was considered as a third cross-linker. Its evidence for stereocilia is from mouse (the Fscn2 hearing-loss allele); the human GOA rows are retinal (TAS) or by IBA/ISS, so per the scope of this human module it is not added. (3) Tip complex. GPSM2 and GNAI3, which mouse work places at the tips of the tallest row with the MYO15A complex, were not added: human GPSM2 and GNAI3 rows in GOA concern spindle orientation and G-protein signalling, and no human stereocilium evidence was found. Part 5: transient ankle links between developing stereocilia

Part 6: anchoring of the stereocilium base, rootlet and apical membrane
MYO6, TRIOBP and PTPRQ at the stereocilium base and cuticular plateBiological Processbase_rootlet_anchoring

Each stereocilium tapers at its base and its core continues into the cuticular plate as a dense rootlet, which makes the pivot stiff and durable. TRIOBP bundles the rootlet actin. Myosin VI, the only minus-end-directed myosin, is needed to keep the apical membrane anchored to the cuticular plate; without it the stereocilia fuse. PTPRQ, a receptor-like phosphoinositide phosphatase, forms the shaft connectors near the base and is needed for the cochlear bundle to mature.
