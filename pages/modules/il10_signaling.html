Interleukin-10 signaling — IL-10 binding to IL10RA and recruitment of IL10RB, JAK1/TYK2 activation, STAT3 docking on phospho-IL10RA and STAT3-driven anti-inflammatory transcription; IL10/IL10RA/IL10RB/JAK1/TYK2/STAT3/STAT1

Interleukin-10 (IL-10), a domain-swapped homodimeric class II cytokine, binds its high-affinity receptor chain IL10RA (IL-10R1); the IL-10:IL10RA complex then recruits the shared low-affinity chain IL10RB (IL-10R2), which cannot bind IL-10 alone. Assembly of the ternary complex activates JAK1, bound to IL10RA, and TYK2, bound to IL10RB. The kinases phosphorylate two tyrosines in the IL10RA cytoplasmic domain that are the docking sites for STAT3; JAK-phosphorylated STAT3 dimerizes, enters the nucleus and drives the transcriptional programme that suppresses pro-inflammatory cytokine production in monocytes and macrophages. STAT1 (and STAT5 in some cells) is also activated but is not recruited through the STAT3 docking sites. Among the STAT3-induced genes is SOCS3, which does not inhibit the IL-10 receptor itself.

Derivation. This module is derived from RGD PW:0000515 (interleukin-10 signaling pathway; 7 RGD-curated human genes: IL10, IL10RA, IL10RB, JAK1, STAT1, STAT3, TYK2). All seven are modelled. The RGD text also describes upstream control of IL10 production (STAT3 downstream of IL-6 and IL-12, TLR2 and pathogen induction) and the many downstream immune effects; IL-10 production is a separate process (GO:0032613) outside this module, and the effects are outputs of the STAT3 transcription step. Relationship to il22_signaling and il6_signaling. IL10RB is the shared second chain of the IL-10, IL-22, IL-26 and type III interferon receptors. modules/il22_signaling.yaml models IL22RA1:IL10RB with JAK1 and TYK2; its receptor-assembly and JAK steps are IL-22-specific (they phosphorylate IL22RA1, not IL10RA), so no step of it is genuinely shared and none is module_ref'd. This module follows its conventions for the shared roles: JAK1 and TYK2 carry non-membrane spanning protein tyrosine kinase activity (GO:0004715) at cytosol/plasma membrane, and STAT3 carries DNA-binding transcription factor activity, RNA polymerase II-specific (GO:0000981). The one deliberate difference is IL10RB: il22_signaling gives it cytokine receptor activity (GO:0004896, from the PAINT node), whereas here it carries coreceptor activity (GO:0015026), the term of the IDA GOA row (PMID:16982608) and of the IL-10 GO-CAM, matching IL10RB's role as the low-affinity chain that only binds IL-10 after IL10RA has. modules/il6_signaling.yaml is IL-6/gp130-specific and exposes no shared step; it is referenced only for the SOCS3 contrast below. The generic STAT dimerization and nuclear import step and the STAT-dependent transcription step are included by module_ref to jak_stat_signaling#stat_dimerization_import and jak_stat_signaling#stat_transcription. SOCS3 (substrate and target-gene rule). RGD's text says IL-10-induced Socs3 inhibits JAK kinases and may terminate IL-10 effects. SOCS3 is indeed a STAT3 target induced by IL-10, but SOCS3 docks on gp130 and inhibits IL-6 signalling, and in SOCS3-deficient macrophages IL-10 signalling is unchanged (PMID:12754507; PMID:31611251). Being an induced gene is not participation in IL-10 signalling, and SOCS3 does no work at the IL-10 receptor, so SOCS3 is recorded as an output of the transcription step in prose and is not a part. SOCS3 is not on RGD's human gene list for this pathway either. STAT1 and STAT5. IL-10 activates STAT1 in T cells and monocytes and STAT5 in some cells, but the IL10RA phosphotyrosines recruit STAT3 and not STAT1 or STAT5 (PMID:8910398), and STAT3 with JAK1 is required for the anti-inflammatory action (PMID:10347215). STAT1 (on RGD's list) is therefore an optional, accessory part; STAT5 is not modelled. Residue numbering. The two STAT3 docking tyrosines were mapped in the murine receptor (Tyr427 and Tyr477, in the YQKQ and YLKQ motifs). Human residue numbers are not asserted here because they were not verified against the human IL10RA sequence. Functions and their support. Part 1: receptor complex assembly
IL-10 binds IL10RA and recruits IL10RBRegulatory Stepil10_receptor_assembly

The IL-10 dimer binds two IL10RA chains with high affinity; IL10RA binding changes the conformation of IL-10 helix A, creating the site that the low-affinity IL10RB chain then engages, so the active complex is assembled in sequence (IL-10:IL10RA first, then IL10RB) as a 2:2:2-type ternary complex at the plasma membrane.
