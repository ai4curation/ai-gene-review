Endolysosomal calcium release — NAADP- and PI(3,5)P2-activated two-pore channels (TPCN1, TPCN2, with the NAADP-binding proteins JPT2 and LSM12) and PI(3,5)P2-activated mucolipin TRPML channels (MCOLN1, MCOLN2, MCOLN3) releasing calcium from endosomes and lysosomes into the cytosol (human)

Endosomes and lysosomes are acidic calcium stores whose luminal free calcium is in the high micromolar range. Two families of cation channels in their limiting membranes release this calcium into the cytosol. The two-pore channels TPC1 (TPCN1), mainly in endosomes, and TPC2 (TPCN2), mainly in late endosomes and lysosomes, are dimeric channels with two Shaker-like repeats per subunit. They are the channels through which the second messenger NAADP releases calcium from acidic organelles; NAADP does not bind the channel itself but small NAADP-binding proteins, JPT2 (HN1L) and LSM12, that associate with the TPCs and confer NAADP sensitivity. TPCs are also opened by the endolysosome-specific lipid PI(3,5)P2, and under PI(3,5)P2 they conduct mainly Na+; their ion selectivity is debated and appears to depend on the activating ligand, NAADP favouring a calcium-permeable state. NAADP-evoked endolysosomal calcium release is often small and local and can be amplified by calcium-induced calcium release through ER IP3 receptors. The mucolipins TRPML1 (MCOLN1), TRPML2 (MCOLN2) and TRPML3 (MCOLN3) are tetrameric TRP channels of the endolysosomal system that are activated by PI(3,5)P2 and release calcium (TRPML1 also Fe2+ and Zn2+) from late endosomes and lysosomes; TRPML1 is ubiquitous, TRPML2 is enriched in immune cells and recycling endosomes, and TRPML3 in early endosomes and in melanocytes and hair cells. Loss of TRPML1 causes mucolipidosis type IV. Local calcium released by these channels drives membrane fusion and fission in the late endocytic pathway, lysosomal exocytosis, and activation of calcineurin, which dephosphorylates TFEB to induce lysosome biogenesis and autophagy.

PW:0001139 is the only PW evidence source_id; its sub-pathway PW:0001140 (calcium/calcium-mediated signaling) is mentioned here in prose only. (1) STRUCTURE. Two parts. Part 1 (optional) is NAADP reception by the NAADP-binding proteins JPT2 and LSM12, which regulates the TPC route only. Part 2 is calcium release, a ONE_OR_MORE ROUTE variant set of two unrelated channel families (TPC and TRPML), each a ONE_OR_MORE REALIZER set of paralogs, because cells express several of them and they act in parallel on overlapping compartments. Each TPC paralog carries two annotons, one per activating ligand (NAADP; PI(3,5)P2), because the experimental record types the two gating modes with different activities and different permeant ions (note 3). (2) MEMBERS ADDED BEYOND RGD'S LIST. RGD lists TPCN1, TPCN2 and MCOLN1. MCOLN2 and MCOLN3 are added because direct patch-clamp of endolysosomes shows PI(3,5)P2 activating TRPML2 and TRPML3 currents (PMID:20802798), TRPML3 is characterized as a calcium channel of endosomes (PMID:19522758, PMID:19497048), and both have experimental endosomal and lysosomal membrane locations in GOA. Their channel activity is authored (note 4). JPT2 (HN1L) and LSM12 are added as NAADP-binding proteins that confer NAADP sensitivity on the TPCs (PMID:33758061, PMID:34362892). They were identified independently and their relationship (alternative or cooperating subunits of one NAADP receptor complex) is unresolved; both are therefore in a ONE_OR_MORE set. JPT2 also couples NAADP to RYR1 on the ER in T cells (PMID:33758062); that ER route is outside this module (RYR1 is in er_calcium_store_cycling) and is noted only. (3) TPC ION SELECTIVITY: A RECORDED CONTROVERSY, NOT RESOLVED HERE. The NAADP-receptor papers (PMID:19387438, PMID:19620632) report TPC-dependent NAADP-evoked calcium release, and GOA has GO:0072345 NAADP-sensitive calcium-release channel activity by IDA for TPCN1 (PMID:19620632) and TPCN2 (PMID:19387438, PMID:19620632, PMID:20880839, PMID:32167471). Endolysosome patch-clamp from TPC double-knockout mice found PI(3,5)P2-activated, Na+-selective channels insensitive to NAADP (PMID:23063126), and TPC1 was described as a voltage-gated Na+ channel (PMID:24776928); from these papers GOA has NOT rows for GO:0072345 (TPCN1 PMID:24776928; TPCN2 PMID:23063126), positive rows for GO:0015280 ligand-gated sodium channel activity and GO:0097682 intracellularly phosphatidylinositol-3,5-bisphosphate-gated monatomic cation channel activity (both paralogs), GO:0005248 voltage-gated sodium channel activity for TPCN1 and NOT for TPCN2. The later finding that NAADP is sensed through JPT2/LSM12 rather than the channel protein explains why NAADP did not activate isolated TPCs, and TPC2 selectivity was shown to depend on the agonist (PMID:32167471): NAADP-mimetic agonism gives calcium-permeable non-selective currents, PI(3,5)P2-mimetic agonism Na+-selective currents. The module models both experimentally typed gating modes for each paralog: GO:0072345 for the NAADP mode (the GO definition allows NAADP to be bound by "the channel complex or one of its constituent parts", which covers binding by JPT2/LSM12) and GO:0097682 for the PI(3,5)P2 mode (cation-generic, so it does not decide Na+ versus calcium). Whether native PI(3,5)P2-activated TPCs release physiologically meaningful calcium remains open. GO:0015280 and GO:0005248 (sodium channel activities) are not used as separate annotons: they describe the same PI(3,5)P2- or voltage-gated pore whose cation-generic function is already on the PI(3,5)P2 annoton, and Na+ flux is recorded on that annoton's process (GO:0035725 sodium ion transmembrane transport, IDA for TPCN2, PMID:30860481; authored for TPCN1).
