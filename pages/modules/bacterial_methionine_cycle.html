Bacterial methionine cycle and homocysteine remethylation

A bacterial cycle converts L-methionine to S-adenosyl-L-methionine (SAM), uses SAM in SAH-producing methyl-transfer reactions, regenerates homocysteine by alternative SAH-processing chemistries, and remethylates homocysteine. Direct SAH hydrolysis, Pfs/LuxS cleavage and deamination followed by SIH hydrolysis have distinct enzyme and evidence requirements. Homocysteine remethylation varies with folate, cobalamin, external methyl donors and enzyme partners. De novo sulfur assimilation, donor uptake, methylthioadenosine salvage and downstream AI-2 reception are adjacent biology. The cycle is an open metabolic system requiring energy and methyl input, not a claim of universal bacterial distribution or demonstrated native flux in every represented species.

This bacterial module is intentionally separate from the existing eukaryotic methionine_cycle, sam_cycle, and homocysteine_metabolism modules. De novo methionine biosynthesis remains in methionine_biosynthesis. Cysteine biosynthesis, transsulfuration, sulfur assimilation, methionine degradation, and methylthioadenosine salvage are outside this cycle boundary.
