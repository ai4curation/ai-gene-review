De novo UMP biosynthesis and conversion to UTP and CTP

The conserved six-reaction pathway that builds UMP from glutamine, hydrogencarbonate, ATP, aspartate, and PRPP, followed by phosphorylation of UMP to UTP and amination of UTP to CTP. Carbamoyl phosphate is formed first, followed by assembly and oxidation of the pyrimidine ring, attachment to phosphoribose, and decarboxylation to UMP. UMP kinase and nucleoside-diphosphate kinase then make UTP, and CTP synthase transfers the glutamine amide nitrogen to UTP to make CTP. Enzyme architecture varies: bacteria commonly use separate CarA, CarB, PyrB, PyrC, PyrE, and PyrF proteins, whereas animals fuse the first three activities in CAD and the final two activities in UMPS; fungi fuse the carbamoyl-phosphate synthetase and aspartate carbamoyltransferase in one protein that also carries an inactive dihydroorotase-like domain, and use separate dihydroorotase, orotate phosphoribosyltransferase and OMP decarboxylase proteins. These fusions do not change the chemical leaves represented by the module. Dihydroorotate oxidation has two implementations: membrane-associated quinone-dependent (class 2) enzymes, which in eukaryotes sit on the mitochondrial inner membrane and couple pyrimidine synthesis to respiration, and soluble fumarate-dependent (class 1A) enzymes, acquired by Saccharomyces yeasts from bacteria, which allow pyrimidine synthesis without oxygen. Bacteria phosphorylate UMP with a PyrH-type UMP kinase, eukaryotes with a UMP/CMP kinase of the adenylate kinase family.

De novo UMP biosynthesis and conversion to UTP and CTPMetabolic Pathwayde_novo_pyrimidine_synthesis
'de novo' UMP biosynthetic processGO:0044205 'de novo' CTP biosynthetic processGO:0044210

ALIGN with the YeastPathways crosswalk (PWY-5686-1, PYRIMID-RNTSYN-PWY, PWY0-162, PWY-7176). The six chemical leaves are conserved despite taxon-specific protein architecture: bacteria commonly use separate CarA/CarB/PyrB/PyrC/PyrE/PyrF chains, animals fuse the first three activities in CAD and the last two in UMPS, and fungi use a CPSase-ATCase fusion (S. cerevisiae URA2, S. pombe ura1) whose dihydroorotase-like domain is inactive (PMID:2679804), plus separate URA4/ura2 dihydroorotase, URA5+URA10/ura5 OPRTase and URA3/ura4 ODCase. Fungal gene names are not orthologous between the two yeasts (S. cerevisiae URA1 = DHODH but S. pombe ura1 = CPSase-ATCase). Dihydroorotate oxidation is modelled as two variants: class 2 quinone DHODH (bacteria, animals, S. pombe ura3 in mitochondria) and the class 1A fumarate DHODH of S. cerevisiae URA1, which is cytosolic and of bacterial origin (PMID:1409592, PMID:15014982). The UMP to UTP and CTP steps (URA6, YNK1, URA7/URA8) were added as part 7 per the crosswalk; bacterial PyrH and eukaryotic UMP/CMP kinases are unrelated families and are modelled as variants. YeastCyc corrections: CPA1/CPA2 are listed for CARBPSYN-RXN in PWY-5686-1 but form the arginine-specific CPSase A (committed CPA1/CPA2 reviews) and are not exemplars here, although CPSase A carbamoyl phosphate can partly reach the pyrimidine pathway; PYRIMID-RNTSYN-PWY draws URA1 with the quinone-dependent reaction (EC 1.3.5.2) instead of the fumarate reaction; UDP kinase has no gene in YeastCyc (YNK1 added, as in the GO-CAM). GO-CAM disagreements: YeastPathways_PYRIMID-RNTSYN-PWY gives URA1 the generic GO:0004152 with no location, URA6 the obsolete GO:0004127 in several models, and labels the pathway GO:0006207 'de novo' pyrimidine nucleobase biosynthetic process although its products are nucleotides. The PomBase model 69a0c46f00003691 (listed in the crosswalk under pyrimidine_salvage) supplies the S. pombe exemplars. Carbamoyl phosphate also feeds arginine biosynthesis, so pathway assignment of bacterial CarA/CarB requires process context rather than reaction identity alone; pyrimidine CPS II must not be conflated with mitochondrial urea-cycle CPS1. PRPP supply (pentose_phosphate_pathway) is upstream and pyrimidine salvage (pyrimidine_salvage) and deoxynucleotide synthesis (dntp_de_novo_synthesis) are separate modules. Regulation (outside the core): UTP feedback-inhibits URA2 CPSase and Ppr1p senses dihydroorotate and orotate to activate later URA genes (PMID:9858611). Human disease and drug examples remain useful context: UMPS deficiency causes hereditary orotic aciduria, DHODH deficiency causes Miller syndrome, CAD deficiency causes a uridine-responsive developmental epileptic encephalopathy, and DHODH is targeted by leflunomide/teriflunomide. Pseudomonas PyrC' is a lineage-specific inactive dihydroorotase-like structural partner of PyrB and is not an additional dihydroorotase reaction leaf.
