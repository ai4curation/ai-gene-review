Proteobacterial nitrogen-regulatory PTS phosphorelay

A regulatory protein phosphorelay in Pseudomonadota in which PtsP enzyme I(Ntr) receives phosphate from phosphoenolpyruvate, a native NPr/HPr carrier relays it to PtsN, and the phosphorylation state of PtsN modulates noncovalent regulation of downstream proteins. The conserved relay is distinct from the species-dependent choice of target and from phosphorylation-coupled sugar transport.

All Pseudomonas strains used in this work were derived from strain P. putida MAD2.

The demonstrated scope includes E. coli K-12, P. putida KT2440-derived strains and C. vibrioides NA1000 (historically C. crescentus); it is not a universal-presence claim for Pseudomonadota. The three required roles must be supported together in the target organism: mixing exemplars from different species does not establish a native route. In KT2440 the established ptsH/Q88PA2/PP_0948 record is the protein called ptsO or NPr in the literature. In NA1000 the carrier is legitimately called HPr. PtsP and NPr are represented as curated role families without an exact PANTHER selector because the available subdivisions do not coincide with those native roles. Their broader PANTHER classifications are reviewed separately, not silently promoted to pathway membership. PtsN is grounded to PTHR47738:SF1, not the heterogeneous parent family. No new ancestral gain/loss node is asserted. Individual gene reviews retain unresolved sugar-process or kinase-output annotations where phylogenetic or experimental evidence does not settle them; the module relies on their independently supported core activities.
