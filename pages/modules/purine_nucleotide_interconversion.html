Purine ribonucleotide interconversion (IMP to AMP/GMP and their di- and triphosphates)

Inosine monophosphate (IMP), the first complete purine nucleotide made by de novo synthesis or by salvage, is the branch point from which the adenine and guanine ribonucleotides are made. Toward AMP, adenylosuccinate synthetase condenses IMP with L-aspartate using GTP to form adenylosuccinate, which adenylosuccinate lyase cleaves to AMP and fumarate. Toward GMP, IMP dehydrogenase oxidises IMP to xanthosine monophosphate (XMP) with NAD+ (the committed, rate-limiting step of guanine-nucleotide synthesis) and GMP synthase aminates XMP to GMP using the amide nitrogen of glutamine. The monophosphates are then phosphorylated by adenylate kinase (AMP to ADP) and guanylate kinase (GMP to GDP), and nucleoside-diphosphate kinase transfers the gamma-phosphate of ATP to GDP (and other diphosphates) to make GTP; ADP is converted to ATP mainly by energy metabolism. AMP deaminase hydrolytically deaminates AMP back to IMP; with adenylosuccinate synthetase and lyase this forms the purine nucleotide cycle, which in animal muscle buffers the adenylate energy charge and releases fumarate and ammonia. Because GTP is required to make AMP and ATP to make GMP, these reactions cross-regulate the adenine and guanine nucleotide pools. The enzymes are cytosolic and conserved from bacteria to fungi and animals, with lineage-specific isozymes (for example, muscle and liver adenylosuccinate synthetases and three AMP deaminases in mammals, and three expressed IMP dehydrogenases in budding yeast). Inherited defects of the human enzymes include ADSS1 distal myopathy, IMPDH1 retinitis pigmentosa, IMPDH2 neurodevelopmental dystonia, myoadenylate deaminase (AMPD1) deficiency and AMPD2 pontocerebellar hypoplasia; IMP dehydrogenase is the target of mycophenolic acid and ribavirin.
