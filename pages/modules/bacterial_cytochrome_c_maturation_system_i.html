Bacterial cytochrome c maturation system I

Bacterial cytochrome c maturation System I couples heme handling and ATP-dependent processing to covalent attachment of heme to exported c-type cytochrome apoproteins. The canonical histidine-CcmE route uses CcmG-supported redox preparation and CcmFH ligation, with lineage-dependent fused or separate CcmI-like accessories. The experimentally reconstructed G20 System I* route uses cysteine-linked CcmE and functions without CcmG/H; its CcmI enhances yield. Sec export, heme biosynthesis and lineage-specific upstream reductant supply are external dependencies. Archaeal and mitochondrial implementations are outside this bacterial module.

Engineered heme/expression conditions do not establish that native cells ordinarily dispense with upstream delivery.

The bacterial taxon is a scope boundary, not a universality assertion. Complete reviewed exemplar panels cover E. coli K-12, P. putida KT2440 and the G20 System I* cassette; RHOCB SB1003-reference proteins and SHEON MR1 CcmB provide selected experimentally informative roles, not complete-route panels. Rhodobacter assay strains/constructs and the G20 CcmC numbering discrepancy are documented in individual source analyses. Family labels alone do not define role: PTHR47870 SF1 contains both redox CcmH and accessory CcmI, PTHR30071 includes other heme-handling systems, and PTHR34128 includes both CcmE linkage chemistries. No ancestral node is invented for newly added exemplars. Conflicting CcmB and CcmCD conduit models remain explicit; structural/contact and conditional overproduction evidence do not settle a universal native conduit. System II and mitochondrial System III are separate implementations outside this boundary. CcmG redox bypasses in altered backgrounds and thiol-independent handling mean this canonical route is not an exhaustive list of all experimental rescue configurations. Engineered CcmFGH-only expression can bypass CcmABCDE under special heme/expression conditions (PMID:24397552); the canonical capacity route models ordinary upstream delivery, not all synthetic bypasses.
