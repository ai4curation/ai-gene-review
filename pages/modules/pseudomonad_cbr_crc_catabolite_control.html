Pseudomonad CbrA/CbrB carbon catabolite control via Crc/Hfq

The pseudomonad CbrA/CbrB two-component system and the post-transcriptional machinery it controls, which together set carbon catabolite repression and balance carbon against nitrogen utilization. The sensor histidine kinase CbrA phosphorylates the conserved receiver aspartate of CbrB, and phosphorylated CbrB acts as a sigma-54-dependent enhancer-binding activator of the crcZ and crcY small-RNA genes. Those small RNAs act as decoys for the Hfq/Crc translational repressor pair: when carbon is poor and crcZ/crcY levels are high, Hfq/Crc is sequestered away from the A-rich catabolite-activity motifs near the ribosome-binding sites of catabolic mRNAs, relieving translational repression. The module spans sensing, phosphotransfer, sigma-54-dependent small-RNA induction, and the translational repression step that the small RNAs antagonise.

The module is scoped to Pseudomonas because the CbrA/CbrB-crcZ/crcY-Crc/Hfq arrangement is characterised in that genus. The sensor kinase CbrA has no gene review in this repository, so the sensor tier is modelled as an abstract phosphorelay-sensor-kinase role; its partner relationship is grounded through the CbrB review. The crcZ and crcY decoy small RNAs are central to the mechanism but are non-coding and therefore appear as the described input to the repression step rather than as protein participants. Crc is likewise described as the co-repressor that acts with Hfq, but is not asserted as a module member because it has no gene review here. Note that UniProt entry Q88DX2 carries the name AlgB by homology while the KT2440 literature studies this locus as CbrB; the gene review records that discrepancy. The wider CbrB regulon and the downstream catabolic pathways whose mRNAs are repressed are separate modules.
