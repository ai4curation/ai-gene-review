SAGA histone acetyltransferase (HAT) module in Saccharomyces cerevisiae

The histone acetyltransferase (HAT) module is the Gcn5-containing subcomplex that carries out histone acetylation for the budding yeast SAGA coactivator, its variant SLIK (also called SALSA), and the smaller ADA complex. It has four subunits: the acetyltransferase Gcn5, the adaptor Ada2, Ngg1 (also called Ada3) and the reader Sgf29. Recombinant Gcn5 acetylates free histones and histone peptides but not nucleosomes. Ada2 binds Gcn5 and raises its activity, in part by increasing its affinity for acetyl-CoA, and links Ada3 to Gcn5; Ada2, Ada3 and Gcn5 together form a catalytic core that suffices for nucleosomal acetylation and for the expanded set of histone H3 lysines acetylated by the native complexes. Sgf29 sits at the periphery of that core. Its tandem Tudor domain binds histone H3 di- or trimethylated at lysine 4, a mark of active promoters, and lets the module acetylate H3K4me3 nucleosomes preferentially and processively; it is needed in vivo for normal H3 acetylation but not for activity on peptides. Ada2 tethers the module to the rest of SAGA through its SWIRM domain, which is not needed for assembly of the ADA complex. The module acetylates histone H3 and H2B tails in promoter chromatin and so contributes to SAGA's coactivation of RNA polymerase II transcription.

Boundary. The module contains the four HAT-module subunits: Gcn5 (catalytic), Ada2 (Gcn5 activator and module anchor), Ngg1/Ada3 and Sgf29 (H3K4me3 reader). Gcn5, Ada2 and Ada3 are modelled together as the catalytic core because together they are necessary and sufficient for nucleosomal acetylation (PMID:11773077); the core annoton lists each as an active unit. Sgf29 is a separate part: it is not needed for activity on peptides or for SAGA integrity, but it targets the module to H3K4me3 nucleosomes. The other SAGA modules (the Tra1/Spt activator-binding module, the TAF core and the Ubp8 deubiquitination module) and the ADA-specific subunits Ahc1 and Ahc2 are not parts; they are the host complexes' own components and belong to SAGA- and ADA-level modules. Histones are substrates, not parts. Ontology. GO has a term for SAGA's deubiquitination module (GO:0071819 DUBm complex) but none for the HAT module, although the GO:0000124 SAGA complex definition lists the module's subunits. The module concept therefore carries no GO id and a term is requested under proposed_new_terms. The three host complexes are GO:0000124 SAGA complex, GO:0046695 SLIK (SAGA-like) complex and GO:0140671 ADA complex, all is_a GO:0070461 SAGA-type complex. GO:0070461 is not the module: it names whole complexes, and its definition says they include TAFs, although the ADA complex classified under it has neither TAFs nor Spt proteins; that mismatch is recorded under proposed_ontology_changes. The gene reviews use GO:0000124 for in_complex because every subunit carries it. Chromatin. GO:0070461 SAGA-type complex is capable of histone acetyltransferase activity, so a reasoner classifies it, its three children and the proposed HAT module term under GO:0000123 histone acetyltransferase complex, which is part_of GO:0000785 chromatin. For this module that inherited location is reasonable: Gcn5 alone cannot acetylate nucleosomes, the module's distinguishing activity is acetylation of nucleosomal histones, and Sgf29 reads a mark on chromatin. Unlike HAT-B (modules/scer_hat_b_complex.yaml) and Rtt109-Vps75 (modules/scer_rtt109_vps75_complex.yaml), no activity of this module on non-chromatin histones is documented here, so no further change to the GO:0000123 edge is proposed. Gene reviews. GCN5, NGG1 and SGF29 have reviews in this repository; ADA2 is being reviewed in parallel. The Gcn5 unit uses GO:0010484 histone H3 acetyltransferase activity, the GCN5 core function, although the module also acetylates H2B. The Ada3 unit uses GO:0004402 histone acetyltransferase activity because the NGG1 review records it as a contribution to that activity; Ada3 has no catalytic activity. The Ada2 unit uses GO:0010698 acetyltransferase activator activity, supported by Ada2's stimulation of Gcn5 and its effect on acetyl-CoA binding (PMID:11773077, PMID:30224453).
