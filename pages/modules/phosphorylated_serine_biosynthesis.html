Phosphorylated L-serine biosynthesis

A reusable three-reaction pathway that converts the glycolytic intermediate 3-phospho-D-glycerate to L-serine through 3-phosphooxypyruvate and O-phospho-L-serine. NAD+-dependent D-3-phosphoglycerate dehydrogenase catalyses the first, committed oxidation; a pyridoxal 5'-phosphate-dependent phosphoserine aminotransferase transfers the amino group of L-glutamate to 3-phosphooxypyruvate; and a Mg2+-dependent HAD-family phosphoserine phosphatase releases free L-serine. The module represents the conserved SerA, SerC and SerB reaction roles independently of genomic arrangement and of which paralog carries them: budding yeast, for example, has two phosphoglycerate dehydrogenase paralogs. L-serine made this way is also the main precursor of glycine and of folate one-carbon units, but those conversions, and the alternative glyoxylate and threonine routes to glycine and serine, are separate modules. Additional activities of individual enzymes, including 2-oxoglutarate reduction to D-2-hydroxyglutarate by some SerA proteins and vitamin B6 precursor transamination by some SerC proteins, are outside this pathway boundary.

L-serine biosynthetic processGO:0006564

Exact UniProt exemplars delimit the three reaction roles without restricting the module taxonomically. No cellular location or molecular function is repeated at module level; in both yeasts all three enzymes are cytosolic, but plants run the pathway in plastids, so no compartment is asserted. SerC can also catalyze phosphohydroxythreonine transamination in DXP-dependent vitamin B6 biosynthesis, but that separate reaction is not a fourth part of this module. Some bacterial SerA proteins use an ACT domain for L-serine feedback, but effector sensitivity is a lineage- and sequence-dependent regulatory property rather than an additional pathway part. No ancestral PTN is asserted: the local PAINT exports identify family-level IBD nodes but do not establish that the PSEPK targets descend from those nodes. ALIGN with YeastPathways (2026-10): SERSYN-PWY maps one-to-one onto the three parts and its catalysts (SER3, SER33, SER1, SER2) are all correct; no yeast enzyme is missing. The reverse-written YeastCyc transaminase equation (PSERTRANSAM-RXN) is the same reaction. The step-1 family anchor PTHR43761:SF1 was removed because S. cerevisiae SER3/SER33 are classified in PTHR42789:SF1 while S. pombe ser3 and the bacterial enzymes are in PTHR43761:SF1; the role is defined by GO:0004617 instead. Ser3/Ser33 also reduce 2-oxoglutarate to D-2-hydroxyglutarate (side activity, excluded). The yeast glyoxylate (AGX1 plus SHMT) and threonine aldolase (GLY1) routes to serine and glycine, which make the SER pathway conditionally dispensable on non-fermentable carbon sources, are in glycine_serine_interconversion. GO-CAM comparison: both models agree with the module (MF, process, cytosol); the YeastPathways model has no causal edges, and the PomBase model links ser3 -> ser1 -> ser2 with RO:0002413.
