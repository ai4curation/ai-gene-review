Ciliary transition zone MKS, NPHP and CEP290 modules

The ciliary transition zone (TZ) is the proximal region of the cilium, just distal to the basal body, where Y-shaped links connect the axonemal microtubules to the ciliary membrane. It acts as a gate that controls which proteins enter and leave the cilium. In nematodes and mammals the TZ is built from the same set of conserved, ciliopathy-associated protein modules. An RPGRIP1L-family assembly hub (MKS-5 in C. elegans) is needed for essentially all other TZ proteins to reach the TZ. CEP290 is a large coiled-coil protein that binds microtubules and membranes; in C. elegans it is a core component of the central cylinder and is needed to localise the MKS module but not the NPHP module. The MKS module contains the three B9-domain proteins MKS1, B9D1 and B9D2, the C2-domain protein CC2D2A, the Tectonic proteins and several transmembrane proteins (TMEM67/meckelin, TMEM216, TMEM107, TMEM231, TMEM237, TMEM17, TMEM218). The NPHP module contains the nephrocystins NPHP1 and NPHP4. The MKS and NPHP modules are partially redundant. Loss of one module leaves cilia nearly normal, whereas combined loss of both causes severe defects in nematodes and mice: the basal body and TZ fail to attach to the membrane, Y-links are lost, axonemes are malformed and non-ciliary membrane proteins leak into the cilium. A further CEP290-dependent branch containing TMEM138 and CDKL1 belongs to neither module. Mutations in the human genes cause Meckel-Gruber syndrome, Joubert syndrome and nephronophthisis. In mammals the nephrocystins also act at epithelial cell-cell junctions, a role outside this module.

Generalisation. This module was first written as a C. elegans-only module (c_elegans_ciliary_transition_zone) and was generalised to Bilateria. Each role is a FAMILY with the C. elegans protein and, where a gene review exists, the human ortholog as representative members. The C. elegans genetics (synthetic defects between but not within modules, and the localisation hierarchy) still provide most of the evidence for the module boundary. The same boundary is supported in mammals by mouse Tctn1;Nphp4 synthetic phenotypes (PMID:26540106), by the NPHP1-4-8, NPHP5-6 and MKS interaction modules (PMID:21565611) and by the Tctn1-containing MKS complex (PMID:21725307). Taxon scope. The context is Bilateria because the module structure, not just the presence of the genes, has been tested only in nematodes and mammals. Many of these proteins are older. In Chlamydomonas NPHP4 sits at the distal TZ near the membrane, distal to CEP290, and the two are incorporated independently (PMID:25150219), which matches the separation of the CEP290 and NPHP roles here. A TMEM17 paper describes TMEM17 as conserved across ciliated eukaryotes (PMID:40841990). Whether the MKS-NPHP redundancy and the RPGRIP1L-family hub hold outside animals has not been tested in the cached evidence, so the broader clade is not asserted. Redundancy. The genetic redundancy between the MKS and NPHP modules is the defining property of this system. It is recorded in the root description, the part descriptions and the evidence rather than as a connection, because the connection vocabulary has no relation for redundancy. The RPGRIP1L-family hub is the exception to module assignment: mks-5 interacts genetically with members of both modules. The human RPGRIP1L review places RPGRIP1L (NPHP8) in the NPHP complex, because it binds NPHP4 directly (PMID:21565611); it is modelled here as the hub part, consistent with the worm assembly data. CEP290. CEP290 is modelled as its own part, distinct from the MKS module, following the human CEP290 review and the mammalian NPHP5-6 module. In C. elegans it behaves genetically like an MKS-module assembly factor and is needed to localise MKS-module proteins and TMEM-138/CDKL-1. Whether worm CEP-290 needs MKS-5 to reach the TZ is disputed: Li et al. 2016 (PMID:26982032) report that it does, while Schouteden et al. 2015 (PMID:26124290) found CCEP-290 recruitment unaffected in mks-5 mutants. The hub-to-CEP290 connection is therefore recorded with that caveat. The worm gene symbol is ccep-290 (WormBase); the literature also writes CEP-290. MKS module layering. Worm TMEM-107 occupies an intermediate layer of the MKS module and recruits MKS-1, TMEM-231, JBTS-14/TMEM237 and TMEM-17 (PMID:26595381). TMEM-17 and TMEM-218 are peripheral members whose loss does not disrupt the core. TCTN-1 is assigned to the MKS module on genetic grounds (synthetic defects with nphp-1 and nphp-4 but not with MKS-module genes); unlike mouse Tctn1 it is not needed to localise the other worm TZ proteins tested (PMID:26540106). TMEM138 branch. Worm TMEM-138 and CDKL-1 need MKS-5 and CEP-290 for TZ localisation but behave as neither MKS nor NPHP module proteins (PMID:26982032). TMEM-138 is modelled as an optional part, not as an MKS-module member. CDKL-1 has no gene review and is named in text only. Not modelled. NPHP-2/inversin (genes/worm/nphp-2) localises to the inversin compartment distal to the TZ and is outside this boundary. The mammalian NPHP1/NPHP4 roles at epithelial cell-cell junctions are recorded in their gene reviews and are outside this boundary. Human TMEM216, TMEM107, TMEM231, TMEM237, TMEM17 and TMEM218 have no gene reviews yet, so those roles carry only the C. elegans representative. Gene names. MKSR-2 and MKSR-1 were earlier named TZA-1 (B9D2) and TZA-2 (B9D1), and MKS-1 was XBX-7 (PMID:18337471). JBTS-14 is also called TMEM-237. Molecular functions. MFs follow the gene reviews' core functions. Most TZ reviews assert no GO MF, because none fits a structural TZ subunit and the generic structural molecule activity term is not used here; those annotons carry a preferred_term only. Where the worm and human reviews differ, the annoton description says which review asserts the MF. PANTHER grounding. Family ids are given only where the worm and human representatives share the PANTHER family or subfamily in their UniProt records, or where the worm protein's family is named for the protein (TMEM107, TMEM237, TMEM138). The ids were checked against interpro/panther/panther.obo. CEP290 is left without a family id because the worm and human proteins fall in different PANTHER families (PTHR43941 and PTHR18879). MKS-2/TMEM216 and TMEM-17 share PTHR13531, whose name does not describe either protein, and TMEM-231 is in PTHR14605, so those are also left without ids. Interfaces. IFT (MODULE:intraflagellar_transport) and the BBSome (MODULE:bbsome) act after or alongside the TZ: IFT rates are largely normal in worm TZ mutants, and IFT and BBS proteins are not needed to form the TZ or to dock it to the membrane (PMID:21422230). NPHP-1 and NPHP-4 do modulate ciliary access of IFT components (PMID:18316409).
