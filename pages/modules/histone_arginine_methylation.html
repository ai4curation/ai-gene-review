Human histone arginine methylation by the PRMT family — type I asymmetric dimethylation by PRMT1 (H4R3), CARM1/PRMT4 (H3R17, H3R26), PRMT6 (H3R2) and weakly PRMT2 (H4), type II symmetric dimethylation of H4R3 and H3R8 by PRMT5 with WDR77 (and COPR5 for H4R3), and type III monomethylation of histones by PRMT7

Arginine residues in the histone tails are methylated by protein arginine methyltransferases (PRMTs), which transfer methyl groups from S-adenosyl-L-methionine to the guanidino nitrogens of arginine and release S-adenosyl-L-homocysteine. All PRMTs make omega-N-monomethylarginine (MMA). Type I enzymes add a second methyl group to the same nitrogen and make asymmetric dimethylarginine (ADMA); the type II enzyme PRMT5 adds it to the other terminal nitrogen and makes symmetric dimethylarginine (SDMA); the type III enzyme PRMT7 stops at MMA. On histones the principal type I marks are H4R3me2a, deposited by PRMT1, the major H4R3 methyltransferase of human cells; H3R17me2a and H3R26me2a, deposited by the coactivator CARM1 (PRMT4); and H3R2me2a, deposited by PRMT6. PRMT2 also makes MMA and ADMA on histone H4, but with a turnover about 800-fold lower than PRMT1. PRMT1 and CARM1 are recruited as transcriptional coactivators by nuclear receptors and other transcription factors, and their marks are associated with gene activation, whereas H3R2me2a by PRMT6 prevents H3K4 trimethylation by MLL complexes and is repressive. PRMT5 acts with its obligate WD40 partner WDR77 (MEP50) and symmetrically dimethylates H4R3 and H3R8; the histone-binding adaptor COPR5 (COPRS) directs nuclear PRMT5-WDR77 to H4R3 and to nucleosomes, and the symmetric marks are associated with transcriptional repression. Asymmetric and symmetric dimethylation of the same residue, H4R3, are mutually exclusive and have opposite transcriptional effects. Human PRMT7 methylates histones in vitro; it is a strict monomethylase, and its role on histones in cells is unsettled.

PRMT2 carries an IDA row for the general term histone methyltransferase activity from PMID:19405910, the only experimental methyltransferase row for this gene. The paper assayed histone H4 and found MMA and ADMA, so the histone H4 child term is used; no residue was resolved in the abstract, so no site-specific term is asserted. The activity is real but weak in vitro (kcat 800-fold below PRMT1), and no study cached here shows PRMT2-dependent histone methylation in cells; PRMT2 is kept as a minor variant because RGD lists it and the activity was measured directly. Its coactivator rows (PMID:12039952, PMID:17587566) are not modelled.

PRMT7 carries an IDA row for histone H4 methyltransferase activity (PMID:15494416), in which immunopurified human PRMT7 methylated histones and an H4 peptide. That paper reported symmetric dimethylarginine as the main product and called PRMT7 a type II enzyme; this was not borne out: purified PRMT7 makes only MMA (PMID:24247247, recombinant mouse PRMT7, used here only for the product type), and the RGD source review classes it as the type III enzyme. The product is therefore written as MMA, and the process is the omega-N-methylation parent rather than either dimethylation child. Histone H2B was the preferred histone substrate of the mouse enzyme, but no human histone site is grounded here, so the residue is left at the histone H4 level of the experimental row. The location is the nucleus (IDA, PMID:15494416) rather than chromatin, as no chromatin association of PRMT7 is shown in the cached sources. PRMT7 is included because it has experimental histone methyltransferase evidence on the human protein; its in vivo histone role is recorded as a knowledge gap.
