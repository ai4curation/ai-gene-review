Ca2+/calmodulin-CaMKII potentiation of interferon-induced STAT1 signaling

Interferon receptor engagement mobilizes a cytosolic Ca2+ flux alongside the canonical JAK-STAT cascade. Ca2+-loaded calmodulin senses the flux and activates CaMKII, which phosphorylates STAT1 on the transcription-activation-domain serine (S727 in human and mouse STAT1). That serine mark is not what drives STAT1 dimerization or nuclear import, which depend on the JAK-dependent tyrosine mark; it is required for maximal transcription of interferon-stimulated genes. The module is therefore a side-input that potentiates, rather than a step in, the JAK-STAT core, and the JAK-STAT and interferon steps are included here by reference. Shigella type III effectors of the OspC family exploit the branch: OspC1 and OspC3 bind Ca2+-free calmodulin, block its Ca2+ loading and so prevent CaMKII activation, STAT1 serine phosphorylation and interferon-stimulated gene expression.

Boundary. The module is a side-input, not a segment of the JAK-STAT core: it supplies the STAT1 transcription-activation-domain serine mark, which raises the transcriptional output of a cascade that runs without it, and PMID:11972023 shows the two inputs are separable in both directions (chelating Ca2+ removes S727 and leaves Y701 intact). Pyk2 is an optional part rather than a core step because its only evidence is one pharmacological inhibitor, the judgement the GoCamReviews reach in calling its absence from the models defensible. OspC2 is named in the variant set's notes as the family's non-inhibiting member rather than modelled as a variant; it would merit a NOT annotation on its own entry. The separate cell-death functions of OspC1 and OspC3, which PMID:35568036 shows are biochemically distinct from this one, are out of scope. Groundings. Mammalia is the narrowest clade that covers the demonstrated instances without asserting conservation that has not been tested. Widen it if the linkage is shown in a non-mammalian vertebrate.
