EGFR signaling pathway in Drosophila melanogaster

Drosophila has a single EGF receptor (Egfr), a receptor tyrosine kinase activated by the TGF-alpha-like ligands Spitz, Keren and Gurken and the neuregulin-like ligand Vein. Spitz, Keren and Gurken are made as inactive membrane-bound precursors; the chaperone Star carries them from the endoplasmic reticulum to the Golgi, where rhomboid intramembrane proteases cleave them to release the secreted ligand. Ligand-bound Egfr recruits the adaptor Drk (Grb2) and the guanine nucleotide exchange factor Son of sevenless, which activates Ras85D. Ras triggers the Raf, Dsor1 (MEK) and Rolled (ERK) kinase cascade, assisted by the scaffolds KSR, CNK and Aveugle. ERK changes nuclear gene expression by activating the ETS factor Pointed and promoting the degradation or export of the repressors Yan (Aop) and Capicua. The pathway acts in oogenesis axis formation, photoreceptor recruitment, wing vein formation, and cell survival and proliferation in many tissues.
