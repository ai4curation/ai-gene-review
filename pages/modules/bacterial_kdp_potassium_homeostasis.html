Bacterial Kdp potassium uptake and regulatory control

A bacterial potassium-homeostasis module combining the KdpABC transport machinery, a variably required KdpF stabilizing component, and lineage-dependent control of pump production and activity. KdpA selects potassium and supplies the inter-subunit pathway; KdpB couples ATP turnover to ion transfer and cytoplasmic release, supported by KdpC. Canonical KdpD-to-KdpE signaling induces pump expression, whereas a Caulobacter regulatory profile retains KdpE output without making cognate KdpD activation obligatory. Characterized KdpD proteins can also restrain KdpE phosphorylation or inhibit existing pumps through KdpB serine phosphorylation. Other potassium uptake systems and non-Kdp virulence outputs are outside this module.

Bacteria is the distribution envelope, not a claim that every bacterium contains this system or the same regulatory architecture. Full matched panels are E. coli, sequence-inferred P. putida KT2440 and experimentally studied Caulobacter NA1000. N. vulgaris KdpB is a partial pump exemplar; Staphylococcus and Deinococcus proteins provide regulatory comparisons rather than complete native pump witnesses. The regulator and pump roles require compatible proteins from a supported panel. Same-polypeptide KdpE receiver and DNA output, and same-panel KdpABC assembly, cannot be reconstructed by arbitrary family-hit mixing. The two pump variants preserve F-containing assembly and specifically supported F-independent capacity. E. coli deletion results at 0.1 mM potassium and the native Caulobacter ABC system support the latter; optionality is not transferred indiscriminately to every species or solubilized assay. KdpC remains part of the transport assembly; no independent catalytic ATPase or transporter is assigned to C or F. The old potassium:proton antiporter-complex mapping is not used: chemical proton bookkeeping is not transmembrane proton antiport. Canonical induction and Caulobacter regulatory profiles are alternative evidence-qualified descriptions, not mutually exclusive evolutionary clades. KdpD phosphatase and serine-kinase functions are distinct. Short KdpD proteins lacking a histidine transmitter may still retain atypical serine kinase activity. Caulobacter KdpE activation has an unidentified phosphorylation source; no invented kinase fills that gap. Dgeo serine-kinase data on the E. coli substrate do not establish a native Dgeo transport cassette. Regulatory capacity and active potassium flux are different endpoints; inhibitory phosphorylation can disable an assembled pump. The historical NCBIfam TIGR02115 KdpF grounding and PANTHER classifications are retained only as identity evidence. Broad family membership alone does not establish matched assembly, signaling specificity, catalytic activity, or equivalent physiological requirements. Mechanistic statements inferred from E. coli remain qualified in the member reviews.
