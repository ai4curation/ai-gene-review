Reelin signaling — RELN binding to VLDLR/LRP8, SRC-family phosphorylation of DAB1, PI3K and LIS1 effector branches, and CRL5(SOCS7) degradation of phospho-DAB1; RELN/VLDLR/LRP8/DAB1/FYN/SRC/PIK3R1/PAFAH1B1/SOCS7/CUL5/RNF7

Reelin (RELN) is a large secreted glycoprotein made by Cajal-Retzius cells in the marginal zone of the developing cortex and by other neurons later in life. It controls where radially migrating neurons stop and so sets up the layers of the cerebral cortex, hippocampus and cerebellum. Reelin binds the ectodomains of two lipoprotein receptors of the LDL receptor family, the very-low-density lipoprotein receptor (VLDLR) and apolipoprotein E receptor 2 (ApoER2, LRP8), and clusters them. The cytoplasmic tails of both receptors carry an NPxY motif bound by the phosphotyrosine-binding domain of the adaptor Disabled-1 (DAB1). Receptor clustering brings DAB1 molecules together at the inner face of the plasma membrane, where the Src-family kinases FYN and SRC phosphorylate DAB1 on tyrosines; phosphorylated DAB1 in turn activates more Src-family kinase, giving a positive feedback loop. Tyrosine-phosphorylated DAB1 is a docking hub for downstream effectors. It binds the p85-alpha regulatory subunit (PIK3R1) of class IA phosphoinositide 3-kinase, which activates AKT and inhibits GSK3B; and it binds LIS1 (PAFAH1B1), the dynein regulator mutated in lissencephaly, linking the Reelin signal to the microtubule machinery of neuronal migration. Phosphorylated DAB1 is also the substrate of the signal's own off-switch. The substrate adaptor SOCS7 recognises phospho-DAB1 and recruits it to a cullin-5 RING ubiquitin ligase (CUL5 with the RING protein RNF7/RBX2 and the Elongin B/C adaptor), which polyubiquitinates DAB1 for proteasomal degradation and so stops neurons at their target layer. In the adult brain the same receptor-DAB1-kinase core modulates NMDA and AMPA receptor function at excitatory synapses and enhances long-term potentiation. Loss of RELN, of DAB1, or of both receptors produces the reeler phenotype in mice, and RELN mutations cause lissencephaly with cerebellar hypoplasia in humans.

(1) SRC is kept as a participant despite lacking GO:0038026, because the double Fyn/Src knockout almost abolishes Dab1 tyrosine phosphorylation and phenocopies dab1 mutants (PMID:16162939), and SRC itself performs the step (phosphorylating DAB1), which is participation, not substrate status. FYN is the main kinase and SRC the redundant one. The SRC GO:0038026 absence looks like a curation gap, not a convention, given that FYN carries the term by IDA in mouse. (2) PIK3R1 is kept for the same reason: Reelin triggers DAB1 binding to p85-alpha, and PI3K activity is required for Reelin-dependent cortical plate formation (PMID:12882964). Its PI3K -> PIP3 -> AKT continuation is not re-modelled; it is included by reference (module_ref) to pi3k_akt_mtor#akt_activation, the AKT step of the generic PI3K-AKT-mTOR module, which is also how RGD draws it (Pik3r1 --+> the PI3K-Akt subpathway PW:0000232). PW:0000232 is therefore mentioned here but not listed as an evidence source, since this module does not cover that pathway. (3) PAFAH1B1 (LIS1) is a participant: it binds phospho-DAB1 in a Reelin-induced manner and genetically interacts with the pathway (PMID:14578885), and it carries GO:0038026 by IMP in mouse. Its activity in this module is its regulation of dynein (GO:0140659), the activity that moves the nucleus during migration; the nucleokinesis module (modules/nucleokinesis.yaml) is the downstream neighbour that models that machinery and treats Reelin signalling as upstream. (4) PAFAH1B2 and PAFAH1B3 are excluded. They are the catalytic alpha2/alpha1 subunits of the platelet-activating factor acetylhydrolase IB heterotrimer (PAF-AH, EC 3.1.1.47), LIS1's partners in that enzyme. RGD draws them only as associated with Vldlr (Zhang et al., PLoS One 2007, an RGD reference). They carry no reelin-pathway annotation in any of the three species, they have no gene reviews, and the reported link is a single binding study. No step of the Reelin signal needs their phospholipase activity. They are recorded as candidate modulators to revisit if receptor binding is confirmed. (5) ITGA3/ITGB1 (alpha3beta1 integrin) are context, not participants. Reelin was reported to bind alpha3beta1, and loss of alpha3beta1 lowers Dab1 levels (PMID:10939329), but later work established VLDLR and ApoER2 as the receptors that are necessary and sufficient for Dab1 phosphorylation (the double receptor knockout is reeler-like, PMID:10380922), and Benhayon et al. explicitly list integrin and protocadherin as proposed alternative receptors (PMID:12670700). Curators have not annotated either integrin to GO:0038026, and neither GO-CAM includes them. RGD's integrin edges point at the separate integrin-mediated signaling subpathway PW:0000286, which is modelled in modules/integrin_fak_src_signaling.yaml; PW:0000286 is therefore mentioned here but not listed as an evidence source. (6) GRIN2A, GRIN2B and GRIA1 are excluded as downstream targets. In the adult, Reelin acting through ApoER2, DAB1 and Src-family kinases causes tyrosine phosphorylation of NMDA receptor subunits and potentiates Ca2+ influx through them (PMID:16148228, PMID:16102539); RGD also draws a PI3K route to AMPA receptors (GRIA1; Qiu et al., J Neurosci 2006, an RGD reference not cached here). In both cases the glutamate receptor is the object the pathway acts on (the kinase substrate, or the channel whose activity or trafficking is modulated), not a protein that carries the Reelin signal: it binds no Reelin, recruits no DAB1 and phosphorylates nothing. Applying the substrate-vs-participant rule, they are outputs of the module, as SREBP target genes are outputs of the SREBP module. The comparator agrees: none of the three carries GO:0038026. The adult synaptic arm is recorded in prose and as a knowledge gap, and should become a separate module (Reelin modulation of glutamatergic synaptic plasticity) built on the ionotropic glutamate receptor modules once those exist. (7) The termination arm is added from the human GO-CAM gomodel:66187e4700002704 and the primary papers (PMID:14645539, PMID:17974915, PMID:24210661). ELOB and ELOC, the Elongin B/C adaptor that links SOCS box proteins to CUL5, are obligate members of this ECS complex but are not modelled as separate annotons, following the GO-CAM, which also omits them; both have gene reviews.
