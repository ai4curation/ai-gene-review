Bacterial 4-hydroxybutyrate oxidation to succinate

A reusable two-reaction bacterial pathway in which 4-hydroxybutyrate dehydrogenase oxidizes 4-hydroxybutyrate to succinate semialdehyde and a succinate-semialdehyde dehydrogenase oxidizes that intermediate to succinate. The second reaction can use NAD+ or NADP+ depending on enzyme family and organism. Uptake, upstream 4-hydroxybutyrate production, the GABA shunt, and downstream TCA-cycle oxidation are outside the core.

Pseudomonas putida KT2440 4-hydroxybutyrate oxidation batch
The focused batch records exact KT2440 candidate implementations and their unresolved pathway assignments.

Pseudomonas putida KT2440 supplies an exact Gbd exemplar but current local evidence does not identify which of Sad-I, Sad-II, or GabD-II is the physiological partner during 4-hydroxybutyrate utilization. Their inclusion as alternative exemplars records candidate coverage, not proof of pathway assignment or proof that only one paralog contributes. GabD-II is also annotated electronically to the GABA catabolic process and may primarily serve that neighboring pathway. PTHR43217 does not reliably resolve NAD versus NADP specificity for the KT2440 Sad candidates. PTHR43353:SF5 is functionally broad and its organelle-centered display label does not imply a mitochondrial location in a bacterium; Q88EN2 is grounded as a candidate by its submitted product and EC assignment, not by that PANTHER label alone.
