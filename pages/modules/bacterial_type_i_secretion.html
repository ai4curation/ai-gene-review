Bacterial type I secretion

A species-neutral module for one-step export of a cytoplasmic protein across both membranes of a diderm bacterium through a tripartite type I secretion conduit. An inner-membrane ABC transporter recognizes an uncleaved secretion signal in its substrate and hydrolyzes ATP; a periplasmic membrane-fusion adaptor anchored in the inner membrane bridges to, and opens, an oligomeric outer-membrane exit duct. Because the three components form a continuous channel, the substrate never appears as a folded periplasmic intermediate. Substrate maturation before export, folding and cofactor acquisition after release, Sec- and Tat-dependent routes, and small-molecule efflux through the same family of outer-membrane ducts are outside this module.

Reusable boundary: the module begins when an ABC transporter engages a cytoplasmic substrate bearing an uncleaved type I secretion signal and ends when that substrate leaves the outer-membrane duct. Only the three conserved machinery parts are admitted. Cargo proteins are deliberately excluded, in line with the sibling type II and type VI modules, because the same conduit serves unrelated substrate classes; the cargo-dependent features that do belong to the machinery are captured as an ABC-transporter subclass variant. Two things commonly conflated with this module are excluded on structural grounds. First, outer-membrane factors of the TolC family are shared with resistance-nodulation-division and major-facilitator efflux pumps, so the presence of a TolC-like duct is not evidence of protein secretion. Second, substrate acylation or other maturation performed by dedicated accessory enzymes acts on the substrate before engagement and is not part of the transport step.

Part 1: Recognize the substrate's uncleaved secretion signal and hydrolyze ATP to drive it across the inner membrane
Type I secretion inner-membrane ABC translocaseTransport Stepinner_membrane_abc_translocase

The ABC transporter is the entry point and the energizing component of the conduit. It binds the substrate's C-terminal secretion signal directly from the cytoplasm, so no cytoplasmic targeting factor or cleavable N-terminal signal peptide is involved. Transporters of this class divide according to whether they also process their substrate.

Variant set: ABC transporter subclass by substrate-processing capability of the ABC transporter (Exactly One)

The axis is a property of the transporter, not of the cargo: an exporter either carries an N-terminal cytoplasmic peptidase domain that cleaves a leader from its substrate or it does not. Only the non-processing subclass satisfies the GO:0030253 requirement that the secreted protein is not proteolytically processed, so the processing subclass is recorded here as a recognized divergence from the canonical definition rather than silently merged into it.

Non-processing type I ABC exporterTransport Stepnon_processing_abc_exporter

The canonical subclass, exemplified by the hemolysin exporter HlyB and the protease exporters PrtD and AprD. The substrate is exported intact and its secretion signal is never removed.

A trimeric channel-tunnel that spans the outer membrane as a beta-barrel and continues into the periplasm as a long alpha-helical barrel. It is closed at its periplasmic end until the adaptor opens it, which is what keeps the envelope sealed between secretion events.

The focused review of TolC retains this molecular function nine times over and uses it in two of its three core functions, including the one covering protein secretion. It declines to assert a protein-specific transporter term on a comparator check verified here: PrtF, AprF and TolC all carry the efflux transporter term, and no outer-membrane factor anywhere carries the protein-transporter term.

The same protein serves resistance-nodulation-division and major-facilitator efflux pumps. Membership in this family is therefore not evidence that a given organism performs type I protein secretion. This annoton previously asserted transmembrane protein transporter activity, which reads more naturally in a protein-secretion module but put the module at odds with its own gene review and with every other outer-membrane factor in GO. The family-wide term is used instead, and the loss of the cargo distinction is recorded as a knowledge gap.
