Glutathione-dependent methylglyoxal detoxification (glyoxalase system)

A two-reaction detoxification module, conserved from bacteria to eukaryotes, in which glyoxalase I converts the spontaneously formed methylglyoxal-glutathione hemithioacetal to (R)-S-lactoylglutathione and glyoxalase II hydrolyzes that intermediate to D-lactate while regenerating glutathione. Glyoxalase II is carried out by non-orthologous metallo-beta-lactamase-fold enzymes in bacteria (GloB) and eukaryotes (HAGH/GLO2), and some fungi have separate cytosolic and mitochondrial glyoxalase II isozymes. Glutathione biosynthesis, methylglyoxal generation, glutathione-independent routes (glyoxalase III, methylglyoxal reductases) and downstream D-lactate oxidation are separate modules.

The non-enzymatic formation of the methylglyoxal-glutathione hemithioacetal is treated as input context rather than a protein-catalyzed part. GshA/GshB glutathione supply and D-lactate oxidation are satisfiability dependencies, not nominal parts. Dedicated methylglyoxal synthesis and glutathione-independent detoxification routes are outside this module. Target-specific candidates and exclusions belong in project batch pages; the PSEPK assessment is recorded in file:projects/P_PUTIDA/batches/ppu00620_methylglyoxal_detoxification.md. ALIGN (YeastPathways project, 2026-10): the P. putida-centred module was widened to bacteria plus eukaryotes. Glyoxalase I gained S. cerevisiae GLO1, S. pombe glo1 and human GLO1, all in PTHR10374:SF30 with the bacterial exemplars. Glyoxalase II became a ONE_OR_MORE variant set because eukaryotic GLO2/HAGH (PTHR11935) and bacterial GloB (PTHR43705:SF1) are different PANTHER families, and because S. cerevisiae has a cytosolic (GLO2) and a glycerol-induced mitochondrial-matrix (GLO4) isozyme (PMID:9261170). YeastCyc gene assignments for PWY-901 are correct. GO-CAM disagreements: the SGD model places GLO4 in cytosol; the PomBase model includes glutathione-independent glyoxalase III (hsp3101-hsp3105; budding-yeast HSP31 family), methylglyoxal reductase and lactate/lactaldehyde dehydrogenases, which are outside this module.
