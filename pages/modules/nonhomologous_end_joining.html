Classical non-homologous end joining of DNA double-strand breaks — Ku70/Ku80 end binding, DNA-PKcs recruitment and autophosphorylation, Artemis/PNKP/APTX/APLF end processing, pol mu/pol lambda/TdT gap filling, and XRCC4-LIG4 ligation with XLF and PAXX; XRCC6/XRCC5/PRKDC/DCLRE1C/PNKP/APTX/APLF/POLM/POLL/DNTT/XRCC4/LIG4/NHEJ1/PAXX

Classical non-homologous end joining (c-NHEJ) rejoins the two ends of a DNA double-strand break (DSB) directly, without a homologous template. It operates throughout the cell cycle, is the predominant DSB repair pathway in G1 and in non-dividing cells, and is the joining step of V(D)J recombination and class switch recombination in lymphocytes. The Ku heterodimer (Ku70/XRCC6 and Ku80/XRCC5) threads onto each broken end through a preformed ring and recruits the DNA-dependent protein kinase catalytic subunit (DNA-PKcs, PRKDC) to form the DNA-PK holoenzyme, which tethers the two ends in a synaptic complex. DNA-PKcs is activated by the DNA end, autophosphorylates (which remodels the synapse and releases the ends for processing) and phosphorylates other NHEJ factors, including the nuclease Artemis (DCLRE1C). Incompatible or damaged ends are processed by enzymes chosen by end chemistry: the Artemis-DNA-PKcs complex opens hairpins and trims 5' and 3' overhangs; the polynucleotide kinase-phosphatase PNKP restores 5'-phosphate and 3'-hydroxyl termini; aprataxin (APTX) removes 5'-adenylate left by abortive ligation; and APLF, recruited by poly(ADP-ribose) and by Ku, acts as a scaffold that retains the ligase complex and has reported nuclease activity. Gaps are filled by the family X polymerases pol mu (POLM) and pol lambda (POLL), and in developing lymphocytes the template-independent terminal deoxynucleotidyl transferase (TdT, DNTT) adds non-templated nucleotides to V(D)J coding ends. The ends are finally sealed by DNA ligase IV (LIG4) in a constitutive complex with its scaffold XRCC4; the XRCC4 paralogs XLF (NHEJ1) and PAXX bind XRCC4 and Ku respectively, bridge and align the ends, and stimulate ligation, with XLF and PAXX partly redundant. Upstream, the 53BP1-RIF1 axis of ATM signalling protects DSB ends from resection in G1 and so favours c-NHEJ over homologous recombination.
