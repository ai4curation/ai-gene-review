Nickel-dependent urease biogenesis and urea hydrolysis

Urease is a nickel metalloenzyme that hydrolyzes urea to ammonia and carbamate, which decomposes to a second ammonia and bicarbonate. Its catalytic unit (the alpha chain or alpha region) is assembled first as an inactive apoprotein. The apoprotein is built from three chains (UreA gamma, UreB beta, UreC alpha) in most bacteria, from two chains in Helicobacter (a fused gamma-beta UreA plus a UreB alpha chain), and from a single chain in plants and fungi. The active site, two nickel ions bridged by a carbamylated lysine, is installed after assembly. That activation is carried out by conserved accessory proteins: UreD (UreH in Helicobacter) binds the apoprotein and, with UreF, recruits the SIMIBI-class GTPase UreG. In bacteria, the nickel metallochaperone UreE supplies nickel to UreG, and GTP hydrolysis by UreG releases nickel into the active site. Plants and fungi use UreD, UreF and UreG but have no UreE. The mature enzyme then hydrolyzes urea. The module does not include nickel uptake or homeostasis, urea transport, regulation of urease gene expression, or assimilation of the released ammonia.

Scope and generalization. Nickel urease and its UreD/UreF/UreG activation machinery are conserved across bacteria, archaea, plants and fungi, so the module is written species-neutrally. Concrete exemplars come from the classic experimental systems: Klebsiella aerogenes (three-chain biochemistry and structure), Helicobacter pylori (two-chain enzyme; UreG/UreF/UreH structure) and Arabidopsis thaliana (single-chain enzyme; UreD/UreF/UreG genetics and in-planta complex). Pseudomonas putida KT2440 is kept as a genomic exemplar, and Nitrososphaera viennensis ureC1 as an archaeal catalytic exemplar. scope is CONCRETE because every leaf is grounded to representative proteins; the lineage differences are modelled as variants of the structural part and as an optional UreE part, not as separate modules. Family grounding. The catalytic chain and the three accessory proteins are grounded to single-subfamily PANTHER families (PTHR43440:SF1, PTHR33643:SF1, PTHR33620:SF1, PTHR31715:SF0), each with a family review. The gamma and beta structural chains are left without a PANTHER id: PANTHER's PTHR33569 lumps them with fungal single-chain urease, which does not identify the role. UreE has no PANTHER family. The InterPro accessions for these roles are given in the descriptors' descriptions. Boundary. Nickel import (for example NikR-regulated transporters), urea transport (Helicobacter UreI), hydrogenase-shared nickel chaperones (HypA/HypB, which also feed urease in Helicobacter), regulation of urease expression, and assimilation of the released ammonia are inputs or consequences, not module parts. Fungal accessory-protein homologues (S. pombe UreD/UreF/UreG-like proteins) are PAINT seeds for the accessory families but are not grounded here.

Part 1: apo-urease structural assembly
Apo-urease structural assemblyCellular Componentapo_urease_assembly

The structural chains associate into the inactive, preorganized apoprotein before the active site is loaded with carbamate and nickel. The number of chains differs between lineages; the catalytic alpha chain or region is conserved in all of them.

Variant set: Urease chain architecture by subunit architecture (taxon-associated) (Exactly One)

Each urease is built on exactly one of these architectures. They differ only in how the gamma, beta and alpha regions are split across polypeptides; the catalytic alpha region and its dinickel ligands are the same in all three.
