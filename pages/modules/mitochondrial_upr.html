Mitochondrial unfolded protein response

The mitochondrial unfolded protein response (UPRmt) is a mitochondria-to-nucleus signaling pathway that raises the expression of nuclear genes encoding mitochondrial chaperones and proteases when mitochondrial protein homeostasis is disturbed. Its executing transcription factor is a bZIP protein of the ATF4/ATF5 family that is partitioned between mitochondria and the nucleus: nematode ATFS-1 and its mammalian functional counterpart ATF5 both carry a mitochondrial targeting sequence, are kept in mitochondria in unstressed cells, and activate chaperone and protease genes when mitochondrial function fails; ATF5 rescues UPRmt signaling in atfs-1 mutant worms. The induced matrix chaperones, the HSP60 chaperonin and the mitochondrial HSP70, are both the classic reporters and the protective output of the response. How mitochondrial stress is relayed to the transcription factor differs between lineages. In Caenorhabditis elegans, where the pathway was defined, the key regulatory point is the import efficiency of ATFS-1: misfolded matrix proteins are degraded by the ClpP protease CLPP-1, the inner-membrane ABC transporter HAF-1 exports the resulting peptides and attenuates protein import under moderate stress, and ATFS-1 that escapes import reaches the nucleus. A nematode chromatin arm (DVE-1/UBL-5 together with the MET-2/LIN-65 H3K9 methylation machinery and the JMJD-3.1 H3K27 demethylase) acts alongside ATFS-1. In mammals, mitochondrial stress such as depolarization, import defects or iron deficiency activates the inner-membrane protease OMA1, which cleaves DELE1; the released DELE1 fragment activates the eIF2alpha kinase HRI (EIF2AK1) in the cytosol, and the resulting integrated stress response raises translation of ATF4 and ATF5. Mammalian CLPP is not required for the UPRmt, so the ClpP peptide signal is not a conserved part of the pathway.

Boundary. The module covers the conserved UPRmt trunk (stress relay, an ATF4/ATF5-family bZIP transcription factor partitioned between mitochondria and nucleus, and induced matrix chaperones), with the relay modeled as a lineage variant set. It is set at Metazoa (NCBITaxon:33208): the ATFS-1/ATF5 logic is documented in nematodes and mammals, and the HSP60 and mtHSP70 outputs are conserved across animals. The two variants are the two documented relays, not an exhaustive survey; insect UPRmt wiring is not modeled. Lineage differences. (a) Nematode: CLPP-1 proteolysis, HAF-1 peptide export and ATFS-1 import competition, plus a chromatin arm (DVE-1/UBL-5, LIN-65, MET-2, JMJD-3.1) that is described only in C. elegans. HAF-1 is needed under moderate stress, not under severe stress that blocks import directly; it is thought to dampen import rather than to be part of the import machinery (see the haf-1 review). (b) Mammalian: OMA1-DELE1-HRI-eIF2alpha drives the integrated stress response, which raises ATF4 and ATF5 translation. DELE1 is a messenger for mitochondrial stress in general (depolarization, import failure, iron deficiency), not specifically a sensor of misfolded proteins. Mammalian CLPP is the same PANTHER subfamily as CLPP-1 (PTHR10381:SF11) but is not required for the mammalian UPRmt (PMID:27154400), so it is not given the CLPP-1 signaling role and is not a member of the mammalian variant. Its ATFS-1-like mitochondrial partitioning is recorded on the ATF5 member of the shared transcription factor part. ATF4 is in the same PANTHER family but does not rescue atfs-1 worms (PMID:27426517); it is modeled as the ISR transcription factor of the mammalian variant. Left out. The C. elegans GCN-2/eIF2alpha arm is complementary to and independent of ATFS-1 activation (PMID:22719267) and is left to the integrated stress response; the gcn-2 review lists it as a core UPRmt process, which is recorded here only as context. The neuronal and germline signals that switch on the intestinal UPRmt cell-non-autonomously (serotonin, the retromer-dependent Wnt EGL-20 signal, PMID:30057120) are not modeled. JMJD-1.2 acts with JMJD-3.1 (PMID:27133168) but has no review and is only named. ULP-4 and the NuRD/HDA-1 regulators (PMID:30642431, PMID:32789178, PMID:32934238) are modulators. The matrix LON protease that degrades imported ATFS-1 (and LONP1, which degrades imported DELE1) is not modeled. Innate immunity and lifespan extension are outputs of the transcriptional program and are not module processes. ATFS-1 dual targeting. ATFS-1 appears twice: in the nematode variant as the matrix pool that binds mitochondrial DNA (mitochondrial transcription factor activity) before degradation, and in the shared part as the nuclear RNA polymerase II activator. The import checkpoint negatively regulates the nuclear part. PANTHER grounding. ATFS-1 (PTHR13044:SF12) and ATF5 (PTHR13044:SF3) are in PTHR13044 and both inherit DNA-binding transcription activator activity from PAINT node PTN000315883 (Opisthokonta); that node is a generic bZIP activator node, not a UPRmt-specific one. HSP-60 and HSPD1 are in PTHR45633:SF17; the family's PAINT node PTN000143510 carries the UPRmt process (seeded by worm hsp-60). HSP-6 and HSPA9 are in the mitochondrial HSP70 subfamily PTHR19375:SF184 and both inherit from the mitochondrial HSP70 node PTN000452554. Related modules. ATFS-1-dependent transcription of mitophagy genes is treated in MODULE:mitophagy, the cytosolic heat shock response in MODULE:heat_shock_response and the ER UPR in MODULE:er_unfolded_protein_response.

The nematode route reads mitochondrial import capacity directly through ATFS-1 import competition, with a ClpP/HAF-1 peptide signal under moderate stress. The mammalian route is the OMA1-DELE1-HRI branch of the integrated stress response. Neither DELE1 nor an OMA1-DELE1 relay is known in C. elegans, and mammalian CLPP is dispensable for the UPRmt (PMID:27154400). EXACTLY_ONE is kept although it is open whether mammalian ATF5 is also controlled by import competition (see knowledge gaps). The variants are lineage routes: the nematode variant is the CLPP-1/HAF-1/ATFS-1 relay, which mammals do not use because CLPP is dispensable there. A mammalian ATF5 import checkpoint, if it is shown, would be a new part of the mammalian variant or a further variant, not the nematode one. No organism is known to use both documented routes, so ONE_OR_MORE would assert a co-occurrence that has not been shown.

Part 2: ATFS-1 mitochondrial import checkpoint
ATFS-1 mitochondrial import and matrix activityRegulatory Stepatfs1_import_checkpoint

ATFS-1 has an N-terminal mitochondrial targeting sequence that is essential for UPRmt repression. In unstressed cells ATFS-1 is efficiently imported into mitochondria and degraded, so it does not reach the nucleus. The import efficiency of ATFS-1 thus reports the capacity of mitochondrial protein import. Imported ATFS-1 also binds OXPHOS gene promoters in mitochondrial DNA and limits OXPHOS transcript accumulation during stress.
