Plasma membrane calcium extrusion — ATP-driven PMCA pumps (ATP2B1-4), Na+/Ca2+ exchangers NCX (SLC8A1-3) and K+-dependent Na+/Ca2+ exchangers NCKX (SLC24A1-4) exporting cytosolic Ca2+ across the plasma membrane

Cells keep cytosolic free calcium near 100 nM, about four orders of magnitude below the extracellular concentration, and terminate calcium signals by removing calcium from the cytosol. Across the plasma membrane, three transporter families export calcium. The plasma membrane calcium ATPases PMCA1-4 (ATP2B1-4) are P-type pumps that couple hydrolysis of one ATP to the export of one Ca2+ (with proton counter-transport); they have high calcium affinity and low capacity, are activated by calcium-calmodulin, and require the single-pass subunit neuroplastin or basigin for activity. The sodium/calcium exchangers NCX1-3 (SLC8A1-3) use the inward sodium gradient to export one Ca2+ for three Na+; they have lower affinity but high capacity and dominate calcium removal in cardiomyocytes. The potassium-dependent sodium/calcium exchangers NCKX1-4 (SLC24A1-4) export one Ca2+ together with one K+ in exchange for four Na+, which lets them keep extruding calcium at low cytosolic concentration, as in rod and cone photoreceptor outer segments. Both exchanger families can run in reverse and admit calcium when the membrane is depolarized or cytosolic sodium is high. PMCA isoform usage and NCX/NCKX expression are tissue-specific, but the three routes act in parallel in most cells.
