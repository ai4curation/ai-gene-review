Toll signaling pathway in Drosophila melanogaster

The Toll pathway is activated when an extracellular serine protease cascade cleaves the pro-domain of the cytokine-like ligand Spaetzle, allowing it to bind the Toll receptor. In the early embryo, a maternally supplied cascade (Gastrulation defective, Snake and Easter), spatially restricted by the Pipe sulfotransferase in the ventral follicle cells, generates ventral Spaetzle and establishes the dorsal-ventral axis. In the immune response, pattern-recognition proteins (PGRP-SA, GNBP1, GNBP3) and the virulence sensor Persephone activate ModSP, Grass and Hayan, converging on the Spaetzle-processing enzyme. Activated Toll recruits the adaptors MyD88 and Tube and the kinase Pelle, which lead to degradation of the IkB protein Cactus and nuclear entry of the NF-kappaB factors Dorsal and Dif. Related Toll receptors such as Toll-7, bound by neurotrophin-like Spaetzle family ligands, act in the nervous system.

Boundary. The module combines the FlyBase dorsal group and the Toll pathway core subgroups. Windbeutel (wbl) is a dorsal-group member that acts as an ER chaperone for Pipe. The IkB protein Cactus is in the FlyBase negative regulator set and is therefore not modelled as a part, although it is the direct target of the pathway; serpins (Spn27A, Necrotic, Spn88Ea) are likewise regulators. PGRP-SC amidases scavenge peptidoglycan and may dampen rather than drive signaling. FlyBase groups realized by this module: DORS (FBgg0001054), TL-CAN (FBgg0001059). Subgroups folded into it: TL-SPZC, TL-NFC. FlyBase also curates regulator sets for this pathway, which are context and not modelled as parts: TL-NFN (negative regulators of toll-nf-kappab signaling pathway, 19 genes); TL-SPZN (negative regulators of spatzle activating pathway, 4 genes); TL-NFP (positive regulators of toll-nf-kappab signaling pathway, 23 genes).
