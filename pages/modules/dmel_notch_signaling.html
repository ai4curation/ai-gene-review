Notch signaling pathway in Drosophila melanogaster

Notch signaling is a juxtacrine pathway in which the transmembrane ligands Delta and Serrate on one cell bind the Notch receptor on a neighbouring cell. Ligand binding, together with ligand endocytosis in the signal-sending cell, exposes Notch to cleavage by the ADAM10 metalloprotease Kuzbanian (S2 cleavage), after which the gamma-secretase complex (Presenilin, Nicastrin, Aph-1 and Pen-2) cleaves within the transmembrane domain (S3 cleavage) and releases the Notch intracellular domain. The intracellular domain enters the nucleus and forms a transcriptional activation complex with the CSL DNA-binding protein Suppressor of Hairless and the coactivator Mastermind, switching Su(H) target genes such as the Enhancer of split complex genes from repression to activation. The pathway controls lateral inhibition, boundary formation and binary cell-fate decisions throughout development.

Boundary. The module contains ligands, receptor, the two proteolytic steps and the nuclear CSL complex. Notch appears twice, as the receptor and as the intracellular domain within the nuclear complex. E3 ligases that drive ligand endocytosis (Neuralized, Mind bomb 1), glycosyltransferases that modify Notch (Ofut1, Fringe, Rumi), the corepressor Hairless and the Enhancer of split target genes are FlyBase regulators and are not modelled as parts.
