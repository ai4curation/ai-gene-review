Selenocysteine biosynthesis and co-translational incorporation (SEPHS2, PSTK, SEPSECS, SECISBP2, EEFSEC)

Selenocysteine (Sec), the 21st amino acid, is not charged onto its tRNA directly; instead it is synthesised on its own tRNA and then inserted co-translationally at recoded UGA codons, so a dedicated pathway both makes selenocysteyl-tRNA(Sec) and decodes UGA as Sec rather than stop. Two arms feed the process. Selenium activation: selenophosphate synthetase SEPHS2 (itself a selenoprotein, autoregulatory) uses ATP to convert hydrogen selenide to monoselenophosphate, the activated Se donor. tRNA-Sec charging: the tRNA(Sec) is first aminoacylated with serine by seryl-tRNA synthetase (SARS1, not in this module) to Ser-tRNA(Sec); the kinase PSTK phosphorylates that seryl residue to O-phosphoseryl-tRNA(Sec); and the PLP-dependent Sec synthase SEPSECS then transfers selenium from selenophosphate onto O-phosphoseryl-tRNA(Sec) to yield selenocysteyl-tRNA(Sec). Incorporation: at selenoprotein mRNAs, the SECIS-binding protein SECISBP2 binds the 3'-UTR SECIS stem-loop and, with the selenocysteine-specific GTPase elongation factor EEFSEC delivering Sec-tRNA(Sec) to the ribosomal A-site, recodes the in-frame UGA as selenocysteine, allowing translation of the ~25 human selenoproteins (GPX, TXNRD, DIO, SELENOP, MSRB1, SELENOI, etc.). The paralog SEPHS1 lacks the selenophosphate-synthetase activity and instead acts in redox/glucose homeostasis (curated separately, not a catalytic node here). Inherited defects cause selenoprotein-deficiency disorders: SEPSECS (pontocerebellar hypoplasia type 2D), SECISBP2 (multisystem selenoprotein deficiency with abnormal thyroid-hormone metabolism) and EEFSEC (neurodevelopmental selenopathy).
