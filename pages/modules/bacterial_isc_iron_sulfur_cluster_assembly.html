Bacterial Isc iron-sulfur cluster assembly

A reusable bacterial module for de novo assembly and transfer of iron-sulfur clusters by the housekeeping Isc system. The PLP-dependent cysteine desulfurase IscS mobilizes sulfur from L-cysteine as an enzyme-bound persulfide and passes it to the scaffold IscU, on which a nascent [2Fe-2S] cluster is assembled; the Isc [2Fe-2S] ferredoxin Fdx supplies an electron needed to reduce the persulfide sulfur. The J-type co-chaperone HscB recognizes cluster-bearing IscU and delivers it to the dedicated Hsp70-type chaperone HscA, whose ATP-driven cycle promotes release and transfer of the cluster. The A-type carrier IscA binds iron and [2Fe-2S] clusters and supports late cluster delivery to target apoproteins. Transcriptional regulation by IscR, the frataxin CyaY and IscX modulators, the Fe-S target proteins themselves, and the alternative Suf and Nif systems are outside the module boundary. Escherichia coli K-12 and Pseudomonas putida KT2440 supply exact exemplars for every role.

The contiguous PP_0842 through PP_0847 locus supplies exact Pseudomonas putida KT2440 accessions for IscS, IscU, IscA, HscB, HscA, and Fdx.

This is an assembly-process module, not a single linear reaction pathway. IscS and IscU define the conserved assembly core. Fdx electron delivery, IscA-assisted transfer, and the HscB-HscA scaffold-remodeling cycle are substantive accessory branches; they are marked optional because Suf-type or lineage-specific substitutes exist, although in E. coli loss of hscB, hscA or fdx is nearly as severe as loss of iscU (PMID:11432781). Taxonomic scope: the full iscSUA-hscBA-fdx gene cluster is best characterized in gamma-proteobacteria (E. coli K-12, Azotobacter vinelandii, P. putida KT2440), and its role-by-role conservation is shown by the E. coli and P. putida exemplars, so the module is kept at Bacteria with the caveat that many Gram-positive bacteria and archaea rely on Suf instead of Isc. The mitochondrial ISC machinery (NFS1-ISD11-ACP, ISCU, FDX2, HSCB, HSPA9/Ssq1, ISCA1/2) descends from this system but adds eukaryote-specific subunits and compartmentation, so it is not merged into this bacterial module; the shared PANTHER (sub)families (PTHR11601:SF34, PTHR10093, PTHR23426:SF65, PTHR14021:SF15, PTHR10072:SF41) record the homology. Family groundings use PANTHER (sub)families whose curated family reviews adjudicate the asserted activity. The IscU role is grounded at the family level: panther.obo defines nine PTHR10093 subfamilies, including SF26 named for IscU, but UniProt cross-references the IscU exemplars only at family level and PANTHER 19.0 classifies E. coli IscU in SF8, so no subfamily is asserted. The E. coli HscA annoton is grounded to PTHR19375:SF176 (CHAPERONE PROTEIN HSCA), where PANTHER 19.0's E. coli sequence classification file places it, rather than to the whole HSP70 family, which also contains DnaK and eukaryotic Hsp70s that do not perform the IscU-specific role. P. putida HscA has no subfamily assignment, so it is a separate family-level (PTHR19375) annoton for the same role.
