Photorespiratory C2 cycle (2-phosphoglycolate to 3-phosphoglycerate) in land plants

The photorespiratory C2 cycle salvages 2-phosphoglycolate, the two-carbon product of the oxygenase reaction of ribulose-1,5-bisphosphate carboxylase/oxygenase (Rubisco), and returns three of every four carbons to the Calvin-Benson cycle as 3-phosphoglycerate. The pathway runs through three organelles. In the chloroplast, 2-phosphoglycolate phosphatase dephosphorylates 2-phosphoglycolate to glycolate, which the inner-envelope glycolate/glycerate translocator exports. In the peroxisome, glycolate oxidase oxidizes glycolate to glyoxylate with O2, generating H2O2 that catalase removes, and glyoxylate is transaminated to glycine, mainly by glutamate:glyoxylate aminotransferase. In the mitochondrion, the glycine decarboxylase (glycine cleavage) complex converts one glycine to CO2, NH3 and 5,10-methylenetetrahydrofolate, and serine hydroxymethyltransferase uses the one-carbon unit and a second glycine to make serine. Back in the peroxisome, serine:glyoxylate aminotransferase converts serine to hydroxypyruvate while aminating a second glyoxylate, and hydroxypyruvate reductase reduces hydroxypyruvate to glycerate; a cytosolic hydroxypyruvate reductase provides a bypass. In the chloroplast, D-glycerate 3-kinase phosphorylates glycerate to 3-phosphoglycerate. The NH3 released in the mitochondrion is refixed in the chloroplast by plastidic glutamine synthetase and ferredoxin-dependent glutamate synthase, which return the glutamate used by the glyoxylate transamination. Loss of most core steps makes plants unable to grow in normal air but viable at elevated CO2. The Rubisco oxygenase reaction itself, the Calvin-Benson cycle, the transporters for the other intermediates (largely unidentified), supply of NADH to the peroxisome, alternative glycolate routes in algae and cyanobacteria, and C4 or CAM carbon-concentrating mechanisms are outside this module's boundary.

Part 5: mitochondrial glycine decarboxylation
Glycine decarboxylase complex (P, H, T and L proteins)glycine_decarboxylation_step

Glycine is oxidatively decarboxylated and deaminated to CO2, NH3, NADH and 5,10-methylenetetrahydrofolate. In Arabidopsis the P protein is encoded by GLDP1 (UniProtKB:Q94B78) and GLDP2 (UniProtKB:O80988), which are redundant; the H and T proteins include GDH1 (UniProtKB:P25855) and GDCST (UniProtKB:O65396). The leaf L protein is mainly mitochondrial LPD1 (mtLPD1, At1g48030, UniProtKB:Q9M5K3); the paralog mtLPD2 (At3g17240, UniProtKB:Q9M5K2) is thought to serve chiefly the 2-oxoacid dehydrogenase complexes, although the two are presumed interchangeable in the matrix. Loss of both P-protein genes is lethal even at high CO2, because the complex is also required for one-carbon metabolism.

Part 10: refixation of photorespiratory ammonia in the chloroplast
Photorespiratory ammonia refixation (GS2 and Fd-GOGAT)Metabolic Pathwayammonia_refixation

NH3 released by glycine decarboxylase is reassimilated in the chloroplast by the plastidic glutamine synthetase (GS2) and ferredoxin-dependent glutamate synthase (Fd-GOGAT), producing the glutamate that the peroxisomal glyoxylate transamination consumes. Of the two Arabidopsis Fd-GOGAT genes only GLU1 mutants (gls) are photorespiratory.
