Human exon junction complex-dependent nonsense-mediated mRNA decay — EJC mark, UPF1-UPF2-UPF3B surveillance complex, SMG1C phosphorylation of UPF1, and SMG6 endonucleolytic and SMG5-SMG7 decay routes

Nonsense-mediated mRNA decay (NMD) removes mRNAs whose translation ends at a premature termination codon. In mammals the best-characterised trigger is a termination event that happens upstream of an exon junction complex (EJC). The EJC is deposited by the spliceosome about 24 nucleotides upstream of each exon-exon junction: the DEAD-box protein EIF4A3 clamps the mRNA in a sequence-independent way and is locked there by the MAGOH-RBM8A (Y14) heterodimer, which inhibits its ATPase; the peripheral factor CASC3 (Barentsz) joins the core. EJCs travel with the mRNA to the cytoplasm and are normally displaced by the first ribosome. When a ribosome terminates upstream of a remaining EJC, the RNA helicase UPF1, bound at the terminating ribosome with the release factors eRF1 and eRF3 and the SMG1 kinase complex (the SURF complex), is connected to the EJC through UPF2 and UPF3B, which binds a composite surface of EIF4A3, MAGOH and RBM8A. UPF2 and UPF3B stimulate the ATPase and helicase activities of UPF1, and SMG1, a PI3K-related kinase held with its regulatory subunits SMG8 and SMG9, phosphorylates UPF1 on SQ motifs. Phosphorylated UPF1 recruits the decay effectors through their 14-3-3-like domains: the PIN-domain endonuclease SMG6 cleaves the mRNA near the premature stop codon, and the SMG5-SMG7 heterodimer recruits deadenylation and decapping activities through SMG7 and, with protein phosphatase 2A, promotes the dephosphorylation of UPF1. The fragments and the deadenylated or decapped mRNA are then degraded by the general cytoplasmic mRNA decay enzymes. The UPF3B paralogue UPF3A is only weakly active in NMD and mainly opposes it by competing with UPF3B for UPF2.

Curator-authored functions without an experimental GOA row or a gene-review core function are: UPF2 ATPase activator activity, the protein-macromolecule adaptor activity of the SMG5-SMG7 heterodimer and of SMG7, and the complex-level mRNA binding of the EJC, which restates the EIF4A3 IDA row for the assembled complex. UPF3B's mRNA binding is its gene-review core function and IDA row. Boundary decisions. (1) The EJC is a part, not context, although modules/mrna_nuclear_export treats it as context. In NMD it does work in the step: it binds UPF3B and so places the UPF complex downstream of the stop codon (the scaffold case of the participation rule), and its core subunits carry experimental NMD rows. Its assembly by the spliceosome (including the escort factor CWC22) belongs to the planned major_spliceosome_cycle module, its disassembly by PYM1 after translation is not modelled, and the peripheral RNPS1-containing ASAP/PSAP complexes, which define an alternative UPF2-dependent route into NMD (PMID:16209946), are left out. MAGOH and MAGOHB are listed as alternative units, as paralogues are in modules/mediator_complex. CASC3 is listed as a peripheral unit; it is part of the tetrameric EJC core used in the UPF3b-EJC structure, but its own requirement for NMD is less well established than that of the core (it carries only a regulation-of-NMD row in GOA). (2) The terminating ribosome and the release factors eRF1 (ETF1) and eRF3 (GSPT1), which are SURF components with UPF1 and SMG1, belong to the sibling modules cytoplasmic_ribosome and translation_elongation_and_termination. They are referenced in prose and not by module_ref, because those modules were drafted in parallel and do not yet exist in modules/; once they do, the upf_surveillance_complex part can reference the termination node. Ribosomal proteins are not listed. (3) UPF3A is an optional annoton with a negative-regulation process, because it is a weak NMD factor that mainly antagonises UPF3B. (4) SMG8 and SMG9 get no molecular function: their effect on SMG1 was measured only on the isolated complex. (5) The decay step is a ROUTE variant set (SMG6 endonucleolytic; SMG5-SMG7) with ONE_OR_MORE selection, because the two routes are partially redundant. SMG6's EJC-binding and the SMG6-SMG5-SMG7 hand-offs are not modelled separately. (7) Out of scope: EJC-independent NMD triggered by long 3' UTRs and its antagonism by PABPC1; NMD regulators and feedback factors such as DHX34 and NBAS; Staufen-mediated decay (PW:0000550); nonstop and no-go decay, which the split plan leaves to a later ribosome-quality-control module; and the non-NMD roles of UPF1, SMG1, SMG5, SMG6 and SMG7 in telomere maintenance, histone mRNA decay and the DNA damage response. Function of the assembled complex: sequence-independent binding of spliced mRNA upstream of the junction. Only EIF4A3 binds the RNA (mRNA binding, IDA, PMID:22961380); MAGOH, MAGOHB and RBM8A have no experimental molecular function in GOA beyond high-throughput RNA binding and are given none here. The location is the cytosol, where NMD acts; the EJC is deposited in the nucleus.

Part 4: decay of the target, initiated by effectors that bind phosphorylated UPF1
Phospho-UPF1 recruits SMG6 for endonucleolytic cleavage and SMG5-SMG7 for deadenylation-dependent decayBiological Processdecay_initiation

SMG5, SMG6 and SMG7 bind phosphorylated UPF1 through 14-3-3-like domains. SMG6 cuts the mRNA near the premature stop codon with its PIN-domain endonuclease. The SMG5-SMG7 heterodimer recruits decay factors through SMG7 and, with protein phosphatase 2A, promotes UPF1 dephosphorylation, which recycles the surveillance complex. The two routes are partially redundant. The general decay enzymes that finish the job (the 5'-3' exonuclease XRN1 on the 3' fragment, the exosome on the 5' fragment, and the CCR4-NOT deadenylase and DCP2 decapping enzyme recruited by SMG5-SMG7) are not modelled here (see notes).
