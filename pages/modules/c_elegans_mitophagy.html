Mitophagy in Caenorhabditis elegans

Mitophagy is the selective macroautophagy of mitochondria. In Caenorhabditis elegans damaged or unwanted mitochondria are marked on their outer membrane and captured by phagophores decorated with the Atg8-family protein LGG-1. Two kinds of mark are known. In the ubiquitin-dependent route, the mitochondrial kinase PINK-1 and the RBR-family E3 ubiquitin ligase PDR-1 (Parkin) act on stressed mitochondria, by analogy with mammalian PINK1 and Parkin. In the receptor-mediated route, outer-membrane proteins bridge the mitochondrion directly to the phagophore. DCT-1, the BNIP3/NIX ortholog, is required for stress-induced mitophagy in adult somatic tissues and acts genetically with PINK-1 and PDR-1. FNDC-1, the FUNDC1 ortholog, acts in hypoxia-reoxygenation mitophagy and in a late, ubiquitin-independent phase of paternal mitochondrial elimination in the embryo. The earlier, ubiquitin-dependent phase of paternal organelle clearance (allophagy) uses the receptor ALLO-1, which binds LGG-1 through an LC3-interacting region and is regulated by the TBK1-family kinase IKKE-1. Mitophagy of stress-damaged mitochondria in muscle also depends on the FIS1-family outer-membrane proteins FIS-1 and FIS-2, which are not needed for fission in this species but act at a late step after MFF/DRP-1-dependent fission; without them, stalled LGG-1-positive intermediates containing mitochondrial fragments accumulate. Mitophagy is coupled to mitochondrial biogenesis through SKN-1-dependent transcription of dct-1, and its decline with age contributes to the accumulation of damaged mitochondria.

Boundary. This is a concrete C. elegans module for the selective capture of mitochondria by the autophagy machinery. It starts at the marking of a mitochondrion (PINK-1/PDR-1 ubiquitin signalling, or an outer-membrane receptor) and ends at receptor engagement of LGG-1 on the phagophore and the FIS-1/FIS-2-dependent completion of disposal. Building the autophagosome (ATG-1/EPG-1 initiation, BEC-1/VPS-34 PI3P synthesis, ATG-9, ATG-2/ATG-18, and the ATG-7/ATG-3/ATG-16 lipidation cascade) is the subject of MODULE:c_elegans_autophagosome_biogenesis and is not re-modeled here; LGG-1 appears only in its role as the docking site that receptors bind. Autophagosome-lysosome fusion (EPG-5, LGG-2, HOPS) and degradation are also downstream and outside this boundary. The phagophore-ER contact that supplies lipid for phagophore growth is MODULE:phagophore_organelle_contact_site. FIS-1 acts at the ER-mitochondria interface (MAM), but that contact is a separate structure from the phagophore-ER contact site module. Fission. Stress-induced mitophagy in muscle uses MFF-dependent DRP-1 fission upstream of the FIS-1/FIS-2 step. The fission machinery itself is in MODULE:c_elegans_mitochondrial_dynamics. Fission is not required for every route: oxidative-stress-induced neuronal mitophagy is drp-1-independent but partly pink-1-dependent (PMID:35916724). Fission is therefore recorded as context, not as a required part. Mechanism carried over from mammals. In worms, PINK-1 and PDR-1 are placed in mitophagy by genetics (reporters, epistasis, LGG-1 colocalization) and by PDR-1 E3 ligase activity toward other substrates. Ubiquitin Ser65 phosphorylation by PINK-1, phosphorylation-driven PDR-1 activation, and ubiquitination of named outer-membrane substrates have not been shown biochemically in C. elegans. The PINK-1 and PDR-1 molecular functions below follow the gene reviews. The step order is taken from the mammalian pathway. Members left out. SQST-1 (p62) is excluded because its review keeps the mitophagy annotation as non-core with no worm evidence; its demonstrated worm role is aggrephagy. LGG-2 acts downstream of LGG-1 in autophagosome maturation and is left to the autophagy modules. MIRO-1 is a Parkin substrate in mammals but has not been shown to act in worm mitophagy. ATFS-1, SKN-1 and DAF-16 regulate mitophagy gene expression and are regulatory context, not mitophagy machinery. IKKE-1 (P32742) regulates ALLO-1 by phosphorylation and is described in the ALLO-1 annoton, not modeled as its own member. The E2 enzymes UBC-18 and UBC-16 that ubiquitinate paternal membranous organelles are also not modeled separately. ALLO-1 (Q9U389) is included even though it has no gene review in this repository. Its role rests on PMID:29255173 and on the accepted LGG-1-ALLO-1 interaction in the lgg-1 review. Accessions. LGG-1 is Q09490, the accession used by the lgg-1 review and UniProt. The project page gives a different accession.

Part 1: ubiquitin-dependent marking of damaged mitochondria
PINK-1/PDR-1 ubiquitin signalling on damaged mitochondriaSignaling Pathwaypink1_pdr1_marking

PINK-1 is a mitochondrially targeted serine/threonine kinase. Mammalian PINK1 is stabilized on the outer membrane of depolarized mitochondria, phosphorylates ubiquitin and Parkin at Ser65, and so activates Parkin. PDR-1 is the worm Parkin, an RBR E3 ubiquitin ligase. In C. elegans, pink-1 is required for the formation of nascent LGG-1-positive mitophagosomes on muscle mitochondria and for stress-induced autophagy. PDR-1 joins the stalled mitophagy intermediates seen in fis-1 fis-2 mutants. Under oxidative stress the receptor DCT-1 is ubiquitinated in a PINK-1-dependent manner.

Part 2: outer-membrane receptor recognition of mitochondria
Mitophagy receptor and adaptor recognitionBiological Processmitophagy_receptor_recognition

Outer-membrane receptor proteins bridge the mitochondrion to LGG-1-decorated phagophore membranes. The worm receptors are used in different contexts and are not interchangeable alternatives for one step. They are grouped as a variant set because each one supplies the mitochondrion-to-phagophore link.

Part 4: completion of orderly disposal of fission-derived mitochondrial fragments
FIS-1/FIS-2-dependent disposal of damaged mitochondriaBiological Processfis1_disposal

After stress-induced, MFF/DRP-1-dependent fission, the FIS1-family outer-membrane proteins FIS-1 and FIS-2 act redundantly at a late step of mitophagy, before fusion with lysosomes. Fis1 joins a DRP-1-containing complex at the ER-mitochondria interface but is not needed for DRP-1 recruitment or for fission. In fis-1 fis-2 mutants, large LGG-1-positive aggregates containing mitochondrial fragments, DRP-1 and PDR-1 build up. Their formation requires mff, drp-1 and pink-1. FIS-1 is also needed to remove UV-C-induced mitochondrial DNA damage.
