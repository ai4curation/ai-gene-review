Neurexin-centred trans-synaptic adhesion complexes

Neurexins are presynaptic type I transmembrane proteins that act as a hub for trans-synaptic adhesion. Their extracellular LNS domains bind a set of structurally unrelated postsynaptic or secreted partners, forming protein bridges that span the synaptic cleft, hold the pre- and postsynaptic membranes together, and signal in both directions to organize synaptic properties. The best-established partners are the neuroligins, which are catalytically inactive esterase-fold transmembrane proteins; the leucine-rich-repeat LRRTM proteins; and a tripartite bridge in which secreted cerebellin hexamers link neurexin to the amino-terminal domain of delta-family ionotropic glutamate receptors (GluD1/GluD2). Which partner a neurexin engages is controlled in large part by alternative splicing, notably at neurexin splice site 4. OUT OF SCOPE: the intracellular scaffolds recruited by the neurexin and partner cytoplasmic tails (CASK, Mint, PSD-95, gephyrin), the synaptic vesicle release machinery, and the many other neurexin ligands (dystroglycan, neurexophilins, latrophilins, calsyntenins, GABA-A receptor, cerebellin-independent C1q-like proteins). Each of these can be added as further partner variants or as a separate module.

Hub-and-partner module. The neurexin hub is the one required part; the postsynaptic side is modelled as a ONE_OR_MORE variant set because a single neurexin population can engage several partner classes, with alternative splicing (neurexin SS4, neuroligin splice site B) biasing the choice. The neurexin hub is anchored to the bilaterian PAINT node PTN002227321; the neuroligin partner is also bilaterian, whereas the LRRTM and cerebellin-GluD partners are documented in vertebrates. No PANTHER id is asserted for the LRRTM partner: UniProt cross-references LRRTM2 to PTHR45842, whereas PANTHER's own classification places it in PTHR45712:SF19, an LRRTM2-only subfamily of a broad leucine-rich-repeat family, so neither id cleanly names the LRRTM family. Molecular functions for the cerebellin and GluD annotons follow GOA (CBLN1 contributes_to GO:0030545, IDA PMID:27418511) and the completed GRID1 gene review. Only GRID1 has a gene review among the grounded members; NRXN1-3, NLGN1-4, LRRTM2, CBLN1 and GRID2 are not yet reviewed.
