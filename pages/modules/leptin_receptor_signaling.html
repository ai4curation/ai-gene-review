Leptin receptor signaling — LepRb-JAK2 activation with SH2B1, STAT3 docking at pY1141, SHP2-ERK and SH2B1-IRS-PI3K branches, and SOCS3/PTP1B negative feedback; LEPR/JAK2/SH2B1/STAT3/PTPN11/IRS1/IRS2/SOCS3/PTPN1

The cell-intrinsic signalling unit of the long leptin receptor isoform (LepRb). Leptin binding brings receptor-associated JAK2 molecules together; JAK2, whose activity is potentiated by the SH2B1 adaptor, autophosphorylates and phosphorylates conserved tyrosines in the LepRb cytoplasmic tail. Phosphotyrosine 1141 (Tyr1138 in mouse) recruits STAT3, which JAK2 phosphorylates; STAT3 dimers enter the nucleus and drive leptin target genes, including SOCS3. Phosphotyrosine 986 (Tyr985 in mouse) recruits the phosphatase SHP2 (PTPN11), which is required for most leptin-induced ERK activation, while SH2B1 bound to JAK2 recruits IRS1 and IRS2 for JAK2 phosphorylation and activation of the PI3K-AKT branch. The response is terminated by STAT3-induced SOCS3, which docks on phosphotyrosine 986 and inhibits signalling, and by the ER-anchored phosphatase PTP1B (PTPN1), which dephosphorylates JAK2.

Derivation. This module is derived from RGD PW:0000363 (leptin system pathway; 11 RGD-curated human genes: INSR, IRS1, IRS2, JAK2, LEP, LEPR, PTPN1, PTPN11, SH2B1, SOCS3, STAT3). RGD's diagram and text describe the receptor-proximal JAK2-STAT3 cascade, the SHP2 (Ptpn11) route to Raf/Mek/Erk, the SH2B1-IRS route to PI3K-Akt, and SOCS3 and PTP1B negative regulation; all ten diagram genes are modelled here. INSR is on RGD's human gene list but not in the diagram or the RGD text, and the insulin receptor does no step of leptin receptor signalling; it is left out (insulin signalling is modules/insulin_receptor_signaling.yaml). The RGD description also opens with the hypothalamic POMC/AgRP/melanocortin axis; that physiology is not cell-intrinsic receptor signalling and is covered by the existing modules/leptin_melanocortin_signaling.yaml. Split with leptin_melanocortin_signaling. That module models the hypothalamic adipostat (leptin release, receptor activation, POMC processing, MC4R agonism, AGRP antagonism) and states in its notes that it deliberately compresses JAK2, STAT3, SOCS3 and PTP1B into the receptor's STAT output rather than re-modelling the cascade. This module is that missing cascade: what happens inside any LepRb-expressing cell (arcuate neurons, but also immune, endothelial and other peripheral cells) from receptor engagement to STAT3 transcription and its termination. The ligand step is included by module_ref to leptin_melanocortin_signaling#leptin_signal_generation rather than restated. The reverse link (this module's STAT3 output is what raises POMC and lowers AGRP transcription in arcuate neurons, i.e. the content of leptin_melanocortin_signaling#leptin_receptor_activation) is recorded here in prose because connections are document-scoped. Reuse of shared steps. STAT dimerisation and nuclear import, and STAT-dependent transcription, are the generic steps of modules/jak_stat_signaling.yaml and are included by module_ref (jak_stat_signaling#stat_dimerization_import, jak_stat_signaling#stat_transcription). The ERK relay and PI3K activation downstream of the two side branches are included by module_ref to erk_cascade#erk_relay and pi3k_akt_mtor#pi3k_activation. modules/il6_signaling.yaml was checked: its nodes are IL-6/gp130-specific (gp130 receptor engagement, gp130-associated JAKs, an IL-6 STAT3 output node) and expose no step that is genuinely shared with LepRb beyond the generic JAK-STAT trunk, so it is referenced in prose only; LepRb and gp130 are related class I cytokine receptors and both use STAT3 and SOCS3, but the docking sites and kinase (JAK2 only for LepRb) differ. Residue numbering. RGD and most primary papers use mouse LepRb numbering (Tyr985, Tyr1077, Tyr1138). In the human LEPR sequence (UniProt P48357, isoform B displayed) the equivalent residues are Tyr986, Tyr1079 and Tyr1141 (UniProt PTM and MUTAGEN lines). Descriptions here give human numbering with the mouse number in parentheses where a paper is quoted. STAT5. Leptin also activates STAT5 through pTyr1079 (mouse Tyr1077). STAT5 is not in the RGD diagram, and Y1077 mutant mice and STAT5 deletion in LepRb cells show only modest or no effect on energy balance (PMID:28270795), so STAT5 is recorded here and not modelled as a part. Substrate and target-gene rule. SOCS3 is both a STAT3 target gene and a participant: it is included as a participant only in the feedback step, where it does work (SH2 docking on pTyr986 and inhibition of JAK2), and the STAT3-to-SOCS3 relation is a POSITIVELY_REGULATES connection (induction), not a claim that SOCS3 takes part in STAT3 transcription. Other leptin STAT3 targets (POMC, AGRP repression) are outputs and are not participants. STAT3 is the substrate of JAK2 in the STAT3 docking step but also performs the transcription that is the pathway's output, so it is a participant by its own DNA-binding transcription factor activity. Part 2: receptor engagement and JAK2 activation
LepRb engagement, JAK2 activation and receptor tyrosine phosphorylationRegulatory Steplepr_jak2_activation

Leptin binding to the long receptor isoform LepRb assembles signalling-competent receptor complexes in which constitutively bound JAK2 molecules (box1/box2 region) trans-activate. JAK2 then phosphorylates the conserved LepRb tail tyrosines Tyr986, Tyr1079 and Tyr1141 (mouse Tyr985, Tyr1077, Tyr1138), creating docking sites for SH2 proteins. SH2B1 binds activated JAK2 and potentiates its kinase activity. The short receptor isoforms lack the tail tyrosines and do not activate STAT3.

SOCS3 feedback and PTP1B dephosphorylationRegulatory Stepleptin_negative_regulation

Two brakes act at the receptor-JAK2 level. STAT3-induced SOCS3 binds phosphorylated Tyr986 and inhibits JAK2, a delayed negative feedback loop. PTP1B (PTPN1), anchored on the cytoplasmic face of the endoplasmic reticulum, dephosphorylates JAK2 (and STAT3 in vitro), setting leptin sensitivity.
