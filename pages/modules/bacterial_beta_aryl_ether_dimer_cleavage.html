Bacterial beta-aryl ether cleavage of lignin-derived dimers

A reusable bacterial module for glutathione-dependent cleavage of the beta-O-4 (beta-aryl ether) interunit linkage of lignin-derived aromatic dimers, exemplified by guaiacylglycerol-beta-guaiacyl ether (GGE). The module has three reaction steps. An NAD+-dependent Calpha-dehydrogenase oxidizes the benzylic Calpha hydroxyl of the dimer to give the ketone alpha-(2-methoxyphenoxy)-beta-hydroxypropiovanillone (MPHPV). A glutathione S-transferase beta-etherase then displaces the aryloxy leaving group at Cbeta with glutathione, releasing guaiacol and alpha-glutathionyl-beta-hydroxypropiovanillone (GS-HPV). Finally a glutathione lyase removes the glutathione moiety using a second glutathione, yielding the achiral monomer beta-hydroxypropiovanillone (HPV) and glutathione disulfide. Because the lignin polymer is racemic at Calpha and Cbeta, each step is implemented by a set of stereochemically complementary enzymes, modeled as variants. Uptake of the dimer, downstream catabolism of HPV and guaiacol to vanillate and protocatechuate, glutathione regeneration, and pathways for other interunit linkages are outside the boundary.

Boundary decision. The module is defined by the interunit linkage cleaved (beta-O-4), not by one model substrate or one strain, because the same three-step chemistry is used on GGE and on the corresponding syringyl dimers and operates in both Sphingobium sp. SYK-6 and Novosphingobium aromaticivorans F199. The three steps are modeled as ordered parts; the stereochemical redundancy within each step is modeled as a variant_set with selection ONE_OR_MORE, since a strain needs several complementary enzymes to process the racemic linkage and most strains carry more than one. Grounding limits. Several pathway members have no UniProtKB entry at the time of curation: LigN (SLG_35900) and LigP (SLG_32600) of SYK-6, and the SYK-6 Nu-class glutathione lyase SLG_04120 (ligQ, SYK6GSTNu), so they are named in prose and variant descriptions but asserted as representative members only where an accession exists. The SYK-6 proteome is not currently retrievable from UniProtKB as a reference proteome, so LigL and LigO are cited from their individual unreviewed entries. The F199 homologs Saro_0205, Saro_0793, Saro_0794, Saro_1875, Saro_2091 and Saro_2405 are identified by homology and GGE-induced expression in PMID:29449375 rather than by individual enzyme assay, and are therefore not asserted as representative members; only the experimentally characterized F199 glutathione lyase NaGSTNu (Saro_2595) is. Adjacency. Downstream, HPV catabolism and guaiacol O-demethylation feed vanillate and protocatechuate; those steps are covered by modules/hydroxycinnamate_vanillate_catabolism.yaml (vanillin to vanillate to protocatechuate) and the ring-cleavage modules modules/catechol_ortho_cleavage.yaml and modules/beta_ketoadipate_lower_pathway.yaml, with modules/bacterial_gallate_catabolism.yaml covering the related gallate route. None of those modules contains a beta-aryl ether step, so there is no overlap. Not modeled here: the 5-5 (DDVA) route (LigXacd, LigZ, LigY, LigW, LigW2), the beta-1 (DGPD) route (LdpA, LdpB, LdpC), the beta-5 (DCA) route (PhcCDFG, PcfL), the beta-beta (pinoresinol) route (PinZ, PinY, PinX, PinW), the lignostilbene dioxygenases that cleave the stilbene intermediates of those routes, and dimer transporters (DdvK, DdvT). Of these the DDVA route is the next best grounded and is the natural follow-up module. Ontology gaps for GO editors: there is no molecular-function term for Calpha-dehydrogenase (arylglycerol-beta-aryl ether Calpha oxidation), for beta-etherase (glutathione-dependent beta-aryl ether cleavage), or for glutathione lyase (beta-S-thioether cleavage yielding glutathione disulfide), and no biological-process term for beta-aryl ether cleavage or for lignin-dimer catabolism distinct from breakdown of the lignin polymer; each annoton therefore uses the nearest existing parent term, with the specific reaction named in preferred_term and role_description. The existing IEA annotation of LigD to GO:0046274 lignin catabolic process sets the precedent for the process term used here. No Rhea reactions exist for these three steps; the UniProt Rhea cross-reference RHEA:15741 on the LigL and LigO entries is a dichlorocyclohexadienediol dehydrogenase reaction from an automatic annotation rule and does not describe the GGE reaction, so it is deliberately not cited.
