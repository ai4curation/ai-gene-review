Galactose catabolism (Leloir pathway)

The Leloir pathway converts D-galactose to glucose 6-phosphate. A galactose mutarotase supplies alpha-D-galactose, galactokinase phosphorylates it to alpha-D-galactose 1-phosphate, and galactose-1-phosphate uridylyltransferase exchanges UMP with UDP-glucose to give glucose 1-phosphate and UDP-galactose. UDP-glucose 4-epimerase regenerates UDP-glucose from UDP-galactose, so the pathway is catalytic in its UDP-sugar co-substrate, and phosphoglucomutase converts glucose 1-phosphate to glucose 6-phosphate for glycolysis. In bacteria and animals the mutarotase and the epimerase are separate proteins; in yeasts a single bifunctional protein carries an N-terminal UDP-glucose 4-epimerase domain and a C-terminal mutarotase domain. The pathway is distinct from nucleotide-sugar biosynthesis or isolated UDP-galactose epimerization in organisms that lack the galactokinase and uridylyltransferase reactions.

file:projects/P_PUTIDA/deep-research/PSEPK__galactose_leloir_pathway__ppu00052-deep-research-openscientist.md
PSEPK Leloir module/pathway/taxon deep research
OpenScientist research found no identifiable GalK or GalT in KT2440 and independently classified the focused catabolic Leloir route as unsatisfiable; PP_1165 remained substrate-uncertain and GalE alone did not establish catabolic pathway coverage.

ALIGN (YeastPathways project, 2026-10): S. cerevisiae and S. pombe exemplars were added to every step, the phosphoglucomutase exit step (glucose 1-phosphate to glucose 6-phosphate) was added as the fifth part because the YeastCyc pathway, the PomBase model and the yeast literature (PMID:18779058, PMID:8119301) treat it as the last Leloir enzyme, and the mutarotase step was given an EXACTLY_ONE architecture variant set: a standalone GalM-family mutarotase (bacteria, animals) versus the C-terminal mutarotase domain of the bifunctional yeast GAL10 protein (PMID:14764091). The fused GAL10 proteins classify in the GalE PANTHER subfamily, so the fused variant carries no PANTHER id and is grounded by its representative members; the same proteins are representatives of the shared epimerase step. S. cerevisiae also has two uncharacterized standalone GalM-family proteins (YHR210C, YNR071C) that are not used as exemplars. Galactokinase family membership alone does not establish activity: S. cerevisiae GAL3, in the same PANTHER subfamily as GAL1, is the galactose sensor of the GAL switch rather than the pathway kinase. S. pombe gal1/gal7/gal10 are silenced in wild-type cells (PMID:21075050); S. pombe also has a second UDP-glucose 4-epimerase, uge1, which serves galactosylation on glucose and which the PomBase model places in the Leloir pathway (PMID:19942659); it is not used as a Leloir exemplar here. Galactose uptake (GAL2, S. pombe ght2) and GAL4/GAL80/GAL3 regulation are outside the module. The previous CURATION-only knowledge gap was retired as a curation rather than biological gap; its content is kept here: aldose 1-epimerase and UDP-glucose 4-epimerase families are substrate- and pathway-promiscuous, so a satisfiable Leloir pathway requires galactokinase and galactose-1-phosphate uridylyltransferase in addition to any mutarotase or epimerase candidate (P. putida KT2440 has only GalE and does not satisfy the module). The GO:0033499 label was updated to its current form ("beta-D-galactose ..."). GO-CAM disagreements: YeastPathways YEAST-GALACT-METAB-PWY places all activities in GO:0019388 galactose catabolic process rather than the Leloir-specific GO:0033499, but otherwise agrees. PomBase 6796b94c00002484 includes uge1 and the ght2 transporter and lacks the gal10 mutarotase activity.
