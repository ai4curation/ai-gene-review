Imd signaling pathway in Drosophila melanogaster

The immune deficiency (Imd) pathway is the main Drosophila defense against Gram-negative bacteria and some Gram-positive bacilli. It is triggered when DAP-type peptidoglycan binds the transmembrane receptor PGRP-LC or the intracellular receptor PGRP-LE. The receptors recruit the death-domain protein Imd, which assembles a complex with the adaptor Fadd and the caspase Dredd. Dredd cleaves Imd, and the E3 ligase Diap2, with the E2 enzymes Bendless and Effete and the E2 variant Uev1A, attaches K63-linked ubiquitin chains. These chains recruit the Tak1-Tab2 kinase complex, which activates the IKK complex (IKKbeta and Kenny). IKK phosphorylates the NF-kappa-B factor Relish, Dredd cleaves it, and the Rel homology domain enters the nucleus to induce antimicrobial peptide genes. Tak1 also activates the JNK branch (Hemipterous, Basket) that acts through AP-1 (Jra, Kayak).
