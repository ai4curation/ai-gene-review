Chitin biosynthesis and catabolism in Drosophila melanogaster

Chitin, a beta-1,4-linked polymer of N-acetylglucosamine, is the fibrous scaffold of the insect cuticle, tracheal linings and the peritrophic matrix of the midgut. It is synthesized at the plasma membrane by chitin synthases: Krotzkopf verkehrt (kkv) makes chitin of the epidermal cuticle and trachea, and Chs2 is the second chitin synthase, expressed in the gut. During molting and tissue remodelling, chitin is broken down by family 18 chitinases (Cht genes), which cleave the polymer into oligosaccharides, and by beta-N-acetylhexosaminidases (Hexo1, Hexo2), which release N-acetylglucosamine from the non-reducing ends.
