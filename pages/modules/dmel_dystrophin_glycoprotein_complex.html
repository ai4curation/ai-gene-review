Dystrophin glycoprotein complex in Drosophila melanogaster

The dystrophin glycoprotein complex (DGC) links the actin cytoskeleton to the extracellular matrix across the plasma membrane, mainly in muscle. Dystroglycan (Dg) spans the membrane: its extracellular part binds laminin and other matrix proteins and its cytoplasmic tail binds dystrophin, which in turn binds actin. Sarcoglycans form a transmembrane subcomplex that stabilizes the link, and the cytoplasmic adaptors dystrobrevin and syntrophins bind dystrophin and recruit signaling proteins. Drosophila has single dystrophin (Dys) and dystroglycan genes, one dystrobrevin, three sarcoglycans and two syntrophin-like genes. Loss of Dys or Dg causes age-dependent muscle degeneration, and Dg also acts in epithelial polarity and in the oocyte.
