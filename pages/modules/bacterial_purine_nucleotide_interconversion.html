Bacterial purine nucleotide interconversion

A reusable bacterial module that partitions inosine monophosphate (IMP) into the two canonical purine nucleotide branches and connects the resulting AMP and GMP pools to nucleotide phosphorylation. The AMP branch uses adenylosuccinate synthetase and adenylosuccinate lyase; the GMP branch uses IMP dehydrogenase and glutamine-hydrolyzing GMP synthase. Adenylate kinase equilibrates AMP, ADP, and ATP, while guanylate kinase and nucleoside diphosphate kinase connect GMP to GDP and GTP. Synthesis of IMP from PRPP, purine uptake and salvage entry, alarmone metabolism, cyclic-nucleotide signaling, and purine degradation are outside this module.

purine nucleotide metabolic processGO:0006163

This reusable bacterial boundary begins with IMP and ends with connected adenine and guanine nucleotide phosphate-state exchange. PurB is included only for its adenylosuccinate-to-AMP role here; its SAICAR-lyase role remains in de novo IMP synthesis. Ndk is included as the physiologically shared GDP- to-GTP step, while its pyrimidine and deoxyribonucleotide substrates are not modeled as separate branches. No localization is asserted because a generic cytoplasm or cytosol term is not part of the reusable pathway definition.
