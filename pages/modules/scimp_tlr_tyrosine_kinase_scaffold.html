SCIMP-scaffolded TLR tyrosine phosphorylation module

A non-TIR adaptor branch of Toll-like receptor signalling in macrophages. After LPS activates TLR4 (see modules/tlr4_md2_lps_signaling.yaml), the palmitoylated transmembrane adaptor SCIMP binds the TLR4 TIR domain. SCIMP carries the Src-family kinase LYN constitutively on its proline-rich region and, once its tyrosines are phosphorylated, recruits SYK through both SYK SH2 domains. LYN and SYK phosphorylate TLR4 on its TIR domain (mouse Y672, human Y674), and SCIMP recruits ERK1/2 to TLR4 in membrane ruffles, leading to c-FOS activation and a selective IL-6 and IL-12p40 response. CSK, docking on a separate SCIMP phosphotyrosine, provides negative feedback. The branch runs alongside the TIRAP-MYD88 and TRAM-TRIF branches and does not replace them.

SCIMP is a non-TIR adaptor that binds the TLR4 TIR domain after LPS stimulation.
SCIMP is a non-TIR-containing adaptor, binding directly to the TLR4-TIR domain in response to lipopolysaccharide.

Evidence is mostly from mouse macrophages (bone-marrow-derived and RAW264.7); human support is a THP-1 experiment (PMID:35337798) and the conserved human TLR4 Y674 (PMID:37058370). Human gene products are used as members for consistency with tlr4_md2_lps_signaling. SCIMP is immune-restricted, so the branch is specific to antigen-presenting cells. GRB2 and SLP65 (BLNK) also dock on phospho-SCIMP after LPS, but what they contribute to TLR output is unknown, so they are not modelled as parts. No production GO-CAM includes SCIMP. The same scaffold acts downstream of TLR1/2, TLR3 and TLR7 and of Dectin-1, but receptor binding and kinase recruitment have only been shown for TLR4 (and TLR2 binding), so TLR4 is the modelled receptor.

SCIMP transmembrane scaffoldRegulatory Stepscimp_scaffold
