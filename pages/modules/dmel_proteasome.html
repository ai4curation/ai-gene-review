26S proteasome in Drosophila melanogaster

The 26S proteasome degrades proteins tagged with polyubiquitin chains. Its 20S core particle is a barrel of four stacked heptameric rings, two outer rings of alpha subunits that gate substrate entry and two inner rings of beta subunits, three of which (beta1, beta2 and beta5) are N-terminal threonine proteases with caspase-like, trypsin-like and chymotrypsin-like specificities. The 19S regulatory particle caps the core. Its base contains six AAA+ ATPases (Rpt1-Rpt6) that unfold substrates and open the core gate, together with the scaffolds Rpn1 and Rpn2. Ubiquitin receptors (Rpn10 and Rpn13) bind ubiquitylated substrates, the lid metalloprotease Rpn11 removes ubiquitin chains before degradation, and Uch-L5 bound to Rpn13 trims chains. Drosophila has a testis-specific 26S proteasome in which paralogs replace several alpha, beta, Rpt, Rpn and Uch-L5 subunits.
