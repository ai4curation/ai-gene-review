Neuromuscular junction acetylcholine receptor clustering — neural agrin, LRP4 coreceptor, MuSK kinase activation by DOK7, CRK/CRKL adaptors and rapsyn anchoring; AGRN/LRP4/MUSK/DOK7/CRK/CRKL/RAPSN

At the vertebrate neuromuscular junction, nicotinic acetylcholine receptors (AChRs) are packed into the postsynaptic membrane of the skeletal muscle fiber at about 10,000 per square micrometre, directly opposite the motor nerve terminal. They get there in two ways: subsynaptic nuclei transcribe the receptor genes at a higher rate, and receptors already in the membrane are clustered by tethering to a subsynaptic scaffold. The clustering signal starts with neural agrin, the z+ splice forms of AGRN that motor neurons secrete into the synaptic basal lamina. Agrin binds LRP4, a single-pass LDL-receptor-family protein on the muscle surface. LRP4 also binds the muscle-specific receptor tyrosine kinase MuSK. The arc-shaped LRP4 ectodomain holds agrin and MuSK together in a ternary complex, and this activates MuSK. Inside the cell, the adaptor DOK7 binds the phosphorylated NPXY juxtamembrane motif of MuSK through its PTB domain. Its PH-PTB region dimerises, which drives trans-autophosphorylation of the MuSK activation loop, so DOK7 acts as an intracellular activator of the kinase. MuSK then phosphorylates two tyrosines in the DOK7 C-terminal tail, and these recruit the adaptors CRK and CRKL, which relay the signal towards the actin cytoskeleton. Rapsyn (RAPSN), a 43 kDa peripheral membrane protein on the cytoplasmic face of the postsynaptic membrane, binds the AChR and links it to the MuSK-containing scaffold. Without rapsyn, receptors do not form clusters at all. Before innervation, MuSK, LRP4, rapsyn and AChRs already form aneural clusters in the central region of the muscle (prepatterning); nerve-derived agrin then sharpens and stabilises these clusters at the synapse. Loss-of-function variants in AGRN, LRP4, MUSK, DOK7 or RAPSN cause congenital myasthenic syndromes. Autoantibodies against MuSK or LRP4 cause forms of myasthenia gravis.

Derivation. This module was split out of modules/acetylcholine_signaling.yaml, whose boundary decision (4) excludes the agrin-LRP4-MuSK-DOK7-rapsyn set. The reason is that those proteins build the synapse rather than receiving or transducing the ACh signal. That module names GO:0071340 and the mouse GO-CAM gomodel:64c3044100000089 as the grounding for a separate module. The members come from the RGD Pathway Ontology diagram PW:0000842, which draws Agrn/Lrp4/Musk/Dok7/Rapsn as a cluster acting on the NMJ nAChR. PW:0000842 is listed as an evidence source of this module as well, because this module is derived from that cluster of the diagram. PW:0000841 (the ACh overview) and PW:0000843 (muscarinic) are not cited, because they do not draw this machinery. Boundary decisions. (1) The muscle AChR is the cargo of this process, not a participant in it, and is therefore not a part. The CLAUDE.md test is whether a product does any of the work of the process. Rapsyn does: it binds the receptor and tethers it to the scaffold, and without it no clusters form (PMID:7675108, PMID:10320756). The AChR pentamer is the thing that is gathered. Comparator check (QuickGO, GO:0071340 with is_a/part_of descendants, human/mouse/rat, October 2026): the term and its regulation children are carried by Agrn, Lrp4, Musk, Rapsn, Fnta, Dnaja3, Colq, Dvl1, Etv5 and Rer1 (several as acts_upstream_of_or_within), and by none of Chrna1, Chrnb1, Chrnd, Chrne or Chrng. Human CHRNA1 instead carries channel, signalling and NMJ-location terms (GO:0022848, GO:0095500, GO:0007274, GO:0031594), and the gene reviews used for acetylcholine_signaling record those as its core. The term's parent GO:0043113 receptor clustering is a protein-localisation process (is_a GO:0072657 protein localization to membrane), and such terms name the machinery that moves or holds the protein, not the protein that is localised. The receptor therefore appears only as the target of the rapsyn adaptor function (acetylcholine-gated channel complex GO:0005892, with CHRNA1 as the representative subunit). Its channel function is modelled in acetylcholine_signaling#nachr_muscle and is not repeated here. A module_ref part was deliberately not used, because it would make the receptor a required step of the clustering process. Agrin's laminin and dystroglycan binding, which anchor it in the basal lamina, are recorded in the AGRN review and are not modelled here.
