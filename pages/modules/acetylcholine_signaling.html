Human acetylcholine transmission and comparative receptor mechanisms

Human cholinergic transmission combines choline uptake by SLC5A7, cytosolic acetylcholine synthesis by CHAT, vesicular loading by SLC18A3, calcium-triggered exocytosis, receptor activation and extracellular acetylcholinesterase-mediated clearance. Nicotinic receptors are specific pentameric cation-channel assemblies: adult and fetal muscle receptors, neuronal heteromers, alpha7 homopentamers and alpha9/alpha10 heteromers. Their subunit requirements and ligand-binding interfaces differ. Muscarinic receptors activate heterotrimeric G proteins: M1/M3/M5 preferentially couple to Gq/11 and M2/M4 predominantly couple to Gi/o, with ligand, tissue and assay-dependent variation. GRKs and RGS proteins regulate signal duration. Channel calcium entry need not cause net excitation: alpha9/alpha10 activation in hair cells recruits calcium-sensitive potassium conductance. Optional rat and chicken receptor segments expose experimentally measured differences in calcium permeability and downstream calcium handling; they do not establish a complete conserved vertebrate circuit. Generic exocytosis and downstream PLC/cAMP pathways are explicit interfaces. Neuromuscular receptor clustering and synapse assembly are separate processes.
