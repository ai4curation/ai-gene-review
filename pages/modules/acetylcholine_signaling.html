Acetylcholine signaling — choline uptake, ACh synthesis and vesicular loading, nicotinic and muscarinic reception, AChE clearance and GRK/RGS termination; SLC5A7/CHAT/SLC18A3/CHRN*/CHRM1-5/GNAQ/GNAI/ACHE/GRK2/GRK5/RGS2/4/6

Acetylcholine (ACh) is the transmitter of motor neurons, of all autonomic preganglionic neurons and of parasympathetic postganglionic neurons, and a neuromodulator in the brain. In the cholinergic terminal, choline is taken up by the sodium-coupled high-affinity choline transporter CHT1 (SLC5A7), the rate-limiting step for synthesis. Choline O-acetyltransferase (CHAT) transfers the acetyl group of acetyl-CoA to choline in the cytosol, and the vesicular ACh transporter VAChT (SLC18A3) exchanges luminal protons for cytosolic ACh to fill synaptic vesicles, which are then released by calcium-triggered exocytosis. Released ACh acts on two receptor classes. Nicotinic receptors are pentameric ACh-gated cation channels of the Cys-loop superfamily: the muscle receptor at the neuromuscular junction (two alpha1 with beta1, delta and either the fetal gamma or the adult epsilon subunit) drives endplate depolarisation and contraction; neuronal heteromers (alpha4beta2* in the brain, alpha3beta4* in autonomic ganglia, alpha6beta2* on dopaminergic and GABAergic terminals, with alpha2, alpha5 and beta3 as additional or accessory subunits) and the alpha7 homomer depolarise neurons and modulate transmitter release; alpha9alpha10 receptors serve cochlear efferent synapses. Muscarinic receptors are G protein-coupled: M1, M3 and M5 couple to Gq/11 and activate phospholipase C beta, while M2 and M4 couple to Gi/o, inhibit adenylyl cyclase and, through G beta-gamma, open GIRK channels; presynaptic M2/M4 autoreceptors inhibit further ACh release. Acetylcholinesterase (ACHE) in the synaptic cleft hydrolyses ACh to choline and acetate within milliseconds, terminating the signal and returning choline for reuptake. Muscarinic signalling is further terminated by G protein-coupled receptor kinases (GRK2 for most subtypes, GRK5 for the M2/M4 autoreceptors), which phosphorylate active receptors and promote their internalisation, and by RGS GTPase-activating proteins (RGS2 for Gq, RGS4 and RGS6 for Gi/o) that shorten the lifetime of active G alpha.

Thirty-five are modelled here; the overview diagram itself draws no genes, so the parts and topology come from the two subpathway diagrams and the record text. Boundary decisions. (1) Ligand supply and clearance are modelled as parts with their own molecular functions: SLC5A7 choline:sodium symporter activity, CHAT choline O-acetyltransferase activity and ACHE acetylcholinesterase activity. VAChT (SLC18A3) vesicular loading and calcium-triggered release are not re-modelled. They are included by reference (module_ref) to the VAChT node of modules/synaptic_vesicle_neurotransmitter_loading.yaml and to modules/synaptic_vesicle_exocytosis.yaml, which already ground SLC18A3 with acetylcholine transmembrane transporter activity GO:0005277 and the release machinery. (2) The reception step is a variant set with two classes. The nicotinic class is a second variant set of receptor subtypes, each modelled as an acetylcholine-gated channel complex (GO:0005892) carrying acetylcholine-gated monoatomic cation-selective channel activity GO:0022848 on the complex. Subunits are active units, because the existing gene reviews (CHRNA1, CHRNA4, CHRNB1, CHRNB2, CHRNG) record GO:0022848 as contributes_to rather than enables. The muscarinic class is a variant set of the Gq/11 branch (M1/M3/M5; GNAQ, GNA11, GNA14) and the Gi/o branch (M2/M4; GNAI1, GNAI2, plus GNAO1). GNAO1 is not in the RGD gene list. It is added because G alpha o is the partner in the M2 receptor-G protein structure (PMID:31073061) and in the human CHRM2 GO-CAM. Their downstream effector steps are referenced, not copied. The Gq branch points to the PLC beta node of modules/gpcr_plc_calcium_pkc_signaling.yaml. The Gi branch negatively regulates the adenylyl cyclase node of modules/gpcr_camp_pka_signaling.yaml, the module that RGD's "Galphai lowers cAMP" edge acts on. G beta-gamma opening of GIRK channels is described in prose and not modelled, because RGD does not draw it. (3) GRK2/GRK5 and RGS2/4/6 form an optional desensitisation/termination part, so that the module can still be satisfied without it. G alpha molecular function is G protein activity GO:0003925, as in the human CHRM2 and mouse Chrm3 GO-CAMs. (4) The agrin-LRP4-MuSK-DOK7-rapsyn set (AGRN O00468, LRP4 O75096, MUSK O15146, DOK7 Q18PE1, RAPSN Q13702) is excluded. It is the neuromuscular-junction AChR clustering pathway (GO:0071340 skeletal muscle acetylcholine-gated channel clustering, part_of GO:0007528 neuromuscular junction development), a synapse-assembly process. These proteins do none of the work of receiving or transducing the ACh signal. They bind no ACh, gate no ion flux and couple to no G protein; their activities are RTK activation, coreceptor activity and receptor anchoring (AGRN, DOK7 and LRP4 reviews: transmembrane receptor protein tyrosine kinase activator activity GO:0030297, protein tyrosine kinase activator activity GO:0030296, coreceptor activity GO:0015026). Their knockout phenotypes show that they are necessary for building the synapse (PMID:10320756), not that they take part in the signalling step. Applying CLAUDE.md's substrate-vs-participant rule and comparator check: in GOA, AGRN and DOK7 carry only GO:0007528 and LRP4 carries GO:0071340/GO:0051124, while the receptor subunits (CHRNA1, CHRNB1) carry GO:0095500, GO:0007271 and GO:0007274. Curators have therefore kept the clustering machinery out of the signalling terms, and the mouse GO-CAM gomodel:64c3044100000089 "Skeletal muscle acetylcholine-gated channel clustering 1 (Mouse)" models Agrn/Lrp4/Musk/Dok7 as a separate clustering pathway. This set is recorded as a candidate separate module (NMJ AChR clustering: agrin -> LRP4 -> MuSK/DOK7 -> rapsyn), to be grounded in GO:0071340 and that GO-CAM. RGD's "Musk --+> NMJ nAChR" edge is read as receptor localisation, not activation. (5) Organophosphate/pralidoxime AChE toxicology (RGD PW:0001511-adjacent records drawing ALB, CYP2B6, PON1) and the autonomic/organ-level outcomes in the RGD diagram (heart rate, bronchoconstriction, adrenal medulla) are out of scope. Part 5: acetylcholine reception
Nicotinic and muscarinic acetylcholine receptorsSignaling Pathwayach_reception

Released ACh binds ionotropic nicotinic receptors, which open a cation pore within milliseconds, and metabotropic muscarinic receptors, which activate heterotrimeric G proteins. A given synapse may use either class or both.

Variant set: Receptor class by receptor class (ionotropic vs metabotropic) (One Or More)

Many cholinergic targets, e.g. autonomic ganglia and hippocampal neurons, express both classes, hence ONE_OR_MORE.

Nicotinic ACh receptor (ligand-gated cation channel) signallingSignaling Pathwaynicotinic_reception

Pentameric ACh-gated cation channels; two ACh sites in heteromers (at alpha(+)/non-alpha(-) interfaces) and five in homomers. Opening depolarises the postsynaptic membrane or, presynaptically, raises terminal calcium to promote transmitter release. Corresponds to PW:0000842.
