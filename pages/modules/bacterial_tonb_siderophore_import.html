Bacterial TonB-energized outer-membrane ferric-siderophore import

A conserved Gram-negative bacterial transport module mediating active import of ferric-siderophore complexes across the outer membrane into the periplasm. Because the outer membrane lacks an electrochemical proton gradient, outer-membrane TonB-dependent ferric-siderophore receptors (such as BfrD1) rely on the inner-membrane-anchored, periplasm-spanning energy transducer TonB (operating within the inner-membrane TonB-ExbB-ExbD complex) to transduce the cytoplasmic-membrane proton-motive force across the periplasmic space. Physical contact between the C-terminal domain of TonB and the N-terminal TonB box of the receptor plug domain drives conformational opening of the 22-stranded outer-membrane beta-barrel channel and translocation of the ferric-siderophore complex into the periplasm. Siderophore biosynthesis, extracellular siderophore secretion, and subsequent periplasmic-to-cytoplasmic ABC transport are outside this module boundary.

Establishes the conserved structural and mechanistic model of TonB-dependent outer-membrane transporters (22-stranded beta-barrel with an N-terminal luminal plug domain and TonB box) energized by the inner-membrane TonB-ExbB-ExbD proton-motive-force transducing complex.
TonB-dependent transporters (TBDTs) are bacterial outer membrane proteins that bind and transport ferric chelates, called siderophores, as well as vitamin B(12), nickel complexes, and carbohydrates. The transport process requires energy in the form of proton motive force and a complex of three inner membrane proteins, TonB-ExbB-ExbD, to transduce this energy to the outer membrane.
