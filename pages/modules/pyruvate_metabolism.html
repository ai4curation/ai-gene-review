Pyruvate fates — pyruvate dehydrogenase complex and pyruvate carboxylase

The two committed eukaryotic fates of pyruvate that feed the TCA cycle. (1) Oxidative decarboxylation by the mitochondrial pyruvate dehydrogenase complex (PDC) links glycolysis to the TCA cycle: pyruvate + CoA + NAD+ -> acetyl-CoA + CO2 + NADH. Pyruvate enters the matrix through the mitochondrial pyruvate carrier. The PDC is a giant mitochondrial-matrix multienzyme machine built on a 60-mer E2 (dihydrolipoyllysine-residue acetyltransferase) core, with the thiamine-diphosphate-dependent E1 decarboxylase (an alpha2-beta2 heterotetramer), the E3-binding structural subunit (protein X / E3BP) that anchors the dihydrolipoyl dehydrogenase E3, and E3 itself, which reoxidises the lipoyl arms and passes electrons to NAD+. The same subunit architecture is conserved from yeasts (PDA1, PDB1, LAT1, PDX1, LPD1) to animals (PDHA1, PDHB, DLAT, PDHX, DLD), and the E3 is shared with the 2-oxoglutarate and branched-chain 2-oxoacid dehydrogenases. PDC flux is switched off by phosphorylation of E1alpha by pyruvate dehydrogenase kinases and on by dephosphorylation by the phosphatases. (2) Carboxylation by the biotin-dependent pyruvate carboxylase (PC): pyruvate + HCO3- + ATP -> oxaloacetate, the principal anaplerotic reaction replenishing oxaloacetate and the committed first step of gluconeogenesis from pyruvate or lactate. PC is a mitochondrial-matrix enzyme in animals, where it is allosterically activated by acetyl-CoA, the PDC product, coordinating the two fates; in budding yeast the two PC isozymes are cytosolic, so the oxaloacetate they make must be imported into mitochondria to replenish the cycle. In humans, inherited defects cause severe lactic acidosis and neurological disease: PDHA1 (X-linked, the commonest), PDHB, DLAT and PDHX deficiencies cause pyruvate dehydrogenase complex deficiency (Leigh syndrome spectrum), PC deficiency causes lactic acidosis with hypoglycemia, and DLD deficiency (the shared E3) gives a combined phenotype.

pyruvate metabolic processGO:0006090

The two committed mitochondrial fates of pyruvate, grounded to the human enzymes of the pyruvate dehydrogenase complex — PDHA1 (UniProtKB:P08559) and PDHB (P11177) of E1 (GO:0004739, EC 1.2.4.1), the E2 core DLAT (P10515, GO:0004742, EC 2.3.1.12), the E3-binding structural subunit PDHX (O00330, GO:0030674) and the shared E3 DLD (P09622, GO:0004148, EC 1.8.1.4), assembled as GO:0045254 — and pyruvate carboxylase PC (P11498, GO:0004736, EC 6.4.1.1). GO molecular-function/cellular-component terms were taken from the human GOA records; Reactome reaction ids and titles were verified against the local reactome cache. Each step uses a PANTHER family selector (generic over paralogs and orthologs) plus a concrete human representative member; the PDC is modelled as a PROTEIN_COMPLEX node with its five subunit types. PDHX is deliberately grounded as a non-catalytic adaptor (its E2-type catalytic His is replaced by Ser); DLD is the shared E3 of multiple 2-oxoacid dehydrogenase complexes. Upstream, pyruvate comes from glycolysis (via the mitochondrial pyruvate carrier); downstream, PDC-derived acetyl-CoA and PC-derived oxaloacetate feed the TCA cycle, and PC also feeds gluconeogenesis. PDC activity is phospho-regulated by the PDK kinases / PDP phosphatases (not modelled here). ALIGN (YeastPathways project, 2026-10): S. cerevisiae (PDA1 P16387, PDB1 P32473, LAT1 P12695, PDX1 P16451, LPD1 P09624) and S. pombe (pda1, pdb1, lat1, pdx1, dld1) exemplars were added to each PDC subunit role; all fall in the same PANTHER families as the human subunits (PTHR11516, PTHR11624, PTHR23151, PTHR22912), checked against UniProt PANTHER cross-references. The pyruvate carboxylase step became an EXACTLY_ONE compartment variant set: mitochondrial-matrix PC in animals versus the cytosolic PYC1/PYC2 isozymes of S. cerevisiae (PMID:2039506, PMID:1898090) and S. pombe pyr1 (cytoplasmic by similarity), so the module-level "mitochondrial matrix" context, which previously claimed both enzymes, was removed and the title broadened; the slug is unchanged. The PDC-to-PC positive-regulation connection is animal-specific. YeastCyc corrections: PDX1 is named in the PYRUVDEHYD-PWY comment but absent from its reaction gene lists, and was added. Out of scope here: the yeast pyruvate dehydrogenase bypass (pyruvate decarboxylase, acetaldehyde dehydrogenase, acetyl-CoA synthetase; PMID:9123965), the mitochondrial pyruvate carrier, and PDC kinases/phosphatases (yeast PKP1/PKP2 and PTC5/PTC6). GO-CAM disagreements: the YeastPathways PYRUVDEHYD-PWY conversion places all PDC activities in the cytosol and omits PDX1. The PomBase model 67086be200000251 types pdx1 as GO:0005198 structural molecule activity rather than the adaptor activity used here, models pkp1/ptc5 regulation, and includes upstream pyruvate kinase and pyruvate import, CoA import, citrate synthase, lipoylation, alanine transaminase and 2-isopropylmalate synthase activities that lie outside this module; it has no pyruvate carboxylase.

Acetyl-CoA produced by the PDC is the obligate allosteric activator of pyruvate carboxylase, coordinating the oxidative and anaplerotic fates of pyruvate (and, together, supplying acetyl-CoA + oxaloacetate for citrate synthesis / the TCA cycle). This direct coupling applies where PDC and PC share the mitochondrial matrix (animals); in yeasts PC is cytosolic and cannot sense matrix acetyl-CoA directly.
