Bacterial post-translational Sec protein export

A bacterial protein-export module in which an unfolded secretory precursor carrying an N-terminal signal peptide is delivered to the SecA ATPase, SecA drives the precursor through the SecYEG protein-conducting channel of the cytoplasmic membrane, an optional SecDF accessory (two proteins in most diderm bacteria, one fused protein in many Firmicutes) associated with YajC uses proton motive force to improve late translocation, and a type I signal peptidase removes the signal peptide on the trans side. Precursor delivery to SecA has two routes: a SecB-dependent route, in which the dedicated holdase SecB keeps precursors unfolded and hands them to SecA, and a SecB-independent route, in which SecA recognizes the signal sequence and preprotein directly, with generic chaperones such as trigger factor or DnaK keeping the chain translocation-competent. SecA, SecY, SecE and the type I signal peptidase are found throughout bacteria, whereas SecB occurs mainly in Proteobacteria, so bacteria such as Bacillus subtilis export precursors only by the SecB-independent route. Cotranslational SRP targeting, YidC-dependent membrane insertion, Tat export of folded proteins, lipoprotein maturation, and outer-membrane secretion are neighboring modules.

OpenScientist module-aware PSEPK protein-export pathway synthesis
Species-aware retrieval confirms the reusable five-part module against the KT2440 ppu03060 candidate set and separates the post-translational Sec route from SRP, YidC, Tat, and lipoprotein maturation/signal peptidase II.

Taxonomic scope. The SecA motor, the SecYEG channel (SecG is absent or replaced in some lineages) and type I signal peptidase are found throughout bacteria, so the module is written for Bacteria. Escherichia coli K-12 and Bacillus subtilis 168 are the two experimental exemplars, with Pseudomonas putida KT2440 kept as a further proteobacterial instance; their members carry completed gene reviews. Only the SecB-dependent delivery variant and the split SecD plus SecF architecture are lineage-restricted. SecB occurs mainly in Proteobacteria (PMID:25538690), and B. subtilis has none. Its precursors reach SecA by the SecB-independent route, which also serves SecB-independent substrates in E. coli. B. subtilis CsaA binds SecA and some precursors but matters for only a few substrates (PMID:10816431), so it is not modelled as a SecB equivalent. Many Firmicutes encode SecD and SecF as one fused SecDF protein (PMID:9694879); the fused and split forms are realizer variants of the same accessory activity. B. subtilis has five type I signal peptidases (SipS, SipT, SipU, SipV, SipW); SipS is the reviewed representative, and the ER-type SipW is not one, because it belongs to a different peptidase family. Modeling choices. SecDF-YajC is an optional accessory assembly rather than the ATPase motor; ATP hydrolysis belongs to SecA, and YajC is given no individual molecular function because its own contribution is unresolved. The SecB-independent delivery variant is represented by SecA's own preprotein and signal-sequence binding (GO:0070678), reviewed as core for E. coli SecA; generic chaperones (trigger factor, DnaK) that keep precursors unfolded are context outside the module. B. subtilis SecA is not listed in that variant because its gene review does not yet record a direct preprotein-binding assay; it is listed as a motor representative. PANTHER subfamily display labels PTHR30612:SF0 (CHLOROPLAST PROTEIN-TRANSPORTING ATPASE) and PTHR43390:SF1 (CHLOROPLAST PROCESSING PEPTIDASE) are dominated by plastid members although they contain the bacterial proteins, so the SecA and signal-peptidase annotons use the parent families PTHR30612 and PTHR43390. PTHR10906 members carry no subfamily assignment in the cached inventory, so SecY also uses the family id. Validator note. The signal-peptidase annoton keeps one intrinsic FAMILY_SCOPE_RESTRICTED warning: the PTHR43390 family review makes signal peptidase activity RESIDUE_DETERMINED (Ser-Lys catalytic dyad), so a family identifier alone cannot establish it for every member. The representatives (E. coli LepB, B. subtilis SipS, P. putida LepB) keep the dyad. Boundary. Tat export of folded proteins, YidC-dependent insertion, lipoprotein maturation by signal peptidase II, SRP cotranslational targeting and outer-membrane secretion systems are separate modules; their absence here is decomposition, not a pathway hole. Ontology check. The harmonized member-review actions rest on GO:0043952 protein transport by the Sec complex being an is_a descendant of GO:0015031 protein transport and GO:0008104 intracellular protein localization, but not of GO:0006886 intracellular protein transport, GO:0065002 intracellular protein transmembrane transport, GO:0009306 protein secretion or GO:0006605 protein targeting. This was checked against the GO release in the local OAK sqlite:obo:go database (is_a/part_of ancestor closure of GO:0043952: GO:0071806, GO:0055085, GO:0015031, GO:0008104, GO:0045184, GO:0033036, GO:0006810 and their roots), which is also why GO:0006612 and GO:0006616 are valid refinements of GO:0006605 and GO:0065002 of GO:0006886.
