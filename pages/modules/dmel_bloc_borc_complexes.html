BLOC and BORC complexes in Drosophila melanogaster

The biogenesis of lysosome-related organelles complexes BLOC-1, BLOC-2 and BLOC-3 are needed to form lysosome-related organelles such as melanosomes in mammals and pigment granules in Drosophila eyes. BLOC-1 is an eight-subunit complex of small coiled-coil proteins (including dysbindin, pallidin, muted and snapin) that cooperates with endosomal sorting to deliver cargo to these organelles. BLOC-2 contains the HPS3, HPS5 and HPS6 subunits in mammals; the Drosophila HPS5 homolog is pink (p). BLOC-3, a heterodimer of HPS1 and HPS4, is a guanine nucleotide exchange factor for Rab32 and Rab38 in mammals. BORC is a related complex that shares Blos1, Blos2 and Snapin with BLOC-1 and adds BORCS5, BORCS6, BORCS7, BORCS8 and KXD1 subunits; it recruits Arl8 to lysosomes and controls their positioning.
