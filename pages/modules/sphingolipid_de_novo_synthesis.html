De novo sphingolipid biosynthesis (ceramide core, with fungal inositol-phosphorylceramide head groups)

De novo sphingolipid synthesis builds ceramide, the hydrophobic backbone of all complex sphingolipids, from serine and fatty acyl-CoA on the endoplasmic-reticulum membrane. The committed, rate-limiting first step is catalysed by serine palmitoyltransferase (SPT), a pyridoxal-5'-phosphate-dependent membrane complex built on a heterodimer of two homologous subunits (SPTLC1/LCB1 and SPTLC2/LCB2). A small, lineage-specific activating subunit is also required: SPTSSA/B in animals, which sets acyl-CoA specificity, and the unrelated Tsc3 in fungi. SPT condenses L-serine with palmitoyl-CoA to 3-ketodihydrosphingosine. 3-ketodihydrosphingosine reductase (KDSR, yeast Tsc10) then reduces this, using NADPH, to dihydrosphingosine (sphinganine). Ceramide synthases of the TLC/Lag1 family N-acylate the sphingoid base with a fatty acyl-CoA. Each animal isoform prefers a different acyl chain length: CERS1 makes C18 ceramide (brain/muscle), CERS2 very-long-chain C22-C24 ceramide (broadly expressed), and CERS3 ultra-long-chain C26+ (acyl)ceramides for the skin barrier. Fungal Lag1 and Lac1 act with the accessory subunit Lip1 and use C26-CoA. The sphingoid base is then modified at C4 by one of two related di-iron enzymes. The dihydroceramide desaturase DEGS1 introduces the 4,5-trans double bond, an O2- and iron-dependent oxidation that converts dihydroceramide to ceramide; this is the predominant route in animals. The sphingoid-base C4-hydroxylase (yeast Sur2) instead converts sphinganine or dihydroceramide to the phyto (4-hydroxy) form, which is the predominant route in fungi and plants. Fungal ceramides are also hydroxylated at C2 of the very long acyl chain (Scs7). Fungi then build their characteristic complex sphingolipids in the Golgi. The Aur1-Kei1 inositol phosphorylceramide (IPC) synthase transfers inositol phosphate from phosphatidylinositol to ceramide. IPC is mannosylated by Csg1/Csh1-Csg2 complexes to MIPC, and in budding yeast Ipt1 adds a second inositol phosphate to make M(IP)2C. Animals instead make sphingomyelin and glycosphingolipids, which are covered by separate modules. Inherited defects of the human ceramide core include HSAN1 (SPTLC1/SPTLC2), spastic paraplegia (SPTSSA), erythrokeratodermia/thrombocytopenia (KDSR), progressive myoclonic epilepsy 8 (CERS1), congenital ichthyosis ARCI9 (CERS3) and hypomyelinating leukodystrophy 18 (DEGS1). The fungal IPC synthase is the target of the antifungal aureobasidin A.

The module was generalized from a human-only ceramide module to a eukaryotic ceramide core with a fungal head-group extension. The human groundings are kept unchanged: the SPT complex (SPTLC1 O15269 + SPTLC2 O15270, GO:0004758, EC 2.3.1.50; small subunit SPTSSA Q969W0; complex GO:0017059), KDSR (Q06136, GO:0047560, EC 1.1.1.102), the ceramide synthases CERS1 (P27544), CERS2 (Q96G23) and CERS3 (Q8IU89) (all GO:0050291, EC 2.3.1.24) and DEGS1 (O15121, GO:0042284, EC 1.14.19.17). Two steps were restructured into variant sets because fungi and animals use different protein architectures: the SPT small subunit (non-homologous ssSPT vs Tsc3) and the ceramide synthase (single-subunit CERS vs the Lag1/Lac1-Lip1 complex). The DEGS1 step became the C4-modification node with desaturation (DEGS family) and C4-hydroxylation (Sur2, sterol-desaturase superfamily) variants. Its id degs1_step was kept for continuity. PANTHER membership was checked in the PANTHER 19.0 classification files: LCB1/lcb1 and LCB2/lcb2 are in PTHR13693 (SF2/SF3) with SPTLC1/SPTLC2; TSC10 and SPCC1450.15 are in PTHR43550:SF3 with KDSR; LAG1, LAC1, lag1 and lac1 are in PTHR12560; dsd1 is in PTHR12879 with DEGS1; SUR2/sur2 are in PTHR11863; SCS7/scs7 share PTHR12863:SF1 with human FA2H; AUR1/aur1 and IPT1 are in PTHR31310 (SF11 and SF8); KEI1/kei1 are in PTHR28077; and SUR1, CSH1 and imt1-3 are in PTHR32385. TSC3 and CSG2 have no PANTHER classification. Out of scope: the long-chain-base kinase / phosphatase / lyase branch (LCB4/LCB5, LCB3/YSR3, DPL1) and the ceramidases (YPC1/YDC1), which belong to sphingoid-base turnover (see sphingosine_1_phosphate_rheostat); ORM/ORMDL feedback regulators of SPT; VLCFA elongation, which supplies the acyl-CoA; ceramide transport to the Golgi; and animal sphingomyelin and glycosphingolipid synthesis (sphingomyelin_cycle and glycosphingolipid_biosynthesis modules). Substrate palmitoyl-CoA comes from fatty-acid synthesis.

Part 4: sphingoid-base C4 modification (desaturation or hydroxylation)
sphingoid-base C4 modification (dihydroceramide to ceramide or phytoceramide)Reactiondegs1_step

Two related O2- and cytochrome-b5-dependent di-iron activities modify C4 of the sphinganine backbone. Delta-4 desaturation gives ceramide (sphingosine backbone). C4 hydroxylation gives phyto (4-hydroxysphinganine) sphingolipids. Desaturation acts on dihydroceramide. The C4-hydroxylase can act on free sphinganine before N-acylation, as in the S. cerevisiae route listed by YeastCyc, or on dihydroceramide. The node therefore follows CerS in the generic chain, and each variant records the substrate order.

Variant set: C4 modification route by chemistry / lineage (One Or More)

S. pombe encodes both activities (dsd1 desaturase and sur2 hydroxylase). Mammals mainly desaturate (DEGS1) but make phyto-ceramides in some tissues with the DEGS-family bifunctional DES2 (PMID:15063729). S. cerevisiae makes only phyto-sphingolipids.

Part 6: fungal inositol-phosphate head groups (IPC, MIPC, M(IP)2C) (optional)
Fungal inositol-phosphorylceramide head-group synthesisMetabolic Pathwayfungal_ipc_head_group_extension

Fungi, and also plants and some protists, transfer inositol phosphate from phosphatidylinositol onto ceramide instead of making sphingomyelin. The Golgi IPC synthase is essential in fungi and is the target of aureobasidin A. IPC is then mannosylated to MIPC, and in S. cerevisiae a second inositol phosphate is added to form M(IP)2C. This extension is optional at the module level because animals do not use it; animal ceramide-to-sphingomyelin and glycosphingolipid synthesis are covered by separate modules.
