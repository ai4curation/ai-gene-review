Human RNA polymerase III transcription — type 1 (TFIIIA-TFIIIC), type 2 (TFIIIC) and type 3 (SNAPc) promoter recognition, BRF1- or BRF2-TFIIIB recruitment, the 17-subunit Pol III with RPC7 paralogue choice, intrinsic termination, and MAF1 repression

RNA polymerase III (Pol III) transcribes short, abundant non-coding RNAs: all tRNAs, 5S rRNA, U6 snRNA, 7SK RNA, the RNA components of RNase P and RNase MRP, 7SL RNA, vault and Y RNAs. It is recruited by the TFIIIB complex, which in turn is placed upstream of the start site by promoter-specific assembly factors. Three promoter types differ in how this happens. Type 1 promoters (5S rRNA genes) have an internal control region bound by the zinc-finger factor TFIIIA, which recruits the six-subunit TFIIIC. Type 2 promoters (tRNA genes and others) have internal A and B boxes bound directly by TFIIIC. Type 3 promoters (U6, 7SK and other gene-external promoters, found in vertebrates) have an upstream proximal sequence element bound by the five-subunit snRNA-activating protein complex SNAPc and a TATA box. TFIIIB consists of the TATA-box binding protein TBP, the SANT-domain protein BDP1 and a TFIIB-related factor, BRF1 at type 1 and type 2 promoters or BRF2 at type 3 promoters. Pol III has 17 subunits: the catalytic RPC1 (POLR3A) and RPC2 (POLR3B), the RPAC1 and RPAC2 subunits shared with Pol I (POLR1C, POLR1D), the five RPABC subunits shared with Pol I and Pol II (POLR2E, POLR2F, POLR2H, POLR2K, POLR2L), the TFIIE-like heterotrimer RPC3-RPC6-RPC7 (POLR3C, POLR3F, and POLR3G or its paralogue POLR3GL), the TFIIF-like heterodimer RPC4-RPC5 (POLR3D, POLR3E), the RPC8-RPC9 stalk (POLR3H, CRCP) and the TFIIS-like RPC10 (POLR3K). Pol III terminates without accessory factors at a run of thymidines on the non-template strand and re-initiates efficiently on the same gene. Under nutrient limitation or stress, the dephosphorylated repressor MAF1 binds Pol III and prevents its recruitment by TFIIIB.

The three types use different assembly factors (TFIIIA then TFIIIC; TFIIIC alone; SNAPc) to reach the same intermediate, TFIIIB bound upstream of the start site, so they are three variants of part 1 (selection ONE_OR_MORE: each gene uses one type and every cell uses all three). TFIIIC is therefore written twice, as separate annotons in the type 1 and type 2 variants with the same six subunits, because annoton ids must be unique and a variant cannot reference a sibling. (2) TFIIIB as a REALIZER variant set. Both forms carry the same activity; BRF1-TFIIIB serves type 1 and type 2 promoters and BRF2-TFIIIB serves type 3 promoters. The pairing of a promoter type with a TFIIIB form is stated in the labels and descriptions; it is not encoded as a constraint because the variant sets are independent. TBP and BDP1 are listed in both forms. The RPC7 position is a FAMILY unit with POLR3G and POLR3GL as representative members, which records the paralogue choice without writing the 17-subunit enzyme out twice. No PANTHER id is asserted for it (the validator's informational notice about this is expected). Units without a function are those whose experimental GOA rows are limited to chromatin binding (POLR3D, POLR3G), zinc binding (POLR3K, POLR2L) or a complex-level polymerase activity assigned to a small subunit (POLR3H, CRCP). (4) Shared subunits. The five RPABC subunits (POLR2E, POLR2F, POLR2H, POLR2K, POLR2L) and the two RPAC subunits (POLR1C, POLR1D, the latter as P0DPB6) are listed in both this module and modules/rna_polymerase_i_transcription.yaml with the same accessions and roles. SUB1 (PC4), which RGD draws with TFIIIB and TFIIIC as a reinitiation and termination factor, is excluded: its experimental functions in GOA are DNA binding and RNA polymerase II coactivator activity, and its Pol III role is stimulatory rather than a step the basal system cannot perform. (6) MAF1 is an optional regulatory part connected to the enzyme by NEGATIVELY_REGULATES; its upstream control by mTORC1 (PW:0000180) is named, not modelled. (10) Substrate versus participant: the DNA template and the RNA products are not participants. GO-CAM links.
