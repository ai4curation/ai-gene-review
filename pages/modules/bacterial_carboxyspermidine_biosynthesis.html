Bacterial carboxyspermidine route to spermidine

A reusable bacterial route in which carboxyspermidine dehydrogenase condenses putrescine with L-aspartate 4-semialdehyde using NADPH to form carboxyspermidine, followed by decarboxylation of carboxyspermidine to spermidine. It replaces the classical S-adenosylmethionine-dependent route, in which S-adenosylmethionine decarboxylase and spermidine synthase aminopropylate putrescine, and draws its aminopropyl unit from aspartate metabolism instead of from methionine. The two enzymes are historically named for their norspermidine substrates (CANSDH/CANSDC, NspC) because that activity was described first; the same proteins carry out both reactions, and which polyamine is made depends on whether putrescine or 1,3-diaminopropane is supplied. Diamine and aspartate-semialdehyde supply are pathway inputs rather than parts of this two-reaction module.

Boundary and scope. The module is the two-reaction core: condensation of a diamine with L-aspartate 4-semialdehyde, then decarboxylation of the carboxypolyamine. Putrescine and aspartate-semialdehyde production are substrate supply and stay outside, as does polyamine transport. The scope is CONCRETE rather than ABSTRACT because both leaves ground to named proteins, but the participants are FAMILY selectors and the taxon context is Bacteria, so the module is species-general; the exemplars orient the reader and are not a membership claim. Generalization (2026-11-02). The module previously used Pseudomonas putida KT2440 exemplars alone, where the pathway is inferred from family membership and nspC adjacency and has never been assayed. Both leaves now lead with the Vibrio cholerae pair, which is where the route was characterized genetically: deletion of either gene abolishes spermidine and norspermidine, deletion of the decarboxylase accumulates the carboxypolyamine intermediate, and overexpression of the dehydrogenase redirects flux from putrescine to spermidine. The route is not Vibrio-specific: the decarboxylase step is established by deletion and complementation in Campylobacter jejuni (Epsilonproteobacteria) and Bacteroides thetaiotaomicron (Bacteroidetes), and Helicobacter pylori relies on it because its spermidine synthase is inactive. KT2440 is retained as one exemplar of the inferred case, marked as such. Enzyme naming. The GO label for GO:0102143 is "carboxynorspermidine dehydrogenase activity", but its definition covers the carboxyspermidine direction too: after writing out the carboxynorspermidine reaction it adds "Also converts carboxyspermidine to putrescine." (checked against the GO release used for validation and against QuickGO on 2026-11-02; the definition states the reaction in the NADP+ direction, so read in the reductive direction this is the putrescine-to-carboxyspermidine step this module uses). The term also carries both Rhea reactions, RHEA:34111 and RHEA:34115, and NCBIFAM:NF043000 maps to it as an exact match for EC 1.5.1.43. The decarboxylase has no GO term of its own: GO:0016831 carboxy-lyase activity is the most specific available, and both NspC gene reviews propose a specific EC 4.1.1.96 term. Grounding choices. The dehydrogenase leaf is grounded on PANTHER PTHR43796:SF2, verified against panther.obo and the member index, and both exemplars are classified at exactly that subfamily. The decarboxylase leaf cites PANTHER PTHR43727:SF1 but relies on NCBIfam TIGR01047 (nspC) and InterPro IPR005730 for the identification, because the enclosing PANTHER family PTHR43727 is heterogeneous: it is named for, and its only PAINT node describes, the LysA diaminopimelate decarboxylases of subfamily SF2, and that node over-propagates diaminopimelate decarboxylase activity into SF1. No ancestral_nodes are declared on either leaf: PTHR43796 has no PAINT node-level annotations at all, and the single PTHR43727 node asserts the LysA function, which this module's participant does not have. Related routes deliberately excluded. Fed 1,3-diaminopropane instead of putrescine, the same two enzymes make sym-norspermidine; that is the same module with a different input, not a separate pathway, and it is why the enzymes carry norspermidine names. The carboxyaminopropylagmatine (CAPA) route of Synechocystis is a different module: it starts from agmatine, passes through CAPA and aminopropylagmatine, and needs a third enzyme, an aminopropylagmatine ureohydrolase, to reach spermidine. Its two enzymes sit in the same PANTHER groupings as this module's, so the distinction is recorded in both family reviews rather than here.
