Cyclic nucleotide-gated channels — rod CNGA1/CNGB1, cone CNGA3/CNGB3 and olfactory CNGA2/CNGA4/CNGB1b heterotetramers gated by intracellular cGMP or cAMP and conducting Na+ and Ca2+ into the cell, with Ca2+/calmodulin feedback inhibition through CNGB1

Cyclic nucleotide-gated (CNG) channels are nonselective cation channels of the plasma membrane of sensory cilia that open when intracellular cGMP or cAMP binds to a cyclic nucleotide-binding domain on each subunit. Each channel is a heterotetramer of homologous six-transmembrane subunits of the voltage-gated channel superfamily, in which the pore-forming alpha subunits (CNGA1, CNGA2, CNGA3) are joined by modulatory subunits (CNGB1, CNGB3, CNGA4) that tune ligand sensitivity, ion permeation, calcium block and calmodulin regulation. Three native channel types are known. In rod photoreceptor outer segments, three CNGA1 and one CNGB1 subunits form the cGMP-gated channel that carries the dark current; in cone outer segments the channel has three CNGA3 and one CNGB3 subunits; and in the cilia of olfactory sensory neurons the channel has two CNGA2, one CNGA4 and one CNGB1b subunit, the short splice form of CNGB1 that lacks the glutamic acid-rich GARP domain, and is opened by cAMP made by the odorant receptor-G(olf)-adenylyl cyclase 3 cascade. Open CNG channels conduct an inward current of Na+ and Ca2+ that depolarizes the cell and raises ciliary Ca2+. The Ca2+ that enters binds calmodulin, which associates with CNGB1 and lowers the apparent affinity of the channel for its cyclic nucleotide; in olfactory neurons this rapid feedback inhibition underlies odorant adaptation. In photoreceptors the light-driven fall in cGMP closes the channels, and inherited loss of rod or cone channel subunits causes retinitis pigmentosa or achromatopsia; loss of CNGB1 also impairs smell.

RGD's diagram draws no CNG gene; the six subunits come from its curated gene list and the record's CNGs sub-page link. (1) MODULE CONCEPT. CNG channels are nonselective cation channels; in rods the dark current is carried mainly by Na+, with Ca2+ a minor but signalling-relevant fraction (larger in cones and olfactory cilia). A calcium-only concept (GO:0070588 calcium ion transmembrane transport, the descendant of the mapped GO:0006816) would misstate the cargo, so the concept is its parent GO:0098655 monoatomic cation transmembrane transport, which the four completed CNG gene reviews give as directly_involved_in. Na+ and Ca2+ are recorded as cargo on each channel annoton, grounded to ChEBI with labels checked in OLS (November 2026): sodium(1+) CHEBI:29101, calcium(2+) CHEBI:29108. The gating ligands are not transported, so they are not cargo; they are named in each function description as 3',5'-cyclic GMP(1-) CHEBI:57746 and 3',5'-cyclic AMP(1-) CHEBI:58165 (OLS-checked; the ionized forms used by Rhea). Outward K+ flux and Mg2+ block are not modelled. (2) STRUCTURE. Two parts. Part 1 is the conduction step, a ONE_OR_MORE REALIZER variant set on sensory cell type with three variants (rod, cone, olfactory), each with its cell type in variant context and one PROTEIN_COMPLEX annoton (GO:0017071) whose active_units give the subunits and stoichiometry (3 CNGA1 + 1 CNGB1; 3 CNGA3 + 1 CNGB3; 2 CNGA2 + 1 CNGA4 + 1 CNGB1b). Part 2 is Ca2+/calmodulin feedback on CNGB1; the conduction step CAUSES it (Ca2+ entry) and it NEGATIVELY_REGULATES the conduction step. (3) COMPLEX-SUBUNIT RULE AND MOLECULAR FUNCTIONS. Binding of intracellular cGMP (photoreceptors) or cAMP (olfactory neurons) to the cyclic nucleotide-binding domains opens the CNG pore, which admits Na+ and Ca2+ from the extracellular side into the cytosol. Each sensory cell type assembles its own heterotetramer from a pore-forming alpha subunit and one or two modulatory subunits.

Variant set: Rod, cone and olfactory CNG channels by sensory cell type [Realizer] (One Or More)

Each cell type expresses one channel type; the organism has all three. CNGB1 is shared between the rod channel (full-length isoform with the GARP domain) and the olfactory channel (short CNGB1b form).

Ca2+/calmodulin binds CNGB1 and lowers the cyclic nucleotide sensitivity of the channelRegulatory Stepcng_calmodulin_feedback

Ca2+ entering through the open channel binds calmodulin, which is associated with the CNGB1 subunit of the olfactory and rod channels. Ca2+/calmodulin lowers the apparent affinity of the channel for its cyclic nucleotide and so closes channels at a given cAMP or cGMP level, a negative feedback that underlies rapid odorant adaptation in olfactory neurons.
