piRNA-initiated transgenerational silencing in Caenorhabditis elegans

The Caenorhabditis elegans germline distinguishes foreign from endogenous transcripts with a small RNA surveillance system. Its silencing can last for many generations. The Piwi Argonaute PRG-1 binds a very large repertoire of 21U-RNAs (the worm piRNAs) and works in perinuclear P granules. There it recognizes germline transcripts through imperfect but extensive base pairing. The P granule protein DEPS-1 binds PRG-1 directly and is needed for the next step. PRG-1 does not silence its targets by cleavage. Instead, recognition triggers RNA-dependent RNA polymerase synthesis of secondary 22G-RNAs, which are loaded into worm-specific Argonautes (WAGOs). PRG-1 is needed to start silencing but not to maintain it. Silencing is maintained and inherited through two parallel loops. In the nuclear loop, the WAGO HRDE-1 carries 22G-RNAs into germ cell nuclei and directs H3K9 trimethylation and transcriptional repression of target loci. In the cytoplasmic loop, the helicase ZNFX-1 and the WAGO-4 Argonaute form Z granules, which separate from P granules during germline development. Z granules hold silenced, poly(UG)-tailed transcripts as templates for further 22G-RNA synthesis in the next generation. Once established, silencing can persist for more than 20 generations without the piRNA trigger. Loss of PRG-1 or HRDE-1 causes a progressive, multigenerational loss of fertility.

Part 3: nuclear heritable silencing
HRDE-1 nuclear RNAi and chromatin silencingBiological Processnuclear_hrde1_loop

The germline nuclear WAGO HRDE-1 (WAGO-9) binds 22G-RNAs, enters germ cell nuclei, and recruits the nuclear RNAi defective (NRDE) pathway. This directs H3K9me3 at target loci and represses their transcription. Its partners HPL-2, SET-25 and SET-32 are needed for multigenerational inheritance of RNAi and piRNA silencing. Without HRDE-1, silencing is lost and fertility declines over generations.

Part 4: Z granule transgenerational memory
ZNFX-1/WAGO-4 Z granule inheritanceBiological Processz_granule_loop

ZNFX-1 and WAGO-4 are in P granules of early germline blastomeres. Later they separate to form Z granules, which join P granules and Mutator foci in ordered PZM assemblages. The two proteins interact and are needed specifically for the inheritance phase of RNAi. ZNFX-1 binds silenced, poly(UG)-tailed transcripts and holds them in perinuclear condensates as templates for 22G-RNA amplification in the inheriting generation. This loop runs in parallel to the nuclear HRDE-1 loop.
