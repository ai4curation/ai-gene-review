Endoplasmic reticulum unfolded protein response in Caenorhabditis elegans

The endoplasmic reticulum unfolded protein response (UPR-ER) matches the folding capacity of the ER to the load of client proteins entering it. As in other animals, Caenorhabditis elegans senses ER stress through three ER-membrane transducers. The kinase-endoribonuclease IRE-1 removes a small intron from the xbp-1 mRNA, and the spliced mRNA encodes the bZIP transcription factor XBP-1 that induces most stress-inducible UPR genes, including the BiP chaperone gene hsp-4. The eIF2alpha kinase PEK-1 (PERK) phosphorylates eIF2alpha to attenuate general translation; reduced ternary complex favours translation of the uORF-containing atf-4 mRNA, and ATF-4 then activates cytoprotective genes. ATF-6 is a membrane-anchored bZIP factor that is needed mainly for genes expressed constitutively during development, and it also regulates ER calcium buffering. The arms are partly redundant: deletion of ire-1 or xbp-1 together with pek-1 or atf-6 causes larval arrest. The two ER HSP70 paralogs HSP-4 and HSP-3 are luminal chaperones and UPR targets, and in the conserved model BiP binding to the luminal domains of the transducers keeps them inactive until misfolded proteins compete it away. In C. elegans, XBP-1 made in neurons can also switch on the UPR-ER in distant tissues.

Part 2: PEK-1/eIF2alpha translational arm with ATF-4 output
PEK-1 eIF2alpha phosphorylation and ATF-4Signaling Pathwaypek1_arm

PEK-1 phosphorylates eIF2alpha on ER stress, attenuating global translation and reducing client load on the ER. It is required for about a quarter of inducible UPR genes but not for constitutive UPR genes. eIF2alpha phosphorylation favours translation of atf-4, whose two 5' UTR uORFs make it a translational-control reporter; ATF-4 then activates chaperone and cytoprotective genes.

Part 4: ER HSP70 (BiP) chaperones as transducer regulators and UPR output
HSP-4 and HSP-3 ER HSP70 chaperonesBiological Processbip_chaperones

C. elegans has two BiP paralogs. hsp-4 is strongly stress-inducible and is the standard UPR-ER reporter; hsp-3 is expressed at higher basal levels. In the conserved model BiP binds the luminal domains of IRE-1, PERK and ATF-6 and keeps them inactive until misfolded clients titrate it away; the induced chaperones then raise ER folding capacity.
