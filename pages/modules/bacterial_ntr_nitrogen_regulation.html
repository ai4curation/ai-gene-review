Bacterial Ntr nitrogen-regulatory phosphorelay and assimilation output

The bacterial nitrogen-regulatory (Ntr) system, which couples intracellular nitrogen status to the sigma-54-dependent nitrogen-scavenging regulon. A PII-family signal transduction protein integrates nitrogen and carbon/energy status through 2-oxoglutarate, ATP and ADP binding and through reversible uridylylation, and relays that status to the sensor histidine kinase NtrB by modulating its kinase/phosphatase balance. NtrB phosphorylates the conserved receiver aspartate of NtrC, and phosphorylated NtrC uses its AAA+ enhancer-binding module together with the sigma-54 holoenzyme to open -24/-12 promoters, inducing ammonium scavenging and ammonia assimilation. The module covers signal integration, His->Asp phosphotransfer, sigma-54-dependent activation, and the immediate assimilatory output.

The module is species-neutral; Pseudomonas putida KT2440 proteins provide the concrete implementation. The sensor kinase NtrB (NRII/GlnL) is modelled as an abstract phosphorelay-sensor-kinase role rather than a concrete exemplar, because no NtrB gene review exists in this repository yet; its partner relationship is grounded through the GlnK and NtrC reviews, which both describe it. Sigma-54 is included as the output partner of NtrC rather than as a member of the phosphorelay, since a single sigma-54 serves many unrelated enhancer-binding proteins and is shared with other modules. The wider NtrC-dependent regulon (urease, nitrate and further nitrogen-source catabolism) and the GS-GOGAT cycle beyond glutamine synthetase are outside this boundary. GlnD-mediated uridylylation is described as the modification that sets the GlnK state, but GlnD itself is not a module member.
