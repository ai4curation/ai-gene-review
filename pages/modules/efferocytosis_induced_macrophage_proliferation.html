Efferocytosis-induced macrophage proliferation

After a macrophage engulfs an apoptotic cell, it can divide. Two signals converge to make it do so. Lysosomal DNase II digests the engulfed cell's DNA in the phagolysosome, and the resulting oligonucleotides activate DNA-PKcs, then mTORC2 and AKT. Separately, GAS6 bound to the apoptotic cell activates the MERTK receptor tyrosine kinase and ERK1/2. Both routes raise MYC, which induces the transcription factor BHLHE40. BHLHE40 represses the anti-proliferative factor c-MAF, and the macrophage enters the cell cycle. The daughter cells keep clearing apoptotic cells, so the programme expands the pool of pro-resolving macrophages at sites of injury.

Boundary: this module starts with apoptotic-cell DNA in the phagolysosome and with GAS6-bound apoptotic cells at the macrophage surface, and ends with macrophage proliferation. Engulfment, phagosome maturation and the rest of cargo digestion belong to the phagocytosis modules (MODULE:phagocytic_engulfment, MODULE:phagosome_maturation). RUBCN and the V-ATPase are needed for this programme only because they are needed for phagolysosome formation, so they are not participants here. GO has no term for this programme. The closest is GO:0061517 macrophage proliferation, used as the module concept. The term "efferocytosis-induced macrophage proliferation" (is_a GO:0061517, preceded_by GO:0043277) has been proposed to the GO editors in go-ontology#32748. Taxon scope: the programme is shown in mouse and its proliferative phenotype in human monocyte-derived macrophages. DNase II and RICTOR are conserved across animals. An OpenScientist phylogenetic profile (file:human/PRKDC/PRKDC-hypotheses/prkdc-animal-distribution/openscientist.md) found DNA-PKcs (PTHR11139:SF68) ancestral to animals, present from sponges and cnidarians through most insects, and lost in Drosophila melanogaster and C. elegans, which keep the related PIKK kinases. All the components of the DNA-sensing route are therefore present in most animals, but the route has only been tested in mouse (see knowledge gaps).
