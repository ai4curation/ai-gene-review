Reductive tricarboxylic acid cycle

The reductive TCA cycle (reverse Krebs, Arnon-Buchanan cycle), which fixes CO2 by running the oxidative TCA cycle backwards. Three steps are not simply the reverse of their oxidative counterparts, and they are what define the cycle: citrate is cleaved by an ATP-dependent citrate lyase rather than made by citrate synthase; 2-oxoglutarate is formed by reductive carboxylation of succinyl-CoA by a ferredoxin-dependent 2-oxoglutarate synthase; and fumarate is reduced rather than succinate oxidized. A fourth ferredoxin-dependent carboxylation, pyruvate synthase, converts the acetyl-CoA released by citrate cleavage into pyruvate for biosynthesis. The cycle fixes two CO2 per turn and is markedly cheaper per carbon than the Calvin cycle, which is why it dominates in green sulfur bacteria and several Aquificae and epsilonproteobacteria; the price is that its ferredoxin-dependent carboxylases are oxygen-sensitive, confining it to anaerobic and microaerophilic niches. A sibling of MODULE:wood_ljungdahl_pathway, MODULE:hydroxypropionate_bicycle and MODULE:calvin_benson_bassham_cycle.

Grounded on Chlorobium limicola, a green sulfur bacterium in which the cycle was characterized; the ATP-citrate lyase and 2-oxoglutarate synthase entries are unreviewed (TrEMBL), which is stated here because no reviewed entries exist for them in this organism. The shared steps that the cycle runs simply in reverse - aconitase, malate dehydrogenase, fumarase, citrate/isocitrate interconversion - are real parts of the cycle but are not modelled as separate parts, because they are not diagnostic and are shared with the oxidative cycle; the module models the three reversal-defining steps plus pyruvate synthase. Some organisms use a citryl-CoA synthetase plus citryl-CoA lyase pair in place of ATP-citrate lyase, which is a realization difference at the citrate-cleavage step rather than a different cycle. 2-oxoglutarate synthase (GO:0047553) and pyruvate synthase (GO:0019164) are the GO terms for the ferredoxin-dependent carboxylations; both enzymes also run oxidatively in other organisms, so direction is a property of the metabolic context rather than of the protein. The reverse oxidative TCA cycle is modelled elsewhere in this corpus if at all; this module is specifically the carbon-fixing direction.

The flavoprotein subunit carrying the succinate/fumarate half-reaction against a quinone pool, run reductively at this step. The two members deliberately span the family's two documented subfamilies rather than sitting on one side of it: A0A101JUB8 is in PTHR11632:SF53 (SUCCINATE DEHYDROGENASE FLAVOPROTEIN SUBUNIT) and P00363 in PTHR11632:SF82 (FUMARATE REDUCTASE FLAVOPROTEIN SUBUNIT, which interpro/panther/PTHR11632/PTHR11632-review.yaml marks diverged_from_family with "Physiologically reduce fumarate"). That spread is the point, and it is the principle this module already states for the ferredoxin carboxylases: direction is a property of the metabolic context, not of the protein. A0A101JUB8's UniProt entry cannot place it on either side of that split, which is why the preferred_term names family membership rather than a direction. Its RecName "Fumarate reductase flavoprotein subunit" and its EC are both ARBA-only ({ECO:0000256|ARBA:ARBA00014044} and ARBA00012792) on an unreviewed TrEMBL entry at PE 3, which CLAUDE.md classes as an unvetted prediction and not support; its gene name sdhA is submitter-supplied ({ECO:0000313|EMBL:KUL33152.1}), equally unvetted. So nothing on that entry establishes a physiological direction either way, and the EC anchor rests on P00363, which is reviewed. The entry's one curated line, "Belongs to the FAD-dependent oxidoreductase 2 family", is family membership, which is what the preferred_term states. EC 1.3.5.4, which once named the quinol:fumarate reductase separately, is a transferred entry merged into 1.3.5.1 (ExPASy: "Transferred entry: 1.3.5.1"), so a two-EC anchor would cite a class that no longer exists. No PANTHER term is asserted, and the reason is granularity rather than breadth. UniProt places A0A101JUB8 in PTHR11632:SF53 and P00363 in PTHR11632:SF82, so no subfamily contains both members. SF82 is unusable for a second reason: it sits outside the reviewed SUBFAMILY_ONLY scope for GO:0000104, so declaring it makes module_family_conformance raise FAMILY_CONFLICT at error level (module_family_conformance.py:131-139), not a warning. The shared base family PTHR11632 does contain both members, and declaring it yields FAMILY_SCOPE_RESTRICTED: "GO:0000104 is SUBFAMILY_ONLY; this family grounding does not establish the allowed subfamily scope" (:143-151). That complaint is about precision: the term is reviewed as valid for SF51/SF53/SF72 only, and a base-family id does not say which subfamily a member falls in. Since the two members fall in different subfamilies and only one is in the allowed set, no single id states the truth, so the descriptor keeps its free-text intent and its members and asserts no family id. NADH-dependent fumarate reductases (GO:0016156, EC 1.3.1.6) occupy this same position in other organisms. That is recorded as context rather than modelled, because both members here are quinone-coupled rather than NAD(P)-coupled - which the curated "FAD-dependent oxidoreductase 2 family" line supports as membership evidence, and which is a separate axis from the physiological direction the ARBA text cannot establish.

succinate dehydrogenase (quinone) activityGO:0008177 Reduction of fumarate to succinate with a quinol as electron donor, run in the reductive direction here. The term's name reads oxidatively, which is not a reason to refuse it: GO defines it by the reversible equation and carries "fumarate reductase (menaquinone)" as an exact synonym, and it xrefs the same EC 1.3.5.1 that P00363 carries on a reviewed entry. A name that reads oxidatively is not a semantic mismatch. This is not the cofactor-over-specificity error corrected elsewhere in this module set: that applies where a node spans both cofactors, as cam_malic_enzyme does, whereas the electron acceptor here is a quinone for both members and the quinone-specific term is the precise one. GO:0016156 would have been wrong, being NADH-specific (EC 1.3.1.6). One tension is worth recording rather than resolving silently. GO:0008177 is_a GO:0000104 succinate dehydrogenase activity, which PTHR11632-review.yaml scopes SUBFAMILY_ONLY to SF51/SF53/SF72 because propagating it from the family root would over-annotate the fumarate reductase branches. P00363 sits outside that set, so asserting GO:0008177 on a descriptor including it entails a term this repo keeps off fumarate reductases. That scope statement guards against family-level propagation of a directionally named parent; it is not a claim that a quinol:fumarate reductase cannot catalyse the succinate/quinone reaction, which it demonstrably does - hence GO:0008177's exact synonym "fumarate reductase (menaquinone)" and its shared EC. So the scope statement does not exclude a member that genuinely runs the reaction in reverse, and no GO change is called for: GO:0000104 is named oxidatively in the same way GO:0008177 is, so by the argument above its parentage is no more defective than the child's name.
