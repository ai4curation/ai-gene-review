Type-II (dissociated) fatty-acid synthesis, bacterial and mitochondrial

A reusable dissociated fatty-acid synthase (FAS II) pathway in which each reaction is catalysed by a separate enzyme acting on acyl chains carried by a free acyl carrier protein (ACP). Malonyl-CoA formed by acetyl-CoA carboxylase is loaded onto ACP, an acyl-ACP chain is initiated, and the chain is iteratively elongated through condensation, beta-keto reduction, dehydration and enoyl reduction. The same architecture operates in bacteria and plastids and, as a conserved, endosymbiont-derived system, in the mitochondrial matrix of eukaryotes, where it is independent of the cytosolic type I FAS and its best-defined product is octanoyl-ACP, the precursor of lipoic acid. Mitochondrial FAS II uses a homomeric multidomain acetyl-CoA carboxylase, a mitochondrial ACP and partly unrelated reductase and dehydratase families. The steps are conserved while the enzyme families are not: chain initiation can run through an acyl-CoA-primed KAS III or through a malonyl-ACP decarboxylase, and the enoyl reduction is carried out by at least four unrelated reductases (FabI, the flavoprotein FabK, FabV, the SDR-fold FabL and the mitochondrial MECR/ETR1-type enzymes), which differ in cofactor and in triclosan sensitivity. An optional FabA/FabB branch introduces and extends cis unsaturation in bacteria. Enzyme-family, cofactor and compartment variants are represented explicitly rather than collapsed into a type-I fatty-acid synthase.
