Human glycinergic transmission through strychnine-sensitive glycine receptors

Fast glycinergic inhibition, mainly in the spinal cord and brainstem. Glycine is loaded into synaptic vesicles by VGAT (SLC32A1), the same vesicular carrier used for GABA, and released by calcium-triggered exocytosis. Released glycine opens pentameric glycine receptors (GlyRs), which are glycine-gated chloride channels. Adult synaptic receptors contain alpha1 and beta subunits; the beta subunit binds gephyrin and anchors the receptor at the postsynaptic density. Homomeric alpha2 receptors predominate before the postnatal switch to alpha1beta. Two sodium- and chloride-coupled transporters clear glycine. Presynaptic GlyT2 (SLC6A5) recaptures glycine into the terminal and is needed to refill vesicles. Glial GlyT1 (SLC6A9) sets glycine concentrations at the synapse. Glycine's separate role as an NMDA-receptor co-agonist is outside this module, as are gephyrin scaffold assembly and glycine biosynthesis.

PTHR18945 family review
In PAINT, glycine-gated chloride channel activity and glycinergic synapse are asserted only at the glycine receptor node PTN001055255. The wider anion-channel node PTN001055382 also carries invertebrate glutamate-, histamine- and acetylcholine-gated chloride channels. Exact subunits are therefore named here.

GlyR stoichiometry is not asserted: the alpha1beta receptor has been reported both as 2alpha:3beta and as 3alpha:2beta (or 4alpha:1beta). The alpha2 homomer is a developmental alternative, not a required part of the adult synapse. Alpha3-containing receptors of the spinal dorsal horn are not yet modelled. GlyT2 is placed under clearance because it removes glycine from the cleft; its role in supplying glycine for vesicle refilling is recorded on the annoton. No GlyR subunit, VGAT, GlyT1 or GlyT2 has a gene review in this repository, so status is DRAFT. Module deep research has not been run.
