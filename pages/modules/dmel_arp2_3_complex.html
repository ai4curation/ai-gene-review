Arp2/3 complex in Drosophila melanogaster

The Arp2/3 complex is a seven-subunit actin nucleator that binds the side of an existing actin filament and starts a new filament at a 70 degree angle, producing branched actin networks. Two actin-related proteins, Arp2 and Arp3, form the template for the daughter filament, while the five ARPC subunits (ARPC1-ARPC5) hold them in place and contact the mother filament. The complex is intrinsically inactive and is switched on by nucleation-promoting factors such as SCAR/WAVE and WASp. In Drosophila, Arp2/3-driven branched actin is needed for lamellipodia, ring canal growth, myoblast fusion and many other processes. Drosophila has two ARPC3 paralogs, Arpc3A and Arpc3B, so two forms of the complex are possible.
