Photoperiodic (long-day) flowering via the CONSTANS-FLOWERING LOCUS T pathway (Arabidopsis)

The external-coincidence mechanism by which Arabidopsis measures day length and converts long days into flowering. In leaf phloem companion cells the morning-phased Dof repressors (CYCLING DOF FACTORs, represented by CDF1) bind the CONSTANS (CO) and FLOWERING LOCUS T (FT) promoters and repress them, partly by recruiting TOPLESS. In long-day afternoons the clock-phased FKF1 and GIGANTEA (GI) proteins coincide with light; blue-light activation of the FKF1 LOV domain promotes FKF1-GI complex formation on the CO promoter and SCF(FKF1)-dependent degradation of the CDFs, relieving CO repression. CO protein is unstable in the dark because the COP1-SPA E3 ubiquitin ligase targets it for proteasomal degradation; in the light, photoactivated CRY2 (blue) and phyA (far-red) inhibit COP1-SPA and FKF1 binds and stabilizes CO, so CO protein accumulates only when CO mRNA expression overlaps with light at the end of a long day. CO, in an NF-Y-like trimer with NF-YB/NF-YC, binds CORE (CCACA) elements and activates FT transcription in companion cells. FT protein (florigen) moves through the phloem to the shoot apical meristem, where 14-3-3 proteins bridge it to the phosphorylated bZIP transcription factor FD; this florigen activation complex activates floral integrator and floral meristem identity genes such as SOC1 and AP1, committing the meristem to flowering. The circadian oscillator that phases GI, FKF1, CDF and CO expression, phytochrome-mediated photomorphogenesis, FT transport machinery and the vernalization/autonomous pathways are outside this module.
