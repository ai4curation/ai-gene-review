D-erythroascorbate (dehydro-D-arabinono-1,4-lactone) biosynthesis

In fungi, D-erythroascorbate, the five-carbon analogue of L-ascorbate, is made from D-arabinose in two oxidations. The IUBMB and ChEBI name for it is dehydro-D-arabinono-1,4-lactone. First, a cytosolic aldo-keto-reductase-type D-arabinose 1-dehydrogenase oxidises D-arabinose to D-arabinono-1,4-lactone, using NAD+ or NADP+. Second, a membrane-bound, FAD-dependent D-arabinono-1,4-lactone oxidase in the mitochondria oxidises the lactone with O2, giving dehydro-D-arabinono-1,4-lactone and hydrogen peroxide. The terminal oxidase belongs to the aldonolactone oxidoreductase group of the vanillyl alcohol oxidase flavoprotein superfamily. Its homologs make L-ascorbate in animals (L-gulono-1,4-lactone oxidase) and plants (L-galactono-1,4-lactone dehydrogenase), and the yeast enzyme can also oxidise L-gulono- and L-galactono-1,4-lactone. Erythroascorbate is thought to act as an antioxidant: yeast cells lacking the oxidase are more sensitive to oxidative stress, although its importance under normal conditions is debated.

Context
Fungi

Boundary: from free D-arabinose to erythroascorbate. Recycling of oxidised erythroascorbate and its antioxidant reactions are outside the module. Homology and generalisation: PANTHER family PTHR43762 contains S. cerevisiae ALO1 and S. pombe alo1 (SF1, D-arabinono-1,4-lactone oxidase), rat and mouse Gulo (SF8) and Arabidopsis GLDH (SF1). The module therefore names the family, but only the fungal enzyme is a representative member, because the animal and plant homologs make L-ascorbate in different pathways. S. pombe alo1 (UniProtKB:Q9HDX8) has its own gene review; it is not listed as a representative because this group has no PomBase GO-CAM. The ARA1 and ARA2 dehydrogenases are both aldo-keto reductases (UniProt similarity), but PANTHER places them in different families. Naming: the literature calls the product D-erythroascorbic acid, while IUBMB and ChEBI (since 2008) call it dehydro-D-arabinono-1,4-lactone; the GO process term uses the latter.
