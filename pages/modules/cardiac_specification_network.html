Cardiac specification transcriptional network module

The evolutionarily conserved core transcriptional network that specifies cardiac progenitors in the mesoderm and launches their myocardial differentiation. An NK-2 class homeodomain factor (Drosophila Tinman, vertebrate NKX2-5) acts with a cardiogenic GATA zinc-finger factor (Pannier, GATA4) and T-box factors (Dorsocross, TBX5/TBX20) in a mutually cross-regulating set; these factors physically associate and synergistically activate cardiac enhancers. Together they activate the bHLH factor Hand and the MADS-box differentiation factor MEF2, which drives the cardiac muscle differentiation program. In the fly the network is deployed in dorsal mesoderm at the intersection of ectodermal Dpp and Wingless signals; in vertebrates its mammalian elaboration underlies first and second heart field development and chamber formation. No single factor is sufficient on its own: heart specification depends on their combination with signalling inputs.

Boundary: the module covers the cardiogenic transcription factor kernel (NK-2, GATA, T-box, Hand) and its MEF2 differentiation output. Inductive signals (BMP/Dpp, Wnt/Wingless, FGF/Heartless), the upstream mesoderm determinant (Twist), second-heart-field regulators (ISL1, TBX1, FOXH1), and chamber/conduction-system patterning (TBX2/3, IRX4, ID2) are context or separate modules. Tinman also specifies visceral mesoderm via bagpipe and some dorsal somatic muscle founders; those outputs are outside this module. In the mouse, Nkx2-5 loss does not block commitment to the cardiac lineage but disrupts looping and ventricular differentiation, so the NK-2 role is framed as heart development rather than strictly cell fate specification across taxa. Fly Dorsocross and Hand representatives are unreviewed UniProt entries, and GATA4, TBX5, TBX20, HAND2, MEF2C, pannier, Dorsocross, Hand and Mef2 do not yet have gene reviews in this repository.
