L-tryptophan biosynthesis

De novo biosynthesis of L-tryptophan from chorismate, the branch-point precursor of the aromatic amino acids, as found in bacteria, archaea, fungi and plants. Five ordered reactions convert chorismate to L-tryptophan, drawing in L-glutamine (amide nitrogen), PRPP (5-phospho-alpha-D-ribose 1-diphosphate) and L-serine, and releasing pyruvate, CO2 and glyceraldehyde 3-phosphate along the way. The reaction chain is invariant, but the enzymes that carry it out are fused in different combinations in different lineages. Anthranilate synthase is a glutamine amidotransferase built from a synthase component (TrpE) and a glutaminase component (TrpG); TrpG can be a separate protein, fused to anthranilate phosphoribosyltransferase (TrpGD of enteric bacteria), fused to indole-3-glycerol-phosphate synthase (budding yeast TRP3, TrpG-TrpC), or the first domain of a trifunctional TrpG-TrpC-TrpF protein (Neurospora trp-1, fission yeast trp1). Phosphoribosylanthranilate isomerase (TrpF) is a standalone enzyme in many bacteria and in budding yeast, but is fused to indole-3-glycerol-phosphate synthase in TrpCF proteins and in the fungal trifunctional protein. The terminal tryptophan synthase is an alpha-2-beta-2 complex of TrpA and TrpB in bacteria and plants and a single fused TrpA-TrpB chain in fungi; in both forms indole made at the TrpA (alpha) active site is channelled through an intramolecular tunnel to the PLP-dependent TrpB (beta) site, where it condenses with L-serine, so free indole is not released. The pathway is feedback-regulated by L-tryptophan at anthranilate synthase and, in many bacteria, also transcriptionally by attenuation and the TrpR repressor.

In the P. putida KT2440 ppu00400 batch, the narrow module is fully satisfied by trpE, PP_0420/trpG-like amidotransferase, trpD, trpF, trpC, trpA, and trpB. Upstream shikimate genes, phenylalanine/tyrosine branch enzymes, and quinate-catabolic genes are outside this module. ALIGN with YeastPathways (2026-10): the five YeastCyc TRPSYN-PWY-1 reactions map one-to-one onto the five parts and the YeastCyc catalysts (TRP2 + TRP3, TRP4, TRP1, TRP3, TRP5) are all correct; no enzymes are missing. Scope widened from "microbial" to bacteria, archaea, fungi and plants (the title no longer says microbial). Because the reaction chain is fixed but the fungal fusion pattern differs from bacteria, architecture variant sets were added: a fungal anthranilate synthase variant (TrpE = S. cerevisiae TRP2 / S. pombe trp3, plus the TrpG domain of S. cerevisiae TRP3 or S. pombe trp1); a PRAI variant set (monofunctional TrpF, including S. cerevisiae TRP1, versus the TrpF domain of TrpCF-type fusions, including E. coli TrpCF and S. pombe trp1); an IGPS variant set (standalone TrpC or TrpCF-type fusions in PTHR22854, versus the TRP3-type TrpG-TrpC protein in PTHR43418:SF4); and a tryptophan synthase variant set (TrpA2-TrpB2 complex versus single-chain TrpA-TrpB, S. cerevisiae TRP5 / S. pombe trp2, PTHR48077:SF3). Fusion choices are correlated across steps: budding yeast uses the fungal anthranilate synthase, monofunctional TrpF, TrpG-TrpC IGPS and fused tryptophan synthase variants; fission yeast uses the fungal anthranilate synthase, TrpCF-type PRAI, TrpCF-type IGPS and fused tryptophan synthase variants. Glutaminase (TrpG) annotons carry GO:0004049 as a contributing subunit, consistent with the S. cerevisiae TRP3 review (contributes_to GO:0004049, part of GO:0005950); the anthranilate-synthase connection to the next step was changed from PROVIDES_INPUT_FOR to PRECEDES for consistency with the other steps. Compartment - every activity is cytosolic in both GO-CAMs and in bacteria, but the plant pathway is plastid-localised, so no module-level compartment is asserted (the previous cytosol context was removed for that reason). GO-CAM disagreements - neither model has causal edges (activities are linked only by shared input/output molecules); the PomBase model (67369e7600004623) shows S. pombe trp3 alone enabling GO:0004049 and does not represent the TrpG glutaminase contribution of trp1 or an anthranilate synthase complex, whereas the SGD model attributes GO:0004049 to the TRP2-TRP3 complex (CPX-1850). Part 1: chorismate to anthranilate (anthranilate synthase)
Chorismate to anthranilate (anthranilate synthase, alternative architectures)Reactionanthranilate_synthase_node

Anthranilate synthase aminates chorismate using the amide nitrogen of L-glutamine, eliminating pyruvate. It is a glutamine amidotransferase whose synthase (TrpE) and glutaminase (TrpG) units are combined differently in different taxa.

Part 5: tryptophan synthase (TrpA + TrpB, channeled)
Indole-3-glycerol phosphate + L-serine to L-tryptophanReactiontryptophan_synthase_step

The TrpA (alpha) active site retro-aldol-cleaves indole-3-glycerol phosphate to indole and glyceraldehyde 3-phosphate; the indole is channeled about 25 angstroms through an intramolecular tunnel to the PLP-dependent TrpB (beta) active site, where it condenses with L-serine to give L-tryptophan. Free indole is not released under normal operation. The alpha and beta units are separate subunits of an alpha-2-beta-2 complex in bacteria and plants and domains of one chain in fungi.
