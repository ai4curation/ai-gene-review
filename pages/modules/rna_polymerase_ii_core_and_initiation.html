Human RNA polymerase II core enzyme and transcription initiation — TFIID and TFIIA promoter recognition, TFIIB, TFIIF-escorted recruitment of the 12-subunit Pol II, TFIIE-recruited TFIIH promoter opening and CDK7 CTD Ser5 phosphorylation

RNA polymerase II (Pol II) transcribes all protein-coding genes and many non-coding genes. The enzyme has 12 subunits: the two large catalytic subunits RPB1 (POLR2A), whose C-terminal domain (CTD) carries the heptapeptide repeats that coordinate the transcription cycle, and RPB2 (POLR2B); the alpha-like assembly heterodimer RPB3-RPB11 (POLR2C, POLR2J); the RPB4-RPB7 stalk (POLR2D, POLR2G); the TFIIS-like RPB9 (POLR2I); and the five RPABC subunits shared with Pol I and Pol III (POLR2E, POLR2F, POLR2H, POLR2K, POLR2L). Pol II cannot recognise promoters on its own. At the core promoter, the general transcription factors assemble with it into the preinitiation complex (PIC). TFIID, made of the TATA-box binding protein TBP and 13 TBP-associated factors (TAF1 to TAF13), binds the core promoter elements, and TBP bends the DNA at the TATA box; TFIIA stabilises the TBP-DNA complex. TFIIB binds TBP and the promoter asymmetrically, which sets the direction of assembly, and bridges TBP to the polymerase. Pol II enters bound to TFIIF (GTF2F1-GTF2F2). TFIIE then recruits TFIIH. The XPB translocase of the TFIIH core opens the promoter DNA to form the transcription bubble, and the CDK-activating kinase module (CDK7, cyclin H, MAT1) phosphorylates CTD Ser5, which helps the polymerase escape the promoter and recruits capping and pausing factors. Pol II then synthesises the first phosphodiester bonds and leaves the promoter.
