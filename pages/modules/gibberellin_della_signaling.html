Gibberellin perception and DELLA derepression (GID1-DELLA-SCF(SLY1) module)

Gibberellin (GA) signaling in vascular plants works by de-repression. In the absence of GA, nuclear DELLA proteins (GRAS-family transcriptional regulators without a DNA-binding domain) restrain growth in part by binding and inactivating growth-promoting transcription factors such as the bHLH PHYTOCHROME-INTERACTING FACTORS (PIF3, PIF4). Bioactive GA (mainly GA4 and GA1) binds the soluble receptor GID1, a non-catalytic member of the hormone-sensitive-lipase alpha/beta-hydrolase family, in a deep pocket that is then capped by the receptor's N-terminal lid. The lid creates a surface that captures the N-terminal DELLA domain (DELLA, VHYNP and LExLE motifs), forming a GA-GID1-DELLA complex in which the DELLA GRAS domain is also contacted by the receptor. The DELLA GRAS domain in this complex is bound with higher affinity by the F-box protein SLEEPY1 (SLY1; GID2 in rice), the substrate adaptor of an SCF E3 ubiquitin ligase (SLY1-ASK1/ASK2-CUL1-RBX1). SCF(SLY1) polyubiquitinates the DELLA, which is then degraded by the 26S proteasome, releasing PIFs and other DELLA-sequestered regulators to drive growth programs such as hypocotyl and stem elongation, seed germination and floral development. GA-GID1 binding also inhibits DELLA activity without proteolysis, apparently by occluding DELLA surfaces needed for partner binding. In Arabidopsis the receptor step is carried out redundantly by GID1A, GID1B and GID1C, and the DELLA step by five paralogs (GAI and RGA, which dominate vegetative growth, and RGL1, RGL2 and RGL3, which act mainly in germination and flower development). Gibberellin biosynthesis and catabolism, which set the GA level, and transcriptional feedback of DELLAs on GA metabolism and receptor genes, are outside this module.

Arabidopsis proteins are used as representative members; the GID1-DELLA-SLY1/GID2 module is conserved in vascular plants (rice has single GID1 and GID2 genes). Family descriptors for GID1 and DELLA carry no PANTHER family id: the GID1 receptors sit in three separate subfamilies of PTHR23024 (named ARYLACETAMIDE DEACETYLASE, a mostly catalytic esterase family) and the DELLA PANTHER family PTHR31636 has an uninformative name, so neither family id faithfully represents the role. PIF3 and PIF4 share PTHR46807. The SCF core subunits ASK1/ASK2, CUL1 and RBX1 are shared with many other SCF ligases and are described rather than modeled as separate annotons. RGL1, RGL2 and RGL3 are named in the description as context; GAI and RGA are the representative DELLAs. No production GO-CAM in gocams/index.tsv contains these Arabidopsis proteins (checked 2026-10-05). GO has no "gibberellin receptor activity" term; the receptor annoton uses gibberellin binding (GO:0010331), matching the GID1A/GID1B/GID1C reviews, which propose the missing term.

Part 1: gibberellin perception
GA binding by GID1 receptorsRegulatory Stepga_perception

Bioactive GA binds the soluble GID1 receptor; closure of the N-terminal lid over the hormone creates the DELLA-binding surface. Arabidopsis has three partially redundant receptors that differ in expression and affinity (GID1B has the highest GA4 affinity; GID1C binds RGL2 weakly).

Part 2: GA-GID1-DELLA complex formation
DELLA capture by GA-GID1Regulatory Stepdella_recognition

The DELLA domain of a DELLA protein binds the lid of GA-bound GID1 and also bridges to the GRAS domain, which contacts GID1 directly. Complex formation both primes the DELLA for SCF(SLY1) recognition and blocks DELLA interaction with transcription-factor partners (proteolysis-independent inhibition).

PIF-driven growth transcriptionRegulatory Steppif_growth_output
