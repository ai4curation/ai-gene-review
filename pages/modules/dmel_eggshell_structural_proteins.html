Eggshell structural proteins in Drosophila melanogaster

The Drosophila eggshell is secreted by the somatic follicle cells that surround the oocyte during late oogenesis. The vitelline membrane, the innermost layer, is laid down first from vitelline membrane proteins (Vm26Aa, Vm26Ab, Vm26Ac, Vm32E, Vm34Ca and Vml) that are cross-linked into an insoluble layer. The chorion is secreted afterwards and is built from chorion proteins encoded mainly by two clusters: an X-linked cluster at 7F (Cp36, Cp38 and Cp7Fa-c) and an autosomal cluster at 66D (Cp15, Cp16, Cp18 and Cp19). The chorion gene clusters are amplified in follicle cells by developmentally regulated DNA re-replication, which allows the large and rapid synthesis of chorion proteins.

Each protein is a separate annoton (complex false): the eggshell layers are cross-linked extracellular structures, not defined stoichiometric complexes. Vitelline membrane proteins are assigned to the vitelline envelope GO component. Several eggshell components outside the two FlyBase groups (for example the dec-1 proteins, and peroxidases and other enzymes that cross-link the layers) are not modelled. The structural-constituent functions of most chorion and vitelline membrane proteins rest on their localization and curator inference rather than gene-by-gene mutant analysis.
