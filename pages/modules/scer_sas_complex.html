SAS-I histone acetyltransferase complex (Sas2-Sas4-Sas5) in Saccharomyces cerevisiae

SAS-I is a budding-yeast histone acetyltransferase that acetylates histone H4 on lysine 16. The native complex is a trimer of about 125 kDa with one copy each of the MYST-family catalytic subunit Sas2, the central subunit Sas4 and the YEATS-domain subunit Sas5. Sas4 bridges Sas2 and Sas5; Sas2 is active only with Sas4, and Sas5 stimulates the activity. The complex acetylates free histones well and nucleosomes only weakly. It interacts with the histone deposition factors Asf1 and CAF-I, and acetylation of H4 lysine 16 appears in chromatin as soon as DNA is replicated, in a SAS-I-dependent manner, so the complex is thought to acetylate new histones at or just before their deposition. SAS-I provides about 60 percent of cellular H4K16 acetylation, mostly within open reading frames. The mark opposes deacetylation by Sir2 and binding of Sir3, so SAS-I limits the spreading of SIR heterochromatin from telomeres and the silent mating-type loci; without it SIR proteins spread into subtelomeric regions and silence the genes there.

Boundary. The module contains the three subunits of the native trimer: Sas2 (catalytic), Sas4 (central, bridging) and Sas5 (accessory). An early gel-filtration estimate of about 450 kDa (PMID:11731479) is superseded by the characterization of the native complex as a 125 kDa trimer composed solely of the three proteins (PMID:15659401). The histone chaperones Asf1 and CAF-I (its large subunit Cac1) bind SAS-I and link it to histone deposition, but are not subunits and belong with replication-coupled chromatin assembly; they are named in the role descriptions. Histones are substrates. Sir2, Sir3 and the Rpd3L deacetylase, which SAS-I opposes at heterochromatin boundaries, and Esa1 (NuA4), which acetylates H4K16 redundantly with SAS-I (PMID:34014972), are outside the module. Activity and sites. The catalytic annoton uses histone H4K16 acetyltransferase activity, the core function in the SAS2 review: the native trimer acetylates H4 lysine 16 exclusively in vitro and is required for bulk H4K16 acetylation in vivo, and an H4 K16R mutation phenocopies sas2 deletion. The recombinant complex also acetylated H3 lysine 14 in one study (PMID:12626510); the SAS2 review keeps that as a secondary function, and it is not modelled here. Sas4 contributes to the activity and bridges Sas2 and Sas5, and its annoton carries its own activity, protein-macromolecule adaptor activity, as in the SAS4 review. Sas5 contributes to the activity without a known activity of its own: an annoton function would assert that Sas5 enables H4K16 acetylation, so its step is left untyped and the contribution is recorded in the SAS5 review (contributes_to GO:0046972). The YEATS domain of Sas5 binds acylated H3 peptides weakly in vitro (PMID:35296687), but no role for that binding in SAS-I has been shown. Where SAS-I acts. The evidence places the activity mainly on histones that are not yet in chromatin, at or just before deposition. Sutton et al. found that neither the recombinant nor the native complex acetylated nucleosomal histones under conditions optimal for free histones, and that H3-H4 bound to Asf1 is not acetylated, which suggested acetylation of free histones before deposition by Asf1 or CAF-I (PMID:12626510). Shia et al. detected nucleosomal activity, but very weak compared with NuA4 (PMID:15659401). In vivo, H4K16 acetylation rises in chromatin immediately upon replication and this depends on Sas4; Boltengagen et al. leave open whether SAS-I is recruited to newly assembled chromatin through CAF-I or acetylates CAF-I/Asf1-bound histones shortly before incorporation (PMID:34014972). SAS-I is not recruited to promoters and does not travel with Pol II, and Sas2 has not been detected on chromatin by ChIP (PMID:21908408). SAS-I therefore has a documented activity on non-chromatin histones, and a chromatin location is not established; the located_in chromatin rows on the three subunits cite a paper without a localization assay and are kept only as non-core in the gene reviews. Ontology. The module concept is GO:0033255 SAS acetyltransferase complex. Unlike the HAT-B and Rtt109-Vps75 terms, it does not inherit part_of chromatin in the current release (go-plus 2026-07-26): its only parent is GO:0032777 piccolo histone acetyltransferase complex, which is_a protein-containing complex, and neither term has a logical definition that places it under GO:0000123 histone acetyltransferase complex. The piccolo parent is wrong on three counts: piccolo is defined as an H4/H2A acetyltransferase that prefers chromatin over free histones and contains a subset of NuA4 subunits, whereas SAS-I acetylates H4 (and H3 in vitro) but not H2A, prefers free histones, and shares no subunit with NuA4. As a result SAS-I is also missing from the histone acetyltransferase complex hierarchy. The reparenting is requested under proposed_ontology_changes. The natural parent, H4 histone acetyltransferase complex (GO:1902562), is logically defined and would be classified under GO:0000123 and so under chromatin, which the evidence above does not support; until the GO:0000123 part_of chromatin edge questioned in modules/scer_hat_b_complex.yaml is resolved, the request names GO:0031248 protein acetyltransferase complex, the parent proposed there for the HAT-B term. Gene reviews. SAS2 (reviewed earlier), SAS4 and SAS5 support the three annotons. The SAS4 and SAS5 reviews modify the subtelomeric heterochromatin formation rows to heterochromatin boundary formation, used as the annoton process here; the SAS2 review still accepts subtelomeric heterochromatin formation and silent mating-type cassette heterochromatin formation as core.

Non-catalytic subunit with coiled-coil regions and a C-terminal cullin-homology Sas4 domain. It binds both Sas2 and Sas5 and holds the trimer together, and Sas2 has no activity without it, so Sas4 also contributes to the H4K16 acetyltransferase activity of the complex. Sas4 depletion lowers bulk H4K16 acetylation to the sas2 null level and prevents H4K16 acetylation of newly replicated chromatin. Sas4 binds Asf1, and reaches the nucleus independently of Sas2 and Sas5.
