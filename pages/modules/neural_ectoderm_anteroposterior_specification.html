Neural ectoderm anteroposterior specification module

The developmental program that, during gastrulation, subdivides newly induced neural ectoderm along the anteroposterior axis into two parallel, lineage-committed brain progenitors: an OTX2-positive anterior neural ectoderm that generates the forebrain and midbrain, and a GBX2/HOX-positive posterior neural ectoderm that generates the hindbrain. The module begins where germ-layer specification ends - with SOX2-positive neural ectoderm established under combined BMP, TGF-beta and WNT inhibition - and is driven by a graded posteriorising input of FGF together with retinoic acid. That input is read out by a pair of mutually repressive homeodomain selectors, OTX2 and GBX2, whose shared border is the site at which the midbrain-hindbrain (isthmic) organizer forms; displacing the border displaces the organizer. The two territories are already committed rather than merely differently positioned: challenged with the opposite region's inducing signals they do not interconvert, and they carry diverging accessible-chromatin landscapes that foreshadow their regional identities. Downstream, the anterior territory resolves into FOXG1/SIX3 forebrain and EN1 midbrain progenitors, and the posterior territory into MAFB/HNF1B/HOXA3 rhombomere 5/6 hindbrain progenitors. Grounded in GO:0021999 (neural plate anterior/posterior regionalization). Upstream neural-versus-epidermal ectoderm allocation is supplied by modules/germ_layer_specification.yaml and modules/body_axis_specification.yaml; the signalling machinery is detailed in the fgfr_signaling, retinoic_acid_receptor_signaling, wnt_signaling, bmp_signaling and tgfb_smad_signaling modules; the cerebellar program that continues from the posterior territory's rhombomere 1 is in modules/cerebellum_development.yaml.

Scope and boundary decisions. (1) The module starts at neural ectoderm, not at the epiblast: allocation of ectoderm to neural versus border versus surface fate under the BMP gradient already lives in germ_layer_specification, and duplicating it here would restate the same BMP switch twice. What is genuinely absent from the repo, and what this module supplies, is everything between "neural ectoderm exists" and "the brain has regions". (2) WNT is deliberately excluded from the posteriorising input even though it is a posteriorising signal. WNT activation at this stage specifies TBXT/CDX2 neuromesodermal progenitors and spinal cord, a parallel route that produces a different kind of posterior character; posterior neural ectoderm is induced by FGF and retinoic acid under WNT inhibition, and in the embryo lies outside the domain of active WNT signalling. Modelling WNT as an input here would merge two distinct posterior programs. It re-enters legitimately one step later, as an inducer of midbrain identity within the anterior territory and of hindbrain progenitors within the posterior one. (3) The exemplars are curated human UniProt accessions used to orient conserved roles; the program itself is not human-specific. Mutually exclusive otx-positive and gbx-positive ectoderm has been observed in mouse, macaque, chicken and zebrafish, and in the hemichordate Saccoglossus kowalevskii, so the scope is set at Deuterostomia rather than Vertebrata. (4) Gene-review coverage is uneven and the derived QC panel will say so: OTX2, GBX2, SOX2, FGF8 and EN1 have reviews in this repo; ALDH1A2, HOXA1, FOXG1, SIX3, MAFB, HNF1B and HOXA3 do not yet. The UniProt groundings are exact regardless, so the leaves are grounded even where the gene review is missing.

Part 1: extracellular input that biases identity posteriorly

WNT is not modelled as an input to this part. In the E7.5 embryo the gbx-positive territory is largely devoid of WNT reporter activity, WNT activity instead marks the posterior-most region where TBXT/CDX2 neuromesodermal and spinal cord progenitors lie, and posterior neural ectoderm induction in vitro requires WNT inhibition. FGF plus retinoic acid and WNT therefore produce two different kinds of posterior character, and conflating them would merge the hindbrain and spinal cord routes.

Graded FGF and retinoic acid posteriorising inputRegulatory Stepposteriorising_signal_input

The extracellular input that biases neural ectoderm towards posterior identity. FGF and retinoic acid are combinatorially required: either alone is insufficient to induce GBX2 and repress OTX2. In the embryo both sources lie adjacent to the prospective posterior territory, and the territory itself is outside the domain of active WNT signalling.

Part 2: the mutually repressive selector pair that partitions the territory
Mutually repressive OTX2/GBX2 selector boundaryDevelopmental Processap_selector_boundary

The core of the module. Two homeodomain selectors partition the neural ectoderm into complementary, non-overlapping territories by repressing one another. The resulting sharp border is where the midbrain-hindbrain organizer assembles, and the border is instructive: shifting it relocates the organizer and correspondingly expands or reduces the brain regions on either side.

Committed regional progenitor outputDevelopmental Processcommitted_regional_output

The regional progenitors each territory produces. This part exists to make the commitment claim modelable: the outputs are determined by which selector territory a cell came from rather than by the regional signals it later encounters, so the two branches do not interconvert even when given the other branch's inducing conditions. WNT re-enters here legitimately, as an instructive signal within each territory rather than as an input to the anteroposterior split.
