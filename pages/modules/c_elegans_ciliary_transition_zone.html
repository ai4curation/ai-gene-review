Ciliary transition zone MKS and NPHP modules in Caenorhabditis elegans

The ciliary transition zone (TZ) is the proximal region of the cilium, just distal to the basal body, where Y-shaped links connect the axonemal microtubules to the ciliary membrane. It acts as a gate that controls which proteins enter and leave the cilium. In Caenorhabditis elegans sensory neurons the TZ is built by two genetically defined protein modules. The MKS module contains the three B9-domain proteins MKS-1, MKSR-1 and MKSR-2, the transmembrane proteins MKS-2/TMEM216 and MKS-3/TMEM67 (meckelin), and the C2-domain protein MKS-6/CC2D2A. The NPHP module contains the nephrocystins NPHP-1 and NPHP-4. MKS-5, the RPGRIP1L/RPGRIP1 ortholog, is the assembly hub: it is needed for all MKS-module proteins to reach the TZ and for normal distribution of the NPHP module, and it anchors two separate assembly branches, an NPHP branch and a CEP-290-dependent MKS branch. The two modules are partially redundant. Single mutants, and even mutants lacking several members of the same module, have nearly normal cilia, whereas combining an MKS-module mutation with an nphp-1 or nphp-4 mutation causes severe defects: the basal body and TZ fail to attach to the membrane, Y-links are lost, axonemes are malformed and non-ciliary membrane proteins leak into the cilium. Human orthologs of these genes are mutated in Meckel-Gruber syndrome, Joubert syndrome and nephronophthisis.

Boundary. This concrete C. elegans module covers the core MKS and NPHP TZ modules and their assembly hub MKS-5. The modules are defined genetically, by synthetic ciliogenesis defects between but not within modules, and by localisation hierarchy. Whether they form stable biochemical complexes in worms is not established. GO:0036038 (MKS complex) and GO:1990957 (NPHP complex) are used as concepts for the two parts, following the gene reviews. Redundancy. The genetic redundancy between the modules is the defining property of this system. It is recorded in the root description, the part descriptions and the evidence rather than as a connection, because the connection vocabulary has no relation for redundancy. MKS-5 is the exception to module assignment: mks-5 interacts genetically with members of both modules. Members not modelled. Further worm TZ proteins are assigned to the MKS module or to MKS-5/CEP-290-dependent assembly in PMID:26982032 and related work: CEP-290, TMEM-218, TMEM-231, JBTS-14/TMEM237, TMEM-107, TMEM-17, TCTN-1, TMEM-138 and CDKL-1. None has a gene review here, so they are named in text only. CEP-290 in particular is the core of the MKS assembly branch and needs the MKS-5 coiled-coil region to reach the TZ. NPHP-2/inversin is reviewed (genes/worm/nphp-2) but localises to the inversin compartment distal to the TZ, so it is outside this boundary. Gene names. MKSR-2 and MKSR-1 were earlier named TZA-1 (B9D2) and TZA-2 (B9D1), and MKS-1 was XBX-7 (PMID:18337471). Molecular functions. MFs follow the gene reviews' core functions. The mks-1 review lists only protein binding, and the mks-6 and nphp-1 reviews assert no MF, so those annotons carry a preferred_term only. Interfaces. IFT (MODULE:c_elegans_intraflagellar_transport) and the BBSome (MODULE:bbsome) act after or alongside the TZ: IFT rates are largely normal in TZ mutants, and IFT and BBS proteins are not needed to form the TZ or to dock it to the membrane (PMID:21422230). NPHP-1 and NPHP-4 do modulate ciliary access of IFT components (PMID:18316409).

Part 1: TZ assembly hub required for both modules
MKS-5 transition-zone assembly hubProtein Complexmks5_assembly_hub

MKS-5 localises to the TZ through its coiled-coil region, which is necessary and sufficient for TZ targeting. In mks-5 mutants every known MKS-module protein fails to reach the TZ and NPHP-1 and NPHP-4 are partly mislocalised. MKS-5 also recruits CEP-290, the core of the MKS assembly branch. mks-5 mutations interact genetically with mutations in both modules.

Part 2: MKS module
MKS moduleProtein Complexmks_module

The MKS module comprises the B9-domain trio MKS-1, MKSR-1 and MKSR-2, whose TZ localisation is largely interdependent, the transmembrane proteins MKS-2 and MKS-3, and the C2-domain protein MKS-6. Mutants lacking one or several MKS-module proteins, including the triple B9 mutant, have nearly normal cilia; combined with nphp-1 or nphp-4 mutations they cause severe ciliogenesis defects.

NPHP moduleProtein Complexnphp_module

NPHP-1 and NPHP-4 localise to the TZ. nphp-1;nphp-4 double mutants lack additive ciliary defects, but loss of either nephrocystin causes subtle axonemal ultrastructure defects and changes ciliary access of IFT and signalling proteins.
