Hypothalamic leptin-melanocortin signaling module

The central adipostat: adipocyte-derived leptin acts on the long leptin receptor isoform of hypothalamic arcuate neurons, whose JAK2-STAT3 output raises POMC and lowers AGRP expression; prohormone convertases cleave POMC in dense-core granules to release alpha-MSH, which activates MC4R on second-order neurons to suppress appetite, while AGRP from a reciprocal neuronal population antagonises the same receptor.

Scope: this module is the hypothalamic appetite-suppressing arm only. Leptin's peripheral actions (angiogenesis, T-cell responses, bone mass, beta-cell function) and POMC's non-hypothalamic products (ACTH-driven adrenal steroidogenesis, alpha-MSH acting at MC1R in skin) are deliberately outside the boundary; they are recorded in the member gene reviews instead. JAK2, STAT3, SOCS3 and PTP1B are the intracellular transduction and feedback machinery; the generic motif is already modelled in modules/jak_stat_signaling.yaml, so this module asserts the receptor's STAT output as a function rather than re-modelling the kinase cascade. PANTHER grounding: family ids and subfamily names were read from the committed PANTHER classifications (interpro/panther/*-entries.csv and the release-pinned human classification), never written from memory, and every family declared here contains its own representative member. Note that the two PANTHER sources disagree about LEPR: the committed PTHR23037 entries file places P48357 at PTHR23037:SF44 while the pinned release classification places it at PTHR23036:SF109. PTHR23037 is used here because the PAINT node cited above (PTN002745476, seeded by P48357) lives in that family; the discrepancy is recorded rather than silently resolved. PTN anchors are asserted only where a local PAINT row names the node together with the member accession; the two convertases carry no PTN anchor (the nodes named in the PCSK1 GOA WITH/FROM field are not in the local PAINT index, so they are not asserted here) and are grounded as UniProt gene products at the subfamily level named in the PANTHER classification, because dibasic-site prohormone specificity is not a property of the whole PTHR42884 family.

Leptin release as an adipostatic signalRegulatory Stepleptin_signal_generation

Part 2: hypothalamic leptin receptor activation
Leptin receptor activation and STAT outputRegulatory Stepleptin_receptor_activation

Leptin binding to the long receptor isoform assembles higher-order leptin:receptor complexes at the neuronal plasma membrane. Receptor-associated JAK2 is activated and phosphorylates STAT3, whose transcriptional programme raises POMC and represses AGRP in arcuate neurons. Only the long isoform signals; the short and soluble isoforms do not.

Part 3: POMC expression and prohormone maturation
POMC synthesis and convertase processing to alpha-MSHRegulatory Steppomc_processing

Leptin-driven STAT3 signalling raises POMC transcription in arcuate neurons. The POMC precursor enters dense-core secretory granules, where prohormone convertase 1/3 makes the first cleavages (releasing ACTH and beta-lipotropin) and convertase 2, with carboxypeptidase E and downstream amidation and acetylation, completes the conversion to alpha-MSH. The precursor is inert until cleaved, which is why convertase loss mimics melanocortin deficiency.

Part 4: melanocortin receptor agonism
alpha-MSH activation of MC4RRegulatory Stepmc4r_agonism

alpha-MSH released from arcuate POMC neurons activates MC4R on second-order neurons, notably in the paraventricular nucleus. The receptor couples to Gs, raising cAMP, and the resulting signal suppresses food intake and supports energy expenditure. This is the effector node of the module and the commonest monogenic obesity locus in humans.

Part 5: competitive antagonism of the effector receptor
AGRP antagonism of MC4RRegulatory Stepagrp_antagonism

AGRP, released from a reciprocally regulated arcuate neuronal population whose expression leptin suppresses, binds MC4R and opposes alpha-MSH, acting as an antagonist and inverse agonist. The appetite set-point is therefore the balance of two POMC- and AGRP-derived signals at one receptor, not the agonist alone.
