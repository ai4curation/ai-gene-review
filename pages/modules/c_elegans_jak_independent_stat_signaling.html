JAK-independent STAT signaling in Caenorhabditis elegans

Caenorhabditis elegans has no Janus kinase, no interferon system and no cytokine receptors that dock JAKs, yet both of its STAT-family transcription factors act in immune gene regulation. Each paralog is wired into a different upstream input and controls transcription in the opposite direction. STA-1 binds promoters in intestinal cells and acts mainly as a constitutive repressor of genes induced by Orsay virus. Its repressive function genetically requires the ACK-family non-receptor tyrosine kinase SID-3, and repression is relieved during infection. STA-2 acts in the epidermis. It is held at apical hemidesmosomes by the matrix receptor MUP-4, physically associates with the SLC6 transporter SNF-12, and is the point of convergence for a fungal-infection and wounding p38 MAPK cascade and for hemidesmosome or cuticle-collagen damage. STA-2 is required to induce antimicrobial peptide genes of the nlp-29 cluster. STA-2 lacks the conserved C-terminal phosphotyrosine motif that JAKs target in canonical STATs.

Boundary. This is a concrete C. elegans module. Its theme is STAT-family transcription factors regulated without JAK kinases. It has two paralog-specific pathway variants that coexist in the same animal (selection ONE_OR_MORE) but differ in tissue, upstream input and transcriptional polarity. Each variant has its own upstream-input part and STAT transcriptional-control part. The STA-2 variant also has an effector-output part. The STA-2 epidermal pathway is the better supported of the two. It has several independent primary papers, physical interactions (SNF-12, MUP-4) and many epistasis studies. The STA-1 antiviral pathway rests on one full primary paper (PMID:28874466) and a 2026 preprint (PMID:42427644). Its upstream link from SID-3 to STA-1 is genetic only. The STA-1 variant is kept because its two parts (a non-JAK tyrosine-kinase input and a promoter-bound STAT repressor) are each directly evidenced, and because the paralog contrast is the point of the module. The knowledge gaps record its weaker grounding. The developmental role of STA-1 in dauer repression and the STA-2/ELT-3 lysosome program at the molt (PMID:31735670) are outside this immune-signaling boundary. Relation to MODULE:jak_stat_signaling. That module is a taxon-neutral receptor-JAK-STAT trunk: cytokine-receptor engagement, JAK trans-activation, receptor phosphotyrosine docking sites, STAT recruitment and tyrosine phosphorylation by JAK, dimerization and import, then GAS/ISRE transcription. Of those parts, only the last one (STAT-dependent transcription) is retained here. C. elegans has no JAK, so there is no receptor-JAK docking step and no JAK phosphorylation of STATs. No cytokine-like ligand or receptor is known for either worm pathway. The upstream inputs are an ACK-family tyrosine kinase (SID-3) for STA-1, and for STA-2 a p38 MAPK cascade plus release from a hemidesmosome receptor (MUP-4) that sequesters it. STA-1 mainly represses transcription in the basal state. Canonical STATs activate transcription after stimulation. The GO terms GO:0007259 (cell surface receptor signaling pathway via JAK-STAT) and GO:0046426 (negative regulation of receptor signaling pathway via JAK-STAT) therefore do not apply. They are deliberately absent from this module (see the sta-2 and gei-17 reviews and IBA_REVIEW section 14). This module does not conform to the jak_stat_signaling template; it is a lineage-specific rewiring around a conserved STAT DNA-binding core. Other inputs and modulators. During larval molting, the astacin NAS-38 drives STA-2/SNF-12-dependent AMP expression through PMK-1 and a TGF-beta/SMA-3 branch (PMID:33259791). The collagen-damage route does not need DBL-1/SMA-6 (PMID:37759445). The SMAD branch is therefore recorded here as context, not as a separate modeled input. A D. coniospora enterotoxin can stop STA-2 from activating AMP genes, which is pathogen subversion of this pathway (PMID:34166401). Negative regulators. The canonical module's SOCS/PTP/PIAS layer has no demonstrated counterpart here. The worm PIAS GEI-17 is not shown by any cached primary paper to act on STA-1 or STA-2, so it is not modeled as a member. It is raised only as an open question (see knowledge_gaps). Family context. Both worm STATs are PANTHER PTHR11801 members. They appear among the seeds of PAINT node PTN000210448 (nucleus, regulation of transcription by RNA polymerase II) in interpro/panther/PTHR11801/PTHR11801-paint.tsv. Concrete GENE_PRODUCT selectors are used here instead of family descriptors because the module is species-scoped. Evolutionary context comes from two reviews. Liongue and Ward 2013 (PMID:24058787) date the assembly of the receptor-JAK-STAT pathway to Bilateria and note that the nematode STATs keep a slime-mould-like architecture. Wang and Levy 2012 (PMID:24058748) infer that the nematode STAT lacking the N-terminal domain reflects the ancestral domain structure. Wang and Levy also describe F58E6.1 (sta-2) as a nematode-specific duplicate of sta-1 that "can no longer be considered a bona fide member" of the STAT family and that did not bind a STAT motif in their assay. That caveat bears on the STA-2 variant's MF term. It does not change the module, because later epidermal work (PMID:21575913, PMID:37759445; reviewed in PMID:26716073) shows STA-2 is the transcriptional regulator the AMP inputs converge on. A cross-gene briefing on JAK-STAT taxon scope across the STAT, JAK and PIAS families and the C. elegans and Dictyostelium STAT modules is at https://claude.ai/artifact/4MLfbbbWWnLchDvspwdPuM.

Variant set: STAT paralog-specific JAK-independent pathways by STAT paralog, tissue and upstream input (One Or More)

The two variants coexist in the same animal and are not alternatives for one biological step. They are grouped because each uses a STAT transcription factor without a JAK. They differ in tissue (intestine vs epidermis), upstream input (SID-3 vs p38 MAPK and hemidesmosome release) and transcriptional polarity (repression vs activation).

STA-1 intestinal antiviral repression pathwaySignaling Pathwaysta1_antiviral

In uninfected animals STA-1 sits on chromatin near the transcription start sites of many genes. It keeps a set of Orsay virus infection-response genes repressed, and this repression genetically requires the tyrosine kinase SID-3. Infection relieves the repression and the antiviral program is induced. sta-1 and sid-3 mutants express these genes constitutively and are less permissive to the virus. The STA-1 pathway acts in parallel to antiviral RNAi.

Part 1: non-JAK tyrosine kinase input required for STA-1 repression
SID-3 tyrosine kinase input to STA-1Regulatory Stepsid3_input

SID-3 is an ACK-family non-receptor tyrosine kinase that is also needed for systemic RNAi. Its role here comes from an RNAi screen of 116 kinases for Orsay virus load, in which sid-3 was the only hit. sid-3 mutants derepress largely the same infection-response genes as sta-1 mutants, and those genes are enriched for STA-1 ChIP-seq binding. That places SID-3 genetically upstream of STA-1. Direct phosphorylation of STA-1 by SID-3 has not been shown.

STA-2 epidermal antimicrobial peptide pathwaySignaling Pathwaysta2_epidermal

Fungal infection by Drechmeria coniospora, needle wounding and damage to apical hemidesmosomes or specific cuticle collagens all induce antimicrobial peptide (AMP) genes in the epidermis (hyp7 syncytium). Two inputs converge on STA-2. The first is a GPCR (DCAR-1), G-protein, PKC-delta (TPA-1), TIR-1 and p38 MAPK (NSY-1/SEK-1/PMK-1) cascade that acts genetically upstream of STA-2 and of its vesicular partner SNF-12. The second is release of STA-2 from the hemidesmosome receptor MUP-4 when the cuticle-hemidesmosome link is disrupted, which bypasses the p38 cascade. STA-2 then drives AMP gene expression cell-autonomously.
