Bacterial type IV secretion apparatus

A species-neutral module for the envelope-spanning machine that transfers macromolecules from a donor bacterium directly into a recipient cell or into the extracellular space. A coupling protein at the inner membrane receives the substrate and feeds it into a channel formed by a conserved three-protein envelope core complex; a VirB4-family ATPase, joined in many lineages by a second VirB11-family ATPase, energizes translocation and the assembly of an extracellular pilus built from processed pilin subunits. In conjugative systems an outer-membrane adhesin stabilizes the donor-recipient pair. The cargo is excluded from this module: the same apparatus transfers a relaxase covalently bound to plasmid DNA in conjugative systems and unrelated protein effectors in others. Relaxosome assembly and DNA nicking, plasmid replication, events inside the recipient cell, and transcriptional regulation of the transfer region are also outside this module.

Reusable boundary: the module begins when the coupling protein engages a substrate at the cytoplasmic face of the inner membrane and ends when that substrate leaves the apparatus. Cargo is excluded for the same reason as in the sibling type II, type V, and type VI modules: the machine is defined by its architecture, and the cargo varies from a relaxase-DNA adduct to standalone protein effectors. A naming hazard deserves recording, because it is easy to conflate proteins across plasmids. The Tra nomenclature is not stable between transfer systems: on the F plasmid TraC is the VirB4-family ATPase and TraB is a VirB10-family core-complex component, whereas on pKM101 TraB is itself the VirB4 homologue. Conserved-role assignments here therefore follow the VirB and VirD4 families, with F proteins named as counterparts, rather than following gene symbols across plasmids. Two consequences for grounding. The ATPase annoton is grounded on VirB4 and VirB11, which carry ATP hydrolysis annotations, and not on F TraC, whose GOA record currently holds only a plasma-membrane location. Conversely F TraB carries an experimentally supported ATP hydrolysis annotation whose cited source concerns the pKM101 VirB4 homologue of the same gene symbol; this module places TraB in the core complex on architectural grounds and asserts no ATPase activity for that part, leaving the annotation itself for gene-level review rather than adjudicating it here.

Part 1: Receive the substrate at the cytoplasmic face and deliver it into the translocation channel
Type IV coupling proteinTransport Stepsubstrate_coupling_protein

The coupling protein is the entry point of the apparatus. It is anchored in the inner membrane, projects a hexameric cytoplasmic domain, and selects which substrate enters the channel. It is the component that makes the apparatus cargo-specific while the rest of the machine stays generic.

Part 5: Build the extracellular filament that contacts the recipient cell
Type IV secretion pilusProtein Complexconjugative_pilus

A filament of processed pilin subunits extends from the apparatus. Pilin is made as a precursor, matured with the help of a dedicated membrane chaperone in F-type systems, and polymerized in a VirB4-dependent manner. The filament retracts after contact is established.
