Bacterial choline uptake and glycine betaine biosynthesis

Choline uptake followed by oxidation to glycine betaine in bacteria. Characterized import implementations include secondary BCCT transporters and ABC importers. The oxidative segment uses separate choline and betaine-aldehyde dehydrogenases, with GMC BetA and type III GbsB as distinct first-step implementations, or a CodA oxidase that performs both steps using oxygen. Glycine betaine can be retained as a compatible solute or feed downstream metabolism, depending on the organism and conditions.

The boundary retains extracellular choline uptake and ends at glycine betaine. Direct betaine uptake, de novo glycine methylation, downstream demethylation, BetI regulation and choline-O-sulfate hydrolysis are outside it. Transporter alternatives are representative characterized implementations, not an exhaustive inventory of every bacterial importer. OpuC, Cbc and ProU architectures reported in comparative literature are not excluded biologically. Missing exact native uptake machinery remains an evidence gap. A Boolean route represents role coverage, not cross-species complex assembly or guaranteed native expression. A dual-substrate assay of a BetA preparation does not establish native BetB dispensability. CodA is represented by two explicit activities: GO:0033713 for choline oxidation and a proposed substrate-specific betaine-aldehyde:oxygen oxidoreductase activity for the second oxidation. Neither a family match nor the first activity alone establishes both. Capacity evaluation must require independently curated betaine-aldehyde:oxygen activity for the second CodA step; a generic aldehyde-oxidase GO annotation and family membership do not establish that substrate.
