JAK-independent STAT signaling in Dictyostelium discoideum

The social amoeba Dictyostelium discoideum has four STAT-family transcription factors (STATa to STATd) but no Janus kinase and no kinases of the tyrosine-kinase group. Its STATs are instead tyrosine phosphorylated by tyrosine kinase-like (TKL) kinases, and they dimerize, accumulate in the nucleus and regulate transcription during multicellular development and stress. The two best-studied paralogs are wired to different inputs. STATc is activated by the prestalk morphogen DIF-1 and by hyperosmotic and other stresses. Its activating Tyr922 is phosphorylated by the TKL kinases Pyk2 and Pyk3, which bind the STATc SH2 domain through autophosphorylated tyrosines. The kinase activity is largely constitutive, so the signal is set mainly by the tyrosine phosphatase PTP3: DIF-1 and stress induce serine phosphorylation of PTP3 (in part by the TKL kinase Phg2), which inhibits it and shifts STATc toward the phosphorylated state. Nuclear STATc activates stress-response genes and restricts graded expression of the prestalk gene ecmA. STATa is tyrosine phosphorylated on Tyr702 within seconds of extracellular cAMP binding the serpentine receptor cAR1, apparently without the G-beta subunit. The receptor-like TKL kinase DrkA phosphorylates STATa in vitro and is a candidate STATa kinase. STATa stays nuclear in the prestalk cells of the slug tip, where it represses the stalk gene ecmB and directly activates cudA, a transcription factor needed for culmination.

Boundary. This is a concrete D. discoideum module. Its theme is STAT transcription factors activated without JAK kinases. It has two paralog-specific pathway variants that coexist in the same organism (selection ONE_OR_MORE) but differ in input, cell type and kinase. Each variant has an upstream-input part and a STAT transcriptional-control part. The STATc variant is the best evidenced. Its kinases (Pyk2, Pyk3) are established by null mutants, a double mutant, in vitro phosphorylation of Tyr922 and co-immunoprecipitation, and its phosphatase arm (PTP3, Phg2) by substrate trapping, overexpression and phosphosite mapping. The STATa variant has a well-established receptor input (cAR1) and transcriptional output (ecmB repression, cudA activation). Its kinase is less certain: DrkA rests on overexpression and in vitro phosphorylation, with no loss-of-function test. STATb and STATd are not modeled as variants. STATb is constitutively nuclear, has an SH2 domain lacking the phosphotyrosine-binding arginine, keeps its behaviour when the predicted phosphotyrosine is mutated, and has no known input (PMID:14701681), so it has no input part to model. STATd (dstD, UniProtKB:Q86I20, unreviewed) has no functional characterization in the cached literature. Relation to MODULE:jak_stat_signaling. That module is a taxon-neutral receptor-JAK-STAT trunk: cytokine-receptor engagement, JAK trans-activation, receptor phosphotyrosine docking sites, STAT recruitment and tyrosine phosphorylation by JAK, dimerization and nuclear import, then GAS/ISRE transcription, with SOCS, phosphatase and PIAS feedback. Here the STAT end of that trunk is conserved: tyrosine phosphorylation of a C-terminal tyrosine, SH2-phosphotyrosine dimerization (shown in the STATa crystal structure), nuclear accumulation and binding to promoter elements (STATa binds a mammalian ISRE). The upstream end is not. There is no JAK and no receptor-docked kinase. For STATc the receptor for DIF-1 is unknown and stress is sensed through F-actin remodelling. The kinase is a TKL that the STAT SH2 domain binds directly through the kinase's own autophosphorylated tyrosines, not through a phosphorylated receptor tail. The kinase is largely constitutive, and the signal is gated by inhibiting a tyrosine phosphatase (PTP3) by serine phosphorylation. In the canonical module phosphatases only terminate signaling. Pyk3 does carry a pseudokinase domain N-terminal to its kinase domain that inhibits the kinase domain, as JAK JH2 does, which is convergent architecture, not JAK homology. For STATa the receptor is a G-protein-coupled cAMP receptor, and its signal to STATa does not need the G-beta subunit. This module therefore does not conform to the jak_stat_signaling template. It is a lineage-specific wiring around a conserved STAT core, as in the C. elegans module (MODULE:c_elegans_jak_independent_stat_signaling), but the two lineages differ: Dictyostelium STATs keep phosphotyrosine-SH2 activation and are activated by TKL kinases, whereas STA-2 in the worm has lost the phosphotyrosine motif. GO terms. The pathway term is GO:0097696 cell surface receptor signaling pathway via STAT, whose definition names D. discoideum and TKL kinases. GO:0007259 (via JAK-STAT) is deliberately absent; the statA and statC reviews modify the inherited IBA to GO:0097696. GO:0007260 tyrosine phosphorylation of STAT protein describes the Pyk2/Pyk3 and DrkA step exactly, but it is currently part_of GO:0007259. Asserting it here would imply JAK-STAT signaling by inference. The kinase annotons therefore carry GO:0097696 and give GO:0007260 only in text. The same part_of link is why the proposed only_in_taxon Metazoa constraint on GO:0007259 (TPV-003 in projects/TAXON_PATHWAY_VARIANCE.md) needs GO:0007260 re-parented under GO:0097696 first; Pyk2 phosphorylating STATc on Tyr922 without a JAK (PMID:22699506) is the counterexample. The case is written up for GO and PAINT curators in the public cross-gene briefing JAK-STAT Without JAK (https://claude.ai/artifact/4MLfbbbWWnLchDvspwdPuM). Relation to other Dictyostelium modules. The two variants coexist in the same organism and are not alternatives for one step. They are grouped because each uses a STAT without a JAK. They differ in input (DIF-1 and stress vs extracellular cAMP via cAR1), kinase (Pyk2/Pyk3 vs candidate DrkA), regulatory logic (phosphatase gating vs receptor-triggered phosphorylation) and principal cell type (pstO and stressed cells vs the pstA tip).

STATc DIF-1 and hyperosmotic stress pathwaySignaling Pathwaystatc_dif_stress

STATc is tyrosine phosphorylated on Tyr922 by the TKL kinases Pyk2 and Pyk3 and dephosphorylated by PTP3. The kinases are largely constitutive. DIF-1 and hyperosmotic stress inhibit PTP3 by serine phosphorylation (S448 and S747), so phosphorylated STATc builds up, dimerizes and accumulates in the nucleus by reduced CRM1-dependent export. Nuclear STATc activates stress genes such as rtoA and gapA, about 20% of the hyperosmotic transcriptional response, and represses ecmA in parts of the prestalk zone.
