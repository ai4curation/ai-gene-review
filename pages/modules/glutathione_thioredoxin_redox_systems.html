NADPH-driven glutathione/glutaredoxin and thioredoxin systems with peroxide and superoxide scavenging

Cells keep their thiols reduced and remove reactive oxygen species with two parallel NADPH-driven thiol-disulfide relays and a set of thiol-independent scavengers. In the glutathione/glutaredoxin system, the FAD enzyme glutathione reductase uses NADPH to reduce glutathione disulfide to glutathione, and glutathione in turn reduces dithiol glutaredoxins, which reduce protein disulfides and glutathione-protein mixed disulfides. In the thioredoxin system, thioredoxin reductase uses NADPH to reduce thioredoxin, which reduces protein disulfides, supplies electrons to biosynthetic reductases such as ribonucleotide reductase and PAPS reductase, and reduces the thiol peroxidases. Thioredoxin reductase is a low-molecular-weight flavoenzyme in fungi, plants and bacteria and a high-molecular-weight selenoprotein related to glutathione reductase in animals. Hydrogen peroxide and organic hydroperoxides are reduced by several families of thioredoxin-dependent thiol peroxidases (typical 2-Cys peroxiredoxins, glutathione-peroxidase-like peroxiredoxins, atypical 2-Cys and BCP-type peroxiredoxins). Superoxide dismutases convert superoxide to hydrogen peroxide (Cu/Zn enzyme in the cytosol and mitochondrial intermembrane space, Mn enzyme in the mitochondrial matrix), and haem catalases dismutate hydrogen peroxide to water and oxygen without thiol reductant. The two thiol systems overlap: in budding yeast at least one cytosolic thioredoxin or dithiol glutaredoxin is required for viability.

NEW (YeastPathways project, 2026-10), built from YeastCyc GLUT-REDOX2-PWY, PWY3O-592, THIOREDOX-PWY and DETOX1-PWY. Boundary: the two NADPH-driven thiol-disulfide relays (glutathione reductase -> glutathione -> dithiol glutaredoxin; thioredoxin reductase -> thioredoxin) plus the peroxide and superoxide scavengers that they or no thiol feed (thiol peroxidases, superoxide dismutases, catalases). Glutathione synthesis and turnover are in glutathione_synthesis_gamma_glutamyl_cycle, which also lists glutathione reductase; glutathione S-transferases (GTT1, GTT2) and vacuolar GS-X pumps (YCF1) are conjugation/transport and are left out; monothiol glutaredoxins (GRX3/4/5) act mainly in iron-sulfur cluster and iron-regulation pathways; thioredoxin-reduced biosynthetic enzymes (PAPS reductase MET16, ribonucleotide reductase) are consumers in other modules. YeastCyc corrections: the frames have no gene for glutaredoxin or thioredoxin reactions (GRX1/GRX2, TRX1/TRX2 added); GPX1, GPX2 and HYR1 are thioredoxin-dependent cysteine peroxiredoxins, not glutathione peroxidases (PMID:16251189, PMID:12437921); the "superoxide radicals degradation" frame does list SOD1 and SOD2 in addition to the catalases. The peroxiredoxins TSA1, TSA2, AHP1 and DOT5 are absent from the YeastCyc frames and were added. Mitochondria have their own thioredoxin system (TRR2 included under the low-Mr reductase; TRX3 is in PANTHER PTHR10438, not PTHR46115, so it is described here rather than listed under the cytosolic thioredoxin selector) and a 1-Cys peroxiredoxin (PRX1) that is not modelled. GO-CAM disagreements: all four SGD models place every activity in cytosol (SOD2 and TRR2 are mitochondrial, CTA1 peroxisomal), THIOREDOX-PWY is part of the obsolete GO:0006125, and GLUT-REDOX2-PWY types the GPx-like enzymes with GO:0004602 glutathione peroxidase activity. Thioredoxin reductase is an EXACTLY_ONE variant set (low-Mr PTHR48105 in fungi vs high-Mr selenoprotein PTHR42737:SF8 in animals). No PomBase GO-CAM is linked for this group, but S. pombe exemplars are included where the ortholog is clear.
