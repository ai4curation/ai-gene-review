Bacterial mannose-active phosphotransferase system

Mannose-active bacterial group translocation couples a cognate EIIA/EIIB histidyl phosphorelay to an EIIC/EIID membrane apparatus. Phospho-HPr supplies the relay, and phosphorylated EIIB donates phosphate to mannose entering the cytoplasm. EIIA and EIIB can be domains of a fused protein or separate chains. Substrate range and the contribution of alternative uptake systems vary across species; bacteriocin recognition and phage entry are distinct outputs of some membrane assemblies.

PMID:16339738
Comparative genomic analyses of the bacterial phosphotransferase system.
A PubMed-indexed review with comparative-genomic analyses: mannose-class EII includes A/B relay and C/D membrane components, with variable fusion organization and frequent strain-level variation. Its older unknown-biochemical-role description of IID does not override the later ManYZ structure. Phylogenetic membership or a partial cassette alone cannot establish matched mannose transport capacity.
PMID:18647176
Ins and outs of glucose transport systems in eubacteria.
The Streptococcus/Enterococcus/Lactococcus and Lactobacillus sections document broad glucose/mannose-class uptake, fused and split relay architectures, and alternate non-PTS uptake. ManO is not universal and was of unresolved function in the cited Lactobacillus context. The review supports taxon discovery, while exact substrates and strains require primary evidence.
PMID:2117666
Levanase operon of Bacillus subtilis includes a fructose-specific phosphotransferase system regulating the expression of the operon.
The abstract explicitly distinguishes Bacillus subtilis LevDEFG from mannose uptake despite homologous components. This is a source-positive negative control for mannose specificity, not a claim that all related families lack mannose activity. Full text was not acquired.
PMID:17284603
Common mechanisms of target cell recognition and immunity for class II bacteriocins.
Native Lactococcus lactis IL1403 ptnAB/C/D deletion reduces growth on glucose/mannose; the same paper explicitly names genome AE005176 and the three genes. Reintroduction and isolated-C/D experiments shown here primarily test bacteriocin receptor behavior, so receptor restoration is not relabeled as a complete sugar-transport reconstitution.
PMID:38047704
Lactococcus lactis mutants resistant to lactococcin A and garvicin Q reveal missense mutations in the sugar transport domain of the mannose phosphotransferase system.
Native IL1403 membrane-component variants have impaired mannose/glucose growth in Fig4, with cellobiose control and exceptions retaining sugar utilization. Bacteriocin resistance is separable from transport. This reinforces native C/D participation without claiming every variant abolishes transport or that all glucose uptake uses this system.
PMID:12902269
Characterization of Streptococcus mutans strains deficient in EIIAB Man of the sugar phosphotransferase system.
UA159 ManL contributes to PEP-dependent glucose/mannose uptake, while inducible alternative mannose transport explains the mutant growth result. Operon Fig1 identifies manL/M/N by flanking loci and the text gives manL length0.99kb. Genetic/regulatory phenotypes do not prove sole-route necessity or separate catalytic measurements for every membrane subunit.
PMID:17951382
Role of RelA of Streptococcus mutans in global control of gene expression.
This independent UA159 primary explicitly equates manL with SMU.1877 and manM with SMU.1878. It supports the nomenclature bridge rather than adding new transport measurements. manN is mapped through the adjacent third CDS in the original2003 operon figure and official genome cross-references.
PMID:24928869
Uptake and metabolism of N-acetylglucosamine and glucosamine by Streptococcus mutans.
Native UA159 assays extend the system to glucosamine/N-acetylglucosamine uptake; downstream ManO modulates amino-sugar uptake. This is broader cargo context, not justification to require ManO for the bacterial mannose module or to add downstream amino-sugar catabolism to the transport mechanism. Native UA159 PEP-dependent uptake assays also compare deletion of the complete manLMN operon, with almost no GlcNAc uptake; ManL-only complementation is partial and a stable full-operon complement was not obtained. Individual membrane-subunit chemistry remains a domain/operon inference, and divergent S. mutans lineages do not establish interchangeable interfaces.
PMID:9004509
Lactobacillus curvatus has a glucose transport system homologous to the mannose family of phosphoenolpyruvate-dependent phosphotransferase systems.
The assayed Lactobacillus curvatus strain is CECT904. The deposited U28163 fragment encodes separate complete IIA/IIB/IIC and only partial IID. Heterologous ManB complementation provides relay-function support, but does not establish arbitrary cross-species cassette compatibility. Original UniProt IDs are now inactive; no complete active quartet is asserted.
PMID:40135874
The Streptococcus pyogenes mannose phosphotransferase system (Man-PTS) influences antimicrobial activity and niche-specific nasopharyngeal infection.
Native Streptococcus pyogenes MGAS8232 experiments distinguish complete-manLMN from ManL-only complementation for mannose/galactose/GlcNAc utilization. The reported additional background mutations make the complementation comparison important. Antimicrobial production and host colonization are downstream context, not core transport roles. Exact accession bridge not established in this consultation, so no representative IDs proposed.

The module represents a conserved functional mechanism with explicit target and interface requirements. It does not assert that every PTHR33799 or PTHR32502 member performs mannose transport. All four roles are explicit; a fused IIAB protein may fill both relay roles and the EIIB input role, while IIA-only proteins cannot fill EIIB. E. coli K-12, L. lactis IL1403 and S. mutans UA159 provide nine individually reviewed representatives. The latter two use C/D subfamilies distinct from E. coli; aliases and strain identities are verified in the literature consultation. Native IL1403 evidence is genetic/physiological; UA159 uptake and alternative-pathway phenotypes are qualified. No universal whole-complex stoichiometry, arbitrary mixed-species capacity, or phage/bacteriocin function is inferred. Three review articles and primary studies supply step-level evidence; separately acquired full texts and immutable-cache limitations are recorded in the audit.
