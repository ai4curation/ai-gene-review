Canonical NLRP3 inflammasome assembly and pyroptotic output

Assembly of the canonical NLRP3 inflammasome and its executioner output. Inactive NLRP3 is a membrane-associated ADP-bound cage; NEK7 licenses its conversion into an active ATP-bound disc whose pyrin-domain filament nucleates ASC (PYCARD) filaments. ASC CARD filaments recruit caspase-1 and activate it by induced proximity; caspase-1 matures pro-IL-1beta and pro-IL-18 and cleaves gasdermin D, whose N-terminal fragment forms plasma-membrane pores that release the mature cytokines and drive pyroptosis.

Review (Cell Mol Immunol 2025) that triggered this module. It states the canonical sensor-ASC-caspase-1-GSDMD architecture modelled here; mechanistic claims in the parts below are grounded in the primary papers it cites.
The inflammasome is a cytoplasmic protein complex that contains an inflammasome sensor protein, the adaptor protein called apoptosis-associated speck-like protein containing a CARD (known as ASC or PYCARD), and the cysteine protease caspase-1

Scope: the canonical NLRP3 route only, sensor through executioner. Deliberately excluded: (1) Priming. Canonical NLRP3 activation is two-step, and the first step is transcriptional licensing by TLR/NF-kB signalling plus poising post-translational modifications. That is upstream regulation of this module's sensor, not a part of it, and the TLR modules already in modules/ are its natural home. (2) The many proximal activation triggers (K+ efflux via P2X7/TWIK2, Ca2+ flux, lysosomal rupture, mitochondrial damage, ER stress). These converge on the sensor from outside and would swamp the module without adding mechanism to the assembly chain. (3) Other inflammasomes. PMID:41062723 reviews NLRP1, NLRP6, NLRP7, NLRP9, NLRP10, NLRP12, NAIP-NLRC4, AIM2, Pyrin, IFI16, CARD8 and MxA, plus the non-canonical caspase-4/5/11 route. Several share parts 2-4 of this module verbatim (ASC filament, caspase-1, GSDMD) and differ only in the sensor, so they are better modelled later as sensor variants over a shared effector core than duplicated here. Two have mechanisms that do not fit this module's shape at all and should not be forced into it: NLRP1 and CARD8 are activated by *functional degradation* - after FIIND autoproteolysis the N-terminal fragment is degraded, releasing a UPA-CARD that oligomerizes - and NAIP-NLRC4 places ligand recognition on NAIP rather than on the NLR that builds the platform, with caspase-1 recruited with or without ASC. The non-canonical route is a separate sensing module whose only link to this one is the K+ efflux feedback recorded in connections; CASP4's own gene review already models it with GO:0160074/GO:0160075. Representation choices: NEK7 is modelled as a licensing annoton inside part 1 rather than as its own part, because it does not act in sequence - it converts the sensor between conformational states. The NLRP3 sensing activity itself has no GO term; the NLRP3 gene review proposes "inflammasome sensor activity" and this module therefore carries only the adaptor/scaffold role for NLRP3. GSDMD is grounded on the processed chain UniProtKB:P57764-PRO_0000437526, following the GO-CAM and the GSDMD gene review, since the pore is formed by the cleavage product and not the autoinhibited precursor.

Part 1: licensed sensor
NEK7-licensed NLRP3 sensor oligomerRegulatory Stepnlrp3_licensed_sensor

Inactive ADP-bound NLRP3 forms membrane-associated cages. Activating stimuli disperse the trans-Golgi network; NLRP3 binds phosphatidylinositol 4-phosphate on the dispersed vesicles and is assembled at the microtubule organizing center. NEK7, through its C-terminal lobe and independently of kinase activity, bridges adjacent NLRP3 subunits and breaks the inactive cage, yielding an ATP-bound disc with a central pyrin-domain filament.

Part 2: adaptor filament
ASC (PYCARD) filament assemblyRegulatory Stepasc_adaptor_filament

The NLRP3 pyrin-domain filament nucleates polymerization of ASC pyrin domains into long helical filaments. This clusters the ASC CARDs, which in turn nucleate caspase-1 CARD filaments. ASC is the bipartite adaptor that converts sensor oligomerization into protease activation, and the resulting macromolecular assembly is the ASC speck.

Part 4: pore-forming executioner
Gasdermin D pore formationRegulatory Stepgsdmd_pore_executioner

Cleavage releases the gasdermin D N-terminal fragment from C-terminal autoinhibition. GSDMD-NT binds inner-leaflet phosphoinositides and phosphatidylserine, oligomerizes and forms large transmembrane pores. The pore conduit is negatively charged and preferentially passes the mature cytokines over their acidic precursors; pore accumulation drives osmotic swelling and pyroptosis.
