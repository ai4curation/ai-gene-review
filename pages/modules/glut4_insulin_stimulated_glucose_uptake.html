Insulin-stimulated GLUT4 glucose uptake — AKT2 inhibition of the TBC1D4/TBC1D1 Rab GAPs, RAB10 or RAB8A/RAB13 activation, motor-driven GLUT4 storage vesicle movement, cortical actin remodelling, STX4-SNAP23-VAMP2 fusion and SLC2A4 glucose transport; AKT2/TBC1D4/TBC1D1/RAB10/DENND4C/RAB8A/RAB13/KIF5B/MYO5A/MYO1C/RAC1/PAK1/STX4/SNAP23/VAMP2/STXBP3/STXBP4/SLC2A4

In fat cells and in skeletal and cardiac muscle, insulin raises glucose uptake by moving the facilitative glucose transporter GLUT4 (SLC2A4) from intracellular stores to the cell surface. In the unstimulated cell most GLUT4 sits in small GLUT4 storage vesicles (GSVs, the insulin-responsive compartment) that are retained inside the cell. Insulin receptor signalling through IRS proteins and class I PI3K activates the kinase AKT2. AKT2 phosphorylates the Rab GTPase-activating proteins TBC1D4 (AS160) and its paralogue TBC1D1, which suppresses their GAP activity toward GSV Rabs. The GTP-bound Rab that results is RAB10 in adipocytes, loaded by the exchange factor DENND4C, and RAB8A and RAB13 in muscle cells. Active Rabs, together with kinesin-1 (KIF5B) on microtubules and the myosins MYO5A (itself an AKT2 substrate) and MYO1C on cortical actin, carry GSVs to the cell periphery. In muscle, insulin also activates RAC1, which through PAK1 and the ARP2/3 complex remodels the cortical actin meshwork beneath the plasma membrane, a cycle of actin branching and cofilin-mediated severing that is needed for GLUT4 to reach the surface. GSVs then fuse with the plasma membrane through a SNARE complex of the vesicle SNARE VAMP2 with the plasma membrane SNAREs syntaxin-4 (STX4) and SNAP23, regulated by the syntaxin-4-binding proteins Munc18c (STXBP3) and Synip (STXBP4); AKT2 phosphorylation of Synip releases it from syntaxin-4. GLUT4 inserted into the plasma membrane then transports D-glucose down its gradient into the cell. Defects at several of these steps (AKT2 mutations, truncating TBC1D4 variants, reduced syntaxin-4) cause insulin resistance.

Part 4: Muscle cortical actin remodelling (RAC1, PAK1, ARP2/3) (optional)
RAC1-PAK1-ARP2/3 cortical actin remodellingRegulatory Stepcortical_actin_remodelling

In muscle, insulin activates RAC1, which through PAK1 and the ARP2/3 actin-branching complex remodels the cortical actin meshwork beneath the plasma membrane. A dynamic cycle of ARP2/3-driven branching and cofilin (CFL1)-mediated severing — cofilin being dephosphorylated by slingshot (SSH1) and rephosphorylated by LIMK1 — is required for GLUT4 to reach the cell surface. Optional because this cortical-actin arm is prominent in muscle and dispensable for the core RabGAP-Rab-SNARE axis in adipocytes.

Part 5: GLUT4 storage-vesicle docking and fusion at the plasma membrane
STX4-SNAP23-VAMP2 SNARE fusion with STXBP3/STXBP4 regulationRegulatory Stepsnare_fusion

GSVs fuse with the plasma membrane through a SNARE complex of the vesicle SNARE VAMP2 with the plasma membrane t-SNAREs syntaxin-4 (STX4) and SNAP23. Two syntaxin-4-binding Sec1/Munc18 and tomosyn-family proteins regulate the step: Munc18c (STXBP3) and Synip (STXBP4).
