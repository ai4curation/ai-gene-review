Intestinal absorption of dietary vitamin A — PNLIP/PNLIPRP2/PLB1 retinyl ester hydrolysis, SCARB1/CD36 carotenoid uptake, BCO1 central cleavage, retinal reduction, CRBP2 binding, LRAT re-esterification, chylomicron export, ABCA1 retinol efflux and ISX feedback

Animals cannot make vitamin A and obtain it from the diet either as preformed retinyl esters of animal foods or as provitamin A carotenoids of plants, chiefly beta-carotene. Both forms are absorbed by the enterocytes of the proximal small intestine. Dietary retinyl esters must first be hydrolysed: in the lumen by pancreatic triglyceride lipase (PNLIP, with its cofactor colipase) and pancreatic lipase-related protein 2 (PNLIPRP2), and at the brush border by phospholipase B1 (PLB1). The free retinol released enters the enterocyte, largely by diffusion. Carotenoids are taken up across the brush border membrane with the help of the class B scavenger receptors SR-BI (SCARB1) and CD36. In the cytosol the carotenoid oxygenase BCO1 cleaves beta-carotene at its central double bond into two molecules of all-trans-retinal, which is reduced to all-trans-retinol by an NADPH-dependent retinal reductase whose identity in human enterocytes is not settled (AKR1B10 and RDH11 are candidates). Retinol from both sources is bound by cellular retinol-binding protein 2 (CRBP2, RBP2), an abundant intestine-specific carrier, and re-esterified with long-chain fatty acids, mainly by LRAT. The new retinyl esters, together with unconverted carotenoids, are packaged into chylomicrons and secreted into the lymph. Some free retinol leaves the cell across the basolateral membrane, partly by an ABCA1-dependent route, and reaches the portal blood. Carotenoid absorption and cleavage are under negative feedback: retinoic acid induces the intestine-specific homeobox transcription factor ISX, which represses transcription of the SCARB1 and BCO1 genes. Loss of SR-BI impairs beta-carotene absorption in mice, loss of CRBP2 reduces retinol uptake and hepatic vitamin A stores, and loss of ISX derepresses SCARB1 and BCO1.

Part 1: hydrolysis of dietary retinyl esters in the gut lumen and at the brush border
Luminal and brush-border retinyl ester hydrolysis (PNLIP, PNLIPRP2, PLB1)Reactionretinyl_ester_hydrolysis

Dietary retinyl esters, mainly retinyl palmitate, cannot be absorbed intact and are hydrolysed to all-trans-retinol and fatty acid before uptake. Several lipases share the work, depending on how the ester is presented (emulsion droplets, mixed micelles or vesicles) and on age: colipase-dependent pancreatic triglyceride lipase (PNLIP) is the main pancreatic retinyl ester hydrolase, pancreatic lipase-related protein 2 (PNLIPRP2) hydrolyses micelle-solubilised esters, and phospholipase B1 (PLB1) is an intrinsic brush-border enzyme. They are modelled as alternatives in one variant set. The released retinol is taken up largely by diffusion, which has no protein participant here.

PMID:21718801
More than one enzyme probably shares luminal retinyl ester hydrolysis, and a brush-border activity has been attributed to phospholipase B.
Thus, more than one enzyme may be responsible for the complete hydrolysis of retinyl esters in the intestinal lumen.

Part 2: carotenoid uptake across the brush border membrane
Class B scavenger receptor-facilitated carotenoid uptake (SCARB1, CD36)Transport Stepcarotenoid_uptake

Beta-carotene and other provitamin A carotenoids are taken up from mixed micelles across the apical brush border. Uptake is saturable and facilitated by the class B scavenger receptors SR-BI (SCARB1), which predominates in the duodenum and is needed for beta-carotene absorption in mice, and CD36. Neither receptor is specific for carotenoids: both also take up cholesterol, phospholipids and fatty acids, and GO has no molecular function term for this lipid-uptake activity, so the annotons carry the carotenoid transport process without a function term. Unconverted carotenoids pass on to chylomicrons; the fraction that is cleaved enters the BCO1 step.

Part 3: central cleavage of beta-carotene to retinal
BCO1 central cleavage of beta-carotene to all-trans-retinalReactionbeta_carotene_cleavage

The cytosolic non-heme iron oxygenase BCO1 cleaves all-trans-beta-carotene at the central 15,15' double bond to give two molecules of all-trans-retinal, the route by which provitamin A carotenoids become vitamin A. The mitochondrial eccentric-cleavage enzyme BCO2, which RGD mentions but does not draw, is not modelled. BCO1 transcription is repressed by ISX (part 8).

Part 4: reduction of retinal to retinol
NADPH-dependent reduction of all-trans-retinal to all-trans-retinol (enzyme unresolved)Reactionretinal_reduction

Retinal made by BCO1 is reduced to retinol, which can then be bound by CRBP2 and esterified. The enzyme that does this in human enterocytes has not been identified. Two candidates with experimental NADP(H)-dependent all-trans-retinal reductase activity are modelled as variants: the cytosolic aldo-keto reductase AKR1B10, the small intestine reductase that RGD draws, and the ER-membrane short-chain dehydrogenase/reductase RDH11, which Reactome proposes. Both GO terms are written in the oxidative direction; the reaction is reversible and here runs toward retinol.

Part 5: binding of cellular retinol and retinal by CRBP2
CRBP2 (RBP2) binding of enterocyte retinolTransport Stepcrbp2_retinoid_binding

Cellular retinol-binding protein 2 is one of the most abundant soluble proteins of jejunal enterocytes. It binds retinol from both the hydrolysis and the carotenoid routes (and retinal), keeps free retinoid low and presents retinol to the esterifying enzymes. Loss of CRBP2 in mice halves the saturable component of intestinal retinol uptake and lowers hepatic vitamin A stores.

Part 8: transcriptional feedback repression of carotenoid uptake and cleavage by ISX (optional)
ISX repression of SCARB1 and BCO1 transcriptionBiological Processisx_feedback

Regulatory part, not a step that handles a retinoid. Retinoic acid, acting through the retinoic acid receptors, induces the intestine-specific homeobox transcription factor ISX, and ISX represses transcription of the SCARB1 and BCO1 genes. When vitamin A is plentiful, carotenoid uptake and cleavage are therefore turned down; in vitamin A deficiency ISX falls and both are derepressed. ISX acts on the target genes, not on carotenoids or retinoids, so it is modelled as a regulator of the uptake and cleavage nodes (NEGATIVELY_REGULATES connections) and not as a participant in them. Optional because absorption runs without it; it sets the rate. The RA induction step belongs to the retinoic acid receptor signalling module and is not repeated here.

Part 9: basolateral efflux of free retinol (optional)
ABCA1-dependent basolateral efflux of free retinolTransport Stepretinol_efflux

Part of the absorbed retinol leaves the enterocyte unesterified, outside lipoproteins, and reaches the portal circulation; this route matters most when chylomicron secretion is low (fasting, or abetalipoproteinemia). In Caco-2 cells the saturable component of retinol efflux is reduced by the ABCA1 inhibitor glyburide and by ABCA1 knockdown. Optional because it is a secondary exit route, and because whether ABCA1 moves retinol itself or acts indirectly through its phospholipid floppase activity and nascent HDL formation is not known.
