Bacterial DNA-directed RNA polymerase core enzyme

The bacterial DNA-directed RNA polymerase core enzyme, the multisubunit machine that carries out DNA-templated RNA synthesis in all bacteria. The conserved core is built from an alpha dimer, the beta and beta-prime subunits that together form the active-centre cleft, and the small omega subunit. Alpha dimerization is the first assembly step and provides the platform on which beta and then beta-prime assemble; omega latches beta-prime and promotes the final assembly step. The beta/beta-prime cleft holds the DNA template, the RNA-DNA hybrid, the incoming nucleotide and the catalytic Mg(2+) ion. Sigma factors convert the core into promoter-specific holoenzymes and are outside this module, as are elongation and termination factors and lineage-specific accessory subunits.

Generalization. The alpha2-beta-beta-prime-omega core is conserved across bacteria, so the module is species-neutral and each role is grounded on a family with representative members from three phylogenetically distant, experimentally characterised bacteria: Escherichia coli K-12 (Gammaproteobacteria, the reference for structure and assembly), Bacillus subtilis 168 (Firmicutes) and Pseudomonas putida KT2440 (Gammaproteobacteria, the original pathway exemplar). All twelve members have complete gene reviews that follow one cross-subunit policy (projects/PROTEIN_COMPLEX_FUNCTIONS.md): only beta and beta-prime carry DNA-directed RNA polymerase activity, with contribution semantics; alpha carries dimerization and assembly; omega carries core-enzyme (beta-prime) binding and assembly. Family grounding. Alpha has no PANTHER family in UniProt (none of the three alpha exemplars carries a PANTHER cross-reference), so it is grounded on InterPro IPR011773, present in all three records. Beta is grounded on PANTHER:PTHR20856 at the family level, because UniProt assigns no subfamily to bacterial rpoB. Beta-prime is grounded on subfamily PTHR19376:SF54 (bacterial beta-prime and plastid rpoC1) and omega on PTHR34476:SF1. The beta and beta-prime families are pan-domain (archaeal Rpo2/Rpo1, eukaryotic Pol I/II/III/IV/V subunits, plastid PEP and large DNA virus homologs); their family reviews record that divergence, and confirm that the lineage-specific PAINT nodes (eukaryotic Pol I/II/III complexes) do not propagate to bacteria. Modelling. Polymerase activity is placed on the beta/beta-prime cleft as a complex, with beta and beta-prime as active units, because neither subunit is active alone; the subunit gene reviews record contribution to GO:0003899. The leaf function checks therefore apply to alpha (protein dimerization) and omega (core enzyme binding) only. Boundary. Sigma factors (RpoD, RpoS, RpoH, RpoN, RpoE, FliA and ECF sigmas), elongation and termination factors (NusA, NusG, Rho, GreA/B, Mfd, RapA, DksA) and promoter-specific regulators belong to adjacent transcription modules. Lineage-specific accessory subunits are also outside the conserved core: Firmicutes RNAP carries the delta (RpoE) and epsilon (RpoY/YkzG) subunits, and cyanobacteria and plastids split beta-prime into rpoC1 and rpoC2. Cytoplasm is used as the shared location because the transcribing enzyme acts on the nucleoid, which lies in the bacterial cytoplasm; E. coli proteomics also detects a free cytosolic pool.

Part 2: beta and beta-prime catalytic cleft
RpoB/RpoC catalytic cleftProtein Complexrpob_rpoc_catalytic_cleft

Beta and beta-prime form the active-centre cleft. Beta-prime carries the catalytic Mg(2+) site (NADFDGD), the clamp, bridge helix, trigger loop and two structural Zn(2+) sites; beta contributes the RNA-DNA hybrid and NTP-contacting surfaces, the RNA exit-channel flap and the rifampicin-binding pocket. The activity belongs to the cleft as a whole.
