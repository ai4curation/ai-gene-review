Camalexin biosynthesis (tryptophan-derived indole phytoalexin)

Camalexin (3-thiazol-2'-yl-indole) is the main phytoalexin of Arabidopsis thaliana and is made from tryptophan after pathogen attack. The ER-anchored cytochrome P450s CYP79B2 and CYP79B3 convert tryptophan to indole-3-acetaldoxime (IAOx), a branch point shared with indole glucosinolate and auxin biosynthesis. CYP71A13, with a minor contribution from its paralog CYP71A12, commits IAOx to the camalexin branch. It monooxygenates IAOx to an alpha-hydroxy-indole-3-acetonitrile (indole-3-cyanohydrin) intermediate and is proposed to dehydrate this to a reactive electrophile (dehydro-IAN) that is captured by the thiol of glutathione or cysteine. CYP71A13 also dehydrates IAOx to indole-3-acetonitrile (IAN), which may be largely an off-pathway product; CYP71A12 releases more of the cyanohydrin as its breakdown products indole-3-carbaldehyde and cyanide. Glutathione transferases, including GSTF6, are implicated in the conjugation, but many GSTs can support it, higher-order phi-class GST mutants make normal camalexin, and the electrophile can react with glutathione without an enzyme. The cytosolic gamma-glutamyl peptidases GGP1 and GGP3 remove the gamma-glutamyl residue of the glutathione conjugate (GS-IAN), and an unidentified peptidase removes glycine to give the cysteine conjugate Cys(IAN). The bifunctional P450 CYP71B15 (PAD3) then closes the thiazoline ring of Cys(IAN) with cyanide release to form dihydrocamalexic acid, and oxidatively decarboxylates it to camalexin. The pathway P450s associate with each other and with P450 reductase in an ER-anchored metabolon that channels intermediates. Pathogen-induced transcription of the biosynthetic genes depends on the MAP kinases MPK3 and MPK6, which phosphorylate the WRKY33 transcription factor; WRKY33 binds and activates the CYP71A13 and PAD3 promoters. These two components regulate the pathway and are not pathway enzymes.

Part 2: indole-3-acetaldoxime to the reactive cyanohydrin / dehydro-IAN intermediate (CYP71A13, minor CYP71A12)
Indole cyanohydrin / dehydro-IAN formationReactionian_formation

The committed camalexin step. With 18O2 labelling, Klein et al. 2013 showed that CYP71A13 and CYP71A12 monooxygenate IAOx to an alpha-hydroxy-IAN (indole-3-cyanohydrin) intermediate. They propose that the enzyme then dehydrates this to a reactive electrophile (dehydro-IAN) that is captured by the free thiol of cysteine or glutathione; the P450 does not itself form the C-S bond. The dehydration of IAOx to IAN (RHEA:23156, EC 4.8.1.3), long taken as the pathway step, is the major in vitro product of CYP71A13, but IAN is turned over about 1000-fold more slowly than IAOx and may be largely an off-pathway product. CYP71A13 forms the thiol-trapped product more efficiently; CYP71A12 releases more of the cyanohydrin as indole-3-carbaldehyde and cyanide. GO has no molecular-function term for the cyanohydrin-forming monooxygenation (see knowledge gaps).

Part 3: glutathione conjugation and processing to the cysteine conjugate (GSTF6, GGP1/GGP3)
Glutathione conjugation and processingMetabolic Pathwaygsh_conjugation

Glutathione, not free cysteine, supplies the thiazole sulfur. The activated IAN intermediate is conjugated to glutathione (GS-IAN); the gamma-glutamyl residue is removed in the cytosol by GGP1 (and GGP3), and glycine is then removed by an unidentified peptidase to give Cys(IAN). The extracellular gamma-glutamyl transpeptidases GGT1/GGT2 were proposed for the gamma-glutamyl step but the intermediate-accumulation, localization and enzyme-activity evidence favours GGP1.
