Bacterial heptaprenylglyceryl ether-lipid (Ac-HepG) pathway

The Bacillales route to the archaeal-type ether lipid heptaprenylglycerol and its acetylated form (Ac-HepG). sn-Glycerol 1-phosphate (G1P) is condensed with all-trans-heptaprenyl diphosphate by the group I GGGPS-family enzyme PcrB to form an ether bond, giving 3-heptaprenyl-sn-glycero-1-phosphate (HepGP); HepGP is dephosphorylated by a phosphatase that has not been assigned to a gene, and the resulting heptaprenylglycerol (HepG) is O-acetylated from acetyl-CoA by YvoF. The product Ac-HepG is not elaborated into a bulk membrane lipid as in Archaea: it is a low-abundance lipophilic acetyl carrier that delivers acetyl groups to the extracytoplasmic face of the membrane, where LhaT (formerly YpjA) transfers them to the alpha-amino group of the N-terminal cysteine of mature diacylglyceryl lipoproteins. The module is of interest because a bacterium here uses three features of archaeal membrane chemistry at once - a G1P backbone, an ether linkage and an isoprenoid chain - to build a carrier cofactor rather than a membrane. Lipoprotein diacylglyceryl transfer and signal-peptide cleavage, which produce LhaT's acceptor, are modelled separately in bacterial_lipoprotein_maturation; isoprenoid (HepPP) supply and the downstream TLR2 immunology are outside the boundary.

Four biosynthetic steps from G1P and heptaprenyl diphosphate to the acetylated ether lipid Ac-HepG, plus the optional terminal step in which the carried acetyl group is handed to a lipoprotein. Part 1: sn-glycerol 1-phosphate supply
sn-Glycerol 1-phosphate supplyReactiong1p_supply

Provision of the G1P acceptor. PcrB accepts G1P and not G3P, so a source of the archaeal-type glycerophosphate enantiomer is required. The only characterized bacterial route is NAD(P)H-dependent reduction of glycerone phosphate (DHAP) by a glycerol-1-phosphate dehydrogenase (EgsA/AraM). Whether that enzyme is in fact the in vivo source of the G1P used by PcrB has not been shown, so the step is modelled as a required activity with one known realizer rather than as a proven EgsA-to-PcrB flux.

Part 3: Dephosphorylation of heptaprenylglyceryl phosphate

Hydrolysis of the phosphomonoester of HepGP to give the neutral ether lipid heptaprenylglycerol (HepG). Part 4: Acetylation of heptaprenylglycerol
Heptaprenylglycerol O-acetylation (YvoF)Reactionhepg_acetylation

YvoF transfers an acetyl group from acetyl-CoA to a glyceryl hydroxyl of HepG, giving the acetylated carrier Ac-HepG. YvoF was identified by screening a B. subtilis knockout library for loss of acetylation, and is a close relative of maltose O-acetyltransferase; the two overlap partly in vitro but maltose O-acetyltransferase does not complement a yvoF knockout in vivo, so the activity is YvoF's own physiological role. Mono-acetylated species on C1 or C2 and a diacetylated species can all be formed.

Part 5: Delivery of the carried acetyl group to a lipoprotein (optional)

Marked optional because PcrB has a broader strain distribution than LhaT, LhaT is pseudogenized in the Bacillus cereus group, and some species encoding both do not make acetylated lipoprotein. The carrier steps can therefore run in genomes where this consumer is absent or inactive, and Ac-HepG may have further uses. Keeping the step inside the module (rather than treating it purely as downstream biology) is what makes the pathway's purpose explicit: the module's real product is a transferred acetyl group, and the carrier is regenerated.

Lipoprotein N-acetylation from Ac-HepG (LhaT/YpjA)Reactionlipoprotein_acetyl_transfer

LhaT, a polytopic membrane protein formerly annotated only as uncharacterized YpjA, transfers the acetyl group from Ac-HepG to the alpha-amino group of the N-terminal cysteine of a mature diacylglyceryl lipoprotein, giving acetylated lipoprotein (Ac-LP) and releasing HepG for reloading. The acceptor is produced by Lgt diacylglyceryl transfer and LspA signal-peptide cleavage, which are modelled in bacterial_lipoprotein_maturation and are not repeated here. How Ac-HepG reaches the outer leaflet is unknown.
