Multiactivity mechanisms contributing to polar auxin transport

Cell-to-cell auxin transport arises from the spatial arrangement and regulation of membrane uptake and export activities. This module represents experimentally grounded land-plant transport combinations: complementary influx/efflux, pair-specific PIN–ABCB cooperation, and carrier activation by kinases or TWD1. Each named mechanism contains at least two substantive activities and native-context evidence; satisfying it identifies a supported mechanistic combination, not sufficiency to generate tissue polarity or a complete transport network. Carrier-mediated uptake is context-dependent because protonated IAA can also enter by diffusion. Native polarity and tissue flow are distinguished from cellular or heterologous export. Arabidopsis provides the characterized multiactivity mechanisms. Rice contributes only an optional sampled influx/efflux segment, and moss/liverwort PINs are exporter comparators; neither alone certifies an equivalent native mechanism. Nuclear signaling, biosynthesis, intracellular short-PIN/PILS compartmentation, general vesicle trafficking and bulk phloem transport are outside the modeled boundary.
