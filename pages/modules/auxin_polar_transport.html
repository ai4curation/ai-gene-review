Polar (cell-to-cell) auxin transport

Polar auxin transport is the directional, cell-to-cell movement of the phytohormone auxin (indole-3-acetic acid, IAA) through plant tissues. It generates the auxin gradients and maxima that pattern embryos, organs and vasculature and that drive tropic growth. The chemiosmotic model explains its directionality. In the acidic apoplast, protonated IAAH can enter cells by diffusion, but most uptake is carried out by AUX1/LAX proton-coupled influx symporters. In the near-neutral cytosol IAA is trapped as the membrane-impermeant anion and must be exported by carriers. PIN-FORMED (PIN) efflux carriers are polarly localized within the plasma membrane, so their position sets the direction of flow. Examples are PIN1 at the basal (rootward) side of shoot vascular cells and apically in the shoot-apex epidermis, and PIN2 at the apical (shootward) side of root epidermal and lateral root cap cells. Full-size ABCB/P-glycoprotein transporters such as ABCB19 use ATP to export auxin from the plasma membrane largely non-polarly. They maintain long-distance streams by limiting reuptake and lateral leakage, and they act together with PINs. PIN activity and polarity are set by phosphorylation of the PIN central hydrophilic loop by plasma-membrane-associated AGCVIII kinases. PINOID/WAG kinases phosphorylate sites that recruit PINs into apical targeting (a phosphorylation-dependent polarity switch opposed by PP2A/PP6 phosphatases), and both PID and the basally localized D6 PROTEIN KINASE (D6PK) activate PIN-mediated efflux. This module covers the transport and regulatory activities at the plasma membrane. Nuclear auxin perception and signaling (TIR1/AFB-Aux/IAA-ARF), auxin biosynthesis, intracellular auxin compartmentation (short PINs, PILS) and the vesicle-trafficking machinery that recycles PINs (GNOM ARF-GEF) are outside its boundary.

Part 1: carrier-mediated auxin uptake
AUX1/LAX auxin influxTransport Stepauxin_influx

Proton-coupled uptake of the IAA anion from the apoplast. AUX1 is the founding member of the AUX1/LAX family (LAX1-3 act in other tissues). It supplies auxin to root apical tissues and is required with PIN2 for root gravitropism.

Part 2: polar auxin efflux by PIN carriers
PIN-mediated polar auxin effluxTransport Steppin_polar_efflux

Long (canonical) PIN carriers export IAA across the plasma membrane from a polar membrane domain, so the side of the cell they occupy sets the direction of intercellular auxin flow. Different PINs act in different tissues; PIN1 and PIN2 are the curated exemplars here (PIN3, PIN4 and PIN7 act in additional tissues and in tropisms).

Part 3: ATP-driven auxin export by ABCB transporters
ABCB19-mediated auxin exportTransport Stepabcb_auxin_export

Full-size ABCB (P-glycoprotein) transporters export auxin using ATP hydrolysis, mostly from non-polar plasma-membrane positions in vascular and bundle-sheath cells. ABCB19 maintains long-distance basipetal transport in shoots and rootward transport in the root central cylinder. It also stabilizes PIN1 in sterol-enriched membrane domains and is inhibited by phototropin 1 phosphorylation. (ABCB19 also exports brassinosteroids; that activity is outside this module.)

Part 4: AGCVIII kinase control of PIN polarity and activity
PIN phosphorylation by PID and D6PKRegulatory Steppin_kinase_regulation

Plasma-membrane-associated AGCVIII kinases phosphorylate serines in the PIN central hydrophilic loop. PINOID (with WAG1/WAG2/PID2) drives apical PIN targeting and also activates efflux; its effect on polarity is opposed by PP2A/PP6 phosphatases. D6PK (with D6PKL1-3) is basally localized, activates PIN efflux without by itself switching polarity, and is required for efficient basipetal transport and tropisms.
