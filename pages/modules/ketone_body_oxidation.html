Ketone-body uptake and oxidation

A reusable pathway for use of D-3-hydroxybutyrate and acetoacetate as a carbon and energy source. D-3-hydroxybutyrate is oxidized to acetoacetate by an NAD+-dependent short-chain dehydrogenase, acetoacetate is activated to acetoacetyl-CoA, and a thiolase cleaves acetoacetyl-CoA to two acetyl-CoA. The same chemistry is carried by different enzyme lineages: animals use the mitochondrial SDR9C dehydrogenase BDH1, whereas bacteria use a separate FabG-type SDR, and acetoacetate is activated either by succinyl-CoA-dependent CoA transfer (animal SCOT) or by ATP-dependent acetoacetate-CoA ligation (bacterial and animal acetoacetyl-CoA synthetases). Substrate import is an optional upstream role for cells that consume extracellular D-3-hydroxybutyrate; D-3-hydroxybutyrate released inside the cell, for example by storage-polymer turnover, enters at the oxidation step.

The exact Q88GH0/PP_3754 record assigns BktB both EC 2.3.1.9 and EC 2.3.1.16, establishing reaction capability but not which thiolase is used physiologically in D-3-hydroxybutyrate catabolism.

Scope is species-neutral: the reaction chain is conserved from bacteria to animals, and lineage differences are modelled as variants (two dehydrogenase lineages, two activation routes) rather than as separate modules. CONCRETE denotes the defined reaction topology; family selectors keep the participants reusable. Human BDH1, OXCT1, AACS and ACAT1 are the experimentally characterized exemplars. Pseudomonas putida KT2440 supplies a complete bacterial instance with the bhbP-hbdH-aacs locus (PP_3074, PP_3073, PP_3071) and BktB (PP_3754) as a reaction-capable thiolase; these assignments rest on family signatures and on the orthologous, (R)-3-hydroxybutyrate-inducible PA2004-bdhA operon of P. aeruginosa, not on assays of the KT2440 proteins. Animal AACS is listed as an exemplar of the ligase chemistry, but in animals it mainly feeds cytosolic lipogenesis; animal ketolysis uses the SCOT route. PANTHER grounding: the uptake, bacterial dehydrogenase and thiolase steps use InterPro signatures because their PANTHER families (PTHR30354 Gnt transporters, PTHR42879 FabG-type SDRs, PTHR18919 thiolases) are substrate-heterogeneous. Family reviews exist for all six PANTHER families of the members (PTHR30354, PTHR42879, PTHR43313, PTHR13707, PTHR42921, PTHR18919) and record the subfamily-level divergence. Storage-polymer-derived D-3-hydroxybutyrate is a possible intracellular entry in bacteria that make poly-3-hydroxybutyrate; KT2440 makes mainly medium-chain-length PHA, so its substrate source remains unresolved.

D-3-hydroxybutyrate oxidationReactionhydroxybutyrate_oxidation
Variant set: D-3-hydroxybutyrate dehydrogenase lineage by dehydrogenase lineage [Realizer] (One Or More)

The same NAD+-dependent reaction is carried by two separate short-chain dehydrogenase/reductase lineages: bacterial 3-hydroxybutyrate dehydrogenases (IPR011294; PANTHER PTHR42879, FabG-type SDRs) and the animal mitochondrial BDH1 clade of the SDR9C family (PTHR43313:SF25). Human BDH1 does not match IPR011294, so the two are modelled as alternative carriers rather than one family.
