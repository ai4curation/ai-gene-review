Kallikrein-kinin cascade — contact activation, kinin release from kininogen, B1/B2 receptor signaling, kininase inactivation and serpin control

The kallikrein-kinin system is an extracellular protease cascade that releases the vasoactive kinin peptides bradykinin and kallidin (Lys-bradykinin) from a single precursor, kininogen, and terminates them by peptidase cleavage. Two routes feed the cascade. In the plasma (contact) route, coagulation factor XII (F12, Hageman factor) binds negatively charged surfaces or misfolded protein aggregates and is autoactivated or activated by traces of protease; surface-bound F12a then activates plasma prekallikrein (KLKB1), which circulates in complex with high-molecular-weight kininogen, and the formed kallikrein activates further F12 in a reciprocal positive-feedback loop. Prolylcarboxypeptidase (PRCP) on the endothelial membrane provides a factor XII-independent prekallikrein activator. Active plasma kallikrein cleaves high-molecular-weight kininogen and liberates the nonapeptide bradykinin. In the tissue route, tissue kallikrein (KLK1), secreted by kidney, pancreas and salivary gland, cleaves low-molecular-weight kininogen at two sites and releases the decapeptide kallidin. The kinins act as short-lived local hormones on two G protein-coupled receptors: the constitutively expressed B2 receptor (BDKRB2), whose agonists are bradykinin and kallidin, and the inducible B1 receptor (BDKRB1), whose agonists are the des-Arg metabolites generated at the kinin C terminus by the carboxypeptidases CPN1 (plasma, kininase I) and CPM (membrane-bound). Receptor occupancy raises intracellular calcium and drives vasodilation, increased vascular permeability, release of inflammatory mediators and stimulation of sensory neurons. Several peptidases act as kininases and destroy kinin activity: angiotensin-converting enzyme (ACE, kininase II), neprilysin (MME) and membrane-bound aminopeptidase P (XPNPEP2). The proteases of the cascade are restrained by serpins and by alpha-2-macroglobulin: C1 inhibitor (SERPING1) and A2M are the principal plasma inhibitors of kallikrein and F12a, and kallistatin (SERPINA4) inhibits tissue kallikrein.

Part 3: kinin release from kininogen
Release of kinins from kininogen by a kallikreinReactionkks_kinin_release

Both active kinins come from kininogen (KNG1), whose alternative transcripts give the high- and low-molecular-weight forms. Which kinin is produced depends on which kallikrein does the cleaving, so the two routes are modeled as alternatives along the enzyme axis. Both can operate in the same organism, in different compartments.

Variant set: Plasma versus tissue kallikrein route by enzyme and substrate route (One Or More)

ONE_OR_MORE rather than EXACTLY_ONE: the plasma route operates in the intravascular compartment on high-molecular-weight kininogen and the tissue route in glandular tissue and their fluids on the low-molecular-weight form, and tissue kallikrein can also cleave the high form. A concrete realization may therefore have one or both.

Plasma kallikrein releasing bradykinin from high-molecular-weight kininogenReactionplasma_kallikrein_route

Active plasma kallikrein cleaves high-molecular-weight kininogen in the intravascular compartment, liberating the nonapeptide bradykinin and leaving the disulfide-linked kinin-free heavy and light chains.

Part 4: kinin receptor signaling
Kinin signaling through the B2 and B1 bradykinin receptorsSignaling Pathwaykks_kinin_signaling

The released kinins act as short-lived local hormones. Bradykinin and kallidin are agonists of the constitutively expressed B2 receptor; the des-Arg metabolites made in part 5 are the agonists of the inducible B1 receptor. Both are G protein-coupled receptors that raise intracellular calcium and drive vasodilation and inflammatory mediator release.

Part 5: carboxy-terminal processing to des-Arg kinins
Carboxypeptidase removal of the C-terminal arginine from kininsReactionkks_des_arg_generation

Two basic-residue carboxypeptidases remove the C-terminal arginine of bradykinin and kallidin: plasma carboxypeptidase N (kininase I) and the GPI-anchored carboxypeptidase M at cell surfaces. The products lose B2 agonism but are the agonists of the B1 receptor, so this step converts rather than only destroys the signal.

Part 6: kinin inactivation by kininases
Destruction of kinin activity by kininasesReactionkks_kinin_inactivation

Cleavage of any peptide bond of bradykinin abolishes its B2 agonism, so several unrelated peptidases act in parallel as kininases: ACE (kininase II) removes C-terminal dipeptides, neprilysin cleaves internally, and the soluble and membrane-bound aminopeptidases P remove the N-terminal residue before the proline. Which one dominates depends on localization and access.
