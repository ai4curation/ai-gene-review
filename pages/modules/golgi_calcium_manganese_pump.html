Golgi calcium and manganese pumping — secretory pathway Ca2+/Mn2+-ATPases SPCA1 (ATP2C1) and SPCA2 (ATP2C2) loading the Golgi lumen with Ca2+ and Mn2+ (human)

The secretory pathway Ca2+/Mn2+-ATPases (SPCAs) are P-type ATPases of the Golgi apparatus that use ATP hydrolysis to pump calcium and manganese ions from the cytosol into the Golgi lumen. Unlike the ER SERCA pumps they are insensitive to thapsigargin and transport Mn2+ with high affinity, a property that supplies the Mn2+ needed by luminal glycosyltransferases and other Mn2+-dependent enzymes and that protects cells against manganese toxicity by routing Mn2+ into the secretory pathway for export. Golgi calcium supports protein sorting, processing and glycosylation and contributes a separate, thapsigargin-resistant calcium store. Humans have two paralogs. SPCA1 (ATP2C1) is ubiquitous, sits mainly in the trans-Golgi, and is the dominant Golgi pump in most cells; heterozygous loss of ATP2C1 causes Hailey-Hailey disease, a blistering skin disorder in which keratinocyte Golgi calcium stores refill poorly. SPCA2 (ATP2C2) is restricted to secretory epithelia (gastrointestinal and respiratory tract, mammary gland), brain and testis, where it is found in Golgi and post-Golgi vesicles. In lactating mammary epithelium and breast cancer cells SPCA2 also reaches the plasma membrane, where it activates the ORAI1 channel independently of its pump activity.

PW:0001139 is the only PW evidence source_id; its sub-pathway PW:0001140 (calcium/calcium-mediated signaling) is mentioned here in prose only. (1) STRUCTURE. The root is a ONE_OR_MORE REALIZER variant set of the two paralogs, each a transport step with two annotons, one for Ca2+ and one for Mn2+, because GO types the two reactions as separate activities (GO:0005388 and GO:0140613) and both are assayed for both paralogs. The two ions compete for the same transport site in one pump cycle, so the annotons are alternative substrates of one activity, not ordered steps; there are no connections. The module is deliberately short: there are no regulatory subunits or partner proteins with experimental support for the pumping activity. (2) SUBSTRATES AND PRODUCTS. Grounded to ChEBI with labels checked in OLS (November 2026): calcium(2+) CHEBI:29108, manganese(2+) CHEBI:29035, ATP(4-) CHEBI:30616, ADP(3-) CHEBI:456216, hydrogenphosphate CHEBI:43474, water CHEBI:15377, hydron CHEBI:15378. Both proteins are multi-pass P-type pumps, so the membrane is not in question. ATP2C1: Golgi membrane IDA (PMID:12804581, PMID:21187401), Golgi apparatus IDA (PMID:11741891, HPA) and trans-Golgi network IDA (PMID:14632183). ATP2C2: Golgi membrane only by IBA and Reactome TAS, and the only experimental location row is plasma membrane (PMID:20887894); the Golgi membrane location is authored on two lines, its multi-pass P-type topology and Golgi-like juxtanuclear immunostaining of endogenous SPCA2 in human colon (PMID:15831496), with endogenous neuronal SPCA2 on trans-Golgi network-derived vesicles (PMID:15677451). The trans-Golgi network child is not added beside Golgi membrane, to avoid a sub-compartment claim resting on one paper per gene. (5) BOUNDARY: SPCA2-ORAI1 STORE-INDEPENDENT CALCIUM ENTRY IS CONTEXT. In breast cancer cells and lactating mammary epithelium SPCA2 reaches the plasma membrane and binds and activates ORAI1, producing constitutive calcium influx independent of ER stores, of STIM1 and STIM2, and of SPCA2's own ATPase activity (PMID:20887894; GOA plasma membrane EXP). This is a different system from Golgi pumping (a channel-activating scaffold role at the plasma membrane, not transport by SPCA2) and it is not modelled as a part. ORAI1 and its store-operated gating by STIM are modelled in modules/store_operated_calcium_entry.yaml, which could take SPCA2 as a store-independent ORAI1 activator in a later revision; such an annoton would need a calcium channel regulator function that no experimental GOA row for ATP2C2 currently provides. Also context: SERCA pumps, which load part of the Golgi (the thapsigargin-sensitive cis-Golgi pool) and are modelled in er_calcium_store_cycling; Golgi calcium release (IP3 receptors in Golgi membranes; GO:0061454) and Golgi luminal calcium-binding proteins (Cab45, nucleobindin), which are not on RGD's list; TMEM165, a Golgi Mn2+/Ca2+ transporter of a different family that also supplies Golgi Mn2+ for glycosylation, not on RGD's list and not modelled here; and the dependent glycosylation and sorting reactions.
