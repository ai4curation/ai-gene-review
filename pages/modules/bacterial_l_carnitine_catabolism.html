Bacterial L-carnitine catabolism through 3-dehydrocarnitine

An aerobic bacterial pathway that converts L-carnitine to glycine betaine through 3-dehydrocarnitine. An NAD-dependent L-carnitine dehydrogenase forms 3-dehydrocarnitine; a beta-keto acid cleavage enzyme (CdhC/BcoG) transfers the trimethylammonio-acetyl (betainyl) group to acetyl-CoA, releasing acetoacetate and forming betainyl-CoA; and a betainyl-CoA thioesterase releases glycine betaine and free CoA. The thioesterase is either a separate hotdog-fold protein (CdhB) or the C-terminal region of a bifunctional dehydrogenase-thioesterase (CdhAB/BcoE-BcoF). Where carnitine is taken up through an ABC importer, the CaiX substrate-binding protein confers carnitine selectivity on the shared CbcWV choline/betaine/carnitine core. The oxygenolytic CntAB route to trimethylamine, anaerobic Cai reduction to gamma-butyrobetaine, compatible-solute retention, acetoacetate activation, and downstream glycine-betaine demethylation are outside this boundary.

The context is Pseudomonadota, with supported gamma- and alphaproteobacterial realizers, not a claim of universal pathway occurrence. The defined reaction boundary starts at free L-carnitine and ends at glycine betaine. Upstream gamma-butyrobetaine conversion, downstream GB demethylation, compatible-solute retention, CntAB trimethylamine production and Cai-dependent anaerobic reduction are separate mechanisms. The reactions themselves do not consume molecular oxygen; aerobic denotes the characterized physiological setting, not an oxygen substrate requirement. Anaerobic CJ25 GB production in PMID:40853000 proposes different downstream chemistry and does not establish this exact betainyl-CoA route. CaiX-CbcWV import is optional because free carnitine may be supplied by other importers or upstream intracellular synthesis. PAO1 CbcWV proteins have direct construct provenance, with native PA14/PAO1 and heterologous-host assay boundaries recorded in their reviews. KT2440 transport and LcdH/CdhB chemistry rely on target architecture, conserved locus and characterized orthologs; the purified P. putida enzyme in PMID:3058208 is from IFP206. KT2440 PP_0303 is an exception to the former blanket unassayed claim: PMID:24240508 Data Sets 2/4 map Q88R31 to BKACE511 and record direct substrate screening. Its raw KAH signal lacks a recovered positive-call threshold, so possible secondary KAH activity remains unresolved; this neither establishes lysine metabolism nor negates its carnitine role. PANTHER BKACE or hydroxyacyl-CoA family membership alone is insufficient to select the exact reaction. For the fused Sinorhizobium realization, cloned 3D0a2 proteins were identical to Rm2011 and independently match the strain 1021 representative sequences. Full-length BcoEF supplies both oxidation and betainyl-CoA hydrolysis; isolated-domain assays and substrate channeling were not demonstrated. Its additional carnitinyl-CoA hydrolysis belongs upstream of this module. Rm1021 GBB-growth failure despite locus identity is retained as a strain-specific phenotype, not a negative carnitine-enzyme assay. The paper's mutant set excludes BcoG; its role is supported by catalytic measurements, not an invented knockout. Broad GO parents GO:0016747 and GO:0016289 do not by themselves identify CdhC or CdhB specificity. The exact substrates/products, EC/Rhea reaction evidence, reviewed representatives and member-level activity constraints define each step. Both families are heterogeneous, and the fused thioesterase is grounded as an ortholog realization rather than assigning its domain an unverified PANTHER id. No complete pathway is inferred from a single dehydrogenase or from carnitine/GB growth alone.
