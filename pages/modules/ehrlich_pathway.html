Ehrlich pathway (amino acid catabolism to fusel alcohols and fusel acids)

Catabolic route by which yeasts and other fungi degrade the branched-chain amino acids (leucine, isoleucine, valine), the aromatic amino acids (phenylalanine, tyrosine, tryptophan) and methionine when these are used as nitrogen sources. Only the amino group is retained by the cell; the carbon skeleton is excreted. The pathway has three steps. A pyridoxal-phosphate aminotransferase first transfers the amino group to an acceptor 2-oxo acid (2-oxoglutarate for the branched-chain aminotransferases; pyruvate or phenylpyruvate for the inducible aromatic aminotransferase), giving the corresponding 2-oxo acid. A thiamine-diphosphate-dependent 2-oxo-acid decarboxylase then irreversibly converts the 2-oxo acid to an aldehyde with one carbon fewer; this is the first step specific to the pathway. Finally, depending on the redox state of the cell, the aldehyde is reduced by NADH alcohol dehydrogenases to a fusel alcohol (isoamyl alcohol, 2-methylbutanol, isobutanol, 2-phenylethanol, tyrosol, tryptophol) or oxidized by aldehyde dehydrogenases to a fusel acid. All three steps are carried out by sets of overlapping isozymes whose use depends on the amino acid, the carbon source and the growth stage. In budding yeast the aminotransferase and decarboxylase of the aromatic branch are co-induced by aromatic amino acids. Oxidative degradation of the 2-oxo acids by the mitochondrial branched-chain 2-oxo acid dehydrogenase complex, the catabolic route that dominates in animals, is outside this module.
