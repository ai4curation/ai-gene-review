Bacterial type III secretion injectisome

A species-neutral module for the injectisome, the envelope-spanning type III secretion machine that moves unfolded substrates from the bacterial cytoplasm through a continuous conduit and across a second, target-cell membrane in one step. A cytoplasmic sorting platform and ATPase select substrates and strip their chaperones; an inner-membrane export apparatus forms the narrow channel; rings in the inner membrane and an outer-membrane secretin complete the basal body; an inner rod and a helical needle extend the conduit beyond the cell surface; and a translocon inserted in the target membrane completes the passage. The substrates themselves are excluded from this module, as are chaperone-substrate pairing for individual cargo, transcriptional regulation of the locus, and the flagellar export system, which is homologous but builds a different structure.

Reusable boundary: the module begins when the sorting platform selects a substrate in the cytoplasm and ends when that substrate passes through the translocon. Substrates are excluded for the same reason as in the sibling type II, type IV, type V, and type VI modules, and here the exclusion matters more than usual because injectisome substrate repertoires are large, lineage specific, and unrelated to each other in function. The flagellar export system is deliberately not admitted, although the export-apparatus and ATPase components are homologous and the repository has a separate module for flagellar assembly and export. The two systems build different structures from that shared core, and merging them would make the needle, secretin, and translocon parts inapplicable to half the module. Grounding is uneven by necessity. Every conserved role carries a reviewed Swiss-Prot exemplar, taken from Salmonella SPI-1 or Yersinia. The enteropathogenic Escherichia coli counterparts are given alongside them, but most of the Esc structural proteins exist only as unreviewed UniProt entries, which is noted on each annoton where it applies rather than hidden. Two Escherichia coli components are reviewed: the translocon protein EspB and the chaperone CesT. Ontology and curation gaps are recorded here rather than as knowledge_gaps, which CLAUDE.md reserves for biological unknowns that only new experiments could close. Part 2: Form the narrow inner-membrane channel and switch substrate specificity
Type III secretion export apparatusTransport Stepexport_apparatus

Five inner-membrane proteins form the export apparatus. Three small components build a helical core that constitutes the narrowest part of the conduit; a fourth undergoes autoproteolysis and thereby switches which class of substrate is exported; and the large export gate surrounds them and connects to the cytoplasmic platform. This subassembly is the part homologous to the flagellar export machinery.

An inner rod continues the channel through the periplasm and a helical needle polymer extends it past the outer membrane. Lineages differ in whether the needle alone spans the gap to the target cell or whether a second, much longer filament extends it further.

Part 5: Complete the conduit across the target-cell membrane
Type III secretion transloconProtein Complextranslocon

A tip complex at the distal end of the conduit senses contact and nucleates insertion of two hydrophobic proteins into the target membrane. Together these form the pore that completes the passage. The translocon is the only part of the machine that resides in a membrane the bacterium does not own.

Part 6: Hold substrates secretion-competent and gate the order of export (optional)
Substrate gating and cytoplasmic chaperonesRegulatory Stepsubstrate_gating_and_chaperones

A gatekeeper protein withholds later substrates until the translocon is in place, and cytoplasmic chaperones hold substrates in a secretion-competent state and deliver them to the sorting platform. Both are conserved in role but variable in identity and number, so the part is optional.
