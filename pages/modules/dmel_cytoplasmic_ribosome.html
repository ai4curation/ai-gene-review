Cytoplasmic (80S) ribosome in Drosophila melanogaster

The cytoplasmic 80S ribosome translates nuclear-encoded mRNAs. It consists of a 40S small subunit, which binds mRNA and decodes it, and a 60S large subunit, which carries the peptidyl transferase centre and the polypeptide exit tunnel. Each subunit is built from ribosomal RNAs and dozens of ribosomal proteins named by the universal (u) and eukaryote-specific (e) nomenclature. In Drosophila several ribosomal proteins are encoded by duplicated genes, and some paralogs are expressed mainly in the germline or testis (for example RpL22-like). FlyBase therefore also defines testis-specific variant compositions of both subunits, in which RpS28-like, RpL22-like and RpL37-2 appear only in the testis variants.

The subunit parts follow the four FlyBase subgroups (RPS, RPL and their testis-specific variants); most proteins are shared and appear in both the canonical and the testis variant of a subunit. The variant groups record composition as curated by FlyBase; whether every listed paralog swap occurs in a single ribosome population in vivo is not established. FlyBase lists ribosomal proteins only, so rRNAs are not participants here, and some members (RpL24-like, a RLP24 ribosome biogenesis homolog; Rack1) are associated proteins rather than core rRNA-binding proteins. Ribosome biogenesis factors, translation factors and mitochondrial ribosomes are separate modules. FlyBase groups realized by this module: CRP (FBgg0000141). Subgroups folded into it: RPS, RPL, RPS-T, RPL-T.
