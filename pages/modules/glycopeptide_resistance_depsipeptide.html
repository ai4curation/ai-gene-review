D-Ala-D-Lac glycopeptide resistance

Inducible, high-level resistance to glycopeptide antibiotics achieved by re-terminating peptidoglycan precursors. A VanS-VanR two-component system induces the resistance operon; VanH reduces pyruvate to D-lactate, a VanA/VanB/VanD-type ligase condenses D-alanine with D-lactate to give the depsipeptide D-Ala-D-Lac in place of the dipeptide D-Ala-D-Ala, and VanX (with VanY) destroys the residual D-Ala-D-Ala pool so that the depsipeptide, not the dipeptide, is the substrate offered to the D-Ala-D-Ala adding enzyme. The resulting UDP-MurNAc-pentadepsipeptide binds glycopeptides with greatly reduced affinity. The module substitutes for, rather than adds to, the Ddl step of peptidoglycan precursor biosynthesis, which it includes by reference together with the MurF ligation step that consumes its product. A lower-level D-Ala-D-Ser route is modeled as an alternative to the depsipeptide route.

Created 2026-11-02 as a species-independent module subsuming the development GO-CAM gomodel:671ae02600002832. Boundary. The module starts at glycopeptide sensing and ends at the reduced-affinity precursor. The housekeeping precursor pathway is included by reference, not duplicated: ddl_step_ref names the step the Van enzymes displace and murf_incorporation_ref the step that consumes their product, both pointing into modules/peptidoglycan_precursor_biosynthesis.yaml, which is not edited. Transglycosylation, transpeptidation, D-serine supply (VanT) and VanZ are outside the boundary. Modeling choices worth flagging. VanS appears as two annotons because the sensor is genuinely bifunctional and the two activities have opposite signs on one VanR annoton; VanR appears once, which is the correction of the GO-CAM's duplicated node. The ligase step is a ROUTE set over two chemistries with a REALIZER set over VanA/VanB/VanD nested inside the depsipeptide route, so the D-Ala-D-Ser types are alternatives to the depsipeptide rather than interchangeable carriers of it. VanY is an optional part, grounded on the statement that it only slightly increases resistance. PANTHER discipline. Terms are asserted only for VanX (PTHR43126) and VanY (PTHR34385), where the official PANTHER name matches the descriptor and the representative members' UniProt records list that family. No term is asserted for the ligases, VanH, VanS or VanR: UniProt does place them in real families (PTHR23132, PTHR43026, PTHR45453, PTHR48111), but those families are the generic Ddl, 2-hydroxyacid dehydrogenase, PhoR-like sensor and RpoS-regulator families, which do not pick out the van-cluster roles these descriptors mean. Per the repository rule, no id is better than a misleading one. Representative members span two phyla where that could be verified against UniProt: VanX has an enterococcal and a Streptomyces coelicolor exemplar, and the VanA-type ligase has an enterococcal and an Amycolatopsis one. No gene reviews exist for any of the enterococcal Van proteins, so module QC will report every grounding as lacking a review; the UniProt accessions were each checked through the UniProt REST API on 2026-11-02.

Part 1: glycopeptide sensing and operon induction
VanS-VanR induction of the resistance operonRegulatory Stepvanrs_induction

A membrane sensor histidine kinase, VanS, controls the phosphorylation state of the response regulator VanR, which activates transcription of the resistance operon. VanS is bifunctional: its kinase arm phosphorylates VanR and its phosphatase arm dephosphorylates phospho-VanR, so the resting state is enforced rather than merely unstimulated.

PMID:1556077
The VanS-VanR two-component regulatory system controls synthesis of depsipeptide peptidoglycan precursors in Enterococcus faecium BM4147.
The resistance enzymes are transcriptionally induced by the VanS-VanR system.
Synthesis of these enzymes was regulated at the transcriptional level by the VanS-VanR two-component regulatory system encoded by the proximal part of the cluster.
