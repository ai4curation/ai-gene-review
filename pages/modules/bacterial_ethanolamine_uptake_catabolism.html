Bacterial ethanolamine access, deamination and acetaldehyde disposition

Ethanolamine reaches a compatible adenosylcobamide-dependent EutB/EutC complex through condition-dependent protein transport or experimentally supported diffusion. The enzyme releases acetaldehyde and ammonium. The oxidative pathway routes acetaldehyde to acetyl-CoA through EutE or to acetate through a non-acylating NAD-dependent aldehyde dehydrogenase. A parallel EutG branch forms ethanol and can regenerate NAD+. Selected implementations couple EutD and acetate kinase to CoA recycling and ATP formation, use EutA to replace inactivated cofactor, or organize enzymes in a bacterial microcompartment. The chemical nitrogen-release, oxidative and reductive endpoints are distinct from sustained native carbon growth.

The root retains EutBC plus an oxidative acetaldehyde fate as required chemical roles. Protein uptake is conditional, with substrate access and active compatible adenosylated cobamide mandatory environmental prerequisites. A separately evaluated EutBC endpoint measures nitrogen liberation; it does not establish the complete oxidative module or nitrogen assimilation. Oxidative and reductive branches can coexist. Optional parts do not prove diffusion, redox closure, cofactor maintenance or shell assembly. The production compiler ignores context, chemicals and complex active-unit expansion, so the scoped evaluator must enforce these explicitly and distinguish unsupported evidence from biological impossibility. Bacterial scope denotes a reusable mechanism sampled across Pseudomonadota and Bacillota, not universal bacterial presence or exclusion from other domains. EutC contributes to the assembled EutBC enzyme; source enables qualifiers remain preserved in gene reviews and are interpreted with source-specific cautions. PA4022 has direct purified native/recombinant biochemical evidence, whereas Q9ZAA1 ExaC is the ATCC17933 comparator and is not PAO1 PA1984. EutE/PduP and broad ALDH families mix substrates; EutG and EutQ scopes likewise do not license whole-subfamily activity transfers. The LT2 EutQ N-terminal scaffold cannot be generalized to shorter Gram-positive homologs. The older outside-compartment EutBC hypothesis is superseded by newer localization/encapsulation evidence. Known shell-negative reference-proteome searches support only their sampled genome and search sensitivity. No PANTHER ancestral node or exact accession is invented for the Acinetobacter physiological comparator.

Family review leaves GO:0008774 UNRESOLVED. CoA-acylating aldehyde oxidation is the diverged reaction of the EutE/PduP branch (SF68), and is not a property of the non-acylating classical ALDHs that make up the rest of the family, which yield the free carboxylate. Within SF68, Salmonella EutE (P41793) has acetaldehyde-to-acetyl-CoA activity on direct assay (PMID:21803884) and E. coli EutE (P77445) is its ortholog, but PANTHER 19.0 (Salmonella classification file) also places Salmonella PduP (Q9XDN1) in SF68, and UniProt records PduP's reaction as the CoA-acylation of propanal in 1,2-propanediol degradation. The acetaldehyde-specific term therefore cannot be granted to the whole subfamily without asserting it for PduP, whose acetaldehyde activity is not reviewed here, and no residue site separating the two substrate preferences is known.
