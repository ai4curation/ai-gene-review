Kdo2-lipid A biosynthesis (Raetz pathway)

The constitutive nine-enzyme Raetz pathway that builds Kdo2-lipid A, the minimal essential lipopolysaccharide anchor, at the cytoplasmic face of the Gram-negative inner membrane. LpxA reversibly transfers an (R)-3-hydroxyacyl chain from acyl carrier protein (ACP) to UDP-GlcNAc; the zinc deacetylase LpxC then irreversibly removes the N-acetyl group, the committed step that sets pathway flux. LpxD adds the second (R)-3-hydroxyacyl chain, LpxH cleaves part of the UDP-diacylglucosamine pool to lipid X, LpxB condenses lipid X with UDP-diacylglucosamine to the beta-1',6-linked disaccharide, and LpxK phosphorylates the 4' position to give lipid IVA. The bifunctional Kdo transferase WaaA adds two Kdo residues from CMP-Kdo, and the late acyltransferases LpxL and LpxM add laurate and myristate to the Kdo2-lipid IVA acceptor to give hexa-acylated Kdo2-lipid A. Flux is set at LpxC by FtsH-mediated proteolysis tuned by the adaptor LapB and the anti-adaptor YejM (PbgA), represented here as an optional regulatory part rather than as pathway chemistry.

Boundary. The module starts at the LpxA acyl transfer onto UDP-GlcNAc and ends at Kdo2-lipid A. Substrate supply is upstream and outside the module: UDP-GlcNAc (shared with peptidoglycan precursor synthesis, see MODULE:peptidoglycan_precursor_biosynthesis), (R)-3-hydroxymyristoyl-ACP from type II fatty acid synthesis (where FabZ competes with LpxA for the same pool), and CMP-Kdo from MODULE:kdo_biosynthesis, whose KdsB product is consumed by the WaaA step modelled here. Core oligosaccharide assembly, MsbA flipping, O-antigen ligation and Lpt-mediated transport to the outer membrane (MODULE:bacterial_lps_transport) are downstream; inducible lipid A modification (e.g. MODULE:lipid_a_l_arabinose_modification) and host recognition of the product (MODULE:tlr4_md2_lps_signaling) are separate modules. Committed step. LpxA is the first pathway-specific reaction but is reversible and thermodynamically unfavourable (Keq about 0.01, PMID:8366124), so commitment occurs at the irreversible LpxC deacetylation. This is recorded on the lpxC_step node and is the reason the regulatory part targets that step and no other. Regulation vs parts. FtsH, LapB (YciM) and YejM (PbgA/LapC) do no lipid A chemistry: they set the steady-state amount (and, for LapB, the activity) of LpxC. They are therefore modelled as a single optional REGULATORY_STEP part connected to lpxC_step by NEGATIVELY_REGULATES, not as steps in the reaction chain. FabZ, PldA/FadD-derived acyl-CoA signalling, the Rod complex and Lpt/OM-derived inputs described in PMID:42264091 are left out entirely: their link to LpxC abundance is genetic or indirect and belongs in the phospholipid/peptidoglycan modules. LapB has two documented activities on LpxC - adaptor-mediated delivery to FtsH and direct allosteric inhibition of deacetylase activity (PMID:38635633). Only the adaptor role is given a GO molecular function here; the inhibitory activity is recorded in role_description and notes because an in vivo contribution has not been established. Grounding. Participants are concrete Escherichia coli K-12 gene products because the reviewed scope is the E. coli pathway; PANTHER families are given alongside each exemplar so the steps can be reused for other Gram-negative lineages. LpxH is not universal among Gram-negative bacteria (PMID:12045108 notes its absence from some Gram-negative genomes, implying another enzyme for this step), but no alternative is asserted here because no second implementation was reviewed. Likewise the cold-shock-induced LpxL paralogue LpxP, which adds palmitoleate in place of laurate at low temperature (PMID:12045108), is outside this constitutive module and is not modelled as a variant of the lpxL_step. No cytoplasm/cytosol term is asserted at module level; per-step locations follow the GOA cellular-component annotations of each enzyme.

Part 10: Proteolytic control of the committed step (optional)
FtsH/LapB/YejM control of LpxC abundanceRegulatory SteplpxC_proteolytic_control

A membrane-embedded regulatory switch that sets the steady-state amount of LpxC and therefore flux through the committed step. The adaptor LapB (YciM) recruits LpxC to the AAA+ protease FtsH for degradation; the anti-adaptor YejM (PbgA/LapC) sequesters the LapB transmembrane helix and is released when LPS accumulates in the periplasmic leaflet of the inner membrane. LapB additionally inhibits LpxC catalytic activity directly in vitro. This part performs no lipid A chemistry and is therefore modelled as regulation rather than as a pathway step.
