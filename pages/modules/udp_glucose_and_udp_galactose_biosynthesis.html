UDP-glucose and UDP-galactose biosynthesis

Bacterial module for de novo production of UDP-glucose from the central glucose 6-phosphate pool and its reversible interconversion with UDP-galactose. Three reactions make up the module: phosphoglucomutase isomerizes glucose 6-phosphate to alpha-D-glucose 1-phosphate, UTP--glucose-1-phosphate uridylyltransferase (GalU) activates glucose 1-phosphate as UDP-glucose, and NAD-dependent UDP-glucose 4-epimerase (GalE) interconverts UDP-glucose and UDP-galactose. The phosphoglucomutase step is supplied by non-homologous or distantly related enzyme families that differ between lineages: a PGM1-type phosphoglucomutase (Escherichia coli Pgm), a PGM2-type phosphoglucomutase (Bacillus subtilis PgcA), or a bifunctional phosphomannomutase/phosphoglucomutase (Pseudomonas aeruginosa AlgC). The activated hexoses feed lipopolysaccharide core, teichoic acid and glycolipid anchors, capsular and exopolysaccharides, trehalose and other glycoconjugates; those consuming pathways, and galactose uptake and catabolism, are outside this module.

Taxonomic scope. The three reactions are conserved across Bacteria, so the module is written species-neutrally with E. coli K-12 (Pgm, GalU, GalE), B. subtilis 168 (PgcA) and P. aeruginosa PAO1 (AlgC) as experimentally characterized exemplars and P. putida KT2440 (Pgm/PP_3578, AlgC/PP_5288, GalU/PP_3821, GalE/PP_3129) kept as a homology-grounded exemplar. The module is scoped to Bacteria rather than all life because the UDP-glucose pyrophosphorylase step is family-specific: bacterial GalU belongs to the prokaryotic UDPGP type 2 family (PANTHER PTHR43197), whereas eukaryotic UGP1/UGP2 belong to a structurally distinct UDPGP type 1 family; the eukaryotic route is better modelled separately. Phosphoglucomutase family choice. E. coli Pgm (PTHR22573:SF57) and B. subtilis PgcA / P. putida Pgm (PTHR45745:SF1) are both dedicated phosphoglucomutases but sit in different PANTHER families of the alpha-D-phosphohexomutase superfamily, so they are modelled as separate variants rather than one family descriptor. The bifunctional AlgC-type route (PTHR43771:SF2) can coexist with a dedicated Pgm; in P. aeruginosa AlgC supplies glucose 1-phosphate for LPS core synthesis, while in P. putida the in-vivo flux split between Pgm and AlgC has not been measured. P. putida CpsG/PP_1777 is excluded because current evidence supports phosphomannomutase rather than phosphoglucomutase activity. GalE grounding. The epimerase is grounded on two PTHR43725 subfamilies: E. coli GalE is in SF47 and P. putida GalE in SF53. SF53's PANTHER name (UDP-ARABINOSE 4-EPIMERASE 1) does not describe the bacterial members, which are UDP-glucose 4-epimerases; a single-subfamily grounding would exclude one exemplar, and the whole-family id would also cover the plant UDP-arabinose 4-epimerase subfamilies (SF33, SF38) to which the activity does not apply. Out of scope. Glucose kinase, aldose mutarotation, galactose uptake and Leloir catabolism (modules/galactose_leloir_pathway.yaml), glycogen synthesis, and downstream polymer assembly are separate modules.
