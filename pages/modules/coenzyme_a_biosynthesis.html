Pantoate-derived coenzyme A biosynthesis

Synthesis of coenzyme A (CoA) from pantoate and beta-alanine. The pantoate branch transfers a hydroxymethyl group to 3-methyl-2-oxobutanoate, reduces 2-dehydropantoate to (R)-pantoate, and reaches 4'-phosphopantothenate by one of two reaction-order variants. Bacteria and eukaryotes generally use PanC to form pantothenate and then one of three unrelated pantothenate kinase families. Most archaea instead phosphorylate pantoate with PoK before PPS condenses 4-phosphopantoate with beta-alanine, so free pantothenate is not an intermediate. Four subsequent reactions add and decarboxylate cysteine, adenylylate 4'-phosphopantetheine, and phosphorylate dephospho-CoA. CoaB and CoaC may be separate or fused, and the final CoaD and CoaE activities may be separate or fused in CoA synthase. The phosphopantothenoylcysteine decarboxylase may be a homo-oligomeric flavoprotein or, in budding yeast, a heterotrimer in which two paralogous subunit types each contribute an essential catalytic residue. Beta-alanine is supplied by lineage-specific routes and is modeled as an optional supply part with two characterized enzymatic variants: PanD-dependent decarboxylation of L-aspartate in bacteria, and the polyamine-oxidation route of Saccharomyces-type yeasts, in which a flavin polyamine oxidase cleaves spermine to spermidine and 3-aminopropanal and cytosolic aldehyde dehydrogenases oxidize 3-aminopropanal to beta-alanine. Pyrimidine degradation and uptake are further sources that are not modeled here. CoA salvage, phosphopantetheine release from acyl carrier proteins, and downstream CoA-dependent metabolism are outside the module boundary.

The module models eight reaction activities in either complete route and keeps molecular functions on leaf annotons. It intentionally has no module-level cellular location: bacterial and archaeal enzymes are commonly cytosolic, whereas eukaryotic isoforms and fused CoA synthases can occupy different compartments. Family selectors identify established catalytic implementations, while the required molecular function remains the decisive criterion. Related reductase families should not satisfy the pantoate step without substrate-specific evidence. Taxon-specific precursor sources, paralogs, and pathway-map spillover belong in pathway-instance reviews rather than this module. ALIGN with YeastPathways (2026-10): the ten PANTOSYN2-PWY reactions map onto the module, and the two-reaction PWY-5760-1 frame is folded in as the polyamine-derived beta-alanine supply variant (FMS1 then ALD2/ALD3); a PanD variant was added alongside it so the optional supply part is cross-taxon. The YeastCyc catalyst lists are correct, but YeastCyc lists CAB3, SIS2 and VHS3 as alternative PPCDC enzymes; the decarboxylase step was re-modeled with an architecture variant set so the budding-yeast Cab3-Sis2/Vhs3 heterotrimer (PMID:19915539) is distinct from homo-oligomeric PPCDCs, including the S. pombe Hal3-thymidylate synthase fusion protein (PMID:23962284). A standalone fungal PPAT (Cab4-type) variant was added because yeasts encode the PPAT and dephospho-CoA kinase as separate proteins rather than as fused CoA synthase. Compartments: both GO-CAMs place the late CoA steps in the cytosol; the PomBase model puts ecm31 and pan5 in mitochondria, and the S. cerevisiae ECM31 gene review likewise places Ecm31 in mitochondria, whereas YeastPathways places it in the cytosol; Cab1 is a separate cytoplasmic, rate-limiting kinase outside the CoA-synthesizing protein complex (GO:1990143, Cab2, Cab3, Cab4, Cab5 with Sis2/Vhs3), and Cab5 sits on the cytosolic face of the mitochondrial outer membrane. Because fungal and bacterial compartments differ, these locations are recorded here rather than as module-level context. GO-CAM disagreements: YeastPathways types FMS1 as N(1)-acetylpolyamine oxidase (GO:0052903) instead of spermine oxidase (GO:0052901) and ALD2/ALD3 as generic GO:0004029 instead of GO:0102244; PomBase 678073a900002636 assigns dephospho-CoA kinase activity to cab4, a PPAT-only protein, and includes acetyl-CoA synthesis and mitochondrial CoA transport, which are outside the module. PAN5 deletion is tolerated in S. cerevisiae (PMID:19266201), so a second ketopantoate reductase may exist in yeast.
