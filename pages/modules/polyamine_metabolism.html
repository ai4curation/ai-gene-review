Eukaryotic polyamine metabolism (biosynthesis, back-conversion and antizyme feedback)

The polyamines putrescine, spermidine and spermine are small polycations needed for eukaryotic cell growth. They stabilise nucleic acids and chromatin, regulate ion channels and act in translation. Spermidine is also the precursor of the hypusine modification of eIF5A, which is why it is essential in yeast. Biosynthesis begins with ornithine decarboxylase (ODC), a short-lived PLP-dependent enzyme that decarboxylates L-ornithine to putrescine and is the main control point. Some lineages also make putrescine by hydrolysing agmatine with agmatinase. In parallel, a pyruvoyl-dependent S-adenosylmethionine decarboxylase makes decarboxylated SAM (dcSAM), the only aminopropyl donor. Spermidine synthase transfers an aminopropyl group from dcSAM to putrescine to make spermidine. Where spermine is made, a spermine synthase adds a second aminopropyl group. Animals use a dedicated SMS family for this, and budding yeasts use the SPE3 paralogue SPE4. Each transfer releases 5'-methylthioadenosine (MTA), which the methionine salvage cycle recycles. Back-conversion catabolism oxidises spermine or N1-acetylated polyamines with FAD-dependent polyamine oxidases to give shorter polyamines plus an aminoaldehyde and H2O2. In animals, SAT1 N1-acetylates polyamines and peroxisomal PAOX then oxidises them, while SMOX oxidises free spermine. In budding yeast a single oxidase, Fms1, oxidises both spermine and N1-acetylpolyamines, and its 3-aminopropanal product is the source of beta-alanine for pantothenate and coenzyme A synthesis. ODC is controlled by antizyme, a protein whose synthesis needs a +1 ribosomal frameshift that polyamines stimulate. Antizyme binds ODC and targets it for ubiquitin-independent proteasomal degradation. In vertebrates, antizyme inhibitors (catalytically dead ODC paralogues) bind antizyme and release ODC.

Generalisation (crosswalk GENERALIZE, scope: eukaryotic polyamine biosynthesis). The original human-grounded module (nine human reviews: ODC1, AMD1, SRM, SMS, SAT1, PAOX, SMOX, OAZ1, AZIN1) is kept in full. The animal-specific enzymes are now lineage variants: SMS-family spermine synthase, SAT1, PAOX, SMOX and the AZIN1 inhibitor step. Fungal exemplars were added for every conserved step. Spermine synthesis, catabolism and the antizyme inhibitor are marked optional because spermine is dispensable in S. cerevisiae (PMID:9573363), S. pombe has no FMS1-like oxidase, and antizyme inhibitors are vertebrate-specific. The crosswalk rationale called FMS1 orthologous to the human oxidases. It is a member of the same FAD amine oxidase family but combines the SMOX and PAOX substrate ranges in one enzyme (PMID:12670477), so it is modelled as its own variant rather than as an orthologue of either. YeastCyc corrections: the four biosynthetic assignments (SPE1, SPE2, SPE3, SPE4; all cytosolic) are kept and agree with the committed SPE2, SPE3 and SPE4 reviews. SPE3 is the only spermidine synthase (PMID:9073064). The PAINT IBA spermidine synthase annotation on SPE4 is over-propagation from SPE3, and SPE4 is modelled with spermine synthase activity only. YeastCyc has no S. cerevisiae polyamine catabolism or regulation. Added here: FMS1 polyamine oxidase (PMID:12670477), which also supplies beta-alanine (PMID:11154694); PAA1 polyamine acetyltransferase (PMID:15723835); and OAZ1 antizyme (PMID:15538383). Regulatory cross-talk: deletion of MEU1 in the methionine salvage cycle raises yeast ODC activity about 8-fold, and the salvage intermediate 4-methylthio-2-oxobutanoate represses ODC (PMID:14506228). Each aminopropyl transfer releases MTA, the entry substrate of methionine_salvage_mta_cycle. (2) The same model includes upstream supply activities (sam1 methionine adenosyltransferase; car1 and aru1 arginases; ort1 mitochondrial ornithine transporter), which are outside the module boundary, and three agmatinases (agm1-3) placed in putrescine biosynthesis. (3) The YeastPathways models agree with this module (MFs GO:0004586, GO:0004014, GO:0004766, GO:0016768; cytosol). Out of scope: eIF5A hypusination (consumes spermidine), polyamine transport (e.g. yeast TPO and DUR3 transporters, human ATP13A3), and the beta-alanine to pantothenate steps (coenzyme_a_biosynthesis). Diseases (human): ODC1 gain of function causes Bachmann-Bupp syndrome; SMS deficiency causes Snyder-Robinson syndrome.
