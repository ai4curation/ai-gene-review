Bacterial non-homologous end joining

A bacterial Ku–LigD implementation of double-strand-break repair in which Ku binds and aligns broken duplex ends and a fused LigD protein seals compatible termini with ATP. Depending on end chemistry and the domains present, repair can additionally involve deoxynucleotide or ribonucleotide addition, 3-prime-phosphate removal, or abasic-site processing. These processing activities are conditional and are not all conserved in every LigD or required for every break. The scope excludes homologous recombination, single-strand annealing, eukaryotic NHEJ, split-protein implementations and alternative backup-ligase routes.

an NHEJ system that depends on a dedicated DNA ligase, LigD, and the DNA-end-binding protein Ku

Bacteria is the taxonomic envelope of this implementation, not a claim of universal occurrence. The panel samples Pseudomonadota, Actinomycetota and Bacillota. Bacillus LigD lacks PE despite sharing PTHR42705:SF2 with PE-containing proteins, so the phosphatase participant is function- and domain-qualified. Ku can itself catalyze AP-site chemistry in measured exemplars; it is not universally an inert scaffold. Required Ku and fused-LigD roles define the modeled canonical route, not the necessity of those proteins for every end-joining event in each organism. KT2440 Cas9 repair can be LigD-independent, and PA14 backup-route findings are not automatically native PAO1 evidence. Family/PTN provenance is distinct from exact member placement: the KT2440 accessions are absent from the sampled trees. Optional chemistry records capabilities, while substrate-specific necessity and compatible native partners must be supplied by the evidence-backed capacity scenarios. Family membership does not establish compatible native partners, the chemistry of a particular break or that all optional activities occur in one event. Evaluate compatible-end, gap, phosphate-blocked and AP-bearing scenarios with explicit evidence-qualified capability requirements; Boolean core satisfaction alone does not prove repair of each substrate.
