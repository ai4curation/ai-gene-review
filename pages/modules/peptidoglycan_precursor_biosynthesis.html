Peptidoglycan precursor biosynthesis and lipid II export

The conserved bacterial route that converts UDP-N-acetylglucosamine to UDP-MurNAc-pentapeptide, transfers that nucleotide precursor to the undecaprenyl carrier to form lipid I, glycosylates lipid I to form lipid II, and translocates lipid II across the cytoplasmic membrane. The ten steps are present in essentially all peptidoglycan-producing bacteria and are catalysed by single orthologous families throughout. D-Ala-D-Ala synthesis is modelled as a convergent input to MurF, and alternative MurE branches represent meso-diaminopimelate- and L-lysine-containing stem peptides, the variation that distinguishes most Gram-negative from most Gram-positive stem peptides. The module ends at lipid II export and does not include glycan polymerization, peptide cross-linking, amino-acid or carrier supply, carrier recycling, or cell-wall remodeling.

Taxonomic scope. The ten steps are conserved across peptidoglycan-producing bacteria, so the module is written species-neutrally and the organisms named are exemplars, not a scope restriction. Reviewed Escherichia coli K-12 proteins ground the conserved meso-diaminopimelate route step by step, Pseudomonas putida KT2440 representatives preserve traceability to the PSEPK pathway satisfiability audit that first assembled this module, and reviewed Staphylococcus aureus MurE grounds the alternative L-lysine branch. Every UniProt accession asserted here has a complete gene review; no organism was added only to raise a count. Stem-peptide chemistry and the GO terms used. The third stem residue is fixed by MurE and is the one real branch point in the pathway, so it is modelled as a variant set. Downstream of it, GO offers no stem-agnostic term for the MurF, MraY and MurG reactions: GO:0047480, GO:0008963 and GO:0050511 are defined on the L-lysine stem peptide, their counterparts GO:0008766, GO:0051992 and GO:0051991 on the meso-diaminopimelate one, and each pair is a pair of siblings rather than a parent and a child (GO:0016881, GO:0016780 and GO:0008375 respectively are the shared parents). These three steps therefore state the meso-diaminopimelate terms, matching the reviewed exemplars, and each role_description names the sibling that applies in Lys-type organisms. The enzymes themselves are not stem-selective: purified MurF of both Escherichia coli and Staphylococcus aureus accepts either tripeptide (PMID:20659527), which is why one step serves both MurE branches. A stem-agnostic parent for these three activities is raised as a question in the gene reviews rather than asserted here. PANTHER and PAINT grounding. Each family descriptor carries the PANTHER family of its members, taken from the DR PANTHER lines of the members' own UniProt records, with the official family name verbatim from interpro/panther/panther.obo; the curator-facing description of the role stays in preferred_term. Ancestral nodes are PAINT IBD nodes read from the checked-in interpro/panther/<family>/<family>-paint.tsv, one per step, each with the GO term it asserts and the snapshot date of the row. Three cases are deliberately asserted at a broader node than the module step: MraY (PTN000531998, GO:0016780) and MurG (PTN000470555, GO:0016757) have substrate-specific descendant nodes that carry the L-lysine-defined terms, which would mis-state the modelled reaction, and the L-lysine MurE variant has no node of its own at all, because the shared family PTHR23135 does not distinguish third-residue specificity. Both Escherichia coli and Staphylococcus aureus MurE in fact map to the same subfamily, PTHR23135:SF4, whose name refers to a chloroplastic homolog; specificity is set by active-site residues, so no family or subfamily identifier can predict it, and none is asserted for that variant. Why three steps assert no PANTHER identifier. A family identifier in a descriptor is read as the grounding for that step's activity, so it is asserted only where the family review grants the activity at that level. MurC therefore cites the subfamily PTHR43445:SF3 rather than the family, whose other subfamilies hold the Mpl recycling ligases and CapB. MurA and both MurE variants cite no PANTHER identifier at all: the family review leaves GO:0008760 UNRESOLVED at family and subfamily level, because the untested fungal and diatom leaves share subfamily SF1 with canonical MurA, and it scopes both MurE stem-specific terms RESIDUE_DETERMINED, since PTHR23135:SF4 holds meso-diaminopimelate-adding and L-lysine-adding enzymes together. In each case the family is named in the descriptor's description, and the grounding is the PAINT node plus the reviewed representatives, which is what those reviews recommend. Boundary. The module intentionally excludes amino-acid precursor supply and the racemases that make D-alanine and D-glutamate (modelled in d_amino_acid_cell_wall_precursor_supply), undecaprenyl-carrier synthesis and recycling, SEDS/PBP polymerization and cross-linking (modelled in bacterial_rod_complex_peptidoglycan_transpeptidation and bacterial_divisome_peptidoglycan_transpeptidation), and peptidoglycan remodeling and recycling. CANDIDATE ASSESSED, NOT MODELLED: the MurA-centred protein-interaction hub reported for Escherichia coli (PMID:42604454), in which MurA binds the LapB scaffold and the lipid A enzymes LpxA, LpxC, and LpxD as well as the phospholipid enzyme FabZ, is not represented here. It adds no part to this module, since MurA already occupies the enolpyruvyl-transfer step and the other five proteins catalyse no peptidoglycan-precursor reaction, and it cannot be represented as a connection either: connections are scoped to node identifiers within one module document, ModuleConnectionTypeEnum has no physical-association type, and four of the five partners (LapB, LpxA, LpxC, LpxD) are not modelled anywhere in modules/ because there is no lipid A biosynthesis module.
