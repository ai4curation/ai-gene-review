Glycine formation and glycine-serine interconversion

Routes that supply the cellular glycine pool and interconvert glycine with L-serine, as found in fungi, bacteria and animals. Glycine is formed by up to three enzymatically unrelated, pyridoxal 5'-phosphate-dependent reactions: aldol cleavage of L-threonine to glycine and acetaldehyde by threonine aldolase; transamination of glyoxylate with L-alanine by alanine:glyoxylate aminotransferase; and the reversible tetrahydrofolate-dependent serine hydroxymethyltransferase (SHMT) reaction, which converts L-serine and tetrahydrofolate to glycine and 5,10-methylenetetrahydrofolate. Run in reverse, SHMT turns glycine made by the first two reactions into L-serine, giving a serine supply that does not depend on the phosphorylated pathway. Eukaryotes commonly have compartment-specific SHMT isozymes in the cytosol and in the mitochondrial matrix, and the direction of net flux through each depends on nutrient supply. In budding yeast, threonine aldolase is the main glycine source during growth on glucose, while the glucose-repressed glyoxylate transamination route supplies glycine and, through SHMT, serine on non-fermentable carbon sources. The relative weight of the routes differs between lineages; in mammals SHMT is the main source of glycine. The phosphorylated serine pathway, the glycine cleavage system, and downstream folate one-carbon reactions are separate modules.

glycine biosynthetic processGO:0006545

NEW module from five YeastPathways frames (PWY3O-261, GLYSYN-PWY, GLYSYN-THR-PWY, GLYSYN-ALA-PWY, PWY3O-230), each of which is a single reaction or a combination of the same reactions. They are alternative routes into one glycine/serine pool, so they are modelled as an optional variant set of serine-independent glycine routes (threonine aldolase, glyoxylate transamination) followed by the reversible SHMT step with cytosolic and mitochondrial isozyme variants. The phosphorylated serine pathway reactions of PWY3O-261 are in phosphorylated_serine_biosynthesis, which supplies serine to the SHMT step; the glycine cleavage system (glycine_cleavage_system) and the downstream folate one-carbon network (folate_one_carbon_interconversion) are separate. The human-specific serine_biosynthesis_one_carbon module overlaps the SHMT part. YeastCyc corrections: all four catalysts are correct and none is missing. PWY3O-230 lists only SHMT although its own comment names AGX1 as the first step; this module includes it. YeastCyc and its GO-CAM give no compartments; SHM1 is mitochondrial (PMID:8132653), SHM2 and GLY1 are cytosolic, and the AGX1 compartment is unresolved. GO-CAM disagreements: YeastPathways_PWY3O-261 places SHM1 in cytosol, uses only the broad process GO:0009070 rather than GO:0006545 or GO:0006564, and has no causal edges. No PomBase GO-CAM is linked, so S. pombe exemplars are not added. No glycine-specific regulation (Bas1/Gcn4, glycine induction) is modelled as a part.
