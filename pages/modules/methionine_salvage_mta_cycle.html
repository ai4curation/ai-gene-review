L-methionine salvage from 5'-methylthioadenosine (MTA cycle)

The methionine salvage pathway, or 5'-methylthioadenosine (MTA) cycle, regenerates L-methionine from MTA, the by-product of S-adenosylmethionine (SAM) use in polyamine synthesis (and, in plants, ethylene synthesis). The methylthio group of MTA and carbons 2-5 of its ribose become the methionine skeleton, so the cycle recovers reduced sulfur that is costly to assimilate, and it also clears MTA, which inhibits spermidine synthase. MTA is first converted to S-methyl-5-thio-alpha-D-ribose 1-phosphate (MTR-1-P), either directly by MTA phosphorylase (eukaryotes and many bacteria) or by MTA nucleosidase followed by ATP-dependent MTR kinase (many bacteria, plants). MTR-1-P isomerase converts MTR-1-P to the ribulose phosphate, which MTRu-1-P dehydratase converts to the 2,3-diketo phosphate. An enolase and a phosphatase then form the acireductone 1,2-dihydroxy-5-(methylthio)pent-1-en-3-one. Fungi and animals do this with one bifunctional enolase-phosphatase (E1); Bacillus uses a RuBisCO-like enolase (MtnW) and a separate phosphatase (MtnX). An Fe(II)-dependent acireductone dioxygenase then oxidises the acireductone to 4-methylthio-2-oxobutanoate (KMTB) and formate. A final transamination converts KMTB to methionine. This step is redundant and is carried out by broad-specificity aromatic and branched-chain amino acid transaminases, not a dedicated enzyme. The cycle needs dioxygen and is largely cytosolic.

Boundary: begins with 5'-methylthioadenosine and ends with L-methionine. MTA-producing reactions (SAM decarboxylase SPE2, spermidine synthase SPE3, spermine synthase SPE4) and SAM synthesis (SAM1/SAM2), which the YeastPathways superpathway PWY3O-351 adds, belong to the polyamine_metabolism and methionine_cycle modules. MTA export and alternative MTA fates (e.g. 5-methylthioribose excretion in E. coli) are outside the module. YeastCyc corrections: none to gene assignments. The SGD summary lists all five dedicated enzymes (MEU1, MRI1, MDE1, UTR4, ADI1) plus ARO8, ARO9, BAT1, BAT2, consistent with Pirkov et al. 2008 (PMID:18625006), which also excluded the UTR4 homologue YNL010w. Compartment: the GO-CAMs place every step in the cytosol, but BAT1 is the mitochondrial branched-chain transaminase, so its contribution to KMTB transamination presumably occurs in the mitochondrial matrix (KMTB import not established); BAT2, ARO8 and ARO9 are cytosolic. GO-CAM disagreements: (1) the YeastPathways models type the R15-RXN transamination for all four transaminases with GO:0052656 L-isoleucine transaminase activity, which is a different reaction. The pathway step is GO:0010326 L-methionine:oxo-acid transaminase activity (EC 2.6.1.88), used here. (2) PomBase model gomodel:69c59f8a00000584 is titled with GO:0033353 L-methionine cycle, but its content is this MTA salvage cycle. GO:0033353 denotes the SAM/SAH/homocysteine cycle, and GO:0071267 L-methionine salvage is the nearest live branch term for MTA-cycle genes because QuickGO marks GO:0019509 obsolete. GO:0033353 annotations on MTA-cycle genes (e.g. UTR4, ADI1, MDE1, MRI1, MEU1) are cross-branch migrations to fix. (3) The PomBase model has two MTRu-1-P dehydratase activities, mde1 (SPAC20H4.05c, PTHR10640:SF7, the MDE1/APIP orthologue) and mta3 (SPAC9.06c, UniProt Q9UT22, "methylthioribulose-1-phosphate dehydratase-like protein", PTHR10640:SF10). mta3's enabled-by evidence in the model is IBA only (PTN000067541). Only mde1 is used as the S. pombe exemplar here, and whether mta3 is a second active dehydratase or a diverged paralogue is left open. (4) The PomBase model has no transamination step. Lineage variation: the dedicated dioxygenase step needs O2, and some bacteria (e.g. E. coli) lack a complete pathway (PMID:19946895). The nucleosidase/kinase entry and the split MtnW/MtnX enolase-phosphatase are bacterial/plant variants and have no yeast representatives. MTA phosphorylase also releases adenine to purine salvage.
