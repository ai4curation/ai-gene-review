Human histone deacetylation — class I HDAC corepressor complexes (SIN3A-HDAC1/2, CoREST RCOR1-KDM1A-HDAC1/2 and NCoR/SMRT-HDAC3 with its class IIa HDAC4/5/7/9 adaptors), NuRD by reference, and the NAD-dependent histone deacetylases SIRT1, SIRT6 and SIRT7

Removal of acetyl groups from lysine residues in histone tails restores their positive charge and the binding sites they lose to bromodomain readers, and is a principal enzymatic route to transcriptional repression and chromatin compaction. Two chemically distinct enzyme families do it in human cells. The zinc-dependent class I deacetylases HDAC1, HDAC2 and HDAC3 hydrolyse the acetyl-lysine amide to release acetate. They are nearly inactive alone and work inside corepressor complexes whose scaffold subunits activate them and bring them to chromatin: HDAC1 and HDAC2 in the SIN3A complex (with RBBP4/7, SAP30, SUDS3 and SAP18), in the CoREST complex (with the SANT-domain corepressor RCOR1, the demethylase KDM1A, HMG20B and PHF21A) and in NuRD; HDAC3 in the NCoR/SMRT complex, where the deacetylase activation domain of NCOR1 or NCOR2, held by inositol tetrakisphosphate, switches on the enzyme and the WD40 proteins TBL1X/TBL1XR1 and GPS2 complete the complex. The class IIa HDACs HDAC4, HDAC5, HDAC7 and HDAC9 carry a histidine in place of a catalytic tyrosine and are about a thousand-fold less active on acetyl-lysine; they repress MEF2 and other transcription factor targets by bridging them to the NCoR/SMRT-HDAC3 complex. The class III sirtuins SIRT1, SIRT6 and SIRT7 instead transfer the acetyl group to NAD+, releasing nicotinamide and 2''-O-acetyl-ADP-ribose: SIRT1 prefers H4K16 and H3K9, SIRT6 deacetylates H3K9 and H3K56 at telomeres and DNA breaks, and SIRT7 deacetylates H3K18 at promoters it is recruited to.

Boundary decisions. (1) Each complex is a part; HDAC1 and HDAC2 appear in the SIN3A and CoREST parts and, by module_ref, in nurd_complex, which is not remodelled. (2) KDM1A is a CoREST unit only, with no function asserted: its demethylase activity belongs to histone_lysine_demethylation. (3) The class IIa HDACs (HDAC4, HDAC5, HDAC7, HDAC9) are an optional adaptor part beside the NCoR/SMRT-HDAC3 part and are given no deacetylase activity, applying the CLAUDE.md rule that measured activity is not automatically function: the catalytic tyrosine is a histidine, the isolated HDAC4 domain is about 1000-fold less active than class I enzymes, activity in class IIa immunoprecipitates comes from copurifying HDAC3, and reconstitution shows class II HDACs do not contribute to the SMRT/N-CoR-HDAC3 activity (PMID:11804585, PMID:17956988). The HDAC5 gene review marks its histone deacetylase rows as over-annotations; the HDAC4 gene review accepted them, which this module does not follow (its core functions there are corepressor and adaptor activities). (4) The sirtuin part is a ONE_OR_MORE variant set of separate enzymes, not a complex; SIRT1 and SIRT6 each have two annotons for their best-supported histone sites. The eNoSC complex (SIRT1 with NML and SUV39H1) is not modelled because only its SIRT1 activity is deacetylation. (5) The SIN3B complex (SIN3B with PHF12 and MORF4L1) and the larger SIN3A-associated proteins (ARID4A/B, SAP130, BRMS1, SINHCAF) are not parts; only the core SIN3A complex with direct human purification evidence is modelled. (6) Further CoREST-associated proteins (ZNF217, ZMYM2, GSE1, GTF2I) and the paralogues RCOR2/RCOR3 and KDM1B are not parts. (7) DNA-binding repressors that recruit these complexes (MAD-MAX, REST, unliganded nuclear receptors, MEF2) are targeting context, and histones are substrates, not participants. Part 4: class IIa HDACs as adaptors to the NCoR/SMRT-HDAC3 complex (optional)
Class IIa HDACs (HDAC4, HDAC5, HDAC7, HDAC9) bridge transcription factors to NCoR/SMRT-HDAC3Molecular Functionclass_iia_hdac_adaptors

The class IIa HDACs bind MEF2 and other DNA-binding transcription factors through their N-terminal regions and, through their HDAC domain, the NCoR/SMRT-HDAC3 complex, so that they bring the active HDAC3 enzyme to target genes. Signal-dependent phosphorylation and 14-3-3 binding export them from the nucleus and relieve repression. Their own deacetylase domain carries a histidine in place of the catalytic tyrosine and is about a thousand-fold less active than class I enzymes on acetyl-lysine; deacetylase activity measured in their immunoprecipitates comes from associated HDAC3. No histone deacetylase activity is therefore asserted for them, although human GOA carries IDA rows for HDAC4, HDAC5 and HDAC9.

Sirtuins that deacetylate histones (SIRT1, SIRT6, SIRT7)Molecular Functionnad_dependent_histone_deacetylases

The nuclear sirtuins transfer the histone acetyl group to the ADP-ribose of NAD+, releasing nicotinamide and 2''-O-acetyl-ADP-ribose. Each acts largely outside the class I complexes, with distinct site preferences and recruitment: SIRT1 at facultative heterochromatin and in the eNoSC complex at rDNA, SIRT6 at telomeres and DNA double-strand breaks, and SIRT7 at promoters bound by ELK4.
