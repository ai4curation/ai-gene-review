Bacterial L-threonine biosynthesis from L-aspartate

The ordered bacterial route from L-aspartate to L-threonine. Aspartate kinase and aspartate-semialdehyde dehydrogenase form the shared aspartate-family trunk that also supplies lysine biosynthesis. Homoserine dehydrogenase then forms L-homoserine, a branch-point precursor also used for methionine biosynthesis. Homoserine kinase and pyridoxal 5'-phosphate-dependent threonine synthase perform the two threonine-specific reactions. The module stops at L-threonine; downstream threonine degradation and conversion to 2-oxobutanoate for isoleucine synthesis are outside its boundary.
