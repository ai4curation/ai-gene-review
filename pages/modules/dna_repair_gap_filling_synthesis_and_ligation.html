Human DNA repair gap filling — RFC (or CTF18-RFC) loading of PCNA, gap-filling synthesis by Pol delta, Pol epsilon or Pol kappa, and nick sealing by LIG1 or LIG3-XRCC1, the shared late steps of nucleotide excision repair and mismatch repair

Excision repair pathways remove a damaged or mismatched stretch of one DNA strand and leave a single-stranded gap whose 3'-OH primer terminus faces an intact template strand. The last two steps, gap-filling DNA synthesis and nick sealing, use the same replication proteins in nucleotide excision repair (NER) and in mismatch repair (MMR). The clamp loader replication factor C (RFC, RFC1-RFC5) binds the primer terminus with ATP, opens the homotrimeric sliding clamp PCNA and closes it around the DNA. PCNA then tethers a DNA polymerase to the template so that it synthesises the patch processively. In MMR the polymerase is DNA polymerase delta (POLD1-POLD4), recruited through its POLD3 subunit, and the reaction has been reconstituted with purified human proteins. In NER, which fills a gap of about 30 nucleotides, three polymerases share the work in human cells: Pol delta together with the Y-family polymerase kappa for about half of repair synthesis, and DNA polymerase epsilon (POLE, POLE2-POLE4), loaded through the alternative clamp loader CTF18-RFC, for the rest. The remaining nick, between the 3' end of the new patch and the 5'-phosphate of the original strand, is sealed by an ATP-dependent DNA ligase that forms an enzyme-AMP intermediate: DNA ligase 1 (LIG1) in both pathways, and in NER also the DNA ligase III alpha-XRCC1 complex. Damage or mismatch recognition, incision and excision belong to the parent pathways and are outside this module, as are base excision repair gap filling by DNA polymerase beta, translesion synthesis, and Okazaki fragment maturation.
