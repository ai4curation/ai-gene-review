Ciliary transition zone components in Drosophila melanogaster

The transition zone is the region at the base of the cilium, just above the basal body, where Y-shaped links connect the axoneme to the ciliary membrane and form a diffusion barrier that controls which proteins enter the cilium. Its components include the MKS module, a membrane-associated complex of B9-domain proteins (B9d1, B9d2, Mks1), Cc2d2a and Tectonic, and Cep290, which links the barrier to the axoneme. In Drosophila, transition zone proteins act in the cilia of sensory neurons and in the long transition-zone-like region of spermatocyte cilia. Cep290, Chibby, Dilatory (Cep131) and Unc localize to the transition zone or basal body and are needed to assemble it and the cilium.
