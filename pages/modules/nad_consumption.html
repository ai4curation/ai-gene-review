NAD+ consumption — sirtuin deacylation, PARP/ARTD poly- and mono-ADP-ribosylation and CD38/BST1 NAD+ glycohydrolysis, with PARG and macrodomain reversal; SIRT1-7/PARP1-4/PARP6/PARP8-12/PARP14-16/TIPARP/TNKS/TNKS2/CD38/BST1/PARG/MACROD1/MACROD2/OARD1

Besides serving as a hydride carrier in redox reactions, NAD+ is a substrate that is broken down by three classes of signalling enzymes. Each cleaves the bond between nicotinamide and ribose, releases free nicotinamide and uses the ADP-ribose moiety. The sirtuins (SIRT1-7) are NAD+-dependent protein deacylases. They transfer an acetyl group, or another acyl group such as succinyl, malonyl, glutaryl, lipoyl or a long-chain fatty acyl, from a protein lysine to the ADP-ribose of NAD+, producing nicotinamide and 2''-O-acyl-ADP-ribose. SIRT1, SIRT6 and SIRT7 act in the nucleus, SIRT2 mainly in the cytoplasm, and SIRT3, SIRT4 and SIRT5 in mitochondria. SIRT5 is chiefly a desuccinylase, demalonylase and deglutarylase, SIRT4 a lipoamidase, and SIRT4 and SIRT6 also mono-ADP-ribosylate proteins. The PARP (ARTD) family of ADP-ribosyltransferases transfers ADP-ribose from NAD+ to protein acceptor residues. PARP1, PARP2 and the tankyrases TNKS and TNKS2 build poly(ADP-ribose) chains; DNA-break-activated PARP1, with its cofactor HPF1, is the largest NAD+ consumer after genotoxic stress. Most other PARPs (PARP3, PARP4, PARP6, PARP8, PARP10-12, PARP14-16 and TIPARP/PARP7) attach a single ADP-ribose. The ectoenzymes CD38 and BST1 (CD157) are NAD+ glycohydrolases and ADP-ribosyl cyclases. They convert NAD+ mostly to ADP-ribose and partly to the calcium-mobilising messenger cyclic ADP-ribose, which they also hydrolyse. Protein ADP-ribosylation is reversed by PAR glycohydrolase (PARG), which cleaves PAR chains, and by the macrodomain hydrolases MACROD1, MACROD2 and OARD1 (TARG1), which remove the last ADP-ribose from glutamate and aspartate residues. The nicotinamide released by all of these reactions is recycled to NAD+ by the NAMPT salvage pathway. Because the consumers share a limited NAD+ pool, strong PARP1 or CD38 activity lowers NAD+ and with it sirtuin activity, coupling DNA damage, inflammation and ageing to metabolic signalling.

Sirtuin NAD+-dependent deacylation and ADP-ribosylationMolecular Functionsirtuin_nad_consumption

Class III protein deacylases (sirtuins 1-7) share a conserved NAD+-binding catalytic core. They cleave the nicotinamide-ribosyl bond of NAD+ and transfer the protein acyl group to the ADP-ribose, so every deacylation consumes one NAD+ and yields nicotinamide and 2''-O-acyl-ADP-ribose. Hydrolytic removal of protein-linked ADP-riboseMolecular Functionadp_ribose_removal

ADP-ribosylation is reversible. PAR glycohydrolase (PARG) hydrolyses the ribose-ribose bonds of poly(ADP-ribose), releasing free ADP-ribose, but cannot remove the proximal ADP-ribose attached to the protein. The macrodomain hydrolases MACROD1, MACROD2 and OARD1 (TARG1) remove mono-ADP-ribose from glutamate and aspartate residues, again releasing ADP-ribose. ADP-ribose made here and by CD38/BST1 is cleaved further by NUDIX hydrolases, outside this module.
