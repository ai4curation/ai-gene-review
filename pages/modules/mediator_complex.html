Human Mediator complex — the 26-subunit core Mediator (head, middle and tail modules on the MED14 scaffold) as the coactivator bridging DNA-bound activators to RNA polymerase II, and the dissociable CDK8 kinase module (CDK8 or CDK19, cyclin C, MED12 or MED12L, MED13 or MED13L)

Mediator is the large multisubunit coactivator through which DNA-binding transcription factors communicate with RNA polymerase II (Pol II). Human core Mediator has 26 subunits arranged in head, middle and tail modules held together by the central scaffold subunit MED14. Activation domains of transcription factors contact flexibly tethered domains, chiefly in the tail (MED15, MED16, MED23, MED24, MED25) and in MED1 of the middle module, which carries the LxxLL boxes that bind ligand-activated nuclear receptors. The head and middle modules bind Pol II and its C-terminal domain (CTD), stabilise the preinitiation complex and present the CTD to the TFIIH kinase CDK7, so that Mediator stimulates both basal and activated transcription and CTD phosphorylation. A fourth, dissociable module, the CDK8 kinase module (CKM), consists of the kinase CDK8 or its paralogue CDK19, cyclin C, MED12 or MED12L and MED13 or MED13L. The CKM binds core Mediator through MED13 and, in doing so, precludes Pol II binding, so that CKM association acts as a switch that restrains initiation and reinitiation; separately, CDK8 and CDK19, activated by cyclin C and MED12, phosphorylate the Pol II CTD, TFIIH, histone H3 and transcription factors such as STAT1 and the Notch intracellular domain, and CDK8-Mediator can also promote elongation at specific genes.

RGD annotates all 33 Mediator genes (the HGNC Mediator group) directly to PW:0000127 and none to its initiation, elongation or termination children, so PW:0000127 is the only PW evidence source; PW:0001274 (initiation) is named here only. The diagram draws Mediator as positively affecting Pol II initiation, elongation and termination, 3'-end processing, splicing and chromatin modification; this module models the complex and its coactivator and kinase activities, not each of those downstream effects. All 33 RGD genes are participants (26 core subunits, 7 CKM subunits); no gene outside the RGD list is added. UniProt accessions and recommended names are from UniProt REST (November 2026); molecular functions are from each protein's experimental human GOA rows in QuickGO (November 2026). Only MED13L has a gene review in this repository; its core function (transcription coregulator activity within the CKM, PMID:22249253) is consistent with treating MED13L as a unit that contributes to the complex. The structure follows modules/dmel_mediator_complex.yaml (structure only; no Drosophila evidence is used). Boundary and modelling decisions. (1) Coactivator activity on the complex. The transcription coactivator activity of core Mediator is placed on the complex annoton and not on individual subunits: the IDA coactivator and coregulator rows on thirteen subunits all derive from assays of purified Mediator complexes or from single-subunit overexpression, so subunits contribute to the activity at most. MED1 (nuclear receptor binding) and MED25 (nuclear retinoic acid receptor binding) carry their own activator-binding functions because these were assayed on the isolated subunits. These are binding terms, not bare protein binding, and are the only subunit-level functions in the core. (2) Head, middle and tail are recorded in each unit's role, not as separate nodes. GO has no cellular-component terms for the human head, middle or tail modules, the coactivator activity is a property of the whole core, and the module assignments of the metazoan-specific subunits MED27, MED28, MED29 and MED30 (head or upper tail) and the position of MED26 differ between structures, so a single core complex annoton with labelled units is used, as in the Drosophila analogue. The assignments follow the human Mediator-PIC structures (PMID:33707221, PMID:33958484). (3) The CDK8 kinase module is a separate part with the one catalytic annoton: protein serine/threonine kinase activity, on the CDK8 and CDK19 units and on the complex, with cyclin C carrying cyclin-dependent kinase regulator activity. CDK8 is given protein serine/threonine kinase activity although its experimental human GOA row is the parent term protein kinase activity (IMP, PMID:30905399); this is a curator-authored refinement from the serine substrates reported in PMID:30905399 and PMID:19047373 and from the CDK19 IDA row, and is the only function in the module not taken directly from an experimental GOA row. RNA polymerase II CTD heptapeptide repeat kinase activity, used for Cdk8 in the Drosophila module, is not used: the CTD is one of several CDK8 substrates and no human GOA row asserts it. (4) Paralogues. CDK8/CDK19, MED12/MED12L and MED13/MED13L are alternative subunits of distinct CKM assemblies (RGD: their roles are not redundant). They are listed together in one CKM descriptor with roles stating the alternatives, rather than as three variant sets, because the paralogue combinations present in cells are not established for all eight possibilities; MED12L has only IBA support for Mediator membership in GOA, and its inclusion rests on RGD and HGNC. (5) The CKM-core connection is NEGATIVELY_REGULATES, following the MED13-dependent switch that precludes Pol II binding (PMID:19240132). The kinase-dependent positive roles (for example CDK8-Mediator recruitment of the super elongation complex, PMID:23746844) are recorded in the CKM evidence, not as a second edge. (6) Kinase substrates are not participants: the Pol II CTD, TFIIH, histone H3, STAT1 and the Notch intracellular domain are acted on by the CKM and belong to their own modules. (7) Out of scope, modelled in sibling modules of this tranche: Pol II and the general transcription factors including TFIIH and its CDK7 kinase (rna_polymerase_ii_core_and_initiation), elongation factors including the super elongation complex, P-TEFb and DSIF/NELF (rna_polymerase_ii_elongation_and_termination), capping (pre_mrna_capping) and 3'-end processing (pre_mrna_3prime_cleavage_polyadenylation). SAGA and ATAC, the other coactivators drawn on the RGD diagram, belong to a later histone-modification tranche and are not modelled. Activators that bind Mediator (nuclear receptors, p53, VP16, SREBP and others) are upstream inputs, not parts. CDK3, which cyclin C also activates (PMID:25344755), is not a Mediator subunit and is excluded. GO-CAM links. In gocams/index.tsv only gomodel:696022cd00003836 (Regulation of NOTCH1 by the Cyclin C-CDK19 pair, Human) contains Mediator subunits: CCNC with cyclin-dependent protein serine/threonine kinase regulator activity directly positively regulating CDK19 protein serine/threonine kinase activity, which negatively regulates NOTCH1 transcription coactivator activity. Both activities are pinned to the CKM kinase annoton; their functions match the module's unit functions. No production model contains a core Mediator subunit, CDK8, MED12, MED12L, MED13 or MED13L. The one catalytic activity of Mediator. CDK19 carries an IDA row for protein serine/threonine kinase activity and cyclin C an IDA row for cyclin-dependent protein serine/threonine kinase regulator activity (PMID:25344755); CDK8 carries an IMP row for protein kinase activity (PMID:30905399, loss of STAT1 Ser727 phosphorylation with patient substitutions). MED12 activates CDK8 (PMID:19047373) but has no experimental human molecular function of its own beyond complex-level coactivator rows, so it is a unit without a function; MED13 and the paralogues MED12L and MED13L likewise. Each CKM contains one member of each paralogue pair (CDK8 or CDK19, MED12 or MED12L, MED13 or MED13L); listing all seven units in one descriptor is a compression of these alternative assemblies.
