L-methionine salvage pathway in Drosophila melanogaster

The methionine salvage pathway recycles the methylthio group of 5'-methylthioadenosine (MTA), a by-product of polyamine synthesis, back into methionine. MTA phosphorylase (Mtap) cleaves MTA to adenine and 5-methylthioribose-1-phosphate, which is isomerised to methylthioribulose-1-phosphate (Mri1) and dehydrated to a diketo phosphate (CG11134). The bifunctional enolase-phosphatase Enoph converts this to acireductone, which acireductone dioxygenase (Adi1) oxidises to 4-methylthio-2-oxobutanoate. A final transamination regenerates methionine. As in other animals, the pathway in flies lacks the methylthioribose kinase step used by bacteria and plants, because MTA is cleaved phosphorolytically.

Boundary. The final transamination of 4-methylthio-2-oxobutanoate to methionine is carried out by broad-specificity aminotransferases that FlyBase does not include in this group, so it is not modelled. Adi1 is given the iron-dependent dioxygenase activity, which yields the methionine precursor; the nickel-dependent activity that yields methylthiopropionate is a side reaction. Molecular functions follow orthology to the characterized enzymes of other organisms; except for Mtap, the fly enzymes have not been assayed directly.
