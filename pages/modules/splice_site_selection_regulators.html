Human splice-site selection regulators: SR and SR-like enhancer-binding activators, hnRNP and SR-like silencer-binding repressors, and cell-type-specific splicing regulators acting on early (E/A) spliceosome assembly

Most human exons are recognised only with the help of auxiliary RNA-binding proteins that read short cis-acting elements in the pre-mRNA. Serine/arginine-rich (SR) proteins and the SR-like TRA2 proteins bind exonic splicing enhancers through their RNA recognition motifs and use their RS domains to recruit and stabilise U1 snRNP at the 5' splice site and U2AF at the 3' splice site, promoting exon definition and formation of the early (E) and prespliceosome (A) complexes. Heterogeneous nuclear ribonucleoproteins (hnRNPs) such as hnRNP A1, hnRNP C, hnRNP H, hnRNP L and PTBP1 bind exonic and intronic splicing silencers or polypyrimidine tracts and repress exon use by blocking splice-site recognition, competing with U2AF65, spreading cooperatively along the exon or looping out exons; the SR-like proteins SRSF10 and SRSF12 antagonise authentic SR proteins. Cell-type-specific regulators (PTBP2, MBNL1, QKI, TIAL1 and SRRM4) bind their own motifs near regulated exons and activate or repress them according to position. The balance of these activators and repressors on each pre-mRNA determines which splice sites are used and so the pattern of alternative splicing.
