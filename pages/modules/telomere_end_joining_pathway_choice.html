NBS1 phosphorylation switch for end-joining choice at dysfunctional telomeres

The phosphorylation state of NBS1 (NBN) at a CDK site in its TRF2-binding motif (human S432, mouse S433) decides how a mammalian telomere is joined once it loses protection. CDK2 phosphorylates the site in S/G2 and PP1-alpha removes the phosphate in G1. Dephosphorylated NBS1 docks on the TRFH domain of TRF2, which keeps MRN away from functional telomere ends. At telomeres that have lost TRF2, phospho-NBS1 binds MRE11, activates ATM and drives classical, DNA ligase IV-dependent NHEJ. At telomeres that have lost POT1-TPP1, TRF2-bound dephospho-NBS1 recruits EXO1 for 5' resection, and the ends are joined by the PARP1- and ligase III-dependent alternative (microhomology-mediated) pathway.

We document that telomere fusions resulting from inhibition of the telomere-protective factor TRF2 are generated by DNA ligase IV-dependent nonhomologous end joining (NHEJ).
Alternative NHEJ via TRF2-bound dephospho-NBS1, EXO1, PARP1 and ligase III (telomeres lacking POT1-TPP1)Biological Processalt_nhej_route

TRF2-bound dephosphorylated NBS1 recruits EXO1 to telomeres lacking POT1-TPP1 to extend 5' resection. The single-stranded ends are joined by the PARP1- and ligase III-dependent, Ku-repressed alternative (microhomology-mediated) pathway. Fusions also need ATR, which is not modelled as a step here.

Non-phosphorylatable NBS1 raises fusions after POT1-TPP1 loss.
