De novo biotin biosynthesis (pimelate-thioester supply and BioF-BioA-BioD-BioB ring assembly)

De novo synthesis of biotin in bacteria, fungi and plants. A seven-carbon pimelate thioester (pimeloyl-ACP or pimeloyl-CoA) is first made by a lineage-specific route, and four conserved reactions then assemble the two rings of biotin. In many bacteria, BioC methylates malonyl-ACP, the shared fatty-acid-synthase reactions extend the masked intermediate to pimeloyl-ACP methyl ester, and BioH removes the methyl group. Filamentous fungi make pimeloyl-CoA by peroxisomal beta-oxidation, and Saccharomyces yeasts need the oxygen-dependent BIO1 protein, whose reaction is not known. The pimelate thioester is condensed with L-alanine to 8-amino-7-oxononanoate (KAPA) by a PLP-dependent KAPA synthase: bacterial BioF, a peroxisomal BioF in filamentous fungi, or, in Saccharomyces, a step that requires the BIO6 protein, a paralogue of the BioA aminotransferase. BioA then transfers an amino group from S-adenosylmethionine to give 7,8-diaminononanoate (DAPA), BioD closes the ureido ring with CO2 and ATP to give dethiobiotin, and the radical-SAM enzyme BioB inserts sulfur to form biotin. In fungi BioB is mitochondrial, and in filamentous fungi and plants BioD and BioA are fused in one protein. Biotin attachment to carboxylases, biotin uptake and regulation are outside the module.

biotin biosynthetic processGO:0009102

Alignment with YeastPathways BIOTIN-SYNTHESIS-PWY and the PomBase model 67c10cc400002026. The module now models the pimelate-thioester supply and the KAPA-forming step as variant sets, because these are the steps that differ between bacteria, filamentous fungi and Saccharomyces; the BioA, BioD and BioB steps are conserved and only gain fungal exemplars. The Saccharomyces route was rebuilt after loss of the ancestral pathway: BIO1 arose by duplication of the PhyH-family gene YJR154W and BIO6 by duplication of the BioA gene BIO3 (PMID:18073433). Many S. cerevisiae strains, including S288C, lack BIO1 and BIO6 and are biotin auxotrophs; the BIO1 and BIO6 exemplars are therefore from strain A364a. BIO5 (KAPA/DAPA uptake), VHT1 (biotin uptake), BPL1 biotin-protein ligase, BirA-type regulation and the human biotin cycle (modules/biotin_cycle.yaml) are outside the module. S. pombe has no BioF, BioA or BioD family member and keeps only bio2, so it can make biotin only from imported dethiobiotin. Compartments: BIO2 and A. nidulans BioB are mitochondrial, A. nidulans BioF is peroxisomal, and UniProt also places A. nidulans BioDA in the mitochondrial matrix by similarity; the SGD GO-CAM places all yeast activities in cytosol. The bifunctional BioDA architecture is noted at the BioA and BioD steps rather than as a separate variant set, because the reactions and their order are unchanged. Molecular functions are attached only to leaf annotons. GO:0102130 retains its official label, malonyl-CoA methyltransferase activity, while its definition and RHEA:17105 grounding specify the physiological malonyl-ACP reaction modeled here. BioC uses NCBIfam:TIGR02072 because its current PANTHER subfamily is mislabeled as a mitochondrial arginine hydroxylase. The BioA family is now asserted at PTHR42684 because the fungal BIO3/BioDA proteins are in a different subfamily (SF3) from the bacterial exemplars (SF17).
