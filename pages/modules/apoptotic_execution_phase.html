Mammalian apoptotic execution: proteolysis, ICAD/CAD and contextual nuclear branches

Mammalian executioner caspases cleave distinct sets of cellular substrates. CASP3 cleavage of ICAD relieves inhibition of the CAD nuclease after ICAD-supported folding, enabling chromatin DNA fragmentation; mouse CASP7 provides a supported alternative ICAD-cleaving activity. XIAP and mitochondrial SMAC regulate execution in a cell-dependent manner. CASP6 proteolysis, Acinus-associated nuclear remodeling and AIF chromatin engagement are separately qualified branches, not an obligatory sequence shared by every dying cell. The template starts downstream of initiation and excludes a complete substrate inventory and phagocyte-mediated disposal.

Human and mouse exemplars ground a mammalian template. Part numbering is organizational, not temporal. A required CASP3/CASP7 variant set encodes at least one principal executioner contribution; neither protease is individually obligatory. CASP6, regulators and nuclear branches are optional additions, and their optional flags do not assert dispensability within every assay. AIF-associated caspase-independent death alone is outside this caspase-mediated implementation. ICAD-dependent folding and disinhibition are internal requirements of the represented CAD route, while CAD-mediated DNA fragmentation is not required for all apoptotic death. Upstream caspase activation and AIF release mechanisms are boundary inputs.
