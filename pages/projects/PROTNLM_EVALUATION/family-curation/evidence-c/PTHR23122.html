This family consists of the p55 subfamily of membrane-associated guanylate kinases (MAGUKs), which includes the membrane protein, palmitoylated (MPP) proteins 1-7 (such as PALS1 and PALS2), as well as the peripheral plasma membrane protein CASK and its invertebrate homologs like LIN-2. MAGUKs are scaffolding proteins that play crucial roles in the assembly of multiprotein complexes at regions of cell-cell contact, such as epithelial tight junctions and neuronal synapses [[cite:PUB00075027], [cite:PUB00050845]]. Members of this family share a conserved core architecture consisting of a PDZ domain, an SH3 domain, and a catalytically inactive guanylate kinase-like (GK) domain [[cite:PUB00026723], [cite:PUB00075264]]. In the MPP proteins, this core is often preceded by one or two L27 domains that mediate homo- and heterodimerisation [[cite:PUB00035412], [cite:PUB00166510]], while in CASK and LIN-2, the core is preceded by an N-terminal calcium/calmodulin-dependent protein kinase (CaMK) domain [[cite:PUB00128518], [cite:PUB00050845]]. The PDZ-SH3-GK tandem forms a structural supramodule that binds to the cytoplasmic tails of transmembrane proteins, such as Crumbs (Crb) and neurexins, regulating cell polarity, junctional integrity, and synaptic function [[cite:PUB00075264], [cite:PUB00166511]]. For example, PALS1 (MPP5) links the Crumbs complex to other polarity proteins [[cite:PUB00156450]], while CASK interacts with neurexins and syndecans to mediate synaptic transmembrane protein anchoring and ion channel trafficking [[cite:PUB00036131], [cite:PUB00050845]].
