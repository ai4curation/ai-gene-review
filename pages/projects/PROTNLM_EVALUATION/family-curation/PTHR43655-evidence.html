The ATP-dependent zinc metalloprotease family, characterized by the presence of peptidase M41 and AAA ATPase domains, is involved in the quality control, assembly, and regulation of mitochondrial and membrane proteins. Members of this family exhibit processive, ATP-dependent metallopeptidase activity, targeting both cytoplasmic and membrane proteins for degradation. They play crucial roles in the maintenance of mitochondrial function, including the formation and regulation of the mitochondrial permeability transition pore, the turnover of misfolded or mistranslated polypeptides, and the assembly of mitochondrial respiratory chain complexes. Some family members are essential for neuron development, mediating the degradation of specific mitochondrial proteins to promote efficient assembly of protein complexes. This family also contributes to the proteolytic maturation of other mitochondrial proteases and is involved in the regulation of mitochondrial dynamics through the processing of key proteins like OPA1.
