This family of proteins includes sentrin-specific proteases that are involved in the post-translational modification of proteins through the process of SUMOylation, where small ubiquitin-like modifier (SUMO) proteins are attached to target proteins. Members of this family are responsible for the cleavage of SUMO2 and SUMO3 from substrates, a process known as desumoylation. They play a crucial role in regulating various cellular processes, including DNA repair, transcriptional activation, and cell cycle progression. Specifically, they act as regulators of the cGAS-STING pathway by desumoylating CGAS, which enhances its DNA-binding activity and subsequent activation. Additionally, they are involved in chromosome alignment and spindle assembly by modulating the kinetochore complex and protecting certain proteins from ubiquitin-mediated degradation.
