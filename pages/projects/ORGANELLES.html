Organelles

Bottom line: this project sets how reviews should treat cellular-component
(CC) annotations to organelles. The first question was whether an annotation to
a root organelle term (GO:0005739 mitochondrion, GO:0005783 endoplasmic reticulum, GO:0005777 peroxisome, ...) is ever informative for a
membrane-bounded organelle, given that every protein in one sits either in a
membrane or in a lumen. The decision (2026-10-16) is that the organelle is
informative, and the call depends on the evidence. A root term from an
experiment that could not resolve the sub-compartment is ACCEPTed. A root term
from synthesis (IBA, IC, IEA and the other propagation codes) is a red flag
that the review must examine. Either way, a review should boldly propose the
membrane, lumen or matrix term when the gene has none and several lines of
evidence (a membrane domain, a targeting signal, a known function) point to
one. The red-flag worklist is
root_organelle_red_flags.md. In the
2026-10-18 run it lists 1,081 synthesis rows for mitochondrion that were not
REMOVEd, 283 of them in reviews that give the gene no mitochondrial
sub-compartment.

The question

Is it ever informative to annotate at the root organelle term, e.g.
mitochondrion? Don't you always know if it's membrane or lumen, at least for
membrane-bounded organelles?

The topology argument is sound. A membrane-bounded organelle splits cleanly
into its membrane(s) and the aqueous space(s) they enclose, and GO models this:
mitochondrial outer membrane, mitochondrial intermembrane space,
mitochondrial inner membrane and mitochondrial matrix are all part_of
mitochondrion. A protein in the mitochondrion is in at least one of them.

Whether you know which one is a separate question, about evidence rather than
biology. Below are the cases where the root may be the honest answer, from most
to least defensible.

  1. The assay cannot resolve the sub-compartment. Organelle proteomics
    (HDA/HTP, e.g. purified-mitochondria mass spectrometry), light-microscopy
    co-localisation with a whole-organelle marker, and GFP-fusion screens report
    "in the organelle" and nothing more. GO practice is to annotate to the
    precision of the evidence, so for that row the root is correct. What we
    still have to decide is whether the review should leave it there when
    other knowledge (a cleavable matrix-targeting sequence, a TM segment, the
    known topology of the pathway) settles the question.
  2. The protein is in more than one sub-compartment. Examples are proteins
    spanning the inner and outer membranes (contact-site and import-supercomplex
    components), proteins dual-targeted to matrix and intermembrane space, and
    proteins that change compartment as they are imported. The root then
    summarises a union. The alternative is to list each sub-compartment, which
    loses nothing.
  3. Peripheral association with the cytosolic face. A cytosolic protein
    recruited to the outer membrane (mitophagy adaptors, innate-immunity
    signalling proteins on stressed mitochondria) is often annotated to
    mitochondrion. The precise place is the outer membrane (cytosolic side).
    GO's intrinsic-component terms are obsolete, and the membrane terms do not
    encode sidedness, so curators may avoid a membrane term to avoid implying
    that the protein is integral to it.
  4. The organelle is a series, not a single compartment. For endosome
    (early, late, recycling, multivesicular body), vacuole and autophagosome
    (phagophore, autophagosome, autolysosome) the unresolved dimension is often
    the maturation stage, not membrane versus lumen.
  5. The nucleus is a special case. It is membrane-bounded, but nearly all
    nuclear proteins sit in the lumen, and what matters is the internal
    structure (chromatin, nucleolus, speckles, nuclear pore), not membrane
    versus lumen. For a transcription factor the useful refinement is chromatin
    (or nucleoplasm as a fallback), not the envelope.

Cases 2 and 3 give no reason to keep the root term as a summary: there is a
precise answer and GO can say it. Case 1 is the real reason root terms exist.
It concerns evidence, so it belongs on evidence rows. Cases 4 and 5 are
separate questions that should get their own sections later.

What the survey showed (first run, 2026-10-16; counts in these bullets are
from that run, and the proportions are the same in the later one):

  • When a sub-compartment is known, the root is still ACCEPTed. For
    mitochondrial genes that also have a sub-compartment, 1,576 of 2,134 root
    rows are ACCEPT, 438 KEEP_AS_NON_CORE, and only 43 MODIFY. No review uses
    TOO_GENERAL for a root organelle row, and under the policy below none should.
    The pattern is the same for nucleus (2,576 ACCEPT) and ER (694 ACCEPT).
  • Membrane and lumen split as expected. The mitochondrial genes divide into
    membrane (284), lumen (239) and both (108). ER genes are overwhelmingly
    membrane (310 of 398), Golgi genes are membrane or "other" (mostly
    sub-cisternae and vesicles), and nucleus genes are lumen or other (chromatin).
    This fits the topology argument.
  • Rows with only the root term are mostly high-throughput or electronic.
    Of the 664 mitochondrial rows with only the root term, 180 are HDA, 148 IEA
    and 90 IBA, and 97 are IDA. Many look like case 1. Some look like plain
    curation gaps, where a known matrix enzyme or tRNA synthetase has no matrix
    term (candidates to check: AARS2, CLYBL, PYCR2, MMADHC). Others look like
    cases 2 and 3: proteins with two locations, or proteins recruited to
    mitochondria under stress (candidates: AMACR, IRF3, RIPK1, NLRP3, PARP1).
  • Chloroplast is mostly predictions. 102 of 144 rows are IEA or ISM, and
    26 have already been REMOVEd. The plastid question is mostly about targeting
    predictions, not sub-compartments.

These are counts, not judgments. The "candidates" above are genes to examine,
not findings.

Policy

Decided 2026-10-16, revised 2026-10-18 (add the sub-compartment alongside the
root rather than substituting it). It applies to the first-pass organelles (mitochondrion, ER,
Golgi apparatus, peroxisome, lysosome); the nucleus, the series organelles and
plastids come later.

  1. The root organelle term is informative. Never use TOO_GENERAL for it.
    Knowing that a protein is in the mitochondrion is a real claim even when a
    sub-compartment is also known. The term is not made redundant by its
    descendants in the way that, say, catalytic activity is.
  2. Direct evidence (IDA, EXP, IMP, HDA, HTP, ...): if the assay cannot
    resolve the sub-compartment (organelle proteomics, whole-organelle
    co-staining, GFP fusion), the row is ACCEPT (or KEEP_AS_NON_CORE if
    the location is peripheral to the gene's function). It says what the
    experiment showed. Use MODIFY only if the paper itself resolved a
    sub-compartment and the curator stopped short.
  3. Synthesis (IBA, IC, IEA, ISS, ISO, ISA, ISM, RCA, ...): a red flag.
    Examine the row rather than accepting it by default. A propagation or an
    inference has no assay limit to excuse the coarse term. Either the source
    knew the sub-compartment and the term lost it, or the source did not know
    the location at all. For IBA, check what the PAINT node's descendants
    actually show. For IEA, check the mapping (UniProt subcellular-location
    keyword, InterPro2GO, ARBA) and whether the gene really has the feature
    the mapping assumes. If the organelle claim is right, the row is ACCEPT
    (or KEEP_AS_NON_CORE) with a reason that records the check, even when
    the source could not see the sub-compartment: the row's claim is the
    organelle, and that claim is true. If the organelle itself is not
    supported, the row is MARK_AS_OVER_ANNOTATED or REMOVE. The
    sub-compartment never replaces the root on such a row; it is added under
    rule 4.
  4. Be bold about the sub-compartment. If the gene has no annotation to a
    sub-compartment of the organelle and several lines of evidence agree, add
    one as a NEW row (usually IC), alongside the root rows. Lines of evidence include
    predicted TM segments or a membrane anchor, a cleavable matrix-targeting
    sequence or a PTS1/PTS2, an ER signal peptide with or without a retention
    motif, and a function that only makes sense on one side (a matrix TCA
    enzyme, an inner-membrane carrier). Record the lines of evidence in
    review.reason. If they disagree or there is only one, stay at the root.
  5. Add, don't substitute. This follows GO's own practice for matrix
    proteins with sub-mitochondrial evidence. DARS2 and MMUT carry
    GO:0005759 mitochondrial matrix and keep their mitochondrion rows
    (IBA, IDA, HTP, TAS) alongside it. It also keeps the review judging claims
    rather than evidence codes: every row on the root term gets the same
    kind of action, so the inconsistent-review-actions check needs no
    exception. (A first version of the pilot MODIFYed the synthesis rows and
    added a validator exemption for that split; both were dropped in review
    of PR #5208.) For the IEA rows, the lasting fix is upstream: a UniProt
    location keyword of SL-0170 (Mitochondrion matrix) instead of SL-0173
    (Mitochondrion) maps to the matrix term directly.
  6. core_functions.locations should name the sub-compartment where it is
    known, and may also carry the root if the organelle-level claim is
    independently supported.

Open questions

  • Fix the IEA rows upstream. The root-only IEA rows on AARS2, CLYBL and
    MMADHC come from UniProt subcellular-location keyword SL-0173
    (Mitochondrion). Genes whose entries use SL-0170 (Mitochondrion matrix),
    such as DARS2, TARS2 and MMUT, get GO:0005759 automatically. A UniProt
    feedback request to change the keyword would fix the IEA row at its source,
    and the same applies to much of the 141 root-only IEA rows in the worklist.
  • Should validation warn about root-only synthesis rows that are ACCEPTed
    with no reason that mentions the sub-compartment? Or should
    core_functions.locations warn when it uses only a root term? Both checks
    are mechanical, using the script's ancestor test.
  • How should we say "peripheral, cytosolic face" now that intrinsic-component
    terms are obsolete? Is the bare membrane term enough, or is this a
    proposed_new_terms or GO-ticket question?
  • For IBA red flags with a consistent pattern (a PAINT node at the root term
    whose experimental descendants all show the same sub-compartment), should
    we raise this with PAINT rather than fixing gene by gene?
  • Is there GO consortium guidance on CC annotation granularity to cite in
    support of this policy?

NOTES

2026-10-18 (MMADHC core-function location)

Asked OpenScientist whether MMADHC's mitochondrial pool does part of the core
AdoCbl-supply job in the matrix (report in
genes/human/MMADHC/MMADHC-hypotheses/mmadhc-mitochondrial-pool-adocbl/). The
verdict was "partially supported": the matrix location is well supported, but
no matrix-side partner or step has been shown, and a cytosolic branch-point
model fits the data equally well. A cached mouse paper (PMID:41411131), which
the report did not use, ties mitochondrial Mmadhc accumulation to more AdoCbl.
So the pool matters, but where its step happens is unknown. Outcome: the matrix
stays as the NEW location row but is removed from the core function's
locations; the mitochondrion rows stay non-core; the open question is a
knowledge gap with a compartment-restricted rescue experiment. This is a useful
general point for the worklist: a sub-compartment can be a well-supported
location without being where the gene's core activity is shown to happen.

2026-10-18 (revision: add, don't substitute)

Agreed with cjm after the PR #5208 review. The pilot's IBA/IEA mitochondrion
rows on AARS2, CLYBL and MMADHC no longer MODIFY to the matrix: their sources
correctly assert the organelle and could not see further, so the row's claim
is true and they are kept (ACCEPT for AARS2 and CLYBL; KEEP_AS_NON_CORE for
MMADHC, matching its other mitochondrion rows). The matrix term stays as the
NEW IC row on each gene. The AARS2 and MMADHC IBA propagation reviews go back
to NO_FAILURE (the GRANULARITY_MISMATCH label meant "true but uninformative",
which contradicts rule 1). With every root row on one action, the validator
exemption for the MODIFY split is no longer needed and was removed. The
comparator that decided it: DARS2 and MMUT carry the matrix term alongside
their mitochondrion rows in GOA. The pilot table below records the first
version.

What the pilot showed:

  • None of the matrix calls rests on a direct sub-mitochondrial assay.
    All three NEW rows are IC built from several agreeing lines. That is what
    rule 4 asks for, and it is why PYCR2, with only a functional hint, stayed at
    the root.
  • The IBA red flag is often structural. For AARS2 and MMADHC, the PAINT
    node that gives the mitochondrion IBA is seeded only by the gene's own
    root-level annotation. The IBA cannot be finer than its seed. Fixing the
    seed (a matrix annotation on the human gene) is the way to improve the
    propagation.
  • (Superseded by the revision entry above: the MODIFY split was dropped, so no
    exception is needed.) The action-consistency check warns on the policy's own pattern. ACCEPT
    or KEEP_AS_NON_CORE on direct rows alongside MODIFY on synthesis rows for
    the same root term gives "Inconsistent review actions for term
    GO:0005739". The warning is expected under this policy. It needs an
    exception (or a different rule) before the policy is rolled out.
  • PYCR1's matrix term is Reactome TAS only; there is no sub-mitochondrial
    assay for either PYCR paralog.

2026-10-16 (policy)

Decided with cjm. TOO_GENERAL is wrong here, because the organelle is still
informative. The call is evidence-based: direct evidence that cannot resolve
the sub-compartment is ACCEPT; IBA, IC and propagation IEA are red flags to
examine; and reviews should propose a sub-compartment when the gene has none
and several lines of evidence agree. The script now classes evidence codes as
direct, synthesis or author (TAS/NAS) and writes the red-flag worklist for the
first-pass organelles.

2026-10-16

Project opened with the question about root organelle terms. The survey script
uses OAK sqlite:obo:go ancestors over is_a and part_of, and treats a
descendant as membrane if it is under GO:0016020 membrane and as lumen if it
is under GO:0031974 membrane-enclosed lumen. "Other" is mostly complexes,
chromatin and sub-organellar structures. Runtime is about 3 minutes over the
full corpus. The ten roots are hard-coded in the script; extend ROOTS to add
more.
