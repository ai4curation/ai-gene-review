Single-Organism IBA Phylogenetic Distance Analysis

Single-Organism IBA Phylogenetic Distance Analysis

Overview

A recurrent question in IBA review is how to evaluate IBA annotations whose WITH/FROM column cites experimental descendants from only a single organism (excluding self-citations where the target gene's species is also a donor).

As established in IBA_REVIEW.md, a single-organism donor list is not intrinsically weak when the donor and target are close orthologs and the PAINT curator placed the ancestral IBD node at an appropriate clade (for example, mouse $\to$ human within Euarchontoglires or Euteleostomi). However, when a single organism's experimental annotation is used to seed an ancestral IBD deep in the tree—such as at Opisthokonta, Eukaryota, or LUCA—and then propagated down across kingdoms, domains, or multiple gene-duplication boundaries, the risk of over-propagation rises substantially.

This sub-project defines four quantitative distance metrics to measure "how far down (and up) the tree" a single-organism IBA travels, benchmarks them against existing review actions across all 25,298 positive GOA IBA annotations in the repository, and uses the resulting high-distance filter to identify candidate genes for targeted re-review.

Corpus-Wide Prevalence

Across genes/*/*/*-goa.tsv and interpro/panther/*/*-paint.tsv (reproducible via uv run python projects/IBA_REVIEW/single_organism_distance.py --summary):

  • Total positive GOA IBA rows scanned: 25,298 (across 6,382 GOA genes; 6,379 reviewed genes)
  • Single-organism GOA IBA rows (including self-species): 6,159 (24.3% of all positive IBAs)
  • Single-organism non-self IBA rows: 3,517 (13.9% of all positive IBAs) across 2,165 genes (3,433 rated with a completed review action; 3,426 with a resolved donor UniProt OC lineage and 7 excluded in Tier 0 due to unresolved donor lineage)
  • Strictly 1 single experimental seed token (n_seed_tokens == 1): 2,867 rows (11.3% of all IBAs)
  • Multiple paralog seed tokens (n_seed_tokens >= 2) from 1 single donor organism: 650 rows (2.6% of all IBAs)
  • Cached PAINT IBD assertions (interpro/panther/*/*-paint.tsv): 8,161 / 20,108 (40.6%) of all cached PAINT IBD ancestral rows rest on experimental evidence from a single resolved taxon.

Action Classification: Propagation Failure vs Term Granularity Refinement

To avoid conflating true homology over-propagation with routine ontology-granularity adjustments, all tables below separate non-retained review actions into two distinct categories:

  • Propagation Failure (Fail = REMOVE + MARK_AS_OVER_ANNOTATED): The reviewer judged that the annotated function or process does not hold as a core or valid biological attribute of the target gene (with % REMOVE reported separately for outright false claims).
  • Term Granularity Refinement (Refn = MODIFY + TOO_GENERAL): The essence of the annotation is true of the target gene, but a more specific or better-scoped GO term is preferred.
  • Total Downgraded (% Down = Fail + Refn): The combined non-retained rate (REMOVE + MARK_AS_OVER_ANNOTATED + MODIFY + TOO_GENERAL), vs Retained (Keep = ACCEPT + KEEP_AS_NON_CORE).

Four Quantitative Distance Metrics

Implemented in single_organism_distance.py:

1. Taxonomic LCA Clade Depth ($d_{\text{LCA}}$) & Branch Steps ($s_{\text{up}} + s_{\text{down}}$)

For each donor–target pair $(O_{\text{donor}}, O_{\text{target}})$, we find their Lowest Common Ancestor (LCA) along the UniProt/NCBI species OC phylogeny:
- $d_{\text{LCA}}$: Clade depth of $\text{LCA}(O_{\text{donor}}, O_{\text{target}})$ from cellular_organisms ($d_{\text{LCA}} = 0$ at cellular_organisms/LUCA, $1$ at Eukaryota/Bacteria, $2$ at Metazoa/Fungi/Viridiplantae, $3\text{–}4$ at Bilateria/Protostomia/Deuterostomia/Ascomycota, $6+$ at Euteleostomi/Tetrapoda/Euarchontoglires).
- $s_{\text{up}} + s_{\text{down}}$: Total taxonomic branch steps up from $O_{\text{donor}}$ to $\text{LCA}$ plus down from $\text{LCA}$ to $O_{\text{target}}$.

Across the 3,426 rated non-self single-organism IBA rows with resolved donor lineages (plus 7 rows in Tier 0 with unresolved donor lineage), Propagation Failure (% Fail) and % REMOVE climb substantially as the donor–target LCA moves from close clades to cross-kingdom and cross-domain (LUCA) splits:

Key observations from Table 1:
- Clade-to-Tier mapping (classify_evolutionary_span): single_organism_distance.py maps each donor–target LCA clade to Tiers 0–7: Unknown $\to$ Tier 0; Cellular organisms (LUCA) $\to$ Tier 7; Eukaryota $\to$ Tier 6 when either species is in Viridiplantae/Algae (Arabidopsis, Oryza, Triticum, Chlamydomonas), else Tier 5 (Amorphea or Opisthokonta); Bacteria/Archaea $\to$ Tier 5 vs intra-phylum bacterial clades (Bacillati, Gammaproteobacteria, Enterobacteriaceae, Actinomycetes) $\to$ Tier 3; Metazoa $\to$ Tier 4; phylum/subphylum clades (Euteleostomi, Dikarya, Ascomycota, Viridiplantae, Embryophyta, Ecdysozoa, Arthropoda) $\to$ Tier 3; order/subphylum fungal and plant clades (Saccharomycotina, Pezizomycotina, Magnoliopsida, Pentapetalae, rosids) $\to$ Tier 2; and close mammalian/model genera and superorders (Eutheria, Boreoeutheria, Euarchontoglires, Primates, Hominidae, Glires, Rodentia, Murinae, Caenorhabditis, Aspergillus, etc.) $\to$ Tier 1, with an explicit fallback (Tier 2: Other ({lca})) routing any unenumerated LCA clade to Tier 2 (see §Limitations item 4).
- Separating Propagation Failure (% Fail) from Term Refinement (% Refn) confirms that the distance effect is driven by genuine over-propagation (REMOVE + MARK_AS_OVER_ANNOTATED), which nearly triples from 8.0% in Tier 1 to 19.3% in Tier 6 and 22.6% in Tier 7, while outright % REMOVE increases nearly 6-fold (2.6% $\to$ 10.8% $\to$ 15.0%).
- By contrast, Term Refinement (% Refn) shows no systematic trend with distance (5.9%–9.0% across most tiers), and separating it explains the apparent anomaly in Tier 2 ($n = 73$): 12 of the 13 downgraded rows (16.4%) are MODIFY/TOO_GENERAL granularity refinements, whereas only 1 row (1.4%, 0.0% REMOVE) is a propagation failure.
- Note that sample sizes in Tier 2 ($n = 73$), Tier 6 ($n = 83$), and Tier 7 ($n = 133$) are much smaller than in Tiers 1, 3, 4, and 5 ($n = 500\text{–}1{,}346$), so point estimates in those smaller tiers carry wider sampling uncertainty.

Note on non-monotonicity and UniProt OC lineage density: Raw taxonomic step counts are non-monotonic at the extremes. The 02–06 steps bin has a small sample size ($n = 59$, where 5 REMOVE rows produce an 8.5% REMOVE rate, largely on bacterial/yeast intragenus paralogs), and the 25+ steps bin (4.7% REMOVE, 15.1% Fail) is lower than 19–24 steps (7.1% REMOVE, 16.1% Fail) because metazoan UniProt OC lineages contain many more intermediate taxonomic ranks (~14–16 ranks in vertebrates/insects) than bacterial or fungal lineages (~6–9 ranks). Consequently, a cross-domain Bacteria $\to$ Bacteria or Yeast $\to$ Human pair often falls into 19–24 steps, whereas a Worm $\to$ Human (Bilateria) pair falls into 25+ steps. Named phylogenetic tiers (Table 1) are therefore a better-calibrated metric than raw OC rank counts.

2. Excess PAINT Node Depth ($\Delta_{\text{depth}} = d_{\text{LCA}} - d_{\text{PTN}}$)

Even when the single experimental donor and target are close relatives (e.g., mouse $\to$ human), a PAINT curator may have placed the ancestral IBD assertion at LUCA or Eukaryota rather than at Mammalia or Vertebrata.

Because UniProt OC rank depth varies across kingdoms, the lookup table PAINT_TAXON_DEPTH in single_organism_distance.py maps all 63 explicit positive-IBD taxon:<id> identifiers across interpro/panther/*/*-paint.tsv to an approximate OC clade depth ($d_{\text{PTN}} = 0$ for taxon:131567 cellular_organisms; $1$ for Eukaryota/Opisthokonta/Bacteria/Archaea; $2\text{–}5$ for intermediate Metazoa/Bilateria/Chordata/kingdom nodes; $6+$ for Euteleostomi/Tetrapoda/Amniota/Mammalia/Euarchontoglires and close genus/species nodes such as Caenorhabditis taxon:6237). Rows in *-paint.tsv whose taxon column is a bare taxon: string (meaning no taxon constraint was recorded in the PAINT GAF; see src/ai_gene_review/etl/panther_paint.py) are reported separately rather than assigned a clade depth. Restricting to all 1,538 rated rows on close donor–target pairs ($d_{\text{LCA}} \ge 6$: 785 in locally cached PAINT families with an explicit node taxid, 226 in cached families with an unrecorded blank taxon:, and 527 in uncached families) holds donor–target evolutionary distance constant while isolating the effect of PAINT ancestral node placement ($d_{\text{PTN}}$):

Within cached PAINT families with an explicit node taxon on close donor–target pairs ($n = 785$), placing a single-organism IBD at LUCA/Eukaryota/Opisthokonta ($d_{\text{PTN}} \le 1$, $n = 121$) or at intermediate pre-vertebrate nodes ($2 \le d_{\text{PTN}} \le 5$, $n = 252$) raises the outright REMOVE rate 7- to 8-fold (3.6%–4.1% vs 0.5% when scoped within the clade, $d_{\text{PTN}} \ge 6$), and increases propagation failure from 6.6% to 8.3%. (An additional 226 rows in cached families have a blank taxon: column in *-paint.tsv—where the PAINT GAF did not record a node taxon constraint, spanning both shallow mouse-only nodes and deeper nodes—and show an 8.4% Fail / 4.9% REMOVE rate.)

3. PANTHER Subfamily Dilution (SAME_SF vs CROSS_SF)

Joining the target protein and single-organism donor protein against cached interpro/panther/*/*-entries.csv tests whether they reside in the same PANTHER subfamily (SAME_SF, typically direct 1:1 orthologs) or different subfamilies (CROSS_SF, separated by at least one gene-duplication/subfamily-divergence event). Across the 3,426 rated rows with known donor lineage (1,156 with resolved subfamily for both donor and target; 2,270 in UNKNOWN_SF where the PANTHER family or external donor seed is not in the local *-entries.csv cache):

Close / moderate taxa ($d_{\text{LCA}} \ge 4$) SAME_SF (same PANTHER subfamily) 430 32 7.4% 27 6.3% 13.7% 10 2.3%
Close / moderate taxa ($d_{\text{LCA}} \ge 4$) CROSS_SF (cross-subfamily paralog) 189 20 10.6% 21 11.1% 21.7% 8 4.2%
Distant taxa ($0 \le d_{\text{LCA}} \le 3$) SAME_SF (same PANTHER subfamily) 260 32 12.3% 20 7.7% 20.0% 15 5.8%
Distant taxa ($0 \le d_{\text{LCA}} \le 3$) CROSS_SF (cross-subfamily paralog) 277 53 19.1% 16 5.8% 24.9% 24 8.7%

Crossing a PANTHER subfamily boundary (CROSS_SF) increases the Propagation Failure (% Fail) rate from 7.4% $\to$ 10.6% in close/moderate taxa and from 12.3% $\to$ 19.1% in distant taxa, while nearly doubling the % REMOVE rate (2.3% $\to$ 4.2% and 5.8% $\to$ 8.7%).

4. Exact PANTHER Tree Edge Steps (steps_from_ibd) & Duplications (duplication_on_path)

The TreeGrafter path audit (projects/TREEGRAFTER/graft_paths_all.tsv) records exact PANTHER tree edge steps (steps_from_ibd) and whether a gene-duplication node lies between the IBD assertion node and the target (duplication_on_path). Because graft_paths_all.tsv audits TreeGrafter (IEA) graft paths on non-PANTHER reference-proteome species (such as 9AVES, 9POAL, AEDAE) rather than the 3,433 GOA IBA population on PANTHER reference species, its direct row-level overlap with GOA IBA annotations is only $n = 2$ rows (Table 4B). Limitations and Methodological Confounds

  1. Circularity between phylogenetic distance and reviewer policy (and how --candidates works): The review actions (REMOVE, MARK_AS_OVER_ANNOTATED, MODIFY, ACCEPT, KEEP_AS_NON_CORE) in genes/*/*/*-ai-review.yaml are not an independent experimental ground truth. They were produced by AI and human reviewers following IBA_REVIEW.md guidelines that explicitly instruct reviewers to check phylogenetic node placement, subfamily divergence, and cross-kingdom pathway differences. Consequently, higher failure rates in Tiers 5–7 and CROSS_SF reflect both genuine biological divergence and heightened reviewer scrutiny of deep-node propagations. Conversely, some high-distance single-organism IBAs in the baseline corpus remained at ACCEPT or KEEP_AS_NON_CORE even when the reviewer's own reason text noted a biological caveat. Specifically, --candidates in single_organism_distance.py filters on tier >= 4, action in {"ACCEPT", "KEEP_AS_NON_CORE"}, and reason matching 13 skeptical keywords ("do not", "does not", "lacks", "absent", "plant", "yeast-specific", "only in", "artifact", "over-annotat", "mislead", "cytosolic", "not directly", "unlike"); it does not filter on CROSS_SF. Thus, --candidates serves as an internal reason-vs-action inconsistency detector, which we supplemented with PANTHER family/ortholog consistency sweeps to assemble the 15 re-review candidates below. Note that harmonizing candidate proposals with sibling reviews on the same PAINT node (e.g., aligning human/ATG4D, human/ATG2B, DICDI/atg1, or human/HSPB2 with existing reviews on human/ATG4C, human/ATG2A, human/ULK1, or DANRE/cryaa) is itself subject to the same-pipeline circularity caveat, since those sibling reviews were generated under the same curation guidelines.
  2. Sample size variation across strata: While Tiers 1, 3, 4, and 5 have large denominators ($n = 500\text{–}1{,}346$), Tier 2 ($n = 73$), Tier 6 ($n = 83$), Tier 7 ($n = 133$), the 02–06 steps bin ($n = 59$), and the >= 10 TreeGrafter steps bins ($n = 2$ and $n = 10$) have small sample sizes where a handful of rows shift percentages by several points.
  3. Partial local cache coverage of PANTHER families: Roughly 34% of close-pair single-organism IBAs (527 / 1,538 in Metric 2, plus 226 / 1,538 with an unrecorded blank taxon: in *-paint.tsv) and 66% of all rated rows (2,270 / 3,426 in Metric 3) belong to PANTHER families or external seed accessions not yet cached or lacking taxon metadata under interpro/panther/. Metrics 1 and 1B cover 99.8% (3,426 / 3,433) of rated rows because they use UniProt OC lineages directly.
  4. Residual clade fallback in Tier 2 (classify_evolutionary_span) and Xenbase: prefix taxon resolution: Because classify_evolutionary_span routes any unenumerated LCA clade string to Tier 2: Other ({lca}), Tier 2 ($n = 73$) is a composite residual bucket combining 28 explicitly mapped fungal/plant order/subphylum rows (Saccharomycotina, Pezizomycotina, Magnoliopsida, Pentapetalae, rosids) with 45 fallback rows across 14 unenumerated LCA strings—spanning both shallower clades (Mammalia, Theria, Saccharomycetaceae, Bacillaceae) and deeper clades (Fungi, Bilateria, Nematoda, Vertebrata, Chordata). In addition, MOD_TO_TAXON maps Xenbase: donor IDs not resolved via build_mod_to_acc() to 8355 (Xenopus laevis), so Xenopus tropicalis (8364) targets with Xenbase: donors score as intragenus transfers with LCA Xenopus (accounting for 15 of the 73 Tier 2 rows).

Candidate Failure Patterns Identified for Re-Review (and Valid Deep Conservation)

Combining uv run python projects/IBA_REVIEW/single_organism_distance.py --candidates (which flags Tier 4–7 ACCEPT/KEEP_AS_NON_CORE rows whose existing reason text contains skeptical keywords) with PANTHER family/ortholog consistency sweeps across sibling reviews surfaces five recurring biological failure mechanisms—comprising 15 candidate genes partitioned into follow-up re-review PRs (#5703 for Tiers 6–7 and #5704 for Tiers 4–5)—alongside a sixth category of valid deep conservation:

  1. Cross-Domain Organelle / Pathway Projection (Bacteria $\leftrightarrow$ Eukaryota, Tier 7)
  2. Mechanism: A bacterial enzyme or plastid-targeted nuclear factor is used to seed a LUCA or bacterial node for a compartment-specific or pathway-specific claim that differs between bacteria, plastids, and eukaryotic cytosol/mitochondria, or aMOD over-annotation on a plastid subunit is propagated to bacteria.

  3. Candidates identified for re-review (PR #5703):

    • human/MDH1 (GO:0006099 tricarboxylic acid cycle from M. tuberculosis mdh UniProtKB:P9WK13 via PTN000600336 at LUCA; baseline action KEEP_AS_NON_CORE $\to$ proposed REMOVE): M. tuberculosis has a single TCA-cycle MDH, whereas eukaryotic MDH1 is the cytosolic malate–aspartate shuttle isoform (mitochondrial MDH2 runs the TCA cycle).
    • BACSU/sigF (GO:0000976 transcription cis-regulatory region binding from an Arabidopsis thaliana plastid sigma factor TAIR:locus:2084420 [specific SIG paralog not identified in cached records] via PTN000779307; baseline action ACCEPT $\to$ proposed REMOVE): Bacterial sigma factors such as B. subtilis SigF recognize promoter DNA only as part of the RNA polymerase holoenzyme (GO:0016987 sigma factor activity; GO:0003677 DNA binding). The baseline review had left GO:0000976 at ACCEPT on the grounds that SigF recognizes the -35 GAATA sequence of forespore promoters, whereas re-reviewing GO:0000976 to REMOVE aligns sigF with paralog BACSU/sigG (where GO:0000976 is already REMOVE because bacterial sigma factors do not bind cis-regulatory DNA autonomously outside the holoenzyme) and with GO:0003899 DNA-directed 5'-3' RNA polymerase activity from the same PTN000779307|TAIR:locus:2084420 seed (already REMOVE in the baseline review).

  4. Plant Secondary Metabolism / Subfamily Expansion Projected to Fungi & Animals (Viridiplantae $\leftrightarrow$ Opisthokonta, Tier 6)

  5. Mechanism: Land plants expanded enzyme families for specialized pathways (agmatine-route putrescine synthesis, purine/cytokinin nucleoside hydrolysis, plastidial MEP-pathway isoprenoid synthesis) that are absent, non-physiological, or reversed in fungi and metazoans.

  6. Candidates identified for re-review (PR #5703):

    • SCHPO/SPAC11D3.09 & SCHPO/SPAPB24D3.03 (GO:0009446 putrescine biosynthetic process from Arabidopsis agmatinases AT4G08870/AT4G08900 via PTN000144486 at Eukaryota; baseline action KEEP_AS_NON_CORE $\to$ proposed MARK_AS_OVER_ANNOTATED): Fungi synthesize putrescine from ornithine via ornithine decarboxylase and lack an endogenous arginine decarboxylase pathway to generate agmatine.
    • SCHPO/SPAC17G8.02 & SCHPO/SPBC1683.06c (GO:0006152 purine nucleoside catabolic process, a single-organism Tier 6 IBA from Arabidopsis NRH1/NRH2 AGI_LocusCode:AT1G05620|AGI_LocusCode:AT2G36310 via PTN000266727 at Eukaryota, baseline action KEEP_AS_NON_CORE $\to$ proposed MARK_AS_OVER_ANNOTATED; re-reviewed alongside the multi-organism IBA GO:0008477 purine nucleosidase activity on the same node PTN000266727 [AT1G05620|AT2G36310|UniProtKB:P22564|UniProtKB:Q4QDN1|UniProtKB:Q9I6Y9], baseline action KEEP_AS_NON_CORE $\to$ proposed MARK_AS_OVER_ANNOTATED for F/P aspect consistency): Fungal Urh1-family enzymes (SPAC17G8.02 and paralog SPBC1683.06c) are pyrimidine-preferring uridine hydrolases (GO:0006218 uridine catabolic process / GO:0045437 uridine nucleosidase activity) with negligible purine nucleosidase activity.
    • human/IDI1 & human/IDI2 (GO:0009240 isopentenyl diphosphate biosynthetic process from Arabidopsis IPP1 AGI_LocusCode:AT5G16440 via PTN000853999 at Eukaryota; baseline action KEEP_AS_NON_CORE $\to$ proposed MARK_AS_OVER_ANNOTATED): In the eukaryotic mevalonate pathway, mevalonate diphosphate decarboxylase (MVD) synthesizes IPP first and IDI1/IDI2 catalyze its isomerization to DMAPP (GO:0004452 isopentenyl-diphosphate delta-isomerase activity / GO:0050992 dimethylallyl diphosphate biosynthetic process), whereas the parent class GO:0009240 treats IPP as the biosynthetic end-product (true in the plastidial/bacterial MEP pathway).

  7. Yeast-Specific Organelle Topologies Projected to Metazoa & Amoebozoa (Fungi $\leftrightarrow$ Metazoa/Amoebozoa, Tier 5–6)

  8. Mechanism: S. cerevisiae closed-mitosis / nucleus–vacuole junction (NVJ) processes are seeded at Eukaryota or Opisthokonta from a single SGD annotation and propagated to organisms that lack a yeast-type vacuole or NVJ.

  9. Candidates identified for re-review (PR #5704):

    • human/ATG4D (GO:0034727 piecemeal microautophagy of the nucleus from S. cerevisiae ATG4 SGD:S000005167 via PTN002503342 at Eukaryota; baseline action KEEP_AS_NON_CORE $\to$ proposed MARK_AS_OVER_ANNOTATED, matching sibling reviews human/ATG4C and worm/atg-4.1), plus human/ATG2B (SGD:S000005186 via PTN000324023) and DICDI/atg1 (SGD:S000003148 via PTN000681273 at Eukaryota; baseline actions KEEP_AS_NON_CORE $\to$ proposed UNDECIDED, matching sibling reviews human/ATG2A and human/ULK1 where yeast-specific NVJ PMN vs broader nuclear-envelope autophagy remains an open ontology-scoping question).

  10. Metazoan Endomembrane / Tissue Specialization Projected to Fission Yeast (Metazoa $\to$ Fungi, Tier 5)

  11. Mechanism: Metazoan-specific organelle organization or cofactor-binding traits from a single human or Drosophila protein are placed at Opisthokonta or LUCA and inherited by fission yeast.
