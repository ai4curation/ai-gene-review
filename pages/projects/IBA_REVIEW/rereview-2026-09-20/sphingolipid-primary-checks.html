SGMS2: distinguish chemical donor, direction and compartment

PMID:14685263 Results/Figs. 4–5 establish PC-dependent forward transfer and SM-dependent reverse transfer to NBD-DAG in extracts expressing human SMS1 or SMS2. Empty-vector and alternative-donor controls distinguish these activities. Ceramide production by the reverse reaction is actual catalytic work, not a substrate-only relationship. The cached publication contains introductory material despite its full-text flag; the indexed primary Results were read separately, and the cache was not replaced with authored text.

Live GO:0047493 specifies CDP-choline, whereas GO:0033188 specifies PC. The existing correction between these terms remains warranted. GO:0002950 specifies CDP-ethanolamine, whereas SMS2 transfers from PE. The two CPE assertions therefore receive MODIFY to GO:0016780, retaining the demonstrated activity while requesting an exact PE:ceramide term. No new GO identifier is invented.

PMID:39510177 full Results/Figs. 3–4 show strong assay-environment dependence. Relative DG production with PE plus ceramide is about 46% in detergent micelles and 6% in proteoliposomes, relative to the PC-plus-ceramide SMS/PC-PLC output. The old review mixed ratios and presented 46% as a general fraction of SMS activity. Purified-enzyme PC-PLC/CPE capacities remain real; their cellular prevalence is a different question.

PMID:18370930 full Results/Figs. 5–12 connect the human SMS2 Golgi pool to sphingomyelin resynthesis and DAG-reporter recruitment, including multiple siRNAs and endogenous ceramide produced after exogenous SMase treatment. A smaller contribution than SMS1 does not negate the core catalytic role at the Golgi.

PMID:36102623 full Fig. 1 uses export-signal chimeras and disease variants to distinguish wild-type Golgi/PM distribution from catalytically active ER-retained SMS2. This evidence makes normal ER activity a substantive question; it neither proves all transient ER activity absent nor makes mutant activity evidence for the wild-type assertion. The shared SGMS2/SGMS1 OpenScientist request covers this question. The paper's alternative initiation interpretation of p.Arg50* is explicitly described as unpublished data, so it qualifies the earlier construct-specific result without establishing every molecular detail.

SMPD2: cellular context and substrate chemistry

PMID:25168245 full Fig. 7 directly depletes endogenous human SMPD2 in Jurkat cells. Stress/Fas-induced nSMase activity and ceramide accumulation decrease while upstream JNK/c-jun phosphorylation persists; baseline ceramide is similar. Localization experiments detect the phosphorylated enzyme in ER/Golgi and nuclear fractions. Recombinant mutant experiments elsewhere include zebrafish constructs and are not treated as human sequence tests. These results support conditional human ceramide formation and intracellular signaling despite earlier unstimulated overexpression results. No nuclear-location or apoptosis NEW annotation was added.

PMID:10608884 supports lyso-PAF metabolism in the tested overexpression setting, not a universal absence of cellular sphingomyelin hydrolysis. PMID:14741383 supports both human nSMase1 and nSMase2 hydrolysis of sphingosylphosphocholine. GO:0004629 covers phosphate–glycerol cleavage; it cannot cover the sphingoid substrate. Broad GO:0008081 assertions are retained, with the glycerophospholipase core limited to lyso-PAF/lyso-PC. The resulting core-term/source-row warning is documented rather than satisfied by adding a redundant NEW assertion. PubMed/Crossref identify a corrigendum to 14741383 (DOI:10.1016/j.febslet.2004.04.001; legacy DOI:10.1016/S0014-5793(04)00396-5), but its contents were inaccessible and are not guessed.

Caveola source identity: a separate unresolved hypothesis

The author-deposited full PMID:12473648 distinguishes rat lung perfusion/activity measurements from bovine endothelial immunoblots. Methods and Fig. 4 use antiserum against the purified bovine neutral SMase from PMID:10713073; Discussion links the caveolar antigen to that enzyme. Later PMID:16517606 describes cloning a new nSMase3 from a purified bovine-enzyme peptide. This is a concrete reagent-provenance question, not a wrong-gene inference from a title or species. Caveola and mechanostimulus rows remain UNDECIDED pending peptide/antigen tracing and later target-specific evidence. The independently demonstrated human SMPD2 ceramide/signaling role remains supported.
