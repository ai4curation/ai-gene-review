Conditional cAMP responses

Bandoh et al., PMID:10488122 is available as author-deposited full text on ResearchGate. The Methods, Results, Fig.7 legend and Discussion were read. EDG4 and EDG7 increased cAMP after forskolin priming in Sf9 cells, with phosphodiesterase inhibition by IBMX; the authors did not observe the response to LPA alone. The Methods specify 2.5 µM LPA while the Fig.7 legend specifies 3 µM. This small protocol discrepancy is preserved rather than silently reconciled. No figure-pixel quantitation was performed.

The EDG4 construct was amplified from human brain cDNA and sequence-checked; this does not establish its exact correspondence to current canonical LPAR2. The early ovarian-tumor EDG4 frameshift described in PMID:10729222 must be mapped to each construct before attributing an effect to it. It is not valid to presume that every historic EDG4 experiment used the same clone.

Im et al., PMID:10727522 supplies independent human LPAR3/Gi2alpha nucleotide-loading evidence and contrasting mammalian-cell cAMP results. Indexed publisher Methods and Discussion describe correction of their EDG4 clone to genomic consensus and discuss conditional adenylyl-cyclase/PKC mechanisms as a possible explanation for the Sf9 result. These mechanistic proposals are not direct proof of the responsible cyclase or G-alpha subtype. Its Fig.7C legend and adjacent Results also identify human forebrain expression, contradicting a general claim of no human brain expression based on the earlier panel.

GO:0007189 does not demand Gs coupling or activation in every context. Conditional activation is therefore retained as non-core for both receptors, with a shared focused OpenScientist question examining biochemical mechanism, construct identity and physiological scope. Direct LPAR2 Gi-mediated inhibition in PMID:16203867 remains valid alongside this conditional observation.

Locations and interactions

GO:0005737 includes internal membrane structures. Membrane topology alone cannot refute it. Full PMID:26473723 Methods identify human LPAR2/LPAR3 GFP constructs in rat C9 cells, and the localization experiments support their internalization. Full PMID:38928196 Results and Discussion document intracellular human LPAR3 and persistent receptor–arrestin association after stimulation. The cytoplasmic IBAs are retained on positive ancestry and compatible target evidence. This does not turn localization images into a demonstrated endosomal G-alpha signaling mechanism; the original is_active_in qualifiers remain unchanged.

PMID:16203867 Fig.6 explicitly distinguishes NHERF2-mediated LPAR2–CFTR co-complex recovery from direct binding. The NHERF2 PDZ refinement remains supported, and the CFTR-specific direct-binding concern remains. BioPlex and HuRI interactions are retained non-core; generic labeling and lack of a defined physiological consequence do not invalidate curated physical evidence. Full PMID:39083597 explicitly includes LPAR2 among receptors supported for all three RAMPs across all capture-detection methods. No stable obligate signaling complex is inferred.

The PMID:19306925 abstract supports RalA association with LPAR2 and GRK2-dependent desensitization. It does not independently prove direct LPAR2–GRK2 binding. The generic interaction is retained; the former kinase-binding replacement is withdrawn. Full text was not available for independent examination of all assay details.

Ortholog provenance and process roles

Live QuickGO identifies rat Lpar3 Q8K5E0 as the G-alpha-binding donor (IMP PMID:16504475) and calcium-transport donor (IMP PMID:11340076), and mouse Lpar3 Q9EQ31 as the axon donor (IDA PMID:22465231) and gene-expression donor (IMP PMID:20864640). These abstracts were read; the original full ortholog figures were unavailable. The rat vascular study coexpresses LPA1 and LPA3, so it is not treated as a purified LPAR3-specific binding assay. Independent human Gi2alpha loading supports the broad receptor–G-alpha engagement claim.

The calcium term includes movement within a cell. Full human PMID:38928196 experiments comparing calcium-free buffer, PLC inhibition and thapsigargin support release from internal stores. The rat microglial influx result is a different context, not evidence that the human receptor must use that route. Mouse curated axon location is retained without requiring a human repetition or equating axonal branching with synaptic transmission. Synapse location and transmission remain unresolved; the full founding paper describes no synaptic experiment. The mouse expression phenotype is real, but whether it supports involved_in mature gene-product production rather than upstream regulation requires closer source/term analysis. These issues have a separate focused adjudication request.

Two old LPAR2 NEW proposals (GO:0007193 and GO:0007200) were withdrawn because each descends from existing GO:0007186. Their mechanisms remain in the biological synthesis. Existing LPAR2 Rho regulation was retained as a proximal regulatory role, not intrinsic GEF activity; LPAR1 carries the same term, while ADRB2 and DRD2 do not. Existing LPAR3 internalization was retained for the demonstrated arrestin-recruiting interface, not solely because LPAR3 is internalized. Full PMID:38791546 Fig.4–8 supports coupling of receptor phosphosite changes to recruitment and uptake, with mutant interpretation limits explicitly acknowledged. The exact term is absent from LPAR1 and ADRB2 but present on DRD2 via rat Drd2 IDA PMID:14617818. Thus absence is not systematic among receptors in the same role. Neither retained NEW term is an is_a/part_of ancestor or descendant of another existing process term. No matching LPAR2/LPAR3 activity was found in the local GO-CAM index.

No additional NEW annotation was introduced. Cached publications, GOA and UniProt records were not manually edited. Additional abstracts were obtained through the official fetch command. Exploratory accession queries that proved unrelated were excluded from the biological evidence.
