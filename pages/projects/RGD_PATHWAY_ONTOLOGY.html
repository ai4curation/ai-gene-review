RGD Pathway Ontology: GO Mappings, GO-CAM Completeness and Module Seeds

Bottom line: The Pathway Ontology (PW) from the Rat Genome Database (RGD)
has 2,703 live terms. It is curated against human, rat and mouse genes, and RGD
draws 200 of its pathways as interactive diagrams. We are using it three ways.
First, as a source of PW→GO mappings: 635 PW terms now have at least one
GO biological-process mapping used for matching, 19 of them from hand curation. Where PW's own definition cites a GO
term and our lexical match also finds one (397 terms), the two agree on 388.
Separately, 44 GO ids cited in PW definitions are obsolete and one no longer resolves. Second, as a
yardstick for GO-CAM completeness. Across the 144 diagram pathways that
describe normal biology, the median pathway has only 29% of its RGD-curated
human genes in any human GO-CAM. The best single GO-CAM covers a median of 2
genes of a pathway, and only 56 of the 144 have any human GO-CAM model whose
process falls under the mapped GO term. Third, as a source of module seeds. We parsed
all 200 RGD diagram pages into structured records (3,209 interaction edges,
1,715 gene links, 651 chemicals, 580 links to other pathways). These were
triaged against our 714 modules. Ninety already have a matching module, 14 are
ready to model, 9 have only a broad GO process term or none, and 56 are disease
or drug pathways and out of scope. Thirteen modules have now been derived from RGD
diagrams: the pilot srebp_signaling; the linked blood set coagulation_cascade,
fibrinolysis and kallikrein_kinin_cascade; a second set,
acetylcholine_signaling, dopamine_receptor_signaling,
visual_phototransduction and prostanoid_biosynthesis; and a third set,
protein_kinase_c_signaling, dna_cytosine_methylation_cycle,
reelin_signaling, trail_apoptotic_signaling and nmj_achr_clustering. All
thirteen pass schema and module validation.

Why this source

GO-CAM and our modules both need a pathway frame: which gene products work
together, in what order, toward which process. Reactome supplies that frame for
human. KEGG has licensing limits, and the UniPathway vocabulary is archived (see the
UniPathway project). PW is CC-BY, actively maintained (release
2026-05-16 used here), and curated by people at RGD. It also has three
properties the others lack:

  • A separate pathway ontology with GO cross-references. Most PW definitions
    cite the GO process they correspond to. That makes PW a natural bridge for
    checking whether GO has a process term for a pathway, and whether the term
    is current.
  • Hand-drawn diagrams with clickable regions. Each diagram's image map
    links genes, chemicals and sub-pathways and records interactions as
    A ---> B, A ---| B or A ---- B. That is close to the causal structure
    a module or GO-CAM needs, but the edge signs cannot be taken at face value:
    in the SREBP diagram, cholesterol and 25-hydroxycholesterol are drawn as
    activating SREBP processing, which they inhibit (see the pilot below).
  • Curated gene membership with evidence. RGD's curated rows (TAS, IDA, IMP,
    ISO and others, 6,205 human rows, excluding RGD's own 33 IEA rows) are kept separate from bulk imports (KEGG IEA,
    SMPDB and PID, 23,480 human rows), so we can measure against the curated set.

Methods

Everything is regenerated by two scripts in
RGD_PATHWAY_ONTOLOGY/scripts/. No result is
hardcoded; the figures on this page are read from data/summary.yaml.

uv run python projects/RGD_PATHWAY_ONTOLOGY/scripts/fetch_rgd_pathways.py
uv run python projects/RGD_PATHWAY_ONTOLOGY/scripts/analyze_rgd_pathways.py

fetch_rgd_pathways.py downloads pw.obo, go-basic.obo, RGD's human and rat
PW gene annotation files and the HGNC symbol table into the git-ignored
.cache/rgd_pw/. It reads the list of diagram pathways from RGD's own pathway
selector page, fetches each diagram page (up to 3 MB of HTML each, 86 MB in all, cached and
not committed), and writes one compact record per pathway to
RGD_PATHWAY_ONTOLOGY/pathways/. A record
holds the title, RGD's free-text description, any GO and KEGG ids the page cites
as its "associated" terms, the diagram image URL, the image-map regions
(sub-pathway links, RGD gene links, chemicals normalized to ChEBI, PubChem,
KEGG or HMDB ids, interaction edges and unlinked labels) and the page's
references. Gene tables are not scraped from the HTML. The bulk annotation
files carry the same rows with evidence codes.

RGD returns HTTP 403 to the default python-requests user agent, so the
fetcher sends its own identifying user agent.

analyze_rgd_pathways.py writes four files to
RGD_PATHWAY_ONTOLOGY/data/:

File Contents
pw_go_mappings.sssom.yaml Candidate PW→GO mappings as an SSSOM mapping set, from three sources: GO ids PW cites in a definition (skos:relatedMatch), the "associated GO term" on a diagram page (skos:closeMatch), and exact normalized matches between labels or exact synonyms, where PW's "X metabolic/biosynthetic/degradation pathway" is read as GO's "X metabolic/biosynthetic/catabolic process" (skos:exactMatch if verbatim, else skos:closeMatch). None is curator-verified. Each row's curation_rule records why it exists (pw2go:src/…) and any QA finding (pw2go:qa/…), as fixed codes defined in the mapping-set description, so one prefix filter selects every problem row. see_also holds only GO's replacement for an obsolete target; the conflicting GO ids for a page citation that disagrees are in other as conflicts_with=<GO id>.
pw_gocam_completeness.tsv One row for each of the 1,677 PW terms with human genes. Genes are taken over the term and its descendants. The basis is the RGD-curated set when one exists and all sources otherwise. Columns give the share of basis genes in any human GO-CAM, the share with a gene review, the best-overlapping GO-CAM, and the GO-CAM models whose process falls under a mapped GO term, with the share of genes they contain.
module_seeds.yaml One seed per diagram pathway: diagram structure, RGD-curated human genes, merged GO mappings, GO-CAM coverage, matches to existing modules, and a triage tier.
summary.yaml Headline counts used on this page.

Module matching uses three tests. A module that cites the PW id matches. A
module matches when at least two of its human members are in the pathway and
they make up at least half its members (mapped through HGNC). A module also
matches when it uses the mapped GO term or one of its descendants. Seeds are
then put in tiers:

Tier Rule Diagram pathways
has_module Cites the PW id as an evidence source, or at least half of a module's genes are in the pathway 131
candidate Mapped GO process, at least 5 curated genes, no matching module 0
related_module_only Only a GO-term match, often a bacterial or other-taxon module 4
no_specific_go_term Curated, but GO has only a broad process term or none (not used for matching) 2
needs_go_mapping At least 5 curated genes but no GO process mapping 0
thin Fewer than 5 RGD-curated human genes 7
out_of_scope Disease or drug branch, or an "altered ..." pathway 56

Seeds with more than 100 curated genes are flagged broad: they should be
split along their sub-pathways before modelling.

Findings

PW→GO mappings

  • Coverage is good for metabolism and named signaling pathways. 635 of
    2,703 live PW terms have at least one current GO process mapping used for
    matching. 19 of them come from the hand-curated set; the other 9 curated terms
    have only a broad GO term or none. 636 terms
    cite GO in their definition and 417 have a lexical match.
  • PW's definition citations are reliable. Where both exist, the definition
    citation and the lexical match agree for 388 of 397 terms. In no case is the
    cited GO term an ancestor of the lexical match. This supports treating PW's
    definition citations as closer to exact than relatedMatch, after spot
    checks. The GO ids typed on RGD's diagram pages are less reliable. 4 of 45
    disagree with the other sources for the same pathway, and three of these look
    wrong: urea cycle is cited as GO:0000076, a DNA replication term whose local id
    repeats the PW id; ERK5 signaling as the JNK cascade; and Toll-like receptor
    signaling as the Drosophila Toll pathway. The fourth, de novo pyrimidine
    synthesis cited as 'de novo' UMP biosynthesis, is defensible, since the
    mammalian route ends at UMP; it is not in an is_a chain with the lexical
    match, so it is flagged as non-equivalent. The script demotes all four to
    relatedMatch and leaves them out of GO-CAM and module matching.
  • PW cites retired GO terms. 44 definition citations point at obsolete GO
    terms, and one at an id that no longer resolves. Several are amino-acid "metabolic process" terms that GO has since
    obsoleted, such as cysteine, histidine, tyrosine, phenylalanine and
    tryptophan metabolic process. PW:0000543 (protein kinase A (PKA) signaling
    pathway) cites GO:0010737, now "obsolete protein kinase A signaling", both in its definition
    and on its diagram page. GO names no replacement, but the current term is
    GO:0141156 cAMP/PKA signal transduction. These can go back to RGD as a
    concrete update list. In the SSSOM file the 45 obsolete citation sites (44
    distinct PW→GO pairs) carry pw2go:qa/OBSOLETE_TARGET and the unresolvable
    one pw2go:qa/MISSING_TARGET; 12 of the 45 list GO's replacement in
    see_also.
  • Some well-known pathways have no GO process match by label. Examples are
    p53 signaling, AMPK signaling, PKA signaling, hypoxia-inducible factor,
    angiotensin II and vasopressin signaling, renin-angiotensin, and
    mitochondrial and cytosolic iron-sulfur cluster assembly. Some do have a GO
    term under another name, for example GO:0072331 (signal transduction by p53
    class mediator) and GO:0141156 (cAMP/PKA signal transduction). All 28 such
    diagram pathways are now mapped by hand; see Curated GO mappings below.

GO-CAM completeness

GO-CAM coverage of these pathways is scattered rather than pathway-shaped. The
144 in-scope diagram pathways have a median of 29% of their RGD-curated human
genes in some human GO-CAM, and 42 reach half. Those genes are spread over many
unrelated models. The best single model covers a median of 2 genes, and the
median share of genes in GO-CAMs about the mapped process is 0. That last
number undercounts modelled pathways (see the caveat below the table). Examples:

PW pathway Curated human genes In any GO-CAM In GO-CAMs for the mapped GO process
canonical Wnt signaling (PW:0000201) 64 11% 0%
Notch signaling (PW:0000204) 36 11% 3%
citric acid cycle (PW:0000026) 11 9% 0% (see below)
mTOR signaling (PW:0000180) 43 56% 44%
IL-10 signaling (PW:0000515) 7 100% 86%
TRAIL signaling (PW:0000682) 9 100% 67%
SREBP signaling (PW:0000753) 7 86% 0% (see below)

The two columns fail in opposite directions, so neither is a completeness
measure on its own. A gene that appears in a GO-CAM about something else does
not make the pathway modelled, so the "any GO-CAM" column overcounts. The
process-level column only counts models in which some activity is annotated to
the mapped GO process or a descendant. GO-CAMs usually annotate each activity to
the step it performs, not to the whole pathway, so this column undercounts.

The SREBP row shows the undercount. The human GO-CAM 6205c24300000880
contains six of the seven curated genes and models the pathway step by step.
Its activities carry step-level process terms: GO:0036316 (SREBP-SCAP complex
retention in ER), GO:0051604, GO:0045542, GO:0006607, GO:0046889 and
GO:1905552. None of these is under the mapped term GO:0032933 (SREBP
signaling pathway), so the pathway scores 0%. A 0 in the process-level column
therefore does not tell an unmodelled pathway apart from one modelled with
step-level terms. best_gocam_overlap, read together with the model title,
separates the two cases for SREBP. Both columns count human GO-CAMs only. The
Wnt and citric acid cycle zeros have other causes. The only GO-CAM carrying the
Wnt term is a Drosophila model, so canonical Wnt signaling has no human model
at all. The one human model carrying the TCA-cycle term contains none of RGD's
curated genes (below).

The citric acid cycle row also shows a limit of the gene basis. Eleven GO-CAMs
carry the TCA-cycle term: one mouse, one fly, eight fission-yeast and one human
model (SIRT3 regulation, with CS and MDH2). The human model contains none of the
11 RGD-curated human genes. RGD's curated human set lists the succinyl-CoA, succinate dehydrogenase,
fumarase and 2-oxoglutarate dehydrogenase steps but omits CS, ACO2, the IDH3
subunits and MDH2, which reach the human term only through the KEGG and SMPDB imports.
The curated basis avoids KEGG's over-broad lists, but it can also miss core
members. The completeness table therefore reports both counts
(n_genes_rgd_curated, n_genes_all).

Two caveats apply to the has_module tier. A gene-overlap match needs only
half of a module's genes in the pathway, so a broad pathway such as extrinsic
apoptosis also matches the NF-kB and NLR modules. A has-module call means a
related module exists, not that the module covers the whole RGD pathway. A PW id
counts as cited only when a module lists it as an evidence source; before
2026-10-26 any mention counted, so a pathway a module's notes named as out of scope
(kinin signaling, PW:0001480, in kallikrein_kinin_cascade) was miscounted as
covered.

Pilot: SREBP signaling

modules/srebp_signaling.yaml was
derived from the RGD SREBP diagram,
PW:0000753. The diagram's
seven gene products and edges map directly onto five module steps:

  1. sterol sensing and ER retention (SCAP, INSIG1, INSIG2)
  2. SCAP-escorted COPII transport to the Golgi
  3. site-1 cleavage (MBTPS1)
  4. site-2 intramembrane cleavage (MBTPS2)
  5. nuclear transcription (SREBF1, SREBF2)

A feedback edge runs from step 5 back to INSIG1. The RGD diagram supplied the
boundary and topology. The molecular functions were then chosen from each
protein's experimental GO annotations, not from the diagram. The edge types
needed curator judgment. RGD's ----> is "positively affects", which covers
causation, provision of a substrate and regulation alike, and the sign can be
wrong. The diagram draws cholesterol and 25-hydroxycholesterol as positively
affecting "Srebf at Golgi", but sterols block SREBP transport and
processing. The module models that link as NEGATIVELY_REGULATES. Anyone
reusing diagram_edges from the other 199 records should check every sign
against the literature.

After the first pass, each step was given quote-checked support from the primary papers that defined it (13 PubMed abstracts). It was also cross-checked against an independent human GO-CAM, 6205c24300000880, which models six of the seven gene products with matching molecular functions (it uses the parent GO:0000981 for SREBF1/2, where the module uses GO:0001228). The model also contains AKT1 and PCK1, which the module leaves out: their reported effect on INSIG opposes retention and rests on one cancer-cell study (corrected separately in ai4curation/ai-gene-review#5055). That model omits SCAP, and it includes KPNB1 for SREBP nuclear import, a step the RGD description mentions but the module does not yet model. A curator-built GO-CAM for the same pathway is the strongest check available on a diagram-derived module, so the seeds' best_gocam field is worth reading before modelling the next candidates. It is a lead, not a ranking. For most pathways no model carries the mapped process, and ties between equally overlapping models then go to the lowest model id.

The pilot shows a limit of the route: the diagram has no step-level GO terms,
so every activity needs separate grounding. SCAP's escort role has only the
generic cargo receptor activity (GO:0038024), which the module now uses along
with sterol sensor activity (GO:0032935), matching SCAP's gene review. Reviews of
all seven member genes merged in ai4curation/ai-gene-review#5215.

  • modules/fibrinolysis.yaml (GO:0042730)
    takes the cross-linked clot as its input. It covers plasminogen activation by
    tPA and uPA, plasmin degradation of fibrin, and inhibition by PAI-1 and the

The triage re-run counts four seeds as has_module that were not before: the
three pathways and the protein C anticoagulant pathway (PW:0000480), which
coagulation_cascade declares as an evidence source because its protein C part
holds five of that pathway's six genes (all but PROCR). Kinin signaling
(PW:0001480), which an earlier count included, is outside kallikrein_kinin_cascade
and went back to needs_go_mapping; it is now curated to a broad GO term (see

Most of the curation work was in the non-enzymatic members, because the RGD
diagrams draw them as plain nodes:

  • Substrates. Kininogen is the substrate the kallikreins cut. It gets only
    the hormone activity of the released kinin, which its own experimental
    annotations and the human GO-CAM 6246724f00000632 both use. It gets no
    kallikrein-kinin process term. A comparator check in QuickGO found no
    kininogen among the annotations to either kallikrein-kinin cascade term.
  • Fibrinogen. In coagulation fibrinogen is thrombin's substrate, but it
    polymerises itself, so it gets structural molecule activity and fibrin
    polymerisation. In fibrinolysis, fibrin is the template that speeds up its own
    lysis (the cofactor case in CLAUDE.md). GO has no molecular-function term for
    that template role, so the participant is given processes and no molecular
    function.
  • Protease cofactors. Tissue factor and factors Va and VIIIa get
    endopeptidase activator activity, and protein S peptidase activator activity.
    These are curator judgments, because these proteins have no experimental
    molecular-function annotation that fits. Protein Z and the factor XIII B
    subunit are left without a molecular function rather than given one GO cannot
    support.

GO-CAM support is thin. Curated models cover protein C activation (THBD, F2,
PROC: 666b894f00000232), bradykinin degradation and receptor binding (ACE,
KNG1, BDKRB2: 6246724f00000632) and plasminogen's role in matrix breakdown
(PLAU, PLAUR, PLG: 68fac5ed00001527). The procoagulant steps, tissue
kallikrein and every inhibitor step have no curated model. Of the roughly 50
member gene products, only VWF, A2M and APOH have gene reviews.
