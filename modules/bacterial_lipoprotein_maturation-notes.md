# Bacterial lipoprotein maturation comparative audit

## 2026-11-07 — literature and taxonomic boundary

The existing module describes Lgt and LspA followed by optional canonical Lnt, but its prose excludes non-orthologous terminal tailoring. That misses established bacterial implementations of the same maturation boundary. Keep the common Lgt–LspA chemistry and distinguish terminal products explicitly: free-amino diacyl lipoprotein, long-chain N-acylated triacyl lipoprotein, intramolecularly rearranged lyso lipoprotein, and N-acetylated lipoprotein. Do not describe Lit as adding a third acyl chain. Lol trafficking and the biological functions of lipoprotein substrates remain downstream. Acetylated heptaprenylglycerol carrier synthesis is an upstream module; LhaT uses its product at this boundary.

The bacterial context is an occurrence envelope supported by representatives, not evidence that every bacterial genome has the same enzymes, essentiality or final chemotype. The missing Lnt family in a sampled Firmicutes inventory does not establish absence of N-acylation.

## Evidence that changes the planned model

- **Lnb, PMID:39495918.** Full Results identify BF9343_0945 from Bacteroides fragilis NCTC 9343 and BT_4364 from B. thetaiotaomicron VPI-5482. E. coli Lnt-depletion rescue and reporter lipid mass spectra establish an alternative N-acylation mechanism; B. thetaiotaomicron native deletion/complementation establishes its role in that strain. Reporter purification is not purification and reconstitution of Lnb itself. The acyl donor must remain unspecified unless direct evidence resolves it. Five-transmembrane topology and the putative H49/N66 residues are predictions; BT C148S and C198S were actually tested. Exact proteins are Q5LGL3 and Q89ZL3, respectively. The paper describes BT Lnb as 398 residues, while its current UniProt sequence has 395; H49, N66, C148 and C198 nevertheless match the current sequence. The cause of that length discrepancy is unresolved. BT residue numbering cannot be copied to the 408-residue BF protein.
- **LnsAB, PMID:32723923 and PMID:42580408.** The 2020 study identifies SAOUHSC_00822 and SAOUHSC_02761 as the two-component S. aureus system. The October 2026 primary supplies direct recombinant reconstitution: both proteins are needed, and the sn-1 chain of phosphatidylglycerol is the preferred donor. Its Methods use LnsA residues 32–189 and recombinant LnsB expressed in an E. coli background lacking endogenous Lnt. An NlpC/P60 catalytic assignment to LnsA does not make LnsB a proven protease. The same study directly tests cross-species pair compatibility: S. epidermidis LnsB works with S. aureus LnsA, whereas S. epidermidis LnsA does not work with S. aureus LnsB under the tested conditions but works with its own LnsB. A module must not silently treat arbitrary family members as freely interchangeable. Synteny and percentage identity alone are explicitly insufficient to assign LnsB specificity among CPBP paralogs.
- **Lit, PMID:32471867.** The enzyme rearranges an existing sn-2 O-linked acyl group onto the N-terminal amino group, producing the lyso chemotype. This is chemically distinct from a donor-dependent third-chain transfer. Full study extraction and exact exemplar verification remain to be completed.
- **LhaT, PMID:40626731.** The Bacillus subtilis enzyme formerly called YpjA uses the acetylated heptaprenylglyceryl carrier pathway to enable lipoprotein N-acetylation. The paper distinguishes intact activity from B. anthracis poly-tyrosine-inactivated homologs and competing Lit conversion. The existing YpjA review from the Ac-HepG audit must be reconciled with that PR's actual merge state before reuse; carrier-production genes should not be duplicated as maturation enzymes.
- **Lgt, PMID:42126242.** The new full primary study compares heterologous Lgt complementation and shows that Lpp and arm/head features affect compatibility. P. aeruginosa assay results do not establish KT2440 measurements; the current review consultation finds no B. subtilis complementation test in the studied panel. Failed rescue, especially where expression is not detected, is not proof that a homolog lacks Lgt chemistry.
