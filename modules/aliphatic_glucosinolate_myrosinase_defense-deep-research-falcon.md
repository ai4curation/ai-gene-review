# Commissioned Review Brief

## Review Topic

Aliphatic glucosinolate biosynthesis and myrosinase activation (glucosinolate-myrosinase defense system)

## Working Scope

The glucosinolate-myrosinase system is the two-component chemical defense of the Brassicales. Methionine-derived (aliphatic) glucosinolates are made in three phases: (1) side-chain elongation, a plastidial cycle recruited from leucine biosynthesis in which methionine is deaminated to its 2-oxo acid (BCAT4), condensed with acetyl-CoA (MAM), isomerized (IPMI), oxidatively decarboxylated (IPMDH1) and transaminated back to a chain-elongated methionine homolog (BCAT3), each turn adding one methylene group; (2) formation of the core glucosinolate structure, in which CYP79F converts the chain-elongated amino acid to an aldoxime, CYP83A1 activates the aldoxime for glutathione conjugation, the conjugate is trimmed by gamma-glutamyl peptidase (GGP1) and cleaved by the C-S lyase SUR1 to a thiohydroximate, which is S-glucosylated (UGT74C1/UGT74B1) and finally sulfated from PAPS by desulfoglucosinolate sulfotransferases (SOT16-18); and (3) side-chain modification, in which flavin monooxygenases (FMO GS-OX) S-oxygenate methylthioalkyl to methylsulfinylalkyl glucosinolates and the 2-oxoglutarate-dependent dioxygenase AOP2 converts these to alkenyl glucosinolates such as allylglucosinolate (sinigrin). The intact glucosinolate is biologically inert. On tissue damage, thioglucosidases (myrosinases, TGG1/TGG2) hydrolyze it to an unstable aglucone that rearranges by default to an isothiocyanate; Fe(II)-dependent specifier proteins (ESP, NSPs, TFP) instead redirect the aglucone to epithionitriles, simple nitriles or organic thiocyanates. Natural variation at MAM, AOP and ESP loci accounts for much of the glucosinolate and breakdown-product diversity between accessions and species. Transcriptional regulation (MYB28/MYB29 with MYC2-4), glucosinolate transport (GTR1/GTR2), indole and aromatic glucosinolates, and sulfate activation to PAPS are outside this module's boundary.

# Aliphatic Glucosinolate Biosynthesis and Myrosinase Activation: The Glucosinolate-Myrosinase Defense System

## 1. Executive Summary

The glucosinolate-myrosinase system represents a paradigmatic two-component chemical defense mechanism characteristic of the plant order Brassicales. This review focuses specifically on methionine-derived (aliphatic) glucosinolate biosynthesis and the damage-activated myrosinase hydrolysis that generates bioactive defensive compounds. Aliphatic glucosinolate biosynthesis proceeds through three major phases: (1) plastidial methionine side-chain elongation via a leucine biosynthesis-derived cycle (BCAT4, MAM, IPMI, IPMDH1, BCAT3); (2) cytosolic assembly of the core glucosinolate structure through aldoxime formation, sulfur conjugation, and sulfation (CYP79F, CYP83A1, glutathione S-transferases, GGP1, SUR1, UGT74, SOT16–18); and (3) optional side-chain modification (FMO GS-OX, AOP2). The intact glucosinolate remains biologically inert until tissue damage brings it into contact with thioglucosidases (myrosinases TGG1/TGG2), which hydrolyze the molecule to an unstable aglucone. This aglucone rearranges by default to an isothiocyanate, or, when Fe(II)-dependent specifier proteins (ESP, NSP, TFP) are present, to epithionitriles, simple nitriles, or thiocyanates. Natural variation at MAM, AOP, and ESP loci accounts for much of the glucosinolate and breakdown-product diversity observed across accessions and species (chhajed2020glucosinolatebiosynthesisand pages 3-5, qin2023developingmultifunctionalcrops pages 4-5, bansal2024geneticvariationin pages 2-3, parchem2020enzymaticactivitiesbehind pages 1-4, roman2020molecularmodelingof pages 6-7, roman2020molecularmodelingof pages 1-3).

## 2. Definition and Biological Boundaries

### 2.1 Scope of the Module

This review defines the aliphatic glucosinolate-myrosinase defense system as encompassing:

**Phase 1: Methionine side-chain elongation.** An iterative plastidial cycle in which methionine is deaminated to 4-methylthio-2-oxobutanoate (BCAT4), condensed with acetyl-CoA (MAM), isomerized (IPMI), oxidatively decarboxylated (IPMDH1), and transaminated (BCAT3) to yield chain-elongated methionine homologs—homomethionine, dihomomethionine, trihomomethionine, and longer species. Each turn adds one methylene group to the side chain (kitainda2021structuralstudiesof pages 2-4, kitainda2024molecularmechanismsbehind pages 25-29, medhanavyn2024characterizationandapplications pages 11-18).

**Phase 2: Core glucosinolate structure formation.** A cytosolic pathway converting chain-elongated amino acids to the shared glucosinolate scaffold. CYP79F converts the amino acid to an aldoxime; CYP83A1 activates the aldoxime for glutathione conjugation via GSTF11/GSTU20; the conjugate is trimmed by gamma-glutamyl peptidase GGP1 and cleaved by the C–S lyase SUR1 to a thiohydroximate; S-glucosylation by UGT74C1/UGT74B1 produces desulfoglucosinolate; and sulfation from PAPS by SOT16–18 completes the core structure (qin2023developingmultifunctionalcrops pages 4-5, chhajed2020glucosinolatebiosynthesisand pages 3-5, bansal2024geneticvariationin pages 2-3, soundararajan2021influenceofgenotype pages 14-15).

**Phase 3: Side-chain modification.** Optional transformations that diversify glucosinolate structure. Flavin monooxygenases (FMO GS-OX) S-oxygenate methylthioalkyl to methylsulfinylalkyl glucosinolates; the 2-oxoglutarate-dependent dioxygenase AOP2 converts these to alkenyl glucosinolates such as sinigrin (ghidoli2023camelinasativa(l.) pages 8-10, chhajed2020glucosinolatebiosynthesisand pages 3-5, bansal2024geneticvariationin pages 2-3).

**Damage-triggered hydrolysis and product specification.** Thioglucosidases (myrosinases TGG1/TGG2) hydrolyze intact glucosinolates to an unstable aglucone that rearranges by default to an isothiocyanate. Fe(II)-dependent specifier proteins (ESP, NSP, TFP) instead redirect the aglucone to epithionitriles, simple nitriles, or organic thiocyanates (parchem2020enzymaticactivitiesbehind pages 1-4, roman2020molecularmodelingof pages 6-7, roman2020molecularmodelingof pages 1-3, blazevic2020glucosinolatestructuraldiversity pages 36-37).

### 2.2 Excluded Components

The following neighboring processes are outside this module's boundary:

- **Indole and aromatic glucosinolates:** tryptophan- and phenylalanine-derived glucosinolates use distinct biosynthetic routes although they share core-assembly enzymes (chhajed2020glucosinolatebiosynthesisand pages 3-5, chhajed2020glucosinolatebiosynthesisand pages 1-3).
- **Transcriptional regulation:** MYB28/MYB29 master regulators and MYC2-4 co-regulators control biosynthetic gene expression but are not themselves enzymatic components of the pathway (tandayu2022doesitcut pages 40-43, chhajed2020glucosinolatebiosynthesisand pages 5-7, ali2025impactsofsulfur pages 3-5).
- **Glucosinolate transport:** GTR1/GTR2 transporters mediate long-distance movement but do not participate in biosynthesis or hydrolysis (bansal2024geneticvariationin pages 10-11).
- **Sulfate activation to PAPS:** ATP sulfurylase and APS kinase supply the sulfate donor PAPS but operate as part of general sulfur metabolism rather than glucosinolate-specific machinery (ghidoli2023camelinasativa(l.) pages 8-10).

### 2.3 Competing Definitions

The literature generally agrees on the three-phase biosynthetic organization and the two-component defense architecture. However, several points of ambiguity persist:

- **Intermediate identity:** The exact chemical identity of the CYP83A1 product remains unresolved, with competing descriptions as an "aci-nitro compound" or "nitrile oxide"; these intermediates are short-lived and have not been isolated (chhajed2020glucosinolatebiosynthesisand pages 3-5).
- **PEN2 versus TGG myrosinases:** Some reviews include the atypical myrosinase PEN2 in glucosinolate defense, but its restricted localization, pathogen-specific activation, and distinct substrate range suggest it operates in a separate pathogen-response module rather than the canonical damage-triggered system reviewed here (chhajed2020glucosinolatebiosynthesisand pages 9-11).
- **Specifier protein classification:** The specifier protein families (ESP, NSP, TFP) show overlapping activities—ESP can produce nitriles, TFP can produce nitriles or epithionitriles—so product-based naming does not fully capture their substrate- and condition-dependent biochemistry (blazevic2020glucosinolatestructuraldiversity pages 36-37, roman2020molecularmodelingof pages 8-9, blazevic2020glucosinolatestructuraldiversity pages 35-36).

## 3. Mechanistic Overview

### 3.1 Three-Phase Biosynthetic Sequence

Aliphatic glucosinolate biosynthesis initiates with methionine and proceeds through sequential biochemical phases distributed across cellular compartments (chhajed2020glucosinolatebiosynthesisand pages 3-5, qin2023developingmultifunctionalcrops pages 4-5, kitainda2021structuralstudiesof pages 2-4).

**Phase 1 (plastidial chain elongation):** Cytosolic BCAT4 converts methionine to 4-methylthio-2-oxobutanoate (4-MTOB), which is imported into the chloroplast, probably via the BAT5 transporter (kitainda2024molecularmechanismsbehind pages 16-20, kitainda2021structuralstudiesof pages 2-4). Inside the plastid, MAM condenses 4-MTOB with acetyl-CoA to form a 2-malate derivative. IPMI stereospecifically isomerizes this to a 3-malate derivative, and IPMDH1 oxidatively decarboxylates it to a one-carbon-elongated 2-oxo acid. BCAT3 then transaminates the product to homomethionine, which can exit for core assembly or re-enter the cycle for additional elongation (kitainda2024molecularmechanismsbehind pages 25-29, medhanavyn2024characterizationandapplications pages 11-18, kitainda2024molecularmechanismsbehind pages 121-125). MAM isoform specificity determines how many cycles occur: in Arabidopsis, MAM1/MAM2 favor C3–C5 products, whereas MAM3 supports C6–C8 elongation (kitainda2024molecularmechanismsbehind pages 20-25, kitainda2021structuralstudiesof pages 6-8).

**Phase 2 (cytosolic core assembly):** CYP79F1/F2 convert the chain-elongated amino acid to an aldoxime. CYP83A1 oxidizes the aldoxime to a reactive intermediate that GSTF11/GSTU20 conjugate with glutathione. GGP1 removes the γ-glutamyl residue, SUR1 cleaves the cysteine conjugate to a thiohydroximate, UGT74C1 S-glucosylates the thiohydroximate to desulfoglucosinolate, and SOT17/SOT18 transfer sulfate from PAPS to produce the intact glucosinolate (qin2023developingmultifunctionalcrops pages 4-5, chhajed2020glucosinolatebiosynthesisand pages 3-5, bansal2024geneticvariationin pages 2-3, kitainda2021structuralstudiesof pages 2-4).

**Phase 3 (side-chain diversification):** FMO GS-OX enzymes S-oxygenate methylthioalkyl side chains to methylsulfinylalkyl forms (e.g., glucoerucin → glucoraphanin). AOP2 then converts selected methylsulfinylalkyl glucosinolates to alkenyl glucosinolates (e.g., glucoiberin → sinigrin) (ghidoli2023camelinasativa(l.) pages 8-10, chhajed2020glucosinolatebiosynthesisand pages 3-5, bansal2024geneticvariationin pages 2-3).

### 3.2 Damage-Triggered Activation

Intact glucosinolates are biologically inert and spatially separated from myrosinases: glucosinolates accumulate in vacuoles of various cell types, whereas myrosinases (TGG1, TGG2) are localized in specialized myrosin cells, phloem-associated cells, and guard cells (parchem2020enzymaticactivitiesbehind pages 1-4, blazevic2020glucosinolatestructuraldiversity pages 34-35, roman2020molecularmodelingof pages 3-5). Tissue damage—from herbivory, pathogen attack, or mechanical disruption—brings these components together. TGG myrosinases hydrolyze the thioglucosidic bond, releasing glucose and an unstable sulfate-bearing aglucone (parchem2020enzymaticactivitiesbehind pages 1-4, chhajed2020glucosinolatebiosynthesisand pages 9-11, blazevic2020glucosinolatestructuraldiversity pages 36-37).

In the absence of specifier proteins, the aglucone spontaneously rearranges to an isothiocyanate, which is volatile, lipophilic, and toxic to many herbivores and pathogens (roman2020molecularmodelingof pages 6-7, roman2020molecularmodelingof pages 1-3, blazevic2020glucosinolatestructuraldiversity pages 36-37). When Fe(II)-dependent specifier proteins are present, they redirect the aglucone: ESP promotes epithionitrile formation from alkenyl glucosinolates or nitrile formation from other substrates; NSP promotes simple nitrile formation; and TFP promotes thiocyanate formation from a restricted substrate set (allyl, benzyl, 4-methylsulfanylbutyl glucosinolates) (sikorskazimny2021themetabolismof pages 5-7, parchem2020enzymaticactivitiesbehind pages 8-11, blazevic2020glucosinolatestructuraldiversity pages 36-37, parchem2020enzymaticactivitiesbehind pages 11-15, roman2020molecularmodelingof pages 8-9, blazevic2020glucosinolatestructuraldiversity pages 35-36). Product outcome thus depends on glucosinolate side-chain structure, specifier protein genotype, pH, and metal availability (roman2020molecularmodelingof pages 7-8, roman2020molecularmodelingof pages 8-9, parchem2020enzymaticactivitiesbehind pages 1-4).

### 4.1 Chain-Elongation Machinery

The plastidial chain-elongation cycle was recruited from leucine biosynthesis through gene duplication and neofunctionalization (kitainda2021structuralstudiesof pages 2-4, kitainda2024molecularmechanismsbehind pages 118-121). MAMS evolved from α-isopropylmalate synthase (IPMS) by substitutions in five active-site residues (L143I, H167L, S216G, N250G, P252G), conferring specificity for methionine-derived 2-oxo acids (das2021glucosinolatebiosynthesisrole pages 3-4). IPMDH1 diverged from the leucine-pathway IPMDH2/3 through a leucine-to-phenylalanine substitution that altered substrate preference (kitainda2024molecularmechanismsbehind pages 121-125, kitainda2021structuralstudiesof pages 6-8). Glucosinolate-specialized IPMI small subunits (SSU2, SSU3) pair with the shared large subunit LSU1 to support longer-chain elongation (kitainda2024molecularmechanismsbehind pages 121-125, kitainda2024molecularmechanismsbehind pages 29-33).

### 4.2 Core-Assembly Enzymes

CYP79F and CYP83A1 are cytochrome P450 monooxygenases whose sequential action introduces nitrogen into the glucosinolate scaffold. Glutathione S-transferases GSTF11 and GSTU20 have partially overlapping but nonredundant roles: double mutants lose aliphatic glucosinolates more severely than either single mutant, and GSTU20 appears more critical than GSTF11 (kitainda2021structuralstudiesof pages 2-4). SUR1 is a C–S lyase whose unique substrate specificity makes it the only identified enzyme capable of producing the thiohydroximate intermediate in most contexts, although recent evidence suggests additional uncharacterized C–S lyases may contribute (chhajed2020glucosinolatebiosynthesisand pages 3-5, soundararajan2021influenceofgenotype pages 14-15).

### 4.3 Myrosinase-Specifier Protein Complexes

Classical myrosinases TGG1 and TGG2 are glycosylated β-glucosidases requiring ascorbate for full activity (chhajed2020glucosinolatebiosynthesisand pages 9-11, roman2020molecularmodelingof pages 5-6). TGG1 is far more abundant than TGG2 in Arabidopsis leaves, and the double tgg1 × tgg2 mutant loses detectable hydrolysis of aliphatic glucosinolates, confirming their major role (roman2020molecularmodelingof pages 5-6). Specifier proteins do not themselves hydrolyze glucosinolates; they act on the short-lived myrosinase-generated aglucone and require physical proximity to myrosinase during the hydrolysis event (roman2020molecularmodelingof pages 6-7, roman2020molecularmodelingof pages 7-8, roman2020molecularmodelingof pages 8-9). ESP function requires Fe2+ and is sensitive to pH; NSP activity is independent of terminal double bonds but still Fe-dependent (roman2020molecularmodelingof pages 7-8, roman2020molecularmodelingof pages 8-9).

## 5. Evolutionary and Cell-Biological Variation

### 5.1 Distribution Across Brassicales

Glucosinolates are defining chemical characteristics of the order Brassicales, occurring across sixteen plant families and approximately 4,700 species, although they are most intensively studied in Brassicaceae (chhajed2020glucosinolatebiosynthesisand pages 1-3, bansal2024geneticvariationin pages 1-2). The glucosinolate-myrosinase pairing is sufficiently widespread to represent a conserved defensive module, though the complete conservation of all pathway components across the order has not been exhaustively documented (chhajed2020glucosinolatebiosynthesisand pages 1-3, kitainda2021structuralstudiesof pages 9-11).

### 5.2 Natural Variation and Chemotype Diversity

**MAM loci:** Variation at MAM determines side-chain length and thereby glucosinolate chemotype. In Brassica nigra, high expression of BniMAM1-2 correlates with accumulation of 3C/4C aliphatic glucosinolates including sinigrin, whereas MAM3 loss is associated with reduced longer-chain products (li2021transcriptomeprofilingreveals pages 17-21, li2021transcriptomeprofilingreveals pages 14-17). In Arabidopsis, Col-0 lost MAMc, but other Arabidopsis species retain it, illustrating lineage-specific gene retention (kitainda2024molecularmechanismsbehind pages 20-25). Brassica juncea possesses four MAMS isoforms with distinct elongation capacities, resulting from genome duplication and allotetraploidy (kitainda2024molecularmechanismsbehind pages 20-25, das2021glucosinolatebiosynthesisrole pages 3-4).

**AOP loci:** AOP2 converts methylsulfinylalkyl glucosinolates to alkenyl forms (e.g., glucoraphanin → gluconapin, glucoiberin → sinigrin), whereas AOP3 produces hydroxyalkyl glucosinolates (ghidoli2023camelinasativa(l.) pages 8-10, chhajed2020glucosinolatebiosynthesisand pages 3-5, kitainda2024molecularmechanismsbehind pages 20-25). Brassica oleracea has nonfunctional AOP2 alleles due to premature stop codons, leading to glucoraphanin accumulation; conversely, functional AOP2 is highly expressed in Brassica nigra and Brassica rapa, explaining their high sinigrin content (li2021transcriptomeprofilingreveals pages 17-21, soundararajan2021influenceofgenotype pages 14-15).

**ESP loci:** Arabidopsis accession Col-0 lacks functional ESP due to a 10-bp promoter deletion, whereas Ler and Cvi possess active ESP (roman2020molecularmodelingof pages 6-7, roman2020molecularmodelingof pages 7-8). ESP presence redirects breakdown toward epithionitriles (if alkenyl substrates are available) or nitriles (with other substrates), substantially altering the defensive chemical profile (roman2020molecularmodelingof pages 6-7, roman2020molecularmodelingof pages 7-8).

### 5.3 Tissue, Cell-Type, and Developmental Variation

Glucosinolate levels and profiles vary widely across organs. In Brassica juncea, seeds accumulate approximately 93% of total plant glucosinolates at maturity, whereas leaves, roots, siliques, and stems contain distinct profiles (tandayu2022doesitcut pages 43-46). In Arabidopsis leaves, glucosinolates concentrate in S-cells near the phloem, whereas TGG1 myrosinase is expressed in scattered myrosin cells, phloem-associated cells, and guard cells (roman2020molecularmodelingof pages 3-5, tandayu2022doesitcut pages 43-46). This spatial segregation is central to the "mustard oil bomb" defense architecture (blazevic2020glucosinolatestructuraldiversity pages 34-35, parchem2020enzymaticactivitiesbehind pages 1-4, roman2020molecularmodelingof pages 1-3).

TGG1 and TGG2 are primarily expressed in shoots, whereas TGG4 and TGG5 are expressed in roots, and TGG3 and TGG6 in specific floral tissues (chhajed2020glucosinolatebiosynthesisand pages 9-11, roman2020molecularmodelingof pages 3-5, roman2020molecularmodelingof pages 1-3). Developmental regulation also influences specifier protein expression: ESP localization and activity vary with tissue, genotype, and developmental stage (roman2020molecularmodelingof pages 7-8).

## 6. Physical and Biological Constraints

### 6.1 Ordered Biosynthetic Steps

The three-phase biosynthetic sequence is obligatory. Chain elongation must precede core assembly because CYP79F enzymes require chain-elongated amino acids as substrates (kitainda2021structuralstudiesof pages 2-4, chhajed2020glucosinolatebiosynthesisand pages 3-5). Core assembly follows a fixed order: aldoxime formation → sulfur conjugation → cleavage to thiohydroximate → S-glucosylation → sulfation. Side-chain modification by FMO GS-OX and AOP2 occurs after core assembly and acts on intact glucosinolates (ghidoli2023camelinasativa(l.) pages 8-10, chhajed2020glucosinolatebiosynthesisand pages 3-5).

### 6.2 Compartment-Specific Constraints

Chain elongation is strictly plastidial, requiring import of cytosolic 4-MTOB into the chloroplast (kitainda2024molecularmechanismsbehind pages 16-20, ghidoli2023camelinasativa(l.) pages 8-10, kitainda2021structuralstudiesof pages 2-4). Core assembly is cytosolic, involving microsomal P450 enzymes (CYP79F, CYP83A1) and cytosolic glycosyltransferases and sulfotransferases (ghidoli2023camelinasativa(l.) pages 8-10, kitainda2021structuralstudiesof pages 2-4). This compartmentalization demands coordinated trafficking: elongated 2-oxo acids must exit the plastid and be transaminated by BCAT3 or BCAT4 before CYP79F can act (qin2023developingmultifunctionalcrops pages 4-5, kitainda2024molecularmechanismsbehind pages 16-20).

Myrosinase-mediated activation requires physical contact between enzyme and substrate, which under normal conditions are separated across cell types (myrosin cells versus S-cells/vacuoles) or subcellular compartments (blazevic2020glucosinolatestructuraldiversity pages 34-35, parchem2020enzymaticactivitiesbehind pages 1-4, roman2020molecularmodelingof pages 1-3). Disruption of this separation through tissue damage, pathogen ingress, or other perturbations is the obligatory trigger for hydrolysis (parchem2020enzymaticactivitiesbehind pages 1-4).

### 6.3 Substrate and Product Constraints

Epithionitrile formation by ESP strictly requires a terminal double bond in the glucosinolate side chain; without alkenyl substrates, ESP instead produces simple nitriles (roman2020molecularmodelingof pages 7-8, roman2020molecularmodelingof pages 8-9). Thiocyanate formation by TFP is restricted to a small set of substrates capable of generating resonance-stabilized cations (allyl, benzyl, 4-methylsulfanylbutyl glucosinolates) (blazevic2020glucosinolatestructuraldiversity pages 36-37, blazevic2020glucosinolatestructuraldiversity pages 35-36). Isothiocyanate formation is the default pathway when specifier proteins are absent or when substrate structure precludes alternative products (blazevic2020glucosinolatestructuraldiversity pages 36-37, parchem2020enzymaticactivitiesbehind pages 11-15, blazevic2020glucosinolatestructuraldiversity pages 35-36).

## 7. Constraints, Dependencies, and Failure Modes

### 7.1 Metabolic Dependencies

Glucosinolate biosynthesis is sulfur-intensive, requiring sulfate for PAPS synthesis and for incorporation into the methionine precursor (ali2025impactsofsulfur pages 3-5, ghidoli2023camelinasativa(l.) pages 8-10). Under sulfur deficiency, the transcriptional repressor SLIM1/EIL3 suppresses glucosinolate biosynthetic genes and promotes glucosinolate catabolism to reallocate sulfur to primary metabolism (ali2025impactsofsulfur pages 3-5, ghidoli2023camelinasativa(l.) pages 8-10). SDI1 and SDI2 proteins further repress biosynthesis by interacting with MYB28 and blocking its transcriptional activation (ali2025impactsofsulfur pages 3-5, ghidoli2023camelinasativa(l.) pages 8-10).

### 7.2 Regulatory Dependencies

Biosynthesis requires coordinated transcriptional activation by MYB28, MYB29, and MYB76 (for aliphatic glucosinolates) and by MYC2, MYC3, and MYC4 (for jasmonate-responsive induction) (tandayu2022doesitcut pages 40-43, chhajed2020glucosinolatebiosynthesisand pages 5-7, yan2025transcriptionalactivationof pages 1-2, gantait2024glucosinolatesmediatedregulation pages 8-10). Triple myc2/myc3/myc4 mutants or myb28/myb29 double mutants lose most aliphatic glucosinolate accumulation, demonstrating the obligatory nature of these regulators (tandayu2022doesitcut pages 40-43, chhajed2020glucosinolatebiosynthesisand pages 5-7, yan2025transcriptionalactivationof pages 1-2).

### 7.3 Failure Modes and Pathogen Evasion

Pathogens can evade or overcome the glucosinolate-myrosinase system through multiple strategies: avoiding tissue damage that would activate myrosinase, degrading intact glucosinolates before hydrolysis, inhibiting myrosinase activity, or detoxifying breakdown products (rahimi2020overcomingglucosinolatemyrosinaseisothiocyanatedefense pages 1-3, rahimi2020overcomingglucosinolatemyrosinaseisothiocyanatedefense pages 6-8). For example, Leptosphaeria maculans infection does not activate the system, and Sclerotinia sclerotiorum can colonize isothiocyanate-rich tissues by unknown mechanisms (rahimi2020overcomingglucosinolatemyrosinaseisothiocyanatedefense pages 6-8). Insect specialists such as Phyllotreta armoraciae rapidly absorb glucosinolates across the gut epithelium and inactivate ingested myrosinases, thereby tolerating and even sequestering glucosinolates for their own defense (parchem2020enzymaticactivitiesbehind pages 1-4, rahimi2020overcomingglucosinolatemyrosinaseisothiocyanatedefense pages 3-6).

## 8. Controversies and Open Questions

### 8.1 Mechanistic Uncertainties

**Intermediate identity:** The exact chemical nature of the CYP83A1 product remains unresolved. Described as an "aci-nitro compound" or "nitrile oxide," this short-lived intermediate has not been isolated or structurally characterized, leaving the mechanism of glutathione conjugation uncertain (chhajed2020glucosinolatebiosynthesisand pages 3-5).

**Alternative C–S lyases:** Although SUR1 is the canonical C–S lyase, recent evidence suggests additional uncharacterized enzymes may contribute to thiohydroximate formation, challenging the single-enzyme model (chhajed2020glucosinolatebiosynthesisand pages 3-5).

**Myrosinase-independent hydrolysis:** Brassica napus seeds lacking the Myr1 myrosinase gene can still produce bioactive glucosinolate breakdown products, raising the possibility of alternative myrosinases or non-enzymatic degradation pathways (parchem2020enzymaticactivitiesbehind pages 4-8).

### 8.2 Regulation and Compartmentation

**PEN2 versus TGG activation:** The mechanisms distinguishing pathogen-triggered activation of the atypical myrosinase PEN2 from damage-triggered activation of TGG1/TGG2 remain unclear, including how pathogens or elicitors selectively engage one pathway versus the other (chhajed2020glucosinolatebiosynthesisand pages 9-11).

**Intact-tissue turnover:** Recent evidence suggests glucosinolates can be hydrolyzed in intact, undamaged tissue and contribute to signaling or anti-pathogen defense, but the mechanisms, biological significance, and regulation of this process are poorly understood (roman2020molecularmodelingof pages 1-3, chhajed2020glucosinolatebiosynthesisand pages 7-9).

**Redox and nutrient regulation:** Although redox state and nutrient availability (sulfur, nitrogen, potassium, manganese, selenium) alter glucosinolate profiles, the molecular mechanisms connecting these environmental inputs to biosynthetic enzyme regulation are largely unknown (chhajed2020glucosinolatebiosynthesisand pages 7-9).

### 8.3 Product Specification and Biological Roles

**Specifier protein complexes:** The composition and regulation of myrosinase-associated protein complexes remain incompletely understood. Proteins such as myrosinase-binding protein, myrosinase-associated protein, and epithiospecifier modifier protein influence product outcome, but their precise molecular interactions and substrate specificities are not established (chhajed2020glucosinolatebiosynthesisand pages 9-11, parchem2020enzymaticactivitiesbehind pages 4-8).

**Biological roles of alternative products:** While isothiocyanates are well established as toxic defensive compounds, much less is known about the ecological and physiological roles of nitriles, epithionitriles, and thiocyanates. Whether these products serve as defense compounds, signals, or metabolic intermediates remains an active area of investigation (roman2020molecularmodelingof pages 1-3, chhajed2020glucosinolatebiosynthesisand pages 1-3).

**Pathogen detoxification mechanisms:** The strategies pathogens use to overcome the glucosinolate-myrosinase system—including membrane modification, enzymatic detoxification, and nutritional exploitation—have been hypothesized but not mechanistically resolved (rahimi2020overcomingglucosinolatemyrosinaseisothiocyanatedefense pages 1-3, rahimi2020overcomingglucosinolatemyrosinaseisothiocyanatedefense pages 6-8).

### 8.4 Broader System Questions

**Heterologous pathway engineering:** Studies in Nicotiana benthamiana and other non-Brassicales hosts have demonstrated that glucosinolate biosynthesis can be reconstituted in heterologous systems, but substrate specificities, flux control, and enzyme compatibility differ from native contexts, raising questions about how faithfully engineered pathways reflect natural diversity (chhajed2020glucosinolatebiosynthesisand pages 3-5).

**Crosstalk with other pathways:** Newly identified regulatory interactions between glucosinolate metabolism and jasmonate, salicylate, auxin, brassinosteroid, phenylpropanoid, flavonoid, and other pathways suggest extensive metabolic integration, but the mechanisms and physiological consequences of these interactions remain largely unresolved (chhajed2020glucosinolatebiosynthesisand pages 7-9).
