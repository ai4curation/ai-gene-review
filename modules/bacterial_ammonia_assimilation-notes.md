# Bacterial NADPH-dependent GS–GOGAT ammonia assimilation: audit notes

## 2026-10-26 — source and comparative-panel foundation

Audit 30 is in progress. No completed gene-review panel, final module assessment, or full-validation result is claimed here. The isolated worktree was created from published main `208f69f8856861536f7208bca23014765b66cf19`, with an independent index and a clean starting tree.

The existing module intentionally covers the NADPH-dependent, two-subunit glutamate-synthase implementation. Ferredoxin-dependent and NADH-dependent GOGAT enzymes are useful boundary comparators, but their existence does not establish the NADPH route. Likewise, the conserved two-reaction chemistry does not imply identical nitrogen regulation, physiological necessity, flux, or glutamine-synthetase family membership in every bacterium.

### Literature leads and what each can establish

- [PMID:22625175](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22625175/), a Bacillus glutamate-homeostasis review, distinguishes GS–GOGAT synthesis from native catabolic glutamate dehydrogenase and describes regulatory functions of these metabolic enzymes. Its organism-specific account should qualify the generic GDH alternative language.
- [Glutamate metabolism in Bacillus subtilis, PMC2395005](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2395005/) and [the native GDH study, PMC107716](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC107716/) provide primary tests separating reversible activity in vitro from physiological assimilation. Native RocG/activated GudB did not replace GOGAT under tested conditions; this is not an assertion that the enzymes cannot catalyze reductive amination in vitro.
- [PMID:10785395](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10785395/) characterizes recombinant **Azospirillum brasilense** GOGAT and isolated subunits. It supports subunit chemistry and assembly for the assayed enzyme; it must not be presented as a native E. coli or KT2440 experiment.
- [The GOGAT structure–function review, DOI:10.1002/iub.52](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/iub.52) is a source for comparing electron donors, redox cofactors, subunit architecture and ammonia transfer. Full source inspection remains necessary before mining individual claims.
- [The E. coli systems review, PMC3973380](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3973380/) and [Reitzer's review, DOI:10.1146/annurev.micro.57.030502.090820](https://www.annualreviews.org/content/journals/10.1146/annurev.micro.57.030502.090820) separate catalytic assimilation from its transport, metabolic and regulatory context. These neighboring functions remain relevant to individual gene reviews even when not required by the minimal module.
- [PMID:11700347](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11700347/) reports that Corynebacterium glutamicum gltB/gltD disruption has little growth impact under the tested regimes. This directly cautions against equating a conserved enzymatic route with universal physiological dependence. Its abstract is accessible; full text and exact assayed-protein linkage are still being recovered.

A recent Bacillus review, [DOI:10.1128/mmbr.00430-25](https://journals.asm.org/doi/10.1128/mmbr.00430-25), also identifies regulatory functions of GS, GOGAT and GDH. Its full text and publication metadata must be checked before final reference curation.

### Initial audit of the three inherited KT2440 reviews

The existing GlnA/GltB core support sometimes cites GO_REF text that merely restates an assigned annotation. Replace that circular support with curated RuleBase/PIRSR statements where available, independent domain/family evidence, and correctly scoped primary literature. Most GltB UniProt free text is ARBA-only; it cannot independently validate the annotation under review. Its experimentally informed domain architecture can instead support a transparent comparative inference.

Existing GltB and GltD MODIFY reasons propose changing ordinary `enables` relationships to `contributes_to`. Ordinary GOA relationship fields are immutable and do not determine this audit's actions. Judge the biological coupled-enzyme claim, preserve the machine payloads, and explain subunit contributions in core functions and evidence. Existing broad nitrogen, catalytic, glutamine-metabolism and cofactor terms also require biological refinement instead of rejection or non-core classification solely because they are general.

The inherited GlnA membrane rejection argues that a soluble oligomer with no transmembrane helices cannot reside at membranes. That inference is too strong: absence of integral membrane architecture does not exclude peripheral association. Trace the actual annotation provenance and seek independent localization evidence before deciding the row. These are identified issues for the systematic annotation review, not yet completed replacement judgments.

The final module preserves the original six exemplars and includes the complete 12-product PSEPK, ECOLI, BACSU and AZOBR review panel. All six family labels were checked against the pinned ontology, six members against PANTHER release 19 classifications and all 12 against an independent current UniProt lookup. No unsupported ancestral node is required by this concrete module.

The four alternative routes are complete cycles rather than independent choices at each reaction. This prevents a Pseudomonas GS from satisfying an E. coli route or a Bacillus large chain from completing an Azospirillum GOGAT. A GOGAT activity requires both native subunits. The generic activity compiler does not independently enforce active units or shared cellular context, so the accompanying oracle resolves exact products and recursively requires both units. All 4,096 subsets agree with the independent complete-native-triple expectation: 1,695 contain a complete route and 2,401 do not. These are capacity checks, not claims about expression, measured assembly, flux or growth sufficiency. Both bare-atom accession matching and an “any chain present” oracle produce documented counterexamples.

### Mined review findings and primary corroboration

The full [E. coli systems review, PMID:24296575](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24296575/) was recovered through the normal PMC URL cache. Its central-assimilation and component sections explain the reciprocal glutamine/glutamate coupling, ATP cost, NADPH-GOGAT subunits, alternative GDH pathway and surrounding transport/regulatory network. The canonical PMID cache remains abstract-only; quotations from the recovered body cite the actual URL cache. [Reitzer, PMID:12730324](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12730324/) supplies abstract-accessible synthesis of global nitrogen regulation; its publisher returned HTTP 403. Neither review makes all regulatory proteins mandatory parts of the minimal catalytic cycle.

[PMID:18421771](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18421771/) explicitly describes the amidotransferase/FMN synthase architecture, intramolecular ammonia tunnel and FAD-dependent pyridine-nucleotide module. Full publisher retrieval returned HTTP 403; its usable abstract findings are corroborated by primary recombinant [PMID:10785395](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10785395/), large-chain structure [PMID:11188694](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11188694/), cryo-EM [PMID:31473159](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31473159/) and full native Bacillus [PMID:34931064](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34931064/). The two [4Fe–4S] centers and FAD belong to the small chain; FMN and [3Fe–4S] belong to the large chain. The catalytic alpha/beta unit is distinguished from condition-dependent higher oligomers. The word “essential” in the review title does not imply universal growth dependence.

[PMID:22625175](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22625175/) supplies the Bacillus-specific synthesis of anabolic GS–GOGAT, catabolic GDH and metabolic-enzyme regulatory functions. Its abstract was mined, with publisher full-text recovery returning HTTP 403. Primary [PMID:18326565](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18326565/) distinguishes native RocG/GudB anabolic failure under studied conditions from heterologous E. coli GDH rescue; this is physiology, not chemical irreversibility. Full [PMID:24158439](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24158439/) establishes native GS substrate/metal chemistry. Full [PMID:35778410](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35778410/) and the member-review sources distinguish GlnR-repressor activation from TnrA inhibition and qualify GS oligomeric states. These other GlnA activities remain in the full gene review without becoming obligatory catalytic-cycle components.

Every GS reaction, GOGAT reaction, active-unit role and reciprocal substrate/product connection now carries actual source evidence. GS synthesis and GOGAT consumption establish the glutamine link; two-glutamate output and the GS glutamate requirement establish cycle closure. This is stoichiometric coupling through metabolite pools, not proof of direct GS-to-GOGAT channeling or a stable supercomplex. Primary [PMID:2887545](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2887545/) adds native species-level isotope/inhibitor corroboration. The PSEPK GS step cites its independent RuleBase reaction and actual native glnA-expression evidence; the GltB/GltD fitness quotation is attached to the GOGAT step and broader physiology, not used as GlnA-specific enzymology.

### Taxon, strain and physiological boundaries

- PSEPK products are KT2440. Native [PMID:35285712](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35285712/) provides nitrogen-source-dependent fitness and exact locus context; comparative enzymology and target domains provide the subunit mechanism. The study does not supply purified KT2440 kinetic constants or show uniform essentiality.
- ECOLI products are K-12. [PMID:2448295](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2448295/) restores GOGAT activity by complementation but uses a polar gltB insertion for broader nitrogen phenotypes. Those phenotypes do not establish direct GltB transcriptional regulation. Historical strain W and modern other-species structures remain explicitly comparative; the ECOLI gene notes resolve the stripped-subunit assays of PMID:770440 without assigning the physiological 2-oxoglutarate center to the small chain.
- BACSU products are strain 168. Full PMID:34931064 uses NCIB3610, whose GudB is active; strain 168 GudB is cryptic. Conserved catalytic architecture transfers with exact-target domain support, but the active-GudB counter-enzyme physiology does not automatically transfer. GltAB heterodimer and GudB-associated higher assembly are distinct.
- AZOBR products retain their Sp7 genomic provenance. Native Sp6 purification, strain-unspecified recombinant/cryo-EM work and species-level tracer experiments are not relabeled as a single matched Sp7 preparation. Raw GltB also has a partial RG sequence source and GltD a partial Sp6 protein source; these do not replace the principal Sp7 gene provenance.

No common high-affinity kinetic constant or universal GS–GOGAT dependence is inferred. The Corynebacterium [PMID:11700347](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11700347/) abstract supports little growth impairment after GOGAT loss in its tested conditions; unresolved exact-sequence/strain linkage prevents adding a Corynebacterium route. Ferredoxin- and NADH-linked GOGAT are alternative mechanisms outside this NADPH module. Ammonium transport, nitrogen fixation/reduction, GDH assimilation, PII/Ntr signaling and regulator interactions are important neighboring biology rather than required additional reactions.
