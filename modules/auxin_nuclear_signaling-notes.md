# Canonical nuclear auxin signaling: taxon and mechanism audit

## Boundary and native implementations

This module covers canonical TIR1/AFB–Aux/IAA–ARF transcriptional signaling in land plants (Embryophyta), with explicitly grounded Arabidopsis and Marchantia implementations. ARF DNA-regulatory capacity is older: the full 2026 comparative review PMID42590969 states that “algal ARFs are not connected to a canonical auxin response pathway.” Thus streptophyte ARF homology alone does not establish this entire circuit. The taxon envelope describes the supported canonical architecture, not a proof of identical physiology in every land plant.

The 14 reviewed exemplars are Arabidopsis TIR1, AFB2, IAA12, IAA17, IAA19, TPL, ARF5, ARF7 and ARF19, and Marchantia MpTIR1, MpIAA, MpTPL, MpARF1 and MpARF2. Exact gene-product selectors determine the asserted role. Each accompanying PANTHER descriptor records that protein's verified current UniProt subfamily classification with the official PANTHER label; it does not grant every subfamily member the module activity. The bounded mapping and OBO-label crosscheck are recorded in `.cache/module-audit-logs/auxin-module-membership.json`. No complete release index was downloaded or manufactured.

Auxin biosynthesis/transport, rapid cytoplasmic and extracellular perception, noncanonical auxin-binding ARFs, broader noncanonical Aux/IAA routes, and downstream organ-development programs remain outside the modeled boundary. Shared SCF core, E1/E2 enzymes, ubiquitin, proteasome, transcription/chromatin machinery, ATP and ligand/cofactor supply are necessary background machinery; their omission as named exemplars does not make them biochemically dispensable. TIR1 is an SCF substrate adaptor, not an autonomous ubiquitin-transfer enzyme. Aux/IAAs are not assigned protein-catabolic participation merely because they are degraded.

## Required roles and what the logic means

Each native variant requires receptor recognition/SCF recruitment, a canonical Aux/IAA corepressor, TPL machinery and class-A ARF output. Arabidopsis receptor roles are coupled in alternative implementations. The experimentally characterized TIR1 arm additionally requires its adenylate-cyclase atom. Removing only that activity while retaining TIR1 receptor/adaptor capacity makes this arm fail. AFB2 is represented by its established receptor/adaptor contributions with AC capacity/dependence explicitly unresolved; it is not represented as an experimentally proven AC-independent sufficient system. Marchantia similarly has no invented AC atom.

The ARF7–IAA19 feedback and Marchantia class-B modulation are optional context branches. Neither alone satisfies the module. Variant alternatives do not establish that all receptor–Aux/IAA–ARF combinations are physiologically equivalent; expression, partner selectivity and chromatin context remain relevant. Four established Arabidopsis node identifiers (`auxin_perception`, `aux_iaa_degradation`, `aux_iaa_tpl_repression`, `arf_activation`) are retained, with the first two now specifically the TIR1 mechanism. A bounded corpus search found no external references to these nodes or this module requiring migration.

The 22 route cases in `auxin-module-route-cases.py` verify structural activity coverage: complete native implementations pass; missing required roles, isolated feedback, incomplete mixed taxa and MpARF2 replacing MpARF1 fail. TIR1 without its AC activity fails. There are 26 enumerated routes, each with at least five activity atoms. These are capacity tests, not a calibrated dynamical model of transcription. The causal graph records signed influences and source conditions; no claim of a complete executable Boolean/kinetic model is made. In particular there is no degradation→transcription CAUSES edge that would assert universal sufficiency.

## Direct mechanism and comparative limits

**Arabidopsis co-receptor and repression.** PMID17410169 directly describes TIR1 ligand/substrate recognition and its InsP6 cofactor; it is not an AFB2 or Marchantia structure. Full PMID22466420 resolves receptor/Aux-IAA combinations; its TIR1/IAA7 affinity must not become an AFB2-specific measurement. PMID11713520 establishes SCF(TIR1)-dependent IAA7/IAA17 recruitment and turnover. PMID15992545 and full PMID20018756 support AFB2 receptor/adaptor involvement and native redundancy. PMID18258861 establishes IAA12–TPL interaction and the BDL/ARF5 embryonic example, while PMID24706860 directly tests ARF7/IAA17 PB1 interfaces. PMID14742873 demonstrates active domain-I repression in engineered constructs. These establish mechanisms without implying every potential pair is equally used in vivo.

**Transcription and feedback.** ARF5 promoter occupancy and DRN expression are supported by PMID19369397, ARF7 protoplast activation by PMID15923351, and ARF19 activation by PMID15960621. PMID17259263 directly supports ARF7/19 LBD targets. The module uses the final gene-review activator MF for all three, without assigning activation to all ARFs or restricting all AuxREs to one hexamer. Full PMID14729917 supports the specific IAA19/ARF7 loop and reports that “auxin inducibility of MSG2/IAA19 gene expression is reduced by 65% in nph4/arf7”; that quantitative result is not propagated to other repressors. Full PMID34075876 tests TPL–Mediator repression in synthetic yeast and plant systems, not a universal stoichiometric HDAC/Mediator assembly.

**TIR1 cAMP branch.** PMID36289340 PubMed cache is abstract-only, but its complete author-accepted manuscript was normally fetched from the Coventry institutional PDF. Full-length Sf9 TIR1, independently expressed truncated TIR1, motif controls and LC-MS/MS support direct chemistry. The full-length measured Vmax is 7.462 fmol/min/µg and KM 0.644 mM; these low purified rates are not native flux estimates, and AFB5's metal preference is not transferred to TIR1. Failed detectable expression of AFB2 in the bacterial screen is neither catalytic absence nor a direct positive assay. Moss PpAFB assays are not Marchantia assays.

Full PMID40044868 provides the crucial separation: ACm1/ACm3 retain comparable degradation of full-length IAA6/IAA7/IAA8/IAA17, while matched DR5 activation is reduced or lost. Concrete quotes from these results ground the module output edge and TIR1 gene follow-up; the ASK1 interaction quote supports assembly only. Engineered local unrelated-AC fusions can bypass receptor activation even with stabilized Aux/IAA, but do not identify a native cAMP–ARF binding interaction. A downstream molecular effector remains unknown. The requirement is encoded in the characterized TIR1 arm rather than hidden by an optional annotation.

**Marchantia.** Full PMID36529527 maps MpTIR1 to the selected native locus, tests auxin-dependent recruitment of MpIAA DII fragments and conditional DII-reporter stabilization, and distinguishes defective organogenesis from survival as cell masses. Its functional receptor fusion accumulates in nucleus-like regions; the separately NLS-tagged reporter is not substituted as receptor-localization evidence. Complete native SCF assembly and ubiquitination were not reconstituted. The paper explicitly leaves MpTIR1 AC activity for future investigation.

PMID26020649 maps the selected MpIAA/MpTPL/ARF KP clones; correction PMID26901620 identifies flipped/reused controls, a mislabeled knockdown panel and method details. Corrected controls and independent native work are used without discarding legitimate clone mappings. PMID26020919 uses an 825-aa MpIAA construct distinct from the selected 828-aa KP877968 record; it corroborates same-locus native repression, not exact sequence identity. Forced MpTPL-domain–MpIAA fusions establish imposed repression, not a native unforced binary interaction. A promoter-driven reporter with added NLS does not localize native MpTPL, so its annotons carry no separate location assertion.

Full PMID32415296 was recovered normally from WUR (`publications/url_https___edepot.wur.nl_523089.md`). MpARF2 does more than compete for DNA: full-protein MpTPL interaction is LFG-dependent in Arabidopsis-protoplast FRET–FLIM; peptide repression is tested in tobacco; native MpARF2 induction represses targets and conditional loss affects growth. These assay systems are distinguished. Full MpARF1 activates, even though an isolated short peptide can repress. Full PMID40168121 adds native MpARF1 transcriptome and DNA-interface evidence; putting the MpARF2 B3 domain into MpARF1 demonstrates a chimera's competence, not activation by native MpARF2. The selected MpARF2 clone and historical domain-construct accessions are not silently conflated.

## Recent review findings and open biology

The module references contain mined findings and short source quotations, not generic review summaries. Full PMID42590969 separates ancient ARF DNA effectors from canonical auxin coupling and class diversification. Full PMID40265222 discusses Aux/IAA-stimulated AC and stable noncanonical Aux/IAAs; its open question about AC and degradation is read alongside the direct separation experiments. The current Cell synthesis PMID42492494 is accessible as an abstract only; its explicit rapid-response distinction supports the boundary, while detailed edges rest on primary studies.

PMID41249070 is a Forum article even though cached metadata classifies it as primary research. Its full published PDF was normally fetched from ISTA as `publications/url_https___research-explorer.ista.ac.at_download_20725_22413_2026_TrendsPlantScience_Friml.pdf.md`. It discusses downstream cAMP targets, low purified AC rates and the still-unresolved role of turnover. Proposed direct cAMP action on ARFs is a hypothesis, not an established molecular interaction. Three BIOLOGY gaps record the unidentified cAMP effector and unmeasured AFB2/MpTIR1 AC contributions. Source-access or curation limitations are notes, not standalone knowledge gaps.
