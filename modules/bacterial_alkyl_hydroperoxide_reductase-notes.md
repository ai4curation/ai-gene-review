# Bacterial AhpC peroxide reduction and regeneration

## 2026-10-26 — boundary and source-based revision

Applied module-curation, review and annotation-reviewer guidance. The existing bacterial slug described only the E. coli AhpC/AhpF pair. It now represents AhpC peroxide chemistry plus the experimentally supported regeneration chains, with six organism-coherent alternatives and 15 exact protein exemplars. Original E. coli activity identifiers are retained. ECOLI AhpF was canonically renamed ahpF by the gene owner; all module paths use the current spelling.

The boundary is deliberately narrower than all bacterial redox defense. Tpx, BCP, AhpE, catalase, superoxide dismutase, glutathione metabolism, general thioredoxin clients and pyruvate-dehydrogenase carbon metabolism are outside it. The separate `glutathione_thioredoxin_redox_systems` module has a Eukaryota envelope and remains unchanged. Molecular oxygen is not a peroxide-reduction substrate; the Salmonella primary explicitly describes anaerobic turnover. Protein and substrate availability are contextual assumptions, not measured minimal growth requirements.

Each complete implementation must include peroxide reduction and every named regeneration step. An AhpC alone, a reductase alone or a cross-species assortment cannot satisfy it. Canonical capability coverage is not an essentiality model: compensated mutants and other unmodeled redox pathways can remain viable or peroxide resistant. The Mtb lipoyl and thioredoxin variants are alternatives, not simultaneous requirements. The 15 accessions are exact selectors; PANTHER descriptors record independently verified classification rather than authorizing every member of a broad family to perform the activity.

### Evidence and comparative limits

- E. coli: full PMID:11717276 distinguishes low-concentration AhpCF scavenging from catalase at higher peroxide concentrations. Full PMID:28270505 directly tests AhpF linker-dependent electron transfer and coupled AhpC turnover. A binding/structural ensemble is not a universal fixed oligomeric stoichiometry.
- Salmonella: PMID:8555198 explicitly reports equal results for native and heterologously expressed Salmonella preparations and both NADH and NADPH use. The large NADH preference is an apparent-Km comparison in a DTNB assay, not a universal physiological flux ratio. PMID:8555199 identifies catalytic disulfide centers. AhpF's N-terminal domain distinguishes its AhpC-directed function from ordinary TrxR despite their shared broad PANTHER classification.
- Bacillus: PMID:8932314 gives native operon, expression and mutant physiology. Pair chemistry is a qualified domain/orthology inference; it is not falsely described as a purified native-pair assay. Mutant H2O2 resistance and exponential-phase cumene-hydroperoxide sensitivity expose compensation and growth-stage dependence.
- H. pylori: full PMID:11222594 author PDF identifies the 26695 proteins, Trx1-specific AhpC reduction, native reductase donor substitution and failure of Salmonella AhpF to substitute. Detailed peroxide kinetic constants use E. coli TrxR. Its initial failure to recover ahpC mutants is not generalized into unconditional essentiality.
- Mtb lipoyl relay: PMID:11799204 identifies Lpd–DlaT (historically SucB)–AhpD–AhpC. The cached abstract is the accessible primary text; Science full-text retrieval failed. PMID:16045627's full publisher PDF Results were inspected through web access, while normal cache retrieval failed; quoted support therefore uses the real cached abstract. It separates Lpd E3 activity from DlaT lipoyl-mediated electron transfer and establishes PDH identity rather than KDH. PMID:15178486 directly demonstrates AhpD–AhpC mixed-disulfide chemistry, but uses bovine DHLDH/free lipoamide upstream. Those assay components are not relabeled native Lpd/DlaT.
- Mtb thioredoxin relay: PMID:14871480 explicitly establishes TrxR–TrxC–AhpC coupled turnover. Official MYCTU trxA/Rv3914 is literature TrxC; official trxB/Rv3913 is TrxB2/TrxR. Full DOI:10.1038/s41598-017-05354-5 uses BCG constructs and reports AhpC sequence identity with Mtb; its NMR and reconstituted turnover corroborate the mechanism, without becoming native H37Rv evidence.
- Full PMID:21238944 demonstrates native metabolic and nitrosative effects of Lpd loss, but greater resistance to the tested H2O2 challenge. It also identifies a different lipoylated carrier in DlaT-deficient cells. Neither a universally essential peroxide pathway nor a unique lipoylated protein is asserted.

Four review articles were mined for concrete comparative findings: full Poole 2005 (PMID:15581580) distinguishes AhpF and multi-protein regeneration architectures and condition-dependent oligomers; PMID:18084895 identifies mycobacterial AhpD/TrxC alternatives; PMID:34258640 distinguishes six peroxiredoxin classes; PMID:38899743 describes cofactor/client specialization. The last three are abstract-only. Their source assessments state this; no broad review is substituted for a target-specific primary edge.
