# Autonomous flowering pathway audit

## Native scope and comparative evidence

The mechanistic scope is **Arabidopsis thaliana**, with 13 individually reviewed native representatives: FCA, FY, FPA, FLD, LD, MSI4/FVE, FLK, ASHH1/SDG26, APRF1, TOPP4, FLC, FT and SOC1. Rice FCA Q6K271 and the assayed OsFY clone Q0R3J2 are two additional reviewed biochemical comparators, not native annoton representatives. Rice FCA partly complements Arabidopsis fca without restoring FLC downregulation or AtFCA autoregulation (PMID:16240176); cloned OsFY interacts with OsFCA-gamma (PMID:16820865). Neither result demonstrates native rice COOLAIR–FLC circuitry. The historical suggestion that rice lacks an FLC homolog is not retained: later comparative work identifies cereal FLC relatives (PMID:23955420), without establishing identical regulatory signs or wiring.

Rice FVE rescue and its failure to restore intermittent-cold responses (PMID:18612241), plus divergent beet FLK/FVE1 complementation (PMID:20974738), further separate conserved proteins from native circuit conservation. These are literature comparators without extra unresolved protein exemplars. The beet ESTs were not mistaken for verified complete coding accessions. PMID:32689136 was recovered as an abstract in the final source retry; the exact rice-clone study PMID:16820865 supplies the required interaction evidence here.

All native participants use exact GENE_PRODUCT selectors. Each also carries a family classification descriptor with that same native representative. Official PANTHER labels were read from the local ontology and checked against each native UniProt DR classification. Broad RRM, KH, WD40, SET and MADS families do not select all their members for the Arabidopsis role. No ancestral node or functional clade was invented. FLD and APRF1 family-function review coverage remains advisory; the individual gene functions are supported.

## Mechanistic boundaries

FCA directly binds COOLAIR (PMID:31032401), while FLL2-dependent nuclear condensates have separate evidence (PMID:31043738). The FCA–FY interaction was originally characterized using FCA pre-mRNA (PMID:12809608); that paper is not retrospectively cited as a direct COOLAIR experiment. Native FY/CPSF100/CPSF160 assemblies are stable while FCA association is transient (PMID:19439664). No isolated FY RNA-binding, nuclease or poly(A)-signal-recognition activity is inferred from WDR33/Pfs2 homology.

**COOLAIR is a lncRNA.** Its processing is represented by GO:0031123 RNA 3'-end processing, with proximal polyadenylation described in prose. The mRNA-specific GO:0110104 and GO:0031124 terms are not used to label the COOLAIR step merely because the same proteins also process mRNAs. The broader FLC-processing node permits the separately sourced locus-level processivity feedback without implying that every feedback measurement was COOLAIR-specific.

FLD's demethylase classification combines conserved LSD1-family evidence with native FLD/LD/SDG26-associated H3K4me1 effects (PMID:17996704; PMID:32541063); a purified target kinetic assay is not claimed. LD is noncatalytic. SDG26/ASHH1's C-terminal region binds FLC-derived double-stranded DNA in the primary EMSA, while APRF1 associates with native FLC chromatin (PMID:35766439). Rescue with SDG26 Y118A supports the authors' catalytic-dispensability inference in that assay, but they did not directly measure mutant inactivity. Earlier SDG26 oligonucleosome methylation is not erased; it remains in the gene review and is not substituted for the modeled DNA-binding contribution.

APRF1, LD and TOPP4 form a CPF-like termination-associated assembly (PMID:38851185). TOPP4's protein serine/threonine phosphatase activity is directly demonstrated on substrates such as RGA/GAI (PMID:25010794); those substrates are not assigned to the FLC complex. The primary paper explicitly leaves the CPF substrate unresolved between possibilities including Pol II CTD and elongation factors. This is the module's one BIOLOGY knowledge gap. LD/APRF1 are not assigned catalytic activities, and APRF1 is not inferred to perform native COMPASS methylation from yeast complementation.

FPA's processing role is distinct from an obligatory FCA/FY-containing assembly. Full PMID:18628965 shows 35S::FPA bypass of fca/fy and a stronger requirement for FLD. This is dosage/genotype-qualified evidence, not proof of unconditional native independence. PMID:20079695 supports alternatively processed FLC antisense RNAs; it does not establish that FPA directly binds COOLAIR. The gene review leaves the precise ncRNA-mediated heterochromatin annotation unresolved after source-specific termination findings. Full PMID:18408043 and PMID:11283346 establish the major FLC branch while cautioning against treating all autonomous-gene developmental or flowering phenotypes as identically FLC-dependent.

FLK's m6A-dependent recognition of FLC **sense mRNA**, with altered stability/splicing, is a distinct route (PMID:37050878). The available abstract explicitly reports direct target binding, writer dependence and reader-mutant rescue failure. No unreported affinity, RNA isoform or detailed assay parameter is added.

FVE/MSI4 contributes histone-binding/chromatin associations. HDA5/HDA6/FLD interaction data and CLF/CUL4-DDB1 associations are not converted into one invariant complex, universal PRC2-core membership or FVE catalytic activity (PMID:14745447; PMID:25922987; PMID:21282611). FVE-associated repression at FT chromatin is retained separately from its repression of FLC; therefore its direct effect on FT can oppose the indirect FT derepression resulting from lower FLC.

FLC's negative output sign is specific to FT and SOC1. Primary ChIP places FLC at the **FT first intron** and the **SOC1 promoter** (PMID:16623882), with tissue-specific effects on systemic FT signaling and meristem competence (PMID:16600915). Other FLC targets can have other signs. FT/FD coactivation at the shoot apex is supported by PMID:16099980 and PMID:16099979; SOC1's own DNA-binding function has direct LFY-regulatory evidence (PMID:18466303). Their full downstream programs and transport machinery remain outside scope.

## Route and graph semantics

The required upstream part selects ONE_OR_MORE of four context-qualified contributions: the characterized FCA/FY–chromatin–termination implementation, FPA processing, FLK sense-RNA recognition or FVE chromatin association. The FLC target is required. FT and SOC1 are optional output readouts, so neither their presence alone nor an empty gene context can satisfy the module. Rice comparators do not satisfy any native selector.

The sampled FCA implementation includes its seven named RNA/chromatin/termination participants to define that particular assembled representation. This does not require every autonomous gene in every physiological response. Conversely, selecting the FPA, FLK or FVE contribution does not claim that one protein is sufficient to reconstruct wild-type repression without cofactors. The Boolean presence test recognizes a represented contribution in its stated context; it does not predict flowering phenotypes from gene inventory. Tissue, genotype, expression level, target methylation and dosage remain biological conditions and are not automatically inferred by the presence predicate.

The 10 signed connections separate RNA/chromatin regulation of FLC expression from FLC protein's direct repression of FT/SOC1. Proximal termination and H3K4me1 regulation have a supported qualitative positive feedback relationship (PMID:38851186). The current abstract supports that model, while exact kinetics and full experiments were not independently inspected. No executable kinetic or Boolean phenotype model is asserted. Graded repression and switching into Polycomb silencing are distinguished rather than reduced to one irreversible handoff (reviews PMID:31740502, PMID:41840059).
