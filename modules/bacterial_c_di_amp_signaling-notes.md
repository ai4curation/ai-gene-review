# Bacterial c-di-AMP module audit

## 2026-11-04 — scope and source decisions

Audit starts from published main `bcead031369d8739c819091f3348deb68b24f321`. The existing fifteen representatives were verified against current UniProt records and normally seeded. Bacillus subtilis DarB (O31698) and KhtT (O07535) were added because primary experiments establish qualitatively different receptor outputs, giving seventeen reviews, 89 original annotation payloads and 34 pinned raw source files. The initial PR preserved all rows without adding NEW recommendations; the review follow-up below supersedes that omission for demonstrated molecular activities and localization. Two CpaA potassium-specific claims remain UNDECIDED because the accessible primary abstract does not establish the ion specificity. This is an evidence-access limitation, not a claim that the annotations are false.

The project requested literature review, taxonomy, family grounding and individual gene review. Searches included receptor-output specificity, DarA/PstA target discovery, DarB/Rel coupling and CpaA reconstitution. PMID:42535852 provides a recent comparative review; decisive claims are checked against primary biochemical/structural work. Actual Falcon calls were attempted for the module and each member. They returned HTTP 403 authentication failures. These notes and member journals are manual research, not provider reports.

- **Cyclases:** membrane CdaA/DacA and soluble DisA have separate leaf locations and regulatory contexts. PMID:18439896 identifies DisA catalysis and branched-DNA inhibition; PMID:30668586 identifies productive association of S. aureus DacA dimers and GlmM inhibition. Detailed oligomer geometry is species-qualified. GO:0004016 makes cAMP whereas GO:0106408 makes c-di-AMP; gene reviews correct that chemical mismatch while retaining source payloads.
- **Turnover:** GdpP and PgpH open c-di-AMP to pApA (PMID:19901023 and PMID:25583510). Pde2 prefers pApA and can relieve feedback inhibition specifically of GdpP (PMID:27834680). This is an optional extension, not a universal necessity. Bacillus ATPase/heme regulation is not silently assigned to S. aureus GdpP (PMID:19901023, PMID:21257773 and PMID:21909268).
- **Receptor outputs:** Ktr/CabP uptake regulation, CpaA antiporter modulation, KhtT export activation, KdpD transcriptional consequences, OpuCA-associated carnitine uptake and DarB-mediated enzyme regulation are distinguished. The blanket claim that every receptor inhibits uptake is removed. PMID:33790011 demonstrates active KhtU alone and restraint by apo-KhtT, relieved by ligand. PMID:33619274, PMID:35714772, PMID:36652515 and PMID:35130724 distinguish DarB binding/regulation from the catalytic activities of Rel and pyruvate carboxylase.
- **Limits:** PMID:27531650 reports a weak isolated OpuCA ATPase insensitive to ligand; a precise ATPase-inhibition mechanism is therefore unasserted. PMID:26195599 measures KdpD binding and kdpFABC expression, not direct autokinase inhibition. The accessible CpaA primary abstract PMID:26171638 establishes antiporter modulation but not an exact ion/sign assignment; publisher full text was unavailable and the Dundee repository offered only the abstract. DarA 2024 suppressor findings (PMID:38832794) do not establish its direct binding target.

## Taxon and family reasoning

Bacillota is the explicit sampled envelope, not a claim that all members carry all branches or that signaling is absent elsewhere. Six strain-level taxa represent four genera. The documented presence of cyclic di-AMP outside Bacillota does not justify asserting that this particular combination of branches occurs there. Full transporter systems, RNA riboswitches, other cyclase/turnover classes and direct metabolic receptors are outside this selected motif. They are known biology, not knowledge gaps.

PANTHER labels and representative membership are checked using the committed classification ontology and unedited UniProt records. RCK_C is not treated as one whole-protein family: Ktr/CabP, CpaA and KhtT have separate assignments. DarB uses PTHR43080:SF30 rather than interpreting the broad parent family's plant mitochondrial name as a bacterial localization. KdpD and OpuCA are explicitly restricted to the c-di-AMP-binding subclasses; classification alone does not establish ligand specificity throughout a family. No ancestral node is invented for the module. Required gene-level PAINT-loss lookups initially encountered slow tree responses. KhtT, KdpD and all four cyclase queries subsequently completed successfully with no IRD/IKR losses on their resolved lineages. The initial timeouts and successful retries are documented separately in the gene notes.

## Verification design

`analysis/module_audits/bacterial_c_di_amp_signaling/seed-baseline.yaml` pins every original machine annotation payload and raw SHA256. `check_module.py` checks preservation, curated gene identity, all module representative coverage, exact source quotations and the production module validator. `check_capacity.py` exercises the production Boolean compiler with 37 native-strain representative-capacity cases. These are capacity checks, not concentration dynamics or growth predictions. Receptor binding can be represented without assuming its entire downstream pump is included; incomplete Listeria/USA300/pneumococcal panels do not imply missing biological circuitry. Different strains are never pooled to fabricate a complete native implementation.

The GO-CAM index was queried for all seventeen accessions; no matches were found in the pinned index. This absence does not imply an annotation gap or absence of function. Taxonomy snapshots preserve fetched lineage information and retrieval URLs. Canonical pApA chemistry is shared with the Pde2 gene proposal. Generated pages are render-tested but excluded from the source PR.
