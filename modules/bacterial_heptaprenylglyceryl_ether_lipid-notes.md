# Bacterial heptaprenylglyceryl ether-lipid (Ac-HepG) module — curation notes

Created 2026-11-03 on branch `lipid-divide-project`, as the headline module for
[LIPID_DIVIDE](../projects/LIPID_DIVIDE.md). File:
`modules/bacterial_heptaprenylglyceryl_ether_lipid.yaml`.

## Why this module exists

The lipid divide is usually stated as a taxonomic rule: G1P backbone + ether
linkage + isoprenoid chain = archaeal. *Bacillus subtilis* does all three in one
pathway. The point of interest, and the reason the boundary is drawn where it
is, is that the product is **not** a membrane: it is a low-abundance lipophilic
acetyl carrier. The pathway crosses the divide chemically while answering a
question (how do you get a high-energy acetyl donor to the outer face of the
membrane?) that has nothing to do with membrane architecture.

## Boundary decisions

**Five parts, four of them required.** G1P supply → ether-bond formation (PcrB)
→ dephosphorylation (unassigned phosphatase) → acetylation (YvoF) → acetyl
delivery to lipoprotein (LhaT, optional).

**Why LhaT is inside the module, not merely adjacent.** The user's framing and
the biology agree here. The module's real output is a *transferred acetyl
group*, and the carrier (HepG) is regenerated by the LhaT step; without the
consumer the preceding four steps are a pathway to a lipid of unknown purpose,
which is exactly how the field described it from 2011 to 2025. Komazin 2025
(PMID:40626731) reconstituted all five steps in one tube and showed that
deleting any of *pcrB*, *yvoF* or *ypjA* abolishes the modification, so the
genetics treat them as one unit. The connection `hepg_acetylation →
lipoprotein_acetyl_transfer` is `PROVIDES_INPUT_FOR`, and the role description
of the LhaT annoton states the cycle explicitly.

**Why that part is nevertheless `optional: true`.** PcrB has a broader strain
distribution than LhaT; LhaT is pseudogenised in the *B. cereus* group (a
poly-tyrosine insertion in *B. anthracis*); and some species encoding both do
not make Ac-LP. So the carrier steps can run where this consumer is absent, and
Ac-HepG may have other uses. Quoted verbatim in the module's second
node-level knowledge gap:
"The co-occurrence of PcrB with YpjA is not absolute, however, with PcrB
enjoying a broader strain distribution than LhaT."

**What is deliberately outside the boundary.**

- **Lgt diacylglyceryl transfer and LspA signal-peptide cleavage.** These make
  LhaT's acceptor (DA-LP) and are already modelled in
  `modules/bacterial_lipoprotein_maturation.yaml`. That module is cited as a
  `file:` evidence item with an explicit "adjacent module, not duplicated here"
  statement, and the LhaT annoton's substrate descriptor names DA-LP as coming
  from it. Nothing was copied, and that module was not edited. It already says
  its own terminal-acylation chemistry is lineage-variable rather than always
  Lnt-type, so LhaT fills a slot it left open rather than contradicting it.
  A `module_ref` to it was considered and rejected: `module_ref` means
  inclusion, which would make the whole Lgt/LspA/Lnt route a required part of
  *this* module. Acceptor supply is upstream context, not a part.
- **Isoprenoid (HepPP) supply**, the TLR2 immunology, the *lit* lyso-lipoprotein
  remodelling branch, and G1P-backbone ester lipids (G1P-PG).

**Scope is `CONCRETE`, taxon Bacillales.** The HepGP route is described in
Bacillales; *yvoF* and *lhaT* are Bacillales/*Bacillus* genes. Family-level
participants keep it usable across the order rather than pinning it to strain
168, but it is not an abstract motif. Note `NCBITaxon:1385` is labelled
**Caryophanales** in current NCBITaxon, so `term.label` is "Caryophanales" (the
validator label-checks it) and `preferred_term` is "Bacillales".

## The unassigned phosphatase (part 3)

Modelled as a real, required step with **no gene product**:
`selector_type: ANY_WITH_FUNCTION`, `required_function: GO:0016791 phosphatase
activity`. PhoB is *not* named as the participant, because Linde 2016 only
suspected it: "We enriched the phosphatase activity from a B. subtilis cell
extract and suppose that dephosphorylation is catalyzed by the phosphatase PhoB
or by any other phosphatase in an unspecific manner." The reconstitution used
calf intestinal alkaline phosphatase in this position, which is further reason
not to assert a *B. subtilis* gene. A node-level `BIOLOGY` knowledge gap records
the unknown (MF_DARK) with that quote as provenance, and names the experiment
that would close it.

Consequence in QC: this leaf shows up under "Leaf nodes lacking representative
members" (1 node, `ANY_WITH_FUNCTION`). That notice is correct and intended —
there is no protein to ground.

## The unproven G1P supply (part 1)

The user's standard (model in vivo biology, not in vitro promiscuity) cuts
*against* a confident EgsA→PcrB edge here. What is established is enzymology:
purified AraM/EgsA reversibly reduces DHAP to G1P with NADH, kinetics strongly
favour the reductive direction (K_M 8.1 µM DHAP vs 1370 µM G1P), and it is the
only characterised bacterial G1P-forming enzyme. What is *not* established is
that it supplies the G1P that PcrB uses:

- no *egsA*-deletion lipidomics measuring HepG/Ac-HepG (per
  `genes/BACSU/egsA/egsA-notes.md`);
- *egsA* was not among the reported Tn-screen hits for loss of lipoprotein
  N-acetylation (Komazin 2025) — not evidence against, but not support either;
- Rieche 2026 (PMID:42286214): "other mechanism(s) of G1P biosynthesis must
  exist in E. coli and possibly other bacteria, as archaeal lipids with the
  correct chiral structure could be produced even in the absence of AraM".

Three hedges, all structural rather than prose-only:

1. The step is a **`variant_set` with `variant_kind: REALIZER`,
   `selection: ONE_OR_MORE`, containing one modelled variant** (the G1PDH
   route). Declaring the layer makes a gap at this step read as "activity
   required, carrier not identified" rather than "no route exists", which is
   exactly the Rieche observation; `abduce` then offers the undetected-carrier
   explanation. A single-variant set is unusual but is the honest encoding: we
   know the activity is needed and know one enzyme that can do it, and we know
   that is not the whole story.
2. The connection `g1p_supply → ether_bond_formation` carries
   **`chaining_status: KNOWLEDGE_GAP`** with a note distinguishing the two
   claims: the connecting metabolite is unambiguous (CHEBI:57685 is the upstream
   product and the obligatory downstream acceptor), but the in vivo flux is not
   demonstrated. Left unVERIFIED so the edge is not read as a proven flux.
3. A node-level **`BIOLOGY` knowledge gap** (BP_DARK) with the Rieche and
   Guldan quotes and the lipidomics experiment that would close it.

The EgsA annoton's `processes: GO:0008611` carries a description saying it is
module context, not a claim that the enzyme's dedicated role is ether-lipid
synthesis — the same G1P pool may feed ester-linked G1P-PG. This matches the
`egsA-notes.md` plan, which proposes `MODIFY` to `GO:0046474` (not `GO:0008611`)
for the gene itself, precisely because the ether bond is PcrB's work.

## Identifiers: what was checked, and what was left unasserted

All four UniProt accessions were fetched from the UniProt REST API on
2026-11-03 and match: P94527 `G1PDH_BACSU`, O34790 `PCRB_BACSU`, O34993
`YVOF_BACSU` ("Putative acetyltransferase YvoF", EC 2.3.1.-), P54392
`YPJA_BACSU` ("Uncharacterized protein YpjA", cell membrane, multi-pass). The
module records that UniProt still calls P54392 uncharacterised and that O34993
is still "putative", so a reader is not misled into thinking the record is up to
date with Komazin 2025.

PANTHER groundings are taken from each entry's own `DR PANTHER` lines and the
label copied **verbatim** from `interpro/panther/panther.obo`; after
`just ensure-panther-members` all four representative-member checks pass:

- `PANTHER:PTHR40029:SF2` "HEPTAPRENYLGLYCERYL PHOSPHATE SYNTHASE" ← O34790.
  Subfamily, not family: Peterhoff 2014 (PMID:24684232) shows only *group I*
  bacterial GGGPS members are HepGP synthases, group II are GGGP synthases, so
  the family would be the wrong level.
- `PANTHER:PTHR43622:SF1` "3-DEHYDROQUINATE SYNTHASE" ← P94527. The official
  name names a different reaction; this is the documented "correct id with a
  surprising name" case (cf. PTHR24322/DHRS3), so the label is kept verbatim and
  `preferred_term` says "bacterial glycerol-1-phosphate dehydrogenase (G1PDH,
  EgsA/AraM) family". The representative-member check is what distinguishes this
  from a guessed id, and it passes.
- `PANTHER:PTHR43300:SF6` "ACETYLTRANSFERASE YVOF-RELATED" ← O34993. Subfamily
  level matters: the parent family contains maltose O-acetyltransferases, which
  overlap YvoF in vitro but do **not** complement a *yvoF* knockout in vivo.
- `PANTHER:PTHR40042:SF1` "DUF1405 DOMAIN-CONTAINING PROTEIN" ← P54392. The
  description states that family membership alone does not imply the activity,
  since LhaT is pseudogenised in the *B. cereus* group.

## GO term gaps found (recorded here, not as knowledge_gaps)

Per repository policy, a missing ontology term is not a `knowledge_gaps` entry.
Checked via the OLS MCP on 2026-11-03:

1. **No process term for archaeal-type (G1P + isoprenoid-ether) membrane-lipid
   biosynthesis**, and none for tetraether/GDGT/membrane-spanning lipids
   (consistent with the OLS search already recorded in LIPID_DIVIDE).
   `GO:0008611 ether lipid biosynthetic process` is used throughout: its
   definition centres on a single ether-linked alcohol at C-1, which is exactly
   what this pathway makes, so it is a genuine fit rather than a forced one. It
   is the module's only `concepts` entry.
2. **No process term for a prenyl-lipid acetyl-carrier cycle.** The closest
   analogy in GO practice is the undecaprenyl carrier, where the carrier
   synthase (*E. coli* UppS/IspU) is annotated to the *process it serves*
   (`GO:0009252`, IMP) rather than to a carrier-specific term. The module
   expresses the carrier logic through the `PROVIDES_INPUT_FOR` connection and
   the regeneration of HepG in the LhaT annoton's products, not through a term.
3. **No MF term for heptaprenylglyceryl phosphate phosphatase activity**; the
   unassigned step uses the broad `GO:0016791`.
4. **No MF term for O-acetylation of a prenyl glycerol ether lipid**; YvoF uses
   `GO:0016413 O-acetyltransferase activity`.
5. **`GO:0004596` protein-N-terminal amino-acid acetyltransferase activity does
   not fit LhaT.** Its definition fixes acetyl-CoA as the donor
   ("acetyl-CoA + an N-terminal L-alpha-aminoacyl-[protein] = ..."), whereas
   LhaT's donor is the lipid Ac-HepG. `GO:0008080 N-acetyltransferase activity`
   is used instead because it does not misstate the donor; the precise chemistry
   is in `preferred_term` and the function description. A specific
   "lipoprotein N-acetyltransferase activity, acetyl-heptaprenylglycerol as
   donor" term would be the right request to GO, and pairs with the
   `GO:0006474` / `GO:0042158` process pair already asserted. No proposed-term
   slot exists on `ModuleReview`, so this is recorded here and should be raised
   on the LhaT gene review when it is created.
