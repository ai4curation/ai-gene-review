# Historical sarcosine dehydrogenase with unresolved sequence identity

The native enzyme in the 1979 study came from Pseudomonas putida var. naraensis C-83. Its approximately 170-kDa native mass and 45–46-kDa SDS subunits support a probable homotetramer. It oxidized sarcosine with artificial electron carriers; dimethylglycine was inactive. Table IV reports formaldehyde values of 26 without added carrier and 100 with PMS or DCPIP. Physiological electron acceptor, ETF coupling and folate dependence were not established. Lack of observed peroxide has no stated detection limit and does not prove absolute oxygen inactivity.

The authors state: “Production of hydrogen peroxide was not observed in the enzyme reaction.”

Source: Oka et al., Sarcosine Dehydrogenase from Pseudomonas putida: Purification and Some Properties, Agricultural and Biological Chemistry 43:1197–1203 (1979), https://doi.org/10.1080/00021369.1979.10863601 ; official full PDF https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/bbb1961/43/6/43_6_1197/_pdf ; assay discussion/Table IV on printed p1202. C-83 is distinct from strain77/AKU875, whose 1985 study uses a different upstream creatinine pathway (https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1574-6968.1985.tb01106.x). Neither strain is demonstrated to be KT2440. Official UniProt searches on 2026-11-06 found two C-83 records, creatininase P83772 and formaldehyde dehydrogenase P46154, but no sarcosine-dehydrogenase accession. Strain77 and P. putida sarcosine-dehydrogenase searches returned no bridge. This is a search limit, not proof that no sequence exists. No gene, family id or PAINT node is invented.

The biochemical alternative remains explicitly ungrounded at the gene/family level. It must not satisfy a sequence-based capacity prediction solely because a genome contains a generic oxidoreductase.
