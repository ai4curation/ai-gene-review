    Action Consistency

    A gene review judges claims about the gene, not evidence codes. GOA often holds several rows for one term (say IBA, IEA and NAS for GPI-anchor transamidase complex); they are independent lines of evidence for one claim and reinforce each other. The review's actions on them may differ in emphasis but must not contradict each other. Picking the "best" evidence code and REMOVEing the rest is wrong: REMOVE says the claim is unlikely to be correct.

    The rules table

    Which action combinations are contradictions, and how severe each is, is declared in src/ai_gene_review/schema/action_consistency.yaml and enforced by ai_gene_review.validation.action_consistency during best-practices validation (check_type: action_consistency).

    Each rule reads as a sentence, left to right: the action_1 row's term is relation the action_2 row's term.

    Column Meaning
    action_1, action_2 An ActionEnum value, or a list of them (any of)
    relation SAME_TERM: both rows carry the same term (symmetric). DESCENDANT_OF: action_1 is on the more specific term, a strict is_a/part_of descendant of action_2's. ANCESTOR_OF: action_1 is on the more general term
    severity OK (documented as consistent, not reported), INFO, WARNING, ERROR
    rationale Shown as the suggestion in validation reports
    negated_1, negated_2 Optional, default false; true selects NOT rows on that side
    term_1, term_2 Optional term filters (below)

    A ANCESTOR_OF B and B DESCENDANT_OF A are the same rule; the loader rejects a table that lists both.

    Exceptions: term filters

    A rule can be restricted to rows whose term matches, with optional term_1 and term_2 (one per side; SAME_TERM rows share a term, so they take term_1 only):

    term_1: {exact: GO:0005515}                         # this term only
term_1: {exact: [GO:0003674, GO:0008150, GO:0005575]}  # any of these

    An exception is just a more specific rule, usually with severity OK. When several rules match a pair of rows, the one with the most term filters wins. Equally specific rules that can match the same rows but disagree on severity are rejected when the table loads, so there is never a silent tie.

    Deliberately not supported: NOT/AND expressions, ancestor-of matchers, and per-gene exceptions in the table (see per-annotation exceptions below for the rare real case). {self_or_descendant: ...} (a term or anything below it) is reserved and still rejected: unlike the hierarchical relations, a filter must also work when the ontology is not loaded. Protein binding wants exact anyway: its descendants (enzyme binding, kinase binding, ...) are informative and follow the normal rules.

    Rows annotated to two different isoforms are not compared with each other. Pairs not in the table are not checked.

    NOT annotations

    Polarity is part of every rule (negated_1/negated_2, default positive), because NOT runs the logic the other way:

    • A positive annotation entails its ancestors; a NOT entails its descendants. Keeping a positive term and keeping NOT on the same term or on an ancestor of it is a contradiction (e.g. NOT cilium vs axoneme, which is part_of it).
    • REMOVE on a NOT row only doubts the negative claim; it does not assert the positive one. So REMOVE on both a term and NOT the term is consistent (agnostic), and so is keeping the NOT while removing the positive.
    • TOO_GENERAL is never used on a NOT row (an ERROR): a NOT on a broad term is the strongest negative claim, not an uninformative one.

    GOA legitimately carries conflicting positive and NOT annotations from different papers. The review should still decide (REMOVE or UNDECIDED one side), or, when both genuinely hold in different contexts, record a per-annotation exception.

    Per-annotation exceptions

    For very rare cases where an apparent contradiction is correct, a row can declare it:

    review:
  action: ACCEPT
  consistency_exception:
    reason: NOT in roots (PMID:...), positive in shoots (PMID:...)

    Conflicts involving that row are still reported, at INFO with the reason, so they stay visible. An exception on a row that is not in any conflict is flagged (WARNING) so it cannot go stale. Exceptions do not apply to single-row checks such as TOO_GENERAL on a NOT row.

    Strictness is ratcheted by editing the table: add a pair, or raise a severity, once the existing reviews that violate it have been fixed.

    Current state

    action_1 relation action_2 severity reads as
    ACCEPT SAME_TERM REMOVE WARNING an ACCEPT row and a REMOVE row share a term
    ACCEPT (term_1 exactly GO:0005515) SAME_TERM REMOVE OK exception: protein binding is tracked by its own policy
    [ACCEPT, KEEP_AS_NON_CORE], negated_1 SAME_TERM [ACCEPT, KEEP_AS_NON_CORE, NEW, TOO_GENERAL] WARNING a kept NOT row and a kept positive row share a term
    [ACCEPT, KEEP_AS_NON_CORE], negated_1 ANCESTOR_OF [ACCEPT, KEEP_AS_NON_CORE, NEW, TOO_GENERAL] WARNING a kept NOT row is on an ancestor of a kept positive term
    TOO_GENERAL ANCESTOR_OF ACCEPT OK the TOO_GENERAL term is more general than an ACCEPTed term
    TOO_GENERAL ANCESTOR_OF KEEP_AS_NON_CORE OK the TOO_GENERAL term is more general than a kept term

    When it was introduced, the ACCEPT/REMOVE rule fired on 35 reviews (325 row pairs; the REMOVEd rows are mostly TAS, IDA, IMP and IPI); with the protein binding exception, 34 reviews (301 row pairs). The NOT rule fired on 23 reviews (54 row pairs; e.g. GAI, RGA, WUS, Akt1), and its ancestor form on 11 reviews (22 row pairs; e.g. ARL6 NOT cilium vs axoneme, DNM1L NOT endoplasmic reticulum vs endoplasmic reticulum membrane, APP NOT dendrite vs dendritic spine). All stay WARNINGs until those are fixed, then can become ERRORs.

    Hierarchical rules and the ontology

    DESCENDANT_OF and ANCESTOR_OF rules use strict GO ancestry over is_a and part_of (a positive annotation to axoneme entails cilium). They need the GO database (sqlite:obo:go via OAK), so they run only when validation loads it: ai-gene-review validate --terms or --ontology-checks, which just validate and just validate-all use. Plain best-practice runs and unit tests skip them, and in those runs per-annotation exceptions are not flagged as stale (the hierarchy was not checked).

    When ontology checks are requested, a missing or broken GO database fails fast: the CLI loads GO and checks a known part_of edge (axoneme -> cilium) before validating any file, and any later lookup error raises. Hierarchical rules are never silently skipped.

    Unlike the older inconsistent_review_actions warning, this check has no exemption for REMOVE rows that cite a different reference: REMOVE is a judgment on the claim, whatever paper the row cites. Until the reviews it flags are fixed and the old check is narrowed or retired, an unexempted ACCEPT/REMOVE pair is reported by both.

    Known limitations

    • The pair rules compare each row's own term.id. MODIFY proposed_replacement_terms are not yet part of the relation graph, so a replacement term never takes part in a pair conflict. (The TOO_GENERAL precondition below does count them.)

    Baseline (October 2026)

    A corpus scan of 6,528 reviews and 20,432 non-negated REMOVE rows, comparing each REMOVE with what the same review asserts as true (ACCEPT, KEEP_AS_NON_CORE, NEW, MODIFY replacement):

    Pattern REMOVE rows Reviews
    REMOVE vs ACCEPT/KEEP_AS_NON_CORE, same term 122 55
    REMOVE on an ancestor of an asserted term (excluding protein binding) 253 168
    REMOVE on GO:0005515 protein binding with an asserted descendant 13,887 ~700

    Protein binding is tracked separately, under its own policy (validation/protein_binding_policy.py).

    Other combinations on the same term worth future rules: MODIFY + REMOVE (437 term groups), MARK_AS_OVER_ANNOTATED + REMOVE (44), NEW + REMOVE (14).

    TOO_GENERAL

    TOO_GENERAL is for a term that is true of the gene but uninformative, because a more specific term asserted elsewhere in the same review already entails it: hydrolase activity beside an accepted metalloendopeptidase activity, or membrane beside an accepted endoplasmic reticulum membrane.

    It judges the term, not the row's evidence, which is weighed separately, and it never means the claim is false. How it differs from the other actions:

    Action Says
    REMOVE The claim is unlikely to be correct
    MODIFY This row's own evidence supports a better term
    MARK_AS_OVER_ANNOTATED The claim goes beyond what the evidence supports
    TOO_GENERAL The claim is true but subsumed by a more specific asserted term

    When the ontology is loaded, a TOO_GENERAL row with no asserted strict descendant in the same review (a positive ACCEPT, KEEP_AS_NON_CORE or NEW row, or a MODIFY replacement term) is a WARNING: without one, the broad term is the review's only statement of the claim and should be kept.

    Use it sparingly. A broader term that still says something useful (a pathway-level process beside a specific step, endoplasmic reticulum from a localization screen beside ER membrane) stays ACCEPT or KEEP_AS_NON_CORE. It is not used for GO:0005515 protein binding.

    Examples: genes/SCHPO/gaa1, genes/PSEAE/rhlB, genes/AGKCO/fibrolase.
