Autophagy, apoptosis, and necroptosis are stress responses governing the 
ultimate fate of a cell. However, the crosstalk between these cellular stress 
responses is not entirely understood. Especially, it is not clear whether the 
autophagy-initiating kinase ULK1 and the cell-death-regulating kinase RIPK1 are 
involved in this potential crosstalk. Here, we identify RIPK1 as a substrate of 
ULK1. ULK1-dependent phosphorylation of RIPK1 reduces complex IIb/necrosome 
assembly and tumor necrosis factor (TNF)-induced cell death, whereas deprivation 
of ULK1 enhances TNF-induced cell death. We observe that ULK1 phosphorylates 
multiple sites of RIPK1, but it appears that especially phosphorylation of S357 
within the intermediate domain of RIPK1 mediates this cell-death-inhibiting 
effect. We propose that ULK1 is a regulator of RIPK1-mediated cell death.
