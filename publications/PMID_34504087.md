# Sequences in the cytoplasmic tail of SARS-CoV-2 Spike facilitate expression at the cell surface and syncytia formation.

The Spike (S) protein of SARS-CoV-2 binds ACE2 to direct fusion with host cells.
S comprises a large external domain, a transmembrane domain, and a short
cytoplasmic tail. Understanding the intracellular trafficking of S is relevant
to SARS-CoV-2 infection, and to vaccines expressing full-length S from mRNA or
adenovirus vectors. Here we report a proteomic screen for cellular factors that
interact with the cytoplasmic tail of S. We confirm interactions with the COPI
and COPII vesicle coats, ERM family actin regulators, and the WIPI3 autophagy
component. The COPII binding site promotes exit from the endoplasmic reticulum,
and although binding to COPI should retain S in the early Golgi where viral
budding occurs, there is a suboptimal histidine residue in the recognition
motif. As a result, S leaks to the surface where it accumulates and can direct
the formation of multinucleate syncytia. Thus, the trafficking signals in the
tail of S indicate that syncytia play a role in the SARS-CoV-2 lifecycle.
