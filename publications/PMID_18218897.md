# Control of genic DNA methylation by a jmjC domain-containing protein in Arabidopsis thaliana.
**Authors:** Saze H, Shiraishi A, Miura A, Kakutani T
**Journal:** Science (2008)
**DOI:** [10.1126/science.1150987](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1150987)

## Abstract

1. Science. 2008 Jan 25;319(5862):462-5. doi: 10.1126/science.1150987.

Control of genic DNA methylation by a jmjC domain-containing protein in
Arabidopsis thaliana.

Saze H(1), Shiraishi A, Miura A, Kakutani T.

Author information:
(1)Department of Integrated Genetics, National Institute of Genetics, Yata 1111,
Mishima, Shizuoka 411-8540, Japan. hsaze@lab.nig.ac.jp

Differential cytosine methylation of repeats and genes is important for
coordination of genome stability and proper gene expression. Through genetic
screen of mutants showing ectopic cytosine methylation in a genic region, we
identified a jmjC-domain gene, IBM1 (increase in bonsai methylation 1), in
Arabidopsis thaliana. In addition to the ectopic cytosine methylation, the ibm1
mutations induced a variety of developmental phenotypes, which depend on
methylation of histone H3 at lysine 9. Paradoxically, the developmental
phenotypes of the ibm1 were enhanced by the mutation in the chromatin-remodeling
gene DDM1 (decrease in DNA methylation 1), which is necessary for keeping
methylation and silencing of repeated heterochromatin loci. Our results
demonstrate the importance of chromatin remodeling and histone modifications in
the differential epigenetic control of repeats and genes.
