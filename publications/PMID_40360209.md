Processing bodies (P-bodies) are membraneless organelles in eukaryotic cells
that play a central role in mRNA metabolism, including mRNA decay, storage, and
translational repression. However, the molecular mechanisms governing their
assembly remain incompletely understood. Here, we identify the C-terminal domain
of EDC4 as the minimal region required for P-body formation, with residues
1266-1401 driving phase separation and EDC4 condensation. To investigate the
functional relevance of P-body integrity, we used the microprotein Nobody (NBDY)
as a selective perturbation tool. Our results revealed that the NBDY 22-41
peptide directly binds the EDC4 C-terminal domain and inhibits its
self-association, thereby selectively dissolving P-bodies without affecting the
canonical mRNA decay pathway. Using this tool, we further examined the impact of
P-body disruption on gene expression. Transcriptome profiling combined with
quantitative validation revealed that P-body loss activates the p53 pathway and
enhances the stability of associated transcripts. Consistent with these
findings, clinical data show that NBDY overexpression is associated with p53
pathway activation in various cancers, and the NBDY 22-41 fragment reduces tumor
cell proliferation and invasion, suggesting a potentially complex role of P-body
dynamics in cancer biology. Together, our study defines the EDC4 C-terminal
domain as a core scaffold for P-body assembly and uncovers a regulatory role of
P-body dynamics in p53-mediated gene expression, with potential implications for
cancer biology.

The enrichment of these factors in P-bodies underscores their role in mRNA turnover, highlighting how P-bodies coordinate deadenylation and decapping to regulate transcript stability in response to cellular needs.

A central player in this process is EDC4 (Enhancer of mRNA decapping 4), which serves as a scaffold for the mRNA decapping complex and facilitates the removal of the 5′ cap structure, a key step in initiating 5′-3′ decay (Braun et al. 2012; Arribas-Layton et al. 2013; Chang et al. 2014). EDC4 has been shown to participate in the process of DNA repair within the nucleus, where it forms a nuclear protein complex with BRCA1, BRIP1, and TOPBP1 to facilitate homologous recombination in the repair of double-strand breaks (DSBs) (Hernandez et al. 2018). Several studies have shown that altered P-body dynamics can lead to deregulated mRNA stability and translation, thereby contributing to the dysregulation of oncogenes and tumor suppressors (Anderson et al. 2015). This suggests that P-body integrity is essential for maintaining proper gene expression balance, and its disruption may contribute to cancer development.

Recent findings have highlighted a novel regulatory mechanism involving Nobody (nonannotated P-body dissociating polypeptide [NBDY]), a microprotein that affects P-body formation by interacting with EDC4 (D'Lima et al. 2017; Nostramo et al. 2019; Na et al. 2020; Bloch et al. 2023). Furthermore, research has identified amino acids 22–41 of NBDY as the key region responsible for binding to EDC4 (D'Lima et al. 2017). Interestingly, the expression levels of NBDY are inversely related to P-body numbers. However, despite significant progress, the exact molecular mechanisms controlling P-body assembly and disassembly remain incompletely understood. Additionally, the potential to manipulate P-body dynamics as a therapeutic target in diseases involving aberrant RNA metabolism is still largely unexplored. Addressing these gaps could pave the way for novel therapeutic interventions aimed at restoring normal RNA processing in pathological conditions.

Given the pivotal role of P-bodies in mRNA regulation, investigating the molecular mechanisms underlying P-body formation and function, particularly through key proteins like EDC4, is essential for understanding their broader impact on gene expression regulation. In this study, we demonstrate that the C-terminal domain of EDC4 is integral to P-body formation. Additionally, our findings indicate that alterations in NBDY expression disrupt EDC4-EDC4 self-interaction, thereby affecting P-body assembly. Furthermore, RNA sequencing results suggest that these disruptions in P-body dynamics are closely linked to the modulation of the p53 signaling pathway, potentially contributing to cancer progression. This study provides novel insights into the role of P-bodies in maintaining cellular homeostasis and how their dysregulation may be implicated in cancer biology.

DISCUSSIONP-bodies are membrane-less organelles found in the cytoplasm, known to participate in mRNA storage, degradation, and translational repression. Recent studies suggest that P-body dysfunction may be linked to various diseases, particularly cancer (Hubstenberger et al. 2017; Luo et al. 2018). Since P-bodies are tightly associated with mRNA metabolism, abnormalities in their formation could impair a cell's ability to respond to environmental stress, potentially promoting or inhibiting tumorigenesis. Although the molecular mechanisms between P-body formation and human diseases remain unclear, evidence indicates that maintaining P-body dynamics is crucial for cellular homeostasis (Nostramo et al. 2019). Under stress conditions, P-bodies may help regulate multiple signaling pathways, including promoting autophagy, apoptosis, or accelerating the degradation of aberrant mRNAs to modulate cell survival (Protter and Parker 2016).In this study, we investigated the mechanisms of P-body formation and their impact on posttranscriptional regulation, providing deeper insights into how these cellular organelles contribute to gene expression control. Our findings identified the C-terminal domain of EDC4 as a key determinant of P-body formation and maintenance, and we further demonstrated that NBDY 22–41 inhibits P-body formation by binding to this region without interfering with EDC4's interaction with core decapping factors. Interestingly, previous studies have shown that full-length NBDY interacts with the N-terminal WD40 domain of EDC4 (Na et al. 2020; Bloch et al. 2023), suggesting that NBDY engages with multiple regions of EDC4. While the functional significance of the N-terminal interaction remains unclear, its presence suggests a more complex regulatory mechanism. By elucidating how NBDY 22–41 selectively targets the C-terminal domain of EDC4, our study provides mechanistic insights into P-body assembly and its regulation. As a key tumor suppressor, p53 oversees DNA damage responses, cell cycle regulation, and apoptosis, making its dysregulation a driving force in cancer development (Harris and Levine 2005; Fischer 2017; Lahalle et al. 2021). These results suggest that impairing P-body formation might act as a protective mechanism against external stress or the buildup of aberrant mRNAs.

While our study demonstrates that disrupting P-body formation through NBDY 22–41 overexpression leads to the upregulation of the p53 pathway and inhibits cancer cell proliferation and migration, it is important to note that P-body dynamics may have dual roles in tumorigenesis. On one hand, P-body disruption can activate tumor suppressor pathways, such as p53, leading to cell cycle arrest and apoptosis, which may suppress tumor growth. On the other hand, P-body dysfunction could also impair the degradation of oncogenic mRNAs or stress-responsive transcripts, potentially promoting cancer progression under certain conditions (Anderson et al. 2015; Lavalee et al. 2021). This suggests a possible dual role for P-body disruption in cancer progression; further studies are needed to establish a clear link between P-body dynamics and their impact on tumorigenesis.

This finding highlights a critical knowledge gap in P-body-mediated gene regulation and emphasizes the need for further studies to elucidate the roles of these genes. Such research could provide valuable insights into the broader impact of P-body disruption on cellular homeostasis.

In conclusion, P-bodies, as critical hubs for posttranscriptional regulation, directly impact mRNA stability and translational efficiency. By elucidating the roles of EDC4 and NBDY in this process, our findings reveal the regulatory influence of P-body formation on the p53 signaling pathway and provide evidence supporting the association between P-body dynamics and tumor development. For validation and rescue experiments, HEK293T wild-type or EDC4-KO cells were either transfected with a GFP control or GFP-EDC4 (either WT, 1–1265, 1–1265 fused TDP43 CTD). Forty-eight hours after transfection, total RNA was extracted and reverse transcribed using oligo(dT)20 primers. Target mRNA amount was subsequently determined by qPCR using the respective primer pair and normalized to GAPDH abundance in the same sample. Relative abundances of target mRNAs in three independent experiments were determined using the Livak method (the wild-type sample was arbitrarily set to 1) (Livak and Schmittgen 2001).
