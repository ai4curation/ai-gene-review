# Structural basis of substrate binding specificity revealed by the crystal structures of polyamine receptors SpuD and SpuE from Pseudomonas aeruginosa.
**Authors:** Wu D, Lim SC, Dong Y, Wu J, Tao F, Zhou L, Zhang LH, Song H
**Journal:** J Mol Biol (2012)
**DOI:** [10.1016/j.jmb.2012.01.010](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jmb.2012.01.010)

## Abstract

Structural basis of substrate binding specificity revealed by the crystal
structures of polyamine receptors SpuD and SpuE from Pseudomonas aeruginosa.

Wu D(1), Lim SC, Dong Y, Wu J, Tao F, Zhou L, Zhang LH, Song H.

Author information:
(1)Institute of Molecular and Cell Biology, 61 Biopolis Drive, Proteos,

The type III secretion system (T3SS) of Pseudomonas aeruginosa is a key
virulence determinant whose expression is induced by polyamine signals from
mammalian host. SpuD and SpuE were postulated to be spermidine-preferential
binding proteins, which regulate the polyamine content in this bacterial
pathogen. In this study, we found that SpuD is a putrescine-preferential binding
protein, while SpuE binds to spermidine exclusively. We have determined the
crystal structures of SpuD in free form and in complex with putrescine and SpuE
in free form and in complex with spermidine. Upon ligand binding, SpuD and SpuE
undergo an "open-to-closed" conformational switch with the resultant closed
ligand-bound forms, SpuD-putrescine and SpuE-spermidine, similar to their
Escherichia coli counterparts PotF-putrescine and PotD-spermidine, respectively.
Structural comparison suggested that two aromatic residues, Trp271 of SpuE and
Phe273 of SpuD in segment II region, are the key structural determinants for
putrescine/spermidine recognition specificity. Mutagenesis combined with
isothermal titration calorimetry showed that substitution of Trp271 by Phe
enabled SpuE to gain substantial binding affinity for putrescine, while
replacement of Phe273 by Trp reduced the binding affinity of SpuD toward
putrescine by 250-fold. Altogether, these results revealed the molecular
mechanism governing polyamine recognition specificity by SpuD and SpuE and
provide the basis for further structural and functional studies of polyamine
signal importation system in P. aeruginosa.
