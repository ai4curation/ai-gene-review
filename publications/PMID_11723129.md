# The germinal isozyme of angiotensin-converting enzyme can substitute for the somatic isozyme in maintaining normal renal structure and functions.
**Authors:** Kessler SP, Gomos JB, Scheidemantel TS, Rowe TM, Smith HL, Sen GC
**Journal:** J Biol Chem (2002)
**DOI:** [10.1074/jbc.M109474200](https://doi.org/10.1074/jbc.M109474200)

## Abstract

The germinal isozyme of angiotensin-converting enzyme can substitute for the
somatic isozyme in maintaining normal renal structure and functions.

Kessler SP(1), Gomos JB, Scheidemantel TS, Rowe TM, Smith HL, Sen GC.

Author information:
(1)Department of Molecular Biology, Lerner Research Institute, Cleveland Clinic

The angiotensin-converting enzyme (ACE) gene encodes two structurally related
isozymes, somatic ACE and germinal ACE, that are uniquely expressed in discrete
locations in the body. The importance of ACE in these cell types was revealed by
generating Ace -/- mice, which exhibit multiple abnormalities including renal
structural defects and functions, hypotension, and male sterility. To test the
hypothesis that specific physiological functions of ACE are mediated by
isozyme-specific and tissue-specific expression patterns, we have used a
transgenic approach to develop mouse strains that express just one ACE isoform
in the target tissue of Ace -/- mice. The mice described in this report produce
germinal ACE in sperm and serum. These mice were as healthy as wild type mice,
and the males were fertile. Interestingly, they had normal kidney structure,
fluid homeostasis, and partially restored urine concentration despite having low
blood pressure. This result demonstrated that circulating germinal ACE is
sufficient for maintaining normal kidney structure and fluid homeostasis but
insufficient for restoring blood pressure to normal levels.
