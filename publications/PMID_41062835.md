Precise control of transcription is essential for cell survival under stress 
conditions, including DNA damage. While mechanisms of DNA damage-induced 
transcriptional silencing are well characterized, how transcription resumes 
remains less understood. Here we identify a new role for poly(ADP-ribose) 
polymerase 1 (PARP1) in transcriptional restart during the DNA damage response 
(DDR) through a mechanism termed poly(ADP-ribose)-mediated stabilization 
(PARSTA) of AFF1. Upon DNA damage, PARP1 binds to and PARylates AFF1 in a region 
targeted by the E3 ligase Siah1, preventing AFF1 ubiquitination and promoting 
its stability. This stabilization supports efficient transcriptional recovery 
after DNA damage. Notably, cells resistant to genotoxic stress exhibit elevated 
PARP1 activity and AFF1 levels, while AFF1 depletion impairs DNA repair and 
survival. Together, these findings expand PARP1's role to the transcriptional 
recovery phase in DDR and suggest that targeting the PARSTA pathway may offer 
therapeutic potential in diseases characterized by hyperactive PARP1 and 
elevated levels of AFF1.
