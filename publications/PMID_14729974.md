# Nicotinamide clearance by Pnc1 directly regulates Sir2-mediated silencing and longevity.
**Authors:** Gallo CM, Smith DL Jr, Smith JS
**Journal:** Mol Cell Biol (2004)
**DOI:** [10.1128/MCB.24.3.1301-1312.2004](https://doi.org/10.1128/MCB.24.3.1301-1312.2004)

## Abstract

1. Mol Cell Biol. 2004 Feb;24(3):1301-12. doi: 10.1128/MCB.24.3.1301-1312.2004.

Nicotinamide clearance by Pnc1 directly regulates Sir2-mediated silencing and
longevity.

Gallo CM(1), Smith DL Jr, Smith JS.

Author information:
(1)Department of Biochemistry and Molecular Genetics, University of Virginia
Health System, Charlottesville, Virginia 22908, USA.

The Saccharomyces cerevisiae Sir2 protein is an NAD(+)-dependent histone
deacetylase (HDAC) that functions in transcriptional silencing and longevity.
The NAD(+) salvage pathway protein, Npt1, regulates Sir2-mediated processes by
maintaining a sufficiently high intracellular NAD(+) concentration. However,
another NAD(+) salvage pathway component, Pnc1, modulates silencing
independently of the NAD(+) concentration. Nicotinamide (NAM) is a by-product of
the Sir2 deacetylase reaction and is a natural Sir2 inhibitor. Pnc1 is a
nicotinamidase that converts NAM to nicotinic acid. Here we show that
recombinant Pnc1 stimulates Sir2 HDAC activity in vitro by preventing the
accumulation of NAM produced by Sir2. In vivo, telomeric, rDNA, and HM silencing
are differentially sensitive to inhibition by NAM. Furthermore, PNC1
overexpression suppresses the inhibitory effect of exogenously added NAM on
silencing, life span, and Hst1-mediated transcriptional repression. Finally, we
show that stress suppresses the inhibitory effect of NAM through the induction
of PNC1 expression. Pnc1, therefore, positively regulates Sir2-mediated
silencing and longevity by preventing the accumulation of intracellular NAM
during times of stress.
