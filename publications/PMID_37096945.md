# A role of cryptochrome for magnetic field-dependent improvement of sleep quality, lifespan, and motor function in Drosophila.
**Authors:** Kawasaki H, Okano H, Ishiwatari H, Kishi T, Ishida N
**Journal:** Genes Cells (2023)
**DOI:** [10.1111/gtc.13030](https://doi.org/10.1111/gtc.13030)

## Abstract

1. Genes Cells. 2023 Jul;28(7):496-502. doi: 10.1111/gtc.13030. Epub 2023 Apr 25.

A role of cryptochrome for magnetic field-dependent improvement of sleep
quality, lifespan, and motor function in Drosophila.

Kawasaki H(1), Okano H(2), Ishiwatari H(3), Kishi T(3), Ishida N(1).

Author information:

Science, Tsukuba, Japan.
(2)Advanced Institute of Innovative Technology, Saitama University, Saitama,

(3)Soken Medical Co., Ltd., Tokyo, Japan.

Understanding the molecular genetic basis of animal magnet reception has been
one of the big challenges in molecular biology. Recently it was discovered that
the magnetic sense of Drosophila melanogaster is mediated by the ultraviolet
(UV)-A/blue light photoreceptor cryptochrome (Cry). Here, using the fruit fly as
a magnet-receptive model organism, we show that the magnetic field exposure
(0.4-0.6 mT) extended lifespan under starvation, but not in cryptochrome mutant
flies (cryb ). The magnetic field exposure increases motor function in wild type
and neurodegenerative disease model flies. Furthermore, the magnetic field
exposure improved sleep quality at night-time specific manner, but not in cryb .
We also showed that repeated AC magnetic field exposure increased climbing
activity in wild-type Drosophila, but not in cryb . The data suggests that
magnetic field-dependent improvement of lifespan, sleep quality, and motor
function is mediated through a cry-dependent pathway in Drosophila.
