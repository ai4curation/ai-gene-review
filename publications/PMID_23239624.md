Comment in

Elongation factor P (EF-P) is a translation factor of unknown function that has
been implicated in a great variety of cellular processes. Here, we show that
EF-P prevents ribosome from stalling during synthesis of proteins containing
consecutive prolines, such as PPG, PPP, or longer proline strings, in natural
and engineered model proteins. EF-P promotes peptide-bond formation and
stabilizes the peptidyl-transfer RNA in the catalytic center of the ribosome.
EF-P is posttranslationally modified by a hydroxylated β-lysine attached to a
lysine residue. The modification enhances the catalytic proficiency of the
factor mainly by increasing its affinity to the ribosome. We propose that EF-P
and its eukaryotic homolog, eIF5A, are essential for the synthesis of a subset
of proteins containing proline stretches in all cells.
