The target of rapamycin (TOR) is a highly conserved protein kinase and a central
controller of growth. Mammalian TOR complex 2 (mTORC2) regulates AGC kinase
family members and is implicated in various disorders, including cancer and
diabetes. Here, we investigated the upstream regulation of mTORC2. A genetic
screen in yeast and subsequent studies in mammalian cells revealed that
ribosomes, but not protein synthesis, are required for mTORC2 signaling. Active
mTORC2 was physically associated with the ribosome, and insulin-stimulated PI3K
signaling promoted mTORC2-ribosome binding, suggesting that ribosomes activate
mTORC2 directly. Findings with melanoma and colon cancer cells suggest that
mTORC2-ribosome association is important in oncogenic PI3K signaling. Thus,
TORC2-ribosome interaction is a likely conserved mechanism of TORC2 activation
that is physiologically relevant in both normal and cancer cells. As ribosome
content determines growth capacity of a cell, this mechanism of TORC2 regulation
ensures that TORC2 is active only in growing cells.
