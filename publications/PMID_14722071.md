# Calcium current in rat cardiomyocytes is modulated by the carboxyl-terminal ahnak domain.
**Authors:** Alvarez J, Hamplova J, Hohaus A, Morano I, Haase H, Vassort G
**Journal:** J Biol Chem (2004)
**DOI:** [10.1074/jbc.M312177200](https://doi.org/10.1074/jbc.M312177200)

## Abstract

1. J Biol Chem. 2004 Mar 26;279(13):12456-61. doi: 10.1074/jbc.M312177200. Epub
2004 Jan 12.

Calcium current in rat cardiomyocytes is modulated by the carboxyl-terminal
ahnak domain.

Alvarez J(1), Hamplova J, Hohaus A, Morano I, Haase H, Vassort G.

Author information:
(1)Physiopathologie Cardiovasculaire, INSERM U-390, CHU Arnaud de Villeneuve,
F-34295 Montpellier Cedex 5, France.

Ahnak, a protein of 5643 amino acids, interacts with the regulatory beta-subunit
of cardiac calcium channels and with F-actin. Recently, we defined the binding
sites among the protein partners in the carboxyl-terminal domain of ahnak. Here
we further narrowed down the beta(2)-interaction sites to the carboxyl-terminal
188 amino acids of ahnak by the recombinant ahnak protein fragments P3 (amino
acids 5456-5556) and P4 (amino acids 5556-5643). The effects of these P3 and P4
fragments on the calcium current were investigated under whole-cell patch clamp
conditions on rat ventricular cardiomyocytes. P4 but not P3 increased
significantly the current amplitude by 22.7 +/- 5% without affecting its voltage
dependence. The slow component of calcium current inactivation was slowed down
by both P3 and P4, whereas only P3 slowed significantly the fast one. The
composite recombinant protein fragment P3-P4 induced similar modifications to
the ones induced by each of the ahnak fragments. In the presence of
carboxyl-terminal ahnak protein fragments, isoprenaline induced a similar
relative increase in current amplitude and shift in current kinetics. The
actin-stabilizing agents, phalloidin and jasplakinolide, did not modify the
effects of these ahnak protein fragments on calcium current in control
conditions nor in the presence of isoprenaline. Hence, our results suggest that
the functional effects of P3, P4, and P3-P4 on calcium current are mediated by
targeting the ahnak-beta(2)-subunit interaction rather than by targeting the
ahnak-F-actin interaction. More specifically they suggest that binding of the
beta(2)-subunit to the endogenous subsarcolemmal giant ahnak protein re-primes
the alpha(1C)/beta(2)-subunit interaction and that the ahnak-derived proteins
relieve the beta(2)-subunit from this inhibition.
