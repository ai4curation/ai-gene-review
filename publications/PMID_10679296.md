# Association of EXT1 and EXT2, hereditary multiple exostoses gene products, in Golgi apparatus.
**Authors:** Kobayashi S, Morimoto K, Shimizu T, Takahashi M, Kurosawa H, Shirasawa T
**Journal:** Biochem Biophys Res Commun (2000)
**DOI:** [10.1006/bbrc.2000.2219](https://doi.org/10.1006/bbrc.2000.2219)

## Abstract

1. Biochem Biophys Res Commun. 2000 Feb 24;268(3):860-7. doi:
10.1006/bbrc.2000.2219.

Association of EXT1 and EXT2, hereditary multiple exostoses gene products, in
Golgi apparatus.

Kobayashi S(1), Morimoto K, Shimizu T, Takahashi M, Kurosawa H, Shirasawa T.

Author information:
(1)Department of Molecular Genetics, Tokyo Metropolitan Institute of
Gerontology, 35-2 Sakae-cho, Itabashi-ku, Tokyo, 173-0015, Japan.

We prepared the specific antibodies for EXT1 and EXT2, hereditary multiple
exostoses (HME) gene products, and characterized their expression, subcellular
localization, and protein association among EXT members. Biochemical analyses
indicate that EXT1 and EXT2 can associate and form homo/hetero-oligomers in vivo
with or without HME-linked mutations, EXT1 (R340C) and EXT2 (D227N), when
exogenously expressed in COS-7 cells. An immunocytochemical analysis showed that
both EXT1 and EXT2 localized in Golgi apparatus, irrespective of HME mutations.
An immunohistochemical analysis on developing bones further showed that both
EXT1 and EXT2 were concomitantly expressed in hypertrophic chondrocytes of
forelimb bones from 1-day-old neonatal mouse, but down-regulated in maturing
chondrocytes of developing cartilage from 21-day-old mouse. Taken together with
the recent finding that EXTs encode for the glycosyltransferase required for the
synthesis of heparan sulfate [Lind, T., Tufaro, F., McCormick, C., Lindahl, U.,
and Lindholt, K. (1998) J. Biol. Chem. 273, 26265-26268], our results implied a
molecular basis that a HME-linked mutation found in EXT genes could interfere
the physiological function(s) of EXT homo/hetero-oligomers as
glycosyltransferases in the developing bones of HME patients.
