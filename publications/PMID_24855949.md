Regulation of nuclear import is fundamental to eukaryotic biology. The majority
of nuclear import pathways are mediated by importin-cargo interactions. Yet not
all nuclear proteins interact with importins, necessitating the identification
of a general importin-independent nuclear import pathway. Here, we identify a
code that determines importin-independent nuclear import of ankyrin repeats
(ARs), a structural motif found in over 250 human proteins with diverse
functions. AR-containing proteins (ARPs) with a hydrophobic residue at the 13th
position of two consecutive ARs bind RanGDP efficiently, and consequently enter
the nucleus. This code, experimentally tested in 17 ARPs, predicts the
nuclear-cytoplasmic localization of over 150 annotated human ARPs with high
accuracy and is acquired by the most common familial melanoma-associated CDKN2A
mutation, leading to nuclear accumulation of mutant p16ink4a. The RaDAR
(RanGDP/AR) pathway represents a general importin-independent nuclear import
pathway and is frequently used by AR-containing transcriptional regulators,
especially those regulating NF-κB/p53.
