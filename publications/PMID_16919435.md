Integrins can alter cellular behavior through the recruitment and activation of
signaling proteins such as non-receptor tyrosine kinases including focal
adhesion kinase (FAK) and c-Src that form a dual kinase complex. The FAK-Src
complex binds to and can phosphorylate various adaptor proteins such as p130Cas
and paxillin. In normal cells, multiple integrin-regulated linkages exist to
activate FAK or Src. Activated FAK-Src functions to promote cell motility, cell
cycle progression and cell survival. Recent studies have found that the FAK-Src
complex is activated in many tumor cells and generates signals leading to tumor
growth and metastasis. As both FAK and Src catalytic activities are important in
promoting VEGF-associated tumor angiogenesis and protease-associated tumor
metastasis, support is growing that FAK and Src may be therapeutically relevant
targets in the inhibition of tumor progression.
