# A nucleosidase required for in vivo function of the S-adenosyl-L-methionine radical enzyme, biotin synthase.
**Authors:** Choi-Rhee E, Cronan JE
**Journal:** Chem Biol (2005)
**DOI:** [10.1016/j.chembiol.2005.04.012](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.chembiol.2005.04.012)

## Abstract

1. Chem Biol. 2005 May;12(5):589-93. doi: 10.1016/j.chembiol.2005.04.012.

A nucleosidase required for in vivo function of the S-adenosyl-L-methionine
radical enzyme, biotin synthase.

Choi-Rhee E(1), Cronan JE.

Author information:
(1)Department of Microbiology, University of Illinois, Urbana 61801, USA.

Biotin synthase is an S-adenosyl-L-methionine (SAM) radical enzyme that inserts
sulfur into dethiobiotin to produce biotin. The reaction proceeds through
5'-deoxyadenosyl radical intermediates that become reduced during the sulfur
insertion step to give another product of the reaction, 5'-deoxyadenosine. We
report that Escherichia coli strains lacking the
5'-methylthioadenosine/S-adenosylhomocysteine nucleosidase encoded by the pfs
gene are deficient in biotin synthase activity due to accumulation of
5'-deoxyadenosine, a new substrate of the pfs-encoded nucleosidase.
Physiological experiments indicate that lipoic acid synthase, another SAM
radical enzyme, is also inhibited by 5'-deoxyadenosine accumulation.
