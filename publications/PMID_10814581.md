# Competitive binding to a charged leucine motif represses transformation by a papillomavirus E6 oncoprotein.
**Authors:** Bohl J, Das K, Dasgupta B, Vande Pol SB
**Journal:** Virology (2000)
**DOI:** [10.1006/viro.2000.0316](https://doi.org/10.1006/viro.2000.0316)

## Abstract

1. Virology. 2000 May 25;271(1):163-70. doi: 10.1006/viro.2000.0316.

Competitive binding to a charged leucine motif represses transformation by a
papillomavirus E6 oncoprotein.

Bohl J(1), Das K, Dasgupta B, Vande Pol SB.

Author information:
(1)Institute of Pathology, Case Western Reserve University School of Medicine,
Cleveland, Ohio 44106, USA.

E6 oncoproteins from HPV-16 and bovine papillomavirus type 1 (BPV-1) bind to
similar leucine-rich peptides termed charged leucine motifs found on the
cellular focal adhesion protein paxillin and the E3 ubiquitin ligase E6AP. BPV-1
E6 (BE6) mutants that do not bind to paxillin are defective at inducing cellular
transformation. It is possible, however, that BE6 mutants that do not bind
paxillin are defective for transformation for an unrelated reason than the
ability to bind to charged leucine motifs. To address the role of BE6
interaction with charged leucine motifs, we fused a BE6-binding charged leucine
motif to the amino terminus of BE6, thereby creating an autoinhibitory binding
domain. We found that the fusion protein failed to bind to paxillin or transform
murine C127 cells. Mutation of the amino terminal binding motif in the fusion
protein restored both interaction with paxillin and transformation. This
demonstrates that BE6 transformation requires binding to charged leucine motifs
on particular cellular proteins and that transformation by papillomavirus
oncoproteins can be repressed by competitive interactions with charged leucine
motifs.
