# The anaerobic ribonucleotide reductase from Escherichia coli. The small protein is an activating enzyme containing a [4fe-4s](2+) center.
**Authors:** Tamarit J, Mulliez E, Meier C, Trautwein A, Fontecave M
**Journal:** J Biol Chem (1999)
**DOI:** [10.1074/jbc.274.44.31291](https://doi.org/10.1074/jbc.274.44.31291)

## Abstract

1. J Biol Chem. 1999 Oct 29;274(44):31291-6. doi: 10.1074/jbc.274.44.31291.

The anaerobic ribonucleotide reductase from Escherichia coli. The small protein
is an activating enzyme containing a [4fe-4s](2+) center.

Tamarit J(1), Mulliez E, Meier C, Trautwein A, Fontecave M.

Author information:
(1)Laboratoire de Chimie et Biochimie des Centres Rédox Biologiques,
Commissariat à l'Energie Atomique (CEA)/Département de Biologie Moléculaire et
Structurale/Chimie et Biochimie 1087 CNRS, Université Joseph Fourier, 17 rue des
Martyrs.

For deoxyribonucleotide synthesis during anaerobic growth, Escherichia coli
cells depend on an oxygen-sensitive class III ribonucleotide reductase. The
enzyme system consists of two proteins: protein alpha, on which ribonucleotides
bind and are reduced, and protein beta, of which the function is to introduce a
catalytically essential glycyl radical on protein alpha. Protein beta can
assemble one [4Fe-4S] center per polypeptide enjoying both the [4Fe-4S](2+) and
[4Fe-4S](1+) redox state, as shown by iron and sulfide analysis, Mössbauer
spectroscopy (delta = 0.43 mm.s(-1), DeltaE(Q) = 1.0 mm.s(-1), [4Fe-4S](2+)),
and EPR spectroscopy (g = 2. 03 and 1.93, [4Fe-4S](1+)). This iron center is
sensitive to oxygen and can decompose into stable [2Fe-2S](2+) centers during
exposure to air. This degraded form is nevertheless active, albeit to a lesser
extent because of the conversion of the cluster into [4Fe-4S] forms during the
strongly reductive conditions of the assay. Furthermore, protein beta has the
potential to activate several molecules of protein alpha, suggesting that
protein beta is an activating enzyme rather than a component of an
alpha(2)beta(2) complex as previously claimed.
