# HIF overexpression correlates with biallelic loss of fumarate hydratase in renal cancer: novel role of fumarate in regulation of HIF stability.
**Authors:** Isaacs JS, Jung YJ, Mole DR, Lee S, Torres-Cabala C, Chung YL, Merino M, Trepel J, Zbar B, Toro J, Ratcliffe PJ, Linehan WM, Neckers L
**Journal:** Cancer Cell (2005)
**DOI:** [10.1016/j.ccr.2005.06.017](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ccr.2005.06.017)

## Abstract

1. Cancer Cell. 2005 Aug;8(2):143-53. doi: 10.1016/j.ccr.2005.06.017.

HIF overexpression correlates with biallelic loss of fumarate hydratase in renal
cancer: novel role of fumarate in regulation of HIF stability.

Isaacs JS(1), Jung YJ, Mole DR, Lee S, Torres-Cabala C, Chung YL, Merino M,
Trepel J, Zbar B, Toro J, Ratcliffe PJ, Linehan WM, Neckers L.

Author information:

Individuals with hemizygous germline fumarate hydratase (FH) mutations are
predisposed to renal cancer. These tumors predominantly exhibit functional
inactivation of the remaining wild-type allele, implicating FH inactivation as a
tumor-promoting event. Hypoxia-inducible factors are expressed in many cancers
and are increased in clear cell renal carcinomas. Under normoxia, the HIFs are
labile due to VHL-dependent proteasomal degradation, but stabilization occurs
under hypoxia due to inactivation of HIF prolyl hydroxylase (HPH), which
prevents HIF hydroxylation and VHL recognition. We demonstrate that FH
inhibition, together with elevated intracellular fumarate, coincides with HIF
upregulation. Further, we show that fumarate acts as a competitive inhibitor of
HPH. These data delineate a novel fumarate-dependent pathway for regulating HPH
activity and HIF protein levels.
