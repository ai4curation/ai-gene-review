# ATR kinase activation mediated by MutSalpha and MutLalpha in response to cytotoxic O6-methylguanine adducts.
**Authors:** Yoshioka K, Yoshioka Y, Hsieh P
**Journal:** Mol Cell (2006)
**DOI:** [10.1016/j.molcel.2006.04.023](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.molcel.2006.04.023)
**PMC:** [PMC2423943](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC2423943/)

## Abstract

1. Mol Cell. 2006 May 19;22(4):501-10. doi: 10.1016/j.molcel.2006.04.023.

ATR kinase activation mediated by MutSalpha and MutLalpha in response to
cytotoxic O6-methylguanine adducts.

Yoshioka K(1), Yoshioka Y, Hsieh P.

Author information:
(1)Genetics and Biochemistry Branch, National Institute of Diabetes and
Digestive and Kidney Diseases, National Institutes of Health, Bethesda, Maryland
20892, USA.

S(N)1-type alkylating agents that produce cytotoxic O(6)-methyl-G (O(6)-meG) DNA
adducts induce cell cycle arrest and apoptosis in a manner requiring the DNA
mismatch repair (MMR) proteins MutSalpha and MutLalpha. Here, we show that
checkpoint signaling in response to DNA methylation occurs during S phase and
requires DNA replication that gives rise to O(6)-meG/T mispairs. DNA binding
studies reveal that MutSalpha specifically recognizes O(6)-meG/T mispairs, but
not O(6)-meG/C. In an in vitro assay, ATR-ATRIP, but not RPA, is preferentially
recruited to O(6)-meG/T mismatches in a MutSalpha- and MutLalpha-dependent
manner. Furthermore, ATR kinase is activated to phosphorylate Chk1 in the
presence of O(6)-meG/T mispairs and MMR proteins. These results suggest that MMR
proteins can act as direct sensors of methylation damage and help recruit
ATR-ATRIP to sites of cytotoxic O(6)-meG adducts to initiate ATR checkpoint
signaling.
