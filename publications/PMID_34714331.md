# Agrin/Lrp4 signal constrains MuSK-dependent neuromuscular synapse development in appendicular muscle.
**Authors:** Walker LJ, Roque RA, Navarro MF, Granato M
**Journal:** Development (2021)
**DOI:** [10.1242/dev.199790](https://doi.org/10.1242/dev.199790)
**PMC:** [PMC8602948](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8602948/)

## Abstract

1. Development. 2021 Nov 1;148(21):dev199790. doi: 10.1242/dev.199790. Epub 2021
Oct 29.

Agrin/Lrp4 signal constrains MuSK-dependent neuromuscular synapse development in
appendicular muscle.

Walker LJ(1), Roque RA(1), Navarro MF(1), Granato M(1).

Author information:
(1)Department of Cell and Developmental Biology, Perelman School of Medicine,
University of Pennsylvania, Philadelphia, PA 19104, USA.

The receptor tyrosine kinase MuSK, its co-receptor Lrp4 and the Agrin ligand
constitute a signaling pathway that is crucial in axial muscle for neuromuscular
synapse development, yet whether this pathway functions similarly in
appendicular muscle is unclear. Here, using the larval zebrafish pectoral fin,
equivalent to tetrapod forelimbs, we show that, similar to axial muscle,
developing appendicular muscles form aneural acetylcholine receptor (AChR)
clusters prior to innervation. As motor axons arrive, neural AChR clusters form,
eventually leading to functional synapses in a MuSK-dependent manner. We find
that loss of Agrin or Lrp4 function, which abolishes synaptic AChR clusters in
axial muscle, results in enlarged presynaptic nerve regions and progressively
expanding appendicular AChR clusters, mimicking the consequences of motoneuron
ablation. Moreover, musk depletion in lrp4 mutants partially restores synaptic
AChR patterning. Combined, our results provide compelling evidence that, in
addition to the canonical pathway in which Agrin/Lrp4 stimulates MuSK activity,
Agrin/Lrp4 signaling in appendicular muscle constrains MuSK-dependent
neuromuscular synapse organization. Thus, we reveal a previously unappreciated
role for Agrin/Lrp4 signaling, thereby highlighting distinct differences between
axial and appendicular synapse development.
