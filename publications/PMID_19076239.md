# Centaurin-like protein Cnt5 contributes to arsenic and cadmium resistance in fission yeast.

Arsenic (As) and cadmium (Cd) are two of the most hazardous substances in the 
environment and have been implicated in a number of human diseases including 
cancer. Their mechanisms of toxicity and subsequent carcinogenesis are not 
understood. To identify the genes involved in As/Cd detoxification, we screened 
a random insertional mutagenesis library of Schizosaccharomyces pombe for 
mutants that are hypersensitive to As/Cd. Mutations were mapped to spc1(+) 
(sty1(+)) and SPBC17G9.08c. Spc1 is a stress-activated protein kinase 
orthologous to human p38. A fragment of SPBC17G9.08c was previously identified 
as csx2, a high-copy suppressor of cut6 that encodes an acetyl-CoA carboxylase 
involved in fatty acid biosynthesis. SPBC17G9.08c is a member of the centaurin 
ADP ribosylation factor GTPase activating protein family found in a variety of 
fungi, plants and metazoans, but not in Saccharomyces cerevisiae. Cnt5, so named 
because its closest human homolog is centaurin beta-5, binds to phosphatidic 
acid and phosphatidyl serine in vitro. Microscopic localization of Cnt5-GFP 
indicates significant redistribution of Cnt5 from the cytoplasm to the cell 
membranes in response to As stress. These data suggest a model in which Cnt5 
contributes to As/Cd resistance by maintaining membrane integrity or by 
modulating membrane trafficking.

Cellular detoxification mechanisms of As and Cd are of significant importance to human health and biological remediation schemes. Model organisms have and continue to play a central role in defining these mechanisms. In the fission yeast Schizosaccharomyces pombe, the enzyme phytochelatin synthase (PCS) has a critical function in the detoxification of Cd. Ycf1p is a homologue of the human MRP drug resistance pump and transports As(GS)3 complex into the vacuole (Liu et al., 2002). In S. cerevisiae, Ycf1p plays a key role in Cd2+ detoxification by transporting bis(glutathionato)cadmium (Cd– [GS]2) complexes into the vacuole (Li et al., 1997).

Here we describe a screen for fission yeast mutants that are hypersensitive to As/Cd. In addition to a mutant of spc1+ (sty1+), which encodes a mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK) homolog activated by various types of cytotoxic stress, we have also identified a mutant in a previously uncharacterized gene that we have named cnt5+. Fission yeast Cnt5 is a novel member of the Centaurin Arf GAP protein family found in mammals and plants but not in the Saccharomyces cerevisiae (budding yeast). Our studies show that Cnt5 plays a role in As/Cd detoxification. Cnt5 binds phospholipids and concentrates near cell membranes after As treatment, suggesting a possible role in membrane integrity or trafficking.

ResultsIdentification of a novel mutant sensitive to cadmium and arsenicA library comprising ~2000 potential mutants of S. pombe was made by random insertion of a ura4+ DNA fragment. This library was screened to identify mutants sensitive to As or Cd. Upon re-screening 160 colonies, 2 mutants (A2 and C1) were obtained in which As/Cd sensitivity clearly co-segregated with the ura4+ marker. Mutant A2 displayed strong sensitivity to both As and Cd on agar plates, whereas mutant C1 was preferentially sensitive to As (Fig. 1A). Mutant A2 was also sensitive to oxidative stress (hydrogen peroxide) and osmotic stress (potassium chloride), whereas mutant C1 was not (Fig. 1A). Indeed, in these assays mutant A2 closely resembled an spc1Δ strain that was included as positive control. The mutants were also tested in microculture growth assays, in which A2 was clearly sensitive to both As and Cd, while mutant C1 appeared preferentially sensitive to Cd and only weakly sensitive to As (Fig. 1B). The phenotypes of mutant C1, in which in agar media it was preferentially sensitive to As while in liquid media it was preferentially sensitive to Cd, were reproduced in multiple experiments. We speculate that there may be differential bioavailability of Cd and As in the two medias, although other explanations are possible.Inverse PCR with genomic DNA was used to map the ura4+ insertion points in the mutants. Mutant A2 contained a ura4+ insertion in spc1+ (data not shown), a finding consistent with the acute sensitivity of this mutant to a variety of cytotoxic stress agents (Fig. 1A), and with our previous finding that spc1Δ cells are sensitive to arsenic (Rodriguez-Gabriel & Russell, 2005). Mutant C1, on the other hand, was found to have a ura4+ insertion in SPBC17G9.08c (Fig. 2A), a gene not previously known to be involved in As/Cd resistance.SPBC17G9.08c/Cnt5 is related to Centaurin proteinsSPBC17G9.08c encodes an 870 amino acid protein, a fragment of which was previously identified as csx2+ (Saitoh et al., 1996). This fragment of SPBC17G9.08c is a multi-copy suppressor of the lethality of cut6 temperature-sensitive mutants that are defective for a putative acetyl-CoA carboxylase (Saitoh et al., 1996). Position-Specific Iterated BLAST (PSI-BLAST) analysis with the full-length sequence protein encoded by SPBC17G9.08c revealed that it has putative orthologs in a variety of fungi, plants and metazoans, but not in S. cerevisiae. These genes have apparently been lost from the budding yeast genome during evolution. Of the putative mammalian orthologs members of the centaurin beta 5 (CENTB5) family are most similar to SPBC17G9.08c; therefore, we named the full-length SPBC17G9.08c gene cnt5+ (centaurin beta 5-like gene) (Fig. 2A). In common with other members of the centaurin protein family, Cnt5 has a Pleckstrin Homology (PH) domain, that often mediates phosphoinositide binding, and ADP Ribosylation Factor GTPase Activating Protein (Arf GAP) domain, that is most typically involved in controlling protein function in vesicular trafficking and cytoskeletal regulation (Fig. 2B). Amino acid alignment (Thompson et al., 1994) of PH domain (Fig. 2C) and Arf GAP domain (Fig. 2D) also revealed the conservation of specific residues among species. The presence of these domains suggests that Centaurin proteins may transduce PI 3-kinase activation into coordinated control of Arf-dependent pathways. DiscussionHere we have reported the discovery and initial characterization of Cnt5, a fission yeast protein involved in tolerance of arsenic and cadmium. One of the most interesting features of Cnt5 is that it is a member of the Centaurin protein family. These proteins are conserved from fission yeast to humans, but their functions are poorly understood. They have been implicated in dendritic differentiation (Moore et al., 2007), cytoskeleton reorganization (Venkateswarlu et al., 2007), and membrane trafficking (Randazzo and Hirsch, 2004; Nie and Randazzo, 2006; Inoue & Randazzo, 2007), but their molecular functions are largely unknown. The discovery of As/Cd-sensitive phenotypes associated with cnt5 mutants presents the opportunity to study centaurins in a genetically tractable microorganism.A fragment of SPBC17G9.08c/cnt5+ was originally identified in a screen for high copy suppressors of a cut6 temperature-sensitive mutant that is defective in a putative acetyl-CoA carboxylase (Saitoh et al., 1996). This gene fragment was named csx2+, however, we propose to name the full-length gene cnt5+ in recognition of its relationship to mammalian centaurin proteins of Arf GAP family. Cnt5 is a member of AZAP type Arf GAPs that are characterized by the presence of PH, Arf GAP and ankyrin repeat motif (Randazzo and Hirsch, 2004; Nie and Randazzo, 2006; Inoue & Randazzo, 2007). ACAP1 (Centaurin beta1) and ACAP2 (Centaurin beta 2) have additional BAR (Bin–Amphiphysin–Rvs) domain (Randazzo and Hirsch, 2004). Cnt5 does not have ortholog in budding yeast. Cnt5 has characteristic PH (pleckstrin homology) and Arf GAP (ADP ribosylation factor GTPase activating protein) domains (Fig. 2B) but lacks additional domains characteristic of centaurin proteins and other member of AZAP class of Arf GAP family proteins. The PH domain is the 11th most abundant protein domain in human genome and is a small protein module of 120 amino acids found in many proteins involved in cell signaling, cytoskeleton rearrangement and other processes (Lemmon & Ferguson, 2000). The Arf GAP proteins are named after their ability to hydrolyze GTP bound to Arf. This activity regulates Arf (ADP ribosylation factor) a family of GTP binding proteins that function as regulator of membrane traffic and actin remodeling (Inoue & Randazzo, 2007). Arf GAP are negative regulators of ARF activity in that they are indispensable for Arf GTPase activity, which converts Arf GTP into its inactive Arf GDP form. Arf GTP conversion to Arf GDP is associated with vesicle uncoating, a necessary event for vesicle fusion with target membrane (Yang et al., 2002). Arf GAP may also function independent of Arf, by being incorporated into transport intermediate lacking Arf, leading to speculation of a structural role in vesicle coat formation (Yang et al., 2002). However, Arf GAPs have multiple domains that can function independent of Arf proteins to elicit structural change and to transduce signals in cell. The presence of these two domains in Cnt5 might be required for execution of one role although the possibility of two independent functions also exists.PH domains have been suggested to bind many proteins but to date the only clearly demonstrated physiological function is to bind membrane phospholipids. In a protein lipid overlay assay for phospholipid binding, full-length Cnt5 protein and its Arf GAP domain strongly bound to phosphatidic acid (PA) and phosphatidyl serine (PS) (Fig. 5D). The arf GAP AGAP1 has been shown to bind PA (Nie et al., 2002). PA and PS are negatively charged abundant phospholipids with established structural roles in cellular membranes (Stace & Ktistakis, 2006). They are also implicated in signaling pathways. PA influences membrane curvature, it acts as signaling lipid, it recruits cytosolic proteins to appropriate positions and it is a precursor for the synthesis of other lipids. PA may be involved in vesicle formation by promoting membrane curvature and by recruiting the proteins to carry out much more energetically unfavorable task of neck formation and pinching (Delon et al., 2004). The full-length Cnt5 protein and Arf GAP domain weakly bound to PI (3,4) P2 (phosphatidyl inositol 3,4 bis phosphate) (Fig. 5D). PI (3,4) P2 is a minor phospholipid component of cell membranes and an important second messenger. The generation of PI (3,4) P2 at plasma membrane activates a number of important cell signaling pathways. The binding of Cnt5 to PI (3,4) might lead to activation of signaling pathways. Curiously, the PH domain alone did not bind to any lipid (Fig. 5D). It is possible that PH domain of Cnt5 alone does not acquire the correct confirmation required for phospholipid binding but it may influence phospholipid binding in the context of full- length Cnt5. The PH domain in number of proteins binds to PtdIns (3,4) P2 including PKB. The generation of PtdIns (3,4) P2 at the plasma membrane upon the activation of class I PI 3-kinases and SHIP phosphatases causes these proteins to translocate at the plasma membrane and accordingly affects their activity (Dowler et al., 2000). In ASAP1, GAP activity is stimulated about 10,000-fold by PI (4,5) P2 and phosphatidic acid (PA)(Brown et al., 1998, Kam et al., 2000). ACAP1 and ACAP2 are also activated by PI (4,5) P2 and PI (3,5) P2 (Jackson et al., 2000). This stimulation is dependent on conformational change brought about by phospholipid binding (Brown et al., 1998, Kam et al., 2000). Arf GAP also directly interacts with lipids to affect the membrane structure (Nie & Randazzo, 2006). The binding of Cnt5 to different phospholipids might regulate its putative GTPase activity. It is also possible that phospholipid binding might influence membrane association of Cnt5. Membrane association of endogenously tagged Cnt5 protein was indicated by high-speed ultracentrifugation (Fig. 6).In vivo localization was performed to gain further insight into biological role of Cnt5. Cnt5-GFP showed predominant cytosolic distribution, but upon As treatment was it accumulated near plasma membranes and also appeared to accumulate near the septum of dividing cells (Fig. 7B). The higher septum signal may simply be reflective of the close juxtaposition of two cell membranes. Membrane localization of Cnt5 upon As treatment suggested it might be playing a role in membrane organization. In mammals, ACAP4 (an Arf GAP) and Arf6 are redistributed to the plasma membrane from cytoplasm upon stimulation with epidermal growth factor. ACAP4 is an important regulator of growth factor regulated membrane cytoskeleton remodeling (Fang et al., 2006). Perhaps the redistribution of Cnt5 to membranes upon As treatment is required for activation or regulation of a component of cellular detoxification pathways of heavy metals.Spc1 is the fission yeast homologue of mammalian p38 mitogen activated protein kinase (MAPK). It plays an important role in survival after exposure to various stress agents (Shiozaki & Russell, 1995; Shiozaki & Russell, 1996; Degols et al., 1996; Kato et al., 1996; Degols & Russell, 1997; Toone et al., 1998). Spc1 is activated upon As treatment (Rodriguez-Gabriel & Russell, 2005). Centaurin α1 is required for proper activation of ERK1/2 MAPK after recruitment to plasma membrane in a PI3K- dependent manner (Hayashi et al.,2006). Since Cnt5 appears to be associated with membranes and to interact with phospholipids, we tested whether Cnt5 is required for Spc1 activation in response to arsenic treatment. However, we found that Spc1 activation was unaffected in a cnt5Δ background (data not shown), showing that Cnt5 is not an upstream activator of Spc1 signaling.S. pombe has two uncharacterized Arf proteins in comparison to 3 of S. cerevisiae and 6 of mammals (D’Souza-Schorey & Chavrier, 2006). Cnt5 is similar to centaurin beta protein of Arf GAP family that has Arf6 as a substrate in mammals. Arf6 localizes to plasma membrane and endosomal compartments and regulates endocytic membrane trafficking and actin remodeling (D’Souza-Schorey & Chavrier, 2006). In fission yeast, the mammalian Arf6 homologue is SPBC1539.08. We found that a cells lacking SPBC1539.08 are viable, not sensitive to arsenic and only slightly sensitive to Cd (data not shown). A double deletion with cnt5Δ showed sensitivity same as that of a cnt5Δ strain. So, upon heavy metal exposure Cnt5 might be acting independently of SPBC1539.08 for vesicle formation and sequestration. It has been shown that Arf GAP may function independent of Arf protein by directly interacting with coat or cargo protein for vesicle formation (Yang et al., 2002). Cnt5 might be regulating other function of Arf and functioning independent of Arf during heavy metal detoxification. Interestingly, S. cerevisiae cells lacking Gcs1 (an Arf GAP) and Arf1 are markedly impaired for growth compared to cell missing either of them and exhibit fluoride sensitivity (Poon et al., 1996).Detoxification of heavy metal ions largely occurs through mechanisms: sequestration or extrusion. Metallothionines (MTs) and phytochelatins (PCs) are known to bind heavy metals directly and are sometimes required for sequestration (Cobbett & Goldsbrough, 2002). MTs are cysteine-rich polypeptides encoded by a family of genes. In contrast, PCs are a family of enzymatically synthesized cysteine-rich peptides (Cobbett & Goldsbrough, 2002). In fission yeast, phytochelatin binds Cd ion to form a low molecular weight complex that is transported to vacuole by HMT1 (Ortiz et al., 1992; Ortiz et al., 1995). Here we have reported the discovery and initial characterization of Cnt5, a fission yeast protein involved in tolerance of arsenic and cadmium. One of the most interesting features of Cnt5 is that it is a member of the Centaurin protein family. These proteins are conserved from fission yeast to humans, but their functions are poorly understood. They have been implicated in dendritic differentiation (Moore et al., 2007), cytoskeleton reorganization (Venkateswarlu et al., 2007), and membrane trafficking (Randazzo and Hirsch, 2004; Nie and Randazzo, 2006; Inoue & Randazzo, 2007), but their molecular functions are largely unknown. The discovery of As/Cd-sensitive phenotypes associated with cnt5 mutants presents the opportunity to study centaurins in a genetically tractable microorganism.

A fragment of SPBC17G9.08c/cnt5+ was originally identified in a screen for high copy suppressors of a cut6 temperature-sensitive mutant that is defective in a putative acetyl-CoA carboxylase (Saitoh et al., 1996). This gene fragment was named csx2+, however, we propose to name the full-length gene cnt5+ in recognition of its relationship to mammalian centaurin proteins of Arf GAP family. Cnt5 is a member of AZAP type Arf GAPs that are characterized by the presence of PH, Arf GAP and ankyrin repeat motif (Randazzo and Hirsch, 2004; Nie and Randazzo, 2006; Inoue & Randazzo, 2007). ACAP1 (Centaurin beta1) and ACAP2 (Centaurin beta 2) have additional BAR (Bin–Amphiphysin–Rvs) domain (Randazzo and Hirsch, 2004). Cnt5 does not have ortholog in budding yeast. Cnt5 has characteristic PH (pleckstrin homology) and Arf GAP (ADP ribosylation factor GTPase activating protein) domains (Fig. 2B) but lacks additional domains characteristic of centaurin proteins and other member of AZAP class of Arf GAP family proteins. The PH domain is the 11th most abundant protein domain in human genome and is a small protein module of 120 amino acids found in many proteins involved in cell signaling, cytoskeleton rearrangement and other processes (Lemmon & Ferguson, 2000). The Arf GAP proteins are named after their ability to hydrolyze GTP bound to Arf. This activity regulates Arf (ADP ribosylation factor) a family of GTP binding proteins that function as regulator of membrane traffic and actin remodeling (Inoue & Randazzo, 2007). Arf GAP are negative regulators of ARF activity in that they are indispensable for Arf GTPase activity, which converts Arf GTP into its inactive Arf GDP form. Arf GTP conversion to Arf GDP is associated with vesicle uncoating, a necessary event for vesicle fusion with target membrane (Yang et al., 2002). Arf GAP may also function independent of Arf, by being incorporated into transport intermediate lacking Arf, leading to speculation of a structural role in vesicle coat formation (Yang et al., 2002). However, Arf GAPs have multiple domains that can function independent of Arf proteins to elicit structural change and to transduce signals in cell. The presence of these two domains in Cnt5 might be required for execution of one role although the possibility of two independent functions also exists.

PH domains have been suggested to bind many proteins but to date the only clearly demonstrated physiological function is to bind membrane phospholipids. In a protein lipid overlay assay for phospholipid binding, full-length Cnt5 protein and its Arf GAP domain strongly bound to phosphatidic acid (PA) and phosphatidyl serine (PS) (Fig. 5D). The arf GAP AGAP1 has been shown to bind PA (Nie et al., 2002). PA and PS are negatively charged abundant phospholipids with established structural roles in cellular membranes (Stace & Ktistakis, 2006). They are also implicated in signaling pathways. PA influences membrane curvature, it acts as signaling lipid, it recruits cytosolic proteins to appropriate positions and it is a precursor for the synthesis of other lipids. PA may be involved in vesicle formation by promoting membrane curvature and by recruiting the proteins to carry out much more energetically unfavorable task of neck formation and pinching (Delon et al., 2004). The full-length Cnt5 protein and Arf GAP domain weakly bound to PI (3,4) P2 (phosphatidyl inositol 3,4 bis phosphate) (Fig. 5D). PI (3,4) P2 is a minor phospholipid component of cell membranes and an important second messenger. The generation of PI (3,4) P2 at plasma membrane activates a number of important cell signaling pathways. The binding of Cnt5 to PI (3,4) might lead to activation of signaling pathways. Curiously, the PH domain alone did not bind to any lipid (Fig. 5D). It is possible that PH domain of Cnt5 alone does not acquire the correct confirmation required for phospholipid binding but it may influence phospholipid binding in the context of full- length Cnt5. The PH domain in number of proteins binds to PtdIns (3,4) P2 including PKB. The generation of PtdIns (3,4) P2 at the plasma membrane upon the activation of class I PI 3-kinases and SHIP phosphatases causes these proteins to translocate at the plasma membrane and accordingly affects their activity (Dowler et al., 2000). In ASAP1, GAP activity is stimulated about 10,000-fold by PI (4,5) P2 and phosphatidic acid (PA)(Brown et al., 1998, Kam et al., 2000). ACAP1 and ACAP2 are also activated by PI (4,5) P2 and PI (3,5) P2 (Jackson et al., 2000). This stimulation is dependent on conformational change brought about by phospholipid binding (Brown et al., 1998, Kam et al., 2000). Arf GAP also directly interacts with lipids to affect the membrane structure (Nie & Randazzo, 2006). The binding of Cnt5 to different phospholipids might regulate its putative GTPase activity. It is also possible that phospholipid binding might influence membrane association of Cnt5. Membrane association of endogenously tagged Cnt5 protein was indicated by high-speed ultracentrifugation (Fig. 6).

In vivo localization was performed to gain further insight into biological role of Cnt5. Cnt5-GFP showed predominant cytosolic distribution, but upon As treatment was it accumulated near plasma membranes and also appeared to accumulate near the septum of dividing cells (Fig. 7B). The higher septum signal may simply be reflective of the close juxtaposition of two cell membranes. Membrane localization of Cnt5 upon As treatment suggested it might be playing a role in membrane organization. In mammals, ACAP4 (an Arf GAP) and Arf6 are redistributed to the plasma membrane from cytoplasm upon stimulation with epidermal growth factor. ACAP4 is an important regulator of growth factor regulated membrane cytoskeleton remodeling (Fang et al., 2006). Perhaps the redistribution of Cnt5 to membranes upon As treatment is required for activation or regulation of a component of cellular detoxification pathways of heavy metals.

Spc1 is the fission yeast homologue of mammalian p38 mitogen activated protein kinase (MAPK). It plays an important role in survival after exposure to various stress agents (Shiozaki & Russell, 1995; Shiozaki & Russell, 1996; Degols et al., 1996; Kato et al., 1996; Degols & Russell, 1997; Toone et al., 1998). Spc1 is activated upon As treatment (Rodriguez-Gabriel & Russell, 2005). Centaurin α1 is required for proper activation of ERK1/2 MAPK after recruitment to plasma membrane in a PI3K- dependent manner (Hayashi et al.,2006). Since Cnt5 appears to be associated with membranes and to interact with phospholipids, we tested whether Cnt5 is required for Spc1 activation in response to arsenic treatment. However, we found that Spc1 activation was unaffected in a cnt5Δ background (data not shown), showing that Cnt5 is not an upstream activator of Spc1 signaling.

S. pombe has two uncharacterized Arf proteins in comparison to 3 of S. cerevisiae and 6 of mammals (D’Souza-Schorey & Chavrier, 2006). Cnt5 is similar to centaurin beta protein of Arf GAP family that has Arf6 as a substrate in mammals. Arf6 localizes to plasma membrane and endosomal compartments and regulates endocytic membrane trafficking and actin remodeling (D’Souza-Schorey & Chavrier, 2006). In fission yeast, the mammalian Arf6 homologue is SPBC1539.08. We found that a cells lacking SPBC1539.08 are viable, not sensitive to arsenic and only slightly sensitive to Cd (data not shown). A double deletion with cnt5Δ showed sensitivity same as that of a cnt5Δ strain. So, upon heavy metal exposure Cnt5 might be acting independently of SPBC1539.08 for vesicle formation and sequestration. It has been shown that Arf GAP may function independent of Arf protein by directly interacting with coat or cargo protein for vesicle formation (Yang et al., 2002). Cnt5 might be regulating other function of Arf and functioning independent of Arf during heavy metal detoxification. Interestingly, S. cerevisiae cells lacking Gcs1 (an Arf GAP) and Arf1 are markedly impaired for growth compared to cell missing either of them and exhibit fluoride sensitivity (Poon et al., 1996).

Detoxification of heavy metal ions largely occurs through mechanisms: sequestration or extrusion. Metallothionines (MTs) and phytochelatins (PCs) are known to bind heavy metals directly and are sometimes required for sequestration (Cobbett & Goldsbrough, 2002). MTs are cysteine-rich polypeptides encoded by a family of genes. In contrast, PCs are a family of enzymatically synthesized cysteine-rich peptides (Cobbett & Goldsbrough, 2002). In fission yeast, phytochelatin binds Cd ion to form a low molecular weight complex that is transported to vacuole by HMT1 (Ortiz et al., 1992; Ortiz et al., 1995). HMT2, a mitochondrial sulfide:quinone oxidoreductase, found in humans and fission yeast but not in budding yeast, is essential in cadmium tolerance, and hmt2Δ cells are hypersensitive to cadmium (Vande Weghe and Ow, 1999Vande Weghe and Ow, 2001). Hmt2 transfers electrons from sulfide (S2−) to ubiquinone (Coenzyme Q, or CoQ). In E. coli, arsenite is transported out of cells with the help of ArsAB pump whereas in budding yeast there are two arsenite transporters, Acr3p in the plasma membrane and Ycf1p in the vacuole (Liu et al., 2002).

In S. cerevisiae, excess manganese is pumped into golgi and ultimately excreted from the cell via secretory pathway vesicles that carry the metal to the cell surface (Luk et al., 2003). We speculate that Cnt5 might be involved in trafficking of As/Cd in the similar manner because it may act upon some of its effectors at Golgi membranes and may be the component of cargo or coat proteins that help in vesicle mediated secretion. Cnt5 might be detoxifying As and Cd by sequestering them inside membrane bound organelle by being the part of transport intermediate as a part of its Arf independent function as in the case of ArfGAP1 which promotes vesicle formation by functioning as a component of COPI coat (Yang et al., 2002). In the context of our findings it is interesting that a partial clone of cnt5+ was previously discovered as csx2+, a high copy suppressor of a cut6 temperature sensitive mutation. Cut6 protein is a putative acetyl-CoA carboxylase required for fatty acid biosynthesis. Since fatty acids are required for formation of the phospholipid bilayer in the plasma membrane, it is tempting to speculate that modulation of Cut6 function may be in someway linked to the role of Cnt5 in As/Cd resistance. Future studies will address this question.

We recently reported the outcome of a genome-wide screen of genes involved in cadmium tolerance in S. pombe (Kennedy et al., 2008). This screen was carried out with 2649 mutants each deleted for a different non-essential gene. Reassuringly, included in the list of 237 genes required for full resistance to Cd were spc1+ and cnt5+. Interestingly, among the many new genes identified in this screen was SPAC19G12.08, which is predicted to encode a fatty acid hydroxylase. Also found were SPBC342.03, which is predicted to encode a glucanosyltransferase that is a GPI anchored protein likely involved in cell wall biosynthesis, and several other genes involved in cell wall biosynthesis and cell morphology (Kennedy et al., 2008). These and other data underscore the potential importance of membrane integrity and trafficking across membranes in maintaining cellular resistance to Cd.

In summary, our studies establish the precedence for centaurin like proteins being involved in heavy metal detoxification, and we anticipate that further studies of Cnt5 will illuminate the function of this poorly understood class of proteins in other organisms.
