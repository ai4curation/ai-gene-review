Cardiolipin (CL) is the signature phospholipid of the inner mitochondrial 
membrane, where it stabilizes electron transport chain protein complexes1. The 
final step in CL biosynthesis relates to its remodelling: the exchange of 
nascent acyl chains with longer, unsaturated chains1. However, the enzyme 
responsible for cleaving nascent CL (nCL) has remained elusive. Here, we 
describe ABHD18 as a candidate deacylase in the CL biosynthesis pathway. 
Accordingly, ABHD18 converts CL into monolysocardiolipin (MLCL) in vitro, and 
its inactivation in cells and mice results in a shift to nCL in serum and 
tissues. Notably, ABHD18 deactivation rescues the mitochondrial defects in cells 
and the morbidity and mortality in mice associated with Barth syndrome. This 
rare genetic disease is characterized by the build-up of MLCL resulting from 
inactivating mutations in TAFAZZIN (TAZ), which encodes the final enzyme in the 
CL-remodelling cascade1. We also identified a selective, covalent, 
small-molecule inhibitor of ABHD18 that rescues TAZ mutant phenotypes in 
fibroblasts from human patients and in fish embryos. This study highlights a 
striking example of genetic suppression of a monogenic disease revealing a 
canonical enzyme in the CL biosynthesis pathway.
