## Abstract

Parks LW(1), Casey WM.

Fungi are among the most primitive organisms that synthesize sterols. The fungal
sterol, ergosterol, is similar to animal sterol, cholesterol, but with
significant structural differences. The genetics and biochemistry for most of
the steps in sterol biosynthesis have been studied in the yeast, Saccharomyces
cerevisiae. Yet, little is known of the precise physiological roles that sterols
play in the cell. Work with strains that are auxotrophic for ergosterol has led
to the prediction of at least four growth-dependent functions for sterols. Most
of the antifungal compounds in medical and agricultural use affect some aspect
of sterol synthesis or function. Extensive studies on the modes of action of
those substances and research on the effects of altering sterol metabolism by
sterol mutants are providing new insights into sterol functions in the cells. In
addition, questioning why fungi require ergosterol rather than the simpler
cholesterol provides heuristic impetus for further experimentation.
