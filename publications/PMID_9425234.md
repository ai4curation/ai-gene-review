# A mutation in guanylate cyclase activator 1A (GUCA1A) in an autosomal dominant cone dystrophy pedigree mapping to a new locus on chromosome 6p21.1.
**Authors:** Payne AM, Downes SM, Bessant DA, Taylor R, Holder GE, Warren MJ, Bird AC, Bhattacharya SS
**Journal:** Hum Mol Genet (1998)
**DOI:** [10.1093/hmg/7.2.273](https://doi.org/10.1093/hmg/7.2.273)

## Abstract

1. Hum Mol Genet. 1998 Feb;7(2):273-7. doi: 10.1093/hmg/7.2.273.

A mutation in guanylate cyclase activator 1A (GUCA1A) in an autosomal dominant
cone dystrophy pedigree mapping to a new locus on chromosome 6p21.1.

Payne AM(1), Downes SM, Bessant DA, Taylor R, Holder GE, Warren MJ, Bird AC,
Bhattacharya SS.

Author information:
(1)Institute of Ophthalmology, Department of Molecular Genetics, 11-43 Bath
Street, London EC1V 9EL, UK.

We report a mutation (Y99C) in guanylate cyclase activator 1A (GUCA1A), the gene
for guanylate cyclase activating protein (GCAP1), in a family with autosomal
dominant cone dystrophy. Linkage analysis excluded all the known cone and
cone-rod dystrophy loci, except the chromosome 6p21.1 region. This is known to
contain the RDS gene, which is associated with dominant cone-rod dystrophy.
Screening of the RDS gene by heteroduplex analysis and direct sequencing failed
to demonstrate sequence changes in the coding region of this gene. The gene for
GCAP1, a calcium binding protein which is highly expressed in photoreceptor
outer segments, is also located in 6p21.1. It was screened for mutations, and
all affected individuals showed a single base pair missense mutation (A-->G) at
codon 99 in exon 2 of this gene generating a tyrosine-to-cysteine change in the
GCAP1 protein. This change was absent from 206 unrelated normal controls. We
propose that this change would at least disrupt the EF3handof GCAP1 thereby
preventing calcium binding and consequently interfere with activation. The
resulting effect on cGMP production would predictably modify the number of open
cGMP gated cation channels, and could explain the ultimate demise of cone
photoreceptor cells.
