Alpha Thalassemia/Mental developmental retardation, X-linked (ATRX) is an
important heterochromatin regulator, frequent mutated in ATR-X syndrome and
various cancers. ATRX binds a histone variant macroH2A, forming a functional
axis crucial for transcription regulation and genome stability. However, the
molecular mechanism underlying the ATRX-macroH2A interaction remains obscure.
Here we demonstrate that the ADD domain of ATRX (ATRX ADD) specifically binds
the histone-fold domain of macroH2A, but not the canonical H2A. The binding
specificity is mediated by a D/E-rich loop of ATRX ADD and the L 12 loop of
macroH2A. A swapping mutation in the L 12 loop of macroH2A disrupts ATRX
binding, whereas the reverse mutation in H2A confers binding capacity with ATRX.
Notably, ATRX ADD employs a conserved interface to recognize both macroH2A and
H3, leading to competition between macroH2A and H3 for ATRX binding.
Furthermore, affinity purification and mass spectrometry identify NuRD
components as the potential ATRX ADD-associating proteins, with CDH4 mimicking
H3 in its direct interaction with ATRX ADD. These findings elucidate the
molecular basis of ATRX's interaction with macroH2A and NuRD, and also
demonstrate the versatility of ATRX ADD in recognizing diverse chromatin
regulators, providing insights into ATRX's multifaceted roles in epigenetic
regulation and pathogenesis.

[1]. ATRX plays central roles in transcription regulation, telomere maintenance, heterochromatin organization, and the DNA damage response
[2]. In mouse models, complete
ATRX knockout results in early embryonic lethality
[3], while its conditional deletion in the central nervous system causes postnatal death [
4,
5] . Mutations in ATRX are the primary cause of ATR-X syndrome [
6,
7] and are frequently observed in various cancers, particularly pediatric gliomas and pancreatic neuroendocrine tumors
[8].
Disease-causing mutations in ATRX are frequently clustered in two structural domains with distinct functions: the ATPase domain and the ADD (ATRX-DNMT3-DNMT3L) domain [
4,
9] . The ATPase domain, a SNF2 (Sucrose Non-Fermenting)-type chromatin remodeling domain, exhibits an ATP hydrolysis-dependent DNA translocase activity [
10,
11] . By leveraging this translocase activity, the ATRX complex can mobilize nucleosomes, facilitating chromatin remodeling and the transcription of specific genes, especially the α-globin gene [
11,
12] . Additionally, the remodeling activity of ATRX resolves G-quadruplex structures, preventing replication fork stalling and maintaining genome stability [
13‒
15] .
The ADD domain of ATRX (ATRX
ADD) is a specialized zinc finger domain that recognizes histone modifications. ATRX
ADD specifically binds a dual marker consisting of unmodified lysine 4 (H3K4me0) and trimethylated lysine 9 (H3K9me3) on histone H3 [
16‒
18] . This selective interaction enables ATRX to localize to heterochromatin regions
[18]. To date, histone H3 remains the only well-characterized binding partner of ATRX
ADD. The limited interaction spectrum of ATRX
ADD stands in sharp contrast to its vital role in heterochromatin organization and its frequent mutations in ATR-X syndrome. Whether ATRX
ADD engages with other proteins beyond histone H3 to fulfil additional regulatory roles remains an open question.
Histone variant incorporation adds significant complexity to epigenetic regulation [
19‒
21] . Two histone variants are closely associated with ATRX: the H3 variant H3.3 and the H2A variant macroH2A. ATRX, together with the H3.3-specific histone chaperone DAXX, deposits H3.3 into heterochromatin regions, including telomeres, pericentromeres, and other repetitive sites [
22,
23] . The interplay between ATRX and H3.3 regulates gene expression, promotes heterochromatin assembly, and maintains genome stability [
12,
22‒
24] .
MacroH2A (abbreviated as mH2A) is a specialized H2A variant characterized by its unique macro domain and is generally regarded as a repressive variant enriched in heterochromatin
[25]. ATRX co-localizes with mH2A at sites of inactive X chromatin [
26,
27] and directly binds mH2A through its N-terminal region (ATRX
1-841)
[28]. Studies have highlighted the crucial roles of the ATRX-mH2A interaction, including promoting α-globin gene expression by removing mH2A from subtelomeric regions
[28]. ATRX also inhibits alternative lengthening of telomeres (ALT) pathway by depositing mH2A to regulate sister telomere cohesion [
29,
30] and alleviate replication stress at telomeres
[31]. Although ATRX and mH2A form a functional axis essential for maintaining genome stability, the specific mechanism by which ATRX recognizes mH2A remains elusive.
This study aims to elucidate molecular mechanism underlying the interaction between ATRX and mH2A. We identified the ADD domain of ATRX and the histone-fold domain of mH2A as the minimum interaction regions. Structural prediction and mutagenesis studies revealed that a D/E rich loop of ATRX
ADD binds mH2A-H2B heterodimer primarily through electrostatic interactions. Sequence variations in the L
12 loop of mH2A determined its binding specificity to ATRX, explaining why the canonical H2A cannot bind ATRX. Notably, the discovered interface of ATRX
ADD-mH2A overlaps significantly with the previously reported ATRX
ADD-H3 interface, leading to competitive binding between mH2A and H3 for ATRX. Moreover, affinity purification and mass spectrometry (AP-MS) identified a spectrum of chromatin regulators as potential new binding partners of ATRX
ADD, with notable enrichment of components of the Nucleosome Remodeling and Deacetylase (NuRD) complex. We further proved that ATRX
ADD recognized a short motif of CHD4, a core NuRD subunit. These findings provide new insights into the binding capabilities of ATRX
ADD in recognizing multiple chromatin regulators, highlighting its multifaceted roles in chromatin remodeling and transcription regulation.

Since mH2A1 and H3 compete for the same ATRX-binding interface, designed ATRX mutations are expected to disrupt ATRX binding to both mH2A1 and H3. As anticipated, the ATRX 6D/E mutation, which abolished mH2A1 binding (
Figure 2D), also eliminated the ability to recognize H3K9me3 (
Figure 4C). Previous studies identified some ATRX mutations with compromised H3K9me3-binding capacity, including Y203A, H189N, and E252L
[18]. We confirmed that these ATRX mutations decreased the interaction with both H3K9me3 (
Figure 4C) and mH2A1 (
Figure 4D) to a similar extent. For example, ATRX H189N and E252L mutations caused greater reductions in H3K9me3 binding compared to the Y203A mutant (
Figure 4C), and correspondingly, these two ATRX mutations (H189N and E252L) also resulted in weaker mH2A1 binding than Y203A (
Figure 4D). These findings indicate that ATRX
ADD possesses a conserved binding interface capable of recognizing multiple partners, including H3 and mH2A.

Identification of new ATRX
ADD-binding proteins
Next, we sought to determine whether ATRX
ADD has additional binding partners beyond histones. To identify potential ATRX
ADD-binding proteins, we overexpressed a Flag-tagged ADD-containing ATRX fragment (ATRX
1-321) in 293T cells, purified Flag-ATRX
1-321 from cell lysates, and performed intensity-based label-free quantitative LC-MS/MS analyses to identify ATRX interactors. Multiple replicates were conducted, with an empty Flag vector serving as a negative control. Data from three replicates were combined and averaged. MS analyses yielded a plethora of putative ATRX
1-321-binding proteins, including many known chromatin regulators (
Figure 5A). Consistent with expectations, mH2A1 and H2B were exclusively identified in Flag-ATRX
1-321 samples, reinforcing the specificity of the ATRX-mH2A1-H2B interaction (
Figure 5A). Notably, the most enriched chromatin regulator was the Nucleosome Remodeling and Deacetylase (NuRD) complex. All NuRD subunits, including nearly all paralogs, were either exclusively identified in Flag-ATRX
1-321 samples (fold change: infinity) (
Figure 5A) or significantly enriched in Flag-ATRX
1-321 samples (fold change > 5) (
Figure 5B).

DiscussionDespite the recognized importance of ATRX and mH2A in transcription regulation, suppression of the ALT pathway, and resolution of telomere cohesion [
28,
29,
31] , the precise mechanism underlying ATRX-mH2A interactions remain incompletely understood. In this study, we dissected the biochemical and structural basis of ATRX-mH2A binding. Our findings confirm that ATRX specifically binds mH2A, but not canonical H2A. This specific interaction is mediated by a D/E rich loop of ATRX
ADD and the L
12 loop of mH2A1. Interestingly, the L
12 loops in H2A variants show significant sequence variation (
Figure 3B), and their distinct structural configurations further influence ATRX binding (
Figure 3E). The unique sequences of the L
12 loop in mH2A1 determines ATRX’s binding specificity. Substituting four residues in canonical H2A conferred strong binding affinity for ATRX, whereas the reverse mutation in mH2A1 disrupted ATRX binding (
Figure 3F). This biochemical binding specificity provides a plausible explanation for the functional interplay between ATRX and mH2A, and offers a mutation toolset for probing how the ATRX-mH2A axis regulates gene expression and genome integrity [
28,
29,
31] .
Although previous studies have revealed that ATRX associates with a spectrum of chromatin regulators, including DAXX [
36‒
38] , MRN
[39], HP1
[17], MECP2
[40], EZH2
[27], and SLF2
[41], none of these interaction are mediated by the ADD domain of ATRX. Current knowledge of ATRX
ADD has been largely limited to its recognition of H3K9me3, in sharp contrast to the ADD domain of DNMT3A (DNMT3A
ADD). DNMT3A
ADD exhibits broad interaction capabilities with multiple protein partners such as SUV39H1
[42], SETDB1
[43], EZH2
[44], MECP2
[45], HP1β
[42], Myc
[46], PU.1
[47], and RP58
[48]. Our results resolve this apparent contradiction and demonstrate that the ADD domain of ATRX is also a versatile protein-protein binding module, capable of interacting with at least two histones (H3 and mH2A) and CHD4. Additional ATRX
ADD-binding partners are likely to be identified in the future, suggesting a central hub role for ATRX in recruiting diverse partners to orchestrate heterochromatin regulation. Furthermore, ATRX
ADD-mediated interactions are sensitive to post-translational modification, as evidenced by H3K9 trimethylation enhancing H3’s binding to ATRX
ADD. Whether other binding partners, such as mH2A and CHD4, undergo specific modifications to modulate their interaction with ATRX
ADD remains an intriguing question for future investigation.
The ADD domain of ATRX is a hotspot for pathogenic mutations associated with ATR-X syndrome
[9], and the ATRX
ADD-mutation knock-in mice recapitulate several aspects of the disorders observed in ATR-X patients
[7]. Previous studies have suggested that ATRX
ADD disease mutations primarily disrupt its ability to bind H3K9me3 and mediate H3K9me3-dependent heterochromatin localization, implicating these defects as a major pathological mechanism by which impaired heterochromatin recruitment contributes to the etiology of ATR-X syndrome
[18]. However, it bears emphasis that ATRX
ADD employs a conserved interface to bind different partners. H3, mH2A, and CHD4
ABM all are recognized by the same binding pocket of ATRX
ADD. Consequently, the same subset of ATRX mutations affects not only H3 and mH2A binding (
Figure 4C,D) but may also impair other as-yet unidentified partners. The competitive binding mode implies that a single disease mutation in ATRX
ADD could disrupt multiple interactions and interfere with several pathways. Therefore, the pathogenic mechanism of ATR-X syndrome mutations within ATRX
ADD is more intricate than previously anticipated. To fully unravel these pathogenic mechanisms, it is essential to re-assess the outcomes of ATRX
ADD mutations beyond their effects on H3K9me3-dependent heterochromatin recruitment. Future studies should systematically examine individual mutation sites to identify disrupted protein-interaction networks, which is critical for elucidating precise pathogenic mechanism underlying ATRX
ADD mutations.
Since NuRD is a critical epigenetic regulator
[49], the discovery of its association with ATRX in this study expands the repertoire of ATRX-interacting proteins and offers new insights into ATRX’s functions. Notably, some NuRD components, including GATAD2A/B, MTA1/2, MBD3, were also captured by a proximity labeling strategy
[41], further supporting ATRX as a bona fide interactor of NuRD. The identification of CHD4 as a direct binding partner for ATRX
ADD, with structural and sequence mimicry to H3, establishes the first link between ATRX and NuRD. It is also plausible that ATRX interacts with other NuRD components in addition to CHD4. Further research is highly desired to validate these interactions and investigate the functional significance of ATRX-NuRD crosstalk in orchestrating chromatin remodeling and epigenetic regulation. Understanding these interactions could uncover additional regulatory mechanisms underlying heterochromatin maintenance and gene expression, as well as potential implications for diseases associated with ATRX or NuRD dysregulation.
