# The peroxiredoxin gene family in Drosophila melanogaster.
**Authors:** Radyuk SN, Klichko VI, Spinola B, Sohal RS, Orr WC
**Journal:** Free Radic Biol Med (2001)

The peroxiredoxin gene family in Drosophila melanogaster.

Radyuk SN(1), Klichko VI, Spinola B, Sohal RS, Orr WC.

Author information:
(1)Department of Biological Sciences, Southern Methodist University, Dallas, TX
75275, USA.

Five peroxiredoxin genes have been identified in Drosophila melanogaster on the
basis of a genome-wide search. Three of the genes (DPx-4156, DPx-4783, and
DPx-5037) fall into the 2-Cys subgroup, while the other two (DPx-2540 and
DPx-6005) belong to the 1-Cys subgroup. Using cDNAs, all five were expressed in
E. coli and the purified recombinant proteins were shown to reduce H(2)O(2) in
the presence of dithiothreitol. The three 2-Cys Prx were also shown to be active
in the thioredoxin system and were, consequently, classified as thioredoxin
peroxidases. Antisera raised against the DPx-4783 recombinant protein
crossreacted with all family members and recognized protein species of the
predicted sizes (22-27 kD). All five family members, when individually
overexpressed in Drosophila S2 cells, conferred some resistance to H(2)O(2)
treatment, as measured by cell viability. Functional diversification of the
Drosophila peroxiredoxin family members was suggested by two lines of evidence:
(i) the patterns of mRNA accumulation varied for the different genes during
development and (ii) recombinant proteins fused to an epitope tag and
overexpressed in Drosophila cells, differed in subcellular localizations--three
proteins occurred in the cytosol, one was localized to the mitochondria, and one
was found to be secreted.
