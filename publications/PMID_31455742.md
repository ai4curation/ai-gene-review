## Full Text

Significance

Lambdoid bacteriophage Q proteins are textbook examples of regulators of gene expression that function at the level of transcription antitermination. Here we report structures defining the mechanism of antitermination by Q from bacteriophage 21 (Q21). The results reveal that Q21 forms a torus, a “nozzle,” that extends and narrows the RNA polymerase RNA-exit channel, that nascent RNA is threaded through the Q21 nozzle, and that threading of nascent RNA through the Q21 nozzle precludes the formation of pause and terminator RNA hairpins. Threading RNA through the Q21 nozzle generates a topological connection, an unbreakable linkage, between Q21 and RNA. This enables exceptionally stable association and exceptionally processive antitermination activity and has implications for engineering highly efficient, tightly regulated, gene expression.

Keywords: transcription antitermination, transcription antitermination factor Q, transcription antitermination factor Q21, transcription elongation complex, RNA polymerase

Abstract

Lambdoid bacteriophage Q protein mediates the switch from middle to late bacteriophage gene expression by enabling RNA polymerase (RNAP) to read through transcription terminators preceding bacteriophage late genes. Q loads onto RNAP engaged in promoter-proximal pausing at a Q binding element (QBE) and adjacent sigma-dependent pause element (SDPE) to yield a Q-loading complex, and Q subsequently translocates with RNAP as a pausing-deficient, termination-deficient Q-loaded complex. Here, we report high-resolution structures of 4 states on the pathway of antitermination by Q from bacteriophage 21 (Q21): Q21, the Q21-QBE complex, the Q21-loading complex, and the Q21-loaded complex. The results show that Q21 forms a torus, a “nozzle,” that narrows and extends the RNAP RNA-exit channel, extruding topologically linked single-stranded RNA and preventing the formation of pause and terminator hairpins.

Lambdoid bacteriophage Q protein regulates gene expression through transcription antitermination, a mechanism of regulation that is widely used in bacteria and bacteriophage, but one that has been poorly understood ( 1 – 10 ; reviewed in refs. 11 – 13 ).

Q mediates the temporal switch from middle to late bacteriophage gene expression by enabling RNA polymerase (RNAP) to read through a transcription terminator preceding bacteriophage late genes ( Fig. 1 A ; refs. 1 – 13 ). The Q-dependent gene regulatory cassette consists of the gene for Q followed by a transcription unit comprising a promoter (PR′), a promoter-proximal σ-dependent pause element (SDPE; a sequence resembling a promoter −10 element, at which σR2, the σ-factor module that recognizes promoter −10 elements, re-establishes sequence-specific protein–DNA interactions), and a terminator, followed by bacteriophage late genes. In the absence of Q, RNAP initiating transcription at the PR′ promoter pauses at the SDPE and terminates at the terminator, and late genes are not expressed; in the presence of Q, RNAP initiating at the PR′ promoter more rapidly escapes the SDPE and reads through the terminator, and late genes are expressed.

Fig. 1.

Q is targeted to the PR′ transcriptional unit through a multistep process entailing 1) formation of a Q-loading complex comprising a Q protein bound to a Q binding element (QBE) and a σ-containing transcription elongation complex (TEC) paused at the SDPE, and 2) transformation into a Q-loaded complex comprising a Q-containing TEC that translocates processively, ignores pause elements, and ignores terminators ( Fig. 1 B ; refs. 3 – 13 ).

Here, we report a set of 4 structures that define the structural basis of antitermination by Q from lambdoid bacteriophage Q21 (Q21; refs. 11 , 14 , and 15 ); i.e., a crystal structure of Q21, a crystal structure of the Q21-QBE complex, a cryo-EM structure of the Q21-loading complex, and a cryo-EM structure of the Q21-loaded complex.

Results and Discussion

We solved a crystal structure of Q21 at 1.9-Å resolution by use of single-wavelength anomalous dispersion ( Fig. 2 and SI Appendix , Table S1 ). The structure reveals that the fold of Q21 is related to the fold of the σ-factor module that recognizes promoter −35 elements, σR4, and is particularly related to the fold of σR4 from extracytoplasmic-function (ECF) σ factors ( Fig. 2 C ) and of σR4 from ECF σ factors bound to anti-σ factors ( Fig. 2 D ; DALI Z scores ≥6.0; refs. 16 and 17 ). σR4 binds DNA through a helix-turn-helix motif (HTH; red in Fig. 2 C and D ; refs. 16 and 17 ). The structural alignment of Q21 and σR4 indicates that Q21 contains an HTH-related, but novel, helix-turn[loop]-helix motif (HT[loop]H; Fig. 2 C and D ), suggesting that Q21 binds DNA through an HTH-related, but novel, HT[loop]H.

Fig. 2.

We solved a crystal structure of the Q21-QBE complex at 2.8-Å resolution by use of molecular replacement ( Fig. 3 and SI Appendix , Fig. S1 and Table S1 ). The structure showed 2 distinct molecular assemblies in the asymmetric unit ( Fig. 3 A and SI Appendix , Fig. S1 A ). The first assembly comprised 2 Q21 protomers (Q-upstream and Q-downstream, Qu and Qd) bound to a direct repeat of two 8-bp subsites (QBE-upstream and QBE-downstream, QBEu and QBEd; Fig. 3 A , Left and SI Appendix , Fig. S1 A , top). The second assembly comprised a single Q21 protomer (Qd) bound to a single 8-bp subsite (QBEd; Fig. 3 A , Right and SI Appendix , Fig. S1 A , bottom). The observed interactions between Q21 and DNA are identical for Qu-QBEu and Qd-QBEd. In each case, the second α-helix of the Q21 HT[loop]H interacts with the DNA major groove, making direct interactions with DNA base edges ( Fig. 3 B and C ), and the loop of the Q21 HT[loop]H interacts with the adjacent DNA minor groove, making direct interactions with DNA base edges ( Fig. 3 B and D ). In the assembly comprising 2 Q21 protomers, Qu and Qd, bound to 2 subsites, QBEu and QBEd, the loop of the HT[loop]H of Qd makes direct protein–protein interactions with Qu ( Fig. 3 E ). Fluorescence-polarization DNA binding experiments indicate that QBEu alone is a low-affinity subsite, QBEd alone is a moderate-affinity subsite, and the intact QBE, comprising both QBEu and QBEd and enabling cooperative protein–DNA interactions by Q21 protomers binding to adjacent subsites, is a high-affinity site ( SI Appendix , Fig. S1 B ).

Fig. 3.

We determined a single-particle-reconstruction cryo-EM structure of the Q21-loading complex at 3.5-Å resolution ( Fig. 4 and SI Appendix , Figs. S2 and S3 and Table S2 ). We prepared the Q21 loading complex by in vitro reconstitution from recombinant Q21, recombinant Escherichia coli RNAP core, recombinant E. coli σ 70 , and a synthetic nucleic acid scaffold containing the PR′21 QBE, a consensus version of the PR′21 SDPE, and an 11-nt RNA ( Fig. 4 A ). The DNA duplex contained a 13-bp noncomplementary region overlapping the SDPE, corresponding to the unwound transcription bubble in a TEC at the point of initial pause capture, before subsequent cycles of scrunching and backtracking ( 18 – 22 ). The structure shows 2 Q21 protomers, Qu and Qd, interacting with QBEu and QBEd in a manner matching that in the crystal structure of Q21-QBE ( Fig. 3 A , Left ) and simultaneously interacting with a σ-containing TEC ( Fig. 4 B and SI Appendix , Fig. S3 A and B ). σR2 and σR3 make interactions with RNAP and DNA equivalent to those in a transcription initiation complex. In contrast, σR4 and the σR3-R4 linker are disengaged from their positions in a transcription initiation complex—where σR4 interacts with the RNAP dock (β' residues 369 to 420; ref. 16 ) and RNAP flap-tip helix (FTH; β residues 897 to 907; ref. 16 ), and where the σR3-R4 linker occupies the RNAP RNA-exit channel ( 16 )—and are disordered ( Fig. 4 B and SI Appendix , Fig. S3 A and B ), consistent with the expectation that interactions of σR4 and the σR3-R4 linker with RNAP are weakened or disrupted upon synthesis of a >10-nt RNA product, entry of the RNA 5′-end into the RNAP RNA-exit channel, and promoter escape ( Fig. 1 B , line 3; ref. 16 ). Clear, traceable density is present for the entire 11-nt RNA: the RNA 5′-end nucleotide and 1 additional RNA nucleotide (positions −11 and −10) are in the RNAP RNA-exit channel, and the other 9 RNA nucleotides (position −9 through −1) are base-paired to the transcription bubble template DNA strand as an RNA–DNA hybrid ( Fig. 4 B and G – H ).

Fig. 4.

Both Qu and Qd interact with RNAP ( Fig. 4 B – D ). Qu makes 3 interactions with RNAP: a “Q torus” comprising the H1-H2 linker, H2, and the H2-H3 linker of Qu interacts at the mouth of, and inside, the RNAP RNA-exit channel; H3 of Qu interacts with the RNAP dock in manner reminiscent of that for interaction of σR4 with the RNAP dock in a transcription initiation complex ( 16 ); and 6 C-terminal residues of Qu interact with the RNAP α I /β-interface ( Fig. 4 C ). The total surface area buried on Qu-RNAP interaction is 4,657 A 2 , indicative of a high-affinity interaction ( 23 ). Qd makes 1 interaction with RNAP: Qd interacts with the RNAP flap-tip helix (FTH; Fig. 4 D ). The total surface area buried upon Qd-RNAP interaction is 1,293 A 2 , indicative of a low-affinity interaction ( 23 ).

Formation of the Q21-loading complex entails remarkable large-scale conformational changes in Qu and in the RNAP FTH ( Fig. 4 E and F ). Comparison of the structures of Qu in the Q21-QBE complex and in the Q21-loading complex shows that, upon formation of the Q21-loading complex, the H1-H2 linker, H2, and the H2-H3 linker of Qu move by 19, 40, and 30 Å, respectively, to form the Q torus and to position the Q torus at the mouth of, and inside, the RNAP RNA-exit channel ( Fig. 4 E ), and 6 C-terminal residues of Qu undergo a disorder-to-order transition to engage the RNAP α I /β interface ( Fig. 4 E ). Comparison of the structures of the RNAP FTH in a transcription initiation complex and in the Q21-loading complex shows that, upon formation of the Q21-loading complex, the RNAP FTH moves by 42 Å to vacate space that will be occupied by Qu and to interact with Qd ( Fig. 4 F )

The binding of the Q torus at the mouth of, and inside, the RNAP RNA-exit channel dramatically restricts the RNAP RNA-exit channel, narrowing and extending the channel ( Fig. 4 G and H ). The Q torus has an opening with a solvent-excluded diameter of just 5 to 7 Å ( Fig. 4 G and H ). The structure of the Q21-loading complex indicates that the presence of the Q torus at, and inside, the mouth of the RNAP RNA-exit channel does not affect accommodation of an 11-nt RNA product, and model building indicates it also would not affect accommodation of 12 to 14 nt RNA products. However, model building indicates that the presence of the Q torus at, and inside, the mouth of the RNAP RNA-exit channel would necessitate >14 nt RNA products either to thread through the Q-torus opening (threading RNA nucleotides, with solvent-excluded diameter of 5 to 7 Å, through the Q-torus opening, with solvent-excluded diameter of 5 to 7 Å), or to induce a major change in the conformation or interactions of the Q torus.

We determined a single-particle reconstruction cryo-EM structure of the Q21-loaded complex at 3.8 Å ( Fig. 5 and SI Appendix , Figs. S4 and S5 and Table S2 ). To prepare a Q21-loaded complex, we performed in vitro transcription with a DNA template comprising positions −40 to −1 of PR′21 followed by a 70-bp C-less cassette containing a SDPE at the position of the PR′21 SDPE, followed by a CC-halt site ( Fig. 5 A ), using E. coli RNAP σ 70 holoenzyme, E. coli GreB (required for efficient SDPE clearance in the presence of Q21; refs. 11 and 24 ), and Q21, in the presence of an NTP subset lacking CTP. Three-dimensional classification of particles showed 3 major classes of molecular assemblies: Q21-loaded complexes (identifiable by presence of Q21 and absence of σ; 23%), TECs (identifiable by absence of Q21 and absence of σ; 21%), and transcription initiation complexes (identifiable by absence of Q21 and presence of σ; 50%; SI Appendix , Fig. S4 C ). The structure of the class comprising Q21-loaded complexes reveals 1 Q21 protomer, corresponding to Qu, interacting with a σ-free TEC, and having the RNA 5′ end threaded into and through the Q torus ( Fig. 5 B and SI Appendix , Fig. S5 ). The conformation and interactions of Q in the loaded complex are similar to those of Qu in the loading complex ( Figs. 4 B and 5 B ). In particular, the Q torus adopts a similar conformation and makes similar interactions with the RNAP RNA-exit channel, narrowing and extending the RNA-exit channel, in the loaded complex ( Figs. 4 B and 5 B ). The sole notable change is an order-to-disorder transition in the loop of the HT[loop]H motif of Q, associated with the loss of Q-QBE interactions in the loaded complex ( Figs. 4 B and 5 B ). The structure shows no density for any part of σ ( SI Appendix , Fig. S5 A and B ), consistent with the expectation that σ is released upon formation of a loaded complex ( Fig. 1 B , line 5). Clear, traceable density is present for the 16 nucleotides at the 3′ end of the 70-nt RNA product: 2 nt of RNA are located upstream of the Q torus, outside the RNAP RNA-exit channel (positions −16 and −15); 5 nt of RNA are located downstream of the Q torus, in the RNAP RNA-exit channel (positions −14 through −10); and 9 nt of RNA are base-paired with the transcription-bubble template DNA strand as an RNA-DNA hybrid (positions −9 through −1; Fig. 5 B – D and SI Appendix , Fig. S5 D ). An additional unassigned density feature upstream of the Q torus potentially is attributable to additional nucleotides of the 70-nt RNA product (probably positions −21 through −17).

Fig. 5.

The structure establishes that, upon formation of the loaded complex, RNA threads into and through the Q torus. Upon extrusion of tens of nucleotides of RNA through the Q torus, especially upon folding of the extruded RNA, rethreading of the RNA 5′ end back through the Q torus is expected to become effectively impossible, and dissociation of Q from the TEC is expected to become effectively impossible. The threading of RNA through the Q torus thus results in a topological linkage between RNA and Q, creating an effectively unbreakable linkage between the TEC and the Q torus that accounts for the ability of Q to remain stably bound to a TEC ( 9 ), resulting in processive antipausing and antitermination over tens of thousands of nucleotide-addition steps ( 11 – 13 ).

The Q torus has high net positive charge (+4, excluding sidechains that interact with RNAP), consistent with accommodation of negatively charged RNA. Protein–RNA interactions by the Q torus involve predominantly the RNA backbone, and, at the narrowest point of the Q torus, protein–RNA interactions by the Q torus involve exclusively the RNA backbone ( Fig. 5 D and SI Appendix , Fig. S5 D ). At the narrowest point of the Q torus, the sidechains of Q21 Ser29 and Thr54 are positioned to form H-bonds with a phosphate of the RNA backbone ( Fig. 5 D ), and are positioned such that rotation about the Cα-Cβ bond could allow each to maintain the H-bond with a phosphate over an ∼3 Å distance as a phosphate threads into and through the narrowest point, potentially providing a “molecular bearing” facilitating threading of RNA through the narrowest point. The presence of high net positive charge, the presence of protein–RNA interactions involving predominantly the RNA backbone, and the presence of a potential molecular bearing, provide a plausible basis for sequence-independent, sequence-nonspecific RNA threading through the Q torus during transcription elongation.

Comparison of the structures of the Q21-loading complex and the Q21-loaded complex reveals the mechanism for transformation of the loading complex to the loaded complex ( Fig. 6 A ). The structures indicate the transformation involves 5 reactions: 1) threading of the RNA 5′ end into and through the Q torus, resulting in steric clash between the RNA 5′ end and the QBE; 2) disruption of protein–DNA interactions by Qu (the Q21 protomer that makes weaker protein–DNA interactions and stronger protein–RNAP interactions); 3) disruption of protein–RNAP interactions by Qd (the Q21 protomer that makes stronger protein–DNA interactions and weaker protein–RNAP interactions); 4) disruption of σ-DNA and σ-RNAP interactions; and 5) rotation of the DNA–helix axis of upstream double-stranded DNA by ∼90° (magenta, green, cyan, brown, and gray arrows in Fig. 6 A ). The structures explain why Q21 loading requires a promoter-proximally paused TEC; namely, only a promoter-proximally paused TEC has an accessible binding site for Q21 on DNA (i.e., QBE, which becomes accessible only after promoter clearance; Fig. 1 B , lines 2 to 3), has an accessible binding site for Q21 on RNAP (i.e., RNAP dock, which becomes accessible only after promoter clearance; Fig. 1 B , lines 2 to 3), and has an RNA product short enough to thread into and through the Q torus, enabling establishment of topological linkage between the RNA product and the Q torus.

Fig. 6.

The structure of the Q21-loaded complex also reveals the mechanisms of antipausing and antitermination by Q21 ( Fig. 6 B ). Hairpin-dependent pausing involves nucleation of an RNA hairpin at the mouth of the RNAP RNA-exit channel, followed by propagation of the RNA hairpin stem and penetration of the RNA hairpin stem into the RNAP RNA-exit channel, where the RNA hairpin stem makes RNA–RNAP interactions that promote pausing ( Fig. 6 B , Top ; refs. 25 – 27 ). RNA hairpin-dependent transcription termination—the mode of termination that is most common in bacterial and bacteriophage transcription, and the mode of termination that occurs at the terminator preceding lambdoid bacteriophage late genes—involves nucleation of an RNA hairpin at the mouth of the RNA-exit channel, followed by propagation of the RNA hairpin stem and penetration of the RNA hairpin stem into the RNAP RNA-exit channel, followed by further propagation of the RNA hairpin stem that exerts mechanical forces that extract the RNA from RNAP or cause RNAP to translocate forward off the RNA ( 28 ). The structure of the Q21-loaded complex shows that Q21 is positioned to constitute a steric barrier to nucleation, propagation, and penetration of the RNA-exit channel by a pause or terminator hairpin ( Fig. 6 B ). The Q body is positioned to overlap, essentially in toto, the loop and loop-proximal stem of a pause or terminator hairpin, and thus is positioned to block nucleation of a hairpin ( Fig. 6 B ). The Q torus is positioned to overlap, in toto for the stem position in the narrowest point of the Q torus, and in part for the stem positions downstream of the narrowest point of the Q torus, 1 strand of the stem of a pause or terminator hairpin, and thus is positioned to block propagation and penetration of RNA-exit channel by a hairpin ( Fig. 6 B ). Most important, the Q torus, with dimensions that accommodate only 1 strand of RNA, constitutes an effectively absolute steric barrier to double-stranded RNA secondary structure at the mouth of, and inside, the RNAP RNA-exit channel ( Fig. 6 B ).

The structures of the Q-loading and Q-loaded complexes suggest that, in addition, Q21 may exert antipausing activity by inhibiting RNAP swiveling—an ∼3° rotation of an RNAP “swivel module,” comprising the RNAP dock, zinc binding domain (ZBD), lid, clamp, shelf, jaw, and SI3, that is thought to be associated with pausing (refs. 25 – 27 , 29 , and 30 and SI Appendix , Fig. S6 ). Model building indicates that interactions of Q21 H2 with the RNAP ZBD and RNAP lid inside the RNAP RNA-exit channel could sterically preclude both the ∼3° swiveling associated with hairpin-dependent pausing ( 25 – 27 ) and the smaller, ∼1.5°, swiveling associated with elemental pausing (ref. 25 ; referred to as “pre-swiveling” in ref. 30 ; SI Appendix , Fig. S6 A and B ). Specifically, model building indicates that swiveling would increase distance and break interactions between Q H2 and the RNAP ZBD and, simultaneously, would decrease distance and create steric clash between H2 and the RNAP lid ( SI Appendix , Fig. S6 A and B ). The steric incompatibility of Q21 with swiveling may contribute to Q21 antipausing and, in particular, may provide the mechanism to trigger the release of the Q-loading complex from the SDPE.

Taken together, the results presented here reveal that Q21 forms a torus, a molecular “nozzle,” that narrows and extends the RNAP RNA exit channel; that the RNA product is threaded through the Q nozzle; and that RNA threading through the Q nozzle prevents the formation of pausing and terminator hairpins and prevents RNAP swiveled state associated with pausing ( Fig. 6 B and SI Appendix , Fig. S6 B ).

Narrowing and extending the RNAP RNA-exit channel by attaching a molecular nozzle is a strikingly simple way to achieve antitermination—and one much simpler than the recently elucidated mechanism of the other textbook example of transcription antitermination factor, lambdoid bacteriophage N ( 31 )—and almost surely will be a generalizable mechanism.

Functional analyses and sequence analyses have identified more than 15,000 lambdoid bacteriophage Q proteins, comprising 3 protein families: the Q21/Q933W family (Pfam PF06530; 5,251 entries in NCBI), the Qλ family (Pfam PF03589, 7,904 entries in NCBI), and the Q82 family (Pfam PF06323, 2,635 entries in NCBI). Q proteins from the 3 protein families perform equivalent regulatory functions and are encoded by genes that exhibit equivalent positions in bacteriophage genomes, but surprisingly, Q proteins from the 3 protein families exhibit no significant sequence similarity to each other ( 11 , 14 , 15 , 32 ). Our crystal structure of Q21 ( Fig. 1 ) shows no significant 3D structural similarity to a previously reported crystal structure of a fragment of Qλ ( 33 ), showing that the lack of sequence similarity is accompanied by a lack of 3D structural similarity. A key priority for further research is to determine whether the functionally equivalent, but nonhomologous, Q proteins of the Qλ and Q82 protein families also function by narrowing and extending the RNAP RNA-exit channel by attaching a molecular nozzle, and if so, how they use nonhomologous sequences and 3D structures to do so. The observation that Q82 can load onto TECs containing long RNA products ( 34 ), something that should be difficult or impossible for a factor functioning as a closed molecular nozzle, suggests that Q82 may not function as a closed molecular nozzle.

Another striking aspect of the molecular nozzle mechanism of Q21 is that the mechanism results in a topological linkage of Q21 and the RNA product ( Fig. 5 ), establishing an unbreakable linkage between Q21 and the TEC that enables Q21 to provide processive antipausing and antitermination activity over tens of thousands of nucleotide-addition steps. A key priority for future research is to determine whether Q21 can function with, or can be engineered to function with, TECs from other species, enabling this exceptional topological linkage of a regulatory factor and a TEC, and the resulting exceptionally processive antipausing and antitermination activity, to be broadly exploited for regulated gene expression, synthetic biology, and metabolic engineering.

While this work was under review for publication, Shi et al. ( 35 ) reported a cryo-EM structure of a Q21 loading complex at 4.1-Å resolution. The structure of Shi et al. is consistent, in all substantive respects, with the structures and conclusions of this work.
