# Induction in the developing compound eye of Drosophila: multiple mechanisms restrict R7 induction to a single retinal precursor cell.
**Authors:** Van Vactor DL Jr, Cagan RL, Krämer H, Zipursky SL
**Journal:** Cell (1991)
**DOI:** [10.1016/0092-8674(91)90291-6](https://doi.org/10.1016/0092-8674(91)90291-6)

## Abstract

1. Cell. 1991 Dec 20;67(6):1145-55. doi: 10.1016/0092-8674(91)90291-6.

Induction in the developing compound eye of Drosophila: multiple mechanisms
restrict R7 induction to a single retinal precursor cell.

Van Vactor DL Jr(1), Cagan RL, Krämer H, Zipursky SL.

Author information:
(1)Howard Hughes Medical Institute, Department of Biological Chemistry, UCLA
School of Medicine 90024-1737.

The development of the Drosophila R7 photoreceptor cell is determined by a
specific inductive interaction between the R8 photoreceptor cell and a single
neighboring precursor cell. This process is mediated by bride of sevenless
(boss), a cell-surface bound ligand, and the sevenless (sev) tyrosine kinase
receptor. The boss ligand is expressed specifically on the surface of the R8
cell, whereas the sev receptor is expressed on 5 cells contacting the developing
R8 cell and other cells not in contact with R8. By altering the spatial and
temporal expression of boss, we demonstrate that sev-expressing cells that do
not contact R8 can assume an R7 cell fate. By contrast, the sev-expressing
precursor cells to the R1-R6 photoreceptor cells that do contact R8 are
nonresponsive to the inductive cue. Using the rough and Nspl mutations, we
demonstrate that an early commitment to an R1-R6 cell fate blocks the pathway of
sev activation in these cells.
