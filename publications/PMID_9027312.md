Comment in
    Science. 1997 Feb 21;275(5303):1081-2. doi: 10.1126/science.275.5303.1081.

Previous genetic studies of the nematode Caenorhabditis elegans identified three
important components of the cell death machinery. CED-3 and CED-4 function to
kill cells, whereas CED-9 protects cells from death. Here CED-9 and its
mammalian homolog Bcl-xL (a member of the Bcl-2 family of cell death regulators)
were both found to interact with and inhibit the function of CED-4. In addition,
analysis revealed that CED-4 can simultaneously interact with CED-3 and its
mammalian counterparts interleukin-1beta-converting enzyme (ICE) and FLICE.
Thus, CED-4 plays a central role in the cell death pathway, biochemically
linking CED-9 and the Bcl-2 family to CED-3 and the ICE family of pro-apoptotic
cysteine proteases.
