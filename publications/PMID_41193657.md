Update of
    bioRxiv. 2025 May 31:2025.05.29.656244. doi: 10.1101/2025.05.29.656244.

The androgen receptor (AR) is a critical driver of prostate cancer (PCa). Here,
to study regulators of AR protein levels and oncogenic activity, we developed a
live-cell quantitative endogenous AR fluorescent reporter. Leveraging this AR
reporter, we performed genome-scale CRISPRi flow cytometry sorting screens to
systematically identify genes that modulate AR protein levels. We identified and
validated known AR protein regulators, including HOXB13 and GATA2, and also
unexpected top hits including PTGES3-a poorly characterized gene in PCa. PTGES3
repression resulted in loss of AR protein, cell-cycle arrest and cell death in
AR-driven PCa models. Clinically, analysis of PCa data demonstrates that PTGES3
expression is associated with AR-directed therapy resistance. Mechanistically,
we show PTGES3 binds directly to AR, regulates AR protein stability and is
necessary for AR function in the nucleus at AR target genes. PTGES3 represents a
potential therapeutic target for overcoming known mechanisms of resistance to
existing AR-directed therapies in PCa.
