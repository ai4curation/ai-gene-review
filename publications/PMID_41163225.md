BACKGROUND: Triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC) is considered a highly
heterogeneous disease. Androgen receptor (AR)-positive TNBC is a subtype with
distinct molecular features. However, the molecular mechanism underlying the
modulation of the AR signaling pathway in TNBC is still elusive.
RESULTS: BPTF-associated protein of 18 kDa (BAP18) was significantly upregulated
in AR-positive TNBC samples and was positively correlated with advanced disease
stage and poor prognosis. BAP18 was shown to act as a transcriptional
corepressor of AR in AR-positive TNBC cells and is involved in the promotion of
AR-positive TNBC. Mechanically, BAP18 associates with AR and the SIN3A/HDAC
subcomplex. BAP18 facilitates the recruitment of SIN3A/HDAC to androgen response
elements (AREs) in the promoter regions of P21 and PTEN, subsequently leading to
a reduced level of histone H4 acetylation on AREs.
CONCLUSION: Our study revealed that BAP18, which acts as a novel AR corepressor,
is involved in AR-positive TNBC progression, suggesting that BAP18 could be a
potential therapeutic target for AR-positive TNBC patients.

Introduction

Triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC) is a group of extremely aggressive breast cancers that are the most refractory to treatment [1]. Emerging evidence suggests that the androgen receptor (AR), which is expressed in 10–50% of TNBC patients, plays a pivotal role in AR-positive TNBC progression [2, 3]. However, the effects of AR antagonists (e.g., enzalutamide) on AR-positive TNBC patients are limited [4, 5]. Therefore, there remains a need to investigate the molecular mechanisms underlying AR modulation in AR-positive TNBC to improve the efficacy of AR antagonists.

In recent years, there have been seemingly paradoxical pieces of evidence concerning the biological mechanism of AR [6]. Previous studies have explored the potential clinical role of AR in the treatment of TNBC, but AR antagonists do not have obvious therapeutic effects in patients who fail multiple lines of therapy, suggesting that the application of AR antagonists may be limited due to the complex roles of AR in TNBC [7]. AR belongs to the steroid receptor superfamily. After binding to androgen ligands, AR translocate into the nucleus and selectively binds to androgen response elements (AREs) [8]. Furthermore, coregulators are recruited to ARs and specifically regulate AR-mediated transcriptional activity through various mechanisms, including epigenetic modulation [9, 10]. Therefore, targeting AR coregulators demonstrate potential for application in AR-positive TNBC treatment.

BAP18, a BPTF-associated protein of 18 kDa, was identified as an AR coactivator in the Drosophila experimental system. Our previous studies revealed that BAP18 facilitates the recruitment of the MILL1 complex, increases H3K4me3 levels, and leads to AR-induced activation-resistant prostate cancer and polycystic ovary syndrome [11, 12]. Furthermore, BAP18 facilitates the transcription of CCND1/2 and promotes oral squamous cell carcinoma progression [13]. In breast cancer, BAP18 promotes the recruitment of the COMPASS complex to ER downstream genes, leading to the development of ERα-positive breast cancer. Furthermore, BAP18 improves the sensitivity of ER-positive cells to endocrine therapy, which has profound implications [14]. However, for AR-positive TNBC, the molecular mechanism of BAP18 is largely unknown.

In this study, BAP18 expression was significantly increased in AR-positive TNBC samples, indicating a poor prognosis in TNBC patients. BAP18 interacted with AR in SUM159PT cells endogenously expressing AR and in MDA-MB-231 cells ectopically expressing AR. We explored whether BAP18 was involved in the repression of AR-mediated transcription through coordination with the SIN3A/HDAC complex. These findings offer new insights into the biological function of BAP18 as an AR coregulator in this context, identifying a promising strategy to suppress AR-positive TNBC progression.

BAP18 physically associates with AR in AR-positive TNBC cell linesTo investigate whether BAP18 interacts with AR in TNBC cells, we performed Co-IP experiments. The results revealed that BAP18 interacted with the endogenous AR in the AR-positive TNBC cell line SUM159PT, and the interaction between AR and BAP18 was stronger in the presence of DTH (Fig. 2A and B). As MDA-MB-231 cells express low levels of AR, we then transfected FLAG-tagged BAP18 and AR expression plasmids into MDA-MB-231 cells for co-IP experiments. Similarly, FLAG-tagged BAP18 also interacted with exogenous AR (Fig. 2C and D). To further investigate the subcellular localization of BAP18 and AR, we performed immunofluorescence and found that both endogenous BAP18 and GFP-tagged BAP18 were still located in the nucleus, regardless of whether AR translocated from the cytoplasm and colocalized with BAP18 in the nucleus in the presence of DHT (Fig. 2E and F). Collectively, these results demonstrate that BAP18 intrinsically interacts with AR in AR-positive TNBC cells.

To verify the importance of BAP18 in repressing AR activity, we transfected highly efficient siRNAs against BAP18 into SUM159PT cells and assayed AR-induced gene expression. As shown in Fig. 3G, a series of AR-induced genes were significantly upregulated at the mRNA level in the presence of DHT. Importantly, knockdown of BAP18 upregulated the expression of AR-induced antioncogenes, including P21, PTEN, KLLN, EAF2, PISD, and KIBRA, while the expression of HER3 was not significantly altered. To verify these results, we examined AR-induced genes at the protein level. Consistently, BAP18 knockdown upregulated the expression of P21 and PTEN and vice versa (Fig. 3H and I). These results suggest that BAP18 plays a role in repressing AR-mediated anticogene transcription in AR-positive TNBC and that it might promote the progression of AR-positive TNBC in this way.BAP18 interacts with the SIN3A/HDAC complex to repress AR-induced transactivationTo elucidate the underlying molecular mechanism of the inhibition of AR action by BAP18, we performed unpublished proteome analysis using BAP18 as bait and showed that BAP18 interacted with SAP130, a member of the SIN3A/HDAC complex (Table 3). We subsequently established a series of BAP18-binding proteins, including DPY30, which was consistent with our previous findings and confirmed the reliability of the underlying molecular mechanism results. The SIN3A/HDAC complex was identified as a global transcriptional corepressor involved in epigenetic repression. IP assays demonstrated that BAP18 and AR interact with core components of SIN3A/HDAC, including SAP130, HDAC1 and SIN3A (Fig. 4A and B). These results suggested that BAP18 and AR were physically associated with the SIN3A/HDAC complex. To investigate whether HDAC1 was involved in the modulatory function of BAP18, we performed a luciferase assay and cotransfected FLAG-BAP18 and HDAC reporters and found that BAP18 repressed AR-mediated transcription in a dose-dependent manner. Trichostatin A (TSA), which acts as an inhibitor of the HDAC family, suppresses HDAC deacetylase activity. We observed that TSA efficiently attenuated the repressive effect of BAP18 on AR-mediated transactivation (Fig. 4C). These results indicate that the histone deacetylation function of the core component of the SIN3A/HDAC complex is required for the modulatory effect of BAP18 on AR activity in AR-positive TNBC.

DiscussionsAR, a member of the nuclear receptor (NR) family, performs both positive and negative functions in various AR-related cancers. The luminal androgen receptor has been identified as a subtype of TNBC. AR-positive TNBC is characterized by an enrichment of pathways. However, treatments targeting the androgen/AR pathway have not met expectations, indicating that AR may play a complex role in AR-positive TNBC [16, 17]. Further elucidation of the molecular mechanisms and exploration of novel strategies with significant efficacy are critical for patients with AR-positive TNBC. The coregulators of AR interact with AR to modulate its actions. These coregulators are critical therapeutic targets for cancers associated with AR. Classical coactivators such as CBP/p300 or BRCA1 mediate the enhancement of AR signalling in prostate cancer [18, 19]. Conversely, corepressors such as SMRT and N-CoR play essential roles in gene silencing mediated by nuclear receptors [20]. In this study, we provide evidence that BAP18 associates with the SIN3A/HDAC/AR subcomplex to diminish AR-induced transactivation in AR-positive TNBC cells.As a reader of histone H3K4me3, BAP18 plays an essential role in the activation of nuclear receptors [11, 12, 14]. BAP18 facilitates the ability of CTCF to increase the transactivation of ERα-related enhancers in BC [21]. In TNBC, BAP18 imparts transcriptional regulation of S100A9 [22]. Additionally, BAP18 facilitates the recruitment of MLL1 subcomplex to the context of AR target genes, resulting in histone modification alteration to regulate AR dependent gene activation. BAP18 regulates AR-induced transactivation and the function of BAP18 might be targeted in prostate cancer to promote tumor growth and progression to castration-resistance [11]. Interestingly, our study revealed that under androgen stimulation, BAP18 colocalizes with AR in the nucleus and interacts with AR to act as a corepressor. BAP18 inhibits the transcription of a subset of AR-induced tumor suppressors, including P21, PTEN, KLLN, EAF2, PISD, and KIBRA [23–29]. More importantly, our results indicate a molecular interaction among BAP18, AR, and the SIN3A/HDAC complex. The knockdown of BAP18 diminishes the ability of the SIN3A/HDAC complex to target the promoters of the AR-induced genes P21 and PTEN while significantly increasing the level of acetylation of histone H4. We found that while the interaction between the SIN3A/HDAC complex and BAP18 or AR was not enhanced by DHT, the recruitment of BAP18, AR, HDAC1, SIN3A, and SAP130 to the promoter regions of P21 and PTEN was significantly increased upon DHT treatment. This suggests that AR may serve as a “molecular bridge” to facilitate the recruitment of the BAP18 and SIN3A/HDAC complex to the promoters of AR target genes. These findings suggest a coordinated action between BAP18 and the SIN3A/HDAC complex in chromatin compaction, leading to the repression of gene expression in AR-positive TNBC.To understand how BAP18 regulate AR in AR-positive TNBC, we sought to identify the interaction complex of BAP18. The SIN3A/HDAC complex causes genes to enter a silent state by virtue of its enzymatic activity and chromatin remodeling capabilities, which are essential epigenetic mechanisms for controlling gene transcription [30, 31]. Its integral components, which include SIN3A, HDAC1/2, SAP30, and SAP130, enable SIN3A to act as a skeletal protein capable of recruiting histone deacetylases and various chromatin remodeling factors to target genes, exerting deacetylase activity and repressing transcription [32, 33]. The SIN3A/HDAC complex mediates the repression of several transcription factors, including ER, LSD1, Nanog, and Oct4 [34–36].The role of histone acetylation and deacetylation in modulating gene transcription is gradually being elucidated and is becoming increasingly clear. Generally, histone deacetylation leads to transcriptional repression [37]. In breast cancer, AR has been shown to be transcriptionally silent by SETDB1 in cooperation with the SIN3A/HDAC complex [38]. Notably, the inhibitory effect of BAP18 on AR function was dose dependent; this inhibition could be significantly reversed upon treatment with the HDAC inhibitor TSA. It is well established that HDAC inhibitors can influence gene expression by inducing epigenetic cycle changes that result in apoptosis while also regulating the tumor microenvironment; these agents have demonstrated potential efficacy in controlling TNBC [39–41]. However, further studies are needed to explore the anticancer efficacy of AR modulators that specifically target AR against AR-positive TNBC. This study provides valuable insights for investigating rational applications that may enhance current treatment strategies for TNBC.Emerging evidence indicates that BAP18 is highly expressed across various cancers and diseases [11–14, 22, 42]. Knockdown of BAP18 leads to transcriptional repression of CCND1/2 and prolongs the G1‒S phase transition in oral squamous cell carcinoma and non-small cell lung carcinoma [13, 42]. In this study, silencing BAP18 markedly inhibited cell proliferation while increasing the number of cells arrested in the G1 phase of the cell cycle. The function of P21 was found to be negatively regulated through the deacetylation activity mediated by SIN3A-HDAC [43]. P21 serves as a critical regulator of the cell cycle, inhibiting excessive cellular proliferation [44]. Given the importance of P21 in suppressing progression through the G1 phase, we propose that BAP18 may promote cell cycle progression at least partially by negatively regulating P21 expression in AR-positive TNBC.

BAP18 inhibits the transcription of a subset of AR-induced tumor suppressors, including P21, PTEN, KLLN, EAF2, PISD, and KIBRA [23–29]. More importantly, our results indicate a molecular interaction among BAP18, AR, and the SIN3A/HDAC complex. The knockdown of BAP18 diminishes the ability of the SIN3A/HDAC complex to target the promoters of the AR-induced genes P21 and PTEN while significantly increasing the level of acetylation of histone H4. We found that while the interaction between the SIN3A/HDAC complex and BAP18 or AR was not enhanced by DHT, the recruitment of BAP18, AR, HDAC1, SIN3A, and SAP130 to the promoter regions of P21 and PTEN was significantly increased upon DHT treatment. This suggests that AR may serve as a “molecular bridge” to facilitate the recruitment of the BAP18 and SIN3A/HDAC complex to the promoters of AR target genes. These findings suggest a coordinated action between BAP18 and the SIN3A/HDAC complex in chromatin compaction, leading to the repression of gene expression in AR-positive TNBC.

To understand how BAP18 regulate AR in AR-positive TNBC, we sought to identify the interaction complex of BAP18. The SIN3A/HDAC complex causes genes to enter a silent state by virtue of its enzymatic activity and chromatin remodeling capabilities, which are essential epigenetic mechanisms for controlling gene transcription [30, 31]. Its integral components, which include SIN3A, HDAC1/2, SAP30, and SAP130, enable SIN3A to act as a skeletal protein capable of recruiting histone deacetylases and various chromatin remodeling factors to target genes, exerting deacetylase activity and repressing transcription [32, 33]. The SIN3A/HDAC complex mediates the repression of several transcription factors, including ER, LSD1, Nanog, and Oct4 [34–36].

The role of histone acetylation and deacetylation in modulating gene transcription is gradually being elucidated and is becoming increasingly clear. Generally, histone deacetylation leads to transcriptional repression [37]. In breast cancer, AR has been shown to be transcriptionally silent by SETDB1 in cooperation with the SIN3A/HDAC complex [38]. Notably, the inhibitory effect of BAP18 on AR function was dose dependent; this inhibition could be significantly reversed upon treatment with the HDAC inhibitor TSA. It is well established that HDAC inhibitors can influence gene expression by inducing epigenetic cycle changes that result in apoptosis while also regulating the tumor microenvironment; these agents have demonstrated potential efficacy in controlling TNBC [39–41]. However, further studies are needed to explore the anticancer efficacy of AR modulators that specifically target AR against AR-positive TNBC. This study provides valuable insights for investigating rational applications that may enhance current treatment strategies for TNBC.

Emerging evidence indicates that BAP18 is highly expressed across various cancers and diseases [11–14, 22, 42]. Knockdown of BAP18 leads to transcriptional repression of CCND1/2 and prolongs the G1‒S phase transition in oral squamous cell carcinoma and non-small cell lung carcinoma [13, 42]. In this study, silencing BAP18 markedly inhibited cell proliferation while increasing the number of cells arrested in the G1 phase of the cell cycle. The function of P21 was found to be negatively regulated through the deacetylation activity mediated by SIN3A-HDAC [43]. P21 serves as a critical regulator of the cell cycle, inhibiting excessive cellular proliferation [44]. Given the importance of P21 in suppressing progression through the G1 phase, we propose that BAP18 may promote cell cycle progression at least partially by negatively regulating P21 expression in AR-positive TNBC.

ConclusionsBAP18 associates with the SIN3A/HDAC/AR subcomplex to diminish AR-induced transactivation in AR-positive TNBC cells, which provides evidence for a novel role of BAP18 in regulating AR-mediated transcription, suggesting that BAP18 could serve as a potential therapeutic target for AR-positive TNBC.

Conclusions

BAP18 associates with the SIN3A/HDAC/AR subcomplex to diminish AR-induced transactivation in AR-positive TNBC cells, which provides evidence for a novel role of BAP18 in regulating AR-mediated transcription, suggesting that BAP18 could serve as a potential therapeutic target for AR-positive TNBC.
