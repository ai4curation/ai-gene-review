# Effects of IL-1 receptor-associated kinase (IRAK) expression on IL-1 signaling are independent of its kinase activity.
**Authors:** Knop J, Martin MU
**Journal:** FEBS Lett (1999)
**DOI:** [10.1016/s0014-5793(99)00322-1](https://doi.org/10.1016/s0014-5793(99)00322-1)

## Abstract

1. FEBS Lett. 1999 Apr 1;448(1):81-5. doi: 10.1016/s0014-5793(99)00322-1.

Effects of IL-1 receptor-associated kinase (IRAK) expression on IL-1 signaling
are independent of its kinase activity.

Knop J(1), Martin MU.

Author information:
(1)Institut für Molekularpharmakologie, Medizinische Hochschule Hannover,
Germany.

Interleukin-1 (IL-1) stimulates the association of the IL-1 receptor-associated
protein kinase (IRAK) with the heterodimer of IL-IRI and IL-IRAcP via the
adapter protein MyD88. In the receptor complex IRAK becomes heavily
phosphorylated and concomitantly activated. Here we show that overexpression of
a kinase-inactive mutant of IRAK (K239S) inhibits neither IL-1-stimulated
activation of the transcription factor NF-kappaB, nor that of the c-Jun
N-terminal kinase nor IL-2 production in murine EL-4 cells, but enhances these
effects in a manner comparable to wild type IRAK. This strongly suggests that
the intrinsic kinase activity is not required for downstream signaling via IRAK.
