# Nod2 is a general sensor of peptidoglycan through muramyl dipeptide (MDP) detection.
**Authors:** Girardin SE, Boneca IG, Viala J, Chamaillard M, Labigne A, Thomas G, Philpott DJ, Sansonetti PJ
**Journal:** J Biol Chem (2003)
**DOI:** [10.1074/jbc.C200651200](https://doi.org/10.1074/jbc.C200651200)

## Abstract

1. J Biol Chem. 2003 Mar 14;278(11):8869-72. doi: 10.1074/jbc.C200651200. Epub
2003  Jan 13.

Nod2 is a general sensor of peptidoglycan through muramyl dipeptide (MDP)
detection.

Girardin SE(1), Boneca IG, Viala J, Chamaillard M, Labigne A, Thomas G, Philpott
DJ, Sansonetti PJ.

Author information:
(1)Unité de Pathogénie Microbienne Moléculaire, INSERM U389, Paris Cedex 15,
France.

Nod2 activates the NF-kappaB pathway following intracellular stimulation by
bacterial products. Recently, mutations in Nod2 have been shown to be associated
with Crohn's disease, suggesting a role for bacteria-host interactions in the
etiology of this disorder. We show here that Nod2 is a general sensor of
peptidoglycan through the recognition of muramyl dipeptide (MDP), the minimal
bioactive peptidoglycan motif common to all bacteria. Moreover, the 3020insC
frameshift mutation, the most frequent Nod2 variant associated with Crohn's
disease patients, fully abrogates Nod2-dependent detection of peptidoglycan and
MDP. Together, these results impact on the understanding of Crohn's disease
development. Additionally, the characterization of Nod2 as the first
pathogen-recognition molecule that detects MDP will help to unravel the well
known biological activities of this immunomodulatory compound.
