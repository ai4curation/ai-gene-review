# Sequencing, mutational analysis, and transcriptional regulation of the Escherichia coli htrB gene.
**Authors:** Karow M, Georgopoulos C
**Journal:** Mol Microbiol (1991)
**DOI:** [10.1111/j.1365-2958.1991.tb02159.x](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1365-2958.1991.tb02159.x)

## Abstract

1. Mol Microbiol. 1991 Sep;5(9):2285-92. doi: 10.1111/j.1365-2958.1991.tb02159.x.

Sequencing, mutational analysis, and transcriptional regulation of the
Escherichia coli htrB gene.

Karow M(1), Georgopoulos C.

Author information:
(1)Department of Cellular, Viral, and Molecular Biology, School of Medicine,
University of Utah, Salt Lake City 84132.

The Escherichia coli htrB gene was originally discovered because its insertional
inactivation led to an exquisitely temperature-sensitive phenotype in rich
media, i.e. the ability to form colonies at temperatures below 32 degrees C, but
not above 33 degrees C. The htrB gene has been sequenced. It can potentially
code for two proteins, with Mr values of 35,407 Da and 8669 Da, that are encoded
by overlapping, divergent open reading frames. Our data are consistent with the
35,407 Da protein being HtrB. Northern blot analysis clearly shows that the
monocistronic htrB message is not under heat-shock regulation. We have also
sequenced the flanking DNA and have discovered a new gene, designated orf39.9,
located immediately adjacent to htrB, but divergently transcribed.
