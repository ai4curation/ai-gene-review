Comment in
    Nat Immunol. 2005 Oct;6(10):961-2. doi: 10.1038/ni1005-961.

EAT-2 is an adaptor expressed in innate immune cells, including natural killer
(NK) cells. It is closely related to the adaptor SAP, which regulates signaling
lymphocyte activation molecule (SLAM)-related receptors by recruiting the kinase
FynT to the receptors. Here we have studied the function of EAT-2 in NK cells by
creating mice lacking or overexpressing EAT-2. Like SAP, EAT-2 was associated
with the SLAM-related receptor 2B4 in NK cells. However, unlike SAP, EAT-2 was
an inhibitor of NK cell function. EAT-2 repressed natural cytotoxicity and
interferon-gamma secretion by a mechanism involving tyrosine phosphorylation of
its C terminus. We have demonstrated a similar function for the adaptor ERT, a
newly identified SAP family member expressed in mouse NK cells. These data
identify a previously unknown mechanism of NK cell inhibition. Moreover, they
indicate that EAT-2 and SAP have distinct and at times opposing functions in
natural immunity.
