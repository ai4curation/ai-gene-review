In cardiac myocytes, the type 2a sarco/endoplasmic reticulum Ca-ATPase (SERCA2a)
plays a key role in intracellular Ca regulation. Due to its critical role in
heart function, SERCA2a activity is tightly regulated by different mechanisms,
including micropeptides. While phospholamban (PLB) is a well-known SERCA2a
inhibitor, dwarf open reading frame (DWORF) is a recently identified SERCA2a
activator. Since PLB phosphorylation is the most recognized mechanism of SERCA2a
activation during adrenergic stress, we studied whether PLB phosphorylation also
affects SERCA2a regulation by DWORF. By using confocal Ca imaging in a HEK293
expressing cell system, we analyzed the effect of the co-expression of PLB and
DWORF using a bicistronic construct on SERCA2a-mediated Ca uptake. Under these
conditions of matched expression of PLB and DWORF, we found that SERCA2a
inhibition by non-phosphorylated PLB prevails over DWORF activating effect.
However, when PLB is phosphorylated at PKA and CaMKII sites, not only PLB's
inhibitory effect was relieved, but SERCA2a was effectively activated by DWORF.
Förster resonance energy transfer (FRET) analysis between SERCA2a and DWORF
showed that DWORF has a higher relative affinity for SERCA2a when PLB is
phosphorylated. Thus, SERCA2a regulation by DWORF responds to the PLB
phosphorylation status, suggesting that DWORF might contribute to SERCA2a
activation during conditions of adrenergic stress.

INTRODUCTION

Heart contraction vitally depends on the well-controlled intracellular Ca homeostasis within cardiac myocytes. A particularly important role in this process is played by the type 2 sarco/endoplasmic reticulum Ca-ATPase (SERCA2a). The speed at which SERCA2a transport Ca from the cytosol into the sarcoplasmic reticulum (SR) controls the heart’s relaxation rate during diastole. SERCA2a activity also sets the total amount of Ca stored within the SR (i.e. SR Ca load), which determines the strength of heart’s contraction during systole. Consequently, defects in SERCA2a regulation cause SR Ca mishandling and contractile dysfunction in a variety of cardiac pathologies, including heart failure [1,2]. Therefore, understanding the molecular mechanisms of SERCA2a regulation is crucial for designing effective therapies to improve heart function during cardiac diseases.

Because SERCA2a-mediated Ca transport plays a central role in cardiac Ca signaling, the pump function is tightly regulated by several mechanisms, including the interaction with small peptides [3,4]. Such regulation allows the heart to quickly adjust its performance during the transition from rest to stress and vice versa. One primary mode of SERCA2a regulation involves its interaction with the small SR transmembrane peptide phospholamban (PLB). Under resting conditions, PLB binding to SERCA2a inhibits SR Ca transport by reducing the pump sensitivity to cytosolic Ca [5–7]. This mechanism reduces SR Ca load and systolic Ca transient, limiting cardiac muscle contraction. During stress, the heart enhances its performance to adapt to the increased body demands of oxygen and nutrients. Stress-induced β-adrenergic receptors (β-AR) stimulation activates the protein kinase A (PKA) and the Ca-Calmodulin kinase II (CaMKII). This, in turn, increases PLB phosphorylation and relieves PLB inhibition of SERCA2a-mediated Ca uptake, leading to elevated SR Ca load, increased systolic Ca transient and eventually enhanced heart contraction [8,9]. While phosphorylated PLB can still bind to SERCA2a, it certainly binds less tightly than the non-phosphorylated form, and the unbound peptides are stabilized by polymerization into homo- pentamers. This is another indirect mechanism of SERCA2a activation during adrenergic stress [10–13].

Another important SERCA2a regulator is the recently identified micropeptide dwarf open reading frame (DWORF) [14]. Unlike PLB and other regulators that inhibit SERCA2a function, DWORF is the only known SERCA2a activator [14–17]. It has been demonstrated that DWORF can displace PLB from the SERCA2a’s inhibitory site [17] or directly bind to SERCA2a to activate Ca transport [15,16]. While DWORF binding to SERCA2a increases at high cytosolic Ca, low Ca reverses this effect, favoring PLB binding to SERCA2a [18,19]. This interchange between DWORF and PLB plays an important role in SERCA2a regulation on a beat-to-beat basis through Ca release during systole and Ca sequestration during diastole [18]. Furthermore, it has been demonstrated that DWORF has a cardioprotective role and its overexpression can improve HF most likely through regulation of SERCA2a mediated SR Ca uptake [17,20,21]. DWORF might also play a significant role in SERCA2a regulation during adrenergic stress. While relieved inhibition by PLB is the most recognized mechanism of SERCA2a activation during β-AR stimulation, lower PLB binding to SERCA2a and higher PLB oligomerization rate might also promote SERCA2a activation by DWORF.

In this study, we used an expressing cell system that mimics general aspects of cardiac Ca signaling to measure the endoplasmic reticulum (ER) Ca dynamics with the ER-targeted Ca sensor R-CEPIA1er. We investigated how co-expression of DWORF and PLB (wild type or double-phosphomimetic) affects SERCA2a-mediated Ca uptake. Förster resonance energy transfer (FRET) analysis was performed to evaluate the energetics of SERCA2a binding to DWORF in the presence of PLB. This study provides novel insights of SERCA2a regulation by co-expression of DWORF and PLB. While PLB phosphorylation is required to relieve its inhibition of SERCA2a, it also indirectly facilitates DWORF binding to the pump, further enhancing SERCA2a activity.

Our experiments using the bicistronic constructs showed the mCer tag at the N-terminus of PLB was abundantly expressed and localized at the ER membrane (Fig. 1B), suggesting proper processing, folding and localization of the studied micropeptides.

Analysis of SERCA2a-mediated Ca uptake in permeabilized T-Rex-293 SERCA2a stable line cells demonstrated that the simultaneous expression of DWORF and non-phosphorylated PLB inhibits the SERCA2A uptake rate and limits the maximal ER Ca load compared to SERCA2a expression alone (Fig. 2B, C). These results are similar to the effect of PLB on SERCA2a Ca transport [15], suggesting that the non-phosphorylated PLB inhibitory effect prevails over the DWORF stimulatory effect. These results are also in good agreement with the previously published studies [16,18], that suggest non-phosphorylated PLB has higher affinity for SERCA2a than DWORF at low [Ca]Cyt. However, the stimulatory effect of DWORF on SERCA2a-mediated Ca uptake became significant when cells expressed PLB containing double phospho-mimetic mutations at PKA-dependent Ser16 and CaMKII-dependent Thr17 (Fig. 2). The SERCA2a uptake rates and the maximal ER Ca load in cells expressing DWORF and phospho-mimetic PLB were significantly increased compared to the rates and maximal ER Ca load obtained in cells expressing SERCA2a alone. These results are similar to the effect of DWORF alone on SERCA2a function [15]. Collectively, these results suggest that SERCA2a inhibition by non-phosphorylated PLB prevails over DWORF activation. However, when PLB is phosphorylated, SERCA2a can be more effectively activated by DWORF.

These results were obtained introducing mutations on PLB that mimic a maximum and irreversible phosphorylation state. We also used forskolin to activate endogenous PKA in intact HEK293 cells. In these more physiological conditions, activation of endogenous PKA is in equilibrium with endogenous phosphatases activity, resulting in partial phosphorylation of PLBs. Even if not at maximum level, PLB phosphorylation by endogenous PKA was able to relieve SERCA2a inhibition and facilitate SERCA2a activation by DWORF. However, the ER Ca loads obtained with forskolin were not as large as the ones seen with PLBDPM. These results are consistent with partial PLB phosphorylation and partial activation of SERCA2a by DWORF during forskolin, in contrast to the results with PLBDPM when the availability of SERCA2a for DWORF binding is maximum.

We also measured FRET between mCer-SERCA2a and YFP-DWORF to investigate whether the SERCA2a regulation by DWORF at different PLB phosphorylation levels was associated with changes of the SERCA2a-DWORF physical interaction. We found that co-expression of non-phosphorylated PLB reduces the apparent binding affinity of DWORF to SERCA2a (Fig. 4B). However, this effect can be partially restored by PLB phosphorylation. These results suggest improved relative competition of DWORF for SERCA2a when PLB is maximally phosphorylated. We and others have previously shown that phosphorylated PLB still binds to SERCA, but its apparent affinity is indirectly reduced as a consequence of increased oligomerization into pentamers [34,41,42]. Thus, phosphorylated PLB or PLBDPM is expected to compete less effectively against DWORF for binding to SERCA. This accounts for the partial reversal of the change in KD observed in Fig. 4B, in which the KD of PLBDPM was midway between the KD values determined for control (SERCA2a-DWORF alone) and SERCA-DWORF co-expressed with PLBWT.

The increased FRETMax is consistent with a decrease in the average distance between the Cer fluorescent protein tag fused to the SERCA N-terminus and the YFP fluorescent tag fused to DWORF. However, previously we tested directly whether such ternary regulatory complexes can form [36]. In that study we found that increasing expression of SERCA decreases PLB-DWORF FRET. This showed that PLB and DWORF can bind to each other, or to SERCA, but all three proteins do not interact in a ternary complex.

We previously found that PLB and DWORF are capable of binding to any of the SERCA2a structures associated with different enzymatic states in the catalytic cycle, but the two micropeptides show differential affinity for particular states [18]. We showed that PLB binds most avidly to the E1-ATP state. In contrast, DWORF binds all states but has a slight preference for E1PCa and E2P states. Perhaps the quaternary conformation of SERCA2a-DWORF complexes in those other enzymatic states is more compact, placing the fluorescent protein tags into closer proximity than they were in the previous structures from which DWORF was displaced. We have previously measured FRET for the SERCA2a-DWORF complex stabilized in various enzymatic states and did not observe large differences in the relative FRETMax values [18]. If those undetermined enzymatic substates form high affinity, conformationally compact complexes with DWORF, they may account for the observed increase in FRETMax in the presence of DWORF.

Taken together, the results create a portrait of dynamic competition between DWORF and PLB in a finely tuned equilibrium. The balance of these opposing forces is modulated by adrenergic signaling. In particular, while phosphorylation of PLB relieves inhibition SERCA2a, it also indirectly facilitates competition of the activator DWORF for binding to the pump (Fig. 7). We propose that this enhances the potency of DWORF activation during stress and increases the magnitude of the response to adrenergic signaling.

The HEK293 expressing cell system used in this study offers several advantages to investigate the function of Ca handling proteins, including SERCA2a [26,43,44]. HEK293 cells, like cardiomyocytes, possess a complex network of the ER. While the ER is not as structured as in myocytes, it is an important Ca storage and can be used to study ER Ca dynamics. HEK293 cells can be easily transfected with large cDNA plasmids (such as Ryr2) [26,43,44]. Another important advantage to HEK293 cells is that they lack endogenous PLB and DWORF, thereby permitting the study of only recombinant proteins [45]. While in cardiomyocytes these Ca handling proteins localize at specific different region of the SR, in HEK293 cells exogenous expression of these proteins does not reproduce cardiac structural organization. Nevertheless, HEK293 cells expressing cardiac SERCA2a and RyR2 are characterized by spontaneous Ca waves similar to the ones elicited in cardiomyocytes [26,44]. Thus, expression of both SERCA2a and RyR2 creates the Ca release/uptake machinery that reproduces key aspects of cardiac Ca cycling. Unlike HEK293 cells, cardiomyocytes have a strong contractile apparatus that is activated by an increase in cytosolic Ca [46]. This is another advantage of the HEK293 cell system as it provides a complex cellular environment, while avoiding motion artifacts during contractions. These features make this HEK293 cell model ideal for studies of SERCA2a regulation by drugs or micropeptides. All in all, HEK293 cells offered an ideal setting for studies of SERCA2a regulation by PLB and DWORF. However, the critical functional and structural differences of HEK293 cells compared to cardiomyocytes might hide or prevent other regulation mechanisms that are occurring in the native physiological environment. Therefore, more studies in a more physiological cardiac setting will be required to investigate the regulation of SERCA2a by DWORF during adrenergic stress.
