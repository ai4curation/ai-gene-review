# Activation of DegP chaperone-protease via formation of large cage-like oligomers upon binding to substrate proteins.
**Authors:** Jiang J, Zhang X, Chen Y, Wu Y, Zhou ZH, Chang Z, Sui SF
**Journal:** Proc Natl Acad Sci U S A (2008)
**DOI:** [10.1073/pnas.0805464105](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.0805464105)
**PMC:** [PMC2575304](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC2575304/)

## Abstract

1. Proc Natl Acad Sci U S A. 2008 Aug 19;105(33):11939-44. doi:
10.1073/pnas.0805464105. Epub 2008 Aug 12.

Activation of DegP chaperone-protease via formation of large cage-like oligomers
upon binding to substrate proteins.

Jiang J(1), Zhang X, Chen Y, Wu Y, Zhou ZH, Chang Z, Sui SF.

Author information:
(1)Department of Biological Sciences and Biotechnology, State-Key Laboratory of
Biomembrane and Membrane Biotechnology, Tsinghua University, Beijing 100084,
China.

Cells use molecular chaperones and proteases to implement the essential quality
control mechanism of proteins. The DegP (HtrA) protein, essential for the
survival of Escherichia coli cells at elevated temperatures with homologues
found in almost all organisms uniquely has both functions. Here we report a
mechanism for DegP to activate both functions via formation of large cage-like
12- and 24-mers after binding to substrate proteins. Cryo-electron microscopic
and biochemical studies revealed that both oligomers are consistently assembled
by blocks of DegP trimers, via pairwise PDZ1-PDZ2 interactions between
neighboring trimers. Such interactions simultaneously eliminate the inhibitory
effects of the PDZ2 domain. Additionally, both DegP oligomers were also observed
in extracts of E. coli cells, strongly implicating their physiological
importance.
