# Characterization of four B-type cyclin genes of the budding yeast Saccharomyces cerevisiae.

Characterization of four B-type cyclin genes of the budding yeast Saccharomyces

B.

Author information:
(1)Cold Spring Harbor Laboratory, New York 11724.

The previously described CLB1 and CLB2 genes encode a closely related pair of
B-type cyclins. Here we present the sequences of another related pair of B-type
cyclin genes, which we term CLB3 and CLB4. Although CLB1 and CLB2 mRNAs rise in
abundance at the time of nuclear division, CLB3 and CLB4 are turned on earlier,
rising early in S phase and declining near the end of nuclear division. When all
possible single and multiple deletion mutants were constructed, some multiple
mutations were lethal, whereas all single mutants were viable. All lethal
combinations included the clb2 deletion, whereas the clb1 clb3 clb4 triple
mutant was viable, suggesting a key role for CLB2. The inviable multiple clb
mutants appeared to have a defect in mitosis. Conditional clb mutants arrested
as large budded cells with a G2 DNA content but without any mitotic spindle.
Electron microscopy showed that the spindle pole bodies had duplicated but not
separated, and no spindle had formed. This suggests that the Clb/Cdc28 kinase
may have a relatively direct role in spindle formation. The two groups of Clbs
may have distinct roles in spindle formation and elongation.
