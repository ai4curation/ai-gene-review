Author information:

The vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) family has recently expanded by
the identification and cloning of three additional members, namely VEGF-B,
VEGF-C, and VEGF-D. In this study we demonstrate that VEGF-B binds selectively
to VEGF receptor-1/Flt-1. This binding can be blocked by excess VEGF, indicating
that the interaction sites on the receptor are at least partially overlapping.
Mutating the putative VEGF receptor-1/Flt-1 binding determinants Asp63, Asp64,
and Glu67 to alanine residues in VEGF-B reduced the affinity to VEGF receptor-1
but did not abolish binding. Mutational analysis of conserved cysteines
contributing to VEGF-B dimer formation suggest a structural conservation with
VEGF and platelet-derived growth factor. Proteolytic processing of the 60-kDa
VEGF-B186 dimer results in a 34-kDa dimer containing the receptor-binding
epitopes. The binding of VEGF-B to its receptor on endothelial cells leads to
increased expression and activity of urokinase type plasminogen activator and
plasminogen activator inhibitor 1, suggesting a role for VEGF-B in the
regulation of extracellular matrix degradation, cell adhesion, and migration.
