Degenerative disorders of motor neurons include a range of progressive fatal
diseases such as amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS), spinal-bulbar muscular
atrophy (SBMA), and spinal muscular atrophy (SMA). Although the causative
genetic alterations are known for some cases, the molecular basis of many SMA
and SBMA-like syndromes and most ALS cases is unknown. Here we show that
missense point mutations in the cytoplasmic dynein heavy chain result in
progressive motor neuron degeneration in heterozygous mice, and in homozygotes
this is accompanied by the formation of Lewy-like inclusion bodies, thus
resembling key features of human pathology. These mutations exclusively perturb
neuron-specific functions of dynein.
