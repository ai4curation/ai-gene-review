# The Arabidopsis chromatin-modifying nuclear siRNA pathway involves a nucleolar RNA processing center.
**Authors:** Pontes O, Li CF, Costa Nunes P, Haag J, Ream T, Vitins A, Jacobsen SE, Pikaard CS
**Journal:** Cell (2006)
**DOI:** [10.1016/j.cell.2006.05.031](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2006.05.031)

## Abstract

1. Cell. 2006 Jul 14;126(1):79-92. doi: 10.1016/j.cell.2006.05.031.

The Arabidopsis chromatin-modifying nuclear siRNA pathway involves a nucleolar
RNA processing center.

Pontes O(1), Li CF, Costa Nunes P, Haag J, Ream T, Vitins A, Jacobsen SE,
Pikaard CS.

Author information:

In Arabidopsis thaliana, small interfering RNAs (siRNAs) direct cytosine
methylation at endogenous DNA repeats in a pathway involving two forms of
nuclear RNA polymerase IV (Pol IVa and Pol IVb), RNA-DEPENDENT RNA POLYMERASE 2
(RDR2), DICER-LIKE 3 (DCL3), ARGONAUTE4 (AGO4), the chromatin remodeler DRD1,
and the de novo cytosine methyltransferase DRM2. We show that RDR2, DCL3, AGO4,
and NRPD1b (the largest subunit of Pol IVb) colocalize with siRNAs within the
nucleolus. By contrast, Pol IVa and DRD1 are external to the nucleolus and
colocalize with endogenous repeat loci. Mutation-induced loss of pathway
proteins causes downstream proteins to mislocalize, revealing their order of
action. Pol IVa acts first, and its localization is RNA dependent, suggesting an
RNA template. We hypothesize that maintenance of the heterochromatic state
involves locus-specific Pol IVa transcription followed by siRNA production and
assembly of AGO4- and NRPD1b-containing silencing complexes within nucleolar
processing centers.
