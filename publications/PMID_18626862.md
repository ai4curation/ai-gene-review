# Effect of gene amplification on threonine production by yeast.

Effect of gene amplification on threonine production by yeast.

Farfán MJ(1), Martín-Rendón E, Calderón IL.

Author information:
(1)Departamento de Genética, Facultad de Biología, Universidad de Sevilla, Ap.
1095, E-41080 Sevilla, Spain.

In this work, we have studied the effect of amplifying different alleles
involved in the threonine biosynthesis on the amino acid production by
Saccharomyces cerevisiae. The genes used were wild-type HOM3, HOM2, HOM6, THR1,
and THR4, and two mutant alleles of HOM3 (namely HOM3-R2 and HOM3-R6), that code
for feedback-insensitive aspartate kinases. The results show that only the
amplification of the HOM3 alleles leads to threonine and, in some instances, to
homoserine overproduction. In terms of the regulation of the pathway, the data
indicate that the main control is exerted by inhibition of the aspartate kinase
and that, probably, a second and less important regulation takes place at the
level of the homoserine kinase, the THR1 gene product. However, amplification of
THR1 in two related Hom3-R2 strains does not increase the amount of threonine
but, in one of them, it does induce accumulation of more homoserine. This result
probably reflects differences between these strains in some undetermined genetic
factor/s related with threonine metabolism. In general, the data indicate that
the common laboratory yeast strains are genetically rather heterogeneous and,
thus, extrapolation of conclusions must be done carefully. (c) 1996 John Wiley &
Sons, Inc.
