# Synergistic, random sequential binding of substrates in cobalamin-independent methionine synthase.

Cobalamin-independent methionine synthase (MetE) catalyzes the transfer of the
N5-methyl group of methyltetrahydrofolate (CH(3)-H(4)folate) to the sulfur of
homocysteine (Hcy) to form methionine and tetrahydrofolate (H(4)folate) as
products. This reaction is thought to involve a direct methyl transfer from one
substrate to the other, requiring the two substrates to interact in a ternary
complex. The crystal structure of a MetE.CH(3)-H(4)folate binary complex shows
that the methyl group is pointing away from the Hcy binding site and is quite
distant from the position where the sulfur of Hcy would be, raising the
possibility that this binary complex is nonproductive. The CH(3)-H(4)folate must
either rearrange or dissociate before methyl transfer can occur. Therefore,
determining the order of substrate binding is of interest. We have used kinetic
and equilibrium measurements in addition to isotope trapping experiments to
elucidate the kinetic pathway of substrate binding in MetE. These studies
demonstrate that both substrate binary complexes are chemically and kinetically
competent for methyl transfer and suggest that the conformation observed in the
crystal structure is indeed on-pathway. Additionally, the substrates are shown
to bind synergistically, with each substrate binding 30-fold more tightly in the
presence of the other. Methyl transfer has been determined to be slow compared
to ternary complex formation and dissociation. Simulations indicate that nearly
all of the enzyme is present as the ternary complex under physiological
conditions.
