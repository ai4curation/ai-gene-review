# Tumor necrosis factor alpha and interleukin 1beta up-regulate gastric mucosal Fas antigen expression in Helicobacter pylori infection.
**Authors:** Houghton J, Macera-Bloch LS, Harrison L, Kim KH, Korah RM
**Journal:** Infect Immun (2000)
**DOI:** [10.1128/IAI.68.3.1189-1195.2000](https://doi.org/10.1128/IAI.68.3.1189-1195.2000)

## Abstract

1. Infect Immun. 2000 Mar;68(3):1189-95. doi: 10.1128/IAI.68.3.1189-1195.2000.

Tumor necrosis factor alpha and interleukin 1beta up-regulate gastric mucosal
Fas antigen expression in Helicobacter pylori infection.

Houghton J(1), Macera-Bloch LS, Harrison L, Kim KH, Korah RM.

Author information:
(1)Division of Gastroenterology, Department of Medicine, University of Medicine
and Dentistry of New Jersey, Newark, USA. houghtjm@umdnj.edu

Fas-mediated gastric mucosal apoptosis is gaining attention as a cause of tissue
damage due to Helicobacter pylori infection. We explored the effects of H.
pylori directly, and the effects of the inflammatory environment established
subsequent to H. pylori infection, on Fas-mediated apoptosis in a nontransformed
gastric mucosal cell line (RGM-1). Exposure to H. pylori-activated peripheral
blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs), but not H. pylori itself, induced Fas antigen
(Fas Ag) expression, indicating a Fas-regulatory role for inflammatory cytokines
in this system. Of various inflammatory cytokines tested, only interleukin 1beta
and tumor necrosis factor alpha induced Fas Ag expression, and removal of either
of these from the conditioned medium abrogated the response. When exposed to Fas
ligand, RGM-1 cells treated with PBMC-conditioned medium underwent massive and
rapid cell death, interestingly, with a minimal effect on total cell numbers
early on. Cell cycle analysis revealed a substantial increase in S phase cells
among cells exposed to Fas ligand, suggesting an increase in their proliferative
response. Taken together, these data indicate that the immune environment
secondary to H. pylori infection plays a critical role in priming gastric
mucosal cells to undergo apoptosis or to proliferate based upon their Fas Ag
status.
