# Essential function of Aco2, a fusion protein of aconitase and mitochondrial ribosomal protein bL21, in mitochondrial translation in fission yeast.

Essential function of Aco2, a fusion protein of aconitase and mitochondrial

A possible interaction between aconitase and a mitochondrial ribosomal protein
was suggested in a genome-wide interactome study. In fission yeast
Schizosaccharomyces pombe, the aco2(+) gene encodes a fusion protein between
aconitase and a putative mitochondrial ribosomal protein bL21 (Mrpl49). Two
types of aco2(+) transcripts are generated via alternative poly (A) site
selection, producing both a single aconitase domain protein and the fusion form.
The bL21-fused Aco2 protein resides in mitochondria as well as in the cytosol
and the nucleus. The viability defect of aco2 mutation is complemented not by
the aconitase domain but by the bL21 domain, which enables mitochondrial
translation.
