Comment in
    Science. 1995 Nov 17;270(5239):1159. doi: 10.1126/science.270.5239.1159.

A member of the inwardly rectifying potassium channel family was cloned here.
The channel, called BIR (Kir6.2), was expressed in large amounts in rat
pancreatic islets and glucose-responsive insulin-secreting cell lines.
Coexpression with the sulfonylurea receptor SUR reconstituted an inwardly
rectifying potassium conductance of 76 picosiemens that was sensitive to
adenosine triphosphate (ATP) (IKATP) and was inhibited by sulfonylureas and
activated by diazoxide. The data indicate that these pancreatic beta cell
potassium channels are a complex composed of at least two subunits--BIR, a
member of the inward rectifier potassium channel family, and SUR, a member of
the ATP-binding cassette superfamily. Gene mapping data show that these two
potassium channel subunit genes are clustered on human chromosome 11 at position
11p15.1.
