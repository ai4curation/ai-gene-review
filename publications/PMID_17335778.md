# Involvement of the calcium channel beta3 subunit in olfactory signal transduction.
**Authors:** Shiraiwa T, Kashiwayanagi M, Iijima T, Murakami M
**Journal:** Biochem Biophys Res Commun (2007)
**DOI:** [10.1016/j.bbrc.2007.02.063](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.bbrc.2007.02.063)

## Abstract

1. Biochem Biophys Res Commun. 2007 Apr 20;355(4):1019-24. doi:
10.1016/j.bbrc.2007.02.063. Epub 2007 Feb 22.

Involvement of the calcium channel beta3 subunit in olfactory signal
transduction.

Shiraiwa T(1), Kashiwayanagi M, Iijima T, Murakami M.

Author information:
(1)Graduate School of Pharmaceutical Sciences, Hokkaido University, Sapporo,
Japan.

Despite the expression of voltage-dependent Ca2+ channels in nasal turbinate
epithelium, their role in odorant chemosensation has remained obscure.
Therefore, we investigated olfactory neurotransduction in beta3-deficient mice.
RT-PCR and Western blots confirmed the expression of various types of Ca2+
channels in the nasal turbinate. Electrophysiological evaluations revealed that
beta3-null mice had a 60% reduction in the high-voltage-dependent Ca2+ currents
in olfactory receptor neurons due to reduced N- and L-type channel currents. The
beta3-null mice showed increased olfactory neuronal activity to triethylamine,
and this effect was mimicked by the perfusion of the specific N-type Ca2+
channel inhibitor omega-conotoxin GVIA in the electro-olfactogram. Diluted male
urine odors induced higher Fos immunoreactivity in the main olfactory bulbs of
beta3-deficient mice, indicating enhanced signal transduction of odor
information in these mice. Our data indicate the involvement of
voltage-dependent Ca2+ channels and importance of the beta3 subunit in olfactory
signal transduction.
