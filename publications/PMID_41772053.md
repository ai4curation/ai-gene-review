The repair of DNA double-strand breaks by homologous recombination is essential
for genomic integrity, and its dysregulation is a hallmark of cancer1. Central
to homologous recombination is the RAD51 recombinase, whose assembly into a
nucleoprotein filament is governed by five RAD51 paralogues (RAD51B, RAD51C,
RAD51D, XRCC2 and XRCC3)2. Mutations in any of these proteins predispose
individuals to multiple cancers or genetic disorders3-6. These paralogues are
thought to form two functionally separate complexes RAD51B-RAD51C-RAD51D-XRCC2
(BCDX2) and RAD51C-XRCC3 (CX3), that act independently at different stages of
homologous recombination7-11. Here we demonstrate that all five paralogues can
assemble into a single, ATP-dependent BCDX2-CX3-RAD51 supercomplex. The
architecture of this assembly bound to single-stranded DNA reveals a contiguous
filament where the CX3 module stacks atop BCDX2, creating a protofilament
template for RAD51 filament formation. We further identify a novel,
RAD51B-independent DX2-CX3 complex (RAD51D-XRCC2-RAD51C-XRCC3) functioning as a
stable RAD51 anchor on single-stranded DNA, and we capture it in multiple
states, including capping RAD51 filament segment. These distinct assemblies are
differentially regulated by ATPase activity, defining a dynamic BCDX2-CX3
'loader' and a stable DX2-CX3 'anchor' that provide functional modularity to the
homologous recombination machinery. This work provides a unifying mechanism for
human RAD51 paralogue function and delivers an atomic blueprint for interpreting
disease-causing mutations.

Introduction.

Homologous recombination (HR) is a high-fidelity DNA repair mechanism critical for cellular defense against genomic instability, enabling cells to access and copy intact DNA sequences to repair DNA damage12. Central to this process is the RAD51 recombinase, which polymerizes onto RPA-coated single-stranded DNA (ssDNA) to form a nucleoprotein filament, known as the presynaptic filament13. This dynamic structure serves as the catalytic center for homology search and subsequent DNA strand invasion and exchange, ensuring accurate restoration of the damaged DNA sequence14,15. In human cells, RAD51 filament formation is regulated by BRCA2 and five RAD51 paralogs (RAD51B, RAD51C, RAD51D, XRCC2, XRCC3), which are critical tumor suppressor genes. Mutations in any of the paralog genes dramatically predispose individuals to multiple cancers, particularly breast and ovarian malignancies, and other debilitating genetic diseases3–5,16,17.

Established models of HR posit that the five human RAD51 paralogs (Extended Data Fig. 1a) operate within two distinct complexes, RAD51B-RAD51C-RAD51D-XRCC2 (BCDX2) and RAD51C-XRCC3 (CX3), that function at different temporal stages of the repair process7,8,10,18–20. Division of labor between these two complexes has remained a central but incompletely understood tenet of the HR field. Moreover, the precise molecular mechanism by which paralog complexes promote RAD51 filament formation remains enigmatic, with competing models suggesting end-capping function, co-filament structures, or molecular chaperone functions18,19,21–26.

ResultsBCDX2-CX3-RAD51-ssDNA complex structureTo investigate the interplay between RAD51 paralog complexes in RAD51 filament formation, we reconstituted and assessed their interactions in vitro. Contrary to the existing paradigm, we discovered a remarkably robust and stable association between the BCDX2 and CX3 complexes (Fig. 1a, b, Extended Data Fig. 1b). This interaction was significantly facilitated by ATP, with an exceptionally tight binding affinity (KD 1.2 nM) measured by surface plasmon resonance (SPR), independent of DNA (Fig. 1c, Extended Data Fig. 1c-f, Supplementary Table 1). Formation of the BCDX2–CX3 supercomplex markedly increased RAD51 recruitment compared with BCDX2 alone, with RAD51 binding to the supercomplex at an affinity of approximately 100 nM (Fig. 1a,b; Extended Data Fig. 1g). Notably, however, the presence of RAD51 modestly reduced the amount of CX3 bound to BCDX2 (Fig. 1a,b; Extended Data Fig. 1e,f), suggesting that CX3 and RAD51 may engage partially overlapping or competitive binding sites on the BCDX2 complex.To elucidate the molecular basis for this unexpected synergy, we determined the cryo-electron microscopy (cryo-EM) structure of the BCDX2-CX3-RAD51-ssDNA assembly at an overall resolution of 2.7 Å (Fig. 1d, e, Extended Data Fig. 1h, Supplementary Fig. 2, Extended Data Table 1). The structure reveals a contiguous helical filament where the CX3 module stacks directly atop the BCDX2 scaffold, creating a platform for RAD51 engagement (Fig. 1d-f). This assembly mimics RAD51 filament structure itself, suggesting that BCDX2-CX3 supercomplex forms an extended filament template for RAD51 recruitment. The complete assembly incorporates two copies of RAD51C and assembles in a specific subunit order: XRCC2-RAD51D-RAD51C-RAD51B-RAD51C-XRCC3-RAD51-RAD51 (Fig. 1d).The RecA-like domain of RAD51B, which was not visible in previous BCDX2 sub-assemblies18,19, is now fully resolved, and sandwiched between two copies of RAD51C: RAD51C from the BCDX2 complex (RAD51CBC) at the bottom and RAD51C from the CX3 module (RAD51CCX3) on top (Fig. 1d, Supplementary Fig. 3a-e). The N-terminal domains (NTDs) of all BCDX2 components interact and stack on top of one another (Fig. 1e), similar to sub-assemblies of BCDX218,19. Furthermore, the RAD51CCX3NTD stacks with NTDs from BCDX2, and its linker region stretches across the face of RAD51B (Fig. 1d, e). On the contrary, the XRCC3NTD does not stack with the other NTDs, but binds to the interface between RAD51B and RAD51CCX3 (Fig. 1e, Supplementary Fig. 3f). Consistent with structural observations, introducing the VUS mutation27 RAD51CQ267R or mutating the RAD51B surface (BMCDX2: R8E, K218Y, E248R) disrupted BCDX2-CX3 supercomplex assembly in solution (Extended Data Fig. 2a, b). Collectively, the structure reveals a cooperative network of interactions that stabilizes the BCDX2-CX3 supercomplex, showing how all five paralogs form a single, integrated functional unit.RAD51C plays a unique role in the assembly, appearing in two separate modules, CX3 and RAD51B-RAD51C (the BC module), and using the same surface to bind either RAD51B or RAD51D (Supplementary Fig. 3b-e). RAD51C primarily recognizes the overall electrostatic and topographical features of its binding partners rather than relying on residue-level specific interactions. A notable exception is RAD51C-H307, which consistently occupies a chemically distinct hydrophobic pocket on both RAD51B and RAD51D (Supplementary Fig. 3d).XRCC3 engages the first RAD51 (RAD511) through a combination of specialized and canonical contacts (Fig. 1). RAD511NTD sits atop XRCC3 and its linker tracks along the XRCC3 surface, positioning RAD511 F86 into a defined pocket reminiscent of RAD51–RAD51 filament contacts (Fig. 1e, Extended Data Fig. 2c-e). Meanwhile, the RAD511 RecA-like domain docks onto XRCC3 in an ATP-stabilized, canonical manner. Consistent with this, an ATP-destabilizing mutation in XRCC3R150S weakened the CX3 association with RAD51 in solution, while the patient mutation27 XRCC3Q152K had a more modest effect (Extended Data Fig. 2a, f). Together, these interactions support a transition from filament template architecture to filament extension structures, with RAD512 docking onto RAD511 in the standard filament geometry and adopting a presynaptic-like conformation poised for filament growth (Fig. 1f).BCDX2 and CX3 cooperate in ssDNA binding.RAD51 nucleoprotein filament assembles and extends the ssDNA by separating it into nucleotide triplets, a mechanism that enables the filament to conduct its homology search in three-base increments28–30. To understand how BCDX2-CX3-RAD51 initiates ssDNA binding, the sample was also reconstituted in the presence of ssDNA (19 nt). Consequently, 16 nt of ssDNA threads through the complex in a 5’ to 3’ direction, where it is handed off between modules in a coordinated process (Fig. 1e-h).The DX2 module binds at the 5’ ssDNA end and ensures ssDNA selectivity (e.g., by using RAD51D Q219, Extended Data Fig. 3a), similarly to the described BCDX2 sub-assembly18,19. Consistent with this, the BCDX2-CX3 supercomplex bound ssDNA and replication fork (RF) DNA with comparable affinities but did not interact with dsDNA (Extended Data Fig. 3b, Supplementary Table 1). The ssDNA transitions into an extended, filament-like conformation along the BC module, guided by the separating RAD51D-R255, which intercalates between the DNA bases and acts as a separator, distinguishing the initial selective ssDNA binding at the DX2 module from the subsequent filament-like DNA binding occurring at the BC module. The 6 nt of ssDNA maintain its extended form without any visible separation of ssDNA into triplets (Fig. 1g, h, Extended Data Fig. 3a). However, upon reaching the CX3 module, the ssDNA is progressively split into nucleotide triplets, facilitated first by RAD51B (L263) and then by RAD51C (I290). These observations suggest that the CX3 module may facilitate homology search, consistent with early biochemical observations31.Consistent with these structural observations, the fully assembled BCDX2-CX3 supercomplex binds ssDNA in an ATP-dependent manner with a ~10-fold higher affinity compared to BCDX2, with weak ssDNA binding observed for the CX3 module (Fig. 2a-c, Supplementary Table 1, Extended Data Fig. 3b). BCDX2 and CX3 mutants defective in supercomplex assembly, or a Fanconi anemia RAD51CR258H mutation that directly interferes with DNA binding by RAD51C, diminished or abolished the cooperative ssDNA binding of the BCDX2-CX3 supercomplex, while inclusion of RAD51 minimally improved BCDX2-CX3 affinity for ssDNA and had no strong effect on BCDX2 or CX3 ssDNA-binding activity (Extended Data Fig. 3c).Overall, this intricate DNA hand-off mechanism, from selective ssDNA binding at the DX2 module to triplet splitting at the CX3 module, might serve as a quality control checkpoint, ensuring only appropriate DNA substrates are used for RAD51 filament nucleation since the RAD51 protein itself is not overly selective for DNA types28,32.Nucleotides stabilize BCDX2-CX3 complexAll human RAD51 paralogs contain ATP-binding sites, similar to the RAD51 recombinase itself14. Cryo-EM revealed that ATP stabilizes the interfaces within the BCDX2 module and, critically, at the junction connecting BCDX2 to the CX3 module (RAD51B:RAD51CCX3 interface), consistent with the ATP requirement for BCDX2-CX3 supercomplex formation (Fig. 1e, Fig. 2d, Extended Data Fig. 1c, d). ATP also stabilizes the XRCC3-RAD511 interface.In stark contrast to all other sites, the RAD51CCX3-XRCC3 interface within the CX3 module is occupied by ADP (Fig. 1e, 2d). The structure revealed a fully competent ATPase site, suggesting that CX3 might hydrolyze ATP during assembly, and that ADP might be sufficient to provide stability to the CX3 module and to support DNA binding activity. Structurally, RAD51CCX3-XRCC3 is the only interface that uses XRCC3-N290 to position the putative γ-phosphate of ATP for hydrolysis, with all other sites using His at the equivalent position (Fig. 2d). XRCC3-N290 functions as a linchpin, forming direct interactions with RAD51C-R249 and RAD51C-Q285, thereby bypassing the requirement for γ-phosphate of ATP and explaining how ADP can support CX3 complex function (Fig. 2d). Lastly, our human CX3-ADP structure showed identical residue conformation to that observed in the Alvinella pompejana CX3-ADP.BeFx structure20, suggesting that ADP.BeFx mimics the ADP-bound state (Extended Data Fig. 4a).Apart from XRCC2 and RAD51D which lack the Walker B glutamate necessary for ATP hydrolysis, RAD51B, RAD51C and XRCC3 subunits form catalytically competent ATPase sites in the fully assembled BCDX2-CX3-RAD51 supercomplex (Fig. 2d). Consistent with this, BCDX2-CX3 exhibited a robust, ssDNA-stimulated ATPase activity that was further enhanced by the presence of RAD51 (Fig. 2e, Extended Data Fig. 4b), while individual BCDX2 and CX3 modules exhibited low ATPase activity (similar in value to previously reported18,19). BCDX2-RAD51 also showed a noticeable increase in ATPase activity compared to other conditions, although at a smaller scale, consistent with a weak interaction between BCDX2 and RAD51 compared to the BCDX2 interaction with CX3 (Fig. 1a, b). Mutating the Walker B motif of RAD51B (BE144Q) or RAD51C (CE161Q) significantly impaired ATPase activity of the BE144QCDX2-CX3 and BCDX2-CE161QX3 complexes in the presence of ssDNA, while nearly abolishing ATPase activity in the absence of ssDNA (Fig. 2e). BE144QCDX2-CX3 and BCDX2-CE161QX3 mutants could still assemble into complexes, and exhibited ssDNA binding activity similar to wild-type proteins (Extended Data Fig. 4c-e). BCDX2 and CX3 mutants defective in BCDX2-CX3 supercomplex assembly also exhibited reduced ATPase activity (Fig. 2e). These results suggest that the RAD51B ATPase is activated by the docking of RAD51CCX3, as the only newly formed ATPase site. A functional RAD51CCX3-XRCC3 ATPase site is also required for full supercomplex ATPase activity, either to provide a functional interaction with BCDX2 or because it is activated by the BCDX2 module itself.The robust ATPase activity of the BCDX2-CX3 supercomplex suggests a highly dynamic assembly, consistent with the short residence time of BCDX2 on ssDNA18,19. Since ATP is required for BCDX2-CX3 supercomplex formation and ssDNA binding, ATP hydrolysis likely promotes its disassembly after substrate hand-off, similar to RAD51 recombinase itself33.DX2-CX3 anchors RAD51 on ssDNA.Our structural model of the BCDX2-CX3 supercomplex raised the tantalizing hypothesis that the foundational DX2 platform might function as a molecular adaptor, capable of binding either the BC module to form the canonical BCDX2 complex, or the CX3 module (RAD51CX3-XRCC3) to form a distinct DX2-CX3 assembly (Fig. 1d, Supplementary Fig. 3a). This hypothesis suggests a novel versatility in the human paralog system. Consistent with this idea, DX2 readily bound CX3 in solution, and DX2-CX3 could associate with RAD51 (Extended Data Fig. 5a, b). To understand the structural basis and function of this novel DX2-CX3 complex, we reconstituted it with RAD51 in the presence of ATP and ssDNA for cryo-EM analysis. This yielded maps for two distinct conformational states: ‘active’ state (2.8 Å, 34% of particles) and ‘intermediate’ state (2.8 Å, 29% of particles) (Fig. 3a, Supplementary Fig. 4, Extended Data Table 1). We also determined a ‘closed’ state (3.2 Å) structure (Fig. 3b) from a separate dataset, which will be detailed below.Globally, we observed that the DX2 module directly recognizes the CX3 module, with XRCC3 bound RAD511 in a manner identical to the BCDX2-CX3-RAD51 complex described above (Fig. 3a). A consistent feature across all states is that DX2 remains persistently bound to the ssDNA, anchoring the complex. However, in this assembly, XRCC3NTD stacks on top of the RAD51C-RAD51D NTDs, effectively replacing RAD51BNTD, with XRCC3NTD mimicking RAD51BNTD at the RAD51C-RAD51D interface (Fig. 3a, Extended Data Fig. 5c). In all structures, we observed ATP bound to DX2, consistent with our and previous DX2 structures18,19, and ADP bound to CX3 and RAD51 (Supplementary Fig. 5).In the ‘active state’, ssDNA is threaded through both DX2-CX3 and RAD511. Similar to the BCDX2-CX3-RAD51 structure, DX2 provides ssDNA selectivity (Extended Data Fig. 5d, e). RAD51CCX3 engages only a DNA doublet; RAD51C-I290 then splits the DNA to deliver a DNA triplet into XRCC3. DNA threads through XRCC3-RAD511 in a conformation similar to the presynaptic filament. RAD512 adopts the canonical RAD51 filament register, indicating that DX2-CX3 is also compatible with pre-synaptic filament formation (Fig. 3c).In the ‘intermediate state’, RAD512 is rotated on the RAD511 surface (Fig. 3a, b, d), disrupting the filament’s helical symmetry and making the complex incompatible with further extension. This conformation may represent a paused, disassembly-prone state. Here, ssDNA terminates at the RAD51C-XRCC3 “gate” (RAD51C loop 290–295 and XRCC3 loop 221–227), which adopts an open conformation (Fig. 3b, e).To distinguish whether these states reflect nucleation or disassembly, we collected a second cryo-EM dataset from an otherwise identical sample vitrified 30 min later (Fig. 3b; Supplementary Fig. 6; Extended Data Table 1). This revealed a DX2–CX3–RAD51 closed state containing only a single RAD51 bound to DX2–CX3, consistent with progressive filament disassembly. In this state, the CX3 gate is fully closed, blocking ssDNA entry into XRCC3 and RAD51 (Fig. 3e).Collectively, these results reveal the structural basis for the novel RAD51B-independent paralog assembly, DX2-CX3, which can directly recruit RAD51 to the ssDNA, and remain associated with RAD51 and ssDNA even when all active ATPase sites hydrolyze ATP to ADP, suggesting a role in anchoring RAD51 to ssDNA when the RAD51 filament has collapsed. This structure might allow rapid re-nucleation of RAD51 filament under conditions of high local RAD51 concentration.DX2-CX3 can cap RAD51 filament segmentIn solution, DX2-CX3 tightly bound with similar affinity to both ssDNA and RF DNA but not dsDNA, similarly to the BCDX2-CX3 supercomplex (Extended Data Fig. 6a, b, Supplementary Table 1). However, in contrast to the BCDX2-CX3 supercomplex, both ATP and ADP could support DX2-CX3 binding to ssDNA, consistent with DX2-CX3 anchoring RAD51 to ssDNA even with bound ADP as observed in our structures (Fig. 3a, b). Furthermore, RAD51 enhanced ssDNA binding of DX2-CX3, indicating that RAD51 itself might be a component of this ssDNA-binding complex (Extended Data Fig. 6a). SPR revealed KD of ~100 nM for DX2-CX3 interaction with RAD51, showing that both BCDX2-CX3 and DX2-CX3 have similar affinity for RAD51 (Extended Data Fig. 6c). Notably, the DX2-CX3 complex by itself displayed high intrinsic ATPase activity (Extended Data Fig. 6d). This activity, however, was suppressed upon binding to either ssDNA or RAD51, indicating that the complex transitions to a more static state once it engages its partners. This biochemical signature contrasts with the more dynamic, highly ATPase-active BCDX2-CX3, suggesting a distinct mechanism of action for the DX2-CX3 complex consistent with the anchoring function.Stable DX2-CX3 association with ssDNA and its low ATPase activity implied that DX2-CX3 should remain associated with the RAD51 filament segment after the nucleation event. We reasoned that RAD51 ATPase activity and short ssDNA (19 nt) prevented the capture of a RAD51 filament bound to DX2-CX3 in initial samples. To overcome this, we reconstituted the DX2-CX3-RAD51 complex in the presence of ATP in a buffer containing CaCl2 instead of MgCl2, as CaCl2 inhibits RAD51 ATPase activity but supports filament formation34, and added a longer 30 nt ssDNA (dT30 sequence) before grid preparation. Consistent with our predictions, we captured DX2-CX3 capping the 5’ end of a RAD51 filament segment, with five copies of RAD51 clearly resolved, and the sixth copy also visible at a lower threshold (2.6 Å, Fig. 4a, Supplementary Fig. 7a-c, Extended Data Table 1). The conformation of the DX2-CX3-RAD511-RAD512 segment was identical to the ‘active’ state structure, confirming our earlier assignment (Extended Data Fig. 6e). As expected, the RAD511NTD docks against XRCC3 RecA-like domain, while the subsequent RAD51 proteins in the filament adopts the canonical conformation with helical pitch ~100 Å, consistent with active, ATP-bound RAD51 filament28,29,34 (Fig. 4a, Extended Data Fig. 6e).At least 26 nt of ssDNA are resolved and threaded through the structure, with the DX2 module providing selective binding of ssDNA at the 5’ end (Fig. 4b, Extended Data Fig. 7a, b). RAD51C still binds a ssDNA doublet, similar to the DX2-CX3 structures described above, but different to the BCDX2-CX3 complex, where RAD51C recognizes a DNA triplet (Fig. 1h). XRCC3, however, recognizes a ssDNA triplet, similar to all other XRCC3 sites described above, while all RAD51 proteins engage the DNA in their typical triplet-binding mode28,29.Consistent with our experimental setup, ATP molecules stabilize nearly all subunit interfaces, with the notable exception of the CX3 module (RAD51C-XRCC3 interface), which still contains ADP, further confirming the strong functional preference of the CX3 module for the ADP-state (Fig. 4b, Supplementary Fig. 7d).Collectively, our work reveals that DX2-CX3-RAD51 creates a stable ssDNA anchor for a RAD51 filament segment and that DX2-CX3 can remain associated with the RAD51 filament, functioning as a 5’ filament cap. Such 5’ cap might be important to prevent filament disassembly from the 5’ end and might serve as a barrier, providing protection against anti-recombinase activities33,35–37.The DX2-CX3 anchor binds a DNA bubbleThe establishment of the DX2-CX3 complex as a stable anchor and a filament cap naturally raises the question of its function beyond initial filament formation. If this complex persists on DNA, what is its role after the RAD51 filament has engaged a homologous template and initiated strand exchange? To investigate this, we sought to mimic engagement of the homologous DNA by reconstituting the DX2-CX3-RAD51 complex in the presence of ATP and MgCl2 with two complementary ssDNA strands added one after another. Cryo-EM analysis of this assembly captured the complex in a new conformation, where the DX2-CX3 core cradles a DNA bubble with the re-formed duplex DNA projecting away from the assembly, stabilized by the entire complex (3.2 Å, Fig. 4c, Supplementary Fig. 8a-e, Extended Data Table 1).The NTDs of both RAD51 protomers rotate and translate into close apposition (Fig. 4d, Extended Data Fig. 7c). The remodeled RAD511NTD then docks against the minor groove of the dsDNA within the bubble (Fig. 4d). RAD51-K64 contacts the DNA phosphate backbone, corroborating prior NMR and RAD51NTD mutagenesis studies38 (Extended Data Fig. 7d), and a similar backbone interaction stabilizes the displacement (D-loop) during homology search39, though without NTD rearrangements. The bubble is further stabilized by contacts with XRCC2, RAD51D, and RAD51C, and is split where RAD51D R255 engages the DNA. The CX3 gate closes, ejecting re-formed dsDNA from the complex (Extended Data Fig. 7e, f). RAD512 RecA-like domain adopts the rotated intermediate state described above (Figs. 3a), and the ATP sites match previous models (Fig. 4e, Supplementary Fig. 8g).This highly specific recognition of the DNA bubble, formed by adding complementary strands one after another, suggests that DX2-CX3 can stay associated with recombination sites where it could serve as a cellular marker of recombination events. This structure can also explain the role of the CX3 module in the later stages of recombination31,40.Paralogs modulate RAD51 activityTo test how paralogs affect RAD51 assembly, we imaged RAD51 on 50 nt ssDNA by cryo-EM with or without paralogs (Extended Data Fig. 7g, h). RAD51 alone formed single and double filaments, as reported for cryo-EM analysis of ATP/ADP-containing RAD51 samples41. Adding BCDX2 or CX3 alone had little effect on filament abundance, whereas BCDX2–CX3, and to a lesser extent DX2–CX3, increased the number of RAD51 filaments. In BCDX2–CX3–RAD51 samples, we also observed free BCDX2 particles, consistent with BCDX2 release from the filament (Supplementary Fig. 9a). Paralog addition also increased RAD51 filament termini, which were capped by either BCDX2–CX3 or DX2–CX3 (Supplementary Fig. 9b, c). BCDX2–CX3 caps were less frequent than DX2–CX3 caps (Extended Data Fig. 7g, h), supporting a model in which BCDX2–CX3 acts as a dynamic loader that disengages after filament growth, whereas DX2–CX3 forms a more stable anchoring cap. Nevertheless, the reconstructions also indicate that BCDX2–CX3 can associate with an extended RAD51 filament.To test paralog effects on RAD51-mediated homology search, we measured D-loop formation in the presence of RPA42. When RPA was added before RAD51, BCDX2-CX3 and CX3 strongly stimulated D-loop formation, whereas BCDX2 or DX2-CX3 had little effect (Extended Data Fig. 8a-c). When RPA was added after RAD51, BCDX2–CX3 and CX3 similarly enhanced D-loop formation more than BCDX2 or DX2-CX3 (Extended Data Fig. 8d-f). Maximal D-loop formation occurred at a 10:1 RAD51:paralog ratio and declined at lower ratios, except for CX3, which remained stimulatory even at a 1:1 ratio. This aligns with the supercomplex model in which CX3 organizes ssDNA into triplets to facilitate homology search (Fig. 1h).The ability of BCDX2-CX3 or CX3 to promote D-loop formation in the presence of RPA contrasts with the absence of stimulation by DX2-CX3 or BCDX2 and highlights distinct roles for each of the paralog complexes. In the absence of RPA, DX2-CX3 was also able to stimulate D-loop formation (Extended Data Fig. 8g-i), consistent with a recent study43, suggesting that while DX2-CX3 can have a stimulatory effect, it might not enable RAD51 to overcome RPA inhibition.BCDX2-CX3 complex assembles in cellsTo validate the existence of BCDX2-CX3 and DX2-CX3 assemblies within a cellular environment, we initially overexpressed CX3 with a 3xFLAG tag on XRCC3 in Expi293F cells (suspension-adapted HEK293 cells). Following a pull-down against the FLAG tag in the presence of nuclease, mass spectrometry (MS) analysis revealed strong enrichment of the BCDX2 components (RAD51B, RAD51D, and XRCC2) alongside the CX3 subunits, with similar levels of enrichment for all paralogs despite CX3 overexpression (Fig. 5a, b). Moreover, we observed enrichment of other related proteins, such as BRCA2, PALB2, and RAD51 itself. This is consistent with reports that RAD51C might interact with BRCA2 in cells and that BRCA2 cooperates with RAD51 paralogs44,45.To directly test our MS analysis and structural model, we performed targeted pull-down experiments using structure-guided mutations within the CX3 complex. As predicted, wild-type CX3 (via FLAG-tag on XRCC3) efficiently pulled down RAD51D, XRCC2, RAD51B, and RAD51 (Fig. 5c). The RAD51CQ267R mutation significantly weakened XRCC3 association with these partners and completely abolished the interaction with RAD51, consistent with our structural predictions and in vitro observations (Fig. 1, Extended Data Fig. 2b). Discussion.This work reshapes the long-standing model of RAD51 paralog function, transitioning from a view of two separate complexes with distinct temporal roles to a unified system of cooperative, dynamic, modular assemblies (Supplementary Fig. 10). Such cooperative interactions provide a rationale for why mutations in any of the five paralog genes can lead to similar cancer predisposition syndromes16,48,49. Specifically, we reveal that human RAD51 paralogs can assemble into two novel architectures at the 5’ end of RAD51 filament segment: a BCDX2–CX3 supercomplex that functions as an ATPase-active loader complex to stimulate RAD51 filament formation, and a RAD51B-independent DX2–CX3 complex that forms an ATPase-inactive anchor and cap at the 5′ end of the filament. These two 5′-end–associated assemblies align with studies of yeast23,24 and nematode21,22,26 RAD51 paralogs, which also localize to the end of the RAD51 filament segment and are proposed to stabilize filament ends and/or modulate filament dynamics. The modular nature of paralog assemblies suggests that the cellular HR machinery can deploy distinct assemblies tailored to the unique structural and temporal demands of diverse DNA lesions, such as double-strand breaks or stalled replication forks50–53.The separate BCDX2 and CX3 complexes observed in earlier studies likely represent snapshots of a highly dynamic lifecycle and intrinsic modular nature of RAD51 paralogs, capturing distinct states and composition of the machinery either before assembly or after the completion of a specific task and subsequent disassembly7–10. While this work was under review, a study detailing DX2-CX3 complex capping RAD51 filaments has been reported43. Our work agrees with the biochemical, structural, and functional characterization of the reported DX2-CX3 complex.By visualizing all five essential paralogs in their functional context within the same structure, we can now map all cancer-associated mutations to critical inter-subunit, protein-DNA, or nucleotide-binding interfaces, providing mechanistic hypotheses for their pathogenicity (Supplementary Fig. 11). This structural and functional blueprint is paramount for guiding the systematic classification of the hundreds of VUSs identified in these genes27.

BCDX2-CX3-RAD51.

An initial reference-free particle picking approach was employed to generate a low-resolution ab initio model. First, particles were picked from the curated micrographs using the blob picker tool, resulting in a stack of 2,150,987 particles. This stack was subjected to multiple rounds of 2D classification to remove junk particles and ice contaminants. To build a high-resolution particle stack, the machine-learning-based picker Topaz was first trained on a manually curated subset of particles and then used to pick particles from both datasets, yielding 922,157 particles from Dataset 1 and 1,065,234 particles from Dataset 2. These particle sets were combined and subjected to two rounds of heterogeneous refinement using the previously generated ab initio model as a reference, which served to remove non-target particles and select for well-defined views. This process resulted in two high-quality, independent particle subsets containing 175,548 and 228,008 particles, respectively.

The two particle subsets were refined independently. Each set was subjected to Non-Uniform (NU) Refinement, which included per-particle CTF refinement (global and local) and reference-based motion correction as implemented in cryoSPARC. This produced two high-resolution reconstructions: one from 62,808 particles at an overall resolution of 2.85 Å, and another from 68,455 particles at an overall resolution of 2.88 Å.

The two refined particle sets were combined into a final stack of 131,263 particles. This combined set was subjected to a final round of NU Refinement, yielding a consensus reconstruction of the BCDX2-CX3-RAD51 complex at an overall resolution of 2.67 Å.

To improve the resolution of individual domains, local refinements were performed. Masks were generated for the BCDX2 body, the CX3 body, and the dimeric RAD51 body. The final particle stack was then subjected to local refinement for each masked region, which improved the resolutions of the BCDX2 body to 2.66 Å, the CX3 body to 2.57 Å, and the 2xRAD51 body to 2.68 Å. A composite map was generated by aligning local refinement onto the composite map and generating a composite map using ChimeraX-1.8. All stated resolutions were estimated using the Fourier Shell Correlation (FSC) at a cutoff of 0.143. The angular distribution of particle views was assessed to ensure good coverage of orientations.
