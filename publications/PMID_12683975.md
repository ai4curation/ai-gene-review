The past decade has revealed the bacterium Wolbachia as the most widespread
symbiont of arthropods and nematodes. Behind this evolutionary success is an
remarkable variety of effects on host biology, ranging from manipulation of
reproduction in favor of females to more classical mutualistic interactions.
Here we discuss the potential of Wolbachia for promoting evolutionary changes in
its hosts.
