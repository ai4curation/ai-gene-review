# HYAL2, a human gene expressed in many cells, encodes a lysosomal hyaluronidase with a novel type of specificity.
**Authors:** Lepperdinger G, Strobl B, Kreil G
**Journal:** J Biol Chem (1998)
**DOI:** [10.1074/jbc.273.35.22466](https://doi.org/10.1074/jbc.273.35.22466)

## Abstract

1. J Biol Chem. 1998 Aug 28;273(35):22466-70. doi: 10.1074/jbc.273.35.22466.

HYAL2, a human gene expressed in many cells, encodes a lysosomal hyaluronidase
with a novel type of specificity.

Lepperdinger G(1), Strobl B, Kreil G.

Author information:
(1)Institute of Molecular Biology, Austrian Academy of Sciences,
Billrothstrabetae 11, A-5020 Salzburg, Austria.

Using Expressed Sequence Tags (ESTs) deposited in the data banks, a cDNA has
been assembled that encodes a protein related to the hyaluronidases from bee
venom and mammalian sperm. Expression of this cDNA yielded a polypeptide termed
HYAL2, which is located in lysosomes. The HYAL2 protein was shown to have
hyaluronidase activity below pH 4. However, it only hydrolyzed hyaluronan of
high molecular mass from umbilical cord, rooster comb, and a Streptococcus
strain. The reaction product was a polysaccharide of about 20 kDa, which was
further hydrolyzed to small oligosaccharides by the sperm hyaluronidase.
Conversely, hyaluronan fragments from vitreous humor, which had a molecular mass
of about 20 kDa, were not cleaved by the HYAL2 enzyme to any detectable extent.
These results provide evidence for the existence of structural domains in
hyaluronan, which are resistant to the action of this enzyme. The structural and
functional implications of these findings are discussed.
