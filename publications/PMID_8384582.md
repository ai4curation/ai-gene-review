# Torso, a receptor tyrosine kinase required for embryonic pattern formation, shares substrates with the sevenless and EGF-R pathways in Drosophila.
**Authors:** Doyle HJ, Bishop JM
**Journal:** Genes Dev (1993)
**DOI:** [10.1101/gad.7.4.633](https://doi.org/10.1101/gad.7.4.633)

## Abstract

1. Genes Dev. 1993 Apr;7(4):633-46. doi: 10.1101/gad.7.4.633.

Torso, a receptor tyrosine kinase required for embryonic pattern formation,
shares substrates with the sevenless and EGF-R pathways in Drosophila.

Doyle HJ(1), Bishop JM.

Author information:
(1)G.W. Hooper Foundation, University of California, San Francisco 94143-0552.

The maternally expressed Drosophila gene torso (tor) is a receptor tyrosine
kinase that, when activated, initiates a signal transduction cascade that is
responsible for the proper differentiation of the terminal, nonsegmented regions
of the embryo. l(1)pole hole, the Drosophila raf-1 serine-threonine kinase
homolog, and corkscrew, a tyrosine phosphatase, have been shown previously to
function in this signal transduction pathway. We have identified other products
in this pathway by carrying out a mutagenesis screen for dominant suppressors of
a tor gain-of-function allele. More than 40 mutations, some of which fall into
seven complementation groups, have been characterized genetically. Two of these
correspond to mutations in ras-1 and Son of sevenless (Sos), which also function
in the sevenless and EGF receptor (Der) tyrosine kinase pathways. The phenotypes
of several other Su(tor) mutations suggest that they also function in other
receptor tyrosine kinase-activated pathways at different times during Drosophila
development.
