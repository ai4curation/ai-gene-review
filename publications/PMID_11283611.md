Comment in
    Nat Cell Biol. 2001 Apr;3(4):E93-5. doi: 10.1038/35070157.

The survival motor neurons (smn) gene in mice is essential for embryonic
viability. In humans, mutation of the telomeric copy of the SMN1 gene causes
spinal muscular atrophy, an autosomal recessive disease. Here we report that the
SMN protein interacts with the zinc-finger protein ZPR1 and that these proteins
colocalize in small subnuclear structures, including gems and Cajal bodies. SMN
and ZPR1 redistribute from the cytoplasm to the nucleus in response to serum.
This process is disrupted in cells from patients with Werdnig-Hoffman syndrome
(spinal muscular atrophy type I) that have SMN1 mutations. Similarly, decreased
ZPR1 expression prevents SMN localization to nuclear bodies. Our data show that
ZPR1 is required for the localization of SMN in nuclear bodies.
