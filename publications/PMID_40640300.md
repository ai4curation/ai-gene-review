Dentin sialophosprotein (DSPP) is cleaved into the N-terminal dentin
sialoprotein (DSP) proteoglycan and the C-terminal dentin phosphoprotein (DPP;
the most acidic proteins in humans). To define the functions of DSP and DPP, we
generated a Dspp-DPP mouse model using CRISPR/Cas9 technology and compared tooth
mineralization in Dspp-DPP and Dspp-/- mice in the C57BL/6 background. In both
mice, the initiation of dentin mineralization was associated with matrix
vesicles. Odontoblasts appeared normal with odontoblastic processes. In Dspp-/-
mice, limited mineralized dentin was observed. In Dspp-DPP/-DPP mice, dentin
globules (calcospherites) with varied mineral density and unmineralized
interglobular dentin were observed throughout the circumpulpal dentin. The area
of predentin was smaller compared to Dspp-/- mice, but larger than wild-type
mice. In Dspp-/- and Dspp-DPP/-DPP mice, enamel formation was comparable to
wild-type. In both mice, Dmp1 expression in differentiating and differentiated
odontoblasts was altered. We propose a model for dentin mineralization in which
DSP, enriched in the peritubular dentin, propagates mineralization within the
hypomineralized calcospherites in the intertubular dentin while DPP is essential
for the maturation of calcospherite mineralization in the circumpulpal dentin.
We conclude that DSP and DPP are dispensable for the initiation of dentin and
enamel mineralization, but critical for circumpulpal dentin mineralization.

Introduction

Biomineralization is a widespread and diverse phenomenon in which the formation of hard tissues is a tightly controlled biological process1. Examples include the silica skeletons in sponges, the calcium carbonate shells of Mollusca, and the calcium phosphate in bones and teeth of vertebrates2. The formation of bone, cementum, dentin, and enamel is guided by osteoblasts, cementoblasts, odontoblasts, and ameloblasts, respectively, under the control of various sets of biomolecules. In dentin formation, an unmineralized collagen-rich extracellular matrix (predentin) is first deposited by ecto-mesenchyme-derived odontoblasts. Calcium phosphate mineral forms on and between the collagen fibrils and coalesces into a continuous mineral layer of dentin that extends up to associate with distal membrane extensions of the overlying, epithelium-derived secretory ameloblasts3 that initiate and elongate enamel ribbons on the surface of dentin4. Secretory ameloblasts extend the enamel ribbons as they retreat to what becomes the future enamel surface and then transition into the maturation stage of enamel formation, where the enamel ribbons thicken, and enamel layer is hardened5. Of all the proteins that play roles in the formation of dentin and enamel, secretory calcium-binding phosphoproteins (SCPPs) are of unique and fundamental importance6.

The SCPP protein family arose from tandem gene duplications of the SPARCL1 gene, which emerged from the SPARC7. There are a total of 22 SCPP genes in human genome7. The human SCPP family can be further divided into two subfamilies8: 5 acidic dentin/bone/cementum SCPPs or SIBLINGs (Small-integrin binding, ligand, N-linked glycoproteins)9and 17 P/Q-rich SCPPs associated with the production of enamel, milk, and saliva. The acidic SCPPs or the SIBLINGs consist of 5 members: SPP1/OPN, IBSP/BSP, MEPE, DMP1 (dentin matrix protein 1), and DSPP (dentin sialophosphoprotein)7,9.

DSPP is expressed predominantly by differentiated odontoblasts, and transiently by early-stage ameloblasts10. The DSPP protein is proteolytically cleaved into the N-terminal DSP (dentin sialoprotein) and the C-terminal DPP (dentin phosphoprotein)11–14. Subsequently DSP is cleaved into smaller fragments15,16. DSP is a proteoglycan17–21. DPP is the most acidic protein known with an isoelectric pH of 1.122 resulting from repetitive Asp-Ser(-Ser) motifs, where aspartates are acidic and many serines are phosphorylated23,24. Results from an in vitro gelatin gel diffusion system suggested that DPP was an initiator and modulator of hydroxyapatite crystal formation in a concentration-dependent manner25and that DSP had limited effects on crystal formation26.

The Dspp null mice that were generated in a mixed 129/SvJ(129);C57BL/6(B6) background displayed thin dentin, enlarged dental pulp, and increased width of predentin27. Several transgenic mouse models have been generated to distinguish the roles of DSP and DPP. A truncated DSP driven by Dspp promoter was inserted into the FVBN mouse genome and crossed with the previously generated Dspp null mice28. A full-length DSP controlled by a 3.6 kb rat Col1a1 promoter was generated in B6 background and bred with the 129;B6 Dspp null mice29. Both mice express transgenic DSP on the Dspp null background but displayed distinct phenotypes. The differences may be attributed to a few factors: strain-specific dental phenotypes30variations in transgene expression caused by localization of transgene insertion sites in the genome, potential tandem insertion of transgenes31,32unclear activities of promoters that are used to drive transgene expression33and confounding phenotypes due to genomic structural variations or disruption of neighboring gene expression because of uncontrolled integrations of transgenes34,35.

We generated a DPP knockout mouse model in the process of applying CRISPR/Cas9 technology to create the Dspp−1fs mouse model36. The modification was introduced to the endogenous mouse Dspp gene, and the founder was bred to the B6 background. We also bred the Dspp null mice into the B6 background. We performed a battery of molecular, histological, and most importantly, ultrastructural characterizations to compare their dental phenotypes to investigate the roles of DSP and DPP in dentin and enamel formation.
