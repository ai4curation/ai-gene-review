# The regulation of the cardiac potassium channel (HERG) by caveolin-1.
**Authors:** Lin J, Lin S, Choy PC, Shen X, Deng C, Kuang S, Wu J, Xu W

## Abstract

1. Biochem Cell Biol. 2008 Oct;86(5):405-15. doi: 10.1139/o08-118.

The regulation of the cardiac potassium channel (HERG) by caveolin-1.

Lin J(1), Lin S, Choy PC, Shen X, Deng C, Kuang S, Wu J, Xu W.

Author information:
(1)Department of Cardiology, Guangdong Provincial People's Hospital, Guangdong
Provincial Cardiovascular Institute, 96 Dongchuan Road, Guangzhou 510080, China.

Protein-protein interaction plays a key role in the regulation of biological
processes. The human potassium (HERG) channel is encoded by the
ether-à-go-go-related gene (herg), and its activity may be regulated by
association with other cellular proteins. To identify cellular proteins that
might play a role in the regulation of the HERG channel, we screened a human
heart cDNA library with the N terminus of HERG using a yeast 2-hybrid system,
and identified caveolin-1 as a potential HERG partner. The interaction between
these 2 proteins was confirmed by coimmunoprecipitation assay, and their
overlapping subcellular localization was demonstrated by fluorescence
immunocytochemistry. The physiologic implication of the protein-protein
interaction was studied in whole-cell patch-clamp electrophysiology experiments.
A significant increase in HERG current amplitude and a faster deactivation of
tail current were observed in HEK293/HERG cells in a membrane lipid rafts
disruption model and caveolin-1 knocked down cells by RNA interference.
Alternatively, when caveolin-1 was overexpressed, the HERG current amplitude was
significantly reduced and the tail current was deactivated more slowly. Taken
together, these data indicate that HERG channels interact with caveolin-1 and
are negatively regulated by this interaction. The finding from this study
clearly demonstrates the regulatory role of caveolin-1 on HERG channels, and may
help to understand biochemical events leading to arrhythmogenesis in the long QT
syndrome in cardiac patients.
