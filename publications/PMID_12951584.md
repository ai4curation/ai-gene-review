## Abstract

1. Nat Rev Cancer. 2003 Sep;3(9):650-65. doi: 10.1038/nrc1169.

The role of FLT3 in haematopoietic malignancies.

Author information:

Normal haematopoietic cells use complex systems to control proliferation,
differentiation and cell death. The control of proliferation is, in part,
accomplished through the ligand-induced stimulation of receptor tyrosine
kinases, which signal to downstream effectors through the RAS pathway. Recently,
mutations in the FMS-like tyrosine kinase 3 (FLT3) gene, which encodes a
receptor tyrosine kinase, have been found to be the most common genetic lesion
in acute myeloid leukaemia (AML), occurring in approximately 25% of cases.
Exploring the mechanism by which these FLT3 mutations cause uncontrolled
proliferation might lead to a better understanding of how cells become cancerous
and provide insights for the development of new drugs.
