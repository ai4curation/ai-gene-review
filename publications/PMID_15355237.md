The mevalonate pathway in insects has two important peculiarities, the absence
of the sterol branch and the synthesis of juvenile hormone (JH), that may have
influenced the mechanisms of regulation. The data available on these mechanisms
indicate that cholesterol does not play a regulatory role and that JH modulates
transcript levels of a number of genes of the mevalonate pathway or can
influence the translatability and/or stability of the transcripts themselves.
These data suggest that the mevalonate pathway in insects can best be
interpreted in terms of coordinated regulation, in which regulators act in
parallel to a number of enzymes, as occurs in the cholesterol-driven pathway in
vertebrates.
