# The serine protease Sp7 is expressed in blood cells and regulates the melanization reaction in Drosophila.
**Authors:** Castillejo-López C, Häcker U
**Journal:** Biochem Biophys Res Commun (2005)
**DOI:** [10.1016/j.bbrc.2005.10.042](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.bbrc.2005.10.042)

## Abstract

1. Biochem Biophys Res Commun. 2005 Dec 16;338(2):1075-82. doi:
10.1016/j.bbrc.2005.10.042. Epub 2005 Oct 21.

The serine protease Sp7 is expressed in blood cells and regulates the
melanization reaction in Drosophila.

Castillejo-López C(1), Häcker U.

Author information:
(1)Department of Experimental Medical Science and Lund Strategic Research Center

26, 22184 Lund, Sweden. Casimiro.Castillejo-Lopez@med.lu.se

Serine proteases play a central role in defense against pathogens by regulating
processes such as blood clotting, melanization of injured surfaces, and
proteolytic activation of signaling pathways involved in innate immunity. Here,
we present the functional characterization of the Drosophila serine protease Sp7
(CG3006) by inducible RNA interference. We show that Sp7 is constitutively
expressed in blood cells during embryonic and larval stages. Silencing of the
gene impairs the melanization reaction upon injury. Our data demonstrate that
Sp7 is required for phenoloxidase activation and its activity is restricted to a
subclass of blood cells, the crystal cells. Transcriptional up-regulation of Sp7
was observed after clean, septic injury and in flies expressing an activated
form of Toll; however, mutations in the Toll or the IMD pathway did not abolish
expression of Sp7, indicating the existence of other regulatory pathways and/or
independent basal transcription.
