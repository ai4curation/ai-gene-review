# pH dependence of the inwardly rectifying potassium channel, Kir5.1, and localization in renal tubular epithelia.
**Authors:** Tucker SJ, Imbrici P, Salvatore L, D'Adamo MC, Pessia M
**Journal:** J Biol Chem (2000)
**DOI:** [10.1074/jbc.C000127200](https://doi.org/10.1074/jbc.C000127200)

## Abstract

1. J Biol Chem. 2000 Jun 2;275(22):16404-7. doi: 10.1074/jbc.C000127200.

pH dependence of the inwardly rectifying potassium channel, Kir5.1, and
localization in renal tubular epithelia.

Tucker SJ(1), Imbrici P, Salvatore L, D'Adamo MC, Pessia M.

Author information:
(1)University Laboratory of Physiology, Parks Road, Oxford OX1 3PT, United
Kingdom. stephen.tucker@physiol.ox.ac.uk

The physiological role of the inwardly rectifying potassium channel, Kir5.1, is
poorly understood, as is the molecular identity of many renal potassium
channels. In this study we have used Kir5.1-specific antibodies to reveal
abundant expression of Kir5.1 in renal tubular epithelial cells, where Kir4.1 is
also expressed. Moreover, we also show that Kir5.1/Kir4.1 heteromeric channel
activity is extremely sensitive to inhibition by intracellular acidification and
that this novel property is conferred predominantly by the Kir5.1 subunit. These
findings suggest that Kir5.1/Kir4.1 heteromeric channels are likely to exist in
vivo and implicate an important and novel functional role for the Kir5.1
subunit.
