Anti-CRISPR (Acr) proteins are natural inhibitors of CRISPR-Cas systems, found
in bacteriophages and other genetic elements. AcrIE3, identified in a
Pseudomonas phage, inactivates the type I-E CRISPR-Cas system in Pseudomonas
aeruginosa by engaging with the Cascade complex. However, its precise inhibition
mechanism has remained elusive. In this study, we present a comprehensive
structural and biochemical analysis of AcrIE3, providing mechanistic insight
into its anti-CRISPR function. Our results reveal that AcrIE3 selectively binds
to the Cas8e subunit of the Cascade complex. The crystal structure of AcrIE3
exhibits an all-helical fold with a negatively charged surface. Through
extensive mutational analyses, we show that AcrIE3 interacts with the
protospacer adjacent motif (PAM) recognition site in Cas8e through its
negatively charged surface residues. These findings enhance our understanding of
the structure and function of type I-E Acr proteins, suggesting PAM interaction
sites as primary targets for divergent Acr inhibitors.
