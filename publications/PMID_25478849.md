Although part of the coenzyme A pathway, vanin 1 (also known as pantetheinase)
sits on the cell surface of many cell types as an ectoenzyme, catalyzing the
breakdown of pantetheine to pantothenic acid (vitamin B5) and cysteamine, a
strong reducing agent. Vanin 1 was initially discovered as a protein involved in
the homing of leukocytes to the thymus. Numerous studies have shown that vanin 1
is involved in inflammation, and more recent studies have shown a key role in
metabolic disease. Here, the X-ray crystal structure of human vanin 1 at 2.25 Å
resolution is presented, which is the first reported structure from the vanin
family, as well as a crystal structure of vanin 1 bound to a specific inhibitor.
These structures illuminate how vanin 1 can mediate its biological roles by way
of both enzymatic activity and protein-protein interactions. Furthermore, it
sheds light on how the enzymatic activity is regulated by a novel allosteric
mechanism at a domain interface.
