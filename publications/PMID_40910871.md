The sarco-endoplasmic reticulum calcium pump (SERCA) is a P-type ATPase that
plays a critical role in intracellular calcium signaling. SERCA maintains the
calcium gradient between the cytosol and the sarco-endoplasmic reticulum, which
is essential for a variety of physiological events including the muscle
contraction-relaxation cycle. In cardiac muscle, SERCA is regulated by
transmembrane peptides phospholamban (PLN) and dwarf open reading frame (DWORF).
These peptides encode the opposing functions of SERCA inhibition by PLN and
SERCA activation by DWORF, though the underlying mechanisms remain unclear.
Herein, we investigated structural elements of DWORF expected to play a role in
SERCA activation. We first measured SERCA activity in the absence and presence
of DWORF variants targeting Leu12 and Pro15. These residues were selected based
on sequence alignment with PLN. Leu12 and Pro15 of DWORF align with the
essential residues Leu31 and Asn34 of PLN, which are required for SERCA
inhibition. We found that both residues are required for SERCA activation by
DWORF and that substitution of Pro15 (to Ala, Asn, or Leu) resulted in potent
inhibition of SERCA. We next investigated the roles of Gly21, Ile23, and Gly25
in SERCA activation and DWORF oligomerization. These residues are part of a
common helix interaction motif, GxxxG (Gly21-Trp-Ile-Val-Gly25) found in DWORF,
which is unique among the regulins. The data suggest that the GxxxG motif does
not play a role in DWORF oligomerization. Instead, this motif appears to
interact with SERCA and provides a smooth interface that promotes activation and
avoids inhibitory interactions with SERCA.

Abstract

The sarco-endoplasmic reticulum calcium pump (SERCA)
is a P-type
ATPase that plays a critical role in intracellular calcium signaling.
SERCA maintains the calcium gradient between the cytosol and the sarco-endoplasmic
reticulum, which is essential for a variety of physiological events
including the muscle contraction-relaxation cycle. In cardiac muscle,
SERCA is regulated by transmembrane peptides phospholamban (PLN) and
dwarf open reading frame (DWORF). These peptides encode the opposing
functions of SERCA inhibition by PLN and SERCA activation by DWORF,
though the underlying mechanisms remain unclear. Herein, we investigated
structural elements of DWORF expected to play a role in SERCA activation.
We first measured SERCA activity in the absence and presence of DWORF
variants targeting Leu 12 and Pro 15 . These residues
were selected based on sequence alignment with PLN. Leu 12 and Pro 15 of DWORF align with the essential residues
Leu 31 and Asn 34 of PLN, which are required for
SERCA inhibition. We found that both residues are required for SERCA
activation by DWORF and that substitution of Pro 15 (to
Ala, Asn, or Leu) resulted in potent inhibition of SERCA. We next
investigated the roles of Gly 21 , Ile 23 , and
Gly 25 in SERCA activation and DWORF oligomerization. These
residues are part of a common helix interaction motif, GxxxG (Gly 21 -Trp-Ile-Val-Gly 25 ) found in DWORF, which is unique
among the regulins. The data suggest that the GxxxG motif does not
play a role in DWORF oligomerization. Instead, this motif appears
to interact with SERCA and provides a smooth interface that promotes
activation and avoids inhibitory interactions with SERCA.

Introduction

Intracellular signaling is an important
mechanism by which cells
respond to a variety of physiological cues. Calcium is one of the
second messengers used to mediate intracellular signaling, which triggers
life-enabling events such as insulin secretion, synaptic transmission,
and muscle contraction. To enable such signaling events, a 2000-fold
gradient is maintained between the low resting-state cytosolic calcium
concentration and the high calcium concentration present in the sarco-endoplasmic
reticulum (SR/ER). This gradient can be mobilized for signaling via
inositol-1,4,5-triphosphate receptor (IP 3 R) or ryanodine
receptor (RyR) calcium channels. These signaling mechanisms are essential
for normal cell physiology, and disordered calcium handling underlies
a diverse array of diseases including cardiomyopathies, skeletal muscle disorders, and neurological diseases. In
cardiac muscle, SERCA maintains SR calcium stores, which is the major
source of calcium for cardiac contractility. While SERCA calcium transport
is essential for all cells, it is particularly important in cardiomyocytes
where defects in SERCA activity or regulation are associated with
cardiomyopathies and heart failure. − This connection with
heart failure has focused attention on SERCA as a possible target
for therapy. An initially promising approach in animal models, increasing
SERCA expression via gene delivery, has
proven challenging in human clinical trials. Nonetheless, the rationale for treating heart failure by improving
calcium transport function remains compelling and novel approaches
are required.

In the heart, SERCA activity is regulated to allow
dynamic calcium
homeostasis and cardiac contractility. A small transmembrane protein,
phospholamban (PLN), is the primary regulatory subunit of SERCA in
cardiac muscle. PLN inhibits SERCA by decreasing its apparent calcium
affinity, reducing both the rate of calcium reuptake and the amount
of calcium transported into the SR. PLN inhibition of SERCA thereby
controls the rate of relaxation and the SR calcium load available
for subsequent contractions, modulating both the lusitropic and inotropic
properties of the heart. SERCA inhibition by PLN is relieved by phosphorylation
via protein kinase A (PKA) and the β-adrenergic pathway. Under resting conditions, PLN is an important
brake that prevents SR calcium overload and the arrhythmogenic consequences. In turn, regulation of PLN by phosphorylation
creates a dynamic SR calcium load that can respond to the sympathetic
need for increased cardiac output, the so-called “fight-or-flight”
response. Thus, there is an extensive pool of SERCA pumps in the SR
membrane and their combined calcium transport capacity can be finely
tuned, either upregulated or downregulated, depending on the requirement
for cardiac contractility.

For many decades, PLN was the only
known regulatory subunit of
SERCA in ventricular muscle. This changed
with the discovery of another small transmembrane subunit of SERCA,
dwarf open reading frame (DWORF). DWORF has weak sequence similarity
to PLN ( Figure ),
and it was found to indirectly activate SERCA by displacing PLN and
relieving inhibition. We subsequently
discovered that DWORF directly activates SERCA by increasing its turnover
rate, even in the absence of PLN. As
an endogenous SERCA activator, DWORF represents an important new therapeutic
target for the treatment of heart failure patients. In this study,
we investigated the activation properties of DWORF using targeted
mutagenesis, coreconstitution of wild-type and mutant forms of DWORF
into proteoliposomes with SERCA, followed by measurement of calcium-dependent
ATPase activity of SERCA. Based on sequence comparison to PLN, we
targeted Leu 12 , Pro 15 , Gly 21 , Ile 23 , and Gly 25 of DWORF. Leu 12 and Pro 15 of DWORF align with the two most important residues for
PLN function, Leu 31 and Asn 34 , respectively. , Gly 21 , Ile 23 , and Gly 25 are part
of a transmembrane helix–helix interaction motif, GxxxG (Gly 21 -Trp-Ile-Val-Gly 25 ), that may play a role in the
function or oligomerization of DWORF. We found that Pro 15 , Gly 21 , and Gly 25 are essential for the structure
and function of DWORF, where multiple variants studied resulted in
loss of function and gain of SERCA inhibition. We suggest that Pro 15 promotes a helix-linker-helix structure of DWORF that avoids
inhibitory interactions with SERCA that are found in PLN. In addition,
we suggest that Gly 21 and Gly 25 interact with
SERCA and provide a smooth interface that promotes activation and
avoids inhibitory interactions with SERCA. These structural elements
of DWORF are optimized for SERCA activation and to avoid SERCA inhibition.

Materials and Methods

Materials

All reagents were of the highest purity available:
octaethylene glycol monododecyl ether (C 12 E 8 ; Barnet Products, Englewood Cliff, NJ); egg yolk phosphatidylcholine
(EYPC), phosphatidylethanolamine (EYPE) and phosphatidic acid (EYPA)
(Avanti Polar Lipids, Alabaster, AL); all reagents used in the coupled
enzyme assay including NADH, ATP, PEP, lactate dehydrogenase, and
pyruvate kinase (Sigma-Aldrich, Oakville, ON Canada).

Coreconstitution of DWORF and SERCA

Recombinant human
DWORF (UniProt DWORF_HUMAN: P0DN84 ) was expressed as a maltose-binding
protein (MBP) fusion with a TEV cleavage site for removal of MBP.
DWORF was purified by a combination of organic extraction (chloroform-isopropanol-water)
and reverse-phase HPLC. Purified DWORF
was stored as lyophilized thin films (63 μg aliquots). SERCA1a
was purified from rabbit skeletal muscle SR. Note that human and rabbit
DWORF are 83% identical and human and rabbit SERCA1a are 84% identical.
Importantly, the inhibitory groove of SERCA where DWORF binds , and the structural elements of DWORF investigated in this study
are completely conserved.

For coreconstitution, lyophilized
DWORF (63 μg) was suspended in a 100 μL mixture of trifluoroethanol–water
(5:1) and mixed with lipids (360 μg EYPC & 40 μg EYPA)
from stock chloroform solutions. The peptide-lipid mixture was dried
to a thin film under nitrogen gas and desiccated under vacuum overnight.
The peptide-lipid thin film was rehydrated in buffer (20 mM imidazole
pH 7.0; 100 mM NaCl; 0.02% NaN 3 ) at 50 °C for 15 min,
cooled to room temperature, and detergent-solubilized by the addition
of C 12 E 8 (0.2% final concentration) with vigorous
vortexing. Detergent-solubilized SERCA1a (UniProt AT2A1_RABIT: P04191 )
was added (300 μg in a total volume of 300 μL) and the
reconstitution was stirred gently at room temperature. Detergent was
slowly removed by the addition of SM-2 Bio-Beads (Bio-Rad, Hercules,
CA) over a 4-h time course (final weight ratio of 25 Bio-Beads to
1 detergent). Following detergent removal, the reconstitution was
centrifuged over a sucrose step-gradient (20% and 50% layers) for
1 h at 100,000 g . The reconstituted proteoliposomes
at the gradient interface were removed, flash-frozen in liquid-nitrogen,
and stored at −80 °C. The final molar ratio was 120 lipids
to 2 DWORF to 1 SERCA. To ensure the proper ratio of ∼2 DWORF
per SERCA in the reconstituted proteoliposomes, quantitative SDS-PAGE
was used with known quantities of SERCA and DWORF proteins as standards. ,,

ATPase Activity Assays of SERCA Reconstitutions

ATPase
activity of the coreconstituted proteoliposomes was measured by a
coupled-enzyme assay over a range of calcium concentrations from 0.1
μM to 10 μM. , The assay has been
adapted to a 96-well format utilizing Synergy 4 (BioTek Instruments)
or SpectraMax M3 (Molecular Devices) microplate readers. Data points
were collected at 340 nm wavelength, with a well volume of 155 μL
containing 10–20 nM SERCA at 30 °C (data points collected
every 28–39 s for 1 h). The reactions were initiated by the
addition of proteoliposomes to the assay solution. The V max (maximal activity) and K Ca (apparent calcium affinity) were determined based on nonlinear least-squares
fitting of the activity data to the Hill equation (Sigma Plot software,
SPSS Inc., Chicago, IL). Errors were calculated as the standard error
of the mean (SEM) for a minimum of three independent reconstitutions.
Statistical comparisons between groups were performed with the one-way
ANOVA (between data sets) followed by the Holm-Sidak test for pairwise
comparison. Differences were considered statistically significant
at p < 0.05.

Denaturing Gel-Electrophoresis of DWORF

Tris-Tricine
gels were run using a Bio-Rad Mini-PROTEAN system with gel-plate dimensions
of 1 × 72 × 100 mm. Polyacrylamide
gel layers were poured as follows: ∼57 mm height for a 16%
separation gel, ∼10 mm height for a 10% spacer gel, and ∼15
mm height for a 4% stacking gel (gel buffer contained 1 M Tris, 0.1%
w/v SDS, pH 8.45). Samples containing 0.1 to 0.5 μg of DWORF
peptide were added to an equal volume of sample buffer (0.1 M Tris,
2% w/v SDS, 32% v/v glycerol, 0.1 M DTT, 0.15 mM bromophenol blue,
pH 6.8) without heating. Gels were run the same day at 140 V until
the dye front was 5 mm from the bottom of the gel (∼1 h; cathode
buffer contained 0.1 M Tris, 0.1 M Tricine, 0.1% w/v SDS, pH 8.25;
anode buffer contained 0.1 M Tris, pH 8.9). Proteins were visualized
by silver staining as described with
a 45 min incubation in fixing solution.

Results

SERCA Activity in the Presence of DWORF

Previous studies
demonstrated that DWORF is a direct activator of SERCA. DWORF was found to increase the turnover rate
and maximal activity ( V max ) of SERCA with
no effect on the apparent calcium affinity ( K Ca ). Using the same experimental approach, wild-type DWORF
was coreconstituted into membrane vesicles with SERCA and the calcium-dependent
ATPase activity was measured ( Figure ). As observed previously, DWORF increased the V max of SERCA ∼1.7-fold with no effect
on the K Ca of SERCA ( Table ). Thus, SERCA in the absence of DWORF served as a negative
control and SERCA in the presence of wild-type human DWORF served
as a positive control for all further experiments.

Targeted Mutagenesis of DWORF Pro 15

We previously
suggested that Pro 15 is an important residue for DWORF
function based on sequence alignments with PLN and SLN. , Asn 34 of PLN and Asn 11 of SLN are essential
for inhibition of SERCA, and these residues align with Pro 15 of DWORF. Considering the opposing functions of SERCA inhibition
by PLN and activation by DWORF, Pro 15 appeared to be an
important structural element of DWORF. We tested the role of this
residue by first mutating Pro 15 to asparagine and we hypothesized
that the activation behavior of wild-type DWORF should convert to
inhibitory behavior for the Pro 15 -Asn substitution. Pro 15 -Asn DWORF was coreconstituted into proteoliposomes with
SERCA and the calcium-dependent ATPase activity was measured ( Figure ). We observed superinhibition
of SERCA in the presence of Pro 15 -Asn DWORF. Compared to
SERCA in the absence and presence of wild-type DWORF, the maximal
activity of SERCA was strongly suppressed in the presence of Pro 15 -Asn DWORF ( V max values of 4.1,
6.9, and 2.4 μmol/min/mg, respectively). The apparent calcium
affinity of SERCA was also decreased in the presence of the Pro 15 -Asn variant ( K Ca values of 0.63
μM calcium), compared to SERCA in the absence and presence of
wild-type DWORF ( K Ca values of 0.42 and
0.48 μM calcium, respectively). As hypothesized, Pro 15 -Asn DWORF inhibited SERCA, though the observed superinhibition was
unexpected ( Table ). Interestingly, the Pro 15 -Asn variant of DWORF increased
the cooperativity for calcium binding, which is a characteristic feature
of PLN. Pro 15 -Asn DWORF altered the Hill coefficient and
increased the cooperativity for calcium binding to SERCA compared
to wild-type DWORF ( n H of 2.4 ± 0.5
compared to 1.8 ± 0.2).

Two additional substitutions were also tested at
this position,
Pro 15 -Ala and Pro 15 -Leu ( Figure ). This latter variant has been identified
through genetic testing with unknown clinical significance (dbSNP

superinhibitors of SERCA, though the impact on SERCA kinetic parameters
is different. Like the Pro 15 -Asn DWORF variant, the Pro 15 -Ala and Pro 15 -Leu variants impacted both the K Ca and V max of SERCA
( Table ). The maximal
activity of SERCA was suppressed in the presence of the Pro 15 -Ala and Pro 15 -Leu variants of DWORF ( V max values of 2.4 and 3.5 μmol/min/mg, respectively),
as compared to SERCA in the absence and presence of wild-type DWORF
( V max values of 4.1 and 6.9 μmol/min/mg,
respectively). The apparent calcium affinity of SERCA was also decreased
in the presence of the Pro 15 -Ala and Pro 15 -Leu
variants ( K Ca values of 0.74 and 0.91
μM calcium, respectively), compared to SERCA in the absence
and presence of wild-type DWORF ( K Ca values
of 0.42 and 0.48 μM calcium, respectively). Compared to wild-type
and Pro 15 -Asn DWORF, the Pro 15 -Ala and Pro 15 -Leu variants decreased the cooperativity for calcium binding
to SERCA ( n H 1.2 ± 0.1 and 1.2 ±

Thus, all three mutants, Pro 15 -Asn, Pro 15 -Ala,
and Pro 15 -Leu, were potent inhibitors of SERCA that impacted
both the V max and K Ca of SERCA.

Targeted Mutagenesis of DWORF Leu 12

With
the superinhibitory characteristics of the Pro 15 mutants
described above, this suggested that the sequence alignment between
PLN, SLN, and DWORF was correct. To further
test this, we focused on Leu 12 of DWORF which is predicted
to align with Leu 31 of PLN and Leu 8 of SLN.
Together, Leu 31 and Asn 34 of PLN and Leu 8 and Asn 11 of SLN are essential residues for inhibition
of SERCA, as alanine substitution of any of these residues results
in complete loss of function. ,, Thus, the Leu-x-x-Asn sequence ( Figure ) seems to be an important structural element
for PLN and SLN inhibition of SERCA. Since DWORF is an activator of
SERCA, not an inhibitor, we wondered if Leu 12 would also
be important for SERCA activation by DWORF. SERCA was coreconstituted
into proteoliposomes in the absence and presence of Leu 12 -Ala DWORF, which resulted in partial loss of activation ( Figure and Table ). The V max value for SERCA in the presence of Leu 12 -Ala
DWORF was 5.0 μmol/min/mg. This value was close to that determined
for SERCA alone (4.1 μmol/min/mg) and distinct from that determined
for SERCA in the presence of wild-type DWORF (6.9 μmol/min/mg).
Thus, Leu 12 of DWORF appears to be important for SERCA
activation, mirroring the importance of Leu 31 and Leu 8 in SERCA inhibition by PLN and SLN. In addition, the inhibitory
dyad of Leu 31 and Asn 34 in PLN and Leu 8 and Asn 11 in SLN appears to be repurposed as a Leu 12 and Pro 15 dyad in DWORF for SERCA activation.

Targeted Mutagenesis of DWORF Gly 21 , Ile 23 , and Gly 25

A seemingly unique structural element
of DWORF is the GxxxG motif consisting of residues Gly 21 -Trp 22 -Ile 23 -Val 24 -Gly 25 ( Figure ). The GxxxG
motif was originally identified as a dimerization motif in glycophorin
A, though we now know it is a common
sequence of residues that favors helix–helix interactions in
membrane proteins. The GxxxG sequence in DWORF aligns with Ile 40 -Cys 41 -Leu 42 -Leu 43 -Leu 44 in PLN. This region of PLN is important for oligomerization,
where Ile 40 and Leu 44 are part of a leucine-isoleucine
zipper that stabilizes the PLN pentamer. , Interestingly, Ile 40 and Leu 44 of PLN align
with Gly 21 and Gly 25 of DWORF, suggesting that
PLN and DWORF may have distinct oligomerization motifs localized to
the same region of the peptides. Therefore, we hypothesized that the
Gly 21 -Trp 22 -Ile 23 -Val 24 -Gly 25 sequence is important for DWORF function and oligomerization.

We first examined the functional importance of Gly 21 , Gly 25 , as well as Ile 23 in the center of
this motif. Ile 23 of DWORF aligns with Leu 42 of PLN ( Figure ),
which is another residue involved in PLN inhibition of SERCA. , A Leu 42 -Ala substitution in PLN results in partial loss
of function. ,, SERCA was coreconstituted into proteoliposomes in the absence and
presence of Gly 21 -Ala, Ile 23 -Ala, and Gly 25 -Ala mutants of DWORF ( Figures and ). Another variant,
Gly 25 -Asp, was identified through genetic testing (dbSNP
rs1200203728; Figure ). The calcium-dependent ATPase activity assays indicated that the
maximal activity ( V max ) of SERCA in the
presence of the Gly 21 -Ala mutant was similar to wild-type
DWORF, though Gly 21 -Ala gained inhibitory function by decreasing
the apparent calcium affinity of SERCA ( Table ). The K Ca values
for SERCA in the presence of wild-type and Gly 21 -Ala DWORF
were 0.48 and 1.38 μM calcium, respectively. The Ile 23 -Ala mutant resulted in partial loss of function, with V max and K Ca values that were
similar to SERCA in the presence of wild-type DWORF ( Table ; K Ca values of 0.57 and 0.48 μM calcium, respectively; V max values of 5.7 and 6.9 μmol/mg/min,
respectively). The final two mutants, Gly 25 -Ala and Gly 25 -Asp, emphasized the importance of this particular residue
in tuning the balance between SERCA activation and SERCA inhibition.
Both mutants were inhibitory, with SERCA K Ca values of 0.87 and 0.66 μM calcium, respectively (compared
to 0.48 μM calcium for SERCA in the presence of wild-type DWORF).
Similarly, the V max values indicated lack
of activation of SERCA by Gly 25 -Asp DWORF (4.5 μmol/min/mg)
and inhibition of SERCA by Gly 25 -Ala DWORF (3.5 μmol/min/mg).
Thus, Gly 21 and Gly 25 were clearly important
for DWORF activation behavior and to avoid inhibitory interactions
with SERCA.

4.

The Gly 21 -Ala and Gly 25 -Ala
mutants demonstrated
the sensitivity of DWORF and SERCA to amino acid substitution at these
positions. However, the GxxxG transmembrane helix interaction motif
is also known as the (small)­xxx­(small) motif because of the common
occurrence of other small residues such as alanine and serine. To
further understand the role in glycines in SERCA activation by DWORF,
we generated phenylalanine substitutions of each residue (Gly 21 -Phe and Gly 25 -Phe), as well as the double mutant
(Gly 21 -Phe, Gly 25 -Phe). The Gly 21 -Phe mutant resulted in complete loss of function, with V max and K Ca values that were
similar to SERCA alone ( Figure and Table ). Interestingly, the Gly 25 -Phe mutant retained the ability
to activate SERCA and was most similar to SERCA in the presence of
wild-type DWORF ( V max values of 5.5 and
6.9 μmol/min/mg; K Ca values of 0.46
and 0.48 μM calcium, respectively). Finally, the double mutant
Gly 21 -Phe, Gly 25 -Phe resulted in complete loss
of function compared to wild-type DWORF ( V max values of 3.7 and 6.9 μmol/min/mg, respectively) and gain
of inhibitory function ( K Ca values of
0.77 and 0.48 μM calcium, respectively). Considering these data,
the overall trend for the alanine substitutions of Gly 21 and Gly 25 was gain of SERCA inhibition, while the trend
for the phenylalanine substitutions was loss of SERCA activation.

Oligomerization of DWORF Mutants

Our initial hypothesis
was that the GxxxG motif of DWORF contributes to oligomer formation.
SDS-PAGE has been widely used as an indicator of PLN oligomerization,
with PLN monomers and pentamers (and sometimes dimers, trimers and
tetramers) clearly visible based on electrophoretic mobility. In particular,
SDS-PAGE has been used as a facile readout for changes in PLN oligomerization
caused by mutations (e.g., alanine-scanning mutagenesis of PLN , ). To examine the oligomeric state of DWORF, we performed SDS-PAGE
and examined the electrophoretic behavior of the peptide ( Figure ). Wild-type DWORF
and the mutants described above were subjected to SDS-PAGE on a 16%
gel under denaturing conditions. All peptides formed two main species
observable by SDS-PAGE and silver staining, which were assumed to
be monomers and dimers of DWORF. However, weaker additional bands
were observed that suggested lower- and higher-order oligomeric species.
In order to characterize a monomeric form of DWORF, we synthesized
a peptide with a scrambled amino acid sequence with the assumption
that it would not form oligomers. The scrambled peptide migrated close
to the correct molecular weight of ∼3.7 kDa by SDS-PAGE (scramb
in Figure A). This
migration behavior for the scrambled peptide indicated that the two
prominent bands observed for DWORF likely corresponded to a dimer
and tetramer. In support of this conclusion, one of the DWORF mutants
studied, Gly 25 -Ala, migrated as a ladder of bands consistent
with a monomer, dimer, trimer, tetramer, pentamer, and hexamer (G25A
in Figure B).

6.

With this interpretation, all DWORF variants in
the present study
were capable of forming dimers. However, some of the mutant peptides
exhibited slight variations in migration behavior compared to wild-type
DWORF, with the most notable being Gly 25 -Asp (G25D in Figure A). Our interpretation
of this variant is that it still forms dimers and tetramers, though
the migration behavior is faster (smaller apparent molecular weight)
due to the added negative charge. We have previously observed anomalous
migration behavior for peptides by SDS-PAGE, as well as changes in migration behavior with amino acid substitution. Nonetheless, the Gly 21 and Gly 25 substitutions were still capable of forming dimers, as well
as tetramers in most cases. Even the double phenylalanine substitution
(Gly 21 -Phe, Gly 25 -Phe; G21F/G25F in Figure A) was capable of
forming stable dimers by SDS-PAGE. Thus, the GxxxG motif of DWORF
did not appear to be a major contributor to oligomerization. Surprisingly,
the Ile 23 -Ala variant appeared to yield the most monomers
by SDS-PAGE (I23A in Figure A), as well as general destabilization of the dimer and tetramer.
This suggested that a different oligomerization motif may be involved
in DWORF oligomerization.

Mimicking PLN

The substitution of Ile 23 had
the largest impact on DWORF dimerization, suggesting that DWORF may
possess a different oligomerization motif. To begin to test this,
we substituted the glycine residues of DWORF with the corresponding
residues in PLN. Gly 21 and Gly 25 in DWORF align
with Ile 40 and Leu 44 in PLN ( Figure ). Ile 40 and Leu 44 have been implicated in the Leu-Ile zipper that stabilizes
the pentameric from of PLN. We generated
Gly 21 -Ile and Gly 25 -Leu variants of DWORF and
first tested their ability to activate SERCA ( Figure ). The Gly 21 -Ile variant resulted
in loss of SERCA activation and gain of SERCA inhibition, and the
Gly 25 -Leu variant resulted in complete loss of function
( Table ). We next
tested the oligomeric state of these variants as observed by SDS-PAGE
( Figure , insets).
Interestingly, both variants formed a stable dimer and tetramer, with
no detectable monomer compared to wild-type DWORF ( Figure A).

Discussion

Pro 15 Is Essential for SERCA Activation

SERCA activity is altered by a wide variety of highly divergent transmembrane
peptides including the regulins, sarcolambans, and even model peptides. , Most peptides are inhibitory and the most common feature is a reduction
in the maximal activity of SERCA. Many peptides also alter the apparent
calcium affinity of SERCA, with PLN and SLN being well-characterized
examples. Therefore, as a transmembrane peptide regulator of SERCA,
DWORF stands out as the only known activator of SERCA–i.e.,
DWORF increases the maximal activity of SERCA without altering the
apparent calcium affinity. This unique
property of DWORF has garnered interest as a gene therapy for the
treatment of cardiomyopathies and muscle
wasting diseases.

In the present
study, our goal was to identify structural elements of DWORF that
are required for SERCA activation. Based on a sequence alignment ( Figure and ref ) and previous studies
of DWORF structure and function, , we focused
on Pro 15 as an essential structural feature of DWORF. Pro 15 of DWORF induces a helix-linker-helix structure of DWORF
that plays a major role in SERCA activation. In PLN and SLN, this
residue is an asparagine that is essential for SERCA inhibition (Asn 34 and Asn 11 , respectively). Thus, the presence
of Pro 15 in DWORF removes an inhibitory interaction found
in PLN and SLN and replaces it with a structural element conducive
for SERCA activation. The first variant tested was a Pro 15 -Asn mutant of DWORF that was expected to become a PLN-like inhibitor
of SERCA ( Figure ).
While this mutant of DWORF was found to inhibit SERCA, the inhibitory

We next generated Pro 15 -Ala and Pro 15 -Leu
mutants of DWORF based on a standard alanine substitution and a human
genetic variant, respectively. These variants were potent inhibitors
of SERCA, altering both the maximal activity ( V max ) and apparent calcium affinity ( K Ca ) of SERCA ( Figure ). This was consistent with the role of Pro 15 in
promoting an appropriate structure of DWORF for SERCA activation,
where the variants tested eliminated activation and promoted SERCA
inhibition. It is instructive to consider these residues–proline,
alanine, and leucine–in terms of their propensity to promote
secondary structure in DWORF. Alanine and leucine have the highest
propensity to form an α-helix, while proline has the lowest
propensity. This suggests that Pro 15 -Ala and Pro 15 -Leu may be more helical forms of
DWORF. To test this hypothesis, we used AlphaFold to generate structural
models of wild-type, Pro 15 -Asn, Pro 15 -Ala, and
Pro 15 -Leu DWORF ( Figure ). All Pro 15 variants of DWORF were predicted
to be more helical than wild-type DWORF supporting the concept that
the inhibitory variants are more helical. In the crystal structure
of the SERCA-PLN inhibitory complex, this
region of PLN is helical. Thus, a more helical form of DWORF emulates
the structure of PLN and results in SERCA inhibition.

GxxxG Motif of DWORF Interacts with SERCA

DWORF is
thought to bind to the inhibitory groove of SERCA and displace PLN,
where the inhibitory groove is formed by transmembrane segments M2,
M6 and M9. However, in binding to the inhibitory groove of SERCA,
DWORF must avoid interactions that contribute to SERCA inhibition. , In part, this is facilitated by Pro 15 and the helix-linker-helix
structure of DWORF. Compared to the PLN inhibitory interactions of
Leu 31 and Asn 34 with SERCA, the corresponding
residues of Leu 12 and Pro 15 in DWORF do not
interact with SERCA. Nonetheless, the transmembrane helix of DWORF
binds to the inhibitory groove , and a large number
of nonspecific transmembrane peptides are known to inhibit SERCA. , This suggested that there must be additional structural elements
that dictate how DWORF interacts with the inhibitory groove while
avoiding SERCA inhibition. A candidate helix–helix interaction
motif in DWORF that we wished to understand was the GxxxG motif, Gly 21 -Trp-Ile-Val-Gly 25 ( Figure ). The GxxxG motif, or (small)­xxx­(small)
motif, is a common sequence of residues found to mediate helix–helix
interactions in membrane proteins. Much of what we know about this
motif comes from the classic example of the glycophorin A transmembrane
dimer. A combination of biochemical and structural studies revealed
the role of the glycine residues in dimer formation, where they provided
a smooth interface that allows close packing of the transmembrane
α-helices. ,, Interestingly, the transmembrane domain of glycophorin A forms dimers
observable by SDS-PAGE and the dimers can be disrupted by mutagenesis.

With this in mind, we expected that the GxxxG motif may play a
role in DWORF dimerization and SERCA
activation. We also expected that dimer
formation would be sensitive to mutation and observable by SDS-PAGE.
We generated alanine and phenylalanine variants of Gly 21 and Gly 25 and tested their ability to form oligomeric
species ( Figure A).
Unlike glycophorin A and PLN, the DWORF variants did not significantly
alter the migration pattern by SDS-PAGE, with dimers and tetramers
being the prevalent species observed. Since the DWORF variants in
the GxxxG motif did not significantly impact the oligomeric state
of DWORF, we considered their effects on SERCA activation. Overall,
the variants had a greater impact on DWORF function and SERCA activation
( Table ) than they
did on the oligomeric state of DWORF ( Figure ). The alanine and phenylalanine variants
of Gly 21 and Gly 25 resulted in either loss of
DWORF function or gain of SERCA inhibition ( Figures and ), indicating
that even the substitution of another small amino acid was not tolerated.
Importantly, the gain of inhibition observed for the Gly 21 -Ala and Gly 25 -Ala variants suggested that the alanine
substitutions create additional contacts with SERCA that resulted
in inhibition. Comparing the effects of the alanine and phenylalanine
substitutions on the V max of SERCA revealed
opposite effects for Gly 21 and Gly 25 of DWORF.
The Gly 21 -Ala and Gly 25 -Phe variants of DWORF
retained the ability to activate SERCA (increased V max ; Table ), while the Gly 21 -Phe and Gly 25 -Ala variants
of DWORF lost the ability to activate SERCA (decreased V max ). The double mutant, Gly 21 -Phe and Gly 25 -Phe, most closely resembled the Gly 21 -Phe variant.
These different requirements for Gly 21 and Gly 25 of DWORF likely reflect distinct interactions with SERCA and a dominant
role for Gly 21 . Overall, the data suggests that the GxxxG
motif of DWORF interacts with SERCA. In combination with the helix-linker-helix
structure of DWORF promoted by Pro 15 , the smooth interface
provided by the GxxxG motif avoids inhibitory interactions with SERCA.

Oligomerization of DWORF

While the GxxxG motif did
not appear to play a role in DWORF oligomerization, one of the variants
studied exhibited more DWORF monomers by SDS-PAGE ( Figure ). Compared to wild-type DWORF,
the Ile 23 -Ala variant appeared to destabilize the dimer
and tetramer and yielded more monomers. This unexpected result led
us to consider that DWORF may contain a Leu-Ile zipper motif like
PLN. While the focus of this study was not on the leucine and isoleucine
residues of DWORF, we nonetheless generated Gly 21 -Ile and
Gly 25 -Leu variants to further investigate the behavior
of the Ile 23 -Ala variant. Based on the sequence alignment
( Figure ), Gly 21 and Gly 25 of DWORF correspond to Ile 40 and Leu 44 of PLN. These latter residues are involved
in PLN pentamer formation. As evaluated
by SDS-PAGE, both variants were similar to wild-type DWORF except
for the absence of a band corresponding to the monomer ( Figure ). This suggested that the
main oligomeric states of DWORF, dimers and tetramers, were stabilized
by these substitutions. In the functional assays, the Gly 21 -Ile and Gly 25 -Leu variants had a larger impact on DWORF
function, which was comparable to the other glycine variants studied.
The Gly 21 -Ile variant resulted in SERCA inhibition and
the Gly 25 -Leu variant resulted in loss of function ( Table ). These results support
the notion that the GxxxG motif of DWORF interacts with SERCA and
that different structural elements are responsible for the oligomerization
of DWORF.

Conclusions

In the present study, five residues of
DWORF were targeted for
mutagenesis and 13 variants were tested for their ability to activate
SERCA and self-associate into DWORF oligomers. Structural elements
of DWORF that are important for SERCA activation versus inhibition
were inferred based on assessment of SERCA activity, denaturing gel-electrophoresis
of DWORF, and structural predictions of DWORF. Our goal was to identify
residues that mediate SERCA–DWORF or DWORF–DWORF interactions.
In this context, the results implicate Pro 15 and the GxxxG
motif as structure elements of DWORF that promote SERCA activation.
The GxxxG motif of DWORF did not appear to contribute to oligomerization.
Instead, the SERCA-DWORF interface was found to be exquisitely sensitive
to changes in structure. Changes in the helical structure of DWORF
(e.g., Pro 15 -Ala) or small changes in side chain structure
(e.g., Gly 21 -Ala) resulted in potent inhibition of SERCA.
We conclude that the sequence and structure of DWORF are optimized
for SERCA activation and to avoid SERCA inhibition.
