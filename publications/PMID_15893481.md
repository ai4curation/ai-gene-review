# Catalases protect cellular proteins from oxidative modification in Saccharomyces cerevisiae.
**Authors:** Lushchak VI, Gospodaryov DV
**Journal:** Cell Biol Int (2005)
**DOI:** [10.1016/j.cellbi.2004.11.001](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cellbi.2004.11.001)

## Abstract

1. Cell Biol Int. 2005 Mar;29(3):187-92. doi: 10.1016/j.cellbi.2004.11.001.

Catalases protect cellular proteins from oxidative modification in Saccharomyces
cerevisiae.

Lushchak VI(1), Gospodaryov DV.

Author information:
(1)Department of Biochemistry, Vassyl Stefanyk Precarpathian National
University, 57 Shevchenko Str., 76025 Ivano-Frankivsk, Ukraine.
lushchak@pu.if.ua

The yeast Saccharomyces cerevisiae cells had higher antioxidant enzyme
activities under growth in ethanol than that in glucose as a carbon and energy
source. The correlations between catalase activity and protein carbonyl level
(r(2)=0.857), between catalase and glucose-6-phosphate dehydrogenase activities
(r(2)=0.924) and between protein carbonyl levels and glucose-6-phosphate
dehydrogenase activity (r(2)=0.988) under growth in ethanol were found. Growing
in ethanol the strain deficient in cytosolic and peroxisomal catalases had
7.1-fold higher level of carbonyl proteins than that of wild-type strain. Our
data suggest that in vivo catalases may protect glucose-6-phosphate
dehydrogenase against oxidative inactivation.
