# Galactose induction of the GAL1 gene requires conditional degradation of the Mig2 repressor.
**Authors:** Lim MK, Siew WL, Zhao J, Tay YC, Ang E, Lehming N
**Journal:** Biochem J (2011)
**DOI:** [10.1042/BJ20102034](https://doi.org/10.1042/BJ20102034)

## Abstract

1. Biochem J. 2011 May 1;435(3):641-9. doi: 10.1042/BJ20102034.

Galactose induction of the GAL1 gene requires conditional degradation of the
Mig2 repressor.

Lim MK(1), Siew WL, Zhao J, Tay YC, Ang E, Lehming N.

Author information:
(1)Department of Microbiology, Yong Loo Lin School of Medicine, National
University of Singapore, 5 Science Drive 2, Block MD4, Singapore 117597.

Skp1 an essential component of the SCF (Skp1/cullin/F-box) E3 ubiquitin ligases,
which target proteins for degradation by the 26S proteasome. We generated a
skp1dM mutant strain that is defective for galactose induction of the GAL1 gene
and we have found that galactose-induced protein degradation of the repressor
Mig2 is defective in this strain. Mig2 degradation was also abolished in cells
lacking the protein kinase Snf1 and the F-box protein Das1, suggesting that Snf1
triggers galactose-induced protein degradation of Mig2 by SCFDas1. Chromatin
immunoprecipitation showed that Mig2 associates with the GAL1 promoter upon the
galactose-induced exit of Mig1 in skp1dM cells, but not in wild-type cells,
suggesting that the conditional degradation of Mig2 is required to prevent it
from binding to the GAL1 promoter under inducing conditions. A galactose-stable
deletion derivative of Mig2 caused a strong Mig (multi-copy inhibition of GAL
gene expression) phenotype, confirming that galactose induction of the GAL1 gene
requires the degradation of the repressor Mig2. Our results shed new light on
the conflicting reports about the functional role of the degradation of
transcriptional activators and indicate that gene expression studies interfering
with proteasome degradation should take the stabilization of potential
repressors into account.
