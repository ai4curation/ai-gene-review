Proper organization of the mitotic spindle is key to genetic stability, but
molecular components of inter-microtubule bridges that crosslink kinetochore
fibers (K-fibers) are still largely unknown. Here we identify a
kinase-independent function of class II phosphoinositide 3-OH kinase α
(PI3K-C2α) acting as limiting scaffold protein organizing clathrin and TACC3
complex crosslinking K-fibers. Downregulation of PI3K-C2α causes spindle
alterations, delayed anaphase onset, and aneuploidy, indicating that PI3K-C2α
expression is required for genomic stability. Reduced abundance of PI3K-C2α in
breast cancer models initially impairs tumor growth but later leads to the
convergent evolution of fast-growing clones with mitotic checkpoint defects. As
a consequence of altered spindle, loss of PI3K-C2α increases sensitivity to
taxane-based therapy in pre-clinical models and in neoadjuvant settings.
