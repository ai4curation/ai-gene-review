Cyclin-dependent kinase-5 (cdk5)/p35 and protein phosphatase-1 (PP1) are two
major enzymes that control a variety of physiological processes within the
nervous system including neuronal differentiation, synaptic plasticity and
axonal transport. Defective cdk5/p35 and PP1 function are also implicated in
several major human neurodegenerative diseases. Cdk5/p35 and the catalytic
subunit of PP1 (PP1C) both bind to the brain-enriched, serine-threonine kinase
lemur tyrosine kinase-2 (LMTK2). Moreover, LMTK2 phosphorylates PP1C on
threonine-320 (PP1Cthr³²⁰) to inhibit its activity. Here, we demonstrate that
LMTK2 is phosphorylated on serine-1418 (LMTK2ser¹⁴¹⁸) by cdk5/p35 and present
evidence that this regulates its ability to phosphorylate PP1Cthr³²⁰. We thus
describe a new signalling pathway within the nervous system that links cdk5/p35
with PP1C and which has implications for a number of neuronal functions and
neuronal dysfunction.
