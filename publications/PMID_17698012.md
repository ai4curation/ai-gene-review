# C. elegans RPM-1 regulates axon termination and synaptogenesis through the Rab GEF GLO-4 and the Rab GTPase GLO-1.
**Authors:** Grill B, Bienvenut WV, Brown HM, Ackley BD, Quadroni M, Jin Y
**Journal:** Neuron (2007)
**DOI:** [10.1016/j.neuron.2007.07.009](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2007.07.009)

## Abstract

1. Neuron. 2007 Aug 16;55(4):587-601. doi: 10.1016/j.neuron.2007.07.009.

C. elegans RPM-1 regulates axon termination and synaptogenesis through the Rab
GEF GLO-4 and the Rab GTPase GLO-1.

Grill B(1), Bienvenut WV, Brown HM, Ackley BD, Quadroni M, Jin Y.

Author information:
(1)Department of Molecular, Cell and Developmental Biology, Sinsheimer
Laboratories, University of California, Santa Cruz, Santa Cruz, CA 95064, USA.

C. elegans RPM-1 (for Regulator of Presynaptic Morphology) is a member of a
conserved protein family that includes Drosophila Highwire and mammalian Pam and
Phr1. These are large proteins recently shown to regulate synaptogenesis through
E3 ubiquitin ligase activities. Here, we report the identification of an
RCC1-like guanine nucleotide exchange factor, GLO-4, from mass spectrometry
analysis of RPM-1-associated proteins. GLO-4 colocalizes with RPM-1 at
presynaptic terminals. Loss of function in glo-4 or in its target Rab GTPase,
glo-1, causes neuronal defects resembling those in rpm-1 mutants. We show that
the glo pathway functions downstream of rpm-1 and acts in parallel to fsn-1, a
partner of RPM-1 E3 ligase function. We find that late endosomes are
specifically disorganized at the presynaptic terminals of glo-4 mutants. Our
data suggest that RPM-1 positively regulates a Rab GTPase pathway to promote
vesicular trafficking via late endosomes.
