# Structural and redox properties of the leaderless DsbE (CcmG) protein: both active-site cysteines of the reduced form are involved in its function in the Escherichia coli periplasm.
**Authors:** Li Q, Hu HY, Wang WQ, Xu GJ
**Journal:** Biol Chem (2001)
**DOI:** [10.1515/BC.2001.203](https://doi.org/10.1515/BC.2001.203)

## Abstract

1. Biol Chem. 2001 Dec;382(12):1679-86. doi: 10.1515/BC.2001.203.

Structural and redox properties of the leaderless DsbE (CcmG) protein: both
active-site cysteines of the reduced form are involved in its function in the
Escherichia coli periplasm.

Li Q(1), Hu HY, Wang WQ, Xu GJ.

Author information:
(1)Institute of Biochemistry and Cell Biology, Shanghai Institutes for
Biological Sciences, Chinese Academy of Sciences, People's Republic of China.

The thiol/disulfide oxidoreductases play important roles in ensuring the correct
formation of disulfide bonds, of which the DsbE protein, also called CcmG, is
the one implicated in electron transfer for cytochrome c maturation in the
periplasm of Escherichia coli. The soluble, N-terminally truncated DsbE was
overexpressed and purified to homogeneity. Here we report the structural and
redox properties of the leaderless form (DsbEL-). During the redox reaction, the
protein undergoes a structural transformation resulting in a more stable reduced
form, but this form shows very low reactivity in thiol/ disulfide exchange of
cysteine residues and low activity in accelerating the reduction of insulin. The
standard redox potential (E'0) for the active thiol/ disulfide was determined to
be -0.186 V; only one of the two cysteines (Cys80) was suggested to be the
active residue in the redox reaction. From the aspect of biochemical properties,
DsbE can be regarded as a weak reductant in the Escherichia coli periplasm. This
implies that the function of DsbE in cytochrome c maturation can be ascribed to
its active-site cysteines and the structure of the reduced form.
