Here, we identify coiled-coil domain-containing protein 13 (Ccdc13) in a
genome-wide RNA interference screen for regulators of genome stability. We
establish that Ccdc13 is a newly identified centriolar satellite protein that
interacts with PCM1, Cep290 and pericentrin and prevents the accumulation of DNA
damage during mitotic transit. Depletion of Ccdc13 results in the loss of
microtubule organisation in a manner similar to PCM1 and Cep290 depletion,
although Ccdc13 is not required for satellite integrity. We show that
microtubule regrowth is enhanced in Ccdc13-depleted cells, but slowed in cells
that overexpress Ccdc13. Furthermore, in serum-starved cells, Ccdc13 localises
to the basal body, is required for primary cilia formation and promotes the
localisation of the ciliopathy protein BBS4 to both centriolar satellites and
cilia. These data highlight the emerging link between DNA damage response
factors, centriolar and peri-centriolar satellites and cilia-associated proteins
and implicate Ccdc13 as a centriolar satellite protein that functions to promote
both genome stability and cilia formation.
