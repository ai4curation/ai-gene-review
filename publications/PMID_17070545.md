# The ribosomal stalk binds to translation factors IF2, EF-Tu, EF-G and RF3 via a conserved region of the L12 C-terminal domain.
**Authors:** Helgstrand M, Mandava CS, Mulder FA, Liljas A, Sanyal S, Akke M
**Journal:** J Mol Biol (2007)
**DOI:** [10.1016/j.jmb.2006.10.025](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jmb.2006.10.025)

## Abstract

1. J Mol Biol. 2007 Jan 12;365(2):468-79. doi: 10.1016/j.jmb.2006.10.025. Epub
2006  Oct 27.

The ribosomal stalk binds to translation factors IF2, EF-Tu, EF-G and RF3 via a
conserved region of the L12 C-terminal domain.

Helgstrand M(1), Mandava CS, Mulder FA, Liljas A, Sanyal S, Akke M.

Author information:
(1)Department of Biophysical Chemistry, Lund University, Box 124, SE-22100 Lund,
Sweden.

Efficient protein synthesis in bacteria requires initiation factor 2 (IF2),
elongation factors Tu (EF-Tu) and G (EF-G), and release factor 3 (RF3), each of
which catalyzes a major step of translation in a GTP-dependent fashion. Previous
reports have suggested that recruitment of factors to the ribosome and
subsequent GTP hydrolysis involve the dimeric protein L12, which forms a
flexible "stalk" on the ribosome. Using heteronuclear NMR spectroscopy we
demonstrate that L12 binds directly to the factors IF2, EF-Tu, EF-G, and RF3
from Escherichia coli, and map the region of L12 involved in these interactions.
Factor-dependent chemical shift changes show that all four factors bind to the
same region of the C-terminal domain of L12. This region includes three strictly
conserved residues, K70, L80, and E82, and a set of highly conserved residues,
including V66, A67, V68 and G79. Upon factor binding, all NMR signals from the
C-terminal domain become broadened beyond detection, while those from the
N-terminal domain are virtually unaffected, implying that the C-terminal domain
binds to the factor, while the N-terminal domain dimer retains its rotational
freedom mediated by the flexible hinge between the two domains. Factor-dependent
variations in linewidths further reveal that L12 binds to each factor with a
dissociation constant in the millimolar range in solution. These results
indicate that the L12-factor complexes will be highly populated on the ribosome,
because of the high local concentration of ribosome-bound factor with respect to
L12.
