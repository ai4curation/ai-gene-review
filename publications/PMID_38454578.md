BACKGROUND AND PURPOSE: α4β2 nicotinic acetylcholine (nACh) receptors assemble
in two stoichiometric forms, one of which is potentiated by calcium. The sites
of calcium binding that underpin potentiation are not known.
EXPERIMENTAL APPROACH: To identify calcium binding sites, we applied
cryo-electron microscopy (cryo-EM) and molecular dynamics (MD) simulations to
each stoichiometric form of the α4β2 nACh receptor in the presence of calcium
ions. To test whether the identified calcium sites are linked to potentiation,
we generated mutants of anionic residues at the sites, expressed wild type and
mutant receptors in clonal mammalian fibroblasts, and recorded ACh-elicited
single-channel currents with or without calcium.
KEY RESULTS: Both cryo-EM and MD simulations show calcium bound to a site
between the extracellular and transmembrane domains of each α4 subunit (ECD-TMD
site). Substituting alanine for anionic residues at the ECD-TMD site abolishes
stoichiometry-selective calcium potentiation, as monitored by single-channel
patch clamp electrophysiology. Additionally, MD simulation reveals calcium
association at subunit interfaces within the extracellular domain. Substituting
alanine for anionic residues at the ECD sites reduces or abolishes
stoichiometry-selective calcium potentiation.
CONCLUSIONS AND IMPLICATIONS: Stoichiometry-selective calcium potentiation of
the α4β2 nACh receptor is achieved by calcium association with topographically
distinct sites framed by anionic residues within the α4 subunit and between the
α4 and β2 subunits. Stoichiometry-selective calcium potentiation could result
from the greater number of calcium sites in the stoichiometric form with three
rather than two α4 subunits. The results are relevant to modulation of
signalling via α4β2 nACh receptors in physiological and pathophysiological
conditions.

Introduction-

Nicotinic acetylcholine receptors (nAChRs) are expressed throughout the central and peripheral nervous systems where they mediate post-synaptic excitation or enhance pre-synaptic release of neurotransmitters (Albuquerque et al. 2009; Dani and Bertrand 2007). They contribute to a diverse range of physiological processes including voluntary muscle contraction, autonomic regulation of blood pressure and heart rate, cortical excitability, cognition, attention, and reward (McGranahan et al. 2011; Sun et al. 2017; Sarter, Parikh, and Howe 2009; Sabri et al. 2018). Dysfunction of nAChRs underpins neurological diseases including congenital myasthenia and epilepsy, and has been implicated in neurodegenerative, neuroinflammatory, and neuropsychiatric disorders (Engel et al. 2015; Bye et al. 2023; Becchetti et al. 2015). The nAChR is also a principal target in nicotine dependence (Laviolette and van der Kooy 2004; Tapper et al. 2004).

The nAChR together with glycine, GABA, and 5-HT3 receptors belong to the superfamily of Cys-loop receptors (Le Novere and Changeux 2001). Members of the superfamily exhibit a pentameric architecture in which five homologous subunits coalesce around a central ion-conducting channel (Grutter and Changeux 2001). Each subunit contains extracellular, transmembrane, and intracellular domains, that across family members, contain sequence differences that endow receptor-specific agonist binding, ion permeability, ion selectivity, and drug sensitivity (Thompson, Lester, and Lummis 2010). The subunits can assemble as either homo- or hetero-pentamers, the latter of which can vary in the stoichiometric ratio of the subunits, further increasing physiological and pharmacological diversity. A prototypical example is the α4β2 nAChR, which expresses abundantly within the CNS and is found in two major stoichiometric forms: (α4)3(β2)2 and (α4)2(β2)3 (Moroni and Bermudez 2006; Moroni et al. 2006; Nelson et al. 2003). The two stoichiometric forms differ in their unitary conductance, calcium permeability, ACh sensitivity, potentiation by divalent cations, and potentiation by the drug NS9283 (Mazzaferro, Bermudez, and Sine 2017; Mazzaferro, Strikwerda, and Sine 2022; Moroni and Bermudez 2006).

An enduring question in the ion channel field is how structure confers function, and in the case of the α4β2 nAChR, how the different stoichiometric forms produce distinct functional and pharmacological signatures. Structures of the two major stoichiometric forms have been determined and provided clues into stoichiometry dependent differences (Walsh et al. 2018). The structures reveal differences in the buried surface area at subunit interfaces suggesting preferential energetics of subunit assembly favoring the two major stoichiometries observed in vitro and in vivo (Fu et al. 2019; Fasoli et al. 2016). The two forms differ in their agonist sensitivities and the number of bound agonist molecules, with the (α4)2(β2)3 form showing high sensitivity and two bound agonist molecules, each at an α4-β2 subunit interface, and the (α4)3(β2)2 form showing low sensitivity and three bound agonist molecules at α4-β2 and α4-α4 subunit interfaces (Moroni et al. 2006; Mazzaferro et al. 2011; Walsh et al. 2018). The two forms also differ in the electrostatic potential along the ion permeation pathway that confers different unitary conductance and calcium permeability (Mazzaferro, Strikwerda, and Sine 2022; Tapia, Kuryatov, and Lindstrom 2007).

The two stoichiometric forms also differ in their potentiation by divalent cations, notably calcium (Mazzaferro, Strikwerda, and Sine 2022). Potentiation manifests as an increased response to ACh in the presence of divalent cations compared to that in their absence (Rodrigues-Pinguet et al. 2005; Buisson et al. 1996). When studied at the single channel level, divalent cations enhance ACh-elicited channel opening of the α4β2 nAChR in a stoichiometry-selective manner, with the (α4)3(β2)2 form showing an increased rate and efficiency of channel opening compared to the (α4)2(β2)3 form (Mazzaferro, Strikwerda, and Sine 2022). This stoichiometry-selective potentiation by divalent cations is expected to be physiologically meaningful, particularly in regions of the central nervous system enriched in the (α4)3(β2)2 stoichiometry and in tight extracellular spaces where the calcium concentration can fluctuate with changes in neuronal activity (Egelman and Montague 1999; Nicholson et al. 1977; Rusakov and Fine 2003).

So far, the identification of divalent cation binding sites in nAChRs has been achieved by indirect means. In a classical study of a chimeric α7 receptor, Galzi and colleagues combined electrophysiological measures of calcium potentiation with site directed mutagenesis of candidate anionic residues; they found several residues near the junction of the extracellular and transmembrane domains that when mutated attenuated calcium potentiation (Galzi et al. 1996). More recently, Natarajan, et al. combined MD simulations with mutagenesis and single channel patch clamp electrophysiology of the α7 nAChR, revealing that potentiation of ACh-elicited channel opening was associated with calcium binding at the entrance of the orthosteric binding sites (Natarajan et al. 2020). In the related bacterial channel DeCLIC, high-resolution structure determination revealed calcium bound near the junction of the extracellular and transmembrane domains (Hu et al. 2020), analogous to the region inferred by Galzi and colleagues.

Here we investigate structural bases for stoichiometry-selective potentiation of the α4β2 nAChR by divalent cations using structure determination, molecular dynamics simulation, mutagenesis, and single channel patch clamp electrophysiology. The results reveal multiple calcium binding sites in topographically distinct regions of the receptor that synergize in conferring potentiation selective for the (α4)3(β2)2 stoichiometry. We also present new structures of the α4β2 nAChR with the endogenous neurotransmitter ACh bound, and a full-length structure including structurally ordered regions of the intracellular domain.

Materials and Methods-Mutagenesis and expression of human (α4)3(β2)2 and (α4)2(β2)3 AChRs for patch clampcDNAs encoding human α4 and β2 subunits, and the chaperones 14-3-3η and NACHO, were individually sub-cloned into a modified pCI mammalian expression vector (Promega), as previously described (Carbone et al. 2009; Mazzaferro et al. 2011). Mutations were generated using the QuikChange method and confirmed by sequencing. 14-3-3η and NACHO were used to bias expression toward either the (α4)3(β2)2 or (α4)2(β2)3 forms, respectively (Jeanclos et al., 2001; Exley et al., 2006; Carbone et al., 2009; Mazzaferro et al., 2021). BOSC 23 cells, an HEK 293 derived cell line (CLS Cat# 300192/p777_HEK293, RRID:CVCL_0045) (Pear et al., 1993), were maintained in Dulbecco’s modified Eagle’s medium (DMEM, Gibco) containing 10% fetal bovine serum, and transfected by calcium phosphate precipitation, as previously described (Sine, 1993; Bouzat et al., 1994; Sine et al., 1994). To bias expression towards the (α4)3(β2)2 form, cells were transfected with a 10:1:10 ratio of α4, β2, and 14-3-3η cDNAs. The amounts of transfected α4 and β2 cDNAs were 6 and 0.6 μg, respectively, per 35 mm culture dish of cells. To bias expression towards the (α4)2(β2)3 form, cells were transfected with a 1:1:0.3 ratio of α4, β2, and NACHO cDNAs. The amounts of transfected α4 and β2 cDNAs were 3 μg for each 35 mm culture dish of cells. A cDNA encoding green fluorescent protein was included in all transfections. Transfections were carried out for 4 to 6 hours, followed by medium exchange. Single-channel recordings were made 48–72 hours post-transfection.DrugsAcetylcholine (ACh) was purchased from Sigma-Aldrich (St Louis, MO, USA).Patch clamp recordingsSingle-channel recordings were obtained in the cell-attached patch configuration at a membrane potential of −70 mV and a temperature of 20°C, as previously described (Mazzaferro et al., 2017, 2021). For all experiments, pipette and extracellular bathing solutions contained (mM): 142 KCl, 5.4 NaCl, and 10 HEPES, adjusted to pH 7.4 with NaOH. A high concentration of potassium is used to negate the cell resting membrane potential, allowing the membrane potential to be established by the patch clamp electronics. Also, as the major current carrying ion, potassium increases the unitary current amplitude by approximately 50 % compared to sodium. To investigate the effects of calcium and magnesium on receptor function, 1.8 mM CaCl2 and/or 1.7 mM MgCl2 were included in both the pipette and extracellular bathing solutions. For divalent-ion-free experiments, 1 mM EGTA was added to both the pipette and extracellular solutions. Concentrated stock solutions of ACh were made in pipette solution, with or without CaCl2 and MgCl2, and stored at −80 °C until the day of each experiment. Patch pipettes were pulled from glass capillary tubes (No.7052, Garner Glass) and coated with Sylgard (Dow Corning).Single-channel currents were recorded using an Axopatch 200B patch-clamp amplifier (Molecular Devices), with a gain of 100 mV/pA and the internal Bessel filter at 10 kHz. Currents were sampled at intervals of 20 μs using a PCI-6111E acquisition card (National Instruments) and recorded to hard disk using the program Acquire (Bruxton Corporation). A typical recording lasted 5 to 10 minutes and included up to 5000 channel opening events. Recordings from at least five patches were obtained for each mutant receptor and experimental condition.Data and AnalysisThe data and statistical analysis comply with the recommendations on experimental design and analysis in pharmacology (Curtis et al. 2018). Statistical analysis was undertaken only for studies in which the size of each group was at least n=5. The group size was defined as the sum of independent recordings obtained from different cells. Group sizes were not pre-determined by statistical methods but were estimated based on previous experiments, literature in the field, and recommendation from Curtis et al., 2018. In addition, no data were excluded from the analysis, and no randomization or blinding was performed during the experiments. Differences between groups were considered significant when the p-value was less than 0.01.Data analysis was performed on single-channel currents recorded throughout the entire period in which the giga-ohm seal between the recording pipette and the plasma membrane was stable. Data collection started 20 to 30s after the giga-ohm seal formed and proceeded as long as the baseline current remained stable.Channel opening and closing transitionsChannel opening and closing transitions were detected using the program TAC 4.2.0 (Bruxton Corporation), which digitally filters the data (Gaussian response, final effective bandwidth 5 kHz), interpolates the digitized points using a cubic spline function, and detects channel opening or closing transitions using the half-amplitude threshold criterion, as described (Colquhoun and Sigworth, 1983). Following detection, each single-channel open dwell time was plotted against its time of occurrence during the recording to assess the channel opening frequency and stability of the gating kinetics. Dwell time histograms were plotted using a logarithmic abscissa and square root ordinate (Sigworth and Sine, 1987), with a uniformly imposed dead time of 40 μs, and a multiexponential function was fitted to the data by maximum likelihood using the program TACFit 4.2.0 (Colquhoun and Sigworth, 1983).Clusters of channel openingsClusters of channel openings were identified as a series of closely spaced openings preceded and followed by closed intervals longer than a specified critical time (τcrit). This duration was taken as the point of intersection between consecutive exponential components in the closed time histogram. Values of τcrit were determined independently for each patch and ranged between 1 and 10 ms. The duration of a given cluster therefore comprises the total time of a series of openings plus the total time of the intervening closings briefer than τcrit. The average cluster distribution and mean cluster duration were obtained from recordings from multiple patches under identical experimental conditions. The non-parametric Mann-Whitney test, which detects shifts in the medians, was used to compare the distributions for experiments under different experimental conditions. Distributions were considered significantly different when the p-value was less than 0.01. Mean cluster duration and statistical tests on the distributions were done using the Prism software package (GraphPad Prism, RRID:SCR_002798).Channel reopening probabilityThe effect of the mutations on the probability a channel will reopen was quantified by plotting the fraction of channel opening episodes with greater than N openings against the number of openings per episode. A channel opening episode was defined as a series of one or more openings separated by closings shorter than τcrit. The reopening distributions were fitted with exponential decay functions, and an F-test was used to determine whether a mono or a biexponential decay best described the reopening data; a mono exponential decay was preferred unless the sum-of-squares F-test had a p-value less than 0.01. Both the exponential fitting and the F-test were carried out using the Prism software package GraphPad Prism, RRID:SCR_002798. In addition, the mean number of openings per episode was calculated as the reciprocal of the decay constant, and an F-test was used to determine whether the fitted decay constant differed significantly between recordings under different experimental conditions. After randomly dispersing 1.8 mM Ca2+ ions and counter ions around the receptor, the system was solvated with TIP3P water molecules and 150 mM NaCl; NaCl was chosen because it is the major physiological salt extracellularly and also because it is the major salt in the TBS solution used in receptor purification and structure determination. The simulations were run using GROMACS software under periodic boundary conditions (Van Der Spoel et al. 2005). The CHARMM36m forcefield (Huang et al. 2017) was employed, and the particle-mesh Ewald sum method was used to model long-range electrostatic interactions (Essmann et al. 1995). The SHAKE algorithm was used to fix the bond lengths involving hydrogen atoms (Ryckaert, Ciccotti, and Berendsen 1977), and a 12 Å cutoff was employed for nonbonded van der Waals interactions. The simulations were initially energy minimized using the steepest descent energy minimization algorithm over a maximum of 10,000 steps. After equilibration in an NPAT (constant particle number, normal pressure, membrane surface area, and temperature) ensemble for 5 ns, the system was subjected to 200 ns of final production MD runs in an NPT (constant particle number, pressure, and temperature) ensemble. The coordinates were saved every 10 ps during the simulations. Additionally, another set of simulations was carried out in the absence of calcium following the same protocol. All MD simulations were carried out using computational facilities at National Center for Supercomputing Applications at University of Illinois Urbana-Campaign, Illinois, USA.

Patch clamp recordingsSingle-channel recordings were obtained in the cell-attached patch configuration at a membrane potential of −70 mV and a temperature of 20°C, as previously described (Mazzaferro et al., 2017, 2021). For all experiments, pipette and extracellular bathing solutions contained (mM): 142 KCl, 5.4 NaCl, and 10 HEPES, adjusted to pH 7.4 with NaOH. A high concentration of potassium is used to negate the cell resting membrane potential, allowing the membrane potential to be established by the patch clamp electronics. Also, as the major current carrying ion, potassium increases the unitary current amplitude by approximately 50 % compared to sodium. To investigate the effects of calcium and magnesium on receptor function, 1.8 mM CaCl2 and/or 1.7 mM MgCl2 were included in both the pipette and extracellular bathing solutions. For divalent-ion-free experiments, 1 mM EGTA was added to both the pipette and extracellular solutions. Concentrated stock solutions of ACh were made in pipette solution, with or without CaCl2 and MgCl2, and stored at −80 °C until the day of each experiment. Currents were sampled at intervals of 20 μs using a PCI-6111E acquisition card (National Instruments) and recorded to hard disk using the program Acquire (Bruxton Corporation). Channel opening and closing transitionsChannel opening and closing transitions were detected using the program TAC 4.2.0 (Bruxton Corporation), which digitally filters the data (Gaussian response, final effective bandwidth 5 kHz), interpolates the digitized points using a cubic spline function, and detects channel opening or closing transitions using the half-amplitude threshold criterion, as described (Colquhoun and Sigworth, 1983). Following detection, each single-channel open dwell time was plotted against its time of occurrence during the recording to assess the channel opening frequency and stability of the gating kinetics. Dwell time histograms were plotted using a logarithmic abscissa and square root ordinate (Sigworth and Sine, 1987), with a uniformly imposed dead time of 40 μs, and a multiexponential function was fitted to the data by maximum likelihood using the program TACFit 4.2.0 (Colquhoun and Sigworth, 1983).

Clusters of channel openingsClusters of channel openings were identified as a series of closely spaced openings preceded and followed by closed intervals longer than a specified critical time (τcrit). This duration was taken as the point of intersection between consecutive exponential components in the closed time histogram. Values of τcrit were determined independently for each patch and ranged between 1 and 10 ms. The duration of a given cluster therefore comprises the total time of a series of openings plus the total time of the intervening closings briefer than τcrit. The average cluster distribution and mean cluster duration were obtained from recordings from multiple patches under identical experimental conditions. The non-parametric Mann-Whitney test, which detects shifts in the medians, was used to compare the distributions for experiments under different experimental conditions. Distributions were considered significantly different when the p-value was less than 0.01. Mean cluster duration and statistical tests on the distributions were done using the Prism software package (GraphPad Prism, RRID:SCR_002798).

Channel reopening probabilityThe effect of the mutations on the probability a channel will reopen was quantified by plotting the fraction of channel opening episodes with greater than N openings against the number of openings per episode. A channel opening episode was defined as a series of one or more openings separated by closings shorter than τcrit. The reopening distributions were fitted with exponential decay functions, and an F-test was used to determine whether a mono or a biexponential decay best described the reopening data; a mono exponential decay was preferred unless the sum-of-squares F-test had a p-value less than 0.01. Both the exponential fitting and the F-test were carried out using the Prism software package GraphPad Prism, RRID:SCR_002798. In addition, the mean number of openings per episode was calculated as the reciprocal of the decay constant, and an F-test was used to determine whether the fitted decay constant differed significantly between recordings under different experimental conditions. Parameters were considered significantly different if the p-value was less than 0.01.

Herein we refer to this 2α:3β stoichiometry from the Bril-embedded full-length construct as “(α4)2(β2)3.” Bril has been utilized for enhancing the stability and crystallization of GPCR-fusion constructs (Chun et al. 2012). Bril is inserted in a structurally disordered region of the M3-M4 loop between residues His531 and Leu532 in α4 and between residues Gly392 and Glu393 in β2. For affinity chromatography, a strep tag was inserted at the C terminus of the β2 subunit after a Ser-Ala linker. The expression and purification protocol is reported in detail (Walsh et al. 2018) with minor modifications on transduction. Briefly, 2.4 L of suspension HEK293S GnTI− (ATCC CRL-3022) cells were grown at 37 °C and 8% CO2 to a density of ~4 × 106 cells/ml. During transduction, 3 mM sodium butyrate (Sigma-Aldrich) and the baculoviruses were added to the culture. The temperature was decreased to 30 °C after transduction with multiplicities of infection (MOIs) of 0.5:0.5:0.25 for α4, β2, and chaperone NACHO (Matta et al. 2017) viruses, respectively. We also refer to 3α:2β or 2α:3β stoichiometries from the M3M4 loop deleted constructs as “Δ(α4)3(β2)2” or “Δ(α4)2(β2)3”. The MOI used was 1:0.5 in the absence of the NACHO virus. Transduced cells were harvested after 72 hr by centrifugation, resuspended in a TBS buffer (20 mM Tris, 150 mM NaCl, pH 7,4) containing 1 mM phenyl-methane-sulfonyl fluoride (Sigma-Aldrich), then lysed using an Avestin Emulsiflex. Lysed cells were centrifuged for 20 minutes at 10,000 g, and the supernatant was centrifuged again for 2 hours at 186,000 g. Membrane pellets were used immediately or stored at −80 °C for future use. The membrane pellet was mechanically homogenized and solubilized for 1 hour at 4 °C using a homogenizer with TBS buffer with 40 mM n-dodecyl-β-D-maltoside (DDM; Anatrace). Solubilized membranes were centrifuged for 40 minutes at 186,000 g, then slowly passed over high-capacity Strep-Tactin affinity resin (IBA) at 0.8 ml/min. The resin was washed with TBS buffer containing 1 mM DDM, 0.2 mM cholesteryl hemisuccinate tris salt (CHS; Anatrace), and 0.01% soy polar lipids, then eluted with wash buffer supplemented by 5 mM desthiobiotin (Sigma-Aldrich). During affinity and size exclusion chromatography, the buffer contained 300 μM acetylcholine and was supplemented by either 10 mM Ca2+ or 5 mM EGTA to chelate calcium.

Prior to simulation, nicotine bound to each structure was removed, and each receptor was positioned into a POPC:POPA:CHOL (3:1:1) membrane bilayer with dimensions of 120 × 120 Å. After randomly dispersing 1.8 mM Ca2+ ions and counter ions around the receptor, the system was solvated with TIP3P water molecules and 150 mM NaCl; NaCl was chosen because it is the major physiological salt extracellularly and also because it is the major salt in the TBS solution used in receptor purification and structure determination. The simulations were run using GROMACS software under periodic boundary conditions (Van Der Spoel et al. 2005). The CHARMM36m forcefield (Huang et al. 2017) was employed, and the particle-mesh Ewald sum method was used to model long-range electrostatic interactions (Essmann et al. 1995). The SHAKE algorithm was used to fix the bond lengths involving hydrogen atoms (Ryckaert, Ciccotti, and Berendsen 1977), and a 12 Å cutoff was employed for nonbonded van der Waals interactions. The simulations were initially energy minimized using the steepest descent energy minimization algorithm over a maximum of 10,000 steps. After equilibration in an NPAT (constant particle number, normal pressure, membrane surface area, and temperature) ensemble for 5 ns, the system was subjected to 200 ns of final production MD runs in an NPT (constant particle number, pressure, and temperature) ensemble. The coordinates were saved every 10 ps during the simulations. Additionally, another set of simulations was carried out in the absence of calcium following the same protocol. All MD simulations were carried out using computational facilities at National Center for Supercomputing Applications at University of Illinois Urbana-Campaign, Illinois, USA.

Each receptor assembly comprises an extracellular domain (ECD) of largely β-sheet structure joined to a transmembrane domain (TMD) of α-helical structure, as reported by Walsh, et al (2018). In addition, the full length (α4)2(β2)3 stoichiometry contains an ICD with substantial α-helical structure.

For each receptor construct, a pebble-shaped density consistent with a calcium ion is observed within each α4 subunit in samples prepared in the presence of calcium, but not in samples prepared in the absence of calcium and the presence of EGTA (Figs. 2c, f, i). The calcium ion density is framed by three anionic residues near the junction between the ECD and TMD. These anionic residues are located within two discontinuous sections of the polypeptide chain of the ECD that coalesce in the 3D structure, comprising Asp49 and Glu52 at the tip of the loop connecting the β1- and β2-strands and Glu182 from the loop connecting the β8- and β9-strands. Of these residues, Glu52 and Glu183 are conserved among all types of human α-subunits, whereas Asp49 is variable (Fig. 2j). For reference we designate the region containing the calcium density the ECD-TMD site. The observation of virtually identical calcium densities in both the Δ(α4)2(β2)3 and the full length (α4)2(β2)3 structures shows that deletion of the M3-M4 loop does not affect calcium binding to the ECD-TMD site.

While the cryo-EM structures inform on details of calcium coordination, we observe only subtle conformational differences in the absence versus the presence of calcium, and in the full-length versus truncated constructs. Comparing the r.m.s.d. differences of the Cα atoms in the absence or presence of calcium, the Δ(α4)3(β2)2 structures reveal a value of 0.67 Å, the Δ(α4)2(β2)3 structures 0.36 Å, and the full length (α4)2(β2)3 structures 0.42 Å. Comparing the Cα r.m.s.d. differences between individual subunits further distinguishes calcium-dependent differences between the two stoichiometric forms. For the Δ(α4)3(β2)2 stoichiometry the r.m.s.d. differences in the absence versus the presence of calcium are two- to three-fold greater for the α4 than the β2 subunits, whereas for the Δ(α4)2(β2)3 stoichiometry the differences are very similar between the two types of subunits. When mapped upon the tertiary structures of the subunits the r.m.s.d. differences appear dispersed in multiple regions of the α4 subunits with the greatest differences in the M4 and MX α-helices, which are distant from the sites of calcium binding (Fig. S4). Comparing the pore profiles using the Hole program shows a clear constriction at the −1’ Glu residue with a diameter smaller than a hydrated sodium ion and typical of the desensitized conformation (Walsh et al. 2018; Noviello et al. 2021; Rahman et al. 2022), but no visible differences in the presence versus the absence of calcium (Fig. 3). Thus, the (α4)3(β2)2 stoichiometric form shows the largest overall differences in the absence versus the presence of calcium, and these are larger in the α4 than the β2 subunits. Furthermore, while structural differences are observed in multiple regions of the α4 subunits, the pore profiles are indistinguishable in the presence or absence of calcium and closely resemble the desensitized conformation.

Analysis of the ECD-TMD site by single channel patch clamp electrophysiology-To monitor receptor function, cDNAs encoding full length α4 and β2 subunits were transiently transfected into BOSC 23 cells followed by patch clamp recording of ACh-elicited single channel currents, without or with divalent cations. A concentration of 100 μM ACh was chosen to maximize activation and minimize channel block by ACh; as measured by whole cell macroscopic current recording, this concentration is approximately the EC50 for the low sensitivity (α4)3(β2)2 stoichiometry (Moroni et al. 2006; Carbone et al. 2009; Mazzaferro et al. 2011). To bias receptor expression toward the (α4)3(β2)2 stoichiometry, the ratio of α4:β2 cDNAs in the transfection mixture was 10:1, and a cDNA encoding the chaperone 14-3-3η was included, as described (Mazzaferro et al. 2021). To assess functional contributions of the anionic residues at the ECD-TMD site, we substituted Ala for each residue and recorded ACh-elicited single channel currents without or with divalent cations. For receptors containing Ala substitutions of D49, E52, or E182, without or with divalent cations, the single channel current traces show mainly single brief openings, or infrequently, several openings in quick succession; these divalent cation independent channel openings contrast with those for the wild type (α4)3(β2)2 receptor, which exhibit single brief channel openings without divalent cations but clusters of many successive channel openings with divalent cations (Fig. 4a). Quantitative analyses confirm the qualitative features of the single channel current traces. For the wild type receptor, histograms of cluster durations diverge without versus with divalent cations, whereas for each of the three Ala substituted receptors the histograms are virtually superimposable (Fig. 4b; Tables S2–S4). Analogously, for the wild type receptor the mean cluster duration increases with versus without divalent cations, but for Ala substituted receptors there is no difference. For the wild type receptor, the exponential rate of decay in channel re-opening per cluster is markedly slower with versus without calcium, but for each receptor with Ala substitutions the decay rates are very similar. Finally, for the wild type receptor and all receptor constructs without divalent cations, the fraction of un-potentiated channel openings greatly exceeds potentiated openings. However, for the wild type receptor with divalent cations, the fraction of potentiated channel openings markedly increases, whereas for each Ala substituted receptor the fraction of potentiated channel openings remains low. Furthermore, the fraction of channel openings classified as potentiated in the presence of divalent cations is significantly greater for the wild type than for any of the three mutant receptors (Fig. S5). Thus, substituting Ala for any of the three anionic residues at the ECD-TMD site abolishes divalent cation potentiation of the (α4)3(β2)2 receptor. Together, the structural, mutagenesis, and single channel functional data show that binding of calcium to the ECD-TMD site is associated with stoichiometry-selective potentiation of the (α4)3(β2)2 receptor.

Molecular dynamics simulations-To complement our findings from cryo-EM structure determination, we performed molecular dynamics (MD) simulations using the published structures of Δ(α4)3(β2)2 and Δ(α4)2(β2)3 (Walsh et al. 2018). By contrast to cryo-EM, MD simulations can detect transient, low affinity protein-ligand interactions as well as ligand association that depends on protein dynamics. However, MD simulations spanning hundreds of nanoseconds are too short to capture conformational changes associated with channel gating or desensitization, which occur on the millisecond to second time scale. Prior to simulation, nicotine molecules that were bound in the original cryo-EM structures were removed, and system ensembles were generated containing one copy of each stoichiometric form imbedded in a POPC:POPA:CHOL (3:1:1) lipid bilayer, TIP3P water molecules, sodium and chloride ions at concentrations of 150 mM, without or with calcium ions plus charge-neutralizing anions at concentrations of 1.8 mM. Each system ensemble was then subjected to 10,000 steps of energy minimization, 5 ns of equilibration, and a production simulation of 200 ns under conditions of constant number, pressure, and temperature.For each MD simulation, the all-atom r.m.s.d. relative to the starting structure increases initially and then approaches a plateau for both the apo and calcium simulations for each stoichiometric form, suggesting relatively stable structures are achieved following 200 nanoseconds of simulation (Fig. S6). For both stoichiometric forms, calcium ions are observed in multiple locations, including the ECD-TMD site observed by cryo-EM (Fig. S7) and additional sites within the ECD (Fig. 5A–D; Fig. S8). Turning to the (α4)3(β2)2 stoichiometry, the ECD sites revealed by simulation show a calcium ion lodged at the entrance to each of the five subunit interfaces. At the two α4-β2 interfaces that form orthosteric sites, calcium is stabilized by α4-Glu198 from the principal subunit and β2-Asp170 from the complementary subunit, with an additional contribution by β2-Ser168 from the complementary subunit; time courses of the distance between calcium and each of these residues show rapid and stable association (Fig. 5b, d). At the α4-α4 interface that forms the third orthosteric site, calcium is stabilized by α4-Glu198 from the principal subunit and α4-Asp176 from the complementary subunit; time courses of the calcium-residue distances again show rapid and stable association (Fig. 5e). At the two remaining interfaces, β2-α4, calcium is stabilized by three anionic residues from the principal subunit, β2-Asp192, β2-Asp193, and β2-Asp198, and one anionic residue from the complementary subunit, α4-Asp176 (Fig. 5a, c). None of these contact residues detected by simulation are conserved among α2-α10 or β2-β4 subunits, suggesting they may contribute to receptor subtype selectivity of divalent cation potentiation. Together the cryo-EM and MD simulation studies indicate that calcium ions bind to topographically distinct ECD-TMD and ECD sites. The ECD sites detected by simulation represent additional candidates for contributing to stoichiometry-selective calcium potentiation of the α4β2 nAChR.

Discussion-Monitoring stoichiometry-selective modulation of the α4β2 nAChR requires a means to distinguish the two stoichiometric forms, and simultaneously register the function of each. Single channel patch clamp electrophysiology described herein meets both requirements; the two stoichiometric forms are distinguished by their unitary current amplitude, while function is registered by the temporal sequences and durations of agonist-elicited single channel currents. Combining cryo-EM structure determination, single channel recording, and mutagenesis of key residues revealed a site between the extracellular and transmembrane domains (ECD-TMD site) framed by three anionic residues; when Ala is substituted for any of these residues, stoichiometry-selective divalent cation potentiation is abolished. Two of these residues, α4-Asp52 and α4-Glu183, are conserved across all types of human α-subunits, and previous studies of the muscle nAChR showed that mutations of residues that align with α4-Asp52 and α4-Glu183 slowed the rate and reduced the efficiency of ACh-elicited channel opening (Lee and Sine 2005; Mukhtasimova and Sine 2013). MD simulations complemented our cryo-EM structure determination, supporting the ECD-TMD site, and revealing additional candidate sites at subunit interfaces within the ECD. Mutagenesis of key residues at the ECD sites reduced or abolished calcium potentiation. While our study defines sites for calcium binding and associates them with stoichiometry-selective potentiation, questions remain of why the (α4)3(β2)2 stoichiometry is potentiated while the (α4)2(β2)3 stoichiometry is not, why the ECD sites are detected by MD simulation but not by cryo-EM, and how topographically distinct ECD-TMD and ECD sites synergize in mediating divalent cation potentiation.Our cryo-EM structures reveal a calcium ion density in the same location within each α4 subunit of both stoichiometric forms, and the MD simulations support this identification. The ECD-TMD site is present in two copies in the non-potentiated (α4)2(β2)3 stoichiometry, and three copies in the potentiated (α4)3(β2)2 stoichiometry. Comparison of cryo-EM structures with and without bound calcium shows only subtle conformational differences perhaps because the structures could not be arrested in the transient open channel state, but instead achieved the more stable desensitized state. Nonetheless, comparison of r.m.s.d. differences with versus without calcium reveals larger differences for the (α4)3(β2)2 stoichiometry owing to greater differences in the α4 than the β2 subunits. Together with established structural differences between the two stoichiometric forms (Walsh et al. 2018), our results suggest the following structural bases for the stoichiometry selectivity. Compared to the (α4)2(β2)3 stoichiometry, the (α4)3(β2)2 stoichiometry contains an additional ECD-TMD site, which places two of the three ECD-TMD sites in adjacent α4-α4 subunits. In addition, the buried surface area at the α4-α4 subunit interface is greater than that of its counterpart at the β2-β2 interface, which may better promote transduction of calcium binding into stabilization of the open channel state. Thus, selective potentiation of the (α4)3(β2)2 stoichiometry is associated with a third calcium binding site, greater structural changes in subunits that harbor the ECD-TMD sites, and the presence of calcium sites in adjacent subunits that flank a subunit interface with increased buried surface area.In addition to differences in the number of calcium binding sites and their locations relative to subunit interfaces, our results provide several additional pieces of the puzzle of the overall potentiation mechanism. In all cases calcium is coordinated by anionic residues within flexible loop structures as opposed to stable secondary structures. Binding of calcium is expected to stabilize these loop structures through relief of electrostatic repulsion between the anionic residues, particularly in regions with high relative electric permittivity. The ECD-TMD sites overlap structurally with the principal coupling pathway between the agonist binding and pore domains (Lee and Sine 2005), and stabilization by calcium may further promote coupling between the two domains. At the ECD sites the stabilization by calcium may favor the capped conformation of loop C that traps the bound agonist (Celie et al. 2004; Gao et al. 2005). Our observations that a mutation at either the ECD-TMD or ECD sites abolishes potentiation suggests that local stabilization at these spatially separated sites is coupled to global structural changes that give rise to the activated state. Structural linkages between the sites that mediate global stabilization emerge as fruitful topics for further research.Whereas the ECD-TMD sites are detected by both cryo-EM and MD simulation, the ECD sites are detected by MD simulation alone. A noteworthy difference between the cryo-EM and MD simulation studies is that ACh was included in the samples for cryo-EM but not the simulations. A further difference is the cryo-EM structures are at steady state, whereas the MD simulations are not long enough to achieve steady state. In addition to these differences, the ECD sites may have been missed by cryo-EM because they have low affinity for calcium, which would reduce steady-state calcium occupancy below detectable levels. The same reasoning may explain why the presumed intermediate sites are detected by MD simulation but not cryo-EM.Selective calcium potentiation of the (α4)3(β2)2 stoichiometry has implications for synaptic transmission. The (α4)3(β2)2 stoichiometry binds ACh with low affinity and therefore is best suited to regions where pre- and post-synaptic neurons are closely juxtaposed and the ACh concentration is greatest. Additionally, the (α4)3(β2)2 stoichiometry is highly permeable to calcium, approaching that of the homomeric α7 nAChR, in contrast to the low calcium permeability of the (α4)2(β2)3 stoichiometry (Tapia, Kuryatov, and Lindstrom 2007). Thus, when located pre-synaptically, the increased influx of calcium by the (α4)3(β2)2 stoichiometry could directly enhance neurotransmitter release, whereas when located post-synaptically, the increase in calcium could engage intracellular calcium signal transduction cascades or release calcium from intracellular stores. In synaptic locations within the neuropil, the extracellular volume is small enough that calcium influx through the receptor could transiently deplete extracellular calcium locally (Egelman and Montague 1999; Rusakov and Fine 2003), enabling feedback inhibition via a decrease in calcium potentiation of the receptor and consequently a decrease in calcium influx.Our results combining mutagenesis with single channel recording show that all three classes of calcium sites contribute to divalent cation potentiation. One possibility is that the three classes of sites synergize in transducing calcium binding into potentiation, so that potentiation involves global interactions among topographically distinct sites. Alternatively, one of these sites may be the key mediator of the binding-transduction process, whereas the other sites may merely contribute to structural integrity required for potentiation and their dependence on the presence of calcium is merely permissive for potentiation. Previous work associated potentiation with sites analogous to either the ECD-TMD (Galzi et al. 1996) or ECD (Natarajan et al. 2020) sites using indirect means in different types of nAChRs. However, the current study is the first to our knowledge in which the ECD-TMD site was demonstrated by direct structure determination, and both the ECD-TMD and ECD sites were shown to be required for potentiation by single channel patch clamp electrophysiology. The multidisciplinary approaches we have applied to the different stoichiometric forms of the α4β2 nAChR may be useful in delineating mechanisms of divalent cation potentiation in other types of nAChR and their stoichiometric forms.

Combining cryo-EM structure determination, single channel recording, and mutagenesis of key residues revealed a site between the extracellular and transmembrane domains (ECD-TMD site) framed by three anionic residues; when Ala is substituted for any of these residues, stoichiometry-selective divalent cation potentiation is abolished. Two of these residues, α4-Asp52 and α4-Glu183, are conserved across all types of human α-subunits, and previous studies of the muscle nAChR showed that mutations of residues that align with α4-Asp52 and α4-Glu183 slowed the rate and reduced the efficiency of ACh-elicited channel opening (Lee and Sine 2005; Mukhtasimova and Sine 2013). MD simulations complemented our cryo-EM structure determination, supporting the ECD-TMD site, and revealing additional candidate sites at subunit interfaces within the ECD. Mutagenesis of key residues at the ECD sites reduced or abolished calcium potentiation. While our study defines sites for calcium binding and associates them with stoichiometry-selective potentiation, questions remain of why the (α4)3(β2)2 stoichiometry is potentiated while the (α4)2(β2)3 stoichiometry is not, why the ECD sites are detected by MD simulation but not by cryo-EM, and how topographically distinct ECD-TMD and ECD sites synergize in mediating divalent cation potentiation.

Our cryo-EM structures reveal a calcium ion density in the same location within each α4 subunit of both stoichiometric forms, and the MD simulations support this identification. The ECD-TMD site is present in two copies in the non-potentiated (α4)2(β2)3 stoichiometry, and three copies in the potentiated (α4)3(β2)2 stoichiometry. Comparison of cryo-EM structures with and without bound calcium shows only subtle conformational differences perhaps because the structures could not be arrested in the transient open channel state, but instead achieved the more stable desensitized state. Nonetheless, comparison of r.m.s.d. differences with versus without calcium reveals larger differences for the (α4)3(β2)2 stoichiometry owing to greater differences in the α4 than the β2 subunits. Together with established structural differences between the two stoichiometric forms (Walsh et al. 2018), our results suggest the following structural bases for the stoichiometry selectivity. Compared to the (α4)2(β2)3 stoichiometry, the (α4)3(β2)2 stoichiometry contains an additional ECD-TMD site, which places two of the three ECD-TMD sites in adjacent α4-α4 subunits. In addition, the buried surface area at the α4-α4 subunit interface is greater than that of its counterpart at the β2-β2 interface, which may better promote transduction of calcium binding into stabilization of the open channel state. Thus, selective potentiation of the (α4)3(β2)2 stoichiometry is associated with a third calcium binding site, greater structural changes in subunits that harbor the ECD-TMD sites, and the presence of calcium sites in adjacent subunits that flank a subunit interface with increased buried surface area.

In addition to differences in the number of calcium binding sites and their locations relative to subunit interfaces, our results provide several additional pieces of the puzzle of the overall potentiation mechanism. In all cases calcium is coordinated by anionic residues within flexible loop structures as opposed to stable secondary structures. Binding of calcium is expected to stabilize these loop structures through relief of electrostatic repulsion between the anionic residues, particularly in regions with high relative electric permittivity. The ECD-TMD sites overlap structurally with the principal coupling pathway between the agonist binding and pore domains (Lee and Sine 2005), and stabilization by calcium may further promote coupling between the two domains. At the ECD sites the stabilization by calcium may favor the capped conformation of loop C that traps the bound agonist (Celie et al. 2004; Gao et al. 2005). Our observations that a mutation at either the ECD-TMD or ECD sites abolishes potentiation suggests that local stabilization at these spatially separated sites is coupled to global structural changes that give rise to the activated state. Structural linkages between the sites that mediate global stabilization emerge as fruitful topics for further research.

Whereas the ECD-TMD sites are detected by both cryo-EM and MD simulation, the ECD sites are detected by MD simulation alone. A noteworthy difference between the cryo-EM and MD simulation studies is that ACh was included in the samples for cryo-EM but not the simulations. A further difference is the cryo-EM structures are at steady state, whereas the MD simulations are not long enough to achieve steady state. In addition to these differences, the ECD sites may have been missed by cryo-EM because they have low affinity for calcium, which would reduce steady-state calcium occupancy below detectable levels. The same reasoning may explain why the presumed intermediate sites are detected by MD simulation but not cryo-EM.

Selective calcium potentiation of the (α4)3(β2)2 stoichiometry has implications for synaptic transmission. The (α4)3(β2)2 stoichiometry binds ACh with low affinity and therefore is best suited to regions where pre- and post-synaptic neurons are closely juxtaposed and the ACh concentration is greatest. Additionally, the (α4)3(β2)2 stoichiometry is highly permeable to calcium, approaching that of the homomeric α7 nAChR, in contrast to the low calcium permeability of the (α4)2(β2)3 stoichiometry (Tapia, Kuryatov, and Lindstrom 2007). Thus, when located pre-synaptically, the increased influx of calcium by the (α4)3(β2)2 stoichiometry could directly enhance neurotransmitter release, whereas when located post-synaptically, the increase in calcium could engage intracellular calcium signal transduction cascades or release calcium from intracellular stores. In synaptic locations within the neuropil, the extracellular volume is small enough that calcium influx through the receptor could transiently deplete extracellular calcium locally (Egelman and Montague 1999; Rusakov and Fine 2003), enabling feedback inhibition via a decrease in calcium potentiation of the receptor and consequently a decrease in calcium influx.

Our results combining mutagenesis with single channel recording show that all three classes of calcium sites contribute to divalent cation potentiation. One possibility is that the three classes of sites synergize in transducing calcium binding into potentiation, so that potentiation involves global interactions among topographically distinct sites. Alternatively, one of these sites may be the key mediator of the binding-transduction process, whereas the other sites may merely contribute to structural integrity required for potentiation and their dependence on the presence of calcium is merely permissive for potentiation. Previous work associated potentiation with sites analogous to either the ECD-TMD (Galzi et al. 1996) or ECD (Natarajan et al. 2020) sites using indirect means in different types of nAChRs. However, the current study is the first to our knowledge in which the ECD-TMD site was demonstrated by direct structure determination, and both the ECD-TMD and ECD sites were shown to be required for potentiation by single channel patch clamp electrophysiology. The multidisciplinary approaches we have applied to the different stoichiometric forms of the α4β2 nAChR may be useful in delineating mechanisms of divalent cation potentiation in other types of nAChR and their stoichiometric forms.
