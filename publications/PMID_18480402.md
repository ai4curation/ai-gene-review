# The PCH family member proline-serine-threonine phosphatase-interacting protein 1 targets to the leukocyte uropod and regulates directed cell migration.
**Authors:** Cooper KM, Bennin DA, Huttenlocher A
**Journal:** Mol Biol Cell (2008)
**DOI:** [10.1091/mbc.e08-02-0225](https://doi.org/10.1091/mbc.e08-02-0225)

## Abstract

1. Mol Biol Cell. 2008 Aug;19(8):3180-91. doi: 10.1091/mbc.e08-02-0225. Epub 2008

The PCH family member proline-serine-threonine phosphatase-interacting protein 1
targets to the leukocyte uropod and regulates directed cell migration.

Cooper KM(1), Bennin DA, Huttenlocher A.

Author information:
(1)Cellular and Molecular Biology Program, University of Wisconsin, Madison, WI
53706, USA.

Pombe Cdc15 homology (PCH) family members have emerged as important regulators
of membrane-cytoskeletal interactions. Here we show that PSTPIP1, a PCH family
member expressed in hematopoietic cells, regulates the motility of
neutrophil-like cells and is a novel component of the leukocyte uropod where it
colocalizes with other uropod components, such as type I PIPKIgamma.
Furthermore, we show that PSTPIP1 association with the regulator of endocytosis,
dynamin 2, and PSTPIP1 expression impairs transferrin uptake and endocytosis. We
also show that PSTPIP1 localizes at the rear of neutrophils with a subpopulation
of F-actin that is specifically detected by the binding of an F-actin probe that
detects a more stable population of actin. Finally, we show that actin
polymerization, but not the microtubule network, is necessary for the polarized
distribution of PSTPIP1 toward the rear of the cell. Together, our findings
demonstrate that PSTPIP1 is a novel component of the leukocyte uropod that
regulates endocytosis and cell migration.
