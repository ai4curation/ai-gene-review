Mitochondrial homeostasis supports sperm motility and fertilization, but the
genetic factors linking mitochondrial dysfunction to human infertility remain
incompletely defined. Here, we investigated the role of MORN5, a previously
uncharacterized member of the membrane occupation and recognition nexus (MORN)
family, in the regulation of sperm mitochondrial homeostasis. Whole-exome
sequencing of men with idiopathic infertility identified rare MORN5 variants,
including a frameshift variant absent from fertile controls that reduces protein
stability. Two independent Morn5 knockout (KO) mouse lines exhibited male
infertility. Detailed analyses, mainly using the Morn5-5bp line, demonstrated
the reduced sperm count and motility, mitochondrial sheath defects, decreased
membrane potential and ATP, increased mitochondrial oxidative stress and sperm
DNA fragmentation, and reduced electron transport chain complex II/III
activities upon Morn5 deficiency. Heterozygous males showed intermediate
defects, consistent with the dosage-sensitive MORN5 function. MORN5 was enriched
in mitochondrial fractions and associated with respiratory-chain and
mitochondrial quality-control proteins. Transcriptomic, single-cell
transcriptomic, and proteomic analyses further revealed the altered oxidative
phosphorylation, electron transport, and autophagy/mitophagy-related signaling
in KO testes. Although KO sperm failed to support fertilization and
preimplantation development in conventional in vitro fertilization, they
retained developmental competence after intracytoplasmic sperm injection,
suggesting that MORN5 loss primarily compromises sperm functional competence
required for fertilization. These findings establish MORN5 as a previously
unrecognized regulator of sperm mitochondrial homeostasis and male fertility.
