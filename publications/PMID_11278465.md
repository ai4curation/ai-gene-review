# The tyrosine kinase Hck is an inhibitor of HIV-1 replication counteracted by the viral vif protein.
**Authors:** Hassaïne G, Courcoul M, Bessou G, Barthalay Y, Picard C, Olive D, Collette Y, Vigne R, Decroly E
**Journal:** J Biol Chem (2001)
**DOI:** [10.1074/jbc.M009076200](https://doi.org/10.1074/jbc.M009076200)

## Abstract

1. J Biol Chem. 2001 May 18;276(20):16885-93. doi: 10.1074/jbc.M009076200. Epub
2001 Feb 27.

The tyrosine kinase Hck is an inhibitor of HIV-1 replication counteracted by the
viral vif protein.

Hassaïne G(1), Courcoul M, Bessou G, Barthalay Y, Picard C, Olive D, Collette Y,
Vigne R, Decroly E.

Author information:
(1)INSERM Unit 372, Université de la Méditerranée, 163 Avenue de Luminy, 13276

The virus infectivity factor (Vif) protein facilitates the replication of human
immunodeficiency virus type 1 (HIV-1) in primary lymphocytes and macrophages.
Its action is strongly dependent on the cellular environment, and it has been
proposed that the Vif protein counteracts cellular activities that would
otherwise limit HIV-1 replication. Using a glutathione S-transferase pull-down
assay, we identified that Vif binds specifically to the Src homology 3 domain of
Hck, a tyrosine kinase from the Src family. The interaction between Vif and the
full-length Hck was further assessed by co-precipitation assays in vitro and in
human cells. The Vif protein repressed the kinase activity of Hck and was not
itself a substrate for Hck phosphorylation. Within one single replication cycle
of HIV-1, Hck was able to inhibit the production and the infectivity of
vif-deleted virus but not that of wild-type virus. Accordingly, HIV-1 vif-
replication was delayed in Jurkat T cell clones stably expressing Hck. Our data
demonstrate that Hck controls negatively HIV-1 replication and that this
inhibition is suppressed by the expression of Vif. Hck, which is present in
monocyte-macrophage cells, represents the first identified cellular inhibitor of
HIV-1 replication overcome by Vif.
