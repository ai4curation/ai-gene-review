Actin is one of the most abundant proteins in eukaryotic cells and the main
component of the microfilament system. It plays essential roles in numerous
cellular activities, including muscle contraction, maintenance of cell
integrity, and motility, as well as transcriptional regulation. Besides
interacting with various actin-binding proteins (ABPs), proper actin function is
regulated by post-translational modifications (PTMs), such as acetylation,
arginylation, oxidation, and others. Here, we explain how actin PTMs can
contribute to filament formation and stability, and may have additional actin
regulatory functions, which potentially contribute to disease development.
