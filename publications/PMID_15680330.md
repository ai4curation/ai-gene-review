# A new class of transcription factors mediates brassinosteroid-regulated gene expression in Arabidopsis.
**Authors:** Yin Y, Vafeados D, Tao Y, Yoshida S, Asami T, Chory J
**Journal:** Cell (2005)
**DOI:** [10.1016/j.cell.2004.11.044](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2004.11.044)

## Abstract

1. Cell. 2005 Jan 28;120(2):249-59. doi: 10.1016/j.cell.2004.11.044.

A new class of transcription factors mediates brassinosteroid-regulated gene
expression in Arabidopsis.

Yin Y(1), Vafeados D, Tao Y, Yoshida S, Asami T, Chory J.

Author information:
(1)Howard Hughes Medical Institute and Plant Biology Laboratory, The Salk
Institute for Biological Studies, 10010 North Torrey Pines Road, La Jolla, CA
92037, USA.

Brassinosteroids (BRs) signal through a plasma membrane-localized receptor
kinase to regulate plant growth and development. We showed previously that a
novel protein, BES1, accumulates in the nucleus in response to BRs, where it
plays a role in BR-regulated gene expression; however, the mechanism by which
BES1 regulates gene expression is unknown. In this study, we dissect BES1
subdomains and establish that BES1 is a transcription factor that binds to and
activates BR target gene promoters both in vitro and in vivo. BES1 interacts
with a basic helix-loop-helix protein, BIM1, to synergistically bind to E box
(CANNTG) sequences present in many BR-induced promoters. Loss-of-function and
gain-of-function mutants of BIM1 and its close family members display BR
response phenotypes. Thus, BES1 defines a new class of plant-specific
transcription factors that cooperate with transcription factors such as BIM1 to
regulate BR-induced genes.
