# Siroheme biosynthesis in Saccharomyces cerevisiae requires the products of both the MET1 and MET8 genes.
**Authors:** Hansen J, Muldbjerg M, Chérest H, Surdin-Kerjan Y
**Journal:** FEBS Lett (1997)
**DOI:** [10.1016/s0014-5793(96)01423-8](https://doi.org/10.1016/s0014-5793(96)01423-8)

## Abstract

1. FEBS Lett. 1997 Jan 13;401(1):20-4. doi: 10.1016/s0014-5793(96)01423-8.

Siroheme biosynthesis in Saccharomyces cerevisiae requires the products of both
the MET1 and MET8 genes.

Hansen J(1), Muldbjerg M, Chérest H, Surdin-Kerjan Y.

Author information:
(1)Carlsberg Research Laboratory, Copenhagen Valby, Denmark. carlgaer@biobase.dk

Siroheme is a uroporphyrinogen III-derivative used by sulfite reductase as a
prosthetic group. We investigated in Saccharomyces cerevisiae the possible
involvement in siroheme biosynthesis of three genes, MET1, MET8 and MET20. The
MET1 gene from S. cerevisiae was cloned and shown to be the same gene as MET20.
Sequence similitudes as well as complementation studies indicate that Met1p and
Met8p are both involved in siroheme biosynthesis. In addition, we show formally
that S. cerevisiae does not need vitamin B12 for growth.
