# Dual role of the fringe connection gene in both heparan sulphate and fringe-dependent signalling events.
**Authors:** Selva EM, Hong K, Baeg GH, Beverley SM, Turco SJ, Perrimon N, Häcker U
**Journal:** Nat Cell Biol (2001)
**DOI:** [10.1038/ncb0901-809](https://doi.org/10.1038/ncb0901-809)

## Abstract

1. Nat Cell Biol. 2001 Sep;3(9):809-15. doi: 10.1038/ncb0901-809.

Dual role of the fringe connection gene in both heparan sulphate and
fringe-dependent signalling events.

Selva EM(1), Hong K, Baeg GH, Beverley SM, Turco SJ, Perrimon N, Häcker U.

Author information:
(1)Department of Genetics, Howard Hughes Medical Institute, Harvard Medical

Comment in
    Nat Cell Biol. 2001 Oct;3(10):E229-31. doi: 10.1038/ncb1001-e229.

The precise regulation of growth factor signalling is crucial to the molecular
control of development in Drosophila. Post-translational modification of
signalling molecules is one of the mechanisms that modulate developmental
signalling specificity. We describe a new gene, fringe connection (frc), that
encodes a nucleotide-sugar transporter that transfers UDP-glucuronic acid,
UDP-N-acetylglucosamine and possibly UDP-xylose from the cytoplasm into the
lumen of the endoplasmic reticulum/Golgi. Embryos with the frc mutation display
defects in Wingless, Hedgehog and fibroblast growth factor signalling. Clonal
analysis shows that fringe-dependent Notch signalling is disrupted in frc mutant
tissue.
