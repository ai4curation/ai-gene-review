# TLR9 signals after translocating from the ER to CpG DNA in the lysosome.
**Authors:** Latz E, Schoenemeyer A, Visintin A, Fitzgerald KA, Monks BG, Knetter CF, Lien E, Nilsen NJ, Espevik T, Golenbock DT
**Journal:** Nat Immunol (2004)
**DOI:** [10.1038/ni1028](https://doi.org/10.1038/ni1028)

## Abstract

1. Nat Immunol. 2004 Feb;5(2):190-8. doi: 10.1038/ni1028. Epub 2004 Jan 11.

TLR9 signals after translocating from the ER to CpG DNA in the lysosome.

Latz E(1), Schoenemeyer A, Visintin A, Fitzgerald KA, Monks BG, Knetter CF, Lien
E, Nilsen NJ, Espevik T, Golenbock DT.

Author information:

Microbial DNA sequences containing unmethylated CpG dinucleotides activate
Toll-like receptor 9 (TLR9). We have found that TLR9 is localized to the
endoplasmic reticulum (ER) of dendritic cells (DCs) and macrophages. Because
there is no precedent for immune receptor signaling in the ER, we investigated
how TLR9 is activated. We show that CpG DNA binds directly to TLR9 in
ligand-binding studies. CpG DNA moves into early endosomes and is subsequently
transported to a tubular lysosomal compartment. Concurrent with the movement of
CpG DNA in cells, TLR9 redistributes from the ER to CpG DNA-containing
structures, which also accumulate MyD88. Our data indicate a previously unknown
mechanism of cellular activation involving the recruitment of TLR9 from the ER
to sites of CpG DNA uptake, where signal transduction is initiated.
