# A novel ryanodine receptor expressed in pancreatic islets by alternative splicing from type 2 ryanodine receptor gene.
**Authors:** Takasawa S, Kuroki M, Nata K, Noguchi N, Ikeda T, Yamauchi A, Ota H, Itaya-Hironaka A, Sakuramoto-Tsuchida S, Takahashi I, Yoshikawa T, Shimosegawa T, Okamoto H

Cyclic ADP-ribose (cADPR), a potent Ca(2+) mobilizing intracellular messenger
synthesized by CD38, regulates the opening of ryanodine receptors (RyRs).
Increases in intracellular Ca(2+) concentrations in pancreatic islets, resulting
from Ca(2+) mobilization from RyRs as well as Ca(2+) influx from extracellular
sources, are important in insulin secretion by glucose. In the present study, by
screening a rat islet cDNA library, we isolated a novel RyR cDNA (the islet-type
RyR), which is generated from the RyR2 gene by alternative splicing of exons 4
and 75. When the expression vectors for the islet-type and the authentic RyRs
were transfected into HEK293 cells, the islet-type RyR2 as well as the authentic
one showed high affinity [(3)H]ryanodine binding. Intracellular Ca(2+) release
in the islet-type RyR2-transfected cells was enhanced in the presence of cADPR
but not in the authentic RyR2-transfected cells. The islet-type RyR2 mRNA was
expressed in a variety of tissues such as in pancreatic islets, cerebrum, and
cerebellum, whereas the authentic RyR2 mRNA was predominantly expressed in heart
and aorta. These results suggest that the islet-type RyR2 may be an
intracellular target for cADPR signaling.
