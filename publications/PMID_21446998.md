# On the aggregation properties of FMRP--a link with the FXTAS syndrome?
**Authors:** Sjekloća L, Pauwels K, Pastore A
**Journal:** FEBS J (2011)
**DOI:** [10.1111/j.1742-4658.2011.08108.x](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1742-4658.2011.08108.x)

## Abstract

1. FEBS J. 2011 Jun;278(11):1912-21. doi: 10.1111/j.1742-4658.2011.08108.x. Epub
2011 May 9.

On the aggregation properties of FMRP--a link with the FXTAS syndrome?

Sjekloća L(1), Pauwels K, Pastore A.

Author information:
(1)MRC National Institute for Medical Research, London, UK.

Fragile X mental retardation protein (FMRP) is an RNA binding protein necessary
for correct spatiotemporal control of neuronal gene expression in humans. Lack
of functional FMRP causes fragile X mental retardation, which is the most common
inherited neurodevelopmental disorder in humans. In a previous study, we
described the biochemical and biophysical aggregation properties of constructs
spanning the conserved region of FMRP and of two other human fragile X related
(FXR) proteins, FXR1P and FXR2P. Here, we show that the same regions have an
intrinsic tendency to aggregate and spontaneously misfold towards β-rich
structures, also under non-destabilizing conditions. These findings pave the way
to future studies of the mechanism of formation of FXR-containing
ribonucleoprotein granules and suggest a possible link with the as yet poorly
understood FXR proteins' associated pathologies.
