Here, we describe altered sorting of sortilin in adipocytes deficient for the
σ1B-containing AP-1 complex, leading to the inhibition of adipogenesis. The AP-1
complex mediates protein sorting between the trans-Golgi network and endosomes.
Vertebrates express three AP1 σ1 subunit isoforms - σ1A, σ1B and σ1C (also known
as AP1S1, AP1S2 and AP1S3, respectively). σ1B-deficient mice display impaired
recycling of synaptic vesicles and lipodystrophy. Here, we show that sortilin is
overexpressed in adipose tissue from σ1B(-/-) mice, and that its overexpression
in wild-type cells is sufficient to suppress adipogenesis. σ1B-specific binding
of sortilin requires the sortilin DxxD-x12-DSxxxL motif. σ1B deficiency does not
lead to a block of sortilin transport out of a specific organelle, but the
fraction that reaches lysosomes is reduced. Sortilin binds to the receptor DLK1,
an inhibitor of adipocyte differentiation, and the overexpression of sortilin
prevents DLK1 downregulation, leading to enhanced inhibition of adipogenesis.
DLK1 and sortilin expression are not increased in the brain tissue of σ1B(-/-)
mice, although this is the tissue with the highest expression of σ1B and
sortilin. Thus, adipose-tissue-specific and σ1B-dependent routes for the
transport of sortilin exist and are involved in the regulation of adipogenesis
and adipose-tissue mass.
