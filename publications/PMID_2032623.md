# Identification and subcellular localization of apolipoprotein N-acyltransferase in Escherichia coli.
**Authors:** Gupta SD, Wu HC
**Journal:** FEMS Microbiol Lett (1991)
**DOI:** [10.1016/0378-1097(91)90251-5](https://doi.org/10.1016/0378-1097(91)90251-5)

## Abstract

1. FEMS Microbiol Lett. 1991 Feb;62(1):37-41. doi: 10.1016/0378-1097(91)90251-5.

Identification and subcellular localization of apolipoprotein N-acyltransferase
in Escherichia coli.

Gupta SD(1), Wu HC.

Author information:
(1)Department of Microbiology, Uniformed Services University of the Health
Sciences, Bethesda, MD 20889-4799.

Apolipoprotein N-acyltransferase, the enzyme catalyzing the conversion of
apolipoprotein to mature lipoprotein, was detected by an in vitro assay using
[35S]methionine-labeled apolipoprotein as the substrate. Triton X-100
solubilized the enzyme, and was required for its activity. The enzyme showed a
broad pH optimum (pH 6.5-7.5). N-Acylation of apolipoprotein with ethanol-washed
membranes was dependent on exogenous phospholipids, with
phosphatidylethanolamine, phosphatidylglycerol and cardiolipin all showing about
10- to 20-times enhancement of the enzyme activity in the delipidated membranes.
Incubation of apolipoprotein with [3H]palmitate-labeled membranes resulted in
the incorporation of [3H]palmitate into lipoprotein. The enzyme was found to be
enriched in the inner membrane and in the inner membrane/outer membrane mixed
fractions of the E. coli cell envelope.
