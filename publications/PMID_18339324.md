# SLP-2 interacts with prohibitins in the mitochondrial inner membrane and contributes to their stability.
**Authors:** Da Cruz S, Parone PA, Gonzalo P, Bienvenut WV, Tondera D, Jourdain A, Quadroni M, Martinou JC
**Journal:** Biochim Biophys Acta (2008)
**DOI:** [10.1016/j.bbamcr.2008.02.006](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.bbamcr.2008.02.006)

## Abstract

1. Biochim Biophys Acta. 2008 May;1783(5):904-11. doi:
10.1016/j.bbamcr.2008.02.006. Epub 2008 Feb 20.

SLP-2 interacts with prohibitins in the mitochondrial inner membrane and
contributes to their stability.

Da Cruz S(1), Parone PA, Gonzalo P, Bienvenut WV, Tondera D, Jourdain A,
Quadroni M, Martinou JC.

Author information:
(1)Department of Cell Biology, University of Geneva, Geneva, Switzerland.

Stomatin is a member of a large family of proteins including prohibitins,
HflK/C, flotillins, mechanoreceptors and plant defense proteins, that are
thought to play a role in protein turnover. Using different proteomic
approaches, we and others have identified SLP-2, a member of the stomatin gene
family, as a component of the mitochondria. In this study, we show that SLP-2 is
strongly associated with the mitochondrial inner membrane and that it interacts
with prohibitins. Depleting HeLa cells of SLP-2 lead to increased proteolysis of
prohibitins and of subunits of the respiratory chain complexes I and IV. Further
supporting the role of SLP-2 in regulating the stability of specific
mitochondrial proteins, we found that SLP-2 is up-regulated under conditions of
mitochondrial stress leading to increased protein turnover. These data indicate
that SLP-2 plays a role in regulating the stability of mitochondrial proteins
including prohibitins and subunits of respiratory chain complexes.
