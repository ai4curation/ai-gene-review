# Apert syndrome mutations in fibroblast growth factor receptor 2 exhibit increased affinity for FGF ligand.
**Authors:** Anderson J, Burns HD, Enriquez-Harris P, Wilkie AO, Heath JK
**Journal:** Hum Mol Genet (1998)
**DOI:** [10.1093/hmg/7.9.1475](https://doi.org/10.1093/hmg/7.9.1475)

## Abstract

1. Hum Mol Genet. 1998 Sep;7(9):1475-83. doi: 10.1093/hmg/7.9.1475.

Apert syndrome mutations in fibroblast growth factor receptor 2 exhibit
increased affinity for FGF ligand.

Anderson J(1), Burns HD, Enriquez-Harris P, Wilkie AO, Heath JK.

Author information:
(1)School of Biochemistry, University of Birmingham, Edgbaston, Birmingham B15

Dominantly acting mutations of the fibroblast growth factor (FGF) receptor 2
(FGFR2) gene have been implicated in various craniosynostosis syndromes. Apert
syndrome, characterized in addition by syndactyly of the limbs, involves
specific mutations at two adjacent residues, Ser252Trp and Pro253Arg, predicted
to lie in the linker region between IgII and IgIII of the FGFR2 ligand-binding
domain. We have analysed the interaction of FGF ligands with wild-type and
Apert-type mutant FGFR2 ectodomains in solution. Wild-type and Apert-type
receptors form a complex with FGF ligands with a stoichiometry of 2:2
(ligand:receptor). The kinetics and specificity of ligand binding to wild-type
and Apert mutant receptors have been analysed using surface plasmon resonance
techniques. This reveals that Apert mutations, compared with wild-type, exhibit
a selective decrease in the dissociation kinetics of FGF2, but not of other FGF
ligands examined. In contrast, the substitution Ser252Leu in FGFR2, previously
observed in several asymptomatic individuals, exhibited wild-type kinetics.
These findings indicate that Apert syndrome arises as a result of increased
affinity of mutant receptors for specific FGF ligands which leads to activation
of signalling under conditions where availability of ligand is limiting.
