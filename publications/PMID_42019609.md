Bovine β-CN is a major milk protein that exists as multiple genetic variants,
distinguished by differences in their AA sequences. Among these, the A1 and A2
variants are the most extensively studied. They differ at position 67, where
β-CN A2 contains a proline residue, whereas β-CN A1 has a histidine. This single
AA substitution may influence the enzymatic hydrolysis of β-CN, potentially
affecting bioactive peptide release during cheese ripening or human digestion,
of most interest are the β-casomorphins (BCM). These BCM have opioid-like
activity that could affect gastrointestinal signaling. There have been a couple
of studies with small-scale experimental cheeses made from milk differing in the
β-CN A1 or A2 variant, which have investigated a few types of BCM. The cheese
ripening process is very complicated, and many factors influence proteolysis,
which could affect the release of BCM. Exploring commercially manufactured
cheeses made from different variants of β-CN that had different levels of
maturity could help clarify if there are significant differences in the types
and concentrations of a wide range of BCM. We investigated whether differences
in the β-CN polymorphism influenced the proteolytic patterns of a wide range of
commercial A1/A2 and A2/A2 Cheddar cheeses. A total of 26 Cheddar cheese
samples, comprising β-CN A1/A2 (n = 16) and A2/A2 (n = 10) of different ages,
were sourced from individual US cheese manufacturers. The β-CN variant present
in each sample was confirmed via capillary electrophoresis. pH 4.6-soluble
nitrogen/total nitrogen, levels of free AA (FAA), and various types of BCM were
analyzed to assess proteolysis. No significant differences were observed in the
composition of the 2 types of Cheddar except for fat levels. Cheeses made with
A2/A2 milks tended to have higher fat levels; most current manufacturers of
these cheeses tended to be smaller plants, where milk standardization facilities
may be limited. Although laboratory studies have suggested that milk from the A2
variant has poorer rennet coagulation properties, we did not observe any
difference in moisture contents between the 2 cheese types. The moisture content
of cheese can be affected by alterations in the coagulation behavior. The A2/A2
cheeses exhibited increased levels of total FAA, which was likely due to having
several raw milk cheeses within that group. There were no significant
differences in levels of total BCM between cheese groups. However, BCM-7 was
only detected in A1/A2 cheeses (≤9.75 mg/kg). High levels of BCM-9 were found in
both types of cheeses (≤127 mg/kg). The type of β-CN variant in milk affect the
release of individual BCM in Cheddar cheese but did not alter the total
concentration of BCM.
