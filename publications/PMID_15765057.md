# The Schizosaccharomyces pombe gene encoding gamma-glutamyl transpeptidase I is regulated by non-fermentable carbon sources and nitrogen starvation.
**Authors:** Kim HG, Park HJ, Kang HJ, Lim HW, Kim K, Park EH, Ahn K, Lim CJ
**Journal:** J Microbiol (2005)

## Abstract

1. J Microbiol. 2005 Feb;43(1):44-8.

The Schizosaccharomyces pombe gene encoding gamma-glutamyl transpeptidase I is
regulated by non-fermentable carbon sources and nitrogen starvation.

Kim HG(1), Park HJ, Kang HJ, Lim HW, Kim K, Park EH, Ahn K, Lim CJ.

Author information:
(1)Division of Life Sciences, Kangwon National University, Chuncheon 200-701,
Republic of Korea.

In our previous study, the first structural gene (GGTI) encoding g-glutamyl
transpeptidase was cloned and characterized from the fission yeast
Schizosaccharomyces pombe, and its transcription, using the GGTI-lacZ fusion
gene, containing the 1,085 bp upstream region from the translational initiation
point, was found to be enhanced by sodium nitroprusside and
L-buthionine-(S,R)-sulfoximine (BSO). In the present work, regulation of the
GGTI gene was further elucidated. Non-fermentable carbon sources, such as
acetate and ethanol, markedly enhanced the synthesis of beta-galactosidase from
the GGTI-lacZ fusion gene. However, its induction by non-fermentable carbon
sources appeared to be independent of the presence of the Pap1 protein. Nitrogen
starvation also gave rise to induction of GGTI gene expression in a
Pap1-independent manner. The three additional fusion plasmids, carrying 754, 421
and 156 bp regions, were constructed. The sequence responsible for the induction
by non-fermentable carbon sources and nitrogen starvation was identified to
exist within a -421 bp region of the GGTI gene. Taken together, the S. pombe
GGTI gene is regulated by non-fermentable carbon sources and nitrogen
starvation.
