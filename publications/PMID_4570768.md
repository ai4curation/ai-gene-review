# Close linkage of the genes serC (for phosphohydroxy pyruvate transaminase) and serS (for seryl-transfer ribonucleic acid synthetase) in Escherichia coli K-12.
**Authors:** Clarke SJ, Low B, Konigsberg WH
**Journal:** J Bacteriol (1973)
**DOI:** [10.1128/jb.113.3.1091-1095.1973](https://doi.org/10.1128/jb.113.3.1091-1095.1973)
**PMC:** [PMC251668](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC251668/)

## Abstract

1. J Bacteriol. 1973 Mar;113(3):1091-5. doi: 10.1128/jb.113.3.1091-1095.1973.

Close linkage of the genes serC (for phosphohydroxy pyruvate transaminase) and
serS (for seryl-transfer ribonucleic acid synthetase) in Escherichia coli K-12.

Clarke SJ, Low B, Konigsberg WH.

Escherichia coli strain K28, isolated after nitrosoguanidine mutagenesis, was
found to be auxotrophic for serine. It was also temperature sensitive for growth
as a result of producing an altered seryl-transfer ribonucleic acid (tRNA)
synthetase (EC 6.1.1.11, l-serine: tRNA ligase [AMP]). The auxotrophy was caused
by a mutation in the structural gene for phosphohydroxy-pyruvate transaminase
(serC), which was distinct from, but closely linked to, the structural gene for
seryl-tRNA synthetase (serS). We conclude that the relevant genes are in the
order gal-serS-serC-aroA.
