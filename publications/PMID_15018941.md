## Abstract

Dendrites show remarkable diversity in morphology and function, but the
mechanisms that produce the characteristic forms is poorly understood. Insect
systems offer a unique opportunity to manipulate and study identified neurons in
otherwise undisturbed environments. Recent studies in Drosophila show that
dendritic targeting, branching patterns, territories, and metamorphic remodeling
are controlled in specific ways, by intrinsic genetic programs and extrinsic
cues, with important implications for function. Here, we review some recent
advances in our understanding of dendritic development in insects, focusing
primarily on insights that have been gained from studies of Drosophila.
