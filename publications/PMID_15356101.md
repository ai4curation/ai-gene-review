# Cutting edge: direct interaction of TLR4 with NAD(P)H oxidase 4 isozyme is essential for lipopolysaccharide-induced production of reactive oxygen species and activation of NF-kappa B.
**Authors:** Park HS, Jung HY, Park EY, Kim J, Lee WJ, Bae YS
**Journal:** J Immunol (2004)
**DOI:** [10.4049/jimmunol.173.6.3589](https://doi.org/10.4049/jimmunol.173.6.3589)

## Abstract

1. J Immunol. 2004 Sep 15;173(6):3589-93. doi: 10.4049/jimmunol.173.6.3589.

Cutting edge: direct interaction of TLR4 with NAD(P)H oxidase 4 isozyme is
essential for lipopolysaccharide-induced production of reactive oxygen species
and activation of NF-kappa B.

Park HS(1), Jung HY, Park EY, Kim J, Lee WJ, Bae YS.

Author information:
(1)Division of Molecular Life Sciences, Center for Cell Signaling Research, Ewha
Womans University, Seoul, Korea.

LPS, the primary constituent of the outer membrane of Gram-negative bacteria, is
recognized by TLR4. Binding of TLR4 to LPS triggers various cell signaling
pathways including NF-kappaB activation and reactive oxygen species (ROS)
production. In this study, we present the data that LPS-induced ROS generation
and NF-kappaB activation are mediated by a direct interaction of TLR4 with
(NAD(P)H oxidase 4 (Nox) 4), a protein related to gp91phox (Nox2) of phagocytic
cells, in HEK293T cells. Yeast two hybrid and GST pull-down assays indicated
that the COOH-terminal region of Nox4 interacted with the cytoplasmic tail of
TLR4. Knockdown of Nox4 by transfection of small interference RNA specific to
the Nox4 isozyme in HEK293T cells expressing TLR4 along with MD2 and CD14
resulted in inhibition of LPS-induced ROS generation and NF-kappaB activation.
Taken together, these results indicate that direct interaction of TLR4 with Nox4
is involved in LPS-mediated ROS generation and NF-kappaB activation.
