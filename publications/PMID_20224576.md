Sumoylation of eIF4E activates mRNA translation.

Eukaryotic translation initiation factor 4E (eIF4E) is the cap-binding protein
that binds the 5' cap structure of cellular messenger RNAs (mRNAs). Despite the
obligatory role of eIF4E in cap-dependent mRNA translation, how the translation
activity of eIF4E is controlled remains largely undefined. Here, we report that
mammalian eIF4E is regulated by SUMO1 (small ubiquitin-related modifier 1)
conjugation. eIF4E sumoylation promotes the formation of the active eIF4F
translation initiation complex and induces the translation of a subset of
proteins that are essential for cell proliferation and preventing apoptosis.
Furthermore, disruption of eIF4E sumoylation inhibits eIF4E-dependent protein
translation and abrogates the oncogenic and antiapoptotic functions associated
with eIF4E. These data indicate that sumoylation is a new fundamental regulatory
mechanism of protein synthesis. Our findings suggest further that eIF4E
sumoylation might be important in promoting human cancers.
