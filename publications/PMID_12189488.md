# Functional characterization of the new human GABA(A) receptor mutation beta3(R192H).
**Authors:** Buhr A, Bianchi MT, Baur R, Courtet P, Pignay V, Boulenger JP, Gallati S, Hinkle DJ, Macdonald RL, Sigel E
**Journal:** Hum Genet (2002)
**DOI:** [10.1007/s00439-002-0766-7](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00439-002-0766-7)

## Abstract

1. Hum Genet. 2002 Aug;111(2):154-60. doi: 10.1007/s00439-002-0766-7. Epub 2002
Jul  16.

Functional characterization of the new human GABA(A) receptor mutation
beta3(R192H).

Buhr A(1), Bianchi MT, Baur R, Courtet P, Pignay V, Boulenger JP, Gallati S,
Hinkle DJ, Macdonald RL, Sigel E.

Author information:
(1)Department of Pharmacology, University of Bern, Friedbuehlstrasse 49, CH-3010
Bern, Switzerland.

We screened 124 individuals for single nucleotide polymorphisms of the alpha1,
beta3 and gamma2 genes of the GABA(A) receptor in the regions corresponding to
the ligand-binding domains on the protein level. In a patient with chronic
insomnia, a missense mutation was found in the gene of the beta3 subunit. This
mutation results in the substitution of the amino acid residue arginine for
histidine in position 192 (beta3(R192H)). The patient was found to be
heterozygous for this mutation. Functional analysis of human
alpha1beta3(R192H)gamma2S GABA(A) receptors using ultra fast perfusion
techniques revealed a slower rate of the fast phase of desensitization compared
with alpha1beta3gamma2S GABA(A) receptors. Additionally, current deactivation [a
major determinant of inhibitory postsynaptic current (IPSC) duration] was faster
in the mutated receptors. This raises the possibility of decreased GABAergic
inhibition contributing to insomnia, as some members of the patient's family
also suffer from insomnia. The mutation beta3(R192H) might, therefore, be linked
to this condition. The intron/exon boundaries of the alpha1 subunit gene were
also established and three additional variants were found in the alpha1 and
beta3 genes.
