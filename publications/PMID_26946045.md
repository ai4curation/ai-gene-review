# Broad taxonomic characterization of Verticillium wilt resistance genes reveals an ancient origin of the tomato Ve1 immune receptor.

Plant-pathogenic microbes secrete effector molecules to establish themselves on
their hosts, whereas plants use immune receptors to try and intercept such
effectors in order to prevent pathogen colonization. The tomato cell
surface-localized receptor Ve1 confers race-specific resistance against race 1
strains of the soil-borne vascular wilt fungus Verticillium dahliae which
secrete the Ave1 effector. Here, we describe the cloning and characterization of
Ve1 homologues from tobacco (Nicotiana glutinosa), potato (Solanum tuberosum),
wild eggplant (Solanum torvum) and hop (Humulus lupulus), and demonstrate that
particular Ve1 homologues govern resistance against V. dahliae race 1 strains
through the recognition of the Ave1 effector. Phylogenetic analysis shows that
Ve1 homologues are widely distributed in land plants. Thus, our study suggests
an ancient origin of the Ve1 immune receptor in the plant kingdom.
