Comment in
    Science. 1996 May 17;272(5264):974-5. doi: 10.1126/science.272.5264.974.

The adenomatous polyposis coli gene (APC) is mutated in familial adenomatous
polyposis and in sporadic colorectal tumors, and its product binds to the
adherens junction protein beta-catenin. Overexpression of APC blocks cell cycle
progression. The APC-beta-catenin complex was shown to bind to DLG, the human
homolog of the Drosophila discs large tumor suppressor protein. This interaction
required the carboxyl-terminal region of APC and the DLG homology repeat region
of DLG. APC colocalized with DLG at the lateral cytoplasm in rat colon
epithelial cells and at the synapse in cultured hippocampal neurons. These
results suggest that the APC-DLG complex may participate in regulation of both
cell cycle progression and neuronal function.
