# Regulation of stress-responsive mitogen-activated protein (MAP) kinase pathways by TAO2.
**Authors:** Chen Z, Cobb MH

**DOI:** [10.1074/jbc.M100681200](https://doi.org/10.1074/jbc.M100681200)

## Abstract

1. J Biol Chem. 2001 May 11;276(19):16070-5. doi: 10.1074/jbc.M100681200. Epub
2001  Mar 8.

Regulation of stress-responsive mitogen-activated protein (MAP) kinase pathways
by TAO2.

Chen Z(1), Cobb MH.

Author information:
(1)Department of Pharmacology, University of Texas Southwestern Medical Center,
Dallas, Texas 75390-9041, USA.

Previous studies demonstrated that in vitro the protein kinase TAO2 activates
MAP/ERK kinases (MEKs) 3, 4, and 6 toward their substrates p38 MAP kinase and
c-Jun N-terminal kinase/stress-activated protein kinase (JNK/SAPK). In this
study, we examined the ability of TAO2 to activate stress-sensitive MAP kinase
pathways in cells and the relationship between activation of TAO2 and potential
downstream pathways. Over-expression of TAO2 activated endogenous JNK/SAPK and
p38 but not ERK1/2. Cotransfection experiments suggested that TAO2 selectively
activates MEK3 and MEK6 but not MEKs 1, 4, or 7. Coimmunoprecipitation
demonstrated that endogenous TAO2 specifically associates with MEK3 and MEK6
providing one mechanism for preferential recognition of MEKs upstream of p38.
Sorbitol, and to a lesser extent, sodium chloride, Taxol, and nocodazole
increased TAO2 activity toward itself and kinase-dead MEKs 3 and 6. Activation
of endogenous TAO2 during differentiation of C2C12 myoblasts paralleled
activation of p38 but not JNK/SAPK, consistent with the idea that TAO2 is a
physiological regulator of p38 under certain circumstances.
