# beta-Arrestin: a protein that regulates beta-adrenergic receptor function.
**Authors:** Lohse MJ, Benovic JL, Codina J, Caron MG, Lefkowitz RJ
**Journal:** Science (1990)
**DOI:** [10.1126/science.2163110](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.2163110)

## Abstract

1. Science. 1990 Jun 22;248(4962):1547-50. doi: 10.1126/science.2163110.

beta-Arrestin: a protein that regulates beta-adrenergic receptor function.

Lohse MJ(1), Benovic JL, Codina J, Caron MG, Lefkowitz RJ.

Author information:
(1)Howard Hughes Medical Institute, Department of Medicine, Biochemistry and
Cell Biology, Durham, NC 27710.

Homologous or agonist-specific desensitization of beta-adrenergic receptors is
thought to be mediated by a specific kinase, the beta-adrenergic receptor kinase
(beta ARK). However, recent data suggest that a cofactor is required for this
kinase to inhibit receptor function. The complementary DNA for such a cofactor
was cloned and found to encode a 418-amino acid protein homologous to the
retinal protein arrestin. The protein, termed beta-arrestin, was expressed and
partially purified. It inhibited the signaling function of beta
ARK-phosphorylated beta-adrenergic receptors by more than 75 percent, but not
that of rhodopsin. It is proposed that beta-arrestin in concert with beta ARK
effects homologous desensitization of beta-adrenergic receptors.
