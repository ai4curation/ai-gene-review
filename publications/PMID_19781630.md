# UNC119 inhibits dynamin and dynamin-dependent endocytic processes.
**Authors:** Karim Z, Vepachedu R, Gorska M, Alam R
**Journal:** Cell Signal (2010)
**DOI:** [10.1016/j.cellsig.2009.09.022](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cellsig.2009.09.022)

## Abstract

1. Cell Signal. 2010 Jan;22(1):128-37. doi: 10.1016/j.cellsig.2009.09.022. Epub
2009 Sep 23.

UNC119 inhibits dynamin and dynamin-dependent endocytic processes.

Karim Z(1), Vepachedu R, Gorska M, Alam R.

Author information:
(1)Division of Allergy and Immunology, Department of Medicine, National Jewish
Health, 1400 Jackson Street, Denver, CO 80206, USA.

Unc119 is an adapter signaling molecule, which regulates activation of tyrosine
kinases in T cells, eosinophils and fibroblasts. It plays an important role in
the photoreceptor synapses of the retina. Recently, we have shown that it
inhibits bacterial uptake through macropinocytosis. In this paper we demonstrate
a role for Unc119 in clathrin- and caveolae-based endocytosis as well as
macropinocytosis. Depletion of Unc119 in fibroblasts increases, whereas
overexpression inhibits uptake of transferrin, FM4-64, albumin, viruses, and
ligand-coated beads. Physiological stimuli that upregulate the expression of
Unc119 also inhibits endocytosis. Unc119 has the opposite effect on cholera
toxin B uptake, which represents a clathrin- and dynamin-independent endocytic
process. Unc119 interacts with dynamin, a key effector molecule of many
endocytic processes. More importantly, Unc119 inhibits the GTPase activity of
dynamin. Binding of Unc119 to dynamin decreases the association with its binding
partner amphiphysin, a known regulator of dynamin activation. Thus, Unc119
regulates various endocytic pathways through dynamin and sets a threshold point
for vesicular trafficking.
