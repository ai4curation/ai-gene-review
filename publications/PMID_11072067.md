# The LipB protein is a negative regulator of dam gene expression in Escherichia coli.
**Authors:** Vaisvila R, Rasmussen LJ, Lobner-Olesen A, von Freiesleben U, Marinus MG
**Journal:** Biochim Biophys Acta (2000)
**DOI:** [10.1016/s0167-4781(00)00209-8](https://doi.org/10.1016/s0167-4781(00)00209-8)

## Abstract

1. Biochim Biophys Acta. 2000 Nov 15;1494(1-2):43-53. doi:
10.1016/s0167-4781(00)00209-8.

The LipB protein is a negative regulator of dam gene expression in Escherichia
coli.

Vaisvila R(1), Rasmussen LJ, Lobner-Olesen A, von Freiesleben U, Marinus MG.

Author information:
(1)New England Biolabs, Beverly. MA 01915-5599, USA.

Transcription initiation of the major promoter (P2) of the Escherichia coli dam
gene increases with growth rate. The presence of three partially palindromic
motifs, (TTCAGT(N(20))TGAG), designated G (growth)-boxes, within the -52 to +31
region of the promoter, may be related to growth rate control. Deletion of two
of these repeats, downstream of the transcription initiation point, result in
constitutive high activity of the promoter. The unlinked cde-4::miniTn10
insertion also results in severalfold higher activity of the dam P2 promoter,
suggesting that this mutation resulted in the loss of a putative dam P2
repressor. The cde-4 mutation was mapped to the lipB (lipoic acid) gene, which
we show encodes a 24 kDa protein initiating at a TTG codon. LipB is a highly
conserved protein in animal and plant species, other bacteria, Archaea, and
yeast. Plasmids expressing the native or hexahistidine-tagged LipB complement
the phenotype of the cde-4 mutant strain. The level of LipB in vivo was higher
in exponentially growing cells than those in the stationary phase. Three G-box
motifs were also found in the lipB region. Models for the regulation of
expression of the two genes are discussed.
