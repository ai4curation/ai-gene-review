# Role of CD5 in growth regulation of B-1 cells.
**Authors:** Bondada S, Bikah G, Robertson DA, Sen G
**Journal:** Curr Top Microbiol Immunol (2000)
**DOI:** [10.1007/978-3-642-57284-5_15](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-642-57284-5_15)

## Abstract

1. Curr Top Microbiol Immunol. 2000;252:141-9. doi: 10.1007/978-3-642-57284-5_15.

Role of CD5 in growth regulation of B-1 cells.

Bondada S(1), Bikah G, Robertson DA, Sen G.

Author information:
(1)Department of Microbiology and Immunology, Sanders Brown Center on Aging,
University of Kentucky, Lexington, KY 40536-0230, USA.

CD5 is a membrane glycoprotein that is expressed on a subset of B lymphocytes
called B-1 cells, thymocytes and T cells. The CD5+ B-1 cells are normally
unresponsive to surface Ig receptor induced growth signals unless the CD5 gene
is deleted or sequestered away. Here we show that CD5 mediated negative
regulation is unique to B cell receptor (BCR) signaling. The CD5 molecule in
normal B-1 cells is constitutively tyrosine phosphorylated and associates
specifically with SHP-1, an SH2 domain containing protein tyrosine phosphatase.
CD5 promotes a prolonged interaction between BCR and SHP-1, which may be
inhibitory to BCR signaling. CD5 was shown to modulate the function of
autoantibody producing B cells in transgenic mice expressing anti-DNA
antibodies.
