# VEGF and VEGF receptor expression after experimental brain contusion in rat.
**Authors:** Sköld MK, von Gertten C, Sandberg-Nordqvist AC, Mathiesen T, Holmin S
**Journal:** J Neurotrauma (2005)
**DOI:** [10.1089/neu.2005.22.353](https://doi.org/10.1089/neu.2005.22.353)

## Abstract

1. J Neurotrauma. 2005 Mar;22(3):353-67. doi: 10.1089/neu.2005.22.353.

VEGF and VEGF receptor expression after experimental brain contusion in rat.

Sköld MK(1), von Gertten C, Sandberg-Nordqvist AC, Mathiesen T, Holmin S.

Author information:
(1)Department of Neuroscience, Retzius Laboratory, Karolinska Institutet,

Angiogenesis following traumatic brain injury (TBI) may be of importance not
only for post-traumatic reparative processes but also for the development of
secondary injuries. Vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) is a major
regulator of endothelial cell proliferation, angiogenesis, and vascular
permeability, though its possible involvement in secondary injuries after TBI is
largely unknown. This study was undertaken to analyze the expression of VEGF and
the VEGF receptors in experimental brain contusion in rat. Twenty-three adult
female Sprague-Dawley rats were subjected to a focal cerebral contusion injury
by use of a weight-drop model. Four additional rats underwent craniotomy only.
The animals were sacrificed 6 h, or 1, 2, 4, 6, 8, or 16 days post-injury.
Expression of VEGF and the VEGF receptors VEGFR1 (Flt-1) and VEGFR2 (Flk-1) were
studied by in situ hybridization and immunohistochemistry. VEGF messenger (m)RNA
and protein expression were detected in astrocytes, neutrophils, and macrophages
in or adjacent to the injury from 1 day after injury, with a peak expression
after 4-6 days. Flt-1 and Flk-1 mRNA and protein were detected in vessels
adjacent to the lesion from 1 day after injury throughout day 6 after injury. It
was also noted that Flt-1/Flk-1 and VEGF-positive vessels often were negative
for SMI-71, a marker for vessels in areas with blood-brain barrier (BBB). In
conclusion, we have demonstrated that TBI leads to an upregulation of VEGF,
Flt-1, and Flk-1 mRNA and protein in and around the lesion. The data provide a
foundation for future pharmacological intervention studies focusing on
posttraumatic angiogenesis and possible injury repair effects of the VEGF system
in TBI.
