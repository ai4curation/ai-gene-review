# E2F1 induces p19INK4d, a protein involved in the DNA damage response, following UV irradiation.
**Authors:** Carcagno AL, Giono LE, Marazita MC, Castillo DS, Pregi N, Cánepa ET

Central to the maintenance of genomic integrity is the cellular DNA damage
response. Depending on the type of genotoxic stress and through the activation
of multiple signaling cascades, it can lead to cell cycle arrest, DNA repair,
senescence, and apoptosis. p19INK4d, a member of the INK4 family of CDK
inhibitors, plays a dual role in the DNA damage response, inhibiting cell
proliferation and promoting DNA repair. Consistently, p19INK4d has been reported
to become upregulated in response to UV irradiation and a great variety of
genotoxic agents. Here, this induction is shown to result from a transcriptional
stimulatory mechanism that can occur at every phase of the cell cycle except
during mitosis. Moreover, evidence is presented that demonstrates that E2F1 is
involved in the induction of p19INK4d following UV treatment, as it is prevented
by E2F1 protein ablation and DNA-binding inhibition. Specific inhibition of this
regulation using triplex-forming oligonucleotides that target the E2F response
elements present in the p19INK4d promoter also block p19INK4d upregulation and
sensitize cells to DNA damage. These results constitute the first description of
a mechanism for the induction of p19INK4d in response to UV irradiation and
demonstrate the physiological relevance of this regulation following DNA damage.
