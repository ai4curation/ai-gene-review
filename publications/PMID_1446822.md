# Cloning of the goat beta-casein-encoding gene and expression in transgenic mice.
**Authors:** Roberts B, DiTullio P, Vitale J, Hehir K, Gordon K
**Journal:** Gene (1992)
**DOI:** [10.1016/0378-1119(92)90129-d](https://doi.org/10.1016/0378-1119(92)90129-d)

## Abstract

1. Gene. 1992 Nov 16;121(2):255-62. doi: 10.1016/0378-1119(92)90129-d.

Cloning of the goat beta-casein-encoding gene and expression in transgenic mice.

Roberts B(1), DiTullio P, Vitale J, Hehir K, Gordon K.

Author information:
(1)Genzyme Corporation, Framingham, MA 01701.

The goat beta-casein-encoding gene (CSN2), which encodes the most abundant
protein of goat milk, has been cloned and sequenced. The intron/exon
organization of the 9.0-kb goat CSN2 gene is similar to that of other CSN2
genes. Expression of the goat gene was principally restricted to the mammary
gland of lactating transgenic animals. A low level of expression was also
observed in skeletal muscle and skin. In contrast to a rat CSN2 transgene [Lee
et al., Nucleic Acids Res. 16 (1988) 1027-1041], the goat gene was expressed to
a high degree in the lactating mammary gland. Differences in the content or
context of regulatory elements may account for the enhanced performance of the
goat relative to the rat CSN2 gene in transgenic mice.
