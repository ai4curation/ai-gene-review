Author information:
(1)Département de Biologie, Université de Sherbrooke, Sherbrooke, QC, Canada.

Mycobacterium tuberculosis is a remarkable pathogen capable of adapting and
surviving in various harsh conditions. Correct gene expression regulation is
essential for the success of this process. The reversible association of
different sigma factors is a common mechanism for reprogramming bacterial RNA
polymerase and modulating the transcription of numerous genes. Thirteen putative
sigma factors are encoded in the M. tuberculosis genome, several being important
for virulence. Here, we analyse the latest information available on
mycobacterial sigma factors and discuss their roles in the physiology and
virulence of M. tuberculosis.
