Polar transport-dependent local accumulation of auxin provides positional cues
for multiple plant patterning processes. This directional auxin flow depends on
the polar subcellular localization of the PIN auxin efflux regulators.
Overexpression of the PINOID protein kinase induces a basal-to-apical shift in
PIN localization, resulting in the loss of auxin gradients and strong defects in
embryo and seedling roots. Conversely, pid loss of function induces an
apical-to-basal shift in PIN1 polar targeting at the inflorescence apex,
accompanied by defective organogenesis. Our results show that a PINOID-dependent
binary switch controls PIN polarity and mediates changes in auxin flow to create
local gradients for patterning processes.
