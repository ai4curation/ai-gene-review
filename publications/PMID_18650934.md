Plant and animal perception of microbes through pathogen surveillance proteins
leads to MAP kinase signalling and the expression of defence genes. However,
little is known about how plant MAP kinases regulate specific gene expression.
We report that, in the absence of pathogens, Arabidopsis MAP kinase 4 (MPK4)
exists in nuclear complexes with the WRKY33 transcription factor. This complex
depends on the MPK4 substrate MKS1. Challenge with Pseudomonas syringae or
flagellin leads to the activation of MPK4 and phosphorylation of MKS1.
Subsequently, complexes with MKS1 and WRKY33 are released from MPK4, and WRKY33
targets the promoter of PHYTOALEXIN DEFICIENT3 (PAD3) encoding an enzyme
required for the synthesis of antimicrobial camalexin. Hence, wrky33 mutants are
impaired in the accumulation of PAD3 mRNA and camalexin production upon
infection. That WRKY33 is an effector of MPK4 is further supported by the
suppression of PAD3 expression in mpk4-wrky33 double mutant backgrounds. Our
data establish direct links between MPK4 and innate immunity and provide an
example of how a plant MAP kinase can regulate gene expression by releasing
transcription factors in the nucleus upon activation.
