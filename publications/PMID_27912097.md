Recent evidence suggests that lncRNAs play an integral regulatory role in
numerous functions, including determination of cellular identity. We determined
global expression (RNA-seq) and genome-wide profiles (ChIP-seq) of histone
post-translational modifications and p53 binding in human embryonic stem cells
(hESCs) undergoing differentiation to define a high-confidence set of 40
lncRNAs, which are p53 transcriptional targets. We focused on lncRNAs highly
expressed in pluripotent hESCs and repressed by p53 during differentiation to
identify lncPRESS1 as a p53-regulated transcript that maintains hESC
pluripotency in concert with core pluripotency factors. RNA-seq of hESCs
depleted of lncPRESS1 revealed that lncPRESS1 controls a gene network that
promotes pluripotency. Further, we found that lncPRESS1 physically interacts
with SIRT6 and prevents SIRT6 chromatin localization, which maintains high
levels of histone H3K56 and H3K9 acetylation at promoters of pluripotency genes.
In summary, we describe a p53-regulated, pluripotency-specific lncRNA that
safeguards the hESC state by disrupting SIRT6 activity.

INTRODUCTION

Embryonic stem cells (ESCs), derived from the inner cell mass, can differentiate into all somatic cell types and grow indefinitely in culture (Thomson et al., 1998). The chromatin architecture of pluripotent ESCs is markedly altered during differentiation, eliciting epigenetic regulation of specific gene expression programs that determine cell fate and oppose pluripotency (Young, 2011). Numerous transcription factors, chromatin modulators and signaling proteins impact these regulatory programs during differentiation (Young, 2011). Alterations in chromatin, including changes in histone modifications and DNA methylation, play critical roles in establishment of a particular cell lineage (Gifford et al., 2013; Xie et al., 2013). The mechanisms linking chromatin alterations to execution of lineage-specific epigenetic programs during ESC differentiation are largely unknown.

A critical histone post-translational modification (PTM) involved in ESC functions is acetylation of lysine 56 on histone H3 (H3K56ac). H3K56ac is highly associated with upstream regulatory DNA sequences of the core transcriptional network in hESCs (Xie et al., 2009). In addition, H3K56ac interacts with OCT4 to promote pluripotency in mouse ESCs (Tan et al., 2013). High levels of H3K56ac correlate with transcriptional activation of pluripotency-specific genes and are diminished significantly during ESC differentiation (Tan et al., 2013; Xie et al., 2009). Deacetylation of H3K9ac and H3K56ac is mediated by SIRT6 to repress expression of OCT4, SOX2 and NANOG and promote hESC differentiation (Etchegaray et al., 2015). SIRT6 is a chromatin-bound (Kugel and Mostoslavsky, 2014; McCord et al., 2009), NAD-dependent deacetylase of H3K9ac (Michishita et al., 2008) and H3K56ac (Michishita et al., 2009; Yang et al., 2009). However, the mechanisms that restrict SIRT6 activity in ESCs to maintain H3K56ac/K9ac at pluripotency genes are undetermined.

Previously, we and others reported that tumor suppressor p53 is actively involved in differentiation of human and mouse ESCs (Akdemir et al., 2014; Jain et al., 2012; Lin et al., 2005). Genome wide profiling of p53-chromatin enrichment revealed that, while p53 directly activates development-specific genes in hESCs (Akdemir et al., 2014), in mouse ESCs p53 binds and represses enhancer elements to abrogate the expression of pluripotency genes (Li et al., 2012; Lin et al., 2005). Although p53-mediated activation of small non-coding RNAs (ncRNAs), such as miRNAs, in hESCs was reported (Jain et al., 2012), by and large studies of p53 functions in ESCs were focused on determining p53-mediated regulation of protein coding genes.

Amongst the different families of ncRNAs, a major population of RNA transcripts is longer than 200 nucleotides, often polyadenylated and devoid of evident open reading frames (ORFs) (Fatica and Bozzoni, 2014). These longer transcripts that do not encode proteins are defined as long ncRNAs (lncRNAs), although recent findings show that certain, annotated lncRNAs are translated as functional micropeptides (Nelson et al., 2016). The intrinsic nature of nucleic acids confers a dual ability on lncRNAs to function as ligands of proteins and to mediate base-pairing interactions that guide lncRNA-containing RNA/protein complexes to specific RNA and DNA sites (Guttman and Rinn, 2012; Wang and Chang, 2011). Thus, lncRNAs can operate through distinct modes, as signals, scaffolds for protein-protein interactions, molecular decoys or guides to target elements in the genome or transcriptome (Wang and Chang, 2011). The expression of lncRNAs is highly specific to cell- and tissue-type, regulated by mechanisms that continue to unfold and with functions that may have broad impact (Fatica and Bozzoni, 2014). For example, ESC-specific lncRNAs have been identified, several of which integrate with epigenetic machinery to regulate the pluripotent state (Guttman et al., 2011; Loewer et al., 2010; Ng et al., 2012). LncRNAs may modulate or potentiate regulatory response, e.g., p53-regulated lncRNA genes have been reported that not only mediate p53 functions but also fine-tune p53’s transcriptional activity (reviewed in (Grossi et al., 2015)). Whether p53-regulated lncRNAs similarly hone p53-regulatory activity and/or govern cell fate during ESC differentiation remains largely unknown.

Here, by integrating genome wide expression data (RNA-Seq) with p53 chromatin-enrichment profiles (ChIP-Seq) in hESCs undergoing differentiation, we generated a p53-regulated transcriptome of protein-coding and non-coding genes. We centered our investigations on non-coding RNA networks, regulated by p53, and identified a high-confidence set of 40 p53-regulated lncRNAs that are expressed in a cell-type-specific manner. We provide experimental evidence for our discovery of a p53-regulated, pluripotency-specific lncRNA, lncPRESS1, which is essential for maintenance of ESC identity. We show that lncPRESS1 acts as a ‘decoy’ to sequester SIRT6 from targeted chromatin interactions, which supports H3K56ac/K9ac enrichment at promoters of ESC-specific genes and safeguards pluripotency. To our knowledge, this is the first evidence of SIRT6 interactions with lncRNAs that restrict SIRT6 activity.

RESULTSp53 regulates several lncRNAs during hESC differentiationWe defined the human stem cell transcriptome regulated by p53 by RNA-seq analysis of hESCs undergoing differentiation. Human ESCs, depleted of p53 by siRNA (siTP53), and non-target control (siControl) were treated with Retinoic Acid (RA) for 4 days (Fig. 1A, and Supplemental Fig. S1A–B). We isolated total RNA and performed strand-specific, paired-end RNA Illumina sequencing with reads of 100 base pairs (bp) length and obtained 10–27 million reads on average per condition. The reads were assembled using Cufflinks and Cuffmerge (Trapnell et al., 2012) (see Methods). A total of 52,501 transcripts were successfully assembled and annotated based on UCSC gene annotations. Biotype distribution of annotated transcripts revealed that 74% (38,857) correspond to protein-coding mRNAs, while the remaining 26% were classified as various types of non-coding transcripts (Fig. 1B). 18.8% of transcripts correspond to lncRNAs, including antisense, intergenic, sense-overlapping, processed transcripts when compared to a recent hESC lncRNA assembly (Kelley and Rinn, 2012). The remaining 7.2% of RNA transcripts include other types of non-coding RNAs, such as transcripts derived from pseudogenes and retained introns (Fig. 1B).Next, we utilized Cuffdiff2 (Trapnell et al., 2013) (see Methods) to determine differentially expressed transcripts during hESC differentiation, which are dependent on p53 (q-value<0.05, Cuffdiff differential expression analysis based on dispersion method). We performed the following comparisons: i) siControl vs. siControl + RA, ii) siControl vs. siTP53, iii) siControl + RA vs. siTP53 + RA, and iv) siTP53 vs. siTP53 + RA, and obtained 2780, 1093, 1663 and 1752 differentially expressed transcripts respectively (Supplemental Fig. S1C). A union of these comparisons revealed a total of 3,902 differentially expressed transcripts including 275 lncRNAs (7% of total transcripts) (Fig. 1B). Of the total number of differentially expressed transcripts, 87% corresponded to protein-coding genes (Fig. 1B), which include p53-targeted developmental genes as reported previously (Supplemental Table S1) (Akdemir et al., 2014). We focused on identifying lncRNAs from the 275 that are differentially regulated by p53 during hESC differentiation. A supervised heatmap revealed five major categories as: a) RA-induced during differentiation, b) RA-repressed during differentiation, c) p53-repressed, d) p53-activated and a set of lncRNAs that differ in expression between sample pairs but without concordance with treatment groups (Fig. 1C). Interestingly, the RA-induced, differential expression pattern of categories a and b was visibly attenuated by siTP53 compared to siControl. This motivated a more stringent analysis (cutoffs; p<0.005, FDR<0.001) streamlined for differentiation-specific (+RA) datasets (Fig. 1D). Such comparisons revealed 102 lncRNAs that exhibited significantly altered, p53-dependent expression during differentiation, encompassing intergenic (75), antisense (24), sense-overlapping (1), pseudogene-derived (1) and processed (1) transcripts (Fig. 1D–E).To nominate lncRNAs likely regulated by direct binding of p53, we intersected the genomic loci of differentially expressed lncRNAs with genome wide profiles of p53-binding to chromatin (ChIP-Seq), previously generated for hESCs treated with RA. We associated p53-binding with specific lncRNAs if the sites of p53-binding occurred within 10 kb of the lncRNA transcription start site (TSS). During differentiation, p53 was enriched at the promoters of 40 lncRNAs: intergenic (30) and antisense (10) lncRNAs (Supplemental Fig S1D and Table S2). Out of 40 lncRNAs, ~45% (18 lncRNAs) were activated and ~55% (22 lncRNAs) were repressed by p53 during hESC differentiation. Since p53 is activated during RA-induced hESC differentiation (Jain et al., 2012), lncRNA transcripts repressed by p53 during differentiation of hESCs may have pluripotency-specific regulatory functions. Thus, we named these lncRNAs as p53-regulated and ES-specific (LncPRESS) (Supplemental Table S2).Lineage-specific expression of protein coding genes is associated with specific epigenetic landscapes that are altered during changing cellular states (Xie et al., 2013). We integrated our lncRNA profiles with active (H3K4me3) and repressive (H3K27me3) histone marks in hESCs, defined by ChIP-Seq of hESCs with or without 2 days of RA (Akdemir et al., 2014). Genome wide histone modifications were averaged and mapped from 3.5 kb upstream and downstream of lncRNA TSS for each treatment condition (Fig. 1F). Promoters of differentiation-specific p53-activated lncRNAs are occupied with bivalent histone (H3K4me3 and H3K27me3) marks, whereas pluripotent-specific p53-regulated lncRNAs repressed during differentiation are enriched of H3K4me3 before RA treatment (Fig. 1F). During differentiation (+RA), there is a robust reduction in H3K27me3 across the 7 kb region and gain of H3K4me3 at p53-activated lncRNAs; whereas, p53-repressed lncRNAs exhibit no change in low H3K27me3 levels and marked loss of H3K4me3 downstream of the TSS after 2 days of RA treatment (Fig. 1F). In summary, we identified a high-confidence set of 40 lncRNAs that are regulated by p53-binding to their promoters during differentiation of hESCs; the expression of which is controlled by epigenetic regulation of chromatin state.Expression of p53-regulated lncRNAs depends on cellular stateNext, we determined if p53 regulated the expression of specific lncRNAs under distinct differentiation paradigms by treating hESCs with RA, which primarily promotes neuroectoderm (Dupe and Lumsden, 2001), or bone morphogenic protein (BMP), which induces mesoderm and trophoblast lineages (Xu et al., 2002). We selected three representative p53-regulated lncRNAs: i) lncPRESS1, a 756-nt-long multi-exonic, intergenic transcript annotated by Ensemble as ENSG00000232301, ii) lncPRESS2, a 1238–nt-long multi-exonic transcript annotated by Ensemble as ENSG00000249152, and iii) HOTAIRM1, a 1052–nt-long multi-exonic, antisense transcript annotated by Ensemble as ENSG00000233429 (Supplemental Fig S1E and Table S2). HOTAIRM1 (HOXA transcript antisense RNA myeloid-specific 1) was previously reported as antisense to HOXA genes and up-regulated during granulocyte differentiation of NB4 promyelocytic leukemia and normal hematopoietic cells (Zhang et al., 2014a). Depletion of HOTAIRM1 suggested that HOTAIRM1 plays a role in myelopoiesis through modulation of gene expression at the HOXA cluster (Zhang et al., 2009).Addition of RA for 5 days to induce hESC differentiation (Jain et al., 2012) caused a significant time-dependent reduction in lncPRESS1, lncPRESS2 and OCT4, and induction of differentiation-specific lncRNA HOTAIRM1 expression (Fig. 2A and Supplemental Fig 2A). Treatment of hESCs with BMP4 for 5 days induced mesodermal differentiation, marked by increased GATA4 and Brachyury (T) and reduction in pluripotency marker genes OCT4 and NANOG, with no apparent changes in ectoderm-specific PAX6 (Supplemental Fig. 2B). BMP4 treatment resulted in significant reduction in pluripotency-specific lncRNAs, lncPRESS1 and lncPRESS2, and activation of differentiation-specific transcript HOTAIRM1 (Fig. 2B). Compared to RA, BMP4-induced activation of HOTAIRM1 is considerably lower, suggesting lineage specificity in regulation of HOTAIRM1 (Fig. 1A–B).Differentiation-mediated regulation of these lncRNAs is dependent on p53, as hESCs stably expressing shRNA for p53 (shTP53) (Akdemir et al., 2014) showed a significant reduction in repression of lncPRESS1 and lncPRESS2, alongside activation of HOTAIRM1 with either RA (Fig. 2C) or BMP4 (Fig. 2D), compared to shControl (and Supplemental Fig. S2D–F). To confirm p53-dependent regulation of these transcripts, we treated hESCs with Nutlin-3a, a small molecule inhibitor of MDM2 that activates p53 and promotes differentiation of hESCs (Maimets et al., 2008). Addition of Nutlin-3a for 24h caused a ~2-fold induction in p53 protein levels and loss of pluripotency marker OCT4 (RNA and protein) in control cells, but no noticeable changes in OCT4 in Nutlin-treated shTP53 hESCs (Fig 2E–F and Supplemental Fig. S2G). Nutlin-3a treatment activated p53-targets CDKN1A and HOTAIRM1, while down regulating lncPRESS1, all in a p53-dependent manner (Fig. 2F).Further support of lncPRESS1, as a highly regulated, pluripotency-associated transcript, comes from mining single-cell RNA-Seq analysis of 124 individual cells from human pre-implantation embryos and hESCs at different passages (Yan et al., 2013). We performed meta-analysis of these transcriptome landscapes of human early embryos, plotted RPKM values and found that expression of lncPRESS1 starts as early as the oocyte stage and is sustained to the morulae stage, with much higher expression in hESCs (Supplemental Fig S2H). This lncPRESS1 expression pattern parallels OCT4 and NANOG levels, suggesting that lncPRESS1 is highly enriched in the early stages of human embryonic development.To determine how expression of these lncRNAs may be regulated, we assessed RA-induced changes in association of p53 and specifically modified histones at genomic lncRNA loci in hESCs. Schematic representation of our p53 and histone ChIP-Seq data (untreated or +RA) showed p53 binding increased at regulatory regions of p53-responsive lncRNAs (Fig. 3A, and Supplemental Fig S3). The lncPRESS1 locus was enriched with H3K4me3 (−RA) that is lost during differentiation (+RA), concomitant with considerably increased, repressive H327me3 (Fig. 3A). In contrast, HOTAIRM1, which is activated during differentiation, exhibited bivalent H3K4me3 and H3K27me3 PTMs during pluripotency and RA-treatment induced a marked loss of H3K27me3 and gain of H3K4me3 (Fig. 3A). ChIP-qPCR revealed that RA-induced changes in H3K4me3 and H3K27me3 levels on the promoters of lncPRESS1 and HOTAIRM1 are largely dependent on p53 (Fig. 3B). H3K79me2, a mark associated with transcription elongation (Jonkers et al., 2014) is likewise reduced at sites across the lncPRESS1 locus during RA-induced differentiation and dependent on p53 (Fig. 3C). We subjected sequences underlying the p53-enriched DNA regions, mapped by ChIP-Seq (Fig. 3A), to de novo motif analysis and identified p53 binding elements (p53 Response Element, p53RE) at lncPRESS1, HOTAIRM1 and lncPRESS2, highly similar to the canonical RRRCWWGYYY-(spacer)-RRRCWWGYYY consensus p53RE with spacer sequences of variable lengths (Fig. 3D). Validation by ChIP-qPCR revealed significant enrichment of p53 binding, within 24 hours of RA exposure, at the p53REs of HOTAIRM1, lncPRESS1, lncPRESS2 and CDKN1A (Fig. 3E). Histone aggregate plots for H3K4me1, an enhancer-enriched histone mark, compared to H3K4me3 at p53REs at 40-lncRNA promoters, further revealed that these p53 binding sites are not enhancers (Supplemental Fig S3C). RNA fractionation analysis revealed that both lncPRESS1 and HOTAIRM1 are primarily localized to nucleus, as previously reported for lncES3 (Supplemental Fig S3B) (Ng et al., 2012). Unfortunately, compatible primers could not be designed for lncPRESS2 and HOTAIRM1 in these analyses. To determine how expression of these lncRNAs may be regulated, we assessed RA-induced changes in association of p53 and specifically modified histones at genomic lncRNA loci in hESCs. Schematic representation of our p53 and histone ChIP-Seq data (untreated or +RA) showed p53 binding increased at regulatory regions of p53-responsive lncRNAs (Fig. 3A, and Supplemental Fig S3). The lncPRESS1 locus was enriched with H3K4me3 (−RA) that is lost during differentiation (+RA), concomitant with considerably increased, repressive H327me3 (Fig. 3A). In contrast, HOTAIRM1, which is activated during differentiation, exhibited bivalent H3K4me3 and H3K27me3 PTMs during pluripotency and RA-treatment induced a marked loss of H3K27me3 and gain of H3K4me3 (Fig. 3A). ChIP-qPCR revealed that RA-induced changes in H3K4me3 and H3K27me3 levels on the promoters of lncPRESS1 and HOTAIRM1 are largely dependent on p53 (Fig. 3B). H3K79me2, a mark associated with transcription elongation (Jonkers et al., 2014) is likewise reduced at sites across the lncPRESS1 locus during RA-induced differentiation and dependent on p53 (Fig. 3C). We subjected sequences underlying the p53-enriched DNA regions, mapped by ChIP-Seq (Fig. 3A), to de novo motif analysis and identified p53 binding elements (p53 Response Element, p53RE) at lncPRESS1, HOTAIRM1 and lncPRESS2, highly similar to the canonical RRRCWWGYYY-(spacer)-RRRCWWGYYY consensus p53RE with spacer sequences of variable lengths (Fig. 3D). Validation by ChIP-qPCR revealed significant enrichment of p53 binding, within 24 hours of RA exposure, at the p53REs of HOTAIRM1, lncPRESS1, lncPRESS2 and CDKN1A (Fig. 3E). Histone aggregate plots for H3K4me1, an enhancer-enriched histone mark, compared to H3K4me3 at p53REs at 40-lncRNA promoters, further revealed that these p53 binding sites are not enhancers (Supplemental Fig S3C). RNA fractionation analysis revealed that both lncPRESS1 and HOTAIRM1 are primarily localized to nucleus, as previously reported for lncES3 (Supplemental Fig S3B) (Ng et al., 2012). Collectively, our findings demonstrate that nuclear lncRNAs HOTAIRM1, lncPRESS1 and lncPRESS2 are regulated by p53-dependent alterations in chromatin state during differentiation of hESCs.

DISCUSSIONThe pivotal roles p53, as a tumor suppressor mutated in more than 50% of all human cancers, are generally attributed to its functions in activating genes that coordinately protect genomic stability and survival of healthy cells (Vousden and Prives, 2009). Numerous studies defined global profiles of p53 chromatin-binding and p53-regulated transcriptome in multiple model systems (Akdemir et al., 2014; Kenzelmann Broz et al., 2013; Li et al., 2012; Younger et al., 2015), and recent studies focused on profiling p53 activity at noncoding regions of the genome (Grossi et al., 2015). Among noncoding transcripts, lncRNAs are involved in diverse biological processes and often interface with epigenetic machinery as effector molecules that fine-tune transcription regulation, including p53-activity (Grossi et al., 2015; Rinn, 2014). LncRNAs can fold into complex secondary or higher order structures and form ribonucleoprotein complexes that modulate gene expression (Guttman and Rinn, 2012), acting either in ‘cis’ (example: HOTTIP) (Wang et al., 2011) or ‘trans’ (example: HOTAIR) (Tsai et al., 2010) to control cell-specific gene expression (Fatica and Bozzoni, 2014). Nuclear lncRNAs can also have indirect regulatory effects on gene loci by acting as ‘molecular decoys’ or ‘sponges’ that sequester transcription factors (Sun et al., 2013; Wang et al., 2013). Several lncRNAs are aberrantly expressed in diseases, such as cancers, with specific lncRNAs proposed as disease biomarkers (Cheetham et al., 2013). Therefore, identification of p53-regulated, non-coding transcripts in ESCs with parallels in cancer cells or other disease states may offer potential therapeutic targets.Utilizing hESC differentiation as a model system, we comprehensively profiled p53-regulated transcriptome that include both coding (Akdemir et al., 2014) and non-coding genes (this report). We focused our investigations on p53-regulated, long non-coding transcripts and identified a signature of 40 lncRNAs with expression that is perturbed by p53 in a cell-type specific manner. We identified differentiation-specific lncRNAs, such as HOTAIRM1, and several pluripotency-specific transcripts, including lncPRESS1, with expression that correlated with their histone-PTM profiles and p53-regulation. The regulation of lncPRESS1 transcription by p53 binding within 10 kb of the TSS, as shown here, may be indirect through regulation of other transcripts, regulation of distal gene loci or additional cellular factors. We hypothesize that binding of p53 enables recruitment of histone modifiers to alter chromatin structure and repress the lncPRESS1 locus. Further analyses are needed to delineate all facets of p53-mediated regulation of lncRNA expression.LncRNAs are differentially expressed across various stages of differentiation, suggesting that they may be involved in determining cell fate (Derrien et al., 2012; Fatica and Bozzoni, 2014). ESC-specific, pluripotency-associated lncRNAs include lncRNAs induced upon reprogramming fibroblasts to iPSCs (lincROR) (Loewer et al., 2010), approximately 30 expressed in mESCs (Guttman et al., 2011) and hESCs (lncRNA-ES1) (Ng et al., 2012). Certain of these lncRNAs when depleted induce exit from pluripotency or up-regulation of lineage-committed gene expression programs (Guttman et al., 2011; Ng et al., 2012). The studies presented here, and analysis of available datasets (Cacchiarelli et al., 2015), suggest that lncPRESS1 interactions with SIRT6 play a significant role in stem cell maintenance of hESCs. SIRT6 belongs to the mammalian family of seven sirtuin proteins that are involved in diverse biological processes, including genomic stability, glucose metabolism and tumor suppression (Kugel and Mostoslavsky, 2014). SIRT6 is a nuclear histone deacetylase that specifically targets H3K56ac and H3K9ac histone PTMs in embryonic stem cells (Etchegaray et al., 2015). Recently, acetylation of H3K56 was linked to the core-pluripotency circuitry and transcriptional activation of pluripotency genes in hESCs (Xie et al., 2009). Our results suggested a likely role for lncPRESS1 as a “molecular decoy” that sequesters SIRT6 away from selective gene loci to maintain high acetylation of H3K9 and H3K56 in hESCs. During differentiation, lncPRESS1 expression is diminished, which results in SIRT6 recruitment at these genomic loci, deacetylation of H3K56/K9ac and loss of pluripotency (Fig. 7E). Signals that challenge hESC pluripotency, either by RA treatment or by lncPRESS1 depletion, modulate the SIRT6-H3K56ac/K9ac axis to favor repression of pluripotency-specific genes and a parallel induction of differentiation-specific genes. Further investigations are underway to determine how lncPRESS1 sequesters SIRT6 specifically from pluripotency-specific genes, potentially by other DNA-binding factors not identified as lncPRESS1-associated due to the stringent conditions of our RNA pull-down assays. Similarly, assessments of whether lncPRESS1 expression increases the efficiency of reprograming or could replace any core reprogramming factors are underway. Lastly, correlation of expression of lncPRESS1 with progression of diseases such as cancer is unknown. Since lncPRESS1 is repressed by p53, we believe that cancers that harbor mutant TP53 will have robust expression of this lncRNA. Based on the stem cell-specific expression pattern of lncPRESS1, we predict that cancers that relapse or otherwise thrive by a stem cell population may amplify expression of this transcript.
