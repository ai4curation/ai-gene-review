Leber congenital amaurosis (LCA4) has been linked to mutations in the
photoreceptor-specific gene Aryl hydrocarbon interacting protein like 1 (Aipl1).
To investigate the essential role of AIPL1 in retina, we generated a mouse model
of LCA by inactivating the Aipl1 gene. In Aipl1(-/-) retinas, the outer nuclear
layer develops normally, but rods and cones then quickly degenerate. Aipl1(-/-)
mice have highly disorganized, short, fragmented photoreceptor outer segments
and lack both rod and cone electroretinogram responses. Recent biochemical
evidence indicates that AIPL1 can enhance protein farnesylation. Our study
reveals that rod cGMP phosphodiesterase, a farnesylated protein, is absent and
cGMP levels are elevated in AIPL1(-/-) retinas before the onset of degeneration.
Our findings demonstrate that AIPL1 enhances the stability of phosphodiesterase
and is essential for photoreceptor viability.
