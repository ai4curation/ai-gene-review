# Selective regulation of tumor necrosis factor-induced Erk signaling by Src family kinases and the T cell protein tyrosine phosphatase.
**Authors:** van Vliet C, Bukczynska PE, Puryer MA, Sadek CM, Shields BJ, Tremblay ML, Tiganis T
**Journal:** Nat Immunol (2005)
**DOI:** [10.1038/ni1169](https://doi.org/10.1038/ni1169)

## Abstract

1. Nat Immunol. 2005 Mar;6(3):253-60. doi: 10.1038/ni1169. Epub 2005 Feb 6.

Selective regulation of tumor necrosis factor-induced Erk signaling by Src
family kinases and the T cell protein tyrosine phosphatase.

van Vliet C(1), Bukczynska PE, Puryer MA, Sadek CM, Shields BJ, Tremblay ML,
Tiganis T.

Author information:
(1)Department of Biochemistry and Molecular Biology, Monash University, Victoria
3800, Australia.

The proinflammatory cytokine tumor necrosis factor (TNF) modulates cellular
responses through the mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK) and nuclear
factor-kappaB (NF-kappaB) signaling pathways, but the molecular mechanisms
underlying MAPK activation are unknown. T cell protein tyrosine phosphatase
(TCPTP) is essential for hematopoietic development and negatively regulates
inflammatory responses. Using TCPTP-deficient fibroblasts, we show here that
TCPTP regulates TNF-induced MAPK but not NF-kappaB signaling. TCPTP interacted
with the adaptor protein TRAF2, and dephosphorylated and inactivated Src
tyrosine kinases to suppress downstream signaling through extracellular
signal-regulated kinases and production of interleukin 6. These results link
TCPTP and Src tyrosine kinases to the selective regulation of TNF-induced MAPK
signaling and identify a previously unknown mechanism for modulating
inflammatory responses mediated by TNF.
