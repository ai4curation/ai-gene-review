# Potential role for a FTZ-F1 steroid receptor superfamily member in the control of Drosophila metamorphosis.
**Authors:** Lavorgna G, Karim FD, Thummel CS, Wu C
**Journal:** Proc Natl Acad Sci U S A (1993)
**DOI:** [10.1073/pnas.90.7.3004](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.90.7.3004)
**PMC:** [PMC46225](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC46225/)

## Abstract

1. Proc Natl Acad Sci U S A. 1993 Apr 1;90(7):3004-8. doi:
10.1073/pnas.90.7.3004.

Potential role for a FTZ-F1 steroid receptor superfamily member in the control
of Drosophila metamorphosis.

Lavorgna G(1), Karim FD, Thummel CS, Wu C.

Author information:
(1)Laboratory of Biochemistry, National Cancer Institute, National Institutes of
Health, Bethesda, MD 20892.

FTZ-F1, a member of the steroid receptor superfamily, has been implicated in the
activation of the homeobox segmentation gene fushi tarazu early in Drosophila
embryogenesis. We have cloned a developmental isoform of FTZ-F1 and found that
it is expressed as a product of the previously identified, midprepupal
chromosome puff at 75CD. The 75CD puff occurs in the midst of a period of
intense puffing activity that is triggered in response to the steroid hormone
ecdysone at the onset of metamorphosis. Indirect immunofluorescent staining for
FTZ-F1 on Drosophila polytene chromosomes reveals binding to over 150
chromosomal targets, which include 75CD itself and prominent late prepupal puffs
that are predicted to be regulated by midprepupal puff proteins. These results
suggest a role for FTZ-F1 as a regulator of insect metamorphosis and underscore
the repeated utilization of a regulatory protein for widely separate
developmental pathways.
