# The structure of the vacuolar ATPase in Neurospora crassa.
**Authors:** Margolles-Clark E, Tenney K, Bowman EJ, Bowman BJ
**Journal:** J Bioenerg Biomembr (1999)
**DOI:** [10.1023/a:1005403427848](https://doi.org/10.1023/a:1005403427848)

## Abstract

1. J Bioenerg Biomembr. 1999 Feb;31(1):29-37. doi: 10.1023/a:1005403427848.

The structure of the vacuolar ATPase in Neurospora crassa.

Margolles-Clark E(1), Tenney K, Bowman EJ, Bowman BJ.

Author information:
(1)Department of Biology, University of California, Santa Cruz 95064, USA.

The filamentous fungus Neurospora crassa contains many small vacuoles. These
organelles contain high concentrations of polyphosphates and basic amino acids,
such as arginine and ornithine. Because of their size and density, the vacuoles
can be separated from other organelles in the cell. The ATP-driven proton pump
in the vacuolar membrane is a typical V-type ATPase. We examined the size and
structure of this enzyme using radiation inactivation and electron microscopy.
The vacuolar ATPase is a large and complex enzyme, which appears to contain at
least thirteen different types of subunits. We have characterized the genes that
encode eleven of these subunits. In this review, we discuss the possible
function and structure of these subunits.
