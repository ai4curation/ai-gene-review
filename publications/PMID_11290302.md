# The Drosophila sex determination hierarchy modulates wingless and decapentaplegic signaling to deploy dachshund sex-specifically in the genital imaginal disc.
**Authors:** Keisman EL, Baker BS
**Journal:** Development (2001)

## Abstract

1. Development. 2001 May;128(9):1643-56. doi: 10.1242/dev.128.9.1643.

The Drosophila sex determination hierarchy modulates wingless and
decapentaplegic signaling to deploy dachshund sex-specifically in the genital
imaginal disc.

Keisman EL(1), Baker BS.

Author information:
(1)Department of Biological Sciences, Stanford University, Stanford, CA 94305,
USA.

The integration of multiple developmental cues is crucial to the combinatorial
strategies for cell specification that underlie metazoan development. In the
Drosophila genital imaginal disc, which gives rise to the sexually dimorphic
genitalia and analia, sexual identity must be integrated with positional cues,
in order to direct the appropriate sexually dimorphic developmental program. Sex
determination in Drosophila is controlled by a hierarchy of regulatory genes.
The last known gene in the somatic branch of this hierarchy is the transcription
factor doublesex (dsx); however, targets of the hierarchy that play a role in
sexually dimorphic development have remained elusive. We show that the gene
dachshund (dac) is differentially expressed in the male and female genital
discs, and plays sex-specific roles in the development of the genitalia.
Furthermore, the sex determination hierarchy mediates this sex-specific
deployment of dac by modulating the regulation of dac by the pattern formation
genes wingless (wg) and decapentaplegic (dpp). We find that the sex
determination pathway acts cell-autonomously to determine whether dac is
activated by wg signaling, as in females, or by dpp signaling, as in males.
