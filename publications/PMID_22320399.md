# Role of individual phosphorylation sites for the 14-3-3-protein-dependent activation of yeast neutral trehalase Nth1.
**Authors:** Veisova D, Macakova E, Rezabkova L, Sulc M, Vacha P, Sychrova H, Obsil T, Obsilova V
**Journal:** Biochem J (2012)
**DOI:** [10.1042/BJ20111615](https://doi.org/10.1042/BJ20111615)

## Abstract

1. Biochem J. 2012 May 1;443(3):663-70. doi: 10.1042/BJ20111615.

Role of individual phosphorylation sites for the 14-3-3-protein-dependent
activation of yeast neutral trehalase Nth1.

Veisova D(1), Macakova E, Rezabkova L, Sulc M, Vacha P, Sychrova H, Obsil T,
Obsilova V.

Author information:
(1)Institute of Physiology, Academy of Sciences of the Czech Republic v.v.i.,
Videnska 1083, 14220 Prague 4, Czech Republic.

Trehalases are important highly conserved enzymes found in a wide variety of
organisms and are responsible for the hydrolysis of trehalose that serves as a
carbon and energy source as well as a universal stress protectant. Emerging
evidence indicates that the enzymatic activity of the neutral trehalase Nth1 in
yeast is enhanced by 14-3-3 protein binding in a phosphorylation-dependent
manner through an unknown mechanism. In the present study, we investigated in
detail the interaction between Saccharomyces cerevisiae Nth1 and 14-3-3 protein
isoforms Bmh1 and Bmh2. We determined four residues that are phosphorylated by
PKA (protein kinase A) in vitro within the disordered N-terminal segment of
Nth1. Sedimentation analysis and enzyme kinetics measurements show that both
yeast 14-3-3 isoforms form a stable complex with phosphorylated Nth1 and
significantly enhance its enzymatic activity. The 14-3-3-dependent activation of
Nth1 is significantly more potent compared with Ca2+-dependent activation.
Limited proteolysis confirmed that the 14-3-3 proteins interact with the
N-terminal segment of Nth1 where all phosphorylation sites are located.
Site-directed mutagenesis in conjunction with the enzyme activity measurements
in vitro and the activation studies of mutant forms in vivo suggest that Ser60
and Ser83 are sites primarily responsible for PKA-dependent and 14-3-3-mediated
activation of Nth1.
