In the first step of tetrapyrrole biosynthesis in Escherichia coli,
glutamyl-tRNA reductase (GluTR, encoded by hemA) catalyzes the NADPH-dependent
reduction of glutamyl-tRNA to glutamate-1-semialdehyde. Soluble homodimeric E.
coli GluTR was made by co-expressing the hemA gene and the chaperone genes dnaJK
and grpE. During Mg(2+)-stimulated catalysis, the reactive sulfhydryl group of
Cys-50 in the E. coli enzyme attacks the alpha-carbonyl group of the tRNA-bound
glutamate. The resulting thioester intermediate was trapped and detected by
autoradiography. In the presence of NADPH, the end product,
glutamate-1-semialdehyde, is formed. In the absence of NADPH, E. coli GluTR
exhibited substrate esterase activity. The in vitro synthesized unmodified
glutamyl-tRNA was an acceptable substrate for E. coli GluTR. Eight
5-aminolevulinic acid auxotrophic E. coli hemA mutants were genetically
selected, and the corresponding mutations were determined. Most of the
recombinant purified mutant GluTR enzymes lacked detectable activity. Based on
the Methanopyrus kandleri GluTR structure, the positions of the amino acid
exchanges are close to the catalytic domain (G7D, E114K, R314C, S22L/S164F,
G44C/S105N/A326T, G106N, S145F). Only GluTR G191D (affected in NADPH binding)
revealed esterase but no reductase activity.
