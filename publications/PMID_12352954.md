# Dimerization allows DNA target site recognition by the NarL response regulator.
**Authors:** Maris AE, Sawaya MR, Kaczor-Grzeskowiak M, Jarvis MR, Bearson SM, Kopka ML, Schröder I, Gunsalus RP, Dickerson RE
**Journal:** Nat Struct Biol (2002)
**DOI:** [10.1038/nsb845](https://doi.org/10.1038/nsb845)

## Abstract

1. Nat Struct Biol. 2002 Oct;9(10):771-8. doi: 10.1038/nsb845.

Dimerization allows DNA target site recognition by the NarL response regulator.

Maris AE(1), Sawaya MR, Kaczor-Grzeskowiak M, Jarvis MR, Bearson SM, Kopka ML,
Schröder I, Gunsalus RP, Dickerson RE.

Author information:
(1)Department of Chemistry and Biochemistry, University of California, Los
Angeles, P.O. Box 951569, Los Angeles, California 90095-1569, USA.

Two-component signal transduction systems are modular phosphorelay regulatory
pathways common in prokaryotes. In the co-crystal structure of the Escherichia
coli NarL signal output domain bound to DNA, we observe how the NarL family of
two-component response regulators can bind DNA. DNA recognition is accompanied
by the formation of a new dimerization interface, which could occur only in the
full-length protein via a large intramolecular domain rearrangement. The DNA is
recognized by the concerted effects of solvation, van der Waals forces and
inherent DNA deformability, rather than determined primarily by major groove
hydrogen bonding. These subtle forces permit a small DNA-binding domain to
perturb the DNA helix, leading to major DNA curvature and a transition from B-
to A-form DNA at the binding site, where valine on the recognition helix
interacts unexpectedly with the polar major groove floor.
