# Nuclear IKKbeta is an adaptor protein for IkappaBalpha ubiquitination and degradation in UV-induced NF-kappaB activation.
**Authors:** Tsuchiya Y, Asano T, Nakayama K, Kato T Jr, Karin M, Kamata H
**Journal:** Mol Cell (2010)
**DOI:** [10.1016/j.molcel.2010.07.030](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.molcel.2010.07.030)

## Abstract

1. Mol Cell. 2010 Aug 27;39(4):570-82. doi: 10.1016/j.molcel.2010.07.030.

Nuclear IKKbeta is an adaptor protein for IkappaBalpha ubiquitination and
degradation in UV-induced NF-kappaB activation.

Tsuchiya Y(1), Asano T, Nakayama K, Kato T Jr, Karin M, Kamata H.

Author information:
(1)Laboratory of Biomedical Chemistry, Department of Molecular Medical Science,
Graduate School of Biomedical Science, Hiroshima University, Kasumi 1-2-3,
Minami-ku, Hiroshima 734-8553, Japan.

Proinflammatory cytokines activate NF-kappaB using the IkappaB kinase (IKK)
complex that phosphorylates inhibitory proteins (IkappaBs) at N-terminal sites
resulting in their ubiquitination and degradation in the cytoplasm. Although
ultraviolet (UV) irradiation does not lead to IKK activity, it activates
NF-kappaB by an unknown mechanism through IkappaBalpha degradation without
N-terminal phosphorylation. Here, we describe an adaptor function of nuclear
IKKbeta in UV-induced IkappaBalpha degradation. UV irradiation induces the
nuclear translocation of IkappaBalpha and association with IKKbeta, which
constitutively interacts with beta-TrCP through heterogeneous
ribonucleoprotein-U (hnRNP-U) leading to IkappaBalpha ubiquitination and
degradation. Furthermore, casein kinase 2 (CK2) and p38 associate with IKKbeta
and promote IkappaBalpha degradation by phosphorylation at C-terminal sites.
Thus, nuclear IKKbeta acts as an adaptor protein for IkappaBalpha degradation in
UV-induced NF-kappaB activation. NF-kappaB activated by the nuclear IKKbeta
adaptor protein suppresses anti-apoptotic gene expression and promotes
UV-induced cell death.
