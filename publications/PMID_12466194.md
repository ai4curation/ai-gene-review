# Two modes of recruitment of E(spl) repressors onto target genes.
**Authors:** Giagtzoglou N, Alifragis P, Koumbanakis KA, Delidakis C
**Journal:** Development (2003)
**DOI:** [10.1242/dev.00206](https://doi.org/10.1242/dev.00206)

## Abstract

1. Development. 2003 Jan;130(2):259-70. doi: 10.1242/dev.00206.

Two modes of recruitment of E(spl) repressors onto target genes.

Giagtzoglou N(1), Alifragis P, Koumbanakis KA, Delidakis C.

Author information:
(1)Institute of Molecular Biology and Biotechnology, Foundation for Research and
Technology Hellas, Heraklion, Greece.

The decision of ectodermal cells to adopt the sensory organ precursor fate in
Drosophila is controlled by two classes of basic-helix-loop-helix transcription
factors: the proneural Ac and Sc activators promote neural fate, whereas the
E(spl) repressors suppress it. We show here that E(spl) proteins m7 and mgamma
are potent inhibitors of neural fate, even in the presence of excess Sc activity
and even when their DNA-binding basic domain has been inactivated. Furthermore,
these E(spl) proteins can efficiently repress target genes that lack cognate DNA
binding sites, as long as these genes are bound by Ac/Sc activators. This
activity of E(spl)m7 and mgamma correlates with their ability to interact with
proneural activators, through which they are probably tethered on target
enhancers. Analysis of reporter genes and sensory organ (bristle) patterns
reveals that, in addition to this indirect recruitment of E(spl) onto enhancers
via protein-protein interaction with bound Ac/Sc factors, direct DNA binding of
target genes by E(spl) also takes place. Irrespective of whether E(spl) are
recruited via direct DNA binding or interaction with proneural proteins, the
co-repressor Groucho is always needed for target gene repression.
