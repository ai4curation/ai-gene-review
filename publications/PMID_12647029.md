## Abstract

1. J Membr Biol. 2003 Mar 1;192(1):1-8. doi: 10.1007/s00232-002-1059-8.

TRPs make sense.

Voets T(1), Nilius B.

Author information:
(1)Department of Physiology, Campus Gasthuisberg O&N, KU Leuven, B-3000 Leuven,
Belgium.

Drosophila flies with the trp mutation exhibit impaired vision due to the lack
of a specific Ca2+ influx pathway in the photoreceptors. The identification of
the trp gene product as a Ca2+-permeable ion channel and the search for TRP
homologues in flies, worms and mammals has opened the way to the discovery of a
whole superfamily of cation channels, baptized TRP channels. In contrast to
voltage-gated K+, Na+, or Ca2+ channels, with whom they share their
transmembrane architecture, TRP channels are not activated by voltage but by a
variety of signals including intra- and extracellular ligands, Ca2+-store
depletion and mechanical or thermal stress. Due to the promiscuity of these
gating mechanisms, TRP channels are privileged candidates as primary sensing
molecules for the recognition and integration of physical and chemical signals
from the environment. In this review we discuss recent evidence that implicates
members of the TRP superfamily in sensory signal transduction.
