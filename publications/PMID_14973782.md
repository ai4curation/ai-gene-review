## Abstract

1. Am J Hum Genet. 2004 Mar;74(3):545-51. doi: 10.1086/382493. Epub 2004 Feb 17.

Congenital disorder of glycosylation type Ik (CDG-Ik): a defect of
mannosyltransferase I.

Kranz C(1), Denecke J, Lehle L, Sohlbach K, Jeske S, Meinhardt F, Rossi R,
Gudowius S, Marquardt T.

Author information:
(1)Klinik und Poliklinik für Kinderheilkunde, Muenster, Germany.

This study describes the discovery of a new inherited disorder of glycosylation
named "CDG-Ik." CDG-Ik (congenital disorder of glycoslyation type Ik) is based
on a defect of human mannosyltransferase I (MT-I [MIM 605907]), an enzyme
necessary for the elongation of dolichol-linked chitobiose during N-glycan
biosynthesis. Mutations in semiconserved regions in the corresponding gene,
HMT-1 (yeast homologue, Alg1), in two patients caused drastically reduced enzyme
activity, leading to a severe disease with death in early infancy. One patient
had a homozygous point mutation (c.773C-->T, S258L), whereas the other patient
was compound heterozygous for the mutations c.773C-->T and c.1025A-->C (E342P).
Glycosylation and growth of Alg1-deficient PRY56 yeast cells, showing a
temperature-sensitive phenotype, could be restored by the human wild-type
allele, whereas only slight restoration was observed after transformation with
the patients' alleles.
