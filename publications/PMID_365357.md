# Failure of sensory adaptation in bacterial mutants that are defective in a protein methylation reaction.
**Authors:** Goy MF, Springer MS, Adler J
**Journal:** Cell (1978)
**DOI:** [10.1016/0092-8674(78)90049-1](https://doi.org/10.1016/0092-8674(78)90049-1)

## Abstract

1. Cell. 1978 Dec;15(4):1231-40. doi: 10.1016/0092-8674(78)90049-1.

Failure of sensory adaptation in bacterial mutants that are defective in a
protein methylation reaction.

Goy MF, Springer MS, Adler J.

Chemotactic bacteria, such as E. coli, detect changes in the chemical
composition of the environment. Addition of an attractant or repellent leads to
an immediate response, characterized by a change in the swimming behavior of the
cells--a process known as sensory excitation. However, the response gradually
disappears with time, despite the continued presence of the chemical--a process
known as sensory adaptation. We report here the behavior of a class of
nonchemotactic mutants (cheX) that can carry out sensory excitation but are
defective in the process of sensory adaptation. These mutants are also defective
in the ability to carry out a protein methylation reaction which has previously
been implicated in the adaptation process (Goy, Springer and Adler, 1977). The
results presented here establish a firm relationship between the methylation
reaction and sensory adaptation.
