Cooperativity between cytoskeletal proteins is crucial for spatiotemporal
coordination in biological processes, like oogenesis. In mammalian and
Drosophila oogenesis, proper assembly and function of actin networks require
coordination between actin assembly factors Spire and formins, as well as
actin-associated proteins like myosins and Rab GTPases. Here, we investigate the
interaction between Spire and Myosin V (MyoV) in Drosophila oogenesis. We
combine in vitro biochemical assays with immunofluorescence and genetics to
probe the interaction and its impact on polarity establishment and the actin
mesh that fills the oocyte during mid-oogenesis. Expressed Spire and MyoV
constructs colocalize in punctae during mid oogenesis, with considerable
enrichment near the oocyte cortex. Purified constructs interact directly
in vitro, and we find that Spire can weakly activate MyoV ATPase activity.
Cytoplasmic flows, critical for polarity establishment, are faster and more
coordinated in the absence of MyoV, although not to the extent of fast
streaming. This intermediate streaming has not been observed before.
Interestingly, this MyoV-dependent change in ooplasm dynamics is sensitive to
Spire levels. Despite this interplay, the actin mesh and polarity establishment
appear normal when binding mutants of Spire and MyoV are expressed in the
Drosophila germline. These findings suggest that direct interaction between
Spire and MyoV is not essential for their primary roles at this stage of
development.

This sequence as well as the target MyoV‐GTD is highly conserved among insect isoforms of Spir, suggesting that the Spir/MyoV interaction could be preserved in insects (Figure 1C,D). The distribution of exogenously expressed MyoV‐GFP was essentially indistinguishable in wild‐type and spir/+ oocytes. In spir‐null oocytes, however, we observed a decrease in posterior enrichment of MyoV‐GFP (Figure 2C,G). A well‐described phenotype of spir‐null oocytes is the premature onset of rapid cytoplasmic flows, which could be responsible for the difference (Dahlgaard et al. 2007; Theurkauf 1994). We, therefore, examined MyoV‐GFP in capu‐null oocytes, which also exhibit premature cytoplasmic flows (Dahlgaard et al. 2007; Theurkauf 1994). Loss of posterior enrichment of MyoV‐GFP was similar in most capu‐null oocytes (11 eggs from six flies = set 1) but all of the eggs examined from a single fly looked wild type (set 2) (Figure S2F,G). Thus, at this time, we cannot determine whether decreased posterior enrichment of MyoV is an indirect effect, due to mechanical disruption caused by premature streaming, or a more direct effect, due to loss of Spir and/or the actin mesh it builds with Capu. If the MyoV loss from the posterior is due to streaming, as opposed to the absence of Spir, it suggests that there are two populations of MyoV at the posterior: one stably associated with the cortex and another that is not, which may explain why MyoV enrichment was not previously observed.
