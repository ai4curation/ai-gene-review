# ASPM and citron kinase co-localize to the midbody ring during cytokinesis.
**Authors:** Paramasivam M, Chang YJ, LoTurco JJ
**Journal:** Cell Cycle (2007)
**DOI:** [10.4161/cc.6.13.4356](https://doi.org/10.4161/cc.6.13.4356)

ASPM and citron kinase co-localize to the midbody ring during cytokinesis.

Paramasivam M(1), Chang YJ, LoTurco JJ.

Author information:
(1)Department of Physiology and Neurobiology, University of Connecticut, Storrs,

Mutations in ASPM (abnormal spindle-like microcephaly associated) and citron
kinase (CITK) cause primary microcephaly in humans and rodents, respectively.
Both proteins are expressed during neurogenesis and play important roles in
neuronal progenitor cell division. ASPM is localized to the spindle pole, and is
essential for maintaining proliferative cell division. CITK is present at the
cytokinesis furrow and midbody ring, and it is essential for cellular
abscission. We report here that ASPM also localizes to the midbody ring in
mammalian cells. ASPM co-localizes with CITK at the midbody ring and
coimmunoprecipitates with CITK in lysates prepared from HeLa cells and embryonic
neuroepithelium. Furthermore, a GFP-tagged fragment of the N-terminus of ASPM
localizes to centrosomes and spindle poles, while a GFP-tagged fragment of the
C-terminus localizes to midbodies. All reported ASPM mutations that cause
microcephaly involve a truncation or mutation of the C-terminus. In addition, at
least two other microcephaly-related proteins, CENPJ and CDK5RAP2, previously
localized to spindle poles, also localize to midbodies. Together our
observations support a model of neurogenesis in which spindle dynamics and
cellular abscission are coordinated.
