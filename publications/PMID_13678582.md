# Aurora-A and an interacting activator, the LIM protein Ajuba, are required for mitotic commitment in human cells.
**Authors:** Hirota T, Kunitoku N, Sasayama T, Marumoto T, Zhang D, Nitta M, Hatakeyama K, Saya H
**Journal:** Cell (2003)
**DOI:** [10.1016/s0092-8674(03)00642-1](https://doi.org/10.1016/s0092-8674(03)00642-1)

## Abstract

1. Cell. 2003 Sep 5;114(5):585-98. doi: 10.1016/s0092-8674(03)00642-1.

Aurora-A and an interacting activator, the LIM protein Ajuba, are required for
mitotic commitment in human cells.

Hirota T(1), Kunitoku N, Sasayama T, Marumoto T, Zhang D, Nitta M, Hatakeyama K,
Saya H.

Author information:
(1)Department of Tumor Genetics and Biology, Graduate School of Medical

Comment in
    Cell. 2003 Sep 5;114(5):531-2. doi: 10.1016/s0092-8674(03)00685-8.

Aurora family kinases contribute to regulation of mitosis. Using RNA
interference in synchronized HeLa cells, we now show that Aurora-A is required
for mitotic entry. We found that initial activation of Aurora-A in late G2 phase
of the cell cycle is essential for recruitment of the cyclin B1-Cdk1 complex to
centrosomes, where it becomes activated and commits cells to mitosis. A
two-hybrid screen identified the LIM protein Ajuba as an Aurora-A binding
protein. Ajuba and Aurora-A interact in mitotic cells and become phosphorylated
as they do so. In vitro analyses revealed that Ajuba induces the
autophosphorylation and consequent activation of Aurora-A. Depletion of Ajuba
prevented activation of Aurora-A at centrosomes in late G2 phase and inhibited
mitotic entry. Overall, our data suggest that Ajuba is an essential activator of
Aurora-A in mitotic commitment.
