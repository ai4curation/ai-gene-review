# Biochemical characterization and regulation of cardiolipin synthase in Saccharomyces cerevisiae.
**Authors:** Tamai KT, Greenberg ML

## Abstract

1. Biochim Biophys Acta. 1990 Sep 18;1046(2):214-22. doi:
10.1016/0005-2760(90)90192-z.

Biochemical characterization and regulation of cardiolipin synthase in
Saccharomyces cerevisiae.

Tamai KT(1), Greenberg ML.

Author information:
(1)Department of Biological Chemistry, University of Michigan, Ann Arbor
48709-0606.

Cardiolipin (CL) synthase activity was characterized in mitochondrial extracts
of the yeast Saccharomyces cerevisiae and was shown for the first time to
utilize CDP-diacylglycerol as a substrate. CL synthase exhibited a pH optimum of
9.0. Maximal activity was obtained in the presence of 20 mM magnesium with a
Triton X-100: phospholipid ratio of 1:1. The apparent Km values for
phosphatidylglycerol and CDP-diacylglycerol were 1 mM and 36 microM,
respectively. CL synthase activity was maximal at 45 degrees C and heat
inactivation studies showed that the enzyme retained greater than 75% of its
activity at temperatures up to 55 degrees C. To study the regulation of CL
synthase, the enzyme was assayed in cells grown under conditions known to affect
general phospholipid synthesis. Unlike many phospholipid biosynthetic enzymes
including PGP synthase, which catalyzes the initial step in CL biosynthesis, CL
synthase was not repressed in cells grown in the presence of the phospholipid
precursor inositol. Detailed procedures for the enzymatic synthesis of
32P-labelled substrates are described.
