# A novel ligand bound ABC transporter, LolCDE, provides insights into the molecular mechanisms underlying membrane detachment of bacterial lipoproteins.
**Authors:** Ito Y, Kanamaru K, Taniguchi N, Miyamoto S, Tokuda H
**Journal:** Mol Microbiol (2006)
**DOI:** [10.1111/j.1365-2958.2006.05378.x](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1365-2958.2006.05378.x)

## Abstract

1. Mol Microbiol. 2006 Nov;62(4):1064-75. doi: 10.1111/j.1365-2958.2006.05378.x.
Epub 2006 Oct 13.

A novel ligand bound ABC transporter, LolCDE, provides insights into the
molecular mechanisms underlying membrane detachment of bacterial lipoproteins.

Ito Y(1), Kanamaru K, Taniguchi N, Miyamoto S, Tokuda H.

Author information:
(1)Institute of Molecular and Cellular Biosciences, University of Tokyo, 1-1-1
Yayoi, Bunkyo-ku, Tokyo 113-0032, Japan.

The LolCDE complex of Escherichia coli belongs to the ABC transporter
superfamily and initiates the lipoprotein sorting to the outer membrane by
catalysing their release from the inner membrane. LolC and/or LolE, membrane
subunits, recognize lipoproteins anchored to the outer surface of the inner
membrane, while LolD hydrolyses ATP on its inner surface. We report here that
ligand-bound LolCDE can be purified from the inner membrane in the absence of
ATP. Liganded LolCDE represents an intermediate of the release reaction and
exhibits higher affinity for ATP than the unliganded form. ATP binding to LolD
weakens the interaction between LolCDE and lipoproteins and causes their
dissociation in a detergent solution, while lipoprotein release from membranes
requires ATP hydrolysis. Liganded LolCDE thus reveals molecular events brought
about through ATP binding and hydrolysis. LolCDE is the first example of an ABC
transporter purified with tightly bound native substrates. A single molecule of
lipoprotein is found to bind per molecule of the LolCDE complex.
