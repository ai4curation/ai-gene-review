Comment in
    Dev Cell. 2009 Dec;17(6):745-6. doi: 10.1016/j.devcel.2009.11.007.

Host-defense mechanisms against transposable elements are critical to protect
the genome information. Here we show that tudor-domain containing 9 (Tdrd9) is
essential for silencing Line-1 retrotransposon in the mouse male germline. Tdrd9
encodes an ATPase/DExH-type helicase, and its mutation causes male sterility
showing meiotic failure. In Tdrd9 mutants, Line-1 was highly activated and
piwi-interacting small RNAs (piRNAs) corresponding to Line-1 were increased,
suggesting that feedforward amplification operates in the mutant. In fetal
testes, Tdrd9 mutation causes Line-1 desilencing and an aberrant piRNA profile
in prospermatogonia, followed by cognate DNA demethylation. TDRD9 complexes with
MIWI2 with distinct compartmentalization in processing bodies, and this
TDRD9-MIWI2 localization is regulated by MILI and TDRD1 residing at
intermitochondrial cement. Our results identify TDRD9 as a functional partner of
MIWI2 and indicate that the tudor-piwi association is a conserved feature, while
two separate axes, TDRD9-MIWI2 and TDRD1-MILI, cooperate nonredundantly in the
piwi-small RNA pathway in the mouse male germline.
