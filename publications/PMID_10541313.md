# KGF and FGF-10 stimulate liquid secretion in human fetal lung.
**Authors:** Graeff RW, Wang G, McCray PB Jr

**DOI:** [10.1203/00006450-199911000-00006](https://doi.org/10.1203/00006450-199911000-00006)

## Abstract

1. Pediatr Res. 1999 Nov;46(5):523-9. doi: 10.1203/00006450-199911000-00006.

KGF and FGF-10 stimulate liquid secretion in human fetal lung.

Graeff RW(1), Wang G, McCray PB Jr.

Author information:
(1)Department of Pediatrics, University of Iowa College of Medicine, Iowa City
52242, USA.

During fetal life, the pulmonary epithelium secretes liquid that distends the
airways and is important for normal lung growth and development. The factors
regulating human fetal lung liquid secretion are poorly understood; however,
recent studies in murine models show that keratinocyte growth factor (KGF,
FGF-7) and fibroblast growth factor 10 (FGF-10) stimulate liquid secretion. We
asked whether KGF and FGF-10 stimulate liquid secretion in human fetal lung.
First trimester fetal lung explants developed dose-dependent increases in
intraluminal volume in response to KGF and FGF-10. Although there were no acute
changes in explant transepithelial potential difference in response to KGF
(0.1-1000 ng/mL), exposure to 5-50 ng/mL KGF over 60 h depolarized
transepithelial potential difference compared with controls. We used
ribonuclease protection assays to quantitate the ontogeny and regulation of mRNA
expression for KGF and its receptor. Both mRNA were expressed in fetal and
postnatal lung. Because the promoter region of the human KGF gene contains cAMP
and IL-6 response elements, we asked whether cAMP or IL-6 stimulated expression
of KGF or its receptor. We have previously shown that cAMP stimulates liquid
secretion in this model. Both cAMP and IL-6 significantly increased expression
of KGF but not KGF receptor during a 48-h experiment. Thus, stimulation of
liquid secretion in explant models by cAMP may be mediated in part by induction
of KGF expression. KGF and FGF-10 may be important paracrine factors regulating
liquid secretion in human fetal lung.
