# Mre11 mediates gene regulation in yeast spore development.
**Authors:** Kugou K, Sasanuma H, Matsumoto K, Shirahige K, Ohta K
**Journal:** Genes Genet Syst (2007)
**DOI:** [10.1266/ggs.82.21](https://doi.org/10.1266/ggs.82.21)

## Abstract

1. Genes Genet Syst. 2007 Feb;82(1):21-33. doi: 10.1266/ggs.82.21.

Mre11 mediates gene regulation in yeast spore development.

Kugou K(1), Sasanuma H, Matsumoto K, Shirahige K, Ohta K.

Author information:
(1)Genetic System Regulation Laboratory, RIKEN, Discovery Research Institute,
Wako, Saitama, Japan.

Mre11, together with Rad50 and Xrs2/NBS, plays pivotal roles in homologous
recombination, repair of DNA double strand breaks (DSBs), activation of
damage-induced checkpoint, and telomere maintenance. Here we demonstrate that
the absence of Mre11 in yeast causes specific effects on regulation of a class
of meiotic genes for spore development. Using DNA microarray assays to analyze
yeast mutants defective for meiotic DSB formation, we revealed that the meiotic
expression profile in the mre11Delta cells was generally unaffected when
compared to the one in the wild-type strain, although the activation of about 90
meiotic genes were severely and specifically impaired in early meiosis. These
defects were confirmed by northern and lacZ reporter gene assays. Interestingly,
a substantial portion of the severely affected genes includes genes responsible
for spore wall biogenesis, the defects of which may account for the fragile
spore wall phenotype of the mre11Delta strain. The transcriptional deficiency
was not observed in other DSB mutants such as rad50Delta, xrs2Delta, spo11Delta,
and spo11Y135F, suggesting the transcriptional defect in mre11Delta is due to
neither lack of meiotic DSB formation, nor disintegrity of Mre11-Rad50-Xrs2
complex. In addition, the deficiency of mre11Delta in gene activation was not
alleviated by the deletion of RAD24. Therefore, it is unlikely that DNA damage
checkpoint activation by mre11Delta caused transcriptional deficiency. We also
found that a C-terminus DNA binding domain truncation mutant (mre11DeltaC49),
which has meiosis-specific defects, exhibited transcriptional defects as
observed in mre11Delta, whereas an N-terminal phosphoesterase mutant (mre11D16A)
does not. Taken together, we propose that Mre11 is involved in the regulation of
a specific class of genes during spore development through its C-terminus
domain.
