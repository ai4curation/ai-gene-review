# Purification and biochemical characterization of the ATH1 gene product, vacuolar acid trehalase, from Saccharomyces cerevisiae.
**Authors:** Alizadeh P, Klionsky DJ
**Journal:** FEBS Lett (1996)
**DOI:** [10.1016/0014-5793(96)00751-x](https://doi.org/10.1016/0014-5793(96)00751-x)

## Abstract

1. FEBS Lett. 1996 Aug 12;391(3):273-8. doi: 10.1016/0014-5793(96)00751-x.

Purification and biochemical characterization of the ATH1 gene product, vacuolar
acid trehalase, from Saccharomyces cerevisiae.

Alizadeh P(1), Klionsky DJ.

Author information:
(1)Section of Microbiology, University of California, Davis 95616, USA.

The disaccharide trehalose plays a critical role in yeast cell survival during
conditions of environmental stress. The vacuole of the yeast Saccharomyces
cerevisiae contains an enzyme, acid trehalase (ATH), that is capable of
degrading trehalose. Recently, a gene required for ATH activity, ATH1, was
cloned and sequenced [Destruelle et al., (1995) Yeast 11, 1015-1025]. The
relationship between ATH1 and ATH, however, was not determined. We have purified
ATH and shown that it is the ATH1 gene product; peptide sequences from the
purified protein correspond to the deduced amino acid sequence of Ath1p. In
addition, antiserum to Ath1p specifically recognizes purified ATH.
