# Changes in tetrahydrobiopterin levels in endothelial cells and adult cardiomyocytes induced by LPS and hydrogen peroxide--a role for GFRP?
**Authors:** Kalivendi S, Hatakeyama K, Whitsett J, Konorev E, Kalyanaraman B, Vásquez-Vivar J
**Journal:** Free Radic Biol Med (2005)
**DOI:** [10.1016/j.freeradbiomed.2004.11.004](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.freeradbiomed.2004.11.004)

Changes in tetrahydrobiopterin levels in endothelial cells and adult
cardiomyocytes induced by LPS and hydrogen peroxide--a role for GFRP?

Kalivendi S(1), Hatakeyama K, Whitsett J, Konorev E, Kalyanaraman B,
Vásquez-Vivar J.

Author information:
(1)Biophysics Research Institute and Free Radical Research Center, Medical
College of Wisconsin, Milwaukee, WI 5322, USA.

Alterations in tetrahydrobiopterin (BH4) levels have significant consequences in
vascular pathophysiology. However, the mechanisms regulating BH4 remain poorly
understood. The activity of GTP cyclohydrolase I (GTPCH-I), the first enzyme in
BH4 biosynthesis, is controlled by protein levels, posttranslational
modifications and interaction with GTPCH-I feedback regulatory protein (GFRP).
This work examined the correlation between GTPCH-I protein levels and activity
and changes in BH4 in human endothelial cells (HAECs) and adult rat
cardiomyocytes (ARCM). Changes in BH4 were stimulated with LPS in HAECs and
ARCM, and with hydrogen peroxide in HAECs only. Biopterin production by HAECs
and ARCM were attained with concentrations of LPS >>1 microg/ml and responses
were nonlinear with respect to LPS concentrations. Western blot analysis
demonstrated that induction of biopterin synthesis in HAECs and ARCM by LPS does
not entail augmentation of constitutive GTPCH-I protein levels. However, LPS
diminished GFRP mRNA, suggesting that disruption of GTPCH-I:GFRP complex
enhances de novo biopterin synthesis. Conversely, treatment with hydrogen
peroxide increased GTPCH-I and GFRP mRNA levels in HAECs while depleting BH4 and
GSH, which was counteracted by catalase. This indicates that GFRP may override
increases in GTPCH-I protein inhibiting enzyme activity. This conclusion is
further supported by depletion of biopterin in cells transiently transfected
with GFRP. Thus, allosteric regulation of GTPCH-I activity in the cardiovascular
system maybe an important mechanism regulating BH4 levels through GFRP
signaling.
