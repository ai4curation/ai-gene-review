# Enzymatic regulation of methionine/homocysteine levels by the folate pathway dictates broad-spectrum defense in Arabidopsis.
**Authors:** Pati D, Sultan E, Naik AKD, Kesh R, Sahu BB

Folate-dependent one‑carbon (1C) metabolism is vital for plant development and
stress responses, but its specific role in immunity remains unclear. Arabidopsis
Methylenetetrahydrofolate reductase 2 (MTHFR2), a prominent enzyme in 1C
metabolism, is crucial in regulating methionine biosynthesis and controlling
broad-spectrum immunity. Here, we show by HRMS profiling that loss-of-function
mutation in MTHFR2 disturbs methionine and homocysteine concentrations, which
lead to differential immune responses against a broad-spectrum of pathogens,
including Magnaporthe oryzae, Rhizoctonia solani, and Pseudomonas syringae. The
untargeted metabolomic study revealed notable alterations in key 1C metabolites
and a toxic buildup of homocysteine thiolactone that worsened stress regulation
in mthfr2 mutants. Further, we observed that exogenous methionine application
dampened fungal growth, indicating its bi-functional role in immunological
signaling and direct protection. Moreover, the synergistic effects of MTHFR2 and
nonhost resistance genes like HEAT SHOCK PROTEIN 90 (HSP90), SUPPRESSOR OF BIR1
(SOBIR1), and RESPIRATORY BURST OXIDASE HOMOLOGY F (AtRBOHF) significantly
enhanced ROS signaling and defense gene expression. These results indicate that
metabolic imbalances impact salicylic acid- and jasmonic acid-mediated pathways,
thereby alerting immune responses. Our study demonstrates that Arabidopsis
MTHFR2, a key enzyme in folate-dependent 1C metabolism, regulates methionine
biosynthesis and is indispensable for the plant's broad-spectrum immunity. By
linking folate metabolism to the plant's immune cascade, we uncover a crucial
biochemical mechanism that strengthens the plant's resistance to pathogens.
