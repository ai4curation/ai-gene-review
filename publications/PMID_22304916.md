# A transcription factor collective defines cardiac cell fate and reflects lineage history.
**Authors:** Junion G, Spivakov M, Girardot C, Braun M, Gustafson EH, Birney E, Furlong EE
**Journal:** Cell (2012)
**DOI:** [10.1016/j.cell.2012.01.030](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2012.01.030)

## Abstract

1. Cell. 2012 Feb 3;148(3):473-86. doi: 10.1016/j.cell.2012.01.030.

A transcription factor collective defines cardiac cell fate and reflects lineage
history.

Junion G(1), Spivakov M, Girardot C, Braun M, Gustafson EH, Birney E, Furlong
EE.

Author information:
(1)Genome Biology Unit, European Molecular Biology Laboratory, D-69117
Heidelberg, Germany.

Cell fate decisions are driven through the integration of inductive signals and
tissue-specific transcription factors (TFs), although the details on how this
information converges in cis remain unclear. Here, we demonstrate that the five
genetic components essential for cardiac specification in Drosophila, including
the effectors of Wg and Dpp signaling, act as a collective unit to cooperatively
regulate heart enhancer activity, both in vivo and in vitro. Their combinatorial
binding does not require any specific motif orientation or spacing, suggesting
an alternative mode of enhancer function whereby cooperative activity occurs
with extensive motif flexibility. A fraction of enhancers co-occupied by
cardiogenic TFs had unexpected activity in the neighboring visceral mesoderm but
could be rendered active in heart through single-site mutations. Given that
cardiac and visceral cells are both derived from the dorsal mesoderm, this
"dormant" TF binding signature may represent a molecular footprint of these
cells' developmental lineage.
