# A novel phosphatidylinositol 4,5-bisphosphate-binding domain targeting the Phg2 kinase to the membrane in Dictyostelium cells.
**Authors:** Blanc C, Charette S, Cherix N, Lefkir Y, Cosson P, Letourneur F
**Journal:** Eur J Cell Biol (2005)
**DOI:** [10.1016/j.ejcb.2005.09.014](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ejcb.2005.09.014)

## Abstract

1. Eur J Cell Biol. 2005 Dec;84(12):951-60. doi: 10.1016/j.ejcb.2005.09.014. Epub
 2005 Oct 18.

A novel phosphatidylinositol 4,5-bisphosphate-binding domain targeting the Phg2
kinase to the membrane in Dictyostelium cells.

Blanc C(1), Charette S, Cherix N, Lefkir Y, Cosson P, Letourneur F.

Author information:
(1)IFR 128 BioSciences Lyon-Gerland, Institut de Biologie et Chimie des
Protéines, UMR5086, CNRS/Université Lyon I, France.

Phg2 is a ser/thr kinase involved in adhesion, motility, actin cytoskeleton
dynamics, and phagocytosis in Dictyostelium cells. In a search for Phg2 domains
required for its localization to the plasma membrane, we identified a new domain
interacting with phosphatidylinositol 4,5-bisphosphate (PI(4,5)P(2)) and
phosphatidylinositol 4-phosphate (PI(4)P) membrane phosphoinositides. Deletion
of this domain prevented membrane recruitment of Phg2 and proper function of the
protein in the phagocytic process. Moreover, the overexpression of this
PI(4,5)P(2)-binding domain specifically had a dominant-negative effect by
inhibiting phagocytosis. Therefore, plasma membrane recruitment of Phg2 is
essential for its function. The PI(4,5)P(2)-binding domain fused to GFP (green
fluorescent protein) (GFP-Nt-Phg2) was also used to monitor the dynamics of
PI(4,5)P(2) during macropinocytosis and phagocytosis. GFP-Nt-Phg2 disappeared
from macropinosomes immediately after their closure. During phagocytosis,
PI(4,5)P(2) disappeared even before the sealing of phagosomes as it was already
observed in mammalian cells. Together these results demonstrate that PI(4,5)P(2)
metabolism regulates the dynamics and the function of Phg2.
