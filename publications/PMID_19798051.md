The MgtE family of Mg(2+) transporters is ubiquitously distributed in all
phylogenetic domains. Recent crystal structures of the full-length MgtE and of
its cytosolic domain in the presence and absence of Mg(2+) suggested a
Mg(2+)-homeostasis mechanism, in which the MgtE cytosolic domain acts as a
'Mg(2+) sensor' to regulate the gating of the ion-conducting pore in response to
the intracellular Mg(2+) concentration. However, complementary functional
analyses to confirm the proposed model have been lacking. Moreover, the limited
resolution of the full-length structure precluded an unambiguous
characterization of these regulatory divalent-cation-binding sites. Here, we
showed that MgtE is a highly Mg(2+)-selective channel gated by Mg(2+) and
elucidated the Mg(2+)-dependent gating mechanism of MgtE, using X-ray
crystallographic, genetic, biochemical, and electrophysiological analyses. These
structural and functional results have clarified the control of Mg(2+)
homeostasis through cooperative Mg(2+) binding to the MgtE cytosolic domain.
