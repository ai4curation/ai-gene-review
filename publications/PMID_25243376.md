# The redundant aminotransferases in lysine and arginine synthesis and the extent of aminotransferase redundancy in Escherichia coli.
**Authors:** Lal PB, Schneider BL, Vu K, Reitzer L
**Journal:** Mol Microbiol (2014)
**DOI:** [10.1111/mmi.12801](https://doi.org/10.1111/mmi.12801)

## Abstract

1. Mol Microbiol. 2014 Nov;94(4):843-56. doi: 10.1111/mmi.12801. Epub 2014 Oct
12.

The redundant aminotransferases in lysine and arginine synthesis and the extent
of aminotransferase redundancy in Escherichia coli.

Lal PB(1), Schneider BL, Vu K, Reitzer L.

Author information:
(1)Department of Molecular and Cell Biology, The University of Texas at Dallas,
Richardson, TX, 75080, USA.

Aminotransferases can be redundant or promiscuous, but the extent and
significance of these properties is not known in any organism, even in
Escherichia coli. To determine the extent of redundancy, it was first necessary
to identify the redundant aminotransferases in arginine and lysine synthesis,
and then complement all aminotransferase-deficient mutants with genes for all
aminotransferases. The enzymes with N-acetylornithine aminotransferase (ACOAT)
activity in arginine synthesis were ArgD, AstC, GabT and PuuE; the major
anaerobic ACOAT was ArgD. The major enzymes with N-succinyl-l,l-diaminopimelate
aminotransferase (SDAP-AT) activity in lysine synthesis were ArgD, AstC, and
SerC. Seven other aminotransferases, when overproduced, complemented the defect
in a triple mutant. Lysine availability did not regulate synthesis of the major
SDAP-ATs. Complementation analysis of mutants lacking aminotransferases showed
that the SDAP-ATs and alanine aminotransferases were exceptionally redundant,
and it is proposed that this redundancy may ensure peptidoglycan synthesis. An
overview of all aminotransferase reactions indicates that redundancy and broad
specificity are common properties of aminotransferases.
