# Structures of the rat proteasomal ATPases: determination of highly conserved structural motifs and rules for their spacing.
**Authors:** Makino Y, Yogosawa S, Kanemaki M, Yoshida T, Yamano K, Kishimoto T, Moncollin V, Egly JM, Muramatsu M, Tamura T
**Journal:** Biochem Biophys Res Commun (1996)
**DOI:** [10.1006/bbrc.1996.0530](https://doi.org/10.1006/bbrc.1996.0530)

## Abstract

1. Biochem Biophys Res Commun. 1996 Mar 27;220(3):1049-54. doi:
10.1006/bbrc.1996.0530.

Structures of the rat proteasomal ATPases: determination of highly conserved
structural motifs and rules for their spacing.

Makino Y(1), Yogosawa S, Kanemaki M, Yoshida T, Yamano K, Kishimoto T, Moncollin
V, Egly JM, Muramatsu M, Tamura T.

Author information:
(1)Department of Biology, Faculty of Science, Chiba University, Japan.

We have isolated rat cDNAs for all of the five known proteasomal ATPases. The
protein sequences of rat TBP1, TBP7, MSS1, S4, and SUG1 predicted from the open
reading frames consist of 439, 418, 433, 440, and 406 amino acid residues,
respectively, and exhibit striking similarities to each human counterpart with
only several amino acid substitutions. These five rat ATPases are also highly
homologous with each other. The N-terminal region in rat TBP1, TBP7, and SUG1
contains a heptad repeat of hydrophobic amino acids reminiscent of a leucine
zipper. Also, in the central region of each rat ATPase, we found four conserved
motifs, Gx4GKT, DEID, SAT, and H/QRxGRx2R, that are characteristic of a large
family of ATP-dependent RNA/DNA helicases. The spacing between individual motifs
was strictly conserved in the rat ATPases. These findings suggest a common
function of the rat proteasomal ATPases in ATP-dependent RNA/DNA unwinding.
