Comment in
    Mol Cell. 2007 Feb 9;25(3):323-5. doi: 10.1016/j.molcel.2007.01.030.

Endonuclease G (EndoG) is located in mitochondria yet translocates into the
nucleus of apoptotic cells during human degenerative diseases. Nonetheless, a
direct involvement of EndoG in cell-death execution remains equivocal, and the
mechanism for mitochondrio-nuclear translocation is not known. Here, we show
that the yeast homolog of EndoG (Nuc1p) can efficiently trigger apoptotic cell
death when excluded from mitochondria. Nuc1p induces apoptosis in yeast
independently of metacaspase or of apoptosis inducing factor. Instead, the
permeability transition pore, karyopherin Kap123p, and histone H2B interact with
Nuc1p and are required for cell death upon Nuc1p overexpression, suggesting a
pathway in which mitochondrial pore opening, nuclear import, and chromatin
association are successively involved in EndoG-mediated death. Deletion of NUC1
diminishes apoptotic death when mitochondrial respiration is increased but
enhances necrotic death when oxidative phosphorylation is repressed, pointing to
dual--lethal and vital--roles for EndoG.
