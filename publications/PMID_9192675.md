# Homo- and heterodimeric interactions between the gene products of PKD1 and PKD2.
**Authors:** Tsiokas L, Kim E, Arnould T, Sukhatme VP, Walz G
**Journal:** Proc Natl Acad Sci U S A (1997)

## Abstract

1. Proc Natl Acad Sci U S A. 1997 Jun 24;94(13):6965-70. doi:
10.1073/pnas.94.13.6965.

Homo- and heterodimeric interactions between the gene products of PKD1 and PKD2.

Tsiokas L(1), Kim E, Arnould T, Sukhatme VP, Walz G.

Author information:
(1)Renal Division, Department of Medicine, Beth Israel Deaconess Medical Center,
Harvard Medical School, Boston, MA 02215, USA.

PKD1 and PKD2 are two recently identified genes that are responsible for the
vast majority of autosomal polycystic kidney disease, a common inherited disease
that causes progressive renal failure. PKD1 encodes polycystin, a large
glycoprotein that contains several extracellular motifs indicative of a role in
cell-cell or cell-matrix interactions, and the PKD2 encodes a protein with
homology to a voltage-activated calcium channel and to PKD1. It is currently
unknown how mutations of either protein functionally cause autosomal polycystic
kidney disease. We show that PKD1 and PKD2 interact through their C-terminal
cytoplasmic tails. This interaction resulted in an up-regulation of PKD1 but not
PKD2. Furthermore, the cytoplasmic tail of PKD2 but not PKD1 formed homodimers
through a coiled-coil domain distinct from the region required for interaction
with PKD1. These interactions suggest that PKD1 and PKD2 may function through a
common signaling pathway that is necessary for normal tubulogenesis and that
PKD1 may require the presence of PKD2 for stable expression.
