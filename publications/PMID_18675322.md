# A protein kinase C activity localized to neuropeptide Y-like neurons mediates ethanol intoxication in Drosophila melanogaster.
**Authors:** Chen J, Zhang Y, Shen P

## Abstract

1. Neuroscience. 2008 Sep 22;156(1):42-7. doi:
10.1016/j.neuroscience.2008.07.008.  Epub 2008 Jul 10.

A protein kinase C activity localized to neuropeptide Y-like neurons mediates
ethanol intoxication in Drosophila melanogaster.

Chen J(1), Zhang Y, Shen P.

Author information:
(1)The Department of Cellular Biology, and Biomedical and Health Sciences
Institute, 500 D.W. Brooks Drive, University of Georgia, Athens, GA 30602, USA.

Neuropeptide Y (NPY) regulates acute ethanol sensitivity and voluntary alcohol
consumption in rodents. In Drosophila melanogaster, NPY-like neuropeptide F
(NPF) and its receptor NPFR1 display a parallel function, suggesting that an
evolutionarily conserved mechanism may underlie similar behavioral effects of
ethanol in diverse organisms. We have used the fly model to uncover novel genes
and molecular pathways important for acute ethanol response. Here we report a
critical role of the conserved protein kinase C (PKC) pathway in mediating the
intoxicating effect of ethanol. Flies expressing a pseudo-substrate inhibitor of
PKC, directed by npf-gal4, displayed decreased ethanol sensitivity. Furthermore,
the RNA interference analysis suggests that a calcium-independent PKC isoform
(PKC98E, related to mammalian novel PKCs) is largely responsible for the
behavioral phenotype. Finally, we provide evidence that the NPF/PKC-dependent
mechanism selectively affects acute sensitivity but not rapid tolerance to
ethanol intoxication. These findings reveal an uncharacterized role of PKC in
NPY/NPF-mediated acute ethanol sensitivity in flies and possibly mammals.
