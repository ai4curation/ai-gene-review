Alanine racemase is a pyridoxal-5'-phosphate (PLP)-dependent enzyme that
reversibly catalyzes the conversion of l-alanine to d-alanine. d-alanine is an
essential constituent in many prokaryotic cell structures. Inhibition of alanine
racemase is lethal to prokaryotes, creating an attractive target for designing
antibacterial drugs. Here we report the crystal structure of biosynthetic
alanine racemase (Alr) from a pathogenic bacteria Pseudomonas aeruginosa PAO1.
Structural studies showed that P. aeruginosa Alr (PaAlr) adopts a conserved
homodimer structure. A guest substrate d-lysine was observed in the active site
and refined to dual-conformation. Two buffer ions, malonate and acetate, were
bound in the proximity to d-lysine. Biochemical characterization revealed the
optimal reaction conditions for PaAlr.
