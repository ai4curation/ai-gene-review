# cki-1 links cell division and cell fate acquisition in the C. elegans somatic gonad.
**Authors:** Kostić I, Li S, Roy R
**Journal:** Dev Biol (2003)
**DOI:** [10.1016/j.ydbio.2003.07.001](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ydbio.2003.07.001)

## Abstract

1. Dev Biol. 2003 Nov 15;263(2):242-52. doi: 10.1016/j.ydbio.2003.07.001.

The formation of a complex multicellular organism requires the precise
specification of many diverse cell types at the correct time and position
throughout development. This may be achieved by coordinating cell fate
specification processes with progression through the cell cycle. Here, we show
that the extra distal tip cells (DTCs) associated with the loss of cki-1, a
Caenorhabditis elegans homologue of the cyclin-dependent kinase inhibitor p27,
do not arise from duplications of pre-existing DTCs, but that they are formed
from another cell type within the somatic gonad. Results from our laser
microsurgery experiments suggest that the extra DTCs are caused by aberrant
somatic gonadal precursor cell divisions in the absence of cki-1, resulting in
abnormal daughter cell fates. cki-1(RNAi) animals also possess extra anchor
cells and ectopic gonad arms with variable sheath cell numbers and positioning.
In addition, cki-1(RNAi) animals display an endomitotic oocyte (Emo) phenotype.
Our results uncover a novel role of this CKI in cell fate acquisition, either by
directly influencing specification, or through a more conventional role in
appropriately linking cell cycle phase with this process.
