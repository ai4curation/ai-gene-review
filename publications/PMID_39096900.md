Heterochromatin enforces transcriptional gene silencing and can be
epigenetically inherited, but the underlying mechanisms remain unclear. Here, we
show that histone deacetylation, a conserved feature of heterochromatin domains,
blocks SWI/SNF subfamily remodelers involved in chromatin unraveling, thereby
stabilizing modified nucleosomes that preserve gene silencing. Histone
hyperacetylation, resulting from either the loss of histone deacetylase (HDAC)
activity or the direct targeting of a histone acetyltransferase to
heterochromatin, permits remodeler access, leading to silencing defects. The
requirement for HDAC in heterochromatin silencing can be bypassed by impeding
SWI/SNF activity. Highlighting the crucial role of remodelers, merely targeting
SWI/SNF to heterochromatin, even in cells with functional HDAC, increases
nucleosome turnover, causing defective gene silencing and compromised epigenetic
inheritance. This study elucidates a fundamental mechanism whereby histone
hypoacetylation, maintained by high HDAC levels in heterochromatic regions,
ensures stable gene silencing and epigenetic inheritance, providing insights
into genome regulatory mechanisms relevant to human diseases.

INTRODUCTION

The organization of eukaryotic genomes into distinct structural and functional domains enables the regulation of genetic information. Open euchromatin domains allow gene expression, while closed heterochromatin domains prevent inappropriate gene expression.1–3 This organization is achieved through functionally opposing chromatin-modifying factors, exemplified by the Trithorax group (TrxG) and Polycomb group (PcG) proteins.4 TrxG proteins promote chromatin unraveling by adding active histone marks, such as histone acetylation and histone H3 lysine 4 methylation (H3K4me), and collaborating with nucleosome remodeling enzymes.5 PcG proteins enforce transcriptional repression by depositing repressive marks like methylation of histone H3 lysine 27 (H3K27me) and working with its associated effectors, including histone deacetylases (HDACs).4 PcG-mediated repression shares similarities with heterochromatic repression through histone H3 lysine 9 methylation (H3K9me), which recruits heterochromatin protein 1 (HP1) and HDACs for gene silencing.6 Despite these advances, a unifying theme has yet to emerge that explains how repressive chromatin modifications prevent the transcriptional machinery from accessing DNA sequences.

The fission yeast Schizosaccharomyces pombe, possessing chromatin-modifying factors that are conserved in metazoans, is an excellent model for studying functional chromatin organization. Histone acetyltransferases (HATs), including the MYST (MOZ, Ybf2/Sas3, Sas2 and Tip60) family member Mst2, and Gcn5, a subunit of the transcriptional coactivator complex SAGA (Spt-Ada-Gcn5 acetyltransferase), help maintain open chromatin domains.7–10 Moreover, ATP-dependent remodeling enzymes, which alter chromatin structure by remodeling nucleosomes, form conserved protein complexes that include SWI/SNF (switch/sucrose non-fermentable), RSC (remodels the structure of chromatin), INO80C (inositol-requiring mutant 80 complex), and SWR1C (Swi2/Snf2-related 1 complex).11–15 On the other hand, repressive heterochromatin domain assembly, such as at centromeres, telomeres, and the silent mating-type (mat) region, relies on the Clr4 histone methyltransferase.6 Clr4 is the S. pombe homolog of Drosophila Su(var)3–9 and the mammalian SUV39H1 and SUV39H2 enzymes, which methylate H3K9.16,17 Other than methylating H3K9 (“write”), Clr4Suv39h binds to trimethylated H3K9 (H3K9me3) via its chromodomain (“read”).18–20 Through read-write, Clr4Suv39h promotes the spreading and epigenetic inheritance of heterochromatin in cis.18 H3K9me also mediates the association of HP1 proteins with chromatin.16,21,22 HP1 family members engage in low-affinity multivalent interactions and undergo liquid phase separation, potentially creating structures that might exclude transcriptional machinery.23–25 HP1 proteins also serve as a platform for recruiting silencing effectors such as HDACs, which are believed to modify chromatin to enforce heterochromatic transcriptional gene silencing (TGS).6,26–29

The class-II HDAC Clr3 is the most crucial silencing effector for heterochromatic TGS. Clr3 forms a protein complex called the Snf2-HDAC repressor complex (SHREC), related to the mammalian nucleosome remodeling and deacetylase (NuRD) complex.27,30 This complex is guided to heterochromatin by both HP1-dependent26,28,29,31,32 and -independent mechanisms,26,33,34 where it selectively deacetylates histone H3 lysine 14 (H3K14) (Figure 1A).35,36 Importantly, histone deacetylation by Clr3 upholds a key feature distinguishing heterochromatin from euchromatin: high levels of Clr3 bound to heterochromatic regions prevent histone turnover, thus stabilizing nucleosomes and rendering the underlying DNA sequences inaccessible to transcriptional machinery.37 Suppression of histone exchange by Clr3 also helps maintain a critical H3K9me3 density threshold that supports the epigenetic inheritance of heterochromatin.38,39 High H3K9me3 levels support an increased local concentration of Clr4Suv39h on chromatin, supporting the cascade of read-write activity necessary for heterochromatin self-propagation.40 Indeed, robust chromatin association of Clr3 is necessary and sufficient for self-propagation of heterochromatin via pre-existing H3K9me3.38 Without deacetylation of histones by Clr3, histone turnover increases, leading to a decrease in H3K9me3 density and defects in heterochromatin spreading and epigenetic inheritance.37,38 Although the importance of histone deacetylation in stabilizing nucleosomes to cause heterochromatic TGS and transmit epigenetic information is well established, the exact mechanism by which it inhibits histone turnover remains undefined.

Here we investigate the role of Clr3 HDAC-mediated histone deacetylation in maintaining heterochromatin stability and promoting transcriptional silencing. We find that Clr3 prevents SWI/SNF subfamily nucleosome remodelers from engaging heterochromatin domains, thus inhibiting histone turnover and preventing transcriptional machinery from accessing the underlying DNA. Loss of nucleosome remodelers restores heterochromatin propagation and TGS in cells lacking Clr3 HDAC activity. Conversely, directly targeting the SWI/SNF remodeler across silenced domains dismantles heterochromatic structures, even in cells containing functional Clr3. We propose that a dynamic balance between HDACs and HATs is critical for maintaining heterochromatin, with a high local concentration of Clr3 HDAC at heterochromatic loci favoring hypoacetylated histones that block SWI/SNF access. Shifting the balance in favor of HATs allows SWI/SNF to destabilize heterochromatin. Our study reveals how histone deacetylation promotes TGS and maintains the high H3K9me3 density essential for heterochromatin propagation. Since hypoacetylation of histones is the most conserved feature of silenced chromatin domains, our findings have important implications for understanding how heterochromatin promotes stable gene silencing in higher eukaryotes, including mammals.

We then used a sensitive reporter system to detect suppression of heterochromatin defects. Within the silent mat region, two distinct mechanisms silence mating-type genes: repressive heterochromatin that coats ~20 kb domain and local silencer elements, named REII and REIII.41–43 However, in cells carrying a deletion of REII, silencing of mat2P relies entirely on heterochromatin assembly across the silent mat interval. In mat1M cells deficient in mating-type switching (mat1M/smt0) and lacking REII, defects in heterochromatin assembly lead to the derepression of mat2P.42 Simultaneous expression of M and P mating-type information in haploid cells triggers abnormal meiosis (haploid meiosis). Colonies undergoing haploid meiosis exhibit a dark staining when exposed to iodine vapor, while wild-type (WT) colonies stain yellow. The expression of ura4+ inserted adjacent to mat2P (mat2P::ura4+) is tested by using counterselective medium containing 5-fluoroorotic acid (FOA) and provides additional confirmation of heterochromatic silencing defects.Combining clr3D232N with the deletion of components of remodeler complexes including SWR1C (swr1Δ), CHD (hrp3Δ), or RSC (rsc1Δ) that is suggested to induce nucleosome-free regions in heterochromatin,44 caused little or no change in heterochromatic silencing at the silent mat region (Figure S1A). The levels of haploid meiosis and growth on FOA medium in the double mutants were comparable to the clr3D232N single mutant. Additionally, although loss of INO80C components suppresses centromeric silencing defects in RNAi mutant cells,15 mutating the essential catalytic subunit of the INO80C complex (ino80K873A) did not suppress the silencing defect in clr3D232N cells, as indicated by FOA sensitivity (Figure S1A). As expected, unlike the complete GFP repression observed in WT cells, clr3D232N cells expressed GFP, indicating a silencing defect. The loss of either Snf22 or Rsc1 reduced GFP expression in clr3D232N cells, although the effect was greater in clr3D232Nsnf22Δ cells (Figures S1C). Moreover, the percentage of cells with a silenced GFP reporter was further enhanced upon removal of both Snf22 and Rsc1 in clr3D232N cells (Figure 1D). DISCUSSIONRepressive heterochromatin stably silences repetitive DNA and prevents inappropriate gene expression during development. Disrupting its assembly can result in human diseases, including cancer.67 Yet, exactly how heterochromatin causes transcriptional silencing has remained unclear. It is believed that H3K9me recruits HP1 proteins to compact chromatin or create specialized structures, such as liquid condensates, that exclude transcription machinery to cause silencing.23–25 Similar mechanisms have been suggested for Polycomb proteins.68 However, recent studies have challenged these models.69,70An alternative view is that HP1 proteins, bound to H3K9me, serve as a scaffold to attract and concentrate silencing effectors like HDACs, which deacetylate histones.6 Although histone deacetylation is crucial for forming repressive chromatin domains from yeast to mammals1,36,60,71–73, how the removal of histone acetyl marks promotes heterochromatic TGS was not known. This study reveals a fundamental mechanism whereby histone hypoacetylation promotes TGS and maintains H3K9me3 essential for the stable propagation of heterochromatin, by thwarting activities that destabilize nucleosomes to prevent the opening of chromatin.Maintaining heterochromatic silencing by excluding SWI/SNF remodelersWe show that histone deacetylation by HDACs enforces heterochromatic TGS by blocking the SWI/SNF subfamily remodeling enzymes from accessing heterochromatin domains. Loss of Clr3 HDAC increases the localization of SWI/SNF and RSC across heterochromatic regions. Furthermore, targeting remodelers to heterochromatin increased histone turnover and decreased H3K9me3 levels, due to perturbations of nucleosomes. Increased histone exchange leads to the chromatin opening and, ultimately, loss of heterochromatic silencing. Highlighting the functional significance, deleting SWI/SNF and RSC components suppresses defects in both heterochromatic silencing and H3K9me3 in Clr3-deficient cells by decreasing histone turnover. Thus, nucleosome destabilization by remodelers is likely a major cause of H3K9me3 loss and heterochromatic silencing defects in Clr3-deficient cells. Our findings elucidate how HDACs enriched across heterochromatin domains maintain heterochromatin integrity and gene silencing by suppressing histone turnover.How does histone deacetylation prevent chromatin remodelers from accessing heterochromatin? SWI/SNF subfamily remodelers contain bromodomains, which recognize acetyl-lysine marks on histones.50,74,75 Binding of bromodomains to acetylated histones is believed to stabilize the association of remodelers with chromatin.56–59,76 Deacetylation of histone tails removes these binding sites for bromodomain-containing proteins, thereby blocking the association of remodeling enzymes with heterochromatin regions. In Clr3-deficient cells with increased H3K14ac,27,35,36,38 the observed SWI/SNF and RSC localization at heterochromatic loci can be reversed by deleting major HATs, which restores histone hypoacetylation. Histone acetylation is therefore crucial for guiding remodelers to heterochromatic loci. Moreover, targeting of chromodomain-fused Mst2 HAT to H3K9me-coated domains increases H3K14ac and SWI/SNF-dependent destabilization of heterochromatin. Mutating the Snf22 bromodomain in Mst2-CDx2 expressing cells prevents heterochromatin destabilization, highlighting the importance of the bromodomain. This bromodomain-mediated engagement of remodelers might be facilitated by other factors, including transcription factors, that could gain access to heterochromatin domains in HDAC mutant cells. Histone tail modifications could also impact SWI/SNF remodeling activity, beyond affecting remodeler anchoring to chromatin. However, we note that simply targeting SWI/SNF effectively disrupts heterochromatin. This underscores the fact that removing acetyl-lysine marks from heterochromatin regions is important to prevent nucleosome disruption by remodelers and preserve heterochromatin integrity.The discovery that histone acetylation levels, controlled by the dynamic balance between HDACs and HATs, impact heterochromatin stability via mechanisms involving SWI/SNF subfamily remodelers significantly advances our understanding of how these repressive chromatin domains are assembled. Clr4Suv39h, guided to specific genomic sites via RNA- or DNA-based mechanisms, methylates H3K9 to facilitate HP1 localization. HP1 proteins, through low-affinity multivalent interactions, create an interface that concentrates associated silencing effectors, such as HDACs.6 This high concentration of HDACs, driven by HP1-dependent and independent mechanisms (such as at the silent mat region), promotes histone deacetylation within heterochromatin domains, thereby preventing bromodomain-containing remodelers from destabilizing nucleosomes and making the DNA sequences inaccessible to trans-acting factors (Figure 7). Although this mechanism primarily accounts for heterochromatic TGS, other HP1-associated properties, such as condensation and phase separation,23–25 may further contribute by excluding chromatin remodeling enzymes and transcriptional machinery from heterochromatic regions.Impact of remodelers on epigenetic inheritance and reprogramming of heterochromatinIntriguingly, heterochromatin can spread and be epigenetically inherited through a self-templating mechanism.6 We previously showed that self-propagation requires a critical density of H3K9me340, which in turn maintains a high local concentration of Clr4Suv39h, facilitating its read-write activity.6 Efficient targeting of Clr3 HDAC is crucial to suppress histone turnover and sustain H3K9me3 for heterochromatin propagation.37,38 However, it remained unclear how the hypoacetylation of histones protects H3K9me3-modified nucleosomes, which serve as carriers of epigenetic information.We show that excluding remodelers from hypoacetylated regions maintains H3K9me3 and supports heterochromatin self-propagation. Specifically, removing SWI/SNF and RSC in the absence of Clr3 restores heterochromatin spreading. Additionally, remodeler loss allows an ectopic heterochromatin domain to self-propagate across multiple cell generations. In contrast, targeting SWI/SNF to H3K9me3 via a chromodomain fusion impedes epigenetic inheritance of heterochromatin. This highlights the critical role of histone deacetylation in protecting nucleosomes from disruptive remodeling enzymes, preserving H3K9me3, and ensuring stable heterochromatin maintenance.Our work also reveals that remodeling enzymes can impact genes responsive to growth conditions by modulating facultative heterochromatin islands. Cells deficient in SWI/SNF and RSC exhibit increased H3K9me3 and Swi6HP1 at heterochromatin islands, altering gene expression. For example, loss of SWI/SNF subunits downregulates the pho1 gene,11 which is regulated by inorganic phosphate availability,77 however, the mechanism was unknown. We show that this downregulation is linked to increased heterochromatin accumulation at the pho1 locus. Moreover, snf22Δ rsc1Δ cells display significant expansion of telomeric heterochromatin into subtelomeric regions, mirroring the phenotypes observed in cells lacking PAF1 transcription elongation complex components.78,79 Remodeler recruitment by the PAF1 complex could possibly reduce H3K9me via increased histone turnover. In this scenario, favoring remodelers restricts heterochromatin accumulation, ensuring proper gene regulation. These findings raise an intriguing possibility that cells modulate heterochromatin by altering remodeler functions in response to growth conditions.SWI/SNF exclusion in regulating chromatin states in development and diseasesOur work may also help reveal how the opposing chromatin-modifying activities, such as PcG and TrxG proteins govern gene expression patterns during development. Notably, TrxG components, including SWI/SNF, facilitate chromatin unraveling, whereas PcG proteins induce chromatin condensation.4 Similar to H3K9me-HP1, H3K27me3 in PcG-targeted regions serve as a multifunctional scaffold for silencing effectors, recruiting HDACs to genomic regions with low histone turnover.80–82 Based on our findings, histone deacetylation may protect these regions from SWI/SNF engagement, thereby maintaining gene repression. In mouse embryonic stem cells, PcG-repressed domains show less SWI/SNFBAF enrichment, indicating that these regions are refractory to SWI/SNFBAF engagement.83 Despite continuous probing by SWI/SNFBAF and RNAPII, the H3K9me3-enriched constitutive heterochromatin regions evade such probing.83 Conceivably, histone hypoacetylation in these regions may prevent engagement of SWI/SNF and the transcriptional machinery. This HDAC-mediated SWI/SNF exclusion likely ensures proper gene expression during development in higher eukaryotes.Repression via chromatin remodeler exclusion has implications for human diseases. The incursion of SWI/SNFBAF, which can oppose Polycomb complexes,84 into PcG-repressed genome regions is linked to certain cancers.85 Conversely, depletion of SWI/SNFBAF can lead to redistribution of PcG complexes from highly occupied domains (such as Hox gene clusters) to sites normally protected by SWI/SNFBAF remodeling activity.86 Such changes in the genome-wide heterochromatin distribution have also been linked to cancer, as seen in atypical teratoid/rhabdoid tumors.86 This aligns with our findings that disruption of SWI/SNF and RSC activities strengthens heterochromatin at many facultative islands and other loci throughout the genome. Thus, any imbalance between chromatin remodeling and silencing activities can lead to undesired genomic consequences. Future research will provide new mechanistic insights into the importance of SWI/SNF exclusion in safeguarding epigenetic memory and maintaining stable gene expression patterns during development and in the context of human diseases.Limitations of the StudyOur work implicates SWI/SNF subfamily remodelers in controlling the stability and epigenetic inheritance of heterochromatin. However, we cannot rule out roles for other remodeling enzymes in modifying heterochromatin, which may only be apparent under specific genetic backgrounds or chromosomal contexts.87 Moreover, it is possible that increased histone acetylation in the absence of HDAC activity permits transcription factors to bind within heterochromatin domains, which then collaborate with acetylated histones to engage bromodomain-containing remodelers, destabilizing nucleosomes and resulting in loss of silencing. Also, cells lacking SWI/SNF and RSC components show increased accumulation of H3K9me3 at facultative islands, but we did not detect substantial enrichment of these remodelers at these loci. Our ChIP assay may lack the sensitivity to detect these remodelers that likely transiently associate with these loci. Finally, although deleting rsc1 in Clr3-deficient cells markedly reduces histone turnover, H3K9me3 and heterochromatic silencing are not correspondingly increased in these double mutant cells. This indicates that in addition to promoting histone turnover, RSC might affect additional steps required for heterochromatin formation.

An alternative view is that HP1 proteins, bound to H3K9me, serve as a scaffold to attract and concentrate silencing effectors like HDACs, which deacetylate histones.6 Although histone deacetylation is crucial for forming repressive chromatin domains from yeast to mammals1,36,60,71–73, how the removal of histone acetyl marks promotes heterochromatic TGS was not known. This study reveals a fundamental mechanism whereby histone hypoacetylation promotes TGS and maintains H3K9me3 essential for the stable propagation of heterochromatin, by thwarting activities that destabilize nucleosomes to prevent the opening of chromatin.

SWI/SNF exclusion in regulating chromatin states in development and diseasesOur work may also help reveal how the opposing chromatin-modifying activities, such as PcG and TrxG proteins govern gene expression patterns during development. Notably, TrxG components, including SWI/SNF, facilitate chromatin unraveling, whereas PcG proteins induce chromatin condensation.4 Similar to H3K9me-HP1, H3K27me3 in PcG-targeted regions serve as a multifunctional scaffold for silencing effectors, recruiting HDACs to genomic regions with low histone turnover.80–82 Based on our findings, histone deacetylation may protect these regions from SWI/SNF engagement, thereby maintaining gene repression. In mouse embryonic stem cells, PcG-repressed domains show less SWI/SNFBAF enrichment, indicating that these regions are refractory to SWI/SNFBAF engagement.83 Despite continuous probing by SWI/SNFBAF and RNAPII, the H3K9me3-enriched constitutive heterochromatin regions evade such probing.83 Conceivably, histone hypoacetylation in these regions may prevent engagement of SWI/SNF and the transcriptional machinery. This HDAC-mediated SWI/SNF exclusion likely ensures proper gene expression during development in higher eukaryotes.Repression via chromatin remodeler exclusion has implications for human diseases. The incursion of SWI/SNFBAF, which can oppose Polycomb complexes,84 into PcG-repressed genome regions is linked to certain cancers.85 Conversely, depletion of SWI/SNFBAF can lead to redistribution of PcG complexes from highly occupied domains (such as Hox gene clusters) to sites normally protected by SWI/SNFBAF remodeling activity.86 Such changes in the genome-wide heterochromatin distribution have also been linked to cancer, as seen in atypical teratoid/rhabdoid tumors.86 This aligns with our findings that disruption of SWI/SNF and RSC activities strengthens heterochromatin at many facultative islands and other loci throughout the genome. Thus, any imbalance between chromatin remodeling and silencing activities can lead to undesired genomic consequences. Future research will provide new mechanistic insights into the importance of SWI/SNF exclusion in safeguarding epigenetic memory and maintaining stable gene expression patterns during development and in the context of human diseases.

Limitations of the StudyOur work implicates SWI/SNF subfamily remodelers in controlling the stability and epigenetic inheritance of heterochromatin. However, we cannot rule out roles for other remodeling enzymes in modifying heterochromatin, which may only be apparent under specific genetic backgrounds or chromosomal contexts.87 Moreover, it is possible that increased histone acetylation in the absence of HDAC activity permits transcription factors to bind within heterochromatin domains, which then collaborate with acetylated histones to engage bromodomain-containing remodelers, destabilizing nucleosomes and resulting in loss of silencing. Also, cells lacking SWI/SNF and RSC components show increased accumulation of H3K9me3 at facultative islands, but we did not detect substantial enrichment of these remodelers at these loci. Our ChIP assay may lack the sensitivity to detect these remodelers that likely transiently associate with these loci. Finally, although deleting rsc1 in Clr3-deficient cells markedly reduces histone turnover, H3K9me3 and heterochromatic silencing are not correspondingly increased in these double mutant cells. This indicates that in addition to promoting histone turnover, RSC might affect additional steps required for heterochromatin formation.

ChIP-seq analysis

Bedgraphs of ChIP enrichment over untagged control (FE) were produced using the MACS89 ‘callpeaks’ function to make broad calls with options,‘-nomodel --extsize 147’, followed by the ‘bdgcmp’ function to compute fold-enrichment over the input normalized untagged control. Snf22 and Snf21 density was plotted across peak centers from the fold enrichment bedgraphs produced by Macs using a combination of the computeMatrix and plotHeatmap programs of the Deeptools91 suite. The Snf22 and Snf21 peak locations upon which the plots were centered were taken from the local maxima across the genome of the FE vs untagged signal computed using a threshold of 1.00. This procedure yielded ~ 4000 Snf21 or Snf22 peaks per strain.

Replicates were combined per bin by taking the mean. Comparisons of Snf22 and Snf21 enrichments between strains were made based on paired t-tests of the mean signal within the bins comprising the region of comparison.
