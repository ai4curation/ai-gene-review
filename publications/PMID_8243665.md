# The Escherichia coli cysG gene encodes the multifunctional protein, siroheme synthase.
**Authors:** Spencer JB, Stolowich NJ, Roessner CA, Scott AI
**Journal:** FEBS Lett (1993)
**DOI:** [10.1016/0014-5793(93)80438-z](https://doi.org/10.1016/0014-5793(93)80438-z)

## Abstract

1. FEBS Lett. 1993 Nov 29;335(1):57-60. doi: 10.1016/0014-5793(93)80438-z.

The Escherichia coli cysG gene encodes the multifunctional protein, siroheme
synthase.

Spencer JB(1), Stolowich NJ, Roessner CA, Scott AI.

Author information:
(1)Department of Chemistry, Texas A&M University, College Station 77843-3255.

Previously, the E. coli cysG gene product had been shown to sequentially
methylate uro'gen III to produce precorrin-2, hence it was given the trivial
name uro'gen III methylase. We now report that in addition to methylase
activity, the CysG protein catalyses both the NAD+ dependent oxidation of
precorrin-2 to sirohydrochlorin, but also the insertion of iron into this
oxidized intermediate, thereby producing siroheme. Thus CysG is a
multifunctional protein solely responsible for siroheme synthesis from uro'gen
III in E. coli, and accordingly is renamed siroheme synthase.
