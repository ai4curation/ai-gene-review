# ARHGAP4 regulates the cell migration and invasion of pancreatic cancer by the HDAC2/β-catenin signaling pathway.
**Authors:** Shen Y, Xu L, Ning Z, Liu L, Lin J, Chen H, Meng Z
**Journal:** Carcinogenesis (2019)
**DOI:** [10.1093/carcin/bgz067](https://doi.org/10.1093/carcin/bgz067)

## Abstract

1. Carcinogenesis. 2019 Nov 25;40(11):1405-1414. doi: 10.1093/carcin/bgz067.

ARHGAP4 regulates the cell migration and invasion of pancreatic cancer by the
HDAC2/β-catenin signaling pathway.

Shen Y(1)(2), Xu L(1)(2), Ning Z(1)(2), Liu L(1)(2), Lin J(1)(2), Chen H(1)(2),
Meng Z(1)(2).

Author information:
(1)Department of Integrative Oncology, Fudan University Shanghai Cancer Center,
Shanghai, China.
(2)Department of Oncology, Shanghai Medical College, Fudan University, Shanghai,
China.

β-catenin is a subunit of the cadherin protein complex and acts as an
intracellular signal transducer in the Wnt signaling pathway that mediates
multiple cellular processes, such as cell migration and invasion. HDAC2 (histone
deacetylase 2), a deacetylase that maintains histone H3 in a deacetylated state
in the promoter region of Wnt-targeted genes where β-catenin is bound,
negatively regulating β-catenin activation. However, the regulation of
HDAC2/β-catenin pathway remains unclear. Here, we report ARHGAP4 as a new
regulator of the β-catenin pathway that regulates cell invasion and migration of
pancreatic cancer as well as the downstream effector MMP2 and MMP9 expression in
vitro. Mechanistically, ARHGAP4 interacts with and ubiquitinates HDAC2, which in
turn inhibits β-catenin activation. Furthermore, treatment of CAY10683, an HDAC2
inhibitor, and XAV939, a Wnt/β-catenin pathway inhibitor, attenuated the effects
of ARHGAP4 silencing on pancreatic cancer cells. Overall, our findings establish
ARHGAP4 as a novel regulator of HDAC2/β-catenin pathway with a critical role in
tumorigenesis.
