Membrane contact sites, where organelle membranes come into close proximity,
function as dynamic hubs for lipid metabolism in response to metabolic and
stress signals. In yeast, the nucleus-vacuole junction (NVJ) expands during
glucose starvation (GS) through the recruitment of stress-specific proteins;
however, the underlying mechanisms and physiological significance have remained
unclear. Here, we identify the aspartyl protease Ypf1 and the yeast INSIG
homologs Nsg1 and Nsg2 as NVJ-localized proteins specifically recruited during
GS. Ypf1 promotes the recruitment of Nsg1, Nsg2, and the HMG-CoA reductases Hmg1
and Hmg2 to the NVJ, likely in association with changes in nuclear membrane
lipid composition caused by suppression of fatty acid elongases. This remodeling
destabilizes Nsg1, thereby activating Hmg1, while stabilizing Nsg2, which
suppresses Hmg1 to fine-tune sterol synthesis. Loss of both Nsg1 and Nsg2 leads
to hyperactivation of Hmg1 and accumulation of squalene, a sterol biosynthetic
intermediate. We propose that suppression of fatty acid elongases drives
GS-dependent NVJ remodeling to regulate ergosterol synthesis.
