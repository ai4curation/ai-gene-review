# Ultrahigh-resolution imaging reveals formation of neuronal SNARE/Munc18 complexes in situ.
**Authors:** Pertsinidis A, Mukherjee K, Sharma M, Pang ZP, Park SR, Zhang Y, Brunger AT, Südhof TC, Chu S
**Journal:** Proc Natl Acad Sci U S A (2013)

Ultrahigh-resolution imaging reveals formation of neuronal SNARE/Munc18
complexes in situ.

Pertsinidis A(1), Mukherjee K, Sharma M, Pang ZP, Park SR, Zhang Y, Brunger AT,
Südhof TC, Chu S.

Author information:
(1)Structural Biology Program, Memorial Sloan-Kettering Cancer Center, New York,
NY 10065, USA. PertsinA@mskcc.org

Membrane fusion is mediated by complexes formed by SNAP-receptor (SNARE) and
Secretory 1 (Sec1)/mammalian uncoordinated-18 (Munc18)-like (SM) proteins, but
it is unclear when and how these complexes assemble. Here we describe an
improved two-color fluorescence nanoscopy technique that can achieve effective
resolutions of up to 7.5-nm full width at half maximum (3.2-nm localization
precision), limited only by stochastic photon emission from single molecules. We
use this technique to dissect the spatial relationships between the neuronal SM
protein Munc18-1 and SNARE proteins syntaxin-1 and SNAP-25 (25 kDa
synaptosome-associated protein). Strikingly, we observed nanoscale clusters
consisting of syntaxin-1 and SNAP-25 that contained associated Munc18-1. Rescue
experiments with syntaxin-1 mutants revealed that Munc18-1 recruitment to the
plasma membrane depends on the Munc18-1 binding to the N-terminal peptide of
syntaxin-1. Our results suggest that in a primary neuron, SNARE/SM protein
complexes containing syntaxin-1, SNAP-25, and Munc18-1 are preassembled in
microdomains on the presynaptic plasma membrane. Our superresolution imaging
method provides a framework for investigating interactions between the synaptic
vesicle fusion machinery and other subcellular systems in situ.
