# Loss-of-function mutation in carotenoid 15,15'-monooxygenase identified in a patient with hypercarotenemia and hypovitaminosis A.
**Authors:** Lindqvist A, Sharvill J, Sharvill DE, Andersson S
**Journal:** J Nutr (2007)
**DOI:** [10.1093/jn/137.11.2346](https://doi.org/10.1093/jn/137.11.2346)

## Abstract

1. J Nutr. 2007 Nov;137(11):2346-50. doi: 10.1093/jn/137.11.2346.

Loss-of-function mutation in carotenoid 15,15'-monooxygenase identified in a
patient with hypercarotenemia and hypovitaminosis A.

Lindqvist A(1), Sharvill J, Sharvill DE, Andersson S.

Author information:
(1)Department of Obstetrics-Gynecology, University of Texas Southwestern Medical
Center, Dallas, TX 75390-9032, USA.

The enzyme carotenoid 15,15'-monooxygenase (CMO1) catalyzes the first step in
the conversion of dietary provitamin A carotenoids to vitamin A in the small
intestine. Plant carotenoids are an important dietary source of vitamin A
(retinol) and the sole source of vitamin A for vegetarians. Vitamin A is
essential for normal embryonic development as well as normal physiological
functions in children and adults. Here, we describe one heterozygous T170M
missense mutation in the CMO1 gene in a subject with hypercarotenemia and mild
hypovitaminosis A. The replacement of a highly conserved threonine with
methionine results in a 90% reduction in enzyme activity when analyzed in vitro
using purified recombinant enzymes. The Michaelis-Menten constant (K(m)) for the
mutated enzyme is normal. Ample amounts of carotenoids are present in plasma of
persons consuming a normal Western diet, suggesting that the enzyme is saturated
with substrate under normal conditions. Therefore, we propose that
haploinsufficiency of the CMO1 enzyme may cause symptoms of hypercarotenemia and
hypovitaminosis A in individuals consuming a carotenoid-containing and vitamin
A-deficient diet.
