# Characterization of a di-leucine-based signal in the cytoplasmic tail of the nucleotide-pyrophosphatase NPP1 that mediates basolateral targeting but not endocytosis.
**Authors:** Bello V, Goding JW, Greengrass V, Sali A, Dubljevic V, Lenoir C, Trugnan G, Maurice M
**Journal:** Mol Biol Cell (2001)
**DOI:** [10.1091/mbc.12.10.3004](https://doi.org/10.1091/mbc.12.10.3004)

## Abstract

1. Mol Biol Cell. 2001 Oct;12(10):3004-15. doi: 10.1091/mbc.12.10.3004.

Characterization of a di-leucine-based signal in the cytoplasmic tail of the
nucleotide-pyrophosphatase NPP1 that mediates basolateral targeting but not
endocytosis.

Bello V(1), Goding JW, Greengrass V, Sali A, Dubljevic V, Lenoir C, Trugnan G,
Maurice M.

Author information:
(1)U538 INSERM, CHU St-Antoine, 75571 Paris Cedex 12, France.

Enzymes of the nucleotide pyrophosphatase/phosphodiesterase (NPPase) family are
expressed at opposite surfaces in polarized epithelial cells. We investigated
the targeting signal of NPP1, which is exclusively expressed at the basolateral
surface. Full-length NPP1 and different constructs and mutants were transfected
into the polarized MDCK cell line. Expression of the proteins was analyzed by
confocal microscopy and surface biotinylation. The basolateral signal of NPP1
was identified as a di-leucine motif located in the cytoplasmic tail. Mutation
of either or both leucines largely redirected NPP1 to the apical surface.
Furthermore, addition of the conserved sequence AAASLLAP redirected the apical
nucleotide pyrophosphatase/phosphodiesterase NPP3 to the basolateral surface.
Full-length NPP1 was not significantly internalized. However, when the
cytoplasmic tail was deleted upstream the di-leucine motif or when the six
upstream flanking amino acids were deleted, the protein was mainly found
intracellularly. Endocytosis experiments indicated that these mutants were
endocytosed from the basolateral surface. These results identify the basolateral
signal of NPP1 as a short sequence including a di-leucine motif that is dominant
over apical determinants and point to the importance of surrounding amino acids
in determining whether the signal will function as a basolateral signal only or
as an endocytotic signal as well.
