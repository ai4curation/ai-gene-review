# Identification, molecular cloning, expression and chromosome mapping of a family of transformation upregulated hnRNP-K proteins derived by alternative splicing.
**Authors:** Dejgaard K, Leffers H, Rasmussen HH, Madsen P, Kruse TA, Gesser B, Nielsen H, Celis JE
**Journal:** J Mol Biol (1994)
**DOI:** [10.1006/jmbi.1994.1116](https://doi.org/10.1006/jmbi.1994.1116)

## Abstract

1. J Mol Biol. 1994 Feb 11;236(1):33-48. doi: 10.1006/jmbi.1994.1116.

Identification, molecular cloning, expression and chromosome mapping of a family
of transformation upregulated hnRNP-K proteins derived by alternative splicing.

Dejgaard K(1), Leffers H, Rasmussen HH, Madsen P, Kruse TA, Gesser B, Nielsen H,
Celis JE.

Author information:
(1)Institute of Medical Biochemistry, University of Aarhus, Denmark.

Acidic nuclear proteins (M(r) between 64,000 and 66,000; pI 4.9 to 5.5) that are
highly upregulated in transformed cells and that belong to the hnRNP-K family
have been identified using a monoclonal antibody (mAB B4B6) that distinguish
between quiescent and proliferating human keratinocytes. The family, which is
composed of four major proteins (hnRNPs-K A, B, C and D) and their modified
forms, is present in similar overall levels in quiescent and proliferating
normal keratinocytes although clear differences were observed in the levels of
some of the individual variants. Immunofluorescence staining of proliferating
normal keratinocytes with mAB B4B6 showed that about 40% of the keratinocytes,
corresponding mainly to G1 and to half of the cells in S-phase, reacted with the
antibody depicting a dotted, nucleoplasmic staining that excluded the nucleolus.
Only 3 to 4% of the quiescent keratinocytes reacted with the antibody while
simian virus 40 (SV40) transformed keratinocytes (K14) stained constitutively
throughout the cell cycle. Using mAB B4B6 as a probe we cloned a cDNA coding for
one member of the family (hnRNP-K B) and this was used to screen for additional
family members. Sequencing of the positive clones revealed four different cDNAs,
all resulting from alternative splicing of a common primary transcript of a gene
that mapped to chromosome 9. Expression of the cDNAs in the vaccinia virus
system confirmed their identity as hnRNPs-K A, B, C and D and showed that their
modified forms are phosphorylated. All four hnRNPs bound poly(rC) on
NorthWestern blots, although the more acidic of the phosphorylated forms, did so
at a much reduced level. hnRNP-K has been implicated in pre-mRNA metabolism of
transcripts containing cytidine-rich sequences and our results point towards a
role during cell cycle progression.
