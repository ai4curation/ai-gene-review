# Lactobacillus curvatus has a glucose transport system homologous to the mannose family of phosphoenolpyruvate-dependent phosphotransferase systems.
**Authors:** Veyrat A, Gosalbes MJ, Pérez-Martínez G
**Journal:** Microbiology (Reading) (1996)
**DOI:** [10.1099/13500872-142-12-3469](https://doi.org/10.1099/13500872-142-12-3469)

## Abstract

1. Microbiology (Reading). 1996 Dec;142 ( Pt 12):3469-77. doi:
10.1099/13500872-142-12-3469.

Lactobacillus curvatus has a glucose transport system homologous to the mannose
family of phosphoenolpyruvate-dependent phosphotransferase systems.

Veyrat A(1), Gosalbes MJ, Pérez-Martínez G.

Author information:
(1)Departamento de Biotecnología, Instituto de Agroquímica y Tecnología de
Alimentos (CSIC), Valencia, Spain.

In Lactobacillus curvatus, a phosphoenolpyruvate:mannose phosphotransferase
system (mannose-PTS) has been characterized and it was shown to be involved in
glucose and mannose transport, but no glucose-specific PTS activity could be
detected. A 2.1 kb DNA fragment amplified by PCR from the L. curvatus genome was
sequenced. Sequence analysis showed four ORFs which could encode proteins
similar to PTS transporters EIIA, EIIB, EIIC and EIID of the mannose class. The
expression of the manB gene (encoding EIIB) from L. curvatus in a mutant of
Lactobacillus sake impaired in EIIMan activity restored this activity.
Furthermore, this DNA fragment complemented the regulatory function of LevE
(EIIB) in a Bacillus subtilis levE-deficient mutant, suggesting that the protein
encoded by manB could also play a regulatory role in L. curvatus.
