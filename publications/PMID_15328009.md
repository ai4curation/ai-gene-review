# SpHnf6, a transcription factor that executes multiple functions in sea urchin embryogenesis.
**Authors:** Otim O, Amore G, Minokawa T, McClay DR, Davidson EH
**Journal:** Dev Biol (2004)
**DOI:** [10.1016/j.ydbio.2004.05.033](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ydbio.2004.05.033)

## Abstract

1. Dev Biol. 2004 Sep 15;273(2):226-43. doi: 10.1016/j.ydbio.2004.05.033.

SpHnf6, a transcription factor that executes multiple functions in sea urchin
embryogenesis.

Otim O(1), Amore G, Minokawa T, McClay DR, Davidson EH.

Author information:
(1)Division of Biology 156-29, California Institute of Technology, Pasadena, CA
91125, USA.

The Strongylocentrotus purpuratus hnf6 (Sphnf6) gene encodes a new member of the
ONECUT family of transcription factors. The expression of hnf6 in the developing
embryo is triphasic, and loss-of-function analysis shows that the Hnf6 protein
is a transcription factor that has multiple distinct roles in sea urchin
development. hnf6 is expressed maternally, and before gastrulation its
transcripts are distributed globally. Early in development, its expression is
required for the activation of PMC differentiation genes such as sm50, pm27, and
msp130, but not for the activation of any known PMC regulatory genes, for
example, alx, ets1, pmar1, or tbrain. Micromere transplantation experiments show
that the gene is not involved in early micromere signaling. Early hnf6
expression is also required for expression of the mesodermal regulator gatac.
The second known role of hnf6 is its participation after gastrulation in the
oral ectoderm gene regulatory network (GRN), in which its expression is
essential for the maintenance of the state of oral ectoderm specification. The
third role is in the neurogenic ciliated band, which is foreshadowed exactly by
a trapezoidal band of hnf6 expression at the border of the oral ectoderm and
where it continues to be expressed through the end of embryogenesis. Neither
oral ectoderm regulatory functions nor ciliated band formation occur normally in
the absence of hnf6 expression.
