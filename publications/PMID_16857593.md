# The Gemin5 protein of the SMN complex identifies snRNAs.
**Authors:** Battle DJ, Lau CK, Wan L, Deng H, Lotti F, Dreyfuss G
**Journal:** Mol Cell (2006)
**DOI:** [10.1016/j.molcel.2006.05.036](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.molcel.2006.05.036)

## Abstract

1. Mol Cell. 2006 Jul 21;23(2):273-9. doi: 10.1016/j.molcel.2006.05.036.

The Gemin5 protein of the SMN complex identifies snRNAs.

Battle DJ(1), Lau CK, Wan L, Deng H, Lotti F, Dreyfuss G.

Author information:
(1)Howard Hughes Medical Institute, University of Pennsylvania School of
Medicine, Philadelphia, Pennsylvania 19104, USA.

The survival of motor neurons protein (SMN) is part of a large complex that
contains six other proteins, Gemins2-7. The SMN complex assembles the heptameric
Sm protein core on small nuclear RNAs (snRNAs) and plays a critical role in the
biogenesis of snRNPs, the major and essential components of mRNA splicing in
eukaryotes. For its function, the SMN complex binds Sm proteins and snRNAs,
which it distinguishes from other RNAs by specific features they contain. We
show here that Gemin5, a 170 kDa WD-repeat protein, is the snRNA binding protein
of the SMN complex. Gemin5 binds directly and specifically to the unique
features, including the Sm site, of snRNAs. Reduction of Gemin5 results in
reduced capacity of the SMN complex to bind snRNAs and to assemble Sm cores.
Gemin5 therefore functions as the factor that allows the SMN complex to
distinguish snRNAs from other cellular RNAs for snRNP biogenesis.
