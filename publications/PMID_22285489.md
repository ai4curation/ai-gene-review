In the yeast Saccharomyces cerevisiae, a working model for nutrient homeostasis
in eukaryotes, inorganic phosphate (Pi) homeostasis is regulated by the PHO
pathway, a set of phosphate starvation induced genes, acting to optimize Pi
uptake and utilization. Among these, a subset of proteins containing the SPX
domain has been shown to be key regulators of Pi homeostasis. In this review, we
summarize the recent progresses in elucidating the mechanisms controlling Pi
homeostasis in yeast, focusing on the key roles of the SPX domain-containing
proteins in these processes, as well as describing the future challenges and
opportunities in this fast-moving field.
