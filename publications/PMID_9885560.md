# Opposing actions of CSW and RasGAP modulate the strength of Torso RTK signaling in the Drosophila terminal pathway.
**Authors:** Cleghon V, Feldmann P, Ghiglione C, Copeland TD, Perrimon N, Hughes DA, Morrison DK
**Journal:** Mol Cell (1998)
**DOI:** [10.1016/s1097-2765(00)80287-7](https://doi.org/10.1016/s1097-2765(00)80287-7)

## Abstract

1. Mol Cell. 1998 Dec;2(6):719-27. doi: 10.1016/s1097-2765(00)80287-7.

Opposing actions of CSW and RasGAP modulate the strength of Torso RTK signaling
in the Drosophila terminal pathway.

Cleghon V(1), Feldmann P, Ghiglione C, Copeland TD, Perrimon N, Hughes DA,
Morrison DK.

Author information:
(1)Molecular Basis of Carcinogenesis Laboratory, National Cancer Institute,
Frederick Cancer Research and Development Center, Maryland 21702, USA.

In Drosophila, specification of embryonic terminal cells is controlled by the
Torso receptor tyrosine kinase. Here, we analyze the molecular basis of positive
(Y630) and negative (Y918) phosphotyrosine (pY) signaling sites on Torso. We
find that the Drosophila homolog of RasGAP associates with pY918 and is a
negative effector of Torso signaling. Further, we show that the tyrosine
phosphatase Corkscrew (CSW), which associates with pY630, specifically
dephosphorylates the negative pY918 Torso signaling site, thus identifying Torso
to be a substrate of CSW in the terminal pathway. CSW also serves as an adaptor
protein for DRK binding, physically linking Torso to Ras activation. The
opposing actions of CSW and RasGAP modulate the strength of the Torso signal,
contributing to the establishment of precise boundaries for terminal structure
development.
