# The human apolipoprotein(a)/plasminogen gene cluster contains a novel homologue transcribed in liver.
**Authors:** Byrne CD, Schwartz K, Meer K, Cheng JF, Lawn RM
**Journal:** Arterioscler Thromb (1994)
**DOI:** [10.1161/01.atv.14.4.534](https://doi.org/10.1161/01.atv.14.4.534)

## Abstract

1. Arterioscler Thromb. 1994 Apr;14(4):534-41. doi: 10.1161/01.atv.14.4.534.

The human apolipoprotein(a)/plasminogen gene cluster contains a novel homologue
transcribed in liver.

Byrne CD(1), Schwartz K, Meer K, Cheng JF, Lawn RM.

Author information:
(1)Falk Cardiovascular Research Center, Stanford University School of Medicine,
CA 94305.

Lipoprotein(a) is an atherogenic lipoprotein whose function and plasma
concentration reflect the structure and regulation of the apolipoprotein(a)
gene. Apolipoprotein(a) is a close homologue of plasminogen, and their genes are
tightly linked on chromosome 6. To further characterize these genes, we analyzed
overlapping human genomic yeast artificial chromosome clones, which revealed a
cluster of four highly homologous genes encoding apolipoprotein(a), plasminogen,
and two apolipoprotein(a)-related genes (rg) or pseudogenes. Hybridization
analysis and reverse transcriptase polymerase chain reaction showed that one of
these novel genes, designated apolipoprotein(a)rg-C, has a domain structure
similar to apolipoprotein(a) and is transcribed in human liver. Three additional
homologues designated as plasminogen-related genes are shown to be unlinked to
this gene cluster and reside on chromosomes 2 and 4.
