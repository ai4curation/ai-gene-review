Hypomyelinating leukodystrophy 17 is an autosomal recessive disease affecting
myelin-forming oligodendroglial cells in the central nervous system. The gene
responsible for HLD17 encodes aminoacyl-tRNA synthase complex-interacting
multifunctional protein 2, whose product proteins form a scaffold that supports
aminoacyl-tRNA synthetases throughout the cell body. Here we show that the
HLD17-associated nonsense mutation (Tyr35-to-Ter [Y35X]) of AIMP2 localizes
AIMP2 proteins as aggregates into the Golgi bodies in mouse oligodendroglial
FBD-102b cells. Wild type AIMP2 proteins, in contrast, are distributed
throughout the cell body. Expression of the Y35X mutant proteins, but not the
wild type proteins, in cells upregulates Golgi stress signaling involving
caspase-2 activation. Cells expressing the wild type proteins exhibit
differentiated phenotypes with web-like structures bearing many processes
following the induction of differentiation, whereas cells expressing the Y35X
mutant proteins fail to differentiate. Furthermore, CASP2 knockdown but not
control knockdown reverses the phenotypes of cells expressing the mutant
proteins. These results suggest that HLD17-associated AIMP2 mutant proteins are
localized in the Golgi bodies where their proteins stimulate Golgi
stress-responsive CASP2 to inhibit differentiation; this effect is ameliorated
by knockdown of CASP2. These findings may reveal some of the molecular and
cellular pathological mechanisms underlying HLD17 and possible approaches to
ameliorating the disease's effects.
