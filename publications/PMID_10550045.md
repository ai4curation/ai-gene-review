Diatom cell walls are regarded as a paradigm for controlled production of
nanostructured silica, but the mechanisms allowing biosilicification to proceed
at ambient temperature at high rates have remained enigmatic. A set of
polycationic peptides (called silaffins) isolated from diatom cell walls were
shown to generate networks of silica nanospheres within seconds when added to a
solution of silicic acid. Silaffins contain covalently modified lysine-lysine
elements. The first lysine bears a polyamine consisting of 6 to 11 repeats of
the N-methyl-propylamine unit. The second lysine was identified as
epsilon-N,N-dimethyl-lysine. These modifications drastically influence the
silica-precipitating activity of silaffins.
