# The cadherin-11 cytoplasmic juxtamembrane domain promotes alpha-catenin turnover at adherens junctions and intercellular motility.
**Authors:** Kiener HP, Stipp CS, Allen PG, Higgins JM, Brenner MB
**Journal:** Mol Biol Cell (2006)
**DOI:** [10.1091/mbc.e05-08-0745](https://doi.org/10.1091/mbc.e05-08-0745)

## Abstract

1. Mol Biol Cell. 2006 May;17(5):2366-76. doi: 10.1091/mbc.e05-08-0745. Epub 2006
 Mar 8.

The cadherin-11 cytoplasmic juxtamembrane domain promotes alpha-catenin turnover
at adherens junctions and intercellular motility.

Kiener HP(1), Stipp CS, Allen PG, Higgins JM, Brenner MB.

Author information:
(1)Department of Medicine, Division of Rheumatology, Immunology, and Allergy,
Brigham and Women's Hospital, Harvard Medical School, Boston, MA 02115, USA.

Cadherins mediate homophilic cell adhesion and contribute to tissue
morphogenesis and architecture. Cadherin cell adhesion contacts are actively
remodeled and impact cell movement and migration over other cells. We found that
expression of a mutant cadherin-11 lacking the cytoplasmic juxtamembrane domain
(JMD) diminished the turnover of alpha-catenin at adherens junctions as measured
by fluorescence recovery after photobleaching. This resulted in markedly
diminished cell intercalation into monolayers reflecting reduced
cadherin-11-dependent cell motility on other cells. Furthermore, the actin
cytoskeleton in cadherin-11 deltaJMD cells revealed a more extensive cortical
F-actin ring that correlated with significantly higher levels of activated Rac1.
Together, these data implicate the cadherin-11 cytoplasmic JMD as a regulator of
alpha-catenin turnover at adherens junctions and actin-cytoskeletal organization
that is critical for intercellular motility and rearrangement in multicellular
clusters.
