# Identification of mutations in the alpha 3(IV) and alpha 4(IV) collagen genes in autosomal recessive Alport syndrome.
**Authors:** Mochizuki T, Lemmink HH, Mariyama M, Antignac C, Gubler MC, Pirson Y, Verellen-Dumoulin C, Chan B, Schröder CH, Smeets HJ
**Journal:** Nat Genet (1994)
**DOI:** [10.1038/ng0994-77](https://doi.org/10.1038/ng0994-77)

## Abstract

1. Nat Genet. 1994 Sep;8(1):77-81. doi: 10.1038/ng0994-77.

Identification of mutations in the alpha 3(IV) and alpha 4(IV) collagen genes in
autosomal recessive Alport syndrome.

Mochizuki T(1), Lemmink HH, Mariyama M, Antignac C, Gubler MC, Pirson Y,
Verellen-Dumoulin C, Chan B, Schröder CH, Smeets HJ, et al.

Author information:
(1)Howard Hughes Medical Institute, Yale University School of Medicine, New
Haven, Connecticut 06536-0812.

Alport syndrome (AS) is an hereditary disease of basement membranes
characterized by progressive renal failure and deafness. Changes in the
glomerular basement membrane (GBM) in AS suggest that the type IV collagen
matrix, the major structural component of GBM, is disrupted. We recently
isolated the genes for two type IV collagens, alpha 3(IV) and alpha 4(IV), that
are encoded head-to-head on human chromosome 2. These chains are abundant in
normal GBM but are sometimes absent in AS. We screened for mutations in families
in which consanguinity suggested autosomal recessive inheritance. Homozygous
mutations were found in alpha 3(IV) in two families and in alpha 4(IV) in two
others, demonstrating that these chains are important in the structural
integrity of the GBM and that there is an autosomal form of AS in addition to
the previously-defined X-linked form.
