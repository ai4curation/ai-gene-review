## Full Text

Abstract

FAM20C is a secretory kinase responsible for the phosphorylation of multiple secreted proteins in mammalian cells; it has been shown to phosphorylate serine residues within a variety of different bone proteins. In this work we demonstrate that FAM20C also phosphorylates threonines, specifically those within the N-terminal domain of the neuroendocrine chaperone 7B2. Analysis of the primary sequence of 7B2 revealed that three threonine residues in its N-terminal domain are located within FAM20C consensus motifs: Thr73, Thr99, and Thr111. The individual substitution of Thr73 and Thr111 residues by neutral alanines caused a marked decrease in the total phosphorylation of 7B2. Furthermore, the phosphomimetic substitution of Thr111 by Glu clearly diminished the ability of 7B2 to activate pro-prohormone convertase 2 (PC2) in 7B2-lacking SK-N-MC neuroblastoma cells, suggesting that the phosphorylation of this residue critically impacts the 7B2-proPC2 interaction. However, the phosphomimetic mutation did not alter 7B2's ability to function as an antiaggregant for human islet amyloid polypeptide. FAM20C-mediated phosphorylation of a common alternatively spliced variant of human 7B2 that lacks Ala100 (thus eliminating the Thr99 phosphorylation consensus site) was similar to the Ala-containing protein, but this variant did not activate proPC2 as efficiently as the Ala-containing protein. Although threonines within 7B2 were phosphorylated efficiently, FAM20C was incapable of performing the well-known regulatory threonine phosphorylation of the molecular chaperone binding immunoglobulin protein. Taken together, these results indicate that FAM20C plays a role in 7B2-mediated proPC2 activation by phosphorylating residue Thr111; and that 7B2 function is regulated by alternative splicing.

The phosphorylation of secreted proteins and prohormones has been widely described in the literature, different groups having proposed that a Golgi casein kinase (termed G-CK) is the enzyme responsible for this posttranslational modification within the secretory pathway (reviewed in reference 1 ). The secreted protein family 20C (FAM20C), also known as dentin matrix protein-4 ( 2 ), has been recently identified as the Golgi casein kinase ( 3 ). As a protein kinase, FAM20C exhibits unique features: it phosphorylates serine residues included in the S-X-D/E motif (in which X is any amino acid) within the Golgi lumen; it prefers manganese over magnesium or calcium; and it is insensitive to the protein kinase inhibitor staurosporine ( 3 ). Furthermore, FAM20C is widely expressed in mammalian tissues, although its expression is especially high in mineralized tissues such as bone and dentin ( 2 ). In bone cells FAM20C is able to phosphorylate many secreted proteins included in the small integrin-binding ligand N-linked glycoprotein and enamel protein families ( 3 , 4 ) as well as the peptide hormone fibroblast growth factor 23 ( 5 , 6 ), implicated in the regulation of bone mineralization and serum phosphate levels. In this regard, loss of FAM20C expression in mice causes bone lesions and tooth abnormalities ( 7 – 9 ). In humans, a rare deficiency in FAM20C activity caused by inactivating mutations produces Raine syndrome, an autosomal recessive osteosclerotic bone dysplasia ( 10 – 12 ).

Prohormone convertase 2 (PC2) is a serine protease present exclusively in neuroendocrine tissues that processes important peptide hormone precursors such as proopiomelanocortin, proinsulin, and proglucagon into bioactive peptides ( 13 ). The neuroendocrine chaperone 7B2 binds to and is absolutely required for the production of active PC2 ( 14 – 17 ). Unlike other members of the family of convertases, proPC2 does not require cleavage of its prodomain to exit the endoplasmic reticulum (ER). In fact, the prodomain is involved in the binding of proPC2 to its chaperone protein 7B2, a process that occurs after proPC2 folding in the ER ( 14 ). Once binding occurs, the proPC2/7B2 complex exits this compartment and travels to the trans-Golgi network, in which the C-terminal domain of 7B2 is cleaved by furin ( 17 , 18 ). In the acidic environment of the trans-Golgi network and secretory granules, 7B2 dissociates from proPC2 either before or during activation, which occurs via autocatalytic prodomain removal ( 14 , 17 – 19 ). Recent studies have shown that 7B2 facilitates proPC2 maturation by preventing its inactivating oligomerization and aggregation ( 20 ). Due to the fact that 7B2 is much more widely distributed in neuronal and endocrine tissues compared with the limited distribution of PC2 ( 21 ), 7B2 likely possesses additional functions other than assisting the proPC2 stability and trafficking. Indeed, 7B2 is a potent antiaggregant capable of blocking the aggregation of amyloid-containing polypeptides ( 22 , 23 ) as well as other secretory polypeptides, such as IGF1 ( 24 ).

7B2-mediated assistance of proPC2 maturation does not require the complete form of this chaperone; the N-terminal domain of 7B2 (here termed 21 kDa 7B2) suffices for PC2 activation ( 15 ). Prior studies have shown that 21 kDa 7B2 can be phosphorylated by Golgi-enriched fractions of neuroendocrine cells, a process that greatly reduces the ability of 7B2 to activate proPC2 ( 25 ). However, the protein kinase responsible for this posttranslational modification is not known. In this work we demonstrate that FAM20C is able to phosphorylate 7B2 within cells at specific threonine residues and that phosphorylation of Thr111 critically reduces proPC2 activation in neural-derived cells but does not affect 7B2's antiaggregant activity.

Materials and Methods

Cell culture and transient transfection

Human embryonic kidney (HEK) 293 cells and human neuroblastoma SK-N-MC cells were maintained in DMEM supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS; Atlanta Biologicals) or MEM with Earle's salts supplemented with nonessential amino acids (Gibco) and 10% FBS, respectively, and were cultured at 37°C in a humidified atmosphere containing 5% CO 2 . AtT-20/7B2 cells were cultured in DMEM containing 10% FBS (Atlanta Biologicals), 10% NuSerum (BD Biosciences), and 100 μg/mL hygromycin (Sigma). The cells were transfected using FuGENE HD transfection reagent (Promega) following the manufacturer's instructions.

Expression vectors and site-directed mutagenesis

The Flag-tagged human FAM20C kinase (and its inactive D478A variant) cloned into the pcDNA3 expression vector (Invitrogen) was obtained from Drs V. Tagliabracci and J. Dixon. Human PC2 and rat 7B2 (21 kDa and 27 kDa forms) cDNAs were directly subcloned into the pcDNA3 and pCEP4 vectors, respectively, as previously described ( 15 , 26 ). The Myc-tagged human binding immunoglobulin protein (BiP) retaining the C-terminal KDEL ER localization sequence and cloned into pCMV plasmid was obtained from Addgene. Mutagenesis reactions were carried out by Genscript using the pCEP4-r7B2 vector (27 kDa form) as a template and confirmed by sequencing of the entire insert. The human 7B2 constructs (27 kDa form), cloned into pCMV6-AC, were purchased from OriGene Technologies and validated by complete insert sequencing.

RNA extraction, reverse transcription, and RT-PCR

Total RNA was isolated from cells treated with Trizol (Ambion) using chloroform and isopropanol extraction as described in the manufacturer's instructions, washed in 75% ethanol, and resuspended in nuclease-free water. Purified RNA was treated with deoxyribonuclease I (DNA free kit; Ambion) for 1 hour at 37°C to remove any genomic DNA. RNA concentrations were measured by spectrophotometry, and the quality/purity was checked to ensure that the 260:280 absorbance ratio of all samples was between 1.9 and 2.0. Two micrograms of RNA were reverse transcribed into cDNA using oligo(deoxythymidine) 18 as primer and RevertAid reverse transcriptase (Thermo Scientific) according to the manufacturer's instructions. Products were amplified by means of Platinum Taq DNA polymerase using specific primer pairs for each gene product. Amplified products were resolved by electrophoresis in a 1% agarose gel containing 40 mM Tris-acetate and 1 mM EDTA (pH 8.0) and SYBR Safe DNA gel stain (1:10 000; Invitrogen) in a horizontal slab gel apparatus using the same Tris acetate/EDTA buffer. The gel was photographed under UV transillumination. Primers were designed to amplify cDNA equally from both mouse and rat cell lines.

Radiolabeling and immunoprecipitation

For analysis of 7B2 phosphorylation, either AtT-20/7B2 or HEK 293 cells plated in six-well plates (80% confluence) were transfected with 2 μg total cDNA using FuGENE (Promega). One day after the transfection, the cells were washed twice with phosphate-free DMEM supplemented with 10 mM HEPES (pH 7.4), starved for 60 minutes, and incubated with 0.5 mCi/mL of H 3 32 PO 4 per well (PerkinElmer). After 4 hours of labeling, the medium was replaced with Opti-MEM (Invitrogen) containing 100 μg/mL of bovine aprotinin (Desert Biologicals) and 100 μM levamisole (LKT Laboratories Inc), and cells were incubated for a further 3 hours. Medium samples were diluted in 5× AG buffer (0.5 M sodium phosphate; 5 mM EDTA; 0.5% Triton X-100; 2.5% Nonidet P-40, pH 7.4) with protease and phosphatase inhibitors (Halt; Roche) and immunoprecipitated using affinity-purified antisera against 7B2 and protein A-Sepharose beads. In the case of BiP, HEK 293 transfected cells were starved for 60 minutes with phosphate-free medium and incubated with 32 P-orthophosphate for 4 hours. The cell lysates were immunoprecipitated using an antibody against the myc epitope (Cell Signaling Technology). Immunoprecipitated samples were separated by SDS-PAGE, and radiolabeled material was detected using a Storm PhosphoImager (Molecular Dynamics). For methionine labeling, a similar protocol was followed, except that 0.5 mCi of 35 S-methionine (PerkinElmer) was used in methionine- and cysteine-free DMEM supplemented with 10 mM HEPES (pH 7.4). All radiolabeling experiments were independently carried out at least three times.

Immunoprecipitation of FLAG-tagged FAM20C

HEK 293 cells plated in 100-mm culture dishes (80% confluence) were transfected with 11.4 μg of FLAG-FAM20C (wild type or D478A) at a 3:1 ratio using Lipofectamine 2000 (Invitrogen). Forty-eight hours after transfection, conditioned medium was collected and centrifuged at 2000 × g for 5 minutes to remove cell debris and was immunoprecipitated with 100 μL of anti-FLAG-M2 affinity gel (Sigma) for 3–4 hours at 4°C. Immunoprecipitates were washed three times with Tris-buffered saline and kept at −20°C.

In vitro kinase assay

In vitro kinase assays were performed in 50 mM HEPES (pH 7.0) and 10 mM MnCl 2 , supplemented with [γ- 32 P] ATP (PerkinElmer) to a final specific activity of 500 μCi/μmol in a 50-μL reaction mixture. Two micrograms of purified prokaryotic recombinant His-tagged rat 7B2, obtained as previously described ( 27 ), were incubated with immunopurified FAM20C at 30°C and the reaction terminated at the indicated time points by adding SDS loading buffer and boiling. Reaction products were separated by SDS-PAGE and visualized by Coomassie blue staining. Destained gels were dried and incorporated radioactivity visualized by autoradiography.

Phosphoamino acid analysis

For phosphoamino acid analysis of 32 P-labeled 7B2, immunoprecipitated 7B2 was subjected to electrophoresis on a 15% SDS-polyacrylamide gel and transferred to a polyvinylidene difluoride (PVDF) membrane. The PVDF membrane was rinsed with PBS, air dried, and exposed to a PhosphorImager screen. The slice containing phosphorylated 7B2 was excised from the PVDF membrane, and the protein was hydrolyzed by incubation in 200 μL of 6 N HCl for 1 hour at 110°C. Amino acids were eluted from the membrane with 30% methanol and 0.1 N HCl (three changes, 200 μL each). The 6 N HCl hydrolysate and the three eluates were pooled and dried by lyophilization. The sample was resuspended in 200 μL of distilled water, lyophilized, and resuspended again in 20 μL of distilled water, and 5 μL were applied to an Eastman Kodak Chromagram sheet (6064 cellulose, 20 × 20 cm 2 ). Phosphoamino acids were applied to a separate lane as well as to a spot containing the sample (to control for any effects of the sample on the migration). The samples were subjected to thin-layer chromatography (TLC) in a solvent composed of 5:3 isobutyric acid and 0.5 M NH 4 OH for 4 hours. The TLC plate was removed and dried under hot air. The plate was then sprayed with ninhydrin solution (0.2% ninhydrin in ethanol) and dried with hot air to visualize the phosphoamino acid standards. The TLC plate was exposed to a PhosphoImager screen to determine the positions of 32 P-labeled phosphoamino acids. To align the autoradiogram with the TLC plate, a radioactive marker was spotted on the dried TLC plate. Each experiment was performed at least three times using duplicate wells.

Western blotting

Human neuroblastoma SK-N-MC cells were plated in six-well plates to 60%–70% confluence, and each well was transfected with 1 μg of human PC2 vector in the presence of 1 μg of either 27 kDa 7B2 (wild type or mutants) or empty vector. One day after transfection, Opti-MEM containing 100 μg/mL bovine aprotinin was added to each well. Cells were incubated for an additional 24 hours before the conditioned medium was harvested. Media samples were centrifuged and supernatants were kept frozen for later analysis. Medium samples were thawed, boiled, and subjected to SDS-PAGE followed by Western blotting using a combination of two different rabbit antisera directed against synthetic peptides within the N terminus of mature PC2 (LSU7) and/or the PC2 propeptide (LSU26) coupled to limpet hemocyanin ( 26 ). Blots were then probed with antirabbit horseradish peroxidase antibody (31460; Thermo Scientific). Visualization of immunoreactive proteins was accomplished using the Clarity Western enhanced chemiluminescence substrate (Bio-Rad Laboratories) and a ChemiDoc MP imaging system (Bio-Rad Laboratories).

Enzyme assay

The enzymatic activity of secreted PC2 was measured in triplicate in 50-μL reactions in a 96-well polypropylene plate containing 25 μL of conditioned medium and final concentrations of 200 μM fluorogenic substrate (p-Glu-Arg-Thr-Lys-Arg-aminomethylcoumarin; Peptides International), 100 mM sodium acetate (pH 5.0), 2 mM CaCl 2 , 0.1% Brij 35, and a protease inhibitor cocktail (final concentrations: 1 μM pepstatin, 0.28 mM L-1-tosylamino-2-phenylethylchloromethyl ketone, 10 μM E-64, and 0.14 mM Nα-tosyl-L-lysine chloromethyl ketone). Reaction mixtures were incubated at 37°C, and fluorescence measurements (380 nm excitation, 460 nm emission) were taken under kinetic conditions every minute for 120 minutes in a SpectraMax M2 microplate reader.

Radioimmunoassay

RIAs were carried out according to protocols described previously ( 28 ). 125 I-labeled 7B2 was prepared by the chloramine-T method originally described by Hunter and Greenwood ( 29 ). The polyclonal anti-7B2 antiserum was used at a final dilution of 1:30 000. Conditioned media were subjected to assay in duplicate. Radioactivity was determined using a Wallac 1470 Wizard γ-counter.

Cytotoxicity assays

Human islet amyloid polypeptide (hIAPP) was purchased from Calbiochem and resuspended in dimethylsulfoxide at a concentration of 500 μM. For cytotoxicity assays, SK-N-MC cells were plated in 96-well plates at a density of about 50% and transiently transfected with vectors encoding 27-kDa 7B2 (wild type or mutants) or empty vector for 24 hours using FuGENE (Promega). Cells were incubated for an additional 24 hours in Opti-MEM containing or lacking 10 μM hIAPP, and cell viability was measured using the WST-1 cell proliferation reagent (Roche). The value for vehicle-treated cells was set as 100%. The experiments were repeated independently three times using triplicate wells.

Results

FAM20C phosphorylates the secreted chaperone 7B2 but not the ER-resident chaperone BiP

Previous work has shown that 7B2 is endogenously phosphorylated in rat insulinoma Rin5F cells but not in pituitary-derived AtT-20 cells ( 25 ). To determine whether the lack of phosphorylation in AtT-20 cells was due to the absence of FAM20C, we analyzed the expression of this gene by RT-PCR. Figure 1 A shows that, in contrast to widely expressed cytoplasmic kinases such as casein kinase 2 (CK2), the expression of FAM20C in AtT-20 cells is undetectable, which supports the idea that FAM20C is the kinase responsible for 7B2 phosphorylation in neuroendocrine cells. To obtain direct evidence for this, we transiently transfected AtT-20 cells (stably transfected with 27 kDa 7B2) with FAM20C cDNA and examined 7B2 phosphorylation after radiolabeling and immunoprecipitation. Figure 1 B shows that that 7B2 is phosphorylated after transfection of cells with wild-type FAM20C, but not by the D478A inactive form, suggesting that FAM20C might be the kinase responsible for 7B2 phosphorylation in vivo. Similarly, we found that 7B2 was phosphorylated in HEK 293 cells transfected with 21 kDa 7B2 cDNA in the presence of FAM20C ( Figure 1 C). Moreover, incubation of bacterially derived purified 21 kDa 7B2 with wild-type active FAM20C in vitro, but not with the inactive mutant FAM20C D478A, clearly results in 7B2 phosphorylation ( Figure 1 D). Given that a controversy exists with respect to whether FAM20C is able to phosphorylate its substrates in the Golgi lumen or in the extracellular medium [because it is a secreted protein ( 30 )], we also examined the intracellular phosphorylation of 7B2 by FAM20C. Our results clearly show P 32 -labeling of 7B2 within cell lysates (data not shown), demonstrating that the FAM20C-mediated phosphorylation of 7B2 can also occur within the secretory pathway.

Figure 1.

BiP is an ER-resident chaperone involved in the unfolded protein response in mammalian cells ( 31 , 32 ). Although previous studies have demonstrated that BiP can be threonine phosphorylated in the ER ( 33 , 34 ), the kinase responsible for BiP phosphorylation has never been identified. The analysis of the primary sequence of BiP revealed the existence of a number of Ser and Thr residues within FAM20C consensus motifs (not shown). However, despite robust protein expression, we were repeatedly unable to detect any BiP phosphorylation in the presence of FAM20C ( Figure 2 ).

Figure 2.

7B2 is phosphorylated by FAM20C at threonine residues

In in vitro assays, FAM20C was capable of phosphorylating both the full-length 7B2 (27 kDa form) and the N-terminal domain of 7B2 (21 kDa form) ( Figure 3 A). Phosphoamino acid analyses performed on radiolabeled 7B2 obtained from in vitro experiments showed that whereas the 27 kDa form of 7B2 was phosphorylated both on Ser and Thr residues, the 21 kDa form of 7B2 was FAM20C phosphorylated mainly on Thr residues ( Figure 3 A). Similarly, 21 kDa 7B2 was phosphorylated by FAM20C at Thr residues in transfected cells ( Figure 3 B). The analysis of the primary sequence of 21 kDa 7B2 revealed the presence of three Thr residues within potential phosphorylation consensus sites for FAM20C ( Figure 4 A). To determine which threonine(s) were phosphorylated by FAM20C, we performed alanine substitutions of Thr73, Thr99, and Thr111 residues. The phosphorylation of these mutants (T73A, T99A, and T111A) was analyzed by radiolabeling with P 32 -orthophosphate followed by immunoprecipitation. Figure 4 B shows that the expression levels of these mutants were similar (S 35 -methionine labeling). As shown by the P 32 -labeling, phosphorylation of 7B2 was notably reduced in T73A- and T111A-containing 7B2s but not in the T99A mutant, indicating that residues Thr73 and Thr111 within 21 kDa 7B2 are preferentially phosphorylated by FAM20C.

The phosphomimetic substitution T111E in 7B2 reduces proPC2 activation but does not affect the antiaggregant properties of 7B2

To determine the effect of phosphorylation of residues Thr73 and Thr111 on 7B2 function, we generated the phosphomimetic mutants T73E and T111E. We used human neuroblastoma SK-N-MC cells as a model system to analyze the effect of these substitutions on the function of 7B2; this is the only known neuroendocrine cell line identified thus far that does not endogenously express 7B2 ( 16 ). To compare the proPC2 activation capacity of mutants T73E and T111E with that of wild-type 7B2, we transiently transfected each of these constructs together with a human proPC2 plasmid (because SK-N-MC cells express only low quantities of endogenous proPC2). The conditioned media were used to measure PC2 protein levels and enzymatic activity. Figure 5 A shows that the substitution of Thr111 profoundly affected 7B2's ability to facilitate proPC2 maturation and activation, whereas the effect of the T73E mutant was very similar to that of wild-type 7B2. Note that despite the high protein levels detected by Western blotting, PC2 was completely inactive in the absence of 7B2 ( Figure 5 B). In contrast, both the wild-type protein and the phosphomimetic mutants were equally capable of reducing the cytotoxic effects of hIAPP aggregates, indicating that the phosphorylation of these residues does not impact the antiaggregant properties of 7B2 ( Figure 5 C). Interestingly, although residue Thr111 is highly conserved among species, it is not present within a FAM20C consensus sequence in either Xenopus tropicalis or Drosophila melanogaster 7B2; in Caenorhabditis elegans this residue is serine and is located within a FAM20C consensus site ( Figure 5 D).

Figure 5.

The alternatively-spliced human 7B2 variant lacking the Ala100 residue is phosphorylated by FAM20C but is less effective in facilitating proPC2 activation than the Ala100-containing variant

Human and rodent tissues contain abundant quantities of two alternatively spliced 7B2 transcripts containing or lacking Ala100 ( 35 ). Figure 6 A shows that both constructs were efficiently expressed and were similarly phosphorylated by FAM20C. Whereas the loss of Ala100 clearly reduced proPC2 activation ( Figure 6 B), it did not affect its ability to block the cytotoxic fibrillation of hIAPP ( Figure 6 C). These results support a role for the Ala100 insertion in 7B2-mediated control of proPC2 activation but not in modulating 7B2's general antiaggregation properties.

Discussion

7B2, a secretory chaperone involved in the blockade of aggregation of secretory proteins, has been shown to represent an obligate cofactor in the cellular maturation of the proprotein convertase proPC2 ( 15 ). The phosphorylation of 7B2 has been previously described in endocrine cells, ie, the insulinoma Rin5F cell line ( 25 ), although thus far the protein kinase responsible for this modification has remained unknown. In 2012, Tagliabracci et al ( 3 ) identified FAM20C as the long-sought Golgi casein kinase, thus establishing the key protein kinase likely responsible for the phosphorylation of most proteins present in the secretory pathway. This study also revealed that the general recognition motif for phosphorylation by FAM20C is S-X-D/E ( 36 ). Numerous secreted proteins including neuropeptide and peptide hormone precursors such as proopiomelanocortin ( 37 ), chromogranin A ( 38 ), proenkephalin ( 39 , 40 ), and the proprotein convertase PCSK9 ( 41 ) are all known to be phosphorylated within S-X-E consensus motifs and likely represent substrates for this new kinase. In vitro peptide screening revealed that FAM20C is also able to phosphorylate threonine residues but in peptide screens threonines were much more weakly phosphorylated than serines ( 3 ). Indeed, protein threonine phosphorylation by FAM20C has not been described to date.

A primary sequence analysis of 7B2 revealed four serine (Ser47, Ser82, Ser115, and Ser158) and three threonine (Thr73, Thr99, and Thr111) residues within FAM20C consensus motifs, implying that the phosphorylation of 7B2 in endocrine cells may also be mediated by this secretory kinase. In the work presented here, we show that whereas FAM20C phosphorylates full-length 7B2 (27 kDa form) at both Ser and Thr residues, this kinase efficiently and preferentially phosphorylates the N-terminal domain of 7B2 (21 kDa form) at threonine rather than at serine residues. This represents the first case of a secretory protein phosphorylated by FAM20C at threonines. Taking into account that the C-terminal region of 7B2 includes the consensus phosphorylation site S 158 -E-E, FAM20C-mediated Ser phosphorylation of the C terminally extended 27 kDa 7B2 form likely occurs at Ser158. In addition to 7B2, previous studies have demonstrated that the molecular chaperone BiP undergoes inactivating phosphorylation at Thr residues in the ER ( 33 , 34 , 42 ). However, our finding that BiP does not become phosphorylated in the presence of FAM20C leads us to conclude that this kinase is not responsible for the regulation of BiP activity in the ER.

In an effort to discover which threonine residues within 7B2 are actually phosphorylated by FAM20C, we generated Ala substitutions of each of the three threonines present within the FAM20C consensus sites and found that Ala substitution of Thr73 and Thr111, but not of Thr99, decreased the total phosphorylation of 7B2. In agreement with our results, the PhosphoSitePlus database indicates that Thr111 phosphorylation occurs in three species [reference 43 and CST Curation Set 4863 ( 44 )]. Given that residue Thr111 is included within the 36-residue peptide already known to be involved in the interaction of 7B2 with proPC2 ( 45 ), our results showing that the phosphomimetic mutant T111E is much less active than wild-type 7B2 in facilitating proPC2 activation are perhaps not surprising. The additional negative charge, and/or the increased length of the side chain due to the addition of the phosphate group, may disturb the 7B2-proPC2 interaction by affecting the binding interface. Conversely, phosphorylation of Thr73, a residue that is completely conserved among species, seems to play no role in proPC2 activation. The phosphorylation of this residue may affect other as-yet-unknown cellular functions of 7B2.

In 1991, Paquet et al ( 35 ) found that humans and mice express two different alternatively spliced 7B2 transcripts, which differ only in the presence or absence of the residue Ala100. Despite the fact that both mRNA forms are abundantly expressed in human and rodent tissues ( 35 ), they have never been biochemically characterized. In this study, we show not only that these two alternatively spliced variants are similarly phosphorylated by FAM20C but also that the Ala-less variant is much less active than the Ala-containing protein in mediating proPC2 activation. Although most of the 7B2 protein appears to be disordered ( 27 ), a secondary structure analysis of the 36-residue peptide sequence that contributes heavily to proPC2 binding reveals that, unlike Thr111, which is predicted to be located within an alpha helix, Ala100 is located in a disordered region. Because the absence of the Ala residue reduces 7B2-mediated protection of proPC2, we suggest that this region is implicated in 7B2 binding to proPC2. Differential splicing of 7B2 may represent a way in which cells modulate the amount of proPC2 that can be activated, although whether this then results in physiological consequences on peptide processing is not clear. Whether specific physiological conditions exist that result in preferential expression of one or the other splice form also requires further investigation.

Apart from its specific role in proPC2 activation, 7B2 can function as an antiaggregation chaperone for a variety of proteins prone to aggregation. Indeed, 7B2 not only prevents the unproductive aggregation of proPC2 ( 20 ) but also blocks the fibrillation of many polypeptides associated with neurodegenerative and metabolic diseases such as certain amyloid-β (Aβ) peptides, α-synuclein, and islet amyloid polypeptide ( 22 – 24 ). Given that the chaperone activity of certain small heat shock proteins, such as αB-crystallin, HSP25, and HSP27, is regulated by phosphorylation ( 46 – 48 ), we analyzed the effect of phosphorylation on the chaperone activity of 7B2. Our results reveal that FAM20C-mediated phosphorylation of 7B2 is not relevant to its function as molecular chaperone, at least with regard to preventing the cytotoxic fibrillation of hIAPP in neural cells. Similarly, the alternative splicing mechanism is apparently not critical to the antiaggregant properties of 7B2 because both variants behaved similarly in preventing the cytotoxic effect of hIAPP aggregates. Taken together, these data suggest that the 36-residue region previously described to be necessary for proPC2 interaction ( 45 ) may not overlap with the portion of 7B2 required for interaction with fibrillating proteins.

In conclusion, our data show that neurons and endocrine cells exhibit two different regulatory mechanisms to control the capacity of 7B2 to adjust total cellular PC2 activity: FAM20C-mediated threonine phosphorylation and alternative splicing. Whether either of these processes becomes dysregulated under pathological conditions is not yet known and will represent an exciting area for further study.
