Nucleokinesis in neuronal migration.

Tsai LH(1), Gleeson JG.

Neuronal migration is a critical phase of nervous system development and can be
divided into two distinct phases: extension of the leading process and movement
of the cell body and nucleus (nucleokinesis). Nucleokinesis appears to require
many of the same cytoskeletal and signaling molecules used in cell mitosis.
Converging studies suggest it requires cytoplasmic dynein, cell polarity genes,
and microtubule-associated proteins that coordinate microtubule remodeling.
These coordinate first the positioning of the centrosome (microtubule organizing
center) in the leading process in front of the nucleus and then the movement of
the nucleus towards the centrosome. The positioning of the centrosome and the
dynamic regulation that couples and uncouples the nucleus underlies directed
migration of neurons.
