Upon ligand binding, RIPK1 is recruited to tumor necrosis factor receptor
superfamily (TNFRSF) and Toll-like receptor (TLR) complexes promoting
prosurvival and inflammatory signaling. RIPK1 also directly regulates
caspase-8-mediated apoptosis or, if caspase-8 activity is blocked,
RIPK3-MLKL-dependent necroptosis. We show that C57BL/6 Ripk1(-/-) mice die at
birth of systemic inflammation that was not transferable by the hematopoietic
compartment. However, Ripk1(-/-) progenitors failed to engraft lethally
irradiated hosts properly. Blocking TNF reversed this defect in emergency
hematopoiesis but, surprisingly, Tnfr1 deficiency did not prevent inflammation
in Ripk1(-/-) neonates. Deletion of Ripk3 or Mlkl, but not Casp8, prevented
extracellular release of the necroptotic DAMP, IL-33, and reduced
Myd88-dependent inflammation. Reduced inflammation in the Ripk1(-/-)Ripk3(-/-),
Ripk1(-/-)Mlkl(-/-), and Ripk1(-/-)Myd88(-/-) mice prevented neonatal lethality,
but only Ripk1(-/-)Ripk3(-/-)Casp8(-/-) mice survived past weaning. These
results reveal a key function for RIPK1 in inhibiting necroptosis and, thereby,
a role in limiting, not only promoting, inflammation.
