Abstract

Defects in spermatogenesis, a process that produces spermatozoa inside seminiferous tubules of the testis, result in male infertility. Spermatogenic progression is highly dependent on a microenvironment provided by Sertoli cells, the only somatic cells and epithelium of seminiferous tubules. However, genes that regulate such an important activity of Sertoli cells are poorly understood. Here, we found that AT-rich interactive domain 4B (ARID4B), is essential for the function of Sertoli cells to regulate spermatogenesis. Specifically, we generated Sertoli cell-specific Arid4b knockout ( Arid4b SCKO) mice, and showed that the Arid4b SCKO male mice were completely infertile with impaired testis development and significantly reduced testis size. Importantly, severe structural defects accompanied by loss of germ cells and Sertoli cell-only phenotype were found in many seminiferous tubules of the Arid4b SCKO testes. In addition, maturation of Sertoli cells was significantly delayed in the Arid4b SCKO mice, associated with delayed onset of spermatogenesis. Spermatogenic progression was also defective, showing an arrest at the round spermatid stage in the Arid4b SCKO testes. Interestingly, we showed that ARID4B functions as a “coactivator” of androgen receptor and is required for optimal transcriptional activation of reproductive homeobox 5, an androgen receptor target gene specifically expressed in Sertoli cells and critical for spermatogenesis. Together, our study identified ARID4B to be a key regulator of Sertoli cell function important for male germ cell development.

Spermatogenesis is a developmental process, whereby mature spermatozoa (also known as sperm cells) are produced inside seminiferous tubules of the testis. Spermatogenesis begins when gonocytes and undifferentiated spermatogonia undergo sequential steps of spermatogenic differentiation to become differentiating spermatogonia, spermatocytes, spermatids, and eventually, the mature spermatozoa. Spermatogenic disorders lead to male infertility. The process of spermatogenesis is highly dependent upon the optimal conditions of the testis. Sertoli cells, the only somatic cells inside the seminiferous tubules, maintain an environment necessary for development of germ cells, by providing both physical and nutritional support for all types of germ cells ( 1 ). Sertoli cells undergo maturation (terminal differentiation) at puberty, during which they stop to proliferate and switch on various functions necessary for support of developing germ cells ( 2 , 3 ). Although defects in Sertoli cell function are expected to result in failure of spermatogenesis, genes responsible for regulating such function of Sertoli cells are poorly understood. Identifying these genes is important as it may provide a better understanding into the cause of male infertility.

AT-rich interactive domain 4B (ARID4B) is a member of the ARID family. The ARID domain contains a helix-turn-helix motif with putative DNA-binding activity ( 4 ). In addition to the ARID domain, ARID4B contains a Tudor domain and a chromo domain ( 5 ). The Tudor domain and chromo domain function as molecular adaptors by binding to the methylated histones, and promote physical interactions and assembly of the chromatin remodeling complexes ( 6 , 7 ). The ARID4 subfamily has 2 members: ARID4A and ARID4B ( 4 ), previously known as retinoblastoma-binding protein 1 ( RBP1 ) ( 8 ) and RBP1- like protein 1 ( 9 ), respectively. ARID4A and ARID4B proteins can interact with each other ( 10 ), and both are components of the SIN3 transcription regulator family member A/histone deacetylase 1 (SIN3A/HDAC1) chromatin remodeling complex ( 5 , 11 , 12 ).

Recently, we reported the importance of Arid4a and Arid4b in normal development and physiology by using mouse conventional knockout models ( 10 , 13 , 14 ). Although mice globally deficient for Arid4b ( Arid4b −/− ) die during embryogenesis (between embryonic day [E]3.5 and E7.5) ( 10 ), we found that ARID4A and ARID4B play a role in male reproductive function using mice that are haploinsufficient for Arid4b and completely deficient for Arid4a ( Arid4a −/− Arid4b +/− ) ( 14 ). The Arid4a −/− Arid4b +/− male mice showed progressive loss of male fertility, accompanied by hypogonadism ( 14 ). Evaluation of germ cell development in the Arid4a −/− Arid4b +/− males identified spermatogenic arrest at the stages of meiotic spermatocytes and postmeiotic haploid spermatids ( 14 ). However, the Arid4a −/− Arid4b +/− males showed seminal vesicle agenesis/hypodysplasia ( 14 ), which may also contribute to the observed infertility, but is independent of the abnormal function of the testis.

In the testis, ARID4A and ARID4B are mainly expressed in Sertoli cells ( 14 ). Because the Arid4b −/− mice die at early embryonic stages, it is impossible to study the effects of global ARID4B ablation on spermatogenesis, a process which starts after birth. To overcome the embryonic lethality, we generated Sertoli cell-specific Arid4b knockout ( Arid4b SCKO) mice. The fact that the Arid4b −/− mice are lethal underscores distinct and nonredundant function between ARID4A and ARID4B ( 10 ). Therefore, we studied the Arid4b SCKO mice to investigate the individual role of Arid4b in male reproduction. In addition, we were able to exclude any secondary effects and elucidate function of Arid4b specifically in Sertoli cells using the Arid4b SCKO mice.

Surprisingly, our results showed that the Arid4b SCKO male mice displayed several unique and more severe phenotypes than the Arid4a −/− Arid4b +/− male mice. The Arid4b SCKO male mice were completely infertile, whereas the Arid4a −/− Arid4b +/− male mice were able to produce litters but lost their fertility at later reproductive age (progressive loss of male fertility). The Arid4b SCKO male testes exhibited spermatogenic failure and depletion of germ cells. Mechanistically, we showed that ARID4B functions as a transcriptional “coactivator” for androgen receptor (AR) and identified reproductive homeobox 5 ( Rhox5 ) as the target gene critical for spermatogenesis. Our current study identified ARID4B as an essential gene for Sertoli cells to support spermatogenesis, and thus is required for male reproductive function.

Materials and Methods

Targeting vector

To construct the targeting vector for the Arid4b flox/flox mice, the neomycin resistance gene flanked by 2 loxP sites were introduced into upstream of exon 1 of Arid4b , and a loxP site was inserted into downstream of exon 1. The targeting vector also included the 5′-homologous arm, 3′-homologous arm, and the herpes simplex viral thymidine kinase expression cassette outside the 3′-homologous region. A 3.3-kb Xho I- Sal I phage clone end fragment was cloned as the 5′-homologous arm and a 2-kb Eco RI phage clone end fragment was cloned as the 3′-homologous arm.

Generation of the Arid4b flox / flox mice

The targeting vector for the Arid4b flox/flox mice was electroporated into AB2.2 ES cells from the 129/SvEv strain provided by Allan Bradley (The Welcome Trust Sanger Institute). Neomycin (G418 sulfate, 200 mg/mL; Life Technologies) was used to select for ES cells undergoing the desired recombination event. The neo gene, flanked by 2 loxP sites, was subsequently removed by the Cre recombinase. The targeted ES clones were injected into C57BL6/J blastocysts and reimplanted into pseudopregnant female mice. Chimeric males were bred to C57BL6/J females to evaluate germline transmission. For genotyping, genomic DNA extracted from mouse tail or testis was analyzed by Southern blot hybridization using the M probes, or by PCR amplification using the next primers: primer 1, 5′-GCTAGCAGCCATCTCTTCTCAAC-3′; primer 2, 5′-GTATAGAACTCAAACTGCCTTAGGC-3′; and primer 3, 5′-CGACACTTCCTGAATCTAGAACTA-3′.

Mouse lines

Mice heterozygous for the Arid4b deletion ( Arid4b +/− ) have been described previously ( 10 ). The Arid4b flox/flox and Arid4b +/− mice are maintained on a hybrid C57BL6/J and 129/SvEv genetic background. The anti-Müllerian hormone ( Amh )-cre transgenic mice were purchased from The Jackson Laboratory. Experimental animals and studies were approved by the Institutional Animal Care and User Committee of The George Washington University (protocol number A208). All of the mice were bred and maintained at the institution's specific pathogen-free mouse facility. The facility is approved by the American Association for Accreditation of Laboratory Animal Care and operated in accordance with current regulations and standards of the United States Department of Agriculture and the Department of Health and Human Services.

Histological analysis and terminal deoxynucleotidyltransferase-mediated deoxyuridine triphosphate-biotin nick end labeling (TUNEL) assays

Testes and epididymis were dissected from male mice and fixed in Bouin's fluid (75% saturated picric acid, 5% glacial acetic acid, 9.3% formaldehyde). Histology was performed on 7-μm-thick paraffin-embedded testis sections by hematoxylin and eosin staining.

For analysis of apoptosis, testis sections were stained by in situ cell death detection kit (Roche Diagnostics), the TUNEL-based assays.

Immunofluorescence staining

Testes were dissected from male mice and fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde in PBS. Immunofluorescence analysis was performed on 7-μm-thick paraffin-embedded testis sections. Antigen retrieval was performed by boiling the testes sections in citric acid based antigen unmasking solution (Vector Laboratories). Samples were blocked with blocking solution (5% donkey serum, 2% bovine serum albumin, and 0.02% Triton X-100 in PBS), followed by incubation with primary antibodies in blocking solution. The primary antibodies are listed in Supplemental Table 1 . Sections were washed with 0.1% Triton X-100/PBS buffer, and incubated with horseradish peroxidase-conjugated or biotinylated secondary antibodies (Jackson ImmunoResearch). Signal detection was carried out by Tyramide Signal Amplification system (Life Technologies) or Alexa Fluor 488 Streptavidin (Molecular Probes, Life Technologies). Sections were mounted with fluorescence mounting medium with 4′,6-diamidino-2-phenylindole (DAPI) (Vector Laboratories). Images were captured with a Nikon Eclipse T i -U fluorescence microscope or an Olympus Fluoview FV1000 confocal microscope.

Southern blot analysis

Genotype identification of offspring from the Arid4b +/ flox mice intercross was performed by Southern blot hybridization. Genomic DNA isolated from tail biopsies was digested with Bam HI for Southern blot analysis using a 1.5-kb PCR fragment as an M probe. Fragment sizes for wild-type and 2 loxP alleles are 8 and 4 kb, respectively.

Quantitative RT-PCR (qRT-PCR) analysis

Testes were dissected from 3 Arid4b SCKO and 3 control males. Total RNA was purified from mouse testes using an RNeasy plus kit (QIAGEN), and was treated by deoxyribonuclease I (QIAGEN). Two micrograms of deoxyribonuclease I-treated total RNA was used for reverse transcription to synthesize the first strand cDNA by the Superscript III First-strand synthesis system (Life Technologies). Quantitative PCR (qPCR) was performed on the ABI StepOne Plus machine using TaqMan gene expression assays (Applied Biosystems) for Ardi4a , Arid4b , and Claudin 3 ( Cldn3 ), and using SYBR Green-based gene expression analyses for other genes. The primer sequences are listed in Supplemental Table 2. The Gapdh transcripts were amplified as an internal control to normalize gene expression. The levels of gene expression were normalized against the level of Gapdh expression in each sample. At least 3 mice for each genotype were analyzed. In each experiment, the normalized level of the gene of interest from 1 of the control mice was set as 1.

Cell lines and culture conditions

TM4 cells were purchased from the American Type Culture Collection and were routinely maintained in a 1:1 mixture of DMEM and F12 media (Life Technologies) supplemented with 5% horse serum and 2.5% fetal bovine serum (HyClone) at 37°C in the presence of 5% CO 2 . When transiently transfected with the Flag-AR plasmid, the TM4 cells were cultured in phenol red-free DMEM/F12 media (Life Technologies) containing 5% charcoal-treated fetal bovine serum (HyClone). Approximately 18 hours after transfection, 17-beta-Hydroxy-17-methylestra-4,9,11-trien-3-one (R1881) or vehicle was added to a final concentration of 100nM. The cells were incubated for additional 18 hours and were used for the luciferase reporter gene assay.

Plasmids

The mammalian expression plasmids for Flag-ARID4A and ARID4B-V5 have been described previously ( 10 ). The plasmid expressing Flag-AR was generated by subcloning the AR cDNA into a modified pCR3.1 vector (Life Technologies) containing a Flag tag at the N terminus. The mouse Rhox5 promoter (−444 to −30 bp) was amplified by PCR from gDNA prepared from mouse testes and cloned into the pGL3-basic vector (Promega). For amplification of the Rhox5 promoter, the next primers were used: the forward primer, 5′-GATGAGATATCTTCCCAGGAAGAG-3′ and the reverse primer, 5′-GCTTCGGCAGATTCTGAGCTTG-3′.

Transfection and luciferase reporter gene assay

Plasmid transfection by FuGene HD (Promega) was carried out according to the manufacturers' instructions. Forty-eight hours after transfection, whole-cell lysates were prepared and the luciferase activity was determined by the luciferase assay system as instructed by the manufacturer (Promega).

Chromatin immunoprecipitation (ChIP)

Testes dissected from wild-type mice at postnatal day (P)30 of age were used for ChIP analysis on the Rhox5 promoter. ChIP assays were performed as described by Millipore. Chromatin extracted from mouse testes was immunoprecipitated with anti-ARID4B antibody (A302–233A; Bethyl Laboratories) or anti-AR antibody (N-20; Santa Cruz Biotechnology, Inc). DNA from immunoprecipitated chromatin was analyzed by qPCR analyses using the primer sets listed in Supplemental Table 2.

Steroid hormone assays

Mice were anesthetized, and blood was obtained from retroorbital venous plexus. Serum was separated by centrifugation. Serum levels of testosterone, LH, and FSH were measured by University of Virginia Ligand Assay and Analysis Core.

Statistical analysis

Means were calculated from at least 3 independent experiments. All results were shown as the mean ± SEM. Two-tailed unpaired Student's t test was used to compare 2 groups. Differences were considered to be statistically significant when a P value is less than the significance level (α-value) of 0.05. We used the indicators (*, P < .01 and **, P < .001) to indicate the statistically significant differences.

Results

Generation of Sertoli cell-specific Arid4b SCKO mice

To generate mice with Sertoli cell-specific deletion of Arid4b , we took advantage of the Cre- loxP recombination system. The targeting strategy to generate the Arid4b flox/flox mice was shown in Figure 1 , A and B. The Arid4b flox/flox mice were mated with the Amh- Cre transgenic mice to specifically delete the Arid4b allele in Sertoli cells of the testis. Expression of the Cre recombinase in the Amh- Cre mice is under control of the Sertoli cell-specific Amh gene promoter ( 15 ). PCR genotyping confirms that loxP recombination on the Arid4b allele occurred only in the testis and not in the tail of the Arid4b +/ flox Amh -Cre mice ( Figure 1 C). To obtain the most complete Arid4b deletion possible, we intercrossed the Arid4b flox/flox mice with the Arid4b +/− Amh -Cre mice to generate the Sertoli cell-specific Arid4b SCKO ( Arid4b −/ flox Amh -Cre) mice, which are referred to as the Arid4b SCKO mice hereafter. The Arid4b +/ flox Amh -Cre mice from the same cross were used as control mice.

Figure 1.

It has been reported that Cre-recombinase activity of the Amh -Cre transgene is restricted to the Sertoli cells and can be detected at E11.5 and beyond ( 16 ). In agreement, ARID4B protein was detected by immunofluorescence analyses in the nuclear region of Sertoli cells in the control testes at E15.5 and P42 but not in the age-matched Arid4b SCKO testes ( Figure 1 D). In addition, qRT-PCR analyses showed that Arid4b was expressed in the control testes at P0.5 with a slight increase at P14 and P42, whereas expression of Arid4b was largely reduced in the Arid4b SCKO testes at P42 ( Figure 1 E). Together, these results confirm Sertoli cell-specific ablation of ARID4B in the Arid4b SCKO testes.

Sertoli cell-specific deletion of Arid4b reduced the size of testis and epididymis

Male and female pups with the Arid4b −/ flox Amh -Cre ( Arid4b SCKO) genotype were born with expected Mendelian ratios, and no external physical abnormalities were observed throughout their lifetime. To determine the effects of Sertoli cell-specific Arid4b ablation on male fertility, 6-week-old Arid4b SCKO and control males were mated with adult wild-type females for over a 10-month period. Although the control males were fertile, the Arid4b SCKO males were fully infertile. Examination of the urogenital system revealed a reduced size of testes and epididymis in the Arid4b SCKO mice, but a comparable size of kidneys and seminal vesicles was found between the Arid4b SCKO and control mice ( Figure 2 A). Measurement clearly showed that the weights of testis and epididymis in the Arid4b SCKO mice were reduced to approximately 25% and 65% of the control values, respectively ( Figure 2 B). On the other hand, the weights of kidney and seminal vesicle in the Arid4b SCKO mice were not significantly different from the controls ( Figure 2 B).

Figure 2.

Because the Arid4b SCKO male mice were infertile and contain smaller testes, we measured the levels of serum testosterone, LH, and FSH. Interestingly, our results showed that although no significant difference in the levels of serum testosterone was found between the control and Arid4b SCKO males, the levels of serum LH and FSH were significantly elevated in the Arid4b SCKO males ( Figure 2 C).

The Arid4b SCKO testes displayed structural defects in the seminiferous tubules

To identify the onset and examine the defects in the Arid4b SCKO mice, testes from mice between P2.5 and P42 were analyzed. Our results showed a significant decrease in testis sizes of the Arid4b SCKO mice from P10 onward ( Figure 3 A). Because Sertoli cells are the epithelium of seminiferous tubules, we therefore investigated the effects of Sertoli cell-specific ARID4B ablation on the structure of seminiferous tubules. Histological analysis revealed expansion of germinal cells in the seminiferous tubules of control testes at P10 ( Figure 3 B, q). In contrast, the Arid4b SCKO testes at P10 contained few germ cells inside the seminiferous tubules ( Figure 3 B, u). Although expansion of germinal cells was found in the Arid4b SCKO testes at P20, sloughing of cells into the lumen of the tubules was clearly evident ( Figure 3 B, v). Furthermore, the Arid4b SCKO testes at P30 and P42 displayed a large increase in tubular diameter and widened central lumen when compared with the age-matched controls ( Figure 3 B, c, d, g, h, k, l, o, and p). In addition, many tubules from the Arid4b SCKO testes underwent vacuolar degeneration ( Figure 3 B, w and x). These results suggest structural defects in the seminiferous tubules of the Arid4b SCKO testes.

Figure 3.

Increased apoptosis in the Arid4b SCKO testes

To examine whether Sertoli cell-specific ablation of ARID4B affects cell survival in the testes, we used TUNEL assay to detect apoptotic DNA fragmentation in the Arid4b SCKO and control testes at P10, P20, and P30. The results showed significant increases of the apoptotic index in the Arid4b SCKO testes at all ages compared with the age-matched control testes ( Figure 4 A). Quantification of the TUNEL assays clearly showed that significant increases in apoptosis were found in the Arid4b SCKO testes at P10, P20, and P30 ( Figure 4 B). Strikingly, extensive apoptosis occurred in the P10 Arid4b SCKO testes, where approximately 60% tubules were TUNEL+ ( Figure 4 B). Although the number of the TUNEL+ tubules decreased in the Arid4b SCKO testes with age, apoptotic cells were still apparent in approximately 25% of tubules at P30 ( Figure 4 B). These results indicate that ARID4B is required for preventing cell death in the testis.

Figure 4.

Depletion of germ cells in the Arid4b SCKO testes

Because Sertoli cells provide physical and nutritional support for germ cells, we examined whether Sertoli cell-specific ablation of ARID4B affects the capability of Sertoli cells to support germ cells. To detect presence of the germ cell population, immunofluorescent staining for the testicular germ cell-specific antigen TRA98 was performed on the Arid4b SCKO and control testes at E15.5, P10, and P20. Although the population of germ cells in the Arid4b SCKO testes at E15.5 was similar to that in the control testes ( Figure 5 , A–D), the Arid4b SCKO testes at P10 contained significantly reduced number of germ cells compared with the controls ( Figure 5 , E–H). By P20, only few seminiferous tubules in the Arid4b SCKO testes contained germ cells ( Figure 5 , J and L), whereas germ cells were present in all seminiferous tubules of the control testes as expected ( Figure 5 , I and K). These results suggest that ablation of ARID4B in Sertoli cells leads to a reduction of germ cells in the seminiferous tubules.

Figure 5.

Deletion of Arid4b delays Sertoli cell maturation

Through change of gene expression, Sertoli cells undergo maturation (terminal differentiation) at puberty and acquire functions that support developing germ cells in the process of spermatogenesis. We examined whether transition of Sertoli cells from their “developmental” to their “adult” state was affected by ablation of ARID4B. AMH is a known marker of immature Sertoli cells, and expression of AMH is switched off during Sertoli cell maturation. Therefore, absence of AMH expression is widely used as an indicator of Sertoli cell maturational development. We performed immunofluorescent staining of AMH in testes from the control and Arid4b SCKO mice at P10, P20, P30, and P42. Our results showed that expression of AMH in the control testis was low at P10 ( Figure 6 A) and undetectable at P20, P30, and P42 ( Figure 6 , C, E, and G), indicating maturation of Sertoli cells. On the other hand, expression of AMH in the P10 Arid4b SCKO testes was readily detectable ( Figure 6 B), and the levels remained prominent in many tubules of the P20 Arid4b SCKO testes ( Figure 6 D). In contrast to the control testes, expression of AMH in some tubules of the Arid4b SCKO testes remained detectable at P30 and P42, despite decreases in intensity ( Figure 6 , F and H). These results suggest that Sertoli cell maturation was delayed in the Arid4b SCKO testes.

Figure 6.

Because appropriate development of Sertoli cells determines the spermatogenic capacity, we investigated whether the delay in Sertoli cell maturation could be the underlying cause of the reduced number of germ cells in the Arid4b SCKO testes ( Figure 5 J). Double immunofluorescent staining for the immature Sertoli cell marker AMH and the germ cell marker TRA98 was performed. The P20 testes from the control and Arid4b SCKO testes were used because expression of AMH was largely diminished in the control testes at this age ( Figure 6 I), but remained prominent in many tubules of the Arid4b SCKO testes ( Figure 6 J). In addition, not all of the tubules in the P20 Arid4b SCKO testes generated germ cells ( Figure 6 L). In contrast, germ cells (TRA98+) were present in all seminiferous tubules of the control testis ( Figure 6 , K, M, and O), where expression of AMH has largely been turned off ( Figure 6 I). Interestingly, although immature Sertoli cells (AMH+) were present in many of the seminiferous tubules in the Arid4b SCKO testes ( Figure 6 J), no germ cells were produced in such tubules, resulting in formation of the Sertoli cell-only tubules ( Figure 6 , L, N, and P). On the other hand, germ cells were produced only in the seminiferous tubules that did not express AMH ( Figure 6 , L, N, and P). These results suggest that immature Sertoli cells in seminiferous tubules of the Arid4b SCKO testes lack the ability to support developing germ cells even when the male mice have reached puberty (P20).

Ablation of Arid4b in Sertoli cells results in spermatogenesis defects

Next, we investigated the effects of Sertoli cell-specific ARID4B ablation on spermatogenesis. We examined the first wave of spermatogenesis in the control and Arid4b SCKO testes at P10, P20, P30, and P42. Spermatogenesis begins after birth when quiescent gonocytes reenter the cell cycle and give rise to spermatogonia. In the P10 control testes, spermatogonia underwent meiosis to generate spermatocytes, and the thickness of germinal epithelium of the seminiferous tubules was increased ( Figure 7 A). However, the Arid4b SCKO tubules at P10 contained only a thin layer of epithelium and few spermatogonia ( Figure 7 B). At P20, spermatocytes became round spermatids in the control testes ( Figure 7 C). On the other hand, no round spermatids have developed yet in the Arid4b SCKO testes at P20, despite spermatocytes being generated in some of the seminiferous tubules ( Figure 7 D). At P30, round spermatids developed into elongating spermatids in the control tubules ( Figure 7 E), whereas the Arid4b SCKO tubules developed only round spermatids, but no elongating spermatids ( Figure 7 F). These results suggest a delay in spermatogenesis in the Arid4b SCKO testes.

Figure 7.

At P42, the first wave of spermatogenesis has completed, and spermatozoa were generated in the control testes ( Figure 7 G). However, no elongating spermatids and spermatozoa were developed in the Arid4b SCKO testes at P42, and germ cell development was arrested at the round spermatid stage ( Figure 7 , H and I). The presence of round spermatids in both control and Arid4b SCKO testes at P30 and P42 was confirmed by immunofluorescent staining for the round spermatid marker, cAMP-responsive element modulator ( Supplemental Figure 1 ). In addition, many seminiferous tubules in the Arid4b SCKO testes at P42 showed severe vacuolar degeneration ( Figure 7 , I and J), and some tubules were totally devoid of germ cells and contained only Sertoli cells at the basal membrane ( Figure 7 J).

The impaired spermatogenesis in the Arid4b SCKO males was confirmed by examining the epididymal lumens. Although the epididymis of the control mice was filled with spermatozoa ( Figure 7 K), the epididymis of the Arid4b SCKO mice was devoid of any spermatozoa and contained only few round spermatids ( Figure 7 L). Together, these results showed that ablation of Arid4b in Sertoli cells resulted in a delay of spermatogenesis and an arrest at the round spermatid stage, suggesting that Arid4b is important for Sertoli cell function to support spermatogenic differentiation of germ cells.

Down-regulation of AR responsive genes Rhox5 and Cldn3 in the Arid4b SCKO testes

To investigate the mechanism responsible for the spermatogenic failure in the Arid4b SCKO testes, we examined the effects of Sertoli cell-specific ARID4B ablation on expression of genes involved in spermatogenesis. Previously, we showed that ARID4B physically associates with the androgen receptor (AR) ( 14 ). AR plays an important role in spermatogenesis, in part by regulating expression of Rhox5 in Sertoli cells. Rhox5 is essential for the survival of male germ cells and sperm production ( 17 ). Because ablation of Arid4b resulted in depletion of germ cells and spermatogenic defects, we examined whether ARID4B regulates expression of Rhox5 . In fact, qRT-PCR analysis showed that the level of the Rhox5 transcript was significantly decreased in the Arid4b SCKO testes at P42 compared with the control testes ( Figure 8 A). Down-regulation of Rhox5 in the Arid4b SCKO testes is specific, because the levels of the transcripts for Ptgds , Ctnnb1 , and the Arid4b -related gene Arid4a , which are mainly expressed in Sertoli cells and important for spermatogenesis ( 14 , 18 – 20 ), were not altered in the Arid4b SCKO testes ( Figure 8 A).

Figure 8.

Cldn3 encodes a component of the tight junction complex of the blood-testis barrier (BTB), and is another AR-responsive gene in Sertoli cells ( 21 , 22 ). Similar to Rhox5 , expression of Cldn3 was decreased in the Arid4b SCKO testes at P42 ( Figure 8 A), whereas expression of other tight junction components of the BTB, including occludin ( Ocln ) ( 21 ), tight junction protein 1 ( Tjp1 ) ( OZ-1 ) ( 22 ), and Tjp2 was not significantly affected ( Figure 8 A).

Given the down-regulation of AR-responsive genes Rhox5 and Cldn3 in the Arid4b SCKO testes, we examined whether expression of AR is affected. Because AR is expressed in Sertoli cells from P8 onward ( 23 ), we examined expression of AR in the Arid4b SCKO and control testes at P10, P20, and P30. Despite the Arid4b SCKO testes exhibiting delayed maturation of Sertoli cells ( Figure 6 A), double immunofluorescent staining for AR and the immature Sertoli cell marker AMH showed that both mature and immature Sertoli cells in the Arid4b SCKO testes expressed AR protein, comparable with the control testes at P10, P20, and P30 (Supplemental Figure 2). These results suggest that the defects of spermatogenesis and down-regulation of the AR-responsive genes Rhox5 and Cldn3 in the Arid4b SCKO testes were not caused by deficiency of AR expression.

ARID4B functions as an AR coactivator to activate Rhox5

Rhox5 is the only direct target gene of AR in Sertoli cells identified thus far ( 17 ). Given the fact that ARID4B protein interacts with AR ( 14 ) and ablation of ARID4B reduces expression of Rhox5 ( Figure 8 A), we examined whether Rhox5 is a direct target of ARID4B. First, we determined whether ARID4B can be recruited to the promoter of Rhox5 by ChIP-qPCR assay using the antibody against endogenous ARID4B. ChIP analysis was performed using testis extracts from the control and Arid4b SCKO mice at P30. Our results clearly showed that ARID4B was recruited to the Rhox5 promoter in the control testes ( Figure 8 B). Recruitment of ARID4B to the Rhox5 promoter was specific, as no recruitment of ARID4B to this promoter was detected in the Arid4b SCKO testes ( Figure 8 B). In addition, ChIP-qPCR assay using antibody against endogenous AR showed that AR was recruited to the same region of the Rhox5 promoter (covered by primer sets 1 and 2) in the control testes ( Figure 8 B). This ARID4B and AR-enriched region contains 4 potential AR-response elements (AREs) found on the Rhox5 promoter ( 24 ). Interestingly, AR was not recruited to the Rhox5 promoter in the Arid4b SCKO testes ( Figure 8 B), suggesting that the recruitment of AR to the Rhox5 promoter is dependent on ARID4B. Together, these results suggest that Rhox5 is a common direct target gene of ARID4B and AR.

Next, we investigated whether ARID4B and its related gene ARID4A collaborate with AR to regulate the activity of the Rhox5 promoter. For this purpose, a reporter plasmid containing the promoter fragment of Rhox5 (from −444 to −30 bp) was constructed and used for luciferase reporter gene assays. We chose this Rhox5 promoter fragment (−444 to −30 bp), because it contains all 4 AREs and harbors the binding sties of both ARID4B and AR ( Figure 8 B). Reporter gene assays were carried out by transient transfection in the Sertoli cell line TM4 followed by treatment with or without AR agonist R1881. Our results showed that cotransfection of ARID4A and ARID4B without AR has no significant effects on the Rhox5 promoter activity ( Figure 8 C, columns 1–4), whereas transfection of AR alone activated the Rhox5 promoter in the presence of R1881 ( Figure 8 C, columns 5 and 6). Interestingly, cotransfection of AR with either ARID4A or ARID4B increased the AR-induced activation of the Rhox5 promoter after treatment of R1881 ( Figure 8 C, compare columns 6 and 8; columns 6 and 10). This up-regulation was further enhanced by cotransfection of both ARID4A and ARID4B with AR ( Figure 8 C, compare columns 8 and 12; columns 10 and 12). These results suggest that ARID4A and ARID4B are able to activate the Rhox5 promoter by acting as coactivators for AR and are required for the optimal expression of Rhox5 .

Discussion

In current study we investigated the importance of a chromatin remodeling gene Arid4b in testis development using the conditional gene targeting approach in Sertoli cells ( Arid4b SCKO). Phenotypic characterization of the Arid4b SCKO mice revealed for the first time a critical and specific function of Arid4b in Sertoli cells. We showed that ablation of Arid4b in Sertoli cells resulted in a legion of defects, including disruption of spermatogenesis, depletion of germ cells, and complete infertility in male mice. Our data also identified potential underlying causes for these severe defects. First of all, Sertoli cells are epithelium of the seminiferous tubules and interact directly with germs cells at all developmental stages. It is expected that the structural defects in seminiferous tubules of the Arid4b SCKO testes might render Sertoli cells incapable of supporting germ cells, leading to apoptosis in the testes. Second, maturation of Sertoli cells at puberty is essential for the nutritional and structural support of developing germ cells during spermatogenesis. Delayed maturation of Sertoli cells in the Arid4b SCKO mice might be a cause for the delay in spermatogenesis. Third, we previously reported that ARID4B physically associated with AR protein ( 14 ). The fact that ARID4B functions as a transcriptional coactivator of AR to modulate expression of AR-responsive genes suggests that ARID4B is an integral part of the AR signaling ( 15 , 25 , 26 ).

AR mediates the action of androgens and is important for spermatogenesis by regulating expression of androgen-responsive genes ( 27 – 29 ). To define the mechanism by which ARID4B regulates spermatogenesis in more detail, we provide evidence that expression of AR-responsive genes Rhox5 and Cldn3 was down-regulated in the Arid4b SCKO testes, suggesting that ablation of ARID4B abolished the AR signaling pathways. It has been shown that expression of Rhox5 in Sertoli cells is essential for the survival of male germ cells and sperm production ( 17 ). Our data indicate that ARID4B was recruited to the ARE-containing region of the Rhox5 promoter. In addition, the fact that ablation of ARID4B in Sertoli cells resulted in decreased expression of Rhox5 indicates that ARID4B contributes to the optimal expression of Rhox5 . Finally, ARID4B and AR interact with each other ( 14 ) and cooperatively activate the Rhox5 promoter. These results suggest that ARID4B functions as a transcriptional coactivator for AR to activate expression of Rhox5 , which is a potential mechanism for the essential role of ARID4B in spermatogenesis. On the other hand, the AR-responsive gene Cldn3 encodes a component of tight junction complex of the BTB ( 21 , 22 ). Sertoli cells establish the BTB to maintain the developing germ cells in an immunologically privileged adluminal compartment, and to prevent passage of cytotoxic substances into the seminiferous tubules. We showed that ablation of ARID4B in Sertoli cells resulted in decreased expression of Cldn3 in the Arid4b SCKO testes. Previously, we showed decreased expression of Cldn3 in the testes of the Arid4a −/− Arid4b +/− mice, and defined the role of ARID4A and ARID4B in the maintenance of the BTB ( 14 ). Together, these results suggest that ablation of ARID4B contributes to the dysfunction of Sertoli cells, in part, due to dysregulation of the AR signaling pathways.

Activation of AR is dependent on the binding of its cognate ligand testosterone. Production of testosterone in Leydig cells is stimulated by gonadotrophin LH which is secreted by the pituitary gland. On the other hand, AR signaling negatively regulates production of hypothalamic GnRH, which stimulates the synthesis and secretion of LH and FSH by the pituitary gland, constituting a classical negative feedback loop ( 30 ). In the Sertoli cell-specific AR knockout male mice, this normal restricting feedback is absent due to the decrease of functional AR, leading to increased production of pituitary LH and FSH. Thus, the Sertoli cell-specific AR knockout males exhibit higher serum LH and FSH levels ( 15 , 25 , 26 ). Consistent with our findings, which support ARID4B as an important coactivator of AR, ablation of ARID4B mimics the effect of loss of functional AR. Accordingly, the Arid4b SCKO male mice also showed elevated levels of serum LH and FSH, although the levels of AR protein and serum testosterone were comparable between the Arid4b SCKO and control males.

Similar to the Sertoli cell-specific AR knockout mice, the Arid4b SCKO mice showed spermatogenesis arrests at the round spermatid stage ( 15 , 25 , 26 ). However, it should be noted that although there is clear phenotypic overlap between the Arid4b SCKO mice and the Sertoli cell-specific AR knockout mice, the Arid4b SCKO testes contain several new phenotypes that have not been observed in the Sertoli cell-specific AR knockout mice. These severe phenotypes include a wide range of structural defects in the seminiferous tubules, presence of Sertoli-cell-only tubules, delayed maturation of Sertoli cells, and a delay in the first wave of spermatogenesis. These observations suggest that the biological activities of ARID4B in Sertoli cells extend beyond its role in the AR signaling pathways.

The ARID4 subfamily contains 2 members, ARID4A and ARID4B ( 4 ). Although ARID4A and ARID4B are chromatin remodeling proteins, they do not contain intrinsic methyltransferase or acethyltransferase activities. Up to date, how ARID4A and ARID4B modulate chromatin modifications to regulate gene expression is unknown. However, ARID4A and ARID4B each contain a Tudor domain and a chromo domain. These domains have been shown to bind to methylated histones and function as molecular adaptors for recruitment and assembly of chromatin remodeling complexes ( 6 , 7 ). ARID4A and ARID4B are components of the Sin3/HDAC chromatin remodeling complex ( 5 , 11 , 12 ), which are involved in the formation of a repressive chromatin structure and suppression of their target genes ( 31 ). ARID4A and ARID4B proteins not only interact with each other ( 10 ), but also are known as RBP1 ( 8 ) and RBP1- like protein 1 ( 9 ), respectively. ARID4A contains a LVCHE sequence, a conserved LXCXE motif known to interact with the pocket of retinoblastoma protein (RB) ( 8 , 32 ). Through association with the pocket of RB, ARID4A/ARID4B recruits the Sin3/HDAC repressor complex to suppress E2F transcription factor-mediated transcription ( 5 , 12 ). Although better known for their corepressor function, our current study demonstrated that ARID4A and ARID4B function as coactivators of AR and are required for the transcriptional activation of the AR direct target gene Rhox5 in vivo. Notably, it was reported that RB also binds to AR and functions as a coactivator of AR ( 33 ). Interestingly, the Sertoli cell-specific Rb knockout mice showed progressive loss of male fertility and down-regulation of the AR-responsive gene Cldn3 in the mutant testes ( 34 ). It is plausible that ARID4A and ARID4B function as positive and negative regulators of transcription in a target gene-specific or signaling-dependent manner, by serving as molecular adaptors to assemble activator and repressor complexes, respectively.

In summary, our study elucidates an important role of ARID4B in male fertility by demonstrating that ARID4B is essential for the function of Sertoli cells to support successful spermatogenic process of developing germ cells. Investigation into the molecular mechanism uncovers that ARID4B is a coactivator of AR and is required for the optimal expression of Rhox5 , an AR target gene in Sertoli cells important for spermatogenesis. To understand how ARID4B modulate chromatin modifications to activate AR target genes, future study to identify the components of the AR/ARID4B coactivator complex is warranted.
