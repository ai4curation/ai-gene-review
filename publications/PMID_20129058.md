# Primase directs the release of DnaC from DnaB.
**Authors:** Makowska-Grzyska M, Kaguni JM
**Journal:** Mol Cell (2010)
**DOI:** [10.1016/j.molcel.2009.12.031](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.molcel.2009.12.031)
**PMC:** [PMC2819048](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC2819048/)

## Abstract

1. Mol Cell. 2010 Jan 15;37(1):90-101. doi: 10.1016/j.molcel.2009.12.031.

Primase directs the release of DnaC from DnaB.

Makowska-Grzyska M(1), Kaguni JM.

Author information:
(1)Department of Biochemistry and Molecular Biology, Michigan State University,
East Lansing, MI 48824-1319, USA.

An AAA+ ATPase, DnaC, delivers DnaB helicase at the E. coli chromosomal origin
by a poorly understood process. This report shows that mutant proteins bearing
alanine substitutions for two conserved arginines in a motif named box VII are
defective in DNA replication, but this deficiency does not arise from impaired
interactions with ATP, DnaB, or single-stranded DNA. Despite their ability to
deliver DnaB to the chromosomal origin to form the prepriming complex, this
intermediate is inactive. Quantitative analysis of the prepriming complex
suggests that the DnaB-DnaC complex contains three DnaC monomers per DnaB
hexamer and that the interaction of primase with DnaB and primer formation
triggers the release of DnaC, but not the mutants, from DnaB. The interaction of
primase with DnaB and the release of DnaC mark discrete events in the transition
from initiation to the elongation stage of DNA replication.
