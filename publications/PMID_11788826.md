# Mutations in DNAH5 cause primary ciliary dyskinesia and randomization of left-right asymmetry.
**Authors:** Olbrich H, Häffner K, Kispert A, Völkel A, Volz A, Sasmaz G, Reinhardt R, Hennig S, Lehrach H, Konietzko N, Zariwala M, Noone PG, Knowles M, Mitchison HM, Meeks M, Chung EM, Hildebrandt F, Sudbrak R, Omran H

## Abstract

1. Nat Genet. 2002 Feb;30(2):143-4. doi: 10.1038/ng817. Epub 2002 Jan 14.

Mutations in DNAH5 cause primary ciliary dyskinesia and randomization of
left-right asymmetry.

Hennig S, Lehrach H, Konietzko N, Zariwala M, Noone PG, Knowles M, Mitchison HM,

Author information:
(1)Department of Pediatrics and Adolescent Medicine, Albert Ludwigs University,

Primary ciliary dyskinesia (PCD, MIM 242650) is characterized by recurrent
infections of the respiratory tract due to reduced mucociliary clearance and by
sperm immobility. Half of the affected offspring have situs inversus (reversed
organs), which results from randomization of left-right (LR) asymmetry. We
previously localized to chromosome 5p a PCD locus containing DNAH5, which
encodes a protein highly similar to the Chlamydomonas gamma-dynein heavy chain.
Here we characterize the full-length 14-kb transcript of DNAH5. Sequence
analysis in individuals with PCD with randomization of LR asymmetry identified
mutations resulting in non-functional DNAH5 proteins.
