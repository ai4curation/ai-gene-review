In Escherichia coli, detoxification of methylglyoxal (MG) requires glyoxalases I
and II. Glyoxalase I (gloA/GlxI) isomerizes the hemithioacetal, formed
spontaneously from MG and glutathione (GSH) to S-lactoylglutathione (SLG), which
is hydrolyzed by glyoxalase II (gloB/GlxII) to lactate and GSH. YcbL from
Salmonella enterica serovar Typhimurium is an unusual type II glyoxalase whose
role in MG detoxification has remained enigmatic. Here we show that YcbL
(gloC/GlxII-2) acts as an accessory type II glyoxylase in E. coli. The two
isoenzymes have additive effects and ensure maximal MG degradation.
