# RNA interference-mediated silencing of synaptotagmin IX, but not synaptotagmin I, inhibits dense-core vesicle exocytosis in PC12 cells.

## Abstract

1. Biochem J. 2004 Jun 15;380(Pt 3):875-9. doi: 10.1042/BJ20040096.

RNA interference-mediated silencing of synaptotagmin IX, but not synaptotagmin
I, inhibits dense-core vesicle exocytosis in PC12 cells.

Fukuda M(1).

Author information:
(1)Fukuda Initiative Research Unit, RIKEN, 2-1 Hirosawa, Wako, Saitama 351-0198,
Japan.

Although PC12 cells express three synaptotagmin isoforms (Syts I, IV and IX),
all of which have been proposed to regulate dense-core vesicle exocytosis, it
remains unknown which of the Sytisoforms acts as the major Ca2+ sensor for
dense-core vesicle exocytosis. In the present study, it has been shown by
immunoaffinity purification and immunocytochemistry that Syts I and IX, but not
Syt IV, are present on the same secretory vesicles in PC12 cells. Silencing of
Syt IX with specific small interfering RNA significantly reduced high
KCl-dependent neuropeptide Y secretion from PC12 cells, whereas silencing of Syt
I with specific small interfering RNA had no significant effect. The results
indicate that Syts I and IX are not functionally equivalent and that Syt IX, and
not Syt I, is indispensable for the regulation of Ca2+-dependent dense-core
vesicle exocytosis in PC12 cells.
