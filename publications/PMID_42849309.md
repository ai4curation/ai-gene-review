Hydrolases represent a diverse class of drug-metabolizing enzymes that catalyze
the hydrolytic cleavage of a broad spectrum of xenobiotics including certain
novel drug modalities, thereby modulating pharmacokinetics and potentially
influencing pharmacodynamics. However, many hydrolases remain poorly
characterized, and their developmental expression and interindividual
variability are not well understood. This study aimed to systematically
characterize hydrolase expression in the human liver. Human hepatocytes obtained
from 8 adult and 50 pediatric donors were subjected to global proteomics
analysis to quantify hydrolase abundance and assess variability across
demographic factors. Two hundred and thirty-nine hydrolases were studied for the
influence of age, sex, and race on their abundance. The key enzymes included
arylacetamide deacetylase (AADAC), alpha/beta hydrolase domain-containing
protein 10 (ABHD10), alkaline phosphatase, tissue-nonspecific isozyme (ALPL),
acyl-peptide hydrolase (APEH), bleomycin hydrolase (BLMH), valacyclovir
hydrolase (BPHL), cytidine deaminase (CDA), carboxylesterase 1 (CES1), CES2,
carboxymethylenebutenolidase homolog (CMBL), cathepsin A (CTSA), cathepsin B
(CTSB), dipeptidyl peptidase 4 (DPP4), epoxide hydrolase 1 (EPHX1), EPHX2,
EPHX3, guanine deaminase (GDA), paraoxonase 1 (PON1), PON2, PON3, and
deoxynucleoside triphosphate triphosphohydrolase SAMHD1 (SAMHD1). Notably,
several enzymes exhibited age-dependent increases in the abundance from neonates
to adults (fold), including CDA (>200), GDA (53), CES1 (14), EPHX3 (12), ALPL
(10), CES2 (3.5), sialate O-acetylesterase (SIAE) (3), and CMBL (2.6). In
contrast, BLMH, CTSA, DPP4, PON2, and SAMHD1 displayed approximately 2-fold to
3-fold lower abundance in adults than in neonates. PON1 was 42% higher in Black
individuals compared with White individuals, whereas PON3 abundance was 32%
higher in males than in females. This study provides the first systematic
quantitative characterization of a broad panel of hydrolases and provides a key
foundation for physiologically-based pharmacokinetic and quantitative systems
pharmacology modeling of the substrates of hydrolytic enzymes. SIGNIFICANCE
STATEMENT: Interindividual variability, including ontogeny, is well
characterized for many drug-metabolizing enzymes but remains poorly defined for
most hydrolases. The influence of age, sex, and race on 239 hepatic hydrolases
was evaluated, revealing important demographic determinants. These data provide
a critical foundation for physiologically-based pharmacokinetic modeling.
Incorporation of proteomics-informed variability into physiologically-based
pharmacokinetic models has the potential to improve the prediction of drug
pharmacokinetics, particularly in pediatric populations.
