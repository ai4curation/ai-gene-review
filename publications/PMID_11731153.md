# Folic acid utilisation related to sulfa drug resistance in Saccharomyces cerevisiae.
**Authors:** Bayly AM, Berglez JM, Patel O, Castelli LA, Hankins EG, Coloe P, Hopkins Sibley C, Macreadie IG
**Journal:** FEMS Microbiol Lett (2001)
**DOI:** [10.1111/j.1574-6968.2001.tb10915.x](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1574-6968.2001.tb10915.x)

## Abstract

1. FEMS Microbiol Lett. 2001 Nov 13;204(2):387-90. doi:
10.1111/j.1574-6968.2001.tb10915.x.

Folic acid utilisation related to sulfa drug resistance in Saccharomyces
cerevisiae.

Bayly AM(1), Berglez JM, Patel O, Castelli LA, Hankins EG, Coloe P, Hopkins
Sibley C, Macreadie IG.

Author information:
(1)CSIRO Health Sciences and Nutrition, Parkville, Vic., Australia.

Saccharomyces cerevisiae mutants deficient in folate synthesis have been
constructed and employed to study the utilisation of exogenous folates in yeast.
One mutant specifically lacked dihydropteroate synthase while the second lacked
dihydrofolate synthase. Exogenous folinic acid restored optimal growth to both
strains. Folic acid did not generally rescue growth but spontaneous isolates
capable of utilising folic acid were selected. The folic acid synthesis pathway
in the folate utilising isolates was restored via transformation with FOL1 or
FOL3 expression plasmids and transformants were tested for resistance to
sulfamethoxazole (SMX). The presence of elevated levels of folic acid led to
greatly reduced SMX sensitivity regardless of whether strains were folate
utilisers or not.
