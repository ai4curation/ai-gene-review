Comment in

CD40 is a 48-kDa phosphorylated transmembrane glycoprotein belonging to the TNF
receptor superfamily. CD40 has been demonstrated on a range of cell types, and
it has an important role in adaptive immunity and inflammation. CD40 has
recently been described on platelets but platelet activation by CD40 has not
been described. In the present study, we use flow cytometry and immunoblotting
to confirm that platelets constitutively express surface CD40. CD40 mRNA was
undetectable, suggesting that the protein is synthesized early in platelet
differentiation by megakaryocytes. Ligation of platelet CD40 with recombinant
soluble CD40L trimer (sCD40LT) caused increased platelet CD62P expression,
alpha-granule and dense granule release, and the classical morphological changes
associated with platelet activation. CD40 ligation also caused beta3 integrin
activation, although this was not accompanied by platelet aggregation. These
actions were abrogated by the CD40L blocking antibody TRAP-1 and the CD40
blocking antibodies M2 and M3, showing that activation was mediated by CD40L
binding to platelet CD40. beta3 integrin blockade with eptifibatide had no
effect, indicating that outside-in signaling via alphaIIbbeta3 was not
contributing to these CD40-mediated effects. CD40 ligation led to enhanced
platelet-leukocyte adhesion, which is important in the recruitment of leukocytes
to sites of thrombosis or inflammation. Our results support a role for
CD40-mediated platelet activation in thrombosis, inflammation, and
atherosclerosis.
