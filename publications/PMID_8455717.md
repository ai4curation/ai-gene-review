Comment in
    Nature. 1993 Mar 25;362(6418):297-8. doi: 10.1038/362297a0.

The N-ethylmaleimide-sensitive fusion protein (NSF) and the soluble NSF
attachment proteins (SNAPs) appear to be essential components of the
intracellular membrane fusion apparatus. An affinity purification procedure
based on the natural binding of these proteins to their targets was used to
isolate SNAP receptors (SNAREs) from bovine brain. Remarkably, the four
principal proteins isolated were all proteins associated with the synapse, with
one type located in the synaptic vesicle and another in the plasma membrane,
suggesting a simple mechanism for vesicle docking. The existence of numerous
SNARE-related proteins, each apparently specific for a single kind of vesicle or
target membrane, indicates that NSF and SNAPs may be universal components of a
vesicle fusion apparatus common to both constitutive and regulated fusion
(including neurotransmitter release), in which the SNAREs may help to ensure
vesicle-to-target specificity.
