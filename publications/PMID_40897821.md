BACKGROUND: m1A, a prevalent RNA modification found in various RNA species, has
recently been reported to modulate cancer progression. However, its effects on
neuroblastoma remain uninvestigated.
METHODS: The PCAT database was utilized to analyze the mRNA levels and survival
probabilities of m1A regulator genes (TRMT6, TRMT61A, ALKBH1, and ALKBH3) in
neuroblastoma patients. Silencing and recovery of TRMT6 were employed to
investigate its role in neuroblastoma in vitro and in vivo. m1A-seq and RIP-qPCR
were performed to identify and confirm the downstream targets of TRMT6.
Additionally, Actinomycin D treatment was administered to assess mRNA stability.
RESULTS: m1A transmethylase TRMT6 expression was significantly elevated in
high-risk and late-stage neuroblastoma patients. Functionally, TRMT6 promotes
the malignancy of neuroblastoma cells in vitro and promotes tumor growth and
metastasis in vivo. Mechanistically, TRMT6 reduces SST mRNA levels by inhibiting
its stability in an m1A-YTHDF2-dependent manner, thereby promoting the
development of neuroblastoma. Furthermore, SST analog octreotide suppresses
neuroblastoma cell malignancy, tumor growth, and metastasis.
CONCLUSIONS: TRMT6 mediates m1A modification of SST to promote neuroblastoma
progression, suggesting that targeting TRMT6 may be a novel potential
therapeutic approach for treating neuroblastoma.

Methods

The PCAT database was utilized to analyze the mRNA levels and survival probabilities of m1A regulator genes (TRMT6, TRMT61A, ALKBH1, and ALKBH3) in neuroblastoma patients. Silencing and recovery of TRMT6 were employed to investigate its role in neuroblastoma in vitro and in vivo. m1A-seq and RIP-qPCR were performed to identify and confirm the downstream targets of TRMT6. Additionally, Actinomycin D treatment was administered to assess mRNA stability.

Results

m1A transmethylase TRMT6 expression was significantly elevated in high-risk and late-stage neuroblastoma patients. Functionally, TRMT6 promotes the malignancy of neuroblastoma cells in vitro and promotes tumor growth and metastasis in vivo. Mechanistically, TRMT6 reduces SST mRNA levels by inhibiting its stability in an m1A-YTHDF2-dependent manner, thereby promoting the development of neuroblastoma. Furthermore, SST analog octreotide suppresses neuroblastoma cell malignancy, tumor growth, and metastasis.

Conclusions

TRMT6 mediates m1A modification of SST to promote neuroblastoma progression, suggesting that targeting TRMT6 may be a novel potential therapeutic approach for treating neuroblastoma.

Introduction

Neuroblastoma is one of the most common pediatric cancers, occupying 7% of childhood malignancies and ~15% mortality in childhood cancers [ 1 ]. The International Staging System for Neuroblastoma (INSS) categorizes patients into four clinical stages: Stage I (tumor located at the origin), Stage II (tumor not crossing the midline), Stage III (tumor crossing the midline), and Stage IV (tumor metastases) [ 2 ]. The International Neuroblastoma Risk Group (INRG) classification system further stratifies patients into low, intermediate, and high-risk categories based on clinical criteria [ 3 ]. Highly heterogeneous is one of its remarkable characteristics. Low-intermediate-risk and I-II stage infants can be spontaneously regressed. However, less than 50% of the 5-year survival was presented for high-risk and IV stage NB patients [ 4 ]. Recent studies have demonstrated that epigenetic modifications play a crucial role in modulating neuroblastoma progression [ 5 ]. The DNA methyltransferase inhibitor 5-aza-deoxycytidine (5-aza or decitabine) has been reported to reduce NB cells proliferation [ 5 ]. Mechanistic investigation has identified MYCN as a key driver and significant prognostic factor for high-risk neuroblastoma, with its amplification being linked to the aggressiveness of neuroblastoma [ 6 , 7 ]. BET inhibitor JQ1, which targets bromodomain-containing protein, effectively reduces the MYCN level and promotes apoptosis in NB [ 8 ]. The application of epigenetic-related therapies in neuroblastoma provides a promising field for neuroblastoma treatment.

N1-methyladenosine is a crucial RNA modification installed at the N1 position of adenosine [ 9 – 11 ], and has been found at tRNAs, mRNAs, and mitochondrial RNAs [ 12 – 14 ]. As a dynamic RNA modification, m1A modification is catalyzed by its “writer” and removed by “eraser” [ 15 ]. To date, the identified m1A “writers” include TRMT6 (tRNA methyltransferase 6), TRMT61A, and TRMT61B. Among these, TRMT6 and TRMT61A are primarily responsible for catalyzing m1A in tRNA and mRNA [ 16 , 17 ], while TRMT61B specifically recognizes mitochondrial 16S rRNA [ 18 ]. It has been reported that ALKBH1 and ALKBH3 could catalyze the removal of m1A in mammalian [ 19 , 20 ]. Analogous to N6-methyladenosine (m6A) modification, the function of m1A requires the recognized by m1A reader, such as YTHDF2 and YTHDF3, which affect RNA metabolism, including mRNA stability, translation, and RNA structure [ 21 , 22 ]. Recent reports demonstrated that m1A modification plays an important role in the development of various cancers [ 23 – 25 ].

TRMT6 and TRMT61A are the earliest identified m1A transmethylases, forming a heterodimeric m1A methylation complex located in the nucleus [ 26 ]. Previous reports have simultaneously targeted TRMT6 and TRMT61A to inhibit the m1A modification and investigate its role in cancer. Monshaugen, I. et al found that depletion of TRMT6/61A decreases the capacity of bladder cancer cells [ 27 ]. Wang, Y. et al. revealed that m1A modification of small RNA derived from TRMT6/61A attenuates gene expression and maintains the unfolded protein response to promote bladder cancer progression [ 24 ]. Recent studies reported that aberrant expression of TRMT6 also affects m1A levels and tumor progression. TRMT6 was known to be overexpressed in hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) and significantly associated with reduced overall survival [ 28 ], which promotes cell proliferation by inhibiting protein expression of the PI3K/AKT pathway [ 29 ]. Wang et al. revealed that TRMT6-suppression inhibited the proliferation, invasion, and migration of glioma cells [ 30 ]. Our previous study showed a significant association between TRMT6 gene rs236110 and Wilms tumor risk [ 31 ]. To date, the role of TRMT6 and TRMT61A in NB occurrence and progression has not yet been explored.

In the present work, we revealed significantly increased TRMT6 expression in high-risk and late-stage neuroblastoma patients and explored the molecular mechanism of TRMT6 in neuroblastoma. Our founding demonstrated that upregulated TRMT6 played an oncogene promoting the NB progression in vitro and in vivo. TRMT6 reduced the mRNA stability of its functional downstream target SST via an m1A-YTHDF2-dependent manner, facilitating NB cell proliferation and migration. Moreover, we discovered that SST analog octreotide significantly suppressed neuroblastoma cell malignancy. This is the first study to explore the role of m1A modification in neuroblastoma development, suggesting that TRMT6 acts as a novel potential diagnosis and therapeutic target.

Discussion

RNA modification plays a crucial role in the neuroblastoma progression [ 43 ]. As a new epigenetics regulator, m1A has been reported to be involved in various tumorigenesis [ 14 ]. However, the role of m1A modification in neuroblastoma is still unclear. In this study, we found that m1A methylase TRMT6 is highly expressed in advanced stages of NB patients and promotes neuroblastoma cell malignancy and tumorigenesis. SST is a direct downstream gene of TRMT6, and its mRNA stability is suppressed in an m1A/YTHDF2-dependent manner. Moreover, SST analog octreotide has been shown to suppress NB progression both in vitro and in vivo, suggesting that octreotide may serve as a potential therapeutic drug in NB (Fig. 6 i).

Accumulating evidence has revealed that m1A modification promotes or inhibits tumor progression through modulating cancer-specific genes. Su et al. found that methyltransferase TRMT6/61 regulates gene silencing of tRF-3s affecting its target protein’s unfolded response to promote bladder cancer development [ 24 ]. Wang et al. reported that TRMT6/61 driver liver tumorigenesis through enhancing tRNA m1A level to increase PPARδ translation [ 21 ]. In this study, we systematically analyzed the role of m1A modification regulator TRMT6, which was upregulated in high-risk and relapse neuroblastoma patients and closely connected with poor overall survival (Fig. 1 ). Additional studies have revealed that TRMT6 is upregulated and considered to a powerful biomarker in various cancer, including HCC [ 28 , 29 ] and glioma [ 30 ]. Thus, it is reasonable to deduce that TRMT6 upregulation may be involved in neuroblastoma development. Herein, we demonstrated that TRMT6 promotes neuroblastoma cell proliferation, clone formation, and migration in vitro (Fig. 2 a–d). Silencing TRMT6 inhibited NB tumorigenesis and metastasis, which could be restored after the re-expression of TRMT6 (Fig. 2 e–k). Hereby, we predicted that an m1A-related strategy, inhibitors against TRMT6, may exert a neuroblastoma therapeutic effect.

To further explore the mechanism of TRMT6 prompting neuroblastoma progression, we analyzed m1A-seq and RNA-seq profile data and identified SST as a downstream target of TRMT6 in neuroblastoma (Fig. 3 h, j). Silencing SST obstructs the inhibition effect of silenced TRMT6 on NB cell migration, proliferation, and colony formation (Fig. 4 g–i, Supplementary Fig. 5 D–F). Early studies reported that SST exerts antiproliferative effects by inhibiting cell proliferation in neoplasms [ 44 , 45 ]. Cattaneo et al. found that SST analogue lanreotide inhibited SY5Y neuroblastoma cell proliferation by suppressing MAP kinase activation [ 46 ]. In this study, we found that SST is one of the downstream targets of TRMT6, whose expression levels are mediated by TRMT6 in an m1A-dependent manner.

Mechanistically, m1A modulates RNA fate through recruiting m1A binding proteins, including YTHDF2 and YTHDF3 [ 10 ]. YTHDF2 was first identified as an RNA-binding protein for m6A modification in 2012 [ 47 ]. Wang et al. revealed that YTHDF2 located at m6A-modified RNA to mRNA decay sites promoting m6A RNA degradation [ 48 ]. A recent study showed that YTHDF2 could destabilize transcript through interacting with m1A-containing RNAs [ 41 ]. Zheng et al. revealed that YTHDF3 could recognize m1A-modified IGF1R and promote its degradation [ 42 ]. Our previous study found that YTHDF2 gene rs3738067 is significantly associated with neuroblastoma susceptibility [ 49 ]. In this study, silencing TRMT6 increases the level of SST transcript by enhancing its RNA stability (Fig. 5 d), suggesting that the m1A readers YTHDF2 or YTHDF3 may participate in this process. We further explored whether YTHDF2 or YTHDF3 affects the SST mRNA level as TRMT6. The result exhibited that YTHDF2 inhibits SST mRNA level, but not YTHDF3 (Fig. 5 h). Notably, the level and stability of SST mRNA, but not m1A modification mutant A277C, is regulated by TRMT6 and YTHDF2 (Fig. 5 g, Supplementary Fig. 6H ). Moreover, the increased SST levels observed in the silence YTHDF2 treatment were restored by the combined knockdown of TRMT6, which removes the m1A modification (Fig. 5 l–n, Supplementary Fig. 6 J, K). These results suggest that TRMT6 reduces SST mRNA stability in a m1A-YTHDF2-dependent manner in neuroblastoma.

RNA methylation has presented strong opportunities in anticancer therapy applications for the last decade [ 50 ]. Previous studies demonstrated that m6A methylase METTL3 inhibitor STM2457 suppressed the proliferation of chemoresistant acute myeloid leukemia cells [ 51 ]. Furthermore, oncometabolite R-2-hydroxyglutarate (R-2HG) exerts an inhibitor of leukemia cell viability by suppressing m6A demethylase FTO activity [ 52 ]. In this study, we demonstrated that TRMT6 promotes neuroblastoma progression in vivo and in vitro. SST, identified as a functional downstream target, is modified with m1A modification and regulated by TRMT6 (Fig. 3 h), playing a crucial role in neuronal differentiation (Supplementary Fig. 4 ). Previous studies have elucidated the regulatory mechanisms, revealing that SST suppresses the activity of Ras stimulated by platelet-derived growth factor receptors (PDGFR) in neuroblastoma cells [ 53 ]. Octreotide, as an SST analog, is used in meningioma therapy and malignant bowel obstruction [ 54 , 55 ], which plays an anti-proliferative effect in gastric carcinomas and colon cancer [ 56 , 57 ]. Borgström, P. et al. previously investigated the role of octreotide by injecting it adjacent to tumors. They found that octreotide significantly reduced tumor volume in nude rats at 10–12 days with a treatment dosage of 10 μg every 12 h [ 58 ]. In our study, we administered octreotide via the tail vein of mice in xenograft and metastasis models at a dosage of 20 mg/kg every three days. We further demonstrated the anti-tumor effect of octreotide on neuroblastoma tumor growth and metastasis (Fig. 6 ). These results confirm the potential of octreotide in neuroblastoma therapy; However, further studies are required to elucidate its regulatory mechanisms.

In summary, our study revealed that m1A transmethylase TRMT6 expression is upregulated in late-stage neuroblastoma patients. TRMT6-dependent m1A methylation inhibits the mRNA stability of the downstream target SST in a YTHDF2-dependent manner, thereby promoting NB progression. Interestingly, we found that octreotide, an analog of SST, effectively suppressed tumorigenesis and metastasis in neuroblastoma cells. Therefore, targeting TRMT6 and its downstream target SST holds significant potential for the diagnosis and therapy of neuroblastoma.
