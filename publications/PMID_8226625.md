## Abstract

1. J Bacteriol. 1993 Nov;175(21):6836-41. doi: 10.1128/jb.175.21.6836-6841.1993.

Mycobacteriophage L5 integrase-mediated site-specific integration in vitro.

Lee MH(1), Hatfull GF.

Author information:
(1)Department of Biological Sciences, University of Pittsburgh, Pennsylvania
15260.

Mycobacteriophage L5, a temperate phage of the mycobacteria, forms stable
lysogens in Mycobacterium smegmatis via site-specific integration of the phage
genome. Recombination occurs within specific phage and bacterial attachment
sites and is catalyzed by the phage-encoded integrase protein in vivo. We
describe here the overexpression and purification of L5 integrase and its
ability to mediate integrative recombination in vitro. We find that L5
integrase-mediated recombination is greatly stimulated by extracts of M.
smegmatis but not by Escherichia coli extracts, purified E. coli integration
host factor, or purified HU, indicating the presence of a novel mycobacterial
integration host factor.
