Liver cancer is one of the most lethal human malignancies in the world. Although 
great efforts have been made to develop novel therapeutic targets, effective 
drug targeting remains limited. C20orf27, located on human chromosome 20, is a 
gene whose function has not been fully elucidated. In this study, we identified 
C20orf27 as a critical gene that facilitates liver cancer progression. We 
detected that C20orf27 was upregulated in liver cancer samples. Gain- and 
loss-of-function experiments demonstrated that C20orf27 enhanced liver cancer 
cell proliferation and migration by regulating cyclin-related proteins, 
including MDM2, PCNA, Cyclin E1, CDK2, and p-Rb. RNA sequencing and 
bioinformatics analyses revealed that NT5E, ACE, and TTR were potential 
downstream targets of C20orf27. Immunohistochemistry analysis of tissue 
microarrays suggested that NT5E expression was significantly associated with 
C20orf27 levels. Moreover, the combination of C20orf27 and NT5E predicted the 
prognosis of liver cancer patients, as higher levels of both were associated 
with poorer overall survival and disease-free survival after surgery. Our 
findings are the first to report the functional role of the C20orf27/NT5E axis 
in promoting hepatocellular carcinoma progression, highlighting its potential as 
a novel therapeutic target and biomarker for HCC.

Introduction

Primary liver cancer is one of the most common malignant tumors of the digestive system [1–3]. Despite recent advances, the prognosis of primary liver cancer patients remains poor, with an overall 5-year survival rate of only 25–30%, and recurrence and/or metastasis occurring in 50–70% of patients within five years after radical resection [4–7]. According to the latest statistics published in 2024, approximately 900,000 new cases of primary liver cancer and nearly 800,000 deaths from this disease occurred globally in 2022. Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) accounts for 75–85% of primary liver cancer cases and ranks sixth in incidence (4.3%) and third in mortality (7.8%) among all malignant tumors worldwide. HCC is highly prevalent in East Asia, Southeast Asia, and Africa [8].

C20orf27 (Chromosome 20 open reading frame 27) is located on human chromosome 20. Although its function remains unclear, current evidence indicates that C20orf27 encodes a protein consisting of 132 amino acids. Studies have shown that C20orf27 expression is elevated in both embryonic and adult tissues, particularly in the heart, lungs, liver, and skeletal muscle. An increasing number of studies suggest that C20orf27 is involved in cell proliferation, differentiation, and apoptosis, and is associated with the initiation and progression of colon cancer [9]. Additionally, C20orf27 has been utilized in genomic and cancer genome analyses to explore gene variants and tumorigenic mechanisms.

In the context of liver cancer progression, recent research has highlighted the role of metabolic reprogramming and the involvement of specific genes in the tumorigenesis process. For instance, long non-coding RNA LINC01234 has been shown to facilitate HCC progression through orchestrating aspartate metabolic reprogramming [10]. Similarly, activation of ACLY by SEC63 plays a significant role in enhancing HCC metastasis under endoplasmic reticulum stress [11]. These findings emphasize the importance of metabolic alterations in cancer progression, including liver cancer. Moreover, NT5E (also known as CD73), which catalyzes the conversion of AMP to adenosine, has been recognized as a key player in the immune evasion mechanisms of cancer. Its expression is associated with poor prognosis in various cancers, including HCC [12]. Preliminary data from our current study indicate that low expression of C20orf27, particularly in combination with low expression of NT5E, may be associated with improved prognosis in HCC patients. The observed correlation between these two genes highlights their potential as biomarkers and may provide new insights into therapeutic strategies for liver cancer.

At present, the function of the C20orf27 gene remains largely unclear. However, its widespread expression across human tissues and organs throughout evolution suggests that it plays a critical role in essential biological processes. With the increasing global burden of cancer, it has become one of the most pressing challenges in modern medicine. In recent years, significant advances have been made in cancer research. Further investigations of the human genome have shed light on the biological significance of C20orf27, revealing its potential involvement in the pathogenesis of various diseases, including cancers.

ResultsC20orf27 is highly expressed in HCC patientsIn order to investigate new drivers of liver cancer progression, we performed a systematic bioinformatics analysis of TCGA clinical datasets. By comparing gene expression profiles in liver cancer tissues versus paracancer tissues, we identified 2,206 genes with differential expressions associated with patient survival, of which 500 were highly expressed in the TCGA dataset. Among these, 133 genes were associated with poorer prognosis when highly expressed (Fig. 1A). Notably, among these genes, C20orf27, an uncharacterized gene located on chromosome 20 in the human genome-drew our attention. We aimed to determine its role in hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) and assess its potential as a novel biomarker. The analysis showed that C20orf27 is highly expressed across various cancers (Fig. 1B) and significantly elevated in liver cancer tissues compared to normal liver tissues (Fig. 1C). Given the scarcity of research, we selected C20orf27 to investigate its specific role and functional mechanisms in liver cancer. To investigate C20orf27 expression levels in HCC cell lines, we conducted RT-qPCR analysis, finding that C20orf27 expression in Hep-G2, PLC/PRF/5, LM3, Huh7, and Hep-3B cells was higher than in normal liver epithelial cells, such as LO2 and QSG (Fig. 1D). Subsequently, RNA was extracted from the tumor and adjacent tissues of nine patients with multifocal liver cancer and nineteen patients with unifocal liver cancer to measure C20orf27 mRNA levels. Results indicated that C20orf27 expression was significantly higher in multifocal liver cancer compared to unifocal liver cancer (Fig. 1E). Furthermore, IHC analysis revealed significantly higher C20orf27 expression in tumor tissues compared to adjacent non- tumor tissues (Fig. 1F), indicating a potential association between C20orf27 and liver cancer malignancy. Immunofluorescence analysis further demonstrated that C20orf27 is primarily localized in the cytoplasm rather than the nucleus (Fig. 1G). Kaplan–Meier survival analysis based on TCGA data revealed that liver cancer patients with higher C20orf27 expression had significantly worse overall survival (OS) and disease-free survival (DFS) compared to those with lower expression (Fig. 1H, I). These findings suggest that C20orf27 not only correlates with increased malignancy but may also serve as a potential prognostic biomarker in HCC.

C20orf27 promotes the migration ability of HCC in vitroWe conducted wound healing and migration assays to assess whether C20orf27 affects HCC cell migration. The wound healing assay indicated that knockdown of C20orf27 significantly inhibited the migration capacity of HCC cells (Fig. 3A, B). Similarly, the migration assay showed that silencing C20orf27 led to a notable reduction in cell migration (Fig. 3C, D). Additionally, KEGG pathway enrichment analysis from our bioinformatics data showed a strong association between hepatoma cells and cell cycle-related pathways. To further investigate this relationship, we used flow cytometry to analyze the cell cycle of HCC cells. By analyzing the cell cycle results, knockdown of C20orf27 altered the cell cycle, which was reflected in the S phase and G2/M phase, with a slight decrease in S phase and an increase in G2/M phase (Fig. 3E). Since the S phase is responsible for the precise replication of DNA. After the completion of the S phase, it enters the G2/M phase and begins to undergo cell division. It is speculated that C20orf27 may affect the proliferation of hepatocellular carcinoma cells by affecting the related cycle regulatory proteins acting in the S phase or G2/M phase. To further investigate how C20orf27 influences cell cycle progression and regulates liver cancer cell proliferation, we performed a Western blot analysis, which revealed decreased protein expression levels of MDM2, PCNA, Cyclin E1, CDK2, and P-Rb in stable C20orf27 knockdown cell lines (Fig. 3F). These findings suggest that C20orf27 plays a critical role in cell cycle regulation, likely affecting cyclin-related proteins and contributing to abnormal cell cycle progression and enhanced proliferation in HCC cells.

A high expression level of C20orf27 is an independent risk factor for HCC patientsTo determine the relationship between C20orf27 expression and clinicopathological parameters, we collected 144 liver cancer tissues, and the correlation between C20orf27 expression and clinical characteristics in HCC is summarized in Fig. 4A. To further investigate how C20orf27 influences cell cycle progression and regulates liver cancer cell proliferation, we performed a Western blot analysis, which revealed decreased protein expression levels of MDM2, PCNA, Cyclin E1, CDK2, and P-Rb in stable C20orf27 knockdown cell lines (Fig. 3F). DiscussionOur study reveals that C20orf27 is significantly upregulated in hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) and acts as a promoter of tumor progression. Functionally C20orf27 enhances HCC cell proliferation and migration in vitro. It is also positively associated with increased tumor number and poorer overall and disease-free survival. These findings suggest that C20orf27 plays a critical oncogenic role in HCC.Previous studies have primarily linked C20orf27 to colorectal cancer, where it activates the TGFβR-TAK1-NFĸB pathway, leading to enhanced cell cycle progression and suppressed apoptosis [9]. Although these mechanisms have not been fully investigated in HCC, our data extend the oncogenic potential of C20orf27 to liver cancer, suggesting that it may function through both shared and tissue-specific downstream pathways.Notably, RNA sequencing and bioinformatics analyses identified NT5E and ACE as potential downstream targets of C20orf27. NT5E, an ecto-5′-nucleotidase, is known to facilitate immune escape by converting extracellular AMP into immunosuppressive adenosine. Its overexpression has been reported in various malignancies, including stomach, breast, and liver cancers, where it promotes angiogenesis and metastasis [12, 15–17]. Additionally, NT5E has emerged as a novel prognostic biomarker on cancer-associated fibroblasts, linked to distinctive features of the tumor microenvironment [18]. Our study reveals that C20orf27 is significantly upregulated in hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) and acts as a promoter of tumor progression. Functionally C20orf27 enhances HCC cell proliferation and migration in vitro. It is also positively associated with increased tumor number and poorer overall and disease-free survival. These findings suggest that C20orf27 plays a critical oncogenic role in HCC.

Previous studies have primarily linked C20orf27 to colorectal cancer, where it activates the TGFβR-TAK1-NFĸB pathway, leading to enhanced cell cycle progression and suppressed apoptosis [9]. Although these mechanisms have not been fully investigated in HCC, our data extend the oncogenic potential of C20orf27 to liver cancer, suggesting that it may function through both shared and tissue-specific downstream pathways.
