The release of exosomes involves the fusion of multivesicular bodies (MVBs) with
the plasma membrane (PM), a process driven by the soluble
N-ethylmaleimide-sensitive factor attachment protein receptor (SNARE) complex.
However, the specific SNARE complex mediating MVB-PM fusion and its regulatory
mechanism remain unclear. Here, we show that Coronin-1a (Coro1a) increases
extracellular vesicle (EV) biogenesis by promoting the assembly of the
STX-12-SNAP-23-VAMP-7 SNARE complex independent of neddylation. Mechanistically,
Coro1a activates pyruvate kinase M2 (PKM2) to enhance SNAP-23 phosphorylation,
thereby driving the recruitment of STX-12 and VAMP-7. Consequently,
Coro1a-induced EV biogenesis is abolished by PKM2 inhibition or SNAP-23
silencing. Furthermore, Coro1a levels are elevated in lung tumor tissues and
correlate with poor patient survival, suggesting that Coro1a-mediated
enhancement of EV production contributes to tumor progression. Our data
demonstrate that Coro1a facilitates EV biogenesis by promoting SNARE complex
assembly during MVB and PM fusion.

Introduction

Extracellular vesicles (EVs) encapsulate a diverse array of proteins, nucleic acids, lipids, and metabolites from their parent cells. This cargo enables EVs to act as crucial mediators of intercellular communication1. Their functional roles are complex and context-dependent. Under physiological conditions, EVs perform essential functions; for instance, placental EVs mediate fetal immune escape, while intestinal EVs help maintain intestinal immune homeostasis2,3. Conversely, EVs are implicated in the pathogenesis of various diseases, including neurodegenerative disorders4, cardiovascular diseases5, and metabolic diseases6. In the context of cancer, EVs have been demonstrated to exert pivotal effects on tumor development, primarily by promoting an immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment. However, it is critical to note that certain EV subpopulations can, under specific conditions, enhance antigen presentation or activate cytotoxic lymphocytes, highlighting the functional heterogeneity of EVs7,8. Tumor cell-derived EVs (TEVs) can profoundly influence immune effector cell activation9. A key mechanism is through TEV-associated PD-L1, which has been reported to induce systemic immunosuppression10. Furthermore, our previous work suggests that TEV PD-L1 contributes to resistance against anti-PD-L1 therapy by acting as a molecular decoy for the therapeutic antibody11. Therefore, targeting the production of specific immunosuppressive TEVs represents a promising strategy to enhance antitumor immunity. Achieving this, however, requires a deeper understanding of the molecular mechanisms that govern EV biogenesis.

EVs are broadly classified into two main subtypes based on their biogenesis pathways: ectosomes and exosomes. Ectosomes are formed through the direct outward budding and fission of the plasma membrane (PM). This process involves the recruitment of Tsg101 to the PM by arrestin domain-containing protein 1, facilitating VPS4-dependent ectosome generation12. In contrast, exosomes are generated through an endosome-dependent mechanism. The initial step involves PM invagination to generate early endosomes. These structures subsequently mature into multivesicular bodies (MVBs) through inward budding of the endosomal membrane, resulting in the accumulation of intraluminal vesicles (ILVs) within the MVB lumen¹. Mature MVBs face two principal destinations. One pathway leads to lysosomal degradation, a process predominantly mediated by the small GTPase Rab7. Previous findings have shown that neddylated Coronin-1a (Coro1a) enhances Rab7 recruitment to MVBs, thereby promoting lysosomal sorting and suppressing EV production13. The alternative fate of MVBs involves their trafficking to and fusion with the PM, resulting in the extracellular release of ILVs as exosomes. This trafficking step is primarily regulated by Rab2714. The fusion event, the final step in exosome biogenesis mainly executed by soluble N-ethylmaleimide-sensitive fusion attachment protein (SNAP) receptor (SNARE) complexes.

SNARE complexes serve as the fundamental molecular machinery driving membrane fusion. Typically composed of one vesicle-associated SNARE (v-SNARE) and three target-membrane-associated SNARE (t-SNARE) proteins, they assemble through interactions between the N-terminal regions of their SNARE motifs. This process nucleates the formation of a four-helix bundle trans-complex, which provides the energy required for vesicle fusion15. Indeed, specific SNARE components-including the v-SNARE VAMP-3, the t-SNARE syntaxin-3 (STX-3), and SNAP-23-have been implicated in the regulation of EV biogenesis16–18. However, fundamental questions remain unresolved, such as which specific SNARE complex orchestrates the fusion of MVBs with the PM during EV release, and what precise regulatory mechanisms govern its assembly? Clarifying these mechanisms is critical for understanding EV-mediated intercellular communication.

Coro1a, a member of the coronin family known for its association with F-actin19, exhibits dual regulatory roles in EV biogenesis. As previously established, neddylated Coro1a facilitates MVB degradation, thereby inhibiting EV release. In this study, Coro1a was also identified to modulate EV biogenesis through a neddylation-independent mechanism13. However, the precise molecular pathways through which Coro1a regulates EV production independently of neddylation remain unclear. To ensure methodological precision, we note that while this study primarily investigates endosomal pathway-dependent EV biogenesis (commonly referred to as exosomes), our EV isolations likely contained a mixture of vesicles, including small ectosomes. Consequently, we consistently employ the term “EVs” throughout this work to accurately reflect this heterogeneity and avoid potential misinterpretation.

Here, we confirmed that Coro1a regulates EV biogenesis through a neddylation-independent pathway. This regulatory role was demonstrated by the observation that Coro1a overexpression enhanced EV release, while its silencing suppressed it, independent of neddylation status. Mechanistically, Coro1a activates pyruvate kinase M2 (PKM2), inducing its phosphorylation. This activation enhances the association between PKM2 and SNAP-23 and consequently increases SNAP-23 phosphorylation. The phosphorylation of SNAP-23, in turn, promotes its recruitment of STX-12 and VAMP-7. This cascade facilitates the assembly of the STX-12-SNAP-23-VAMP-7 SNARE complex, which in turn drives the fusion of MVBs with the PM, ultimately augmenting EV production. Collectively, our findings elucidate a previously unrecognized mechanism wherein Coro1a orchestrates the formation of the SNARE complex to promote MVB-PM fusion and EV biogenesis.

DiscussionWhile mounting evidence underscores the critical involvement of EVs in disease progression, their functional outcomes are highly context-dependent, varying with cellular origin, cargo composition, and the surrounding microenvironment. Consequently, although certain pathogenic EV subpopulations present attractive therapeutic targets, any intervention strategy must acknowledge the potential duality of EV functions. Although several approaches have been developed to reduce EV production, they remain unsuitable for clinical translation33. A deeper understanding of EV biogenesis mechanisms is therefore imperative for the identification of specific and druggable targets suitable for clinical translation. Previous work demonstrated that neddylated Coro1a localizes to MVB membranes, where it directly binds and recruits Rab7, thereby directing MVBs toward lysosomal degradation and suppressing EV release. Nevertheless, we observed that Coro1a knockout still suppressed EV secretion even in the presence of MLN4924 neddylation inhibitor, suggesting an alternative, neddylation-independent regulatory role for Coro1a in EV biogenesis13. In this study, we uncover that non-neddylated Coro1a promotes EV secretion by facilitating MVB-PM fusion. Mechanistically, Coro1a activates PKM2, promotes PKM2-mediated phosphorylation of SNAP-23, and drives the assembly of the STX12-SNAP23-VAMP7 SNARE complex, which is essential for the tethering and fusion of MVBs with the PM to enable EV release. To directly validate the necessity of STX-12 in mediating MVB-PM fusion, we performed functional knockdown and rescue experiments for STX-12. The results show that specific siRNA-mediated knockdown of STX-12 significantly suppressed the Coro1a overexpression-induced increase in EV secretion. This inhibitory effect was fully rescued by STX-12 overexpression. This “knockdown-rescue” paradigm establishes the ‌causal role‌ of STX-12 downstream of the Coro1a-PKM2 signaling axis in driving SNARE complex assembly and MVB-PM fusion. In addition, PKM2 inhibitor Shikonin or PKM2 silencing treatment blocked Coro1a-induced SNAP-23 phosphorylation and STX-12-SNAP-23-VAMP-7 complex formation. This phenotype is entirely consistent with that observed in the direct STX-12 knockdown experiments. Together with the in vitro liposome fusion data, these results ‌collectively construct a comprehensive evidence chain that Coro1a, by activating PKM2, promotes SNAP-23 phosphorylation, enhances its interaction with STX-12 and VAMP-7, thereby specifically assembling the fusion-competent STX-12-SNAP-23-VAMP-7 SNARE complex that ultimately executes the MVB-PM fusion event.
