# Structure of mycobacterial β-oxidation trifunctional enzyme reveals its altered assembly and putative substrate channeling pathway.
**Authors:** Venkatesan R, Wierenga RK
**Journal:** ACS Chem Biol (2013)
**DOI:** [10.1021/cb400007k](https://doi.org/10.1021/cb400007k)

## Abstract

1. ACS Chem Biol. 2013 May 17;8(5):1063-73. doi: 10.1021/cb400007k. Epub 2013 Mar
 27.

Structure of mycobacterial β-oxidation trifunctional enzyme reveals its altered
assembly and putative substrate channeling pathway.

Venkatesan R(1), Wierenga RK.

Author information:
(1)Department of Biochemistry and Biocenter Oulu, University of Oulu, Oulu

The incidence of tuberculosis is increasing due to the appearance of new
drug-resistant variants. A thorough understanding of the disease organism is
essential in order to create more effective drugs. In an attempt to understand
better the poorly studied lipid metabolism of Mycobacterium tuberculosis (Mtb),
we identified and characterized its fatty acid β-oxidation complex
(trifunctional enzyme (TFE)). TFE is an α(2)β(2) complex consisting of two types
of polypeptides catalyzing three of the four reactions of the β-oxidation of
fatty acids. The kinetic constants (k(cat) and K(m)) show that the complexed α
chain is more active than the individual α chain. Crystal structures of Mtb TFE
(mtTFE) reveal that the quaternary assembly is strikingly different from the
already known Pseudomonas fragi TFE (pfTFE) assembly due to the presence of a
helical insertion (LA5) in the mtTFE-β subunit. This helical insertion prevents
the pfTFE mode of assembly, as it would clash with helix H9A of the TFE-α chain.
The mtTFE assembly appears to be more rigid and results in a different substrate
channeling path between the α and the β subunits. Structural comparisons suggest
that the mtTFE active sites can accommodate bulkier fatty acyl chains than in
pfTFE. Although another thiolase (FadA2), more closely related to human
TFE-β/thiolase, is present in the Mtb genome, it does not form a complex with
mtTFE-α. Extensive phylogenetic analyses show that there are at least four TFE
subfamilies. Our studies highlight the molecular properties of mtTFE,
significantly extending the structural knowledge on this type of very
interesting multifunctional enzymes.
