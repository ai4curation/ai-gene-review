# Drosophila mutants in the 55 kDa regulatory subunit of protein phosphatase 2A show strongly reduced ability to dephosphorylate substrates of p34cdc2.
**Authors:** Mayer-Jaekel RE, Ohkura H, Ferrigno P, Andjelkovic N, Shiomi K, Uemura T, Glover DM, Hemmings BA
**Journal:** J Cell Sci (1994)
**DOI:** [10.1242/jcs.107.9.2609](https://doi.org/10.1242/jcs.107.9.2609)

## Abstract

1. J Cell Sci. 1994 Sep;107 ( Pt 9):2609-16. doi: 10.1242/jcs.107.9.2609.

Drosophila mutants in the 55 kDa regulatory subunit of protein phosphatase 2A
show strongly reduced ability to dephosphorylate substrates of p34cdc2.

Mayer-Jaekel RE(1), Ohkura H, Ferrigno P, Andjelkovic N, Shiomi K, Uemura T,
Glover DM, Hemmings BA.

Author information:
(1)Friedrich Miescher-Institut, Basel, Switzerland.

The 55 kDa regulatory subunit of Drosophila protein phosphatase 2A is located in
the cytoplasm at all cell cycle stages, by the criterion of immunofluorescence.
We are unable to detect significant change in protein phosphatase activity
during the nuclear division cycle of syncytial embryos. However, cell cycle
function of the enzyme is suggested by the mitotic defects exhibited by two
Drosophila mutants, aar1 and twinsP, defective in the gene encoding the 55 kDa
subunit. The reduced levels of the 55 kDa subunit correlate with the loss of
protein phosphatase 2A-like, okadaic acid-sensitive phosphatase activity of
brain extracts against caldesmon and histone H1 phosphorylated by p34cdc2/cyclin
B kinase, but not against phosphorylase a. Thus the mitotic defects of aar1 and
twinsP are likely to result from the lack of dephosphorylation of specific
substrates by protein phosphatase 2A.
