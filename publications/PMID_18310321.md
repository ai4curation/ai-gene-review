# In vitro self-assembly of tailorable nanotubes from a simple protein building block.
**Authors:** Ballister ER, Lai AH, Zuckermann RN, Cheng Y, Mougous JD
**Journal:** Proc Natl Acad Sci U S A (2008)
**DOI:** [10.1073/pnas.0712247105](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.0712247105)
**PMC:** [PMC2268831](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC2268831/)

## Abstract

1. Proc Natl Acad Sci U S A. 2008 Mar 11;105(10):3733-8. doi:
10.1073/pnas.0712247105. Epub 2008 Feb 29.

In vitro self-assembly of tailorable nanotubes from a simple protein building
block.

Ballister ER(1), Lai AH, Zuckermann RN, Cheng Y, Mougous JD.

Author information:
(1)Molecular Foundry, Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory, Berkeley, CA 94720,
USA.

We demonstrate a method for generating discretely structured protein nanotubes
from the simple ring-shaped building block, homohexameric Hcp1 from Pseudomonas
aeruginosa. Our design exploited the observation that the crystal lattice of
Hcp1 contains rings stacked in a repeating head-to-tail pattern. High-resolution
detail of the ring-ring interface allowed the selection of sites for specific
cysteine mutations capable of engaging in disulfide bond formation across rings,
thereby generating stable Hcp1 nanotubes. Protein nanotubes containing up to 25
subunits ( approximately 100 nm in length) were self-assembled under simple
conditions. Furthermore, we demonstrate that the tube ends and interior can be
independently and specifically functionalized to generate nanocapsules.
