# Arabidopsis thaliana contains a single gene encoding squalene synthase.
**Authors:** Busquets A, Keim V, Closa M, del Arco A, Boronat A, Arró M, Ferrer A
**Journal:** Plant Mol Biol (2008)
**DOI:** [10.1007/s11103-008-9299-3](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11103-008-9299-3)

## Abstract

Arabidopsis thaliana contains a single gene encoding squalene synthase.

Busquets A(1), Keim V, Closa M, del Arco A, Boronat A, Arró M, Ferrer A.

Author information:
(1)Departament de Bioquímica i Biologia Molecular, Facultat de Farmàcia,

Squalene synthase (SQS) catalyzes the condensation of two molecules of farnesyl
diphosphate (FPP) to produce squalene (SQ), the first committed precursor for
sterol, brassinosteroid, and triterpene biosynthesis. Arabidopsis thaliana
contains two SQS-annotated genomic sequences, At4g34640 (SQS1) and At4g34650
(SQS2), organized in a tandem array. Here we report that the SQS1 gene is widely
expressed in all tissues throughout plant development, whereas SQS2 is primarily
expressed in the vascular tissue of leaf and cotyledon petioles, and the
hypocotyl of seedlings. Neither the complete A. thaliana SQS2 protein nor the
chimeric SQS resulting from the replacement of the 69 C-terminal residues of
SQS2 by the 111 C-terminal residues of the Schizosaccharomyces pombe SQS were
able to confer ergosterol prototrophy to a Saccharomyces cerevisiae erg9 mutant
strain lacking SQS activity. A soluble form of SQS2 expressed in Escherichia
coli and purified was unable to synthesize SQ from FPP in the presence of NADPH
and either Mg2+ or Mn2+. These results demonstrated that SQS2 has no SQS
activity, so that SQS1 is the only functional SQS in A. thaliana. Mutational
studies revealed that the lack of SQS activity of SQS2 cannot be exclusively
attributed to the presence of an unusual Ser replacing the highly conserved Phe
at position 287. Expression of green fluorescent protein (GFP)-tagged versions
of SQS1 in onion epidermal cells demonstrated that SQS1 is targeted to the
endoplasmic reticulum (ER) membrane and that this location is exclusively
dependent on the presence of the SQS1 C-terminal hydrophobic trans-membrane
domain.
