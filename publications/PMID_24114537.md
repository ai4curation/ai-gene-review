# Orphan receptor GPR179 forms macromolecular complexes with components of metabotropic signaling cascade in retina ON-bipolar neurons.
**Authors:** Orlandi C, Cao Y, Martemyanov KA
**Journal:** Invest Ophthalmol Vis Sci (2013)
**DOI:** [10.1167/iovs.13-12907](https://doi.org/10.1167/iovs.13-12907)
**PMC:** [PMC3813323](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3813323/)

## Abstract

1. Invest Ophthalmol Vis Sci. 2013 Oct 29;54(10):7153-61. doi:
10.1167/iovs.13-12907.

Orphan receptor GPR179 forms macromolecular complexes with components of
metabotropic signaling cascade in retina ON-bipolar neurons.

Orlandi C(1), Cao Y, Martemyanov KA.

Author information:
(1)Department of Neuroscience, The Scripps Research Institute, Jupiter, Florida.

PURPOSE: In the mammalian retina, synaptic transmission between light-excited
rod photoreceptors and downstream ON-bipolar neurons is indispensable for dim
vision, and disruption of this process leads to congenital stationary night
blindness in human patients. The ON-bipolar neurons use the metabotropic
signaling cascade, initiated by the mGluR6 receptor, to generate depolarizing
responses to light-induced changes in neurotransmitter glutamate release from
the photoreceptor axonal terminals. Evidence for the identity of the components
involved in transducing these signals is growing rapidly. Recently, the orphan
receptor, GPR179, a member of the G protein-coupled receptor (GPCR) superfamily,
has been shown to be indispensable for the synaptic responses of ON-bipolar
cells. In our study, we investigated the interaction of GPR179 with principle
components of the signal transduction cascade.
METHODS: We used immunoprecipitation and proximity ligation assays in
transfected cells and native retinas to characterize the protein-protein
interactions involving GPR179. The influence of cascade components on GPR179
localization was examined through immunohistochemical staining of the retinas
from genetic mouse models.
RESULTS: We demonstrated that, in mouse retinas, GPR179 forms physical complexes
with the main components of the metabotropic cascade, recruiting mGluR6, TRPM1,
and the RGS proteins. Elimination of mGluR6 or RGS proteins, but not TRPM1,
detrimentally affects postsynaptic targeting or GPR179 expression.
CONCLUSIONS: These observations suggest that the mGluR6 signaling cascade is
scaffolded as a macromolecular complex in which the interactions between the
components ensure the optimal spatiotemporal characteristics of signal
transduction.
