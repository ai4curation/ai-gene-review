# The dolichol pathway of N-linked glycosylation.
**Authors:** Burda P, Aebi M
**Journal:** Biochim Biophys Acta (1999)
**DOI:** [10.1016/s0304-4165(98)00127-5](https://doi.org/10.1016/s0304-4165(98)00127-5)

## Abstract

1. Biochim Biophys Acta. 1999 Jan 6;1426(2):239-57. doi:
10.1016/s0304-4165(98)00127-5.

The dolichol pathway of N-linked glycosylation.

Burda P(1), Aebi M.

Author information:
(1)Mikrobiologisches Institut, ETH Zürich, Schmelzbergstr. 7, CH-092 Zürich,
Switzerland.

The oligosaccharide substrate for the N-linked protein glycosylation is
assembled at the membrane of the endoplasmic reticulum. Dolichyl pyrophosphate
serves as a carrier in this biosynthetic pathway. In this review, we discuss the
function of the lipid carrier dolichol in oligosaccharide assembly and give an
overview of the biosynthesis of the different sugar donors required for the
building of the oligosaccharide. Yeast genetic techniques have made it possible
to identify many different loci encoding specific glycosyltransferases required
for the precise and ordered assembly of the dolichyl pyrophosphate-linked
oligosaccharide. Based on the knowledge obtained from studying this pathway in
yeast, we compare it to the process of N-linked protein glycosylation in
archaea. We suggest that N-linked glycosylation in eukaryotes and in archaea
share a common evolutionary origin.
