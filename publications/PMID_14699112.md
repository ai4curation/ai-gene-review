# ZmYS1 functions as a proton-coupled symporter for phytosiderophore- and nicotianamine-chelated metals.
**Authors:** Schaaf G, Ludewig U, Erenoglu BE, Mori S, Kitahara T, von Wirén N
**Journal:** J Biol Chem (2004)
**DOI:** [10.1074/jbc.M311799200](https://doi.org/10.1074/jbc.M311799200)

## Abstract

1. J Biol Chem. 2004 Mar 5;279(10):9091-6. doi: 10.1074/jbc.M311799200. Epub 2003
 Dec 29.

ZmYS1 functions as a proton-coupled symporter for phytosiderophore- and
nicotianamine-chelated metals.

Schaaf G(1), Ludewig U, Erenoglu BE, Mori S, Kitahara T, von Wirén N.

Author information:
(1)Institut für Pflanzenernährung, Universität Hohenheim, D-70593 Stuttgart,
Germany.

Among higher plants graminaceous species have the unique ability to efficiently
acquire iron from alkaline soils with low iron solubility by secreting
phytosiderophores, which are hexadentate metal chelators with high affinity for
Fe(III). Iron(III)-phytosiderophores are subsequently taken up by roots via YS1
transporters, that belong to the OPT oligopeptide transporter family. Despite
its physiological importance at alkaline pH, uptake of Fe-phytosiderophores into
roots of wild-type maize plants was greater at acidic pH and sensitive to the
proton uncoupler CCCP. To access the mechanism of Fe-phytosiderophore
acquisition, ZmYS1 was expressed in an iron uptake-defective yeast mutant and in
Xenopus oocytes, where ZmYS1-dependent Fe-phytosiderophore transport was
stimulated at acidic pH and sensitive to CCCP. Electrophysiological analysis in
oocytes demonstrated that Fephytosiderophore transport depends on proton
cotransport and on the membrane potential, which allows ZmYS1-mediated transport
even at alkaline pH. We further investigated substrate specificity and observed
that ZmYS1 complemented the growth defect of the zinc uptake-defective yeast
mutant zap1 and transported various phytosiderophore-bound metals into oocytes,
including zinc, copper, nickel, and, at a lower rate, also manganese and
cadmium. Unexpectedly, ZmYS1 also transported Ni(II), Fe(II), and Fe(III)
complexes with nicotianamine, a structural analog of phytosiderophores, which
has been shown to act as an intracellular metal chelator in all higher plants.
Our results show that ZmYS1 encodes a proton-coupled broad-range
metal-phytosiderophore transporter that additionally transports Fe- and
Ni-nicotianamine. These biochemical properties indicate a novel role of YS1
transporters for heavy metal homeostasis in plants.
