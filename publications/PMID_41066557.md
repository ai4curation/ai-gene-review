Comment in
    Science. 2025 Oct 9;390(6769):126-127. doi: 10.1126/science.aeb6125.

Efficient DNA repair might make possible the longevity of naked mole-rats.
However, whether they have distinctive mechanisms to optimize functions of DNA
repair suppressors is unclear. We find that naked mole-rat cyclic guanosine
monophosphate-adenosine monophosphate synthase (cGAS) lacks the suppressive
function of human or mouse homologs in homologous recombination repair through
the alteration of four amino acids during evolution. The changes enable cGAS to
retain chromatin longer upon DNA damage by weakening TRIM41-mediated
ubiquitination and interaction with the segregase P97. Prolonged chromatin
binding of cGAS enhanced the interaction between repair factors FANCI and RAD50
to facilitate RAD50 recruitment to damage sites, thereby potentiating homologous
recombination repair. Moreover, the four amino acids mediate the function of
cGAS in antagonizing cellular and tissue aging and extending life span.
Manipulating cGAS might therefore constitute a mechanism for life-span
extension.
