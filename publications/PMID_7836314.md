# Saccharomyces cerevisiae has a single glutamate synthase gene coding for a plant-like high-molecular-weight polypeptide.
**Authors:** Cogoni C, Valenzuela L, González-Halphen D, Olivera H, Macino G, Ballario P, González A
**Journal:** J Bacteriol (1995)
**DOI:** [10.1128/jb.177.3.792-798.1995](https://doi.org/10.1128/jb.177.3.792-798.1995)
**PMC:** [PMC176658](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC176658/)

## Abstract

1. J Bacteriol. 1995 Feb;177(3):792-8. doi: 10.1128/jb.177.3.792-798.1995.

Saccharomyces cerevisiae has a single glutamate synthase gene coding for a
plant-like high-molecular-weight polypeptide.

Cogoni C(1), Valenzuela L, González-Halphen D, Olivera H, Macino G, Ballario P,
González A.

Author information:
(1)Dipartimento di Biopatologia Umana Policlinico Umberto I. Universitá di Roma
La Sapienza, Italy.

Purification of the glutamate synthase (GOGAT) enzyme from Saccharomyces
cerevisiae showed that it is an oligomeric enzyme composed of three identical
199-kDa subunits. The GOGAT structural gene was isolated by screening a yeast
genomic library with a yeast PCR probe. This probe was obtained by amplification
with degenerate oligonucleotides designed from conserved regions of known GOGAT
genes. The derived amino-terminal sequence of the GOGAT gene was confirmed by
direct amino-terminal sequence analysis of the purified protein of 199 kDa.
Northern (RNA) analysis allowed the identification of an mRNA of about 7 or 8
kb. An internal fragment of the GOGAT gene was used to obtain null GOGAT mutants
completely devoid of GOGAT activity. The results show that S. cerevisiae has a
single NADH-GOGAT enzyme, consisting of three 199-kDa monomers, that differs
from the one found in prokaryotic microorganisms but is similar to those found
in other eukaryotic organisms such as alfalfa.
