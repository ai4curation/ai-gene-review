# Use of haloacetate dehalogenase genes as selection markers for Escherichia coli and Pseudomonas vectors.
**Authors:** Kawasaki H, Kuriyama H, Tonomura K
**Journal:** Biodegradation (1995)
**DOI:** [10.1007/BF00700459](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF00700459)

## Abstract

1. Biodegradation. 1995 Sep;6(3):213-6. doi: 10.1007/BF00700459.

Use of haloacetate dehalogenase genes as selection markers for Escherichia coli
and Pseudomonas vectors.

Kawasaki H(1), Kuriyama H, Tonomura K.

Author information:
(1)Department of Agricultural Chemistry, University of Osaka Prefecture, Japan.

The haloacetate dehalogenase gene, dehH2, cloned from Moraxella sp. strain B
could be used a selection marker gene for vectors in Escherichia coli and
Pseudomonas putida. Haloacetates, especially iodoacetate, inhibit the growth of
some microorganisms. The dehH2 gene introduced into the cells conferred
iodoacetate resistance on them. Therefore, E. coli and P. putida transformed
with vectors marked with dehH2 could be easily selected on plates containing
iodoacetate.
