# Reconstitution of an efficient thymidine salvage pathway in Saccharomyces cerevisiae.
**Authors:** Vernis L, Piskur J, Diffley JF

## Abstract

1. Nucleic Acids Res. 2003 Oct 1;31(19):e120. doi: 10.1093/nar/gng121.

Reconstitution of an efficient thymidine salvage pathway in Saccharomyces
cerevisiae.

Vernis L(1), Piskur J, Diffley JF.

Author information:
(1)Cancer Research UK, London Research Institute, Clare Hall Laboratories, South

The budding yeast Saccharomyces cerevisiae is unable to incorporate exogenous
nucleosides into DNA. We have made a number of improvements to existing
strategies to reconstitute an efficient thymidine salvage pathway in yeast. We
have constructed strains that express both a nucleoside kinase as well as an
equilibrative nucleoside transporter. By also deleting the gene encoding
thymidylate synthase (CDC21) we have constructed strains that are entirely
dependent upon exogenous thymidine for viability and that can grow with normal
kinetics at low thymidine concentrations. Using this novel approach, we show
that depletion of a single deoxyribonucleoside causes reversible arrest of cells
in S phase with concomitant phosphorylation and activation of the S phase
checkpoint kinase, Rad53. We show that this strain also efficiently incorporates
the thymidine analogue, BrdU, into DNA and can be used for pulse-chase
labelling.
