# Phospholipase D and the SNARE Sso1p are necessary for vesicle fusion during sporulation in yeast.
**Authors:** Nakanishi H, Morishita M, Schwartz CL, Coluccio A, Engebrecht J, Neiman AM
**Journal:** J Cell Sci (2006)
**DOI:** [10.1242/jcs.02841](https://doi.org/10.1242/jcs.02841)

## Abstract

1. J Cell Sci. 2006 Apr 1;119(Pt 7):1406-15. doi: 10.1242/jcs.02841.

Phospholipase D and the SNARE Sso1p are necessary for vesicle fusion during
sporulation in yeast.

Nakanishi H(1), Morishita M, Schwartz CL, Coluccio A, Engebrecht J, Neiman AM.

Author information:
(1)Department of Biochemistry and Cell Biology, SUNY Stony Brook, Stony Brook,
NY 4-5215, USA.

Spore formation in Saccharomyces cerevisiae requires the de novo formation of
prospore membranes. The coalescence of secretory vesicles into a membrane sheet
occurs on the cytoplasmic surface of the spindle pole body. Spo14p, the major
yeast phospholipase D, is necessary for prospore membrane formation; however,
the specific function of Spo14p in this process has not been elucidated. We
report that loss of Spo14p blocks vesicle fusion, leading to the accumulation of
prospore membrane precursor vesicles docked on the spindle pole body. A similar
phenotype was seen when the t-SNARE Sso1p, or the partially redundant t-SNAREs
Sec9p and Spo20p were mutated. Although phosphatidic acid, the product of
phospholipase D action, was necessary to recruit Spo20p to the precursor
vesicles, independent targeting of Spo20p to the membrane was not sufficient to
promote fusion in the absence of SPO14. These results demonstrate a role for
phospholipase D in vesicle fusion and suggest that phospholipase D-generated
phosphatidic acid plays multiple roles in the fusion process.
