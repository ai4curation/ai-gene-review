# The DEAD box protein Ddx42p modulates the function of ASPP2, a stimulator of apoptosis.
**Authors:** Uhlmann-Schiffler H, Kiermayer S, Stahl H
**Journal:** Oncogene (2009)
**DOI:** [10.1038/onc.2009.75](https://doi.org/10.1038/onc.2009.75)

## Abstract

1. Oncogene. 2009 May 21;28(20):2065-73. doi: 10.1038/onc.2009.75. Epub 2009 Apr
20.

The DEAD box protein Ddx42p modulates the function of ASPP2, a stimulator of
apoptosis.

Uhlmann-Schiffler H(1), Kiermayer S, Stahl H.

Author information:
(1)Department of Medical Biochemistry and Molecular Biology, The Saarland
University, Homburg, Germany. heike.uhlmann.schiffler@uniklinikum-saarland.de

Ddx42p is a recently characterized mammalian DEAD box protein with unknown
cellular function. We found that in human cells Ddx42p physically interacts with
ASPP2, a major apoptosis inducer known to enhance p53 transactivation of
proapoptotic genes. The proteins interact via a domain within the
carboxy-terminal part of Ddx42p and a mid-amino-terminal sequence as well as the
ankyrin-SH3 region of ASPP2. Overexpression of Ddx42p interferes with apoptosis
induction by ASPP2, whereas Ddx42p knockdown reduces the survival rate of
cultured human cells. In addition, ASPP2 is found in cytoplasm and nucleus at
low Ddx42p level, and predominantly in cytoplasm at high concentration of
Ddx42p, respectively. Our results show that Ddx42p is capable of modulating
ASPP2 function.
