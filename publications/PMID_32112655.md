# TMEM16E/ANO5 mutations related to bone dysplasia or muscular dystrophy cause opposite effects on lipid scrambling.
**Authors:** Di Zanni E, Gradogna A, Picco C, Scholz-Starke J, Boccaccio A
**Journal:** Hum Mutat (2020)
**DOI:** [10.1002/humu.24006](https://doi.org/10.1002/humu.24006)

## Abstract

1. Hum Mutat. 2020 Jun;41(6):1157-1170. doi: 10.1002/humu.24006. Epub 2020 Mar
18.

TMEM16E/ANO5 mutations related to bone dysplasia or muscular dystrophy cause
opposite effects on lipid scrambling.

Di Zanni E(1), Gradogna A(1), Picco C(1), Scholz-Starke J(1), Boccaccio A(1).

Author information:
(1)Institute of Biophysics, Consiglio Nazionale delle Ricerche, Genova, Italy.

Mutations in the human TMEM16E/ANO5 gene are causative for gnathodiaphyseal
dysplasia (GDD), a rare bone malformation and fragility disorder, and for two
types of muscular dystrophy (MD). Previous studies have demonstrated that
TMEM16E/ANO5 is a Ca2+ -activated phospholipid scramblase and that the mutation
c.1538C>T (p.Thr513Ile) causing GDD leads to a gain-of-function phenotype. Here,
using established HEK293-based functional assays, we investigated the effects of
MD-related and further GDD-related amino acid exchanges on TMEM16E/ANO5 function
in the same expression system. These experiments also revealed that the gradual
changes in HEK293 cell morphology observed upon expression of TMEM16E/ANO5GDD
mutants are a consequence of aberrant protein activity. Our results collectively
demonstrate that, on the level of protein function, MD mutations are associated
to loss-of-function and GDD mutations to gain-of-function phenotypes, confirming
conjectures made on the basis of inheritance modes.
