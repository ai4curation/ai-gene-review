# ALEX1, a novel human armadillo repeat protein that is expressed differentially in normal tissues and carcinomas.
**Authors:** Kurochkin IV, Yonemitsu N, Funahashi SI, Nomura H
**Journal:** Biochem Biophys Res Commun (2001)
**DOI:** [10.1006/bbrc.2000.4125](https://doi.org/10.1006/bbrc.2000.4125)

## Abstract

1. Biochem Biophys Res Commun. 2001 Jan 12;280(1):340-7. doi:
10.1006/bbrc.2000.4125.

ALEX1, a novel human armadillo repeat protein that is expressed differentially
in normal tissues and carcinomas.

Kurochkin IV(1), Yonemitsu N, Funahashi SI, Nomura H.

Author information:
(1)Chugai Research Institute for Molecular Medicine, 153-2 Nagai, Niihari,
Ibaraki 300-4101, Japan. igork@cimmed.com

Members of the armadillo (arm) repeat family of proteins are implicated in
tumorigenesis, embryonic development, and maintenance of tissue integrity. We
have cloned cDNA for a novel human arm repeat protein, ALEX1, encoding 453 amino
acids. ALEX1 shares significant homology with uncharacterized ORF KIAA0512 and
putative protein product of unknown mRNA (GenBank AF211175), designated here as
ALEX2 and ALEX3, respectively. The genes encoding ALEX1, ALEX2 and ALEX3
co-localize to the same region in Xq21.33-q22.2. ALEX1 and ALEX2 transcripts are
found in all human tissues examined except peripheral blood leukocytes.
Expression of ALEX1 and ALEX2 mRNA is lost or significantly reduced in human
lung, prostate, colon, pancreas, and ovarian carcinomas and also in cell lines
established from different human carcinomas. These genes are, however, normally
expressed in cell lines derived from other types of tumors, e.g., sarcomas,
neuroblastomas, and gliomas. We speculate that ALEX genes may play a role in
suppression of tumors originating from epithelial tissue, i.e., carcinomas.
