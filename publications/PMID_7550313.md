# Mice lacking tissue non-specific alkaline phosphatase die from seizures due to defective metabolism of vitamin B-6.
**Authors:** Waymire KG, Mahuren JD, Jaje JM, Guilarte TR, Coburn SP, MacGregor GR
**Journal:** Nat Genet (1995)
**DOI:** [10.1038/ng0995-45](https://doi.org/10.1038/ng0995-45)

## Abstract

1. Nat Genet. 1995 Sep;11(1):45-51. doi: 10.1038/ng0995-45.

Mice lacking tissue non-specific alkaline phosphatase die from seizures due to
defective metabolism of vitamin B-6.

Waymire KG(1), Mahuren JD, Jaje JM, Guilarte TR, Coburn SP, MacGregor GR.

Author information:
(1)Department of Genetics and Molecular Medicine, Emory University School of
Medicine, Atlanta, Georgia 30322, USA.

In humans, deficiency of the tissue non-specific alkaline phosphatase (TNAP)
gene is associated with defective skeletal mineralization. In contrast, mice
lacking TNAP generated by homologous recombination using embryonic stem (ES)
cells have normal skeletal development. However, at approximately two weeks
after birth, homozygous mutant mice develop seizures which are subsequently
fatal. Defective metabolism of pyridoxal 5'-phosphate (PLP), characterized by
elevated serum PLP levels, results in reduced levels of the inhibitory
neurotransmitter gamma-aminobutyric acid (GABA) in the brain. The mutant seizure
phenotype can be rescued by the administration of pyridoxal and a semi-solid
diet. Rescued animals subsequently develop defective dentition. This study
reveals essential physiological functions of TNAP in the mouse.
