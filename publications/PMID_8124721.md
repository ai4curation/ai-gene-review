# Nef induces CD4 endocytosis: requirement for a critical dileucine motif in the membrane-proximal CD4 cytoplasmic domain.
**Authors:** Aiken C, Konner J, Landau NR, Lenburg ME, Trono D
**Journal:** Cell (1994)
**DOI:** [10.1016/0092-8674(94)90360-3](https://doi.org/10.1016/0092-8674(94)90360-3)

## Abstract

1. Cell. 1994 Mar 11;76(5):853-64. doi: 10.1016/0092-8674(94)90360-3.

Nef induces CD4 endocytosis: requirement for a critical dileucine motif in the
membrane-proximal CD4 cytoplasmic domain.

Aiken C(1), Konner J, Landau NR, Lenburg ME, Trono D.

Author information:
(1)Infectious Disease Laboratory, Salk Institute for Biological Studies, La
Jolla, California 92037-1099.

CD4 is crucial for antigen-driven helper T cell signaling and is used as
receptor by the human immunodeficiency virus (HIV). The HIV early protein Nef
causes a loss of CD4 from cell surfaces through a previously undefined
posttranscriptional mechanism. Here, we demonstrate that Nef acts by inducing
CD4 endocytosis, resulting in its degradation in lysosomes. CD4 down-regulation
is strongly enhanced by the association of Nef with cell membranes through
myristoylation. The study of chimeric molecules reveals that 20
membrane-proximal residues of the CD4 cytoplasmic domain are sufficient to
confer Nef sensitivity. Within this region, a dileucine motif, reminiscent of an
endocytosis and lysosomal targeting signal found in the CD3 gamma and delta
chains, is crucial for CD4 response to Nef.
