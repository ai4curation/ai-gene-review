Palmitoylation of cysteine residues at the C-terminal hypervariable regions in
human HRAS and NRAS, which is necessary for RAS signaling, is catalyzed by the
acyltransferase DHHC9 in complex with its accessory protein GCP16. The molecular
basis for the acyltransferase activity and the regulation of DHHC9 by GCP16 is
not clear. Here we report the cryo-electron microscopy structures of the human
DHHC9-GCP16 complex and its yeast counterpart-the Erf2-Erf4 complex,
demonstrating that GCP16 and Erf4 are not directly involved in the catalytic
process but stabilize the architecture of DHHC9 and Erf2, respectively. We found
that a phospholipid binding to an arginine-rich region of DHHC9 and
palmitoylation on three residues (C24, C25 and C288) were essential for the
catalytic activity of the DHHC9-GCP16 complex. Moreover, we showed that GCP16
also formed complexes with DHHC14 and DHHC18 to catalyze RAS palmitoylation.
These findings provide insights into the regulatory mechanism of RAS
palmitoyltransferases.
