E-cadherin (E-cad) is the main component of epithelial junctions in
multicellular organisms, where it is essential for cell-cell adhesion. The
localisation of E-cad is often strongly polarised in the apico-basal axis.
However, the mechanisms required for its polarised distribution are still
largely unknown. We performed a systematic RNAi screen in vivo to identify genes
required for the strict E-cad apical localisation in C. elegans epithelial
epidermal cells. We found that the loss of clathrin, its adaptor AP-1 and the
AP-1 interactor SOAP-1 induced a basolateral localisation of E-cad without
affecting the apico-basal diffusion barrier. We further found that SOAP-1
controls AP-1 localisation, and that AP-1 is required for clathrin recruitment.
Finally, we also show that AP-1 controls E-cad apical delivery and actin
organisation during embryonic elongation, the final morphogenetic step of
embryogenesis. We therefore propose that a molecular pathway, containing SOAP-1,
AP-1 and clathrin, controls the apical delivery of E-cad and morphogenesis.
