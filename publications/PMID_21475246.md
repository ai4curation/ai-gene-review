Studies using Escherichia coli DNA polymerase (Pol) III as the prototype for
bacterial DNA replication have suggested that--in contrast to eukaryotes--one
replicase performs all of the main functions at the replication fork. However,
recent studies have revealed that replication in other bacteria requires two
forms of Pol III, one of which seems to extend RNA primers by only a few
nucleotides before transferring the product to the other polymerase--an
arrangement analogous to that in eukaryotes. Yet another group of bacteria
encode a second Pol III (ImuC), which apparently replaces a Pol Y-type
polymerase (Pol V) that is required for induced mutagenesis in E. coli. A
complete understanding of complex bacterial replicases will allow the
simultaneous biochemical screening of all their components and, thus, the
identification of new antibacterial compounds.
