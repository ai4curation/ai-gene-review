# Lymphotoxin beta, a novel member of the TNF family that forms a heteromeric complex with lymphotoxin on the cell surface.
**Authors:** Browning JL, Ngam-ek A, Lawton P, DeMarinis J, Tizard R, Chow EP, Hession C, O'Brine-Greco B, Foley SF, Ware CF
**Journal:** Cell (1993)
**DOI:** [10.1016/0092-8674(93)90574-a](https://doi.org/10.1016/0092-8674(93)90574-a)

## Abstract

1. Cell. 1993 Mar 26;72(6):847-56. doi: 10.1016/0092-8674(93)90574-a.

Lymphotoxin beta, a novel member of the TNF family that forms a heteromeric
complex with lymphotoxin on the cell surface.

Browning JL(1), Ngam-ek A, Lawton P, DeMarinis J, Tizard R, Chow EP, Hession C,
O'Brine-Greco B, Foley SF, Ware CF.

Author information:
(1)Biogen Incorporated 14 Cambridge Center, Massachusetts 02142.

The lymphokine tumor necrosis factor (TNF) has a well-defined role as an inducer
of inflammatory responses; however, the function of the structurally related
molecule lymphotoxin (LT alpha) is unknown. LT alpha is present on the surface
of activated T, B, and LAK cells as a complex with a 33 kd glycoprotein, and
cloning of the cDNA encoding the associated protein, called lymphotoxin beta (LT
beta), revealed it to be a type II membrane protein with significant homology to
TNF, LT alpha, and the ligand for the CD40 receptor. The gene for LT beta was
found next to the TNF-LT locus in the major histocompatibility complex (MHC), a
region of the MHC with possible linkage to autoimmune disease. These
observations raise the possibility that a surface LT alpha-LT beta complex may
have a specific role in immune regulation distinct from the functions ascribed
to TNF.
