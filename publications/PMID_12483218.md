# Selective cognitive dysfunction in acetylcholine M1 muscarinic receptor mutant mice.
**Authors:** Anagnostaras SG, Murphy GG, Hamilton SE, Mitchell SL, Rahnama NP, Nathanson NM, Silva AJ
**Journal:** Nat Neurosci (2003)
**DOI:** [10.1038/nn992](https://doi.org/10.1038/nn992)

## Abstract

1. Nat Neurosci. 2003 Jan;6(1):51-8. doi: 10.1038/nn992.

Selective cognitive dysfunction in acetylcholine M1 muscarinic receptor mutant
mice.

Anagnostaras SG(1), Murphy GG, Hamilton SE, Mitchell SL, Rahnama NP, Nathanson
NM, Silva AJ.

Author information:
(1)Department of Neurobiology, Brain Research Institute, 2554 Gonda Center, Box
951761, University of California, Los Angeles, California 90095-1761, USA.

Blockade of cholinergic neurotransmission by muscarinic receptor antagonists
produces profound deficits in attention and memory. However, the antagonists
used in previous studies bind to more than one of the five muscarinic receptor
subtypes. Here we examined memory in mice with a null mutation of the gene
coding the M1 receptor, the most densely distributed muscarinic receptor in the
hippocampus and forebrain. In contrast with previous studies using nonselective
pharmacological antagonists, the M1 receptor deletion produced a selective
phenotype that included both enhancements and deficits in memory. Long-term
potentiation (LTP) in response to theta burst stimulation in the hippocampus was
also reduced in mutant mice. M1 null mutant mice showed normal or enhanced
memory for tasks that involved matching-to-sample problems, but they were
severely impaired in non-matching-to-sample working memory as well as
consolidation. Our results suggest that the M1 receptor is specifically involved
in memory processes for which the cortex and hippocampus interact.
