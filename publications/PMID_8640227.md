# Glucose-6-phosphatase dependent substrate transport in the glycogen storage disease type-1a mouse.
**Authors:** Lei KJ, Chen H, Pan CJ, Ward JM, Mosinger B Jr, Lee EJ, Westphal H, Mansfield BC, Chou JY
**Journal:** Nat Genet (1996)
**DOI:** [10.1038/ng0696-203](https://doi.org/10.1038/ng0696-203)

## Abstract

1. Nat Genet. 1996 Jun;13(2):203-9. doi: 10.1038/ng0696-203.

Glucose-6-phosphatase dependent substrate transport in the glycogen storage
disease type-1a mouse.

Lei KJ(1), Chen H, Pan CJ, Ward JM, Mosinger B Jr, Lee EJ, Westphal H, Mansfield
BC, Chou JY.

Author information:
(1)Heritable Disorders Branch, National Institute of Child Health and Human
Development, National Institutes of Health, Bethesda, Maryland 20892, USA.

Glycogen storage disease type 1a (GSD-1a) is caused by a deficiency in
microsomal glucose-6-phosphatase (G6Pase), the key enzyme in glucose
homeostasis. A G6Pase knockout mouse which mimics the pathophysiology of human
GSD-1a patients was created to understand the pathogenesis of this disorder, to
delineate the mechanisms of G6Pase catalysis, and to develop future therapeutic
approaches. By examining G6Pase in the liver and kidney, the primary
gluconeogenic tissues, we demonstrate that glucose-6-P transport and hydrolysis
are performed by separate proteins which are tightly coupled. We propose a
modified translocase catalytic unit model for G6Pase catalysis.
