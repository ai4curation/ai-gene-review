Dietary cholesterol consumption and intestinal cholesterol absorption contribute
to plasma cholesterol levels, a risk factor for coronary heart disease. The
molecular mechanism of sterol uptake from the lumen of the small intestine is
poorly defined. We show that Niemann-Pick C1 Like 1(NPC1L1) protein plays a
critical role in the absorption of intestinal cholesterol. NPC1L1 expression is
enriched in the small intestine and is in the brush border membrane of
enterocytes. Although otherwise phenotypically normal, NPC1L1-deficient mice
exhibit a substantial reduction in absorbed cholesterol, which is unaffected by
dietary supplementation of bile acids. Ezetimibe, a drug that inhibits
cholesterol absorption, had no effect in NPC1L1 knockout mice, suggesting that
NPC1L1 resides in an ezetimibe-sensitive pathway responsible for intestinal
cholesterol absorption.
