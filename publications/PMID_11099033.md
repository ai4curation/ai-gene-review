# Functional genomic analysis of C. elegans chromosome I by systematic RNA interference.
**Authors:** Fraser AG, Kamath RS, Zipperlen P, Martinez-Campos M, Sohrmann M, Ahringer J
**Journal:** Nature (2000)
**DOI:** [10.1038/35042517](https://doi.org/10.1038/35042517)

## Abstract

1. Nature. 2000 Nov 16;408(6810):325-30. doi: 10.1038/35042517.

Functional genomic analysis of C. elegans chromosome I by systematic RNA
interference.

Fraser AG(1), Kamath RS, Zipperlen P, Martinez-Campos M, Sohrmann M, Ahringer J.

Author information:
(1)Wellcome/CRC Institute, University of Cambridge, UK.

Complete genomic sequence is known for two multicellular eukaryotes, the
nematode Caenorhabditis elegans and the fruit fly Drosophila melanogaster, and
it will soon be known for humans. However, biological function has been assigned
to only a small proportion of the predicted genes in any animal. Here we have
used RNA-mediated interference (RNAi) to target nearly 90% of predicted genes on
C. elegans chromosome I by feeding worms with bacteria that express
double-stranded RNA. We have assigned function to 13.9% of the genes analysed,
increasing the number of sequenced genes with known phenotypes on chromosome I
from 70 to 378. Although most genes with sterile or embryonic lethal RNAi
phenotypes are involved in basal cell metabolism, many genes giving
post-embryonic phenotypes have conserved sequences but unknown function. In
addition, conserved genes are significantly more likely to have an RNAi
phenotype than are genes with no conservation. We have constructed a reusable
library of bacterial clones that will permit unlimited RNAi screens in the
future; this should help develop a more complete view of the relationships
between the genome, gene function and the environment.
