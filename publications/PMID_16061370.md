1. Matrix Biol. 2005 Sep;24(6):389-99. doi: 10.1016/j.matbio.2005.06.008.

The extracellular matrix provides a framework for cell adhesion, supports cell
movement, and serves to compartmentalize tissues into functional units.
Fibronectin is a core component of many extracellular matrices where it
regulates a variety of cell activities through direct interactions with cell
surface integrin receptors. Fibronectin is synthesized by many adherent cells
which then assemble it into a fibrillar network. The assembly process is
integrin-dependent and fibronectin-integrin interactions initiate a step-wise
process involving conformational activation of fibronectin outside and
organization of the actin cytoskeleton inside. During assembly, fibronectin
undergoes conformational changes that expose fibronectin-binding sites and
promote intermolecular interactions needed for fibril formation. In this review,
the main steps of fibronectin assembly are described and recent studies on
fibronectin conformational changes are discussed.
