# Identification and functional characterization of the natural variant MRP3-Arg1297His of human multidrug resistance protein 3 (MRP3/ABCC3).
**Authors:** Lee YM, Cui Y, König J, Risch A, Jäger B, Drings P, Bartsch H, Keppler D, Nies AT
**Journal:** Pharmacogenetics (2004)
**DOI:** [10.1097/00008571-200404000-00001](https://doi.org/10.1097/00008571-200404000-00001)

## Abstract

1. Pharmacogenetics. 2004 Apr;14(4):213-23. doi:
10.1097/00008571-200404000-00001.

Identification and functional characterization of the natural variant
MRP3-Arg1297His of human multidrug resistance protein 3 (MRP3/ABCC3).

Lee YM(1), Cui Y, König J, Risch A, Jäger B, Drings P, Bartsch H, Keppler D,
Nies AT.

Author information:
(1)Division of Tumour Biochemistry, Deutsches Krebsforschungszentrum, D-69120
Heidelberg, Germany.

The human multidrug resistance protein 3 (MRP3, symbol ABCC3) is an ATP-binding
cassette transporter that mediates the efflux of organic anions, including
lipophilic substances conjugated with glucuronate, sulphate or glutathione,
across the basolateral membrane of polarized cells (e.g. hepatocytes) into
blood. Genetic variants of MRP3 may affect the transport of these substances out
of cells. The aims of this study were: (i) to identify MRP3 polymorphisms; (ii)
to functionally characterize one relatively frequent MRP3 polymorphism; and
(iii) to establish whether MRP3 transports bilirubin glucuronosides. Exonic
nucleotide variants in the ABCC3 gene were identified by single-strand
conformation polymorphism analysis. The 3890G>A mutation, resulting in
MRP3-ArgHis, was introduced into the ABCC3 cDNA which was stably transfected
into MDCKII cells. For the functional characterization of MRP3-ArgHis in
comparison with MRP3, ATP-dependent transport was analysed in isolated membrane
vesicles. Two non-synonymous MRP3 variants were identified with an allele
frequency of 0.003 for 1643T>A (MRP3-LeuGln) and 0.08 for 3890G>A (MRP3-ArgHis).
Because of the high frequency of the 3890G>A mutation, and because of the close
proximity of Arg to the second nucleotide-binding domain, we pursued the
functional characterization of the MRP3-ArgHis polymorphic variant. MRP3-ArgHis
was correctly localized to the basolateral membrane of polarized MDCKII cells.
We identified monoglucuronosyl bilirubin, bisglucuronosyl bilirubin and
leukotriene C4 as substrates for both MRP3 and MRP3-ArgHis.
Dehydroepiandrosterone-3-sulphate and 17beta-glucuronosyl oestradiol were
transported with similar kinetics by MRP3 and MRP3-ArgHis. This experimental
setup provides a useful tool to analyse the functional consequences of
polymorphic variants of MRP3.
