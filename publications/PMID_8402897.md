# SREBP-1, a basic-helix-loop-helix-leucine zipper protein that controls transcription of the low density lipoprotein receptor gene.
**Authors:** Yokoyama C, Wang X, Briggs MR, Admon A, Wu J, Hua X, Goldstein JL, Brown MS
**Journal:** Cell (1993)

## Abstract

1. Cell. 1993 Oct 8;75(1):187-97.

SREBP-1, a basic-helix-loop-helix-leucine zipper protein that controls
transcription of the low density lipoprotein receptor gene.

Yokoyama C(1), Wang X, Briggs MR, Admon A, Wu J, Hua X, Goldstein JL, Brown MS.

Author information:
(1)Department of Molecular Genetics, University of Texas Southwestern Medical
Center, Dallas 75235.

Sterol regulatory element 1 (SRE-1), a decamer (5'-ATC-ACCCCAC-3') flanking the
low density lipoprotein (LDL) receptor gene, activates transcription in
sterol-depleted cells and is silenced by sterols. We report the cDNA cloning of
human SREBP-1, a protein that binds SRE-1, activates transcription, and thereby
mediates the final regulatory step in LDL metabolism. SREBP-1 contains a
basic-helix-loop-helix-leucine zipper (bHLH-ZIP) motif, but it differs from
other bHLH-ZIP proteins in its larger size (1147 amino acids) and target
sequence. Instead of an inverted repeat (CANNTG), the target for all known
bHLH-ZIP proteins, SRE-1 contains a direct repeat of CAC. Overexpression of
SREBP-1 activates transcription of reporter genes containing SRE-1 in the
absence (15-fold) and presence (90-fold) of sterols, abolishing sterol
regulation. We suggest that SREBP-1 is regulated by an unknown factor that is
overwhelmed when SREBP-1 is overexpressed. Understanding the regulation of
SREBP-1 may be crucial for understanding the control of plasma cholesterol in
humans.
