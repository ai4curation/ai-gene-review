# Opening the DNA repair toolbox: localization of DNA double strand breaks to the nuclear periphery.
**Authors:** Oza P, Peterson CL
**Journal:** Cell Cycle (2010)
**DOI:** [10.4161/cc.9.1.10317](https://doi.org/10.4161/cc.9.1.10317)

## Abstract

1. Cell Cycle. 2010 Jan 1;9(1):43-9. doi: 10.4161/cc.9.1.10317. Epub 2010 Jan 13.

Opening the DNA repair toolbox: localization of DNA double strand breaks to the
nuclear periphery.

Oza P(1), Peterson CL.

Author information:
(1)Interdisciplinary Graduate Program, University of Massachusetts Medical
School, Worcester, MA, USA.

Efficient repair of DNA double strand breaks is essential for cells to avoid
increased mutation rates, genomic instability, and even cell death.
Consequently, cells have evolved multiple mechanisms for rapidly repairing these
DNA lesions, including error-free homologous recombination as well as
error-prone pathways such as nonhomologous end joining. What happens to DSBs
that are repaired inefficiently or not at all? Recently, several studies in
budding yeast have shown that these more recalcitrant DSBs are localized to the
nuclear periphery through interactions between the nuclear envelope protein,
Mps3, and proteins associated with DSB chromatin. Why these DSBs are tethered to
the nuclear periphery is still not clear, though the current view is that
alternative repair pathways may be activated at the periphery in a final attempt
to repair the lesion. In this Extra View, we discuss these recent reports, and
we show that the Est1 component of the telomerase machinery plays an essential
role in anchoring DSB chromatin to the nuclear envelope protein, Mps3.
