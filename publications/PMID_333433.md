# Sensory transduction in Escherichia coli: two complementary pathways of information processing that involve methylated proteins.
**Authors:** Springer MS, Goy MF, Adler J
**Journal:** Proc Natl Acad Sci U S A (1977)
**DOI:** [10.1073/pnas.74.8.3312](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.74.8.3312)
**PMC:** [PMC431543](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC431543/)

## Abstract

1. Proc Natl Acad Sci U S A. 1977 Aug;74(8):3312-6. doi: 10.1073/pnas.74.8.3312.

Sensory transduction in Escherichia coli: two complementary pathways of
information processing that involve methylated proteins.

Springer MS, Goy MF, Adler J.

The properties of two classes of behavioral mutants of Escherichia coli (called
tsr and tar) are described. The mutations in these strains define two
complementary pathways of information flow in bacterial chemotaxis: behavioral
responses to one set of stimuli are defective in tsr mutants, while responses to
a complementary set of stimuli are defective in tar mutants. A double mutant
containing both genetic lesions is defective in responses to all stimuli tested.
The behavioral defects are shown to correlate with alterations in the properties
of a methylation reaction involved in chemotaxis. Two independent sets of
methyl-accepting proteins are demonstrated in the wild type, each set
functioning in one of the two pathways mentioned above. Methylation of one set
of proteins is defective in tsr mutants, while methylation of the complementary
set is defective in tar mutants. The double mutant shows no methylation of
either set. The relationship between the genetic loci (tsr and tar) and the
methyl-accepting proteins is discussed.
