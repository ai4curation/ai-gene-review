# New insights into corticosteroid-binding globulin and glucocorticoid delivery.
**Authors:** Henley DE, Lightman SL
**Journal:** Neuroscience (2011)
**DOI:** [10.1016/j.neuroscience.2011.02.053](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroscience.2011.02.053)

## Abstract

1. Neuroscience. 2011 Apr 28;180:1-8. doi: 10.1016/j.neuroscience.2011.02.053.
Epub  2011 Mar 1.

New insights into corticosteroid-binding globulin and glucocorticoid delivery.

Henley DE(1), Lightman SL.

Author information:
(1)Faculty of Medicine, Dentistry and Health Sciences, University of Western
Australia, 35 Stirling Highway, Crawley WA 6009, Australia.

Corticosteroid-binding globulin (CBG) binds cortisol with high affinity and
facilitates its transport in the blood. A recent discovery suggests that CBG may
have a role beyond that of a simple transport carrier protein. CBG functions as
a protein thermocouple that is exquisitely sensitive to temperature change,
releasing cortisol in response to increasing temperatures within the human
physiological range. It is also expressed in the human hypothalamus and
cerebrospinal fluid, while in the rodent it is also found in other intracellular
neuronal locations, suggesting a role in regulating access of glucocorticoids to
their receptors in the CNS. Genetic variants of CBG have been detected in man
and have been associated with fatigue-pain syndromes and hypotension, again
suggesting a potential effect of CBG on the access of cortisol to brain
glucocorticoid receptors. These new findings provide the basis for a novel
concept of the mechanisms through which the body regulates access of
glucocorticoids to the brain and other tissues of the body.
