# Cloning of a mammalian transcriptional activator that binds unmethylated CpG motifs and shares a CXXC domain with DNA methyltransferase, human trithorax, and methyl-CpG binding domain protein 1.
**Authors:** Voo KS, Carlone DL, Jacobsen BM, Flodin A, Skalnik DG
**Journal:** Mol Cell Biol (2000)
**DOI:** [10.1128/MCB.20.6.2108-2121.2000](https://doi.org/10.1128/MCB.20.6.2108-2121.2000)
**PMC:** [PMC110827](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC110827/)

## Abstract

1. Mol Cell Biol. 2000 Mar;20(6):2108-21. doi: 10.1128/MCB.20.6.2108-2121.2000.

Cloning of a mammalian transcriptional activator that binds unmethylated CpG
motifs and shares a CXXC domain with DNA methyltransferase, human trithorax, and
methyl-CpG binding domain protein 1.

Voo KS(1), Carlone DL, Jacobsen BM, Flodin A, Skalnik DG.

Author information:
(1)Herman B. Wells Center for Pediatric Research, Section of Pediatric
Hematology/Oncology, Department of Pediatrics, Indianapolis, Indiana 46202, USA.

Ligand screening was utilized to isolate a human cDNA that encodes a novel CpG
binding protein, human CpG binding protein (hCGBP). This factor contains three
cysteine-rich domains, two of which exhibit homology to the plant homeodomain
finger domain. A third cysteine-rich domain conforms to the CXXC motif
identified in DNA methyltransferase, human trithorax, and methyl-CpG binding
domain protein 1. A fragment of hCGBP that contains the CXXC domain binds to an
oligonucleotide probe containing a single CpG site, and this complex is
disrupted by distinct oligonucleotide competitors that also contain a CpG
motif(s). However, hCGBP fails to bind oligonucleotides in which the CpG motif
is either mutated or methylated, and it does not bind to single-stranded DNA or
RNA probes. Furthermore, the introduction of a CpG dinucleotide into an
unrelated oligonucleotide sequence is sufficient to produce a binding site for
hCGBP. Native hCGBP is detected as an 88-kDa protein by Western analysis and is
ubiquitously expressed. The DNA-binding activity of native hCGBP is apparent in
electrophoretic mobility shift assays, and hCGBP trans-activates promoters that
contain CpG motifs but not promoters in which the CpG is ablated. These data
indicate that hCGBP is a transcriptional activator that recognizes unmethylated
CpG dinucleotides, suggesting a role in modulating the expression of genes
located within CpG islands.
