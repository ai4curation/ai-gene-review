Temperature is a crucial environmental cue that governs plant growth and
adaptation in natural habitats. PHYTOCHROME INTERACTING FACTOR 4 (PIF4)
functions as a central regulator promoting thermomorphogenesis in Arabidopsis.
An understanding of its precise regulation is essential for optimal
thermomorphogenic growth. Here, we identified two BBX proteins, BBX24 and BBX25,
as novel components of the PIF4-mediated thermosensory pathway that promote
growth at elevated temperatures. Single and double mutants of bbx24 and bbx25
exhibit moderate to strong temperature-insensitive hypocotyl and cotyledon
growth. Elevated temperatures induce BBX24 and BBX25 mRNA expression and protein
accumulation. Genetic and biochemical analyses reveal that BBX24 and BBX25
enhance PIF4-mediated thermosensory growth by counteracting ELF3, a key
component of the evening complex. Whereas ELF3 represses BBX24 and BBX25 gene
expression at low ambient temperatures during the evening, the inhibition of
ELF3 activity under elevated temperatures increases BBX24 and BBX25 activity.
Moreover, BBX24 and BBX25 suppress ELF3 function through direct physical
interaction, likely relieving its repression of PIF4 and enhancing
thermomorphogenesis. These findings identify the ELF3-BBX24/BBX25-PIF4 axis as a
key regulatory module controlling plant growth and development in response to
temperature cues.

IntroductionEcological cues such as light and temperature are key regulators of plant growth, architecture, and fitness (Franklin et al., 2014; Casal and Questa, 2018; Casal and Balasubramanian, 2019; Legris et al., 2019; Lippmann et al., 2019; Quint et al., 2023). Plants have evolved sophisticated and multifaceted mechanisms to perceive, integrate, and process these cues (Legris et al., 2019; Krahmer and Fankhauser, 2023). Because plants are exposed to conflicting signals, continuous sensing and integration are required to optimize growth and acclimate to local environments. Warm ambient temperatures exert a strong influence on plant growth by accelerating vegetative and reproductive transitions, thus shortening the life-cycle duration (Koini et al., 2009; Crawford et al., 2012; Kumar et al., 2012; Casal and Balasubramanian, 2019; Krahmer and Fankhauser, 2023; Quint et al., 2023). However, elevated temperatures compromise immunity and severely reduce seed set and yield (Koester et al., 2014; Zhao et al., 2017; Burroughs et al., 2023). Increases in seasonal growth temperature due to global warming impair plant development and productivity, threatening farmer livelihoods and food security (Peng et al., 2004; Zhao et al., 2017). Therefore, an understanding of how plants perceive and respond to temperature cues is essential for defining thermosensory signaling mechanisms and developing climate-resilient crops.The light signaling pathway, composed of photoreceptors and downstream signaling components, plays a pivotal role in temperature perception and integration (Li et al., 2016; Legris et al., 2019). Key studies have demonstrated that the red-light-absorbing phytochrome B (phyB) functions as a thermosensor (Jung et al., 2016; Legris et al., 2016). phyB exists in two interconvertible forms: a physiologically active form (Pfr) and an inactive form (Pr). Warm temperatures favor the conversion from Pfr to Pr, thereby reducing phyB activity (Legris et al., 2016). phyB targets a group of growth-promoting transcription factors, PHYTOCHROME INTERACTING FACTORS (PIFs), for phosphorylation and degradation in a red-light-dependent manner (Huq and Quail, 2002; de Lucas et al., 2008; Lorrain et al., 2008). Among PIFs, PIF4 and PIF7 have been shown to mediate thermomorphogenesis under short-day (SD) and long-day (LD) photoperiods, respectively (Koini et al., 2009; Kumar et al., 2012; Chung et al., 2020; Fiorucci et al., 2020; Burko et al., 2022; Delker et al., 2022). PIF4 is essential for hypocotyl and petiole elongation, rosette expansion, hyponasty, and flowering under elevated temperatures (Koini et al., 2009; Kumar et al., 2012; Oh et al., 2012; Quint et al., 2016, 2023; Casal and Balasubramanian, 2019; Favero et al., 2020; Zhao and Bao, 2021). PIF4-dependent thermomorphogenesis has been linked to the induction of genes associated with auxin and brassinosteroid (BR) biosynthesis and signal transduction (Franklin et al., 2011; Sun et al., 2012; Ibanez et al., 2018; Krahmer and Fankhauser, 2023), cell elongation (Gangappa and Kumar, 2017), and floral transition (Kumar et al., 2012). Reduction in phyB activity under warm conditions enhances PIF4 function, thereby promoting thermosensory growth (Legris et al., 2016). Unlike phyB, the blue light receptor cryptochrome 1 (CRY1) suppresses thermomorphogenesis by sequestering PIF4 (Ma et al., 2016). Core circadian clock components, including the evening complex (EC), exert critical control over thermomorphogenesis through modulation of PIF4 activity. EARLY FLOWERING 3 (ELF3), a central EC component and thermosensor, represses PIF4 transcription and additionally sequesters PIF4 via heterodimer formation (Nusinow et al., 2011; Box et al., 2015; Nieto et al., 2015; Jung et al., 2020). Upstream regulatory factors such as CONSTITUTIVE PHOTOMORPHOGENIC 1 (COP1), DE-ETIOLATED 1 (DET1), and SUPPRESSOR OF PHYTOCHROME A-105 1 (SPA1) promote PIF4-driven thermomorphogenic responses by stabilizing PIF4 protein (Gangappa and Kumar, 2017; Park et al., 2017; Lee et al., 2020). Additionally, recent work indicates that the HEMERA/REGULATOR OF CHLOROPLAST BIOGENESIS (HMR/RCB) module cooperatively stabilizes PIF4 during the day to enhance thermomorphogenesis (Qiu et al., 2019, 2021). Members of the heat shock transcription factor (HSF) family, HSFA1a/b/d/e, interact with PIF4 and stabilize it under light by disrupting its association with phyB, thereby supporting thermomorphogenesis during the photoperiod (Tan et al., 2023).The B-BOX-containing zinc-finger (BBX) proteins are highly conserved transcription factors across eukaryotes that regulate gene expression by directly binding DNA or acting as transcriptional co-activators or co-repressors through protein–protein interactions. BBX proteins have emerged as crucial integrators of various environmental cues (Khanna et al., 2009; Gangappa and Botto, 2014; Ding et al., 2018; Vaishak et al., 2019; Song et al., 2020; Yuan et al., 2021). BBX24 and BBX25, which are close homologs, function as transcriptional co-repressors of HY5 to inhibit photomorphogenesis (Gangappa et al., 2013; Job et al., 2018). Additionally, BBX24 participates in multiple signaling pathways, including UV-B response (Jiang et al., 2012; Huang et al., 2022), shade avoidance (Crocco et al., 2015; Saura-Sanchez et al., 2023), and flowering (Li et al., 2014). However, the role of BBX24 and BBX25 in temperature-responsive regulation of growth and development remains unclear. Here, we report that BBX24 and BBX25 serve as key regulators of PIF4-mediated thermosensory growth. BBX24 and BBX25 gene expression and corresponding protein abundance are elevated at warm temperatures, resulting in enhanced PIF4 accumulation and thermomorphogenic responses. Our study demonstrates that ELF3 acts upstream of BBX24 and BBX25, repressing their gene expression during the evening under low temperatures. In contrast, warm temperature-dependent inhibition of ELF3 activity increases BBX24 and BBX25 function, thereby promoting PIF4-mediated thermomorphogenic growth. Furthermore, BBX24 and BBX25 reduce ELF3 protein accumulation, alleviating its negative regulatory effect on PIF4 transcript and protein levels. Together, this study reveals a novel regulatory mechanism through which BBX24 and BBX25 promote PIF4-mediated thermosensory growth by antagonizing ELF3 function.

Introduction

However, elevated temperatures compromise immunity and severely reduce seed set and yield (Koester et al., 2014; Zhao et al., 2017; Burroughs et al., 2023). Increases in seasonal growth temperature due to global warming impair plant development and productivity, threatening farmer livelihoods and food security (Peng et al., 2004; Zhao et al., 2017). Therefore, an understanding of how plants perceive and respond to temperature cues is essential for defining thermosensory signaling mechanisms and developing climate-resilient crops.

The light signaling pathway, composed of photoreceptors and downstream signaling components, plays a pivotal role in temperature perception and integration (Li et al., 2016; Legris et al., 2019). Key studies have demonstrated that the red-light-absorbing phytochrome B (phyB) functions as a thermosensor (Jung et al., 2016; Legris et al., 2016). phyB exists in two interconvertible forms: a physiologically active form (Pfr) and an inactive form (Pr). Warm temperatures favor the conversion from Pfr to Pr, thereby reducing phyB activity (Legris et al., 2016). phyB targets a group of growth-promoting transcription factors, PHYTOCHROME INTERACTING FACTORS (PIFs), for phosphorylation and degradation in a red-light-dependent manner (Huq and Quail, 2002; de Lucas et al., 2008; Lorrain et al., 2008). Among PIFs, PIF4 and PIF7 have been shown to mediate thermomorphogenesis under short-day (SD) and long-day (LD) photoperiods, respectively (Koini et al., 2009; Kumar et al., 2012; Chung et al., 2020; Fiorucci et al., 2020; Burko et al., 2022; Delker et al., 2022). PIF4 is essential for hypocotyl and petiole elongation, rosette expansion, hyponasty, and flowering under elevated temperatures (Koini et al., 2009; Kumar et al., 2012; Oh et al., 2012; Quint et al., 2016, 2023; Casal and Balasubramanian, 2019; Favero et al., 2020; Zhao and Bao, 2021). PIF4-dependent thermomorphogenesis has been linked to the induction of genes associated with auxin and brassinosteroid (BR) biosynthesis and signal transduction (Franklin et al., 2011; Sun et al., 2012; Ibanez et al., 2018; Krahmer and Fankhauser, 2023), cell elongation (Gangappa and Kumar, 2017), and floral transition (Kumar et al., 2012). Additionally, recent work indicates that the HEMERA/REGULATOR OF CHLOROPLAST BIOGENESIS (HMR/RCB) module cooperatively stabilizes PIF4 during the day to enhance thermomorphogenesis (Qiu et al., 2019, 2021). Members of the heat shock transcription factor (HSF) family, HSFA1a/b/d/e, interact with PIF4 and stabilize it under light by disrupting its association with phyB, thereby supporting thermomorphogenesis during the photoperiod (Tan et al., 2023).

The B-BOX-containing zinc-finger (BBX) proteins are highly conserved transcription factors across eukaryotes that regulate gene expression by directly binding DNA or acting as transcriptional co-activators or co-repressors through protein–protein interactions. BBX proteins have emerged as crucial integrators of various environmental cues (Khanna et al., 2009; Gangappa and Botto, 2014; Ding et al., 2018; Vaishak et al., 2019; Song et al., 2020; Yuan et al., 2021). BBX24 and BBX25, which are close homologs, function as transcriptional co-repressors of HY5 to inhibit photomorphogenesis (Gangappa et al., 2013; Job et al., 2018). BBX24 and BBX25 gene expression and corresponding protein abundance are elevated at warm temperatures, resulting in enhanced PIF4 accumulation and thermomorphogenic responses. Together, this study reveals a novel regulatory mechanism through which BBX24 and BBX25 promote PIF4-mediated thermosensory growth by antagonizing ELF3 function.

ResultsBBX24 and BBX25 are essential for warm temperature–mediated growthTo investigate the role of BBX24 and BBX25 in temperature-responsive regulation of hypocotyl elongation, we examined the hypocotyl growth of wild type (Col-0) and bbx24 and bbx25 mutants grown at 22°C and 27°C. The data indicated that bbx24 and bbx25 mutants exhibited moderately but significantly shorter hypocotyls than Col-0 at both temperatures under SD and LD photoperiods (Figures 1A–1C). Compared with Col-0, bbx24 showed a stronger reduction in hypocotyl elongation than bbx25 at 27°C (Figures 1A–1C). Notably, the bbx24 bbx25 double mutant displayed a considerably shorter hypocotyl at 27°C than either single mutant (Figures 1A–1C), suggesting an additive effect in promoting warm temperature-induced hypocotyl growth. In contrast to the mutants, the BBX24 overexpression line (hereafter referred to as BBX24-OE) exhibited elongated hypocotyls compared with Col-0 at 22°C and 27°C under both SD and LD conditions (Figures 1D–1F). However, the relative increase in hypocotyl length in BBX24-OE was less pronounced than that in Col-0 (Figures 1D–1F). To determine whether BBX25 overexpression similarly promotes hypocotyl elongation, we generated multiple transgenic lines overexpressing BBX25 (Supplemental Figures 1A and 1B). Similar to BBX24-OE, BBX25 overexpression lines showed enhanced hypocotyl elongation compared with Col-0 at both temperatures under SD and LD (Figures 1D–1F; Supplemental Figures 1C–1E). Hypocotyl elongation under warm temperatures is primarily attributed to increased cell elongation driven by elevated levels of growth-promoting hormones such as auxin and BR (Franklin et al., 2011; Oh et al., 2012; Ibanez et al., 2018). To assess whether BBX24/BBX25-dependent hypocotyl elongation is associated with increased cell length, we measured the length of hypocotyl epidermal cells in seedlings grown at 22°C and 27°C under SD. The results revealed significantly longer epidermal cells in Col-0 at 27°C than at 22°C (Figure 1G). In contrast, the bbx24 bbx25 double mutant exhibited a strong reduction in cell length (Figure 1G). Conversely, BBX24 and BBX25 overexpression lines displayed significantly longer cells than Col-0 at both temperatures (Figure 1G). Moreover, increased PIF4 accumulation in the elf3 mutant at ZT23 and ZT8 under both temperature conditions was dependent on BBX24 and BBX25 because PIF4 protein levels were greatly reduced in the bbx24 bbx25 elf3 triple mutant (Supplemental Figures 13B and 13C). This increase in PIF4 protein levels in the elf3 mutant is likely driven by elevated PIF4 transcript levels. Accordingly, PIF4 transcript abundance was significantly lower in the bbx24 bbx25 elf3 triple mutant than in elf3 (Supplemental Figure 13D). Consistent with suppression of the elf3 phenotype by bbx24 bbx25, expression levels of PIF4-regulated growth genes—such as XTR7, IAA19, PRE5, and IAA29—were significantly downregulated in the bbx24 bbx25 elf3 mutant compared with elf3 (Supplemental Figure 13E). Together, these findings confirm that BBX24- and BBX25-mediated inhibition of ELF3 function is essential for optimal PIF4 gene expression and protein accumulation.

Consistent with the roles of BBX24 and BBX25 in thermosensory hypocotyl and cotyledon growth, we also observed their involvement in later developmental stages, including petiole elongation and flowering. The elongated petiole and early flowering phenotypes of PIF4-OE and elf3 mutants were strongly suppressed by bbx24 bbx25 mutations under both SD (Supplemental Figure 14) and LD (Supplemental Figure15) conditions.
