The fundamental biochemical and stereochemical difference in how cell membrane
lipids are built between Archaea on one side, and Bacteria and Eukarya on the
other is known as the lipid divide. It is often assumed to translate in the
impossibility of combining membranes of both types. It was established early
when little sampling of the biodiversity was available. Since then, exceptions
have been revealed and accumulated. Here, we review those exceptions and
complement them with a broad survey, revealing diverse bacteria with mixed
membrane-lipid features. Together with liposome assays and synthetic-biology
experiments, studies on these organisms suggest that the lipid divide can be
bridged. We conclude by discussing the evolutionary implications for LUCA, the
origin of the prokaryotic domains and eukaryogenesis.
