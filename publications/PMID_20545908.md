# Autophagosome formation depends on the small GTPase Rab1 and functional ER exit sites.
**Authors:** Zoppino FC, Militello RD, Slavin I, Alvarez C, Colombo MI
**Journal:** Traffic (2010)
**DOI:** [10.1111/j.1600-0854.2010.01086.x](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1600-0854.2010.01086.x)

## Abstract

1. Traffic. 2010 Sep;11(9):1246-61. doi: 10.1111/j.1600-0854.2010.01086.x. Epub 
2010 Jun 10.

Zoppino FC(1), Militello RD, Slavin I, Alvarez C, Colombo MI.

Author information:
(1)Instituto de Histología y Embriología (IHEM-CONICET), Facultad de Ciencias

Autophagy is an important cellular degradation pathway present in all eukaryotic 
cells. Via this pathway, portions of the cytoplasm and/or organelles are 
sequestered in double-membrane structures called autophagosomes. In spite of the 
significant advance achieved in autophagy, the long-standing question about the 
source of the autophagic membrane remains unsolved. We have investigated the 
role of the secretory pathway in autophagosome biogenesis. Sar1 and Rab1b are 
monomeric GTPases that control traffic from the endoplasmic reticulum (ER) to 
the Golgi. We present evidence indicating that the activity of both proteins is 
required for autophagosome formation. Overexpression of dominant-negative 
mutants and the use of siRNAs impaired autophagosome generation as determined by 
LC3 puncta formation and light chain 3 (LC3)-II processing. In addition, our 
results indicate that the autophagic and secretory pathways intersect at a level 
preceding the brefeldin A blockage, suggesting that the transport from the 
cis/medial Golgi is not necessary for autophagosome biogenesis. Our present 
results highlight the role of transport from the ER in the initial events of the 
autophagic vacuole development.
