# Formation of signal transfer complexes between stem cell and platelet-derived growth factor receptors and SH2 domain proteins in vitro.
**Authors:** Herbst R, Shearman MS, Jallal B, Schlessinger J, Ullrich A
**Journal:** Biochemistry (1995)
**DOI:** [10.1021/bi00017a026](https://doi.org/10.1021/bi00017a026)

## Abstract

1. Biochemistry. 1995 May 2;34(17):5971-9. doi: 10.1021/bi00017a026.

Formation of signal transfer complexes between stem cell and platelet-derived
growth factor receptors and SH2 domain proteins in vitro.

Herbst R(1), Shearman MS, Jallal B, Schlessinger J, Ullrich A.

Author information:
(1)Department of Molecular Biology, Max-Planck-Institut für Biochemie,
Martinsried, Germany.

Cellular growth and differentiation signals are generated and defined by the
interaction of specific phosphotyrosine residues of activated receptor tyrosine
kinases (RTKs) and src homology-2 (SH2) domain-containing intracellular signal
transducers. This appears to involve for both the p145c-kit and beta
platelet-derived growth factor receptor (PDGF-R) cytoplasmic domains the
formation of multiprotein signal transfer complexes, which include combinations
of noncatalytic and enzymatically active subunits of phosphatidylinositol
3'-kinase (PI3'-K), phospholipase C-gamma (PLC gamma), and guanosine
trisphosphatase activating protein (GAP). In vitro association experiments
indicate that PLC gamma and PI3'-K bind the beta PDGF-R simultaneously, while
these two SH2 proteins compete for association to p145c-kit binding sites, with
p85/PI3'-K exhibiting higher affinity. Interestingly, GAP and p85/PI3'-K binding
to distinct p145c-kit phosphotyrosines is cooperative, enhancing formation of a
heterotetrameric signaling complex, which may include different combinations of
p85 alpha and p85 beta with p110, p112, and p116 by interaction with the same
tyrosine 721 docking site. The diversity of molecular interactions observed for
PDGF-R and p145c-kit suggests a new mode of signal definition and modulation.
