Frizzled receptors (FZDs) are class-F G-protein-coupled receptors (GPCRs) that
function in Wnt signalling and are essential for developing and adult
organisms1,2. As central mediators in this complex signalling pathway, FZDs
serve as gatekeeping proteins both for drug intervention and for the development
of probes in basic and in therapeutic research. Here we present an
atomic-resolution structure of the human Frizzled 4 receptor (FZD4)
transmembrane domain in the absence of a bound ligand. The structure reveals an
unusual transmembrane architecture in which helix VI is short and tightly
packed, and is distinct from all other GPCR structures reported so far. Within
this unique transmembrane fold is an extremely narrow and highly hydrophilic
pocket that is not amenable to the binding of traditional GPCR ligands. We show
that such a pocket is conserved across all FZDs, which may explain the
long-standing difficulties in the development of ligands for these receptors.
Molecular dynamics simulations on the microsecond timescale and mutational
analysis uncovered two coupled, dynamic kinks located at helix VII that are
involved in FZD4 activation. The stability of the structure in its ligand-free
form, an unfavourable pocket for ligand binding and the two unusual kinks on
helix VII suggest that FZDs may have evolved a novel ligand-recognition and
activation mechanism that is distinct from that of other GPCRs.
