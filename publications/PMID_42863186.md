PURPOSE: This study aimed to explore the effects and mechanisms of
endosome-associated trafficking regulator 1 (ENTR1) in osteoarthritis (OA),
thereby providing a novel therapeutic target.
METHODS: We established OA rat model via the destabilization of the medial
meniscus (DMM) surgery and OA cell model via IL-1β-stimulated ATDC5 cells. HE
and safranin O/fast green staining were employed to assess the pathological
changes of cartilage tissues in rats. Moreover, TUNEL staining was applied to
examine chondrocyte apoptosis in rat cartilage tissues. Protein and mRNA
expressions were measured employing ELISA, qRT-PCR, immunohistochemical
staining, western blot, immunofluorescence staining and flow cytometry. The
apoptosis of ATDC5 cells was determined using flow cytometry. Besides, co-IP and
immunofluorescence co-localization were employed to assess the binding of ENTR1
to PTPN13.
RESULTS: ENTR1 was notably downregulated in the cartilage tissues of patients
with OA and rats with DMM-induced OA, and in IL-1β-stimulated ATDC5 cells. ENTR1
overexpression attenuated the development of DMM-induced OA in rats via
inhibiting inflammation, mitigating cartilage degradation and preventing
chondrocyte apoptosis In the cell model of OA, upregulation of ENTR1 alleviated
apoptosis, and mitigated inflammation and ECM degradation. Moreover, we found
that ENTR1 overexpression decreased the FAS surface level in IL-1β-induced ATDC5
cells by binding PTPN13. Besides, ENTR1 silencing promoted apoptosis and
inflammation by increasing the FAS surface level in IL-1β-induced ATDC5 cells.
CONCLUSION: ENTR1 could ameliorate OA progression through decreasing FAS surface
level by binding PTPN13.
