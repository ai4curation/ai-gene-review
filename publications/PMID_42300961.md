# Blocking protein quality control degradation leads to structural stabilization of DHFR indel variants.
**Authors:** Larsen-Ledet S, Suhr CH, Gersing S, Hackney CM, Stein A, Teilum K, Hartmann-Petersen R
**Journal:** FEBS J (2026)
**DOI:** [10.1111/febs.70625](https://doi.org/10.1111/febs.70625)

## Abstract

1. FEBS J. 2026 Jun 16. doi: 10.1111/febs.70625. Online ahead of print.

Blocking protein quality control degradation leads to structural stabilization
of DHFR indel variants.

Larsen-Ledet S(1), Suhr CH(1), Gersing S(1), Hackney CM(1), Stein A(1), Teilum
K(1), Hartmann-Petersen R(1).

Author information:
(1)Department of Biology, University of Copenhagen, Denmark.

Gene variants leading to insertions or deletions of amino acid residues (indels)
often have detrimental consequences for the folding of the encoded protein. Yet,
at some positions, indels are tolerated or result merely in partial unfolding
and hypomorphic (reduced function) phenotypes. Typically, unfolded proteins are
targeted for protein quality control (PQC) degradation via the
ubiquitin-proteasome system, which in yeast is mediated by specific E3
ubiquitin-protein ligases, including Ubr1 and San1. Here, we systematically
probed the folding of a library of indel variants in the DHFR protein using a
sensitive yeast-based protein folding reporter. We show that deletion of Ubr1 or
San1 leads to a greater fraction of folded DHFR indel variants, primarily
positioned toward the N- and C-terminal regions in DHFR. Intriguingly, most of
the DHFR indels that are structurally stabilized in the E3 knockout strains are
also stabilized at lowered temperatures and upon binding the DHFR inhibitor
methotrexate. This suggests that blocking PQC degradation can restore sufficient
folding and increase function of hypomorph variants, thus providing a potential
therapeutic avenue for protein misfolding diseases.
