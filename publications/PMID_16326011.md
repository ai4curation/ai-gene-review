# Acetohydroxyacid synthase isozyme I from Escherichia coli has unique catalytic and regulatory properties.
**Authors:** Vinogradov V, Vyazmensky M, Engel S, Belenky I, Kaplun A, Kryukov O, Barak Z, Chipman DM
**Journal:** Biochim Biophys Acta (2006)
**DOI:** [10.1016/j.bbagen.2005.10.008](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.bbagen.2005.10.008)

## Abstract

1. Biochim Biophys Acta. 2006 Mar;1760(3):356-63. doi:
10.1016/j.bbagen.2005.10.008. Epub 2005 Nov 10.

Acetohydroxyacid synthase isozyme I from Escherichia coli has unique catalytic
and regulatory properties.

Vinogradov V(1), Vyazmensky M, Engel S, Belenky I, Kaplun A, Kryukov O, Barak Z,
Chipman DM.

Author information:
(1)Department of Life Sciences, Ben-Gurion University of the Negev, POB 657,
Beer-Sheva 84105, Israel.

AHAS I is an isozyme of acetohydroxyacid synthase which is apparently unique to
enterobacteria. It has been known for over 20 years that it has many properties
which are quite different from those of the other two enterobacterial AHASs
isozymes, as well as from those of "typical" AHASs which are single enzymes in a
given organism. These include a unique mechanism for regulation of expression
and the absence of a preference for forming acetohydroxybutyrate. We have cloned
the two subunits, ilvB and ilvN, of this Escherichia coli isoenzyme and examined
the enzymatic properties of the purified holoenzyme and the enzyme reconstituted
from purified subunits. Unlike other AHASs, AHAS I demonstrates cooperative
feedback inhibition by valine, and the kinetics fit closely to an exclusive
binding model. The formation of acetolactate by AHAS I is readily reversible and
acetolactate can act as substrate for alternative AHAS I-catalyzed reactions.
