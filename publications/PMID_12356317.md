# Direct measurements of heterotypic adhesion between the cell surface proteins CD2 and CD48.
**Authors:** Zhu B, Davies EA, van der Merwe PA, Calvert T, Leckband DE
**Journal:** Biochemistry (2002)
**DOI:** [10.1021/bi020296g](https://doi.org/10.1021/bi020296g)

## Abstract

1. Biochemistry. 2002 Oct 8;41(40):12163-70. doi: 10.1021/bi020296g.

Direct measurements of heterotypic adhesion between the cell surface proteins
CD2 and CD48.

Zhu B(1), Davies EA, van der Merwe PA, Calvert T, Leckband DE.

Author information:
(1)Departments of Chemical Engineering and Biochemistry, University of Illinois
at Urbana-Champaign, Urbana, IL 61801, USA.

Direct force measurements were used to investigate the molecular mechanism of
heterophilic adhesion between the murine T-cell adhesion glycoprotein CD2 and
its ligand CD48. From the distance dependence of the protein-protein interaction
potential, we demonstrate directly that the full-length extracellular domains
adhere in a head-to-head orientation. The absence of long-range electrostatic
protein-protein attraction further indicates that the salt bridges between the
binding surfaces only influence the interaction at short range. Despite the loss
of a stabilizing disulfide bond in domain 1 (D1) of CD2, adhesive failure occurs
abruptly with no evidence of partial protein unfolding during detachment.
Finally, these measurements between extended membrane surfaces directly confirm
that the low-affinity CD2-CD48 bond generates weak adhesion and that lateral
receptor mobility is required for the development of appreciable adhesion. This
is the first direct measurement of the range and magnitude of the forces
governing heterotypic adhesion mediated by cell surface proteins. These results
both verified the head-to-head CD2-CD48 docking alignment and demonstrated the
ability to elucidate the structure-function relationships of adhesion proteins
from the measured distance dependence of their interaction potentials.
