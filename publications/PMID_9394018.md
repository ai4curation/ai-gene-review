# In vivo association of lamins with nucleic acids in Drosophila melanogaster.
**Authors:** Rzepecki R, Bogachev SS, Kokoza E, Stuurman N, Fisher PA
**Journal:** J Cell Sci (1998)
**DOI:** [10.1242/jcs.111.1.121](https://doi.org/10.1242/jcs.111.1.121)

## Abstract

1. J Cell Sci. 1998 Jan;111 ( Pt 1):121-9. doi: 10.1242/jcs.111.1.121.

In vivo association of lamins with nucleic acids in Drosophila melanogaster.

Rzepecki R(1), Bogachev SS, Kokoza E, Stuurman N, Fisher PA.

Author information:
(1)Department of Pharmacological Sciences, University Medical Center at Stony
Brook, State University of New York at Stony Brook, Stony Brook, NY 11794-8651,
USA.

A 32P-labeling strategy was developed to study the interaction(s) in tissue
culture cells between proteins and nucleic acids. Interphase and mitotic nuclear
lamins were studied in Drosophila Kc cells. After bromodeoxyuridine
incorporation and in vivo photo-crosslinking with 366 nm light, it was found
that interphase lamins were associated with nucleic acid. Interactions with DNA
as well as RNA were detected. In contrast, interaction of nucleic acids with
mitotic lamin was not observed. Photo-crosslinking in the presence of
antibiotics distamycin and/or chromomycin suggested that interphase lamins
interacted with both A-T-rich DNA and G-C-rich DNA; interactions with G-C-rich
DNA predominated. These results have implications for understanding the
interphase organization of the higher eukaryotic cell nucleus as well as the
transition of cells from interphase to mitosis. A model of nuclear organization,
consistent with our results, is proposed.
