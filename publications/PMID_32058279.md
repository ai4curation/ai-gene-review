Covalent DNA-protein crosslinks (DPCs) are highly toxic DNA adducts, which
interfere with faithful DNA replication. The proteases Wss1 and SPRTN degrade
DPCs and have emerged as crucially important DNA repair enzymes. Their
protective role has been described in various model systems ranging from yeasts,
plants, worms and flies to mice and humans. Loss of DPC proteases results in
genome instability, cellular arrest, premature ageing and cancer predisposition.
Here we discuss recent insights into the function and molecular mechanism of
these enzymes. Furthermore, we present an in-depth phylogenetic analysis of the
Wss1/SPRTN protease continuum. Remarkably flexible domain architectures and
constantly changing protein-protein interaction motifs indicate ongoing
evolutionary dynamics. Finally, we discuss recent data, which suggest that
further partially-overlapping proteolytic systems targeting DPCs exist in
eukaryotes. These new developments raise interesting questions regarding the
division of labour between different DPC proteases and the mechanisms and
principles of repair pathway choice.
