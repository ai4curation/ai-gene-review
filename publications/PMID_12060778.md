A protein-interaction network centered on the replication machinery of Bacillus
subtilis was generated by genome-wide two-hybrid screens and systematic
specificity assays. The network consists of 91 specific interactions linking 69
proteins. Over one fourth of the interactions take place between homologues of
proteins known to interact in other organisms, indicating the high biological
significance of the other interactions we report. These interactions provide
insights on the relations of DNA replication with recombination and repair,
membrane-bound protein complexes, and signaling pathways. They also lead to the
biological role of unknown proteins, as illustrated for the highly conserved
YabA, which is shown here to act in initiation control. Thus, our interaction
map provides a valuable tool for the discovery of aspects of bacterial DNA
replication.
