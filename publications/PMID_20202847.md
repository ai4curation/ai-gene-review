In addition to the sugar phosphotransferase system (sugar PTS) dedicated to
carbohydrate uptake, many Gram-negative bacteria possess a so-called nitrogen
PTS (PTS(Ntr)). Although fulfilling very different functions, both systems can
communicate with each other by phosphate exchange. PTS(Ntr) regulates diverse
processes implicated in metabolism of nitrogen and carbon, and is essential for
virulence in some bacteria. Additionally, it plays a role in potassium
homeostasis by regulating the expression and activity of a high- and a
low-affinity K(+) transporter, respectively. In this article, we review recent
advances in the understanding of the regulatory roles of PTS(Ntr) in various
organisms.
