# The histone mark H3K36me3 regulates human DNA mismatch repair through its interaction with MutSα.
**Authors:** Li F, Mao G, Tong D, Huang J, Gu L, Yang W, Li GM
**Journal:** Cell (2013)
**DOI:** [10.1016/j.cell.2013.03.025](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2013.03.025)

## Abstract

1. Cell. 2013 Apr 25;153(3):590-600. doi: 10.1016/j.cell.2013.03.025.

The histone mark H3K36me3 regulates human DNA mismatch repair through its
interaction with MutSα.

Li F(1), Mao G, Tong D, Huang J, Gu L, Yang W, Li GM.

Author information:
(1)Graduate Center for Toxicology, Markey Cancer Center, University of Kentucky

Comment in
    Cell. 2013 Apr 25;153(3):513-5. doi: 10.1016/j.cell.2013.04.018.

DNA mismatch repair (MMR) ensures replication fidelity by correcting mismatches
generated during DNA replication. Although human MMR has been reconstituted
in vitro, how MMR occurs in vivo is unknown. Here, we show that an epigenetic
histone mark, H3K36me3, is required in vivo to recruit the mismatch recognition
protein hMutSα (hMSH2-hMSH6) onto chromatin through direct interactions with the
hMSH6 PWWP domain. The abundance of H3K36me3 in G1 and early S phases ensures
that hMutSα is enriched on chromatin before mispairs are introduced during DNA
replication. Cells lacking the H3K36 trimethyltransferase SETD2 display
microsatellite instability (MSI) and an elevated spontaneous mutation frequency,
characteristic of MMR-deficient cells. This work reveals that a histone mark
regulates MMR in human cells and explains the long-standing puzzle of
MSI-positive cancer cells that lack detectable mutations in known MMR genes.
