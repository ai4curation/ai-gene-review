# Glutathione is a low-affinity substrate of the human sodium-dependent dicarboxylate transporter.
**Authors:** Schorbach L, Krick W, Burckhardt G, Burckhardt BC
**Journal:** Nephron Physiol (2013)
**DOI:** [10.1159/000356419](https://doi.org/10.1159/000356419)

## Abstract

1. Nephron Physiol. 2013;124(1-2):1-5. doi: 10.1159/000356419. Epub 2013 Nov 14.

Glutathione is a low-affinity substrate of the human sodium-dependent
dicarboxylate transporter.

Schorbach L(1), Krick W, Burckhardt G, Burckhardt BC.

Author information:
(1)Institute of Systemic Physiology and Pathophysiology, University Medical
Center Göttingen, Göttingen, Germany.

BACKGROUND/AIMS: During a single pass through the kidneys, more than 80% of
glutathione (GSH) is excreted, indicating not only glomerular filtration, but
also tubular secretion. The first step in tubular secretion is the uptake of a
substance across the basolateral membrane of proximal tubule cells by
sodium-dependent and -independent transporters. Due to the dicarboxylate-like
structure, we postulated that GSH uptake across the basolateral membrane is
mediated by the sodium-dependent dicarboxylate transporter 3 (NaDC3).
METHODS: Tracer uptake and electrophysiologic measurements using a two-electrode
voltage clamp device were performed in Xenopus laevis oocytes expressing the
human (h)NaDC3.
RESULTS: Uptake of succinate, the reference substrate of hNaDC3, was inhibited
by GSH in a dose-dependent manner with an IC50 of 1.88 mM. GSH evoked
potential-dependent inward currents, which were abolished under sodium-free
conditions. At -60 mV, GSH currents showed saturation kinetics with a KM of 1.65
mM.
CONCLUSION: hNaDC3 present at the basolateral membrane of proximal tubule cells
mediates sodium-dependent GSH uptake. The kinetic data show that NaDC3 is a
low-affinity GSH transporter.
