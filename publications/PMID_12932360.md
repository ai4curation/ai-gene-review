Comment in
    Immunity. 2003 Aug;19(2):155-6. doi: 10.1016/s1074-7613(03)00212-7.

Lipopolysaccharide binding protein (LBP) has a well-established role in
LPS-induced immune responses. Here, we report that LBP also plays an essential
role in the innate immune response to Gram-positive pneumococci, specifically to
their major inflammatory component, pneumococcal cell wall (PCW). LBP was
present in the CSF of patients with meningitis, and LBP-deficient mice failed to
develop meningeal inflammation. LBP enhanced PCW-induced cell signaling and
TNF-alpha release. LBP bound specifically to PCW multimers, indicating novel
lipid-independent binding capability for LBP. We propose the iterative anionic
groups along the glycan backbone of the cell wall are a crucial structure for
recognition by LBP. Such a function for LBP expands its role to Gram-positive
infections.
