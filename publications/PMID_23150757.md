Author information:

MDM2 oncogenic protein is the principal cellular antagonist of the p53 tumor
suppresser gene. p53 activity needs exquisite control to elicit appropriate
responses to differential cellular stress conditions. p53 becomes stabilized and
active upon various types of stresses. However, too much p53 is not beneficial
to cells and causes lethality. At the steady state, p53 activity needs to be
leashed for cell survival. Early studies suggested that the MDM2 oncoprotein
negatively regulates p53 activity through the induction of p53 protein
degradation. MDM2 serves as an E3 ubiquitin ligase of p53; it catalyzes
polyubiquitination and subsequently induces proteasome degradation to
downregulate p53 protein level. However, the mechanism by which MDM2 represses
p53 is not a single mode. Emerging evidence reveals another cellular location of
MDM2-p53 interaction. MDM2 is recruited to chromatin, specifically the p53
responsive promoter regions, in a p53 dependent manner. MDM2 is proposed to
directly inhibit p53 transactivity at chromatin. This article provides an
overview of the mechanism by which p53 is repressed by MDM2 in both
ubiquitination dependent and ubiquitination independent pathways.
