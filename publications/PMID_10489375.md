Comment on
    Science. 1999 Sep 17;285(5435):1861. doi: 10.1126/science.285.5435.1861.

Antithrombin, a member of the serpin family, functions as an inhibitor of
thrombin and other enzymes. Cleavage of the carboxyl-terminal loop of
antithrombin induces a conformational change in the molecule. Here it is shown
that the cleaved conformation of antithrombin has potent antiangiogenic and
antitumor activity in mouse models. The latent form of intact antithrombin,
which is similar in conformation to the cleaved molecule, also inhibited
angiogenesis and tumor growth. These data provide further evidence that the
clotting and fibrinolytic pathways are directly involved in the regulation of
angiogenesis.
