# Direct and long-range action of a DPP morphogen gradient.
**Authors:** Nellen D, Burke R, Struhl G, Basler K
**Journal:** Cell (1996)
**DOI:** [10.1016/s0092-8674(00)81114-9](https://doi.org/10.1016/s0092-8674(00)81114-9)

## Abstract

1. Cell. 1996 May 3;85(3):357-68. doi: 10.1016/s0092-8674(00)81114-9.

Direct and long-range action of a DPP morphogen gradient.

Nellen D(1), Burke R, Struhl G, Basler K.

Author information:
(1)Zoologisches Institut, Universität Zürich, Switerland.

During development of the Drosophila wing, the decapentaplegic (dpp) gene is
expressed in a stripe of cells along the anteroposterior compartment boundary
and gives rise to a secreted protein that exerts a long-range organizing
influence on both compartments. Using clones of cells that express DPP, or in
which DPP receptor activity has been constitutively activated or abolished, we
show that DPP acts directly and at long range on responding cells, rather than
by proxy through the short-range induction of other signaling molecules.
Further, we show that two genes, optomotor-blind and spalt are transcriptionally
activated at different distances from DPP-secreting cells and provide evidence
that these genes respond to different threshold concentrations of DPP protein.
We propose that DPP acts as a gradient morphogen during wing development.
