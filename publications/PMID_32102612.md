ABSTRACT

Primary cilium-dependent macroautophagy/autophagy is induced by the urinary flow in epithelial cells of the kidney proximal tubule. A major physiological outcome of this cascade is the control of cell size. Some components of the ATG machinery are recruited at the primary cilium to generate autophagic structures. Shear stress induced by the liquid flow promotes PtdIns3P synthesis at the primary cilium, and this lipid is required both for ciliogenesis and initiation of autophagy. We showed that PtdIns3P is generated by PIK3C2A, but not by PIK3C3/VPS34, during flow-associated primary cilium-dependent autophagy, in a ULK1-independent manner. Along the same line BECN1 (beclin 1), a partner of PIK3C3 in starvation-induced autophagy, is not recruited at the primary cilium under shear stress. Thus, kidney epithelial cells mobilize different PtdIns 3-kinases, i.e ., PIK3C2A or PIK3C3, to produce PtdIns3P in order to initiate autophagy depending on the stimuli (shear stress or starvation).

Abbreviations

PtdIns3P: phosphatidylinositol-3-phosphate; PIK3C2A: class two alpha phosphatidylinositol 3-kinase; PIK3C3/VPS34: class three phosphatidylinositol 3-kinase; ATG: autophagy associated genes.

KEYWORDS: PIK3C2A, Ptdins3p, autophagy, primary cilium, shear stress

Autophagic process depends on ATG proteins engaged in different molecular modules from the phase of initiation to the closure of autophagosomes. The phosphoinositide phosphatidylinositol-3-phosphate (PtdIns3P) plays a central role to recruit the ATG12–ATG5-ATG16L1 conjugation system. This process is initiated by the PtdIns3P-binding protein WIPI2, a partner of ATG16L1. In most cases the PtdIns3P synthesis required for the early stage of autophagosome formation is ensured by the class III PtdIns 3-kinase complex (PIK3C3/VPS34, PIK3R4/VPS15, BECN1, ATG14 and NRBF2). Our previous work, and work from others, showed that autophagy is triggered by the primary cilium in response to various stimuli such as serum deprivation and shear stress. The primary cilium is a microtubule-based appendix present at the surface of many cell types. This antenna is constituted of an axoneme that faces the extracellular milieu and of a basal body. Many receptors and transporters are localized at the axoneme, and the primary cilium can also integrate mechanical forces such as shear stress induced by biological fluids. Finally, the urinary flow induces a primary cilium-dependent autophagy in kidney proximal tubule epithelial cells.

The formation of autophagosomes can be initiated at the primary cilium where many ATGs are recruited in response to starvation or shear stress. We previously showed that primary cilium-dependent autophagy is important to control cell size in response to shear stress in epithelial cells. Recently we questioned the early stage of autophagy processes in this context and we focused our attention on the generation of PtdIns3P [ 1 ]. Importantly, it was previously observed that BECN1 is not recruited at the primary cilium under shear stress and, accordingly, deletion of the corresponding gene has no effect on primary cilium-dependent autophagy and shear stress-dependent cell size regulation. Similar results were observed following PIK3C3/VPS34 deletion. However, PIK3C2A, the other kinase implicated in PtdIns3P synthesis (a class II PtdIns 3-kinase), is present at the primary cilium and contributes to ciliogenesis. Interestingly, the deletion of PIK3C2A drastically impairs primary cilium-dependent autophagy and shear stress-dependent cell size regulation. The inverse situation was observed in starved kidney epithelial cells, as deletion of PIK3C3/VPS34 blocks, as expected, starvation-induced autophagy, whereas deletion of PIK3C2A has no effect in response to this stimulus. Production of PtdIns3P by PI3KC2A at the primary cilium in response to shear stress also induces the recruitment of WIPI2 at the basal body. This and other experiments, e.g., formation of LC3-II in response to shear stress, suggest that downstream events of PtdIns3P production are identical whatever the autophagy stimulus considered. However, in contrast to starvation-induced autophagy, the ULK complex is not involved in primary cilium-dependent autophagy. Previously we have shown that the activation of AMPK and the inhibition of MTOR are elements of the signaling pathway that controls primary cilium-dependent autophagy and cell size regulation, but the link between these signaling modules and activation of PIK3C2A remains to be elucidated. Another question to be solved is how and where autophagosomes are formed in response to primary cilium-dependent autophagy. In other words, is there a phagophore assembly site close to the primary cilium? In line with this we observed that PIK3C2A is localized in a RAB11 compartment in response to shear stress. Recently RAB11-positive endosomes have been proposed to be a phagophore assembly site to recruit WIPI2 and ATG16L1, and we now show that these proteins are involved as well with PIK3C2A in primary cilium-dependent autophagy.

We therefore propose that PIK3C3/VPS34 and PIK3C2A are drafted to produce PtdIns3P during autophagy in response to different stimuli ( Figure 1 ). Thus, we can surmise that cellular adaptation to shear stress mobilizes PIK3C2A-dependent PtdIns3P synthesis without depending on PIK3C3/VPS34, which in turn is available for the robust endosomal trafficking in cells under shear stress as well for autophagic response to other stimuli, e.g., nutrient starvation.

Figure 1.

A defect in cell-size regulation is observed in proximal tubule epithelial cells in a PikI3c2a ± mouse model during our study. Interestingly, a defect in the ciliogenesis and a defect of PKD2/polycystin-2 localization at the primary cilium have been previously observed in these mice. Moreover, these animals are prone to develop kidney cysts in response to ischemia-reperfusion. Whether a defect in autophagy is an early event during cyst formation is an open question that remains to be answered. A conditional inactivation of PIK3C3/VPS34 has been recently reported in kidney proximal tubules. These animals develop a Fanconi-like syndrome, a kidney failure, and die. Thus, the proximal tubule phenotype of PIK3C3/VPS34-deficient mice is distinct from that observed in the proximal tubule of Pik3c2a ± mice. As far as autophagy is concerned, no impairment of autophagosome formation is observed in PIK3C3/VPS34-deficient mice, but rather an impairment of lysosome positioning that will finally alter the autophagic flux. These results suggest that PIK3C3/VPS34 is not required for autophagosome formation in the proximal tubule facing fluid flow. At this point it should be emphasized that the deletion of Pik3c3/Vps34 in mice has a tissue-specific effect on autophagy as reported in the literature (e.g., autophagosomes are not formed in heart and liver, but are still observed in motor neurons).

Our findings open up the question of the role of PIK3C2A in autophagy in specific conditions. PIK3C2A activity is involved in many physiological processes beyond its role in kidney physiology, by notably controlling mast cells degranulation, angiogenesis, thrombosis and systemic glucose homeostasis. Whether shear stress autophagy is a downstream effector of PIK3C2A in these activities remains to be investigated.
