# A new mechanism for repairing oxidative damage to DNA: (A)BC excinuclease removes AP sites and thymine glycols from DNA.
**Authors:** Lin JJ, Sancar A
**Journal:** Biochemistry (1989)
**DOI:** [10.1021/bi00446a002](https://doi.org/10.1021/bi00446a002)

## Abstract

1. Biochemistry. 1989 Oct 3;28(20):7979-84. doi: 10.1021/bi00446a002.

A new mechanism for repairing oxidative damage to DNA: (A)BC excinuclease
removes AP sites and thymine glycols from DNA.

Lin JJ(1), Sancar A.

Author information:
(1)Department of Biochemistry, School of Medicine, University of North Carolina,
Chapel Hill 27599.

Escherichia coli (A)BC excinuclease is the major enzyme responsible for removing
bulky adducts, such as pyrimidine dimers and 6-4 photoproducts, from DNA.
Mutants deficient in this enzyme are extremely sensitive to UV and UV-mimetic
agents, but not to oxidizing agents, or ionizing radiation which damages DNA in
part by generating active oxygen species. DNA glycosylases and AP1 endonucleases
play major roles in repairing oxidative DNA damage, and thus it has been assumed
that nucleotide excision repair has no role in cellular defense against damage
by ionizing radiation and oxidative damage. In this study we show that the E.
coli nucleotide excision repair enzyme (A)BC excinuclease removes from DNA the
two major products of oxidative damage, thymine glycol and the baseless sugar
(AP site). We conclude that nucleotide excision repair is an important cellular
defense mechanism against oxidizing agents.
