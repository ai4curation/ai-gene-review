# A distinct type of alcohol dehydrogenase, adh4+, complements ethanol fermentation in an adh1-deficient strain of Schizosaccharomyces pombe.
**Authors:** Sakurai M, Tohda H, Kumagai H, Giga-Hama Y
**Journal:** FEMS Yeast Res (2004)
**DOI:** [10.1016/j.femsyr.2003.12.009](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.femsyr.2003.12.009)

## Abstract

1. FEMS Yeast Res. 2004 Mar;4(6):649-54. doi: 10.1016/j.femsyr.2003.12.009.

A distinct type of alcohol dehydrogenase, adh4+, complements ethanol
fermentation in an adh1-deficient strain of Schizosaccharomyces pombe.

Sakurai M(1), Tohda H, Kumagai H, Giga-Hama Y.

Author information:
(1)ASPEX Division, Asahi Glass Co. Ltd., 1150 Hazawa-cho, Kanagawa-ku,
Yokohama-shi, Kanagawa 221-8755, Japan.

In the fission yeast Schizosaccharomyces pombe, only one alcohol dehydrogenase
gene, adh1(+), has been identified. To elucidate the influence of adh1(+) on
ethanol fermentation, we constructed the adh1 null strain (delta adh1). The
delta adh1 cells still produced ethanol and grew fermentatively as the wild-type
cells. Both DNA microarray and RT-PCR analysis demonstrated that this ethanol
production is caused by the enhanced expression of a Saccharomyces cerevisiae
ADH4-like gene product (SPAC5H10.06C named adh4(+)). Since the strain lacking
both adh1 and adh4 genes (delta adh1 delta adh4) showed non-fermentative
retarded growth, only these two ADHs produce ethanol for fermentative growth.
This is the first observation that a S. cerevisiae ADH4-like alcohol
dehydrogenase functions in yeast ethanol fermentation.
