# Adherens junctions and beta-catenin-mediated cell signalling in a non-metazoan organism.
**Authors:** Grimson MJ, Coates JC, Reynolds JP, Shipman M, Blanton RL, Harwood AJ
**Journal:** Nature (2000)
**DOI:** [10.1038/35047099](https://doi.org/10.1038/35047099)

## Abstract

1. Nature. 2000 Dec 7;408(6813):727-31. doi: 10.1038/35047099.

Adherens junctions and beta-catenin-mediated cell signalling in a non-metazoan
organism.

Grimson MJ(1), Coates JC, Reynolds JP, Shipman M, Blanton RL, Harwood AJ.

Author information:
(1)Dept of Biological Sciences, Texas Tech University, Lubbock 79409, USA.

Mechanical forces between cells have a principal role in the organization of
animal tissues. Adherens junctions are an important component of these tissues,
connecting cells through their actin cytoskeleton and allowing the assembly of
tensile structures. At least one adherens junction protein, beta-catenin, also
acts as a signalling molecule, directly regulating gene expression. To date,
adherens junctions have only been detected in metazoa, and therefore we looked
for them outside the animal kingdom to examine their evolutionary origins. The
non-metazoan Dictyostelium discoideum forms a multicellular, differentiated
structure. Here we describe the discovery of actin-associated intercellular
junctions in Dictyostelium. We have isolated a gene encoding a beta-catenin
homologue, aardvark, which is a component of the junctional complex, and,
independently, is required for cell signalling. Our discovery of adherens
junctions outside the animal kingdom shows that the dual role of beta-catenin in
cell-cell adhesion and cell signalling evolved before the origins of metazoa.
