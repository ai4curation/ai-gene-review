# Isolation and characterization of the Saccharomyces cerevisiae SDH4 gene encoding a membrane anchor subunit of succinate dehydrogenase.
**Authors:** Bullis BL, Lemire BD
**Journal:** J Biol Chem (1994)

## Abstract

1. J Biol Chem. 1994 Mar 4;269(9):6543-9.

Isolation and characterization of the Saccharomyces cerevisiae SDH4 gene
encoding a membrane anchor subunit of succinate dehydrogenase.

Bullis BL(1), Lemire BD.

Author information:
(1)Department of Biochemistry, University of Alberta, Edmonton, Canada.

Succinate dehydrogenase (EC 1.3.99.1) is an intrinsic bacterial or inner
mitochondrial membrane protein that catalyses the oxidation of succinate and
donates electrons to the respiratory chain via quinone acceptors. It is a
heterotetramer composed of a flavoprotein, an iron-sulfur, and two hydrophobic
subunits. We purified succinate dehydrogenase by blue native gel
electrophoresis, determined the amino-terminal sequence of the Sdh4p subunit and
used this information to clone the SDH4 gene. It encodes a precursor protein of
181 amino acids that is converted to the 150-amino acid mature Sdh4p protein
with a mass of 16,638 Da. Hydrophobicity analysis predicts that Sdh4p forms
three transmembrane alpha-helices. We have constructed an SDH4 mutant by
targeted gene disruption; it retains the ability to grow on rich glycerol
medium. Western blot analysis of SDH4 disruption mutant membrane fractions
indicates that membrane attachment of the flavoprotein and iron-sulfur subunits
is impaired but not abolished. This membrane-bound enzyme is able to reduce
ubiquinone, although less efficiently than the wild-type enzyme. These findings
indicate that Sdh4p contributes both to the membrane attachment of the catalytic
flavoprotein and iron-sulfur subunits and to electron transfer to ubiquinone.
