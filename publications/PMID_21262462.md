# Dual roles of Notch in regulation of apically restricted mitosis and apicobasal polarity of neuroepithelial cells.
**Authors:** Ohata S, Aoki R, Kinoshita S, Yamaguchi M, Tsuruoka-Kinoshita S, Tanaka H, Wada H, Watabe S, Tsuboi T, Masai I, Okamoto H
**Journal:** Neuron (2011)
**DOI:** [10.1016/j.neuron.2010.12.026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2010.12.026)

## Abstract

1. Neuron. 2011 Jan 27;69(2):215-30. doi: 10.1016/j.neuron.2010.12.026.

Dual roles of Notch in regulation of apically restricted mitosis and apicobasal
polarity of neuroepithelial cells.

Ohata S(1), Aoki R, Kinoshita S, Yamaguchi M, Tsuruoka-Kinoshita S, Tanaka H,
Wada H, Watabe S, Tsuboi T, Masai I, Okamoto H.

Author information:
(1)RIKEN Brain Science Institute, 2-1 Hirosawa, Wako, Saitama 351-0198, Japan.

How the mitosis of neuroepithelial stem cells is restricted to the apical
ventricular area remains unclear. In zebrafish, the mosaic eyes(rw306)
(moe/epb41l5(rw306)) mutation disrupts the interaction between the putative
adaptor protein Moe and the apicobasal polarity regulator Crumbs (Crb), and
impairs the maintenance of neuroepithelial apicobasal polarity. While Crb
interacts directly with Notch and inhibits its activity, Moe reverses this
inhibition. In the moe(rw306) hindbrain, Notch activity is significantly
reduced, and the number of cells that proliferate basally away from the apical
area is increased. Surprisingly, activation of Notch in the moe(rw306) mutant
rescues not only the basally localized proliferation but also the aberrant
neuroepithelial apicobasal polarity. We present evidence that the Crb⋅Moe
complex and Notch play key roles in a positive feedback loop to maintain the
apicobasal polarity and the apical-high basal-low gradient of Notch activity in
neuroepithelial cells, both of which are essential for their apically restricted
mitosis.
