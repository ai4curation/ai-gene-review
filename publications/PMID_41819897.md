The effects of different bovine-to-caprine milk ratios (B/C: 100:0, 50:50, and
0:100) on the antioxidant peptide profile and activity of cheese were analyzed,
and the mechanism of action of key peptides was elucidated. The research was
conducted using simulated gastrointestinal digestion, ultrafiltration,
nano-LC-MS/MS identification, bioinformatic screening, molecular docking, and in
vitro validation. The results showed that the antioxidant activity of all
cheeses was significantly enhanced after simulated digestion, and the activity
was primarily concentrated in the fraction with a molecular weight (MW) below
3 kDa. 18, 38, and 38 peptides identified in cheeses with B/C ratios of 100:0,
50:50, and 0:100, respectively. These peptides were mainly derived from
α-casein. Three peptides (LIRKLPFQRL, AMKPWIQPK, and LNENLLRF) were screened,
all of which exhibited good absorption potential and were predicted to be
non-toxic. Molecular docking indicated that these peptides could bind stably to
the active pocket of the Keap1 protein (with binding energies all below
-7.0 kcal/mol) and target its key residues through interactions such as hydrogen
bonding. In vitro experiments confirmed the free radical scavenging ability of
the synthesized peptides. These findings provide a theoretical basis for the
screening and development of novel, food-derived antioxidant peptides, advancing
the functional understanding of dairy-derived antioxidants.
