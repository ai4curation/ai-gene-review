# Identification of a penicillin-sensitive carboxypeptidase in the cellular slime mold Dictyostelium discoideum.
**Authors:** Yasukawa H, Kuroita T, Tamura K, Yamaguchi K
**Journal:** Biol Pharm Bull (2003)
**DOI:** [10.1248/bpb.26.1018](https://doi.org/10.1248/bpb.26.1018)

## Abstract

1. Biol Pharm Bull. 2003 Jul;26(7):1018-20. doi: 10.1248/bpb.26.1018.

Identification of a penicillin-sensitive carboxypeptidase in the cellular slime
mold Dictyostelium discoideum.

Yasukawa H(1), Kuroita T, Tamura K, Yamaguchi K.

Author information:
(1)Division of Bioengineering, Faculty of Engineering, Toyama University, Japan.
hiro@eng.toyama-u.ac.jp

Penicillin binding proteins (PBPs) are penicillin-sensitive DD-peptidases
catalyzing the terminal stages of bacterial cell wall assembly. We identified a
Dictyostelium discoideum gene that encodes a protein of 522 amino acids showing
similarity to Escherichia coli PBP4. The D. discoideum protein conserves three
consensus sequences (SXXK, SXN and KTG) that are responsible for the catalytic
activities of PBPs. The gene product prepared in the cell-free translation
system showed carboxypeptidase activity but the activity was not detected in the
presence of penicillin G. These results demonstrate that the D. discoideum gene
encodes a eukaryotic form of penicillin-sensitive carboxypeptidase.
