# Roles of cheY and cheZ gene products in controlling flagellar rotation in bacterial chemotaxis of Escherichia coli.
**Authors:** Kuo SC, Koshland DE Jr
**Journal:** J Bacteriol (1987)
**DOI:** [10.1128/jb.169.3.1307-1314.1987](https://doi.org/10.1128/jb.169.3.1307-1314.1987)

## Abstract

1. J Bacteriol. 1987 Mar;169(3):1307-14. doi: 10.1128/jb.169.3.1307-1314.1987.

Roles of cheY and cheZ gene products in controlling flagellar rotation in
bacterial chemotaxis of Escherichia coli.

Kuo SC, Koshland DE Jr.

To understand output control in bacterial chemotaxis, we varied the levels of
expression of cellular cheY and cheZ genes and found that the overproduction of
the corresponding proteins affected Escherichia coli swimming behavior. In the
absence of other signal-transducing gene products, CheY overproduction made
free-swimming cells tumble more frequently. A plot of the fraction of the
population that are tumbling versus the CheY concentration was hyperbolic, with
half of the population tumbling at 30 microM (25,000 copies per cell) CheY
monomers in the cytosol. Overproduction of aspartate receptor (Tar) by 30-fold
had a negligible effect on CheY-induced tumbling, so Tar does not sequester
CheY. CheZ overproduction decreased tumbling in all tumbling mutants except
certain flaAII(cheC) mutants. In the absence of other chemotaxis gene products,
CheZ overproduction inhibited CheY-induced tumbling. Models for CheY as a
tumbling signal and CheZ as a smooth-swimming signal to control flagellar
rotation are discussed.
