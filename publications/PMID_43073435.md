Brain injury after cardiac arrest and cardiopulmonary resuscitation (CA-CPR) is
a major cause of poor neurological outcomes among survivors, yet effective
mechanism-based interventions remain lacking. Mitophagy maintains mitochondrial
homeostasis, but whether increased mitophagy contributes to brain injury after
CA-CPR and which upstream factors regulate it remain unclear. Transcriptomic
analysis of hippocampal tissue from rats subjected to asphyxial CA-CPR
identified tribbles pseudokinase 3 (TRIB3) as one of the most strongly
upregulated genes, with its expression approximately 68-fold higher than that in
sham controls. TRIB3 was knocked down by adeno-associated virus (AAV) injection
into the hippocampal CA1 region 4 weeks before CA-CPR and by lentiviral
transduction in H19-7 cells before oxygen-glucose deprivation/reoxygenation
(OGD/R). TRIB3 knockdown improved neurological function and attenuated
hippocampal CA1 neuronal injury in CA-CPR rats. It also attenuated mitophagy,
alleviated mitochondrial dysfunction, and reduced neuronal apoptosis in vivo and
in vitro. Valproic acid partially counteracted these mitochondrial and
anti-apoptotic effects in vitro. Mechanistic analyses in OGD/R-treated cells
showed that TRIB3 knockdown increased the p-AKT/AKT and p-FOXO3a/FOXO3a ratios,
reduced FOXO3a nuclear accumulation and BNIP3 expression, and decreased FOXO3a
enrichment at the Bnip3 promoter. Co-immunoprecipitation assays showed increased
TRIB3-AKT association and reduced AKT-FOXO3a association after OGD/R. In vivo,
TIC10 treatment partially counteracted the improvements in mitochondrial
function and neurological outcomes following TRIB3 knockdown. Together, these
findings suggest that TRIB3 may act as a previously unrecognized upstream
modulator of mitophagy after CA-CPR, implicate AKT-FOXO3a-BNIP3 signaling, and
support further evaluation of TRIB3 as a potential neuroprotective target.
