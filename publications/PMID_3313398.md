Protein phosphorylation is involved in bacterial chemotaxis.

Hess JF(1), Oosawa K, Matsumura P, Simon MI.

Author information:
(1)Division of Biology, California Institute of Technology, Pasadena 91125.

The nature of the biochemical signal that is involved in the excitation response
in bacterial chemotaxis is not known. However, ATP is required for chemotaxis.
We have purified all of the proteins involved in signal transduction and show
that the product of the cheA gene is rapidly autophosphorylated, while some
mutant CheA proteins cannot be phosphorylated. The presence of stoichiometric
levels of two other purified components in the chemotaxis system, the CheY and
CheZ proteins, induces dephosphorylation. We suggest that the phosphorylation of
CheA by ATP plays a central role in signal transduction in chemotaxis.
