# Identification of a new gene (secA) and gene product involved in the secretion of envelope proteins in Escherichia coli.
**Authors:** Oliver DB, Beckwith J
**Journal:** J Bacteriol (1982)
**DOI:** [10.1128/jb.150.2.686-691.1982](https://doi.org/10.1128/jb.150.2.686-691.1982)
**PMC:** [PMC216417](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC216417/)

## Abstract

1. J Bacteriol. 1982 May;150(2):686-91. doi: 10.1128/jb.150.2.686-691.1982.

Identification of a new gene (secA) and gene product involved in the secretion
of envelope proteins in Escherichia coli.

Oliver DB, Beckwith J.

We have constructed lambda specialized transducing phages which carry an
Escherichia coli gene (secA) involved in the secretion of certain envelope
proteins. These phage have been used to show that secA is a new gene to the
clockwise side of envA. The secA mutation previously described, secA5l(Ts) (D.
B. Oliver and J. Beckwith, Cell 25:765-772, 1981), is recessive to the wild-type
allele. We have also isolated Tn5 insertions in the gene carried on the
transducing phage to further define the gene. These phage were used to infect
UV-irradiated cells to allow the identification of the secA gene product as a
92-kilodalton polypeptide and to show that transcription of secA is clockwise
relative to the E. coli genetic map.
