# Lagging strand DNA synthesis at the eukaryotic replication fork involves binding and stimulation of FEN-1 by proliferating cell nuclear antigen.
**Authors:** Li X, Li J, Harrington J, Lieber MR, Burgers PM
**Journal:** J Biol Chem (1995)
**DOI:** [10.1074/jbc.270.38.22109](https://doi.org/10.1074/jbc.270.38.22109)

## Abstract

1. J Biol Chem. 1995 Sep 22;270(38):22109-12. doi: 10.1074/jbc.270.38.22109.

Lagging strand DNA synthesis at the eukaryotic replication fork involves binding 
and stimulation of FEN-1 by proliferating cell nuclear antigen.

Li X(1), Li J, Harrington J, Lieber MR, Burgers PM.

Author information:
(1)Department of Biochemistry, Washington University School of Medicine, St. 
Louis, Missouri 63110, USA.

The 5'-->3'-exonuclease domain of Escherichia coli DNA polymerase I is required 
for the completion of lagging strand DNA synthesis, and yet this domain is not 
present in any of the eukaryotic DNA polymerases. Recently, the gene encoding 
the functional and evolutionary equivalent of this 5'-->3'-exonuclease domain 
has been identified. It is called FEN-1 in mouse and human cells and RTH1 in 
Saccharomyces cerevisiae. This 42-kDa enzyme is required for Okazaki fragment 
processing. Here we report that FEN-1 physically interacts with proliferating 
cell nuclear antigen (PCNA), the processivity factor for DNA polymerases delta 
and epsilon. Through protein-protein interactions, PCNA focuses FEN-1 on 
branched DNA substrates (flap structures) and on nicked DNA substrates, thereby 
stimulating its activity 10-50-fold but only if PCNA can functionally assemble 
as a toroidal trimer around the DNA. This interaction is important in the 
physical orchestration of lagging strand synthesis and may have implications for 
how PCNA stimulates other members of the FEN-1 nuclease family in a broad range 
of DNA metabolic transactions.
