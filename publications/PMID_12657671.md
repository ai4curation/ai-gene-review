# The EGL-21 carboxypeptidase E facilitates acetylcholine release at Caenorhabditis elegans neuromuscular junctions.
**Authors:** Jacob TC, Kaplan JM
**Journal:** J Neurosci (2003)
**DOI:** [10.1523/JNEUROSCI.23-06-02122.2003](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.23-06-02122.2003)

## Abstract

1. J Neurosci. 2003 Mar 15;23(6):2122-30. doi:
10.1523/JNEUROSCI.23-06-02122.2003.

The EGL-21 carboxypeptidase E facilitates acetylcholine release at
Caenorhabditis elegans neuromuscular junctions.

Jacob TC(1), Kaplan JM.

Author information:
(1)Department of Molecular and Cell Biology, University of California Berkeley,
Berkeley, California 94720, USA.

Proneuropeptides are packaged into dense-core vesicles in which they are
processed into active peptides by copackaged enzymes. Proprotein convertases
(PCs) cleave precursors after dibasic residues, and carboxypeptidases remove
basic residues from the C terminals. We show here that the Caenorhabditis
elegans egl-21 gene encodes a protein that is very similar to carboxypeptidase E
(CPE) and is broadly expressed in the nervous system. Mutants lacking either
egl-21 CPE or egl-3, which encodes the C. elegans ortholog of PC type 2 (PC2),
were defective for processing endogenously expressed FMRFamide
(Phe-Met-Arg-Phe-NH2)-related peptides (FaRPs). Mutants lacking the unc-104
kinesin motor protein were defective for anterograde movement of dense-core
vesicle components, including egl-3 PC2, egl-21 CPE, and FaRPs. We provide
evidence that egl-3 PC2 and egl-21 CPE mutants have diminished acetylcholine
release at neuromuscular junctions (NMJs). Taken together, these results suggest
that egl-21 CPE and egl-3 PC2 process endogenous neuropeptides that facilitate
acetylcholine release at C. elegans NMJs.
