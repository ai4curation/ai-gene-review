# Short-range and long-range guidance by Slit and its Robo receptors: a combinatorial code of Robo receptors controls lateral position.
**Authors:** Simpson JH, Bland KS, Fetter RD, Goodman CS
**Journal:** Cell (2000)
**DOI:** [10.1016/s0092-8674(00)00206-3](https://doi.org/10.1016/s0092-8674(00)00206-3)

## Abstract

1. Cell. 2000 Dec 22;103(7):1019-32. doi: 10.1016/s0092-8674(00)00206-3.

Short-range and long-range guidance by Slit and its Robo receptors: a
combinatorial code of Robo receptors controls lateral position.

Simpson JH(1), Bland KS, Fetter RD, Goodman CS.

Author information:
(1)Howard Hughes Medical Institute, Division of Neurobiology, Department of
Molecular and Cell Biology, 519 LSA, University of California, Berkeley CA
94720, USA.

Slit is secreted by midline glia in Drosophila and functions as a short-range
repellent to control midline crossing. Although most Slit stays near the
midline, some diffuses laterally, functioning as a long-range chemorepellent.
Here we show that a combinatorial code of Robo receptors controls lateral
position in the CNS by responding to this presumptive Slit gradient. Medial
axons express only Robo, intermediate axons express Robo3 and Robo, while
lateral axons express Robo2, Robo3, and Robo. Removal of robo2 or robo3 causes
lateral axons to extend medially; ectopic expression of Robo2 or Robo3 on medial
axons drives them laterally. Precise topography of longitudinal pathways appears
to be controlled by a combination of long-range guidance (the Robo code
determining region) and short-range guidance (discrete local cues determining
specific location within a region).
