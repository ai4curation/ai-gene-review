# Expression of cDNA encoding the human "protective protein" associated with lysosomal beta-galactosidase and neuraminidase: homology to yeast proteases.
**Authors:** Galjart NJ, Gillemans N, Harris A, van der Horst GT, Verheijen FW, Galjaard H, d'Azzo A
**Journal:** Cell (1988)
**DOI:** [10.1016/s0092-8674(88)90999-3](https://doi.org/10.1016/s0092-8674(88)90999-3)

## Abstract

1. Cell. 1988 Sep 9;54(6):755-64. doi: 10.1016/s0092-8674(88)90999-3.

Expression of cDNA encoding the human "protective protein" associated with
lysosomal beta-galactosidase and neuraminidase: homology to yeast proteases.

Galjart NJ(1), Gillemans N, Harris A, van der Horst GT, Verheijen FW, Galjaard
H, d'Azzo A.

Author information:
(1)Department of Cell Biology and Genetics, Erasmus University, Rotterdam, The
Netherlands.

The "protective protein" is a glycoprotein that associates with lysosomal
beta-galactosidase and neuraminidase and is deficient in the autosomal recessive
disorder galactosialidosis. We have isolated the cDNA encoding human "protective
protein". The clone recognizes a 2 kb mRNA in normal cells that is not evident
in fibroblasts of an early infantile galactosialidosis patient. The cDNA directs
the synthesis of a 452 amino acid precursor molecule that is processed in vivo
to yield mature "protective protein," a heterodimer of 32 kd and 20 kd
polypeptides held together by disulfide bridges. This mature form is also
biologically functional since it restores beta-galactosidase and neuraminidase
activities in galactosialidosis cells. The predicted amino acid sequence of the
"protective protein" bears homology to yeast carboxypeptidase Y and the KEX1
gene product. This suggests a protease activity for the "protective protein."
