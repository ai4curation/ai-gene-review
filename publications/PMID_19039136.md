# A genetic framework for the control of cell division and differentiation in the root meristem.

Plant growth and development are sustained by meristems. Meristem activity is
controlled by auxin and cytokinin, two hormones whose interactions in
determining a specific developmental output are still poorly understood. By
means of a comprehensive genetic and molecular analysis in Arabidopsis, we show
that a primary cytokinin-response transcription factor, ARR1, activates the gene
SHY2/IAA3 (SHY2), a repressor of auxin signaling that negatively regulates the
PIN auxin transport facilitator genes: thereby, cytokinin causes auxin
redistribution, prompting cell differentiation. Conversely, auxin mediates
degradation of the SHY2 protein, sustaining PIN activities and cell division.
Thus, the cell differentiation and division balance necessary for controlling
root meristem size and root growth is the result of the interaction between
cytokinin and auxin through a simple regulatory circuit converging on the SHY2
gene.
