HMG-CoA reductase (Hmgcr) is the rate-limiting enzyme in the mevalonate pathway
and is inhibited by statins. In addition to cholesterol, Hmgcr activity is also
required for synthesizing nonsterol isoprenoids, such as dolichol, ubiquinone,
and farnesylated and geranylgeranylated proteins. Here, we investigated the
effects of Hmgcr inhibition on nonsterol isoprenoids in the liver. We have
generated new genetic models to acutely delete genes in the mevalonate pathway
in the liver using AAV-mediated delivery of Cre-recombinase (AAV-Cre) or
CRISPR/Cas9 (AAV-CRISPR). The genetic deletion of Hmgcr by AAV-Cre resulted in
extensive hepatocyte apoptosis and compensatory liver regeneration. At the
biochemical level, we observed decreased levels of sterols and depletion of the
nonsterol isoprenoids, dolichol and ubiquinone. At the cellular level,
Hmgcr-null hepatocytes showed ER stress and impaired N-glycosylation. We further
hypothesized that the depletion of dolichol, essential for N-glycosylation,
could be responsible for ER stress. Using AAV-CRISPR, we somatically disrupted
dehydrodolichyl diphosphate synthase subunit (Dhdds), encoding a branch point
enzyme required for dolichol biosynthesis. Dhdds-null livers showed ER stress
and impaired N-glycosylation, along with apoptosis and regeneration. Finally,
the combined deletion of Hmgcr and Dhdds synergistically exacerbated hepatocyte
ER stress. Our data show a critical role for mevalonate-derived dolichol in the
liver and suggest that dolichol depletion is at least partially responsible for
ER stress and apoptosis upon potent Hmgcr inhibition.
