# Non-polarized distribution of synaptotagmin IV in neurons: evidence that synaptotagmin IV is not a synaptic vesicle protein.
**Authors:** Ibata K, Hashikawa T, Tsuboi T, Terakawa S, Liang F, Mizutani A, Fukuda M, Mikoshiba K
**Journal:** Neurosci Res (2002)
**DOI:** [10.1016/s0168-0102(02)00066-4](https://doi.org/10.1016/s0168-0102(02)00066-4)

## Abstract

1. Neurosci Res. 2002 Aug;43(4):401-6. doi: 10.1016/s0168-0102(02)00066-4.

Non-polarized distribution of synaptotagmin IV in neurons: evidence that
synaptotagmin IV is not a synaptic vesicle protein.

Ibata K(1), Hashikawa T, Tsuboi T, Terakawa S, Liang F, Mizutani A, Fukuda M,
Mikoshiba K.

Author information:
(1)Laboratory for Developmental Neurobiology, Brain Science Institute, RIKEN
(The Institute of Physical and Chemical Research), 2-1 Hirosawa, Wako, Saitama
351-0198, Japan.

Synaptotagmin IV (Syt IV) expression is regulated by neuronal development and by
depolarization in the brain and in neuronal cell cultures. In cultures,
immunocytochemical analysis has shown that Syt IV is localized at the Golgi and
at the tips of growing neurites, but little was known about associations between
Syt IV and vesicles or organelles [J. Neurochem. 74 (2000) 518]. In this study
we performed an electron microscopic (EM) analysis of developing mouse neocortex
to determine the exact localization of Syt IV in native mouse tissues. In
neurons of layer II/III, Syt IV was found to be localized in the dendrites and
axons, and at the Golgi in the cell body. Some Syt IV signals were clearly
associated with vesicles and/or organelles, but EM and cell fractionation
studies showed no Syt IV signals at synaptic vesicles. Detection of fluorescence
protein-tagged Syt IV (Syt IV-EGFP) in hippocampal neurons also showed the
presence of Syt IV-EGFP vesicles or organelles in the axons and dendrites. These
results suggest that Syt IV regulates non-polarized membrane trafficking in
neurons, which may be involved in synaptic plasticity or neuronal development.
