BACKGROUND: Alcohol-associated liver disease (ALD) is characterized by
progressive liver injury, which is influenced by aberrant oxidative stress,
ferroptosis, and sphingolipid metabolism. Although the inhibition of hepatic
ceramide production has shown promise in ALD management, the specific roles of
ceramide kinase (CERK) and its metabolite ceramide-1-phosphate in ALD
pathogenesis and ferroptosis have not been fully explored.
METHODS: To investigate the role of CERK in ALD, we established an
alcohol-induced liver injury model using the Gao-binge protocol in C57BL/6
wild-type, Cerk knockout, and Cerk overexpression mice. The molecular mechanisms
underlying CERK-regulated ALD were identified and characterized through liver
transcriptomics and pharmacological inhibition of CERK.
RESULTS: Alcohol consumption significantly increased the expression of CERK in
the liver. Functional studies demonstrated that CERK mitigates alcohol-induced
liver injury by attenuating oxidative stress and ferroptosis both in vivo and in
vitro. Mechanistically, these effects were mediated through the activation of
heat shock protein beta-1 via the p38 mitogen-activated protein kinase signaling
pathway, highlighting HSPB1's role in suppressing oxidative stress and
ferroptosis.
CONCLUSIONS: Our findings indicated that enhancing CERK expression or activity
may represent a novel therapeutic strategy for mitigating alcohol-induced liver
injury. This approach could offer a promising avenue for treating ALD and its
associated ferroptotic damage.

INTRODUCTION

Alcohol-associated liver disease (ALD) remains a major global health issue, affecting millions of individuals worldwide.1 It is one of the leading causes of chronic liver disease, with increasing prevalence, particularly as alcohol consumption continues to rise in many regions.234 The progression of ALD typically follows a distinct pathological trajectory, starting with simple steatosis, advancing to steatohepatitis, then fibrosis, cirrhosis, and ultimately HCC in severe cases.5 The global burden of ALD represents a major contributor to liver-related morbidity and mortality. Recent studies suggest that ALD has an intricate and multifaceted pathogenesis, with cell-to-cell and organ-to-organ crosstalk playing a significant role in its development.6 Despite progress in understanding its underlying mechanisms, effective treatments for ALD remain limited, with current management focused primarily on lifestyle changes such as alcohol abstinence and nutritional support. Pharmacological therapies are still in the experimental phase, and no standard treatment exists to halt or reverse disease progression. This therapeutic gap highlights the urgent need for further research to uncover the molecular mechanisms underlying ALD and identify novel therapeutic targets.

Recent research has highlighted the importance of ferroptosis, a form of iron-dependent programmed cell death, in the pathogenesis of ALD.789 Ferroptosis is characterized by the excessive accumulation of iron, oxidative stress, and lipid peroxidation, ultimately leading to cellular damage and death.10 In the context of ALD, alcohol metabolism generates an overproduction of reactive oxygen species, which exacerbates oxidative stress in liver cells, promotes hepatocyte injury, and potentially drives the progression from steatosis to more severe forms of liver damage.1112 Ferroptosis has thus emerged as a key player in ALD pathology; however, the detailed mechanisms by which it contributes to the disease remain incompletely understood.

In parallel, sphingolipid metabolism, particularly the roles of ceramide and its metabolites, has gained increasing attention in liver disease research. Ceramide, a key sphingolipid molecule, plays a critical role in regulating cellular stress responses and has been implicated in various pathological conditions.13 Within the sphingolipid metabolic pathway, ceramide kinase (CERK) phosphorylates ceramide to generate ceramide-1-phosphate (C1P), a metabolite with emerging significance in cell survival, inflammation, and stress response.141516 Notably, aberrant accumulation of ceramide and its associated metabolites has been shown to contribute to hepatic injury in ALD.1718 However, the specific roles of CERK and C1P in modulating ferroptosis and oxidative stress in ALD remain largely unexplored.

In this study, we aimed to investigate the role of CERK in ALD. We hypothesized that CERK functions as a crucial regulator of oxidative stress and ferroptosis, activating the p38 mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK) signaling pathway and heat shock protein beta-1 (HSPB1),19 a well-known negative regulator of ferroptosis.20212223 By elucidating this signaling cascade, we sought to provide novel insights into the molecular mechanisms driving ALD and identify the CERK–p38 MAPK–HSPB1 axis as a potential therapeutic target for preventing or mitigating liver damage in this widespread and debilitating disease.

CERK reduces alcohol-induced liver ferroptosis via the p38 MAPK–HSPB1 pathway

Given that CERK-mediated C1P can activate the p38 MAPK pathway,343536 which is itself responsive to cellular stress,3738 and that p38 MAPK activation has been shown to induce HSPB139—a known inhibitor of oxidative stress and ferroptosis2023404142—we hypothesized that CERK/C1P alleviates alcohol-induced liver ferroptosis by activating the p38 MAPK/HSPB1 signaling cascade. To test this hypothesis, we evaluated the expression and phosphorylation of p38 MAPK and HSPB1 in liver cells following Cerk-OE or knockdown, both in vivo and in vitro. Cerk-OE increased p38 MAPK phosphorylation, while Cerk knockdown reduced it (Figure 6A and Supplemental Figures S4A, B, http://links.lww.com/HC9/C14), indicating that CERK activates p38 MAPK. Consistent with this, Cerk-OE enhanced the expression of HSPB1 and its phosphorylated form (p-HSPB1), whereas Cerk knockdown decreased both HSPB1 and p-HSPB1 levels (Figure 6B and Supplemental Figures S4A, B, http://links.lww.com/HC9/C14). These results suggest that CERK may reduce ALD-induced oxidative stress and ferroptosis by positively regulating the p38 MAPK–HSPB1 signaling axis.

Given the established roles of HSPB1 in protecting against oxidative stress and regulating iron metabolism,202340 we further explored its relevance in alcohol-associated hepatitis using the GSE28619 and GSE155926 patient datasets. Analysis of these datasets revealed a positive correlation between HSPB1 expression and the expression of multiple ferroptosis-protective genes, as well as a negative correlation with several ferroptosis-inducing genes (Figure 6C). To assess the role of C1P produced by CERK in activating this pathway, we treated AML12 cells with NVP-231. Western blot analysis showed that NVP-231 treatment significantly reduced the phosphorylation levels of p38 MAPK and HSPB1 in AML12 cells (Figure 6D), confirming that CERK-generated C1P is involved in the activation of the p38 MAPK/HSPB1 pathway.

Next, to directly investigate the functional relevance of p38 MAPK in ferroptosis, we used SB203580, a selective inhibitor of p38 MAPK. The treatment of AML12 cells with 10 μM SB203580 significantly reduced the levels of p-p38, HSPB1, and p-HSPB1 (Figure 6E). Excessive ceramide levels are strongly associated with hepatocyte apoptosis, inflammatory responses, and oxidative stress. In contrast, CERK-generated C1P exhibits pro-survival, anti-inflammatory, and anti-oxidative properties.1415164546 Our findings highlight the duality of sphingolipid metabolism in ALD, suggesting that while ceramide exacerbates liver injury, CERK/C1P provides a protective balance that mitigates alcohol-induced ferroptosis.Sphingolipid metabolism is orchestrated by several key enzymes, including ceramide synthase (CerS), sphingosine kinase (SphK), and glucosylceramide synthase (UGCG).13 In ALD, excessive alcohol consumption disrupts the delicate balance of this pathway.1747 Increased ceramide synthesis by CerS promotes hepatocyte apoptosis, inflammation, and oxidative stress,4448 thereby accelerating the progression of liver injury.262728 Conversely, CERK-mediated conversion of ceramide to C1P is hepatoprotective, promoting cell survival by modulating oxidative stress and inflammation. Understanding the metabolic flux of ceramide and C1P in vivo could also shed light on their roles at different stages of ALD, enabling the development of stage-specific therapeutic interventions.
