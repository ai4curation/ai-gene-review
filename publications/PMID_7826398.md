# Identification of a brain- and reproductive-organs-specific gene responsive to DNA damage and retinoic acid.
**Authors:** Li L, Yoo H, Becker FF, Ali-Osman F, Chan JY
**Journal:** Biochem Biophys Res Commun (1995)
**DOI:** [10.1006/bbrc.1995.1108](https://doi.org/10.1006/bbrc.1995.1108)

## Abstract

1. Biochem Biophys Res Commun. 1995 Jan 17;206(2):764-74. doi:
10.1006/bbrc.1995.1108.

Identification of a brain- and reproductive-organs-specific gene responsive to
DNA damage and retinoic acid.

Li L(1), Yoo H, Becker FF, Ali-Osman F, Chan JY.

Author information:
(1)Department of Experimental Pediatrics, University of Texas M.D. Anderson
Cancer Center, Houston 77030.

We have identified and sequenced a new gene from human cells that is responsive
to DNA damage and retinoic acid treatment, and it is highly expressed in brain
and reproductive organs (BRE). This BRE gene encodes an mRNA of 1.7-1.9 kb, with
an open reading frame of 1,149 bp, and gives rise to a deduced polypeptide of
383 amino acid residues. Treatment of fibroblast cell with UV and
4-nitroquinoline-1-oxide caused more than 90% and 50% decreases in BRE mRNA,
respectively. Similar decreases in BRE expression were observed in RA-treatment
of the brain glioma cell U-251 and the promyelocytic cell HL-60. Decrease in BRE
mRNA was also observed in a squamous carcinoma cell, 1483, that showed X-ray
resistance and has a more aggressive tumorigenic phenotype, but BRE expression
was unchanged in cells after growth inhibition. These data indicate that BRE is
a house-keeping gene and it may play a role in homeostatis or in certain
pathways of differentiation in cells of neural, epithelial and germ line
origins.
