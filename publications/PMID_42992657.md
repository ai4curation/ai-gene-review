# Quaternary structure and activity of glutamate dehydrogenase are regulated by reversible S-palmitoylation and mitochondrial acyl-protein thioesterases.
**Authors:** Salsaa M, Huynh HTA, Dixon CL, St-Germain J, Zein HS, Potvin EM, Langelaan DN, Raught B, Fairn GD

Glutamate dehydrogenase (GDH) is a mitochondrial enzyme that links amino acid
and carbohydrate metabolism through the reversible conversion of glutamate to
α-ketoglutarate. GDH functions as a hexamer and is subject to extensive
allosteric regulation. Here, we demonstrate that GDH undergoes
auto-palmitoylation in the presence of palmitoyl-CoA, accompanied by
concentration-dependent inhibition of both reductive amination and oxidative
deamination. Acyl-PEG exchange assays indicated predominantly one palmitoyl
modification per GDH subunit, while mass spectrometry identified Cys55, Cys115,
and Cys197 as putative modification sites. Blue native PAGE revealed that
palmitoylation promotes dissociation of GDH hexamers into species consistent
with dimers. Treatment with acyl-protein thioesterase 1 (APT1) restored both
hexameric assembly and enzymatic activity, whereas α/β-hydrolase
domain-containing protein 10 (ABHD10) restored activity less efficiently under
the conditions tested. Acyl-resin-assisted capture also detected endogenous
S-acylated GDH in three human cell lines, and treatment with the APT1 inhibitor
ML348 increased GDH S-acylation in HepG2 cells. Together, these findings
identify reversible S-palmitoylation as a mechanism regulating GDH assembly and
activity, with the potential to couple mitochondrial lipid metabolism to amino
acid catabolism.
