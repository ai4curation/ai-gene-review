Cytochrome P450 2U1 (CYP2U1) is an extrahepatic monooxygenase that metabolizes
both endogenous fatty acids and xenobiotic substrates. Because of its high
expression in both healthy and cancerous thymus tissues, this study investigated
CYP2U1-mediated biotransformation of 2 tumor-targeting tyrosine kinase
inhibitors, sorafenib and sunitinib, both are commonly prescribed for thymus
cancers. Recombinantly expressed CYP2U1 and CYP2D6 were incorporated into
nanodiscs, and their metabolism of sorafenib and sunitinib was investigated.
Liquid chromatography-tandem mass spectrometry metabolite profiling revealed
that both CYP2U1 and CYP2D6 nanodiscs oxidized sorafenib to generate sorafenib
N-oxide and both enzymes catalyzed the dealkylation of sunitinib to form
N-desethyl sunitinib. Spectroscopic studies (UV-Visible and fluorescence)
confirmed favorable binding interactions of CYP2U1 and CYP2D6 to both drugs
tested. Molecular dynamics simulations demonstrated binding of sorafenib and
sunitinib in the CYP2U1 active site and identified key interactions between the
drug and key residues at the enzyme's active site. Rates of metabolite formation
were quantified by targeted metabolomics, and inhibition of tyrosine kinase was
assessed by ELISA assay. Both the parent compound sorafenib and metabolite
sorafenib N-oxide showed similar inhibitory effects on cellular migration in
HepG2 cells. The metabolite sorafenib N-oxide was approximately twice as potent
as the parent compound in inhibiting cancer cell migration. In contrast,
N-desethyl sunitinib failed to show similar extent of inhibition. Together,
these findings highlight the potential role of extrahepatic CYP2U1 in the local
metabolism of tyrosine kinase inhibitors and suggest that CYP2U1-mediated
transformations directly influence antitumor efficacy at thymic tumor sites.
SIGNIFICANCE STATEMENT: Understanding the interactions between cytochrome P450
2U1 and cytochrome P450 2D6 in nanodiscs and thymus tumor-targeting drugs,
sorafenib and sunitinib, led to discovery of new bioactive metabolites that
carry differential anticancer properties compared with their parent compounds.

Despite previous metabolism studies on the enzyme, there has been little understanding toward CYP2U1’s role in local drug metabolism in relation to thymus-targeting drugs. Excretion of sorafenib via urination is also carried out as its glucuronidated products.24 Upon an oral dose of 50 mg, sunitinib reaches its peak plasma level of 30.2 ng/mL at ∼6–12 hours post dose, whereas its metabolite N-desethyl sunitinib reaches its maximum plasma level of 3.4 ng/mL at the same time point.25,26 Pharmacokinetics profiles also indicate that sunitinib has a half-life of ∼41–86 hours in phase I study,27 whereas the metabolite of interest N-desethyl sunitinib has the half-life of 80–100 hours.28 Differing from sunitinib’s usage in both thymoma and thymic carcinoma, sorafenib is primarily used to treat relapsed thymic carcinoma.29 The drug was found to have the half-life of ∼25–48 hours30 and a median plasma concentration of 1001.3 ng/mL after 1 hour post dosage (800 mg/d), whereas its N-oxide metabolite has the plasma level of 110 ng/mL.31 Both sorafenib and sunitinib are known to be metabolized by hepatic CYP3A4,32 while the latter can also be metabolized by CYP1A2 and CYP2D6, producing quinoneimine.33

Phylogenetically, CYP2U1 shares the highest similarity with CYP2R1, followed by CYP2D6.7,34 Amino acid sequence comparison shows that the common motifs shared among multiple CYP2s are also present in CYP2U1 (Supplemental Table 1). Unlike CYP2U1, CYP2D6 is a well characterized CYP that is highly expressed in both the liver and the brain and is known to metabolize a wide range of commercially available drugs, including antidepressants,35 anticancer drugs,36 and antipsychotics.37 Despite differences in their tissue expressions, CYP2U1 and CYP2D6 share similar metabolic patterns because of their high phylogenetic and amino acid sequence similarity. Such patterns shed lights on how similar metabolites generated via local metabolism might affect the systematic metabolism in the downstream pharmacokinetic processes.

Herein, this study investigated the metabolism of sorafenib and sunitinib by CYP2U1 and CYP2D6 in nanodiscs, and the results showed that the 2 enzymes yielded similar metabolism reactions but differed in their respective metabolic rates. To execute this study, in vitro metabolism studies were performed using recombinantly expressed CYP2U1 and CYP2D6 incorporated into lipid nanodiscs for enhanced stability. Nanodiscs38 are discoidal lipid bilayers that are stabilized by the presence of an engineered membrane scaffolding protein (MSP), and have been previously used as a method to study membrane proteins in vitro with enhanced stability.39 To further characterize CYP2U1’s interaction with sunitinib and sorafenib, molecular dynamics simulations were used to identify the binding poses of the 2 drugs within the active site, revealing several key protein residues that contribute to their binding and subsequent metabolism. The heme was integrated into the CYP2U1 model by first aligning the iron-coordinated cysteine (C490 in CYP2U1 and C443 in CYP2D6) and its adjacent residues. Next, the coordinates of the heme group and an associated iron-coordinated water molecule were transferred into the model. Covalent bonds were defined between the heme iron as well as the sulfur of C490 and the oxygen of the coordinated water to maintain the planarity of the porphyrin ring. Finally, the complete protein-membrane system was solvated and neutralized with 0.15 M NaCl.To fully equilibrate the assembled system, an initial 10,000 steps of energy minimization using the steepest descent algorithm was performed, followed by multistep equilibration with gradually reduced harmonic restraints.54 The process began with a 1-ns simulation in a constant number of particles, volume, and temperature ensemble, during which only the phospholipid tails were unrestrained, allowing them to melt. This was followed by a 10-ns phase where all components excluding the protein heavy atoms were unrestrained in a constant number of particles, pressure, and temperature ensemble. Subsequently, an additional 10-ns simulation was performed to allow all components except the protein backbone to move freely under an NPT ensemble. A force constant of 10 kcal/mol/Å2 was applied to the restrained atoms in all equilibration steps. Finally, all restraints were removed, and the system was subjected to a 2-μs production run, which was used for subsequent docking studies.2.14Template-based induced-fit docking of sorafenib and sunitinib to CYP2U1 active siteTo characterize the substrate binding site, Fpocket55 was initially applied to an ensemble of 2000 apo protein conformations at 1-ns interval sampled from the 2-μs equilibrium simulation to identify all existing pockets in CYP2U1. The active site pocket providing direct access to the heme group was selected for further analysis. To determine an optimal protein conformation for accommodating substrates in the active site, the volume of this heme-adjacent pocket was calculated across the entire trajectory by Fpocket.55 The protein conformation at 1381 ns exhibited the largest volume of the heme-adjacent pocket and was therefore chosen as the receptor structure for all subsequent docking studies.To account for protein conformational flexibility upon substrate binding, induced-fit docking was implemented using Molecular Operating Environment (2024.0601 Chemical Computing Group ULC). The placement method of docking was template-guided toward the catalytically productive orientations of substrates by using heme-bound compounds with structurally similar functional group to either sorafenib or sunitinib in existing experimentally resolved structures of a generic paralog of CYP2U1 and CYP2D6, as references. Accordingly, for sorafenib N-oxide, where metabolism occurs at the pyridine nitrogen, the heme-bound prinomastat from the crystal structure of CYP2D6 (PDB ID: 6CSB) was used as the template (https://doi.org/10.2210/pdb6CSB/pdb). For N-desethyl sunitinib, where the reaction occurs at the terminal amine nitrogen, the heme-bound HEGA-10 structure in CYP2D6 (PDB ID: 6CSD) served as the template (https://doi.org/10.2210/pdb6CSB/pdb). The structures of sorafenib and sunitinib were prepared by Molecular Operating Environment, and only the Z-isomer of sunitinib was used for docking.In the template-based induced-fit docking workflow, 30 initial placements were generated based on the position of the template compound by triangle matcher method. These initial poses were refined by induced-fit refinement and were subsequently scored using the GBVI/WSA dG scoring function.56,57 We saved the top 20 docking poses for each drug.Since the docking scores were comparable, the final poses for subsequent equilibrium simulations were selected based on the proximity between the substrate’s site of metabolism and the iron-coordinated water oxygen. For sorafenib, this resulted in the selection of 2 poses poised for N-oxidation (binding mode 1: score of −8.58 kcal/mol, root-mean-square deviation [RMSD] of 3.07 Å, and Npyridine–Owater distance of 3.87 Å; binding mode 2: score of −8.32 kcal/mol, RMSD of 2.33 Å, and Npyridine–Owater distance of 4.05 Å). For sunitinib, one pose was selected for N-desethylation (binding mode 1: score of −8.30 kcal/mol, RMSD of 2.20 Å, and Namine–Owater distance of 3.81 Å), and the second was chosen for its correspondence to a previously described defluorination pathway58 (binding mode 2: score of −8.21 kcal/mol, RMSD of 3.79 Å, and Cfluorine–Owater distance of 3.63 Å). These 4 selected poses were subjected to further equilibrium simulations.2.15Equilibrium simulations with docked sorafenib and sunitinibFor each selected pose, the simulation system was constructed by combining the docked substrate with the protein, heme, membrane, water, and ions from the 1381 ns snapshot of the initial equilibrium simulation with apo protein. Water molecules in the active site were removed to prevent steric clashes with the docked substrate. To relax the protein residues around the substrate, each system underwent a 2-step equilibration. Initially, the heavy atoms of the docked substrate and the protein backbone were restrained with a force constant of 10 kcal/mol/Å2 while relaxing everything else for 10 ns. Subsequently, all restraints were removed, and the simulation continued for an additional 15 ns. Throughout both equilibration steps, an extra bond with a force constant of 10 kcal/mol/Å2 was applied between the substrate’s metabolic site and the heme’s iron-coordinated water oxygen to preserve the initial docked distance, thus maintaining the substrate’s position within the active site. Each of the 4 selected poses was prepared in triplicates. After the equilibration, each replica was simulated for 1 μs.2.16Simulation protocolAll simulations were performed using the NAMD3 engine.59,60 CHARMM36m and CHARMM36 force fields were used for the protein and lipids, respectively,61,62 whereas water molecules were represented by the TIP3P model.63 Parameters for sorafenib and sunitinib were generated using the CGenFF web server by SilcsBio (https://cgenff.com/).64,65 Parameters for the covalent bonds between the heme iron and the sulfur of cysteine or the oxygen of water were adapted from existing CHARMM patches for heme-thiolate and heme-tyrosine linkages. Temperature was maintained at 310 K using a Langevin thermostat with a damping coefficient of γ = 1 ps−1, whereas pressure control was achieved with the Nosé–Hoover piston method set at 1 bar.66,67 Electrostatic interactions were calculated without truncation within periodic boundary conditions using the particle mesh Ewald method.68 Nonbonded interactions were computed with a cutoff distance of 12 Å and a switching distance of 10 Å. A flexible cell was enabled, allowing the system dimensions to change independently while maintaining a constant x/y ratio for the membrane. A 2-fs integration timestep was used, with covalent bonds involving hydrogen atoms constrained by the SHAKE69 and SETTLE70 algorithms.2.17AnalysisBefore protein–substrate interaction analysis, the conformational ensemble for each selected pose, comprising 3000 frames gathered from 3 independent 1-μs replicas (1 frame/ns), was clustered based on the RMSD of the substrate’s heavy atoms using a distance cutoff of 3 Å by the Clustering plugin in visual molecular dynamics.71 The most populated (predominant) cluster for each binding mode was then used for contact analysis to characterize the protein residues contributing to the substrate association. And a contact was defined as when any protein heavy atom is within 4 Å of a substrate heavy atom. A representative snapshot for each predominant cluster was identified using a scoring method that considers both the substrate’s RMSD (R) with respect to the initial frame and the interaction energy (E) consisting of electrostatics and van der Waals interactions between protein and substrate calculated by the NAMD Energy Plugin in visual molecular dynamics.71 Both R and E were rescaled to a range of [0,1], and each conformation was scored according to the equation S=R2+E2. The conformation with the minimum score was chosen as the representative snapshot for visualization of a binding mode.In accordance with journal guidelines on data robustness, we note that this investigation was exploratory and not designed around a prespecified statistical null hypothesis.

2.7In vitro metabolism of sorafenib and sunitinib by CYP2U1/CYP2D6 in nanodiscsIn vitro metabolism was done to quantify the metabolism of sorafenib and sunitinib by CYP2U1 and CYP2D6 in nanodiscs. A flexible cell was enabled, allowing the system dimensions to change independently while maintaining a constant x/y ratio for the membrane. 3.7CYP2U1 and CYP2D6 metabolism of sunitinib was quantified on HPLC-UVThe rates of sunitinib metabolism by CYP2U1 and CYP2D6 were quantified using an HPLC coupled UV-VIS detection method. Sunitinib has UV-Vis absorbance peaks close to 430 and 267 nm, whereas N-desethyl sunitinib has absorbance peaks at 432 and 268 nm.79 The metabolites were quantified using standard curves and areas under the curve, based on their distinct absorbance peaks and elution times on the HPLC (Supplemental Fig. 11). Both sunitinib (retention time = 17.5 and 25.5 minutes) and N-desethyl sunitinib (retention time = 15.5 and 23.5 minutes) standards showed 2 major peaks in the respective wavelengths (Supplemental Fig. 11, E and F). The average KM for CYP2U1-ND and CYP2D6-ND mediated metabolism of sunitinib are 37 ± 6 μM and 43 ± 9 μM, respectively. Vmax for CYP2U1-ND and CYP2D6-ND mediated metabolism are 75 and 309 pmol/min/nmol (pmol of metabolite per minute per nmol of CYP).

4DiscussionThis study presents the metabolism of 2 tumor-targeting drugs, sunitinib and sorafenib, mediated by recombinant CYP2U1 and CYP2D6 incorporated into nanodiscs. In regard to endogenous substrates, CYP2U1 has previously been shown to act as a hydroxylase to long-chain polyunsaturated fatty acids and as an oxidase to lipidated serotonin.7,9 Besides its previous characterization in lipid metabolism, little is known about other biological and xenobiotic ligands of CYP2U1. Although CYP2U1 is showed high transcriptional levels in the thymus, its endogenous biochemical role in this tissue remains poorly understood. Given that other extrahepatic CYP enzymes are known to mediate the metabolism of both endogenous ligands and xenobiotics, CYP2U1 may play an important role in shaping the pharmacology and pharmacokinetics of thymus-targeting drugs. Herein, this study demonstrates that CYP2U1 has a role in drug metabolism of the thymus-targeting drugs sunitinib and sorafenib, functioning as an oxidase for sorafenib and a dealkylase for sunitinib.
