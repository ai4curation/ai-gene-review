# Requirement of human renal water channel aquaporin-2 for vasopressin-dependent concentration of urine.
**Authors:** Deen PM, Verdijk MA, Knoers NV, Wieringa B, Monnens LA, van Os CH, van Oost BA
**Journal:** Science (1994)
**DOI:** [10.1126/science.8140421](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.8140421)

## Abstract

1. Science. 1994 Apr 1;264(5155):92-5. doi: 10.1126/science.8140421.

Requirement of human renal water channel aquaporin-2 for vasopressin-dependent
concentration of urine.

Deen PM(1), Verdijk MA, Knoers NV, Wieringa B, Monnens LA, van Os CH, van Oost
BA.

Author information:
(1)Department of Cell Physiology, University of Nijmegen, Netherlands.

Concentration of urine in mammals is regulated by the antidiuretic hormone
vasopressin. Binding of vasopressin to its V2 receptor leads to the insertion of
water channels in apical membranes of principal cells in collecting ducts. In
nephrogenic diabetes insipidus (NDI), the kidney fails to concentrate urine in
response to vasopressin. A male patient with an autosomal recessive form of NDI
was found to be a compound heterozygote for two mutations in the gene encoding
aquaporin-2, a water channel. Functional expression studies in Xenopus oocytes
revealed that each mutation resulted in nonfunctional water channel proteins.
Thus, aquaporin-2 is essential for vasopressin-dependent concentration of urine.
