The search for novel enzymes and enzymatic activities is important to map out
all metabolic activities and reveal cellular metabolic processes in a more
exhaustive manner. Here we present biochemical and physiological evidence for
the function of the uncharacterized protein YihU in Escherichia coli using
metabolite profiling by capillary electrophoresis time-of-flight mass
spectrometry. To detect enzymatic activity and simultaneously identify possible
substrates and products of the putative enzyme, we profiled a complex mixture of
metabolites in the presence or absence of YihU. In this manner, succinic
semialdehyde was identified as a substrate for YihU. The purified YihU protein
catalyzed in vitro the NADH-dependent reduction of succinic semialdehyde to
gamma-hydroxybutyrate. Moreover, a yihU deletion mutant displayed reduced
tolerance to the cytotoxic effects of exogenous addition of succinic
semialdehyde. Profiling of intracellular metabolites following treatment of E.
coli with succinic semialdehyde supports the existence of a YihU-catalyzed
reduction of succinic semialdehyde to gamma-hydroxybutyrate in addition to its
known oxidation to succinate and through the tricarboxylic acid cycle. These
findings suggest that YihU is a novel gamma-hydroxybutyrate dehydrogenase
involved in the metabolism of succinic semialdehyde, and other potentially toxic
intermediates that may accumulate under stress conditions in E. coli.
