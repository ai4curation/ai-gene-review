# NMDA receptors formed by NR1 in Xenopus laevis oocytes do not contain the endogenous subunit XenU1.
**Authors:** Green T, Rogers CA, Contractor A, Heinemann SF
**Journal:** Mol Pharmacol (2002)
**DOI:** [10.1124/mol.61.2.326](https://doi.org/10.1124/mol.61.2.326)

## Abstract

1. Mol Pharmacol. 2002 Feb;61(2):326-33. doi: 10.1124/mol.61.2.326.

NMDA receptors formed by NR1 in Xenopus laevis oocytes do not contain the
endogenous subunit XenU1.

Green T(1), Rogers CA, Contractor A, Heinemann SF.

Author information:
(1)Molecular Neurobiology Laboratory, the Salk Institute for Biological Studies,
La Jolla, California, USA. green@salk.edu

Activation of N-methyl-D-aspartate-selective ionotropic glutamate receptors
(NMDA receptors) requires two agonists, glutamate and glycine. These ligands are
thought to bind to the NR2 and NR1 subunits, respectively, apparently ruling out
the formation of functional homomeric receptors. However, NMDA-mediated currents
are observed when the mammalian NR1 subunit is expressed alone in Xenopus laevis
oocytes. These currents have been generally ascribed to a functional association
between NR1 and the endogenous glutamate receptor subunit XenU1. To determine
whether such a functional association does in fact occur, we have isolated cDNAs
for both XenU1 and XenU1a, a presumed nonallelic counterpart. We investigated
whether the coexpression of either XenU1 or XenU1a with NR1 in either X. laevis
oocytes and human embryonic kidney (HEK) 293 cells had any effect on the
observed NMDA receptor responses. In oocytes, coinjection of XenU1 with NR1 did
not increase the observed currents compared with injection of NR1 alone;
similarly, in HEK 293 cells, coexpression of XenU1 and NR1 did not result in the
formation of functional channels. We also found no pharmacological or
biochemical evidence for interaction between the two subunits. We conclude,
therefore, that XenU1 does not associate with the NR1 subunit and that an
alternative explanation must be sought for the channels observed when NR1 is
expressed alone in oocytes.
