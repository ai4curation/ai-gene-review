# Post-translational processing of Drosophila nucleoside diphosphate kinase.
**Authors:** Stenberg LM, Stenflo J, Holmgren P, Brown MA
**Journal:** Biochem Biophys Res Commun (2002)
**DOI:** [10.1016/s0006-291x(02)00737-4](https://doi.org/10.1016/s0006-291x(02)00737-4)

## Abstract

1. Biochem Biophys Res Commun. 2002 Jul 19;295(3):689-94. doi:
10.1016/s0006-291x(02)00737-4.

Post-translational processing of Drosophila nucleoside diphosphate kinase.

Stenberg LM(1), Stenflo J, Holmgren P, Brown MA.

Author information:
(1)Department of Clinical Chemistry, Lund University, University Hospital,
Malmö, Sweden.

Nucleoside diphosphate kinase (NDPK) was purified from Drosophila melanogaster
by a combination of anion-exchange, hydroxyapatite, and reversed-phase
chromatography. The identity of the purified enzyme was confirmed by sequencing
internal peptides (the N-terminus appeared to be blocked). Post-translational
modifications were investigated by using protein chemical and mass spectrometric
methods. Analysis by nanoelectrospray ionization-mass spectrometry revealed that
the mass of the enzyme was considerably smaller than that predicted from its
amino acid sequence. Although its open-reading frame predicts a 153-residue
polypeptide, the mature enzyme was found to comprise 152 amino acids, being
modified by proteolytic removal of the initiator Met and N-acetylation of Ala2.
This explains why the observed pI of the Drosophila enzyme is more acidic than
that predicted from its amino acid sequence. No additional post-translational
modifications such as glycosylation or O-phosphorylation, which have been
identified on homologous NDPKs from other organisms, were detected on the
Drosophila enzyme.
