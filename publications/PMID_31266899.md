The order of enzymatic activity across Golgi cisternae is essential for complex
molecule biosynthesis. However, an inability to separate Golgi cisternae has
meant that the cisternal distribution of most resident proteins, and their
underlying localization mechanisms, are unknown. Here, we exploit differences in
surface charge of intact cisternae to perform separation of early to late Golgi
subcompartments. We determine protein and glycan abundance profiles across the
Golgi; over 390 resident proteins are identified, including 136 new additions,
with over 180 cisternal assignments. These assignments provide a means to better
understand the functional roles of Golgi proteins and how they operate
sequentially. Protein and glycan distributions are validated in vivo using
high-resolution microscopy. Results reveal distinct functional
compartmentalization among resident Golgi proteins. Analysis of transmembrane
proteins shows several sequence-based characteristics relating to pI,
hydrophobicity, Ser abundance, and Phe bilayer asymmetry that change across the
Golgi. Overall, our results suggest that a continuum of transmembrane features,
rather than discrete rules, guide proteins to earlier or later locations within
the Golgi stack.
