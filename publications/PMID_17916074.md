# Role of 14-3-3 proteins in the regulation of neutral trehalase in the yeast Saccharomyces cerevisiae.
**Authors:** Panni S, Landgraf C, Volkmer-Engert R, Cesareni G, Castagnoli L
**Journal:** FEMS Yeast Res (2008)
**DOI:** [10.1111/j.1567-1364.2007.00312.x](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1567-1364.2007.00312.x)

## Abstract

1. FEMS Yeast Res. 2008 Feb;8(1):53-63. doi: 10.1111/j.1567-1364.2007.00312.x.
Epub  2007 Oct 3.

Role of 14-3-3 proteins in the regulation of neutral trehalase in the yeast
Saccharomyces cerevisiae.

Panni S(1), Landgraf C, Volkmer-Engert R, Cesareni G, Castagnoli L.

Author information:
(1)Department of Biology, University of Rome Tor Vergata, Rome, Italy.
panni@uniroma2.it

In higher eukaryotes, 14-3-3 proteins participate in numerous cellular
processes, and carry out their function through a variety of different molecular
mechanisms, including regulation of protein localization and enzyme activation.
Here, it is shown that the two yeast 14-3-3 homologues, Bmh1p and Bmh2p, form a
complex with neutral trehalase (Nth1p), an enzyme that is responsible for
trehalose degradation and is required in a variety of stress conditions. In a
purified in vitro system, either one of the two 14-3-3 yeast isoforms are
necessary for complete activation of neutral trehalase (Nth1p) after
phosphorylation by PKA. It is further demonstrated that Bmh1p and Bmh2p bind to
the amino-terminal region of phosphorylated trehalase, thereby modulating its
enzymatic activity. This work represents the first demonstration of enzyme
activation mediated by 14-3-3 binding in yeast.
