# A new visualization approach for identifying mutations that affect differentiation and organization of the Drosophila ommatidia.
**Authors:** Pichaud F, Desplan C
**Journal:** Development (2001)
**DOI:** [10.1242/dev.128.6.815](https://doi.org/10.1242/dev.128.6.815)

## Abstract

1. Development. 2001 Mar;128(6):815-26. doi: 10.1242/dev.128.6.815.

A new visualization approach for identifying mutations that affect
differentiation and organization of the Drosophila ommatidia.

Pichaud F(1), Desplan C.

Author information:
(1)Laboratory of Molecular Genetics, Department of Biology, New York University,
100 Washington Square East, New York NY 10003, USA.

The Drosophila eye is widely used as a model system to study neuronal
differentiation, survival and axon projection. Photoreceptor differentiation
starts with the specification of a founder cell R8, which sequentially recruits
other photoreceptor neurons to the ommatidium. The eight photoreceptors that
compose each ommatidium exist in two chiral forms organized along two axes of
symmetry and this pattern represents a paradigm to study tissue polarity. We
have developed a method of fluoroscopy to visualize the different types of
photoreceptors and the organization of the ommatidia in living animals. This
allowed us to perform an F(1) genetic screen to isolate mutants affecting
photoreceptor differentiation, survival or planar polarity. We illustrate the
power of this detection system using known genetic backgrounds and new mutations
that affect ommatidial differentiation, morphology or chirality.
