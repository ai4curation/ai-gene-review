Carotenoids are integral and essential components of the photosynthetic
membranes in all plants. Within the past few years, genes encoding nearly all of
the enzymes required for the biosynthesis of these indispensable pigments have
been identified. This review focuses on recent findings as to the structure and
function of these genes and the enzymes they encode. Three topics of current
interest are also discussed: the source of isopentenyl pyrophosphate for
carotenoid biosynthesis, the progress and possibilities of metabolic engineering
of plants to alter carotenoid content and composition, and the compartmentation
and association of the carotenogenic enzymes. A speculative schematic model of
carotenogenic enzyme complexes is presented to help frame and provoke insightful
questions leading to future experimentation.
