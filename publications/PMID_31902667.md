Naturally occurring or drug-induced DNA-protein crosslinks (DPCs) interfere with
key DNA transactions if not repaired in a timely manner. The unique family of
DPC-specific proteases Wss1/SPRTN targets DPC protein moieties for degradation,
including stabilized topoisomerase-1 cleavage complexes (Top1ccs). Here, we
describe that the efficient DPC disassembly requires Ddi1, another conserved
predicted protease in Saccharomyces cerevisiae. We found Ddi1 in a genetic
screen of the tdp1 wss1 mutant defective in Top1cc processing. Ddi1 is recruited
to a persistent Top1cc-like DPC lesion in an S phase-dependent manner to assist
in the eviction of crosslinked protein from DNA. Loss of Ddi1 or its putative
protease activity hypersensitizes cells to DPC trapping agents independently
from Wss1 and 26S proteasome, implying its broader role in DPC repair. Among the
potential Ddi1 targets, we found the core component of Pol II and show that its
genotoxin-induced degradation is impaired in ddi1. We propose that the Ddi1
protease contributes to DPC proteolysis.
