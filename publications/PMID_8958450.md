# Translocation of phospholipase C-gamma 2 induced by in vitro activation of protein tyrosine kinase activity in mast cell lysates.
**Authors:** Atkinson TP, Yang Q
**Journal:** Cell Signal (1996)
**DOI:** [10.1016/s0898-6568(96)00073-3](https://doi.org/10.1016/s0898-6568(96)00073-3)

## Abstract

1. Cell Signal. 1996 Sep;8(6):461-5. doi: 10.1016/s0898-6568(96)00073-3.

Translocation of phospholipase C-gamma 2 induced by in vitro activation of
protein tyrosine kinase activity in mast cell lysates.

Atkinson TP(1), Yang Q.

Author information:
(1)Department of Pediatrics, University of Alabama at Birmingham 35294, USA.

Aggregation of the high-affinity receptor for IgE (Fc eta RI) on the surface of
intact or permeabilized rodent mast cells results in tyrosine phosphorylation of
phospholipase C-gamma 1 (PLC gamma 1) and PLC gamma 2, and translocation of both
isozymes to the particulate fraction. We report here that activation of resident
tyrosine kinases by the addition of adenosine triphosphate (ATP), orthovanadate,
and Mg2+ to rat basophilic leukemia cell (RBL) lysates induces an association of
PLC gamma 2 with the Triton-insoluble particulate fraction, with a parallel
increase in tyrosine phosphorylation of cellular proteins. Both PLC gamma 2
translocation and tyrosine phosphorylation are supported by millimolar Mg2+ or
Mn2+ but not by Ca2+. Both tyrosine phosphorylation and PLC gamma 2
translocation are inhibited by genistein. These data suggest that in vitro
activation of tyrosine kinase activity in broken cell preparations induces the
formation of association between PLC gamma 2 and ligands with the
Triton-insoluble fraction.
