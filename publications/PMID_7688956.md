# Structure of the CAMPATH-1 antigen, a glycosylphosphatidylinositol-anchored glycoprotein which is an exceptionally good target for complement lysis.
**Authors:** Xia MQ, Hale G, Lifely MR, Ferguson MA, Campbell D, Packman L, Waldmann H
**Journal:** Biochem J (1993)
**DOI:** [10.1042/bj2930633](https://doi.org/10.1042/bj2930633)
**PMC:** [PMC1134413](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC1134413/)

## Abstract

1. Biochem J. 1993 Aug 1;293 ( Pt 3)(Pt 3):633-40. doi: 10.1042/bj2930633.

Structure of the CAMPATH-1 antigen, a glycosylphosphatidylinositol-anchored
glycoprotein which is an exceptionally good target for complement lysis.

Xia MQ(1), Hale G, Lifely MR, Ferguson MA, Campbell D, Packman L, Waldmann H.

Author information:
(1)Department of Pathology, University of Cambridge, U.K.

CAMPATH-1 antibodies recognize a unique molecule on human lymphocytes and are
unusually efficient at causing cell lysis with homologous complement. They have
been successfully used for lymphocyte depletion in vivo in a variety of
diseases. We find that the antigen is a very small glycosylphosphatidylinositol
(GPI)-anchored glycoprotein with a mature peptide comprising only 12 amino
acids. It can be separated into two distinct antigenic fractions which differ in
their susceptibility to phosphatidylinositol-specific phospholipase C. There is
one N-linked glycosylation site, but no evidence for O-glycosylation despite the
presence of several serine and threonine residues. The antibodies were found to
bind, albeit with a generally reduced affinity, to a proteolytic fragment
containing the C-terminal tripeptide and the GPI anchor. We postulate that one
of the reasons why the CAMPATH-1 antibodies are so good for cell lysis is
because they bind to an epitope which is likely to be very close to the lipid
bilayer.
