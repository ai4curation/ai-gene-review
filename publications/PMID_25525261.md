# The olfactomedin domain from gliomedin is a β-propeller with unique structural properties.

All members of the olfactomedin (OLF) family have a conserved extracellular OLF
domain, for which a structure has not been available. We present here the
crystal structure of the OLF domain from gliomedin. Gliomedin is a protein
expressed by Schwann cells in peripheral nerves, important for the formation of
the nodes of Ranvier. Gliomedin interacts with neuronal cell adhesion molecules,
such as neurofascin, but the structural details of the interaction are not
known. The structure of the OLF domain presents a five-bladed β-propeller fold
with unusual geometric properties. The symmetry of the structure is not 5-fold,
but rather reveals a twisted arrangement. The conserved top face of the
gliomedin OLF domain is likely to be important for binding to neuronal ligands.
Our results provide a structural basis for the functions of gliomedin in Schwann
cells, enable the understanding of the role of the gliomedin OLF domain in
autoimmune neuropathies, and unravel the locations of human disease-causing
mutations in other OLF family members, including myocilin.
