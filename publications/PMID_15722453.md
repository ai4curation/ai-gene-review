# Ligand-binding regulation of LXR/RXR and LXR/PPAR heterodimerizations: SPR technology-based kinetic analysis correlated with molecular dynamics simulation.

Liver X receptor (LXR) and peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor (PPAR) are
two members of nuclear receptors involved in the nutrient metabolisms of dietary
fatty acid and cholesterol. They are found to be of cross-talk function in that
LXR regulates fatty acid synthesis and PPAR controls fatty acid degradation.
LXRs (LXRalpha and LXRbeta) function by forming obligate heterodimers with the
retinoid X receptor (RXR), and subsequently binding to specific DNA response
elements within the regulatory regions of their target genes. In this work, the
kinetic features concerning LXR/RXR and LXR/PPAR interactions have been fully
investigated using surface plasmon resonance (SPR) technology. It is found that
LXRs could bind to all the three PPAR subtypes, PPARalpha, PPARgamma and
PPARdelta with different binding affinities, and such receptor/receptor
interactions could be regulated by ligand binding. Moreover, molecular dynamics
(MD) simulations were performed on six typical complex models. The results
revealed that ligands may increase the interaction energies between the receptor
interfaces of the simulated receptor/receptor complexes. The MD results are in
agreement with the SPR data. Further analyses on the MD results indicated that
the ligand binding might increase the hydrogen bonds between the interfaces of
the receptor/receptor complex.
