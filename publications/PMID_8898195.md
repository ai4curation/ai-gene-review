# Sterol resistance in CHO cells traced to point mutation in SREBP cleavage-activating protein.
**Authors:** Hua X, Nohturfft A, Goldstein JL, Brown MS
**Journal:** Cell (1996)
**DOI:** [10.1016/s0092-8674(00)81362-8](https://doi.org/10.1016/s0092-8674(00)81362-8)

## Abstract

1. Cell. 1996 Nov 1;87(3):415-26. doi: 10.1016/s0092-8674(00)81362-8.

Sterol resistance in CHO cells traced to point mutation in SREBP
cleavage-activating protein.

Hua X(1), Nohturfft A, Goldstein JL, Brown MS.

Author information:
(1)Department of Molecular Genetics, University of Texas Southwestern Medical
Center, Dallas 75235, USA.

Through expression cloning we have isolated a cDNA-encoding SREBP
cleavage-activating protein (SCAP), which regulates cholesterol metabolism by
stimulating cleavage of transcription factors SREBP-1 and -2, thereby releasing
them from membranes. The cDNA was isolated from Chinese hamster ovary cells with
a dominant mutation that renders them resistant to sterol-mediated suppression
of cholesterol synthesis and uptake. Sterol resistance was traced to a G-->A
transition at codon 443 of SCAP, changing aspartic acid to asparagine. The D443N
mutation enhances the cleavage-stimulating ability of SCAP and renders it
resistant to inhibition by sterols. SCAP has multiple membrane-spanning regions,
five of which resemble the sterol-sensing domain of HMG CoA reductase, an
endoplasmic reticulum enzyme whose degradation is accelerated by sterols. SCAP
appears to be a central regulator of cholesterol metabolism in animal cells.
