# ASNA-1 activity modulates sensitivity to cisplatin.
**Authors:** Hemmingsson O, Kao G, Still M, Naredi P
**Journal:** Cancer Res (2010)
**DOI:** [10.1158/0008-5472.CAN-10-1548](https://doi.org/10.1158/0008-5472.CAN-10-1548)

## Abstract

1. Cancer Res. 2010 Dec 15;70(24):10321-8. doi: 10.1158/0008-5472.CAN-10-1548.
Epub  2010 Oct 21.

ASNA-1 activity modulates sensitivity to cisplatin.

Hemmingsson O(1), Kao G, Still M, Naredi P.

Author information:
(1)Division of Surgery, Department of Surgical and Perioperative Sciences, Umeå
University, Umeå, Sweden.

Cancer can be cured by platinum-based chemotherapy, but resistance is a major
cause of treatment failure. Here we present the nematode Caenorhabditis elegans
as a model to study interactions between the platinum drug cisplatin and
signaling pathways in vivo. Null mutation in a single gene, asna-1, makes worms
hypersensitive to cisplatin. The metalloregulated ATPase ASNA-1 promotes insulin
secretion and membrane insertion of tail-anchored proteins. Using structural
data from ASNA-1 homologues, we identify specific ASNA-1 mutants that are
sensitive to cisplatin while still able to promote insulin signaling. Mutational
analysis reveals that hypersensitivity of ASNA-1 mutants to cisplatin remains in
absence of CEP-1/p53 or apoptosis. Human ASNA1 can substitute for the worm gene,
indicating a conserved function. Cisplatin sensitivity is not affected by
decreased insulin signaling in wild-type nematodes or restored insulin signaling
in asna-1 mutants. These findings provide a functional insight into ASNA-1,
demonstrate that C. elegans can be used to characterize cisplatin resistance
mechanisms, and suggest that rationally designed drugs against ASNA-1 can
sensitize cancer cells to cisplatin.
