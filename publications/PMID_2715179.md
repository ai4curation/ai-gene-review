# Molecular genetic characterization of a developmentally regulated human perinatal myosin heavy chain.
**Authors:** Feghali R, Leinwand LA
**Journal:** J Cell Biol (1989)
**DOI:** [10.1083/jcb.108.5.1791](https://doi.org/10.1083/jcb.108.5.1791)

## Abstract

1. J Cell Biol. 1989 May;108(5):1791-7. doi: 10.1083/jcb.108.5.1791.

Molecular genetic characterization of a developmentally regulated human
perinatal myosin heavy chain.

Feghali R(1), Leinwand LA.

Author information:
(1)Department of Microbiology and Immunology, Albert Einstein College of
Medicine, Bronx, New York 10461.

We have isolated a human cDNA which corresponds to a developmentally regulated
sarcomeric myosin heavy chain. RNA hybridization and DNA sequence analysis
indicate that this cDNA, called SMHCP, encodes a perinatal myosin heavy chain
isoform. The nucleotide and deduced amino acid sequences of the 3.4-kb cDNA
insert show strong homology with other sarcomeric myosin heavy chains. The
strongest homology is to a previously described 970-bp cDNA encoding a rat
perinatal isoform (Periasamy, M., D. F. Wieczorek, and B. Nadal-Ginard. 1984. J.
Biol. Chem. 259:13573-13578). The homology between the analogous human and rat
perinatal myosin heavy chain cDNAs is maintained through the highly
isoform-specific final 20 carboxyl-terminal amino acids, as well as the 3'
untranslated region. Ribonuclease protection studies show that the mRNA encoding
this isoform is expressed at high levels in 21-wk fetal skeletal tissue and not
in fetal cardiac muscle. In contrast to the rat perinatal isoform, which was not
found to be expressed in adult hind-leg tissue, the gene encoding SMHCP
continues to be expressed in adult human skeletal tissue, but at lower levels
relative to fetal skeletal tissue.
