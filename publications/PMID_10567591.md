# Disruption of muREC2/RAD51L1 in mice results in early embryonic lethality which can Be partially rescued in a p53(-/-) background.
**Authors:** Shu Z, Smith S, Wang L, Rice MC, Kmiec EB
**Journal:** Mol Cell Biol (1999)
**DOI:** [10.1128/MCB.19.12.8686](https://doi.org/10.1128/MCB.19.12.8686)

## Abstract

1. Mol Cell Biol. 1999 Dec;19(12):8686-93. doi: 10.1128/MCB.19.12.8686.

Disruption of muREC2/RAD51L1 in mice results in early embryonic lethality which
can Be partially rescued in a p53(-/-) background.

Shu Z(1), Smith S, Wang L, Rice MC, Kmiec EB.

Author information:
(1)Department of Biological Sciences, University of Delaware, Newark, Delaware
19716, USA.

muREC2/RAD51L1 is a radiation-inducible gene that regulates cell cycle
progression. To elucidate the biological function of muREC2/RAD51L1, the gene
was disrupted in embryonic stem cells by homologous recombination. Mice
heterozygous for muREC2/RAD51L1 appear normal and fertile; however, no
homozygous pups were born after interbreeding of heterozygous mice. Timed
pregnancy studies showed that homozygous mutant embryos were severely retarded
in growth as early as ca. 5 days gestation (E5.5) and were completely resorbed
by E8.5. Mutant blastocyst outgrowth was also severely impaired in a
double-knockout embryo, but embryonic development did progress further in a
p53-null background. These results suggest that muREC2/RAD51L1 plays a role in
cell proliferation and early embryonic development, perhaps through interaction
with p53.
