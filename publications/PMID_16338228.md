# A molecule that is associated with Toll-like receptor 4 and regulates its cell surface expression.
**Authors:** Konno K, Wakabayashi Y, Akashi-Takamura S, Ishii T, Kobayashi M, Takahashi K, Kusumoto Y, Saitoh S, Yoshizawa Y, Miyake K
**Journal:** Biochem Biophys Res Commun (2006)

## Abstract

1. Biochem Biophys Res Commun. 2006 Jan 27;339(4):1076-82. doi:
10.1016/j.bbrc.2005.11.123. Epub 2005 Dec 5.

A molecule that is associated with Toll-like receptor 4 and regulates its cell
surface expression.

Konno K(1), Wakabayashi Y, Akashi-Takamura S, Ishii T, Kobayashi M, Takahashi K,
Kusumoto Y, Saitoh S, Yoshizawa Y, Miyake K.

Author information:
(1)Division of Infectious Genetics, The Institute of Medical Science, The

Toll-like receptors (TLRs) recognize microbial products and induce immune
responses. Their subcellular distribution is believed to be optimized for their
pathogen recognition. Little is known, however, about molecular mechanisms
regulating the subcellular distribution of TLR. Lipopolysaccharide, a principal
membrane component of the Gram-negative bacteria, is recognized by the receptor
complex consisting of Toll-like receptor 4 (TLR4) and MD-2. We here show that a
novel molecule, a PRotein Associated with Tlr4 (PRAT4B), regulates cell surface
expression of TLR4. PRAT4B has a signal peptide followed by a mature peptide.
PRAT4B is associated with the hypoglycosylated, immature form of TLR4 but not
with MD-2 or TLR2. Downregulation of PRAT4B mRNA with small interfering RNA
decreased cell surface TLR4 on HEK293 cells. These results suggest a novel
mechanism regulating the subcellular distribution of TLR4.
