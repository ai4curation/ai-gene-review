# Regulation of p34CDC28 tyrosine phosphorylation is not required for entry into mitosis in S. cerevisiae.
**Authors:** Amon A, Surana U, Muroff I, Nasmyth K
**Journal:** Nature (1992)
**DOI:** [10.1038/355368a0](https://doi.org/10.1038/355368a0)

## Abstract

1. Nature. 1992 Jan 23;355(6358):368-71. doi: 10.1038/355368a0.

Regulation of p34CDC28 tyrosine phosphorylation is not required for entry into
mitosis in S. cerevisiae.

Amon A(1), Surana U, Muroff I, Nasmyth K.

Author information:
(1)Institute of Molecular Pathology, Vienna, Austria.

Progression from G2 to M phase in eukaryotes requires activation of a protein
kinase composed of p34cdc2/CDC28 associated with G1-specific cyclins. In some
organisms the activation of the kinase at the G2/M boundary is due to
dephosphorylation of a highly conserved tyrosine residue at position 15 (Y15) of
the cdc2 protein. Here we report that in the budding yeast Saccharomyces
cerevisiae, p34CDC28 also undergoes cell-cycle regulated dephosphorylation on an
equivalent tyrosine residue (Y19). However, in contrast to previous observations
in S. pombe, Xenopus and mammalian cells, dephosphorylation of Y19 is not
required for the activation of the CDC28/cyclin kinase. Furthermore, mutation of
this tyrosine residue does not affect dependence of mitosis on DNA synthesis nor
does it abolish G2 arrest induced by DNA damage. Our data imply that regulated
phosphorylation of this tyrosine residue is not the 'universal' means by which
the onset of mitosis is determined. We propose that there are other unidentified
controls that regulate entry into mitosis.
