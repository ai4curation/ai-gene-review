# Structure of the glycosyl-phosphatidylinositol membrane anchor of the Leishmania major promastigote surface protease.
**Authors:** Schneider P, Ferguson MA, McConville MJ, Mehlert A, Homans SW, Bordier C
**Journal:** J Biol Chem (1990)

## Abstract

1. J Biol Chem. 1990 Oct 5;265(28):16955-64.

Structure of the glycosyl-phosphatidylinositol membrane anchor of the Leishmania
major promastigote surface protease.

Schneider P(1), Ferguson MA, McConville MJ, Mehlert A, Homans SW, Bordier C.

Author information:
(1)Institut de Biochimie, Université de Lausanne, Epalinges, Switzerland.

In common with many other plasma membrane glycoproteins of eukaryotic origin,
the promastigote surface protease (PSP) of the protozoan parasite Leishmania
contains a glycosyl-phosphatidylinositol (GPI) membrane anchor. The GPI anchor
of Leishmania major PSP was purified following proteolysis of the PSP and
analyzed by two-dimensional 1H-1H NMR, compositional and methylation linkage
analyses, chemical and enzymatic modifications, and amino acid sequencing. From
these results, the structure of the GPI-containing peptide was found to be
Asp-Gly-Gly-Asn-ethanolamine-PO4-6Man alpha 1-6Man alpha 1-4GlcN alpha
1-6myo-inositol-1-PO4-(1-alkyl-2-acyl-glycerol). The glycan structure is
identical to the conserved glycan core regions of the GPI anchor of Trypanosoma
brucei variant surface glycoprotein and rat brain Thy-1 antigen, supporting the
notion that this portion of GPIs are highly conserved. The phosphatidylinositol
moiety of the PSP anchor is unusual, containing a fully saturated, unbranched
1-O-alkyl chain (mainly C24:0) and a mixture of fully saturated unbranched
2-O-acyl chains (C12:0, C14:0, C16:0, and C18:0). This lipid composition differs
significantly from those of the GPIs of T. brucei variant surface glycoprotein
and mammalian erythrocyte acetylcholinesterase but is similar to that of a
family of glycosylated phosphoinositides found uniquely in Leishmania.
