# Sea urchin Forkhead gene family: phylogeny and embryonic expression.

Sea urchin Forkhead gene family: phylogeny and embryonic expression.

Author information:

Transcription factors of the Forkhead (Fox) family have been identified in many
metazoans, and play important roles in diverse biological processes. Here we
define the set of fox genes present in the sea urchin genome, and survey their
usage during development. This genome includes 22 fox genes, only three of which
were previously known. Of the 23 fox gene subclasses identified in vertebrate
genomes, the Strongylocentrotus purpuratus genome has orthologues of all but
four (E, H, R and S). Phylogenetic analysis suggests that one S. purpuratus fox
gene is equally related to foxA and foxB of vertebrates; this gene defines a new
class. Two other genes appear to be specific to the sea urchin, with respect to
the genomes so far sequenced. Fox genes orthologous with those of vertebrates
but lacking in arthropod or nematode genomes may be deuterostome-specific
(subclasses I, J1, J2, L1, M and Q1), while the majority are pan-bilaterian. All
but one of the S. purpuratus fox genes (SpfoxQ1) are expressed during
embryogenesis, most in a very specific temporal and spatial manner. The sea
urchin fox genes clearly execute many different regulatory functions, and almost
all of them participate in the process of embryonic development.
