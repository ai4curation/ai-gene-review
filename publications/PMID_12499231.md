# Activation and deactivation of a broad-spectrum antiviral drug by a single enzyme: adenosine deaminase catalyzes two consecutive deamination reactions.

## Abstract

1. Antimicrob Agents Chemother. 2003 Jan;47(1):426-31. doi:
10.1128/AAC.47.1.426-431.2003.

Activation and deactivation of a broad-spectrum antiviral drug by a single
enzyme: adenosine deaminase catalyzes two consecutive deamination reactions.

Wu JZ(1), Walker H, Lau JY, Hong Z.

Author information:
(1)Research & Development, Ribapharm Corporation, Costa Mesa, California 92626,
USA. jwu@icnpharm.com

Ribavirin is an approved broad-spectrum antiviral drug. A liver-targeting
prodrug of ribavirin, viramidine, is in clinical trial in an attempt to provide
a better therapeutic index. The conversion of viramidine to ribavirin, and of
ribavirin to an inactive metabolite through adenosine deaminase, is reported.
Kinetic analysis indicates that adenosine deaminase is likely involved in
activation of viramidine in vivo, and the process is highly pH sensitive. The
differential activities of two consecutive deamination reactions are kinetically
studied and interpreted based on adenosine deaminase structural information. A
comprehensive understanding of the viramidine and ribavirin deamination
mechanism should help in designing better nucleoside therapeutics in the future.
