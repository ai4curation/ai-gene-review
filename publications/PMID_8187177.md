# The sorting receptor for yeast vacuolar carboxypeptidase Y is encoded by the VPS10 gene.
**Authors:** Marcusson EG, Horazdovsky BF, Cereghino JL, Gharakhanian E, Emr SD
**Journal:** Cell (1994)
**DOI:** [10.1016/0092-8674(94)90219-4](https://doi.org/10.1016/0092-8674(94)90219-4)

## Abstract

1. Cell. 1994 May 20;77(4):579-86. doi: 10.1016/0092-8674(94)90219-4.

The sorting receptor for yeast vacuolar carboxypeptidase Y is encoded by the
VPS10 gene.

Marcusson EG(1), Horazdovsky BF, Cereghino JL, Gharakhanian E, Emr SD.

Author information:
(1)Division of Cellular and Molecular Medicine, University of California, San
Diego School of Medicine, La Jolla 92093-0668.

The S. cerevisiae VPS10 (vacuolar protein sorting) gene encodes a type I
transmembrane protein of 1577 amino acids required for the sorting of the
soluble vacuolar protein carboxypeptidase Y (CPY). Mutations in VPS10 result in
the selective missorting and secretion of CPY; all other vacuolar proteins
tested are delivered to the vacuole in vps10 mutants. Chemical cross-linking
studies demonstrate that Vps10p and the Golgi-modified precursor form of CPY
directly interact. A single amino acid change in the CPY vacuolar sorting signal
prevents this interaction. Vps10p also interacts with a hybrid protein
containing the CPY sorting signal fused to the normally secreted enzyme
invertase. Subcellular fractionation indicates that the majority of Vps10p is
localized to a late Golgi compartment where vacuolar proteins are sorted. We
propose that VPS10 encodes a CPY sorting receptor that executes multiple rounds
of sorting by cycling between the late Golgi and a prevacuolar endosome-like
compartment.
