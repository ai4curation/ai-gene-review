# ATP-dependent branch migration of Holliday junctions promoted by the RuvA and RuvB proteins of E. coli.
**Authors:** Tsaneva IR, Müller B, West SC
**Journal:** Cell (1992)
**DOI:** [10.1016/0092-8674(92)90638-s](https://doi.org/10.1016/0092-8674(92)90638-s)

## Abstract

1. Cell. 1992 Jun 26;69(7):1171-80. doi: 10.1016/0092-8674(92)90638-s.

ATP-dependent branch migration of Holliday junctions promoted by the RuvA and
RuvB proteins of E. coli.

Tsaneva IR(1), Müller B, West SC.

Author information:
(1)Imperial Cancer Research Fund, Clare Hall Laboratories, South Mimms, Herts,
England.

The RuvA and RuvB proteins of E. coli, which are induced as part of the cellular
response to DNA damage, act together to promote the branch migration of Holliday
junctions. Addition of purified RuvA and RuvB to a RecA-mediated recombination
reaction stimulates the rate of strand exchange and the formation of
hetero-duplex DNA. Stimulation does not occur via interaction with RecA;
instead, RuvA and RuvB act directly upon recombination intermediates (Holliday
junctions) made by RecA. We show that RuvAB-mediated branch migration requires
ATP and can bypass UV-induced DNA lesions. At high RuvB concentrations, the
requirement for RuvA is overcome, indicating that the RuvB ATPase provides the
motor force for branch migration. RuvA protein provides specificity by binding
to the Holliday junction, thereby reducing the requirement for RuvB by 50-fold.
The newly discovered biochemical properties of RuvA, RuvB, and RuvC are
incorporated into a model for the postreplicational repair of DNA following UV
irradiation.
