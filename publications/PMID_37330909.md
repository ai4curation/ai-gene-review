Dynamic regulation of integrin activation and inactivation is critical for
precisely controlled cell adhesion and migration in physiological and
pathological processes. The molecular basis for integrin activation has been
intensively studied; however, the understanding of integrin inactivation is
still limited. Here, we identify LRP12 as an endogenous transmembrane inhibitor
for α4 integrin activation. The LRP12 cytoplasmic domain directly binds to the
integrin α4 cytoplasmic tail and inhibits talin binding to the β subunit, thus
keeping integrin inactive. In migrating cells, LRP12-α4 interaction induces
nascent adhesion (NA) turnover at the leading-edge protrusion. Knockdown of
LRP12 leads to increased NAs and enhanced cell migration. Consistently,
LRP12-deficient T cells show an enhanced homing capability in mice and lead to
aggravated chronic colitis in a T cell-transfer colitis model. Altogether, LRP12
is a transmembrane inactivator for integrins that inhibits α4 integrin
activation and controls cell migration by maintaining balanced NA dynamics.
