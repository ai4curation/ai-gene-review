# Targeting assay to study the cis functions of human telomeric proteins: evidence for inhibition of telomerase by TRF1 and for activation of telomere degradation by TRF2.
**Authors:** Ancelin K, Brunori M, Bauwens S, Koering CE, Brun C, Ricoul M, Pommier JP, Sabatier L, Gilson E
**Journal:** Mol Cell Biol (2002)
**DOI:** [10.1128/MCB.22.10.3474-3487.2002](https://doi.org/10.1128/MCB.22.10.3474-3487.2002)

## Abstract

1. Mol Cell Biol. 2002 May;22(10):3474-87. doi: 10.1128/MCB.22.10.3474-3487.2002.

Targeting assay to study the cis functions of human telomeric proteins: evidence
for inhibition of telomerase by TRF1 and for activation of telomere degradation
by TRF2.

Ancelin K(1), Brunori M, Bauwens S, Koering CE, Brun C, Ricoul M, Pommier JP,
Sabatier L, Gilson E.

Author information:
(1)Laboratoire de Biologie Moléculaire et Cellulaire, UMR5665 CNRS/ENSL, Ecole
Normale Supérieure de Lyon, Lyon, France.

We investigated the control of telomere length by the human telomeric proteins
TRF1 and TRF2. To this end, we established telomerase-positive cell lines in
which the targeting of these telomeric proteins to specific telomeres could be
induced. We demonstrate that their targeting leads to telomere shortening. This
indicates that these proteins act in cis to repress telomere elongation.
Inhibition of telomerase activity by a modified oligonucleotide did not further
increase the pace of telomere erosion caused by TRF1 targeting, suggesting that
telomerase itself is the target of TRF1 regulation. In contrast, TRF2 targeting
and telomerase inhibition have additive effects. The possibility that TRF2 can
activate a telomeric degradation pathway was directly tested in human primary
cells that do not express telomerase. In these cells, overexpression of
full-length TRF2 leads to an increased rate of telomere shortening.
