Comment in
    J Clin Invest. 2006 Mar;116(3):637-41. doi: 10.1172/JCI27999.

The growth hormone (GH) secretagogue receptor (GHSR) was cloned as the target of
a family of synthetic molecules endowed with GH release properties. As shown
recently through in vitro means, this receptor displays a constitutive activity
whose clinical relevance is unknown. Although pharmacological studies have
demonstrated that its endogenous ligand--ghrelin--stimulates, through the GHSR,
GH secretion and appetite, the physiological importance of the GHSR-dependent
pathways remains an open question that gives rise to much controversy. We report
the identification of a GHSR missense mutation that segregates with short
stature within 2 unrelated families. This mutation, which results in decreased
cell-surface expression of the receptor, selectively impairs the constitutive
activity of the GHSR, while preserving its ability to respond to ghrelin. This
first description, to our knowledge, of a functionally significant GHSR
mutation, which unveils the critical importance of the GHSR-associated
constitutive activity, discloses an unusual pathogenic mechanism of growth
failure in humans.
