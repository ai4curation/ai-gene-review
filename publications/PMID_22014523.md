# MADD-4 is a secreted cue required for midline-oriented guidance in Caenorhabditis elegans.
**Authors:** Seetharaman A, Selman G, Puckrin R, Barbier L, Wong E, D'Souza SA, Roy PJ
**Journal:** Dev Cell (2011)
**DOI:** [10.1016/j.devcel.2011.07.020](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.devcel.2011.07.020)

## Abstract

1. Dev Cell. 2011 Oct 18;21(4):669-80. doi: 10.1016/j.devcel.2011.07.020.

MADD-4 is a secreted cue required for midline-oriented guidance in 
Caenorhabditis elegans.

Seetharaman A(1), Selman G, Puckrin R, Barbier L, Wong E, D'Souza SA, Roy PJ.

Author information:
(1)Department of Molecular Genetics, University of Toronto, Toronto, ON M5S 1A8, 
Canada.

The netrins and slits are two families of widely conserved cues that guide axons 
and cells along the dorsal-ventral (D-V) axis of animals. These cues typically 
emanate from the dorsal or ventral midlines and provide spatial information to 
migrating cells by forming gradients along the D-V axis. Some cell types, 
however, extend processes to both the dorsal and ventral midlines, suggesting 
the existence of additional guidance cues that are secreted from both midlines. 
Here, we report that a previously uncharacterized protein called MADD-4 is 
secreted by the dorsal and ventral nerve cords of the nematode C. elegans to 
attract sensory axons and muscle membrane extensions called muscle arms. 
MADD-4's activity is dependent on UNC-40/DCC, a netrin receptor, which functions 
cell-autonomously to direct membrane extension. The biological role of MADD-4 
orthologs, including ADAMTSL1 and 3 in mammals, is unknown. MADD-4 may therefore 
represent the founding member of a family of guidance proteins.
