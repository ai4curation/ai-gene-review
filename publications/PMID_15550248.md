Mutual control of membrane fission and fusion proteins.

Peters C(1), Baars TL, Bühler S, Mayer A.

Comment in
    Cell. 2004 Nov 24;119(5):581-2. doi: 10.1016/j.cell.2004.11.026.

Membrane fusion and fission are antagonistic reactions controlled by different
proteins. Dynamins promote membrane fission by GTP-driven changes of
conformation and polymerization state, while SNAREs fuse membranes by forming
complexes between t- and v-SNAREs from apposed vesicles. Here, we describe a
role of the dynamin-like GTPase Vps1p in fusion of yeast vacuoles. Vps1p forms
polymers that couple several t-SNAREs together. At the onset of fusion, the
SNARE-activating ATPase Sec18p/NSF and the t-SNARE depolymerize Vps1p and
release it from the membrane. This activity is independent of the SNARE
coactivator Sec17p/alpha-SNAP and of the v-SNARE. Vps1p release liberates the
t-SNAREs for initiating fusion and at the same time disrupts fission activity.
We propose that reciprocal control between fusion and fission components exists,
which may prevent futile cycles of fission and fusion.
