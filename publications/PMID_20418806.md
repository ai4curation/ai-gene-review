# LC3 and GATE-16/GABARAP subfamilies are both essential yet act differently in autophagosome biogenesis.
**Authors:** Weidberg H, Shvets E, Shpilka T, Shimron F, Shinder V, Elazar Z
**Journal:** EMBO J (2010)
**DOI:** [10.1038/emboj.2010.74](https://doi.org/10.1038/emboj.2010.74)
**PMC:** [PMC2885923](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC2885923/)

## Abstract

1. EMBO J. 2010 Jun 2;29(11):1792-802. doi: 10.1038/emboj.2010.74. Epub 2010 Apr
23.

LC3 and GATE-16/GABARAP subfamilies are both essential yet act differently in
autophagosome biogenesis.

Weidberg H(1), Shvets E, Shpilka T, Shimron F, Shinder V, Elazar Z.

Author information:
(1)Department of Biological Chemistry, The Weizmann Institute of Science,
Rehovot, Israel.

Autophagy, a critical process for bulk degradation of proteins and organelles,
requires conjugation of Atg8 proteins to phosphatidylethanolamine on the
autophagic membrane. At least eight different Atg8 orthologs belonging to two
subfamilies (LC3 and GATE-16/GABARAP) occur in mammalian cells, but their
individual roles and modes of action are largely unknown. In this study, we
dissect the activity of each subfamily and show that both are indispensable for
the autophagic process in mammalian cells. We further show that both subfamilies
act differently at early stages of autophagosome biogenesis. Accordingly, our
results indicate that LC3s are involved in elongation of the phagophore membrane
whereas the GABARAP/GATE-16 subfamily is essential for a later stage in
autophagosome maturation.
