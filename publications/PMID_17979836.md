Death receptors (DRs) are more than simple killers: they control cell growth,
proliferation, and survival, thereby playing a pivotal role in immune and
inflammatory responses. Some of these phenomena might be explained by aberrant
reactive oxygen species (ROS) production and metabolism, which can lead to
oxidative stress. A key signaling molecule of DR-initiated intracellular
pathways, receptor-interacting protein 1 (RIP1), orchestrates a complex control
of multiple responses and may link DR-associated signaling complexes to ROS
production by mitochondria. Yet, RIP1 is also an important regulator of
endogenous anti-oxidants and ROS scavenging enzymes, because it is required for
nuclear factor kappaB activation that results in expression of anti-apoptotic
and anti-oxidant proteins. Alteration of RIP1 function may result in ROS
accumulation and abnormal c-Jun N-terminal protein kinase activation, affecting
inflammatory responses, innate immunity, stress responses, and cell survival.
These molecular mechanisms may be involved in neoplastic, autoimmune,
neurodegenerative, inflammatory, and metabolic diseases.
