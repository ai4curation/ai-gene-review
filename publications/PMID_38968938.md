Contactin 2 (CNTN2) is a cell adhesion molecule involved in axon guidance,
neuronal migration, and fasciculation. The ectodomains of CNTN1-CNTN6 are
composed of six Ig domains (Ig1-Ig6) and four FN domains. Here, we show that
CNTN2 forms transient homophilic interactions (KD ∼200 nM). Cryo-EM structures
of full-length CNTN2 and CNTN2_Ig1-Ig6 reveal a T-shaped homodimer formed by
intertwined, parallel monomers. Unexpectedly, the horseshoe-shaped Ig1-Ig4
headpieces extend their Ig2-Ig3 tips outwards on either side of the homodimer,
while Ig4, Ig5, Ig6, and the FN domains form a central stalk. Cross-linking mass
spectrometry and cell-based binding assays confirm the 3D assembly of the CNTN2
homodimer. The interface mediating homodimer formation differs between CNTNs, as
do the homophilic versus heterophilic interaction mechanisms. The CNTN family
thus encodes a versatile molecular platform that supports a very diverse
portfolio of protein interactions and that can be leveraged to strategically
guide neural circuit development.

Introduction

The contactins (CNTNs) form a family of cell surface adhesion molecules containing six members, CNTN1-CNTN6, that are expressed in unique patterns in the central nervous system.1,2 CNTNs play an important role in brain development and neural circuit formation by taking part in axon guidance and fasciculation.1–3 In particular, CNTN2 promotes axon finding by guiding commissural axons to project across the midline of the nervous system.4 CNTN2-expressing axons also promote the migration of neurons, e.g., GABAergic interneurons in the cortex, and various neuron types in the cerebellum during development, while specifically homophilic CNTN2 interactions guide motor neurons within the spinal cord.2,5,6 CNTN2 is found at the axon initial segment and juxtaparanodes of myelinated axons together with proteins that regulate axonal excitability.7 CNTN2 is furthermore found localized to synaptic plasma membranes, but its exact role there for synaptic physiology is unclear.1 A pattern of selective interactions has emerged between the different CNTNs, whereby CNTNs wire subsets of neurons together. For instance, in developing chick retina, CNTN1–5 play crucial roles in directing retinal neurites to the appropriate sublaminae in the inner plexiform layer (IPL).8 CNTN2-expressing cells aggregate homophilically with each other, but not heterophilically with cells expressing CNTN1, CNTN3, CNTN4, or CNTN5. By contrast, cells expressing CNTN1, CNTN3, and CNTN5 do not homophilically aggregate.8 CNTN4 also forms homophilic interactions in these cell-based assays, yet does not bind CNTN2.8 While CNTN1 does not homodimerize, it does bind partners related to CNTNs, like neurofascin 155, heterophilically.9 CNTN2 is particularly versatile because neuronal CNTN2 is found not just on axons, but also on neuronal cell bodies and neuronal processes,6,10 and it is expressed not just on neurons but also on oligodendrocytes.11 Alterations in CNTN2 function are linked to epilepsy12,13 and multiple sclerosis,14,15; CNTN2 knockout mice demonstrate spontaneous seizures, motor, and behavioral deficits, as well as cognitive impairments.12,16 Deficits in CNTN4, CNTN5, and CNTN6 function are implicated in autism spectrum disorder;17–21 CNTN4 is linked to schizophrenia,22,23 delayed language development,24 and eating disorders,25 while CNTN6 is linked to Tourette’s syndrome.26 Despite the importance of CNTN2 (and its family members) in neural circuit formation and the pathogenesis of neurological and neuropsychiatric disorders, its molecular mechanisms are still poorly understood.

Many studies have sought to delineate the structure-function relationships of CNTNs given their important roles in brain development that stem from their adhesive and protein-network organizing properties. CNTN1 through CNTN6 share the same domain structure of six Ig-like domains (Ig1 through Ig6), four fibronectin III-like domains (here abbreviated to FN1, FN2, FN3, and FN4) tethered to the cell membrane by a glycosylphosphatidylinositol (GPI) anchor with 45–65% sequence identity.3 The Ig1-Ig4 region forms a horseshoe-shape in crystal structures whereby Ig1-Ig2 doubles back on Ig3-Ig4 connected by a six-residue linker between Ig2 and Ig3.9,27–29 The C-terminal domains form elongated arrangements in crystal structures of fragments, e.g., Ig5-FN2 from CNTN3 and FN1-FN3 from CNTN1–6.30 Full-length CNTN1 appears to adopt an extended structure in small-angle X-ray scattering (SAXS) studies, and the crystal structure of the CNTN1 Ig1-Ig6 fragment (PDB ID 7ol2) reveals that the Ig1-Ig4 horseshoe connects to Ig5-Ig6 in an extended arrangement.9 Little is known about the architecture of full-length CNTN2 or how its domains are arranged to provide a structural platform that can promote cell-cell adhesion and the organization of protein networks.

The mechanism of CNTN2 homophilic assembly is controversial. Strategies to reveal the molecular mechanism have leveraged aggregation of CNTN2-expressing cells, binding of CNTN2 (attached to beads or as FC-fusion proteins) to the surface of CNTN2-expressing cells and deletion mutants to map interacting domains.10,11,27,31–35 However, it has not been possible to determine the stoichiometry of the CNTN2 homomeric complex, or whether the complex occurs solely in trans (an interaction that is easily measured via cell aggregation studies) or can also occur in cis or even involves cis-assisted trans-interactions.36–38 Likewise, it is unclear which domains in CNTN2 are required for homophilic interaction.36–38 Guided by crystal structures of Ig1-Ig4, models for CNTN2 homophilic interactions have been proposed. In one model, Ig1-Ig4 headpieces form a continuous, anti-parallel array, and in another model, they mediate a discrete, symmetric dimer with the FN1-FN4 regions further aligning the monomers in cis or in trans.27,28,38 Surprisingly, attempts to unravel the mechanism of CNTN2 homophilic assembly using biochemical and biophysical methods such as analytical ultracentrifugation (AUC), surface plasmon resonance (SPR) and size exclusion chromatography (SEC) have been lacking. However recently, the 3D structure of the CNTN1 Ig1-Ig6 in complex with a related protein, neurofascin155 Ig1-Ig6, (PDB ID 7ol4) revealed the Ig1-Ig4 headpieces to bind heterophilically via their respective Ig2 domains forming an intermolecular super β-sheet very similar to homodimers formed by neurofascin155 Ig1-Ig6 itself (PDB ID 7ok5).9 But it is not known if CNTN2 utilizes a similar mechanism to form a homomeric assembly.

In this study, we delineate the mechanism by which CNTN2 can form homodimers using biophysical methods, mass-spectrometry, structural biology, and cell-based assays. We show that CNTN2 forms homodimers with relatively weak ~200 nanomolar affinity in a concentration-dependent, transient manner consistent with a guidance molecule that carries out transitory interactions. Cryo-EM structures of the CNTN2 homodimer reveal a T-shaped particle. Site-directed mutagenesis confirms the critical role of Ig3, Ig4, Ig5, and Ig6 in mediating homophilic interaction of CNTN2 in cell-based assays. Furthermore, the molecular features of CNTNs in this region suggest a molecular explanation for the observed binding preference between the different CNTNs.

ResultsCNTN2 forms homophilic interactions.We generated the human CNTN2 ectodomain and a panel of fragments using baculovirus-mediated overexpression in High Five insect cells (Figure 1A; 1B; C). To investigate if purified CNTN2 ectodomain (CNTN2_FL) interacts homophilically, we used surface plasmon resonance (SPR) to test if the soluble ectodomain and its fragments bind CNTN2_FL immobilized on a biosensor (CNTN2_FLimm) (Figure 1D). We immobilized CNTN2_FL sparsely on the chip and used low concentrations of the soluble CNTN2 proteins to limit self-association of the mobile phase which would lead to an underestimation of the binding affinity. The resulting data could be fitted to a model describing a 1:1 (bimolecular) association reaction. Soluble CNTN2_FL bound to CNTN2_FLimm with a KD of 194.0 ± 55.0 nM (Figure 1D; 1F). Strikingly, while the soluble CNTN2_Ig1-Ig6 also still bound CNTN2_FLimm with KD of 341.9 ± 230.4 nM, the CNTN2_Ig1-Ig4 headpiece and the CNTN2_FN1-FN4 fragment showed negligible binding (Figure 1D; 1F). To assess if the Ig domains alone were sufficient for homophilic binding, we also immobilized CNTN2_Ig1-Ig6 (CNTN2_Ig1-Ig6imm) (Figure 1E). Both soluble CNTN2_FL and CNTN2_Ig1-Ig6 bound to CNTN2_Ig1-Ig6imm (KD 955.9 ± 502.8 nM and KD of 650.1 ± 494.7 nM, respectively). Again, neither CNTN2_Ig1-Ig4 nor CNTN2_FN1-FN4 displayed significant binding (Figure 1E; 1F). We observed analogous binding trends for CNTN2_FL and the different fragments under Ca2+-free conditions as well (Suppl. Figure S1). The relatively low affinity of the full-length CNTN2 ectodomain for itself (KD ~0.2 μM) and the fast on- and off-rates (ka ~6.1 × 104 M−1s−1 and kd ~1.3 × 10−2 s−1, respectively, see Figure 1D) suggest that, although homophilic interactions readily take place between CNTN2 molecules, they could be transient. Furthermore, CNTN2 homophilic interaction must involve a novel mode of interaction because neither the Ig1-Ig4 headpiece nor the FN1-FN4 region shows appreciable binding, despite previously being implicated in homophilic interactions.27,28,36–38CNTN2 homophilic assemblies as a function of protein concentration.To further investigate homomeric assemblies of CNTN2 in solution using orthogonal methods, we analyzed CNTN2_FL and fragments by sedimentation velocity AUC (SV-AUC) and SEC (Figure 2). CNTN2_Ig1-Ig6 showed protein-concentration dependent oligomerization, sedimenting at low concentration (0.2 mg/ml, 3.1 μM) as a single major species with S20,w of 6.5 S, but at higher concentrations as a mixture of larger species (tested up to 0.8 mg/ml, 12.4 μM) (Figure 2A). CNTN2_FL also converted to a larger species as a function of protein concentration (in the range 0.2 to 0.8 mg/ml (1.8 – 7.2 μM)) (Figure 2B). Analysis of CNTN2 and CNTN2_Ig1-Ig6 species by SEC was complicated by the fact that their elution profiles could be interpreted as proteins migrating as compact dimers or as elongated monomers (Figure 2C). For instance, CNTN2_FL has a predicted molecular weight (Mwpredict) of ~108.6 kDa based on its amino acid sequence, but the protein migrated with an apparent molecular weight (Mwapp) of ~221.9 ± 3.3 kDa on a Superose 6 Increase column. Similarly, CNTN2_Ig1-Ig6 has a Mwpredict ~65.3 kDa but migrated with Mwapp ~147.6 ± 0.4 kDa. As their Mwapp was roughly twice the Mwpredict, these data are compatible with CNTN2_FL and CNTN2_Ig1-Ig6 forming a homodimer. By contrast, for the fragments, CNTN2_Ig1-Ig4 and CNTN2_FN1-FN4, the Mwpredict and Mwapp matched more closely suggesting they adopt monomeric, though non-globular shapes. Strikingly, the homophilic association of CNTN2 as a function of protein concentration could be captured by SEC in the range of 0.5 mg/ml to 8 mg/ml (Figure 2D). As the protein concentration increased, CNTN2_FL shifted towards smaller elution volumes, representing species with larger hydrodynamic radii, while CNTN2 Ig1-Ig6 converted increasingly into a second, discrete larger species. By contrast, CNTN2_Ig1-Ig4 remained monomeric within this same protein concentration range. Taken together, these data suggest that the CNTN2 ectodomain interacts homophilically at low or sub-micromolar concentrations, forming an assembly that requires Ig1-Ig6, while Ig1-Ig4 alone is not sufficient.CNTN2 homomeric assemblies for structural studies.The relatively low affinity and transient nature of the CNTN2 homophilic interactions suggested by our SPR, AUC, and SEC studies presented an obstacle to probing the mechanism of CNTN2 assembly using structural methods. We, therefore, developed a method to trap CNTN2 homomers biochemically using disuccinimidyl sulfoxide (DSSO), a cleavable crosslinker that covalently connects pairs of lysines with an ~10.3 Å linker.39 Both full-length cross-linked CNTN2 (CNTN2_FL_XL) and cross-linked CNTN2 Ig1-Ig6 (CNTN2_Ig1-Ig6_XL) migrated as well-defined, discrete species with significantly larger size and shape by SEC and SDS-PAGE than their non-trapped versions (Figure 3A; Figure 3B). Although SEC cannot reveal accurate estimates for the molecular weight of non-globular, flexible proteins, CNTN2_FL_XL and CNTN2_Ig1-Ig6_XL both migrated with an Mwapp roughly twice as large as their non-trapped versions (Mwapp 597.4 ± 5.8 kDa compared to 221.9 ± 3.3 kDa, and Mwapp 287.1 ± 2.7 kDa compared to 147.6 ± 0.4 kDa, respectively) (see Figure 3C; Figure 2C). However, neither CNTN2-Ig1-Ig4 nor CNTN2_FN1-FN4 was trapped significantly by DSSO in agreement with our SPR results that these fragments do not form homophilic interactions efficiently (Suppl. Figure S2). Importantly, the stabilized proteins remained as uniform, high-molecular-weight assemblies even at low protein concentration in SV-AUC studies as shown for CNTN2_Ig1-Ig6_XL (0.2 mg/ml; 3.1 μM), suggesting that they were suitable for structural studies (Figure 3D; Suppl. Figure S3). Taken together, these data suggest that CNTN2 homomeric assemblies can be successfully stabilized into a discrete species that requires minimally the Ig-containing region of the extracellular domain.3D architecture of CNTN2 homodimers.To probe the 3D arrangement of CNTN2 homodimers, we used cryo-electron microscopy (cryo-EM). We imaged 9,049 micrographs and picked 1,742,667 particles to determine a near-atomic-resolution structure of CNTN2_FL_XL homodimers. These exhibited an irregular shape with two ring-shaped densities connected by two long tails (Figure 4A; 4B). To reduce the noise and enhance image contrast, we selected 1,487,361 particles after calculating reference-free 2D class averages (Suppl. Figure S4; Suppl. Table S1). The class averages displayed two intertwined molecules arranged like a ribbon bow, with at one end the domains forming two loops, and at the other, the two loose ends (Figure 4C; Suppl. Figure S4B). To reconstruct the 3D density map of CNTN2_FL_XL (Figure 4D), we utilized the single-particle analysis approach by classifying and averaging particle shapes, which yielded an electron potential map at a resolution of 3.54 Å (Figure 4E). The density map revealed a dimeric structure, formed by two monomers each adopting the shape of a number ‘9’, which assembled to form the ribbon bow. The density for each monomer showed nine globular densities, corresponding to Ig1-Ig6 and FN1-FN3. Density for the FN4 domain was visible only at a very low contour level, likely due to conformational variability between the particles, causing it to be eliminated during the averaging procedure. The highest resolution was achieved where the loops of the bow cross over (Figure 4D). Indeed, in this region, clear density was seen for β-strands and many side chains (Figure 4F). To fit the cryo-EM density map, we constructed an initial model by rigid-body docking crystal structures of individual domains into the density and then adjusting main chains and side chains (Ig1-Ig4, PDB IDs 2om5, human CNTN2, resolution of 3.3 Å; Ig5-Ig6: PDB ID 7ol2, mouse CNTN1, 3.9 Å; FN1-FN3, PDB ID 5e7l, mouse CNTN2, 2.0 Å) as well as consulting the predicted AlphaFold2 model40 of human CNTN2 (AF-A1L3A3). The domains Ig1 through FN1 could be unambiguously fitted into the density map. The density for FN2- FN3 was less clear so side chains were not further optimized, and the weak density for the FN4 domain permitted only the domain to be generally placed but not further refined. Although the seven predicted N-linked glycosylation sites located on the CNTN2_Ig1-Ig6 were modified by glycans based on our mass spectrometry studies (Suppl. Each Ig1-Ig4 headpiece is oriented so that the tips formed by Ig2 and Ig3 point outwards on opposite sides of the particle maximizing solvent accessibility and exposure. The Ig6-FN1-FN2-FN3-FN4 region forms two parallel, extended stalks with the two FN1 domains coming within 8 Å of each other. Table S1).Validating the existence of CNTN2 homodimers by cross-linking mass spectrometry (XL-MS).To validate the arrangement of the individual ectodomains in the CNTN2 homodimer, we investigated our CNTN2_Ig1-Ig6_XL and CNTN2_FL_XL particles by XL-MS. MS analysis of CNTN2_Ig1-Ig6_XL revealed four pairs of DSSO cross-linked lysines, while ten pairs were identified in CNTN2_FL_XL particles that included all four pairs found in CNTN2_Ig1-Ig6_XL (Table 1). We mapped the identified cross-linked pairs onto our cryo-EM structures, applying the commonly used cutoff of <~26–30 Å between DSSO cross-linked lysine Cα atoms that accounts for the possibility of different rotamers and/or disordered side chains in 3D structures.41 Importantly, the pair Lys353 (Ig4)-Lys566 (Ig6) was found covalently attached by DSSO consistent with their proximity in the cryo-EM structures CNTN2_Ig1-Ig6_XL (16.5 Å between Cα-atoms) and CNTN2_FL_XL (15.7 Å between Cα-atoms), respectively. Interestingly, the Lys702 crosslink suggested that the FN1 domains can approach each other within ~11 to 30 Å, i.e., much closer than seen in the cryo-EM structure of CNTN2_FL_XL. It is possible that the FN-domain-containing region is flexible and adopts multiple arrangements (one of which was detected by XL-MS). Figure S2). CNTN2 homophilic assemblies as a function of protein concentration.To further investigate homomeric assemblies of CNTN2 in solution using orthogonal methods, we analyzed CNTN2_FL and fragments by sedimentation velocity AUC (SV-AUC) and SEC (Figure 2). CNTN2_Ig1-Ig6 showed protein-concentration dependent oligomerization, sedimenting at low concentration (0.2 mg/ml, 3.1 μM) as a single major species with S20,w of 6.5 S, but at higher concentrations as a mixture of larger species (tested up to 0.8 mg/ml, 12.4 μM) (Figure 2A). CNTN2_FL also converted to a larger species as a function of protein concentration (in the range 0.2 to 0.8 mg/ml (1.8 – 7.2 μM)) (Figure 2B). Analysis of CNTN2 and CNTN2_Ig1-Ig6 species by SEC was complicated by the fact that their elution profiles could be interpreted as proteins migrating as compact dimers or as elongated monomers (Figure 2C). For instance, CNTN2_FL has a predicted molecular weight (Mwpredict) of ~108.6 kDa based on its amino acid sequence, but the protein migrated with an apparent molecular weight (Mwapp) of ~221.9 ± 3.3 kDa on a Superose 6 Increase column. Similarly, CNTN2_Ig1-Ig6 has a Mwpredict ~65.3 kDa but migrated with Mwapp ~147.6 ± 0.4 kDa. As their Mwapp was roughly twice the Mwpredict, these data are compatible with CNTN2_FL and CNTN2_Ig1-Ig6 forming a homodimer. By contrast, for the fragments, CNTN2_Ig1-Ig4 and CNTN2_FN1-FN4, the Mwpredict and Mwapp matched more closely suggesting they adopt monomeric, though non-globular shapes. Strikingly, the homophilic association of CNTN2 as a function of protein concentration could be captured by SEC in the range of 0.5 mg/ml to 8 mg/ml (Figure 2D). As the protein concentration increased, CNTN2_FL shifted towards smaller elution volumes, representing species with larger hydrodynamic radii, while CNTN2 Ig1-Ig6 converted increasingly into a second, discrete larger species. By contrast, CNTN2_Ig1-Ig4 remained monomeric within this same protein concentration range. Taken together, these data suggest that the CNTN2 ectodomain interacts homophilically at low or sub-micromolar concentrations, forming an assembly that requires Ig1-Ig6, while Ig1-Ig4 alone is not sufficient.

CNTN2 homomeric assemblies for structural studies.The relatively low affinity and transient nature of the CNTN2 homophilic interactions suggested by our SPR, AUC, and SEC studies presented an obstacle to probing the mechanism of CNTN2 assembly using structural methods. We, therefore, developed a method to trap CNTN2 homomers biochemically using disuccinimidyl sulfoxide (DSSO), a cleavable crosslinker that covalently connects pairs of lysines with an ~10.3 Å linker.39 Both full-length cross-linked CNTN2 (CNTN2_FL_XL) and cross-linked CNTN2 Ig1-Ig6 (CNTN2_Ig1-Ig6_XL) migrated as well-defined, discrete species with significantly larger size and shape by SEC and SDS-PAGE than their non-trapped versions (Figure 3A; Figure 3B). Although SEC cannot reveal accurate estimates for the molecular weight of non-globular, flexible proteins, CNTN2_FL_XL and CNTN2_Ig1-Ig6_XL both migrated with an Mwapp roughly twice as large as their non-trapped versions (Mwapp 597.4 ± 5.8 kDa compared to 221.9 ± 3.3 kDa, and Mwapp 287.1 ± 2.7 kDa compared to 147.6 ± 0.4 kDa, respectively) (see Figure 3C; Figure 2C). However, neither CNTN2-Ig1-Ig4 nor CNTN2_FN1-FN4 was trapped significantly by DSSO in agreement with our SPR results that these fragments do not form homophilic interactions efficiently (Suppl. Figure S2). Importantly, the stabilized proteins remained as uniform, high-molecular-weight assemblies even at low protein concentration in SV-AUC studies as shown for CNTN2_Ig1-Ig6_XL (0.2 mg/ml; 3.1 μM), suggesting that they were suitable for structural studies (Figure 3D; Suppl. Figure S3). Taken together, these data suggest that CNTN2 homomeric assemblies can be successfully stabilized into a discrete species that requires minimally the Ig-containing region of the extracellular domain.

Molecular mechanism of CNTN2 homodimer interaction.

Our cryo-EM structures, XL-MS and SPR data suggested that the CNTN2 homophilic interactions were largely driven by Ig4 from one monomer (‘mol A’) docking against Ig5 and Ig6 from the other monomer (‘mol B’) in the homodimer. We identified residues from Ig3-Ig6 that participated in the two symmetrical interfaces within the homodimer, i.e., Ig3 (Phe298), Ig4 (Glu328, Leu330, Lys331, Ala351, Arg355); Ig5 (Leu427, Arg506); and Ig6 (Thr537, Met538, Asp539, Val592, Val593) (Figure 5A; Suppl. Table S2; Suppl. Data S1A; S1B). Key features of each interface included two hydrophobic “pegs”, Phe298 (Ig3) and Lys353 (Ig4) from one molecule packing against Pro536 (Ig6) and Thr537 (Ig6) from the other, and Leu330 (Ig4) from one molecule packing against several residues from the other, including Ig5 (Pro429, Arg506) and Ig6 (Met538, Val592, Val593). The interface also featured prominent intermolecular salt bridges between Glu328 (Ig4) and Arg506 (Ig5), and Arg355 (Ig4) and Glu567 (Ig6), respectively.

CNTNs work as homophilic and heterophilic cell adhesion molecules.

CNTN family members have unique biological roles in the brain by leveraging distinct cell-type- and region-specific expression patterns, and their distinct profile of binding partners.3 For instance, in the retina, CNTNs promote the targeting of neurites to specific lamina within the IPL, a region where ~50 types of interneurons and ~25 types of retinal ganglion cells specifically connect to form layers for processing light detected by photoreceptors and passing this information to the brain.8 There, CNTN1 through CNTN5 are uniquely expressed by select subsets of retinal neurons; they are thought to form unique patterns of homophilic and heterophilic interactions.8 Indeed, in cell-based assays, CNTN2- and CNTN4-expressing cells aggregate homophilically, while those expressing CNTN1, CNTN3, and CNTN5 do not; furthermore, CNTN2-expressing cells do not aggregate with cells expressing CNTN1, CNTN3, CNTN4, or CNTN5.8 Likewise, in vivo, CNTN2 exhibits homophilic interactions that facilitate the migration of spinal cord neurons.6 Also, cell-surface binding studies confirm that soluble CNTN2-FC-fusion protein binds to cells expressing CNTN2, but not CNTN1,10 and biophysical studies show that CNTN1 does not homodimerize though it binds partners heterophilically.9 Yet the underlying mechanisms with which CNTNs form such biologically important and distinct homophilic versus heterophilic interactions have remained enigmatic until now.

Our cryo-EM structures of the CNTN2 homodimer suggest a complex, multi-level basis for the specificity CNTN interactions (Figure 6). First, the homodimer interface in CNTN2 is not conserved among CNTN1–6 (Figure 6B; Suppl. Data S1B). This interface features a specific distribution of hydrophobic, polar, and charged residues that are only partially conserved (Figure 6B; 6C). For instance, key residues, Phe298, Leu330, and Arg355, which are critical for CNTN2 homodimerization, based on our cell-based studies, are poorly conserved (Figure 6B; Suppl. Data S1B). CNTN2 Phe298 and Leu330 each form hydrophobic pegs that insert into a hydrophobic pocket where precise shape complementary is paramount. In the CNTN family (CNTN1/2/3/4/5/6), CNTN2 F298 (L/F/Q/Q/L/Q) is replaced by the much smaller, polar glutamine in CNTN3 and CNTN4, while CNTN1 and CNTN5 carry the smaller leucine (Figure 6D). Leu330 (V/L/V/I/V/E) shows even more pronounced variations, with CNTN6 harboring a negatively charged glutamic acid, with valine and isoleucine requiring adjustments to the CNTN2 surface to accommodate the smaller or differently shaped residues (Figure 6D). Arg355 (I/R/K/K/R/N), which forms a critical salt bridge with Glu567 in a variable loop, appears incompatible with CNTN1 (which carries an isoleucine) and CNTN6 (which carries an asparagine) (Figure 6D). Thus, the CNTNs have very different side chains at key positions on the CNTN2 homodimerization surfaces.

A second level of regulation to control the binding specificity between the CNTNs is likely generated via N-linked glycosylation. Human CNTN1 carries an N-linked glycosylation site at the edge of the CNTN2 homodimer interface (Asn338), which is conserved in CNTN5 (Asn397), and attached carbohydrates would be expected to cause steric clashes between subunits disrupting interaction (Figure 6E; Suppl. S5A; S5B and Data S1B). Proximity to the homodimerization interface alone, however, is not sufficient to predict the impact of N-linked carbohydrates, because N-linked glycosylation at CNTN2 Asn498 (conserved in CNTN1 and CNTN3, Suppl. Figure S5B) near the interface does not prevent homodimerization. The carbohydrates are likely very flexible and can bend away from the interface. Indeed, the carbohydrate branches attached to CNTN2 observed in the cryo-EM structures are highly truncated compared to what is observed by MS (Suppl. Figure S5B). Furthermore, CNTN4 does not bind CNTN2, though it can form homodimers,8 and both CNTN2 and CNTN4 lack any N-linked glycosylation sites near the homodimer interface that might disrupt a CNTN2:CNTN4 complex and explain their lack of interaction. Thus, binding specificity between CNTN family members is rooted in a complex manner based on the amino acid sequence at the homodimer interface and N-linked glycosylation.

Of note, during preparation and submission of this manuscript, a report describing the crystal structure of mouse CNTN2 Ig1-Ig6 was published.50,71 The crystal packing shows homodimeric interactions via Ig2, as well as via Ig3-Ig6 (like we observe in our cryo-EM study of CNTN2 Ig1-Ig6_XL; Suppl. Fig. S4). Our studies show unambiguously that Ig3-Ig6 mediates CNTN2 homophilic interaction and that N-linked glycosylation alone cannot explain the preference of different CNTNs for homophilic versus heterophilic interactions.

This versatility enables different partners to be assembled into protein complexes (Figure 6F). The tips of the headpieces (Ig2-Ig3) which are thought to recruit heterophilic partners, project out from either side of the T-shaped dimer into the solvent. However, Ig2 is not needed for homophilic binding based on our structural studies and cell-based studies testing mutants of Ig2, CNTN2 Phe173Ala, and Phe173Asp. The dimensions of the CNTN2 homodimer enable the protein to fulfill multiple biological roles, e.g., serving as an axon guidance molecule, or organizing axo-glial contacts at the juxtaparanodes of the Nodes of Ranvier. The 3D architecture of the CNTN2 homodimer, as observed in our cryo-EM structures, allows for both homophilic and heterophilic interactions to occur simultaneously, facilitating both in-cis and in-trans interactions. This versatility enables different partners to be assembled into protein complexes (Figure 6F). Together, the precise symphony of protein complexes formed likely enables CNTN2, along with other CNTN family members, to uniquely shape cell-cell contacts in neural circuits and exert specific protein network-organizing functions.

For quantification, sets of cells were stained simultaneously and imaged with identical settings. For the in situ surface binding assays, the signal of bound soluble (His)6-tagged CNTN2_FL protein was thresholded to measure the number of extracted puncta on HA-positive areas. The average intensity and area of surface HA-tagged signal was also measured. COS7 cells expressing similar levels of HA-tagged proteins were selected to quantify bound soluble CNTN2-His protein.

LLC; #MIR2305) and cultured in Dulbecco’s modified Eagle’s medium (DMEM) (Gibco; #11965118) containing 10% (v/v) fetal bovine serum (FBS) (Wisent; #080–150). After 48 hours, the transfected HEK293T cells were harvested by trypsinization and resuspended in 250 μl of DMEM containing 10% (v/v) FBS before mixing with the appropriate GFP- or mCherry-expressing cells in a 1:1 ratio. The mixed transfected HEK293T cells groups (containing both GFP- and mCherry-labeled cells) were rotated at room temperature for 2 hours to allow the cells to aggregate. Cell mixtures (500 μl) were added to 24-well plates and then imaged on an IncuCyte S3 Live-Cell Analysis System (Sartorius) with a 4x air objective. Images were acquired as 16-bit grayscale, and the aggregation state quantified by Metamorph 7.8. The signals corresponding to mCherry and GFP channels were thresholded and binarized, and the resulting images were summed to obtain the total number of aggregates composed of magenta and green cells. The relative proportion of cell area from mCherry-labeled cells versus GFP-labeled ones per aggregate was measured.
