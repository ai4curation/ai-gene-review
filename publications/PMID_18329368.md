# Active caspase-1 is a regulator of unconventional protein secretion.
**Authors:** Keller M, Rüegg A, Werner S, Beer HD
**Journal:** Cell (2008)
**DOI:** [10.1016/j.cell.2007.12.040](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2007.12.040)

## Abstract

1. Cell. 2008 Mar 7;132(5):818-31. doi: 10.1016/j.cell.2007.12.040.

Active caspase-1 is a regulator of unconventional protein secretion.

Keller M(1), Rüegg A, Werner S, Beer HD.

Author information:
(1)Institute of Cell Biology, Department of Biology, ETH Zurich, CH-8093 Zurich,
Switzerland.

Mammalian cells export most proteins by the endoplasmic
reticulum/Golgi-dependent pathway. However, some proteins are secreted via
unconventional, poorly understood mechanisms. The latter include the
proinflammatory cytokines interleukin(IL)-1beta, IL-18, and IL-33, which require
activation by caspase-1 for biological activity. Caspase-1 itself is activated
by innate immune complexes, the inflammasomes. Here we show that secretion of
the leaderless proteins proIL-1alpha, caspase-1, and fibroblast growth factor
(FGF)-2 depends on caspase-1 activity. Although proIL-1alpha and FGF-2 are not
substrates of the protease, we demonstrated their physical interaction.
Secretome analysis using iTRAQ proteomics revealed caspase-1-mediated secretion
of other leaderless proteins with known or unknown extracellular functions.
Strikingly, many of these proteins are involved in inflammation, cytoprotection,
or tissue repair. These results provide evidence for an important role of
caspase-1 in unconventional protein secretion. By this mechanism, stress-induced
activation of caspase-1 directly links inflammation to cytoprotection, cell
survival, and regenerative processes.
