## Abstract

Trichocyte keratins differ considerably from their epithelial cousins in having
a higher number of cysteine residues, of which the greater proportion are
located in the head and tail regions of these proteins. Coupled with this is the
presence of a large number of keratin associated proteins in these fibres that
are high in their cysteine content, the high sulfur proteins and ultra-high
sulfur proteins. Thus it is the crosslinking that occurs between the cysteines
in the keratins and KAPs that is an important determinant in the functionality
of wool and hair fibres. Studies have shown the majority of the cysteine
residues are involved in internal crosslinking in the KAPs leaving only a few
specific cysteines to interact with the keratins, with most evidence pointing to
interactions between these KAP cysteines and the keratin head groups.
