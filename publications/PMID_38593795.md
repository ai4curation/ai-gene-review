# Human V-ATPase function is positively and negatively regulated by TLDc proteins.

Proteins that contain a highly conserved TLDc domain (Tre2/Bub2/Cdc16 LysM
domain catalytic) offer protection against oxidative stress and are widely
implicated in neurological health and disease. How this family of proteins
exerts their function, however, is poorly understood. We have recently found
that the yeast TLDc protein, Oxr1p, inhibits the proton pumping vacuolar ATPase
(V-ATPase) by inducing disassembly of the pump. While loss of TLDc protein
function in mammals shares disease phenotypes with V-ATPase defects, whether
TLDc proteins impact human V-ATPase activity directly is unclear. Here we
examine the effects of five human TLDc proteins, TLDC2, NCOA7, OXR1, TBC1D24,
and mEAK7 on the activity of the human V-ATPase. We find that while TLDC2,
TBC1D24, and the TLDc domains of OXR1 and NCOA7 inhibit V-ATPase by inducing
enzyme disassembly, mEAK7 activates the pump. The data thus shed new light both
on mammalian TLDc protein function and V-ATPase regulation.

INTRODUCTION

The vacuolar H+-ATPase, or V-ATPase, is a large (~1 MDa) multisubunit proton pump found on the endomembrane system of organelles in all eukaryotic cells and the plasma membrane of specialized acid secreting cells in higher organisms.1 V-ATPase-mediated acidification of intracellular compartments is critical for such processes as endocytosis, synaptic vesicle loading, autophagy, protein degradation and antigen processing whereas acidification of the extracellular space is required for bone remodeling, urinary acidification, and sperm maturation.1–5 V-ATPase also plays a vital role in signaling pathways including mTOR, Notch and Wnt.2 In line with its role in fundamental cellular processes, total loss of V-ATPase function is embryonic lethal in animals.6 However, aberrant enzyme function is associated with osteopetrosis, renal tubular acidosis, sensorineural deafness, immunodeficiency, congenital disorders of glycosylation and neurological disease.7–13

V-ATPase is a member of the family of rotary motor ATPases.14,15 The enzyme is composed of a cytoplasmic V1-ATPase, containing subunits A3B3CDE3FG3H, and an integral proton translocation subcomplex called Vo, formed from core subunits a,d,e,f,c9,c” and two “accessory” proteins, Ac45 (ATP6AP1) and prorenin receptor (ATP6AP2) (Figure 1A, left).16–19 V1 contains three catalytic ATP binding sites located at alternating interfaces of a hexamer of A and B subunits.1,20 ATP hydrolysis drives rotation of a central rotor composed of subunits D,F,d and ultimately, a proton-carrying ring of c-subunits (c-ring) past two aqueous half channels in subunit a for proton transport.21,22 A stator complex (subunits C, E, G, H, and a) holds the catalytic hexamer stationary relative to the proton channel for efficient energy coupling.23–25 Several high-resolution structures are now available of the V-ATPase from yeast to human that show the central rotor of the enzyme in three distinct angular orientations (“states” 1–3) separated by 120°.19,26,27

An important mode of V-ATPase regulation is by reversible disassembly of the enzyme into autoinhibited V1 and Vo subcomplexes (Figure 1A, right).28–30 Regulation of V-ATPase’s assembly state has been linked to diverse signals such as nutrient levels, viral infection, pH, and cell maturation.28,31–35 Although reversible disassembly is well characterized on a cellular level in yeast, how different cellular cues initiate this dramatic structural rearrangement at the level of the enzyme has remained largely elusive.

Recently, we have found that disassembly of yeast V-ATPase can be induced by binding of Oxidation Resistance 1 (Oxr1p).26 Yeast Oxr1p belongs to the TLDc family of proteins that are characterized by a conserved C-terminal TLDc (Tre2/Bub2/Cdc16 (TBC) Lysin domain catalytic) domain.36 The human genome encodes at least five such proteins; nuclear receptor co-activator 7 (NCOA7), OXR1, TBC1 domain family member 24 (TBC1D24), mammalian enhancer of Akt 7 (mEAK7) and TLDC2 (Figure 1B).37 While widely expressed, OXR1, NCOA7 and TBC1D24 are enriched in the brain, TLDC2 in B-type renal intercalated cells and mEAK7 is tethered to the lysosomal membrane, required for its role in mTOR activation.38–43 OXR1 was first identified in a screen for proteins capable of protecting cells against oxidative damage, but the mechanism of action remains unclear.44 In addition to the oxidative stress response, the members of this family have been implicated in such processes as nuclear receptor activation, longevity, mTOR signaling, vesicular traffic, lysosomal function, and the innate immune response.40,42,45–48

TLDc proteins are important players in neurological health and disease.37 Loss of OXR1 or TBC1D24 function results in severe and sometimes overlapping clinical phenotypes, including neurodegeneration, developmental delay, hearing loss and seizures.39,49–51 Moreover, OXR1 is upregulated in the spinal cord of amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS) patients.38 Neurological manifestations of NCOA7 loss of function correlate with social and behavioral phenotypes, and loss of NCOA7 has been observed in autism spectrum disorder.40,52 Notably, loss of NCOA7 is also associated with increased urine pH and partial distal renal tubular acidosis, phenocopying loss of renal V-ATPase subunit isoform function.40,53,54 Indeed, both OXR1 and NCOA7 were identified as high scoring interactors in a renal V-ATPase interactomics study and NCOA7 co-precipitates with the V1-ATPase subcomplex from brain.40,55 Moreover, mEAK7 has been found to co-purify with V-ATPase from several tissues and cryoEM structures are now available of the mEAK7 bound enzyme.56–58 More recently, direct interactions have been observed between five mammalian TLDc family members and the V-ATPase.59

Together, the available data indicates that V-ATPase function or regulation may depend on the interaction or action of TLDc proteins. As a step towards better understanding such relationships, we have purified human TBC1D24, mEAK7, TLDC2 and the TLDc domains of OXR1 and NCOA7 to examine their impact on human V-ATPase function directly. We find that TBC1D24, TLDC2 and the TLDc domains of OXR1 and NCOA7 inhibit human V-ATPase activity. In contrast, we find that mEAK7 is an activator of the human V-ATPase and using available structural data we speculate on the mechanism by which mEAK7 enhances enzyme activity.

RESULTSFive mammalian TLDc proteins have been found to interact with V-ATPase and mutations in or loss of function of some TLDc proteins share disease phenotypes in common with V-ATPase mutants, suggesting functional interdependence.40,53,59 The direct effects of such interactions on V-ATPase function have only been explored for mEAK7, however, no significant impact was observed in these studies.56,57 Here, we sought to examine whether and how a broader set of TLDc proteins influence V-ATPase activity using purified components. Recently, our lab described a method for purification of active human V-ATPase in native lipid-containing nanodiscs.60 In this system, FLAG-tagged subunit a isoform 4 (a4) is stably expressed in suspension HEK 293F cells where it assembles with endogenously expressed subunits (Figure 1C) to yield active and functionally coupled V-ATPase (Figure 1D).Construct design and purification of human TLDc proteinsTLDc family members are characterized by a C-terminal TLDc domain of ~160 amino acids in length, the 3-D structure of which is highly conserved from yeast to human (Figure S1). Most TLDc proteins have N-terminal extensions containing other conserved domains and can range in overall size from ~200–950 residues (Figure 1B).37,38 In accord with the finding that the TLDc domain alone is sufficient for function, TLDC2 and the shortest splice variants of OXR1 and NCOA7 are composed almost entirely of the TLDc domain.37–39,47,61,62 Here, we purified full length TBC1D24 via over-expression in human cells (293F) and bacterially expressed and purified the TLDc domains of human OXR1 (OXR1TLDc) and NCOA7 (NCOA7TLDc), full length TLDC2, and mEAK7 lacking the N-terminal myristoylation motif (Figure 1E, S2), to examine their impact on human V-ATPase activity directly. With exception of TBC1D24, TLDc proteins were expressed from a modified pET28a vector carrying N-terminal 7xHis and Avi tags and an HRV 3C protease cleavage site. TBC1D24 carrying N-terminal FLAG and Avi tags and an HRV 3C cleavage site was expressed from a pCDNA3.1 vector transiently transfected into 293F cells. Reference sequences used for TLDc protein amino acid numbering can be found in the legend to Figure 1B and STAR Methods. Initial expression and purification of full length mEAK7 (amino acids 1–456) revealed substantial degradation and as these studies began before experimental structural models were available, AlphaFold was employed to predict mEAK7’s structure for examination of possible truncation sites to improve protein stability and expression.63 Guided by the AlphaFold model, we removed residues 1–13 and found that the truncated construct allowed high-level expression of soluble and stable mEAK7 14–456. For NCOA7, we initially expressed the short interferon-inducible isoform (also referred to as NCOA7-AS, alternative start), that in addition to the TLDc domain, contains 25 N-terminal amino acids (1-MRGQRLPLDIQIFYCARPDEEPFVK-25) encoded by a unique exon (exon 10a).47,64 In our hands, when expressed in E. coli, NCOA7-AS was found in inclusion bodies and while we were able to refold the protein, this was inefficient and the limited yield impacted our ability to carry out necessary experiments. Thus, NCOA7-AS was truncated at the same sites as were used for crystallization of the protein65 (pdb 7obp, residues 54–219 of NCOA7-AS), which expressed in soluble form as expected. As this truncation site yielded ample, soluble protein, the equivalent site was chosen for generation of OXR1TLDc. Using InterPro for defining TLDc domain boundaries in the amino acid sequences of long isoforms of OXR1 and NCOA7 (Figure 1B), the OXR1TLDc, construct (amino acids 704–873) includes seven, and NCOA7TLDc, (amino acids 777–942) three residues N-terminal to the TLDc domain.66 TLDC2 (amino acids 1–215) was expressed in full length form (Figure 1B). Production of TBC1D24 in E. coli was unsuccessful, with little to no expression and upregulation of chaperones, whereas expression in 293F cells yielded milligram quantities of protein in soluble form. The recombinant TLDc proteins elute as monomeric species at their expected molecular masses on size exclusion chromatography with exception of TBC1D24 that elutes as a larger species from the column, perhaps reflecting a more elongated shape of the molecule (Figure S2).TLDc proteins modulate human V-ATPase activityPurified human V-ATPase was mixed with increasing concentrations of TLDc proteins and pre-incubated overnight before measuring ATPase activity. The data show that NCOA7TLDc, TLDC2, OXR1TLDc and TBC1D24 all exhibited an inhibitory effect on the V-ATPase in a concentration dependent manner but displayed different efficiencies (Figure 2A–2D). NCOA7TLDc was the most potent V-ATPase inhibitor, showing ~50% inhibition when pre-incubated at a 75-fold molar excess (75×, ~5 μM) over V-ATPase, whereas 150× of TLDC2 or TBC1D24 and 300× OXR1TLDc, were required to achieve the same level of inhibition (see dashed lines in Figure 2A–2D). Since samples were diluted ~1:45 into the assay for measuring ATPase activity, this means that the effective TLDc protein concentrations acting on the enzyme during turnover were in the low to mid nanomolar range. This suggests that the efficiency of TLDc protein-mediated V-ATPase inhibition is significantly higher than would be implied from the pre-incubation TLDc protein concentrations.Strikingly, and in contrast to the other TLDc proteins, mEAK7 was found to increase V-ATPase activity in a concentration dependent fashion starting at a 75-fold molar excess and reaching saturation at a 300-fold molar excess over V-ATPase (Figure 2E). Again, samples were diluted into the ATPase assay, yielding final assay concentrations of TLDc proteins in the nanomolar to low micromolar range (~40 nM – 1.3 μM). To ensure that the observed activity enhancement was not due to crowding or an artifact due to high concentration of added protein, we carried out control ATPase assays on V-ATPase pre-incubated in presence of 60 μM BSA and found no impact on V-ATPase activity (Figure 2E, gray bar). Thus, while some TLDc proteins inhibit V-ATPase, at least one, mEAK7, activates the enzyme.ATP enhances the inhibitory effect of human TLDc proteinsInterestingly, for samples pre-incubated with OXR1TLDc, NCOA7TLDc, TLDC2 and TBC1D24, rather than V-ATPase being fully inhibited upon initiation of ATPase assays, we observed a decrease in activity over the relatively short ~15–30 min measurement time (Figure S3A–S3D). This decrease in V-ATPase activity despite pre-incubation with a large molar excess of TLDc proteins suggested that conditions in the assay enhance the inhibitory effects of the TLDc proteins. Due to the dynamic nature of the V-ATPase, TLDc domain proteins may bind preferentially to a specific conformation, or rotary state, of the enzyme populated during ATP hydrolysis. Support for TLDc proteins having a state-dependent preference for binding V-ATPase comes from recent structural studies showing yeast Oxr1p bound to the V1 complex in rotary state 1, whereas mEAK7 binds to the intact mammalian enzyme in state 2.26,57 Thus, if the preferred rotary state is not the dominant one in solution during the overnight incubation, then TLDc proteins would bind only to a subpopulation of V-ATPases and inhibition would appear inefficient. Therefore, we reasoned that pre-incubation of human V-ATPase and TLDc proteins with ATP would induce the enzyme to sample different rotary states and increase efficiency of binding/inhibition.To test the effect of ATP on TLDc protein-mediated inhibition, purified human V-ATPase was pre-incubated in presence or absence of MgATP with or without NCOA7TLDc, TLDC2, OXR1TLDc, TBC1D24, and two different concentrations of mEAK7 (20 μM and 65 μM), and the activity measured (Figure 3). Pre-incubation of V-ATPase with NCOA7TLDc, TLDC2 or OXR1TLDc, in presence of 2 mM ATP led to a ~20% reduction of the activity over preincubation with TLDc protein alone (Figure 3A–3C). While pre-incubation with ATP did not enhance TBC1D24’s inhibitory effect on the enzyme’s initial rate of ATP hydrolysis (Figure 3D), kinetic traces recorded in presence of TBC1D24 show a decrease of V-ATPase activity during turnover as seen for NCOA7TLDc, TLDC2 and OXR1TLDc (Figure S3D). On the other hand, mEAK7-mediated V-ATPase activation was not affected by pre-incubation with nucleotide (Figure 3E). Notably, addition of 1.3 μM mEAK7 directly into the ATPase assay led to a statistically significant increase in the rate of turnover over time (Figure S3E), indicating that mEAK7 can bind to, and enhance the activity of, V-ATPase that is actively hydrolyzing ATP.In summary, while pre-incubation of V-ATPase with NCOA7TLDc, TLDC2 or OXR1TLDc (but not TBC1D24) in presence of ATP leads to an enhancement in the efficiency of inhibition, pre-incubation with ATP does not alter the level of mEAK7-mediated V-ATPase activation. The ATP-dependent increase in V-ATPase inhibition for NCOA7TLDc, TLDC2, and OXR1TLDc is consistent with our expectations based on the observed preference for TLDc proteins binding a specific rotary state of the enzyme. The lack of an effect for ATP on mEAK7-mediated V-ATPase activation was unexpected since cryoEM data indicates a clear preference for binding rotary state 2.56–58 However, by virtue of mEAK7 being a V-ATPase activator, upon binding in state 2 the enzyme would be induced to undergo rotational catalysis and thus attain a new, and unfavorable, rotational state for binding.TLDc proteins inhibit human V-ATPase activity by inducing enzyme disassemblyIn addition to the role of ATP in attainment of a favorable rotational state for TLDc protein binding, we hypothesized that ATP may be inherent to the mechanism of TLDc protein-mediated V-ATPase inhibition as ATP hydrolysis has been shown to be important for regulation of V-ATPase activity itself.67 As V-ATPase is regulated by reversible disassembly into autoinhibited V1 and Vo subcomplexes (Figure 1A, right) and yeast Oxr1p has been shown to disassemble the pump, we were curious as to whether TLDc proteins have an impact on the assembly state of the human enzyme. To examine V-ATPase assembly state upon incubation with TLDc proteins in presence and absence of ATP, we immobilized the human enzyme via a FLAG tag on Vo subunit a and probed for the release of V1 using immunoblotting (Figure 4A–4B). After a 20 min incubation of immobilized V-ATPase with TLDc proteins with and without ATP, the beads were pelleted and the supernatant and pellet fractions examined for presence of V1 and Vo by probing for subunit A (part of V1) and FLAG (part of Vo). In absence of added ATP, there was little V1 complex in the supernatant for any of the TLDc proteins or controls (Figure 4B, C). Strikingly, upon incubation with NCOA7TLDc, TLDC2, OXR1TLDc or TBC1D24 in presence of ATP, the supernatant fractions contained significant amounts of V1 complex, indicating V-ATPase disassembly (Figure 4B, C). While pre-incubation with ATP did not enhance the inhibitory effects of TBC1D24 (Figure 3D), addition of ATP increased the amount of V1 in the supernatant of TBC1D24-treated V-ATPase (Figure 4B, C), consistent with the loss of activity caused by TBC1D24 during enzyme turnover (Figure S3D). Moreover, consistent with mEAK7’s ability to activate the enzyme (Figures 2E, 3E), V1 was not substantially increased in the supernatant after treatment with mEAK7 (Figure 4B, C). Though a small but significant fraction of V1 increased in the supernatant upon treatment with mEAK7 and ATP, this was similar to the ATP only control (Figure 4B, C). Together, the pulldown data demonstrates that V-ATPase inhibition by NCOA7TLDc, OXR1TLDc, TLDC2 and TBC1D24 is due to disassembly of the enzyme into V1 and Vo subcomplexes, and confirms that the process is greatly accelerated in presence of ATP. mEAK7 on the other hand, activates the V-ATPase and does not disrupt enzyme assembly.To further explore the molecular basis for TLDc protein-mediated V-ATPase inhibition, and the role of ATP in the process, we used negative stain electron microscopy (EM) (Figure 5). The V-ATPase is a large complex (~1 MDa) that is easily recognized in negative stain EM images due to its characteristic “dumbbell” shape. The isolated V1 and Vo subcomplexes, on the other hand, have a globular appearance, permitting analysis of the proportion of assembled and disassembled complexes in a given sample (Figure 5A and S4). For EM analysis, V-ATPase was incubated in presence and absence of NCOA7TLDc, TLDC2 and OXR1TLDc with or without ATP, as done for the experiments shown in Figures 3 and 4. Human V-ATPase incubated in buffer alone had ~80% of particles assembled into intact V-ATPase, with ~20% of the complexes disassembled into V1 and Vo subcomplexes (Figure 5B–D). In presence of ATP, there was a slight, but significant, increase in disassembled complexes (~25%). In absence of added ATP, V-ATPase incubated with NCOA7TLDc, or TLDC2 or OXR1TLDc display a similar level of assembly to that of the ATP control, with OXR1-treated V-ATPase being slightly less assembled (Figure 5B–D). Strikingly, when incubated with NCOA7TLDc, TLDC2 or OXR1TLDc in presence of ATP, ~40–50% of V-ATPases disassembled into V1 and Vo subcomplexes (Figure 5B–D), consistent with our observations using the pulldown assays in Figure 4.Together, these data indicate that the mechanism of human V-ATPase inhibition by four of the TLDc proteins studied here is by disassembly of the enzyme. We further conclude that ATP plays at least two roles in TLDc protein mediated V-ATPase inactivation: 1.) populating a rotary state that favors TLDc protein binding, and 2.) overcoming activation barriers to enzyme disassembly, which requires breaking of several subunit-subunit interactions at the V1–Vo interface. Thus, while TLDc proteins alone can disassemble the V-ATPase, likely driven by the stability of the resulting subcomplexes, presence of ATP (as would be found in the cell) greatly accelerates the process.

TLDc family members are characterized by a C-terminal TLDc domain of ~160 amino acids in length, the 3-D structure of which is highly conserved from yeast to human (Figure S1). Most TLDc proteins have N-terminal extensions containing other conserved domains and can range in overall size from ~200–950 residues (Figure 1B).37,38 In accord with the finding that the TLDc domain alone is sufficient for function, TLDC2 and the shortest splice variants of OXR1 and NCOA7 are composed almost entirely of the TLDc domain.37–39,47,61,62 Here, we purified full length TBC1D24 via over-expression in human cells (293F) and bacterially expressed and purified the TLDc domains of human OXR1 (OXR1TLDc) and NCOA7 (NCOA7TLDc), full length TLDC2, and mEAK7 lacking the N-terminal myristoylation motif (Figure 1E, S2), to examine their impact on human V-ATPase activity directly. With exception of TBC1D24, TLDc proteins were expressed from a modified pET28a vector carrying N-terminal 7xHis and Avi tags and an HRV 3C protease cleavage site. TBC1D24 carrying N-terminal FLAG and Avi tags and an HRV 3C cleavage site was expressed from a pCDNA3.1 vector transiently transfected into 293F cells. Reference sequences used for TLDc protein amino acid numbering can be found in the legend to Figure 1B and STAR Methods. Initial expression and purification of full length mEAK7 (amino acids 1–456) revealed substantial degradation and as these studies began before experimental structural models were available, AlphaFold was employed to predict mEAK7’s structure for examination of possible truncation sites to improve protein stability and expression.63 Guided by the AlphaFold model, we removed residues 1–13 and found that the truncated construct allowed high-level expression of soluble and stable mEAK7 14–456. For NCOA7, we initially expressed the short interferon-inducible isoform (also referred to as NCOA7-AS, alternative start), that in addition to the TLDc domain, contains 25 N-terminal amino acids (1-MRGQRLPLDIQIFYCARPDEEPFVK-25) encoded by a unique exon (exon 10a).47,64 In our hands, when expressed in E. coli, NCOA7-AS was found in inclusion bodies and while we were able to refold the protein, this was inefficient and the limited yield impacted our ability to carry out necessary experiments. Thus, NCOA7-AS was truncated at the same sites as were used for crystallization of the protein65 (pdb 7obp, residues 54–219 of NCOA7-AS), which expressed in soluble form as expected. As this truncation site yielded ample, soluble protein, the equivalent site was chosen for generation of OXR1TLDc. Using InterPro for defining TLDc domain boundaries in the amino acid sequences of long isoforms of OXR1 and NCOA7 (Figure 1B), the OXR1TLDc, construct (amino acids 704–873) includes seven, and NCOA7TLDc, (amino acids 777–942) three residues N-terminal to the TLDc domain.66 TLDC2 (amino acids 1–215) was expressed in full length form (Figure 1B). Production of TBC1D24 in E. coli was unsuccessful, with little to no expression and upregulation of chaperones, whereas expression in 293F cells yielded milligram quantities of protein in soluble form. The recombinant TLDc proteins elute as monomeric species at their expected molecular masses on size exclusion chromatography with exception of TBC1D24 that elutes as a larger species from the column, perhaps reflecting a more elongated shape of the molecule (Figure S2).

DISCUSSIONHere we have examined the effects of human TLDc domain proteins mEAK7, TBC1D24, TLDC2, NCOA7TLDc and OXR1TLDC on the activity and assembly state of the human V-ATPase. Interestingly, while mEAK7 was found to increase the activity of the enzyme, TBC1D24, TLDC2, NCOA7TLDc and OXR1TLDC led to V-ATPase inhibition. Notably, pre-incubation with ATP enhanced the inhibitory effects, in accord with a preference for TLDc protein binding to specific rotary states of the V-ATPase, as seen previously for yeast Oxr1p and mEAK726,56,57. Furthermore, using pulldown assays and negative stain EM, we elucidated that the mechanism for human V-ATPase inhibition by TLDc proteins is by inducing enzyme disassembly in an ATP dependent manner. Importantly, the use of pulldown and EM permitted analysis of impacts of TLDc protein binding in absence of added nucleotide as activity measurements require presence of ATP. From the EM analysis, it appears that the TLDc proteins can bind and alter assembly of a small proportion of V-ATPases in absence of added ATP. In line with our activity measurements showing an enhancement in enzyme inhibition, preincubation of V-ATPase with NCOA7TLDC, OXR1TLDC, or TLDC2 in presence of ATP led to a significant increase in V-ATPase disassembly. While TBC1D24 mediated inhibition appeared to not be impacted by pre-incubation with ATP, inclusion of ATP in the pulldown sample enhanced disassembly of the enzyme. Along with allowing the enzyme to sample different, possibly preferable rotary states for TLDc protein binding, ATP may be playing a multifaceted role in TLDc protein-mediated V-ATPase inhibition. It is well established that V-ATPase activity is required for reversible disassembly to occur, and we have speculated that once interactions are destabilized in the complex by unknown factors, the torque of rotation may disrupt remaining contacts linking V1 to the membrane.67–69 Moreover, ATP hydrolysis is known to destabilize V-ATPase interactions with subunits H and C, which are both critical for regulation and function of the enzyme.70,71 Lastly, ATP hydrolysis may balance the energetic cost of breaking favorable contacts at the V1Vo interface.72Proposed mechanism of V-ATPase activation/inhibition from available structural dataWe have recently reported that yeast Oxr1p, composed almost entirely of a TLDc domain, inhibits the V-ATPase in a subunit C dependent fashion, also by inducing enzyme disassembly26. A cryoEM structure of the V1(C)Oxr1p complex, showed that Oxr1p binds yeast V1 in rotary state 1, and that the interaction is mediated by a B subunit of an open catalytic site, subunit C, and peripheral stalk EG2 (Figure 6A)26. The structure suggests that the mechanism by which binding of Oxr1p inhibits the enzyme and causes disassembly is through structural rearrangements of the C subunit and peripheral stalk EG2, both of which would be incompatible with maintaining a stable V1–Vo interface (Figure 6A, B, S5A). We speculate that both the mechanism of disassembly and TLDc protein binding sites are conserved from yeast to human, and studies to determine these parameters for the human V-ATPase/TLDc protein complexes is ongoing in our laboratory.In contrast to the inhibition observed for the other TLDc proteins, we find that mEAK7 activates the V-ATPase. mEAK7 contains a large N-terminal domain with an N-terminal myristoylation motif, a TLDc domain, and a small α helix at the C-terminus (Figure S1).56,57 Previously, cryoEM and mass spectrometry analysis revealed mEAK7 bound to a subset of particles in purified V-ATPase samples and showed that mEAK7 binds to intact V-ATPase in rotary state 2.56–58 Like yeast Oxr1p, mEAK7’s TLDc domain binds to the C-terminal domain of a B subunit, but in case of mEAK7, the B subunit is next to peripheral stalk EG3 (Figure 6C third and fourth panels from left, S5B). While mEAK7’s N-terminal domain extends outward from the V-ATPase, where (in cells) its myristoylated N-terminus could interact with the membrane, its C-terminal α helix (mEAK7-CT) interacts with rotor subunit D within the catalytic hexamer.56–58 The observed interactions link the rotor (mEAK7-CT/subunit D) and stator (TLDc domain/subunit B/EG3) of the V-ATPase, and thus would appear to be inhibitory, contrary to what we observed. Strikingly, mEAK7 binding in state 2 results in two open catalytic sites, the one that is open in state 2 in absence of mEAK7, and the one that would be open in state 3, thus yielding a transition state-like complex on its way from state 2 to state 3 (Figure 6D, third panel from left). In EM analyses of the V-ATPase, state 3 is always the least populated, indicating that state 3 has the highest energy. Taken together, this suggests that state 3 is the most difficult to attain (or maintain) and that mEAK7 binding in state 2 eases the transition to state 3, thus increasing the rate of turnover. While we have found that mEAK7 binds to and enhances V-ATPase activity under turnover conditions, treatment with ATP was previously found to destabilize the interaction.56 The observed destabilization of the mEAK7-V-ATPase interaction likely reflects the specificity of rotary state binding, which ATP hydrolysis will necessarily change. Furthermore, as mEAK7 is binding to peripheral stalk EG3 (the interface that lacks subunit C), there is no distortion of the subunits at the V1–Vo interface upon mEAK7 binding (unlike as seen with yeast Oxr1p), and thus no disruption of V-ATPase assembly is observed.Physiological roles for the TLDc protein-mediated modulation of human V-ATPase activityWhile mEAK7 is now an established V-ATPase binding partner, previous studies to examine the impacts of mEAK7 on V-ATPase function in cells and in vitro saw little effect.56,57 The activation by mEAK7 observed in our work is in line with some overlapping cellular functions and localization of mEAK7 and V-ATPase. mEAK7’s N-terminal myristoylation motif links the protein to the plasma membrane (PM) and to the lysosomal surface.42 On the lysosome, mEAK7 is required for recruitment of mTOR, activation of S6K2, and inhibition of 4E-BP1.42 V-ATPase is also required for lysosomal recruitment and activation of mTOR, with V-ATPase displaying amino acid sensitive interactions with the Ragulator complex.73 mEAK7 binding to mTOR is increased in presence of amino acids and required the mEAK7’s C-terminal domain.42 From cryoEM structures of the complex, mEAK7’s C-terminal α-helix, however, is buried in the catalytic hexamer of the V-ATPase, suggesting that interactions between mEAK7’s C-terminus and V-ATPase or mTOR are mutually exclusive.56,57 Interestingly, V-ATPase assembly is likewise tuned by amino acid levels, with disassembly favored in their presence.33,74 The amino acid-induced disassembly of the V-ATPase would remove the binding site for mEAK7’s C-terminal domain from the lysosomal membrane, thus freeing it up to interact with mTOR (Figure S5C). Moreover, under conditions of starvation when mTOR is inactive, V-ATPase assembly increases for acidification of the lysosomal lumen to support catabolic processes.74 Under these conditions, mEAK7’s C-terminal domain could then bind in the catalytic hexamer and activate V-ATPase. While the TLDc domain is dispensable for mEAK7 binding to mTOR, it is required for full mEAK7-mediated mTOR activation, hinting at a possible collaboration with V-ATPase in mTOR signaling.42 At present, however, the details of this putative interplay between mEAK7-mediated V-ATPase activation and mTOR signaling are not clear.We have shown here that TBC1D24, TLDC2 and the TLDc domains of NCOA7 and OXR1 all inhibit V-ATPase by inducing enzyme disassembly. However, recent work in cells or in animals seems to indicate a positive effect on V-ATPase function. For example, loss of NCOA7 phenocopies renal V-ATPase subunit mutation, displaying partial distal renal tubular acidosis, and mutations in TBC1D24 or V-ATPase subunit B (isoform B2) are associated with DOORS syndrome.12,50,53,54 Further, induction of a short isoform of NCOA7 by interferon limits viral infection while increasing proteolytic capacity of lysosomes.47,64 Previously, we have proposed a role for TLDc proteins in V-ATPase quality control, in line with the finding that mice lacking NCOA7 displayed lower levels of V-ATPase subunits at the protein level.53 In addition, induction of enzyme disassembly by TLDc proteins could be important for synaptic transmission. Indeed, along with regulating V-ATPase activity, the assembly state of the V-ATPase is tightly linked to the exo-endocytic cycle of synaptic vesicles (SVs).4,75 The proton gradient generated by the V-ATPase is required for loading neurotransmitters into SVs and V-ATPase disassembly occurs when the SV is filled and its lumen becomes acidic, thereby distinguishing filled from unfilled SVs.75 In turn, SVs lacking assembled V-ATPase have a higher likelihood of fusion with the active zone of the presynaptic membrane, thus ensuring efficiency of synaptic transmission. Interestingly, V-ATPase activity is observed at the PM shortly after SV exocytosis, suggesting rapid enzyme re-assembly on the membrane which can then be endocytosed for re-use in the SV cycle.75 As V-ATPase is spatially regulated by trafficking of the pump from intracellular compartments to the plasma membrane in specialized acid secreting cells, it is tempting to speculate that a similar disassembly-triggered transport mechanism could be a general means of moving V-ATPases around the cell, particularly as V-ATPase function is costly and likely not required during transport. In such situations, if TLDc proteins play a role in initiating disassembly of the enzyme to ready it for transport, or if this phenomenon is required for V-ATPase traffic, then loss of TLDc protein function would undoubtedly manifest in V-ATPase loss of function. Though the TLDc domain on its own has been shown to be sufficient for function, it is possible that the longer isoforms have different impacts on V-ATPase activity.38,39 From available cryoEM structures of TLDc protein/V-ATPase complexes, the TLDc domain binds to one B subunit and one peripheral stalk of the V-ATPase, both present in three copies per enzyme.26,56,57 However, additional and unique interactions between the different TLDc proteins and V-ATPase are also observed in the structures, in the form of subunit C for yeast Oxr1p and subunit D for the C-terminal domain of mEAK7. We speculate that the preference for binding to a specific B subunit/peripheral stalk may be driven by the extra domains present in the TLDc protein. For example, the TLDc proteins with larger N-terminal domains, including the longer isoforms of NCOA7 and OXR1, may be sterically excluded from some potential binding sites on V-ATPase. On the other hand, we find that despite TBC1D24 containing a large N-terminal domain, it is capable of V-ATPase inhibition, suggesting that the protein may bind to the same inhibitory site as seen for yeast Oxr1p. The isoform complexity of both the V-ATPase and TLDc proteins along with the large number of cellular processes impacted by these proteins makes teasing apart functional consequences of such interactions a huge challenge. Here, we have examined the direct impacts of TLDc protein binding on human V-ATPase function and find that of the five tested, four were inhibitory by regulating V-ATPase’s assembly state whereas one activates V-ATPase. Together these findings shed new light on TLDc protein function and V-ATPase regulation and define TLDc proteins as positive and negative regulators of V-ATPase activity.

Here we have examined the effects of human TLDc domain proteins mEAK7, TBC1D24, TLDC2, NCOA7TLDc and OXR1TLDC on the activity and assembly state of the human V-ATPase. Interestingly, while mEAK7 was found to increase the activity of the enzyme, TBC1D24, TLDC2, NCOA7TLDc and OXR1TLDC led to V-ATPase inhibition. Notably, pre-incubation with ATP enhanced the inhibitory effects, in accord with a preference for TLDc protein binding to specific rotary states of the V-ATPase, as seen previously for yeast Oxr1p and mEAK726,56,57. Furthermore, using pulldown assays and negative stain EM, we elucidated that the mechanism for human V-ATPase inhibition by TLDc proteins is by inducing enzyme disassembly in an ATP dependent manner. Importantly, the use of pulldown and EM permitted analysis of impacts of TLDc protein binding in absence of added nucleotide as activity measurements require presence of ATP. From the EM analysis, it appears that the TLDc proteins can bind and alter assembly of a small proportion of V-ATPases in absence of added ATP. In line with our activity measurements showing an enhancement in enzyme inhibition, preincubation of V-ATPase with NCOA7TLDC, OXR1TLDC, or TLDC2 in presence of ATP led to a significant increase in V-ATPase disassembly. While TBC1D24 mediated inhibition appeared to not be impacted by pre-incubation with ATP, inclusion of ATP in the pulldown sample enhanced disassembly of the enzyme. Along with allowing the enzyme to sample different, possibly preferable rotary states for TLDc protein binding, ATP may be playing a multifaceted role in TLDc protein-mediated V-ATPase inhibition. It is well established that V-ATPase activity is required for reversible disassembly to occur, and we have speculated that once interactions are destabilized in the complex by unknown factors, the torque of rotation may disrupt remaining contacts linking V1 to the membrane.67–69 Moreover, ATP hydrolysis is known to destabilize V-ATPase interactions with subunits H and C, which are both critical for regulation and function of the enzyme.70,71 Lastly, ATP hydrolysis may balance the energetic cost of breaking favorable contacts at the V1Vo interface.72

Proposed mechanism of V-ATPase activation/inhibition from available structural dataWe have recently reported that yeast Oxr1p, composed almost entirely of a TLDc domain, inhibits the V-ATPase in a subunit C dependent fashion, also by inducing enzyme disassembly26. A cryoEM structure of the V1(C)Oxr1p complex, showed that Oxr1p binds yeast V1 in rotary state 1, and that the interaction is mediated by a B subunit of an open catalytic site, subunit C, and peripheral stalk EG2 (Figure 6A)26. The structure suggests that the mechanism by which binding of Oxr1p inhibits the enzyme and causes disassembly is through structural rearrangements of the C subunit and peripheral stalk EG2, both of which would be incompatible with maintaining a stable V1–Vo interface (Figure 6A, B, S5A). We speculate that both the mechanism of disassembly and TLDc protein binding sites are conserved from yeast to human, and studies to determine these parameters for the human V-ATPase/TLDc protein complexes is ongoing in our laboratory.In contrast to the inhibition observed for the other TLDc proteins, we find that mEAK7 activates the V-ATPase. mEAK7 contains a large N-terminal domain with an N-terminal myristoylation motif, a TLDc domain, and a small α helix at the C-terminus (Figure S1).56,57 Previously, cryoEM and mass spectrometry analysis revealed mEAK7 bound to a subset of particles in purified V-ATPase samples and showed that mEAK7 binds to intact V-ATPase in rotary state 2.56–58 Like yeast Oxr1p, mEAK7’s TLDc domain binds to the C-terminal domain of a B subunit, but in case of mEAK7, the B subunit is next to peripheral stalk EG3 (Figure 6C third and fourth panels from left, S5B). While mEAK7’s N-terminal domain extends outward from the V-ATPase, where (in cells) its myristoylated N-terminus could interact with the membrane, its C-terminal α helix (mEAK7-CT) interacts with rotor subunit D within the catalytic hexamer.56–58 The observed interactions link the rotor (mEAK7-CT/subunit D) and stator (TLDc domain/subunit B/EG3) of the V-ATPase, and thus would appear to be inhibitory, contrary to what we observed. Strikingly, mEAK7 binding in state 2 results in two open catalytic sites, the one that is open in state 2 in absence of mEAK7, and the one that would be open in state 3, thus yielding a transition state-like complex on its way from state 2 to state 3 (Figure 6D, third panel from left). In EM analyses of the V-ATPase, state 3 is always the least populated, indicating that state 3 has the highest energy. Taken together, this suggests that state 3 is the most difficult to attain (or maintain) and that mEAK7 binding in state 2 eases the transition to state 3, thus increasing the rate of turnover. While we have found that mEAK7 binds to and enhances V-ATPase activity under turnover conditions, treatment with ATP was previously found to destabilize the interaction.56 The observed destabilization of the mEAK7-V-ATPase interaction likely reflects the specificity of rotary state binding, which ATP hydrolysis will necessarily change. Furthermore, as mEAK7 is binding to peripheral stalk EG3 (the interface that lacks subunit C), there is no distortion of the subunits at the V1–Vo interface upon mEAK7 binding (unlike as seen with yeast Oxr1p), and thus no disruption of V-ATPase assembly is observed.
