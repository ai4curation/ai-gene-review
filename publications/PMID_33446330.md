BACKGROUND: Eosinophilic esophagitis (EoE) is an emerging, chronic, rare
allergic disease associated with marked eosinophil accumulation in the
esophagus. Previous genome-wide association studies have provided strong
evidence for 3 genome-wide susceptibility loci.
OBJECTIVE: We sought to replicate known and suggestive EoE genetic risk loci and
conduct a meta-analysis of previously reported data sets.
METHODS: An EoE-Custom single-nucleotide polymophism (SNP) Chip containing 956
candidate EoE risk single-nucleotide polymorphisms was used to genotype 627
cases and 365 controls. Statistical power was enhanced by adding 1959 external
controls and performing meta-analyses with 2 independent EoE genome-wide
association studies.
RESULTS: Meta-analysis identified replicated association and genome-wide
significance at 6 loci: 2p23 (2 independent genetic effects) and 5q22, 10p14,
11q13, and 16p13. Seven additional loci were identified at suggestive
significance (P < 10-6): 1q31, 5q23, 6q15, 6q21, 8p21, 17q12, and 22q13. From
these risk loci, 13 protein-coding EoE candidate risk genes were expressed in a
genotype-dependent manner. EoE risk genes were expressed in disease-relevant
cell types, including esophageal epithelia, fibroblasts, and immune cells, with
some expressed as a function of disease activity. The genetic risk burden of
EoE-associated genetic variants was markedly larger in cases relative to
controls (P < 10-38); individuals with the highest decile of genetic burden had
greater than 12-fold risk of EoE compared with those within the lowest decile.
CONCLUSIONS: This study extends the genetic underpinnings of EoE, highlighting
13 genes whose genotype-dependent expression expands our etiologic understanding
of EoE and provides a framework for a polygenic risk score to be validated in
future studies.

The role of allergic inflammation in EoE is strongly supported by the efficacy of food elimination diets, the co-occurrence of EoE with other allergic diseases (e.g. asthma and atopic dermatitis), animal models demonstrating that experimental EoE can be induced by allergen exposure, and the necessity of allergic mediators of inflammation, such as IL-5 and IL-13, on the basis of animal models and clinical studies 3–12. Furthermore, an interplay of gene-gene (particularly between IL4 and TSLP) and gene-environment interactions have been implicated in the development of EoE 22–24. A phenome-wide association study (PheWAS) approach identified PTEN, TGFBR1/TGFBR2/PBN, and IL5/IL13 as EoE risk loci 17. Twin and epidemiologic studies have demonstrated substantial heritability for EoE, likely resulting from gene-environment interactions, especially those involving early life exposures 4.

With a primary goal of replicating and refining the association at loci that demonstrated association p<10−4 in our Discovery GWAS, we developed an EoE-Custom SNP Chip (EoE-CSC) containing 956 candidate EoE risk single-nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) to genotype 627 cases and 365 controls. Statistical power was further enhanced by including 1,959 additional controls and performing a meta-analysis with 553 cases and 9,290 controls from previous GWAS 18, resulting in 6 genetic effects at genome-wide significance and an additional 7 EoE risk loci at strong suggestive significance (p < 10−6). A secondary goal of the study was to incorporate the most robust signals from an independent GWAS, and this assessment further replicated and enhanced association at 2p23, 5q22, and 11q13. Genes expressed as a function of the EoE risk allele were assessed in the context of EoE in esophageal biopsies. The overall strategy is presented in Figure 1. Altogether, we replicated and identified new EoE risk loci and provide biological data to substantiate relevant genes at these loci.

SNP-based Meta-analysis of EoE-CSC Study and Discovery GWAS Data

A meta-analysis using the Discovery GWAS data 18 identified genome-wide significant association (p < 5×10−8) with statistical support from both the Discovery and EoE-CSC cohorts at six loci (2p23 [two independent effects], 5q22, 10p14, 11q13, and 16p13) (Fig 2B, Table I, Table E7). CAPN14 is dynamically upregulated as a function of EoE disease activity and after exposure of epithelial cells to IL-13 18, 49. Genotype-dependent expression of CAPN14 in the esophageal biopsies of patients with EoE supports the role of CAPN14 in the genetic etiology of EoE 18, 50. Molecular studies demonstrated that the risk variant at 2p23 dampens CAPN14 expression in differentiated esophageal epithelial cells following IL-13/STAT6 induction of CAPN14 promoter activity 50.

Previous studies established TSLP as expressed as a function of the EoE risk genotype in the esophagus of patients with EoE 51. DiscussionHerein, we report a number of advances in the genetic understanding of EoE by a combination of approaches, including a replication study (EoE-CSC), meta-analysis, biological interrogation, and clinical assessment. First, we employed a relatively dense custom SNP chip to validate and further refine previously suggested EoE risk alleles. Though the original custom chip was limited to 956 genotyped SNPs, this was expanded to 8,141 SNPs following imputation. This approach provided strong supportive evidence for three known EoE loci (2p23, 5q22, and 11q13) and five novel loci (2p23, 2q13, 5q31, 6q15, and 10p14) and identified 63 suggestive loci that deserve follow-up investigation. Second, we conducted a meta-analysis combining the Custom SNP analysis with the prior Discovery GWAS, resulting in the identification of six loci with genome-wide significance; these were at 2p23 (two independent effects), 5q22, 10p14, 11q13, and 16p13. Fine-mapping identified the physical region and genetic linkage at each locus and was notable for the presence of two independent associations at 2p23, suggesting the presence of two genetic mechanisms that regulate the association of EoE susceptibility with CAPN14. Third, because none of the associated EoE risk variants changed amino acid usage, we analyzed human datasets for genotype-dependent expression as a function of the EoE-associated genetic variant. This eQTL approach identified 13 protein-coding genes, six lncRNAs, and a pseudogene associated with genotype-dependent expression (eQTLs) as a function of EoE-associated (p < 10−6) genetic variants in our study. These include genes near previously associated EoE risk loci (WDR36, TSLP, CAPN14, and CLEC16A) and new candidate genes, including ATP10A, BACH2, EMSY (C11orf30), LRRC32, GATA3, PNPLA3, SAMM50, TNFRSF10B, and TNFRSF10C. Biological assessment demonstrated expression of many of these genes in disease-relevant tissue and/or cells, such as the esophagus (TSLP, WDR36, CAPN14, and SAMM50), skin (GATA3 and PNPLA3), lymphocytes (BACH and GATA3), esophageal epithelial cells (CAPN14, SAMM50, and WDR36) and fibroblasts (TSLP). The expression of GATA3 by pathogenic memory effector type 2 CD4+ T cells, which have been implicated in EoE disease etiology, is particularly notable 58. While constitutive expression of ATP10A (15q12) was not observed in esophageal biopsies, genetic variants and DNA methylation at this maternally-expressed gene have been tentatively associated with food allergy 76–79, and genetic associations with viral response and acute myeloid leukemia suggest a role in immune signaling 80, 81. Furthermore, the expression of a subset of these genes in the esophagus was sufficient to differentiate patients with EoE from controls and tracked with disease activity. Fourth, in order to translate the genetic risk at individual loci into a patient-based assessment of genetic risk, we assessed the genetic burden of identified EoE risk variants using a polygenic risk scoring system. Herein, we report a number of advances in the genetic understanding of EoE by a combination of approaches, including a replication study (EoE-CSC), meta-analysis, biological interrogation, and clinical assessment. First, we employed a relatively dense custom SNP chip to validate and further refine previously suggested EoE risk alleles. Though the original custom chip was limited to 956 genotyped SNPs, this was expanded to 8,141 SNPs following imputation. This approach provided strong supportive evidence for three known EoE loci (2p23, 5q22, and 11q13) and five novel loci (2p23, 2q13, 5q31, 6q15, and 10p14) and identified 63 suggestive loci that deserve follow-up investigation. Second, we conducted a meta-analysis combining the Custom SNP analysis with the prior Discovery GWAS, resulting in the identification of six loci with genome-wide significance; these were at 2p23 (two independent effects), 5q22, 10p14, 11q13, and 16p13. Fine-mapping identified the physical region and genetic linkage at each locus and was notable for the presence of two independent associations at 2p23, suggesting the presence of two genetic mechanisms that regulate the association of EoE susceptibility with CAPN14. Third, because none of the associated EoE risk variants changed amino acid usage, we analyzed human datasets for genotype-dependent expression as a function of the EoE-associated genetic variant. This eQTL approach identified 13 protein-coding genes, six lncRNAs, and a pseudogene associated with genotype-dependent expression (eQTLs) as a function of EoE-associated (p < 10−6) genetic variants in our study. These include genes near previously associated EoE risk loci (WDR36, TSLP, CAPN14, and CLEC16A) and new candidate genes, including ATP10A, BACH2, EMSY (C11orf30), LRRC32, GATA3, PNPLA3, SAMM50, TNFRSF10B, and TNFRSF10C. Biological assessment demonstrated expression of many of these genes in disease-relevant tissue and/or cells, such as the esophagus (TSLP, WDR36, CAPN14, and SAMM50), skin (GATA3 and PNPLA3), lymphocytes (BACH and GATA3), esophageal epithelial cells (CAPN14, SAMM50, and WDR36) and fibroblasts (TSLP). The expression of GATA3 by pathogenic memory effector type 2 CD4+ T cells, which have been implicated in EoE disease etiology, is particularly notable 58. While constitutive expression of ATP10A (15q12) was not observed in esophageal biopsies, genetic variants and DNA methylation at this maternally-expressed gene have been tentatively associated with food allergy 76–79, and genetic associations with viral response and acute myeloid leukemia suggest a role in immune signaling 80, 81. Furthermore, the expression of a subset of these genes in the esophagus was sufficient to differentiate patients with EoE from controls and tracked with disease activity. Fourth, in order to translate the genetic risk at individual loci into a patient-based assessment of genetic risk, we assessed the genetic burden of identified EoE risk variants using a polygenic risk scoring system. Indeed, individuals with the highest decile of genetic burden had a greater than 12-fold increased risk of EoE compared to those with the lowest decile. Polygenic risk scores will likely become more clinically useful as more genetic risk loci are identified and additional demographic, environmental, and clinical factors are included. Additionally, as the same cohort was used to generate and test the polygenic risk score we developed, its clinical utility remains to be assessed.
