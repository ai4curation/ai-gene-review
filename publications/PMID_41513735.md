# The A2357T substitution in the kinase domain of yeast Tor1 confers growth promoting TORC1 activity independent of Gtr1/2 and Pib2.

The highly conserved Target of Rapamycin Complex 1 (TORC1) plays a central role
in controlling eukaryotic cell growth in response to diverse environmental cues,
including nitrogen availability. In yeast, TORC1 activity is stimulated through
direct interaction with at least two upstream regulators: the Gtr1/2 GTPase
complex, homologous to mammalian RagAB/CD and which binds the fungus-specific
Tco89 protein, and the FYVE-domain-containing protein Pib2, an amino acid
sensor. Here, using a novel genetic approach, we first confirmed that in cells
expressing neither Gtr1/2 nor Pib2, no other factor is capable of supporting
sufficient TORC1 activity to sustain cell growth, even under 25 different
nitrogen supply conditions. We then identified a novel, dominant TOR1 mutation
introducing an A2357T substitution into the Tor1 kinase domain and conferring
sufficient TORC1 activity to support cell growth despite the loss of both Gtr1/2
and Pib2. Interestingly, previous studies have identified substitutions at the
corresponding A2420 residue in human mTOR from multiple cancer cells.

IntroductionThe TORC1 (Target of Rapamycin Complex 1) kinase complex1,2 plays a central role in integrating internal and environmental signals to control eukaryotic cell growth3–5. These signals include nutrient availability, growth hormone presence, the energetic status of the cell, and stress conditions. Upon integration of these cues, TORC1, when active, phosphorylates a wide variety of effector proteins, and this results in stimulation of anabolic functions (e.g. protein synthesis) and inhibition of catabolic processes (e.g. macroautophagy)6. In yeast, TORC1 localizes to the limiting membrane of the vacuole, where it phosphorylates the S6K1-related Sch9 kinase, among other target proteins7,8. A subpopulation of TORC1 also localizes to a vacuole-derived compartment, termed the signaling endosome, where it phosphorylates specific effector proteins such as Atg13, Vps27, and Fab18–10. One of these positive factors is the heterodimeric Gtr1/2 GTPase complex bound to the lipidated EGO complex, which anchors it to the membrane13. The activity of Gtr1/2 is regulated through opposite association of its two subunits with GTP or GDP, a function modulated by the SEACIT and Lst4-Lst7 GTPase-activating protein (GAP) complexes14,15. In its active Gtr1GTP/Gtr2GDP configuration, the heterodimer binds to the C-terminus of Tco89, which mediates association between Gtr1/2 and Kog112. The second direct positive activator of TORC1 is Pib216,17. This protein displays the properties of a cysteine sensor, also involved in glutamine sensing18–20. It associates, via its essential FYVE domain, with phosphatidylinositol-3-phosphate (PI3P) on the membranes of the vacuole and signaling endosomes12,21,22. Introduction

The TORC1 (Target of Rapamycin Complex 1) kinase complex1,2 plays a central role in integrating internal and environmental signals to control eukaryotic cell growth3–5. These signals include nutrient availability, growth hormone presence, the energetic status of the cell, and stress conditions. Upon integration of these cues, TORC1, when active, phosphorylates a wide variety of effector proteins, and this results in stimulation of anabolic functions (e.g. protein synthesis) and inhibition of catabolic processes (e.g. macroautophagy)6. In yeast, TORC1 localizes to the limiting membrane of the vacuole, where it phosphorylates the S6K1-related Sch9 kinase, among other target proteins7,8. A subpopulation of TORC1 also localizes to a vacuole-derived compartment, termed the signaling endosome, where it phosphorylates specific effector proteins such as Atg13, Vps27, and Fab18–10. Yeast TORC1 is a dimer of tetramers, each consisting of a Tor kinase (Tor1 or Tor2, homologous to mammalian mTor), the proteins Kog1 and Lst8 (respectively homologous to mammalian Raptor and Lst8)2,3, and a fungus-specific subunit, namely Tco8911,12. The basal activity of yeast TORC1 is stimulated through direct interaction between its Kog1 subunit and two positive regulators presumed to function as allosteric modulators of TORC1. One of these positive factors is the heterodimeric Gtr1/2 GTPase complex bound to the lipidated EGO complex, which anchors it to the membrane13. The activity of Gtr1/2 is regulated through opposite association of its two subunits with GTP or GDP, a function modulated by the SEACIT and Lst4-Lst7 GTPase-activating protein (GAP) complexes14,15. In its active Gtr1GTP/Gtr2GDP configuration, the heterodimer binds to the C-terminus of Tco89, which mediates association between Gtr1/2 and Kog112. The second direct positive activator of TORC1 is Pib216,17. This protein displays the properties of a cysteine sensor, also involved in glutamine sensing18–20. It associates, via its essential FYVE domain, with phosphatidylinositol-3-phosphate (PI3P) on the membranes of the vacuole and signaling endosomes12,21,22. Pib2 also harbors a C-terminal loop involved in TORC1 activation and a large N-terminal domain that inhibits TORC1 independently of Gtr1/221.

Yeast mutants having lost either Gtr1 or Pib2 are viable, whereas mutants lacking both Gtr1 and Pib2 (or both Tco89 and Pib2) are not12,16,18,21,23. The kog1Δ mutant is likewise non-viable. This observation has led to the assumption that Gtr1/2-Tco89 and Pib2 are the two main, if not the sole, direct positive regulators of TORC1, and that each, independently of the other, is capable of activating TORC1 to at least some extent. In support of this view, in nitrogen-starved mutant cells lacking either Gtr1 or Pib2, TORC1 remains activatable upon addition of any one of several specific amino acids19,24. On the other hand, conditions have also been described under which both Gtr1/2 and Pib2 are required for TORC1 activation19,21,24,25. This supports, rather, a model where Gtr1/2 and Pib2 act cooperatively to activate TORC112,17. Accordingly, Pib2 has been found to interact with several subunits of the active EGO-Gtr1/2 complex12,16. The relative contributions of Gtr1/2 and Pib2 to TORC1 activation may depend not only on the input signal triggering the activation19,26 but also on the membrane where TORC1 is located12. They may also depend on whether one considers initial or sustained TORC1 activation17,27. For instance, in cells grown on a poor nitrogen source such as proline, a fast, transient increase in TORC1 activity is detectable upon addition of any nitrogenous compound, including poor nitrogen sources or even beta-alanine, which cannot be used as a nitrogen source27,28. We have reported results indicating that the signal for this early TORC1 activation is the H+ influx coupled to the transport reaction at the plasma membrane28. Sensing of this local H+ increase involves the plasma membrane H+-ATPase, Pma128, whose phosphoactivation is feedback controlled via TORC129. In plant cells, furthermore, TORC1 is similarly activated upon stimulation of homologous H+-ATPases30. The early, transient activation of yeast TORC1 triggered by the transport reaction is dependent on Gtr1/2, not Pib227,28. It is followed by a more sustained activation, observed only when the supplied nitrogen source supports optimal growth27. The proposed intracellular signal for this late TORC1 activation, largely independent of Gtr1/2, is an intracellular increase in glutamine (Gln)27, an amino acid sensed via Pib218,20.

That Gtr1/2-Tco89 and Pib2 are the main upstream activators of TORC1 is mostly supported by the inability of gtr1Δ pib2Δ double mutants to grow in the context of meiotic germination12,16,18,21,23 and by the apparent inactivation of TORC1 upon repression of PIB2 expression in a gtr1Δ mutant18. In the latter experimental scheme, however, substantial growth of the gtr1Δ mutant subsisted after Pib2 depletion18. This observation, together with the high TORC1 activation observed under specific conditions in gtr1Δ and pib2Δ single-mutant cells27,28, raises the question of whether situations exist under which TORC1 remains partially active, independently of Gtr1/2 and Pib2, thanks to the contribution of still another, as yet unknown, direct TORC1 activator.

In this study we first describe a genetic approach aimed at re-assessing the view that yeast cannot grow without expressing either Gtr1/2 or Pib2, regardless of the nitrogen supply conditions. We then report the isolation, during these experiments, of a dominant mutation in TOR1 causing an A2357T substitution in the kinase domain of Tor1 and enabling TORC1 to be active independently of Gtr1/2 and Pib2. Interestingly, this alanine in Tor1 is conserved in human mTOR, and missense mutations altering this residue have been found in multiple cancers.

Results and discussionAddition of NH4+ to yeast cells grown on a poor nitrogen source such as proline stimulates TORC1 activity27,34, which in turn triggers several adaptive responses. Among these are phospho-inhibition of the Npr1 kinase, with subsequent downregulation of several plasma membrane transporters34,35, and transcriptional repression of genes subject to the Nitrogen Catabolite Repression (NCR) circuit36,37. Previous studies have shown that these responses to NH4+ supply are more pronounced in the Σ1278b wild type than in the more commonly used S288c strain or its BY derivatives38,39. This difference is due to multiple recessive mutations in the S288c background, which remain uncharacterized38. We considered the possibility that the stronger response of TORC1 to NH4+ in the Σ1278b strain might be due to the contribution, in this strain but not in the S288c background, of a direct upstream TORC1 activator (in addition to Gtr1/2 and Pib2) that would be stimulated upon NH4+ supply. This hypothesis seemed conceivable, since the synthetic lethality between the gtr1Δ and pib2Δ mutations was demonstrated in experiments carried out in the BY strain background16,18,21,23.Before exploring this model, we examined the contributions of Gtr1/2 and Pib2 to TORC1 activation upon NH4+ addition. The experiment was carried out in an amino-acid-prototrophic derivative of Σ1278b, initially grown on a glucose (Glu) medium with proline (Pro) as sole nitrogen source. As readout of TORC1 activation, we first used rapamycin-sensitive reduction of the electrophoretic mobility of the HA-tagged Npr1 kinase, indicative of increased phosphorylation via TORC140,41. The response was examined at early (4 and 10 min) and late (30, 60 min) time points after NH4+ addition, so as to allow comparing initial and sustained TORC1 activation27. In w-t cells, as expected, NH4+ addition triggered at both early and late time points an increase in HA-Npr1 phosphorylation, not detected in cells pretreated with rapamycin (Fig. 1A). In the gtr1Δ gtr2Δ strain, in contrast, stimulation of HA-Npr1 phosphorylation was impaired at early time points, although largely preserved at later time points. Sixty minutes after NH4+ addition, no significant difference vs. the w-t was observed (P = 0.47) (Fig. 1A). This phenotype of the gtr1Δ gtr2Δ strain was also seen in an experiment where more time points were examined (Fig. S1). These observations, in agreement with those of previous studies27,28,42, confirm that the Gtr1/2 complex is essential for the initial activation of TORC1 in response to NH4+ addition but largely dispensable for its later, more sustained activation.It has been reported that an increase in intracellular glutamine (Gln) is the metabolic signal promoting late TORC1 activation in nitrogen-starved cells supplied with an optimal nitrogen source such as NH4+27. Other studies, furthermore, have shown Pib2 to be part of a Gln-sensing complex18,20. When we supplied NH4+ to pib2Δ mutant cells, HA-Npr1 phosphorylation was found, at early time points, to increase only slightly less markedly than in the w-t. After 60 min, no significant difference was observed (P = 0.46) (Fig. 1B). The simplest interpretation of the latter result, corroborated by a previous study42, is that Gtr1/2 and Pib2 redundantly support late NH4+-induced TORC1 activation, for instance in response to a Gln increase.We also examined TORC1 activation in response to NH4+ addition using, as a readout, phosphorylation of residue Thr737 in the Sch9 kinase7. In keeping with previous studies27,28. NH4+ supply to w-t cells triggered a marked increase in Sch9 phosphorylation for at least 30 min (Fig. 1C). In both the gtr1Δ gtr2Δ and pib2Δ strains, early stimulation of Sch9 phosphorylation was much reduced, indicating Gtr1/2 and Pib2 function cooperatively to promote early TORC1 activation by NH4+. At later time points, however, the negative impact of the gtr1Δ gtr2Δ and pib2Δ mutations on TORC1 activation was less pronounced. The latter observation, reminiscent of the results obtained when examining HA-Npr1 phosphorylation, suggests that Gtr1/2 and Pib2 act in a less cooperative manner when supporting late NH4+-induced TORC1 activation.The apparent variable cooperativity of Gtr1/2 or Pib2 in early and late TORC1 activation upon NH4+ supply prompted us to consider the possibility that an additional, highly NH4+-responsive upstream activator of TORC1 might contribute, in parallel with Gtr1/2 and Pib2, to late TORC1 activation, this being true in the Σ1278b but not the S288c background (because of loss-of-function mutations). To assess this hypothesis we first examined, on an amino-acid-rich medium, tetrads of a sporulated gtr1Δ /GTR1+
pib2Δ /PIB2+ double-heterozygous diploid derived from Σ1278b. Under these germination conditions, the results clearly showed synthetic lethality in all gtr1Δ pib2Δ double-mutant ascospores (Fig. S2A). We considered the possibility that this lethality might not be observed if the germination medium contained NH4+ as sole N source, but this was not the case (Fig. S2B). To investigate beyond the context of meiotic sporulation and germination the phenotype of a strain lacking both Gtr1/2 and Pib2, we next constructed a gtr1Δ gtr2Δ strain where PIB2 transcription is driven by a galactose-inducible promoter (Fig. 3A). This gtr1Δ gtr2Δ GAL-PIB2 strain grew normally on amino-acid-rich media containing galactose (Gal) or raffinose and galactose (Raf/Gal) as carbon source(s), but it failed to grow on glucose medium (Fig. 3B). This confirms that under these growth conditions, simultaneous lack of Gtr1/2 and Pib2 expression is incompatible with cell growth16,18,21. The same result was obtained on minimal media where NH4+ (Fig. 3C) or any one of 24 other nitrogenous compounds (Fig. S3) was supplied as sole nitrogen source. These results support the view that Gtr1/2 and Pib2 are the sole direct upstream activators of TORC1 capable of sustaining cell growth, at least under the tested growth conditions. Yeast, however, possesses an ortholog, named Rhb1, of the mammalian small GTPase Rheb, a key allosteric activator of mTORC13–5. Another small GTPase reported to promote mTORC1 activation, namely Arf143–45, is also conserved in yeast. We therefore isolated gtr1Δ gtr2Δ rhb1Δ and gtr1Δ gtr2Δ arf1Δ mutant strains to determine whether Rhb1 or Arf1 might contribute to late NH4+-induced TORC1 activation, but this proved not to be the case (Fig. S4). Furthermore, as compared to the gtr1Δ gtr2Δ strain, neither of these two mutants displayed any growth defect under 25 different nitrogen supply conditions (Fig. S5).

Interestingly, although the gtr1Δ gtr2Δ GAL-PIB2 strain did not grow on amino-acid-rich Glu medium, isolated colonies developed after prolonged incubation (Figs. 2A, 3B,C). We selected one of these apparent suppressor mutants and first established by qRT-PCR that its restored growth on Glu medium was not due to defective repression of GAL-driven PIB2 transcription by glucose (Fig. 2B). To make sure that growth of the suppressor mutant on solid Glu medium was not due to barely detectable PIB2 expression, we transformed the isolated strain with a PCR product aimed at replacing its chromosomal GAL-PIB2 construct with a hygromycin resistance gene (Fig. S6). As a control, we also transformed the parental gtr1Δ gtr2Δ GAL-PIB2 strain. Many hygromycin-resistant colonies were obtained after transformation of the suppressor mutant, but none with the parental strain, and subsequent PCR tests confirmed deletion of the GAL-PIB2 construct from the mutant (Fig. S6). This confirms that the suppressor mutant can grow without expressing either Gtr1/2 or Pib2. We next crossed the suppressor mutant with the gtr1Δ gtr2Δ GAL-PIB2 strain and found the resulting diploid, isolated on non-selective Gal medium, to grow on Glu medium (Fig. 2A). This shows that the ability of the suppressor mutant to grow independently of Gtr1/2 and Pib2 is a dominant phenotype.Subsequent genome sequencing revealed many mutations in the suppressor mutant that were not present in the parental strain. One of these mutations drew our attention, as it was located in the TOR1 gene. It is a G-to-A transition introducing the A2357T substitution into the Tor1 protein. The TOR1A2357T mutant gene was cloned into a low-copy-number plasmid and introduced into the GAL-PIB2 gtr1Δ gtr2Δ strain. It was found, unlike the wild-type TOR1 allele, to restore growth of gtr1Δ gtr2Δ GAL-PIB2 cells on rich Glu medium (Fig. 2C). Thus, the A2357T substitution in Tor1 confers to cells the ability to grow even when Gtr1/2 and Pib2 are not expressed. However, when we repeated the experiment with a less optimal minimal medium containing NH4+ as sole N source, gtr1Δ gtr2Δ GAL-PIB2 cells expressing the TOR1A2357T gene grew more slowly than the w-t, gtr1Δ gtr2Δ, and pib2Δ strains (Fig. 2C). This was confirmed when we compared growth of the strains more quantitatively in liquid medium (Fig. 2D). Furthermore, growth of the gtr1Δ gtr2Δ GAL-PIB2 / pTOR1A2357T strain was inhibited by rapamycin, even at lower doses that did not impact the growth of w-t cells (Fig. 2E). This observation, together with the suboptimal growth supported by the TOR1A2357T gene (Fig. 2C,D), suggests that TORC1 activity is only partially restored in gtr1Δ gtr2Δ GAL-PIB2 / pTOR1A2357T cells. To further assess this conclusion, we compared TORC1 activity in w-t and gtr1Δ gtr2Δ GAL-PIB2 / pTOR1A2357T cells. As the latter strain already contained a plasmid, we could not transform it with the one expressing HA-Npr1. We therefore assayed TORC1 activity by measuring phosphorylation of residue Thr737 in the Sch9 kinase7. We also tested, as controls, the gtr1Δ gtr2Δ and pib2Δ strains, both of which are reported to display reduced TORC1 activity. While loss of Gtr1/2 or Pib2 did lead to reduced Sch9 Thr737 phosphorylation as compared to the w-t (by ∼20% and ∼25%, respectively), the gtr1Δ gtr2Δ GAL-PIB2 / pTOR1A2357T strain showed a much greater reduction (∼70%) (Fig. 2F). Its residual phosphorylation was inhibited by rapamycin, as expected (Fig. S7). In conclusion, the Tor1 kinase encoded by the dominant TOR1A2357T supports weak though significant TORC1 activation that is independent of Gtr1/2 and Pib2, inhibited by rapamycin, and sufficient to sustain growth, albeit slower than that of the gtr1Δ gtr2Δ and pib2Δ strains.It has been reported previously that viability of a gtr1Δ pib2Δ strain is restored by an I1954S missense mutation within the TOR1 gene16. This mutation was initially isolated for its ability to reverse the hypersensitivity of a gtr1Δ mutant to caffeine16, an inhibitor of TORC146. In another study, the I1954V and I1957V substitutions in Tor1 were reported to confer both resistance to caffeine and increased Tor1 kinase activity in vitro, without altering sensitivity to rapamycin46. The I1954 and I1957 residues altered by these missense mutations are located within the FRB domain of Tor1, which includes the binding site of the inhibitory FKBP12–rapamycin complex. Results and discussion

Addition of NH4+ to yeast cells grown on a poor nitrogen source such as proline stimulates TORC1 activity27,34, which in turn triggers several adaptive responses. Among these are phospho-inhibition of the Npr1 kinase, with subsequent downregulation of several plasma membrane transporters34,35, and transcriptional repression of genes subject to the Nitrogen Catabolite Repression (NCR) circuit36,37. Previous studies have shown that these responses to NH4+ supply are more pronounced in the Σ1278b wild type than in the more commonly used S288c strain or its BY derivatives38,39. This difference is due to multiple recessive mutations in the S288c background, which remain uncharacterized38. We considered the possibility that the stronger response of TORC1 to NH4+ in the Σ1278b strain might be due to the contribution, in this strain but not in the S288c background, of a direct upstream TORC1 activator (in addition to Gtr1/2 and Pib2) that would be stimulated upon NH4+ supply. This hypothesis seemed conceivable, since the synthetic lethality between the gtr1Δ and pib2Δ mutations was demonstrated in experiments carried out in the BY strain background16,18,21,23.

Before exploring this model, we examined the contributions of Gtr1/2 and Pib2 to TORC1 activation upon NH4+ addition. The experiment was carried out in an amino-acid-prototrophic derivative of Σ1278b, initially grown on a glucose (Glu) medium with proline (Pro) as sole nitrogen source. As readout of TORC1 activation, we first used rapamycin-sensitive reduction of the electrophoretic mobility of the HA-tagged Npr1 kinase, indicative of increased phosphorylation via TORC140,41. The response was examined at early (4 and 10 min) and late (30, 60 min) time points after NH4+ addition, so as to allow comparing initial and sustained TORC1 activation27. In w-t cells, as expected, NH4+ addition triggered at both early and late time points an increase in HA-Npr1 phosphorylation, not detected in cells pretreated with rapamycin (Fig. 1A). In the gtr1Δ gtr2Δ strain, in contrast, stimulation of HA-Npr1 phosphorylation was impaired at early time points, although largely preserved at later time points. Sixty minutes after NH4+ addition, no significant difference vs. the w-t was observed (P = 0.47) (Fig. 1A). This phenotype of the gtr1Δ gtr2Δ strain was also seen in an experiment where more time points were examined (Fig. S1). These observations, in agreement with those of previous studies27,28,42, confirm that the Gtr1/2 complex is essential for the initial activation of TORC1 in response to NH4+ addition but largely dispensable for its later, more sustained activation.

It has been reported that an increase in intracellular glutamine (Gln) is the metabolic signal promoting late TORC1 activation in nitrogen-starved cells supplied with an optimal nitrogen source such as NH4+27. Other studies, furthermore, have shown Pib2 to be part of a Gln-sensing complex18,20. When we supplied NH4+ to pib2Δ mutant cells, HA-Npr1 phosphorylation was found, at early time points, to increase only slightly less markedly than in the w-t. After 60 min, no significant difference was observed (P = 0.46) (Fig. 1B). The simplest interpretation of the latter result, corroborated by a previous study42, is that Gtr1/2 and Pib2 redundantly support late NH4+-induced TORC1 activation, for instance in response to a Gln increase.

We also examined TORC1 activation in response to NH4+ addition using, as a readout, phosphorylation of residue Thr737 in the Sch9 kinase7. In keeping with previous studies27,28. NH4+ supply to w-t cells triggered a marked increase in Sch9 phosphorylation for at least 30 min (Fig. 1C). In both the gtr1Δ gtr2Δ and pib2Δ strains, early stimulation of Sch9 phosphorylation was much reduced, indicating Gtr1/2 and Pib2 function cooperatively to promote early TORC1 activation by NH4+. At later time points, however, the negative impact of the gtr1Δ gtr2Δ and pib2Δ mutations on TORC1 activation was less pronounced. The latter observation, reminiscent of the results obtained when examining HA-Npr1 phosphorylation, suggests that Gtr1/2 and Pib2 act in a less cooperative manner when supporting late NH4+-induced TORC1 activation.

The apparent variable cooperativity of Gtr1/2 or Pib2 in early and late TORC1 activation upon NH4+ supply prompted us to consider the possibility that an additional, highly NH4+-responsive upstream activator of TORC1 might contribute, in parallel with Gtr1/2 and Pib2, to late TORC1 activation, this being true in the Σ1278b but not the S288c background (because of loss-of-function mutations). To assess this hypothesis we first examined, on an amino-acid-rich medium, tetrads of a sporulated gtr1Δ /GTR1+
pib2Δ /PIB2+ double-heterozygous diploid derived from Σ1278b. Under these germination conditions, the results clearly showed synthetic lethality in all gtr1Δ pib2Δ double-mutant ascospores (Fig. S2A). We considered the possibility that this lethality might not be observed if the germination medium contained NH4+ as sole N source, but this was not the case (Fig. S2B). To investigate beyond the context of meiotic sporulation and germination the phenotype of a strain lacking both Gtr1/2 and Pib2, we next constructed a gtr1Δ gtr2Δ strain where PIB2 transcription is driven by a galactose-inducible promoter (Fig. 3A). This gtr1Δ gtr2Δ GAL-PIB2 strain grew normally on amino-acid-rich media containing galactose (Gal) or raffinose and galactose (Raf/Gal) as carbon source(s), but it failed to grow on glucose medium (Fig. 3B). This confirms that under these growth conditions, simultaneous lack of Gtr1/2 and Pib2 expression is incompatible with cell growth16,18,21. The same result was obtained on minimal media where NH4+ (Fig. 3C) or any one of 24 other nitrogenous compounds (Fig. S3) was supplied as sole nitrogen source. These results support the view that Gtr1/2 and Pib2 are the sole direct upstream activators of TORC1 capable of sustaining cell growth, at least under the tested growth conditions. Yeast, however, possesses an ortholog, named Rhb1, of the mammalian small GTPase Rheb, a key allosteric activator of mTORC13–5. Another small GTPase reported to promote mTORC1 activation, namely Arf143–45, is also conserved in yeast. We therefore isolated gtr1Δ gtr2Δ rhb1Δ and gtr1Δ gtr2Δ arf1Δ mutant strains to determine whether Rhb1 or Arf1 might contribute to late NH4+-induced TORC1 activation, but this proved not to be the case (Fig. S4). Furthermore, as compared to the gtr1Δ gtr2Δ strain, neither of these two mutants displayed any growth defect under 25 different nitrogen supply conditions (Fig. S5).

Interestingly, although the gtr1Δ gtr2Δ GAL-PIB2 strain did not grow on amino-acid-rich Glu medium, isolated colonies developed after prolonged incubation (Figs. 2A, 3B,C). We selected one of these apparent suppressor mutants and first established by qRT-PCR that its restored growth on Glu medium was not due to defective repression of GAL-driven PIB2 transcription by glucose (Fig. 2B). To make sure that growth of the suppressor mutant on solid Glu medium was not due to barely detectable PIB2 expression, we transformed the isolated strain with a PCR product aimed at replacing its chromosomal GAL-PIB2 construct with a hygromycin resistance gene (Fig. S6). As a control, we also transformed the parental gtr1Δ gtr2Δ GAL-PIB2 strain. Many hygromycin-resistant colonies were obtained after transformation of the suppressor mutant, but none with the parental strain, and subsequent PCR tests confirmed deletion of the GAL-PIB2 construct from the mutant (Fig. S6). This confirms that the suppressor mutant can grow without expressing either Gtr1/2 or Pib2. We next crossed the suppressor mutant with the gtr1Δ gtr2Δ GAL-PIB2 strain and found the resulting diploid, isolated on non-selective Gal medium, to grow on Glu medium (Fig. 2A). This shows that the ability of the suppressor mutant to grow independently of Gtr1/2 and Pib2 is a dominant phenotype.

Subsequent genome sequencing revealed many mutations in the suppressor mutant that were not present in the parental strain. One of these mutations drew our attention, as it was located in the TOR1 gene. It is a G-to-A transition introducing the A2357T substitution into the Tor1 protein. The TOR1A2357T mutant gene was cloned into a low-copy-number plasmid and introduced into the GAL-PIB2 gtr1Δ gtr2Δ strain. It was found, unlike the wild-type TOR1 allele, to restore growth of gtr1Δ gtr2Δ GAL-PIB2 cells on rich Glu medium (Fig. 2C). Thus, the A2357T substitution in Tor1 confers to cells the ability to grow even when Gtr1/2 and Pib2 are not expressed. However, when we repeated the experiment with a less optimal minimal medium containing NH4+ as sole N source, gtr1Δ gtr2Δ GAL-PIB2 cells expressing the TOR1A2357T gene grew more slowly than the w-t, gtr1Δ gtr2Δ, and pib2Δ strains (Fig. 2C). This was confirmed when we compared growth of the strains more quantitatively in liquid medium (Fig. 2D). Furthermore, growth of the gtr1Δ gtr2Δ GAL-PIB2 / pTOR1A2357T strain was inhibited by rapamycin, even at lower doses that did not impact the growth of w-t cells (Fig. 2E). This observation, together with the suboptimal growth supported by the TOR1A2357T gene (Fig. 2C,D), suggests that TORC1 activity is only partially restored in gtr1Δ gtr2Δ GAL-PIB2 / pTOR1A2357T cells. To further assess this conclusion, we compared TORC1 activity in w-t and gtr1Δ gtr2Δ GAL-PIB2 / pTOR1A2357T cells. As the latter strain already contained a plasmid, we could not transform it with the one expressing HA-Npr1. We therefore assayed TORC1 activity by measuring phosphorylation of residue Thr737 in the Sch9 kinase7. We also tested, as controls, the gtr1Δ gtr2Δ and pib2Δ strains, both of which are reported to display reduced TORC1 activity. While loss of Gtr1/2 or Pib2 did lead to reduced Sch9 Thr737 phosphorylation as compared to the w-t (by ∼20% and ∼25%, respectively), the gtr1Δ gtr2Δ GAL-PIB2 / pTOR1A2357T strain showed a much greater reduction (∼70%) (Fig. 2F). Its residual phosphorylation was inhibited by rapamycin, as expected (Fig. S7). In conclusion, the Tor1 kinase encoded by the dominant TOR1A2357T supports weak though significant TORC1 activation that is independent of Gtr1/2 and Pib2, inhibited by rapamycin, and sufficient to sustain growth, albeit slower than that of the gtr1Δ gtr2Δ and pib2Δ strains.

It has been reported previously that viability of a gtr1Δ pib2Δ strain is restored by an I1954S missense mutation within the TOR1 gene16. This mutation was initially isolated for its ability to reverse the hypersensitivity of a gtr1Δ mutant to caffeine16, an inhibitor of TORC146. In another study, the I1954V and I1957V substitutions in Tor1 were reported to confer both resistance to caffeine and increased Tor1 kinase activity in vitro, without altering sensitivity to rapamycin46.
