# Gaucher disease in Spanish patients: analysis of eight mutations.
**Authors:** Cormand B, Vilageliu L, Burguera JM, Balcells S, Gonzàlez-Duarte R, Grinberg D, Chabás A
**Journal:** Hum Mutat (1995)
**DOI:** [10.1002/humu.1380050406](https://doi.org/10.1002/humu.1380050406)

## Abstract

1. Hum Mutat. 1995;5(4):303-9. doi: 10.1002/humu.1380050406.

Gaucher disease in Spanish patients: analysis of eight mutations.

Cormand B(1), Vilageliu L, Burguera JM, Balcells S, Gonzàlez-Duarte R, Grinberg
D, Chabás A.

Author information:
(1)Department de Genètica, Facultat de Biologia, Universitat de Barcelona,

Gaucher disease is particularly prevalent among Ashkenazi Jews; thus most
studies have been reported on this ethnic group. We present the first data on
Spanish patients with Gaucher disease and provide one of the first reports on a
fairly well defined, large, non-Jewish population. Eight mutations were analyzed
in 35 patients, with different clinical subtypes, by restriction enzyme
digestion or allele-specific oligonucleotide (ASO) hybridization, after PCR
amplification of genomic DNA. Analysis of the eight mutations allowed
identification of 77.2% of the disease alleles, N370S and L444P alone accounting
for 70%. Mutation N370S, carried by 31 alleles (44.3%), appeared to be the most
prevalent in the Spanish population. The frequency of this mutation and of the
N370S/N370S genotype is closer to those described for Ashkenazi Jews than to the
frequencies found in other non-Jewish populations. Mutation L444P, the second
most abundant mutation, occurred in 25.7% of the disease alleles. Four alleles
carrying mutation D409H (5.7%) were detected in patients of different clinical
expression and one RecNciI allele in a type I patient. Mutations 84GG, IVS2 + 1,
R463C, and RecTL were also screened but were not found in any of our patients.
