# The pyruvate dehydrogenase complex of Saccharomyces cerevisiae is regulated by phosphorylation.
**Authors:** James AG, Cook RM, West SM, Lindsay JG
**Journal:** FEBS Lett (1995)
**DOI:** [10.1016/0014-5793(95)01020-f](https://doi.org/10.1016/0014-5793(95)01020-f)

## Abstract

1. FEBS Lett. 1995 Oct 9;373(2):111-4. doi: 10.1016/0014-5793(95)01020-f.

The pyruvate dehydrogenase complex of Saccharomyces cerevisiae is regulated by
phosphorylation.

James AG(1), Cook RM, West SM, Lindsay JG.

Author information:
(1)Division of Biochemistry and Molecular Biology, University of Glasgow,
Scotland, UK.

Mitochondria were isolated from Saccharomyces cerevisiae grown on different
carbon sources prior to incubation with [gamma-32P]ATP. A major 46,000-M(r)
phosphoprotein, corresponding in M(r) value to the E1 alpha subunit of the yeast
pyruvate dehydrogenase complex (PDC), was detected only in mitochondria isolated
from cells grown on a fermentable carbon source such as galactose.
Immunoprecipitation with subunit-specific antiserum to the E1 component of
mammalian or yeast PDC confirmed the identity of this polypeptide. PDC activity
in isolated yeast mitochondria could be inactivated in an ATP-dependent fashion
and reactivated in the presence of Ca2+ ions.
