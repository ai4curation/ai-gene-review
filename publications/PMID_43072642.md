Identifying upstream regulators of the STING pathway is critical for advancing
cancer immunotherapy. Although pescadillo ribosomal biogenesis factor 1 (PES1)
is overexpressed in multiple cancer types, its immunoregulatory role within the
tumor microenvironment remains poorly characterized. Here, we show that
tumor-cell PES1 knockdown is sufficient to induce an antitumor immune phenotype
in mice, characterized by enhanced CD8+ T-cell infiltration. We further define a
cell-intrinsic mechanism by which PES1 promotes immune evasion. PES1 loss
upregulates interferon (IFN)-stimulated genes in tumor cells, and this
transcriptional program is attenuated by STING knockdown, indicating that the
PES1-IFN signature and immune activation are dependent on STING signaling.
Mechanistically, PES1 interacts with the RNA helicase DDX21, and PES1 depletion
impairs DDX21 recruitment to R-loops. This defect is accompanied by the
accumulation of R-loops and RNA-DNA hybrids, which trigger STING-dependent type
I IFN production. More importantly, depletion of PES1 sensitizes melanoma to
anti-PD-1 therapy, resulting in significantly improved responses to immune
checkpoint blockade (ICB). These findings reveal a previously unrecognized
function of PES1 as a negative regulator of innate immune sensing in cancer.
Targeting PES1 may provide an upstream strategy for engaging the cGAS-STING axis
and overcoming resistance to ICB.
