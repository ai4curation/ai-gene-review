# Regulation of cell cycles in Drosophila development: intrinsic and extrinsic cues.

**Journal:** Annu Rev Genet (2003)

## Abstract

1. Annu Rev Genet. 2003;37:545-78. doi: 10.1146/annurev.genet.37.110801.143149.

Regulation of cell cycles in Drosophila development: intrinsic and extrinsic
cues.

Lee LA(1), Orr-Weaver TL.

Author information:
(1)Whitehead Institute and Massachusetts Institute of Technology, Cambridge,
Massachusetts 02142, USA. llee@wi.mit.edu

An intriguing aspect of cell cycle regulation is how cell growth and division
are coordinated with developmental signals to produce properly patterned
organisms of the appropriate size. Using the foundation laid by a detailed
understanding of the regulators that intrinsically control progression through
the cell cycle, links between developmental signals and the cell cycle are being
elucidated. Considerable progress has been made using Drosophila melanogaster,
both in identifying new cell cycle regulators that respond to developmental cues
and in defining the impact of extrinsic signals on homologs of mammalian
oncogenes and tumor suppressors. In this review, we discuss each cell cycle
phase, highlighting differences between archetypal and variant cell cycles
employed for specific developmental strategies. We emphasize the interplay
between developmental signals and cell cycle transitions. Developmental control
of checkpoints, cell cycle exit, and cell growth are also addressed.
