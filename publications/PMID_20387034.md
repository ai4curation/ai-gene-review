## Abstract

1. Mol Cells. 2010 Mar;29(3):283-90. doi: 10.1007/s10059-010-0035-x. Epub 2010

Phosphorylation dependent nucleocytoplasmic shuttling of BES1 is a key
regulatory event in brassinosteroid signaling.

Ryu H(1), Cho H, Kim K, Hwang I.

Author information:
(1)Department of Life Sciences, Pohang University of Science and Technology,
Pohang, 790-894, Korea.

Brassinosteroids BRs)play important roles in plant growth and development.BRs
modulate the phosphorylation status of two crucial transcription factors, BRI1
EMS SUPPRESSOR1 BES1)and BRASSINAZOLE RESISTANT1 (BZR1).Here we show that BES1
functions as a nucleocytoplasmic signal transmitter, and that its subcellular
localization modulates the output intensity of the BR signal.BRASSINOSTEROID
INSENSITIVE2 (BIN2)and other group II GLYCOGEN SYNTHASE KINASE 3 GSK3)-like
kinases phosphorylate BES1 and induce its nuclear export by regulating its
binding affinity with 14-3-3 proteins.We identified twelve putative
phosphorylation residues in BES1.Two of these residues, Ser 171 and Thr 175, are
critical for interaction with 14-3-3 proteins.The other putative phosphorylation
sites in the N-terminal region are required for the BIN2-mediated nuclear export
of BES1.Mutations of these motifs result in increased nuclear accumulation of
BES1 and enhanced BR responses in transgenic plants.Taken together, our results
indicate that the spatial redistribution of BES1 is important for regulation of
the BR signaling output.
