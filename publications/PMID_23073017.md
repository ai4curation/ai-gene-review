## Full Text

Abstract

JAs are important hormones for plant development and defense, and JA signaling is regulated by diverse mechanisms. We have recently identified two RING-type ubiquitin ligases, RGLG3 and RGLG4, as essential JA signaling regulators. In this addendum, we discuss some characters of RGLG3 and RGLG4, which further support their important roles in JA pathway. RGLG3 and RGLG4 didn’t interact with known key factors of the core JA pathway, rather, it might target on unknown protein that negatively regulated JA signaling. RGLG3 and RGLG4 expression was suppressed by SA treatment in an NPR1-independent manner, and rglg3 rglg4 moderated SA-inhibited JA-responsive PDF1.2 expression, suggesting RGLG3 and RGLG4 took roles in SA-JA antagonism. RGLG3 and RGLG4 could be important players of a regulatory network and coordinated diverse signals to modulate JA signaling.

Keywords: RGLG3, RGLG4, ubiquitin ligases, JA, SA, antagonism

Jasmonates (JAs) are a group of oxylipins critical for plant growth and survival. 1 Development or stress activates rapid biosynthesis of JAs which can be further converted into numerous conjugates including the highly bioactive (+)-7-iso-jasmonoyl- l -isoleucine (JA-Ile). 2 The JA-Ile is perceived by a receptor complex consisting of coronatine insensitive 1 (COI1), JAZs, and inositol pentakisphosphate (InsP5). 3 In unstimulated condition, JAZs recruit the co-repressor TOPLESS through the linker NOVEL INTERACTOR OF JAZ (NINJA) and associate with numerous transcription factors to inhibit their effect on downstream JA signaling. 4 , 5 While COI1 form a large ubiquitin ligase complex with other partners, and, upon JA perception, promotes degradation of JAZ proteins, thus releasing their inhibitory effect. 6 , 7 The Downstream JA responses are regulated by a number of important transcription factors that controls transcriptional reprogramming of a large group of JA-responsive genes and determine the specificity of JA-mediated responses. 5

Ubiquitination has been established as a fundamental post-translational modification in plants. 8 Ubiquitin attachment is completed by sequential actions of ubiquitin activating enzyme (E1), ubiquitin conjugating enzyme (E2), and ubiquitin ligase (E3). Ubiquitin chains linked through lysine at position 48 of ubiquitin (Lys 48) target substrates for degradation by the 26S proteasome, whereas polyubiquitin chains with alternative linkages (such as Lys 63) perform signaling functions. 9 A number of E3s have been identified in plant hormone signaling. 10 However, in the JA pathway, ubiquitin-mediated regulation is only exemplified by COI-mediated degradation of JAZs during JA perception. 6 , 7 , 11

Recently, we have characterized two novel RING-type ubiquitin ligases, RGLG3 and RGLG4, that essentially modulate several typical JA-related responses, including JA-inhibited root elongation, JA-dependent wound response, coronatine-suppressed root growth and, consistently, JA pathway-hijacking virulent pathogen Pst DC3000 invasion. In the double mutant rglg3 rglg4 , these processes are suppressed, while in the overexpression plants, they are promoted in a COI1-dependent manner. Based on these data, we propose that RGLG3 and RGLG4 are two important upstream modulators involved in the perception process of bioactive JA-Ile by the co-receptor COI. 12

The most fascinating issue raised in this work is the mechanism underlying roles of RGLG3 and RGLG4. In the yeast two-hybrid system, RGLG3 and RGLG4 didn’t interact with COI1 or its associating partners AXR1, 11 , 13 neither with MYC2, MYC3 or MYC4, 14 suggesting they couldn’t be targets of RGLG3 and RGLG4. Different from their homolog, RGLG2, which promotes formation of both Lys 48 and Lys 63-linked ubiquitin chains, 15 RGLG3 and RGLG4 likely catalyze only Lys 48-type ubiquitination. 12 As the ubiquitin ligase activities of RGLG3 and RGLG4 contributed to their function in JA signaling, 12 there should be a target negatively regulating JA signaling. Such a negative regulator, if exists, could participate in JA-Ile metabolism. The Arabidopsis cytochrome P450 CYP94B3 has been reported to promote JA-Ile metabolism, resulting in its inactivation. 16 However, it didn’t interact with either RGLG3 or RGLG4 in our yeast-two hybrid system, suggesting RGLG3 and RGLG4 couldn’t target on it. After excluding all these possibilities, we may consider if certain regulatory mechanism exists during the perception process of JA-Ile and it’s receptor, as these two parts are differently localized in the cell, with JA-Ile sequestered in the chloroplast, while COI1 in the nucleus. 1 , 17 , 18 This reminds us to check if RGLG3 and RGLG4 regulate JA transport in the future studies. Very interestingly, their homolog, RGLG2, has been connected to auxin transport in Arabidopsis. 15

JA pathway is integrated into a complex signaling network depending on the response. Previous work has revealed that some kinase cascades act upstream of the JA pathway in mediating various responses. 19 , 20 Our work also identified RGLGs-interacting protein kinases, implying RGLG3 and RGLG4 probably acted to divert upstream signals to JA pathway. It’s well known SA can suppress JA signaling in an NPR1-dependent manner and through actions of such factors as TGAs, WRKY70 and GRX48. 21 Using the transgenic plants expressing promoter:gus construct, we observed that SA treatment suppressed both RGLG3 and RGLG4 promoter activities, and the double mutant rglg3 rglg4 affected SA repression of JA-responsive PDF1.2 , suggesting RGLG3 and RGLG4 provided novel upstream sites for SA-JA antagonism. Furthermore, SA suppression of RGLG3 and RGLG4 remained unaffected in npr1–3 background, 22 indicating an NPR1-independent pathway mediates SA repression of JA pathway through RGLG3 and RGLG4. Together, these characters implied RGLG3 and RGLG4 could be important players in a complex regulatory network and coordinated diverse signals to modulate JA signaling.

Just like MYC2, 23 - 25 RGLG3 and RGLG4 could modulate stress-related responses, but altered RGLG3 and RGLG4 caused no defects related to JA deficiency like male sterility under our laboratory conditions, prompting us to believe that RGLG3 and RGLG4 function mainly in response to biotic or abiotic stimuli. Pathogen ( Pst DC3000) and mechanical wounding, are representative stresses commonly encountered by plants in nature. In these unfavorable conditions, plants will generally redistribute their limited resources from growth and development to defense to ensure their survival, as plants are sessile and cannot avoid stresses. 26 Our results showed that RGLG3 and RGLG4 affected root elongation and even overall plant growth. Therefore, the roles of RGLG3 and RGLG4 in essence highlight their contributions to the plant’s decision whether to grow or defend.
