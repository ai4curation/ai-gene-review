Tbx5 is involved in congenital heart disease, however, the mechanisms leading to
organ malformation are greatly unknown. We hypothesized a model by which the
Tbx5 binding protein Pdlim7 controls nuclear/cytoplasmic shuttling and function
of the transcription factor. Using the zebrafish, we present in vivo
significance for an essential role of Tbx5/Pdlim7 protein interaction in the
regulation of cardiac formation. Knock-down of Pdlim7 results in a non-looped
heart, strikingly reminiscent of the tbx5 heartstrings mutant phenotype.
However, while misregulation of Pdlim7 and Tbx5 produce similar aberrant cardiac
morphology, molecular and histological analysis uncovered that the Pdlim7 and
Tbx5 cardiac phenotypes are due to opposite effects on valve development. Loss
of Pdlim7 function causes no valve tissue to develop while lack of Tbx5 results
in increased valve tissue. These opposing defects are evident before valve
formation and are the result of distinct gene misregulation during specification
of the atrio-ventricular (AV) boundary. We show that Pdlim7/Tbx5 interactions
affect the expression of Tbx5 target genes nppa and tbx2b at the AV boundary,
and their domains of misexpression directly correlate with the identified valve
defects. These studies demonstrate that controlling the correct balance of Tbx5
activity is crucial for the specification of the AV boundary and valve
formation.

Introduction

Shaping the mature vertebrate heart with separated chambers and valves requires complex orchestration of gene expression. Several genes have been identified to be critical for cardiac morphogenesis, however, their function as it relates to cell activities is poorly understood (Harvey, 2002). The zebrafish lends itself for cardiovascular studies since the heart is simpler in design and most of the developmental signaling and genetic pathways appear to be highly conserved between teleosts and higher vertebrates (Chico et al., 2008). In the zebrafish, the heart forms from cells in the anterior lateral plate mesoderm aligned into two bilateral stripes of cardiac progenitors (Stainier, 2001). The bilateral heart fields migrate together towards the midline and form a cardiac cone, which then telescopes out to give rise to the linear heart tube; consisting of an inner endocardial cell layer and an outer myocardial layer. The zebrafish heart tube loops and constricts to morphologically and functionally delineate a single atrium and ventricle. Cells located at the boundary between the two chambers will finally give rise to the mature valve (Beis et al., 2005; Scherz et al., 2008).

One class of genes important for heart development and disease is the family of T-box transcription factors (Isphording et al., 2004; Stennard and Harvey, 2005). Mutations in the T-box gene TBX5 in humans (Basson et al., 1997; Li et al., 1997), knock-out studies in mice (Bruneau et al., 2001; Rallis et al., 2003), and knock-down experiments in zebrafish (Garrity et al., 2002) revealed comparable developmental defects of the heart and limbs in all species. Thus, the functional role of Tbx5 appears to be evolutionarily conserved. Studies in humans have indicated that TBX5 function during heart development is sensitive to intracellular protein levels, since either haploinsufficiency or gene duplication can produce cardiac malformations (Basson et al., 1997; Vaughan and Basson, 2000; Hatcher and Basson, 2001). The sensitivity to dose may be due to synergistic interactions with other transcription factors in the nucleus such as Nkx2-5 (Hiroi et al., 2001) or Gata4 (Garg et al., 2003), but may similarly be a result of yet unknown functions in the cytoplasm. We have identified a PDZ-LIM protein, LMP4, which specifically binds Tbx5 (Krause et al., 2004) and enables the transcription factor to shuttle between the nucleus and the actin cytoskeleton (Camarata et al., 2006).

PDZ-LIM proteins are thought to act as signal mediators involved in a variety of cellular processes such as migration, signal transduction, and differentiation (Dawid et al., 1998; Bach, 2000; Kadrmas and Beckerle, 2004). During cardiogenesis PDZ-LIM proteins have crucial functional roles. Knock-out mice of actinin-associated LIM-domain protein (ALP) develop cardiomyopathy (Pashmforoush et al., 2001). Similar cardiac defects were also detected in Cypher mutant mice (Zhou et al., 2001) and by respective morpholino knock-down in zebrafish (van der Meer et al., 2006). However, in depth analysis of cardiac defects and the specific roles of PDZ-LIM proteins in heart development remain to be investigated.

The functional consequence of Tbx5/LMP4 interactions during cardiogenesis has not been explored. To gain insight into the biological significance of this protein-protein interaction, we utilized the zebrafish, which can survive for an extended time during embryogenesis without a functional cardiovascular system (Glickman and Yelon, 2002). In this study, we build upon and extend our previous cellular work to investigate LMP4 function in cardiogenesis and its role in modulating Tbx5 activity.

ResultsZebrafish pdlim7 is coexpressed with tbx5 in the heart and pectoral finsWe used information available from chicken LMP4 and complementary strategies, including genome database searches and RT-PCR to clone a zebrafish cDNA with high sequence homology to the chicken gene. Sequence and phylogenetic analysis identified the isolated cDNA as the zebrafish lmp4 ortholog, which exists as a single copy in the zebrafish genome (Supplemental Fig. 1). Since there are multiple names for several PDZ-LIM proteins in the literature, to avoid further confusion, we adopted the simplified naming scheme recently proposed by GenBank. Both chicken and zebrafish LMP4 orthologs will now be referred to as Pdlim7.To generate a developmental expression series, we employed whole mount in situ hybridization and detected pdlim7 mRNA in single-, two-, four- and eight-cell stage embryos, revealing maternal deposition (Fig. 1A, B; data not shown). In addition, we observed expression throughout the embryo from gastrulation to the bud stage at 10 hours post-fertilization (hpf) (Fig. 1C, D). Later during development, pdlim7 expression became restricted to developed somites and was maintained in maturing skeletal muscle (Fig. 1E-H). A low-level of mRNA was also observed in head mesoderm and branchial arches. By 48 hpf, pdlim7 expression resolved to a distinctive chevron shape in tail muscle (Fig. 1H).Pdlim7 and Tbx5 are interacting proteins and it was therefore of interest to assess the domains of coexpression. We detected pdlim7 transcripts in the developing heart and pectoral fins; both structures which have previously been shown to express tbx5 (Begemann and Ingham, 2000). Just prior to the 18-somite stage, tbx5 is expressed in a single domain in the lateral plate mesoderm, which later separates into an anterior cardiac and posterior pectoral fin field (Fig. 1L; Ahn et al., 2002). At the 18-somite stage, pdlim7 was distributed in two distinct regions in the lateral plate mesoderm that correspond to the tbx5 expressing cardiac and pectoral fin precursors (Fig. 1I). Comparable to tbx5 (Fig. 1M, N), cardiac expression of pdlim7 was maintained at 24 hpf during heart tube formation and at 48 hpf during looping and chamber patterning, displaying a predominantly ventricular distribution (Fig. 1J, K). Comparable to tbx5 (Fig. 1M, N), cardiac expression of pdlim7 was maintained at 24 hpf during heart tube formation and at 48 hpf during looping and chamber patterning, displaying a predominantly ventricular distribution (Fig. 1J, K). In the developing pectoral fins, in situ hybridization detected pdlim7 first in the newly induced fin buds at 32-33 hpf. During fin bud outgrowth, expression was maintained in the mesenchyme at 48 hpf until it became restricted to proximal and lateral sub-domains by 72 hpf (Fig. 1O-Q). The developmental time window of pdlim7 pectoral fin expression coincides with tbx5 fin expression (Fig. 1R-T; Begemann and Ingham 2000; Garrity et al., 2002). Moreover, the cardiac and limb expression domains of zebrafish pdlim7 correspond with the gene’s expression in the developing chicken, further supporting the evolutionary conservation in the two species (Krause et al., 2004). In contrast to the chicken, however, eye expression for pdlim7 was not detected in the zebrafish. pdlim7 displays a broad and dynamic expression and revealed coexpression with tbx5 in the heart and forelimb, providing a foundation for an interaction of the encoded proteins in these organs.

We note that loss of AV boundary restriction of versican, bmp4, and notch1b was also detected in hst mutants (Fig. 6P-R; Garrity et al., 2002). DiscussionTbx5/Pdlim7 transcriptional regulation modelWe have previously developed a working model whereby Pdlim7 is able to regulate the subcellular localization and transcriptional activity of Tbx5 (Krause et al., 2004; Camarata et al., 2006; Bimber et al., 2007; Kulisz and Simon, 2008). The concept of the model is that Tbx5 is dynamically shuttling between nuclear and cytoplasmic sites. Regulated by yet unknown signals, Tbx5 exits the nucleus and binds to Pdlim7 along cytoplasmic actin filaments. The sequestration of Tbx5 by Pdlim7 prevents the transcription factor from activating target genes in the nucleus. Evidence supporting this model has come from in vitro binding assays, coimmunoprecipitations from eukaryotic cells, and in vivo protein localization studies (Krause et al., 2004; Camarata et al., 2006; Bimber et al., 2007; Kulisz and Simon, 2008). In the present report we provide the first in vivo evidence that Pdlim7 interacts with Tbx5 and can control its function. The Tbx5 target gene, nppa, appears to be regulated by both Tbx5 and Pdlim7 in cells bordering the AV boundary (Fig. 8). In Pdlim7 MO injected embryos, which are predicted to have increased Tbx5 levels in the nucleus, nppa expression is expanded into the AV boundary region. We observed the opposite effect in hst mutants that lack functional Tbx5, resulting in a reduction of nppa expression. These findings support the notion that Pdlim7/Tbx5 interactions regulate nppa expression. However, we note that nppa transcriptional regulation is complex and in addition to Tbx5, several transcription factor pathways as well as cellular stress are involved in its control (Houweling et al., 2005; Gopalan et al., 2003; Auman et al., 2007). In addition, we demonstrate that the expression of the AV boundary gene tbx2b expands after pdlim7 knock-down but is completely lost in hst embryos (Fig. 9). These data along with the presence of a putative Tbx5 binding site in the promoter of tbx2b (Chi et al., 2008), strongly argue that this gene is a novel direct target of Tbx5. The modulation of Pdlim7 levels in Tbx5 hst/+ embryos and its effect on tbx2b cardiac expression demonstrate in vivo support of our Tbx5/Pdlim7 regulation model.Appropriate levels of Tbx5 activity appear to be crucial for normal embryonic development, especially in the heart. In mouse models, knock-out or overexpression of Tbx5 results in severe cardiac defects (Bruneau et al., 2001; Liberatore et al., 2000). Mutations that produce nonfunctional products of human TBX5 result in heart and limb malformations, known as Holt-Oram syndrome (HOS) (Li et al., 1997; Basson et al., 1997). However, TBX5 gain of function mutations have also been reported to lead to HOS, such as a 12q2 chromosomal duplication, which includes the TBX5 gene locus, as well as a TBX5 mutation that results in a protein with higher affinity for target promoters (Vaughan and Basson, 2000; Hatcher and Basson, 2001; Postma et al., 2008). Therefore, Tbx5 levels are critical for normal function and Pdlim7 appears to modulate Tbx5 functional thresholds in developing organs and structures including the AV boundary.Pdlim7 is required for cardiac morphogenesis and AV boundary formationAfter morpholino knock-down of Pdlim7, the heart tube formed but displayed a more compact presumptive atrium; it then failed to loop and further differentiation was arrested (Figs. 2 and 5). We could not detect changes in the expression of early patterning and chamber identity genes, suggesting other causes for the malformed heart tube (Fig. 5). It is well known that during heart morphogenesis several activities must be orchestrated in a cascading manner including cell proliferation, differentiation, and migration (Harvey, 2002). Central to these physiological processes is actin remodeling, and misregulation of the actin cytoskeleton in pdlim7 morphants may explain the improper heart shape at 24 hpf. Several PDZ-LIM proteins, including Pdlim7, alter actin dynamics when localized to actin filaments (Guy et al., 1999; Vallenius et al., 2000; Nakagawa et al., 2000; Pashmforoush et al., 2001; Krause et al., 2004; Camarata et al., 2006). Therefore, loss of Pdlim7 function in the heart tube could lead to Tbx5 independent misregulation of the actin cytoskeleton, which in turn may have consequences on how the heart is shaped.Cardiac morphogenesis was significantly affected at 48 hpf in Pdlim7 morphants where gene restriction at the AV boundary was lost. Specification of cells at the AV boundary begins around 36 hpf both morphologically and molecularly (Beis et al., 2005). Cells at this boundary adopt a cuboidal shape and express genes such as versican, bmp4, notch1b, and tbx2b (Walsh and Stanier, 2001; Chi et al., 2008). We could also demonstrate a high level of tbx5 expression at the zebrafish AV boundary, which to our knowledge has not been reported previously. MO knock-down of pdlim7 resulted in misexpression of genes including tbx5 in cells at the AV boundary and occasional blood regurgitation indicating a valve deficiency. We analyzed the valve in both pdlim7 morphant and tbx5 hst mutant embryos and found opposing valve defects (Fig. 7). Knock-down of Pdlim7 results in loss of detectable valve structures while lack of Tbx5 function leads to increased amounts of valve tissue. Pdlim7 morphant and tbx5 mutant embryos reveal, along with problems in valve development, significant changes in gene expression (Figs. 6, 8, and 9). In pdlim7 MO injected embryos that do not develop a valve, at the AV boundary in endocardial cells, notch1b was lost along with the endocardial differentiation marker Dm-grasp. The boundary gene tbx2b and the chamber myocardial marker nppa, however, revealed an expanded expression domain. Conversely, in hst mutants that develop too much valve tissue, notch1b and Dm-grasp were expanded whereas tbx2b and nppa were downregulated. Consistent with these findings, over-activation of Notch signaling in zebrafish and mouse leads to an increase in expression of the Notch target snail, increased cell proliferation in the atrio-ventricular canal (AVC), and enlarged valves (Watanabe et al., 2006; Timmerman et al., 2004). Tbx2 is known as a transcriptional repressor (Carreira et al., 1998; Jacobs et al., 2000) and has been shown to negatively control cell cycle genes in the heart (Cai et al., 2005). In zebrafish heart development, Tbx2a appears to function as a repressor of proliferation and is required for proper AV boundary formation (Ribeiro et al., 2007). Therefore, the increase in valve tissue in tbx5 hst embryos may be due to an increase in Notch signaling and loss of Tbx2b repression, resulting in hypertrophy of the valve. The increase in AV boundary myocardial cells is designated by the reduction in nppa expression to more compact ventricular and atrial domains, complementing ectopic Dm-grasp in the underlying endocardium. In contrast, in Pdlim7 morphants the over-activation of Tbx5 may cause increased Tbx2b repression, loss of Notch signaling, resulting in a smaller or absent valve due to decreased AV boundary cell proliferation and differentiation. Consequently, in pdlim7 knock-down embryos, this failure of signaling and gene regulation leads to an expansion of chamber myocardium into the AV boundary, as indicated by the extended expression of nppa and loss of the differentiated endocardial marker Dm-grasp. The malformed valve phenotype may also explain the linear heart phenotype in tbx5 hst and pdlim7 morpholino injected embryos. The AV boundary including the forming valve may act as a pivot point for cardiac looping, and AV boundary expansion or retraction could have a significant impact on morphogenesis. Similar looping defects in zebrafish have also been observed after knock-down of Tbx3 and Tbx2a (Ribeiro et al., 2007). However, problems in AV boundary specification may only be one mechanism for producing a non-looped heart, as loss of cell contractility can also lead to looping defects (Beis et al., 2005; Auman et al., 2007). Our data demonstrate that the interaction between Pdlim7 and Tbx5 is critical in the cells of the AV boundary to insure correct cell specification and valve size. These new findings warrant future experiments towards cell cycle regulation by Pdlim7 and Tbx5 and the affects on valve formation.In the developing chicken heart, we have detected Tbx5/Pdlim7 complexes colocalized along actin filaments at comparable locations, namely the atrio-ventricular cushion (AVC), but not in the chamber myocardium (Bimber et al., 2007). The AVC in avians and mammals, similar to the AV boundary in zebrafish, will give rise to chamber valves, contribute to the septum, and conduction system (Christoffels et al., 2004; Eisenberg and Markwald, 1995; Markwald et al., 1996; Bies et al., 2005; Scherz et al., 2008; De la Cruz et al., 1983; Davis et al., 2001; Rentschler et al., 2001). In this context it is of interest that Tbx5del/+ mice display ventricular myocardial but also septal and conduction system defects (Bruneau et al., 2001; Moskowitz et al., 2004). The myocardial and AVC associated phenotypes in Tbx5 mutant mice may be, therefore, due to separate nuclear and non-nuclear Tbx5 functions depending on Pdlim7 interactions. Further analysis using genetically engineered mice with altered pdlim7 levels will help to understand the role of Tbx5/Pdlim7 interactions in cushion and valve development and these investigations are currently ongoing in our laboratory.TBX5 regulation in congenital heart diseaseIn the present study we have provided evidence that both zebrafish Tbx5 and Pdlim7 operate in one pathway and that their direct interaction is critical for AV boundary and valve formation, possibly by directly regulating the expression of tbx2b at the boundary. In chicken embryos we have shown that Tbx5 and Pdlim7 can colocalize outside the nucleus, for example in cells of the AVC (Bimber et al., 2007). Importantly, AVC associated phenotypes have been observed in mutant mouse models of Tbx5 and Pdlim7, suggesting a distinct functional role for both proteins when complexed to actin filaments (Bruneau et al., 2001; Krcmery and Simon, unpublished observations). In humans, mutations in TBX5 cause HOS, a dominant congenital condition associated with a range of upper-limb and cardiac malformations (Basson et al., 1997; Li et al., 1997). The varying cardiac clinical presentations include conduction system abnormalities and mitral or tricuspid valve defects (Basson et al., 1997; Li et al., 1997; Bruneau et al., 1999: Reamon-Buettner and Borlak, 2004; Borozdin et al., 2006). The valves are derived almost entirely from endocardial cushion cells located at the AV boundary (de Lange et al., 2004; Lincoln et al., 2004). Supplemental Fig. 3: pdlim7 splice-site targeted morpholino causes heart-looping defects. (A) Schematic of pdlim7 splice morpholino (MO2) function. Red arrow denotes MO2 target sequence at exon 2 donor site. Exon 2 contains the translation initiation sequence (B) RT-PCR assay to detect improper splicing of pdlim7 mRNA after injection of MO2. Black arrows in A show position of primers used for PCR. (C) Sequencing of wild-type (top) and splice mutant (bottom) PCR products to confirm improper splicing. (D) Quantification of heart looping-defects in MO2 injected embryos stained with cmcl2. n, number of embryos scored.

Supplemental Fig. 4: Non-specific morpholino head defects are p53 dependent. (A-D) Lateral view of head region of 28 hpf embryos. (A) Uninjected wild-type, (B) embryo injected with p53 MO alone, (C) embryo injected with pdlim7 MO2 alone, and (D) embryo coinjected with p53 MO and pdlim7 MO2. Note the significant difference in head size in C and D. Injection of the p53 MO had no effect on heart and pectoral fin phenotypes.

Supplemental Fig. 5: Synergistic activity of pdlim7 initiation and splice morpholinos. (A-D) Lateral view of the heart in live embryos 48 hpf. (A) Wild-type control. (B) Initiation ATG MO1 injected, 1ng. (C) Splice MO2 injected, 1.5ng. Atrium is in focus in A-C and ventricle is out of focus due to normal heart looping. Position of looping denoted by arrowhead. (D) Co-injected embryo with MO1 and MO2, 1ng/1.5ng respectively. Both atrium and ventricle are in the same focal plane and no looping observed (arrowhead). (E-F) Ventral view of in situ hybridization showing cmlc2 expression in 48 hpf embryos. (E) Wild-type control. (F) pdlim7 MO co-injected embryo. (G) Quantification of embryos with cardiac looping defects. n, number of embryos scored. V, ventricle. A, atrium.
