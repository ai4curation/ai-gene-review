# Antithrombin-S195A factor Xa-heparin structure reveals the allosteric mechanism of antithrombin activation.

Author information:
(1)Department of Haematology, Division of Stuctural Medicine, Thrombosis

Regulation of blood coagulation is critical for maintaining blood flow, while
preventing excessive bleeding or thrombosis. One of the principal regulatory
mechanisms involves heparin activation of the serpin antithrombin (AT).
Inhibition of several coagulation proteases is accelerated by up to 10,000-fold
by heparin, either through bridging AT and the protease or by inducing
allosteric changes in the properties of AT. The anticoagulant effect of short
heparin chains, including the minimal AT-specific pentasaccharide, is mediated
exclusively through the allosteric activation of AT towards efficient inhibition
of coagulation factors (f) IXa and Xa. Here we present the crystallographic
structure of the recognition (Michaelis) complex between heparin-activated AT
and S195A fXa, revealing the extensive exosite contacts that confer specificity.
The heparin-induced conformational change in AT is required to allow
simultaneous contacts within the active site and two distinct exosites of fXa
(36-loop and the autolysis loop). This structure explains the molecular basis of
protease recognition by AT, and the mechanism of action of the important
therapeutic low-molecular-weight heparins.
