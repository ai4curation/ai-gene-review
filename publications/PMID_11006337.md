# Jasmonic acid signaling modulates ozone-induced hypersensitive cell death.
**Authors:** Rao MV, Lee H, Creelman RA, Mullet JE, Davis KR
**Journal:** Plant Cell (2000)
**DOI:** [10.1105/tpc.12.9.1633](https://doi.org/10.1105/tpc.12.9.1633)

## Abstract

1. Plant Cell. 2000 Sep;12(9):1633-46. doi: 10.1105/tpc.12.9.1633.

Jasmonic acid signaling modulates ozone-induced hypersensitive cell death.

Rao MV(1), Lee H, Creelman RA, Mullet JE, Davis KR.

Author information:
(1)Plant Biotechnology Center, Ohio State University, Columbus, Ohio 43210, USA.

Recent studies suggest that cross-talk between salicylic acid (SA)-, jasmonic
acid (JA)-, and ethylene-dependent signaling pathways regulates plant responses
to both abiotic and biotic stress factors. Earlier studies demonstrated that
ozone (O(3)) exposure activates a hypersensitive response (HR)-like cell death
pathway in the Arabidopsis ecotype Cvi-0. We now have confirmed the role of SA
and JA signaling in influencing O(3)-induced cell death. Expression of
salicylate hydroxylase (NahG) in Cvi-0 reduced O(3)-induced cell death. Methyl
jasmonate (Me-JA) pretreatment of Cvi-0 decreased O(3)-induced H(2)O(2) content
and SA concentrations and completely abolished O(3)-induced cell death. Cvi-0
synthesized as much JA as did Col-0 in response to O(3) exposure but exhibited
much less sensitivity to exogenous Me-JA. Analyses of the responses to O(3) of
the JA-signaling mutants jar1 and fad3/7/8 also demonstrated an antagonistic
relationship between JA- and SA-signaling pathways in controlling the magnitude
of O(3)-induced HR-like cell death.
