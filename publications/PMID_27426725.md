Activation of NF-κB signaling plays pivotal roles in innate immune responses
against pathogens. It requires strict control to avert inflammatory diseases.
However, the mechanisms underlying this tight regulation are not completely
understood. Here, we identified LRRC14, a novel member of LRR (leucine-rich
repeat) protein family, as a negative regulator in TLR signaling. Expression of
LRRC14 resulted in decreased activation of NF-κB, whereas knockdown of LRRC14
enhanced NF-κB activation as well as the production of inflammatory cytokines.
Mechanistically, LRRC14 bound to HLH domain of IKKβ to block its interaction
with NEMO and thereby inhibiting the phosphorylation of IKKβ and NF-κB
activation. In addition, our data showed that TLR signaling led to lower
expression of LRRC14. Together, LRRC14 may function as a checkpoint to prevent
overzealous inflammation.
