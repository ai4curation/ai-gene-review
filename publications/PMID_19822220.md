# The mitA gene of Aspergillus fumigatus is required for mannosylation of inositol-phosphorylceramide, but is dispensable for pathogenicity.
**Authors:** Kotz A, Wagener J, Engel J, Routier F, Echtenacher B, Pich A, Rohde M, Hoffmann P, Heesemann J, Ebel F
**Journal:** Fungal Genet Biol (2010)
**DOI:** [10.1016/j.fgb.2009.10.001](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.fgb.2009.10.001)

## Abstract

1. Fungal Genet Biol. 2010 Feb;47(2):169-78. doi: 10.1016/j.fgb.2009.10.001. Epub
 2009 Oct 12.

The mitA gene of Aspergillus fumigatus is required for mannosylation of
inositol-phosphorylceramide, but is dispensable for pathogenicity.

Kotz A(1), Wagener J, Engel J, Routier F, Echtenacher B, Pich A, Rohde M,
Hoffmann P, Heesemann J, Ebel F.

Author information:
(1)Max-von-Pettenkofer-Institute, Ludwig-Maximilians-University, Munich,
Germany.

GDP-mannose:inositol-phosphorylceramide (MIPC)-derived glycosphingolipids are
important pathogen-associated molecular patterns (PAMP) of Candida albicans and
according to recently published data also of Aspergillus fumigatus. MIPC
transferases are essential for the synthesis of MIPC, but have so far been
studied only in Saccharomyces cerevisiae and C. albicans. Here, we have
identified MitA as the only MIPC transferase in A. fumigatus. The DeltamitA
mutant lacks MIPC and MIPC-derived glycosphingolipids and accumulates the
precursor IPC. The mutant grows normally, shows no defects in cell wall or
membrane organization and a normal resistance to different stressors. It is,
however, sensitive to high Ca(2+) concentrations, especially during germination.
Germination of DeltamitA mutant conidia is also decelerated under normal growth
conditions, but neither the virulence of this mutant in a systemic model of
infection nor its ability to trigger a cytokine response in macrophages is
impaired, arguing against a role of MIPC-derived glycosphingolipids as important
A. fumigatus PAMPs.
