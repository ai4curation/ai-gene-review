Pyroptosis is a pathogenenic mechanism of polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS).
N5-methylcytosine (m5C) methylation is a RNA modification that affects the
progression of diseases. However, its role in PCOS remains not understood. This
study aimed to investigate the role of NSUN7 in PCOS, particularly its impact on
pyroptosis. KGN cells were treated with LPS to establish a cell model, and
pyroptosis was evaluated using enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay, flow
cytometry, and western blotting. The effect of NSUN7 on the m5C modification of
NLRP3 was assessed using methylated RNA immunoprecipitation, dual-luciferase
reporter assay, and actinomycin D treatment experiment. A PCOS rat model was
generated, hormones were detected using ELISA, and pyroptosis was evaluated in
the ovaries and primary granulosa cells. The results showed that NSUN7
expression was reduced in patients with PCOS and LPS-treated KGN cells.
Silencing of NSUN7 inhibited LPS-induced cell pyroptosis. Moreover, NSUN7
knockdown inhibited m5C methylation of NLRP3 and reduced NLRP3 mRNA stability.
Overexpression of NLRP3 reversed the pyroptosis inhibition induced by NSUN7
knockdown. Furthermore, interfering with NSUN7 alleviated hormone abnormalities
and suppressed granulosa cell pyroptosis in PCOS rats. In conclusion, silencing
of NSUN7 attenuates PCOS progression by inhibiting pyroptosis of granulosa cells
by suppressing m5C methylation of NLRP3.

Introduction

Polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS) is a prevalent endocrine disorder that affects approximately 4%−18% of reproductive-aged women globally1. In China, the prevalence of PCOS has increased over the last 10 years2. It is characterized by hyperandrogenism, ovulatory dysfunction, polycystic ovaries, and metabolic aberrations3. Despite advancing research on PCOS, its diagnostic difficulties and non-specific treatment are still huge clinical challenges4. The pathogenesis of PCOS remains elusive, but likely involves complex genetic-environmental-lifestyle interactions5,6. Thus, it is urgent to clarify the pathogenesis of PCOS, which will help to provide a scientific basis for the diagnosis and treatment of PCOS.

Recent research has suggested that pyroptosis, a form of programmed cell death distinct from apoptosis and necrosis, may play a critical role in the pathophysiology of PCOS7. This process is orchestrated by the activation of the inflammasomes. The activation of inflammasomes leads to caspase processing and gasdermin D cleavage, inducing cell membrane rupture and the secretion of pro-inflammatory cytokines, such as interleukin-1β (IL-1β) and IL-188. Due to the close relationship between inflammation and PCOS9, this inflammatory cell death is receiving increasing attention in PCOS research. Nevertheless, its regulatory mechanism remains largely unknown.

Recent evidence has highlighted the role of epigenetic modifications in the development of PCOS, particularly in the granulosa cells10. N5-methylcytosine (m5C) methylation is a kind of RNA modification that is involved in diseases by affecting mRNA stability, splicing, and translation11. NOP2/Sun RNA methyltransferase family member 7 (NSUN7) is an RNA methyltransferase that catalyzes the transfer of a methyl group to the carbon 5 position of cytosine12. It is a sperm motility regulator13, and recent studies suggest that NSUN7 is involved in human disease14,15. However, the role of NSUN7 in ovarian physiology and the pathogenesis of PCOS are not clear.

This study aimed to investigate the role of NSUN7 in the regulation of pyroptosis in ovarian granulosa cells and its potential contribution to the pathogenesis of PCOS. We hypothesized that NSUN7-mediated m5C methylation of NLRP3 was involved in PCOS progression. This study identifies that NSUN7-mediated m5C methylation of NLRP3 is a novel epigenetic regulation governing granulosa cell pyroptosis in PCOS, and the NSUN7-NLRP3 axis may be new therapeutic targets for PCOS.

Ovarian morphology results showed that the ovaries were enlarged in PCOS rats, while knockdown of NSUN7 reduced the diameter of the ovaries (Fig. 5H). Pathological changes of the ovaries in rats were observed after H&E staining. The ovarian morphology of the rats in the control group was normal, with granulosa cells neatly arranged and follicles and corpus luteum visible. Subsequently, granulosa cells were isolated from the ovarian tissues. Pyroptosis was evaluated using these cells. The results showed that IL-18 and IL-1β levels were increased in the model group, while after knocking down NSUN7, their levels were reduced (Fig. 5K and L). The pyroptotic cell rate was elevated in PCOS rats, which was counteracted by NSUN7 knockdown (Fig. 5M). Western blotting results showed that the levels of NLRP3, cleaved caspase1, and GSDMD-N were upregulated in the granulosa cells from PCOS rats, whereas NSUN7 knockdown decreased their levels in the PCOS model; however, pro-caspase1 and GSDMD levels were not affected by letrozole and NSUN7 (Fig. 5N). DiscussionThis study provides novel insight into the role of NSUN7 in ovarian granulosa cell pyroptosis and the pathogenesis of PCOS. The results showed that knockdown of NSUN7 inhibited LPS-induced pyroptosis in KGN cells, an effect associated with decreased m5C modification and reduced stability of NLRP3. Moreover, NSUN7 knockdown impeded PCOS progression and pyroptosis in vivo.With increasing research on RNA modifications, evidence suggests their important roles in human diseases, including PCOS, particularly the regulation of RNA modifying enzymes on disease development22,23. However, the current research on RNA modification in PCOS is still in the initial stage, and whether m5C is involved in the progression of PCOS has not been identified. A previous study has indicated that the deletion of the m5C writer, NSUN5, can impede follicular development and lead to ovarian dysfunction24. Additionally, YBX1, a reader of m5C methylation, has been found to promote granulosa cell proliferation in a m5C-dependent manner and is thus involved in ovarian insufficiency25. Based on these studies, we hypothesized that m5C methylation could play an important role in PCOS with ovarian insufficiency. In this study, another m5C writer, NSUN7, was identified to be highly expressed in patients with PCOS and LPS-treated KGN cells. Furthermore, the AUC value of 0.9853 for the ROC curve demonstrates the diagnostic value of NSUN7. NSUN7, a m5C writer, like other m5C writers, has been reported to regulate cellular function26. Pyroptosis in granulosa cells is increased in patients with PCOS, suggesting that pyroptosis is a pathogenesis of PCOS7,27. Therefore, in the present study, the role of NSUN7 in PCOS was explored via identifying cell pyroptosis. The in vitro and animal study results showed that knockdown of NSUN7 inhibited PCOS by suppressing pyroptosis of granulosa cells. These data position NSUN7 as a pivotal regulator of ovarian granulosa cell fate in PCOS, filling the gap in the field of the impact of NSUN7 on ovarian function. The results suggest that NSUN7 may be a promising diagnostic biomarker, and targeting NSUN7 is a possible therapeutic strategy for PCOS.Elucidating the underlying mechanisms of NSUN7 in PCOS is urgent. Given the importance of the m5C methylation function of NSUN7, it is plausible that NSUN7 may modulate m5C methylation of pyroptosis-related factors. The results indicated that silencing of NSUN7 inhibited m5C methylation of NLRP3 rather than other pyroptosis-associated factors. The m5C modification of NLRP3 revealed by this study uncovers a novel epigenetic mechanism for inflammasomes. Moreover, since m5C can modulate RNA stability and translation28, the stability of NLRP3 was explored. The data showed that NSUN7 knockdown inhibited NLRP3 stability. Inflammasomes such as NLRP3 are pyroptosis-inducible factors that are associated with several inflammatory diseases29. Chronic inflammation is a feature of PCOS. Therefore, aberrant NLRP3 and subsequent pyroptosis contribute to the pathogeneses of PCOS30. Inhibition of NLRP3 activation and pyroptosis has been considered to be a promising therapeutic strategy31. Notably, Liu et al.32 have reported that α-ketoglutarate inhibits NLRP3-dependent KGN cell pyroptosis. Xie et al.33 have revealed that exosomes derived from bone marrow mesenchymal stem cells inhibit pyroptosis of KGN cells by suppressing the NLRP3/caspase1 pathway. These studies demonstrate the important role of NLRP3 in KGN cell pyroptosis. In this study, the results showed that overexpression of NLRP3 reversed the inhibition of pyroptosis caused by NSUN7 knockdown, suggesting that the NSUN7/m5C/NLRP3 axis promotes pyroptosis of granulose cells. These data elucidate the underlying molecular mechanism of the function of NSUN7, establishing a linear causal relationship.There are limitations of this study. For example, PCOS is a heterogeneous disease, and the clinical samples collected in this work may not fully cover different subtypes. The PCOS rat model only reflect hormonal disorders, but cannot fully recapitulate human PCOS. Additionally, this work only focused on the impact of the NSUN7/m5C/NLRP3 axis on cell pyroptosis, but the regulation of other programmed cell death modes by NSUN7 and its modification of other RNA molecules remain unclear, and further studies are needed to clarify its global influence.In conclusion, this study sheds light on the role of NSUN7 in the regulation of granulosa cell pyroptosis and the pathogenesis of PCOS. The results demonstrated that interfering with NSUN7 inhibited ovarian granulosa cell pyroptosis by inhibiting m5C methylation of NLRP3, thereby attenuating PCOS-induced hormone abnormality. This work opens up new avenues for understanding the complex interplay between m5C modification, pyroptosis, and PCOS. Moreover, it highlights the potential of NSUN7 as a novel therapeutic target in PCOS. However, translating these basic findings to clinical practice faces significant challenges. In the future, we will further explore the diagnostic value of NSUN7 in a multi-center and large-sample PCOS population. In addition, we will focus on exploring small molecule inhibitors or drugs targeting NSUN7, compare their advantages and disadvantages with existing drugs, and investigate their safety and efficacy in the treatment of PCOS.
