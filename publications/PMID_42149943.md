BACKGROUND AND AIMS: Fatal ventricular tachyarrhythmias (FVTs) are a major cause
of sudden cardiac death globally. Despite their clinical importance, current
antiarrhythmic therapies remain constrained by limited efficacy and
proarrhythmic risks. Although endogenous cardiac cholinergic signalling
contributes to electrophysiological regulation, the therapeutic potential of
targeting ventricular α4β2 nicotinic acetylcholine receptors (nAChRs), a pivotal
component of this system, for FVT treatment remains to be elucidated.
METHODS: A pharmacological screen identified positive allosteric modulators
(PAMs) of α4β2 nAChRs as possessing antiarrhythmic properties. Through
structure-based design, salvage-1 was developed and its efficacy and safety
profile were evaluated in rodent, porcine, and human ex vivo heart models of
FVTs. The underlying mechanism was investigated using patch-clamp
electrophysiology, high-resolution optical mapping, and molecular dynamics
simulations.
RESULTS: Pharmacological screening validated the potentiation of α4β2 nAChRs as
a promising antiarrhythmic strategy. Structure-guided development yielded
salvage-1, a PAM that selectively engages Phe312 and Phe316 on the α4 subunit to
stabilize the receptor in an open-channel state. Across rodent, porcine, and
human ex vivo heart models of FVTs, salvage-1 consistently prevented
arrhythmogenesis and rapidly restored sinus rhythm. Mechanistically, salvage-1
enhanced acetylcholine-gated currents, selectively improved conduction velocity
in injured myocardium, and suppressed re-entry, without compromising
electrophysiology in healthy tissue.
CONCLUSIONS: This study identifies ventricular α4β2 nAChRs as a druggable target
for FVTs and introduces salvage-1 as a first-in-class therapeutic candidate,
thereby establishing a new direction for the pharmacological therapy of cardiac
arrhythmias.

This study identifies ventricular α4β2 nicotinic acetylcholine receptors as a previously unrecognized druggable target for fatal ventricular arrhythmias (FVTs). The first-in-class, positive allosteric modulator salvage-1 demonstrates potent antiarrhythmic efficacy across rodent, porcine, and human ex vivo heart models, outperforming the standard-of-care lidocaine in both effectiveness and safety. These properties support its potential application in both FVT prevention, such as in patients with a history of sudden cardiac arrest or those with underlying structural heart disease, and FVT treatment, including acute episodes of ventricular tachycardia and ventricular fibrillation, as well as an adjunctive therapy during cardiac surgery or catheter ablation procedures. These findings establish a new therapeutic paradigm that shifts from non-selective ion channel blockade to targeted receptor modulation.

Introduction

Despite significant advancements in arrhythmia management, the safe and effective pharmacological treatment of cardiac arrhythmias remains a critical challenge in modern clinical practice.1–4 Currently approved antiarrhythmic agents, such as ion channel blockers, are limited by their narrow therapeutic windows and substantial proarrhythmic risks. These drugs not only have the potential to worsen existing arrhythmias but also to trigger new, life-threatening arrhythmic events, thereby increasing the risk of all-cause and cardiovascular mortality.2,3,5–10 These safety concerns underscore the urgent need for more selective, mechanism-based antiarrhythmic strategies.

Clinically, fatal ventricular tachyarrhythmias (FVTs), such as ventricular tachycardia (VT) and ventricular fibrillation (VF), are among the deadliest arrhythmias, often leading to sudden cardiac death within minutes if untreated.5,11–14 Our recent research has revealed that the endogenous cholinergic system (ECS) in ventricular cardiomyocytes modulates local electrophysiology independently of autonomic input, in which nicotinic acetylcholine receptors (nAChRs) act as key signalling mediators. Importantly, our experimental evidence establishes that dysregulated α4β2 nAChR signalling contributes to FVT pathogenesis and demonstrates that targeted receptor potentiation effectively suppresses arrhythmia susceptibility.15 These findings collectively point to α4β2 nAChR as a potential therapeutic target for antiarrhythmic intervention. To translate this promise into clinical reality, a dual approach is essential: systematic validation of its target credibility, coupled with the development of therapeutically viable agents featuring innovative structural architectures.

In this work, we therefore aimed to explore the antiarrhythmic potential of targeting α4β2 nAChRs in the setting of FVTs. Using a combination of structural analysis and pharmacological screening, we developed modulators of α4β2 nAChRs and evaluated their ability to influence receptor function and suppress arrhythmogenesis. Our findings identify modulation of α4β2 nAChRs as an effective antiarrhythmic strategy and highlight a non-canonical target beyond the traditional ion-channel–centric framework of antiarrhythmic drug discovery.

RESULTSFunctional screening identifies α4β2 nAChRs as a novel therapeutic target for FVTsTo identify novel druggable targets for FVTs, we conducted a systematic pharmacological screening of compounds targeting the ECS—a recently identified local regulator of ventricular electrophysiology.15 Using an ex vivo rat model of ischaemia-reperfusion (I/R)-induced FVTs that reliably produced sustained arrhythmic episodes (Supplementary data online, Figure S1A), we screened 56 compounds sourced from DrugBank and PubChem databases,20,21 including 19 clinically approved drugs and 37 tool compounds targeting ACh-related pathways (Figure 1A and Supplementary data online, Table S1). Notably, varenicline, a partial agonist of α4β2 nAChRs, and desformylflustrabromine (dFBr), a positive allosteric modulator (PAM) of α4β2 nAChRs, emerged as the most effective candidates (Supplementary data online, Table S2 and S3). At 10 µM, varenicline reduced FVT incidence from 100% to 25% (P < .0001) and shortened arrhythmia duration from 9.5 min (95% CI 5.59–13.36) to 0.76 min (95% CI 0.07–1.45) (P < .0001) (Figure 1B and C). Strikingly, dFBr exhibited even stronger efficacy, significantly lowering FVT incidence and duration (Figure 1D and E). These antiarrhythmic effects were further confirmed in vivo, where both compounds (10 mg/kg) significantly suppressed FVT inducibility and duration in I/R-injured rats, with dFBr consistently outperforming varenicline across all endpoints (Figure 1F–I; Supplementary data online, Figure S2). To validate the in vivo relevance of α4-containing nAChRs, we generated cardiomyocyte-specific Chrna4 knockout (Chrna4-CKO) mice (Supplementary data online, Figure S7). Following optical mapping and ECG recording, we observed that salvage-1 failed to induce statistically significant changes in FVT incidence, FVT duration, phase singularities, or dominant frequency or CV in Chrna4-CKO mice (Supplementary data online, Figure S8), thereby confirming that α4β2 nAChRs are essential for its antiarrhythmic effects.

Structural analysis using chemical cross-linking mass spectrometry revealed multiple cross-link products in the absence of salvage-1, most of which exceeded the distance constraint of the cross-linker DCD (31.86 Å),34 and were classified as over-length cross-links (Figure 6B and Supplementary data online, Table S8). Upon salvage-1 binding, we observed a marked reduction in total and over-length cross-links (Figure 6B and Supplementary data online, Table S8), indicating that salvage-1 enhances structural stability and locks the receptor into a defined conformation. Single-channel recordings confirmed that salvage-1 significantly increases open-channel probability without affecting unitary conductance (Figure 6C).

To identify the structural basis for salvage-1 binding, we performed molecular docking against the α4β2 nAChRs cryo-EM structure (PDB: 8ST0),35 identifying five candidate sites on the α4 subunit (Figure 7A). Alanine-scanning mutagenesis revealed that mutations at Phe312 and Phe316 (Mut.5) markedly impaired salvage-1 binding (Supplementary data online, Figure S9A and B). Consistently, F312A and F316A abolished salvage-1–induced potentiation of ACh-gated currents (Figure 7B and Supplementary data online, Figure S9C and D), establishing these residues as critical for its activity.

Molecular dynamics simulations of four receptor states (apo, salvage-1–bound, ACh-bound, ACh&salvage-1–bound) showed the highest open probability (∼35%) in the ACh&salvage-1–bound state vs lowest (∼7%) in the apo state (Figure 7C). In summary, these results demonstrate that salvage-1 binds specifically to Phe312 and Phe316 on the α4 subunit of α4β2 nAChRs, stabilizing the open-channel state and enhancing antiarrhythmic efficacy through allosteric potentiation.
