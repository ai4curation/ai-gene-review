# Kruppel-like factor 2 regulates thymocyte and T-cell migration.
**Authors:** Carlson CM, Endrizzi BT, Wu J, Ding X, Weinreich MA, Walsh ER, Wani MA, Lingrel JB, Hogquist KA, Jameson SC
**Journal:** Nature (2006)
**DOI:** [10.1038/nature04882](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature04882)

## Abstract

1. Nature. 2006 Jul 20;442(7100):299-302. doi: 10.1038/nature04882.

Kruppel-like factor 2 regulates thymocyte and T-cell migration.

Carlson CM(1), Endrizzi BT, Wu J, Ding X, Weinreich MA, Walsh ER, Wani MA,
Lingrel JB, Hogquist KA, Jameson SC.

Author information:
(1)Center for Immunology, Department of Laboratory Medicine and Pathology,
University of Minnesota Medical School, Minneapolis, Minnesota 55455, USA.

Mammalian Kruppel-like transcription factors are implicated in regulating
terminal differentiation of several tissue types. Deficiency in Kruppel-like
factor (KLF) 2 (also known as LKLF) leads to a massive loss of the peripheral
T-cell pool, suggesting KLF2 regulates T-cell quiescence and survival. Here we
show, however, that KLF2 is essential for T-cell trafficking. KLF2-deficient
(Klf2-/-) thymocytes show impaired expression of several receptors required for
thymocyte emigration and peripheral trafficking, including the
sphingosine-1-phosphate (S1P) receptor S1P1, CD62L and beta7 integrin.
Furthermore, KLF2 both binds and transactivates the promoter for S1P1--a
receptor that is critical for thymocyte egress and recirculation through
peripheral lymphoid organs. Our findings suggest that KLF2 serves to license
mature T cells for trafficking from the thymus and recirculation through
secondary lymphoid tissues.
