Ischemia reperfusion-induced acute kidney injury (IRI-AKI) is a prevalent
clinical complication among hospitalized patients with high morbidity and
mortality. Mitochondria, as crucial organelles for maintaining cellular
homeostasis, play a pivotal role in IRI-AKI pathogenesis. Currently, no
effective and definitive therapeutic targets have been established for IRI-AKI
treatment. Previous sequencing data analysis revealed significant upregulation
of Arfgef3 following renal ischemia-reperfusion injury, suggesting its potential
as a therapeutic target. This study demonstrates that Arfgef3 expression is
markedly elevated after ischemia reperfusion injury. Furthermore, Arfgef3
knockout (KO) significantly ameliorated renal injury induced by
ischemia-reperfusion in mice. In addition, Arfgef3 knockout effectively
attenuates renal inflammation (IL6, MCP1, TNF-α), oxidative stress and apoptosis
levels. In addition, Arfgef3 knockout substantially improves ischemia
reperfusion-induced mitochondrial abnormalities including mitochondrial
biogenesis markers and ATP production capacity. In conclusion, Arfgef3
participates in IRI-AKI pathogenesis by regulating mitochondrial function.
Arfgef3 knockout confers renal protection through improving mitochondrial
dysfunction, thereby reducing inflammatory response, oxidative stress and
apoptosis. These findings provide novel insights into IRI-AKI mechanisms and
highlight Arfgef3 as a potential therapeutic target.

Introduction

Acute kidney injury (AKI) is characterized by rapid renal function decline with high morbidity and mortality [1]. The Kidney Disease: Improving Global Outcomes (KDIGO) guidelines stratify AKI severity into three stages based on serum creatinine elevation level, or oliguria duration or severity. Notably, even at the earliest stage (i.e. Stage I), AKI is associated with significant mortality risk [2]. Ischemia-reperfusion kidney injury predominantly occurs in clinical settings such as kidney transplantation and hemorrhagic shock. Its primary pathophysiological process involves ischemic necrosis of renal tubules, making it one of the most common AKI etiologies [3].

Currently, no definitive therapeutic targets or approaches have been established for IRI-AKI. Nesovic Ostojic J et al. found that hyperbaric oxygen (HBO) alleviates IRI-AKI by modulating oxidative stress, DNA damage, heme oxygenase-1 (HO-1), and apoptosis [4,5]. Moreover, Zhang et al. demonstrated that remote ischemic preconditioning attenuates IRI-AKI by modulating the DUSP6/JNK signaling axis [6]. In addition, multiple substances (e.g. Selenium, Silibinin, MiR-20a-5p) have been investigated to reduce IRI-AKI by targeting inflammation and ferroptosis [7–9]. Despite extensive animal studies demonstrating the potential therapeutic efficacy of various interventions in IRI-AKI, no effective treatment has yet been established. Therefore, it is urgent to seek specific diagnosis markers and clinical treatment of IRI-AKI.

Arfgef3, also known as BIG3 and located on human chromosome 6q13, is a member of the BIG protein family. Similar to other members from this family, such as BIG1 and BIG2, Arfgef3 contains an A-kinase anchoring protein sequence and uniquely features a canonical PP1Cα-binding motif [10,11]. In estrogen receptor (ERα)-positive breast cancer, Arfgef3 expression is significantly upregulated, and this upregulation is strongly associated with poor prognosis [12]. Wade performed a stratified genome-wide association analysis conducted in bull terriers and identified Arfgef3 as a newly discovered gene potentially associated with the Bull Terrier height development [13]. However, no relevant studies on Arfgef3 in renal diseases have been conducted to date.

Maintaining the integrity and function of mitochondria, the primary organelles responsible for energy production, is crucial for cellular homeostasis. Mitochondrial dysfunction often results in abnormalities in energy metabolism, inflammation, or both; thus, structural or functional disruption of mitochondria is universally observed in AKIs due to varied etiologies [14]. Moreover, a study observed multiple mitochondrial lesions in the renal tubular epithelial cells (TECs) of ischemia reperfusion kidney injury mice [15]. Specific interventions to restore mitochondrial function may be promising therapeutic strategies for kidney injury treatment. However, the precise molecular mechanisms and targets remain inadequately understood.

Mitochondrial dysfunction is considered a key factor in the pathogenesis of IRI-AKI. Therefore, identifying mitochondrial dysfunction-related therapeutic targets is crucial for this condition. Public sequencing databases analysis revealed upregulated Arfgef3 expression following ischemia-reperfusion injury, yet its role in kidney diseases has not been investigated. We therefore hypothesize that Arfgef3 may contribute to IRI-AKI by modulating mitochondrial dysfunction. In this study, we found that Arfgef3 knockout ameliorates ischemia-reperfusion kidney injury in mice, including inflammatory responses, oxidative stress, apoptosis and mitochondrial dysfunction. These findings may provide novel insights into underlying mechanisms and treatment strategies for ischemia-reperfusion kidney injury.

Renal histopathological assessmentFor renal histopathological evaluation, kidney tissues from each experimental group were fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde and subsequently embedded in paraffin blocks. Tissue sections (3 μm thick) were prepared and subjected to hematoxylin and eosin (HE) and Periodic Acid–Schiff (PAS) staining. Stained sections were visualized under a Slideview VS200 (Olympus) and blindly evaluated by an independent board-certified pathologist. Renal tubular injury severity was quantitatively assessed on the basis of HE-stained and PAS-stained sections by using our predefined 5-point scoring system established previously [16]. The renal pathological score was evaluated based on morphological alterations, including loss of brush border in tubular epithelial cells, tubular dilatation, cast formation and cellular degeneration/necrosis. Tubular injury was scored in accordance with the following semi-quantitative grades: normal (score = 0), below 25% (score = 1), 25–49% (score = 2), 50–74% (score = 3), and ≥ 75% (score = 4) of the damaged areas. This histopathological analysis was systematically blinded across all experimental cohorts to ensure unbiased interpretation of tubular damage.
