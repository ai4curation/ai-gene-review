# Developmental analysis and squamous morphogenesis of the peripodial epithelium in Drosophila imaginal discs.

## Abstract

1. Development. 2005 Nov;132(22):5033-42. doi: 10.1242/dev.02092. Epub 2005 Oct

Developmental analysis and squamous morphogenesis of the peripodial epithelium
in Drosophila imaginal discs.

McClure KD(1), Schubiger G.

Author information:
(1)University of Washington, Department of Biology, Seattle, WA 98195, USA.

Imaginal discs of Drosophila provide an excellent system with which to study
morphogenesis, pattern formation and cell proliferation in an epithelium. Discs
are sac-like in structure and are composed of two epithelial layers: an upper
peripodial epithelium and lower disc proper. Although development of the disc
proper has been studied extensively in terms of cell proliferation, cell
signaling mechanisms and pattern formation, little is known about these same
processes in the peripodial epithelium. We address this topic by focusing on
morphogenesis, compartmental organization, proliferation and cell lineage of the
PE in wing, second thoracic leg (T2) and eye discs. We show that a subset of
peripodial cells in different imaginal discs undergo a cuboidal-to-squamous cell
shape change at distinct larval stages. We find that this shape change requires
both Hedgehog and Decapentapelagic, but not Wingless, signaling. Additionally,
squamous morphogenesis shifts the anteroposterior (AP) compartment boundary in
the peripodial epithelium relative to the stationary AP boundary in the disc
proper. Finally, by lineage tracing cells in the PE, we surprisingly find that
peripodial cells are displaced into the disc proper during larval development
and this movement leads to Ubx repression.
