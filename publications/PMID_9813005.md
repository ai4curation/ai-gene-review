# IRS pleckstrin homology domains bind to acidic motifs in proteins.
**Authors:** Burks DJ, Wang J, Towery H, Ishibashi O, Lowe D, Riedel H, White MF
**Journal:** J Biol Chem (1998)
**DOI:** [10.1074/jbc.273.47.31061](https://doi.org/10.1074/jbc.273.47.31061)

## Abstract

1. J Biol Chem. 1998 Nov 20;273(47):31061-7. doi: 10.1074/jbc.273.47.31061.

IRS pleckstrin homology domains bind to acidic motifs in proteins.

Burks DJ(1), Wang J, Towery H, Ishibashi O, Lowe D, Riedel H, White MF.

Author information:
(1)Howard Hughes Medical Institute, Joslin Diabetes Center, Harvard Medical
School, Boston, Massachusetts 02215, USA.

Using a yeast two-hybrid system, we identified several proteins that interact
with the PH domains in IRS-1 and IRS-2, including Lon protease, myeloblast
protein, and nucleolin. Although the roles of these molecules in insulin action
are not yet known, each protein contained an acidic motif that interacted with
the PH domain of IRS-2. However, only the acidic motif in nucleolin bound to
IRS-1, suggesting that the PH domain in IRS-1 and IRS-2 are not identical.
Moreover, synthetic peptides based on the acidic motif in Lon protease and
myeloblast protein inhibited the binding of nucleolin to the PH domain of IRS-2
but not to the PH domain of IRS-1, confirming the selectivity of these PH
domains. The ability to bind acidic motifs may be a specific function of the PH
domain in IRS proteins, because the PH domains in betaARK, phospholipase Cgamma,
or spectrin did not bind nucleolin. In 32D cells, nucleolin bound to both IRS-1
and IRS-2, and expression of the acidic motif of nucleolin inhibited
insulin-stimulated tyrosine phosphorylation of IRS-1 and IRS-2. These results
suggest that the binding of acidic motifs to the PH domain of IRS-1 and IRS-2
disrupts coupling to the activated insulin receptor. Our results are consistent
with the hypothesis that the PH domain in the IRS proteins may ordinarily bind
acidic peptide motifs in membrane proteins or other acidic membrane elements
that couple IRS proteins to activated membrane receptors.
