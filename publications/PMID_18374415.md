# Distribution of the partitioning protein KorB on the genome of IncP-1 plasmid RK2.
**Authors:** Chiu CM, Manzoor SE, Batt SM, Muntaha St, Bingle LE, Thomas CM
**Journal:** Plasmid (2008)
**DOI:** [10.1016/j.plasmid.2008.02.001](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.plasmid.2008.02.001)

## Abstract

1. Plasmid. 2008 May;59(3):163-75. doi: 10.1016/j.plasmid.2008.02.001.

Distribution of the partitioning protein KorB on the genome of IncP-1 plasmid
RK2.

Chiu CM(1), Manzoor SE, Batt SM, Muntaha St, Bingle LE, Thomas CM.

Author information:
(1)School of Biosciences, University of Birmingham, Edgbaston, Birmingham B15
2TT, UK.

The ParB family partitioning protein, KorB, of plasmid RK2 is central to a
regulatory network coordinating replication, maintenance and transfer genes.
Previous immunofluorescence microscopy indicated that the majority of KorB is
localized in plasmid foci. The 12 identified KorB binding sites on RK2 are
differentiated by: position relative to promoters; binding strength; and
cooperativity with other repressors and so the distribution of KorB may be
sequestered around a sub-set of sites. However, chromatin immunoprecipitation
analysis showed that while RK2 DNA molecules appear to sequester KorB to create
a higher local concentration, cooperativity between DNA binding proteins does
not result in major differences in binding site occupancy. Thus under steady
state conditions all operators are close to fully occupied and this correlates
with gene expression on the plasmid being highly repressed.
