# Ebola virus glycoprotein-mediated anoikis of primary human cardiac microvascular endothelial cells.
**Authors:** Ray RB, Basu A, Steele R, Beyene A, McHowat J, Meyer K, Ghosh AK, Ray R
**Journal:** Virology (2004)
**DOI:** [10.1016/j.virol.2003.12.014](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.virol.2003.12.014)

## Abstract

1. Virology. 2004 Apr 10;321(2):181-8. doi: 10.1016/j.virol.2003.12.014.

Ebola virus glycoprotein-mediated anoikis of primary human cardiac microvascular
endothelial cells.

Ray RB(1), Basu A, Steele R, Beyene A, McHowat J, Meyer K, Ghosh AK, Ray R.

Author information:
(1)Department of Pathology, Saint Louis University, St. Louis, MO 63110, USA.
rayrb@slu.edu

Ebola virus glycoprotein (EGP) has been implicated for the induction of
cytotoxicity and injury in vascular cells. On the other hand, EGP has also been
suggested to induce massive cell rounding and detachment from the plastic
surface by downregulating cell adhesion molecules without causing cytotoxicity.
In this study, we have examined the cytotoxic role of EGP in primary endothelial
cells by transduction with a replication-deficient recombinant adenovirus
expressing EGP (Ad-EGP). Primary human cardiac microvascular endothelial cells
(HCMECs) transduced with Ad-EGP displayed loss of cell adhesion from the plastic
surface followed by cell death. Transfer of conditioned medium from
EGP-transduced HCMEC into naive cells did not induce loss of adhesion or cell
death, suggesting that EGP needs to be expressed intracellularly to exert its
cytotoxic effect. Subsequent studies suggested that HCMEC death occurred through
apoptosis. Results from this study shed light on the EGP-induced anoikis in
primary human cardiac endothelial cells, which may have significant pathological
consequences.
