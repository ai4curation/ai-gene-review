Manganese (Mn2+) is extruded from the cell by the zinc transporter 10 (ZnT10).
Loss of ZnT10 expression caused by autosomal mutations in the ZnT10 gene leads
to hypermanganesemia in multiple organs. Here, combining fluorescent monitoring
of cation influx in HEK293-T cells expressing human ZnT10 with molecular
modeling of ZnT10 cation selectivity, we show that ZnT10 is exploiting the
transmembrane Ca2+ inward gradient for active cellular exchange of Mn2+ In
analyzing ZnT10 activity we used the ability of Fura-2 to spectrally distinguish
between Mn2+ and Ca2+ fluxes. We found that (a) application of Mn2+-containing
Ca2+-free solution to ZnT10-expressing cells triggers an influx of Mn2+, (b)
reintroduction of Ca2+ leads to cellular Mn2+ extrusion against an inward Mn2+
gradient, and (c) the cellular transport of Mn2+ by ZnT10 is coupled to a
reciprocal movement of Ca2+ Remarkably, replacing a single asparagine residue in
ZnT10 (Asp-43) with threonine (ZnT10 N43T) converted the Mn2+/Ca2+ exchange to
an uncoupled channel mode, permeable to both Ca2+ and Mn2+ The findings in our
study identify the first ion transporter that uses the Ca2+ gradient for active
counter-ion exchange. They highlight a remarkable versatility in metal
selectivity and mode of transport controlled by the tetrahedral metal transport
site of ZnT proteins.
