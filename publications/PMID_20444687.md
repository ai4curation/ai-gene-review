# Crystal structure of full-length Mycobacterium tuberculosis H37Rv glycogen branching enzyme: insights of N-terminal beta-sandwich in substrate specificity and enzymatic activity.

The open reading frame Rv1326c of Mycobacterium tuberculosis (Mtb) H37Rv encodes
for an alpha-1,4-glucan branching enzyme (MtbGlgB, EC 2.4.1.18, Uniprot entry
Q10625). This enzyme belongs to glycoside hydrolase (GH) family 13 and catalyzes
the branching of a linear glucose chain during glycogenesis by cleaving a 1-->4
bond and making a new 1-->6 bond. Here, we show the crystal structure of
full-length MtbGlgB (MtbGlgBWT) at 2.33-A resolution. MtbGlgBWT contains four
domains: N1 beta-sandwich, N2 beta-sandwich, a central (beta/alpha)(8) domain
that houses the catalytic site, and a C-terminal beta-sandwich. We have assayed
the amylase activity with amylose and starch as substrates and the glycogen
branching activity using amylose as a substrate for MtbGlgBWT and the N1
domain-deleted (the first 108 residues deleted) MtbDelta108GlgB protein. The N1
beta-sandwich, which is formed by the first 105 amino acids and superimposes
well with the N2 beta-sandwich, is shown to have an influence in substrate
binding in the amylase assay. Also, we have checked and shown that several GH13
family inhibitors are ineffective against MtbGlgBWT and MtbDelta108GlgB. We
propose a two-step reaction mechanism, for the amylase activity (1-->4 bond
breakage) and isomerization (1-->6 bond formation), which occurs in the same
catalytic pocket. The structural and functional properties of MtbGlgB and
MtbDelta108GlgB are compared with those of the N-terminal 112-amino acid-deleted
Escherichia coli GlgB (ECDelta112GlgB).
