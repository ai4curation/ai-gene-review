More than 20 years ago, Raf was discovered as a cellular oncogene transduced by
transforming retroviruses. Since then, the three Raf isoforms have been
intensively studied, mainly as the kinases linking Ras to the MEK/ERK signaling
module. As this pathway is activated in human cancer, the Raf kinases are
considered promising therapeutic targets, and we have learned a lot about their
regulation, targets, and functions. Do they still hold surprises? Recent gene
targeting studies indicate that they do. This review focuses on the regulation
and biology of the best-studied Raf isoform, Raf-1, in the context of its
kinase-independent functions.
