The mechanisms driving pathological beta-amyloid (Aβ) generation in late-onset
Alzheimer's disease (AD) are unclear. Two late-onset AD risk factors, Bin1 and
CD2AP, are regulators of endocytic trafficking, but it is unclear how their
endocytic function regulates Aβ generation in neurons. We identify a novel
neuron-specific polarisation of Aβ generation controlled by Bin1 and CD2AP We
discover that Bin1 and CD2AP control Aβ generation in axonal and dendritic early
endosomes, respectively. Both Bin1 loss of function and CD2AP loss of function
raise Aβ generation by increasing APP and BACE1 convergence in early endosomes,
however via distinct sorting events. When Bin1 levels are reduced, BACE1 is
trapped in tubules of early endosomes and fails to recycle in axons. When CD2AP
levels are reduced, APP is trapped at the limiting membrane of early endosomes
and fails to be sorted for degradation in dendrites. Hence, Bin1 and CD2AP keep
APP and BACE1 apart in early endosomes by distinct mechanisms in axon and
dendrites. Individuals carrying variants of either factor would slowly
accumulate Aβ in neurons increasing the risk for late-onset AD.
