# A new glucose-6-phosphate dehydrogenase variant, G6PD Orissa (44 Ala-->Gly), is the major polymorphic variant in tribal populations in India.
**Authors:** Kaeda JS, Chhotray GP, Ranjit MR, Bautista JM, Reddy PH, Stevens D, Naidu JM, Britt RP, Vulliamy TJ, Luzzatto L
**Journal:** Am J Hum Genet (1995)
**PMC:** [PMC1801405](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC1801405/)

## Abstract

1. Am J Hum Genet. 1995 Dec;57(6):1335-41.

A new glucose-6-phosphate dehydrogenase variant, G6PD Orissa (44 Ala-->Gly), is
the major polymorphic variant in tribal populations in India.

Kaeda JS(1), Chhotray GP, Ranjit MR, Bautista JM, Reddy PH, Stevens D, Naidu JM,
Britt RP, Vulliamy TJ, Luzzatto L, et al.

Author information:
(1)Department of Haematology, Royal Postgraduate Medical School, London, United
Kingdom.

Deficiency of glucose-6-phosphate dehydrogenase (G6PD) is usually found at high
frequencies in areas of the world where malaria has been endemic. The frequency
and genetic basis of G6PD deficiency have been studied in Africa, around the
Mediterranean, and in the Far East, but little such information is available
about the situation in India. To determine the extent of heterogeneity of G6PD,
we have studied several different Indian populations by screening for G6PD
deficiency, followed by molecular analysis of deficient alleles. The frequency
of G6PD deficiency varies between 3% and 15% in different tribal and urban
groups. Remarkably, a previously unreported deficient variant, G6PD Orissa (44
Ala-->Gly), is responsible for most of the G6PD deficiency in tribal Indian
populations but is not found in urban populations, where most of the G6PD
deficiency is due to the G6PD Mediterranean (188 Ser-->Phe) variant. The KmNADP
of G6PD Orissa is fivefold higher than that of the normal enzyme. This may be
due to the fact that the alanine residue that is replaced by glycine is part of
a putative coenzyme-binding site.
