# Interaction of E. coli single-stranded DNA binding protein (SSB) with exonuclease I. The carboxy-terminus of SSB is the recognition site for the nuclease.
**Authors:** Genschel J, Curth U, Urbanke C
**Journal:** Biol Chem (2000)
**DOI:** [10.1515/BC.2000.025](https://doi.org/10.1515/BC.2000.025)

## Abstract

1. Biol Chem. 2000 Mar;381(3):183-92. doi: 10.1515/BC.2000.025.

Interaction of E. coli single-stranded DNA binding protein (SSB) with
exonuclease I. The carboxy-terminus of SSB is the recognition site for the
nuclease.

Genschel J(1), Curth U, Urbanke C.

Author information:
(1)Medizinische Hochschule Hannover, Biophysikalische Chemie, Hannover, Germany.

The 3'-5' single-stranded DNA(ssDNA) degrading exonuclease I of E. coli directly
interacts with the E. coli ssDNA binding protein (EcoSSB). Analytical
ultracentrifugation shows that all 4 carboxy-termini of an EcoSSB tetramer bind
exonuclease I. Binding is weakened by increasing salt concentrations, indicating
the involvement of the negatively charged amino acids of the carboxy-terminus of
SSB. Mutant SSB proteins EcoSSBP176S (ssb-113) and EcoSSBF177C do not
bindtoexonuclease I while EcoSSBG15D (ssb-3) does bind. In a co-precipitation
assay we show that the absence of the lastten amino acids (PMDFDDDIPF)
completely abolishes binding of EcoSSB to exonuclease I. The interaction does
not depend on the presence of the correct amino-terminal DNA binding domain or
the amino acid sequences between the DNA binding domain and the last ten amino
acids. A synthetic peptide (WMDFDDDIPF), corresponding to the last nine amino
acids of EcoSSB, specifically inhibits the interaction. Both EcoSSBP176S and
EcoSSBF177C SSBs bind DNA similar to wild-type EcoSSB, indicating that the
phenotype of ssb-113 is not an indication of altered DNA binding. The repair
deficiency of either ssb-3 or ssb-113 strain can be complemented by
overexpression of the respective other mutant.
