Septins are polymerizing GTPases required for cytokinesis and cortical
organization. The principles by which they are targeted to, and assemble at,
specific cell regions are unknown. We show that septins in mammalian cells
switch between a linear organization along actin bundles and cytoplasmic rings,
approximately 0.6 microm in diameter. A recombinant septin complex
self-assembles into rings resembling those in cells. Linear organization along
actin bundles was reconstituted by adding an adaptor protein, anillin.
Perturbation of septin organization in cells by expression of a
septin-interacting fragment of anillin or by septin depletion via siRNA causes
loss of actin bundles. We conclude that septins alone self-assemble into rings,
that adaptor proteins recruit septins to actin bundles, and that septins help
organize these bundles.
