# Insight into the activation mechanism of Escherichia coli octaprenyl pyrophosphate synthase derived from pre-steady-state kinetic analysis.
**Authors:** Pan JJ, Kuo TH, Chen YK, Yang LW, Liang PH
**Journal:** Biochim Biophys Acta (2002)
**DOI:** [10.1016/s0167-4838(01)00283-7](https://doi.org/10.1016/s0167-4838(01)00283-7)

## Abstract

1. Biochim Biophys Acta. 2002 Jan 31;1594(1):64-73. doi:
10.1016/s0167-4838(01)00283-7.

Insight into the activation mechanism of Escherichia coli octaprenyl
pyrophosphate synthase derived from pre-steady-state kinetic analysis.

Pan JJ(1), Kuo TH, Chen YK, Yang LW, Liang PH.

Author information:
(1)Institute of Biological Chemistry, Academia Sinica, Taipei 11529, Taiwan.

Octaprenyl pyrophosphate synthase (OPPs) catalyzes the sequential condensation
of five molecules of isopentenyl pyrophosphate with farnesyl pyrophosphate to
generate all-trans C40-octaprenyl pyrophosphate, which constitutes the side
chain of ubiquinone. Due to the slow product release, a long-chain polyprenyl
pyrophosphate synthase often requires detergent or another factor for optimal
activity. Our previous studies in examining the activity enhancement of
Escherichia coli undecaprenyl pyrophosphate synthase have demonstrated a switch
of the rate-determining step from product release to isopentenyl pyrophosphate
(IPP) condensation reaction in the presence of Triton [12]. In order to
understand the mechanism of enzyme activation for E. coli OPPs, a
single-turnover reaction was performed and the measured IPP condensation rate (2
s(-1)) was 100 times larger than the steady-state rate (0.02 s(-1)). The high
molecular weight fractions and Triton could accelerate the steady-state rate by
3-fold (0.06 s(-1)) but insufficient to cause full activation (100-fold). A
burst product formation was observed in enzyme multiple turnovers indicating a
slow product release.
