# A conserved domain of herpes simplex virus ICP34.5 regulates protein phosphatase complex in mammalian cells.
**Authors:** Zhang C, Tang J, Xie J, Zhang H, Li Y, Zhang J, Verpooten D, He B, Cao Y
**Journal:** FEBS Lett (2008)
**DOI:** [10.1016/j.febslet.2007.11.082](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.febslet.2007.11.082)

## Abstract

1. FEBS Lett. 2008 Jan 23;582(2):171-6. doi: 10.1016/j.febslet.2007.11.082. Epub
2007 Dec 7.

A conserved domain of herpes simplex virus ICP34.5 regulates protein phosphatase
complex in mammalian cells.

Zhang C(1), Tang J, Xie J, Zhang H, Li Y, Zhang J, Verpooten D, He B, Cao Y.

Author information:
(1)College of Life Science, Nankai University, 94 Weijin Road, Tianjin 300071,

ICP34.5, encoded by herpes simplex virus 1, is a protein phosphatase 1 (PP1)
regulatory subunit that mediates dephosphorylation of the alpha subunit of
translation initiation factor 2 (eIF2alpha). However, the mechanism of its
action remains poorly understood. Here, we show that amino acid substitutions in
the arginine-rich motif have differential effects on ICP34.5 activity. The
phenotypes parallel with viral protein synthesis and cytopathic effects in virus
infected cells. Besides the consensus PP1 binding motif, the Arg-motif appears
to enhance the interaction between ICP34.5 and PP1. These results suggest that
concerted action between the PP1 binding domain and the effector domain of
ICP34.5 is crucial for eIF2alpha dephosphorylation and viral protein synthesis.
