# Transport and pharmacological properties of nine different human Na, K-ATPase isozymes.
**Authors:** Crambert G, Hasler U, Beggah AT, Yu C, Modyanov NN, Horisberger JD, Lelièvre L, Geering K
**Journal:** J Biol Chem (2000)
**DOI:** [10.1074/jbc.275.3.1976](https://doi.org/10.1074/jbc.275.3.1976)

## Abstract

1. J Biol Chem. 2000 Jan 21;275(3):1976-86. doi: 10.1074/jbc.275.3.1976.

Transport and pharmacological properties of nine different human Na, K-ATPase
isozymes.

Crambert G(1), Hasler U, Beggah AT, Yu C, Modyanov NN, Horisberger JD, Lelièvre
L, Geering K.

Author information:
(1)Institut de Pharmacologie et de Toxicologie de l'Université, Rue du Bugnon

Na,K-ATPase plays a crucial role in cellular ion homeostasis and is the
pharmacological receptor for digitalis in man. Nine different human Na,K-ATPase
isozymes, composed of 3 alpha and beta isoforms, were expressed in Xenopus
oocytes and were analyzed for their transport and pharmacological properties.
According to ouabain binding and K(+)-activated pump current measurements, all
human isozymes are functional but differ in their turnover rates depending on
the alpha isoform. On the other hand, variations in external K(+) activation are
determined by a cooperative interaction mechanism between alpha and beta
isoforms with alpha2-beta2 complexes having the lowest apparent K(+) affinity.
alpha Isoforms influence the apparent internal Na(+) affinity in the order
alpha1 > alpha2 > alpha3 and the voltage dependence in the order alpha2 > alpha1
> alpha3. All human Na,K-ATPase isozymes have a similar, high affinity for
ouabain. However, alpha2-beta isozymes exhibit more rapid ouabain association as
well as dissociation rate constants than alpha1-beta and alpha3-beta isozymes.
Finally, isoform-specific differences exist in the K(+)/ouabain antagonism which
may protect alpha1 but not alpha2 or alpha3 from digitalis inhibition at
physiological K(+) levels. In conclusion, our study reveals several new
functional characteristics of human Na,K-ATPase isozymes which help to better
understand their role in ion homeostasis in different tissues and in digitalis
action and toxicity.
