# Identification of VAV2 on 9q34 and its exclusion as the tuberous sclerosis gene TSC1.
**Authors:** Henske EP, Short MP, Jozwiak S, Bovey CM, Ramlakhan S, Haines JL, Kwiatkowski DJ
**Journal:** Ann Hum Genet (1995)
**DOI:** [10.1111/j.1469-1809.1995.tb01603.x](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1469-1809.1995.tb01603.x)

## Abstract

1. Ann Hum Genet. 1995 Jan;59(1):25-37. doi: 10.1111/j.1469-1809.1995.tb01603.x.

Identification of VAV2 on 9q34 and its exclusion as the tuberous sclerosis gene
TSC1.

Henske EP(1), Short MP, Jozwiak S, Bovey CM, Ramlakhan S, Haines JL, Kwiatkowski
DJ.

Author information:
(1)Division of Experimental Medicine, Brigham and Women's Hospital, Harvard
Medical School, Boston, MA 02115, USA.

A novel widely expressed homologue of the VAV oncogene, VAV2 (53% identical
residues), has been identified within the critical region for the tuberous
sclerosis gene, TSC1, on human chromosome 9q34. By Southern blot analysis,
analysis of allele-specific transcription, and direct sequencing of the VAV2
mRNA/cDNA from patient lymphoblastoid cell lines, we demonstrate that both
alleles of this gene are expressed in TSC patients and there are no significant
mutations. VAV consists of a novel array of signalling domains and is thought to
play an important role in signal transduction in haematopoietic tissues where it
is exclusively expressed. VAV2 is likely to serve a similar role more generally
in mammalian cells, but is not the TSC1 gene.
