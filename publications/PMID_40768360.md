The perception of the phytohormone gibberellin (GA) by its nuclear receptor
GIBBERELLIN INSENSITIVE DWARF1 (GID1) triggers polyubiquitination and
proteasomal degradation of master growth regulators-DELLA proteins-mediated by
the SCFSLY1/GID2 E3 ubiquitin ligase complex. DELLA-encoding genes are known as
'Green Revolution' genes, as their dominant mutations lead to semidwarf cereal
varieties with significantly higher yields due to reduced GA response. DELLAs
function as central signaling hubs, coordinating diverse physiological responses
by interacting with key transcription factors across multiple cellular pathways.
While the DELLA domain mediates GA-GID1 binding, the mechanism of SCFSLY1/GID2
recruitment remained unknown. Additionally, GA-GID1 binding can inhibit DELLA
protein activity independently of its proteolysis, although the underlying
mechanism was unclear. Here, we present the cryo-EM structures of GA3-GID1A
complexed with a full-length DELLA protein in Arabidopsis, RGA (REPRESSOR OF
ga1-3), and the GA3-GID1A-RGA-SLY1-ASK1 complex. We show that the DELLA domain
of RGA functions as a molecular bridge to enhance its GRAS domain binding to
GID1A through direct interactions with both the GRAS domain and GID1A.
Disrupting either intramolecular (DELLA-GRAS) or intermolecular (GRAS-GID1A)
interactions weakens RGA-GID1 binding. Contrary to prior models, SLY1 binds the
GRAS domain's concave surface without inducing conformational changes. Combining
AlphaFold modeling and yeast three-hybrid assays, we demonstrate that GID1
binding to the RGA GRAS domain blocks its interactions with INDETERMINATE DOMAIN
(IDD) transcription factors, explaining how GA-GID1 relieves growth suppression
independently of DELLA degradation.

## Full Text

Bioactive gibberellins (GAs) are diterpenoid phytohormones that regulate key processes in the plant life cycle, from seed germination to vegetative and reproductive development (1). GA activates its nuclear receptor, GIBBERELLIN INSENSITIVE DWARF1 (GID1) (2), leading to polyubiquitination and degradation of downstream repressors, DELLA proteins, via the 26S proteasome (3–5). This process is mediated by F-box proteins [GID2 in rice and SLEEPY1 (SLY1) in Arabidopsis], part of the Skp1-Cullin-F-box (SCF) E3 ubiquitin ligase complex (6, 7). DELLAs, a subfamily of the GRAS (for GAI, RGA, and SCARECROW) transcription regulators, lack a DNA-binding domain but act as master growth regulators by integrating multiple signaling pathways through protein–protein interactions (5, 8–11). They feature a conserved C-terminal GRAS domain and a unique N-terminal DELLA domain connected by a Poly-S/T linker (Fig. 1A) (12–14).

Biochemical and structural studies have clarified the mechanism of GA perception and DELLA recognition by the GA receptor GID1. GA promotes GID1 binding to DELLA proteins, with the DELLA domain essential for this interaction (2, 15–17). X-ray crystallography revealed 3D structures of the GA-GID1 and GA-GID1-DELLA domain complexes, showing that GA binding induces a conformational change in GID1, creating a hydrophobic surface for DELLA domain binding (18, 19). The intrinsically disordered DELLA domain converts to a stable conformation upon GID1 binding, with conserved motifs (DELLA, LExLE, VHYNP) directly contacting GID1.

In vitro pulldown assays, along with yeast two-hybrid (Y2H) and yeast three-hybrid (Y3H) experiments, suggested that DELLA proteins interact with SLY1/GID2 via their C-terminal GRAS domain (20, 21). This interaction is enhanced in the presence of GA-bound GID1 in Y3H assays (15, 21). Intriguingly, specific mutations in the GRAS domain of the rice DELLA protein SLR1 were shown to reduce SLR1 interaction with both GA-GID1 and the F-box protein GID2 (21), suggesting that the GRAS domain may also bind to GID1 to further stabilize the GA-GID1-DELLA complex. Taken together, these observations led to the proposal that GA-GID1 binding to the DELLA domain triggers a conformational change in the GRAS domain for GID1 binding, which then promotes recruitment of SCFSLY1/GID2 for the ubiquitination of DELLA proteins. However, the molecular details of this conformational transition in the GRAS domain to promote F-box protein binding remained elusive because existing structure analysis of the GA-GID1-DELLA domain complex did not contain a full-length DELLA protein (18). Previous studies also showed that the GA-GID1 binding can attenuate DELLA protein activity without degradation as observed in the sly1/gid2 mutant backgrounds, although the mechanism remained unclear (22, 23).

Here, we report cryo-EM structures of GA3-GID1A complexed with a full-length DELLA protein, RGA (REPRESSOR OF ga1-3), and their complex with SLY1 and ARABIDOPSIS SKP1 HOMOLOGUE 1 (ASK1) (the GA3-GID1A-RGA-SLY1-ASK1 complex). In contrast to the previous model of the allosteric change of the GRAS domain conformation, we found that the docking of the Arabidopsis F-box protein, SLY1, onto the RGA GRAS domain does not require a conformational change of the RGA GRAS domain. Combining AlphaFold3 modeling (24) with Y2H and Y3H analyses, we show that the DELLA domain of RGA functions as a molecular bridge to enhance the RGA GRAS domain-GID1A interaction. Additionally, GID1A binding to the GRAS domain blocks the RGA interaction with target transcription factors, IDDs, unveiling the molecular mechanism of GA-GID1-mediated inhibition of DELLA activity without its degradation.

ResultsCryo-EM Structure of Full-Length RGA in Complex with GA3-GID1A.Our previous X-ray crystallography structure analysis of the GA-GID1A-GAI DELLA domain complex did not contain the full-length DELLA protein GAI (for GA INSENSITIVE) (18). Arabidopsis has three GID1 paralogs, GID1A, GID1B, and GID1C, all of which function as GA receptors with partially overlapping roles at different stages of plant development (15, 25). As a first step toward a molecular understanding of how the Arabidopsis GA-receptor GID1 regulates DELLA protein function and degradation, we coexpressed and purified full-length RGA with GID1A in the presence of GA3. Although plants contain many gibberellin (GA) derivatives, most are biosynthetic intermediates or inactive catabolites, with only a few functioning as bioactive growth regulators (5). GA1 and GA4 are the major bioactive GA in plants, while GA3 (gibberellic acid) is more abundantly produced by fungi. Our prior structural studies indicated that GID1A adopts similar conformations when complexed with either GA3 or GA4 (18). Here, we used GA3 for all our structural analyses due to its availability for large-scale studies. To minimize proteolytic cleavage of RGA during purification, a cluster of three basic residues (K163Q/R164Q/K166Q) in the disordered Poly-S/T loop (P111-G203; Fig. 1A) connecting the DELLA and GRAS domains were mutated. The resulting GA3-GID1A-RGA (K163Q/R164Q/K166Q) complex was eluted as a stable heterodimer on size exclusion chromatography with a measured molecular weight of 106 kDa by mass photometry (SI Appendix, Fig. S1 A and B), matching the predicted molecular weight of 105 kDa. Cryo-EM analysis of this GA3-GID1A-RGA (K163Q/R164Q/K166Q) complex yielded a 3.06 Å resolution cryo-EM map (Fig. 1A and SI Appendix, Fig. S1 C and D and Table S1). In the remainder of this study, we will refer to this complex as the GA3-GID1A-RGA complex.The GA3-GID1A-RGA complex adopts a heart-shaped overall structure (Fig. 1A). One half of the heart contains the RGA DELLA domain positioned atop GID1A, and the other half consists of the RGA GRAS domain. The GA3-GID1A and RGA DELLA portions closely resemble the previously reported crystal structure of the GA3-GID1A in complex with the DELLA domain of another Arabidopsis DELLA protein GAI (18). In both complexes, GA3 is encapsulated between the GID1A α/β-hydrolase-fold core and its N-terminal extension of three helices (αA-αC in orange color in Fig. 1B), with the latter binding to the RGA or GAI DELLA domain that folds into a four-helix bundle (RGA αA-αD in pale blue color in Fig. 1B, GAI αA-αD in pink color in SI Appendix, Fig. S2A).Intramolecular Interaction Between the DELLA and GRAS Domains.Although similar features of RGA and GAI DELLA domains are observed, the αC helix of the RGA DELLA domain is longer and shifted in comparison with that of GAI DELLA in the crystal structure (SI Appendix, Fig. S2A). This difference is unlikely caused by the sequence variation between RGA and GAI, as the AlphaFold3 model of the GA3-GID1-full-length GAI complex predicts a similar conformation of the DELLA domain to that of the RGA cryo-EM structure (SI Appendix, Fig. S2A). Instead, the shifted and extended αC helix in RGA DELLA is likely due to the intramolecular interaction of the RGA DELLA domain with its GRAS domain. Indeed, we observed the DELLA-GRAS domain interactions between the amide group of DELLA H90 (located adjacent to the C-terminus of the αC helix) with the sidechain of S537 of GRAS and between the carbonyl of DELLA H90 with the amide of S537 in GRAS (Fig. 1C). Following these backbone hydrogen bonds, the sidechain of Y91 glides over G536 and forms a hydrogen bond through its terminal hydroxy group with E315 (Fig. 1C).Intermolecular Interaction Between GID1A and RGA GRAS Domain.Similar to other GRAS proteins (26, 27), the RGA GRAS domain consists of a six-helix bundle and an α/β core (Fig. 1B). Overlay of the RGA GRAS domain with the rice SCL7 GRAS domain reveals an overall similar fold, with an RMSD of 1.7 Å (SI Appendix, Fig. S2B). The GID1A-RGA GRAS interaction is predominantly mediated by the α/β core and a unique N-terminal loop preceding the six-helix bundle of RGA. The α/β core of RGA interacts with the αC helix of the GID1A N-terminal extension through both hydrophobic (between GID1A Y48 and RGA F540) and charge interactions (between GID1A R51 and RGA E560) (Fig. 1D).The second cluster of the interactions occurs around a unique N-terminal extended loop adjacent to the GRAS domain that is located at the conjunction of the Poly-S/T region and the GRAS domain, with a conserved pattern from residue 205-213 of xxxxϕϕϕϕ(d/e) only in the DELLA subfamily of GRAS proteins, where x stands for any amino acid, and ϕ for hydrophobic residues (SI Appendix, Fig. S2 B and C). This RGA loop packs against the six-helix bundle of RGA and forms contiguous backbone hydrogen bonds with the GID1A β1-strand and its preceding loop (Fig. 1B). These backbone hydrogen bonds are buttressed by a set of hydrophobic interactions between F66, V68, L69 of GID1A and V209, L211, and V212 of RGA GRAS, as well as a salt bridge between GID1A R172 and RGA GRAS D213 (Fig. 1E).Validation of DELLA-GRAS and GRAS-GID1A Binding Interfaces by Y3H Assays.Previous Y2H assays demonstrated that full-length DELLA proteins (Arabidopsis RGA and GAI, and rice SLR1) interact strongly with GID1 only in the presence of GA, with the DELLA domain being essential for this interaction (2, 15, 16, 21). The DELLA domain alone interacts with GA-bound GID1, although weaker than the full-length DELLA protein, whereas the GRAS domain alone does not show detectable binding to GID1 in Y2H assays. Our cryo-EM structure of the GA3-GID1A-RGA complex reveals that the DELLA and GRAS domains of RGA bind GID1A at distinct and nonoverlapping interfaces (Fig. 1). Given that these domains are connected by a long, disordered Poly-S/T loop that is invisible in the cryo-EM density map, we investigated whether the DELLA domain promotes GRAS domain binding to GID1A using split RGA fragments in yeast three-hybrid (Y3H) assays. We fused RGA-CT1 (107–587; Poly-S/T + GRAS domain) to the Gal4 DNA-binding) domain (BD) (bait) and RGA-NT1 (1–121; DELLA domain) to the Gal4 transactivation domain (AD) (prey) (Fig. 1F). Interaction strength was assessed by growth on –His media containing varying concentrations of 3-aminotriazole (3-AT, a competitive HIS3 inhibitor), with or without 100 µM GA3 and GID1A. Strikingly, a robust DELLA-GRAS interaction occurred only when both GA3 and GID1A were present (Fig. 1F and SI Appendix, Fig. S3). These results support that DELLA domain binding to GID1A promotes GRAS domain recruitment via DELLA-GRAS interaction, and that subsequent GRAS-GID1A binding further stabilizes the DELLA-GRAS-GID1A complex.The Y3H system featuring the split RGA enabled us to validate key residues for DELLA-GRAS and GRAS-GID1A interactions observed by the cryo-EM structural analysis. In this split Y3H assay, disruption of DELLA-GID1A, DELLA-GRAS, or GRAS-GID1A would destabilize the DELLA-GRAS-GID1A complex. Consistent with the structural data, three single mutations, Y91A, G536V, and S537A, disrupted the DELLA-GRAS interaction completely (Fig. 1F and SI Appendix, Fig. S3). Additionally, a single mutation E560K and a double mutation L211E/D213K in the GRAS domain abolished the GRAS-GID1A interaction, which was inferred by the lack of DELLA-GRAS interaction in this split Y3H assay (Fig. 1F and SI Appendix, Fig. S3). Taken together, our structural analysis and Y3H assay results support that the DELLA domain serves as a molecular bridge to bring GID1A and RGA GRAS together and that the effective GID1A binding to RGA requires intact interactions between the DELLA domain and GRAS domain as well as GID1A with the RGA GRAS domain. Supporting this notion, in rice the G576V mutation in SLR1 (corresponding to G536V in RGA) disrupts its binding to GID1 (21). The dominant sly1-d (aka gar2-1) mutation, corresponding to SLY1E138K, was previously shown to enhance DELLA protein binding in Y2H and in vitro pulldown assays (20, 28). This sly1-d mutation was also more efficient in promoting RGA degradation in planta (20, 28) and therefore was used in our cryo-EM analysis of the protein complex. Copurification of SLY1E138K-ASK1 with GA3-GID1A-RGA yielded a stable quaternary complex, as demonstrated by size-exclusion chromatography, with an experimentally measured molecular weight of 148 kDa by mass photometry (SI Appendix, Fig. S4 A and B), consistent with the predicted molecular weight of 147 kDa.Cryo-EM analysis of the quaternary GA3-GID1A-RGA-SLY1E138K-ASK1 complex yielded a map of 2.72 Å resolution (Fig. 2A and SI Appendix, Table S1). Together, these in planta results demonstrate that the DELLA domain facilitates GA-GID1-dependent GRAS domain degradation in transgenic Arabidopsis expressing split RGA.Degradation-Independent Inhibition of DELLA Activity by GA-GID1.As master regulators of the GA-response genes without any DNA binding domain, DELLA proteins, including Arabidopsis RGA and rice SLR1, directly interact with a variety of transcription factors and transcription regulators to suppress GA signaling (5, 8–11, 32). In the canonical GA regulation model, perception of GA by its receptor GID1 triggers DELLA polyubiquitination by the SCF complex and subsequent degradation by proteasomes, which are considered key events to relieve the repressive effect of DELLA in GA signaling. However, overexpression of GID1 in rice and Arabidopsis was shown to partially rescue the dwarf phenotype of the gid2 and sly1 mutants, respectively, indicating that the GID1 binding to DELLAs can directly interfere with DELLA function without proteolysis (22, 23).To gain insights into how the GA perception by GID1A negatively regulates RGA activity without its degradation, we investigated the RGA interactions with the INDETERMINATE DOMAIN (IDD) subfamily of C2H2 zinc finger (ZF) transcription factors that regulate root development, floral induction, and GA homeostasis (33, 34). IDDs have been shown to act as docking transcription factors, which recruit DELLA proteins to target promoters for transcription of DELLA-induced genes. DiscussionThe DELLA Domain Acts as a Molecular Bridge to Promote GRAS Domain Binding to GID1A.The DELLA domain is required for GA-mediated GID1-RGA interaction (15). Although it was initially thought that the DELLA domain alone contributes to the GID1-RGA binding (18), mutations in the GRAS domain of the rice DELLA protein SLR1 were found to weaken its interaction with GA-GID1 and the F-box protein GID2 (21). These observations suggest that, after the initial DELLA domain binding to GID1A, the GRAS domain may also interact with GID1 to further stabilize the GA-GID1-DELLA complex for the F-box protein binding. However, the direct GRAS-GID1 interaction had not been demonstrated. Interestingly, the DELLA domain and GRAS domain are connected by a long, disordered Poly-S/T linker, which is invisible in the cryo-EM density maps of the GA3-GID1A-RGA and GA3-GID1A-RGA-SLY1-ASK1 complexes, suggesting that this linker does not contribute to synergistic interactions of the RGA DELLA and GRAS domains with GID1A. Supporting this view, split RGA system containing separate DELLA and GRAS could fully reconstitute the GA responsiveness of RGA in planta (Fig. 3), indicating that the Poly-S/T linker is dispensable. Instead, our cryo-EM structure shows that DELLA domain, through its tight binding to GA3-bound GID1A, expands the GID1A interface as GID1A-DELLA and serves as a molecular bridge to enhance the GRAS domain binding. Accordingly, mutation of the DELLA-GRAS interfacial residues weakens the GID1A-RGA GRAS binding. Such GA-regulated enhancement of the GID1A in RGA GRAS binding might be required for function, as GID1A needs to bind efficiently to RGA GRAS in a GA-regulated manner to compete with many transcription factors and regulators that occupy the same binding surface of RGA GRAS as GID1A.RGA GRAS-SLY1 Interaction Interface.SLY1 does not contain the typical protein–protein interaction domain, e.g., leucine-rich (LRR), Kelch and WD40 repeats, commonly found in the C terminus of F-box proteins (36). Its C-terminal region, containing the conserved GGF and LSL motifs (residue 98–100 and 129–131 respectively in Arabidopsis SLY1; SI Appendix, Fig. S6), is required for binding of DELLA proteins (6, 20, 37). Our structural analysis shows that the GGF motif likely has a structural role to maintain the folding of the SLY1 C-terminal helices, whereas the LSL motif is located at the center of the α7 helix and directly engages in the interaction with the RGA GRAS domain.Recently, Ji et al. (37) identified several additional amino acid substitutions in SLY1 that further enhance DELLA protein binding. While SLY1 K126 interacts with Q311 of RGA, and its K126R mutation may enhance this interaction, the remaining residues (H104Y, P114L/S, or T128A) are not directly engaged in the SLY1-RGA GRAS binding. However, these residues may contribute indirectly through enhancing the folding stability of SLY1 or promoting the SLY1-ASK1 interaction. For example, T128A removes a buried hydroxyl group, and alanine is a well-known helix stabilizer; both factors may contribute to enhanced folding and packing of the RGA GRAS-binding α7 helix of SLY1 in the C-terminal four-helix bundle of SLY1.Although most of gain-of-function GA-unresponsive mutations in DELLA proteins are located in the DELLA domain, a rare dominant mutation was found in the GRAS domain of DELLA proteins that disrupt GA signaling. In Arabidopsis, the Q341R mutation in RGA (equivalent to Q328R in Brrga1-d in Brassica rapa) abolishes interaction with SLY1 and sly1-d, in Y2H assays (38). This mutation confers a GA-resistant, dominant dwarf phenotype in both species. Our structural analysis reveals that Q341R in RGA disrupts a critical hydrogen bond with S130 of SLY1 and may introduce charge repulsion with K126 (Fig. 2D). Previous Ala scanning mutagenesis in SLR1 also identified SLR1 HFT327-329 (equivalent to RGA HFT306-308) to be important for F-box protein GID2 binding (21), consistent with our structural data showing that F307 in RGA GRAS interacts with hydrophobic residues L127, L131, and I134 in SLY1 (Fig. 2D).Possible Mechanism for Promoting SLY1-RGA GRAS Interaction by GA-GID1.A central role of GA signaling is the destruction of RGA through the SCFSLY1 complex. It was speculated that GA-binding by GID1 induces the folding of an otherwise disordered DELLA domain, which then docks on the GA-bound receptor GID1, and in turn triggers a conformational switch of GRAS, leading to enhanced binding of RGA toward its F-box adaptor SLY1. Our cryo-EM structure of the GA3-GID1A-RGA-SLY1-ASK1 complex shows that although the binding of DELLA domain to GID1A expands the binding surface of GID1A (as GID1A-DELLA) toward GRAS for enhanced GID1-GRAS interaction, SLY1 binds exclusively to the RGA GRAS domain and has no direct interaction with GID1A or DELLA.Given the conserved architecture of the RGA GRAS domain and its lack of conformational flexibility like other GRAS domain proteins [e.g., Arabidopsis SCARECROW (SCR) and SHORT ROOT (SHR) (26), and rice SCL7 (27)], it raises an intriguing question: How does GID1-GA binding to RGA enhance the RGA GRAS-SLY1 interaction in the absence of any conformational change? We speculate that the conformational change may indeed happen, but not through the rigid GRAS domain, but instead from the “disordered” DELLA domain. ConclusionOur structural and in planta studies establish that the DELLA domain in RGA acts as a molecular bridge, enhancing the interaction between the RGA GRAS domain and GID1A. Using split-RGA systems in both yeast and plants, we demonstrate the functional cooperativity between the DELLA and GRAS domains in mediating GA signaling. Our structural analysis precisely defines the GRAS-SLY1 interaction interface, elucidating the molecular mechanism of SCFSLY1/GID2 recruitment after initial GA-GID1-DELLA recognition. Furthermore, we show that GA-GID1A binding to RGA attenuates its activity by competitively blocking RGA’s interactions with transcription factors such as IDDs. Together, these findings provide a structural framework for understanding both proteolysis-dependent (through SCFSLY1/GID2 recruitment) and -independent (through competitive transcription factor displacement) mechanisms of DELLA activity regulation by GA-GID1 (Fig. 4D).

Data Processing and Model Building.Data processing for the GA3-GID1A-RGA dataset is summarized in SI Appendix, Fig. S1C and follows the general procedures as described previously (44). Briefly, movie alignment and contrast transfer function (CTF) estimation were performed with the patch motion correction and patch CTF estimation modules in cryoSPARC (45). 6,722 micrographs were selected from a total of 11,558 images based on the CTF fitting resolution with a cutoff value of 4.0 Å. Initial 2D classes were generated from Blob picker of a small set of 965 micrographs. The curated particles were then used for TOPAZ training and TOPAZ particle extraction (46), yielding 2,940,603 particles. After 2D classification, a set of 2,333,357 particles were selected for ab initio reconstruction and heterogeneous refinement containing three classes. The class containing high-resolution features was selected for homogeneous refinement. Particles with preferred orientations were rebalanced using the Balance Orientation module, and the resulting particles were polished with the Reference Based Motion Correction module and used for the final non-uniform refinement, yielding a cryo-EM map of 3.06 Å resolution. The map was sharpened by postprocessing in DeepEMhancer-0.16 (47). Overall composite maps were generated from local refinement maps and non-uniform refinement maps using Phenix (48).
