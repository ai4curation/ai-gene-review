# The catabolism of amino acids to long chain and complex alcohols in Saccharomyces cerevisiae.
**Authors:** Dickinson JR, Salgado LE, Hewlins MJ
**Journal:** J Biol Chem (2003)
**DOI:** [10.1074/jbc.M211914200](https://doi.org/10.1074/jbc.M211914200)

## Abstract

1. J Biol Chem. 2003 Mar 7;278(10):8028-34. doi: 10.1074/jbc.M211914200. Epub
2002  Dec 23.

The catabolism of amino acids to long chain and complex alcohols in
Saccharomyces cerevisiae.

Dickinson JR(1), Salgado LE, Hewlins MJ.

Author information:
(1)Cardiff School of Biosciences, Cardiff University, United Kingdom.
Dickinson@cardiff.ac.uk

The catabolism of phenylalanine to 2-phenylethanol and of tryptophan to
tryptophol were studied by (13)C NMR spectroscopy and gas chromatography-mass
spectrometry. Phenylalanine and tryptophan are first deaminated (to
3-phenylpyruvate and 3-indolepyruvate, respectively) and then decarboxylated.
This decarboxylation can be effected by any of Pdc1p, Pdc5p, Pdc6p, or Ydr380wp;
Ydl080cp has no role in the catabolism of either amino acid. We also report that
in leucine catabolism Ydr380wp is the minor decarboxylase. Hence, all amino acid
catabolic pathways studied to date use a subtly different spectrum of
decarboxylases from the five-membered family that comprises Pdc1p, Pdc5p, Pdc6p,
Ydl080cp, and Ydr380wp. Using strains containing all possible combinations of
mutations affecting the seven AAD genes (putative aryl alcohol dehydrogenases),
five ADH genes, and SFA1, showed that the final step of amino acid catabolism
(conversion of an aldehyde to a long chain or complex alcohol) can be
accomplished by any one of the ethanol dehydrogenases (Adh1p, Adh2p, Adh3p,
Adh4p, Adh5p) or by Sfa1p (formaldehyde dehydrogenase.)
