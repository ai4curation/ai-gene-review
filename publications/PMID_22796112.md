# Acyl-CoA binding domain containing 3 (ACBD3) recruits the protein phosphatase PPM1L to ER-Golgi membrane contact sites.
**Authors:** Shinoda Y, Fujita K, Saito S, Matsui H, Kanto Y, Nagaura Y, Fukunaga K, Tamura S, Kobayashi T
**Journal:** FEBS Lett (2012)
**DOI:** [10.1016/j.febslet.2012.06.050](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.febslet.2012.06.050)

## Abstract

1. FEBS Lett. 2012 Sep 21;586(19):3024-9. doi: 10.1016/j.febslet.2012.06.050.
Epub  2012 Jul 10.

Acyl-CoA binding domain containing 3 (ACBD3) recruits the protein phosphatase 
PPM1L to ER-Golgi membrane contact sites.

Shinoda Y(1), Fujita K, Saito S, Matsui H, Kanto Y, Nagaura Y, Fukunaga K, 
Tamura S, Kobayashi T.

Author information:
(1)Department of Biochemistry, Institute of Development, Aging and Cancer, 
Tohoku University, Japan.

The metal-dependent protein phosphatase family (PPM) governs a number of 
signaling pathways. PPM1L, originally identified as a negative regulator of 
stress-activated protein kinase signaling, was recently shown to be involved in 
the regulation of ceramide trafficking at ER-Golgi membrane contact sites. Here, 
we identified acyl-CoA binding domain containing 3 (ACBD3) as an interacting 
partner of PPM1L. We showed that this association, which recruits PPM1L to 
ER-Golgi membrane contact sites, is mediated by a GOLD (Golgi dynamics) domain 
in ACBD3. These results suggested that ACBD3 plays a pivotal role in ceramide 
transport regulation at the ER-Golgi interface.
