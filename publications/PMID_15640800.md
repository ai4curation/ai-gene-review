# An aspartic acid repeat polymorphism in asporin inhibits chondrogenesis and increases susceptibility to osteoarthritis.
**Authors:** Kizawa H, Kou I, Iida A, Sudo A, Miyamoto Y, Fukuda A, Mabuchi A, Kotani A, Kawakami A, Yamamoto S, Uchida A, Nakamura K, Notoya K, Nakamura Y, Ikegawa S

**DOI:** [10.1038/ng1496](https://doi.org/10.1038/ng1496)

## Abstract

1. Nat Genet. 2005 Feb;37(2):138-44. doi: 10.1038/ng1496. Epub 2005 Jan 9.

An aspartic acid repeat polymorphism in asporin inhibits chondrogenesis and
increases susceptibility to osteoarthritis.

Kizawa H(1), Kou I, Iida A, Sudo A, Miyamoto Y, Fukuda A, Mabuchi A, Kotani A,
Kawakami A, Yamamoto S, Uchida A, Nakamura K, Notoya K, Nakamura Y, Ikegawa S.

Osteoarthritis is the most common form of human arthritis. We investigated the
potential role of asporin, an extracellular matrix component expressed
abundantly in the articular cartilage of individuals with osteoarthritis, in the
pathogenesis of osteoarthritis. Here we report a significant association between
a polymorphism in the aspartic acid (D) repeat of the gene encoding asporin
(ASPN) and osteoarthritis. In two independent populations of individuals with
knee osteoarthritis, the D14 allele of ASPN is over-represented relative to the
common D13 allele, and its frequency increases with disease severity. The D14
allele is also over-represented in individuals with hip osteoarthritis. Asporin
suppresses TGF-beta-mediated expression of the genes aggrecan (AGC1) and type II
collagen (COL2A1) and reduced proteoglycan accumulation in an in vitro model of
chondrogenesis. The effect on TGF-beta activity is allele-specific, with the D14
allele resulting in greater inhibition than other alleles. In vitro binding
assays showed a direct interaction between asporin and TGF-beta. Taken together,
these findings provide another functional link between extracellular matrix
proteins, TGF-beta activity and disease, suggesting new therapeutic strategies
for osteoarthritis.
