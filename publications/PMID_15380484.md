# Transport kinetics of FMRP containing the I304N mutation of severe fragile X syndrome in neurites of living rat PC12 cells.
**Authors:** Schrier M, Severijnen LA, Reis S, Rife M, van't Padje S, van Cappellen G, Oostra BA, Willemsen R
**Journal:** Exp Neurol (2004)
**DOI:** [10.1016/j.expneurol.2004.05.039](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.expneurol.2004.05.039)

## Abstract

1. Exp Neurol. 2004 Oct;189(2):343-53. doi: 10.1016/j.expneurol.2004.05.039.

Transport kinetics of FMRP containing the I304N mutation of severe fragile X
syndrome in neurites of living rat PC12 cells.

Schrier M(1), Severijnen LA, Reis S, Rife M, van't Padje S, van Cappellen G,
Oostra BA, Willemsen R.

Author information:
(1)Department of Clinical Genetics, Erasmus MC, Rotterdam, The Netherlands.

Lack of fragile X mental retardation protein (FMRP) causes the fragile X
syndrome, a common form of inherited mental retardation. The syndrome usually
results from the expansion of a CGG repeat in the FMR1 gene with consequent
transcriptional silencing of FMR1. However, one missense mutation (Ile304Asn)
was reported in the second KH domain of the protein involved in RNA binding. The
protein containing this mutation showed an impaired function, leading to an
extremely severe phenotype. In the present report, we have studied the role of
FMRP I304N in living PC12 cells to better understand the (dys) function of this
mutant FMRP. We have generated an FMR1 I304N-EGFP stably transfected PC12 cell
line with an inducible expression system (Tet-On) for regulated expression of
the FMRP I304N-EGFP fusion protein. After Dox-induction, FMRP I304N-EGFP was
localized in the neurites of PC12 cells; however, no granules were formed as has
been recently demonstrated for the normal FMRP. Time-lapse microscopy in
combination with bleaching technology illustrated that although FMRP I304N-EGFP
does not form visible granules, the transport into the neurites is microtubule
dependent. Immunoprecipitation with antibodies against GFP demonstrates that
FMRP I304N-EGFP coprecipitate with both the 60S ribosomal protein P0 and FXR1P,
suggesting that the mutant FMRP is still able to form complexes, however, with
different characteristics compared to normal FMRP.
