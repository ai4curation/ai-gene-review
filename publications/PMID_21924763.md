Alternative splicing (AS) is a key process underlying the expansion of proteomic
diversity and the regulation of gene expression. Here, we identify an
evolutionarily conserved embryonic stem cell (ESC)-specific AS event that
changes the DNA-binding preference of the forkhead family transcription factor
FOXP1. We show that the ESC-specific isoform of FOXP1 stimulates the expression
of transcription factor genes required for pluripotency, including OCT4, NANOG,
NR5A2, and GDF3, while concomitantly repressing genes required for ESC
differentiation. This isoform also promotes the maintenance of ESC pluripotency
and contributes to efficient reprogramming of somatic cells into induced
pluripotent stem cells. These results reveal a pivotal role for an AS event in
the regulation of pluripotency through the control of critical ESC-specific
transcriptional programs.
