# Differentially regulated malate synthase genes participate in carbon and nitrogen metabolism of S. cerevisiae.
**Authors:** Hartig A, Simon MM, Schuster T, Daugherty JR, Yoo HS, Cooper TG
**Journal:** Nucleic Acids Res (1992)
**DOI:** [10.1093/nar/20.21.5677](https://doi.org/10.1093/nar/20.21.5677)
**PMC:** [PMC334402](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC334402/)

## Abstract

1. Nucleic Acids Res. 1992 Nov 11;20(21):5677-86. doi: 10.1093/nar/20.21.5677.

Differentially regulated malate synthase genes participate in carbon and
nitrogen metabolism of S. cerevisiae.

Hartig A(1), Simon MM, Schuster T, Daugherty JR, Yoo HS, Cooper TG.

Author information:
(1)Institut fuer Allgemeine Biochemie der Universitaet, Wien, Austria.

We have isolated a second gene (MLS1), which in addition to DAL7, encodes malate
synthase from S. cerevisiae. Expression of the two genes is specific for their
physiological roles in carbon and nitrogen metabolism. Expression of MLS1, which
participates in the utilization of non-fermentable carbon sources, is sensitive
to carbon catabolite repression, but nearly insensitive to nitrogen catabolite
repression. DAL7, which participates in catabolism of the nitrogenous compound
allantoin, is insensitive to carbon catabolite repression, but highly sensitive
to nitrogen catabolite repression. Results obtained with null mutations in these
genes suggest that S. cerevisiae contains at least one and perhaps two
additional malate synthase genes.
