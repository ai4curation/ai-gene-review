The cardiac conduction system (CCS) consists of distinctive components that
initiate and conduct the electrical impulse required for the coordinated
contraction of the cardiac chambers. CCS development involves complex regulatory
networks that act in stage-, tissue- and dose-dependent manners, and recent
findings indicate that the activity of these networks is sensitive to common
genetic variants associated with cardiac arrhythmias. Here, we review how these
findings have provided novel insights into the regulatory mechanisms and
transcriptional networks underlying CCS formation and function.
