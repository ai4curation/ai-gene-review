# Cloning, overproduction, and purification of native and mutant recombinant yeast orotate phosphoribosyltransferase and the demonstration from magnetization inversion transfer that a proposed oxocarbocation intermediate does not have a kinetic lifetime.
**Authors:** Witte JF, Tsou R, McClard RW
**Journal:** Arch Biochem Biophys (1999)
**DOI:** [10.1006/abbi.1998.0971](https://doi.org/10.1006/abbi.1998.0971)

## Abstract

1. Arch Biochem Biophys. 1999 Jan 1;361(1):106-12. doi: 10.1006/abbi.1998.0971.

Cloning, overproduction, and purification of native and mutant recombinant yeast
orotate phosphoribosyltransferase and the demonstration from magnetization
inversion transfer that a proposed oxocarbocation intermediate does not have a
kinetic lifetime.

Witte JF(1), Tsou R, McClard RW.

Author information:
(1)Arthur F. Scott Laboratory of Chemistry, Reed College, Portland, Oregon,
97202-8199, USA.

The gene for orotate phosphoribosyltransferase from Saccharomyces cerevisiae has
been subcloned into an Escherichia coli overexpression vector and the enzyme has
been produced in large quantities, thus simplifying the purification to one
step. We were able to repeat the published (J. Victor, L. B. Greenberg, and D.
L. Sloan J. Biol. Chem. 254, 2647-2655, 1979). 32PPi/5-phosphorylribose
1-alpha-diphosphate exchange experiments and could demonstrate the exchange by
magnetization inversion transfer NMR experiments as well. However, when
contaminating orotidine 5'-monophosphate (OMP) was eliminated with OMP
decarboxylase, any evidence of magnetization transfer vanished. Consequently, it
is concluded that a ping pong mechanism is not operable and that a previously
proposed oxocarbocation intermediate along the pathway to OMP does not persist
long enough in the catalytic cycle of this enzyme to be recognized by NMR
exchange experiments.
