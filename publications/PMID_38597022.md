ALG3-CDG is a rare congenital disorder of glycosylation (CDG) with a clinical
phenotype that includes neurological manifestations, transaminitis, and frequent
infections. The ALG3 enzyme catalyzes the first step of endoplasmic reticulum
(ER) luminal glycan extension by adding mannose from Dol-P-Man to
Dol-PP-Man5GlcNAc2 (Man5) forming Dol-PP-Man6. Such glycan extension is the
first and fastest cellular response to ER stress, which is deficient in
ALG3-CDG. In this study, we provide evidence that the unfolded protein response
(UPR) and ER-associated degradation activities are increased in ALG3-CDG
patient-derived cultured skin fibroblasts and there is constitutive activation
of UPR mediated by the IRE1-α pathway. In addition, we show that N-linked Man3-4
glycans are increased in cellular glycoproteins and secreted plasma
glycoproteins with hepatic or non-hepatic origin. We found that like other CDGs
such as ALG1- or PMM2-CDG, in transferrin, the assembling intermediate Man5 in
ALG3-CDG, are likely further processed into a distinct glycan,
NeuAc1Gal1GlcNAc1Man3GlcNAc2, probably by Golgi mannosidases and
glycosyltransferases. We predict it to be a mono-antennary glycan with the same
molecular weight as the truncated glycan described in MGAT2-CDG. In summary,
this study elucidates multiple previously unrecognized biochemical consequences
of the glycan extension deficiency in ALG3-CDG which will have important
implications in the pathogenesis of CDG.

Introduction

ALG3-CDG, formerly CDG-Id, is one of the less common type-1 congenital disorders of glycosylation (CDG), with the defect in the Endoplasmic Reticulum (ER) resident alpha-1,3- mannosyltransferase (ALG3) 1,2. The ALG3 enzyme (MIM #601110) catalyzes the transfer of mannose from Dol-P-Man to the lipid linked oligosaccharide (LLO) Dol-PP Man5GlcNAc2 (Man5) to form LLO-Man6, which is the first ER luminal-facing extension from LLO-Man5 to LLO-Man9 prior to glucosylation and transferring to newly synthesized proteins in the ER3. Accordingly, ALG3 deficiency results in the accumulation of LLO-Man5 intermediate and subsequent reduction of the LLO-Man9 pool in the ER 2,4,5. Defects in ALG3 gene have been associated with the abnormal protein N-glycosylation and are predicted to increase protein misfolding 3,5–7

The glycan extension from LLO-Man5 to LLO-Man9 is an integral part of protein folding quality control involving three ER luminal mannosyltransferases (ALG3, ALG9, and ALG12) and it is the first and fastest unfolded protein response (UPR) mechanism triggered by increased protein misfolding and ER stress 4,8
9. Both LLO-Man5 and -Man9 can be glucosylated with three glucoses and transferred efficiently to nascent proteins 2,10–12, although the former is a minor and presumably conditional pathway, and little is known about the process downstream of N-linked Glc3Man5, or the physiological consequences. The α1,2-mannose residues on nascent proteins are often trimmed by ER or Golgi type 1 mannosidases, during the protein folding quality control. Recently, EDEM3-CDG was identified and loss of the EDEM3 leads to blocked processing of Man5 to Man4 on nascent proteins and Glc1Man5 accumulated 13. With the LLO glycan extension reduced in ALG3-CDG, we previously reported small N-linked high mannoses, Man0-4, accumulated in plasma glycoproteins from 4 affected individuals 2,4. Thus, we suspect that this minor glycosylation pathway from LLO-Man5 may be amplified in ALG3-CDG.

In Golgi, to form a mature complex N-glycan, all 1,2-mannoses are removed before the first GlcNAc is added by GlcNAc transferase-1 (GNT1 encoded by MGAT1) 8,12,13. However, GNT1 requires 1,3-mannose and 1,6 mannose motifs on branch B and C to dock/function on the processed/trimmed Man514. These two motifs are reduced or missing when glycan extension is deficient in ALG3-CDG. In ALG3 deficient plant cells, the glycosylation process in the Golgi is indeed also truncated and produce N-glycans with only one antenna presumably through GNT26

In this study, we describe the biochemical consequences of deficient glycan extension in ALG3-CDG and its impact on N-glycan structures in cellular proteins and secreted mature glycoproteins from human plasma. First, we performed N-glycan profiling on cellular proteins from ALG3-CDG patient-derived cultured skin fibroblasts in combination with differential mannosidase digestion using matrix-assisted laser desorption ionization time-of-flight (MALDI-QTOF) mass spectrometry. Then, we semi-quantitatively profiled secreted proteins (extracted from total plasma or three separate plasma protein fractions) using a clinically validated isotope dilution method by electrospray ionization time-of-flight mass spectrometry (ESI-QTOF MS) to test whether the increased Man0-Man4 detected in plasma is from changes in glycoprotein concentration or glycosylation processes. Secondly, to evaluate ER stress, we measured the expression of multiple UPR target genes. We also measured ER associated degradation (ERAD) activities in ALG3-CDG fibroblast cells using a deglycosylation-dependent venus (ddVenus) fluorescence reporter assay 15. In addition, we showed that like what we previously reported in transferrin (Tf) from PMM2- or ALG1-CDG patients which the glycan assembling intermediate, GlcNAc2 (Man0), was processed to NeuAc1Gal1GlcNAc2 by Golgi glycosyltransferases 16, a distinct mono-antennary glycan, NeuAc1Gal1GlcNAc1Man3GlcNAc2 (M-antenna) is also present in Tf from ALG3-CDG, presumably derived from the assembling intermediate Man5 indicating the heterogeneity of the alternative glycosylation pathways in human.

Plasma glycoprotein fractionationFifteen µL of plasma was separated into three fractions: purified Tf , IgGs and the remaining protein fraction as described before 19 Briefly, Protein G column (Millipore-Sigma), IgG & HSA depletion column (Pierce-Thermo Fisher), and a rabbit anti-human Tf affinity column (anti-sera obtained from Cedarlane) were used to remove albumin, to extract purified Tf, IgGs and the remaining plasma protein fraction with negligible contamination. Plasma glycoprotein fractionation

Fifteen µL of plasma was separated into three fractions: purified Tf , IgGs and the remaining protein fraction as described before 19 Briefly, Protein G column (Millipore-Sigma), IgG & HSA depletion column (Pierce-Thermo Fisher), and a rabbit anti-human Tf affinity column (anti-sera obtained from Cedarlane) were used to remove albumin, to extract purified Tf, IgGs and the remaining plasma protein fraction with negligible contamination. Quantification of N-glycans by mass spectrometryWe assessed N-glycans released from total plasma and fractionated plasma glycoproteins, using a semi-quantitative method as described before 19,20. Complete N-linked glycans were released by PNGase F digestion, derivatized with RapiFluor-MS label, and extracted using a HILIC plate. Mass spectrometric (MS) analysis by flow injection was performed on the Waters’ Synapt G2Si QTOF in positive ion mode as previously described 20. The glycans were identified by accurate mass and the concentration of each glycan were calculated using a semi-quantitative approach by comparing the peak area of each glycan against a 13C-labeled disialo-glycan released from internal standard. Reference ranges of N-glycan abundance in total plasma glycoproteins were collected using 115 normal/non-CDG control plasma from different age groups (a collection of control material in our clinical lab); and normal ranges of N-glycan abundance in fractionated plasma glycoproteins was obtained from 60 normal/non-CDG controls. These small high mannose glycans are newly described glycosylation biomarkers for CDG 20. Here, we unequivocally demonstrated that small high mannose N-glycans, Man3 and Man4 present in secreted mature glycoproteins from ALG3-CDG patient blood with both hepatic and extra-hepatic origin and is abundant in cultured skin fibroblasts from a severely affected patient.

Fractionated plasma N-glycan profiling identifies that, although the mature primary glycan structures are not reduced in all 9 ALG3-CDG plasma, N-linked Man3 and Man4 glycans are increased in all samples. They are significantly increased in Tf, IgG, and the remaining protein fractions. Interestingly, Man9 and Hybrid1 are reduced in the remaining protein fraction from all 9 ALG3-CDG patients, mirroring the changes in total plasma proteins. Importantly, this study shows that even though LLO-Man5 is known to be abundant due to glycan extension deficiency in ALG3-CDG, the cellular and secreted glycoproteins (Tf, IgG) do not have total N-linked Man5 level increased, in contrast, N-linked total Man5 in the remaining plasma glycoprotein fraction is reduced (Figure 1D, Supplemental Table 1A–C). Thus, despite LLO analysis, the increases of N-linked Man3 and 4 glycan intermediates are diagnostic markers for ALG3-CDG patients (Figure 4). We predict that in the remaining fraction, most of the N-linked total Man5 in human plasma glycoproteins are derived from of the processed/trimmed Man5, thus the total N-linked Man5 is reduced in the ALG3-CDG plasma fraction depleted with Tf and IgG. In contrast, N-linked Man3 and Man4 are probably from glycan assembling intermediate Man5 that is more directly associated with LLO-Man5, therefore the increase of N-Man3 and Man4 on glycoproteins represents a key biochemical consequence of defective glycan extension in ER that can be potentially generalized in other ALG3-CDG body fluid/ tissues/cells.

Importantly, this study also offers evidence that constitutive ER stress, abnormal UPR and increased ERAD could be seen in cultured fibroblasts from an ALG3-CDG patient (Figure 5), which warrants further investigation. We also observed mild accumulation of N-linked Man3 and Man4 abundance on cellular glycoproteins when the normal cultured fibroblasts were treated with a combination of cycloheximide and MG132 suggesting that the ERAD pathway can influence the production of these truncated glycans (Supplemental Table 3). Given the role of EDEM3 in processing Man5 to Man4, and in regulating UPR, it will be interesting to test whether EDEM3 is upregulated in such conditions in a cell line that is known to expresses EDEM3 in the future13. Nonetheless our study suggests that a generalized increase of truncated Man3 and Man4 in ALG3-CDG may be partially associated with overall ER stress and chronic activating UPR in this disease, therefore potentially a candidate of disease biomarker.

ER stresses in CDG cells or animal model had been reported in multiple publications in the past4,30–33 . With emerging therapies that successfully reducing ER stressed in a variety of human diseases including CDG31,33 , our studies here not only provide additional insight in the disease mechanism in ALG3-CDG, but also potential associated biomarkers that could facilitate future development in novel therapies.

Consistent with our findings in plasma glycoproteins, the N-glycan structure analysis with mannosidase digestion of cell lysate has shown that while N-Man5 in normal cell lysate resists 1,2-α–mannosidase, it was largely trimmed into Man3 in ALG3-CDG cells (Supplemental Table 2). In contrast, the Man4 in both cells are subject to 1,2- α -mannosidase digestion, which better represents the LLO-Man5 that skips glycan extension, is glucosylated, then transferred from LLO to proteins and trimmed presumably by EDEM3 or other 1,2- α– mannosidases 13.Given the highly skewed Man4/5 ratio in ALG3-CDG fibroblasts, we suspect that LLO-Man5 transferred to nascent proteins are subject to different consequences: some are trimmed into Man4 by EDEM3 in ER; some are trimmed to Man0-Man3 outside ER, probably in Golgi or endosomes/lysosomes; some are further processed to complex glycans in Golgi; and some are probably degraded through ERAD. Since ERAD activity is increased in ALG3-CDG cells, we suspect that the later may contribute to a significant amount of “vanished” LLO-Man5.

Similar to what we observed in Tf from other CDG, some of the Tf, presumably well folded with glycan assembling intermediate Man5, are further processed in Golgi and produce M-antenna N-glycan. These are increased in Tf N-glycan from ALG3-CDG and also detectable on intact Tf proteins by CDT test. This signature Tf glycoform, though its structure remains unknown, has the same molecular mass as the glycoform reported in MGAT2-CDG and is a distinct diagnostic marker for ALG3-CDG by MS-based clinical CDT analysis. Please note in this manuscript, we only predicted glycan structures based on accurate mass measurement and our limited knowledge of CDG cases described previously for the purpose of improving CDG diagnosis, however there are many possible isomers and future studies are warranted to fully solve the structure of these unusual glycans.

It had been reported previously that N-linked Man0 causes cell cycle arrest and death in cancer cells 34. Our lab previously reported that the Man0 in ALG1- or PMM2-CDG as an intermediate from biosynthesis pathway in ER was transferred onto proteins and further processed into Gal1GlcNAc2 (Gal1) and Sial1Gal1GlcNAc2 (Tetra) 16. Here we found that in ALG3-CDG, despite the increase of Man0 in the secreted plasma proteins, neither Gal1 or Tetra was detected suggesting that the Man0 in ALG3-CDG was likely formed from other pathways.

In conclusion, our study elucidates the biochemical consequence of glycan extension deficiency in ALG3-CDG, which includes constitutive activation of UPR through IRE1-α pathway and increased ERAD activity. However, because it is extremely rare to have patient derived cells with homozygous mutations in a CDG gene, our cell-based analysis was collected on one ALG3-CDG cell line, which has significant limitations. Nonetheless, the study here provides unequivocal evidence showing truncated N-linked small high mannose glycans Man3, Man4 (not total Man5) are increased in cellular, secreted hepatic and extra-hepatic proteins, and potentially important disease markers for ALG3-CDG. We showed that some of the glycan assembling intermediate Man5 are likely further processed by Golgi mannosidases and glycotransferase and turned into a M-antenna glycan in Tf, with some presumably processed into even smaller glycans including Man0-4 and excreted into blood, which sheds lights on the important alternative glycosylation pathways that are yet to be discovered.
