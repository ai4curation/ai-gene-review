# Sphingobium sp. SYK-6 LigG involved in lignin degradation is structurally and biochemically related to the glutathione transferase ω class.
**Authors:** Meux E, Prosper P, Masai E, Mulliert G, Dumarçay S, Morel M, Didierjean C, Gelhaye E, Favier F
**Journal:** FEBS Lett (2012)
**DOI:** [10.1016/j.febslet.2012.09.036](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.febslet.2012.09.036)

## Abstract

1. FEBS Lett. 2012 Nov 16;586(22):3944-50. doi: 10.1016/j.febslet.2012.09.036.
Epub  2012 Oct 8.

Sphingobium sp. SYK-6 LigG involved in lignin degradation is structurally and
biochemically related to the glutathione transferase ω class.

Meux E(1), Prosper P, Masai E, Mulliert G, Dumarçay S, Morel M, Didierjean C,
Gelhaye E, Favier F.

Author information:
(1)Université de Lorraine, IAM, UMR 1136, IFR 110 EFABA, Vandoeuvre-les-Nancy,
F-54506, France.

SpLigG is one of the three glutathione transferases (GSTs) involved in the
process of lignin breakdown in the soil bacterium Sphingobium sp. SYK-6.
Sequence comparisons showed that SpLigG and several proteobacteria homologues
form an independent cluster within cysteine-containing GSTs. The relationship
between SpLigG and other GSTs was investigated. The X-ray structure and
biochemical properties of SpLigG indicate that this enzyme belongs to the omega
class of glutathione transferases. However, the hydrophilic substrate binding
site of SpLigG, together with its known ability to stereoselectively
deglutathionylate the physiological substrate
α-glutathionyl-β-hydroxypropiovanillone, argues for broadening the definition of
the omega class.
