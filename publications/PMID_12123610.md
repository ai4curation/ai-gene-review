Comment in
    Neuron. 2002 Jul 3;35(1):1-3. doi: 10.1016/s0896-6273(02)00765-1.

Synaptic remodeling has been postulated as a mechanism underlying synaptic
plasticity, and cadherin adhesion molecules are thought to be a regulator of
such a process. We examined the effects of cadherin blockage on synaptogenesis
in cultured hippocampal neurons. This blockade resulted in alterations of
dendritic spine morphology, such as filopodia-like elongation of the spine and
bifurcation of its head structure, along with concomitant disruption of the
distribution of postsynaptic proteins. The accumulation of synapsin at
presynaptic sites and synaptic vesicle recycling were also perturbed, although
these synaptic responses to the cadherin blockade became less evident upon the
maturation of the synapses. These findings suggest that cadherin regulates
dendritic spine morphogenesis and related synaptic functions, presumably
cooperating with cadherin-independent adhesive mechanisms to maintain spine-axon
contacts.
