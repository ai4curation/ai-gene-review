# Nuclear structure in normal and Bloom syndrome cells.
**Authors:** Yankiwski V, Marciniak RA, Guarente L, Neff NF

Nuclear structure in normal and Bloom syndrome cells.

Yankiwski V(1), Marciniak RA, Guarente L, Neff NF.

Author information:
(1)Laboratory of Molecular Genetics, New York Blood Center, 310 East 67th
Street, New York, NY 10021, USA.

Bloom syndrome (BS) is a rare cancer-predisposing disorder in which the cells of
affected persons have a high frequency of somatic mutation and genomic
instability. BLM, the protein altered in BS, is a RecQ DNA helicase. This report
shows that BLM is found in the nucleus of normal human cells in the nuclear
domain 10 or promyelocytic leukemia nuclear bodies. These structures are
punctate depots of proteins disrupted upon viral infection and in certain human
malignancies. BLM is found primarily in nuclear domain 10 except during S phase
when it colocalizes with the Werner syndrome gene product, WRN, in the
nucleolus. BLM colocalizes with a select subset of telomeres in normal cells and
with large telomeric clusters seen in simian virus 40-transformed normal
fibroblasts. During S phase, BS cells expel micronuclei containing sites of DNA
synthesis. BLM is likely to be part of a DNA surveillance mechanism operating
during S phase.
