# Isolation and primary structure of the ERG9 gene of Saccharomyces cerevisiae encoding squalene synthetase.
**Authors:** Fegueur M, Richard L, Charles AD, Karst F
**Journal:** Curr Genet (1991)
**DOI:** [10.1007/BF00317063](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF00317063)

## Abstract

1. Curr Genet. 1991 Nov;20(5):365-72. doi: 10.1007/BF00317063.

Isolation and primary structure of the ERG9 gene of Saccharomyces cerevisiae
encoding squalene synthetase.

Fegueur M(1), Richard L, Charles AD, Karst F.

Author information:
(1)Laboratoire de Biochimie et Génétique des Microorganismes, Université de
Poitiers, France.

The ERG9 gene of Saccharomyces cerevisiae has been cloned by complementation of
the erg9-1 mutation which affects squalene synthetase. From the 5 kb insert
isolated, the functional gene has been localized on a DNA fragment of 2.5 kb.
The presence of squalene synthetase activity in E. coli bearing the yeast DNA
fragment isolated, indicates that the structural gene encoding squalene
synthetase has been cloned. The sequence of the 2.5 kb fragment contains an open
reading frame which could encode a protein of 444 amino acids with a deduced
relative molecular mass of 51,600. The amino acid sequence reveals one to four
potential transmembrane domains with a hydrophobic segment in the C-terminal
region. The N-terminus of the deduced protein strongly resembles the signal
sequence of yeast invertase suggesting a specific mechanism of integration into
the membranes of the endoplasmic reticulum.
