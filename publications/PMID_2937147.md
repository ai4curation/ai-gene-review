# Molecular genetics of human color vision: the genes encoding blue, green, and red pigments.
**Authors:** Nathans J, Thomas D, Hogness DS
**Journal:** Science (1986)
**DOI:** [10.1126/science.2937147](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.2937147)

## Abstract

1. Science. 1986 Apr 11;232(4747):193-202. doi: 10.1126/science.2937147.

Molecular genetics of human color vision: the genes encoding blue, green, and
red pigments.

Nathans J, Thomas D, Hogness DS.

Human color vision is based on three light-sensitive pigments. The isolation and
sequencing of genomic and complementary DNA clones that encode the apoproteins
of these three pigments are described. The deduced amino acid sequences show 41
+/- 1 percent identity with rhodopsin. The red and green pigments show 96
percent mutual identity but only 43 percent identity with the blue pigment.
Green pigment genes vary in number among color-normal individuals and, together
with a single red pigment gene, are proposed to reside in a head-to-tail tandem
array within the X chromosome.
