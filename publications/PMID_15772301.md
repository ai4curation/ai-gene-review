Humans excrete uric acid as the final breakdown product of unwanted purine
nucleotides. Urate scavenges potential harmful radicals in our body. However, in
conjunction with genetic or environmental (especially dietary) factors, urate
may cause gout, nephrolitiasis, hypertension, and vascular disease. Blood levels
of urate are maintained by the balance between generation and excretion.
Excretion requires specialized transporters located in renal proximal tubule
cells, intestinal epithelial cells, and vascular smooth muscle cells. The
recently identified human urate transporters URAT1, MRP4, OAT1, and OAT3 are
thought to play central roles in homeostasis and may prove interesting targets
for future drug development.
