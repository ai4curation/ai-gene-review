Stimulator of interferon genes (STING) is activated in many pathophysiological
conditions, leading to TBK1-dependent interferon production in higher organisms.
However, primordial functions of STING independent of TBK1 are poorly
understood. Here, through proteomics and bioinformatics approaches, we identify
lysosomal biogenesis as an unexpected function of STING. Transcription factor EB
(TFEB), an evolutionarily conserved regulator of lysosomal biogenesis and host
defense, is activated by STING from multiple species, including humans, mice,
and frogs. STING-mediated TFEB activation is independent of TBK1, but it
requires STING trafficking and its conserved proton channel. GABARAP lipidation,
stimulated by the channel of STING, is key for STING-dependent TFEB activation.
STING stimulates global upregulation of TFEB-target genes, mediating lysosomal
biogenesis and autophagy. TFEB supports cell survival during chronic sterile
STING activation, a common condition in aging and age-related diseases. These
results reveal a primordial function of STING in the biogenesis of lysosomes,
essential organelles in immunity and cellular stress resistance.

INTRODUCTION

Cytosolic exposure of endogenous or exogenous DNA is a common feature of many pathophysiological conditions including microbial infection, cancer, senescence, and neurodegeneration.1,2 The cytosolic DNA sensor cyclic GMP-AMP (cGAMP) synthase (cGAS) is activated in these conditions to upregulate innate immune signaling.1,2 Upon DNA binding, cGAS produces a second messenger cGAMP which binds to and activates STING,3,4 an endoplasmic reticulum (ER)-anchored signaling adaptor.5-7 cGAMP triggers STING trafficking from the ER to perinuclear vesicle clusters where TBK1 is activated, leading to transcriptional upregulation of inflammation, mostly through type I interferons.8 While STING-mediated inflammation plays a key role in host defense against microbes and cancer, chronic activation of STING in aging and neurodegeneration promotes disease pathogenesis.1,2 Thus, a comprehensive understanding of the molecular mechanisms and functions of STING signaling is crucial for context-dependent therapeutics.

The cGAS-STING pathway exhibits a high degree of conservation, predating the emergence of type I interferons which relatively recently appeared in vertebrates.9 The primordial functions of the DNA sensing pathway independent of TBK1/interferons are not well characterized. Noncanonical LC3 lipidation,10 a TBK1-independent function of STING, has been recently found to depend on the highly conserved proton channel of STING.11,12 However, it remains unknown how such LC3 lipidation exerts anti-microbial effects and whether any transcriptional response lies downstream of the primordial STING channel.

Lysosomes are essential quality control organelles that resolve cellular stress by selectively removing abnormal cellular components such as damaged macromolecules, irreparable organelles, and invading microbes.13 In response to diverse cellular stress stimuli, cellular lysosomal numbers and activity are typically upregulated to handle stress-induced damages.13,14 Transcription factor EB (TFEB) is a highly conserved regulator of lysosomal biogenesis which can be activated by cell stress.14,15 TFEB belongs to the microphthalmia family of basic helix-loop-helix leucine-zipper (bHLH-Zip) transcription factors (MiT family) which also contains TFE3, TFEC, and MITF in mammals.14,15 Among the four MiT family members, TFEB and TFE3 are the major transcription factors upregulating lysosomal biogenesis via binding to the coordinated lysosomal expression and regulation (CLEAR) motifs of lysosomal genes.16,17 Besides lysosomal biogenesis, TFEB is also an evolutionarily ancient regulator of autophagy and innate host defense.18,19 Although both STING and TFEB are essential in innate immunity, the relationship between the two ancient pathways was not previously investigated.

In this study, we hypothesized that STING might have TBK1-independent transcriptional functions. Through unbiased proteomics and bioinformatics approaches, we unexpectedly identified TFEB activation as a conserved function of STING. TFEB is activated by STING from multiple species, independent of TBK1/interferon signaling. The recently discovered, primordial proton channel of STING is essential for TFEB activation, through V-ATPase-mediated GABARAP lipidation. We further validated STING-stimulated transcriptional activity of TFEB through RNA sequencing which revealed broad upregulation of TFEB target genes in a STING-dependent manner. Consistent with established roles of TFEB in cellular stress clearance, we found TFEB was critical for cell survival during chronic STING signaling, a condition commonly found in senescence and neurodegeneration.

RESULTSIdentification of TFEB/TFE3 nuclear translocation upon STING activationTo search for new transcription factors regulated by STING, we employed the recently developed TransitID20 to detect proteins undergoing cyto-nuclear translocation upon STING activation (Figure 1A). We used human BJ fibroblasts for these experiments, as they have endogenous cGAS-STING signaling and are commonly used for mechanistic studies of this pathway.21-23 This approach allowed us to capture proteins that were initially biotinylated in the cytosol but enriched in the nucleus after STING activation (Figures 1B, S1A, and S1B). Several proteins expected to be activated by STING signaling were among the top hits (Figure 1C): LYAR, an inhibitor of IRF3;24 STAT2, a transcription factor activated by interferon receptors;25 and IFIT2, an interferon-induced protein.26 This approach did not capture IRF3 (Table S1), despite strong nuclear translocation of IRF3 upon STING activation (Figure S1C), suggesting that it was not sufficiently sensitive to identify all candidates. Nevertheless, one of the top hits, 14-3-3β, was confirmed to translocate into the nucleus upon STING activation (Figures 1C-E). The nuclear translocation of 14-3-3β is known to be driven by the loss of binding to its substrates – typically phosphorylated proteins.27 Thus, STING-dependent nuclear translocation of 14-3-3β suggests potential protein dephosphorylation events, in contrast to the canonical TBK1-mediated phosphorylation reactions for the upregulation of type I interferons.To search for 14-3-3β substrates that could be dephosphorylated upon STING activation, we used a bioinformatics approach to analyze all the known 1067 interacting proteins of 14-3-3β (Figure 1F, Tables S2). This yielded a final list of 17 transcription factors falling into 7 groups, all known to be activated by dephosphorylation (Figure 1G). Among them, c-Myc was found to be depleted, whereas TFEB, FOXO1, and FOXO4 became enriched in the nucleus of BJ cells upon STING activation (Figure 1H). TFEB and FOXO proteins are established positive regulators of autophagy and/or lysosome pathways,14,15,28 whereas c-Myc is an inhibitor of TFEB by competing for the binding to promoters of TFEB target genes.29 The simultaneous nuclear depletion of c-Myc and enrichment of TFEB/FOXOs suggest potential transcriptional upregulation of lysosome/autophagy pathways by STING. Given the evolutionarily ancient roles of TFEB not only in lysosome biogenesis but also in autophagy upregulation and innate immunity,18,19,30 we decided to focus on TFEB as a potential STING effector for further characterization.The cGAMP-induced nuclear enrichment of TFEB was accompanied by its depletion in the cytosol, its dephosphorylation as detected by phospho-specific TFEB antibodies, and a robust mobility shift (Figure 1I) commonly used as an indicator of TFEB activation.31 Similar to TFEB, TFE3 also translocated from the cytosol to the nucleus upon STING activation by cGAMP (Figure 1I). Both TFEB and TFE3 exhibited dose-dependent activation by cGAMP in BJ cells (Figure 1J). In THP-1 human monocytes, a cell type completely different from BJ, both TFEB and TFE3 were similarly activated upon cellular treatment with diABZI, a synthetic STING agonist (Figure 1K). ML-SA1 is a small molecule agonist of transient receptor potential mucolipin 1 (TRPML1, a cation-permeable channel, also known as Mucolipin1 or MCOLN1) and a known activator of TFEB.32,33 The level of TFEB activation by STING agonists was comparable to that by ML-SA1 (Figure 1K). However, ML-SA1/TRPML1-mediated TFEB activation did not involve STING signaling (Figure S1D). Similarly, STING agonist-induced TFEB activation was independent of TRPML1 (Figures S1E-G). Thus, STING signaling activates TFEB/TFE3 and drives their nuclear translocation.TFEB/TFE3 are conserved effectors of the cGAS-STING pathwayWe further investigated TFEB/TFE3 activation by the cGAS-STING pathway. Herring testes DNA (HT-DNA), an established cGAS ligand upstream of STING,4 triggered the activation of both TFEB and TFE3 in BJ cells, similarly to the STING agonists cGAMP and diABZI or the STING-independent TFEB activator ML-SA1 (Figure 2A). While they all activated TFEB in a dose- and time-dependent manner, HT-DNA required a longer time than cGAMP and diABZI to trigger a full band shift of TFEB (Figures S2A-E), consistent with the time needed for cGAMP production by cGAS. G150, a cGAS inhibitor,34 fully blocked TFEB activation by HT-DNA but not by diABZI (Figure 2B), the latter of which activates STING downstream of cGAS. Thus, cGAS activates TFEB/TFE3 through cGAMP production.Consistent with STING being downstream of cGAS, HT-DNA-induced TFEB activation was abolished in STING knockout (STING-KO) cells (Figure 2C). Similarly, cGAMP-stimulated TFEB activation was abrogated in STING-KO cells, which was fully rescued by the reconstitution of wild-type STING but not its R238A/Y240A mutant35 deficient in cGAMP binding (Figure 2D). The cGAMP-stimulated nuclear translocation of TFEB was also further validated by fluorescence microscopy in wild-type but not STING-KO cells stably expressing TFEB-EGFP (Figure 2E).To further examine whether STING signaling is sufficient for TFEB activation, we reconstituted STING in three distinct cell lines, U2OS, HeLa, and HEK 293T, all of which had almost no endogenous STING (Figure S2F). These cells, when treated with STING agonists, showed no STING/TBK1/IRF3 signaling, STING-dependent LC3 lipidation, or TFEB activation (Figures 2F, S2G, and S2H, Left two lanes). However, stable expression of human STING (HsSTING) in these cells fully reconstituted all events upon delivery of STING agonists, including the band shift of TFEB and TFE3 as well as TFEB dephosphorylation (Figures 2F, S2G, and S2H, right two lanes). Expression of mouse or xenopus Sting (MmSting, XtSting) in U2OS cells was also sufficient to reconstitute TFEB/TFE3 activation by STING agonists (Figures S2I and S2J). Such activation was further validated by the nuclear translocation of TFEB-EGFP, upon the activation of STING by various agonists (Figures 2G and 2H). Note that while cGAMP activated STING from all three species, diABZI failed to activate XtSting, and DMXAA was specific for MmSting (Figures 2G, 2H, and S2K-M). Although HsSTING and MmSting can activate TBK1, XtSting is deficient in TBK1 signaling due to a lack of the C-terminal TBK1-binding motif.10 Thus, TFEB/TFE3 activation is a conserved function of the cGAS-STING pathway (Figure 2I), which is likely independent of TBK1 signaling.TFEB/TFE3 activation is independent of TBK1 but requires STING traffickingTo investigate whether STING-dependent TFEB activation required TBK1 signaling, we activated STING in the presence of two structurally distinct TBK1 inhibitors, MRT67307 and BX-795. Although both fully blocked IRF3 phosphorylation by TBK1, neither had any impact on STING-dependent TFEB activation (Figures S3A-C). Similarly, two STING mutants S366A and L374A, deficient in IRF3 and TBK1 recruitment, respectively,36,37 fully lost their activity in stimulating IRF3 phosphorylation but were still potent in TFEB activation (Figure 3A). However, TBK1 phosphorylation was still detected in all these conditions (Figures 3A and S3A). Moreover, TBK1 and IKKε have redundant roles in STING signaling.38,39 To completely remove TBK1 activity, we generated TBK1/IKKε double knockout (TBK1/IKKε-DKO) U2OS and BJ cells. In all the TBK1/IKKε-DKO clones, the phosphorylation of STING, TBK1, and IRF3 were completely lost, but STING-dependent TFEB activation and nuclear translocation were intact (Figures 3B-D and S3D), indicating that TFEB activation by STING is independent of TBK1 signaling.Canonical STING signaling is mediated through the C-terminal tail (CTT) of STING that directly recruits TBK1 and IRF3 via two different motifs.36 We explored whether part of the STING CTT domain was necessary for TFEB activation. Deleting the last part of STING-CTT essential for TBK1/IRF3 recruitment abolished STING phosphorylation at S366 by TBK1 but did not affect STING-mediated TFEB activation (Figures 3E and 3F). Consistently, these CTT deletion mutants can still activate the nuclear translocation of TFEB-EGFP (Figures 3G-I). However, longer deletions from the C-terminal end (STING340) caused defects in TFEB activation (Figures 3E-I). Although STING340 had normal expression levels comparable to the full-length protein (Figure 3F), its trafficking from the ER is almost completely lost (Figure 3J), suggesting that STING trafficking is likely essential for TFEB activation. Consistently, Golgicide A (GCA) and brefeldin A (BFA), two different chemicals that disassemble the Golgi and thus block STING trafficking through the Golgi complex,10 fully abolished STING-mediated TFEB activation and nuclear translocation (Figures 3K-M and S3E). Compound C53, a recently discovered HsSTING agonist,40 is known to block cGAMP- and diABZI-induced STING trafficking at a pre-Golgi stage (Figure 4A),11 but it is however able to activate TBK1 signaling.11,40 The addition of C53 fully abolished STING-mediated TFEB activation with little impact on TBK1 signaling (Figures 4B and S3F). Thus, STING-dependent TFEB activation requires STING trafficking but not TBK1 signaling.The conserved channel of STING mediates TFEB/TFE3 activationSTING trafficking not only correlates with TBK1 activation but also allows the proton channel activity of STING.11,12 We thus explored if the channel of STING is required for TFEB activation. Compound C53 is a unique HsSTING agonist that binds to the channel of STING acting as a blocker of STING-dependent vesicle de-acidification.11,12 However, although C53 alone can stimulate STING-dependent TBK1 signaling, the presence of C53 blocks cGAMP- and diABZI-induced STING trafficking from the ER to the Golgi complex.11 Thus, C53 cannot be directly used as a STING channel blocker. We therefore employed a previously described strategy in which compound C53 was added 30 min after cGAMP or 60 min after diABZI to selectively block the channel of STING in vesicle de-acidification without affecting its trafficking through the Golgi (Figure 4A).11 Under these conditions, C53 fully disrupted STING-dependent TFEB activation and its nuclear translocation, in both BJ cells and STING-expressing U2OS cells (Figures 4C-E and S3G-I), suggesting that the channel of STING mediates TFEB activation.To rule out nonspecific effects of C53 other than STING channel blocking, we also employed two recently described STING channel mutants H50A and S53L that lack the ability to bind C53.40 These mutants completely lost C53-stimulated TBK1 signaling (Figure S3J), but still exhibited robust perinuclear trafficking upon cGAMP treatment (Figure S3K), indicating normal cGAMP binding. Both STING H50A and S53L were no longer inhibited by C53 in cGAMP-stimulated TFEB activation and nuclear translocation (Figures 4F-H). Similarly, C53 can only bind to the channel of HsSTING but not MmSting and as such, C53 did not inhibit MmSting-mediated TFEB activation and nuclear translocation (Figures 4I-K) or vesicle de-acidification (Figures S3L and S3M). Together, these data indicate that TFEB is activated by the conserved transmembrane channel of STING (Figure 4L).GABARAP lipidation mediates STING-dependent TFEB/TFE3 activationWe further explored how the channel of STING stimulated TFEB activation. Although TRPML1-mediated calcium signaling was reported to mediate TFEB activation,33,41,42 STING-induced TFEB activation appeared to be calcium-independent (Figures S4A-D). ATG8 lipidation, specifically GABARAP lipidation, has been recently identified as a mechanism for TRPML1-mediated TFEB activation.33
33,43,44 Interestingly, the channel of STING, which is essential for TFEB activation by STING (Figure 4), can trigger noncanonical LC3 lipidation, also known as noncanonical autophagy, conjugation of ATG8s to single membranes (CASM), or V-ATPase-ATG16L1-induced LC3 lipidation (VAIL).11,12,45,46 STING-induced LC3 lipidation is primarily detected in post-Golgi, endosome-like STING trafficking vesicles.11 We found that the channel of STING triggered not only noncanonical LC3 lipidation, but also robust GABARAP lipidation (Figures S3F-I). We thus investigated whether STING-mediated GABARAP lipidation contributed to TFEB activation (Figure 5A).One approach to block V-ATPase-mediated ATG8 lipidation is to use a small molecule bafilomycin A1 (BafA1) which inhibits V-ATPase assembly.47 However, we were not able to use BafA1 as a valid approach to assess the role of V-ATPase assembly in STING-dependent TFEB activation, because low levels of BafA1 alone triggered TFEB activation independent of STING (Figures S4E and S4F). An alternative approach to block CASM is overexpressing SopF, a Salmonella effector protein that ADP-ribosylates the V-ATPase to disrupt the recruitment of the ATG16L1-ATG12-ATG5 complex.47 Consistent with previous findings,33 SopF overexpression strongly blocked TRPML1-mediated ATG8 lipidation and TFEB activation (Figures S4G and S4H). Similarly, overexpression of wild-type but not the Y224D ribosylase-dead mutant of SopF strongly suppressed STING-mediated ATG8 lipidation and TFEB activation, especially when relatively low levels of STING agonists were used (Figures 5B and S4I). Similar to SopF overexpression, knockout of either ATG5 or ATG7 also suppressed TFEB activation in response to lower levels of STING activation (Figures 5C and 5D).Downstream of the ATG16L1-ATG12-ATG5 machinery, recent studies revealed distinct roles of the ATG8 family members, LC3s and GABARAPs, in autophagy and TFEB activation.33,44,48 Consistently, ATG8 hexa-knockout (ATG8-HKO) and GABARAP/GABARAPL1/GABARAPL2 triple knockout (GAB-TKO), but not LC3A/LC3B/LC3C triple knockout (LC3-TKO), strongly blocked TFEB activation by STING (Figures 5E, 5F, and S4J). ATG2A/B and ATG13, which are essential for macroautophagy but not V-ATPase-mediated ATG8 lipidation,49 were not required for STING-dependent TFEB activation (Figure S4K). An additional approach to antagonize ATG8 lipidation is to express RavZ, a Legionella effector that directly extracts and cleaves lipidated ATG8 from membranes (Figure 5A).50 Overexpression of wild-type RavZ but not its enzymatically dead mutant C258A completely removed ATG8-II and strongly suppressed STING-dependent TFEB activation (Figures 5G and 5H).GABARAP lipidation is known to inhibit the FLCN-FNIP-RagC/D axis, thus blocking mTORC1-mediated TFEB phosphorylation (Figure 5I).33 Consistently, overexpression of RagC-T90N,51 which constitutively binds to GDP and is thus no longer regulated by the GABARAP-FLCN/FNIP nexus for TFEB phosphorylation, fully blocked TFEB activation by either STING (Figure 5J) or TRPML1 agonist (Figure S5A). STING activation caused no obvious change in mTORC1 localization to lysosomes (Figure S5B-D), with a modest reduction in mTORC1 activity towards the canonical substrate S6K but not 4EBP1 (Figure S5E). GABARAP-TKO and ATG8-HKO, each strongly blocking STING-dependent TFEB activation, did not affect mTORC1 activity on S6K or 4EBP1 (Figure 5K). The mTORC1 inhibitor Torin 1 can still fully activate TFEB in GABARAP-TKO and ATG8-HKO cells (Figure 5K). Thus, the STING-GABARAP axis mainly acts downstream of mTROC1, selectively inhibiting its activity toward TFEB.Taken together, GABARAP lipidation triggered by the channel of STING provides a robust mechanism for TFEB activation.STING stimulates transcriptional upregulation of lysosomal biogenesis and autophagyThe data suggest that STING might stimulate lysosomal expansion as a primordial function of its channel. To analyze the transcriptional function of STING in the absence of TBK1 signaling, we conducted RNA-seq experiments using U2OS cells stably expressing MmSting350 which lacked the C-terminal TBK1/IRF3 activation motifs (Figure 3E). In these cells, DMXAA, a MmSting-specific agonist, should only trigger TBK1-independent transcription by STING. This approach identified numerous differentially expressed genes upon DMXAA treatment (Figures S5F-I). Gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) revealed both lysosome and autophagy genes as significantly enriched in the upregulated genes (Figure 6A).To better understand how STING transcriptionally regulates lysosomal biology, we generated a TFEB/CLEAR target gene list (Table S3) combining two previously developed gene sets including experimentally verified TFEB targets or CLEAR genes.52-54 Expression analysis identified increased transcription of more than 400 CLEAR genes after DMXAA stimulation of U2OS MmSting350 cells for 8 hours (Figure 6B). Enrichment analysis revealed “lysosome” and “autophagy” as top represented Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathways by the DMXAA-upregulated CLEAR genes (Figures 6C and 6D). Among the top upregulated CLEAR targets are lysosomal genes such as GPR137B (a lysosomal GPCR), CTSB (a lysosomal protease), NEU1 (a lysosomal neuraminidase), and FLCN (a mTOR regulator) as well as autophagy genes such as GABARAPL1, SQSTM1, EPG5, and PLEKHM1 (Figures 6B and 6D).To further validate the transcriptional upregulation of lysosome and autophagy genes by endogenous STING, we performed RNA-seq experiments using WT and STING-KO BJ cells (Figures S5J-Q). The activation of endogenous STING by cGAMP was demonstrated by the robust upregulation of type Ib interferon (IFNB1), and ISGs such as CXCL10, ISG15, and ISG20 in WT but not STING-KO BJ cells (Figure S6A). Further expression analysis revealed that hundreds of CLEAR genes were also STING-dependently upregulated by cGAMP in BJ cells (Figures S6B and S6C). Enrichment analysis confirmed “lysosome” and “autophagy” as top represented KEGG pathways by these genes (Figures 6E and 6F). STING- and cGAMP-dependent transcriptional upregulation of both lysosome and autophagy genes were more prominent 12 hours after cGAMP delivery compared with 6 hours (Figures 6G, S6D, and S6E).To verify the RNA-seq results, we examined a list of CLEAR genes that were upregulated in both U2OS MmSting350 cells and BJ cells by Real-Time Quantitative Reverse Transcription PCR (Table S4). This approach detected similar expression changes compared with RNA-seq (Figures 6H and S7A). Thus, STING activation triggers a previously unrecognized TBK1-independent transcriptional response that upregulates the biogenesis of lysosomes and autophagosomes, essential organelles for immunity.TFEB/TFE3 maintain cell survival under chronic sterile STING signalingTo verify the functional relevance of STING-stimulated upregulation of TFEB target genes, we used LysoTracker staining as a measure of lysosomal number and acidification in BJ cells before and after STING activation. LysoTracker staining was substantially increased 24 hours after cGAMP stimulation, which was not observed in STING-KO cells (Figures 7A and 7B). Similarly, LAMP1 staining also revealed STING-dependent increase in lysosomal mass after cGAMP treatment (Figures 7C and 7D). Consistent with the STING-stimulated upregulation of autophagy genes, we have previously shown that STING activation triggers autophagy for the clearance of cytosolic DNA and viruses.10 Given the well-established roles of TFEB, lysosomes, and autophagy in antiviral responses, our data are consistent with a primordial anti-viral function of STING through TFEB-dependent lysosomal biogenesis and autophagy.Both the TFEB and cGAS-STING pathways are also tightly connected to senescence, a cellular status during organismal aging characterized by permanent cell cycle arrest and senescence associated secretion phenotype (SASP). In multiple senescence models, the upregulation of sterile inflammation through the cGAS-STING pathway contributes to SASP and is critical for senescence induction.55-57 TFEB/TFE3 activation and subsequent lysosomal biogenesis play key roles in maintaining cellular survival in senescence.58 Since chronic, sterile cGAS/STING signaling is common in aging and age-related diseases, we investigated whether TFEB/TFE3 maintain cell survival in this context.We observed a remarkable cell proliferation arrest by continuously stimulating BJ cells with the cell-permeable STING agonist diABZI (Figures 7E and S7B). Knockout of STING fully abolished such proliferation arrest (Figures 7E and S7B), suggesting that chronic STING signaling causes substantial cell stress. STING-dependent cell proliferation arrest was accompanied by the upregulation of multiple cell cycle regulators commonly detected in senescence, such as p21, p16, and p-H2A.X, as well as the downregulation of Lamin B1 (Figure 7F). However, these cells did not appear to be senescent, as their proliferation recovered upon removal of the STING agonist (Figure 7G). Indeed, chronic STING activation appears to cause non-senescent G1 cell cycle arrest, as we observed in diABZI-arrested cells a loss of GTSE1 (Figure 7F), a cell cycle regulator specifically depleted in G1, consistent with a previous report.59 To test if TFEB/TFE3-mediated transcriptional response supported cell survival during STING-dependent cell cycle arrest, we generated TFEB/TFE3-DKO BJ cells (Figure 7H). Similarly, overexpression of wild-type but not the Y224D ribosylase-dead mutant of SopF strongly suppressed STING-mediated ATG8 lipidation and TFEB activation, especially when relatively low levels of STING agonists were used (Figures 5B and S4I). Similar to SopF overexpression, knockout of either ATG5 or ATG7 also suppressed TFEB activation in response to lower levels of STING activation (Figures 5C and 5D).

Downstream of the ATG16L1-ATG12-ATG5 machinery, recent studies revealed distinct roles of the ATG8 family members, LC3s and GABARAPs, in autophagy and TFEB activation.33,44,48 Consistently, ATG8 hexa-knockout (ATG8-HKO) and GABARAP/GABARAPL1/GABARAPL2 triple knockout (GAB-TKO), but not LC3A/LC3B/LC3C triple knockout (LC3-TKO), strongly blocked TFEB activation by STING (Figures 5E, 5F, and S4J). ATG2A/B and ATG13, which are essential for macroautophagy but not V-ATPase-mediated ATG8 lipidation,49 were not required for STING-dependent TFEB activation (Figure S4K). An additional approach to antagonize ATG8 lipidation is to express RavZ, a Legionella effector that directly extracts and cleaves lipidated ATG8 from membranes (Figure 5A).50 Overexpression of wild-type RavZ but not its enzymatically dead mutant C258A completely removed ATG8-II and strongly suppressed STING-dependent TFEB activation (Figures 5G and 5H).

GABARAP lipidation is known to inhibit the FLCN-FNIP-RagC/D axis, thus blocking mTORC1-mediated TFEB phosphorylation (Figure 5I).33 Consistently, overexpression of RagC-T90N,51 which constitutively binds to GDP and is thus no longer regulated by the GABARAP-FLCN/FNIP nexus for TFEB phosphorylation, fully blocked TFEB activation by either STING (Figure 5J) or TRPML1 agonist (Figure S5A). STING activation caused no obvious change in mTORC1 localization to lysosomes (Figure S5B-D), with a modest reduction in mTORC1 activity towards the canonical substrate S6K but not 4EBP1 (Figure S5E). GABARAP-TKO and ATG8-HKO, each strongly blocking STING-dependent TFEB activation, did not affect mTORC1 activity on S6K or 4EBP1 (Figure 5K). The mTORC1 inhibitor Torin 1 can still fully activate TFEB in GABARAP-TKO and ATG8-HKO cells (Figure 5K). Thus, the STING-GABARAP axis mainly acts downstream of mTROC1, selectively inhibiting its activity toward TFEB.

Taken together, GABARAP lipidation triggered by the channel of STING provides a robust mechanism for TFEB activation.

DISCUSSIONSTING is a major innate immune adaptor chronically activated in aging and many age-related diseases. While STING-mediated anti-viral signaling and inflammation upregulation are well-established, the primordial functions of STING are much less understood. In this study, we identified transcriptional lysosomal biogenesis as a primordial function of STING (Figure 7P). TFEB, a major regulator of lysosomal biogenesis and host defense, is activated by the conserved channel of STING. GABARAP lipidation, triggered by the channel of STING, provides a robust platform for TFEB activation, resembling many other contexts.33,43,44 Compared with other transcription factors downstream of STING such as IRF3 and NF-κB that upregulate their target genes by several orders of magnitude,3-5,10,38,39,60,61 TFEB transcriptional targets in this study, and in many others,16,17,31,33,41,44 are only induced a few folds over baseline. This is consistent with a typical two-fold increase in lysosomal mass during stress-induced lysosomal biogenesis.16,17,31,33,41,44 However, the relatively weak transcriptional upregulation by TFEB might not be easily captured without deep RNA sequencing. This might explain why TFEB-mediated transcriptional responses were missed in previous studies on STING.We and others have previously observed STING-dependent autophagy which promotes cellular clearance of stressed ER and cytosolic DNA.10,62 While substantial progress has been recently made in STING-dependent noncanonical LC3 lipidation, how STING stimulates autophagic activity remains unresolved. Our current study indicates that STING-induced autophagic activity involves transcriptional upregulation of both macroautophagy and lysosomal biogenesis.TFEB activation by the channel of STING appears to be primordial. Similar to cGAS and STING, TFEB is highly conserved from primitive metazoans to vertebrates,63 more ancient than type I interferons which emerged relatively recently in vertebrates.9 TFEB is also an evolutionarily ancient factor in innate immunity due to its transcriptional upregulation of autophagy, lysosomes, and proinflammatory cytokines.18,19,30 Thus, the cGAS-STING-TFEB axis is an ancient form of the antiviral DNA-sensing pathway that predates type I interferons.The cGAS-STING innate immune pathway, with its broad activation across various medical conditions, presents promising clinical applications. TFEB-mediated lysosomal biogenesis and autophagy upregulation are activated in many settings for cellular stress clearance.13-15 Given cytosolic DNA as a common danger signal of diverse stressed conditions, it might not be surprising that the DNA-sensing pathway activates TFEB. This indeed aligns with earlier perplexing observations of TFEB nuclear translocation stemming from the absence of the cytosolic exonuclease TREX1 or the presence of an extra chromosome.64,65 A role for TFEB/TFE3 in cellular stress clearance is apparent as they support cell survival not only in senescence58 but also during chronic STING signaling in non-senescent cells (Figure 7I). As such, TFEB activation by the STING channel appears to be cytoprotective, despite previous observations that excessive STING channel activity causes cell death.11 It suggests that opting for the targeted suppression of TBK1-interferon signaling while preserving the channel functions of STING could yield greater advantages compared to complete STING inhibition when inflammation is a concern. Therefore, it would be crucial to identify the exact mechanism of cell death (channel versus inflammation) in diseases exhibiting aberrant cGAS-STING signaling for precise therapeutic interventions.Limitations of the StudyOur study has several limitations. First, this study relied on cell culture. In future studies, it will be important to examine this pathway in vivo. Particularly, assessing the impact of this pathway on aging and age-related disease will help elucidate the pathophysiological functions of this pathway. Second, high levels of STING activation likely activates TFEB through mechanisms in addition to GABARAP lipidation, which needs to be further investigated in future studies. Specifically, SopF-overexpressing, ATG5-KO, and ATG7-KO cells showed limited or no defect on TFEB activation in response to higher levels of STING activation. While this suggests that non-lipidated GABARAPs may contribute to STING-mediated TFEB activation, an alternative explanation is that additional mechanisms in parallel of GABARAPs is likely activated by STING in response to higher levels of STING agonists. Third, additional technical strategies to block the channel of STING, if available in the future, will be useful in further validating the channel function in TFEB activation and lysosomal biogenesis. Fourth, while we were able to observe STING-dependent TFEB activation by the endogenous ligand cGAMP in BJ and other reconstitution cell lines, we were not able to deliver cGAMP into THP1 cells by digitonin without causing cell death. THP-1 cells pre-differentiated with phorbol 12-myristate 13-acetate (PMA) will likely increase cellular resistance to digitonin-induced cell death. Finally, while our screen identified TFEB as an effector of STING, it did not capture TFE3. Thus, it is likely that our screen also missed other important STING effectors which should be explored in the future to fully understand the primordial functions of STING.
