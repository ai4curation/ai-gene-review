## Full Text

Abstract

The rapid, massive synthesis of storage proteins that occurs during seed development stresses endoplasmic reticulum (ER) homeostasis, which activates the ER unfolded protein response (UPR). However, how different storage proteins contribute to UPR is not clear. We analyzed vegetative tissues of transgenic Arabidopsis ( Arabidopsis thaliana) plants constitutively expressing the common bean ( Phaseolus vulgaris ) soluble vacuolar storage protein PHASEOLIN (PHSL) or maize ( Zea mays) prolamins (27-kDa γ-zein or 16-kDa γ-zein) that participate in forming insoluble protein bodies in the ER. We show that 16-kDa γ-zein significantly activates the INOSITOL REQUIRING ENZYME1/BASIC LEUCINE ZIPPER 60 (bZIP60) UPR branch—but not the bZIP28 branch or autophagy—leading to induction of major UPR-controlled genes that encode folding helpers that function inside the ER. Protein blot analysis of IMMUNOGLOBULIN-BINDING PROTEIN (BIP) 1 and 2, BIP3, GLUCOSE REGULATED PROTEIN 94 (GRP94), and ER-localized DNAJ family 3A (ERDJ3A) polypeptides confirmed their higher accumulation in the plant expressing 16-kDa γ-zein. Expression of 27-kDa γ-zein significantly induced only BIP3 and ERDJ3A transcription even though an increase in GRP94 and BIP1/2 polypeptides also occurred in this plant. These results indicate a significant but weaker effect of 27-kDa γ-zein compared to 16-kDa γ-zein, which corresponds with the higher availability of 16-kDa γ-zein for BIP binding, and indicates subtle protein-specific modulations of plant UPR. None of the analyzed genes was significantly induced by PHSL or by a mutated, soluble form of 27-kDa γ-zein that traffics along the secretory pathway. Such variability in UPR induction may have influenced the evolution of storage proteins with different tissue and subcellular localization.

The evolution of seed storage proteins in grasses can be related to their increasing stress-inducing properties in the endoplasmic reticulum.

Introduction

In storage tissues of developing seeds, endoplasmic reticulum (ER) homeostasis is challenged by the massive synthesis of storage proteins synthesized by ribosomes attached to the ER and co-translationally inserted into its lumen ( Vitale and Boston, 2008 ). This causes a stress similar, at least in part, to that imposed by the synthesis of secretory proteins with genetic mutations that hamper proper folding or by certain environmental stresses ( Vitale and Boston, 2008 ; Bao and Howell, 2017 ). ER stress activates signaling mechanisms, collectively termed unfolded protein response (UPR), leading to the increase of ER residents that help the folding of newly synthesized polypeptides, as well as a general reduction in the synthesis of proteins that enter the ER (Liu and Howell, 2010 ; Angelos et al., 2017 ). If ER stress cannot be relieved, UPR can eventually induce autophagy and cell death ( Liu et al., 2012 ; Mishiba et al., 2013 ; Yang et al., 2016 ). The details and additional consequences can vary, since several UPR branches and processes have been identified, with differences among kingdoms ( Hollien, 2013 ). In addition, different folding helpers and enzymes are involved in distinct steps of protein maturation in the ER, such as folding into secondary and tertiary structure, disulfide-bond formation, glycosylation, proline isomerization, oligomerization, and general avoidance of irreversible aggregation ( Vitale and Boston, 2008 ; Liu and Howell, 2010 ).

Three major classes of seed storage proteins have been identified. After folding and assembly in the ER, the monomeric 2S albumins and the trimeric 7S/11S globulins accumulate in storage vacuoles, in most cases through traffic along the Golgi-mediated secretory pathway ( Shewry et al., 1995 ). Prolamins, which exist only in grasses, instead assemble into large, heterotypic polymers forming accretions, termed protein bodies (PBs) within the ER lumen ( Shewry et al., 1995 ; Pedrazzini et al., 2016 ). PBs, often round-shaped structures with diameter between 0.5 and 2.0 µm, are unable to traffic along the Golgi-mediated secretory pathway, although in certain cereals they can be incorporated into vacuoles by autophagy at late stages of seed maturation ( Shewry et al., 1995 ; Tosi, 2012 ). It is thus reasonable to hypothesize that the different classes of seed storage proteins challenge the ER folding machinery in protein-specific ways, but this remains to be investigated and compared in details.

As outlined above, prolamins are the most unique and structurally peculiar seed storage proteins. Typical prolamins evolved from 2S albumins by the addition of novel domains that promote extensive polymerization, mediated by disulfide bonds and by hydrophobic interactions ( Pedrazzini et al., 2016 ). The 27-kDa γ-zein (27γz), a major prolamin of maize (Zea mays ), is one of the first prolamins to be synthesized during maize seed development ( Lending and Larkins, 1989 ; Woo et al., 2001 ), is essential for the correct initiation of maize PBs ( Guo et al., 2013 ), and is able to form PB-like polymers also when expressed in transgenic plant vegetative tissues ( Geli et al., 1994 ). No crystal structure of any prolamin has been elucidated to date, but mutagenesis on 27γz has established that cysteine residues in its N-terminal domain form inter-chain disulfide bonds essential for its polymerization and its insolubility in the absence of reducing agents ( Mainieri et al., 2014 ). Eight consecutive repeats of the amphipathic proline-rich peptide VHLPPP also characterize the N-terminal domain and contribute to polymerization into PB ( Llop-Tous et al., 2010 ), whereas the C-terminal domain is a 2S albumin-like sequence.

A 27γz likely paralog, 16-kDa γ-zein (16γz), has evolved upon the whole-genome duplication that led to the present structure of maize genome ( Xu and Messing, 2008 ). The following features characterize 16γz with respect to 27γz ( Prat et al., 1985 ): the almost complete deletion of the proline-rich repeats; the absence of three out of the seven cysteine residues of the N-terminal domain; the change in position of one of the eight typical Cys residues of the C-terminal domain. 16γz is much less abundant than 27γz and resides at a precise location in maize PBs, at the border between the peripheral layer mainly composed of 27γz and 15-kDa β-zein and the major core of the PB, mainly composed of the alcohol-soluble α-zeins ( Yao et al., 2016 ). 16γz can be partially solubilized from maize PB with reducing agents or with alcohol, but neither of these solvents fully solubilize it ( Mainieri et al., 2018 ), supporting the hypothesis that 16γz connects the central PB core to the peripheral layer, favoring correct PB assembly.

IMMUNOGLOBULIN-BINDING PROTEIN (BIP), the ER chaperone of the HSP70 class, inhibits irreversible aggregation of folding/assembly intermediates of most secretory proteins ( Pobre et al., 2019 ). For example, association of BIP to PHASEOLIN (PHSL), the 7S vacuolar storage protein of common bean ( Phaseolus vulgaris ), occurs only before PHSL trimers assemble ( Vitale et al., 1995 ). However, BIP is found within cereal PB ( Li et al., 1993 ), and has prolonged interactions with 27γz ( Geli et al., 1994 ) as well as zeolin, a recombinant fusion between the 27γz N-terminal domain and PHSL ( Mainieri et al., 2004 ). This suggests that prolonged interactions with the ER folding machinery are involved in PB formation.

BIP is also the major controller of UPR ( Pobre et al., 2019 ). Two main UPR signal transduction branches are active in plants, involving respectively the INOSITOL REQUIRING ENZYME1 (IRE1) and the transcription factor BASIC LEUCINE ZIPPER 28 (bZIP28), transmembrane sensors that constitutively reside in the ER membrane and associate with BIP ( Liu and Howell, 2016 ). Increased demands for BIP create a competition for the chaperone ( Srivastava et al., 2013 ). Release of BIP allows IRE1 binding to unfolded or misfolded polypeptides in the ER lumen, in turn promoting its dimerization and nuclease activity ( Pincus et al., 2010 ). IRE1 thus splices bZIP60 mRNA, allowing the synthesis of the bZIP60 transcription factor, which activates UPR responsive genes such as the ER folding helpers ( Nagashima et al., 2011 ). IRE1 also degrades a significant proportion of mRNAs encoding secretory proteins ( Mishiba et al., 2013 ). Once freed from BIP, bZIP28 traffics to the Golgi complex, where proteolysis releases its cytosolic transcription factor domain, which enter the nucleus and also activates UPR-responsive genes. Another ER-located transcription factor precursor, bZIP17, is activated by salt stress through a pathway similar to that of bZIP28 ( Liu et al., 2007 ).

bZIP60 expression and unconventional splicing increase when synthesis of storage proteins is active in the endosperm of maize ( Gayral et al., 2017 ) and of Brachypodium distachyon , a grass evolutionarily close to wheat and barley ( Kim et al., 2017 ). However, it is not known if and how individual storage proteins challenge ER folding machinery. We have therefore investigated here the UPR effects of individual storage proteins that have different polymerization features, solubility properties and ability to traffic along the secretory pathway, by comparing transgenic Arabidopsis ( Arabidopsis thaliana ) constitutively expressing each of the four polypeptides described below. Schematic cartoons of these four polypeptides are presented in Supplemental Figure S1 .

27γzf and 16γzf are the full-length copies of zeins 27γz and 16γz, respectively, C-terminal tagged with the FLAG epitope ( Mainieri et al., 2018 , 2014 ). Both constructs have been expressed in transgenic Arabidopsis under CaMV35S constitutive promoter ( Mainieri et al., 2018 ). 27γzf forms insoluble, ER-located PBs and can be fully solubilized by buffer containing a reducing agent. 16γzf instead forms very large, stable reticulate structures, which markedly enlarge the ER lumen and are mostly insoluble unless reduced; however, a proportion of 16γzf remains in the ER as polymers that are soluble even in oxidizing conditions ( Mainieri et al., 2018 ).

1C-γ-zein (hereon 27γzf 1C, for better clarity) is a mutated form of 27γzf in which the first six cysteine residues have been mutated to serine, thus leaving only one of the Cys residues of the N-terminal domain available for inter-chain disulfide-bond formation ( Mainieri et al., 2014 ). By transient expression in tobacco protoplasts, it has been shown that the mutations largely inhibit the formation of insoluble polymers and lead to 27γzf 1C traffic along the secretory pathway, finally resulting in part to secretion and in part to vacuolar delivery ( Mainieri et al., 2014 ). Therefore, 27γzf 1C can be defined as a 27γzf mutant affected in solubility and ER retention. The PHSL construct used here is a mutated form of β-PHSL (a protein devoid of Cys residues), in which the second N-glycosylation site has been inactivated by a Thr to Phe mutation, to make its SDS–PAGE banding pattern and glycosylation status easier to identify ( Pedrazzini et al., 1997 ). When expressed in transgenic tobacco, PHSL correctly assembles into soluble trimers and efficiently traffics through the Golgi complex to the vacuole, like wild-type PHSL ( Pedrazzini et al., 1997 ; Mainieri et al., 2014 ).

We show here that 16γzf and, to a lesser extent, 27γzf induce UPR, whereas the traffic-competent 27γzf 1C and PHSL are not UPR inducers. This relates the ER stress effects of seed storage proteins to their ability to form insoluble, traffic-incompetent polymers. The stronger UPR effect of 16γzf compared to 27γzf is in agreement with its higher availability for BIP binding, pointing to subtle protein-specific UPR modulations. On the whole, such UPR-inducing variability may have influenced the evolution of storage proteins.

Results

16γzf activates bZIP60, but not bZIP28 or autophagy

To compare the UPR-inducing ability of different storage proteins, we individually expressed in transgenic Arabidopsis 27γzf, 27γzf 1C, 16γzf, and PHSL under the regulation of the constitutive 35SCaMV promoter, and we used Arabidopsis transformed with the empty vector as a control. The SDS–PAGE banding pattern of each recombinant protein was consistent in independent transgenic plants ( Figure 1, A and B ). As already reported ( Mainieri et al., 2018 ), 27γzf accumulated as polypeptides with apparent molecular mass around 30 and 40 kDa ( Figure 1A ). Most probably, the higher molecular mass of the 40-kDa polypeptide originates from post-translational hydroxylation of the abundant Pro residues ( Mainieri et al., 2018 , 2014 ), as also suggested by the fact that radioactively labeled newly synthesized polypeptides are only of the 30-kDa type ( Mainieri et al., 2018 , and see Figure 8A here). 16γzf migrates around 20 kDa and, in minor proportion, as dimers around 40 kDa, not fully disassembled by the SDS–PAGE buffer ( Figure 1A and see Mainieri et al., 2018 ). 27γzf 1C consists exclusively of a 30-kDa polypeptide, possibly because it rapidly traffics from the ER, and in all plants analyzed is present in much lower amounts than the wild-type form. Upon transient expression, 27γzf 1C is largely delivered to vacuoles or secreted ( Mainieri et al., 2014 ): in transgenic plants, the protein is probably unstable in vacuoles and the apoplast, which contain numerous proteases. Correct trimerization of PHSL leads to the expected sorting to vacuoles in transgenic tobacco leaves ( Pedrazzini et al., 1997 ). Because of the higher hydrolytic activity of vegetative vacuoles compared to storage vacuoles, the protein is cleaved in fragments migrating in the 20–25 kDa range, corresponding to approximately half of the polypeptide ( Pedrazzini et al., 1997 ). This processing is indicative of correct vacuolar sorting in vegetative tissues, since a secreted form, in which the vacuolar sorting signal has been deleted, and an ER-retained form, tagged with C-terminal KDEL, do not undergo fragmentation ( Frigerio et al., 1998 , 2001 ). In transgenic Arabidopsis leaves, PHSL ( Figure 1B ) behaves as in tobacco: the abundant 20- to 25-kDa fragments indicate efficient vacuolar sorting, whereas the minor amounts of intact polypeptides around 45 kDa represent the newly synthesized protein that has not yet reached the vacuole.

To reduce possible effects that may be simply due to differences in the number of recombinant polypeptides entering the ER, transgenic plants were selected in which the transgene expression levels were not markedly different. Transgene transcripts were measured by absolute quantitative PCR, using specific primers. The best available choices for 27γzf and 27γzf 1C plants had higher levels of transgene transcripts compared to 16γzf and PHSL plants, but statistical difference was only significant with respect to the PHSL plant ( Figure 1C ). These plants were used in the subsequent experiments.

Despite the variability in mRNA levels of bZIP60 in biological replicates, spliced bZIP60 , identified with primers specific for the processed form, was significantly, markedly increased by 16γzf expression, as was the ratio between the spliced and unspliced form ( Figure 2A ). No statistically significant increase was instead observed in plants expressing 27γzf, 27γzf 1C, or PHSL, although, after 16γzf plants, those expressing 27γzf had the highest mean levels of both spliced bZIP60 and of its ratio with the unspliced form. Protein blot analysis with anti-bZIP28 indicated that bZIP28 proteolytic maturation was not induced by any of the recombinant proteins, whereas it was clearly detected upon treatment with the N-glycosylation inhibitor tunicamycin (Tm), a chemical UPR inducer used as a control ( Figure 2B; total proteins stained with Ponceau S, as loading control, are shown in Supplemental Figure S2A ). No significant effect of any of the recombinant proteins was detected on bZIP28 and bZIP17 mRNA levels ( Figure 2A ).

Canonical UPR genes are differently induced by the proteins analyzed, pointing to 16γzf as the one with more extensive effects

We then investigated transcript levels of major Arabidopsis ER residents known to be positively regulated upon treatments with Tm or the reducing agent dithiothreitol (DTT), chemical UPR inducers that inhibit proper folding of many secretory proteins. Three BIP genes are present in arabidopsis: BIP1 and BIP2 have very similar sequences and are abundantly expressed in all tissues, whereas transcripts of BIP3 , a less conserved paralog, are almost undetectable in physiological conditions ( Martinez and Chrispeels, 2003 ; Noh et al., 2003 ). Tm and DTT lead to about two to four-fold increase of BIP1/2 transcription and about 40-fold increase of BIP3 ( Martinez and Chrispeels, 2003 ; Noh et al., 2003 ; Kamauchi et al., 2005 ). Transcripts of the ER-located hsp90 chaperone GLUCOSE REGULATED PROTEIN 94 ( GRP94 ) and the BIP co-chaperone ER-localized DNAJ family 3A ( ERDJ3A ) are also markedly induced by Tm or DTT ( Martinez and Chrispeels, 2003 ; Kamauchi et al., 2005 ; Klein et al., 2006 ; Iwata et al., 2008 ; Yamamoto et al., 2008 ). PROTEIN DISULFIDE ISOMERASE-LIKE 1-2 ( PDIL1-2 ), a member of the very large Arabidopsis PDI-Like family, had the highest increase in expression under UPR stress ( Kamauchi et al., 2005 ). In a search for genes upregulated by Tm, TUNICAMYCIN INDUCED PROTEIN 1 ( TIN1 ) was identified as the second most induced after BIP3 (Iwata et al., 2008 , 2010 ).

BIP3 and ERDJ3A transcripts were significantly increased in plants expressing the two ER-resident γ-zeins, more strongly in 16γzf plants, but were not induced by the traffic-competent 27γzf 1C mutant or by PHSL ( Figure 4 ). 16γzf was the only construct that also significantly induced BIP1/2 (the primers used in this work recognize both BIP sequences), GRP94 , PDIL1-2 , and TIN1 ( Figure 4 ). BIP3 , detected with specific primers, was more sensitive than BIP1/2 to 16γzf expression, consistent with the results obtained with chemical UPR effectors and the very low level of BIP3 expression in unstressed conditions.

Figure 4.

To verify if increased transcript levels result in higher protein accumulation, seedling extracts were analyzed by protein blot with specific antibodies. Seedlings were first homogenized in the absence of reducing agent, and the soluble material separated from the insoluble pellet by centrifugation. The two fractions were denatured in the presence of SDS and reducing agent and analyzed by protein blot with antibodies specific for tobacco BIP (which recognize Arabidopsis BIP1/2, these will be referred to as anti-BIP heron), BIP3, ERDJ3A, or GRP94. Representative blots are shown in Figure 5A (for each blot, total proteins stained with Ponceau S, as loading control, are shown in Supplemental Figure S2B ). The total amount (soluble plus insoluble) of each chaperone was quantified by densitometry of blot bands and expressed as increase versus the amount in the control plant ( Figure 5B ). 16γzf and 27γzf enhanced the accumulation of GRP94, BIP1/2, and ERDJ3A, the smaller γ-zein having a stronger effect. Of the two traffic competent soluble proteins, 27γzf 1C had no significant effect, whereas PHSL led to mild but significantly higher accumulation of BIP1/2 and GRP94. From the representative gel ( Figure 5A ), it can be appreciated that the increase of the otherwise almost undetectable BIP3 and ERDJ3A polypeptides was striking in 16γzf plants, and that the higher amount of BIP1/2 was especially evident in the insoluble fraction from plants expressing the two insoluble, ER-resident γ-zeins ( Figure 5A ). When direct comparison was performed between 27γzf and 16γzf plants, the induction of BIP3 by the latter was the only statistically significant difference ( Figure 5B ). A transgenic line, named 16γzf Low (16γzf L), in which expression of 16γzf was one-ninth relatively to the standard 16γzf line used in this study, also had significant induction of the analyzed UPR hallmarks ( Supplemental Figure S3 ), confirming the UPR-inducing property of this γ-zein. Protein blot analysis indicated that, at such lower expression level, 16γzf causes accumulation of BIP polypeptides to levels similar or lower than those induced by 27γzf, indicating a dose effect in UPR induction ( Supplemental Figure S4 ).

Figure 5.

Our experiments, unless differently stated, were performed on 20-d-old seedling, a growth period that we found most convenient to collect the material. We observed that at 7 d after sowing the relative extent of accumulation of BIP1/2, BIP3, or ERDJ3A polypeptides detected in the different transgenic plants does not change with respect to the amounts at 20 d ( Supplemental Figure S5 , and compare with Figure 5A ). This indicates that, when occurring, UPR induction depends on the recombinant protein and does not change during the first 3 weeks of growth. We conclude that the effects we detected on UPR sensors are in most part reflected in the actual increase of folding helper polypeptides known to be under UPR control. The effects of 16γzf can be distinguished from those of 27γzf when the individual chaperones are analyzed, pointing to subtle protein-specific modulations of plant UPR.

BIP and ERDJ3A can be further induced by Tm in plants expressing 16γzf

Given the bZIP28 activation by Tm but not by the recombinant proteins analyzed, and the observed protein-specific range of UPR induction, we tested the effect of Tm on the accumulation of BIP3 and AtERDJ3A, the two folding helpers most induced by 16γzf, in the different transgenic plants. Tm-induced increase of BIP3 and AtERDJ3A was very marked in all plants analyzed, even in those expressing 16γzf ( Figure 6 ). This indicates that the relatively high induction activity of the small γ-zein is anyway far from saturating UPR signal transduction.

Figure 6.

The chemical STF-083010 specifically inhibits X-BOX BINDING PROTEIN 1 mRNA splicing mediated by IRE1 in human myeloma cells ( Papandreou et al., 2011 ). Plants treated with this inhibitor, have a short root phenotype, similar to the one caused by ire1a/b double mutant of ( Zhang et al., 2016 ). Treatment of Arabidopsis Columbia-0 (AtCol-0) seedlings with STF-083010 in the absence or presence of Tm showed significant decrease in the spliced/unspliced bZIP60 mRNA ratio, measured by RT-qPCR of the two forms, confirming the results obtained in mammalian cells ( Figure 7A ). When 16γzf seedlings were similarly treated with STF-083010, accumulation of BIP3 decreased by 80%, and this treatment had a similar inhibitory effect when UPR was induced by Tm as a control ( Figure 7, B and C ). Therefore, IRE1-mediated bZIP60 splicing is required for the markedly increased BIP3 accumulation promoted by 16γzf. The residual increase in BIP3 level upon concomitant treatment with Tm and STF-083010 indicates that the additional UPR induction is only partially dependent on IRE1 activity and is thus probably also mediated by bZIP28 ( Figure 7B , lanes 1 and 4).

BIP associates more extensively to 16γzf than to 27γzf

The higher increase of BIP1/2 and BIP3 in 16γzf compared to 27γzf suggests that the smaller γ-zein could interact more extensively with BIP, thus leading to stronger UPR activation. To test the initial interactions of BIP with newly synthesized γ-zeins, vectors expressing 16γzf, 27γzf, or the empty vector as a control were transfected into tobacco protoplasts, which were then pulse-labeled for 1 h with a mixture of 35 S-labeled methionine and cysteine before immunoprecipitation with anti-FLAG or anti-BIP antibodies, SDS–PAGE and radiography. The anti-BIP antibodies used are able to co-immunoprecipitate intermediates of protein folding/assembly as well as structurally defective proteins, when these interact with BIP ( Vitale et al., 1995 ; Pedrazzini et al., 1997 ; Maitrejean et al., 2011 ). Protoplast homogenation was performed in the absence of reducing agent, to detect interaction of newly synthesized polypeptides that had not yet polymerized into insoluble polymers ( Mainieri et al., 2018 ). Almost the same amount of newly synthesized 16γzf polypeptides were immunoprecipitated with either antibody, whereas the amount of 27γzf was much lower with anti-BIP than with anti-FLAG ( Figure 8A ). This indicates that newly synthesized 16γzf is a preferred BIP substrate. Notice that the less intense labeling of 27γzf compared to 16γzf reflects in large part the fact that methionine, present in the two proteins as one and three residues, respectively, constitutes about 73% of the amino acid labeling mixture.

The persistence of the interaction between γ-zeins and BIP was analyzed by immunoprecipitation experiments at steady state. Transgenic arabidopsis homogenates were immunoprecipitated with anti-BIP or anti-FLAG antibodies. The immunoselected proteins were then analyzed by protein blot using anti-BIP antibodies. Besides the expected detection of BIP1/2 in all extracts immunoprecipitated with the same antibodies, anti-FLAG antisera co-immunoselected BIP1/2 from the 16γzf plant homogenate only, indicating a prolonged interaction over time with the chaperone, not occurring in the case of 27γzf ( Figure 8B ).

The results in Figure 8 indicate a more extensive BIP association to 16γzf than to 27γzf, detectable both when newly synthesized polypeptides were analyzed upon transient expression and when accumulation was at steady state in transgenic plants.

We analyzed in silico the potential BIP binding sites of the two γ-zeins, using the BIPPRED algorithm ( Schneider et al., 2016 ). BIPPRED operates by scanning the polypeptide sequence using a 7mer window and assigning scores based on known structural characteristics of 7mer peptides recognized by BIP. BIP binding scores in the N-terminal domain region, where sequence identity between the two zeins is lower, were markedly higher in 16γz, with the sequence spanning amino acids 33–49 steadily >0.8 ( Supplemental Figure S6 ).

Discussion

γ-zeins and UPR

Prolamin accumulation in the ER is a unique plant process, with no counterpart in other eukaryotes. We have shown here that 16γzf activates the bZIP60 branch of UPR in a process dependent on IRE1 RNAse activity, inducing higher transcription of UPR-induced genes and accumulation of their encoded proteins. To lower extents, BIP3 and ERDJ3A transcripts are also significantly increased by 27γzf expression, although in this case the observed bZIP60 induction was below the statistical significance threshold. 27γzf 1C and PHSL, which are competent for traffic from the ER, did not have significant effects on bZIP60 and the genes it regulates, apart from a minor but significant induction in the accumulation of BIP1/2 and GRP94 polypeptides by PHSL with respect to the control. Since these effects of PHSL were not reflected in transcript increases, there may be due to specific higher translational activity or chaperone stability, which was not investigated further.

The specific UPR induction by the two ER-resident insoluble zeins was confirmed when chaperone protein accumulation was compared in the different transgenic plants. It should be underlined that the higher levels of expression of 27γzf and 27γzf 1C transgenes with respect to PHSL (see Figure 1C ) do not affect, and actually strengthen, our conclusions on the high UPR induction by 16γzf with respect to the other constructs, and on the direct relationship between ER retention and UPR induction. Overall, these results indicate that model prolamins challenge the ER folding system more extensively than a model vacuolar storage globulin, relating the detected UPR activation to the ability of the two zeins to assemble into insoluble polymers that are unable to leave the ER. At least for 27γzf, analysis of plants expressing the mutated 27γzf 1C construct directly links the inducing activity to the disulfide-mediated formation of PBs.

Both zeins interact with the chaperone BIP, but the more extended interactions and higher BIP binding score of 16γzf may explain its stronger activation of UPR. The polymerization features of the two individually expressed zeins are different ( Mainieri et al., 2018 ): relatively rapid assembly of 27γzf into insoluble PBs may eventually subtract the protein from interactions with the ER folding machinery, whereas the much slower and somehow permanently unresolved assembly of 16γzf in Arabidopsis ( Mainieri et al., 2018 ) may challenge the folding machinery for extended time ( Figure 9 ). In maize, the high affinity to BIP may help in maintaining 16γzf in a soluble form, until its interactions with both 27γzf and α-zeins promote ordered formation of the maize PB, a process that obviously cannot occur when 16γzf is individually expressed.

A recent study in human HeLa cells, in which the ER folding machinery was challenged by the expression of a defective polypeptide, has provided quantitative evidence that UPR activation is a consequence of insufficient BIP availability, due to its excessive sequestration by newly synthesized proteins with high affinity to this chaperone ( Vitale et al., 2019 ). UPR and ER expansion in HeLa cells are mainly driven by the ACTIVATING TRANSCRIPTION FACTOR 6, the homolog of bZIP28, with little role of the IRE1 and PROTEIN KINASE R-LIKE ER KINASEUPR branches ( Vitale et al., 2019 ). It is noteworthy that none of the proteins tested in the present work instead activated bZIP28 to a significant level. Our results obtained in Arabidopsis, therefore, indicate what seems to be a different use of UPR branches with respect to HeLa cells. The strong bZIP60-mediated induction of BIP3 by 16γzf is also consistent with the observation that BIP3 is more dependent on bZIP60 than on bZIP28, whereas the opposite is true for BIP1 and 2 ( Iwata et al., 2008 ), although both Arabidopsis BIP1 and BIP3 bind bZIP28 ( Srivastava et al., 2013 ). In this respect, one explanation for the discrepancy between HeLa cells and Arabidopsis could reside in the presence of multiple BIP genes in plants but not in other organisms.

Autophagy

The PB-forming chimeric protein zeolin ( Mainieri et al., 2004 , 2018 ) transiently expressed in Arabidopsis protoplasts induced both UPR and autophagy, indicated by the formation of autophagic bodies ( Yang et al., 2016 ). Conversely, we have shown here that 16γzf and 27γzf do not induce ATG8e , and even the high UPR inducing activity of 16γzf does not promote a significant increase in autophagic bodies. Examples of ER stress without autophagy induction have been recently reported in mouse and human cells ( Jiang et al., 2019 ; Shah et al., 2019 ). It should also be noticed that, in Arabidopsis, the induction of autophagy by ER stress is specifically dependent on the mRNA degrading activity of IRE1b, not on bZIP60 splicing ( Bao et al., 2018 ). Among the degraded targets are mRNAs synthesizing proteins that may directly interfere with the formation of autophagosomes. Consistently, overexpression of such targets represses ER stress-induced autophagy ( Bao et al., 2018 ). It is thus possible that prolonged high expression of the recombinant γ-zeins in transgenic plants similarly results in autophagy inhibition, a process that may be relevant for zein accumulation in maize endosperm. This hypothesis would contradict the observation that ATG8e , together with other genes positively involved in autophagy, is actually induced during endosperm development ( Gayral et al., 2017 ). However, it has been suggested this may be due to hypoxia and not to massive prolamin synthesis, since it mainly occurs in the central part of the endosperm, where oxygen is more limiting ( Rolletschek et al., 2005 ; Gayral et al., 2017 ) and synthesis of prolamins is lower with respect to the peripheral parts ( Woo et al., 2001 ; Gayral et al., 2017 ). Induction of UPR by wild-type zeins with concomitant inhibition of ER stress-associated autophagy may be not surprising: it is reasonable to hypothesize that prolamins must have evolved taking full advantage of the folding machinery to accumulate to very high amounts in the ER without inducing their own disposal.

The different extents of UPR induction provide clues on the process of PB formation and the evolution of prolamins

UPR induction in maize endosperm, originally detected as an increase in BIP accumulation, occurs when natural mutations negatively affect the structure of individual zein genes ( Galante et al., 1983 ; Boston et al., 1991 ; Zhang and Boston, 1992 ). Besides UPR activation, these mutants usually have misshapen PBs, overall reduction of zein accumulation and an opaque phenotype ( Zhang and Boston, 1992 ). One of them, mucronate , is a deletion that causes a frameshift mutation 65 residues from the C-terminus of 16γz, determining a novel open reading frame of 60 residues before a new stop codon ( Kim et al., 2006 ). Compared to wild-type 16γz, the mutated zein has lower solubility also in the presence of reducing agents and accumulates at lower amounts ( Kim et al., 2006 ). In agreement with our results of Tm treatments, these observations support the conclusion that wild-type 16γz is far from being an extensively defective protein, consistent with the view that a PB has much more the features of a very large heteropolymeric complex than those of an aggregate ( Pedrazzini et al., 2016 ), and that the individual zein genes expressed ectopically behave as polypeptides synthesized in the absence of partner subunits. Some of these subunits, such as 27γz, rapidly self-polymerize in the ER and thus markedly decrease their affinity to BIP (BIP avoidance; Figure 9 ), probably because are synthesized first and their main role is to establish interactions with the ER membrane and to form the scaffold where other subunits will find their location. Others, like 16γz, have roles in connecting different prolamin layers in an ordered fashion within the PB, and therefore their release from the folding machinery may depend more on such partners (BIP engagement; Figure 9 ).

The peculiar relationships between maize prolamins and UPR is further underlined by the recent discovery of UPR induction in quality protein maize (QPM), generated by breeders to restore the vitreous kernel phenotype in the opaque2 genetic background ( Li et al., 2020 ). The opaque2 mutation inhibits the activity of a transcription factor that supports many genes, including those of the very abundant α-zeins. Homozygous opaque2 has therefore a markedly decrease amount of zeins and, as a result, an increase in the relative amounts of the essential amino acids lysine and tryptophan, absent or nearly absent in α-zeins. Drawbacks of the mutation are the increased kernel fragility and susceptibility to pathogen attack associated to the opaque phenotype. The most important genetic feature of QPM is the duplication of the 27γz, fundamental to restore the vitreous phenotype and overcome the opaque2 drawbacks. Whereas the opaque2 mutation does not induce significant UPR, transcript analysis revealed that the major upregulated genes in QPM are related to chaperone binding, protein folding, and unfolded protein binding, including GRP94 , one of the two BIP genes, PDI and ERD3JA ( Li et al., 2020 ). Although protein interactions in the highly heteropolymeric natural PBs are most likely more complex than in cells of transgenic plants synthesizing individual zein polypeptides, the results of QPM analysis are consistent with the data presented here, supporting the hypothesis that chaperone induction in QPM could be directly due to the markedly enhanced γ-zein synthesis.

Soluble storage proteins, such as PHSL, are present in seeds of all species, but prolamins evolved later, with the appearance of grasses ( Shewry et al., 1995 ). Among the latter, 27γz and its orthologs in other grasses were among the oldest to evolve, whereas 16γz is particularly recent, having originated after the split of maize and sorghum from their unknown common ancestor ( Xu and Messing, 2008 ). The different UPR inducting activities of the three storage proteins studied here may be related to these evolutionary events. Soluble storage proteins accumulate in parenchyma cells of cotyledons, which are a living tissue at the end of seed maturation. It may be hypothesized that an excess of ER stress in these cells may compromise their activity during seed filling and, in turn, germination; low UPR-inducing properties may have therefore been favored in the evolution of abundant cotyledonary storage proteins. The endosperm of grasses, where prolamins are synthesized, instead undergoes programmed cell death at the end of seed development: a destiny that would make the higher ER stress imposed by the massive synthesis of proteins such as 27γz a less challenging drawback in terms of evolutionary fitness. The even higher ER UPR-inducing properties of 16γz might be detrimental even for endosperm development, especially under environmental stress that is easily encountered during seed filling. However, the role of 16yz in PB assembly does not require its presence in high amounts; given the dose effect of UPR induction we have shown here, this feature and the interactions with 27γz, which rapidly lead to incorporation of 16γz into insoluble polymers ( Mainieri et al., 2018 ) might have allowed the evolution of this peculiar prolamin.

Materials and methods

Plant material

Transgenic Arabidopsis plants ( A. thaliana ecotype Col-0) transformed with empty vector (Co, control plant) or constructs encoding for the different storage proteins (27γzf; 16γzf; PHSL; 27γzf 1C) were grown in sterile conditions on half-concentrated Murashige and Skoog (MS) media (Duchefa Biochemie, Haarlem, The Netherlands), 10 g/L Sucrose, with or without 0.8% (w/v) phyto agar (Duchefa Biochemie) at 23°C under a 16-h light/8-h light cycle.

Antibodies

The following antisera or antibodies were used, at the indicated dilutions. Rabbit polyclonal anti-FLAG (1:2,000; Sigma Aldrich, St Louis, MO, USA); rabbit polyclonal anti-PHSL (1:5,000; Pedrazzini et al., 1997 ); rabbit polyclonal anti-tobacco BIP (1:10,000; Pedrazzini et al., 1997 ); rabbit polyclonal anti-GRP94 (1:1,000; Klein et al., 2006 ); rabbit polyclonal anti-BIP3, kindly provided by Prof. S. Nishikawa (1:2,000; Maruyama et al., 2014 ); rabbit polyclonal anti-ERDJ3A, kindly provided by Prof. S. Nishikawa (1:5,000 dilution; Yamamoto et al., 2008 ); rabbit polyclonal anti-bZIP28, kindly provided by Prof. N. Koizumi (1:10,000; Nagashima et al., 2011 ); goat anti-rabbit IgG-peroxidase conjugate (1:16,000; Pierce Biotechnology Rockford, IL, USA).

Plasmid construction

Construction of the pDHA vector containing the coding sequence of 27-kD γ-zein 1C followed by the FLAG epitope DYKDDDDK (termed here 27γzf 1C) has been described ( Mainieri et al., 2014 ). The EcoRI fragments containing 27γzf 1C expression cassettes were excised from the pDHA vector and subcloned into EcoRI-linearized pGreenII0179 binary vector for plant stable transformation ( http://www.pgreen.ac.uk , John Innes Centre, Norwich, Norfolk, UK). Agrobacterium tumefaciens strain GV3101, containing the pSoup helper plasmid, was transformed with the pGreenII empty vector, as control, or the pGreenII-27γzf 1C resulting construct by electroporation. Agrobacterium tumefaciens strain LBA4404 transformed with the pGA470 vector containing the coding sequence of a PHSL construct in which the second N-glycosylation consensus site has been inactivated (T343F, here termed PHSL for more clarity) has been previously described (pGA-T343F; Pedrazzini et al., 1997 ).

Generation of transgenic Arabidopsis plants

Transgenic Arabidopsis plants expressing 16γzf or 27γzf have been generated in a previous study ( Mainieri et al., 2018 ). Transgenic Arabidopsis plants expressing 27γzf 1C were generated by floral dipping with A. tumefaciens strain GV3101 containing pGreenII-27γzf 1C. Arabidopsis control plants were generated by floral dipping with A. tumefaciens strain GV3101 containing the pGreenII0179 empty vector. Hygromycin-resistant plants (T0) were selected and the homozygous progenies were isolated. Transgenic Arabidopsis plants expressing PHSL were generated by floral dipping with A. tumefaciens LBA4404 transformed with pGA-T343F. Kanamycin-resistant plants (T0) were identified and the homozygous progenies were selected. Experiments were performed using T3 or successive plant generations.

RNA isolation and RT-qPCR

Total RNA was isolated from 20-d-old seedlings of different transgenic lines using TRIzol (Ambion by Life Technologies, Waltham, MA, USA), following the manufacturer’s instructions. After Turbo DNAase (Ambion) treatment, RNA concentration and quality were determined by spectrophotometry and agarose–formaldehyde gel electrophoresis, respectively. One microgram RNA was reverse transcribed to cDNA by SuperScript IV (Invitrogen, Waltham, MA, USA) with oligo d(T) 20 primers. Real-time PCR was performed in the ABI Prism 7300 Sequence Detection System (Applied Biosystems, Waltham, MA, USA) using the SYBR Green (Applied Biosystem) master mix and 4 µL of 1:8 diluted cDNA. All primers used in this study were designed online using Universal ProbeLibrary software (Roche life Science, https://lifescience.roche.com/en_it/brands/universal-probe-library.html#assay-design-center ) and are listed in Supplemental Table S1 . Spliced bZIP60 mRNA was identified with a primer specific for the processed mRNA. Reactions, performed in triplicate, contained 4 μL 1:8 diluted cDNA, 1 μL of each 10 μM forward and reverse primers, 10 μL of SYBR Green Buffer in a total volume of 20 μL. RT-qPCR was run as follows: 2 min, 50°C; 10 min (95°C; 15 s, 95°C, 1 min, 60°C) 40 cycles; 15 s, 95°C; 30 s, 60°C; 15 s, 95°C. Relative quantification of mRNAs was performed using comparison of threshold cycles, with ACTIN 8 (ACT8) as normalizer and the control plant (Co: AtCol-0 plant transformed with the empty vector) as calibrator. Data were expressed as fold change relative to the Co = 1 (relative expression level), calculated with the 2 − ΔΔCT method. For bZIP60 , ratios of fold changes of spliced/unspliced forms ( bZIP60s/bZIP60u) were also determined, setting ratios to 1 in Co ( Figure 2 ), or to 100% in AtCol-0supplemented with DMSO or TM ( Figure 7 ).

Unless otherwise specified, data are mean ± sd from four fully independent biological replicates.

Absolute qPCR for transgene mRNA quantification

Total RNA was extracted from Arabidopsis seedlings expressing 27γzf, 16γzf, 27γzf 1C, or PHSL and reverse transcribed to cDNA as described in the previous paragraph. Transgene mRNA levels were measured by absolute qPCR quantification using the standard curve method. Values were expressed as mRNA transgene copy number/ng total RNA and are mean ± sd from four fully independent biological replicates. Primers used are listed in Supplemental Table S2 .

Seedling treatments

Tm : 10- or 7-d-old seedlings of different transgenic lines and Co grown in 1/2 MS liquid medium, were incubated with 5 µg/mL Tm dissolved in 10 mM NaOH (+Tm) or 10 mM NaOH (−Tm, control) for 16 or 24 h in the dark. Seedlings were either homogenized or stained with MDC, and analyzed as indicated below.

IRE1 RNAse activity inhibitor : 9-d-old AtCol-0 or 16γzf seedlings, grown on 1/2 MS agar plates, were transferred in half MS liquid medium supplemented with 0.1% DMSO (solvent control), or 5 µg/mL (UPR-induced control), or 20 µM IRE1 RNAse activity inhibitor (STF-083010; Sigma-Aldrich, St Louis, MO, USA), or 5 µg/mL Tm and 20 µM STF-083010, and incubated in growth chamber for 42 h in the dark. Seedlings were homogenized in the presence of reducing agent (see below) and equal amounts of each homogenate were analyzed by SDS–PAGE, followed by protein blot with anti-BIP3 antibodies. Total proteins were stained with Ponceau S, for normalization and as loading control. The total amount of BIP3 in 16γzf treated seedlings was quantified by densitometry of its specific protein blot band, normalized on the amount of Rubisco large subunit (RbcL, empty arrowhead in A), and was expressed as percentage in the STF-083010 treated samples relatively to their untreated controls (DMSO = 100 or TM = 100). Data are mean ± sd from three fully independent biological replicates.

Protein analysis

Seedlings or leaves of different transgenic lines and control, grown for the indicated time, were homogenized with five volumes of homogenation buffer (150 mM Tris–Cl, pH 7.5, 150 mM NaCl, 1.5 mM EDTA, 1.5% Triton X-100, 4% 2-Mercaptoethanol, cOmplete™ Protease Inhibitor Cocktail [Roche, Basel, Switzerland]). In fractionation experiments, seedlings were homogenized in the absence of 2-Mercaptoethanol, and soluble and insoluble proteins were separated by centrifugation at 1,500 g av , 10 min, 4°C. Samples were then adjusted to 1.0% SDS, 4% 2-ME and analyzed by SDS–PAGE followed by protein blot incubation with the appropriate antibodies and detection with SuperSignal West Pico Chemiluminescent substrate (Thermo Scientific, Rockford, USA). Unstained Protein MW Marker (Fermentas, Vilnius, Lithuania) were used as molecular mass markers. For quantification analysis, the total amount of the indicated protein was quantified by densitometry of its specific protein blot bands using Image Lab software (BioRad, Hercules, CA, USA), normalized on the amount of Rubisco small (RbcS), and expressed as protein increase calculated as the Log2 of the fold change (Log2FC) relative the Co plant (Co = 1). Total proteins were stained with Ponceau S, for normalization and as loading control.

Immunoprecipitation from seedling homogenates

Twenty-day-old seedlings from Co, 27γzf or 16γzf transgenic lines were homogenized with five volumes of homogenization buffer in the absence of 2-ME as described in the previous paragraph. The homogenates were clarified by centrifugation at 1,500 g av for 2 min, 4°C in minifuge (Heraeus Biofuge). An aliquot of 100 µL of supernatants were immunoselected using anti-FLAG antibody or anti-BIP antiserum and protein A Sepharose (GE Healthcare, Chicago, IL, USA) as described ( Pedrazzini et al., 1997 ). Immunocomplexes were denatured and analyzed by 15% SDS–PAGE and protein blot with anti-BIP serum.

Transient expression in tobacco leaf protoplasts and pulse labeling with radioactive amino acids

Protoplasts were isolated from 4- to 7-weeks old tobacco leaves and subjected to polyethylene glycol-mediated transfection, as described ( Pedrazzini et al., 1997 ). An aliquot of 40 µg of pDHA plasmid without insert (as a negative control) or with the inserted recombinant sequence were used in each transfection, at a concentration of 10 6 cells/mL. After recovery overnight in the dark at 25°C in K3 medium, protoplast radioactive labeling was performed for 1 h at 25°C, using 35 S-labeled Met and Cys (Easytag, PerkinElmer), 100 μCi mL −1 . Protoplasts were collected by centrifugation at 60 g av for 10 min as described ( Mainieri et al., 2018 ). Collected protoplasts were homogenized with two volumes of ice-cold 150 mM NaCl, 1.5 mM EDTA, 1.5% Triton X-100, 150 mM Tris–Cl pH 7.5, supplemented with cOmplete Protease Inhibitor Cocktail. After centrifugation at 10,000 g av , the supernatants were immunoselected using anti-FLAG antibodies or anti-BIP antiserum and protein A Sepharose (GE Healthcare). Immunocomplexes were denatured and analyzed by 15% SDS–PAGE and radiography. 14 C-methylated proteins (Sigma-Aldrich) were used as molecular mass markers. Radioactive proteins were detected with the Starion FLA-9000 Phosphoimage System (Fujifilm, Tokyo, Japan) and quantified by TotalLab Quant (TotalLab, Newcastle upon Tyne, UK).

Fluorescence microscopy

Seven-day-old seedlings from control or 16γzf plants were treated with 5 µg/mL Tm (+TM) or 10 mM NaOH (–TM) for 16 h in the dark. Staining was with MDC (Sigma-Aldrich) at a concentration 0.05 mM in PBS for 10 min, followed by washing 3 times in PBS. Roots were placed on a microscope multiwell slide and visualized with a 40× objective mounted on an Axiovert 200 microscope (Carl Zeiss, Oberkochen, Germany) equipped for epifluorescence. MDC fluorescence was visualized by DAPI filter. Images were analyzed with ImageJ (Fiji) software using the “Multi Point” tool. Autophagosome numbers per cell are quantified as mean ± SD from three fully independent biological replicates, in three to six images per replicate, with four to nine cells per image.

Statistical analysis

RT-qPCR and protein quantification data were subjected to one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) statistical analysis to assess for differences in gene expression and chaperone polypeptide levels in the Arabidopsis transgenic lines. Data from RT-qPCR or protein quantification, expressed as fold change to control plants, were subjected to a log2 transformation prior to analysis to satisfy the assumption of homogeneity of variance. All the analyses were carried out using the software RStudio ( https://www.rstudio.com/ ) and the significance level was set to P ˂0.05 (* P < 0.05; ** P < 0.01; *** P < 0.001). The post hoc Tukey’s Honest Significant Difference test (TukeyHSD) was run to adjust P -values for multiple comparisons to determine which samples have significantly different means in paired sample comparison. Student’s t test was applied for samples <3. Data presented are mean ± sd from four or three fully independent biological replicates.
