The functional consequence of N6-methyladenosine (m6A) RNA modification is
mediated by "reader" proteins of the YTH family. YTH domain-containing 2
(YTHDC2) is essential for mammalian fertility, but its molecular function is
poorly understood. Here, we identify U-rich motifs as binding sites of YTHDC2 on
3' UTRs of mouse testicular RNA targets. Although its YTH domain is an
m6A-binder in vitro, the YTH point mutant mice are fertile. Significantly, the
loss of its 3'→5' RNA helicase activity causes mouse infertility, with the
catalytic-dead mutation being dominant negative. Biochemical studies reveal that
the weak helicase activity of YTHDC2 is enhanced by its interaction with the
5'→3' exoribonuclease XRN1. Single-cell transcriptomics indicate that Ythdc2
mutant mitotic germ cells transition into meiosis but accumulate a transcriptome
with mixed mitotic/meiotic identity that fail to progress further into meiosis.
Finally, our demonstration that ythdc2 mutant zebrafish are infertile highlights
its conserved role in animal germ cell development.
