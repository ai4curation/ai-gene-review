# Functionally important amino acids in Saccharomyces cerevisiae aspartate kinase.
**Authors:** Bareich DC, Wright GD
**Journal:** Biochem Biophys Res Commun (2003)
**DOI:** [10.1016/j.bbrc.2003.10.042](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.bbrc.2003.10.042)

## Abstract

1. Biochem Biophys Res Commun. 2003 Nov 21;311(3):597-603. doi:
10.1016/j.bbrc.2003.10.042.

Functionally important amino acids in Saccharomyces cerevisiae aspartate kinase.

Bareich DC(1), Wright GD.

Author information:
(1)Department of Biochemistry, McMaster University, 1200 Main St, W, Hamilton,
Ont, Canada L8N 3Z5.

Saccharomyces cerevisiae aspartate kinase (AK(Sc)) phosphorylates L-Asp as the
first step in the aspartate pathway responsible for the biosynthesis of L-Thr,
L-Met, and L-Ile in microorganisms and plants. Using site-directed mutagenesis,
we have evaluated the importance of residues in AK(Sc) that are strongly
conserved among aspartate kinases or in other small molecule kinases. Steady
state kinetic analysis of the purified AK(Sc) variants reveals that several of
the targeted amino acids, particularly K18 and H292, have important roles in the
enzymatic reaction. These results provide the first identification of amino acid
residues crucial to the action of this important metabolic enzyme.
