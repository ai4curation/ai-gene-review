The capacity to rectify DNA double-strand breaks (DSBs) is crucial for the
survival of all species. DSBs can be repaired either by homologous recombination
(HR) or non-homologous end joining (NHEJ). The long-standing notion that
bacteria rely solely on HR for DSB repair has been overturned by evidence that
mycobacteria and other genera have an NHEJ system that depends on a dedicated
DNA ligase, LigD, and the DNA-end-binding protein Ku. Recent studies have
illuminated the role of NHEJ in protecting the bacterial chromosome against DSBs
and other clastogenic stresses. There is also emerging evidence of functional
crosstalk between bacterial NHEJ proteins and components of other DNA-repair
pathways. Although still a young field, bacterial NHEJ promises to teach us a
great deal about the nexus of DNA repair and bacterial pathogenesis.
