# Molecular cloning and characterization of a novel human gene homologous to the murine ecotropic retroviral receptor.
**Authors:** Yoshimoto T, Yoshimoto E, Meruelo D
**Journal:** Virology (1991)
**DOI:** [10.1016/0042-6822(91)90748-z](https://doi.org/10.1016/0042-6822(91)90748-z)

## Abstract

1. Virology. 1991 Nov;185(1):10-7. doi: 10.1016/0042-6822(91)90748-z.

Molecular cloning and characterization of a novel human gene homologous to the
murine ecotropic retroviral receptor.

Yoshimoto T(1), Yoshimoto E, Meruelo D.

Author information:
(1)Department of Pathology, New York University Medical Center, New York 10016.

A novel human cDNA homologous to the murine ecotropic retroviral receptor was
cloned from a cDNA library derived from a human T-cell line. The human cDNA is
highly homologous to the murine counterpart (87.6% amino acid identity), and its
sequence predicts a protein with 629 amino acids (approximately 68 kDa), which
is 7 amino acids more than the murine counterpart (622 amino acids). The
predicted protein is highly hydrophobic and contains 14 potential
transmembrane-spanning domains. No other gene and protein with significant
homology to the cloned human gene and the predicted protein were identified by a
computer-based search of sequence data banks other than the murine T-cell early
activation gene (52.5% amino acid identity) and the murine ecotropic retroviral
receptor gene. The human gene is ubiquitously expressed in human tissues and
conserved among mammalian species. The genomic gene was also isolated from a
cosmid library derived from human lymphocytes, and its organization was
elucidated. The gene mapped to human chromosome 13.
