The nucleus-vacuole junction (NVJ) is a central membrane contact site in yeast
that connects the nuclear endoplasmic reticulum and the vacuole. This organelle
interface is heavily involved in the handling of lipids: it houses numerous
lipid metabolism enzymes and lipid transfer proteins, and acts as a hotspot for
lipid droplet biogenesis. The NVJ shows plastic responses to metabolic cues,
adapting both its architecture and its proteome. Many NVJ-resident proteins
display alternative, metabolically controlled localizations at other contact
sites. This close-knit communication with diverse cellular structures makes the
NVJ an important model for understanding general principles of contact site
regulation. In this Cell Science at a Glance article and the accompanying
poster, we highlight the multifunctionality and metabolic plasticity of the NVJ,
as well as its integration into the cellular contact site network.
