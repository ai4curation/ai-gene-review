# The Matrix Protein of Human Parainfluenza Virus Type 3 Induces Mitophagy that Suppresses Interferon Responses.

Mitophagy is a form of autophagy that selectively removes damaged mitochondria.
Impaired mitochondria can be tagged by the kinase PINK1, which triggers
recruitment of the E3-ubiquitin ligase Parkin and subsequent mitochondrial
sequestration within autophagosomes. We previously found that human
parainfluenza virus type 3 (HPIV3) infection induces autophagy, but the type and
mechanisms of autophagy induction remain unknown. Here, we show that matrix
protein (M) of HPIV3 translocates to mitochondria and interacts with Tu
translation elongation factor mitochondrial (TUFM). M-mediated mitophagy does
not require the Parkin-PINK1 pathway but rather an interaction between M and the
LC3 protein that mediates autophagosome formation. These interactions with both
TUFM and LC3 are required for the induction of mitophagy and lead to inhibition
of the type I interferon response. These results reveal that a viral protein is
sufficient to induce mitophagy by bridging autophagosomes and mitochondria.
