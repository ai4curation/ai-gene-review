# The leukocyte common antigen (CD45): a putative receptor-linked protein tyrosine phosphatase.
**Authors:** Charbonneau H, Tonks NK, Walsh KA, Fischer EH

1. Proc Natl Acad Sci U S A. 1988 Oct;85(19):7182-6. doi:

The leukocyte common antigen (CD45): a putative receptor-linked protein tyrosine
phosphatase.

Charbonneau H(1), Tonks NK, Walsh KA, Fischer EH.

Author information:
(1)Department of Biochemistry, University of Washington, Seattle 98195.

A major protein tyrosine phosphatase (PTPase 1B) has been isolated in
essentially homogeneous form from the soluble and particulate fractions of human
placenta. Unexpectedly, partial amino acid sequences displayed no homology with
the primary structures of the protein Ser/Thr phosphatases deduced from cDNA
clones. However, the sequence is strikingly similar to the tandem C-terminal
homologous domains of the leukocyte common antigen (CD45). A 157-residue segment
of PTPase 1B displayed 40% and 33% sequence identity with corresponding regions
from cytoplasmic domains I and II of human CD45. Similar degrees of identity
have been observed among the catalytic domains of families of regulatory
proteins such as protein kinases and cyclic nucleotide phosphodiesterases. On
this basis, it is proposed that the CD45 family has protein tyrosine phosphatase
activity and may represent a set of cell-surface receptors involved in signal
transduction. This suggests that the repertoire of signal transduction
mechanisms may include the direct control of an intracellular protein tyrosine
phosphatase, offering the possibility of a regulatory balance with those protein
tyrosine kinases that act at the internal surface of the membrane.
