# Cloning and characterization of human CADPS and CADPS2, new members of the Ca2+-dependent activator for secretion protein family.
**Authors:** Cisternas FA, Vincent JB, Scherer SW, Ray PN
**Journal:** Genomics (2003)
**DOI:** [10.1016/s0888-7543(02)00040-x](https://doi.org/10.1016/s0888-7543(02)00040-x)

## Abstract

1. Genomics. 2003 Mar;81(3):279-91. doi: 10.1016/s0888-7543(02)00040-x.

Cloning and characterization of human CADPS and CADPS2, new members of the
Ca2+-dependent activator for secretion protein family.

Cisternas FA(1), Vincent JB, Scherer SW, Ray PN.

Author information:
(1)Department of Molecular and Medical Genetics, University of Toronto, Toronto,
Ontario, Canada.

The recent identification of some of the components involved in regulated and
constitutive exocytotic pathways has yielded important insights into the
mechanisms of membrane trafficking and vesicle secretion. To understand
precisely the molecular events taking place during vesicle exocytosis, we must
identify all of the proteins implicated in these pathways. In this paper we
describe the full-length cloning and characterization of human CADPS and CADPS2,
two new homologs of the mouse Cadps protein involved in large dense-core vesicle
(LDCV)-regulated exocytosis. We show that these two genes have disparate RNA
expression patterns, with CADPS restricted to neural and endocrine tissues and
CADPS2 expressed ubiquitously. We also identify a C2 domain, a known protein
motif involved in calcium and phospholipid interactions, in both CADPS and
CADPS2. We propose that CADPS functions as a calcium sensor in regulated
exocytosis, whereas CADPS2 acts as a calcium sensor in constitutive vesicle
trafficking and secretion. CADPS and CADPS2 were determined to span 475 kb and
561 kb on human chromosomes 3p21.1 and 7q31.3, respectively. The q31-q34 of
human chromosome 7 has recently been identified to contain a putative
susceptibility locus for autism (AUTS1). The function, expression profile, and
location of CADPS2 make it a candidate gene for autism, and thus we conducted
mutation screening for all 28 exons in 90 unrelated autistic individuals. We
identified several nucleotide substitutions, including only one that would
affect the amino acid sequence. No disease-specific variants were identified.
