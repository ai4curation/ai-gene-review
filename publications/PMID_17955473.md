# Proteomic analysis of p16ink4a-binding proteins.
**Authors:** Souza-Rodrígues E, Estanyol JM, Friedrich-Heineken E, Olmedo E, Vera J, Canela N, Brun S, Agell N, Hübscher U, Bachs O, Jaumot M
**Journal:** Proteomics (2007)
**DOI:** [10.1002/pmic.200700133](https://doi.org/10.1002/pmic.200700133)

## Abstract

1. Proteomics. 2007 Nov;7(22):4102-11. doi: 10.1002/pmic.200700133.

Proteomic analysis of p16ink4a-binding proteins.

Souza-Rodrígues E(1), Estanyol JM, Friedrich-Heineken E, Olmedo E, Vera J,
Canela N, Brun S, Agell N, Hübscher U, Bachs O, Jaumot M.

Author information:
(1)Departament de Biologia Cellular i Anatomia Patològica, Facultat de Medicina,
Universitat de Barcelona, Spain.

The p16(ink4a) tumor suppressor protein plays a critical role in cell cycle
control, tumorogenesis and senescence. The best known activity for p16(ink4a) is
the inhibition of the activity of CDK4 and CDK6 kinases, both playing a key role
in cell cycle progression. With the aim to study new p16(ink4a) functions we
used affinity chromatography and MS techniques to identify new
p16(ink4a)-interacting proteins. We generated p16(ink4a) columns by coupling the
protein to activated Sepharose 4B. The proteins from MOLT-4 cell line that bind
to p16(ink4a) affinity columns were resolved by SDS-PAGE and identified by MS
using a MALDI-TOF. Thirty-one p16(ink4a) -interacting proteins were identified
and grouped in functional clusters. The identification of two of them,
proliferating cell nuclear antigen (PCNA) and minichromosome maintenance protein
6 (MCM6), was confirmed by Western blotting and their in vivo interactions with
p16(ink4a) were demonstrated by immunoprecipitation and immunofluorescence
studies. Results also revealed that p16(ink4a) interacts directly with the DNA
polymerase delta accessory protein PCNA and thereby inhibits the polymerase
activity.
