# Late-G1 cyclin-CDK activity is essential for control of cell morphogenesis in budding yeast.
**Authors:** Moffat J, Andrews B
**Journal:** Nat Cell Biol (2004)
**DOI:** [10.1038/ncb1078](https://doi.org/10.1038/ncb1078)

## Abstract

1. Nat Cell Biol. 2004 Jan;6(1):59-66. doi: 10.1038/ncb1078. Epub 2003 Dec 14.

Late-G1 cyclin-CDK activity is essential for control of cell morphogenesis in
budding yeast.

Moffat J(1), Andrews B.

Author information:
(1)Department of Medical Genetics & Microbiology, University of Toronto, 1 Kings
College Circle, Rm. 4287, Medical Sciences Building, Toronto, Ontario, M5S 1A8,
Canada.

The accurate spatial and temporal coordination of cell polarization with DNA
replication and segregation guarantees the fidelity of genetic transmission.
Here we report that in Saccharomyces cerevisiae, a build-up or burst of G1
cyclin-dependent kinase (CDK) activity through activation of the cyclin genes
CLN1,2 and PCL1,2 is essential for cell morphogenesis, but not for other events
associated with the G1-S-phase transition, including DNA replication. Strains
lacking a burst of late-G1 cyclin-CDK activity (LG1C(-)) undergo a catastrophic
morphogenesis and halt the nuclear cycle at the morphogenesis checkpoint in G2
phase. Consistent with a role in morphogenesis, the Pho85 G1 cyclins Pcl1 and
Pcl2 show a unique pattern of localization to sites of polarized cell growth,
and strains lacking PCL1 and PCL2 show genetic interactions with the cell
polarity GTPase Cdc42, its regulators and downstream effectors. Our data suggest
that inability to assemble a septin ring and localize the GTP exchange factor
Cdc24 at the incipient bud site may be the primary morphogenetic defects in
LG1C-depleted cells. We conclude that a burst of late G1 cyclin-CDK activity is
essential for establishing cell polarity and development of the cleavage
apparatus.
