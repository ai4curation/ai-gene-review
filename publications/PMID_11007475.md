Comment in
    Cell. 2000 Sep 1;102(5):541-4. doi: 10.1016/s0092-8674(00)00075-1.

The activation-induced cytidine deaminase (AID) gene, specifically expressed in
germinal center B cells in mice, is a member of the cytidine deaminase family.
We herein report mutations in the human counterpart of AID in patients with the
autosomal recessive form of hyper-IgM syndrome (HIGM2). Three major
abnormalities characterize AID deficiency: (1) the absence of immunoglobulin
class switch recombination, (2) the lack of immunoglobulin somatic
hypermutations, and (3) lymph node hyperplasia caused by the presence of giant
germinal centers. The phenotype observed in HIGM2 patients (and in AID-/- mice)
demonstrates the absolute requirement for AID in several crucial steps of B cell
terminal differentiation necessary for efficient antibody responses.
