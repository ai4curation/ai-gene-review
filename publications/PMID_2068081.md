Squalene synthetase (farnesyl-diphosphate: farnesyl-diphosphate
farnesyltransferase, EC 2.5.1.21) is a critical branch point enzyme of
isoprenoid biosynthesis that is thought to regulate the flux of isoprene
intermediates through the sterol pathway. The structural gene for this enzyme
was cloned from the yeast Saccharomyces cerevisiae by functional complementation
of a squalene synthetase-deficient erg9 mutant. Identification of this ERG9
clone was confirmed by genetic linkage analysis in yeast and expression of
enzyme activity in Escherichia coli. The predicted squalene synthetase
polypeptide of 444 amino acids (Mr, 51,753) lacks significant homology to known
protein sequences, except within a region that may represent a prenyl
diphosphate (substrate) binding site. The ERG9-encoded protein contains a PEST
consensus motif (rich in proline, glutamic acid, serine, and threonine) present
in many proteins with short cellular half-lives. Modeling of the protein
suggests that it contains at least one, and possibly two, membrane-spanning
domains. Disruption of the chromosomal squalene synthetase coding region by
insertional mutagenesis indicates that ERG9 is a single copy gene that is
essential for cell growth in yeast.
