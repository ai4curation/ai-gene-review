Chromosome segregation is an essential cellular process that has the potential
to yield numerous targets for drug development. This pathway is presently
underutilized partially due to the difficulties in the development of robust
reporter assays suitable for high throughput screening. In bacteria, chromosome
segregation is mediated by two partially redundant systems, condensins and
ParABS. Based on the synthetic lethality of the two systems, we developed an
assay suitable for screening and then screened a library of fungal extracts for
potential inhibitors of the ParABS pathway, as judged by their enhanced activity
on condensin-deficient cells. We found such activity in extracts of Humicola sp.
Fractionation of the extract led to the discovery of four new analogues of
sterigmatocystin, one of which, 4-hydroxy-sterigmatocystin (4HS), displayed
antibacterial activity. 4HS induced the phenotype typical for parAB mutants
including defects in chromosome segregation and cell division. Specifically,
bacteria exposed to 4HS produced anucleate cells and were impaired in the
assembly of the FtsZ ring. Moreover, 4HS binds to purified ParB in a
ParS-modulated manner and inhibits its ParS-dependent CTPase activity. The data
describe a small molecule inhibitor of ParB and expand the known spectrum of
activities of sterigmatocystin to include bacterial chromosome segregation.

of four new sterigmatocystin derivatives, together with three known
compounds, sterigmatocystin,22 3-deoxysterimatocystin,23 and methoxysterigmatocystin.24 Herein, we report the isolation, structure elucidation,

Sterigmatocystin
belongs to a broadly spread family of xanthone
derivatives. Several fungal species such as Aspergillus
versicolor,25Humicola sp.,23Actinoplanes sp.,26 and Ascodemis sphaerospora(27) are well-known as sources of this class of compounds. Xanthones
exhibit a variety of biological activities including antimicrobial,
cytotoxic, antitubercular, and antitumor.25,27,28 The xanthone moiety of sterigmatocystin
is conjugated to a furofuran. The furofuran group of sterigmatocystin
and its infamous derivative aflatoxin can undergo metabolic activation
and form an adduct with DNA,29−31 rendering the compounds carcinogenic,
mutagenic, and teratogenic.32−34 The activity of sterigmatocystin
analogues that we characterized in this study varied depending on
the chemical modification of the xanthone group, thereby implicating
it in the activity of the compound.

The most active isolated
