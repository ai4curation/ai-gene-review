# Lysine biosynthesis in Saccharomyces. Conversion of -aminoadipate into -aminoadipic -semialdehyde.
**Authors:** Sinha AK, Bhattacharjee JK
**Journal:** Biochem J (1971)
**DOI:** [10.1042/bj1250743](https://doi.org/10.1042/bj1250743)
**PMC:** [PMC1178178](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC1178178/)

## Abstract

1. Biochem J. 1971 Dec;125(3):743-9. doi: 10.1042/bj1250743.

Lysine biosynthesis in Saccharomyces. Conversion of -aminoadipate into
-aminoadipic -semialdehyde.

Sinha AK, Bhattacharjee JK.

The reduction of alpha-aminoadipate to alpha-aminoadipic delta-semialdehyde by a
cell-free extract of Saccharomyces is shown to be a three-step process. First
the amino acid reacts with ATP to form an adenylyl derivative. Then the adenylyl
derivative of alpha-aminoadipate is reduced in the presence of NADPH. In the
third step the reduced adenylyl derivative of the amino acid is cleaved to form
alpha-aminoadipic delta-semialdehyde. The presence of Mg(2+) is necessary for
the first and second steps. The third step does not need any cofactors. The
product of the first step was isolated by chromatography after incubating the
cell-free extract of Saccharomyces with alpha-aminoadipate, ATP and Mg(2+). The
isolated product was identified as an adenylyl derivative of alpha-aminoadipate
and could be converted into alpha-aminoadipic delta-semialdehyde under the
stated experimental conditions. The product of the second step was too unstable
to be identified.
