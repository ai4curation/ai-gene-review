# The transcriptional coactivator CAMTA2 stimulates cardiac growth by opposing class II histone deacetylases.

Postnatal cardiac myocytes respond to diverse signals by hypertrophic growth and
activation of a fetal gene program. In an effort to discover regulators of
cardiac hypertrophy, we performed a eukaryotic expression screen for activators
of the atrial natriuretic factor (ANF) gene, a cardiac-specific marker of
hypertrophic signaling. We discovered that a family of transcriptional
coactivators, called CAMTAs, promotes cardiomyocyte hypertrophy and activates
the ANF gene, at least in part, by associating with the cardiac homeodomain
protein Nkx2-5. The transcriptional activity of CAMTAs is governed by
association with class II histone deacetylases (HDACs), which negatively
regulate cardiac growth. Mice homozygous for a mutation in a CAMTA gene are
defective in cardiac growth in response to pressure overload and neurohumoral
signaling, whereas mice lacking HDAC5, a class II HDAC, are sensitized to the
prohypertrophic actions of CAMTA. These findings reveal a transcriptional
regulatory mechanism that modulates cardiac growth and gene expression by
linking hypertrophic signals to the cardiac genome.
