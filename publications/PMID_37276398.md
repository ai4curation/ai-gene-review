Osmotic stresses, such as drought and high salinity, adversely affect plant
growth and productivity. The phytohormone abscisic acid (ABA) accumulates in
response to osmotic stress and enhances stress tolerance in plants by triggering
multiple physiological responses through ABA signaling. Subclass III
SNF1-related protein kinases 2 (SnRK2s) are key regulators of ABA signaling.
Although SnRK2s have long been considered to be self-activated by
autophosphorylation after release from PP2C-mediated inhibition, they were
recently revealed to be activated by two independent subfamilies of group B
Raf-like kinases, B2-RAFs and B3-RAFs, under osmotic stress conditions. However,
the relationship between SnRK2 phosphorylation by these RAFs and SnRK2
autophosphorylation and the individual physiological roles of each RAF subfamily
remain unknown. In this study, we indicated that B2-RAFs are constantly active
and activate SnRK2s when released from PP2C-mediated inhibition by ABA-binding
ABA receptors, whereas B3-RAFs are activated only under stress conditions in an
ABA-independent manner and enhance SnRK2 activity. Autophosphorylation of
subclass III SnRK2s is not sufficient for ABA responses, and B2-RAFs are needed
to activate SnRK2s in an ABA-dependent manner. Using plants grown in soil, we
found that B2-RAFs regulate subclass III SnRK2s at the early stage of drought
stress, whereas B3-RAFs regulate SnRK2s at the later stage. Thus, B2-RAFs are
essential kinases for the activation of subclass III SnRK2s in response to ABA
under mild osmotic stress conditions, and B3-RAFs function as enhancers of SnRK2
activity under severe stress conditions.

## Full Text

Abiotic stresses, particularly osmotic stresses such as drought and high salinity, adversely affect plant growth, survival, distribution, and productivity (1–3). In response to osmotic stress, the levels of the phytohormone abscisic acid (ABA) are increased in plant cells, and the accumulated ABA enhances osmotic stress tolerance in plants by triggering multiple physiological responses through the ABA signal transduction pathway. As sessile organisms, plants are constantly exposed to changing water conditions. Therefore, the robustness of the ABA signal transduction system is critical for plants to adapt and survive under changing environmental conditions (4, 5).

SNF1-related protein kinase 2 (SnRK2) family proteins are central regulators of ABA signaling. Arabidopsis harbors ten members of the SnRK2 family, and they are categorized into three subclasses (I, II, and III). The subclass I–III SnRK2s are activated by osmotic stress, but only three subclass III SnRK2s, SRK2D/SnRK2.2, SRK2E/SnRK2.6/OST1, and SRK2I/SnRK2.3, are strongly activated by ABA and osmotic stress (6–8). These subclass III SnRK2s regulate ABA-mediated osmotic stress responses and act as essential positive regulators of ABA signaling downstream of pyrabactin-resistance1/PYR1-like/regulatory components of ABA receptors (PYR/PYL/RCARs) and group A protein phosphatase 2Cs (PP2Cs) (9–16). Under nonstress conditions in the absence of ABA, the kinase activity of subclass III SnRK2s is inhibited by PP2Cs (15–17). Under osmotic stress conditions, ABA accumulates at high levels, and the accumulated ABA binds its receptors, which subsequently inhibit PP2C activity, resulting in the release of SnRK2s from negative regulation (18, 19). Activated subclass III SnRK2s regulate various ABA responses, including the induction of many stress-responsive genes, through phosphorylation and activation of the bZIP-type transcription factors ABA-responsive element (ABRE)-binding protein/ABRE-binding factors (AREB/ABFs) (20–24). Activated subclass III SnRK2s also regulate stomatal closure in response to drought stress by activating slow anion channel-associated 1 (SLAC1) through phosphorylation (25–27).

Although subclass III SnRK2s have been thought to be self-activated by autophosphorylation after release from PP2C-mediated inhibition, specific protein kinases have recently been reported to phosphorylate and activate these subclass III SnRK2s. First, ABA AND ABIOTIC STRESS-RESPONSIVE RAF-LIKE KINASE/ABA NON-RESPONSIVE (ARK/ANR) was identified as a protein kinase that phosphorylates and activates SnRK2-type kinases in response to ABA and osmotic stress in the moss Physcomitrella patens (28, 29). ARK/ANR is a single-copy gene encoding a GROUP B3-RAF-LIKE MITOGEN ACTIVATED PROTEIN KINASE KINASE KINASE (B3 Raf-like kinase). Three ARK/ANR homologs, M3Kδ1/RAF3, M3Kδ6/RAF5/SIS8, and M3Kδ7/RAF4, were then identified as activators of subclass III SnRK2s under osmotic stress in Arabidopsis (30–32). Arabidopsis group B Raf-like kinases are divided into four subfamilies, B1 to B4, and these three ARK/ANR homologs are classified as B3 Raf-like kinases (B3-RAFs). Moreover, three B4 Raf-like kinases (B4-RAFs), RAF18, RAF20, and RAF24, have been identified as activators of ABA-unresponsive subclass I SnRK2s under osmotic stress (32, 33). Unlike subclass III SnRK2s, subclass I SnRK2s (subclass I SnRK2: SRK2A/SnRK2.4, SRK2B/SnRK2.10, SRK2G/SnRK2.1, SRK2H/SnRK2.5 and SRK2J/SnRK2.9 in Arabidopsis) are not activated by ABA and are directly activated by osmotic stress. They phosphorylate and activate the mRNA decapping enhancer VARICOSE, which controls the mRNA population to regulate plant growth (34–36).

On the other hand, one of the B2 Raf-like kinases (B2-RAFs), RAF10, was also reported to phosphorylate and activate subclass III SnRK2s by releasing them from PP2Cs (37), which suggests that in addition to B3-RAFs, B2-RAFs may also be involved in the activation of subclass III SnRK2s. Using multiple mutant plants, B2-RAFs and B3-RAFs were recently shown to phosphorylate and activate subclass III SnRK2s under osmotic stress, and phosphorylation by these RAFs has been indicated to be essential for the activation of subclass III SnRK2s (30–32, 37, 38). B2-RAFs and B3-RAFs regulate the ABA signaling pathway by phosphorylation and activation of ABA-responsive subclass III SnRK2s (31), whereas B4-RAFs regulate the ABA-independent signaling pathway by phosphorylation and activation of ABA-unresponsive subclass I SnRK2s (33). Thus, subclass III SnRK2s are activated under osmotic stress by two independent subfamilies of group B Raf-like kinases. However, the individual physiological roles of each RAF subfamily under osmotic stress conditions and the relationship between SnRK2 phosphorylation by these RAFs and SnRK2 autophosphorylation are still unknown (39–41). To elucidate the mechanism underlying the regulation of ABA-responsive subclass III SnRK2 activation, it is necessary to clarify the respective roles of B2-RAFs and B3-RAFs.

Here, we attempted to identify interactors of subclass III SnRK2s using co-IP coupled with LC–MS/MS analysis. Four B2-RAFs, but not B3-RAFs, were identified as interactors. We found that B3-RAFs strongly activate subclass III SnRK2s under drought stress conditions, whereas B2-RAFs are constantly active and regulate ABA-dependent activation of subclass III SnRK2s under drought stress conditions. Our findings also revealed the mechanisms underlying the regulation of subclass III SnRK2s under early and late drought stress conditions by two different subfamilies of group B Raf-like kinases.

These data indicate that the subclass III SnRK2-AREB1 signaling pathway was activated by the four B2-RAFs in vitro.

We then performed an in-gel kinase assay to analyze the activity of native B2- and B3-RAFs using SRK2DKN-MBP as a substrate. We purified GFP-tagged RAF11 (RAF11-GFP) and RAF5 (RAF5-GFP), which has been reported as one of the B3-RAFs involved in the regulation of subclass III SnRK2 activity under osmotic stress conditions (30, 38), from transgenic Arabidopsis plants expressing RAF11-GFP or RAF5-GFP by using an anti-GFP antibody. These Arabidopsis plants were treated with ABA or dehydration stress. Although the phosphorylation activity of RAF5-GFP was strongly enhanced under dehydration stress conditions, RAF11-GFP was found to constantly phosphorylate the substrate, regardless of ABA treatment and stress conditions (Fig. 2 D and E). These observations indicate that B2-RAFs are constitutively active and can constantly phosphorylate subclass III SnRK2s regardless of growth conditions. However, B3-RAFs are activated in response to dehydration stress and phosphorylate subclass III SnRK2s. These different activation mechanisms between the two independent Raf-like kinase subfamilies, B2-RAFs and B3-RAFs, indicate their different roles in the activation of subclass III SnRK2 in plants.

To determine whether subclass III SnRK2s are truly phosphorylated and activated by B2-RAFs in vivo, we generated a quadruple mutant of the four RAF genes, raf7 raf10 raf11 raf12 (raf7/10/11/12), by crossing four T-DNA insertion mutants of each gene into one another. RT–PCR analysis confirmed the absence of the expression of RAF7, RAF10, RAF11, and RAF12 in the raf7/10/11/12 mutant plants (SI Appendix, Fig. S8A). The growth phenotypes of the raf7/10/11/12 plants grown on agar plates were similar to those of the wild-type plants (SI Appendix, Fig. S8B). We performed an in-gel kinase assay to analyze the kinase activity of subclass III SnRK2s in the raf7/10/11/12 mutant. The kinase activity of native subclass III SnRK2s under dehydration stress was significantly decreased in raf7/10/11/12 and was clearly absent in the srk2d srk2e srk2i (srk2dei) mutant plants, which lacked all subclass III SnRK2s (Fig. 2F), indicating that these B2-RAFs activate subclass III SnRK2s under dehydration stress. The activity of subclass III SnRK2s in raf7/10/11/12 was clearly reduced compared with that of the wild type in the early stage of dehydration stress (0 to 60 min), but subclass III SnRK2s were activated in the later stage of dehydration stress (120 to 240 min) in raf7/10/11/12 (Fig. 2F). These results suggest that RAF7/10/11/12 positively regulate the activity of subclass III SnRK2s in the early stage of the drought stress response, but in the later stage, the other kinases may be involved in activation. Then, we generated a triple mutant of B3-RAF genes, raf3 raf4 raf5 (raf3/4/5), which have been reported as functional B3-RAFs (30), by crossing three T-DNA insertion mutants of each gene with one another (SI Appendix, Fig. S9) to elucidate whether B3-RAFs are involved in the activation of subclass III SnRK2s in the late stage of dehydration stress. We performed an in-gel kinase assay to analyze the kinase activity of subclass III SnRK2s under dehydration stress in the raf7/10/11/12 and raf3/4/5 mutants. The activity of subclass III SnRK2s in raf3/4/5 was similar to that of the wild type in the early stage of dehydration stress (0 to 60 min) but was lower than that of the wild type in the late stage of dehydration stress (120 to 240 min) (Fig. 2G). These results suggest that the B2-RAFs RAF7/10/11/12 positively regulate the activity of subclass III SnRK2s in the early stage of dehydration stress, but the B3-RAFs RAF3/4/5 positively regulate their activity in the late stage of dehydration stress. Next, we checked the activity of subclass III SnRK2s in response to ABA treatment. Subclass III SnRK2s were barely activated in raf7/10/11/12 but were activated similarly in raf3/4/5 compared to those in the wild type in the presence of ABA (Fig. 2H). These findings indicate that B2-RAFs regulate subclass III SnRK2s in the early stage of dehydration stress in an ABA-dependent manner, whereas B3-RAFs activate subclass III SnRK2s in the later stage of dehydration in an ABA-independent manner.

DiscussionSubclass III SnRK2s have been thought to be activated by autophosphorylation via derepression of group A PP2Cs by ABA-binding PYR/PYL/RCARs (9–14, 16, 22, 47). Recently, two subfamilies of group B Raf-like kinases, B3-RAFs and B2-RAFs, were shown to function in the phosphorylation and activation of subclass III SnRK2s in response to osmotic stress (30–32, 37, 38). Subclass III SnRK2s have been thought to be activated by autophosphorylation via derepression of group A PP2Cs by ABA-binding PYR/PYL/RCARs (9–14, 16, 22, 47).
