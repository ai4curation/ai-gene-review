Stable anchoring of titin within the muscle Z-disk is essential for preserving
muscle integrity during passive stretching. One of the main candidates for
anchoring titin in the Z-disk is the actin cross-linker α-actinin. The
calmodulin-like domain of α-actinin binds to the Z-repeats of titin. However,
the mechanical and kinetic properties of this important interaction are still
unknown. Here, we use a dual-beam optical tweezers assay to study the mechanics
of this interaction at the single-molecule level. A single interaction of
α-actinin and titin turns out to be surprisingly weak if force is applied.
Depending on the direction of force application, the unbinding forces can more
than triple. Our results suggest a model where multiple α-actinin/Z-repeat
interactions cooperate to ensure long-term stable titin anchoring while allowing
the individual components to exchange dynamically.
