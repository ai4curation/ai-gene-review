# A nucleolus-localized activator of Cdc14 phosphatase supports rDNA segregation in yeast mitosis.
**Authors:** Geil C, Schwab M, Seufert W
**Journal:** Curr Biol (2008)
**DOI:** [10.1016/j.cub.2008.06.025](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2008.06.025)

## Abstract

1. Curr Biol. 2008 Jul 8;18(13):1001-5. doi: 10.1016/j.cub.2008.06.025.

A nucleolus-localized activator of Cdc14 phosphatase supports rDNA segregation
in yeast mitosis.

Geil C(1), Schwab M, Seufert W.

Author information:
(1)Department of Genetics, University of Regensburg, D-93040 Regensburg,
Germany.

Cdc14 phosphatase is an important regulator of mitosis in budding yeast. Cdc14
antagonizes cyclin-dependent kinases and promotes multiple postmetaphase events,
including segregation of the ribosomal RNA gene array (rDNA) and the nucleolus
assembled around this gene cluster. During most of the cell cycle, Cdc14 is
anchored to the nucleolus and kept inactive by binding to Net1 (also known as
Cfi1). Cdc14 and Net1 are part of a larger nucleolar-protein network, which also
contains the Net1-related protein Tof2. Tof2 contributes to the transcriptional
silencing of rDNA regions, but the precise cellular and molecular functions of
Tof2 remain unclear. Here, we report that, like Net1, Tof2 can bind to Cdc14
directly. Unlike Net1, however, Tof2 did not inhibit Cdc14 but supported Cdc14
phosphatase activity and in vivo function. Deletion of TOF2 delayed rDNA
segregation with little effect on mitotic exit, impaired relocalization of
condensin to the nucleolus in anaphase, and caused rDNA-dependent synthetic
lethality when a cdc14 mutation was present. Thus, Tof2 collaborates with Cdc14
specifically in rDNA segregation, presumably by targeting Cdc14 phosphatase
activity to the nucleolus during anaphase to support resolution and compaction
of this repetitive and highly transcribed DNA locus.
