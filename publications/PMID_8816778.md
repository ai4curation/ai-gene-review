# The endothelial cell protein C receptor augments protein C activation by the thrombin-thrombomodulin complex.
**Authors:** Stearns-Kurosawa DJ, Kurosawa S, Mollica JS, Ferrell GL, Esmon CT
**Journal:** Proc Natl Acad Sci U S A (1996)
**DOI:** [10.1073/pnas.93.19.10212](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.93.19.10212)
**PMC:** [PMC38363](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC38363/)

## Abstract

1. Proc Natl Acad Sci U S A. 1996 Sep 17;93(19):10212-6. doi:
10.1073/pnas.93.19.10212.

The endothelial cell protein C receptor augments protein C activation by the
thrombin-thrombomodulin complex.

Stearns-Kurosawa DJ(1), Kurosawa S, Mollica JS, Ferrell GL, Esmon CT.

Author information:
(1)Cardiovascular Biology Research Program, Oklahoma Medical Research
Foundation, Oklahoma City, USA.

Protein C activation on the surface of the endothelium is critical to the
negative regulation of blood coagulation. We now demonstrate that monoclonal
antibodies that block protein C binding to the endothelial cell protein C
receptor (EPCR) reduce protein C activation rates by the thrombin-thrombomodulin
complex on endothelium, but that antibodies that bind to EPCR without blocking
protein C binding have no effect. The kinetic result of blocking the
EPCR-protein C interaction is an increased apparent Km for the activation
without altering the affinity of thrombin for thrombomodulin. Activation rates
of the protein C derivative lacking the gamma-carboxyglutamic acid domain, which
is required for binding to EPCR, are not altered by the anti-EPCR antibodies.
These data indicate that the protein C activation complex involves protein C,
thrombin, thrombomodulin, and EPCR. These observations open new questions about
the control of coagulation reactions on vascular endothelium.
