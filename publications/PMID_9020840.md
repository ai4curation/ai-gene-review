# A human homologue of the Drosophila eyes absent gene underlies branchio-oto-renal (BOR) syndrome and identifies a novel gene family.

## Abstract

1. Nat Genet. 1997 Feb;15(2):157-64. doi: 10.1038/ng0297-157.

A human homologue of the Drosophila eyes absent gene underlies
branchio-oto-renal (BOR) syndrome and identifies a novel gene family.

Cruaud C, Sahly I, Leibovici M, Bitner-Glindzicz M, Francis M, Lacombe D,
Vigneron J, Charachon R, Boven K, Bedbeder P, Van Regemorter N, Weissenbach J,

Author information:
(1)Unité de Génétique Moléculaire Humaine, URA CNRS 1968, Institut Pasteur,

A candidate gene for Branchio-Oto-Renal (BOR) syndrome was identified at
chromosome 8q13.3 by positional cloning and shown to underlie the disease. This
gene is a human homologue of the Drosophila eyes absent gene (eya), and was
therefore called EYA1. A highly conserved 271-amino acid C-terminal region was
also found in the products of two other human genes (EYA2 and EYA3),
demonstrating the existence of a novel gene family. The expression pattern of
the murine EYA1 orthologue, Eya1, suggests a role in the development of all
components of the inner ear, from the emergence of the otic placode. In the
developing kidney, the expression pattern is indicative of a role for Eya1 in
the metanephric cells surrounding the 'just-divided' ureteric branches.
