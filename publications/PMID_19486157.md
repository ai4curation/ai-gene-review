# The major outer membrane protein OprG of Pseudomonas aeruginosa contributes to cytotoxicity and forms an anaerobically regulated, cation-selective channel.
**Authors:** McPhee JB, Tamber S, Bains M, Maier E, Gellatly S, Lo A, Benz R, Hancock RE
**Journal:** FEMS Microbiol Lett (2009)
**DOI:** [10.1111/j.1574-6968.2009.01651.x](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1574-6968.2009.01651.x)

## Abstract

1. FEMS Microbiol Lett. 2009 Jun;296(2):241-7. doi:
10.1111/j.1574-6968.2009.01651.x. Epub 2009 May 14.

The major outer membrane protein OprG of Pseudomonas aeruginosa contributes to
cytotoxicity and forms an anaerobically regulated, cation-selective channel.

McPhee JB(1), Tamber S, Bains M, Maier E, Gellatly S, Lo A, Benz R, Hancock RE.

Author information:
(1)Centre for Microbial Diseases and Immunity Research, University of British
Columbia, Lower Mall Research Station, Vancouver, BC, Canada.

OprG of Pseudomonas aeruginosa is a member of the very large and widely
distributed but poorly characterized OmpW (PF0392) family of outer membrane
proteins. It was established here that OprG was highly transcribed in anaerobic
environments rich in iron via the ANR regulator. In the absence of OprG, P.
aeruginosa was significantly less cytotoxic toward human bronchial epithelial
cells. Planar bilayer studies indicated that purified OprG formed
cationic-selective channels with a conductance of 500 pS in 1 M KCl; however,
contrary to previous reports, OprG did not appear to be involved in either iron
or antibiotic uptake.
