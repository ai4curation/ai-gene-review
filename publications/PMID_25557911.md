# Cyclin F suppresses B-Myb activity to promote cell cycle checkpoint control.

Cells respond to DNA damage by activating cell cycle checkpoints to delay
proliferation and facilitate DNA repair. Here, to uncover new checkpoint
regulators, we perform RNA interference screening targeting genes involved in
ubiquitylation processes. We show that the F-box protein cyclin F plays an
important role in checkpoint control following ionizing radiation. Cyclin
F-depleted cells initiate checkpoint signalling after ionizing radiation, but
fail to maintain G2 phase arrest and progress into mitosis prematurely.
Importantly, cyclin F suppresses the B-Myb-driven transcriptional programme that
promotes accumulation of crucial mitosis-promoting proteins. Cyclin F interacts
with B-Myb via the cyclin box domain. This interaction is important to suppress
cyclin A-mediated phosphorylation of B-Myb, a key step in B-Myb activation. In
summary, we uncover a regulatory mechanism linking the F-box protein cyclin F
with suppression of the B-Myb/cyclin A pathway to ensure a DNA damage-induced
checkpoint response in G2.
