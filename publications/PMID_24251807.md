# Adenomatous polyposis coli (APC) membrane recruitment 3, a member of the APC membrane recruitment family of APC-binding proteins, is a positive regulator of Wnt-β-catenin signalling.
**Authors:** Brauburger K, Akyildiz S, Ruppert JG, Graeb M, Bernkopf DB, Hadjihannas MV, Behrens J
**Journal:** FEBS J (2014)
**DOI:** [10.1111/febs.12624](https://doi.org/10.1111/febs.12624)

## Abstract

1. FEBS J. 2014 Feb;281(3):787-801. doi: 10.1111/febs.12624. Epub 2013 Dec 12.

Adenomatous polyposis coli (APC) membrane recruitment 3, a member of the APC
membrane recruitment family of APC-binding proteins, is a positive regulator of
Wnt-β-catenin signalling.

Brauburger K(1), Akyildiz S, Ruppert JG, Graeb M, Bernkopf DB, Hadjihannas MV,
Behrens J.

Author information:
(1)Nikolaus Fiebiger Centre for Molecular Medicine, University
Erlangen-Nuremberg, Erlangen, Germany.

The adenomatous polyposis coli (APC) membrane recruitment (Amer) family proteins
Amer1/Wilms tumour gene on the X chromosome and Amer2 are binding partners of
the APC tumour suppressor protein, and act as negative regulators in the Wnt
signalling cascade. So far, nothing has been known about the third member of the
family, Amer3. Here we show that Amer3 binds to the armadillo repeat domain of
APC, similarly to Amer1 and Amer2. Amer3 also binds to the Wnt pathway regulator
conductin/axin2. Furthermore, we identified Amer1 as binding partner of Amer3.
Whereas Amer1 and Amer2 are linked to the plasma membrane by an N-terminal
membrane localization domain, Amer3 lacks this domain. Amer3 localizes to the
cytoplasm and nucleus of epithelial cells, and this is dependent on specific
nuclear import and export sequences. Functionally, exogenous Amer3 enhances the
expression of a β-catenin/T-cell factor-dependent reporter gene, and knockdown
of endogenous Amer3 reduces Wnt target gene expression in colorectal cancer
cells. Thus, Amer3 acts as an activator of Wnt signalling, in contrast to Amer1
and Amer2, which are inhibitors, suggesting a nonredundant role of Amer proteins
in the regulation of this pathway. Our data, together with those of previous
studies, provide a comprehensive picture of similarities and differences within
the Amer protein family.
