DISCUSSION
It has been confirmed that partially purified 
homoserine dehydrogenase of B. flavusn is 
strongly inhibited by low concentrations of

threonine as reported with various bacteria 
and yeast ( 12). The inhibition curve is sig
moidal, indicating that threonine is an allosteric 
inhibitor. Threonine inhibits the enzyme in 
the mixed manner with both ASA and NADPH ; 
thus, threonine gives rise to strong inhibition 
by binding with the enzyme at a sitedifferent 
from the sites for both substrates.
The enzyme activity is also inhibited by 
isoleucine at higher concentrations and the 
inhibition curve becomes sigmoidal as in the 
case of threonine. The inhibition type, how
ever, is competitive with both substrates in 
contrast with that for threonine. It has been 
reported with Rhodospirillum rubru»t (13 ( that 
isoleucine reverses the inhibition by threonine. 
With B. fiavutn, however, isoleucine itself is 
an inhibitor of the enzyme and it neither 
recovers the inhibition by threonine nor in
creases the inhibition significantly. Aspartate 
family amino acids except threonine and iso
leucine did not show any significant inhibition 
nor activation. Datta reported that homoserine 
dehydrogenases from some photosynthetic bac
teria were fairly susceptible to inhibition by 
cysteine and it was also the case with Esch
erichia coli ( 11 ). With B. flavurn, however, 
cystein is not a so strong inhibitor for homo
serine dehydrogenase. Thus, the rate of in
hibition by it is 121 at 1 mm, and only 40%, 
even at 10 mM (not shown in Table II).
It is of interest that the inhibition by 
threonine or isoleucine depends on pH of 
the reaction media and at the lower pH, the 
stronger inhibition is observed.
Double reciprocal plots of the reaction rate 
against NADPH concentration in the presence 
of threonine or isoleucine do not give a straight 
line and indicate the existence of homotropic 
cooperativity of NADPH, which means that 
homoserine dehydrogenase of B. flavui n is an 
allosteric enzyme, and threonine and isoleucine 
are allosteric inhibitors.
Double reciprocal plots for the reaction 
rate against the concentrations of one sub
strate at various fixed levels of another sub
strate gave straight lines. Homoserine and 
NADP+, the products of the reaction, inhibits 
the activity in the mixed and non-competitive 
manner with ASA, respectively, and in the
mixed and competitive manner with NADPH. 
From these results, the mechanism for the 
reaction catalyzed by homoserine dehydrogenase 
of B. flavurn seems to be "Ordered Bi Bi " 
according to the report of Cleland ( 15). Thus, 
it can be considered that, NADPH firstly, then 
ASA secondly, binds to the enzyme; homoserine 
releases itself from the enzyme before NADP+ 
does.
For the control of threonine biosynthesis, 
aspartate kinase is strongly inhibited by co-
operative action of lysine plus threonine as 
reported previously (4 (. However, it is con-
firmed from the present results that homoserine 
dehydrogenase is more strongly inhibited by 
threonine alone than aspartate kinase ; for 
instance, homoserine dehydrogenase is inhibited 
about 75`,c at 0. 1 mM threonine, while aspartate 
kinase is scarcely inhibited at the same con
centration of threonine even in the presence 
of four-fold concentration (0.4 mM) of lysine 
( 4 ). Thus, threonine biosynthesis seems to 
be controlled through the inhibition of homo
serine dehydrogenase by threonine. Level of 
homoserine dehydrogenase in B, fiavuna is about 
15-fold higher than that of dihydrodipicolinate 
synthetase, another enzyme at the branching 
point for ASA which is the first enzyme in 
the lysine biosynthesis (Table IV;. The K,,, 
values of homoserine dehydrogenase and dihy
drodipicolinate synthetase for ASA, a common 
substrate of the two enzymes, were 1.90 x 10-4 M 
and 1.96~ 10-4 M, respectively. From the above 
results, it can be considered that ASA, the 
common key intermediate of lysine and thre
onine biosynthesis, may be utilized to form 
homoserine and dihydrodipicolinate at a ratio 
of about 15 : 1, according to the levels of the 
two enzymes if the reaction conditions are 
similar to the standard assay conditions. Homo
serine produced is then led to form threonine, 
methionine and isoleucine, and dihydrodipicolin
ate to lysine. When these intracellular amino 
acids come up to sufficient levels, methionine
