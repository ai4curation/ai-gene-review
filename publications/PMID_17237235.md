# characterization of the cytochrome c oxidase assembly factor Cox19 of Saccharomyces cerevisiae.
**Authors:** Rigby K, Zhang L, Cobine PA, George GN, Winge DR
**Journal:** J Biol Chem (2007)
**DOI:** [10.1074/jbc.M610082200](https://doi.org/10.1074/jbc.M610082200)

## Abstract

1. J Biol Chem. 2007 Apr 6;282(14):10233-42. doi: 10.1074/jbc.M610082200. Epub
2007  Jan 19.

characterization of the cytochrome c oxidase assembly factor Cox19 of
Saccharomyces cerevisiae.

Author information:
(1)Department of Medicine, University of Utah Health Sciences Center, Salt Lake

Cox19 is an important accessory protein in the assembly of cytochrome c oxidase
in yeast. The protein is functional when tethered to the mitochondrial inner
membrane, suggesting its functional role within the intermembrane space. Cox19
resembles Cox17 in having a twin CX(9)C sequence motif that adopts a helical
hairpin in Cox17. The function of Cox17 appears to be a Cu(I) donor protein in
the assembly of the copper centers in cytochrome c oxidase. Cox19 also resembles
Cox17 in its ability to coordinate Cu(I). Recombinant Cox19 binds 1 mol eq of
Cu(I) per monomer and exists as a dimeric protein. Cox19 isolated from the
mitochondrial intermembrane space contains variable quantities of copper,
suggesting that Cu(I) binding may be a transient property. Cysteinyl residues
important for Cu(I) binding are also shown to be important for the in vivo
function of Cox19. Thus, a correlation exists in the ability to bind Cu(I) and
in vivo function.
