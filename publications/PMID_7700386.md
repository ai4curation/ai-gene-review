# RPA involvement in the damage-recognition and incision steps of nucleotide excision repair.
**Authors:** He Z, Henricksen LA, Wold MS, Ingles CJ
**Journal:** Nature (1995)
**DOI:** [10.1038/374566a0](https://doi.org/10.1038/374566a0)

## Abstract

1. Nature. 1995 Apr 6;374(6522):566-9. doi: 10.1038/374566a0.

RPA involvement in the damage-recognition and incision steps of nucleotide
excision repair.

He Z(1), Henricksen LA, Wold MS, Ingles CJ.

Author information:
(1)Banting and Best Department of Medical Research, University of Toronto,
Ontario, Canada.

Human replication protein (RPA) functions in DNA replication, homologous
recombination and nucleotide excision repair. This multisubunit single-stranded
DNA-binding protein may be required to make unique protein-protein contacts
because heterologous single-stranded binding proteins cannot substitute for RPA
in these diverse DNA transactions. We report here that, by using affinity
chromatography and immunoprecipitation, we found that human RPA bound
specifically and directly to two excision repair proteins, the xeroderma
pigmentosum damage-recognition protein XPA (refs 8, 9) and the endonuclease XPG
(refs 10-13). Although it had been suggested that RPA might function before the
DNA synthesis repair stage, our finding that a complex of RPA and XPA showed a
striking cooperativity in binding to DNA lesions indicates that RPA may function
at the very earliest stage of excision repair. In addition, by binding XPG, RPA
may target this endonuclease to damaged DNA.
