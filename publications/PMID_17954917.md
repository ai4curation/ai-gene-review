## Full Text

Abstract

The Eph (erythropoietin-producing hepatoma) family of receptor tyrosine kinases and their membrane-bound ligands, the ephrins, have been implicated in regulating cell adhesion and migration during development by mediating cell-to-cell signaling events. The transmembrane ephrinB (Eph receptor interactor B) protein is a bidirectional signaling molecule that sends a forward signal through the activation of its cognate receptor tyrosine kinase, residing on another cell. A reverse signal can be transduced into the ephrinB-expressing cell via tyrosine phosphorylation of its conserved C-terminal cytoplasmic domain. Although some insight has been gained regarding how ephrinB may send signals affecting cytoskeletal components, little is known about how ephrinB1 reverse signaling affects transcriptional processes. Here we report that signal transducer and activator of transcription 3 (STAT3) can interact with ephrinB1 in a phosphorylation-dependent manner that leads to enhanced activation of STAT3 transcriptional activity. This activity depends on the tyrosine kinase Jak2, and two tyrosines within the intracellular domain of ephrinB1 are critical for the association with STAT3 and its activation. The recruitment of STAT3 to ephrinB1, and its resulting Jak2-dependent activation and transcription of reporter targets, reveals a signaling pathway from ephrinB1 to the nucleus.

Keywords: FGF receptor, Eph receptor, neuroepithelial cells, tyrosine phosphorylation, Jak2

Membrane-anchored ephrinB (erythropoietin-producing hepatoma interactor B) proteins are ligands for EphB receptors and have been involved in many biological processes such as cell adhesion, neural crest migration, brain segmentation, and vasculogenesis ( 1 – 3 ). Upon cell-to-cell contact, ephrinB1 not only sends a forward signal through the activation of its cognate receptor, but it also rapidly undergoes tyrosine phosphorylation at the C terminus and recruits the adaptor protein growth-factor-receptor-bound protein 4 (Grb4)/Nck adaptor protein 2 (Nck2), resulting in an increase of focal adhesion kinase (FAK) catalytic activity as well as cell rounding ( 4 ). In addition, the C-terminal PDZ-binding motif of ephrinB1 is able to mediate reverse signaling by recruitment of PDZ-RGS3, a GTP exchange factor that regulates the migration of cerebellar granule cells ( 5 ). Moreover, it has been reported that ephrinB1 signals through its intracellular domain to control retinal progenitor cell movement into the eye field by interacting with the scaffold protein Dishevelled ( 6 ). Thus, several signaling molecules have been identified to transmit reverse signaling of ephrinB1 by either phosphorylation-dependent or -independent means. ephrinB1 can be phosphorylated in response to binding a cognate Eph receptor ( 7 , 8 ) or the tight junction protein Claudin ( 9 ) or in response to FGF receptor activation ( 10 ). Among the six tyrosine residues present in the intracellular domain of ephrinB1, one resides in a well conserved YXXQ motif at the C terminus and is rapidly phosphorylated upon engagement of the EphB ectodomain ( 11 ) or in response to FGF receptor activation ( 10 , 12 ). Recent studies indicate that the YXXQ motif in the intracellular domain of the gp130 receptor (an IL-6 receptor subunit) and EGFR is important for the binding, phosphorylation and activation of STAT3 (signal transducer and activator of transcription 3) ( 13 , 14 ).

STAT3 plays pivotal roles in development, proliferation, apoptosis and tumorigenesis. STAT3 is activated in response to several agents, including growth factors, cytokines, and oncoproteins ( 15 – 21 ). For example, the receptor for IL-6 is composed of an IL-6-specific receptor subunit (α-chain) and a signal transducer, gp130 (β-chain). The binding of IL-6 to an α-chain results in the formation of a hexameric complex containing two molecules of IL-6, α-chain and gp130, which is followed by the activation of JAK (janus kinase) family protein tyrosine kinases. The activated tyrosine kinase in turn phosphorylates STAT3, which dimerizes, translocates to the nucleus, recognizes target DNA, and induces gene expression ( 22 ). It has been shown that gp130 mutants lacking the YXXQ motifs fail to activate STAT3 and thus do not mediate IL-6 function ( 13 ). In this study, we provide evidence that ephrinB1 interacts with STAT3 in a tyrosine phosphorylation-dependent manner, resulting in the phosphorylation and enhanced transcriptional activation of STAT3.

Results and Discussion

ephrinB1 Associates with STAT3 in a Phosphorylation-Dependent Manner.

Several lines of evidence suggest that STAT3 may be a candidate mediator of ephrinB1 signaling: ( i ) the cytoplasmic domain of ephrinB1 possesses a conserved YXXQ motif that is associated with STAT3 binding to the gp130 subunit of the IL6 receptor [ supporting information (SI) Fig. 6 B ]; ( ii ) a yeast-two-hybrid screen for proteins that interact with the cytoplasmic domain of ephrinB1 identified LMO4, a LIM-domain-only protein that also binds to the gp130 subunit and regulates STAT3 function (data not shown and SI Fig. 6 A ); ( iii ) LMO4 and ephrinB1 were coimmunoprecipitated from Xenopus oocytes coexpressing both proteins ( SI Fig. 6 A ); and ( iv ) in situ hybridization analysis demonstrated significant overlap in expression patterns between ephrinB1 and STAT3 in neural tissue of developing Xenopus embryos ( SI Fig. 6 C ), confirming that these proteins may have the opportunity to interact in vivo . Thus, we conducted an immunoprecipitation (IP) analysis of ephrinB1 from murine embryonic brain lysates to assess whether endogenous ephrinB1 and STAT3 proteins interact. As expected, Western blot analysis of the ephrinB1 immune complexes indicated that ephrinB1 was tyrosine phosphorylated in this tissue ( Fig. 1 A ). Most importantly, STAT3 was present in the ephrinB1 immune complexes from this embryonic neural tissue, indicating that an in vivo interaction exists between ephrinB1 and STAT3 protein.

Fig. 1.

We have previously shown that FGFR1 induces the tyrosine phosphorylation of ephrinB1 in vitro and in vivo ( 10 , 12 ). Thus, in an effort to determine whether the association between ephrinB1 and STAT3 was tyrosine phosphorylation-dependent, Flag-tagged Xenopus ephrinB1 was coexpressed with HA-tagged STAT3 in Xenopus oocytes in the absence of FGFR1 or in the presence of a constitutively active FGFR1 (FGFR1 KE) or kinase-dead FGFR1 (FGFR1 KD). IP and Western blot analysis show that STAT3 can only weakly associate with unphosphorylated ephrinB1 ( Fig. 1 B ); however, a marked increase in STAT3 association is observed in ephrinB1 IPs where ephrinB1 is tyrosine phosphorylated due to expression of FGFR1 KE. Reciprocal experiments show dramatically increased amounts of ephrinB1 in STAT3 IPs from oocyte extracts expressing an activated FGFR1 when compared with those extracts expressing an FGFR1 KD or no FGFR1 at all ( Fig. 1 B ).

Because the nonreceptor tyrosine kinase, c-Src, has also been shown to induce the phosphorylation of ephrinB1 ( 7 , 8 , 23 ), we examined whether the coimmunoprecipitation (Co-IP) of ephrinB1 and STAT3 is also observed in mammalian cell culture in a phosphorylation-dependent manner. Co-IP analysis of extracts from HeLa cells expressing STAT3 and ephrinB1 in the absence or presence of a constitutively active c-Src (Y529F) also demonstrated an enhanced association between ephrinB1 and STAT3 in response to ephrinB1 phosphorylation ( Fig. 1 C ).

Because ephrinB1 is known to be tyrosine phosphorylated upon binding to the ectodomain of its cognate Eph receptor, we tested whether this interaction also elicited an association between ephrinB1 and STAT3. Co-IP analysis of extracts from HeLa cells expressing STAT3 and ephrinB1 in the presence of the Fc portion of human Ig (Fc) or EphB2 ectodomain fused to human Fc (EphB2-Fc) demonstrated a clear enhancement of the association between ephrinB1 and STAT3 in response to EphB2-Fc ( Fig. 1 D ). These data indicate that phosphorylation of ephrinB1 in response to either the cognate receptor binding or an activated FGFR1 or Src leads to an enhanced physical interaction between ephrinB1 and STAT3.

Phosphorylation of ephrinB1 Specifically Recruits STAT3 and Results in Tyrosine Phosphorylation of STAT3 at Position 705.

Because we established that STAT3 associates with phosphorylated ephrinB1 using in vivo and in vitro systems, we extended our analysis to determine whether other STAT family members (STAT1 and STAT5b) also interact with ephrinB1 in a phosphorylation-dependent manner. Co-IPs were performed with lysates from Xenopus oocytes expressing ephrinB1 and either STAT1, -3, or -5b, along with FGFR1 KE or FGFR1 KD. STAT1 showed a significant ability to associate with ephrinB1 even in the absence of ephrinB1 tyrosine phosphorylation, whereas only a weak association between ephrinB1 and STAT5b was observed in response to ephrinB1 phosphorylation ( Fig. 2 A ). Of particular interest, STAT1 was only modestly tyrosine phosphorylated in these oocytes, whereas STAT3 was robustly phosphorylated, as was STAT5b ( Fig. 2 A ). Tyrosine phosphorylation of STAT proteins often correlates with the activation of their transcriptional activity ( 24 ). This finding suggests that STAT1 was not strongly activated in response to expression of ephrinB1 and FGFR1 KE, although it was able to associate with ephrinB1. In contrast, STAT5b was markedly tyrosine phosphorylated but showed poor association with ephrinB1. Moreover, STAT5b was tyrosine phosphorylated in the presence of activated FGFR1 regardless of whether ephrinB1 was present ( Fig. 2 C ). These data suggested that STAT1 was not markedly activated by FGFR1 in the presence or absence of ephrinB1 and that STAT5b activation was only FGFR1-dependent. Thus, we examined whether the marked phosphorylation of STAT3 depended on the presence of ephrinB1.

Fig. 2.

Wild-type STAT3 and mutants of STAT3 harboring substitutions in tyrosine 705 or serine 727 (associated with transcriptional activity) or both were coexpressed with ephrinB1 and/or FGFR1 KE ( Fig. 2 B ). Western blot analyses of these lysates probed with a Tyr 705-specific antibody demonstrated that STAT3 is phosphorylated on Tyr 705 (required for STAT3 dimerization and activation) only when both FGFR1 and ephrinB1 are present ( Fig. 2 B ). Collectively, these data suggest that STAT3 is specifically recruited to tyrosine phosphorylated ephrinB1, and phosphorylation Tyr 705 of STAT3 depends on this recruitment.

Both Tyr 294 and Tyr 310 of ephrinB1 Contribute to the Formation of a Complex Between ephrinB1 and STAT3.

ephrinB1 has six tyrosine residues in its cytoplasmic domain. Tyrosine residues at position 298, 305, 310, and 324 are phosphorylated in response to ephrin binding to the ectodomain of the cognate Eph receptor or activation of the FGF receptor ( 10 – 12 ). Tyr-298 of x-ephrinB1 (Tyr 304 in murine) is critical for the recruitment of the Grb4/Nck2 adaptor protein ( 12 , 25 , 26 ).

To determine which specific tyrosines within ephrinB1 are important for the phosphorylation-dependent association with STAT3, several mutants harboring substitutions of phenylalanine for tyrosine in the intracellular domain of ephrinB1 were generated ( Fig. 3 A ), coexpressed with ephrinB1 and FGFR1 KE, and tested by Co-IPs ( Fig. 3 B ). Although single substitution of Phe for Tyr at position 294 had little effect, a marked loss in the association between ephrinB1 and STAT3 was observed with ephrinB1 Y310F, and a more substantial decrease was observed with Y294F/Y310F and Y294F/Y305F/Y310F mutants ( Fig. 3 B ). Commensurate with reduced association in Co-IPs, STAT3 displays dramatically reduced tyrosine phosphorylation when coexpressed with the ephrinB1 mutants ( Fig. 3 B ). In addition, both ephrinB2 and ephrinB3 possess YXXQ motifs at their C termini but lack a tyrosine at position 294. Although all B-type ephrins yielded similar results with regard to binding the various STATs, less STAT3 phosphorylation was observed when compared with that of ephrinB1, raising the possibility that Tyr 294 might act by enhancing STAT3 phosphorylation ( SI Fig. 7 A and B ). Collectively, these data identify two tyrosines (Y294 and Y310) within ephrinB1 that play critical roles for an association with STAT3 and the phosphorylation of STAT3.

Fig. 3.

To test whether functional activities associated with the various domains of STAT3 are critical for the ability of STAT3 to interact with phosphorylated ephrinB1, several point mutants were generated in STAT3 that disrupt specific activities. These point mutants were coexpressed with ephrinB1 and FGFR1 KE, and Co-IPs were performed ( Fig. 3 C and D ). Only disruption of the SH2 binding pocket (R609L) ( 27 ) displayed a dramatic reduction in the association of STAT3 and ephrinB1 ( Fig. 3 D ). Other mutations, such as disrupting amino terminal oligomerization (W37A) ( 28 ); the coil–coil protein interaction domain important for the STAT3/gp130 receptor interaction (D170A) ( 29 ); the DNA binding activity (N466A) ( 30 ); IFN γ transcription by STAT1 (K551A/E552A) ( 31 ); and maximal transcriptional activity (S727A) ( 32 ) all failed to affect binding to ephrinB1. Thus, the SH2 domain of STAT3 is critical for its association with ephrinB1.

ephrinB1 Increases STAT3 Transcriptional Activity Through the Induction of STAT3 Phosphorylation, Nuclear Translocation, and DNA Binding.

To confirm the biochemical results indicating a positive regulatory role for ephrinB1 on STAT3 activation, we examined the effect of expressing ephrinB1 and the STAT-binding mutant of ephrinB1 (Y294F/Y310F) on STAT3 transcriptional activity. We introduced a STAT3 reporter construct (M67-tk-Luc) into COS-1 cells that express very low levels of endogenous STAT3 and assayed the effect of ephrinB1 and its mutants along with an activated FGFR1 or EphB2-Fc treatment on STAT3 activity as evidenced by luciferase activity. Wild-type ephrinB1 enhances the FGFR1-induced STAT3 reporter activation in COS-1 cells ( Fig. 4 A ). Comparable results were observed by using similar conditions with both CyclinD1 and C/EBPδ reporter constructs that contain STAT3 response elements (data not shown). EphB2-Fc treatment of cells expressing ephrinB1 and STAT3 also markedly enhanced STAT3 reporter activity when compared with control Fc treatment ( Fig. 4 C ). In contrast, an ephrinB1 mutant (Y294F/Y310F) that is compromised in its ability to bind STAT3 ( Fig. 3 B ) markedly diminished STAT3 activation ( Fig. 4 A and C ). As expected, expressing a dominant-negative (DN)-STAT3 (Y705F) blocked the luciferase induction associated with ephrinB1 ( Fig. 4 A ). In support of a role for ephrinB1 in enhancing STAT3 activation, Western blot analysis of the COS-1 cell lysates showed increased STAT3 phosphorylation on Tyr 705 (a prerequisite phosphorylation event for STAT3 activation) when wild-type ephrinB1 was expressed along with FGFR1 KE or in the presence of EphB2-Fc, but not when the STAT3-binding mutant of ephrinB1 (Y294F/Y310F) was expressed ( Fig. 4 B and D ). Consistent with these results, STAT3 DNA binding activity was also enhanced as evidenced by EMSA using an oligonucleotide containing the STAT3 consensus sequence ( SI Fig. 8 ). Together, these data are consistent with an ephrinB1-mediated induction of STAT3 phosphorylation, DNA binding, and transcriptional activation.

Fig. 4.

As an in vivo test of whether STAT3 can be activated through an ephrinB-dependent mechanism, neuroepithelial cells were isolated from embryonic day 13.5 mouse brains and cultured in the presence of FGF2, which is critical for the maintenance of neural precursor cells ( 33 ). These cells are highly dependent on STAT3 activity and possess a high level of endogenous ephrinB1 ( 33 , 34 ). The cells were cultured for 2 days in the absence FGF to reduce endogenous STAT3 phosphorylation and were subsequently treated with control Fc, EphB2-Fc, or FGF2. As expected, an increase in STAT3 association with ephrinB1 was observed in IP and Western blot analyses of lysates from EphB2-Fc-treated neuroepithelial cells ( SI Fig. 9 ). Activation of STAT3 was assessed by immunofluorescence analysis using phospho-specific STAT3 antibodies ( Fig. 4 E ). In response to EphB2-Fc treatment, 69.3% (SD ± 5.5) of the neuroepithelial cells displayed nuclear localized phosphorylated STAT3, whereas control Fc-treated cells showed only 16% (SD ± 3) phospho-STAT3-positive cells. As expected 76.1% (SD ± 7.1) of FGF2-treated positive control cells displayed phosphorylated STAT3 in the nucleus ( Fig. 4 E ).

Thus, in primary neuroepithelial cells, stimulation of endogenous ephrinB via an interaction with the cognate Eph receptor ectodomain leads to a dramatic increase in STAT3 phosphorylation and nuclear localization.

DN-Jak2 Inhibits ephrinB1-Mediated STAT3 Transcriptional Activity.

In the case of the gp130 receptor, members of the Jak family of nonreceptor tyrosine kinases are associated with the cytokine receptor and are responsible for phosphorylating and activating the STAT proteins ( 35 ). Although we have established that phosphorylation of ephrinB1 leads to recruitment of STAT3, it was a distinct possibility that Jak family members may be involved in STAT3 phosphorylation and activation. To test this possibility, we coexpressed Jak2 along with ephrinB1 in the absence or presence of FGFR1 KE in Xenopus oocytes. Interestingly, an interaction between ephrinB1 and Jak2 was observed in ephrinB1 IPs from these oocyte lysates ( Fig. 5 A ). This association occurred in the absence of FGFR1 KE and was independent of ephrinB1 phosphorylation ( Fig. 5 A ). Furthermore, in COS-1 cells, both ephrinB1-mediated STAT3 tyrosine phosphorylation and transcriptional activation were markedly decreased in the presence of DN-Jak2 ( Fig. 5 B and C ). The inhibition of STAT3 activity was specific for DN-Jak2, as the expression of other DN-Jak family members had little effect ( Fig. 5 B ). Moreover, an increase in wild-type Jak2 tyrosine phosphorylation (associated with increased activity) was observed in the presence of ephrinB1 (data not shown). Taken together, these results suggest that ephrinB1 can form a complex with Jak2, which specifically mediates STAT3 transcriptional activation.

Fig. 5.

For over a decade, it has been known that the extracellular portions of EphB receptors can induce tyrosine phosphorylation of the intracellular domain of B-type ephrins ( 7 , 8 ). Src family kinases have been implicated in mediating this event ( 23 ), and activation of the FGF receptor has been shown to induce tyrosine phosphorylation as well ( 10 , 12 ). Three conserved tyrosines in the cytoplasmic domain of ephrinB1 have been identified as in vivo phosphorylation sites ( 11 ), and all these data suggested that ephrinBs may transmit signals through proteins containing SH2 domains. At least one SH2-domain-containing protein has previously been shown to bind tyrosine-phosphorylated ephrinB1 ( 4 , 12 , 25 , 26 ). This protein, Grb4/Nck2, is an adaptor that may link ephrinBs to other signaling molecules that modify cell morphology through reorganization of the actin cytoskeleton by affecting Fak activity and the distribution of the Fak binding protein, paxillin ( 4 ). Here we describe another SH2-containing protein, STAT3, which can be recruited to ephrinB1, resulting in the phosphorylation and activation of STAT3 in a Jak2-dependent manner.

STAT3 is critical for murine embryonic development ( 36 ) and in Zebrafish , STAT3 controls cell movements during gastrulation and the polarity of cells neighboring the gastrula organizer ( 37 , 38 ). The analysis of mice in which STAT3 has been disrupted in various adult tissues has led to a recognition that STAT3 participates in a diverse set of cellular responses ( 39 , 40 ). These responses include the migration of keratinocytes, the survival of thymic epithelial cells, apoptosis in the mammary gland epithelium, modulation of inflammation, and the survival of sensory and motor neurons ( 41 – 44 ). STAT3 has also emerged as a critical mediator in the pathogenesis of a variety of human cancers ( 45 , 46 ). Our study provides evidence for a link between ephrinB1 signaling at the cell surface to an important transcriptional mediator of signaling in the nucleus.

Materials and Methods

Plasmids.

cDNAs encoding wild-type Xenopus ephrinB1 ( 12 ), Xenopus ephrinB2 (MGC:68890; ATCC, Mannassus, VA), Xenopus ephrinB3 (a gift from A. Brandli, Eidgenössiche Technische Hochschule, Zurich, Switzerland) and mouse ephrinB1 (MGC:14047, ATCC) were subcloned into pCS2 + . Both wild-type rat STAT1 and rat STAT3 (a gift from A. Perantoni, National Cancer Institute/National Institutes of Health) were subcloned into pCS2 + and a HA-epitope was introduced into the C termini (see SI Materials and Methods for mutant construction and plasmids).

Preparation of Synthetic RNA, Xenopus Oocytes, and Microinjection.

For all oocyte injections, a synthetic capped mRNA was made by using the SP6 mMessage mMachine kit (Ambion, Austin, TX) and was microinjected into oocytes as described in refs. 10 and 12 .

IP and Western Blot Analysis.

IPs and Western blot analyses were performed as described in refs. 10 and 12 (see also SI Material and Methods ).

Cell Culture, DNA Transfections, Luciferase Assays, and Immunofluorescence.

COS-1 and HeLa cells were cultured according to American Type Culture Collection guidelines. COS-1 cells were cotransfected with p4xM67-tk-Luc, 1 μg of wild-type or mutant forms of ephrinB1, STAT3, DN-STAT3, FGFR1 KE, DN-Jak1, DN-Jak2, DN-Tyk2, or pCS2 + control vector. EphB2-Fc (R & D Systems, Minneapolis, MN) was clustered using human Ig as described in ref. 10 and was added to the culture medium at a concentration of 2.5 μg/ml for 18 h. Data were normalized to the activity of β-gal. Cell lysates were prepared for luciferase and β-gal activity assays according to the manufacturer's instructions 24–48 h after transfection (Promega, Madison, WI). For the detection of the phosphorylation status of STAT3, ephrinB1, and Jak2, an equal amount of protein lysates were assayed by immunoblot using anti-pY 705 STAT3, anti-Lerk2, anti-pY 324 ephrinB1 (rabbit polyclonal antibody against pY 324 x-ephrinB1), anti-Jak1, anti-Tyk2, anti-Jak2, and anti-pY 1007/1008 Jak2 (Cell Signaling Technology, Beverly, MA), respectively. Neuroepithelial cells were isolated and cultured as described in ref. 47 , and immunofluorescence was performed as described (see SI Materials and Methods for culture and immunofluorescence).

EMSA.

Nuclear extracts from COS-1 cells were prepared as described in ref. 21 , and STAT3 mobility shift assays were performed (see SI Materials and Methods for extract preparation and assay conditions).

Whole-Mount in Situ Hybridization.

Both Xenopus ephrinB1 and STAT3 probes were prepared and whole-mount in situ hybridizations were performed as described in ref. 48 (see SI Materials and Methods ).
