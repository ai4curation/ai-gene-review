# Functional identification of the product of the Bacillus subtilis yvaL gene as a SecG homologue.
**Authors:** van Wely KH, Swaving J, Broekhuizen CP, Rose M, Quax WJ, Driessen AJ
**Journal:** J Bacteriol (1999)

## Abstract

1. J Bacteriol. 1999 Mar;181(6):1786-92. doi: 10.1128/JB.181.6.1786-1792.1999.

Functional identification of the product of the Bacillus subtilis yvaL gene as a
SecG homologue.

van Wely KH(1), Swaving J, Broekhuizen CP, Rose M, Quax WJ, Driessen AJ.

Author information:
(1)Department of Microbiology, Groningen Biomolecular Sciences and Biotechnology
Institute, University of Groningen, 9751 NN Haren, The Netherlands.

Protein export in Escherichia coli is mediated by translocase, a multisubunit
membrane protein complex with SecA as the peripheral subunit and the SecY, SecE,
and SecG proteins as the integral membrane domain. In the gram-positive
bacterium Bacillus subtilis, SecA, SecY, and SecE have been identified through
genetic analysis. Sequence comparison of the Bacillus chromosome identified a
potential homologue of SecG, termed YvaL. A chromosomal disruption of the yvaL
gene results in mild cold sensitivity and causes a beta-lactamase secretion
defect. The cold sensitivity is exacerbated by overexpression of the secretory
protein alpha-amylase, whereas growth and beta-lactamase secretion are restored
by coexpression of yvaL or the E. coli secG gene. These results indicate that
the yvaL gene codes for a protein that is functionally homologous to SecG.
