# PKA-induced resistance to tamoxifen is associated with an altered orientation of ERalpha towards co-activator SRC-1.
**Authors:** Zwart W, Griekspoor A, Berno V, Lakeman K, Jalink K, Mancini M, Neefjes J, Michalides R

PKA-induced resistance to tamoxifen is associated with an altered orientation of

Zwart W(1), Griekspoor A, Berno V, Lakeman K, Jalink K, Mancini M, Neefjes J,
Michalides R.

Author information:
(1)Department of Tumor Biology, The Netherlands Cancer Institute, Amsterdam, The

Resistance to tamoxifen is observed in half of the recurrences in breast cancer,
where the anti-estrogen tamoxifen acquires agonistic properties for
transactivating estrogen receptor alpha (ERalpha). In a previous study, we
showed that protein kinase A (PKA)-mediated phosphorylation of serine 305 (S305)
of ERalpha results in resistance to tamoxifen. Now, we demonstrate that
phosphorylation of S305 in ERalpha by PKA leads to an altered orientation
between ERalpha and its coactivator SRC-1, which renders the transcription
complex active in the presence of tamoxifen. This altered orientation involves
the C-termini of ERalpha and SRC-1, which required a prolonged AF-1-mediated
interaction. This intermolecular reorientation as a result of PKA-mediated
phosphorylation of ERalpha-S305 and tamoxifen binding provides a unique model
for resistance to the anticancer drug tamoxifen.
