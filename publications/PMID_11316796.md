# PIE-1 is a bifunctional protein that regulates maternal and zygotic gene expression in the embryonic germ line of Caenorhabditis elegans.
**Authors:** Tenenhaus C, Subramaniam K, Dunn MA, Seydoux G
**Journal:** Genes Dev (2001)
**DOI:** [10.1101/gad.876201](https://doi.org/10.1101/gad.876201)
**PMC:** [PMC312670](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC312670/)

## Abstract

1. Genes Dev. 2001 Apr 15;15(8):1031-40. doi: 10.1101/gad.876201.

PIE-1 is a bifunctional protein that regulates maternal and zygotic gene
expression in the embryonic germ line of Caenorhabditis elegans.

Tenenhaus C(1), Subramaniam K, Dunn MA, Seydoux G.

Author information:
(1)Department of Molecular Biology and Genetics, Johns Hopkins University School
of Medicine, Baltimore, Maryland 21205, USA.

The CCCH zinc finger protein PIE-1 is an essential regulator of germ cell fate
that segregates with the germ lineage during the first cleavages of the
Caenorhabditis elegans embryo. We have shown previously that one function of
PIE-1 is to inhibit mRNA transcription. Here we show that PIE-1 has a second
function in germ cells; it is required for efficient expression of the
maternally encoded Nanos homolog NOS-2. This second function is genetically
separable from PIE-1's inhibitory effect on transcription. A mutation in PIE-1's
second CCCH finger reduces NOS-2 expression without affecting transcriptional
repression and causes primordial germ cells to stray away from the somatic
gonad, occasionally exiting the embryo entirely. Our results indicate that PIE-1
promotes germ cell fate by two independent mechanisms as follows: (1) inhibition
of transcription, which blocks zygotic programs that drive somatic development,
and (2) activation of protein expression from nos-2 and possibly other maternal
RNAs, which promotes primordial germ cell development.
