BACKGROUND: Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) is one of the most prevalent
malignancies, and the treatment options are limited. Growing evidence shows that
long non-coding RNAs (LncRNAs) encode peptides, suggesting that lncRNAs-derived
peptides may play a role in HCC progression and explore their potential as
therapeutic targets.
METHODS: We used RNA-sequencing and bioinformatics analysis to identify a
107-amino acid peptide, SMIM45-107aa, encoded by LINC00634. The expression
levels and prognostic significance of SMIM45-107aa in HCC tissues were assessed
by immunohistochemistry (IHC) and Kaplan-Meier. Wound-healing and cell colony
formation evaluate the effects of SMIM45-107aa on cell migration and
proliferation. A mouse xenograft model was used to examine the tumor formation.
Interactions between SMIM45-107aa and MTDH were explored and the effects on MTDH
ubiquitination were investigated by immunoprecipitation. Proteomic analysis and
Western blotting confirmed the mechanism of SMIM45-107aa effected HCC.
Additionally, a short peptide, peptide 5 derived from SMIM45-107aa was analyzed
by cell migration in SK-Hep1 cells and by zebrafish model.
RESULTS: SMIM45-107aa was highly expressed in HCC tissues and associated with
poor prognosis. It promoted cell migration and proliferation in vitro and tumor
formation in vivo. SMIM45-107aa interacted with MTDH, inhibiting its
ubiquitination and stabilizing the protein. Proteomic and Western blot analysis
revealed that SMIM45-107aa upregulated MGST1 and phosphorylated AKT (pAKT).
Importantly, peptide 5 inhibited SMIM45-107aa-induced cell migration and
demonstrated anticancer activity in zebrafish models.
CONCLUSION: We identified SMIM45-107aa, a novel peptide encoded by LINC00634,
which promoted HCC progression via the MGST1-pAKT-MTDH axis. The derived peptide
5 exhibited anticancer activity, suggesting a potential therapeutic strategy for
HCC.

Introduction

Primary liver cancer ranks fourth in terms of incidence and second in both mortality and mortality rate among malignancies [1, 2]. Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) accounts for approximately 85% of liver cancer [3, 4]. The major high-risk factors for HCC include hepatitis B virus (HBV) and/or hepatitis C virus (HCV) infection, excessive alcohol consumption, fatty liver disease or exposure to aflatoxin B1 in diet, and liver cirrhosis [5]. Currently, the advent of immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs), such as atezolizumab (anti-PD-L1) and bevacizumab (anti-VEGF), has significantly prolonged the survival of patients with HCC [6]. However, compared with other tumors, the survival improvement in HCC patients is still relatively limited [1, 7]. Therefore, there is an urgent need for reliable prognostic markers and novel therapeutic targets for HCC.

Long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs) are generally defined as transcripts longer than 200 nucleotides. Recent breakthroughs have revealed that some lncRNAs possess the ability to encode small peptides in multiple cancers [8, 9]. It has been reported that CIP2A-BP, encoded by LINC00665, promoted HCC cell proliferation and metastasis [10]. Additionally, the peptide SMIM30, encoded by LINC0099, was upregulated in HCC tissues and promoted HCC progression by interacting with SRC and YES1, activating the MAPK pathway, and enhancing the G1/S translation via Rb pathway and cytosolic calcium levels [11, 12]. LncRNA-encoded peptides not only contributed to tumorigenesis but also impact metabolism. A 53-aa peptide was encoded by LncRNA HOXB-AS3 and inhibited colon cancer cell proliferation by reducing PKM formation and lactic acid generation [13]. Growing evidence indicated that the peptides acted as either oncogenic drivers or tumor suppressor [14, 15]. Recently, peptide drugs have garnered increasing attention as a novel and promising approach for clinical application due to their specificity, lower cytotoxicity, and reduced immunogenicity to normal tissues [16, 17]. It has been reported that sh-RASON-targeted peptide RASON inhibited the growth of xenografted tumors and enhanced the sensitivity of KRAS-mutant pancreatic cancer cells to cetuximab in mouse models [18]. The lncRNA-encoded peptides offer new insights into therapeutic approaches for HCC.

In our study, we employed bioinformatics analysis to identify the LINC00634 having potential ability of peptide-coding. Experimental evidence subsequently confirmed that LINC00634 encoded a peptide consisting of 107 amino acids, which we designated as SMIM45-107aa. Thereafter, we conducted a comprehensive investigation into the function and underlying molecular mechanisms of this peptide in HCC. To our knowledge, this is the first report on the role and molecular mechanisms of SMIM45-107aa in HCC.

SMIM45-107aa activated the AKT signaling pathway by modulating MGST1 and MTDH

To uncover potential pathways associated with SMIM45-107aa in HCC, we conducted proteomics analysis in SK-Hep1 cells with either overexpression or knockdown of SMIM45-107aa. The results revealed that SMIM45-107aa overexpression led to the upregulation of 145 genes and the downregulation of 130 genes, while knockdown resulted in the upregulation of 144 genes and the downregulation of 134 genes (fold-change >1.5 and p < 0.05, Fig. 5A). Venn diagram analysis identified 42 differentially expressed genes common to both conditions (Fig. 5B). Gene Ontology (GO) analysis indicated that the targets regulated by SMIM45-107aa were involved in glycolipid binding, phosphoric diester hydrolase activity, and nuclear receptor binding (Fig. 5C). The most significant KEGG pathway identified was the glutathione metabolism pathway, which included six genes such as MGST1, ANPEP, and GSTK1 (Fig. 5D, Table S4). MGST1, a member of the glutathione S-transferase superfamily and membrane protein superfamily, is involved in arachidonic acid and glutathione metabolism and has been implicated in tumorigenesis [21]. MGST1 has been shown to promote gastric cancer progression by activating the AKT-GSK-3β/β-catenin pathway [22]. Our results demonstrated that overexpression of SMIM45-107aa significantly increased MGST1 protein levels in SNU449 and SK-Hep1 cells (Fig. 5E-F). Concurrently, we observed an increase in phosphorylated AKT (pAKT) levels, while AKT levels remained unchanged (Fig. 5E-F). These findings suggested that SMIM45-107aa upregulated MTDH protein levels and activated the AKT pathway through the promotion of MGST1.
