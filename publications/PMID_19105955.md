Trichomonas vaginalis is the cause of human trichomoniasis, the most common
non-viral sexually transmitted disease worldwide. Although acquisition of iron
by binding to host hemoglobin through distinct receptor(s) has been described,
no specific heme- or hemoglobin-binding site has been reported in this parasite.
To determine the presence of hemoglobin-binding protein(s), membrane proteins
were subjected to hemoglobin-affinity chromatography. Eluted proteins were
analysed by SDS-PAGE. Two protein bands of 48 and 63 kDa were detected.
Competition assay with an excess amount of hemoglobin or hemin in
hemoglobin-affinity chromatography could block the 63- and 48-kDa bands,
respectively. Further analysis by mass spectrometry indicated that the 48- and
63-kDa proteins had identity with two T. vaginalis adhesins: AP51 and AP65,
respectively. This study confirms the existence of multifunctional proteins in
T. vaginalis, and suggested that AP51 and AP65, besides serving as adhesion
molecules, could also act as heme- and hemoglobin-binding proteins.
