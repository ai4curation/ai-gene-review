# Characterization of recombinant adrenodoxin reductase homologue (Arh1p) from yeast. Implication in in vitro cytochrome p45011beta monooxygenase system.
**Authors:** Lacour T, Achstetter T, Dumas B

Characterization of recombinant adrenodoxin reductase homologue (Arh1p) from
yeast. Implication in in vitro cytochrome p45011beta monooxygenase system.

Lacour T(1), Achstetter T, Dumas B.

Author information:
(1)Biotechnology Department, Hoechst Marion Roussel, 102 route de Noisy, 93230

The mammalian electron transfer chain of mitochondrial cytochrome P450 forms
involved in steroidogenesis includes very specific proteins, namely adrenodoxin
reductase and adrenodoxin. Adrenodoxin reductase transfers electrons from NADPH
to adrenodoxin, which subsequently donates them to the cytochrome P450 forms.
The Saccharomyces cerevisiae ARH1 gene product (Arh1p) presents homology to
mammalian adrenodoxin reductase. We demonstrate the capacity of recombinant
Arh1p, made in Escherichia coli, to substitute for its mammalian homologue in
ferricyanide, cytochrome c reduction, and, more importantly, in vitro
11beta-hydroxylase assays. Electrons could be transferred from NADPH and NADH as
measured in the cytochrome c reduction assay. Apparent Km values were determined
to be 0.5, 0.6, and 0.1 microM for NADPH, NADH, and bovine adrenodoxin,
respectively. These values differ slightly from those of mammalian adrenodoxin
reductase, except for NADH, which is a very poor electron donor to the mammalian
protein. Subcellular fractionation studies have localized Arh1p to the inner
membrane of yeast mitochondria. The biological function of Arh1p remains
unknown, and to date, no mitochondrial cytochrome P450 has been identified. ARH1
is, however, essential for yeast viability because an ARH1 gene disruption is
lethal not only in aerobic growth conditions but also, surprisingly enough,
during fermentation.
