# Symmetrical mutant phenotypes of the receptor EphB4 and its specific transmembrane ligand ephrin-B2 in cardiovascular development.
**Authors:** Gerety SS, Wang HU, Chen ZF, Anderson DJ
**Journal:** Mol Cell (1999)
**DOI:** [10.1016/s1097-2765(00)80342-1](https://doi.org/10.1016/s1097-2765(00)80342-1)

## Abstract

1. Mol Cell. 1999 Sep;4(3):403-14. doi: 10.1016/s1097-2765(00)80342-1.

Symmetrical mutant phenotypes of the receptor EphB4 and its specific
transmembrane ligand ephrin-B2 in cardiovascular development.

Gerety SS(1), Wang HU, Chen ZF, Anderson DJ.

Author information:
(1)Division of Biology, Howard Hughes Medical Institute, California Institute of
Technology, Pasadena 91125, USA.

Ephrin-B2 is a transmembrane ligand that is specifically expressed on arteries
but not veins and that is essential for cardiovascular development. However,
ephrin-B2 is also expressed in nonvascular tissues and interacts with multiple
EphB class receptors expressed in both endothelial and nonendothelial cell
types. Thus, the identity of the relevant receptor for ephrin-B2 and the site(s)
where these molecules interact to control angiogenesis were not clear. Here we
show that EphB4, a specific receptor for ephrin-B2, is exclusively expressed by
vascular endothelial cells in embryos and is preferentially expressed on veins.
A targeted mutation in EphB4 essentially phenocopies the mutation in ephrin-B2.
These data indicate that ephrin-B2-EphB4 interactions are intrinsically required
in vascular endothelial cells and are consistent with the idea that they mediate
bidirectional signaling essential for angiogenesis.
