# Purification and properties of tryptophan synthase from baker's yeast (Saccharomyces cerevisiae).
**Authors:** Bailey CJ, Turner PD
**Journal:** Biochem J (1983)
**DOI:** [10.1042/bj2090151](https://doi.org/10.1042/bj2090151)
**PMC:** [PMC1154066](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC1154066/)

## Abstract

1. Biochem J. 1983 Jan 1;209(1):151-7. doi: 10.1042/bj2090151.

Purification and properties of tryptophan synthase from baker's yeast
(Saccharomyces cerevisiae).

Bailey CJ, Turner PD.

Tryptophan synthase was purified from baker's yeast. The purified enzyme
exhibited one band on polyacrylamide-gel electrophoresis, had no detectable
N-terminal amino acid and C-terminal alanine. The amino acid composition was
close to that predicted by recent studies on the DNA sequence of the structural
gene for the enzyme. Kinetic parameters for the following three activities were
measured: indole-serine condensation, indolylglycerol phosphate lyase and the
overall reaction of serine with 1-(indol-3-yl)glycerol 3-phosphate. The Km for
indole was much lower than suggested by previous investigations, and the value
of 11 microM was measured by a fluorimetric assay.
