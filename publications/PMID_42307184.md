Developmental and epileptic encephalopathies (DEEs) are severe early-onset
disorders featuring refractory seizures, abnormal EEGs, and developmental
delays. Clinically and etiologically heterogeneous, they include subtypes such
as Dravet syndrome and EIMFS. An increasing number are recognized as
etiology-specific, with distinct genetic causes corresponding to characteristic
phenotypes, highlighting the need for thorough diagnostic evaluation. This study
utilized a multidisciplinary diagnostic approach to investigate DEE. In this
descriptive case series, the reported novel variants include a PLPBP duplication
(c.596dup), an SCN1A insertion (c.3879dup), and an SLC6A1 in-frame deletion
(c.943_945del). Bioinformatics and segregation analysis supported their
pathogenicity, with each variant linked to a distinct clinical phenotype. The
reported novel variants broaden the phenotypic and genotypic spectrum of these
disorders and inform clinical management and genetic counseling.
