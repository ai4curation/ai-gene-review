Gastric cancer metastasis involves the interaction between tumor cells and their
stromal microenvironment. Cancer-associated fibroblasts (CAF) play a pivotal
role in this process, and elucidation of the molecular mechanisms underlying
gastric cancer cell-CAF interactions could uncover potential therapeutic targets
to block metastatic progression. In this study, through transcriptomic profiling
of gastric cancer cell-CAF communications, we identified
galactose-3-O-sulfotransferase 1 (GAL3ST1) as a key regulator of CAF-induced
gastric cancer cell metastatic potential. Mechanistically, GAL3ST1 functioned as
a histone sulfotransferase to sulfate nascent histone H3 at tyrosine 99
(H3Y99sulf) in the cytosol of gastric cancer cells. The sulfated histones were
subsequently translocated to the nucleus via AP2B1, in which they recruited
KAT2A to establish H3K56 acetylation marks that resulted in activation of
β-catenin transcription and drove epithelial-mesenchymal transition.
Furthermore, CAF-derived SEMA7A engaged ITGB1 on gastric cancer cells and
initiated ERK1/2-CEBPB signaling to transcriptionally upregulate GAL3ST1.
Collectively, these findings reveal a role for GAL3ST1 in histone
sulfation-mediated epigenetic regulation and elucidate the
SEMA7A/GAL3ST1/H3Y99sulf axis as a crucial mediator of tumor-stromal cross-talk
in gastric cancer metastasis.
SIGNIFICANCE: Cancer-associated fibroblasts stimulate GAL3ST1 expression in
gastric cancer cells that catalyzes histone H3 sulfation to enable
β-catenin-driven metastasis, highlighting the potential of disrupting
stromal-mediated epigenetic reprogramming to improve patient outcomes.

Introduction

Gastric cancer is one of the most prevalent malignant tumors, being the fifth most common cancer and the fourth leading cause of cancer-related fatalities worldwide (1). Patients with metastatic gastric cancer have poor outcomes, with a median overall survival of approximately 1 year (2). Therefore, it is important to characterize metastatic mechanisms in gastric cancer and develop preventive and therapeutic strategies to improve clinical outcomes for affected patients.

The tumor stroma creates a unique microenvironment that is essential for tumor progression and treatment resistance. Specifically, cancer-associated fibroblasts (CAF) are a substantial component of the tumor stroma, and they exert their tumor-promoting effects via actively communicating with cancer cells (3). CAFs orchestrate tumor–stromal cross-talk through paracrine signaling, matrix remodeling, and epigenetic reprogramming of cancer cells. Notably, CD10+ GPR77+ CAFs have been shown to establish stem cell niches that sustain tumor initiation and confer chemoresistance in breast and lung cancers (4). Our previous research on gastric cancer has revealed that CAFs promote gastric cancer growth and metastasis via secretion of IL6, hepatocyte growth factor (HGF), and IL33 (5–7), whereas CNN1hi CAF-mediated matrix stiffening contributes to acquired resistance to 5-fluorouracil chemotherapy (8). Despite these advances, the molecular circuitry governing gastric cancer cell–CAF communication, particularly the epigenetic mechanisms of tumor–stromal cross-talk, remains incompletely characterized, hindering the development of effective microenvironment-targeted therapies for gastric cancer.

Epigenetic modifications are one of the primary mechanisms governing determination of cellular fate during tumorigenesis and metastatic progression. Throughout cancer development, malignant cells undergo sequential epigenetic modifications that enable critical hallmarks of progression, such as primary tumor escape, circulatory survival, therapy resistance, and distant organ colonization (9–11). Posttranslational histone modification (PTM) is a form of nonmutagenic gene regulation that modulates chromatin architecture and transcriptional accessibility (12). Notably, histone tyrosine sulfation at the H3Y99 residue (H3Y99sulf) has recently emerged as a novel PTM in mammalian cells (13). This modification is catalyzed in the cytoplasm by the sulfotransferase SULT1B1 using 3′-phosphoadenosine-5′-phosphosulfate (PAPS) as a sulfate donor, with subsequent nuclear import and preferential localization to gene promoter regions (13). Although this modification offers a new dimension of epigenetic control, its functional significance in cancer, particularly in gastric cancer, has not yet been established. Moreover, the specificity of multiple sulfotransferases toward histone substrates and their functional roles in tumor–stromal cross-talk remain insufficiently understood.

The aim of this study was to elucidate the epigenetically mediated mechanisms through which CAFs promote metastasis in gastric cancer. Through transcriptomic profiling of gastric cancer cell–CAF communication, we identified galactose-3-O-sulfotransferase 1 (GAL3ST1) as a critical mediator of CAF-induced metastatic potential in gastric cancer cells. We further demonstrated the ability of GAL3ST1 to act as a novel histone sulfotransferase, which catalyzes H3Y99 sulfation on nascent histone H3 in the cytoplasm. We also showed that following AP2B1-dependent nuclear import, H3Y99sulf recruits KAT2A to establish H3K56ac at the CTNNB1 promoter, thereby activating its transcription and promoting epithelial–mesenchymal transition (EMT). Additionally, we showed the ability of CAF-derived SEMA7A to activate ITGB1–ERK1/2–CEBPB signaling to transcriptionally upregulate GAL3ST1 in gastric cancer cells. These findings revealed a previously unrecognized epigenetic axis by which CAFs reprogram gastric cancer cells via GAL3ST1-mediated histone sulfation, providing a mechanistic rationale for targeting this pathway in metastatic gastric cancer.

Functional validation through wound healing and Transwell migration assays confirmed the CAFs’ superior ability to enhance gastric cancer-cell motility relative to NFs (Fig. 1A and B; Supplementary Fig. S1A and S1B). To further elucidate the underlying mechanisms of CAF-mediated tumor promotion, we performed comprehensive transcriptomic analysis of gastric cancer cells following CAF coculture and identified significant enrichment of sphingolipid metabolic pathway genes, with marked upregulation of the key enzyme GAL3ST1 in gastric cancer cells following coculture (Fig. 1C and D). Subsequent qRT-PCR and immunoblotting analyses verified GAL3ST1 induction in gastric cancer cells following coculture with CAFs (Fig. 1E and F; Supplementary Fig. S1C–S1F), whereas other sphingolipid pathway enzymes exhibited minimal alteration (Supplementary Fig. S1G and S1H). To address potential functional redundancy among semaphorins, we analyzed the expression of other ITGB1-modulating semaphorins (SEMA3A, 3C, 4D, and 6A; Supplementary Fig. S6F–S6I; refs. 41, 42). In addition to SEMA7A, only SEMA3C was upregulated in CAFs. However, there was no correlation between SEMA3C and GAL3ST1 expression in gastric cancer tissues (Supplementary Fig. S6J). More importantly, unlike SEMA7A, SEMA3C did not induce GAL3ST1-dependent H3Y99sulf. Thus, although multiple semaphorins are dysregulated in CAFs, SEMA7A is the predominant ligand activating the ITGB1–ERK1/2–GAL3ST1 axis in gastric cancer cells.

To further delineate the transcriptional regulation of GAL3ST1, we employed bioinformatics analysis across four online databases and identified 13 potential transcription factors (TF) for GAL3ST1 (Fig. 5F). CEBPB knockdown in gastric cancer cells resulted in reduced GAL3ST1 expression at both mRNA and protein levels (Fig. 5H and I). Taken together, these findings establish CEBPB as a key transcriptional regulator of GAL3ST1 and demonstrate that CAF-derived SEMA7A promotes GAL3ST1 expression and gastric cancer cell metastatic potential through the ITGB1–ERK1/2–CEBPB signaling pathway.

In summary, the present study identified a previously undocumented role of GAL3ST1 in histone tyrosine sulfation and established the SEMA7A–GAL3ST1–H3Y99sulf signaling axis as a critical mediator of the tumor–stromal cell interactions in gastric cancer metastasis (Fig. 7J). Although further research is needed to explore the therapeutic potential of targeting this pathway, our findings advance the understanding of histone sulfation modifications and underscore the importance of disrupting CAF–gastric cancer cell cross-talk to improve outcomes for patients with gastric cancer.
