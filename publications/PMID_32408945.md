Iron sulfur (Fe-S) clusters rank among the most ancient and conserved prosthetic
groups. Fe-S clusters containing proteins are present in most, if not all,
organisms. Fe-S clusters containing proteins are involved in a wide range of
cellular processes, from gene regulation to central metabolism, via gene
expression, RNA modification or bioenergetics. Fe-S clusters are built by
biogenesis machineries conserved throughout both prokaryotes and eukaryotes. We
focus mostly on bacterial ISC machinery, but not exclusively, as we refer to
eukaryotic ISC system when it brings significant complementary information.
Besides covering the structural and regulatory aspects of Fe-S biogenesis, this
review aims to highlight Fe-S biogenesis facets remaining matters of discussion,
such as the role of frataxin, or the link between fatty acid metabolism and Fe-S
homeostasis. Last, we discuss recent advances on strategies used by different
species to make and use Fe-S clusters in changing redox environmental
conditions.
