# Constitutive transcriptional activation by a beta-catenin-Tcf complex in APC-/- colon carcinoma.

Comment in
    Science. 1997 Mar 21;275(5307):1752-3. doi: 10.1126/science.275.5307.1752.

The adenomatous polyposis coli (APC) tumor suppressor protein binds to
beta-catenin, a protein recently shown to interact with Tcf and Lef
transcription factors. The gene encoding hTcf-4, a Tcf family member that is
expressed in colonic epithelium, was cloned and characterized. hTcf-4
transactivates transcription only when associated with beta-catenin. Nuclei of
APC-/- colon carcinoma cells were found to contain a stable beta-catenin-hTcf-4
complex that was constitutively active, as measured by transcription of a Tcf
reporter gene. Reintroduction of APC removed beta-catenin from hTcf-4 and
abrogated the transcriptional transactivation. Constitutive transcription of Tcf
target genes, caused by loss of APC function, may be a crucial event in the
early transformation of colonic epithelium.
