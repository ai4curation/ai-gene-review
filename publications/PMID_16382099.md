The family of voltage-gated calcium channels serves as the key transducers of
cell surface membrane potential changes into local intracellular calcium
transients that initiate many different physiological events. There are 10
members of the voltage-gated calcium channel family that have been characterized
in mammals, and they serve distinct roles in cellular signal transduction. This
article presents the molecular relationships and physiological functions of
these calcium channel proteins and provides comprehensive information on their
molecular, genetic, physiological, and pharmacological properties.
