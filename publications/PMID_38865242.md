Protein kinase A (PKA) is a conserved kinase crucial for fundamental biological
processes linked to growth, development, and metabolism. The PKA catalytic
subunit is expressed as multiple isoforms in diverse eukaryotes; however, their
contribution to ensuring signaling specificity in response to environmental cues
remains poorly defined. Catalytic subunit activity is classically moderated via
interaction with an inhibitory regulatory subunit. Here, a quantitative mass
spectrometry approach is used to examine heat-stress-induced changes in the
binding of yeast Tpk1-3 catalytic subunits to the Bcy1 regulatory subunit. We
show that Tpk3 is not regulated by Bcy1 binding but, instead, is deactivated
upon heat stress via reversible sequestration into cytoplasmic granules. These
"Tpk3 granules" are enriched for multiple PKA substrates involved in various
metabolic processes, with the Hsp42 sequestrase required for their formation.
Hence, regulated sequestration of Tpk3 provides a mechanism to control
isoform-specific kinase signaling activity during stress conditions.
