The suppressors of cytokine signaling (SOCS) proteins inhibit cytokine action by
direct interaction with Janus kinases or activated cytokine receptors. In
addition to the N-terminal and Src homology 2 domains that mediate these
interactions, SOCS proteins contain a C-terminal SOCS box. DNA data base
searches have identified a number of other protein families that possess a SOCS
box, of which the ankyrin repeat and SOCS box-containing (Asb) proteins
constitute the largest. Although it is known that the SOCS proteins are involved
in the negative regulation of cytokine signaling, the biological and biochemical
functions of the Asbs are largely undefined. Using a proteomics approach, we
demonstrate that creatine kinase B (CKB) interacts with Asb-9 in a specific,
SOCS box-independent manner. This interaction increases the polyubiquitylation
of CKB and decreases total CKB levels within the cell. The targeting of CKB for
degradation by Asb-9 was primarily SOCS box-dependent and suggests that Asb-9
acts as a specific ubiquitin ligase regulating levels of this evolutionarily
conserved enzyme.
