# Identification and structure of AIMP2-DX2 for therapeutic perspectives.
**Authors:** Kim HJ, Jeong MS, Jang SB
**Journal:** BMB Rep (2024)

Identification and structure of AIMP2-DX2 for therapeutic perspectives.

Regulation of cell fate and lung cell differentiation is associated with
Aminoacyl-tRNA synthetases (ARS)-interacting multifunctional protein 2 (AIMP2),
which acts as a non-enzymatic component required for the multi-tRNA synthetase
complex. In response to DNA damage, a component of AIMP2 separates from the
multi-tRNA synthetase complex, binds to p53, and prevents its degradation by
MDM2, inducing apoptosis. Additionally, AIMP2 reduces proliferation in TGF-β and
Wnt pathways, while enhancing apoptotic signaling induced by tumor necrosis
factor-β. Given the crucial role of these pathways in tumorigenesis, AIMP2 is
expected to function as a broad-spectrum tumor suppressor. The full-length AIMP2
transcript consists of four exons, with a small section of the pre-mRNA
undergoing alternative splicing to produce a variant (AIMP2-DX2) lacking the
second exon. AIMP2-DX2 binds to FBP, TRAF2, and p53 similarly to AIMP2, but
competes with AIMP2 for binding to these target proteins, thereby impairing its
tumor-suppressive activity. AIMP2-DX2 is specifically expressed in a diverse
range of cancer cells, including breast cancer, liver cancer, bone cancer, and
stomach cancer. There is growing interest in AIMP2-DX2 as a promising biomarker
for prognosis and diagnosis, with AIMP2-DX2 inhibition attracting significant
interest as a potentially effective therapeutic approach for the treatment of
lung, ovarian, prostate, and nasopharyngeal cancers. [BMB Reports 2024; 57(7):
318-323].

INTRODUCTION

Multi-tRNA synthetase complex (MSC), a macromolecular protein complex, is composed of minoacyl-transfer RNA synthetase-interacting multifunctional protein 2 (AIMP2) (1). When AIMP2 separates from the MSC, it affects the p53, TGF-β, TNF-α, and WNT signaling pathways, each of which functions to prevent tumorigenesis (2-4). AIMP2-DX2, an alternative splicing variant of AIMP2 generated by pre-mRNA splicing that eliminates exon 2, correlates positively with the aggressiveness of certain cancers. AIMP2-DX2 interacts competitively with p53, FBP, and TRAF2 to reduce the tumor-suppressive activity of native AIMP2 (5). AIMP2-DX2 is a tumorigenic factor that is frequently upregulated in a variety of cancers. AIMP2-DX2 is highly expressed in the tissues of colon, lung, and pancreatic cancers (6). Despite its significance as a therapeutic target against cancer, the structure and mechanism of AIMP2-DX2 are not yet known. Here, we elucidate structural insight into AIMP2-DX2 as a potential target for cancer.

AIMPsAminoacyl-tRNA synthetases (ARSs) are essential enzymes that join amino acids to tRNAs in the initial step of protein translation. In mammal, eight different ARSs form a complex with three proteins: glutamyl-prolyl-tRNA synthetase (EPRS), isoleucyl-tRNA synthetase (IRS), leucyl-tRNA synthetase (LRS), glutaminyl-tRNA synthetase (QRS), lysyl-tRNA synthetase (KRS), arginyl-tRNA synthetase (RRS), aspartyl-tRNA synthetase (DRS), and methionyl-tRNA synthetase (MRS), along with AIMP1/p43, AIMP2/p38, and AIMP3/p18 (7).AIMP1, comprising 312 amino acids, binds in particular to RRS in the MRS. It is secreted upon hypoxia or cytokine stimulation, and acts as a cytokine on diverse target cells, such as monocytes/macrophages, endothelial cells, fibroblasts, pancreatic α cells, and dendritic cells (8-13). Secreted AIMP1 functions as a cytokine controlling immune response, angiogenesis, glucose homeostasis, and tissue regeneration (8, 11, 12, 14, 15). AIMP1 induces dendritic cells and increases IL-6 and IL-12 production, vital cytokines that inhibit cancer prevalence (Fig. 1) (16). The 146 amino acid N-terminal domain of AIMP1 enhances collagen synthesis and fibroblast stimulation by TNF-α (11). Additionally, AIMP1 plays a dual role in angiogenesis, promoting endothelial cell migration under low conditions, and inducing apoptosis under high conditions (8). It also targets the liver and adipose tissue, promoting glycogen production for blood glucose supply, while suppressing triacylglycerol conversion to glycerol and free fatty acids (7). Thus, AIMP1 is a signaling factor that can perform several different roles based on the situation and the target cell.AIMP2 interacts with most of the component proteins among the three AIMPs. Its N-domain interacts with RRS, QRS, and AIMP1 to form a single subdomain, while its C-domain clusters with other elements of the complex that include IRS, DRS, KRS, EPRS, and LRS (17). AIMP2 scaffolds the assembly of the multi-tRNA synthetase complex, controlling cell fate via antiproliferative and proapoptotic activities. Upon TNF-β stimulation, AIMP2 translocates to the nucleus to bind to FUSE-binding protein (FBP), promoting ubiquitination and proteasomal-dependent degradation of FBP, leading to c-myc transcriptional suppression (18, 19). AIMP2 binds to p53, blocking MDM2-mediated ubiquitination, and acts as a pro-apoptotic mediator by downregulating TNF-α signaling via TRAF2 degradation (2). Despite lacking E1, E2, or E3 enzyme activities, AIMP2 facilitates c-IAP1 and TRAF2 binding, resulting in TRAF2 ubiquitin-dependent degradation (20, 21).AIMP3, the smallest subunit of the MSC, specifically interacts with MRS (22). In response to DNA damage, AIMP3 is induced and translocated to the nucleus, where it upregulates p53 through the activation of ataxia-telangiectasia mutated (ATM) and ATM and Rad3 related (ATR), which are serine/threonine kinases involved in DNA repair processes. AIMP3 plays a role in enhancing the function of aged tissues and cells, possibly through its involvement in cellular processes. Furthermore, AIMP3 induces cellular senescence by regulating lamin A (23). These results suggest that AIMP3 plays a crucial role in regulating senescence and tumorigenesis (24).

AIMP2-DX2AIMP2 is a 35 kDa protein with a C-terminal GST domain (25). The pre-mRNA of AIMP2 undergoes alternative splicing to produce a splicing variant lacking exon 2, known as AIMP2-DX2. AIMP2-DX2 comprises an N-terminal flexible region (NFR, residues 1-50), a GST N-terminal domain (residues 51-151), and a GST-C terminal domain (residues 152-251). AIMP2-DX2 works as a potent competitor for the tumor-suppressive activities of AIMP2 (Fig. 2A) (5). Additionally, AIMP2-DX2 expression is positively correlated with c-Jun N-terminal kinase (JAK)1-3 and STAT1 of JAK-STAT signaling, and p38α and JNK1 of mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK) signaling (26).Recent studies show that AIMP2-DX2 is closely related to cancer development. Elevated levels of AIMP2-DX2 interfere with the tumor-suppressive activities of AIMP2. Similar to AIMP2, AIMP2-DX2 compromises tumor suppression through competitive interactions with FBP, TRAF2, and p53. While rarely expressed in normal tissues, AIMP2-DX2 is highly expressed in lung, pancreatic, colon, and ovarian cancer tissues. Moreover, overexpression of AIMP2-DX2 can induce the transformation of normal cells into cancerous ones (Fig. 2B) (5, 27, 28). In non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC), glucose uptake is associated with Glucose transporter-1 (Glut-1) expression, a strong predictor of poor prognosis (29). Down-regulation of AIMP2-DX2 significantly reduces glucose uptake, potentially decreasing the rate of glucose consumption in sh-DX2 treated cells (30). In acute myeloid leukemia (AML), the AIMP2-DX2/AIMP2 expression ratio significantly correlates with important cancer pathways, including the MAPK signaling pathway. Importantly, patient-specific therapeutic strategies can be produced by subclassifying hematologic cancers according to their AIMP2-DX2/AIMP2 expression ratios (26). Furthermore, the elevated AIMP2-DX2/AIMP2 ratio significantly correlates with the prognosis of patients with Nasopharyngeal carcinoma (NPC). AIMP2-DX2 is highly expressed in NPC tissues (31). Therefore, inhibition of AIMP2-DX2 expression is a potential therapeutic strategy for cancer control.

AIMP2-DX2 INHIBITORSAIMP2-DX2 has been reported to be highly expressed in a variety of cancers, including nasopharyngeal, lung, breast, ovarian, hematologic, stomach, and bone cancers (26, 28, 30, 31, 43). Inhibition of AIMP2-DX2 has attracted much attention as a promising therapeutic strategy for various cancers.AIMP2-DX2 directly binds to and inhibits p14/ARF. This prevents oncogene-induced apoptosis and senescence, and promotes drug resistance in human lung cancer. Oh et al. identified SLCB050 through chemical screening. SLCB050 inhibits the interaction between AIMP2-DX2 and p14/ARF in vitro and in vivo (43). Lee et al. reported that BC-DX101 reduces the cellular levels of AIMP2-DX2 in lung cancer cells. When BC-DXI01 was administered to a group of mice with AIMP2-DX2-driven tumor xenografts using H460 cells, the tumor sizes and volumes were reduced by up to 60%, compared to the vehicle-treated mice group (44). However, Lee et al. reported that SLCB050 had limited inhibitory effects on non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC, GI50 > 50 μM), while BC-DX101 showed insufficient activity (IC50 = 20.1 μM) in the AIMP2-DX2 luciferase inhibition assay. They discovered AIMP2-DX2 inhibitors based on screening using AIMP2-DX2 luciferase assay and cell cytotoxicity assay. Inhibitor 1 demonstrated an IC50 of 10.4 μM in the AIMP2-DX2 luciferase assay, and a GI50 of 6.5 μM against the lung cancer cell line A549. Inhibitors 2 and 3 can directly bind to AIMP2-DX2, leading to a reduction in AIMP2-DX2 protein levels. In the H460 xenograft model, inhibitors 2 and 3 significantly reduced both tumor size and weight. No experimental group observed a change in body weight (47). Lim et al. structurally characterized the interaction between AIMP2-DX2 and HSP70 using x-ray crystallography and NMR analyses (36). They screened chemicals to find inhibitors that can disrupt the interaction of AIMP2-DX2 and HSP70. One of the chemicals, BC-DXI-495, specifically decreased the levels of DX2 (IC50 = 4.2 μM), but not AIMP2-F, in lung cancer cells. BC-DXI-495 was docked into the hydrophobic pocket of AIMP2-DX2 surrounding L80, T82, F116, and T117 residues, effectively suppressing the AIMP2-DX2-HSP70 interaction. Additionally, BC-DXI-495 significantly reduced tumor growth and weight in mice expressing AIMP2-DX2 WT in vivo. BC-DXI-843 also acts as a tumor-suppressive function in an AIMP2-DX2-dependent manner, via the inhibition of two protein interactions. The IC50 for AIMP2-DX2 degradation by BC-DXI-843 was determined to be 0.92 μM after treating nanoluciferase-AIMP2-DX2 in a dose-dependent manner for 4 h. BC-DXI-843 (50 mg/kg) was intraperitoneally injected into xenografted H460 cells in mice every other day for 15 days, resulting in a steady decline in embedded tumor volume after BC-DXI-843 administration (48). Kim et al. screened for chemicals that could specifically suppress the binding of AIMP2-DX2 and KRAS (1,102). They selected the 2-phenylthiophene analogue, BC-DXI-32982, from a structural diversity chemical library consisting of (1,697) in-house and commercially available compounds. BC-DXI-32982 emerged as the most potent inhibitor of the interaction between AIMP2-DX2 and KRAS4B, with an IC50 of 0.18 μM. Additionally, in a mouse xenograft assay using the H460 cell line, treatment with BC-DXI-32982 five times at doses of 1 and 5 mg/kg over 12 days reduced tumor size and weight in a dose-dependent manner (42). The degree of malignancy in both lung and chemo-resistant ovarian cancer is correlated with an increase in the expression ratio of AIMP2-DX2 to AIMP2 (5, 28).Kim et al. discovered pyrimethamine at the Korea Chemical Bank, Korea Institute of Chemical Technology. Pyrimethamine can suppress the level of AIMP2-DX2 (IC50 = 0.73 μM) in A549 cells expressing nano luciferase-tagged AIMP2-DX2 (49). Furthermore, intraperitoneal administration of pyrimethamine significantly decreased tumor size and weight in an in vivo mouse xenograft assay using H460 cells. Glut-1, a potent indicator of a poor prognosis, is related to glucose uptake in NSCLC (50). Numerous cellular processes, such as cell division and growth, interactions between cells, cancer metastasis, and differentiation, depend on protein glycosylation (51). Chang et al. revealed that in NCI-H460 cells, the inhibition of AIMP2-DX2 through lentiviral-mediated short hairpin (sh)RNA (sh-DX2) reduced the rate of glucose uptake and gluts. In the Golgi apparatus, down-regulation of AIMP2-DX2 decreased glycosyltransferase (GnT)-V, while promoting the GnT-V antagonist GnT-III. Additionally, the epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR)/MAPK signaling pathway was suppressed by down-regulating AIMP2-DX2, which decreased the expression of the proliferation marker Ki-67 in nuclei (30).

Chang et al. revealed that in NCI-H460 cells, the inhibition of AIMP2-DX2 through lentiviral-mediated short hairpin (sh)RNA (sh-DX2) reduced the rate of glucose uptake and gluts. In the Golgi apparatus, down-regulation of AIMP2-DX2 decreased glycosyltransferase (GnT)-V, while promoting the GnT-V antagonist GnT-III. Additionally, the epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR)/MAPK signaling pathway was suppressed by down-regulating AIMP2-DX2, which decreased the expression of the proliferation marker Ki-67 in nuclei (30).

DISCUSSIONAIMP2 plays a variety of roles in response to stress signals, and aids in regulating the fate of a cell. It exhibits antiproliferative properties by enhancing the growth-arresting signal of TGF-β. Additionally, it stimulates p53 and the apoptotic signal of TNF-α, thereby inducing cell death. In vivo mouse models of AIMP2’s antiproliferative and proapoptotic functions have been validated. In lung, colon, and skin carcinogenesis models, AIMP2-heterozygous mice are more susceptible to tumorigenesis than wild-type mice, indicating that AIMP2 is a haploinsufficient tumor suppressor with a unique mechanism (2, 4, 19, 21, 52). AIMP2-DX2 is a variant of AIMP2 produced by alternative splicing, resulting in the deletion of exon 2. It has been found in a variety of cancer cell lines and tissues. Through competitive interactions with p53, FBP, and TRAF2, this variant can still inhibit the tumor-suppressive function of native AIMP2, even though it is no longer able to associate with MSCs (3, 5, 27, 28, 53). Tumor growth was inhibited by siRNA or chemical inhibition of AIMP2-DX2 expression, suggesting that it may be a promising target for therapy.
