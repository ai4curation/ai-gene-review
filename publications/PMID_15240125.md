# Low pKa lysine residues at the active site of sarcosine oxidase from Corynebacterium sp. U-96.
**Authors:** Mukouyama EB, Oguchi M, Kodera Y, Maeda T, Suzuki H
**Journal:** Biochem Biophys Res Commun (2004)
**DOI:** [10.1016/j.bbrc.2004.05.219](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.bbrc.2004.05.219)

## Abstract

1. Biochem Biophys Res Commun. 2004 Jul 30;320(3):846-51. doi:
10.1016/j.bbrc.2004.05.219.

Low pKa lysine residues at the active site of sarcosine oxidase from
Corynebacterium sp. U-96.

Mukouyama EB(1), Oguchi M, Kodera Y, Maeda T, Suzuki H.

Author information:
(1)Department of Biosciences, School of Science, Kitasato University, 1-15-1
Kitasato, Sagamihara, Kanagawa 228-8555, Japan. mukoyama@sci.kitasato-u.ac.jp

Sarcosine oxidase from Corynebacterium sp. U-96 is inactivated by iodoacetamide
with the modification of two specific residues. Comparing the amino acid
sequence and mass spectra of the peptide fragments containing the modified
residues with those from the native enzyme, the modified residues were
identified to be lysine. The pKa of these residues were estimated to be 8.5 and
6.7 from the pH dependence of inactivation in the presence and absence of the
competitive inhibitor, acetate. These estimated pKa values are much lower than
that of the epsilon-amino group of lysine residue. There may be unique
microenvironments around these residues that activate their -amino groups to be
susceptible to iodoacetamide. A possible role of the lysine residue with pKa 6.7
is discussed.
