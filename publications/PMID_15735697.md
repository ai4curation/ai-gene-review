# Ubiquitin-proteasome degradation of KLF5 transcription factor in cancer and untransformed epithelial cells.
**Authors:** Chen C, Sun X, Ran Q, Wilkinson KD, Murphy TJ, Simons JW, Dong JT
**Journal:** Oncogene (2005)
**DOI:** [10.1038/sj.onc.1208497](https://doi.org/10.1038/sj.onc.1208497)

## Abstract

1. Oncogene. 2005 May 5;24(20):3319-27. doi: 10.1038/sj.onc.1208497.

Ubiquitin-proteasome degradation of KLF5 transcription factor in cancer and
untransformed epithelial cells.

Chen C(1), Sun X, Ran Q, Wilkinson KD, Murphy TJ, Simons JW, Dong JT.

Author information:
(1)Department of Oncology and Hematology, Winship Cancer Institute, Emory
University School of Medicine, 1365-C Clifton Road, Atlanta, GA, USA.

Ubiquitin-mediated proteolysis plays a central role in controlling intracellular
levels of essential regulatory molecules such as p53, cyclins, myc, BRCA1,
HIF-1alpha, etc. The Kruppel-like factor 5 (KLF5) transcription factor regulates
biological processes involved in carcinogenesis, angiogenesis, and smooth muscle
cell differentiation. In carcinogenesis, KLF5's role has been indicated by
frequent genetic deletion as well as functional studies. Here we show that KLF5
is an unstable protein with a short half-life. Destruction of KLF5 was prevented
by each of the proteasome-specific inhibitors tested but not by an inhibitor for
trypsin-like proteases and cysteine proteases or by a lysosome inhibitor in
epithelial cells. Furthermore, KLF5 underwent ubiquitination, and deletion of a
56-amino-acid sequence adjacent to a known transactivation domain of KLF5
significantly reduced its ubiquitination and degradation. Interestingly, cancer
cells appeared to be more active in KLF5 degradation than untransformed
epithelial cells, yet their proteasome activity was not higher. These results
suggest that KLF5 protein is degraded at least in part through
ubiquitination-proteasome pathway, which may have become hyperactive for KLF5 in
cancer cells.
