Author information:

michael.kertesz@man.ac.uk

Microorganisms require sulfur for growth, and obtain it either from inorganic
sulfate or from organosulfur compounds such as sulfonates, sulfate esters, or
sulfur-containing amino acids. Transport of sulfate into the cell is catalyzed
either by ATP binding cassette (ABC)-type transporters (SulT family) or by major
facilitator superfamily-type transporters (SulP family). By contrast, the
sulfonate and sulfate ester transporters identified to date are all ABC-type
systems, whose synthesis is tightly regulated by the sulfur supply to the cell,
mediated by the CysB protein and other transcriptional regulators of the
LysR-family.
