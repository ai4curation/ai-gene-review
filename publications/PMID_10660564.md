# The bifunctional active site of S-adenosylmethionine synthetase. Roles of the basic residues.
**Authors:** Taylor JC, Markham GD
**Journal:** J Biol Chem (2000)
**DOI:** [10.1074/jbc.275.6.4060](https://doi.org/10.1074/jbc.275.6.4060)

## Abstract

1. J Biol Chem. 2000 Feb 11;275(6):4060-5. doi: 10.1074/jbc.275.6.4060.

The bifunctional active site of S-adenosylmethionine synthetase. Roles of the
basic residues.

Taylor JC(1), Markham GD.

Author information:
(1)Institute for Cancer Research, Fox Chase Cancer Center, Philadelphia,
Pennsylvania 19111, USA.

S-adenosylmethionine (AdoMet) synthetase catalyzes a unique two-step enzymatic
reaction leading to formation of the primary biological alkylating agent. The
crystal structure of Escherichia coli AdoMet synthetase shows that the active
site, which lies between two subunits, contains four lysines and one histidine
as basic residues. In order to test the proposed charge and hydrogen bonding
roles in catalytic function, each lysine has been changed to an uncharged
methionine or alanine, and the histidine has been altered to asparagine. The
resultant enzyme variants are all tetramers like the wild type enzyme; however,
circular dichroism spectra show reductions in helix content for the K245*M and
K269M mutants. (The asterisk denotes that the residue is in the second subunit.)
Four mutants have k(cat) reductions of approximately 10(3)-10(4)-fold in AdoMet
synthesis; however, the k(cat) of K165*M variant is only reduced 2-fold. In each
mutant, there is a smaller catalytic impairment in the partial reaction of
tripolyphosphate hydrolysis. The K165*A enzyme has a 100-fold greater k(cat) for
tripolyphosphate hydrolysis than the wild type enzyme, but this mutant is not
activated by AdoMet in contrast to the wild type enzyme. The properties of these
mutants require reassessment of the catalytic roles of these residues.
