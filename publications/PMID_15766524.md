# A unique RNA Fold in the RumA-RNA-cofactor ternary complex contributes to substrate selectivity and enzymatic function.
**Authors:** Lee TT, Agarwalla S, Stroud RM
**Journal:** Cell (2005)
**DOI:** [10.1016/j.cell.2004.12.037](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2004.12.037)

## Abstract

1. Cell. 2005 Mar 11;120(5):599-611. doi: 10.1016/j.cell.2004.12.037.

A unique RNA Fold in the RumA-RNA-cofactor ternary complex contributes to
substrate selectivity and enzymatic function.

Lee TT(1), Agarwalla S, Stroud RM.

Author information:
(1)Department of Biochemistry and Biophysics, University of California, San
Francisco, San Francisco, CA 94143, USA.

A single base (U1939) within E. coli 23S ribosomal RNA is methylated by its
dedicated enzyme, RumA. The structure of RumA/RNA/S-adenosylhomocysteine
uncovers the mechanism for achieving unique selectivity. The single-stranded
substrate is "refolded" on the enzyme into a compact conformation with six key
intra-RNA interactions. The RNA substrate contributes directly to catalysis. In
addition to the target base, a second base is "flipped out" from the core loop
to stack against the adenine of the cofactor S-adenosylhomocysteine. Nucleotides
in permuted sequence order are stacked into the site vacated by the everted
target U1939 and compensate for the energetic penalty of base eversion. The 3'
hairpin segment of the RNA binds distal to the active site and provides binding
energy that contributes to enhanced catalytic efficiency. Active collaboration
of RNA in catalysis leads us to conclude that RumA and its substrate RNA may
reflect features from the earliest RNA-protein era.
