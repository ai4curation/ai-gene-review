# The solution structure of bacteriophage lambda protein W, a small morphogenetic protein possessing a novel fold.
**Authors:** Maxwell KL, Yee AA, Booth V, Arrowsmith CH, Gold M, Davidson AR
**Journal:** J Mol Biol (2001)
**DOI:** [10.1006/jmbi.2001.4582](https://doi.org/10.1006/jmbi.2001.4582)

## Abstract

1. J Mol Biol. 2001 Apr 20;308(1):9-14. doi: 10.1006/jmbi.2001.4582.

The solution structure of bacteriophage lambda protein W, a small morphogenetic
protein possessing a novel fold.

Maxwell KL(1), Yee AA, Booth V, Arrowsmith CH, Gold M, Davidson AR.

Author information:
(1)Department of Molecular and Medical Genetics, University of Toronto, Ontario,
Canada.

Protein W (gpW) from bacteriophage lambda is required for the stabilization of
DNA within the phage head and for attachment of tails onto the head during
morphogenesis. Although comprised of only 68 residues, it likely interacts with
at least two other proteins in the mature phage and with DNA. Thus, gpW is an
intriguing subject for detailed structural studies. We have determined its
solution structure using NMR spectroscopy and have found it to possesses a novel
fold consisting of two alpha-helices and a single two-stranded beta-sheet
arranged around a well-packed hydrophobic core. The 14 C-terminal residues of
gpW, which are essential for function, are unstructured in solution.
