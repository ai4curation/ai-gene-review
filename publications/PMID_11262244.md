# A requirement for Notch in the genesis of a subset of glial cells in the Drosophila embryonic central nervous system which arise through asymmetric divisions.
**Authors:** Udolph G, Rath P, Chia W
**Journal:** Development (2001)
**DOI:** [10.1242/dev.128.8.1457](https://doi.org/10.1242/dev.128.8.1457)

## Abstract

1. Development. 2001 Apr;128(8):1457-66. doi: 10.1242/dev.128.8.1457.

A requirement for Notch in the genesis of a subset of glial cells in the
Drosophila embryonic central nervous system which arise through asymmetric
divisions.

Udolph G(1), Rath P, Chia W.

Author information:
(1)Developmental Neurobiology Laboratory, Institute of Molecular and Cell
Biology, 117609 Singapore.

In the Drosophila central nervous system (CNS) glial cells are known to be
generated from glioblasts, which produce exclusively glia or neuroglioblasts
that bifurcate to produce both neuronal and glial sublineages. We show that the
genesis of a subset of glial cells, the subperineurial glia (SPGs), involves a
new mechanism and requires Notch. We demonstrate that the SPGs share direct
sibling relationships with neurones and are the products of asymmetric
divisions. This mechanism of specifying glial cell fates within the CNS is novel
and provides further insight into regulatory interactions leading to glial cell
fate determination. Furthermore, we show that Notch signalling positively
regulates glial cells missing (gcm) expression in the context of SPG
development.
