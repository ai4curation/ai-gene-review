# Human serum albumin enhances the hemolytic activity of Vibrio vulnificus.
**Authors:** Choi MH, Sun HY, Park RY, Bai YH, Chung YY, Kim CM, Shin SH
**Journal:** Biol Pharm Bull (2006)
**DOI:** [10.1248/bpb.29.180](https://doi.org/10.1248/bpb.29.180)

## Abstract

1. Biol Pharm Bull. 2006 Jan;29(1):180-2. doi: 10.1248/bpb.29.180.

Human serum albumin enhances the hemolytic activity of Vibrio vulnificus.

Choi MH(1), Sun HY, Park RY, Bai YH, Chung YY, Kim CM, Shin SH.

Author information:
(1)Research Center for Resistant Cells, Chosun University Medical School,
Gwangju, Republic of Korea.

Vibrio vulnificus hemolysin (VvhA) is inactivated in the late growth phase by
its oligomerization. Albumin is known to affect the activities of many bacterial
toxins. In this study, we investigated the effects of human or bovine serum
albumin (HSA or BSA) on the production and activity of VvhA. HSA did not affect
V. vulnificus growth and vvhA transcription. However, VvhA hemolytic activity in
culture supernatants was significantly higher in the presence of HSA than in the
absence of HSA. By Western blot analysis, the oligomerization of VvhA was
inhibited and the remaining active VvhA monomer was increased in culture
supernatants containing HSA. BSA produced similar results. These findings
indicate that both HSA and BSA stabilize VvhA and delay VvhA inactivation by
oligomerization, and thus enhance VvhA activity.
