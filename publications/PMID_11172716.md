# Transcriptional coactivator, CIITA, is an acetyltransferase that bypasses a promoter requirement for TAF(II)250.
**Authors:** Raval A, Howcroft TK, Weissman JD, Kirshner S, Zhu XS, Yokoyama K, Ting J, Singer DS
**Journal:** Mol Cell (2001)
**DOI:** [10.1016/s1097-2765(01)00159-9](https://doi.org/10.1016/s1097-2765(01)00159-9)

## Abstract

1. Mol Cell. 2001 Jan;7(1):105-15. doi: 10.1016/s1097-2765(01)00159-9.

Transcriptional coactivator, CIITA, is an acetyltransferase that bypasses a
promoter requirement for TAF(II)250.

Raval A(1), Howcroft TK, Weissman JD, Kirshner S, Zhu XS, Yokoyama K, Ting J,
Singer DS.

Author information:
(1)Experimental Immunology Branch, National Cancer Institute, Building 10, Room
4B-36, National Institutes of Health, Bethesda, MD 20892, USA.

The CIITA coactivator is essential for transcriptional activation of MHC class
II genes and mediates enhanced MHC class I transcription. We now report that
CIITA contains an intrinsic acetyltransferase (AT) activity that maps to a
region within the N-terminal segment of CIITA, between amino acids 94 and 132.
The AT activity is regulated by the C-terminal GTP-binding domain and is
stimulated by GTP. CIITA-mediated transactivation depends on the AT activity.
Further, we report that, although constitutive MHC class I transcription depends
on TAF(II)250, CIITA activates the promoter in the absence of functional
TAF(II)250.
