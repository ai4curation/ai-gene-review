# PP2A regulatory subunit PP2A-B' counteracts S6K phosphorylation.
**Authors:** Hahn K, Miranda M, Francis VA, Vendrell J, Zorzano A, Teleman AA
**Journal:** Cell Metab (2010)
**DOI:** [10.1016/j.cmet.2010.03.015](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cmet.2010.03.015)

## Abstract

1. Cell Metab. 2010 May 5;11(5):438-44. doi: 10.1016/j.cmet.2010.03.015.

PP2A regulatory subunit PP2A-B' counteracts S6K phosphorylation.

Hahn K(1), Miranda M, Francis VA, Vendrell J, Zorzano A, Teleman AA.

Author information:
(1)German Cancer Research Center (DKFZ), Im Neuenheimer Feld 580, 69120
Heidelberg, Germany.

The insulin/TOR signaling pathway plays a crucial role in animal homeostasis,
sensing nutrient status to regulate organismal growth and metabolism. We
identify here the Drosophila B' regulatory subunit of PP2A (PP2A-B') as a novel,
conserved component of the insulin pathway that specifically targets the PP2A
holoenzyme to dephosphorylate S6K. PP2A-B' knockout flies have elevated S6K
phosphorylation and exhibit phenotypes typical of elevated insulin signaling
such as reduced total body triglycerides and reduced longevity. We show that
PP2A-B' interacts with S6K both physically and genetically. The human homolog of
PP2A-B', PPP2R5C, also counteracts S6K1 phosphorylation, indicating a conserved
mechanism in mammals. Since S6K affects development of cancer and metabolic
disease, our data identify PPP2R5C as a novel factor of potential medical
relevance.
