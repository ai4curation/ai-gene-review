# Mutations of two PMS homologues in hereditary nonpolyposis colon cancer.
**Authors:** Nicolaides NC, Papadopoulos N, Liu B, Wei YF, Carter KC, Ruben SM, Rosen CA, Haseltine WA, Fleischmann RD, Fraser CM
**Journal:** Nature (1994)
**DOI:** [10.1038/371075a0](https://doi.org/10.1038/371075a0)

## Abstract

1. Nature. 1994 Sep 1;371(6492):75-80. doi: 10.1038/371075a0.

Mutations of two PMS homologues in hereditary nonpolyposis colon cancer.

Nicolaides NC(1), Papadopoulos N, Liu B, Wei YF, Carter KC, Ruben SM, Rosen CA,
Haseltine WA, Fleischmann RD, Fraser CM, et al.

Author information:
(1)Johns Hopkins Oncology Center, Baltimore, Maryland 21231.

Hereditary nonpolyposis colorectal cancer (HNPCC) is one of man's commonest
hereditary diseases. Several studies have implicated a defect in DNA mismatch
repair in the pathogenesis of this disease. In particular, hMSH2 and hMLH1
homologues of the bacterial DNA mismatch repair genes mutS and mutL,
respectively, were shown to be mutated in a subset of HNPCC cases. Here we
report the nucleotide sequence, chromosome localization and mutational analysis
of hPMS1 and hPMS2, two additional homologues of the prokaryotic mutL gene. Both
hPMS1 and hPMS2 were found to be mutated in the germline of HNPCC patients. This
doubles the number of genes implicated in HNPCC and may help explain the
relatively high incidence of this disease.
