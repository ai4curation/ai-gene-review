# Apolipoprotein L-I promotes trypanosome lysis by forming pores in lysosomal membranes.
**Authors:** Pérez-Morga D, Vanhollebeke B, Paturiaux-Hanocq F, Nolan DP, Lins L, Homblé F, Vanhamme L, Tebabi P, Pays A, Poelvoorde P, Jacquet A, Brasseur R, Pays E
**Journal:** Science (2005)
**DOI:** [10.1126/science.1114566](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1114566)

## Abstract

1. Science. 2005 Jul 15;309(5733):469-72. doi: 10.1126/science.1114566.

Apolipoprotein L-I promotes trypanosome lysis by forming pores in lysosomal
membranes.

Pérez-Morga D(1), Vanhollebeke B, Paturiaux-Hanocq F, Nolan DP, Lins L, Homblé
F, Vanhamme L, Tebabi P, Pays A, Poelvoorde P, Jacquet A, Brasseur R, Pays E.

Author information:
(1)Laboratory of Molecular Parasitology, IBMM, Université Libre de Bruxelles,
12, rue des Profs Jeener et Brachet, B6041 Gosselies, Belgium.

Apolipoprotein L-I is the trypanolytic factor of human serum. Here we show that
this protein contains a membrane pore-forming domain functionally similar to
that of bacterial colicins, flanked by a membrane-addressing domain. In lipid
bilayer membranes, apolipoprotein L-I formed anion channels. In Trypanosoma
brucei, apolipoprotein L-I was targeted to the lysosomal membrane and triggered
depolarization of this membrane, continuous influx of chloride, and subsequent
osmotic swelling of the lysosome until the trypanosome lysed.
