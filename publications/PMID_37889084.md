ABSTRACT

ENDOG, a mitochondrial intermembrane space located endonuclease, participates in DNA fragmentation and apoptosis by translocating to the nucleus. ENDOG can also relocate to the mitochondrial matrix, where it regulates mitochondrial genome cleavage. However, the biological function of cytoplasm-translocated ENDOG remains unclear. Our previous study reported that starvation induces the release of ENDOG from mitochondria to the cytoplasm, promoting macroautophagy/autophagy in a process conserved across species. We demonstrate that ENDOG can be phosphorylated by GSK3B, which enhances ENDOG binding to YWHAG/14-3-3γ, and leads to the release of TSC2 and PIK3C3/VPS34 from YWHAG/14-3-3γ, followed by MTORC1 pathway suppression and autophagy initiation. Additionally, we recently reported that ENDOG can also activate the MTORC2-AKT-ACLY signaling axis by promoting the release of RICTOR and TSC2 from YWHAG/14-3-3γ, resulting in acetyl-CoA production. Furthermore, cytoplasmic ENDOG can translocate to the endoplasmic reticulum, where it binds with HSPA5/BIP to release ERN1/IRE1a-EIF2AK3/PERK to activate the endoplasmic reticulum stress response, eventually promoting lipid synthesis. Collectively, ENDOG will be released from the mitochondrial intermembrane space, and translocated to the mitochondrial matrix, cytoplasm, and nucleus during different stress stimulation, where it digests DNA or interacts with crucial proteins to regulate different biological functions, including apoptosis, autophagy, mitophagy, and lipid synthesis.

KEYWORDS: Autophagy, ENDOG, endoplasmic reticulum, MTORC1, MTORC2

ENDOG (endonuclease G), a nuclear-encoded DNA/RNA nonspecific ββα-Me-finger nuclease, is mainly located in the mitochondrial intermembrane space. ENDOG is well-known to be able to translocate from mitochondria to the nucleus, where it induces fragmentation of genomic DNA, causing genome instability and apoptosis. ENDOG can also relocate from the mitochondrial intermembrane space to the mitochondrial matrix, regulating mitochondrial genome cleavage and replication. Our previous study reported that paternal ENDOG relocates to the mitochondrial matrix to digest the paternal mtDNA and promote paternal mitochondrial elimination through the maternal autophagy machinery after fertilization. However, little is known about the biological function of cytoplasm-translocated ENDOG.

In our previous work, we demonstrated that cytoplasm-translocated ENDOG promotes autophagy, in a process that is conserved across species. Mechanistically, ENDOG promotes autophagy flux by suppressing MTORC1 signaling and activating the DNA damage response. Upon starvation, ENDOG is released from mitochondria to the cytosol, where it is phosphorylated by GSK3B. The phosphorylation enhances the interaction between ENDOG and YWHAG/14-3-3γ, which dissociates TSC2 and PIK3C3/VPS34 from YWHAG/14-3-3γ, and eventually suppresses the MTORC1 pathway and promotes autophagy initiation. In parallel, nuclear-translocated ENDOG activates the DNA damage response and triggers autophagy through its endonuclease activity.

Serendipitously, when visualizing the autophagosome formation of endog knockout cells and mouse livers by transmission electron microscopy (TEM), we also observed strikingly decreased lipid droplets, suggesting that ENDOG may play an essential role in regulating lipid metabolism. Indeed, we demonstrated that cytoplasmic ENDOG competitively binds with YWHAG/14-3-3γ to dissociate the RICTOR protein, activating the MTORC2-AKT-ACLY axis and eventually contributing to acetyl-CoA production and lipid accumulation [ 1 ]. Furthermore, we observed that cytoplasmic ENDOG can also translocate to the endoplasmic reticulum and then bind with HSPA5/BIP to activate the endoplasmic reticulum stress response, which promotes the expression of lipid synthesis enzymes. Taken together, we demonstrate that ENDOG depletion suppresses acetyl-CoA production and lipid synthesis by suppressing the MTORC2-AKT-ACLY axis, along with reducing the endoplasmic reticulum stress response, which eventually alleviates high-fat diet-induced nonalcoholic fatty liver disease in female mice.

In summary, we revealed the biological functions of cytoplasm-translocated ENDOG. Under stress, CASP8-BID or VDAC (voltage-dependent anion channel) proteins mediate the release of ENDOG from mitochondria. In the cytosol, ENDOG is phosphorylated at T128 and S288 by GSK3B, which enhances the interaction between ENDOG and YWHAG/14-3-3γ. ENDOG competitively binds with YWHAG/14-3-3γ causing the dissociation of TSC2, PIK3C3/VPS34, and RICTOR from YWHAG/14-3-3γ. The released TSC2 suppresses the MTORC1 pathway and promotes autophagy initiation. However, the released RICTOR and TSC2 both can activate the MTORC2 pathway. Besides, the cytoplasmic ENDOG could also be translocated to the endoplasmic reticulum, then bind with HSPA5/BIP and release ERN1/IRE1a-EIF2AK3/PERK to activate the endoplasmic reticulum stress response, promoting lipid synthesis. In addition, cytoplasmic ENDOG can translocate into the nucleus to activate the DNA damage response, which promotes autophagy. Besides, paternal ENDOG relocates to the mitochondrial matrix during fertilization to digest the paternal mtDNA and promote paternal mitochondrial elimination, which maintains the mitochondrial maternal inheritance ( Figure 1 ).

Future work will be needed to determine the underlying mechanism that manipulates the translocation of ENDOG to the mitochondrial matrix, cytoplasm, and nucleus. Moreover, could ENDOG translocate to other organelles than the endoplasmic reticulum? Our data showed that the mitochondrial targeting sequence-deleted form of ENDOG fails to induce the DNA damage response, but it can still promote autophagy. In addition, we detected mature ENDOG, which needs to be imported into mitochondria, in the ER fraction. Thus, we hypothesize that the maturation of ENDOG is essential for its translocation, including translocating to the mitochondrial matrix, cytoplasm, endoplasmic reticulum, and nucleus. Further studies are needed to resolve these questions.
