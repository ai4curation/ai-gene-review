# Trading places on DNA--a three-point switch underlies primer handoff from primase to the replicative DNA polymerase.
**Authors:** Yuzhakov A, Kelman Z, O'Donnell M
**Journal:** Cell (1999)
**DOI:** [10.1016/s0092-8674(00)80968-x](https://doi.org/10.1016/s0092-8674(00)80968-x)

## Abstract

1. Cell. 1999 Jan 8;96(1):153-63. doi: 10.1016/s0092-8674(00)80968-x.

Trading places on DNA--a three-point switch underlies primer handoff from
primase to the replicative DNA polymerase.

Yuzhakov A(1), Kelman Z, O'Donnell M.

Author information:
(1)The Rockefeller University, New York, New York 10021, USA.

This study reports a primase-to-polymerase switch in E. coli that closely links
primase action with extension by DNA polymerase III holoenzyme. We find that
primase tightly grips its RNA primer, protecting it from the action of other
proteins. However, primase must be displaced before the beta sliding clamp can
be assembled on the primed site. A single subunit of the holoenzyme, chi, is
dedicated to this primase displacement task. The displacement mechanism depends
on a third protein, SSB. Primase requires contact to SSB for its grip on the
primed site. The chi subunit also binds SSB, upon which the primase-to-SSB
contact is destabilized leading to dissociation of primase and assembly of beta
onto the RNA primer. The conservation of this three-point switch, in which two
proteins exchange places on DNA via mutually exclusive interaction with a third
protein, is discussed.
