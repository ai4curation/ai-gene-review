# Phosphorylation of the cohesin subunit Scc1 by Polo/Cdc5 kinase regulates sister chromatid separation in yeast.
**Authors:** Alexandru G, Uhlmann F, Mechtler K, Poupart MA, Nasmyth K
**Journal:** Cell (2001)
**DOI:** [10.1016/s0092-8674(01)00362-2](https://doi.org/10.1016/s0092-8674(01)00362-2)

## Abstract

1. Cell. 2001 May 18;105(4):459-72. doi: 10.1016/s0092-8674(01)00362-2.

Phosphorylation of the cohesin subunit Scc1 by Polo/Cdc5 kinase regulates sister
chromatid separation in yeast.

Alexandru G(1), Uhlmann F, Mechtler K, Poupart MA, Nasmyth K.

Author information:
(1)Research Institute of Molecular Pathology (IMP), Dr Bohr-Gasse 7, A-1030,
Vienna, Austria.

At the onset of anaphase, a caspase-related protease (separase) destroys the
link between sister chromatids by cleaving the cohesin subunit Scc1. During most
of the cell cycle, separase is kept inactive by binding to an inhibitory protein
called securin. Separase activation requires proteolysis of securin, which is
mediated by an ubiquitin protein ligase called the anaphase-promoting complex.
Cells regulate anaphase entry by delaying securin ubiquitination until all
chromosomes have attached to the mitotic spindle. Though no longer regulated by
this mitotic surveillance mechanism, sister separation remains tightly cell
cycle regulated in yeast mutants lacking securin. We show here that the
Polo/Cdc5 kinase phosphorylates serine residues adjacent to Scc1 cleavage sites
and strongly enhances their cleavage. Phosphorylation of separase recognition
sites may be highly conserved and regulates sister chromatid separation
independently of securin.
