# Formaldehyde lacks genotoxicity in formaldehyde-hyperresistant strains of the yeast Saccharomyces cerevisiae.
**Authors:** Wehner E, Brendel M
**Journal:** Mutat Res (1993)
**DOI:** [10.1016/0027-5107(93)90134-2](https://doi.org/10.1016/0027-5107(93)90134-2)

## Abstract

1. Mutat Res. 1993 Sep;289(1):91-6. doi: 10.1016/0027-5107(93)90134-2.

Formaldehyde lacks genotoxicity in formaldehyde-hyperresistant strains of the
yeast Saccharomyces cerevisiae.

Wehner E(1), Brendel M.

Author information:
(1)Institut für Mikrobiologie, J.W. Goethe-Universität, Frankfurt/Main, Germany.

Formaldehyde is a weak mutagen and recombinagen in wild type strains of
Saccharomyces cerevisiae. Genotoxicity of formaldehyde is influenced by the
activity of the SFA yeast gene. Yeast transformants containing multiple copies
of the plasmid-contained SFA gene are hyperresistant to the chemical and grow in
the presence of 5 mM formaldehyde, a concentration lethal for the wild type. The
SFA-encoded protein mediates the degradation of formaldehyde and its activity is
most probably responsible for the low or absent genotoxic effects in
formaldehyde-treated cells. Multi-copy vectors containing the SFA gene are
retained in yeast transformants growing in complex media supplemented with 5 mM
formaldehyde. Cells harboring such multi-copy vectors may, therefore, be
propagated in large batch cultures in undefined media in the presence of
formaldehyde concentrations toxic to the wild type.
