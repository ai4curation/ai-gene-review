Dornfeld, C., Weisberg, A.J., Ritesh, K.C., Dudareva, N., Jelesko, J.G., and Maeda, H.A. (2014). Phylobiochemical characterization of class-Ib aspartate/prephenate aminotransferases reveals evolution of the plant arogenate phenylalanine pathway. Plant Cell 26: 3101–3114 .

During on-going study, the authors realized that there were two related mistakes in the descriptions of Arabidopsis prephenate aminotransferase (PPA-AT) mutant enzymes in Figure 6 , Table 3 , and Supplemental Table 4 . First, the mutated amino acid residues were incorrectly labeled as K169S rather than K169V. Resequencing of the original cloning plasmids confirmed that all three mutant plasmids had expected Thr-84 to Val and/or Lys-169 to Val changes. Thus, this was merely an editorial mistake. Second, the expression of the K169V (previously labeled as K169S) single mutant in E. coli host (Rossetta2) was found to carry the T84V mutant plasmid, instead of the K169V plasmid. It appears that an incorrect plasmid (T84V instead of K169V) was inadvertently transformed into Rossetta2 cell. We apologize for the mistakes and here present recharacterization of PPA-AT mutants containing the correct K169V mutation.

The correct K169V plasmid was transformed to the Rossetta2 cell, and the K169V mutant protein was produced and purified as described in the method of the original article. The other three recombinant enzymes, wild type, T84V single, and T84VK169V double mutants, were also obtained, purified, and characterized along with the new K169V single mutant. While wild type, T84V single, and T84VK169V double mutants showed consistent results as described in the original article, the new K169V mutant had very low PPA-AT and Asp-AT activities, similar to the T84VK169V double mutants ( Figures 6A and 6B ). The new K169V mutant also exhibited partial HPP-AT activity with Ala or Trp amino donor ( Figure 6C ), although its catalytic efficiencies were much lower than that of the T84VK169V double mutant ( Table 3 ). These new results indicate that the K169V mutation had more drastic effects than the T84V mutation on the loss of PPA-AT (and Asp-AT) activity with Asp amino donor ( Figures 6A and 6B ) and the gain of HPP-AT activity with Ala and Trp amino donors ( Figure 6C ). However, both Thr-84 and Lys-169 residues are still involved in the specific recognition of dicarboxylic acid keto (prephenate) and amino acid (Asp) substrates. Thus, the correction does not alter the overall conclusions of the study.
