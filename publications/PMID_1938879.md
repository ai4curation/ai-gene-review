# The Psu protein of bacteriophage P4 is an antitermination factor for rho-dependent transcription termination.
**Authors:** Linderoth NA, Calendar RL
**Journal:** J Bacteriol (1991)
**DOI:** [10.1128/jb.173.21.6722-6731.1991](https://doi.org/10.1128/jb.173.21.6722-6731.1991)
**PMC:** [PMC209021](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC209021/)

## Abstract

1. J Bacteriol. 1991 Nov;173(21):6722-31. doi: 10.1128/jb.173.21.6722-6731.1991.

The Psu protein of bacteriophage P4 is an antitermination factor for
rho-dependent transcription termination.

Linderoth NA(1), Calendar RL.

Author information:
(1)Department of Molecular and Cell Biology, University of California, Berkeley
94720.

A 0.7-kbp DNA fragment from bacteriophage P4 that contained the polarity
suppression (psu) gene was cloned in an expression plasmid. Induction of the
plasmid-borne psu gene resulted in the overproduction of a protein having the
biological properties of the P4-induced polarity suppressor. In vivo, Psu
protein acted in trans to suppress rho-dependent polarity in the late genes of
an infecting P2 phage, in plasmid operons, and in the host chromosome. Psu
action did not require the presence of other P2 or P4 phage genes. Psu caused
efficient readthrough (antitermination) by Escherichia coli RNA polymerase at
the rho-dependent terminators tR1 and TIS2, individually and in tandem, but did
not affect termination at rho-independent sites. Neither the conserved
antitermination sequence boxA nor any unique promoter or utilization sequence
was required for Psu activity.
