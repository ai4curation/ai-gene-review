# Two rat homologs of clathrin-associated adaptor proteins.
**Authors:** Pevsner J, Volknandt W, Wong BR, Scheller RH
**Journal:** Gene (1994)
**DOI:** [10.1016/0378-1119(94)90306-9](https://doi.org/10.1016/0378-1119(94)90306-9)

## Abstract

1. Gene. 1994 Sep 2;146(2):279-83. doi: 10.1016/0378-1119(94)90306-9.

Two rat homologs of clathrin-associated adaptor proteins.

Pevsner J(1), Volknandt W, Wong BR, Scheller RH.

Author information:
(1)Department of Molecular and Cellular Physiology, Howard Hughes Medical
Institute, Beckman Center, Stanford University Medical School, CA 94305.

A cDNA clone predicted to encode a 46,757-Da protein was isolated from a library
derived from the electric lobe of the ray Discopyge ommata. Two rat homologs,
p47A and p47B, were subsequently isolated. These three proteins share approx.
80% amino acid (aa) identity to each other and have 27-30% aa identity to rat
AP50 and mouse AP47, the medium-chain subunits of adaptor complexes associated
with clathrin-coated vesicles. These complexes are involved in receptor-mediated
pathways of intracellular transport. Rat p47A mRNA is expressed in all tissues
examined, including brain, heart, kidney, liver, lung, muscle and spinal cord.
Rat p47B mRNA is detected exclusively in brain and spinal cord, and may
participate in nervous system-specific functions such as biogenesis or recycling
of synaptic vesicles.
