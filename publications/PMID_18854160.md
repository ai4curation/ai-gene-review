# A tripartite complex containing MRCK modulates lamellar actomyosin retrograde flow.
**Authors:** Tan I, Yong J, Dong JM, Lim L, Leung T
**Journal:** Cell (2008)
**DOI:** [10.1016/j.cell.2008.09.018](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2008.09.018)

## Abstract

1. Cell. 2008 Oct 3;135(1):123-36. doi: 10.1016/j.cell.2008.09.018.

A tripartite complex containing MRCK modulates lamellar actomyosin retrograde
flow.

Tan I(1), Yong J, Dong JM, Lim L, Leung T.

Author information:
(1)GSK-IMCB Group, Institute of Molecular & Cell Biology, 61 Biopolis Drive,
Singapore 138673, Singapore.

Actomyosin retrograde flow underlies the contraction essential for cell
motility. Retrograde flow in both lamellipodia and lamella is required for
membrane protrusion and for force generation by coupling to cell adhesion. We
report that the Rac/Cdc42-binding kinase MRCK and myosin II-related MYO18A
linked by the adaptor protein LRAP35a form a functional tripartite complex,
which is responsible for the assembly of lamellar actomyosin bundles and of a
subnuclear actomyosin network. LRAP35a binds independently to MYO18A and MRCK.
This binding leads to MRCK activation and its phosphorylation of MYO18A,
independently of ROK and MLCK. The MRCK complex moves in concert with the
retrograde flow of actomyosin bundles, with MRCK being able to influence other
flow components such as MYO2A. The promotion of persistent protrusive activity
and inhibition of cell motility by the respective expression of wild-type and
dominant-negative mutant components of the MRCK complex show it to be crucial to
cell protrusion and migration.
