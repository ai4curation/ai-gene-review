Comment in
    Science. 1994 Mar 18;263(5153):1570-2. doi: 10.1126/science.8128243.

In wild-type Drosophila, the period protein (PER) is found in nuclei of the eyes
and brain, and PER immunoreactivity oscillates with a circadian rhythm. The
studies described here indicate that the nuclear localization of PER is blocked
by timeless (tim), a second chromosome mutation that, like per null mutations,
abolishes circadian rhythms. PER fusion proteins without a conserved domain
(PAS) and some flanking sequences are nuclear in tim mutants. This suggests that
a segment of PER inhibits nuclear localization in tim mutants. The tim gene may
have a role in establishing rhythms of PER abundance and nuclear localization in
wild-type flies.
