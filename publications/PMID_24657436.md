Amyloid fibrils play important roles in HIV-1 infection. We found peptides
derived from the HIV-1 gp120 co-receptor binding region, which are defined as
enhancing peptides (EPs), could form amyloid fibrils and remarkably enhance
HIV-1 infection. EPs bound to the virus and promoted the interaction between
HIV-1 and target cells. The antiviral efficacy of antiretroviral drugs (ARVs)
was substantially impaired in the presence of EPs. Epigallocatechin gallate
(EGCG) could both inhibit the formation of fibrils composed of EPs and
counteract the EP-mediated enhancement of HIV-1 infection. Our findings identify
viral derived amyloid fibrils that hold potential for biochemical applications.
