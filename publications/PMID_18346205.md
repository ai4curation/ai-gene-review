# FK506 binding protein 12 differentially accelerates fibril formation of wild type alpha-synuclein and its clinical mutants A30P or A53T.
**Authors:** Gerard M, Debyser Z, Desender L, Baert J, Brandt I, Baekelandt V, Engelborghs Y
**Journal:** J Neurochem (2008)
**DOI:** [10.1111/j.1471-4159.2008.05342.x](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1471-4159.2008.05342.x)

## Abstract

1. J Neurochem. 2008 Jul;106(1):121-33. doi: 10.1111/j.1471-4159.2008.05342.x.
Epub  2008 Jul 1.

FK506 binding protein 12 differentially accelerates fibril formation of wild
type alpha-synuclein and its clinical mutants A30P or A53T.

Gerard M(1), Debyser Z, Desender L, Baert J, Brandt I, Baekelandt V, Engelborghs
Y.

Author information:
(1)Laboratory of Biomolecular Dynamics, K. U. Leuven, Flanders, Belgium.

Aggregation of alpha-synuclein (alpha-SYN) plays a key role in Parkinson's
disease. We have previously shown that aggregation of alpha-SYN in vitro is
accelerated by addition of FK506 binding proteins (FKBP) and that this effect
can be counteracted by FK506, a specific inhibitor of these enzymes. In this
paper, we investigated in detail the effect of FKBP12 on early aggregation and
on fibril formation of wild-type, A53T and A30P alpha-SYN. FKBP12 has a much
smaller effect on the fibril formation of these two clinical mutants alpha-SYN.
Using an inactive enzyme, we were able to discriminate between catalytic and
non-catalytic effects that differentially influence the two processes. A model
explaining non-linear concentration dependencies is proposed.
