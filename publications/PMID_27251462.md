DNA demethylation is an essential cellular activity to regulate gene expression;
however, the mechanism that triggers DNA demethylation remains unknown.
Furthermore, DNA demethylation was recently demonstrated to be induced by
oxidative stress without a clear molecular mechanism. In this manuscript, we
demonstrated that 8-oxoguanine DNA glycosylase-1 (OGG1) is the essential protein
involved in oxidative stress-induced DNA demethylation. Oxidative stress induced
the formation of 8-oxoguanine (8-oxoG). We found that OGG1, the 8-oxoG binding
protein, promotes DNA demethylation by interacting and recruiting TET1 to the
8-oxoG lesion. Downregulation of OGG1 makes cells resistant to oxidative
stress-induced DNA demethylation, while over-expression of OGG1 renders cells
susceptible to DNA demethylation by oxidative stress. These data not only
illustrate the importance of base excision repair (BER) in DNA demethylation but
also reveal how the DNA demethylation signal is transferred to downstream DNA
demethylation enzymes.
