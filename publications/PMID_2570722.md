# Multiple functions of a Drosophila homeotic gene, zeste-white 3, during segmentation and neurogenesis.
**Authors:** Perrimon N, Smouse D
**Journal:** Dev Biol (1989)
**DOI:** [10.1016/0012-1606(89)90180-2](https://doi.org/10.1016/0012-1606(89)90180-2)

## Abstract

1. Dev Biol. 1989 Oct;135(2):287-305. doi: 10.1016/0012-1606(89)90180-2.

Multiple functions of a Drosophila homeotic gene, zeste-white 3, during
segmentation and neurogenesis.

Perrimon N(1), Smouse D.

Author information:
(1)Howard Hughes Medical Institute, Boston, Massachusetts.

Lack of both maternal and zygotic gene activity at the zeste-white 3 (zw3) locus
causes severe developmental transformations. Embryos derived from germ cells
that lack zw3+ gene activity die during embryogenesis and have a phenotype that
is similar to that of embryos mutant for the segment polarity gene naked (nkd).
In both nkd and germ line clone-derived zw3 embryos the pattern elements derived
from the anterior-most part of each segment, the denticle belts, are deleted.
Similar abnormal patterns of the zygotically expressed genes engrailed and
Ultrabithorax are detected in both mutants, suggesting that the two genes are
involved in the same developmental process. Additionally, the induction of
clones of zw3 mutant cells in imaginal discs causes homeotic transformations of
noninnervated hair cells into innervated sensory bristles. The multiple roles of
zw3 during development and its possible interactions with the zygotic gene nkd
are discussed.
