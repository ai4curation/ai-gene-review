# PIAS1 selectively inhibits interferon-inducible genes and is important in innate immunity.
**Authors:** Liu B, Mink S, Wong KA, Stein N, Getman C, Dempsey PW, Wu H, Shuai K

## Abstract

1. Nat Immunol. 2004 Sep;5(9):891-8. doi: 10.1038/ni1104. Epub 2004 Aug 15.

PIAS1 selectively inhibits interferon-inducible genes and is important in innate
immunity.

Comment in
    Nat Immunol. 2004 Sep;5(9):875-6. doi: 10.1038/ni0904-875.

Interferon (IFN) activates the signal transducer and activator of transcription
(STAT) pathway to regulate immune responses. The protein inhibitor of activated
STAT (PIAS) family has been suggested to negatively regulate STAT signaling. To
understand the physiological function of PIAS1, we generated Pias1(-/-) mice.
Using PIAS1-deficient cells, we show that PIAS1 selectively regulates a subset
of IFN-gamma- or IFN-beta-inducible genes by interfering with the recruitment of
STAT1 to the gene promoter. The antiviral activity of IFN-gamma or IFN-beta was
consistently enhanced by Pias1 disruption. Pias1(-/-) mice showed increased
protection against pathogenic infection. Our data indicate that PIAS1 is a
physiologically important negative regulator of STAT1 and suggest that PIAS1 is
critical for the IFN-gamma- or IFN-beta-mediated innate immune responses.
