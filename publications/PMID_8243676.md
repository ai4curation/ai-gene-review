# Botulinum neurotoxins serotypes A and E cleave SNAP-25 at distinct COOH-terminal peptide bonds.
**Authors:** Schiavo G, Santucci A, Dasgupta BR, Mehta PP, Jontes J, Benfenati F, Wilson MC, Montecucco C
**Journal:** FEBS Lett (1993)
**DOI:** [10.1016/0014-5793(93)80448-4](https://doi.org/10.1016/0014-5793(93)80448-4)

## Abstract

1. FEBS Lett. 1993 Nov 29;335(1):99-103. doi: 10.1016/0014-5793(93)80448-4.

Botulinum neurotoxins serotypes A and E cleave SNAP-25 at distinct COOH-terminal
peptide bonds.

Schiavo G(1), Santucci A, Dasgupta BR, Mehta PP, Jontes J, Benfenati F, Wilson
MC, Montecucco C.

Author information:
(1)Centro CNR Biomembrane, Università di Padova, Italy.

SNAP-25, a membrane-associated protein of the nerve terminal, is specifically
cleaved by botulinum neurotoxins serotypes A and E, which cause human and animal
botulism by blocking neurotransmitter release at the neuromuscular junction.
Here we show that these two metallo-endopeptidase toxins cleave SNAP-25 at two
distinct carboxyl-terminal sites. Serotype A catalyses the hydrolysis of the
Gln197-Arg198 peptide bond, while serotype E cleaves the Arg180-Ile181 peptide
lineage. These results indicate that the carboxyl-terminal region of SNAP-25
plays a crucial role in the multi-protein complex that mediates vesicle docking
and fusion at the nerve terminal.
