# Alginate production by an Azotobacter vinelandii mutant unable to produce alginate lyase.
**Authors:** Trujillo-Roldán MA, Moreno S, Segura D, Galindo E, Espín G
**Journal:** Appl Microbiol Biotechnol (2003)
**DOI:** [10.1007/s00253-002-1173-7](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00253-002-1173-7)

## Abstract

1. Appl Microbiol Biotechnol. 2003 Feb;60(6):733-7. doi:
10.1007/s00253-002-1173-7.  Epub 2002 Dec 18.

Alginate production by an Azotobacter vinelandii mutant unable to produce
alginate lyase.

Trujillo-Roldán MA(1), Moreno S, Segura D, Galindo E, Espín G.

Author information:
(1)Department of Bioengineering, Instituto de Biotecnología, Universidad
Nacional Autónoma de México, Apartado Postal 510-3, Cuernavaca 62250 Morelos,
Mexico.

Alginate is an industrially relevant linear copolymer composed of
beta-1,4-linked D-mannuronic acid and its C-5 epimer L-guluronic acid. The
rheological and gel-forming properties of alginates depend on the molecular
weight and the relative content of the two monomers. Alginate produced by
Azotobacter vinelandii was shown to be degraded towards the end of the culture,
an undesirable situation in terms of potential alginate applications. A gene (
algL) encoding the alginate lyase activity AlgL is present within the alginate
biosynthetic gene cluster of A. vinelandii. We constructed strain SML2, an A.
vinelandii strain carrying a non-polar mutation within algL. No alginate lyase
activity was detected in SML2. Under 3% dissolved oxygen tension, higher values
of maximum mean molecular weight alginate were obtained (1240 kDa) with strain
SML2, compared to those from the parental strain ATCC 9046 (680 kDa). These data
indicate that AlgL activity causes the drop in the molecular weight of alginate
produced by A. vinelandii.
