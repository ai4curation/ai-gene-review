# Autosomal mutations affecting adhesion between wing surfaces in Drosophila melanogaster.
**Authors:** Prout M, Damania Z, Soong J, Fristrom D, Fristrom JW
**Journal:** Genetics (1997)
**DOI:** [10.1093/genetics/146.1.275](https://doi.org/10.1093/genetics/146.1.275)

## Abstract

1. Genetics. 1997 May;146(1):275-85. doi: 10.1093/genetics/146.1.275.

Autosomal mutations affecting adhesion between wing surfaces in Drosophila
melanogaster.

Prout M(1), Damania Z, Soong J, Fristrom D, Fristrom JW.

Author information:
(1)Department of Molecular and Cell Biology, University of California, Berkeley

Integrins are evolutionarily conserved transmembrane alpha,beta heterodimeric
receptors involved in cell-to-matrix and cell-to-cell adhesions. In Drosophila
the position-specific (PS) integrins mediate the formation and maintenance of
junctions between muscle and epidermis and between the two epidermal wing
surfaces. Besides integrins, other proteins are implicated in integrin-dependent
adhesion. In Drosophila, somatic clones of mutations in PS integrin genes
disrupt adhesion between wing surfaces to produce wing blisters. To identify
other genes whose products function in adhesion between wing surfaces, we
conducted a screen for autosomal mutations that produce blisters in somatic wing
clones. We isolated 76 independent mutations in 25 complementation groups, 15 of
which contain more than one allele. Chromosomal sites were determined by
deficiency mapping, and genetic interactions with mutations in the beta PS
integrin gene myospheroid were investigated. Mutations in four known genes
(blistered, Delta, dumpy and mastermind) were isolated. Mutations were isolated
in three new genes (piopio, rhea and steamer duck) that affect myo-epidermal
junctions or muscle function in embryos. Mutations in three other genes (kakapo,
kiwi and moa) may also affect cell adhesion or muscle function at hatching.
These new mutants provide valuable material for the study of integrin-dependent
cell-to-cell adhesion.
