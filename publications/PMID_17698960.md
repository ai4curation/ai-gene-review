Yeast aconitase binds and provides metabolically coupled protection to
mitochondrial DNA.

Chen XJ(1), Wang X, Butow RA.

Author information:
(1)Department of Molecular Biology, University of Texas Southwestern Medical

chenx@upstate.edu

Aconitase (Aco1p) is a multifunctional protein: It is an enzyme of the
tricarboxylic acid cycle. In animal cells, Aco1p also is a cytosolic protein
binding to mRNAs to regulate iron metabolism. In yeast, Aco1p was identified as
a component of mtDNA nucleoids. Here we show that yeast Aco1p protects mtDNA
from excessive accumulation of point mutations and ssDNA breaks and suppresses
reductive recombination of mtDNA. Aconitase binds to both ds- and ssDNA, with a
preference for GC-containing sequences. Therefore, mitochondria are
opportunistic organelles that seize proteins, such as metabolic enzymes, for
construction of the nucleoid, an mtDNA maintenance/segregation apparatus.
