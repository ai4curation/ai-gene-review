# The nirB and nirD genes are essential for both assimilatory and dissimilatory reduction of nitrite in Pseudomonas putida Y-9 under aerobic conditions.

NADH-dependent nitrite reductase NirBD is known to mediate nitrite (NO2-)
reduction to ammonium (NH4+), but whether the encoding genes nirB and nirD
function independently or synergistically, and whether they influence other
nitrogen transformation processes, remain unclear. In this study, the individual
roles of nirB and nirD in nitrogen metabolism were systematically investigated
in Pseudomonas putida Y-9. Deletion of nirB or nirD severely impaired NO2-
reduction. After 4 d of cultivation in medium containing 50 mg/L nitrate (NO3-),
the mutants exhibited lower NO3- removal efficiencies (77% and 61%,
respectively) than the wild-type strain (87%), accompanied by substantial NO2-
accumulation (35.69 and 28.38 mg/L vs. undetectable in the wild-type) and
pronounced growth inhibition. Although deletion of one gene triggered strong
upregulation of the remaining gene (11.21- to 17.10-fold) and elevated
expression of nitrate reductase genes napA and narG, this transcriptional
response was insufficient to restore NO2- reduction. 15N isotope tracing
provided direct evidence that nirB and nirD mediate both assimilatory nitrate
reduction (ANRA) and dissimilatory nitrate reduction to ammonium (DNRA) in
strain Y-9 under aerobic conditions. In NH4+ medium, the mutants displayed
growth comparable to the wild-type strain but exhibited reduced total nitrogen
removal, accompanied by upregulation of glnA expression (3.39- and 2.00-fold).
Collectively, these findings reveal that nirB and nirD are essential for both
ANRA and DNRA pathways while influencing NH4+ assimilation in P. putida Y-9.
This study extends current understanding of aerobic DNRA and provides a
molecular and physiological basis for nitrogen management in soil and wastewater
treatment systems.
