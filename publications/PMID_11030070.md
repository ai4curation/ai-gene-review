# A family with X-linked dominant Charcot-Marie-Tooth caused by a connexin32 mutation.
**Authors:** Verhelst HE, Lofgren A, Van Coster RN
**Journal:** Eur J Paediatr Neurol (2000)
**DOI:** [10.1053/ejpn.2000.0311](https://doi.org/10.1053/ejpn.2000.0311)

## Abstract

1. Eur J Paediatr Neurol. 2000;4(5):235-8. doi: 10.1053/ejpn.2000.0311.

A family with X-linked dominant Charcot-Marie-Tooth caused by a connexin32
mutation.

Verhelst HE(1), Lofgren A, Van Coster RN.

Author information:
(1)Department of Pediatrics, Ghent University Hospital, Belgium.

A family with a hereditary peripheral neuropathy is presented. Pedigree analysis
suggested X-linked dominant mode of inheritance. The index patient became
symptomatic at the age of 12 years. Clinical examination at 14 years revealed
footdrop on the left, bilateral pes cavus, slight atrophy of thenar eminences,
decreased muscle strength in both legs and brisk deep tendon reflexes.
Electrophysiological studies were compatible with an axonal neuropathy. A novel
point mutation located in codon 126 of the connexin32 gene, substituting a
histidine for a tyrosine, was found in the index patient, in the mother, in two
sisters and in a brother. The mother and the eldest sister had pes cavus
bilaterally although they were asymptomatic. The younger brother and sister
showed no signs of the disease.
