BACKGROUND: The activation of stimulator of interferon genes (STING) for the
treatment of cancer is a long-standing therapeutic goal. However, the clinical
use of orthosteric STING agonists has shown limited utility in patients with
advanced/metastatic cancer. C92 is a potent representative of a first-in-class
chemical series of allosteric small-molecule human STING agonists that are
distinguished by their binding site which lies within STING's proton channel and
their consequently unique functional properties. This study characterizes the
pharmacology and the anticancer efficacy of C92.
METHODS: Allosteric binding was established using radioligand binding assays.
Type I interferon (IFN)-mediated immune signaling due to the activation of STING
by C92 was studied in human and murine primary cells. IFN independent actions of
C92 were assessed by monitoring inflammasome and autophagy markers in the human
monocytic THP-1 cell line. Anticancer activity of CRD3874-SI by the intravenous
route as a single agent or in combination with checkpoint inhibitors (CPIs) was
evaluated in human STING knock-in C57BL/6 mice. The comparative effects of
activating STING in either tumor fibroblasts or non-tumor host tissue were
studied in human STING-expressing murine cells that were implanted in wt C57BL/6
and BALB/c mice.
RESULTS: C92 is an allosteric agonist that activates STING in the absence of
cyclic guanosine monophosphate-adenosine monophosphate (cGAMP). In addition, C92
can also potentiate the binding of cGAMP to STING. The allosteric binding site
lies within a hydrophobic transmembrane proton channel formed by intertwined
helices of two STING monomers. C92 generates strong Type I IFN responses, but
without IFN-independent actions such as autophagy and inflammasome formation.
Intravenous, oral or intratumoral administration of C92 as a single agent led to
robust antitumor immune responses and synergized with CPI treatment providing a
path for the clinical translation of C92. The unique species selectivity profile
of C92 enabled a demonstration that anticancer effects can be mediated by STING
either in tumor cells or in non-tumor host cells.
CONCLUSION: The allosteric small-molecule human STING agonist C92 that blocks
STING's proton channel is significantly differentiated from orthosteric STING
agonists by its unique pharmacology making it a promising immune therapeutic for
the treatment of cancer.

ResultsC92 is an allosteric STING agonistRadioligand bindingThe binding of C92 to STING was investigated using a [3H]cGAMP radioligand displacement assay using lysates isolated from HEK293T cells stably transfected with the R232 variant of full-length human STING (R232.hSTING.HEK293T) or full-length murine STING (mSTING.HEK293T). As described earlier, the binding and functional activation of STING by C92 does not require cGAMP.31 Control lysates from untransfected wt HEK293T cells bound negligible amounts of [3H]cGAMP (figure 1A). The cGAMP competitive ligands ADU-S100 and diABZI-3 (referred to as diABZI in this manuscript)34 as well as unlabeled cGAMP were used as controls. These ligands displaced [3H]cGAMP from STING’s CDN binding site as expected. An early and unexpected result was that C92 enhanced rather than reduced the levels of [3H]cGAMP bound to STING. The effect was specific to human STING (hSTING) as there was no enhancement observed from the lysates of the mSTING.HEK293T cell line (figure 1B). The C92 induced potentiation of the binding of [3H]cGAMP to hSTING was further investigated in a saturation binding study of [3H]cGAMP in the presence of a fixed concentration of C92. From the equilibrium binding constants generated from cGAMP binding isotherms in the presence (Kd=1.3 ± 0.1 nM) and absence (Kd=8.6 ± 0.6 nM) of C92, it is estimated that C92 causes a 6.6-fold enhancement in the binding affinity of [3H]cGAMP to hSTING (figure 1C). The EC50 (concentration of a drug that produces 50% of its maximum effect) for C92 induced potentiation of [3H]cGAMP binding to hSTING was 36.1±1.2 nM (figure 1D). The inability of C92 to displace [3H]cGAMP from its binding site and the significant potentiation of [3H]cGAMP binding to STING in its presence, suggested that C92 bound to hSTING at an allosteric binding site that was distinct from the cGAMP binding pocket.Figure 1C92 is an allosteric hSTING agonist. Tumor cell-specific STING activation by C92 is sufficient to generate anticancer efficacy

The species selectivity31 of C92 was exploited to demonstrate that tumor cell STING activation was sufficient to generate anticancer activity. Tumors established in naïve C57BL/6 and BALB/c wild-type mice implanted with human STING-transfected murine cancer cell lines provided an opportunity to study the anticancer effects of C92 entirely driven by the activation of hSTING within tumor cells since mSTING from the host is not activated by the drug31 (figure 1B).

CT26 and B16F10 murine cell lines stably expressing the human R232 STING variant were created for this study. In vitro treatment of these cell lines (R232.hSTING.CT2635 and R232.hSTING.B16F10) with C92 led to the phosphorylation of murine pIRF3 and the production of murine CXCL10 transcripts, confirming earlier findings that activated hSTING could functionally couple with downstream murine STING pathway proteins leading to the secretion of murine proinflammatory cytokines (online supplemental figure S5A-C). In contrast, control untransfected parent wild-type CT26 and B16F10 cells were insensitive to C92 even at high concentrations (online supplemental figure S5).

Single-agent administration of C92 delivered by the IV route (10 mg/kg) caused 61% TGR in wt BALB/c mice carrying R232.hSTING.CT26 tumors (figure 5A; online supplemental figure S6 A-B). Elevated levels of IFNβ above baseline were detected in both tumor and plasma compartments 6 hours post dose (figure 5B).
