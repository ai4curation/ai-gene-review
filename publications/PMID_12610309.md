# Steroid control of longevity in Drosophila melanogaster.
**Authors:** Simon AF, Shih C, Mack A, Benzer S
**Journal:** Science (2003)
**DOI:** [10.1126/science.1080539](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1080539)

## Abstract

1. Science. 2003 Feb 28;299(5611):1407-10. doi: 10.1126/science.1080539.

Steroid control of longevity in Drosophila melanogaster.

Simon AF(1), Shih C, Mack A, Benzer S.

Author information:
(1)Division of Biology 156-29, California Institute of Technology, 1201
California Boulevard, Pasadena, CA 91125, USA.

Ecdysone, the major steroid hormone of Drosophila melanogaster, is known for its
role in development and reproduction. Flies that are heterozygous for mutations
of the ecdysone receptor exhibit increases in life-span and resistance to
various stresses, with no apparent deficit in fertility or activity. A mutant
involved in the biosynthesis of ecdysone displays similar effects, which are
suppressed by feeding ecdysone to the flies. These observations demonstrate the
importance of the ecdysone hormonal pathway, a new player in regulating
longevity.
