# Substrate-assisted catalysis by PARP10 limits its activity to mono-ADP-ribosylation.
**Authors:** Kleine H, Poreba E, Lesniewicz K, Hassa PO, Hottiger MO, Litchfield DW, Shilton BH, Lüscher B
**Journal:** Mol Cell (2008)
**DOI:** [10.1016/j.molcel.2008.08.009](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.molcel.2008.08.009)

## Abstract

1. Mol Cell. 2008 Oct 10;32(1):57-69. doi: 10.1016/j.molcel.2008.08.009.

Substrate-assisted catalysis by PARP10 limits its activity to
mono-ADP-ribosylation.

Kleine H(1), Poreba E, Lesniewicz K, Hassa PO, Hottiger MO, Litchfield DW,
Shilton BH, Lüscher B.

Author information:
(1)Institut für Biochemie und Molekularbiologie, Klinikum, RWTH Aachen

ADP-ribosylation controls many processes, including transcription, DNA repair,
and bacterial toxicity. ADP-ribosyltransferases and poly-ADP-ribose polymerases
(PARPs) catalyze mono- and poly-ADP-ribosylation, respectively, and depend on a
highly conserved glutamate residue in the active center for catalysis. However,
there is an apparent absence of this glutamate for the recently described
PARP6-PARP16, raising questions about how these enzymes function. We find that
PARP10, in contrast to PARP1, lacks the catalytic glutamate and has transferase
rather than polymerase activity. Despite this fundamental difference, PARP10
also modifies acidic residues. Consequently, we propose an alternative catalytic
mechanism for PARP10 compared to PARP1 in which the acidic target residue of the
substrate functionally substitutes for the catalytic glutamate by using
substrate-assisted catalysis to transfer ADP-ribose. This mechanism explains why
the novel PARPs are unable to function as polymerases. This discovery will help
to illuminate the different biological functions of mono- versus
poly-ADP-ribosylation in cells.
