# Recombinant expression and isolation of human L-arginine:glycine amidinotransferase and identification of its active-site cysteine residue.
**Authors:** Humm A, Fritsche E, Mann K, Göhl M, Huber R
**Journal:** Biochem J (1997)
**DOI:** [10.1042/bj3220771](https://doi.org/10.1042/bj3220771)
**PMC:** [PMC1218254](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC1218254/)

## Abstract

1. Biochem J. 1997 Mar 15;322 ( Pt 3)(Pt 3):771-6. doi: 10.1042/bj3220771.

Recombinant expression and isolation of human L-arginine:glycine
amidinotransferase and identification of its active-site cysteine residue.

Humm A(1), Fritsche E, Mann K, Göhl M, Huber R.

Author information:
(1)Max-Planck-Institut für Biochemie, Am Klopferspitz 18a, 82152 Martinsried,
Germany.

Creatine and its phosphorylated form play a central role in the energy
metabolism of muscle and nerve tissues. l-Arginine:glycine amidinotransferase
(AT) catalyses the committed step in the formation of creatine. The
mitochondrial and cytosolic forms of the enzyme are believed to derive from the
same gene by alternative splicing. We have expressed recombinant human AT in
Escherichia coli with two different N-termini, resembling the longest two forms
of the enzyme that we had isolated recently from porcine kidney mitochondria as
a mixture. The enzymes were expressed with N-terminal histidine tags followed by
factor Xa-cleavage sites. We established a new method for the removal of
N-terminal fusion peptides by means of an immobilized snake venom prothrombin
activator. We identified cysteine-407 as the active-site residue of AT by
radioactive labelling and isolation of labelled peptides, and by site-directed
mutagenesis of the protein.
