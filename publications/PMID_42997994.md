# Metadherin Impairs PPARα-Mediated Fatty Acid Oxidation to Aggravate Renal Lipotoxicity in Diabetic Kidney Disease.

Impaired fatty acid oxidation (FAO) and subsequent lipotoxicity in renal tubules
play a key role in diabetic kidney disease (DKD) progression; however, the
underlying mechanisms remain unclear. Metadherin (MTDH) is linked to DKD;
however, its potential role in renal lipotoxicity remains poorly characterized.
A DKD murine model was established via unilateral nephrectomy, a high-fat diet,
and streptozotocin (STZ) injection. Palmitic acid (PA)-stimulated renal tubular
epithelial cells (RTECs) were also used. Renal MTDH in mice was knocked down
using an adeno-associated virus serotype 9 (AAV9) vector, and MTDH was silenced
or overexpressed in vitro. In mice, MTDH knockdown improved renal function,
enhanced FAO via peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor alpha (PPARα), and
reduced lipotoxicity. In vitro, MTDH silencing mitigated lipid accumulation and
activated FAO, whereas overexpression aggravated the injury, an effect that was
reversed by a PPARα agonist. In conclusion, MTDH upregulation in the diabetic
kidney disrupts lipid homeostasis by repressing PPARα-mediated FAO, thereby
promoting lipotoxic injury. These findings offer new insights into DKD metabolic
dysregulation.
