# A role of SMAD4 in iron metabolism through the positive regulation of hepcidin expression.
**Authors:** Wang RH, Li C, Xu X, Zheng Y, Xiao C, Zerfas P, Cooperman S, Eckhaus M, Rouault T, Mishra L, Deng CX
**Journal:** Cell Metab (2005)
**DOI:** [10.1016/j.cmet.2005.10.010](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cmet.2005.10.010)

## Abstract

1. Cell Metab. 2005 Dec;2(6):399-409. doi: 10.1016/j.cmet.2005.10.010.

A role of SMAD4 in iron metabolism through the positive regulation of hepcidin
expression.

Wang RH(1), Li C, Xu X, Zheng Y, Xiao C, Zerfas P, Cooperman S, Eckhaus M,
Rouault T, Mishra L, Deng CX.

Author information:
(1)Genetics of Development and Disease Branch, 10/9N105, National Institute of
Diabetes and Digestive and Kidney Diseases, National Institutes of Health,
Bethesda, Maryland 20892, USA.

Hereditary hemochromatosis, characterized by iron overload in multiple organs,
is one of the most common genetic disorders among Caucasians. Hepcidin, which is
synthesized in the liver, plays important roles in iron overload syndromes.
Here, we show that a Cre-loxP-mediated liver-specific disruption of SMAD4
results in markedly decreased hepcidin expression and accumulation of iron in
many organs, which is most pronounced in liver, kidney, and pancreas. Transcript
levels of genes involved in intestinal iron absorption, including Dcytb, DMT1,
and ferroportin, are significantly elevated in the absence of hepcidin. We
demonstrate that ectopic overexpression of SMAD4 activates the hepcidin promoter
and is associated with epigenetic modification of histone H3 to a
transcriptionally active form. Moreover, transcriptional activation of hepcidin
is abrogated in SMAD4-deficient hepatocytes in response to iron overload,
TGF-beta, BMP, or IL-6. Our study uncovers a novel role of TGF-beta/SMAD4 in
regulating hepcidin expression and thus intestinal iron transport and iron
homeostasis.
