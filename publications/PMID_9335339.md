# The AP-3 adaptor complex is essential for cargo-selective transport to the yeast vacuole.
**Authors:** Cowles CR, Odorizzi G, Payne GS, Emr SD
**Journal:** Cell (1997)
**DOI:** [10.1016/s0092-8674(01)80013-1](https://doi.org/10.1016/s0092-8674(01)80013-1)

## Abstract

1. Cell. 1997 Oct 3;91(1):109-18. doi: 10.1016/s0092-8674(01)80013-1.

The AP-3 adaptor complex is essential for cargo-selective transport to the yeast
vacuole.

Cowles CR(1), Odorizzi G, Payne GS, Emr SD.

Author information:
(1)Division of Cellular and Molecular Medicine and Howard Hughes Medical
Institute, University of California, San Diego 92093-0668, USA.

Three distinct adaptor protein (AP) complexes involved in protein trafficking
have been identified. AP-1 and AP-2 mediate protein sorting at the trans-Golgi
network and plasma membrane, respectively, whereas the function of AP-3 has not
been defined. A screen for factors specifically involved in transport of
alkaline phosphatase (ALP) from the Golgi to the vacuole/lysosome has identified
Ap16p and Ap15p of the yeast AP-3 complex. Deletion of each of the four AP-3
subunits results in selective mislocalization of ALP and the vacuolar t-SNARE,
Vam3p (but not CPS and CPY), while deletion of AP-1 and AP-2 subunits has no
effect on vacuolar protein delivery. This study, therefore, provides evidence
that the AP-3 complex functions in cargo-selective protein transport from the
Golgi to the vacuole/lysosome.
