# Xenopus Cep57 is a novel kinetochore component involved in microtubule attachment.
**Authors:** Emanuele MJ, Stukenberg PT
**Journal:** Cell (2007)
**DOI:** [10.1016/j.cell.2007.07.023](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2007.07.023)

## Abstract

1. Cell. 2007 Sep 7;130(5):893-905. doi: 10.1016/j.cell.2007.07.023.

Xenopus Cep57 is a novel kinetochore component involved in microtubule
attachment.

Emanuele MJ(1), Stukenberg PT.

Author information:
(1)Department of Biochemistry and Molecular Genetics, University of Virginia,
Charlottesville, VA 22908, USA.

For chromosomes to congress and segregate during cell division, kinetochores
must form stable attachments with spindle microtubules. We find that the
centrosome protein, xCep57, localizes to kinetochores and interacts with the
kinetochore proteins Zwint, Mis12, and CLIP-170. Immunodepletion of xCep57 from
egg extracts yields weakened and elongated bipolar spindles which fail to align
chromosomes. In the absence of xCep57, tension is lost between sister
kinetochores, and spindle microtubules are no longer resistant to low doses of
nocodazole. xCep57 inhibition on isolated mitotic chromosomes inhibits
kinetochore-microtubule binding in vitro. xCep57 also interacts with
gamma-tubulin. In xCep57 immunodepleted extracts, sperm centrosomes nucleate
with normal kinetics, but are unable maintain microtubule anchorage. This
characterization places xCep57 in a novel class of proteins required for stable
microtubule attachments at the kinetochore and at the centrosome and suggests
that the mechanism of microtubule binding at these two places is mechanistically
similar.
