Abstract

Transforming growth factor (TGF-β1) is a critical profibrotic mediator in chronic lung disease, and there are no specific strategies to mitigate its adverse effects. Activation of TGF-β1 signaling is a multipart process involving ligands, transmembrane receptors, and transcription factors. In addition, an intricate network of adaptor proteins fine-tunes the signaling strength, duration, and activity. Namely, Smad7 recruits growth arrest and DNA damage (GADD34) protein that then interacts with the catalytic subunit of phosphoprotein phosphatase 1 (PP1c) to inactivate TGF-β receptor (TβR)-I and downregulate TGF-β1 signaling. Little is known about how TGF-β1 releases TβR-I from the GADD34-PP1c inhibition to activate its signaling. Transmembrane lemur tyrosine kinase 2 (LMTK2) is a PP1c inhibitor, and our published data showed that TGF-β1 recruits LMTK2 to the cell surface. Here, we tested the hypothesis that TGF-β1 recruits LMTK2 to inhibit PP1c, allowing activation of TβR-I. First, LMTK2 interacted with the TGF-β1 pathway in the human bronchial epithelium at multiple checkpoints. Second, TGF-β1 inhibited PP1c by an LMTK2-dependent mechanism. Third, TGF-β1 used LMTK2 to activate canonical Smad3-mediated signaling. We propose a model whereby the LMTK2-PP1c and Smad7-GADD34-PP1c complexes serve as on-and-off switches in the TGF-β1 signaling in human bronchial epithelium.

INTRODUCTION

Transforming growth factor (TGF-β) signaling plays a vital role in lung health and disease (reviewed in Ref. 1 ). It is essential during lung development and repair after acute injury ( 2 – 4 ). TGF-β1 signaling plays a critical role in the pathogenesis of asthma and lung fibrosis in chronic obstructive pulmonary disease and aggravates lung disease in cystic fibrosis (CF) ( 5 – 13 ). Diverse mechanisms include suppression of epithelial cell growth and differentiation, fibroblast activation, extracellular matrix (ECM) organization, and T cell homeostasis.

The disease-modifying activity of TGF-β1 is fascinating, a role extensively studied in CF, a disease caused by mutations in the cystic fibrosis transmembrane conductance regulator ( CFTR ) gene ( 14 , 15 ). In people with CF (PwCF), including siblings carrying the most common disease-associated F508del-CFTR variant, non-CFTR genes are responsible for more than 50% of the variation in lung function ( 6 , 16 – 20 ). The TGF-β1 gene polymorphisms associated with increased TGF-β1 levels significantly accelerate lung disease progression in PwCF ( 5 , 6 , 21 , 22 ). It has been shown that TGF-β1 decreases CFTR mRNA and protein levels, impairing functional rescue of F508del-CFTR by CFTR modulators ( 23 – 28 ). There are no strategies to mitigate the adverse effects of TGF-β1 because the signaling circuitry is poorly understood in a disease-specific context. Precisely, the activation-inactivation balance of the signaling has not been elucidated.

TGF-β1 signaling is mediated by tissue- and cell-dependent mechanisms. Members of the TGF-β superfamily function as ligands with heteromeric complexes of specific transmembrane types I and II Ser/Thr kinase receptors. Activation of the canonical signaling pathway involves TGF-β1 binding to the constitutively active TGF-β receptor (TβR)-II, which phosphorylates and activates TβR-I, followed by activation of the receptor (R)-Smads, Smad2, and Smad3, and finally signal transduction ( 29 – 31 ). Phosphoprotein phosphatase-1 (PP1) was shown to dephosphorylate TβR-I, protecting it from constitutive activation by TβR-II in co-transfected mink lung (Mv1Lu) cells ( 32 – 34 ). Upon TGF-β1 stimulation of Mv1Lu cells, Smad7 interacts with growth arrest and DNA damage (GADD34) protein, which recruits the catalytic subunit of PP1 (PP1c) to dephosphorylate TβR-I. Indeed, the Smad7/GADD34/PP1c complex significantly inhibits TGF-β1-mediated cellular responses, suggesting that it functions to switch off the TGF-β1 signaling pathway ( 34 ). It can be predicted that activation of TβR-I requires dissociation of the Smad7/GADD34/PP1c complex. However, the protein-protein interactions leading to the Smad7/GADD34/PP1c dissociation are mainly unknown.

Various PP1 holoenzymes are thought to catalyze ∼30% of phospho(p)Ser/pThr dephosphorylation reactions in eukaryotes, and more than 200 validated, structurally unrelated PP1 interacting proteins create an extensive number of PP1 holoenzymes with substrate specificity, diverse mechanisms of action, and precisely regulated enzymatic activity ( 35 – 39 ). The PP1 interacting proteins are frequently referred to as PIPs. Due to potential confusion with phosphoinositidies, also abbreviated as PIPs, in this manuscript, the PP1 interacting proteins are referred to as regulatory interactors of protein phosphatase one (RIPPOs), as proposed by Wu et al. ( 39 ). The binding of RIPPOs is nucleated primarily by a 4–8 amino acid long docking motif that interact with the hydrophobic grove in PP1c. Up to 30 other, nonoverlapping docking sites for RIPPOs are estimated on PP1c ( 38 ). The Arg-Val-x-Phe (RVxF) primary docking motif is present in ∼70% of RIPPOs. Its role includes anchoring PP1c to allow secondary interactions that determine the activity and substrate specificity of the holoenzyme ( 38 , 40 ). The motif is degenerate and its consensus sequence has been determined as [Arg/Lys]-x(0-1)-[Vali/Iso]-[P]-[Phe/Trp], where x denotes any residue and [P] any residue except Pro ( 41 , 42 ). The RVxF sequences differ in their affinity for PP1c. Some RIPPOs catalyze inhibitory phosphorylation of the PP1c at Thr 320 (pPP1c-T 320 ) ( 43 ). The transmembrane lemur tyrosine kinase 2 (LMTK2) serves as RIPPO but, despite its name, is in fact, a Ser/Thr kinase ( 40 , 44 , 45 ). Indeed, LMTK2 binds to PP1c via a canonical RVxF motif, Lys-Ala-Val-Thr-Phe (KAVTF), and phosphorylates PP1c-T 320 , inhibiting catalytic activity in co-transfected HeLa cells ( 44 – 46 ). We have previously shown that LMTK2 is recruited to the plasma membrane by TGF-β1 in human bronchial epithelial cell models, including immortalized CFBE41o− cells and primary differentiated cells from CF and non-CF individuals, suggesting a role of LMTK2 in TGF-β1 signaling ( 47 ). We have also demonstrated a novel interplay between LMTK2 and TGF-β1 in the molecular mechanisms of cancer in human bronchial epithelial cells ( 48 ).

This study examined whether LMTK2 switches on the TGF-β1 signaling by acting on the PP1c regulatory network. Using complementary approaches in human bronchial epithelial cells CFBE41o−, we demonstrate that LMTK2 is involved in TGF-β1 signaling at multiple checkpoints. One of the checkpoints is the activation of the canonical TGF-β1 transcription factor Smad3 by inhibiting PP1c.

DISCUSSION

The work presented here unveils LMTK2 as an essential regulator of the TGF-β1 signaling pathway in human bronchial epithelial cells. Specifically, LMTK2 phosphorylates PP1c-T 320 , reducing phosphatase activity to activate the canonical, Smad3-mediated TGF-β1 signaling pathway. Our conclusions were supported by RNAseq analysis revealing that LMTK2 plays a role in the TGF-β1 signaling at multiple checkpoints in human bronchial epithelial cells ( Tables 1 , 2 , 3 and 4 , and Fig. 1 ). We showed that TGF-β1 stimulated the inhibitory phosphorylation of PP1c-T 320 in an LMTK2-dependent manner ( Figs. 2 and 3 ). LMTK2 kinase activity was necessary for TGF-β1-mediated phosphorylation of PP1c-T 320 and so was the phosphorylation of LMTK2 at S 1450 but with much lower strength than the kinase ( Fig. 4 ). LMTK2 KD or RVxF domain-containing peptide PDP3 specifically attenuated activation of Smad3 ( Figs. 5 , 6 , and 7 ). Taken together, the results support a model of a regulatory circuitry in TGF-β1 signaling, orchestrated by LMTK2 and GADD34, and centered on PP1c to switch the signaling on and off, respectively ( Fig. 8 ). As shown in Fig. 8, A and B , Smad7-GADD34 recruits PP1c to inactivate TβR-I and downregulate TGF-β1 signaling ( 34 , 45 , 73 ). Thus, the PP1c-GADD34-Smad7 complex is an “off-switch” in TGF-β1 signaling. To turn off the inhibition, TGF-β1 recruits LMTK2 to the cell surface ( 47 ), where it induces inhibitory phosphorylation of PP1c-T 320 , thus allowing activation of TβR-I ( Fig. 8, C and D ). Phosphorylated PP1c-T 320 increases activation of Smad3-mediated TGF-β1 signaling ( Fig. 8 E ). Thus, the PP1c-LMTK2 complex is an “on-switch” in the Smad3-mediated TGF-β1 signaling. By contrast, the RVxF motif - containing PDP3 decreases pPP1c-T 320 levels and attenuates Smad3 activation and translocation to the nucleus ( Figs. 8 F ). These data provide the mechanistic basis for activation of Smad3 signaling mediated by the LMTK2-PP1c complex in CFBE41o− cells. TGF-β1 recruits LMTK2 to the apical membrane domain in primary differentiated human bronchial epithelial cells from CF and non-CF individuals with kinetics similar to CFBE41o− cells ( 47 ). These data suggest that activation of Smad3 by the LMTK2-PP1c complex is not restricted to CFBE41o− cells and the CF disease state. The repertoire of human airway epithelium has been recently transformed by single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq). The revised airway epithelial cell landscape contains newly identified cell types and lineages at the different anatomical sites and is modifiable by disease states ( 74 – 76 ). Determining which type of human airway epithelial cells use the LMTK2-mediated activation of Smad3 and the role of the LMTK2-PP1c circuitry in airway disease states await future studies.

Our work bridges the knowledge gap in understanding the TGF-β1 signaling pathways in the airway epithelium. Smad3 plays a pivotal role in extracellular matrix metabolism associated with pulmonary fibrosis ( 77 ). Although the basal level of Smad3 activity is essential for alveolar integrity, excessive signaling via the Smad3 pathway leads to fibrosis through decreased ECM degradation and enhanced EMC deposition. Compelling evidence shows that Smad2 and Smad3 play distinct roles in TGF-β1 ( 78 ). Our data propose a novel mechanism for selective activation of Smad3 versus Smad2 mediated by LMTK2. Strategies for targeting the TGF-β network have been suggested, such as broad TGF-β pathway inhibition at the receptor or ligand level, targeted blockade of downstream signaling components, or focusing on modifiers and downstream products ( 79 ). However, these approaches are limited by off-target globally affecting the TGF-β1 signaling. LMTK2, activating specifically the Smad3 pathway, emerges as a potential target and biomarker of lung disease progression in PwCF.

A study in mink lung epithelial cells showed the Smad7-GADD34-PP1c complex inactivates TGF-β1 signaling ( 34 ). Our RNAseq data suggest a similar mechanism in human bronchial epithelia whereby prolonged TGF-β1 treatment (24 h) upregulated the genes encoding Smad7 and GADD34 ( Table 1 ). However, it has not been determined how TGF-β1 inactivates the Smad7/GADD34/complex to induce its downstream signaling. It was previously shown that LMTK2 binds to PP1c and inhibits its phosphatase activity and that TGF-β1 recruits LMTK2 to the plasma membrane in Rab11-positive recycling vesicles ( 44 , 45 , 47 ). We provide evidence that the LMTK2-PP1c interactome positively regulates TGF-β1 signaling and explicitly activates the Smad3 pathway in human bronchial epithelia ( Figs. 2 , 3 , 4 , 5 , and 6 ).

The PP1c substrate specificity is controlled by the RIPPO binding to the PP1c docking motifs, including RVxF. We have shown that, similar to the LMTK2 KD, the RVxF mimetic PDP3 attenuated the TGF-β1 mediated inhibition of PP1c and Smad3 activation. We propose that PDP3 displaces PP1c from binding to LMTK2 and, thus, prevents the LMTK2-mediated inhibitory phosphorylation of PP1c. These effects demonstrate that the RVxF motif is, at least in part, responsible for the specificity of PP1c for the Smad3-mediated canonical pathway activation in our model of human bronchial epithelial cells. The Smad2 activation was unaffected under the aforementioned experimental conditions, indicating that a different set of PP1c regulatory subunits and/or docking motifs may control the Smad2 activity. Manser et al. ( 46 ) have shown that LMTK2 signaling regulates kinesin light chain-2 phosphorylation and binding of Smad2 as well as Smad2 nuclear accumulation after TGF-β1 treatment in cervical cancer (HeLa) cells. The cell-specific characteristics of TGF-β1 signaling, availability of RIPPOs, and the PP1c substrate specificity could be responsible for the difference in the Smad2 activation in the HeLa and CFBE41o− cells. The effects of LMTK2 on Smad3 signaling were not examined by Manser et al.

TGF-β1 activates numerous intracellular signaling cascades to achieve distinct responses simultaneously in various cells and conditions. There is growing evidence that the canonical TGF-β1 signaling reflects differential and cell-type specific roles of Smad2 and Smad3, including differential activation mechanisms and functions ( 78 ). Little is known about how Smad2 and Smad3 activation diversity is achieved. Our data suggest that the LMTK2-PP1c interaction specifically facilitates Smad3 signaling. Although our work provides a model of the Smad3 activation in bronchial epithelial cells, previous studies demonstrated that Smad3 signaling plays a critical role in airway inflammation and fibrosis ( 80 – 82 ). We previously showed that TGF-β1 and LMTK2 co-mediate molecular mechanisms, cellular functions, and disease pathways in human bronchial epithelial cells ( 53 ). Here, we show that LMTK2 mediates TGF-β1 activation of the c-JUN N-terminal kinase (JNK) and mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK) signaling pathways, including p38 MAPK ( Tables 3 and 4 ). The downstream effects of JNK and p38 include Smad3 activation ( 83 , 84 ). These data link the LMTK2-PP1c interactome with the downstream of Smad3 signaling. LMTK2 has been associated with several disorders, including CF, neurodegeneration, and cancer ( 48 ). LMTK2 has been described to co-immunoprecipitate with CFTR in different models of human airway epithelial cells, including primary bronchial epithelial cells, where it phosphorylated CFTR at Ser 737 , facilitating its endocytosis and decreasing its plasma membrane levels and function ( 50 ). TGF-β1 inhibits CFTR biogenesis and function in primary bronchial epithelial cells from controls and disease states, including chronic obstructive pulmonary disease and CF ( 24 , 26 ). Thus, in light of the current results, this kinase appears as a relevant link between CFTR, CF, and TGF-β1 signaling pathways. Future studies may examine the cell-type-specific effects of LMTK2 in TGF-β1 signaling and the cross talk between other signaling pathways.

In summary, TGF-β1 activation of Smad3 is mediated through phosphorylation of PP1c-T 320 by LMTK2. Understanding the LMTK2 protein network may help to elucidate the role of TGF-β1 signaling in CF and other forms of airway disease and validate it as a potential biomarker or future therapeutic target to prevent the harmful effects of TGF-β1 signaling.
