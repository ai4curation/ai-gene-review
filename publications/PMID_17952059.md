# Spatial regulation of Fus3 MAP kinase activity through a reaction-diffusion mechanism in yeast pheromone signalling.
**Authors:** Maeder CI, Hink MA, Kinkhabwala A, Mayr R, Bastiaens PI, Knop M
**Journal:** Nat Cell Biol (2007)
**DOI:** [10.1038/ncb1652](https://doi.org/10.1038/ncb1652)

## Abstract

1. Nat Cell Biol. 2007 Nov;9(11):1319-26. doi: 10.1038/ncb1652. Epub 2007 Oct 21.

Spatial regulation of Fus3 MAP kinase activity through a reaction-diffusion
mechanism in yeast pheromone signalling.

Maeder CI(1), Hink MA, Kinkhabwala A, Mayr R, Bastiaens PI, Knop M.

Author information:
(1)Cell Biology and Biophysics Unit, EMBL-Heidelberg, Meyerhofstrasse 1, D-69117
Heidelberg, Germany.

Signal transduction through mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK) cascades is
thought to occur through the assembly of macromolecular complexes. We quantified
the abundance of complexes in the cytoplasm among the MAPKs Ste11, Ste7, Fus3
and the scaffold protein Ste5 in yeast pheromone signalling using fluorescence
cross-correlation spectroscopy (FCCS). Significant complex concentrations were
observed that remained unchanged on pheromone stimulation, demonstrating that
global changes in complex abundances do not contribute to the transmission of
signal through the cytoplasm. On the other hand, investigation of the
distribution of active Fus3 (Fus3(PP)) across the cytoplasm using fluorescence
lifetime imaging microscopy (FLIM) revealed a gradient of Fus3(PP) activity
emanating from the tip of the mating projection. Spatial partitioning of Fus3
activating kinases to this site and deactivating phosphatases in the cytoplasm
maintain this Fus3(PP)-activity distribution. Propagation of signalling from the
shmoo is, therefore, spatially constrained by a gradient-generating
reaction-diffusion mechanism.
