# A gene mapping to the sex-determining region of the mouse Y chromosome is a member of a novel family of embryonically expressed genes.
**Authors:** Gubbay J, Collignon J, Koopman P, Capel B, Economou A, Münsterberg A, Vivian N, Goodfellow P, Lovell-Badge R
**Journal:** Nature (1990)
**DOI:** [10.1038/346245a0](https://doi.org/10.1038/346245a0)

## Abstract

1. Nature. 1990 Jul 19;346(6281):245-50. doi: 10.1038/346245a0.

A gene mapping to the sex-determining region of the mouse Y chromosome is a
member of a novel family of embryonically expressed genes.

Gubbay J(1), Collignon J, Koopman P, Capel B, Economou A, Münsterberg A, Vivian
N, Goodfellow P, Lovell-Badge R.

Author information:
(1)Laboratory of Eukaryotic Molecular Genetics, MRC National Institute for
Medical Research, Mill Hill, London, UK.

Comment in
    Nature. 1990 Jul 19;346(6281):216-7. doi: 10.1038/346216a0.

A gene mapping to the sex-determining region of the mouse Y chromosome is
deleted in a line of XY female mice mutant for Tdy, and is expressed at a stage
during male gonadal development consistent with its having a role in testis
determination. This gene is a member of a new family of at least five mouse
genes, related by an amino-acid motif showing homology to other known or
putative DNA-binding domains.
