The wild-type Caenorhabditis elegans nematode ages rapidly, undergoing
development, senescence, and death in less than 3 weeks. In contrast, mutants
with reduced activity of the gene daf-2, a homolog of the insulin and
insulin-like growth factor receptors, age more slowly than normal and live more
than twice as long. These mutants are active and fully fertile and have normal
metabolic rates. The life-span extension caused by daf-2 mutations requires the
activity of the gene daf-16. daf-16 appears to play a unique role in life-span
regulation and encodes a member of the hepatocyte nuclear factor 3
(HNF-3)/forkhead family of transcriptional regulators. In humans, insulin
down-regulates the expression of certain genes by antagonizing the activity of
HNF-3, raising the possibility that aspects of this regulatory system have been
conserved.
