# Trans-synaptic interaction of GluRdelta2 and Neurexin through Cbln1 mediates synapse formation in the cerebellum.
**Authors:** Uemura T, Lee SJ, Yasumura M, Takeuchi T, Yoshida T, Ra M, Taguchi R, Sakimura K, Mishina M
**Journal:** Cell (2010)
**DOI:** [10.1016/j.cell.2010.04.035](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2010.04.035)

## Abstract

1. Cell. 2010 Jun 11;141(6):1068-79. doi: 10.1016/j.cell.2010.04.035. Epub 2010
May  27.

Trans-synaptic interaction of GluRdelta2 and Neurexin through Cbln1 mediates
synapse formation in the cerebellum.

Uemura T(1), Lee SJ, Yasumura M, Takeuchi T, Yoshida T, Ra M, Taguchi R,
Sakimura K, Mishina M.

Author information:
(1)Department of Molecular Neurobiology and Pharmacology, Graduate School of
Medicine, University of Tokyo, Tokyo 113-0033, Japan.

Elucidation of molecular mechanisms that regulate synapse formation is required
for the understanding of neural wiring, higher brain functions, and mental
disorders. Despite the wealth of in vitro information, fundamental questions
about how glutamatergic synapses are formed in the mammalian brain remain
unanswered. Glutamate receptor (GluR) delta2 is essential for cerebellar synapse
formation in vivo. Here, we show that the N-terminal domain (NTD) of GluRdelta2
interacts with presynaptic neurexins (NRXNs) through cerebellin 1 precursor
protein (Cbln1). The synaptogenic activity of GluRdelta2 is abolished in
cerebellar primary cultures from Cbln1 knockout mice and is restored by
recombinant Cbln1. Knockdown of NRXNs in cerebellar granule cells also hinders
the synaptogenic activity of GluRdelta2. Both the NTD of GluRdelta2 and the
extracellular domain of NRXN1beta suppressed the synaptogenic activity of Cbln1
in cerebellar primary cultures and in vivo. These results suggest that
GluRdelta2 mediates cerebellar synapse formation by interacting with presynaptic
NRXNs through Cbln1.
