# Evidence for a Two-Step Model for Activation of GPR25 by the Chemoattractant CXCL17.
**Authors:** Yang W, Giblin SP, Pease JE
**Journal:** Basic Clin Pharmacol Toxicol (2026)
**DOI:** [10.1111/bcpt.70255](https://doi.org/10.1111/bcpt.70255)

## Abstract

1. Basic Clin Pharmacol Toxicol. 2026 Jul;139(1):e70255. doi: 10.1111/bcpt.70255.

Evidence for a Two-Step Model for Activation of GPR25 by the Chemoattractant
CXCL17.

Yang W(1), Giblin SP(1), Pease JE(1).

Author information:
(1)National Heart and Lung Institute, Imperial College London, London, UK.

CXCL17 was recently reported to activate GPR25, a receptor expressed by
T-regulatory cells. Although classified as a chemokine, the activity of CXCL17
is ablated by minor C-terminal truncation, suggesting a novel mode of receptor
activation. We set out to test this hypothesis by mutagenesis. GPR25 was
expressed in the murine pre-B cell line L1.2 and mediated robust migration of
transfectants to nanomolar concentrations of recombinant CXCL17 (24-119). The
N-terminally truncated form of CXCL17 (64-119) was also chemotactic for GPR25
transfectants, albeit with severely reduced potency. Modelling of CXCL17:GPR25
implied multiple interactions between the N- and C-termini of CXCL17 with GPR25,
which was validated by mutagenesis. Cells expressing a chimeric FPR1:GPR25
construct responded chemotactically to CXCL17 but with significantly reduced
potency compared with wild-type GPR25 transfectants, implicating the GPR25
N-terminus in CXCL17 recognition. Mutagensis of the GPR25 residues W95, R178 and
R264 resulted in a complete loss of chemotactic responsiveness to CXCL17,
consistent with the residues interacting with the C-terminal CXCL17 motif. In
conclusion, we verify GPR25 as a bona fide CXCL17 receptor and suggest a
two-step model of GPR25 activation, in which the receptor N-terminus orients
CXCL17 for activation of GPR25 via its C-terminus. We also advocate the
reclassification of CXCL17 as a chemoattractant distinct from the chemokine
family.

Plain Language Summary: Inflammatory diseases are characterised by the
recruitment of white blood cells (leukocytes) to inflamed tissues, which
dictates the progression of disease. Key to this process are chemoattractants,
small molecules released in the tissues which bind to receptors on the
leukocytes, directing their migration from the circulation to the tissue.
Modulating this process therefore offers potential for novel therapeutic
interventions. In this study, we confirm the recent identification of the
chemoattractant CXCL17 as a ligand for the receptor GPR25 and identify binding
sites within GPR25 that are involved in responses to CXCL17. Because GPR25 is a
marker for cells with anti‐inflammatory activity, this information may aid the
development of pharmacological approaches to reduce inflammation.
