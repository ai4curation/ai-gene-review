# Structural organization of the gene for human CD36 glycoprotein.
**Authors:** Armesilla AL, Vega MA
**Journal:** J Biol Chem (1994)

## Abstract

1. J Biol Chem. 1994 Jul 22;269(29):18985-91.

Structural organization of the gene for human CD36 glycoprotein.

Armesilla AL(1), Vega MA.

Author information:
(1)Hospital de la Princesa, Madrid, Spain.

The cell-surface glycoprotein CD36 interacts with a large variety of ligands,
including collagen types I and IV, thrombospondin, erythrocytes parasitized with
Plasmodium falciparum, platelet-agglutinating protein p37, oxidized low density
lipoprotein, and long-chain fatty acids. Its expression is restricted to
platelets, monocytes, adipocytes, and some endothelial and epithelial cells and
is regulated during cell activation, differentiation, and development. CD36
belongs to a novel gene family of structurally related glycoproteins that
includes CLA-1 and the lysosomal membrane glycoprotein LIMPII. To advance our
knowledge on the genomic organization and the regulation of the cellular
expression of the genes of this family, we have investigated the structural
organization of the human CD36 gene and of its 5'-proximal flanking region. The
CD36 gene is encoded by 15 exons that extend more than 32 kilobases on the human
genome. Interestingly, the CD36 mRNA 5'-untranslated region is encoded by three
exons. The 3'-untranslated region is contained in two exons, whose expression
pattern can originate two mRNA forms. The cytoplasmic and transmembrane regions
predicted at both terminal ends of the polypeptide chain are encoded by single
exons, while the extracellular domain is encoded by 11 exons. The transcription
initiation site of the CD36 gene is located 289 nucleotides upstream from the
translational start codon. Sequence analysis of the proximal 5'-flanking region
of the gene reveals the existence of a TATA box appropriately located with
respect to the transcription initiation site and several potential
cis-regulatory elements that might contribute to the transcriptional regulation
of the CD36 gene. Delineation of the structural organization of the CD36 gene
may help in defining the boundaries of relevant structural and/or functional
domains in CD36 and, by extension, in the other members of the family.
