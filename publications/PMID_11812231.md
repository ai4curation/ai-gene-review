# Expression, purification, and characterization of a cobalt-activated chitin deacetylase (Cda2p) from Saccharomyces cerevisiae.
**Authors:** Martinou A, Koutsioulis D, Bouriotis V
**Journal:** Protein Expr Purif (2002)
**DOI:** [10.1006/prep.2001.1547](https://doi.org/10.1006/prep.2001.1547)

## Abstract

1. Protein Expr Purif. 2002 Feb;24(1):111-6. doi: 10.1006/prep.2001.1547.

Expression, purification, and characterization of a cobalt-activated chitin
deacetylase (Cda2p) from Saccharomyces cerevisiae.

Martinou A(1), Koutsioulis D, Bouriotis V.

Author information:
(1)Enzyme Biotechnology Division, Institute of Molecular Biology and
Biotechnology, Vassilika Vouton 711 10, Heraklion, Crete, Greece.

Chitin deacetylase (Cda2p) (EC 3.5.1.41) from Saccharomyces cerevisiae has been
purified from vegetative cells grown in galactose and further characterized. The
enzyme is a glycoprotein with an apparent molecular mass of approximately 43 kDa
and a carbohydrate content of approximately 18% by weight. With glycol chitin as
substrate, the optimum temperature for enzyme activity is 50 degrees C and the
pH optimum is 8.0. The enzyme requires at least two N-acetyl-D-glucosamine
residues (chitobiose) for catalysis and is partially inhibited by acetate.
Deglycosylation of the enzyme causes total loss of enzyme activity, which can be
restored by the addition of COCl(2).
