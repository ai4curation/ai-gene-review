The immune and nervous systems display considerable overlap in their molecular
repertoire. Molecules originally shown to be critical for immune responses also
serve neuronal functions that include normal brain development, neuronal
differentiation, synaptic plasticity, and behavior. We show here that
FcgammaRIIB, a low-affinity immunoglobulin G Fc receptor, and CD3 are involved
in cerebellar functions. Although membranous CD3 and FcgammaRIIB are crucial
regulators on different cells in the immune system, both CD3epsilon and
FcgammaRIIB are expressed on Purkinje cells in the cerebellum. Both
CD3epsilon-deficient mice and FcgammaRIIB-deficient mice showed an impaired
development of Purkinje neurons. In the adult, rotarod performance of these
mutant mice was impaired at high speed. In the two knockout mice, enhanced
paired-pulse facilitation of parallel fiber-Purkinje cell synapses was shared.
These results indicate that diverse immune molecules play critical roles in the
functional establishment in the cerebellum.
