# N-ethylmaleimide sensitive factor is required for fusion of the C. elegans uterine anchor cell.
**Authors:** Choi J, Richards KL, Cinar HN, Newman AP
**Journal:** Dev Biol (2006)
**DOI:** [10.1016/j.ydbio.2006.04.471](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ydbio.2006.04.471)

## Abstract

1. Dev Biol. 2006 Sep 1;297(1):87-102. doi: 10.1016/j.ydbio.2006.04.471.

N-ethylmaleimide sensitive factor is required for fusion of the C. elegans
uterine anchor cell.

Choi J(1), Richards KL, Cinar HN, Newman AP.

Author information:
(1)Verna and Marrs Maclean Department of Biochemistry and Molecular Biology,
Baylor College of Medicine, One Baylor Plaza, Houston, TX 77030, USA.

The fusion of the Caenorhabditis elegans uterine anchor cell (AC) with the
uterine-seam cell (utse) is an excellent model system for studying cell-cell
fusion, which is essential to animal development. We obtained an egg-laying
defective (Egl) mutant in which the AC fails to fuse with the utse. This defect
is highly specific: other aspects of utse development and other cell fusions
appear to occur normally. We find that defect is due to a missense mutation in
the nsf-1 gene, which encodes N-ethylmaleimide-sensitive factor (NSF), an
intracellular membrane fusion factor. There are two NSF-1 isoforms, which are
expressed in distinct tissues through two separate promoters. NSF-1L is
expressed in the uterus, including the AC. We find that nsf-1 is required
cell-autonomously in the AC for its fusion with the utse. Our results establish
AC fusion as a paradigm for studying cell fusion at single cell resolution and
demonstrate that the NSF ATPase is a key player in this process.
