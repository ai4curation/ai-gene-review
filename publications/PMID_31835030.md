During cell division, newly replicated DNA is actively segregated to the
daughter cells. In most bacteria, this process involves the DNA-binding protein
ParB, which condenses the centromeric regions of sister DNA molecules into
kinetochore-like structures that recruit the DNA partition ATPase ParA and the
prokaroytic SMC/condensin complex. Here, we report the crystal structure of a
ParB-like protein (PadC) that emerges to tightly bind the ribonucleotide CTP.
The CTP-binding pocket of PadC is conserved in ParB and composed of signature
motifs known to be essential for ParB function. We find that ParB indeed
interacts with CTP and requires nucleotide binding for DNA condensation in vivo.
We further show that CTP-binding modulates the affinity of ParB for centromeric
parS sites, whereas parS recognition stimulates its CTPase activity. ParB
proteins thus emerge as a new class of CTP-dependent molecular switches that act
in concert with ATPases and GTPases to control fundamental cellular functions.
