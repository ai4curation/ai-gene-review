Extracellular growth factors are required for the survival of most animal cells.
They often signal through the activation of the Ras pathway. However, the
molecular mechanisms by which Ras signaling inhibits the intrinsic cell death
machinery are not well understood. Here, we present evidence that in Drosophila,
activation of the Ras pathway specifically inhibits the proapoptotic activity of
the gene head involution defective (hid). By using transgenic animals and
cultured cells, we show that MAPK phosphorylation sites in Hid are critical for
this response. These findings define a novel mechanism by which growth factor
signaling directly inactivates a critical component of the intrinsic cell death
machinery. These studies provide further insights into the function of ras as an
oncogene.
