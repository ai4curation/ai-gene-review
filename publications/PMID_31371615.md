The phytohormone salicylic acid (SA) controls biotic and abiotic plant stress
responses. Plastid-produced chorismate is a branch-point metabolite for SA
biosynthesis. Most pathogen-induced SA derives from isochorismate, which is
generated from chorismate by the catalytic activity of ISOCHORISMATE SYNTHASE1.
Here, we ask how and in which cellular compartment isochorismate is converted to
SA. We show that in Arabidopsis, the pathway downstream of isochorismate
requires only two additional proteins: ENHANCED DISEASE SUSCEPTIBILITY5, which
exports isochorismate from the plastid to the cytosol, and the cytosolic
amidotransferase avrPphB SUSCEPTIBLE3 (PBS3). PBS3 catalyzes the conjugation of
glutamate to isochorismate to produce isochorismate-9-glutamate, which
spontaneously decomposes into SA and 2-hydroxy-acryloyl-N-glutamate. The minimal
requirement of three compartmentalized proteins controlling unidirectional
forward flux may protect the pathway against evolutionary forces and pathogen
perturbations.
