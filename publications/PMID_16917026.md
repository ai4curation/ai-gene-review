Congenital myasthenic syndromes (CMSs) are a group of inherited disorders of
neuromuscular transmission characterized by fatigable muscle weakness. One major
subgroup of patients shows a characteristic "limb girdle" pattern of muscle
weakness, in which the muscles have small, simplified neuromuscular junctions
but normal acetylcholine receptor and acetylcholinesterase function. We showed
that recessive inheritance of mutations in Dok-7, which result in a defective
structure of the neuromuscular junction, is a cause of CMS with proximal muscle
weakness.
