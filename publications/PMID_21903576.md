The product of the SSeCKS/GRAVIN/AKAP12 gene ("SSeCKS") is a major protein
kinase (PK) C substrate that exhibits tumor- and metastasis-suppressing activity
likely through its ability to scaffold multiple signaling mediators such as PKC,
PKA, cyclins, calmodulin, and Src. Although SSeCKS and PKCα bind
phosphatidylserine, we demonstrate that phosphatidylserine-independent binding
of PKC by SSeCKS is facilitated by two homologous SSeCKS motifs,
EG(I/V)(T/S)XWXSFK(K/R)(M/L)VTP(K/R)K(K/R)X(K/R)XXXEXXXE(E/D) (amino acids
592-620 and 741-769). SSeCKS binding to PKCα decreased kinase activity and was
dependent on the two PKC-binding motifs. SSeCKS scaffolding of PKC was increased
in confluent cell cultures, correlating with significantly increased SSeCKS
protein levels and decreased PKCα activity, suggesting a role for SSeCKS in
suppressing PKC activation during contact inhibition. SSeCKS-null mouse embryo
fibroblasts displayed increased relative basal and phorbol ester (phorbol
12-myristate 13-acetate)-induced PKC activity but were defective in phorbol
12-myristate 13-acetate-induced actin cytoskeletal reorganization and cell shape
change; these responses could be rescued by the forced expression of full-length
SSeCKS but not by an SSeCKS variant deleted of its PKC-binding domains. Finally,
the PKC binding sites in SSeCKS were required to restore cell rounding and/or
decreased apoptosis in phorbol ester-treated LNCaP, LNCaP-C4-2, and MAT-LyLu
prostate cancer cells. Thus, PKC-mediated remodeling of the actin cytoskeleton
is likely regulated by the ability of SSeCKS to control PKC signaling and
activity through a direct scaffolding function.
