# Evidence for a novel glycinamide ribonucleotide transformylase in Escherichia coli.
**Authors:** Nygaard P, Smith JM
**Journal:** J Bacteriol (1993)
**DOI:** [10.1128/jb.175.11.3591-3597.1993](https://doi.org/10.1128/jb.175.11.3591-3597.1993)

## Abstract

1. J Bacteriol. 1993 Jun;175(11):3591-7. doi: 10.1128/jb.175.11.3591-3597.1993.

Evidence for a novel glycinamide ribonucleotide transformylase in Escherichia 
coli.

Nygaard P(1), Smith JM.

Author information:
(1)Institute of Biological Chemistry B, University of Copenhagen, Denmark.

We demonstrate here that Escherichia coli synthesizes two different glycinamide 
ribonucleotide (GAR) transformylases, both catalyzing the third step in the 
purine biosynthetic pathway. One is coded for by the previously described purN 
gene (GAR transformylase N), and a second, hitherto unknown, enzyme is encoded 
by the purT gene (GAR transformylase T). Mutants defective in the synthesis of 
the purN- and the purT-encoded enzymes were isolated. Only strains defective in 
both genes require an exogenous purine source for growth. Our results suggest 
that both enzymes may function to ensure normal purine biosynthesis. 
Determination of GAR transformylase T activity in vitro required formate as the 
C1 donor. Growth of purN mutants was inhibited by glycine. Under these 
conditions GAR accumulated. Addition of purine compounds or formate prevented 
growth inhibition. The regulation of the level of GAR transformylase T is 
controlled by the PurR protein and hypoxanthine.
