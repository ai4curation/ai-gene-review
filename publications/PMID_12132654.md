# The role of protein kinase C in the transient association of p57, a coronin family actin-binding protein, with phagosomes.
**Authors:** Itoh S, Suzuki K, Nishihata J, Iwasa M, Oku T, Nakajin S, Nauseef WM, Toyoshima S
**Journal:** Biol Pharm Bull (2002)
**DOI:** [10.1248/bpb.25.837](https://doi.org/10.1248/bpb.25.837)

## Abstract

1. Biol Pharm Bull. 2002 Jul;25(7):837-44. doi: 10.1248/bpb.25.837.

The role of protein kinase C in the transient association of p57, a coronin
family actin-binding protein, with phagosomes.

Itoh S(1), Suzuki K, Nishihata J, Iwasa M, Oku T, Nakajin S, Nauseef WM,
Toyoshima S.

Author information:
(1)Department of Biochemistry, Hoshi University, Tokyo, Japan.

Phagocytosis of opsonized zymosan (OpZ) particles by differentiated cells of the
human leukemic cell line HL-60 induced transient periphagosomal association of
p57, a coronin family actin-binding protein, and F-actin with dissociation from
the phagosomes after ingestion was completed. Coincident with OpZ ingestion, p57
phosphorylation increased transiently and peaked with its dissociation from
phagosomes. Since p57 contains several putative sites for protein kinase C (PKC)
phosphorylation, we examined the effect of PKC on p57 phosphorylation and
association with the phagosome. Purified p57 was phosphorylated in vitro by PKC
isoforms alpha and delta, and PMA, an activator of PKC, induced p57
phosphorylation in HL-60 cells. Furthermore, chelerythrine, a specific PKC
inhibitor, blocked p57 phosphorylation and the dissociation of p57 and F-actin
from phagosomes, whereas wortmannin, genistein, and H-89 did not. Chelerythrine
also inhibited the translocation of LAMP-1, a marker protein of lysosomes, to
the OpZ-containing phagosomes, indicating that PKC-mediated phosphorylation is
required for phagosome-lysosome fusion. Taken together, these data suggest that
PKC-mediated phosphorylation of p57 triggers its dissociation from phagosomes,
an event that may be necessary for the fusion of phagosomes with lysosomes.
