    Nature. 1999 Dec 16;402(6763):740-1. doi: 10.1038/45422.

Caenorhabditis elegans senses environmental signals through ciliated sensory
neurons located primarily in sensory organs in the head and tail. Cilia function
as sensory receptors, and mutants with defective sensory cilia have impaired
sensory perception. Cilia are membrane-bound microtubule-based structures and in
C. elegans are only found at the dendritic endings of sensory neurons. Here we
show that mutations that cause defects in sensory cilia or their support cells,
or in sensory signal transduction, extend lifespan. Our findings imply that
sensory perception regulates the lifespan of this animal, and suggest that in
nature, its lifespan may be regulated by environmental cues.
