# Purification and characterization of a novel glycine oxidase from Bacillus subtilis.
**Authors:** Nishiya Y, Imanaka T
**Journal:** FEBS Lett (1998)
**DOI:** [10.1016/s0014-5793(98)01313-1](https://doi.org/10.1016/s0014-5793(98)01313-1)

## Abstract

1. FEBS Lett. 1998 Nov 6;438(3):263-6. doi: 10.1016/s0014-5793(98)01313-1.

Purification and characterization of a novel glycine oxidase from Bacillus
subtilis.

Nishiya Y(1), Imanaka T.

Author information:
(1)Tsuruga Institute of Biotechnology, Toyobo Co., Ltd., Fukui, Japan.
yoshiaki_nishiya@bio-toyobo.co.jp

The open reading frame yjbR which had been sequenced as a part of the Bacillus
subtilis genome project encodes a putative 40.9-kDa protein. The yjbR-coding
sequence was slightly similar to those of bacterial sarcosine oxidases and
possibly compatible with the tertiary structure of the porcine kidney D-amino
acid oxidase. The yjbR gene product was overproduced in Escherichia coli,
purified to homogeneity from the recombinant strain, and characterized. This
protein effectively catalyzed the oxidation of sarcosine (N-methylglycine),
N-ethylglycine and glycine. Lower activities on D-alanine, D-valine, and
D-proline were detected although no activities were shown on L-amino acids and
other D-amino acids. Since glycine is a product and not a substrate for
sarcosine oxidase, this protein is not a type of demethylating enzymes but a
novel deaminating oxidase, named glycine oxidase as a common name. Several
enzymatic properties of the B. subtilis glycine oxidase were also investigated.
