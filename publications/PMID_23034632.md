# Kif7 regulates Gli2 through Sufu-dependent and -independent functions during skin development and tumorigenesis.

## Abstract

Kif7 regulates Gli2 through Sufu-dependent and -independent functions during
skin development and tumorigenesis.

Li ZJ(1), Nieuwenhuis E, Nien W, Zhang X, Zhang J, Puviindran V, Wainwright BJ,
Kim PC, Hui CC.

Author information:
(1)Program in Developmental and Stem Cell Biology, The Hospital for Sick

Abnormal activation of Hedgehog (Hh) signaling leads to basal cell carcinoma
(BCC) of the skin, the most common human cancer. Gli2, the major transcriptional
activator of Hh signaling, is essential for hair follicle development and its
overexpression in epidermis induces BCC formation and maintains tumor growth.
Despite its importance in skin development and tumorigenesis, little is known
about the molecular regulation of Gli2. Sufu and Kif7 are two evolutionarily
conserved regulators of Gli transcription factors. Here, we show that Sufu and
Kif7 regulate Gli2 through distinct mechanisms in keratinocytes. Sufu restricts
the activity of Gli2 through cytoplasmic sequestration. Kif7 possesses
Sufu-dependent and -independent regulatory functions in Hh signaling: while it
promotes Hh pathway activity through the dissociation of Sufu-Gli2 complex, it
also contributes to the repression of Hh target genes in the absence of Sufu.
Deletion of both Sufu and Kif7 in embryonic skin leads to complete loss of
follicular fate. Importantly, although inactivation of Sufu or Kif7 alone in
adult epidermis cannot promote BCC formation, their simultaneous deletion
induces BCC. These studies establish Sufu and Kif7 as crucial components in the
regulation of Gli2 localization and activity, and illustrate their overlapping
functions in skin development and tumor suppression.
