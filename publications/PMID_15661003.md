# Why are pathogenic staphylococci so lysozyme resistant? The peptidoglycan O-acetyltransferase OatA is the major determinant for lysozyme resistance of Staphylococcus aureus.
**Authors:** Bera A, Herbert S, Jakob A, Vollmer W, Götz F
**Journal:** Mol Microbiol (2005)
**DOI:** [10.1111/j.1365-2958.2004.04446.x](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1365-2958.2004.04446.x)

## Abstract

1. Mol Microbiol. 2005 Feb;55(3):778-87. doi: 10.1111/j.1365-2958.2004.04446.x.

Why are pathogenic staphylococci so lysozyme resistant? The peptidoglycan
O-acetyltransferase OatA is the major determinant for lysozyme resistance of
Staphylococcus aureus.

Bera A(1), Herbert S, Jakob A, Vollmer W, Götz F.

Author information:
(1)Microbial Genetics, University of Tübingen, D-72076 Tübingen, Auf der
Morgenstelle, Germany.

Staphylococcus species belong to one of the few bacterial genera that are
completely lysozyme resistant, which greatly contributes to their persistence
and success in colonizing the skin and mucosal areas of humans and animals. In
an attempt to discover the cause of lysozyme resistance, we identified a gene,
oatA, in Staphylococcus aureus. The corresponding oatA deletion mutant had an
increased sensitivity to lysozyme. HPLC and electrospray ionization tandem mass
spectrometry analyses of the cell wall revealed that the muramic acid of
peptidoglycan of the wild-type strain was O-acetylated at C6-OH, whereas the
muramic acid of the oatA mutant lacked this modification. The complemented oatA
mutant was lysozyme resistant. We identified the first bacterial
peptidoglycan-specific O-acetyltransferase in S. aureus and showed that OatA, an
integral membrane protein, is the molecular basis for the high lysozyme
resistance in staphylococci.
