BACKGROUND: Sheep milk proteins (SMPs) are key nutritional and functional
components of sheep milk and have attracted increasing interest because of their
high digestibility, distinctive compositional profile, and reported
bioactivities. Compared with bovine milk proteins, SMPs contain a high
proportion of β-casein, relatively low αs1-casein, and multiple low-abundance
bioactive proteins and peptides. However, their high value utilization remains
limited by uneven global milk supply, raw-material variability, challenges in
separation and purification, and insufficient in vivo and clinical evidence.
SCOPE AND APPROACH: This review focuses specifically on sheep milk proteins as
nutritional, structural, functional, and industrial. It summarizes sheep milk
production, SMP composition and nutrition, and factors affecting SMP yield and
quality, including breed, lactation stage, parity, genotype, season, and
nutritional status. Enrichment, separation, purification, and identification
strategies are critically evaluated, together with reported bioactivities and
food related application potential.
KEY FINDINGS AND CONCLUSIONS: SMPs are promising multifunctional ingredients for
dairy innovation, infant nutrition, and bio-based food materials. Their value
should not be inferred from protein abundance alone, but from the combined
effects of protein composition, casein micelle structure, digestion-derived
peptide release, bioaccessibility, and processing feasibility. However,
translation remains constrained by inconsistent raw-material supply,
compositional variation, limited structure and activity understanding, and the
predominance of preclinical evidence. Future studies should prioritize
ACE-inhibitory and DPP-IV-inhibitory sheep milk peptides by validating their
structure activity relationships, bioavailability, and in vivo mechanisms in
relevant cardiometabolic models.
