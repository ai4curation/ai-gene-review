# Tyrosine phosphorylation controls brassinosteroid receptor activation by triggering membrane release of its kinase inhibitor.
**Authors:** Jaillais Y, Hothorn M, Belkhadir Y, Dabi T, Nimchuk ZL, Meyerowitz EM, Chory J
**Journal:** Genes Dev (2011)
**DOI:** [10.1101/gad.2001911](https://doi.org/10.1101/gad.2001911)
**PMC:** [PMC3034898](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3034898/)

## Abstract

1. Genes Dev. 2011 Feb 1;25(3):232-7. doi: 10.1101/gad.2001911.

Tyrosine phosphorylation controls brassinosteroid receptor activation by
triggering membrane release of its kinase inhibitor.

Jaillais Y(1), Hothorn M, Belkhadir Y, Dabi T, Nimchuk ZL, Meyerowitz EM, Chory
J.

Author information:
(1)Plant Biology Laboratory, The Salk Institute for Biological Studies, La
Jolla, CA 92037, USA.

Receptor tyrosine kinases control many critical processes in metazoans, but
these enzymes appear to be absent in plants. Recently, two Arabidopsis receptor
kinases--BRASSINOSTEROID INSENSITIVE 1 (BRI1) and BRI1-ASSOCIATED KINASE1
(BAK1), the receptor and coreceptor for brassinosteroids--were shown to
autophosphorylate on tyrosines. However, the cellular roles for tyrosine
phosphorylation in plants remain poorly understood. Here, we report that the
BRI1 KINASE INHIBITOR 1 (BKI1) is tyrosine phosphorylated in response to
brassinosteroid perception. Phosphorylation occurs within a reiterated [KR][KR]
membrane targeting motif, releasing BKI1 into the cytosol and enabling formation
of an active signaling complex. Our work reveals that tyrosine phosphorylation
is a conserved mechanism controlling protein localization in all higher
organisms.
