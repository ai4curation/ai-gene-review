# A pathway for disulfide bond formation in vivo.
**Authors:** Bardwell JC, Lee JO, Jander G, Martin N, Belin D, Beckwith J
**Journal:** Proc Natl Acad Sci U S A (1993)
**DOI:** [10.1073/pnas.90.3.1038](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.90.3.1038)

## Abstract

1. Proc Natl Acad Sci U S A. 1993 Feb 1;90(3):1038-42. doi:
10.1073/pnas.90.3.1038.

A pathway for disulfide bond formation in vivo.

Bardwell JC(1), Lee JO, Jander G, Martin N, Belin D, Beckwith J.

Author information:
(1)Department of Microbiology and Molecular Genetics, Harvard Medical School,
Boston, MA 02115.

Protein disulfide bond formation in Escherichia coli requires the periplasmic
protein DsbA. We describe here mutations in the gene for a second protein, DsbB,
which is also necessary for disulfide bond formation. Evidence suggests that
DsbB may act by reoxidizing DsbA, thereby regenerating its ability to donate its
disulfide bond to target proteins. We propose that DsbB, an integral membrane
protein, may be involved in transducing redox potential across the cytoplasmic
membrane.
