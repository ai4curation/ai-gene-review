# Molecular cloning and functional expression of Ca(v)3.1c, a T-type calcium channel from human brain.
**Authors:** Cribbs LL, Gomora JC, Daud AN, Lee JH, Perez-Reyes E
**Journal:** FEBS Lett (2000)
**DOI:** [10.1016/s0014-5793(99)01756-1](https://doi.org/10.1016/s0014-5793(99)01756-1)

## Abstract

1. FEBS Lett. 2000 Jan 21;466(1):54-8. doi: 10.1016/s0014-5793(99)01756-1.

Molecular cloning and functional expression of Ca(v)3.1c, a T-type calcium
channel from human brain.

Cribbs LL(1), Gomora JC, Daud AN, Lee JH, Perez-Reyes E.

Author information:
(1)Department of Physiology and the Cardiovascular Institute, Loyola University
Medical Center, Maywood, IL, USA.

Erratum in
    FEBS Lett 2000 Mar 31;470(3):378.

Low voltage-activated T-type calcium channels are encoded by a family of at
least three genes, with additional diversity created by alternative splicing.
This study describes the cloning of the human brain alpha1G, which is a novel
isoform, Ca(v)3.1c. Comparison of this sequence to genomic sequences deposited
in the GenBank allowed us to identify the intron/exon boundaries of the human
CACNA1G gene. A full-length cDNA was constructed, then used to generate a
stably-transfected mammalian cell line. The resulting currents were analyzed for
their voltage- and time-dependent properties. These properties identify this
gene as encoding a T-type Ca(2+) channel.
