# Cloning and characterization of the Saccharomyces cerevisiae C-22 sterol desaturase gene, encoding a second cytochrome P-450 involved in ergosterol biosynthesis.
**Authors:** Skaggs BA, Alexander JF, Pierson CA, Schweitzer KS, Chun KT, Koegel C, Barbuch R, Bard M
**Journal:** Gene (1996)
**DOI:** [10.1016/0378-1119(95)00770-9](https://doi.org/10.1016/0378-1119(95)00770-9)

## Abstract

1. Gene. 1996 Feb 22;169(1):105-9. doi: 10.1016/0378-1119(95)00770-9.

Cloning and characterization of the Saccharomyces cerevisiae C-22 sterol
desaturase gene, encoding a second cytochrome P-450 involved in ergosterol
biosynthesis.

Skaggs BA(1), Alexander JF, Pierson CA, Schweitzer KS, Chun KT, Koegel C,
Barbuch R, Bard M.

Author information:
(1)Department of Biology, Indiana University-Purdue University at Indianapolis
46202, USA.

The ERG5 gene from Saccharomyces cerevisiae was cloned by complementation of an
erg5-1 mutation using a negative selection protocol involving screening for
nystatin-sensitive transformants. ERG5 is the putative gene encoding the C-22
sterol desaturase required in ergosterol biosynthesis. The functional gene was
localized to a 2.15-kb SacI-EcoRI DNA fragment containing an open reading frame
of 538 amino acids (aa). ERG5 contains a 10-aa motif consistent with its role as
a cytochrome P-450 (CyP450) enzyme and is similar to a number of mammalian
CyP450 enzymes. Gene disruption demonstrates that ERG5 is not essential for cell
viability.
