# SNT-2 interacts with ERK2 and negatively regulates ERK2 signaling in response to EGF stimulation.
**Authors:** Huang L, Gotoh N, Zhang S, Shibuya M, Yamamoto T, Tsuchida N
**Journal:** Biochem Biophys Res Commun (2004)
**DOI:** [10.1016/j.bbrc.2004.09.152](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.bbrc.2004.09.152)

## Abstract

1. Biochem Biophys Res Commun. 2004 Nov 19;324(3):1011-7. doi:
10.1016/j.bbrc.2004.09.152.

SNT-2 interacts with ERK2 and negatively regulates ERK2 signaling in response to
EGF stimulation.

Huang L(1), Gotoh N, Zhang S, Shibuya M, Yamamoto T, Tsuchida N.

Author information:
(1)Department of Molecular Cellular Oncology and Microbiology, Graduate School,
Tokyo Medical and Dental University, 1-5-45 Yujima, Bunkyo-ku, Tokyo 113-8510,
Japan.

The control of cellular responses with fibroblast growth factors and
neurotrophins is mediated through membrane-linked docking proteins, SNT
(suc1-binding neurotrophic target)-1/FRS2alpha and SNT-2/FRS2beta. ERK1/2 are
members of the mitogen-activated protein kinase family that regulate diverse
cellular activities in response to various stimuli. Here, we demonstrate that
SNT-2 does not become tyrosine phosphorylated significantly in response to EGF
but forms a complex with ERK2 via the region of 186-252 amino acid residues, and
the complex formation is enhanced upon EGF stimulation. SNT-2 downregulates ERK2
phosphorylation, suppresses and delays ERK2 nuclear accumulation which occurs
following EGF stimulation. In contrast, the mutant SNT-2 which carries deletion
of 186-252 amino acids and lacks ERK2 binding does not have these effects. These
observations suggest that SNT-2 negatively regulates ERK2 signaling activated
via EGF stimulation through direct binding to ERK2.
