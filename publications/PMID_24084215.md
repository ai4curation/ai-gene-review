# Inhibition of phosphatidylinositol 3-kinase/Akt signaling attenuates hypoxia-induced pulmonary artery remodeling and suppresses CREB depletion in arterial smooth muscle cells.
**Authors:** Garat CV, Crossno JT Jr, Sullivan TM, Reusch JE, Klemm DJ

Hypoxia-induced pulmonary hypertension is characterized by progressive
remodeling of the pulmonary artery (PA) system and loss of the transcription
factor, cAMP response element binding protein (CREB) in PA smooth muscle cells
(SMCs). Previous in vitro studies suggested that platelet-derived growth factor,
a mitogen produced in the hypoxic arterial wall, elicits loss of CREB in medial
SMCs via the PI3K/Akt pathway. These events trigger switching of SMCs from a
quiescent, contractile phenotype to a proliferative, migratory,
dedifferentiated, and synthetic phenotype, which contributes to PA thickening.
Here, we investigated whether inhibition of PI3K or Akt could attenuate arterial
remodeling in the lung and prevent CREB loss in PA medial SMCs in rats subjected
to chronic hypoxia. Inhibition of either enzyme-blunted hypoxia-induced PA
remodeling and SMC CREB depletion and diminished SMC proliferation and collagen
deposition. Inhibition of Akt, but not PI3K, suppressed muscularization of
distal arterioles and blunted right ventricular hypertrophy. Interestingly, mean
PA pressure was elevated equally by hypoxia in untreated and inhibitor-treated
groups but was normalized acutely by the Rho kinase inhibitor, Fasudil. We
conclude that PI3K and Akt inhibitors can attenuate hypoxia-induced PA
remodeling and SMC CREB depletion but fail to block the development of pulmonary
hypertension because of their inability to repress Rho kinase-mediated
vasoconstriction.

Introduction

Pulmonary arterial hypertension (PAH) is characterized by thickening or remodeling of the pulmonary artery (PA) wall and persistent vasoconstriction of the pulmonary vasculature.1 In small animals like rats, the thickening of the PA wall does not seem to reduce lumen volume and therefore does not contribute to the increased workload placed on the right ventricle (RV) in PAH.2–4 However, in larger animals and humans, remodeling of the PA wall is progressive, and reduction in lumen volume and occlusive lesions are found in both primary and secondary forms of human PAH. Although most current therapies for PAH target persistent vasoconstriction, few if any have been designed to address remodeling of the PA wall.1

Thickening of the PA wall is the result of medial smooth muscle cells (SMCs) proliferation, hypertrophy and extracellular matrix (ECM) production, and collagen and elastin deposition in the adventitia. In addition, SMCs appear around previously thin-walled nonmuscularized distal arterioles, transforming them into “resistance vessels” with decreased distensibility. Hypoxia triggers these events by eliciting the expression of growth factors including platelet-derived growth factor (PDGF)-BB, vascular endothelial growth factor, transforming growth factor-β, endothelin-1, and thrombospondin-1 that are potent SMC mitogens.5–7 These growth factors stimulate several intracellular signaling systems including MAP kinases,8,9 protein kinase C,10,11 Rho/RhoA kinase,12,13 and the PI3-kinase/Akt pathway.14–20

The PI3K/Akt signaling pathway plays especially important roles in vascular development and normal vascular function. Angiogenesis and vascular development involve the activation of PI3K/Akt signaling in response to extracellular stimuli like vascular endothelial growth factor21,22 and various fibroblast growth factors.23,24 During vascular development, PI3K/Akt signaling pathways promote the proliferation, differentiation, and survival of resident cells in the vessel wall and the recruitment of cells from other regions of the vasculature. The importance of PI3K/Akt signaling in normal vascular development is exemplified by gene knockout studies such as the Akt-1 null mouse, which exhibits delayed vessel maturation and increased vascular permeability.25 Another mouse model having an endothelial-specific knockdown of phosphatase and tensin homolog (PTEN), an inhibitor of PI3K/Akt, exhibits increased angiogenesis.26

In addition to participating in normal vascular development and function, the PI3K/Akt pathway participates in vascular remodeling and the development of various vascular pathologies. In particular, growth factors that are elevated in the vessel wall during remodeling like angiotensin II,14,15 insulinlike growth factor 1,16,17 PDGF-BB,18,19 and endothelin-120 stimulate PI3K/Akt signaling in medial SMCs. Activation of PI3K/Akt by these factors promotes SMC proliferation, hypertrophy, survival, and neointimal growth; increases SMC contractility, polyploidization, and migration; and downregulates expression of SMC markers. Recently, Furgeson et al27 demonstrated that targeted knockdown of PTEN in SMCs promotes vascular remodeling and that highly proliferative SMCs from injured aortas exhibit constitutive Akt signaling. Thus, PI3K/Akt signaling seems to be an important pathway governing SMC phenotype and arterial remodeling.

We have previously shown that levels of the transcription factor, cAMP response element binding protein (CREB) are reduced in medial SMCs in remodeled PAs from hypertensive calves and rats exposed to chronic hypoxia.28 In vitro studies with PA SMCs in culture demonstrate that forced depletion of CREB with small interfering RNA promotes SMC proliferation and migration, leads to decreased expression of SMC markers like SM-myosin and calponin, and stimulates collagen and elastin production. These changes are consistent with those observed in the PA wall as it remodels, wherein SMC dedifferentiation, proliferation, migration, and ECM synthesis are upregulated and contribute to vessel thickening. PDGF, which is produced in the hypoxic PA wall during remodeling, stimulates CREB depletion in primary PA SMCs. This occurs through the PI3K/Akt pathway, which promotes nuclear export, ubiquitination, and proteasomal degradation of CREB.29 The data suggest that PDGF activation of the PI3K/Akt pathway in response to hypoxia leads to CREB depletion in SMCs, which in turn promotes switching of SMCs from a quiescent contractile phenotype to a proliferative, migratory, dedifferentiated, and synthetic phenotype, which contribute to PA thickening.

With these factors in mind, we investigated whether inhibition of PI3K with LY294002 or Akt with triciribine in vivo could block PA remodeling and inhibit CREB loss in medial PA SMCs in a chronic hypoxic rat model of PAH. Inhibition of either PI3K or Akt reduced medial PA thickening and blockade of Akt reduced neomuscularization of distal arterioles. No reduction in mean PA pressure was observed with either compound. The inhibitors also prevented loss of CREB in medial PA SMCs in rats exposed to hypoxia. Repression of hypoxia-induced remodeling seemed to be due in part to decreased medial SMC proliferation and ECM deposition. We conclude that targeting of the PI3K/Akt signaling pathway may be an effective strategy to address the PA wall thickening in human PAH.

First, CREB is reduced in medial PA SMCs in bovine and rodent models of chronic hypoxia–induced pulmonary hypertension (PH).28,39 Second, loss of CREB in PA SMCs can be recapitulated in vitro by treating cells with PDGF-BB, which activates the PI3-kinase/Akt pathway resulting in CREB polyubiquitination and proteasomal degradation.29 Third, small interfering RNA–induced loss of CREB in SMCs in vitro stimulates their proliferation and alters their expression of contractile proteins and ECM components in a manner consistent with changes in SMC phenotype observed in the hypertensive PA wall. Both inhibitors also suppressed SMC proliferation and collagen deposition in the PA wall. Inhibition of Akt also attenuated muscularization of distal arterioles and blunted RV hypertrophy. These results support a model in which chronic hypoxia stimulates sustained PI3K/Akt signaling, most likely through growth factors like PDGF that are upregulated in the hypoxic PA wall. The PI3K/Akt pathway subsequently regulates several aspects of PA remodeling including SMC proliferation and migration and increased deposition of ECM components by medial and adventitial cell populations. This pathway participates in multiple cellular activities including proliferation, adhesion, migration, metabolism, and survival. Previous studies from our laboratory have demonstrated that activation of the PI3K/Akt signaling pathway in cultured PA SMCs promotes phosphorylation of CREB by casein kinase 2, which targets CREB for polyubiquitination and proteosomal degradation.25 A recent review focused on the role of PI3K/Akt in angiogenesis through loss of PTEN and/or increased expression of growth factor receptors such as epidermal growth factor receptor.41 Furgeson et al27 further suggested that PTEN negatively regulates SMC phenotypic modulation with loss of PTEN in SMC associated with multiple downstream events regulating neointima formation, including promotion of an activated SMC phenotype. In closing, we conclude that inhibition of the PI3K/Akt signaling pathway may be an effective means of blocking the remodeling of the PA tree in response to hypoxia and other insults. Although vessel remodeling does not seem to contribute to the development of PH in rodent models, encroachment of the arterial wall into the vessel lumen in conjunction with vasoconstriction both contribute to PH in humans. Thus, therapies that target PI3-kinase and Akt in particular may be especially effective when used in combination with vasorelaxant agents.
