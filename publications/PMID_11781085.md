# Structure and orientation of sarcolipin in lipid environments.
**Authors:** Mascioni A, Karim C, Barany G, Thomas DD, Veglia G
**Journal:** Biochemistry (2002)
**DOI:** [10.1021/bi011243m](https://doi.org/10.1021/bi011243m)

## Abstract

1. Biochemistry. 2002 Jan 15;41(2):475-82. doi: 10.1021/bi011243m.

Structure and orientation of sarcolipin in lipid environments.

Mascioni A(1), Karim C, Barany G, Thomas DD, Veglia G.

Author information:
(1)Department of Chemistry, University of Minnesota, Minneapolis, Minnesota
55455, USA.

Sarcolipin (SLN) is a 31 amino acid integral membrane protein that regulates
Ca-ATPase activity in skeletal muscle. Here, we report the three-dimensional
structure and topology of synthetic SLN in lipid environments, as determined by
solution and solid-state NMR spectroscopy. 2D solution NMR experiments were
performed on SLN solubilized in sodium dodecyl sulfate (SDS) micelles. We found
that SLN adopts a highly defined alpha-helical conformation from F9 through R27,
with a backbone RMSD of 0.65 A and a side chain RMSD of 1.66 A. The N-terminus
(M1 through L8) and the C-terminus (S28 through Y31) are mostly unstructured.
The orientation of the SLN was determined using one-dimensional (15)N NMR
solid-state spectroscopy. The protein was incorporated into phospholipid
bilayers prepared from a mixture of 1,2-dioleoyl-sn-glycero-3-phosphocholine and
1,2-dioleoyl-sn-glycero-3-phosphoethanolamine. The (15)N chemical shift
solid-state spectra from selectively labeled SLN samples indicate that SLN
orients perpendicularly to the plane of the membrane bilayers. These results
support the proposed mechanism of Ca-ATPase regulation of SLN via
protein-protein intramembranous interactions between the highly conserved
transmembrane domains of the Ca-ATPase and the conserved transmembrane domain of
SLN.
