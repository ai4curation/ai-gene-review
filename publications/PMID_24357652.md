Comment in
    EMBO Rep. 2014 Jan;15(1):5-6. doi: 10.1002/embr.201338224.

PINK1 is a mitochondrial kinase proposed to have a role in the pathogenesis of
Parkinson's disease through the regulation of mitophagy. Here, we show that the
PINK1 main cleavage product, PINK152, after being generated inside mitochondria,
can exit these organelles and localize to the cytosol, where it is not only
destined for degradation by the proteasome but binds to Parkin. The interaction
of cytosolic PINK1 with Parkin represses Parkin translocation to the
mitochondria and subsequent mitophagy. Our work therefore highlights the
existence of two cellular pools of PINK1 that have different effects on Parkin
translocation and mitophagy.
