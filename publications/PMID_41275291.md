BACKGROUND: Glioblastoma (GBM), the most aggressive primary brain tumor, has a
dismal prognosis largely due to therapy-resistant stem-like cells that drive
recurrence. While N6-methyladenosine modifications in GBM are well-studied, the
role of 5-methylcytosine (m5C) modifications specifically in circular RNAs
(circRNAs) remains poorly understood. METHODS: Bioinformatic analysis, qRT-PCR
and Western blot assays were used to investigate the expression of NSUN7,
circNTRK2, YBX3, STK31, IKZF1 in GBM tissues and cell lines. m5C dot blot
assays, m5C-bisulfite sequence assays, Sanger’s sequencing, RNA pull down and
RIP assays, in vitro kinase assays, chromatin immunoprecipitation and luciferase
reporter gene assays are used to clarify the interaction between factors above.
Colony formation assays, sphere formation assays, and stemness marker (OCT4,
DCLK1) analysis were utilized to assess the impact of the factors above on GBM
stemness. Subcutaneous heterotopic and orthotopic xenograft are utilized to
demonstrate the function of YBX3/circNTRK2/STK31/IKZF1 axis in GBM in vivo.
RESULTS: NSUN7 and YBX3 are both upregulated in GBM tissues and cell lines.
NSUN7 catalyzes m5C modification of circNTRK2. YBX3 decreasing circNTRK2
stability and function via binding m5C of circNTRK2. CircNTRK2 binds to and
activates STK31, leading to phosphorylation of IKZF1 at S63, which decreases the
O-GlcNAc levels and transcriptional activity of IKZF1, thereby promoting the
stem cell characteristics of GBM cells. CONCLUSIONS: This study unveils that
NSUN7-mediated m5C modification of circNTRK2 fuels GBM stemness via
circNTRK2-dependent STK31 activation, thus identifying potential biomarkers for
targeted molecular therapy of GBM and providing novel therapeutic targets for
GBM treatment.

The loss of differentiated phenotypes and acquisition of stem-like characteristics are critical processes underlying tumor initiation, therapeutic resistance, and metastatic dissemination [17]. Therefore, deciphering the mechanisms that sustain stemness in GBM may reveal novel therapeutic targets.

5-Methylcytosine (m5C) RNA modification is a highly conserved epigenetic mark that influences RNA localization, stability, and translational efficiency. This modification is dynamically regulated by m5C methyltransferases (writers), demethylases (erasers), and binding proteins (readers). For instance, circRREB1 mediates metabolic reprogramming and stemness maintenance to facilitate pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma progression [18]. He et al. reported that m5C-modified circRNAs are closely associated with hepatocellular carcinoma progression [19]. Neurotrophic receptor tyrosine kinase 2 (NTRK2) plays essential roles in neural development. Recently, it is reported that circNTRK2 promotes the progression of nasopharyngeal carcinoma via sponging miR-384 to regulate SOX4 expression [20]. However, there are no reports of m5C modification of circNTRK2 yet. The DNA methyltransferase-2 (DNMT2) and NSUN (NOL1/NOP2/Sun) families function as m5C writers across multiple tumor types. NOP2, a member of the NSUN family, facilitates EZH2-mediated epithelial-mesenchymal transition by enhancing EZH2 mRNA stability via m5C methylation in lung cancer progression [22]. NSUN1 interacts with BRD4 and RNA polymerase II to establish an active chromatin state that confers resistance to 5-azacytidine in leukemia [23]. Epigenetic inactivation of the 5-methylcytosine RNA methyltransferase NSUN7 is associated with clinical outcome and therapeutic vulnerability in liver cancer [25]. NSUN7, a member of the NSUN family located on chromosome 4p14, was proposed by Sato et al. as a candidate biomarker for low-grade glioma based on transcriptomic analysis of 18,413 glioma-associated genes [26]. Nevertheless, the expression and functional significance of NSUN7 in GBM remain unexplored.

Non-coding RNAs also contribute to tumorigenesis by modulating kinase activity. For example, SNORA73A/B induces PARP-1 ribosylation to facilitate breast cancer proliferation [27], while circRNA-derived double-stranded RNAs activate PKR, a key regulator of innate immunity [28]. Serine/threonine kinase 31 (STK31), encoded on chromosome 7p15.3, is a member of the STK family often dysregulated in human cancers. Ectopic expression of STK31 was shown to enhance proliferation, migration, and colony formation in gastric cancer cells and promote tumor growth in nude mice [29], whereas in colorectal cancer, it reportedly suppresses proliferation and increases chemosensitivity [30]. Despite these implications, the role of STK31 in GBM has not been thoroughly investigated.

IKAROS family zinc finger 1 (IKZF1) acts as a transcriptional activator of CD180, supporting myeloma cell proliferation [31]. Its transcriptional activity is modulated by post-translational modifications such as phosphorylation, SUMOylation, and ubiquitination [32–34]. In B-cell acute lymphoblastic leukemia, IKZF1 recruits HDAC1 to the BCL2L1 promoter and suppresses its expression through chromatin remodeling [35].

Doublecortin-like kinase 1 (DCLK1), a member of the protein kinase superfamily, promotes stemness in colorectal cancer [36], non-small cell lung cancer [37], osteosarcoma [38], and renal clear cell carcinoma [39]. Similarly, OCT4, a well-established stemness marker, is highly expressed in GBM and correlates with poor prognosis in glioma patients.

In this study, we evaluated the expression of NSUN7, YBX3, STK31, and IKZF1 in GBM tissues and cell lines, and explored the mechanistic basis of m5C-dependent modification of circNTRK2 and its role in regulating GBM stemness. Our findings aim to provide new insights into the function of m5C-modified circNTRK2 in GBM pathogenicity and to identify potential therapeutic targets.

CircNTRK2 activates STK31 by promoting autophosphorylation at S884Next, we explored potential biochemical outcomes of circNTRK2-STK31 interactions. STK31 expression levels remained unchanged following circNTRK2 overexpression and the circNTRK2 expression levels remained unchanged following STK31 overexpression (Additional file 1: Supplementary Fig. 4A, B). RNA pull-down assays validated STK31 enrichment using wild-type probe in the STK31-WT group, but not in the STK31-ΔTudor group (Fig. 4A). We found that the presence of wild-type STK31 with circNTRK2 increased STK31 phosphorylation levels, whereas phosphorylation decreased upon Tudor domain deletion or S884 site mutation (Additional file 1: Supplementary Fig. 4C). Then we predicted the presence of a phosphorylation site in the protein kinase domain of STK31 to discover that there is phosphorylation site in the kinase domain (Additional file 1: Supplementary Fig. 4D). Therefore, we hypothesized that dsRNA of circNTRK2 activates the kinase activity of STK31 via binding to the Tudor domain of STK31 and promoting its autophosphorylation at S884 site. In vitro kinase assays revealed that STK31 failed to undergo autophosphorylation in both ΔTudor and S884 mutant groups in the presence of circNTRK2, while wild-type STK31 showed robust autophosphorylation (Fig. 4B), indicating that the S884 site and the Tudor domain are necessary for STK31 auto-phosphorylation. Immunofluorescence and fluorescence in situ hybridization assays showed that cricNTRK2 and STK31 were mainly colocalized in the cell cytoplasm (Additional file 1: Supplementary Fig. 4E). In addition, overexpression of wild-type STK31 enhanced the inhibitory effect exerted by circNTRK2 overexpression, whereas overexpression of mutant STK31 did not alter the suppression of stemness properties and DCLK1/OCT4 expression mediated by circNTRK2 overexpression, and STK31 knockdown abolished the inhibitory effects mediated by circNTRK2 overexpression (Fig. 4C, D, Additional file 1: Supplementary Fig. 4F). These results indicate that the dsRNA structure of circNTRK2 binds STK31’s Tudor domain, promotes its autophosphorylation at S884, thereby activating STK31 and regulating GBM cell stemness properties.

CircNTRK2 activates STK31 by promoting autophosphorylation at S884Next, we explored potential biochemical outcomes of circNTRK2-STK31 interactions. STK31 expression levels remained unchanged following circNTRK2 overexpression and the circNTRK2 expression levels remained unchanged following STK31 overexpression (Additional file 1: Supplementary Fig. 4A, B). RNA pull-down assays validated STK31 enrichment using wild-type probe in the STK31-WT group, but not in the STK31-ΔTudor group (Fig. 4A). We found that the presence of wild-type STK31 with circNTRK2 increased STK31 phosphorylation levels, whereas phosphorylation decreased upon Tudor domain deletion or S884 site mutation (Additional file 1: Supplementary Fig. 4C). Then we predicted the presence of a phosphorylation site in the protein kinase domain of STK31 to discover that there is phosphorylation site in the kinase domain (Additional file 1: Supplementary Fig. 4D). Therefore, we hypothesized that dsRNA of circNTRK2 activates the kinase activity of STK31 via binding to the Tudor domain of STK31 and promoting its autophosphorylation at S884 site. In vitro kinase assays revealed that STK31 failed to undergo autophosphorylation in both ΔTudor and S884 mutant groups in the presence of circNTRK2, while wild-type STK31 showed robust autophosphorylation (Fig. 4B), indicating that the S884 site and the Tudor domain are necessary for STK31 auto-phosphorylation. Immunofluorescence and fluorescence in situ hybridization assays showed that cricNTRK2 and STK31 were mainly colocalized in the cell cytoplasm (Additional file 1: Supplementary Fig. 4E). In addition, overexpression of wild-type STK31 enhanced the inhibitory effect exerted by circNTRK2 overexpression, whereas overexpression of mutant STK31 did not alter the suppression of stemness properties and DCLK1/OCT4 expression mediated by circNTRK2 overexpression, and STK31 knockdown abolished the inhibitory effects mediated by circNTRK2 overexpression (Fig. 4C, D, Additional file 1: Supplementary Fig. 4F). These results indicate that the dsRNA structure of circNTRK2 binds STK31’s Tudor domain, promotes its autophosphorylation at S884, thereby activating STK31 and regulating GBM cell stemness properties.
