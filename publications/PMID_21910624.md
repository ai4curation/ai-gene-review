Engulfment and destruction of invading microorganisms by phagocytosis are
critical components of the innate immune response. In addition, phagocytosis is
also required for the clearance of apoptotic bodies, an essential aspect of
tissue homeostasis and remodeling. Here, we summarize the current knowledge of
the cellular and molecular basis of phagosome formation and maturation. We
discuss the manner in which phagocytosis is subverted by certain pathogens and
consider congenital disorders that affect phagocyte function.
