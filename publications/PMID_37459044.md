Teratozoospermia with cephalic defects is one of the most severe types of sperm
defects known to date. While several monogenic factors are linked to cephalic
abnormalities, such as globozoospermia and macrozoospermia, the genetic cause of
vacuolated spermatozoa remains inadequately described. Here, we analyzed
whole-exome sequencing (WES) data for an individual from a consanguineous family
with severely vacuolated spermatozoa. The analysis revealed a novel homozygous
c.520A>G (p.Thr174Ala) variant in the archaelysin family metallopeptidase 2 (
AMZ2 ), a gene that encodes a zinc metalloprotease previously shown to be highly
expressed in the testes and sperm. Multiple algorithms predicted this variant to
be a damaging mutation. Consistent with an autosomal recessive mode of
inheritance, this variant was inherited from heterozygous parental carriers. To
investigate the potential pathogenicity of the identified variant, we compared
the AMZ2 expression in sperm cells from the patient with the AMZ2 variant and
from a healthy control. Immunoblot analysis revealed that the homozygous
missense variant in AMZ2 abolished AMZ2 expression in the spermatozoa. Our
findings reveal a candidate causative gene for vacuolated spermatozoa.

INTRODUCTION

Approximately 15% of couples worldwide suffer from infertility, and around 50% of male infertility is linked in part to defects in sperm morphology.1 Teratozoospermia, a common cause of male infertility, is characterized by morphological abnormalities in more than 96% of an individual’s spermatozoa,23 including those in the sperm head, neck, midpiece, and/or endpiece.4 Cephalic defects are one of the most severe types of sperm defects.4 A number of monogenic factors have been reportedly associated with cephalic abnormalities such as globozoospermia and macrozoospermia.567 Some cephalic defects are related to the presence of abnormally large vacuoles, but it is unclear specifically how these defects function and whether they have monogenic causes.

Sperm-head vacuoles are considered a novel abnormality in male infertility.89 Previous studies of sperm-head vacuoles have primarily focused on clinical and structural characteristics, such as the relationship between sperm-head vacuoles presence and embryo quality.10111213 Some studies have found that large sperm-head vacuoles are related to increased DNA fragmentation,1415 occurrence of chromatin coagulation defects,1016 and aneuploidy rates.8 However, previous studies have not identified a monogenic cause of sperm-head vacuole presence. Whole exome sequencing (WES) has enabled the discovery of the genetic causes of numerous monogenic diseases in recent years. Therefore, we hypothesized that WES could reveal monogenic causes of sperm-head vacuole formation.

Here, the genetic cause of severely vacuolated spermatozoa was analyzed in a Chinese man from a consanguineous family. WES was conducted to uncover a potentially monogenic factor responsible for the phenotype. Remarkably, we identified a novel homozygous mutation in the archaelysin family metallopeptidase 2 (AMZ2) gene.

Analysis of the semen revealed normal semen volume, sperm concentration, and sperm motility (Table 1). However, semen morphological analysis revealed severe teratozoospermia, with 99.5% of the sperm having head defects with obvious vacuoles (Table 1 and Figure 1a).

Due to the severely poor sperm morphology and history of infertility, intra-cytoplasmic sperm injection (ICSI) was carried out for the proband and partner. Twelve oocytes at metaphase II were retrieved from the partner and all 12 were fertilized through ICSI using the proband’s spermatozoa (Table 2), suggesting that the process of fertilization was not affected by the abnormal sperm morphology. Two embryos at day 3 (D3) of good quality were transferred to the partner, but both failed to develop in utero (Table 2). After intra-conjugal ICSI failure, the proband and partner proceeded with assisted reproductive technology (ART) using donor sperm, which resulted in a successful clinical pregnancy. This indicated that the proband’s partner possessed normal reproductive capability. Taken together, these findings suggested that the proband had severe teratozoospermia, which caused infertility in the male.

Identification of a homozygous AMZ2 mutation in the probandWe therefore analyzed WES data to identify the causative mutation responsible for the observed severe teratozoospermia phenotype. Because most individuals with severe teratozoospermia are infertile, the pathogenic variants responsible for teratozoospermia are expected to be rare in human populations. Thus, we excluded variants that were found in the Genome Aggregation Database (gnomAD), the Exome Aggregation Consortium (ExAC), or the 1000 Genomes Project to have allele frequencies ≥0.01. We preferred loss-of-function (LoF) variants (nonsense and frameshift variants, canonical ±1 or 2 splice variants, and start-loss variants) and missense variants predicted in silico to be damaging when filtering possible causative mutations. Variants in X-linked genes and those with two alleles were retained for further evaluation. Genetic analyses ultimately revealed a homozygous missense variant, c.520A>G (p.Thr174Ala), in AMZ2. This gene encodes a zinc metalloprotease and is highly expressed in the testes.2223 The WES data did not show that the proband had variants in any other genes that may be involved in male reproductive development. We validated the c.520A>G variant through Sanger sequencing of DNA samples from the proband and his parents (Figure 1b). Both parents were heterozygous carriers, consistent with an autosomal recessive mode of inheritance.

DISCUSSIONOver the past several years, many studies have explored the relationships between male reproductive ability and sperm-head vacuoles based on clinical and ultrastructural characteristics.16283233 However, there have been few previous reports on monogenic factors that lead to sperm-head vacuolation. The development of high-throughput sequencing technology has enabled the discovery of genetic factors underlying an increasing number of sperm-head deformities.567 The present study used WES data to identify the candidate causative gene (AMZ2) for severe teratozoospermia characterized by vacuolated spermatozoa. These findings expand our knowledge of the genetic basis underlying sperm-head vacuoles and provide new understandings of the causes of teratozoospermia and male infertility.The Human Protein Atlas database shows that AMZ2 is highly expressed in human testes and sperm,23 indicating that this gene may play an important role in testicle or sperm functioning. AMZ2 encodes a zinc metalloprotease of unknown function; however, other zinc metalloproteases have been reported to greatly influence the maintenance of normal epigenetic status.2930 Moreover, fluorescent immunocytochemistry and high-magnification microscopy have indicated that sperm-head vacuoles are associated with the enrichment of histone H3 trimethylation at lysine 4 (H3K4me3), which appears to be harmful to embryo development and offspring health.31 Here, ICSI with an individual who had severely vacuolated spermatozoa did not result in clinical pregnancy. We therefore hypothesize that abolishment of AMZ2 function may affect the epigenetic status of human sperm cells, resulting in sperm-head defects, including large vacuoles, ultimately affecting embryonic development.This study provides new insights into a monogenic cause of male infertility caused by sperm-head vacuoles. Screening for deleterious mutations in AMZ2 is of potential clinical relevance for genetic diagnosis of male infertility and prediction of ART outcomes. Further investigations are necessary to confirm the pathogenicity of this variant and to elucidate the mechanism of action associated with this gene. Such studies could involve the construction of an AMZ2 gene-edited mouse model and subsequent assessment of fertility and sperm morphology in the mutant. A combination of multi-omics methods would be efficient in studying the role of AMZ2 in sperm-head vacuole formation.
