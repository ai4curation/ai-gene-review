Immunotherapy resistance remains a substantial barrier to improving treatment 
outcomes for patients with lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD). Identifying effective 
immunotherapy target is crucial for enhancing therapeutic efficacy in LUAD. 
Through database analysis, we discovered that ADP ribosylhydrolase-like 1 
(ADPRHL1, ARH2) is associated with immunosuppression. In this study, we first 
demonstrated that the increased presence of ARH2-positive macrophages in LUAD 
tumors is associated with immunosuppression. Furthermore, ARH2 promotes M2 
macrophage polarization and suppresses immune responses by regulating the 
FPR2/PI3K/AKT signaling pathway. Additionally, we found that artesunate (ART) 
can induce necroptosis in LUAD cells and activate antitumor immune responses. To 
translate these findings into a clinically viable therapeutic approach, we 
developed a pH/ROS-responsive nanosystem capable of co-delivering siARH2 and 
ART. This nanosystem effectively activated immune responses in both tumor cells 
and tumor-associated macrophages. Furthermore, the nanosystem demonstrated 
excellent in vivo safety, precise PD-L1 targeting, and responsiveness to ROS and 
pH variations. It considerably suppressed the malignant phenotype of tumor cells 
induced by macrophages and enhanced T-cell-mediated immune responses. Overall, 
targeting ARH2 in combination with ART represents a promising novel strategy for 
the treatment of LUAD.

Introduction

Lung cancer remains the most prevalent malignant tumor globally and is associated with the highest mortality rate among all cancers [1,2]. The incidence of lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD), which constitutes approximately half of all lung cancer cases, has increased in recent years. Immunotherapy has emerged as a first-line treatment option for patients with non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) [3,4]. However, a significant proportion of lung cancer patients exhibit resistance to immunotherapy [5], primarily due to the failure to activate antitumor immune responses [6]. The underlying mechanisms of this resistance remain poorly understood.

To increase the efficacy of immunotherapy, reversing the immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment (TME) is essential [7,8]. Macrophages play a pivotal role in shaping this immunosuppressive microenvironment [9,10]. Through analysis of the Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA), we identified that high expression of ARH2 is associated with immune suppression. ARH2, a member of the ARH family, includes ADPRH1 (ARH1), ADPRHL1 (ARH2), and ADPRHL2 (ARH3). Members of the ARH family exhibit diverse subcellular distributions but can bind ADP-ribose [11]. ARHs possess ADP-ribose hydrolase activity, participate in transcriptional regulation, and are critical for maintaining normal cellular functions [12,13]. In this study, we investigated the role of ARH2 in the LUAD immune response and evaluated its potential as a therapeutic target for LUAD treatment.

Immunogenic tumor cell death is highly beneficial for activating antitumor immune responses [14,15]. Necroptosis, characterized by the destruction of the plasma membrane, is a crucial mechanism of immunogenic cell death [16,17]. Following necroptosis, inflammatory factors and immune complexes are released into the extracellular space, where they recruit immune cells to the site of action [18,19]. In this study, we demonstrated that artesunate (ART) could induce necroptosis in LUAD cells. ART, a derivative of artemisinin, has long been used as a first-line antimalarial drug due to its excellent biocompatibility [20]. Recent studies have demonstrated that ART exhibits significant antitumor activity in vitro and promotes immunogenic tumor cell death [[21], [22], [23]]. In some small-scale clinical studies, ART has shown promising antitumor potential and good safety profiles [24]. However, owing to its short half-life, ART cannot achieve effective antitumor concentrations in vivo [25].

To address these challenges, we synthesized a multifunctional nanosystem capable of codelivering siARH2 and ART to treat LUAD. This nanosystem was designed to have ROS/pH-responsive properties, enabling it to reactivate the immune response within the tumor microenvironment (TME). The therapeutic strategy of this nanosystem involves enhancing anticancer efficacy through the induction of tumor cell necroptosis and the activation of macrophage-mediated immune responses. Owing to their excellent reactive oxygen species (ROS) responsiveness and ability to amplify ROS, vitamin E succinate (VES) and Se-Se have been incorporated into nanosystems [26,27]. A mPEG shell was used to ensure that the nanosystem degrades specifically within the mildly acidic TME [28,29]. Additionally, PD-L1-targeted peptides were integrated to facilitate attachment to tumor cells and enhance the intracellular uptake of the nanomedicines, thus promoting drug accumulation at the tumor site [30]. PLL-siARH2 inhibits the activation of M2-type macrophages, thus enhancing the overall immune response [31,32]. Moreover, ART triggers necroptosis in tumor cells. The combined effects of promoting necroptosis and inhibiting ARH2 expression effectively reverse the immunosuppressive TME and promote T cell activation, resulting in a systemic immune effect (Graphical Abstract).

After stratifying immune scores into high and low immune score groups, we identified differentially expressed genes (DEGs) between the two groups and performed Cox regression analysis on these DEGs. High ARH2 expression was associated with the suppression of multiple immune regulatory pathways and correlated with poor patient prognosis (Fig. 1E and F). 2.4ART induces necroptosis and promotes ROS production in tumor cellsART increased the proportion of apoptosis tumor cells, as determined by flow cytometry (FCM) (Fig. S4A). To investigate the siRNA loading capacity of nanosystems, we initially characterized the size and zeta potential of VPA/VG complexes with nucleic acids at various ratios. Notably, when the ratio reached 30:1, the size and zeta potential of complexes reverted to precomplex levels (Fig. 4I and J). Furthermore, transmission electron microscopy was employed to visualize the drug structure of the VPA/VG complexes in different pH/ROS solutions. The complex was relatively stable in a PBS solution at pH 7.4 and maintained its spherical structure under weakly acidic conditions (Fig. 4L). 3DiscussionImmunotherapy has considerably improved the prognosis and prolonged the survival of patients with LUAD [34,35]. However, a considerable proportion of patients remain insensitive to immunotherapy [36]. There may be a lack of immune cell infiltration into tumors following tumor cell death, which prevents the induction of effective immune responses [[37], [38], [39]]. In this study, we aimed to remodel the immunosuppressive TME by targeting both tumor cells and tumor-associated immune cells.Through analysis of the TCGA database, we identified that ARH2 was associated with immunosuppression in LUAD. ARH2, an ADP-ribose hydrolase [40], has been previously reported as a potential biomarker for circulating tumor cells and patient prognosis [41,42]. However, its role and mechanisms in tumor biology remain largely unexplored. In this study, we demonstrated that ARH2 is closely linked to immunosuppression in LUAD patients and is predominantly expressed in M2-type macrophages. Macrophages are the most abundant immune cells in the TME [43], and M2-type macrophages play a critical role in establishing an immunosuppressive TME [[44], [45], [46]]. Our study demonstrated that inhibiting ARH2 expression in macrophages can promote M2 polarization and enhance lymphocyte activation both in vivo and in vitro. Some studies have also shown that M2 macrophage polarization facilitates tumor cell migration, invasion, and other malignant behaviors, which is consistent with our findings [47,48]. Our findings revealed that ARH2 suppresses the expression of FPR2. FPR2 is a key receptor that inhibits M2 polarization of macrophages by blocking AKT pathway activation [[49], [50], [51], [52], [53]]. Furthermore, we demonstrated that knockdown of ARH2 expression effectively inhibited M2 polarization of macrophages by regulating FPR2/AKT pathway. On the basis of these findings, we selected ARH2 as a therapeutic target in the design and tested of the nanosystem described in this study.ART has been extensively utilized in antimalarial therapy and has demonstrated a favorable safety profile in vivo. Our research revealed that ART induces the production of ROS, leading to necroptosis in tumor cells. This necroptotic process results in the release of a substantial amount of tumor antigens and immune response factors, which considerably enhances immune cell infiltration and activates antitumor immune responses [54,55]. However, the rapid metabolic clearance of ART in vivo poses a significant challenge for achieving therapeutically effective blood concentrations, thus limiting its clinical application in cancer treatment [20].To address this limitation and effectively harness the dual activation of antitumor immune responses from both tumor cells and immune cells, we developed a TME-responsive nanosystem. This innovative system is designed to simultaneously deliver siARH2 and ART. The nanosystem has composed of a core of VPA-PD-L1pep and a shell of VG. The integration of mPEG not only prolongs the in vivo half-life of ART, but also imparts pH-responsive characteristics to the system [56,57]. Moreover, the incorporation of Se-Se and VES into the nanosystem further enhances ROS generation, which can additionally promote tumor cell death, as confirmed in our study [58,59]. Additionally, PLL was employed to facilitate siARH2 adsorption, thus effectively suppressing ARH2 expression. This optimized nanosystem showed enhanced accumulation in tumor tissue, which was attributed to its responsiveness to the weakly acidic and high ROS conditions prevalent in the TME [60,61].The nanosystem employed in this study is an advanced nanocarrier system that can effectively overcome multiple challenges in cancer therapy. This innovative device not only induces necroptosis and ROS in tumor cells but also robustly activates antitumor immune responses. Specifically, ART has demonstrated considerably antitumor efficacy by triggering necroptosis in tumor cells. This necroptotic process resulted in the release of immune-stimulating factors, which enhance antigen presentation, activate immune responses and promote T-cell-mediated antitumor activity [62,63]. Concurrently, the downregulation of ARH2 expression via siARH2 inhibited M2 macrophage polarization, leading to a reduction in M2 macrophage infiltration and an increase in M1 macrophages within the TME. The shift from M2 phenotype to the M1 macrophage phenotype reactivated tumor immune responses, augmented lymphocyte infiltration and facilitated the effective elimination of tumor cells [64,65]. We observed that the nanosystem delivering siARH2 alone showed limited antitumor efficacy, likely because the tumor cells failed to release sufficient tumor antigens to activate immune responses. ART induces necroptosis in tumor cells, leading to the release of tumor-associated antigens and pro-inflammatory factors. During this process, downregulation of ARH2 expression enhances macrophage-mediated immune responses, thereby significantly improving antitumor efficacy. Additionally, the incorporation of PD-L1-targeted peptides into the nanosystem enhances tumor-specific targeting while simultaneously blocking PD-L1, thus further amplifying antitumor immune responses [30,66]. The synthesized nanosystem demonstrated the ability to suppress tumor growth, inhibit metastatic lesion formation and enhance anti-tumor immune responses.
