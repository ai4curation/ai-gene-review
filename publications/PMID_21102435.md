    Nat Immunol. 2011 Jan;12(1):11-2. doi: 10.1038/ni0111-11.

The poly(ADP-ribose) polymerases (PARPs) participate in many biological and
pathological processes. Here we report that the PARP-13 shorter isoform (ZAPS),
rather than the full-length protein (ZAP), was selectively induced by
5'-triphosphate-modified RNA (3pRNA) and functioned as a potent stimulator of
interferon responses in human cells mediated by the RNA helicase RIG-I. ZAPS
associated with RIG-I to promote the oligomerization and ATPase activity of
RIG-I, which led to robust activation of IRF3 and NF-κB transcription factors.
Disruption of the gene encoding ZAPS resulted in impaired induction of
interferon-α (IFN-α), IFN-β and other cytokines after viral infection. These
results indicate that ZAPS is a key regulator of RIG-I signaling during the
innate antiviral immune response, which suggests its possible use as a
therapeutic target for viral control.
