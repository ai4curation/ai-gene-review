# The spermatogenic Ig superfamily/synaptic cell adhesion molecule mast-cell adhesion molecule promotes interaction with nerves.
**Authors:** Furuno T, Ito A, Koma Y, Watabe K, Yokozaki H, Bienenstock J, Nakanishi M, Kitamura Y
**Journal:** J Immunol (2005)
**DOI:** [10.4049/jimmunol.174.11.6934](https://doi.org/10.4049/jimmunol.174.11.6934)

## Abstract

1. J Immunol. 2005 Jun 1;174(11):6934-42. doi: 10.4049/jimmunol.174.11.6934.

The spermatogenic Ig superfamily/synaptic cell adhesion molecule mast-cell
adhesion molecule promotes interaction with nerves.

Furuno T(1), Ito A, Koma Y, Watabe K, Yokozaki H, Bienenstock J, Nakanishi M,
Kitamura Y.

Author information:
(1)Graduate School of Pharmaceutical Sciences, Nagoya City University, Japan.

Nerve-mast cell interaction is involved in both homeostatic and pathologic
regulations. The molecules that sustain this association have not been
identified. Because synaptic cell adhesion molecule (SynCAM), alternatively
named spermatogenic Ig superfamily (SgIGSF), is expressed on both nerves and
mast cells and because it binds homophilically, this molecule may be a
candidate. To examine this possibility, mast cells with or without SgIGSF/SynCAM
were cocultured with superior cervical ganglion neurons that express
SgIGSF/SynCAM, and the number of mast cells attached to neurites was counted.
The attachment of mast cells with SgIGSF/SynCAM, i.e., bone marrow-derived mast
cells (BMMC) from wild-type mice, was inhibited dose-dependently by blocking Ab
to SgIGSF/SynCAM. Mast cells without SgIGSF/SynCAM, i.e., BMMC from
microphthalmia transcription factor-deficient mice and BMMC-derived cell line
IC-2 cells, were defective in attachment to neurite, and transfection with
SgIGSF/SynCAM normalized this. When the nerves were specifically activated by
scorpion venom, one-quarter of the attached IC-2 cells mobilized Ca(2+) after a
few dozen seconds, and ectopic SgIGSF/SynCAM doubled this proportion. At points
of contact between neurites and wild-type BMMC, SgIGSF/SynCAM was locally
concentrated in both neurites and BMMC. SgIGSF/SynCAM on mast cells appeared to
predominantly mediate attachment and promote communication with nerves.
