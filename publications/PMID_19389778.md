# The MrpA, MrpB and MrpD subunits of the Mrp antiporter complex in Bacillus subtilis contain membrane-embedded and essential acidic residues.
**Authors:** Kajiyama Y, Otagiri M, Sekiguchi J, Kudo T, Kosono S
**Journal:** Microbiology (Reading) (2009)
**DOI:** [10.1099/mic.0.025205-0](https://doi.org/10.1099/mic.0.025205-0)

## Abstract

1. Microbiology (Reading). 2009 Jul;155(Pt 7):2137-2147. doi:
10.1099/mic.0.025205-0. Epub 2009 Apr 23.

The MrpA, MrpB and MrpD subunits of the Mrp antiporter complex in Bacillus
subtilis contain membrane-embedded and essential acidic residues.

Kajiyama Y(1)(2), Otagiri M(2), Sekiguchi J(1), Kudo T(3)(2), Kosono S(2).

Author information:
(1)Department of Applied Biology, Shinshu University, Japan.
(2)Environmental Molecular Biology Laboratory, RIKEN, Japan.
(3)Graduate School of Integrated Science, Yokohama City University, Japan.

Bacillus subtilis Mrp is a unique Na+/H+ antiporter with a multicomponent
structure consisting of the mrpABCDEFG gene products. We have previously
reported that the conserved and putative membrane-embedded Glu-113, Glu-657,
Asp-743 and Glu-747 of MrpA (ShaA) are essential for the transport function. In
this study, we further investigated the functional involvement of the equivalent
conserved acidic residues of other Mrp proteins in heterologous Escherichia coli
and natural B. subtilis backgrounds. Asp-121 of MrpB and Glu-137 of MrpD were
additionally identified to be essential for the transport function in both
systems. Glu-137 of MrpD and Glu-113 of MrpA were found to be conserved in the
homologous MrpD/MrpA proteins as well as in the homologous subunits of
H+-translocating primary active transporters such as Nuo and Mbh, suggesting
their critical role in ion binding. The remaining essential acidic residues
clustered in the C-terminal domain of MrpA (Glu-657, Asp-743 and Glu-747) and
MrpB (Asp-121); these subunits are fused in some Gram-negative species. It is
possible that the MrpA, MrpB and MrpD subunits, which contain essential
transmembrane acidic residues, form the ion translocation site(s) of the Mrp
antiporter complex.
