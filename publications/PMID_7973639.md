# The function of KGF in morphogenesis of epithelium and reepithelialization of wounds.
**Authors:** Werner S, Smola H, Liao X, Longaker MT, Krieg T, Hofschneider PH, Williams LT
**Journal:** Science (1994)
**DOI:** [10.1126/science.7973639](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.7973639)

## Abstract

1. Science. 1994 Nov 4;266(5186):819-22. doi: 10.1126/science.7973639.

The function of KGF in morphogenesis of epithelium and reepithelialization of
wounds.

Werner S(1), Smola H, Liao X, Longaker MT, Krieg T, Hofschneider PH, Williams
LT.

Author information:
(1)Cardiovascular Research Institute, University of California at San Francisco
(UCSF) 94143-0130.

The function of keratinocyte growth factor (KGF) in normal and wounded skin was
assessed by expression of a dominant-negative KGF receptor transgene in basal
keratinocytes. The skin of transgenic mice was characterized by epidermal
atrophy, abnormalities in the hair follicles, and dermal hyperthickening. Upon
skin injury, inhibition of KGF receptor signaling reduced the proliferation rate
of epidermal keratinocytes at the wound edge, resulting in substantially delayed
reepithelialization of the wound.
