# Fukutin-related protein localizes to the Golgi apparatus and mutations lead to mislocalization in muscle in vivo.

Mutations in the fukutin-related protein gene (FKRP) are associated with a
spectrum of diseases from mild limb-girdle muscular dystrophy type 2I to severe
congenital muscular dystrophy type 1C, muscle-eye-brain disease (MEB), and
Walker-Warburg syndrome (WWS). The effect of mutations on the transportation of
the mutant proteins may constitute the underlying mechanisms for the
pathogenesis of these diseases. Here we examined the subcellular localization of
mouse and human normal and mutant FKRP proteins in cells and in muscle in vivo.
Both normal human and mouse FKRPs localize in part of the Golgi apparatus in
muscle fibers. Mutations in the FKRP gene invariably altered the localization of
the protein, leading to endoplasmic reticulum retention within cells and
diminished Golgi localization in muscle fibers. Our results therefore suggest
that an individual missense point mutation can confer at least two independent
effects on the protein, causing (1) reduction or loss of the presumed
glycosyltransferase activity directly and (2) mislocalization that could further
alter the function of the protein. The complexity of the effect of individual
missense point mutations may partly explain the wide variation of the
FKRP-related myopathies.
