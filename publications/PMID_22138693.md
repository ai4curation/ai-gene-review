## Abstract

1. Nat Genet. 2011 Dec 4;44(1):23-31. doi: 10.1038/ng.1009.

Dnmt3a is essential for hematopoietic stem cell differentiation.

Challen GA(1), Sun D, Jeong M, Luo M, Jelinek J, Berg JS, Bock C, Vasanthakumar
A, Gu H, Xi Y, Liang S, Lu Y, Darlington GJ, Meissner A, Issa JP, Godley LA, Li
W, Goodell MA.

Author information:
(1)Stem Cells and Regenerative Medicine Center, Baylor College of Medicine,
Houston, Texas, USA.

Loss of the de novo DNA methyltransferases Dnmt3a and Dnmt3b in embryonic stem
cells obstructs differentiation; however, the role of these enzymes in somatic
stem cells is largely unknown. Using conditional ablation, we show that Dnmt3a
loss progressively impairs hematopoietic stem cell (HSC) differentiation over
serial transplantation, while simultaneously expanding HSC numbers in the bone
marrow. Dnmt3a-null HSCs show both increased and decreased methylation at
distinct loci, including substantial CpG island hypermethylation. Dnmt3a-null
HSCs upregulate HSC multipotency genes and downregulate differentiation factors,
and their progeny exhibit global hypomethylation and incomplete repression of
HSC-specific genes. These data establish Dnmt3a as a critical participant in the
epigenetic silencing of HSC regulatory genes, thereby enabling efficient
differentiation.
