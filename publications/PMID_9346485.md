Activation of the transcription factor nuclear factor kappa B (NF-kappaB) by
inflammatory cytokines requires the successive action of NF-kappaB-inducing
kinase (NIK) and IkappaB kinase-alpha (IKK-alpha). A widely expressed protein
kinase was identified that is 52 percent identical to IKK-alpha. IkappaB
kinase-beta (IKK-beta) activated NF-kappaB when overexpressed and phosphorylated
serine residues 32 and 36 of IkappaB-alpha and serines 19 and 23 of
IkappaB-beta. The activity of IKK-beta was stimulated by tumor necrosis factor
and interleukin-1 treatment. IKK-alpha and IKK-beta formed heterodimers that
interacted with NIK. Overexpression of a catalytically inactive form of IKK-beta
blocked cytokine-induced NF-kappaB activation. Thus, an active IkappaB kinase
complex may require three distinct protein kinases.
