β-casomorphins (BCMs) are bioactive peptides generated from milk β-casein
digestion, which interact with opioid and other receptors to affect both tumor
cell proliferation and immune responses within the tumor microenvironment. This
review is aimed at summarizing current evidence on the anti-proliferative and
immunomodulatory activities of BCMs, in particular BCM-5 and BCM-7, and their
relevance to cancer biology. Studies have shown that BCM-5 primarily inhibits
cancer cell growth, whereas BCM-7, in addition, enhances anti-tumor immunity by
promoting CD8+ T cell infiltration and reducing regulatory T cell populations.
The review also discusses synthetic cyclic analogs designed to improve peptide
stability and bioavailability, which demonstrate opioid-independent
antiproliferative activities. By integration of mechanistic, in vitro, and in
vivo data, this review emphasizes the potential of β-casomorphins and their
derivatives as modulators of tumor progression and immune surveillance and
identifies gaps in knowledge to guide future research in oncology.
