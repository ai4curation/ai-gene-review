MicroRNAs (miRNAs) are single-stranded small non-coding RNAs, generally 18-25
nucleotides in length, that act as repressors of gene expression. miRNAs are
encoded by independent genes or processed from a variety of different RNA
species. So far, there is no evidence showing that the ribosomal DNA-hosted
microRNA is implicated in vertebrate development. Currently, we found a highly
expressed small RNA hosted in ribosomal DNA was predicted as a novel miRNA,
named miR-ntu1, in zebrafish endothelial cells by deep sequencing analysis. The
miRNA was validated by custom-designed Taqman PCR, Northern Blot, and in silico
analysis. Furthermore, we demonstrated that miR-ntu1 played a crucial role in
zebrafish angiogenesis via modulation of Notch signaling. Our findings provide a
notable case that a miRNA hosted in ribosomal DNA is involved in vertebrate
development.
