# The human CD46 molecule is a receptor for measles virus (Edmonston strain).
**Authors:** Dörig RE, Marcil A, Chopra A, Richardson CD
**Journal:** Cell (1993)
**DOI:** [10.1016/0092-8674(93)80071-l](https://doi.org/10.1016/0092-8674(93)80071-l)

## Abstract

1. Cell. 1993 Oct 22;75(2):295-305. doi: 10.1016/0092-8674(93)80071-l.

The human CD46 molecule is a receptor for measles virus (Edmonston strain).

Dörig RE(1), Marcil A, Chopra A, Richardson CD.

Author information:
(1)Biotechnology Research Institute, National Research Council of Canada,
Montréal, Québec.

Measles virus normally causes disease in human beings, and the host range of
this virus may be determined by a specific receptor on the surface of primate
cells. Human-rodent somatic cell hybrids were tested for their ability to bind
measles virus, and only cells that contained human chromosome 1 were capable of
binding virus. A study of lymphocyte markers suggested that the complement
regulator known either as membrane cofactor protein or CD46 was the measles
virus receptor. We proved this hypothesis by demonstrating that hamster cell
lines that expressed human CD46 could subsequently bind virus. Furthermore,
infected CD46+ cells produced syncytia and viral proteins. Finally, polyclonal
antisera against CD46 inhibited virus binding and infection. These results prove
that human CD46 permits cells both to bind measles virus and to support
infection.
