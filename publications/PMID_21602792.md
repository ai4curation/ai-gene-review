Mechanisms of signal transduction regulation remain a fundamental question in a
variety of biological processes and diseases. Previous evidence indicates that
the primary cilium can act as a signalling hub, but its exact role in many of
the described pathways has remained elusive. Here, we investigate the mechanism
of cilia-mediated regulation of the canonical Wnt pathway. We found that primary
cilia dampen canonical Wnt signalling through a spatial mechanism involving
compartmentalization of signalling components. The cilium, through regulated
intraflagellar transport, diverts Jouberin (Jbn), a ciliopathy protein and
context-specific Wnt pathway regulator, away from the nucleus and limits
β-catenin nuclear entry. This repressive regulation does not silence the
pathway, but instead maintains a discrete range of Wnt responsiveness; cells
without cilia have potentiated Wnt responses, whereas cells with multiple cilia
have inhibited responses. Furthermore, we show that this regulation occurs
during embryonic development and is disrupted in cancer cell proliferation.
Together these data explain a spatial mechanism of Wnt signalling regulation
that may provide insight into ciliary regulation of other signalling pathways.
