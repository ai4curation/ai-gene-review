Neovascular inflammatory vitreoretinopathy (NIV) is a rare eye disease that
ultimately leads to complete blindness and is caused by mutations in the gene
encoding calpain-5 (CAPN5), with six pathogenic mutations identified. In
transfected SH-SY5Y cells, five of the mutations resulted in decreased membrane
association, diminished S-acylation, and reduced calcium-induced autoproteolysis
of CAPN5. CAPN5 proteolysis of the autoimmune regulator AIRE was impacted by
several NIV mutations. R243, L244, K250 and the adjacent V249 are on β-strands
in the protease core 2 domain. Conformational changes induced by Ca2+binding
result in these β-strands forming a β-sheet and a hydrophobic pocket which docks
W286 side chain away from the catalytic cleft, enabling calpain activation based
on comparison with the Ca2+-bound CAPN1 protease core. The pathologic variants
R243L, L244P, K250N, and R289W are predicted to disrupt the β-strands, β-sheet,
and hydrophobic pocket, impairing calpain activation. The mechanism by which
these variants impair membrane association is unclear. G376S impacts a conserved
residue in the CBSW domain and is predicted to disrupt a loop containing acidic
residues which may contribute to membrane binding. G267S did not impair membrane
association and resulted in a slight but significant increase in autoproteolytic
and proteolytic activity. However, G267S is also identified in individuals
without NIV. Combined with the autosomal dominant pattern of NIV inheritance and
evidence that CAPN5 may dimerize, the results are consistent with a dominant
negative mechanism for the five pathogenic variants which resulted in impaired
CAPN5 activity and membrane association and a gain-of-function for the G267S
variant.

Introduction

Neovascular inflammatory vitreoretinopathy (NIV) is a rare, progressive, inflammatory autoimmune eye disease that ultimately leads to complete blindness [1, 2]. NIV has clinical features that mimic several common eye diseases including autoimmune eye disease and retinitis pigmentosa, especially in the early stages of NIV [3]. Current treatment options for NIV are limited and only slow disease progression [4, 5]. The autosomal dominant inheritance pattern of NIV suggests that the underlying mutations result in either a gain-of-function or dominant negative effect [6]. Determination of the mechanisms underlying the pathogenesis of NIV is critical to the development of improved treatments for this rare but devastating eye disorder.

NIV is caused by pathogenic variants in the calpain-5/CAPN5 gene [3, 7–10] (Table 1). Calpains are a family of calcium-dependent, non-lysosomal thiol proteases that regulate biological processes by either targeting substrate proteins for degradation or modifying the activity of the proteolytic products [11–13]. CAPN5 contains the protease core domains PC1 and PC2 along with the calpain beta sandwich (CBSW) domain typical of the calpain family, but contains a C-terminal C2 domain instead of the penta EF-hand domain present in classical calpains [14]. CAPN5 is therefore classified as a non-classical calpain. Critical residues involved in the catalytic triad and in activation following Ca2+ binding to the protease core domains are conserved in CAPN5 [14, 15] (Supplemental Fig. 1A). The crystal structure of the protease core domain of calcium-free (inactive) CAPN5 has been determined (PDB ID 6P3Q [16]) but is not available for calcium-bound protease cores or full-length CAPN5. A full-length predicted structure is available from AlphaFold2 [17, 18] (AF-O15484-F1-model_v4.pdb).

Four pathogenic missense NIV-causing CAPN5 mutations are identified in the ClinVar database [23, 24]: R243L, L244P, K250N, and R289W (Table 1). Each has been identified in multiple individuals. Two additional NIV-causing CAPN5 missense mutations have been reported, G267S and G376S [8], although they are not currently indicated as pathogenic in ClinVar. Both were identified in single individuals but not in additional family members [8]. G267S and G376S are also included in Genome Aggregation Database (GnomAD) v2.11 along with other missense mutations to residues impacted by the pathogenic mutations (Table 2). GnomAD incorporates data from large-scale exome and genome sequencing studies and includes variants that may be common in the healthy population [25, 26].

Each of the NIV mutations is localized within or adjacent to a surface exposed loop structure [7, 9, 16, 27] (Fig. 1) In the PC2 domain, R243L, L244P, and K250N are within a loop (237–250) referred to as gating loop G1 based on the proposal that it may limit substrate access to the catalytic cleft [7, 28] [27]. Within this loop, R243 is on a short β9 strand and K250 is on a β10 strand, based on the CAPN5 PC crystal structure (6p3q.pdb) and comparison with CAPN1 [29]. G267S is on a helical region of a long loop in the PC2 domain, the PC2L2 loop (265–274) [16]. R289W is within a third loop, gating loop G2 (E288-E300), which contains two acidic residues which help coordinate Ca2+ binding, E290 and D297 [9]. G376S is located on a long loop between β strands 1 and 2 of the CBSW domain based on the AlphaFold2 predicted structural model. The G1 loop is adjacent to H252 of the catalytic triad, and the G2 gating loop is adjacent to N284 of the catalytic triad as well as W286, whose side chain is situated between the PC1 and PC2 domains and blocks the catalytic site in the inactive state [9, 15, 29].

To investigate effects of select pathogenic mutations in the CAPN5 PC2 domain, mini-calpain models consisting of protease core domains of CAPN5, or of CAPN1 with CAPN5 residues inserted, have been utilized for the variants R243L, R289W, and G267S [9, 27, 30]. The results obtained with these mini-calpain models suggest that NIV-related CAPN5 mutations result in a reduced calcium threshold for CAPN5 activation, a gain-of-function, proposed to be the result of mutation-induced alterations in the gating loops [7, 9, 16, 27, 31]. The influence of other pathogenic variants on CAPN5 activity has not been investigated previously.

CAPN5 is predominantly localized to the plasma membrane [3, 32, 33] and is thought to initially associate with membranes via acidic residues in its C-terminal C2 domain [32]. This membrane association is further stabilized by S-acylation at up to three cysteine residues [33]. CAPN5 with R243L or L244P mutations exhibits decreased localization at the plasma membrane with a more cytoplasmic localization [3]. Experimental mutations which interfere with membrane association or S-acylation result in decreased CAPN5 activity [32, 33]. Based on these observations, mutations that reduce membrane association would be predicted to result in decreased CAPN5 activity. Accordingly, we sought to determine if other pathogenic NIV mutations alter the membrane association, and if the mutations also influence S-acylation and activity of CAPN5.

DiscussionIn SH-SY5Y neuroblastoma cells transfected with full-length CAPN5 with a C-terminal 3×FLAG tag, five of the six reported NIV-causing CAPN5 variants (R243L, L244P, K250N, R289W, and G376S) resulted in a profound impairment of membrane association, S-acylation, and calcium-induced CAPN5 autoproteolysis. The exception was G267S which exhibited similar membrane localization as well as a modest but significant increase in calcium-induced autoproteolysis relative to WT CAPN5. These results, combined with the autosomal dominant pattern of NIV inheritance, are consistent with a dominant-negative mechanism for five NIV-causing variants and a gain-of-function for G267S.Evaluation of CAPN5 activity was largely based on autoproteolysis. The relationship between autoproteolysis and calpain activity has been debated for classical calpains with more recent studies indicating that autoproteolysis occurs subsequent to enzyme activation [15, 44]. In classical calpains, autoproteolysis occurs at both the N-terminal anchor peptide and C-terminal CBSW and penta-EF-hand domains in the large subunit [44]. CAPN5 lacks the N-terminal anchor peptide [14] and CAPN5 autoproteolysis appears to occur in the C-terminal CBSW and C2 domains, with the resultant C-terminal fragments being rapidly degraded [32]. These domains are remote from the catalytic cleft and autoproteolysis is likely the result of intermolecular cleavage by other CAPN5 molecules, rather than intramolecular cleavage [44], with CAPN5 serving as a CAPN5 substrate. Although the CBSW domain is vulnerable to other proteases [45], autoproteolytic fragments of CAPN5 are not observed with the catalytically dead C81A mutant confirming that the breakdown products observed are the result of autoproteolysis [32]. To address concerns that autoproteolysis may not accurately reflect CAPN5 activity, we also examined the proteolysis of a putative CAPN5 substrate, AIRE, identified through a proteomic analysis (Gal, Geddes, Croall et al., manuscript in preparation). The pattern of in vitro AIRE proteolysis by WT CAPN5, C81A, R243L, G267S, and R289W paralleled CAPN5 autoproteolysis, supporting that autoproteolysis results are indicative of CAPN5 activity.The reduced S-acylation observed with most pathogenic variants is most likely the consequence of impaired membrane association. According to the consensus “kinetic trapping” model of S-acylation, the interaction of a soluble protein with the DHHC acyltransferases, themselves integral membrane proteins, requires another initial mode of membrane association [33]. For the five pathogenic CAPN5 variants with reduced membrane association, the decrease in the intensity of all upshifted bands rather than reduction of a single band following acyl-PEG exchange suggests that impairment of membrane association is the cause of the reduced S-acylation.Proposed mechanisms underlying impaired CAPN5 activity.The results demonstrate both decreased activity and impaired membrane association with five pathogenic CAPN5 variants, and increased activity without impaired membrane association for G267S. We sought to identify possible mechanisms by which the mutations would influence CAPN5 activation. Although the Ca2+-bound CAPN5 structure is not available, the similarity of the protease core domains to those of other calpains in the Ca2+ unbound state [16] and the availability of Ca2+-bound structures for CAPN1, CAPN2, and CAPN9 [29, 46, 47], enables prediction of the conformational changes in CAPN5 during activation.The mechanism of the Ca2+-induced activation of calpains was first characterized using protease cores of CAPN1 [29]. In the inactive conformation of rat CAPN1 protease core, the residues of the catalytic triad (C115, H272, N296) are too far apart to facilitate proton transfer. Also, the catalytic site is blocked by the W298 side chain. The protease core binds two Ca2+ ions, one in each of the PC1 and PC2 domains. Ca2+ binding triggers a conformational shift, bringing the residues of the catalytic triad closer together. The conformational shift also results in strands β9 (261–263 EAI) and β10 (269–271 VRG) in the PC2 domain coming together to form a β-sheet and creates a hydrophobic pocket composed of I263, V269, and V301. This pocket interacts with the non-polar region of the indole ring of W298 which also H-bonds to E261. The result is that the W298 side chain swings away from its position between the PC1 and PC2 domains, enabling the two domains to come together to form a catalytically competent active site [29].Conserved residues in CAPN5 include catalytic triad residues C81, H252, N284; acidic residues coordinating Ca2+ binding in the PC1 and PC2 domain, W286 located between the PC1 and PC2 domains in the inactive conformation, and E241 which is assumed to H-bond with W286 in the active conformation (Supplemental Fig. 1A, [14–16]). The residues equivalent to CAPN1 strand β9 are EAR 241–243 and to β10 are VKG 249–251 (Supplemental Fig 1A). These residues are in β-strands in the crystal structure of the inactive CAPN5 protease core (PDB ID 6P3Q [16]).R243 and R289 are basic residues in contrast to the aliphatic hydrophobic nature of the corresponding residues I263 and V301 in rat CAPN1. CAPNs 3, 9, and 12 also have an arginine at the position equivalent to R243 in CAPN5 [16] (Supplemental Fig 1A). Arginine residues are amphipathic, the side chain closest to the backbone is hydrophobic while the end of the side chain is positively charged [48]. Hydrophobic regions of the two R side chains could readily contribute to a hydrophobic pocket, along with the conserved V249. This suggests that similar to CAPN1, CAPN5 activation results in a hydrophobic pocket formed by the R243, V249 and R289, which docks the W286 aromatic side chain following its swing from the catalytic cleft during activation.It is striking that four of the pathogenic variants impact residues in the β-strands and hydrophobic pocket essential to calpain activation. R243 is in the β9 strand, L244 is immediately adjacent and may also be part of the β9 stand which appears to extend from A242 to A245 in the crystal structure 6P3Q. K250 is in the β10 strand. R289 is on a separate loop and also contributes to the hydrophobic pocket (Fig. 1, Supplemental Fig 1A). We therefore sought to evaluate whether the pathogenic variants would disrupt the β-strands, β-sheet, and hydrophobic pocket to which these residues contribute.For R243L, the substituted hydrophobic leucine would facilitate formation of the hydrophobic pocket, similar to isoleucine in CAPN1, but may disrupt the β-strand as leucines prefer α-helices over β-strands [49]. R243L would result in an ALLA sequence for A242-A245 which could form an α-helix instead of a β-strand, similar to the α-helix at or near LLA 268–270.GnomAD contains two additional R243 variants, R243H and R243C, which are not linked to retinal disorders. Cysteine is considered hydrophobic on the Kyte and Doolittle scale [50], but closer to neutral in other hydrophobicity scales such as Eisenberg [51] and can serve as a polar or non-polar residue depending on the environment [52]. Histidine can function as a basic residue, similar to arginine, but is often found in buried locations similar to hydrophobic residues [48, 53]. Both the cysteine and histidine substitutions are apparently less detrimental than the non-polar hydrophobic leucine substitution, and do not cause retinal disorders.The L244 side chain packs against the aliphatic portion of the K250 side chain as well as I232 and L248 but is exposed to solvent on one side. Mutation to proline, a smaller heterocyclic and more polar residue, would expose the hydrophobic core on the non-solvent side of L244 and could cause rearrangement and reduce the flexibility of the loop. Proline is disruptive to β-sheet formation and would likely introduce a turn in the β9 strand [54], which already has tight turns at A245 and C246 based on the crystal structure. This would disrupt both the β9 strand and β-sheet formed from the β9 and β10 strands during activation.K250 is on the β10 strand, as is V249 which contributes to the hydrophobic pocket, and is nearby H252 of the catalytic triad. K250 side chains point out towards solvent and make extensive van der Waals contacts and H-bonds with D239 [7]. K250 main chain also forms H-bonds with the backbone of E241 and A242.The substitution of the uncharged but polar asparagine for a basic lysine is generally considered to be neutral for intracellular proteins and favored for membrane proteins [48]. However, asparagine residues often cause turns in β-sheets, and when followed by glycine introduce a bulge in the secondary structure [55]. Thus, the K250N mutation likely disrupts the β10-strand, and impairs formation of the β sheet by β9 and β10 strands during CAPN5 activation.R289 is in an extended open coil region, referred to as the G2 gating loop [9], and is nearby N284 of the catalytic triad [9]. The adjacent E290 likely coordinates Ca2+ binding, based on conserved acidic residues in CAPN1, along with D297 on the same loop and D320 on a separate loop. R289 makes salt bridges with E288 and D297 and H-bonds with the main chain carbonyl of W291 and D320 in the crystal structure. The tryptophan substitution for arginine in R289W replaces a basic residue with an aromatic hydrophobic residue, which may support formation of the hydrophobic pocket with R243 and V249, although substitution to tryptophan is predicted to disrupt the ionic and electrostatic interactions and cause clashes because of the bulky side chain. Previously, it was proposed that W289 may stack with other nearby tryptophan residues (W286, W291, W295, W302) and stabilize the wedge tryptophan W286 in the open conformation resulting in enhanced activity even in the absence of added calcium [9]. However, we observed reduced vs. enhanced activity of R289W. The contribution of R289 to the hydrophobic pocket stabilizing W286 during activation and the interaction of R289 with presumed Ca2+ binding residues D297 and D320 suggests that the disruption to these interactions caused by R289W may be very deleterious, contributing to the earlier onset and greater clinical severity associated with this variant.The association of G267S and G376S with NIV is not as well established as the other pathogenic variants, as they have only been identified in single individuals and are also in the GnomAD database which includes exome and genome sequences from healthy individuals. Another G376 variant, G376V, was also identified in GnomAD but is not included in ClinVar and does not appear to be associated with retinal disorders. In contrast to the other four NIV-causing CAPN5 mutations, G267S and G376S are not associated with the β9 and β10 strands or formation of the hydrophobic pocket.G267 is in the PC2 domain at the outer edge of a long loop which is followed by a short helical segment, 269–272, based on secondary structure prediction algorithms [56, 57] and 268–270 in the AlphaFold2 model although with low confidence. Converting to a serine that is slightly more hydrophilic and energetically more favorable in a helix could cause that helical turn to begin earlier. However, it does not appear that G267S would impact β9 and β10 strands or the hydrophobic pocket.G376 is part of a large, twisted loop in the CBSW domain. It is a highly conserved residue at the end of a tri-glycine sequence which would be highly flexible. In contrast to G267 which is surface exposed, G376 is buried and the mutation to serine would be more disruptive due to steric clashes. The backbone angles at G376 in the AlphaFold model (Φ=76°, Ψ=178°) are poor angles for any non-glycine residue, suggesting that the serine substitution would result in a conformational change and disruption to the loop structure. Although the CBSW domain is present in classical calpains and most non-classical calpains [13, 58, 59], its function and role in calpain activation have not been determined.Possible mechanisms underlying the decreased membrane association.CAPN5 is predominantly localized to the plasma membrane and is also present in the cytosol and subcellular organelles including the nucleus and mitochondria [3, 32, 33, 60–62]. The membrane association of CAPN5 is facilitated by the C2 domain and stabilized by S-acylation of up to three cysteine residues (C4, C507, C512) [32, 33].None of the residues impacted by pathogenic CAPN5 variants were previously implicated in membrane association of CAPN5. However, immediately following G267 are five hydrophobic residues (LLAFF) which form an α-helix, are part of an inserted sequence unique to CAPN5 and CAPN6 [16], and are predicted to be membrane inserted. As discussed above in relation to activity, G267S may extend the α-helix formed by these residues which could enhance membrane association. The residues predicted to be membrane inserted are on the same face of CAPN5 as residues previously shown to contribute to membrane association.The localization of four pathogenic mutations to β-strands and a hydrophobic pocket critical to Ca2+-induced activation provides a mechanism by which these mutations could lead to impaired CAPN5 activity. However, impaired activity is unlikely to be responsible for the reduced membrane association as CAPN5 is associated with the plasma membrane in the absence of added Ca2+, as is catalytically dead CAPN5-C81A [32]. Three basic residues are impacted by the NIV-causing mutations, R243, K250, and R289. These basic residues are clustered together on the surface of the PC2 domain. The polar nature of these residues is largely conserved in calpains with a CysPC-CBSW-C2 domain structure [63] with the exception of the calpain-like protein from Trichoplax adhaerens (Uniprot ID B3RIE5) (Suppl. Fig. 1B). In classical calpains the residues corresponding to R243 and R289 are often non-polar hydrophobic residues (Suppl. Fig. 1A). The conservation of the basic residues in calpains with C2 domains and the finding that mutations which substitute a non-basic residue result in a loss of membrane association suggests that this basic cluster is involved in membrane binding.One possible mechanism is that the basic residues bind to negatively charged phospholipids, including phosphoinositides and phosphatidyl serine, on the plasma membrane. Clusters of basic residues are often found on the cytoplasmic face of integral membrane proteins and on cytoplasmic proteins that bind transiently to membranes, such as protein kinase Cα [64]. The interaction between basic or cationic clusters and membrane phospholipids has been documented and extensively studied for numerous proteins [65–69]. Arguing against this is that basic clusters are typically four or more residues and do not contain acidic residues, with the exception of C2 domains. Adjacent to R289 are acidic residues E288 and E290. However, E290 likely contributes to Ca2+ binding and the bound Ca2+ would neutralize the effect of the acidic residue. The R243-K250-R289 cluster is on a face distal from residues predicted to contribute to the plasma membrane association of CAPN5 and is therefore unlikely to also contribute, although it could participate in binding to membranes of other organelles.A second possible explanation for the impaired membrane association is disruption of protein-protein interactions. Arginine residues are often involved in salt bridges with negatively charged amino acids which can stabilize protein structures or facilitate protein-protein interactions. In synaptotagmin 1 basic residues on the concave side of the C2B-domain interact with acidic residues in membrane associated proteins syntaxin-1 and SNAP-25 [70]. Another example is calmodulin, in which acidic residues interact with polybasic regions of membrane-associated target proteins [71].A third possibility is that the basic cluster is important in the self-association of CAPN5 into dimers or multimers. Once again using synaptotagmin 1 as an example, it self-associates into ring-like multimers in the presence of anionic phospholipids [72]. Mutations that neutralize lysine residues abolish the self-association and also impair activity when synaptotagmin 1 mutants are expressed in neurons [72]. A second example is focal adhesion kinase for which binding to PI(4,5)P2 in the membrane occurs via a basic patch and triggers oligomerization, also via the basic patch, and induces conformational changes leading to activation [73]. In this example, the basic cluster is involved in membrane binding, enzyme self-association, and enzyme activation.The G376S mutation in the CBSW domain also resulted in impaired membrane association and reduced CAPN5 activity. The CBSW domain was previously referred to as a C2-like domain, as it contains 8 anti-parallel β strands similar to C2 domains although with a different topology [15, 74]. The CBSW domain of CAPN2 was hypothesized to participate in membrane binding based on the pattern of acidic and basic residues similar to those in C2 domains [43, 74], to bind phospholipids in a Ca2+-dependent manner [75] and to be responsible for the membrane localization of calpain-2 via binding to phosphoinositides [76, 77]. However, the crystal structure of Ca2+-bound calpain-2 did not support Ca2+ binding to this domain [47]. An intriguing feature of G376-containing loop in CAPN5 is the inserted sequence of five residues unique to CAPNs 5 and 6 (367–371, EDPRQ for CAPN5), located on the outmost region of the loop. The two acidic residues from this insert, E367 and D368, combined with D382 resembles the triad of membrane binding acidic residues in the CAPN5 C2 domain [32]. Individual mutations of these acidic residues to their corresponding amide resulted in a decrease in plasma membrane localization, particularly for D368 and D382, supporting their importance in membrane association. In the AlphaFold2 model, the acidic residues in the CBSW and C2 domains point in opposite directions, suggesting that the G376-containing loop in the CBSW domain is unlikely to participate in the plasma membrane binding contributed by residues in the C2 domain, S-acylated residues, and other sites discussed previously. However, this orientation is similar to acidic residues in Ca2+ binding loops in the C2A and C2B domains in Synaptotagmin 1, which point in opposite directions and facilitate binding to both the plasma membrane and secretory vesicles [78].It is also possible that the pathogenic mutations indirectly influence membrane association by causing CAPN5 instability. CAPN5 is predicted to turn over rapidly unless protected by membrane association [32]. Reduced CAPN5 stability could result in an inability of the protein to be transported to reach the membrane. In support of this possible mechanism, the reduction in CAPN5 levels and activity for five pathogenic variants was most pronounced in the membrane fraction. It could also be argued that this reduction in CAPN5 levels at the membrane is the result of hyperactivity and excessive autoproteolysis. However, in vitro proteolytic and autoproteolytic activity of immunoprecipitated CAPN5 was impaired for pathologic NIV variants with the exception of G267S. This demonstrates that the observed reduction in CAPN5 activity is not simply a result of reduced levels, whether the reduced levels are the result of instability, hyperactivity, or some other mechanism.As discussed above, reduced activity is unlikely to be the cause of the impaired membrane association. However, impaired membrane association could contribute to the reduced activity as membrane association appears to be required for the full activity of CAPN5 and there are many examples of membrane association being required for enzyme activation, one example being protein kinase Cα [79, 80]. Additional studies are needed to identify the mechanism by which the five pathogenic variants result in impaired membrane association.Gain-of-function, loss-of-function, or dominant negative mechanism?The autosomal dominant pattern of NIV inheritance suggests either a gain-of-function or dominant-negative mechanism [6, 81]. Although some dominant mutations can cause loss-of-function via haploinsufficiency, CAPN5 gene variants resulting in a loss-of-function do not cause NIV [82]. Additionally, Capn5 heterozygous mice (C57BL/6J-Capn5tm1Dgen/J; Capn5+/LacZ) appear normal and CAPN5 protein levels are similar to WT mice [61]. The clustering of several of the mutations in 3D space further supports either a dominant-negative or gain-of-function mechanism [6, 83].Previous studies supported a gain-of-function mechanism for the R243L, G267S, and R289W variants [9, 16, 27]. For the R243L variant, an insert including the G1 gating loop from human CAPN5 (amino acids 233–247 KAVTAADMEARLACG) replaced the corresponding rat sequence (residues 253–267 NISDIRDLEAITFKN) in a recombinant CAPN1 protease core domain mini-CAPN1/5 hybrid model, with R243L simulated by replacing the R with L in the inserted sequence [28]. The R289W variant was simulated with a V301W mutation in rat mini-CAPN1, the residue/mutation equivalent to R289W in CAPN5 [9]. G267S was simulated by residues 260–275 (RKVRLGHGLLAFFKSE) of CAPN5 for residues 280–287 (KQVTYQGQ) of rat mini-CAPN1 [16], and was also modeled in recombinant CAPN5 protease core with an N-terminal maltose binding protein tag (MBP-rCAPN1/5-PC) [16]. In each of the above recombinant protease core models, synthetic peptide substrates were cleaved more rapidly in the models with the NIV mutant residue, as compared to WT residue, in the presence of up to 100 mM calcium [9, 16, 27]. The pathogenic mutations are proposed to alter the structure of the G1 and G2 gating loops, which may allow substrate access to the catalytic site in the absence of added calcium [7, 9, 16, 27].The small but significant increase in G267S autoproteolysis is consistent with the previously reported increase in activity of mini-CAPN5-G267S [16], and supports a gain-of-function mechanism. The decrease in autoproteolytic activity at the membrane for the other five pathologic variants (R243L, L244P, K250N, R289W, G376S) suggests either a loss-of-function or dominant-negative mechanism. This contrasts with the increased activity observed for mini CAPN1/5 hybrid models of R243L and R289W.One possible explanation for the contrasting results is that the substituted leucine in the G1 gating loop insert into CAPN1 is more similar to the corresponding CAPN1 I263 residue than is the R243 in CAPN5. Also, for R289W the substituted tryptophan is closer in hydrophobicity to the WT valine than is arginine at position 301 of rat CAPN1. Thus, it is reasonable that these substitutions result in increased activity in the mini CAPN1/5 hybrid models as the hydrophobic properties of the mutant residues are closer to the WT residues in CAPN1, but not the basic residues in CAPN5. Presumably there is an evolutionary preference for the arginine residues at the 243 and 289 positions of CAPN5 and hydrophobic residues at the equivalent positions of CAPN1 and CAPN2. It will be important to evaluate the effects of the R243L and R289W mutations in non-hybrid CAPN5 protease core models, as was done with G267S. Additionally, membrane association may be required for the full activity of CAPN5.In support of a dominant-negative mechanism, the co-immunoprecipitation results indicate that CAPN5 may exist as a dimer/multimer or possibly as part of a larger macromolecular complex. The results also indicate that pathologic variants R243L and R289W, but not G267S, may act in a dominant-negative manner. The formation of a dimer or multimer is consistent with the observation that dominant-negative mutations are often found at interface positions where they are typically associated with intermolecular interactions and assembly of proteins into homomeric and heteromeric complexes [6]. Classical calpains with penta-EF hand domains exist as homomeric and heteromeric dimers [84, 85]. Dimers or multimers have not been previously demonstrated for CAPN5 or other non-classical calpains. However, other C2 domain proteins have been shown to dimerize through their C2 domains, with the membrane associated proteins being mainly dimers [86–88]. This clustering has been proposed to be a universal feature of Ca2+−dependent membrane-binding proteins utilizing C2-domains [89].The early stages of NIV clinically resemble retinitis pigmentosa. Mutations in the rhodopsin gene are the most common cause of autosomal dominant retinitis pigmentosa (adRP) [90, 91]. The mechanisms by which mutant rod opsin leads to adRP include improper transport to the plasma membrane, protein misfolding, mutant rod opsin protein being retained in the ER where it triggers the unfolded protein response and ER stress, and protein dimerization and aggregation [92–96]. The magnitude of rhodopsin destabilization correlates with the amount of protein reaching the membrane and with disease severity [97, 98]. Although CAPN5 is not an integral membrane protein, it stably associates with membranes via S-acylation, appears to be a dimer or multimer, and most pathogenic variants result in reduced enzyme levels and decreased activity at the membrane.There are several caveats to these findings and their interpretation. It is recognized that any prediction of the impact of the pathogenic mutations on the activation mechanism is speculative in the absence of structures for WT and mutant Ca2+−bound CAPN5. It is possible that mutants may be more sensitive to experimental treatments such as permeabilization. It is also possible that the observed decrease in membrane association of the five pathogenic variants results from extreme hyperactivity and autoproteolysis at the membrane, but not at the cytosol. Additionally, the N1C1 anti-CAPN5 antibody used might miss autoproteolytic fragments specific to cytosolic CAPN5. Further studies are needed to further validate the dimerization of CAPN5 and dominant-negative interactions between WT and mutant variants. Calcium binding to residues in the PC2, CBSW, and C2 domains needs to be demonstrated, along with structural analysis of Ca2+-bound protease core or preferably full-length CAPN5.In conclusion, five of six CAPN5 variants reported to be causal for NIV result in reduced enzyme levels at the plasma membrane, decreased calcium-induced proteolytic and autoproteolytic activity, and a reduction in S-acylation when expressed in SH-SY5Y cells. An exception was the G267S variant which is adjacent to residues predicted to be membrane inserted and exhibited a slight but significant enhancement in calcium-induced autoproteolytic activity, consistent with previously reported hyperactivity and a gain-of-function mechanism [16]. However, the G267S variant, and possibly G376S, may not be causal for NIV based on their identification in individuals without NIV. Three of the pathogenic variants in the PC2 domain (R243, L244P, K250N) impact residues on β-strands which undergo conformational changes during activation. V249, adjacent to K250N, along with R243 and R289 form a hydrophobic pocket which docks W286 away from the catalytic site, enabling calpain activation. Each of these variants is predicted to be disruptive to the β-strand, β-sheet and/or hydrophobic pocket formed during activation. The G376S variant impacts a conserved glycine residue on a surface loop in the CBSW domain which contains basic and acidic residues. Mutation of the acidic residues to their corresponding amide reduced membrane association, although these residues are on a face of the enzyme opposite that of other residues implicated in membrane association. Co-immunoprecipitation studies suggest the possibility that CAPN5 exists as a dimer or multimer, consistent with other C2 domain proteins. The mechanisms responsible for the impaired membrane association of the five variants have not been fully identified. Together, the results are consistent with pathogenic variants, other than G267S, acting in a dominant-negative manner.

In SH-SY5Y neuroblastoma cells transfected with full-length CAPN5 with a C-terminal 3×FLAG tag, five of the six reported NIV-causing CAPN5 variants (R243L, L244P, K250N, R289W, and G376S) resulted in a profound impairment of membrane association, S-acylation, and calcium-induced CAPN5 autoproteolysis. The exception was G267S which exhibited similar membrane localization as well as a modest but significant increase in calcium-induced autoproteolysis relative to WT CAPN5. These results, combined with the autosomal dominant pattern of NIV inheritance, are consistent with a dominant-negative mechanism for five NIV-causing variants and a gain-of-function for G267S.

Evaluation of CAPN5 activity was largely based on autoproteolysis. The relationship between autoproteolysis and calpain activity has been debated for classical calpains with more recent studies indicating that autoproteolysis occurs subsequent to enzyme activation [15, 44]. In classical calpains, autoproteolysis occurs at both the N-terminal anchor peptide and C-terminal CBSW and penta-EF-hand domains in the large subunit [44]. CAPN5 lacks the N-terminal anchor peptide [14] and CAPN5 autoproteolysis appears to occur in the C-terminal CBSW and C2 domains, with the resultant C-terminal fragments being rapidly degraded [32]. These domains are remote from the catalytic cleft and autoproteolysis is likely the result of intermolecular cleavage by other CAPN5 molecules, rather than intramolecular cleavage [44], with CAPN5 serving as a CAPN5 substrate. Although the CBSW domain is vulnerable to other proteases [45], autoproteolytic fragments of CAPN5 are not observed with the catalytically dead C81A mutant confirming that the breakdown products observed are the result of autoproteolysis [32]. To address concerns that autoproteolysis may not accurately reflect CAPN5 activity, we also examined the proteolysis of a putative CAPN5 substrate, AIRE, identified through a proteomic analysis (Gal, Geddes, Croall et al., manuscript in preparation). The pattern of in vitro AIRE proteolysis by WT CAPN5, C81A, R243L, G267S, and R289W paralleled CAPN5 autoproteolysis, supporting that autoproteolysis results are indicative of CAPN5 activity.

The reduced S-acylation observed with most pathogenic variants is most likely the consequence of impaired membrane association. According to the consensus “kinetic trapping” model of S-acylation, the interaction of a soluble protein with the DHHC acyltransferases, themselves integral membrane proteins, requires another initial mode of membrane association [33]. For the five pathogenic CAPN5 variants with reduced membrane association, the decrease in the intensity of all upshifted bands rather than reduction of a single band following acyl-PEG exchange suggests that impairment of membrane association is the cause of the reduced S-acylation.

Proposed mechanisms underlying impaired CAPN5 activity.

The results demonstrate both decreased activity and impaired membrane association with five pathogenic CAPN5 variants, and increased activity without impaired membrane association for G267S. We sought to identify possible mechanisms by which the mutations would influence CAPN5 activation. Although the Ca2+-bound CAPN5 structure is not available, the similarity of the protease core domains to those of other calpains in the Ca2+ unbound state [16] and the availability of Ca2+-bound structures for CAPN1, CAPN2, and CAPN9 [29, 46, 47], enables prediction of the conformational changes in CAPN5 during activation.

The mechanism of the Ca2+-induced activation of calpains was first characterized using protease cores of CAPN1 [29]. In the inactive conformation of rat CAPN1 protease core, the residues of the catalytic triad (C115, H272, N296) are too far apart to facilitate proton transfer. Also, the catalytic site is blocked by the W298 side chain. The protease core binds two Ca2+ ions, one in each of the PC1 and PC2 domains. Ca2+ binding triggers a conformational shift, bringing the residues of the catalytic triad closer together. The conformational shift also results in strands β9 (261–263 EAI) and β10 (269–271 VRG) in the PC2 domain coming together to form a β-sheet and creates a hydrophobic pocket composed of I263, V269, and V301. This pocket interacts with the non-polar region of the indole ring of W298 which also H-bonds to E261. The result is that the W298 side chain swings away from its position between the PC1 and PC2 domains, enabling the two domains to come together to form a catalytically competent active site [29].

Conserved residues in CAPN5 include catalytic triad residues C81, H252, N284; acidic residues coordinating Ca2+ binding in the PC1 and PC2 domain, W286 located between the PC1 and PC2 domains in the inactive conformation, and E241 which is assumed to H-bond with W286 in the active conformation (Supplemental Fig. 1A, [14–16]). The residues equivalent to CAPN1 strand β9 are EAR 241–243 and to β10 are VKG 249–251 (Supplemental Fig 1A). These residues are in β-strands in the crystal structure of the inactive CAPN5 protease core (PDB ID 6P3Q [16]).

R243 and R289 are basic residues in contrast to the aliphatic hydrophobic nature of the corresponding residues I263 and V301 in rat CAPN1. CAPNs 3, 9, and 12 also have an arginine at the position equivalent to R243 in CAPN5 [16] (Supplemental Fig 1A). Arginine residues are amphipathic, the side chain closest to the backbone is hydrophobic while the end of the side chain is positively charged [48]. Hydrophobic regions of the two R side chains could readily contribute to a hydrophobic pocket, along with the conserved V249. This suggests that similar to CAPN1, CAPN5 activation results in a hydrophobic pocket formed by the R243, V249 and R289, which docks the W286 aromatic side chain following its swing from the catalytic cleft during activation.

It is striking that four of the pathogenic variants impact residues in the β-strands and hydrophobic pocket essential to calpain activation. R243 is in the β9 strand, L244 is immediately adjacent and may also be part of the β9 stand which appears to extend from A242 to A245 in the crystal structure 6P3Q. K250 is in the β10 strand. R289 is on a separate loop and also contributes to the hydrophobic pocket (Fig. 1, Supplemental Fig 1A). We therefore sought to evaluate whether the pathogenic variants would disrupt the β-strands, β-sheet, and hydrophobic pocket to which these residues contribute.

For R243L, the substituted hydrophobic leucine would facilitate formation of the hydrophobic pocket, similar to isoleucine in CAPN1, but may disrupt the β-strand as leucines prefer α-helices over β-strands [49]. R243L would result in an ALLA sequence for A242-A245 which could form an α-helix instead of a β-strand, similar to the α-helix at or near LLA 268–270.

GnomAD contains two additional R243 variants, R243H and R243C, which are not linked to retinal disorders. Cysteine is considered hydrophobic on the Kyte and Doolittle scale [50], but closer to neutral in other hydrophobicity scales such as Eisenberg [51] and can serve as a polar or non-polar residue depending on the environment [52]. Histidine can function as a basic residue, similar to arginine, but is often found in buried locations similar to hydrophobic residues [48, 53]. Both the cysteine and histidine substitutions are apparently less detrimental than the non-polar hydrophobic leucine substitution, and do not cause retinal disorders.

The L244 side chain packs against the aliphatic portion of the K250 side chain as well as I232 and L248 but is exposed to solvent on one side. Mutation to proline, a smaller heterocyclic and more polar residue, would expose the hydrophobic core on the non-solvent side of L244 and could cause rearrangement and reduce the flexibility of the loop. Proline is disruptive to β-sheet formation and would likely introduce a turn in the β9 strand [54], which already has tight turns at A245 and C246 based on the crystal structure. This would disrupt both the β9 strand and β-sheet formed from the β9 and β10 strands during activation.

K250 is on the β10 strand, as is V249 which contributes to the hydrophobic pocket, and is nearby H252 of the catalytic triad. K250 side chains point out towards solvent and make extensive van der Waals contacts and H-bonds with D239 [7]. K250 main chain also forms H-bonds with the backbone of E241 and A242.The substitution of the uncharged but polar asparagine for a basic lysine is generally considered to be neutral for intracellular proteins and favored for membrane proteins [48]. However, asparagine residues often cause turns in β-sheets, and when followed by glycine introduce a bulge in the secondary structure [55]. Thus, the K250N mutation likely disrupts the β10-strand, and impairs formation of the β sheet by β9 and β10 strands during CAPN5 activation.

R289 is in an extended open coil region, referred to as the G2 gating loop [9], and is nearby N284 of the catalytic triad [9]. The adjacent E290 likely coordinates Ca2+ binding, based on conserved acidic residues in CAPN1, along with D297 on the same loop and D320 on a separate loop. R289 makes salt bridges with E288 and D297 and H-bonds with the main chain carbonyl of W291 and D320 in the crystal structure. The tryptophan substitution for arginine in R289W replaces a basic residue with an aromatic hydrophobic residue, which may support formation of the hydrophobic pocket with R243 and V249, although substitution to tryptophan is predicted to disrupt the ionic and electrostatic interactions and cause clashes because of the bulky side chain. Previously, it was proposed that W289 may stack with other nearby tryptophan residues (W286, W291, W295, W302) and stabilize the wedge tryptophan W286 in the open conformation resulting in enhanced activity even in the absence of added calcium [9]. However, we observed reduced vs. enhanced activity of R289W. The contribution of R289 to the hydrophobic pocket stabilizing W286 during activation and the interaction of R289 with presumed Ca2+ binding residues D297 and D320 suggests that the disruption to these interactions caused by R289W may be very deleterious, contributing to the earlier onset and greater clinical severity associated with this variant.

The association of G267S and G376S with NIV is not as well established as the other pathogenic variants, as they have only been identified in single individuals and are also in the GnomAD database which includes exome and genome sequences from healthy individuals. Another G376 variant, G376V, was also identified in GnomAD but is not included in ClinVar and does not appear to be associated with retinal disorders. In contrast to the other four NIV-causing CAPN5 mutations, G267S and G376S are not associated with the β9 and β10 strands or formation of the hydrophobic pocket.

G267 is in the PC2 domain at the outer edge of a long loop which is followed by a short helical segment, 269–272, based on secondary structure prediction algorithms [56, 57] and 268–270 in the AlphaFold2 model although with low confidence. Converting to a serine that is slightly more hydrophilic and energetically more favorable in a helix could cause that helical turn to begin earlier. However, it does not appear that G267S would impact β9 and β10 strands or the hydrophobic pocket.

G376 is part of a large, twisted loop in the CBSW domain. It is a highly conserved residue at the end of a tri-glycine sequence which would be highly flexible. In contrast to G267 which is surface exposed, G376 is buried and the mutation to serine would be more disruptive due to steric clashes. The backbone angles at G376 in the AlphaFold model (Φ=76°, Ψ=178°) are poor angles for any non-glycine residue, suggesting that the serine substitution would result in a conformational change and disruption to the loop structure. Although the CBSW domain is present in classical calpains and most non-classical calpains [13, 58, 59], its function and role in calpain activation have not been determined.

Possible mechanisms underlying the decreased membrane association.CAPN5 is predominantly localized to the plasma membrane and is also present in the cytosol and subcellular organelles including the nucleus and mitochondria [3, 32, 33, 60–62]. The membrane association of CAPN5 is facilitated by the C2 domain and stabilized by S-acylation of up to three cysteine residues (C4, C507, C512) [32, 33].None of the residues impacted by pathogenic CAPN5 variants were previously implicated in membrane association of CAPN5. However, immediately following G267 are five hydrophobic residues (LLAFF) which form an α-helix, are part of an inserted sequence unique to CAPN5 and CAPN6 [16], and are predicted to be membrane inserted. As discussed above in relation to activity, G267S may extend the α-helix formed by these residues which could enhance membrane association. The residues predicted to be membrane inserted are on the same face of CAPN5 as residues previously shown to contribute to membrane association.The localization of four pathogenic mutations to β-strands and a hydrophobic pocket critical to Ca2+-induced activation provides a mechanism by which these mutations could lead to impaired CAPN5 activity. However, impaired activity is unlikely to be responsible for the reduced membrane association as CAPN5 is associated with the plasma membrane in the absence of added Ca2+, as is catalytically dead CAPN5-C81A [32]. Three basic residues are impacted by the NIV-causing mutations, R243, K250, and R289. These basic residues are clustered together on the surface of the PC2 domain. The polar nature of these residues is largely conserved in calpains with a CysPC-CBSW-C2 domain structure [63] with the exception of the calpain-like protein from Trichoplax adhaerens (Uniprot ID B3RIE5) (Suppl. Fig. 1B). In classical calpains the residues corresponding to R243 and R289 are often non-polar hydrophobic residues (Suppl. Fig. 1A). The conservation of the basic residues in calpains with C2 domains and the finding that mutations which substitute a non-basic residue result in a loss of membrane association suggests that this basic cluster is involved in membrane binding.One possible mechanism is that the basic residues bind to negatively charged phospholipids, including phosphoinositides and phosphatidyl serine, on the plasma membrane. Clusters of basic residues are often found on the cytoplasmic face of integral membrane proteins and on cytoplasmic proteins that bind transiently to membranes, such as protein kinase Cα [64]. The interaction between basic or cationic clusters and membrane phospholipids has been documented and extensively studied for numerous proteins [65–69]. Arguing against this is that basic clusters are typically four or more residues and do not contain acidic residues, with the exception of C2 domains. Adjacent to R289 are acidic residues E288 and E290. However, E290 likely contributes to Ca2+ binding and the bound Ca2+ would neutralize the effect of the acidic residue. The R243-K250-R289 cluster is on a face distal from residues predicted to contribute to the plasma membrane association of CAPN5 and is therefore unlikely to also contribute, although it could participate in binding to membranes of other organelles.A second possible explanation for the impaired membrane association is disruption of protein-protein interactions. Arginine residues are often involved in salt bridges with negatively charged amino acids which can stabilize protein structures or facilitate protein-protein interactions. In synaptotagmin 1 basic residues on the concave side of the C2B-domain interact with acidic residues in membrane associated proteins syntaxin-1 and SNAP-25 [70]. Another example is calmodulin, in which acidic residues interact with polybasic regions of membrane-associated target proteins [71].A third possibility is that the basic cluster is important in the self-association of CAPN5 into dimers or multimers. Once again using synaptotagmin 1 as an example, it self-associates into ring-like multimers in the presence of anionic phospholipids [72]. Mutations that neutralize lysine residues abolish the self-association and also impair activity when synaptotagmin 1 mutants are expressed in neurons [72]. A second example is focal adhesion kinase for which binding to PI(4,5)P2 in the membrane occurs via a basic patch and triggers oligomerization, also via the basic patch, and induces conformational changes leading to activation [73]. In this example, the basic cluster is involved in membrane binding, enzyme self-association, and enzyme activation.The G376S mutation in the CBSW domain also resulted in impaired membrane association and reduced CAPN5 activity. The CBSW domain was previously referred to as a C2-like domain, as it contains 8 anti-parallel β strands similar to C2 domains although with a different topology [15, 74]. Possible mechanisms underlying the decreased membrane association.

CAPN5 is predominantly localized to the plasma membrane and is also present in the cytosol and subcellular organelles including the nucleus and mitochondria [3, 32, 33, 60–62]. The membrane association of CAPN5 is facilitated by the C2 domain and stabilized by S-acylation of up to three cysteine residues (C4, C507, C512) [32, 33].

None of the residues impacted by pathogenic CAPN5 variants were previously implicated in membrane association of CAPN5. However, immediately following G267 are five hydrophobic residues (LLAFF) which form an α-helix, are part of an inserted sequence unique to CAPN5 and CAPN6 [16], and are predicted to be membrane inserted. As discussed above in relation to activity, G267S may extend the α-helix formed by these residues which could enhance membrane association. The residues predicted to be membrane inserted are on the same face of CAPN5 as residues previously shown to contribute to membrane association.

The localization of four pathogenic mutations to β-strands and a hydrophobic pocket critical to Ca2+-induced activation provides a mechanism by which these mutations could lead to impaired CAPN5 activity. However, impaired activity is unlikely to be responsible for the reduced membrane association as CAPN5 is associated with the plasma membrane in the absence of added Ca2+, as is catalytically dead CAPN5-C81A [32]. Three basic residues are impacted by the NIV-causing mutations, R243, K250, and R289. These basic residues are clustered together on the surface of the PC2 domain. The polar nature of these residues is largely conserved in calpains with a CysPC-CBSW-C2 domain structure [63] with the exception of the calpain-like protein from Trichoplax adhaerens (Uniprot ID B3RIE5) (Suppl. Fig. 1B). In classical calpains the residues corresponding to R243 and R289 are often non-polar hydrophobic residues (Suppl. Fig. 1A). The conservation of the basic residues in calpains with C2 domains and the finding that mutations which substitute a non-basic residue result in a loss of membrane association suggests that this basic cluster is involved in membrane binding.

One possible mechanism is that the basic residues bind to negatively charged phospholipids, including phosphoinositides and phosphatidyl serine, on the plasma membrane. Clusters of basic residues are often found on the cytoplasmic face of integral membrane proteins and on cytoplasmic proteins that bind transiently to membranes, such as protein kinase Cα [64]. The interaction between basic or cationic clusters and membrane phospholipids has been documented and extensively studied for numerous proteins [65–69]. Arguing against this is that basic clusters are typically four or more residues and do not contain acidic residues, with the exception of C2 domains. Adjacent to R289 are acidic residues E288 and E290. However, E290 likely contributes to Ca2+ binding and the bound Ca2+ would neutralize the effect of the acidic residue. The R243-K250-R289 cluster is on a face distal from residues predicted to contribute to the plasma membrane association of CAPN5 and is therefore unlikely to also contribute, although it could participate in binding to membranes of other organelles.

A second possible explanation for the impaired membrane association is disruption of protein-protein interactions. Arginine residues are often involved in salt bridges with negatively charged amino acids which can stabilize protein structures or facilitate protein-protein interactions. In synaptotagmin 1 basic residues on the concave side of the C2B-domain interact with acidic residues in membrane associated proteins syntaxin-1 and SNAP-25 [70]. Another example is calmodulin, in which acidic residues interact with polybasic regions of membrane-associated target proteins [71].

A third possibility is that the basic cluster is important in the self-association of CAPN5 into dimers or multimers. Once again using synaptotagmin 1 as an example, it self-associates into ring-like multimers in the presence of anionic phospholipids [72]. Mutations that neutralize lysine residues abolish the self-association and also impair activity when synaptotagmin 1 mutants are expressed in neurons [72]. A second example is focal adhesion kinase for which binding to PI(4,5)P2 in the membrane occurs via a basic patch and triggers oligomerization, also via the basic patch, and induces conformational changes leading to activation [73]. In this example, the basic cluster is involved in membrane binding, enzyme self-association, and enzyme activation.

Gain-of-function, loss-of-function, or dominant negative mechanism?

The autosomal dominant pattern of NIV inheritance suggests either a gain-of-function or dominant-negative mechanism [6, 81]. Although some dominant mutations can cause loss-of-function via haploinsufficiency, CAPN5 gene variants resulting in a loss-of-function do not cause NIV [82]. Additionally, Capn5 heterozygous mice (C57BL/6J-Capn5tm1Dgen/J; Capn5+/LacZ) appear normal and CAPN5 protein levels are similar to WT mice [61]. The clustering of several of the mutations in 3D space further supports either a dominant-negative or gain-of-function mechanism [6, 83].

Previous studies supported a gain-of-function mechanism for the R243L, G267S, and R289W variants [9, 16, 27]. For the R243L variant, an insert including the G1 gating loop from human CAPN5 (amino acids 233–247 KAVTAADMEARLACG) replaced the corresponding rat sequence (residues 253–267 NISDIRDLEAITFKN) in a recombinant CAPN1 protease core domain mini-CAPN1/5 hybrid model, with R243L simulated by replacing the R with L in the inserted sequence [28]. The R289W variant was simulated with a V301W mutation in rat mini-CAPN1, the residue/mutation equivalent to R289W in CAPN5 [9]. G267S was simulated by residues 260–275 (RKVRLGHGLLAFFKSE) of CAPN5 for residues 280–287 (KQVTYQGQ) of rat mini-CAPN1 [16], and was also modeled in recombinant CAPN5 protease core with an N-terminal maltose binding protein tag (MBP-rCAPN1/5-PC) [16]. In each of the above recombinant protease core models, synthetic peptide substrates were cleaved more rapidly in the models with the NIV mutant residue, as compared to WT residue, in the presence of up to 100 mM calcium [9, 16, 27]. The pathogenic mutations are proposed to alter the structure of the G1 and G2 gating loops, which may allow substrate access to the catalytic site in the absence of added calcium [7, 9, 16, 27].

The small but significant increase in G267S autoproteolysis is consistent with the previously reported increase in activity of mini-CAPN5-G267S [16], and supports a gain-of-function mechanism. The decrease in autoproteolytic activity at the membrane for the other five pathologic variants (R243L, L244P, K250N, R289W, G376S) suggests either a loss-of-function or dominant-negative mechanism. This contrasts with the increased activity observed for mini CAPN1/5 hybrid models of R243L and R289W.

One possible explanation for the contrasting results is that the substituted leucine in the G1 gating loop insert into CAPN1 is more similar to the corresponding CAPN1 I263 residue than is the R243 in CAPN5. Also, for R289W the substituted tryptophan is closer in hydrophobicity to the WT valine than is arginine at position 301 of rat CAPN1. Thus, it is reasonable that these substitutions result in increased activity in the mini CAPN1/5 hybrid models as the hydrophobic properties of the mutant residues are closer to the WT residues in CAPN1, but not the basic residues in CAPN5. Presumably there is an evolutionary preference for the arginine residues at the 243 and 289 positions of CAPN5 and hydrophobic residues at the equivalent positions of CAPN1 and CAPN2. It will be important to evaluate the effects of the R243L and R289W mutations in non-hybrid CAPN5 protease core models, as was done with G267S. Additionally, membrane association may be required for the full activity of CAPN5.

In support of a dominant-negative mechanism, the co-immunoprecipitation results indicate that CAPN5 may exist as a dimer/multimer or possibly as part of a larger macromolecular complex. The results also indicate that pathologic variants R243L and R289W, but not G267S, may act in a dominant-negative manner. The formation of a dimer or multimer is consistent with the observation that dominant-negative mutations are often found at interface positions where they are typically associated with intermolecular interactions and assembly of proteins into homomeric and heteromeric complexes [6]. Classical calpains with penta-EF hand domains exist as homomeric and heteromeric dimers [84, 85]. Dimers or multimers have not been previously demonstrated for CAPN5 or other non-classical calpains. However, other C2 domain proteins have been shown to dimerize through their C2 domains, with the membrane associated proteins being mainly dimers [86–88]. This clustering has been proposed to be a universal feature of Ca2+−dependent membrane-binding proteins utilizing C2-domains [89].

The early stages of NIV clinically resemble retinitis pigmentosa. Mutations in the rhodopsin gene are the most common cause of autosomal dominant retinitis pigmentosa (adRP) [90, 91]. The mechanisms by which mutant rod opsin leads to adRP include improper transport to the plasma membrane, protein misfolding, mutant rod opsin protein being retained in the ER where it triggers the unfolded protein response and ER stress, and protein dimerization and aggregation [92–96]. The magnitude of rhodopsin destabilization correlates with the amount of protein reaching the membrane and with disease severity [97, 98]. Although CAPN5 is not an integral membrane protein, it stably associates with membranes via S-acylation, appears to be a dimer or multimer, and most pathogenic variants result in reduced enzyme levels and decreased activity at the membrane.

There are several caveats to these findings and their interpretation. It is recognized that any prediction of the impact of the pathogenic mutations on the activation mechanism is speculative in the absence of structures for WT and mutant Ca2+−bound CAPN5. It is possible that mutants may be more sensitive to experimental treatments such as permeabilization. It is also possible that the observed decrease in membrane association of the five pathogenic variants results from extreme hyperactivity and autoproteolysis at the membrane, but not at the cytosol. Additionally, the N1C1 anti-CAPN5 antibody used might miss autoproteolytic fragments specific to cytosolic CAPN5. Calcium binding to residues in the PC2, CBSW, and C2 domains needs to be demonstrated, along with structural analysis of Ca2+-bound protease core or preferably full-length CAPN5.
