# [Fe]-Hydrogenase, Cofactor Biosynthesis and Engineering.

[Fe]-hydrogenase catalyzes the heterolytic cleavage of H2 and reversible hydride
transfer to methenyl-tetrahydromethanopterin. The iron-guanylylpyridinol (FeGP)
cofactor is the prosthetic group of this enzyme, in which mononuclear Fe(II) is
ligated with a pyridinol and two CO ligands. The pyridinol ligand fixes the iron
by an acyl carbon and a pyridinol nitrogen. Biosynthetic proteins for this
cofactor are encoded in the hmd co-occurring (hcg) genes. The function of HcgB,
HcgC, HcgD, HcgE, and HcgF was studied by using structure-to-function analysis,
which is based on the crystal structure of the proteins and subsequent enzyme
assays. Recently, we reported the catalytic properties of HcgA and HcgG, novel
radical S-adenosyl methionine enzymes, by using an in vitro biosynthesis assay.
Here, we review the properties of [Fe]-hydrogenase and the FeGP cofactor, and
the biosynthesis of the FeGP cofactor. Finally, we discuss the expected
engineering of [Fe]-hydrogenase and the FeGP cofactor.
