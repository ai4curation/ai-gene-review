# alpha-Lactalbumins and lysozymes.
**Authors:** McKenzie HA
**Journal:** EXS (1996)

## Abstract

1. EXS. 1996;75:365-409.

alpha-Lactalbumins and lysozymes.

McKenzie HA(1).

Author information:
(1)School of Chemistry, University College, University of New South Wales,
Australian Defence Force Academy, Canberra, Australia.

Lysozyme is ubiquitous in a variety of tissues and secretions. Chick-type
(c-type) lysozymes lyse the cell walls of certain bacteria. In contrast,
alpha-lactalbumin appears to occur only in mammalian milk and colostrum. It has
the unusual property of acting as a modifier protein to modify the action of
galactosyl transferase to a lactose synthase. Both proteins have diverged from a
common ancestor. This is reflected in the striking relationship between their
amino acid sequences, and the high conservation of disulfide bridges, their
intron-exon organization, and three-dimensional structures. In studying their
evolutionary relationships some important differences are noted, e.g., all
alpha-lactalbumins strongly bind Ca(II), but only some c-type lysozymes do so.
These properties point the way to future investigations that are necessary
before firm conclusions can be made about their evolutionary history.
