Lanthanides serve as essential cofactors in the methanol dehydrogenases of
methylotrophic bacteria. While biological function has been characterized for
the naturally occurring members of the series, promethium, the only lanthanide
existing as exclusively radioactive isotopes and virtually absent from nature,
has remained unexplored. Here, the acidophilic methanotroph Methylacidiphilum
fumariolicum SolV grows on β⁻-emitting Pm-147 as its sole lanthanide source,
removing 98% of the isotope from the medium over 60 h despite a calculated
radiation dose of 3.6 Gy. Reconstituted methanol dehydrogenase of the type
XoxF-MDH displays Pm-dependent activity precisely between the neodymium and
samarium variants. Bacterial lanthanide metabolism thus incorporates a naturally
absent and radioactive member of the lanthanide series.
