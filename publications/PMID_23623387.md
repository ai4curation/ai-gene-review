# Yunis-Varón syndrome is caused by mutations in FIG4, encoding a phosphoinositide phosphatase.

Yunis-Varón syndrome is caused by mutations in FIG4, encoding a phosphoinositide
phosphatase.

Corona-Rivera J, Morandi L, Mora M, Reutter H, Vulto-van Silfhout AT, Faivre L,

Author information:
(1)Department of Molecular and Human Genetics, Baylor College of Medicine,

Yunis-Varón syndrome (YVS) is an autosomal-recessive disorder with cleidocranial
dysplasia, digital anomalies, and severe neurological involvement. Enlarged
vacuoles are found in neurons, muscle, and cartilage. By whole-exome sequencing,
we identified frameshift and missense mutations of FIG4 in affected individuals
from three unrelated families. FIG4 encodes a phosphoinositide phosphatase
required for regulation of PI(3,5)P(2) levels, and thus endosomal trafficking
and autophagy. In a functional assay, both missense substitutions failed to
correct the vacuolar phenotype of Fig4-null mouse fibroblasts. Homozygous
Fig4-null mice exhibit features of YVS, including neurodegeneration and enlarged
vacuoles in neurons. We demonstrate that Fig4-null mice also have small
skeletons with reduced trabecular bone volume and cortical thickness and that
cultured osteoblasts accumulate large vacuoles. Our findings demonstrate that
homozygosity or compound heterozygosity for null mutations of FIG4 is
responsible for YVS, the most severe known human phenotype caused by defective
phosphoinositide metabolism. In contrast, in Charcot-Marie-Tooth disease type 4J
(also caused by FIG4 mutations), one of the FIG4 alleles is hypomorphic and
disease is limited to the peripheral nervous system. This genotype-phenotype
correlation demonstrates that absence of FIG4 activity leads to central nervous
system dysfunction and extensive skeletal anomalies. Our results describe a role
for PI(3,5)P(2) signaling in skeletal development and maintenance.
