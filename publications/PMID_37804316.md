AFG3L2 is a mitochondrial protease exerting protein quality control in the inner
mitochondrial membrane. Heterozygous AFG3L2 mutations cause spinocerebellar
ataxia type 28 (SCA28) or dominant optic atrophy type 12 (DOA12), while
biallelic AFG3L2 mutations result in the rare and severe spastic ataxia type 5
(SPAX5). The clinical spectrum of SPAX5 includes childhood-onset cerebellar
ataxia, spasticity, dystonia and myoclonic epilepsy. We previously reported that
the absence or mutation of AFG3L2 leads to the accumulation of
mitochondria-encoded proteins, causing the overactivation of the
stress-sensitive protease OMA1, which over-processes OPA1, leading to
mitochondrial fragmentation. Recently, OMA1 has been identified as the pivotal
player communicating mitochondrial stress to the cytosol via a pathway involving
the inner mitochondrial membrane protein DELE1 and the cytosolic kinase HRI,
thus eliciting the integrated stress response. In general, the integrated stress
response reduces global protein synthesis and drives the expression of
cytoprotective genes that allow cells to endure proteotoxic stress. However, the
relevance of the OMA1-DELE1-HRI axis in vivo, and especially in a human CNS
disease context, has been poorly documented thus far. In this work, we
demonstrated that mitochondrial proteotoxicity in the absence/mutation of AFG3L2
activates the OMA1-DELE1-HRI pathway eliciting the integrated stress response.
We found enhanced OMA1-dependent processing of DELE1 upon depletion of AFG3L2.
Also, in both skin fibroblasts from SPAX5 patients (including a novel case) and
in the cerebellum of Afg3l2-/- mice we detected increased phosphorylation of the
α-subunit of the eukaryotic translation initiation factor 2 (eIF2α), increased
levels of ATF4 and strong upregulation of its downstream targets (Chop, Chac1,
Ppp1r15a and Ffg21). Silencing of DELE1 or HRI in SPAX5 fibroblasts (where OMA1
is overactivated at basal state) reduces eIF2α phosphorylation and affects cell
growth. In agreement, pharmacological potentiation of integrated stress response
via Sephin-1, a drug that selectively inhibits the stress-induced eIF2alpha
phosphatase GADD34 (encoded by Ppp1r15a), improved cell growth of SPAX5
fibroblasts and cell survival and dendritic arborization ex vivo in primary
Afg3l2-/- Purkinje neurons. Notably, Sephin-1 treatment in vivo extended the
lifespan of Afg3l2-/- mice, improved Purkinje neuron morphology, mitochondrial
ultrastructure and respiratory capacity. These data indicate that activation of
the OMA1-DELE1-HRI pathway is protective in the context of SPAX5.
Pharmacological tuning of the integrated stress response may represent a future
therapeutic strategy for SPAX5 and other cerebellar ataxias caused by impaired
mitochondrial proteostasis.

Introduction

AFG3L2 belongs to the AAA-protease subfamily (ATPases Associated with various cellular Activities) and assembles into homo-oligomers and hetero-oligomers with paraplegin in the inner mitochondrial membrane (IMM), named m-AAA proteases. These complexes are a crucial component of the quality control system in the IMM and mediate the selective degradation of non-assembled and damaged proteins.1

Heterozygous mutations in the proteolytic domain of AFG3L2 cause autosomal dominant spinocerebellar ataxia type 28 (SCA28) (MIM#610246), characterized by slowly progressive gait and limb ataxia with oculomotor abnormalities.2 Biallelic mutations in the same domain cause spastic ataxia type 5 (SPAX5) (MIM#614487), a severe childhood onset condition characterized by cerebellar ataxia, spasticity resulting in significantly impaired ambulation, oculomotor apraxia, dystonia and myoclonic epilepsy.3-5 Recent papers further expanded the clinical spectrum of SPAX5.6,7 More recently, heterozygous mutations in the AFG3L2-ATPase domain have been associated with dominant optic atrophy type 12 (DOA12) (MIM#618977).8,9

We provided evidence that AFG3L2 mutations in the proteolytic domain are loss-of-function variants, and the spectrum of associated phenotypes depends on the extent of the impairment of m-AAA functionality.10 Accordingly, the Afg3l2+/− haploinsufficient mouse recapitulates SCA28 patient features displaying late onset defects in motor coordination and balance,11 while the Afg3l2−/− mouse resembles SPAX5 patients, presenting a severe neurological syndrome with ataxia and spasticity, which leads to early lethality.12

We previously demonstrated that mutations of AFG3L2 lead to accumulation of mitochondria-encoded proteins in SPAX5 and SCA28 fibroblasts, as well as in Afg3l2−/− murine embryonic fibroblasts (MEFs). Mitochondrial proteotoxicity triggers the overactivation of the IMM stress-sensitive protease OMA1, which in turns overprocesses the long forms of the IMM fusion protein OPA1 (L-OPA1), leading to mitochondrial fragmentation.10 As a proof-of-concept of this mechanism, attenuation of mitochondrial protein synthesis by chloramphenicol stabilizes OMA1 levels and restores L-OPA1 and mitochondrial tubulation in SCA28 and SPAX5 cellular models.10

In mammalian cells, mitochondrial dysfunction triggers the integrated stress response (ISR), in which the phosphorylation of the α-subunit of the eukaryotic translation initiation factor 2 (eIF2α) reduces global protein synthesis, but in parallel also increases the translation of the transcription factor ATF4. The latter mediates a mitochondrion-to-nucleus communication, driving the expression of cytoprotective genes that reprogramme and rewire cellular metabolism.13 Recently, the players that communicate mitochondrial stress to the cytosol have been identified. In experimental cell models of mitochondrial stress, it has been shown that OMA1 cleaves the IMM protein DELE1, releasing a short form of DELE1 to the cytosol to stimulate Heme-regulated eIF2α kinase (HRI)-dependent eIF2α phosphorylation.14,15

So far, the relevance of the OMA1-DELE1-HRI pathway driving the ISR in vivo or in a human disease context has been poorly characterized. In recent months, two papers described the protective role of this pathway in the skeletal muscle and heart in mouse models of mitochondrial myopathy.16,17 However, little is known about the activation of this pathway in the CNS, and nothing in the cerebellum. Additionally, it is not known whether activation of the mitochondria-mediated ISR may be protective against mitochondrial stress or instead detrimental in neurodegeneration. It is indeed generally accepted that, according to the disease nature, duration and severity, the ISR can work as an adaptive prosurvival response, or conversely can be maladaptive promoting cell death.18

In this work, we sought to address these issues in the context of SPAX5. In both SPAX5 patient skin fibroblasts and in the cerebellum of Afg3l2−/− mice, we demonstrated the activation of the OMA1-DELE1-HRI axis leading to ISR. Potentiation of this pathway by Sephin-1, a selective inhibitor of the stress-induced eIF2α phosphatase GADD34 (encoded by PPP1R15A),19 increased cell viability in SPAX5 fibroblasts and improved survival and arborization of Afg3l2−/− Purkinje neurons ex vivo. In vivo, this treatment prolonged the life span of Afg3l2−/− mice, ameliorating Purkinje neuron health as well as mitochondrial morphology and respiratory capacity.

Future studies will elucidate whether potentiation of the OMA1-mediated ISR may have a significant therapeutic value in SPAX5 and other ataxias caused by altered mitochondrial proteostasis.

Patient 2 carries a novel compound heterozygous AFG3L2 mutations (c.245 dup;p.Asn82Lysfs*6 and the c.1875G>A;p.Met625Ile). She is an 8-year-old female, presenting with severe ataxia and pyramidal signs. She was symptomatic before the age of 12 months, started to walk at 14 months and her cognitive ability were correct. At 3 years of age, the neurological clinical examination showed gait instability, with ataxia. She was destabilized during orientation changes. She did not show a decrease in driving force. The reflexes were present and symmetrical, there was no hypertonia, a left Babinski sign was present and oculomotricity was correct. Brain MRI scan disclosed a hyper signal on T2-weighted, FLAIR, diffusion weighted imaging and hyposignal on T1-weighted involving bilaterally the globi pallidi, the cerebral peduncles at the level of the midbrain, and at the level of the pons. Enlarged interfollicular space at the level of the cerebellar hemispheres was observed (Fig. 2B). Spectro-MRI did not show any lactate peak. Progressively, she developed cerebellar symptoms with dysmetria, dysarthria and pyramidal involvement, with bilateral Babinski signs. After 6 years of age, she lost the ability to walk. She had several epileptic seizures controlled with Levetiracetam, but frequent non-epileptic myoclonic bursts appear (Table 2).

We previously characterized Patient 1's skin fibroblasts for key mitochondrial phenotypes and showed no alteration in ΔΨm and moderate mitochondrial fragmentation.10 We thus measured ΔΨm by TMRM fluorescence and found a significant reduction in Patient 2’s fibroblasts at steady state (Fig. 2C). The same alterations were detected in galactose conditions, which enhance mitochondrial oxidative metabolism (Supplementary Fig. 2A and B).28

To directly test that the OMA1-mediated ISR was activated through DELE1, we tested if DELE1 cleavage was increased upon reduction of AFG3L2 protein level. No antibodies specifically recognizing DELE1 are available,14,15 preventing us from testing its cleavage by OMA1 in the cerebellum of the Afg3l2−/− mouse and in SPAX5 fibroblasts in endogenous conditions. The average number of Purkinje neurons was completely restored in Afg3l2−/− cultures upon Sephin-1 administration, and the area of the dendrites was remarkably increased (Fig. 4D and E).
