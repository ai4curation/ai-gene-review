# Deregulated human Cdc14A phosphatase disrupts centrosome separation and chromosome segregation.
**Authors:** Mailand N, Lukas C, Kaiser BK, Jackson PK, Bartek J, Lukas J
**Journal:** Nat Cell Biol (2002)
**DOI:** [10.1038/ncb777](https://doi.org/10.1038/ncb777)

## Abstract

1. Nat Cell Biol. 2002 Apr;4(4):317-22. doi: 10.1038/ncb777.

Deregulated human Cdc14A phosphatase disrupts centrosome separation and
chromosome segregation.

Mailand N(1), Lukas C, Kaiser BK, Jackson PK, Bartek J, Lukas J.

Author information:
(1)Institute of Cancer Biology, Danish Cancer Society, Strandboulevarden 49,

Erratum in
    Nat Cell Biol. 2006 Aug;8(8):897.

We show that human Cdc14A phosphatase interacts with interphase centrosomes, and
that this interaction is independent of microtubules and Cdc14A phosphatase
activity, but requires active nuclear export. Disrupting the nuclear export
signal (NES) led to Cdc14A being localized in nucleoli, which in unperturbed
cells selectively contain Cdc14B (ref. 1). Conditional overproduction of Cdc14A,
but not its phosphatase-dead or NES-deficient mutants, or Cdc14B, resulted in
premature centrosome splitting and formation of supernumerary mitotic spindles.
In contrast, downregulation of endogenous Cdc14A by short inhibitory RNA
duplexes (siRNA) induced mitotic defects including impaired centrosome
separation and failure to undergo productive cytokinesis. Consequently, both
overexpression and downregulation of Cdc14A caused aberrant chromosome
partitioning into daughter cells. These results indicate that Cdc14A is a
physiological regulator of the centrosome duplication cycle, which, when
disrupted, can lead to genomic instability in mammalian cells.
