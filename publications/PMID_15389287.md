# A highly conserved proapoptotic gene, IKIP, located next to the APAF1 gene locus, is regulated by p53.
**Authors:** Hofer-Warbinek R, Schmid JA, Mayer H, Winsauer G, Orel L, Mueller B, Wiesner Ch, Binder BR, de Martin R
**Journal:** Cell Death Differ (2004)
**DOI:** [10.1038/sj.cdd.4401502](https://doi.org/10.1038/sj.cdd.4401502)

## Abstract

1. Cell Death Differ. 2004 Dec;11(12):1317-25. doi: 10.1038/sj.cdd.4401502.

A highly conserved proapoptotic gene, IKIP, located next to the APAF1 gene
locus, is regulated by p53.

Hofer-Warbinek R(1), Schmid JA, Mayer H, Winsauer G, Orel L, Mueller B, Wiesner
Ch, Binder BR, de Martin R.

Author information:
(1)Competence Center Biomolecular Therapeutics GmbH, Vienna, Austria.

We describe here the identification and initial characterization of a novel
human gene termed IKIP (I kappa B kinase interacting protein) that is located on
chromosome 12 in close proximity to APAF1 (apoptotic protease-activating
factor-1). IKIP and APAF1 share a common 488 bp promoter from which the two
genes are transcribed in opposite directions. Three IKIP transcripts are
generated by differential splicing and alternative exon usage that do not show
significant homology to other genes in the databases. Similar to APAF1,
expression of IKIP is enhanced by X-irradiation, and both genes are dependent on
p53. Moreover, IKIP promotes apoptosis when transfected into endothelial cells.
We conclude that IKIP is a novel p53 target gene with proapoptotic function.
