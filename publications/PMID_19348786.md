# The high-affinity peptidoglycan binding domain of Pseudomonas phage endolysin KZ144.
**Authors:** Briers Y, Schmelcher M, Loessner MJ, Hendrix J, Engelborghs Y, Volckaert G, Lavigne R
**Journal:** Biochem Biophys Res Commun (2009)
**DOI:** [10.1016/j.bbrc.2009.03.161](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.bbrc.2009.03.161)

## Abstract

1. Biochem Biophys Res Commun. 2009 May 29;383(2):187-91. doi:
10.1016/j.bbrc.2009.03.161. Epub 2009 Apr 5.

The high-affinity peptidoglycan binding domain of Pseudomonas phage endolysin
KZ144.

Briers Y(1), Schmelcher M, Loessner MJ, Hendrix J, Engelborghs Y, Volckaert G,
Lavigne R.

Author information:
(1)Department of Biosystems, Division of Gene Technology, Katholieke
Universiteit Leuven, Leuven, Belgium.

The binding affinity of the N-terminal peptidoglycan binding domain of endolysin
KZ144 (PBD(KZ)), originating from Pseudomonas aeruginosa bacteriophage varphiKZ,
has been examined using a fusion protein of PBD(KZ) and green fluorescent
protein (PBD(KZ)-GFP). A fluorescence recovery after photobleaching analysis of
bound PBD(KZ)-GFP molecules showed less than 10% fluorescence recovery in the
bleached area within 15 min. Surface plasmon resonance analysis confirmed this
apparent high binding affinity revealing an equilibrium affinity constant of
2.95 x 10(7)M(-1) for the PBD(KZ)-peptidoglycan interaction. This unique domain,
which binds to the peptidoglycan of all tested Gram-negative species, was
harnessed to improve the specific activity of the peptidoglycan hydrolase domain
KMV36C. The chimeric peptidoglycan hydrolase (PBD(KZ)-KMV36C) exhibits a
threefold higher specific activity than the native catalytic domain (KMV36C).
These results demonstrate that the modular assembly of functional domains is a
rational approach to improve the specific activity of endolysins from phages
infecting Gram-negatives.
