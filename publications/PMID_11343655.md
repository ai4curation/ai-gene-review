# Nucleoside diphosphate kinase, a source of GTP, is required for dynamin-dependent synaptic vesicle recycling.
**Authors:** Krishnan KS, Rikhy R, Rao S, Shivalkar M, Mosko M, Narayanan R, Etter P, Estes PS, Ramaswami M
**Journal:** Neuron (2001)
**DOI:** [10.1016/s0896-6273(01)00273-2](https://doi.org/10.1016/s0896-6273(01)00273-2)

## Abstract

1. Neuron. 2001 Apr;30(1):197-210. doi: 10.1016/s0896-6273(01)00273-2.

Nucleoside diphosphate kinase, a source of GTP, is required for
dynamin-dependent synaptic vesicle recycling.

Krishnan KS(1), Rikhy R, Rao S, Shivalkar M, Mosko M, Narayanan R, Etter P,
Estes PS, Ramaswami M.

Author information:
(1)Department of Biological Sciences, Tata Institute for Fundamental Research,
Homi Bhabha Road, 400005, Colaba, Bombay, India.

Nucleoside diphosphate kinase (NDK), an enzyme encoded by the Drosophila
abnormal wing discs (awd) or human nm23 tumor suppressor genes, generates
nucleoside triphosphates from respective diphosphates. We demonstrate that NDK
regulates synaptic vesicle internalization at the stage where function of the
dynamin GTPase is required. awd mutations lower the temperature at which
behavioral paralysis, synaptic failure, and blocked membrane internalization
occur at dynamin-deficient, shi(ts), mutant nerve terminals. Hypomorphic awd
alleles display shi(ts)-like defects. NDK is present at synapses and its
enzymatic activity is essential for normal presynaptic function. We suggest a
model in which dynamin activity in nerve terminals is highly dependent on
NDK-mediated supply of GTP. This connection between NDK and membrane
internalization further strengthens an emerging hypothesis that endocytosis,
probably of activated growth factor receptors, is an important tumor suppressor
activity in vivo.
