# An activity-dependent network of interactions links the Rel protein Dorsal with its cytoplasmic regulators.
**Authors:** Edwards DN, Towb P, Wasserman SA
**Journal:** Development (1997)
**DOI:** [10.1242/dev.124.19.3855](https://doi.org/10.1242/dev.124.19.3855)

## Abstract

1. Development. 1997 Oct;124(19):3855-64. doi: 10.1242/dev.124.19.3855.

An activity-dependent network of interactions links the Rel protein Dorsal with
its cytoplasmic regulators.

Edwards DN(1), Towb P, Wasserman SA.

Author information:
(1)Department of Molecular Biology and Oncology, UT Southwestern Medical Center,
Dallas, Texas 75235-9148, USA.

A signaling pathway active on the ventral side of the Drosophila embryo defines
dorsoventral polarity. A cell surface signal relayed by Toll, Tube and Pelle
releases the Rel-related protein Dorsal from its cytoplasmic inhibitor Cactus;
free Dorsal translocates into nuclei and directs expression of ventral fates.
Using the yeast two-hybrid system and immunoprecipitation experiments, we define
scaffolding and anchoring interactions among the pathway components. We show
that Dorsal binds specifically to Tube, Pelle and Cactus, and that the protein
kinase activity of Pelle differentially regulates its interactions with Dorsal
and Tube. We also identify Drosophila Filamin as a potential adaptor linking the
interaction network, via Tube, to the transmembrane receptor Toll.
