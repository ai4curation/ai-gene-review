Plants undergo contrasting developmental programs in dark and light.
Photomorphogenesis, a light-adapted programme is repressed in the dark by the
synergistic actions of CUL4(COP1-SPA) E3 ubiquitin ligase and a subset of basic
helix-loop-helix transcription factors called phytochrome interacting factors
(PIFs). To promote photomorphogenesis, light activates the phytochrome family of
sensory photoreceptors, which inhibits these repressors by poorly understood
mechanisms. Here, we show that the CUL4(COP1-SPA) E3 ubiquitin ligase is
necessary for the light-induced degradation of PIF1 in Arabidopsis. The
light-induced ubiquitylation and subsequent degradation of PIF1 is reduced in
the cop1, spaQ and cul4 backgrounds. COP1, SPA1 and CUL4 preferentially form
complexes with the phosphorylated forms of PIF1 in response to light. The cop1
and spaQ seeds display strong hyposensitive response to far-red light-mediated
seed germination and light-regulated gene expression. These data show a
mechanism by which an E3 ligase attenuates its activity by degrading its
cofactor in response to light.
