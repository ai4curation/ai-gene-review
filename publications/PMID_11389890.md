# Do mammalian cells synthesize lipoic acid? Identification of a mouse cDNA encoding a lipoic acid synthase located in mitochondria.
**Authors:** Morikawa T, Yasuno R, Wada H
**Journal:** FEBS Lett (2001)
**DOI:** [10.1016/s0014-5793(01)02469-3](https://doi.org/10.1016/s0014-5793(01)02469-3)

## Abstract

1. FEBS Lett. 2001 Jun 1;498(1):16-21. doi: 10.1016/s0014-5793(01)02469-3.

Do mammalian cells synthesize lipoic acid? Identification of a mouse cDNA
encoding a lipoic acid synthase located in mitochondria.

Morikawa T(1), Yasuno R, Wada H.

Author information:
(1)Department of Biology, Faculty of Sciences, Kyushu University, Ropponmatsu,
Fukuoka 810-8560, Japan.

Lipoic acid is a coenzyme essential to the activity of enzymes such as pyruvate
dehydrogenase, which play important roles in central metabolism. However,
neither the enzymes responsible for biosynthesis nor the biosynthetic event of
lipoic acid has been reported in mammalian cells. In this study, a mouse mLIP1
cDNA for lipoic acid synthase has been identified. We have shown that the cDNA
encodes a lipoic acid synthase by its ability to complement a mutant of
Escherichia coli defective in lipoic acid synthase and that mLIP1 is targeted
into the mitochondria. These findings suggest that mammalian cells are able to
synthesize lipoic acid in mitochondria.
