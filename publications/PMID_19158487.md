# Nek1 regulates cell death and mitochondrial membrane permeability through phosphorylation of VDAC1.

The mammalian NIMA-related protein kinase 1 (Nek1) is important for keeping
cells alive after DNA damage, but the mechanism by which injured cells die
without functional Nek1 has not yet been demonstrated. Here we show that Nek1
regulates the pathway to mitochondrial cell death through phosphorylation of
voltage dependent anion channel 1 (VDAC1) on serine 193. Nek1 associates with
VDAC1 in a yeast two-hybrid system, as well as by GST pull-down assays and by
reciprocal immunoprecipitation. A portion of Nek1 in cells also localizes at
mitochondria. Ectopic expression of a kinase-dead Nek1 mutant results in cell
death, which is immediately preceded by loss of the Nek1-dependent VDAC1-S193
phosphorylation. UV irradiation of Nek1-deficient cells or silencing of
endogenous Nek1 expression similarly results in loss of the specific S193
phosphorylation before cells die. Nek1-deficient cells are characterized by
exaggerated mitochondrial membrane permeability (MMP) and accelerated cell
death. Ectopic expression of a VDAC1-Ser193Ala mutant, which cannot be
phosphorylated by Nek1, also results in cell death. A VDAC1-Ser193Glu mutant,
designed to mimic constitutive phosphorylation by Nek1, rescues exaggerated MMP
and keeps cells alive after DNA damaging injury, but only transiently. The
direct interaction between Nek1 and VDAC1 provides a mechanism to explain how
Nek1 prevents excessive cell death, as well as the first direct evidence that a
specific kinase regulates VDAC1 activity.

Introduction

Cytotoxic and genotoxic stresses, when the damage they cause cannot be repaired, result in mitochondrial cell death, such that irreparably damaged cells are removed before inciting bystander inflammation and before passing mutations on to subsequent generations of cells. This form of intrinsic cell death is unique in many ways from strictly defined apoptosis, i.e., the type that occurs during development, and may stem from a limited form of ATP depletion without frank necrosis. Abrupt collapse of mitochondrial membrane potential (MMP) through opening of the mitochondrial permeability transition pore (MPTP) and subsequent mitochondrial permeabilization are seminal events in oxidative injury- and DNA damage-induced cell death.1-3 The MPTP is opened by low intracellular pH, relative paucity of ATP, Ca2+ overload,4 and perhaps by the balance between pro-apoptotic and anti-apoptotic members of the Bcl-2 family of proteins, which interact with VDAC1 at the outer mitochondrial membrane.2,5-7

Controversy exists about the role of specific components of the MPTP in all forms of intrinsic apoptosis8-10 and about the details by which the mitochondrial pores open or remain open in apoptotic versus necrotic cell death from different stimuli.11 Nonetheless, most researchers agree that the stress-induced form of apoptosis is triggered by release of cytochrome c into cytoplasm from the mitochondrial intermembrane space. After its release, cytochrome c complexes with Apaf-1 (apoptosis protease activating factor) and cleaves a series of caspases that ultimately dismantle the cell by breaking down cell-cell contacts, cytoskeletal elements and nuclear structures.12,13

One paradigm holds that the mitochondrial apoptosis cascade is initiated when cytochrome c exits through a pore composed of VDAC1, the inner mitochondrial membrane protein ANT (adenine nucleotide translocator), and the inner mitochondrial membrane protein cyclophilin D (cypD).14-17 Other proposed mechanisms suggest that the pore in VDAC is too narrow to allow passage of a macromolecule as large as cytochrome c, that the primary function of VDAC in pro-apoptotic conditions is to regulate ATP flux by closure rather than by opening, and that cytochrome c release from the mitochondrial intermembrane space occurs as part of a general rupture of the outer membrane.8 Recent studies have confirmed the importance of cypD in cell death after ischemia-reperfusion/oxidative injury (necrosis more than classically defined apoptosis).18-21 The precise roles of ANT and VDAC in the MPTP are still unsettled, but abundant evidence suggests that they are involved fundamentally in initiating mitochondrial cell death.10,22,23 To date, however, little is known about how VDAC1, the outermost barrier in allowing the crucial event of cytochrome c release, is itself modified such that it initiates the mitochondrial cell death cascade.

Hexokinases have been shown to protect cells against mitochondrial apoptosis, through direct interaction with VDAC1 to keep the channel closed and to prevent cytochrome c leakage from mitochondria.24,25 Hexokinases are thought to allow continuous ATP flux, such that mitochondria are provided energy continuously for phosphorylation of glucose,26 but they do not phosphorylate VDAC. The catalytic subunit of cAMP dependent protein kinase A can phosphorylate VDAC crudely purified from rat liver, so VDAC has been suggested, but not rigorously proven, to be a target of PKA.27 Indirect methods have also been used to suggest that VDAC may be phosphorylated by PKC-ε.26 A specific kinase that phosphorylates VDAC1 and that regulates its open-closed status has thus been sought, but not yet found or reported.

In Aspergillus nidulans, the protein kinase NIMA (never in mitosis A) keeps chromosome transmission to daughter cells faithful. Without functional NIMA, fungi arrest at the G2/M phase transition.28,29 If induced to bypass this arrest, NIMA mutant cells undergo faulty mitosis, forming bipolar spindles and failing to separate nucleoplasm completely when spindle poles attempted to separate.29 Mammalian NIMA-related kinases (Nrks or Neks), at least ten of which have been identified, may have similar functions.30,31 The first mammalian NIMA-related kinase, Nek1, is known to have dual serine-threonine and tyrosine kinase activity in vitro,32 but neither its precise functions nor its cellular substrates are known. We have shown that Nek1 is involved as a kinase early in the response to DNA damage, that Nek1-deficient cells are unusually sensitive to the lethal effects of DNA damage,33 and that deficient damage responses in Nek1-/- cells result in unrepaired DNA damage after injury, faulty mitoses and excessive chromosome breaks.34 Another group has used yeast two-hybrid screening with the coiled-coil domains of human Nek1 as bait and identified proteins involved in the DNA damage response (ZBRK1, MRE11, 53BP1) and in apoptosis signaling (14-3-3) as potential substrates for Nek1.35 None of these substrates, however, has been verified to date. Here we demonstrate that VDAC1 is a bona fide and biologically important substrate of Nek1. In the basal state and in response to injury that includes DNA damage, Nek1 phosphorylates VDAC1 to limit mitochondrial cell death.

Rabbit polyclonal anti-Nek1 antibodies recognized only a single protein on Western blot (Fig. 1B), and were suitable for use in immunocytochemistry (Suppl. Fig. S1). We thereby identified VDAC1, a key protein involved in the mitochondrial events in cell death, as a Nek1-interacting protein. To eliminate the possibility that the yeast two-hybrid interaction was artifactual, we confirmed the specificity of the interaction between VDAC1 and Nek1 by two other methods: in vitro using GST pull-down assays (Fig. 3A) and in cells by reciprocal co-immunoprecipitation (Fig. 3B).Nek1 phosphorylates VDAC1 on serine 193We next examined the potential of Nek1 to act as a kinase for phosphorylation of VDAC1. The structure of the VDAC1 channel in the outer mitochondrial membrane has recently been defined by combining x-ray crystallography and NMR spectroscopy.39,40 Human VDAC1 has a barrel structure composed of 19 β-strands and an α-helix located horizontally midway through the pore. Only one of the three potential NIMA/Nek phosphorylation sites41,42 in human VDAC1, serine 193, is located at the site precisely inside the barrel wall, at a position readily accessible from the cytoplasmic side (Fig. 3C). This site is within a putative cytoplasmic protein binding motif thought to be critical for regulating the opening and closing of the barrel structure of the channel.9,43 We mutated this serine 193 residue, in separate constructs, to alanine (S193A), such that it could no longer be phosphorylated, or to glutamic acid (S193E), to mimic constitutive phosphorylation. We then placed the mutants into the same vectors used to express wild type VDAC1. Whereas Nek1 immune complexes could specifically phosphorylate wild type GST-VDAC1, they could not phosphorylate the GST-VDAC1-S193A mutant (Fig. 3D). In addition, the Nek1-K33A mutant, which when overexpressed caused cells to die, could not phosphorylate wild type VDAC1 when immunoprecipitated from the same cells (Fig. 3D, lanes 8 and 9). This result provides further evidence that phosphorylation of VDAC1 is primarily due to Nek1 rather than due to another kinase.To determine further whether the S193 site in VDAC1 is an in vivo target of Nek1, we synthesized a peptide (VS1) corresponding to phosphorylated S193 and its surrounding residues, and then generated antibodies that specifically recognize the S193-phosphorylated form of VDAC1 (Fig. 3E). In the same BSC1 cells used to show the lethal effects of expression of the Nek1-K33A kinase-dead mutant, the specific S193 phosphorylation of VDAC1 was lost soon after the mutant protein was expressed and well before the cells died (Fig. 3F). This result confirmed that the kinase-dead Nek1-K33A mutant does indeed function in a dominant negative manner. We also noted that Nek1 phosphorylates VDAC1 on S193 in the basal state, i.e., before any cellular injury (Fig. 3F, lane 4).To assure that the loss of VDAC1-S193 phosphorylation immediately prior to mitochondrial cell death is not a peripheral consequence of ectopic expression of the dominant negative mutant Nek1 construct rather than a consequence of specific inhibition of Nek1 activity, we also examined the effects of silencing endogenous Nek1 expression by siRNA (Fig. 3G). Partial inhibition of Nek1 expression with a single copy of the specific Nek1 siRNA oligonucleotide (Nek1-1x, lane 2) did not totally prevent the phosphorylation of endogenous VDAC1 on S193, but more complete inhibition of Nek1 expression with a construct containing four tandem repeats of the same oligonucleotide did (lane 3). The so-called kat (kidneys-anemia-testis) and kat2J mouse strains both develop pleiotropic defects, most notably growth retardation and polycystic kidney disease (PKD), and they age and die prematurely.44 The kat2J mutation results in early truncation of Nek1, eliminating all of the coiled-coil domains as well as part of the N-terminal kinase domain.45 Cells from Nek1kat2J-/- mice express no detectable Nek1 protein (Suppl. Fig. S1A) and are hypersensitive to the lethal effects of DNA-damage.33,34 Compared to wild type cells, in Nek1-/- cells the same dose of UV radiation resulted in much faster disappearance of VDAC1 phospho-S193, again well before the cells died (Fig. 4A).Our data indicate that Nek1 is a key kinase for keeping VDAC1 phosphorylated in the basal state. Although other redundant kinases must also phosphorylate the same serine 193 residue, in the absence of Nek1 they may not be sufficient to keep VDAC1 phosphorylated and properly regulated when the cells are stressed. In the absence of any stresses other than adenoviral infection, for example, expression of the dominant negative Nek1-K33A mutant caused wild type cells to die within 48 h (Suppl. Fig. S2). Similar expression of the K33A mutant in Nek1-/- cells from sex-matched littermates caused cells to die more quickly under the same conditions. The dominant negative Nek1-K33A mutant thus must also inhibit the other, redundant kinases as well as Nek1 itself, perhaps by binding to and preventing phosphorylation of VDAC1 at S193 or by specifically activating a phosphatase. The redundant kinase or kinases and the potentially affected phosphatase remain unknown at present.Transfection of unphosphorylatable, GFP-tagged VDAC1-S193A into HeLa cells also resulted rapidly in cell death (within 24 h) (Fig. 4B—D and Suppl. Fig. S3). Expression of the VDAC1-S193E mutant, in contrast, did not cause the same cells to die rapidly. GFP alone had no effect whatsoever and wild type GFP-VDAC1 resulted in only a minor increase in cell death 24 h later. Cell death after expression of wild type GFP-VDAC1 probably occurred because the overexpressed VDAC1 could not be phosphorylated by the limited amount of endogenous Nek1 or other kinases with potentially redundant functions.Taken together, the results presented thus far show that S193 in VDAC1 is indeed the bona fide target of phosphorylation by Nek1 in vivo, and that the S193 phosphorylation is important for keeping the cells alive after the stress of a genotoxic insult. We reasoned next that if the Nek1-dependent phosphorylation of VDAC1 on serine 193 is biologically important, then ectopic overexpression of the constitutively closed VDAC1-S193E mutant should guard against Nek1-/- cells after injury that includes DNA damage. We confirmed this hypothesis directly by demonstrating that the VDAC1-S193E mutant does indeed protect cultured Nek1-/- cells from dying quickly after DNA-damaging UV irradiation, much better than wild type VDAC1 does (Fig. 4E).Nek1 deficiency results in exaggerated loss of mitochondrial membrane potential (MMP)To determine if the absence of functional Nek1 results in abnormalities in mitochondrial permeability, we examined Nek1-/- cells for loss of normal MMP using the cationic dye JC-1. When compared with otherwise identical wild type cells from sex-matched littermates, Nek1-/- cells die at greater rates (data not shown) and show greater loss of MMP in the basal state (Fig. 5A and B). Even more dramatic differences in mitochondrial permeability were noted after the cells were induced to die by DNA-damaging UV irradiation (Fig. 5B). Cells heterozygous for the Nek1kat2J mutation and expressing intermediate amounts of functional Nek1 protein were intermediately sensitive to UV radiation: the proportion that lost MMP soon after injury was close to that of the wild type cells, but by 6 hours after injury MMP was lost in the majority of Nek1+/- cells, just as it was in identically treated Nek1-/- cells. Adenovirus-mediated re-expression of human Nek1 restored the wild type pattern and protected against UV radiation-induced loss of MMP in Nek1-/- cells (Fig. 5B), proving that Nek1 deficiency was indeed the reason for the excessive mitochondrial membrane permeability.Using embryonic fibroblasts from vdac1-/- mice (Fig. 5C), which express normal or slightly high amounts of Nek1 (Fig. 5D), we were also able to test the effects of wild type and mutant VDAC1 on loss of MMP and on cell death, without the complicating effects of endogenous Vdac1. A relatively high percentage of these vdac1-/- cells lost their normal MMP after the stress of transfection alone, and ectopic expression of either wild-type VDAC1 or VDAC1-S193E mutant protected against the aberrant mitochondrial permeability (Fig. 5E). After UV irradiation, almost all of the vdac1-/- cells mock transfected or transfected with the GFP vector alone lost normal MMP within 1.5 hours. Expression of the VDAC1-S193E mutant, however, afforded much better protection against this loss of MMP (Fig. 5E) and against subsequent cell death (Fig. 6) than did expression of wild type VDAC1. Expression of the VDAC1-S193A mutant, in contrast, resulted in the death of all vdac1-/- cells within 24 h (Fig. 6A and B). The protection from UV-induced cell death by the constitutively closed VDAC1-S193E mutant, however, was ultimately only transient. By 72 h after exposure to UV (96 h after transfection), even GFP-VDAC1-S193E-reconstituted, vdac1-/- cells were almost all dead (Fig. 6C). This transient rather than complete and lasting rescue from injury-induced cell death was expected, however, since a constitutively closed VDAC1 channel should not control passage of anions, ATP and cytochrome c like a physiologically regulated one. Just as too little or too much VDAC1 results in cell death,46 both open and constitutively closed VDAC1 does also, albeit in different time frames after expression.

Cells easily lose mitochondrial membrane potential in the absence of functional Nek1. Our data show that in the basal state, Nek1 phosphorylates VDAC1 and seems to keep MMP intact. Recombinant Nekl adenovirusesAdenoviruses were generated using an AdEasy Vector System (Stratagene). Briefly, wild type Nekl or Nekl-K33A mutant was fused first in-frame to GFP in pEGFP-Cl vector (Invitrogen, LaJolla, CA). Multiplicities of infection of approximately 30 viral particles per cell were used to obtain efficient gene transduction.

Mitochondrial membrane potential assayA kit from Stratagene was used to measure the loss of normal mitochondrial membrane potential (MMP). Briefly, cells were cultured on coverslips and incubated with centrifuged fluorescent cationic dye, JC-1 (5,5′,6,6′-tetrachloro-l,l′,3,3′-tetraethylbenzimidazolylcarbo-cyanine iodide), for 25 minutes before observation at green and red emission wavelengths using a Ziess AxioPlan II fluorescence microscope. In healthy, non-apoptotic cells, the lipophilic dye, bearing a delocalized positive charge, accumulates in the mitochondrial matrix and stains the mitochondria bright red; cells in which MMP has collapsed keep the dye diffusely in the cytoplasm, where it fluoresces green.53,54
