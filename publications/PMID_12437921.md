# A thiol peroxidase is an H2O2 receptor and redox-transducer in gene activation.
**Authors:** Delaunay A, Pflieger D, Barrault MB, Vinh J, Toledano MB
**Journal:** Cell (2002)
**DOI:** [10.1016/s0092-8674(02)01048-6](https://doi.org/10.1016/s0092-8674(02)01048-6)

## Abstract

1. Cell. 2002 Nov 15;111(4):471-81. doi: 10.1016/s0092-8674(02)01048-6.

A thiol peroxidase is an H2O2 receptor and redox-transducer in gene activation.

Delaunay A(1), Pflieger D, Barrault MB, Vinh J, Toledano MB.

Author information:
(1)Laboratoire Stress Oxydants et Cancers, SBGM, DBJC, CEA-Saclay, 91191
Gif-sur-Yvette, Cedex, France.

The Yap1 transcription factor regulates hydroperoxide homeostasis in S.
cerevisiae. Yap1 is activated by oxidation when hydroperoxide levels increase.
We show that Yap1 is not directly oxidized by hydroperoxide. We identified the
glutathione peroxidase (GPx)-like enzyme Gpx3 as a second component of the
pathway, serving the role of sensor and transducer of the hydroperoxide signal
to Yap1. When oxidized by H2O2, Gpx3 Cys36 bridges Yap1 Cys598 by a disulfide
bond. This intermolecular disulfide bond is then resolved into a Yap1
intramolecular disulfide bond, the activated form of the regulator. Thioredoxin
turns off the pathway by reducing both sensor and regulator. These data reveal a
redox-signaling function for a GPx-like enzyme and elucidate a eukaryotic
hydroperoxide-sensing mechanism. Gpx3 is thus a hydroperoxide receptor and
redox-transducer.
