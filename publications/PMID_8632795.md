Secreted signalling molecules provide cells with positional information that
organizes long-range pattern during the development of multicellular animals.
Evidence is presented that localized expression of Decapentaplegic instructs
cells about their position along the anterior-posterior axis of the Drosophila
wing in two distinct ways. One mechanism is based on the local concentration of
the secreted protein; the other is based on the ability of the cells to retain
an instruction received at an earlier time when their progenitors were in close
proximity to the signal. Both mechanisms are involved in axis formation.
