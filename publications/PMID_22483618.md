Methylation of lysine 4 (K4) within histone H3 has been linked to active
transcription and is removed by LSD1 and the JmjC domain-containing proteins by
amino-oxidation or hydroxylation, respectively. Here, we describe the
deamination catalyzed by Lysyl oxidase-like 2 protein (LOXL2) as an
unconventional chemical mechanism for H3K4 modification. Infrared spectroscopy
and mass spectrometry analyses demonstrated that recombinant LOXL2 specifically
deaminates trimethylated H3K4. Moreover, LOXL2 activity is linked with the
transcriptional control of CDH1 gene by regulating H3K4me3 deamination. These
results reveal another H3 modification and provide a different mechanism for
H3K4 modification.
