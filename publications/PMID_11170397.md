# Identification and characterization of novel tPA- and plasminogen-binding sites within fibrin(ogen) alpha C-domains.
**Authors:** Tsurupa G, Medved L
**Journal:** Biochemistry (2001)
**DOI:** [10.1021/bi001789t](https://doi.org/10.1021/bi001789t)

## Abstract

1. Biochemistry. 2001 Jan 23;40(3):801-8. doi: 10.1021/bi001789t.

Identification and characterization of novel tPA- and plasminogen-binding sites
within fibrin(ogen) alpha C-domains.

Tsurupa G(1), Medved L.

Author information:
(1)Department of Biochemistry, The American Red Cross Holland Laboratory,
Rockville, Maryland 20855, USA.

Molecular defects in the alphaC-domains of some abnormal fibrinogens have been
associated with impaired fibrin-mediated activation of plasminogen (Pg) by its
activator tPA, suggesting the involvement of these domains in fibrinolysis. To
test this suggestion, we expressed in E. coli the alphaC-fragment (residues
Aalpha221-610) corresponding to the entire alphaC-domain as well as its NH(2)-
and COOH-terminal halves (residues Aalpha221-391 and Aalpha392-610) and tested
their effects on activation of Pg and their interaction with Pg and tPA. When
the activation was monitored by cleavage of a chromogenic substrate with newly
formed plasmin, the reaction was much more efficient in the presence of the
alphaC-fragment. This stimulation was abolished upon digestion of the
alphaC-fragment with plasmin. In surface plasmon resonance experiments, both tPA
and Pg bound to the immobilized alphaC-fragment with K(d)s of 33 and 32 nM,
respectively. Similar results were obtained by ELISA. This binding occurred via
independent sites since saturating amounts of Pg did not prevent binding of tPA
and vice versa. Both sites were localized in the COOH-terminal half of the
alphaC-domain since the Aalpha392-610 fragment bound both tPA and Pg and was an
effective stimulator whereas Aalpha221-391 was inactive. These results indicate
that the fibrinogen alphaC-domains contain novel high-affinity tPA- and
Pg-binding sites that play an important role in the regulation of fibrinolysis.
