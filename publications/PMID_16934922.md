# The TCL1 oncoprotein binds the RNase PH domains of the PNPase exoribonuclease without affecting its RNA degrading activity.
**Authors:** French SW, Dawson DW, Chen HW, Rainey RN, Sievers SA, Balatoni CE, Wong L, Troke JJ, Nguyen MT, Koehler CM, Teitell MA
**Journal:** Cancer Lett (2007)
**DOI:** [10.1016/j.canlet.2006.07.006](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.canlet.2006.07.006)

## Abstract

1. Cancer Lett. 2007 Apr 18;248(2):198-210. doi: 10.1016/j.canlet.2006.07.006.
Epub  2006 Aug 28.

The TCL1 oncoprotein binds the RNase PH domains of the PNPase exoribonuclease
without affecting its RNA degrading activity.

French SW(1), Dawson DW, Chen HW, Rainey RN, Sievers SA, Balatoni CE, Wong L,
Troke JJ, Nguyen MT, Koehler CM, Teitell MA.

Author information:
(1)Department of Pathology and Laboratory Medicine, David Geffen School of
Medicine at UCLA, Los Angeles, CA 90095-1732, USA.

TCL1 is an AKT kinase coactivator that, when dysregulated, initiates mature
lymphocyte malignancies in humans and transgenic mice. While TCL1 augments AKT
pathway signaling, additional TCL1 interacting proteins that may contribute to
cellular homeostasis or transformation are lacking. Here, an exoribonuclease,
PNPase, was identified in a complex with TCL1. The AKT interaction domain on
TCL1 bound either RNase PH repeat domain of PNPase without influencing its RNA
degrading activity, which was compatible with predicted docking models for a
TCL1-PNPase complex. Our data provide a novel protein interaction for mammalian
PNPase that may impact TCL1 mediated transformation.
