Infantile spasms syndrome (ISs) is characterized by clinical spasms with ictal
electrodecrement, usually occurring before the age of 1 year and frequently
associated with cognitive impairment. Etiology is widely heterogeneous, the
cause remaining elusive in 40% of patients. We searched for de novo mutations in
10 probands with ISs and their parents using whole-exome sequencing (WES).
Patients had neither consanguinity nor family history of epilepsy. Common causes
of ISs were excluded by brain magnetic resonance imaging (MRI), metabolic
screening, array-comparative genomic hybridization (CGH) and testing for
mutations in CDKL5, STXBP1, and for ARX duplications. We found a probably
pathogenic mutation in four patients. Missense mutations in SCN2A (p.Leu1342Pro)
and KCNQ2 (p.Ala306Thr) were found in two patients with no history of epilepsy
before the onset of ISs. The p.Asn107Ser missense mutation of ALG13 had been
previously reported in four females with ISs. The fourth mutation was an
in-frame deletion (p.Phe110del) in NR2F1, a gene whose mutations cause
intellectual disability, epilepsy, and optic atrophy. In addition, we found a
possibly pathogenic variant in KIF3C that encodes a kinesin expressed during
neural development. Our results confirm that WES improves significantly the
diagnosis yield in patients with sporadic ISs.
