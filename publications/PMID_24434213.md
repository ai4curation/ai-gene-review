Erratum in
    Cancer Cell. 2026 Aug 10;44(8):1705-1709. doi: 10.1016/j.ccell.2026.06.014.

Current antiangiogenic therapy is limited by its cytostatic nature and systemic
side effects. To address these limitations, we have unveiled the role of RhoJ,
an endothelial-enriched Rho GTPase, during tumor progression. RhoJ blockade
provides a double assault on tumor vessels by both inhibiting tumor angiogenesis
and disrupting the preformed tumor vessels through the activation of the
RhoA-ROCK (Rho kinase) signaling pathway in tumor endothelial cells,
consequently resulting in a functional failure of tumor vasculatures. Moreover,
enhanced anticancer effects were observed when RhoJ blockade was employed in
concert with a cytotoxic chemotherapeutic agent, angiogenesis-inhibiting agent,
or vascular-disrupting agent. These results identify RhoJ blockade as a
selective and effective therapeutic strategy for targeting tumor vasculature
with minimal side effects.
