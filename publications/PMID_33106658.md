Erratum in
    Nat Struct Mol Biol. 2020 Dec;27(12):1209. doi: 10.1038/s41594-020-00538-6.

The molecular function of Atg9, the sole transmembrane protein in the 
autophagosome-forming machinery, remains unknown. Atg9 colocalizes with Atg2 at 
the expanding edge of the isolation membrane (IM), where Atg2 receives 
phospholipids from the endoplasmic reticulum (ER). Here we report that yeast and 
human Atg9 are lipid scramblases that translocate phospholipids between outer 
and inner leaflets of liposomes in vitro. Cryo-EM of fission yeast Atg9 reveals 
a homotrimer, with two connected pores forming a path between the two membrane 
leaflets: one pore, located at a protomer, opens laterally to the cytoplasmic 
leaflet; the other, at the trimer center, traverses the membrane vertically. 
Mutation of residues lining the pores impaired IM expansion and autophagy 
activity in yeast and abolished Atg9's ability to transport phospholipids 
between liposome leaflets. These results suggest that phospholipids delivered by 
Atg2 are translocated from the cytoplasmic to the luminal leaflet by Atg9, 
thereby driving autophagosomal membrane expansion.
