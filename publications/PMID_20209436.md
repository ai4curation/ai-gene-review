# Ecdysone signaling is required for proper organization and fluid secretion of stellate cells in the Malpighian tubules of Drosophila melanogaster.
**Authors:** Gautam NK, Tapadia MG
**Journal:** Int J Dev Biol (2010)
**DOI:** [10.1387/ijdb.092910ng](https://doi.org/10.1387/ijdb.092910ng)

## Abstract

1. Int J Dev Biol. 2010;54(4):635-42. doi: 10.1387/ijdb.092910ng.

Ecdysone signaling is required for proper organization and fluid secretion of
stellate cells in the Malpighian tubules of Drosophila melanogaster.

Gautam NK(1), Tapadia MG.

Author information:
(1)Department of Zoology, Banaras Hindu University, Varanasi, India.

Drosophila development is a tightly regulated process involving metamorphosis of
a relatively less mobile larva to a highly motile adult, triggered by secretion
of 20-hydroxyecdysone. Under the influence of ecdysone, most of the larval
tissues degenerate, while the imaginal cells differentiate and form adult
specific structures. Although the larval Malpighian tubules do not seem to be
affected by ecdysone during metamorphosis, we show that ecdysone signaling plays
an important role in the early development and functioning of Malpighian
tubules. Disruption of ecdysone receptor function, using targeted expression of
dominant negative ecdysone receptor in stellate cells, results in disruption of
organization of Malpighian tubules. The number of stellate cells is reduced in
such Malpighian tubules. Further, they get clustered rather than distributed in
their characteristic wild type pattern. We also demonstrate that expression of
Drosophila integrin protein (DRIP), an aquaporin responsible for trans-cellular
water transport, is also reduced in stellate cells when ecdysone signaling is
disrupted. Our results show that of the three ecdysone receptor isoforms, only
EcR-B2 rescues these phenotypes. A similar pattern of stellate cell clustering
and reduced expression of DRIP is observed in ecd(1), a temperature sensitive
mutant, under non-permissive conditions. These results suggest that ecdysone
signaling is required for proper patterning and functioning of stellate cells
and that EcR-B2 may be the primary isoform required for ecdysone signaling in
stellate cells.
