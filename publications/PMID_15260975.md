# A dimerized coiled-coil domain and an adjoining part of geminin interact with two sites on Cdt1 for replication inhibition.
**Authors:** Saxena S, Yuan P, Dhar SK, Senga T, Takeda D, Robinson H, Kornbluth S, Swaminathan K, Dutta A
**Journal:** Mol Cell (2004)
**DOI:** [10.1016/j.molcel.2004.06.045](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.molcel.2004.06.045)

## Abstract

1. Mol Cell. 2004 Jul 23;15(2):245-58. doi: 10.1016/j.molcel.2004.06.045.

A dimerized coiled-coil domain and an adjoining part of geminin interact with
two sites on Cdt1 for replication inhibition.

Saxena S(1), Yuan P, Dhar SK, Senga T, Takeda D, Robinson H, Kornbluth S,
Swaminathan K, Dutta A.

Author information:
(1)Department of Biochemistry and Molecular Genetics, University of Virginia,
Charlottesville 22908, USA.

Geminin is a cellular protein that associates with Cdt1 and inhibits Mcm2-7
loading during S phase. It prevents multiple cycles of replication per cell
cycle and prevents episome replication. It also directly inhibits the HoxA11
transcription factor. Here we report that geminin forms a parallel coiled-coil
homodimer with atypical residues in the dimer interface. Point mutations that
disrupt the dimerization abolish interaction with Cdt1 and inhibition of
replication. An array of glutamic acid residues on the coiled-coil domain
surface interacts with positive charges in the middle of Cdt1. An adjoining
region interacts independently with the N-terminal 100 residues of Cdt1. Both
interactions are essential for replication inhibition. The negative residues on
the coiled-coil domain and a different part of geminin are also required for
interaction with HoxA11. Therefore a rigid cylinder with negative surface
charges is a critical component of a bipartite interaction interface between
geminin and its cellular targets.
