# Prohormone convertase 2 enzymatic activity and its regulation in neuro-endocrine cells and tissues.
**Authors:** Li QL, Naqvi S, Shen X, Liu YJ, Lindberg I, Friedman TC
**Journal:** Regul Pept (2003)
**DOI:** [10.1016/s0167-0115(02)00207-0](https://doi.org/10.1016/s0167-0115(02)00207-0)

## Abstract

1. Regul Pept. 2003 Feb 28;110(3):197-205. doi: 10.1016/s0167-0115(02)00207-0.

Prohormone convertase 2 enzymatic activity and its regulation in neuro-endocrine
cells and tissues.

Li QL(1), Naqvi S, Shen X, Liu YJ, Lindberg I, Friedman TC.

Author information:
(1)Division of Endocrinology, Charles R. Drew University of Medicine and
Sciences, UCLA School of Medicine, 1731 E. 120th Street, Los Angeles, CA 90059,
USA.

We used the fluorometric substrate, pGlu-Arg-Thr-Lys-Arg-MCA and the C-terminal
peptide of human 7B2(155-185), a specific inhibitor of prohormone convertase 2
(PC2), to specifically measure the enzymatic activity of the prohormone
convertases, PC2. Using lysates from the pancreatic alpha cell line, alphaTC1-6
cells, which contain moderate levels of PC2 enzymatic activity, we determined
that the PC2 assay was linear with respect to time of incubation and protein
added and had a pH optimum of 5.5 and a calcium optimum of 2.5 mM. Rat pituitary
contained high levels of PC2 enzymatic activity, while the hypothalamus and
other brain regions contained moderate levels. This enzyme assay was used to
document that both mice null for PC2 as well as mice null for the PC2 cofactor,
7B2, had only trace levels of PC2 activity in various brain regions, while mice
heterozygous for these alleles had approximately half of the PC2 activity in
most brain regions. PC2 enzymatic activity and PC2 mRNA levels were somewhat
discordant suggesting that PC2 mRNA levels do not always reflect PC2 enzymatic
activity.
