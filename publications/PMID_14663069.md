# Use of a two-hybrid assay to study the assembly of a complex multicomponent protein machinery: bacterial septosome differentiation.
**Authors:** Di Lallo G, Fagioli M, Barionovi D, Ghelardini P, Paolozzi L
**Journal:** Microbiology (Reading) (2003)
**DOI:** [10.1099/mic.0.26580-0](https://doi.org/10.1099/mic.0.26580-0)

## Abstract

1. Microbiology (Reading). 2003 Dec;149(Pt 12):3353-3359. doi:
10.1099/mic.0.26580-0.

Use of a two-hybrid assay to study the assembly of a complex multicomponent
protein machinery: bacterial septosome differentiation.

Di Lallo G(1), Fagioli M(1), Barionovi D(1), Ghelardini P(2), Paolozzi L(1).

Author information:
(1)Dipartimento di Biologia, Università "Tor Vergata", Via della Ricerca
Scientifica, I-00133 Rome, Italy.
(2)Istituto di Biologia e Patologia Molecolari del CNR, Rome, Italy.

The ability of each of the nine Escherichia coli division proteins (FtsZ, FtsA,
ZipA, FtsK, FtsQ, FtsL, FtsW, FtsI, FtsN) to interact with itself and with each
of the remaining eight proteins was studied in 43 possible combinations of
protein pairs by the two-hybrid system previously developed by the authors'
group. Once the presumed interactions between the division proteins were
determined, a model showing their temporal sequence of assembly was developed.
This model agrees with that developed by other authors, based on the
co-localization sequence in the septum of the division proteins fused with GFP.
In addition, this paper shows that the authors' assay, which has already proved
to be very versatile in the study of prokaryotic and eukaryotic protein
interaction, is also a powerful instrument for an in vivo study of the
interaction and assembly of proteins, as in the case of septum division
formation.
