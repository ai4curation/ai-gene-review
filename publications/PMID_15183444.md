# Cyclic strain influences the expression of the vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) and the hypoxia inducible factor 1 alpha (HIF-1alpha) in tendon fibroblasts.
**Authors:** Petersen W, Varoga D, Zantop T, Hassenpflug J, Mentlein R, Pufe T
**Journal:** J Orthop Res (2004)
**DOI:** [10.1016/j.orthres.2003.11.009](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.orthres.2003.11.009)

## Abstract

1. J Orthop Res. 2004 Jul;22(4):847-53. doi: 10.1016/j.orthres.2003.11.009.

Cyclic strain influences the expression of the vascular endothelial growth
factor (VEGF) and the hypoxia inducible factor 1 alpha (HIF-1alpha) in tendon
fibroblasts.

Petersen W(1), Varoga D, Zantop T, Hassenpflug J, Mentlein R, Pufe T.

Author information:
(1)Department of Trauma, Hand, and Reconstructive Surgery, Westfaelische
Wilhelms University, 48149 Muenster, Germany. wolfpetersen@hotmail.com

Neovascularization is involved in beneficial and detrimental processes of tendon
pathology. We investigated the influence of repetitive motion on the expression
of the most important angiogenic factor, the vascular endothelial growth factor
(VEGF) in the 3T3 NIH fibroblast cell line and in cultures of rat Achilles
tendon fibroblasts. Monolayers of subconfluently grown cells were stretched in
rectangular silicone dishes with cyclic uniaxial movement. Strain was applied
over 24 h varying the frequency (0.5-1 Hz). Fibroblasts (3T3 fibroblasts and rat
Achilles tendon cultures) cultivated without the application of cyclic strain
released measurable VEGF amounts into their culture supernatants. Cyclic
stretching of the cells with a frequency of 1 Hz resulted in an increased
expression of VEGF. A low frequency (0.5 Hz) reduced VEGF expression to control
levels. RT PCR revealed VEGF 121 and VEGF 165 as the only splice forms that were
induced by cyclic stretching. Western blot experiments could further show that
cyclic stretching induced activation of the transcription factor HIF-1alpha.
These results demonstrate that mechanical factors are involved in the regulation
of VEGF expression in tendon tissue.
