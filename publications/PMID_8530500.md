# Interaction between G proteins and tyrosine kinases upon T cell receptor.CD3-mediated signaling.
**Authors:** Stanners J, Kabouridis PS, McGuire KL, Tsoukas CD
**Journal:** J Biol Chem (1995)
**DOI:** [10.1074/jbc.270.51.30635](https://doi.org/10.1074/jbc.270.51.30635)

## Abstract

1. J Biol Chem. 1995 Dec 22;270(51):30635-42. doi: 10.1074/jbc.270.51.30635.

Interaction between G proteins and tyrosine kinases upon T cell
receptor.CD3-mediated signaling.

Stanners J(1), Kabouridis PS, McGuire KL, Tsoukas CD.

Author information:
(1)Department of Biology and Molecular Biology Institute, San Diego State
University, California 92182, USA.

Engagement of the T cell receptor (TCR).CD3 complex results in the induction of
multiple intracellular events, with protein tyrosine kinases playing a pivotal
role in their initiation. Biochemical studies also exist suggesting the
involvement of heterotrimeric GTP-binding proteins (G proteins); however, the
functional consequence of this participation in TCR.CD3-mediated signaling is
unresolved. Here, we report TCR.CD3-mediated guanine nucleotide exchange among
the 42-kDa G protein alpha subunits of the G alpha q/11 family, their physical
association with CD3 epsilon, and the G alpha 11-dependent activation of
phospholipase C beta. Protein tyrosine kinase inhibitors, however, abrogate
TCR.CD3-mediated G protein activation. Quite interesting is the observation that
cells transfected with a function-deficient mutant of G alpha 11 display
diminished tyrosine phosphorylation of TCR.CD3 zeta and epsilon chains, as well
as ZAP-70, upon anti-CD3 antibody triggering. These data indicate the
involvement of the G alpha q/11 family in TCR.CD3 signaling at a step proximal
to the receptor and suggest a reciprocal regulation between tyrosine kinases and
G proteins in T cells.
