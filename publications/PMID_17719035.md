# Occurrence of more than one important source of ADPglucose linked to glycogen biosynthesis in Escherichia coli and Salmonella.
**Authors:** Morán-Zorzano MT, Alonso-Casajús N, Muñoz FJ, Viale AM, Baroja-Fernández E, Eydallin G, Pozueta-Romero J

## Abstract

1. FEBS Lett. 2007 Sep 18;581(23):4423-9. doi: 10.1016/j.febslet.2007.08.017.
Epub  2007 Aug 15.

Occurrence of more than one important source of ADPglucose linked to glycogen
biosynthesis in Escherichia coli and Salmonella.

Morán-Zorzano MT(1), Alonso-Casajús N, Muñoz FJ, Viale AM, Baroja-Fernández E,
Eydallin G, Pozueta-Romero J.

Author information:
(1)Instituto de Agrobiotecnología, (CSIC, UPNA, Gobierno de Navarra), Mutiloako
etorbidea zenbaki gabe, 31192 Mutiloabeti, Nafarroa, Spain.

To explore the possible occurrence of sources, other than GlgC, of ADPglucose
linked to bacterial glycogen biosynthesis we characterized Escherichia coli and
Salmonella DeltaglgCAP deletion mutants lacking the whole glycogen biosynthetic
machinery. These mutants displayed the expected glycogen-less phenotype but
accumulated ADPglucose. Importantly, DeltaglgCAP cells expressing the glycogen
synthase encoding glgA gene accumulated glycogen. Protein chromatographic
separation of crude extracts of DeltaglgCAP mutants and subsequent activity
measurement analyses revealed that these cells possess various proteins
catalyzing the conversion of glucose-1-phosphate into ADPglucose. Collectively
these findings show that enterobacteria possess more than one important source
of ADPglucose linked to glycogen biosynthesis.
