# Structure of the bacteriophage phi6 nucleocapsid suggests a mechanism for sequential RNA packaging.
**Authors:** Huiskonen JT, de Haas F, Bubeck D, Bamford DH, Fuller SD, Butcher SJ
**Journal:** Structure (2006)
**DOI:** [10.1016/j.str.2006.03.018](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.str.2006.03.018)

## Abstract

1. Structure. 2006 Jun;14(6):1039-48. doi: 10.1016/j.str.2006.03.018.

Structure of the bacteriophage phi6 nucleocapsid suggests a mechanism for
sequential RNA packaging.

Huiskonen JT(1), de Haas F, Bubeck D, Bamford DH, Fuller SD, Butcher SJ.

Author information:
(1)Institute of Biotechnology and Department of Biological and Environmental
Sciences, University of Helsinki, P.O. Box 65 (Viikinkaari 1), FIN-00014
Helsinki, Finland.

Bacteriophage phi6 is an enveloped dsRNA virus with a segmented genome. Phi6
specifically packages one copy of each of its three genome segments into a
preassembled polymerase complex. This leads to expansion of the polymerase
complex, minus and plus strand RNA synthesis, and assembly of the nucleocapsid.
The phi6 in vitro assembly and packaging system is a valuable model for dsRNA
virus replication. The structure of the nucleocapsid at 7.5 A resolution
presented here reveals the secondary structure of the two major capsid proteins.
Asymmetric P1 dimers organize as an inner T = 1 shell, and P8 trimers organize
as an outer T = 13 laevo shell. The organization of the P1 molecules in the
unexpanded and expanded polymerase complex suggests that the expansion is
accomplished by rigid body movements of the P1 monomers. This leads to exposure
of new potential RNA binding surfaces to control the sequential packaging of the
genome segments.
