    Cell Res. 2017 Nov;27(11):1304-1306. doi: 10.1038/cr.2017.119.

DNA-dependent protein kinase (DNA-PK) is a serine/threonine protein kinase
complex composed of a catalytic subunit (DNA-PKcs) and KU70/80 heterodimer bound
to DNA. DNA-PK holoenzyme plays a critical role in non-homologous end joining
(NHEJ), the major DNA repair pathway. Here, we determined cryo-electron
microscopy structure of human DNA-PK holoenzyme at 6.6 Å resolution. In the
complex structure, DNA-PKcs, KU70, KU80 and DNA duplex form a 650-kDa
heterotetramer with 1:1:1:1 stoichiometry. The N-terminal α-solenoid (∼2 800
residues) of DNA-PKcs adopts a double-ring fold and connects the catalytic core
domain of DNA-PKcs and KU70/80-DNA. DNA-PKcs and KU70/80 together form a
DNA-binding tunnel, which cradles ∼30-bp DNA and prevents sliding inward of
DNA-PKcs along with DNA duplex, suggesting a mechanism by which the broken DNA
end is protected from unnecessary processing. Structural and biochemical
analyses indicate that KU70/80 and DNA coordinately induce conformational
changes of DNA-PKcs and allosterically stimulate its kinase activity. We propose
a model for activation of DNA-PKcs in which allosteric signals are generated
upon DNA-PK holoenzyme formation and transmitted to the kinase domain through
N-terminal HEAT repeats and FAT domain of DNA-PKcs. Our studies suggest a
mechanism for recognition and protection of broken DNA ends and provide a
structural basis for understanding the activation of DNA-PKcs and
DNA-PK-mediated NHEJ pathway.
