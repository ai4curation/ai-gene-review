ACTA2 pathogenic variants altering arginine 179 cause childhood-onset strokes 
due to moyamoya disease (MMD)-like occlusions of the distal internal carotid 
arteries, but the mechanisms of pathogenesis are unknown and no preventive 
treatments exist. Here we show that Acta2R179C/+ smooth muscle cells (SMCs) fail 
to fully differentiate and maintain stem cell-like features, including increased 
migration and glycolytic flux compared to wildtype (WT) SMCs. Increasing 
mitochondrial respiration with nicotinamide riboside (NR) drives differentiation 
and decreases migration of Acta2R179C/+ SMCs. Carotid artery injury of 
Acta2SMC-R179C/+ mice leads to premature death, intraluminal SMC accumulation 
leading to MMD-like occlusive lesions, neurologic symptoms, and neuron loss, 
whereas injured WT mice have none of these phenotypes, and all are prevented by 
NR treatment in the Acta2SMC-R179C/+ mice. These data show that driving 
differentiation and quiescence of Acta2R179C/+ SMCs by altering cellular 
metabolism attenuates MMD-like disease in the Acta2SMC-R179C/+ mice, 
highlighting a role of immature and highly migratory SMCs in the pathogenesis of 
MMD.

Introduction

Moyamoya disease (MMD), a common cause of pediatric strokes, is characterized by occlusion of the distal internal carotid arteries (ICAs) and their branches and compensatory collateral vessel formation1. Cerebral ischemia and hemorrhagic stroke are complications of MMD2,3, with ischemic events (transient ischemic attack or ischemic infarcts) being the most common presentation3–5. The molecular pathogenesis of occlusion of the distal ICAs in MMD has not been delineated. Histology of the arterial occlusive lesions provides some insight by showing a lack of atherosclerotic features, such as macrophage foam cells and cholesterol and calcium deposition. Rather, arterial lumens are filled with neointimal cells that stain positive for smooth muscle cell (SMC) markers6,7. Genetic variants are a major risk factor for MMD and also provide insight into the molecular etiology of MMD8–10. Heterozygous pathogenic variants in ACTA2 predispose to thoracic aortic disease, and a subset of these variants also predispose to MMD-like disease11. ACTA2 is expressed in SMCs and not in endothelial cells, thus implicating SMCs in MMD pathogenesis. Cerebrovascular disease in these individuals is characterized by typical occlusive lesions in the distal ICAs, as well as unique features, including a lack of collateral vessels, dilatation of the proximal ICA, straightened cerebral arteries, and periventricular white matter hyperintensities12–14. De novo ACTA2 variants disrupting arginine 179 to any of six amino acids lead to Smooth Muscle Dysfunction Syndrome (SMDS), a childhood-onset condition characterized by MMD-like cerebrovascular disease and thoracic aortic disease, along with patent ductus arteriosus, pulmonary hypertension, aberrant lung development, fixed dilated pupils, gut malrotation, hypoperistalsis, and hypotonic bladder12,15,16. Similar to MMD lesions, involved arteries in SMDS patients have neointimal lesions filled with SMC marker-positive cells17.

ACTA2 encodes the most abundant protein in differentiated vascular SMCs, the SMC-specific isoform of α-actin (SMA)18–20. Monomers of SMA polymerize to form the thin filaments of SMC contractile units, and ACTA2 R179H disrupts SMA polymerization21. We engineered a mouse model with inducible insertion of Arg179Cys mutation into an Acta2 allele, and the mutation was introduced during development in SMCs, termed Acta2SMC-R179C/+ mice22. Single-cell RNA-sequencing (scRNA-seq) of ascending aortic tissue from these mice revealed that 67% of the SMCs have correct insertion of the mutation, which leads to two phenotypically distinct SMC clusters, wild-type (WT) SMCs and SMCs heterozygous for the Acta2 R179C mutation22. The mutant SMCs are incompletely differentiated based on decreased expression of SMC-specific markers (e.g., Myh11, Actg2, Tagln, and Cnn1) and increased expression and accessibility of Klf4 (based on in vitro ATAC-seq), a transcription factor associated with SMC de-differentiation and plasticity23,24. Explanted SMCs from Acta2SMC-R179C/+ ascending aortas (designated as Acta2R179C/+ SMCs) are uniformly heterozygous for the Acta2 R179C mutation based on RNA-sequencing and show reduced differentiation, continued expression of pluripotency markers, and increased proliferation and migration when compared to WT SMCs22,24. We identified a novel role of SMA in the nucleus that is critical for SMC differentiation, and mutant SMA with an altered R179 disrupts this nuclear function. We propose that the loss of functional nuclear SMA underlies the failure of Acta2R179C/+ SMCs to properly differentiate into contractile and quiescent SMCs24.

Undifferentiated stem cells rely on glycolysis rather than oxidative phosphorylation (OXPHOS) for the production of ATP and nucleotides25. Stem cells have immature mitochondria with poorly developed mitochondrial infrastructure, lower membrane potential, and reduced electron transport chain (ETC) function25–27. With differentiation, cells transition to more efficient ATP production through the mitochondrial ETC and OXPHOS27–29. Moreover, altering stem cell metabolism from glycolysis to OXPHOS can drive differentiation, and this metabolic shift is actually required for complete cellular differentiation and quiescence30–33. Here, we show that the lack of differentiation of Acta2R179C/+ SMCs is associated with high glycolytic flux and low OXPHOS, and exposure to nicotinamide riboside (NR) not only decreases glycolysis and increases OXPHOS but also drives differentiation and decreases migration. Furthermore, we demonstrate that 22% of Acta2SMC-R179C/+ mice die after left carotid artery ligation (LCAL) surgery, and surviving mice have neuronal cell loss and evidence of hypoxic brain injury, along with intraluminal MMD-like occlusive lesions in the injured carotid arteries, whereas the WT mice have no neuronal cell loss, no hypoxic brain injury, and patent carotid artery lumens. Thus, we have identified SMC phenotypic changes that lead to MMD-like carotid artery lesions in the Acta2SMC-R179C/+ mice, and treatment with NR prior to carotid artery injury prevents mortality and attenuates MMD-like lumen occlusions and hypoxic brain injury in the Acta2SMC-R179C/+ mice.

Seahorse analysis confirms that NR significantly boosts OXPHOS based on increases in basal, ATP-linked, and maximal OCR in the mutant SMCs (Figs. 2A and S2A). Lactate assays confirm that NR exposure also reduces the elevated anaerobic glycolytic activity in Acta2R179C/+ SMCs (Figs. 2B and S2B). NR treatment also increases differentiation in the Acta2R179C/+ SMCs based on increased levels of SMC contractile markers (Figs. 2C, D and S2C) and decreased migration in both WT and Acta2R179C/+ SMCs (Fig. 2E). NR treatment does not alter proliferation (Fig. 2F) or increase apoptosis or necrosis in Acta2R179C/+ or WT SMCs (Fig. S3A, B). Acta2R179C/+ SMCs were also exposed to media with galactose without glucose, along with low- and high-glucose media. The death rate was higher in female Acta2SMC-R179C/+ mice (n = 8/28, p = 0.012 vs. WT females) compared with male Acta2SMC-R179C/+ mice (n = 3/24, p = 0.09 vs. WT males) (Fig. 4A, B). Hypertension can adversely alter vascular remodeling, but we previously reported that Acta2SMC-R179C/+ are mildly hypotensive at 8 weeks of age22, and female Acta2SMC-R179C/+ mice continue to be hypotensive after LCAL (Fig. 4D). Male Acta2SMC-R179C/+ mice show a trend towards hypertension after LCAL, but insufficient power for this experiment prevents a statistically significant finding (Fig. 4D). Necropsy of mutant mice revealed no evidence of hemorrhagic stroke, aortic rupture, intramural hematoma, or gastrointestinal distension. However, the mutant mice on POD 1 and 2 exhibited behavioral deficits consistent with ischemic injury to the brain in the hippocampus and striatum, which we quantified using an established neurologic score scale for mice39,40. Female Acta2SMC-R179C/+ mice had significantly increased neurologic scores, indicating worsened neurobehavioral changes, on POD 1 post LCAL compared with WT mice, and both sexes had a trend toward increased scores on POD 2 (Fig. 4C). Mice displaying higher neurological deficit scores (score ≥ 3) died the following day, and we concluded that mice scoring ≥3 should be preemptively euthanized for tissue harvests and brain histologic analyses. To further assess cerebral ischemia, female WT and Acta2SMC-R179C/+ mice (n = 4–8 per group), regardless of neurologic score, were euthanized at POD 3 and subjected to TTC staining to identify cerebral infarcts. We confirmed that an Acta2SMC-R179C/+ mouse that scored 3 on the neurologic score scale showed a large infarct, whereas no ischemic injury was observed in WT mice (n = 4) (Fig. 4E). None of the Acta2SMC-R179C/+ mice scoring <3 had large infarcts. We demonstrate that decreasing glycolysis and increasing OXPHOS with NR treatment not only effectively shifts the metabolic profile of mutant SMCs towards OXPHOS but also drives differentiation by increasing the expression of SMC contractile genes and decreasing migration. Unexpectedly, the increased OXPHOS with NR exposure in the mutant SMCs is driven through increased levels and function of specific ETC proteins and not by increased mitochondrial biogenesis. Taken together, our in vitro evidence suggests a crucial link between SMC metabolism and cellular phenotype, and future work will focus on elucidating the molecular mechanisms linking metabolic shifts with SMC differentiation.

Acta2SMC-R179C/+ mice subjected to vascular injury by LCAL show MMD-like occlusive lesions, increased behavioral/neurological deficit, and significant neuron loss in the brain compared to WT mice, with female mice showing an increased susceptibility towards ischemia and mortality. This sex-specific vulnerability aligns with the female predominance of cerebrovascular disease in SMDS cases14. Due to a lower number of cerebrovascular events in male mice, in vivo administration of NR was pursued in female mice, which confirmed that NR treatment significantly prevents deaths, neuron loss, and occlusive lesions in injured carotid arteries in the Acta2SMC-R179C/+ mice post LCAL. MMD-like lesions are elicited in Acta2SMC-R179C/+ mice post LCAL despite mosaicism for the mutation, with 67% of SMCs harboring the variant22. SMCs in atherosclerotic plaques arise from the migration and clonal expansion of a few medial SMCs53. Thus, the occluded arteries in our mouse model may be derived from a small subset of medial SMCs primed to migrate into the lumen and then proliferate, and given the dramatic increase in migration of the Acta2R179C/+ SMCs, it is possible that the majority of the SMCs that enter the neointima will be mutant cells. These observations have implications for gene therapy that use genome editing technology to correct the ACTA2 p.Arg179 variants in SMCs. Specifically, this approach may not prevent MMD cerebrovascular occlusive lesions unless most, if not all, the mutant SMCs are correctly edited.

The distal ICA lesions in SMDS patients are similar in both location and histology to MMD lesions, whether idiopathic or associated with pathogenic variants in other genes7,14,54. These similarities raise the possibility that altered SMC phenotype and metabolism may contribute more generally to MMD pathogenesis. MMD was originally described in Japan, in part due to a founder genetic variant (RNF213, p.Arg4810Lys) responsible for a 10-fold higher incidence of MMD in Japan and other East Asian countries compared with the United States55,56. We also identified de novo pathogenic variants in CHD4 and SETD5 in children with MMD who also had congenital disorders10 and inherited pathogenic variants in YY1AP1 as a cause of MMD-like cerebrovascular disease60. All of these genes are involved in chromatin remodeling, raising the possibility that disrupted SMC differentiation may contribute to MMD in these cases.

We defined an altered phenotype of the Acta2R179C/+ SMCs that is characterized by incomplete differentiation and the retention of stem cell features, including increased glycolysis and migration. Mouse scRNA-seq and analysisTissues were isolated from 8-week-old female Acta2SMC-R179C/+ and WT mouse littermates (n = 5 mice per genotype) and digested for 1.5 h at 37 °C in collagenase type II (Worthington), collagenase type XI (Millipore Sigma), hyaluronidase (Millipore Sigma), and elastase (Worthington) to obtain single-cell suspensions76. Viable single cells were detected and collected using flow cytometry, and cells were pooled to make one sample per genotype. Barcoded cDNA was generated using a Chromium Single Cell 3′ v2 Reagent Kit (10x Genomics). This step was followed by cDNA amplification, truncation, and library preparation. A NovaSeq 6000 Next Generation Sequencing system (Illumina) was used to perform sequencing at the Baylor College of Medicine Single Cell Genomics Core. Data files from previously reported scRNA-seq data from ascending aortic/transverse arch tissue from six mice were analyzed. Raw sequencing data were demultiplexed and aligned to the mm10 mouse genome using the CellRanger v.6.0.0 scRNA analysis pipeline (10X Genomics). Data from control and Acta2SMC-R179C/+ were merged into a single object and analyzed in parallel using the Seurat package (v.4.1.0) in R. Following standard quality control metrics to remove doublets and debris, data were normalized, scaled, and regressed to read depth. Principal component analysis, nonlinear dimensional reduction, and unsupervised clustering at a pre-set resolution of 0.3 were performed. SMC subsets were selected manually for downstream analyses. Differential gene expression testing was performed in Seurat using the “FindMarkers” function to perform Wilcoxon rank-sum testing. Genes with an average [log2Fold-change(FC)] > 0.25 and adjusted p value < 0.05 were considered significant. Raw and processed sequencing data are available through the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) repository (GEO accession # GSE201091). scRNA-seq data were analyzed in the Seurat package using “sctransform” based normalization of the control and Acta2SMC-R179C/+ samples, followed by cell clustering with the resolution parameter set at 0.3.
