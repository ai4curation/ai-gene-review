# Human Trypanosoma evansi infection linked to a lack of apolipoprotein L-I.
**Authors:** Vanhollebeke B, Truc P, Poelvoorde P, Pays A, Joshi PP, Katti R, Jannin JG, Pays E
**Journal:** N Engl J Med (2006)
**DOI:** [10.1056/NEJMoa063265](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa063265)

## Abstract

1. N Engl J Med. 2006 Dec 28;355(26):2752-6. doi: 10.1056/NEJMoa063265.

Human Trypanosoma evansi infection linked to a lack of apolipoprotein L-I.

Vanhollebeke B(1), Truc P, Poelvoorde P, Pays A, Joshi PP, Katti R, Jannin JG,
Pays E.

Author information:
(1)Laboratory of Molecular Parasitology, Institut de Biologie et de Médecine
Moléculaires, Université Libre de Bruxelles, Gosselies, Belgium.

Humans have innate immunity against Trypanosoma brucei brucei that is known to
involve apolipoprotein L-I (APOL1). Recently, a case of T. evansi infection in a
human was identified in India. We investigated whether the APOL1 pathway was
involved in this occurrence. The serum of the infected patient was found to have
no trypanolytic activity, and the finding was linked to the lack of APOL1, which
was due to frameshift mutations in both APOL1 alleles. Trypanolytic activity was
restored by the addition of recombinant APOL1. The lack of APOL1 explained the
patient's infection with T. evansi.
