Dysfunction of cilia is associated with common genetic disorders termed
ciliopathies. Knowledge on the interaction networks of ciliary proteins is
therefore key for understanding the processes that are underlying these severe
diseases and the mechanisms of ciliogenesis in general. Cep104 has recently been
identified as a key player in the regulation of cilia formation. Using a
combination of sequence analysis, biophysics, and x-ray crystallography, we
obtained new insights into the domain architecture and interaction network of
the Cep104 protein. We solved the crystal structure of the tumor overexpressed
gene (TOG) domain, identified Cep104 as a novel tubulin-binding protein, and
biophysically characterized the interaction of Cep104 with CP110, Cep97,
end-binding (EB) protein, and tubulin. Our results represent a solid platform
for the further investigation of the microtubule-EB-Cep104-tubulin-CP110-Cep97
network of proteins. Ultimately, such studies should be of importance for
understanding the process of cilia formation and the mechanisms underlying
different ciliopathies.
