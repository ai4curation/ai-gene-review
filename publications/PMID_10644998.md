# EB3, a novel member of the EB1 family preferentially expressed in the central nervous system, binds to a CNS-specific APC homologue.
**Authors:** Nakagawa H, Koyama K, Murata Y, Morito M, Akiyama T, Nakamura Y
**Journal:** Oncogene (2000)
**DOI:** [10.1038/sj.onc.1203308](https://doi.org/10.1038/sj.onc.1203308)

## Abstract

1. Oncogene. 2000 Jan 13;19(2):210-6. doi: 10.1038/sj.onc.1203308.

EB3, a novel member of the EB1 family preferentially expressed in the central
nervous system, binds to a CNS-specific APC homologue.

Nakagawa H(1), Koyama K, Murata Y, Morito M, Akiyama T, Nakamura Y.

Author information:
(1)Division of Clinical Genetics, Biomedical Research Center, Osaka University
Medical School, Osaka 565-0871, Japan.

APCL, a homologue of the adenomatous polyposis coli (APC) tumor suppressor, can
deplete cytoplasmic beta-catenin like APC. However, as its biological function
remains unclear, we have been using a yeast two-hybrid system to search for
proteins that associate with its carboxyl region. Among several cDNA clones we
isolated from a fetal-brain cDNA library as candidates, six included an
identical sequence with significant homology to EB1, a protein known to bind to
APC. The full-length cDNA of this novel homologue of EB1, named EB3, encoded a
protein of 282 amino acids with 54% identity to EB1, and it was expressed
preferentially in brain tissue on Northern blots. Confocal microscopy
demonstrated that exogenous EB3, like EB1, is associated with the cytoplasmic
microtubule network. Moreover, in these experiments EB3 and APCL appeared
together in the perinucleus and the cytoplasmic microtubule network. Since APCL
is also expressed highly and specifically in the central nervous system,
APCL-EB3 interaction may be specific to the CNS, possibly involving stability
and/or extension of microtubules during neuritogenesis.
