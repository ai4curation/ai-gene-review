# Molecular identification and characterization of novel members of the human organic anion transporter (OATP) family.
**Authors:** Tamai I, Nezu J, Uchino H, Sai Y, Oku A, Shimane M, Tsuji A
**Journal:** Biochem Biophys Res Commun (2000)
**DOI:** [10.1006/bbrc.2000.2922](https://doi.org/10.1006/bbrc.2000.2922)

## Abstract

1. Biochem Biophys Res Commun. 2000 Jun 24;273(1):251-60. doi:
10.1006/bbrc.2000.2922.

Molecular identification and characterization of novel members of the human
organic anion transporter (OATP) family.

Tamai I(1), Nezu J, Uchino H, Sai Y, Oku A, Shimane M, Tsuji A.

Author information:
(1)Faculty of Pharmaceutical Sciences, Kanazawa University, Kanazawa, 920-0934,
Japan.

We identified three novel transporters structurally belonging to the organic
anion transporting polypeptide (OATP) family in humans. Since previously known
rat oatp1 to 3 do not necessarily correspond to the human OATPs in terms of
either tissue distribution or function, here we designate the newly identified
human OATPs as OATP-B, -D and -E, and we rename the previously known human OATP
as OATP-A. OATP-C proved to be identical with the recently reported LST1/OATP-2.
Expression profiles of the five OATPs and the prostaglandin transporter PGT (a
member of OATP family) in human tissues showed that OATP-C is exclusively
localized in liver, OATP-A and PGT are expressed in restricted ranges of
tissues, and OATP-B, -D and -E show broad expression profiles. OATP-B, -C, -D
and -E exhibited transport activity for [(3)H]estrone-3-sulfate as a common
substrate. OATP-C has a high transport activity with broad substrate
specificity.
