# The Rac-RhoGDI complex and the structural basis for the regulation of Rho proteins by RhoGDI.
**Authors:** Scheffzek K, Stephan I, Jensen ON, Illenberger D, Gierschik P
**Journal:** Nat Struct Biol (2000)
**DOI:** [10.1038/72392](https://doi.org/10.1038/72392)

## Abstract

1. Nat Struct Biol. 2000 Feb;7(2):122-6. doi: 10.1038/72392.

The Rac-RhoGDI complex and the structural basis for the regulation of Rho
proteins by RhoGDI.

Scheffzek K(1), Stephan I, Jensen ON, Illenberger D, Gierschik P.

Author information:

klaus@mpimf-heidelberg.mpg.de

Rho family-specific guanine nucleotide dissociation inhibitors (RhoGDIs)
decrease the rate of nucleotide dissociation and release Rho proteins such as
RhoA, Rac and Cdc42 from membranes, forming tight complexes that shuttle between
cytosol and membrane compartments. We have solved the crystal structure of a
complex between the RhoGDI homolog LyGDI and GDP-bound Rac2, which are abundant
in leukocytes, representing the cytosolic, resting pool of Rho species to be
activated by extracellular signals. The N-terminal domain of LyGDI (LyN), which
has been reported to be flexible in isolated RhoGDIs, becomes ordered upon
complex formation and contributes more than 60% to the interface area. The
structure is consistent with the C-terminus of Rac2 binding to a hydrophobic
cavity previously proposed as isoprenyl binding site. An inner segment of LyN
forms a helical hairpin that contacts mainly the switch regions of Rac2. The
architecture of the complex interface suggests a mechanism for the inhibition of
guanine nucleotide dissociation that is based on the stabilization of the
magnesium (Mg2+) ion in the nucleotide binding pocket.
