# Structural and thermodynamic characterization of the adrenodoxin-like domain of the electron-transfer protein Etp1 from Schizosaccharomyces pombe.
**Authors:** Müller JJ, Hannemann F, Schiffler B, Ewen KM, Kappl R, Heinemann U, Bernhardt R
**Journal:** J Inorg Biochem (2011)
**DOI:** [10.1016/j.jinorgbio.2011.04.001](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jinorgbio.2011.04.001)

## Abstract

1. J Inorg Biochem. 2011 Jul;105(7):957-65. doi: 10.1016/j.jinorgbio.2011.04.001.
 Epub 2011 Apr 9.

Structural and thermodynamic characterization of the adrenodoxin-like domain of
the electron-transfer protein Etp1 from Schizosaccharomyces pombe.

Müller JJ(1), Hannemann F, Schiffler B, Ewen KM, Kappl R, Heinemann U, Bernhardt
R.

Author information:
(1)Max-Delbrück-Centrum für Molekulare Medizin, Berlin, Germany.

The protein Etp1 of Schizosaccharomyces pombe consists of an amino-terminal
COX15-like domain and a carboxy-terminal ferredoxin-like domain, Etp1(fd), which
is cleaved off after mitochondrial import. The physiological function of
Etp1(fd) is supposed to lie in the participation in the assembly of iron-sulfur
clusters and the synthesis of heme A. In addition, the protein was shown to be
the first microbial ferredoxin being able to support electron transfer in
mitochondrial steroid hydroxylating cytochrome P450 systems in vivo and in
vitro, replacing thereby the native redox partner, adrenodoxin. Despite a
sequence similarity of 39% and the fact that fission yeast is a mesophilic
organism, thermodynamic studies revealed that Etp1(fd) has a melting temperature
more than 20°C higher than adrenodoxin. The three-dimensional structure of
Etp1(fd) has been determined by crystallography. To the best of our knowledge it
represents the first three-dimensional structure of a yeast ferredoxin. The
structure-based sequence alignment of Etp1(fd) with adrenodoxin yields a
rational explanation for their observed mutual exchangeability in the cytochrome
P450 system. Analysis of the electron exchange with the S. pombe redox partner
Arh1 revealed differences between Etp1(fd) and adrenodoxin, which might be
linked to their different physiological functions in the mitochondria of mammals
and yeast.
