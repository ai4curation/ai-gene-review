# Cytoplasmic transport signal is involved in phogrin targeting and localization to secretory granules.
**Authors:** Torii S, Saito N, Kawano A, Zhao S, Izumi T, Takeuchi T
**Journal:** Traffic (2005)
**DOI:** [10.1111/j.1600-0854.2005.00353.x](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1600-0854.2005.00353.x)

## Abstract

1. Traffic. 2005 Dec;6(12):1213-24. doi: 10.1111/j.1600-0854.2005.00353.x.

Cytoplasmic transport signal is involved in phogrin targeting and localization
to secretory granules.

Torii S(1), Saito N, Kawano A, Zhao S, Izumi T, Takeuchi T.

Author information:
(1)Laboratory of Secretion Biology, Institute for Molecular and Cellular
Regulation, Gunma University, Maebashi, Gunma 371-8512, Japan.

Phogrin is an integral glycoprotein primarily expressed in neuroendocrine cells.
The predominant localization of phogrin is on dense-core secretory granules, and
the lumenal domain has been shown to be involved in its efficient sorting to the
regulated secretory pathway. Here, we present data showing that a leucine-based
sorting signal [EExxxIL] within the cytoplasmic tail contributes its
steady-state localization to secretory granules. Deletion mutants in the tail
region failed to represent granular distribution in pancreatic beta-cell line,
MIN6, and anterior pituitary cell line, AtT-20. A sorting signal mutant with two
glutamic acids substituted into alanines (EE/AA) is primarily accumulated in the
Golgi area instead of secretory granules, and another mutant (IL/AA) is trapped
at the plasma membrane due to a defect in endocytosis. We further demonstrate
that the leucine-based sorting signal of phogrin specifically interacts with
both adaptor protein (AP)-1 and AP-2 clathrin adaptor complexes in vitro. These
observations, along with previous studies, suggest that distinct domains of
phogrin mediate proper localization of this transmembrane protein on secretory
granules.
