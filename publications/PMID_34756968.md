# Tbx5a and Tbx5b paralogues act in combination to control separate vectors of migration in the fin field of zebrafish.
**Authors:** Boyle-Anderson EAT, Mao Q, Ho RK

The T-box containing family member, TBX5, has been shown to play important
functional roles in the pectoral appendages of a variety of vertebrate species.
While a single TBX5 gene exists in all tetrapods studied to date, the zebrafish
genome retains two paralogues, designated as tbx5a and tbx5b, resulting from a
whole genome duplication in the teleost lineage. Zebrafish deficient in tbx5a
lack pectoral fin buds, whereas zebrafish deficient in tbx5b exhibit misshapen
pectoral fins, showing that both paralogues function in fin development. The
mesenchymal cells of the limb/fin bud are derived from the Lateral Plate
Mesoderm (LPM). Previous fate mapping work in zebrafish has shown that wildtype
(wt) fin field cells are initially located adjacent to somites (s)1-4. The wt
fin field cells migrate in opposing diagonal directions placing the limb bud
between s2-3 and lateral to the main body. To better characterize tbx5 paralogue
functions in zebrafish, time-lapse analyses of the migrations of fin bud
precursors under conditions of tbx5a knock-down, tbx5b knock-down and
double-knock-down were performed. Our data suggest that zebrafish tbx5a and
tbx5b have functionally separated migration direction vectors, that when
combined recapitulate the migration of the wt fin field. We and others have
shown that loss of Tbx5a function abolishes an fgf24 signaling cue resulting in
fin field cells failing to converge in an Antero-Posterior (AP) direction and
migrating only in a mediolateral (ML) direction. We show here that loss of Tbx5b
function affects initial ML directed movements so that fin field cells fail to
migrate laterally but continue to converge along the AP axis. Furthermore, fin
field cells in the double Tbx5a/Tbx5b knock-down zebrafish do not engage in
directed migrations along either the ML or AP axis. Therefore, these two
paralogues may be acting to instruct separate vectors of fin field migration in
order to direct proper fin bud formation.

The role of the tbx5 paralogues during zebrafish pectoral fin bud development

In zebrafish, fate mapping has shown that the pectoral fin field is located adjacent to somites 1-4 in the lateral plate mesoderm (LPM) (Mao et al., 2015). Detailed analysis of the cellular dynamics of the fin field has shown that these cells migrate to form the fin bud (Mao et al., 2015). Starting at 18 hours post fertilization (hpf), the pectoral fin field cells migrate laterally away from the midline while also converging along the anterior-posterior axis (AP) to form a fin bud located at somite 2/3. Previous cell tracking analyses show that the anterior fin field cells must migrate a greater distance than the more posterior cells, and therefore migrate with an increased speed and in a more persistent manner (Mao et al., 2015). During this migration, the cells of the fin field generally maintain their nearest neighbor relationships (Mao et al., 2015).

Due to the whole-genome duplication in teleosts, TBX5 has two paralogues in zebrafish: tbx5a and tbx5b. Loss of either paralogue has differing effects on the fin: zebrafish embryos injected with a morpholino designed against the tbx5a sequence (hereafter referred to as Tbx5a knock-down embryos) and tbx5a −/− mutant embryos do not form pectoral fins, while both Tbx5b knock-down and tbx5b −/− mutant embryos form fins that are small and misshapen (Figure 1) (D. Ahn et al., 2002; Boyle Anderson and Ho, 2018; Garrity et al., 2002; Parrie et al., 2013a; Pi-Roig et al., 2014). Previously reported cell tracking data has found that in Tbx5a knock-down embryos, the cells of the fin field do not converge along the AP axis while migration away from the midline is not affected, as the Tbx5a knock-down cells continue to migrate laterally past the position where wt cells would normally converge to form a fin bud (Mao et al., 2015).

The AP convergence of pectoral fin field cells is controlled by Tbx5a regulation of Fgf24 expression in a subset of cells of the fin field (Fischer et al., 2003; Mao et al., 2015). In Fgf24-knock-down embryos, the cells of the limb field fail to converge and instead migrate laterally past the site where a fin bud would normally form. Furthermore, ectopic application of FGF protein through microbead implantation can partially rescue the convergence defects of Tbx5a-and Fgf24 knock-down embryos (Mao et al., 2015). This suggests that Fgf24 is the convergence cue responsible for the AP convergence of the cells in the fin field (Mao et al., 2015).

In contrast to Tbx5a knock-down and mutant embryos which fail to form a pectoral fin (Figure 1b’) knock-down of Tbx5b and mutation of the tbx5b gene results in misshapen fins (Figure 1b”). Both Parrie et al. and Pi-Roig et al. suggest that the primary cause of the fin phenotype of Tbx5b knock-down embryos is a delay in fin bud formation, citing both the delayed transition of fgf24 from the mesenchyme to the epithelium and the delay in apical fold (AF) specification indicated by expression patterns of bmp4 (Parrie et al., 2013b; Pi-Roig et al., 2014). However, it is unknown how loss of zebrafish Tbx5b affects the Apical Ectodermal Ridge (AER) or the Zone of Polarizing activity (ZPA), two signaling centers involved in regulating proximal-distal and AP patterning of the fin bud, respectively (Riddle et al., 1993; Saunders, 1948).

In this paper, we analyze the cellular dynamics of the cells of the fin field in Tbx5b knock-down embryos and compare these data to previously published data on the effects of loss of Tbx5a on the cellular dynamics of the fin field. In Tbx5b knock-down embryos, the fin field cells converge along the AP axis, although the convergence movements are decreased compared to wt embryos. Notably, Tbx5b knock-down embryos also display a significant defect in migration along a novel dimension - unlike in both wt and Tbx5a knock-down embryos, the fin field cells of the Tbx5b knock-down embryos do not initially migrate laterally away from the midline. This suggests that in zebrafish, the two tbx5 paralogues may be working in combination to produce the full dynamics of wt fin development. Further supporting this, embryos deficient in both paralogues of Tbx5 display a general loss of directed migration with an increased randomness of migration, suggesting that both paralogues are required for directed migration of the cells in the fin field.

In situ hybridizations were performed as described by Ahn et al (2002). Probe sequences used in this paper referenced as follows: for tbx5a (Ahn et al., 2002); dlx2a (Akimenko et al., 1994); bmp4 (Nikaido et al., 1997); fgf24 (Draper et al., 2003); and shha (Krauss et al., 1993). Morpholino injections were performed as described by Nasevicius and Ekker (2000). Amounts used as follows: 3.7 ng of Tbx5a morpholino (5’-CCTGTACGATGTCTACCGTGAGGC-3’) (D. Ahn et al., 2002) and/or 5 ng of Tbx5b translation blocking morpholino (5’-GGATTCGCCATATTCCCGTCTGAGT-3’) (Parrie et al., 2013) or control morpholino obtained from Gene Tools (Oregon) was injected per embryo. In this paper, zebrafish embryos injected with morpholinos and displaying a specific phenotype are referred to as ‘knock-down’ embryos; for example, embryos injected with a morpholino designed to the tbx5b gene sequence are designated as “Tbx5b knock-down” embryos. Embryos injected simultaneously with morpholinos directed to both tbx5a and tbx5b are referred to as “double knock-down” embryos. Both tbx5a knock-down and tbx5b knock-down embryos have been shown to phenocopy their respective homozygous mutants (Ahn et al, 2002; Garrity et al, 2002; Boyle Anderson and Ho, 2018). For cell polarity studies, 100 ng/μL of Xcentrin::Cherry and 20 ng/μL of H2B:CFP RNA were injected per embryo. Transgenic lines used include Tg(h2afx:h2afv-mCherry)mw3 (McMahon et al., 2009), Et(hand2:GFP)ch2 (Mao et al., 2015) and Tg(tbx5a::GFP) (Ocaña et al., 2017).Imaging and cell trackingWhole mount in situ imaging was performed on a Zeiss Axioplan microscope and photographed using a Nikon D5000 digital camera. For cell tracking, live, dechorionated Et(hand2:GFP)ch2; Tg(h2afx:h2afv-mCherry)mw3 embryos were mounted in 0.8% low melting temperature agarose in E3 media with 0.16% tricaine in a glass bottomed dish as described in Mao et al., (2015). A heated stage at 28 °C was used for time-lapse movies using a 20x objective on a Zeiss Laser Scanning Microscope 710. Live imaging for individual time-lapse movies was performed for a minimum of 6 hours. Embryos were imaged at intervals of 8 minutes starting at 18 hours post fertilization. Cell tracking was performed using the Manual Tracking plugin on FIJI. Embryo registration and cell tracking analysis was performed using the R package CellTrackingEBA (https://github.com/erinboyleanderson/CellTrackingEBA) and custom R scripts. Eight (8) embryos were imaged per condition. Between 40 and 70 cells were tracked between somites 1-5 per embryo. Cell movement data collected on AP position and ML position were normalized to a reference track created by averaging the positions of 3 designated notochord cells per embryo over time. The following metrics were calculated from the original parameters: Average Speed, Average Track Length, AP displacement, AP scatter, AP compaction, ML displacement, ML scatter, ML compaction, persistence, and mean square displacement.

Live imaging for individual time-lapse movies was performed for a minimum of 6 hours. Embryos were imaged at intervals of 8 minutes starting at 18 hours post fertilization. Cell tracking was performed using the Manual Tracking plugin on FIJI. Embryo registration and cell tracking analysis was performed using the R package CellTrackingEBA (https://github.com/erinboyleanderson/CellTrackingEBA) and custom R scripts. Eight (8) embryos were imaged per condition. Between 40 and 70 cells were tracked between somites 1-5 per embryo. Cell movement data collected on AP position and ML position were normalized to a reference track created by averaging the positions of 3 designated notochord cells per embryo over time. The following metrics were calculated from the original parameters: Average Speed, Average Track Length, AP displacement, AP scatter, AP compaction, ML displacement, ML scatter, ML compaction, persistence, and mean square displacement.

Parrie et al. report that some markers in the early fin bud are normal, such as hand2 expression, while other markers of fin development, such as versican a, are defective (Parrie et al., 2013a). Likewise, Pi-Roig et al. report that the Fgf signaling cascade involved in fin patterning is still present in Tbx5b knock-down fin buds, although there are some delays and misregulations, notably that fgf24 remains expressed for longer in the fin mesenchyme of Tbx5b knock-down embryos than in wt embryos (Pi-Roig et al., 2014). At 32 hpf, the fin buds of both the Tbx5b knock-downs and the tbx5b −/− mutants are notably smaller, however shha expression is still present in the posterior region of the fin bud (arrows, Figure 2a′,a″). Each red line is the average AP position over time of all cells that begin their migration adjacent to somite 1 for each embryo. Similar lines are also plotted in yellow for cells adjacent to somite 2, green for cells adjacent to somite 3 and blue for cells adjacent to somite 4. In this work, we have characterized the role of zebrafish tbx5b during limb bud formation by comparing the migration dynamics of LPM cells in Tbx5b knock-down embryos to previously published, but contemporaneously re-analyzed data on wt and Tbx5a knock-down embryos (Mao et al., 2015). We also analyze the migration parameters displayed in double Tbx5a/Tbx5b knock-down embryos.Whereas Tbx5a knock-down zebrafish lack fin buds and pectoral fins, Tbx5b knock-down zebrafish form small, misshapen pectoral fins (D. The knock-down of Tbx5b affects the ability of fin field cells to migrate away from the dorsal midline along the ML axis (Figure 4k) during the period of our time-lapse analyses, but cells still converge, albeit less than wt, along the AP axis (Figure 4g). In both the tbx5b −/− mutants and Tbx5b knock-down embryos, Fgf24 is expressed in the LPM mesenchyme at the proper time of development to function as a convergence cue for the Tbx5b knock-down fin field cells.

In contrast, the data we show here demonstrate that Tbx5b plays an important role in the proper formation of the early fin bud, at a similar developmental stage to that of Tbx5a.

In this study, we have shown that Tbx5a and Tbx5b mediate different migration parameters of the early fin field LPM cells. We hypothesize that the two paralogous zebrafish genes may have subfunctionalized different components of the migration pathway mechanisms such that the two vectors of AP and ML movements have been distributed amongst Tbx5a and Tbx5b respectively. However, to make more definitive interpretations about sub- versus neo-functionalization, it would be necessary to reconstruct the likely migration trajectories of LPM cells in the ancestral organism that possessed a single TBX5 gene. While the tetrapod condition may in fact retain the ancestral state, the trajectories of individual cell migrations are not yet well documented in either chick or mouse beyond the important demonstration that LPM cells do in fact migrate at late stages into the limb bud region (Wyngaarden et al., 2010).

As transcription factors, changes to coding regions of the genes could affect interactions with co-factors such as shuttling proteins (Camarata et al., 2006), with downstream binding sites, or even with other T-box gene partners, to potentially affect separate downstream gene regulatory networks. If Tbx5a and Tbx5b have indeed subfunctionalized, then the shared and individual functions of the two paralogues should closely resemble the complete suite of essential functions of the ancestral parent gene. As tetrapods exhibit the ancestral condition of a single Tbx5 gene, characterization of the phenotypes displayed by zebrafish embryos that are doubly deficient for both Tbx5a and Tbx5b may shed light on the mechanisms leading to the loss of limb phenotype in knock-out mice and possibly on the limb defects observed in Holt-Oram syndrome patients in which early cell migration studies are more difficult to perform.

Our studies show that double knock-down embryos exhibit defects in directional migration along both the AP and ML axes. Double knock-down cells still retain the ability to move, albeit at a slightly reduced overall speed compared to wt. However, their movements are more random as indicated by both changes in the alpha value and persistence measurements (Figure 5j–l). Cell tracking data also illustrates that groups of cells from all regions of the fin field will migrate in variously distributed directions, with cells from each somite group having migration angles pointing both towards and away from the midline and anterior and posterior (Figure 7d). The end result of these randomized movements is the lack of any net group movement of the fin field cells away from their initial starting positions to eventually form an operational fin bud.
