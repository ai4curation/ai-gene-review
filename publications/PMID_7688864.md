Comment in
    Nature. 1993 Aug 12;364(6438):572-3. doi: 10.1038/364572a0.

The crystal structure of T7 RNA polymerase reveals a molecule organized around a
cleft that can accommodate a double-stranded DNA template. A portion
(approximately 45%) of the molecule displays extensive structural homology to
the polymerase domain of Klenow fragment and more limited homology to the human
immunodeficiency virus HIV-1 reverse transcriptase. A comparison of the
structures and sequences of these polymerases identifies structural elements
that may be responsible for discriminating between ribonucleotide and
deoxyribonucleotide substrates, and RNA and DNA templates. The relative
locations of the catalytic site and a specific promoter recognition residue
allow the orientation of the polymerase on the template to be defined.
