# AS160, the Akt substrate regulating GLUT4 translocation, has a functional Rab GTPase-activating protein domain.
**Authors:** Mîinea CP, Sano H, Kane S, Sano E, Fukuda M, Peränen J, Lane WS, Lienhard GE
**Journal:** Biochem J (2005)
**DOI:** [10.1042/BJ20050887](https://doi.org/10.1042/BJ20050887)
**PMC:** [PMC1237142](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC1237142/)

## Abstract

1. Biochem J. 2005 Oct 1;391(Pt 1):87-93. doi: 10.1042/BJ20050887.

AS160, the Akt substrate regulating GLUT4 translocation, has a functional Rab
GTPase-activating protein domain.

Mîinea CP(1), Sano H, Kane S, Sano E, Fukuda M, Peränen J, Lane WS, Lienhard GE.

Author information:
(1)Department of Biochemistry, Dartmouth Medical School, Hanover, NH 03755, USA.

Recently, we described a 160 kDa protein (designated AS160, for Akt substrate of
160 kDa) with a predicted Rab GAP (GTPase-activating protein) domain that is
phosphorylated on multiple sites by the protein kinase Akt. Phosphorylation of
AS160 in adipocytes is required for insulin-stimulated translocation of the
glucose transporter GLUT4 to the plasma membrane. The aim of the present study
was to determine whether AS160 is in fact a GAP for Rabs, and, if so, what its
specificity is. We first identified a group of 16 Rabs in a preparation of
intracellular vesicles containing GLUT4 by MS. We then prepared the recombinant
GAP domain of AS160 and examined its activity against many of these Rabs, as
well as several others. The GAP domain was active against Rabs 2A, 8A, 10 and
14. There was no significant activity against 14 other Rabs. GAP activity was
further validated by the finding that the recombinant GAP domain with the
predicted catalytic arginine residue replaced by lysine was inactive. Finally,
it was found by immunoblotting that Rabs 2A, 8A and 14 are present in GLUT4
vesicles. These results indicate that AS160 is a Rab GAP, and suggest novel Rabs
that may participate in GLUT4 translocation.
