# The amino acid transporter, SLC1A3, is plasma membrane-localised in adipocytes and its activity is insensitive to insulin.
**Authors:** Krycer JR, Fazakerley DJ, Cater RJ, C Thomas K, Naghiloo S, Burchfield JG, Humphrey SJ, Vandenberg RJ, Ryan RM, James DE
**Journal:** FEBS Lett (2017)
**DOI:** [10.1002/1873-3468.12549](https://doi.org/10.1002/1873-3468.12549)

## Abstract

1. FEBS Lett. 2017 Jan;591(2):322-330. doi: 10.1002/1873-3468.12549. Epub 2017
Jan  13.

The amino acid transporter, SLC1A3, is plasma membrane-localised in adipocytes
and its activity is insensitive to insulin.

Krycer JR(1), Fazakerley DJ(1), Cater RJ(2), C Thomas K(1), Naghiloo S(1),
Burchfield JG(1), Humphrey SJ(1), Vandenberg RJ(2), Ryan RM(2), James DE(1)(3).

Author information:

(2)Discipline of Pharmacology, Sydney Medical School, The University of Sydney,

(3)Sydney Medical School, The University of Sydney, NSW, Australia.

The hormone insulin coordinates the catabolism of nutrients by protein
phosphorylation. Phosphoproteomic analysis identified insulin-responsive
phosphorylation of the Glu/Asp transporter SLC1A3/EAAT1 in adipocytes. The role
of SLC1A3 in adipocytes is not well-understood. We show that SLC1A3 is localised
to the plasma membrane and the major regulator of acidic amino acid uptake in
adipocytes. However, its localisation and activity were unaffected by insulin or
mutation of the insulin-regulated phosphosite. The latter was also observed
using a heterologous expression system in Xenopus laevis oocytes. Thus, SLC1A3
maintains a constant import of acidic amino acids independently of nutritional
status in adipocytes.
