Caspase-9 (C9) is a key initiator of the intrinsic apoptotic pathway and
contains an N-terminal caspase activation and recruitment domain (CARD) that
mediates homotypic and heterotypic interactions. While previous studies have
shown that C9CARD can form filamentous assemblies under specific conditions, its
intrinsic structural properties and interaction modes below saturation
concentration remain incompletely understood. Here, we present a 2.4 Å
resolution crystal structure of the isolated human C9CARD, revealing a
sulfate-mediated dimer-of-dimers assembly involving a conserved arginine-rich
surface. Notably, these residues overlap with those previously shown in filament
formation and in interactions with APAF-1CARD, indicating reuse of a common
interaction hotspot across distinct assembly states. Complementary biophysical
analyses show that C9CARD remains predominantly monomeric under physiological
conditions, while sulfate induces changes in its hydrodynamic properties.
Compared to WT C9CARD, a triple-arginine mutant (R10E/R52E/R56E) shows different
sulfate-dependent behavior and abolishes formation of higher-order assemblies.
Additionally, WT C9CARD exhibits reversible temperature-dependent transitions
between filamentous and droplet-like assemblies. Finally, AlphaFold3 predictions
suggest that filament-forming propensity and electrostatically regulated
higher-order assemblies may extend to other human CARDs. Together, our findings
suggest a model in which a conserved electrostatic interaction motif enables
multiple structurally distinct assembly modes of C9CARD. This work highlights
conserved electrostatic interaction modes in CARD-CARD association and suggests
that environmental conditions can modulate their assembly behavior.

To probe whether the sulfate-dependent loss of NMR signal reflected exchange with a sparsely populated NMR-invisible state on the millisecond timescale, 15N chemical exchange saturation transfer (CEST) measurements were performed. No additional exchange state was detected under the conditions tested (exemplary peak shown in Supplementary Fig. 9B), arguing against the presence of an exchanging, NMR-invisible state. Two distinct interface types are present in the crystal structure: interfaces A and A′ involve comparable sets of residues, as do interfaces B and B′. Interfaces A and A′ are symmetry-related and comprises chains A-B and C—D, respectively, whereas interfaces B and B′ include chains A-C and B—D. All four interfaces are mediated by electrostatic interactions between an arginine-rich surface patch and sulfate ions, but they differ in the number and spatial arrangement of sulfate molecules. Whereas interface A contains three sulfate ions, interface A′ features only a single, central sulfate ion that is coordinated by the same set of arginine residues. The interface A is stabilized by charge-mediated interaction between helix α1 and α4 arginines (R10A, R13 A, R15A, R56A) of chain A and helix α1 and α4 arginines (R10B, R13B, R52B, R56B) of chain B with sulfate ions S1, S2 and S3 (Fig. 2B, Supplementary Fig. 3 A–C). Interface A′ is maintained by interaction between helix α1 arginines (R10D and R13D) of chain D and arginine of helix α4 (R56C) of chain C with the sulfate ion S4 (Fig. 2D, Supplementary Fig. 3D). Interface B is similarly stabilized by electrostatic interactions via the charged side chains of arginine and the negative sulfate ions. This interface is formed by the interaction between helix α1 and α4 arginines (R10A and R13A) of chain A and arginine of helix α1 (R15C) of chain C with the sulfate ion S3 (Fig. 2C, Supplementary Fig. 3C). Interface B′ is stabilized by interaction between helix α1 arginines (R10D and R13D) of chain D and arginine of helix α1 (R15B) of chain B with the sulfate ion S4 (Fig. 2E, Supplementary Fig. 3D). The buried surface area (BSA) for interface A, A′, B and B′ correspond to 489.2 Å2, 494.2 Å2, 486.3 Å2 and 558.0 Å2 respectively (Supplementary Table 2).

Together, these interfaces define a sulfate-mediated dimer-of-dimers arrangement centred on a conserved arginine-rich surface. The overlap of these residues with interaction sites observed in previously determined C9CARD structures identify this region as a recurrent and structurally accessible interaction surface across distinct structural contexts.

Furthermore, the crystal structure of the isolated human C9CARD shows a conserved fold with the C9CARD within the filamentous assembly with an RMSD value of 0.68 Å (Supplementary Fig. 4 A–C) and with previously determined structures of C9CARD in presence of APAF-1CARD with an RMSD values in the range of 0.54 Å to 0.58 Å (Supplementary Fig. 5 A–D). This structural conservation indicates that C9CARD retains a highly similar fold across different assembly states, suggesting that self-association occurs without major conformational rearrangements of the monomer. Interestingly, the residues involved in making the crystal contact of C9CARD (R10, R13, R15, R52, and R56) (PDB: 9R44) overlap with the type-I interface residues described for the C9CARD filament (R10, R13, R52, and R56) (PDB: 9S6F) (Fig. 2F). Moreover, the interacting interfaces for the C9CARD and APAF-1CARD complex (PDB: 4RHW) involves a subset of same arginine residues of C9CARD (R13 and R52) (Fig. 2G). These observations further support the involvement of a conserved arginine-rich surface in mediating C9CARD self-association across different structural contexts.

Direct superposition of isolated C9CARD (PDB 9R44), filament-associated C9CARD (PDB 9S6F), and APAF-1-bound C9CARD (PDB 4RHW) further demonstrates conservation of the overall CARD fold. However, the arginine side chains show local differences in orientation and spatial positioning (Supplementary Fig. 6). This suggests that the observed structural differences arise primarily from rearrangement of solvent-exposed arginine side chains rather than from major conformational changes of the C9CARD fold.

Recent work demonstrated thar C9CARD can self-assemble into structuted filaments under specific solution conditions (Rawal et al., 2025), raising the possibility that this domain may access multiple assembly states beyond its canonical apoptosome-associated conformation. Therefore, we used C9CARD as a model system to perform structural analysis of its condition-dependent assembly behavior.

Importantly, the sulfate-mediated assembly observed in the crystal structure likely represents a structurally accessible interaction mode rather than a dominant physiological state. The solution measurements further define the limits of this structural interpretation. Sulfate produced clear changes in the DLS profiles and NMR chemical shifts, demonstrating that C9CARD is responsive to changes in its ionic environment. The modest increase in rotational correlation time measured by TRACT and the absence of an additional substantially populated state in the CEST analysis further claims the absence of a stable sulfate-induced higher-order oligomer under the conditions tested. Thus, the crystallographic dimer-of-dimers is best interpreted as a sulfate-stabilized, structurally accessible assembly state rather than a dominant solution species.

It is important to note that the crystallographic dimer-of-dimers was captured under a combination of conditions that strongly favor intermolecular encounters, including high protein concentration, high sulfate concentration, prolonged equilibration, and the crystallization environment. The observed assembly may therefore require the combined effect of several physicochemical parameters for stabilization and need not represent a substantially populated species in dilute solution. Nevertheless, the sulfate-dependent DLS and NMR changes demonstrate that C9CARD responds to sulfate in solution. Additionally, the mutational analysis supports functional relevance of the broader arginine-rich region for C9CARD assembly behavior. The triple-arginine mutant retained a predominantly α-helical secondary structure but showed a strongly reduced sulfate-dependent DLS response and abolished formation of higher-order assemblies in negative-stain EM and DIC microscopy. These observations indicate that the positively charged surface contributes to C9CARD assembly behavior.

Furthermore, WT C9CARD displayed a reversible temperature-dependent transition between filamentous bundles at 4 °C and droplet-like assemblies at room temperature. Although 4 °C is not physiologically relevant, the reversibility of this behavior illustrates the sensitivity of C9CARD higher-order assembly to environmental conditions. A key parameter governing this phase transition is the initial protein concentration. In a previous study, C9CARD was saturated until it reached Csat driving the system directly into an aggregated, filamentous state (Rawal et al., 2025). However, when we use C9CARD concentration below its Csat, we do not force immediate filament formation. Instead, C9CARD can dynamically exchange between filamentous and droplet-like assembly. The absence of comparable filamentous or droplet-like assemblies for the triple-arginine mutant under the tested conditions further suggests that the arginine-rich region contributes to this condition-dependent higher-order assembly behavior. Such behavior suggests that, under appropriate cellular conditions (e.g. locally increased concentration, changes in ionic strength, or association with lipids, ATP or RNA), C9CARD may undergo analogous droplet-like-to-filament transitions, similar to anion-associated phase behaviors described for other CARD-containing proteins (Shen et al., 2021; Huang et al., 2022; Cao et al., 2025; Chai et al., 2025; Pizzuto et al., 2022; Elliott et al., 2018). Together with the anion-mediated interfaces described above, these observations emphasize that relatively small physicochemical changes can shift C9CARD between distinct assembly states.

C9CARD has an overall near-neutral net charge, with an isoelectric point of approximately pH 6.5. Although it is not a canonical low-complexity disordered domain, its amino-acid sequence is compositionally biased: nearly 50% of the residues are Ala, Arg, Asp, or Leu (Fig. 3A). The primary sequence is considerably enriched in charged residues (33% of all positions), with an equal distribution of positively charged (15 Arg, 1 Lys) and negatively charged residues (9 Asp, 7 Glu) (Fig. 3A–B). Together, these features suggest that the conserved charged surface of C9CARD is consistent with a strong sensitivity to changes in the electrostatic environment. Thus, both sequence composition and structurally recurrent charged surfaces position C9CARD as an electrostatically responsive interaction module.

Several CARD- and DDF-containing proteins have been reported to form cellular foci, condensates or filamentous assemblies, including NLRP6CARD, MAVSCARD, RIP2CARD, ASCCARD and caspase-2CARD (Li et al., 2018; Shen et al., 2021; Tenthorey et al., 2017; Diebolder et al., 2015; Yang et al., 2018; Xu et al., 2014; Gong et al., 2018; Dick et al., 2016; Sborgi et al., 2015; Su et al., 2008; Ge et al., 2024). These observations illustrate the broader capacity of death-domain-fold (DDF) proteins to access diverse supramolecular states. Whether the droplet-like C9CARD assemblies observed here correspond to a physiologically relevant cellular state, and whether such assemblies participate in filament nucleation or signaling, remain open questions requiring direct cellular and biophysical investigation.

Given that several CARD-containing proteins exhibit similar foci and higher-order assemblies, we next asked whether filament-forming propensity might be a more general property of CARD domains. To explore this, we employed the AF3 webserver to predict multimeric CARD-CARD assemblies across a range of human CARDs. The predictions revealed that a subset of CARDs exhibit a helical filament-forming propensity, suggesting that oligomerization and higher-order assembly could be a general feature of the CARD family. Experimental structure determination and functional validation will be required to elucidate whether, and under which conditions, these predicted CARD-CARD assemblies correspond to physiologically significant CARD-mediated supramolecular signaling complexes.

Taken together, our data position C9CARD as a versatile oligomerization module whose electrostatically driven assemblies are highly responsive to environmental conditions. In a broader context, we propose a working model in which C9CARD samples a range of assembly states depending on physicochemical conditions (Fig. 5). Sulfate stabilizes one such arrangement in the crystal and perturbs C9CARD in solution without generating a detectable substantially populated discrete oligomer. At higher protein concentrations and under temperature-dependent conditions, C9CARD can additionally access filamentous and droplet-like assemblies. Mutational analysis further suggests that the arginine-rich region is a key player in this varied electrostatic interaction modes. Instead of representing discrete functional endpoints, these states may reflect a continuous assembly landscape governed by electrostatic interactions and local environmental cues. Linking specific structural interfaces to distinct supramolecular states might provide a framework for understanding how CARD-mediated interactions can be dynamically regulated. Future structural and cellular studies on other CARDs, guided by AF3-based predictions, will be important to determine how general these assembly principles are and how they relate to CARD-dependent signaling pathways.

While this study provides structural and biophysical insight into the assembly behavior of C9CARD, several limitations should be considered. The assembly states described here are characterized under in vitro conditions, and their physiological relevance remains to be established. The sulfate-mediated interfaces observed in the crystal structure likely represent an accessible interaction mode highlighting a conserved arginine-rich surface, rather than a dominant solution state, as discrete oligomers were not resolved under the tested conditions. Although increasing anion concentration promotes self-association, the underlying species remain difficult to define using the approaches applied here. Finally, while reversible transitions between filamentous and droplet-like states illustrate the different interaction modes of C9CARD, the molecular nature and functional relevance of these intermediate states require further investigation. Finally, the AF3-predicted assemblies require direct experimental validation. Nonetheless, the combined structural, NMR, biophysical and mutational data identify a conserved arginine-rich surface as an important contributor to the condition-dependent assembly behavior of C9CARD.
