# Cep164 triggers ciliogenesis by recruiting Tau tubulin kinase 2 to the mother centriole.

Primary cilia play critical roles in development and disease. Their assembly is
triggered by mature centrioles (basal bodies) and requires centrosomal protein
164kDa (Cep164), a component of distal appendages. Here we show that loss of
Cep164 leads to early defects in ciliogenesis, reminiscent of the phenotypic
consequences of mutations in TTBK2 (Tau tubulin kinase 2). We identify Cep164 as
a likely physiological substrate of TTBK2 and demonstrate that Cep164 and TTBK2
form a complex. We map the interaction domains and demonstrate that complex
formation is crucial for the recruitment of TTBK2 to basal bodies. Remarkably,
ciliogenesis can be restored in Cep164-depleted cells by expression of chimeric
proteins in which TTBK2 is fused to the C-terminal centriole-targeting domain of
Cep164. These findings indicate that one of the major functions of Cep164 in
ciliogenesis is to recruit active TTBK2 to centrioles. Once positioned, TTBK2
then triggers key events required for ciliogenesis, including removal of CP110
and recruitment of intraflagellar transport proteins. In addition, our data
suggest that TTBK2 also acts upstream of Cep164, contributing to the assembly of
distal appendages.
