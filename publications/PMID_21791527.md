# Fgf signaling controls pharyngeal taste bud formation through miR-200 and Delta-Notch activity.
**Authors:** Kapsimali M, Kaushik AL, Gibon G, Dirian L, Ernest S, Rosa FM
**Journal:** Development (2011)
**DOI:** [10.1242/dev.058669](https://doi.org/10.1242/dev.058669)

## Abstract

1. Development. 2011 Aug;138(16):3473-84. doi: 10.1242/dev.058669.

Fgf signaling controls pharyngeal taste bud formation through miR-200 and
Delta-Notch activity.

Kapsimali M(1), Kaushik AL, Gibon G, Dirian L, Ernest S, Rosa FM.

Author information:
(1)Ecole Normale Supérieure, Institut de Biologie, 75005 Paris, France.
kapsimal@biologie.ens.fr

Taste buds, the taste sensory organs, are conserved in vertebrates and composed
of distinct cell types, including taste receptor, basal/presynaptic and support
cells. Here, we characterize zebrafish taste bud development and show that
compromised Fgf signaling in the larva results in taste bud reduction and
disorganization. We determine that Fgf activity is required within pharyngeal
endoderm for formation of Calb2b(+) cells and reveal miR-200 and Delta-Notch
signaling as key factors in this process. miR-200 knock down shows that miR-200
activity is required for taste bud formation and in particular for Calb2b(+)
cell formation. Compromised delta activity in mib(-/-) dramatically reduces the
number of Calb2b(+) cells and increases the number of 5HT(+) cells. Conversely,
larvae with increased Notch activity and ascl1a(-/-) mutants are devoid of
5HT(+) cells, but have maintained and increased Calb2b(+) cells, respectively.
These results show that Delta-Notch signaling is required for intact taste bud
organ formation. Consistent with this, Notch activity restores Calb2b(+) cell
formation in pharyngeal endoderm with compromised Fgf signaling, but fails to
restore the formation of these cells after miR-200 knock down. Altogether, this
study provides genetic evidence that supports a novel model where Fgf regulates
Delta-Notch signaling, and subsequently miR-200 activity, in order to promote
taste bud cell type differentiation.
