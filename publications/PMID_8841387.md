Acetyl-CoA hydrolase, catalyzing the hydrolysis of acetyl-CoA, is presumably
involved in regulating the intracellular acetyl-CoA or CoASH pools. The yeast
enzyme is encoded by ACHl (acetyl-CoA hydrolase) and the expression of ACH1 is
repressed by glucose (Lee, F.-J.S., Lin, L.-W. and Smith, J.A. (1990) J. Biol.
Chem. 265, 7413-7418). In order to study the biological function of the
acetyl-CoA hydrolase, a null mutation (achl-1) was created by gene replacement.
The mutation, while not lethal, slows down acetate utilization. In comparison to
wild-type, homozygote achl-l diploids, the onset of sporulation was delayed.
When measuring the levels of ACH1 mRNA and acetyl-CoA hydrolase activity, we
demonstrated that ACHl was highly expressed during sporulation process. These
results indicated that acetyl-CoA hydrolase in yeast cells involved in acetate
utilization and subsequently affected the sporulation process.
