# A complete shikimate pathway in Toxoplasma gondii: an ancient eukaryotic innovation.
**Authors:** Campbell SA, Richards TA, Mui EJ, Samuel BU, Coggins JR, McLeod R, Roberts CW
**Journal:** Int J Parasitol (2004)
**DOI:** [10.1016/j.ijpara.2003.10.006](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijpara.2003.10.006)

## Abstract

1. Int J Parasitol. 2004 Jan;34(1):5-13. doi: 10.1016/j.ijpara.2003.10.006.

A complete shikimate pathway in Toxoplasma gondii: an ancient eukaryotic
innovation.

Campbell SA(1), Richards TA, Mui EJ, Samuel BU, Coggins JR, McLeod R, Roberts
CW.

Author information:
(1)Department of Immunology, Strathclyde Institute for Biomedical Sciences,

The shikimate pathway is essential for survival of the apicomplexan parasites
Plasmodium falciparum, Toxoplasma gondii and Cryptosporidium parvum. As it is
absent in mammals it is a promising therapeutic target. Herein, we describe the
genes encoding the shikimate pathway enzymes in T. gondii. The molecular
arrangement and phylogeny of the proteins suggests homology with the eukaryotic
fungal enzymes, including a pentafunctional AROM. Current rooting of the
eukaryotic evolutionary tree infers that the fungi and apicomplexan lineages
diverged deeply, suggesting that the arom is an ancient supergene present in
early eukaryotes and subsequently lost or replaced in a number of lineages.
