# Two closely related human nuclear export factors utilize entirely distinct export pathways.
**Authors:** Yang J, Bogerd HP, Wang PJ, Page DC, Cullen BR
**Journal:** Mol Cell (2001)
**DOI:** [10.1016/s1097-2765(01)00303-3](https://doi.org/10.1016/s1097-2765(01)00303-3)

## Abstract

1. Mol Cell. 2001 Aug;8(2):397-406. doi: 10.1016/s1097-2765(01)00303-3.

Two closely related human nuclear export factors utilize entirely distinct 
export pathways.

Yang J(1), Bogerd HP, Wang PJ, Page DC, Cullen BR.

Author information:
(1)Howard Hughes Medical Institute, Department of Genetics, Duke University 
Medical Center, Durham, NC 27710, USA.

Nuclear mRNA export mediated by the human protein TAP requires a 
carboxy-terminal domain that directly interacts with components of the nuclear 
pore complex. Here we demonstrate that NXF3, a human RNA binding protein related 
to TAP, lacks this domain yet retains the ability to export tethered RNA 
transcripts and to shuttle between the nucleus and the cytoplasm. NXF3 contains 
a novel Crm1-dependent nuclear export signal that compensates in cis for the 
loss of the nuclear pore targeting domain. NXF3-dependent RNA export is 
therefore blocked by Crm1-specific inhibitors that do not affect TAP function. 
Thus, while the related TAP and NXF3 proteins are both capable of mediating 
nuclear RNA export, they do so via unrelated export pathways.
