Comment in
    Nat Rev Mol Cell Biol. 2014 Jun;15(6):368. doi: 10.1038/nrm3804.

Chromatin reorganization and the incorporation of specific histone modifications
during DNA damage response are essential steps for the successful repair of any
DNA lesion. Here, we show that the histone-fold protein CHRAC14 plays an
essential role in response to DNA damage in Drosophila. Chrac14 mutants are
hypersensitive to genotoxic stress and do not activate the G2/M cell-cycle
checkpoint after damage induction. Even though the DNA damage repair process is
activated in the absence of CHRAC14, lesions are not repaired efficiently. In
the absence of CHRAC14, the centromere-specific histone H3 variant CENP-A
localizes to sites of DNA damage, causing ectopic kinetochore formation and
genome instability. CENP-A and CHRAC14 are able to interact upon damage. Our
data suggest that CHRAC14 modulates chromatin composition in response to DNA
damage, which is required for efficient DNA damage repair in Drosophila.
