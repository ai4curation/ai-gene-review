# Phosphorylation modulates direct interactions between the Toll receptor, Pelle kinase and Tube.
**Authors:** Shen B, Manley JL
**Journal:** Development (1998)
**DOI:** [10.1242/dev.125.23.4719](https://doi.org/10.1242/dev.125.23.4719)

## Abstract

1. Development. 1998 Dec;125(23):4719-28. doi: 10.1242/dev.125.23.4719.

Phosphorylation modulates direct interactions between the Toll receptor, Pelle
kinase and Tube.

Shen B(1), Manley JL.

Author information:
(1)Department of Biological Sciences, Columbia University, New York, NY 10027,
USA.

Determination of dorsal/ventral polarity in Drosophila requires 12 genetically
defined, maternally encoded proteins. These include Toll, a transmembrane
receptor, Pelle, a ser/thr protein kinase and Tube, all of which function
intracytoplasmically to initiate the cascade that ultimately activates Dorsal,
an NF-kappaB family transcription factor. Here we describe biochemical
interactions between recombinant Toll, Pelle and Tube that provide insights into
early events in activation of the signaling cascade. We first show that Pelle
binds directly to a region within the Toll intracytoplasmic domain, providing
the first evidence that these two evolutionarily conserved molecules physically
interact. We then demonstrate that Pelle can be autophosphorylated, and that
this prevents binding to Toll as well as Tube. Autophosphorylation occurs in the
N-terminal, death-domain-containing region of Pelle, which is dispensable for
binding to Toll but required for enzymatic activity. We also show that Pelle
phosphorylates Toll, within the region required for Pelle interaction, but this
phosphorylation can be blocked by a previously characterized inhibitory domain
at the Toll C terminus. These and other results allow us to propose a model by
which multiple phosphorylation-regulated interactions between these three
proteins lead to activation of the Dorsal signaling pathway.
