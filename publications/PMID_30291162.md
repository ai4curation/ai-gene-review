# Inhibition of cell fate repressors secures the differentiation of the touch receptor neurons of Caenorhabditis elegans.
**Authors:** Zheng C, Jin FQ, Trippe BL, Wu J, Chalfie M
**Journal:** Development (2018)
**DOI:** [10.1242/dev.168096](https://doi.org/10.1242/dev.168096)

## Abstract

1. Development. 2018 Nov 15;145(22):dev168096. doi: 10.1242/dev.168096.

Inhibition of cell fate repressors secures the differentiation of the touch
receptor neurons of Caenorhabditis elegans.

Zheng C(1), Jin FQ(1), Trippe BL(1), Wu J(1), Chalfie M(2).

Author information:

Terminal differentiation generates the specialized features and functions that
allow postmitotic cells to acquire their distinguishing characteristics. This
process is thought to be controlled by transcription factors called 'terminal
selectors' that directly activate a set of downstream effector genes. In
Caenorhabditis elegans, the differentiation of both the mechanosensory touch
receptor neurons (TRNs) and the multidendritic nociceptor FLP neurons uses the
terminal selectors UNC-86 and MEC-3. The FLP neurons fail to activate TRN genes,
however, because a complex of two transcriptional repressors (EGL-44/EGL-46)
prevents their expression. Here, we show that the ZEB family transcriptional
factor ZAG-1 promotes TRN differentiation not by activating TRN genes but by
preventing the expression of EGL-44/EGL-46. As EGL-44/EGL-46 also inhibits the
production of ZAG-1, these proteins form a bistable, negative-feedback loop that
regulates the choice between the two neuronal fates.
