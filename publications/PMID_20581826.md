Channeling of misfolded proteins into repair, assembly or degradation pathways
is often mediated by complex and multifunctional cellular factors. Despite
detailed structural information, the underlying regulatory mechanisms governing
these factors are not well understood. The extracytoplasmic heat-shock factor
DegP (HtrA) is a well-suited model for addressing mechanistic issues, as it is
regulated by the common mechanisms of allostery and activation by
oligomerization. Site-directed mutagenesis combined with refolding and
oligomerization studies of chemically denatured DegP revealed how substrates
trigger the conversion of the resting conformation into the active conformation.
Binding of specific peptides to PDZ domain-1 causes a local rearrangement that
is allosterically transmitted to the substrate-binding pocket of the protease
domain. This activated state readily assembles into larger oligomeric particles,
thus stabilizing the catalytically active form and providing a degradation
cavity for protein substrates. The implications of these data for the mechanism
of protein quality control are discussed.
