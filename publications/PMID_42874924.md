# The malonyl-CoA synthetase ACSF3 is required for metabolic editing downstream of biotin carboxylation.

The malonyl-CoA synthetase ACSF3 is required for metabolic editing downstream of
biotin carboxylation.

Acsf3, a mitochondrial malonyl-CoA synthetase, is mutated in humans with inborn
error Combined Malonic and Methylmalonic Aciduria. To clarify its role in
mammalian metabolism, we generated Acsf3 knockout mice. We show that the loss of
Acsf3 results in high concentrations of malonate and methylmalonate and exhibits
expected defects in mitochondrial metabolism, but the KO mice are largely
healthy, similar to the human inborn error. Acsf3 KO mice exhibit redundancy in
malonyl-CoA synthetase activity, suggesting a genetic modifier of disease
severity. Malonate and methylmalonate are metabolic poisons generated as a
by-product of biotin-dependent carboxylation that require metabolic editing.
Indeed, the liver-specific loss of the biotin ligase, Holocarboxylase Synthetase
(hlcs), inhibits all biotin-dependent carboxylation and results in broad changes
in liver mRNA, protein, posttranslational, and metabolic regulation. Loss of
Hlcs suppresses the metabolic deficits in Acsf3KO liver due to the inhibition of
malonate and methylmalonate generation. Mice with a double knockout of Acsf3 and
Hlcs maintained mitochondrial lipoylation, demonstrating the highly redundant
generation of mitochondrial malonyl-CoA. These data define the role of Acsf3 in
metabolic editing of malonate and methylmalonate to maintain mitochondrial
metabolism in the face of endogenously produced anti-metabolites.
