# Structural modeling of heteromeric protein complexes from disassembly pathways and ion mobility-mass spectrometry.
**Authors:** Hall Z, Politis A, Robinson CV
**Journal:** Structure (2012)
**DOI:** [10.1016/j.str.2012.07.001](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.str.2012.07.001)

## Abstract

1. Structure. 2012 Sep 5;20(9):1596-609. doi: 10.1016/j.str.2012.07.001. Epub
2012  Jul 26.

Structural modeling of heteromeric protein complexes from disassembly pathways
and ion mobility-mass spectrometry.

Hall Z(1), Politis A, Robinson CV.

Author information:
(1)Physical and Theoretical Chemistry Laboratory, Department of Chemistry,
University of Oxford, Oxford OX1 3QZ, UK.

Structure determination of macromolecular protein assemblies remains a challenge
for well-established methods. Here, we provide an assessment of an emerging
structural technique, ion mobility-mass spectrometry (IM-MS), and examine the
use of collision cross-sections (CCSs), derived from IM-MS, as restraints for
structure characterization of heteromeric protein assemblies. Using 15 complexes
selected from the Protein Data Bank, we validate the use of low-resolution
models by comparing their CCSs with those calculated for all-atom structures. We
then select six heteromeric complexes, disrupting them in solution to form
subcomplexes. Experimental and calculated CCSs reveal close similarity for 18 of
the 21 (sub)complexes. Exploring the use of CCS as a restraint, we incorporate
it into a scoring function and show good correlation between the score and
similarity to the native structure for heteromers, especially when an additional
symmetry restraint was introduced.
