Flowering in plants is a consequence of the transition of the shoot apex from
vegetative to reproductive growth in response to environmental and internal
signals. The indeterminate1 gene (id1) controls the transition to flowering in
maize. We show by cloning the id1 gene that it encodes a protein with zinc
finger motifs, suggesting that the id1 gene product functions as a
transcriptional regulator of the floral transition. id1 mRNA expression studies
and analyses of transposon-induced chimeric plants indicate that id1 acts
non-cell-autonomously to regulate the production of a transmissible signal in
the leaf that elicits the transformation of the shoot apex to reproductive
development. These results provide molecular and genetic data consistent with
the florigen hypothesis derived from classical plant physiology studies.
