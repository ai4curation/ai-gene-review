# A different conformation for EGC stator subcomplex in solution and in the assembled yeast V-ATPase: possible implications for regulatory disassembly.
**Authors:** Diepholz M, Venzke D, Prinz S, Batisse C, Flörchinger B, Rössle M, Svergun DI, Böttcher B, Féthière J
**Journal:** Structure (2008)
**DOI:** [10.1016/j.str.2008.09.010](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.str.2008.09.010)

## Abstract

1. Structure. 2008 Dec 10;16(12):1789-98. doi: 10.1016/j.str.2008.09.010.

A different conformation for EGC stator subcomplex in solution and in the
assembled yeast V-ATPase: possible implications for regulatory disassembly.

Diepholz M(1), Venzke D, Prinz S, Batisse C, Flörchinger B, Rössle M, Svergun
DI, Böttcher B, Féthière J.

Author information:
(1)EMBL, Structural and Computational Biology Unit, Meyerhofstrasse 1, 69117
Heidelberg, Germany.

Vacuolar ATPases (V-ATPases) are ATP-dependent proton pumps that maintain the
acidity of cellular compartments. They are composed of a membrane-integrated
proton-translocating V(0) and an extrinsic cytoplasmic catalytic domain V(1),
joined by several connecting subunits. To clarify the arrangement of these
peripheral connections and their interrelation with other subunits of the
holocomplex, we have determined the solution structures of isolated EG and EGC
connecting subcomplexes by small angle X-ray scattering and the 3D map of the
yeast V-ATPase by electron microscopy. In solution, EG forms a slightly kinked
rod, which assembles with subunit C into an L-shaped structure. This model is
supported by the microscopy data, which show three copies of EG with two of
these linked by subunit C. However, the relative arrangement of the EG and C
subunits in solution is more open than that in the holoenzyme, suggesting a
conformational change of EGC during regulatory assembly and disassembly.
