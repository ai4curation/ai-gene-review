# The Spemann organizer signal noggin binds and inactivates bone morphogenetic protein 4.
**Authors:** Zimmerman LB, De Jesús-Escobar JM, Harland RM
**Journal:** Cell (1996)
**DOI:** [10.1016/s0092-8674(00)80133-6](https://doi.org/10.1016/s0092-8674(00)80133-6)

## Abstract

1. Cell. 1996 Aug 23;86(4):599-606. doi: 10.1016/s0092-8674(00)80133-6.

The Spemann organizer signal noggin binds and inactivates bone morphogenetic
protein 4.

Zimmerman LB(1), De Jesús-Escobar JM, Harland RM.

Author information:
(1)Department of Molecular and Cell Biology, Division of Biochemistry and
Molecular Biology, University of California, Berkeley 94720, USA.

Signals released by the Spemann organizer of the amphibian gastrula can directly
induce neural tissue from ectoderm and can dorsalize ventral mesoderm to form
muscle. The secreted polypeptide noggin mimics these activities and is expressed
at the appropriate time and place to participate in the organizer signal. Neural
induction and mesoderm dorsalization are antagonized by bone morphogenetic
proteins (BMPs), which induce epidermis and ventral mesoderm instead. Here we
report that noggin protein binds BMP4 with high affinity and can abolish BMP4
activity by blocking binding to cognate cell-surface receptors. These data
suggest that noggin secreted by the organizer patterns the embryo by
interrupting BMP signaling.
