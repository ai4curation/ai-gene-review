Skeletal muscle development is regulated by transcriptional and
post-translational mechanisms. While ankyrin repeat proteins participate in
post-translational modifications, their role in myogenesis remains unclear. This
study identifies ankyrin repeat domain-containing protein 9 (ANKRD9) as a novel
negative regulator of chicken skeletal muscle development. ANKRD9 showed dynamic
expression during postnatal muscle growth and downregulated cell cycle and DNA
replication-related genes. Functionally, ANKRD9 overexpression inhibited
myoblast proliferation and differentiation, while its knockdown enhanced these
processes. In vivo, siRNA-mediated knockdown of ANKRD9 markedly increased muscle
mass and myofiber diameter in chicks. Mechanistically, ANKRD9 bound directly to
inosine monophosphate dehydrogenase 2 (IMPDH2), a rate-limiting enzyme in purine
synthesis, and promoted its ubiquitin-mediated degradation without affecting
mRNA levels. Crucially, rescue experiments confirmed that restoring IMPDH2
expression effectively reversed the inhibitory effects of ANKRD9 on myoblast
proliferation and differentiation. Thus, this study unveils a novel regulatory
axis in which ANKRD9 negatively regulates skeletal myogenesis by mediating the
ubiquitination of IMPDH2. This discovery not only provides new insights into the
post-translational regulatory network governing muscle development but also
offers a potential target for genetic improvement of meat yield in poultry.

Introduction

Chicken skeletal muscle is an important agricultural economic trait (Du et al., 2025), and its developmental process directly determines meat production performance and economic benefits (Li et al., 2022; Wang et al., 2024). Skeletal muscle development is a highly complex biological process, primarily regulated by myogenic regulatory factors (MRFs) and involving the precise coordination of multi-level signaling networks (Chen et al., 2024; Li et al., 2024b). Skeletal muscle development initiates from myogenic progenitor cells located in the somitic dermomyotome (Whitlock, 2024). Under the guidance of key signaling pathways, including Shh, Notch, and Wnt, these progenitor cells further differentiate into myogenic precursor cells (Musumeci et al., 2015; Yang et al., 2021). Subsequently, stimulated by various growth factors, such as fibroblast growth factor (FGF) and insulin-like growth factor 1 (IGF-1), these precursor cells are activated and enter an active proliferation phase (Duclos, 2005). The cell cycle progression at this stage is mainly driven by cyclins and their cyclin-dependent kinase 4/6 (CDK4/6) (Jiang et al., 2024; Wood and Endicott, 2018), which play a decisive role in determining the final number of muscle fibers. After proliferation, the differentiation process of skeletal muscle cells is precisely regulated by several myogenic regulatory factors, including myogenic determination protein 1 (MyoD1), myogenic factor 5 (Myf5), Myogenin, and myogenic regulatory factor 4 (Mrf4) (Rudnicki et al., 1993), which form an intricate hierarchical transcriptional regulatory network that dictates the fate of the myogenic lineage (Choi et al., 1990; Tapscott et al., 1988). Although this basic regulatory framework has been preliminarily elucidated, the fine upstream regulation of this network, particularly the mechanisms involving post-translational modifications such as ubiquitination, remains largely unexplored.

In recent years, E3 ubiquitin ligases, as key executors of ubiquitination, have garnered increasing attention for their roles in skeletal muscle development (Baehr et al., 2025; Baptista et al., 2010). They specifically recognize substrate proteins and mediate their ubiquitination and degradation, thereby precisely regulating the stability and activity of myogenic regulatory factors, as well as the intensity of related signaling pathways (Hughes et al., 2023; Hughes et al., 2021). The ankyrin repeat domain is a widely existing protein-protein interaction module, and several members of this family have been reported to serve as adaptors for E3 ligases, participating in various cellular activities (Michaely and Bennett, 1993; Wolf et al., 2019). ANKRD9 is a relatively understudied member of this family, predicted to function as a substrate receptor for the Cullin-RING E3 ubiquitin ligase complex (Lee et al., 2018). However, current understanding of ANKRD9's function remains extremely limited. Preliminary studies only suggest its potential involvement in cellular metabolic activities, while its specific function and molecular mechanisms in developmental biology, particularly in skeletal myogenesis, remain largely unexplored.

Our previous transcriptome sequencing analysis revealed a crucial clue: ANKRD9 shows high abundance in chicken breast muscle tissue and exhibits significant differential expression patterns across different developmental stages (Li et al., 2019). This finding strongly suggests that ANKRD9 may be a potential regulatory factor playing an important role in chicken skeletal muscle development. Based on its predicted function as an E3 ligase receptor and its dynamic expression during muscle development, we propose the scientific hypothesis that ANKRD9 may exert critical regulatory functions during the proliferation and/or differentiation of chicken myoblasts by mediating ubiquitination and degradation of specific downstream target proteins. To test this hypothesis, this study aims to achieve the following three objectives: (1) to analyze the effects of ANKRD9 gain- and loss-of-function on proliferation and differentiation in chicken primary myoblasts; (2) to screen and validate key downstream targets interacting with ANKRD9; and (3) to elucidate the molecular mechanism through which ANKRD9 regulates myogenesis via these targets.

This study not only reveals for the first time the biological function of ANKRD9 as a novel negative regulator in skeletal myogenesis, but more importantly, successfully identifies IMPDH2 as its key downstream target, thereby establishing an "ANKRD9-IMPDH2" regulatory axis in skeletal muscle development. This finding connects a previously poorly characterized ankyrin repeat protein with a core metabolic enzyme during myogenesis, providing both a fresh perspective and experimental evidence for understanding the complex regulatory network of skeletal muscle development. Furthermore, it offers valuable candidate genes and theoretical foundation for molecular breeding strategies aimed at improving meat yield in poultry.

DiscussionThis study focuses on the precise regulatory mechanisms governing skeletal muscle development. Skeletal myogenesis is a complex biological process orchestrated by multi-layered transcriptional factors and signaling pathways (Liang et al., 2025; Lou et al., 2025). Although the core regulatory network has been preliminarily elucidated, the upstream precise regulatory mechanisms, particularly those involving post-translational modifications, remain a major focus and challenge in the field (Jin et al., 2019; Maggs et al., 2000). Here, we investigate a previously uncharacterized ankyrin repeat domain-containing protein—ANKRD9. Through both in vivo and in vitro functional experiments, we demonstrate that ANKRD9 serves as a novel negative regulator of chicken skeletal myogenesis. More importantly, we further elucidate its molecular mechanism: acting as an adaptor for the Cullin-RING E3 ubiquitin ligase complex (Hayward et al., 2019), ANKRD9 interacts with IMPDH2 and mediates its ubiquitination, thereby playing a crucial role in regulating myoblast proliferation and differentiation. The characterization of the "ANKRD9-IMPDH2" regulatory axis in skeletal muscle development not only provides new perspectives for understanding the regulatory network of myogenesis but also highlights the non-canonical function of metabolic enzymes in determining cell fate.To systematically analyze the function of ANKRD9 in myogenesis, we first performed expression profiling, which revealed a dynamic expression pattern of ANKRD9 during the rapid growth phase of chicken breast muscle, suggesting its potential involvement in muscle development. Transcriptome analysis further demonstrated that ANKRD9 overexpression significantly suppressed processes such as DNA replication and mitotic cell cycle progression, while activating immune system processes and mRNA metabolic processes. These findings suggest that, in addition to its role in skeletal muscle development, ANKRD9 may also participate in muscular metabolic processes, echoing previous reports of its involvement in lipid metabolism in liver tissue (Wang et al., 2009). Concurrently, the activation of immune-related biological functions by ANKRD9 implies its potential broader role in muscle pathological conditions. Furthermore, ANKRD9 overexpression led to the downregulation of multiple proliferation-related marker genes (e.g., CDK1 and PCNA) and the key differentiation regulator MyoD1. CDK1 acts as a critical kinase initiating mitosis (Crncec and Hochegger, 2019; Xiong et al., 2023), while PCNA serves as an essential auxiliary protein for DNA replication (Baserga, 1991; Johnson and Allen, 1993). Together with molecules such as CDK2 and CCND2, they form a sophisticated regulatory network that ensures efficient proliferation of skeletal muscle cells (Gupta et al., 2025; Zhu et al., 2013). MyoD1, as a core member of the myogenic regulatory factors, is a master gene driving myoblast differentiation and fusion (Luo et al., 2015; Mao et al., 2023). These results provide molecular insights into the potential mechanism by which ANKRD9 inhibits myogenesis. In vivo experiments further confirmed that knockdown of ANKRD9 significantly promoted muscle growth and myofiber hypertrophy, collectively establishing its role as a negative regulator of myogenesis.As the significant effects of ANKRD9 on myoblast proliferation and differentiation, we sought to identify factors that might compensate for its function in order to further explore its underlying mechanisms. It is well known that ankyrin-repeat protein families primarily exert their biological functions through protein-protein interactions (Yoon et al., 2020). Therefore, this study employed IP-MS to explore the direct targets of ANKRD9. The results showed that IMPDH2, a key enzyme in the purine metabolism pathway, interacts with ANKRD9, which was further confirmed by cellular expression pattern analysis. IMPDH2 is the rate-limiting enzyme in the de novo purine synthesis pathway (Yam et al., 2006). This study found that after overexpression or interference with ANKRD9, the mRNA level of IMPDH2 did not change significantly, but interference with ANKRD9 significantly increased the protein level of IMPDH2. This result is consistent with previous studies, suggesting that ANKRD9 regulates the protein level of IMPDH2 rather than its transcriptional level (Lee et al., 2018), suggesting that this mode of regulation may be conserved and warrants further investigation across species. Given that ANKRD9 is a substrate receptor subunit of the ubiquitin ligase complex (Lee et al., 2018), we hypothesize that ANKRD9 may regulate the stability of IMPDH2 through the ubiquitination system. Under CHX treatment, the half-life of IMPDH2 protein was shortened. Therefore, IMPDH2 has also been widely studied and confirmed to promote cell proliferation (Wang et al., 2022; Xu et al., 2020; Yuan et al., 2024), but its specific role in myoblasts has not been definitively confirmed.To definitively confirm that the ANKRD9-mediated phenotype depends on IMPDH2, we conducted functional rescue experiments. The results showed that re-expression of IMPDH2 effectively reversed the ANKRD9 overexpression-induced suppression of myoblast proliferation and differentiation blockade, conclusively demonstrating that IMPDH2 serves as the key downstream effector molecule through which ANKRD9 exerts its function. IMPDH2 is established as a central hub linking cellular metabolism and proliferation (Li et al., 2025), responsible for providing nucleotide precursors and GTP energy (Kofuji and Sasaki, 2020; Li et al., 2024a). Furthermore, emerging evidence suggests that cellular metabolic status can influence the binding affinity between ANKRD9 and IMPDH2 (Hayward et al., 2019). Building on these findings, we propose the following regulatory model: in normal myoblasts, IMPDH2 maintains basal expression levels to ensure stable purine nucleotide supply, thereby meeting metabolic demands during proliferation and differentiation. However, when ANKRD9 is aberrantly overexpressed, it compromises IMPDH2 protein stability via ubiquitin-mediated degradation, restricts de novo purine nucleotide biosynthesis, and ultimately impedes myogenesis. In summary, the posttranslational precision regulation of IMPDH2 by ANKRD9 represents a crucial molecular mechanism that coordinates cellular metabolic status with myogenic fate determination through reciprocal feedback.Overall, this study systematically reveals the biological function and molecular mechanism by which ANKRD9, as a novel adaptor for an E3 ubiquitin ligase (Lee et al., 2018), inhibits skeletal muscle development through the negative regulation of IMPDH2 (Fig. 7). This finding expands the functional understanding of ankyrin repeat family proteins and innovatively establishes a direct link between the ubiquitination regulatory system and skeletal myogenesis. Our work not only deepens the comprehension of the complex regulatory network governing skeletal muscle development but also provides an important theoretical foundation and potential targets for molecular breeding aimed at enhancing poultry muscle yield. IMPDH2 is established as a central hub linking cellular metabolism and proliferation (Li et al., 2025), responsible for providing nucleotide precursors and GTP energy (Kofuji and Sasaki, 2020; Li et al., 2024a). Furthermore, emerging evidence suggests that cellular metabolic status can influence the binding affinity between ANKRD9 and IMPDH2 (Hayward et al., 2019). Building on these findings, we propose the following regulatory model: in normal myoblasts, IMPDH2 maintains basal expression levels to ensure stable purine nucleotide supply, thereby meeting metabolic demands during proliferation and differentiation. However, when ANKRD9 is aberrantly overexpressed, it compromises IMPDH2 protein stability via ubiquitin-mediated degradation, restricts de novo purine nucleotide biosynthesis, and ultimately impedes myogenesis. In summary, the posttranslational precision regulation of IMPDH2 by ANKRD9 represents a crucial molecular mechanism that coordinates cellular metabolic status with myogenic fate determination through reciprocal feedback.

Overall, this study systematically reveals the biological function and molecular mechanism by which ANKRD9, as a novel adaptor for an E3 ubiquitin ligase (Lee et al., 2018), inhibits skeletal muscle development through the negative regulation of IMPDH2 (Fig. 7). This finding expands the functional understanding of ankyrin repeat family proteins and innovatively establishes a direct link between the ubiquitination regulatory system and skeletal myogenesis. Our work not only deepens the comprehension of the complex regulatory network governing skeletal muscle development but also provides an important theoretical foundation and potential targets for molecular breeding aimed at enhancing poultry muscle yield.
