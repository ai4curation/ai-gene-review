# Genes for iron metabolism influence circadian rhythms in Drosophila melanogaster.
**Authors:** Mandilaras K, Missirlis F
**Journal:** Metallomics (2012)
**DOI:** [10.1039/c2mt20065a](https://doi.org/10.1039/c2mt20065a)

## Abstract

1. Metallomics. 2012 Aug;4(9):928-36. doi: 10.1039/c2mt20065a.

Genes for iron metabolism influence circadian rhythms in Drosophila
melanogaster.

Mandilaras K(1), Missirlis F.

Author information:
(1)School of Biological and Chemical Sciences, Queen Mary University of London,
London, UK. k.mandilaras@qmul.ac.uk

Haem has been previously implicated in the function of the circadian clock, but
whether iron homeostasis is integrated with circadian rhythms is unknown. Here
we describe an RNA interference (RNAi) screen using clock neurons of Drosophila
melanogaster. RNAi is targeted to iron metabolism genes, including those
involved in haem biosynthesis and degradation. The results indicate that
Ferritin 2 Light Chain Homologue (Fer2LCH) is required for the circadian
activity of flies kept in constant darkness. Oscillations of the core components
in the molecular clock, PER and TIM, were also disrupted following Fer2LCH
silencing. Other genes with a putative function in circadian biology include
Transferrin-3, CG1358 (which has homology to the FLVCR haem export protein) and
five genes implicated in iron-sulfur cluster biosynthesis: the Drosophila
homologues of IscS (CG12264), IscU (CG9836), IscA1 (CG8198), Iba57 (CG8043) and
Nubp2 (CG4858). Therefore, Drosophila genes involved in iron metabolism are
required for a functional biological clock.
