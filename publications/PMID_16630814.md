    Cell. 2006 Dec 15;127(6):1079; author reply 1080-1. doi:
10.1016/j.cell.2006.12.002.

Postmenopausal osteoporosis, a global public health problem, has for decades
been attributed solely to declining estrogen levels. Although FSH levels rise
sharply in parallel, a direct effect of FSH on the skeleton has never been
explored. We show that FSH is required for hypogonadal bone loss. Neither
FSHbeta nor FSH receptor (FSHR) null mice have bone loss despite severe
hypogonadism. Bone mass is increased and osteoclastic resorption is decreased in
haploinsufficient FSHbeta+/- mice with normal ovarian function, suggesting that
the skeletal action of FSH is estrogen independent. Osteoclasts and their
precursors possess G(i2alpha)-coupled FSHRs that activate MEK/Erk, NF-kappaB,
and Akt to result in enhanced osteoclast formation and function. We suggest that
high circulating FSH causes hypogonadal bone loss.
