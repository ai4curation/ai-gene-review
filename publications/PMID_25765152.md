The biogenesis of mitochondrial respiratory chain components is complex.
Mammalian complex I (NADH:ubiquinone oxidoreductase) contains 44 different
subunits, an FMN and seven iron-sulfur centers. Its assembly involves at least
twelve additional proteins, called assembly factors. One of these is FOXRED1, a
486-amino acid FAD-dependent oxidoreductase. FOXRED1 is a member of the d-amino
acid oxidase (DAO) family. A structural model of FOXRED1 reveals a large
substrate-binding cavity and a putative oxygen-binding site. These features
strongly suggest that FOXRED1 is catalytically active as an oxidoreductase. A
metabolic role for FOXRED1 in the biogenesis of complex I should be considered.
