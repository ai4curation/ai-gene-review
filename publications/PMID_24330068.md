# Nuclear Raf-1 kinase regulates the CXCR5 promoter by associating with NFATc3 to drive retinoic acid-induced leukemic cell differentiation.
**Authors:** Geil WM, Yen A

## Abstract

1. FEBS J. 2014 Feb;281(4):1170-80. doi: 10.1111/febs.12693. Epub 2014 Jan 10.

Nuclear Raf-1 kinase regulates the CXCR5 promoter by associating with NFATc3 to
drive retinoic acid-induced leukemic cell differentiation.

Geil WM(1), Yen A.

Author information:
(1)Department of Biomedical Sciences, Cornell University, Ithaca, NY, USA.

Novel functions of signaling molecules have been revealed in studies of cancer
stem cells. Retinoic acid (RA) is an embryonic morphogen and stem cell regulator
that controls the differentiation of a patient-derived leukemic cell line,
HL-60, which is composed of progenitor cells with bipotent myelo-monocytic
differentiation capability. RA treatment of HL-60 cells causes unusually
long-lasting mitogen-activated protein kinase signaling, with the cells
exhibiting the beginning of G0 cell cycle arrest and functional differentiation
by 48 h after treatment with RA. This event coincides with the nuclear
translocation of Raf-1, phosphorylated at serine 621. The present study shows
how the novel localization of Raf-1 to the nucleus results in transcriptional
changes that contribute to the differentiation of HL-60 cells induced by RA. We
find that nuclear pS621 Raf-1 associates with NFATc3 near its cognate binding
site in the promoter of CXCR5, a gene that must be up-regulated to drive
RA-induced differentiation. NFATc3 becomes immunoprecipitable with
anti-phosphoserine serum, and CXCR5 is transcriptionally up-regulated upon
RA-induced differentiation. Inhibiting the pS621 Raf-1/NFATc3 association with
PD98059 inhibits these processes and cripples RA-induced differentiation. In
this novel paradigm for Raf-1 and RA function, Raf-1 has a role in driving the
nuclear signaling of RA-induced differentiation of leukemic progenitor cells.

Introduction

Retinoic acid (RA), a metabolite of Vitamin A, is a necessary dietary factor with a role in the differentiation processes of multiple cell types.[1] As a chemotherapeutic agent, it is best known for inducing myeloid differentiation in acute promyelocytic leukemia (APL, FAB M3), a subtype of acute myeloblastic leukemia (AML), which features undifferentiated cancerous progenitor cells.[2] While the therapeutic benefit of RA has been evident for several decades, unresponsive myeloid leukemias, as well as occurrence of resistance in APL motivate our efforts to seek further mechanistic insight. The classical paradigm of RA-induced differentiation of these leukemic cells focuses on two types of transcription factors, retinoic acid receptors (RARs) and retinoid X receptors (RXRs), that are activated by binding to their ligands, RA and other retinoids; however, many major aspects of leukemic progenitor cell differentiation also implicate other signaling pathways, notably MAPK.

HL-60 cells (FAB M2) are lineage bipotent, RA-sensitive leukemic progenitor cells derived from a patient with AML. RA induces these cells to undergo myeloid differentiation and G0 cell cycle arrest. This process requires RA-induced expression of the CXCR5 gene product, also known as BLR1, which is necessary for RA-induced differentiation.[3] The expression of CXCR5, which is also found in mature B cells and Burkitt’s lymphoma, is transcriptionally up-regulated by RA and promotes signaling along the MAPK axis and ERK activation,[4] triggering a positive feedback loop of MAPK signaling. NFATc3 and Oct1 binding at their cognate sites in the CXCR5 promoter are necessary for the transcriptional activation of CXCR5 by RA.[5]

Raf-1 (also called c-Raf) also plays a major role in RA-induced differentiation. In its classical MAPK pathway role, Raf-1 is recruited to the cytosolic side of the plasma membrane and activated by Ras; then Raf-1 phosphorylates MEK, which phosphorylates ERK, which constitutes the transient MAPK signaling leading to mitogenesis.[6] Site-specific phosphorylation of Raf-1 regulates its activity; for example, phosphorylation of serine 259 is required for the autophosphorylation of serine 621, which is required for Raf-1 to bind to an adaptor 14-3-3ζ.[7] However, in HL-60 cells, differentiation due to RA depends on a long-lasting MAPK signal with the up-regulation and unanticipated translocation of Raf-1 to the nucleus.[3, 8, 9] Whereas the transient signal is predominantly associated with the classical paradigm of growth factor induced mitogenesis, the durable, long lasting signal is thought to be seminal to differentiation. The prototype of this paradigm is that of PC12 pheochromocytoma cells undergoing mitogenesis versus neural differentiation when treated with EGF versus NGF.[10]

It is an outstanding question as to what the durable signal causes that is mechanistically distinct from the transient signal. While activated ERK is also up-regulated,[11] other MAPK kinases, including JNK/SAPK and p38, are not involved in RA-induced differentiation.[12] Differentiation is independent of Ras, further indicating the non-canonical characteristics of this pathway.[6, 13] The overexpression of c-Cbl, Irf-1, or AhR can drive prolonged MAPK signaling leading to enhanced differentiation.[14–16] Transfectants overexpressing Raf-1 also differentiate faster compared to control transfectants. In contrast, siRNA knockdown of Raf-1 diminishes RA-induced CXCR5 expression and differentiation.[3] RA-resistant HL-60 cells do not show RA-induced up-regulation of Raf-1, nor increased phosphorylation at serine 621; however, the Src family kinase inhibitor PP2 restores the ability for the cells to undergo RA-induced differentiation.[17] Consistent with the dependence of RA-induced differentiation on Raf-1 signaling, inhibition of differentiation is observed in HL-60 cells after treatment with the Raf-1 inhibitor, GW5074, or the MAPK pathway inhibitor, PD98059.[3, 11] However, the functional consequences of the RA-induced nuclear translocation of Raf-1 remain to be elucidated.

In this study, the RA-dependent association of nuclear Raf-1 kinase to the transcription factor NFATc3 at the CXCR5 promoter is described. Consistent with RA-induced Raf-1 binding, NFATc3 becomes immunoprecipitable with an anti-phosphoserine antibody in an RA-dependent manner. The association of pS621 Raf-1 with the CXCR5 promoter in the proximity of the NFATc3 recognition site is shown through chromatin immunoprecipitation (ChIP). The MAPK pathway inhibitor PD98059 inhibits the RA-induced pS621 Raf-1 association with the transcription factor NFATc3. There is diminished putative serine phosphorylation of NFATc3 in response to PD98059 treatment. Consistent with this, pS621 Raf-1 association at the CXCR5 promoter in the proximity of the NFATc3 recognition site is inhibited by PD98059. Furthermore, transcription of CXCR5 is blocked as evidenced by lack of the CXCR5 transcript in cells treated with PD98059 plus RA compared to RA alone. As anticipated, PD98059 cripples RA-induced differentiation. These results suggest that the nuclear signaling function of pS621 Raf-1 provides propulsion to the process of RA-induced differentiation in leukemic progenitor cells.

Here, we show a novel mechanism of transcriptional regulation by the Raf-1 kinase used to propel RA-induced differentiation. In particular, we find that pS621 Raf-1 associates with NFATc3 in the nucleus, making NFATc3 immunoprecipitable with an anti-phosphoserine antibody. In chromatin immunoprecipitation experiments, Raf-1 co-precipitates with the NFATc3 binding site on the CXCR5 promoter. An illustration of the role of nuclear Raf-1 is shown in Figure 5. This is the prototype of a hypothetical paradigm whereby RA causes translocation of pS621 Raf-1 to the nucleus where it associates with NFATc3, causing its serine phosphorylation, at the promoter of CXCR5, enabling transcriptional activation of CXCR5, expression of which is needed to drive differentiation.[4] The MAPK pathway inhibitor, PD98059, stops this process, blocking the upregulation of CXCR5, a gene necessary for RA-induced differentiation, and thus inhibiting differentiation.

There is accumulating evidence for the role of Raf-1 in driving RA-induced differentiation. The total amount of phosphorylated Raf-1 increases in the process of RA-induced differentiation in HL-60 cells in a RA dose-dependent manner.[9, 22] Raf-1 phosphorylation and differentiation of HL-60 cells also increase during TPA-induced monocytic differentiation of HL-60 cells.[23] The association of pS259 Raf-1 and Lyn increases during RA-induced differentiation.[19] The amount of pS621 Raf-1 in the cytoplasm increases with RA treatment, as shown in Figure 1, as does pS621 Raf-1 that is localized to the nucleus.[8] Both serine 259 and serine 621 are in the CR3 domain of Raf-1; and the overexpression of the CR3 domain, like the overexpression of Raf-1 or CXCR5, enhances RA-induced differentiation.[3] Interestingly, the ectopic expression of CR3 restores the ability of CXCR5 knockout cells to undergo RA-induced differentiation,[3] but the expression of CXCR5 is both Erk dependent and Erk activating.[4, 21]

While historically regarded as a cytosolic signaling protein recruited to the plasma membrane to drive the Raf/Mek/Erk axis of MAPK mitogenic signaling,[24] Raf-1 has also been shown to have nuclear localization and function in non-canonical MAPK signaling. In an in silico model of HL-60 cells, Raf-1, but not Erk, was identified as the critical component in the RA-induced MAPK cascade resulting in differentiation and G0 arrest.[25] Nuclear Mek increases, as does the nuclear Mek/Raf-1 interaction, when JAK proteins are inhibited in leukemic progenitor cells.[26] Raf-1 has kinase activity in the nucleus of HL-60 cells where it can phosphorylate chromatin regulatory proteins.[26] Its RA-induced association with the CXCR5 promoter and putative phosphorylation of NFATc3 suggest that phosphorylation by Raf-1 may cause the conformational change in NFATc3 protein structure needed to cause the up-regulation of CXCR5 that drives differentiation and G0 arrest.

We found that PD98059 effectively blocks the RA-induced association of pS621 Raf-1 with NFATc3 and thus transcriptional activation of the CXCR5 promoter (figures 2A, 3, and 4). PD98059 inhibits the phosphorylation of Erk by Mek1 or Raf-1 in vitro,[27] it also in vivo inhibits the global RA-induced phosphorylation of Raf-1,[9] and the phosphorylation of Erk 1/2 in HL-60 cells.[3, 11] Previous experiments showed that PD98059 decreases the amount of pS621 Raf-1 in total cell lysate after 12 hr of treatment; [3] however, the present results show increased amounts of Raf-1 and pS621 Raf-1 (Figure 2B and 2C) after 48 hr of treatment, all compared to untreated control cells. This may indicate that the inhibition of the MAPK kinase pathway drives the stoichiometric increase of Raf-1 at later times after treatment (Figure 2B and 2C), possibly betraying feedback pathways. Ergo, the mechanistic details of exactly how PD98059 prevented the association of pS621 Raf-1 and NFATc3 may be complex and remain to be elucidated.

Raf-1 function in various contexts is controlled by site-specific phosphorylation. The evidence reported in this paper and in our earlier work suggests that in HL-60 and related leukemic progenitor cells, phosphorylation at serine 621 is associated with Raf-1 activation in a non-canonical, durable MAPK signal that propels RA-induced differentiation.[8] Raf-1 auto-phosphorylates serine 621,[28] and its phosphorylation is necessary for binding to 14-3-3 proteins.[7] Other phosphorylation sites (serines, threonines, and tyrosines) on Raf-1 control binding to various other proteins.[6] While serine 621 appears to be implicated in differentiation control, the potential roles of other sites remain to be elucidated.

Non-canonical MAPK signaling is a key component in the differentiation of certain cells (reviewed in Murakami et al).[29] Pheochromocytoma cells treated with NGF exhibit prolonged MAPK signal activation that led to differentiation in contrast to transient activation by EGF that led to mitogenesis (10) Primary cultures of rat heptacytes show different MAPK pathway activation effects; transient pathway activation in response NGF led to increased DNA synthesis, whereas prolonged pathway activation from Erk overexpression or chemically-induced sensitivity to NGF led to an increase in cell cycle arrest proteins and inhibition of DNA synthesis.[30] In an early study, Kuo and coworkers showed that Raf-1, and not Mek or Erk, was sufficient to induce differentiation in rat hippocampal neurons.[31] Small cell lung cancer cells (which were demonstrated to be undifferentiated) and prostate cancer cells that were transfected to overexpress Raf-1 underwent growth arrest.[32, 33] In an elegant study, the ability of Raf-1 to induce transformation and differentiation was disconnected from the rest of the Mek/Erk cascade (and thus proliferation) by the mutation of serine 259 to alanine; specifically, S259A Raf-1 overexpression blocked PC12 cells differentiation, as opposed to WT Raf-1.[34] In the development of Xenopus embryos, Raf-1 and MAPK signaling are tightly regulated, with Raf-1 required for early stages of development.[35, 36] Ras-independent Raf/Mek/Erk activation is a prominent feature of calcium-induced differentiation of human keratinocytes.[37] Non-canonical MAPK signaling in stem cells and differentiation is thus revealing new paradigms for MAPK signaling.

NFATc3, part of the NFAT/Rel1 family of proteins, and Oct1, a POUF1-domain protein are required for CXCR5 activation, and both seem to be constitutively bound to the CXCR5 promoter.[5] Both are active in a variety of signaling pathways, and they are known to be regulated by phosphorylation in the nucleus.[38, 39] In T cells, NFAT proteins are hyperphosphorylated in the cytoplasm; and when the T cell is activated, they becomes dephosphorylated by Ca2+/calmodulin activated-calcineurin, and they enter the nucleus to regulate transcription.[38] In contrast, here we report that the NFATc3 in the cytoplasm is not phosphorylated, but is in the nucleus. Unlike T cells that are quiescent until activated, the myeloid cells studied here are actively proliferating, and exhibit a mechanism of action different from the classical model. Interestingly, it is reported that Ca2+ and calmodulin are not regulatory mediators of RA-induced differentiation of these cells. This is consistent with the presently revealed lack of need to convert hyperphosphorylated NFAT to hypophosphorylated NFAT since the cytoplasmic NFAT is already hypophosphorylated.[40] The present study shows that activation of the CXCR5 promoter by NFATc3 is likely due to its phosphorylation by Raf-1 or a related signaling partner bound to Raf-1. This thus suggests a new paradigm for Raf-1 function and MAPK signaling in regulating NFATc3-dependent gene expression.

In conclusion, the novel translocation of the MAPK kinase Raf-1 to the nucleus of lineage uncommitted leukemic stem-like cells undergoing RA-induced differentiation has signaling effects on the process of differentiation. Raf-1 associates with a vital transcription factor, NFATc3, for the up-regulation of a gene required for the differentiation process. This new regulatory role for gene transcription further augments the picture of the function of Raf-1.
