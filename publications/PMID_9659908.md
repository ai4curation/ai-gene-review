# FMRP associates with polyribosomes as an mRNP, and the I304N mutation of severe fragile X syndrome abolishes this association.
**Authors:** Feng Y, Absher D, Eberhart DE, Brown V, Malter HE, Warren ST
**Journal:** Mol Cell (1997)
**DOI:** [10.1016/s1097-2765(00)80012-x](https://doi.org/10.1016/s1097-2765(00)80012-x)

## Abstract

1. Mol Cell. 1997 Dec;1(1):109-18. doi: 10.1016/s1097-2765(00)80012-x.

FMRP associates with polyribosomes as an mRNP, and the I304N mutation of severe
fragile X syndrome abolishes this association.

Feng Y(1), Absher D, Eberhart DE, Brown V, Malter HE, Warren ST.

Author information:
(1)Howard Hughes Medical Institute, Department of Biochemistry, Emory University
School of Medicine, Atlanta, Georgia 30322, USA.

Fragile X mental retardation is caused by the lack of FMRP, a selective
RNA-binding protein associated with ribosomes. A missense mutation, I304N, has
been found to result in an unusually severe phenotype. We show here that normal
FMRP associates with elongating polyribosomes via large mRNP particles. Despite
normal expression and cytoplasmic mRNA association, the I304N FMRP is
incorporated into abnormal mRNP particles that are not associated with
polyribosomes. These data indicate that association of FMRP with polyribosomes
must be functionally important and imply that the mechanism of the severe
phenotype in the I304N patient lies in the sequestration of bound mRNAs in
nontranslatable mRNP particles. In the absence of FMRP, these same mRNAs may be
partially translated via alternative mRNPs, although perhaps abnormally
localized or regulated, resulting in typical fragile X syndrome.
