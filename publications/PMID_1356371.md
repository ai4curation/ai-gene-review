# Molecular cloning of a retina-specific membrane guanylyl cyclase.
**Authors:** Shyjan AW, de Sauvage FJ, Gillett NA, Goeddel DV, Lowe DG
**Journal:** Neuron (1992)
**DOI:** [10.1016/0896-6273(92)90035-c](https://doi.org/10.1016/0896-6273(92)90035-c)

## Abstract

1. Neuron. 1992 Oct;9(4):727-37. doi: 10.1016/0896-6273(92)90035-c.

Molecular cloning of a retina-specific membrane guanylyl cyclase.

Shyjan AW(1), de Sauvage FJ, Gillett NA, Goeddel DV, Lowe DG.

Author information:
(1)Department of Molecular Biology, Genentech, Inc., South San Francisco,
California 94080.

We have isolated and characterized cDNA clones encoding the human retinal
guanylyl cyclase (retGC), a novel member of the membrane guanylyl cyclase gene
family. Like other membrane guanylyl cyclases, the 1101 aa retGC is predicted to
have a hydrophobic amino-terminal signal sequence followed by a large
extracellular domain, a single membrane spanning domain, a kinase homology
domain, and a guanylyl cyclase catalytic domain. In contrast to other membrane
guanylyl cyclases, such as natriuretic peptide receptors, retGC has a relatively
high basal level of activity when expressed in human 293 cells. cGMP production
by retGC is unaffected by any of the known natriuretic peptides. In situ
hybridization analysis of a variety of rhesus monkey tissues showed retGC
transcripts to be localized exclusively along the retinal outer nuclear layer,
corresponding to the nuclei of the rod and cone photoreceptor cells. Our results
suggest that retGC may synthesize cGMP required for recovery of the dark state
after phototransduction.
