The alarmone ppGpp selectively inhibits the isoform A of the human small GTPase
Sar1.

Author information:
(1)Cornell High Energy Synchrotron Source (CHESS), Cornell University, Ithaca,

Transport of newly synthesized proteins from endoplasmic reticulum (ER) to Golgi
is mediated by coat protein complex II (COPII). The assembly and disassembly of
COPII vesicles is regulated by the molecular switch Sar1, which is a small
GTPase and a component of COPII. Usually a small GTPase binds GDP (inactive
form) or GTP (active form). Mammals have two Sar1 isoforms, Sar1a and Sar1b,
that have approximately 90% sequence identity. Some experiments demonstrated
that these two isoforms had distinct but overlapping functions. Here we found
another instance of differing behavior: the alarmone ppGpp could bind to and
inhibit the GTPase activity of human Sar1a but could not inhibit the GTPase
activity of human Sar1b. The crystal structures of Sar1a⋅ppGpp and Sar1b⋅GDP
have been determined. Superposition of the structures shows that ppGpp binds to
the nucleotide-binding pocket, its guanosine base, ribose ring and
5'-diphosphate occupying nearly the same positions as for GDP. However, its
3'-diphosphate points away from the active site and, hence, away from the
surface of the protein. The overall structure of Sar1a⋅ppGpp is more similar to
Sar1b⋅GDP than to Sar1b⋅GTP. We also find that the Asp140-Arg138-water-ligand
interaction net is important for the binding of ppGpp to Sar1a. This study
provides further evidence showing that there are biochemical differences between
the Sar1a and Sar1b isoforms of Sar1.

Besides its well-established role in ER-to-Golgi trafficking, Sar1 also has other functions independent of COPII. Sar1 may have a role within the nucleus that relates to the proper formation of the nuclear envelope. Inhibition of either the nuclear entry or retention of Sar1 leads to a severe growth defect of yeast. In vivo deletion of Sar1 results in an abnormal nuclear envelope shape14. Sar1 can also regulate the size of ER-mitochondrial contact sites through its effects on membrane curvature15. Recently, Sar1b has been reported to act as a leucine sensor that regulates mTORC1 signaling in response to intracellular levels of leucine16.

Sar1 is well conserved throughout evolution17,18. Human Sar1 and the Sar1 from the single cell organism yeast have ~80% homology (identical and strongly similar) in their amino acid sequences. Yeast and many invertebrates have a single Sar1 protein. Mammals and most other vertebrates have two isoforms, which have approximately 90% sequence identity between them. In human, the two isoforms of Sar1 are designated Sar1a and Sar1b respectively; they differ by 20 out of the 198 amino acid residues.

Genetic, cellular and biochemical evidence suggests that the two Sar1 isoforms, Sar1a and Sar1b, have divergent roles. Sar1a and Sar1b are expressed in enterocytes. Sar1b, but not Sar1a, regulates transporting newly-synthesized chylomicrons out of epithelial cells. Preparation of ppGppppGpp was produced according to the methods described by Mechol et al. with modification23. Briefly, a 3ml reaction solution containing 50mM Tris-acetate, pH7.8, 3.3mM magnesium acetate, 60mM potassium acetate, 30mM ammonium acetate, 1mM DTT, 8mM ATP and 8mM GDP was initiated by adding 300μg of purified RelP and incubated at 37°C. At intervals of 15 minutes, 20μl of the reaction mixture was withdrawn and ppGpp was assayed using the method described by Zheng24, till the amount of ppGpp didn’t increase anymore. After adding 1.5ml of phenol-chloroform-isoamyl alcohol (25:24:1, v/v/v) to the mixture, it was mixed briefly and then centrifuged at 10,000g for 5 minutes at 4°C. The suspension was mixed, incubated on ice for 30 minutes and pelleted at 6,000g for 10 minutes. The pellet was washed twice with absolute ethanol to remove residual LiCl and dried, then dissolved in distilled water to make 100mM stock solution (assuming all GDP in the reaction mixture has been converted into ppGpp; the actual concentration should be less than 100mM.). The crude ppGpp prepared by this method contains residual ATP, ADP and GDP.

Sar1am2 is a dimer in the crystal. As shown in Figure 4, the ligand is still ppGpp in subunit A, while it is GDP in subunit B. There are no “calcium bridges” present in Sar1am2. In Sar1aec and Sar1aic, the side chain of R138 interacts with ppGpp though a water molecule and also forms H-bonds with the side chain of Asp140, forming a D140-R138-water-ppGpp (or GDP) interaction net (Fig. 5A). In contrast, in Sar1b the side chain of R138 moves away from the nucleotide-binding pocket and does not form any interaction with the ligand through water molecules. It is possible that the side chain of residue140 results in this difference: residue140 is Asp in Sar1a while it is Glu in Sar1b; Glu has a side chain longer than that of Asp; in Sar1b, the long side chain of E140 sits between the ligand and R138, forcing the side chain of R138 to move away from the ligand; in contrast, D140 of Sar1a has a short side chain which doesn’t block the side chain of R138. However, in the presence of 20mM CaCl2 (or 20mM MgCl2), the molecular weight of Sar1aec is 23kDa as determined by gel filtration, corresponding to a monomer (Fig. S8). SAXS also shows that Sar1aec is a monomer in solution at the presence of calcium (Fig. S9). Therefore, Ca2+ or Mg2+ cannot help Sar1a·ppGpp to form a stable dimer in solution. On the other hand, the double mutant (D104A and D140A) Sar1am2 could be crystallized in conditions without Ca2+ or Mg2+. The crystal structure shows that the ligand of Sar1am2 is ppGpp (partially), but the “calcium bridges” do not exist (Fig. 4), suggesting that the Ca2+ or Mg2+ ions are not necessary for the binding of ppGpp to Sar1a.

Therefore, Sar1aec is a monomer in solution; the “calcium bridges” do not exist in solution. Ca2+ or Mg2+ ions can help Sar1aec to crystallize but can neither help it to form a stable dimer in solution nor help it to bind ppGpp.

Sar1a·GDP (Sar1aic), Sar1aT39N, Sar1aH79G, Sar1b·GDP, Sar1bT39N and Sar1bH79G (GTP-bound form) are all monomers in solution with apparent molecular weight around 23 kDa as determined by gel filtration (Fig. S10).

The bacterial GTPase Obg is the first reported structure of a GTPase in complex with ppGpp41. Superposition of Sar1a·ppGpp and the GTPase domain of Obg·ppGpp shows that ppGpp binds to a very similar position in Sra1a and Obg, with the GDP moiety being partially buried and the 3’-diphosphate group of ppGpp pointing away from the surface of the protein, with no interaction with the protein (Fig. 7A). It appears that the 3’-diphosphate group is not involved in the interaction between ppGpp and the protein, and it is only the GDP moiety of ppGpp that is involved in ligand-protein interaction. Therefore, it is reasonable to infer that a GTPase is able to bind to both ppGpp and GDP if its GDP-binding site is similar to those of human Sar1a and Obg (i.e. the protein itself does not interfere with the 3’-diphosphate group of ppGpp). However, this is not true: ppGpp can bind to and inhibit the GTPase activity of human Sar1a but cannot inhibit the GTPase activity of Sar1b although Sar1b has a GDP-binding site similar to that of Sar1a.

The Sar1b isoform of human Sar1 has 90% amino acid sequence identity to the Sar1a isoform and similar three-dimensional structure. Like Sar1a, it is a monomer in solution. When a ppGpp molecule is docked into its nucleotide-binding pocket according to the Sar1a·ppGpp structure, the 3’-diphosphate group of ppGpp points away from the surface of the protein, with no interaction with the protein, and will not block the formation of quaternary structure. The GDP moiety of the docking ppGpp overlaps the GDP molecule bound at the active site of Sar1b almost exactly (Fig. 7B). The residues that have interactions with the GDP moiety are nearly all the same among Sar1a and Sar1b. Accordingly, the isoform Sar1b is expected to be able to bind to ppGpp and be inhibited by ppGpp. However, in our study ppGpp did not inhibit the GTPase activity of Sar1b (Table 2). Even when Sar1b was incubated with a high concentration of ppGpp (~5mM) before crystallization, no ppGpp could be seen in the crystal structure. In contrast, when Sar1aic (GDP-bound) was incubated with a high concentration of ppGpp (~5mM) before crystallization, ppGpp was seen in the crystal structure.

Human Cdc42 is another small GTPase42; it has low sequence homology to Sar1a but a similar three-dimensional structure. When a ppGpp molecule is docked into its nucleotide-binding pocket according to the Sar1a·ppGpp structure, the GDP moiety of ppGpp overlaps the GDP molecule bound at the active site of Cdc42, with the 3’-diphosphate group of ppGpp extending out from the surface of the protein (Fig S11). Hence, Cdc42 should also be able to bind to ppGpp and be inhibited by ppGpp. However, in fact ppGpp does not inhibit the GTPase activity of Cdc42 (Table 2).

Similarly, for other small GTPases, such as Rab and Ran, and even the hamster Sar1, which were expressed in E.coli, their crystal structures contain GDP but not ppGpp7. The question now is why Sar1b and other small GTPases were not able to bind to ppGpp as Sar1a did even under conditions with a high concentration of ppGpp (such as the E.coli. cell which might contain up to mM amount of ppGpp)? Comparison of the protein-ligand interactions between Sar1a and Sar1b shows that Sar1a has more protein-ligand interactions than Sar1b: Sar1a has the extra D140-R138-water-ppGpp interaction net. Sar1a also has more extensive protein-ligand interactions than Cdc42. Therefore, a GTPase binding to ppGpp may require more protein-ligand interactions than binding to GDP.
