# Msx1 deficient mice exhibit cleft palate and abnormalities of craniofacial and tooth development.
**Authors:** Satokata I, Maas R
**Journal:** Nat Genet (1994)
**DOI:** [10.1038/ng0494-348](https://doi.org/10.1038/ng0494-348)

## Abstract

1. Nat Genet. 1994 Apr;6(4):348-56. doi: 10.1038/ng0494-348.

Msx1 deficient mice exhibit cleft palate and abnormalities of craniofacial and
tooth development.

Satokata I(1), Maas R.

Author information:
(1)Department of Medicine, Brigham and Women's Hospital, Boston, Massachusetts
02115.

Comment in
    Nat Genet. 1994 Apr;6(4):329-30. doi: 10.1038/ng0494-329.

The Msx1 homeobox gene is expressed at diverse sites of epithelial-mesenchymal
interaction during vertebrate embryogenesis, and has been implicated in
signalling processes between tissue layers. To determine the phenotypic
consequences of its deficiency, we prepared mice lacking Msx1 function. All
Msx1- homozygotes manifest a cleft secondary palate, a deficiency of alveolar
mandible and maxilla and a failure of tooth development. These mice also exhibit
abnormalities of the nasal, frontal and parietal bones, and of the malleus in
the middle ear. Msx1 thus has a critical role in mediating
epithelial-mesenchymal interactions during craniofacial bone and tooth
development. The Msx1-/Msx1- phenotype is similar to human cleft palate, and
provides a genetic model for cleft palate and oligodontia in which the defective
gene is known.
