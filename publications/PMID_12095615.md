# Characterization of bacterial homocitrate synthase involved in lysine biosynthesis.
**Authors:** Wulandari AP, Miyazaki J, Kobashi N, Nishiyama M, Hoshino T, Yamane H
**Journal:** FEBS Lett (2002)
**DOI:** [10.1016/s0014-5793(02)02877-6](https://doi.org/10.1016/s0014-5793(02)02877-6)

## Abstract

1. FEBS Lett. 2002 Jul 3;522(1-3):35-40. doi: 10.1016/s0014-5793(02)02877-6.

Characterization of bacterial homocitrate synthase involved in lysine
biosynthesis.

Wulandari AP(1), Miyazaki J, Kobashi N, Nishiyama M, Hoshino T, Yamane H.

Author information:
(1)Biotechnology Research Center, The University of Tokyo, 1-1-1 Yayoi,
Bunkyo-ku, Japan.

In Thermus thermophilus homocitrate synthase (HCS) catalyzes the initial
reaction of lysine biosynthesis through alpha-aminoadipic acid, synthesis of
homocitrate from 2-oxoglutarate and acetyl-CoA. HCS is strongly inhibited by
lysine, indicating that the biosynthesis is regulated by the endproduct at the
initial reaction in the pathway. HCS also catalyzes the reaction using
oxaloacetate in place of 2-oxoglutarate as a substrate, similar to citrate
synthase in the tricarboxylic acid cycle. Several other properties of Thermus
HCS and an evolutionary relationship of the biosynthetic pathway in the
bacterium to other metabolic pathways are also described.
