# The Sec34/35 Golgi transport complex is related to the exocyst, defining a family of complexes involved in multiple steps of membrane traffic.
**Authors:** Whyte JR, Munro S
**Journal:** Dev Cell (2001)
**DOI:** [10.1016/s1534-5807(01)00063-6](https://doi.org/10.1016/s1534-5807(01)00063-6)

## Abstract

1. Dev Cell. 2001 Oct;1(4):527-37. doi: 10.1016/s1534-5807(01)00063-6.

The Sec34/35 Golgi transport complex is related to the exocyst, defining a
family of complexes involved in multiple steps of membrane traffic.

Whyte JR(1), Munro S.

Author information:
(1)MRC Laboratory of Molecular Biology, Cambridge, United Kingdom.

The specificity of intracellular vesicle transport is mediated in part by
tethering factors that attach the vesicle to the destination organelle prior to
fusion. We have identified a protein, Dor1p, that is involved in vesicle
targeting to the yeast Golgi apparatus and found it to be associated with seven
further proteins. Identification of these revealed that they include Sec34p and
Sec35p, the two known components of the Sec34/35 complex previously proposed to
tether vesicles to the Golgi. Of the six previously uncharacterized components,
four have homologs in higher eukaryotes, including a subunit of a mammalian
Golgi transport complex. Furthermore, several of the proteins show distant
homology to components of two other putative tethering complexes, the exocyst
and the Vps52/53/54 complex, revealing that tethering factors involved in
different membrane traffic steps are structurally related.
