# Structure of the coat protein-binding domain of the scaffolding protein from a double-stranded DNA virus.
**Authors:** Sun Y, Parker MH, Weigele P, Casjens S, Prevelige PE Jr, Krishna NR
**Journal:** J Mol Biol (2000)
**DOI:** [10.1006/jmbi.2000.3620](https://doi.org/10.1006/jmbi.2000.3620)

## Abstract

1. J Mol Biol. 2000 Apr 14;297(5):1195-202. doi: 10.1006/jmbi.2000.3620.

Structure of the coat protein-binding domain of the scaffolding protein from a
double-stranded DNA virus.

Sun Y(1), Parker MH, Weigele P, Casjens S, Prevelige PE Jr, Krishna NR.

Author information:
(1)Comprehensive Cancer Center, Birmingham, AL, 35294, USA.

Scaffolding proteins are required for high fidelity assembly of most high T
number dsDNA viruses such as the large bacteriophages, and the herpesvirus
family. They function by transiently binding and positioning the coat protein
subunits during capsid assembly. In both bacteriophage P22 and the herpesviruses
the extreme scaffold C terminus is highly charged, is predicted to be an
amphipathic alpha-helix, and is sufficient to bind the coat protein, suggesting
a common mode of action. NMR studies show that the coat protein-binding domain
of P22 scaffolding protein exhibits a helix-loop-helix motif stabilized by a
hydrophobic core. One face of the motif is characterized by a high density of
positive charges that could interact with the coat protein through electrostatic
interactions. Results from previous studies with a truncation fragment and the
observed salt sensitivity of the assembly process are explained by the NMR
structure.
