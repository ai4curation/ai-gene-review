# Isolation and characterization of a hydrogen peroxide resistant mutant of Bacillus subtilis.
**Authors:** Hartford OM, Dowds BC
**Journal:** Microbiology (Reading) (1994)
**DOI:** [10.1099/13500872-140-2-297](https://doi.org/10.1099/13500872-140-2-297)

## Abstract

1. Microbiology (Reading). 1994 Feb;140 ( Pt 2):297-304. doi:
10.1099/13500872-140-2-297.

Isolation and characterization of a hydrogen peroxide resistant mutant of
Bacillus subtilis.

Hartford OM(1), Dowds BC.

Author information:
(1)Department of Biology, St Patrick's College, Maynooth, Co. Kildare, Ireland.

A mutant of Bacillus subtilis has been isolated by continuous selection in
increasing concentrations of H2O2. It grew with a doubling time of 85 min in
minimal medium containing 150 mM H2O2, whereas the wild-type parent lysed in 100
mM H2O2. The mutant was also more resistant to organic peroxides than the
wild-type. Further resistance to H2O2 could not be induced by pretreatment with
low concentrations of the oxidant. The mutant synthesized a number of proteins
at a much higher rate than the wild-type, including constitutive synthesis of
all of the proteins which were induced by H2O2 in the wild-type. Four of these
proteins were sequenced; three were identified as catalase and two subunits of
alkyl hydroperoxide reductase. Two proteins whose synthesis was repressed in the
mutant were sequenced, and one was identified as flagellin. The mutant grew as
non-flagellated, partially septate, filaments of cells, and fragments of
flagella were seen in the surrounding medium.
