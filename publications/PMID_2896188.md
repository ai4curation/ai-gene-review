# Arg-220 of the PstA protein is required for phosphate transport through the phosphate-specific transport system in Escherichia coli but not for alkaline phosphatase repression.
**Authors:** Cox GB, Webb D, Godovac-Zimmermann J, Rosenberg H
**Journal:** J Bacteriol (1988)
**DOI:** [10.1128/jb.170.5.2283-2286.1988](https://doi.org/10.1128/jb.170.5.2283-2286.1988)
**PMC:** [PMC211119](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC211119/)

## Abstract

1. J Bacteriol. 1988 May;170(5):2283-6. doi: 10.1128/jb.170.5.2283-2286.1988.

Arg-220 of the PstA protein is required for phosphate transport through the
phosphate-specific transport system in Escherichia coli but not for alkaline
phosphatase repression.

Cox GB(1), Webb D, Godovac-Zimmermann J, Rosenberg H.

Author information:
(1)Department of Biochemistry, John Curtin School of Medical Research,
Australian National University, Canberra City.

The pstA gene encodes an integral membrane protein of the phosphate-specific
transport system of Escherichia coli. The nucleotide change in the previously
described pstA2 allele was found to be a G----A substitution at position 276 of
the nucleotide sequence, resulting in the premature termination of translation.
Three mutations in the pstA gene were produced by site-directed mutagenesis. The
amino acid substitutions resulting from the three site-directed mutations were
Arg-170----Gln, Glu-173----Gln, and Arg-220----Gln. These amino acid residues
were selected because a previous PstA protein structure prediction placed them
within the membrane. The Arg-220----Gln mutation resulted in the loss of
phosphate transport through the phosphate-specific transport system, but the
alkaline phosphatase activity remained repressed. Neither the Arg-170----Gln nor
the Glu-173----Gln mutation affected phosphate transport. The results are
discussed in relation to a proposed structure of the PstA protein.
