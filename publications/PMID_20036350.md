Patients with Joubert syndrome 2 (JBTS2) suffer from a neurological disease
manifested by psychomotor retardation, hypotonia, ataxia, nystagmus, and
oculomotor apraxia and variably associated with dysmorphism, as well as retinal
and renal involvement. Brain MRI results show cerebellar vermis hypoplasia and
additional anomalies of the fourth ventricle, corpus callosum, and occipital
cortex. The disease has previously been mapped to the centromeric region of
chromosome 11. Using homozygosity mapping in 13 patients from eight Ashkenazi
Jewish families, we identified a homozygous mutation, R12L, in the TMEM216 gene,
in all affected individuals. Thirty individuals heterozygous for the mutation
were detected among 2766 anonymous Ashkenazi Jews, indicating a carrier rate of
1:92. Given the small size of the TMEM216 gene relative to other JBTS genes, its
sequence analysis is warranted in all JBTS patients, especially those who suffer
from associated anomalies.
