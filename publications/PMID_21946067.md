# The role of 14-3-3β in transcriptional activation of estrogen receptor α and its involvement in proliferation of breast cancer cells.
**Authors:** Kim Y, Kim H, Jang SW, Ko J
**Journal:** Biochem Biophys Res Commun (2011)
**DOI:** [10.1016/j.bbrc.2011.09.056](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.bbrc.2011.09.056)

## Abstract

1. Biochem Biophys Res Commun. 2011 Oct 14;414(1):199-204. doi:
10.1016/j.bbrc.2011.09.056. Epub 2011 Sep 16.

The role of 14-3-3β in transcriptional activation of estrogen receptor α and its
involvement in proliferation of breast cancer cells.

Kim Y(1), Kim H, Jang SW, Ko J.

Author information:
(1)School of Life Sciences and Biotechnology, Korea University, Seoul 136-701,
South Korea.

The estrogen receptor (ER) functions as a transcription factor that mediates the
effects of estrogen. ERα, which plays a crucial role in the development and
progression of breast cancer, is activated by estrogen binding, leading to
receptor phosphorylation, dimerization, and recruitment of co-activators and
chaperons to the estrogen-bound receptor complex. The 14-3-3 proteins bind to
target proteins via phosphorylation and influence many cellular events by
altering their subcellular localization or acting as a chaperone. However,
regulation of ERα expression and transactivation by the 14-3-3 proteins has not
been reported. We demonstrate that 14-3-3β functions as a positive regulator of
ERα through a direct protein-protein interaction in an estrogen-dependent
manner. Ectopic expression of 14-3-3β stimulated ERα-mediated transcriptional
activity in MCF-7 breast cancer cells. Enhanced ERα transcriptional activity due
to 14-3-3β increased the expressions of the endogenous ERα target genes, leading
to proliferation of breast cancer cells. We suggest that 14-3-3β has oncogenic
potential in breast cancer via binding to ERα and activation of the
transcriptional activity of ERα.
