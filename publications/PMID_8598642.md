# Characterization of glucocerebrosidase in Greek Gaucher disease patients: mutation analysis and biochemical studies.
**Authors:** Michelakakis H, Dimitriou E, Van Weely S, Boot RG, Mavridou I, Verhoek M, Aerts JM
**Journal:** J Inherit Metab Dis (1995)
**DOI:** [10.1007/BF02436006](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02436006)

## Abstract

1. J Inherit Metab Dis. 1995;18(5):609-15. doi: 10.1007/BF02436006.

Characterization of glucocerebrosidase in Greek Gaucher disease patients:
mutation analysis and biochemical studies.

Michelakakis H(1), Dimitriou E, Van Weely S, Boot RG, Mavridou I, Verhoek M,
Aerts JM.

Author information:
(1)Department of Enzymology and Cellular Function, Children's Hospital Ag.
Sophia, Athens, Greece.

Gaucher disease is the most frequent lysosomal storage disease in Greece,
accounting for 24% of all lysosomal disorders diagnosed during the last 13 years
at the Institute of Child Health in Athens. The nature of the defects in
glucocerebrosidase in Greek Gaucher patients with non-neuronopathic (type 1) and
neuronopathic (types 2 and 3) phenotypes was investigated at the level of the
glucocerebrosidase gene and enzyme activity. Mutation analysis performed in
10/23 Gaucher patients with different types of the disorder led to the
identification of four mutations, N370S, L444P, R463C and D409H, comprising 75%
of the investigated alleles. N370S was only found in association with type 1
disease. The genotype D409H/R463C was identified for the first time and was
associated with the severe type 2 disorder. There was no correlation between
residual in vitro enzyme activity and either phenotype or genotype. However, in
cultured fibroblast of the neuronopathic cases, glucocerebrosidase protein
concentration was reduced and the capacity to degrade exogenous
C6NBD-glucosylceramide was more severely impaired.
