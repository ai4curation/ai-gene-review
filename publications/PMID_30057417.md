Osteoarthritis (OA), a major cause of pain and disability, is a serious public 
issue worldwide. Some microRNAs (miRNAs) and SOX9 have been found to be 
expressed in OA. Therefore, the aim of this study is to investigate effects of 
microRNA-384-5p (miR-384-5p) on cartilage cell proliferation and apoptosis in 
mice with OA by targeting SOX9 through the NF-κB signaling pathway. First, 
bioinformatics was used to predict the SOX9-mediated miRNA (miR-384-5p), and 
dual luciferase reporter gene assay was conducted to further verify the 
relationship between miR-384-5p and SOX9. Then, the expression of miR-384-5p, 
SOX9, and NF-kB in mice modeled with OA was detected. To investigate the 
specific mechanism of miR-384-5p in OA, mimic and inhibitor of miR-384-5p and 
siRNA against SOX9 were used to transfect cartilage cells. Finally, 
proliferation, cell cycle, and cell apoptosis were detected using MTT assay and 
flow cytometry, respectively. Our results indicated that OA mice exhibited 
decreased expression of SOX9 and NF-kB but higher miR-384-5p expression. In 
addition, over-expressed miR-384-5p or silenced SOX9 could inhibit cell 
proliferation, and block cell cycle entry and induces apoptosis. SOX9 was a 
target gene of miR-384-5p. The NF-kB signaling pathway was inactivated after 
overexpression of miR-384-5p. Furthermore, we also observed that the effect of 
miR-384-5p inhibition was rescued when SOX9 was knocked down. The results 
support the view that inhibition of miR-384-5p could impede apoptosis and 
promote proliferation of cartilage cells through activating the NF-κB signaling 
pathway by promoting SOX9, thereby preventing the development of OA.
