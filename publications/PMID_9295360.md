# Molecular mechanism of base excision repair of uracil-containing DNA in yeast cell-free extracts.
**Authors:** Wang Z, Wu X, Friedberg EC
**Journal:** J Biol Chem (1997)
**DOI:** [10.1074/jbc.272.38.24064](https://doi.org/10.1074/jbc.272.38.24064)

## Abstract

1. J Biol Chem. 1997 Sep 19;272(38):24064-71. doi: 10.1074/jbc.272.38.24064.

Molecular mechanism of base excision repair of uracil-containing DNA in yeast
cell-free extracts.

Wang Z(1), Wu X, Friedberg EC.

Author information:
(1)Graduate Center for Toxicology, University of Kentucky, Lexington, Kentucky
40536, USA. zwang@pop.uky.edu

Base excision repair (BER) constitutes a ubiquitous excision repair mechanism,
which is responsible for the removal of multiple types of damaged and
inappropriate bases in DNA. We have employed a yeast cell-free system to examine
the biochemical mechanism of the BER pathway in lower eukaryotes. Using
uracil-containing DNA as a model substrate, we demonstrate that yeast BER
requires Apn1 protein, an Escherichia coli endonuclease IV homolog. In extracts
of an apn1 deletion mutant, the 5'-incision at AP (apurinic/apyrimidinic) sites
is not detectable, supporting the notion that yeast contains only one major
5'-AP endonuclease. The processing of the 5'-deoxyribose phosphate moieties was
found to be a rate-limiting step. During BER of uracil-containing DNA, repair
patch sizes of 1-5 nucleotides were detected, with single nucleotide repair
patches predominant.
