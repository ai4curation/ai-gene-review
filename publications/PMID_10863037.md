# Cloning and characterization of a human brain Na(+)-independent transporter for small neutral amino acids that transports D-serine with high affinity.
**Authors:** Nakauchi J, Matsuo H, Kim DK, Goto A, Chairoungdua A, Cha SH, Inatomi J, Shiokawa Y, Yamaguchi K, Saito I, Endou H, Kanai Y
**Journal:** Neurosci Lett (2000)
**DOI:** [10.1016/s0304-3940(00)01169-1](https://doi.org/10.1016/s0304-3940(00)01169-1)

## Abstract

1. Neurosci Lett. 2000 Jun 30;287(3):231-5. doi: 10.1016/s0304-3940(00)01169-1.

Cloning and characterization of a human brain Na(+)-independent transporter for
small neutral amino acids that transports D-serine with high affinity.

Nakauchi J(1), Matsuo H, Kim DK, Goto A, Chairoungdua A, Cha SH, Inatomi J,
Shiokawa Y, Yamaguchi K, Saito I, Endou H, Kanai Y.

Author information:
(1)Department of Neurosurgery, Kyorin University School of Medicine, Tokyo,
Japan.

We isolated a cDNA for the human homologue of system asc transporter Asc-1 from
human brain. The encoded protein designated as hAsc-1 (human Asc-1) exhibited 91
% sequence identity to mouse Asc-1. Consistent with mouse Asc-1, hAsc-1 required
4F2 heavy chain for its functional expression in Xenopus oocytes. hAsc-1
exhibited the properties of amino acid transport system asc which transports
small neutral amino acids in a Na(+)-independent manner. hAsc-1 transported
D-serine at high affinity with a K(m) value of 22.8 microM. In brain, 2.0 kb
mRNA was highly expressed. hAsc-1 gene was mapped to human chromosome 19, region
q12-q13.1. Because of the high-affinity transport with the K(m) value close to
the physiological concentration of D-serine, together with the high levels of
expression in brain, hAsc-1 is proposed to play significant roles in the
D-serine mobilization in brain.
