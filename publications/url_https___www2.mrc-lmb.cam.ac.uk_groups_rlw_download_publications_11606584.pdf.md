Phospholipase C /H9253(PLC/H9253) isoforms are regulated
through activation of tyrosine kinase-linked receptors.
The importance of growth factor-stimulated phospho-
rylation of specific tyrosine residues has been docu-
mented for PLC
/H92531; however, despite the critical impor-
tance of PLC/H92532 in B-cell signal transduction, neither the
tyrosine kinase(s) that directly phosphorylate PLC /H92532
nor the sites in PLC/H92532 that become phosphorylated after
stimulation are known. By measuring the ability of hu-
man PLC
/H92532 to restore calcium responses to the B-cell
receptor stimulation or oxidative stress in a B-cell line
(DT40) deficient in PLC
/H92532, we have demonstrated that
two tyrosine residues, Tyr753 and Tyr759, were important
for the PLC/H92532 signaling function. Furthermore, the dou-
ble mutation Y753F/Y759F in PLC/H92532 resulted in a loss of
tyrosine phosphorylation in stimulated DT40 cells. Of
the two kinases that previously have been proposed to
phosphorylate PLC
/H92532, Btk, and Syk, purified Btk had
much greater ability to phosphorylate recombinant
PLC
/H92532 in vitro, whereas Syk efficiently phosphorylated
adapter protein BLNK. Using purified proteins to ana-
lyze the formation of complexes, we suggest that func-
tion of Syk is to phosphorylate BLNK, providing binding
sites for PLC
/H92532. Further analysis of PLC/H92532 tyrosine res-
idues phosphorylated by Btk and several kinases from
the Src family has suggested multiple sites of phospho-
rylation and, in the context of a peptide incorporating
residues Tyr
753 and Tyr759, shown preferential phospho-
rylation of Tyr753.
The hydrolysis of phosphatidylinositol 4,5-bisphosphate by
phosphoinositide-specific phospholipase C occurs in response to
a large number of extracellular signals (reviewed in Refs. 1–4).
Four families of mammalian phosphoinositide-specific phos-
pholipase C (PLC),
1 PLC/H9252(/H92521–/H92524), PLC /H9253(/H92531, /H92532), PLC /H9254(/H92541-
/H92544), and PLC /H9280, have been described. Each family is character-
ized by the distinct domain organization and type of signaling
pathways that regulate enzyme activity.
PLC/H9253isoforms are mainly regulated through receptors with
intrinsic tyrosine kinase activity ( e.g. growth factor receptors)
or receptors (such as B- and T-cell antigen receptors) that are
linked to the activation of nonreceptor tyrosine kinases
through a complex signaling network (3–5). The two isoforms of
PLC
/H9253have distinct tissue distributions; whereas PLC /H92531i s
expressed ubiquitously, the pattern of expression of PLC /H92532i s
characterized by high levels in cells of hematopoietic origin.
Transgenic studies suggested that the biological function of
these isoforms is reflected in their cellular distribution. Thus, a
deficiency in PLC
/H92531 is embryonic lethal in mice (6), whereas
homozygous disruption of PLC/H92532 allowed normal development
but resulted in functional and signaling disorders in a subset of
cell types including B-cells, platelets, and mast cells (7).
The importance of PLC
/H92532 in signaling in B-cells has not only
been documented in experiments using transgenic animals de-
ficient in PLC
/H92532 (7) but also by studies of a chicken B-cell
lymphoma cell line (DT40) (reviewed in Refs. 8 and 9) with the
property of extraordinarily high frequency of homologous re-
combination when DNA constructs are introduced into the
cells. Generation of a number of targeted mutations in specific
genes in DT40 cells provided valuable information about sig-
naling components linking the activation of the B-cell receptor
(BCR) to an increase in intracellular calcium concentrations.
Using this system, it has been found that protein-tyrosine
kinases from Src, Tec ( e.g. Btk), and Syk/ZAP70 families are
essential signaling components of the BCR pathway (10, 11). In
addition, an adapter BLNK (B-cell linker protein), inositol
1,4,5-trisphosphate receptors, and PLC /H92532 itself were required
for calcium responses triggered by the BCR (12–15). Although
each of these components may have more than one function and
could be integrated in different pathways in B-cells, the current
model (8, 9) suggests that the Src family kinase Lyn interacts
with BCR and becomes activated upon the receptor aggrega-
tion. Activation of Syk kinase results in phosphorylation of
BLNK that could provide binding sites for PLC
/H92532 and a num-
ber of other proteins. Syk, together with Btk, has also been
implicated in phosphorylation of PLC
/H92532, which, through inosi-
tol 1,4,5-trisphosphate production, results in calcium mobiliza-
tion. A similar pathway seems to be involved in calcium re-
sponses to oxidative stress after exposure of B-cells to hydrogen
peroxide (16–18). It has been reported that the BCR complex
and tyrosine kinases Syk, Lyn, and Btk, are components re-
quired for calcium responses. In addition, phosphorylation of
several protein components, including BLNK and PLC
/H92532, has
been described.

RESULTS
Calcium Responses in DT40 Cell Lines after B-cell Receptor
and Hydrogen Peroxide Stimulation — PLC/H92532 is an essential
component in calcium signaling triggered either by the stimu-
lation of BCR (8, 9) or, as described below (Fig. 2 B), by stress
responses to hydrogen peroxide in DT40 cells. The stimulation
of the PLC
/H92532 activity in these cells by both agonists is accom-
panied by phosphorylation of the enzyme at tyrosine residues
(15, 17). To analyze which tyrosine residues may be involved in
activation of PLC
/H92532 in these systems, the sequences of PLC /H92531
and PLC/H92532 from two regions were compared. The first region
corresponds to linker between the C-terminal SH2 (C-SH2)
domain and the SH3 domain (within the “specific array of
domains” unique to the PLC
/H9253family ( /H9253SA)), and the second
region is located at the C terminus of PLC/H9253(Fig. 1). In PLC/H92531,
two phosphorylated residues have been mapped to region I
(Tyr
771 and Tyr783) and one residue (Tyr 1254) within region II.
However, only one of these residues, Tyr 783, appears to be
critical for the enzyme signaling function after platelet-derived
growth factor stimulation (21). The amino acid sequence align-
ment of mammalian PLC
/H92531 and PLC/H92532 enzymes shows conser-

Protein-tyrosine kinase Syk has also been implicated in B-
cell signaling and shown to be required for both PLC /H92532 phos-
phorylation and calcium responses (10). It has been shown that
the essential adapter protein BLNK, forming complexes with a
number of signaling components including PLC
/H92532, needs to be
phosphorylated by Syk in order to bind other proteins (13, 14).
Therefore, the role of Syk in calcium responses could be to
phosphorylate both PLC
/H92532 and BLNK or to phosphorylate only
BLNK, thereby enabling formation of signaling complexes. The
data presented here (Fig. 5) show that Syk does not efficiently
phosphorylate PLC
/H92532, but it does phosphorylate BLNK. Fur-
thermore, phosphorylation of BLNK by Syk, in the absence of
additional components, could be sufficient to provide docking
sites for direct binding of PLC
/H92532 (Fig. 7).
In summary, we identified tyrosine residues Tyr 753 and
Tyr759 as important for activation and tyrosine phosphoryla -
tion of PLC/H92532 in B-cells. Based on this observation, the roles of
various tyrosine kinases that genetic analysis has implicated in
regulation of PLC/H92532 were further assessed. Direct phosphoryl-
ation of PLC/H92532 by Btk is observed; however, the role of Syk may
not be to phosphorylate PLC /H92532 directly but to provide docking
phosphotyrosine sites on the adapter protein BLNK, essential
in B-cell signaling.
