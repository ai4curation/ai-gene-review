# A role for CSLD3 during cell-wall synthesis in apical plasma membranes of tip-growing root-hair cells.
**Authors:** Park S, Szumlanski AL, Gu F, Guo F, Nielsen E
**Journal:** Nat Cell Biol (2011)
**DOI:** [10.1038/ncb2294](https://doi.org/10.1038/ncb2294)

## Abstract

1. Nat Cell Biol. 2011 Jul 17;13(8):973-80. doi: 10.1038/ncb2294.

A role for CSLD3 during cell-wall synthesis in apical plasma membranes of
tip-growing root-hair cells.

Park S(1), Szumlanski AL, Gu F, Guo F, Nielsen E.

Author information:
(1)Department of Molecular, Cellular, and Developmental Biology, University of
Michigan, Ann Arbor, Michigan 48109, USA.

In plants, cell shape is defined by the cell wall, and changes in cell shape and
size are dictated by modification of existing cell walls and deposition of newly
synthesized cell-wall material. In root hairs, expansion occurs by a process
called tip growth, which is shared by root hairs, pollen tubes and fungal
hyphae. We show that cellulose-like polysaccharides are present in root-hair
tips, and de novo synthesis of these polysaccharides is required for tip growth.
We also find that eYFP-CSLD3 proteins, but not CESA cellulose synthases,
localize to a polarized plasma-membrane domain in root hairs. Using biochemical
methods and genetic complementation of a csld3 mutant with a chimaeric CSLD3
protein containing a CESA6 catalytic domain, we provide evidence that CSLD3
represents a distinct (1→4)-β-glucan synthase activity in apical plasma
membranes during tip growth in root-hair cells.
