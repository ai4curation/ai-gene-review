MPC1 and MPC2 are two well-known components of the mitochondrial pyruvate
carrier (MPC) complex maintaining MPC activity to transport pyruvate into
mitochondria for tricarboxylic acid (TCA) cycle entry in mammalian cells. It is
currently unknown whether there is an additional MPC component crucially
maintaining MPC complex activity for pyruvate mitochondrial import. Here we show
that ALDH4A1, a proline-metabolizing enzyme localized in mitochondria, serves as
a previously unrecognized MPC component maintaining pyruvate mitochondrial
import and the TCA cycle independently of its enzymatic activity. Loss of
ALDH4A1 in mammalian cells impairs pyruvate entry to mitochondria, resulting in
defective TCA cycle entry. ALDH4A1 forms an active trimeric complex with
MPC1-MPC2 to maintain the integrity and oligomerization of MPC1-MPC2 and
facilitates pyruvate transport in an in vitro system. ALDH4A1 displays tumour
suppression by maintaining MPC complex activity. Our study identifies ALDH4A1 as
an essential component of MPC for pyruvate mitochondrial import, TCA cycle entry
and tumour suppression.

## Full Text

Pyruvate generated from glucose metabolism is a key nutrient for cell metabolism critical for maintaining energy production, cell homeostasis and biological processes1,2. It sits at crossroads for anaerobic glycolysis and the mitochondrial TCA cycle1. Recent studies indicated that elevated pyruvate in cells plays an important role in promoting cancer progression and metastasis3,4. Cytosolic pyruvate is imported to mitochondria through the MPC complex, which is thought to be constituted primarily by MPC1 and MPC2 subunits in mammalian cells5–7. MPC1 and MPC2 proteins are localized in the mitochondrial inner membrane (matrix) and form heterodimers (oligomers) that are essential for pyruvate entry to mitochondrial matrix for subsequent pyruvate oxidation and energy production via the TCA cycle. As such, defect in MPC activity impairs pyruvate import into the mitochondria for TCA cycle entry, leading to elevated glycolysis, cytosolic pyruvate accumulation and lactate acidosis, which are linked to various diseases, including cancer1,2,7,8. MPC1 or MPC2 deficiency in mice causes embryonic lethality, indicative of the crucial role of the MPC complex activity for mitochondrial pyruvate, which is important in embryonic development9–11. Notably, loss of MPC1 expression is commonly found in numerous human cancers and correlated with poor survival outcome7,12,13. Consistently, loss of MPC1 in colon and lung promotes tumourigenesis in preclinical models13–15, whereas its overexpression inhibits tumour growth16. However, MPC1 expression is correlated with favourable clinical outcome in patients with prostate cancer17, and MPC inhibition suppresses tumourigenesis in prostate cancer9. Thus, MPC1 appears to display tumour-suppressive activity in numerous tumour types, but this is probably not the case for prostate cancer.

MPC1 and MPC2 are two well-known components forming an MPC complex. Currently, it is unknown whether there is an additional MPC component crucially orchestrating MPC integrity and activity. Our study identifies ALDH4A1 as a crucial MPC component maintaining MPC complex integrity and activity for mitochondrial pyruvate import leading to TCA cycle entry, glycolysis suppression and tumour suppression.

ResultsALDH4A1 is lost in diverse human cancersProline metabolism orchestrating proline levels in cells has been associated with cancer progression and metastasis18–21. ALDH4A1 (also known as P5CDH), catalyses the conversion of l-glutamine-5 semialdehyde (GSA), which is derived from l-proline, to glutamate in a NAD+-dependent manner22,23, thereby depleting proline and GSA levels in cells. Interestingly, GSA can be converted to ornithine by ornithine aminotransferase, which shunts into the urea cycle and is involved in subsequent arginine production24. Thus, ALDH4A1 is a metabolic enzyme triggering glutamate generation but facilitating proline depletion. As such, the defect in ALDH4A1 functions causes hyperprolinaemia syndrome25,26. Although the elevation of proline is linked to cancer progression, the role of ALDH4A1 in cancer control has been elusive thus far. To understand the role of ALDH4A1 in cancer regulation, we analysed Gene Expression database of Normal and Tumor tissues (GENT) and Oncomine datasets for ALDH4A1 expression and found that ALDH4A1 expression is lost in diverse cancer types, including hepatic cancer, breast cancer and lung cancer (Extended Data Fig. 1a,b). In addition, loss of copy number variation of ALDH4A1 appeared in several cancer types in accordance with The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) dataset (Extended Data Fig. 1e). Of note, PrognoScan database revelated that low expression of ALDH4A1 was significantly correlated with reduced overall survival in lung, brain, bladder and breast cancer compared with high expression of ALDH4A1 (Extended Data Fig. 1c). Kaplan–Meier Plotter also revealed that low expression of ALDH4A1 in liver cancer significantly reduces progression-free survival rate compared with high expression of ALDH4A1 (Extended Data Fig. 1d). Thus, loss of ALDH4A1 expression is detected in numerous human cancers and predicts poor survival outcome.ALDH4A1 restricts cancer cell proliferation and tumourigenesisBecause ALDH4A1 expression is downregulated in various cancers and its loss is associated with reduced patient survival rate, ALDH4A1 may display tumour-suppressive activity. Consistent with this notion, ALDH4A1 knockdown in diverse cancer cell lines markedly enhanced in vitro cancer cell phenotypes including cancer cell proliferation and survival as determined by MTT (3-(4,5-dimethylthiazol-2-yl)-2,5-diphenyltetrazolium bromide) assay and colony-formation assay, cancer cell migration through the transwell migration assay and cancer stemness as determined by cancer sphere assay (Figs. 1a–g and 2c–g and Extended Data Figs. 2a–h, 3a–e and 5a–c). Conversely, overexpression of ALDH4A1 restricted cancer cell proliferation, cancer cell migration and cancer stemness (Figs. 1b–g and 8h and Extended Data Figs. 2a–h, 3b–e and 5a–c). We compared the effects of ALDH4A1 knockdown with knockdown of the tumour suppressors p53 or pRB on cancer cell proliferation, migration and sphere formation. Loss of ALDH4A1 promoted an about 1.6-fold change in cell proliferation, 3-fold change in cell migration and 2-fold change in cancer sphere formation compared with control knockdown cells (Fig. 1f,g and Extended Data Fig. 3a–e). Similarly, knockdown of either p53 or pRB promoted an about 1.5-fold change in cell proliferation, 4-fold change in cell migration and 2-fold change in cancer sphere formation (Fig. 1f,g and Extended Data Fig. 3a–e). Thus, ALDH4A1 loss significantly promoted cancer cell phenotypes as p53 and pRB loss.Remarkably, ALDH4A1 knockdown in non-transformed NIH3T3 cells was sufficient to drive cell transformation in soft agar assay, whereas its restoration could reverse cell transformation upon ALDH4A1 knockdown (Extended Data Fig. 4a–c), indicating that ALDH4A1 probably displays tumour-suppressive activity. Similarly, in in vivo preclinical Hep3B xenograft models, loss of ALDH4A1 robustly enhanced tumourigenesis (Figs. 1h–j and 2h–j), whereas its overexpression caused tumour suppression (Fig. 2h–j). Furthermore, loss of ALDH4A1 significantly promoted liver cancer formation using the Hep3B orthotopic liver implanted tumour model (Extended Data Fig. 4d,e). These results, along with xenograft models, suggest that ALDH4A1 displays tumour-suppressive activity.ALDH4A1 restricts oncogenesis independently of its enzymatic activityTo determine whether the enzymatic activity of ALDH4A1 is essential for cancer cell proliferation, we first generated an enzymatic dead Ser325Leu mutant of ALDH4A1 (ALDH4A1(S352L))27. The restoration of wild-type ALDH4A1, but not the ALDH4A1(S352L) mutant, could reverse the increase in proline levels and decrease in glutamate levels upon ALDH4A1 knockdown (Fig. 2a,b). To our surprise, ALDH4A1 appeared to inhibit cancer cell proliferation independently of its enzymatic activity in regulating proline metabolism, because restoration of either wild-type ALDH4A1 or ALDH4A1(S352L) in ALDH4A1-knockdown cancer cells could reverse the promotion effect on cancer cell proliferation upon ALDH4A1 knockdown (Fig. 2–g). Likewise, adding various concentrations of proline, arginine or ornithine to control, ALDH4A1-overexpression or ALDH4A1-knockdown cancer cells irrespective of ALDH4A1 restoration did not impact cancer cell proliferation (Extended Data Fig. 5a–c). Moreover, knockdown of PRODH, which is a metabolic enzyme that catalyses the conversion of proline to GSA, resulting in proline depletion18,20, failed to promote cancer cell proliferation as ALDH4A1 knockdown did (Extended Data Fig. 5d). Consistently, overexpression of both wild-type ALDH4A1 and ALDH4A1(S352L) restricted tumourigenesis in a similar efficiency (Fig. 2h–m). Collectively, our findings suggest that ALDH4A1 displays tumour-suppressive activity in a manner independent of its enzymatic activity.ALDH4A1 maintains mitochondrial pyruvate import for TCA cycle entryBecause the conversion of GSA to glutamate by ALDH4A1 occurs mainly in mitochondria28,29, it is conceivable that ALDH4A1 may also be localized in mitochondria. Indeed, ALDH4A1 expression was detected mainly in the mitochondrial fraction using biochemical fraction assays, whereas ALDH4A1 knockdown significantly reduced its levels in the mitochondrial fraction (Extended Data Fig. 6a). Restoration of either wild-type ALDH4A1 or ALDH4A1(S352L) mutant rescued the expression of ALDH4A1 in mitochondria in ALDH4A1-knockdown cells (Extended Data Fig. 6b). In addition, an immunofluorescence assay revealed that ALDH4A1 colocalized with TOMM20 (also known as Tom20), a well-known mitochondrial marker (Fig. 3a). Collectively, these data suggest that ALDH4A1 is localized in mitochondria.Because of the mitochondrial localization of ALDH4A1, we speculated that ALDH4A1 may affect cancer metabolism and mitochondrial functions to orchestrate cancer phenotypes. Systematic metabolomics analysis revealed that loss of ALDH4A1 expectedly increased the levels of numerous metabolites involved in proline and urea cycle pathways including proline, ornithine and arginine (Fig. 3b and Extended Data Fig. 10). Notably, the levels of the metabolite pyruvate were markedly increased in ALDH4A1-knockdown cells, which was reversed by restoration of either wild-type ALDH4A1 or ALDH4A1(S352L), indicative of the enzymatic activity-independent role of ALDH4A1 in regulating pyruvate levels (Fig. 3c). ALDH4A1 also displayed enzymatic activity-independent regulation of histidine levels in cells, albeit to a lesser extent (Extended Data Fig. 10). Remarkably, the loss of ALDH4A1 markedly enhanced the levels of pyruvate in the cytosol, while impairing the levels of pyruvate in mitochondria (Extended Data Fig. 6c,d), which is reminiscent of MPC inactivation5,6. Similar to ALDH4A1 loss, MPC inactivation by pharmacological inactivation by treatment with UK5099 or MPC1 knockdown also increased pyruvate levels in cytosol, while reducing pyruvate levels in mitochondria (Extended Data Fig. 6e,f), albeit to a lesser extent compared with ALDH4A1 loss. Consistently, ALDH4A1 acts through its enzymatic-independent activity to maintain pyruvate levels in mitochondria, as restoration of either wild-type ALDH4A1 or mutant ALDH4A1(S352L) rescued the defect of mitochondrial pyruvate levels with a similar efficiency (Extended Data Fig. 6d). As a result, loss of ALDH4A1 reduced the oxygen consumption rate (OCR), as determined by the OCR assay and acetyl-CoA levels, indicative of the defect in TCA cycle entry, which was rescued by restoration of wild-type ALDH4A1 or ALDH4A1(S352L) (Fig. 3d–f and Extended Data Fig. 6g).To investigate the role of ALDH4A1 in regulating mitochondrial coupling efficiency, we isolated mitochondria from control knockdown cells and ALDH4A1-knockdown cells, and subjected them to mitochondrial coupling assay using Seahorse OCR analysis. Mitochondria begin in a coupled state with the substrates, succinate and rotenone, followed by ADP, oligomycin, FCCP (carbonyl cyanide 4-(trifluoromethoxy)phenylhydrazone) and antimycin A treatment. The respiratory control ratio (RCR) was calculated by the ratio of state 3 and state 4o (state 3/state 4o) and the ratio of state 3u and state 4o (state 3u/state 4o). Neither ALDH4A1 knockdown nor restoration of either wild-type ALDH4A1 or mutant ALDH4A1(S352L) affected state 3/state 4o and state 3u/state 4o compared with control knockdown (Extended Data Fig. 6h–j). These results suggest that loss of ALDH4A1 does not affect the RCR with succinate as a complex-II-linked substrate to initiate mitochondrial oxidative phosphorylation.Next, we used pyruvate and malate as the substrates, followed by treatment with ADP, oligomycin, FCCP or antimycin A to examine mitochondrial coupling efficiency. ALDH4A1 knockdown impaired the RCR (state 3/state 4o and state 3u/state 4o) compared with control knockdown (Extended Data Fig. 6k–m). Notably, restoration of either wild-type ALDH4A1 or mutant ALDH4A1(S352L) rescued the defect in the RCR upon ALDH4A1 knockdown (Extended Data Fig. 6k–m). Collectively, ALDH4A1 maintains mitochondrial coupling efficiency via mitochondrial complex I initiated by pyruvate or malate independently of its enzymatic activity.To further validate the role of ALDH4A1 in TCA cycle entry, we conducted 13C-pyruvate tracing experiments in cells and found that the levels of diverse metabolites generated from the TCA cycl, such as citrate, α-ketoglutarate, succinate, fumarate, malate and oxaloacetate, were also reduced upon ALDH4A1 knockdown, and this defect could be rescued by retroration of wild-type ALDH4A1 or mutant ALDH4A1(S352L) (Fig. 3g,h). However, ALDH4A1 knockdown did not change pyruvate dehydrogenase (PDH) levels, activity or phosphorylation compared with control knockdown, or ADLH4A1-knockdown cells restored with wild-type ALDH4A1 or ALDH4A1(S352L) (Extended Data Figs. 6b and 7a), indicating that ALDH4A1 did not affect pyruvate metabolism. To examine whether ALDH4A1 loss also affects OCR derived from fatty acid and glutamine, Hep3B cells were treated with palmitate or glutamine and the OCR was determined by Seahorse analysis. While ALDH4A1 knockdown reduced the OCR in steady-state or low-glutamine conditions, adding either palmitate or glutamine could rescue the impaired OCR by ALDH4A1 knockdown (Fig. 3i,j and Extended Data Fig. 7b,c), indicating that ALDH4A1 loss does not affect glutamine and fatty acid oxidation. We noticed that there was a high non-mitochondrial respiratory rate observed in Hep3B cells, which may be affected by numerous factors related to cellular metabolism, experimental conditions and/or mycoplasma contamination. However, we ruled out that this was due to mycoplasma contamination as all cell lines used in our study were not contaminated by mycoplasma, verified by PCR mycoplasma detection (Extended Data Fig. 7d). Collectively, ALDH4A1 critically maintains mitochondrial pyruvate levels for TCA cycle entry.ALDH4A1 maintains mitochondria pyruvate import dependently of MPC1–MPC2The finding that loss of ALDH4A1 reduces mitochondrial pyruvate levels for TCA cycle while causing cytosolic pyruvate accumulation suggests that ALDH4A1 may be an essential component for maintaining MPC complex activity towards pyruvate import into the mitochondria. To test this hypothesis, we isolated mitochondria from control, ALDH4A1-knockdown and MPC1-knockdown cells and incubated cells with pyruvate to determine the uptake of pyruvate into the mitochondria by analysing pyruvate penetration to mitochondria levels normalized by control group. Both ALDH4A1 loss and MPC1 loss significantly impaired pyruvate uptake in mitochondria (Extended Data Fig. 7e). Restoration of ALDH4A1 rescued the defect in pyruvate uptake in mitochondria in ALDH4A1-knockdown cells (Extended Data Fig. 7f). To further validate this notion, we performed a similar assay by incubating isolated mitochondrial with 14C-labelled pyruvate and determined the uptake of 14C-pyruvate into the mitochondria by quantifying 14C levels in mitochondria using a scintillation counter as previously described6,30. Consistently, loss of ALDH4A1 compromised the uptake of 14C-pyruvate to mitochondria in a time-dependent manner (Extended Data Fig. 7g). Loss of ALDH4A1 significantly impaired pyruvate uptake into mitochondria in multiple cell types (Figs. 1a, 4a–e,g and 5j,l). Similar to MPC1 knockdown (Fig. 4f,g), ALDH4A1 loss caused the defective 14C-pyruvate uptake into mitochondria, which was rescued by restoration of ALDH4A1 (Figs. 4d,e,g and 5j,l). Thus, ALDH4A1 is essential for maintaining pyruvate import into mitochondria in diverse cell types.To demonstrate the MPC1–MPC2-dependent manner for ALDH4A1 in mitochondrial pyruvate entry, we examined pyruvate entry into mitochondria upon ALDH4A1 knockdown in conjunction with or without MPC1–MPC2 knockdown. MPC1–MPC2 knockdown did not further impair pyruvate entry upon ALDH4A1 knockdown (Extended Data Fig. 7h,i). Conversely, ALDH4A1 overexpression significantly promoted 14C-pyruvate uptake into mitochondria (Fig. 4f). Moreover, the increase of 14C-pyruvate uptake into mitochondria upon ALDH4A1 overexpression was abrogated by either MPC1 knockdown or MPC inhibition by UK5099 (Fig. 4f), indicating that ALDH4A1 facilitates pyruvate uptake in an MPC1–MPC2-dependent manner.We further performed kinetic pyruvate import assay in a time- and concentration-dependent manner using 14C-pyruvate. According to the Michaelis–Menten kinetics presented in a concentration-dependent manner, we demonstrated Michaelis constant (Km; 1.00 ± 0.56 mM) and the maximum transport rate (Vmax; 1.12 ± 0.26 pmol min−1 μg−1) in isolated mitochondria upon ALDH4A1 knockdown and Km (0.81 ± 0.33 mM) and Vmax (1.96 ± 0.3 pmol min−1 μg−1) in isolated mitochondria from control knockdown cells (Fig. 4h), further validating the role of ALDH4A1 in maintaining pyruvate import into mitochondria. We then determined Michaelis–Menten kinetics in a time-dependent manner to calculate the pyruvate transport rate. ALDH4A1 knockdown significantly impaired pyruvate import into mitochondria in a time-dependent manner (T0.5 = 9.1 min) compared with control knockdown cells (T0.5 = 5.1 min) (Fig. 4i). Remarkably, restoration of either wild-type ALDH4A1 (T0.5 = 4.6 min) or ALDH4A1(S352L) (T0.5 = 5.16 min) (Fig. 4i) could rescue impaired pyruvate import into mitochondria in time-dependent manner. However, ALDH4A1 could not form the complex with MPC1 and MPC2 oligomer, as expected, upon MPC1–MPC2 knockdown (Fig. 6k). Notably, loss of ALDH4A1 significantly disrupted the interaction of MPC2 with MPC1 oligomer under native conditions (Fig. 6l). Hence, ALDH4A1 forms the complex with MPC1 and MPC2 to maintain MPC1–MPC2 complex formation and oligomerization.As UK5099 is an MPC inhibitor that is known to block pyruvate mitochondrial transport36, we further investigated the effect of UK5099 on the MPC1–MPC2–ALDH4A1 complex formation. While UK5099 treatment did not affect the protein levels of MPC1, MPC2 and ALDH4A1 (Fig. 6m), it disrupted the interaction of ALDH4A1 with MPC1–MPC2 oligomer under native BN-PAGE assay and crosslinked conditions (Fig. 6n and Extended Data Fig. 8d–f). ALDH4A1 is lost in diverse human cancersProline metabolism orchestrating proline levels in cells has been associated with cancer progression and metastasis18–21. ALDH4A1 (also known as P5CDH), catalyses the conversion of l-glutamine-5 semialdehyde (GSA), which is derived from l-proline, to glutamate in a NAD+-dependent manner22,23, thereby depleting proline and GSA levels in cells. Interestingly, GSA can be converted to ornithine by ornithine aminotransferase, which shunts into the urea cycle and is involved in subsequent arginine production24. In addition, loss of copy number variation of ALDH4A1 appeared in several cancer types in accordance with The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) dataset (Extended Data Fig. 1e). Of note, PrognoScan database revelated that low expression of ALDH4A1 was significantly correlated with reduced overall survival in lung, brain, bladder and breast cancer compared with high expression of ALDH4A1 (Extended Data Fig. 1c). Kaplan–Meier Plotter also revealed that low expression of ALDH4A1 in liver cancer significantly reduces progression-free survival rate compared with high expression of ALDH4A1 (Extended Data Fig. 1d). Thus, loss of ALDH4A1 expression is detected in numerous human cancers and predicts poor survival outcome.

DiscussionMPC1 and MPC2 are two components that maintain MPC complex activity for mitochondrial pyruvate import in mammalian cells. It has been known that metabolic enzymes have non-canonical functions beyond their canonical, enzymatic activity38. Our study surprisingly reveals that proline-metabolizing enzyme ALDH4A1 constitutes an intact and functional MPC complex with MPC1 and MPC2 for mitochondrial pyruvate import. Similar to MPC1, ALDH4A1 displays tumour-suppressive activity. To our surprise, these processes regulated by ALDH4A1 do not require its enzymatic activity towards proline metabolism. Our study, therefore, reveals that ALDH4A1 not only displays canonical enzymatic activity for proline metabolism but also serves as an additional and crucial component for constituting a functional MPC trimeric complex with MPC1 and MPC2 to maintain mitochondrial pyruvate import for TCA cycle entry and tumour suppression.Using mass spectrometry analysis, we identified MPC2 as one of the ALDH4A1-interacting proteins. We further verified that ALDH4A1 forms a trimeric complex with MPC1–MPC2 by using co-immunoprecipitation and sequential co-immunoprecipitation in cells. Importantly, we characterized the binding region (amino acids 182–199) of ALDH4A1 with MPC1–MPC2 proteins by co-immunoprecipitation and AlphaFold 2 analysis, as the crystal structure of the human MPC has not been resolved yet and only computational models are available so far39. Using proteoliposomes and performing a pyruvate transport assay using 14C-pyruvate, either wild-type ALDH4A1 or ALDH4A1(S352L) could markedly enhance pyruvate transport into proteoliposomes by MPC1–MPC2. Collectively, our results support the notion that ALDH4A1 displays non-enzymatic functions by serving as a core protein component of MPC complex.In summary, our study identifies ALDH4A1 as the third component of MPC complex critical for constituting an active MPC trimeric complex consisting of ALDH4A1, MPC1 and MPC2 for mitochondrial pyruvate import for TCA cycle entry in mammalian cells (Fig. 8m and Extended Data Fig. 9i). Our study indicates that loss of MPC components, such as ALDH4A1, MPC1 and MPC2, commonly observed in diverse human cancers with defects in mitochondrial pyruvate import probably provides the molecular basis for why cancer cells often display defective mitochondrial TCA cycles that lead to the Warburg effect for maintaining cancer cell growth and tumourigenesis (Extended Data Fig. 9i). Because pyruvate homeostasis orchestrates mitochondrial functions and diverse biological processes, the defect in ALDH4A1 leading to cytosolic pyruvate accumulation and, subsequently, defective mitochondrial pyruvate entry may have global impacts on numerous human diseases other than cancer.

In brief, 2 × 106 Hep3B cells with indicated knockdown and restoration were seeded in 10-cm plates with culture medium. After 16 h, cells were collected according to the NW-MRC’ instructions3. The culture medium was removed, and cells were gently rinsed with ice-cold PBS two times, followed by immediately adding 1 ml methanol/HPLC-grade H2O (80:20 (v/v) (−75 °C, on dry ice) into the plates placed on dry ice. After incubation for 30 min on dry ice to quench metabolism, cells were scraped from the plate and transferred into a 2-ml Eppendorf tube. Then, 0.7 ml of methanol/HPLC-grade H2O (80:20 (v/v) was added to the plate on the dry ice, and the remaining cells in the plate were scraped and transferred into the Eppendorf tube. Next, the eppendorf tube with 1.7 ml cell lysates was spun down at 13,000g for 5 min at 4 °C. The tubes with supernatants were subjected to metabolomics analysis. For 13C3-labelled-pyruvate tracing assay, Hep3B cells were seeded in the 10-cm plate overnight. The culture medium was removed and changed to the glucose-free DMEM with 25 mM 13C3-labelled pyruvate for 4 h. Targeted metabolomics analysis was performed using liquid chromatography-triple quadrupole mass spectrometer (LC–TQMS) by NW-MRC.

Mitochondrial isolation.Mitochondrial isolation was conducted according to the earlier study43 with some modifications. Hep3B cells from three confluent 15-cm dishes were collected and then added in 1 ml of HIM buffer (200 mM mannitol, 70 mM sucrose, 10 mM HEPES, 1 mM EGTA at pH 7.5 and 0.2% fatty-acid-free bovine serum albumin (BSA) (Sigma)). Cells were homogenized in a tight-fitting Dounce homogenizer with 25 strokes on ice, followed by centrifugation at 800g for 10 min to remove the nuclei and unbroken cells from the cell pellet. The supernatants were centrifuged at 8,000g for 15 min. The supernatants from the cytosol fraction were collected. The pellet with mitochondria was washed in 1 ml HIM buffer again and centrifuged at 800g for 10 min. The mitochondria-containing supernatants were centrifuged at 12,000g for 5 min. Mitochondria in the pellet was used for the following experiments or resuspended in MRM-S buffer (250 mM sucrose, 10 mM HEPES, 1 mM ATP, 5 mM succinate, 0.08 mM ADP and 2 mM K2HPO4 at pH 7.4) for the functional assays. To ensure the purity of the subfractionation, we took 10% of the mitochondria-containing pellet to run the SDS–PAGE after protein denatured in Laemmli buffer.

Pyruvate uptake assay.Pyruvate import was assessed in isolated mitochondria by incubating with 14C-pyruvate (American Radiolabel Chemicals, ARC 0223) with minor modifications as described previously6,30. In brief, 2 × 107 Hep3B cells with indicated treatments, knockdown and restoration were collected to isolate mitochondria followed by resuspension of mitochondria in the import buffer (120 mM KCl, 5 mM KH2PO4, 1 mM EGTA and 3 mM HEPES pH 7.4). Resuspended mitochondria were placed on the ice and used within 5 h of collection without freezing. The final concentration of the reaction mixture included 0.05 mM malate, 0.09 mM pyruvate and 0.1 mM 14C-labelled pyruvate in the import buffer at pH 6.8. Fifty millilitres of mitochondria was added to 100 ml of reaction mixture in room-temperature import buffer at pH 6.8 in a microcentrifuge tube. Fifty millilitres of each sample was spotted onto membrane filter with 0.45 μm pore size (MF-Millipore) at 10, 15, 20, 30 or 40 min timepoints. The filter was then washed with 3 ml of ice-cold TBS using an aspirator to draw the wash buffer through the filter and dry it, followed by placing the filters in a scintillation cocktail. The radioactivity was determined by using a liquid scintillation counter (Beckman Coulter, LS6500). Blank samples in which 50 ml of mitochondria was replaced by import buffer with reaction mixture were collected at corresponding timepoints. The blank values were subtracted from experimental data, and three independent experiments were analysed in each experimental group.
