Insufficient sarco/endoplasmic reticulum calcium ATPase (SERCA) activity
significantly contributes to heart failure, which is a leading cause of death
worldwide. A characteristic pathology of cardiac disease is the slow and
incomplete Ca2+ removal from the myocyte cytoplasm in diastole, which is
primarily driven by SERCA, the integral transmembrane Ca2+ pump. Phospholamban
(PLB) allosterically inhibits SERCA by reducing its apparent Ca2+ affinity.
Recently, the 34-codon novel dwarf open reading frame (DWORF) micropeptide has
been identified as a muscle-specific SERCA effector, capable of reversing the
inhibitory effects of PLB and independently activating SERCA in the absence of
PLB. However, the structural basis for these functions has not yet been
determined in a system of defined molecular components. We have used electron
paramagnetic resonance (EPR) spectroscopy to investigate the protein-protein
interactions of DWORF, co-reconstituted in proteoliposomes with SERCA and
spin-labeled PLB. We analyzed the change of PLB rotational mobility in response
to varying DWORF concentration, to quantify competitive binding of DWORF and
PLB. We determined that DWORF competes with PLB for binding to SERCA at low
[Ca2+], although the measured affinity of DWORF for SERCA is an order of
magnitude weaker than that of PLB for SERCA, indicating cooperativity. The
sensitivity of EPR to structural dynamics, using stereospecifically attached
spin labels, allows us to obtain new information needed to refine the molecular
model for regulation of SERCA activity, as needed for development of novel
therapeutic remedies against cardiac pathologies.

Introduction

Heart failure (HF) is a leading cause of morbidity and mortality worldwide [1]. Abnormal Ca2+ transport has been identified as a potential determinant of decreased contractility in the failing heart [2]. The sarcoplasmic reticulum (SR) Ca-ATPase (SERCA, Fig. 1, gray) is responsible for initiating muscle relaxation by transporting cytosolic Ca2+ against a 100–10,000-fold concentration gradient into the SR lumen [3]. For every ATP hydrolyzed, SERCA transports up to two Ca2+ ions from the cytosol to the SR, enabling muscle relaxation. The cardiac isoform of SERCA (SERCA2a) is regulated primarily by phospholamban (PLB, Fig. 1, red), a 52-residue integral transmembrane protein comprised of a dynamic N-terminal cytosolic helix, followed by a short loop and a C-terminal trans-membrane helix [4–6], and is abundantly co-expressed in ventricular myocyte SR. When bound, PLB inhibits SERCA activity by decreasing its apparent Ca2+ affinity. In high (μM) Ca2+ concentrations [7] or in the phosphorylated state, P-PLB [8–10], inhibition is relieved and SERCA activity is restored.

Genome-wide studies suggest that hundreds of functional micropeptides could be found in RNA transcripts that are annotated as long noncoding (lncRNAs) [11–13]. Recently, an open reading frame (ORF) was identified within a muscle-specific RNA transcript [14]. This sequence, named dwarf open reading frame (DWORF, Fig. 1, orange), encodes a 34 amino acid peptide that has been shown to interact with SERCA2a [10,14–16]. PLB and DWORF are two of the seven known regulins, which are small regulatory membrane proteins that fine tune Ca2+ uptake by SERCA [17]. Recent studies have shown that DWORF binds to and enhances the activity of SERCA in SR, but many of these studies involve fusion of DWORF to much larger fluorescent proteins, so DWORF’s structural mechanism of action remains unknown [10,14,15]. A recent NMR study suggests that a kink in DWORF’s helical structure plays an important role [18]. DWORF mRNA is downregulated, resulting in decreased expression, in dilated transgenic hearts of 6-month-old transgenic mice and in ischemic failing human hearts, so changes in DWORF expression are linked with human HF [14]. Overexpression of DWORF in a DCM mouse model enhances Ca2+ cycling and cardiac contractility, preventing HF [10].

Altered Ca2+ homeostasis by depressed SERCA2a activity initiates a cascade of pathologies that commonly lead to HF; so therapeutic approaches often seek to partially reverse the allosteric inhibition of SERCA2a by peptide regulators [19]. As DWORF is the sole endogenous regulator that has been shown to enhance SERCA activity, elucidating the molecular mechanism of action underlying DWORF’s functional effect is important in the efficient design of therapeutic remedies to manage and alleviate HF.

Using a SERCA2a FRET biosensor in live HEK (human embryonic kidney) cells, we recently showed that DWORF has dual actions on SERCA2a: (a) In the presence of PLB, DWORF relieves PLB inhibition at subsaturating calcium levels, (b) DWORF activates SERCA2a enzymatic activity directly, even in the absence of PLB [20], as recently confirmed by an independent report [21]. DWORF (fused to a fluorescent fusion protein) has slightly stronger affinity for SERCA (fused to another fluorescent fusion protein) at high [Ca2+] than at low [Ca2+] [21,22], while PLB (fused to another fluorescent fusion protein) had a markedly stronger affinity for SERCA at low [Ca2+] than at high [Ca2+] [22].

In the present study, we use electron paramagnetic resonance (EPR) spectroscopy to study the dynamics of DWORF’s interaction with SERCA and PLB, using purified proteins in reconstituted membrane vesicles of defined composition. The probes used in EPR are nitroxide spin labels attached to amino acid side chains. These probes are much smaller than fluorescent fusion proteins and provide a sensitive measure of binding of labeled peptides to SERCA. We previously used EPR to measure changes in spin-label mobility, to resolve the populations of SERCA-bound and free PLB in SR vesicles [23,24]. In the present study, we have covalently attached a nitroxide spin label to PLB, in order to monitor changes in PLB binding to SERCA in the absence and presence of DWORF at low [Ca2+]. We find that as the fraction of DWORF increases in the SERCA:PLB complex in the SR vesicles, DWORF disrupts PLB binding to SERCA. We also find that the affinity of DWORF is ~15-fold less than that of PLB for isolated SERCA, suggesting cooperative effects of DWORF in regulation of SERCA.

4.DiscussionThe spectrum of spin-labeled PLB in the absence of SERCA (Figs. 2 and 3A, blue) is composed entirely of the mobile component, and the monomeric variant of PLB (AFA-PLB) ensures that homo-oligomerization is prevented within the lipid bilayer. The resonance lines of this spectrum are narrow, resulting in sharp lines of high amplitude, indicating high rotational mobility in the nanosecond time range. In contrast, the spectrum of labeled PLB in the presence of equimolar SERCA (Figs. 2 and 3A, red) possesses a mean mobile component mole fraction of XM = 0.3 ± 0.05. Most of the spectrum shifted to a low-field resonance line that is broadened, indicating substantially restricted rotational mobility. This restricted component is absent from the spectrum of labeled PLB-only vesicles. The 1:1:1 sample (equimolar PLB, SERCA, and DWORF) has a mobile (unbound) PLB mole fraction of XM = 0.36 ± 0.05 and the 1:1:4 sample has an unbound PLB mole fraction of XM = 0.45 ± 0.05. The highest DWORF concentration tested (1:1:8), eight-fold molar ratio of DWORF to both SERCA and PLB, showed the largest mobile (unbound PLB) component: XM = 0.49 ± 0.05.There is a positive correlation between the fraction of unrestricted (unbound) PLB and the concentration of DWORF, indicating that DWORF displaces SERCA-bound PLB. Qualitatively, these results agree with our previous FRET results with fluorescently tagged SERCA and PLB biosensors in live cells, where it was shown that the fraction of SERCA-bound PLB was significantly reduced (shown by a decrease in FRET efficiency between probes on SERCA and PLB) upon titration of DWORF DNA into the cells [20]. The quantitative differences in the two studies are presumably due to differences in the variants of PLB used (the monomeric mutant AFA-PLB in the present study, vs the primarily pentameric wild-type PLB [20]), different preparation methods (reconstitution of purified components in the present study, vs expression in live cells), and different fluorescent labels (small fluorescent probes in the present study, vs. large fluorescent proteins). In the FRET studies inside living cells, the ratio of SERCA:PLB:DWORF was not determined, so it was not established how much DWORF peptide was required to displace the wildtype PLB [20]. Additionally, Cleary et al. [22] recently showed that AFA-PLB accelerates the PLB-SERCA re-association rate, leading to the destabilization of the PLB pentamer.Our results show that DWORF competes with PLB for SERCA binding, but that DWORF’s apparent affinity for SERCA is an order of magnitude weaker than that of PLB for SERCA at low [Ca] (Fig. 3B). This is consistent with FRET in living cells, showing that DWORF has lower affinity for SERCA in low [Ca] compared to PLB [22]. Thus it is likely that the allosteric cooperativity plays a significant role in the functional effects of these regulins, involving multiple binding sites on SERCA [18,35,36]. Thus it is plausible that PLB and DWORF can bind simultaneously to SERCA. There remain many questions to be resolved in this important regulatory system.

5.SummaryWe have used EPR spectroscopy to investigate protein-protein interactions of the novel micropeptide DWORF, co-reconstituted in proteoliposomes with purified SERCA and spin-labeled PLB. We related the change of PLB rotational motion, in response to varying protein concentration, to the equilibrium population of unbound PLB, in order to quantitate the competitive binding of DWORF and PLB. We conclude that DWORF competes with PLB for binding to SERCA, but the measured affinity of DWORF for SERCA is an order of magnitude weaker than that of PLB for SERCA. Future studies are needed to define the structure-function relationships in this system. For example, the use of spin-labeled DWORF would provide information complementary to the present study. Studies with disease-causing mutants of PLB would also provide essential insights needed for therapeutic strategy.This work is the first investigation of PLB/DWORF competition in a well-defined system of purified proteins and lipids. Understanding the structural model by which SERCA is regulated by PLB and DWORF is essential for the development of therapeutic remedies for pathological heart conditions. The results of this work provide more evidence describing the ability of DWORF to compete with PLB for SERCA binding, which makes the novel peptide a valid target for drug discovery in the therapeutic management of heart failure.
