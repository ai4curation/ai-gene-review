# A photosystem II-associated carbonic anhydrase regulates the efficiency of photosynthetic oxygen evolution.
**Authors:** Villarejo A, Shutova T, Moskvin O, Forssén M, Klimov VV, Samuelsson G
**Journal:** EMBO J (2002)
**DOI:** [10.1093/emboj/21.8.1930](https://doi.org/10.1093/emboj/21.8.1930)

## Abstract

1. EMBO J. 2002 Apr 15;21(8):1930-8. doi: 10.1093/emboj/21.8.1930.

A photosystem II-associated carbonic anhydrase regulates the efficiency of
photosynthetic oxygen evolution.

Villarejo A(1), Shutova T, Moskvin O, Forssén M, Klimov VV, Samuelsson G.

Author information:
(1)Umeå Plant Science Center, Department of Plant Physiology, University of
Umeå, S-901 87 Umeå, Sweden.

We show for the first time that Cah3, a carbonic anhydrase associated with the
photosystem II (PSII) donor side in Chlamydomonas reinhardtii, regulates the
water oxidation reaction. The mutant cia3, lacking Cah3 activity, has an
impaired water splitting capacity, as shown for intact cells, thylakoids and
PSII particles. To compensate this impairment, the mutant overproduces PSII
reaction centres (1.6 times more than wild type). We present compelling evidence
that the mutant has an average of two manganese atoms per PSII reaction centre.
When bicarbonate is added to mutant thylakoids or PSII particles, the O2
evolution rates exceed those of the wild type by up to 50%. The donor side of
PSII in the mutant also exhibits a much higher sensitivity to overexcitation
than that of the wild type. We therefore conclude that Cah3 activity is
necessary to stabilize the manganese cluster and maintain the water-oxidizing
complex in a functionally active state. The possibility that two manganese atoms
are enough for water oxidation if bicarbonate ions are available is discussed.
