Septacidin (1) represents a group of nucleoside antibiotics possessing a unique
N6-glycosylated adenine core. They exhibit some fascinating bioactivities that
are rare for other nucleoside antibiotics. Here we demonstrate that this unique
structure in septacidin is formed by SepE and SepF. SepE is an unprecedented
Fe(II)-dependent glycosyltransferase decorating the N6-position of AMP using
ADP-l-glycero-β-d-manno-heptose (6) as a sugar donor. The Fe(II) may help SepE
to bind AMP. SepF is an unusual glycosidase that detaches the
N9-ribosyl-5-phosphate.
