# P-domain and lectin site are involved in the chaperone function of Saccharomyces cerevisiae calnexin homologue.
**Authors:** Xu X, Azakami H, Kato A
**Journal:** FEBS Lett (2004)
**DOI:** [10.1016/j.febslet.2004.06.039](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.febslet.2004.06.039)

## Abstract

1. FEBS Lett. 2004 Jul 16;570(1-3):155-60. doi: 10.1016/j.febslet.2004.06.039.

P-domain and lectin site are involved in the chaperone function of Saccharomyces
cerevisiae calnexin homologue.

Xu X(1), Azakami H, Kato A.

Author information:
(1)Department of Biological Chemistry, Yamaguchi University, Yamaguchi 753-8515,
Japan.

Cne1p, a calnexin homologue from Saccharomyces cerevisiae, has been shown to
possess a conserved P-domain and lectin site as mammalian calnexin. The effect
of P-domain and lectin site on the function of Cne1p was investigated in vitro
using recombinant P-domain, P-domain deletion mutant of Cne1p, and lectin site
mutant of Cne1ps (E181A and E398A) The binding of monoglucosylated
oligosaccharide (G1M9) with Cne1p was clearly demonstrated using lectin site
mutants. The P-domain deletion mutant and the letcin site mutants partially
decreased the ability to suppress the aggregation of citrate synthase (CS) and
chicken egg yolk immunoglobulin at levels different from Cne1p. Furthermore, the
P-domain deletion mutant and the lectin site mutants decreased the ability to
enhance the refolding of CS. These results suggest that the cooperation between
the P-domain and the lectin site are important for the complete function of
Cne1p. Thus, we conclude that P-domain in cooperation with the lectin site of
Cne1p functions as a chaperone.
