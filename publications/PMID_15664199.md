# An important role for RNase R in mRNA decay.
**Authors:** Cheng ZF, Deutscher MP
**Journal:** Mol Cell (2005)
**DOI:** [10.1016/j.molcel.2004.11.048](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.molcel.2004.11.048)

## Abstract

1. Mol Cell. 2005 Jan 21;17(2):313-8. doi: 10.1016/j.molcel.2004.11.048.

An important role for RNase R in mRNA decay.

Cheng ZF(1), Deutscher MP.

Author information:
(1)Department of Biochemistry and Molecular Biology, University of Miami School
of Medicine, Miami, FL 33101, USA.

mRNA decay is a major determinant of gene expression. In Escherichia coli,
message degradation initiates with an endoribonucleolytic cleavage followed by
exoribonuclease digestion to generate 5'-mononucleotides. Although the 3' to 5'
processive exoribonucleases, PNPase and RNase II, have long been considered to
be mediators of this digestion, we show here that another enzyme, RNase R, also
participates in the process. RNase R is particularly important for removing mRNA
fragments with extensive secondary structure, such as those derived from the
many mRNAs that contain REP elements. In the absence of RNase R and PNPase,
REP-containing fragments accumulate to high levels. RNase R is unusual among
exoribonucleases in that, by itself, it can digest through extensive secondary
structure provided that a single-stranded binding region, such as a poly(A)
tail, is present. These data demonstrate that RNase R, which is widespread in
prokaryotes and eukaryotes, is an important participant in mRNA decay.
