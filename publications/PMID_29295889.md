The actin cytoskeleton-a collection of actin filaments with their accessory and
regulatory proteins-is the primary force-generating machinery in the cell. It
can produce pushing (protrusive) forces through coordinated polymerization of
multiple actin filaments or pulling (contractile) forces through sliding actin
filaments along bipolar filaments of myosin II. Both force types are
particularly important for whole-cell migration, but they also define and change
the cell shape and mechanical properties of the cell surface, drive the
intracellular motility and morphogenesis of membrane organelles, and allow cells
to form adhesions with each other and with the extracellular matrix.
