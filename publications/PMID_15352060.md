# Arabidopsis glycosyltransferases as biocatalysts in fermentation for regioselective synthesis of diverse quercetin glucosides.
**Authors:** Lim EK, Ashford DA, Hou B, Jackson RG, Bowles DJ
**Journal:** Biotechnol Bioeng (2004)
**DOI:** [10.1002/bit.20154](https://doi.org/10.1002/bit.20154)

## Abstract

1. Biotechnol Bioeng. 2004 Sep 5;87(5):623-31. doi: 10.1002/bit.20154.

Arabidopsis glycosyltransferases as biocatalysts in fermentation for
regioselective synthesis of diverse quercetin glucosides.

Lim EK(1), Ashford DA, Hou B, Jackson RG, Bowles DJ.

Author information:
(1)Department of Biology, CNAP, Heslington, University of York, York, North
Yorkshire YO10 5DD, United Kingdom.

Regioselectivity of glycosyltransferases offers an important means to overcome
the limitations of chemical synthesis of small molecule glycosides. In this
study we explore a large multigene family of UDP-glucose:glycosyltransferases of
Arabidopsis for their potential as novel biocatalysts for in vitro synthesis and
whole-cell catalysis. We used quercetin as a substrate for this study because
the flavonol and its glycosides have important medicinal properties and the
metabolite provides a complex structure for regioselective glucosylation. We
analyzed the activity of 91 recombinant enzymes for in vitro activity toward
quercetin and discovered 29 that are capable of glucosylating the substrate. We
demonstrate the first enzymic synthesis of a range of glucosides in vitro,
including the 3-O-, 7-O-, 3'-O-, and 4'-O-monoglucosides, 3,7-di-O-glucoside,
and 7,3'-di-O-glucoside. We also show that the regioselectivity of glucosylation
can be maintained when the enzymes are used as whole-cell biocatalysts in
Escherichia coli.
