# CBFβ Regulates RUNX3 ADP-Ribosylation to Mediate Homologous Recombination Repair.

RUNX3 is a master developmental transcriptional factor that has been implicated
as a tumor suppressor in many cancers. However, the exact role of RUNX3 in
cancer pathogenesis remains to be completely elucidated. Recently, it has
emerged that RUNX3 is involved in the DNA damage response. Here, we demonstrate
that heterodimerization of RUNX3 with CBFβ is necessary for its stability by
protecting RUNX3 from RUNX3 ADP-ribosylation-dependent ubiquitination and
degradation. We further identify new amino acid residues that are targets for
PARylation and demonstrate that RUNX3 PARylation at these residues is necessary
for localization of RUNX3 to DNA double strand break sites (DBSs). We also
demonstrate that both RUNX3 PARylation and CBFβ heterodimerization with RUNX3
positively regulates homologous recombination (HR) repair, in part by promoting
the recruitment of CtIP and phospho-RPA2 to the DBSs to mediate HR repair. In
summary, we provide evidence that RUNX3 regulates HR repair activity in a
PARylation-dependent manner.

We found that the PAR chains on the D114AD117A and the R192Q mutants was drastically reduced (Figure 3D), indicating that the D114, D117, and R192Q residues are important PARylation sites in the RUNT domain of RUNX3.

We next wanted to determine if mutation of these novel PARylation sites had any functional effect. To test if the RUNX3 PARylation deficient mutants D114AD117A and R192Q affected the ability of RUNX3 to localize at DSB sites, we reconstituted the sgRNA resistant SFB-tagged WT and PARylation-deficient RUNX3 mutants in the U2OS 2-6-3 reporter cell line. This cell line harbors expression of a mCherry-LacI-FokI nuclease fused to a destabilization domain, whose expression is inducible when treated with 4-OHT and stabilized if treated with Shield-1 ligand. (Tang et al. 2013). Thus, using this cell line, we can visually monitor sites of DSBs as mCherry foci and concomitantly perform RUNX3 immunofluorescence to monitor if RUNX3 localizes to these DSB sites. Our results demonstrate that the ability of both the D114AD117A and the R192Q PARylation-deficient mutants to localize to DSBs is significantly reduced compared with the WT (Figure 3E–G, Supporting Information S1: Figure S2C). Interestingly, the RUNX3 T179A mutant, which fails to bind to CBFβ, also showed a significantly decreased ability to localize to DSBs (Figure 3E–G, Supporting Information S1: Figure S2C). These results demonstrate that both PARylation and CBFβ heterodimerization are important for RUNX3 localization to DSB sites. To investigate whether RUNX3 ADP-ribosylation is involved in recognizing DSBs after DNA damage, we conducted in-situ PLA following IR treatment. Our results show that the RUNX3 D114 AD117A and R192Q mutants, which are deficient in PARylation, exhibit reduced binding of DSB ends, preventing their localization at DSBs (Supporting Information S1: Figure S2D–G). Furthermore, the PLA assay demonstrated that these PARylation-deficient mutants of RUNX3 also decrease the interaction between RUNX3 and CtIP following IR (Supporting Information S1: Figure S2H–I). This suggests that RUNX3 ADP-ribosylation is essential for its localization at DSBs through its interaction with CtIP.
