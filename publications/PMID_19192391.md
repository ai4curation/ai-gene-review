# PKD2 interacts with Lck and regulates NFAT activity in T cells.
**Authors:** Li Q, Sun X, Wu J, Lin Z, Luo Y
**Journal:** BMB Rep (2009)
**DOI:** [10.5483/bmbrep.2009.42.1.035](https://doi.org/10.5483/bmbrep.2009.42.1.035)

## Abstract

1. BMB Rep. 2009 Jan 31;42(1):35-40. doi: 10.5483/bmbrep.2009.42.1.035.

PKD2 interacts with Lck and regulates NFAT activity in T cells.

Li Q(1), Sun X, Wu J, Lin Z, Luo Y.

Author information:
(1)Department of Life Science and Biotechnology, Shanghai Jiaotong University,
1954 Huashan Road, Shanghai, 200030, China.

Protein kinase D2 (PKD2) is a member of the PKD serine/threonine protein kinase
family that has been implicated in the regulation of a variety of cellular
processes including proliferation, survival, protein trafficking and immune
response. In the present study, we report a novel interaction between PKD2 and
Lck, a member of the Src tyrosine protein kinase family that is predominantly
expressed in T cells. This interaction involved the C-terminal kinase domains of
both PKD2 and Lck. Moreover, co-expression of Lck enhanced the tyrosine
phosphorylation of PKD2 and increased its kinase activity. Finally, we report
that PKD2 enhanced T cell receptor (TCR)-induced nuclear factor of T cell (NFAT)
activity in Jurkat T cells. These results suggested that Lck regulated the
activity of PKD2 by tyrosine phosphorylation, which in turn may have modulated
the physiological functions of PKD2 during TCR-induced T cell activation. [BMB
reports 2009; 42(1): 35-40].
