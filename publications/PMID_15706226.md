## Abstract

1. Neuroreport. 2005 Feb 28;16(3):235-8. doi: 10.1097/00001756-200502280-00006.

Dystrophin Dp71 in PC12 cell adhesion.

Enríquez-Aragón JA(1), Cerna-Cortés J, Bermúdez de León M, García-Sierra F,
González E, Mornet D, Cisneros B.

Author information:
(1)Department of Genetics and Molecular Biology, Centro de Investigación y de
Estudios Avanzados del IPN, 07360 México, D. F., México.

Previously, we reported that PC12 cells with decreased Dp71 expression
(antisense-Dp71 cells) display deficient nerve-growth-factor-induced neurite
outgrowth. In this study, we show that disturbed neurite outgrowth of
antisense-Dp71 cells is accompanied by decreased adhesion activity on laminin,
collagen and fibronectin. In wild-type cells, the immunostaining of Dp71 and
beta1-integrin overlaps in the basal area contacting the substrate, but staining
of both proteins decrease in the antisense-Dp71 cells. Morphology of
antisense-Dp71 cells at the electron microscopic level is characterized by the
lack of filopodia, cellular projections involved in adhesion. Our findings
suggest that Dp71 is required for the efficient PC12 cell attachment to
beta1-integrin-dependent substrata and that decreased adhesion activity of the
antisense-Dp71 cells could determine their deficiency to extend neurites.
