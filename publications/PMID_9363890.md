    Nature. 1997 Nov 6;390(6655):18-9. doi: 10.1038/36210.

A new gene, termed klotho, has been identified that is involved in the
suppression of several ageing phenotypes. A defect in klotho gene expression in
the mouse results in a syndrome that resembles human ageing, including a short
lifespan, infertility, arteriosclerosis, skin atrophy, osteoporosis and
emphysema. The gene encodes a membrane protein that shares sequence similarity
with the beta-glucosidase enzymes. The klotho gene product may function as part
of a signalling pathway that regulates ageing in vivo and morbidity in
age-related diseases.
