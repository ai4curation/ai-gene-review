# Functional loss of Cdkal1, a novel tRNA modification enzyme, causes the development of type 2 diabetes.
**Authors:** Wei FY, Tomizawa K
**Journal:** Endocr J (2011)
**DOI:** [10.1507/endocrj.ej11-0099](https://doi.org/10.1507/endocrj.ej11-0099)

## Abstract

1. Endocr J. 2011;58(10):819-25. doi: 10.1507/endocrj.ej11-0099. Epub 2011 Sep 8.

Functional loss of Cdkal1, a novel tRNA modification enzyme, causes the
development of type 2 diabetes.

Wei FY(1), Tomizawa K.

Author information:
(1)Department of Molecular Physiology, Faculty of Life Sciences, Kumamoto
University, Kumamoto, Japan.

A number of whole-genome association studies show the cdk5 regulatory associated
protein 1-like 1 (cdkal1) gene to be one of the most reproducible risk genes in
type 2 diabetes (T2D). Variations in the gene are associated with impaired
insulin secretion but not insulin resistance or obesity. Although the
physiological functions of Cdkal1 had been unclear, recent studies show that it
is a tRNA modification enzyme, a mammalian methylthiotransferase that
biosynthesizes 2-methylthio-N(6)-threonylcarbamoyladenosine (ms(2)t(6)A) at
position 37 of tRNA(Lys)(UUU). The ms(2)t(6)A modification in tRNA(Lys)(UUU) is
important for preventing the misreading of its cognate codons, especially when
the rate of translation is relatively high. In both general and pancreatic
β-cell-specific cdkal1-deficient mice, impaired mitochondrial ATP generation and
first-phase insulin secretion are observed. Moreover, the β-cell-specific
knockout mice show pancreatic islet hypertrophy and impaired blood glucose
control. The mice are also hypersensitive to high-fat diet-induced ER stress. In
this review, we provide an overview of the physiological functions of Cdkal1 and
the molecular pathogenesis of T2D in patients carrying cdkal1 risk alleles.
