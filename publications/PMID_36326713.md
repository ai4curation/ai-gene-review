# Insertion of 4-Demethylwyosine in tRNA(Phe) Catalyzed by the Radical S-Adenosyl-l-methionine Enzyme TYW1 Entails Oxidative Cleavage of Pyruvate to Form CO(2).

The radical S-adenosyl-l-methionine (SAM) enzyme TYW1 catalyzes the condensation
of C-2 and C-3 atoms of pyruvate with N-methylguanosine containing tRNAPhe to
form 4-demethylwyosine (imG-14) modified tRNAPhe. The fate of C-1 is not known,
and either formate or carbon dioxide (CO2) has been proposed. In this study, a
coupled assay that transforms either CO2 or formate to oxaloacetate (OAA) was
used to determine the fate of C-1. In the presence of [1-13C1]-pyruvate,
13C-enriched OAA was observed in a process that is concomitant with the
formation of imG-14, under conditions that preferentially transform CO2 and not
formate to OAA. These findings are discussed in the context of the cofactor
content of TYW1 and a new role for the auxiliary cluster in catalyzing the
oxidative cleavage of C-1-C-2 bond of pyruvate in the catalytic cycle of TYW1.

## Full Text

The radical S-adenosyl-l-methionine (SAM) enzyme superfamily is comprised of well over 100,000 enzymes1 that catalyze a wide range of transformations in all aspects of cellular physiology2. The defining feature of the radical SAM (RS) superfamily is the presence of a 4Fe-4S cluster, which is typically ligated by the cysteine rich motif CxxxCxxC3. Three of the iron atoms in the 4Fe-4S cluster are ligated via the cysteines, with the remaining iron atom coordinating SAM via the α-amino and α-carboxylate moieties4–6. The 4Fe-4S cluster cycles between two oxidation states, the oxidized +2 state (inactive) and the reduced +1 state (active). In the reduced state the cluster reductively cleaves SAM forming methionine and the high energy intermediate, 5′-deoxyadenosyl radical (dAdo•), which typically abstracts an un-activated hydrogen from substrate to initiate the catalytic cycle7.

In addition to the 4Fe-4S cluster that coordinates SAM, a subset of RS enzymes also harbor additional so-called auxiliary iron-sulfur cluster(s), whose role(s) remain enigmatic8,9. Emerging studies suggest that they play a role in coordinating the substrate, or engaging in electron transfer in the course of the reaction10–13.

TYW1 is a RS enzyme with both a RS cluster and a single auxiliary cluster11. TYW1 is found throughout archaea and eukarya, where it catalyzes the condensation of C-2 and C-3 of pyruvate and N-methylguanosine (m1G) containing tRNAPhe, to form 4-demethylwyosine (imG-14) containing tRNAPhe with C-1 forming an unidentified product (Scheme 1)14–17. All TYW1 homologs contain two 4Fe-4S clusters, while the eukaryal homolog is unique among RS enzymes and binds an equivalent of flavin mononucleotide18. The two 4Fe-4S clusters in TYW1 are hypothesized to bind two different substrates. One of the 4Fe-4S clusters, bound by the characteristic CxxxCxxC motif, binds and reductively cleave SAM, which is typical of RS enzymes2. The auxiliary 4Fe-4S cluster, however, has been proposed to interact with pyruvate, which itself is bound via a Schiff base to an invariant Lys residue in the active site of the enzyme (Scheme 1). The covalent adduct and interaction between the imino nitrogen and carboxylate oxygen and the auxiliary cluster has been visualized in a X-ray crystal structure11,14–16 and detected by electron paramagnetic resonance (EPR) and Mössbauer spectroscopy19. Based on the intimate interaction between the covalently bound pyruvate and the auxiliary cluster, it has been proposed that the cluster likely plays a role in either accepting an electron from C-1 to form carbon dioxide (CO2), or reducing C-1 to generate formate11,14–17. The identity of the C-1 derived product has not been determined and so the role of the auxiliary cluster in the reaction catalyzed by TYW1 has remained enigmatic.

To facilitate identification of the C-1 based product, TYW1 was coupled to formate dehydrogenase (FD) and/or phosphoenolpyruvate carboxylase (PEPc) to generate oxaloacetate (OAA). As shown in Scheme 2, if CO2 is the fate of C-1 of pyruvate, then one would expect that it would undergo hydration to bicarbonate in the assay mixture and be transformed to oxaloacetate by the action of PEPc in the presence of phosphopenolpyruvate (PEP). On the other hand, if formate were the product, then in the presence of FD it would be converted to CO2 in an NAD+ dependent manner, and subsequently transformed to OAA as described above. Of course, the high concentrations of dissolved CO2 that is present in the solvents and reagents used could confound the analysis. Therefore, we utilized [1-13C1]-pyruvate to circumvent the dissolved CO2.

Methanocaldococcus jannaschii TYW1 (MjTYW1) was incubated with SAM, tRNA stem loop corresponding to nt 27-43 of the S. cerevisiae tRNAPhe, [1-13C1]-pyruvate, methyl viologen, and dithionite in an anaerobic chamber, and the reaction was allowed to proceed at 50 °C overnight. At the end of the incubation period, PEPc and PEP were added to all reactions, and FD and NAD+ to half of the samples. The OAA resulting from the coupled reaction was analyzed by LC-MS with separations occurring on a hydrophilic exchange column.

The zoomed in negative ion MS for oxaloacetate is shown in Figure 1, and the full mass spectrum is shown in Figure S1. The monoisotopic peak of OAA exhibits a peak at m/z 130.997. The intensity of the peak corresponding to OAA containing a single 13C is at m/z of 132.001. The height of this peak relative to that of the natural abundance feature is expected to be 4.3% of the monoisotopic feature (as shown in Figure 1). However, in the presence of [1-13C1]-pyruvate, the OAA that is formed is enriched with 13C, regardless of whether PEPc/PEP and/or FD/NAD+ were present in the reaction mixture. Both conditions show enrichment of the OAA, with the peak observed with PEPc/PEP corresponding to 28% (±5.6%), and that obtained with PEPc/PEP/FD/NAD+ corresponding to 28% (± 3.3%) of the monoisotopic feature, as shown in Figure 1 and panels B and C of Figure S1 (relative percentage peak heights are shown in Table S1). We note that while we made every attempt to minimize the background CO2, we were unable to remove it entirely. In addition to potential dissolved CO2, the water used in the assays was DEPC-treated. The removal of DEPC by autoclaving can also produce residual carbonate/CO2 as by-products. Fortunately, the amount of product appears to be of the same order of magnitude as the background CO2, allowing the enrichment to be clearly observed. Collectively, these data clearly show CO2 as the fate of the C-1 of pyruvate released during turnover of TYW1.

To confirm that the 13C enriched OAA that was detected is due to the CO2 produced by TYW1 and was accompanied by formation of the imG-14 base, a series of control assays in the absence of SAM, dithionite, [1-13C1]-pyruvate, MjTYW1, or tRNA stem loop were carried out. Following an incubation with PEPc and PEP, the OAA produced was analyzed for 13C enrichment and the tRNA was analyzed for the presence of imG-14. Analysis of the OAA produced when all components were present again revealed 13C enrichment (23 ± 5%) as had been observed above. In the absence of pyruvate, SAM, tRNA stem loop, or MjTYW1 the peak for 13C was essentially indistinguishable from that observed, suggesting that in each case, they were resulting from the dissolved CO2. Interestingly, we observe significant enrichment of OAA with 13C when dithionite was not included. We cannot exclude the possibility that some fraction of the enzyme is in the correct oxidation state for the reaction and requires no additional reductant to be active.

Figure 2 shows representative data with the monoisotopic peak shown along with the 13C peak for each of the control assays described above and Table S2 shows the relative percentage for the 13C peak in each sample. These control experiments also rule out the possibility that the labeled OAA results from PEPc-catalyzed addition of the contaminating unlabeled CO2 to enolate of the labeled pyruvate to form OAA20.

In order to determine if the enrichment of OAA occurs simultaneously with the conversion of m1G to imG-14, tRNA was extracted from each assay mixtures, digested to the nucleoside level, and analyzed by LC-MS (see Figure S2). The pyruvate used in these experiments was 13C labeled at C-1; therefore, no enrichment of the imG-14 product was expected. Indeed, in the presence of all assay components, we observe the product peak at m/z 322.1146, which is identical to the expected mass for the product. Based on the oxaloacetate enrichment results shown in Table S2 it was expected that the samples containing all the components but dithionite should also produce imG-14, as there was enrichment in the no dithionite experiment. The no dithionite sample also contained imG-14 (m/z 322.114), which likely results from a residual fraction of the TYW1 protein that is already reduced and does not require additional reductive activation. Unexpectedly, the no pyruvate sample also contained imG-14 (m/z 322.114). While this may be surprising, X-ray crystal structure of TYW1 clearly revealed that pyruvate can co-purify with the enzyme. The 13C enrichment in OAA when pyruvate was omitted, was within error of that expected with unlabeled pyruvate, as would be expected if the pyruvate co-purified with TYW1. The remaining three controls where SAM, TYW1, or the RNA stem loop substrate were omitted all contained no imG-14 (Figure S2). The peaks corresponding to the imG-14 product in the no dithionite and no pyruvate traces are denoted with an *. Their intensities are much lower due to much lower pyruvate and reduced enzyme concentrations.

Collectively, the data presented in this manuscript show that in the presence of [1-13C1]-pyruvate, MjTYW1 produces 13CO2. The CO2 was detected by incorporation into OAA with PEPc/PEP regardless of whether FD/NAD+ were present. Therefore, our results suggest that the role of auxiliary cluster, which has been shown to interact with pyruvate, is to promote oxidative cleavage of the C1-C2 bond in pyruvate.

An earlier EPR study of MjTYW1 showed that the auxiliary cluster cycles between the +1 and +2 oxidation states19. We propose that in the resting state, the auxiliary cluster is in the +2 oxidation state, and that oxidative cleavage of pyruvate leads to reduction of the cluster to the +1 oxidation state. A role for auxiliary clusters in RS enzymes has been proposed for several members of the superfamily. In a recent EPR study, SuiB, which catalyzes a C-C cross link a Trp and Lys residue in a peptide, revealed that it has an auxiliary cluster that is in the +2 oxidation state at the outset of a reaction cycle, and then reduces to the +1 state during the course of the reaction, presumably accepting an electron from the substrate during the course of the reaction13.

The mechanistic proposal shown in Scheme 3 implicates the auxiliary cluster as having a role in oxidative C-C cleavage, suggesting that the cluster is in the +1 state at the end of the cycle. TYW1 requires reductive cleavage of SAM on each turnover. We cannot exclude the possibility that the pyruvate-derived electron that remains in the auxiliary cluster is recycled to the radical SAM cluster upon binding of the substrate. This would allow the enzyme to carry out multiple turnovers after an initial reductive activation event. Indeed, there is literature precedent for the proposed transfer of an electron from an auxiliary cluster to the radical SAM binding cluster in anaerobic sulfatase-maturing enzyme wherein flavodoxin was used to initially reduce the protein and the concentration of reduced flavodoxin remained constant throughout the reaction21. TYW1 used in this study is inactive with the flavodoxin/flavodoxin reductase systems that are commonly used with RS enzymes. As a consequence of this, the strong reductant dithionite was used as the reductant in these studies.

In conclusion, using a coupled assay that would convert either formate or CO2 to OAA it has been determined that the second product of TYW1 derived from pyruvate is CO2. Based on this observation we are proposing an updated role for the auxiliary cluster in which in addition to coordinating pyruvate, oxidizes the product of the reaction.
