# Human actin depolymerizing factor mediates a pH-sensitive destruction of actin filaments.
**Authors:** Hawkins M, Pope B, Maciver SK, Weeds AG
**Journal:** Biochemistry (1993)
**DOI:** [10.1021/bi00089a014](https://doi.org/10.1021/bi00089a014)

## Abstract

1. Biochemistry. 1993 Sep 28;32(38):9985-93. doi: 10.1021/bi00089a014.

Human actin depolymerizing factor mediates a pH-sensitive destruction of actin
filaments.

Hawkins M(1), Pope B, Maciver SK, Weeds AG.

Author information:
(1)MRC Laboratory of Molecular Biology, Cambridge, England.

ADF (actin depolymerizing factor) is an M(r) 19,000 actin-binding protein
present in many vertebrate tissues and particularly abundant in neuronal cells.
We have cloned human ADF and here show it to be identical in sequence to porcine
destrin. Human ADF expressed in Escherichia coli behaves like native ADF from
porcine brain. It binds to G-actin at pH 8 with a 1:1 stoichiometry and Kd
approximately 0.2 microM, thereby sequestering monomers and preventing
polymerization. It does not cosediment with F-actin at this pH, but severs actin
filaments in a calcium-insensitive manner. The severing activity is only about
0.1% efficient. By contrast, at pH values below 7, ADF binds to actin filaments
in a highly cooperative manner and at a 1:1 ratio to filament subunits. When the
pH is raised to 8.0, the decorated filaments are rapidly severed and
depolymerized.
