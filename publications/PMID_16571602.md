# The nuclear microspherule protein 58 is a novel RNA-binding protein that interacts with fragile X mental retardation protein in polyribosomal mRNPs from neurons.
**Authors:** Davidovic L, Bechara E, Gravel M, Jaglin XH, Tremblay S, Sik A, Bardoni B, Khandjian EW
**Journal:** Hum Mol Genet (2006)
**DOI:** [10.1093/hmg/ddl074](https://doi.org/10.1093/hmg/ddl074)

## Abstract

1. Hum Mol Genet. 2006 May 1;15(9):1525-38. doi: 10.1093/hmg/ddl074. Epub 2006

The nuclear microspherule protein 58 is a novel RNA-binding protein that
interacts with fragile X mental retardation protein in polyribosomal mRNPs from
neurons.

Davidovic L(1), Bechara E, Gravel M, Jaglin XH, Tremblay S, Sik A, Bardoni B,
Khandjian EW.

Author information:
(1)Unité de Recherche en Génétique Humaine et Moléculaire, Centre de recherche
Hôpital Saint-François d'Assise, le CHUQ, Québec, Canada G1L 3L5.

The fragile X syndrome, the leading cause of inherited mental retardation, is
due to the inactivation of the fragile mental retardation 1 gene (FMR1) and the
subsequent absence of its gene product FMRP. This RNA-binding protein is thought
to control mRNA translation and its absence in fragile X cells leads to
alteration in protein synthesis. In neurons, FMRP is thought to repress specific
mRNAs during their transport as silent ribonucleoparticles (mRNPs) from the cell
body to the distant synapses which are the sites of local synthesis of
neuro-specific proteins. The mechanism by which FMRP sorts out its different
mRNAs targets might be tuned by the intervention of different proteins. Using a
yeast two-hybrid system, we identified MicroSpherule Protein 58 (MSP58) as a
novel FMRP-cellular partner. In cell cultures, we found that MSP58 is
predominantly present in the nucleus where it interacts with the nuclear isoform
of FMRP. However, in neurons but not in glial cells, MSP58 is also present in
the cytoplasmic compartment, as well as in neurites, where it co-localizes with
FMRP. Biochemical evidence is given that MSP58 is associated with polyribosomal
poly(A)+ mRNPs. We also show that MSP58, similar to FMRP, is present on
polyribosomes prepared from synaptoneurosomes and that it behaves as an
RNA-binding protein with a high affinity to the G-quartet structure. We propose
that this novel cellular partner for FMRP escorts FMRP-containing mRNP from the
nucleus and nucleolus to the somato-dendritic compartment where it might
participate in neuronal translation regulation.
