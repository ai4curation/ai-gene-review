From yeast to humans, mRNAs harboring premature termination codons (PTCs) are
recognized and degraded by nonsense-mediated mRNA decay (NMD). However,
degradation mechanisms of NMD have been suggested to differ between species. In
Drosophila melanogaster, NMD is initiated by endonucleolysis near the PTC,
whereas in yeast and human cells the current view posits that NMD occurs by
exonucleolysis from one or both RNA termini. Here we report that degradation of
human nonsense mRNAs can be initiated by PTC-proximal endonucleolytic cleavage.
We identify the metazoan-specific NMD factor SMG6 as the responsible
endonuclease by demonstrating that mutation of conserved residues in its
nuclease domain--the C-terminal PIN motif--abolishes endonucleolysis in vivo and
in vitro. Our data lead to a revised mechanistic model for degradation of
nonsense mRNA in human cells and suggest that endonucleolytic cleavage is a
conserved feature in metazoan NMD.
