# Genetic analysis of the glucose-6-phosphatase mutation of type 1a glycogen storage disease in a Chinese family.
**Authors:** Lee WJ, Lee HM, Chi CS, Shu SG, Lin LY, Lin WH
**Journal:** Clin Genet (1996)
**DOI:** [10.1111/j.1399-0004.1996.tb02627.x](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1399-0004.1996.tb02627.x)

## Abstract

1. Clin Genet. 1996 Oct;50(4):206-11. doi: 10.1111/j.1399-0004.1996.tb02627.x.

Genetic analysis of the glucose-6-phosphatase mutation of type 1a glycogen
storage disease in a Chinese family.

Lee WJ(1), Lee HM, Chi CS, Shu SG, Lin LY, Lin WH.

Author information:
(1)Department of Medicine, Taichung Veterans General Hospital, Taiwan.

Type 1a glycogen storage disease (GSD) is an autosomal recessive metabolic
disorder caused by a deficiency in glucose-6-phosphatase (G6Pase). Polymerase
chain reaction (PCR) and nucleotide sequence analysis were used to identify the
location and nature of mutations at the G6Pase locus in two siblings affected
with type 1a GSD. Both patients are compound heterozygotes with two different
single nucleotide substitutions in the two G6Pase alleles. A guanine to adenine
transition was identified at base position 327 in the exon 2, converting an
arginine to a histidine at codon 83. The second substitution was a thymine to
adenine transversion at base position 1101 in the exon 5, converting an
isoleucine to an asparagine at codon 341. Family study reveals that both parents
are heterozygous carriers: the father with a mutant G6Pase allele at exon 2, the
mother with another mutant G6Pase allele at exon 5. This is the first family
study in Taiwan on type 1a GSD identified by molecular analysis. The mutations
identified herein are novel substitutions in the G6Pase gene. In addition, an
adenine to guanine substitution was observed at base position 653 in the exon 5
of G6Pase gene in both sibling patients and their parents, as well as in 15
normal Chinese subjects and three normal Caucasian subjects.
