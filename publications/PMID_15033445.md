# RASL11A, member of a novel small monomeric GTPase gene family, is down-regulated in prostate tumors.
**Authors:** Louro R, Nakaya HI, Paquola AC, Martins EA, da Silva AM, Verjovski-Almeida S, Reis EM
**Journal:** Biochem Biophys Res Commun (2004)
**DOI:** [10.1016/j.bbrc.2004.02.091](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.bbrc.2004.02.091)

## Abstract

1. Biochem Biophys Res Commun. 2004 Apr 9;316(3):618-27. doi:
10.1016/j.bbrc.2004.02.091.

RASL11A, member of a novel small monomeric GTPase gene family, is down-regulated
in prostate tumors.

Louro R(1), Nakaya HI, Paquola AC, Martins EA, da Silva AM, Verjovski-Almeida S,
Reis EM.

Author information:
(1)Departamento de Bioquímica, Instituto de Química, Universidade de São Paulo,
05508-900 São Paulo, SP, Brazil.

We performed a genome-wide search for novel loci encoding for Ras-related
proteins based on the genome mapping coordinates of the cancer-derived EST
dataset at GenBank. Partial sequences from two novel human genes were identified
and subsequently used for full length transcript cloning. RASL11A and ARL9
belong to two novel subfamilies coding for small GTPases that we found to be
highly conserved among eukaryotes. The Arl9/Arl10 subfamily displays a conserved
interswitch toggle that places it evolutionarily closer to the Arf family.
Rasl11 proteins are more closely related to the Ras branch of GTPases. All
orthologues newly identified here exhibit an Asn residue in place of the highly
conserved Thr35 of the G domain, suggesting that the universal switch mechanism
of small GTPases may be structurally different in this subfamily. We determined
by Northern blot that RASL11A is transcribed in several human tissues and that
it is down-regulated in prostate tumors as measured by quantitative real-time
PCR. These results highlight a previously uncharacterized subfamily of
Ras-related genes that may have a tumor suppressor role in prostate cancer.
