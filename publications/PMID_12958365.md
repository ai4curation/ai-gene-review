Comment in
    Science. 2003 Sep 5;301(5638):1338-9. doi: 10.1126/science.1089552.

beta-Arrestins bind to activated seven transmembrane-spanning (7TMS) receptors
(G protein-coupled receptors) after the receptors are phosphorylated by G
protein-coupled receptor kinases (GRKs), thereby regulating their signaling and
internalization. Here, we demonstrate an unexpected and analogous role of
beta-arrestin 2 (betaarr2) for the single transmembrane-spanning type III
transforming growth factor-beta (TGF-beta) receptor (TbetaRIII, also referred to
as betaglycan). Binding of betaarr2 to TbetaRIII was also triggered by
phosphorylation of the receptor on its cytoplasmic domain (likely at threonine
841). However, such phosphorylation was mediated by the type II TGF-beta
receptor (TbetaRII), which is itself a kinase, rather than by a GRK. Association
with betaarr2 led to internalization of both receptors and down-regulation of
TGF-beta signaling. Thus, the regulatory actions of beta-arrestins are broader
than previously appreciated, extending to the TGF-beta receptor family as well.
