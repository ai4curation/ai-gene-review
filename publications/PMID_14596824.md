# High-throughput methods to detect dimerization of Bcl-2 family proteins.
**Authors:** Whitfield J, Harada K, Bardelle C, Staddon JM
**Journal:** Anal Biochem (2003)
**DOI:** [10.1016/j.ab.2003.07.014](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ab.2003.07.014)

## Abstract

1. Anal Biochem. 2003 Nov 15;322(2):170-8. doi: 10.1016/j.ab.2003.07.014.

High-throughput methods to detect dimerization of Bcl-2 family proteins.

Whitfield J(1), Harada K, Bardelle C, Staddon JM.

Author information:
(1)Eisai London Research Laboratories Ltd., Bernard Katz Building, University
College London, Gower Street, WC1E 6BT, London, UK.

Members of the Bcl-2 family are critical regulators of apoptosis. Antiapoptotic
family proteins such as Bcl-2 and Bcl-x(L) function, at least in part, by
binding proapoptotic members such as Bax and Bak and thereby preventing release
of apoptotic proteins, including cytochrome c, from the mitochondria. "BH3-only"
members of the family disrupt this interaction by binding, via their BH3 domain,
to a hydrophobic pocket on the surface of the antiapoptotic members. Disruption
of heterodimerizations by small-molecule inhibitors could be used to modulate
cell death in both cancer (to increase apoptosis) and degenerative disorders (to
decrease apoptosis), and assays are necessary to screen compound libraries.
Fluorescence polarization and enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay-based methods to
detect Bcl-2 protein interactions have been described. Here, two further methods
that are rapid, "mix and read," homogeneous reactions, insensitive to compound
autofluorescence, and amenable to high-throughput screening, are described: a
scintillation proximity assay and a time-resolved fluorescence resonance energy
transfer assay (HTRF). The assays are designed using tags such that different
Bcl-2 family members or BH3 domain peptides can be readily applied to either
format, as exemplified by the use here of histidine-tagged Bcl-x(L) and
biotinylated BH3 peptides.
