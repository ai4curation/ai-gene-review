# Adenylate kinase 1 deficiency induces molecular and structural adaptations to support muscle energy metabolism.
**Authors:** Janssen E, de Groof A, Wijers M, Fransen J, Dzeja PP, Terzic A, Wieringa B
**Journal:** J Biol Chem (2003)
**DOI:** [10.1074/jbc.M211465200](https://doi.org/10.1074/jbc.M211465200)

## Abstract

1. J Biol Chem. 2003 Apr 11;278(15):12937-45. doi: 10.1074/jbc.M211465200. Epub
2003 Jan 31.

Adenylate kinase 1 deficiency induces molecular and structural adaptations to
support muscle energy metabolism.

Janssen E(1), de Groof A, Wijers M, Fransen J, Dzeja PP, Terzic A, Wieringa B.

Author information:
(1)Department of Cell Biology, Nijmegen Center for Molecular Life Sciences,
University Medical Center, University of Nijmegen, The Netherlands.

Genetic ablation of adenylate kinase 1 (AK1), a member of the AK family of
phosphotransfer enzymes, disturbs muscle energetic economy and decreases
tolerance to metabolic stress, despite rearrangements in alternative high energy
phosphoryl transfer pathways. To define the mechanisms of this adaptive
response, soleus and gastrocnemius muscles from AK1(-/-) mice were characterized
by cDNA array profiling, Western blot and ultrastructural analysis. We
demonstrate that AK1 deficiency induces fiber-type specific variation in groups
of transcripts involved in glycolysis and mitochondrial metabolism and in gene
products defining structural and myogenic events. This was associated with
increased phosphotransfer capacities of the glycolytic enzymes pyruvate kinase
and 3-phosphoglycerate kinase. Moreover, in AK1(-/-) mice, fast-twitch
gastrocnemius, but not slow-twitch soleus, had an increase in adenine nucleotide
translocator (ANT) and mitochondrial creatine kinase protein, along with a
doubling of the intermyofibrillar mitochondrial volume. These results provide
molecular evidence for wide-scale remodeling in AK1-deficient muscles aimed at
preservation of efficient energetic communication between ATP producing and
utilizing cellular sites.
