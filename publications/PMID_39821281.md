# Gene pseudogenization in fertility-associated genes in cheetah (Acinonyx jubatus), a species with long-term low effective population size.
**Authors:** Peers JA, Nash WJ, Haerty W
**Journal:** Evolution (2025)
**DOI:** [10.1093/evolut/qpaf005](https://doi.org/10.1093/evolut/qpaf005)

## Abstract

1. Evolution. 2025 Apr 2;79(4):574-585. doi: 10.1093/evolut/qpaf005.

Gene pseudogenization in fertility-associated genes in cheetah (Acinonyx
jubatus), a species with long-term low effective population size.

Peers JA(1), Nash WJ(1), Haerty W(1).

Author information:
(1)Earlham Institute, Norwich Research Park, Norwich, United Kingdom.

We are witnessing an ongoing global biodiversity crisis, and an increasing
number of mammalian populations are at risk of decline. Species that have
survived severe historic bottlenecks, such as the cheetah (Acinonyx jubatus)
exhibit symptoms of inbreeding depression including reproductive and
developmental defects. Although it has long been suggested that such defects
stem from an accumulation of weakly deleterious mutations, the implications of
such mutations leading to pseudogenization has not been assessed. Here, we use
comparative analysis of eight felid genomes to better understand the impacts of
deleterious mutations in the cheetah. We find novel pseudogenization events
specific to the cheetah. Through careful curation, we identify 65 genes with
previously unreported premature termination codons (PTCs) that likely affect
gene function. With the addition of population data (n = 6), we find 22 of these
PTCs in at least one resequenced individual, four of which (DEFB116, ARL13A,
CFAP119, and NT5DC4) are also found in a more recent reference genome. Mutations
within three of these genes are linked with sterility, including azoospermia,
which is common in cheetahs. Our results highlight the power of comparative
genomic approaches for the discovery of novel causative variants in declining
species.
