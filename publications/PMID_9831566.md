# Control of cyclin ubiquitination by CDK-regulated binding of Hct1 to the anaphase promoting complex.
**Authors:** Zachariae W, Schwab M, Nasmyth K, Seufert W
**Journal:** Science (1998)
**DOI:** [10.1126/science.282.5394.1721](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.282.5394.1721)

## Abstract

1. Science. 1998 Nov 27;282(5394):1721-4. doi: 10.1126/science.282.5394.1721.

Control of cyclin ubiquitination by CDK-regulated binding of Hct1 to the
anaphase promoting complex.

Zachariae W(1), Schwab M, Nasmyth K, Seufert W.

Author information:
(1)Research Institute of Molecular Pathology, Dr. Bohr-Gasse 7, A-1030 Vienna,
Austria.

Proteolysis of mitotic cyclins depends on a multisubunit ubiquitin-protein
ligase, the anaphase promoting complex (APC). Proteolysis commences during
anaphase, persisting throughout G1 until it is terminated by cyclin-dependent
kinases (CDKs) as cells enter S phase. Proteolysis of mitotic cyclins in yeast
was shown to require association of the APC with the substrate-specific
activator Hct1 (also called Cdh1). Phosphorylation of Hct1 by CDKs blocked the
Hct1-APC interaction. The mutual inhibition between APC and CDKs explains how
cells suppress mitotic CDK activity during G1 and then establish a period with
elevated kinase activity from S phase until anaphase.
