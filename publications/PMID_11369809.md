# Localization of apolipoprotein E receptor 2 to caveolae in the plasma membrane.
**Authors:** Riddell DR, Sun XM, Stannard AK, Soutar AK, Owen JS
**Journal:** J Lipid Res (2001)

## Abstract

1. J Lipid Res. 2001 Jun;42(6):998-1002.

Localization of apolipoprotein E receptor 2 to caveolae in the plasma membrane.

Riddell DR(1), Sun XM, Stannard AK, Soutar AK, Owen JS.

Author information:
(1)Department of Medicine, Royal Free and University College Medical School,
University College London, Royal Free Campus, London NW3 2PF, United Kingdom.

The LDL receptor (LDL-R) promotes the specific endocytosis and lysosomal
delivery of extracellular lipoprotein ligands via clathrin-coated pits. It was
widely assumed that other closely related members of the LDL-R gene family would
have similar functions, but recent experimental evidence has revealed that one
such protein, apolipoprotein E receptor 2 (apoER2), has a critical role as an
"outside-in" signal transducer in the brain. ApoER2 signaling appears to require
interaction between its cytoplasmic domain and adapter molecules such as Dab1,
JIP 1 and JIP 2, and PSD-95. Many of the receptors for other signaling pathways
affected by such adapter molecules are compartmentalized into specialized
microdomains within the plasma membrane termed caveolae. Here, we show that
apoER2, but not LDL-R, is localized to caveolae, supporting the concept that its
physiological role is in cell signaling, rather than in endocytosing ligands.
