Vitamin K-dependent γ-glutamyl carboxylase (GGCX) is an enzyme that catalyzes
the conversion of glutamic acid to gamma-carboxyglutamic acid in substrate
proteins. Among GGCX target proteins, recent evidence indicates that osteocalcin
regulates insulin sensitivity and secretion. However, the precise contribution
of GGCX to glucose metabolism remains to be clarified. To address this question,
we generated osteoblast-specific Ggcx-deficient (i.e., conditional knockout
[cKO]) mice using collagen type 1 α1 (Col1)-Cre mice. Ggcx cKO mice exhibited
altered metabolism compared with their controls; serum glucose levels could be
maintained with low amounts of insulin, and the weight of white adipose tissue
(WAT) significantly decreased in Ggcx cKO mice. Our findings suggest that GGCX
expressed in osteoblasts is critical for the maintenance of blood glucose and
WAT.
