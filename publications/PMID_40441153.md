Comment in
    Cell Chem Biol. 2025 Jun 19;32(6):786-788. doi:
10.1016/j.chembiol.2025.05.011.

Changes in cellular output from one G protein-coupled receptor in another's
presence have been attributed to dimerization and/or signal crosstalk. However,
data distinguishing models are often lacking, and, in many cases, the molecular
basis remains controversial. One such interaction is the reversal, inhibition to
excitation, at the dopamine D2 receptor in the ghrelin receptor's presence in
the spinal defecation center. This reversal is required for the physiological
control of defecation, where dopamine is the relevant neurotransmitter. Using
native neurons from mice and recombinant cells, we show that this reversal of
dopamine D2 activity occurs downstream of calcium mobilization through a
dominant signaling switch. Our results show that this reversal is a consequence
of the ghrelin receptor's constitutive activity, where neither its agonism nor
dimerization with the D2 receptor is required, in the systems tested. Dependence
on constitutive activity may account for conservation of central-nervous-system
ghrelin receptor in the absence of endogenous agonist.
