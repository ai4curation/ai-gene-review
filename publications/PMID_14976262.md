Double-stranded ribonucleic acid (dsRNA) serves as a danger signal associated
with viral infection and leads to stimulation of innate immune cells. In
contrast, the immunostimulatory potential of single-stranded RNA (ssRNA) is
poorly understood and innate immune receptors for ssRNA are unknown. We report
that guanosine (G)- and uridine (U)-rich ssRNA oligonucleotides derived from
human immunodeficiency virus-1 (HIV-1) stimulate dendritic cells (DC) and
macrophages to secrete interferon-alpha and proinflammatory, as well as
regulatory, cytokines. By using Toll-like receptor (TLR)-deficient mice and
genetic complementation, we show that murine TLR7 and human TLR8 mediate
species-specific recognition of GU-rich ssRNA. These data suggest that ssRNA
represents a physiological ligand for TLR7 and TLR8.
