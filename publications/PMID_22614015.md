# The tumour suppressor HACE1 controls cell migration by regulating Rac1 degradation.
**Authors:** Castillo-Lluva S, Tan CT, Daugaard M, Sorensen PH, Malliri A
**Journal:** Oncogene (2013)
**DOI:** [10.1038/onc.2012.189](https://doi.org/10.1038/onc.2012.189)

## Abstract

1. Oncogene. 2013 Mar 28;32(13):1735-42. doi: 10.1038/onc.2012.189. Epub 2012 May
 21.

The tumour suppressor HACE1 controls cell migration by regulating Rac1
degradation.

Castillo-Lluva S(1), Tan CT, Daugaard M, Sorensen PH, Malliri A.

Author information:
(1)Cell Signalling Group, Cancer Research UK Paterson Institute for Cancer

The small GTPase Rac1 is a key regulator of cell motility. Multiple mechanisms
regulate Rac1 activity including its ubiquitylation and subsequent degradation.
Here, we identify the tumour suppressor HACE1 (HECT domain and Ankyrin repeat
Containing E3 ubiquitin-protein ligase 1) as an E3 ubiquitin ligase responsible
for Rac1 degradation following activation by a migration stimulus. We show that
HACE1 and Rac1 interaction is enhanced by hepatocyte growth factor (HGF)
signalling, a Rac activator and potent stimulus of cell migration. Furthermore,
HACE1 catalyses the poly-ubiquitylation of Rac1 at lysine 147 following its
activation by HGF, resulting in its proteasomal degradation. This negative
feedback mechanism likely restricts cell motility. Consistent with this, HACE1
depletion is accompanied by increased total Rac1 levels and accumulation of Rac1
in membrane ruffles. Moreover, HACE1-depletion enhances cell migration
independently of growth factor stimulation, which may have significance for
malignant conversion. A non-ubiquitylatable Rac1 rescues the migration defect of
Rac1-null cells to a greater extent than wild-type Rac1. These findings identify
HACE1 as an antagonist of cell migration through its ability to degrade active
Rac1.
