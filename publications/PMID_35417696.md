Blood vessel morphogenesis is driven by coordinated endothelial cell behaviors.
Active remodeling of cell-cell junctions promotes cellular plasticity while
preserving vascular integrity. Here, we analyze the dynamics of endothelial
adherens junctions during lumen formation in angiogenic sprouts in vivo. Live
imaging in zebrafish reveals that lumen expansion is accompanied by the
formation of transient finger-shaped junctions. Junctional fingers are
positively regulated by blood pressure, whereas flow inhibition prevents their
formation. Using fluorescent reporters, we show that junctional fingers contain
the mechanotransduction protein vinculin. Furthermore, genetic deletion of
vinculin prevents finger formation, a junctional defect that could be rescued by
transient endothelial expression of vinculin. Our findings suggest a mechanism
whereby lumen expansion leads to an increase in junctional tension, triggering
recruitment of vinculin and formation of junctional fingers. We propose that
endothelial cells employ force-dependent junctional remodeling to counteract
external forces in order to maintain vascular integrity during sprouting
angiogenesis.
