RNA polymerase I (Pol I) is the central, 14-subunit enzyme that synthesizes the
ribosomal RNA (rRNA) precursor in eukaryotic cells. The recent crystal structure
of Pol I at 2.8 Å resolution revealed two novel elements: the `expander' in the
active-centre cleft and the `connector' that mediates Pol I dimerization [Engel
et al. (2013), Nature (London), 502, 650-655]. Here, a Pol I structure in an
alternative crystal form that was solved by molecular replacement using the
original atomic Pol I structure is reported. The resulting alternative structure
lacks the expander but still shows an expanded active-centre cleft. The
neighbouring Pol I monomers form a homodimer with a relative orientation
distinct from that observed previously, establishing the connector as a hinge
between Pol I monomers.
