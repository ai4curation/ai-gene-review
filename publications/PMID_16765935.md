# Changes in intranuclear chromatin architecture induce bipolar nuclear localization of histone variant H1T2 in male haploid spermatids.
**Authors:** Catena R, Ronfani L, Sassone-Corsi P, Davidson I
**Journal:** Dev Biol (2006)
**DOI:** [10.1016/j.ydbio.2006.04.458](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ydbio.2006.04.458)

## Abstract

1. Dev Biol. 2006 Aug 1;296(1):231-238. doi: 10.1016/j.ydbio.2006.04.458.

Changes in intranuclear chromatin architecture induce bipolar nuclear 
localization of histone variant H1T2 in male haploid spermatids.

Catena R(1), Ronfani L, Sassone-Corsi P, Davidson I.

Author information:
(1)Institut de Génétique et de Biologie Molécularie et Cellulaire, 
CNRS/INSERM/ULP, 1 Rue Laurent Fries, 67404 Illkirch Cedex, France.

Spermiogenesis entails a major biochemical and morphological restructuring of 
the germ cell packing the DNA into the condensed spermatid nucleus. H1T2 is a 
histone H1 variant selectively and transiently expressed in male haploid germ 
cells during spermiogenesis that specifically localizes to a chromatin domain at 
the apical pole under the acrosome. We explored the mechanisms determining polar 
localization of H1T2 in spermatids. In acrosome-deficient round spermatids of 
hrb -/- and gopc -/- mice, H1T2 localization is not altered, indicating that 
proper acrosome development is not required for specifying nuclear polarity. In 
contrast, in late round spermatids from trf2 -/- or hmgb2 -/- mice, a bipolar 
H1T2 localization was observed revealing that polarity is modified by loss of 
proteins specifying chromatin architecture. Our results show that intranuclear 
chromatin organization is critical for correct polar localization of H1T2 and 
that H1T2 can be a useful molecular marker revealing chromatin disorganization 
in spermatids.
