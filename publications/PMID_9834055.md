# BASH, a novel signaling molecule preferentially expressed in B cells of the bursa of Fabricius.
**Authors:** Goitsuka R, Fujimura Y, Mamada H, Umeda A, Morimura T, Uetsuka K, Doi K, Tsuji S, Kitamura D
**Journal:** J Immunol (1998)

## Abstract

1. J Immunol. 1998 Dec 1;161(11):5804-8.

BASH, a novel signaling molecule preferentially expressed in B cells of the
bursa of Fabricius.

Goitsuka R(1), Fujimura Y, Mamada H, Umeda A, Morimura T, Uetsuka K, Doi K,
Tsuji S, Kitamura D.

Author information:
(1)Inheritance and Variation Group, PREST, JST, Kyoto, Japan.
ryogoi@rs.noda.sut.ac.jp

The bursa of Fabricius is a gut-associated lymphoid organ that is essential for
the generation of a diversified B cell repertoire in the chicken. We describe
here a novel gene preferentially expressed in bursal B cells. The gene encodes
an 85-kDa protein, designated BASH (B cell adaptor containing SH2 domain), that
contains N-terminal acidic domains with SH2 domain-binding phosphotyrosine-based
motifs, a proline-rich domain, and a C-terminal SH2 domain. BASH shows a
substantial sequence similarity to SLP-76, an adaptor protein functioning in
TCR-signal transduction. BASH becomes tyrosine-phosphorylated with the B cell Ag
receptor (BCR) cross-link or by coexpression with Syk and Lyn and associates
with signaling molecules including Syk and a putative chicken Shc homologue.
Overexpression of BASH results in suppression of the NF-AT activation induced by
BCR-cross-linking. These findings suggest that BASH is involved in BCR-mediated
signal transduction and could play a critical role in B cell development in the
bursa.
