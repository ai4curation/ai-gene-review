Acid-sensitive ion channels (ASIC) are proton-gated ion channels expressed in
neurons of the mammalian central and peripheral nervous systems. The functional
role of these channels is still uncertain, but they have been proposed to
constitute mechanoreceptors and/or nociceptors. We have raised specific
antibodies for ASIC1, ASIC2, ASIC3, and ASIC4 to examine the distribution of
these proteins in neurons from dorsal root ganglia (DRG) and to determine their
subcellular localization. Western blot analysis demonstrates that all four ASIC
proteins are expressed in DRG and sciatic nerve. Immunohistochemical experiments
and functional measurements of unitary currents from the ASICs with the
patch-clamp technique indicate that ASIC1 localizes to the plasma membrane of
small-, medium-, and large-diameter cells, whereas ASIC2 and ASIC3 are
preferentially in medium to large cells. Neurons coexpressing ASIC2 and ASIC3
form predominantly heteromeric ASIC2-3 channels. Two spliced forms, ASIC2a and
ASIC2b, colocalize in the same population of DRG neurons. Within cells, the
ASICs are present mainly on the plasma membrane of the soma and cellular
processes. Functional studies indicate that the pH sensitivity for inactivation
of ASIC1 is much higher than the one for activation; hence, increases in proton
concentration will inactivate the channel. These functional properties and
localization in DRG have profound implications for the putative functional roles
of ASICs in the nervous system.
