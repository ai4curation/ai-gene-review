# C-mip interacts with the p85 subunit of PI3 kinase and exerts a dual effect on ERK signaling via the recruitment of Dip1 and DAP kinase.
**Authors:** Kamal M, Pawlak A, BenMohamed F, Valanciuté A, Dahan K, Candelier M, Lang P, Guellaën G, Sahali D
**Journal:** FEBS Lett (2010)
**DOI:** [10.1016/j.febslet.2009.12.015](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.febslet.2009.12.015)

## Abstract

1. FEBS Lett. 2010 Feb 5;584(3):500-6. doi: 10.1016/j.febslet.2009.12.015. Epub
2009 Dec 16.

C-mip interacts with the p85 subunit of PI3 kinase and exerts a dual effect on
ERK signaling via the recruitment of Dip1 and DAP kinase.

Kamal M(1), Pawlak A, BenMohamed F, Valanciuté A, Dahan K, Candelier M, Lang P,
Guellaën G, Sahali D.

Author information:
(1)INSERM, U 955, Equipe 21, Créteil F-94010, France.

In naive T cells, Lck exerts a negative control on the ERK/MAPK pathway. We show
that c-mip (c-maf inducing protein) interacts with the p85 subunit of PI3 kinase
and inactivates Lck, which results in Erk1/2 and p38 MAPK activation. This
effect is not enough to activate AP1 given the inability of ERK to migrate into
the nucleus and to transactivate its target genes. We demonstrate that c-mip
interacts with Dip1 and upregulates DAPK, which blocks the nuclear translocation
of ERK1/2. This dual effect of c-mip is unique and might represent a potential
mechanism to prevent the development of an immune response.
