# Sensorineural hearing loss and the incidence of Cx26 mutations in Austria.
**Authors:** Löffler J, Nekahm D, Hirst-Stadlmann A, Günther B, Menzel HJ, Utermann G, Janecke AR
**Journal:** Eur J Hum Genet (2001)
**DOI:** [10.1038/sj.ejhg.5200607](https://doi.org/10.1038/sj.ejhg.5200607)

## Abstract

1. Eur J Hum Genet. 2001 Mar;9(3):226-30. doi: 10.1038/sj.ejhg.5200607.

Sensorineural hearing loss and the incidence of Cx26 mutations in Austria.

Löffler J(1), Nekahm D, Hirst-Stadlmann A, Günther B, Menzel HJ, Utermann G,
Janecke AR.

Author information:
(1)Institute of Medical Biology and Human Genetics, University of Innsbruck,
Innsbruck, Austria.

A clinical evaluation and Cx26 mutation analysis was performed in 92 consecutive
patients with sensorineural hearing loss in order to delineate the spectrum of
genetically caused hearing loss. Among patients of Austrian origin, 53% were
classified with hereditary hearing loss. Cx26 mutations were found in 26% of
NSHL patients (40% of familial vs 18% of sporadic cases). The mutation 35delG
accounted for 52.8% of all presumed GJB2 disease alleles. The second most
frequent mutation was L90P (16.7%) having been reported with a prevalence of
0.7-3.5% in other populations. Three novel mutations were found. The novel
mutation, R143Q, was associated with dominant high-frequency hearing loss.
Pseudodominant transmission of NSHL was seen in four families with Cx26
mutations. A mutation 35delG carrier rate of 0.9% was observed among 672
controls from West-Austria. Cx26 mutations were found associated with mild to
profound, and with asymmetric hearing impairment.
