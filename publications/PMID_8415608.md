# Phosphorylation-dependent binding of a signal molecule to the flagellar switch of bacteria.
**Authors:** Welch M, Oosawa K, Aizawa S, Eisenbach M

## Abstract

1. Proc Natl Acad Sci U S A. 1993 Oct 1;90(19):8787-91. doi:
10.1073/pnas.90.19.8787.

Phosphorylation-dependent binding of a signal molecule to the flagellar switch
of bacteria.

Welch M(1), Oosawa K, Aizawa S, Eisenbach M.

Author information:
(1)Department of Membrane Research and Biophysics, Weizmann Institute of
Science, Rehovot, Israel.

Regulation of the direction of flagellar rotation is central to the mechanism of
bacterial chemotaxis. The transitions between counterclockwise and clockwise
rotation are controlled by a "switch complex" composed of three proteins (FliG,
FliM, and FliN) and located at the base of the flagellar motor. The mechanism of
function of the switch is unknown. Here we demonstrate that the diffusible
clockwise-signal molecule, the CheY protein, binds to the switch, that the
primary docking site is FliM, that the extent of CheY binding to FliM is
dependent upon the phosphorylation level of CheY, and that it is unaffected by
the other two switch proteins. This study provides a biochemical demonstration
of binding of a signal molecule to the bacterial switch and demonstrates
directly that phosphorylation regulates the activity of this molecule.
