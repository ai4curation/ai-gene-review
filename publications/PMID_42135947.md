Atg18, Atg21 and Hsv2 are homologous proteins that fulfill
macroautophagic/autophagic and non-autophagic functions. We now found that Atg21
interacts with Pep8/Vps26, Vps29 and Vps35, the components of the cargo
selective complex of the retromer. We identified Atg21 residues required for
retromer binding and focused on two of them. The first, T106, is part of an
STS-motif, which also mediates Atg18-binding to the retromer, while in Hsv2 this
motif is not conserved. As a second retromer binding residue, we identified D28
of Atg21. Interestingly, the corresponding D45 of Hsv2 also confers retromer
binding, but the analogous E34 of Atg18 does not. Together, Atg18 uses binding
residue 1, while Atg21 uses 1 and 2 and Hsv2 only 2. During autophagy, Atg21
organizes the Atg8-lipidation machinery by interacting with Atg16 via the bottom
side of its β-propeller. Partial overlap between the Atg16 binding residues and
the retromer binding residues indicates mutually exclusive interaction. Indeed,
lack of Atg16 enhances Atg21 binding to the retromer. The Atg21-retromer shows
vacuole fission activity, which requires both retromer binding residues and the
membrane-bending activity of its loop 6 C/D. Additionally, overexpression of
Atg21 led to mislocalization of the Prc1/carboxypeptidase Y cargo receptor
Pep1/Vps10 from the Golgi to Vps17-positive endosomes and to Prc1 secretion. We
detected a cross-talk among the different retromer complexes. In the absence of
the canonical retromer component Vps5, more Atg21-retromer complexes were
formed. Furthermore, the vacuole hyper-fragmentation of vps17Δ cells
cooperatively required Atg18 and Atg21. Along this line, we found that Atg21
interacts with Atg18 and Hsv2.Abbreviation: Atg: autophagy related, CSC: cargo
specific complex (of the retromer), PAS: phagophore assembly site,
Prc1/CPY/carboxypeptidase Y: proteinase C, PROPPIN: beta-propeller that binds
phosphoinositides.

Introduction

Macroautophagy (hereafter autophagy), is highly conserved in all eukaryotes. It removes superfluous or damaged intracellular components, such as protein aggregates or parts of organelles, by sorting them to the lysosome (vacuole in S. cerevisiae), where they are degraded and recycled. Through this, it has a vital role in cell homeostasis and adaptation. Disruptions of the process can lead to various health conditions, including cancer and neurodegenerative diseases [1]. Thus, understanding the autophagic process has garnered considerable interest.

In S. cerevisiae, autophagy begins with the formation of a double-layered membrane, known as the phagophore. This is stimulated by starvation which leads to the activation of Atg1 and results in the formation of a complex with Atg13, Atg17, Atg29 and Atg31 at the phagophore assembly site (PAS) [2,3]. Then Atg9-vesicles associate with the phagophore [4]. Afterward, the class III phosphatidylinositol 3-kinase/PtdIns3K complex I is recruited to generate phosphatidylinositol-3-phosphate (PtdIns3P) [5]. This phospholipid is sensed by a group of proteins called PROPPINs, short for β-propellers that bind polyphosphoinositides [6–8].

PROPPINs are WD40 repeat-containing proteins that fold into a 7-bladed β-propeller. They have two binding sites for PtdIns3P at their circumference and can also bind PtdIns(3,5)P2. Furthermore, an amphipathic α-helix within their loop 6C/D can penetrate into the membrane and induce membrane bending [9].

In yeast, the PROPPIN family consists of three homologous proteins: Atg18, Atg21, and Hsv2. Atg18 is a core autophagic protein. It binds via Atg2 to the Atg2-Atg9 complex and stabilize it at the phagophore by binding to PtdIns3P [10–12]. This complex plays a crucial role in the elongation of the phagophore and is responsible for the influx of lipids from the endoplasmic reticulum. Atg2 mediates this non-vesicular lipid flux to the phagophore, while Atg9 acts as a scramblase and is responsible for the distribution of lipids between the two membrane leaflets [3,13,14].

Atg21 organizes the Atg8 lipidation complex at the vacuolar contact site of the phagophore and binds the Atg12–Atg5-Atg16 complex [15,16]. The coupling of Atg8 to phosphatidylethanolamine (PE) is crucial for autophagy [17]. Atg8–PE is part of a coat-like structure around the phagophore, and in
selective autophagy binds cargo receptors on the inside of the phagophore [18,19].

The function of Hsv2 in autophagy is elusive. Recently, it has been shown to interact with Atg2 similar to Atg18 [20].

PtdIns3P is further distributed along endosomes and PtdIns(3,5)P2 at the vacuole [21]. In line, PROPPINs are also found at the vacuolar membrane and at endosomes. Atg18 has a non-autophagic function in vacuole fission, especially in response to hyperosmotic stress. For this role Atg18 forms a complex with Pep8/Vps26, Vps29 and Vps35 [22,23]. Pep8/Vps26, Vps29 and Vps35 constitute the cargo specific complex (CSC) of the retromer and form arch-like structures. The canonical retromer complex, which is involved in retrograde transport from the endosome to the Golgi, contains this CSC, and the sorting nexins Vps5 and Vps17 [24,25]. The sorting nexins facilitate the membrane binding of the retromer complex. They have a PX domain, which binds PtdIns3P, and a BAR domain, which confers membrane binding. In the newly found Atg18-retromer complex, Atg18 binds the CSC competitively to the sorting nexins Vps5 and Vps17. Like the sorting nexins, Atg18 can bind membranes via PtdIns3P and can confer membrane bending via the partial insertion of its loop 6C/D. Atg18 is further known to negatively regulate the PtdIns3P 5-kinase Fab1 [26].

In mammals, there are four PROPPINs termed WIPI (WD repeat domain, phosphoinositide interacting): WIPI1, WIPI2, and WDR45 (WD repeat domain 45): WDR45B/WIPI3, and WDR45/WIPI4. WDR45 and WDR45B interact with ATG2A and ATG2B, like Atg18 and Hsv2, while WIPI2, the functional counterpart of Atg21, binds to ATG16L1 and is required for the lipidation of the Atg8-ortholog MAP1LC3/LC3 [27]. Like Atg21, WIPI2 is also localized to endosomes [28], where it interacts with RAB11, a RAB-GTPase known for its role in the maturation of autophagosomes and endosomes [29,30].

We here demonstrate the existence of an Atg21-retromer complex and identify residues at the bottom side of the Atg21 β-propeller, which are involved in binding the retromer CSC. We focus on two of these binding residues and found that the first is shared among Atg18 and Atg21. Hsv2 on the other hand uses the second residue similar to Atg21 for binding the CSC of the retromer. We further describe the role of the Atg21-retromer during vacuole fragmentation and analyze its effect on Prc1 secretion when overexpressed. Interestingly, the membrane bending activity of the Atg21 loop 6C/D is not only required for its role in vacuole fragmentation and Prc1 secretion upon overexpression, but also for the cytoplasm-to-vacuole targeting (Cvt) pathway.

ResultsAtg21 forms a novel retromer complex with the retromer components Pep8/Vps26, Vps29 and Vps35An Atg18-retromer complex has already been identified and characterized [22,23]. This prompted us to evaluate a putative interaction of the Atg18 homolog Atg21 with the retromer CSC consisting of Pep8/Vps26, Vps29 and Vps35. Co-immunoprecipitation experiments with anti-GFP magnetic beads using crude extracts from S. cerevisiae cells expressing GFP-Atg21 and Vps35-Ha showed the interaction of Atg21 with Vps35 (Figure 1(A,B,F)). An Atg21 [FTTG] mutant, which is impaired in phosphoinositide binding, exhibited a significantly reduced binding compared to wild-type Atg21 (Figure 1(B)). This suggests that the interaction of Atg21 and Vps35 is favored by membrane association of Atg21.

Atg18 binds the retromer CSC competitively to the SNX-BAR sorting nexins Vps5 and Vps17 [22,23]. In line, co-immunoprecipitation of GFP-Atg21 in vps5Δ cells showed an approximately three-fold increased interaction with Vps35-Ha compared to wild-type cells. In contrast, deletion of either VPS17, VPS26 or VPS29 did not significantly affect the interaction of Atg21 with Vps35 (Figure 1(C)). We further confirmed the existence of a novel Atg21-retromer complex by co-immunoprecipitation of GFP-Atg21 with Vps26-Ha (Figure 1(D)) and Vps29-Ha (Figure 1(E)). Interestingly, in vps35Δ cells no interaction of GFP-Atg21 with Vps29-Ha was detectable, while the interaction with Vps26-Ha was significantly reduced to ~25% (Figure 1(D,E)). This suggests that Atg21 preferentially interacts via Vps35 within the assembled CSC, but that some direct interaction with Vps26 May also occur.

Atg21 organizes the Atg8-lipidation complex by interacting with Atg16 [15,31], we were thus further interested, if the presence of Atg16 affects the interaction of Atg21 with Vps35. To this end, we made co-immunoprecipitations of GFP-Atg21 expressed from its endogenous promotor in wild-type and atg16∆ cells. The ~70% increased interaction in atg16∆ cells suggests competition between the binding of Atg21 to either Atg16 or Vps35 (Figure 1(F)).

The interaction network between Atg18, Atg21 and Hsv2Each PROPPIN-retromer complex contains two PROPPIN molecules (Figure 1(A)) [22]. To check for putative protein-protein interactions among the PROPPINs, we performed co-immunoprecipitations using mCherry and GFP-tagged versions. For Atg21, we detected an interaction with both Atg18 and Hsv2, while Atg18 showed no interaction with Hsv2 (Figure 4(A-C)). Interestingly, mCherry-Atg21 showed interaction with GFP-Atg21, while we could not detect such a self-interaction for Atg18 (Figure 4(D,E)). For Hsv2, self-interaction has already been published [20]; also the interaction between Atg18 and Atg21 was already shown [33]. We further analyzed in co-immunoprecipitations the interaction of Atg21 with Vps35 in atg18∆ hsv2∆ (Figure 4(F,G)) and in atg18∆ atg21∆ cells (Figure 4(H,I)) without detecting significant alterations compared to wild-type cells. Additionally, we analyzed in co-immunoprecipitations the interaction of Atg21 with the other PROPPINs in the absence of Vps35. In vps35∆ cells the interaction of Atg21 with Atg18 was slightly, but significantly increased, the self-interaction of Atg21 was somewhat decreased and that of Atg21 with Hsv2 was unaffected (Figure 4(J-M)).

DiscussionWe here uncovered a novel Atg21-retromer complex (Figure 1). This raises the question why all three yeast PROPPINs form retromer complexes by replacing the sorting nexins Vps5 and Vps17. A closer look at the assembly of the Vps5-Vps17-retromer complex offers initial insights. Formation of the pentameric retromer complex consisting of Vps5, Vps17 and the CSC subcomplex containing Vps26, Vps29 and Vps35 primarily depends on the amino-terminal domain of Vps5 [24,41,42]. In this domain, three additively acting motifs were identified: (i) a LF-motif, which helps in the interaction with Vps35, (ii) a [K/R]F-motif and (iii) a DPL-motif, in which PL is required. On the other hand, the Vps17 amino-terminus does not interact with retromer. This shows a clear functional difference between Vps5 and Vps17, in fact in vps17∆ cells, a retromer containing two Vps5 molecules is formed, and sorting of the retromer cargo Ear1 is normal, while Vps10 is missorted [43]. In contrast in vps5∆ cells sorting of both Ear1 and Vps10 is disturbed. Vps5 and Vps17 thus have different functions in both retromer assembly and cargo selection.In analogy, the PROPPINs differ in their effects on retromer assembly and their functions. The interaction of Atg21 with Vps35 significantly increased in vps5∆ cells, but the absence of Vps17 had no influence (Figure 1). This indicates that Atg21 only competes with Vps5, but not with Vps17. In contrast, the binding of Atg18 to Vps35 was clearly competitive to both Vps5 and Vps17 [22,23], highlighting different binding modes of Atg18 and Atg21 to the retromer CSC. Furthermore, Hsv2 binding to Vps35 was unaffected in the absence of Vps5 and only slightly enhanced in vps5∆ atg18∆ cells, suggesting some competition with Atg18 binding [32]. The Vps35 interaction of Atg18 and Atg21 was also differently
affected by the absence of CSC components. Atg18 interacted with Vps35 only in the presence of Vps29, suggesting that the interaction occurs with the assembled dimeric Vps35 arch. However, the interaction of Atg21 with Vps35 was intact even in the absence of Vps26 or Vps29 (Figure 1). This suggests that the binding of Atg21 to Vps35 can occur without formation of the dimeric Vps35 complex. As discussed below, this might be due to different retromer binding modes of Atg18 and Atg21. An alternative explanation comes from the ability of Atg21 to self-interact, which we did not observe for Atg18 (Figure 4). Atg21 dimerization, especially at membranes could also enhance the avidity to Vps35. Supporting such an alternative view an Atg21 [FTTG] mutant impaired in phosphoinositide binding only showed ~20% of normal Vps35 binding (Figure 1), while Atg18 [FTTG] showed 60% residual binding [23], emphasizing the importance of membrane binding for retromer assembly.Atg18, Atg21 and Hsv2 interact with the retromer CSC, but additionally Atg18 and Hsv2 interact with Atg2 and Atg21 with Atg16. To better understand these different complexes, we identified the amino acid residues the PROPPINs use to form retromer complexes. Previously, only T56 at the bottom side of the Atg18 β-propeller has been shown to be required for interaction with Vps35 [22]. Atg18 T56 is part of an STS-motif, which is conserved in Atg21 (Figure 9). Indeed, we found that Vps35 binding is significantly affected in the corresponding Atg21T106E mutant and even stronger in Atg21S105E,T106E,S107E (Figure 2). This points to the importance of the whole STS-motif in retromer binding for Atg18 and Atg21. We thus termed the residue T106 of Atg21 retromer binding residue 1 (BR1). In Hsv2, the STS-motif corresponds to RTN (residues 76 to 78) and is not involved in retromer, but in Atg2 binding, since a Hsv2T77E mutant normally bound Vps35, whereas Hsv2N78K was unable to interact with Atg2 [32].

We thus expect that overexpression of Atg21D28R, which still contains binding residue 1 interfere with the balance of the PROPPIN and canonical retromer complexes.A joint function of PROPPINs is the ability to bend membranes by partial membrane insertion of their amphipathic helix in loop 6C/D [34,35]. In contrast, the Atg21F426K,I430D mutant showed that the bending activity is required for the Cvt-pathway, the selective autophagy of prApe1 especially under non-starvation conditions (Figure 7). Atg18 furthermore interacts with Atg2 bound to Atg9 and an amphipathic α-helix at the carboxy-terminus of Atg2 stabilizes recruitment [10,11]. Since WIPI1 supports the autophagic role of WIPI2 [51], these two WIPIs are considered Atg21 orthologs in mammalian cells. On the other side, both WDR45B/WIPI3, and WDR45/WIPI4 interact with ATG2A and thus confer membrane influx for autophagosome elongation [20,49,52]. They must thus be considered functional homologs of Atg18 and Hsv2. Besides their autophagic role non-autophagic functions were ascribed to the WIPIs. Especially for WIPI1 a role in protein transport from early endosomes to the Golgi and from early/recycling endosomes to the lysosome has been demonstrated [53]. Here the formation of tubules dependent on the membrane bending activity of its loop 6C/D was observed. Furthermore PtdIns3P-binding by WIPI1 is proposed to cause membrane tubulation, while PtdIns(3,5)P2-binding mediates fission. Interestingly, these retromer-like functions are defective in a WIPI1S69E mutant [22]. WIPI1 S69 corresponds to Atg18 T56, which is the retromer binding residue 1, suggesting the conservation of this binding mode among the PROPPINs. Indeed, the STS-motif characteristic for BR 1 is well conserved as an SSS-motif in WIPI1 and WIPI2 (Figure 9(C)). In contrast BR 1 is not conserved in WDR45B/WIPI3 and WDR45/WIPI4. Instead, they share a conserved N, which mediates interaction with Atg2 in Hsv2 N78 and with ATG2A in HsWDR45/WIPI4 N61 [20]. As Hsv2 T77 is not required for retromer binding [32], we expect that this is also the case for the corresponding HsWDR45/WIPI4 S60. In sum, the retromer binding residue 1 is shared among Atg18 and Atg21 in yeast and WIPI1 and WIP2 in higher organisms. We were curious about the conservation of our newly discovered retromer binding residue 2 found within DPL (residues 45 to 47) of Hsv2 and in DPF (residues 28 to 30) of Atg21. In WIPI1 and WIPI2 BR 2 appears not to be conserved (Figure 9(C)). However, in WDR45B/WIPI3 and WDR45/WIPI4 corresponding motifs are present for example DPL (residues 39 to 41) in HsWDR45B/WIPI3 and EPL (residues 35 to 37) in HsWDR45/WIPI4. Since the EPF-motif in Atg18 was not involved in retromer interaction, it should be considered that retromer binding by WDR45B/WIPI3 and WDR45/WIPI4 might also use different strategies. Together, also the non-autophagic functions seem to be quite well conserved among the PROPPIN family. We thus believe our study on the yeast PROPPINs will also help to better understand the roles of the mammalian WIPIs in autophagy, protein transport and diseases.

We here uncovered a novel Atg21-retromer complex (Figure 1). This raises the question why all three yeast PROPPINs form retromer complexes by replacing the sorting nexins Vps5 and Vps17. A closer look at the assembly of the Vps5-Vps17-retromer complex offers initial insights. Formation of the pentameric retromer complex consisting of Vps5, Vps17 and the CSC subcomplex containing Vps26, Vps29 and Vps35 primarily depends on the amino-terminal domain of Vps5 [24,41,42]. In this domain, three additively acting motifs were identified: (i) a LF-motif, which helps in the interaction with Vps35, (ii) a [K/R]F-motif and (iii) a DPL-motif, in which PL is required. On the other hand, the Vps17 amino-terminus does not interact with retromer. This shows a clear functional difference between Vps5 and Vps17, in fact in vps17∆ cells, a retromer containing two Vps5 molecules is formed, and sorting of the retromer cargo Ear1 is normal, while Vps10 is missorted [43]. In contrast in vps5∆ cells sorting of both Ear1 and Vps10 is disturbed. Vps5 and Vps17 thus have different functions in both retromer assembly and cargo selection.

In analogy, the PROPPINs differ in their effects on retromer assembly and their functions. The interaction of Atg21 with Vps35 significantly increased in vps5∆ cells, but the absence of Vps17 had no influence (Figure 1). This indicates that Atg21 only competes with Vps5, but not with Vps17. In contrast, the binding of Atg18 to Vps35 was clearly competitive to both Vps5 and Vps17 [22,23], highlighting different binding modes of Atg18 and Atg21 to the retromer CSC. Furthermore, Hsv2 binding to Vps35 was unaffected in the absence of Vps5 and only slightly enhanced in vps5∆ atg18∆ cells, suggesting some competition with Atg18 binding [32]. The Vps35 interaction of Atg18 and Atg21 was also differently
affected by the absence of CSC components. Atg18 interacted with Vps35 only in the presence of Vps29, suggesting that the interaction occurs with the assembled dimeric Vps35 arch. However, the interaction of Atg21 with Vps35 was intact even in the absence of Vps26 or Vps29 (Figure 1). This suggests that the binding of Atg21 to Vps35 can occur without formation of the dimeric Vps35 complex. As discussed below, this might be due to different retromer binding modes of Atg18 and Atg21. An alternative explanation comes from the ability of Atg21 to self-interact, which we did not observe for Atg18 (Figure 4). Atg21 dimerization, especially at membranes could also enhance the avidity to Vps35. Supporting such an alternative view an Atg21 [FTTG] mutant impaired in phosphoinositide binding only showed ~20% of normal Vps35 binding (Figure 1), while Atg18 [FTTG] showed 60% residual binding [23], emphasizing the importance of membrane binding for retromer assembly.

Atg18, Atg21 and Hsv2 interact with the retromer CSC, but additionally Atg18 and Hsv2 interact with Atg2 and Atg21 with Atg16. To better understand these different complexes, we identified the amino acid residues the PROPPINs use to form retromer complexes. Previously, only T56 at the bottom side of the Atg18 β-propeller has been shown to be required for interaction with Vps35 [22]. Atg18 T56 is part of an STS-motif, which is conserved in Atg21 (Figure 9). Indeed, we found that Vps35 binding is significantly affected in the corresponding Atg21T106E mutant and even stronger in Atg21S105E,T106E,S107E (Figure 2). This points to the importance of the whole STS-motif in retromer binding for Atg18 and Atg21. We thus termed the residue T106 of Atg21 retromer binding residue 1 (BR1). In Hsv2, the STS-motif corresponds to RTN (residues 76 to 78) and is not involved in retromer, but in Atg2 binding, since a Hsv2T77E mutant normally bound Vps35, whereas Hsv2N78K was unable to interact with Atg2 [32].

Our in-depth analyses of Atg21 mutants identified D28 as another retromer binding residue, which we termed binding residue 2 (BR2) (Figure 3). Atg21 D28 is part of a NCDPFG motif, which corresponds to Atg18-NCEPFG and to Hsv2-NTDPLT (Figure 3). This allowed us to check the relevance of this residue 2 for retromer interaction of Atg18 and Hsv2. Interestingly, Atg18E34R interacted normally with Vps35, while Hsv2D45R did not bind Vps35 (Figure 3). Surprisingly, the exchange of D in Atg21 with E in Atg18 renders the site ineffective to interact with the CSC, probably the accessibility of this site is affected by other domains of Atg18. In sum, retromer binding in Atg18 is mediated by BR1, in Atg21 by BR1 and BR2 and in Hsv2 by BR2 (Figure 9B). The presence of both retromer binding residues in Atg21 might indicate a stronger interaction, but it must also be considered that unidentified interacting residues exist in Atg18 or Hsv2. BR2 is within a DPF-motif in Atg21 and a DPL-motif in Hsv2, this is reminiscent of the DPL-motif discussed above, which mediates retromer binding of Vps5. This might indicate a common binding mode shared between the PROPPINs and Vps5.

We also tested previously described Atg21 mutants affected in Atg16 binding [15] for their ability to bind Vps35 (Figure 2). This demonstrated a partial overlap of the Atg16 and Vps35 binding residues, indicating a mutually exclusive binding. Indeed, in the absence of Atg16 the interaction of Atg21 with Vps35 was enhanced (Figure 1(F)). Atg21 thus either forms a complex with Atg16 or retromer.

PROPPIN-retromer complexes contain two PROPPIN molecules [22]. The detected interactions between Atg18, Atg21 and Hsv2 and the ability of Atg21 and Hsv2 to self-interact (Figure 4), combined with their different retromer binding modes, open the possibility to form multiple retromer complexes. Most likely, mixed retromer complexes containing two different PROPPINs exist as well. We expect that these complexes have different functions and also differ in their cargo selectivity during protein sorting.

To learn more about the functional cross-talk among the different PROPPIN-retromer complexes, we focused on their role during vacuole fission and their effects on CPY sorting. Atg21T106E and Atg21D28R mutants were impaired in vacuole fragmentation, confirming the functional requirement of both binding residues for Atg21-retromer (Figure 5). To eliminate putative effects of other PROPPIN-retromer complexes, we additionally expressed these Atg21 variants in atg18Δ atg21Δ hsv2Δ triple knock-out cells. This ensured that only Atg21-retromer complexes are formed. Under these conditions, the presence of binding residue 2 was sufficient for normal vacuole fission (Figure 5). However, a major problem of using triple knock-out cells is that Atg18 alone has a very strong impact on vacuole fission. This makes it difficult to exactly measure the small residual vacuole fragmentation rate in an atg18Δ background. Thus, triple knock-out cells are not well suited to study the individual effects of Atg21 and Hsv2 retromer complexes. We therefore used an alternate approach by dissecting vacuole fragmentation in vps5Δ and in vps17Δ
cells. The absence of these sorting nexins causes vacuole hyper-fragmentation, most likely by shifting the equilibrium toward enhanced formation of PROPPIN-retromer complexes [22,23]. This severe fragmentation makes it easier to measure putative additive effects of the different PROPPIN retromer complexes. Interestingly, in vps5Δ cells, we found a cooperative role for Atg18 together with Hsv2 [32], while Atg18 together with Atg21 were required in vps17Δ cells
(Figure 6). This defines pairs of functionally cooperating PROPPINs. Interestingly, the Atg18 Hsv2 pair contains BR1 and BR2, respectively, while the Atg18 Atg21 pair has BR1 and 2, so in both pairs each of the two binding residues are present. The requirement of Atg18 for vacuole fragmentation in both vps5Δ and vps17Δ cells fits nicely with the dominant role of Atg18 in vacuole fission. Together, this shows a functional cross-talk and network between the PROPPIN-retromer complexes.

This complex interrelationship is also illustrated by our analysis of CPY-secretion induced by strong overexpression of ATG21. This should shift the equilibrium to Atg21-retromer complexes and reduce the levels of other retromer complexes. We found that this leads to a shift of the CPY-receptor Vps10 from a Sec7-positive Golgi pool to Vps17-positive endosomes (Figure 8(A,B)). This is in line with a defect in retrograde transport of Vps10 from endosomes to the Golgi. As expected, overexpression of Atg21T106E reduced the CPY secretion compared to wild-type Atg21. The lack of CPY secretion upon overexpression of the loop 6C/D mutant Atg21F426K,I430D further underscores the importance of the membrane bending activity of Atg21 for this phenotype. Unexpectedly, overexpression of Atg21D28R increased the level of CPY secretion compared to wild-type Atg21. A possible explanation comes from our observation that the deletion of either ATG18 or HSV2 increased CPY secretion, while their simultaneous deletion prevented CPY secretion almost completely (Figure 8(E,F)). We thus expect that overexpression of Atg21D28R, which still contains binding residue 1 interfere with the balance of the PROPPIN and canonical retromer complexes.

A joint function of PROPPINs is the ability to bend membranes by partial membrane insertion of their amphipathic helix in loop 6C/D [34,35]. Despite their localization at the highly curved edge of phagophores for Atg18 and Hsv2 this bending activity is dispensable during starvation-induced autophagy, but needed for their retromer functions [32,34]. In contrast, the Atg21F426K,I430D mutant showed that the bending activity is required for the Cvt-pathway, the selective autophagy of prApe1 especially under non-starvation conditions (Figure 7). Under prolonged starvation the requirement of the bending activity decreased (Figure 7), well in line with the decreasing requirement of Atg21 during starvation. So why does the Cvt-function of Atg21 depend on membrane bending by its loop 6C/D? Two studies showed that the phagophore binding of Atg18 not only depends on its interaction with PtdIns3P. Atg18 furthermore interacts with Atg2 bound to Atg9 and an amphipathic α-helix at the carboxy-terminus of Atg2 stabilizes recruitment [10,11]. Since Hsv2 also binds to Atg2 [20,49], this provides an easy explanation why the membrane binding of loop 6C/D of Atg18 and Hsv2 is dispensable for autophagy. Atg21 as part of the Atg8-lipidation complex on the other hand depends on its amphipathic α-helix within loop 6C/D.
