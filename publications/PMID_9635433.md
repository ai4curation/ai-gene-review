# Geminin, an inhibitor of DNA replication, is degraded during mitosis.
**Authors:** McGarry TJ, Kirschner MW
**Journal:** Cell (1998)
**DOI:** [10.1016/s0092-8674(00)81209-x](https://doi.org/10.1016/s0092-8674(00)81209-x)

## Abstract

1. Cell. 1998 Jun 12;93(6):1043-53. doi: 10.1016/s0092-8674(00)81209-x.

Geminin, an inhibitor of DNA replication, is degraded during mitosis.

McGarry TJ(1), Kirschner MW.

Author information:
(1)Department of Cell Biology, Harvard Medical School, Boston, Massachusetts
02115, USA.

We describe a novel 25 kDa protein, geminin, which inhibits DNA replication and
is degraded during the mitotic phase of the cell cycle. Geminin has a
destruction box sequence and is ubiquitinated anaphase-promoting complex (APC)
in vitro. In synchronized HeLa cells, geminin is absent during G1 phase,
accumulates during S, G2, and M phases, and disappears at the time of the
metaphase-anaphase transition. Geminin inhibits DNA replication by preventing
the incorporation of MCM complex into prereplication complex (pre-RC). We
propose that geminin inhibits DNA replication during S, G2, and M phases and
that geminin destruction at the metaphase-anaphase transition permits
replication in the succeeding cell cycle.
