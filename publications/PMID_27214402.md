# NSUN3 methylase initiates 5-formylcytidine biogenesis in human mitochondrial tRNA(Met).
**Authors:** Nakano S, Suzuki T, Kawarada L, Iwata H, Asano K, Suzuki T
**Journal:** Nat Chem Biol (2016)
**DOI:** [10.1038/nchembio.2099](https://doi.org/10.1038/nchembio.2099)

## Abstract

1. Nat Chem Biol. 2016 Jul;12(7):546-51. doi: 10.1038/nchembio.2099. Epub 2016
May  23.

NSUN3 methylase initiates 5-formylcytidine biogenesis in human mitochondrial
tRNA(Met).

Nakano S(1), Suzuki T(1), Kawarada L(1), Iwata H(1), Asano K(1), Suzuki T(1).

Author information:
(1)Department of Chemistry and Biotechnology, Graduate School of Engineering,
University of Tokyo, Tokyo, Japan.

In human mitochondria, the AUA codon encodes methionine via a mitochondrial
transfer RNA for methionine (mt-tRNA(Met)) that contains 5-formylcytidine
(f(5)C) at the first position of the anticodon (position 34). f(5)C34 is
required for deciphering the AUA codon during protein synthesis. Until now, the
biogenesis and physiological role of f(5)C34 were unknown. We demonstrate that
biogenesis of f(5)C34 is initiated by S-adenosylmethionine (AdoMet)-dependent
methylation catalyzed by NSUN3, a putative methyltransferase in mitochondria.
NSUN3-knockout cells showed strong reduction in mitochondrial protein synthesis
and reduced oxygen consumption, leading to deficient mitochondrial activity. We
reconstituted formation of 5-methylcytidine (m(5)C) at position 34 (m(5)C34) on
mt-tRNA(Met) with recombinant NSUN3 in the presence of AdoMet, demonstrating
that NSUN3-mediated m(5)C34 formation initiates f(5)C34 biogenesis. We also
found two disease-associated point mutations in mt-tRNA(Met) that impaired
m(5)C34 formation by NSUN3, indicating that a lack of f(5)C34 has pathological
consequences.
