## Full Text

Introduction

Early life ethanol exposure is known to program hypothalamic proopiomelanocortin (POMC) neurons to express a reduced level of POMC and its control of stress axis functions throughout the life span. In this study, we tested whether miRNAs contribute to the ethanol-induced suppression of Pomc gene expression during the developmental period.

Methods

In in vivo studies, POMC-EGFP male mice were fed with 2.5 g/kg ethanol using milk formula (AF), pair-fed isocaloric milk formula, or left in the litter during postnatal days (PNDs) 2–6. In in vitro studies, mHypoA-POMC/GFP cells were treated with ethanol (50 m M ) for a 24-h period. Hypothalamic tissues or cell extracts were used for measurement of miRNAs and POMC mRNA.

Results

Determination of genome-wide microRNA expression profile identified 40 miRNAs significantly altered in hypothalamic tissues of AF mice. In silico analysis further identified miRNA-383, -384, and -488 have putative binding sites at the POMC 3′UTR. However, only miR-383 and miR-384 are identified to be responsive to ethanol. Administration of miR-383 or -384 inhibitor oligos suppressed ethanol-stimulated miR-383 or -384 expression and restored Pomc mRNA and protein expression in AF mice. mHypoA-POMC/GFP cells when treated with ethanol showed elevated levels of miR-383 and miR-384 and reduced level of Pomc mRNA. Treatment with miR-383 or -384 mimic oligos reduced the level of Pomc mRNA, while treatment with miR-383 or -384 inhibitor oligos increased the level of Pomc mRNA. Reporter assay further confirms the binding specificity of miR-383 and miR-384 to Pomc 3’UTR.

Conclusion

These data suggest that miR-383 and miR-384 suppress Pomc gene expression and may contribute to the ethanol-induced alteration of the stress axis functions.

Fetal alcohol syndrome (FASD) often results from alcohol exposure during mother’s pregnancy, causing craniofacial abnormalities, cognitive and behavioral deficits, retarded growth, and neuroendocrine and other physiological abnormalities [1–4] . Global prevalence of FASD among children and youth is about 8 of 1,000 in the general population, and 1 in every 13 pregnant women who consumed alcohol during pregnancy have a child with FASD [5] . Many of these FASD offspring often show an increased response to stress, particularly elevated circulating levels of corticosterone and heightened anxiety-like behaviors [6–8] . Studies employing animal model of FASD have connected the hyper-stress response with a reduced function of proopiomelanocortin (POMC) neurons in the hypothalamus in prenatal alcohol exposed (PAE) offspring [7, 9–13] . It has been reported that PAE offspring show persistently lower numbers of neurons immunolabeled with β-endorphin, a POMC-derived peptide, and reduced levels of Pomc gene in the hypothalamus [9, 12, 14] . Also, in PAE offspring, supplementation of POMC neurons via neuronal transplants has been shown to ameliorate stress hyper-response and increased anxiety-like behaviors (9). In addition to stress control, POMC-derived neuropeptides play regulatory roles in various cellular processes such as energy homeostasis, immune functions, and brain reward mechanisms [15–17] . Disruption of the POMC neuronal system causes hyperphagia, obesity, glucose dysregulation, immune abnormalities, and alcohol drinking behaviors [18–22] .

Alcohol exposure during the developmental period has pronounced effect on the genome and epigenome in offspring, leading to phenotype changes. We previously reported an increase in Pomc promoter DNA methylation and recruitment of Mecp2 onto the Pomc promoter in PAE rat offspring without showing any sex differences [11, 12] . Our recent reports also showed elevated Pomc promoter DNA methylation in both male and female PAE children [8] . Additionally, we have presented evidence that suppression of DNA methylation using a pharmacological blocker normalized Pomc gene expression and stress hyper-response in PAE rat offspring [11] . Noncoding RNAs have emerged as another class of key epigenetic modifiers regulating gene expression. MicroRNAs are small noncoding RNAs which are 21–22 nucleotides in length that bind to the 3’ untranslated region of their target mRNAs to repress their expression. Studies have reported that ethanol exposure during fetal development altered the expression of different miRNAs in neural stem cells and neural progenitor cells [23] . Wang and colleagues also showed ethanol exposure during fetal development altered the expression of various miRNAs and induced fetal teratogenesis [24] . Chronic intermittent ethanol exposure on mouse cerebral cortical neurons during the fetal developmental stage also increased expression of several miRNAs [25] . MicroRNA dysregulation is also associated with disease etiology of FASD [26] . In addition, several studies also reported that alterations of miRNA expression, induced by stress, affect the homeostatic functions of the hypothalamic pituitary adrenal (HPA) axis [27–29] . However, the role of miRNAs in PAE-induced alteration in Pomc gene expression remains to be elucidated. In this study, we determined whether ethanol exposure increases miR-383 and miR-384 expression to reduce Pomc gene expression in the hypothalamus. We further evaluated if these miRNAs regulate Pomc gene expression by targeting their binding sites at the 3′UTR of Pomc .

Discussion

In this study, we used a mouse model of alcohol feeding during PND-2–6, equivalent to the third trimester of human pregnancy. This animal model has been used to study PAE effects on various physiological paradigms including the stress system and its signaling mechanisms [13, 21] . Prenatal or postnatal alcohol exposures alter metabolic defects caused by cell signaling deficiency in POMC neurons in the hypothalamus. POMC neurons are critical regulators of stress response, energy balance, and glucose homeostasis [9, 40, 45, 46] . The current study investigated the role of miRNAs in the PAE-induced alteration of Pomc gene expression in the hypothalamus. Our genome-wide microRNA expression profiling revealed that PAE altered expression of several hypothalamic miRNAs, which are predicted to have a role in various disease processes. We also showed that miRNA-383 and miRNA-384, but not miRNA-488, that are predicted by a target scan webtool to bind on POMC 3′ UTR, were increased while expression of Pomc was decreased in the hypothalamus of PAE mice. We did not find any effect of pair-feeding the isocaloric liquid diet (PF) on Pomc gene or on miRNA-383, miRNA-384 expression in the offspring, suggesting that the observed effects are due to alcohol and not due to liquid-diet feeding paradigm. The PAE effects on miRNA-383 and miRNA-384 and Pomc were observed not only on PND-6 but also on PND-60 when animals were not exposed to alcohol, suggesting that alcohol effects on these gene expression profiles are long-lasting.

We also observed that alcohol enhances miRNA-383 and miRNA-384 expression with a concomitant reduction in Pomc gene expression in mHypoA GFP POMC neurons in cultures. These neuronal cells are immortalized cells and represent the fluorescence-activated, cell-sorted POMC cells of mouse hypothalamus [47, 48] . The POMC gene expression response of these cells to a 50 m m dose (∼2.5 g/dL, a binge-like alcohol dose) is very similar to those found in rat and mouse primary hypothalamic cells [36, 37] . The responses of POMC mRNA and miRNA-383 and miRNA-384 to ethanol of these cells are also in agreement with those observed in vivo in this study, suggesting that mHypoA GFP POMC cell culture model is useful for cellular studies in vitro.

The data presented here show that miRNA-383 and miRNA-384 were increased while Pomc mRNA expression was decreased following ethanol treatments both in vivo and in vitro. This raises the possibility that miRNA-383 and miRNA-384 may participate in alcohol inhibitory action on Pomc gene . Evidence supporting this view is demonstrated in the data showing that anti-miRNA-383 and miRNA-384 increased the expression level of Pomc in the hypothalamus in vivo and also in mHypoA POMC neurons in cultures. We also showed that overexpression of these miRNAs by transfecting the Hypo-A GFP POMC cells with mimic oligos reduced the expression of Pomc . We further showed that anti-miRNA-383 and anti-miRNA-384 prevented ethanol’s suppressive effect on Pomc expression. In addition, we presented evidence that these two-miRNA mimic oligos were able to reduce the reporter activity of a plasmid in which Pomc 3′UTR was cloned downstream of a reporter gene, suggesting that these miRNAs target binding sites on Pomc 3′UTR to reduce Pomc expression.

The reduction of Pomc expression in the hypothalamus of PAE offspring is believed to be partly responsible for the elevated HPA axis response to stress in PAE rat offspring [9, 12, 14, 32, 33, 40] . Accumulating evidence has shown that several miRNAs are altered with acute or chronic restraint stress in rodents [27, 49] . Several miRNAs are also reported to affect the response of the HPA axis. It has been shown that miRNA-34C participates in suppression of stress-induced anxiety [28] . Also, miRNA-212 has been shown to regulate corticotropin-releasing hormone expression by binding at the Crh 3′UTR [29] . A study showed that miRNAs miRNA-383, miRNA-384, and miRNA-488 were upregulated in the hypothalamus of mice that were deficient in leptin or non-functional leptin receptor and exhibited reduced Pomc expression. They further showed that leptin modulates the expression of these miRNAs that target POMC via JAK2-STAT3 and PI3K-Akt pathways [48] . Another study demonstrated that miRNA-375 mediates the signaling pathway of CRF action on POMC expression by targeting MAP3K8 pathway in the pituitary gland [50] . However, no studies have determined the role of miRNAs in PAE-induced reduction in POMC that are believed to be involved in the increased stress response. Data obtained from the present study identify a mediatory role of miRNA-383 and miRNA-384 in the PAE suppressive action on Pomc gene expression. It is suggested that the mechanism by which miRNAs regulate the gene expression is canonical or non-canonical binding [51, 52] . It is generally assumed that seed base pairing at position 2-7 or 8 nucleotide of miRNA is the recognition sequence and binding occurs within the 3′UTR to inhibit the transcription. Here in this study, we showed that these miRNAs repress the POMC expression by binding to its target sites on POMC 3′UTR.

In conclusion, the current study suggests that PAE-induced increase in miRNA-383 and miRNA-384 may regulate Pomc gene expression by targeting its binding sites on the Pomc 3′UTR. The study also provides some insights into our understanding of epigenetic regulation of hypothalamic POMC in PAE mouse offspring. However, further studies are needed to characterize in detail the role of various miRNAs in PAE altered stress axis functions.
