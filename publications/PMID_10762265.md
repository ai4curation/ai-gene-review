# DnaA, the initiator of Escherichia coli chromosomal replication, is located at the cell membrane.
**Authors:** Newman G, Crooke E
**Journal:** J Bacteriol (2000)

## Abstract

1. J Bacteriol. 2000 May;182(9):2604-10. doi: 10.1128/JB.182.9.2604-2610.2000.

DnaA, the initiator of Escherichia coli chromosomal replication, is located at
the cell membrane.

Newman G(1), Crooke E.

Author information:
(1)Department of Biochemistry, Georgetown University Medical Center, Washington,
D.C. 20007, USA.

Given the lack of a nucleus in prokaryotic cells, the significance of spatial
organization in bacterial chromosome replication is only beginning to be fully
appreciated. DnaA protein, the initiator of chromosomal replication in
Escherichia coli, is purified as a soluble protein, and in vitro it efficiently
initiates replication of minichromosomes in membrane-free DNA synthesis
reactions. However, its conversion from a replicatively inactive to an active
form in vitro occurs through its association with acidic phospholipids in a
lipid bilayer. To determine whether the in situ residence of DnaA protein is
cytoplasmic, membrane associated, or both, we examined the cellular location of
DnaA using immunogold cryothin-section electron microscopy and
immunofluorescence. Both of these methods revealed that DnaA is localized at the
cell membrane, further suggesting that initiation of chromosomal replication in
E. coli is a membrane-affiliated event.
