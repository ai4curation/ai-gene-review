# Nuclear import of yeast Gcn4p requires karyopherins Srp1p and Kap95p.
**Authors:** Pries R, Bömeke K, Draht O, Künzler M, Braus GH
**Journal:** Mol Genet Genomics (2004)
**DOI:** [10.1007/s00438-003-0955-7](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00438-003-0955-7)

## Abstract

1. Mol Genet Genomics. 2004 Apr;271(3):257-66. doi: 10.1007/s00438-003-0955-7.
Epub  2003 Nov 27.

Nuclear import of yeast Gcn4p requires karyopherins Srp1p and Kap95p.

Pries R(1), Bömeke K, Draht O, Künzler M, Braus GH.

Author information:
(1)Institute of Microbiology and Genetics, Georg-August-University,
Grisebachstrasse 8, 37077 Göttingen, Germany.

The yeast transcription factor Gcn4p contains two stretches of amino acid
residues, NLS1 and NLS2, which are independently able to relocate the
cytoplasmic protein chorismate mutase into the nucleus. Only NLS2 is conserved
among fungi. A truncated version of CPCA (the counterpart of Gcn4p in
Aspergillus nidulans), which lacks the conserved NLS, accumulates in the
cytoplasm instead of the nucleus. Nuclear uptake mediated by the NLS1 of Gcn4p
is impaired by defects in genes for several different karyopherins, whereas
NLS2-dependent nuclear import specifically requires the alpha-importin Srp1p and
the beta-importin Kap95p. Yeast strains that are defective in either of these
two karyopherins are unable to respond to amino acid starvation. We have thus
identified Gcn4p as a substrate for the Srp1p/Kap95p transport complex. Our data
suggest that NLS2 is the essential and specific nuclear transport signal; NLS1
may play only an unspecific or accessory role.
