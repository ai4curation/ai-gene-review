The glycine decarboxylase complex (GDC) plays a central role in
photorespiration. GDC is localized in the mitochondria and together with serine
hydroxymethyltransferase it converts two molecules of glycine to one molecule of
serine, CO2 and NH3. Overexpression of GDC subunits in the C3 species
Arabidopsis thaliana can increase the metabolic flux through the
photorespiratory pathway leading to enhanced photosynthetic efficiency and
consequently to an enhanced biomass production of the transgenic plants.
Changing the spatial expression patterns of GDC subunits was an important step
during the evolution of C3-C4 intermediate and likely also C4 plants.
Restriction of the GDC activity to the bundle sheath cells led to the
establishment of a photorespiratory CO2 pump.
