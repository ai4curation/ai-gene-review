# PH-domain-driven targeting of collybistin but not Cdc42 activation is required for synaptic gephyrin clustering.
**Authors:** Reddy-Alla S, Schmitt B, Birkenfeld J, Eulenburg V, Dutertre S, Böhringer C, Götz M, Betz H, Papadopoulos T
**Journal:** Eur J Neurosci (2010)
**DOI:** [10.1111/j.1460-9568.2010.07149.x](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1460-9568.2010.07149.x)

## Abstract

1. Eur J Neurosci. 2010 Apr;31(7):1173-84. doi: 10.1111/j.1460-9568.2010.07149.x.
 Epub 2010 Mar 19.

PH-domain-driven targeting of collybistin but not Cdc42 activation is required
for synaptic gephyrin clustering.

Reddy-Alla S(1), Schmitt B, Birkenfeld J, Eulenburg V, Dutertre S, Böhringer C,
Götz M, Betz H, Papadopoulos T.

Author information:
(1)Department of Neurochemistry, Max-Planck Institute for Brain Research, 60528
Frankfurt/Main, Germany.

Collybistin (Cb) is a brain-specific guanine nucleotide exchange factor (GEF)
that is essential for the synaptic clustering of gephyrin and GABAA receptors in
selected regions of the mammalian central nervous system. It has been previously
proposed that Cb regulates gephyrin clustering by activating Cdc42, and thus
acts as a signal transducer in a membrane activation process which labels
postsynaptic membrane domains for inhibitory synapse formation. Here, we
dissected the functional roles of the Dbl-homology (DH) and pleckstrin homology
(PH) domains of the constitutively active splice variant Cb II by substituting
conserved amino acid residues that are required for GEF activity towards Cdc42
and phosphoinositide binding, respectively. A Cb II mutant lacking any
detectable GEF activity towards Cdc42 was still fully active in inducing
gephyrin scaffold formation, both in transfected NIH-3T3 cells and in cultured
hippocampal neurons. Furthermore, mice with a forebrain-specific inactivation of
the Cdc42 gene displayed normal densities of gephyrin and GABA(A) receptor
clusters in the hippocampus. In contrast, substitution of Cb II PH-domain
residues essential for phosphoinositide binding abolished gephyrin recruitment
to synaptic sites. Our results provide evidence that the formation of gephyrin
scaffolds at inhibitory synapses requires an intact Cb II PH-domain but is
Cdc42-independent.
