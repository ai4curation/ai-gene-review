## Abstract

1. Science. 1998 Nov 27;282(5394):1711-4. doi: 10.1126/science.282.5394.1711.

Zebrafish hox clusters and vertebrate genome evolution.

Amores A(1), Force A, Yan YL, Joly L, Amemiya C, Fritz A, Ho RK, Langeland J,
Prince V, Wang YL, Westerfield M, Ekker M, Postlethwait JH.

Author information:
(1)Institute of Neuroscience, University of Oregon, Eugene, OR 97403, USA.

HOX genes specify cell fate in the anterior-posterior axis of animal embryos.
Invertebrate chordates have one HOX cluster, but mammals have four, suggesting
that cluster duplication facilitated the evolution of vertebrate body plans.
This report shows that zebrafish have seven hox clusters. Phylogenetic analysis
and genetic mapping suggest a chromosome doubling event, probably by whole
genome duplication, after the divergence of ray-finned and lobe-finned fishes
but before the teleost radiation. Thus, teleosts, the most species-rich group of
vertebrates, appear to have more copies of these developmental regulatory genes
than do mammals, despite less complexity in the anterior-posterior axis.
