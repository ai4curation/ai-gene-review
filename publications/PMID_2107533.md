# The roles of indoleglycerol phosphate and the TrpI protein in the expression of trpBA from Pseudomonas aeruginosa.
**Authors:** Chang M, Crawford IP

## Abstract

1. Nucleic Acids Res. 1990 Feb 25;18(4):979-88. doi: 10.1093/nar/18.4.979.

The roles of indoleglycerol phosphate and the TrpI protein in the expression of
trpBA from Pseudomonas aeruginosa.

Chang M(1), Crawford IP.

Author information:
(1)Department of Microbiology, University of Iowa, Iowa City 52242.

The TrpI protein belongs to the LysR-family of procaryotic regulatory proteins.
Members of this family share a characteristic similarity of their N-terminal
amino acid sequences, and many of them are activators of divergently transcribed
genes or operons. In Pseudomonas aeruginosa, the genes for tryptophan synthase,
trpBA, are regulated by indoleglycerol phosphate (InGP) and TrpI. We demonstrate
here that in the absence of InGP, the binding site of TrpI is located in the -52
to -77 region of the trpBA promoter; in the presence of InGP, the binding region
is extended to the -32 region. In addition, two major, slow moving protein-DNA
complexes are seen in gel retardation assays: the faster moving complex is
formed in the absence of InGP and the amount of the slower moving complex is
greatly enhanced in the presence of InGP. These results suggest that the binding
of a second TrpI protein molecule, promoted by InGP, plays a crucial role in
activating the expression of the trpBA gene pair.
