# Is HAP2-GCS1 an ancestral gamete fusogen?
**Authors:** Wong JL, Johnson MA

## Abstract

2010 Jan 18.

Is HAP2-GCS1 an ancestral gamete fusogen?

Wong JL(1), Johnson MA.

Author information:
(1)Department of Molecular Biology, Cell Biology, and Biochemistry, Brown

Eukaryotes employ a diversity of strategies to ensure that gametes come
together, but the cellular climax is less varied: gamete plasma membranes must
fuse to allow the combination of parental genomes. Recent studies of HAP2-GCS1,
a sex-restricted transmembrane protein found in genomes representing all major
eukaryotic taxa except fungi, suggest that a broad array of eukaryotic organisms
could share a common mechanism for gamete fusion. Plant, protozoan, and algal
gametes carrying loss-of-function mutations in HAP2-GCS1 fail to fuse with their
complements. We propose that HAP2-GCS1 is a crucial component of an ancient
mechanism that mediates the fusion of gamete plasma membranes and could have
been a key early innovation in the evolution of sexual reproduction.
