# Mouse model of X-linked chronic granulomatous disease, an inherited defect in phagocyte superoxide production.
**Authors:** Pollock JD, Williams DA, Gifford MA, Li LL, Du X, Fisherman J, Orkin SH, Doerschuk CM, Dinauer MC
**Journal:** Nat Genet (1995)
**DOI:** [10.1038/ng0295-202](https://doi.org/10.1038/ng0295-202)

## Abstract

1. Nat Genet. 1995 Feb;9(2):202-9. doi: 10.1038/ng0295-202.

Mouse model of X-linked chronic granulomatous disease, an inherited defect in
phagocyte superoxide production.

Pollock JD(1), Williams DA, Gifford MA, Li LL, Du X, Fisherman J, Orkin SH,
Doerschuk CM, Dinauer MC.

Author information:
(1)Department of Pediatrics, James Whitcomb Riley Hospital for Children,
Indianapolis, Indiana, USA.

Chronic granulomatous disease (CGD) is a recessive disorder characterized by a
defective phagocyte respiratory burst oxidase, life-threatening pyogenic
infections and inflammatory granulomas. Gene targeting was used to generate mice
with a null allele of the gene involved in X-linked CGD, which encodes the 91 kD
subunit of the oxidase cytochrome b. Affected hemizygous male mice lacked
phagocyte superoxide production, manifested an increased susceptibility to
infection with Staphylococcus aureus and Aspergillus fumigatus and had an
altered inflammatory response in thioglycollate peritonitis. This animal model
should aid in developing new treatments for CGD and in evaluating the role of
phagocyte-derived oxidants in inflammation.
