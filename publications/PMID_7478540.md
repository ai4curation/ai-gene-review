# Colon carcinoma kinase-4 defines a new subclass of the receptor tyrosine kinase family.
**Authors:** Mossie K, Jallal B, Alves F, Sures I, Plowman GD, Ullrich A
**Journal:** Oncogene (1995)

## Abstract

1. Oncogene. 1995 Nov 16;11(10):2179-84.

Colon carcinoma kinase-4 defines a new subclass of the receptor tyrosine kinase
family.

Mossie K(1), Jallal B, Alves F, Sures I, Plowman GD, Ullrich A.

Author information:
(1)SUGEN, Inc., Redwood City, California 94063, USA.

Complementary DNA (cDNA) encoding a novel member of the receptor tyrosine kinase
(RTK) family has been isolated from colon carcinoma tissue. Colon carcinoma
kinase 4 (CCK-4) mRNA is highly expressed in human lung tissue and at lower
levels in the thyroid gland and ovary. While no mRNA was found in human adult
colon tissues, expression varied remarkably in colon carcinoma-derived cell
lines. CCK-4 cDNA encodes a chicken KLG-related, 1071 amino acid-long
transmembrane glycoprotein containing several genetic alterations within the RTK
consensus sequences. These define CCK-4 as a catalytically inactive member of
the RTK family of proteins and, in analogy to HER3, suggest a potentially
tumor-characteristic role as a signal amplifier or modulator for an as yet
unidentified kinase-competent partner.
