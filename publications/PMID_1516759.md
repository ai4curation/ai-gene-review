# Immunocytochemical localization of heat-shock protein 60-related protein in beta-cell secretory granules and its altered distribution in non-obese diabetic mice.
**Authors:** Brudzynski K, Martinez V, Gupta RS
**Journal:** Diabetologia (1992)
**DOI:** [10.1007/BF00401198](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF00401198)

## Abstract

1. Diabetologia. 1992 Apr;35(4):316-24. doi: 10.1007/BF00401198.

Immunocytochemical localization of heat-shock protein 60-related protein in
beta-cell secretory granules and its altered distribution in non-obese diabetic
mice.

Brudzynski K(1), Martinez V, Gupta RS.

Author information:
(1)Department of Medicine, University of Western Ontario, London, Canada.

Immuno-electron microscopy technique was employed to investigate the cellular
distribution of 60 kDa heat-shock protein (HSP60) in pancreatic Beta cells of
control and non-obese diabetic mice. In thin sections prepared from control
mice, antibody to mammalian HSP60 cross-reacted with protein(s) located to
mitochondria and secretory granules. In particular, prominent binding of the
antibody was seen to the insulin core of the mature insulin-secreting granules.
In comparison, very little immunoreactivity was observed with immature secretory
granules or with the Golgi apparatus. No binding to secretory granules or
mitochondria was observed with normal mouse serum or with unrelated sera. On
Western blots, HSP60 antibody specifically interacted with a single 62 kDa islet
cell protein. These results suggest the existence of an HSP60-related protein
with a novel location in mature secretory granules of Beta cells. The
preferential association of the HSP60-related protein with the insulin core was
gradually lost in Beta cells of pre-diabetic non-obese diabetic mice, and
correlated with the progression of insulitis. The decrease in the granular
binding of the HSP60 antibody was accompanied by an increase in cytoplasm
staining, and was concomitant with a significant expansion of the insulin core
diameter. The altered distribution of the HSP60-related protein in prediabetic
mice, together with our observation that immature secretory granules accumulate
in these animals indicate that the presence of HSP60-related protein in
secretory granules might be associated with the secretory function of Beta
cells.
