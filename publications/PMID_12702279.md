Glutathione (GSH: L-gamma-glutamyl-L-cysteinylglycine) is present in high
concentrations up to 10 mM in yeast cells. Its very low redox potential
(E'(o)=-240 mV for thiol disulfide exchange) gives this tripeptide the
properties of a cellular redox buffer. In Saccharomyces cerevisiae and
non-conventional yeasts (NCY), GSH may be involved in basic cellular functions
such as the maintenance of mitochondrial and membrane integrity. GSH also
assumes pivotal roles in (i) response to sulfur and nitrogen starvation; (ii)
detoxification of endogenous toxic metabolites, such as excess formaldehyde
produced during the growth of the methylotrophic yeasts Hansenula polymorpha,
Candida boidinii and Kloeckera sp.; (iii) protection against oxidative stress
provoked by exposure of the cells to reactive oxygen species including peroxides
and hydroperoxides; (iv) detoxification of xenobiotics such as halogenated
aromatics, alkylating agents and arsenite; (v) resistance to heavy-metal stress
exemplified by the responses of S. cerevisiae and Schizosaccharomyces pombe to
cadmium salts; (vi) yeast<-->mycelium transition in Candida and Aureobasidium
sp.
