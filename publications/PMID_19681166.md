# Reticulon 3 is an interacting partner of the SALM family of adhesion molecules.
**Authors:** Chang K, Seabold GK, Wang CY, Wenthold RJ
**Journal:** J Neurosci Res (2010)
**DOI:** [10.1002/jnr.22209](https://doi.org/10.1002/jnr.22209)

## Abstract

1. J Neurosci Res. 2010 Feb 1;88(2):266-74. doi: 10.1002/jnr.22209.

Reticulon 3 is an interacting partner of the SALM family of adhesion molecules.

Chang K(1), Seabold GK, Wang CY, Wenthold RJ.

Author information:
(1)Laboratory of Neurochemistry, National Institute on Deafness and Other
Communication Disorders, National Institutes of Health, Bethesda, Maryland
20892-8027, USA.

Synaptic adhesion-like molecules (SALMs) are a recently discovered family of
adhesion molecules that is widely distributed in the central nervous system and
has been implicated in neurite outgrowth and synapse formation. To identify
proteins that interact with extracellular domains of SALMs, we carried out yeast
two-hybrid screening using the extracellular domain of SALM1 as bait. A clone
encoding full-length reticulon 3A1 was isolated. This interaction was shown to
occur through the LRR domain, which is found on all SALMs. To determine whether
this relationship also occurs in brain, we performed immunoprecipitation using
antibodies to SALMs 1-4. A 19-kDa band, identified as reticulon 3C, bound to all
four SALMs, whereas a 90-kDa band, which did not comigrate with any known
reticulon 3 variant, bound to SALMs 2 and 3. These results show that reticulon 3
may play a role in the trafficking of the SALM family of adhesion molecules.
