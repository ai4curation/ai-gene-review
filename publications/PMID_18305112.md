# Tumor suppressor ARF promotes non-classic proteasome-independent polyubiquitination of COMMD1.
**Authors:** Huang Y, Wu M, Li HY
**Journal:** J Biol Chem (2008)
**DOI:** [10.1074/jbc.M708544200](https://doi.org/10.1074/jbc.M708544200)

## Abstract

1. J Biol Chem. 2008 Apr 25;283(17):11453-60. doi: 10.1074/jbc.M708544200. Epub
2008 Feb 27.

Tumor suppressor ARF promotes non-classic proteasome-independent
polyubiquitination of COMMD1.

Huang Y(1), Wu M, Li HY.

Author information:
(1)Division of Molecular and Cell Biology, School of Biological Sciences,
College of Science, Nanyang Technological University, Singapore 637551.

Although the tumor suppressor ARF is generally accepted for its essential role
in activating the p53 pathway, its p53-independent function has also been
proposed. Here, we report that ARF associates with COMMD1 and promotes
Lys(63)-mediated polyubiquitination of COMMD1 in a p53-independent manner. We
found that ARF interacts with COMMD1 in vivo. Deletion analysis of ARF suggested
that the N-terminal amino acids 15-45 are important for its interaction with
COMMD1. In addition, we found that endogenous ARF redistributes from the
nucleolus to the nucleoplasm and interacts with COMMD1 when DNA is damaged by
actinomycin D. Interestingly, we found that ARF promotes the polyubiquitination
of COMMD1 through Lys(63) of ubiquitin but not the polyubiquitination of
Lys(48), which does not target COMMD1 for proteasome-dependent proteolysis.
Moreover, ARF mutants lacking the domain interacting with COMMD1 did not promote
COMMD1 polyubiquitination, indicating that physical association is a
prerequisite condition for the polyubiquitination process. Together, these data
suggest that the ability to promote Lys(63)-mediated polyubiquitination of
COMMD1 is a novel property of ARF independent of p53.
