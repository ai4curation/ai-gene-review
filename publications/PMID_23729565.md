# In vitro maturation of Drosophila melanogaster Spätzle protein with refolded Easter reveals a novel cleavage site within the prodomain.
**Authors:** Ursel C, Fandrich U, Hoffmann A, Sieg T, Ihling C, Stubbs MT

## Abstract

Dorsoventral patterning during Drosophila melanogaster embryogenesis is mediated
by a well-defined gradient of the mature NGF-like ligand Spätzle. Easter, the
ultimate protease of a ventrally-restricted serine protease cascade, plays a key
role in the regulation of the morphogenic gradient, catalyzing the activation
cleavage of proSpätzle. As a result of alternative splicing, proSpätzle exists
in multiple isoforms, almost all of which differ only in their prodomain.
Although this domain is unstructured in isolation, it has a stabilizing
influence on the mature cystine knot domain and is involved in the binding to
the Toll receptor. Here, we report the expression and refolding of Easter, and
show that the renatured enzyme performs the activation cleavage of two Spätzle
isoforms. We determine the affinity of the prodomain for the cystine knot
domain, and show that Easter performs a previously unknown secondary cleavage in
each prodomain.
