# CASP4 binds LPS micelles
**Reactome ID:** [R-HSA-9686096](https://reactome.org/content/detail/R-HSA-9686096)
**Species:** Homo sapiens

## Summary

Caspase-4 (CASP4), an inflammatory caspase, is activated when intracellular bacterial lipopolysaccharide (LPS) binds to the N-terminal caspase activation and recruitment domain (CARD) of CASP4 (Shi J et al., 2014; An J et al., 2019, 2022; Wang K et al., 2020). CASP4 recognizes both hexa- and tetra-acylated lipid A moieties of LPS (Lagrange et al., 2018). LPS can enter the host cell cytosol through several mechanisms, including endocytosis of LPS-containing outer membrane vesicles (OMVs), which are naturally secreted by Gram-negative bacteria (Wacker MA et al., 2017; Bitto NJ et al., 2018; reviewed by Barker JH & Weiss JP 2019; Page MJ et al., 2022). Additionally, LPS may be released from phagocytosed bacteria following the rupture of phagolysosomal compartments and the fragmentation of bacterial membranes within the host cytosol. Due to its amphiphilic nature, LPS can self-assemble into micellar structures, with hydrophobic lipid A moieties clustered internally. These intracellular LPS micelles are recognized by CASP4 (and CASP5), which may disaggregate LPS micelles, breaking them into smaller structures (Wacker MA et al., 2017; An J et al., 2019).<p>Upon LPS micelles binding, CASP4 oligomerizes and undergoes auto-proteolytic cleavage at specific sites, resulting in full proteolytic activation (Wang K et al., 2020; Chan AH et al., 2023). Activated CASP4 subsequently cleaves gasdermin D (GSDMD), which is also a substrate of CASP1, CASP5, and Casp11, a murine ortholog of human CASP4/CASP5 (Shi J et al., 2014, 2015; Kayagaki N et al., 2015; Zhao Y et al., 2018; Wang K et al., 2020). The N-terminal fragment of GSDMD oligomerizes to form pores in the cell membrane, leading to pyroptosis, a lytic, pro-inflammatory form of cell death that eliminates infected cells and prevents pathogen spread (Liu X et al., 2016; Ding J et al., 2016; Sborgi L et al., 2016; Aglietti RA et al., 2016). CASP4 (and CASP5) also efficiently processes pro-interleukin-18 (pro-IL-18) at aspartic acid residue D36 to generate its mature, biologically active form (Shi X et al., 2023; Exconde PM et al., 2023; Devant P et al., 2023; reviewed by Exconde PM, 2024). In contrast, cleavage of pro-IL-1β by CASP4 and CASP5 at D27 produces an inactive fragment that lacks receptor-stimulating activity (Exconde PM et al., 2023; reviewed by Exconde PM, 2024). An alternative CASP4-mediated cleavage at D116, the canonical pro-IL-1β activation site, has been observed but occurs with lower efficiency comparing to pro-IL-18 processing (Bibo-Verdugo B et al., 2020; Chan AH et al., 2023; Devant P et al., 2023).<p>Intracellular bacterial pathogens have evolved strategies to suppress host inflammatory responses. For example, Shigella flexneri secretes the type III secretion system (T3SS) effector OspC3, which catalyzes ADP-riboxanation of CASP4 at the active site arginine residue R314, thereby inhibiting its enzymatic activity and blocking CASP4-mediated pyroptosis (Li Z et al., 2021; Hou Y et al., 2023).
