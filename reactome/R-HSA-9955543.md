## Summary

Carbamoyl phosphate synthetase (CPS) 1 deficiency (OMIM 237300) is an autosomal recessive disorder that arises due to mutations in the CPS1 gene whose protein product catalyzes the first and rate limiting step of the urea cycle, the conversion of bicarbonate and ammmonia to carbonyl phosphate (Freeman et al, 1970; reviewed in Martinez et al, 2010; Nitzahn and Litpshutz 2000; Zhang et al, 2023). CPS1 deficiency may present neonatally or with delayed onset, and is characterized by hyperammonemia and neurological disorders.<br>Because CPS1 is autosomal recessive, and most variants are identified in patients as compound heterozygotes. This can present a challenge to the unambiguous characterization of the pathological implication of a given allele, especially as in vitro studies are more representative of the homozygous state. <br>Keeping this caveat in mind, missense, nonsense and other mutations that abrogate or impair catalytic activity or compromise the stability of the protein have been identified across all domains of the CPS1 gene. <br>The following variants are annotated as causing CPS1 deficiency, either as candidates or members depending on the levels of experimental evidence available.
