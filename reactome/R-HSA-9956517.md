# ASS1 variants don't synthesize arginosuccinate
**Reactome ID:** [R-HSA-9956517](https://reactome.org/content/detail/R-HSA-9956517)
**Species:** Homo sapiens

## Summary

Type 1 Citrullinemia (CTLN1) arises due to mutations in the ASS1 gene that encodes argininosuccinate synthase 1. This enzyme normally facilitates the ATP-dependent conversion of citrulline and aspartate to argininosuccinate, a critical step in the detoxification of nitrogen through urea formation.<br>The clinical features of CTLN1 include vomiting, lethargy, seizures, and potentially coma or death if untreated—particularly in neonates. Milder, later-onset forms may present with episodic symptoms due to partial enzyme activity (reviewed in Matsumoto et al, 2019; Schmitt-Ribas et al, 2022; Quinonez and Lee, 2022).<br>The following mutations are classified as pathogenic or likely pathogenic by ClinVar, and are listed as candidate or member alleles depending on the extent of their biochemical characterization:<br><br>Variants with decreased affinity for aspartate and compromised ASS1 activity:<br>Candidates:<br>ASS1 T284I (Engel et al, 2009; Diez-Fernandez et al, 2017)<br>ASS1 Y359D (Engel et al, 2009; Diez-Fernandez et al, 2017)<br><br>Members:<br>ASS1 T91P (Miller et al, 2014; Diez-Fernandez et al, 2016)<br>ASS1 A118T (Berning et al, 2008;
