# factor V -> factor Va intermediate + factor V activation peptide (FXa catalyst)
**Reactome ID:** [R-HSA-9769737](https://reactome.org/content/detail/R-HSA-9769737)
**Species:** Homo sapiens

## Summary

This Reactome event describes proteolysis of factor V (FV (29-2224)) catalyzed by activated factor X (FXa), which cleaves FV preferentially at Arg737 and Arg1046, producing FV intermediates during the initiation phase. The N-terminal region of the inhibitory B domain, FV (738-1046) is released, while the C-terminal acidic region of the B domain remains attached to the FV light chain, forming a FV (1047-2224) polypeptide. Thrombin (FIIa), even in trace amounts, may also contribute to the conversion of FV to FVa in the early stages of coagulation, cleaving all three sites (including Arg1573) (Brummel KE et al., 2002; Orfeo T et al., 2004; reviewed by Camire RM & Bos MHA, 2009).<p>Factor V (FV, encoded by the F5 gene) circulates in the blood as an inactive procofactor composed of the following domains: A1-A2-B-A3-C1-C2. The B domain maintains FV in its inactive state via an electrostatic interaction between its basic region (BR) and acidic region (AR) (Bos MH & Camire RM, 2012; Camire RM, 2016; Dahlbäck B et al., 2017; Ruben EA et al., 2021; Mohammed BM et al., 2024). Activation of FV involves proteolytic cleavage at Arg737, Arg1046, and Arg1573, releasing the inhibitory B domain, which separates the heavy FV (29–737) and light FV (1574–2224) chains of FV (Monkovic DD & Tracy PB, 1990; Thorelli E et al., 1997; Orfeo T et al., 2004; Toso R et al., 2004; Schuijt TJ et al., 2013; Maag A et al., 2022; reviewed by Camire RM & Bos MHA, 2009). In the early phase of coagulation, FV is proteolytically cleaved by either activated factor X (FXa) (Monkovic DD & Tracy PB, 1990; Thorelli E et al., 1997; Schuijt TJ et al., 2013; Maag A et al., 2022) or thrombin (FIIa) (Monkovic DD & Tracy PB, 1990; Brummel KE et al., 2002; Orfeo T et al., 2004; reviewed by Camire RM & Bos MHA, 2009). Meizothrombin, an intermediate in prothrombin (FII) activation, may also convert FV to FVa during this phase (Tans G et al., 1994; Bradford HN & Krishnaswamy S, 2019).<p>The B domain of FV is cleaved sequentially generating partially activated FVa intermediate forms that retain the acidic region (AR) (Steen M & Dahlbäck B 2002; Dahlbäck B, 2023). Cleavage at Arg737 and Arg1046 is catalyzed by either FXa or thrombin. This cleavage removes the N-terminal region of the B domain (FV (738-1046)), disrupting the inhibitory BR-AR interaction, exposing the AR, and enhancing the prothrombinase activity of FV intermediates. At this stage, partially cleaved FV intermediates are procoagulant-active but remain regulated by tissue factor pathway inhibitor α (TFPIα), which binds to exposed AR of the FVa intermediates with high affinity (Wood JP et al., 2013; Petrillo T et al., 2021; Mohammed BM et al., 2024). The interaction of TFPIα with FVa intermediate prevents cleavage of FV intermediate species at Arg1573 (van Doorn P et al., 2017), inhibiting FV prothrombinase cofactor activity during the initiation phase of coagulation. The final cleavage at Arg1573 (catalyzed mostly by thrombin) completely removes the B domain, fully converting FV into its activated form, FVa, which exhibits increased binding affinity to FXa (Bunce MW et al., 2013; Bos MHA & Camire RM, 2012; Mohammed BM et al., 2023, 2024). FVa binds FXa in the presence of calcium ions to form the prothrombinase complex (FVa:FXa) on the cell surface, where FVa:FXa converts factor II (FII) to thrombin (FIIa) (Mann KG et al., 1988; reviewed by Stoilova-McPhie S, 2021). Lacking the AR, FVa is no longer subject to inhibition by TFPIα, though it remains sensitive to inactivation by activated protein C (APC).
