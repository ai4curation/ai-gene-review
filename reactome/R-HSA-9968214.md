# CD5L (AIM) binds damage-associated patterns (DAMPs)
**Reactome ID:** [R-HSA-9968214](https://reactome.org/content/detail/R-HSA-9968214)
**Species:** Homo sapiens

## Summary

CD5L binds damage-associated molecular patterns (DAMPs), such as PRDX1, S100A9, and HMGB1, which are released from damaged human or mouse cells (Maehara et al. 2021). The binding of CD5L to DAMPs occurs through Ca²⁺-mediated interactions and disulfide bonds (mainly via Cys191 of CD5L) (Chen et al. 2024). CD5L forms circulating complexes with soluble IgM (Arai et al. 2013; Hiramoto et al. 2018, Chen et al. 2024, Wang et al. 2024), and dissociates from IgM in disease conditions such as acute or chronic kidney injury (Arai et al. 2016, Yasuda et al. 2024), and facilitates DAMP removal through its interactions with the scavenger receptors such as CD36 and KIM-1 (inferred from mouse homologs in Kurokawa et al. 2010; Arai et al. 2016). CD5L is also transcriptionally induced in CD11b+ cells in the brain during cerebral stroke and binds to DAMPs at the injured area (Maehara et al. 2021).
