# TF:FVII+FX -> TF:FVII:FXa + FX activation peptide (TF:F7 catalyst)
**Reactome ID:** [R-HSA-9822513](https://reactome.org/content/detail/R-HSA-9822513)
**Species:** Homo sapiens

## Summary

This Reactome event describes binding of factor X (FX) to the tissue factor (TF) : factor VII (FVII) complex, followed by cleavage and activation of FX within the ternary complex TF:VII:FX. The amino terminal part of the heavy chain of FX, the FX activation peptide (183-234), is released (this peptide has no known function). <p>The proteolytic activity of FVIIa and FVII is allosterically induced upon binding to TF, which is exposed on the membrane surfaces of TF-bearing cells at sites of vascular injury (Bach R et al., 1986; Nakagaki T et al., 1991; Butenas S et al., 1996; Banner DW et al., 1996; Higashi S et al., 1996; Kelley RF et al., 2004; Bajaj SP et al., 2006; Persson E & Olsen OH 2011; Madsen JJ et al., 2015; Sorensen AB et al., 2019; Madsen JJ & Olsen OH 2021). The phospholipid composition in the cell membrane regulates pro-coagulant activity of TF:FVII (and TF:FVIIa) (Shaw AW et al., 2007; Muller MP et al., 2022). The TF:FVII zymogen complex has low (comparing to TF:FVIIa) but measurable proteolytic activity on factor X, suggesting that this complex can initiate the TF-dependent clotting at earlier stages through a generation of a small amount of factor Xa, which in turn catalyzes the proteolytic activation of FVII (Rao LV et al. 1986, 1988; reviewed by Nemerson Y 1988). As factor VIIa accumulates, the formed TF:FVIIa complexes accelerate the process of FXa generation through a reciprocal activation of FXa and FVIIa toward each other. Upon activation, FXa can remain associated with the TF:FVII (and TF:FVIIa) complex, forming the ternary complexes TF:VII:FXa and TF:VIIa:FXa (Norledge BV et al., 2003; Venkateswarlu D et al., 2003; reviewed by Ruf W 2021). The docking models and mutagenesis studies suggest that TF interacts with the gamma-carboxyglutamic acid (Gla) domain and epidermal growth factor (EGF)-like 1 domain of FX, while serine protease (SP) domain of FVII/FVIIa interacts with the Gla, EGF-2, and SP domains of FX. These interactions allow for the alignment of the active site of FVIIa with the substrate, leading to the cleavage of FX (Zhong D et al., 2002; Norledge BV et al., 2003; Venkateswarlu D et al., 2003; reviewed in Lee CJ et al., 2010; Vadivel K & Bajaj SP 2012). Besides generation of FXa, this ternary complex (1) proteolytically activates FVIII and thereby promotes the TF-independent mode of the clotting cascade, (2) activates protease-activated receptors (PARs) 1 and 2, influencing immune responses and inflammation, (3) and regulates the formation of the quaternary TF:FVII:FXa:TFPI complex with TF pathway inhibitor (TFPI), which blocks activities of both FXa and FVIIa (reviewed by Ruf W 2021).
