# factor VIIIa associates with cell membrane
**Reactome ID:** [R-HSA-5607043](https://reactome.org/content/detail/R-HSA-5607043)
**Species:** Homo sapiens

## Summary

Cleavage of factor VIII (FVIII) at R1689 disrupts the interaction between FVIII and von Willebrand Factor (VWF), facilitating FVIIIa's dissociation and subsequent high-affinity binding to anionic phospholipid surfaces (Saenko EL et al., 1998). The membrane binding is mediated primarily by the C1 and C2 domains of FVIIIa, which engage phosphatidyl-L-serine (PS) on the membrane surface as shown by structural (Ngo JC et al., 2008; Shen BW et al., 2008; Liu Z et al., 2010; Svensson LA et al., 2013; Madsen JJ et al., 2015; Peters SC et al., 2022; reviewed by Childers KC et al., 2022) and biochemical studies (Gilbert GE et al., 2002; Hsu TC et al., 2008; Meems H et al., 2009; Lu J et al., 2011; Novakovic VA et al., 2011). Cleavage-induced conformational changes in the C2 domain of FVIII likely drive stable membrane association (reviewed by Childers KC et al., 2022). Additional mechanisms, including interactions with fibrin on activated platelets, may contribute to FVIII-membrane association (Gilbert GE et al., 2015). Membrane-bound, thrombin-activated factor VIII (FVIIIa) functions as a cofactor for factor IXa in the FIXa:FVIIIa complex, also known as a tenase (or X-ase) complex. Factors VIIIa and IXa associate with anionic phospholipid surfaces with high affinity (Gilbert GE et al., 1990, Mertens K & Bertina RM 1984; Panteleev et al 2004). Kd values ranging from 0.01 to 4.8 nM have been reported for FVIIIa binding to phospholipids (Gilbert GE et al., 1990, 1992; Spaargaren J et al., 1995; Raut S et al., 1999; Ahmad SS et al., 2000). Studies using physiologic surfaces provide evidence for coordinated binding interactions of the enzyme, cofactor and substrate to discrete surface structures. For example, the presence of both active site-modified factor IXa and factor X increased both the number and the affinity of binding sites on activated platelets for factor VIIIa (Ahmad SS et al., 2000). However classical receptors for the constituents of tenase complex have not been identified (Fay PJ 2004).
