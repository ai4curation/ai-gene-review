# CASP4 cleaves IL1B at D116
**Reactome ID:** [R-HSA-9960526](https://reactome.org/content/detail/R-HSA-9960526)
**Species:** Homo sapiens

## Summary

While caspase-1 (CASP1) is the primary enzyme responsible for efficient cleavage of pro-interleukin-1β (pro-IL-1β) at aspartic acid residue D116 to generate its mature, biologically active form, caspase-4 (CASP4) can also cleave at this site, albeit with considerably lower efficiency compared to either CASP1-mediated processing of pro–IL‑1β or CASP4-mediated cleavage of pro–IL‑18 (Bibo-Verdugo B et al., 2020; Chan AH et al., 2023; Devant P et al., 2023). Notably, CASP4 more efficiently cleaves pro–IL‑1β at residue D27, producing a truncated fragment that lacks interleukin-1 receptor (IL-1R)–activating potential, thereby blocking IL-1β–mediated inflammatory signaling (Exconde PM et al., 2023; reviewed in Exconde PM, 2024). CASP4 may also contribute to the processing and maturation of pro–IL‑1α, though this function remains less well characterized and is not depicted here (Casson CN et al., 2015; Wiggins KA et al., 2019). Following caspase activation, mature IL-1 family cytokines are released through gasdermin D (GSDMD) pores, amplifying the inflammatory response in mammals (Shi J et al., 2015; Kayagaki N et al., 2015; reviewed by Broz P et al., 2020; Liu X et al., 2021).<br>
