# SRC promotes RAC1 and CDC42 activation by phosphorylating RAC1 and CDC42 GEFs
**Reactome ID:** [R-HSA-9958892](https://reactome.org/content/detail/R-HSA-9958892)
**Species:** Homo sapiens

## Summary

<p>SRC promotes RAC1 activation primarily through tyrosine phosphorylation of RAC1 guanine nucleotide exchange factors (GEFs), which increases their activity in catalyzing formation of RAC1:GTP complexes.</p><p>SRC phosphorylates ELMO1, a component of bipartite RAC1 GEF DOCK1:ELMO1 (also known as DOCK180:ELMO1), on a conserved tyrosine residue Y724 and possibly Y720, which promotes RAC1 activation (Makino et al. 2015: human DOCK1 and ELMO1, and chicken SRC, expressed in human embryonic kidney cell line HEK293). SRC was also reported to directly phosphorylate DOCK1 (Feng et al. 2012: chicken SRC and endogenous proteins in human glioblastoma cell lines were used; Slomiany and Slomiany 2015: endogenous proteins in rat gastric mucosa cells and SRC family inhibitor were used) on tyrosine residue Y722 (Feng et al. 2012), leading to RAC1 activation (Feng et al. 2012; Slomiany and Slomiany 2015).</p><p>SRC phosphorylates RAC1 GEFs TIAM1 and VAV2, leading to RAC1 activation (Servitja et al. 2003: chicken SRC, human TIAM1 and mouse VAV2 expressed in HEK293 cell line). The involvement of both SRC and TIAM1 in RAC1 activation are supported by findings in bovine aortic endothelial cells using endogenous bovine proteins (Gonzalez et al. 2006). In human umbilical vein endothelial cells (HUVECs), VEGF treatment leads to RAC1 activation, which is dependent on SRC-mediated tyrosine phosphorylation of VAV2 (Garrett et al. 2008, Ju et al. 2017) on tyrosine residue Y172 (Ju et al. 2017). SRC-mediated phosphorylation of VAV2 on Y172 was also shown to be required in human colon adenocarcinoma cell line HT29 (Gianni et al. 2008: endogenous human proteins). SRC was also reported to phosphorylate VAV2 in mouse podocytes, activating RAC1 (Lu et al. 2008: endogenous mouse proteins). SRC-mediated phosphorylation of VAV2 and the subsequent RAC1 activation may require prior SRC-mediated activation of CDC42 by phosphorylation of the CDC42 GEF FARP2 (also known as FERM or FRG) (Kawakatsu et al. 2005: human SRC, mouse VAV2 and RAC1 of unspecified species origin expressed in mouse fibroblast L cells were used; human and mouse RAC1 share 100% sequence identity). It was previously shown that SRC phosphorylates FARP2 (also known as FERM or FRG), leading to increased CDC42 activation (Miyamoto et al. 2003: human proteins expressed in HEK293 cells were used).</p><p>SRC phosphorylates RAC1 and CDC42 GEF ARHGEF4 (also known as ASEF) on tyrosine residue 165, leading to RAC1 activation (Itoh et al. 2008: endogenous proteins in human lung adenocarcinoma cell line A431 were used, with ARHGEF4 isoform in the paper corresponding to the splicing isoform 3 in UniProt, which lacks the first 71 amino acids, so that in this isoform the phosphorylated tyrosine is at position Y94).</p><p>When it comes to SRC-mediated phosphorylation of the RAC1 GEF ARHGEF7 (also known as βPix-b), contradictory findings have been reported. SRC was found to phosphorylate ARHGEF7, leading to RAC1 activation (Shin et al. 2019). A subsequent study, however, found that SRC promotes RAC1 activation through ARHGEF7 indirectly, not by phosphorylating ARHGEF7, but by promoting its recruitment to the plasma membrane (Kawase et al. 2022).</p><p>RAC1 itself may be a direct phosphorylation target of SRC (Chang et al. 2011).</p><p>SRC may also stimulate RAC1 activity indirectly. Namely, SRC has been reported to contribute to activating phosphorylation of STAT3, leading to STAT3-medatied transcription of RAC1 GEF DOCK8 (Wang et al. 2015). SRC-mediated phosphorylation of RAC1 GDP dissociation inhibitors ARHGDIB (also known as RhoGDI2) (Wu et al. 2009) and ARHGDIA (also known as RhoGDI1 or RhoGDI) (DerMardirossian et al. 2006: phosphorylation of human ARHGDIA on Y156 by chicken SRC; Lee et al. 2015) decreases their association with RAC1/CDC42/RHOA, leading to increased RAC1/CDC42/RHOA activity (DerMardirossian et al. 2006, Wu et al. 2009, Lee et al. 2015).</p>
