# EPOR binds EPOR agonists
**Reactome ID:** [R-HSA-9709454](https://reactome.org/content/detail/R-HSA-9709454)
**Species:** Homo sapiens

## Summary

Erythropoietin (EPO) is a growth factor produced in the kidneys that stimulates the production of red blood cells. It works by promoting the division and differentiation of committed erythroid progenitors in the bone marrow. EPO binds to the erythropoietin receptor (EPOR) and triggers the JAK2/STAT5 signalling cascade which ultimately signals the activatation of nuclear target genes involved in cell division or differentiation, including the apoptosis inhibitor Bcl-x. The inhibition of apoptosis is critical in erythroid differentiation. EPO also activates other intracellular proteins involved in erythroid cell proliferation and survival.<br><br>The PEGylated functional analogue of EPO (peginesatide) and recombinant EPO forms (the epoetins) are used for the treatment of patients with anemia associated with various clinical conditions, such as chronic renal failure, antiviral drug therapy and chemotherapy (Bennet et al. 2004, Singh et al. 2006, Besarab et al. 2012, Zabaneh et al. 2015, Fishbane & Shah 2015, Zadeh 2017)
