# BCKDHA or BCKDHB loss-of-function mutants don't synthesize BCAA-CoA
**Reactome ID:** [R-HSA-9865121](https://reactome.org/content/detail/R-HSA-9865121)
**Species:** Homo sapiens

## Summary

Mutations of residues in BCKDHA that participate in subunit associations or that form part of the hydrophobic core destabilize the overall assembly of BCKDH (Aevarsson et al, 2000; reviewed in Straus et al, 2020; Biswas et al, 2019). The mutations listed below generally result in "classic" MSUD phenotypes, characterized by 0-2% of normal BCKDH activity, and are classified as "pathogenic" or "likely pathogenic" in ClinVar (reviewed in Strauss et al, 2020; Biswas et al 2019):<br><br>BCKDHA mutations:<br><br>G249S (Aevarsson et al, 2000; Wynn et al, 1998)<br><br>A285P (Aevarsson et al, 2000; Wynn et al, 1998)<br><br>G290R (Aevarsson et al, 2000; Wynn et al, 1998, Chuang et al, 1995)<br><br>T310R (Aevarsson et al, 2000; Wynn et al, 1998)<br><br>F409C (Aevarsson et al, 2000; Wynn et al, 1998, Chuang et al, 1998)<br><br>Y413C (Aevarsson et al, 2000; Wynn et al, 1998, Chuang et al, 1994)<br><br>Y438N (Fisher et al, 1991; Wynn et al, 1998; Aevarsson et al, 2000)<br><br><br>Mutations of residues in BCKDHB that participate in subunit associations destabilize the overall assembly of BCKDH (Aevarsson et al, 2000; reviewed in Straus et al, 2020; Biswas et al, 2019). These mutations include BCKDHB N176Y and H206R, both of which result in complexes with <2% of normal BCKDH enzymatic activity, characteristic of "classic" MSUD (Aevarsson et al, 2000). Based on in silico modelling from the crystal structure, mutations R170H and Q346R (Wang et al, 2012) and R183P and G278S (Edelmann et al, 2001) are also predicted to destabilize subunit association and are associated with classic MSUD. Each of the mutations in BCKDHB annotated in this pathway are categorized as "pathogenic" or "likely pathogenic" in ClinVar, with the exception of H206R which is undesignated.
