# factor XII + prekallikrein:kininogen:cell surface receptor -> prekallikrein:kininogen:cell surface receptor:factor XII
**Reactome ID:** [R-HSA-9855685](https://reactome.org/content/detail/R-HSA-9855685)
**Species:** Homo sapiens

## Summary

Coagulation factor XII (FXII, Hageman factor, encoded by the F12 gene) binds to cell surface receptors in a Zn²⁺-dependent manner (Mahdi F et al., 2002). The cell surface receptors involved in this process include complement C1q-binding protein (C1QBP, also known as globular C1q receptor or gC1qR), cytokeratin 1 (CK1, encoded by the KRT1 gene), and the urokinase plasminogen activator receptor (uPAR, encoded by the PLAUR gene). Both gC1qR (C1QBP) and uPAR (PLAUR) interact with CK1 (KRT1), forming heterodimers gC1qR:CK1 and uPAR:CK1, respectively (Pixley RA et al. 2011). Additionally, gC1qR (C1QBP) may function as a homotrimer (Ghebrehiwet B et al., 1994; Joseph K et al., 1999, 2001, 2004; Mahdi F et al., 2002, 2003; Kaira BG et al., 2020; reviewed by Pathak M et al., 2018). <p>Surface binding induces a conformational change in FXII, enabling proteolytic autoactivation and the formation of enzymatically active FXIIa (Samuel M et al., 1992; Shamanaev A, Ivanov I et al., 2022; reviewed by Shamanaev A, Litvak M, Gailani D, 2022). In the context of kallikrein-kinin system, the binding of FXII to cell surface receptors facilitates the assembly of high-molecular-weight kininogen (HK, encoded by the KNG1 gene), prekallikrein (PK, encoded by the KLKB1 gene), and FXII into higher-order ternary complexes, where FXIIa and plasma kallikrein (PKa) mutually activate each other in a Zn²⁺-dependent manner creating a positive feedback loop on the cell surface (Joseph K et al., 1999, 2001, 2004; Mahdi F et al., 2002, 2003; Kaira BG et al., 2020; reviewed by Pathak M et al., 2018). Although gC1qR and CK1 receptors are expressed on various cell types, including activated platelets (Peerschke EIB et al., 2003; Khan MM et al., 2006; reviewed by Schmaier AH, 2016), the FXII-induced activation of kallikrein-kinin system is believed to occur predominantly on endothelial cell surfaces, but the precise data showing this is lacking (Lin Y et al., 1997; Motta G et al., 1998; Joseph K et al., 2001; Mahdi F et al., 2002, 2003; Pixley RA et al., 2011; Kaira BG et al., 2020). Additionally, studies showed that FXII binding to cultured endothelial cells does not result in FXII activation for up to 2 hours. However, in the presence of HK and PK, FXII becomes secondarily activated (Merkulova AA et al. 2023).<br>
