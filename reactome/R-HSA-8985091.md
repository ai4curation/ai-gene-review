# OAS3 produces oligoadenylates
**Reactome ID:** [R-HSA-8985091](https://reactome.org/content/detail/R-HSA-8985091)
**Species:** Homo sapiens

## Summary

2′-5′-Oligoadenylate synthetase 3 (OAS3) is a template-independent nucleotidyl transferase that, upon viral infection, forms condensates on double-stranded RNA (dsRNA) near viral replication organelles, where it polymerizes ATP into 5′-triphosphorylated 2′–5′-linked oligoadenylates (2–5A) (Rebouillat D et al., 1999; Ibsen MS et al., 2014; Donovan J et al., 2015; Li Y et al., 2016; Cusic R & Burke JM, 2024; Briggs S et al., 2025). Structurally, OAS3 contains three linearly arranged OAS-like domains (DI, DII, and DIII) (reviewed by Schwartz SL & Conn GL, 2019). The N-terminal DI is catalytically inactive but binds long dsRNA (>50 bp) with high affinity, whereas DII contributes lower-affinity binding (Donovan J et al., 2015). dsRNA engagement by DI positions the RNA duplex for interaction with the catalytically active C-terminal DIII, inducing a conformational rearrangement that aligns the catalytic triad (Asp816, Asp818, Asp888) required for activity. This structural transition enables Mg²⁺-dependent synthesis of 2–5A from ATP (Ibsen MS et al., 2014; Donovan J et al., 2015).<p>The resulting 2–5A molecules bind to and activate latent RNase L by promoting its dimerization and high-order oligomerization (Tanaka N et al., 2004; Tanaka N et al., 2005; Han Y et al., 2012; Cusic R & Burke JM, 2024). Activated RNase L cleaves viral and host RNAs at U-rich motifs (UU or UA), leading to translation arrest and inhibition of viral replication. The RNA cleavage fragments generated by RNase L serve as secondary activators of RIG-I (DDX58), further amplifying innate immune signaling. Functional studies show that OAS3 is the major OAS family member responsible for RNase L activation in human cells, as OAS3-deficient cells produce minimal 2–5A, whereas OAS1- or OAS2-deficient cells retain near-normal activity (Li Y et al., 2016; Briggs S et al., 2025).
