# NuRD hydrolyzes ATP to affect nucleosome organization
**Reactome ID:** [R-HSA-9944091](https://reactome.org/content/detail/R-HSA-9944091)
**Species:** Homo sapiens

## Summary

This reaction shows a generic representation of the histone ATP hydrolysis activity of the NuRD complex on a non-specific acetylated chromatin template. The NuRD complex is recruited to specific regions in the genome by virtue of the DNA- and histone-binding properties of its components, as well as through protein-protein interactions with sequence-specific transcription factors, some of which are illustrated in this pathway (reviewed in Asmamaw et al, 2024; Alendar and Berns, 2021). NuRD binds to a di-nucleosome substrate and interacts with adjacent acetylated nucleosomes (Millard et al, 2013). ATP hydrolysis activity is provided by the CHD component of the NuRD complex, with slightly different outcomes depending on which subunit is present and in which genomic context. CHD3 and CHD4 predominantly use the energy of ATP hydrolysis to slide nucleosomes, while CHD5 disrupts nucleosomes without sliding (Lusser et al, 2005; Liu et al, 2014; Hoffmeister et al, 2017; Quan and Yusufzai, 2014; Manning and Yusufzai, 2017; reviewed in Clapier et al, 2017; Alendar and Berns, 2021). Although it has not been conclusively demonstrated, it is thought that ATP-dependent nucleosome repositioning may facilitate access to the chromatin to allow other interactions, including the interaction of the HDAC components of NuRD with their substrates. Consistent with this, the NuRD complex is only able to promote deacetylation of nucleosome in the presence of ATP (Xue et al, 1998; Tong et al, 1998; Verreault et al, 1998).
