# Dengue virion envelope phosphatidylserine binds liganded TAM-family receptor
**Reactome ID:** [R-HSA-9918496](https://reactome.org/content/detail/R-HSA-9918496)
**Species:** Homo sapiens

## Summary

Dengue virus can infect cells by utilizing TAM family receptors, such as TYRO3 and AXL. These receptors facilitate viral entry by interacting with phosphatidylserine on the virion envelope, an interaction often mediated by bridging molecules like Growth arrest-specific protein 6 (GAS6) or Protein S (PROS) (Meertens et al., 2012; Morizono & Chen, 2014). The TAM family, which also includes MERTK, is expressed on various cell types, including macrophages, and plays a role in the PtdSer-dependent clearance of apoptotic cells (Morizono & Chen, 2014; reviewed in Oliveira & Peron, 2019). On megakaryocytes, which possibly express AXL, an additional viral entry factor is the Platelet glycoprotein Ib complex (CD42b, GP1B) (Attatippaholkun et al., 2018).
