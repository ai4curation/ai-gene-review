# factor VIIIa + factor IXa -> factor VIIIa:factor IXa
**Reactome ID:** [R-HSA-158278](https://reactome.org/content/detail/R-HSA-158278)
**Species:** Homo sapiens

## Summary

Factors VIIIa (FVIIIa) and FIXa associate on membrane surfaces to form FVIIIa:FIXa, the so-called "tenase" (or X-ase) complex, which catalyzes the conversion of factor X (FX) to its activated form, FXa (reviewed by Stoilova-McPhie S 2021; Childers KC et al., 2022; Bos MHA et al., 2024). FVIIIa acts as a cofactor significantly enhancing the catalytic efficiency of FIXa on FX (Mutucumarana VP et al., 1992; Fay PJ 2004, 2006). In the absence of FVIIIa, FIXa activity is minimal due to an autoinhibitory loop in its catalytic domain (Kolkman JA & Mertens K 2000; Sichler K et al., 2003; Zögg T et al., 2009; Freato N et al., 2021). Binding with FVIIIa induces structural rearrangements in this loop through an allosteric mechanism, enabling efficient substrate interaction (Zögg T et al., 2009). The FVIIIa A2 and A3 domains of FVIIIa bind the catalytic domain of FIXa, while interactions between the FVIIIa C2 domain and the FIXa Gla domain further stabilize the complex on the surface (Fay PJ et al., 1994; O'Brien LM et al., 1995; Lenting PJ et al., 1996; Bajaj SP et al., 2001; Blostein MD et al., 2003; Jenkins PV et al., 2004; Ngo JC et al., 2008). Calcium ions and the phospholipid bilayer also contribute to this stabilization. In vitro, negatively charged phospholipids can provide an appropriate surface. In the body, the surface is provided by the plasma membranes of activated platelets (Gilbert GE and Arena AA 1996; Gilbert GE 2019).
