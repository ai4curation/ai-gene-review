# OAS3 binds viral dsRNA intermediates produced by (+)ssRNA virus
**Reactome ID:** [R-HSA-8985157](https://reactome.org/content/detail/R-HSA-8985157)
**Species:** Homo sapiens

## Summary

Oligoadenylate synthetase 3 (OAS3) is an interferon-inducible cytosolic enzyme that detects double-stranded RNA (dsRNA) replication intermediates produced by diverse RNA and DNA viruses (Donovan J et al., 2015). This reaction describes OAS3 recognition of dsRNA replication intermediates from single-stranded, positive-sense RNA viruses, including flaviviruses such as West Nile virus (WNV) (Li Y et al., 2016; Briggs S et al., 2025) and Dengue virus (DENV) (Lin RJ et al., 2009), as well as alphaviruses such as Sindbis virus (SINV) (Li Y et al., 2016). Upon viral infection, OAS3 condenses on viral dsRNA near replication organelles (Cusic R & Burke JM, 2024; Briggs S et al., 2025), utilizing its N-terminal domain I to engage long dsRNA molecules (>50 bp) with high affinity (Donovan J et al., 2015). Domain II contributes weaker dsRNA-binding capacity (Donovan J et al., 2015). This binding triggers a conformational rearrangement that activates the C-terminal catalytic domain III by aligning the catalytic triad (Asp816, Asp818, Asp888). This conformational activation enables Mg²⁺-dependent synthesis of 5′-triphosphorylated 2′–5′-linked oligoadenylates (2–5A) from ATP (Ibsen MS et al., 2014; Donovan J et al., 2015). Short dsRNA molecules that cannot bridge all three domains fail to trigger this rearrangement, ensuring length-selective sensing and preventing inappropriate OAS3 activation (Donovan J et al., 2015; reviewed by Schwartz SL & Conn GL, 2019). The resulting 2–5A molecules bind latent RNase L, inducing its dimerization and activation (Tanaka N et al., 2004; Tanaka N et al., 2005; Han Y et al., 2012). Activated RNase L cleaves viral and cellular RNAs preferentially at U-rich motifs (UU or UA), causing translation arrest and blocking viral replication. The RNA fragments produced in this process serve as secondary ligands for RIG-I (DDX58) and MDA5 (IFIH1), amplifying innate immune signaling. Functional studies demonstrate that OAS3 is the predominant human OAS enzyme responsible for RNase L activation, as OAS3-deficient human cells show minimal 2–5A production, whereas OAS1- or OAS2-deficient cells retain near-normal responses (Li Y et al., 2016; Briggs S et al., 2025). The RNA cleavage products subsequently serve as secondary ligands for RIG-I and MDA5, amplifying interferon-mediated innate immune signaling.<p>In addition, OAS3 activation has been linked to host defense against positive-sense picornaviruses, including enterovirus EV71 (Zhou X et al., 2022), negative-sense RNA orthomyxovirus such as Influenza A Virus (IAV) (Li Y et al., 2016), and large dsDNA viruses, such as vaccinia virus (Li Y et al., 2016) (not shown here). Multiple viruses, including influenza A virus through its NS1 protein, have evolved antagonistic mechanisms that sequester dsRNA to evade OAS3 detection (Min J & Krug RM, 2006; Li Y et al., 2016). For example, influenza A virus NS1 protein sequesters viral dsRNA, thereby preventing activation of OAS enzymes (Min J & Krug RM, 2006; Li Y et al., 2016).
