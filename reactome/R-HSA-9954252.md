# GGC-GBP2, GGC-GBP5 bind furin
**Reactome ID:** [R-HSA-9954252](https://reactome.org/content/detail/R-HSA-9954252)
**Species:** Homo sapiens

## Summary

Guanylate-binding proteins 2 and 5 (GBP2 and GBP5) are interferon-inducible GTPases that inhibit viral infectivity by interacting with the host proprotein convertase furin (also known as PCSK3) (Braun E et al., 2019; Cui W et al., 2021; Mesner D et al., 2023). Both GBP2 and GBP5 bind to the C-terminal cytoplasmic tail of furin, blocking its proteolytic activity required for cleavage of viral envelope (Env) glycoproteins (Braun E et al., 2019). This inhibition disrupts proper maturation and virion incorporation of viral glycoproteins, thereby reducing the infectivity of progeny virions. GBP2 and GBP5 restrict a wide range of furin-dependent viruses, including HIV-1, Zika virus, Measles virus, Influenza A virus (Braun E et al., 2019) and SARS-CoV-2 (Mesner D et al., 2023). Specifically, in HIV-1-infected primary human monocyte-derived macrophages and CD4⁺ T cells, GBP2 and GBP5 impair the proteolytic processing of the HIV-1 Env precursor gp160 into gp120 and gp41, leading to decreased infectivity of newly formed viral particles in a furin-dependent manner (Braun E et al., 2019; Cui W et al., 2021). In addition, GBP5 impairs the furin-dependent processing and activation of Syncytin-1 (ERVW-1), an endogenous cell-fusion protein of retroviral origin, that participates in trophoblast fusion into the multinucleated syncytiotrophoblast layer, which is vital for placental development and function (Krchlikova V et al., 2025). While furin inhibition is thought to be a central mechanism, GBP2 and GBP5 may also restrict viral infectivity by altering Env trafficking and N-linked glycosylation (Krapp C et al., 2016; Cui W et al., 2021; Veler H et al., 2024; reviewed by Sauter D & Kirchhoff F 2024). The antiviral activity of GBPs depends on prenylation at their C-terminal CAAX motifs, which is necessary for membrane localization and function (Britzen-Laurent N et al., 2010; Braun E et al., 2019).<br><br>
