# OAS1 oligomerizes
**Reactome ID:** [R-HSA-8983688](https://reactome.org/content/detail/R-HSA-8983688)
**Species:** Homo sapiens

## Summary

Although structural studies of human 2′–5′-oligoadenylate synthetase 1 (OAS1) demonstrate that a monomeric OAS1 is sufficient to form a catalytically competent enzyme, with dsRNA-induced conformational rearrangement positioning the catalytic residues Asp75, Asp77, and Asp148 for Mg²⁺ coordination and ATP binding (Donovan J et al., 2013), biophysical analyses indicate that OAS1 can additionally self-associate at higher protein concentrations in solution (Koul A et al., 2020). Evidence from the 9-2 isozyme, an oligoadenylate (2-5A) synthetase found in mice, suggests that such oligomerization may influence enzymatic activity (Ghosh A et al., 1997). The conserved C-terminal CFK motif (Cys331–Phe332–Lys333) is thought to contribute to OAS1 self-association (Koul A et al., 2020; Ghosh A et al., 1997). In the mouse 9-2 isozyme, alanine substitution of the corresponding CFK residues disrupted oligomer (tetramer) formation and was associated with loss of catalytic activity (Ghosh A et al., 1997). Computational modeling has further suggested that certain human OAS1 isoforms (e.g., p40 and p48) may form higher-order assemblies stabilized by two planar protein-protein interaction interfaces, with the CFK-containing helix 313–335 contributing directly to oligomer stabilization (Torshin IY, 2005). Overall, these findings indicate that, although monomeric OAS1 is structurally capable of dsRNA-induced catalysis, oligomerization via the CFK motif may represent an additional regulatory mechanism governing OAS1 function.
