INTERPRO This entry represents a family of copper amine oxidase enzymes, which includes but is not restricted to:

Primary amine oxidase, which prefers aromatic over aliphatic amines in Escherichia coli, and is active on tyramine, tryptamine, beta-phenethylamine and dopamine in Klebsiella aerogenes.
Amine oxidase [copper-containing] 3, which catalyses the oxidative deamination of primary amines to the corresponding aldehydes, with the concomitant production of hydrogen peroxide and ammonia [[cite:PUB00077231], [cite:PUB00165650], [cite:PUB00165652]]. It has a preference for the primary monoamines methylamine and benzylamine [[cite:PUB00077231], [cite:PUB00165652]], and can also act on 2-phenylethylamine, though much less efficiently [[cite:PUB00077231]]. At the endothelial cell surface, it can also function as a cell adhesion protein that participates in lymphocyte extravasation and recirculation by mediating the binding of lymphocytes to peripheral lymph node vascular endothelial cells in an L-selectin-independent fashion [[cite:PUB00165651], [cite:PUB00165652]].
Diamine oxidase [copper-containing], which catalyses the oxidative deamination of primary amines to the corresponding aldehydes, with the concomitant production of hydrogen peroxide and ammonia [[cite:PUB00165647], [cite:PUB00165648], [cite:PUB00165649], [cite:PUB00002898]]. Its preferred substrates are the diamines histamine and 1-methylhistamine, and it could therefore play a role in allergic and immune responses [[cite:PUB00165647]]. It has a broad specificity for diamines and can also act on cadaverine and putrescine, two products of amino acid catabolism [[cite:PUB00165647]]. It could also act, though less efficiently, on polyamines such as spermidine and spermine, thereby regulating various biological processes [[cite:PUB00165647], [cite:PUB00165649]]. Amine oxidases (AO) are enzymes that catalyse the oxidation of a wide range of biogenic amines, including many neurotransmitters, histamine and xenobiotic amines. There are two classes of amine oxidases: flavin-containing ([ec:1.4.3.4]) and copper-containing ([ec:1.4.3.6]). Copper-containing AOs act as disulphide-linked homodimers. They catalyse the oxidation of primary amines to aldehydes, with the subsequent release of ammonia and hydrogen peroxide. This reaction requires one copper ion per subunit and topaquinone as a cofactor [[cite:PUB00005251]]: 
RCH2NH2 + H2O + O2 = RCHO + NH3 + H2O2

Copper-containing amine oxidases are found in bacteria, fungi, plants and animals. In prokaryotes, these enzymes enable various amine substrates to be used as sources of carbon and nitrogen [[cite:PUB00010699], [cite:PUB00010700]]. In eukaryotes, they have a broader range of functions, including cell differentiation and growth, wound healing, detoxification and cell signalling [[cite:PUB00010701]].

The copper amine oxidases occur as mushroom-shaped homodimers, with each subunit (70-95 kDa) containing a copper ion and a covalently bound redox cofactor, topaquinone (TPQ). TPQ is formed by post-translational modification of a conserved tyrosine residue. The copper ion is coordinated by three histidine residues and two water molecules in a distorted square pyramidal geometry and has a dual function in catalysis and TPQ biogenesis.
