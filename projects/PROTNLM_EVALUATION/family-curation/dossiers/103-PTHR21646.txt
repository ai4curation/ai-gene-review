INTERPRO The ubiquitin carboxyl-terminal hydrolase family, also known as deubiquitinating enzymes (DUBs), plays a crucial role in various cellular processes by cleaving ubiquitin from ubiquitinated proteins, thus regulating protein turnover and preventing degradation [[cite:PUB00163319], [cite:PUB00163320], [cite:PUB00163321]].. Members of this family are involved in cell cycle regulation, DNA repair, transcriptional regulation, autophagy, and signal transduction [[cite:PUB00163321], [cite:PUB00163322]]. They exhibit specificity for different ubiquitin linkages and substrates, including histones, signalling receptors, and structural proteins, thereby influencing pathways such as NF-kappa-B activation, TGF-beta receptor signaling, and the spindle assembly checkpoint. Their activities contribute to the maintenance of protein homeostasis, genomic stability, and cellular response to stress and damage.
