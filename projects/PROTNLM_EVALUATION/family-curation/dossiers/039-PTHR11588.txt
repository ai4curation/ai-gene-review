INTERPRO Microtubules are polymers of tubulin, a dimer of two 55kDa subunits, designated alpha and beta [[cite:PUB00000039], [cite:PUB00000978]]. Within the microtubule lattice, α-β heterodimers associate in a head-to-tail fashion, giving rise to microtubule polarity. Fluorescent labelling studies have suggested that tubulin is oriented in microtubules with beta-tubulin toward the plus end [[cite:PUB00006112]].

For maximal rate and extent of polymerisation into microtubules, tubulin requires GTP. Two molecules of GTP are bound at different sites, termed N and E. At the E (Exchangeable) site, GTP is hydrolysed during incorporation into the microtubule. Close to the E site is an invariant region rich in glycine residues, which is found in both chains and is thought to control access of the nucleotide to its binding site [[cite:PUB00002443]].

Most species, excepting simple eukaryotes, express a variety of closely-related alpha- and beta-isotypes. A third family member, gamma tubulin, has also been identified in a number of species. Gamma tubulin is found at microtubule-organising centres, such as the spindle poles or the centrosome, 
suggesting that it is involved in minus-end nucleation of microtubule assembly [[cite:PUB00000721]]. Further eukaryotic tubulins (gamma, epsilon, zeta) that are restricted to certain lineages or species have been reported [[cite:PUB00072959], [cite:PUB00099927]].

Bacterial and archaeal homologues of tubulin have been discovered. BtubA and BtubB, two bacterial homologues in the genus Prosthecobacter, have probably been derived by horizontal gene transfer [[cite:PUB00072957], [cite:PUB00039756]].
