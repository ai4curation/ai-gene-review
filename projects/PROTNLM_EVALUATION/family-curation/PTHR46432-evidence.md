This family of proteins serves as substrate-recognition components within SCF-type E3 ubiquitin ligase complexes. They are involved in the targeted ubiquitination and subsequent proteasomal degradation of specific proteins, notably the tumor suppressor p53. By recognizing and binding to p53, they facilitate its ubiquitination, which is a post-translational modification leading to the protein's degradation. This regulatory mechanism is crucial for controlling the cell cycle and preventing the accumulation of damaged DNA, thereby playing a significant role in maintaining cellular homeostasis and preventing tumorigenesis.
