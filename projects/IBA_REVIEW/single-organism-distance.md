# Single-Organism IBA Phylogenetic Distance Analysis

## Overview

A recurrent question in IBA review is how to evaluate `IBA` annotations whose `WITH/FROM` column cites experimental descendants from **only a single organism** (excluding self-citations where the target gene's species is also a donor).

As established in [`IBA_REVIEW.md`](../IBA_REVIEW.md), a single-organism donor list is **not** intrinsically weak when the donor and target are close orthologs and the PAINT curator placed the ancestral `IBD` node at an appropriate clade (for example, mouse $\to$ human within `Euarchontoglires` or `Euteleostomi`). However, when a single organism's experimental annotation is used to seed an ancestral `IBD` deep in the tree—such as at `Opisthokonta`, `Eukaryota`, or `LUCA`—and then propagated down across kingdoms, domains, or multiple gene-duplication boundaries, the risk of over-propagation rises substantially.

This sub-project defines five quantitative distance and donor-topology metrics to measure "how far down (and up) the tree" a single-organism or single-clade IBA travels, benchmarks them against existing review actions across all 29,427 positive GOA `IBA` annotations in the repository, and uses the resulting high-distance filter to identify candidate genes for targeted re-review.

## Action Classification: Propagation Failure vs Term Granularity Refinement

To avoid conflating true homology over-propagation with routine ontology-granularity adjustments, all tables below separate non-retained review actions into two distinct categories:

- **Propagation Failure (`Fail` = `REMOVE` + `MARK_AS_OVER_ANNOTATED`)**: The reviewer judged that the annotated function or process does not hold as a core or valid biological attribute of the target gene (with **`% REMOVE`** reported separately for outright false claims).
- **Term Granularity Refinement (`Refn` = `MODIFY` + `TOO_GENERAL`)**: The essence of the annotation is true of the target gene, but a more specific or better-scoped GO term is preferred.
- **Total Downgraded (`% Down` = `Fail` + `Refn`)**: The combined non-retained rate (`REMOVE` + `MARK_AS_OVER_ANNOTATED` + `MODIFY` + `TOO_GENERAL`), vs **Retained (`Keep` = `ACCEPT` + `KEEP_AS_NON_CORE`)**.

## Five Quantitative Distance & Donor-Topology Metrics

Implemented in [`single_organism_distance.py`](single_organism_distance.py):

### 1. Taxonomic LCA Clade Depth ($d_{\text{LCA}}$) & Branch Steps ($s_{\text{up}} + s_{\text{down}}$)

For each donor–target pair $(O_{\text{donor}}, O_{\text{target}})$, we find their Lowest Common Ancestor (LCA) along the UniProt/NCBI species `OC` phylogeny:
- **$d_{\text{LCA}}$**: Clade depth of $\text{LCA}(O_{\text{donor}}, O_{\text{target}})$ from `cellular_organisms` ($d_{\text{LCA}} = 0$ at `cellular_organisms`/`LUCA`, $1$ at `Eukaryota`/`Bacteria`, $2$ at `Metazoa`/`Fungi`/`Viridiplantae`, $3\text{–}4$ at `Bilateria`/`Protostomia`/`Deuterostomia`/`Ascomycota`, $6+$ at `Euteleostomi`/`Tetrapoda`/`Euarchontoglires`).
- **$s_{\text{up}} + s_{\text{down}}$**: Total taxonomic branch steps up from $O_{\text{donor}}$ to $\text{LCA}$ plus down from $\text{LCA}$ to $O_{\text{target}}$.

Across the 3,981 rated non-self single-organism IBA rows with resolved donor lineages (plus 7 rows in `Tier 0` with unresolved donor lineage), **Propagation Failure (`% Fail`)** and **`% REMOVE`** climb substantially as the donor–target LCA moves from close clades to cross-kingdom and cross-domain (`LUCA`) splits:

|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|

Key observations from Table 1:
- **Clade-to-Tier mapping (`classify_evolutionary_span`)**: `single_organism_distance.py` maps each donor–target LCA clade to Tiers 0–7: `Unknown` $\to$ Tier 0; `Cellular organisms (LUCA)` $\to$ Tier 7; `Eukaryota` $\to$ Tier 6 when either species is in `Viridiplantae`/Algae (`Arabidopsis`, `Oryza`, `Triticum`, `Chlamydomonas`), else Tier 5 (`Amorphea` or `Opisthokonta`); `Bacteria`/`Archaea` $\to$ Tier 5 vs intra-phylum bacterial clades (`Bacillati`, `Gammaproteobacteria`, `Enterobacteriaceae`, `Actinomycetes`) $\to$ Tier 3; `Metazoa` $\to$ Tier 4; phylum/subphylum clades (`Euteleostomi`, `Dikarya`, `Ascomycota`, `Viridiplantae`, `Embryophyta`, `Ecdysozoa`, `Arthropoda`) $\to$ Tier 3; order/subphylum fungal and plant clades (`Saccharomycotina`, `Pezizomycotina`, `Magnoliopsida`, `Pentapetalae`, `rosids`) $\to$ Tier 2; and close mammalian/model genera and superorders (`Eutheria`, `Boreoeutheria`, `Euarchontoglires`, `Primates`, `Hominidae`, `Glires`, `Rodentia`, `Murinae`, `Caenorhabditis`, `Aspergillus`, etc.) $\to$ Tier 1, with an explicit fallback (`Tier 2: Other ({lca})`) routing any unenumerated LCA clade to Tier 2 (see §Limitations item 4).
- Separating **Propagation Failure (`% Fail`)** from **Term Refinement (`% Refn`)** confirms that the distance effect is driven by genuine over-propagation (`REMOVE` + `MARK_AS_OVER_ANNOTATED`), which more than triples from **8.1%** in Tier 1 to **28.4%** in Tier 6 and **25.2%** in Tier 7, while outright **`% REMOVE`** increases nearly 7-fold (**2.4%** $\to$ **12.6%** $\to$ **16.6%**).
- By contrast, **Term Refinement (`% Refn`)** shows no systematic trend with distance (4.2%–9.8% across Tiers 1 and 3–7), and separating it explains the high total downgrade rate in **Tier 2** ($n = 104$): 19 of the 26 downgraded rows (**18.3%**) are `MODIFY`/`TOO_GENERAL` granularity refinements, whereas only 7 rows (**6.7%**, **1.9%** `REMOVE`) are propagation failures.
- Note that sample sizes in **Tier 2** ($n = 104$), **Tier 6** ($n = 95$), and **Tier 7** ($n = 163$) are much smaller than in Tiers 1, 3, 4, and 5 ($n = 558\text{–}1{,}548$), so point estimates in those smaller tiers carry wider sampling uncertainty.

*Note on non-monotonicity and UniProt `OC` lineage density*: Raw taxonomic step counts are non-monotonic at the extremes. The `02–06 steps` bin ($n = 188$, 12.2% Fail, 6.4% `REMOVE`) is enriched for intragenus/intraphylum bacterial and fungal paralog transfers, and the `25+ steps` bin (4.2% `REMOVE`, 19.9% Fail) has a lower `REMOVE` rate than `19–24 steps` (7.7% `REMOVE`, 18.7% Fail) because metazoan UniProt `OC` lineages contain many more intermediate taxonomic ranks (~14–16 ranks in vertebrates/insects) than bacterial or fungal lineages (~6–9 ranks). Consequently, a cross-domain Bacteria $\to$ Bacteria or Yeast $\to$ Human pair often falls into `19–24 steps`, whereas a Worm $\to$ Human (`Bilateria`) pair falls into `25+ steps`. Named phylogenetic tiers (Table 1) are therefore a better-calibrated metric than raw `OC` rank counts.

### 2. Excess PAINT Node Depth ($\Delta_{\text{depth}} = d_{\text{LCA}} - d_{\text{PTN}}$)

Even when the single experimental donor and target are close relatives (e.g., `mouse` $\to$ `human`), a PAINT curator may have placed the ancestral `IBD` assertion at `LUCA` or `Eukaryota` rather than at `Mammalia` or `Vertebrata`.

Because UniProt `OC` rank depth varies across kingdoms, the lookup table `PAINT_TAXON_DEPTH` in [`single_organism_distance.py`](single_organism_distance.py) maps all 63 explicit positive-IBD `taxon:<id>` identifiers across `interpro/panther/*/*-paint.tsv` to an approximate `OC` clade depth ($d_{\text{PTN}} = 0$ for `taxon:131567` `cellular_organisms`; $1$ for `Eukaryota`/`Opisthokonta`/`Bacteria`/`Archaea`; $2\text{–}5$ for intermediate `Metazoa`/`Bilateria`/`Chordata`/kingdom nodes; $6+$ for `Euteleostomi`/`Tetrapoda`/`Amniota`/`Mammalia`/`Euarchontoglires` and close genus/species nodes such as `Caenorhabditis` `taxon:6237`). Rows in `*-paint.tsv` whose `taxon` column is a bare `taxon:` string (meaning no taxon constraint was recorded in the PAINT GAF; see `src/ai_gene_review/etl/panther_paint.py`) are reported separately rather than assigned a clade depth. Restricting to all **1,798 rated rows on close donor–target pairs ($d_{\text{LCA}} \ge 6$: 958 in locally cached PAINT families with an explicit node taxid, 245 in cached families with an unrecorded blank `taxon:`, and 595 in uncached families)** holds donor–target evolutionary distance constant while isolating the effect of **PAINT ancestral node placement ($d_{\text{PTN}}$)**:

| PAINT Ancestral Node Placement ($d_{\text{PTN}}$) on Close Taxonomic Pairs ($d_{\text{LCA}} \ge 6$, total $n = 1{,}798$) | Rated ($n$) | Fail | **% Fail** | Refn | **% Refn** | **% Down** | `REMOVE` | **% `REMOVE`** |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| **Scoped within clade / Vertebrata** ($d_{\text{PTN}} \ge 6$, e.g. `Euteleostomi`, `Tetrapoda`, `Mammalia`, `Caenorhabditis`) | 493 | 33 | **6.7%** | 36 | **7.3%** | **14.0%** | 2 | **0.4%** |
| **Intermediate Metazoa / Bilateria / Chordata** ($2 \le d_{\text{PTN}} \le 5$) | 327 | 23 | **7.0%** | 25 | **7.6%** | **14.7%** | 10 | **3.1%** |
| **Over-deeply placed at `LUCA` / `Eukaryota` / `Opisthokonta`** ($d_{\text{PTN}} \le 1$, $\Delta_{\text{depth}} \ge 5$) | 138 | 12 | **8.7%** | 8 | **5.8%** | **14.5%** | 7 | **5.1%** |
| *Unrecorded / unmapped PAINT taxon (blank `taxon:` in `*-paint.tsv`)* | *245* | *21* | *8.6%* | *11* | *4.5%* | *13.1%* | *10* | *4.1%* |
| *Uncached PAINT family (no local `interpro/panther/*/*-paint.tsv` record)* | *595* | *62* | *10.4%* | *43* | *7.2%* | *17.6%* | *18* | *3.0%* |

Within cached PAINT families with an explicit node taxon on close donor–target pairs ($n = 958$), placing a single-organism `IBD` at `LUCA`/`Eukaryota`/`Opisthokonta` ($d_{\text{PTN}} \le 1$, $n = 138$) or at intermediate pre-vertebrate nodes ($2 \le d_{\text{PTN}} \le 5$, $n = 327$) raises the outright `REMOVE` rate **8- to 13-fold** (**3.1%–5.1%** vs **0.4%** when scoped within the clade, $d_{\text{PTN}} \ge 6$), and increases propagation failure from **6.7%** to **7.0%–8.7%**. (An additional 245 rows in cached families have a blank `taxon:` column in `*-paint.tsv`—where the PAINT GAF did not record a node taxon constraint, spanning both shallow mouse-only nodes and deeper nodes—and show an 8.6% Fail / 4.1% `REMOVE` rate.)

### 3. PANTHER Subfamily Dilution (`SAME_SF` vs `CROSS_SF`)

Joining the target protein and single-organism donor protein against cached `interpro/panther/*/*-entries.csv` tests whether they reside in the **same PANTHER subfamily** (`SAME_SF`, typically direct 1:1 orthologs) or **different subfamilies** (`CROSS_SF`, separated by at least one gene-duplication/subfamily-divergence event). Across the 3,981 rated rows with known donor lineage (1,469 with resolved subfamily for both donor and target; 2,512 in `UNKNOWN_SF` where the PANTHER family or external donor seed is not in the local `*-entries.csv` cache):

| Taxonomic Regime | Subfamily Relationship | Rated ($n$) | Fail | **% Fail** | Refn | **% Refn** | **% Down** | `REMOVE` | **% `REMOVE`** |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| **Close / moderate taxa** ($d_{\text{LCA}} \ge 4$) | `SAME_SF` (same PANTHER subfamily) | 543 | 47 | **8.7%** | 39 | **7.2%** | **15.8%** | 13 | **2.4%** |

| **Distant taxa** ($0 \le d_{\text{LCA}} \le 3$) | `CROSS_SF` (cross-subfamily paralog) | 328 | 81 | **24.7%** | 23 | **7.0%** | **31.7%** | 32 | **9.8%** |

Crossing a PANTHER subfamily boundary (`CROSS_SF`) increases the **Propagation Failure (`% Fail`)** rate from **8.7% $\to$ 11.0%** in close/moderate taxa and from **15.8% $\to$ 24.7%** in distant taxa, while roughly doubling the **`% REMOVE`** rate (**2.4% $\to$ 5.3%** and **5.4% $\to$ 9.8%**).

### 5. Donor-Support Topology Regimes: Single-Gene vs Multi-Paralog vs Single-Clade Jump vs Bracketed Multi-Species ($d_{\text{MRCA}}$ vs $d_{\text{LCA}}$)

A natural follow-up question is whether the elevated propagation failure rate of single-organism IBAs is caused by having only **one experimental seed gene**, or by **lack of phylogenetic bracketing** around the target organism. To test this across all **9,563 resolved non-self GOA `IBA` rows** (**9,394** rated with a completed review action: **9,243** with resolved donor lineages and **151** in `Tier 0`), [`single_organism_distance.py --regimes`](single_organism_distance.py) computes both the **donor-set MRCA clade depth ($d_{\text{MRCA}}$)** across all multi-species donor taxa in `WITH/FROM` and the **closest donor–target LCA depth ($d_{\text{LCA}}$)**, partitioning every non-self IBA into four topological regimes:

1. **Regime 1 (`R1_SINGLE_GENE`: 1 taxon, 1 distinct seed)**: Strictly one experimental donor gene in a single non-self species ($n = 3{,}226$ rated with resolved donor lineage; deduplicated on resolved accession via `canonical_seed_gene`, with `all_non_self = 0` and `rated_resolved_lineage = 0` rows reclassified from `R2` to `R1` as reported in `Section 5E`, `mrca_clade = "single_taxon"`, and `mrca_depth = -1`).
2. **Regime 2 (`R2_MULTI_PARALOG_SINGLE_TAXON`: 1 taxon, $\ge 2$ distinct seeds)**: Multiple experimental paralog/co-annotated seed genes, all from the same single non-self species ($n = 755$ rated; note that $3{,}226 + 755 = 3{,}981$, matching the Table 1 resolved-lineage single-organism total).
3. **Regime 3 (`R3_SINGLE_CLADE_JUMP`: $\ge 2$ donor taxa, $d_{\text{MRCA}} > d_{\text{LCA}}$)**: Multiple donor species are cited in `WITH/FROM`, but all donor species coalesce within a single restricted clade ($d_{\text{MRCA}} > d_{\text{LCA}}$) before jumping out to the target organism—for example, `mouse` + `rat` (`Rodentia` / `Glires`) jumping to `worm` or `yeast`, `worm` + `fly` (`Ecdysozoa`) jumping to `human`, or `M. tuberculosis` + `P. aeruginosa` (`Bacteria`) jumping to `human` ($n = 1{,}283$ rated).
4. **Regime 4 (`R4_BRACKETED_MULTI_SPECIES`: $\ge 2$ donor taxa, $d_{\text{MRCA}} \le d_{\text{LCA}}$)**: Multiple donor species span across or phylogenetically bracket the target organism's LCA—for example, `yeast` + `mouse` bracketing `human`, or `fly` + `zebrafish` bracketing `human` ($n = 3{,}979$ rated).

|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| **R1: Single donor gene** (`1 taxon, 1 distinct seed`) | 3,226 | 2,554 | 431 | **13.4%** | 241 | **7.5%** | **20.8%** | **4.8%** |

Key takeaways from Tables 5A–5D:
- **Multi-species single-clade jumps (`R3`: $d_{\text{MRCA}} > d_{\text{LCA}}$) behave like single-organism jumps (`R1`/`R2`), not like bracketed multi-species IBAs (`R4`)**: Citing two or more donor species from the *same* donor clade (e.g. `mouse` + `rat`, `worm` + `fly`, or two bacteria) yields an overall failure rate of **11.6% (`4.1% REMOVE`, $n = 1{,}283$)**—double that of phylogenetically bracketed multi-species IBAs (**5.9% Fail, `2.2% REMOVE`, $n = 3{,}979$**). Holding closest-donor distance constant, unbracketed single-clade jumps fail at **2.4-fold higher rates in Tiers 3–4** (**12.7% Fail** in `R3`, $n = 464$, vs **5.4% Fail** in `R4`, $n = 1{,}988$; and **16.5% Fail, 5.9% `REMOVE`** in Tier 4 Bilateria jumps, $n = 321$, vs **4.6% Fail, 1.4% `REMOVE`** in `R4`, $n = 565$) and **2.0-fold higher rates in Tiers 5–7** (**15.9% Fail** in `R3`, $n = 492$, vs **8.0% Fail** in `R4`, $n = 634$).
- **Band-stratified comparison of multi-paralog (`R2`) vs single-gene (`R1`) single-taxon IBAs**: Although the unstratified failure rates of `R2` (**12.6%**) and `R1` (**13.4%**) are similar, `R2` has a more proximal tier composition than `R1` (**47.0%** of `R2` rows are in Tiers 1–2 vs **40.2%** of `R1` rows). Within **Tiers 1–2**, `R2` has a slightly lower failure rate than `R1` (**6.2%** on $n = 355$ vs **8.6%** on $n = 1{,}297$), whereas in **Tiers 3–4 (Phylum/Bilateria Split)** `R2` reverses above `R1` (**18.7% Fail**, 95% Wilson CI $14.5\text{–}23.8\%$, $n = 267$, vs **14.6% Fail**, 95% CI $12.5\text{–}17.0\%$, $n = 978$), and in small-$n$ **Tier 6 (`Viridiplantae` $\leftrightarrow$ `Opisthokonta`)** `R2` reaches **48.0% Fail** ($12/25$, 95% CI $30.0\text{–}66.5\%$, hypothesis-generating given small $n$) because multiple co-annotated paralogs in one distant donor species often reflect a lineage-specific gene-family expansion (`CROSS_SF`: **18.8% Fail** in Table 5D).
- **Subfamily divergence (`CROSS_SF`) more than triples the failure rate even inside bracketed multi-species (`R4`) transfers**: In Table 5D, `R4` rows where at least one resolved donor shares the target's PANTHER subfamily (`R4 + SAME_SF`, $n = 1{,}368$) have only a **3.1% Fail (`1.4% REMOVE`)** rate—and restricting to rows where **all** resolved-SF donors sit in the target's subfamily gives an identical **3.2% Fail (`1.4% REMOVE`, $n = 805$)** (with **3.0% Fail, `1.4% REMOVE`** on the $n = 563$ mixed-subfamily rows where the target's subfamily still has direct donor support; the smaller `R3 + SAME_SF` mixed-donor cell at **19.4% Fail**, $7/36$, 95% Wilson CI $9.8\text{–}35.0\%$, vs **9.8% Fail**, 95% CI $6.6\text{–}14.4\%$ on $n = 224$ all-donor rows has wide overlapping confidence intervals and is hypothesis-generating given small $n$)—whereas `R4` rows where the target sits in a different subfamily from all resolved donors (`R4 + CROSS_SF`, $n = 1{,}191$) jump to **10.3% Fail (`4.0% REMOVE`)**. Species bracketing (`R4`) therefore does not protect against paralog divergence when no experimental donor resides in the target's subfamily.
- **Term refinement (`% Refn`) is invariant across all four regimes (`7.2%–7.7%`)**, confirming that the separation between unbracketed (`R1`, `R2`, `R3`: **11.6%–13.4% Fail**) and bracketed (`R4`: **5.9% Fail**) transfers is driven specifically by homology over-propagation rather than ontology granularity preferences.

## Limitations and Methodological Confounds

1. **Circularity between phylogenetic distance and reviewer policy (and how `--candidates` works)**: The review actions (`REMOVE`, `MARK_AS_OVER_ANNOTATED`, `MODIFY`, `ACCEPT`, `KEEP_AS_NON_CORE`) in `genes/*/*/*-ai-review.yaml` are **not** an independent experimental ground truth. They were produced by AI and human reviewers following [`IBA_REVIEW.md`](../IBA_REVIEW.md) guidelines that explicitly instruct reviewers to check phylogenetic node placement, subfamily divergence, and cross-kingdom pathway differences. Consequently, higher failure rates in Tiers 5–7, `CROSS_SF`, and unbracketed regimes (`R1`–`R3`) reflect both genuine biological divergence and heightened reviewer scrutiny of deep-node propagations. Conversely, some high-distance single-organism and single-clade IBAs in the baseline corpus remained at `ACCEPT` or `KEEP_AS_NON_CORE` even when the reviewer's own `reason` text noted a biological caveat. Specifically, `--candidates` in [`single_organism_distance.py`](single_organism_distance.py) filters on `tier >= 4`, `action in {"ACCEPT", "KEEP_AS_NON_CORE"}`, and `reason` matching 13 skeptical keywords (`"do not"`, `"does not"`, `"lacks"`, `"absent"`, `"plant"`, `"yeast-specific"`, `"only in"`, `"artifact"`, `"over-annotat"`, `"mislead"`, `"cytosolic"`, `"not directly"`, `"unlike"`); it does **not** filter on `CROSS_SF`, and passing `--all-regimes` extends `--candidates`, `--tier`, `--tsv`, and `--json` across all four regimes (`R1`–`R4`). Thus, `--candidates` serves as an internal **reason-vs-action inconsistency detector**, which we supplemented with PANTHER family/ortholog consistency sweeps to assemble the re-review candidates below. Note that harmonizing candidate proposals with sibling reviews on the same PAINT node is itself subject to the same-pipeline circularity caveat, since those sibling reviews were generated under the same curation guidelines.
2. **Sample size variation across strata**: While Tiers 1, 3, 4, and 5 have large denominators ($n = 558\text{–}1{,}548$), Tier 2 ($n = 104$), Tier 6 ($n = 95$, including $n = 25$ in `R2`), Tier 7 ($n = 163$, including $n = 16$ in `R2` and $n = 2$ in `R4`), the `02–06 steps` bin ($n = 188$), and the `>= 10` TreeGrafter steps bins ($n = 2$ and $n = 10$) have smaller sample sizes where a handful of rows shift percentages by several points.
3. **Partial local cache coverage, differential unresolved-seed exclusion, and Table 5D subfamily-resolution differences across regimes**: Roughly 33% of close-pair single-organism IBAs (`595 / 1,798` in Metric 2, plus `245 / 1,798` with an unrecorded blank `taxon:` in `*-paint.tsv`) and 63% of all rated single-organism rows (`2,512 / 3,981` in Metric 3) belong to PANTHER families or external seed accessions not yet cached or lacking taxon metadata under `interpro/panther/`. Furthermore, because a GOA `IBA` row enters the regime analysis only when **every** `WITH/FROM` seed token resolves (`not has_unresolved`), multi-seed rows have a higher chance of containing at least one uncached `UniProtKB:` token: as reported by `single_organism_distance.py --regimes` (`Section 5E`, using `n_distinct_donors` via `canonical_seed_gene`), **307 single-distinct-seed** vs **1,568 multi-distinct-seed** non-self GOA `IBA` rows (`1,875` total) are excluded due to $\ge 1$ unresolved seed token (this `307 / 1,568` split is provably invariant between raw `len(seed_toks)` and deduplicated `n_distinct_donors`, because any token present in `mod_to_acc` resolves by construction so no row with `len(seed_toks) > 1` and `n_distinct_donors == 1` can satisfy `has_unresolved`). Because multi-donor rows (`R3`/`R4`) are retained only when all donor tokens come from locally cached PANTHER families or MOD namespaces—and require only $\ge 1$ donor to have a cached PANTHER subfamily to enter `SAME_SF`/`CROSS_SF`—resolved subfamily coverage in Table 5D rises from **35.4%** in `R1` (`1,143 / 3,226`) and **43.2%** in `R2` (`326 / 755`) to **60.7%** in `R3` (`779 / 1,283`) and **64.3%** in `R4` (`2,559 / 3,979`). Within-regime `SAME_SF` vs `CROSS_SF` contrasts are unaffected by this coverage gradient, but cross-regime Table 5D cell comparisons (e.g. `R1 + CROSS_SF` vs `R4 + CROSS_SF`) compare a 35% subset of `R1` against a 64% subset of `R4`. Among resolved rows, Metrics 1 and 1B cover 99.8% (`3,981 / 3,988`) of rated single-organism rows (`7` excluded in `Tier 0` from *Sus scrofa* `9823` lacking a local `*-uniprot.txt` file), and Metric 5 covers 98.4% (`9,243 / 9,394`) of rated non-self resolved IBA rows across Regimes 1–4 (`151` excluded in `Tier 0`: `7` single-taxon and `144` multi-taxon rows citing at least one donor species without a local `*-uniprot.txt` lineage).
4. **Coarse `Xenbase` taxon mapping and Tier 2 residual clade fallback**: In `MOD_TO_TAXON`, `Xenbase` is mapped at the prefix level to `("8355", "Xenopus laevis")` when the `Xenbase:` token is not resolved via a local `genes/XENTR/*/*-uniprot.txt` cross-reference (`build_mod_to_acc`). For multi-donor rows that cite both an uncached `Xenbase:` token (mapped to `8355` *X. laevis*) and a *Xenopus tropicalis* `UniProtKB:` token (`8364`), this prefix fallback treats them as two congeneric *Xenopus* species (`n_donor_taxa = 2`) rather than a single donor species, shifting such rows from `R1`/`R2` into `R3`/`R4`. Separately, because `classify_evolutionary_span` routes any unenumerated LCA clade string to `Tier 2: Other ({lca})`, Tier 2 ($n = 104$ rated single-organism rows) combines 99 explicitly mapped fungal/plant order/subphylum rows (`Magnoliopsida`: 51, `Saccharomycotina`: 39, `rosids`: 6, `Pentapetalae`: 2, `Pezizomycotina`: 1) with 5 fallback rows (`Pseudomonadota`: 2, `Pseudomonadati`: 2, `Poaceae`: 1).
5. **Exact vs term-fallback review-action matching (`rev_by_exact` vs `rev_by_term`)**: Many older `*-ai-review.yaml` files omit `supporting_entities` on `IBA` entries. When matching a GOA `IBA` row to its review action, `single_organism_distance.py` first looks up `(go_id, frozenset(supporting_entities))` (`EXACT`) and falls back to `rev_by_term[go_id]` (`TERM_FALLBACK`, which retains the last `IBA` entry for that `go_id`). Across the 9,243 resolved-lineage rated rows (`Section 5E`), `EXACT` matching covers **69.8%** of `R1` (`2,251 / 3,226`), **72.2%** of `R2` (`545 / 755`), **74.4%** of `R3` (`955 / 1,283`), and **76.6%** of `R4` (`3,046 / 3,979`). When a gene review omits `supporting_entities` and has multiple GOA `IBA` rows for the same `go_id` in different regimes, `TERM_FALLBACK` assigns the same review action to each row.

## Candidate Failure Patterns Identified for Re-Review (and Valid Deep Conservation)

Combining `uv run python projects/IBA_REVIEW/single_organism_distance.py --candidates` (and `--candidates --all-regimes`) with PANTHER family/ortholog consistency sweeps across single-organism (`R1`/`R2`), single-clade jump (`R3`), and subfamily-divergent (`CROSS_SF`) reviews surfaces six recurring biological failure mechanisms—comprising **15 initial single-organism candidate genes** ([PR #5703](https://github.com/ai4curation/ai-gene-review/pull/5703) for Tiers 6–7 and [PR #5704](https://github.com/ai4curation/ai-gene-review/pull/5704) for Tiers 4–5), **17 sibling-harmonization genes** ([PR #5857](https://github.com/ai4curation/ai-gene-review/pull/5857)), **2 cross-subfamily high-distance audit genes** ([PR #5861](https://github.com/ai4curation/ai-gene-review/pull/5861)), **5 multi-paralog (`R2`), single-clade jump (`R3`), and subfamily-divergent (`R4 + CROSS_SF`) candidate genes** ([PR #6157](https://github.com/ai4curation/ai-gene-review/pull/6157)), and **20 follow-on `--candidates --all-regimes` genes across Batches A–D** ([PR #6213](https://github.com/ai4curation/ai-gene-review/pull/6213), [PR #6236](https://github.com/ai4curation/ai-gene-review/pull/6236), [PR #6271](https://github.com/ai4curation/ai-gene-review/pull/6271), and [PR #6289](https://github.com/ai4curation/ai-gene-review/pull/6289))—alongside valid deep conservation:

1. **Cross-Domain Organelle / Pathway Projection (Bacteria $\leftrightarrow$ Eukaryota, Tier 7)**
