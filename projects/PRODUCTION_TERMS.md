# "X production" Terms — Readouts or Mechanisms?

**Bottom line:** GO has 430 live terms named "X production" (137 production
terms, 293 regulation terms), almost all for cytokines and other immune
mediators, such as GO:0032637 *interleukin-8 production*. GO keeps them as a
stated "historical exception" to its rule against process terms for making
one named protein. Since 2020 they have been blocked for direct annotation:
curators annotate to the "regulation of X production" children instead. GO
has obsoleted the comparable terms for proteins that are not secreted
mediators, including the immune cell-surface proteins CD4, CD80, CD86 and FasL
and the enzyme nitric-oxide synthase.

Analysed from first principles, a production term names an **outcome**: the
amount of X goes up. It is not a pathway. Most of these terms (about 73 of 137)
read out shared programmes (NF-κB, STAT, NFAT/AP-1 signalling) whose only
X-specific parts are the X gene's own regulatory DNA. A minority sit on top of
**dedicated machinery** that is worth modelling: inflammasome processing of
IL-1β/IL-18, ADAM17 shedding of TNF, TGF-β latency and activation, cytotoxic
granule exocytosis, interferon induction through IRF3/IRF7, and
immunoglobulin production. This project proposes **modules for the
machines, not for the cytokines**. It also proposes moving the product's
identity into the annotation (an input or a GO-CAM edge) and the "amount of X"
observation into a readout layer.

This page records the GO issue history, the first-principles analysis, the
evidence from this repo's reviews, and a plan. Nothing here is yet a ruling for
reviews.

the target of that logical definition.

### Symbiont, ROS/NO and small-RNA families

- **Symbiont effects on host production.** Terms such as *induction by
  symbiont of host cytokine production* were obsoleted
  ([#21743](https://github.com/geneontology/go-ontology/issues/21743),
  [#25395](https://github.com/geneontology/go-ontology/issues/25395),
  [#26746](https://github.com/geneontology/go-ontology/issues/26746),
  [#29194](https://github.com/geneontology/go-ontology/issues/29194),
  [#29259](https://github.com/geneontology/go-ontology/issues/29259)).
  The reasons were that the host does the work ("it is not an evolved function
  of an symbiont, to induce host cytokine production; this is a response by the
  host", pgaudet) and that suppressing production "represents a read out"
  (#29259). The replacements name the host signalling step the effector hits.
  New terms requested as "suppression of host … cytokine production" were
  created as "… cytokine signaling" instead
  ([#27129](https://github.com/geneontology/go-ontology/issues/27129)).
- **ROS and NO.** GO has no plain "ROS production" term. GO:1903409 *reactive
  oxygen species biosynthetic process* has been proposed for obsoletion since
  2022 ([#23668](https://github.com/geneontology/go-ontology/issues/23668),
  still open). For: "Unless any evidence of biosynthesis I suggest we obsolete
  the branch?" (ValWood). Against: dedicated H2O2-producing enzymes exist, and
  "Maybe a taxon constraint would be more appropriate ?" (pgaudet).
- **Small RNAs.** "Production of siRNA/miRNA involved in Y" terms were
  relabelled "X processing", and an siRNA transcription term was added for the
  RNA-templated step ([#19303](https://github.com/geneontology/go-ontology/issues/19303),
  [#25084](https://github.com/geneontology/go-ontology/issues/25084)). The
  objection was that RNA-dependent RNA polymerase activity is production, not
  processing: "I don't think the merge of 'production' into 'processing' is a
  generally safe assumption" (ValWood).

### Still open

- [#23668](https://github.com/geneontology/go-ontology/issues/23668): the ROS biosynthesis branch, above.
- [#30457](https://github.com/geneontology/go-ontology/issues/30457): GO:1903607
  *cytochrome c biosynthetic process*. "Maybe this is a specific product and
  should be obsoleted ?" (pgaudet). The latest plan is to move its annotations
  to cytochrome c–heme linkage and obsolete it.

### The reasons, collected

**For keeping** a production term:

- it covers an increase in level when the mechanism is unknown, which is what
  immunology papers measure;
- which cytokine appears is informative about the type of immune response, and
  the terms feed enrichment analysis;
- the Cell Ontology and OBI reference them;
- GO's own regulation terms need them as the target of their logical definitions.

**For obsoleting** one:

- it names a specific gene product (all non-immune cases);
- it is a readout, or the host does the work (symbiont terms);
- it is a precomposed "X involved in Y" term;
- the entity does not exist (IL-14);
- it is a tissue variant of a pathway (hepatic glucose production).

GO's own obsoletion comments on the 45 obsolete production terms break down as:

- 18 "not a real process" (symbiont terms);
- 14 "represents a gene product";
- 4 "gene shown not to exist";
- 3 "pre-composed, should be a GO-CAM model";
- 2 "unnecessary grouping";
- 1 duplicate of a signalling term;
- 1 readout;
- 1 poorly defined;
- 1 other.

**The line in practice is the molecule class.** Secreted immune mediators
keep production terms; other proteins, immune or not, lose them. A few
non-immune production terms remain (oxytocin, neurotrophins, endothelin, cell
adhesion molecule), but they have never been reviewed as a group. External use
by itself protects nothing: the nitric-oxide synthase term was obsoleted
although a CL class used it.

## First principles: what "X production" denotes

The definition describes a change in an amount. Getting from no X to more X
runs through a pipeline that is almost entirely generic:

1. **Induction.** A receptor triggers a signalling pathway that activates
   transcription factors (NF-κB, IRF3/7, STATs, NFAT/AP-1).
2. **Transcription** of the X gene. The only X-specific part is the gene's
   promoter and enhancers.
3. **mRNA stability and translation.** For TNF, IL-6, IL-8 and IL-2 this step
   is controlled by shared AU-rich-element decay machinery.
4. **Maturation**: proteolytic processing, or latency.
5. **Export**: constitutive secretion, regulated granule release, shedding from
   the membrane, or unconventional routes.
6. **Extracellular activation or clearance.**

**The test for a module:** is any step performed by machinery dedicated to X,
or to a small family that includes X? If so, that step is a real pathway and
worth a module. If not, the term labels the output of shared programmes, and
the X-specific information is which gene is the input, a fact that belongs in
the annotation or the GO-CAM model rather than in the term.

The 2020 merge of secretion into production is consistent with this test. Most
cytokines are secreted constitutively once made, so secretion is not the
controlled step. Insulin is the contrast. Its release is controlled at
secretion, so GO keeps GO:0030073 *insulin secretion* and GO:0030070 *insulin
processing*, and this repo has a
[glucose-stimulated insulin secretion module](../modules/beta_cell_glucose_stimulated_insulin_secretion.html).
The planned #19117 exception for regulated cytokine exocytosis was the same
idea applied to cytokines.

About 41 of the 137 production terms rest on a dedicated or genuinely specific
step. **IL-8 is the
typical member of the readout group.** Its production is NF-κB/AP-1-driven
transcription, p38–MK2-dependent mRNA stabilisation and constitutive
secretion. Where a cell type stores a cytokine in granules, the machinery
belongs to the granule, not to the cytokine.

One shared module would absorb many "negative regulation of X production"
rows: **AU-rich-element mRNA decay**. Its parts are TTP (ZFP36), Regnase-1
(ZC3H12A) and Roquin (RC3H1), restrained by p38–MK2. The GO process is
GO:0061158 *3'-UTR-mediated mRNA destabilization*, and the same machinery acts
across TNF, IL-6, IL-8 and IL-2.

### Type I interferon: the regulation term is used as the pathway's name

Examples are *TBK1 activation via TRAF3 autoubiquitination*, *USP22 activation
of IRF3 via KPNA2 stabilization*, the poxvirus inhibition models and the
cGAS–STING models.

- **Every step of the chain is filed under the regulation term.** That covers
  TRAF3, TICAM1 and TICAM2, MAVS, TBK1, IKBKE, DDX3X, TANK, KPNA2, USP22, and
  IRF3 or IRF7 at the end, all under *positive regulation of type I interferon
  production*.
- **Each activity gets the sign of its own effect.** Negative regulators
  (TRIM27, XAF1, KAT8, CTPS1, the viral proteins) are filed under the negative
  term. The core steps in the same model stay under the positive one.
- **The interferon is mostly absent.** 11 IRF3 or IRF7 activities sit on these
  terms. Four name IFNB1 as their input; seven name nothing. 17 of the 21
  models never name an interferon gene, so the production term is the only
  place the product appears.
- **The same steps sit under different processes in different models.**
  Across every cached model:

  | Gene | Process each activity is part of |
  |---|---|
  | TBK1 (40 activities) | type I interferon production term 13, cytoplasmic pattern recognition receptor signalling 6, antiviral innate immune response 5, activation of innate immune response 5, cGAS/STING signalling 3, others 8 |
  | IRF3 (19 activities) | type I interferon production term 8, cGAS/STING signalling 3, cytoplasmic pattern recognition receptor signalling 2, TLR4 signalling 1, mRNA transcription 1, none 4 |

  Even within one cGAS–STING model the pathway is split. CGAS and STING1 are
  under *cGAS/STING signaling pathway*, TBK1 phosphorylating STING under
  *activation of innate immune response*, and TBK1's other activity under
  *positive regulation of type I interferon production*.

The reading: curators use the regulation term because no process term names
the IRF3/IRF7 interferon-induction arm. This is the strongest evidence for
that module candidate. A module would give these steps one consistent home,
and the receptor-pathway terms that exist could then be used consistently.

### Inflammation models: a precomposed term, with the cytokine sometimes named

These are the cholinergic anti-inflammatory models (CHRNA7 through JAK2–STAT3,
or through calcium and PKC to HMOX1, human and mouse), REG3A/REG3G acting
through EXTL3, and XBP1 in dendritic cells.

- **The cytokine is named in 4 of the 10, by a causal edge to its cytokine
  activity.** In the wounding models, MAPK9 has edges to TNF and IL6. In the
  IL22–REG3 airway models, EXTL3 has edges to IL33 and TSLP.
- **The CHRNA7 and XBP1 models name no cytokine.**
- **The human CHRNA7–STAT3 model routes through TTP.** STAT3's input is ZFP36,
  and ZFP36 sits under *negative regulation of 3′-UTR-mediated mRNA
  stabilization*. That is the AU-rich-element decay machinery proposed above
  as a module, already present in a production model.
- **The XBP1 models use GO:1903409.** They place respiratory chain complex I
  under *reactive oxygen species biosynthetic process*, the term proposed for
  obsoletion in the still-open #23668. Obsoleting it would touch both models.
- **The terms are precomposed "X involved in Y" terms.** GO has been
  obsoleting these when nothing uses them
  ([INVOLVED_IN_PRECOMPOSITION](INVOLVED_IN_PRECOMPOSITION.md), #31254). Use
  in these models would block that.

### What retiring the terms would take in these models

- **Type I interferon:** 17 models would need the interferon gene added, as
  the input of an IRF3 or IRF7 step. Their signalling steps would need a
  pathway process.
- **Inflammation:** 6 models would need the cytokine named.
- **Other:** the *C. elegans* model would need its antimicrobial peptide
  genes.
- **Who to ask:** almost all of this is one group's convention (UniProt), so
  a change would mostly be one conversation.

## A from-scratch design

1. **Model the mechanisms.** Gene expression and secretion stay generic. Each
   dedicated machine in the table becomes a process and a module.
   *Immunoglobulin production* stays, because it is a real composite process.
   Small-molecule "production" collapses into biosynthesis plus transport.
2. **Put the product in the annotation, not the term.** "Regulates expression
   of CXCL8" becomes regulation of gene expression with the gene as input, or
   a GO-CAM causal edge to that gene's expression. This is already GO's rule
   for every non-cytokine protein (#28860, #29735).
3. **Move readouts to a readout layer.** "Amount of IL-8 in serum" is a trait
   (OBA) or an assay (OBI), which is where phenotype-like observations belong.
4. **Drop the Cell Ontology dependency.** CL currently uses `capable_of` a GO
   production term in logical definitions. For example, *group 1 innate
   lymphoid cell* is defined using GO:0032609 *interferon-gamma production*.
   RO already has `produces` (RO:0003000): "a produces b if some process that
   occurs_in a has_output b". `produces` with a Protein Ontology term states the
   same fact without a GO class.
5. **Compute enrichment sets.** Derive sets such as "genes upstream of IL-8
   levels" from the extensions and models in step 2, instead of maintaining
   about 430 hand-made classes.

GO's present arrangement is a halfway house to this design: production terms
are kept but blocked, and they serve as regulation targets. The regulation
terms are where far-upstream perturbation results collect.

## What this could mean for reviews (proposal, not a ruling)

For a row on *regulation of X production*:

- **The gene performs a step in a dedicated machine for X** (CASP1 for IL-1β,
  ADAM17 for TNF), or **in the induction pathway of its class** (IRF3 for type
  I interferon). The row can be core; link it to the module.
- **The gene is in a shared upstream pathway** (TLR4, MYD88, NF-κB). The term
  is true but reads out the pathway. The signalling-pathway term is the core
  function, and the production row is KEEP_AS_NON_CORE.
- **The gene changes X levels with no mechanism shown** (knockdown or
  overexpression, far upstream). This row is a phenotype: KEEP_AS_NON_CORE, or
  MARK_AS_OVER_ANNOTATED under the existing rules.

Rows on the production term itself (do-not-annotate) need their own worklist.

## Observations for GO (candidate tickets, not filed)

- **Six cytokine production terms lack `gocheck_do_not_annotate`**, despite
  the #19115 checklist: GO:0010573 VEGF production, GO:0071604 TGF-β
  production, GO:0032905/GO:0032906/GO:0032907 TGF-β1/2/3 production, and
  GO:0034343 type III interferon production. Fifteen production terms outside
  the cytokine branch are also unflagged; the script lists them.
- **A merge went to the wrong level.** GO:0050754 *positive regulation of
  fractalkine biosynthetic process* is an alt_id of GO:0032603 *fractalkine
  production* (do-not-annotate). It should have gone to GO:0032724 *positive
  regulation of fractalkine production* (#19254).
- **The #19117 exception was not made.** No regulation-of-secretion term
  remains for genes in the regulated cytokine exocytosis machinery.
- **The CL dependency could move to RO:0003000 `produces`.** That would remove
  the main argument for keeping production classes at all.
- **For the GO-CAM curators (mostly UniProt):**
  - the type I interferon models file signalling steps under a
    regulation-of-production term instead of a pathway process;
  - 17 of the 21 name no interferon gene.

## Open questions

1. Should reviews treat a production row as core only when the gene is in a

   before it becomes guidance.
2. Is the type I interferon group a real pathway or a readout of several
   receptor pathways? IRF3/IRF7 also induce non-interferon genes.
3. Would GO and CL accept moving the CL definitions to `produces`? Without
   that, GO cannot retire the classes even if it wanted to.
4. Should the 21 rows on do-not-annotate production terms be MODIFY to the

## Reproducing

```bash
# go-edit.obo from a go-ontology checkout, or a release go.obo
python projects/PRODUCTION_TERMS/scripts/inventory.py \
    --go path/to/go-edit.obo --out projects/PRODUCTION_TERMS/data
```

Outputs:

- `production_terms.yaml`: every production-named term, live or obsolete, with
  flags, parents, merges, obsoletion comment and heuristic class.
- `gocam_models.yaml`: every cached GO-CAM with an activity on such a term,
  with all of its activities, their inputs and causal edges, and the two
  product-naming signals. Those signals are a transcription factor's input,
  and a causal edge to a cytokine activity. A kinase's substrate input is not
  counted.
- `summary.yaml`: all counts on this page. Review rows are found by term id
with a line-based scan of `existing_annotations`. A full YAML parse of every
review takes minutes, and this scan reads only each row's term id and action.
