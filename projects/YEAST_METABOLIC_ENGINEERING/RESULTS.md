### Why These Two Routing Artifacts Matter

1. **The Sedoheptulose-1,7-Bisphosphate (`PFK_3`/`FBA3`/`TALA`) Bypass**:
   Because `TALA` ($\text{F6P} + \text{E4P} \to \text{S7P} + \text{G3P}$) + `PFK_3` ($\text{S7P} + \text{ATP} \to \text{S1,7BP} + \text{ADP}$) + `FBA3` ($\text{S1,7BP} \to \text{DHAP} + \text{E4P}$) has the exact net stoichiometry of $\text{F6P} + \text{ATP} \to \text{DHAP} + \text{G3P} + \text{ADP}$, standard LP solvers route `8.9249 mmol/gDW/h` through `PFK_3`/`FBA3` and `-8.6614` through reverse `TALA`, leaving canonical `PFK = 0.0` and `FBA = 0.0`. Notably, in biochemical reality sedoheptulose-1,7-bisphosphate is formed in yeast during riboneogenesis by reverse Fba1p condensation and dephosphorylated by *SHB17* (`YKR043C`, sedoheptulose-1,7-bisphosphatase; Clasquin et al. 2011, `PMID:21685363`), not as the main glycolytic conduit.
2. **The Glycerol–Dihydroxyacetone Transhydrogenase Cycle (`G3PD1ir` $\to$ `G3PT` $\to$ `GLYCDy` $\to$ `DHAK`)**:
   Under anaerobic standard FBA, `iMM904` synthesizes `1.0888 mmol/gDW/h` glycerol via `G3PD1ir` + `G3PT`, but secretes **zero glycerol** (`EX_glyc_e = 0.0`). Instead, it runs the opposing catabolic arm (`GLYCDy` / `GCY1` + `DHAK` / `DAK1,2`) simultaneously at `1.0888 mmol/gDW/h`, turning the two opposing arms of [`modules/glycerol_metabolism.yaml`](../../modules/glycerol_metabolism.yaml) into an ATP-driven $\text{NADH} \to \text{NADPH}$ transhydrogenase cycle.

### 4a. Repair 1: `GPD1` (`YDL022W`) / `GPD2` (`YOL059W`) GPR & Anaerobic Glycerol Redox Sink

In *S. cerevisiae* ([`genes/yeast/GPD1/GPD1-ai-review.yaml`](../../genes/yeast/GPD1/GPD1-ai-review.yaml), [`genes/yeast/GPD2/GPD2-ai-review.yaml`](../../genes/yeast/GPD2/GPD2-ai-review.yaml), [`modules/glycerol_metabolism.yaml`](../../modules/glycerol_metabolism.yaml); Ansell et al. 1997 `PMID:9171333`), glycerol synthesis is the **obligate anaerobic redox sink** that re-oxidizes excess cytosolic NADH generated by biomass synthesis. Single `gpd1Δ` or `gpd2Δ` mutants continue to produce glycerol and grow anaerobically, whereas `gpd1Δ gpd2Δ` double mutants make no glycerol and are inviable under anaerobic conditions.

In unmodified `iMM904`, anaerobic glycerol secretion is `0.0000` even in wild-type because of three non-physiological redox bypasses:
1. **Catabolic `GCY1`/`DAK` cycling (`GLYCDy` + `DHAK`)** re-assimilating glycerol internally.
2. **Soluble mitochondrial `fadh2_m` fumarate respiration (`G3PDm` / `GUT2` $\to$ `FRDcm`/`FRDm`)**, which dumps cytosolic NADH via the G3P shuttle into secreted succinate (`EX_succ_e = 0.6752 mmol/gDW/h`), even though Gut2p transfers electrons directly to membrane ubiquinone (`q6_m`) and requires an active aerobic respiratory chain (`PMID:11502169`).
3. **Unconstrained NADH-dependent glutamate synthase (`GLUSx` / `GLT1`) and threonine aldolase (`THRA` / `GLY1`)**, which substitute for NADPH-GDH (`GDH1`) and cleave threonine to glycine + acetaldehyde ($\to$ ethanol) to re-oxidize cytosolic NADH without making glycerol.

When those three anaerobic bypass loops are closed, the consequence of `iMM904`'s `GPD2` compartment/GPR error becomes stark:

### 4b. Repair 2: `DAL3` (`YIR032C`) & `DUR1,2` (`YBR208C`) on Allantoin as Sole Nitrogen Source

In *S. cerevisiae* ([`genes/yeast/DAL3/DAL3-ai-review.yaml`](../../genes/yeast/DAL3/DAL3-ai-review.yaml), [`genes/yeast/DUR1,2/DUR1,2-ai-review.yaml`](../../genes/yeast/DUR1,2/DUR1,2-ai-review.yaml)), allantoin degradation proceeds via `DAL1` (allantoinase) $\to$ `DAL2` (allantoicase, releasing 1 urea + `(S)`-ureidoglycolate) $\to$ `DAL3` (ureidoglycolate lyase, `EC 4.3.2.3`, releasing glyoxylate + a second urea). Both urea molecules are then degraded to $\text{CO}_2 + 2\,\text{NH}_4^+$ by the bifunctional urea amidolyase `DUR1,2` (`YBR208C`) at the cost of 1 ATP per urea. Consequently, **`dur1,2Δ` mutants cannot grow on allantoin as sole nitrogen source**.

Because `iMM904` models `DAL3` (`UGLYCH`) as the bacterial ureidoglycolate amidohydrolase (`EC 3.5.3.19`: `urdglyc_c + h2o_c + 2 h_c <=> co2_c + glx_c + 2 nh4_c`), half of allantoin's nitrogen is released directly as free $\text{NH}_4^+$ without passing through urea or `DUR1,2`:

### 4c. Repair 3: `BNA3` (`YJL060W`) vs. `BNA7` (`YDR428C`) & De Novo $\text{NAD}^+$ Biosynthesis

In `iMM904`, de novo nicotinate-nucleotide synthesis (`NNDPR`) has two compounding errors:
1. It includes the prokaryotic `ASPOcm` (`EC 1.4.3.16`, L-aspartate oxidase) + `QULNS` (`EC 2.5.1.72`, quinolinate synthase from iminoaspartate + DHAP) reactions with empty GPRs, allowing *S. cerevisiae* to synthesize $\text{NAD}^+$ anaerobically (`NNDPR = 0.5700 mmol/gDW/h`), contradicting the $\text{O}_2$-dependence of the eukaryotic kynurenine pathway (`Panozzo et al. 2002, PMID:12147290`).
2. Once the non-yeast `ASPOcm`/`QULNS` bypass is removed, `iMM904`'s obsolete assignment of `BNA3` (`YJL060W`) to `FKYNH` (`EC 3.5.1.9`) causes a `bna3Δ` knockout to completely block de novo $\text{NAD}^+$ synthesis (`0.5280 -> 0.0000`), whereas reassigning `FKYNH` to the true formamidase `BNA7` (`YDR428C`; Wogulis et al. 2008 `PMID:18205391`) restores wild-type `bna3Δ` prototrophy (`0.5280`) and transfers the block to `bna7Δ` (`0.0000`).

## 5. AST-Based E-Flux vs. ChatGEM's Regex Parser & Condition-Specific Isozyme Shares

In `pnnl/ChatGEM`'s reference script `46._automatic_E_flux.py`, GPR strings are parsed with `re.split(r"\(|\)", gpr)` and reduced with `max(stack)` at the top level.
