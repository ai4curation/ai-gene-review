## 1. Executive Summary

*Pseudomonas putida* KT2440 synthesizes the polyamine **spermidine** by the **alternative carboxyspermidine pathway**, not by the classical aminopropyltransferase route used by *E. coli* and by its own genus-mate *P. aeruginosa*. The decisive genomic signal is a clean absence: the KT2440 proteome (UP000000556) contains **no S‑adenosylmethionine decarboxylase (SpeD, EC 4.1.1.50)**. Because the classical route requires SpeD to generate the decarboxylated‑SAM aminopropyl donor consumed by spermidine synthase, the lone spermidine synthase annotation in the genome (PP_1867 / Q88LR2) cannot support de novo spermidine synthesis on its own. Lacking SpeD while still making spermidine is the recognized molecular signature of organisms that run the carboxyspermidine route ([PMID: 22025614](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22025614/)).

The two committed enzymes of the carboxyspermidine route are present and, importantly, **genomically adjacent** — the hallmark cluster organization described for this pathway. **PP_2929 (nspC / Q88IS0)** is a bona fide **carboxyspermidine/carboxynorspermidine decarboxylase (CASDC/CANSDC, EC 4.1.1.96)**: it is 66% identical to a reviewed nspC ortholog and only ~28% identical to its own genome's fold‑III PLP decarboxylase paralogs, so it is not an over‑propagated annotation. Immediately upstream, **PP_2928 (Q88IS1)**, currently mis‑annotated as a "saccharopine dehydrogenase," is in fact the **carboxyspermidine dehydrogenase (CASDH, EC 1.5.1.43)** — it belongs to the correct saccharopine‑dehydrogenase/homospermidine‑synthase family (IPR005097), is the right length (414 aa), sits directly next to nspC, and is 46.8% identical over its full length to the experimentally characterized *Vibrio cholerae* CANSDH (VC_1624). For module curation, the carboxyspermidine spermidine‑biosynthesis module is **satisfiable** in KT2440: putrescine is supplied redundantly (SpeA/AguA/SpeB/SpeC, all in the candidate set), aspartate‑β‑semialdehyde is supplied by Asd (PP_1989), CASDH is PP_2928, and CASDC is PP_2929. ## 2. Target-Organism Pathway Definition

### What the process is

"Carboxyspermidine biosynthesis" is the committed, two‑enzyme core of the **alternative (SAM‑independent) spermidine biosynthetic pathway**. In this route the aminopropyl donor is **L‑aspartate‑β‑semialdehyde (ASA)** rather than decarboxylated S‑adenosylmethionine. The core chemistry is:

1. **CASDH (EC 1.5.1.43)** condenses **putrescine + ASA** in an **NADPH‑dependent** reductive amination to form **carboxyspermidine** ([PMID: 36283333](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36283333/)).
2. **CASDC (EC 4.1.1.96)**, a **PLP‑dependent fold‑III decarboxylase**, removes the carboxyl group to yield **spermidine**.

When the same two enzymes act on 1,3‑diaminopropane instead of putrescine they make sym‑norspermidine via carboxynorspermidine — hence the CANSDH/CANSDC nomenclature. The enzymes are substrate‑promiscuous; which polyamine is produced in vivo depends on the diamine supplied ([PMID: 22025614](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22025614/)).

### Boundaries — what to keep separate

- **Putrescine supply** (arginine/agmatine and ornithine decarboxylase branches) feeds this module but is upstream metabolism, largely mapped under KEGG `ppu00330`-adjacent arginine metabolism. It should be treated as substrate supply, not part of the committed carboxyspermidine core.
- **Aspartate‑β‑semialdehyde supply** (Asd, EC 1.2.1.11) is shared with lysine/DAP and threonine/methionine biosynthesis; it is a co‑substrate node, not part of the module proper.
- **Classical spermidine synthesis** (SpeD EC 4.1.1.50 + SpeE EC 2.5.1.16) is the *competing* route and should be modeled as a **mutually exclusive alternative**, marked not‑expected here.
- The nominal bucket **`ppu00330` "Arginine and proline metabolism"** is an overview map that co‑locates dozens of only loosely related reactions (arginine succinyltransferase AST pathway, proline catabolism PutA, 5‑oxoprolinases, creatinase, opine oxidase, etc.). Most of the 39 supplied candidates belong to those neighboring processes and are **not** part of carboxyspermidine biosynthesis. The review deliberately narrows to the polyamine sub‑network.

### Alternate names / database definitions

- **CASDH** = carboxyspermidine dehydrogenase; **CANSDH** = carboxynorspermidine dehydrogenase (same enzyme family, EC 1.5.1.43).
- **CASDC/CANSDC** = carboxyspermidine/carboxynorspermidine decarboxylase, gene symbol **nspC**, EC 4.1.1.96.
- The pathway is variously called the "alternative," "carboxyspermidine," or "aspartate‑semialdehyde" spermidine pathway. It is **not** the classical "aminopropyltransferase" route.

## 4. Candidate Genes and Evidence

### High-confidence, module-defining genes

**PP_2929 — nspC / CASDC (Q88IS0, EC 4.1.1.96) — COVERED, high confidence.**
This is the decarboxylase that produces spermidine. Global Needleman–Wunsch alignments place it firmly in the CANSDC/CASDC family: **66.2% identity** to reviewed *Herminiimonas arsenicoxydans* nspC (Q5QCP2), **51.0%** to *V. cholerae* nspC (Q9KRL4), and **41.7%** to *Campylobacter jejuni* nspC (A8FNH9). By contrast, identity to the co‑encoded fold‑III PLP decarboxylase paralogs in KT2440 is only **28.3%** (lysA/DAP decarboxylase, Q88L58) and **29.2%** (speC/ornithine decarboxylase, Q88PI6). The >20‑percentage‑point margin over the paralogs shows PP_2929 is a genuine CASDC, not an over‑propagated fold‑III annotation. Evidence type: strong homology to *reviewed/experimentally validated* orthologs; direct KT2440 experiment absent but transfer is **strong**. Curation caveat: because CASDC and CANSDC are the same enzyme, the label should note substrate promiscuity; the *in vivo* product is spermidine when putrescine is the diamine ([PMID: 22025614](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22025614/)).

**PP_2928 — CASDH (Q88IS1, EC 1.5.1.43) — NOT in candidate list; PROMOTE and RE-ANNOTATE.**
PP_2928 is currently annotated "saccharopine dehydrogenase," which is why it was excluded from the polyamine candidate set. Three independent lines of evidence identify it as the missing CASDH: (i) it carries **IPR005097 (Saccharopine dehydrogenase/homospermidine synthase, NADP‑dependent domain)** plus IPR032095 and the IPR036291 NAD(P)‑binding superfamily fold — the exact family to which characterized CASDH/CANSDH proteins belong; (ii) it is **414 aa**, matching CASDH references (*V. cholerae* VC_1624 Q9KRL3 = 414 aa; *V. alginolyticus* P0DPE4 = 414 aa); and (iii) it sits **immediately adjacent to PP_2929/nspC**, reproducing the CASDH–CASDC gene cluster reported across bacteria ([PMID: 22025614](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22025614/)). A full‑length global alignment to *V. cholerae* CANSDH gives **46.8% identity (203/434 columns)** — well above over‑propagation noise and consistent with true orthology. Evidence type: strong homology + syntenic context; transfer **strong**. This is the single most important curation action from the review.

### Putrescine-supply genes (substrate provision — covered)

| Gene | Locus | EC | Role | Note |
|------|-------|----|------|------|
| speA | PP_0567 | 4.1.1.19 | Arginine decarboxylase → agmatine | Covered |
| aguA | PP_0266 | 3.5.3.12 | Agmatine deiminase → N‑carbamoylputrescine | Covered |
| speB | PP_2196 | 3.5.3.11 | Agmatinase → putrescine | Covered; note a second agmatinase annotation (PP_4523) exists — paralog ambiguity |
| speC | PP_0864 | 4.1.1.17 | Ornithine decarboxylase → putrescine | Covered (primary bucket `ppu04148` in metadata) |

These provide redundant putrescine supply, so S1 is robustly satisfied regardless of which branch dominates under a given condition.

### Candidate-list genes that are NOT part of carboxyspermidine biosynthesis

The great majority of the 39 supplied candidates are `ppu00330` neighbors unrelated to the module and should not be counted toward module satisfiability. Notable examples and their true homes:

- **AST arginine‑succinyltransferase pathway** (arginine catabolism): astA‑I/PP_4479, astA‑II/PP_4480, astB/PP_4477, astD/PP_4478, astE/PP_4475.
- **Proline metabolism:** putA/PP_4947 (Pro→Glu), proI/PP_5095 (P5C reductase), pip/PP_5028 (prolyl aminopeptidase), proA/PP_4811, proB/PP_0691.
- **Guanidino/arginine side reactions:** aruH/PP_3721, aruI/PP_3723, kauB/PP_5278, creA/PP_3667 (creatinase), ocd/PP_3533 (ornithine cyclodeaminase).
- **5‑oxoprolinase:** oplA/PP_3515, oplB/PP_3514.
- **Opine oxidase:** ooxA/PP_4456, ooxB/PP_4457.
- **Generic oxidoreductases/aminotransferases with broad EC (annotation risk):** PP_3146, PP_4548, PP_5273, PP_2588 (class III aminotransferase), PP_2589 (aldehyde dehydrogenase family), spuC‑II/PP_5182 (polyamine:pyruvate transaminase — polyamine *catabolism/interconversion*, not biosynthesis).
- **Clearly mis-bucketed:** codA/PP_3189 (cytosine deaminase, pyrimidine salvage), PP_4983 (tryptophan 2‑monooxygenase), ldcC/PP_4140 (lysine decarboxylase → cadaverine), patD/PP_1481 and prr/PP_2801 (γ‑aminobutyraldehyde dehydrogenases, putrescine *catabolism*).

None of these should be marked as covering carboxyspermidine steps.

### The non-operative classical route

**PP_1867 (Q88LR2)** is annotated as a spermidine synthase (SpeE‑type, 251 aa, IPR029063). It is present but **cannot** drive de novo spermidine synthesis because its co‑substrate, decarboxylated SAM, is not made — there is **no SpeD (EC 4.1.1.50)** anywhere in the proteome. This mirrors the *Helicobacter pylori* precedent, where a spermidine‑synthase annotation coexists with an incomplete, non‑operative classical pathway ([PMID: 28648602](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28648602/)). PP_1867 may function in a salvage/interconversion context but should not be scored as the biosynthetic route.

## 5. Gaps, Ambiguities, and Likely Over-Annotations

**Resolved apparent gap (the key result).** At first pass CASDH looks missing because PP_2928 is labeled "saccharopine dehydrogenase." This is a **false gap**: the saccharopine‑dehydrogenase and CASDH activities share the same NADP‑binding fold family (IPR005097), and the syntenic + length + sequence‑identity evidence collectively identify PP_2928 as CASDH. Marking S3 as a gap would be an error; it should be **covered after re‑annotation**.

**Genus-level over-propagation risk (hard caveat).** *P. aeruginosa* PAO1 encodes the **classical** machinery — two SAM decarboxylases (speD/PA0654 Q9I5R7; speH/PA4773 Q9HV35) and two spermidine synthases (speE1/PA1687 Q9X6R0; speE2/PA4774 Q9HV34) — and has **zero** CASDC (EC 4.1.1.96) or CANSDH (EC 1.5.1.43). *P. putida* KT2440 is the inverse: zero SpeD, one SpeE‑type synthase (PP_1867), and a complete PP_2928–PP_2929 carboxyspermidine operon. Automated transfer of polyamine annotations from *P. aeruginosa* to *P. putida* (or vice versa) would be **wrong** despite same‑genus proximity. This is a concrete, documented anti‑over‑propagation example.

**Paralog ambiguities to flag.**
- Two agmatinase annotations (speB/PP_2196 and PP_4523) — resolve which is the physiological agmatinase.
- PP_2929 vs. the fold‑III decarboxylase paralogs (lysA Q88L58, speC Q88PI6): resolved here by the >20‑point identity margin, but worth recording so future automated pipelines do not reassign it.
- Multiple "oxidoreductase"/aminotransferase candidates (PP_3146, PP_4548, PP_5273, PP_2588) carry broad EC/GO terms and are prime over‑annotation candidates; none belong to this module.

**Broad EC/GO mappings.** spuC‑II/PP_5182 (polyamine:pyruvate transaminase) and the aldehyde‑dehydrogenase family proteins are polyamine‑*catabolic* and should not inflate biosynthetic module coverage.

## 6. Module and GO-Curation Recommendations

| Module step | Recommended status | Rationale |
|-------------|--------------------|-----------|
| Putrescine supply (S1) | **covered** | SpeA/AguA/SpeB/SpeC all present (candidate list) |
| ASA supply (S2) | **covered** | Asd/PP_1989 (housekeeping, EC 1.2.1.11) |
| Carboxyspermidine synthesis — CASDH (S3) | **covered — requires re-annotation of PP_2928** | family + synteny + 46.8% identity to *V. cholerae* CANSDH |
| Carboxyspermidine decarboxylation — CASDC (S4) | **covered** | PP_2929/nspC, 66% to reviewed nspC |
| Classical SpeD (dcSAM generation) | **not_expected_in_target_taxon** | zero EC 4.1.1.50 in proteome |
| Classical SpeE step | **not the operative route** | PP_1867 present but non‑functional without SpeD |

**Module-boundary verdict:** the carboxyspermidine spermidine‑biosynthesis module is **satisfiable** in *P. putida* KT2440. The generic module should explicitly model the classical vs. carboxyspermidine routes as **mutually exclusive alternatives** and record that KT2440 uses the latter.

**GO / annotation requests:**
- Re‑annotate **PP_2928** with GO:0034070 / EC 1.5.1.43 activity (carboxyspermidine dehydrogenase / "carboxynorspermidine dehydrogenase") and demote the saccharopine‑dehydrogenase label to a fold‑level note.
- Confirm **PP_2929** annotation as CASDC (EC 4.1.1.96) with the caveat that in vivo product is spermidine.
- Add a taxon‑scoped rule preventing propagation of classical SpeD/SpeE polyamine annotations into *P. putida*‑type genomes lacking SpeD.

**No new module document is required** — the existing alternative‑spermidine module fits — but the KT2440 instance needs the PP_2928 re‑annotation and the SpeD not‑expected flag to be recorded.

## 8. Key References

- **[PMID: 19196710](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19196710/)** — Lee et al. *An alternative polyamine biosynthetic pathway is widespread in bacteria and essential for biofilm formation in Vibrio cholerae.* Defines the ASA‑dependent route (fused DABA AT/DC + CANSDH + CANSDC) and shows deletion of either CANSDH or CANSDC abolishes both norspermidine and spermidine — the basis for requiring both PP_2928 and PP_2929.
- **[PMID: 22025614](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22025614/)** — *Alternative spermidine biosynthetic route… dominant polyamine pathway in human gut microbiota.* Establishes that lacking SpeD/spermidine synthase is the signature of carboxyspermidine users, that CASDH/CASDC are clustered with putrescine‑biosynthetic genes, and that the nspC ortholog makes spermidine (not norspermidine) in vivo.
- **[PMID: 36283333](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36283333/)** — *Structural analysis of carboxyspermidine dehydrogenase from Helicobacter pylori.* Defines the CASDH reaction (putrescine + ASA → carboxyspermidine, NADPH‑dependent) that PP_2928 is proposed to catalyze.
- **[PMID: 28648602](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28648602/)** — *Helicobacter pylori does not use spermidine synthase to produce spermidine.* Precedent that a spermidine‑synthase annotation can coexist with an incomplete, non‑operative classical pathway (parallels PP_1867 in a SpeD‑null genome).
- **[PMID: 35931895](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35931895/)** — Engineered *E. coli* co‑expressing HSD + CASDH + CASDC for spermidine production; independent confirmation of the two committed enzyme activities and their NADPH requirement.
- Supporting *P. putida* polyamine context: [PMID: 29886249](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29886249/) (P. putida modulation of host polyamines), [PMID: 26662997](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26662997/) (KT2440 spermidine/putrescine chemoreceptor binding), [PMID: 31451546](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31451546/) (arginine biosynthesis in P. putida).

## Limitations and Knowledge Gaps

- All KT2440‑specific assignments rest on **sequence homology, InterPro domains, protein length, and genomic synteny** — there is **no direct biochemical or genetic experiment in KT2440** confirming CASDH activity for PP_2928 or CASDC activity for PP_2929. Transfer from *V. cholerae*, *H. pylori*, and reviewed nspC orthologs is judged strong but remains inferential.
- The physiological product (spermidine vs. sym‑norspermidine) has not been measured in KT2440; the norspermidine branch appears absent, but this was inferred, not assayed.
- The role of the orphan spermidine synthase PP_1867 in a SpeD‑null background is unresolved.
- Putrescine‑supply redundancy (two agmatinases) was noted but the physiologically dominant route was not established.

## Proposed Follow-up Experiments / Actions

1. **Re-annotate PP_2928 as CASDH** in the curated record (highest‑value single action) and promote it to full `fetch-gene` review.
2. **Targeted deletions** of PP_2928 and PP_2929 in KT2440 with LC‑MS polyamine profiling — the definitive test (mirrors the *V. cholerae* knockouts, [PMID: 19196710](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19196710/)).
3. **In vitro assays**: express PP_2928 and test NADPH‑dependent condensation of putrescine + ASA → carboxyspermidine; express PP_2929 and test carboxyspermidine → spermidine.
4. **Metabolite confirmation**: quantify spermidine and check for sym‑norspermidine to confirm the operative diamine input.
5. **Add a taxon-scoped propagation guard** preventing classical SpeD/SpeE polyamine annotations from being transferred into SpeD‑null *P. putida*‑type genomes.
