Species-Aware Review: Bacterial RNase E RNA Degradosome in Pseudomonas putida KT2440

Target taxon: Pseudomonas putida KT2440 (PSEPK; NCBI taxon 160488; proteome UP000000556) Target bucket: KEGG ppu03018 "RNA degradation" (15 primary genes; 17 candidate genes) Commissioned module: Reusable bacterial RNase E-centered RNA degradosome Date: 2026-08-31 · Iterations: 1–2 of 3

1. Executive summary

The RNase E-centered RNA degradosome is present and satisfiable in P. putida KT2440, but the organism-specific composition diverges from the Escherichia coli textbook model, and the KEGG ppu03018 bucket is substantially broader than the commissioned module.

  • Core scaffold + endonuclease is solid. RNase E (rne, PP_1905) is present, essential, and — as in all Pseudomonadota — nucleates the degradosome through its C-terminal domain (CTD). Direct Pseudomonas evidence (from P. aeruginosa) shows the CTD binds the core partners PNPase and RhlB through short linear motifs (SLiMs) (40096066).
  • PNPase and a DEAD-box helicase are present (pnp PP_4708; rhlB PP_1295 plus paralogs). Which helicase is the bona fide degradosome partner in P. putida is ambiguous — Pseudomonads can use RhlE rather than RhlB, and can recruit the hydrolytic exoribonuclease RNase R in place of/alongside PNPase (15705581).
  • Enolase membership is homology-inferred and uncertain in this lineage; its glycolytic role is certain, its degradosome role in KT2440 is not demonstrated (contrast E. coli, 23927922).
  • The bucket over-reaches. recQ (DNA repair), rho (transcription termination), ppk/ppkB (polyphosphate), and groEL/dnaK (chaperones) are neighboring-process or over-propagated members that should be kept out of the degradosome-core module.
  • Direct KT2440 experimental evidence exists for RNase E, PNPase, RNase R (deletion phenotypes; 33089610) and for Hfq (Crc-Hfq catabolite repression; 24803210) — the latter clarifies that Hfq's characterized role here is translational regulation, not degradosome scaffolding.

Bottom line: Mark the four degradosome-core steps covered (with a candidate_uncertain flag on the enolase step and on helicase/exonuclease paralog identity). Recommend module_needs_revision on the fixed RhlB/PNPase wiring, not_expected_in_target_taxon/wrong-process for recQ, and separation of the 5′-trigger, 3′-decay, chaperone and polyphosphate members into neighboring modules.

2. Target-organism pathway definition

What the module includes (biochemical scope): the multi-protein machine that carries out bulk mRNA turnover and processing in Gram-negative bacteria — endonucleolytic initiation by RNase E, ATP-dependent unwinding of structured RNA by a DEAD-box helicase, and 3′→5′ exonucleolytic degradation by PNPase — physically organized on the intrinsically disordered RNase E CTD scaffold, which in Pseudomonads also tethers the complex to the inner membrane (40096066).

Neighboring processes to keep separate (do NOT fold into the degradosome-core module): - 5′-end deprotection / decay initiation — RppH (rppH, PP_5146): removes 5′-pyrophosphate to license RNase E cleavage; upstream trigger, not a core subunit (18202662). - 3′-end-dependent decay & tailing — RNase R (rnr, PP_4880) and poly(A) polymerase I (pcnB, PP_4697). - Transcription termination — Rho (rho, PP_5214). - DNA metabolism — RecQ (recQ, PP_4516): a DNA helicase; not RNA degradation. - Protein folding / stress — GroEL (groEL, PP_1361), DnaK (dnaK, PP_4727): chaperones (primary bucket ppu04156); DnaK is at most a stress-induced minor degradosome associate in E. coli (16139413). - Polyphosphate metabolism — PPK/PPK2 (ppk PP_5217, ppkB PP_0712): KEGG map03018 "others" box; peripheral. - Glycolysis — Enolase (eno, PP_1612): primary role in ppu00010.

Alternate names / database definitions: KEGG map03018 "RNA degradation" ≈ the "RNA degradosome" concept, but the KEGG map is deliberately inclusive (degradosome + exosome + accessory/others boxes). GO terms of interest: degradosome (GO:0030679 "cytoplasmic side"/complex terms), ribonuclease E activity (GO:0008995), 3′→5′ exoribonuclease/polyribonucleotide nucleotidyltransferase (GO:0004654), ATP-dependent RNA helicase (GO:0004004), RNA pyrophosphohydrolase (GO:0034353-adjacent Nudix activity).

4. Candidate genes and evidence

High-confidence degradosome-core genes

rne — Ribonuclease E, PP_1905 (Q88LM4). Role: endoribonuclease + degradosome scaffold (CTD). Evidence: strong. Directly studied in KT2440 (RNase E deletion/variant among a panel of RNase mutants, 33089610); Pseudomonas CTD scaffold function mapped in the close relative P. aeruginosa (40096066). Caveat: the Pseudomonas CTD is sequence-divergent from E. coli, so which SLiMs recruit which partners must be read from Pseudomonas data, not E. coli homology. Promote to full review.

pnp — PNPase (polyribonucleotide nucleotidyltransferase), PP_4708 (Q88DW0). Role: 3′→5′ phosphorolytic exoribonuclease; classic degradosome core partner and Pseudomonas CTD binder (40096066). Evidence: strong; KT2440 pnp deletion characterized (33089610). Caveat: PNPase has well-documented degradosome-independent functions (e.g., cold/oxidative stress in related genera; 23082859), so presence ≠ obligate degradosome participation. Promote to full review.

rhlB — ATP-dependent RNA helicase RhlB, PP_1295 (Q88NB7). Role: unwinds structured RNA to feed PNPase; Pseudomonas CTD binder (40096066). Evidence: moderate (homology + P. aeruginosa interaction data). Caveat: paralog ambiguity — see below. Promote to full review.

eno — Enolase, PP_1612 (Q88MF9). Role: glycolytic 2-phosphoglycerate dehydratase; in E. coli also a degradosome structural component (23927922 16139413). Evidence for degradosome role in Pseudomonas: weak/absent — the P. aeruginosa CTD map named PNPase and RhlB (not enolase) as core partners (40096066). Caveat: likely over-propagated as a "degradosome" annotation in this lineage; glycolytic annotation is solid. candidate_uncertain.

Accessory / neighboring RNA-metabolism genes (present, but not degradosome-core)

rnr — RNase R, PP_4880 (Q88DE6). Hydrolytic 3′→5′ exoribonuclease for structured RNA. Species-relevant twist: in P. syringae Lz4W, RNase R (not PNPase) co-purifies with RNase E and RhlE — a "degradosome of novel structure" (15705581). KT2440 rnr deletion characterized (33089610). Relevant to step 4; promote to full review to resolve whether it is a KT2440 degradosome partner.

rppH — RNA pyrophosphohydrolase, PP_5146 (Q88CN4). 5′-triphosphate→monophosphate deprotection that licenses RNase E cleavage (18202662; H. pylori ortholog 27974459). Status: upstream decay-initiation step, adjacent to but outside the degradosome core. Homology-only in KT2440.

pcnB — Poly(A) polymerase I, PP_4697 (Q88DX1). Adds 3′ poly(A) tails that mark RNA for exonucleolytic decay. Accessory to 3′-decay; homology-based.

hfq — RNA-binding protein Hfq, PP_4894 (Q88DD3). In KT2440, characterized role is Crc-Hfq catabolite repression (binds AAnAAnAA CA motifs, inhibits translation; regulates CrcZ/CrcY sRNAs) — 24803210 27777366 37348756. Its degradosome/decay role is not the primary demonstrated function here. candidate_uncertain for this module.

Helicase paralogs deaD PP_1868, rhlE PP_4980, rhlE-I/RhpA PP_4766. Additional DEAD-box helicases (EC 3.6.4.13). Create genuine ambiguity about which helicase serves the degradosome (RhlB vs RhlE, the latter favored in Pseudomonas per 15705581).

Likely over-propagated / wrong-process bucket members

  • recQ — DNA helicase RecQ, PP_4516 (EC 5.6.2.4). DNA repair/recombination. Not an RNA-degradation enzyme; over-propagation into ppu03018. → not_expected_in_target_taxon for the degradosome module (wrong process).
  • rho — Rho, PP_5214. Transcription termination factor. Adjacent (transcription), not degradosome.
  • ppk PP_5217, ppkB PP_0712. Polyphosphate kinases (KEGG "others" box). Peripheral.
  • groEL PP_1361, dnaK PP_4727. Chaperones (primary bucket ppu04156). DnaK is only a stress-induced minor degradosome associate in E. coli, not a structural subunit (16139413).

5. Gaps, ambiguities, and likely over-annotations

  1. Helicase identity gap (step 3). RhlB is annotated, but Pseudomonas degradosomes can use RhlE (15705581). With four DEAD-box paralogs (rhlB, rhlE, rhpA, deaD), the actual KT2440 degradosome helicase is unresolved. → candidate_uncertain.
  2. Exonuclease substitution (step 4). PNPase is present, but RNase R may substitute or co-participate in Pseudomonas (15705581). The generic module's rigid "PNPase" wiring may under-represent KT2440. → module_needs_revision.
  3. Enolase membership (step 1). No KT2440 evidence that enolase is a structural degradosome subunit; homology transfer from E. coli is weak across Pseudomonadota. → candidate_uncertain; guard against auto-propagating a degradosome GO term to PP_1612.
  4. Bucket over-inclusion. recQ (DNA), rho (transcription), ppk/ppkB (polyphosphate), groEL/dnaK (chaperones) inflate the module. Keep separate.
  5. Divergent physiology. KT2440 RNase deletion phenotypes "diverged significantly" from E. coli (33089610) — a general caution that E. coli functional annotations transfer imperfectly.
  6. RNase E regulators not captured by the bucket. RraA-type regulators bind the RNase E regulatory domain and modulate its activity; an RraA-like protein exists in P. putida F1 (structurally characterized alongside HMG/CHA aldolases, 24359411). These regulatory inputs are relevant to degradosome biology but are absent from the ppu03018 candidate list — a note for curators rather than a module step. (Not present as a KT2440 candidate gene; homology-level relevance only.)

Module-boundary recommendations: - The generic RNase E module is broadly correct but should (a) allow RhlE as an alternative helicase player, (b) allow RNase R as an alternative/added exonuclease player for Pseudomonads, and (c) demote enolase to an optional, lineage-variable structural participant. - Consider a separate "5′-end-dependent decay initiation" module (RppH) and a "3′-end decay/polyadenylation" module (PcnB + RNase R + PNPase), which the KEGG bucket currently conflates with the degradosome.

GO-curation notes: avoid propagating degradosome-complex GO terms onto eno (PP_1612), the chaperones, recQ, rho, and the polyphosphate kinases without direct evidence. A GO term / annotation request may be warranted to capture the Pseudomonas-specific RNase R-containing degradosome variant if curation needs to represent it.
