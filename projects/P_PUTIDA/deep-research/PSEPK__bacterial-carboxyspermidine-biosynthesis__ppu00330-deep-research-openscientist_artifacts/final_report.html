Commissioned Review: Bacterial Carboxyspermidine Biosynthesis in Pseudomonas putida KT2440

Target taxon: Pseudomonas putida KT2440 (PSEPK; NCBI taxon 160488; proteome UP000000556) Nominal bucket: KEGG ppu00330 — "Arginine and proline metabolism" (30 primary genes; area: amino_acid_metabolism) Review focus: the alternative (carboxyspermidine) route of spermidine biosynthesis and its satisfiability in the target strain.

1. Executive Summary

Pseudomonas putida KT2440 synthesizes the polyamine spermidine by the alternative carboxyspermidine pathway, not by the classical aminopropyltransferase route used by E. coli and by its own genus-mate P. aeruginosa. The decisive genomic signal is a clean absence: the KT2440 proteome (UP000000556) contains no S‑adenosylmethionine decarboxylase (SpeD, EC 4.1.1.50). Because the classical route requires SpeD to generate the decarboxylated‑SAM aminopropyl donor consumed by spermidine synthase, the lone spermidine synthase annotation in the genome (PP_1867 / Q88LR2) cannot support de novo spermidine synthesis on its own. Lacking SpeD while still making spermidine is the recognized molecular signature of organisms that run the carboxyspermidine route (PMID: 22025614).

The two committed enzymes of the carboxyspermidine route are present and, importantly, genomically adjacent — the hallmark cluster organization described for this pathway. PP_2929 (nspC / Q88IS0) is a bona fide carboxyspermidine/carboxynorspermidine decarboxylase (CASDC/CANSDC, EC 4.1.1.96): it is 66% identical to a reviewed nspC ortholog and only ~28% identical to its own genome's fold‑III PLP decarboxylase paralogs, so it is not an over‑propagated annotation. Immediately upstream, PP_2928 (Q88IS1), currently mis‑annotated as a "saccharopine dehydrogenase," is in fact the carboxyspermidine dehydrogenase (CASDH, EC 1.5.1.43) — it belongs to the correct saccharopine‑dehydrogenase/homospermidine‑synthase family (IPR005097), is the right length (414 aa), sits directly next to nspC, and is 46.8% identical over its full length to the experimentally characterized Vibrio cholerae CANSDH (VC_1624). PP_2928 is not in the supplied candidate list; it is the single most consequential curation action arising from this review.

For module curation, the carboxyspermidine spermidine‑biosynthesis module is satisfiable in KT2440: putrescine is supplied redundantly (SpeA/AguA/SpeB/SpeC, all in the candidate set), aspartate‑β‑semialdehyde is supplied by Asd (PP_1989), CASDH is PP_2928, and CASDC is PP_2929. The classical SpeD/SpeE step should be marked not_expected_in_target_taxon. Critically, polyamine biosynthesis annotations must not be transferred from P. aeruginosa to P. putida: the two species use opposite routes, making this a concrete anti‑over‑propagation caveat within a single genus.

2. Target-Organism Pathway Definition

What the process is

"Carboxyspermidine biosynthesis" is the committed, two‑enzyme core of the alternative (SAM‑independent) spermidine biosynthetic pathway. In this route the aminopropyl donor is L‑aspartate‑β‑semialdehyde (ASA) rather than decarboxylated S‑adenosylmethionine. The core chemistry is:

  1. CASDH (EC 1.5.1.43) condenses putrescine + ASA in an NADPH‑dependent reductive amination to form carboxyspermidine (PMID: 36283333).
  2. CASDC (EC 4.1.1.96), a PLP‑dependent fold‑III decarboxylase, removes the carboxyl group to yield spermidine.

When the same two enzymes act on 1,3‑diaminopropane instead of putrescine they make sym‑norspermidine via carboxynorspermidine — hence the CANSDH/CANSDC nomenclature. The enzymes are substrate‑promiscuous; which polyamine is produced in vivo depends on the diamine supplied (PMID: 22025614).

Boundaries — what to keep separate

  • Putrescine supply (arginine/agmatine and ornithine decarboxylase branches) feeds this module but is upstream metabolism, largely mapped under KEGG ppu00330-adjacent arginine metabolism. It should be treated as substrate supply, not part of the committed carboxyspermidine core.
  • Aspartate‑β‑semialdehyde supply (Asd, EC 1.2.1.11) is shared with lysine/DAP and threonine/methionine biosynthesis; it is a co‑substrate node, not part of the module proper.
  • Classical spermidine synthesis (SpeD EC 4.1.1.50 + SpeE EC 2.5.1.16) is the competing route and should be modeled as a mutually exclusive alternative, marked not‑expected here.
  • The nominal bucket ppu00330 "Arginine and proline metabolism" is an overview map that co‑locates dozens of only loosely related reactions (arginine succinyltransferase AST pathway, proline catabolism PutA, 5‑oxoprolinases, creatinase, opine oxidase, etc.). Most of the 39 supplied candidates belong to those neighboring processes and are not part of carboxyspermidine biosynthesis. The review deliberately narrows to the polyamine sub‑network.

Alternate names / database definitions

  • CASDH = carboxyspermidine dehydrogenase; CANSDH = carboxynorspermidine dehydrogenase (same enzyme family, EC 1.5.1.43).
  • CASDC/CANSDC = carboxyspermidine/carboxynorspermidine decarboxylase, gene symbol nspC, EC 4.1.1.96.
  • The pathway is variously called the "alternative," "carboxyspermidine," or "aspartate‑semialdehyde" spermidine pathway. It is not the classical "aminopropyltransferase" route.

4. Candidate Genes and Evidence

High-confidence, module-defining genes

PP_2929 — nspC / CASDC (Q88IS0, EC 4.1.1.96) — COVERED, high confidence. This is the decarboxylase that produces spermidine. Global Needleman–Wunsch alignments place it firmly in the CANSDC/CASDC family: 66.2% identity to reviewed Herminiimonas arsenicoxydans nspC (Q5QCP2), 51.0% to V. cholerae nspC (Q9KRL4), and 41.7% to Campylobacter jejuni nspC (A8FNH9). By contrast, identity to the co‑encoded fold‑III PLP decarboxylase paralogs in KT2440 is only 28.3% (lysA/DAP decarboxylase, Q88L58) and 29.2% (speC/ornithine decarboxylase, Q88PI6). The >20‑percentage‑point margin over the paralogs shows PP_2929 is a genuine CASDC, not an over‑propagated fold‑III annotation. Evidence type: strong homology to reviewed/experimentally validated orthologs; direct KT2440 experiment absent but transfer is strong. Curation caveat: because CASDC and CANSDC are the same enzyme, the label should note substrate promiscuity; the in vivo product is spermidine when putrescine is the diamine (PMID: 22025614).

PP_2928 — CASDH (Q88IS1, EC 1.5.1.43) — NOT in candidate list; PROMOTE and RE-ANNOTATE. PP_2928 is currently annotated "saccharopine dehydrogenase," which is why it was excluded from the polyamine candidate set. Three independent lines of evidence identify it as the missing CASDH: (i) it carries IPR005097 (Saccharopine dehydrogenase/homospermidine synthase, NADP‑dependent domain) plus IPR032095 and the IPR036291 NAD(P)‑binding superfamily fold — the exact family to which characterized CASDH/CANSDH proteins belong; (ii) it is 414 aa, matching CASDH references (V. cholerae VC_1624 Q9KRL3 = 414 aa; V. alginolyticus P0DPE4 = 414 aa); and (iii) it sits immediately adjacent to PP_2929/nspC, reproducing the CASDH–CASDC gene cluster reported across bacteria (PMID: 22025614). A full‑length global alignment to V. cholerae CANSDH gives 46.8% identity (203/434 columns) — well above over‑propagation noise and consistent with true orthology. Evidence type: strong homology + syntenic context; transfer strong. This is the single most important curation action from the review.

The non-operative classical route

PP_1867 (Q88LR2) is annotated as a spermidine synthase (SpeE‑type, 251 aa, IPR029063). It is present but cannot drive de novo spermidine synthesis because its co‑substrate, decarboxylated SAM, is not made — there is no SpeD (EC 4.1.1.50) anywhere in the proteome. This mirrors the Helicobacter pylori precedent, where a spermidine‑synthase annotation coexists with an incomplete, non‑operative classical pathway (PMID: 28648602). PP_1867 may function in a salvage/interconversion context but should not be scored as the biosynthetic route.

5. Gaps, Ambiguities, and Likely Over-Annotations

Resolved apparent gap (the key result). At first pass CASDH looks missing because PP_2928 is labeled "saccharopine dehydrogenase." This is a false gap: the saccharopine‑dehydrogenase and CASDH activities share the same NADP‑binding fold family (IPR005097), and the syntenic + length + sequence‑identity evidence collectively identify PP_2928 as CASDH. Marking S3 as a gap would be an error; it should be covered after re‑annotation.

Genus-level over-propagation risk (hard caveat). P. aeruginosa PAO1 encodes the classical machinery — two SAM decarboxylases (speD/PA0654 Q9I5R7; speH/PA4773 Q9HV35) and two spermidine synthases (speE1/PA1687 Q9X6R0; speE2/PA4774 Q9HV34) — and has zero CASDC (EC 4.1.1.96) or CANSDH (EC 1.5.1.43). P. putida KT2440 is the inverse: zero SpeD, one SpeE‑type synthase (PP_1867), and a complete PP_2928–PP_2929 carboxyspermidine operon. Automated transfer of polyamine annotations from P. aeruginosa to P. putida (or vice versa) would be wrong despite same‑genus proximity. This is a concrete, documented anti‑over‑propagation example.

Paralog ambiguities to flag. - Two agmatinase annotations (speB/PP_2196 and PP_4523) — resolve which is the physiological agmatinase. - PP_2929 vs. the fold‑III decarboxylase paralogs (lysA Q88L58, speC Q88PI6): resolved here by the >20‑point identity margin, but worth recording so future automated pipelines do not reassign it. - Multiple "oxidoreductase"/aminotransferase candidates (PP_3146, PP_4548, PP_5273, PP_2588) carry broad EC/GO terms and are prime over‑annotation candidates; none belong to this module.

Broad EC/GO mappings. spuC‑II/PP_5182 (polyamine:pyruvate transaminase) and the aldehyde‑dehydrogenase family proteins are polyamine‑catabolic and should not inflate biosynthetic module coverage.

Defines the ASA‑dependent route (fused DABA AT/DC + CANSDH + CANSDC) and shows deletion of either CANSDH or CANSDC abolishes both norspermidine and spermidine — the basis for requiring both PP_2928 and PP_2929.
  • PMID: 22025614 — Alternative spermidine biosynthetic route… dominant polyamine pathway in human gut microbiota. Establishes that lacking SpeD/spermidine synthase is the signature of carboxyspermidine users, that CASDH/CASDC are clustered with putrescine‑biosynthetic genes, and that the nspC ortholog makes spermidine (not norspermidine) in vivo.
  • PMID: 36283333 — Structural analysis of carboxyspermidine dehydrogenase from Helicobacter pylori. Defines the CASDH reaction (putrescine + ASA → carboxyspermidine, NADPH‑dependent) that PP_2928 is proposed to catalyze.
  • PMID: 28648602 — Helicobacter pylori does not use spermidine synthase to produce spermidine. Precedent that a spermidine‑synthase annotation can coexist with an incomplete, non‑operative classical pathway (parallels PP_1867 in a SpeD‑null genome).

Limitations and Knowledge Gaps

  • All KT2440‑specific assignments rest on sequence homology, InterPro domains, protein length, and genomic synteny — there is no direct biochemical or genetic experiment in KT2440 confirming CASDH activity for PP_2928 or CASDC activity for PP_2929. Transfer from V. cholerae, H. pylori, and reviewed nspC orthologs is judged strong but remains inferential.
  • The physiological product (spermidine vs. sym‑norspermidine) has not been measured in KT2440; the norspermidine branch appears absent, but this was inferred, not assayed.
  • The role of the orphan spermidine synthase PP_1867 in a SpeD‑null background is unresolved.
  • Putrescine‑supply redundancy (two agmatinases) was noted but the physiologically dominant route was not established.
