rbsD (b3748) and growth on D-ribose in iML1515: a constraint-based modelling audit

Summary

The two model variants give different answers. In the E. coli K‑12 MG1655 genome‑scale model iML1515 as published by BiGG Models, gene b3748 (rbsD) is not required for growth on D‑ribose: the wild type and the b3748 single‑gene deletion both grow at 0.688913 h⁻¹ on ribose minimal medium, so the KO/WT ratio is 1.00. This is because b3748 appears in the published model in exactly one place — as one of three redundant isozyme complexes inside the gene‑protein‑reaction (GPR) rule of the ribose ABC‑transporter reaction RIBabcpp — and deleting it disables zero reactions. When the model is reannotated so that rbsD is represented by the enzyme it is actually believed to catalyse — D‑ribose pyranase, EC 5.4.99.62 (β‑D‑ribopyranose ⇌ β‑D‑ribofuranose) — with the furanose product feeding ribokinase and b3748 removed from the transporter GPR, b3748 becomes strictly essential: WT = 0.688913 h⁻¹, KO = 0 (KO/WT = 0.00). The glucose‑minimal baseline is 0.876997 h⁻¹, matching the published iML1515 value and validating the pipeline.

Provenance and honesty caveat. The task asked for COBRApy. In this sandbox the Python import allowlist blocked import cobra ("Import of 'cobra' is not allowed"), so COBRApy itself could not be used. Rather than fabricate a number, every growth rate below was computed by solving the identical linear program directly with SciPy HiGHS (scipy.optimize.linprog, method highs, scipy 1.17.1), built from the stoichiometric matrix, reversibility and bounds parsed from the exact BiGG iML1515.json (3,062,491 bytes). The pipeline was validated against the published glucose growth rate (0.876997 h⁻¹) and against positive controls (deleting the true ribokinase gene b3752 correctly kills growth on ribose). The numbers are therefore genuine solver outputs, not reasoned estimates — but they were produced with HiGHS through a hand‑built LP rather than through the COBRApy API, and that substitution is disclosed here in full.

Bottom line for interpretation. The published model's "still grows" call happens to match the experimental fact that rbsD mutants can still grow on ribose — but it does so for the wrong mechanistic reason (rbsD is miscast as a redundant transporter subunit, a 1986‑era annotation). The strict reannotation is mechanistically faithful to modern enzymology but over‑predicts essentiality, because it ignores the spontaneous (non‑enzymatic) mutarotation of ribose that lets rbsD mutants survive. A third, "spontaneous‑bypass" variant reconciles the two and reproduces the true phenotype (KO/WT = 1.0 by a correct mechanism).

Key findings

Finding 1 — In the published model, rbsD is dispensable on ribose (KO/WT = 1.00)

In the BiGG iML1515.json, gene b3748 occurs in the model only once: inside the GPR of the ribose ABC transport reaction RIBabcpp (periplasm→cytoplasm ribose transport). The published pathway is EX_rib__D_e → RIBtex (porin) → RIBabcpp (ABC import) → rib__D_c → RBK (ribokinase, b3752) → r5p_c → pentose‑phosphate / biomass. The published RIBabcpp GPR is a disjunction of three alternative isozyme complexes:

RIBabcpp GPR (published):
  (b4230 and b4227 and b4485 and b4231)      # generic ABC transporter complex
  or (b3750 and b3751 and b3749 and b3748)   # rbs operon complex containing rbsD
  or (b4088 and b4086 and b4087)             # third alternative complex

Because two other complexes can carry RIBabcpp, deleting b3748 leaves the reaction fully functional. The single‑gene deletion therefore knocks out zero reactions, and growth is unchanged:

  • WT ribose growth = 0.688913 h⁻¹
  • b3748 KO ribose growth = 0.688913 h⁻¹
  • KO/WT = 1.000

WT flux distribution on ribose (published model): EX_rib__D_e = −10, RIBtex = 10, RIBabcpp = 10, RBK = 10 — i.e., ribose enters through the transporter and is phosphorylated directly by ribokinase, with no distinct pyranase step present at all. This finding was recorded as F001.

Crucially, the literature confirms rbsD is not a transporter subunit — the high‑affinity ribose transporter is RbsABC, and RbsD/RbsK operate on the sugar after transport (PMID: 23651393):

"RbsABC forms the ABC-type high-affinity d-ribose transporter, while RbsD and RbsK are involved in the conversion of d-ribose into d-ribose 5-phosphate."

So the placement of b3748 inside the RIBabcpp GPR is an annotation error: rbsD is doing enzymatic conversion, not transport.

Finding 2 — Reannotating rbsD as D-ribose pyranase makes it essential (KO/WT = 0.00)

The reannotated variant implements the modern functional assignment. Three edits were made to a fresh copy of the model (the loaded model was not mutated in place across experiments):

  1. Added metabolite ribfur__D_c (β‑D‑ribofuranose, C5H10O5, cytosol).
  2. Added reaction RBSDpyr "D‑ribose pyranase (EC 5.4.99.62)": rib__D_c ⇌ ribfur__D_c, bounds [−1000, 1000], GPR = b3748.
  3. Modified RBK substrate from atp_c + rib__D_c → adp_c + h_c + r5p_c to atp_c + ribfur__D_c → adp_c + h_c + r5p_c (GPR unchanged = b3752). Since ribfur__D_c is produced only by RBSDpyr, the phosphorylating step now depends on the pyranase product.
  4. Removed b3748 from the RIBabcpp GPR (the transporter complex disjunct (b3750 and b3751 and b3749 and b3748) had b3748 deleted).

  • KO/WT = 0.000

The glucose baseline is unchanged at 0.876997 h⁻¹ (the edits touch only the ribose route). This finding was recorded as F002 and is justified by:

PMID: 21276853:

"Ribose pyranase RbsD catalyzes the conversion of ribose from the pyranose to furanose form. This is the key step for substrate supply to ribokinase RbsK, which converts ribose to ribose-5-phosphate for further metabolism."

PMID: 15060078:

"We show that RbsD catalyzes the pyran to furan conversion of ribose"

Finding 3 — Deletion machinery validated, and an alternative implementation restores KO/WT = 1.0

To confirm the flip in Finding 2 reflects biology and not a broken deletion routine, positive and negative controls were run on the published model on ribose medium:

  • Deleting b3752 (rbsK / ribokinase) → disables RBK → growth = 0 (correctly flagged essential).
  • Deleting b3748 (rbsD) → growth = 0.688913 (unaffected, as in F001).
  • Deleting an unrelated gene b0688 → growth = 0.688913 (unaffected).

This confirms the GPR‑based single‑deletion routine correctly identifies genuinely essential genes while leaving redundant/irrelevant ones untouched. Recorded as F003.

The reannotation in (b) can be implemented in more than one defensible way, as the task anticipates. The strict implementation (Finding 2) assumes ribose can be phosphorylated only after enzymatic pyranase conversion, giving KO/WT = 0. An equally defensible spontaneous‑bypass implementation adds a gene‑free reaction RIBMUTAROT_spont (rib__D_c ⇌ ribfur__D_c, no GPR, bounds [−1000, 1000]) in parallel with RBSDpyr, representing the slow non‑enzymatic mutarotation that occurs in solution. This variant was explicitly built and solved:

  • WT = 0.688913 h⁻¹ (routes flux through the enzymatic RBSDpyr)
  • b3748 KO = 0.688913 h⁻¹ (routes the full flux of 10 through the spontaneous bypass; RBSDpyr flux = 0, spont = 10, RBK = 10)
  • KO/WT = 1.000

So the KO answer for the reannotated model is implementation‑dependent: strict → 0.0; with spontaneous mutarotation → 1.0. The strict version is reported as variant (b) because the task explicitly asks to make the phosphorylating step depend on the pyranase product and to remove b3748 elsewhere; the spontaneous‑bypass version is the more biologically faithful one (see Interpretation).

Finding 4 — The published mis-annotation traces to 1986

The reason b3748 sits in a transporter GPR at all is historical. Bell et al. 1986 sequenced rbsD/rbsA/rbsC and described them as encoding transport components (PMID: 3011793):

"These genes encode components of the high affinity ribose transport system in Escherichia coli"

The functional reassignment of RbsD to pyran→furan conversion came ~18 years later (Ryu et al. 2004, 15060078), and Shimada et al. 2013 (23651393) explicitly separates the transporter (RbsABC) from the RbsD/RbsK conversion step. iML1515 inherited the older transporter placement, which is the annotation the reannotation corrects. Recorded as F004.

Mechanistic model / interpretation

The two competing wiring diagrams

PUBLISHED iML1515 (b3748 cast as a redundant transporter subunit)
  D-ribose(e) --RIBtex--> D-ribose(p) --RIBabcpp--> rib__D_c --RBK[b3752]--> R5P --> PPP --> biomass
                                         ^
                                         | GPR = complexA OR (rbs complex incl. b3748) OR complexC
                                         |  (three redundant complexes; b3748 optional)
  Delete b3748 -> RIBabcpp still carried by complexA/complexC -> growth UNCHANGED (KO/WT = 1.0)

REANNOTATED, STRICT (b3748 = D-ribose pyranase)
  D-ribose(e) --RIBtex--> D-ribose(p) --RIBabcpp--> rib__D_c(pyranose) --RBSDpyr[b3748]--> ribfur__D_c --RBK[b3752]--> R5P --> biomass
                                                                          ^ sole source of furanose
  Delete b3748 -> no RBSDpyr -> no ribfur__D_c -> RBK starved -> NO GROWTH (KO/WT = 0.0)

REANNOTATED + SPONTANEOUS BYPASS (biologically faithful)
  ... rib__D_c --RBSDpyr[b3748]------\
                                      >--> ribfur__D_c --RBK[b3752]--> R5P --> biomass
      rib__D_c --RIBMUTAROT_spont----/  (slow, gene-free)
  Delete b3748 -> spontaneous mutarotation still supplies furanose -> GROWTH (KO/WT = 1.0)

Does the published prediction match experiment?

Experimentally, rbsD single mutants remain able to grow on ribose — the pyranase merely accelerates a mutarotation that also happens spontaneously in aqueous solution, so ribokinase is still supplied, just more slowly. The committed, indispensable catabolic step is ribokinase (RbsK / b3752), not rbsD. Therefore:

  • Published model (KO/WT = 1.0): the categorical answer (still grows) matches experiment, but for the wrong reason — it treats rbsD as a redundant transporter subunit rather than a metabolic enzyme.
  • Strict reannotation (KO/WT = 0.0): mechanistically faithful about what rbsD is, but it over‑predicts essentiality by assuming enzyme catalysis is the only route to the furanose. This contradicts the observed viability of rbsD mutants.
  • Spontaneous‑bypass reannotation (KO/WT = 1.0): matches both the phenotype and the mechanism, and is the recommended representation.

The disagreement between (a) and (b) is thus real and instructive: it is a textbook case of a genome‑scale model getting the right growth call for the wrong reason. An automated gene‑essentiality screen using the strict reannotation would flag rbsD as a novel essential gene on ribose — a false positive that only the spontaneous‑mutarotation term prevents.

Caveat on the phenotype citation. Despite targeted PubMed searches across the investigation, a primary paper reporting a growth curve of an E. coli ΔrbsD single mutant on D‑ribose as sole carbon source was not retrieved. The statement that rbsD mutants remain viable on ribose is therefore grounded in the enzymology (spontaneous mutarotation supplies the furanose; RbsK is the committed step) and general gene‑essentiality knowledge, and is flagged here as the one uncited phenotype claim rather than being backed by a fabricated PMID.

The code that was run and its raw output

COBRApy could not be imported in the sandbox (import cobra blocked by the allowlist), so the LP was constructed directly from the BiGG iML1515.json and solved with SciPy HiGHS. The code below is the verbatim runnable script (saved as /data/rbsd_iML1515_fba.py).

# COBRApy is NOT importable in this sandbox (import allowlist blocks 'cobra').
# FBA implemented directly on the official BiGG iML1515 JSON using
# scipy.optimize.linprog (HiGHS). Validated vs published glucose rate 0.877/h.
import json, requests, numpy as np, scipy
from scipy.sparse import lil_matrix, csr_matrix
from scipy.optimize import linprog

URL="http://bigg.ucsd.edu/static/models/iML1515.json"
RAW=requests.get(URL,timeout=180,headers={"User-Agent":"Mozilla/5.0"}).content
BASE=json.loads(RAW)

def build_lp(model):
    mets=[m["id"] for m in model["metabolites"]]; mi={x:i for i,x in enumerate(mets)}
    rxns=[r["id"] for r in model["reactions"]]; ri={x:j for j,x in enumerate(rxns)}
    S=lil_matrix((len(mets),len(rxns))); nR=len(rxns)
    lb=np.zeros(nR); ub=np.zeros(nR); c=np.zeros(nR); gprs=[]
    for j,rx in enumerate(model["reactions"]):
        lb[j]=rx["lower_bound"]; ub[j]=rx["upper_bound"]
        for mid,co in rx["metabolites"].items(): S[mi[mid],j]=co
        if rx.get("objective_coefficient",0): c[j]=rx["objective_coefficient"]
        gprs.append(rx.get("gene_reaction_rule",""))
    return rxns,ri,csr_matrix(S),lb,ub,c,gprs

def gpr_active(gpr,deleted,genes):
    if not gpr.strip(): return True
    ns={g:True for g in genes}
    for g in deleted: ns[g]=False
    return bool(eval(gpr,{"__builtins__":{}},ns))

def solve(S,lb,ub,c):
    r=linprog(-c,A_eq=S,b_eq=np.zeros(S.shape[0]),bounds=list(zip(lb,ub)),method="highs")
    return (-r.fun if r.success else 0.0), r.status

def set_medium(ri,lb,carbon_ex,carbon_lb=-10.0):
    lb=lb.copy()
    for e in ["EX_glc__D_e","EX_rib__D_e"]:
        if e in ri: lb[ri[e]]=0.0
    lb[ri[carbon_ex]]=carbon_lb; return lb

def run(model,carbon_ex,deleted=()):
    genes=[g["id"] for g in model["genes"]]
    rxns,ri,S,lb,ub,c,gprs=build_lp(model)
    lb=set_medium(ri,lb,carbon_ex); ub=ub.copy(); dis=[]
    if deleted:
        for j,g in enumerate(gprs):
            if not gpr_active(g,set(deleted),genes): lb[j]=0.0;ub[j]=0.0;dis.append(rxns[j])
    gr,st=solve(S,lb,ub,c); return gr,st,dis

# sanity: glucose minimal
print("GLUCOSE baseline WT: %.6f h^-1"%run(BASE,"EX_glc__D_e")[0])

# (a) published
wtA=run(BASE,"EX_rib__D_e")[0]
koA,_,disA=run(BASE,"EX_rib__D_e",deleted=["b3748"])
print("(a) PUBLISHED   ribose WT=%.6f  KO=%.6f  KO/WT=%.4f  disabled=%s"%(wtA,koA,koA/wtA,disA))

# (b) reannotated: rbsD = D-ribose pyranase EC 5.4.99.62
R=json.loads(RAW)
gr=lambda rid:next(x for x in R["reactions"] if x["id"]==rid)
R["metabolites"].append({"id":"ribfur__D_c","name":"beta-D-ribofuranose","compartment":"c",
                         "formula":"C5H10O5","charge":0,"annotation":{},"notes":{}})
R["reactions"].append({"id":"RBSDpyr","name":"D-ribose pyranase (EC 5.4.99.62)",
    "metabolites":{"rib__D_c":-1.0,"ribfur__D_c":1.0},"lower_bound":-1000.0,"upper_bound":1000.0,
    "gene_reaction_rule":"b3748","subsystem":"Ribose utilization","notes":{},
    "annotation":{"ec-code":["5.4.99.62"]}})
gr("RBK")["metabolites"]={"atp_c":-1.0,"adp_c":1.0,"h_c":1.0,"r5p_c":1.0,"ribfur__D_c":-1.0}
gr("RIBabcpp")["gene_reaction_rule"]=("(b4230 and b4227 and b4485 and b4231) or "
                                      "(b3750 and b3751 and b3749) or (b4088 and b4086 and b4087)")
wtB=run(R,"EX_rib__D_e")[0]
koB,stB,disB=run(R,"EX_rib__D_e",deleted=["b3748"])
print("(b) REANNOTATED ribose WT=%.6f  KO=%.6f  KO/WT=%.4f  disabled=%s  (KO LP status=%s)"
      %(wtB,koB,(koB/wtB if wtB else 0.0),disB,stB))

Limitations and knowledge gaps

  1. COBRApy was not usable. The task explicitly asked for COBRApy; the sandbox allowlist blocked it ("Import of 'cobra' is not allowed"). The LP was rebuilt from the JSON and solved with HiGHS. While the pipeline reproduces the published glucose growth rate exactly (a strong validation), it does not exercise COBRApy's single_gene_deletion / find_gene_knockout_reactions code paths. A confirmatory run in a COBRApy environment is the single most important follow‑up.
  2. GPR evaluation was implemented manually. Gene deletion was modelled by evaluating each Boolean GPR with the target gene set False and disabling reactions whose GPR becomes False. For b3748 this is unambiguous (it appears in one OR‑clause of one reaction), but COBRApy's parser is the reference implementation.
  3. The reannotation is not unique. As shown, strict vs. spontaneous‑bypass gives 0.0 vs. 1.0 for the KO. Both are defensible; the report states the choice, why, and the alternative outcome, as required.
  4. FBA predicts capability, not rate faithfully. The 0.688913 h⁻¹ ribose growth is an FBA optimum with a fixed 10 mmol gDW⁻¹ h⁻¹ uptake cap; it is a modelling quantity, not a measured doubling rate. Only the ratios and the essential/ dispensable calls should be over‑interpreted, not the absolute rate. Growth "0" in variant (b) reflects LP infeasibility under ATP maintenance — no feasible steady‑state flux supports biomass/maintenance — interpreted as no growth.
  5. No primary phenotype PMID for the rbsD KO. Despite multiple targeted PubMed searches, no paper reporting the ΔrbsD single‑mutant ribose growth curve was retrieved; that phenotype statement rests on enzymology and essentiality knowledge and is flagged as uncited rather than backed by a fabricated citation.
