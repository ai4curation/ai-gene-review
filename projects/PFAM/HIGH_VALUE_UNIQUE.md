## Question

The [headroom analysis](HEADROOM.md) counted Pfam families that get no GO term through InterPro2GO. That count overstates the opportunity in two ways. Many of the obvious mappings are binding terms, which say little about what a protein does. And a family whose own InterPro entry has no GO term may still get the function through a different InterPro entry that the same proteins match. This page keeps only the candidates that survive both objections.

## Method

1. **Function of a member.** Its GO molecular-function terms plus the GO terms that ec2go gives for its complete EC numbers, with all ancestors.
2. **High value.** The most specific terms carried by the required share of a family's reviewed members. Terms that sit only in the binding branch are dropped, and so are terms common enough to be uninformative.
3. **Unique.** For each member, the union of interpro2go terms over *every* InterPro entry the protein matches. A member is supplied when that union holds the term or a descendant. This is where a function arriving through a separate, unlinked entry is caught.
4. **Where the gain lands.** For shortlisted pairs, UniProt is asked how many unreviewed proteins match the family and how many already carry the term or a descendant from any pipeline.
5. **Route.** `UNINTEGRATED`: in no InterPro entry, so only a Pfam-level mapping reaches it. `SOLE_SIGNATURE`: the only signature of its entry, so adding the term to interpro2go on that entry is equivalent. `SHARED_ENTRY`: the entry has other signatures; the entry-level purity says whether the term still holds there.

Limits: purity is measured on reviewed members only, and an unreviewed protein may carry the domain in a different architecture. A Swiss-Prot GO term may itself be electronic, so the count of members with experimental evidence is given beside each pair. Only molecular function is tested.
