Enzyme Cutoff Check

Most enzyme misannotation in automatically annotated databases is overprediction: a protein looks like a family but does not belong to it. Scoring the protein against the family's HMM and its curated trusted cutoff catches much of it.

The Schnoes protocol

Schnoes et al. (2009) judged an enzyme annotation correct only if the sequence passed four checks in order. The tool built here mechanises the two that cover the family. The other two remain where they already were in this repository.

  1. Step 1 · NSAMatches the superfamily?Not attempted: only known matches are scored, so no hit is not evidence.
  2. Step 2 · SFAMatches the annotated family, not just the superfamily?Checked: verdict SFA when no target family is hit.
  3. Step 3 · MFRKeeps the functionally important residues?Separate: family_residue_validator and the Pseudoenzymes project.
  4. Step 4 · BTCScores above the family's trusted cutoff?Checked: verdict BTC when below the noise cutoff.

In GenBank NR, 54% of the misannotations Schnoes found failed step 4 and 31% failed step 2. Only 6% were missing catalytic residues.

How it did on reviewed rows

The test set is every IEA or IBA molecular-function row mapping to one specific reaction, in 20 Pseudomonas putida KT2440 reviews plus human HADH. The genes were chosen because each has a REMOVE on a paralog or wrong-reaction call, so the set is enriched for removals. Treat the counts as agreement on this set, not as an error rate.

Score against the trusted cutoff

Each row is one annotated activity. The dot sits at the protein's best score for a family that carries that activity, divided by the family's trusted cutoff (TC); where several families carry it, the one with the highest ratio is shown. At or right of the dashed line, the protein qualifies as a family member. An open ring marks rows where the protein also passes a family for a different reaction.

Rows with no target-family hit

    NO_MODEL: no NCBIfam model carries the activity, so the check is silent. SFA: a model exists, but the protein matches other families only. Several rows still name a family the protein fits instead.

    Using it in a review

    • Run it on electronic enzyme rows (EC2GO, InterPro2GO, ARBA, UniRule, IBA), especially in superfamilies where families catalyse different reactions: enolase, HAD, amidohydrolase, crotonase, VOC.
    • A BTC or SFA verdict with a competing family that passes supports replacing the term. Record the scores in the review reason.
    • A BTC with no competitor points to MODIFY toward a more general activity, the paper's own remedy.
    • A pass does not prove activity. Check catalytic residues and whether the family is broader than the specificity at issue.
    • Never use the check alone to remove an experimental annotation.
