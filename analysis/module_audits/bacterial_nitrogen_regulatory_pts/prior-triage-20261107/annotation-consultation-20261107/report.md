## Scope and family decisions

Model the **GAF-containing EI(Ntr) → native HPr/NPr → PtsN regulatory relay**, with native organism alternatives. Evidence supports gamma- and alphaproteobacterial instances. Do not describe it as present in every bacterium, every proteobacterium, every PtsN-bearing genome, or every member of the three families. A broad bacterial taxon range is a permitted range, not a universal-presence assertion; explain the demonstrated scope explicitly. No new deep ancestral origin or loss claim is established here.

* PTHR46244:SF1 is the current gamma selector. Verified alpha PtsP candidates lie in **SF6**. SF6 also contains E. coli **PtsI P08839**, the carbohydrate EI. Therefore neither SF1 alone, a union of SF1/SF6, nor the parent family alone correctly defines this mechanism. Prefer exact supported gene-product alternatives with real family metadata, or a role descriptor without an unproven universal family grant. GAF/EI architecture plus native relay evidence distinguishes positive exemplars; architecture alone does not prove every physiological output.
* PTHR33705:SF2 is officially **PHOSPHOCARRIER PROTEIN NPR**, but includes **B. subtilis HPr P08877**, and other carbohydrate HPr proteins. The current module's omission of an NPr family ID is a defensible warning, not a slot to fill from the label. E. coli HPr P0AA04 is in **SF1**, so it is a related role control, not the same-SF2 counterexample. Alpha proteins legitimately called HPr can serve in the nitrogen relay; do not reject them merely for that name.
* PTHR47738:SF1 membership establishes classification, not a universal output target or phosphostate effect. PtsN is the terminal phosphoacceptor/regulatory effector in these routes; **GO:0019887 protein kinase regulator activity cannot be the sole mandatory terminal function across them**. Keep evidence-supported kinase regulation in organism-specific optional output nodes. Reassess an exact general MF against the ontology before authoring one; no replacement ID is proposed from memory here.
* Preserve reversible transfer chemistry and distinguish a canonical complete route from bypass capacity. A PtsO-negative mutant that still phosphorylates PtsN is not evidence that the conventional PtsP→NPr→PtsN route has all its components. Conversely, deleting PtsO must not predict that PtsN can never be phosphorylated. Phosphorylation routing, measured regulatory consequences and sustained native flux are distinct claims.

Official names and exact member-table counterexamples are recorded in `family-controls.json`. This consultation does not reconstruct a PAINT gain/loss node or adjudicate the whole family.

## Evidence by step and output

**E. coli entry and relay:** PMID10473571 reports purified EI(Ntr), radiolabeled PEP transfer to NPr, Mg dependence, and discrimination against HPr. The abstract explicitly supports the measured entry reaction; full paper should be acquired normally before detailed kinetic/strain assertions. PMID7876255 reports purified NPr/IIA(Ntr) exchange and also cross-reactivity with carbohydrate components. Do not turn its in-vitro exchange into a claim that the two native systems are absolutely insulated. PMID15741344 is an additional primary NMR partner-interface lead, not yet mined here. PMID23517463 is a glutamine/alpha-ketoglutarate input-regulation lead; the PMC request hit CAPTCHA, so its detailed assays are not asserted here.

**KT2440 native relay:** PMID18296519 measures PtsN phosphostate across mutants/carbon conditions and supports the primary PEP→PtsP→NPr→PtsN route plus fructose/FruB cross-talk. Its local file declares full text but contains only Abstract and Discussion; obtain the missing methods/results normally before detailed assay-level claims. The source supports an in-vivo route inference, not purified reconstitution of every binary step. PMID17416664 currently provides abstract-level PHA phenotype support.

**Alpha entry and relay:** PMID24633875 directly assays purified S. meliloti EI(Ntr)→HPr and glutamine interaction with the isolated GAF domain. Alpha-ketoglutarate did not reproduce the E. coli regulatory effect under the tested conditions; do not universalize the E. coli input logic. PMID27109061 provides native NA1000 genetic/phosphostate support, plus [32P]PEP assays in **EI-containing protein fractions**, not homogeneous isolated EI. Its downstream SpoT link combines (p)ppGpp/cell-cycle measurements with bacterial two-hybrid evidence for phosphorylated PtsN; HPr has an indirect effect and did not bind SpoT in that assay. Do not claim all three proteins form a stable physical complex.

**Potassium outputs differ even within gamma lineages.** PMID19400808 supports E. coli unphosphorylated PtsN stimulation of KdpD kinase activity (local abstract explicitly includes biochemical assays). PMID26224366 supports KT2440 KdpD interaction and phosphostate/K-dependent promoter repression. Its available abstract does not independently establish the same catalytic direction; preserve the native distinction. **PMID39742999**, issued February 2025 after online publication in December 2024, directly examines E. coli K-12 PtsN–YcgO copurification, phosphostate dependence, genetics, and YcgO antiport in everted vesicles. The proposed detailed CorC/RCK mechanism remains a model. This adds a non-kinase output; it does not require YcgO in every relay.

**Other PtsN targets are not interchangeable.** PMID21236318 links KT2440 PtsN to AceE through immunoprecipitation/mass spectrometry, cytoplasmic colocalization and PDH assays in cell extracts. Keep those assay limits rather than describing a purified binary inhibition experiment. PMID24515609 reports H16 PtsN–SpoT1 interaction with an unphosphorylated preference and no native KdpD interaction in its assays; that contrasts with the Caulobacter phosphostate association. Full text remains to be inspected. H16's PtsI/HPr upstream architecture (PMID21478317 table 1) is not automatically the GAF-PtsP/NPr architecture defined here.

**Newest Pseudomonas primaries were checked, not inferred from the review.** PMID37074168 (2023) assays **UCBPP-PA14**, showing H68-dependent PtsN phosphorylation, FruB bypass when PtsO is absent, and native PtsN phosphorylation without PtsO. These are genetic/phosphostate results, not purified proof of each bypass edge. PMID40748077 (2025; use the published paper, not preprint PMID39975224) also uses **PA14**. Its perturbation/reporter/transcriptome experiments separate PtsO effects from PtsN and show that the GAF domain is dispensable for several tested quorum-sensing outputs. Do not substitute PAO1 accessions or make LasR a demonstrated direct PtsN-binding target. A PtsP knockout phenotype does not establish that its effect passes through terminal PtsN.

**Rhizobium comparison and a newer primary:** PMID32341157 (2020) studies **R. leguminosarum bv. viciae 3841**, distinguishing PtsN/ABC-transport and ManX/carbon-metabolic outputs, HPrK/Ser48 modulation, and PtsN–ChvI interaction. ManX is not a replacement for PtsN in the module. The 2026-09-30 primary **DOI10.1128/aem.01073-26** measures native mutant/complement cell-lysate enzyme activity, oxygen consumption and storage polymers. It explicitly reports inconclusive ManX purification and no detected ManX–MDH/SucA interaction in BACTH. This does not refute binding under every condition, but cannot support a direct native binding edge. HprK effects and phosphostate interpretations remain partly inferred. Exact Rlv3841 member accessions are intentionally unresolved here: the small live UniProt search returned HTTP503, as recorded without retry/cache replacement.

The 2026 minireview PMID41870250 is useful discovery context. It is not a primary experiment or evidence that every homolog outside the sampled lineages belongs to an intact relay. The September 2026 Rhizobium paper postdates that review.
