# E. coli ISC member consultation

All 142 original rows across IscS, IscU, IscA, HscB, HscA and Fdx were examined. No new annotation row was added. The immutable baseline and row ledger expose every retained or revised decision.

## Module-relevant conclusions

- IscS performs multiple native sulfur-mobilization branches, including Fe-S assembly, tRNA thiolation and thiamine biosynthesis. A module-specific focus does not make the other direct branches non-core.
- IscU binds Fe(II) in direct E. coli experiments (PMID32131593, PMID36605734). Earlier negative assays concern specific conditions. Iron-first versus sulfur-first models remain unsettled; no unique iron-donor mechanism is imposed.
- HscA ATP hydrolysis and HscB activation support cluster transfer. The HscA conformational endpoint in PMID22782893 is disordered-IscU stabilization. Neither ATP dependence nor successful cofactor delivery automatically establishes folding/refolding. Core activities remain ATP hydrolysis and ATPase activation. HscB structured-state stabilization is distinguished from a measured folding endpoint.
- Fdx can receive electrons through an entirely E. coli NADPH/FNR/Fdx reconstitution (PMID25688831; FNR P28861). This supports a nonrespiratory electron-transfer chain, without proving exclusive native donor use. The canonical HscBA-assisted module may require this role while explicitly limiting its condition scope; anaerobic bypass does not make all assisted roles universally optional.
- IscA has measured cluster-transfer and iron-donation capabilities; their relative physiological contributions remain uncertain. IscA is not asserted to be the unique iron supplier.
- E. coli IscU supports Fur maturation and iron-regulatory output (PMID38452854, MC4100). This overturns the old absence-of-bacterial-evidence rationale, but does not justify an unrestricted family-wide iron-homeostasis grant.
