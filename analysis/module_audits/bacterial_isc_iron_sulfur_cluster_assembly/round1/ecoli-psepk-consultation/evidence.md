## E. coli HscA: model substrates and IscU are different experiments

PMID9852006 measures turbidity for guanidine-denatured bovine rhodanese and thermally denatured porcine citrate synthase. Hsc66 suppresses aggregation, but ATP weakens that protection. Suppressing turbidity is not a recovered-fold activity assay. Importantly, Figure3 separately measures firefly luciferase activity after denaturation and40minutes in refolding buffer at30°C with1mM ATP. Hsc66 was4μM and Hsc20 was varied from0.5 to10μM; alternative DnaJ/GrpE conditions were also tested. The exact short Results clause is: “Hsc66 was also unable to assist refolding of denatured luciferase”. The DnaK/DnaJ/GrpE control restored luciferase activity. This is a real substrate-specific negative assay, not proof that all HscA substrates can never fold. Purified bacterial chaperones are compared with non-E. coli model clients; this is not a native-cell luciferase pathway.

PMID22782893 independently measures conformational exchange of E. coli apo-IscU by NMR. HscA slows D→S and stabilizes disordered IscU; it does not increase S→D, arguing against an unfoldase mechanism under these conditions. Native transfer function is supported by other ATP-dependent cluster-transfer studies, not inferred from the model-client assay. GO0044183 explicitly distinguishes a folding chaperone from a holdase. The folding/refolding assignments are therefore marked over-annotated for E. coli HscA: the measured mechanisms and negative refolding control do not justify the broader inherited folding interpretation. This is not a reconstructed evolutionary loss at PTN000452648 or a claim of absolute impossibility. PMID9852006 is now explicitly weighed on each disputed row.

## HscB and other species

HscB differs from HscA: PMID22782893 shifts IscU toward the structured state. Binding/stabilization of an existing structured state does not itself settle whether HscB assists formation of the functional fold. PMID9852006 found no autonomous Hsc20 antiaggregation on the model clients; it did not establish a universal absence of folding assistance as a cofactor. HscB protein-folding BP remains UNDECIDED, with this real uncertainty explicit. Pseudomonas KT2440 HscA and HscB have no equivalent target model-client or folding-kinetic assay in the reviewed sources. Their uncertainty is retained, rather than transferring the E. coli negative assay as a target measurement. HscA's specific ATPase/cluster-transfer core and HscB's activation/targeting core remain supported. The pre-existing PSEPK HscA retired GO0140662 proposal is unchanged in its entirety; its lifecycle is separately parent-owned.

For GO0051259, both official InterPro entries map the C-terminal HscB fold to protein complex oligomerization. Their prose instead describes an open three-helical bundle that binds and targets proteins to HscA; cited primary11124030 is the Hsc20 structure and22306468 concerns Jac1–Isu interaction. The mapping is thus over-specific relative to the demonstrated fold/targeting role. Direct E. coli18702525 maps the IscU-binding surface and tests HscA targeting by ATPase assays. Neither isolated monomericity nor mere presence in a transient heterocomplex settles oligomerization: the term permits heteromers, but requires the process of creating oligomers. Both assigned HscB rows are MARK_AS_OVER_ANNOTATED because domain membership and the established targeting function do not ground that extra process assignment. No negative oligomer-assembly assay or evolutionary loss is invented.

## IscS sulfur-compound transport

GO0072348 concerns directed movement of sulfur-containing compounds, within/out of/between cells. It is not restricted to membrane crossing. GO0097163 explicitly describes covalent sulfur binding and delivery to an acceptor. The latter fits the directly supported IscS transpersulfidation chemistry in10753862 and16387657; that molecular function does not automatically establish the former transport process. The prior ACCEPT reason collapsed these distinct claims.

The original IDA10739946 cache describes purified cysteine/selenocysteine desulfurization and active-site mutations but remains abstract-only. DOI resolution to the publisher returned403; an institutional thesis URL returned an HTML access page rather than a PDF. We have not inspected the original full experimental context and do not assert that its curator was wrong. The transport row is UNDECIDED pending that evidence and GO mapping convention, while sulfur-carrier activity and the directly supported biosynthetic chemistry remain intact. This is not a negative claim about intracellular sulfur movement.
