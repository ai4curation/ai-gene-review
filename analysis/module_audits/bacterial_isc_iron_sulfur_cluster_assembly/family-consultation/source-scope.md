# Evidence scope for the two Isc family reconciliations

All sources were already cached and remain unchanged. This consultation adds no genes, raw classifications, phylogenetic nodes or residue claims.

PTHR10093 contains B. subtilis SufU O32163 in its committed member inventory. The early positive study PMID20097860 reports chemical reconstitution and transfer to Leu1, while distinguishing the cluster-retaining D43A variant. PMID24321018 directly compares B. subtilis SufU with A. vinelandii IscU, finds tight zinc binding important for SufU sulfur transfer, and disputes a standard scaffold role. PMID29292548 reports bidirectional complementation of SufSU with E. coli SufSE rather than IscSU and places assembly on SufBCD. The family review retains all three observations: physiological role, chemical capacity and phylogenetic loss are different claims. No precise SufU subfamily boundary or loss node was established.

PMID15153099's older isolated bacterial IscU iron-binding negative remains cited. Later E. coli native-MS results in PMID32131593 show Fe(II) interactions; PMID36605734 also supports iron interaction but disputes a compulsory iron-first sequence under its conditions. This removes a categorical bacterial-versus-eukaryotic iron-binding split without establishing a uniform sequence of events or blanket activity for every family member. The PTN000009661 seed identity and original IBD remain immutable; only our interpretation changes.

PMID38452854 includes E. coli native deletion/complementation context and in-vitro Fur maturation with and without IscU. Restored Fur cluster-associated spectra and DNA binding support a concrete client-maturation contribution to bacterial iron regulation. Spectral subtraction and unsuccessful product repurification limit claims of a fully resolved direct handoff route. The findings refute an exclusively eukaryotic rationale while leaving full ancestral node scope unresolved.

For PTHR19375, canonical folding evidence remains positive: PMID18706386 measures denatured luciferase reactivation by yeast Ssa1 variants relative to the wild-type mechanism. E. coli HscA evidence instead establishes ATPase-dependent IscU cluster transfer (PMID18986169), preferential stabilization of disordered IscU (PMID22782893) and model-substrate antiaggregation (PMID9852006). The current GO0140662 definition requires assistance to protein folding. These results do not establish that endpoint across HscA, but they also do not demonstrate a universal absence or phylogenetic loss. No broad ATPase grant replaces the withdrawn universal folding grant.

The existing Hsp110 nucleotide-exchange/holdase judgment must remain visible independently of universal Hsp70 scope. No new negative exception for HscA is justified. All unrelated localization, PAINT and residue-site judgments are preserved exactly by the scoped verifier.
