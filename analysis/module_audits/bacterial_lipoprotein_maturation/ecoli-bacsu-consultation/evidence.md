# E. coli and B. subtilis maturation enzymes: consultation, 2026-11-07

## Chemical and ontology boundaries

The five reviews cover 61 original rows, including the historical E. coli LspA lipoprotein-biosynthesis proposal. No row is added or retired. Lgt performs diacylglyceryl transfer; LspA directly cleaves a signal peptide; Lnt performs phospholipid-dependent N-acyl transfer onto the alpha-amino group of lipidated N-terminal cysteine. These enzymes perform actual maturation work, unlike a substrate that is merely required for the reaction.

Official QuickGO definitions and is_a/part_of ancestry are frozen in ontology.json, with each exact API URL. GO:0009003 signal peptidase activity is active and reinstated, and descends from GO:0004175. It fits the measured signal cleavage of LspA. GO:0004190 separately captures its aspartic mechanism, so both supported descendants replace generic endopeptidase; the core uses the substrate-directed signal-peptidase genus. The existing lipoprotein-specific proposal still adds lipidated-Cys and type-II specificity absent from the generic term. GO:0016485 protein processing descends from proteolysis; GO:0042158 does not. Protein processing therefore preserves the chemical breakdown claim, with lipoprotein biosynthesis retained separately as pathway context.

GO:0140186 protein N-acyltransferase activity specifies acyl-CoA and lysine-N6, not the phospholipid and N-terminal cysteine alpha-amino chemistry of Lnt. GO:0016410 is obsolete. GO:0016747 remains a valid broad catalytic genus; the existing apolipoprotein-specific proposal remains separate. A protein name or EC label is not used as the positive evidence for that reaction.

## Lgt source and sequence scope

PMID26729647 explicitly amplified lgt from BL21 genomic DNA and expressed tagged protein in C43(DE3); complementation used an E. coli BW25113 depletion background. Official RCSB entity 5AZB/1 is saved as 5azb-entity.json. The reproducible sequence comparison in lgt-sequence-check.json finds the entire native P60955 segment 2–291 in the deposited construct, with an N-terminal MV prefix and C-terminal LEHHHHHH suffix. This is a defined construct bridge, not a claim that an unmodified K-12 protein was purified from native cells. Its activity, seven-TM structure and independent topology/complementation support the reference enzyme's function.

PMID22287519 combines cell fractionation, PhoA/LacZ fusions, cysteine accessibility and complementation. It establishes membrane insertion and a cytoplasmic C terminus, while important catalytic features face the membrane/periplasmic side. GO:0009898 includes embedded proteins; it does not require a wholly peripheral protein or cytoplasmic catalysis. Retaining sided presence as a non-core location does not endorse an aqueous native catalytic model. Core activity remains assigned to the plasma membrane.

The protected PMID18602442 cache is abstract-only. Its full primary article was read through the public rendering at https://www.academia.edu/99693874/Localization_and_characterization_of_prolipoprotein_diacylglyceryl_transferase_Lgt_critical_in_bacterial_lipoprotein_biosynthesis on 2026-11-07. Methods use pT7GT-expressing BL21(DE3) inverted membrane vesicles; S. aureus supplies radiolabeled phosphatidylglycerol, not the enzyme. Two short source excerpts are: “from BL21(DE3) cells expressing Lgt under T7” and “artifact due to hyper-expression or manipulations in vesicle”. The latter continues into preparation and qualifies the observed soluble fraction. These experiments do not establish native cytoplasmic lipidation or override the later integral topology. No publication cache was rewritten and no unverified full construct sequence is asserted for pT7GT.

## LspA and Lnt source scope

The protected E. coli LspA primary caches are abstract-only. PMID6368552 directly describes an in-vitro signal-peptidase assay and inner-membrane localization; PMID1894646 describes sequence-verified PhoA/LacZ fusion junctions and four membrane spans. PMID11894915 mutates the lipoprotein substrate cysteine, not the protease catalytic residue: failure of maturation demonstrates substrate dependence, not a newly measured protease active site. PMID6363408 identifies the lipidated free-amino intermediate between cleavage and final N-acylation. The true experimental annotations are retained with those assay limits.

For Lnt, full PMID21676878 uses tagged recombinant protein in the PAP105 background, endogenous apo-Lpp and a defined synthetic lipidated peptide, and directly demonstrates acyl-enzyme formation and product N-acylation. Phospholipid-donor dependence is established; no exclusive universal phosphatidylethanolamine requirement is imposed. Full PMID28885614 explicitly uses an E. coli K-12 lnt gene with a C-terminal His8 tag. Its structure places the catalytic nitrilase domain in the periplasm above an eight-helix membrane domain. The membrane anchor does not negate the experimentally supported periplasmic domain localization in the earlier PMID15513925 annotation. No freely soluble periplasmic enzyme is asserted. High-throughput interactions remain real observations while bare protein-binding rows are removed as uninformative.

The PMID15919996 target-specific supplementary entries were not independently inspected in this audit. Existing curated IDA membrane rows are retained with corroborating enzyme-specific topology; no false claim of reading the missing target table is made. Other older abstracts are used only for statements they actually contain, not invented strain-specific measurements.

## Bacillus scope and downstream maturation

PMID10096076 reports loss of lipomodification in B. subtilis lgt mutants and defective cell retention of PrsA/BlaP, while mutants remain viable in the tested conditions. PMID9846746 maps gerF mutations to lgt and proposes impaired maturation of germination proteins; this is not an independent germination chemistry. PMID9141696 demonstrates B. subtilis LspA activity by complementation of E. coli Y815 and pulse labeling in E. coli. That heterologous assay is distinguished from the B. subtilis loss-of-function work in PMID9880550. The latter shows temperature-dependent growth effects and altered PrsA processing, not universal essentiality. PMID10497172 identifies activity-important residues and proposes the Asp102/Asp129 catalytic pair; the proposed catalytic mechanism is not relabeled as a complete direct transition-state demonstration.

The older Bacillus papers remain abstract-only locally, and their abstracts do not resolve every exact strain/construct sequence. Curated strain168 records and specific HAMAP assignments support target transfer, with no fabricated native kinetic constants. Membrane localization uses the target topology features, curated HAMAP location and membrane-substrate mechanism; monoderm status is not treated as proof that a sporulating bacterium has only one possible membrane compartment.

PMID40626731 establishes downstream LhaT/YpjA-dependent lipoprotein N-acetylation in B. subtilis. Absence of canonical Lnt is therefore not evidence that all Bacillus mature products remain diacylated or have an unmodified alpha-amino group. Lgt and LspA provide intermediates for later modification; this audit does not add LhaT activity to either enzyme.

## New 2026 Lgt compatibility study

PMID42126242 full text compares E. coli SLEC67 (Δlgt) with SLEC68 (ΔlgtΔlpp) using induced low-copy constructs. Results report that “some Lgt homologs are less efficient in modifying E. coli lipoproteins”. Salmonella complementation improves upon Lpp removal, whereas protein-production failures confound some non-complementing donors. B. subtilis is not a directly tested complementation donor. This constrains heterologous compatibility, not the supported native Bacillus chemistry; no universal replacement of one organism’s enzyme by another is asserted.
