## Primary evidence and source scope

* **PMID26729647**, Mao et al., full cached article: E. coli Lgt structure, PG-dependent lipid transfer, lipoGFP activity and complementation. The Results identify a seven-TM fold and functional roles for R143/R239. The chemistry attaches a diacylglyceryl group to the lipobox cysteine thiol while the signal peptide remains attached. “N-terminal cysteine” here means the prospective mature terminus, not the current terminus of the uncleaved precursor. These E. coli assays are comparative evidence, not KT2440 measurements.
* **PMID26912896**, Vogeley et al., cached primary abstract: P. aeruginosa LspA structure and mutagenesis establish signal-peptide removal by an aspartyl peptidase. The Science publisher returned403; no inaccessible full-text methods, exact target kinetics or KT2440 assay is asserted. This explicit abstract result, target family/topology and curated reaction are sufficient for the basic conserved activity; unavailable target-specific details remain unresolved.
* **PMID21676878**, Hillmann et al., full cached article: purified E. coli Lnt modifies Lpp/FSL-1 and exhibits a ping-pong mechanism through a thioester intermediate. Results show that PE is an effective donor and PG/PA also donate at lower rates under those conditions. The reaction is transfer of a phospholipid acyl chain to the free alpha-amino group of the already S-diacylglyceryl-modified cysteine. It is not acyl-CoA-dependent lysine-N6 acylation. Neither PE exclusivity nor E. coli kinetic constants are transferred to KT2440.
* **PMID28675161**, Wiktor et al., full cached article: P. aeruginosa and E. coli Lnt structures show an integral membrane domain and a periplasm-facing catalytic domain. The E. coli structure has eight transmembrane helices; the seven predicted features in the target record are not an experimental target topology. Periplasm-facing catalysis does not make Lnt a soluble periplasmic enzyme.
* **PMID17416655**, Vidal-Ingigliardi et al., cached primary abstract: E. coli complementation and mutagenesis identify the E267/K335/C387 triad and four other functionally important sites, explicitly named in the abstract. Attempts to obtain PMC1913372 full text returned reCAPTCHA, including the ?pdf=1 route. The independent sequence comparison verifies conservation, not catalytic activity or fold geometry.

* **PMID42126242**, Legood et al. (2026), full cached Results/Figures3,6/Discussion: P. aeruginosa Lgt is tested by complementation in E. coli, with outcome differences among homologs and dependence on the host lipoprotein context. R143/R239 remain among the functionally important E. coli residues. Arm/head variation and substrate-specificity differences mean conserved chemistry does not imply identical substrate repertoire or interchangeability. The Pseudomonas comparison is not a KT2440 experiment; the main text does not identify P. putida or P. fluorescens assays. No native PSEPK essentiality or quantitative complementation phenotype is inferred.

## Sequence evidence and limitations

The panel uses global BLOSUM62 alignments with declared gap penalties and frozen source/target records. Lgt's E. coli R143/R239 map to target R139/R216 throughout the sensitivity panel. Lnt's E267/K335/C387 and Y388/E389/W237/E343 map to target E269/K329/C381 and Y382/E383/W240/E337 throughout. Those observations support conserved enzyme identity; they do not establish native rates, all substrate preferences or essentiality.

LspA's D141 maps to target D145 in all tested settings. D123 mapping is parameter-sensitive: gap extension -0.5 with opening -10 places a gap at that residue, while extension -1 with openings -10,-12,-15,-20 maps it to target D126. Target D126/D145 are independently annotated catalytic features and occur in conserved local motifs. The simple global alignment therefore supports the family-level inference but is not an unambiguous residue-geometry proof. No AlphaFold distance is adopted from the provider as an independently measured result.

See `sequence-results.yaml`, `sequence-sensitivity.yaml` and `RESULTS.md` for reproducibility. A failed alignment correspondence would not by itself be evidence of catalytic loss.
