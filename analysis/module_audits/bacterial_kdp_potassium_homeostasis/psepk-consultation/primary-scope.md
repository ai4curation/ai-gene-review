## KdpC ATP binding: PMID21711450

Title: The KdpC subunit of the Escherichia coli K+-transporting KdpB P-type ATPase acts as a catalytic chaperone.

The complete publisher article was read through the web tool at https://febs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j.1742-4658.2011.08224.x . A direct requests capture received403 and is retained only as an access log; it is not article text. The publication cache remains its original abstract-only content.

Results, “Formation of a KdpBN/KdpCsol/ATP ternary complex”, reports that ATP binding to isolated KdpCsol “could therefore not be shown, at least for the ATP concentrations applicable in this assay”. The stated maximum was2.8mM ATP and heat was at the ligand/buffer control level. Earlier photoaffinity labeling used8-azido-ATP; competition required high nucleotide concentrations, and this paper notes nonspecificity concerns for250mM ribose competition. In contrast, isolated KdpBN and KdpCsol preincubated with5mM ATP yielded an interacting ternary complex by ITC (mean Kd3.4µM across two experiments); KdpC Q140 substitutions in the assembled complex affected growth and nucleotide kinetics. These real observations must be retained without equating photoaffinity labeling with physiological high-affinity ATP binding.

The experimental proteins and strains are E. coli. Later intact-complex structures (PMID28636601 full cache; PMID34272288 full cache) put KdpC's soluble domain in the periplasm, challenging the proposed contact with the cytoplasmic ATP-binding KdpB domain. Topology does not establish a universal negative for ATP binding by isolated KdpC. For KT2440, physiological ATP binding remains unresolved; no direct target assay was identified.

## KdpF: PMID10608856

Title: The KdpF subunit is part of the K(+)-translocating Kdp complex of Escherichia coli and is responsible for stabilization of the complex in vitro.

The protected cache is abstract-only. DOI https://doi.org/10.1074/jbc.274.53.37901 resolves to Elsevier PII S0021925819529763; the JBC fulltext URL returned403 through the web tool. No full-text negative claim is manufactured. The abstract explicitly reports E. coli deletion under0.1mM K+, nearly unchanged membrane ATPase activity, loss of activity after detergent purification, and restoration by added purified KdpF or excess E. coli lipids. These are condition-specific ortholog observations, not a KT2440 deletion or universal dispensability result. Together with intact-complex structures they support a stabilizing contribution, which can be a core role even when the subunit is dispensable in a tested native membrane condition.

## Mechanistic transfer boundary

PMID34272288 full text uses E. coli KdpFABC, including KdpA Q116R/KdpB S162A constructs for structural state trapping; its authors check transport coupling in proteoliposomes. It supports ion selection in KdpA, transfer through the intersubunit tunnel, and KdpB-mediated cytoplasmic release. It is not a KT2440 structural assay, and earlier KdpA-only translocation models are retained as historical sources rather than current endpoints.

PMID38622146 full text establishes E. coli KdpD's N-terminal atypical serine kinase and explores divergent KdpD architectures/controls. Full-length KdpD architecture in KT2440 does not itself establish that activity or its regulatory input. The PSEPK histidine-kinase core remains supported; serine-kinase activity and KdpB inhibitory-site phosphorylation remain questions, without new annotations.
