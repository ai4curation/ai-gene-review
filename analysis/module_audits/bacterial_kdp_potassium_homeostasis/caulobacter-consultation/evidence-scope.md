## Primary evidence and limits

PMID39133005 (2024), DOI10.1128/jb.00107-24, full text in the normally fetched, protected publication cache, explicitly maps the native NA1000 kdpABCDE locus to CCNA_01663–1667. Methods use NA1000 derivatives; its tests include growth curves, Tn-seq, RNA-seq, ChIP-seq, deletions, complementation and point mutants.

- **KdpABC transport capacity:** native deletion and fitness data establish system-level importance at limiting potassium. The paper compares limiting0.025mM and abundant0.5mM potassium; residual growth without Kdp is possible. This is not a purified pump-flux, affinity or coupling-stoichiometry assay. Exact subunit chemistry is inferred from curated HAMAP/domain evidence and comparative Kdp architecture.
- **KdpE output:** ChIP-seq supplies a confident direct kdp-promoter target. Asp56 mutations and promoter reporters support phosphorylation-dependent activation. Changes elsewhere in the transcriptome do not establish binding to all affected promoters or a direct negative regulatory mechanism for every downregulated gene.
- **KdpD control:** H670N and F832L mutants, growth-phase reporters and epistasis support a kinase contribution early and a predominant proposed phosphatase role later. The paper explicitly acknowledges that F832L might change phosphatase strength. No isolated NA1000 catalytic kinetics were measured. Both catalytic roles are therefore qualified in core synthesis; no new speculative annotation is added simply to encode the proposed model.
- **Unresolved phosphorylation input:** kdp-promoter activity persists without KdpD. Another source is inferred; PhoR is discussed as a candidate, not demonstrated. No named alternate kinase or fabricated required pathway component is asserted.
- **Architecture:** the primary says KdpD lacks the recognizable Ecoli GAF domain. The broad InterPro GAF-like superfamily cross-reference does not by itself contradict that domain-specific statement. Conserved Walker motifs and KdpB serine only motivate an untested serine-kinase hypothesis in NA1000.
- **KdpC:** frozen HAMAP text propagates the older ATP-dependent catalytic-chaperone model. PMID21711450 is abstract-only in the protected cache; its ATP-related observations are not denied. Full PMID28636601 places the soluble Ecoli KdpC domain periplasmically in a KdpA-Q116R-containing complex. That topology challenges physiological interpretation, but is not a negative NA1000 ATP-binding assay. The target ATP-binding annotation remains UNDECIDED. Complex transport contribution is retained without independent ATPase activity.
- **KdpB caution:** HAMAP reports missing conserved feature residue(s), without identifying which one in the caution. Asp314, ATP/Mg features and target P-type architecture remain explicitly present. No catalytic loss is invented; exact feature-level resolution and purified coupling are open questions.

## Recommended conditional positive profile

The matched native **NA1000 KdpABC + KdpE** panel supports potassium-limited uptake/homeostasis with KdpE-dependent expression. KdpD is a supported native regulatory participant with condition-dependent positive/negative effects, not an obligatory sole positive phosphorylation source. Keep wild-type regulated-system coverage distinct from minimum pump capacity and from the ΔkdpD experimental phenotype. Absence of a KdpF annotation in this panel is not a claim that every small ORF has been exhaustively excluded; it is not a reason to invent a required F witness.

A production capacity checker may use the three matched pump components with their reviewed functional contributions as a qualified positive transport witness, and the matched KdpE promoter function as the native expression-output witness. It must not require a named unidentified kinase, grant cross-taxon assembly from generic family hits, or claim that all required cells exhibit identical flux under all potassium concentrations. A lone KdpB ATPase, a generic P-type ATPase, an unrelated OmpR regulator, or ATP-binding KdpC alone cannot fulfill the pump/output profile.
