# Accessory source and construct scope

PMID37821449 (2023) and PMID41285762 (2025) were read in full from the normal immutable publication caches. Both use E. coli K-12 BW25113-derived cellular backgrounds, plasmid complementation/overexpression, and tagged purified complexes. The 2023 interaction, disulfide-maturation and BAM-residence measurements establish LptM-mediated assembly support; LptM is not itself a disulfide oxidoreductase. The 2025 work additionally measures translocon conformations, domain-specific binding, and cellular phenotypes under DsbA loss or LptC overproduction. Antibiotic/bile sensitivity and LPS migration changes support impaired envelope/bridge function; the paper hedges the inference that altered LPS reflects colanic-acid decoration. These are not isolated-protein transport flux measurements.

LptM positioning at the gate and LptY stabilization of the LptD beta-taco provide structural work performed by these proteins, beyond a necessity-only argument. Molecular dynamics supplies a mechanistic model of gate opening; it is not direct observation of every LPS-translocation stroke. Both proteins are lineage-qualified, condition-dependent accessory components, not universal essential parts of every bacterial Lpt system.

PDB 9IA5 entity3 is exactly the 67-residue P0ADN6 precursor followed by His8. Only part of mature LptM is structurally resolved; a full deposited entity sequence is not evidence every residue was visible. The 2025 LptDEMY density includes LptY docked with an AlphaFold model; the deposited 9IA5 atomic model contains LptDEM, not a complete experimental LptY chain. Independently, PDB9FZ5 entity3 maps to P31063 and exactly matches residues21–137. Its citation is PMID40127101; this receipt uses that deposition only as identity provenance and makes no uninspected additional phenotype claim. UniProt's yedD symbol is retained; LptY is the new literature name. The 8H1R hybrid Pseudomonas LptDE/E. coli LptM structure is not mislabeled a native Pseudomonas LptM assay.

Official RCSB endpoints and exact sequence checks are in sequence-bridge.json. These natural/deposited sequence comparisons support identity, not a claim that every construct and tag in every assay was identical.
