## Transport evidence and limits

[PMID:19919675](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1365-2958.2009.06962.x) was read in its protected full-text cache. Its Results distinguish P. syringae DC3000/B728a and P. aeruginosa PA14/PAO1 proteins, with heterologous E. coli uptake experiments, from any KT2440 assay. CbcWV is the shared transport core; CaiX supplies carnitine recognition, whereas CbcX supports choline and BetX supports glycine betaine. CaiX can confer uptake in a background without conferring catabolism. This explicitly separates transport participation from downstream chemical degradation. KT2440 target-domain architecture and conserved locus support transfer of the transport roles, not target kinetic constants.

The low-osmolarity nutritional phenotype does not demonstrate that KT2440 CbcV can never participate in osmoprotection. Its existing osmotic-response row remains MARK_AS_OVER_ANNOTATED for extrapolating a stress function from a compatible-solute importer. This is not a universal negative. ATP binding is integral to CbcV's energy-coupling role and changes from KEEP_AS_NON_CORE to ACCEPT. The original qualifiers remain untouched; qualifier-only defects in review commentary are removed under the current review policy. CbcW's physical contribution remains accepted. No assertion that a free ATPase or permease alone transports substrate is made.

Fresh QuickGO definitions are archived in ontology.yaml. The existing CbcW ontology analysis places quaternary-ammonium substrate terms outside the amino-group amine branch; its lateral substrate-class MODIFY decisions are retained. A six-pass topology is supported by target UniProt features. The claim that CbcW has no intrinsic specificity is replaced by the narrower finding that SBPs select substrates while intrinsic target-permease selectivity is untested.

## Enzyme evidence and an important correction

[PMID:19406895](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2857723/) was read in the full PMC web rendering, including Table 1, Results and Discussion, despite the protected local cache containing only an abstract and download stub. The PA14 cdhCA double deletion loses most carnitine-dependent NADH formation; this does not isolate the catalytic role of each protein. PA5385 insertion growth defects are rescued by the coding sequence, and the cdhC insertion/deletion results support the locus's catabolic role. The biochemical scheme proposed in this 2009 paper is historical and does not itself establish the subsequently assigned CdhC/CdhB chemistry.

The protected [PMID:3058208](https://doi.org/10.1016/0167-4838(88)90222-1) abstract explicitly describes purified P. putida IFP 206 L-carnitine dehydrogenase, not KT2440. Its L-carnitine/NAD specificity, target HAMAP assignment and sequence comparison support LcdH chemistry. NAD+ binding is integral to the redox reaction and changes from KEEP_AS_NON_CORE to ACCEPT. The fatty-acid-process rejection is retained as an overbroad domain-to-function transfer, without claiming exhaustive negative target substrate assays.

The [PMID:24240508 publisher page](https://www.nature.com/articles/nchembio.1387) exposes an abstract and supplementary files; the main text remains inaccessible. The authentic [supplementary PDF](https://static-content.springer.com/esm/art%3A10.1038%2Fnchembio.1387/MediaObjects/41589_2014_BFnchembio1387_MOESM244_ESM.pdf), [Data Set 2](https://static-content.springer.com/esm/art%3A10.1038%2Fnchembio.1387/MediaObjects/41589_2014_BFnchembio1387_MOESM246_ESM.xlsx) and [Data Set 4](https://static-content.springer.com/esm/art%3A10.1038%2Fnchembio.1387/MediaObjects/41589_2014_BFnchembio1387_MOESM248_ESM.xlsx) were inspected. Its dehydrocarnitine columns T1/T2 contain 146.407 and 237.1; its (S)-KAH columns contain 50.55 and 81.021. These are the source's pre-process screening values, not purified-target kcat or Km values. The CdhC and CdhB reaction assignments retain the independently curated UniProt statements linked to PMID:24240508; this access limitation is explicit, rather than using the supplement as if it contained those auxiliary target assays.

[PMID:30670548](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6416908/?pdf=1) full text was inspected from the RHIME consultation's genuine capture (see its primary-scope.md and provenance.json). It independently describes carnitine cleavage and thioester hydrolysis in S. meliloti, and reports failure to cultivate KT2440 on gamma-butyrobetaine. That upstream substrate phenotype does not disprove KT2440 carnitine catabolism. No S. meliloti fused-enzyme activity or native growth result is silently reassigned to KT2440. No conclusion here depends on the incorrect attribution of an Rm2011 BcoG mutant to that study.
