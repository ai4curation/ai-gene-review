Description

Assigns the UniProt keyword Protease (KW-0645) through 1010 alternative condition sets. About half are single InterPro entries or PANTHER families; the rest are conjunctions of two or three conditions, some with a taxon.

The domain overlap table is not shown for this review. It is drawn from a cached analysis of the rule's condition sets, and the cache does not match the sets recorded here.

Review Summary

Accuracy: the first 10 of the 1010 live condition sets were examined. They select ClpP, HslV, leucyl aminopeptidase, peptidase M48 and signal peptidase II families, and every reviewed match carries the Protease keyword KW-0645 (5150 of 5150, summed over sets). The other 1000 sets were not checked against proteins. Meaning and parsimony: the keyword names an enzyme class and the sampled sets select families of that class, so the rule is a coherent statement. Its construction is not economical. Protease is the parent of eight class keywords (Aminopeptidase, Aspartyl protease, Carboxypeptidase, Dipeptidase, Metalloprotease, Serine protease, Thiol protease, Threonine protease), each with its own ARBA rule, and 605 of the 1010 sets are word-for-word copies of a set in one of those eight rules. UniProtKB adds the parent keyword whenever a child keyword is present (no entry carries any of the eight without Protease), so those 605 sets cannot add an annotation.

Action Rationale

Nothing in the sample is wrong, and the keyword is meaningful at the level of a UniProt keyword. The specific defect is redundancy with the child-keyword rules, measured on the whole rule and not on the sample. The verdict is from set identity across rules; the 405 remaining sets were not examined one by one, which is why confidence is moderate.
