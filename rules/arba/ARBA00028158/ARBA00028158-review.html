Description

A 55-condition-set ARBA rule that assigns GO:0006366 (transcription by RNA polymerase II) to eukaryotic nuclear proteins. Eight condition sets use interpretable InterPro combinations and identify genuine components of the Pol II machinery (Spt6, ELL, TFIIF-beta/RAP30, TAF4, TAF2, Elongin A) plus one fungal RSC/polybromo chromatin-remodelling subunit. The remaining 47 sets are built from CATH FunFam identifiers, 27 of which fire on a single FunFam. Three named FunFam clauses reveal that the rule also reaches an ATP-dependent chromatin remodeller (CHD1), a ligand-activated DNA-binding transcription factor (AHR) and the Integrator endonuclease (INTS11) - classes for which the whole-process term is either uninformative or a distant ancestor of the term that actually describes them.

Review Summary

ARBA00028158 is a mega-rule that OR-s together 55 condition sets to emit a single whole-process term, GO:0006366 (transcription by RNA polymerase II). Its interpretable core is sound: condition sets 1, 2, 4, 5, 7 and 8 use multi-signature InterPro combinations that identify Spt6, ELL, TFIIF-beta/RAP30, TAF4, Elongin A and TAF2 - proteins that act directly in Pol II initiation or elongation, and for which the term is well supported by current structural and review literature. The problem is everything else. Forty-seven sets are keyed on CATH FunFam identifiers rather than named families, and 27 of those fire on a SINGLE FunFam with no corroborating signature; 13 have neither a second signature nor a taxon restriction. Because all 55 sets are disjunctive, any one over-broad FunFam independently produces the annotation, and the family identity behind a bare numeric FunFam cannot be reconstructed from the rule text. Where the FunFams ARE named, they expose the failure mode that GO curators reported. Condition set 33 (two AHR FunFams, restricted to Glires) reaches the aryl hydrocarbon receptor, a ligand-activated DNA-binding regulator for which GO:0006357 is the diagnostic term; mouse Ahr (P30561) demonstrably carries this rule's GO:0006366 row next to a seven-species GO:0006357 PAINT IBA. Condition set 34 reaches INTS11, whose established roles are snRNA 3'-end processing and promoter-proximal termination. Condition set 3 reaches a fungal RSC/polybromo remodelling subunit. And condition set 14, which is unnamed, is verifiably the route by which S. cerevisiae CHD1 (P32657) - an ATP-dependent chromatin remodeller - receives the term, duplicating far more specific SGD annotations (GO:0006338 IDA, GO:0006357 IDA, GO:0006368 IGI/IPI, GO:0006369 IMP). Two corrections to the framing in geneontology/go-annotation#6562 are worth recording. First, the reported protein, S. pombe spt16 (O94267), does NOT match any condition set in the current rule version: none of its five FunFams (2.30.29.150:FF:000002, 2.30.29.30:FF:000017, 3.40.350.10:FF:000006, 2.30.29.210:FF:000001, 3.90.230.10:FF:000005) and none of its eleven InterPro entries appear anywhere in the rule JSON, so the complained-of annotation is not reproducible from the rule as it now stands and appears to be a stale row surviving a rule edit. Second, the complaint is best understood as over-breadth rather than a wrong-branch error: the term that properly describes Spt16, GO:0140673 (transcription elongation-coupled chromatin remodeling), is a DESCENDANT of GO:0006366 via GO:0006368 -> GO:0006354 -> GO:0006366. The ARBA row is therefore true-but-uninformative for FACT and CHD1, and genuinely mis-branched only for the regulator class exemplified by AHR, whose proper term GO:0006357 sits outside GO:0006366 entirely. The general class of complaint is real and reproducible even though the specific instance cited is not. Status is IN_PROGRESS rather than COMPLETE for two reasons, both tooling limits rather than open scientific questions. First, only one deep-research provider could be run: perplexity returned HTTP 401 insufficient_quota, cyberian is not a registered provider, and the openai route failed with model_not_found for o3-deep-research-2025-06-26, leaving falcon as the sole report against a two-provider convention. Second, `just analyze-rule` refuses this rule outright because 55 condition sets exceed its maximum of 12, so there is no -analysis.yaml, no `entries`, and no computed pairwise overlap; the condition_overlap assessment below is structural (shared CATH superfamilies across clauses) rather than protein-set-based. The SPLIT recommendation does not depend on either gap.

Action Rationale

The rule's condition sets do not share a mechanistic basis for the term they emit. Basal initiation factors (TFIID subunits, TFIIF-beta), elongation factors (Spt6, ELL, Elongin A), ATP-dependent chromatin remodellers (RSC/polybromo, CHD1), a ligand-activated sequence-specific transcription factor (AHR) and a termination/RNA-processing endonuclease (INTS11) are collapsed onto one whole-process parent. That is what makes the output simultaneously defensible for a minority of members and unhelpful or wrong for the rest, and it is not fixable by editing the GO term alone - different members need different terms, which requires different rules. SPLIT rather than DEPRECATE because the InterPro-based clauses are genuinely good: they use family-specific signatures alongside the generic folds, and the literature directly supports the term for the proteins they identify. SPLIT rather than MODIFY because the change needed is structural, not a parameter adjustment: six or so mechanistically coherent rules, each emitting the most specific supported term, would replace one disjunction of 55 clauses. The 27 bare-FunFam clauses should be suspended rather than carried into the successor rules until each FunFam's family identity, membership and taxonomic range have been documented, because no reviewer can assess them from the rule as published.

Assessments

OVERLY_COMPLEX

Fifty-five disjunctive condition sets for one whole-process GO term, spanning at least six mechanistically distinct classes of protein. Forty-seven sets are keyed on CATH FunFam identifiers rather than named families; 27 of those fire on a single FunFam, and 13 carry neither a second signature nor a taxon restriction. Only 8 sets use interpretable InterPro combinations. Twenty-five distinct taxon constraints appear, 21 of them exactly once, including single-genus restrictions to Rattus, Mus and Homo. The rule also exceeds this repository's own analysis threshold - `just analyze-rule` refuses it outright - so no pairwise overlap statistics can be computed and the rule is literally not auditable by the standard tooling.

MODERATE

Sharply bimodal. For the proteins identified by the eight InterPro clauses the literature is strong and recent: TFIID is the basal factor that nucleates the Pol II preinitiation complex, TFIIF-beta is a core GTF, and Spt6, ELL and Elongin A are established elongation factors, with 2023 cryo-EM work resolving the ELOA-Pol II interface and showing causally that the ELOA latch is required for elongation stimulation. For the 47 FunFam clauses there is no literature support of any kind available to a reviewer, because the clauses do not say which families they denote. The overall assessment is therefore MODERATE rather than STRONG: the rule's evidence base covers a minority of its clauses, and the majority is not merely under-supported but unassessable.

Supporting Evidence:

  • PMID:38397413: The Pol II core enzyme can itself synthesize RNA using a template DNA, but promoter-specific transcription initiation requires the canonical general transcription factors (GTFs): TFIIA, TFIIB, TFIID, TFIIE, TFIIF, and TFIIH
  • PMID:38397413: Elongation factors include Poly (ADP-ribose) Polymerases (PARPs), elongation factor for RNA Pol II (ELL), TFIIS, elongin A, DSIF, and Spt6, among others
  • PMID:37932450: The latch is required for the elongation-stimulatory activity of Elongin, but not for Pol II binding, indicating that Elongin functions by allosterically regulating the conformational mobility of the polymerase active center.
  • PMID:36180603: U snRNA processing is dependent on the catalytic site of INTS11
  • file:rules/arba/ARBA00028158/ARBA00028158-deep-research-falcon.md: These 47 sets cannot be responsibly evaluated family by family from numeric identifiers alone.
SIGNIFICANT

Quantitative pairwise overlap could not be computed because `just analyze-rule` refuses rules with more than 12 condition sets, so the figures below are structural rather than protein-set-based. Nine CATH superfamilies are reused across multiple condition sets, strongly suggesting that separate clauses are matching different structural fragments of the same protein families: 1.10.20.10 appears in six sets (CS12, CS21, CS26, CS37, CS46, CS47), 1.10.10.10 in four (CS9, CS17, CS24, CS25), 1.10.10.60 in four (CS14, CS24, CS35, CS36), 3.40.50.10810 in three (CS11, CS14, CS44), 3.40.50.300 in three (CS11, CS44, CS45), 3.30.40.10 in three (CS26, CS31, CS41), and 1.10.10.2740, 1.20.5.170 and 3.40.50.10890 in two each. The overlap matters more than usual here: because the sets are OR-ed, a redundant fragment clause is not harmless padding but an independent trigger, and CS46/CS47 in particular are bare 1.10.20.10 FunFams with no second signature at all.

TOO_BROAD

GO:0006366 spans the entire Pol II transcription cycle, so it is technically true for most proteins this rule reaches while being the most specific term for almost none of them. The cost is visible in the annotations it produces. S. cerevisiae CHD1 (P32657) receives GO:0006366 from condition set 14 while SGD already records GO:0006338 (IDA), GO:0006357 (IDA), GO:0006368 (IGI/IPI) and GO:0006369 (IMP) - the ARBA row is a strictly less informative ancestor of terms the gene already has. S. pombe spt16 (O94267) likewise already carries GO:0140673 transcription elongation-coupled chromatin remodeling, which is a descendant of GO:0006366 via GO:0006368 and GO:0006354; the objection in go-annotation#6562 is therefore over-breadth, not a wrong-branch assignment. The one clause where the term is genuinely mis-branched is CS33: for the aryl hydrocarbon receptor the correct term is GO:0006357 regulation of transcription by RNA polymerase II, which is NOT under GO:0006366, and mouse Ahr (P30561) carries both the ARBA GO:0006366 row and a seven-species GO:0006357 PAINT IBA. The rule should emit stage-specific children per family and let GO:0006366 be inferred.

TOO_NARROW

The dominant defect is over-restriction, not over-reach. Pol II transcription is pan-eukaryotic, yet the rule carries 25 distinct taxon constraints of which 21 are used exactly once, including single-genus restrictions to Rattus (CS19), Mus (CS36) and Homo (CS42) and narrow primate clades Catarrhini (CS29), Haplorrhini (CS28) and Hominidae (CS37). These read as boundaries of the sequence sets the FunFams were built from rather than as origins of function. Two named cases make the point concretely: AHR is restricted to Glires (CS33) although it is broadly vertebrate, and INTS11 to Mammalia (CS34) although Integrator extends well beyond mammals. The consequence is systematic false negatives - the same protein escapes the rule in one species and is caught in another - which is also why the rule's own reviewed-protein count is uninformative. A handful of restrictions are defensible (Fungi for the RSC/polybromo clause, Eukaryota for TAF4, Metazoa for Elongin A).
