## 1. Executive summary

**Overall assessment: biologically coherent at the level of its recognizable families, but not yet auditable enough to be considered uniformly safe.** The first eight condition sets identify established components or regulators of RNA polymerase II (Pol II) transcription: Spt6, ELL, a fungal RSC/polybromo subunit, TFIIFβ/RAP30, TAF4, Elongin A, and TAF2. For Spt6, ELL, TFIIF, TAF4/TAF2, and Elongin A, annotation to **GO:0006366, “transcription by RNA polymerase II,”** is well supported. These proteins act directly in initiation or elongation; a 2024 review explicitly classifies Spt6, ELL, and Elongin A as Pol II elongation factors. It also reports that cellular Pol II elongation can exceed 2,000 nucleotides per minute, compared with approximately 300 nucleotides per minute for purified Pol II, illustrating the importance of auxiliary factors (Archuleta et al., published February 2024; https://doi.org/10.3390/biom14020176). (archuleta2024mechanismsandfunctions pages 11-12)

The rule becomes less satisfactory for proteins whose primary mechanistic descriptions are **regulation, chromatin remodeling, termination, or RNA processing**. Fungal RSC/polybromo proteins participate in transcription through chromatin remodeling but also function in DNA repair and chromosome biology. AHR is a ligand-regulated transcription factor, making the broad process term true but uninformative. INTS11 is most specifically a Pol II-associated RNA endonuclease involved in snRNA 3′ processing and promoter-proximal termination; GO:0006366 is therefore substantially broader than its best-established role. Recent Integrator literature supports annotations to Pol II transcription regulation, termination, RNA processing, and nuclease activity rather than relying on the parent transcription process alone (Welsh and Gardini, published September 2023; https://doi.org/10.1038/s41580-022-00534-2). (welsh2023genomicregulationof pages 17-19, welsh2023genomicregulationof pages 19-20)

The largest unresolved concern is rule architecture. Forty-seven condition sets rely wholly or partly on numeric CATH FunFam identifiers that cannot be biologically interpreted from the rule text. Because all 55 sets are connected by **OR**, one broad, obsolete, or functionally heterogeneous FunFam can independently trigger the annotation. The present output count of **zero annotated proteins** means there is no current false-positive burden, but it also prevents empirical validation through inspection of predicted entries. Zero coverage may indicate a dormant or overconstrained rule rather than a demonstrably precise one.

**Recommendation:** retain the high-specificity InterPro combinations, separate mechanistically different families into family-specific rules, replace or supplement GO:0006366 with stage-specific child terms, and audit every numeric FunFam against current CATH membership and experimentally characterized proteins before activating the rule.

### 2.1 Spt6: condition set 1

The conjunction of a RuvA-domain-2-like fold, an RNase-H-like superfamily fold, and the **Spt6-specific helix–turn–helix DNA-binding domain** is much more diagnostic than either generic fold alone. Spt6 is an established Pol II elongation and chromatin factor, functioning with transcribing polymerase and helping restore nucleosome organization behind it. Current synthesis explicitly places Spt6 among core Pol II elongation factors. Thus, GO:0006366 is appropriate, with a more informative additional annotation to Pol II transcription elongation and transcription-coupled chromatin organization (Archuleta et al., 2024). (archuleta2024mechanismsandfunctions pages 11-12)

The generic RNase-H-like and RuvA-like folds occur in many unrelated proteins, so the Spt6-specific third signature is essential. The three-part conjunction is logically sound; none of these generic domains should be used alone.

### 2.2 ELL/occludin family: condition set 2

ELL proteins directly increase the catalytic rate of Pol II elongation. The combination of an **ELL N-terminal signature** with ELL/occludin-family and occludin-homology domains is substantially more specific than the shared C-terminal domain by itself. Current Pol II reviews continue to classify ELL as a direct elongation factor. (archuleta2024mechanismsandfunctions pages 11-12)

This family is multifunctional. The occludin-homology region is shared with the epithelial tight-junction protein occludin, and isolated ELL C-terminal regions have reported cytoplasmic effects on canonical Wnt signaling. ELL activity is also context dependent: under genotoxic stress, ELL phosphorylation and increased EAF1 association can contribute to global transcriptional inhibition. Accordingly, the full architecture supports Pol II transcription, whereas an occludin-homology domain alone would not. The preferred process annotation is **Pol II transcription elongation** or its regulation, with GO:0006366 retained as a valid parent.

### 2.3 Fungal RSC/polybromo proteins: condition set 3

A bromodomain plus the RSC/polybromo signature under a fungal restriction plausibly identifies a fungal RSC chromatin-remodeling subunit. ATP-dependent SWI/SNF-family remodelers alter nucleosome position or occupancy and thereby regulate access by Pol II and other DNA-associated machinery. Nevertheless, these complexes also function in DNA replication, repair, genome stability, and chromosome segregation; transcriptional effects can be activating or repressive.

PBRM1 illustrates the specificity problem. It contains six bromodomains, two BAH domains, and an HMG DNA-binding domain, and its bromodomains can recognize acetylated proteins and RNA as well as acetylated histones. Related BRD7 and BRD9 subunits can occupy overlapping regulatory contexts. Therefore, a bromodomain is not diagnostic for either RSC/polybromo identity or Pol II transcription (Park and Kirkland, published January 2025 from a 2024 manuscript; https://doi.org/10.1139/bcb-2024-0224). (park2025theroleof pages 4-6, park2025theroleof pages 8-9, park2025theroleof pages 6-8)

The full condition is likely valid, but **ATP-dependent chromatin remodeling** and **regulation of Pol II transcription** describe the biology better than GO:0006366 alone.

### 2.4 TFIIFβ/RAP30: condition set 4

The TFIIFβ family signature, RAP30/RAP74-interaction module, and winged-helix-like domain form a coherent TFIIFβ architecture. TFIIF recruits and stabilizes Pol II in the preinitiation complex, suppresses nonspecific DNA binding, participates in promoter opening and escape, and can stimulate elongation. The literature therefore strongly supports GO:0006366 and more specific initiation, promoter-escape, and elongation annotations. (archuleta2024mechanismsandfunctions pages 18-19)

The caution is evolutionary reuse. Pol I A49/A34.5 and Pol III machinery contain structurally related TFIIF/TFIIE-like modules. In Pol I, the related A49 tandem winged-helix contributes DNA binding and processivity, while other regions support cleavage and polymerase binding. Thus, generic dimerization or winged-helix signatures are not Pol II-specific; the TFIIFβ family signature must remain mandatory (Geiger et al., published August 2010; https://doi.org/10.1016/j.molcel.2010.07.028). (geiger2010rnapolymerasei pages 9-10)

### 2.5 TAF4/TFIID: condition sets 5 and 6

TAF4 is a component of TFIID, the basal factor that recognizes promoter elements and nucleates Pol II preinitiation-complex assembly. Human TFIID is approximately **1,300 kDa** and comprises TBP plus **13 TAFs**. This provides strong support for GO:0006366 when TAF4-specific signatures are present (Archuleta et al., 2024). (archuleta2024mechanismsandfunctions pages 1-2)

The individual folds are not sufficient. TAFH/NHR1 is a protein-interaction hub also found in ETO-family transcriptional corepressors, and histone-fold domains occur in multiple TAFs and other chromatin complexes. Alternative TAF4 isoforms and paralogs can have tissue- or developmental-stage-specific functions. Condition set 5 is persuasive because TAFH is paired with the TAF4 C-terminal signature and TAFH superfamily call. Condition set 6 is also reasonable because the histone fold is combined with a TAF4 family signature and eukaryotic restriction. More specific annotations should include TFIID assembly, preinitiation-complex assembly, and Pol II initiation.

### 2.6 Elongin A/SIII: condition set 7

This is one of the strongest clauses. The SIII-A and Elongin-A signatures, combined with a metazoan restriction, identify ELOA rather than the smaller ELOB/ELOC proteins that also participate in ubiquitin-ligase complexes.

A 2023 cryo-EM study resolved three human Pol II–SPT6–Elongin structures. ELOA binds the RPB2 side of Pol II through a C-terminal linker and a “latch” near the polymerase active center; both are required for elongation stimulation. Its binding overlaps with SEC, PAF1–LEO1, and RTF1, indicating mutually exclusive elongation-factor states rather than nonspecific association (Chen et al., published November 2023; https://doi.org/10.1038/s41594-023-01138-w). (chen2023structureofthe pages 9-10)

GO:0006366 is correct, but **positive regulation of Pol II transcription elongation** is more informative.

### 2.7 TAF2: condition set 8

TAF2 is a TFIID component. Its aminopeptidase-N-like N-terminal and peptidase M4/M1-like C-terminal folds reflect structural ancestry and should not be interpreted as evidence of ordinary aminopeptidase activity. Pairing these folds with a TAF2-specific signature is therefore essential. Because TFIID nucleates Pol II preinitiation-complex formation, the broad transcription term is reasonable, with more specific annotations to TFIID, core-promoter recognition, and Pol II initiation. (archuleta2024mechanismsandfunctions pages 1-2)

### 2.8 AHR: condition set 33

The two named AHR FunFams plus Glires identify the aryl hydrocarbon receptor, a ligand-activated bHLH–PAS DNA-binding transcription factor. Activated AHR regulates target genes through Pol II machinery, but its output varies with ligand, partner, tissue, and chromatin context and can include activation, repression, and noncanonical signaling. A 2024 review emphasizes AHR’s context-dependent functions in barrier defense, inflammation, infection, and disease (Xu et al., published April 2024; https://doi.org/10.3389/fimmu.2024.1367734).

GO:0006366 is defensible as an ancestor process, but **regulation of transcription by RNA polymerase II**, sequence-specific transcription-factor activity, and xenobiotic-response signaling are more diagnostic. The Glires restriction appears unnecessarily narrow unless the FunFams were trained specifically on rodent/lagomorph AHR and cannot distinguish orthologues elsewhere.

### 2.9 INTS11: condition set 34

INTS11 is the catalytic RNA endonuclease of Integrator. Integrator is recruited broadly around the transition from initiation to promoter-proximal pausing, cleaves nascent RNA, regulates coding and noncoding transcription, and participates in Pol II termination. It also performs snRNA 3′-end maturation. The complex includes separable cleavage, nucleic-acid-binding, enhancer, and phosphatase modules, underscoring that INTS11 is not simply a generic transcription factor (Welsh and Gardini, 2023). (welsh2023genomicregulationof pages 17-19, welsh2023genomicregulationof pages 19-20)

Recent structural work showed how the complete Integrator–PP2A complex rearranges to terminate paused Pol II, including opening the DSIF DNA clamp and preventing Pol II rebinding after termination (Fianu et al., published April 2024; https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-07269-4). The best annotations are therefore Integrator-dependent Pol II termination, negative regulation of Pol II transcription, snRNA 3′ processing, and RNA endonuclease activity. GO:0006366 may be retained as a broad ancestor, but it should not be the sole prediction.

### 2.10 Numeric CATH FunFams: condition sets 9–32 and 35–55

These 47 sets cannot be responsibly evaluated family by family from numeric identifiers alone. Taxonomic constraints suggest that they represent orthologous or lineage-specific subfamilies of transcription proteins, but structural membership does not itself establish participation in Pol II transcription. Some CATH folds are repeatedly reused in Pol I, Pol III, chromatin, RNA-processing, signaling, and DNA-repair proteins.

Their opacity is especially problematic because each clause independently triggers the annotation. The rule needs a machine-readable mapping from each FunFam to protein name, representative experimentally characterized members, domain coverage, CATH release, and evidence supporting GO:0006366. Until that audit exists, these clauses should be considered **unvalidated**, not assumed equivalent to the interpretable InterPro sets.

## 3. GO-term evaluation

### Appropriateness

GO:0006366 denotes the complete process of transcription by Pol II, spanning initiation through elongation and termination. It is consequently valid as a broad biological-process annotation for proteins that directly and specifically participate in that cycle. Current reviews emphasize that Pol II machinery acts across a continuum of preinitiation-complex assembly, promoter opening, initiation, productive elongation, and termination (Archuleta et al., 2024). (archuleta2024mechanismsandfunctions pages 11-12, archuleta2024mechanismsandfunctions pages 1-2)

The term is:

- **Well supported but broad** for Spt6, ELL, TFIIFβ, TAF4, Elongin A, and TAF2.
- **Biologically defensible but mechanistically weak** for RSC/polybromo and AHR, because these regulate selected Pol II loci rather than constituting the universal catalytic machinery.
- **Overbroad as a sole annotation** for INTS11, whose most direct roles are nascent-RNA cleavage, snRNA processing, transcription attenuation, and termination.
- **Not assessable** for the anonymous numeric FunFams without membership data.

### Better companion or replacement concepts

The rule should favor the most specific experimentally supported process:

1. **Spt6, ELL, Elongin A:** Pol II transcription elongation or positive regulation of elongation.
2. **TFIIFβ:** Pol II preinitiation-complex assembly, initiation, promoter escape, and elongation.
3. **TAF4 and TAF2:** TFIID complex assembly/function, core-promoter recognition, and Pol II initiation.
4. **RSC/polybromo:** ATP-dependent chromatin remodeling and regulation of Pol II transcription.
5. **AHR:** ligand-activated, sequence-specific regulation of Pol II transcription.
6. **INTS11:** Integrator-dependent Pol II termination, negative regulation/attenuation, snRNA 3′-end processing, and RNA endonuclease activity.

These should be emitted from separate family rules, allowing GO:0006366 to propagate as an ancestor where the ontology supports it rather than using it as the only explicit prediction.

## 4. Evidence review

### Strong direct evidence

The strongest recent primary evidence concerns Elongin A. The 2023 cryo-EM structures identify the physical ELOA–Pol II interface and show experimentally that its latch is required for elongation stimulation, establishing causality rather than mere colocalization. They also explain competition among elongation factors at overlapping Pol II surfaces (Chen et al., 2023). (chen2023structureofthe pages 9-10)

TFIIF has extensive biochemical and structural support for roles in recruitment, initiation, promoter escape, and elongation. Comparative structural evidence also shows why the complete family architecture is necessary: TFIIF-like modules have been incorporated into Pol I and Pol III systems (Geiger et al., 2010). (archuleta2024mechanismsandfunctions pages 18-19, geiger2010rnapolymerasei pages 9-10)

TFIID’s central role is well established. TFIID binds core-promoter elements and nucleates the Pol II preinitiation complex; its size and 14-component composition illustrate why individual generic folds should not substitute for subunit-level identification (Archuleta et al., 2024). (archuleta2024mechanismsandfunctions pages 1-2)

### Evidence supporting narrower or context-dependent interpretation

Integrator exemplifies a family for which “involved in Pol II transcription” is correct but incomplete. The 2023 review describes recruitment at paused Pol II, nascent-RNA cleavage, ncRNA biogenesis, enhancer regulation, and phosphatase coupling. These data support transcription regulation and termination but also establish a separate RNA-processing function (Welsh and Gardini, 2023). (welsh2023genomicregulationof pages 17-19, welsh2023genomicregulationof pages 19-20)

Polybromo/BAF evidence likewise argues against a single-function interpretation. PBRM1 and related bromodomain proteins are multifunctional chromatin regulators; they bind diverse acetylated substrates and RNA, and can activate or repress genes while also participating in DNA damage responses and chromosome maintenance. Thus, transcription is one output of a broader chromatin-remodeling role. (park2025theroleof pages 4-6, park2025theroleof pages 8-9, park2025theroleof pages 6-8)

### Contradictory or qualifying findings

No retrieved study contradicts the direct Pol II roles of Spt6, ELL, TFIIF, TAF4/TAF2, or Elongin A. The qualifications concern **functional breadth and context**, not absence of transcriptional involvement:

- ELL can promote productive elongation yet participate in stress-induced transcriptional inhibition.
- Polybromo/RSC complexes can activate or repress transcription and perform non-transcriptional genome-maintenance functions.
- AHR responses vary by ligand and cellular setting.
- INTS11 can suppress productive transcription through premature termination while simultaneously processing Pol II-derived RNAs.
- Shared folds such as bromodomains, histone folds, RNase-H-like folds, winged helices, and metallo-β-lactamase folds are not independently diagnostic.

### Taxonomic considerations

The fundamental Pol II system is highly conserved across eukaryotes, although subunit composition and regulatory specialization vary. (archuleta2024mechanismsandfunctions pages 11-12)

- **Fungi for RSC/polybromo:** sensible if the signature is specifically a fungal RSC subunit; the homologous PBAF system exists in animals, so an equivalent animal rule may be warranted using animal-specific signatures.
- **Eukaryota for TAF4:** appropriate in principle, though deeply divergent eukaryotes may have weak or altered domain signatures.
- **Metazoa for Elongin A:** consistent with the retrieved structural study’s description of heterotrimeric Elongin as conserved among metazoa. (chen2023structureofthe pages 9-10)
- **Glires for AHR:** probably too narrow biologically; AHR is broadly vertebrate. Expansion should follow phylogenetic testing of the particular FunFams.
- **Mammalia for INTS11:** also likely too narrow biologically; Integrator is present beyond mammals. Expansion requires ensuring that both FunFams discriminate INTS11 from related metallo-β-lactamase nucleases.
- Very narrow restrictions in the numeric FunFam block—Rattus, Mus, Homo, primates, Haplorrhini, Catarrhini, and several fungal subclades—may reflect classifier boundaries rather than genuine origins of Pol II function. Such restrictions should be justified by paralogy or domain divergence, not merely by the training sequences available.

## 5. Recommendations

1. **Split ARBA00028158 by mechanistic family.** A single OR-rule currently conflates basal initiation factors, elongation factors, chromatin remodelers, sequence-specific transcription factors, and a termination/RNA-processing nuclease. Separate rules will permit appropriate GO specificity and independent quality control.

2. **Retain condition sets 1, 2, 4, 5, 7, and 8 after sequence-level spot checks.** Their multi-domain combinations are coherent and substantially reduce false positives from shared folds. Condition set 6 should be retained only if IPR045144 is demonstrably TAF4-specific across current InterPro releases.

3. **Revise condition set 3 to predict chromatin remodeling/regulation first.** Keep GO:0006366 only as a supported ancestor or secondary process. Require the RSC/polybromo family signature and fungal taxonomy; never use the bromodomain alone.

4. **Replace the AHR output with regulation-specific terms.** Expand beyond Glires only after testing orthologues and AHR paralogs across vertebrates.

5. **Replace or supplement the INTS11 output with termination and processing terms.** Require both INTS11 FunFams, preservation of catalytic residues, and preferably an Integrator-specific family signature. Metallo-β-lactamase-fold membership alone is insufficient.

6. **Audit all 47 opaque numeric FunFam clauses.** For each, document current name, representative proteins, taxonomic range, domain boundaries, paralogs, experimental evidence, and false-positive results against Swiss-Prot. Suspend clauses whose family identity cannot be reconstructed.

7. **Check redundancy and hierarchy.** Several FunFam conjunctions may be different structural fragments of the same protein family. If one specific whole-family model provides equal or better discrimination, redundant fragment clauses can be consolidated. Conversely, no fragment should independently trigger a broad process annotation unless it is family-specific.

8. **Add negative safeguards where necessary.** Examples include excluding occludin-like membrane proteins from ELL predictions, non-TAF aminopeptidases from TAF2, Pol I/III TFIIF-like proteins from TFIIFβ, and unrelated metallo-β-lactamase nucleases from INTS11.

9. **Investigate the zero-hit status.** Validate the rule against a positive-control panel of reviewed Spt6, ELL, GTF2F2, TAF4, ELOA, TAF2, AHR, and INTS11 proteins. A rule that fails known positives because current domain models no longer co-occur should be repaired or retired; zero predictions cannot establish precision.

10. **Overall disposition:** retain only after refactoring. The recognizable InterPro clauses have strong biological foundations, but the broad GO output and unaudited OR-ed FunFams make the present aggregate rule harder to defend than a set of smaller, family-specific rules.
