Description

Assigns GO:0006661 (phosphatidylinositol biosynthetic process) from eleven alternative condition sets covering PI synthase, phosphoinositide kinases and phosphatases, and two lipid-transfer protein families.

The domain overlap table is not shown for this review. It is drawn from a cached analysis of the rule's condition sets, and the cache does not match the sets recorded here.

Review Summary

Accuracy: all eleven condition sets of the rule as served on 2026-11-10 were queried in UniProtKB and the reviewed matches of each were looked up in QuickGO. In every set the reviewed human, mouse, yeast or Arabidopsis members carry GO:0006661 (phosphatidylinositol biosynthetic process) on evidence other than this rule: Reactome TAS from the pathways R-HSA-1660499, R-HSA-1660516, R-HSA-1483248 and R-HSA-1483226, MGI IDA and IGI for the mouse PIP5K1 kinases (PMID:20622009), TIGR TAS for the plant PI 4-kinases, and IDA for PI synthase. So the rule reproduces what is in the annotation files. Set 11 is the exception: its FunFam is the FYVE zinc finger and it also matches Lst2 homologs, which have no phosphatase domain and carry the term only from this rule. Meaning and parsimony: reproducing annotations is not the same as saying something sensible. Only sets 8 and 9 select the enzyme that makes phosphatidylinositol, PI synthase. Sets 1, 2, 3, 5 and 11 select phosphoinositide phosphatases whose experimentally curated process is GO:0046856 (phosphatidylinositol dephosphorylation); sets 4 and 7 select phosphoinositide kinases, whose own term is GO:0046854 (phosphatidylinositol phosphate biosynthetic process); sets 6 and 10 select lipid transfer proteins curated to lipid transport terms. None of those terms is under GO:0006661, which has no descendants. The biosynthesis term reaches the non-synthase families through a pathway-membership mapping (Reactome "Synthesis of PIPs" pathways), so the rule puts "PI biosynthesis" on enzymes that remove phosphates from, or move, already made phosphoinositides. The rule is heterogeneous and should be split by what each family does.

Action Rationale

The first pass kept the term for the kinase, phosphatase and transfer-protein sets because curated annotations carry it, and left the choice of term to Reactome, MGI and GO. That deference answers whether the proteins were annotated, not whether the term is meaningful for them, and the experimental annotations of the same proteins answer the second question directly: MTM1, MTMR3, SAC1 and SYNJ1 are curated by IDA to phosphatidylinositol dephosphorylation, PITPNM1 to phospholipid transport and PLEKHA8 to lipid transport. One rule cannot carry a single term that is right for a synthase, its product's kinases, their opposing phosphatases and lipid carriers. Splitting keeps the sound part (PI synthase) and gives each other family its own term. The set 11 defect found in the first pass stands and is carried into the split.

Assessments

OVERLY_COMPLEX

Eleven sets assemble eight families under one term that names what one of them does. rule_redundancy.tsv: go_descendants 0, condition_sets 11, covered_sets 0, so no set is idle in the structural sense; QuickGO returns 5,394 annotations with withFrom ARBA:ARBA00028655 (2026-11-12). Within the rule, sets 1 and 3 both select myotubularins (5 and 3 reviewed members with curated support, overlapping in MTM1, MTMR1, MTMR2), and set 11 is loose (126 of 3,192 matches lack the phosphatase domain). rules/arba/_interpro2go.txt maps one InterPro entry to GO:0006661, IPR026825 (Vacuole morphology and inheritance protein 14), which none of the sets use.

MISMATCHED

For sets 8 and 9 the term names what PI synthase does. For the other nine sets it is the wrong kind of term: QuickGO (2026-11-12) does not place GO:0046856 (phosphatidylinositol dephosphorylation), GO:0046854 (phosphatidylinositol phosphate biosynthetic process) or GO:0015914 (phospholipid transport) under GO:0006661, and those are the terms the reviewed members carry on experimental or phylogenetic evidence. The mismatch originates in the curated Reactome TAS annotations and is reproduced, not created, by the rule.

TOO_NARROW

The narrow restrictions follow the species of the annotations each set copies, not the biology: Primates, Haplorrhini, Catarrhini and Hominidae where the support is human Reactome TAS (sets 1, 2, 3, 5, 6, 10), and Mus where it is MGI IDA and IGI on the mouse PIP5K1 kinases (set 4). These are pan-eukaryotic enzyme families, so that is narrow coverage, not wrong annotation. The PI synthase sets are scoped to Chordata (set 8) and Fungi (set 9), which is reasonable. Two sets are wider than their evidence: set 7 is Eukaryota (482 matches) on two Arabidopsis TAS annotations, and set 11 is Metazoa and reaches the Lst2 proteins.
