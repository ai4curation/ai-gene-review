Description

Assigns GO:0006720 isoprenoid metabolic process through 89 alternative condition sets built from InterPro entries, PANTHER families and CATH FunFams, 70 of them with a taxon condition. The sets cover terpene synthases and cyclases, prenyl diphosphate synthases, carotenoid synthesis and cleavage enzymes, gibberellin and abscisic acid oxidases, mevalonate pathway enzymes and retinoid-metabolising enzymes.

The domain overlap table is not shown for this review. It is drawn from a cached analysis of the rule's condition sets, and the cache does not match the sets recorded here.

Review Summary

Accuracy: all 89 live condition sets were queried against Swiss-Prot on 2026-11-09, counting a match as supported when it carries GO:0006720 (isoprenoid metabolic process) or an is_a/part_of descendant with evidence other than ARBA. Every set has reviewed matches; in 75 sets every match is supported and in 14 some are not. One defect is demonstrable: CS18 (PANTHER PTHR43734 in Pseudomonadati) matches GlpB, a glycerol-3-phosphate dehydrogenase subunit with no isoprenoid role, in 8 of its 22 reviewed matches. Q6PQJ9, a 3-oxo-Delta(4,5)-steroid 5-beta-reductase (CS37), and Q54E48 (CS1) are doubtful. QuickGO lists 9043 annotations attributed to the rule. Meaning and parsimony: isoprenoid metabolic process is the common ancestor of pathways that have little else in common, and the sets divide among them: plant and microbial terpene synthases, carotenoid biosynthesis enzymes, animal carotenoid oxygenases, retinoid-metabolising enzymes, gibberellin oxidases, prenyl diphosphate synthases, the mevalonate and methylerythritol phosphate pathways and juvenile hormone synthesis. Each has a specific curated descendant. rule_redundancy.tsv finds 65 of 89 sets already matched by a rule asserting a more specific term. Four sets select proteins for which the term is nominal: the triglyceride lipases LIPE and PNPLA2 (sets 40, 59), the endocytic receptors LRP1 and LRP2 (set 57) and the drug-metabolising cytochromes P450 2C and 3A4 (sets 35, 74). Their curated roles are triglyceride catabolism, receptor-mediated endocytosis and xenobiotic metabolism; the isoprenoid term comes from a retinyl ester side activity, a Reactome pathway step or one terpene substrate.

Action Rationale

The first pass kept the rule and asked for CS18 to be fixed. That fix still stands, but the larger point is that the term is the wrong level for nearly every set: the units are separable and each has a better term, two thirds of the sets are already covered by rules with such terms, and several sets select proteins whose function lies elsewhere. The rule should be split by pathway, the nominal sets dropped and CS18 tightened. Units were assigned for 43 of the 89 sets from signature labels with one member looked up per unit; confidence reflects that.

Assessments

REDUNDANT

rule_redundancy.tsv: 65 of 89 condition sets are matched by a rule asserting a more specific term and emit nothing; the rest account for the 9043 annotations QuickGO attributes to the rule. Farnesyl diphosphate synthase alone enters through four sets and phytoene desaturase through at least five. rules/arba/_interpro2go.txt has no InterPro entry mapped to GO:0006720.

TOO_BROAD

GO:0006720 has 200 is_a/part_of descendants (rule_redundancy.tsv). It is the common ancestor of the terpenoid, carotenoid, retinoid and prenyl diphosphate sets, which is why the first pass called it appropriate, but ten of thirteen units examined have a specific curated descendant and the other three have their informative term outside it.

APPROPRIATE

Most taxon conditions are plant clades on terpene synthase and P450 FunFams (Lamiaceae, Nepetoideae, asterids, Pinopsida), which follow lineage-specific specialised metabolism. Some confine conserved enzymes to where their annotations are, such as cytochrome P450 3A4 to Homo (set 74), P450 2C9 to Primates (set 35) and farnesyl diphosphate synthase to Glires (set 63); that is narrow coverage, not wrong annotation. CS18 is too broad in effect because its signature, not its taxon, admits GlpB.
