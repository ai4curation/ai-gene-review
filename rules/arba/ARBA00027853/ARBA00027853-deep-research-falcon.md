# Evaluation of UniProt rule ARBA00027853

## 1. Executive summary

**Overall judgment: biologically coherent at the GO-term level, but unsafe as a single 94-branch OR rule.** GO:0006720, *isoprenoid metabolic process*, is an appropriate broad parent annotation for authentic enzymes of the mevalonate/MEP pathways, prenyl-diphosphate synthesis, terpene cyclization, carotenoid metabolism, gibberellin and abscisic-acid metabolism, retinoid metabolism, sterol/triterpenoid metabolism, and juvenile-hormone metabolism. Recent pathway reviews explicitly place carotenoids, gibberellins, ABA, strigolactones, brassinosteroids and related hormones within metabolism originating from IPP/DMAPP and prenyl diphosphates (bajguz2023biosyntheticpathwaysof pages 18-20, bajguz2023biosyntheticpathwaysof pages 26-28, bajguz2023biosyntheticpathwaysof pages 12-14, bajguz2023biosyntheticpathwaysof pages 22-24).

The rule nevertheless mixes highly diagnostic catalytic families with broad or unrelated folds. Core terpene synthases, phytoene synthases/desaturases, lycopene cyclases, prenyl diphosphate synthases, HMGR, mevalonate kinase, diphosphomevalonate decarboxylase, DXS, HMBPP reductase and named gibberellin enzymes strongly support GO:0006720. In contrast, generic P450, glycosyltransferase, SDR, ALDH, ADH, lipase/phospholipase and receptor families do not establish pathway membership. Plant P450s are particularly plastic: one experimentally studied CYP706A3 accepts more than 20 mono- and sesquiterpenes plus herbicides, with 29 documented substrates, while CYP720B4 acts on 8 of 24 tested diterpene skeletons (werckreichhart2023promiscuityadriver pages 2-4, werckreichhart2023promiscuityadriver pages 1-2).

Several branches appear clearly off-target—notably CYP2C9, CYP83B1, hormone-sensitive lipase, PNPLA2, LDLR/LRP1, phenylalanine aminomutase, class-4 alcohol dehydrogenase and formaldehyde dehydrogenase. Opaque or unnamed FunFams also cannot be accepted without inspecting their experimentally characterized seed members. This concern is amplified by the stated outcome of **zero currently annotated proteins**: there is no present production set from which empirical precision can be measured.

**Recommended disposition:** split ARBA00027853 into pathway-specific rules; retain diagnostic core branches; manually validate narrow named FunFams; remove unrelated branches; and require subfamily, catalytic-site and domain-architecture evidence for broad tailoring enzymes. GO:0006720 may be retained as an ancestor annotation, but more specific descendant process terms should be assigned whenever the signature identifies a particular pathway.

## 2. Domain and signature analysis

### 2.1 Canonical terpene synthases and cyclases

Conditions 1–3, 10–12, 19–22, 25, 27, 40, 46–47, 56, 61–62 and 70–71 represent plant, microbial or amoebozoan terpene synthases/cyclases. The canonical class-I catalytic α-domain contains the metal-binding machinery that supports ionization of prenyl diphosphates; plant enzymes frequently carry additional N-terminal β or βγ domains. Conditions combining the N-terminal TPS domain with the metal-binding C-terminal domain are substantially stronger than either domain alone.

These signatures are diagnostic for **terpene-forming chemistry**, but usually not for a particular product. Plant TPS families evolved through duplication, domain loss/gain/fusion and functional divergence; small active-site changes can redirect carbocation cascades, substrate choice and product profiles (karunanithi2019terpenesynthasesas pages 14-16). Thus, signatures for “terpene synthase,” “terpene cyclase,” or the α-toroid fold support GO:0006720, whereas product-specific annotations such as carene, linalool or taxadiene biosynthesis require narrower FunFam/subfamily or experimental evidence.

The taxonomic restrictions are plausible where they reflect known lineage expansions—Ocimeae/Nepetoideae/Elsholtzieae, *Pinus*, Poaceae, Fagales and Amoebozoa—but taxonomy cannot compensate for an incomplete catalytic domain or a pseudogene. Full-length architecture, intact DDxxD/NSE-type metal-binding motifs for class-I enzymes, and the appropriate class-II acidic motif where relevant should be required.

Condition 20 is unusually stringent because it requires simultaneous assignment to two differently named FunFams, ent-kaurene synthase and carene synthase. If these FunFams correspond to different domains of a multidomain protein, the logic may be valid; if they are alternative classifications of the same domain region, the conjunction may be internally contradictory or yield no proteins. Its zero-hit behavior should be investigated.

### 2.2 Isoprenoid precursor and prenyl-diphosphate enzymes

Conditions 16, 34, 41–42, 45, 52, 67–69, 88 and 93 cover HMGR, mevalonate kinase, diphosphomevalonate decarboxylase, DXS, HMBPP reductase, farnesyl/geranylgeranyl/decaprenyl diphosphate synthases and related polyprenyl synthetases. These are among the rule’s strongest branches because they either produce IPP/DMAPP or assemble the prenyl donors used in downstream isoprenoid pathways.

The polyprenyl/isoprenoid-synthase α-helical fold is conserved across enzymes with different chain lengths and cis/trans geometries. It is therefore diagnostic of prenyl-chain chemistry when paired with a sufficiently narrow subfamily, but not by itself diagnostic of FPP, GGPP or a particular long-chain product. Conditions using named FunFams or FPPS-like domains with taxonomic context are reasonable for GO:0006720; exact product terms should follow experimentally supported chain-length specificity.

### 2.3 Squalene/phytoene synthases and carotenoid enzymes

Conditions 5, 13, 17–18, 24, 30, 32–33, 53, 59–60 and 79–80 represent squalene/phytoene synthases, carotenoid desaturases, lycopene cyclases and related proteins. These are direct isoprenoid-metabolism signatures. Phytoene synthase condenses GGPP into phytoene; desaturation and cyclization then generate lycopene and cyclic carotenes. Carotenoids are isoprenoids, and their oxidative cleavage yields ABA, strigolactones, retinal and other apocarotenoids (bajguz2023biosyntheticpathwaysof pages 12-14, bajguz2023biosyntheticpathwaysof pages 22-24).

The IPR002060 plus conserved-site combination in condition 5 is strong for the squalene/phytoene synthase family but cannot, without subfamily evidence, distinguish sterol/triterpene from carotenoid entry reactions. GO:0006720 is therefore safer than either a squalene- or phytoene-specific process term.

Condition 7, mammalian carotenoid oxygenase, is also on-target because carotenoid cleavage is carotenoid/isoprenoid catabolism. Condition 9 is weaker: amine-oxidase, carotenoid/retinoid oxidoreductase and generic FAD/NAD(P)-binding signatures may identify a relevant oxidoreductase family, but broad cofactor-binding folds are not independently diagnostic.

### 2.4 Gibberellin, ABA and other plant isoprenoid-hormone enzymes

Conditions 21, 25, 28–29, 31, 57–58, 74, 76, 79, 86 and 90–92 include CPS/KS-related proteins, ent-kaurene oxidases, GA20ox, GA3ox, GA2ox, ABA-related P450s, carotene monooxygenases and squalene monooxygenase.

Gibberellins are diterpenoid isoprenoids synthesized through GGPP → ent-copalyl diphosphate → ent-kaurene → ent-kaurenoic acid → GA12. CPS, KS, KO and KAO catalyze the early steps; GA20ox and GA3ox produce bioactive GAs, while GA2ox and selected P450s deactivate them (bajguz2023biosyntheticpathwaysof pages 18-20, bajguz2023biosyntheticpathwaysof pages 20-22, bajguz2023biosyntheticpathwaysof pages 33-34). Both biosynthesis and catabolism fall under “metabolic process,” so catabolic GA2ox or ABA 8′-hydroxylase branches are legitimate even though they do not synthesize the hormone.

The named GA FunFams are strong. Bare IDs in conditions 90 and 92 require validation before transfer. Condition 74 is likely intended to denote an ABA 8′-hydroxylase family, but the truncated label “Abscisic acid 8” should be corrected and mapped to a characterized CYP707-type family before use.

### 2.5 Cytochrome P450s and other tailoring enzymes

Conditions 6, 8, 23, 26, 35, 37, 57–58, 72–78 and some unnamed branches use P450 signatures. A narrow FunFam for ent-kaurene oxidase, CYP26, taxadiene hydroxylase or carotene monooxygenase can strongly support an isoprenoid pathway. A generic P450 domain, broad PANTHER family and taxon restriction generally cannot.

The recent consensus is that P450 promiscuity is common rather than exceptional. CYP76M8 hydroxylates seven diterpene skeletons and contributes to three phytoalexin pathways; CYP701 paralogs differ markedly in substrate breadth; CYP71 members act on terpenoids, oximes, cyanogenic glucosides and glucosinolates; and CYP76 enzymes, although often isoprenoid-related, have also been recruited into betalain chemistry (werckreichhart2023promiscuityadriver pages 2-4, werckreichhart2023promiscuityadriver pages 7-8, werckreichhart2023promiscuityadriver pages 12-14). P450s can also be multifunctional within one pathway, catalyzing sequential oxidations, hydroxylation plus epoxidation, or multiple oxidation states (wang2023multifunctionalenzymesin pages 8-10, wang2023multifunctionalenzymesin pages 10-12).

Consequently:

- **Retain after validation:** named CYP26A1/B1 (36, 72), ent-kaurene/kaurenoic-acid oxidases (57–58), taxadiene 5α-hydroxylase (75), and carotene ε-monooxygenase (76).
- **Review closely:** taxon-constrained CYP/PANTHER or CYP FunFam branches 6, 8, 23, 26, 35, 74 and 77.
- **Remove:** CYP2C9 in primates (37), which is chiefly a drug/xenobiotic-metabolizing family, and CYP83B1 in Asterales (73), whose canonical role is glucosinolate rather than isoprenoid metabolism.

### 2.6 Retinoid metabolism

Conditions 7, 9, 15, 36, 50, 54, 65 and 72 encompass carotenoid cleavage, retinol oxidation/reduction, retinyl ester formation, retinaldehyde oxidation and retinoic-acid catabolism. These reactions operate on carotenoid-derived isoprenoid metabolites and therefore support GO:0006720 when the family assignment is specific.

CYP26A1/B1, LRAT and a verified retinol dehydrogenase family are defensible. ALDH1A enzymes catalyzing retinaldehyde oxidation are also relevant, but condition 43 combines mitochondrial ALDH2, a generic aldehyde dehydrogenase and ALDH1A3-like FunFams. This conjunction is biologically suspicious and risks transferring retinoid metabolism to ubiquitous aldehyde-detoxification enzymes. Condition 65 is safer only if the resulting FunFam truly corresponds to retinaldehyde dehydrogenase rather than the broader ALDH superfamily.

Condition 15 (PTHR10543:SF57, Metazoa) cannot be evaluated from its opaque identifier alone and requires Panther-member inspection. Condition 50, RDH12 in Haplorrhini, is plausible but the taxon restriction is unnecessarily narrow if orthologous RDH12 function is conserved more broadly in vertebrates.

### 2.7 Glycosyltransferases, SDRs, ALDHs, ADHs, lipases and receptors

Conditions 14, 43–44, 49–51, 54–55, 63–65, 82–85 and 94 illustrate the central weakness of the rule: broad folds can act on an isoprenoid in one subfamily but on unrelated metabolites in many others.

- IPR002213 UDP-glycosyltransferases modify terpenoids, flavonoids, phenolics, hormones and xenobiotics. Caryophyllaceae taxonomy does not make the domain diagnostic; conditions 14 and 83 require a terpenoid-specific subfamily.
- SDR, ALDH and ADH folds cover extensive lipid, steroid, retinoid, sugar, alcohol and detoxification chemistry. Conditions 49 and 50 may be valid narrow FunFams, but 43, 51 and 55 are not suitable proxies for isoprenoid metabolism.
- Hormone-sensitive lipase (44) and PNPLA2/ATGL-like phospholipase (64) mediate neutral-lipid hydrolysis, not isoprenoid metabolism.
- LDLR/LRP1 (63) mediates ligand uptake/trafficking and is not an isoprenoid metabolic enzyme.
- Phenylalanine aminomutase (66) belongs to amino-acid specialized metabolism.

These branches should not be retained merely because their substrates or transported particles can contain lipid-soluble isoprenoids.

### 2.8 CATH FunFam evidence and limitations

FunFams are more functionally coherent than broad structural superfamilies and are useful for automated annotation. However, published benchmarks show that domain-based transfer is more reliable for molecular function than biological process. DomFun achieved Fmax 0.624 for molecular function versus 0.492 for biological process in a no-knowledge partial evaluation; 18.42% of biological-process training proteins and 606 of 3,089 test proteins lacked CATH mappings (rojano2022assigningproteinfunction pages 12-14, rojano2022assigningproteinfunction pages 7-9). FunPredCATH reported limited-knowledge Fmax values of 0.66 for molecular function and 0.64 for biological process, with conservative intersection models improving precision at the expense of coverage and union models increasing false-positive risk (bonello2024funpredcathanensemble pages 7-10, bonello2024funpredcathanensemble pages 10-12).

At the residue level, FunFam members agreed on only 36.9 ± 0.6% of binding-residue annotations—better than random grouping by 6.7-fold, but far from complete uniformity. A stringent consensus reached 60.8 ± 0.4% precision (scheibenreif2019funfamproteinfamilies pages 1-2). Thus, a named, well-populated FunFam can be strong evidence, but a bare FunFam ID is not self-validating, particularly for a biological-process term.

## 3. GO-term evaluation

### Appropriateness

GO:0006720 is **neither too narrow nor intrinsically incorrect** for the genuine pathway enzymes in this rule. Its breadth is advantageous because it accommodates:

1. precursor formation by MVA/MEP enzymes;
2. prenyl-chain assembly;
3. terpene, carotenoid and sterol scaffold formation;
4. oxidative, reductive, cleavage, methylation, glycosylation and esterification steps; and
5. anabolic and catabolic metabolism of isoprenoid-derived hormones.

For example, PSY and carotenoid enzymes support carotenoid metabolism; CCDs participate in ABA/strigolactone/apocarotenoid formation; KO/KAO/GA20ox/GA3ox/GA2ox collectively constitute gibberellin metabolism (bajguz2023biosyntheticpathwaysof pages 18-20, bajguz2023biosyntheticpathwaysof pages 20-22, bajguz2023biosyntheticpathwaysof pages 12-14, bajguz2023biosyntheticpathwaysof pages 22-24).

### Specificity

For many high-confidence branches, GO:0006720 is **too broad as the only annotation**, although it remains a valid ancestor. More informative alternatives include:

- terpene biosynthetic/metabolic process for TPS/cyclases;
- carotenoid biosynthetic or carotenoid metabolic process for PSY, PDS/ZDS, lycopene cyclases and carotenoid oxygenases;
- gibberellin biosynthetic or metabolic process for CPS/KS/KO/KAO/GA20ox/GA3ox/GA2ox;
- abscisic-acid metabolic process for ABA-pathway enzymes;
- retinoid metabolic process for CYP26, RDH, RALDH and LRAT;
- sterol or triterpenoid biosynthetic process for squalene synthase/monooxygenase;
- juvenile-hormone biosynthetic/metabolic process for JHAMT; and
- isoprenoid precursor or prenyl-diphosphate biosynthetic descendants for MVA/MEP and prenyl synthases.

The exact GO descendant should be verified against the current ontology before rule deployment. Narrow terms should not be inferred from a broad fold when product or substrate specificity is unresolved.

## 4. Evidence review and current research context

The strongest evidence is pathway-level experimental biochemistry accumulated over decades and summarized in recent authoritative reviews. The 2023 plant-hormone review documents GGPP-derived gibberellin synthesis through CPS, KS, KO and KAO, followed by GA20ox and GA3ox, and identifies carotenoid-derived ABA and strigolactone pathways (published July 2023; https://doi.org/10.3390/metabo13080884) (bajguz2023biosyntheticpathwaysof pages 18-20, bajguz2023biosyntheticpathwaysof pages 20-22, bajguz2023biosyntheticpathwaysof pages 12-14, bajguz2023biosyntheticpathwaysof pages 22-24).

Recent research emphasizes metabolic networks and enzyme promiscuity rather than simple one-enzyme/one-product pathways. The 2023 P450 analysis documents extensive substrate breadth and lineage-specific recruitment (published February 2023; https://doi.org/10.3390/biom13020394) (werckreichhart2023promiscuityadriver pages 2-4, werckreichhart2023promiscuityadriver pages 12-14, werckreichhart2023promiscuityadriver pages 1-2). This does not contradict annotation of narrow experimentally anchored CYP families, but it directly contradicts any assumption that a generic CYP domain plus plant taxon predicts isoprenoid metabolism.

TPS research similarly supports the broad process annotation while warning against product-level transfer. Active-site substitutions can profoundly redirect products, and P450s frequently elaborate TPS-generated scaffolds after cyclization (https://doi.org/10.3389/fpls.2019.01166) (karunanithi2019terpenesynthasesas pages 14-16).

Current computational-annotation research supports FunFam use but not uncritical transfer. The 2024 FunPredCATH study reported competitive Fmax values while identifying coverage, sparse-family and precision/recall limitations (published February 2024; https://doi.org/10.1016/j.bbapap.2023.140985) (bonello2024funpredcathanensemble pages 7-10, bonello2024funpredcathanensemble pages 10-12). The 2022 DomFun benchmark likewise found molecular-function predictions stronger than biological-process predictions (https://doi.org/10.1186/s12859-022-04565-6) (rojano2022assigningproteinfunction pages 12-14, rojano2022assigningproteinfunction pages 1-2).

No literature contradiction was found for assigning GO:0006720 to the established core enzyme classes. The contradiction lies instead in several individual rule branches whose named proteins have primary functions outside isoprenoid metabolism. The zero-protein output prevents a direct estimate of false-positive frequency or coverage; validation must therefore be performed against known positive and negative protein sets before activation.

## 5. Rule-logic and taxonomic assessment

The AND logic within many individual conditions is sensible: combining complementary TPS domains, a catalytic conserved site with a synthase domain, or a narrow subfamily with an appropriate taxon improves precision. The top-level OR, however, means that the weakest single branch determines the rule’s overall false-positive liability.

Taxonomic restrictions are useful where pathway families genuinely expanded in a lineage—plant TPSs in Lamiaceae or conifers, insect JHAMT, mammalian CYP26, and microbial 2-methylisoborneol synthases. They become problematic when used to rescue a generic fold. A Caryophyllaceae glycosyltransferase, Asterales P450 or mammalian ALDH is not automatically an isoprenoid enzyme.

Several restrictions may be unnecessarily narrow: CYP26, RDH12, LRAT, GA enzymes and many prenyl synthases are conserved beyond the listed crown taxa. Conversely, broad restrictions such as Eukaryota, Metazoa or Viridiplantae may be too permissive for a poorly resolved family.

Redundancy is extensive among TPS InterPro/PANTHER/FunFam conditions, carotenoid synthase/desaturase/cyclase conditions, prenyl synthases and GA enzymes. Redundancy is not inherently harmful if branches cover complementary database assignments, but it complicates maintenance and can conceal inconsistent thresholds or obsolete family labels.

## 6. Recommendations

1. **Do not deploy the rule unchanged.** Split it into separate rules for: precursor/backbone metabolism; terpene synthases; carotenoid metabolism; gibberellin/ABA pathways; retinoid metabolism; sterol/triterpenoid metabolism; and juvenile-hormone metabolism.

2. **Retain high-confidence core branches.** These include complete TPS architectures or narrow TPS FunFams; HMGR, mevalonate kinase, diphosphomevalonate decarboxylase, DXS and HMBPP reductase; prenyl diphosphate synthases; squalene/phytoene synthases with conserved sites; carotenoid desaturases/cyclases; CPS/KS/KO/KAO/GA oxidases; carotenoid oxygenases; CYP26; LRAT; and JHAMT.

3. **Remove clearly off-target branches:** conditions 37, 43–44, 51, 55, 63–64, 66 and 73. These correspond to CYP2C9, mixed ALDH2/ALDH1A logic, hormone-sensitive lipase, alcohol/formaldehyde dehydrogenases, LDLR/LRP1, PNPLA2, phenylalanine aminomutase and CYP83B1.

4. **Hold opaque branches for manual validation:** conditions 15, 38, 78, 80, 82, 84–85, 87, 89–90, 92 and 94, plus PTHR31225 subfamilies 10, 12 and 19 if their current Panther descriptions and seed proteins cannot be audited. A bare database identifier should not be considered evidence in itself.

5. **Strengthen broad-family conditions.** Generic P450, UGT, SDR, ALDH and oxidoreductase branches should require a narrow experimentally anchored subfamily, conserved catalytic residues, suitable domain architecture and, where possible, orthology to characterized proteins. Taxonomy should be supporting rather than primary evidence.

6. **Use positive and negative controls.** Positive controls should cover characterized representatives of every retained subfamily. Negative controls should include neighboring TPS/prenyltransferase subfamilies with different chemistry, non-terpenoid P450s, flavonoid/phenolic UGTs, housekeeping ALDH/ADH enzymes and inactive TPS pseudogenes.

7. **Review conjunctions producing zero hits.** In particular, determine whether multi-FunFam conditions describe complementary domains or mutually exclusive classifications. The rule’s current zero-hit result may reflect obsolete identifiers, over-constrained AND logic, no proteome coverage, or deliberate retirement—not biological precision.

8. **Annotate at two levels when justified.** Retain GO:0006720 as a broad ancestor, but add a specific pathway descendant for signatures whose substrate and pathway are secure. Do not infer a product-specific biological process from a broad TPS or P450 fold.

9. **Institute release-based monitoring.** Recalculate coverage, reviewed/unreviewed distribution and precision after every InterPro, Panther, CATH and taxonomy update. FunFam-based biological-process transfer should be periodically revalidated because performance depends strongly on family population and annotation quality (rojano2022assigningproteinfunction pages 12-14, bonello2024funpredcathanensemble pages 10-12, bonello2024funpredcathanensemble pages 5-7).

**Final assessment:** the rule contains a substantial, well-supported biological core, but its current omnibus logic is not curatorially defensible. After removal of unrelated families and manual validation of opaque/broad branches, GO:0006720 is an appropriate parent prediction for the retained conditions.
