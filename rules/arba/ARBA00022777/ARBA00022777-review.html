Description

Assigns the UniProt keyword KW-0418 (Kinase) through 1,392 alternative condition sets built from InterPro, PANTHER and CATH FunFam signatures.

The domain overlap table is not shown for this review. It is drawn from a cached analysis of the rule's condition sets, and the cache does not match the sets recorded here.

Review Summary

Accuracy: all 1,392 live condition sets were screened with two UniProtKB queries each (2026-11-10): the Swiss-Prot entries that match the whole conjunction, and how many of them carry KW-0418 (Kinase). Every set matches at least one Swiss-Prot entry, and all 46,549 set-level matches carry the keyword. Ten sets are recorded with official signature names; the others were checked by count only. The screen shows no conflict with curated keywords; it does not measure precision on the 3,762,398 unreviewed entries the rule annotates. Meaning and parsimony: Kinase is an enzyme class label and the recorded sets select kinase families (GHMP kinases, adenylate, thymidylate and cytidylate kinases, the amino acid kinase family, phosphoglycerate kinase, plant protein kinases), so the label fits and, for the many small-molecule kinases, it is the most specific keyword UniProt has. The keyword hierarchy has two narrower keywords, Serine/threonine-protein kinase and Tyrosine-protein kinase, each with its own ARBA rule; 648 of the 1,392 sets (47%) are structurally covered by a set of one of those two rules. Seen from above, 1,292 of the 1,392 sets are in turn covered by the Transferase rule ARBA00022679. Keywords stack, so every set emits Kinase, but nearly half restate what the protein-kinase rules establish.

Action Rationale

The first pass left the rule undecided because the sets were screened by count and not read. The class label is coherent on the recorded sample and on the complete Swiss-Prot screen, which found no match without the keyword. The construction can be simplified: assigning Kinase wherever one of its two child keywords is assigned would replace 648 sets and leave 744 for kinases with no narrower keyword. The annotations are not wrong. Confidence is modest because the proposal depends on ARBA being able to chain a keyword from a keyword, which was not verified, and because precision on unreviewed entries is unmeasured.

APPROPRIATE

Not reviewed beyond the recorded sets.
