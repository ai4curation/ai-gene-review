## 1. Executive summary

**Overall verdict: the rule is not valid in its present OR-ed form.** It combines well-supported membrane-lipid biosynthetic enzymes with lipid-degrading enzymes, lipid-remodeling enzymes, generic catalytic domains, ligand-binding proteins, and apparently unrelated metabolic or signaling proteins. Because any single condition triggers GO:0046467, the weakest condition determines the rule’s practical specificity.

The strongest conditions describe enzymes that directly construct membrane lipids: ceramide synthases, 3-ketodihydrosphingosine reductase (KDSR), serine palmitoyltransferase (SPT), lipid-A pathway enzymes, glycolipid glycosyltransferases, GPI-anchor enzymes, and plastid galactolipid synthases. SPT initiates de novo sphingolipid synthesis, KDSR reduces 3-ketodihydrosphingosine, and ceramide synthases acylate sphingoid bases to make dihydroceramide/ceramide; these are appropriately associated with membrane-lipid biosynthesis, although specific child terms are preferable (gomezlarrauri2021ceramidemetabolismenzymes—therapeutic pages 2-4).

Several others require orthology, catalytic-site, topology, or substrate-specificity checks. The present record of **zero annotated proteins** prevents empirical estimation of precision or taxonomic coverage; it does not establish that the logic is sound.

### 2.1 De novo sphingolipid and ceramide synthesis

Conditions 1, 4, 8, 9, 18, and 36 cover the central de novo sphingolipid pathway. Their biological direction is well established: SPT catalyzes pathway entry in the ER; KDSR carries out the subsequent reduction; CerS/Lag1 proteins incorporate acyl-CoA to produce dihydroceramide or ceramide (gomezlarrauri2021ceramidemetabolismenzymes—therapeutic pages 2-4).

The signatures are not equally diagnostic. CerS5 and KDSR FunFams are comparatively specific, whereas the wider TRAM/LAG1/CLN8-homology or TLC family includes divergent proteins. Thus, condition 1 should require the CerS/Lag1-specific signature, conserved catalytic residues, expected multipass membrane topology, and preferably phylogenetic placement outside CLN8-like branches. This is a general concern in homology transfer: even close structural or sequence relatives can evolve different subfunctions (das2015functionalclassificationof pages 1-2).

Conditions 27 and 35 illustrate related problems. An SPT2 assignment supports sphingolipid synthesis, but conjunction with an apparently unrelated 2-amino-3-ketobutyrate CoA-ligase FunFam is suspicious and should be re-examined as a domain segmentation or FunFam mapping artifact. Very-long-chain 3-oxoacyl-CoA reductases produce fatty-acid precursors used by several pathways; they are not intrinsically diagnostic for membrane lipids and are better annotated to very-long-chain fatty-acid biosynthesis unless lipid-specific evidence is available.

### 2.2 Sphingolipid degradation and interconversion

Conditions 2, 5, 15, 19, and 45 describe hydrolytic enzymes. Ceramidases cleave ceramide to sphingosine and fatty acid; sphingomyelinases convert sphingomyelin to ceramide; glucosylceramidase removes glucose from glucosylceramide. These reactions can supply salvage synthesis, but participation in a metabolic network does not make the hydrolytic protein a participant in the biosynthetic process itself (gomezlarrauri2021ceramidemetabolismenzymes—therapeutic pages 2-4, breiden2021acidsphingomyelinasea pages 1-2).

Condition 31, sphingosine kinase 2, produces sphingosine-1-phosphate. It belongs to sphingolipid interconversion/signaling, not synthesis of a membrane structural lipid. Conditions 28 and 29 similarly describe dephosphorylation or lipid-A tailoring. They may warrant broader lipid-metabolic or lipid-modification terms, but blanket propagation of a biosynthetic term should depend on the exact GO relation used.

### 2.3 Glycosphingolipid and other glycolipid glycosyltransferases

Conditions 3, 13, 20, 21, 32, 34, 39–42, 44, 49, and 51 concern glycosyltransferases or sphingomyelin-synthase-related proteins. Named ortholog-specific families—galactosylceramide synthase, ceramide glucosyltransferase, phosphatidylinositol mannosyltransferase, and processive diacylglycerol glycosyltransferase—provide strong evidence for glycolipid synthesis.

By contrast, a DXD motif, generic hexosyltransferase label, GT29 membership, or sialyltransferase family alone is insufficient. Such enzymes can modify glycoproteins, soluble glycans, cell-wall polymers, or glycolipids. Conditions 3, 20, and 49 therefore need evidence that the ortholog’s physiological acceptor is a glycolipid or ganglioside, while conditions 6 and 51 should be removed. Domain-based methods improve coverage, but functional relatives can diverge and annotations used to train classifications are themselves incomplete or biased (das2015functionalclassificationof pages 1-2).

### 2.4 GPI-anchor biosynthesis

Conditions 22, 23, 46, 47, and 50 are biologically coherent. GPI is a membrane glycolipid whose conserved core contains ethanolamine phosphate, three mannoses, glucosamine, and an inositol phospholipid. Its fatty chains insert into the outer leaflet of the plasma membrane; over 150 human proteins and more than 60 budding-yeast proteins are reported as GPI anchored (kinoshita2016biosynthesisofgpianchored pages 1-3).

GPI ethanolamine-phosphate transferases and dolichol-phosphate mannosyltransferase act directly in constructing GPI or its lipid-linked mannose donor. GPI-anchor-specific terms are more informative than GO:0046467. Conditions 23 and 50 appear redundant and should be merged after checking whether they represent distinct historical FunFam models. GPI biology is broadly eukaryotic but not uniform: individual protist lineages may lack particular ethanolamine-phosphate additions, so absence or divergence of pathway components should be respected rather than inferred from a generic eukaryotic profile.

GPI maturation also demonstrates why reaction direction matters. PGAP1 and PGAP5 remove groups, whereas PGAP2/PGAP3 participate in lipid remodeling; these proteins are involved in GPI maturation but should not automatically inherit every GPI-biosynthetic annotation (kinoshita2016biosynthesisofgpianchored pages 7-9).

### 2.5 Bacterial lipid-A and envelope-lipid pathways

Conditions 7, 24, 38, and likely 14, 32, and 34 describe bona fide bacterial membrane-glycolipid synthesis. LpxC deacetylates UDP-3-O-acyl-GlcNAc and drives the first committed step of lipid-A biosynthesis; LpxH hydrolyzes UDP-2,3-diacylglucosamine to lipid X as an obligatory biosynthetic reaction. Thus, hydrolytic chemistry does not imply catabolism when the reaction lies inside an anabolic pathway (valvano2022remodellingofthe pages 3-4, maniyeri2023suppressorsoflapc pages 1-2).

Recent experimental work reinforces this assignment. A 2023 study showed that altered LapC/LapD regulation changes LpxC abundance and membrane homeostasis; it also reports that E. coli requires more than 30 gene products for LPS synthesis and transport and maintains an approximately **1:0.15 phospholipid:LPS balance** in the envelope (published 14 October 2023; https://doi.org/10.3390/ijms242015174) (maniyeri2023suppressorsoflapc pages 1-2). In 2024, all five chemically distinct inhibitors tested bound purified E. coli LpxC; inhibition altered cell shape, increased susceptibility to vancomycin and rifampin, and four of five compounds caused lysophosphatidylethanolamine accumulation. LPS can cover up to **75% of the bacterial cell surface**, underscoring that this is membrane-lipid synthesis in a direct physiological sense (published 6 March 2024; https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jbc.2024.107143) (moller2024commonandvaried pages 1-2).

However, “Bacteria” is too broad for every lipid-A signature. Lipid-A pathways are evolutionarily plastic: some taxa replace LpxH with nonhomologous LpxI, and acyltransferase orthologs vary in chain-length preference. E. coli LpxA, for example, exhibits approximately **100–1000-fold** preference for C14 over C12/C10 acyl-ACP, whereas other bacteria use shorter chains or display relaxed specificity (valvano2022remodellingofthe pages 3-4, xiao2017biosynthesisandstructureactivity pages 2-4).

PagP and lipid-A phosphatases are downstream tailoring enzymes rather than core synthesis enzymes. PagP transfers palmitate from phospholipid to lipid A, while LpxE/LpxF remove phosphates. These support lipid-A modification/remodeling terms; treating them as generic biosynthesis without qualification blurs a meaningful biological distinction (xiao2017biosynthesisandstructureactivity pages 2-4).

### 2.6 Plant and animal specialized membrane lipids

Monogalactosyldiacylglycerol synthase 3 (condition 17) directly constructs a major plastid membrane glycolipid and is a strong positive. Fatty-acid 2-hydroxylase and delta-8 desaturase conditions can support specialized sphingolipid production, but substrate specificity and orthology should be verified because desaturase/hydroxylase folds contain paralogs acting on different lipid classes.

PNPLA1 (condition 25) is an instructive case of neofunctionalization within a nominal phospholipase family: unlike several PNPLA lipases, PNPLA1 catalyzes synthesis of epidermal omega-O-acylceramides. This supports a specific acylceramide-biosynthetic annotation, but not general propagation from a patatin-like domain alone (grabner2022smallmoleculeinhibitorstargeting pages 1-2).

### 2.7 Unrelated or noncatalytic signatures

Conditions 12, 26, and 37—protein kinases, phosphoglycerate kinase, and 5-aminolevulinate synthase—have no direct basis for GO:0046467. Condition 43 is a lipid-binding protein rather than a biosynthetic catalyst. Condition 48 is a generic metallophosphoesterase. Their presence suggests erroneous FunFam mappings, overly broad training labels, domain-boundary problems, or annotation leakage. Taxonomic restrictions such as Primates, Streptophyta, or Bacteroidota cannot rescue a mechanistically unrelated signature.

## 3. GO-term evaluation

GO:0046467 is a broad biological-process term encompassing production of lipids that are components of biological membranes. It is suitable as a high-level ancestor for direct synthesis of sphingolipids, glycerolipids, glycolipids, GPI anchors, and lipid A. It is nevertheless **too broad for most reliable conditions** because pathway-specific terms are available:

- ceramide or sphingolipid biosynthetic process for CerS, SPT, and KDSR;
- glycosphingolipid, glucosylceramide, galactosylceramide, or ganglioside biosynthesis for lipid-directed glycosyltransferases;
- GPI-anchor biosynthetic process for PIG/DPM/GPI de-N-acetylase proteins;
- lipid-A or lipopolysaccharide biosynthetic process for Lpx enzymes;
- monogalactosyldiacylglycerol biosynthesis for plastid MGD synthases.

For hydrolases, the term is directionally wrong rather than merely broad. The appropriate alternatives are sphingomyelin, ceramide, glucosylceramide, phospholipid, or glycosphingolipid **catabolic** processes. For PagP, lipid-A phosphatase, sphingoid-base phosphatase, and sphingosine kinase, a lipid-modification or sphingolipid-metabolic/interconversion term is preferable.

The rule should annotate the most specific experimentally supported process and rely on GO’s ancestor propagation to supply GO:0046467 where that ancestry is valid. Hard-coding the parent across heterogeneous families conceals errors and discards useful specificity.

## 4. Evidence review and current applications

### Strength of evidence

Evidence is strongest for LpxC/LpxH, SPT/KDSR/CerS, GPI enzymes, acid sphingomyelinase, and PNPLA1. It combines purified-enzyme assays, inhibitor binding, mutant phenotypes, pathway intermediates, genetic complementation, disease phenotypes, and cellular lipid measurements. The 2024 LpxC inhibitor study is especially relevant because it directly connects enzyme inhibition to envelope composition and permeability (moller2024commonandvaried pages 1-2).

GPI-pathway assignments are supported by the conserved chemical structure and genetic dissection of its enzyme complexes. GPI anchors carry diverse enzymes, receptors, adhesion molecules, and complement regulators, and GPI assembly is essential for mammalian development and yeast growth (kinoshita2016biosynthesisofgpianchored pages 1-3). These findings support conditions for the biosynthetic enzymes, not for arbitrary GPI-bound proteins.

### Applications

The principal real-world implementation is computational annotation of uncharacterized proteins. CATH FunFams were designed as functionally coherent subdivisions of structural superfamilies; the original implementation reported **110,439 FunFams in 2,735 superfamilies**, covering over **16 million domain sequences**, while noting that less than 1% of UniProtKB proteins then had experimental characterization (publication 2 July 2015; https://doi.org/10.1093/bioinformatics/btv398) (das2015functionalclassificationof pages 1-2). This scale explains the utility of family rules, but also the need for stringent reaction-direction and substrate checks.

LpxC is also pursued as an antibacterial target because it is essential in many Gram-negative organisms, conserved, and lacks close mammalian homologs. The 2024 comparison of five inhibitor classes demonstrates both direct target engagement and membrane-sensitizing effects, although LpxC inhibitors remain clinically unexploited (moller2024commonandvaried pages 1-2).

Sphingolipid enzymes are therapeutic targets in cancer and inflammatory/metabolic disease because ceramide, sphingosine, and sphingosine-1-phosphate form a regulated metabolic/signaling network. This clinical interest does not collapse synthesis, degradation, and signaling into one process; rather, it makes correct directional annotation more important (gomezlarrauri2021ceramidemetabolismenzymes—therapeutic pages 2-4).

### Contradictory or cautionary evidence

The main contradiction is internal to the rule: families experimentally defined as catabolic trigger a biosynthetic GO term. Acid sphingomyelinase’s broad in-vitro substrate range—more than 20 phospholipids—also shows that even a seemingly specific enzyme name may not imply narrow substrate specificity (breiden2021acidsphingomyelinasea pages 1-2).

Taxonomic conservation is pathway-level rather than reaction-identical. Lipid-A enzymes vary in acyl-chain specificity, some bacteria use nonhomologous replacements, and downstream modifications differ among taxa (valvano2022remodellingofthe pages 3-4, xiao2017biosynthesisandstructureactivity pages 2-4). Generic domains and taxonomic filters therefore cannot substitute for orthology and catalytic-site evidence.

## 5. Recommendations

1. **Deactivate the current composite rule and split it by pathway and reaction direction.** Create separate rules for sphingolipid synthesis, sphingolipid catabolism, lipid-A synthesis, lipid-A modification, GPI synthesis, and specific glycolipid pathways.

2. **Retain as high-confidence biosynthetic conditions:** 4, 7–9, 11, 13–14, 17–18, 22–24, 34, 36, 38, 40–41, 46–47, and 50. Conditions 1, 3, 16, 20–21, 25, 30, 32, 39, 42, 44, and 49 should require additional orthology, active-site, topology, or substrate-specificity validation.

3. **Remove catabolic or non-membrane-lipid conditions:** 2, 5, 12, 15, 19, 26, 31, 37, 43, 45, and likely 48. Replace their annotations with the appropriate catabolic, signaling, binding, glycolytic, tetrapyrrole-biosynthetic, or generic catalytic terms.

4. **Remove generic signatures:** conditions 6, 33, and 51 are not diagnostic. A DXD motif or generic glycosyltransferase/hexosyltransferase FunFam cannot establish a lipid acceptor.

5. **Handle remodeling separately:** conditions 10, 28, and 29 should receive lipid-A or sphingolipid modification/metabolic terms. Do not infer biosynthesis merely because modification changes a membrane lipid.

6. **Correct anomalous FunFam conjunctions:** manually inspect conditions 12, 26, 27, and 37 for CATH domain-boundary errors, multidomain proteins, label contamination, or taxonomically implausible family assignments.

7. **Merge redundancy:** conditions 23 and 50 both describe DPM1 and should be consolidated. Conditions 4, 9, and 36 overlap KDSR at different specificity levels; condition 9 can serve broadly, with 4 as an InterPro fallback and 36 retained only if it adds validated fungal coverage.

8. **Refine taxonomy:** replace broad Bacteria/Eukaryota restrictions with clades in which orthology and pathway presence are demonstrated. Permit nonhomologous lipid-A alternatives rather than forcing E. coli pathway architecture across bacteria.

9. **Annotate specific children first.** Use GO ancestor propagation to obtain GO:0046467 only where the ontology formally supports it. This preserves biological detail and prevents inappropriate propagation from catabolic or remodeling reactions.

10. **Benchmark before reactivation.** Assemble experimentally characterized positive and negative sets for each family, estimate precision/recall per condition, and manually review borderline sequences. Given the current zero-output status, no performance statistic can yet be inferred.

**Final assessment:** ARBA00028538 contains a substantial core of biologically valid lipid-biosynthesis signatures, but its current OR logic is unsafe. It should be replaced by several pathway-specific rules with reaction-direction controls, stronger orthology requirements, and removal of generic, catabolic, and unrelated signatures.
