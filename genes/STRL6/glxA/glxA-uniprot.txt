CC   -!- FUNCTION: A copper-dependent oxidase that plays an essential role in
CC       the organism's morphological development (PubMed:26205496). It is
CC       required for glycan deposition at hyphal tips (PubMed:26205496). This
CC       glycan plays an important role in morphogenesis, and is essential for
CC       the formation of reproductive aerial hyphae on solid media and for the
CC       formation of pellets in liquid environments (PubMed:26205496). The
CC       enzyme shows sequence homology with fungal galactose oxidases (Gox),
CC       but it displays only residual activity with substrates that are readily
CC       turned over by fungal Gox (PubMed:26205496). In vitro, it can use
CC       glycolaldehyde, the smallest molecule that contains both an aldehyde
CC       and a hydroxyl group, producing H(2)O(2), however with low catalytic
CC       efficiency (PubMed:26205496, PubMed:28093470). The H(2)O(2) may
CC       contribute to protection of the hyphal tip from oxidative damage
CC       (PubMed:26740586). {ECO:0000269|PubMed:26205496,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:26740586, ECO:0000269|PubMed:28093470}.

CC       Note=Binds 1 Cu(2+) ion per subunit. {ECO:0000269|PubMed:26205496,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:28093470};
CC   -!- ACTIVITY REGULATION: Maturation of the enzyme requires the DyP-type
CC       peroxidase DtpA and the copper chaperone Sco.
CC       {ECO:0000269|PubMed:26740586}.

CC         Note=kcat is 0.14 sec(-1) with glycolaldehyde as substrate.

CC   -!- SUBCELLULAR LOCATION: Cell membrane {ECO:0000305|PubMed:26205496};
CC       Single-pass membrane protein {ECO:0000255}.
CC   -!- DOMAIN: The active site consists of a mononuclear copper (Cu) ion and a
CC       cross-linked tyrosine-cysteine (Y-C) cofactor (PubMed:26205496). The
CC       active site and the putative substrate binding pocket are buried in the
CC       enzyme, but can be accessed through channels leading down from three
CC       separate surface locations to the active site (PubMed:26205496). Trp-
CC       288 plays an important role in the kinetics of copper loading and
CC       maturation of GlxA, with its presence essential for stabilizing the
CC       metalloradical site required for coupling catalytic activity and
CC       morphological development (PubMed:28093470).
CC       {ECO:0000269|PubMed:26205496, ECO:0000269|PubMed:28093470}.
CC   -!- PTM: Contains a tyrosine-cysteine (Y-C) (Tyr-289-Cys-121) cross-linked
CC       cofactor, which is redox active (PubMed:26205496). Oxidation of the
CC       enzyme generates a protein radical localized on the Y-C cross-link
CC       (PubMed:26205496). The Y-C cofactor probably works together with the
CC       copper ion to catalyze the two-electron oxidation of a substrate
CC       (PubMed:26205496). {ECO:0000269|PubMed:26205496}.
CC   -!- DISRUPTION PHENOTYPE: A null-mutant leads to loss of glycan
CC       accumulation at hyphal tips (PubMed:26205496). The mutant does not form
CC       aerial hyphae on solid media and cannot form dense compact pellets in
CC       liquid-grown cultures (PubMed:26205496). The mutant loses the
CC       stimulation of development by copper (PubMed:26205496).
CC       {ECO:0000269|PubMed:26205496}.
