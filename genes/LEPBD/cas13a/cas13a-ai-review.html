Gene Description

Cas13a (originally C2c2) is the single-protein effector of the class 2, type VI-A CRISPR-Cas adaptive immune system of Leptotrichia buccalis, and a member of the only class of single-component CRISPR effector that targets RNA rather than DNA. The 1159-residue protein is bilobed: a crRNA-recognition lobe (residues 1-360) anchors the repeat portion of the guide, and a nuclease lobe (361-1159) binds the spacer-target duplex and carries two HEPN folds. It performs two chemically and mechanistically distinct ribonuclease reactions at two separate sites. First, it matures its own guide, cleaving precursor CRISPR RNA within the repeat by a metal-independent reaction that leaves a 2',3'-cyclic phosphate on the 5' product and a 5'-hydroxyl on the 3' product; this depends on the structure of the repeat hairpin and its flanking regions rather than on the spacer sequence, and is unaffected by mutation of the HEPN residues. Second, loaded with the mature crRNA it recognises a complementary single-stranded transcript whose 3' protospacer flanking site is accessible, and cleaves it preferentially before uridines. Formation of the guide-target duplex drives the HEPN1 fold against HEPN2 to assemble a single composite catalytic site; this requires a duplex of at least twenty base pairs and converts the enzyme into a substrate-non-specific ribonuclease that degrades unrelated bystander RNA, thousands of molecules per target recognised. Target cleavage is roughly eightyfold faster than guide processing, proceeds at target concentrations as low as ten femtomolar, and depends on a divalent metal ion, unlike guide processing. The two activities are separable in both directions by mutation: HEPN substitutions abolish target cleavage while leaving processing and RNA binding intact, and an arginine substitution outside the HEPN motifs attenuates processing while leaving target cleavage intact. A duplex DNA target can also base-pair with the guide and activate the HEPN site, but the DNA itself is not degraded. The biological output is spacer-programmed destruction of invading transcripts, accompanied by indiscriminate degradation of cellular RNA; type VI-A immunity is accordingly understood to act by arresting the infected cell rather than by clearing the invader from it.

Proposed New Ontology Terms

guide RNA-dependent nucleic acid endonuclease activity

Definition: Catalysis of the cleavage of ester linkages within a nucleic acid molecule by creating internal breaks, where the position of cleavage is determined by base pairing between the substrate and a separate guide RNA bound non-covalently by the enzyme. The guide RNA is neither substrate nor product; it programs the sequence specificity of an enzyme whose active site is otherwise sequence-non-specific, so that the specificity of the catalysis is a property of the bound guide rather than of the protein.

Justification: GO has no molecular-function term for guide-RNA-programmed target recognition or cleavage. Checked against the current GO release: no molecular-function label contains "RNA-guided", "guide RNA" or "CRISPR". The consequence is that every CRISPR effector, and every other guide-dependent nuclease, can only be annotated with a term that describes its chemistry (GO:0004520, GO:0004521, GO:0004530, GO:1990238) and is therefore indistinguishable in GO from an unguided nuclease of the same chemistry. LbuCas13a makes the cost unusually visible, because its two reactions are separable in both directions by mutation, differ in metal requirement and in product chemistry, and sit in different domains, yet both have to be annotated with the same single term GO:0004521 - and the property that distinguishes them is precisely guide dependence. Two children are needed, guide RNA-dependent DNA endonuclease activity (Cas9 and Cas12 families) and guide RNA-dependent RNA endonuclease activity (Cas13 and type III effectors), and the parent would also accommodate class 1 multi-subunit effectors through contributes_to. This request is deliberately scoped to the nuclease function rather than to recognition in general, so that catalytically dead guide-loaded effectors - which Cas13a HEPN mutants are, and which retain full crRNA and target binding - are handled by a binding term instead. This is the same request as in the reviews of U2UMQ6, A0Q7Q2 and P0DOC6 and should be merged with them.

defense response to virus by induction of host dormancy

Definition: A defense response to virus in which the infected cell halts its own growth and enters a reversible non-growing state, so that the viral replication cycle cannot be completed. The invader is not cleared from the infected cell and the cell does not necessarily lyse; the protective output is realised at the level of the population, because an arrested cell continues to adsorb virions while producing no progeny.

Justification: Type VI-A immunity does not work by destroying the invader. Target recognition licenses indiscriminate cleavage of cellular transcripts, and it is that degradation, not clearance of the phage, that halts the infection; trans-cleavage alone is sufficient to abort the infectious cycle. GO cannot express this. The nearest existing terms fail in different ways: GO:0051607 defense response to virus is correct but silent about the mechanism and about the fate of the infected cell; GO:0022611 dormancy process and its children GO:0097436 and GO:0097437 descend from GO:0032502 developmental process and describe a developmental programme, so applying them to phage-induced bacterial growth arrest would be a branch error rather than a stretch; and GO:0012501 programmed cell death is wrong because the arrest is reversible and the cell is not killed. On the question of which entity performs the step, the answer here favours the term: Cas13a itself degrades the host transcripts, and the arrest follows directly from that degradation rather than being executed by something else. The gap is not specific to CRISPR: abortive-infection defence by growth arrest is shared with toxin-antitoxin systems, retrons and Thoeris, none of which can be annotated for it either, so a term under GO:0051607 would serve a whole class of prokaryotic defence systems. No annotation to this term is asserted on C7NBY4, because the dormancy outcome was demonstrated on the Listeria seeligeri ortholog and this entry has no in vivo infection data of its own. This is the same request as in the review of P0DOC6 and should be merged with it.

Core Functions

Matures its own CRISPR guide, cleaving the precursor crRNA transcribed from the array within the repeat, with no tracrRNA and no dedicated Cas6-type ribonuclease. The reaction is metal-independent, unaffected by EDTA or EGTA, and leaves a 2',3'-cyclic phosphate on the 5' product and a 5'-hydroxyl on the 3' product, so it is a transesterification rather than a hydrolysis to a phosphomonoester. Recognition depends on the structure of the repeat hairpin and the sequences flanking it, with no dependence on the spacer. The catalytic site is distinct from the HEPN target-cleavage site, as shown reciprocally: HEPN substitutions leave processing intact, and an R1079A substitution outside the HEPN motifs attenuates processing while leaving target cleavage and crRNA binding intact. Processing is not strictly required for target cleavage but enhances the use of guides encoded internally in the array.

Suggested Questions for Experts

Q: Is the Leptotrichia buccalis type VI-A system functional as immunity in its own host, and what do its native spacers match? No interference experiment on this ortholog exists in the cited literature, which is why the process annotation here is IC rather than IMP.

Q: What are the end-group products of LbuCas13a target-RNA cleavage, as opposed to pre-crRNA cleavage? The processing chemistry is settled (metal-independent, 2',3'-cyclic phosphate and 5'-OH) but the HEPN reaction is not, and no term below GO:0004521 can be justified for it until it is.

Q: Does target-activated trans cleavage arrest a Leptotrichia buccalis host as it arrests a Listeria seeligeri host, and is the arrest reversible? This bears directly on whether the proposed dormancy term would ever be annotated to this gene.

Q: Does activation by a DNA target, reported without PFS or PAM restriction and without cleavage of the DNA, occur at physiological guide and target concentrations, and does it have any consequence for a cell carrying this locus?

Q: What holds the HEPN site inactive in the guide-loaded but unengaged enzyme, such that activation needs a twenty-base-pair duplex yet responds to ten femtomolar target and then turns over thousands of bystander RNAs?

Q: What determines the functional split in the Cas13a family, which could not be predicted bioinformatically? Until this is understood, guide and substrate specificity should not be propagated between orthologs by sequence similarity.

Suggested Experiments

Experiment: Challenge a Leptotrichia buccalis strain, or a tractable host carrying the reconstituted locus, with phages matched to its native spacers, and compare wild type against the HEPN and R1079A mutants, to convert the IC process annotation into an IMP one and to test whether the system confers immunity at all.

Experiment: Determine the chemical end groups of LbuCas13a target-RNA cleavage products by alkaline phosphatase and T4 polynucleotide kinase treatment, exactly as was done for the pre-crRNA products, to establish whether the HEPN reaction is hydrolytic and which GO child term applies.

Experiment: Follow growth, viability and colony-forming ability of cells after targeted infection, with and without the HEPN mutants, to test whether this ortholog arrests its host and whether arrested cells resume growth.

Experiment: Repeat the DNA-target activation experiment with physiological concentrations and a non-diagnostic readout, and ask whether a DNA target competes with RNA targets for the loaded enzyme, which would make DNA binding a liability for immunity rather than an extension of it.

Experiment: Sequence the native Leptotrichia buccalis CRISPR array and search its spacers against phage and plasmid databases, to establish whether the system is directed at RNA phages, at transcripts of DNA phages, or at mobile elements.

2. Two chemically and mechanistically distinct RNase activities

This is the entry on which the two-activity model was established:
PMID:27669025.

2a. pre-crRNA processing — metal-independent, non-HEPN site

PMID:27669025 Products were established by end-group chemistry: the
5' flanking product carries a 2',3'-cyclic phosphate (removable by T4 PNK) and the 3' product a
5'-hydroxyl. UniProt records this as "pre-crRNA processing yields a 5'-OH and probably a 2',3'-cyclic
phosphate".

Recognition is structural, not sequence-based in the spacer: the repeat hairpin and the regions
flanking it are required, while "there was no dependence on the spacer sequence for kinetics of
processing".

2b. target and collateral ssRNA cleavage — composite HEPN site

The site separation was proved both ways round, which is why this is a genuinely two-site enzyme
rather than one site with two substrate preferences:
- HEPN mutants lose target cleavage, keep processing:
PMID:27669025
- R1079A loses processing, keeps target cleavage:
PMID:27669025
- conclusion stated explicitly:
PMID:27669025 (quotation truncated at a page break in the cached text)

Target binding then unleashes indiscriminate cleavage of bystander RNA, with enormous turnover:
PMID:27669025. UniProt adds the quantitative details: target
cleavage is ~80-fold faster than processing and uses a different active site; activation occurs with
10 fM target RNA (PMID:28475872); and crRNA maturation is not required for activation, though its
absence lowers it.

The structural mechanism of activation is an induced-fit assembly of the HEPN site:
PMID:28757251 This also explains the 20-nt
minimum duplex length UniProt records for activation. Structures are of this protein:

5. What is deliberately NOT asserted

  • Nucleic-acid diagnostics. The majority of the literature on LbuCas13a is detection technology
    (SHERLOCK, SUREST and relatives), and UniProt's BIOTECHNOLOGY block ("Can be used to detect low
    levels of cellular transcripts") is in that register. None of it is a function of the gene in
    L. buccalis; excluded from description and core_functions.
  • Any DNA nuclease activity. See §3. The one paper reporting DNA targeting states explicitly that
    the DNA is not degraded.
  • GO:0051607 defense response to virus. No phage-challenge data for this ortholog. Asserted on
    P0DOC6 instead, where there is a quantitative phenotype with four catalytic mutants.
  • A dormancy / abortive-infection process annotation. The outcome is established for type VI-A
    (PMID:31142834, on the Listeria seeligeri ortholog) and GO has no term for it. Raised as a
    proposed_new_terms entry, identical to the one in the L. shahii review so the two can be merged.
  • GO:0003723 RNA binding. True and keyworded, and the HEPN mutants retain crRNA and ssRNA
    binding, which makes it a measured property rather than an inference — but it does not distinguish a
    programmable guide-loaded surveillance enzyme from any RNA-binding protein. The informative version
    of the claim is the missing guide-dependence term.
  • A multimeric assertion. UniProt's SUBUNIT block reports crystal contacts between the target
    RNA 3' end and an adjacent protein molecule, with mutagenesis effects, but flags that "it is not
    clear if this is physiological" (ECO:0000303). No complex or oligomerisation claim is made.

description: >-
  Cas13a (originally C2c2) is the single-protein effector of the class 2, type VI-A
  CRISPR-Cas adaptive immune system of Leptotrichia buccalis, and a member of the only
  class of single-component CRISPR effector that targets RNA rather than DNA. The
  1159-residue protein is bilobed: a crRNA-recognition lobe (residues 1-360) anchors the
  repeat portion of the guide, and a nuclease lobe (361-1159) binds the spacer-target
  duplex and carries two HEPN folds. It performs two chemically and mechanistically
  distinct ribonuclease reactions at two separate sites. First, it matures its own guide,
  cleaving precursor CRISPR RNA within the repeat by a metal-independent reaction that
  leaves a 2',3'-cyclic phosphate on the 5' product and a 5'-hydroxyl on the 3' product;
  this depends on the structure of the repeat hairpin and its flanking regions rather than
  on the spacer sequence, and is unaffected by mutation of the HEPN residues. Second,
  loaded with the mature crRNA it recognises a complementary single-stranded transcript
  whose 3' protospacer flanking site is accessible, and cleaves it preferentially before
  uridines. Formation of the guide-target duplex drives the HEPN1 fold against HEPN2 to
  assemble a single composite catalytic site; this requires a duplex of at least twenty
  base pairs and converts the enzyme into a substrate-non-specific ribonuclease that
  degrades unrelated bystander RNA, thousands of molecules per target recognised. Target
  cleavage is roughly eightyfold faster than guide processing, proceeds at target
  concentrations as low as ten femtomolar, and depends on a divalent metal ion, unlike
  guide processing. The two activities are separable in both directions by mutation: HEPN
  substitutions abolish target cleavage while leaving processing and RNA binding intact,
  and an arginine substitution outside the HEPN motifs attenuates processing while leaving
  target cleavage intact. A duplex DNA target can also base-pair with the guide and
  activate the HEPN site, but the DNA itself is not degraded. The biological output is
  spacer-programmed destruction of invading transcripts, accompanied by indiscriminate
  degradation of cellular RNA; type VI-A immunity is accordingly understood to act by
  arresting the infected cell rather than by clearing the invader from it.

proposed_new_terms:
- proposed_name: guide RNA-dependent nucleic acid endonuclease activity
  proposed_definition: >-
    Catalysis of the cleavage of ester linkages within a nucleic acid molecule by creating internal
    breaks, where the position of cleavage is determined by base pairing between the substrate and
    a separate guide RNA bound non-covalently by the enzyme. The guide RNA is neither substrate nor
    product; it programs the sequence specificity of an enzyme whose active site is otherwise
    sequence-non-specific, so that the specificity of the catalysis is a property of the bound
    guide rather than of the protein.
  proposed_parent:
    id: GO:0004519
    label: endonuclease activity
  justification: >-
    GO has no molecular-function term for guide-RNA-programmed target recognition or cleavage.
    Checked against the current GO release: no molecular-function label contains "RNA-guided",
    "guide RNA" or "CRISPR". The consequence is that every CRISPR effector, and every other
    guide-dependent nuclease, can only be annotated with a term that describes its chemistry
    (GO:0004520, GO:0004521, GO:0004530, GO:1990238) and is therefore indistinguishable in GO from
    an unguided nuclease of the same chemistry. LbuCas13a makes the cost unusually visible, because
    its two reactions are separable in both directions by mutation, differ in metal requirement and
    in product chemistry, and sit in different domains, yet both have to be annotated with the same
    single term GO:0004521 - and the property that distinguishes them is precisely guide dependence.
    Two children are needed, guide RNA-dependent DNA endonuclease activity (Cas9 and Cas12 families)
    and guide RNA-dependent RNA endonuclease activity (Cas13 and type III effectors), and the parent
    would also accommodate class 1 multi-subunit effectors through contributes_to. This request is
    deliberately scoped to the nuclease function rather than to recognition in general, so that
    catalytically dead guide-loaded effectors - which Cas13a HEPN mutants are, and which retain full
    crRNA and target binding - are handled by a binding term instead. This is the same request as in
    the reviews of U2UMQ6, A0Q7Q2 and P0DOC6 and should be merged with them.

  proposed_definition: >-
    A defense response to virus in which the infected cell halts its own growth and enters a
    reversible non-growing state, so that the viral replication cycle cannot be completed. The
