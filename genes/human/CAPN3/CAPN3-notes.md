# CAPN3 evidence notes

## 2026-10-04 — identity and functional synthesis

Human CAPN3 is reviewed UniProt P20807 (CAN3_HUMAN), an 821-residue protein in the authenticated normal source record. The five source products are I/P20807-1, II/P20807-2, III/P20807-3, IV/P20807-4 and V/P20807-5. Their sequence notes remain exactly as fetched. In particular, product II carries both VSP_005227 and VSP_005228; an experimental construct described only as IS1-deleted cannot automatically be assigned to that product. The very short source products IV and V must not silently inherit every activity of the full-length protease.

CAPN3 has a calcium-dependent cysteine endopeptidase function and a separately supported nonproteolytic muscle-organizing function. Human genetic and enzyme evidence, titin binding, and mouse null-versus-inactive-protease experiments support that synthesis. The clinical recessive/dominant calpainopathy context comes from the reviewed UniProt DISEASE block; this review does not claim to have reanalyzed all cited clinical cohorts. The isolated normal research-provider attempt used Falcon with the configured perplexity-lite fallback. Both failed with connection errors, produced no report, and are documented in the TMP attempt receipt. These notes are manual literature assessment, not a provider-generated research report.

## Proteolysis, titin and self processing

PMID:9642272, Functional defects of a muscle-specific calpain, p94, caused by mutations associated with limb-girdle muscular dystrophy type 2A, distinguishes cleavage of an external substrate from autolysis and titin binding. Some disease mutants retain the latter properties despite loss of proteolytic competence. PMID:12482600 tests calcium-dependent intramolecular processing of a recombinant 47-kDa core construct. Neither abstract-only source establishes identical processing behavior for all products.

PMID:18310072, Multiple molecular interactions implicate the connectin/titin N2A region as a modulating scaffold for p94/calpain 3 activity in skeletal muscle, supports focused titin binding, suppression of autolysis and MARP2 cleavage. Titin is the scaffold in those experiments. PMID:20860623 provides CARP cleavage and NF-kappaB-pathway context. The muscle-interactome PMID:23414517 adds interaction evidence, not a complete native substrate map. The source protein-binding rows with titin are refined to titin binding, while the other supported generic associations are retained as non-core under the project instruction.

The mouse C129S knock-in study PMID:20592470 connects loss of protease activity to impaired sarcomeric redistribution and exercise-sensitive dystrophy. Its normal cache is abstract-only; the separately saved primary JCI consultation includes selected Results/Methods and the distinction between endogenous mouse genetics and full-length human constructs in mouse myotubes. These findings support adaptation and maintenance rather than establishing each substrate proposed from proteomics.

The selected full Methods/Results in PMID:20694146 demonstrate human CAPN3-dependent cleavage of particular PIAS proteins and reduced SUMO-conjugation signals. Predicted proteins in its large candidate list are not all validated substrates. Reduced SUMO conjugation follows PIAS proteolysis and does not make CAPN3 a SUMO deconjugating enzyme.

## Nonproteolytic muscle organization

The dated official MGI donor graph connects the structural-muscle, molecular-adaptor and complex-assembly transfers to mouse PMID:18676612, Novel role of calpain-3 in the triad-associated protein complex regulating calcium release in skeletal muscle. Actual primary Results and captions were read, including recombinant CAPN3-C129S/aldolase-A binding, triad-enriched fractionation, loss of the normal aldolase localization in null muscle, and reduced triad RyR abundance without a corresponding RyR transcript reduction. The RyR pull-down used a muscle membrane extract: it does not isolate direct binary binding. The paper explicitly leaves formation versus stability open and does not exclude an unidentified proteolytic intermediate. The CAPN3 bait's species was not independently recovered and is not described as necessarily human.

PMID:21295580, Non-proteolytic functions of calpain-3 in sarcoplasmic reticulum in skeletal muscles, provides a distinct C129S comparison. Selected full Methods, Results and Discussion were read. Inactive CAPN3 retains aldolase localization and normal calcium-release kinetics; native complexes co-associate with SR components. The authors explicitly leave direct SR binding partners unidentified. The human IS2 peptide pull-down from mouse muscle and the human constructs in COS7 cells are separate assays. These convergent data support structural integrity and membrane localization beyond necessity alone. They do not resolve a stoichiometric CAPN3–aldolase–RyR bridge, a titin-to-triad tether, or formation of a new complex. Structural constituent of muscle is retained; the more specific adaptor and complex-assembly claims remain undecided. CAPN3 is not a calcium-channel pore.

The precise plasma-membrane, T-tubule and Z-disc transfers remain undecided because mixed fractions, neighboring structures and broad sarcomeric localization do not resolve every subcompartment. The later SR work is not used to declare an uninspected rat localization experiment false. GO:0072657 includes maintaining a protein at a membrane location, so aldolase retention/recruitment can support that broad process without proving a transport route.

## Sodium, calcium and the structural withdrawal

PMID:20460380, Skeletal muscle-specific calpain is an intracellular Na+-dependent protease, was read beyond its title: selected full Methods, Results and Discussion include partially purified N358D protein, mouse and rat extracts, human constructs, mutagenesis, calcium/sodium combinations and ouabain/monensin-treated living myotubes. The cell perturbations also change calcium. It is inaccurate to reduce this paper to a single high-salt tube experiment.

PMID:37298357, Calpain-3 Is Not a Sodium Dependent Protease and Simply Requires Calcium for Activation, tests endogenous rat and human muscle under controlled ionic conditions. Selected full Results, Discussion and Methods were read. Physiological potassium prevents the sodium-associated autolysis seen after potassium removal, while human CAPN3 responds to micromolar calcium. The later paper still considers sodium binding at a normally calcium-sensitive site plausible under nonphysiological conditions. Thus physiological sodium activation is disputed, while absence of activation does not prove absence of binding. Both sodium-binding rows and the salt-stress-response transfers remain undecided. A GO cellular response can include changed enzyme activity; it is the stimulus/mechanism/transfer that remains unresolved, not a supposed rule excluding biochemical responses.

The same later study finds no detectable RyR1 cleavage under its conditions. Concurrent CAPN1 activation and junctophilin cleavage are not assigned to CAPN3. The result limits a substrate claim without disproving all possible cleavage conditions or the separately supported nonproteolytic SR role. Complete autolysis-associated redistribution also differs from partially autolysed protein that remains bound.

PMID:39725035, Human calpain-3 and its structural plasticity: Dissociation of a homohexamer into dimers on binding titin, is explicitly retracted and marked invalid in the reference assessment. Its predecessor preprint is not independent corroboration. PMID:42508284 is the reciprocal withdrawal notice. The normal notice cache is bibliographic-only and has full_text_available false. The substantive explanation was read separately in the official indexed PMC withdrawal: the characterized sample was bacterial ArnA rather than CAPN3, as supported by mass spectrometry. No ArnA sentence is quoted as cached notice text. The legacy UniProt EMD-43646 cross-reference is preserved as source data and does not rescue the withdrawn native-hexamer interpretation. None of the accepted structural-muscle decisions relies on this paper.

## Myogenic cell-state regulation

PMID:20139084, Down-regulation of MyoD by Calpain 3 Promotes Generation of Reserve Cells in C2C12 Myoblasts, was read in indexed primary Results/Discussion. Mouse CAPN3 perturbation changes the reserve-cell population and MyoD protein half-life. Reporter experiments also use C3H10T1/2 cells. Direct purified MyoD digestion was explicitly not feasible, so faster turnover is not represented as a fully resolved binary cleavage reaction. The experiment favors reserve-cell formation and reversible quiescence; it does not directly show activation from quiescence into G1 and division. The narrow satellite-activation transfers are refined to regulation of myoblast differentiation. The G1-to-G0 process remains a non-core mouse-derived context. Actual adult human satellite-cell regeneration is an open question.

Muscle organ development, myofibril assembly and broad muscle-structure development are not concluded solely from muscular dystrophy, damage, expression or reserve-cell shifts. Their uninspected specific donor/TAS evidence remains unresolved. The broad sarcomere-organization and muscle-homeostasis functions have independent physical and catalytic support.

## Context-dependent nucleolar and signaling observations

PMID:23357851 includes direct human CAPN3 knockdown and Def/UTP25 co-immunoprecipitation in human cells, alongside zebrafish studies. It is not a wrong-gene or fish-only reference. PMID:27657329 includes human endogenous localization and Def-dependent recruitment, recombinant pull-downs and p53 turnover experiments. Its Methods explicitly state that human CAPN3 expression constructs lack IS1. The supplied p53-containing material in one cleavage experiment is an extract, not purified p53 alone. These limits are preserved while retaining human-cell nucleolar localization, protein catabolism and destabilization as non-core functions. Def is the recruiting protein; its role is not transferred to CAPN3 as adaptor activity.

PMID:23707407 supports CAPN3 association with PLEIAD/SIMC1, suppression of autolysis and cell-expression CTBP1 cleavage. The preserved canonical cache is abstract-only; selected full Methods/Results were separately read from the authenticated normal-fetch XML variant. Endogenous CTBP1 cleavage in mouse primary cells was below detection under the reported test. PLEIAD supplies the proposed scaffold role.

PMID:18073330 supports selective c-FLIP expression and survival in human muscle cells. Cytokine-dependent NF-kappaB DNA-binding activation can still occur without CAPN3, so the interpretation is selective regulation rather than an obligatory upstream switch. PMID:19386580 reports unusual human melanoma variants and cisplatin-related changes. Calpeptin/apoptosis correlations and abstract-only access leave a causal apoptotic step unresolved. The two contexts are not collapsed into a universal pro- or antiapoptotic property.

## 2026-10-04: follow-up after the external review of PR #4093

This dated reassessment updates the earlier structural-core, adaptor and salt/death conclusions above; earlier notes are retained as the session record. All 96 source assertions and five products remain unchanged. No new annotation is introduced.

The official [structural-constituent definition](https://amigo.geneontology.org/amigo/term/GO:0008307) concerns muscle-fiber integrity, whereas [molecular adaptor activity](https://amigo.geneontology.org/amigo/term/GO:0060090) concerns selective interactions that coordinate molecules. The definitions are compatible rather than mutually exclusive. Mouse loss of CAPN3 selectively displaces aldolase A from triad fractions; controlled recombinant binding and preservation with inactive C129S favor recruitment rather than substrate turnover [PMID:18676612; PMID:21295580]. The 2011 study also contrasts sarcomere/thick-filament disorganization in null mice with preserved alignment in C129S mice, and finds preserved triad morphology. These positive observations support a contribution to muscle structural integrity, so the structural ACCEPT actions are retained with this explicit rationale. The existing adaptor rows are now also accepted, and the core summarizes that more informative recruitment mechanism. Neither definition requires CAPN3 to be the sole load-bearing element of a fiber. Direct CAPN3-RyR contact, fixed stoichiometry and the proposed titin-to-triad bridge remain unresolved. The second core now uses adaptor activity and omits the previously unmatched SR location. The papers' SR-associated observations remain qualified in prose; no new compartment annotation is manufactured to repair a summary.

The official [salt-response term](https://amigo.geneontology.org/amigo/term/GO:0071472) is under osmotic-stress responses. Extract autolysis and myotube drug perturbations do not establish CAPN3 participation in that environmental response: monensin/ouabain also alter calcium, and physiological potassium removes the later study's apparent sodium effect [PMID:20460380; PMID:37298357]. Both transferred rows are now over-annotated, without denying the observed autolysis. The [programmed-cell-death term](https://amigo.geneontology.org/amigo/term/GO:0012501) requires a pathway ending in death. The dated official MGI record points to PMID:20460380, whose candidate-substrate experiments do not identify such a CAPN3-dependent step. That electronic row is now over-annotated. The separate melanoma apoptotic-process row remains undecided: its abstract and calpeptin result do not replace the unavailable full evidence [PMID:19386580].

PMID:19386580 is medium relevance to this review and contextual for muscle homeostasis. Its original TAS citation is preserved, while the accepted homeostasis rationale and support now point explicitly to independent catalytic/adaptation evidence [PMID:9642272; PMID:20592470]. Parent self-proteolysis remains as its original row; the calcium-dependent child is not counted as a second independent function.

The canonical and supplemental PMID:18676612 caches are both abstract-only. Follow-up reading used the official indexed PMC Results, Discussion, Methods and textual captions separately; no cache flag or source byte was changed. Relevant unique passages in the canonical 2010, 2011 and 2023 full-text caches were inspected. No raw figure or supplemental-data reanalysis is claimed.
