# FANCA source guide

This guide unifies the original-evidence and database assessments for human FANCA. The [campaign instructions](../../../projects/CLINGEN_MENDELIAN.md#curation-instructions) permit supported generic interactions to remain non-core after specific-function assessment and prohibit new publication quotations. Historical quotations retain their original text, reference association and position; they are not enlarged into claims beyond the underlying evidence.

## Assembly, repair and molecular activities

[PMID:22343915](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3321854/) Figures 2I and 3 distinguish FANCA-mediated organization from partner abundance alone: restored FAAP20 expression does not restore its FANCG/FANCM coassociation in FANCA-deficient cells. Distinct FANCA regions support FANCG and FAAP20 contacts, with recombinant fragments mapping the FAAP20 contact. The human HeLa/HEK293/FA-A cell experiments support a bounded scaffold role, without a purified ternary stoichiometry or direct FANCA–FANCF interface. Detailed mammalian construct information is partly in an unread supplement; the nominal supplemental PDF response was HTML. Figure 5 and the accompanying Results establish chromatin-associated FANCA.

[PMID:22266823](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22266823/) supports FANCA–FAAP20 association, but the measured ubiquitin/Rev1 bridge belongs to FAAP20. [PMID:17396147](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17396147/) separates human core coassociation from negative FAAP100–FANCA hybrid testing and a separable FANCB–FANCL–FAAP100 module. [PMID:20347428](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20347428/) identifies human FANCA-containing preparations while much FANCM/MHF exists outside them. [PMID:22705371](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22705371/) supplies human purification, patient-cell reconstitution and damage-recruitment evidence; its chicken experiments remain distinct. FANCL/UBE2T ubiquitin transfer and partner nuclease, kinase or polymerase reactions are not assigned to FANCA.

The specific activity is [protein complex scaffold activity, GO:0140378](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/QuickGO/term/GO:0140378): an integral complex component holds the complex together. The current [GO:0030674 comment](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/QuickGO/term/GO:0030674) explicitly directs such scaffolds to GO:0140378. This corrects the earlier adaptor subtype without discarding the target assembly evidence. The inherited proposal and assembly core use the scaffold term. Official QuickGO responses retrieved on 2026-10-25 at 14:33 and independently rechecked at 15:04 UTC contain exact human GO:0140378 IDA annotations for CCDC6 (Q16204; PMID:41986709; annotation date 20260427) and CHIC2 (Q9UKJ5; PMID:36108743; annotation date 20260615). The [public comparator evidence](FANCA-scaffold-comparators.json) preserves literal response bodies and records, query URLs, retrieval timestamps and SHA256 hashes. The independent unfiltered CHIC2 response contains 74 records, including the same scaffold record. The preserved repository CHIC2 GOA file differs from this live capture; its fetch date is not inferred and its bytes are unchanged. Comparator annotation records and prior abstract reading support examples of the convention, not an identical FANCA mechanism or a newly read full comparator paper. The earlier negative exact-adaptor query among selected FA proteins is historical and does not override the explicit ontology instruction. The FA complex term remains membership, separately from anatomical locations.

[PMID:22194614](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3281618/) Experimental Procedures and Figures 1–5 assay sequenced human NM_000135-derived His-tagged FANCA produced in insect cells. Purified protein binds ssDNA, dsDNA and ssRNA with substrate-dependent affinities. C-terminal 772–1455 retains binding whereas Q772X is deficient; this does not establish a natural-product equivalence, cellular RNA process or an obligatory fork-recruitment mechanism.

[PMID:30057198](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6097932/) Results, Figures 1–4 and STAR Methods demonstrate human FANCA-mediated annealing and strand exchange without added ATP. Mutants can retain DNA binding while losing these activities. Human U2OS SSA reporter knockdown, knockout and complementation link performed annealing to repair; D598N separates the SSA endpoint from FANCD2 monoubiquitination in the tested system. RAD52 double perturbation supports separable contributions there, without establishing universal independence. Purified oligomerization and FANCG/FANCB comparisons are preparation-specific; no invariant cellular stoichiometry is assigned. FANCA is not made an ATPase, nuclease or general homologous-recombination enzyme.

The synthesis uses [DNA/DNA annealing activity, GO:1990814](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/QuickGO/term/GO:1990814), whose current definition directly matches formation of complementary duplex DNA. The older GO:0000739 is obsolete. GO:1990814 is a specific descendant of ssDNA binding, so the core uses that one activity rather than duplicating an ancestor core. Strand exchange is retained as measured biochemical breadth in the same unit. The inherited NEW ssDNA-binding proposal is now refined to GO:1990814 with PMID:30057198 as its primary IDA source, rather than adding a redundant ancestor/descendant pair. Its two unchanged PMID:22194614 quotations remain binding context only; the annealing evidence is the separately cited 2018 experiment. The original publication quotations have not been added to, moved or reassigned. [ICL repair](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/QuickGO/term/GO:0036297), [protein-complex assembly](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/QuickGO/term/GO:0065003) and [SSA repair](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/QuickGO/term/GO:0045002) are mutually non-ancestral in the captured is_a/part_of closure. No new annotation row is created to mirror these core facets. The fixed committed GO-CAM index contains no FANCA entry; this is not evidence of a coverage gap.

## Location and natural products

The [UniProt O15360 record](https://www.uniprot.org/uniprotkb/O15360/entry) and SL-0191/SL-0086 mappings support nuclear and cytoplasmic pools. The [HPA subcellular record](https://www.proteinatlas.org/ENSG00000187741-FANCA/subcellular) classifies supported nucleoplasm with HPA063236 in MCF7, SiHa and U2OS. MCF7 and SiHa pixels were read in the evidence lanes; U2OS pixels were not. The antibody's 80-residue antigen maps exactly to residues 1056–1135 in the 1455- and 1424-residue products and is absent from the 297-residue product. The images cannot distinguish the two long products or locate the short one.

All three broad nucleus contexts are retained. [Nucleoplasm](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/QuickGO/term/GO:0005654) explicitly excludes chromosomes, whereas human target experiments also identify chromatin-associated FANCA. Narrowing the encompassing nucleus solely to nucleoplasm would lose supported breadth. The earlier database-only refinement is superseded explicitly in the journal; its sealed source record remains unchanged. Nucleoplasmic and chromatin locations remain distinct finer facets of the assembly core. The SSA unit uses the broader nuclear location without claiming that a particular finer pool was resolved for that reaction.

The literal natural products are O15360-1 (1455 residues), O15360-2 (297 residues; deletion of 298–1455) and O15360-3 (1424 residues; a different terminal segment). Historical full-length constructs, engineered truncations, antibody epitopes and transcript labels do not establish their functional equivalence. Cytoplasmic and cytosolic FANCA are positive contextual pools, not universally inactive material.
