### Native biochemical identity

PMID:12859189 is a primary human structural/biochemical study of a fully active truncated enzyme. Its abstract states: "Arginase is a binuclear manganese metalloenzyme that catalyzes the hydrolysis of l-arginine to form l-ornithine and urea." The curated UniProt cofactor and subunit sections explicitly tie two manganese ions per subunit and homotrimer formation to this source. This chemistry is the core activity. Human mutagenesis PMID:16128822 distinguishes native arginase from engineered substrate switching: "the Asn149Asp mutant expressed almost undetectable activity on arginine, but significant activity on agmatine." That mutant is not evidence for physiological agmatinase activity of wild-type ARG2.

The human import study PMID:8898077 says: "The arginase II precursor synthesized in vitro was imported into isolated mitochondria and proteolytically processed." This supports mitochondrial targeting independently of high-throughput PMID:34800366 and HPA localization. Existing Reactome matrix assignments are retained as curated source-level compartment assertions, not claimed as four separate topology assays. R-HSA-9838035/R-HSA-9838081/R-HSA-9838093/R-HSA-9838289 model mitochondrial proteostasis: ARG2 is a protease substrate there, not a protease or a participant in the catalytic work of its own degradation. Their summaries mainly describe the protease machinery and do not individually show ARG2 topology. R-HSA-452036 instead models ARG2 catalysis.

The urea-cycle IBA and UniPathway claims are retained as non-core. ARG2 performs the terminal urea-producing reaction and Reactome describes mitigation of ARG1-deficient urea synthesis. That is not a claim that ordinary ARG2-expressing extrahepatic tissues all execute a complete hepatic cycle. General arginine metabolism is refined to the actual catabolic step. The PAINT ancestry is respected: self-evidence in the descendant list is legitimate, donor counts are not a quality metric, and no tree/MSA reconstruction or ancestral gain/loss is claimed.

PMID:8898077 did not detect hepatic transcript in its samples; PMID:9144563 reported weak adult liver expression along with strong kidney expression. Therefore describe ARG2 as prominent in extrahepatic tissues, not absolutely absent from liver. The original nitric-oxide-biosynthesis citation actually proposed down-regulation, rather than NO synthesis by ARG2. Human endothelial perturbations in PMID:22928666 support a contextual inhibitory effect on coupled eNOS/NO availability; the review proposes the regulatory term GO:0045019, verified against QuickGO. This does not universalize the sign in every cell context.

### Immune functions require cellular and species context

* Human fetal dendritic-cell primary PMID:28614294, full text: arginine replenishment and arginase inhibitors restore T-cell TNF, and "the reduced TNF-α production was mediated through arginase-2 activity." Keep the three negative-TNF assertions consistently non-core. The shorthand UniProt statement about promoting TNF must not reverse this primary result. The experiment distinguishes TNF suppression from the separate Treg induction mechanism.
* Full PMID:27214549 combines human airway metabolism/cell work with mouse knockout asthma experiments. It reports "mice lacking Arg2 had greater Th2 inflammation than WT mice" with increased IL-13/IL-17 and dependence on allergen challenge. Retain transferred type-2/cytokine regulatory claims with that mouse context; these are not direct human knockout cytokine assays.
* Full PMID:27074721, mouse H. pylori infection: "Arg2−/− and Arg2−/−;Nos2−/− mice had significantly increased protein levels of C–C chemokine ligand (CCL) 3 (MIP-1α), CCL4 (MIP-1β), and CCL5 (RANTES)". These support conditional chemokine restraint. Reduced bacterial colonization remains in the double knockout, so the defense effect cannot be explained solely by NOS2 competition. The IL-1β/TNF M1-marker response has a different dependence on Nos2 genotype; do not merge all readouts into one mechanism. These are retained contextual ortholog inferences, not claimed as direct human infection experiments.
* PMID:25009204, abstract: Arg2 derepression in mouse dendritic cells depletes extracellular arginine and impairs T-cell proliferation. This is an extrinsic nutrient-availability mechanism. In contrast, full PMID:27745970 says "inhibition of arginases in human T cells or deletion of ARG2 in mouse T cells did not affect cell proliferation" under its conditions. These findings are compatible because cell type, perturbation and nutrient regime differ.
* Full PMID:31751318 supports a cell-intrinsic role in mouse CD8 activation, effector function and persistence, with human inhibitor experiments corroborating accelerated activation. It does not turn all mouse survival results into direct human apoptosis assays.

### Other functions and annotation-policy choices

Human endothelial primary PMID:22928666 supports senescence/ROS-linked effects through knockdown and overexpression. Human smooth-muscle primary PMID:23832324 compares native ARG2 with inactive H160F and reports similar senescence/apoptosis and mitochondrial effects. Human endothelial PMID:25484082 likewise reports autophagy impairment with inactive H160F and differences between silencing and catalytic inhibition. These establish that not every ARG2 phenotype is an output of ureahydrolysis. Existing ROS and senescence annotations are retained as non-core; no extra autophagy or apoptosis row is manufactured from this broader literature.

The ARG2–FOXD4L6 interaction from PMID:32814053 is retained in the reasoning, while bare GO:0005515 is REMOVE under the current policy because it is uninformative, not because the binding measurement is false. Broader catalytic/cofactor/location/process parents are refined to established specific terms. No source payload or fetched publication is edited.

### Module implications

ARG2 can supply mitochondrial ornithine to OAT; that does not mean ARG2 is the sole source of ornithine or that all ARG2-made ornithine must be degraded. Polyamine and other routing compete. OAT is reversible and development/tissue-dependent. ALDH4A1 can oxidize the GSA generated by OAT but also accepts GSA generated through proline catabolism. Thus the module is a defined catabolic route, not a universal flux assertion for all cells expressing these enzymes.
