Gene Description

ARG2 is a mitochondrial arginase that hydrolyzes L-arginine to L-ornithine and urea using a binuclear manganese center. The enzyme forms a homotrimer and is prominent in extrahepatic tissues. It controls arginine availability and supplies ornithine for pathways including mitochondrial OAT-dependent degradation and polyamine synthesis. Although it catalyzes the same urea-forming reaction as ARG1, its usual roles extend beyond hepatic cyclic ureagenesis. Its immunological and vascular effects depend strongly on cellular context: fetal dendritic-cell ARG2 suppresses T-cell TNF production, whereas endothelial ARG2 can promote nitric-oxide-synthase uncoupling, oxidative stress and senescence. Some vascular signaling effects persist with a catalytically inactive variant and therefore cannot all be attributed to arginine hydrolysis.

Core Functions

Mitochondrial arginase catalyzes hydrolysis of L-arginine to L-ornithine and urea using two catalytic manganese ions per subunit in a homotrimer. The direct catabolic chemistry can supply ornithine to OAT-dependent mitochondrial degradation or other pathways, depending on tissue and substrate routing. Immune and vascular phenotypes are contextual consequences or additional signaling functions, not requirements of this catalytic step.

Native biochemical identity

PMID:12859189 is a primary human structural/biochemical study of a fully active truncated enzyme. Its abstract states: "Arginase is a binuclear manganese metalloenzyme that catalyzes the hydrolysis of l-arginine to form l-ornithine and urea." The curated UniProt cofactor and subunit sections explicitly tie two manganese ions per subunit and homotrimer formation to this source. This chemistry is the core activity. Human mutagenesis PMID:16128822 distinguishes native arginase from engineered substrate switching: "the Asn149Asp mutant expressed almost undetectable activity on arginine, but significant activity on agmatine." That mutant is not evidence for physiological agmatinase activity of wild-type ARG2.

The human import study PMID:8898077 says: "The arginase II precursor synthesized in vitro was imported into isolated mitochondria and proteolytically processed." This supports mitochondrial targeting independently of high-throughput PMID:34800366 and HPA localization. Existing Reactome matrix assignments are retained as curated source-level compartment assertions, not claimed as four separate topology assays. R-HSA-9838035/R-HSA-9838081/R-HSA-9838093/R-HSA-9838289 model mitochondrial proteostasis: ARG2 is a protease substrate there, not a protease or a participant in the catalytic work of its own degradation. Their summaries mainly describe the protease machinery and do not individually show ARG2 topology. R-HSA-452036 instead models ARG2 catalysis.

The urea-cycle IBA and UniPathway claims are retained as non-core. ARG2 performs the terminal urea-producing reaction and Reactome describes mitigation of ARG1-deficient urea synthesis. That is not a claim that ordinary ARG2-expressing extrahepatic tissues all execute a complete hepatic cycle. General arginine metabolism is refined to the actual catabolic step. The PAINT ancestry is respected: self-evidence in the descendant list is legitimate, donor counts are not a quality metric, and no tree/MSA reconstruction or ancestral gain/loss is claimed.

PMID:8898077 did not detect hepatic transcript in its samples; PMID:9144563 reported weak adult liver expression along with strong kidney expression. Therefore describe ARG2 as prominent in extrahepatic tissues, not absolutely absent from liver. The original nitric-oxide-biosynthesis citation actually proposed down-regulation, rather than NO synthesis by ARG2. Module implications

ARG2 can supply mitochondrial ornithine to OAT; that does not mean ARG2 is the sole source of ornithine or that all ARG2-made ornithine must be degraded. Polyamine and other routing compete. OAT is reversible and development/tissue-dependent. ALDH4A1 can oxidize the GSA generated by OAT but also accepts GSA generated through proline catabolism. Thus the module is a defined catabolic route, not a universal flux assertion for all cells expressing these enzymes. Histories, strict/term validation, section backfill and rendered HTML accompany this complete 42-row review.
