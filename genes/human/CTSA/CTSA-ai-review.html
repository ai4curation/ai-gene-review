Gene Description

CTSA encodes cathepsin A, a lysosomal serine carboxypeptidase and protective component of complexes containing neuraminidase NEU1 and beta-galactosidase GLB1. It removes C-terminal amino acids from peptides and also hydrolyzes terminal amides of selected tachykinins, whereas its nonproteolytic association stabilizes lysosomal glycosidases and activates NEU1. The precursor enters the secretory pathway and is processed into disulfide-linked heavy and light chains. CTSA also controls LAMP2A turnover and chaperone-mediated autophagy through proteolysis at the luminal face of the lysosomal membrane. CTSA deficiency causes galactosialidosis, with combined impairment of lysosomal glycosidase activities.

Core Functions

CTSA catalyzes serine-dependent removal of C-terminal amino acids from peptides in lysosomes. The enzyme additionally deamidates selected peptide carboxamides; that chemistry is distinct from the carboxypeptidase reaction represented here. Its processed chains form the same catalytic protein.
