CC   -!- FUNCTION: Component of neuronal acetylcholine receptors (nAChRs) that
CC       function as pentameric, ligand-gated cation channels with high calcium
CC       permeability among other activities. nAChRs are excitatory
CC       neurotrasnmitter receptors formed by a collection of nAChR subunits
CC       known to mediate synaptic transmission in the nervous system and the
CC       neuromuscular junction. Each nAchR subunit confers differential
CC       attributes to channel properties, including activation, deactivation
CC       and desensitization kinetics, pH sensitivity, cation permeability, and
CC       binding to allosteric modulators (PubMed:15609996, PubMed:33735609,
CC       PubMed:8145738). CHRNA7 forms homopentameric neuronal acetylcholine
CC       receptors abundantly expressed in the central nervous system,
CC       characterized by fast desensitization and high calcium permeability
CC       (PubMed:31560909, PubMed:33735609, PubMed:38382524, PubMed:8145738).
CC       Also forms heteropentamers with CHRNB2, mainly expressed in basal
CC       forebrain cholinergic neurons. Involved in the modulation of calcium-
CC       dependent signaling pathways and influences the release of
CC       neurotransmitters, including dopamine, glutamate and GABA
CC       (PubMed:33239400). Also expressed in non-neuronal cells such as immune
CC       cells like lymphocytes, monocytes and macrophages (PubMed:12508119,
CC       PubMed:16968406, PubMed:25259522). In T cells, activation induces
CC       metabotropic signaling that results in an increase of intracellular
CC       Ca2+ concentrations, independent of ionotropic receptor functions
CC       (PubMed:17709503). In macrophages, required for acetylcholine-mediated
CC       inhibition of TNF and other inflammatory cytokine release
CC       (PubMed:12508119). Once activated by acetylcholine, nicotine or other
CC       agonists, selectively inhibits production of pro-inflammatory cytokines
CC       while leaving anti-inflammatory cytokines undisturbed (PubMed:12508119,
CC       PubMed:25259522). Stimulates the cholinergic anti-inflammatory pathway,
CC       controlling inflammation by inhibiting NFKB nuclear translocation and
CC       activating the JAK2-STAT3 pathway, independently of ion channel
CC       activity (PubMed:16968406, PubMed:25259522). Also expressed in the
CC       urothelium where it modulates reflex bladder activity by increasing
CC       intracellular calcium through internal stores and decreasing basal ATP
CC       release (By similarity). {ECO:0000250|UniProtKB:Q05941,
