# LPL literature notes

## Search and provenance

Provider deep research was intentionally skipped, as permitted for this phase. I searched PubMed directly for primary studies of human LPL catalysis, intravascular localization, GPIHBP1 transport and stabilization, APOC2 activation, LMF1-dependent maturation, ANGPTL inhibition, and noncatalytic lipoprotein binding. Selected records were fetched with the project's `ai-gene-review fetch-pmid` command. Statements below distinguish direct human-protein experiments from mouse physiology, cultured-cell assays, database records, and family-level inference.

## Core extracellular capillary function

- LPL is the extracellular triglyceride hydrolase for circulating triglyceride-rich particles. Direct human mutagenesis established the catalytic machinery: [PMID:1371284, "These combined results strongly support the conclusion that Ser-132, Asp-156, and His-241 form the catalytic triad of LPL and are essential for LPL hydrolytic activity."] The same study frames its physiological substrates as [PMID:1371284, "triglycerides present in chylomicrons and very low density lipoproteins."]
- The physiological site of action is the luminal surface of capillaries, but LPL is synthesized by parenchymal cells. GPIHBP1 resolves that topological problem: [PMID:20620994, "LPL is synthesized and secreted by myocytes and adipocytes, but then finds its way into the lumen of capillaries, where it hydrolyzes lipoprotein triglycerides."] In mouse and endothelial-cell experiments, [PMID:20620994, "GPIHBP1 is located at the basolateral surface of capillary endothelial cells and actively transports LPL across endothelial cells."] This is decisive mechanism but includes mouse genetics and cultured endothelial systems; it should not be paraphrased as LPL synthesis by endothelial cells.
- GPIHBP1-bound LPL also captures/marginates triglyceride-rich lipoproteins at capillaries: [PMID:24726386, "Both cell-culture and in vivo studies showed that TRL margination depends on LPL bound to GPIHBP1."] The authors conclude [PMID:24726386, "Our studies show that GPIHBP1-bound LPL is the main determinant of TRL margination."] This particle-bridging step is mechanistically upstream of catalytic hydrolysis and is not equivalent to lipase activity itself.

## GPIHBP1, HSPGs, and structural stabilization

- The LPL-GPIHBP1 structure shows how endothelial capture stabilizes the enzyme: [PMID:30559189, "GPIHBP1's LU domain binds to LPL's C-terminal domain, largely by hydrophobic interactions."] The acidic domain is positioned against a basic patch spanning both LPL domains [PMID:30559189, "GPIHBP1's acidic domain was not defined in the electron density map but was positioned to interact with LPL's large basic patch, providing a likely explanation for how GPIHBP1 stabilizes LPL."]
- HSPG binding is dynamic, not a permanent tissue tether. Experiments showed [PMID:27811232, "LPL detaches from HSPGs on cultured cells and moves to: 1) soluble GPIHBP1 in the cell culture medium; 2) GPIHBP1-coated agarose beads; and 3) nearby GPIHBP1-expressing cells."] The bounded conclusion is [PMID:27811232, "HSPG-bound LPL in the interstitial spaces of tissues is mobile, allowing the LPL to move to GPIHBP1 on endothelial cells."]
- A human heparin-binding-site mutant retained catalytic competence but was unstable and mistargeted in transgenic mice: [PMID:11342582, "Three basic amino acids in the carboxyl terminal region of LpL were mutated, yielding an active enzyme with reduced heparin binding."] The physiology was abnormal [PMID:11342582, "Compared with mice expressing native hLpL in the muscle, hLpL(HBM) transgenic mice had increased postprandial FFAs, decreased lipid uptake in muscle tissue, and increased lipid uptake in kidneys."] This supports HSPG-dependent stabilization/targeting while preserving the human-protein/mouse-model boundary.

## Maturation and oligomeric-state boundary

- LMF1 is an ER-resident maturation factor required before secretion and activity: [PMID:24909692, "LPL requires an endoplasmic reticulum (ER)-resident, transmembrane protein known as lipase maturation factor 1 (LMF1) for secretion and enzymatic activity."] Truncation experiments found [PMID:24909692, "N-terminal truncations of LMF1 show that all the domains are necessary for LPL maturation."] This is a biogenesis dependency; LMF1 is not a component of the extracellular catalytic complex.
- Older biochemical work describes active LPL as a labile dimer: [PMID:16179346, "The active form of lipoprotein lipase (LPL) is a noncovalent homodimer of 55-kDa subunits."] Calcium promoted the folding path to active dimers [PMID:16179346, "The second step was promoted by Ca2+ and converted LPL monomers from the molten globule state to dimerization-competent and more tightly folded monomers that rapidly formed active LPL dimers."] Modern structural work can accommodate active monomeric LPL when stabilized by GPIHBP1, so dimer status should not be stated as an invariant requirement across preparations and physiological complexes.

## APOC2 activation and ANGPTL inhibition

- APOC2 is the physiological activator/cofactor on triglyceride-rich lipoprotein surfaces. A human APOC2 peptide study found direct binding to LPL and proposed [PMID:10727238, "activation occurs via direct helix-helix interactions between apoC-II39-62 and the loop covering the active site of LpL."] A later mutational study revised the minimal-fragment interpretation: [PMID:20042600, "Finally, fragment 39-62, previously claimed to activate LPL, was found to be completely inactive."] The robust conclusion is APOC2-dependent activation; exact minimal-residue models are assay-dependent and historically disputed.
- ANGPTL4 catalytically destabilizes the LPL hydrolase domain: [PMID:27929370, "ANGPTL4 inactivates LPL by catalyzing the unfolding of its hydrolase domain"] while [PMID:27929370, "binding to GPIHBP1 renders LPL largely refractory to this inhibition"]. This defines a mechanistic competition between inhibitor-driven unfolding and endothelial-partner stabilization.
- Earlier work similarly found that GPIHBP1 stabilized rather than activated LPL: [PMID:19542565, "Like heparin, GPIHBP1 stabilized but did not activate LPL."] That paper reported protection against ANGPTL3/4 under its conditions, while later work demonstrated that ANGPTL4 can inactivate GPIHBP1-bound LPL. These results should be presented as condition-dependent rather than forced into a single absolute rule.
- One cell-based mechanism proposed that ANGPTL3 promotes proprotein-convertase cleavage and release: [PMID:20581395, "By enhancing LPL cleavage, ANGPTL3 dissociates LPL from the cell surface, inhibiting both the catalytic and noncatalytic functions of LPL."] ANGPTL3/8 regulation is also supported, but much of the direct physiology is mouse-based: [PMID:28413163, "Collectively, these data show that ANGPTL8 has a functional LPL inhibitory motif, but only inhibits LPL and increases plasma TG levels in mice in the presence of ANGPTL3."]

## Noncatalytic and bridging roles

- LPL has particle- and matrix-binding functions separable from hydrolysis. The GPIHBP1-LPL complex mediates TRL margination [PMID:24726386, "Notably, the expression of LPL by endothelial cells in Gpihbp1⁻/⁻ mice did not restore defective TRL margination, implying that the binding of LPL to HSPGs is ineffective in promoting TRL margination."] This supports a specific endothelial GPIHBP1-dependent bridge, not a generic claim that every reported LPL-protein interaction is functional.
- ANGPTL3-enhanced cleavage was reported to inhibit both catalytic and noncatalytic LPL functions [PMID:20581395, "By enhancing LPL cleavage, ANGPTL3 dissociates LPL from the cell surface, inhibiting both the catalytic and noncatalytic functions of LPL."] Noncatalytic uptake or receptor-bridging annotations require their own direct assay and must not be inferred solely from the enzyme's ability to bind lipoprotein particles or HSPGs.

## Disease, species, isoform, and processing boundaries

- Biallelic loss of LPL catalytic function causes familial chylomicronemia/severe hypertriglyceridemia; individual variant papers are useful mainly when they connect a residue to secretion, stability, binding, or catalysis. For example, [PMID:1969408, "By contrast, the mutant enzyme expressed in vitro was catalytically inactive and displayed a lower affinity for heparin than the normal enzyme."] Disease association alone should not be expanded into unrelated cellular processes.
- Mouse Gpihbp1, Angptl3/4/8, and transgenic human-LPL experiments establish powerful physiology but are not direct observations of every corresponding event in humans. Human cell lines and purified human proteins establish biochemical possibilities but not necessarily the dominant tissue mechanism in vivo.
- The reviewed UniProt record presents a signal peptide followed by a mature secreted chain. The signal peptide is removed during secretory-pathway entry; it is not an extracellular regulatory propeptide. No alternative splice isoforms are curated in the record, so variant/propeptide or isoform-specific functions should not be invented.
- Important unresolved boundaries include the active monomer/dimer distribution in native human capillaries, tissue-specific effects of ANGPTL complexes, the relative contribution of catalytic versus bridging functions, and which reported interactome hits form physiologically stable LPL complexes.
