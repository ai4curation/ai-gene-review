# TET3 manual deep research notes

Automated deep research could not run in this sparse runtime because the configured Falcon and Perplexity providers were unavailable, so this file records the literature synthesis used for the review.

TET3 is a nuclear methylcytosine dioxygenase with an N-terminal CXXC domain and a C-terminal TET catalytic core. A human Mendelian-disease study tested TET3 missense variants in a catalytic assay and showed that variants in the catalytic domain are commonly hypomorphic, supporting the DNA 5-methylcytosine dioxygenase activity of the human protein [PMID:31928709]. UniProt infers the iterative 5mC-to-5hmC-to-5fC-to-5caC reaction sequence from TET orthology and records mouse-supported zygotic localization of TET3 to the male pronucleus [genes/human/TET3/TET3-uniprot.txt].

The best direct promoter-targeting evidence in the GOA set comes from Xenopus Tet3. Xu et al. cloned human and mouse TET3 but used Xenopus Tet3 for the mechanistic experiments; the Xenopus CXXC domain and catalytic activity cooperate to regulate target-gene promoters during eye and neural development [PMID:23217707]. The CXXC domain co-crystallized with 5mC-containing DNA and CpG-containing DNA, and the authors concluded that Tet3 CXXC binds an unmodified cytosine in a shifted register with only slight CpG preference. That evidence supports retaining the existing methyl-CpG binding rows while keeping generic DNA binding broad rather than narrowing it to unmethylated CpG binding.

Like TET2, TET3 binds OGT and promotes OGT-dependent O-GlcNAcylation/H3K4 methylation at active promoters [PMID:23222540; PMID:23353889]. That mechanism is a non-core chromatin-regulatory context for TET3's recruitment function. The male-pronucleus, chromosome, and nucleus rows reflect the zygotic and chromatin localization of the enzyme; the female-pronucleus IEA row is left unresolved because the cached evidence confirms paternal-pronucleus enrichment but does not separate female-pronucleus residence from protection of maternal chromatin.
