## Human integrin activation and adhesive behavior

[PMID:19234460](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2857384/) provides human platelet/leukocyte measurements and KINDLIN3 rescue/knockdown. Read Figures 1C,D and 2F–H for activation-dependent antibody/ligand binding with preserved receptor abundance; Figures 2B,C for leukocyte aggregation; and Figures 1B and 4C–F for substrate contacts and spreading. Main Methods, “Cell lines and kindlin rescue experiments,” identifies IMAGE clone 6709978 as the rescue source. The actual main Results and Methods and Figures 1–4 captions were read; supplementary panels and movies were not independently inspected. P-selectin induction and several upstream signaling responses remain present, so the defect is not loss of all signaling. A patient 56-kDa band and proposed downstream initiation do not define a normal splice product. The osteopetrosis mechanism is exploratory.

[PMID:19234463](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2680140/) independently identifies human FERMT3 lesions, reduced protein expression and defective adhesive contacts and migration. Figure 4A measures contact with immobilized ICAM-1; Figures 4B,C measure migration. The Methods explicitly calls the rescuing construct **mouse EGFP-KINDLIN3**, expressed in human patient B cells. Human genetics, recipient-cell behavior and cross-species complementation are distinct evidence. The human 663-residue diagram in Figure 2 is not an exact sequence join to the mouse rescue construct or proof of a current human splice-product-specific effect. Main Results/Methods and all four figure captions were read; supplementary panels and movies were not.

Integrin activation is retained as the directly resolved process. Leukocyte adhesion, migration, regulation of integrin-mediated cell-cell adhesion, signaling-associated spreading and platelet behavior remain positive contextual annotations, without inventing separate enzymatic activities. For cell-cell adhesion, the living-cell aggregation results in PMID:19234460 complement the ICAM-1-coated substrate assays in PMID:19234463. Inside-out activation alone is not treated as proof of every outside-in signaling event.

## Phospholipid interaction

[PMID:27101375](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4839668/) directly tests the recombinant Leu142–Gln224 F1 loop. Methods describes the fragment, vesicles and NMR measurements; Results and Figure 3 report interaction with POPS and POPG, with much weaker POPC perturbation. These substrates support [GO:0005543 phospholipid binding](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/QuickGO/term/GO:0005543), a verified descendant of lipid binding. Selecting only phosphatidylserine binding would omit the tested phosphatidylglycerol interaction. This paper does not measure phosphoinositide-specific binding by the PH domain.

Main Methods, Results, Discussion and all six captions were read. Figure 6 is explicitly hypothetical, and the text does not establish its full-length membrane-orientation model experimentally. The loop endpoints agree with the current human sequence, but Methods does not state its donor organism. The printed EK21 peptide contains 22 residues despite its stated 21-residue E146–K166 description; it has an extra initial E compared with current human residues 146–166. That discrepancy is preserved in the product-context table, not converted into an inferred natural isoform, a negative binding result or a source-defect assertion. Neither fragment is a newly authored natural product.
