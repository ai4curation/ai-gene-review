Gene Description

ARC (activity-regulated cytoskeleton-associated protein, Arg3.1) is a neuronal immediate-early gene product that is, structurally and functionally, a domesticated retrotransposon capsid protein. Its 396-residue chain folds into two lobes whose arrangement matches the capsid domain of retroviral Gag polyproteins, and tetrapod Arc genes derive from a vertebrate lineage of Ty3/gypsy retrotransposons; fly Arc genes come from a separate insect lineage of the same element family, so the two are convergent domestications rather than orthologues of a single cellular ancestor. The protein does two things that reflect that ancestry. First, it self-assembles. A short helical coil in the N-terminal domain drives oligomerisation beyond the dimer stage, and the assembled protein forms double-shelled capsids roughly 32 nm across that encapsidate RNA. RNA is required for efficient assembly, and the RNA taken up is not sequence-selective - abundant transcripts are packaged roughly in proportion to their abundance, with Arc's own mRNA prominent because it is abundant where Arc is made. Neurons release these capsids in extracellular vesicles; recipient neurons endocytose them, and the delivered mRNA reaches the cytoplasm where it can be translated on demand. Arc is therefore a vehicle for intercellular RNA transfer in the nervous system. Second, Arc weakens synapses. Induced within minutes of strong activity and delivered as mRNA to stimulated dendritic regions, the protein accumulates at the postsynaptic density and the lateral margins of dendritic spines, anchored to the membrane by palmitoylation of an N-terminal cysteine cluster rather than by a transmembrane segment. There it binds endophilin 2/3 and dynamin 2 through separate regions and accelerates clathrin- and dynamin-dependent endocytosis of AMPA-type glutamate receptors into early endosomes. Losing Arc raises surface AMPA receptor levels and slows their internalisation; raising Arc does the reverse. This single mechanism underlies the late, protein-synthesis-dependent phase of long-term potentiation, metabotropic-receptor long-term depression, homeostatic downscaling of synaptic strength during chronic inactivity, and the consolidation of long-term memory. Targeting is paradoxical and deliberate: CaMKII-beta in its calmodulin-free state recruits Arc preferentially to synapses that have fallen silent, so the protein prunes the weak rather than the active. A separate nuclear pool, controlled by a dedicated localisation signal, retention domain and export signal, raises promyelocytic leukaemia body formation and lowers GluA1 transcription, giving the same downscaling by a transcriptional route. During cerebellar development Arc mediates the final stage of climbing-fibre synapse elimination. Both activities depend on the same interface. Mutating the seven-residue oligomerisation motif abolishes capsid formation and also blocks Arc-facilitated endocytosis; CaMKII phosphorylation of the Gag N-lobe disrupts the same surface and switches high-order assembly off. Protein levels are held brief by ubiquitination - by RNF216/Triad3A at two adjacent lysines, and by UBE3A - so that the signal is transient. Outside the nervous system, Arc is confined to skin-migratory dendritic cells, where it accelerates migration through myosin II-dependent actin dynamics and thereby shapes T-cell activation.

Proposed New Ontology Terms

virus-like capsid assembly

Definition: The aggregation, arrangement and bonding together of protein subunits encoded by endogenous, non-viral genes to form a virus-like capsid, a non-infectious protein coat that surrounds nucleic acid.

Justification: GO already recognises the product of this process: GO:0170047 virus-like capsid is a cellular component whose definition explicitly names fly and tetrapod Arc. There is no corresponding biological-process term. The only capsid-assembly terms in the ontology are viral - GO:0019069 viral capsid assembly and its children GO:0039708 and GO:0039709 - and none of them can be used for a protein encoded by a host gene that assembles a non-infectious particle. As a result the assembly step of Arc, the step that every other Arc function depends on, has to be annotated as GO:0051260 protein homooligomerization, which is true of any trimer and says nothing about the shell. The same gap will be hit by every domesticated Gag gene as they are curated: fly Arc1 and Arc2, the PNMA/MOAP family, and PEG10 and the other retroelement-derived mammalian genes.

structural constituent of virus-like capsid

Definition: The action of a molecule that contributes to the structural integrity of a virus-like capsid, a non-infectious protein coat encoded by endogenous, non-viral genes that surrounds nucleic acid.

Justification: The molecular-function half of the same gap. GO:0039660 structural constituent of virion exists for viral capsid proteins, but a domesticated Gag protein cannot use it, so Arc's capsid role is annotated to the bare parent GO:0005198 structural molecule activity - a term shared with keratins, tubulins and ribosomal proteins. A sibling of GO:0039660 under GO:0005198 would make the annotation say what the structure actually is, and would pair with the CC term GO:0170047 that already exists.

Core Functions

Retroelement Gag-derived capsid protein. Arc self-assembles, through a seven-residue motif in an N-terminal helical coil and through conserved inter-protomer surfaces on both Gag lobes, into double-shelled virus-like particles roughly 32 nm across. This is the protein's defining structural activity: it contributes to the integrity of a higher-order assembly, and disrupting the interface arrests the protein at the dimer stage. The assembly is RNA-dependent in the same way retroviral Gag assembly is - stripping bound nucleic acid suppresses capsid formation and adding back an unrelated mRNA restores it - and the requirement is quantitative rather than sequence-specific.

Postsynaptic endocytic adaptor for AMPA receptor removal. Arc bridges the endocytic machinery to the postsynaptic membrane: separate regions of the protein bind endophilin 2/3 and dynamin 2, both interactions are needed for endosome association, and the resulting assembly accelerates internalisation of AMPA-type glutamate receptors into early endosomes. The role is that of an adaptor bringing components together rather than of an enzyme - Arc has no catalytic activity - and it is the same oligomerisation interface that supports capsid formation, since mutating the coil motif blocks Arc-facilitated endocytosis. Membrane attachment is by palmitoylation of an N-terminal cysteine cluster, not by a transmembrane segment. Perturbation works in both directions: losing Arc raises surface AMPA receptors and slows endocytosis, raising Arc blocks the inactivity-induced increase.

NARROWING BIOLOGY RESIDUAL_SUBGAP

What is known: Firmly established for the human protein: the sequence is 396 aa with the Gag N-lobe and C-lobe solved on human material (PDB 6TN7, 6TNQ, 6TQ0, 6YTU, 7R1Z, 7R23, 8QF4, 8QF5); purified recombinant human Arc reversibly self-associates into large soluble oligomers; and the 0.95 A structure of the human oligomerisation coil exists. Not established for the human protein: that human Arc capsids form, package RNA, are released in extracellular vesicles, transfer mRNA between human neurons, bind human endophilin or dynamin, or drive AMPA receptor endocytosis in human neurons. Every one of those is rodent-only.

Significance: Human and rodent Arc are the same length and highly conserved, so the transfers are reasonable and the annotations are honestly coded. But ARC is implicated in human neurodevelopmental and neurodegenerative disease, and there is a specific reason to check: the capsid pathway is a horizontal-transfer mechanism whose efficiency could differ between species without any change to the fold. Human iPSC-derived neurons make this testable now.

What would resolve it: Repeat the core Pastuzyn experiments with human sequence and human cells: purify recombinant human Arc and look for 32 nm double-shelled particles by cryo-EM; isolate extracellular vesicles from human iPSC-derived cortical neurons and assay Arc protein and ARC mRNA content; and test neuron-to-neuron transfer using ARC-knockout human neurons as recipients.

Gap: It is unresolved whether the Arc protein is a component of canonical dendritic transport RNP granules, or whether its only RNA-containing assembly is the virus-like capsid.

OPEN BIOLOGYCURATION CC_DARK

What is known: Established: ARC mRNA carries an A2RE element, is a bona fide granule cargo, and is translated in bursts at granules; and Arc protein binds RNA and builds capsids that encapsidate it. Not established: that Arc protein resides in the granules that carry its transcript. The single annotation asserting it (mouse Arc IDA on PMID:23936366, which seeds the human IBA) comes from a TOG/Ckap5 knockout study in which Arc protein is imaged only as nascent translation output near granules.

Significance: The two structures have opposite fates - a transport granule keeps mRNA inside the cell and releases it at a synapse, a capsid exports it to another cell - so which one Arc protein occupies determines whether GO:0071598 or GO:0170047 describes its RNA-associated pool. At present the record asserts both, on very unequal evidence.

What would resolve it: Co-immunostain endogenous Arc protein with established granule markers (hnRNP A2, Staufen, FMRP) in dendrites of stimulated neurons, and separate the two assemblies biochemically: granules and capsids differ in density and in detergent and RNase sensitivity, so a gradient fractionation with RNase and Triton controls would distinguish them directly.

Gap: GO can say that Arc forms a virus-like capsid but not that it assembles one, and not that it is a structural constituent of one.

OPEN ONTOLOGY RESIDUAL_SUBGAP

What is known: The cellular component exists and names Arc in its definition (GO:0170047). The matching process and function terms exist only in viral form (GO:0019069, GO:0039660) and are unusable for a host-encoded protein. Consequently Arc's assembly step is annotated as GO:0051260 protein homooligomerization and its capsid role as bare GO:0005198 structural molecule activity.

Significance: Domesticated retroelement Gag genes are a growing class - fly Arc1 and Arc2, PEG10, the PNMA family - and all of them will hit the same two missing terms. Without them the annotations cannot distinguish a capsid builder from any protein that trimerises.

What would resolve it: Add the two terms proposed in proposed_new_terms, as siblings of the viral terms under GO:0065003 and GO:0005198 respectively, mirroring the precedent already set by GO:0170047.

Gap: How the two halves of Arc's biology relate is unknown: whether capsid-mediated intercellular mRNA transfer is part of the same mechanism as AMPA receptor endocytosis, or a second function that happens to use the same interface.

OPEN BIOLOGY MF_DARK

What is known: Established: the same seven-residue N-terminal coil motif is required for capsid formation and for Arc-facilitated endocytosis, and CaMKII phosphorylation of the Gag N-lobe switches off both high-order oligomerisation and Arc's synaptic weakening action. Not established: whether an Arc capsid is the species that recruits endophilin and dynamin at the postsynaptic membrane, or whether endocytosis uses lower-order oligomers and the capsid is a separate, exported form. UniProt raises but does not settle a third possibility - that capsids are how eliminated synaptic material is removed.

Significance: This determines whether Arc has one molecular function with two outputs or two distinct functions, which is exactly the distinction core_functions is meant to make. It also determines whether the intercellular transfer is a signalling mechanism in its own right or a by-product of synaptic protein turnover.

What would resolve it: Separation-of-function mutants are the route. Point mutations that block higher-order assembly while preserving the endophilin- and dynamin-binding regions (and the converse) would test whether endocytosis needs the capsid state; combining them with single-particle imaging of tagged Arc at spines would show which oligomeric species is present when receptors are internalised.

1. What the protein is

Arc is the product of a domesticated Ty3/gypsy retrotransposon Gag gene. UniProt's DOMAIN
block states it directly: "it contains large N- and C-terminal domains that form a bi-lobar
architecture similar to the capsid domain of human immunodeficiency virus (HIV) gag protein"
and "Tetrapod and fly Arc protein-coding genes originated independently from distinct lineages
of Ty3/gypsy retrotransposons."

The structural claim is directly evidenced. Zhang et al. solved the two lobes
[PMID:25864631, "We report crystal structures of Arc subdomains that form a bi-lobar architecture
remarkably similar to the capsid domain of human immunodeficiency virus (HIV) gag protein."] and
named them: [PMID:25864631, "We term RnArc (207-278) Arc N-lobe, and RnArc (278-370) Arc C-lobe;
together they form the Arc Gag domain."]. The human entry carries eight PDB structures
(6TN7, 6TNQ, 6TQ0, 6YTU, 7R1Z, 7R23, 8QF4, 8QF5), i.e. the Gag lobes and the
oligomerisation coil have been solved on the human sequence.

Functionally, Arc is two things at once:

  1. A capsid protein. It self-assembles into virus-like particles that encapsidate RNA,
    is released from neurons in extracellular vesicles, and delivers its own mRNA into
    recipient neurons [PMID:29328916, "Endogenous Arc protein is released from neurons in
    extracellular vesicles that mediate the transfer of Arc mRNA into new target cells, where
    it can undergo activity-dependent translation."; "Purified Arc capsids are endocytosed and
    are able to transfer Arc mRNA into the cytoplasm of neurons."]. RNA is required for
    assembly [PMID:29328916, "Stripping RNA resulted in significantly fewer fully-formed
    capsids (Figure 2E), suggesting that Arc capsids require RNA for normal assembly."], and
    the RNA it takes is not sequence-selective [PMID:29328916, "suggesting that prArc capsids
    show little specificity for a particular mRNA, but encapsulate abundant RNA according to
    stoichiometry."]. Intact capsids are needed for uptake [PMID:29328916, "Strikingly,
    prArc(RNA−) was unable to be taken up but instead coated the outside of neurons (Figure S6),
    further suggesting that intact Arc capsids are required for uptake and transfer."].

  2. A postsynaptic effector of AMPA-receptor removal. Arc binds endophilin 2/3 and dynamin 2
    and accelerates AMPAR endocytosis [PMID:17088211, "Here, we demonstrate that Arc/Arg3.1
    protein interacts with dynamin and specific isoforms of endophilin to enhance receptor
    endocytosis."], with the loss-of-function direction shown in the same paper [PMID:17088211,
    "Conversely, Arc KO neurons display increased levels of surface AMPARs and decreased rates
    of endocytosis."]. This is the mechanism behind homeostatic downscaling [PMID:17088213,
    "Overexpression of Arc blocks the upregulation of surface AMPARs and mEPSCs induced by
    chronic neuronal inactivity."; "Conversely, loss of Arc results in increased AMPAR function
    and abolishes homeostatic scaling of AMPARs."].

The bridge between the two is oligomerisation. The 7-residue motif in the N-terminal coil that
drives capsid formation is also required for Arc-facilitated endocytosis [PMID:33175445,
"The NT coil-coil interaction was also validated in live neurons using fluorescence lifetime
FRET imaging, and mutation of the oligomerization motif disrupted Arc-facilitated endocytosis."],
and CaMKII phosphorylation of the Gag N-lobe switches it off [PMID:31151856, "CaMKII
phosphorylates the N-lobe of the Arc GAG domain and disrupts an interaction surface essential
for high-order oligomerization."].

Other established roles: LTP maintenance and long-term memory consolidation [PMID:10818134,
"Our studies show that disruption of Arc protein expression impairs the maintenance phase of
LTP without affecting its induction and impairs consolidation of LTM for spatial water task
training without affecting task acquisition or short-term memory."]; LTD [PMID:17088210,
"In addition, long-term depression is significantly impaired."]; nuclear signalling
[PMID:23749147, "Arc localization to the nucleus promotes an activity-induced increase in the
expression of promyelocytic leukemia nuclear bodies, which decreases GluA1 (also called Gria1)
transcription and synaptic strength."]; developmental synapse elimination [PMID:23791196,
"We conclude that Arc mediates the final stage of CF synapse elimination downstream of P/Q-type
VDCCs by removing CF synapses from PC somata."]; and, outside neurons, skin dendritic-cell
migration [PMID:28783680, "Mechanistically, Arc/Arg3.1 was required for accelerated DC
migration during inflammation because it regulated actin dynamics through nonmuscle myosin II."].

Membrane attachment is by palmitoylation of a cysteine cluster [PMID:29264923, "We further show
that Arc can be palmitoylated on cysteines clustered in a motif, 94CLCRC98."], which puts a
substantial pool of synaptic Arc in rafts [PMID:29264923, "We confirmed that ~50% of
synaptosomal Arc that is not extracted by cold Triton X-100 associates with membrane rafts
(Figure 1C)."]. Targeting to inactive synapses is via CaMKIIβ [PMID:22579289, "Here, we show
targeting of Arc to inactive synapses via a high-affinity interaction with CaMKIIβ that is not
bound to calmodulin."]. Levels are limited by RNF216/Triad3A ubiquitination [PMID:24945773,
"Here, we demonstrate that the RING domain ubiquitin ligase Triad3A/RNF216 ubiquitinates Arc,
resulting in its rapid proteasomal degradation."], which UniProt maps to Lys-268/Lys-269
(CROSSLNK 268, CROSSLNK 269, MUTAGEN 268 K->A: Complete loss of RNF216-mediated ubiquitination; when associated by A-269.).

description: 'ARC (activity-regulated cytoskeleton-associated protein, Arg3.1) is a neuronal immediate-early
  gene product that is, structurally and functionally, a domesticated retrotransposon capsid protein.
  Its 396-residue chain folds into two lobes whose arrangement matches the capsid domain of retroviral
  Gag polyproteins, and tetrapod Arc genes derive from a vertebrate lineage of Ty3/gypsy retrotransposons;
  fly Arc genes come from a separate insect lineage of the same element family, so the two are convergent
  domestications rather than orthologues of a single cellular ancestor.


  The protein does two things that reflect that ancestry. First, it self-assembles. A short helical coil
  in the N-terminal domain drives oligomerisation beyond the dimer stage, and the assembled protein forms
  double-shelled capsids roughly 32 nm across that encapsidate RNA. RNA is required for efficient assembly,
  and the RNA taken up is not sequence-selective - abundant transcripts are packaged roughly in proportion
  to their abundance, with Arc''s own mRNA prominent because it is abundant where Arc is made. Neurons
  release these capsids in extracellular vesicles; recipient neurons endocytose them, and the delivered
  mRNA reaches the cytoplasm where it can be translated on demand. Arc is therefore a vehicle for intercellular
  RNA transfer in the nervous system.


  Second, Arc weakens synapses. Induced within minutes of strong activity and delivered as mRNA to stimulated
  dendritic regions, the protein accumulates at the postsynaptic density and the lateral margins of dendritic
  spines, anchored to the membrane by palmitoylation of an N-terminal cysteine cluster rather than by
  a transmembrane segment. There it binds endophilin 2/3 and dynamin 2 through separate regions and accelerates
  clathrin- and dynamin-dependent endocytosis of AMPA-type glutamate receptors into early endosomes. Losing
  Arc raises surface AMPA receptor levels and slows their internalisation; raising Arc does the reverse.
  This single mechanism underlies the late, protein-synthesis-dependent phase of long-term potentiation,
  metabotropic-receptor long-term depression, homeostatic downscaling of synaptic strength during chronic
  inactivity, and the consolidation of long-term memory. Targeting is paradoxical and deliberate: CaMKII-beta
  in its calmodulin-free state recruits Arc preferentially to synapses that have fallen silent, so the
  protein prunes the weak rather than the active. A separate nuclear pool, controlled by a dedicated localisation
  signal, retention domain and export signal, raises promyelocytic leukaemia body formation and lowers
  GluA1 transcription, giving the same downscaling by a transcriptional route. During cerebellar development
  Arc mediates the final stage of climbing-fibre synapse elimination.


  Both activities depend on the same interface. Mutating the seven-residue oligomerisation motif abolishes
  capsid formation and also blocks Arc-facilitated endocytosis; CaMKII phosphorylation of the Gag N-lobe
  disrupts the same surface and switches high-order assembly off. Protein levels are held brief by ubiquitination
  - by RNF216/Triad3A at two adjacent lysines, and by UBE3A - so that the signal is transient. Outside
  the nervous system, Arc is confined to skin-migratory dendritic cells, where it accelerates migration
  through myosin II-dependent actin dynamics and thereby shapes T-cell activation.'
