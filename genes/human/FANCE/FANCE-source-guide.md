# FANCE evidence and source guide

This agent-authored guide combines the original-evidence and database-source assessments. Their separate complete reading scopes remain labeled in the reference notes; an image read by one assessment is not attributed to the other.

FANCE is a nuclear FA core component that recognizes the FANCD2 substrate. Association with another subunit, substrate recognition and ubiquitin-transfer chemistry are different claims. The sources below identify which experiments establish each claim. Natural alternative products are not asserted: the official displayed protein has 536 residues, while tagged proteins, engineered deletions and external transcript models have their own boundaries.

## Substrate recognition and core function

[PMID:12093742, Results and Methods, Figures 1, 4 and 6](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC125396/) reports endogenous and transfected human FANCE in HeLa nuclear foci, association with the FA core, mammalian two-hybrid interactions, and FANCE–FANCC/FANCD2 preparations from insect cells. Figure 1 and Figure 6 pixels were inspected in the direct-original assessment; the independent database assessment read the stated Methods, Results and captions without inspecting those pixels. Figure 6 supports FANCE–FANCD2 binding through complementary assays; it is not a purified ubiquitin-transfer experiment or a simultaneous stoichiometry determination of the entire substrate-bound complex. The discussion expressly leaves FANCC cotransport versus nuclear retention unresolved.

[PMID:24451376, Methods and Results, Figures 1–2](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3945361/) provides a more specific functional distinction. Human FANCE CΔ25/F522D perturbations impair FANCD2 recognition and downstream monoubiquitination while retaining measured associations with other core subunits. Human complementation, cross-linker sensitivity and chromosome endpoints connect this recognition step to repair. Figures 1–2 pixels were inspected in the direct-original assessment; the independent database assessment read the stated Methods, Results and Discussion without inspecting those pixels. The authors assume retained intrinsic catalytic competence of the mutant complex and cannot exclude additional affected interactions; those limits remain part of the interpretation. No autonomous ubiquitin-transfer activity is assigned to FANCE.

The inherited proposal's original reference is **PMID:12093742**; PMID:12649160 is a supporting reference. Its authored proposal is refined in place, preserving its original PMID, evidence and five quotation anchors. The specific term, [GO:1990756 ubiquitin-like ligase-substrate adaptor activity](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/QuickGO/term/GO:1990756), describes bringing a ubiquitin-like ligase and its substrate together. It is supported here by the recognition evidence rather than generic membership in a stable complex. FANCL/UBE2T, rather than FANCE, perform ubiquitin transfer.

Current official human comparator queries recover GO:1990756 for VHL (IDA PMID:24899725/34290272) and FBXW7 (IDA PMID:15150404/34741373, including an isoform-specific record). The bounded SPOP query has no exact or descendant hit. This mixed result does not establish a universal convention or a missing-annotation claim. The fixed-source GO-CAM index has no FANCE/Q9HB96 entry. The specific authored proposal now occupies its original position with action NEW; the earlier broad term and duplicate last proposal are no longer asserted. This is an in-place refinement of an authored annotation, not a change to an imported GOA term, and it adds no distinct activity or quotation. One core unit represents this substrate-recognition activity in interstrand cross-link repair, with nucleoplasm as a supported functional pool and FA nuclear-complex membership recorded separately. ## Nuclear and chromatin evidence

Its nuclear-localization signals are predictions, not imaging. Independent direct nuclear imaging in PMID:12093742 supports the broad nucleus annotation. The figure-level limitation does not establish absence, and the accepted association does not make every nuclear FANCE molecule chromatin-bound.

[PMID:22266823, Results and Figure 2a/e](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3280818/) identifies FANCE with FANCA/FANCC in FLAG-FAAP20 immunoprecipitates from human 293T cells and separately measures FANCE abundance after FAAP20 depletion/MG132 in HeLa cells. This is not evidence that human FANCE cannot localize to the nuclear periphery and does not justify a NOT annotation.

## CHEK1 phosphorylation and cross-link resistance

[PMID:17296736, original Methods, Results, Figures 1–2 and Table 1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1899922/) establishes CHEK1-mediated phosphorylation of human FANCE at Thr346 and Ser374. Full-length FLAG-tagged human FANCE and the TS/AA mutant were expressed in patient-derived FA-E cells; bacterial GST fragments spanning residues 332–365, 349–382 or 332–382 and purified human-FANCE residues 149–536 were used in recombinant kinase assays. The kinase is Chk1, not FANCE. Phosphosite-mutant and kinase controls support the site assignment; the mutant restores basal FANCD2 monoubiquitination and nuclear foci but does not restore normal damage-induced increases or mitomycin C resistance. These distinct outcomes should not be compressed into a claim that all FANCD2 regulation is phosphorylation-independent. The downstream phosphorylation-dependent mechanism remains unresolved.
