# EIF2AK4 evidence review

GCN2 couples amino-acid and translation stress to eIF2alpha phosphorylation and altered initiation. The evidence supports one integrated eIF2alpha kinase mechanism. Reduced bulk initiation and increased translation of selected stress transcripts are compatible outcomes of that mechanism. The review retains 52 source contexts: 23 ACCEPT, 15 MODIFY, 12 KEEP_AS_NON_CORE and two UNDECIDED, with no NEW assertions or publication excerpts.

## Catalytic mechanism and experimental scope

The early mammalian biochemistry uses mouse GCN2 AJ243533, expressed in human 293/293T cells or obtained from mouse liver. Human eIF2alpha is the substrate, including a Ser51 mutant control. The human GCN2 sequence reported in that study is only a 548-residue EST-derived fragment; it is not the enzyme used in the assays. Serum-withdrawal activation and the lack of an amino-acid response in that particular transfected preparation should not be generalized to every physiological stress or to every human protein product. [PMID:10504407](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10504407/)

Human SQ20B GCN2 depletion reduces eIF2alpha phosphorylation during leucine limitation. The p21 study separates those human experiments from mouse reporter, polysome and translation experiments. Its selective increase in a p21 transcript's translation is consistent with repression of bulk synthesis. The later human knockout/rescue work supports GCN2-dependent initiation control in rRNA quality control, while RNF10 performs the ubiquitination step. Neither downstream RNA decay nor ubiquitin-ligase chemistry is assigned to GCN2. [PMID:26102367](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26102367/), [PMID:39947182](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39947182/)

The collision-response experiments use human MCF10A cells. GCN2 inhibition and shRNA reduce eIF2alpha phosphorylation; kinase-dead ZAKalpha rescues this branch, while simple glutamine-starvation signaling persists without ZAK. GCN1 was captured on collided ribosomes, but GCN2 and GCN20 were not captured in that preparation. Positive yeast and mouse ribosome-association evidence is retained separately: association does not require GCN2 to be an obligatory structural ribosome subunit, and a preparation-specific failure to capture it does not establish universal absence. [PMID:32610081](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32610081/), [PMID:2038314](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2038314/), [PMID:23447528](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23447528/)

The core uses eIF2alpha kinase activity, GCN2-mediated signaling and stress-dependent negative regulation of initiation in the cytosol. The two process terms have no ancestor/descendant relation in the inspected typed ontology closure. The phosphorylation-dependent initiation term describes the same mechanism already explicit in the catalytic function and narrative. Additional broad response labels, cellular phenotypes and organismal effects do not require separate catalytic cores.

## Ortholog context and directional refinements

All 29 ISS contexts have exact current term matches in the captured mouse Q9QZ05 or yeast P15442 donor annotations. Those joins identify the curator's evidence chain; they are not substitutions for the experimental scope. Human expression hosts are kept distinct from mouse constructs. In the antiviral study, the Northwestern probe is viral GAR RNA, while the tRNA experiment concerns activation. The viral-translation assertion is refined to negative regulation of viral translation, preserving the measured sign. [PMID:16601681](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16601681/)

## Unresolved source claims

The IPR016255 family match is real, and the conserved kinase function is supported. Its checkpoint premise is fungal, however, and the sources do not establish the same DNA-damage-checkpoint role for human EIF2AK4. The fungal response is stimulus-dependent. The mouse UV study demonstrates translational attenuation and discusses a possible cell-cycle consequence; it does not resolve the human checkpoint transfer. This annotation remains UNDECIDED without asserting absence of any mammalian checkpoint contribution. [PMID:17369398](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17369398/), [PMID:19033384](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19033384/), [PMID:12176355](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12176355/)

The distinct cold-response annotation also remains UNDECIDED. The PMID:25329545 abstract and a selected caption were accessible, but the operative cold assay was not recovered. Independent evidence supports the source's central kinase and amino-acid stress claims; general stress biology cannot verify the cold stimulus. The positive imported experimental assertion is preserved. [PMID:25329545](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25329545/)

## Source clarification after external review

The evidence for mouse GCN2 in polysomal fractions is independent of the paper's emphasis on IMPACT. In [PMID:23447528](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3624466/), the Polysome Profiles Methods describe N2a-cell sucrose gradients, and Figure 5C–F explicitly detects and quantifies GCN2, GCN1 and IMPACT separately. Panel E normalizes to input; panel F normalizes to polysomal rpS6. The selected Results and Discussion retain GCN2 association after the latter normalization. This supports the existing cytoplasmic and ribosome-association interpretations without asserting direct human localization, direct molecular contact or obligatory ribosomal membership. The accessible indexed Results contain a discontinuity; the complete caption provides an independent locator for the GCN2 measurements. No figure pixels or separately downloaded supplement were assessed.

The fungal checkpoint evidence is positive. [PMID:17369398](https://genesdev.cshlp.org/content/21/6/649.full.html), Figure 3B–D, links Gcn2 to UV-induced eIF2alpha phosphorylation, translation attenuation and delayed pre-RC formation. [PMID:19033384](https://doi.org/10.1242/jcs.035428), Figure 2A–E, reports a Gcn2-dependent G1 delay after hydrogen peroxide; Mcm6 loading distinguishes this from a later delay that remains in the mutant. Lack of a response to every damaging stimulus does not refute DNA-damage-checkpoint participation. GO:0000077 and its parents describe checkpoint signaling rather than a universal response to DNA damage. The human transfer remains UNDECIDED because conservation of this specific checkpoint function is unresolved, not because fungal biology is presumed irrelevant or because human inactivity has been established.

The optional availability field for PMID:24310610 was omitted to avoid collapsing partial access into a binary claim. Its normal cache remains marked full text, while its inspected contents omit Methods and separately published supplements. Conversely, PMID:23447528 remains an unchanged abstract-only cache even though selected original sections were separately accessed. These access distinctions and precise primary-source locators are recorded in the [source guide](EIF2AK4-source-guide.md#claim-specific-primary-source-locators).
