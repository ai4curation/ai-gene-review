Gene Description

CNNM2 is a multipass membrane protein that controls cellular and systemic magnesium balance. It is present at basolateral membranes of renal tubules, where its dysfunction causes renal magnesium wasting, and is also expressed in the nervous system. CNNM2 supports magnesium export and interacts with the TRPM7 channel to regulate divalent-cation entry. Its cytoplasmic tandem CBS domain binds Mg–ATP and regulatory partners including ARL15 and PRL phosphatases; these interactions link magnesium handling to changes in protein conformation and channel activity. The mechanism of autonomous human magnesium translocation remains unresolved. Pathogenic variants can cause hypomagnesemia with seizures and neurodevelopmental impairment.

Core Functions

CNNM2 supports magnesium transfer across membranes and maintains magnesium balance, including renal reabsorption at basolateral tubular membranes. Its tandem CBS domain binds Mg–ATP and regulatory proteins that modulate magnesium handling; the intrinsic permeation and coupling mechanism remains unresolved.

CNNM2 review notes

CNNM2 is a membrane-associated magnesium-handling protein with separable evidence for magnesium export and TRPM7 regulation. This review preserves all 13 original annotations, three alternative-product objects and six original reference identities. Eleven original assertions are accepted and the two synaptic locations are retained as non-core. One nonredundant molecular-function annotation, ion channel regulator activity, is proposed by ortholog inference; no biological process is added.

Transport evidence and its limits

The original mouse study cloned a 693-residue ACDP2 sequence from distal convoluted tubule cells and measured magnesium-dependent currents and reversal shifts after expression in Xenopus oocytes, with water and poly(A) controls. This historical construct is not equated with every current human product. Authentic original Methods and Results were recovered as author-uploaded article text, separately from the unchanged normal abstract (PMID:15899945(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15899945/)). The complete 2010 abstract reports human splice-variant 1 complementation and magnesium uptake in deficient Salmonella, while splice-variant 2 and empty-vector controls were negative; its full body remains unavailable (PMID:20519162(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20519162/)).

A substantive conflicting result must be retained. The 2016 study expresses human NM_017649.4 and NM_199076.2 in HEK293 cells and detects no expression-dependent magnesium currents, influx or efflux under its conditions. TRPM7 and SLC41A1 controls establish that the assays can detect transport. Its authentic institutional full PDF was inspected without modifying the normal abstract (PMID:27068403(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27068403/)). This context-dependent negative result is not dismissed, but neither is it a universal disproof of the positive CNNM2-specific export results.

Mouse CNNM2 expression drives magnesium efflux after extracellular magnesium withdrawal, and CBS deletion or T568I disrupts that response. Later mouse CNNM2 experiments show magnesium lowering even in TRPM7-knockout cells, and ARL15 inhibits efflux in a binding-dependent manner (PMID:24706765(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24706765/), PMID:34928937(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34928937/), PMID:37449820(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37449820/)). These observations support retaining the existing broad magnesium-transporter inference. They do not settle autonomous human permeation, a sodium-coupling stoichiometry or activity of every natural product. Transport is not retained merely because CNNM2 regulates TRPM7.

The original patient study shows renal magnesium wasting with segregating CNNM2 variants and human basolateral renal staining. Its electrophysiology uses corrected full-length mouse AK032006.1 and measures magnesium-sensitive sodium currents, with no observed magnesium current. HEK293 host identity does not make this a human-protein assay (PMID:21397062(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21397062/)). Human NM_017649.5 expression and 25Mg-uptake measurements independently support cellular magnesium handling and variant effects; uptake alone is not isolated-protein translocation (PMID:38519529(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38519529/)). Zebrafish loss/rescue and brain phenotypes support biological relevance without establishing an extra developmental process performed by CNNM2 (PMID:24699222(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24699222/)).

ATP recognition and channel regulation

Purified human CNNM2 residues 429–584 bind adenine nucleotides in NMR and crystallographic experiments, with magnesium favoring ATP binding and changing the CBS-domain conformation. ATPase activity remains speculation, and no ATPase annotation is proposed (PMID:25184538(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25184538/)). The mouse CBS-domain binding study supplies an independent direct donor premise, not a human-domain experiment (PMID:24706765(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24706765/)). ATP binding is integrated into the magnesium-handling narrative instead of being presented as a separate physiological core.

The proposed ion channel regulator activity is supported by physical association plus functional modulation. Wild-type mouse CNNM2 stimulates TRPM7-dependent zinc entry; loss of TRPM7, an inactive TRPM7 pore or an inhibitor blocks the response (PMID:34928937(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34928937/)). The 2025 study maps mouse CNNM2 contacts with TRPM7 and uses export-disabled S269P or CBS-deletion constructs to separate channel regulation from magnesium lowering. The CNBH domain and the ARL15-binding site affect channel modulation, but the exact gating mechanism remains unresolved. The modest isolated-domain stimulation of TRPM7 kinase with a generic MBP substrate is not promoted to a separate physiological kinase-activator function (PMID:40962059(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40962059/)).

Two cores retain these distinct activities: magnesium transport/homeostasis and TRPM7 regulation. The latter is an ISS inference from authenticated mouse Cnnm2/Q3TWN3, not human IDA. Human ARL15–CNNM2 fragment structures and binding experiments corroborate the regulatory interface; mouse full-length efflux assays remain separate (PMID:37449820(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37449820/)). Current GO definitions and typed ancestry show that GO:0099106 adds coverage beyond the two seeded molecular functions. No suitable magnesium-specific regulator child is available among its current children. The target has no match in the selected GO-CAM index; this is not used as evidence of a missing process.
