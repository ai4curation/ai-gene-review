## 2026-10-10 evidence follow-up at source head ca3d9ba

The complete notes above and the incoming history are preserved. This appendix supersedes the earlier generic-only PP2A interpretation and the statement that these notes contain no primary quotations. All original annotation fields, fetched product descriptors and publication caches remain unchanged.

CHK2 binds PP2A through regulatory B subunits. The original abstract reports [PMID:15380617 "The A and C subunits of PP2A were present in the complexes"], while exact curated interaction records distinguish the PPP2R5C alternatives and PPP2R5A/B/E assays. GO:0051721 names binding to PP2A, not catalytic phosphatase activity; recognizing a regulatory subunit does not transfer its complex's chemistry to CHEK2. The later phage-display record maps a CHEK2 interacting region 68–79 against PPP2R5A 1–486 (PMID:37219487), with motif-screen scope rather than full-length native-complex or affinity confirmation. Seven source-specific refinements retain those different experimental limits.

Alternative products cannot all be described as equivalent kinases. The del2–3 sequence AY551303 matches current O96017-4 exactly and lacks canonical residues 107–197. The original Figure 7 reports [PMID:15361853 "activity approximately 60% of wt CHK2"] after expression of Myc-tagged variants in U-2-OS cells and kinase assays with GST-p53. This is positive activity, despite the unchanged UniProt inactive-product description. The authored product description now states the conflict; it neither edits the source descriptor nor generalizes the result to every product or context. Original primary: https://www.researchgate.net/publication/8352461_Alternative_splicing_and_mutation_status_of_CHEK2_in_stage_III_breast_cancer (Figure 7; Transfection and functional analyses; accession list).

The BRCA2 regulatory mechanism has reaction-level support. Figure 1D tests wild-type and T3387A BRCA2 C-terminal fragments and reports [PMID:18317453 "phosphorylation by Chk2"], with silver-stained protein amounts. This is distinct from CHK1/RAD51 site chemistry. The source also tests RAD51 associations and irradiation response with human constructs and mouse Chk2-mutant cells. These support CHEK2's regulatory kinase contribution to homologous-recombination repair, not autonomous strand exchange. The exact longer primary anchor is attached to that annotation; the genuine cached abstract is not rewritten.

Ten other positive generic associations remain non-core under the standing ClinGen instruction when no further informative class is established. Binding-class refinements for CDC25A, VCP and CDK11B identify the tested partner class; they do not transfer the partner's activity or assert a physiological regulation mechanism absent from the source. Independent self-association source rows are preserved with their transient dimer/activation-loop and isolated-construct limits. The core retains nucleoplasm rather than repeating its nucleus parent; the broader original nuclear annotations remain unchanged. Protein phosphorylation remains an existing performed catalytic process, not a NEW redundant proposal.
