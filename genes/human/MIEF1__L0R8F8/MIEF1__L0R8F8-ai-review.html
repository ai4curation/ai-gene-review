Gene Description

AltMIEF1 (also called AltMiD51 or MIEF1-MP; UniProt L0R8F8) is a 70-amino-acid protein translated from an upstream open reading frame located in exon 2 of the bicistronic human MIEF1 gene, in the 5' leader of the mRNA that also encodes MIEF1/MiD51. It is encoded in a different reading frame and shares no sequence with MiD51 (Q9NQG6), the outer-mitochondrial-membrane DRP1 adaptor of mitochondrial fission; the two are distinct proteins that happen to share a transcript and a gene symbol. In cultured human cells and colon tissue AltMIEF1 is two- to six-fold more abundant than MiD51. AltMIEF1 is a member of the LYR-motif (complex I LYR / LYRM) family, consisting of three alpha-helices with an N-terminal LYR tripeptide, and it resides in the mitochondrial matrix. Through its LYR motif it binds the 4'-phosphopantetheine arm of the mitochondrial acyl carrier protein NDUFAB1 (mt-ACP), and together with MALSU1 and mt-ACP it forms a heterotrimeric module that sits on the intersubunit face of late-stage mitoribosomal large-subunit (mt-LSU) assembly intermediates next to uL14m and bL19m. This MALSU1-AltMIEF1-mt-ACP module persists through multiple maturation steps alongside factors such as NSUN4-MTERF4, GTPBP5, GTPBP7 and GTPBP10, and sterically blocks premature joining of the small subunit until maturation is complete. Knockout of AltMIEF1 impairs mt-LSU assembly, and depletion with rescue experiments indicates that it supports the overall rate of mitochondrial protein synthesis. Overexpression in HeLa cells causes DRP1-dependent mitochondrial fragmentation, but this phenotype has not been linked to a loss-of-function defect.

Core Functions

LYR-motif subunit of the MALSU1-AltMIEF1-mt-ACP anti-association module, which binds late-stage mitoribosomal large-subunit assembly intermediates in the mitochondrial matrix next to uL14m. Through its LYR motif, AltMIEF1 docks the 4'-phosphopantetheine arm of mt-ACP (NDUFAB1), and the module sterically prevents premature joining of the small subunit until mt-LSU maturation is complete. Loss of AltMIEF1 impairs mt-LSU assembly and lowers the rate of mitochondrial translation.

Suggested Questions for Experts

Q: Is the complex I assembly defect reported for AltMiD51 knockout cells (PMID:31666358) distinguishable from the secondary effect of reduced mitochondrial translation of ND subunits? For example, are nuclear-encoded complex I modules affected differently from mtDNA-encoded ones, and is the defect rescued by restoring translation?

Q: Does the mitochondrial fragmentation caused by AltMIEF1 overexpression (PMID:29083303) have any loss-of-function counterpart, or is it a secondary stress response to matrix overexpression?

Q: What does AltMIEF1 contribute that MALSU1 and mt-ACP alone cannot? For example, is it required for mt-ACP recruitment to the module, or for module release before subunit joining?

Suggested Experiments

Experiment: In AltMIEF1 knockout cells made by frameshifting only the uORF, so that MiD51 is preserved (as in PMID:30181344), perform cryo-EM or sucrose-gradient analysis of mt-LSU particles. This would test whether MALSU1 and mt-ACP still bind and whether premature subunit association occurs.

Experiment: Run complexome profiling of complex I assembly intermediates in uORF-specific knockout cells, with and without re-expression of AltMIEF1 or of an LYR->AAA mutant. This would separate a direct assembly-factor role from a translation-dependent effect.

    LYR-motif subunit of the MALSU1-AltMIEF1-mt-ACP anti-association module, which binds
