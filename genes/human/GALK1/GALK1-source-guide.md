## Established reaction and substrate scope

Human GALK1 catalyzes ATP-dependent phosphorylation of galactose at carbon 1, committing the sugar to the Leloir pathway. Human cDNA expression in COS cells and purified recombinant-human enzyme kinetics independently support this reaction ([PMID:7542884](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7542884/), [PMID:12694189](https://febs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1046/j.1432-1033.2003.03538.x)). The latter original Methods and Results were read, including the human IMAGE3501788 construct, affinity tag, product-inhibition analysis and mutant kinetics. NADH is the coupled assay reporter; it is not a GALK1 cofactor.

Selected original Results, Figure 3 caption and Methods of [PMID:14596685](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC280648/) show robust in-vitro 2-deoxygalactose phosphorylation, with kinetic parameters within fourfold of those measured for galactose. No phosphorylation was detected for glucose, fucose, arabinose or N-acetylgalactosamine under the reported assay conditions. This supports substrate discrimination without claiming absolute galactose exclusivity or established physiological use of 2-deoxygalactose. The paper's introductory structural image is from bacterial *L. lactis*; the separately read human complex provides the direct human structural evidence. Km is not an equilibrium binding constant.

The single core describes the cytosolic galactokinase reaction and Leloir process. Broad kinase and galactose-metabolism refinements use verified typed GO paths. Carbohydrate phosphorylation remains a valid original claim: no current is_a/part_of path makes the Leloir term its descendant. The inherited `NEW` galactose-catabolism term is retired because it is an ancestor of the Leloir term already present in two original contexts. The complete historical object and both quotations remain in the archive; no original GOA context is removed.

## Sequence and assembly limits

Official UniProt JSON and direct FASTA establish one canonical P51570 sequence of 392 residues and no declared alternative products. The sequence equals the inherited record and the actual PAINT alignment. Historical HuRI identifier P51570-1 is retained literally as dataset nomenclature; it does not create a natural product declaration.

The [human structure paper](https://holdenlab.biochem.wisc.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/1467/2021/05/133Holden_ThodenJBC2005.pdf) ([PMID:15590630](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15590630/)) was read from the author-institution PDF, including selected expression, assembly and active-site Results. Official [1WUU](https://www.rcsb.org/structure/1WUU) has 399 residues, exactly the current sequence with AHHHHHH inserted after Met1. The bound nucleotide is Mg-AMPPNP. Crystal dimers contain Cys391 disulfides, but the authors leave physiological assembly unresolved; the earlier human kinetic study reports a monomer by gel filtration, with data not shown. The current UniProt homodimer statement is therefore treated cautiously. Deposited mutation counts, crystal packing and affinity tags are not natural isoforms or established physiological assembly states.

## Three distinct localization contexts

The current membrane IDA context from [PMID:8908517](https://genome.cshlp.org/content/6/10/980.full.pdf) is explicitly **NOT** located in membrane. The indexed publisher-original Results/Figure 3 caption and expression Methods show human GALK1 translated with canine microsomes remaining protease-accessible and in the supernatant after membrane pelleting at neutral and carbonate pH. This supports the retained negative assertion and soluble cytosolic setting in the tested system. The source does not exclude all conditional peripheral associations. Direct PDF download and screenshot access failed; an error body was not counted as an inspected PDF, and figure pixels were not independently interpreted.

The YTS membrane-proteomics annotation ([PMID:19946888](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19946888/)) is a separate positive context. Its authentic abstract includes transiently associated proteins, but the GALK1-specific table and peptide assignment remain unrecovered. It remains UNDECIDED, without alleging contamination or misattribution from the enzyme's cytosolic role.

The urinary-vesicle context has positive target evidence. [PMID:19056867](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2637050/) Table 1 names GALK1, and the authentic expanded NHLBI table identifies GALK1/NP_000145 with one reported peptide and reference 2. Selected isolation and identification Methods were read. This supports limited occurrence in the isolated fraction as non-core; it does not establish encapsulation, membrane topology or a vesicle function. No target peptide sequence was recovered. The original `part_of UBERON:0001088` urine atom is preserved literally and is restored on actual native row 25 as a flat BFO:0000050 extension. Imported GO columns in the table are not independent experimental evidence.

## Interaction, phylogeny and curated models

The authentic [HuRI dataset described in the original paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7169983/) contains three assay records for one GALK1–Q9NPJ4 pair, using human ORFs in yeast. The exact clone identifiers, source suffixes and pooled/array/validated two-hybrid methods remain in the archive. The paper's Methods and data-availability context were read ([PMID:32296183](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7169983/)). Removing uninformative generic protein binding does not deny this positive interaction or establish a different binding function.

The actual PTHR10457 tree places P51570 at AN489/PTN002470832. All three IBA assertions originate at AN0/PTN000048421; every donor identifier was resolved in the actual tree, and the only retained activity-loss node is off the human lineage. The canonical sequence equals the ungapped human MSA. Human self-donation is legitimate experimental grounding. Full book/MSA files remain in retained provenance rather than being duplicated here.

Official Reactome objects establish [normal reaction R-HSA-70355](https://reactome.org/content/detail/R-HSA-70355) → catalyst 70354 → physical entity 70353 → P51570, residues 1–392, in cytosol. [Pathway R-HSA-70370](https://reactome.org/content/detail/R-HSA-70370) includes that reaction. The original [R-HSA-5610026](https://reactome.org/content/detail/R-HSA-5610026) is a FailedReaction of a mutant set with no output and an explicit normalReaction link. That model is not a positive wild-type turnover experiment; normal activity is independently supported by human primary studies. No model or normal cache is rewritten.
