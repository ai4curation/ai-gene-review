Gene Description

ASTN1 (astrotactin 1) is a large neuronal membrane protein with two transmembrane helices and an extracellular region carrying three EGF-like repeats and a fibronectin type III domain. It is expressed in postmitotic neuronal precursors of laminated brain regions and acts as a neuronal ligand for adhesion to radial and Bergmann glial fibres during glial-guided migration. Its paralog ASTN2 forms a complex with it and controls its surface level. ASTN1 is endocytosed and recycled to the front of the leading process, which may coordinate making and releasing glial contacts as the neuron moves. Astn1-null mice have slowed granule cell migration, small cerebella and poor motor coordination. Bi-allelic ASTN1 variants in humans cause neurodevelopmental disorders with variable cortical and cerebellar malformations.

Core Functions

Neuronal surface adhesion ligand that binds glial fibres during glial-guided migration of postmitotic neurons; its surface level is set by ASTN2 and endocytic recycling.

OPEN BIOLOGY MF_DARK

What is known: Established: ASTN1 confers neuron-glia binding and is needed for normal migration rates (mouse antibody, transfection and knockout studies). It is a surface membrane protein whose level is controlled by ASTN2 and endocytosis. Not established: the glial binding partner, which ASTN1 domain contacts it, and whether ASTN1 also signals.

Current model (mechanistic narrative)

ASTN1 (astrotactin 1) is a neuronal cell-surface protein that mediates glial-guided neuronal migration in laminar brain structures, accumulating in the forward aspect of the leading process of migrating cerebellar granule cells where new neuron-glial adhesion sites form PMID:20573900. Its surface levels are governed by a complex with the paralog ASTN2, which controls how much ASTN1 reaches the plasma membrane during migration PMID:20573900, and the cyclical release of adhesions to the glial fiber depends on dynamin-dependent endocytosis of the receptor, since dynamin inhibition rapidly and reversibly arrests migration PMID:20573900. Bi-allelic loss-of-function variants in ASTN1 cause human neurodevelopmental disorders with defects in radial-glia-guided neuronal migration, and ASTN1 genetically interacts with ASTN2 in this pathway PMID:41544630. Beyond its neuronal role, ASTN1 overexpression suppresses migration and invasion of liver cancer cells by inhibiting Wnt/β-catenin signaling, downregulating β-catenin and downstream targets in a manner reversed by the Wnt inhibitor XAV939 PMID:32945491. ASTN1 expression is itself subject to post-transcriptional control by miR-sc3, which directly targets Astn1 mRNA for translational suppression PMID:26786955.
