## 2026-10-28 — biological follow-up to PR5708 review

FLNA binds and crosslinks F-actin into networks through its dimeric structure. Human recombinant and cellular evidence anchors that activity, with reagent and species limits preserved ([PMID:25358863](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25358863/), [PMID:10051605](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10051605/)). A second core now distinguishes coupling of those networks to integrin cytoplasmic tails. Reconstituted F-actin–FLNA–beta7-tail experiments directly measure strain-dependent association, and human FLNA-repeat experiments establish the ligand-binding interface ([PMID:21926999](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21926999/), [PMID:17690686](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17690686/)). This adaptor role is bounded to the demonstrated connection, without assigning all partner functions to FLNA.

The two questioned process decisions remain contextual. In the rat mesangial system, FLNA recruits Smad factors into a nuclear promoter-associated complex and supports laminin-gene induction; the scaffold/shuttle is a positive contribution to transcription ([PMID:20167606](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20167606/)). In injured mouse sensory axons, disrupting the FLNA–HDAC5 interaction reduces local HDAC5 and tubulin deacetylation. Human M2/A7 experiments distinguish this FLNA-dependent response from HDAC5 nuclear export ([PMID:26157139](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4566247/)). The former does not establish direct FLNA-DNA binding or a specific Pol II catalytic step, and the latter assigns the chemistry to HDAC5. Species, construct and natural-product limits remain explicit in the [evidence synthesis](FLNA-source-evidence.md).
