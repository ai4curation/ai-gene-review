# Human LEPR: functional annotation and research report

**Identity verified.** The target is **human LEPR**, the gene encoding the **leptin receptor** (UniProt [P48357](https://www.uniprot.org/uniprotkb/P48357/entry); also OB-R and CD295), **not LEP**, which encodes its leptin ligand. Human LEPR lies at chromosome 1p31.3. Receptor clones isolated from human CD34-positive cells establish its identity as a cytokine receptor and demonstrate human splice variants. Its class-I cytokine-receptor architecture agrees with the supplied UniProt annotations: an extracellular cytokine-receptor region with a WSXWS-related motif, immunoglobulin-like and fibronectin-type-III domains, one membrane-spanning segment in membrane isoforms, and an intracellular signaling tail in the long isoform. The database’s “type 2” designation should not be confused with the separate *class II* cytokine-receptor family. (collet2024exploringthetherapeutic pages 1-2, bennett1996arolefor pages 1-2, saxton2023structuralinsightsinto pages 1-2, gorska2010leptinreceptors pages 1-2)

## Primary molecular function

LEPR is a **cell-surface receptor for the adipocyte-secreted hormone leptin**, rather than an enzyme or a general-purpose nutrient transporter. Its principal function is to convert extracellular information about available energy stores into intracellular signaling. The long isoform, **LEPRb/Ob-Rb**, is the major signaling-competent form. LEPR has no intrinsic kinase activity: leptin-induced receptor assembly and conformational change activate the cytoplasmic kinase **JAK2**, which associates with the receptor’s membrane-proximal Box1/Box2 region. Short-tailed isoforms retain leptin binding but do not reproduce LEPRb’s full JAK–STAT response. (bodur2025molecularmechanismsand pages 1-2, bodur2025molecularmechanismsand pages 2-3)

**How leptin engages the receptor.** In a March 2023 cryo-electron-microscopy study, leptin bound the receptor’s second cytokine-homology region at a higher-affinity interface and its immunoglobulin-like region at a second, lower-affinity interface. The resulting assembly is *asymmetric*: one leptin bridges two receptor chains; the observed, partially open complex can also contain a second leptin bound to one chain. Thus, “one leptin bridges two receptors” describes the activating interface, not necessarily the full complex’s total stoichiometry. Crucially, the purified structural complex used **mouse** proteins; the authors separately tested signaling with **human leptin and full-length human LEPR in cultured cells**. The focused ligand-binding map reached **3.8 Å** resolution. Structure-guided leptin variants produced partial and pathway-biased responses in experimental systems, but are not established LEPR-directed treatments. [Saxton *et al.*, *Nature Communications*, March 2023](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-023-37169-6). (saxton2023structuralinsightsinto pages 1-2, saxton2023structuralinsightsinto pages 2-3, saxton2023structuralinsightsinto pages 8-9, saxton2023structuralinsightsinto pages 5-7)

**Intracellular pathway.** Activated JAK2 phosphorylates three especially informative LEPRb tail residues:

- **Tyr1138 → STAT3:** recruited STAT3 is phosphorylated, dimerizes and regulates nuclear transcription. This is the principal STAT-dependent route for leptin action in mouse genetic experiments; downstream programs include regulation of hypothalamic appetite-related genes and induction of **SOCS3**. (bodur2025molecularmechanismsand pages 2-3, fruhbeck2006intracellularsignallingpathways pages 3-4)
- **Tyr985 → SHP2/PTPN11–GRB2–ERK1/2**, or recruitment of **SOCS3**. SOCS3 suppresses LEPRb–JAK2 signaling, creating feedback as leptin exposure increases. Mouse mutational evidence suggests feedback inhibition is a particularly important physiological role of this site; an ERK signal should not be equated with the entire leptin response. (bodur2025molecularmechanismsand pages 2-3)
- **Tyr1077 → STAT5:** biochemically demonstrable, but mouse receptor-mutant and cell-specific genetic experiments find it substantially less consequential for energy balance than Tyr1138–STAT3. Additional SH2B1/IRS–PI3K–AKT signaling is implicated, although the precise contributions of receptor-tail pathways vary by neuronal population. These detailed physiological assignments are predominantly **mouse-derived**, not measurements of pathway necessity in human hypothalamic neurons. (bodur2025molecularmechanismsand pages 2-3, zhou2013leptinsignalingand pages 4-6)

## Where LEPR acts

The **plasma membrane** is the principal site of leptin recognition and initiation of LEPRb signaling. Functionally important LEPRb-expressing populations include hypothalamic neurons participating in the arcuate and connected feeding circuits. Leptin signaling can favor POMC–melanocortin activity and restrain orexigenic AgRP/NPY activity; however, neither neuronal population alone accounts for all receptor-dependent control of feeding. Human receptor expression has also been demonstrated in CD34-positive hematopoietic progenitors, although its best-established organismal role concerns central energy sensing. (bodur2025molecularmechanismsand pages 1-2, zhou2013leptinsignalingand pages 4-6, bennett1996arolefor pages 1-2)

Isoform and trafficking distinctions are essential to interpreting any localization annotation:

|---|---|---|---|
| **LEPRb (Ob-Rb; long signaling isoform)** | Plasma membrane; especially important in specialized hypothalamic neurons | Long cytoplasmic tail recruits JAK2 through Box1/Box2. Activated JAK2 phosphorylates Tyr985 (SOCS3 negative feedback and SHP2–GRB2–ERK), Tyr1077 (STAT5), and Tyr1138 (principally STAT3). Tyr1138–STAT3 is the dominant STAT-dependent pathway in mouse physiology; STAT3 also induces *SOCS3*, completing feedback inhibition. (bodur2025molecularmechanismsand pages 2-3) | Established receptor-signaling mechanism, supported strongly by biochemical and mouse genetic evidence; human biallelic loss-of-function genetics validates its physiological importance. |
| **LEPRa and other short membrane isoforms** | Plasma membrane and endosomal trafficking compartments; LEPRa is also expressed in choroid-plexus epithelium | Share the leptin-binding ectodomain but have short cytoplasmic tails containing Box1 without the complete LEPRb signaling apparatus; they can weakly activate JAK2 in overexpression systems but are **not equivalent to fully signaling-competent LEPRb**. The ER is therefore a biogenesis/quality-control compartment, **not the normal site of leptin signal transduction**. The receptor is synthesized and quality-controlled in the **endoplasmic reticulum (ER)**, but ER retention is not its normal site of extracellular-leptin sensing. A September 2024 peer-reviewed study found that disrupting **SEL1L–HRD1 ER-associated degradation** reduced receptor maturation/surface delivery and leptin responses in experimental systems; POMC-neuron deletion studies were in **mice**, while experiments also examined the obesity-associated **human LEPR C604S** variant. This establishes a plausible, experimentally tested link between protein folding, membrane availability and signaling without showing that the same ER defect causes common human obesity. [Mao *et al.*, *Nature Communications*, September 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-024-52743-2). (mao2024regulationofleptin pages 1-2, mao2024theroleof pages 91-95)

A further proposed role for **short LEPRa** is participation with LRP1 in leptin uptake at the choroid plexus. A July 2023 **bioRxiv preprint** reports interaction and uptake experiments using mouse models and cultured cells; whether this mechanism makes a quantitatively important contribution to **human** brain leptin entry remains unproven. The circulating **soluble receptor**, produced by a secreted isoform and receptor-ectodomain shedding, binds leptin outside cells but cannot initiate signaling through an intracellular tail. [Uner *et al.*, bioRxiv, July 2023](https://doi.org/10.1101/2023.07.03.547520). (uner2023lrp1mediatesleptin pages 5-8, uner2023lrp1mediatesleptin pages 1-5, bodur2025molecularmechanismsand pages 2-3)

## Human evidence and clinical implementation

**Loss of receptor function is direct human evidence for the pathway.** In a deliberately enriched cohort of **300 people with hyperphagia and severe early-onset obesity**, sequencing found pathogenic LEPR variants in **8 (about 3%)**: seven homozygotes and one compound heterozygote. Tested missense variants impaired receptor signaling. Affected individuals showed severe hyperphagia and obesity, delayed puberty associated with hypogonadotropic hypogonadism, and measurable immune abnormalities. Importantly, circulating leptin was generally **appropriate for the increased fat mass**, so a very high leptin concentration relative to adiposity is **not** required to diagnose LEPR deficiency. The 3% figure applies to this selected cohort, **not the general population**. [Farooqi *et al.*, *New England Journal of Medicine*, January 2007](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa063988). (farooqi2007clinicalandmolecular pages 1-2, farooqi2007clinicalandmolecular pages 5-6)

**Precision therapy bypasses, rather than repairs, LEPR.** Because receptor-deficient patients cannot reliably respond to leptin replacement, treatment of genetically confirmed **biallelic LEPR deficiency** uses the downstream **MC4R agonist setmelanotide**; this differs from replacing the missing hormone with metreleptin in **LEP deficiency**. In the LEPR phase-3 study, **5 of 11 participants (45%)** in the reported one-year analysis achieved **at least 10% body-weight loss**; the reported mean change in maximum-hunger score was **−43.7%**. These encouraging results come from a small study, with an open-label treatment period and a hunger measure whose limitations the 2024 review notes. The [ClinicalTrials.gov record NCT03287960](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT03287960) lists **15 enrolled overall**; that enrollment total should **not** be substituted for the **11-person** published weight-loss denominator. [Qamar *et al.*, *touchREVIEWS in Endocrinology*, October 2024](https://doi.org/10.17925/EE.2024.20.2.9); [Collet and Schwitzgebel, *Frontiers in Nutrition*, December 2024](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnut.2024.1509994). (qamar2024setmelanotideamelanocortin4 pages 6-7, NCT03287960 chunk 1, collet2024exploringthetherapeutic pages 1-2)

**Overall assessment.** Human cloning, functional analysis of patient variants and the clinical consequences of biallelic loss establish LEPR as a leptin-responsive **signaling receptor** essential to the human leptin–melanocortin axis. The strongest molecular model is ligand-dependent LEPRb activation of JAK2–STAT3 with SOCS3 feedback at the **cell surface**. Precise receptor geometry, contributions of individual tail pathways, ER quality control and short-isoform transport are informed substantially by structural, cultured-cell and animal experiments; these should not be presented as equally established mechanisms in living humans. (bennett1996arolefor pages 1-2, farooqi2007clinicalandmolecular pages 1-2, saxton2023structuralinsightsinto pages 2-3, bodur2025molecularmechanismsand pages 2-3, mao2024regulationofleptin pages 1-2, uner2023lrp1mediatesleptin pages 5-8)
