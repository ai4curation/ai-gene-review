# BLNK review notes

## 2026-10-01 — manual evidence review and temporary proposal

BLNK is a noncatalytic B-cell signaling adaptor. Its tyrosine-phosphorylated docking sites recruit effector SH2 domains, while proline-rich motifs recruit SH3-domain proteins such as CIN85 and CD2AP. These interactions assemble rather than catalyze the receptor-proximal reactions. SYK phosphorylates BLNK, BTK can phosphorylate PLCgamma2, and PLCgamma hydrolyzes membrane phospholipid. BLNK itself performs the structural coordination that makes it a participant in BCR signal transmission, rather than merely a substrate consumed by another protein's chemistry (PMID:9697839; PMID:11606584; PMID:21822214).

The two proposed core functions describe signaling adaptor activity and molecular condensate scaffold activity. They are distinct, already represented molecular functions, not a new disease-derived process collection. Both contribute to the existing BCR pathway. Lipid binding and multivalent CIN85 binding support spatial assembly of signaling granules; human recombinant SLP65 fragments, a defined CIN85 deletion construct and synthetic vesicles were used in reconstitution. Human SLP65 complementation in chicken DT40 cells links the relevant interaction motifs to granules and calcium responses. Endogenous concentration measurements in human B cells are a separate observation. The source expressly identifies in-situ vesicle organization within cellular granules as a remaining question (PMID:32051419; PMID:25140054). No NEW annotation is proposed, and no gene-specific GO-CAM index entry was found by the exact BLNK/Q8WV28 search.

## 2026-10-01 — SCIMP interface follow-up

This entry supersedes the earlier reason for leaving the SCIMP interaction generic. BLNK recognizes a phosphorylated residue on SCIMP through its own SH2 domain; cooperative GRB2 recruitment does not negate that mapped interface. The existing IPI annotation is refined to [phosphotyrosine residue binding, GO:0001784](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/QuickGO/term/GO:0001784). The reciprocal SH2-domain-binding term describes a different binding direction.
