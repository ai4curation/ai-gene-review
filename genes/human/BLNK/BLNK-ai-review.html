Gene Description

BLNK (SLP-65/BASH) is a noncatalytic adaptor that organizes signaling downstream of the B-cell antigen receptor. Its phosphorylated tyrosines and proline-rich motifs recruit effectors including phospholipase Cgamma, VAV, GRB2 and NCK, coupling SYK-dependent receptor-proximal events to calcium mobilization and downstream signaling. Constitutive association with CIN85 and binding to lipid vesicles help assemble cytoplasmic signaling granules that can be recruited to activated receptor sites at the plasma membrane. BLNK supplies docking and spatial organization, while associated kinases and phospholipases perform the enzymatic reactions. Disruption of human BLNK can arrest the transition from pro-B to pre-B cells and cause antibody deficiency.

Core Functions

BLNK provides noncatalytic docking sites that assemble B-cell receptor signaling effectors. SYK-dependent phosphorylation enables recruitment of SH2-containing proteins, while proline-rich motifs bind SH3-domain adaptors. The resulting complexes couple receptor activation to PLCgamma-dependent calcium signaling and other downstream responses. BLNK supplies the organizing scaffold; SYK, BTK and PLCgamma supply their respective catalytic reactions.

BLNK is a noncatalytic B-cell signaling adaptor. Its tyrosine-phosphorylated docking sites recruit effector SH2 domains, while proline-rich motifs recruit SH3-domain proteins such as CIN85 and CD2AP. These interactions assemble rather than catalyze the receptor-proximal reactions. SYK phosphorylates BLNK, BTK can phosphorylate PLCgamma2, and PLCgamma hydrolyzes membrane phospholipid. BLNK itself performs the structural coordination that makes it a participant in BCR signal transmission, rather than merely a substrate consumed by another protein's chemistry (PMID:9697839; PMID:11606584; PMID:21822214).

The two proposed core functions describe signaling adaptor activity and molecular condensate scaffold activity.
