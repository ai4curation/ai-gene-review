# YTHDF1 manual evidence notes

Falcon and Perplexity-lite were unavailable for this review, so the review uses the reviewed UniProt record, the cached GOA rows, cached primary publications, the YTHDF3 review, and manual synthesis.

## Core m6A-reader role

The high-throughput mRNA-interactome rows from PMID:22658674 and PMID:22681889 support only generic RNA binding and are less informative than the YTH-domain m6A-reader evidence.

PMID:24284625 identified YTHDF1 alongside the other cytoplasmic YTHDF paralogs: In addition to previously reported YTHDF2 and YTHDF3, we also discovered YTHDF1 as another m6A-selective binding protein by using methylated RNA bait containing the known consensus sites of G(m6A)C and A(m6A)C versus unmethylated control.

PMID:26046440 reported the original YTHDF1 translation model: Looking at additional functional roles for the modification, we find that another m(6)A reader protein, human YTHDF1, actively promotes protein synthesis by interacting with translation machinery.

PMID:32492408 directly challenges the interpretation that DF paralogs select different transcripts or induce translation as their main action: In contrast to the prevailing model, we show that DF proteins bind the same m6A-modified mRNAs rather than different mRNAs.

PMID:32492408 states the shared decay model: Instead, the DF paralogs act redundantly to mediate mRNA degradation and cellular differentiation.

YTHDF1 is a cytoplasmic YTHDF paralog that binds m6A-modified mRNAs, redundantly promotes mRNA degradation with YTHDF2 and YTHDF3, and partitions with polymethylated RNAs into P-bodies and cytoplasmic stress granules.

## Condensate and granule biology

PMID:31292544 connects the low-complexity YTHDF regions and polymethylated RNAs to endogenous ribonucleoprotein granules: The resulting mRNA-YTHDF complexes then partition into different endogenous phase-separated compartments, such as P-bodies, stress granules or neuronal RNA granules.

PMID:32451507 makes the stress-granule role direct for YTHDF1 and YTHDF3: Here, we show that m6A-modified mRNAs are enriched in SGs, and that m6A-binding YTHDF proteins are critical for SG formation.

## Propagated mouse outputs

The mouse-derived YTHDF1 rows for regulation of antigen processing and presentation, learning, memory, regulation of long-term synaptic potentiation, and regulation of axon guidance are conserved but non-core outputs of the m6A reader activity rather than separate molecular functions.

## Protein binding policy

The GO:0005515 IPI rows are curated physical associations and are removed under the project bare-protein-binding policy, not because the reported interactions are false.
