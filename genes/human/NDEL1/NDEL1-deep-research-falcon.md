# Human NDEL1 (Q9GZM8): Functional-Annotation Research Report

## Executive conclusion

Human **NDEL1** encodes nuclear distribution protein nudE-like 1—also called **NUDEL**, protein Nudel, EOPA, or MITAP1. The specified identity is correct: **NDEL1, UniProt Q9GZM8, Homo sapiens**, a NudE-family protein containing the NUDE/NUDE_C architecture. It is distinct from the related paralog **NDE1**; the two human proteins are about 56% identical and 69% similar and have overlapping but nonidentical expression, interactions, and functions. Findings about NDE1 therefore should not automatically be assigned to NDEL1. Open Targets independently identifies human NDEL1 as ENSG00000166579, approved name “nudE neurodevelopment protein 1 like 1.” (OpenTargets Search: -NDEL1, garrott2022nde1andndel1 pages 2-4)

The best-supported primary function of NDEL1 is **not catalysis or transport of a substrate**. It is a nonmotor, intracellular **scaffold and spatial regulator of cytoplasmic dynein-1 and LIS1**. NDEL1 helps recruit or position LIS1 and dynein, regulates assembly/activation of dynein–dynactin–adaptor complexes, and couples dynein force production to centrosomes, kinetochores, spindle poles, neuronal nuclei, axons, and selected cargo. A reported serine-oligopeptidase activity remains a less-established secondary function because physiologically validated endogenous substrates and its relationship to the dynein pathway remain uncertain. (garrott2022nde1andndel1 pages 6-7, okada2023conservedrolesfor pages 6-7)

## 1. Identity, family, and structural organization

### Mandatory identity verification

The literature consistently uses **NDEL1**, **Ndel1**, or the older name **NUDEL** for the protein matching human Q9GZM8. It belongs to the conserved NudE family and is a paralog—not a synonym—of NDE1. NDEL1 expression is comparatively sustained through life and is prominent in the nervous system, whereas NDE1 is more strongly associated with early proliferative stages. NDEL1 knockout is embryonically lethal in mice, while NDE1-null mice can survive, supporting overlapping but biologically nonredundant functions. (wynshawboris2001lis1anddynein pages 7-8, garrott2022nde1andndel1 pages 2-4)

This matches the supplied InterPro/Pfam annotations—**NUDE (IPR033494), NUDE_dom (IPR006964), and NUDE_C (PF04880)**—and the experimentally established elongated coiled-coil architecture. No evidence was found that the requested symbol referred to a different organism or protein.

### Domain-level functional map

NDEL1 contains an N-terminal NUDE/coiled-coil scaffold followed by a more flexible, phosphorylation-rich C-terminal region. The protein can form homodimers and heterodimers with NDE1, although their relative abundance in vivo is uncertain. The following experimentally mapped sites are especially informative:

- Approximately **residues 85–169** form a principal LIS1-binding region. NDEL1 substitutions **E116A** and **R127A** abolish LIS1 association; the interaction has high-nanomolar affinity.
- The N-terminal approximately 200 residues bind the N-terminal segment of dynein intermediate chain, with **E48A** disrupting this interaction; measured affinity is in the low-micromolar range.
- The flexible C-terminal region makes additional dynein contacts and contains regulatory phosphorylation sites.
- **Arg105**, altered in the 2024 cortical-malformation cases, lies in the coiled-coil region involved in self-association and LIS1 binding.
- **Ser285** is an Aurora-regulated residue controlling mitotic dynein behavior near spindle poles. (garrott2022nde1andndel1 pages 6-7, tsai2024novellissencephalyassociatedndel1 pages 4-5, janczyk2023auroraaphosphorylates pages 1-2)

## 2. Primary molecular function: regulation of dynein and LIS1

Cytoplasmic dynein-1 is the principal microtubule-minus-end motor in animal cells. Processive movement generally requires dynein, dynactin, and an activating cargo adaptor. LIS1 facilitates assembly of this activated complex. NDEL1 acts upstream and spatially within this pathway rather than functioning as the motor itself.

### Current handoff model

The strongest recent mechanistic update is the September 2023 Nature Communications study by Okada and colleagues ([DOI 10.1038/s41467-023-41466-5](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-023-41466-5)). Purified-protein, mutational, evolutionary, and structural-modeling results support the following sequence:

1. NDEL1 binds the N terminus of dynein intermediate chain.
2. It recruits or concentrates LIS1 near dynein.
3. NDEL1 and the dynein motor domain contact overlapping surfaces on LIS1.
4. LIS1 therefore must be transferred—or “handed off”—from NDEL1 to the motor in temporally distinct steps.
5. Dynactin and an activating adaptor then stabilize the active transport complex.

This replaces an overly simple model in which NDEL1, LIS1, and the dynein motor remain together as one stable tripartite complex. Wild-type LIS1/Pac1 formed 1:1 dimeric complexes with NDEL1/Ndl1 in the biochemical system, and mutations in the LIS1/Pac1 motor-binding surface disrupted the competition relationship. (okada2023conservedrolesfor pages 6-7)

An independent 2023 preprint reported that purified NDEL1 can inhibit dynein–dynactin–adaptor assembly and sequester LIS1, with C-terminal phosphomimetic substitutions strengthening inhibition. Its proposed reconciliation is that NDEL1 locally scaffolds dynein and LIS1 in a primed but inactive state and must then be released for synchronized activation. Because this study was a preprint and purified systems omit many cellular regulators, this inhibitory model should be treated as provisional rather than as evidence that NDEL1 is globally a negative regulator in cells. (garrott2023ndel1modulatesdynein pages 11-12)

### Relation to earlier motility results

Earlier microtubule-gliding experiments found that LIS1 suppressed dynein-mediated microtubule translocation, NDEL1’s C terminus promoted dynein dissociation from microtubules, and its N-terminal LIS1-binding region restored movement after LIS1 inhibition. These direct observations established region-specific effects but predated current understanding of activated dynein–dynactin–adaptor assembly. They are best interpreted as biochemical states sampled by NDEL1 during recruitment, inhibition, handoff, and release—not as one universal effect on motility. (torisawa2011functionaldissectionof pages 1-1)

Thus, authoritative expert reviews describe NDEL1’s precise molecular effect as context-dependent and still incompletely resolved. Its established role is to control **where and when** LIS1 and dynein interact; whether a particular assay observes activation, inhibition, microtubule release, or cargo recruitment depends on complex composition and phosphorylation state. (garrott2022nde1andndel1 pages 6-7, garrott2022nde1andndel1 pages 2-4)

## 3. Cellular localization and site-specific functions

NDEL1 is an intracellular, cytoplasmic/cytoskeletal protein rather than a secreted or membrane transporter. Its localization is dynamic and interaction-dependent.

### Centrosomes and microtubule-organizing centers

NDEL1 colocalizes with LIS1 and dynein at centrosomes/MTOCs. In developing neurons it links microtubule-based dynein force to nucleus–centrosome coupling. Depletion of Ndel1, LIS1, or dynein in developing neocortex disrupts coupling between the centrosome and nucleus, impairs nuclear translocation, and causes abnormal neuronal positioning. LIS1 overexpression partially rescued Ndel1 depletion, whereas the reverse rescue did not occur, placing NDEL1 upstream of or alongside LIS1-mediated dynein regulation. (wynshawboris2001lis1anddynein pages 7-8, shu2004ndel1operatesin pages 1-2)

This mechanical process—**nucleokinesis**—is more precise than the broad annotation “neuronal migration.” The centrosome advances into the leading process; dynein-associated forces then move the nucleus toward it. NDEL1 organizes the LIS1–dynein machinery needed to maintain this mechanical coupling.

### Kinetochores, spindle poles, and the mitotic checkpoint

NDEL1/NDE1–LIS1–dynein complexes are recruited to kinetochores through proteins including CENP-F. Dynein removes spindle-assembly-checkpoint components from attached kinetochores and transports them toward spindle poles, contributing to checkpoint silencing and spindle organization. NDE1 may make a larger contribution to kinetochore dynein recruitment than NDEL1 in some cultured-cell systems, emphasizing that the paralogs are not interchangeable. (garrott2022nde1andndel1 pages 6-7)

A January 2023 Molecular Biology of the Cell study ([DOI 10.1091/mbc.e21-09-0438](https://doi.org/10.1091/mbc.e21-09-0438)) established a spatial phosphorylation switch. Aurora A at spindle poles phosphorylates **NDEL1 Ser285**. Aurora-A inhibition with 200 nM MLN8054 increased pole-associated Mad1, while replacement of endogenous NDEL1 with nonphosphorylatable S285A increased both Mad1 and NuMA accumulation at poles and weakened arrest in 20 nM nocodazole. The data support local down-regulation of NDEL1/dynein as complexes approach Aurora-A-rich poles, although whether phosphorylation releases cargo, releases NDEL1, or lowers dynein–microtubule engagement is unresolved. Experiments used human HeLa T-REx cells, Xenopus cells, recombinant kinase assays, and Xenopus egg-extract asters. (janczyk2023auroraaphosphorylates pages 1-2, janczyk2023auroraaphosphorylates pages 5-7, janczyk2023auroraaphosphorylates pages 4-5)

### Neuronal axons and cargo selection

As neurons mature, NDEL1 and LIS1 occur in neuronal soma, growth-associated regions, and axons. NDEL1 is enriched at the axon initial segment, where it works with LIS1 and dynein to maintain polarized cargo sorting. In mature rodent sensory axons, NDEL1/LIS1 perturbation reduces retrograde mitochondrial trafficking and changes mitochondrial length. Acidic organelles and mitochondria show different NDEL1 requirements, indicating cargo-specific regulation rather than a universal requirement for every dynein cargo. (wynshawboris2001lis1anddynein pages 7-8, garrott2022nde1andndel1 pages 14-15)

NDEL1 also contributes to Golgi and lipid-droplet positioning. Individual depletion of NDEL1 or NDE1 causes relatively mild Golgi dispersal, whereas combined depletion causes a stronger phenotype, demonstrating partial redundancy in non-neuronal dynein functions. (garrott2022nde1andndel1 pages 6-7)

## 4. Biological pathways

The most defensible pathway-level annotation is:

**NDEL1 → recruitment/positioning of LIS1 and dynein → LIS1 handoff to the dynein motor → dynein–dynactin–activating-adaptor assembly → minus-end-directed force or transport.**

This module is deployed in several defined settings:

1. **Neuronal migration and nucleokinesis:** force transmission between centrosome, microtubules, and nucleus.
2. **Mitotic spindle organization:** pole focusing, centrosome behavior, and chromosome-associated dynein function.
3. **Spindle-assembly-checkpoint transport:** removal of Mad1-containing checkpoint complexes from kinetochores, with Aurora-A/Ser285 regulation near poles.
4. **Axonal transport and polarity:** retrograde transport and selective entry or exclusion of cargo at the axon initial segment.
5. **Organelle positioning:** Golgi, lipid droplets, mitochondria, and selected endomembrane cargo.

NDEL1 is also regulated by kinases including Cdk5 and Aurora A. These phosphorylation events provide plausible switches coupling neural differentiation or cell-cycle state to dynein activity. (wynshawboris2001lis1anddynein pages 7-8, janczyk2023auroraaphosphorylates pages 1-2)

## 5. Enzymatic activity: evidence and caveats

NDEL1 has been reported to possess **serine-oligopeptidase activity** against unstructured peptides, and DISC1 can inhibit that activity. However, the retrieved authoritative review emphasizes that its physiological relevance to dynein regulation remains unresolved. A defined, validated endogenous substrate spectrum and an in-vivo catalytic mechanism comparable to established metabolic peptidases have not been demonstrated. (garrott2022nde1andndel1 pages 6-7)

Accordingly:

- **Primary annotation:** nonenzymatic dynein/LIS1 scaffold-regulator.
- **Secondary/provisional annotation:** serine oligopeptidase acting on unstructured peptides.
- **Substrate specificity:** insufficiently defined for a confident physiological annotation.

Clinical studies measuring “NDEL1 activity” should therefore be understood as biomarker-assay studies, not proof that peptide cleavage is the protein’s principal cellular function.

## 6. Human disease evidence and 2023–2024 developments

### First compelling NDEL1 cortical-malformation variant

The most important 2024 advance was the January Acta Neuropathologica report by Tsai and colleagues ([DOI 10.1007/s00401-023-02665-y](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00401-023-02665-y)). Two unrelated patients with pachygyria, one with associated subcortical band heterotopia, carried the same de novo postzygotic **NDEL1 c.314G>C, p.Arg105Pro** variant. Mosaic fractions were approximately **25–30%** and **15–20%**, and the variant had a CADD score of 32. Both patients had developmental impairment and drug-resistant epilepsy. (tsai2024novellissencephalyassociatedndel1 pages 1-2, tsai2024novellissencephalyassociatedndel1 pages 4-5)

Mechanistically, p.Arg105Pro disrupted NDEL1–LIS1 binding. Ndel1 knockdown in the developing cortex impaired migration and was not rescued by the mutant; mutant expression itself lengthened the neuronal leading process and disrupted nucleus–centrosome coupling. Single-cell and spatial analyses showed NDE1 enriched in cycling ventricular-zone radial glia, while NDEL1 was more prominent in postmitotic neurons of the intermediate zone and cortical plate. This explains why NDE1 loss is strongly associated with progenitor/microcephaly phenotypes, whereas NDEL1 dysfunction can preferentially impair postmitotic migration and cortical lamination. (tsai2024novellissencephalyassociatedndel1 pages 1-2)

The result is mechanistically strong but clinically early: only two mosaic cases were reported, so prevalence, penetrance, and the range of germline versus mosaic NDEL1 phenotypes remain unknown.

### Autism cortical-organoid study

A May 2024 Molecular Autism study ([DOI 10.1186/s13229-024-00602-8](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13229-024-00602-8)) analyzed 4,910 cortical organoids derived from 10 toddlers with autism and 6 controls. ASD organoids were 39–41% larger in two production batches and grew nearly three times faster. NDEL1 activity correlated inversely with organoid growth rate (Spearman ρ = −0.757, p = 0.014) and size (Pearson r = −0.697, p = 0.025); expression correlated inversely with size (r = −0.932, p = 0.006). Larger organoids were associated with more severe social phenotypes. (courchesne2024embryonicoriginofa pages 12-13, courchesne2024embryonicoriginof pages 11-12)

These are provocative patient-derived model data, not evidence that NDEL1 deficiency causes autism generally. The subject-level sample was small, multiple developmental pathways can affect organoid size, and neither diagnostic sensitivity nor specificity was established.

### Schizophrenia pharmacodynamic study

A 2024 Brazilian Journal of Psychiatry study ([DOI 10.47626/1516-4446-2023-3315](https://doi.org/10.47626/1516-4446-2023-3315)) measured NDEL1 oligopeptidase activity after sodium-nitroprusside treatment. Fifteen patients participated in a double-blind randomized crossover experiment using 0.5 μg/kg/min for four hours weekly over four weeks. Activity was lower after sodium nitroprusside than placebo (t = 7.756, df = 97, p < 0.0001; Cohen’s d = 1.44), with parallel rodent observations.

This is preliminary pharmacodynamic-biomarker evidence. The cohort was extremely small; the result does not establish diagnostic validity, clinical utility, or NDEL1 as the causal drug target.

## 7. Current applications and translational status

There is presently no established NDEL1-directed therapy or routine clinical diagnostic test. Current applications are primarily research-oriented:

- **Genetic interpretation:** NDEL1 should be considered in mosaic sequencing of unexplained pachygyria, subcortical band heterotopia, epilepsy, and migration-pattern cortical malformations, especially when standard germline testing is negative. The p.Arg105Pro report supports deep sequencing across affected and accessible tissues.
- **Mechanistic disease modeling:** patient-derived organoids and in-utero electroporation allow direct testing of NDEL1-dependent proliferation, neuronal migration, and nucleus–centrosome mechanics.
- **Dynein-regulation assays:** mapped NDEL1 interfaces and phosphosites provide tools for dissecting LIS1 recruitment, handoff, and activation.
- **Exploratory biomarkers:** measured oligopeptidase activity has been studied in schizophrenia and autism models, but is not clinically validated.
- **Potential therapeutic logic:** rather than inhibiting NDEL1 globally—which would risk disrupting mitosis, neuronal transport, and organelle positioning—future strategies would likely need to target a specific interface, phosphorylation state, tissue, or disease-associated variant.

Open Targets lists NDEL1 associations with several phenotypic terms, but the displayed evidence consists primarily of ClinVar-derived records and moderate association scores rather than validated drug-target relationships. These annotations should not be interpreted as proof of therapeutic tractability. (OpenTargets Search: -NDEL1)

## 8. Evidence-weighted functional annotation

**Recommended concise annotation:**

> NDEL1 is a conserved intracellular coiled-coil scaffold and regulator of cytoplasmic dynein-1. It binds the dynein intermediate chain and LIS1, recruits and spatially organizes these proteins, and facilitates a regulated LIS1-to-dynein handoff preceding dynein–dynactin–adaptor activation. Through this mechanism, NDEL1 controls centrosome–nucleus coupling and neuronal nucleokinesis, mitotic spindle and checkpoint-protein transport, axonal polarity and retrograde cargo movement, and selected organelle-positioning events. Its activity is modulated by phosphorylation, including Aurora-A-dependent Ser285 phosphorylation at spindle poles. A reported serine-oligopeptidase activity has uncertain physiological substrates and should be treated as secondary/provisional.

## 9. Principal uncertainties

1. The exact cellular sequence and kinetics of NDEL1 recruitment, LIS1 handoff, NDEL1 release, and dynein activation remain incompletely resolved.
2. NDEL1 can appear activating or inhibitory in different purified systems; these outcomes likely reflect different biochemical states rather than a single constitutive effect.
3. Functional redundancy with NDE1 varies by tissue, developmental stage, and cargo.
4. The endogenous substrates and biological importance of reported oligopeptidase activity remain uncertain.
5. Human monogenic evidence is newly compelling but still based on two mosaic p.Arg105Pro cases.
6. Psychiatric and organoid biomarker findings are hypothesis-generating and not ready for clinical use.
