Gene Description

ARL13B is a membrane-associated regulatory GTPase enriched in primary cilia. It promotes guanine-nucleotide exchange on ARL3 and cooperates with ARL2BP/BART to regulate the active ARL3 pool involved in ciliary protein trafficking. ARL13B also binds tubulin through its GTPase domain and contributes to the organization and retention of the ciliary membrane relative to the axoneme. Nucleotide-dependent exocyst association and additional trafficking interactions connect it to ciliary protein organization. Its own GTP hydrolysis and its exchange-factor activity are distinct biochemical properties. ARL13B-dependent ciliary organization influences developmental signaling, and pathogenic variants cause Joubert syndrome.

ARL13B functional assessment

2026-09-28. Human ARL13B, UniProt Q3SXY8, HGNC:25419. All 176 original annotations and three alternative products are preserved. The review distinguishes GTP hydrolysis, guanine-nucleotide exchange on ARL3, and tubulin binding. It proposes the latter two molecular functions; it adds no biological-process annotation.

Molecular activities and their limits

PMID:26551564(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26551564/) establishes ARL13B as an ARL3 exchange factor. The initial yeast two-hybrid constructs are murine, and much of the structure and interface work uses Chlamydomonas proteins. The decisive human biochemical experiment instead uses human ARL13B residues 18–278 purified from insect cells and murine ARL3, with human ARL2 as a negative comparator. Human full-length ARL13B expression also increases active ARL3 in HEK293 and mouse IMCD3 cells. The paper states, “the human Arl13B also exhibits strong GEF activity for Arl3 (Figure 6A).” This supports GO:0005085 directly, without relabelling algal structures as human experiments. The proposed ciliary ARL3-GTP gradient is a mechanistic model, not a directly measured endogenous gradient in every tissue.

PMID:33438581(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33438581/) adds a distinct reaction context: human ARL13B and ARL2BP/BART cooperate in ARL3 nucleotide exchange, and BART also stabilizes active ARL3 until displacement by other effectors. The GDP-to-GDP control distinguishes exchange stimulation from merely binding the GTP-loaded product. The source says, “BART was able to accelerate the nucleotide exchange approximately 70-fold”. This does not establish an autonomous BART GEF, a stable BART–ARL13B complex, or uniform exchange efficiency under all nucleotide conditions. Those distinctions also prevent treating the 2015 and 2021 results as contradictory binary classifications of GEF activity.

PMID:28487361(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28487361/) examines purified full-length mouse and human ARL13B from mammalian expression cells. The abstract foregrounds murine protein; that does not mean every experiment is murine. CK2 phosphorylates the murine preparation but not the human protein, and CK2 can use GTP as a phosphate donor. The actual nucleotide-binding and GTP-hydrolysis controls therefore matter. Solution-based binding avoids confusing phosphate transfer with reversible nucleotide binding. The weak intrinsic hydrolysis signal may include contaminating nucleotidase activity, as the authors explicitly acknowledge. Bovine-brain extract stimulates ARL13B-dependent turnover with no-protein and GST controls, excess cold nucleotide, and reduced activity of K34A; the stimulating factor was not isolated. Retain GTPase activity with those limits rather than claiming a well-established high autonomous turnover rate.

The same 2017 study finds that T35N retains nucleotide binding and intrinsic/stimulated hydrolysis but loses ARL3 exchange-factor activity. G75Q is also not established as a constitutively active GTPase mutant. Thus the older labels “GDP-locked” or “GTPase-inactive” cannot be used as premises when interpreting later cellular rescues. The physiological hydrolysis-stimulating factor and the coupling of nucleotide state to individual effector functions remain unresolved.

PMID:29592971(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29592971/) supplies direct evidence for GO:0015631 tubulin binding. The original Results distinguish recombinant human GST-ARL13B binding to purified bovine tubulin from the initial mouse TAP bait in human RPE1 cells. GST-only controls and domain mapping support a G-domain interaction. The source states, “ARL13B can directly interact with tubulin dimers.” Human fragments were also tested with Chlamydomonas axonemes, while cellular rescue used mouse fibroblasts. This supports binding and a role in membrane–axoneme organization; it does not establish a motor or polymerase activity. Retained tubulin binding of T35N alone cannot prove independence from GTP hydrolysis, because the earlier 2017 biochemical measurements show preserved hydrolysis. No conflicting experimental observation is discarded to preserve the mutant label.

The tubulin study separately measures ciliary length and the distributions of SMO and SSTR3 in Arl13b-null mouse cells. Wild-type human or mouse ARL13B restores ciliary length. G-domain deletion disrupts membrane-protein distributions, and T35N separates some distribution phenotypes from complete ciliary-length rescue. These findings corroborate existing ciliary assembly and receptor-localization annotations, with mouse experimental provenance explicit. The physical mechanism of membrane–axoneme anchoring remains a supported model rather than a solved molecular bridge.
