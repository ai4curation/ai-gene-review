CC   -!- DOMAIN: Sequence consists of two domains: the C-terminal glutamine
CC       amidotransferase type-1 domain catalyzes the hydrolysis of glutamine;
CC       the N-terminal synthase domain catalyzes the amination of UTP.
CC       Structure shows each subunit consists of three distinct segments: the
CC       N-terminal amidoligase (ALase) domain, which mediates oligomerization
CC       and contains the ALase active site where ATP and UTP substrates bind,
CC       and an interrupted helical interdomain linker segment that connects the
CC       ALase domain to the Type I glutamine amidotransferase (GATase) C-
CC       terminal domain, which generates ammonia via glutamine hydrolysis. A
CC       gated channel that spans 25 Angstroms between the glutamine hydrolysis
CC       and amidoligase active sites provides a path for ammonia diffusion; the
CC       channel is accessible to solvent at the base of a cleft adjoining the
CC       glutamine hydrolysis active site, providing an entry point for
CC       exogenous ammonia. {ECO:0000305|PubMed:34583994}.
