# Human SKP1 (UniProt P63208): Functional-Annotation Research Report

## Executive summary

Human **SKP1** is a small, conserved intracellular adaptor protein whose best-established function is to connect the CUL1 scaffold of SCF Cullin–RING E3 ubiquitin ligases to interchangeable F-box substrate receptors. SKP1 is **not an enzyme**, does not itself select degradation substrates, and should not be confused with **SKP2**, which is one particular F-box substrate receptor. Through this architectural role, SKP1 enables ubiquitination of proteins governing the cell cycle, DNA replication and repair, centrosome duplication, mitosis, apoptosis, and signal transduction. Its experimentally supported locations include cytoplasm, nucleus, and centrosomes. The strongest recent development is a 2024 chemical-biology study showing that SKP1 can itself be recruited by heterobifunctional degraders to eliminate neo-substrates such as BRD4 and androgen receptor; this remains preclinical.

## 1. Mandatory identity verification

The requested target is correctly identified as **human S-phase kinase-associated protein 1**, gene **SKP1**, UniProt **P63208**. The literature describes a prototypic 163-amino-acid protein and historical names including p19, OCP2, and TCEB1L, consistent with the supplied UniProt record; the human gene is located at 5q31.1. A shorter 160-aa transcript product has also been described, although the 163-aa form is the principal experimentally characterized species. (hussain2022asmallmoleculeskp1 pages 1-2, thompson2022aberrantskp1expression pages 1-2, thompson2022aberrantskp1expression pages 2-4)

The supplied InterPro assignments—SKP1, SKP1-like, SKP1 dimerization-like, and SKP1/BTB/POZ superfamily—are concordant with structural literature. Human SKP1 contains an N-terminal BTB/POZ-like region participating in CUL1 association and a helical, conformationally adaptable C-terminal region that binds F-box domains. (bhattacharya2022disorderinthe pages 20-23, thompson2022aberrantskp1expression pages 2-4)

**Ambiguity control:** SKP1 is not **SKP2**. SKP2 is an F-box substrate receptor that binds SKP1 in the SCF^SKP2 complex; papers about SKP2 inhibition or SKP2-regulated substrates do not by themselves establish a direct function of SKP1. Likewise, glycosylation and oxygen-sensing mechanisms reported for *Dictyostelium*, plant, fungal, or protist Skp1 proteins cannot automatically be assigned to human P63208. Only explicitly relevant comparative findings are used here. (thompson2022aberrantskp1expression pages 4-5, thompson2022aberrantskp1expression pages 2-4, bhattacharya2022disorderinthe pages 35-38)

## 2. Primary molecular function

### 2.1 Non-catalytic adaptor in SCF ubiquitin ligases

The canonical SCF complex comprises **SKP1–CUL1–F-box protein–RBX1**. CUL1 is the elongated scaffold; RBX1 recruits the ubiquitin-charged E2 machinery; SKP1 bridges CUL1 to an interchangeable F-box receptor; and the F-box receptor binds the substrate. Consequently, SKP1 provides structural connectivity rather than catalytic chemistry. It has no established intrinsic enzymatic reaction, catalytic substrate, or transporter function. (thompson2022aberrantskp1expression pages 1-2, thompson2022aberrantskp1expression pages 4-5, thompson2022aberrantskp1expression pages 2-4)

Substrate specificity resides primarily in the variable F-box protein. Mammals encode approximately 69 F-box proteins, grouped broadly into FBXW, FBXL, and FBXO families. The receptor’s substrate-binding domain recognizes a target—frequently through a regulated degron—while its F-box domain docks on SKP1. SKP1 therefore supports a large repertoire of SCF complexes but does not possess a single meaningful “substrate specificity” of its own. (thompson2022aberrantskp1expression pages 4-5, thompson2022aberrantskp1expression pages 2-4)

In a productive SCF assembly, ubiquitin is transferred through E1 and E2 enzymes to receptor-bound proteins, often generating K48-linked chains that direct the target to the 26S proteasome. The catalytic geometry can be further diversified by neddylation and, for some substrates, SCF–ARIH1 E3–E3 assemblies; SKP1’s role remains adaptor-level organization of the substrate-receptor arm.

### 2.2 Structural basis

NMR and biophysical analysis of recombinant human SKP1 found a predominantly monomeric protein with an approximately 19-kDa MALDI-TOF peak and sedimentation near 2.4 S. The solution structure, PDB **5XYL**, contains a conserved helical core, flexible loop 1 at residues 35–49, especially mobile loop 2 at residues 67–84, a partially formed H7 helix, and a long disordered C-terminal tail. This mixture of a stable core and mobile interaction surfaces provides a plausible physical basis for accommodating many structurally distinct F-box domains. (bhattacharya2022disorderinthe pages 20-23)

This interpretation is consistent with the landmark CUL1–RBX1–SKP1–SKP2 structural model, in which SKP1 forms the bridge between the CUL1 N terminus and the F-box receptor. The important functional point is modularity: a conserved SKP1–CUL1 core can be combined with different receptors without redesigning the catalytic half of the ligase. (thompson2022aberrantskp1expression pages 2-4, bhattacharya2022disorderinthe pages 35-38)

## 3. Cellular localization

SKP1 is intracellular and has been detected in the **cytoplasm, nucleus, and centrosome**, fitting the locations of its many SCF partners and substrates. Affinity-purified antibody imaging detected SKP1 at interphase and mitotic centrosomes and in the interphase nucleus; biochemical purification and immunoelectron microscopy supported an authentic pericentriolar association rather than merely diffuse cytosolic staining. (freed1999componentsofan pages 1-2, freed1999componentsofan pages 2-4)

Quantitatively, approximately **97% of purified centriole doublets** stained for SKP1, centrosome preparations were estimated to contain at least approximately **500 SKP1 molecules per centrosome**, and SKP1 occupied a pericentriolar region extending to roughly 2 µm. Centrosomal staining resisted microtubule depolymerization, supporting association with centrosomal material rather than passive carriage on microtubules. Cellular SKP1 abundance in NIH-3T3 cells was estimated at approximately 1 µM. (freed1999componentsofan pages 2-4, freed1999componentsofan pages 4-6)

These localization experiments used mammalian mouse or hamster cells, while antibody-blocking and proteasome-perturbation assays included *Xenopus* systems. They therefore strongly support conserved mammalian localization but are not, by themselves, exclusively human-cell measurements. Human-cell functional studies nevertheless support relevant nuclear and centrosome-associated roles through effects on replication, micronuclei, chromosome number, and spindle/centrosome regulation. (freed1999componentsofan pages 1-2, thompson2022aberrantskp1expression pages 6-7)

## 4. Principal pathways and biological processes

### Cell-cycle control

Distinct SCF receptors use the SKP1-containing core to turn over cyclins, CDK regulators, phosphatases, and checkpoint proteins. Examples summarized in authoritative reviews include Cyclin D1, CDC25A, CDT1, Claspin, RRM2, and eEF2K. This timed proteolysis controls S-phase entry, replication licensing, checkpoint recovery, nucleotide supply, translation, and apoptosis. These are functions of particular SCF assemblies; SKP1 enables them but is not the substrate-recognition determinant. (thompson2022aberrantskp1expression pages 4-5)

### Centrosome duplication and mitosis

SKP1 and CUL1 localize to centrosomes throughout the cell cycle. Anti-Skp1 or anti-Cul1 antibodies inhibited centriole separation in a cell-free system, while proteasome inhibitors blocked centriole separation and centrosome duplication in experimental embryos. More recent pathway models place SKP1-containing SCFs upstream of centrosomal proteins including CCP110, PLK4, SASS6, and BORA/AURKA, thereby coupling proteolysis to centriole number and spindle organization. (freed1999componentsofan pages 1-2, thompson2022aberrantskp1expression pages 5-6, thompson2022aberrantskp1expression pages 6-7)

### DNA replication, repair, and chromosome stability

Human SKP1 depletion increases Cyclin E1 and produces replication stress, DNA double-strand-break markers, micronuclei, altered chromosome numbers, and chromothriptic events. Cyclin E1 correction substantially rescued these abnormalities, although incomplete rescue implies contributions from additional SCF substrates. Similar dynamic chromosome-instability phenotypes followed reduction of SKP1 or CUL1 in non-transformed fallopian-tube secretory epithelial cells over approximately three months. (thompson2022aberrantskp1expression pages 6-7)

Thus, the most precise interpretation is not that SKP1 constitutes a stand-alone “genome-stability pathway,” but that an adequate pool of SKP1 is required to assemble multiple SCFs whose coordinated substrate turnover prevents replication and mitotic errors.

## 5. Disease relevance and strength of evidence

Cancer is the best-supported disease context, but SKP1 should not be labeled simply as an oncogene or tumor suppressor. Because it is shared by tumor-suppressive and tumor-promoting SCFs, either decreased or inappropriate activity may be harmful depending on the receptor, substrate, cell state, and tissue. Reduced SKP1 can cause Cyclin E1 accumulation and chromosome instability; conversely, broadly blocking SKP1 can disrupt many essential degradation programs. (thompson2022aberrantskp1expression pages 1-2, thompson2022aberrantskp1expression pages 4-5, thompson2022aberrantskp1expression pages 6-7)

Across TCGA data summarized for **12 solid-tumor types**, SKP1 mutation was rare and copy-number losses exceeded gains in **11 of 12** cancers, with shallow losses especially frequent in ovarian and prostate cancers. These observations establish recurrent alteration, not therapeutic causality. Open Targets also links SKP1 to cancer, HIV infection, neurodegenerative disease, CNS autoimmunity, and keratoconus, but such database associations aggregate heterogeneous evidence and should be treated as hypothesis-generating rather than proof of a direct disease mechanism. (OpenTargets Search: -SKP1, thompson2022aberrantskp1expression pages 6-7)

In a mouse model using dominant-negative Cul1-mediated Skp1 inactivation, more than **80%** of animals developed T-cell lymphomas together with centrosomal and chromosomal abnormalities. This supports a causal connection between impaired SCF-core function and tumorigenesis, although it does not isolate a human SKP1-specific mechanism. (thompson2022aberrantskp1expression pages 5-6)

Expert reviews accordingly regard SKP1 as a plausible biomarker or therapeutic node but emphasize that causal, receptor-resolved studies are still needed before altered SKP1 abundance can be interpreted clinically. (thompson2022aberrantskp1expression pages 1-2)

## 6. Recent developments and applications

### Direct SKP1 inhibition

Hussain et al. reported the small molecule **Z0933M**, which bound SKP1 with a reported **K_D of 0.054 mM** and engaged a hydrophobic region involving F101 and F139 near the C-terminal F-box-binding extension. The compound disrupted SKP1–F-box interactions, altered SCF-substrate handling, induced cell-cycle arrest, and produced p53-dependent cell death. The approximately 54-µM affinity and cell-based nature of the work make this a preclinical chemical probe rather than a validated drug. Published July 2022: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.isci.2022.104591. (hussain2022asmallmoleculeskp1 pages 1-2)

### 2024: recruiting SKP1 for targeted protein degradation

Hong et al. screened **1,284 cysteine-reactive compounds** and identified **EN884**, an acrylamide recruiter that modifies SKP1 **C160** in its flexible C-terminal region. Binding was much stronger in an assembled SKP1–FBXO7–CUL1–RBX1 complex than against isolated SKP1, showing that protein context and conformation matter. Cellular chemoproteomics measured approximately **7% C160 engagement**, while a clickable derivative enriched SKP1 **2.8-fold**. Published February 16, 2024: https://doi.org/10.1021/acschembio.3c00642. (hong2024exploitingthecullin pages 11-13, hong2024exploitingthecullin pages 1-2, hong2024exploitingthecullin pages 2-4)

Linking the recruiter to JQ1 produced BRD4-directed degraders. The lead compound caused **94% BRD4 loss after 24 hours**, compared with **42%** for a non-reactive analog. Degradation was blocked by SKP1 knockdown and attenuated by the proteasome inhibitor bortezomib and the neddylation inhibitor MLN4924, supporting dependence on SKP1, the proteasome, and an active Cullin–RING system. Related molecules degraded androgen receptor in LNCaP prostate-cancer cells in a proteasome-dependent manner. (hong2024exploitingthecullin pages 6-8, hong2024exploitingthecullin pages 4-6, hong2024exploitingthecullin pages 13-19)

This work provides proof of concept that an essential core adaptor—not only a conventional substrate receptor—can be exploited for targeted degradation. However, EN884 derivatives displayed off-target activity, early-hit potency/selectivity limitations, and evidence of reversible recruitment by nominally non-reactive analogs. Complete AR dependency testing was complicated because SKP1 knockdown was lethal and MLN4924 itself reduced AR. No clinical implementation has yet been established. (hong2024exploitingthecullin pages 6-8, hong2024exploitingthecullin pages 4-6)

## 8. Overall functional annotation

**Recommended primary annotation:** Human SKP1/P63208 is a conserved, non-catalytic intracellular adaptor of CUL1-based SCF E3 ubiquitin ligases. Its N-terminal SKP1/BTB-like region associates with CUL1, while its structurally adaptable C-terminal region binds the F-box domain of interchangeable substrate receptors. It thereby positions receptor-bound targets near the RBX1/E2 ubiquitin-transfer machinery for ubiquitination and, commonly, proteasomal degradation.

**Primary operating locations:** cytoplasm, nucleus, and centrosomes; the exact distribution is partner- and cell-state-dependent.

**Most directly supported biological roles:** assembly of SCF complexes controlling cell-cycle transitions, replication and DNA-damage responses, centrosome duplication, mitotic-spindle integrity, chromosome segregation, and apoptosis.

**Confidence assessment:** High confidence for protein identity, family/domain consistency, SCF-adaptor mechanism, and lack of intrinsic catalytic activity; moderate-to-high confidence for conserved mammalian nuclear/cytoplasmic/centrosomal localization; moderate confidence for disease-specific interpretation because many phenotypes reflect disruption of the whole SCF repertoire. Direct pharmacological targeting and SKP1-recruited degradation are promising but remain preclinical.
