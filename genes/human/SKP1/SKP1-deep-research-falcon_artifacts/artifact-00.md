| Identity and aliases | The research target is human **SKP1**, the S-phase kinase-associated protein 1 specified as **UniProt P63208**. Literature describes a 163-aa prototypic protein and historical aliases **p19**, **OCP2**, and **TCEB1L**; the gene maps to 5q31.1. | Protein annotation supplied for P63208 agrees with literature on human SKP1 identity, size, aliases, and SCF-complex function. A shorter 160-aa splice isoform has also been reported. | Thompson et al., March 2022, https://doi.org/10.3389/fcell.2022.859582; Hussain et al., July 2022, https://doi.org/10.1016/j.isci.2022.104591 |
| Primary function and distinction from SKP2 | SKP1 is a **non-catalytic adaptor/scaffold** in SCF ubiquitin ligases. Its N-terminal region binds **CUL1**, while its C-terminal region binds the F-box domain of a substrate receptor. **SKP2 is one such F-box substrate receptor—not SKP1 and not a synonym for it.** | Mechanistic and structural evidence assigns ubiquitin-transfer machinery to RBX1/E2 and substrate selection to the variable F-box protein. SKP1 physically couples these modules and has no demonstrated catalytic reaction or intrinsic substrate specificity. Approximately 69 mammalian F-box proteins can potentially diversify SCF complexes. | Zheng et al., April 2002, https://doi.org/10.1038/416703a; Thompson et al., March 2022, https://doi.org/10.3389/fcell.2022.859582 |
| Structure and domains | The literature agrees with the supplied SKP1-family, SKP1-like, dimerization-superfamily, and BTB/POZ-superfamily annotations. SKP1 has a conserved helical core and N-terminal BTB/POZ-like CUL1-binding region, plus a structurally adaptable C-terminal F-box-binding extension. | NMR of predominantly monomeric human SKP1 produced PDB **5XYL** and identified flexible loop 1 (residues 35–49), especially flexible loop 2 (67–84), a partially formed helix H7, and a long disordered C-terminal tail. Sedimentation remained near **2.4 S**, and MALDI-TOF showed a major approximately **19-kDa** species. | Bhattacharya et al., November 2022, https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jmb.2022.167830 |
| Cellular localization | SKP1 operates principally as an intracellular protein in the **cytoplasm, nucleus, and centrosome**, consistent with SCF-dependent control of soluble, nuclear, and centrosomal substrates. Centrosomal SKP1 persists through interphase and mitosis. | Immunofluorescence, purified-centrosome biochemistry, deconvolution microscopy, and immunoelectron microscopy detected SKP1 around and between centrioles. About **97%** of purified centriole doublets stained positive; preparations contained at least approximately **500 SKP1 molecules per centrosome**, and cellular abundance in NIH-3T3 cells was estimated at approximately **1 μM**. Some localization evidence used mouse or hamster cells and Xenopus functional assays rather than human cells. | Freed et al., September 1999, https://doi.org/10.1101/gad.13.17.2242 |
| Pathways and genome stability | By enabling different SCF complexes, SKP1 supports timed degradation of regulators of cell-cycle transitions, DNA replication and repair, apoptosis, centrosome duplication, spindle formation, and chromosome segregation. Reduced SKP1 can stabilize Cyclin E1 and provoke chromosome instability. | Human-cell silencing increased nuclear area, micronuclei, abnormal chromosome numbers, replication stress, DNA double-strand breaks, and chromothriptic events. Cyclin E1 rescue experiments explained much—but not all—of the phenotype. In TCGA analyses summarized across **12 solid-tumor types**, SKP1 losses exceeded gains in **11 of 12**, although mutations were rare. | Thompson et al., February 2020, https://doi.org/10.3390/cancers12030531; Thompson et al., March 2022, https://doi.org/10.3389/fcell.2022.859582 |
| Experimental inhibitor Z0933M | Z0933M was reported as a direct small-molecule SKP1 inhibitor/modulator that interferes with SKP1–F-box interactions, disrupts SCF function, and induces cell-cycle arrest and p53-dependent cell death. | Reported binding affinity **KD = 0.054 mM**. Binding was mapped near a C-terminal hydrophobic hotspot involving **F101 and F139**. Evidence is preclinical; potency is modest and no clinical efficacy was established. | Hussain et al., July 2022, https://doi.org/10.1016/j.isci.2022.104591 |
| EN884 and SKP1-based targeted degradation | EN884 is an acrylamide-containing recruiter that engages SKP1 **C160** preferentially in an assembled SCF complex. EN884-derived heterobifunctional degraders redirected the machinery toward neo-substrates **BRD4** and androgen receptor. | EN884 emerged from a **1,284-compound** cysteine-reactive screen. Cellular engagement of C160 was reported at **7%**, and a clickable probe enriched SKP1 **2.8-fold**. The lead BRD4 degrader caused **94%** loss at 24 h versus **42%** for a non-reactive analog; activity was blocked by SKP1 knockdown and attenuated by bortezomib and MLN4924. AR degradation in LNCaP cells was proteasome-dependent. | Hong et al., February 16, 2024, https://doi.org/10.1021/acschembio.3c00642 |
| Evidence limitations | The core adaptor assignment is well supported, but many downstream phenotypes reflect entire SCF complexes and cannot be attributed to a unique SKP1 substrate. Direct SKP1 drug discovery remains preclinical, and broad inhibition may be toxic because SKP1 is shared by many essential SCF ligases. | Human-specific localization data are less complete than mammalian/Xenopus evidence. Z0933M has millimolar-to-high-micromolar affinity. EN884 shows off-target labeling and early-hit potency/selectivity limitations; non-reactive analogs also retained degradation activity, and complete SKP1-dependency testing for AR was impeded by lethality of SKP1 knockdown. Disease associations are therefore mechanistically plausible but not equivalent to clinical validation. | Thompson et al., March 2022, https://doi.org/10.3389/fcell.2022.859582; Hussain et al., July 2022, https://doi.org/10.1016/j.isci.2022.104591; Hong et al., February 2024, https://doi.org/10.1021/acschembio.3c00642 |
