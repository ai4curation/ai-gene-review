CC   -!- FUNCTION: G protein-coupled photoreceptor that selectively activates
CC       transducin G alpha protein (G(t)) in response to medium-wavelength
CC       (green) light, thereby stimulating the G(t)/transducin protein pathway
CC       (PubMed:2937147, PubMed:6140680, PubMed:15773913). This activation
CC       leads to increased cGMP phosphodiesterase activity, resulting in
CC       decreased intracellular cGMP and subsequent closure of cGMP-gated ion
CC       channels (By similarity). The process is initiated when the opsin-bound
CC       cis-retinal chromophore absorbs a photon and isomerizes to all-trans-
CC       retinal, inducing a conformational change in the opsin that triggers
CC       the G protein-mediated phototransduction cascade (By similarity).
CC       Signal termination occurs via receptor phosphorylation
CC       (PubMed:15773913). Receptor phosphorylation promotes binding of cone
CC       arrestin (ARR3) and displacement of the G protein alpha subunit (By
CC       similarity). Mediates visual perception of green light in cone
CC       photoreceptor cells (PubMed:12051694, PubMed:1302020, PubMed:8666378).
CC       {ECO:0000250|UniProtKB:O35599, ECO:0000250|UniProtKB:P04000,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:12051694, ECO:0000269|PubMed:1302020,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:15773913, ECO:0000269|PubMed:2937147,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:6140680, ECO:0000269|PubMed:8666378}.

CC       Absorption:
CC         Abs(max)=530 nm {ECO:0000269|PubMed:6140680};
CC   -!- SUBUNIT: Monomer. Homodimer. Homotetramer. Interacts (light-activated
CC       phosphorylated form) with ARR3 (By similarity).
CC       {ECO:0000250|UniProtKB:O35599, ECO:0000269|PubMed:28402104}.

CC       {ECO:0000255}.
CC   -!- TISSUE SPECIFICITY: The red (OPN1LW), green (OPN1MW), and blue (OPN1SW)
CC       photoreceptor pigments are found in cone cells.
CC       {ECO:0000269|PubMed:2937147}.
CC   -!- INDUCTION: Maximally activated by medium-wavelength light near 530 nm,
CC       corresponding to the green region of the visible spectrum in retinal
CC       cone cell. {ECO:0000269|PubMed:6140680}.
CC   -!- PTM: Contains one retinal chromophore covalently bound to a lysine
CC       residue via a Schiff base. Upon light absorption, the bound 11-cis-
CC       retinal isomerizes to all-trans-retinal. Following activation, the
CC       covalently bound all-trans-retinal is released through hydrolysis of
CC       the Schiff base, and OPN1 is regenerated by binding a new 11-cis-
CC       retinal molecule. {ECO:0000269|Ref.6}.
CC   -!- PTM: N-glycosylated (PubMed:30948514). O-glycosylated
CC       (PubMed:30948514). {ECO:0000269|PubMed:30948514}.
CC   -!- PTM: Phosphorylated on some or all of the serine and threonine residues
CC       present in the C-terminal region. Phosphorylated in a light-dependent
CC       manner by GRK7 (PubMed:15773913). Can be phosphorylated in a light-
CC       dependent manner by GRK1 (By similarity).
CC       {ECO:0000250|UniProtKB:O35599, ECO:0000269|PubMed:15773913,
CC       ECO:0000305|PubMed:2937147}.
CC   -!- DISEASE: Colorblindness, partial, deutan series (CBD) [MIM:303800]: An
CC       X-linked color vision defect characterized by a dichromasy in which red
CC       and green are confused, without loss of luminance or shift or
CC       shortening of the spectrum. Dichromasy is due to the use of only two
CC       types of photoreceptors, blue plus red in deuteranopia and blue plus
CC       green in protanopia. {ECO:0000269|PubMed:12051694,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:1302020}. Note=The disease is caused by variants
CC       affecting the gene represented in this entry.
CC   -!- DISEASE: Blue cone monochromacy (BCM) [MIM:303700]: A rare X-linked
CC       congenital stationary cone dysfunction syndrome characterized by the
CC       absence of functional long wavelength-sensitive and medium wavelength-
CC       sensitive cones in the retina. Color discrimination is severely
CC       impaired from birth, and vision is derived from the remaining preserved
CC       blue (S) cones and rod photoreceptors. BCM typically presents with
CC       reduced visual acuity, pendular nystagmus, and photophobia. Patients
CC       often have myopia. {ECO:0000269|PubMed:30065301,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:34445325, ECO:0000269|PubMed:35769953,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:8666378}. Note=The disease is caused by variants
CC       affecting the gene represented in this entry.
CC   -!- DISEASE: Cone dystrophy 5 (COD5) [MIM:303700]: An X-linked cone
CC       dystrophy. Cone dystrophies are retinal dystrophies characterized by
CC       progressive degeneration of the cone photoreceptors with preservation
CC       of rod function, as indicated by electroretinogram. However, some rod
CC       involvement may be present in some cone dystrophies, particularly at
CC       late stage. Affected individuals suffer from photophobia, loss of
CC       visual acuity, color vision and central visual field. Another sign is
CC       the absence of macular lesions for many years. Cone dystrophies are
CC       distinguished from the cone-rod dystrophies in which some loss of
CC       peripheral vision also occurs. {ECO:0000269|PubMed:20579627}. Note=The
CC       disease is caused by variants affecting the gene represented in this
CC       entry.
CC   -!- SIMILARITY: Belongs to the G protein-coupled receptor 1 family. Opsin
CC       subfamily. {ECO:0000255|PROSITE-ProRule:PRU00521}.
CC   -!- CAUTION: Medium-wave-sensitive opsin genes vary in number among
CC       individuals and, together with a single red pigment gene, reside in a
CC       head-to-tail tandem array within the X chromosome. In the GRCh38
CC       reference genome assembly, there are 3 genes in tandem coding for
CC       identical proteins AC P04001, AC P0DN77 and P0DN78. {ECO:0000305}.
