# ERCC5 review notes

## 2026-10-24 — annotation and evidence review

ERCC5/XPG is best characterized as a nuclear DNA endonuclease that recognizes DNA junctions and performs the 3′ incision in nucleotide-excision repair. Human reconstitution, purified-protein incision, footprinting and current pathway catalyst records agree on this role. The human protein is not assigned the helicase chemistry of its TFIIH partners. Additional experiments support repair-protein assembly/loading in homologous recombination and stimulation of NTHL1 in biochemical base-excision repair assays. These additional roles remain distinct from XPG nuclease catalysis. [PMID:11259578](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC86862/), [PMID:12644470](https://doi.org/10.1074/jbc.M213155200), [PMID:26833090](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4761302/), [PMID:9927729](https://academic.oup.com/nar/article/27/4/979/2902566).

All 77 imported annotation contexts have been reviewed: 35 ACCEPT, 14 KEEP_AS_NON_CORE, 20 MODIFY and 8 UNDECIDED. No imported source object or natural-product identifier was changed. No NEW annotation or publication quotation was added. Core-function synthesis remains a separate next stage. The source guide records actual original access, operative locators and specific unread material; availability flags in the normal publication caches remain untouched.

Full-length XPG and engineered reagents must be kept separate. The XPGcat structural reagent replaces a large segment with archaeal FEN sequence and has a different substrate range from full-length XPG. Another structural study links human residues 1–112 to 750–990. Neither is the natural 419-aa or 232-aa splice product, and the engineered ΔN136 reagent used in earlier self-association experiments is not a natural isoform. The functions and localization of the two shorter natural products remain unresolved. [PMID:32522879](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7321962/), [PMID:32821917](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7515719/), [PMID:10026181](https://doi.org/10.1074/jbc.274.9.5637).

Human XPG contacts both the duplex and a short single-stranded segment beside a junction. This is not a claim of unrestricted high-affinity binding to isolated DNA strands. Isolated circular ssDNA cleavage is independently demonstrated in the original human study, rather than inferred from bubble-DNA incision. The specific damaged-DNA binding assertion remains uncertain because the inspected structural substrate is synthetic splayed-arm DNA; the GO term itself does not require preferential affinity for damaged DNA. [PMID:12644470](https://doi.org/10.1074/jbc.M213155200), [PMID:8078765](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC523723/?page=0).

Nucleus and chromosome terms were refined to nucleoplasm and chromatin where controlled human repair-site evidence provides that specificity. The HPA record has an explicit cross-gene antibody caution. The biochemical P100 fraction contains chromatin, nuclear matrix and other insoluble material; it is not purified chromatin. These limits remain in the review. The electronic source mappings are valid, and the refinements address term scope rather than claiming a source misattribution. [Human localization study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4761302/), [HPA ERCC5](https://www.proteinatlas.org/ENSG00000134899-ERCC5/subcellular).

The TC-NER annotation is supported by an explicit human XPG catalyst chain: pathway R-HSA-6781827 contains dual incision and the XPG 3′-incision reaction, whose active unit maps to full-length human P28715. This establishes participation through a defined step. It does not verify all upstream recognition or polymerase-backtracking models, nor substitute for access to the original PMID:16246722 interaction assays. [TC-NER pathway](https://reactome.org/content/detail/R-HSA-6781827), [XPG incision](https://reactome.org/content/detail/R-HSA-6782224).

The eight remaining uncertainties concern the precise RNA polymerase II, CSB, p62 and p44 interaction claims; the damaged-DNA binding claim; the inaccessible ERCC5–BRAF supplementary screening record; and the original RPA-associated claim. RPA association is not recast as constitutive RPA-subunit membership. The review preserves positive curator assertions as undecided when their precise experiments cannot be assessed.

## 2026-10-24 — core-function synthesis

One core unit now represents DNA endonuclease activity in nucleotide-excision repair. Human full-length XPG cuts structured DNA junctions and covalently closed ssDNA; the selected catalytic term covers both measured substrate geometries. Its principal repair action is the 3′ incision in global-genome and transcription-coupled NER. The NER process term covers both without a redundant parent-and-child pair. [Human ssDNA nuclease experiments](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC523723/?page=0), [full-length junction assays](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7321962/).

The complex context describes functional repair assemblies, not permanent preassembly or constitutive transcriptional TFIIH membership. Human reconstitution and salt-sensitive associations support this distinction. Current human GG-NER and TC-NER catalyst records each identify full-length P28715 as the DNA-endonuclease active unit and a direct member of the respective incision assembly. The noncatalytic HR recruitment and NTHL1 activation roles remain separate non-core functions; neither is assigned to XPG nuclease chemistry. All 77 annotation reviews, all reference assessments and the three natural-product records are unchanged. No new annotation or publication quotation was added. [Functional human repair complexes](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC86862/), [GG-NER incision](https://reactome.org/content/detail/R-HSA-5690988), [TC-NER incision](https://reactome.org/content/detail/R-HSA-6782224).
