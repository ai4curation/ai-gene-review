### Receptor activity and calcium regulation

One core molecular activity summarizes CASR: G protein-coupled receptor activity at the plasma membrane. Extracellular calcium sensing is coupled to receptor dimer rearrangement and G-protein activation. Calcium binding, amino-acid binding and homodimerization are supported aspects of that receptor mechanism, rather than separate catalytic cores. The core includes calcium detection and the PLC-activating and adenylate-cyclase-inhibiting receptor pathways already present in the source annotations. No NEW assertion is proposed.

Official GO:0004930 describes extracellular signal reception coupled to activation of a heterotrimeric G protein. GO:0006874 concerns intracellular calcium homeostasis and includes regulation of cytosolic calcium concentration among its descendants. The calcium-response annotations are retained in the context of receptor-dependent mobilization and feedback. This does not make CASR a calcium channel, pump or intracellular calcium buffer, and does not conflate cellular calcium dynamics with systemic serum-calcium balance. GO:0016020 membrane is refined to the directly supported plasma-membrane location while preserving intracellular biosynthetic and retained pools.

The 2024 coupling papers require careful construct and assay distinctions. PMID:38326620 reports two complementary cellular BRET approaches favoring Gi/o and Gq/11, alongside nanodisc complexes made with truncated or engineered components. PMID:38632411 uses truncated receptor and engineered G proteins for structures, full-length receptor for cellular assays, and qi9/qs5 chimeras to redirect Gi/Gs-like receptor recognition into an IP1 output. Those chimeric IP1 measurements are not native Gi/Gs second-messenger measurements. Separate TRUPATH assays address Gi1, Gi3 and Gq. The stated failure to detect Gs signaling in two cell lines is not universal absence; the Gs Methods paragraph also names a Gq donor, an unresolved internal description ambiguity. Neither structural miniGs nor redirected IP1 justifies a general physiological Gs claim. The existing adenylate-cyclase-inhibiting rows retain curator support for the Gi pathway, explicitly distinguishing direct Gi activation from a cAMP endpoint not measured in the inspected assays.

### Ligand interpretation and trafficking

PMID:27434672 directly demonstrates extracellular-domain tryptophan binding and calcium-binding sites, but its amino-acid agonist interpretation is not treated as the only activation model. PMID:32817431 uses dialyzed membranes and a conformational sensor to distinguish calcium activation from amino-acid potentiation. PMID:33603117 describes a tryptophan-stabilized intermediate and cooperative calcium-dependent activation. PMID:34467854 uses nanobody-stabilized states and calcium/TNCA/allosteric-modulator conditions, acknowledges limitations in assigning ions from cryo-EM density alone, and discusses the unsettled ligand terminology. Binding is retained without asserting calcium-independent amino-acid agonism or treating engineered structural states as endogenous tissue behavior.

The one generic protein-binding row concerns TMED2/p24A (PMID:20361938; exact source partner UniProtKB:Q15363). Human-kidney two-hybrid screening and reciprocal coimmunoprecipitation of human constructs support an association of the immature receptor before Golgi maturation. TMED2 is the trafficking cargo receptor and CASR is its cargo. TMED2 and Sar1 perturbations alter stability and delivery; they do not establish a transporter or chaperone activity of CASR. The standing CLINGEN_MENDELIAN instruction retains a supported generic interaction as KEEP_AS_NON_CORE when a finer molecular activity is not justified. It supersedes generic advice to remove protein binding solely for being uninformative.

### Context-dependent effects and unresolved transfers

Human CASR-dependent ERK responses are retained non-core. The three NLRP3 assembly/regulation assertions are also retained non-core using the human monocyte/THP-1 and mouse myeloid evidence in PMID:32843625. CaSR promotes a calcium/phosphate- and calciprotein-particle-dependent upstream response, with NLRP3 dependence, inflammasome readouts and residual CaSR-independent activation. It is not an inflammasome structural component, and this inflammatory output does not replace calcium sensing as the receptor core.

Many peripheral annotations are rat- or mouse-derived. Clear conserved calcium detection, cell-surface localization and PLC signaling are independently corroborated by human data. Specific developmental, polarized-localization, vascular, neuronal and stimulus-response transfers remain unresolved where their donor experiment has not been read. Expression responses can be legitimate GO response annotations; they are not automatically rejected, but they must not be mistaken for evidence that CASR mediates the stimulus. Likewise, calcium mobilization is not necessarily calcium import, and receptor-driven chloride flux would not make CASR a chloride channel. The draft retains these distinctions without blanket REMOVE decisions.

The historical TAS assertions for ossification, chemosensory behavior and anatomical morphogenesis remain UNDECIDED because complete relevant primary text was not retrieved. An abstract centered on another endpoint cannot establish mis-citation or absence of an experiment. The 34 unresolved assertions determine the DRAFT state; validation advisories do not determine it.

A distinct donor-provenance consultation recovered dated rat evidence-code/reference joins for 35 of the 37 rat-containing source assertions from the official MGI/Alliance comparative graphs. The displayed graphs date to 2023 and 2007; they do not establish the complete current transfer chain. Source indices 75 and 96 were not traced in the accessible indexed segments. The donor primary experiments were not read in that consultation. ### Publication access and factual assessments

- PMID:10077597: The complete abstract reports coexpression of inactive human CaSR mutant pairs in HEK293 cells, partial signaling rescue for selected combinations and biochemical heterodimers. This supports receptor function and functional communication between subunits. The cache is abstract-only; uninspected controls are not claimed.

- PMID:15572418: The complete abstract reports human R227Q and R227L receptors expressed in HEK293 cells, comparable glycosylation and cell-surface abundance, and different extracellular-calcium concentration responses. It supports plasma-membrane localization of the tested receptors. The cache is abstract-only.

- PMID:16740594: The complete abstract reports surface localization of wild-type receptor, ER retention of R66H, R66C and N583X mutants, and FRET evidence for wild-type and R66H/R66C dimers. Dimerization is necessary but not sufficient for efficient export in this experiment. The cache is abstract-only, so the full imaging and FRET controls were not independently inspected.

- PMID:17376781: The complete abstract describes CaSR-dependent intracellular calcium oscillations, T888 phosphorylation, PKC feedback and phosphatase-sensitive behavior in receptor-expressing HEK293 cells, with a separate bovine parathyroid component. This supports receptor-dependent calcium regulation without identifying CASR as a calcium channel. The cache is abstract-only.

- PMID:17555508: The complete abstract reports a de novo heterozygous R551K variant in cis with A986S in one child with neonatal severe hyperparathyroidism, later mild hypercalcemia, and reduced calcium sensitivity in HEK293 cells. It supports receptor signaling and localization; this family-specific presentation does not redefine the usual recessive severe-disease association. The cache is abstract-only.

- PMID:19789209: The complete abstract reports Q459R in a consanguineous family, mild hypercalcemia in homozygotes, largely normal heterozygotes, and reduced signaling despite cell-surface expression. This is a particular allele/family with apparent recessive mild disease, not a universal inheritance rule. The cache is abstract-only.

- PMID:20361938: Human CaSR and human TMED2/p24A constructs were tested in HEK293 cells after a human-kidney yeast two-hybrid screen. Selected complete Methods, Results and Discussion passages show reciprocal coimmunoprecipitation of immature CaSR, truncation and p24A-mutant comparisons, surface/total expression controls and Sar1 perturbations. TMED2 is the trafficking cargo receptor; CASR is its cargo. Direct purified-protein binding and a chaperone activity of CASR were not established.

- PMID:20798521: Selected complete Methods, Results and Discussion passages from the normal HTML cache show FLAG-tagged human CaSR expressed in HEK293 cells, background-corrected surface/total ELISA, ERK responses, arginine-motif mutants and phosphorylation-site substitutions. They support regulated surface abundance. The historical epilepsy implication is not accepted as current gene-disease validity; the frozen ClinGen record classifies that association as refuted. Cellular trafficking results and disease causality are separate claims. Direct 14-3-3 binding was explicitly left unresolved by the authors.

- PMID:21566075: The complete abstract reports reduced inositol-phosphate signaling by human T550I CaSR in COS7 cells and an intermediate cotransfection response. It supports GPCR signaling; dominant-negative interpretation remains qualified. The cache is abstract-only.

- PMID:22114145: The complete abstract describes total internal reflection fluorescence measurements of agonist-driven CaSR insertion into the plasma membrane, ongoing biosynthetic delivery and constitutive endocytosis. These results support cell-surface localization and dynamic receptor delivery. The cache is abstract-only; detailed imaging controls were not inspected.

- PMID:22789683: The complete abstract reports Q681R and P221L in hypocalcemic families and functional testing of Q681R in HEK293 cells, with increased calcium sensitivity despite similar expression and surface targeting. It supports receptor localization and signaling. The cache is abstract-only.

- PMID:23169696: The complete abstract reports human N802I and N802S CaSR in COS7 cells, MAPK signaling and allosteric modulation. N802I showed ligand-independent activity whereas N802S was inactivating. These are variant-dependent receptor phenotypes, not universal constitutive signaling. The cache is abstract-only.

- PMID:23966241: The complete abstract reports gain- and loss-of-function human CaSR variants, calcium-dependent MAPK responses, surface-expression comparisons and rescue by allosteric modulators. It supports receptor signaling. The cache is abstract-only; assay details not stated in the abstract are not inferred.

- PMID:25104082: The complete abstract reports Myc-tagged human CaSR expression in HEK293 cells, absent detectable E707V expression and reduced signaling sensitivity for G774S, with additional family screening. This supports receptor signaling and the tested localization observations, not a conclusion that all hypercalcemic families have CASR variants. The cache is abstract-only.

- PMID:25292184: Selected complete Results, Methods and Discussion passages show Myc-tagged human T972M CaSR in HEK293 cells, wild-type and F351V controls, altered mature glycosylation and reduced calcium-evoked ERK phosphorylation. The father/son hypercalciuric presentation is an atypical family-specific finding. Tissue-selective effects on parathyroid versus kidney are an interpretation rather than directly measured tissue signaling.

- PMID:25766501: The complete abstract reports human P136L CaSR in HEK293 cells, surface localization, increased intracellular-calcium and MAPK responses, and segregation with hypocalcemia in a family. This supports the tested receptor localization and signaling. The cache is abstract-only.

- PMID:26386835: The complete abstract reports human G571W CaSR in COS7 cells, surface expression comparable to wild type and reduced Fura-2 calcium responses. This supports receptor signaling and localization, with no claim to have inspected the complete imaging controls. The cache is abstract-only.

- PMID:27434672: Selected complete structural, ligand-binding, mutational and cellular-assay passages were read. Human extracellular-domain constructs were expressed in insect cells, whereas full-length human receptors were tested in HEK293 cells with surface-expression controls, IP1 and calcium readouts. Scintillation proximity directly demonstrated tryptophan binding; anomalous diffraction supported calcium-binding sites. The paper interprets amino acids as agonists, but later controlled-membrane work distinguishes positive allosteric modulation. Amino-acid binding remains supported irrespective of this activation-model dispute.

- PMID:32817431: Selected complete Results and Discussion passages describe a SNAP-tag TR-FRET receptor sensor, cellular IP1/calcium assays, cell-surface controls, cross-linking and dialyzed membrane preparations. Under the controlled membrane conditions calcium alone drove the full conformational transition, whereas amino acids potentiated calcium responses. This differs from a universal amino-acid-alone agonist interpretation. Detailed experimental protocols delegated to the separate SI Appendix were not independently read.

- PMID:32843625: Selected complete Results, captions, Discussion and Methods passages were read, including human monocytes, one sequenced CaSR-deficient THP-1 clone and a non-targeting clone, myeloid-specific mouse deletion, pharmacological controls, calciprotein-particle uptake, NLRP3 dependence, IL-1beta release and ASC-speck methodology. CaSR contributes upstream to inflammatory activation under calcium/phosphate/particle conditions; residual CaSR-independent activation is explicit. This does not make CASR a structural inflammasome component or establish a universal receptor output. Figures and supplementary data were not independently reanalyzed.

- PMID:33603117: Selected complete cryo-EM interpretation, construct and cellular-assay passages were read. Full-length human CaSR with substituted signal sequence/tags was expressed in insect cells; HEK293T mutant assays measured IP1 with surface-expression controls. Tryptophan closes the Venus flytrap domain but does not alone complete transmembrane activation; calcium and amino acids cooperate in the authors' model. Ambient ligands and conformational intermediates limit absolute ligand-sufficiency conclusions.

- PMID:34194040: Selected complete structural, engineered-dimer, BRET, IP1 and expression-method passages were read. Human CaSR residues 20-894 were used for structural and cellular assays, with signaling competence assessed; forced dimers used GABAB-derived targeting elements. The results support GPCR signaling, asymmetric activation and allosteric modulation, but engineered targeting constructs and the C-terminal truncation must not be presented as unmodified endogenous receptor behavior.

- PMID:34467854: Selected complete structural, ligand, mutational, expression-control and Discussion passages were read. A human 1-870 construct was purified for structures and full-length mutant receptors were tested in cells. Inhibitory nanobody stabilization and calcium/TNCA/cinacalcet conditions define the structural states. The authors acknowledge that cryo-EM density alone does not uniquely identify calcium and that amino-acid activation terminology remains contested. Cellular calcium responses and expression controls support receptor activity.

- PMID:38326620: The complete abstract and selected complete Results/Methods paragraphs were read. Two complementary cellular BRET approaches primarily support Gi/o and Gq/11 activation, with receptor surface-expression controls. Human receptor constructs include a 20-894 truncation; structural preparations additionally use GABAB-derived tails, tethering and engineered G proteins. These experiments support receptor coupling in the tested systems, not a universal native-tissue G-protein preference or a direct cAMP measurement.

- PMID:38632411: The complete abstract and selected complete construct/functional Methods paragraphs were read. Structures use truncated human receptor and dominant-negative or mini/chimeric G proteins, whereas cellular assays use full-length receptor. Gi/Gs-tail chimeras redirect coupling to an IP1 readout in Gq/11-deficient cells; this is not native Gi/Gs second-messenger measurement. Separate TRUPATH assays address Gi1, Gi3 and Gq. The authors report no detectable CaSR-induced Gs activity in HEK293T or MCF7, although that method paragraph names a Gq donor, an unresolved internal description ambiguity. Surface-expression controls and calcium-dependent FRET-background subtraction are explicit. No universal physiological Gs claim is justified.

- PMID:7759551: The complete abstract reports cloning of a 1078-residue human parathyroid calcium-sensing receptor, a rare insertion-containing transcript and functional cation responses in Xenopus oocytes. This supports receptor function and calcium sensing. A complete primary body was not retrieved, and organismal chemosensory behavior cannot be adjudicated from the abstract alone.

- PMID:7874174: PubMed and the complete cached abstract identify the human Glu128Ala hypocalcemia study, with increased calcium-evoked inositol-trisphosphate signaling in Xenopus oocytes. This supports altered receptor signaling. Complete primary text was not retrieved; the ossification assertion remains unresolved rather than being rejected from the abstract.

- PMID:7916660: The complete abstract reports human CASR variants in familial hypocalciuric hypercalcemia and neonatal severe hyperparathyroidism with oocyte functional tests. This supports disturbed calcium sensing. Complete primary text was not retrieved; a specific direct morphogenesis role cannot be adjudicated from the abstract alone.

- PMID:8636323: The complete abstract reports human family variant screening and Xenopus oocyte functional tests, with defective calcium responses for three of four tested mutant receptors. It supports receptor signaling while preserving variant-specific differences. The cache is abstract-only.

- PMID:8702647: The complete abstract reports eight human CaSR variants expressed in HEK293 cells, calcium/gadolinium responses, glycosylation and surface-expression differences. It supports receptor signaling and localization. Trafficking versus coupling defects are interpreted within each mutant context. The cache is abstract-only.

- PMID:8878438: The complete abstract reports eleven human CaSR variants expressed in HEK293 cells with altered extracellular-calcium response curves corresponding to loss- or gain-of-function phenotypes. This supports receptor signaling. The cache is abstract-only.

- PMID:9694886: The complete abstract reports human CaSR in HEK293 cells, PKC modulators, phosphorylation-site mutants and in-vitro phosphorylation identifying T888 as a major site. It supports feedback regulation of receptor-dependent cytosolic calcium responses; CASR is the kinase substrate, not the kinase. The cache is abstract-only.
