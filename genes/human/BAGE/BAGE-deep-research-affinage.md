# Affinage mechanistic annotation for BAGE (human)

## Current model (mechanistic narrative)

BAGE encodes a 43-amino-acid miniprotein that functions as a cancer-testis antigen, defined by the CTL-recognized peptide AARAVFLAL presented on HLA-Cw1601 to trigger cytolytic T lymphocyte killing of human melanoma cells [PMID:7895173]. Its expression is governed primarily by an epigenetic switch: BAGE loci reside in juxtacentromeric regions of chromosomes 9, 13, 18, and 21 and are densely methylated in normal somatic tissues but hypomethylated in the large majority of tumors, with promoter-region hypomethylation causally driving re-expression in cancer [PMID:15704127]. Consistent with methylation-based silencing, BAGE transcription in negative cells can be reactivated pharmacologically by the DNA demethylating agent 5-aza-2'-deoxycytidine [PMID:9816341], and BAGE-locus hypomethylation is a consistent event in colorectal neoplastic transformation that distinguishes tumor from matched normal tissue [PMID:18541613]. The family arose through hominoid-lineage chromosomal rearrangements that duplicated an 84-kb fragment containing part of MLL3 into juxtacentromeric regions, and dN/dS analysis indicates the coding sequences are under selective pressure consistent with functional protein products [PMID:12676563]. Beyond its antigenicity and epigenetic regulation, no intrinsic biochemical or cellular function for the BAGE protein has been characterized in the available corpus.
