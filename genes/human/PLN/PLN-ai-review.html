Gene Description

Phospholamban is a 52-residue single-pass transmembrane phosphoprotein of the cardiac sarcoplasmic reticulum (SR) and the founding member of the regulin family of SERCA-regulating micropeptides. Monomeric unphosphorylated PLN binds the transmembrane groove of the SERCA2a Ca2+-ATPase (M2/M4/M6/M9) and lowers the pump's apparent Ca2+ affinity, slowing Ca2+ re-uptake into the SR and thereby limiting cardiac relaxation and contractility. Phosphorylation of Ser16 by PKA and Thr17 by CaMKII during beta-adrenergic stimulation relieves inhibition and shifts PLN into a homopentameric reservoir held together by a transmembrane leucine/isoleucine zipper. PLN also inhibits SERCA3, forms hetero-oligomers with sarcolipin, DWORF and other regulins, and binds HAX1. Loss of PLN in mice enhances basal contractility, and human mutations (R9C, R14del) cause dilated or arrhythmogenic cardiomyopathy.

Core Functions

Reversible inhibitor of the SERCA2a Ca2+ pump in the cardiac SR membrane: monomeric, unphosphorylated PLN binds the SERCA transmembrane groove and lowers its apparent Ca2+ affinity, limiting Ca2+ re-uptake into the SR and thereby setting basal relaxation and contractility; PKA/CaMKII phosphorylation relieves inhibition. The inhibitory monomer is in equilibrium with a homopentamer held by a transmembrane leucine/isoleucine zipper, a less active reservoir whose assembly phosphorylation favors.

description: Phospholamban is a 52-residue single-pass transmembrane phosphoprotein of the cardiac sarcoplasmic
  reticulum (SR) and the founding member of the regulin family of SERCA-regulating micropeptides. Monomeric unphosphorylated
  PLN binds the transmembrane groove of the SERCA2a Ca2+-ATPase (M2/M4/M6/M9) and lowers the pump's apparent Ca2+
  affinity, slowing Ca2+ re-uptake into the SR and thereby limiting cardiac relaxation and contractility. Phosphorylation
  of Ser16 by PKA and Thr17 by CaMKII during beta-adrenergic stimulation relieves inhibition and shifts PLN into
  a homopentameric reservoir held together by a transmembrane leucine/isoleucine zipper. PLN also inhibits SERCA3,
  forms hetero-oligomers with sarcolipin, DWORF and other regulins, and binds HAX1. Loss of PLN in mice enhances
  basal contractility, and human mutations (R9C, R14del) cause dilated or arrhythmogenic cardiomyopathy.

    PLN binds the SERCA transmembrane groove and lowers its apparent Ca2+ affinity, limiting Ca2+ re-uptake into
    the SR and thereby setting basal relaxation and contractility; PKA/CaMKII phosphorylation relieves inhibition.
    The inhibitory monomer is in equilibrium with a homopentamer held by a transmembrane leucine/isoleucine zipper,
    a less active reservoir whose assembly phosphorylation favors.'
