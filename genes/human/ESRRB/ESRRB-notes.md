## 2026-10-24 — Human hearing loss and evidence-scope clarification

Biallelic ESRRB variants cause DFNB35, an autosomal recessive nonsyndromic hearing disorder. The original study identifies segregating variants in five families and severe-to-profound hearing loss; its cochlear transcript assay uses human fetuses, while developmental in situ and postnatal localization use mouse and rat, respectively. The missense structural effects are modeled. These findings establish auditory relevance without specifying whether each pathogenic allele disrupts developmental differentiation, mature ion homeostasis or both ([PMID:18179891, Methods; Figures 1–7 and Table 2](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2253958/?page=0)). The inner-ear-development transfer remains unresolved at that mechanistic/stage level; this does not discount the human disease association.

Stem-cell population maintenance is retained as a contextual transcriptional contribution. The original mouse-cell rescue is positive, and independent mouse studies link Esrrb occupancy and transcription to self-renewing populations with culture dependence and Nr5a2 redundancy ([PMID:25522312, Figures 3–4](https://doi.org/10.1002/stem.1918); [PMID:34397088, Figures 1–3](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8451941/); [PMID:23040478, Figures 3–4](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3465555/)). The historical rescue insert is not sequence-mapped, and the human iPSC arm perturbs nucleostemin. Human ESRRB transcription assays support conserved molecular capability, not universal human stem-cell dependence.

For human transcription, Supplementary Figure S5b measures chromatin activation with trace HeLa extract; S5j measures DNA binding; S5k compares WT and DNA-binding mutants in transcription; S5l tests competitive inhibition by added TFIIHp52. The human and mouse panels are distinguished ([PMID:36646760, Methods and Supplementary Figure S5](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9892517/)). The independently read retinal original, rather than its abstract-only normal cache, supplies the DY131/4-hydroxytamoxifen and promoter-mutant evidence ([PMID:20534447, Results and Figure 1K](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2895124/?page=0)). The reporter insert remains unmapped to a particular human product. The [UniProt ESRRB FUNCTION record](https://www.uniprot.org/uniprotkb/O95718/entry) also records ERRE recognition; the specific human activity is grounded in the primary binding/transcription experiments already cited.
