TRPC4 subunit identity in the kisspeptin-evoked GnRH-neuron current — Hypothesis Review

Gene: TRPC4 (human, Q9UBN4) · Hypothesis slug: kisspeptin-trpc-subunit-identity Focus: free_text / core-function & function-assignment reasoning Seed hypothesis: Among TRPC1/3/4/5/6/7 co-expressed in GnRH neurons, TRPC4 carries the kisspeptin-evoked cation current because it alone combines the cSrc-phosphorylated tyrosine and the PIP2-interaction determinants that the published gating mechanism requires.

Executive Judgment

Verdict: Weakly / partially supported — the specific molecular-determinant rationale is REFUTED.

The residue-level comparison that the hypothesis requests does not do the work the hypothesis claims it does:

  1. cSrc tyrosines (TRPC4 Y959/Y972; Odell 2005, 16144838) are TRPC4-specific — but for the wrong reason. They sit in the hypervariable distal C-tail (not conserved in TRPC5/1/3/6/7), and they were characterised as trafficking / membrane-insertion determinants in EGF-stimulated COS-7/HEK cells, not as gating residues of a kisspeptin current. They are also present in both TRPC4α and TRPC4β, so they cannot explain the α-specific identification in GnRH neurons.

  2. The PIP2 determinant does not discriminate TRPC4 from TRPC5. Phosphoinositide regulation is the only common regulatory feature of all TRP channels (Rohacs 2024, 37871124). The PIP2-depletion-activated behaviour that the kisspeptin mechanism requires (Zhang 2013, 23744639) is shared by TRPC5 (Trebak 2009, 18665391) and is a property of the whole TRPC1/4/5 subfamily; the TRPC4-specific part of PIP2 regulation is isoform-level (α vs β; Otsuguro 2008, 18230622), not paralog-level.

  3. The two determinants are not a single coherent "TRPC4-only gating module." They live in different C-terminal regions and serve different roles (distal-tail trafficking vs α-insert / subfamily PIP2 gating).

  4. Pharmacology points to a heteromer, not a TRPC4 homomer. The native kisspeptin current is not blocked by 100 µM La³⁺ (Zhang 2008, 18434521), whereas homomeric TRPC4/TRPC5 are potentiated by micromolar La³⁺; native TRPC1/4/5 subunits form PIP2-regulated heteromers (Shi 2012, 21968068). This favours a heteromeric TRPC1/4(/5) channel.

Net: TRPC4α remains a plausible contributor (its mRNA is in GnRH neurons and its PIP2-depletion pharmacology fits), but it is not uniquely singled out by the cSrc+PIP2 determinants. The strong "alone combines the determinants" claim should not be used as curation-grade evidence. Most important caveat: subunit identity of the native current was never established functionally (only by blocker pharmacology + TRPC4α mRNA), so any subunit-exclusive statement is under-determined.

Mechanistic Scope

  • Immediate molecular activity being tested: gating of a receptor-operated, PIP2-depletion-sensitive, non-selective cation (Na⁺/Ca²⁺) current downstream of Gαq/PLCβ.
  • Direct gene-product activity: TRPC4α can form a PIP2-inhibited, PIP2-depletion-activated cation channel (recombinant evidence).
  • Downstream / inferred (not direct determinant evidence): depolarization and sustained firing of GnRH neurons, GnRH secretion, reproductive-axis activation. These are pathway/physiology outcomes, not proof that TRPC4 homomers carry the current.
  • Trafficking vs gating confound: the cSrc-Y959/Y972 evidence is about surface delivery of TRPC4, which the hypothesis mis-casts as a gating determinant.

Conflicts and Alternatives

  1. TRPC5 (closest co-expressed paralog): identical PIP2-depletion gating (18665391), shares the …VTTRL NHERF/PDZ motif (27994151) → equally qualified on the stated determinants.
  2. Heteromeric TRPC1/4/5 or TRPC1/4: native heteromers exist and are PIP2-regulated (21968068); the La³⁺-insensitive native current (18434521) is more consistent with a heteromer than a homomer.
  3. Isoform confound: the PIP2 determinant is α-vs-β (18230622), while the cSrc tyrosines are α=β — the hypothesis conflates two different "specificities."
  4. mRNA ≠ protein/function: TRPC4α was identified by single-cell RT-PCR (23744639); other subunits are co-expressed, and pharmacology cannot resolve subunit stoichiometry.
