CC   -!- FUNCTION: Homotetrameric, non-selective cation channel that is
CC       permeable to sodium, potassium, magnesium and calcium (PubMed:10517637,
CC       PubMed:11959145, PubMed:25820328, PubMed:27754867, PubMed:29425510,
CC       PubMed:30004384). Also forms functional heteromeric channels with PKD1,
CC       PKD1L1 and PKD1L3 (PubMed:23212381, PubMed:24336289). Pore-forming
CC       subunit of a heterotetrameric, non-selective cation channel, formed by
CC       PKD1L2 and PKD1L3, that is permeable to sodium, potassium, magnesium
CC       and calcium and which may act as a sour taste receptor in gustatory
CC       cells; however, its contribution to sour taste perception is unclear in
CC       vivo and may be indirect (PubMed:19812697, PubMed:23212381). The
CC       homomeric and heteromeric channels formed by PKD1L2 and PKD1L3 are
CC       activated by low pH and Ca(2+), but opens only when the extracellular
CC       pH rises again and after the removal of acid stimulus
CC       (PubMed:23212381). Pore-forming subunit of a calcium-permeant ion
CC       channel formed by PKD1L2 and PKD1L1 in primary cilia, where it controls
CC       cilium calcium concentration, without affecting cytoplasmic calcium
CC       concentration, and regulates sonic hedgehog/SHH signaling and GLI2
CC       transcription (PubMed:24336289). The PKD1L1:PKD2L1 complex channel is
CC       mechanosensitive only at high pressures and is highly temperature
CC       sensitive (PubMed:24336289). Pore-forming subunit of a calcium-permeant
CC       ion channel formed by PKD1L2 and PKD1 that produces a transient
CC       increase in intracellular calcium concentration upon hypo-osmotic
CC       stimulation (200 mOsm) (By similarity). May play a role in the
CC       perception of carbonation taste (By similarity). May play a role in the
CC       sensory perception of water, via a mechanism that activates the channel
CC       in response to dilution of salivary bicarbonate and changes in salivary
CC       pH (By similarity). {ECO:0000250|UniProtKB:A2A259,

CC   -!- ACTIVITY REGULATION: The non-selective cation channel is gated
CC       following an off-response property by acid: gated open after the
CC       removal of acid stimulus, but not during acid application
CC       (PubMed:23212381). Channel activity is inhibited by
CC       phosphatidylinositol-4,5-bisphosphate (PIP2) (PubMed:29425510). Non-
CC       selective cation channel activity is substantially increased when
CC       either the extracellular or intracellular calcium-ion concentration is
CC       raised (PubMed:10517637). Regulation of non-selective cation channel
CC       activity by external calcium is bimodal, first sensitizing and
CC       subsequently inactivating the current (PubMed:26066678).
CC       {ECO:0000269|PubMed:10517637, ECO:0000269|PubMed:23212381,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:26066678, ECO:0000269|PubMed:29425510}.
CC   -!- SUBUNIT: Oligomer (PubMed:25820328). Functional PKD2L1 homotetramer can
CC       be formed either through C-terminal trimerization followed by N-
CC       terminal dimerization of a fourth subunit with a subunit in the trimer
CC       or through dimerization followed by trimerization (PubMed:25820328,
CC       PubMed:30004384). Heterotetramer with either PKD1L1, PKD1L3 or PKD1;
CC       the heterotetrameric complex contains three PKD1L2 chains plus one
CC       chain from another family member (PubMed:23212381, PubMed:25820328).
CC       Interacts with PKD1L1, forming a ciliary calcium channel
CC       (PubMed:24336289). Interacts with PKD1L3, forming a cation channel that
CC       is activated by low extracellular pH (PubMed:23212381,
CC       PubMed:25820328). Interacts with PKD1; this heteromeric functional
CC       cation channels is opened by hypo-osmotic stimulation (By similarity).
CC       Interacts with RACK1; inhibits the channel activity possibly by
CC       impairing localization to the cell membrane (PubMed:22174419).
CC       {ECO:0000250|UniProtKB:A2A259, ECO:0000269|PubMed:22174419,

CC   -!- CAUTION: PKD1L3 and PKD2L1 has been initially identified as sour taste
CC       receptor in type III gustatory cells, but its role in sour taste
CC       reception is controversial (By similarity). Some data confirm this
CC       hypothesis in human: in 2 patients with sour ageusia that are
CC       unresponsive to sour stimuli, but show normal responses to bitter,
CC       sweet, and salty stimuli, expression of PKD1L3 and PKD2L1 is absent in
CC       the anterior part of the tongue (PubMed:19812697). However, a number of
CC       experiments have recently shown that the sour taste receptor activity
CC       is probably indirect; mice lacking Pkd2l1 only show partial defects in
CC       sour taste perception (By similarity). {ECO:0000250|UniProtKB:A2A259,
CC       ECO:0000305|PubMed:19812697}.
