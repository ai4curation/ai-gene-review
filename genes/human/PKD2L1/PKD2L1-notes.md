# PKD2L1 notes

Automated deep research could not be generated for this review because the configured provider was unavailable, so this note records manual curation context only.

PKD2L1/TRPP3 is a cation channel that can function as a homomeric channel and as a PKD1L1- or PKD1L3-containing heteromeric channel; this supports the cation-channel, ion-transport, plasma-membrane, ciliary-membrane, cell-surface, generic membrane, tetramerization, and specific alpha-actinin/transmembrane-transporter/cytoskeletal binding rows, while leaving ER, cytoplasmic-vesicle, water/osmolarity-response, and Smoothened rows peripheral and the signaling-receptor-complex rows over-annotated. UniProt summarizes sodium, potassium, magnesium, and calcium permeability, PKD1L3-dependent surface localization in the acid-gated heteromer, and PKD1L1/PKD2L1 ciliary channel evidence for ciliary calcium and Hedgehog signaling.

PKD2L1 is historically connected to sour taste, but the process rows overstate the current mouse genetics. Horio et al. found that Pkd2l1 knockout reduced anterior-tongue chorda tympani responses to sour stimuli by only 25-45%, while Pkd1l3 knockout and Pkd1l3/Pkd2l1 double knockout did not eliminate sour responses [PMID:21625513, "receptors other than PKDs would be involved in sour detection"]. This supports keeping the directly assayed pH-gated/cation-channel molecular functions, but downgrading `sour taste receptor activity`, `sensory perception of sour taste`, and broad taste-detection process transfers.

The root PTHR10877 membrane and calcium-channel IBA rows are safe for PKD2L1, as are the PTN002587230 sodium, potassium, calcium-binding, and alpha-actinin-binding rows. The family-root `detection of mechanical stimulus` IBA is not supported by the reviewed PKD2L1 evidence and should be treated as over-propagated.

The `GO:0005515 protein binding` rows on PKD2L1 record direct physical interactions but stop at a bare binding term. They should be removed under the repository protein-binding policy, with actinin-binding and channel-complex assertions kept separately where the evidence supports those more informative terms.
