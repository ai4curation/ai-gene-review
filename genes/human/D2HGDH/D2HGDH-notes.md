# D2HGDH biological evidence notes

D2HGDH oxidizes D-2-hydroxyglutarate to 2-oxoglutarate. The 2004 work combines purified rat enzyme with human full-open-reading-frame expression in HEK293 cells; the 2021 study uses human residues 51–521, with an N-terminal MBP tag removed and a C-terminal His6 tag retained. Both support the reaction, without identifying a unique physiological electron acceptor. FAD binds in the catalytic pocket, and zinc-containing substrate complexes plus mutagenesis explain the metal-coordinating active site. [PMID:15070399; PMID:15609246; PMID:33431826]

D-malate is not a weak incidental substrate in the human assay: its turnover is comparable to, and at the stated concentration slightly above, D-2-hydroxyglutarate. D-lactate turnover is weaker but positive. Meso-tartrate oxidation is reported for purified rat enzyme, including results not shown. These performed chemical reactions are retained as non-core processes; their physiological fluxes are not established. Neither L-malate/TCA-cycle chemistry nor an NAD-dependent decarboxylating activity follows from these experiments. [PMID:15070399; PMID:33431826]

Zinc, cobalt and manganese stimulate enzyme activity under the measured conditions. Magnesium and calcium did not stimulate the purified rat enzyme. These are biochemical observations; the GO response terms describe cellular or organismal responses. Three positive process transfers exceed the assays. The two original NOT assertions remain literal and unresolved at that broader process scope; the negative enzyme observations are preserved rather than reversed. [PMID:15070399]

The three official products are Q8N465-1 (521 residues), Q8N465-2 (307) and Q8N465-3 (319). Each sequence was reconstructed from the actual annotated splice edits and matched to its official FASTA. The short products omit much of the canonical C-terminal substrate/metal-binding region. This supports caution about equal activity, not a claim that their inactivity was measured. The predicted 1–13 transit peptide, Reactome's modeled 14–521 chain and the experimental 51–521 crystallography construct are separate facts. No original GOA isoform field was added or changed.

Its membrane-association classification is peripheral. These are actual target measurements; the matrix annotation column is separately imported prior knowledge. Reactome's actual Q8N465 catalyst supplies the accepted matrix model and 2-oxoglutarate pathway context. [PMID:34800366; GO_REF:0000052; Reactome:R-HSA-880007; Reactome:R-HSA-880009]

The mitochondrial IBA is not rejected for its donor count or human self evidence. The actual PTHR43716 tree and MSA place the 521-residue target beneath PTN000202300; the literal 2017-02-28 IBD has the same rat, yeast and human descendants as the source row. Complete current pages contain the relevant experimental mitochondrial assertions. The DPP8/9-inhibitor study provides much more than a proteomic association. Full-length D2HGDH is depleted; proteasome/cullin-pathway inhibition blocks the effect; removal of residues 1–51 increases stability and prevents depletion. Transferring those residues to CARD8-ZUC permits inhibitor-induced GSDMD cleavage and LDH release in engineered CARD8-null THP-1 cells. Nevertheless, native D2HGDH is the degradation target, and the fusion remains an engineered substrate. These results do not establish a native D2HGDH process of destabilizing other proteins. The existing process is marked over-annotated while all construct evidence is retained. [PMID:33053349, Results and Figure 4 caption; Methods cloning and limited proteolysis]

The two clinical activity sources with only complete abstracts available (PMID:16037974 and PMID:20020533) explicitly report impaired mutant enzyme activity. The catalytic function is independently secure, so their experimental annotations are retained with curator deference and without invented protocol details. PMID:15609246 differs: its cache is abstract-only, but the original PMC1196381 HTML Table 1 and radiochemical notes were independently recovered and inspected. Sequence-resource and clinical-context caches were also assessed for relevance; they do not add enzyme functions simply because they were downloaded.

## Final source-scope clarifications

Protein destabilization can involve a protein acting in cis; it does not require an effect on another protein. The earlier “destabilizing other proteins” wording is superseded by the distinction between performing a native protein-destabilization step and being degradation cargo. The positive N-terminal deletion and CARD8 fusion evidence remains preserved. It establishes an engineered transferable susceptibility element but does not establish the native D2HGDH process assignment. [PMID:33053349]

For PMID:20020533, the abstract reports mutations in 24 of 50 patients, rather than 24 distinct mutations or 24 individually expressed mutant constructs. All mutation-positive patients had impaired enzyme activity. Patients with normal enzyme activity had no detected mutations; the converse, that every mutation-negative patient had normal activity, is not asserted. This patient-enzyme evidence supports the retained catalytic annotation with curator deference. It narrows the earlier combined clinical-source wording: PMID:16037974 reports mutant overexpression, whereas PMID:20020533 reports patient measurements. Detailed original protocols for those two sources remain unavailable.
