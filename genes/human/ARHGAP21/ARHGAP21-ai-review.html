Gene Description

ARHGAP21 (also known by its former symbol ARHGAP10, which now belongs to a different gene) is a 1958-residue multidomain Rho-family GTPase-activating protein built from an N-terminal PDZ domain, a central pleckstrin-homology domain and a C-terminal RhoGAP domain. Its RhoGAP domain accelerates GTP hydrolysis on Rho-family GTPases using the conserved arginine finger R1184; reported substrates differ between studies and include Cdc42, RhoA and RhoC, and the preference appears to depend on cell type and stimulus. The PH domain and an adjacent C-terminal helix together form an ARF-binding domain that engages the switch regions of GTP-loaded ARF1, and this interaction recruits ARHGAP21 to Golgi membranes; ARHGAP21 is an ARF effector rather than an ARF GAP. At the Golgi it restrains Cdc42 to control Arp2/3-dependent F-actin dynamics and dynein-driven, microtubule-dependent positioning of the Golgi ribbon near the centrosome. A separate pool is recruited transiently to nascent cell-cell contacts, where ARHGAP21 binds alpha-catenin through a region in its C-terminal half and is required for alpha-catenin to be recruited to adherens junctions, linking it to junction integrity, epithelial-mesenchymal transition and InlA-dependent entry of Listeria monocytogenes. Its GAP activity is inhibited by direct binding of beta-arrestin 1 across the GAP domain downstream of angiotensin II type 1A receptor stimulation. The protein is widely expressed with highest levels in brain, heart, skeletal muscle and placenta, is extensively phosphorylated and binds 14-3-3 proteins, and is modified by SUMO2/3.

Suggested Questions for Experts

Q: Is ARHGAP21's GAP substrate preference intrinsic or contextual? No study has assayed Cdc42, RhoA and RhoC side by side with the same purified protein and the same assay; the two 2005 papers used different constructs and readouts, and the PC3-versus-LNCaP split in the prostate study hints that cell context matters. A single comparative kinetic experiment would settle whether the literature disagreement is a real biological switch or a methodological artefact.

Suggested experts: Philippe Chavrier, Anne J. Ridley, Sara T. O. Saad

Q: Should GO reinstate substrate-specific GAP terms, or should has_input extensions become the standard way to record GAP substrates? GO:0005099 (Ras), GO:0005100 (Rho), GO:0008060 (ARF) and GO:0005097 (Rab) GAP terms have all been merged into GO:0005096, which now has zero is_a children, so the most heavily curated property of the GAP families is no longer expressible. ARHGAP21 is a good test case because its substrate is genuinely contested and a curator would want to record both readings rather than choose.

Q: Should GO add a term for the PDZ-domain side of a PDZ/PDZ-binding-motif interaction? GO:0030165 'PDZ domain binding' models only the ligand side (binding TO a PDZ domain). Systematic PDZ affinity surveys now measure thousands of interactions from the PDZ-domain side, and those results are currently unrepresentable for the PDZ-containing protein -- ten of ARHGAP21's generic binding rows are exactly this case.

Suggested experts: Gilles Travé, Gergo Gogl

Q: Which PDZ ligands does ARHGAP21 engage in cells, and where? The holdup survey gives affinities for isolated fragments, and only one cellular ligand has been reported for the domain so far -- filamin A, in hepatocellular carcinoma cells, and the mapping is hedged as a 'potential mediator'. Whether the PDZ domain is occupied at the Golgi, at junctions, or not at all in a given cell type remains untested.

Suggested experts: Gilles Travé, Philippe Chavrier

Q: Does the alpha-catenin interaction require ARHGAP21's GAP activity? The alpha-catenin interaction region (roughly 1592-1861) is separate from the RhoGAP domain (1147-1339), so binding and catalysis are in principle separable. Testing the catalytically dead R1184A mutant for alpha-catenin recruitment would show whether ARHGAP21 acts at junctions as a scaffold, as an enzyme, or as both.

Suggested experts: Pascale Cossart, Sandra Sousa

Q: Would curators consider annotating PMID:16184169? It is a Nature Cell Biology paper whose title finding concerns ARHGAP21, and it has produced two GO annotations in all of GOA, both on its partner alpha-catenin and neither on ARHGAP21. Note that the paper calls the protein ARHGAP10, which is now a different gene's symbol -- a plausible reason it was passed over, and worth checking systematically for other genes that have been renamed into a symbol collision.

Current model (mechanistic narrative)

ARHGAP21 is a large multifunctional Rho-family GTPase-activating protein that couples membrane and adhesion signaling to actin cytoskeletal remodeling, vesicular trafficking, and tissue morphogenesis [PMID:17347647, PMID:23235160]. It is recruited to the Golgi by GTP-bound ARF1 through a bipartite Arf-binding domain comprising a PH domain and an adjacent C-terminal alpha helix, both of which engage the ARF1 switch regions PMID:17347647. At its catalytic core it inactivates Cdc42, and also RhoA and RhoC, thereby controlling actin dynamics in contexts ranging from dynein-dependent retrograde transport of Shiga toxin to the Golgi and surface delivery of influenza neuraminidase [PMID:19692570, PMID:22318733], to cell-cell junction stability and HGF-induced epithelial-mesenchymal transition, where it additionally binds alpha-tubulin to promote its acetylation [PMID:23200924, PMID:23235160]. Its GAP activity is dynamically restrained by direct binding of beta-arrestin 1 across the GAP domain following angiotensin II receptor stimulation, gating RhoA-driven stress fiber formation PMID:21173159, and the same beta-arrestin1/ARHGAP21/Cdc42 scaffold operates downstream of membrane-associated PTEN to orient the mitotic spindle during 3D glandular morphogenesis PMID:28749339. ARHGAP21 further acts as a scaffold for FAK and PKCzeta signaling to suppress cell migration [PMID:19268501, PMID:18662671], stabilizes filamin A by recruiting HSP90alpha through its PDZ domain to block FLNA ubiquitination PMID:41957357, and is regulated by SUMO2/3 modification at lysine K1443 PMID:22922005. Through RhoC inactivation it controls hematopoietic progenitor adhesion, mobilization, and erythroid commitment PMID:29212046.

1. Nomenclature: this gene used to be called ARHGAP10, and ARHGAP10 is now a different gene

Checked, and clean. reference_coverage.py queries QuickGO by reference for every

ARHGAP10/A1A4S6 is also not a member of ARHGAP21's PANTHER family (see §6), so it is
not a paralog in the phylogenetic sense either — only a symbol collision.
