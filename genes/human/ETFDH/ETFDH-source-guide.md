# ETFDH direct experimental sources

ETFDH accepts electrons from reduced electron-transfer flavoprotein and reduces ubiquinone. The experiments below distinguish this membrane-associated quinone reductase from the soluble ETFA–ETFB carrier. The human long precursor, recombinant host, native porcine structure and current shorter product are kept separate.

## Human enzyme chemistry and localization

- **PMID:8306995**, [original article](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1432-1033.1994.tb19939.x). Actual publisher-indexed original pages were read despite direct PDF access failures: Methods p. 280, Results pp. 283–284 and Figures 6–10 captions. The human 617-residue precursor was expressed in yeast after untranslated-sequence and codon-usage changes. Mitochondrial fractionation, active submitochondrial extracts and processing support the imported activity/location assertions. The succinate-to-ETF experiment through endogenous ubiquinone is reported as data not shown. Early membrane-spanning predictions are not adopted. This was partial original access, without figure-pixel inspection.
- **PMID:12049629**, [journal DOI](https://doi.org/10.1042/BJ20020042), [actual author-uploaded original](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/11323267_Expression_of_human_electron_transfer_flavoprotein-ubiquinone_oxidoreductase_from_a_baculovirus_vector_kinetic_and_spectral_characterization_of_the_human_protein). Read Methods pp. 660–661, Results pp. 662–664 and the relevant Discussion pp. 665–666. Human precursor expressed in Sf9 cells yielded processed enzyme with ETF-dependent quinone reduction and cofactor spectra. Its exact inner-membrane localization was inferred in this paper; the independent 1994 experiments supply more precise localization evidence. The apparent recombinant dimer is not treated as universal native oligomerization.
- **PMID:14640977**, [journal DOI](https://doi.org/10.1042/BJ20031272), [actual author-uploaded manuscript](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/8988907_Alternative_quinone_substrates_and_inhibitors_of_human_electron-transfer_flavoprotein-ubiquinone_oxidoreductase). Read the Immediate Publication manuscript dated 26 November 2003, distinguishing it from the final 2004 version. The enzyme-preparation/assay Methods and substrate-kinetic Results, Figures 2–3 captions and fluorescence-binding Methods support human ETFDH quinone reduction and binding. Menadione, duroquinone and DBMIB provide positive broader quinone evidence; their in-vitro behavior does not establish new physiological substrates. No figure pixels or supplements were inspected.

The tested coupled reaction passes electrons from octanoyl-CoA through MCAD and ETF to quinone. ETFDH performs the terminal redox step in that assay; it is not itself an acyl-CoA dehydrogenase or proton pump. This supports respiratory electron-transport and acyl-CoA-dehydrogenase beta-oxidation participation without claiming complete respiratory flux was reconstituted.
