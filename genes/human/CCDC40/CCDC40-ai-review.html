Gene Description

CCDC40 is a coiled-coil structural component of the axoneme of motile cilia. Together with CCDC39, it forms part of the longitudinal scaffold organizing the 96-nm repeat and the positioning of inner dynein arms and associated regulatory structures. Human respiratory-cell studies link loss of CCDC40 to disrupted axonemal periodicity, defective inner dynein arms and ineffective ciliary beating, while the outer dynein-arm repeat can remain intact. CCDC40-dependent organization also supports recruitment or retention of other axonemal proteins. Its ciliary role contributes to mucociliary transport and embryonic left-right patterning. Biallelic loss-of-function variants cause primary ciliary dyskinesia, frequently with laterality abnormalities; the functions of its individual alternative products are not separately established.

Core Functions

CCDC40 contributes the coiled-coil structural scaffold of the motile axoneme with CCDC39, organizing the 96-nm repeat and the docking or retention of inner dynein-arm and regulatory components. This physical organization supports ciliary beating and normal beat frequency. Human structures and mutant respiratory epithelia establish the target role; mouse localization and algal structural guidance are complementary rather than interchangeable evidence. The scaffold is not itself a dynein motor or an established active transporter.

CCDC40 manual research notes

CCDC40 contributes structure to the motile-cilium axoneme. The most informative synthesis is one CCDC39–CCDC40 structural unit that organizes the 96-nm repeat and the recruitment or retention of inner dynein-arm and regulatory components. It is not a dynein ATPase, a demonstrated active cargo transporter, or an extracellular protein merely because cilia move extracellular fluid. Human structural evidence supports refining the existing unknown molecular-function row to structural constituent of cytoskeleton, GO:0005200. No NEW annotation is proposed.
