Gene Description

DNAAF4, also known as DYX1C1, is a CS-domain and tetratricopeptide-repeat protein that supports cytoplasmic preassembly of outer and inner axonemal dynein arms. It associates with DNAAF2, other assembly factors and protein-folding chaperones. Tagged Xenopus ortholog experiments support localization to dynein axonemal particles, where dyneins and chaperones concentrate. Deficient assembly impairs motile-ciliary beating and causes primary ciliary dyskinesia, often with laterality defects. DNAAF4 also has context-dependent nuclear and neuronal localization; cellular experiments support association with estrogen receptors and CHIP, accompanied by reduced estrogen-receptor abundance and signaling.

DNAAF4 evidence and assay scope

DNAAF4 is the approved human symbol for DYX1C1 (HGNC:21493; UniProt Q8WXU2). Its central contribution is cytoplasmic preassembly of outer and inner axonemal dynein arms. Ciliary beating, extracellular-fluid movement and laterality are physiological consequences of that assembly work. The core includes binding to protein-folding chaperones, supported by controlled human HSP70/HSP90 associations. No autonomous motor ATPase or protein-folding reaction is assigned to DNAAF4.

Assembly and localization

Tarkar et al., PMID:23872636, selected Results and Methods, establishes dynein-arm defects despite preserved cilia and other axonemal structures. Human respiratory fractionation enriches DYX1C1 in cytoplasm; the paper's respiratory immunofluorescence of the target itself is mouse. Human outer-arm defects vary along the axoneme, with residual proximal arms in some samples. Full-length yeast two-hybrid and tagged HEK293 co-immunoprecipitation support the DNAAF2 interaction. The Results describe a mouse tracheal chaperone interactome, while the immunoprecipitation Methods specify mouse brain lysates; neither is a human tissue assay. HSP70/HSP90/CCT co-immunoprecipitation supports chaperone association, without establishing purified contact or an autonomous folding reaction.

Maurizy et al., PMID:29844425, Figure 4, Results and Methods, distinguishes DYX1C1 iso-a/DNAAF2 from iso-c/PIH1D3 associations. Its R2SD model contains SPAG1, DNAAF2 and RUVBL proteins and remains hypothetical; it is not the DYX1C1/DNAAF2 pair. PIH1D2 client-stabilization experiments are not transferred to DNAAF4.

Huizar et al., PMID:30561330, Table 1 explicitly lists Dyx1C1/Dnaaf4 in DynAPs and Table 2 reports its fluorescence recovery. The plasmid Methods identify Xenopus cDNA-derived fluorescent constructs. The normal publication text omits these table rows, which were checked in the original article XML. Human LRRC6 imaging in that paper is separate evidence and is not called human DNAAF4 imaging. DynAP is a membraneless cellular structure, so it is represented as a location, not a protein-containing complex.

The human HPA record independently reports supported cytosol localization. That evidence supports a cytosolic pool separately from DynAPs; the word cytoplasmic alone would not prove soluble cytosol. The broad cytoplasm annotations are refined to these two supported locations without claiming that every cited microscopy experiment resolved both.

Nuclear, neuronal and receptor observations

Taipale et al., PMID:12954984, localization Methods and Figures 2–4, reports human DYX1C1 in transfected COS-1 nuclei and nuclear staining in subsets of human neurons and glia. Ischemic human brain also shows cytoplasmic and neuronal-process staining, with preimmune controls. These positive findings are retained; the genetic association study is not itself a molecular-function assay.

Wang et al., PMID:16989952, Results and Figure 3, distinguishes rat neuronal RNAi/rescue from human full-length protein expressed in COS-1. The latter produces different N- and C-terminal staining patterns and approximately 24-kDa bands. Cleavage is proposed rather than mapped to natural isoforms, and the authors could not resolve endogenous brain localization. Selected original text was accessed through the author-uploaded article; the normal cache remains abstract-only.

Massinen et al., PMID:19423554, Figures 1–4 and the construct, co-immunoprecipitation, reporter and proximity-assay Methods, supports estrogen-receptor/CHIP association and reduced receptor abundance and signaling. MG132 blocks the measured receptor loss. Human expression constructs in COS-7, MCF-7 and SH-SY5Y experiments are distinguished from endogenous rat neurite proximity signals. The separately accessed author-uploaded original supplies these sections while the normal cache remains abstract-only. This is a secondary regulatory role, not proof of DNAAF4 ubiquitin catalysis, DNA binding or an endogenous neuronal signaling mechanism.

Current GO:0030331 is obsolete and advises nuclear receptor binding, GO:0016922, with the estrogen receptor identified as input. Imported term IDs are preserved, with the replacement recorded in review fields. Direction-resolved reporter and MG132 experiments support negative estrogen-receptor signaling and positive proteasomal protein breakdown, respectively.
