# BCOR review notes

## Biological synthesis

BCOR links DNA-bound transcriptional regulators to repression-associated proteins. Human BCL6 reporter experiments establish corepressor activity, and biochemical purification places BCOR with PCGF1 and Polycomb enzymes in PRC1.1. BCOR contributes to the complex's ubiquitin-transferase activity; RNF2 supplies the catalytic machinery. Partner composition varies with context, including HDAC-associated repression and a CBX8-containing complex in germinal-center B cells (PMID:10898795, PMID:16943429, PMID:26687479, PMID:27505670).

The human OFCD mesenchymal-cell study links BCOR to TFAP2A repression and restrained mineralization. Its ChIP assay shows regulatory-region occupancy, not a resolved BCOR–DNA interface, and its antibody does not detect the mutant BCOR protein. The IEA sequence-specific binding assertion also requires its mouse donor experiment to be resolved. The official MGI Bcor graph lists GO:0000977 with IDA and reference J:218799; the underlying assay and PMID were not resolved from the accessible reference pages. This is an experimental donor whose interpretation remains open, not an unsupported transfer. These two DNA-binding rows remain uncertain rather than being declared wrong (PMID:19578371).

The developmental rows describe contextual consequences: human OFCD clinical features and Xenopus laterality experiments are kept distinct. The original study does not demonstrate that BCOR directly activates PITX2C. Mineralization roles are supported by human-cell perturbation and rescue, but are non-core relative to the molecular corepressor mechanism (PMID:17517692, PMID:19578371).

Generic interaction rows are retained as non-core where the curated pair is corroborated by independent complex evidence or the exact normal UniProt interaction entry. Source-specific supplementary pairs were not re-audited. The three Q6W2J9-4 rows retain their tested isoform and do not imply isoform exclusivity. The AKT1 interaction remains undecided because the source studies mutant-selective interactions and the BCOR-specific wild-type/mutant comparison remains unresolved (PMID:25416956, PMID:35512704).

The heterochromatin-formation assertion is broader than the BCOR-specific evidence inspected in the PRC1-family paper; its compaction comparison addresses PRC1.4. Broad chromatin remodeling is retained because the official definition includes chromatin reorganization through histone modification, not only ATP-dependent nucleosome movement (PMID:22325352, PMID:16943429).
