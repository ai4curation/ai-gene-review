# FGF12 curation notes

## 2026-10-27 — first review

FGF12/FHF1 is primarily a sodium-channel regulator. Human FGF12B binding and functional measurements in [PMID:22705208](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22705208/) support the channel mechanism independently of the rat FHF1B donor experiments in [PMID:12401812](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12401812/). The 2016 variant study links disturbed channel regulation to developmental and epileptic encephalopathy ([PMID:27164707](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27164707/); preserved UniProt DISEASE lines 131–137, DEE47/MIM:617166). Neither the human channel nor a human host cell makes the rat donor protein human. Gating direction depends on the tested channel, product and variant.

The two short-product receptor-negative contexts need reconciliation with later direct binding and receptor-signaling results ([PMID:12815063](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12815063/), [PMID:32357892](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32357892/)). Mitogenesis, receptor activation and survival are distinct endpoints. GO growth-factor activity requires growth or proliferation; receptor-dependent survival alone does not settle it. The old TAS rows retain source-specific uncertainty even where a later study supports a related gene-level signaling role. The inaccessible 1999 full paper is not declared erroneous: official U76381.2 resolves its FGF12b identity.

FGF12-dependent nucleolar protein association is positive evidence, but the productive process it serves remains unresolved. [PMID:36411431](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36411431/) and [PMID:38468333](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38468333/) support interactions/localization, without isolating an FGF12-dependent ribosome-output step. The 2015 NOLC1/TCOF1 platform work ([PMID:26399832](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26399832/)) does not itself identify a FGF12 neural-crest experiment in the inspected material. A scaffold can participate in a process without catalysis; uncertainty here concerns the process served, not a requirement for an enzyme.
