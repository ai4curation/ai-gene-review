## Channel regulation and disease

**PMID:22705208** — the [cached original](../../../publications/PMID_22705208.md), Figure 3, Table 2, corresponding Results and construct Methods. The solved crystal complex uses FGF13, while independent calorimetry, co-purification and functional experiments use human FGF12B NM_004113 with NaV1.5. Those target-specific results support row 1's refinement and the channel-regulator core; no FGF12 crystal structure is claimed.

**PMID:12401812** — [author-hosted original](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/11061717_Modulation_of_the_Cardiac_Sodium_Channel_Nav15_by_Fibroblast_Growth_Factor_Homologous_Factor_1B), Methods molecular constructs, binding material and abstract. Rat FHF1B AF348446, human NaV1.5 and HEK293 cells are distinct components. The reported hyperpolarizing inactivation result must not be generalized to all FGF12/channel combinations. This supports ISS transfer on rows 41, 44 and 47. The NAS cardiac action-potential context remains non-core: no contracting human cardiomyocyte action potential was recorded here.

**PMID:27164707** — [original article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4898311/), indexed Methods, variant identity, zebrafish Results/Figure 4 and normal abstract. The Neuro2A channel experiment is distinct from zebrafish B-type ortholog overexpression. Complete electrophysiology Results were not independently retrieved; the preserved UniProt FUNCTION entry records curation of the same study, not independent replication. The study supports excitability/channel regulation, not a general inference from disease severity to every developmental term.

## Receptor-negative annotations and later positive evidence

**PMID:12815063** — [author-hosted original](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/6950318_Fibroblast_Growth_Factor_FGF_Homologous_Factors_Share_Structural_but_Not_Functional_Homology_with_FGFs), Protein Purification, Results Figures 1–3 and IB2 discussion. The human short FHF1b preparation includes C144A. Heparin affinity and binding to a murine IB2 fragment are positive. Figure 2 measures proliferation/antagonism, so absence of mitogenesis cannot alone prove absence of every receptor-binding or signaling event. IB2 binding does not identify a JNK-specific step; the discussion concerns p38 recruitment. Rows 0/38 retain NOT and P61328-2 with UNDECIDED reviews; row 27 is also unresolved, while heparin binding remains non-core.

**PMID:32357892** — [official full original](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12964-020-00573-2), Methods, Results Figures 1–3 and Discussion, plus the normal cached full text. Short human FHF1b binds FGFRs in direct assays and produces receptor-dependent signaling and survival responses in recipient cells. The study does not find a mitogenic response in its tested systems. This supports a bounded non-core signaling role while leaving GO growth-factor activity and the historical negative contexts unresolved. Construct and endpoint differences require explicit comparison.

## Historical and nucleolar sources

**1996 discovery and localization — PMID8790420.** The [original PNAS article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC38518/) was read from all eight scanned pages, 9850–9857. Human retinal cDNA supplied FHF1; mouse expression mapping and macaque immunohistochemistry are separate experiments. Methods p. 9851 and Results p. 9856/Figure 8 demonstrate nuclear accumulation of expressed human FHF1 in 293 cells and map amino-terminal targeting with artificial deletions and β-galactosidase fusions. Those constructs are not natural splice products. The text calls FHF1 244 residues while Figure 1 labels its sequence 243; no exact current-product sequence match is asserted. Figure 7 compares cell and medium fractions, with secreted growth hormone as a control: FHF1 was not detectably released under those conditions. This does not exclude secretion of another product or in another setting. Figures 4–5 establish mouse neural expression; Figure 6’s macaque antibody staining has acknowledged possible FHF-family cross-reactivity. Discussion p. 9857 explicitly proposes receptor signaling and developmental/growth roles for future functional testing. Nuclear localization is supported; the other historical source claims remain unresolved pending matched independent evidence.

**1999 heart-derived cDNA — PMID10049777.** The [published abstract and identity](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10049777/) were verified, but bounded publisher/public searches did not recover the [full article](https://doi.org/10.1006/bbrc.1999.0178). The official [U76381.2 sequence record](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/nuccore/U76381.2) explicitly identifies FGF12b, cites this PMID and records a July 1999 sequence update. Thus, the abstract’s 168-residue and FGF9-homology wording does not establish misattribution. The updated historical CDS is not silently equated with either current natural product. Heart-enriched expression alone establishes neither cardiac development nor growth-factor activity. Extracellular location and signaling assays remain inaccessible; the five source claims retain uncertainty.

**2022 nucleolar interactions — PMID36411431.** Relevant Methods, Results, Discussion and captions of the [full article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9677703/) were read. Methods, “Cells” (cached lines 100–102), specifies tagged a isoforms in human U2OS cells; recombinant-protein Methods use short-chain numbering without a resolved clone-to-current-product sequence join. Preserve row17’s imported P61328-2 context without treating every experiment as isoform-2-specific. Figure 1D–F supports nucleolar localization; Figures 2–3 support FGF12 association with NOLC1 and TCOF1. Endogenous HEK293 PLA, Supplementary Figure S4, is not isoform-resolved. Figure 4 and its Results show loss of NOLC1–TCOF1 PLA after FGF12 depletion. Discussion’s final paragraph leaves ribosome-biogenesis versus DNA-damage-response involvement unresolved. Abstract/Results partner comparisons differ, and Figure 4B’s caption says FGF2 despite surrounding FGF12 text; neither discrepancy erases the consistent FGF12 interaction evidence. Figure pixels and supplementary data were not inspected in this restart read.

**2015 platform biology — PMID26399832.** The [full article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4602398/), relevant Methods and all ten extended-data captions were read. Human embryonic-stem-cell and Xenopus experiments concern CUL3/KBTBD8 and the NOLC1/TCOF1 platform. No named FGF12 experiment was identified in the cached body; uninspected supplementary tables are not claimed to lack one. Partner functions cannot establish FGF12 neural-crest participation. Positive platform assembly evidence also leaves FGF12’s ribosome-biogenesis attribution unresolved.

**2024 galectin regulation — PMID38468333.** The [full original](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10926643/) Methods, Results/Discussion, Conclusions and all captions were read, with Figure 4–5 pixels inspected. Bacterial-protein SPR supports direct FGF12–galectin binding. In the inherited tagged human U2OS model, galectin-1 depletion changes three pairwise PLA signals and increases detected extracellular FGF12. Results’ final subsection/Figure 4 perturbs galectin-1, not FGF12; the earlier FGF12-depletion result is cited from 2022. Figure 5 remains a hypothetical model with unresolved nuclear function. A scaffold could participate without catalysis, but productive ribosome biogenesis is not established by these interaction endpoints. Supplementary PDF retrieval failed; its contents were not assessed.

## Database evidence and product scope

The [compact database evidence](FGF12-database-evidence/README.md) records the actual official ARBA, InterPro and PANTHER sources, their acquisition receipts, target-specific projections and a read-only verifier.

The three ARBA outputs are genuine target matches, not guesses from an FGF family name. P61328's FunFam `2.80.10.50:FF:000001` matches ARBA00028183 with Haplorrhini, ARBA00027428 with Homo, and ARBA00093068 with Primates. IPR002209 independently maps the canonical target to growth-factor activity. These traceable mapping mechanisms do not establish all biological outputs by experiment.

The four positive IBD joins have actual target ancestry support: leaf PTN002479516 lies below PTN000160075 and PTN000160077. The canonical alignment matches all 243 residues. The tree download was incomplete, but its received prefix contains the complete target path and leaf; the full MSA response was available. Only compact projections are included with the gene, so independent extraction requires the original captures identified by their hashes and URLs. No full-tree review, rereview of every descendant paper or function of both splice products is claimed.

The electronic nuclear mapping comes from UniProt SL-0191 with ECO:0000305. It is distinct from the experimental localization evidence in PMID36411431: tagged a-form constructs in U2OS cells and complexes in HEK293 cells lacking ectopic FGF12. The shorter product must not be assigned the same localization merely from the gene-level mapping. Cytoplasmic activity and nuclear localization can coexist in different protein pools and contexts.

Cell-cell signaling is assessed against the actual extracellular receptor-response evidence, including PMID32357892, rather than inferred from the absence or presence of a signal peptide. That paper's receptor binding and downstream signaling are distinct from mitogenesis. GO:0008083 specifically requires stimulation of growth or proliferation, leaving the broad growth-factor activity assertion unresolved. The two original P61328-2 NOT source objects remain intact; later positive receptor results require comparison of species, sequence, substitutions and assay endpoints before resolving their polarity.

Nervous-system development and neurogenesis remain non-core inherited roles. The target path supports the ancestral judgment; neural expression and altered excitability are not relabeled as independent measurements of neuron generation.
