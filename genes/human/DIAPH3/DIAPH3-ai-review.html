Gene Description

DIAPH3 is a Rho-regulated diaphanous-related formin that binds actin and promotes filament assembly. Its FH1–FH2 region supports actin filament growth and bundling, while terminal regulatory regions restrain assembly activity. DIAPH3 also associates with microtubules; mDia2 fragment experiments separate microtubule binding and stabilization from actin assembly. Human DIAPH3 occupies cytosolic and cytoskeletal pools and accumulates at the cleavage furrow and spindle midzone during cytokinesis. Regulated nuclear transport and membrane recruitment provide additional cellular contexts. Increased DIAPH3 activity is associated with auditory neuropathy. Multiple natural splice products differ in regulatory and C-terminal sequences, and their individual activities are not established by the historical engineered constructs.

DIAPH3 binds actin and promotes filament assembly through its FH1–FH2 region. N-terminal and C-terminal regulatory regions restrain this activity. An activated human construct lacking the C-terminal regulatory segment promotes filopodia and concentrates at their tips. DIAPH3 source and construct guide

Human DIAPH3 is UniProt Q9NSV4, HGNC:15480. Mouse Diaph3 is Q9Z207, historically called mDia2. Mouse mDia3/Diaph2 is a different paralog. This distinction matters for the RhoD-specific study below.

Two molecular functions

Actin binding and filament assembly form one integrated function. The PMID:18755006 author manuscript, Methods on DNA constructs and Results on filopodia induction, identifies a historical human 1110-residue construct assembled from BX649186 and BC048963. Its active deletion removes residues 1056–1110. Full-length protein is cytosolic in mouse B16-F1 cells; the active construct accumulates at filopodial tips, with human VA-13 cell corroboration. These are cellular experiments rather than purified current-human-product polymerization kinetics.

Microtubule binding is a separate function because it persists in actin-assembly-defective fragments. PMID:18458159(https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2364705/) reports controlled cosedimentation, saturable binding and protection from cold or dilution, including actin-defective and dimerization-impaired mutants. Its Methods identify historical Alberts/Wen construct provenance without explicitly resolving the template species. The current experimental donor annotation belongs to mouse Q9Z207; that curated assignment is distinguished from the unresolved exact historical fragment sequence. NIH3T3 host cells or a common construct provider do not establish template identity.

The author-uploaded original Methods for PMID:16556604(https://doi.org/10.1074/jbc.M510923200) explicitly identify mouse mDia2. The separately inspected Methods for PMID:15311282(https://doi.org/10.1038/ncb1160) describe residues 521–1040 but do not explicitly close the species link for the later experiment. Neither establishes equivalence to a current natural human splice product.

The full original PMID:19457867(https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2740432/), Results and Figure 1, independently localizes endogenous human protein at the HeLa cleavage furrow and central spindle. The engineered-fragment experiments are separate. The Human Protein Atlas DIAPH3 page, inspected 17 October 2026, reports supported plasma-membrane and microtubule staining with HPA032151 in A-431, U-251MG and U2OS cells. That localization supports refining the broad microtubule-cytoskeleton annotation to microtubule; the imaging itself is not a binding assay.
