# COXFA4 evidence notes

## 2026-10-12: accepted symbol and respiratory function

COXFA4 is the accepted human symbol for the protein historically called NDUFA4 (HGNC:7687; UniProt O00483). The 81-aa protein is a single-pass mitochondrial inner-membrane subunit of cytochrome c oxidase. Its central role is a structural contribution to complex IV and its terminal respiratory activity, rather than autonomous oxidase catalysis. The historical complex-I classification is superseded by affirmative later experiments; earlier cloning or copurification observations are not thereby erased.

The original Balsa study is available in an [author-deposited UAM thesis](https://repositorio.uam.es/bitstream/handle/10486/11203/55920_balsa_martinez.pdf?sequence=1), PDF pages 93–101 (article pages 1–9; thesis pages 85–93). The independent core consultation read its main Methods, Results and Discussion and inspected selected original figure pages. Endogenous human HeLa immunocapture, two shRNAs and interference-resistant myc-NDUFA4 rescue distinguish complex-IV effects from complex I. The human experiments and separate mouse mutant-complex comparisons are not interchangeable. [PMID:22902835](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22902835/)

Human patient muscle and fibroblast findings independently establish complex-IV dysfunction. Native gels retain detectable holoenzyme without abnormal subassemblies when COXFA4 is deficient. Thus structural/function participation is supported, whereas an obligatory assembly-factor role is not established. Detergent sensitivity depends on buffer and preparation: the patient study explicitly reports different dissociation thresholds from the earlier HeLa work. [PMID:23746447](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3701321/)

The human complex-IV cryo-EM structure resolves COXFA4 within a 14-subunit enzyme derived from a respirasome. The selected normal cache is partial even though it has a full-text flag; the bounded core consultation used the actual original Results/Methods. Human structural observations are kept distinct from separate porcine detergent experiments. A particular dimer-interface geometry is not a universal claim excluding all physiological oligomers. [PMID:30030519](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41422-018-0071-1)

The one core therefore uses contribution to cytochrome-c oxidase activity, in complex IV at the inner membrane, with the existing terminal cytochrome-c-to-oxygen and proton-transport processes. Both belong to the primary respiratory role, with proton participation limited to the structural contribution to the coupled enzyme. The inherited oxidase proposal remains an inherited NEW record; no additional process is introduced. The proton-transport annotation is retained at structural participation in proton-coupled complex IV. Its present electronic GO:0008137 premise is separately wrong: the literal current record does not infer from GO:0004129, and the source review explicitly records that failure. No isolated COXFA4 proton-channel assay or pumping stoichiometry is asserted.

## Disease and regulated abundance

The new human deletion report supports reduced complex-IV activity in patient fibroblasts while complex I lies within the measured normal range. Its clinical scope is kept separate from a molecular activity assay. [PMID:38674434](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11050323/)

Human-cell C15ORF48/miR-147 experiments provide direct evidence for COXFA4 abundance regulation, respiratory effects and context-dependent innate-response phenotypes. Selected actual Results/Discussion were read; detailed supplementary Methods were not inspected. The precise COXFA4-performed step connecting these effects remains unresolved, so no new immune-process annotation is manufactured from perturbation alone. [PMID:38917002](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11228508/)
