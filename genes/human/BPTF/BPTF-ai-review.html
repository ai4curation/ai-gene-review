Gene Description

BPTF is a large chromatin-recognition subunit of nuclear NURF complexes. Its C-terminal PHD finger recognizes H3K4me3, and the adjacent bromodomain recognizes acetylated H4, including H4K16ac in a coupled nucleosome-recognition mechanism. These contacts help recruit or stabilize NURF at regulatory chromatin. NURF can contain either SMARCA1/SNF2L or SMARCA5/SNF2H as its ATP-hydrolyzing motor; complex activity depends on the motor and its splice form. BPTF supplies histone recognition and regulatory interactions that support transcription at developmental and differentiation-associated loci. Historical FAC1 constructs also display sequence-specific DNA binding, transcriptional repression and regulated nuclear/cytoplasmic localization. Alternative splicing alters internal regions of BPTF.

Core Functions

The bromodomain-proximal PHD finger recognizes H3K4me3 and helps recruit or stabilize NURF on chromatin. This is the H3-recognition arm of a coupled PHD–bromodomain mechanism. BPTF contributes targeting and regulation to NURF remodeling; the associated ISWI motor supplies ATP hydrolysis.

Chromatin recognition and remodeling

BPTF is a regulatory subunit of NURF. Human purification experiments identify it with SNF2L and RbAP46/48. Tagged-BPTF purification and reciprocal complex isolation support membership and co-association; they do not prove that every subunit pair forms a purified binary interaction. The primary paper measures ATP hydrolysis and remodeling for the complex, while separate BPTF depletion and promoter-occupancy experiments support Engrailed regulation. The accessible neurite-outgrowth manipulation is SNF2L. PMID:14609955(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14609955/)

The source29 contributes_to annotation is preserved. Its proposed refinement to ATP-dependent chromatin remodeler activity describes the complex-level remodeling to which BPTF contributes; it does not assign ATPase catalysis to BPTF. The own molecular function is histone reader activity, supported by refinement of source5 rather than a redundant new annotation. The core combines this reader function, contribution to remodeling, NURF membership, nuclear location and transcriptional regulation as one mechanism. Official GO definitions and their parents were inspected: histone reader activity, ATP-dependent chromatin remodeler activity.

The 2011 study distinguishes peptide affinity from nucleosome recognition. Its bromodomain binds several acetylated H4 peptides; in the tested mononucleosomes, coupled PHD–bromodomain recognition favors H3K4me3 with H4K16ac. Full-length BPTF mutant ChIP at HOXA9 in HEK293 cells links the recognition module to chromatin recruitment or stabilization. Mutations in the pockets and their relative arrangement weaken localization. This supports reader/adaptor function beyond isolated histone affinity. It does not establish a universal preference across all nucleosome substrates or assign an assay peptide to a unique histone gene. PMID:21596426(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21596426/)

Later primary work provides useful context without becoming fabricated cached support. The 2024 study tests a human C-terminal reader construct and reports H3K4me3 combined with H3K14/H3K18 acetylation; it did not test the older combined H3K4me3/H4K16ac condition, so the results are not a direct refutation. Its enrichment/EC50 values are not silently treated as equilibrium dissociation constants. The 2026 study's accessible captions identify human PHD2 assays and structural work on combined H3K4me3 and H3K9AcS10p recognition; complete complex recruitment and in-vivo species assignments remain outside the inspected boundary. These papers are web-only contextual notes, not cached references or quote sources in the YAML.
