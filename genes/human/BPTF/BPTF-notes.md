### Chromatin recognition and remodeling

BPTF is a regulatory subunit of NURF. Human purification experiments identify it with SNF2L and RbAP46/48. Tagged-BPTF purification and reciprocal complex isolation support membership and co-association; they do not prove that every subunit pair forms a purified binary interaction. The primary paper measures ATP hydrolysis and remodeling for the complex, while separate BPTF depletion and promoter-occupancy experiments support Engrailed regulation. The accessible neurite-outgrowth manipulation is SNF2L. [PMID:14609955](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14609955/)

The source29 contributes_to annotation is preserved. Its proposed refinement to ATP-dependent chromatin remodeler activity describes the complex-level remodeling to which BPTF contributes; it does not assign ATPase catalysis to BPTF. The own molecular function is histone reader activity, supported by refinement of source5 rather than a redundant new annotation. The core combines this reader function, contribution to remodeling, NURF membership, nuclear location and transcriptional regulation as one mechanism. Official GO definitions and their parents were inspected: [histone reader activity](https://amigo.geneontology.org/amigo/term/GO:0140566), [ATP-dependent chromatin remodeler activity](https://amigo.geneontology.org/amigo/term/GO:0140658).

The 2011 study distinguishes peptide affinity from nucleosome recognition. Its bromodomain binds several acetylated H4 peptides; in the tested mononucleosomes, coupled PHD–bromodomain recognition favors H3K4me3 with H4K16ac. Full-length BPTF mutant ChIP at HOXA9 in HEK293 cells links the recognition module to chromatin recruitment or stabilization. Mutations in the pockets and their relative arrangement weaken localization. This supports reader/adaptor function beyond isolated histone affinity. It does not establish a universal preference across all nucleosome substrates or assign an assay peptide to a unique histone gene. [PMID:21596426](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21596426/)

Later primary work provides useful context without becoming fabricated cached support. The 2024 study tests a human C-terminal reader construct and reports H3K4me3 combined with H3K14/H3K18 acetylation; it did not test the older combined H3K4me3/H4K16ac condition, so the results are not a direct refutation. Its enrichment/EC50 values are not silently treated as equilibrium dissociation constants. The 2026 study's accessible captions identify human PHD2 assays and structural work on combined H3K4me3 and H3K9AcS10p recognition; complete complex recruitment and in-vivo species assignments remain outside the inspected boundary. These papers are web-only contextual notes, not cached references or quote sources in the YAML. [PMID:38319148](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38319148/), [PMID:42165139](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42165139/)

Human erythroid BPTF ChIP/sequential ChIP supports promoter chromatin occupancy. The genetic knockout described in the inspected associated experiments is Setd1a, not Bptf; BPTF is not a histone methyltransferase. The nuclear-damage study's longer authenticated supplemental Results explicitly include BPTF among recruited bromodomain proteins despite its ZMYND8-centered title. This supports nuclear localization without transferring ZMYND8's particular NuRD/repair mechanism. [PMID:27141965](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27141965/), [PMID:25593309](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25593309/)

### FAC1, localization and products

The FAC1 studies establish sequence-selective DNA binding, sensitivity of that binding to phosphorylation, and transcriptional reporter repression. The MAZ study describes recombinant association and interaction-dependent regulation in NIH3T3 cells. The KEAP1 study describes cytoplasmic colocalization and perinuclear redistribution in the tested cell context; association does not confer KEAP1 enzymatic activity on FAC1. These are retained as supported context-specific activities and locations. [PMID:10403843](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10403843/), [PMID:10575013](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10575013/), [PMID:10727212](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10727212/), [PMID:15379550](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15379550/)

Official figure text identifies GST-FAC1 residues 1–398 in the older binding assay. The NURF paper distinguishes a FAC1 construct from full-length BPTF; its caption numbering is not used to manufacture a current amino-acid/product equivalence. UniProt associates the original FAC1 sequence study with a region of BPTF and records sequence cautions. The current seed contains exactly products 1 (Q12830-1), 2 (Q12830-2, VSP_020402) and 4 (Q12830-4, VSP_020405). Their identities and sequence notes remain unchanged. No product 3 is invented and no legacy construct is assigned to one of these products without exact mapping.

Human developmental-brain and spinal-cord studies describe changing FAC1 nuclear/cytoplasmic/cell-body distributions. Localization in ALS tissue is not interpreted as a causal ALS mechanism. The transferred cell-body and perinuclear annotations are corroborated by relevant human observations; the exact rat-source dendritic and nerve-growth-factor-response experiments remain unresolved. Unspecified neurites are not automatically dendrites. [PMID:7621746](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7621746/), [PMID:9225734](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9225734/)
