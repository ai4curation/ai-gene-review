### Chromatin recognition and remodeling

BPTF is a regulatory subunit of NURF. Human purification experiments identify it with SNF2L and RbAP46/48. Tagged-BPTF purification and reciprocal complex isolation support membership and co-association; they do not prove that every subunit pair forms a purified binary interaction. The primary paper measures ATP hydrolysis and remodeling for the complex, while separate BPTF depletion and promoter-occupancy experiments support Engrailed regulation. The accessible neurite-outgrowth manipulation is SNF2L. [PMID:14609955](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14609955/)

The source29 contributes_to annotation is preserved. Its proposed refinement to ATP-dependent chromatin remodeler activity describes the complex-level remodeling to which BPTF contributes; it does not assign ATPase catalysis to BPTF. The own molecular function is histone reader activity, supported by refinement of source5 rather than a redundant new annotation. The core combines this reader function, contribution to remodeling, NURF membership, nuclear location and transcriptional regulation as one mechanism. Official GO definitions and their parents were inspected: [histone reader activity](https://amigo.geneontology.org/amigo/term/GO:0140566), [ATP-dependent chromatin remodeler activity](https://amigo.geneontology.org/amigo/term/GO:0140658).

The 2011 study distinguishes peptide affinity from nucleosome recognition. Its bromodomain binds several acetylated H4 peptides; in the tested mononucleosomes, coupled PHD–bromodomain recognition favors H3K4me3 with H4K16ac. Full-length BPTF mutant ChIP at HOXA9 in HEK293 cells links the recognition module to chromatin recruitment or stabilization. Mutations in the pockets and their relative arrangement weaken localization. This supports reader/adaptor function beyond isolated histone affinity. It does not establish a universal preference across all nucleosome substrates or assign an assay peptide to a unique histone gene. [PMID:21596426](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21596426/)

Later primary work provides useful context without becoming fabricated cached support. The 2024 study tests a human C-terminal reader construct and reports H3K4me3 combined with H3K14/H3K18 acetylation; it did not test the older combined H3K4me3/H4K16ac condition, so the results are not a direct refutation. Its enrichment/EC50 values are not silently treated as equilibrium dissociation constants. The 2026 study's accessible captions identify human PHD2 assays and structural work on combined H3K4me3 and H3K9AcS10p recognition; complete complex recruitment and in-vivo species assignments remain outside the inspected boundary. These papers are web-only contextual notes, not cached references or quote sources in the YAML. [PMID:38319148](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38319148/), [PMID:42165139](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42165139/)

Human erythroid BPTF ChIP/sequential ChIP supports promoter chromatin occupancy. The genetic knockout described in the inspected associated experiments is Setd1a, not Bptf; BPTF is not a histone methyltransferase. The nuclear-damage study's longer authenticated supplemental Results explicitly include BPTF among recruited bromodomain proteins despite its ZMYND8-centered title. This supports nuclear localization without transferring ZMYND8's particular NuRD/repair mechanism. [PMID:27141965](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27141965/), [PMID:25593309](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25593309/)

### FAC1, localization and products

The FAC1 studies establish sequence-selective DNA binding, sensitivity of that binding to phosphorylation, and transcriptional reporter repression. The MAZ study describes recombinant association and interaction-dependent regulation in NIH3T3 cells. The KEAP1 study describes cytoplasmic colocalization and perinuclear redistribution in the tested cell context; association does not confer KEAP1 enzymatic activity on FAC1. These are retained as supported context-specific activities and locations. [PMID:10403843](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10403843/), [PMID:10575013](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10575013/), [PMID:10727212](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10727212/), [PMID:15379550](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15379550/)

Official figure text identifies GST-FAC1 residues 1–398 in the older binding assay. The NURF paper distinguishes a FAC1 construct from full-length BPTF; its caption numbering is not used to manufacture a current amino-acid/product equivalence. UniProt associates the original FAC1 sequence study with a region of BPTF and records sequence cautions. The current seed contains exactly products 1 (Q12830-1), 2 (Q12830-2, VSP_020402) and 4 (Q12830-4, VSP_020405). Their identities and sequence notes remain unchanged. No product 3 is invented and no legacy construct is assigned to one of these products without exact mapping.

Human developmental-brain and spinal-cord studies describe changing FAC1 nuclear/cytoplasmic/cell-body distributions. Localization in ALS tissue is not interpreted as a causal ALS mechanism. The transferred cell-body and perinuclear annotations are corroborated by relevant human observations; the exact rat-source dendritic and nerve-growth-factor-response experiments remain unresolved. Unspecified neurites are not automatically dendrites. [PMID:7621746](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7621746/), [PMID:9225734](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9225734/)

### Specific, coupled reader activities

The original H4-partner binding object is refined to [histone H4K16ac reader activity](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/QuickGO/term/GO:0140046). A separately supported [histone H3K4me3 reader activity](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/QuickGO/term/GO:0140002) represents the PHD arm in the core synthesis. These sibling terms replace the generic parent core; they describe coupled arms of one NURF recruitment mechanism, not independent pathways. Remodeling remains a contribution to the complex, never intrinsic BPTF ATPase activity. Definitions and parent paths were checked against the official GO release dated 2026-03-25; unsuccessful live API access is not represented as an October refresh.

The [2011 primary study, PMC3135172](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3135172/) provides peptide, reconstituted-nucleosome and full-length BPTF mutant-ChIP evidence at HOXA9. Its Figure 6B links the paired recognition module to recruitment; genome-wide mapping used the reader cassette. H4K16ac preference depends on the nucleosome context, whereas isolated acetyl-H4 peptides show broader binding. The [2006 H3-recognition paper](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16728978/) provides structural and affinity evidence. The [companion primary paper](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16728976/), also available as an [author-hosted Nature PDF](https://lab.rockefeller.edu/chait/pdf/06/06_wysocka_nature.pdf), tests human BPTF recognition and chromatin recruitment; its Xenopus knockdown/rescue is a separately identified developmental experiment. The [2007 structural paper](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18042461/) changes methylation-state preference with an engineered PHD mutation, not a new wild-type activity inferred from L3MBTL1.

The immutable UniProt INTERACTION section separately records Q12830-4 contacts with H4C9/P62805 and H3C15/Q71DI3. This resolves those database product assignments, but does not establish that every historical reader assay used that product. The original GOA objects are not rewritten to add an unprovided isoform identifier; legacy FAC1 construct mapping remains distinct.

### NURF partners and developmental interpretation

The [2017 ISWI study, PMC5623870](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5623870/) supports endogenous BPTF association with both SMARCA1 and SMARCA5 through ATPase immunoprecipitation and reciprocal BPTF antibody precipitation. These are cellular coassociation experiments, with nuclease treatment and ATPase depletion controls. Residual nucleic acid and trace alternative ATPase are acknowledged by the authors; direct binary BPTF–SMARCA5 contact is not established by these assays. The recombinant double-affinity purification panel expressly excluded the 340-kDa BPTF protein, and the listed expression constructs also omit BPTF. Consequently, its purified direct-interaction, ATPase and nucleosome-sliding measurements cannot be assigned to an intact recombinant BPTF/NURF-5 complex. The normal HTML cache contains Table 1's heading and footnotes but not its cells. NURF-1 and NURF-5 composition/naming here is explicitly attributed to the immutable reviewed UniProt SUBUNIT record, not an inspected primary table. The UniProt statement comparing their ATP hydrolysis rates is not independently established by this recombinant panel and is not adopted in the biological summary.

The [2004 SNF2L splice-variant paper](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15310751/) shows in its cached abstract that the inactive SMARCA1/SNF2L+13 variant retains incorporation into multiprotein complexes. That abstract does not name BPTF. UniProt and the 2017 paper attribute stable BPTF association to this earlier study, but its original BPTF-specific assay remains uninspected. The +13 splice event concerns the SMARCA1 motor, not a BPTF isoform. These findings support separating motor competence from complex assembly without implying that reader activity guarantees remodeling in every associated complex.

The four pairwise binding objects previously left UNDECIDED are proposed as KEEP_AS_NON_CORE with independent same-partner corroboration and explicit curator deference. Original CERF-comparison, epidermal-study and OpenCell assay details remain uninspected; none is marked independently verified on the strength of another paper. Unlike a multi-partner aggregate with unknown additional edges, each object asserts the single preserved SMARCA1 or SMARCA5 partner that is corroborated. No ATPase, chaperone or catalytic activity is transferred from the partner.

For the existing brain-development annotations, BPTF promoter occupancy and reduced en-1 expression after BPTF depletion provide a mechanistic connection, while reviewed UniProt explicitly records the developmental role. The proposed non-core retention defers to the existing curator process interpretation; it does not claim an independently inspected BPTF whole-brain phenotype. [The original paper's Figure 7](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14609955/) distinguishes BPTF depletion from SNF2L depletion. Its [neurite-assay Methods, PMC275440](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC275440/) use SNF2L constructs. The unchanged positive-transcription annotation concerns a measured transcriptional endpoint; that distinction is retained.

NEDDFL (MIM:617755) provides relevant disease context. The [human haploinsufficiency study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28942966/) reports developmental/speech delay and frequent postnatal microcephaly, with separately identified zebrafish loss-of-function experiments. Its abstract and captions were read, not a complete clinical or experimental audit. This supports a dosage-mechanism question, not additional GO process annotations. The exosome HDA, rat-source dendrite and nerve-growth-factor-response objects remain UNDECIDED; nuclear identity alone does not refute an experimental exosome identification.
