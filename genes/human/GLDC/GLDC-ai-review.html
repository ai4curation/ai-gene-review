Gene Description

GLDC is the pyridoxal 5-phosphate-dependent P protein of the mitochondrial glycine cleavage system. It decarboxylates glycine and transfers the remaining aminomethyl group to lipoylated H-protein (GCSH). Together with aminomethyltransferase and dihydrolipoamide dehydrogenase, this reaction supports glycine degradation and one-carbon metabolism. The enzyme acts in the mitochondrial matrix. Loss of GLDC activity causes nonketotic hyperglycinemia, also called glycine encephalopathy.

GLDC (P23378) — review notes

Glycine dehydrogenase (decarboxylating), mitochondrial / Glycine cleavage system P protein
/ Glycine decarboxylase. HGNC:4313. EC 1.4.4.2.

Deep research: falcon out of credits (HTTP 402); no -deep-research-falcon.md. Review grounded
in GLDC-uniprot.txt, seeded GOA, and cached publications/PMID_*.md.

Core biology (verified)

  • P-protein of the mitochondrial glycine cleavage system (GCS): P=GLDC, H=GCSH, T=AMT/GCST,
    L=DLD. Catalyses the first, committed step of glycine catabolism.
  • PLP-dependent (pyridoxal-5'-phosphate) enzyme; PLP is covalently bound as
    N6-(pyridoxal phosphate)lysine at Lys754 (FT MOD_RES 754; ISS from P15505).
    [file:human/GLDC/GLDC-uniprot.txt "N6-(pyridoxal phosphate)lysine"]
  • Reaction: decarboxylates glycine (releases CO2) and transfers the residual aminomethyl
    moiety to the lipoyl (lipoamide) arm of the H-protein (GCSH).
    UniProt FUNCTION: [file:human/GLDC/GLDC-uniprot.txt "The P protein (GLDC) binds the alpha-amino group of glycine"]
    and "CO(2) is released"; "remaining methylamine moiety is then transferred to the lipoamide".
  • Mechanism paper PMID:24467211 (Go et al 2014, Biochemistry, abstract only): GLDC is an
    "unusual PLP-containing alpha-amino acid decarboxylase" that removes CO2 without releasing free
    methylamine; "aminomethyl moiety is instead transferred to an accessory H-protein"; the GCS
    "couples the decarboxylation of glycine to the biosynthesis of serine". Also frames GLDC as a
    metabolic oncogene, but that is tumour context, not the core catalytic function.
  • Homodimer; interacts with GCSH (By similarity). [file: "Homodimer (By similarity)"]
  • Localisation: mitochondrion / mitochondrial matrix. UniProt SUBCELLULAR LOCATION: Mitochondrion
    (ECO:0000269|PubMed:28244183). Reactome places reaction in mitochondrial matrix. HPA IDA
    (GO_REF:0000052) and HTP mito-proteome (PMID:34800366) both localise to mitochondrion.
  • Structure: PDB 6I33/6I34/6I35 (human, res 45-1020), PLP-dependent transferase fold.

Disease

  • Deficiency is the most common cause (~80%) of nonketotic hyperglycinemia (NKH) / glycine
    encephalopathy     (MIM:605899): neonatal seizures, encephalopathy, raised CSF:plasma glycine.
    Many NKH missense variants lose glycine catabolic activity (PMID:28244183; PMID:1996985 Phe756del
    abolishes activity in COS7).

2026-11-06: current interpretation superseding the earlier judgments

The earlier notes above are retained as a journal. Their categorical electron-transfer
and lyase objections, unqualified human homodimer statement, broad-process ACCEPTs,
and attribution of P15505 modulation evidence to rat are superseded here. P15505 is
the chicken donor. The current decisions are 14 ACCEPT, five TOO_GENERAL, one MODIFY
and five UNDECIDED, with no NEW assertions and one catalytic core.

Human expression supports the catalytic role: PMID:1996985(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1996985/),
“normal P-protein cDNA was expressed”. The specific glycine-cleavage process subsumes
the three broader metabolic/catabolic terms, equally across evidence codes. PLP binding
supports the catalytic core rather than forming a separate binding core.

The five uncertainties concern electron transfer, partial-reaction lyase classification,
human oligomerization and the two chemical-stimulus responses. Their evidence and limits
are recorded in the review and external evidence.
The structural deposits and published monomer result concern recombinant preparations;
the native human assembly remains unresolved. No human response process is inferred solely
from chicken enzyme inhibition/stimulation or from H-protein lipoylation.

Original annotation contexts, historical source/quotation identities and evidence codes
remain traceable in the context archive. No new research-provider
output was generated for this reassessment.

The earlier listing of 6I34 as human is also superseded: it is a Neanderthal
construct. Modern-human assembly comparisons here use 6I33 and 6I35.

GLDC external evidence and source limits

This evidence record distinguishes original observations from biological interpretation.
The normal cached publications remain unchanged; external originals were read separately.

Catalysis and reaction boundaries

PMID:24467211(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24467211/) was checked against selected
published Methods/Results reproduced with the article.
Human GLDC and H-protein were expressed in bacteria. Excess PLP supported H-free CO2
release; productive aminomethyl transfer used lipoylated H. The paper reports an active
monomer and no comparable stimulation by free lipoate in its preparation. Its methylamine
reverse-reaction experiment does not test inhibition. The normal cache contains only the
abstract; the external reading did not cover a complete PDF or every supplement.

PMID:2268343(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2268343/) remains abstract-only after publisher
access attempts. GO:0009055 includes enzymatic redox activity. Lack of a carrier role or
the abstract's genomic focus cannot establish a source error. GO:0016829 excludes oxidative
cleavage, but the demonstrated partial decarboxylation prevents dismissal of the lyase
assignment solely from EC classification. Both questions remain UNDECIDED.

Chicken donor and cofactor identity

The original P15505 record and its QuickGO annotations identify chicken experiments.
PMID:7440562(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7440562/) reports purified liver-mitochondrial
P protein, homodimer formation, PLP, methylamine inhibition and free-lipoate stimulation.
PMID:7440563(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7440563/) has no accessible abstract; JBC
full-text requests returned 403. PMID:3426593(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3426593/)
identifies the PLP-linked chicken peptide. Its peptide Lys-35 must not be mistaken for a
human residue number. In the actual family MSA, human K754 aligns with chicken K738 at
column 1271. Conservation supports homology, not a new target experiment.

The current GO:0036255 and GO:1903442 definitions describe cellular or organismal stimulus
responses. Purified-enzyme modulation and lipoylated-H dependence do not by themselves
establish human participation in those processes.

Human assembly deposits

6I33 and 6I35
are recombinant human GLDC deposits. Their polymer sequence exactly matches P23378
residues 45–1020 with an additional M prefix and GHHHHHH suffix. Author/PISA dimer
assemblies cite gel filtration and SAXS, respectively. Those method labels are deposited
provenance: underlying solution curves and conditions were not obtained, and the shared
citation remains unpublished. This supports a dimer model while leaving its relationship
to the published monomer preparation unresolved. 6I34 is a Neanderthal construct and was
excluded from the modern-human proof. Neither crystal-chain counts nor PISA alone were
treated as evidence of the native solution assembly.

Localization

The original MitoCoP paper and Table S1 include
P23378/GLDC in pool 1. Its subtractive, spatial and importomics classifications are each
class/cluster 1. This is organelle evidence; carried-over annotation columns do not turn
the experiment into matrix-specific localization.

The current HPA record
supports mitochondrial staining in human cell lines. Its additional uncertain nuclear
signal is not used to manufacture a new annotation. The current Reactome reaction
explicitly places the human GLDC catalytic entity in the mitochondrial matrix. That
compartment assessment is retained separately from the event's unresolved dimer model.

Phylogenetic evidence

Original PANTHER19 PTHR11773 tree/MSA/attributes and the retained PAINT IBD/IRD records
were replayed: 287 nodes, 157 leaves, 2010 alignment columns; human AN46/PTN002482993
matches the complete 1020-aa target sequence. AN0/PTN000206531 bears the catalytic and
glycine-cleavage IBDs; AN28/PTN000206533 bears the mitochondrial IBD. All are ancestors
of the target, and all three incoming donor sets match their actual descendant positions.
The on-path cytosol IRD does not negate the mitochondrial assignment. Human self evidence
is legitimate descendant grounding, and donor count is not treated as a weakness.

Source URLs, original-byte digests and target-specific checks are retained in
source evidence. The three broad process terms are verified
ancestors of GO:0019464; PLP and pyridoxal binding are distinct ligand terms.
