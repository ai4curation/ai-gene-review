Gene Description

CNGB1 encodes beta subunits of sensory cyclic-nucleotide-gated cation channels and the soluble glutamic acid-rich product GARP2. In rod photoreceptors, CNGB1 assembles with three CNGA1 subunits at the outer-segment plasma membrane, contributing to the pore, cGMP recognition, gating and channel targeting. Olfactory beta-containing channels assemble with CNGA2 and CNGA4 and couple cAMP signals to depolarizing cation currents under calcium/calmodulin feedback. Human CNGB1 variants cause recessive retinitis pigmentosa. The separate GARP2 product is inferred to restrain basal rod PDE6 activity from biochemical studies of its bovine ortholog.

Channel-bearing products and sensory signaling

Human rod preparations resolve three CNGA1 subunits and one CNGB1, with beta contributing a ligand-binding domain and part of the pore. cGMP drives opening; high cAMP partially activates the rod heteromer. The structural beta sequence is NP_001288.3 residues 456–1251, with full-length functional comparisons separately reported PMID:34699778. The older electrophysiology uses human HA-tagged beta with bovine alpha in Xenopus oocytes, not an entirely human receptor PMID:24164424. Actual bovine experiments measure sodium/calcium permeability, rather than relying on calcium block as proof of permeation PMID:10352032.

The human olfactory preparation contains two CNGA2, one CNGA4 and one CNGB1 and exhibits cAMP-dependent activation plus calcium/calmodulin feedback. It also uses residues 456–1251, an engineered 796-aa sequence PMID:41131016. Original rat olfactory cloning defines a distinct 858-aa B1b product and directly tests native-like sodium/potassium conduction and gating PMID:10377344. These assays support one contributed cyclic-nucleotide-gated cation-channel core, while retaining the original ligand-specific rows.

The four exact human products remain RCNC2B/Q14028-1 (1,251 aa), RCNC2A/Q14028-2 (623 aa), GARP2/Q14028-3 (299 aa), and Q14028-4 (1,245 aa). Official FASTAs match independent VAR_SEQ reconstruction. RCNC2A is not automatically the physiological olfactory B1b, and the engineered human construct is not any complete listed product. GARP2 enzyme inhibition

A separate GARP2 core and one NEW GO:0004857 assertion use ISS, human Q14028-3, bovine Q28181-5 and PMID:16407240. Actual native bovine purification, association and enzyme assays support suppression of basal PDE6 activity, with no significant inhibition in the tested transducin- or trypsin-activated conditions. Human activity is inferred; neither equivalent kinetics nor a demonstrated reduction in physiological human dark noise is claimed.

The original human GARP clone has the same 299-aa architecture and terminal splice sequence, with the documented R100H variant relative to current Q14028-3 PMID:7590744. Bovine and human products are not sequence-identical. The official enzyme-inhibitor term fits a direct, noncovalent inhibitory interaction. Its 61 typed descendants contain no suitable PDE6-specific child, and there is no ancestor/descendant overlap with the seeded terms. Human PDE6G is an informative same-enzyme inhibitor comparator; it is not experimental evidence for human GARP2. No new dark-adaptation, noise-control or other process assertion is added.
