CC   -!- FUNCTION: Pore-forming subunit of the rod cyclic nucleotide-gated
CC       channel. Mediates rod photoresponses at dim light converting transient
CC       changes in intracellular cGMP levels into electrical signals. In the
CC       dark, cGMP levels are high and keep the channel open enabling a steady
CC       inward current carried by Na(+) and Ca(2+) ions that leads to membrane
CC       depolarization and neurotransmitter release from synaptic terminals.
CC       Upon photon absorption cGMP levels decline leading to channel closure
CC       and membrane hyperpolarization that ultimately slows neurotransmitter
CC       release and signals the presence of light, the end point of the
CC       phototransduction cascade (By similarity) (PubMed:34699778). Pore-
CC       forming subunit of the olfactory cyclic nucleotide-gated channel.
CC       Operates in the cilia of olfactory sensory neurons where chemical
CC       stimulation of the odorant is converted to an electrical signal.
CC       Mediates odorant-induced cAMP-dependent Ca(2+) influx triggering neuron
CC       depolarization. The rise of intracellular Ca(2+) levels potentiates the
CC       olfactory response by activating Ca(2+)-dependent Cl(-) channels, but
CC       it also serves as a negative feedback signal to desensitize the channel
CC       for rapid adaptation to odorants (By similarity). Conducts cGMP- and
CC       cAMP-gated ion currents, with permeability for monovalent and divalent
CC       cations. The selectivity for Ca(2+) over Na(+) increases with cGMP
CC       concentrations, whereas the selectivity among monovalent ions is
CC       independent of the cGMP levels (By similarity) (PubMed:34699778).
CC       {ECO:0000250|UniProtKB:A0A8I5ZN27, ECO:0000250|UniProtKB:Q28181,
CC       ECO:0000250|UniProtKB:Q9NQW8, ECO:0000269|PubMed:34699778}.
CC   -!- FUNCTION: [Isoform GARP2]: High affinity rod photoreceptor
CC       phosphodiesterase (PDE6)-binding protein that modulates its catalytic
CC       properties: it is a regulator of spontaneous activation of rod PDE6,
CC       thereby serving to lower rod photoreceptor 'dark noise' and allowing
CC       these sensory cells to operate at the single photon detection limit.
CC       {ECO:0000269|PubMed:16407240}.

CC   -!- SUBUNIT: The rod cyclic nucleotide-gated channel is a heterotetramer
CC       composed of CNGA1 and CNGB1 subunits with 3:1 stoichiometry.
CC       CNGA1:CNGB1 channel binds Ca(2+)-bound CALM1 via CaM1 and CaM2 regions
CC       of the CNGB1 subunit; this interaction modulates the affinity of the
CC       channel for cNMPs in response to intracellular Ca(2+) levels (By
CC       similarity) (PubMed:34699778). The olfactory cyclic nucleotide-gated
CC       channel is a heterotetramer composed of CNGA2, CNGA4 and CNGB1 subunits
CC       with 2:1:1 stoichiometry (By similarity). Interacts with ANK3; the
CC       interaction is required for localization at the rod photoreceptor outer
CC       segment (PubMed:19299621). {ECO:0000250|UniProtKB:A0A8I5ZN27,

CC   -!- DOMAIN: The cyclic nucleotide-binding domain (CNBD) comprises three
CC       helices and a beta-roll of eight beta-strands from CNGA1 and CNGB1
CC       subunits. Upon cNMP binding transmits the conformational changes to the
CC       C-linker domain of the S6 helix to open the ion conduction pathway.
CC       {ECO:0000269|PubMed:34699778}.
CC   -!- DOMAIN: The ion conduction pathway consists of S5, S6 and pore helices
CC       from CNGA1 and CNGB1 subunits. It contains a central hydrophobic gate
CC       that opens upon cNMP binding. CNGB1 displays an additional charged
CC       arginine gate below the central gate to regulate ion permeation.
CC       {ECO:0000269|PubMed:34699778}.
CC   -!- DISEASE: Retinitis pigmentosa 45 (RP45) [MIM:613767]: A retinal
CC       dystrophy belonging to the group of pigmentary retinopathies. Retinitis
CC       pigmentosa is characterized by retinal pigment deposits visible on
CC       fundus examination and primary loss of rod photoreceptor cells followed
CC       by secondary loss of cone photoreceptors. Patients typically have night
CC       vision blindness and loss of midperipheral visual field. As their
CC       condition progresses, they lose their far peripheral visual field and
CC       eventually central vision as well. {ECO:0000269|PubMed:11379879,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:24339724, ECO:0000269|PubMed:28056120,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:29800053}. Note=The disease is caused by variants
CC       affecting the gene represented in this entry.
