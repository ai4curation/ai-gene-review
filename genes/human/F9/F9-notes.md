## 2026-10-25: core-function synthesis

FIXa supplies the protease chemistry of intrinsic tenase. The human FIXa peptide assay in [PMID:34502392](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34502392/), Methods 4.5–4.6 and Results 2.3, directly supports catalytic activity without added FVIIIa. It differs from the coupled FX-conversion assay in [PMID:2592373](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2592373/), which uses human FIX/FVIII, bovine FX and rabbit-brain cephalin; generated Xa cleaves the final reporter. The WT XIa-activated control is distinct from experimental rescue of Nagoya FIX. FVIIIa-dependent acceleration therefore does not imply FIXa lacks its own molecular activity.

Calcium binding and surface assembly are facets of this catalytic unit. The synthetic human Gla1–47/Fab structure in [PMID:14722079](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14722079/) resolves residues 1–45 with six calcium ions; it is not a native ion-occupancy count for every product. Reactome [158164](https://reactome.org/content/detail/R-HSA-158164) and [5607023](https://reactome.org/content/detail/R-HSA-5607023) supply the native human surface/catalyst context. The precise protein substrate is human [F10/P00742](https://www.uniprot.org/uniprotkb/P00742/entry), verified from its current official identifier, taxon and recommended name. This human pathway assertion is distinguished from the bovine substrate used in the inspected original assay.

One core records serine endopeptidase activity, intrinsic coagulation and the performed downstream zymogen-activation step. The two process terms are not ancestors of one another in current is_a/part_of closure. No biosynthetic process is assigned because FIX is modified or transported. Binding to FVIII domains supports assembly; XXYLT1 binding describes a substrate interaction, not an additional FIX enzyme.
