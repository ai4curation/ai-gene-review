Gene Description

CD79A (Ig-alpha) is a type I membrane protein that forms the signaling heterodimer of the B-cell antigen receptor with CD79B (Ig-beta). Its extracellular and transmembrane regions help assemble and express the receptor complex, while its cytoplasmic immunoreceptor tyrosine-based activation motif provides phosphorylation-dependent docking sites for intracellular signaling proteins. Antigen recognition is supplied by the associated membrane immunoglobulin; CD79A contributes signal transmission and receptor organization rather than intrinsic kinase activity. This receptor unit supports B-cell development, activation and survival. Loss-of-function variants can disrupt the pro-B to pre-B transition and cause autosomal recessive agammaglobulinemia. An alternative splice transcript lacks part of the extracellular region.

Biological synthesis

CD79A is the Ig-alpha component of the CD79A/CD79B signaling heterodimer. The human IgM-BCR structure directly places this heterodimer alongside the membrane immunoglobulin heavy and light chains. The heterodimer contributes assembly and signal transmission; immunoglobulin provides antigen recognition. CD79A's cytoplasmic ITAM is phosphorylated by kinases and supplies docking sites for signaling proteins, rather than performing kinase chemistry itself. PMID:35981043(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35981043/), Reactome:R-HSA-983700.

Human surface-expression experiments support the requirement for intact receptor assembly. The study includes tonsillar B cells and lymphoma cell lines; loss of CD79A or CD79B removes surface IgM, while consequences for fitness differ among models. Its surface-staining methods were accessible beyond the abstract. This should not become a universal claim that every CD79A-deficient cell dies immediately. PMID:36426942(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36426942/).

The human agammaglobulinemia study identifies a splice defect disrupting the membrane receptor subunit and a block at the pro-B to pre-B transition. Receptor adaptor mechanism — follow-up 2026-10-09

CD79A couples the antigen-recognition module to cytoplasmic signaling by providing a physical receptor-associated platform for signaling proteins. The phosphorylated ITAM recruits SYK. A separate phosphorylated site outside the ITAM recruits BLNK, positioning this adaptor to link receptor-proximal kinase activity to downstream pathways. The direct recruitment study reports that "BLNK bound directly to phospho-Y204" and that fusion of BLNK to a mutant Ig-alpha tail restored downstream signaling. The site number is the study's numbering; the human UniProt record identifies the corresponding BLNK site as Tyr210 by similarity. These are distinct docking sites, and the non-ITAM BLNK experiment is not recast as an ITAM-binding assay. PMID:11909947(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11909947/), Reactome:R-HSA-983700.

The BLNK study was assessed from its primary abstract; its full Methods were not accessible, so construct/cell species is not independently established here. A separate biochemical study directly tests CD79A phospho-ITAM binding to purified human SYK tandem-SH2 domains. The selected Results and Methods include target-specific TR-FRET and phosphopeptide competition, with nonphosphorylated controls. This supports the ITAM docking arm. CD79A was part of the binding panel, not one of the three solved phosphopeptide structures, and these purified components are distinct from native-cell signaling measurements. PMID:39442513(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39442513/).

This mechanism supports signaling receptor complex adaptor activity, GO:0030159, alongside the existing contribution to transmembrane signaling receptor activity in the same core unit.
