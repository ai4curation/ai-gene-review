| Identity and receptor class | **TLR9 (CD289; UniProt Q9NR96)** is a type-I single-pass transmembrane glycoprotein and intracellular pattern-recognition receptor in the Toll-like receptor family. It is a signaling receptor, not an enzyme or transporter. | The requested identity is human TLR9; mechanistic findings from mouse systems are identified separately below. | (pqac-00000008, pqac-00000009) |
| Overall architecture | An N-terminal luminal ectodomain is followed by one transmembrane helix and a cytoplasmic C-terminal Toll/interleukin-1 receptor (**TIR**) domain of approximately 150 residues. | This architecture is conserved across mammalian TLR9 orthologs. | (pqac-00000008, pqac-00000009) |
| Leucine-rich-repeat ectodomain | The horseshoe-shaped ectodomain contains approximately **25–26 leucine-rich repeats (LRRs)**; differing counts reflect annotation conventions. The LRR surface binds DNA and supports ligand-dependent receptor assembly. | The LRR architecture agrees with the supplied InterPro annotations: Leu-rich repeats, typical LRR subtype, LRR_12, and LRR superfamily. | (pqac-00000008, pqac-00000009, pqac-00000014) |
| Z-loop and proteolytic maturation | An inserted **Z-loop between LRR14 and LRR15** is cleaved by endolysosomal proteases. Proteolytic processing generates signaling-competent TLR9 and helps restrict activation to acidic intracellular compartments. | Demonstrated extensively in mammalian experimental systems; cleavage is a key safeguard against inappropriate recognition of cellular DNA. | (pqac-00000008, pqac-00000009, pqac-00000016) |
| TIR signaling domain | Ligand-induced receptor assembly brings cytoplasmic TIR domains together, permitting recruitment of the adaptor **MyD88** and formation of the downstream signalosome. | The TIR domain is consistent with the supplied IPR000157 annotation. | (pqac-00000009, pqac-00000011) |
| Primary ligand | TLR9 preferentially recognizes DNA containing **unmethylated CpG dinucleotides**, which are enriched in many bacterial and viral genomes relative to vertebrate nuclear DNA. It can also respond to appropriately delivered mitochondrial or nuclear self-DNA. | CpG methylation is an important but incomplete self/non-self discriminator; sequence, length, backbone chemistry, structure, uptake, nuclease processing, and endosomal delivery also determine activity. | (pqac-00000002, pqac-00000004, pqac-00000007) |
| Human ligand preference | A commonly reported optimal human stimulatory hexamer is **5′-GTCGTT-3′**. Human TLR9 generally responds best when two CpG motifs are positioned appropriately within an oligonucleotide. | Human sequence preferences must not be inferred directly from mouse CpG-ODN studies. | (pqac-00000002, pqac-00000008) |
| Mouse ligand preference | A commonly reported murine stimulatory hexamer is **5′-GACGTT-3′**; mouse TLR9 can be activated efficiently by a single CpG motif located approximately 4–6 nucleotides from the 5′ end in some experimental contexts. | This species difference explains why mouse CpG reagents such as ODN1826 are not automatically optimal for human TLR9. | (pqac-00000002, pqac-00000008) |
| DNA-binding mechanism | Agonistic DNA bridges two ectodomains in a symmetric **2:2 TLR9–DNA complex**. DNA contacts the N-terminal LRR region of one protomer and C-terminal LRRs of the other, functioning as molecular glue that stabilizes the active dimer. | Structural descriptions derive largely from crystallographic and biochemical studies of mammalian TLR9 ectodomains. | (pqac-00000000, pqac-00000013, pqac-00000014) |
| Resting localization | Newly synthesized TLR9 is folded, glycosylated, and retained initially in the **endoplasmic reticulum (ER)** rather than being constitutively exposed at the cell surface. | Surface TLR9 has nevertheless been reported on selected cells, including neutrophils, B cells, and erythrocytes; this is distinct from canonical signaling by endosomal TLR9. | (pqac-00000018, pqac-00000021, pqac-00000064) |
| Secretory-pathway transit | TLR9 exits the ER in **COPII-positive vesicles**, transits the **Golgi**, and is sorted toward endosomal compartments. | TMED2, gp96, PRAT4A, and other biosynthetic factors can contribute, but UNC93B1 is the central dedicated trafficking chaperone. | (pqac-00000018, pqac-00000021) |
| Functional localization | DNA recognition and canonical signaling occur primarily in **acidified endosomes and endolysosomes/lysosomes**, where ligand processing and TLR9 proteolytic maturation take place. | Spatial restriction reduces inappropriate activation by abundant extracellular or intracellular self-DNA. | (pqac-00000016, pqac-00000019, pqac-00000023) |
| UNC93B1 | **UNC93B1** binds TLR9 in the ER, enables ER exit and endosomal delivery, and is released from TLR9 in endolysosomes before productive signaling. UNC93B1 glycosylation at **Asn272** is required for efficient TLR9–MyD88 recruitment without necessarily changing receptor localization. | The Asn272 glycosylation requirement was selective for TLR9 over TLR7 in the cited 2022 study. | (pqac-00000019, pqac-00000035) |
| AP-2 | UNC93B1 recruits the clathrin adaptor **AP-2** through a C-terminal YxxF sorting motif, supporting internalization and delivery of TLR9 to endosomes. | Much of the detailed genetic evidence comes from murine macrophage and dendritic-cell systems. | (pqac-00000018, pqac-00000019, pqac-00000035) |
| AP-3 | **AP-3** promotes progression from early endosomes through late endosomes to LAMP2-positive lysosomal compartments and helps switch output from early NF-κB signaling toward later IRF7/type-I-interferon signaling. | Loss of AP-3 can reduce interferon production while increasing inflammatory-cytokine-biased signaling. | (pqac-00000032, pqac-00000034) |
| SLC15A4 | **SLC15A4** supports delivery or retention of TLR9 and CpG ligand in specialized LAMP2-positive endolysosomal compartments, especially in plasmacytoid dendritic cells. | Loss-of-function reduces receptor–ligand colocalization and TLR9 responses without necessarily abolishing receptor cleavage. | (pqac-00000023) |
| S-palmitoylation | A 2024 study mapped murine TLR9 S-palmitoylation to **C258 and C265** in an ectodomain loop. Golgi-localized **DHHC3** promotes palmitoylation, facilitating Golgi-to-endosome trafficking, cleavage, ligand binding, and signaling. Human TLR9 was also shown to be palmitoylated. | C258/C265 are experimentally mapped murine sites; equivalent site-specific assignment in endogenous human TLR9 should not be assumed without direct confirmation. | (pqac-00000055, pqac-00000057, pqac-00000060) |
| Depalmitoylation | Lysosomal **PPT1** removes palmitate after endosomal delivery, promoting release of TLR9 from UNC93B1. Blocking either palmitoylation or depalmitoylation diminishes productive signaling. | PPT1 inhibition reduced IFN-α from human and mouse plasmacytoid dendritic cells ex vivo, whereas lupus-disease improvement was demonstrated in mice. | (pqac-00000055, pqac-00000057, pqac-00000061) |
| Tyrosine phosphorylation | A 2025 study reported sequential phosphorylation of cytoplasmic **Y870 by Syk** and **Y980 by EGFR**. Mutation of either site abolished cytokine induction and impaired TLR9–MyD88 coupling. Upstream scavenger receptor A activates Lyn and then Syk. | This is a recent mechanistic extension beyond the established canonical model and should be interpreted in the studied cellular systems pending broader validation in primary human cells. | (pqac-00000048, pqac-00000049, pqac-00000051) |
| MyD88 and IRAKs | Activated TLR9 recruits **MyD88**, which assembles a Myddosome containing **IRAK4 and IRAK1**. This is the obligatory core of canonical TLR9 signaling. | Unlike TLR3, TLR9 is principally MyD88-dependent rather than TRIF-dependent. | (pqac-00000025, pqac-00000029) |
| TRAF and TAK1 branch | IRAK activation recruits **TRAF6** and, for interferon-associated signaling, TRAF3. TRAF6 activates **TAK1**, which feeds into NF-κB and MAPK pathways. | Output depends on cell type and endosomal compartment. | (pqac-00000026, pqac-00000029) |
| NF-κB and MAPK/AP-1 outputs | TAK1 activates the IKK–IκB–**NF-κB** axis and MAPKs including p38/JNK, culminating in **AP-1** activation and transcription of inflammatory mediators such as TNF, IL-6, IL-1-family cytokines, IL-12, and chemokines. | Cytokine composition varies by ligand class, cell type, dose, and trafficking route. | (pqac-00000024, pqac-00000026, pqac-00000030) |
| IRF7/type-I-interferon output | In plasmacytoid dendritic cells, a TRAF3/IKKα-associated branch activates **IRF7**, producing large amounts of type-I interferon, especially **IFN-α**. | This response is strongest when ligand reaches the appropriate IRF-signaling endolysosomal compartment; other compartments favor NF-κB-biased output. | (pqac-00000028, pqac-00000029, pqac-00000034) |
