Gene Description

ACAN encodes aggrecan, the large secreted chondroitin-sulfate proteoglycan that gives cartilage its ability to bear load. The core protein is built from three globular regions: an N-terminal G1 (an immunoglobulin domain followed by two Link modules) that binds the glycosaminoglycan hyaluronan, a structurally similar G2, and a C-terminal G3 carrying EGF, C-type lectin and complement-control modules. Between G2 and G3 lies a large region substituted with roughly a hundred chondroitin sulfate chains and a smaller number of keratan sulfate chains. Chondrocytes secrete aggrecan into the cartilage matrix, where many monomers dock onto a single hyaluronan filament through G1, each junction clamped by a hyaluronan and proteoglycan link protein, producing supramolecular aggregates in excess of 200 MDa. The dense fixed negative charge of the glycosaminoglycan chains draws in water; the resulting swelling pressure, restrained by the surrounding collagen II network, is what allows cartilage to resist compression and to recover its shape after load. The G3 C-type lectin adds a second layer of matrix integration, binding tenascins, fibulins, matrilins and cartilage oligomeric matrix protein. A distinct pool of aggrecan is secreted in the central nervous system, where it is the lectican most characteristic of perineuronal nets, the honeycomb-like specialized matrix that encloses parvalbumin-positive interneurons and stabilizes their synapses once developmental plasticity has closed. Aggrecan is a prominent protease substrate: cleavage by the ADAMTS aggrecanases and by matrix metalloproteinases within the interglobular region between G1 and G2 releases the glycosaminoglycan-bearing bulk of the molecule while leaving the G1 fragment anchored to hyaluronan, and this loss underlies matrix degradation in osteoarthritis and inflammatory arthritis. Human variants span a dosage series: complete loss produces lethal chondrodysplasia, while heterozygous truncating and G3 missense alleles cause spondyloepiphyseal and spondyloepimetaphyseal dysplasias, short stature with advanced bone age, early osteoarthritis and familial osteochondritis dissecans.

Proposed New Ontology Terms

cartilage extracellular matrix

Definition: A specialized extracellular matrix produced by chondrocytes, consisting of a collagen type II fibril network interpenetrated by a hydrated gel of hyaluronan-bound aggregating proteoglycan, and characterised by high fixed negative charge density and resistance to compressive load.

Justification: GO describes cartilage extensively as a process but has no cellular component for the matrix itself. There are more than thirty cartilage biological-process terms (cartilage development GO:0051216, cartilage morphogenesis GO:0060536, cartilage condensation GO:0001502, articular cartilage development GO:0061975, growth plate cartilage development GO:0003417, cartilage homeostasis GO:1990079, and so on), yet the only cellular components available for the tissue that all of those processes build are the generic extracellular matrix GO:0031012 or, worse, gel phase of interstitial matrix GO:0140150, which is scoped under interstitial matrix GO:0005614 and therefore describes interstitial connective tissue rather than cartilage. GO:0140047 specialized extracellular matrix already carries siblings of exactly the right kind - perineuronal net GO:0072534, interphotoreceptor matrix GO:0033165, hyaline layer GO:0033166, cuticular extracellular matrix GO:0060102, egg coat GO:0035805 - and cartilage matrix is arguably the best-characterised specialized extracellular matrix in vertebrate biology, so its absence looks like an oversight rather than a deliberate exclusion. The gap bites hardest on exactly the genes it should serve best. Aggrecan is the defining constituent of cartilage matrix, and there is no term that says so, which is why its GOA record ends up with a misscoped interstitial-matrix term. The same is true of COL2A1, COL9A1, COL11A1, COMP, MATN1, MATN3, HAPLN1, CHAD, PRG4 and the ADAMTS aggrecanases, all of which currently have to be located in the generic parent. A cartilage-specific component term would also let the two ends of aggrecan's biology be told apart, since the same protein sits in cartilage matrix and in the perineuronal net, and only one of those two locations is currently expressible.

Core Functions

Hyaluronan binding through the N-terminal G1 region, the activity that makes aggrecan an aggregating proteoglycan. G1 comprises an immunoglobulin domain and two tandem Link modules that act as one structural unit, with the hyaluronan chain clamped in a groove running the length of the tandem Link domains at an affinity of roughly 230 nM. Many aggrecan monomers load onto a single hyaluronan filament, each junction stabilised by a hyaluronan and proteoglycan link protein, and the resulting ternary complex immobilises aggrecan into aggregates exceeding 200 MDa. This is the assembly step that builds both the cartilage matrix gel and the perineuronal net; in the perineuronal net it is important but not strictly required, since hyaluronan-binding-null mutants still incorporate, less efficiently.

Suggested Questions for Experts

Q: What is the hyaluronan-independent activity that recruits aggrecan into perineuronal nets? Point mutations that abolish hyaluronan binding entirely still allow aggrecan to incorporate into perineuronal nets, only less efficiently, which means the long-standing model in which the hyaluronan-lectican-link protein ternary complex is the sole anchor is incomplete. Identifying the second binding partner would settle whether the cartilage and central nervous system pools of aggrecan assemble by the same mechanism, and it would supply the molecular function term that the perineuronal net localisation currently lacks.

Q: Should aggrecan carry a biological process term for building the matrix? After removing proteolysis and cell adhesion, the only process terms left in GOA are two broad development terms, and nothing in the record states that aggrecan assembles the matrix it is a constituent of, even though the aggregation event is the whole reason the protein exists. Extracellular matrix assembly GO:0085029 and extracellular matrix organization GO:0030198 both exist; whether a structural constituent that self-assembles with hyaluronan and a link protein should be annotated to them, or whether those terms are reserved for the enzymes and receptors that remodel matrix, is a modelling decision GO should make explicitly rather than by omission.

Q: How should a protein that is a major protease substrate be annotated? Aggrecanase cleavage of the interglobular domain is one of the best-characterised proteolytic events in human pathology and is the mechanistic core of cartilage loss in osteoarthritis, but the only way it currently appears on ACAN is as an incorrect involved_in proteolysis annotation. GO has no substrate role to assign, so curators either state the relationship wrongly, as here, or leave a central piece of the gene's biology unrecorded.

Q: Is the vascular pool of aggrecan functionally significant, or incidental? Aggrecan has been identified by proteomics in human aorta and saphenous vein, is depleted in varicose veins at both protein and transcript level, and yet UniProt still describes its tissue distribution as restricted to cartilage. Whether vascular aggrecan contributes to vessel wall mechanics determines whether the interstitial-matrix localisation terms are appropriate for this protein at all.

Q: Which of aggrecan's three globular regions should each annotation be attributed to? The hyaluronan binding is G1, the matrix ligand binding is the G3 C-type lectin, the compression resistance is the chondroitin sulfate region between G2 and G3, and G2 has no assigned function despite Link modules nearly identical to those of G1. Human disease alleles separate these functions cleanly - interglobular repeat variants, truncations and G3 missense variants give distinguishable phenotypes - so region-resolved annotation is achievable and would make the record far more useful.

Current model (mechanistic narrative)

ACAN encodes aggrecan, a large extracellular matrix proteoglycan that anchors into cartilage matrix and into CNS perineuronal nets (PNNs) through its N-terminal G1 region, whose immunoglobulin domain and tandem Link modules form a single structural unit that clamps a hyaluronan decasaccharide within a continuous binding groove; mutation of this glycosaminoglycan-binding site abolishes hyaluronan binding yet only partially reduces PNN incorporation, showing that hyaluronan binding contributes to but is not strictly required for PNN integration. At the C-terminus, integrity of the G3 domain C-type lectin repeat is required for efficient aggrecan secretion and for binding to cartilage matrix ligands, and missense variants there reduce both PMID:35338222. ACAN transcription in chondrocytes is driven by SOX9 cooperating with the SOX trio, a step gated upstream by TET1-mediated 5hmC deposition at chondrocyte-specific SOX9 sites that licenses SOX9 occupancy PMID:33134768 and by SIRT1 deacetylation of SOX9, which promotes its importin-β-dependent nuclear entry and binding to a -10 kb ACAN enhancer PMID:26910618; SHOX2 contributes by physically partnering with SOX5/SOX6 to transactivate ACAN regulatory elements PMID:24421874, and the locus carries at least eleven evolutionarily conserved, partly SOX9-independent enhancers PMID:22820679. ACAN output is further tuned post-transcriptionally by miR-140 acting through RALA and translationally by the mTOR/4E-BP1/eIF4E axis coupled to Smad signaling [PMID:24063364, PMID:32485037]. Loss-of-function mutations — nonsense/frameshift and aberrant-splicing alleles triggering nonsense-mediated decay, and locus-disrupting rearrangements — produce ACAN haploinsufficiency that causes chondrodysplasia and proportionate short stature [PMID:17952705, PMID:38782218, PMID:29302920]. In the CNS, aggrecan-containing PNNs serve cell-type-specific roles: deletion from CA2 pyramidal neurons impairs social memory and reversal learning while deletion from PV interneurons impairs contextual fear memory, and HDAC2 in PV+ cells sustains Acan expression to gate PNN aggregation and fear-memory extinction PMID:37131076.

G1 (Ig + two Link modules) that binds hyaluronan, a G2 (two further Link modules), a huge central
keratan-sulfate/chondroitin-sulfate attachment region, and a C-terminal G3 (EGF + C-type lectin +

Core biology

Aggregate formation is the function. Many aggrecan monomers bind a single hyaluronan filament
through G1, each junction stabilised by a HAPLN link protein, generating >200 MDa assemblies. The
dense negative charge of the ~100 chondroitin-sulfate chains draws in water; the resulting swelling
pressure, restrained by the collagen II network, is what makes cartilage resist compression.

Annotation problems found

  1. involved_in GO:0006508 proteolysis (NAS, PMID:1569188) is backwards. The cited paper shows
    aggrecan being cleaved by a cartilage proteinase. Being a substrate is not participating in
    proteolysis; the agents are ADAMTS4/ADAMTS5 and the MMPs. Substrate-as-agent role conflation.
  2. involved_in GO:0007155 cell adhesion (IEA, InterPro:IPR000538). The Link-domain family
    mapping carries both hyaluronic acid binding and cell adhesion. The cell-adhesion half comes from
    the cell-surface HA receptors in the family (CD44, TSG-6), not from the secreted lecticans.
    Aggrecan is not a cell adhesion molecule and has no membrane anchor.
  3. is_active_in GO:0045202 synapse (IBA). GO deliberately places perineuronal net outside the
    synapse: GO:0072534's ancestors run perisynaptic extracellular matrix (GO:0098966) →
    synapse-associated extracellular matrix (GO:0099535) → specialized extracellular matrix
    (GO:0140047)/extracellular matrix, and GO:0045202 is not among them (checked via QuickGO
    /ontology/go/terms/GO:0072534/ancestors). The same IBA batch already gives the precise term.
  4. Reactome reaction-level export dominates the record. 14 of 40 rows are located_in Golgi lumen and 6 are located_in extracellular region, one per keratan-sulfate biosynthesis reaction.
    Five of the Golgi rows come from defective-enzyme disease reactions (R-HSA-3656230,
    R-HSA-3656258, R-HSA-3656269, R-HSA-9035949, R-HSA-9035950), where the biology being modelled is
    a congenital disorder of glycosylation, not aggrecan function.
  5. No correct biological process is annotated at all. After removing proteolysis and cell
    adhesion, GOA's only BP terms are two broad development terms. Nothing states that aggrecan
    builds the matrix — extracellular matrix organization (GO:0030198) / assembly (GO:0085029) is the
    obvious gap.
  6. GO:0005540 hyaluronic acid binding is still only IEA despite a 2025 human cocrystal
    structure and BLI affinity measurement (PMID:40273987). Straightforward IDA/IPI upgrade.
  7. UniProt's DR block lists GO:0005615 extracellular space (IBA), GO:0030246 carbohydrate binding
    and GO:0046872 metal ion binding (both IEA:UniProtKB-KW), none of which appear in the GOA TSV
    snapshot. The keyword-derived pair is expected — GO_REF:0000043 SPKW annotations were withdrawn.

Things deliberately not annotated

  • The three UniProt DISEASE entries (SEDK, SEMDAG, SSOAOD) are disease associations, not GO
    processes. Short stature is a phenotype of haploinsufficiency.
  • The affinage narrative is dominated by regulators of ACAN transcription — SOX9, the SOX trio,
    SHOX2, TET1, SIRT1, HDAC2, miR-140, mTOR/4E-BP1. Being a transcriptional target of SOX9 is not an
    ACAN function; those findings belong on the regulators.
  • Two affinage findings rest on bioRxiv preprints with no PMID. One of them (the G1-HA cocrystal /
    PNN integration study) has since been published as PMID:40273987 and is used here in its published
    form; the other (Amigo2-Acan CA2 conditional knockout) has not been, and is not used.

suggested_questions:
- question: What is the hyaluronan-independent activity that recruits aggrecan into perineuronal nets?
    Point mutations that abolish hyaluronan binding entirely still allow aggrecan to incorporate into
    perineuronal nets, only less efficiently, which means the long-standing model in which the
    hyaluronan-lectican-link protein ternary complex is the sole anchor is incomplete. Identifying the
    second binding partner would settle whether the cartilage and central nervous system pools of
    aggrecan assemble by the same mechanism, and it would supply the molecular function term that the
    perineuronal net localisation currently lacks.
- question: Should aggrecan carry a biological process term for building the matrix? After removing
    proteolysis and cell adhesion, the only process terms left in GOA are two broad development terms,
    and nothing in the record states that aggrecan assembles the matrix it is a constituent of, even
    though the aggregation event is the whole reason the protein exists. Extracellular matrix assembly
    GO:0085029 and extracellular matrix organization GO:0030198 both exist; whether a structural
    constituent that self-assembles with hyaluronan and a link protein should be annotated to them, or
    whether those terms are reserved for the enzymes and receptors that remodel matrix, is a modelling
    decision GO should make explicitly rather than by omission.
- question: How should a protein that is a major protease substrate be annotated? Aggrecanase cleavage of
    the interglobular domain is one of the best-characterised proteolytic events in human pathology and is
    the mechanistic core of cartilage loss in osteoarthritis, but the only way it currently appears on
    ACAN is as an incorrect involved_in proteolysis annotation. GO has no substrate role to assign, so
    curators either state the relationship wrongly, as here, or leave a central piece of the gene's
    biology unrecorded.
- question: Is the vascular pool of aggrecan functionally significant, or incidental? Aggrecan has been
    identified by proteomics in human aorta and saphenous vein, is depleted in varicose veins at both
    protein and transcript level, and yet UniProt still describes its tissue distribution as restricted to
    cartilage. Whether vascular aggrecan contributes to vessel wall mechanics determines whether the
    interstitial-matrix localisation terms are appropriate for this protein at all.
- question: Which of aggrecan's three globular regions should each annotation be attributed to? The
    hyaluronan binding is G1, the matrix ligand binding is the G3 C-type lectin, the compression
    resistance is the chondroitin sulfate region between G2 and G3, and G2 has no assigned function
    despite Link modules nearly identical to those of G1. Human disease alleles separate these functions
    cleanly - interglobular repeat variants, truncations and G3 missense variants give distinguishable
    phenotypes - so region-resolved annotation is achievable and would make the record far more useful.
