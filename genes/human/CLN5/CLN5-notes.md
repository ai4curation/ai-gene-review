## Lipid-enzyme function and substrate boundaries

The most coherent core is lysosomal BMP-forming transacylase capacity. PMID:37708259 reports that activity and cellular LPG/BMP changes, although its full Methods were not recovered. Independently, the published Cell study PMID:39423811 purifies CLN5 and reproduces BMP formation. Crucially, that experiment produces R,racemic BMP rather than the S,S-BMP made through the PLD3/4 stereoinversion-associated route. Positive CLN5 chemistry is retained without making it the sole or universally dominant physiological S,S-BMP enzyme.

The cellular results are also conditional. PMID:39423811 finds unchanged BMP after CLN5 deletion in its HEK293T model. PMID:39548099 finds unchanged basal BMP under standard serum conditions, but substantial depletion after extended culture with heat-inactivated serum. These observations demonstrate how extracellular lipid processing and uptake can mask the phenotype. The 2026 preprint PMID:41890119 adds lysosomal-lysate conversion and recombinant human CLN5 rescue data, but neither peer-review status nor stereochemical resolution is claimed for it.

The live GO:0160121 definition and RHEA:77619 use different glycerol numbering in their reaction text. RHEA explicitly identifies R,S-BMP as the acyl-transfer product. The original GO source identifier is unchanged, and the discrepancy is raised for expert resolution rather than silently repaired in the machine annotation. The core narrative describes measured BMP-forming capacity without asserting S,S stereoinversion.

PMID:35427157 provides strong purified human CLN5 evidence for synthetic palmitoyl-thioester hydrolysis, including active-site and pocket mutants, enzyme controls and inhibitor comparisons. DPP-5 offers a peptide-like context, but the authors leave natural substrates unresolved. The two existing palmitoyl-(protein) hydrolase rows are retained as non-core assay capacity. The MU substrate experiment includes added beta-glucosidase, so its fluorescence is not an independent CLN5 glycosidase assay. The neuronal-progenitor Results and Figure 5 legend have inconsistent mouse/human labels; neither is generalized into an unqualified human cellular result.

The acyl-CoA-specific annotation requires a different substrate claim. No such experiment was verified in the read body and Methods, and the supplement was inaccessible; both original rows remain undecided. The exact mouse donor has direct curation from the same source as well as propagated records, so this is not dismissed as a source-free or circular transfer. Likewise, PMID:29128403 explicitly reports both human and Dictyostelium glycosidase activity. Its full purification and substrate assays remain inaccessible, leaving the inherited glycosyl-hydrolase claim undecided without a wrong-organism inference. Synthetic-probe activity does not establish the physiological macromolecule-depalmitoylation process; the obsolete original process identifier is preserved while its biological scope is marked over-annotated.

## Processing, compartment and participant roles

CLN5 is glycosylated and processed through the secretory pathway. The historical 407-residue constructs and current 358-residue UniProt sequence use different numbering; the absence of seeded alternative products is preserved rather than filling it with guessed correspondences. In PMID:12134079, antibody choice and translation-start constructs distinguish lysosomal protein from ER/Golgi precursor signals. Later studies demonstrate luminal localization, secretion and association with the luminal lysosomal membrane. These findings can coexist: a cleaved or soluble protein can associate with a membrane without remaining a permanent transmembrane chain.

The mannose-binding annotation is assessed against the actual entity performing recognition. The original PMID:16399764 target table positively detects CLN5 among mannose-6-phosphorylated glycoproteins captured by mannose-6-phosphate receptors. The receptor is the recognition reagent. Direct glycosylation experiments in PMID:24058541 show CLN5 bearing covalent glycans and document their effects on folding and transport. This supports a source-specific over-annotation judgment for a CLN5 lectin function, while preserving the positive target detection and incomplete full-text access to the original proteomics article.

The maturation annotation has a related participant distinction. The original PMID:20052765 abstract and the original author's thesis description of study I show CLN5 itself undergoing processing. The recovered thesis is not a reproduction of the journal article, whose full text remains unavailable. Independent full PMID:26342652 shows endogenous and tagged precursor-to-mature conversion, pulse-chase behavior and sensitivity to cysteine-protease inhibitors. Those experiments identify CLN5 as the processed substrate; they do not demonstrate its own autocatalytic, scaffold or cofactor work in protein maturation. This is a scoped judgment about the cited evidence, not a universal claim that CLN5 can never affect another protein's maturation.

The original developmental expression study PMID:10992246 remains abstract-only. Its positive embryonic human expression is retained in the reference assessment, but the source-specific brain-development, neurogenesis, neuron-maturation and perinuclear-localization assertions remain undecided. The unavailable figures are not treated as negative evidence. The exosome study's main Methods are available, but the exact CLN5 entry in its original supplementary Table S2 remains inaccessible; that HDA row is also undecided.

## Interactions and trafficking

Supported generic binding assertions remain non-core under the campaign policy. Specific refinements require an authenticated positive pair, the actual term definition and an established partner class. The TPP1, Rab5/Rab7, IGF2 and PLPP6 refinements meet that bounded standard; they do not transfer the partner's catalytic or signaling activity to CLN5.

The TPP1 experiment in PMID:12134079 captures precursor material, while a separate mouse CLN5 pull-down in PMID:19941651 preferentially captures mature TPP1. Both observations remain at their actual species and product scope. The IGF2 screen partner is P01344-3. Reconstruction using its explicit UniProt alternative-sequence feature and independent official FASTA shows that the tested 57–236 region equals the canonical precursor sequence. It contains the growth-factor region but is not an isolated mature hormone preparation. The PLPP6 refinement identifies a phosphatase partner without asserting inhibition. HuRI's verification and sequence-confirmation procedures support the interaction records, while native encounters and post-translational processing remain unresolved.

PMID:22431521 directly supports cellular associations with sortilin, Rab5 and Rab7. Depletion and rescue experiments connect CLN5 to Rab7 GTP loading, retromer recruitment and recycling of lysosomal sorting receptors. The paper does not establish purified nucleotide-exchange chemistry or a luminal-to-cytosolic scaffold mechanism. Retromer fractionation is not evidence that mature CLN5 resides in the cytosol. The transport and GTP-loading observations are retained as non-core context, separately from the lipid-enzyme core.

The three PAINT assertions preserve the actual PTN and source identifiers. Current experimental donor records and target assays are assessed term by term. A target appearing among descendants is legitimate experimental grounding, and donor count is not used as a confidence metric. Historical ancestral placement and alignment were not reconstructed.
