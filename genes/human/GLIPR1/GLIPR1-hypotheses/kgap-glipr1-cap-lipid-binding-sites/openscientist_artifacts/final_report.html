GLIPR1 (P48060) CAP domain: which lipid-binding features are actually retained?

Hypothesis under test (kgap-glipr1-cap-lipid-binding-sites): the human GLIPR1 CAP domain retains the caveolin-binding-motif (CBM) sterol-binding loop of yeast Pry1 and the hydrophobic palmitate/fatty-acid channel of tablysin-15, alongside its zinc-coordinating His/Glu cavity, so its initiating biochemical action is plausibly binding of a sterol or a fatty-acid lipid.

Summary

Verdict: partially supported on the metal site, refuted in its stated structural rationale, unresolved on the conclusion. The hypothesis bundles three structural claims and one functional inference, and they do not stand or fall together. GLIPR1 carries the complete CAP tetrad (His79, Glu98, Glu120, His137), the tetrad is invariant across 141 vertebrate orthologs, and the Zn2+ ion in PDB 3Q2R is coordinated by His79 NE2 at 2.66 A and His137 NE2 at 2.78 A with nothing else inside 3.0 A. That part of the hypothesis is correct and directly supported by primary coordinates.

The two lipid-binding premises do not survive examination, for reasons that differ from the ones a first pass suggests. There is no caveolin-binding motif in the SCP domain of GLIPR1, and there is none in CRISP1, CRISP2, CRISP3 or PI16 either. Structurally, the loop equivalent to Pry1 Y237-DFSNPG-F244 keeps 2 of 3 aromatics in GLIPR1 and exactly 2 of 3 in CRISP2, which binds cholesterol in vitro and complements yeast pry1 pry2 (PMID: 23027975). A human CAP protein with GLIPR1-level CBM degeneracy is a demonstrated sterol binder, so CBM status has no discriminating power in either direction. The tablysin-15 acyl channel is similarly uninformative: across all 27 CAP-domain structures in the PDB, the three tablysin-15 entries are the only ones that combine a palmitate-sized cavity with hydrophobic lining, and the residue that opens the channel in tablysin-15 (Gly129) is a tryptophan in all eight other CAP domains examined, including fatty-acid-binding Pry1.

The practical result for curation is a double negative. The evidence does not support assigning GLIPR1 a lipid, sterol or fatty-acid binding term, because the structural rationale for doing so is wrong at the premise. The evidence does not support a NOT annotation either, because the same structural arguments that would justify one are shown here to fail their positive controls. No direct lipid-binding assay has been performed on GLIPR1. The defensible curation position is that the initiating biochemical action of GLIPR1 is untested, with zinc ion binding (GO:0008270) or metal ion binding (GO:0046872) as the better-supported structural molecular function, treated as non-core until a physiological role for the site is shown.

Executive judgment

The four components of the hypothesis resolve as follows.

1. Zinc-coordinating His/Glu cavity: supported. GLIPR1 has the complete CAP tetrad. Superposing 3Q2U chain A onto GAPR-1 1SMB with a TM-align-style algorithm implemented in this job (TM 0.606, 145 aligned pairs, 124 core pairs under 5 A, core RMSD 1.70 A) maps the GAPR-1 tetrad one-to-one: His54 to His79 (CA 0.62 A), Glu65 to Glu98 (0.66 A), Glu86 to Glu120 (1.48 A), His103 to His137 (1.00 A). Across 141 vertebrate GLIPR1 orthologs His79 is 99.3 % identical and the other three are 100 % identical. The Zn-soaked structure 3Q2R confirms the site experimentally.

2. CBM sterol-binding loop: the premise is factually wrong for every human CAP protein. No CBM consensus (ΦXΦXXXXΦ, ΦXXXXΦXXΦ, ΦXΦXXXXΦXXΦ with Φ = W/F/Y) occurs in the SCP domain of GLIPR1 (38-175), CRISP1 (45-175), CRISP2 (41-169), CRISP3 (43-171) or PI16 (37-165). GLIPR1's only match, YYSVVYPGW at 217-225, is juxtamembrane and outside both the CAP domain and the crystallised construct. Pry1 and GAPR-1 do match (YDFSNPGF at 237, YNFQQPGF at 90). The structural equivalents of the Pry1 loop keep 2 of 3 aromatics in GLIPR1 (Y124, F126, then Cys131) and 2 of 3 in CRISP2 (F121, Y123, then Lys128), 2 of 3 in CRISP3 and GLIPR1L1, and 1 of 3 in CRISP1 and PI16.

3. Fatty-acid channel: present only in tablysin-15, across the entire structural family. All 27 PF00188 entries in the PDB were run through the same cavity detection. Seventeen have a buried cavity of at least 150 A^3, the palmitate requirement. Ranked by hydrophobic lining fraction, the three tablysin-15 entries take ranks 1 to 3 (3U3N 0.74, 3U3U 0.70, 3U3L 0.69), rank 4 is 5VHG at 0.55, and the gap to rank 4 exceeds the spread of ranks 4 through 17. GLIPR1 sits mid-distribution at 0.45. No non-tablysin CAP structure combines palmitate-sized volume with hydrophobic lining.

4. The final inference, that the initiating biochemical action of GLIPR1 is sterol or fatty-acid binding: unresolved and currently unsupported. The two structural features the hypothesis rests on cannot carry the inference. The ligand census over all 27 CAP structures adds the sharpest limit: the only lipids ever seen in a CAP protein are palmitate in 3U3L and 3U3N and the leukotriene E4 analogue EAH in 3U3U, all tablysin-15. No sterol has been observed bound to any CAP protein in any structure, so the sterol site is inferred entirely from mutagenesis and solution binding and there is no structural template from which it could be transferred to GLIPR1 even in principle. The only measure that separates GLIPR1 from its paralogs is weak: among six human CAP domains modelled and analysed identically (AlphaFold v6, domain-trimmed, pLDDT >= 70), GLIPR1's most hydrophobic buried cavity has the lowest hydrophobic lining fraction, 0.50, against CRISP1 0.73, GLIPR1L1 0.68, CRISP3 0.67, CRISP2 0.62 and PI16 0.62. With n = 6, no replication and AlphaFold side chains, that is a ranking, not a result.

Caveats that bound all of this. These are static structures, so an induced-fit site would be invisible. The GLIPR1 crystal (3Q2U, 3Q2R) is one truncated soluble construct in one crystal form with glycerol bound. The CBM mutations in Pry1 abolished cholesteryl acetate export but not cholesterol binding (PMID: 24598142), so even an intact CBM would not have been evidence of binding. GLIPR1 is a single-pass membrane protein (signal 1-21, SCP 38-175, TM 233-255), unlike the secreted Pry1, tablysin-15, CRISPs and PI16, which changes what lipid pools the domain could contact.

What changed across the three iterations

Iteration 1 concluded that GLIPR1 had lost the fatty-acid channel because Trp117 and Phe44 occlude it. Iteration 2 added the controls that claim needed and withdrew it: the Trp is family-wide, including in fatty-acid-binding Pry1, and the rigid transplantation metric fails its positive control. Iteration 3 replaced the small reference set with the complete one, every CAP-domain structure in the PDB, censused for ligands and measured for cavities under identical settings. The verdict on the metal site is unchanged throughout. The verdict on lipid binding moved from "refuted" to "the structural arguments on both sides are uninformative", and then gained the family-wide context that makes tablysin-15 an outlier rather than a representative.

Findings in detail

The CAP tetrad is intact, invariant, and binds zinc

Structural superposition places all four tetrad residues of GAPR-1 on GLIPR1 within 1.5 A of CA deviation. An independent check against the Pry1 CAP crystal 5ETE (TM 0.555) reproduces three of the four: Glu215 to Glu98 (1.32 A), Glu233 to Glu120 (1.11 A), His250 to His137 (1.27 A). Pry1 His208 falls in a loop where GLIPR1 carries an insertion, so that one position aligns poorly (7.15 A); the GAPR-1 mapping and the direct Zn coordination in 3Q2R both identify His79 independently, so the tetrad assignment is secure. The sequence motifs in P48060 are GENI at 97-100 and GHYTQVVW at 136-143.

Ortholog scoring over 141 vertebrate GLIPR1 sequences (UniProt, GN=GLIPR1, Vertebrata, lamprey to human, 37-100 % identity to human, median 70 %) gives His79 99.3 % identity and Glu98, Glu120 and His137 100 % identity. The site is under strong purifying selection. It is a divalent-cation site, not by itself evidence of lipid binding: the same tetrad binds Zn2+ in GAPR-1 (PMID: 30700571) and Mg2+ in Pry1 (PMID: 27344972).

The central cavity of GLIPR1 is large, polar and holds glycerol

The largest buried cavity in 3Q2U chain A is 422 A^3 with 44 lining residues and a hydrophobic fraction of 0.45. It contains the full CAP tetrad. Of the four glycerol molecules in the structure, GOL666 sits in this cavity contacting His79, Glu98, Asn99, Val134, Gly136 and His137. Comparable values from the same calculation: Pry1 CAP 369 A^3 at 0.43, GAPR-1 1SMB 227 A^3 at 0.38, tablysin-15 central cavity 437 A^3 at 0.27, which is a separate site from its 166 A^3 palmitate channel at 0.74.

A polar cavity of this size with a divalent-cation site is consistent with a small polar or amphipathic ligand or with a metal ion. It is a poor match to a sterol or a long-chain fatty acid. In the crystal it holds a cryoprotectant, in contrast to tablysin-15, which copurified a fatty acid from E. coli (PMID: 22311975).

The caveolin-binding motif is absent from every human CAP domain

The motif scan and the structural mapping agree. Restricting the regular-expression scan to the UniProt-annotated SCP domain of each protein gives matches in Pry1 and GAPR-1 and no match in GLIPR1, CRISP1, CRISP2, CRISP3 or PI16. GLIPR1L1 matches at a different position (YNETQFYDF at 118). Superposing each human CAP AlphaFold model onto 5ETE (TM 0.689-0.760) and reading off the residues equivalent to Pry1 Y237-DFSNPG-F244 gives the segments in the table below.

The decisive comparison is CRISP2. It carries exactly the same 2-of-3 degeneracy as GLIPR1, with the third position replaced by a non-aromatic (Lys128 against GLIPR1's Cys131), and purified CRISP2 binds cholesterol in vitro (PMID: 23027975). Any argument from CBM status to sterol binding in a human CAP protein has to explain CRISP2 first, and none does.

The tablysin-15 channel is a tablysin-specific configuration

Each CAP domain was superposed onto tablysin-15 3U3N chain C and the palmitate (PLM, 18 heavy atoms) transplanted into the target frame by the inverse transform. Pry1 binds fatty acids at micromolar affinity (PMID: 28365570) and scores no better than GLIPR1. The rigid transplant metric therefore does not discriminate fatty-acid binders from non-binders and cannot be used to claim GLIPR1 lost a site. What the computation does establish is narrower and still useful: the open channel seen in the tablysin-15 crystal is created by a glycine at position 129, and the rest of the superfamily packs a tryptophan into that space. Any fatty-acid binding by Pry1, CRISP2 or GLIPR1 uses a site of different geometry or requires a conformational change that a static apo structure does not show.

No CAP protein has ever been crystallised with a sterol

An RCSB Search API v2 query for polymer entities annotated with Pfam PF00188 returns 27 distinct entries: 1CFE, 1QNX, 1RC9, 1SMB, 1WVR, 1XTA, 1XX5, 2DDA, 2DDB, 2EPF, 2GIZ, 2VZN, 3MZ8, 3Q2R, 3Q2U, 3U3L, 3U3N, 3U3U, 4AIW, 4D53, 4IFA, 4TPV, 5ETE, 5JYS, 5VHG, 5WEE, 6IMF. A GraphQL query of their nonpolymer components, after removing waters, ions and common cryo/buffer species and filtering above 120 Da, finds bound ligands in six: palmitic acid in 3U3L and 3U3N, EAH in 3U3U, inositol hexakisphosphate in 4AIW, NAG/FUC glycans in 5WEE, MES buffer in 6IMF. There is no sterol, cholesterol, cholesteryl ester or sterol analogue anywhere in the family.

The polyanion alternative is not supported by the one structure that could support it

GAPR-1 binding to negatively charged phospholipid membranes has been proposed as a general CAP property, which would make a phosphoinositide head group the ligand of the conserved cavity. 4AIW is the only CAP structure with a polyanion bound. Its inositol hexakisphosphate contacts exactly one residue within 4.0 A, Lys7 NZ at 2.73 A, on the N-terminal surface outside the tetrad cavity. Superposing GLIPR1 3Q2U onto 4AIW (TM 0.608, 125 core pairs, core RMSD 2.02 A) places GLIPR1 Asn30 at that position, with net contact charge 0 against GAPR-1's +1. The alternative is neither confirmed nor excluded; this structure is not evidence for it.

Mechanistic model

The immediate molecular question is whether the GLIPR1 CAP domain presents a site that can engage a sterol or a long-chain fatty acid. The computation addresses the geometry and chemistry of its cavities and the conservation of the candidate site residues, nothing downstream.

What the analysis shows: GLIPR1 presents one large central cavity that contains the His/Glu tetrad and holds glycerol and, on soaking, Zn2+; it does not present the open tablysin-15 acyl channel, and neither does any other CAP structure except tablysin-15 itself. Everything else attributed to GLIPR1 in the literature (apoptosis, mitotic catastrophe, c-Myc destabilisation, mesenchymal transformation, glioma invasion) is a cellular phenotype measured after overexpression, recombinant protein delivery or gene ablation, several steps removed from any CAP-domain biochemistry. None of those studies tested lipid binding.

GO curation implications

All items are leads requiring curator verification.

  • Do not assign a lipid-binding MF term to GLIPR1 on the strength of this hypothesis. lipid binding (GO:0008289), sterol binding (GO:0032934), cholesterol binding (GO:0015485) and fatty acid binding (GO:0005504) have no direct evidence for GLIPR1 and the structural rationale offered for them does not hold. If such a term is present with an IEA/ISS/IBA code propagated from Pry1, tablysin-15 or CRISP2, flag it as unsupported for this protein. The PDB-wide census gives the curator a one-line reason: no CAP protein has ever been crystallised with a sterol, and the only CAP structures with a lipid of any kind are the three tablysin-15 entries, so there is no structural template behind such a propagation.
  • Equally, do not record a NOT annotation. The structural arguments do not refute lipid binding either. CRISP2 binds cholesterol with the same degenerate CBM, and the only paralog-discriminating measure is a weak cavity ranking. The honest state is "untested in this protein".
  • Metal ion binding is the better-supported structural MF. zinc ion binding (GO:0008270) is directly supported by 3Q2R with PMID: 21931216; metal ion binding (GO:0046872) is the safe generalisation if the curator does not want to commit to zinc from a crystallographic soak. Treat as non-core until a physiological role for the site is shown.
  • CC: the UniProt topology (TM helix 233-255, single-pass membrane) argues against extracellular-region CC terms carried over from the secreted CAP relatives. Check whether existing CC annotations were inherited that way. GLIPR1 is the only one of the six human CAP-domain proteins examined here that is membrane-anchored by a transmembrane helix; GLIPR1L1 is GPI-anchored, GAPR-1 is myristoylated and Golgi-associated, and CRISP1/2/3 and PI16 are secreted.
  • Core function: none of the lipid candidates qualifies. The documented GLIPR1 activities in human cells (Hsc70 association, AURKA/TPX2 suppression, CK1alpha redistribution, ROS-JNK proapoptotic signalling, N-WASP interaction) are cellular phenotypes from overexpression and knockout, none traced to a defined biochemical activity of the CAP domain. The defensible curation position is that the initiating biochemical action of GLIPR1 is unknown.
  • Avoid protein binding (GO:0005515) as a headline recommendation; where partners are supported (HSPA8, WASL, HNRNPK) prefer the partner-specific IPI annotations.

Mechanistic scope

Direct gene-product activity that the analysis addresses: coordination of a divalent cation by His79 and His137 in a 422 A^3 polar cavity, and the geometry and chemistry of every buried cavity in the domain. These are measured on primary coordinates.

Inference, clearly separated: that this cavity binds a physiological ligand at all, and what that ligand is. The crystal shows glycerol and, on soaking, Zn2+. Nothing in the data identifies a physiological ligand.

Downstream phenotypes, not direct activity: p53-dependent induction and tumour-suppressor behaviour in prostate cancer (PMID: 14871827, PMID: 18199537), Hsc70-mediated suppression of AURKA and TPX2 (PMID: 23333597), CK1alpha redistribution and c-Myc destruction (PMID: 22025562), N-WASP and hnRNPK binding in glioma migration (PMID: 26305187), and STAT3/IL-6-dependent mesenchymal transformation (PMID: 26267319). All are measured after overexpression, recombinant protein delivery or gene ablation. None connects to a CAP-domain biochemical activity, and none assays lipid binding.

Conflicts and alternatives

  • The strongest conflict is internal to the human family. CRISP2 binds cholesterol (PMID: 23027975) while carrying the same CBM degeneracy as GLIPR1 (2 of 3 aromatics, third position a non-aromatic: Lys128 in CRISP2, Cys131 in GLIPR1). Any argument from CBM status must explain CRISP2 first.
  • The fungal data point the other way. In MpPR-1 proteins a single CBM point mutation converts a non-binder into a sterol binder (PMID: 29042440), and in Pry1 the CBM loop is intact and functional for export (PMID: 24598142). The CBM is a real determinant in fungi and yeast, and the human CAP proteins appear to work differently. That is an open question.
  • The polyanion alternative, tested and not supported by structure. 4AIW places inositol hexakisphosphate against a single surface lysine, outside the tetrad cavity, and GLIPR1 has Asn30 at the equivalent position. The alternative is neither confirmed nor excluded; this structure is not evidence for it.
  • Tablysin-15 as template, reassessed against the whole family. The PDB-wide sweep shows it is a three-entry outlier, ranks 1 to 3 on cavity hydrophobicity among the 17 CAP structures with a palmitate-sized cavity, with a 0.14 gap to rank 4. Arguments that run "GLIPR1 lacks the CAP fatty-acid channel" are really arguing that GLIPR1 is not tablysin-15, which is true of 24 other CAP structures as well.
  • Metric failure, stated plainly. The rigid palmitate transplant was used in iteration 1 to argue that GLIPR1 lost the fatty-acid channel. With Pry1 as a positive control the metric fails (52 contacts, 0 of 18 free). The argument is withdrawn.
  • Static-structure limitation. Both GLIPR1 structures are the same truncated soluble construct in the same crystal environment with glycerol bound. An induced-fit site would not be seen. The counterweight is that the residues around the putative channel (Trp117, Ala113, Ile114, Phe171, Tyr175) sit at 0.0-0.4 % relative SASA and are 75-99 % conserved, but the same is likely true in Pry1.
  • AlphaFold caveat. CRISP1, CRISP3, PI16 and GLIPR1L1 have no experimental structure (the RCSB Search API returns HTTP 204 for all four), so the paralog comparison uses predicted models. Backbones are high-confidence (mean pLDDT 96.2-98.0) but side-chain packing, which is what cavity hydrophobicity depends on, is the least reliable part of a prediction.
  • Alignment caveat. The Pry1 mapping placed Pry1 His208 at GLIPR1 Leu83 (CA 7.15 A), which is wrong; GLIPR1 carries a loop insertion there. The GAPR-1 mapping (His54 to His79, 0.62 A) and the direct Zn coordination in 3Q2R both identify His79, so the tetrad assignment is safe.
  • Isoform. RTVP-1b (PMID: 17825796) shares only the N-terminal 141 residues; these structural conclusions apply to the canonical P48060 CAP domain.

Limitations and knowledge gaps

  1. No direct lipid-binding assay on GLIPR1. Checked: PubMed returns nothing for GLIPR1 with lipid, cholesterol, sterol or fatty acid. This gap makes the whole curation decision rest on homology and structure, and neither can carry it.
  2. How does CRISP2 bind cholesterol without a CBM? Checked: PMID: 23027975 reports the binding but does not map the site. Until this is known, no structural feature can be used to predict sterol binding in a human CAP protein, including GLIPR1.
  3. Does the GLIPR1 CAP domain complement yeast pry1 pry2? Checked: PMID: 23027975 tested CRISP2, not GLIPR1. This is the single cheapest experiment that would settle it.
  4. Is the 422 A^3 polar central cavity functional? Checked: it holds glycerol and, on soaking, Zn2+. Its physiological ligand is unknown, and it is the only credible ligand site left in the domain.
  5. Conformational accessibility. Checked only in two crystal forms of one construct and in static AlphaFold models. Molecular dynamics with cavity tracing, or a structure with a bound hydrophobic ligand, would say whether anything opens.
  6. No CAP protein has been crystallised with a sterol. Checked: all 27 PF00188 entries in the PDB, censused for nonpolymer components. The sterol site that the hypothesis treats as characterised exists only as mutagenesis and solution binding in Pry1, MpPR-1 and BmVAL-1. A CAP-sterol co-structure from any organism would, for the first time, give a template against which GLIPR1's residues could be scored.
  7. Membrane topology of the CAP domain. Checked: UniProt annotates single-pass membrane by inference (ECO:0000305). Which side of the membrane the CAP domain faces is not experimentally settled and determines which lipid pools it could contact. The GLIPR1-deltaTM work (PMID: 24590455) uses a soluble construct and does not resolve this.

Discriminating tests

  1. Direct binding. Delipidate the recombinant GLIPR1 CAP domain (the sGLIPR1 construct of PMID: 21931216) and test NBD-cholesterol, dehydroergosterol and 11-dansylamino-undecanoic acid, exactly as done for Pry1 and tablysin-15, with CRISP2 run side by side as a positive control. This is decisive and directly comparable to published assays.
  2. Yeast complementation. Express the GLIPR1 CAP domain in S. cerevisiae pry1 pry2 and score cholesteryl acetate export, with CRISP2 positive and empty vector negative (PMID: 23027975 provides both).
  3. Map the CRISP2 cholesterol site. Co-crystallise or run HDX or photo-crosslinking on CRISP2 with cholesterol. Knowing where a human CAP protein binds sterol would make a sequence-based prediction for GLIPR1 possible for the first time.
  4. Cavity engineering. If CRISP2's site is defined, graft the corresponding residues into GLIPR1 and re-test. Gain of binding would identify the determinants directly.
  5. Structure with a hydrophobic ligand. Soak sGLIPR1 with palmitate, arachidonate or cholesteryl hemisuccinate. A null at 1.8 A would be meaningful given glycerol already occupies the central cavity.
  6. Lipidomic pulldown. Immunoprecipitate endogenous GLIPR1 from a high-expressing glioma line and run untargeted lipidomics against an isotype control, to catch a physiological ligand that no crystal would show.
