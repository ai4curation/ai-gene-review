Gene Description

Complement factor I is a secreted regulatory serine protease that cleaves the complement fragments C3b and C4b in complexes with appropriate cofactors, including factor H, C4b-binding protein, CD46 and CR1. Its noncatalytic heavy chain and catalytic light chain are produced from one precursor and remain linked by a disulfide bond. The mature enzyme has a zymogen-like resting conformation; substrate and cofactor contacts stabilize a proteolytically competent state. Its limited cleavage prevents complement amplification and also generates fragments with distinct complement-receptor recognition properties. Factor I acts in extracellular fluids and can associate transiently with membrane-attached substrate/cofactor complexes.

Core Functions

Factor I catalyses cofactor-dependent cleavage of C3b and C4b, limiting complement amplification and producing fragments with different receptor-recognition properties. The noncatalytic heavy chain helps organize substrate/cofactor contacts and regulate the catalytic light chain; both derive from one precursor. The mature enzyme can remain zymogen-like until a productive complex forms. The 2017 inactive S525A ternary structure is distinguished from separate active-enzyme cleavage assays; the 2011 free-enzyme structure and ternary docking model are not the same experiment.
