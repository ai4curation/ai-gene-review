### Central chemistry and evidence boundaries

CAII is a soluble zinc-dependent carbonate dehydratase. Its own active site catalyzes reversible CO2/bicarbonate interconversion; its physiological consequences include acid-base equilibration and the substrate supply coupled to epithelial transport. The core is therefore one carbonate-dehydratase/pH-regulation unit, rather than a collection of inhibitor, receptor or transporter functions. The official [GO:0004089 definition](https://amigo.geneontology.org/amigo/term/GO%3A0004089) describes the reversible bicarbonate-dehydration reaction, and [GO:0051453](https://amigo.geneontology.org/amigo/term/GO%3A0051453) covers intracellular pH regulation.

The many source assertions for this same enzyme activity were assessed individually. The available abstracts distinguish kinetic mutants, substrate/metal structures, inhibitor complexes, comparative isozyme panels and human deficiency studies. Acceptance of the well-established activity does not mean that each paper's full experiment was independently read. In particular, PMID:17705204 and PMID:4994926 are bibliographic-only caches, PMID:8485129 explicitly truncates its abstract at 250 words, and the CAI-mutant focus of PMID:7758465 or CA XIII focus of PMID:18618712 does not establish misattribution of the curated CAII annotation. These limitations are recorded in their reference reviews; the activity itself has independent direct human evidence.

The actual cached Methods and catalytic Results in PMID:18942852 identify recombinant human CAII variants expressed in bacteria, enzyme purification, isotope exchange and wild-type comparisons. The catalytic and Discussion passages were read, but crystallographic images and supplementary data were not. PMID:19170619 uses a human CAII A65S/N67Q construct as a CAIX active-site mimic with wild-type CAII controls. The complete abstract and construct/purification Methods were read; the cancer biology of CAIX is not assigned to native CAII. The human CAII structures and mutational kinetics, not a domain label alone, support zinc-dependent catalysis.

PMID:3126084 explicitly reports hydrolysis of 4-nitrophenyl acetate by human CAII variants. This substrate is compatible with [GO:0004064](https://amigo.geneontology.org/amigo/term/GO%3A0004064), but does not establish an independent major physiological aryl-ester pathway. PMID:10550681 and PMID:11015219 report cyanamide hydration and structural intermediates in human CAII. These secondary activities are retained as non-core. The cyanamide term's reaction is conventionally written in the reversible dehydration direction; that is not a contradiction of the measured hydration direction.

### Membrane association, transport and signaling

The exact three seeded partners are SLC4A7/NBC3 Q9Y6M7 (PMID:14736710), SLC4A4/kNBC1 Q9Y6R1 (PMID:15218065), and SLC26A6 isoform 4 Q9BXS9-4 (PMID:15990874). The first two canonical caches are abstract-only. The third contains actual main text: the complete available Results, Discussion and relevant construct, binding, immunoprecipitation and transport Methods were read. The SLC26A6 experiments use human SLC26A6a/S+Q and CAII in HEK293 cells. They support the reported interaction and reversible recruitment of endogenous CAII to the cytosolic face of the plasma membrane. Images and supplementary experiments were not independently inspected.

Direct binding and substrate-channeling models require assay-specific care. The later primary [PMID:17090540](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17090540/) reports no binding of purified SLC4-A1/A4/A8 C-terminal peptides to CAII, while reproducing orientation-dependent binding of GST fusions. That is a genuine challenge to direct SLC4A4 C-terminal recognition, but is not proof against all full-length transporter association, and did not test SLC4A7 or SLC26A6. The normal source was requested before final integration. The review distinguishes observed association from a universal transport-metabolon mechanism, and does not assign transmembrane transport activity to CAII. In addition, CAII changes the kinetics of acid-base equilibration itself, which matters when pH change is the transport readout.

In PMID:15990874, angiotensin receptor activation displaces CAII from SLC26A6 through a PKC-dependent mechanism; phosphorylation of SLC26A6 at Ser553 is proposed rather than directly established. The enzyme's demonstrated contribution is carbonate chemistry coupled to transport; its release is a regulated effector mechanism. The [GO:0038166 definition](https://amigo.geneontology.org/amigo/term/GO%3A0038166) describes angiotensin-triggered GPCR signal transduction. The draft treats a separate signaling-process assignment as overextended, while retaining the directly supported transport-regulation context. This is based on the actual experiment, not an argument from the title or abstract omission.

Supported generic binding is retained as non-core under the project's standing user instruction. The contested SLC4A4 edge remains uncertain pending assay reconciliation. No generic annotation is removed merely for being uninformative, and no finer molecular activity is invented from the name of a binding partner.
