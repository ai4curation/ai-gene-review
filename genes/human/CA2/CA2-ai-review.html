Gene Description

Carbonic anhydrase II is a soluble zinc-dependent enzyme that rapidly and reversibly interconverts carbon dioxide and water with bicarbonate and protons. Its cytosolic catalysis supports intracellular pH control and the supply or removal of substrates coupled to epithelial acid-base transport. Human enzyme structures and mutational kinetics define the catalytic zinc environment and proton-transfer network. CAII can associate with the cytosolic face of membrane transport proteins, although direct binding and substrate-channeling interpretations depend on the transporter and assay. It also displays arylesterase and cyanamide-hydration activities in vitro. Biallelic CA2 deficiency causes osteopetrosis with renal tubular acidosis and cerebral calcification.

Central chemistry and evidence boundaries

CAII is a soluble zinc-dependent carbonate dehydratase. Its own active site catalyzes reversible CO2/bicarbonate interconversion; its physiological consequences include acid-base equilibration and the substrate supply coupled to epithelial transport. The core is therefore one carbonate-dehydratase/pH-regulation unit, rather than a collection of inhibitor, receptor or transporter functions. The official GO:0004089 definition describes the reversible bicarbonate-dehydration reaction, and GO:0051453 covers intracellular pH regulation.

The many source assertions for this same enzyme activity were assessed individually. The available abstracts distinguish kinetic mutants, substrate/metal structures, inhibitor complexes, comparative isozyme panels and human deficiency studies. Acceptance of the well-established activity does not mean that each paper's full experiment was independently read. In particular, PMID:17705204 and PMID:4994926 are bibliographic-only caches, PMID:8485129 explicitly truncates its abstract at 250 words, and the CAI-mutant focus of PMID:7758465 or CA XIII focus of PMID:18618712 does not establish misattribution of the curated CAII annotation. These limitations are recorded in their reference reviews; the activity itself has independent direct human evidence.

The actual cached Methods and catalytic Results in PMID:18942852 identify recombinant human CAII variants expressed in bacteria, enzyme purification, isotope exchange and wild-type comparisons.
