CC   -!- FUNCTION: RNA-binding protein implicated in numerous RNA metabolic
CC       processes (PubMed:29967381, PubMed:39019044). Catalyzes the
CC       phosphorolysis of single-stranded polyribonucleotides processively in
CC       the 3'-to-5' direction (PubMed:29967381, PubMed:39019044).
CC       Mitochondrial intermembrane factor with RNA-processing exoribonulease
CC       activity (PubMed:29967381, PubMed:39019044). Component of the
CC       mitochondrial degradosome (mtEXO) complex, that degrades 3' overhang
CC       double-stranded RNA with a 3'-to-5' directionality in an ATP-dependent
CC       manner (PubMed:29967381, PubMed:39019044). Involved in the degradation
CC       of non-coding mitochondrial transcripts (MT-ncRNA) and tRNA-like
CC       molecules (PubMed:29967381, PubMed:39019044). Required for correct
CC       processing and polyadenylation of mitochondrial mRNAs. Plays a role as
CC       a cytoplasmic RNA import factor that mediates the translocation of
CC       small RNA components, like the 5S RNA, the RNA subunit of ribonuclease
CC       P and the mitochondrial RNA-processing (MRP) RNA, into the
CC       mitochondrial matrix. Plays a role in mitochondrial morphogenesis and
CC       respiration; regulates the expression of the electron transport chain
CC       (ETC) components at the mRNA and protein levels. In the cytoplasm,
CC       shows a 3'-to-5' exoribonuclease mediating mRNA degradation activity;
CC       degrades c-myc mRNA upon treatment with IFNB1/IFN-beta, resulting in a
CC       growth arrest in melanoma cells. Regulates the stability of specific
CC       mature miRNAs in melanoma cells; specifically and selectively degrades
CC       miR-221, preferentially. Also plays a role in RNA cell surveillance by
CC       cleaning up oxidized RNAs. Binds to the RNA subunit of ribonuclease P,
CC       MRP RNA and miR-221 microRNA. {ECO:0000269|PubMed:12473748,

CC   -!- DISEASE: Spinocerebellar ataxia 25 (SCA25) [MIM:608703]: An autosomal

CC       heterogeneous group of cerebellar disorders. Patients show progressive
CC       incoordination of gait and often poor coordination of hands, speech and
CC       eye movements, due to degeneration of the cerebellum with variable
CC       involvement of the brainstem and spinal cord. SCA25 is characterized by
CC       the onset of lower limb ataxia and gait difficulties in the first few
CC       decades of life, although later onset has been reported. There is
CC       incomplete penetrance and variable expressivity, even within families.
CC       {ECO:0000269|PubMed:35411967}. Note=The disease is caused by variants
