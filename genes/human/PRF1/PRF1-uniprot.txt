CC   -!- FUNCTION: Pore-forming protein that plays a key role in granzyme-
CC       mediated programmed cell death, and in defense against virus-infected
CC       or neoplastic cells (PubMed:20889983, PubMed:21037563, PubMed:24558045,
CC       PubMed:9058810, PubMed:9164947). Plays an important role in killing
CC       other cells that are recognized as non-self by the immune system, e.g.
CC       in transplant rejection or some forms of autoimmune disease
CC       (PubMed:9058810). Can insert into the membrane of target cells in its
CC       calcium-bound form, oligomerize and form large pores (PubMed:20889983,
CC       PubMed:21037563). Promotes cytolysis and apoptosis of target cells by
CC       mediating the passage and uptake of cytotoxic granzymes
CC       (PubMed:20038786, PubMed:20225066, PubMed:24558045, PubMed:32299851).
CC       Facilitates the delivery of cationic cargo protein, while anionic or
CC       neural proteins are not delivered efficiently (PubMed:24558045).
CC       Perforin pores allow the release of mature caspase-7 (CASP7) into the
CC       extracellular milieu (By similarity). {ECO:0000250|UniProtKB:P10820,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:20038786, ECO:0000269|PubMed:20225066,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:20889983, ECO:0000269|PubMed:21037563,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:24558045, ECO:0000269|PubMed:32299851,

CC   -!- DOMAIN: Perforin consists of three domains: (1) the MACPF domain, which
CC       includes the central machinery of pore formation, (2) the EGF-like
CC       domain, which forms a 'shelf-like' assembly connecting the MACPF and C2
CC       domains, and (3) the C2 domain, which mediates calcium-dependent
CC       binding to lipid membranes. The C2 domain is critical for initial
CC       calcium-dependent interaction with lipid membranes of the target cell:
CC       calcium-binding causes a significant structural rearrangement, leading
CC       to oligomerization and deployment of the two transmembrane beta-strands
CC       (named CH1/TMH1 and CH2/TMH2) that enter the membrane as amphipathic
CC       beta-hairpins. The third calcium-binding site (Ca(2+) 3), which
CC       constitutes the weakest affinity site, triggers structural
CC       rearrangements in the C2 domain that facilitate its interaction with
CC       lipid membranes. {ECO:0000250|UniProtKB:P10820}.
CC   -!- PTM: N-glycosylated. {ECO:0000250|UniProtKB:P10820}.
CC   -!- DISEASE: Hemophagocytic lymphohistiocytosis, familial, 2 (FHL2)
CC       [MIM:603553]: A rare disorder characterized by immune dysregulation
CC       with hypercytokinemia, defective function of natural killer cell, and
CC       massive infiltration of several organs by activated lymphocytes and
CC       macrophages. The clinical features of the disease include fever,
CC       hepatosplenomegaly, cytopenia, and less frequently neurological
CC       abnormalities ranging from irritability and hypotonia to seizures,
CC       cranial nerve deficits and ataxia. {ECO:0000269|PubMed:10583959,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:11179007}. Note=The disease is caused by variants
CC       affecting the gene represented in this entry.
