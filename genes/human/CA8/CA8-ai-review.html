Gene Description

Carbonic anhydrase 8 (CA8/CARP VIII) is a soluble carbonic-anhydrase-family protein that lacks the canonical zinc-dependent carbon dioxide hydration activity. Human CA8 carries Arg116 at a position occupied by a zinc-coordinating histidine in active family members. CA8 binds the cytoplasmic regulatory region of the endoplasmic-reticulum calcium-release channel ITPR1 and can reduce IP3 sensitivity and IP3-evoked calcium release. It is abundant in cerebellar Purkinje cells, and biallelic pathogenic variants cause congenital cerebellar ataxia with variable neurodevelopmental impairment. Biochemical interaction and channel-regulation studies support this inhibitory mechanism, while its detailed contact surface and its contribution to every neuronal phenotype remain unresolved.

Core Functions

Binds the cytoplasmic regulatory region of ITPR1 and inhibits IP3-dependent calcium release from intracellular stores. CA8 itself supplies the channel-regulatory activity; ITPR1 forms the calcium-conducting pore. Mouse biochemical mapping, human CA8-fragment interaction assays and receptor-defined HeLa calcium imaging support this mechanism. Exact contact residues and allosteric transmission remain unresolved, and the separate negative Purkinje-culture binding result limits claims of universal interaction across assay conditions. Purkinje-cell enrichment and human loss-of-function phenotypes give neuronal context without proving that every CA8-dependent phenotype is exclusively mediated by ITPR1.
