# FOXP1 review notes

FOXP1 recognizes regulatory DNA and acts with transcriptional partners in the nucleus. Human full-length repression, the 431–559 DNA-binding fragment and SMRT recruitment support the repressor core ([PMID:15286807](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC484980/), [PMID:18347093](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2275427/), [PMID:20950788](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2978954/)). Its output depends on context: human FBXL7 activation and the altered DNA preference and pluripotency-associated regulation of FOXP1-ES are retained ([PMID:28218735](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28218735/), [PMID:21924763](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21924763/)). This is not a claim that every product or target behaves identically.

The original proteomic Table S6 establishes FOXP1 association with FOXP2 and FOXP4 in chromatin and soluble fractions. Chromatin supports nuclear association; soluble recovery does not independently locate the complex in the nucleus. Self entries marked Bait are not homomerization measurements. Independent cellular co-IP and BRET support the partner and self-association judgments without proving isolated binary affinity or native stoichiometry ([PMID:25609649, Table S6](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/biostudies/files/S-EPMC4332150/msb0011-0775-sd10.xlsx), [PMID:25027557](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25027557/), [PMID:26647308](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26647308/)).

Human vascular and B-cell experiments support context-dependent migration, proliferation and survival. Human FOXP1 expressed in a mouse macrophage model is distinguished from a mouse-protein assay, and each cytokine endpoint is evaluated separately. Mouse Foxp1 loss increases IL21 and follicular-helper differentiation, supporting the bounded direction corrections while preserving the original human transfer and donor records ([PMID:24023716](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3759435/), [PMID:25267198](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4246697/), [PMID:18799727](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2597137/), [PMID:24859450](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4142638/)).
