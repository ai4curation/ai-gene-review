ACAP3 (Q96P50) PH domain phosphoinositide pocket: hypothesis evaluation

Hypothesis slug: kgap-acap3-ph-phosphoinositide-pocket Source file: genes/human/ACAP3/ACAP3-ai-review.yaml Target: human ACAP3, UniProt Q96P50, NCBITaxon:9606 Focus type: free_text

Summary

Verdict: partially supported. The seed hypothesis has two clauses and they resolve in opposite directions.

The PH clause holds. The pleckstrin homology domain of human ACAP3 does retain a basic pocket at the structural position where PH domains coordinate inositol phosphates, so binding to acidic phospholipid is predicted. Three independent structural superpositions of the AlphaFold model AF-Q96P50-F1-v6 (residues 258-375) onto PH domains crystallised with ligand recover the same ACAP3 surface each time: ASAP1 with diC4-PtdIns(4,5)P2 (PDB 5C79, TM-score 0.785, 100 aligned residues, 23.0% identity), GRP1/CYTH3 with Ins(1,3,4,5)P4 (PDB 1FGY, TM 0.698, 99 residues, 25.3%) and PLCdelta1 with Ins(1,4,5)P3 (PDB 1MAI, TM 0.600, 92 residues, 17.4%). The ACAP3 residues recovered by all three are K277, R278, A279, S280, N281, Y300, K302 and T309, with R289 added by two of the three. K277, R278, R289, K302 and K339 are Lys, Arg or His in 100% of 91 vertebrate ACAP3 orthologs. The beta1-beta2 loop is nearly identical to both paralogs (ACAP3 274-290 YLFKRASNAFKTWNRRW, ACAP2 272-288 the same, ACAP1 271-287 differing at two positions), and ACAP1 and ACAP2 have measured phosphoinositide-dependent Arf GAP activity (PMID: 11062263).

The qualifier that keeps this short of "supported" is that the ACAP3 pocket is measurably less basic than every reference. Basic residues within 5 Å of the bound ligand: 4 of 12 for ACAP3 against 6 of 13 for ASAP1, 3 of 8 against 6 of 11 for PLCdelta1, 4 of 12 against 7 of 18 for GRP1. Two of the six ASAP1 canonical-site basic positions are not basic in ACAP3: ASAP1 R354 maps to ACAP3 F283 and ASAP1 R378 maps to ACAP3 A307. F283 is Phe in 100% of 91 orthologs, so this is a fixed feature of the ACAP branch rather than human-specific decay. No direct lipid-binding measurement on ACAP3 exists in the literature retrieved. The defensible curation lead is therefore GO:1901981 (phosphatidylinositol phosphate binding) as ISS, not GO:0005546 (phosphatidylinositol-4,5-bisphosphate binding).

The BAR clause fails. Pang et al. (PMID: 25284369) showed experimentally that the ACAP1 BAR domain can neither bind membrane nor impart curvature, and that residues within the adjacent PH domain carry both functions. My own screened-Coulomb calculation agrees in direction: the concave-minus-convex potential is +0.313 kcal/mol/e for the amphiphysin BAR dimer (PDB 1URU, positive control) and -0.021 kcal/mol/e for the ACAP1 BAR dimer (PDB 4NSW). This result argues in favour of the PH clause, because it makes the PH domain the membrane-binding module of the ACAP BAR-PH unit.

Key findings

F1. The ACAP3 PH domain retains 4 of 6 basic residues of the ASAP1 canonical site

The canonical ("C") phosphoinositide site of the ACAP/ASAP Arf-GAP PH fold is structurally present and still basic in ACAP3.

No experimental structure of ACAP3 exists. An RCSB search API query for Q96P50 returned no entries, so the AlphaFold model AF-Q96P50-F1-v6 (834 residues) was used throughout. The reference was the ASAP1 PH domain crystallised with dibutyryl-PtdIns(4,5)P2 (PDB 5C79 chain A, ligand PBU 800), the structure that defines the C site and the atypical A site (PMID: 26365802).

phenix.superpose_pdbs failed on this pair twice. With similarity_matrix=identity it reported "Number of atoms for LS fitting = 3"; with blosum50 it crashed with AssertionError: ['F', 'Y']. The sequence identity (23.0%) is too low for its sequence-driven alignment. I wrote a TM-align style superposition instead (Kabsch SVD fit, iterative dynamic-programming realignment, d0 = max(1.24·(L-15)^(1/3) - 1.8, 0.5) = 3.74 Å, 8 Å pair cutoff, gap penalty -0.6). That routine gave TM-score 0.785 over 100 aligned residue pairs.

F2. The same ACAP3 surface maps to the pocket of three independent reference PH domains

The identity of the ACAP3 surface assigned to the phosphoinositide site does not depend on which reference structure is used.

The seed hypothesis asked for alignment against PLCdelta1, ACAP1, ASAP1 and GRP1. I ran the comparison structurally rather than by sequence, because at 17-25% identity a sequence alignment is not reliable enough to place individual side chains. Each reference is a PH domain solved with an inositol phosphate bound.

K277, R278, A279, S280, N281, Y300, K302 and T309 appear in all three mappings. R289 and T285 appear in the ASAP1 and GRP1 mappings; F283 appears in the ASAP1 and GRP1 mappings. Three structurally distinct references, differing in ligand and in fold detail, converge on the same ACAP3 face. That convergence is the strongest part of the computational case: a single low-identity superposition could place an arbitrary surface at the ligand, three cannot do so by chance.

Every mapping gives ACAP3 fewer basic residues than its reference. The prediction that follows is binding to anionic phospholipid at this site with lower positive charge density than ASAP1, PLCdelta1 or GRP1. Moderate-affinity, non-stereospecific binding to polyanionic lipid fits the structural evidence better than high-affinity recognition of a single phosphoinositide headgroup.

F3. The mapped pocket residues are invariant across 91 vertebrate orthologs

91 non-fragment vertebrate ACAP3 sequences were retrieved from UniProt (gene:ACAP3 AND taxonomy_id:7742 AND fragment:false), one longest entry per species, and mapped onto human Q96P50 by Smith-Waterman alignment (BLOSUM62, gap open -11, extend -1). The species set runs from teleosts (Gadus morhua, Lates calcarifer, Denticeps clupeoides) and amphibians (Geotrypetes seraphini, Leptobrachium leishanense) through reptiles (Alligator sinensis, Crocodylus porosus, Chrysemys picta), birds (Coturnix japonica, Aquila chrysaetos, Dromaius novaehollandiae) to placental mammals.

The four basic residues the structural mapping places at the inositol phosphate site are under strict purifying selection across roughly 430 million years of vertebrate divergence. That is the expected signature of a functional surface, though it does not by itself prove the function under selection is lipid binding.

The two positions where ASAP1 has an arginine and ACAP3 does not behave differently from each other, and the difference matters for interpretation. F283 (equivalent to ASAP1 R354) is Phe in every one of the 91 orthologs, so the substitution is a fixed property of the ACAP branch and not a human-specific loss. A307 (equivalent to ASAP1 R378) is Ala in only 27.5% of orthologs, commonly Val or Ser elsewhere, so that position carries no conserved constraint at all. ACAP3 therefore has a genuinely different, less positively charged pocket than ASAP1 rather than a decayed one.

F4. The BAR domain concave surface is not a membrane-binding electropositive face

The BAR clause of the seed hypothesis is answered negatively for the one family member with experimental data.

Pang et al. (PMID: 25284369) solved the ACAP1 BAR-PH crystal structure (PDB 4NSW) and helical cryo-EM reconstructions of ACAP1 BAR-PH decorating membrane tubules. Their result: the BAR domain "can neither bind membrane nor impart curvature, but instead requires a neighboring PH (Pleckstrin Homology) domain to achieve these functions", and "Specific residues within the PH domain are responsible for both membrane binding and curvature generation." The BAR domain instead makes BAR-BAR contacts between neighbouring ACAP1 molecules to cluster them on the membrane.

I tested the electrostatics independently. A first metric, counting Lys + Arg minus Asp + Glu over the concave surface tertile, gave amphiphysin -3, ACAP1 -2 and ACAP3 -2. That scores the amphiphysin positive control as net acidic, which contradicts its known basic membrane-binding face, so the metric failed its control and was discarded rather than reported as a result.

The replacement was a screened-Coulomb (Debye-Hückel) potential: formal charges on Lys NZ, Arg CZ, Asp CG, Glu CD and His NE2, dielectric 78, Debye length 8 Å (150 mM monovalent salt), probe grid placed 6 Å above the surface, probes within 4 Å of protein discarded. The concave side was identified by fitting a parabola to the CA coordinates in the principal-axis frame and taking the side the parabola opens toward. That assignment was validated on amphiphysin: K132, K133, K134, K137 and R140, the known membrane-binding residues, all land on the concave face at offsets +7.8 to +11.5 Å.

The control passes and ACAP1 shows no concave polarization, consistent with the experimental result. The ACAP3 value of +0.220 is about 70% of the control, but it rests on an AlphaFold monomer superposed onto an assumed ACAP1-like dimer (TM 0.854, 228 aligned residues, mean deviation 2.14 Å, 45.2% identity). That is a lead, not a measurement, and it cannot overturn the ACAP1 experiment.

F5. ACAP3 has an InterPro-supported BAR domain that UniProt does not annotate

An InterPro API query for Q96P50 returns IPR004148 (BAR domain) at residues 3-236, IPR027267 (AH/BAR domain superfamily) at 1-291, IPR042695 (ACAP3, BAR domain) at 16-215, CDD cd07637 (BAR domain of ArfGAP with coiled-coil, ANK repeat and PH domains 3) at 16-215, and Pfam PF16746 at 3-236. Equivalent calls exist for ACAP1 (Q15027, IPR004148 at 5-238) and ACAP2 (Q15057, IPR004148 at 5-238).

The UniProt feature table for Q96P50 lists only Domain PH 268-363, Domain Arf-GAP 403-525 and Zinc finger C4-type 418-441. It carries no BAR feature, while Q15027 and Q15057 both carry an explicit Domain BAR 1-226 feature. This is a UniProt annotation gap, not evidence that ACAP3 lacks a BAR domain: four domain resources call it, one of them with an ACAP3-specific model, and the ACAP3 AlphaFold BAR superposes onto the ACAP1 BAR crystal at TM 0.854 with 45.2% identity over 228 residues.

Mechanistic model

The immediate molecular function being tested is binding of the ACAP3 PH domain to anionic membrane lipid. The predicted site is K277, R278, R289, K302 and K339, on the beta1-beta2 and beta3-beta4 loops, with N281 and Y300 contributing non-charged contacts.

The consequence of that binding in this family is allosteric. Kam et al. (PMID: 10734117) found that "phosphatidylinositol 4, 5-bisphosphate (PtdIns-4,5-P(2)) specifically activated Arf GAP. D3 phosphorylated phosphoinositides were less effective. Activation involved PtdIns-4,5-P(2) binding to the PH domain", and that binding produced a conformational change in the GAP domain detectable by limited proteolysis. The same paper separates recruitment from activation: "(i) the PH domain was dispensable for targeting to PDGF-induced ruffles; (ii) activation and recruitment could be uncoupled". That separation constrains how negative cell-biological data on ACAP3 should be read: a failure to see PH-dependent membrane recruitment would not refute lipid binding.

One step removed from the direct activity is membrane engagement and curvature generation by the BAR-PH module. PMID: 25284369 assigns both to the PH domain in ACAP1, with the BAR domain contributing clustering contacts.

Downstream of the Arf6 GTP/GDP cycle sit the reported phenotypes, and these are not molecular function. Neurite outgrowth in mouse hippocampal neurons (PMID: 27330119) requires ACAP3 GAP activity, since the GAP-dead mutant does not rescue, and requires nucleotide cycling, since a fast-cycling Arf6 mutant rescues while GTP-locked and GDP-locked mutants do not. EGFR recycling and lysosomal degradation in lung adenocarcinoma (PMID: 41520057) is GAP-activity-dependent and further downstream. The papillary thyroid carcinoma phenotype via HDAC2 (PMID: 39098591) is an expression-level association. None of these support a lipid-binding term.

The computational evidence supports a phosphoinositide-binding site but does not resolve which phosphoinositide. GO:1901981 (phosphatidylinositol phosphate binding) states exactly that and is the term to lead with, annotated ISS with ASAP1 (UniProt Q9ULH1) or ACAP1 (Q15027) in the With field, since those are the paralogs carrying the measured activity. GO:0005546 asserts the PI(4,5)P2 headgroup, which rests entirely on ASAP1 transfer across a pocket that has lost two of six basic C-site positions, one of them fixed across the whole ACAP branch. That is a step beyond what the evidence carries.

The core molecular function of ACAP3 remains GO:0005096, measured directly in PMID: 27330119 with a GAP-dead rescue control. Lipid binding should be curated as a modulator of that core function rather than as an independent core function, because in ASAP1 the PH domain acts as an allosteric site on the GAP domain (PMID: 10734117, PMID: 16038802).

Separate from GO: UniProt Q96P50 lacks a BAR domain feature that InterPro, Pfam and CDD all call at residues 3-236, and that UniProt does annotate in ACAP1 and ACAP2. This is worth flagging upstream.

Conflicts and alternatives

Paralog transfer is the main risk. Every piece of direct biochemical evidence for phosphoinositide-dependent GAP activity here comes from ASAP1, ACAP1 or ACAP2, not from ACAP3. ACAP3 is the least studied of the three ACAPs. The pocket differences are real and measurable: 2 of 6 ASAP1 C-site basic positions are absent, and the shell is 4/12 basic rather than 6/13. An annotation copying ASAP1's PI(4,5)P2 specificity onto ACAP3 would be over-annotation.

The BAR clause is contradicted. The hypothesis asks whether the BAR domain carries a basic concave surface, with the implication that it contributes to membrane binding. For ACAP1, the one family member with experimental data, the answer is no on both counts. My ACAP3 BAR calculation gives a modestly positive value (+0.220 against the amphiphysin control +0.313), but it depends on a dimer model I built rather than observed.

The pocket may be occluded in the intact protein. PMID: 18675341 found that the ASAP1 PH-Arf GAP interdomain contact partially occludes the phosphoinositide site in soluble protein, with access restored on a lipid bilayer. A lipid-binding measurement on an isolated ACAP3 PH domain would therefore not fully predict behaviour of the intact protein.

Organism and isoform. The ACAP3 GAP data are from mouse neurons and HEK293T cells (PMID: 27330119). The structural work is on the human sequence. The 91-ortholog scan shows the pocket positions are invariant across vertebrates, so a mouse-human difference at this site is unlikely. Isoform-specific effects were not examined.

Methodological caveats in the computational work. At 17-25% identity, side-chain mapping is approximate. phenix.superpose_pdbs failed on these pairs, so a TM-align style routine was written for the analysis; that code and its outputs are saved. The residue-counting metric for BAR concave charge was discarded because it failed the amphiphysin positive control, and replaced with the screened-Coulomb calculation that passes it. The EBI Clustal Omega REST endpoint returned a temporary submission block, so the planned multiple sequence alignment was replaced with structural superposition plus local Smith-Waterman.

Database carry-over. UniProt's omission of the BAR domain in Q96P50 is a curation gap, not evidence of absence.

Limitations and knowledge gaps

  1. No direct lipid-binding measurement on ACAP3. Checked: PubMed searches for ACAP3 combined with phosphoinositide, PI(4,5)P2, lipid binding and centaurin beta terms, which returned either nothing or ASAP1/ACAP1 papers. This matters because every MF lipid-binding term for ACAP3 would currently be ISS from a paralog. A liposome co-sedimentation or SPR titration series on the isolated ACAP3 PH domain would resolve it.
  2. No experimental structure of ACAP3. Checked: the RCSB search API returned no entries for Q96P50. All pocket geometry is from AlphaFold, so the rotamers at K277, R289 and K302 are predicted. A crystal or cryo-EM structure of ACAP3 BAR-PH, ideally with ligand, would resolve it.
  3. Whether ACAP3 GAP activity is phosphoinositide dependent. PMID: 27330119 measured GAP activity but not its lipid dependence. This decides whether lipid binding modulates the core function or is a separate recruitment function. A GAP assay on vesicles of varying PI(4,5)P2 content, with and without the PH domain, would resolve it.
  4. Headgroup specificity. The pocket is less basic than ASAP1's and the two lost arginines sit at positions that in ASAP1 contact the inositol phosphates. ACAP3 may prefer phosphatidylserine or PI4P over PI(4,5)P2. A quantitative titration against PI3P, PI4P, PI5P, PI(3,4)P2, PI(4,5)P2, PI(3,4,5)P3 and PS would resolve it.
  5. Whether the ACAP3 BAR domain dimerises like ACAP1. The ACAP3 BAR electrostatics assumed the 4NSW dimer arrangement. SEC-MALS or crosslinking on recombinant ACAP3 BAR, or a predicted-dimer model with PAE support, would resolve it.
  6. Subcellular localisation of ACAP3 in human cells. Checked: no primary localisation figure for human ACAP3 appears in the papers retrieved. This matters for any CC term. Immunofluorescence or a tagged knock-in line would resolve it.

Proposed follow-up experiments and actions

Ranked by how efficiently each separates the live alternatives.

  1. Charge-reversal mutants K277E, R289E, K302E and K339E in a GAP assay on PI(4,5)P2 vesicles. If these abolish lipid stimulation without abolishing basal GAP activity, the mapped residues are the site. This is the single most informative experiment, because it tests the exact residues the structural mapping identifies.
  2. Liposome co-sedimentation of recombinant ACAP3 PH (roughly residues 258-375) across a PI(4,5)P2 dose series, with ASAP1 PH in parallel as a positive control. This separates "ACAP3 binds anionic lipid with reduced affinity" from "ACAP3 has lost the site". A right-shifted but sigmoidal curve supports the hypothesis at reduced affinity.
  3. Lipid titration across PI3P, PI4P, PI5P, PI(3,4)P2, PI(4,5)P2, PI(3,4,5)P3 and PS. Decides between GO:1901981 and GO:0005546, and tests whether the loss of the two ASAP1 arginines shifts preference away from the 4,5-pattern.
  4. Gain-of-function mutants F283R and A307R, restoring the ASAP1 arginines. If these increase lipid affinity, the structural mapping is correct and the reduced charge is functionally real rather than an alignment artefact.
  5. Liposome tubulation by ACAP3 BAR alone against ACAP3 BAR-PH. Repeats the PMID: 25284369 experiment on ACAP3 and settles the BAR clause for this paralog by measurement rather than transfer.
