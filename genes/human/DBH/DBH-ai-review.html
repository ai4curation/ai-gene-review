Gene Description

DBH (dopamine beta-hydroxylase, also called dopamine beta-monooxygenase; EC 1.14.17.1) is a copper- and ascorbate (vitamin C)-dependent monooxygenase that catalyzes the penultimate step of catecholamine biosynthesis: hydroxylation of the beta-carbon of dopamine to form (R)-noradrenaline (norepinephrine), with reduction of molecular O2 and oxidation of two ascorbate molecules to monodehydroascorbate radicals. Each subunit binds two type-2 copper ions (CuH, the electron-transfer site, and CuM, the dioxygen-binding/substrate-hydroxylation site) and belongs to the copper type II, ascorbate-dependent monooxygenase family together with peptidylglycine monooxygenase (PAM/PHM), MOXD1 and insect tyramine beta-monooxygenase. The enzyme is a glycosylated homotetramer (two disulfide-linked dimers) with an N-terminal DOMON domain, a two-lobed catalytic core, and a C-terminal dimerization domain. DBH is expressed in noradrenergic and adrenergic cells (sympathetic neurons, central noradrenergic neurons, and adrenal medulla chromaffin cells), where it resides in the membrane and soluble lumenal content of catecholamine secretory (chromaffin) granules and synaptic vesicles; it is synthesized as a type II single-pass membrane protein, and proteolytic cleavage of the membrane anchor releases a soluble form that is co-released with catecholamines by exocytosis and appears in plasma and cerebrospinal fluid. Loss of DBH function in humans causes autosomal-recessive congenital dopamine beta-hydroxylase deficiency (orthostatic hypotension 1), in which noradrenaline and adrenaline are absent while dopamine accumulates.

Copper- and ascorbate-dependent dopamine beta-monooxygenase that hydroxylates the beta-carbon of dopamine to produce (R)-noradrenaline, catalyzing the noradrenaline- forming step of catecholamine biosynthesis within catecholamine secretory granules of noradrenergic and adrenergic cells.

DBH (Dopamine beta-hydroxylase) — Review Notes

UniProt: P09172 (DOPO_HUMAN). Gene: DBH. HGNC:2689. Chromosome 9q34.2.
Taxon: Homo sapiens (NCBITaxon:9606).

Core identity and reaction

DBH = dopamine beta-hydroxylase / dopamine beta-monooxygenase; EC 1.14.17.1.
It catalyzes the penultimate step of catecholamine biosynthesis: hydroxylation of
dopamine at the beta-carbon to give (R)-noradrenaline (norepinephrine).

Catalytic activity (from UniProt CATALYTIC ACTIVITY, RHEA:19117):
"dopamine + 2 L-ascorbate + O2 = (R)-noradrenaline + 2 monodehydro-L-ascorbate
radical + H2O" [file:human/DBH/DBH-uniprot.txt "dopamine + 2 L-ascorbate + O2 = (R)-noradrenaline + 2"].

  • Cofactor: Cu(2+). "Binds 2 copper ions per subunit" (CuH electron-transfer site;
    CuM dioxygen-binding/substrate-hydroxylation site) [file:human/DBH/DBH-uniprot.txt
    "Binds 2 copper ions per subunit"]. Confirmed by crystal structure
    PMID:27152332.
  • Electron donor: ascorbate (vitamin C). "The reaction also requires two electrons
    provided by two ascorbate molecules" PMID:27152332. UniProt keyword "Vitamin C".
  • KW: Copper; Metal-binding; Monooxygenase; Oxidoreductase; Vitamin C;
    Catecholamine biosynthesis.

Family: copper type II, ascorbate-dependent monooxygenase family (with PHM/PAM,
MOXD1, insect TBH) [file:human/DBH/DBH-uniprot.txt "Belongs to the copper type II
ascorbate-dependent monooxygenase family"].

Localization

Secretory (chromaffin) granules / synaptic vesicles of noradrenergic and adrenergic
cells; exists as membrane-bound and soluble forms; soluble form is secreted with
catecholamines by exocytosis.
- UniProt SUBCELLULAR LOCATION: secretory vesicle lumen; chromaffin granule lumen;
Secreted; secretory vesicle membrane (Single-pass type II membrane protein);
chromaffin granule membrane.
- Reactome R-HSA-209891: "The enzyme exists in the secretory vesicles as both soluble
and membrane-bound forms. The soluble form is secreted with catecholamines by
exocytosis whereas the membrane-bound form is recycled into the vesicles."
- Topology: N-terminal cytoplasmic (1-16), TM signal-anchor (17-37), intragranular
lumenal domain (38-617). Type II single-pass membrane protein; proteolytic cleavage
after the anchor releases the soluble form.

description: >-
  DBH (dopamine beta-hydroxylase, also called dopamine beta-monooxygenase; EC
  1.14.17.1) is a copper- and ascorbate (vitamin C)-dependent monooxygenase that
  catalyzes the penultimate step of catecholamine biosynthesis: hydroxylation of
  the beta-carbon of dopamine to form (R)-noradrenaline (norepinephrine), with
  reduction of molecular O2 and oxidation of two ascorbate molecules to
  monodehydroascorbate radicals. Each subunit binds two type-2 copper ions (CuH,
  the electron-transfer site, and CuM, the dioxygen-binding/substrate-hydroxylation
  site) and belongs to the copper type II, ascorbate-dependent monooxygenase family
  together with peptidylglycine monooxygenase (PAM/PHM), MOXD1 and insect tyramine
  beta-monooxygenase. The enzyme is a glycosylated homotetramer (two disulfide-linked
  dimers) with an N-terminal DOMON domain, a two-lobed catalytic core, and a
  C-terminal dimerization domain. DBH is expressed in noradrenergic and adrenergic
  cells (sympathetic neurons, central noradrenergic neurons, and adrenal medulla
  chromaffin cells), where it resides in the membrane and soluble lumenal content of
  catecholamine secretory (chromaffin) granules and synaptic vesicles; it is
  synthesized as a type II single-pass membrane protein, and proteolytic cleavage of
  the membrane anchor releases a soluble form that is co-released with catecholamines
  by exocytosis and appears in plasma and cerebrospinal fluid. Loss of DBH function
  in humans causes autosomal-recessive congenital dopamine beta-hydroxylase
  deficiency (orthostatic hypotension 1), in which noradrenaline and adrenaline are
  absent while dopamine accumulates.
