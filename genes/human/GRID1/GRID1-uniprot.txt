CC   -!- FUNCTION: Member of the ionotropic glutamate receptor family, which
CC       plays a crucial role in synaptic organization and signal transduction
CC       in the central nervous system. Although it shares structural features
CC       with ionotropic glutamate receptors, does not bind glutamate as a
CC       primary ligand (PubMed:38060673). Instead, forms trans-synaptic
CC       adhesion complexes with presynaptic neurexins and cerebellins,
CC       regulating NMDA and AMPA receptor activity and influencing synaptic
CC       plasticity through signal transduction (By similarity). In the presence
CC       of neurexins and cerebellins, forms cation-selective channels that are
CC       proposed to be gated by glycine and D-serine (By similarity). However,
CC       recent research disputes this ligand-gated cation channel activity
CC       (PubMed:39052831). Cation-selective ion channel can be triggered by
CC       GRM1 in dopaminergic neurons (By similarity). Also acts as a receptor
CC       for GABA, modulating inhibitory synaptic plasticity through non-
CC       ionotropic mechanisms (PubMed:38060673). {ECO:0000250|UniProtKB:O43424,
CC       ECO:0000250|UniProtKB:Q61627, ECO:0000269|PubMed:38060673,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:39052831}.

CC   -!- SUBUNIT: Homodimer (PubMed:38060673). Interacts (via extracellular N-

CC       CBLN2; the interactions mediate the trans-synaptic adhesion complexes
CC       also with neurexins and are required for ligand-gated cation channel
CC       activity. {ECO:0000250|UniProtKB:Q61627, ECO:0000269|PubMed:38060673}.
