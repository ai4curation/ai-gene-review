# MCM2 manual deep research

Automated Falcon and Perplexity deep-research generation was unavailable in this workstream, so this file records the manual synthesis used for the review.

MCM2 is one of the six AAA+ ATPase subunits of the MCM2-7 hexamer. Human CMG biochemical work shows that MCM2-7 becomes the core of the CDC45-MCM-GINS helicase, that CMG binds ssDNA in the presence of magnesium and ATP, hydrolyses ATP, and unwinds duplex DNA during leading-strand synthesis [PMID:22474384]. A human CMG cryoEM structure places all six MCM subunits around the bound DNA spiral and shows that the MCM2-MCM5 interface remains open in the captured ATPgammaS state [PMID:32453425].

MCM2 is incorporated into the canonical MCM2-7 complex and into human CMG; it is therefore reviewed as a contributor to complex-level DNA helicase and ATPase activities rather than as an autonomous helicase chain. MCM2 also has a subunit-specific histone H3-H4 binding module in its N-terminal tail; structural and biophysical work localized the H3-H4-binding region to MCM2 residues 69-138 and showed direct binding to the H3-H4 tetramer [PMID:25618846].

Several MCM2 rows come from protein-interaction screens and disease-context work. The R44C deafness paper found MCM2 expression in cochlear hair cells and a slight apoptosis increase when the variant was overexpressed in HEK293 cells, but this does not establish that MCM2 normally performs an apoptosis or cochlea-development step [PMID:26196677]. A later RAGE paper supports a replication-stress interaction with MCM2/MCM7 but is still only generic protein binding in GOA [PMID:36807739].

No PAINT IRD or IKR loss applies to the human MCM2 lineage in PTHR11630.
