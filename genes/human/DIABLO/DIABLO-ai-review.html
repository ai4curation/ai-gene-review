Gene Description

DIABLO, also called SMAC, is an inhibitor-of-apoptosis protein antagonist. The canonical precursor enters mitochondria and is processed by mitochondrial processing peptidase and PARL, exposing an N-terminal IAP-binding motif. After mitochondrial outer-membrane permeabilization, mature SMAC is released from the intermembrane space into the cytosol, where its N-terminal motif and dimeric interaction surfaces engage IAP proteins including XIAP and BIRC6. These interactions relieve restraint of apoptotic caspases and promote intrinsic apoptotic signaling. Alternative splice products differ in targeting and IAP-regulatory properties.

DIABLO / SMAC manual curation notes

2026-09-30

  • Completed a manual DIABLO/SMAC review from GOA, UniProt, cached PubMed
    records, Reactome events, the PTHR32247 PANTHER family, and annotation-reviewer
    follow-up.
  • Canonical DIABLO is a mitochondrial IAP antagonist rather than a caspase or
    ubiquitin-ligase enzyme. The founding SMAC and DIABLO papers identify a
    mitochondrial protein that is released to the cytosol during apoptosis and
    promotes caspase activation by binding IAPs and removing their inhibitory
    activity [PMID:10929711, "Smac promotes caspase-9 activation by binding to
    inhibitor of apoptosis proteins"; PMID:10929712, "DIABLO may promote
    apoptosis by binding to IAPs and preventing them from inhibiting caspases"].
  • The molecular surface is the processed N terminus. Structural work showed
    that the SMAC AVPI motif engages IAP BIR grooves and that SMAC dimerization is
    required for strong apoptotic activity [PMID:10972280, "the amino-terminal
    four residues of Smac/DIABLO are indispensable for its function"]. PARL
    cleavage in mitochondria generates the mature N-terminal IAP-binding motif;
    loss of PARL maturation leaves SMAC unable to bind XIAP, while cytosolic
    cleaved SMAC rescues apoptosis in PARL-deficient cells PMID:28288130.
  • GO currently lacks a narrow "IAP antagonist activity" term. Direct XIAP,
    cIAP1/BIRC2, ML-IAP/BIRC7, and BIRC6 rows were therefore narrowed from
    GO:0005515 protein binding to broad GO:0140678 molecular function inhibitor activity; less mechanistic survivin, ARTS-context, PRKCD, AREL1,
    and high-throughput binary interactome rows were removed as generic partner
    edges.
  • BIRC6/BRUCE is both a SMAC antagonist and a SMAC substrate context. Bartke et

DIABLO evidence guide

DIABLO/SMAC antagonizes inhibitor-of-apoptosis proteins after mitochondrial release. One BIR-binding core summarizes this activity, including XIAP and BIRC6, in the cytosol. Mature SMAC occupies the mitochondrial intermembrane space; its canonical processing intermediate is associated with the inner membrane. MPP and PARL perform the precursor cleavage; DIABLO is not assigned their protease activity.

This review applies the ClinGen project curation instructions. Positive interaction evidence is retained as non-core when no supported, more informative molecular-function term is available. A partner-class refinement describes binding capacity; it does not assign the partner's catalytic or transport activity to DIABLO. Broader sound annotations use MODIFY with supported replacements, including where the specific term is already represented. No new annotation is proposed.

Propagation and contextual processes

The PAINT PTHR32247 family places the mitochondrial, oxidative-stress-apoptosis and neuronal-apoptosis IBDs at ancestral node PTN001283560, on the human lineage. Human leaf PTN002529272 and mouse leaf PTN000790380 descend from it. Human DIABLO appearing as an experimental source for mitochondrial localization is legitimate target grounding. The aligned mature N-terminal AVPI motif is human 56–59 and mouse 54–57; conservation supports compatibility, not automatic proof of every cellular context.

The oxidative-stress donor is supported by PMID:16085184(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16085184/), abstract only: C2C12 expression/antisense and peroxide experiments. PMID:14623868(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14623868/), selected original Methods/Results, tests recombinant SMAC plus cytochrome c in mouse sympathetic neurons with a motif control. Endogenous SMAC release alone is insufficient in the tested wild-type context. Both process inferences are retained as contextual, non-core roles; neither a short donor list nor target self-inclusion invalidates PAINT placement.

PMID:20614026(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20614026/), original Table 1, recruitment Results and capture Methods, detects mouse SMAC in activated CD40 complexes. Tail deletion and TRAF2 controls support the contextual complex and membrane-side ISS annotations. The human-ectodomain/mouse-tail chimera does not turn this into a human-cell experiment, and co-complex recruitment does not prove direct CD40 binding.

PMID:12642862(https://www.nature.com/articles/1206322), published abstract, reports G0/G1 arrest and reduced BrdU incorporation after mature-SMAC overexpression in human K562/CEM cells. This independently supports a non-core cell-cycle effect. The imported PMID:19153467 cell-cycle linkage remains explicitly unverified; no physiological mechanism or second core is inferred.

The current survivin-complex definition, GO:1990713, specifies an anti-apoptotic complex and states: “Complex contains a component which belongs to the family of baculoviral IAP (inhibitor of apoptosis) repeat-containing proteins.” Its definition xrefs include PMID:19153467(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19153467/). The current BIRC5 example and narrow survivin-homodimer synonym do not replace that broader definition. The original abstract and curated peptide/domain records (IM-22028-2, IM-22028-4) establish human SMAC 56–61 peptide association with BIRC2 BIR3, without establishing an endogenous full-length BIRC5 assembly. The annotation remains contextual and non-core.

The official ARBA00027745 and ARBA00028303 condition sets include target-matching FunFam 1.20.58.70:FF:000012, with Vertebrata and Mammalia respectively. Their broad positive-apoptosis and intrinsic-signaling conclusions are distinguished. InterPro family/fold mappings through IPR015142 and IPR009062 support broad localization/process calls; independent target experiments support the specific refinements.
