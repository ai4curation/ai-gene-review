Gene Description

PPP1R15B encodes CReP (constitutive repressor of eIF2alpha phosphorylation), the constitutively expressed, short-lived paralog of GADD34 (PPP1R15A) and a non-catalytic regulatory subunit of protein phosphatase 1. CReP binds the PP1 catalytic subunit through a C-terminal PP1-binding region and captures the trimeric eIF2 substrate through a largely disordered central recruitment module whose helical elements clamp eIF2gamma and the C-terminal domain of eIF2alpha, far from the Ser51 phosphosite, presenting it to PP1 for dephosphorylation. The resulting holophosphatase keeps basal eIF2alpha phosphorylation low in unstressed cells; loss of CReP raises phospho-eIF2alpha and activates the integrated stress response. The CReP-PP1 complex is a target of the eIF2alpha dephosphorylation inhibitor salubrinal. Biallelic PPP1R15B variants that impair PP1 or eIF2 binding cause a syndrome of microcephaly, short stature, intellectual disability and impaired glucose metabolism.

Core Functions

Constitutive PP1 regulatory subunit that binds PP1c and recruits trimeric eIF2, directing dephosphorylation of eIF2alpha Ser51 to keep basal eIF2alpha phosphorylation low and translation initiation active in unstressed cells.

Suggested Questions for Experts

Q: Where in the cell does endogenous CReP act? The ER is assigned only by phylogenetic inference from GADD34-type N-terminal targeting; is CReP ER-membrane-associated in human cells?

Suggested Experiments

Experiment: Localize endogenous PPP1R15B (tagged at the native locus) by imaging and fractionation with ER and mitochondrial markers, and test whether its N-terminal region is required for membrane association and basal eIF2alpha dephosphorylation.

Hypothesis: CReP, like GADD34, is targeted to the cytoplasmic face of the ER by its N-terminal region, and this targeting is needed for efficient basal dephosphorylation.
