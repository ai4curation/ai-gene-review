# CACNA1F: channel function and retinal context

CACNA1F supplies the CaV1.4 alpha1 pore. Voltage-dependent calcium entry links retinal membrane voltage to transmitter-release signaling. The direct functional evidence is heterologous electrophysiology of the human channel and its variants, while human mutation families connect the mechanism to visual dysfunction (PMID:15897456; PMID:27226626; PMID:9662399; PMID:9662400). This supports one integrated channel core: high voltage-gated calcium channel activity, calcium import across the plasma membrane and visual perception. Calcium-dependent modulation is an aspect of the pore's behavior, rather than a separate calcium-sensor or auxiliary-subunit activity assigned to CACNA1F.

The original CSNB2 studies identified CACNA1F variants in affected human families. Predicted protein truncation is distinguished from measured current loss for each individual allele (PMID:9662399; PMID:9662400). A later variant study directly showed a hyperpolarizing activation shift and slower inactivation, demonstrating that abnormal function can involve increased activity rather than uniform loss of function (PMID:15897456).
