Gene Description

CACNA1F encodes the pore-forming alpha1 subunit of the CaV1.4 L-type voltage-gated calcium channel. Calcium entry through CaV1.4 couples retinal membrane voltage to neurotransmitter release and supports visual signaling. Its C-terminal splice variants differ in voltage-dependent activation, calcium-dependent inactivation and modulation by CaBP4. Pathogenic variants cause X-linked incomplete congenital stationary night blindness, cone-rod dystrophy and Aland Island eye disease; the molecular effects include altered splicing, predicted loss of channel function and experimentally demonstrated changes in gating.

Alternative C-terminal splicing and CaBP4 change channel activation and inactivation, so the kinetic behavior depends on the product and its molecular partners.

CACNA1F: channel function and retinal context

CACNA1F supplies the CaV1.4 alpha1 pore. Voltage-dependent calcium entry links retinal membrane voltage to transmitter-release signaling. The direct functional evidence is heterologous electrophysiology of the human channel and its variants, while human mutation families connect the mechanism to visual dysfunction (PMID:15897456; PMID:27226626; PMID:9662399; PMID:9662400). This supports one integrated channel core: high voltage-gated calcium channel activity, calcium import across the plasma membrane and visual perception. Calcium-dependent modulation is an aspect of the pore's behavior, rather than a separate calcium-sensor or auxiliary-subunit activity assigned to CACNA1F.

The original CSNB2 studies identified CACNA1F variants in affected human families. Predicted protein truncation is distinguished from measured current loss for each individual allele (PMID:9662399; PMID:9662400). A later variant study directly showed a hyperpolarizing activation shift and slower inactivation, demonstrating that abnormal function can involve increased activity rather than uniform loss of function (PMID:15897456). The paper's I745T coordinate and the reviewed record's I756T coordinate remain distinct descriptions until an explicit sequence alignment establishes their relation.

C-terminal splicing materially changes CaV1.4 behavior. The exon 47-lacking variants in PMID:27226626 display calcium-dependent inactivation and altered activation; CaBP4 suppresses their inactivation without reproducing every activation effect seen for full-length channels. The canonical abstract and indexed primary Methods were inspected. Those Methods use human channel constructs with beta2x13 and alpha2delta4 in HEK-293T cells, and human/primate retinal RNA for splice analysis. These defined heterologous conditions do not establish every native retinal cell's subunit mixture or product abundance. Original images and complete Results were not reanalyzed.

Six reviewed products are preserved exactly, including display name 3 mapped to O60840-4 and display names 4/5/6 mapped to O60840-5/-6/-7. The historical 1966-residue sequence cited by PMID:9662399 is associated with source isoform 2 and does not replace the current 1977-residue reference. No extra product was invented to fill the absent accession suffix 3.

CORDX3 is supported by a primary human family and transcript study (PMID:16505158). Its fetched Letter/COMMENT_EDITORIAL classification does not erase the reported sequencing and RNA data. The altered splicing predicts channel truncation or internal deletion; the abstract does not measure the affected channel currents. The distinct Aland Island eye disease association is supported by the original family study (PMID:17525176). Human DNA sequencing and lymphoblast RNA analysis identify an exon 30 deletion and its transcript consequence; altered channel topology is predicted. The complete normal cached abstract was read, while full text and images were unavailable. The earlier failure to find a mutation in the classic AIED family (PMID:12111638) is a historical observation rather than a reason to dismiss the later result.
