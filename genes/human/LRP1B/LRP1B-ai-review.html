Gene Description

LRP1B is a very large type-I single-pass cell-surface member of the LDL-receptor family, with an extracellular repeat-rich ligand-binding region and a short cytoplasmic tail containing two endocytic NPxY motifs. Human LRP1B minireceptors bind, internalize, and degrade selected protease and protease-inhibitor ligands and also internalize VLDL; recombinant ectodomains bind fibrinogen and apoE-bearing lipoproteins. LRP1B minireceptors also bind selected APP forms and alter APP processing. Internalization is markedly slower than for LRP1, prolonging cargo residence at the cell surface. In cultured human non-small-cell lung cancer cells, depletion of endogenous LRP1B increases proliferation, whereas expression of full-length mouse Lrp1b or a human LRP1B minireceptor suppresses growth. The responsible cargo and in-vivo mechanism remain unresolved.

Core Functions

At the plasma membrane, LRP1B binds VLDL and mediates receptor-dependent internalization. Human minireceptor experiments separately show binding, internalization, and degradation of RAP, uPA, tPA, and PAI-1, while recombinant ectodomains bind fibrinogen and apoE-bearing lipoproteins. A targeted minireceptor study also demonstrates APP association and altered processing, and cytoplasmic- tail experiments show that either NPxY motif can support uptake. LRP1B internalizes cargo much more slowly than LRP1, which can prolong surface retention. Most direct trafficking assays used region-IV minireceptors, so they do not establish the full endogenous ligand repertoire or a major systemic plasma-lipoprotein-clearance role.

Direct receptor and trafficking evidence

The strongest direct core evidence uses a human LRP1B region-IV minireceptor.
RAP/LRPAP1, uPA/PLAU, tPA/PLAT, and PAI-1/SERPINE1 each showed binding,
internalization, and degradation [PMID:11384978 The putative tumor suppressor
LRP1B, "each demonstrate binding, internalization, and degradation via mLRP1B4"].
These experiments support a multiligand endocytic-receptor role, but they do not
show that every LRP1 ligand is an LRP1B ligand or establish the behavior of
endogenous full-length receptor in a native tissue.

LRP1B trafficking is not simply LRP1 trafficking transferred by homology.
Minireceptor experiments found LRP1B "is internalized at a 15-fold slower rate
than LRP1" [PMID:17658514 Slow endocytosis of the LDL receptor-related protein
1B]. Either NPXY motif could support internalization, while the complete tail
contributed to inefficient adaptor recognition. This slow-endocytosis regime is
a direct paralog-specific distinction.

PMID:21420681 provides direct support for lipoprotein-particle receptor activity:
"ApoE-carrying lipoproteins" bound LRP1B ectodomains, and cells expressing the
region-IV minireceptor bound and internalized VLDL [PMID:21420681 LRP1b shows
restricted expression in human tissues, "binding as well as internalization of very low density lipoproteins"].
This evidence is stronger than the mixed LRP1/LRP1B phylogenetic inference, but
the uptake assay used a minireceptor in CHO cells. The authors explicitly argued
against a major systemic plasma-clearance role because LRP1B expression is low
and tissue-restricted. The same study verified fibrinogen binding to recombinant
ectodomains [PMID:21420681, "fibrinogen was verified by co-immunoprecipitation."].

APP and screen evidence

A targeted minireceptor study showed an immunoprecipitable LRP1B-APP complex and
altered APP trafficking. The result was functionally coherent with slow LRP1B
endocytosis: mLRP1B4 increased surface APP, reduced A-beta production, and
increased soluble APP secretion [PMID:15126508 The low density lipoprotein
receptor-related protein 1B retains beta-amyloid precursor protein, "APP produced less Abeta and secreted more soluble APP"].
The tested soluble APP form containing a Kunitz protease-inhibitor domain was
bound and degraded, whereas the tested form lacking that domain was not. This is
APP-isoform selectivity, not evidence for an LRP1B-specific isoform. The cultured
minireceptor result does not establish that LRP1B prevents Alzheimer disease in
humans.

PMID:32814053 is a systematic yeast-two-hybrid/interactome screen whose abstract
describes approximately 30,000 "candidate interactions" [PMID:32814053
Interactome Mapping Provides a Network of Neurodegenerative Disease Proteins].
The associated APP, CHRNA4, and PPP2R2A records should remain screen-level
candidate interactions unless targeted experiments establish consequences. APP
has independent targeted support from PMID:15126508; the other screen partners
do not. PMID:23382219 establishes general PX-FERM recognition of NPxY/NxxY cargo
motifs and broad putative-cargo screening, but LRP1B is absent from the cached
main text. It therefore does not independently establish a stable LRP1B
signaling-receptor complex.

Species, paralog, and isoform boundaries

Mouse Lrp1b loss lacking a membrane-inserted receptor produced viable, fertile
animals, with the authors invoking restricted expression and "functional
compensation by LRP1 or other receptors" [PMID:15082773 Normal development and
fertility of knockout mice lacking LRP1b]. This result cannot be used to erase
the direct human-cell functions, but it makes universal developmental or
nonredundant-organismal claims unsafe. Mouse Lrp1b was a single 600-kDa species
rather than undergoing LRP1-like furin processing, another paralog boundary.

The mouse study reported a differentially spliced form in adrenal gland and
testis [PMID:15082773, "a differentially spliced form is present in the adrenal gland and in"].
The reviewed human Q9NZR2 record has no curated alternative-products section,
and PMID:21420681 did not detect the predicted exon-90-skipped human transcript
in the tissue panels tested. Mouse splice-form evidence must not be projected as
an established human Q9NZR2 isoform.

PANTHER PTHR22722:SF5 includes both LRP1 and LRP1B rather than resolving an exact
LRP1B-only subfamily. Family or IBA transfer must therefore be checked against
direct LRP1B evidence. In particular, LRP1B's much slower internalization,
different processing, restricted expression, and distinct growth effects show
why broad paralog transfer is unsafe.

Disease and model boundaries

The discovery study found homozygous deletions and abnormal LRP1B transcripts in
human NSCLC cell lines [PMID:10766186, "Homozygous deletions in LRP-DIT were detected in 17%"].
This is strong evidence of recurrent somatic inactivation in that panel, not by
itself proof of a normal molecular function or a hereditary human disorder.

Functional follow-up supports a cell-autonomous growth-suppressive effect, but
the model is mixed-species and construct-dependent. Full-length mouse Lrp1b and
a human LRP1B minireceptor reduced proliferation in human NSCLC cells, while
siRNA against endogenous human LRP1B increased Calu-1 proliferation
[PMID:27626682 Expression of a recombinant full-length LRP1B receptor, "siRNA-mediated LRP1B knockdown significantly enhanced cellular proliferation"].
LRP1 overexpression did not reproduce the effect, supporting paralog specificity.
These are cultured cancer-cell results, not an in-vivo human tumor-suppression
experiment, and they do not make every large cancer sequencing association a
functional LRP1B result. Biomarker, mutation-burden, prognosis, and therapy-
response screens should remain disease associations unless backed by targeted
mechanistic evidence.

Bounded functional conclusion

Direct evidence supports LRP1B as a slowly internalizing, multiligand,
single-pass endocytic receptor. Human minireceptors bind, internalize, and
degrade selected protease/inhibitor ligands, internalize VLDL, and bind selected
APP forms with consequences for APP processing; recombinant ectodomains bind
apoE-bearing lipoproteins and fibrinogen. The best-supported paralog-specific
consequence is prolonged surface retention of cargo relative to LRP1. Normal
endogenous roles in individual human tissues, the physiological importance of
systemic lipid clearance, stable signaling-complex membership, and the in-vivo
mechanism of tumor suppression remain less certain.
