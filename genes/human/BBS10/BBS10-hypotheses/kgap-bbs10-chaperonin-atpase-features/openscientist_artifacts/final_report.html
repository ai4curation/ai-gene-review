BBS10 (Q8TAM1): does the chaperonin-like fold retain ATP binding and hydrolysis?

Hypothesis slug: kgap-bbs10-chaperonin-atpase-features Target: human BBS10, UniProt Q8TAM1, NCBITaxon:9606 Source: genes/human/BBS10/BBS10-ai-review.yaml, focus type free_text Date of database retrieval: 2026-11-24

Every number below comes from an analysis executed in this job. Code, intermediate JSON, CSV tables and the figures are saved under provenance/bbs10/ and provenance/plot_*.png. No local *-bioinformatics file was read. Where a resource could not be used, that is stated plainly in the Limitations section.

Summary

Human BBS10 is a genuine group II chaperonin by fold and has an assembled, open, ATP-sized cavity in its equatorial domain, but it does not retain the residues that group II chaperonins and GroEL use to hydrolyse ATP. The verdict on the seed hypothesis is partially supported, split by sub-claim: the structural arm holds, the catalytic arm is refuted, and the prediction for curation is nucleotide binding plausible but unmeasured, hydrolysis predicted absent, with BBS10 acting as a non-catalytic chaperonin-fold scaffold.

The structural test was built from scratch rather than quoted from memory. Nucleotide-bound group II chaperonin structures were downloaded (PDB 3RUW, the Methanococcus maripaludis chaperonin with ADP-AlFx-Mg2+ as a transition-state mimic at 2.70 Å; 3RUV and 1Q3Q with AMP-PNP), the nucleotide site was defined empirically as the 28 residues with any heavy atom within 4.2 Å of ADP, AlF3 or Mg2+ in 3RUW chain A, and a TM-align style aligner was implemented and used to map those positions onto AlphaFold models of BBS10, BBS6/MKKS, BBS12 and all eight human CCT subunits. BBS10 superimposes on 3RUW chain A at TM-score 0.836, between CCT4 (0.87) and BBS6 (0.784). Transplanting the ligands into the BBS10 frame gives 122 protein heavy atoms within 5 Å and only 10 closer than 2.5 Å, so the cavity exists and is not occluded. Of the six residues that form the catalytic and Mg2+ part of the site, BBS10 retains one (Asp-81), against 6 of 6 for CCT1 through CCT7.

Two further results sharpen the picture. A register-robust scan over whole helices, rather than a single aligned column, finds no aspartate or glutamate side chain within 7.27 Å of the transplanted Mg/AlF3 centre anywhere in BBS10, against 3.40 Å for Mm-cpn Asp-386 and 4.13 Å for CCT5 Asp-404. Across 15 full-length vertebrate orthologs, the 18 mapped site positions are significantly more conserved than the other 702 scorable positions (median identity 0.931 versus 0.533, Mann-Whitney p = 3.0e-05, rank-biserial r = 0.553), while the catalytic-aspartate column carries glycine in 12 of 15 and aspartate in none. The pocket is maintained by selection; the catalyst is not. The current GO record reflects none of this: GO:0005524 on BBS10, BBS6 and BBS12 is an InterPro IEA rule firing on PF00118, and BBS10 carries zero EXP and zero IDA annotations against four EXP and nine IDA for the CCT4 control.

Three caveats carry the most weight for a curator. There is no experimental BBS10 structure in the PDB and no published measurement of BBS10 nucleotide binding or ATPase, so every structural statement here is a prediction from an AlphaFold model, well ordered over the site (pLDDT 88-96) but in an apo-like conformation. The pocket is under purifying selection, which argues against describing it as a dead relic and keeps a weak or regulatory nucleotide interaction open. The transfer of the GroEL mutational result to BBS10 is inference from structural equivalence, not a measurement on BBS10.

Key findings

F1. BBS10 has the group II chaperonin fold but places only 1 of 6 catalytic residues correctly

Structure-based alignment onto chain A of 3RUW (516 residues) gives TM-scores of 0.94 for CCT1, 0.93 for CCT5 and CCT6A, 0.92 for CCT2, CCT3, CCT7 and CCT8, 0.87 for CCT4, 0.836 for BBS10, 0.784 for BBS6 and 0.732 for BBS12. Fold-level homology is therefore not in doubt for any of the three BBS proteins, and BBS10 sits closest to the canonical chaperonins of the three. After superposition the 28 site positions land with median CA deviations of 0.98 Å for CCT5, 1.37 Å for BBS10, 1.63 Å for BBS6 and 2.57 Å for BBS12, with median AlphaFold pLDDT of 91 to 96 at those positions in all four models, so the mapping is not being read off disordered loops.

Identity at the six Mg2+, gamma-phosphate and catalytic positions of Mm-cpn (Asp-60, Asp-91, Thr-93, Thr-94, Lys-161, Asp-386) separates the two groups cleanly: 6 of 6 for CCT1 through CCT7, 5 of 6 for CCT8 (Thr-94 to Asn-102), 2 of 6 for BBS6, 1 of 6 for BBS10, 0 of 6 for BBS12.

A methodological note that matters for reading this figure: an earlier version of the aligner used a linear gap penalty and could not skip BBS10's roughly 170-residue low-pLDDT insertion. It produced a false continuous diagonal, TM-score 0.521, and site residues 17 to 56 Å from their true partners. Switching to TM-align's gap-open-only scheme fixed it and moved the median CA deviation at site positions to 1.37 Å. The superseded numbers appear nowhere in this report.

F2. No carboxylate reaches the Mg2+ and gamma-phosphate centre in BBS10, BBS6 or BBS12

A single aligned column is a weak basis for declaring a catalytic residue absent, because a one- or two-residue register shift along a helix can move the correct side chain in or out. The test was therefore made register-robust. After superposing each AlphaFold model on 3RUW chain A, the ADP-AlF3-Mg2+ group was transplanted into the model frame, and the closest side-chain heavy atom of every residue in a 15 to 20 residue window spanning the catalytic helix was measured against the Mg2+/AlF3 centre.

Human Asp-412 was the serious alternative: it sits in the sequence EDALHG, which closely resembles the GroEL EDALHA context of Asp-398, so a naive sequence-only assignment would have picked it. It fails on two independent grounds, at 9.08 Å in the model and not conserved across orthologs. The nearest alternative in CCT5 is Glu-397 at 7.14 Å, which calibrates what "too far" means.

The same scan over the NDG loop puts BBS10 Asn-50 at 3.01 Å from the transplanted centre, in the right geometric position but with no Asp or Glu within 6.6 Å, against CCT5 Asp-73 at 3.16 Å. At the catalytic-lysine position BBS10 places Arg-181 with NH1 0.81 Å from the transplanted ligand and BBS6 places Lys-161 at 0.76 Å, so the basic side chain is present in both. BBS12 has no basic residue closer than His-277 at 4.34 Å but does place Arg-525 1.86 Å from the ligand on the catalytic helix, a different and equally non-catalytic arrangement.

Steric transplant counts show the cavity is open in all four: protein heavy atoms within 5 Å are CCT5 110, BBS6 101, BBS10 122, BBS12 124, with atoms closer than 2.5 Å at CCT5 4, BBS6 10, BBS10 10, BBS12 18. BBS10 is therefore not a chaperonin that filled in its nucleotide site with bulky substitutions. The site is there and empty.

F3. The nucleotide pocket is under purifying selection, yet no vertebrate BBS10 has the catalytic aspartate

Ensembl Compara returns BBS10 orthologues in 180 species, all vertebrate except two one-to-many hits in Ciona intestinalis. A control run on CCT5 returns 194 species and does include C. elegans, D. melanogaster, S. cerevisiae and both Ciona species, so BBS10's absence from fly, worm and yeast is a property of the gene and not of database coverage. Twenty-two ortholog sequences were downloaded and aligned to human Q8TAM1 with a Needleman-Wunsch implementation written for this job (BLOSUM62, gap open -11, gap extend -1, free end gaps). Seven were truncated Ensembl gene models and were flagged and scored separately rather than counted as losses: the two Ciona models (100 and 235 residues aligned), Danio rerio (150), Salmo salar (424), Takifugu rubripes (511), Oryzias latipes (581) and Eptatretus burgeri (578). Identity to human across the 15 full-length models runs from 36.6% (Anolis carolinensis) to 88.4% (Callithrix jacchus).

The 18 mapped site positions are more conserved than the 702 other scorable positions: site median identity 0.931 against background median 0.533, Mann-Whitney U = 9812, p = 3.0e-05, rank-biserial r = 0.553. Split by subsite, the adenine and ribose positions give n = 12, mean 0.833, p = 2.1e-04, r = 0.594, and the catalytic and Mg positions give n = 6, mean 0.767, p = 0.023, r = 0.472.

The substitutions that break the catalytic set are themselves fixed rather than polymorphic, which rules out the reading that human BBS10 is a degraded copy while other vertebrates keep a working enzyme. At the Mm-cpn Thr-93 column 14 of 15 carry alanine; at Thr-94 15 of 15 carry lysine; at the catalytic-lysine column 15 of 15 carry a basic residue (Arg 12, Lys 3); at the Mm-cpn Asp-386 catalytic-aspartate column 12 carry glycine, 2 serine and 1 glutamate, with zero aspartate. Human Asp-412, the alternative register, is not conserved (Ser 5, Asp 3, Ala 3, Arg 2, Asn 1, Cys 1).

The fixed lysine at the Mm-cpn Thr-94 column is the one substitution that points toward retained binding. Putting a positive charge where the beta and gamma phosphates sit is what one would do to hold a nucleotide in place without catalysing anything.

F4. The NDG-loop aspartate is retained outside primates and lost in human and marmoset

The position-50 column behaved unexpectedly: human BBS10 carries asparagine there, but the column is poorly conserved in the direction of human rather than being a family-wide loss. Across the 15 full-length orthologs the column is covered in 14 and carries aspartate in 13 of 14 (93%). The single exception is Callithrix jacchus. The sequence context is RDGG in non-primates (Bos taurus EVLLSRDGGCLLK, Mus musculus EVLLSRDGGCLLE, Anolis carolinensis EILLSRDGERVLR) against RNGG in human and marmoset. The human residue was verified directly from the UniProt Q8TAM1 FASTA (length 723, residues 46-54 = LLSRNGGRL), independently of the cached sequence used for alignment, to rule out a file-handling error.

BBS10 therefore lost the two group II aspartates at different times. The Asp-398 equivalent went before the divergence of ray-finned fishes and tetrapods and is absent from all vertebrate BBS10. The Asp-52 equivalent survived in the vertebrate lineage and became asparagine in primates. Asp to Asn is the conservative substitution used experimentally to abolish catalysis while preserving the fold and the hydrogen-bonding geometry. In GroEL the D52A mutation leaves about 20% of wild-type ATPase but combines with D398A to give less than 0.01% (PMID: 25202010), so a non-primate BBS10 carrying only the NDG aspartate is not predicted to be catalytically competent either. The result does mean that structural statements about human BBS10 should not be generalised to the family without checking the column, and that an experimental test of residual ATPase would be more informative on a non-primate ortholog or a human N50D construct.

F5. The existing GO:0005524 is an InterPro rule, not a measurement

QuickGO (EBI REST, retrieved 2026-11-24) returned 12 annotations over 8 distinct terms for BBS10, 57 over 33 terms for BBS6/MKKS, 12 over 6 terms for BBS12 and 55 over 21 terms for the CCT4 control. All three BBS chaperonin-like proteins carry GO:0005524 ATP binding with evidence code IEA and reference GO_REF:0000002, assigned by InterPro, which is domain-rule propagation from PF00118 Cpn60_TCP1 / IPR002423. Q8TAM1 also carries IPR042619 (BBS10), IPR027409 (GroEL-like apical domain superfamily) and IPR027413 (GroEL-like equatorial superfamily).

None of BBS10, BBS6 or BBS12 carries GO:0016887 ATP hydrolysis activity, GO:0140662 ATP-dependent protein folding chaperone or GO:0044183 protein folding chaperone. CCT4 carries GO:0016887 with EXP evidence (23612981) in addition to IEA, plus GO:0140662 (IEA) and GO:0044183 (IDA, 25467444 and 30955883). Evidence-code counts are BBS10 with 0 EXP, 0 IDA, 0 ISS, 4 IMP, 4 IPI, 3 IEA and 1 IBA, against CCT4 with 4 EXP, 9 IDA, 3 HDA and 5 IMP. Besides GO:0005524 the only molecular function terms on BBS10 are GO:0005515 protein binding (IPI, 20080638, 28514442, 33961781) and GO:0061629 (IPI, 22302990).

The gap between CCT4 and BBS10 in experimental evidence codes measures how much of the chaperonin functional description applied to BBS10 is carried rather than observed.

Mechanistic model and interpretation

Four independent lines converge on one reading. BBS10 is a structural group II chaperonin with an assembled, selection-maintained nucleotide cavity and no hydrolytic machinery. The ATP-driven activity required for BBSome assembly comes from CCT, which BBS10, BBS6 and BBS12 recruit (PMID: 20080638).

The one result that resists a simple "decayed pseudo-enzyme" story is the conservation signal. If the cavity were functionless one would expect it to drift at the background rate of 0.533 identity, and instead the site positions sit at 0.931. Three readings fit the data, and the analysis cannot separate them: ATP binds without turnover and has a conformational or regulatory effect; the residues are conserved for folding and packing of the equatorial domain rather than for ligand contact; or hydrolysis happens by a mechanism that cannot be reconstructed from GroEL geometry. The first is the reason the verdict is partially supported rather than a clean confirmation of "non-catalytic scaffold with a dead site".

The practical point for the review: the single nucleotide-related statement on BBS10 is a domain rule firing on PF00118. The structural and evolutionary evidence here says that rule is half right. The binding half survives as a prediction. The catalytic half should be explicitly fenced off so that future InterPro, CCT-based or orthology-based propagation does not introduce GO:0016887 or GO:0140662 onto BBS10, BBS6 or BBS12.

Mechanistic scope

Direct activity under test. Whether the Q8TAM1 polypeptide binds Mg-ATP in its equatorial domain and catalyses its hydrolysis. The analysis bears on this and on nothing else.

What the analysis supports. A group II chaperonin fold with an assembled, open, sequence-conserved nucleotide cavity, and the absence of the carboxylate that positions the catalytic water in GroEL and the archaeal thermosome.

Downstream and not under test. BBSome assembly, BBS7 stability, ciliary membrane protein trafficking, leptin receptor signalling, retinal degeneration, obesity, polydactyly and renal disease. These are phenotypes of loss of function and of the BBS6/BBS10/BBS12-CCT complex as a whole. They are consistent with a scaffold role and do not test the ATPase question. None of them should be used to justify a nucleotide MF term.

The mechanistic reading. CCT supplies the ATP-driven activity in the BBS-chaperonin complex, and BBS10 contributes a chaperonin-shaped surface that recruits BBS7 and the other BBSome subunits. On that reading the conserved cavity in BBS10 is either a vestige preserved for folding and packing of the equatorial domain, or a site for a nucleotide interaction that is structural rather than catalytic.

Conflicts and alternatives

  1. Purifying selection argues against a pure vestige. Site positions sit at 0.931 identity against a background of 0.533. Something maintains them. Candidate readings are non-catalytic nucleotide binding with a conformational effect, conservation for folding of the equatorial domain, or an unconventional hydrolytic mechanism. The data presented cannot separate these.

  2. The fixed Lys-84 in the phosphate loop. Replacing Mm-cpn Thr-94 with a lysine conserved in 15 of 15 orthologs puts a positive charge where the phosphates sit. It could substitute as a phosphate contact, which would favour binding while doing nothing for catalysis.

  3. AlphaFold conformational state. The model is apo-like, and group II chaperonins change conformation on nucleotide binding, so a closed-state model could place residues differently. The register scan was run partly to limit this risk: no carboxylate exists anywhere in the relevant sequence window, so a modest conformational shift would not recover one.

  4. Primate-specific Asn-50. Human BBS10 is the worst case in its own family at the NDG-loop position. Conclusions drawn from human alone overstate the loss. In GroEL the D52A single mutant retains about 20% of ATPase, so a non-primate BBS10 with one aspartate is still not predicted to be catalytically competent, but the asymmetry matters for experimental design and for cross-species inference from mouse and zebrafish models.

  5. Paralog confusion risk. BBS6, BBS10 and BBS12 all carry PF00118 and all three received GO:0005524 from the same InterPro rule. They differ substantially at the site: BBS6 retains 2 of 6 catalytic positions with Glu-385 at 6.15 Å, BBS12 retains 0 of 6 but places Thr-521 at 4.80 Å with Arg-525 at 1.86 Å. They should not be curated as a block on nucleotide terms.

  6. Review-level consensus is consistent but not independent. PMID: 28824921 asserts the loss of ATP-dependent folding activity without citing a direct measurement on these proteins, and PMID: 35373910 describes the chaperonin function as debated. Agreement between this analysis and the reviews is partly agreement about the same absence of data.

Further limitations of the analysis itself:

  • The alignment, contact calculation and TM-score code were written for this job because no structural-bioinformatics library was importable in the executor (Biopython and subprocess are both unavailable). Results were checked by positive control, with CCT1 through CCT8 recovering 6 of 6 catalytic residues at TM 0.87 to 0.94, and by an independent register scan, but they are not the output of a published tool.
  • The transfer of GroEL and Mm-cpn catalytic requirements to BBS10 assumes the same chemistry in the same geometry. BBS10 is 36.6% to 88.4% identical to its own vertebrate orthologs and far more distant from the archaeal reference, so an unconventional mechanism is not formally excluded.
  • Orthology rests on Ensembl Compara gene models. Seven of 22 were truncated and were excluded from scoring rather than counted as losses, which is conservative but reduces the panel at positions near the termini.
  • The PubMed search interface used returns nothing for long multi-term queries, so short queries were used throughout. A relevant primary paper on BBS10 nucleotide handling could have been missed, although the two independent reviews both describe the question as open.

Discriminating tests

  1. Binding against hydrolysis, directly. Purify recombinant human BBS10 and measure Mg-ATP binding by ITC or MANT-ATP and turnover by a coupled ATPase assay. The three outcomes map onto the three hypotheses: binding with no turnover supports the scaffold-with-nucleotide model, no binding supports a fully decayed site, measurable turnover refutes the prediction made here.

  2. Gain-of-function reconstruction. Make BBS10 G416D, optionally with N50D, A83T and K84T, and test for restored ATPase. If the site is otherwise intact, restoring the catalytic aspartate should produce detectable turnover. This is the sharpest test of whether the cavity is competent.

  3. Loss-of-binding mutant in cells. Mutate the conserved adenine subsite residues (Ile-640, Leu-644, Glu-679, Lys-684, Gly-30, Pro-31) and ask whether BBSome assembly and BBS7 stability are affected. If a nucleotide-null BBS10 still supports assembly, the conserved pocket is structural and GO:0005524 should be weakened further.

  4. Non-primate comparison. Run the same assays on mouse Bbs10, which retains the NDG-loop aspartate. A difference would locate the functional weight on that residue.

  5. Cryo-EM of the BBS-chaperonin complex with AMP-PNP. Would settle the oligomeric state, whether the pocket is occupied in context and whether a neighbouring subunit contributes catalytic residues in trans.
