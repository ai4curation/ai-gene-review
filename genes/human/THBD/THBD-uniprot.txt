CC   -!- FUNCTION: Endothelial cell receptor that plays a critical role in
CC       regulating several physiological processes including hemostasis,
CC       coagulation, fibrinolysis, inflammation, and angiogenesis
CC       (PubMed:10761923). Acts as a cofactor for thrombin activation of
CC       protein C/PROC on the surface of vascular endothelial cells leading to
CC       initiation of the activated protein C anticoagulant pathway
CC       (PubMed:29323190, PubMed:33836597, PubMed:9395524). Also accelerates
CC       the activation of the plasma carboxypeptidase B2/CPB2, which catalyzes
CC       removal of C-terminal basic amino acids from its substrates including
CC       kinins or anaphylatoxins leading to fibrinolysis inhibition
CC       (PubMed:26663133). Plays critical protective roles in changing the
CC       cleavage specificity of protease-activated receptor 1/PAR1, inhibiting
CC       endothelial cell permeability and inflammation (By similarity).
CC       Suppresses inflammation distinctly from its anticoagulant cofactor
CC       activity by sequestering HMGB1 thereby preventing it from engaging
CC       cellular receptors such as RAGE and contributing to the inflammatory
CC       response (PubMed:15841214). {ECO:0000250|UniProtKB:P15306,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:10761923, ECO:0000269|PubMed:15841214,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:26663133, ECO:0000269|PubMed:29323190,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:33836597, ECO:0000269|PubMed:9395524}.
CC   -!- SUBUNIT: Interacts with ITGAL, ITGAM and ITGB2. Interacts with
CC       thrombin/F2; this interaction switches the specificity of thrombin from
CC       a procoagulant to an anticoagulant and antifibrinolytic protease
CC       (PubMed:10761923). Interacts with ANGP1 and ANGP2; these interactions
CC       significantly inhibit the generation of activated PC and TAFIa/CPB2 by
CC       the thrombin/thrombomodulin complex (PubMed:29323190). Interacts with
CC       PF4; this interaction enhances generation of activated protein C
CC       (PubMed:9395524). Interacts with HMGB1; this interaction inhibits HMGB1
CC       inflammatory activity (PubMed:15841214). {ECO:0000269|PubMed:10761923,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:15841214, ECO:0000269|PubMed:27055590,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:29323190, ECO:0000269|PubMed:9395524}.

CC   -!- DISEASE: Thrombophilia due to thrombomodulin defect (THPH12)
CC       [MIM:614486]: A hemostatic disorder characterized by a tendency to
CC       thrombosis. {ECO:0000269|PubMed:12139752, ECO:0000269|PubMed:7811989,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:9198186}. Note=The disease may be caused by variants
CC       affecting the gene represented in this entry. The role of
CC       thrombomodulin in thrombosis is controversial. It is likely that
CC       genetic or environmental risk factors in addition to THBD variation are
CC       involved in the pathogenesis of venous thrombosis.
CC   -!- DISEASE: Hemolytic uremic syndrome, atypical, 6 (AHUS6) [MIM:612926]:
CC       An atypical form of hemolytic uremic syndrome. It is a complex genetic
CC       disease characterized by microangiopathic hemolytic anemia,
CC       thrombocytopenia, renal failure and absence of episodes of
CC       enterocolitis and diarrhea. In contrast to typical hemolytic uremic
CC       syndrome, atypical forms have a poorer prognosis, with higher death
CC       rates and frequent progression to end-stage renal disease.
CC       {ECO:0000269|PubMed:19625716, ECO:0000269|PubMed:20513133}.
CC       Note=Disease susceptibility is associated with variants affecting the
CC       gene represented in this entry. Other genes may play a role in
CC       modifying the phenotype.
