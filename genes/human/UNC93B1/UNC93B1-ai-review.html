Gene Description

UNC93B1 is a polytopic (twelve-transmembrane) endoplasmic reticulum membrane protein of the unc-93 family that acts as a dedicated chaperone and trafficking factor for the nucleotide-sensing Toll-like receptors. It physically binds the transmembrane and juxtamembrane regions of TLR3, TLR7, TLR8 and TLR9 in the ER and delivers them through the secretory pathway to endolysosomal compartments, where the receptors encounter nucleic-acid ligands and signal; it also relocalizes from the ER to endosomes and lysosomes upon stimulation. Its architecture resembles the major facilitator superfamily transporters, and cryo-EM structures show TLR ectodomains and transmembrane regions docking onto its N-terminal helical bundle. UNC93B1 is required for the signalling of all endosomal TLRs, but it is dispensable for ligand recognition and signal initiation by the receptors themselves, acting upstream to position and regulate them rather than transducing the signal. Beyond the endosomal TLRs it is also needed for plasma-membrane localization and signalling of the cell-surface flagellin receptor TLR5. By compartmentalizing nucleic-acid-sensing TLRs it both enables antimicrobial and antiviral innate immunity and limits inappropriate recognition of self nucleic acids. Loss-of-function mutations cause autosomal recessive UNC93B1 deficiency, which predisposes to herpes simplex encephalitis through impaired type I and type III interferon responses. Conversely, UNC93B1 also restrains signalling after delivery (particularly by TLR7 and TLR8), and rare missense variants in its negative regulatory regions cause monogenic systemic lupus erythematosus, chilblain lupus and related autoimmune or inflammatory disease through enhanced TLR7 and/or TLR8 responses.

Comprehensive Research Report: UNC93B1 (Unc-93 Homolog B1)

Gene Identity and Verification

The gene UNC93B1 (UniProt: Q9H1C4) encodes a protein designated as "Unc-93 homolog B1" (Unc-93B1 or hUNC93B1) in humans (ishida2021cryoemstructuresof pages 1-2). This protein belongs to the unc-93 family and contains the characteristic UNC93B1 domain (IPR043268) (klammer2023expressionundfunktion pages 19-22, ishida2021cryoemstructuresof pages 1-2). The gene identity matches the UniProt record provided, confirming we are researching the correct human protein.

Primary Molecular Function

Core Identity as a Trafficking Chaperone

UNC93B1 functions primarily as a non-enzymatic, multipass transmembrane chaperone protein that regulates intracellular nucleic-acid-sensing Toll-like receptors (TLRs) (song2022nglycosylationofunc93b1 pages 1-2, ishida2021cryoemstructuresof pages 1-2, rael2024largescalemutationalanalysis pages 17-18). It is not a transporter with conventional small-molecule substrates but rather a protein-protein interaction partner whose "substrates" are membrane receptor clients (ishida2021cryoemstructuresof pages 1-2, ishida2021cryoemstructuresof pages 5-6). The protein acts as an essential trafficking factor that mediates the exit of TLR3, TLR7, TLR8, and TLR9 from the endoplasmic reticulum (ER) and their subsequent delivery to endosomal compartments where these receptors detect microbial or viral nucleic acids (ishida2021cryoemstructuresof pages 1-2, song2022nglycosylationofunc93b1 pages 1-2, mielcarska2021cellsurfaceexpression pages 2-4).

Structural Organization

UNC93B1 is a 12-transmembrane (TM) domain protein with structural similarity to atypical major facilitator superfamily (MFS) members (ishida2021cryoemstructuresof pages 1-2, ishida2021cryoemstructuresof pages 2-3). High-resolution cryo-electron microscopy structures of TLR3–UNC93B1 and TLR7–UNC93B1 complexes, published in 2021, revealed that UNC93B1 comprises two pseudo-symmetrical six-helix bundles (TM1–TM6 and TM7–TM12) that create an outward-facing, solvent-accessible central cavity (ishida2021cryoemstructuresof pages 2-3, ishida2021cryoemstructuresof pages 5-6). The N-terminal six-helix bundle provides the principal TLR-binding interface, contacting TLR transmembrane helices and luminal juxtamembrane regions (ishida2021cryoemstructuresof pages 1-2, ishida2021cryoemstructuresof pages 2-3, ishida2021cryoemstructuresof pages 3-4).

Key structural features include:
- Luminal loops: The TM5–TM6 loop (residues 245–280) extends across the luminal surface and contains conserved N-glycosylation sites at Asn251 and Asn272 (ishida2021cryoemstructuresof pages 2-3, ishida2021cryoemstructuresof pages 6-8).
- Intracellular elements: The TM6–TM7 connecting region forms membrane-associated helices that mediate UNC93B1 oligomerization and contains Lys333, which is subject to ubiquitylation (ishida2021cryoemstructuresof pages 3-4, ishida2021cryoemstructuresof pages 6-8).
- Regulatory regions: The N-terminal disordered region contains D34, and the C-terminal region includes a YxxF sorting motif and phosphorylation sites that recruit adaptors such as syntenin-1 (song2022nglycosylationofunc93b1 pages 1-2, ishida2021cryoemstructuresof pages 6-8).

Binding Partners and Specificity

UNC93B1 directly binds nucleic-acid-sensing endosomal TLRs, including TLR3, TLR7, TLR8, and TLR9 (ishida2021cryoemstructuresof pages 1-2, mielcarska2021cellsurfaceexpression pages 2-4, rael2024largescalemutationalanalysis pages 17-18). TLR5 has also been reported as an UNC93B1-associated receptor, whereas TLR4, a cell-surface receptor, does not detectably associate with UNC93B1 in pull-down experiments (ishida2021cryoemstructuresof pages 1-2). The structural basis of TLR recognition involves contacts between TLR transmembrane helices (especially near UNC93B1 TM3 and TM6) and luminal juxtamembrane β-turn-like regions that insert into pockets formed by UNC93B1 loops (ishida2021cryoemstructuresof pages 2-3, ishida2021cryoemstructuresof pages 3-4). TLR3 forms a 1:1 complex with UNC93B1, while TLR7 assembles into a preformed 2:2 (TLR7)₂–(UNC93B1)₂ dimer (ishida2021cryoemstructuresof pages 6-8, ishida2021cryoemstructuresof pages 2-3, ishida2021cryoemstructuresof pages 5-6).

Beyond TLRs, UNC93B1 also binds STING (stimulator of interferon genes) and regulates its stability through an independent mechanism discussed below (zhu2022unc93b1attenuatesthe pages 1-1, zhu2022unc93b1attenuatesthe pages 4-4).

Subcellular Localization

UNC93B1 is primarily an ER-resident membrane protein that functions along the ER-to-endosome trafficking route (klammer2023expressionundfunktion pages 19-22, mielcarska2021cellsurfaceexpression pages 2-4, song2022nglycosylationofunc93b1 pages 1-2, ishida2021cryoemstructuresof pages 1-2). Under resting conditions, it resides in the ER where it binds nascent TLR clients and supports their folding, stabilization, and quality control (mielcarska2021cellsurfaceexpression pages 2-4, song2022nglycosylationofunc93b1 pages 1-2). UNC93B1 then escorts TLRs through COPII-dependent ER export vesicles, via the Golgi apparatus, to endosomal and endolysosomal compartments (song2022nglycosylationofunc93b1 pages 1-2, robinson2025recentinsightsinto pages 7-8). This compartmentalization is functionally critical because it restricts TLR activation to specialized intracellular locations, preventing inappropriate recognition of extracellular or plasma-membrane-accessible self-nucleic acids (ishida2021cryoemstructuresof pages 1-2).

UNC93B1 also participates in the autophagy–lysosome system, where it delivers STING for degradation (zhu2022unc93b1attenuatesthe pages 6-6, zhu2022unc93b1attenuatesthe pages 9-9, zhu2022unc93b1attenuatesthe pages 1-1, zhu2022unc93b1attenuatesthe pages 4-5).

Biological Pathways and Signaling Regulation

TLR-Mediated Innate Immunity

UNC93B1 regulates multiple branches of nucleic-acid-sensing TLR signaling, which converge on NF-κB-driven inflammatory cytokine production and IRF-mediated type I interferon (IFN-α/β) responses (han2024anovelunc93b1 pages 4-6, song2022nglycosylationofunc93b1 pages 1-2, rael2024largescalemutationalanalysis pages 1-2, robinson2025recentinsightsinto pages 7-8). Each TLR exhibits distinct ligand specificity and signaling outcomes:

  • TLR3 senses double-stranded RNA and signals through the TRIF adaptor to activate IRF3 and NF-κB (ishida2021cryoemstructuresof pages 6-8).
  • TLR7 and TLR8 recognize single-stranded RNA and RNA degradation products, respectively, signaling via MyD88 to activate NF-κB and IRF7 (han2024anovelunc93b1 pages 4-6, song2022nglycosylationofunc93b1 pages 1-2, david2024gainoffunctionhumanunc93b1 pages 1-2).
  • TLR9 detects unmethylated CpG DNA motifs and signals through MyD88 to produce inflammatory cytokines and type I interferons (song2022nglycosylationofunc93b1 pages 1-2, song2022nglycosylationofunc93b1 pages 4-6).

UNC93B1 does not uniformly activate all client TLRs; rather, it exerts receptor-specific positive (trafficking, stabilization) and negative (signal termination, competitive inhibition) regulatory effects (rael2024largescalemutationalanalysis pages 17-18, rael2024largescalemutationalanalysis pages 1-2).

Differential Regulation of TLR7 Versus TLR9

A major discovery in UNC93B1 biology is its differential regulation of TLR7 and TLR9 after endosomal delivery (rael2024largescalemutationalanalysis pages 1-2, song2022nglycosylationofunc93b1 pages 1-2, ishida2021cryoemstructuresof pages 6-8). TLR9 is released from UNC93B1 in endosomes, permitting DNA binding and downstream signaling (song2022nglycosylationofunc93b1 pages 1-2, ishida2021cryoemstructuresof pages 6-8). In contrast, TLR7 remains associated with UNC93B1 in endosomes, exposing it to additional regulatory mechanisms (rael2024largescalemutationalanalysis pages 1-2, ishida2021cryoemstructuresof pages 6-8). Phosphorylation of UNC93B1 C-terminal regions recruits the adaptor syntenin-1 (SDCBP), which directs the TLR7–UNC93B1 complex into intraluminal vesicles of multivesicular bodies, terminating signaling (ishida2021cryoemstructuresof pages 6-8). This mechanism depends on ubiquitylation of UNC93B1 Lys333 (ishida2021cryoemstructuresof pages 6-8).

Post-translational modifications further differentiate TLR regulation. N-glycosylation of UNC93B1 at Asn272 is specifically required for TLR9 signaling—apparently by enabling recruitment of the MyD88 adaptor—but is dispensable for TLR7 responses (song2022nglycosylationofunc93b1 pages 1-2, song2022nglycosylationofunc93b1 pages 4-6, song2022nglycosylationofunc93b1 pages 12-13). The S282A mutation in UNC93B1 strengthens TLR9 binding and prevents productive receptor release, impairing TLR9 function without affecting TLR3 or TLR7 (zhu2022unc93b1attenuatesthe pages 9-9, ishida2021cryoemstructuresof pages 6-8).

This differential regulation helps balance RNA versus DNA sensing and prevents excessive TLR7-driven autoimmunity (rael2024largescalemutationalanalysis pages 17-18, rael2024largescalemutationalanalysis pages 1-2).

Structural Basis of TLR Activation Control

Cryo-EM structures reveal that UNC93B1 binding is sterically incompatible with the ligand-induced activated dimeric states of TLR3 and TLR7 (ishida2021cryoemstructuresof pages 5-6). This structural restraint implies that UNC93B1 holds receptors in pre-activation conformations during trafficking, and that receptor release or conformational rearrangement provides a compartmental activation checkpoint (ishida2021cryoemstructuresof pages 5-6, ishida2021cryoemstructuresof pages 6-8).

cGAS–STING Pathway Regulation

In addition to TLR regulation, UNC93B1 negatively regulates the cytosolic DNA-sensing cGAS–STING pathway (zhu2022unc93b1attenuatesthe pages 1-1, zhu2022unc93b1attenuatesthe pages 6-7). UNC93B1 physically interacts with STING and promotes its degradation through the autophagy–lysosome pathway, thereby attenuating downstream TBK1–IRF3 signaling, IFN-β production, and interferon-stimulated gene (ISG) expression (zhu2022unc93b1attenuatesthe pages 1-1, zhu2022unc93b1attenuatesthe pages 4-5, zhu2022unc93b1attenuatesthe pages 4-4, zhu2022unc93b1attenuatesthe pages 8-9). Pharmacological experiments using chloroquine and ammonium chloride (lysosome inhibitors), but not MG132 (a proteasome inhibitor), demonstrated that STING degradation occurs via the autophagy–lysosome system (zhu2022unc93b1attenuatesthe pages 4-5, zhu2022unc93b1attenuatesthe pages 9-9, zhu2022unc93b1attenuatesthe pages 5-5). Loss of UNC93B1 enhances cGAS–STING-mediated antiviral responses, reduces STING degradation, and increases TBK1 phosphorylation and IRF3 nuclear translocation (zhu2022unc93b1attenuatesthe pages 1-1, zhu2022unc93b1attenuatesthe pages 6-7, zhu2022unc93b1attenuatesthe pages 8-9). In vivo, Unc93b1-deficient mice showed enhanced interferon responses and improved survival against herpes simplex virus-1 (HSV-1) infection (zhu2022unc93b1attenuatesthe pages 8-9, zhu2022unc93b1attenuatesthe pages 9-9).

UNC93B1 does not interact with cGAS, indicating that its effect on the cGAS–STING pathway is mediated exclusively through STING (zhu2022unc93b1attenuatesthe pages 4-4).

Disease Associations and Clinical Relevance

Gain-of-Function Mutations and Autoimmune Disease

Beginning in 2023–2024, multiple independent studies identified rare human UNC93B1 coding variants that cause monogenic autoimmune and autoinflammatory diseases (han2024anovelunc93b1 pages 26-31, he2025casereportnovel pages 5-7, david2024gainoffunctionhumanunc93b1 pages 1-2, alazab2024geneticvariantsin pages 1-4, rael2024largescalemutationalanalysis pages 4-6, he2025casereportnovel pages 2-5, rael2024largescalemutationalanalysis pages 1-2). These gain-of-function mutations typically enhance TLR7 and/or TLR8 signaling without correspondingly increasing TLR9 responses, demonstrating that UNC93B1 is an active signaling regulator rather than a passive trafficking escort (rael2024largescalemutationalanalysis pages 1-2, david2024gainoffunctionhumanunc93b1 pages 1-2, alazab2024geneticvariantsin pages 1-4).

Clinical phenotypes associated with UNC93B1 gain-of-function variants include:

  • Childhood-onset systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE): Multiple variants (I317M, G325C, T314A, V117L, R95L) cause early-onset SLE with features including antinuclear antibodies (ANA), anti-dsDNA antibodies, hypocomplementemia, proteinuria, and lupus nephritis (han2024anovelunc93b1 pages 26-31, david2024gainoffunctionhumanunc93b1 pages 1-2, alazab2024geneticvariantsin pages 1-4).

  • Cutaneous lupus: Chilblain lupus has been linked to L330R, R466S, and R525P variants, which preferentially enhance TLR8 signaling (he2025casereportnovel pages 5-7, david2024gainoffunctionhumanunc93b1 pages 1-2). Tumid lupus was reported in families carrying the T93I variant (rael2024largescalemutationalanalysis pages 4-6, rael2024largescalemutationalanalysis pages 1-2).

  • Rheumatoid arthritis and juvenile arthritis: The novel R336H variant was associated with rheumatoid arthritis, polyarthritis, and interstitial pneumonia across four affected family members (he2025casereportnovel pages 5-7, he2025casereportnovel pages 2-5). The R336C variant caused juvenile idiopathic arthritis with neuroinflammatory manifestations (rael2024largescalemutationalanalysis pages 4-6, rael2024largescalemutationalanalysis pages 1-2).

  • Other manifestations: Reported cases include immune thrombocytopenia (ITP), autoimmune hemolytic anemia, pulmonary artery hypertension, and interstitial lung disease (he2025casereportnovel pages 5-7, he2025casereportnovel pages 2-5).

The inheritance pattern varies by allele: some variants (e.g., I317M, R95L) are homozygous/recessive, while others (e.g., G325C, T93I, R336C) are heterozygous/dominant (david2024gainoffunctionhumanunc93b1 pages 1-2, alazab2024geneticvariantsin pages 1-4, rael2024largescalemutationalanalysis pages 4-6, rael2024largescalemutationalanalysis pages 1-2). The V117L variant represents a rare susceptibility allele with incomplete penetrance rather than a fully Mendelian mutation (alazab2024geneticvariantsin pages 1-4).

Mechanistically, these variants disrupt negative regulatory regions in UNC93B1, leading to excessive TLR7/8-dependent NF-κB and type I interferon signaling (han2024anovelunc93b1 pages 4-6, rael2024largescalemutationalanalysis pages 1-2, david2024gainoffunctionhumanunc93b1 pages 1-2, alazab2024geneticvariantsin pages 1-4). Patient-derived cells and engineered mouse models confirmed that the variants cause hyperresponsiveness to TLR7/8 agonists, elevated inflammatory cytokines (IL-6, TNF, IFN-α), and increased interferon-stimulated gene expression (han2024anovelunc93b1 pages 4-6, han2024anovelunc93b1 pages 26-31, rael2024largescalemutationalanalysis pages 9-11).

Loss-of-Function Mutations and Immunodeficiency

In contrast to gain-of-function autoimmune alleles, loss-of-function UNC93B1 mutations cause primary immunodeficiency with susceptibility to severe viral infections, particularly herpes simplex encephalitis (alazab2024geneticvariantsin pages 1-4). The H412R trafficking-defective mutation disrupts the hydrophobic core of TM9, preventing UNC93B1 and associated TLRs from reaching endosomes (ishida2021cryoemstructuresof pages 6-8). Without functional UNC93B1, TLR3, TLR7, TLR8, and TLR9 remain trapped in the ER and cannot mount effective antiviral responses (mielcarska2021cellsurfaceexpression pages 2-4). Human patients with biallelic UNC93B1 deficiency show impaired TLR3-driven interferon production and recurrent herpesvirus infections (alazab2024geneticvariantsin pages 1-4).

Experimental Evidence and Model Systems

The functional characterization of UNC93B1 has employed diverse experimental approaches:

Structural Biology

  • Cryo-EM structures of mouse TLR3–UNC93B1 and human TLR7–UNC93B1 complexes resolved in 2021 provided atomic-level detail of receptor recognition and revealed the structural incompatibility between UNC93B1 binding and activated TLR conformations (ishida2021cryoemstructuresof pages 1-2, ishida2021cryoemstructuresof pages 2-3, ishida2021cryoemstructuresof pages 5-6).

Cellular and Molecular Systems

  • Human cell lines: HEK293T cells with transient or stable expression of UNC93B1 and TLRs, THP-1 monocytic cells, and engineered knockout/knockdown lines have been used for luciferase reporter assays, qPCR, Western blotting, co-immunoprecipitation, and flow cytometry (zhu2022unc93b1attenuatesthe pages 6-6, han2024anovelunc93b1 pages 4-6, david2024gainoffunctionhumanunc93b1 pages 22-22, zhu2022unc93b1attenuatesthe pages 5-5, zhu2022unc93b1attenuatesthe pages 4-5).

  • Primary cells: Patient-derived PBMCs, fibroblasts, and peritoneal macrophages from Unc93b1-deficient mice have been analyzed using single-cell transcriptomics, ELISA, viral infection assays, and signaling pathway readouts (zhu2022unc93b1attenuatesthe pages 9-9, han2024anovelunc93b1 pages 4-6, han2024anovelunc93b1 pages 26-31).

Genetic and Animal Models

  • Genome-edited mice: CRISPR-engineered mice carrying human disease variants (T93I, R336C, R95L, V138L) developed spontaneous autoimmune phenotypes including splenomegaly, lymphoid expansion, autoantibodies, glomerulonephritis, and enhanced TLR7 responses (rael2024largescalemutationalanalysis pages 9-11, alazab2024geneticvariantsin pages 1-4, han2024anovelunc93b1 pages 4-6, rael2024largescalemutationalanalysis pages 1-2).

  • Mutagenesis screens: Large-scale alanine-scanning mutagenesis of all cytosolic and luminal UNC93B1 residues identified receptor-specific positive and negative regulatory regions (rael2024largescalemutationalanalysis pages 1-2).

Functional Assays

  • TLR-specific stimulation with R848 (TLR7/8), CL307 (TLR7), TL8-506 (TLR8), CpG DNA (TLR9), and poly(I:C) (TLR3) in conjunction with NF-κB and IRF reporter assays, cytokine ELISAs, and transcriptional profiling (han2024anovelunc93b1 pages 4-6, david2024gainoffunctionhumanunc93b1 pages 22-22).

  • Inhibitor experiments using chloroquine, ammonium chloride, MG132, and TLR7/8 inhibitors to distinguish degradation pathways and validate receptor dependence (zhu2022unc93b1attenuatesthe pages 4-5, han2024anovelunc93b1 pages 4-6).

  • HSV-1 infection models in cells and mice to assess antiviral immunity and STING regulation (zhu2022unc93b1attenuatesthe pages 8-9, zhu2022unc93b1attenuatesthe pages 9-9).

Summary

UNC93B1 is a 12-transmembrane endoplasmic reticulum chaperone protein that serves as a master regulator of intracellular nucleic-acid-sensing innate immunity (ishida2021cryoemstructuresof pages 1-2, rael2024largescalemutationalanalysis pages 17-18, robinson2025recentinsightsinto pages 7-8). Its primary molecular function is to bind, stabilize, and traffic endosomal Toll-like receptors (TLR3, TLR7, TLR8, TLR9) from the ER to endolysosomal compartments, where these receptors detect viral and microbial nucleic acids and initiate antiviral and inflammatory responses (song2022nglycosylationofunc93b1 pages 1-2, mielcarska2021cellsurfaceexpression pages 2-4, ishida2021cryoemstructuresof pages 1-2, rael2024largescalemutationalanalysis pages 17-18). Beyond simple trafficking, UNC93B1 exerts receptor-specific regulatory control: it differentially regulates TLR7 versus TLR9 through post-delivery retention, N-glycosylation-dependent adaptor recruitment, and syntenin-1-mediated signal termination (rael2024largescalemutationalanalysis pages 1-2, song2022nglycosylationofunc93b1 pages 1-2, ishida2021cryoemstructuresof pages 6-8). UNC93B1 also negatively regulates the cGAS–STING DNA-sensing pathway by targeting STING for autophagy–lysosome degradation (zhu2022unc93b1attenuatesthe pages 1-1, zhu2022unc93b1attenuatesthe pages 4-5, zhu2022unc93b1attenuatesthe pages 4-4).

Recent human genetic studies (2023–2024) have identified gain-of-function UNC93B1 variants as causes of monogenic childhood-onset systemic lupus erythematosus, chilblain lupus, rheumatoid arthritis, and other autoimmune diseases, underscoring the protein's critical role in preventing TLR7/8-driven autoimmunity (han2024anovelunc93b1 pages 26-31, david2024gainoffunctionhumanunc93b1 pages 1-2, alazab2024geneticvariantsin pages 1-4, rael2024largescalemutationalanalysis pages 4-6, he2025casereportnovel pages 2-5, rael2024largescalemutationalanalysis pages 1-2). Conversely, loss-of-function mutations cause immunodeficiency with susceptibility to herpes simplex virus and other infections (alazab2024geneticvariantsin pages 1-4). These findings establish UNC93B1 as a dual checkpoint regulator that balances protective antiviral immunity against inappropriate recognition of self nucleic acids, with dysregulation leading to either immunodeficiency or autoimmunity depending on the functional direction of the variant (rael2024largescalemutationalanalysis pages 1-2, rael2024largescalemutationalanalysis pages 17-18).

Core biology

  • ER-resident polytopic membrane protein; its function is to deliver nucleotide-sensing TLRs
    (TLR7, TLR9; also TLR3, TLR8) from the ER to endolysosomes
    PMID:18305481.
  • Physically interacts with TLR3/7/8/9 in the ER via their transmembrane regions
    [PMID:18305481 "essential for\nsignalling of TLR3, TLR7 and TLR9 in both humans and mice, physically interacts\nwith these TLRs in the endoplasmic reticulum (ER)"; PMID:33432245 "Both TLRs interact with the UNC93B1\namino-terminal six-helix bundle through their transmembrane and luminal\njuxtamembrane regions"].
  • Cryo-EM structures of human/mouse TLR3-UNC93B1 and human TLR7-UNC93B1 complexes; UNC93B1
    resembles MFS transporters and is indispensable for TLR ER-to-endosome trafficking
    PMID:33432245.
  • UNC93B1 also physically associates with human TLR8 and is critical for TLR8 signalling
    PMID:22164301.
  • Crucially, UNC93B1 is DISPENSABLE for ligand recognition and signal initiation by the TLRs
    themselves — it acts upstream as a trafficking chaperone, not as a signal transducer
    PMID:18305481.
  • The mouse 3d mutation (H412R, Unc93b1) abolishes TLR3/7/9 signalling and antigen cross-presentation
    PMID:16415873.
  • Human UNC-93B (UNC93B1) deficiency causes isolated herpes simplex encephalitis via impaired
    type I/III IFN responses PMID:16973841.
  • UNC93B1-deficient human cells are unresponsive to TLR3, TLR7, TLR8 and TLR9 and patients show
    defective B-cell tolerance checkpoints PMID:19006693.
  • Also required for TLR5 (a cell-surface TLR) plasma membrane localization and signalling
    PMID:24778236.

Participation-test analysis for TLR signaling-pathway process terms (project focus)

UNC93B1 is a trafficking chaperone. Applying the CLAUDE.md participation test: the "work" of a TLR
signaling pathway (ligand binding, receptor dimerization, TIR/adaptor recruitment, kinase
activation) is performed by the TLR and downstream components, NOT by UNC93B1. UNC93B1 is
required for the pathway (knockout abolishes signalling), but this is necessity, not
participation — and the same paper that establishes necessity states UNC93B1 is dispensable for
signal initiation PMID:18305481. UNC93B1 acts upstream as the chaperone that positions the
receptor.

However, GO curators have consistently annotated both human (Q9H1C4) and mouse (Q8VCW4) UNC93B1
to GO:0034138/0034154/0034162 (TLR3/7/9 signaling pathway) with experimental (IMP) and
phylogenetic (IBA, PANTHER:PTN002928790) evidence, and the mouse rows use
acts_upstream_of_or_within. Per the "do not overrule curators from incomplete evidence" rule,
these experimental annotations are not removed. Instead they are marked KEEP_AS_NON_CORE: the core
molecular function is Toll-like receptor binding / protein carrier (trafficking) activity and the
core process is intracellular protein transport; the TLR signaling-pathway terms record the
downstream process UNC93B1 enables as an upstream chaperone, which is non-core.

Comparator: CNPY3 (Q9BT09), another TLR chaperone, carries signaling receptor binding (GO:0005102)
but is not annotated to the TLR signaling-pathway process terms in QuickGO — consistent with the
view that a chaperone's core representation is receptor binding + trafficking rather than pathway
participation. UNC93B1's pathway annotations are a curator convention grounded in strong knockout
data, retained here as non-core.

  UNC93B1 is a polytopic (twelve-transmembrane) endoplasmic reticulum membrane protein of the
  unc-93 family that acts as a dedicated chaperone and trafficking factor for the nucleotide-sensing
  Toll-like receptors. It physically binds the transmembrane and juxtamembrane regions of TLR3,
  TLR7, TLR8 and TLR9 in the ER and delivers them through the secretory pathway to endolysosomal
  compartments, where the receptors encounter nucleic-acid ligands and signal; it also relocalizes
  from the ER to endosomes and lysosomes upon stimulation. Its architecture resembles the major
  facilitator superfamily transporters, and cryo-EM structures show TLR ectodomains and
  transmembrane regions docking onto its N-terminal helical bundle. UNC93B1 is required for the
  signalling of all endosomal TLRs, but it is dispensable for ligand recognition and signal
  initiation by the receptors themselves, acting upstream to position and regulate them rather than
  transducing the signal. Beyond the endosomal TLRs it is also needed for plasma-membrane
  localization and signalling of the cell-surface flagellin receptor TLR5. By compartmentalizing
  nucleic-acid-sensing TLRs it both enables antimicrobial and antiviral innate immunity and limits
  inappropriate recognition of self nucleic acids. Loss-of-function mutations cause autosomal
  recessive UNC93B1 deficiency, which predisposes to herpes simplex encephalitis through impaired
  type I and type III interferon responses. Conversely, UNC93B1 also restrains signalling after
  delivery (particularly by TLR7 and TLR8), and rare missense variants in its negative regulatory
  regions cause monogenic systemic lupus erythematosus, chilblain lupus and related autoimmune or
  inflammatory disease through enhanced TLR7 and/or TLR8 responses.
