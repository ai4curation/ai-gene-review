Gene Description

DHDDS is the catalytic subunit of the human cis-prenyltransferase complex at the endoplasmic reticulum membrane. Together with NUS1 (NgBR), it extends farnesyl diphosphate by successive Mg2+-dependent additions of isopentenyl diphosphate to form long-chain, unsaturated polyprenyl diphosphates. These products supply dolichol and dolichyl phosphate biosynthesis, providing lipid carriers for N-linked glycosylation, O-mannosylation and GPI-anchor biosynthesis in the ER. DHDDS contains the substrate-binding pocket, completed by the C-terminal tail of NUS1; the active assembly is a heterotetramer consisting of two DHDDS–NUS1 pairs. Human pathogenic variants can cause retinitis pigmentosa, developmental and epileptic encephalopathy, or severe glycosylation defects.

Core Functions

Catalytic subunit of the ER-associated DHDDS–NUS1 cis-prenyltransferase complex. The substrate-binding active-site pocket lies in DHDDS, while the NUS1 C-terminal RxG motif contributes across the interface. This cooperative site catalyzes Mg2+-dependent extension of farnesyl diphosphate with isopentenyl diphosphate. The resulting unsaturated long-chain polyprenyl diphosphates provide precursors for dolichyl diphosphate and monophosphate biosynthesis; DHDDS does not perform the subsequent reduction or phosphorylation steps.

DHDDS (Q86SQ9) review notes

Human DHDDS = dehydrodolichyl diphosphate synthase complex catalytic subunit
(a.k.a. hCIT / cis-isoprenyltransferase / cis-IPTase). HGNC:20603. 333 aa.
UPP synthase family (cis-prenyltransferase / undecaprenyl-PP-synthase-like fold).

Core biology (grounded in UniProt Q86SQ9 and cited literature)

DHDDS is the catalytic subunit of the human cis-prenyltransferase (cis-PT)
complex. Together with the non-catalytic partner NUS1 / NgBR, it catalyzes the
committed / first step of dolichol biosynthesis: sequential cis (Z) head-to-tail
condensation of many isopentenyl diphosphate (IPP) units onto (2E,6E)-farnesyl
diphosphate (FPP) to make long-chain dehydrodolichyl (polyprenyl) diphosphate
(C85-100). This product is dephosphorylated/reduced (SRD5A3) and phosphorylated (DOLK)
to dolichyl phosphate (Dol-P), the essential glycan carrier lipid for N-linked
glycosylation, O-mannosylation and GPI-anchor synthesis in the ER.

Catalytic subunit vs partner (important curation caveat)

Several key papers (25066056, 28842490, 32817466) foreground NgBR/NUS1 in title/abstract,
but throughout they co-assay hCIT = DHDDS. DHDDS carries the catalytic (FPP+IPP) active
site; NgBR is a pseudo-cis-prenyltransferase that lacks its own substrate-binding cavity
but contributes an RXG motif to the DHDDS active site and allosterically enhances activity.
- PMID:33077723 (full text): "the only active-site of the complex is situated within the
cis-prenyltransferase homology domain of DHDDS"; the S1 site binds FPP, S2 binds IPP; Mg2+
is coordinated by the conserved D34 of DHDDS. NgBR "lacks detectable catalytic activity".
- PMID:28842490 (abstract): "both NgBR and hCIT subunits function in catalysis and substrate
binding" — DHDDS is genuinely catalytic, not a scaffold.
- PMID:25066056 (full text): human hCIT (DHDDS) + NgBR reconstitute cis-PTase in a yeast
triple-KO (nus1 rer2 srt1) strain; both required for a functional enzyme.

Catalysis and complex architecture

PMID:25066056(https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4161961/), Results on evolutionary conservation and Figure 2, tests human hCIT/DHDDS with NgBR in a yeast strain lacking endogenous cis-prenyltransferase subunits. Co-translated pairs made long-chain products; separately translated proteins mixed afterwards did not reproduce activity. The human DHDDS K42E experiments in the later Results show reduced activity in that system. The title's emphasis on NUS1 does not make DHDDS an untested paralog.

PMID:28842490(https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5655512/), Results on purification and substrate binding and the corresponding Experimental procedures, uses human complex purified from Expi293F membranes. Both subunits contribute to the reaction. Its Results explicitly reports yeast rescue with Q86SQ9-1 and -2 but not -3, with those comparisons described as data not shown. A tolerated N-terminal hCIT tag, disruptive C-terminal tag, and internal NgBR tag are distinct engineered constructs. This limited rescue result does not establish that isoform 3 is universally inactive, and isoform 4 was not tested.

PMID:32817466(https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7456142/), structural Results, subunit-interface mutations and Methods, resolves DHDDS 1–333 with NgBR 79–293. IPP occupies both crystal sites. FPP was used with radiolabeled IPP in activity assays; it is not the ligand observed in that crystal. Product TLC follows dephosphorylation, so the displayed polyprenols are not evidence that the enzyme immediately releases an unphosphorylated product. Product lengths are distributed and context dependent.

PMID:33077723(https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7573591/), assembly and active-site Results plus native MS, SEC-MALS, crosslinking and enzyme Methods, establishes a 2:2 assembly using full-length human DHDDS and engineered soluble NgBR. It shows FPP/Mg and phosphate at the second crystal site. The DHDDS pocket is completed by the NUS1 C-terminal tail. The heterodimeric unit in the earlier structure is compatible with this dimer-of-heterodimers assembly. Neither study establishes equal activity for all four natural DHDDS products.

PMID:35584224(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35584224/), Results on substrate-associated states, limited proteolysis, fluorescent substrate and nanodiscs, plus Protein expression and purification and Yeast complementation assay, further tests full-length human DHDDS with engineered NgBR. DHDDS catalyses precursor formation used in dolichol and dolichyl phosphate biosynthesis; it does not catalyse all subsequent reduction or phosphorylation reactions.
