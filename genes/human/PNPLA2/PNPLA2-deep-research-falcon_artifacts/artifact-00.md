| **Primary reaction:** TAG + H₂O → DAG + free fatty acid | Schweiger et al. (2006) showed that ATGL/PNPLA2 removes the first fatty acid from TAG and, together with HSL, accounts for **>95%** of murine white-adipose TAG-hydrolase activity. [DOI: 10.1074/jbc.M608048200](https://doi.org/10.1074/jbc.M608048200) (pqac-00000009) | Strong physiological evidence for initiation of intracellular TAG lipolysis, but the quantitative tissue experiment was performed in mice. Human biochemical evidence supports preferential TAG hydrolysis, with much lower DAG and phospholipid activity and essentially no monoacylglycerol, cholesteryl-ester, or retinyl-ester hydrolysis (pqac-00000000, pqac-00000010). |
| **Patatin-domain catalysis and ABHD5-sensitive region:** Ser47–Asp166 catalytic dyad; residues N209–N215 contribute to coactivation | Human-family analyses identify a PNPLA domain at residues **10–179** and the conserved catalytic architecture; Ser47 is the nucleophile and Asp166 the second catalytic residue (pqac-00000000, pqac-00000016). Kulminskaya et al. (2024) found that mouse ATGL N209A, I212A/I212S, and N215A retained basal TAG-hydrolase activity but had only **~1.5–4-fold** CGI-58 activation; activation EC₅₀ values ranged from **200 ± 20 to 500 ± 50 nM**, versus **178 ± 20 nM** for wild type. [DOI: 10.1016/j.jlr.2023.100491](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jlr.2023.100491) (pqac-00000020, pqac-00000021) | Ser47/Asp166 is the established patatin-family catalytic dyad. The N209–N215 experiments used truncated **mouse** ATGL and bacterial lysates, so they inform a conserved mechanism but are not direct measurements of full-length human protein. The modeled ATGL–ABHD5 interface remains provisional because no experimental atomic structure was available (pqac-00000019). |
| **Canonical site of action:** cytosolic surface of intracellular lipid droplets | Murugesan et al. (2013) used GFP-tagged human ATGL constructs in oleate-loaded cells and showed that the isolated C-terminal segment **320–504** was sufficient for lipid-droplet targeting. [DOI: 10.1371/journal.pone.0064950](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0064950) (pqac-00000014) | Supports catalysis at the lipid-droplet phospholipid monolayer, where ATGL accesses core TAG. Both lipid-droplet-associated and cytoplasmic pools occur. A hydrophobic segment around residues **315–360** is implicated, but localization also depends on cellular partners and post-translational regulation (pqac-00000000, pqac-00000006). |
| **Principal coactivator and interaction network:** ABHD5/CGI-58 activates ATGL; G0S2 and other partners regulate it | Lass et al. (2006) demonstrated direct CGI-58–ATGL interaction and up to **20-fold** stimulation of TAG-hydrolase activity; disease-associated CGI-58 variants failed to activate ATGL. [DOI: 10.1016/j.cmet.2006.03.005](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cmet.2006.03.005) (pqac-00000013). Kohlmayr et al. (2024) screened **>4,000** ATGL substitutions against CGI-58, G0S2, PLIN1, PLIN5, and CIDEC, validating **23** interaction-perturbing variants and **11** partner-selective switch mutations. [DOI: 10.1038/s41467-024-46937-x](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-024-46937-x) (pqac-00000017, pqac-00000018) | Establishes ATGL as a tightly regulated enzyme rather than a constitutively active lipase. Much foundational activation work used mouse proteins or heterologous cells; the 2024 interaction map provides amino-acid-resolution mechanism but does not quantify regulation in intact human tissues. |
| **Human loss-of-function phenotype:** autosomal-recessive neutral lipid storage disease with myopathy (NLSDM) | Wang et al. (2024) reported two men homozygous for PNPLA2 c.757+1G>T with severe dilated cardiomyopathy and mild skeletal involvement, and summarized **49 previously reported cardiomyopathy cases** in the article abstract. The review estimated cardiac involvement in **40–50%** of NLSDM and described one patient with LVEF **22%**. [DOI: 10.3389/fgene.2024.1415156](https://doi.org/10.3389/fgene.2024.1415156) (pqac-00000024, pqac-00000027, pqac-00000029) | Human genetics strongly validates the necessity of PNPLA2-mediated TAG mobilization in skeletal and cardiac muscle. Frequencies derive from rare, heterogeneous published cases rather than population-based cohorts; genotype–phenotype correlations remain uncertain. PNPLA2 disease generally lacks ichthyosis, distinguishing it from ABHD5-related Chanarin–Dorfman disease (pqac-00000001, pqac-00000028). |
| **Human pharmacological target engagement:** NG-497 selectively inhibits human ATGL | Grabner et al. (2022) reported NG-497 as a reversible competitive human ATGL inhibitor with biochemical **IC₅₀ ≈1 μM** and **Kᵢ ≈0.5 μM**. In human SGBS adipocytes, cellular IC₅₀ was **~1.5 μM**, with near-complete suppression of stimulated lipolysis at **≥10 μM**; primary human-adipocyte fatty-acid release fell to basal levels. [DOI: 10.1021/jacs.1c10836](https://doi.org/10.1021/jacs.1c10836) (pqac-00000042, pqac-00000044) | Strong human-enzyme and human-cell proof of target engagement, but not evidence of clinical safety or efficacy. Species selectivity is crucial: mouse Atglistatin does **not** inhibit human ATGL, whereas NG-497 primarily inhibits human and rhesus ATGL; mouse therapeutic results therefore cannot be directly extrapolated to humans (pqac-00000041, pqac-00000046). |
| **Secondary physiological context:** endothelial lipid-droplet turnover and vascular homeostasis | Boutagy et al. (2024) found that endothelial-specific **mouse** Atgl deletion caused vascular neutral-lipid accumulation, impaired nitric-oxide signaling and vasodilation, ER-stress and inflammatory activation, and accelerated atherosclerosis; aortic eNOS protein was reduced by approximately **50%**. [DOI: 10.1172/JCI170453](https://doi.org/10.1172/JCI170453) (pqac-00000022, pqac-00000023, pqac-00000026) | Demonstrates a causal endothelial role in vivo, but entirely in mouse genetic models. It supports broader PNPLA2 biology beyond adipocytes without establishing a human vascular indication or showing that systemic ATGL activation would be safe. |
| **Alternative context-dependent function:** PEDF-responsive retinal membrane protein with PLA₂ activity | Notari et al. (2006) identified human PNPLA2/PEDF-R as a PEDF-binding protein in retinal systems, reporting approximately **3 nM** affinity, cell-surface accessibility, and PEDF-stimulated phosphatidylcholine hydrolysis. Recombinant activity was **5.7 ΔA₂₃₄/min/nmol**, versus **1.3** for pancreatic PLA₂ under the assay conditions. [DOI: 10.1074/jbc.M600353200](https://doi.org/10.1074/jbc.M600353200) (pqac-00000034, pqac-00000035, pqac-00000038) | Supports an additional retinal membrane-associated PEDF-receptor/PLA₂ model, not replacement of the well-validated intracellular TAG-lipase function. The evidence is tissue- and assay-specific and does not establish PEDF signaling as PNPLA2’s predominant function across human tissues (pqac-00000033, pqac-00000039). |
