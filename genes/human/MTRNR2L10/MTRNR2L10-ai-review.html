Gene Description

MTRNR2L10 is a nuclear human locus on the X chromosome that resembles the humanin small open reading frame of the mitochondrial large-subunit rRNA gene MT-RNR2, one of thirteen such MT-RNR2-like sequences in the human genome that are generally interpreted as nuclear insertions of mitochondrial DNA. Its predicted product, humanin-like 10 (HN10), is a 24-residue peptide (MTTRGFSCLLLLIREIDLSAKRRI) that matches humanin at 17 of 24 aligned positions, differing by Ala2->Thr, Pro3->Thr, Thr13->Ile, Ser14->Arg, Pro19->Ser, Val20->Ala and Ala24->Ile; it retains the Leu9-Leu12 stretch but is the only member of the family that alters both residues of the Pro19-Val20 segment humanin requires for secretion, and it carries substitutions at three positions whose mutation abolishes or reduces humanin activity. The locus is transcribed: RT-PCR detects it in human tissues, most strongly in testis, and its transcript is down-regulated 6 hours and up-regulated 24 hours after staurosporine treatment of cultured cells. Whether a peptide is actually made from this transcript, and what it does, is unestablished: no HN10 peptide has been detected by mass spectrometry in human tissue or fluid, no molecular activity has been measured for it, and no binding partner or receptor interaction has been reported; reports of reduced serum humanin-like 10 in restless legs syndrome rest on an immunoassay that has not been shown to discriminate this peptide from humanin or from its nuclear siblings. The humanin peptide from which it is named is cytoprotective and antiapoptotic, binds BAX, BID, BIM and IGFBP3 intracellularly and signals through FPRL1/FPR2 and a CNTFR-alpha/WSX-1/gp130 complex when secreted, but those properties were measured on the mitochondrially encoded peptide and on synthetic analogues of it, not on this gene product.

Suggested Questions for Experts

Q: Is a peptide produced from the MTRNR2L10 locus at all? Everything annotated to this gene follows from the assumption that it is, and the assumption rests on an intact open reading frame plus RT-PCR detection of the transcript [PMID:19477263]. Targeted mass spectrometry of testis for the HN10-specific tryptic peptides would settle it, and HN10 is unusually tractable for this because the IREIDLSA region differs from humanin at four positions.

Suggested experts: Alan Saghatelian, Pinchas Cohen, Jonathan Wren

Q: What does the serum humanin-like 10 ELISA actually measure? Reduced levels are reported in restless legs syndrome [PMID:38630326], but HN10 matches humanin at 17 of 24 positions and its nuclear siblings more closely, and no locus-specific immunodetection has been demonstrated anywhere in this family. Epitope mapping of the antibody against synthetic HN1-HN13 and humanin would establish what the clinical literature on humanin-like peptides is reporting.

Suggested experts: Pinchas Cohen

Q: How should GO treat a family in which one member has all the experimental evidence and twelve nuclear copies have none? The present solution propagates humanin's activity to every copy through PANTHER:PTN002141596, which produces thirteen gene products annotated as receptor antagonists and apoptosis regulators on the strength of one mitochondrial peptide. A node restricted to MT-RNR2 plus MTRNR2L5, the two sequences with functional data, would say what is actually known. MTRNR2L10 shows the downstream cost - a clinical study selected this locus for investigation on the basis of the propagated term [PMID:38630326], so the annotation is now generating the literature that appears to confirm it.

Q: Do the rat sequences manipulated in the status-epilepticus study [PMID:42289503] correspond to human MTRNR2L1 and MTRNR2L10? These are pericentromeric nuclear insertions of mitochondrial rRNA, so one-to-one orthology across rodents and primates should not be assumed, and knockdown constructs against one such sequence may hit several. Stating the rat loci and the construct specificity controls would make that work interpretable for human gene annotation.

Suggested Experiments

Experiment: Targeted (PRM) mass spectrometry of human testis and of plasma for peptides unique to HN10, using the positions at which it differs from humanin (Ala2->Thr, Pro3->Thr, Thr13->Ile, Ser14->Arg, Pro19->Ser, Val20->Ala and Ala24->Ile) to design discriminating transitions. This is the one experiment that would convert the entire annotation set on this gene from inference to observation.

Experiment: Ribosome profiling or ORF-capture of the MTRNR2L10 transcript in testis, to test whether the predicted ORF is translated rather than merely transcribed.

Experiment: Synthesise the HN10 peptide and test it side by side with humanin and with HN5 in the secretion assay used to map the humanin Pro19-Val20 determinant, and in staurosporine- and amyloid-beta-induced death, FPRL1 competition and BAX binding. HN10 is the natural test of whether the Pro19-Val20 segment is required for secretion in a natural sequence context rather than only in arginine-substituted mutants, and whether Thr13->Ile plus Ser14->Arg together abolish cytoprotection as the single-substitution data predict.

Experiment: Epitope mapping of the commercial humanin-like 10 antibody against synthetic humanin and all thirteen HN-like peptides, to determine whether published serum measurements [PMID:38630326] are locus-specific. This is a prerequisite for interpreting any clinical study of this family.

The family, and why these reviews look alike

Humanin is a 24-residue peptide encoded by a small open reading frame inside the mitochondrial 16S
rRNA gene MT-RNR2, and it is the member of this family with all the experimental evidence:
cytoprotection, BAX/BID/BIM binding, FPRL1/FPR2 engagement, the CNTFR-alpha/WSX-1/gp130 receptor
complex. Bodzioch et al. 2009 (PMID:19477263, verified - Genomics 94:247-256) found thirteen
MT-RNR2-like sequences in the nuclear genome whose reading frames would encode humanin-like peptides
HN1-HN13
PMID:19477263, and showed that at least ten are transcribed and respond to staurosporine and
beta-carotene PMID:19477263.

That paper is the single primary source behind every UniProt MTRNR2L entry, and its own title calls
the functionality potential. UniProt agrees, and says so in a CAUTION line on each entry: the
active peptide is the product of the mitochondrial gene, and whether any of the nuclear loci makes a
physiologically active peptide is open. HGNC classes these loci as pseudogenes ("MT-RNR2 like 10
(pseudogene)"). A 2022 review restates the position PMID:35372353.

    row, not less.

Residues: where similarity stops being an argument

Humanin has an unusually complete mutagenesis map (UniProt Q8IVG9). Positions where a single
substitution abolishes neuroprotective activity: Pro3, Ser7, Cys8, Leu9, Leu12, Thr13, Ser14,
Pro19. Secretion needs Leu9-Leu11 and Pro19-Val20.

HN10 loses Pro3, Thr13, Ser14, Pro19 and Val20 - five mapped positions, including a substitution
(Ser14->Arg) that UniProt records as abolishing, and a substitution (Thr13->Ile) measured to reduce
cytoprotection in the one nuclear peptide that was ever assayed. The usual counterweight for this
family - synthetic HN5, carrying Leu12->Ser, remains cytoprotective - does not help HN10, because
HN10 keeps Leu12 and diverges instead exactly where HN5 remained humanin-like. That is why this
review removes four rows where most of the siblings only flag them: the inheritance assumption fails
on the target side, not merely for want of confirmation.

Negative and orthogonal evidence

A lookup across all thirteen loci in CARDIoGRAMplusC4D found no significant association with
coronary artery disease and no significant expression QTL
PMID:31753007.

Methodological caution that applies to every clinical report on this family

Serum "humanin-like 10" is measured by ELISA and reported as reduced in restless legs syndrome
PMID:38630326. HN10 matches humanin at 17 of 24 positions and its siblings more closely; locus-specific
immunodetection has not been demonstrated anywhere in this family, so no such measurement is used
here as evidence that this particular locus makes a peptide.
