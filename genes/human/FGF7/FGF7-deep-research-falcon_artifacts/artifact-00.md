| Gene and protein identity | Human **FGF7** encodes fibroblast growth factor 7, also called **keratinocyte growth factor (KGF)**. It maps to chromosome **15q15–21.1** and is synthesized as a **194-amino-acid precursor** with the characteristic FGF β-trefoil fold and heparin-binding region. | (pqac-00000000, pqac-00000001) |
| Receptor specificity | FGF7 is a high-specificity ligand for the epithelial splice isoform **FGFR2b (FGFR2-IIIb)**. Cell-surface or extracellular-matrix **heparan sulfate** acts as a cofactor that stabilizes the signaling complex and promotes receptor dimerization and activation. | (pqac-00000004, pqac-00000005, pqac-00000006) |
| Expression and localization | FGF7 is a classically secreted, extracellular **paracrine** signal produced mainly by mesenchymal or stromal cells, especially fibroblasts. It acts locally on neighboring **FGFR2b-positive epithelial cells**, including keratinocytes; heparan sulfate helps retain and present it in the pericellular extracellular environment. | (pqac-00000015, pqac-00000017, pqac-00000021) |
| Signaling pathways | Ligand binding activates FGFR2b dimerization and tyrosine autophosphorylation. Major downstream branches are **FRS2–GRB2–SOS–RAS–RAF–MEK–ERK/MAPK**, **FRS2–GAB1–PI3K–AKT–mTOR**, **PLCγ–IP3/DAG–Ca²⁺/PKC**, and context-dependent **STAT** signaling. Emerging preprint evidence proposes an epithelial **FGFR2b–MEK/ERK–KLF4** anti-inflammatory feedback circuit. | (pqac-00000023, pqac-00000025, pqac-00000029, pqac-00000050) |
| Primary functions | FGF7 is not an enzyme or transporter; it is a receptor-activating growth factor. Its central function is mesenchymal-to-epithelial communication that promotes epithelial-cell proliferation, survival, differentiation, barrier maintenance, and morphogenesis. Documented roles span skin and mucosal repair, alveolar epithelial growth, gland branching, pancreatic development, osteogenesis, and other organ-development programs. | (pqac-00000009, pqac-00000030, pqac-00000044) |
| Tissue repair and wound healing | FGF7 supports re-epithelialization and barrier restoration, limits epithelial apoptosis and tight-junction damage, and can increase mucus production while reducing injury-associated inflammation. Experimental evidence implicates it in repair of skin, intestine, kidney tubules, and other epithelial tissues. | (pqac-00000030, pqac-00000036, pqac-00000039) |
| Clinical application | **Palifermin** is a truncated recombinant human FGF7 marketed as **Kepivance**. Its established application is prevention or treatment of severe therapy-associated oral mucositis in selected cancer-treatment settings; a cited clinical regimen is **60 μg/kg/day**. Other epithelial-injury, transplantation, and wound-repair indications remain investigational or context dependent. | (pqac-00000036, pqac-00000038, pqac-00000042) |
| Cancer and tumor microenvironment | FGF7 can become pathogenic when stromal or tumor signaling is dysregulated. Cancer-associated fibroblast-derived FGF7 and tumor-cell FGFR2b signaling have been associated with proliferation, invasion, angiogenesis, metastasis, and drug resistance. One defined mechanism in gastric cancer is **FGFR2–PI3K/AKT/mTOR–THBS1** signaling. | (pqac-00000056, pqac-00000057, pqac-00000058) |
| Inflammation | FGF7 has context-dependent immunomodulatory effects. Emerging evidence indicates that it can suppress IL6 and interferon-associated epithelial transcription through an FGFR2b–MEK/ERK–KLF4 mechanism, but earlier keratinocyte work reported induction of TNF-α through FGFR2–AKT–NF-κB. Anti-inflammatory activity should therefore be treated as tissue-, stimulus-, and timing-dependent rather than universal. | (pqac-00000048, pqac-00000050, pqac-00000051, pqac-00000055) |
