Gene Description

C9orf72 is a DENN-family regulator of small-GTPase-dependent membrane trafficking. Its long product forms a stable complex with SMCR8 and WDR41 that helps control GTPase cycling and endolysosomal homeostasis. C9orf72 also recognizes active Rab1A to recruit autophagy-initiation machinery, and purified C9orf72 has been reported to promote Rab39A nucleotide exchange upstream of HOPS-dependent autophagosome–lysosome fusion. The protein is predominantly cytoplasmic, with nutrient-dependent lysosomal association and enrichment in neuronal presynaptic compartments. It also influences stress-granule responses and neuronal actin dynamics. Pathogenic intronic repeat expansions can reduce normal protein expression while generating toxic repeat RNA and dipeptide-repeat products; these are distinct from the normal protein and its alternative products.

C9orf72 functional review

C9orf72 is a native protein regulator of small-GTPase-dependent membrane traffic. Its long product forms the C9orf72–SMCR8–WDR41 complex. C9orf72 also has experimentally reported Rab39A exchange activity outside that complex and acts as an active-Rab1A effector during autophagy-initiation machinery recruitment. These are distinct biochemical operations, not interchangeable uses of the legacy GEF name. Pathogenic repeat RNA and translated dipeptide-repeat species are separate from the two source-record C9orf72 protein products.

Mechanistic evidence and limits

PMID:27103069(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27103069/) reports complex-level GDP release on Rab8A and Rab39B from human constructs purified using insect cells. C9orf72 alone had little effect on those substrates in that experiment. Rab39B-QL rescue supports autophagy regulation, but does not uniquely identify catalytic direction. PMID:27617292(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27617292/) also reports exchange with complex preparations. Those are genuine positive experiments. They cannot be dismissed from a later title or from the observation that SMCR8 supplies a catalytic residue in a different assay.

PMID:37821429(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37821429/) directly tests bacterially expressed C9orf72 and Rab39A, including purified binding, Mant-GDP release, titration, cellular loss/rescue and HOPS recruitment. The exchange assays used near-equimolar concentrations and long observation windows, approximately 7,200–14,000 seconds. The supplied C9orf72/SMCR8 preparation behaved differently. This supports a bounded C9orf72-alone Rab39A mechanism, not automatic replication of holo-complex Rab8A/Rab39B exchange or ARF-GAP experiments. HOPS performs tethering and SNAREs execute membrane fusion; those activities are not transferred to C9orf72.

PMID:27334615(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27334615/) supports a separate recruitment mechanism: C9orf72 preferentially binds active Rab1A, interacts with ULK1-complex components, and is required for recruitment of that machinery in human cell lines and rat neurons with human rescue. This is Rab1A-effector behavior, not evidence for Rab1A exchange. Loss of C9orf72 did not prevent the measured ULK1 activation response. The synthesis uses the existing specific small-GTPase-binding and assembly-regulation terms; it does not manufacture another adaptor or kinase annotation merely to label the mechanism.

PMID:27559131(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27559131/) shows nutrient-dependent lysosomal recruitment of endogenously tagged C9orf72. Its mTORC1 phenotypes differ between C9orf72 and SMCR8 single knockouts and the double knockout. The broad signaling-regulation assertion is retained with those context limits, without inventing a uniform activating or inhibitory role, Rag-GAP activity or intrinsic mTOR kinase activity.

PMID:32848248(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32848248/) and PMID:34145292(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34145292/) are now normal canonical full-text caches. Their unique Results/Discussion and selected mechanistic Methods were read. Human HEK293-purified complex promotes ARF1/5/6 hydrolysis; SMCR8 supplies Arg147 and C9orf72 forms part of the substrate pocket. C9orf72 interface mutations impair GAP activity. The 2021 structure uses an ARF1–SMCR8 fusion and GDP–BeF3 analog, while separate biochemical assays support the function. ARF1 lacks its N-terminal membrane anchor. Rab8A required substantially more complex than ARF1, and physiological local substrate pools remain unresolved. Lysosomal recruitment does not prove ARF hydrolysis occurs in cis at the lysosomal membrane.

The 2020 negative exchange experiment tested Rab1A/5A/7A/8A/39B with a positive Rabex5 control over 1,400 seconds, not Rab39A. The older positive complex assays and the later C9orf72-alone Rab39A assay therefore require preparation- and substrate-specific reconciliation. The three contributes_to exchange rows and GEF-complex classification remain UNDECIDED; two enables exchange rows retain the bounded Rab39A finding. Finding-level DISPUTED records point to the actual negative experiment without declaring the original papers invalid.
