# ACTN1 curation notes

## 2026-11-25 — evidence and scope

ACTN1 (HGNC:163; UniProt P12814) encodes alpha-actinin-1, an actin-filament crosslinker and adhesion-associated structural protein. Native acquisition yielded 136 GOA rows and 128 distinct seeded annotation contexts; eight rows collapse under the deterministic seeder's complete source key. The review retains the original term, evidence, reference, partner and negation fields. In particular, the two native NOT annotations remain identifiable.

The [source guide](ACTN1-source-guide.md) records the primary experiments, access limits and isoform distinctions. Actin crosslinking is a direct biochemical activity. Platelet size and proplatelet-tip organization are physiological consequences of that structural activity; the disease studies do not establish that normal ACTN1 universally prevents platelet activation or aggregation. The experiments also do not show that every disease variant abolishes calcium regulation or increases actin association to the same degree.

ACTN1 splice variants have different calcium-binding properties. The isolated-domain affinities in PMID:26020004 and PMID:27272015 are not intracellular activation thresholds. Manuscript names and residue numbering are retained in the evidence discussion without silently mapping them onto every current UniProt isoform.

The postsynaptic study PMID:29429936 directly tests alpha-actinin-1, including purified-protein interactions and rescue with PSD-95-binding mutants after combined alpha-actinin-1/-2/-4 depletion. It supports a structural anchoring function in that experimental setting; it does not establish ACTN1 as the only neuronal alpha-actinin. Likewise, articles centered on ACTN4, ACTN2 or another interacting protein are not grounds for rejecting an ACTN1 annotation without checking their experiments.

Supported generic protein-binding annotations follow the standing ClinGen instruction: retain correct interaction evidence as non-core when a more specific molecular activity has not been established. Complex copurification does not prove direct binary binding. When an independent study verifies the same interaction, retain the supported claim while recording the original source-access limit separately; otherwise leave unadjudicated interactions unresolved. Broad terms are refined only when the original row's own evidence supports the refinement.

## 2026-11-26 — repository review follow-up

[Repository review 6067188633](https://github.com/ai4curation/ai-gene-review/pull/7002#pullrequestreview-6067188633) prompted focused source and specificity checks. The apoptosis claim is now non-core after resolving construct identity; the [source guide](ACTN1-source-guide.md#follow-up-source-access-and-paint-evidence) records the newly inspected sections and their access limits. The change reflects positive support for conditional regulation rather than an assertion of a core apoptotic mechanism.

The repository follow-up retains GO:0048646 anatomical structure formation involved in morphogenesis and GO:0097435 supramolecular fiber organization as informative process classes. QuickGO confirms platelet formation is_a anatomical structure formation involved in morphogenesis and actin filament organization is_a supramolecular fiber organization. A supported descendant alone is not sufficient for TOO_GENERAL under the 2026-11-14 maintainer ruling. The original ARBA fields remain unchanged, and independent corroboration is not represented as evidence that ARBA itself encoded a narrower experiment. Identical protein binding remains TOO_GENERAL because it gives an imprecise account of the same ACTN1 self-association already specified as homodimerization.

The third core entry records a directly tested structural anchoring mechanism in a defined neuronal setting. PMID:29429936 combines purified ACTN1–PSD-95 binding with binding-site mutant replacement experiments, so ACTN1 itself performs the anchoring work. Its support here is concentrated in that one study of cultured rat hippocampal neurons after combined ACTN1/ACTN2/ACTN4 depletion. This does not establish a unique requirement for endogenous ACTN1, its quantitative predominance, or a universal neuronal contribution. General non-muscle actin cross-linking remains the primary summary of ACTN1 function.

Evidence-recovery tasks are retained here as curation follow-up: identify the original assays behind sarcomeric and granule-lumen annotations and inspect unresolved ACTN1-specific exosome peptide tables and dsRNA-binding evidence. These are not posed as biological unknowns for domain experts. The replacement expert question asks how endogenous ACTN1, ACTN2 and ACTN4 divide the postsynaptic anchoring role across neuronal contexts.

Keeping extracellular-region and platelet-alpha-granule-lumen annotations does not resolve extracellular-exosome localization. The exosome term specifies a distinct vesicle compartment. Four source assignments remain UNDECIDED because decisive ACTN1 identification tables have not been adjudicated; this is compatible with retaining the independently supported broader and granule locations.
