## Primary Enzymatic Function and Substrate Specificity

### Catalytic Reaction

LFNG functions as a **β-1,3-N-acetylglucosaminyltransferase** that catalyzes the transfer of N-acetylglucosamine (GlcNAc) from the donor substrate UDP-GlcNAc to O-fucose residues, forming a β-1,3-glycosidic linkage (sparrow2006mutationofthe pages 4-5, c2024investigatingtherelationship pages 45-49, sparrow2006mutationofthe pages 5-7). The complete reaction can be written as:

**UDP-α-D-GlcNAc + O-Fucose-EGF-Notch → GlcNAc-β1,3-Fucose-EGF-Notch + UDP**

This reaction releases UDP in a 1:1 stoichiometric ratio with product formation, which serves as a functional readout for enzymatic activity (c2024investigatingtherelationship pages 45-49).

### Substrate Recognition

The **donor substrate** is UDP-α-D-GlcNAc, which has been directly demonstrated using radiolabeled UDP-[³H]GlcNAc in enzymatic assays (sparrow2006mutationofthe pages 5-7, hou2012galactosedifferentiallymodulates pages 1-2).

The **acceptor substrates** are highly specific: LFNG recognizes O-fucose residues attached to serine or threonine residues within epidermal growth factor-like (EGF) repeats of Notch receptors (song2016lunaticmanicand pages 1-2, gomezUnknownyearmasterproject pages 6-10, pennarubia2021modulationofthe pages 1-2). Importantly, LFNG exhibits site-selectivity, as not all O-fucosylated EGF repeats are equally modified. This specificity reflects recognition of both the fucose moiety and the surrounding folded EGF-domain context (pennarubia2021modulationofthe pages 1-2).

### Catalytic Mechanism

LFNG employs an **inverting, metal-dependent GT-A fold mechanism**. The enzyme contains a conserved DxD motif (residues D200-D202 in human LFNG) that coordinates a divalent manganese ion (Mn²⁺) and positions the UDP-GlcNAc donor substrate (c2024investigatingtherelationship pages 45-49). The catalytic base D290 deprotonates the fucose O3 hydroxyl, enabling nucleophilic attack on the anomeric carbon of GlcNAc in an SN2-like displacement reaction, which inverts the stereochemistry from α to β configuration (c2024investigatingtherelationship pages 45-49, gomezUnknownyearmasterproject pages 23-30, gomezUnknownyearmasterproject pages 6-10). Additional catalytic residues include H314, which helps chelate Mn²⁺ and stabilize the active site architecture (c2024investigatingtherelationship pages 45-49).

The LFNG protein contains approximately 356 amino acids, with an N-terminal transmembrane domain that anchors it to the Golgi membrane and a C-terminal catalytic domain (residues 108-358) containing the Fringe domain responsible for enzymatic activity (c2024investigatingtherelationship pages 45-49, gomezUnknownyearmasterproject pages 6-10).

## Subcellular Localization

LFNG is **localized to the Golgi apparatus**, where it functions as a Golgi-resident glycosyltransferase (c2024investigatingtherelationship pages 45-49, sparrow2006mutationofthe pages 5-7, sparrow2006mutationofthe pages 4-5). The N-terminal transmembrane domain anchors the full-length protein within the Golgi lumen, positioning the catalytic domain to access Notch receptors as they transit through the secretory pathway (c2024investigatingtherelationship pages 45-49).

Experimental evidence demonstrates that wild-type LFNG colocalizes with the Golgi marker GM130 in transfected cells (sparrow2006mutationofthe pages 5-7, c2024investigatingtherelationship pages 88-92). Disease-associated mutations that disrupt Golgi localization, such as F188L, result in loss of enzymatic function and inability to modulate Notch signaling, emphasizing the critical importance of proper subcellular targeting (sparrow2006mutationofthe pages 5-7, sparrow2006mutationofthe pages 4-5, wengryn2023functionalcharacterizationof pages 8-10). 

The protein can undergo proteolytic processing by the Golgi-resident protease SPC6, generating both full-length and processed forms, both of which retain catalytic activity (c2024investigatingtherelationship pages 45-49, wengryn2023functionalcharacterizationof pages 8-10). While secreted forms of LFNG have been detected following overexpression, the biologically relevant function occurs within the Golgi, where LFNG modifies Notch receptors before they reach the cell surface (sparrow2006mutationofthe pages 5-7, sparrow2006mutationofthe pages 1-2).

## Role in Notch Signaling Pathway

### Molecular Mechanism of Notch Modulation

LFNG functions as a critical modulator of Notch receptor-ligand interactions rather than as a simple on/off switch for Notch signaling. The enzyme modifies O-fucose glycans on specific EGF repeats of Notch receptors, altering the receptor's affinity for different ligands and the efficiency of subsequent proteolytic activation (hou2012galactosedifferentiallymodulates pages 9-10, yang2005fringeglycosyltransferasesdifferentially pages 1-2, yuan2009functionsoflunatic pages 18-23).

### Ligand-Selective Effects

The most well-established pattern is that LFNG **enhances Notch1 activation by Delta-like ligands** (DLL1 and DLL4) while **attenuating activation by Jagged ligands** (JAG1 and JAG2) (yang2005fringeglycosyltransferasesdifferentially pages 1-2, yuan2009functionsoflunatic pages 18-23, yuan2009functionsoflunatic pages 151-157, yang2005fringeglycosyltransferasesdifferentially pages 2-4). This differential modulation occurs through multiple mechanisms:

1. **Altered ligand binding affinity**: LFNG modification increases the binding of soluble DLL1 and DLL4 to Notch1, while maintaining or slightly increasing Jagged1 binding (hou2012galactosedifferentiallymodulates pages 9-10, ristori2026bmp9regulatesnotch pages 9-11).

2. **Differential proteolytic efficiency**: Even when Jagged1 can bind to LFNG-modified Notch1, the bound receptor is less efficiently processed by ADAM metalloprotease-mediated S2 cleavage, reducing generation of the Notch intracellular domain (NICD) and downstream transcriptional activation (yang2005fringeglycosyltransferasesdifferentially pages 1-2, yang2005fringeglycosyltransferasesdifferentially pages 2-4, yang2005fringeglycosyltransferasesdifferentially pages 8-10).

3. **Context-dependent effects**: The magnitude and direction of LFNG effects depend on the specific Notch receptor isoform, the particular EGF repeat modified, the extent of glycan extension (particularly galactose addition), ligand presentation mode, and cellular context (hou2012galactosedifferentiallymodulates pages 9-10, yuan2009functionsoflunatic pages 18-23).

### Relationship to Other Fringe Family Members

Mammals express three Fringe glycosyltransferases: LFNG, MFNG (Manic Fringe), and RFNG (Radical Fringe). While all three can modify Notch receptors, they exhibit different site preferences and functional effects. In comparative studies, LFNG frequently exerts a **dominant effect** over MFNG and RFNG when co-expressed, particularly for Notch1 modification (pennarubia2021modulationofthe pages 1-2). Mass spectrometric glycoproteomic mapping demonstrates that individual Fringe enzymes modify distinct but overlapping sets of O-fucose sites on Notch (pennarubia2021modulationofthe pages 1-2).

### Integration with Glycan Extension

The biological effects of LFNG modification can be further modulated by subsequent glycan extension. Addition of galactose to the Fringe-added GlcNAc by β1,4-galactosyltransferases (B4GALT1) **enhances LFNG's positive effect on DLL1-induced Notch signaling** but is required for LFNG-mediated inhibition of JAG1 signaling (hou2012galactosedifferentiallymodulates pages 9-10). This indicates that the complete O-fucose glycan structure, not just the initial GlcNAc addition, determines the functional outcome.

## Biological Processes and Developmental Roles

### Somitogenesis and Vertebral Development

LFNG plays a **central role in the vertebrate segmentation clock** that controls somitogenesis during embryonic development (wengryn2023functionalcharacterizationof pages 1-2, c2024investigatingtherelationship pages 45-49, sparrow2006mutationofthe pages 1-2). In the presomitic mesoderm, LFNG expression oscillates with a periodicity matching somite formation, and this oscillatory expression is essential for proper segmentation (c2024investigatingtherelationship pages 45-49, c2024investigatingtherelationship pages 40-45).

The molecular mechanism involves LFNG-dependent modulation of Notch signaling dynamics: oscillatory LFNG expression promotes pulsatile Notch1-DLL1 interactions that drive anterior somite gene expression, while LFNG repression by HES7 allows sustained Notch1-DLL4 signaling required for posterior somite development (wengryn2023functionalcharacterizationof pages 1-2, c2024investigatingtherelationship pages 66-71). This temporal regulation establishes somite polarity, rostrocaudal organization, and proper boundaries between forming somites (c2024investigatingtherelationship pages 66-71, sparrow2006mutationofthe pages 2-4).

LFNG function is **dosage-sensitive**: different levels of LFNG activity produce varying numbers and morphologies of somites and vertebral bodies, with thoracic vertebrae requiring the highest LFNG potency (c2024investigatingtherelationship pages 45-49). The sclerotome derived from properly patterned somites subsequently forms the vertebrae and ribs, linking LFNG-dependent signaling to axial skeletal development (wengryn2023functionalcharacterizationof pages 1-2, c2024investigatingtherelationship pages 66-71).

### Human Genetic Disease: Spondylocostal Dysostosis Type III

Biallelic pathogenic variants in LFNG cause **autosomal recessive spondylocostal dysostosis type III (SCD3)**, a severe skeletal dysplasia characterized by congenital vertebral segmentation defects, malformed or fused vertebrae (including hemivertebrae), abnormal ribs (missing, fused, or dysplastic), short trunk or stature, scoliosis, and potentially restrictive respiratory disease (c2024investigatingtherelationship pages 66-71, sparrow2006mutationofthe pages 2-4, wengryn2023functionalcharacterizationof pages 3-4, sparrow2006mutationofthe pages 1-2, wengryn2023functionalcharacterizationof pages 1-2).

The original reported mutation was a homozygous **F188L substitution** that resulted in Golgi mislocalization, complete loss of GlcNAc-transferase activity, and inability to modulate Notch signaling (sparrow2006mutationofthe pages 5-7, sparrow2006mutationofthe pages 2-4, sparrow2006mutationofthe pages 4-5). Functional studies demonstrated that this mutation disrupts a highly conserved phenylalanine near the enzyme's active site (sparrow2006mutationofthe pages 4-5, sparrow2006mutationofthe pages 5-7).

### Recent Advances in SCD3 Variant Analysis (2023)

In July 2023, Wengryn et al. published functional characterization of two novel compound heterozygous LFNG variants: **p.R174H and p.G256S** (wengryn2023functionalcharacterizationof pages 2-3, wengryn2023functionalcharacterizationof pages 3-4, wengryn2023functionalcharacterizationof pages 1-2). Their findings revealed important mechanistic insights:

- **p.G256S** is enzymatically nonfunctional, representing a complete loss-of-function allele
- **p.R174H** retains reduced but measurable activity, representing the **first documented hypomorphic LFNG allele**
- Both variants maintained proper Golgi localization and normal protein processing, demonstrating that pathogenicity can result from **quantitative catalytic impairment without trafficking defects** (wengryn2023functionalcharacterizationof pages 8-10, wengryn2023functionalcharacterizationof pages 3-4)

The hypomorphic p.R174H variant was associated with some phenotypic differences, including potentially improved stature (lower arm span-to-height ratio) and more vertebrae, suggesting that residual LFNG activity may modify certain clinical features without preventing severe vertebral malformations (wengryn2023functionalcharacterizationof pages 2-3, wengryn2023functionalcharacterizationof pages 1-2).

These findings establish that SCD3 pathogenic mechanisms include not only complete loss of function through mislocalization or catalytic inactivation, but also partial reduction in enzymatic activity (c2024investigatingtherelationship pages 66-71, c2024investigatingtherelationship pages 133-137).

### Hematopoiesis and T Cell Development

LFNG plays important roles in **T-cell development and hematopoiesis** by modulating Notch signaling in thymic progenitors (yuan2009functionsoflunatic pages 173-182, yuan2009functionsoflunatic pages 145-151, song2016lunaticmanicand pages 1-2). LFNG strengthens Delta-like ligand (particularly DLL4) responses in hematopoietic stem and progenitor cells, promoting T-lineage commitment and suppressing alternative B-cell and myeloid fates (yuan2009functionsoflunatic pages 173-182, yuan2009functionsoflunatic pages 136-145).

In thymic development, LFNG enhances the competitive fitness of T-cell progenitors by increasing their sensitivity to limiting Notch signals, thereby promoting progression through double-negative (DN) to double-positive (DP) stages (yuan2009functionsoflunatic pages 145-151, song2016lunaticmanicand pages 1-2). Loss of all three Fringe genes (LFNG, MFNG, and RFNG) results in reduced DLL4 binding to early thymic progenitors, decreased Notch target gene expression, fewer DN1, DN2, and DP precursors, and altered proportions of mature CD4⁺ and CD8⁺ single-positive T cells (song2016lunaticmanicand pages 1-2, song2016lunaticmanicand pages 11-12).

However, LFNG is **not absolutely essential** for T-cell development, as its function partially overlaps with MFNG and RFNG. Expression of any single Fringe gene can support normal proportions of major T- and B-cell populations, although combined loss impairs T-cell activation, proliferation, and generates immune defects (song2016lunaticmanicand pages 12-13, song2016lunaticmanicand pages 11-12, song2016lunaticmanicand pages 10-11).

### Angiogenesis and Vascular Development

LFNG functions as a critical regulator of **endothelial Notch signaling during angiogenesis**, controlling tip/stalk cell selection, vessel branching, and vascular patterning (ristori2026bmp9regulatesnotch pages 23-28, ristori2026bmp9regulatesnotch pages 3-5, ristori2026bmp9regulatesnotch pages 1-3). The enzyme influences the temporal dynamics of angiogenic sprouting by modulating Dll4-Notch1 binding affinity and activation kinetics (ristori2026bmp9regulatesnotch pages 7-9, ristori2026bmp9regulatesnotch pages 5-7).

#### BMP9-LFNG-Notch Signaling Axis

Recent research has identified an important regulatory pathway linking **BMP9/ALK1 signaling to LFNG expression** in endothelial cells (ristori2026bmp9regulatesnotch pages 3-5, ristori2026bmp9regulatesnotch pages 11-13, ristori2026bmp9regulatesnotch pages 1-3). BMP9 rapidly and transiently induces LFNG through SMAD1/5 signaling, which in turn enhances Dll4-mediated Notch1 activation and regulates the timing of tip/stalk cell selection (ristori2026bmp9regulatesnotch pages 3-5, ristori2026bmp9regulatesnotch pages 5-7). This pathway coordinates multiple aspects of vascular development:

1. **Temporal regulation**: LFNG controls the pace of tip cell selection and the frequency of tip/stalk cell rearrangement or "shuffling" during sprout formation (ristori2026bmp9regulatesnotch pages 23-28, ristori2026bmp9regulatesnotch pages 7-9)

2. **Spatial patterning**: Loss of LFNG increases tip cell selection, vascular density, and sprout length, while reducing the spacing between branches (ristori2026bmp9regulatesnotch pages 23-28, ristori2026bmp9regulatesnotch pages 5-7)

3. **Endothelial-pericyte communication**: LFNG enhances Dll4-Notch1 signaling in endothelial cells while redirecting Jag1 toward Notch3 activation in pericytes, promoting vessel stabilization and pericyte coverage (ristori2026bmp9regulatesnotch pages 9-11, ristori2026bmp9regulatesnotch pages 11-13)

#### Clinical Relevance to Vascular Disease (2024)

A January 2024 study by Tabosh et al. investigated the transcriptomic response to BMP9/BMP10 in endothelial cells from hereditary hemorrhagic telangiectasia (HHT) and pulmonary arterial hypertension (PAH) patients carrying heterozygous ALK1 mutations (tabosh2024impactofheterozygous pages 13-14). The study found that **LFNG regulation by BMP9 was impaired** in some ALK1-mutated endothelial cell models, and reduced Lfng expression was observed in mouse HHT models. However, the authors emphasized that the precise causal role of LFNG dysregulation in HHT/PAH pathogenesis requires further investigation (tabosh2024impactofheterozygous pages 13-14).

In mosaic vessel modeling, LFNG-deficient cells preferentially accumulate at vessel tips and form abnormal vascular structures, potentially contributing to arteriovenous malformation development (ristori2026bmp9regulatesnotch pages 23-28, ristori2026bmp9regulatesnotch pages 7-9).

## Cancer Biology

### Context-Dependent Roles

LFNG exhibits **context-dependent and sometimes opposing roles** in cancer, reflecting its fundamental function as a modulator of ligand-specific Notch signaling. The enzyme has been reported as tumor suppressive in some cancers (breast, prostate, cervical) but is associated with aggressive disease and poor prognosis in others (liver, colorectal, lung, pancreatic) (gong2023apancanceranalysis pages 1-2).

### Pan-Cancer Analysis (2023)

A comprehensive pan-cancer analysis published in November 2023 by Gong et al. examined LFNG expression across 33 cancer types from The Cancer Genome Atlas (gong2023apancanceranalysis pages 6-10, gong2023apancanceranalysis pages 12-14, gong2023apancanceranalysis pages 10-12, gong2023apancanceranalysis pages 1-2). Key findings included:

- **High LFNG expression** was associated with **poor overall survival** in multiple cancers, particularly pancreatic adenocarcinoma (PAAD), uveal melanoma, lower-grade glioma, and mesothelioma (gong2023apancanceranalysis pages 6-10)

- LFNG expression correlated with **copy number variation** (positive correlation) and **DNA methylation** (negative correlation), indicating genomic and epigenetic regulation (gong2023apancanceranalysis pages 6-10)

- **Reduced immune cell infiltration**: High LFNG expression was negatively correlated with tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes, especially T cells, suggesting an immunosuppressive microenvironment (gong2023apancanceranalysis pages 6-10, gong2023apancanceranalysis pages 12-14)

- In vitro experiments with PANC1 pancreatic cancer cells demonstrated that **LFNG overexpression increased proliferation, migration, invasion**, and pseudopodia formation, while altering cell cycle progression (gong2023apancanceranalysis pages 10-12)

- In a clinical PAAD cohort, high LFNG expression was associated with advanced T stage, poor differentiation, higher pathological grade, distant metastasis, and significantly worse overall survival (gong2023apancanceranalysis pages 10-12)

### Pancreatic Cancer Cell-of-Origin Studies (2024-2025)

Groundbreaking research published in 2025 by Chung et al. identified LFNG as a marker of a **unique cell-of-origin for p53-deficient pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC)** (chung2025lfngexpressingcentroacinarcell pages 7-10, chung2025lfngexpressingcentroacinarcell pages 5-7, chung2025lfngexpressingcentroacinarcell pages 3-4, chung2025lfngexpressingcentroacinarcell pages 1-3). Using lineage-tracing approaches in mice, the study revealed:

- LFNG marks a **limited population of centroacinar cells** in the adult pancreas that are particularly susceptible to transformation when oncogenic KRAS is combined with p53 loss (chung2025lfngexpressingcentroacinarcell pages 3-4, chung2025lfngexpressingcentroacinarcell pages 1-3)

- **Genetic deletion of Lfng blocked tumor initiation** from LFNG-expressing centroacinar cells and suppressed tumors arising from acinar- and ductal-derived lineages (chung2025lfngexpressingcentroacinarcell pages 7-10, chung2025lfngexpressingcentroacinarcell pages 1-3)

- **Notch3 was identified as the functional Notch receptor** mediating LFNG-dependent PDAC initiation and progression in this model (chung2025lfngexpressingcentroacinarcell pages 7-10, chung2025lfngexpressingcentroacinarcell pages 5-7)

- LFNG deletion reduced proliferation, altered cell fate toward acinar differentiation, and improved survival in multiple PDAC mouse models (chung2025lfngexpressingcentroacinarcell pages 5-7)

- In human PDAC tissues, **LFNG expression positively correlated with tumor grade** and **high LFNG was associated with shorter overall survival** (chung2025lfngexpressingcentroacinarcell pages 5-7, chung2025lfngexpressingcentroacinarcell pages 1-3)

These findings establish LFNG as a potential **therapeutic target** in PDAC, though they also highlight context-dependency, as earlier mouse models had suggested tumor-suppressive roles (chung2025lfngexpressingcentroacinarcell pages 1-3).

## Current Applications and Limitations

### Established Clinical Applications

The primary established real-world application of LFNG research is **molecular diagnosis and variant interpretation for spondylocostal dysostosis type III**. Functional assays that measure glycosyltransferase activity, Golgi localization, and protein processing are particularly valuable for classifying rare LFNG variants and explaining genotype-phenotype correlations (wengryn2023functionalcharacterizationof pages 2-3, wengryn2023functionalcharacterizationof pages 8-10, c2024investigatingtherelationship pages 133-137).

### Emerging Applications

Beyond genetic diagnosis, LFNG has several emerging applications:

1. **Biomarker development**: LFNG expression shows potential as a prognostic biomarker in pancreatic cancer and other malignancies (gong2023apancanceranalysis pages 6-10, gong2023apancanceranalysis pages 10-12, chung2025lfngexpressingcentroacinarcell pages 5-7)

2. **Research tools**: LFNG is used extensively in basic research to study Notch glycosylation, signaling dynamics, and disease modeling (pennarubia2021modulationofthe pages 1-2, yuan2009functionsoflunatic pages 18-23)

3. **Therapeutic target exploration**: LFNG represents a potential therapeutic target in specific cancer contexts, particularly PDAC, though no LFNG-specific drugs are currently in clinical use (chung2025lfngexpressingcentroacinarcell pages 1-3)

### Evidence Limitations

It is important to note the following limitations:

- **Human loss-of-function evidence** is strongest for SCD3, with over 300 citations for the seminal 2006 paper (sparrow2006mutationofthe pages 4-5)

- Many broader roles in **angiogenesis, hematopoiesis, and cancer** derive primarily from mouse genetics, cell culture systems, transcriptomic associations, or computational models rather than direct human genetic evidence (yuan2009functionsoflunatic pages 18-23, gong2023apancanceranalysis pages 6-10, tabosh2024impactofheterozygous pages 13-14)

- Ligand-specific effects are **context-dependent**, varying with receptor isoform, EGF repeat, glycan extension, ligand presentation, and cellular environment (hou2012galactosedifferentiallymodulates pages 9-10, yuan2009functionsoflunatic pages 18-23)

- Precise human LFNG structural determinants remain **partially model-based**, as detailed atomic structures derive from related family members like mouse Manic Fringe rather than complete human LFNG crystal structures (kadirvelraj2021comparisonofhuman pages 4-5, kadirvelraj2021comparisonofhuman pages 6-7)

## Expert Opinions and Analysis

Contemporary understanding positions LFNG as a **context-dependent fine-tuner of Notch signaling** rather than an absolute on/off switch. As emphasized by recent reviews, the enzyme's effects depend critically on which Notch receptor, which ligand, which EGF repeat, and which cellular context is examined (yuan2009functionsoflunatic pages 18-23). This nuanced view has replaced earlier simplified models.

The 2021 work by Pennarubia et al. demonstrated that when multiple Fringe enzymes are co-expressed, **LFNG exerts a dominant effect over MFNG and RFNG**, particularly for Notch1 modification, establishing a functional hierarchy among mammalian Fringe proteins (pennarubia2021modulationofthe pages 1-2).

Recent mechanistic studies emphasize that LFNG's biological effects extend beyond simple enhancement or suppression of Notch signaling to include **temporal regulation of signaling dynamics**, which is particularly important in oscillatory systems like the segmentation clock and in dynamic processes like angiogenic sprouting (c2024investigatingtherelationship pages 45-49, ristori2026bmp9regulatesnotch pages 23-28, ristori2026bmp9regulatesnotch pages 7-9).

## Conclusions

LFNG (Q8NES3) encodes a Golgi-localized β-1,3-N-acetylglucosaminyltransferase that modifies O-fucose residues on Notch receptor EGF repeats, functioning as a critical regulator of Notch signaling in development, immunity, and disease. The enzyme catalyzes GlcNAc transfer from UDP-GlcNAc to O-fucose through an inverting, metal-dependent mechanism, preferentially enhancing Delta-like ligand-induced Notch activation while suppressing Jagged ligand responses.

Loss-of-function mutations cause spondylocostal dysostosis type III, with recent 2023 studies identifying the first hypomorphic alleles and demonstrating that pathogenicity can result from quantitative catalytic impairment without trafficking defects. Beyond skeletal development, LFNG plays important roles in T-cell development, angiogenesis, and cancer biology.

Recent developments (2023-2025) have particularly advanced understanding of LFNG in pancreatic cancer, where it marks transformation-susceptible centroacinar cells and functions as an oncogene through Notch3 signaling, and in vascular biology, where the BMP9-LFNG-Notch axis regulates endothelial patterning and may contribute to hereditary hemorrhagic telangiectasia pathogenesis. While established clinical applications focus on genetic diagnosis of SCD3, emerging evidence supports potential roles as a cancer biomarker and therapeutic target, though these applications require further validation.
