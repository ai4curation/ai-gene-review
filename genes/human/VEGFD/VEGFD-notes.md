# VEGFD manual review notes

Manual synthesis was used for this review because Perplexity deep research was unavailable during PR preparation.

## Core biology

VEGFD is the VEGFC-like branch of the human VEGF family. It is a secreted precursor with N- and C-terminal propeptides flanking the VEGF homology domain, and processing increases activity toward VEGFR2 and VEGFR3.

PMID:9435229 identified human VEGFD as a ligand for VEGFR2/KDR and VEGFR3/FLT4, showed that the receptor-binding capacity resides in the VEGF homology domain, showed that VEGFD does not bind VEGFR1, and found mitogenic activity on bovine aortic endothelial cells. This is the central evidence for VEGFD receptor binding, receptor activation and endothelial growth factor activity.

PMID:17392173 showed that VEGF-D expression induced peri-insular and peritumoral lymphangiogenesis in a mouse pancreatic beta-cell tumor model. PMID:21515745 showed that proteolytic processing is required for VEGF-D to bind neuropilins and to promote tumor angiogenesis, tumor lymphangiogenesis, and lymph node metastasis in a mouse tumor model.

The early FIGF cloning paper, PMID:9479493, established the gene structure and described mouse c-fos-induced growth factor as a secreted PDGF/VEGF-family factor. Its abstract is adequate for secretion and growth-factor-family context but does not support platelet-derived growth factor receptor binding for human VEGFD.

## Peripheral and unresolved rows

PMID:19275959 directly showed that VEGFD induces human mast-cell chemotaxis. As with VEGFC, this supports a peripheral inflammatory chemotaxis branch rather than the main VEGFR2/VEGFR3 mechanism.

QuickGO source audit: mouse Vegfd/P97946 carries GO:0071542 dopaminergic neuron differentiation from PMID:22535492, a stromal-factor study in NTera2 and human embryonic stem-cell dopaminergic neuron differentiation.

The PMID:27107012 VAMP3 protein-binding row is a pooled yeast two-hybrid result. The current review did not recover a pair-specific primary-table record or orthogonal validation, so it remains unresolved rather than being treated as part of extracellular VEGFD signaling.
