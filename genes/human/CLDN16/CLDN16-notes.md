### Channel route and positive magnesium evidence

The important distinction is the route taken by ions. GO:0015095 and GO:1903830 describe passage across a membrane, whereas CLDN16 contributes to a junctional pathway between epithelial cells. The original PMID:10390358 abstract supports renal tight-junction and magnesium-resorption biology, but its full text was not recovered after publisher failures and bounded searches. No original assay species, figure, or absent experiment is inferred from that access limit. The intracellular-cation homeostasis assertion therefore remains UNDECIDED.

Later direct evidence preserves the magnesium result. Human CLDN16 expressed in MDCK-C7 layers increases magnesium permeability measured by atomic absorption with Ussing-chamber methods; some variants reach junctions but fail to increase flux ([PMID:16528408](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16528408/)). Human CLDN16 and CLDN19 in LLC-PK1 layers yield strong sodium/chloride selectivity, with a different magnesium response in that epithelial background ([PMID:18188451](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18188451/)). Dog CLDN16/19 reconstitution in claudin-deficient MDCKII cells produces substantial magnesium and calcium permeability, with endogenous CLDN16 explicitly controlled and discussed ([PMID:39821681](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39821681/)). These are complementary, context-dependent experiments, not grounds for declaring magnesium permeability absent or converting a junctional channel into a cellular magnesium pump.

The broad intercellular-transport and metal-ion-transport assertions are refined to the existing paracellular process. The latter is biologically valid as written: its definition includes movement between cells. The replacement makes the route explicit and changes the axis of specificity; it does not claim an ontology parent/child relationship or discard metal-ion selectivity. No NEW process is needed because both the channel and process already occur in the seed.
