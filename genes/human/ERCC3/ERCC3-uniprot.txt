CC   -!- FUNCTION: ATP-dependent 3'-5' DNA helicase/translocase
CC       (PubMed:17466626, PubMed:27193682, PubMed:33902107, PubMed:8465201,
CC       PubMed:8663148). Binds dsDNA rather than ssDNA, unzipping it in a
CC       translocase rather than classical helicase activity (PubMed:27193682,
CC       PubMed:33902107). Component of the general transcription and DNA repair
CC       factor IIH (TFIIH) core complex (PubMed:10024882, PubMed:17466626,
CC       PubMed:8157004, PubMed:8465201). When complexed to CDK-activating
CC       kinase (CAK), involved in RNA transcription by RNA polymerase II. The
CC       ATPase activity of XPB/ERCC3, but not its helicase activity, is
CC       required for DNA opening; it may wrap around the damaged DNA wedging it
CC       open, causing localized melting that allows XPD/ERCC2 helicase to
CC       anchor (PubMed:10024882, PubMed:17466626). In transcription, TFIIH has
CC       an essential role in transcription initiation (PubMed:30894545,
CC       PubMed:8157004). When the pre-initiation complex (PIC) has been
CC       established, TFIIH is required for promoter opening and promoter escape
CC       (PubMed:8157004). The ATP-dependent helicase activity of XPB/ERCC3 is
CC       required for promoter opening and promoter escape (PubMed:10024882). In
CC       transcription pre-initiation complexes induces and propagates a DNA
CC       twist to open DNA (PubMed:27193682, PubMed:33902107). Also involved in
CC       transcription-coupled nucleotide excision repair (NER) of damaged DNA
CC       (PubMed:17466626, PubMed:2111438, PubMed:8157004). In NER, TFIIH acts
CC       by opening DNA around the lesion to allow the excision of the damaged
CC       oligonucleotide and its replacement by a new DNA fragment. The
CC       structure of the TFIIH transcription complex differs from the NER-TFIIH
CC       complex; large movements by XPD/ERCC2 and XPB/ERCC3 are stabilized by
CC       XPA (PubMed:31253769, PubMed:33902107). XPA retains XPB/ERCC3 at the 5'
CC       end of a DNA bubble (mimicking DNA damage) (PubMed:31253769).
CC       {ECO:0000269|PubMed:10024882, ECO:0000269|PubMed:17466626,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:30894545, ECO:0000269|PubMed:31253769,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:33902107, ECO:0000269|PubMed:7724549,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:8157004, ECO:0000269|PubMed:8663148,
CC       ECO:0000305|PubMed:8465201}.

CC   -!- SUBUNIT: Component of the 7-subunit TFIIH core complex composed of
CC       XPB/ERCC3, XPD/ERCC2, GTF2H1, GTF2H2, GTF2H3, GTF2H4 and GTF2H5, which
CC       is active in NER. The core complex associates with the 3-subunit CDK-
CC       activating kinase (CAK) module composed of CCNH/cyclin H, CDK7 and
CC       MNAT1 to form the 10-subunit holoenzyme (holo-TFIIH) active in
CC       transcription (PubMed:8663148, PubMed:9852112). Interacts with PUF60
CC       (PubMed:10882074, PubMed:11239393). Interacts with ATF7IP
CC       (PubMed:19106100). Interacts with KAT2A; leading to KAT2A recruitment
CC       to promoters and acetylation of histones (PubMed:30894545). Part of
CC       TBP-based Pol II pre-initiation complex (PIC), in which Pol II core
CC       assembles with general transcription factors and other specific
CC       initiation factors including GTF2E1, GTF2E2, GTF2F1, GTF2F2, TCEA1,
CC       ERCC2, ERCC3, GTF2H2, GTF2H3, GTF2H4, GTF2H5, GTF2A1, GTF2A2, GTF2B and
CC       TBP; this large multi-subunit PIC complex mediates DNA unwinding and
CC       targets Pol II core to the transcription start site where the first
CC       phosphodiester bond forms. {ECO:0000269|PubMed:10882074,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:11239393, ECO:0000269|PubMed:19106100,
