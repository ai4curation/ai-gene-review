## Catalytic synthesis: principal GlcA transfer and secondary GlcNAc capacity

Two transfer reactions describe the enzyme more precisely than a generic glycosyltransferase or autonomous-polymerase label. EXT1 GT-B supplies the principal glucuronyl-transfer reaction in the Golgi EXT1–EXT2 complex. Positive human EXT1 single-transfer and chain-extension assays also support GlcNAc-transfer capacity. That capacity is retained with an explicit quantitative boundary: the modern complex studies favor EXT2 as the dominant GlcNAc-transferase, differ in the effects of EXT1 GT-A substitutions, and report stability or preparation-integrity limitations. Neither a blanket EXT1 GlcNAc-inactivity claim nor equivalence of isolated EXT1 to the native heterocomplex follows.

The older human soluble EXT1 29–746 preparation in PMID:12907669 was affinity captured from HEK293 conditioned medium. Single-transfer radiochemical assays and product digestion complement its polymerization experiments. The reported isoelectric-focusing assessment of endogenous partner contamination is data not shown, and apparent gel-filtration size does not establish an obligatory native oligomer. The 2022 and 2023 human preparations use different soluble boundaries and assays; low-yield, substantially proteolyzed EXT1 alone in the latter study permits qualitative activity but not a reliable quantitative comparison.

The core location is the Golgi membrane, supported by the accepted topology/localization evidence and native-participant model, not by secretion of engineered soluble constructs. The two catalytic units share this setting and one biosynthetic process; no developmental, generic-binding, ancestor-process or additional complex-activity unit is added. Questions focus on native GT-A contribution, catalytic versus stability effects and coordination of alternating sugar transfer. The accepted source discrepancy queries remain unchanged.
