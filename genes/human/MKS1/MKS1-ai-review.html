Gene Description

MKS1 is a B9/C2-domain protein of the ciliary transition zone and a core subunit of the MKS (Meckel syndrome) module. Together with B9D2 and B9D1 it forms a soluble heterotrimeric B9-domain complex. The complex localizes to the distal transition zone at the level of the Y-links, between the axonemal microtubules and the ciliary membrane. There it is needed for the diffusion barrier that keeps the ciliary membrane's protein composition distinct from the plasma membrane. MKS1 is also needed for normal ciliogenesis and the control of centrosome number in some cell types. Loss-of-function mutations cause Meckel-Gruber syndrome and Joubert syndrome.

Core Functions

MKS1 is a soluble B9/C2-domain subunit of the transition-zone MKS module. It binds B9D2 directly to form the MKS1-B9D2-B9D1 complex, and the three proteins localize to the distal transition zone in an interdependent way. In that position the complex helps maintain the diffusion barrier that keeps ciliary membrane composition (e.g. ARL13B, INPP5E) distinct. Loss of MKS1 also causes variable defects in cilium assembly and centrosome number. MKS1 has no characterized catalytic activity.
