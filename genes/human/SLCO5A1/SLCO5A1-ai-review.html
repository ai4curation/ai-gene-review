Gene Description

Solute carrier organic anion transporter family member 5A1 (OATP5A1), a 12-transmembrane plasma membrane protein and the most divergent member of the human SLCO/OATP (SLC21) family. It is expressed widely, including in brain neurons and astrocytes, heart, gastrointestinal tract and many tumours, and is found at the plasma membrane of ductal epithelial cells in normal breast tissue, with an additional cytoplasmic pool in breast tumours. Unlike the hepatic and intestinal OATPs, which carry bile acids, conjugated steroids and many widely used drugs, OATP5A1 long had no identified substrate. A single characterisation in overexpressing HEK293 cells reports it as a low-affinity carrier that takes up the conjugated steroid estrone-3-sulfate, the aromatic amino acid L-tyrosine and the vitamin thiamine, and that exports the neutral amino acids glutamine and glycine; pantothenic acid and thymine inhibit its tyrosine uptake. That substrate profile is not yet corroborated: two earlier studies in Xenopus oocytes found no estrone-3-sulfate uptake, and the bidirectional behaviour, while consistent with the exchange mechanism generally attributed to OATPs, has not been resolved into a coupled antiport cycle. Variation at the SLCO5A1 locus has been associated with impulsivity in juvenile myoclonic epilepsy.

Proposed New Ontology Terms

estrone 3-sulfate transmembrane transporter activity

Definition: Enables the transfer of estrone 3-sulfate from one side of a membrane to the other.

Justification: Estrone-3-sulfate is the canonical probe substrate of the OATP/SLCO family and has been measured for most human OATPs, yet GO has no molecular-function term for it. The two terms curators would previously have used, GO:0015347 (sodium-independent organic anion transmembrane transporter activity) and GO:0008514 (organic anion transmembrane transporter activity), are both obsolete, so OATP estrone-3-sulfate transport is currently annotated to the root-level GO:0022857 transmembrane transporter activity - which is what SLCO5A1 carries here. A specific term would let the widely measured, and in this case contested, estrone-3-sulfate result be recorded at the granularity at which it was actually determined, for this gene (Km 102.2 uM) and across the family.

Parent term: transmembrane transporter activity

Core Functions

OATP5A1 is a 12-transmembrane member of the organic anion transporting polypeptide (SLCO/SLC21) family that resides in the plasma membrane and, on family grounds, moves solutes by coupling to another solute gradient rather than to ATP hydrolysis. It was the last well-expressed orphan of the family, and its substrate profile has been reported only once, from a single laboratory using HEK293 cells overexpressing the protein: uptake of estrone-3-sulfate (Km 102 uM), L-tyrosine (Km 170 uM) and thiamine (Km 16 uM), together with efflux of glutamine and glycine, with pantothenic acid and thymine inhibiting tyrosine uptake. The molecular function is stated at this general level deliberately. The bidirectional profile is readily explained if OATP5A1 is an electroneutral exchanger, which would make the influx and efflux substrates two halves of one antiport cycle, but no counter-substrate or stoichiometry has been measured; and the flagship substrate, estrone-3-sulfate, was not transported in two earlier Xenopus oocyte studies, so the profile is demonstrably system-dependent. The specific substrate molecular functions are recorded in this review as reviewer proposals awaiting independent replication rather than as established activities.

Suggested Questions for Experts

Q: Does OATP5A1 transport estrone-3-sulfate at all? PMID:42231149 reports uptake with a Km of 102.2 uM in HEK293 cells, but states that two earlier studies in Xenopus laevis oocytes found no significant uptake. Since estrone-3-sulfate is the reference substrate against which the whole OATP family is calibrated, this single disagreement determines how much of the rest of the substrate profile should be believed. A third, independent system is needed.

Suggested experts: Joerg Koenig, Martin F Fromm

Q: Is OATP5A1 an obligate exchanger, and if so what is the counter-substrate? The reported profile - tyrosine, thiamine and estrone-3-sulfate in, glutamine and glycine out, in the same cells - is exactly what an electroneutral antiporter would produce, and would make the individual substrate assignments mechanistically interdependent rather than independent. Nothing in the paper measures coupling, stoichiometry or electrogenicity.

Suggested experts: Joerg Koenig, Anna Gessner

Q: Do any of these transport activities occur at endogenous expression levels? Every functional result comes from stably transfected HEK293 cells overexpressing OATP5A1. Knockdown or knockout in a cell type with meaningful endogenous expression - astrocytes or neurons for the glutamine and glycine efflux claim, intestinal epithelium for thiamine - would convert a heterologous activity into a cellular function.

Suggested experts: Joerg Koenig, Martin F Fromm

Q: Is SLCO5A1 basolateral in any polarised epithelium? The IBA-derived GO:0016323 annotation has been generalised to GO:0005886 in this review because no SLCO5A1-specific observation establishes polarity - HEK293 cells are unpolarised and the breast-tissue study reports only plasma membrane. Polarised filter-grown epithelial monolayers (Caco-2, MDCK) with domain-selective biotinylation would settle it, and would matter for the proposed intestinal thiamine absorption role, which requires apical localisation rather than basolateral.

Suggested experts: Joerg Koenig

Q: Are the reported affinities compatible with a physiological role? The Km values for estrone-3-sulfate (102 uM) and tyrosine (170 uM) sit well above the plasma concentrations of those compounds, and thiamine's (15.6 uM) is roughly two orders of magnitude above plasma thiamine. If OATP5A1 is a genuine low-affinity high-capacity carrier, the tissues where it matters are those with locally high substrate concentrations, such as the intestinal lumen or tumour interstitium - which would be a testable prediction rather than a caveat.

Suggested experts: Martin F Fromm

    tissue-level validation.
  2. Direct contradiction with prior work on the flagship substrate.
    PMID:42231149 The authors' own explanation is system-dependent:
    PMID:42231149 So the very first substrate is already a
    two-against-one disagreement, resolved here in favour of the newer study by the newer
    study.
  3. Affinities are low relative to physiological concentrations.
    PMID:42231149 Thiamine's Km (15.6 µM) sits far above plasma thiamine
    (40–120 nM); the authors argue relevance only in the intestinal lumen.
  4. Bidirectionality is unusual for an OATP but not incoherent. OATPs are widely modelled
    as electroneutral exchangers; an obligate antiporter naturally produces net uptake of
    one substrate and net efflux of another in the same cell, and the family-level IBA term
    already on this gene, GO:0015291 secondary active transmembrane transporter activity, is
    exactly the term that accommodates this. I read the uptake/efflux pattern as supporting
    rather than undermining the IBA — but the exchange stoichiometry and counter-ion were not
    determined, so this remains a model, not a result.
  5. Several "substrates" are inferred from inhibition only:
    pantothenic acid and thymine inhibited tyrosine uptake but showed no direct uptake because
    of unremovable background. I do not annotate those.

SLCO2B1 (O94956), SLCO1B3 (Q9NPD5), SLCO1C1 (Q9NYB5), SLCO3A1 (Q9UIG8), SLCO1B1 (Q9Y6L6),
