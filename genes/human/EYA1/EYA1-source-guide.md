# EYA1: evidence and source boundaries

This guide records the original experiments behind the direct annotations. Existing publication excerpts are preserved verbatim; their presence does not expand the assay scope. The three natural human products (592, 559 and 557 residues) are distinct. A human host, an accession label or a shared-peptide identification does not establish that every product was tested.

## Exact SIX1 screen records

The four direct binding contexts all name [human SIX1, Q15475](https://www.uniprot.org/uniprotkb/Q15475/entry), an independently established DNA-binding transcription factor. GO:0140297 therefore specifies the partner class without inferring coactivation from an interaction screen alone.

- [BioPlex 2.0, PMID:28514442](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature22366): official `BioPlex_interactionList_v2.tsv`, row 5179, Q15475–Q99502; `BioPlex_2.0_293T_DirectedEdges.tsv`, rows 17120–17121, both orientations. Actual original Methods/Results and the full [public tables](https://bioplex.hms.harvard.edu/downloadInteractions.php) were inspected. C-terminal HA-FLAG AP-MS in HEK293T establishes cellular association, not an isolated two-protein reaction.
- [HuRI, PMID:32296183](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-020-2188-x): original PSI table records 1089, 59259 and 61370 pair Q15475/ORF 5442 with Q99502-1/ORF 56381, with validated two-hybrid, array and prey-pooling methods. Human protein identities, yeast host, N-terminal GAL4 fusions and partial sequence identification are explicit. Actual main XML and [public records](https://www.interactome-atlas.org/download) were read; supplementary Methods were not recovered. Study-wide orthogonal validation is not pair-specific evidence.
- [BioPlex 3.0, PMID:33961781](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2021.04.011): `BioPlex_293T_Network_10K_Dec_2019.tsv`, row 36249; directed rows 42548–42549. ## Interpretation of preserved excerpts

The 2009 minimal-pSer excerpt is an Eya3-fragment result, and the general SIX/EYA bipartite-factor sentence is background rather than an EYA1-specific reporter result. The 2003 phosphatase-switch excerpt includes Eya3-specific mechanistic work. These inherited texts remain unchanged but are explicitly contextualized in the revised reasons. No new publication quotations were added.

The positive H2AX step is a repair-regulatory mechanism; detailed Eya3 ChIP, rescue and recruitment assays are not relabeled EYA1. Cytoplasmic detection is positive evidence and does not establish an inactive storage pool. Morphogenesis is retained as a conserved developmental role; precise sensory-perception participation remains unresolved. The two existing NEW rows are historical assertions being reassessed, not additional annotations or a newly asserted coverage gap.

## Independent localization and conserved source chains

The current [Human Protein Atlas EYA1 page](https://www.proteinatlas.org/ENSG00000104313-EYA1/subcellular) assigns supported nucleoplasm and nuclear-body localization. Selected HPA028917 Rh30 and U2OS composites show diffuse/granular nuclear signal and brighter puncta; the selected RPE1 image is weaker. The 146-residue antigen is present in all three source products. The images do not establish isoform-specific localization, a particular nuclear-body subtype or a specialized nuclear-body mechanism. Broad nucleus and specific nucleoplasm annotations can coexist without requiring every observation to have the same spatial resolution.

In [Reactome R-HSA-5683964](https://reactome.org/content/detail/R-HSA-5683964), input set 5683966 hasMember EYA1 5683625; output set 5683980 hasCandidate phosphorylated EYA1 5683951. Both entities independently identify human Q99502 residues 1–592 and their own nucleoplasm compartment. EYA1 is the phosphorylation substrate of ATM in this event. The modeled location does not make EYA1 the event's kinase or resolve shorter products.

## SUMO substrate versus conjugation activity

The mouse P97767 donor's current SUMO annotation points to [PMID:16990542](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1128406). The actual main text and supplementary Methods describe Myc-Eya1, HA-SUMO1 and UBC9 in 293T cells, followed by detection of conjugates on Eya1. The source numbering follows cited mouse Eya1 work; a human expression host is not a human-construct identifier. [PMID:24954506](https://doi.org/10.1038/onc.2014.179) independently detects SUMO on endogenous human EYA1 and studies mouse Eya1 constructs with SENP2 and lysine-mutant controls. Its site interpretation favors K43/K146 rather than simply transferring the earlier K459 interpretation.

These are positive demonstrations of EYA1 regulation by SUMO. They do not demonstrate EYA1 executing the attachment step performed by UBC9 and the conjugation machinery. Removing the transferred protein-sumoylation process attribution therefore preserves the observed modification while correcting the assigned role.


## Core-function synthesis: operative evidence and inherited context

The ordered coactivator core uses the original [mouse Eya1/Six study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC84678/), Methods (mouse cDNAs and COS7 reporters), Figures 2–3 and Table 1. The [SIX1 mutation study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC419562/), Methods and Figures 3–4, provides Eya1/Six1 interaction and reporter cooperation; its human HEK293 host does not establish the Eya1 insert's species or natural product. Human EYA1–SIX1 screen records independently support the conserved partnership. In the [2009 biochemical study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2742842/), Figure 5B is the EYA1 nuclear-localization experiment, whereas the purified binding and reporter work is EYA2. The inherited bipartite-factor quotation is retained as family context. The inherited [SIX1–DACH switch quotation](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature02083) also retains its position; the detailed coactivator-recruitment assays use Eya3 and do not prove a universal EYA1 switch.

The ordered phosphatase core uses [H2AX damage-response experiments](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2692521/), Figures 2D and 4B–E and the in-vitro phosphatase/siRNA Methods. The Eya1 affinity isolate and endogenous human knockdown support H2AX phosphotyrosine regulation. The bacterially purified peptide assay, detailed I-PpoI/MDC1 work and plotted rescue are Eya3; the additional Eya1 rescue is reported in the main text but its supplement was not inspected. The retained broad EYA quotations therefore do not claim that every experiment individually tested EYA1. [PMID:14628052](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14628052/) provides the independently inspected mouse Eya1-domain tyrosine-phosphatase assay described above.

The core retains [GO:0004725](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/QuickGO/term/GO:0004725) and explicitly describes the H2AX reaction. The current classification of [GO:0140793](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/QuickGO/term/GO:0140793) reaches histone phosphatase and [chromatin remodeling](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/QuickGO/term/GO:0006338), but does not contain GO:0004725 in its captured ancestry. The two activities are not duplicated to conceal that classification boundary. Positive repair regulation remains distinct from the enzymes that restore DNA strands. The conserved inner-ear developmental assertion is retained independently of any untested otic promoter mechanism.

The Ser/Thr attribution question retains the positive preparation assays and the competing partner evidence: [PMID:27795300](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5192086/), Figures 1–3 and Methods; [PMID:29535359](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5849647/), Figure 3 versus the mouse Eya3 mechanistic experiments; and [PMID:33472197](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8118085/), Figure 6, Figure S6 and protein-purification Methods. Figure S6 includes positive DRG/SF9 lysate controls. No new original or figure acquisition was needed for synthesis; the earlier direct/database read scopes and access limits remain authoritative.
