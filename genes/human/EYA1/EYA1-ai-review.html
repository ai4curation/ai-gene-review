Gene Description

EYA1 (eyes absent homolog 1) is a nuclear transcriptional coactivator and protein tyrosine phosphatase of the haloacid dehalogenase (HAD) superfamily. It lacks DNA-binding activity and is recruited to target genes by SIX homeodomain transcription factors (SIX1, SIX2, SIX4, SIX5), which also translocate it from the cytoplasm into the nucleus; the N-terminal region supplies transactivation activity and the conserved C-terminal Eya domain mediates SIX binding and phosphatase catalysis. Its phosphatase activity switches SIX1-DACH complexes from repression to activation. After DNA damage, EYA1 (with EYA3) dephosphorylates histone H2AX on Tyr-142, favouring recruitment of repair factors and cell survival over apoptosis. EYA1 is required for development of the inner ear, kidney (metanephric induction upstream of GDNF), branchial arch derivatives, craniofacial skeleton and hypaxial muscle; haploinsufficiency causes branchio-oto-renal and branchio-otic syndromes and otofaciocervical syndrome, and rare missense variants have been reported with congenital cataract and anterior segment anomalies. SUMO1 modification attenuates and Akt phosphorylation enhances its transcriptional activity.

Identity and domains

  • Eyes absent homolog 1; N-terminal proline/serine/threonine-rich transactivation region and a
    conserved ~270 aa C-terminal Eya domain (ED) that belongs to the HAD phosphatase superfamily.
    PMID:19497856
  • Family-level: Eya is a non-thiol HAD protein tyrosine phosphatase (Drosophila eya)
    PMID:14628053.

Transcriptional coactivator for SIX proteins

  • EYA has no DNA-binding domain; SIX supplies DNA binding, EYA transactivation
    PMID:19497856.
  • Six2/4/5 translocate Eya1 into the nucleus; synergistic activation of myogenin promoter
    PMID:10490620.
  • Human EYA1 is cytoplasmic without SIX1 and nuclear with SIX1; SIX1 V17E BOR mutant fails
    PMID:19497856.
  • Eya phosphatase switches Six1-Dach from repression to activation
    PMID:14628042.
  • SUMO1 modification of EYA1 inhibits its transcriptional activity; Akt phosphorylation reduces it
    PMID:24954506. EYA1 is the substrate, so
    "protein sumoylation" (ISS) is a substrate-type annotation, not participation.

Protein tyrosine phosphatase / H2AX pY142

  • Cook et al. 2009: HA-Eya1 dephosphorylates H2AX pY142 in vitro; D323A dead mutant inactive;
    Eya1 siRNA increases pY142 after IR; rescue requires catalytic activity
    PMID:19234442.
  • Ser/Thr activity: the same paper reports minimal activity toward a pSer H2AX peptide (Eya3 ED) and
    says Eya primarily acts as a tyrosine phosphatase in vivo
    PMID:19234442.
    Ser/Thr phosphatase annotations (EXP + IEA) therefore look like over-annotation for EYA1.
  • Krishnan et al. 2009 showed EYA2/EYA3 (not EYA1) specificity for H2AX Y142 PMID:19351884.
  • BOR missense mutations abolish EYA1 phosphatase activity whereas ocular-defect mutations do not

description: EYA1 (eyes absent homolog 1) is a nuclear transcriptional coactivator and protein tyrosine phosphatase
  of the haloacid dehalogenase (HAD) superfamily. It lacks DNA-binding activity and is recruited to target genes
  by SIX homeodomain transcription factors (SIX1, SIX2, SIX4, SIX5), which also translocate it from the cytoplasm

  to activation. After DNA damage, EYA1 (with EYA3) dephosphorylates histone H2AX on Tyr-142, favouring recruitment
  of repair factors and cell survival over apoptosis. EYA1 is required for development of the inner ear, kidney

  haploinsufficiency causes branchio-oto-renal and branchio-otic syndromes and otofaciocervical syndrome, and rare
  missense variants have been reported with congenital cataract and anterior segment anomalies. SUMO1 modification
