Gene Description

CDK13 is a nuclear cyclin-dependent protein kinase that couples RNA-polymerase-II transcription with RNA processing. In association with cyclin K, it phosphorylates serines in the polymerase C-terminal repeat domain and supports transcriptional output. Its N-terminal arginine/serine-rich region directs enrichment in nuclear speckles, and CDK13 influences alternative splice-site selection. A distinct substrate, ZC3H14, is phosphorylated at Ser475 by a cyclin-associated CDK13 complex to promote recruitment of nuclear RNA-surveillance machinery. Heterozygous kinase-domain variants cause a developmental syndrome that can include congenital heart defects, distinctive facial features and intellectual disability.

Core Functions

Phosphorylates the RNA-polymerase-II CTD as the catalytic subunit of a cyclin K complex, supporting nuclear transcriptional output and the elongation-associated program. Human biochemical assays establish Ser2/Ser5 activity; preference for a pre-phosphorylated Ser7 substrate is not Ser7 catalysis.

In a cyclin-associated context, CDK13 influences splice-site selection; independently, CDK13/cyclin T1 directly phosphorylates ZC3H14 Ser475 to promote recruitment of nuclear RNA-surveillance machinery. These are protein-kinase functions, not intrinsic RNA-cleavage activities.
