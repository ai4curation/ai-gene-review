The authentic [publisher PDF](https://pure.mpg.de/rest/items/item_2241897_4/component/file_2241895/content) was inspected for selected Results, Figures 1, 4, 5 and 7 and construct/rescue Methods. Existing transcription/elongation annotations are retained on phosphoregulation and transcript-output evidence, without inventing a measured elongation velocity. GO:0008024 describes the transcriptional cyclin/CDK complex class; the core uses the specific cyclin K-CDK13 complex.

The second catalytic core is protein serine kinase activity on RNA-processing proteins. [PMID:37079685](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37079685/) was read in the genuine full XML-derived cache, especially Figure 4D–I and corresponding Results. Human melanoma CDK13 interacts with nuclear ZC3H14; CDK13/cyclin T1 directly phosphorylates ZC3H14 Ser475 in a wild-type versus S475A substrate assay. Phosphomimetic recruitment experiments connect this site to PAXT-associated RNA-surveillance machinery. CDK13 performs phosphotransfer, not RNA degradation. No new RNA-decay process is inferred merely from perturbation or rescue. Zebrafish and mouse experiments in the paper retain their separate species scope.

The human CDC2L5 abstract in [PMID:16721827](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16721827/) independently establishes nuclear/speckle localization and changes in splice-site selection. [PMID:18480452](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18480452/) explicitly connects CDK13 to ASF/SF2 phosphorylation and splicing; only its authentic abstract was recovered, so uninspected kinase controls are not claimed. The directly read ZC3H14 assay supplies an independent catalytic example rather than treating a splicing phenotype as proof of every substrate relationship. Neither a splicing influence nor localization assigns intrinsic RNA cleavage to CDK13.
