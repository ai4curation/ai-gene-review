Gene Description

CDKL5 is a serine/threonine protein kinase with an N-terminal catalytic domain and a long regulatory C-terminal region. It phosphorylates cytoskeletal regulatory proteins and supports dendritic microtubule dynamics and excitatory postsynaptic organization. Human neuronal models link loss of CDKL5 to reduced substrate phosphorylation and abnormal synaptic structure. A nuclear pool participates in transcriptional repression near induced DNA breaks, while additional centrosomal, midbody and ciliary functions have been demonstrated in defined cell-culture contexts. Alternative C-terminal products differ in expression and regulation. Pathogenic variants cause CDKL5 deficiency disorder, characterized by early seizures and severe neurodevelopmental impairment.

This appended assessment supersedes the opening statement that only the neuronal setting is represented in the core. The original journal is preserved. A second Ser/Thr-kinase core now describes the human cultured-cell response to induced DNA breaks, without generalizing it to constitutive neuronal transcription or DNA repair.

The full CDKL5 primary study, PMID:34605059 supports an elongation model more strongly than the earlier short rationale conveyed. ELOA is both an elongation factor and a component of an RNAPII-directed ubiquitin ligase; CDKL5 phosphorylates its Ser311 site. DRB prevents both proteins from accumulating at laser-damage tracks (Figures 3 and 6; associated Results). These observations make an elongation mechanism plausible. They do not show that CDKL5-dependent phosphorylation changes ELOA's elongation or ligase activity, or that Ser311 is necessary or sufficient for repression. ELOA recruitment itself persists after CDKL5 disruption and for the S311A mutant. The Discussion explicitly leaves the relevant substrate mechanism unresolved.

The assay order matters. In Methods, MS2 foci, FokI is induced for 3 hours before doxycycline is added for another 3 hours to induce reporter transcription. The separate FokI recruitment experiment activates transcription before damage. Thus recruitment's requirement for ongoing transcription does not identify the stage inhibited in the silencing assay. Figure 8 establishes CDKL5-dependent repression of endogenous SLCO5A1/RYR2, with WT but not kinase-dead K42R rescue and cleavage/repair controls, but RNA abundance does not isolate initiation, promoter escape or elongation.
