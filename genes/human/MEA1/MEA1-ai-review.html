Gene Description

MEA1 is a small (~20 kDa), intrinsically disordered cytosolic protein that acts as an assembly chaperone for the heterotetrameric clathrin adaptor complex AP-1. It has no recognizable fold or sequence similarity to other protein families, yet it is functionally bipartite: its N-terminal region binds and stabilizes the mu1 subunit of AP-1 while its C-terminal region independently binds and stabilizes the beta1 subunit, so that a single molecule holds two different unassembled subunits at once. This MEA1-bound mu1:beta1 hemicomplex is completed when it meets the complementary gamma:sigma1 hemicomplex held by the assembly chaperone AAGAB; the two chaperones never contact each other, and both are ejected as the AP-1 tetramer closes, MEA1 in particular because it occupies the gamma-binding surface of beta1. MEA1 therefore acts only on free subunits and is not part of the mature adaptor, and it remains cytosolic while assembled AP-1 is membrane-associated. Cells lacking MEA1 lose all four AP-1 subunits and the trans-Golgi AP-1 puncta, mis-sort constitutive AP-1 cargo such as folate receptor alpha, and fail to terminate STING signalling. MEA1 is dedicated mainly to AP-1, contributes little to AP-2 and nothing detectable to AP-3, AP-4 or AP-5. Despite its name, which comes from its original cloning out of a testis cDNA library in 1989, MEA1 is expressed ubiquitously and is conserved across vertebrates and in some invertebrates.

Proposed New Ontology Terms

AP-type membrane coat adaptor complex subunit binding

Definition: Binding to a free, unassembled subunit of a heterotetrameric AP-type membrane coat adaptor complex (GO:0030119) - for example the mu1 or beta1 subunit of AP-1, the gamma or sigma1 subunit of AP-1, or the alpha or sigma2 subunit of AP-2 - as distinct from binding the assembled adaptor complex.

Justification: This is the same term proposed in this repository's AAGAB review, repeated here with MEA1 evidence attached rather than duplicated as a separate proposal, so that the two assembly chaperones argue for one term. GO's adaptor-binding terms are all complex-level: AP-1 (GO:0035650), AP-2 (GO:0035612) and AP-3 (GO:0035651) adaptor complex binding all denote binding to the assembled heterotetramer, which is precisely what assembly chaperones do not do. MEA1 makes the point sharply: it binds free mu1 and free beta1, and structural modelling shows it occupies the gamma-binding site on beta1, so MEA1 physically cannot be bound to a complete AP-1. Annotating MEA1 with GO:0035650 therefore asserts the one interaction the published mechanism excludes. A single subunit-level term would serve MEA1, AAGAB and CCDC32 alike.

protein complex assembly chaperone activity

Definition: Binding to one or more folded subunits of a protein complex in order to stabilise them and promote their ordered assembly into that complex, where the binding protein does not form part of the finished complex.

Justification: Also carried over from the AAGAB review. GO's chaperone molecular functions concern folding (GO:0044183 protein folding chaperone is defined around binding an unfolded protein to fold it), whereas MEA1's clients are folded adaptor subunits whose problem is exposed interaction surfaces and rapid degradation, not misfolding - the structural models suggest MEA1 binding shields exposed hydrophobic residues on mu1 and beta1. The biological process side is covered by GO:0051131 chaperone-mediated protein complex assembly, but there is no matching molecular function, which is why both MEA1 and AAGAB can currently only be annotated with complex-binding terms that misstate what they do. MEA1 adds a second, independent example of the gap and a new mechanistic variant of it - two chaperones loading complementary halves of one complex and colliding.

Core Functions

Assembly chaperone for the AP-1 clathrin adaptor complex. MEA1 is an intrinsically disordered cytosolic protein with two functionally independent hands: its N-terminal domain binds and stabilizes the free mu1 subunit, its C-terminal domain binds and stabilizes the free beta1 subunit, and neither domain recognises the other's client. Holding both at once it forms a soluble mu1:beta1 hemicomplex that is otherwise degraded, and this hemicomplex is completed by collision with the gamma:sigma1 hemicomplex held by AAGAB. The two chaperones do not touch each other and both are released as the tetramer closes; MEA1 must leave because it sits on the gamma-binding surface of beta1. MEA1 therefore never binds assembled AP-1, stays in the cytosol while mature AP-1 goes to membranes, and is not a subunit of the adaptor. Its client range is narrow - AP-1 above all, AP-2 weakly, AP-3/AP-4/AP-5 not at all. The molecular_function term below is a stand-in: it names the assembled complex, while MEA1 in fact binds the free subunits, which is the interaction the mechanism turns on. The accurate terms are the two entries under proposed_new_terms.

Suggested Questions for Experts

Q: Is MEA1 genuinely dispensable for spermatogenesis? Every testis-related annotation on this gene descends from a 1989 expression study, and a germline knockout with a fertility readout has never been reported. Given that the gene is named for the hypothesis, testing it would settle a 35-year-old annotation.

Suggested experts: Jingshi Shen

Q: What sets the stoichiometry of the dual-chaperone collision? MEA1 and AAGAB do not interact, so nothing obviously couples production of the mu1:beta1 and gamma:sigma1 hemicomplexes. Is there a quality-control route that disposes of unpaired MEA1:mu1:beta1, and does either chaperone become limiting under stress?

Suggested experts: Jingshi Shen, Chun Wan

Q: Does MEA1's role in AP-2 matter in any cell type? AP-2 subunits fall only moderately in MEA1 knockouts, which could mean a weak direct activity or redundancy with AAGAB and CCDC32. A triple-depletion series would distinguish these.

Suggested experts: Jingshi Shen

Q: Do AP-3 and AP-5 have their own assembly chaperones, and is any of them a MEA1 paralogue or analogue? MEA1 has no sequence similarity to anything, so the family of "bi-handed" chaperones may be structurally unrecognisable and only findable functionally.

Suggested experts: Qian Yin

Suggested Experiments

Experiment: Determine the structure of the reconstituted MEA1:mu1:beta1 ternary complex by cryo-EM or crystallography, and compare the conformational state of free MEA1 by NMR or hydrogen- deuterium exchange mass spectrometry. This would test the AlphaFold trimeric model directly and establish whether the two hands are used together or alternately.

Hypothesis: MEA1 is an intrinsically disordered protein that folds only on engaging its clients, and one MEA1 molecule can bridge mu1 and beta1 simultaneously in cells.

Type: structural biology of the reconstituted chaperone-client complex

Experiment: Generate a germline Mea1 knockout and a germ-cell-conditional knockout in mouse and assess fertility, sperm counts and spermatid morphology alongside AP-1 subunit levels in testis and in somatic tissues. A fertility phenotype that tracks AP-1 loss would reframe the old annotation as a downstream consequence rather than a distinct function; no phenotype would retire it.

Hypothesis: The testis annotations on MEA1 are an artefact of its name and its expression pattern, not a reflection of a germ-cell-specific requirement.

Type: mouse genetics with fertility and AP-1 biochemistry readouts

Experiment: Reconstitute assembly in vitro from purified components with fluorescently labelled MEA1 and AAGAB and follow single molecules, measuring the order and timing of chaperone release and testing whether tetramer formation requires both hemicomplexes to be pre-loaded. Titrating individual free subunits would show whether any sequential path exists at all.

Hypothesis: AP-1 assembly proceeds by collision of two independently loaded hemicomplexes rather than by sequential subunit addition.

Type: single-molecule in vitro reconstitution

How MEA1 differs from AAGAB (cross-check against genes/human/AAGAB/AAGAB-ai-review.yaml)

The AAGAB review in this repository describes AAGAB as a cytosolic assembly chaperone for
AP-type adaptors that binds already-folded subunits, enforces an ordered assembly pathway,
protects intermediates from degradation, and is released before the tetramer is complete.
Everything in that general description applies equally to MEA1, and the two files should read
consistently on those points. The differences are specific and worth stating precisely:

AAGAB MEA1
Clients within AP-1 γ and σ1 μ1 and β1
Adaptors served AP-1, AP-2, AP-4; specifically not AP-3 AP-1 primarily; limited role in AP-2; not AP-3/AP-4/AP-5
Architecture N-terminal type I pseudoGTPase domain (σ subunits) + C-terminal dimerization domain (γ/α); homodimer that monomerizes on client binding Intrinsically disordered, ~20 kDa, no recognizable fold or sequence homology; NTD binds μ1, CTD binds β1
Hand-off For AP-2, passes the α:σ2 intermediate to CCDC32 No hand-off partner; released by collision with the AAGAB-bound hemicomplex
Disease PPKP1A (punctate palmoplantar keratoderma) none reported

Both are called "bi-handed" in the 2026 paper, meaning each grips two different subunits at once.
The mechanism the paper adds is that the two chaperones work on complementary halves and neither
hands off to the other:
PMID:41559075
PMID:41559075
PMID:41559075

So AAGAB's mechanism as recorded in its review (sequential hand-off to a downstream chaperone,
for AP-2) and MEA1's (collision of two independently loaded hemicomplexes, for AP-1) are
genuinely different solutions, not two descriptions of one thing.

The GO term problem, and why nothing new is proposed here

GO still has no molecular-function term for an assembly chaperone that binds free, folded
subunits of a complex it will not join. The AAGAB review already worked this through and filed
two proposed_new_terms:

  1. AP-type membrane coat adaptor complex subunit binding - binding to a free, unassembled
    subunit of a heterotetrameric AP-type adaptor, as distinct from binding the assembled complex.
  2. protein complex assembly chaperone activity - binding folded subunits to stabilise them
    and impose assembly order, without forming part of the finished complex.

Both cover MEA1 exactly (MEA1's clients are μ1/β1 rather than γ/σ1, which the first term's
wording already anticipates), so this review deliberately does not propose a third term. It
reuses the AAGAB proposals verbatim in substance, with MEA1 evidence attached, so the two files
argue for the same two terms rather than two overlapping sets.

In the meantime the nearest existing MF term is GO:0035650 AP-1 adaptor complex binding, and
it is used here as a flagged stand-in in exactly the way AAGAB's review uses GO:0035612. The
flag matters: GO defines GO:0035650 as binding to the assembled heterotetramer, and MEA1 is
released before the tetramer exists - structural modeling places MEA1 on the γ-binding site of
β1, so MEA1 and γ cannot both be bound. The stand-in names the right complex at the wrong
altitude.

Open questions

  • Is AP-1 assembly ordered or concurrent? MEA1 and AAGAB do not interact, so what determines the
    stoichiometry and timing of the two hemicomplexes, and is there a quality-control step that
    disposes of unpaired MEA1:μ1:β1?
  • Does the limited AP-2 effect reflect genuine redundancy with AAGAB and CCDC32, or a distinct,
    weaker MEA1 activity on β2/μ2?
  • MEA1 is intrinsically disordered with no fold. Does it fold on binding μ1 and β1, and can a
    single MEA1 molecule hold both at once in vivo, as the trimeric AlphaFold model implies?
  • No human disease is yet linked to MEA1, although AAGAB truncations cause PPKP1A and AP-1
    subunit defects cause MEDNIK and related syndromes. Is MEA1 essential, or is there a viable
    hypomorphic range?

core_functions:
- description: >-
    Assembly chaperone for the AP-1 clathrin adaptor complex. MEA1 is an intrinsically
    disordered cytosolic protein with two functionally independent hands: its N-terminal domain
    binds and stabilizes the free mu1 subunit, its C-terminal domain binds and stabilizes the
    free beta1 subunit, and neither domain recognises the other's client. Holding both at once
    it forms a soluble mu1:beta1 hemicomplex that is otherwise degraded, and this hemicomplex is
    completed by collision with the gamma:sigma1 hemicomplex held by AAGAB. The two chaperones
    do not touch each other and both are released as the tetramer closes; MEA1 must leave
    because it sits on the gamma-binding surface of beta1. MEA1 therefore never binds assembled
    AP-1, stays in the cytosol while mature AP-1 goes to membranes, and is not a subunit of the
    adaptor. Its client range is narrow - AP-1 above all, AP-2 weakly, AP-3/AP-4/AP-5 not at
    all. The molecular_function term below is a stand-in: it names the assembled complex, while
    MEA1 in fact binds the free subunits, which is the interaction the mechanism turns on. The
    accurate terms are the two entries under proposed_new_terms.
