Gene Description

Desmin is a type III intermediate-filament protein that forms a mechanically resilient cytoskeletal network in muscle cells. Its filaments connect and support myofibrillar structures around Z discs and at cardiac intercalated discs. Desmin self-assembly and interactions with cytoskeletal partners help organize this network; the small heat-shock protein alpha-B-crystallin binds assembly intermediates and modulates filament organization. Disruptive DES variants can impair filament assembly and produce protein aggregates in cardiac and skeletal muscle.

Structural function and species boundaries

The synthesis is one filament-forming structural activity: desmin self-associates and binds cytoskeletal partners to organize a mechanically connected network around Z discs, intercalated discs and the peripheral sarcolemma. Human cardiac myofilament measurements support its cardiac-myofibril location. These locations are nonexclusive. Purified human DES assembly in PMID 28470624 provides direct support. Its CRYAB experiments support protein-folding chaperone binding by DES: CRYAB is the chaperone, and DES is its binding partner. A text/figure-caption inconsistency prevents assignment of a precise DES binding-site boundary.

The full original of PMID 9736733 uses full-length hamster desmin with a deletion corresponding to the human patient variant. Human MCF7 and monkey COS host cells do not make that construct human. The patient observations and homologous assembly experiments support the structural interpretation with this distinction retained. The full original of PMID 9697706 is a human genetic/segregation report; it is not represented as a direct force, assembly or cardiac regulatory assay.

The originals for PMID 24200904 and PMID 26724190 could not be recovered. Their accessible abstracts report localization or assembly abnormalities, and independent human assembly and ultrastructural evidence support the existing curator assertions.
