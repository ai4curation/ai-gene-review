Gene Description

CACNA1D encodes the pore-forming alpha1D subunit of CaV1.3, an L-type voltage-gated calcium channel. Calcium entry through the channel couples membrane depolarization to electrical and secretory responses. Its relatively negative activation range within the L-type family supports sinoatrial pacemaking and calcium-dependent neurotransmitter release from cochlear inner hair cells, with additional functions in neurons and endocrine cells. Beta and alpha2delta subunits regulate channel expression and gating, and intracellular partners organize local signaling. C-terminal alternative splicing changes activation and calcium-dependent inactivation. Loss-of-function variants can impair hearing and sinoatrial-node function, whereas activating variants can perturb adrenal and neuronal calcium signaling.

CACNA1D functional review

Mechanism and tissue context

CACNA1D encodes the pore-forming alpha1D subunit of CaV1.3. Human channel reconstitution establishes voltage-gated, dihydropyridine-sensitive calcium conductance, with auxiliary-subunit effects that depend on the expression system (PMID:1309651; PMID:11160515). CaV1.3 activates at relatively negative potentials within the L-type family; this does not make it a T-type channel. One channel core combines this molecular activity with calcium import, sinoatrial pacemaker depolarization and auditory neurotransmitter release. In the latter setting the channel supplies presynaptic calcium for synaptic encoding; it is not the mechanically gated sound-transduction channel (PMID:21131953).

Human loss-of-function and gain-of-function observations are distinguished from measurements in native mouse cells and heterologous expression systems. The complete PMID:21131953 abstract describes human SANDD families and nonconducting channels alongside mouse pacemaker and hair-cell mechanism context. Direct human sinoatrial-cell recording was not established by the material accessed.
