# CACNA1D functional review

## Mechanism and tissue context

CACNA1D encodes the pore-forming alpha1D subunit of CaV1.3. Human channel reconstitution establishes voltage-gated, dihydropyridine-sensitive calcium conductance, with auxiliary-subunit effects that depend on the expression system (PMID:1309651; PMID:11160515). CaV1.3 activates at relatively negative potentials within the L-type family; this does not make it a T-type channel. One channel core combines this molecular activity with calcium import, sinoatrial pacemaker depolarization and auditory neurotransmitter release. In the latter setting the channel supplies presynaptic calcium for synaptic encoding; it is not the mechanically gated sound-transduction channel (PMID:21131953).

Human loss-of-function and gain-of-function observations are distinguished from measurements in native mouse cells and heterologous expression systems. The complete PMID:21131953 abstract describes human SANDD families and nonconducting channels alongside mouse pacemaker and hair-cell mechanism context. Direct human sinoatrial-cell recording was not established by the material accessed. Primary variant studies demonstrate altered activation or inactivation, with peak-current effects varying among variants; reduced peak amplitude can coexist with persistent calcium entry (PMID:23913001; PMID:25620733; PMID:28472301). These experiments are not a new clinical disease-validity assessment or treatment recommendation.

C-terminal splicing modulates activation and calcium-dependent inactivation. PMID:18482979 uses a human channel backbone with a rat exon 42A sequence whose encoded amino acids match the human sequence, heterologous recordings, C-terminal fragments and FRET. Tissue expression and human RT-PCR are distinct from those functional assays. The later 43s construct in PMID:28472301 is not automatically identified with named product 42A. The four normal source mappings remain literal: Neuronal-type/Q01668-1; Beta-cell-type/Q01668-2; 4 (Ca(V)1.3(42A))/Q01668-4; and 3/Q01668-3, with their original sequence notes. Additional splice products can exist.

## Binding and localization decisions

Twenty generic interaction assertions were refined to the domain-binding activity actually studied: the SHANK3-1 edge to SH3 domain binding, and 19 fragmentomics edges to PDZ domain binding. The complete SHANK abstract supports binding of Shank SH3 domains to a channel region; the exact human partner is preserved from GOA and UniProt (PMID:30058071). The fragmentomics study tests protein-derived motifs against recombinant human PDZ domains by equilibrium holdup assays (PMID:36115835). The exact curated positive edges, rather than the mere existence of a screen, identify the target interactions. Individual CACNA1D supplementary values, motif sequences, domain ordinals and pair-specific orthogonal validation were not inspected. No numerical affinity, native full-channel assembly or physiological function of each interaction is inferred.

The [official correction to PMID:36115835](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-022-35177-6) was read through the complete indexed publisher text; direct page access failed. It supplies omitted Figure 2 correlation values and axis labels, the omitted leftmost Figure 4a column, and Figure 5 labels/panel ordering. The publisher states that the PDF and HTML were corrected. Corrected images were not inspected. The notice does not state withdrawal of the CACNA1D interactions. This access boundary is also recorded in the reference assessment.

Ankyrin-dependent targeting and alpha-actinin-linked coupling to SK2 remain contextual binding functions rather than separate pore activities. PMID:21859974 includes mouse atrial/fibroblast assays, channel fragments and motif peptides, human atrial lysates and computational modeling. Rat CaV1.3 P27732 in the ISS assertion is an ortholog donor, not an ankyrin binding partner. Motif numbering is not silently transferred to the current human sequence. PMID:17110593 supports alpha-actinin2-mediated channel association and coupled atrial repolarization; it does not assign potassium conductance to CaV1.3.

Six decisions remain UNDECIDED: the precise G-protein-coupled adenylate-cyclase pathway, positive adenylate-cyclase regulation, two Z-disc assertions, a T-tubule assertion and the synaptic-ribbon assertion. The accessible evidence does not resolve those specific donor assays or fine locations. An ontology relation for the channel complex, partner binding or a trafficking phenotype is not substituted for a target localization assay. The official ribbon definition describes the membraneless ribbon structure; nearby presynaptic membrane localization alone does not settle the finer source assertion. These are unresolved claims, not declarations that the original curators were wrong.
