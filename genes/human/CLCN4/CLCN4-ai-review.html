Gene Description

CLCN4 encodes ClC-4, a voltage-dependent chloride:proton exchanger of intracellular membranes. Association with ClC-3 shapes its stability and routes it to endosomal, recycling-endosomal and lysosomal compartments, while unpartnered ClC-4 can accumulate in the endoplasmic reticulum. TMEM9-family partners further couple transporter stability to regulation of transport activity. ClC-4 supports neuronal endomembrane ion homeostasis. Pathogenic CLCN4 variants cause an X-linked neurodevelopmental disorder with intellectual disability and variable epilepsy and behavioral features; their effects on transport depend on voltage, pH and the associated ClC-3 partner.

CLCN4 functional review

Transport mechanism and core function

CLCN4 encodes ClC-4, an electrogenic chloride:proton exchanger. The historical channel labels reflect real CLC-family homology and positive expression-system currents. They are refined to GO:0062158 using later coupled-transport evidence, without deleting the observations or rewriting the original assertion objects. In the original Scheel study, voltage clamp combined with pH measurements distinguishes ClC-4 from channel and untransfected controls and demonstrates proton transport against its gradient. The reported coupling estimate was rough, approximately 1–5 chloride ions per proton, with a ClC-4 estimate of 1.6 ± 0.7; it is not a precise experimental determination of 2:1 stoichiometry.
