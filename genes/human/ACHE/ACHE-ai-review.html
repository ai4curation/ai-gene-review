Gene Description

Acetylcholinesterase is a serine hydrolase that rapidly converts acetylcholine to choline and acetate, terminating cholinergic signaling at neuromuscular and neuronal synapses. Alternative C-terminal isoforms form soluble, GPI-anchored or anchoring-protein-associated assemblies. The synaptic T isoform forms tetramers associated with COLQ or PRIMA1, whereas the H isoform forms GPI-anchored dimers. Acetylcholinesterase also binds extracellular matrix proteins and amyloid-beta and has context-dependent non-synaptic roles. Nuclear accumulation occurs in apoptotic cells; it is not the usual location of synaptic acetylcholine hydrolysis.

Human mutagenesis and curated catalytic statements establish acetylcholine + water → choline + acetate (PMID:1517212, PMID:8349597). Isoform-specific anchoring supports extracellular, basal-lamina and plasma-membrane localization. Nuclear accumulation in apoptotic SK-N-SH cells (PMID:11985878) and Golgi colocalization in osteoblastic cells (PMID:15454088) retain the root organelle claims.
