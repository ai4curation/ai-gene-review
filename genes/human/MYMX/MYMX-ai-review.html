Gene Description

MYMX (myomixer, also called myomerger or Minion) is an 84-amino-acid, muscle-specific microprotein encoded by a small open reading frame that was originally mis-annotated as a long non-coding RNA. It is a single-pass plasma membrane protein: an N-terminal hydrophobic helix anchors it in the bilayer while two short helices of its C-terminal ectodomain face the outside of the cell. MYMX is expressed transiently in differentiating myoblasts and in activated satellite cells, and together with the seven-pass membrane protein myomaker (MYMK) it drives the fusion of mononucleated muscle cells into multinucleated myofibers. The two fusogens act at different steps: MYMK brings the two bilayers to hemifusion, and the MYMX ectodomain then stresses the outer leaflet of the apposed membranes - shifting its spontaneous curvature to positive values in a phosphatidylserine-dependent manner - so that the hemifusion diaphragm resolves into an expanding fusion pore. MYMX activity is required on only one of the two fusing cells and can operate without a physical MYMK interaction. Loss of the protein leaves myoblasts able to differentiate and assemble sarcomeres but unable to fuse, so mouse and zebrafish nulls lack multinucleated myofibers, and conditional deletion in adult satellite cells prevents skeletal muscle regeneration after injury. In humans, homozygosity for a truncating variant that removes the ectodomain causes an autosomal recessive congenital myopathy resembling Carey-Fineman-Ziter syndrome. MYMX arose de novo in early vertebrates and, despite weak sequence conservation, retains fusogenic function across fish, reptiles and mammals.

Core Functions

MYMX is the pore-forming half of the vertebrate myoblast fusogen pair. Anchored in the plasma membrane by its N-terminal helix, its two extracellular helices insert into the outer leaflet of apposed membranes and shift its spontaneous curvature to positive values, creating elastic stress that resolves the hemifusion diaphragm made by MYMK into an expanding fusion pore. Helix insertion is licensed by externalised phosphatidylserine, which orders the membrane-proximal helix and restricts the destabilising activity to sites and times of fusion. The activity works from the cell surface, is needed on only one of the two fusing cells, does not require a physical MYMK interaction, and is sufficient on its own to complete fusion initiated by an unrelated fusogen.

Suggested Questions for Experts

Q: Should GO acquire a child of GO:0140522 fusogenic activity for proteins that execute only the hemifusion-to-pore transition rather than the whole bilayer merger? MYMX cannot fuse membranes by itself in the absence of a hemifusion intermediate, so the parent term arguably overstates its activity, while no existing child matches its mechanism.

Suggested experts: Douglas P Millay, Leonid V Chernomordik

Q: Should the MYMX molecular function be modelled as membrane-curvature generation rather than as fusogenic activity? The measured biophysical property is a positive shift in outer-leaflet spontaneous curvature, which GO currently has no molecular-function term for.

Suggested experts: Michael M Kozlov, Leonid V Chernomordik

Q: Does human MYMX have a role in adult muscle regeneration comparable to the mouse satellite-cell requirement? All regeneration annotations on the human gene are transferred from mouse; the human patients described so far present with a congenital, developmental phenotype.

Suggested experts: Eric N Olson, Douglas P Millay

Q: Is MYMX present in the ER and Golgi beyond biosynthetic transit, and is any of its activity exerted there? The ER and Golgi annotations derive from a membrane fraction that pooled all three compartments, and no imaging has resolved them.

  • Single-pass membrane protein; N-terminal hydrophobic helix anchors in plasma membrane, C-terminal region extracellular (ectodomain) PMID:30197239.

Mechanism (the MF question)

  • Myomaker (MYMK, 7-TM) drives hemifusion; MYMX drives pore formation PMID:30197239.
  • MYMX works independently of MYMK in a heterologous HA-driven hemifusion system PMID:30197239.
  • Recombinant ectodomain (aa 26-84) rescues fusion of Mymx-null myoblasts in trans PMID:30197239.
  • Biophysics: ectodomain induces positive spontaneous curvature in the outer leaflet PMID:33479215; two ectodomain helices regulated by externalized PS PMID:36095199.
  • Required on only one of the two fusing cells PMID:35642635.
  • Co-IP with Myomaker PMID:28386024 but interaction not required for function PMID:30197239. So "protein binding"/MYMK binding is not the core MF.
  • MF term: GO:0140522 fusogenic activity ("The activity of joining two lipid bilayers to form a single membrane.") is the closest existing term. None of its children fit (GTPase-dependent, refolding-mediated, helical-bundle zippering). GO:0140912 membrane destabilizing activity is defined around permeabilisation/lysis — not a good fit.
  • Participation test: MYMX itself does the work of the pore-opening step (its ectodomain stresses the outer leaflet); it is not a substrate. Passes.
  • Comparator check (QuickGO 2026-09-30): GO:0140522 has very few experimental annotations (e.g. Chlamydomonas HAP2 IDA). Mouse Mymx (Q2Q5T5) and Mymk carry no MF other than protein binding (IPI) — i.e. no MF at all has been assigned; the absence reflects the recency of the fusogen MF branch rather than a deliberate convention excluding MYMX. Proposed as NEW with caution; flagged as a question for experts whether a partial (pore-completing) fusogen should carry the parent term.

description: >-
  MYMX (myomixer, also called myomerger or Minion) is an 84-amino-acid, muscle-specific
  microprotein encoded by a small open reading frame that was originally mis-annotated as a
  long non-coding RNA. It is a single-pass plasma membrane protein: an N-terminal hydrophobic
  helix anchors it in the bilayer while two short helices of its C-terminal ectodomain face the
  outside of the cell. MYMX is expressed transiently in differentiating myoblasts and in
  activated satellite cells, and together with the seven-pass membrane protein myomaker (MYMK)
  it drives the fusion of mononucleated muscle cells into multinucleated myofibers. The two
  fusogens act at different steps: MYMK brings the two bilayers to hemifusion, and the MYMX
  ectodomain then stresses the outer leaflet of the apposed membranes - shifting its spontaneous
  curvature to positive values in a phosphatidylserine-dependent manner - so that the hemifusion
  diaphragm resolves into an expanding fusion pore. MYMX activity is required on only one of the
  two fusing cells and can operate without a physical MYMK interaction. Loss of the protein
  leaves myoblasts able to differentiate and assemble sarcomeres but unable to fuse, so mouse and
  zebrafish nulls lack multinucleated myofibers, and conditional deletion in adult satellite
  cells prevents skeletal muscle regeneration after injury. In humans, homozygosity for a
  truncating variant that removes the ectodomain causes an autosomal recessive congenital myopathy
  resembling Carey-Fineman-Ziter syndrome. MYMX arose de novo in early vertebrates and, despite
  weak sequence conservation, retains fusogenic function across fish, reptiles and mammals.

- description: >-
    MYMX is the pore-forming half of the vertebrate myoblast fusogen pair. Anchored in the
    plasma membrane by its N-terminal helix, its two extracellular helices insert into the outer
    leaflet of apposed membranes and shift its spontaneous curvature to positive values, creating
    elastic stress that resolves the hemifusion diaphragm made by MYMK into an expanding fusion
    pore. Helix insertion is licensed by externalised phosphatidylserine, which orders the
    membrane-proximal helix and restricts the destabilising activity to sites and times of fusion.
    The activity works from the cell surface, is needed on only one of the two fusing cells, does
    not require a physical MYMK interaction, and is sufficient on its own to complete fusion
    initiated by an unrelated fusogen.
