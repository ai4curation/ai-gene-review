CC   -!- FUNCTION: Mediates the transport of prostaglandins (PGs, mainly PGE2,
CC       PGE1, PGE3, PGF2alpha, PGD2, PGH2) and thromboxanes (thromboxane B2)
CC       across the cell membrane (PubMed:11997326, PubMed:26692285,
CC       PubMed:8787677). PGs and thromboxanes play fundamental roles in diverse
CC       functions such as intraocular pressure, gastric acid secretion, renal
CC       salt and water transport, vascular tone, and fever (PubMed:15044627).
CC       Plays a role in the clearance of PGs from the circulation through
CC       cellular uptake, which allows cytoplasmic oxidation and PG signal
CC       termination (PubMed:8787677). PG uptake is dependent upon membrane
CC       potential and involves exchange of a monovalent anionic substrate (PGs
CC       exist physiologically as an anionic monovalent form) with a
CC       stoichiometry of 1:1 for divalent anions or of 1:2 for monovalent
CC       anions (PubMed:29204966). Uses lactate, generated by glycolysis, as a
CC       counter-substrate to mediate PGE2 influx and efflux (PubMed:11997326).
CC       Under nonglycolytic conditions, metabolites other than lactate might
CC       serve as counter-substrates (PubMed:11997326). Although the mechanism
CC       is not clear, this transporter can function in bidirectional mode
CC       (PubMed:29204966). When apically expressed in epithelial cells, it
CC       facilitates transcellular transport (also called vectorial release),
CC       extracting PG from the apical medium and facilitating transport across
CC       the cell toward the basolateral side, whereupon the PG exits the cell
CC       by simple diffusion (By similarity). In the renal collecting duct,
CC       regulates renal Na+ balance by removing PGE2 from apical medium (PGE2
CC       EP4 receptor is likely localized to the luminal/apical membrane and
CC       stimulates Na+ resorption) and transporting it toward the basolateral
CC       membrane (where PGE2 EP1 and EP3 receptors inhibit Na+ resorption) (By
CC       similarity). Plays a role in endometrium during decidualization,
CC       increasing uptake of PGs by decidual cells (PubMed:16339169). Involved
CC       in critical events for ovulation (PubMed:27169804). Regulates
CC       extracellular PGE2 concentration for follicular development in the
CC       ovaries (By similarity). Expressed intracellularly, may contribute to
CC       vesicular uptake of newly synthesized intracellular PGs, thereby
CC       facilitating exocytotic secretion of PGs without being metabolized (By
CC       similarity). Essential core component of the major type of large-
CC       conductance anion channel, Maxi-Cl, which plays essential roles in
CC       inorganic anion transport, cell volume regulation and release of ATP
CC       and glutamate not only in physiological processes but also in
CC       pathological processes (By similarity). May contribute to regulate the
CC       transport of organic compounds in testis across the blood-testis-
CC       barrier (Probable). {ECO:0000250|UniProtKB:Q00910,
CC       ECO:0000250|UniProtKB:Q9EPT5, ECO:0000269|PubMed:11997326,

CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:27169804, ECO:0000269|PubMed:8787677,

CC   -!- DISEASE: PHOAR2-enteropathy syndrome (PHOAR2E) [MIM:614441]: An
CC       autosomal recessive disease characterized by primary hypertrophic
CC       osteoarthropathy and/or chronic non-specific ulcers of the small
CC       intestine. Affected individuals present with digital clubbing,
CC       periostosis, acroosteolysis, painful joint enlargement, and variable
CC       features of pachydermia that include thickened facial skin and a
CC       thickened scalp. Chronic ulcers of the small intestine result in
CC       abdominal pain and watery diarrhea, and are associated with chronic
CC       anemia. Other developmental anomalies include delayed closure of the
CC       cranial sutures and congenital heart disease.
CC       {ECO:0000269|PubMed:22197487, ECO:0000269|PubMed:22331663,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:22553128, ECO:0000269|PubMed:22696055,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:23509104, ECO:0000269|PubMed:33852188}. Note=The
CC       disease is caused by variants affecting the gene represented in this
CC       entry.
CC   -!- DISEASE: Hypertrophic osteoarthropathy, primary, autosomal dominant
CC       (PHOAD) [MIM:167100]: A form of primary hypertrophic osteoarthropathy,
CC       a disease characterized by digital clubbing, periostosis,
CC       acroosteolysis, painful joint enlargement, and variable features of
CC       pachydermia that include thickened facial skin and a thickened scalp.
CC       PHOAD patients may also experience joint swelling and pain, and some
CC       have reported gastrointestinal symptoms, including watery diarrhea.
CC       Males are more commonly affected, and more severely affected, than
CC       females. {ECO:0000269|PubMed:23509104, ECO:0000269|PubMed:33852188}.
CC       Note=The disease is caused by variants affecting the gene represented
CC       in this entry.
