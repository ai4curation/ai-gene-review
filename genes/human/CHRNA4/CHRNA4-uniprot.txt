CC   -!- FUNCTION: Component of neuronal acetylcholine receptors (nAChRs) that
CC       function as pentameric, ligand-gated cation channels with high calcium
CC       permeability among other activities. nAChRs are excitatory
CC       neurotrasnmitter receptors formed by a collection of nAChR subunits
CC       known to mediate synaptic transmission in the nervous system and the
CC       neuromuscular junction. Each nAchR subunit confers differential
CC       attributes to channel properties, including activation, deactivation
CC       and desensitization kinetics, pH sensitivity, cation permeability, and
CC       binding to allosteric modulators (PubMed:22361591, PubMed:27698419,
CC       PubMed:29720657, PubMed:38454578). CHRNA4 forms heteropentameric
CC       neuronal acetylcholine receptors with CHRNB2 and CHRNB4, as well as
CC       CHRNA5 and CHRNB3 as accesory subunits. Is the most abundant nAChR
CC       subtype expressed in the central nervous system (PubMed:16835356,
CC       PubMed:22361591, PubMed:27698419, PubMed:29720657, PubMed:38454578).
CC       Found in two major stoichiometric forms,(CHRNA4)3:(CHRNB2)2 and
CC       (CHRNA4)2:(CHRNB2)3, the two stoichiometric forms differ in their
CC       unitary conductance, calcium permeability, ACh sensitivity and
CC       potentiation by divalent cation (PubMed:27698419, PubMed:29720657,
CC       PubMed:38454578). Involved in the modulation of calcium-dependent
CC       signaling pathways, influences the release of neurotransmitters,
CC       including dopamine, glutamate and GABA (By similarity).

CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:22361591, ECO:0000269|PubMed:27698419,

CC       receptor is achieved by calcium association with topographically
CC       distinct sites framed by anionic residues within the CHRNA4 subunit and

CC       activity is inhibited by the antagonist alpha-conotoxins BuIA, PnIA,
CC       GID and MII, small disulfide-constrained peptides from cone snails (By

CC   -!- SUBUNIT: Neuronal AChR is composed of two different types of subunits:
CC       alpha and beta (PubMed:22361591). CHRNA4 forms heteropentameric
CC       neuronal acetylcholine receptors with CHRNB2 and CHRNB4, as well as
CC       CHRNA5 and CHRNB3 as accesory subunits (PubMed:20881005,
CC       PubMed:22361591, PubMed:27698419, PubMed:29720657, PubMed:38454578).
CC       Found in two major stoichiometric forms, LS (low agonist sensitivity):
CC       (CHRNA4)3:(CHRNB2)2 and HS (high agonist sensitivity):
CC       (CHRNA4)2:(CHRNB2)3, the two stoichiometric forms differ in their
CC       unitary conductance, calcium permeability, ACh sensitivity and
CC       potentiation by divalent cation (PubMed:12527804, PubMed:17132685,
CC       PubMed:22361591, PubMed:27698419, PubMed:29720657, PubMed:38454578).
CC       Cells produce predominantly an (CHRNA4)3:(CHRNB2)2 nAChR. The
CC       (CHRNA4)2:(CHRNB2)3 expression is selectively up-regulated by nicotine
CC       and has lower single channel conductance and calcium permeability
CC       (PubMed:17132685). In the striatum, also forms CHRNA4:CHRNA6:CHRNB2
CC       complexes (By similarity). Also found in the stoichiometric form:
CC       (CHRNA4:CHRNB2)2:CHRNB3 (PubMed:16835356). Interacts with RIC3; which
