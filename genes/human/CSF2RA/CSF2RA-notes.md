# CSF2RA evidence and review notes

## 2026-10-13: receptor function and source reconciliation

CSF2RA is the ligand-recognizing alpha chain of the GM-CSF receptor. Human alpha alone binds GM-CSF; alpha and the common beta chain reconstitute high-affinity binding. Alpha-interface mutations also impair productive signaling, so its role includes ligand recognition and receptor assembly rather than intrinsic kinase catalysis. The compact core combines GO:0004901, GO:0038157, plasma membrane and the GM-CSF receptor complex. No new assertion is proposed. [PMID:2555171](https://doi.org/10.1002/j.1460-2075.1989.tb08541.x), “specificity for human GM-CSF”; [PMID:1702217](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.87.24.9655); [PMID:27396825](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.str.2016.05.017), “interactions playing a major role in receptor signaling”.

The two early originals were recovered as authentic page scans and read by the independent core reviewer. The owner independently read the 2016 indexed Results/Methods and supplemental construct, binding and proliferation methods. The engineered crystallographic ectodomain (mature L1–G301 with three glycosylation-site mutations, expressed in Sf21) is not an official soluble splice product. The full ternary alpha arrangement combines resolved coordinates and modeling; it does not establish one obligatory native oligomeric state. For PMID:18692472 the main article remained unavailable, but authentic publisher supplemental Methods, captions, tables and the independent reviewer's S5/S6 image inspection establish JAK2/STAT5 assay context and receptor-dependent colony assays. JAK2 association with beta is distinct from intrinsic alpha kinase activity. The original abstract-only repository caches remain unchanged. [2008 supplemental data](https://ars.els-cdn.com/content/image/1-s2.0-S0092867408008131-mmc1.pdf), Methods and Figures S5–S6; [2016 supplemental information](https://ars.els-cdn.com/content/image/1-s2.0-S0969212616301241-mmc1.pdf), protein expression, ligand binding and proliferation methods.

## Reviewer follow-up, 2026-10-13

PR #4980 review items 1–2 prompted a distinction between direct alpha-chain cytokine binding and its contribution to cooperative GM-CSF receptor activity. The existing human receptor expression, binding and mutagenesis evidence (PMID:2555171, PMID:1702217, PMID:27396825) supports both ligand recognition and productive receptor assembly. The alpha chain is not merely passive, and neither chain is assigned intrinsic kinase activity. The core uses cytokine binding plus contributes_to receptor activity; the external side of plasma membrane specifies the ligand-recognition site. Source qualifiers remain unchanged.

The cytosol IBA remains UNDECIDED. Its on-path IBD is real, but an intracellular pool is not evidence of activity there. The unresolved issue is the CSF2RA product and site of activity, not just access to the bovine GHR paper. The newly cached and PubMed-checked PMID:16174355 is donor context, with full text unavailable; ligand divergence alone does not prove a compartment was lost.

The peptide-hormone-binding and broad proliferation/location refinements reconcile individual source assertions to already retained terms; they add no NEW rows. Reviewer-access flags and cache-coverage flags describe different things. The existing source index includes original scan URLs and hashes for all five pages of each isoform paper and the independent primary-read records for the other originals; the reference notes now link those sections explicitly. A suggested question records the plain GM-CSF-binding ontology gap without inventing an identifier.
