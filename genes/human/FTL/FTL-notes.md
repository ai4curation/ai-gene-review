# FTL biological notes

FTL encodes the 175-residue human ferritin light chain. Its principal role is within the cytosolic ferritin shell, where L-chain composition supports mineral formation and controls iron availability. Recombinant human L-chain homopolymers incorporate iron, and experiments with mixed H/L shells distinguish shell effects from H-chain catalytic oxidation (PMID:19923220; PMID:20159981). Human L-chain-deficient fibroblasts can sequester more iron in H homopolymers while their labile iron pool falls; wild-type L-chain transduction partly restores availability (PMID:23940258). Thus FTL loss need not simply reduce total iron storage.

Identical-subunit binding is a characteristic shell-assembly property, supported by recombinant L-chain structures and positive wild-type self-association (PMID:16790936; PMID:25910212). Interaction screens also recover FTH1 and contextual partners. Human proteins in yeast, tagged proteins in mammalian cells, co-complex AP-MS and purified assemblies answer different questions. A screen-wide validation rate does not certify an individual FTL pair. The exact 2015 Table S3A distinguishes positive wild-type FTL–FTH1/self interactions from A96T/H133P loss; its GPCA table contains no FTL pair. The 2014 and 2019 original variant calls differ and remain attributed to their own assays (PMID:25502805; PMID:31515488).

Ferritin containing FTL is cargo during receptor-dependent turnover. NCOA4-associated recovery, lysosomal delivery and cargo-composition knockout/rescue do not alone show that FTL performs an autophagy step (PMID:24695223; PMID:25327288). The 2024 original actually tests human wild-type FTL and engineered L2H; it distinguishes productive FTH1 receptor contact from the properties of wild-type L chain (PMID:38714719). A separately encountered 2019 indexed original excerpt reports FTL knockout/rescue effects on degradation and colocalization; this necessity evidence is retained as a caveat, without claiming that its full Methods were read or that cargo necessity resolves participation (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6597882/).

Cytosol and ferritin-complex membership describe the principal storage setting. Granule/extracellular and autophagic compartments are contextual locations. Human urinary exosome proteomics directly identifies FTL with the original urine scope (PMID:19056867); another urine-fraction study has accessible main text but an unrecovered exact FTL supplementary row (PMID:23533145). The membrane-proteomics claim remains unresolved without its exact target row (PMID:19946888). The inspected erythropoiesis study perturbs mouse NCOA4 and iron supply, leaving FTL-specific differentiation unresolved (PMID:26776506).
