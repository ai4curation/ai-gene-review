| Identity and architecture | The target is human **CDC20** (UniProt **Q12834**; cell division cycle protein 20 homolog), distinct from **CDC20B** and the related APC/C coactivator **FZR1/CDH1**. | Human CDC20 has an N-terminal regulatory region (approximately residues 1–173) containing BM1, KILR, C-box, KEN-box and CRY-box elements, followed by a C-terminal WD40 β-propeller (approximately residues 174–470), matching the CDC20/Fizzy WD-repeat family. | Strong: curated human target identity plus human-cell structural–functional mapping (pqac-00000000, pqac-00000009) |
| Primary molecular function | CDC20 is a **nonenzymatic coactivator and substrate receptor** for the anaphase-promoting complex/cyclosome (**APC/C**); APC/C—not CDC20—is the E3 ubiquitin ligase. | CDC20’s C-box and C-terminal IR motif engage/activate APC/C, while its WD40 propeller recruits selected substrates. APC/C uses UBE2C/UBE2D to initiate ubiquitination and UBE2S to extend predominantly K11-linked chains. | Strong: structural, reconstituted biochemical and review-level synthesis (pqac-00000004, pqac-00000021, pqac-00000025) |
| Substrate recognition | CDC20 recognizes short APC/C degrons, with combinatorial degron content and accessibility determining substrate timing and affinity. | Distinct WD40 surfaces bind the **D-box** (typically RxxLxxxxN) and **KEN-box** (KENxxxN); the **ABBA motif** (Fx[ILV][FY]x[DE]) supplies an additional CDC20-binding interaction. | Strong: human structural evidence and biochemical degron studies (pqac-00000002, pqac-00000003, pqac-00000004) |
| Securin and cyclin B outcomes | APC/C–CDC20 polyubiquitinates **securin** and **cyclin B1** at the metaphase–anaphase transition, targeting them for proteasomal degradation. | Securin loss releases separase to cleave cohesin and separate sister chromatids; cyclin B1 loss lowers CDK1 activity and drives mitotic exit. CDC20 is subsequently replaced functionally by CDH1 during late mitosis/G1. | Strong: established biochemical and cell-cycle mechanism (pqac-00000021, pqac-00000023, pqac-00000024) |
| Spindle-assembly checkpoint | CDC20 has a dual role: it activates APC/C after correct chromosome attachment but is incorporated into the inhibitory **mitotic checkpoint complex (MCC)** while unattached or improperly attached kinetochores persist. | MCC comprises CDC20, MAD2, BUBR1 and BUB3. MCC-bound CDC20 and an APC/C-associated second CDC20 generate an inhibited APC/C–MCC assembly, preventing premature securin/cyclin-B destruction. | Strong: structural, biochemical and genetic evidence (pqac-00000002, pqac-00000007, pqac-00000034) |
| Localization and spatial action | CDC20 acts in the mitotic compartment where it engages kinetochores, checkpoint proteins and APC/C; a simple fixed “nuclear” or “cytoplasmic” localization is inadequate because nuclear-envelope breakdown reorganizes this space. | Human-cell immunofluorescence found kinetochore localization of tested CDC20 constructs except a mutant lacking the ABBA-binding region. HeLa-cell proximity assays detected cell-cycle-dependent APC/C engagement, with APC8–CDC20 proximity peaking in prometaphase and APC3–CDC20 proximity in metaphase. Direct evidence for stable human CDC20 localization along spindle microtubules is less secure. | Moderate-to-strong for kinetochore/APC-C engagement; limited for broad spindle, nuclear or cytosolic assignments (pqac-00000010, pqac-00000030, pqac-00000036) |
| 2024 CRY-box advance | A 2024 human-cell study showed that the conserved **CRY box** is not merely a CDC20 degradation signal: it is essential for robust spindle-checkpoint signaling. | CRISPR-engineered, RNAi-sensitive HeLa cells complemented with CDC20 mutants showed that the CRY box promotes MCC formation and stabilizes MCC association with APC/C; its impairment weakened checkpoint-dependent inhibition. | Strong recent mechanistic evidence: genetic complementation, live-cell phenotyping, co-immunoprecipitation and reconstituted ubiquitination assays (pqac-00000001, pqac-00000009, pqac-00000012) |
| Disease and translation | Biallelic CDC20 dysfunction is associated with oocyte-maturation defects/female infertility, whereas elevated CDC20 expression is repeatedly associated with proliferative cancers and adverse features. These findings support biomarker and therapeutic-target research but do not establish CDC20 overexpression as an independent driver in every tumor type. | 2024 studies linked CDC20 to breast- and lung-cancer outcomes and reported growth inhibition after knockdown; a bladder cohort found higher expression in high-grade tumors but no significant overall-survival association. Small molecules such as Apcin or proTAME and CDC20 knockdown remain preclinical strategies. **No approved CDC20-directed therapy or relevant CDC20-directed clinical trial was identified.** | Human genetic evidence for infertility; mostly retrospective/bioinformatic cancer associations plus cell-line/xenograft experiments for oncology translation (pqac-00000000, pqac-00000013, pqac-00000015, pqac-00000017) |
