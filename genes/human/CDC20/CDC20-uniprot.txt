CC   -!- FUNCTION: Substrate-specific adapter and activator of the anaphase
CC       promoting complex/cyclosome (APC/C) that confers substrate specificity
CC       by binding to substrates and targeting them to the APC/C for
CC       ubiquitination and degradation (PubMed:9734353, PubMed:27030811,
CC       PubMed:29343641, PubMed:27120157, PubMed:27509861). Essential for
CC       activating APC/C in the metaphase/anaphase transition of the cell
CC       cycle, targeting the degradation of cyclin B and securin
CC       (PubMed:27120157, PubMed:27509861, PubMed:32666501). Recognizes and
CC       binds the destruction box (D box), KEB box and ABBA motifs on protein
CC       substrates (PubMed:29343641, PubMed:27509861). The CDC20-APC/C complex
CC       positively regulates the formation of synaptic vesicle clustering at
CC       active zone to the presynaptic membrane in postmitotic neurons (By
CC       similarity). CDC20-APC/C-induced degradation of NEUROD2 induces
CC       presynaptic differentiation (By similarity). The CDC20-APC/C complex
CC       promotes proper dilation formation and radial migration by degrading
CC       CCDC41 (By similarity). Component of the mitotic checkpoint complex
CC       (MCC), which inhibits APC/C and delays chromosome segregation until all
CC       chromosomes are properly attached to the mitotic spindle
CC       (PubMed:27509861). The MCC, which consists of CDC20, MAD2L1/MAD2,
CC       BUB1B/BUBR1 and BUB3, is a key player of the spindle assembly
CC       checkpoint (PubMed:27509861). The MCC-bound APC/C complex is inactive
CC       and this delays the metaphase/anaphase transition (PubMed:9811605,
CC       PubMed:9637688, PubMed:27509861). When the spindle assembly checkpoint
CC       is silenced, the MCC-APC/C complex recruits the E2 enzyme UBCH10 and
CC       triggers the auto-ubiquitination of CDC20 in the MCC, thereby
CC       reactivating APC/C for transition into anaphase (PubMed:27509861).
CC       {ECO:0000250|UniProtKB:Q9JJ66, ECO:0000269|PubMed:27030811,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:27120157, ECO:0000269|PubMed:27509861,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:29343641, ECO:0000269|PubMed:32666501,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:9637688, ECO:0000269|PubMed:9734353,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:9811605}.
CC   -!- PATHWAY: Protein modification; protein ubiquitination.

CC   -!- SUBUNIT: Associates with phosphorylated anaphase promoting
CC       complex/cyclosome (APC/C) to form the CDC20-APC/C complex, which is
CC       crucial for metaphase/anaphase transition in cell cycle; this
CC       interaction is dependent on APC/C phosphorylation (PubMed:27120157,
CC       PubMed:27509861). Within the CDC20-APC/C complex, interacts (via KILR
CC       motif and C-box motif) with CDC23/ANAPC8 and (via IR tail) CDC27/ANAPC3
CC       (PubMed:27120157, PubMed:27509861). The phosphorylated form interacts
CC       with APC/C (PubMed:9637688, PubMed:9734353, PubMed:9811605). Component
CC       of the mitotic checkpoint complex (MCC), composed of CDC20,
CC       MAD2L1/MAD2, BUB1B/BUBR1 and BUB3; within the complex interacts with
CC       BUB1B/BUBR1 and MAD2L1/MAD2 in its closed conformation
CC       (PubMed:15525512, PubMed:18997788, PubMed:19098431, PubMed:33094908,
CC       PubMed:27509861). The MCC associates with CDC20-APC/C to form the
CC       CDC20-APC/C-MCC complex; within the complex BUB1B/BUBR1 interacts with
CC       both the CDC20 in MCC and the CDC20 in CDC20-APC/C, stabilizing the
CC       interaction between the two complexes (PubMed:15525512,
CC       PubMed:18997788, PubMed:19098431, PubMed:33094908, PubMed:27509861).
CC       Component of a complex with CDC20, CDC27, SPATC1 and TUBG1 (By
CC       similarity). Interacts with NEUROD2 (By similarity). Interacts with
CC       dimeric MAD2L1 in its closed conformation form (PubMed:15525512,
CC       PubMed:19098431, PubMed:29162720, PubMed:9637688, PubMed:9811605).

CC       with SIRT2 (PubMed:22014574). Interacts with isoform 1 of NEK2

CC       interaction occurs in mitosis in a MAD2L1-dependent manner and prevents
CC       PLK1-stimulated degradation of HSF1 by blocking the recruitment of the
CC       SCF(BTRC) ubiquitin ligase complex (PubMed:18794143). Interacts (via
