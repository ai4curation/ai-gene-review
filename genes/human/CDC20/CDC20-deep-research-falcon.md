# Human CDC20 (UniProt Q12834): Functional-Annotation Research Report

## Executive summary

The requested target is unambiguously **human CDC20**, UniProt **Q12834**, also called cell division cycle protein 20 homolog or p55CDC. It is distinct from **CDC20B** and from the related APC/C coactivator **FZR1/CDH1**. Open Targets maps human CDC20 to ENSG00000117399, while separately identifying CDC20B and FZR1, resolving the main symbol-confusion risk (OpenTargets Search: -CDC20).

CDC20 is **not an enzyme by itself**. Its primary function is to act as a WD40-repeat substrate receptor and allosteric coactivator for the anaphase-promoting complex/cyclosome, **APC/C**, a multisubunit RING-type E3 ubiquitin ligase. APC/C–CDC20 recognizes mitotic proteins bearing D-box, KEN-box, and related degrons and directs their polyubiquitylation. Destruction of securin activates separase and cohesin cleavage; destruction of cyclin B1 lowers CDK1 activity and enables mitotic exit. Before all chromosomes are correctly attached to spindle microtubules, the spindle-assembly checkpoint instead incorporates CDC20 into the inhibitory MAD2–BUBR1–BUB3 mitotic checkpoint complex (MCC). Thus, CDC20 is the molecular switch coupling chromosome attachment to irreversible anaphase onset (yamano2019apcccurrentunderstanding pages 3-5, liu2020theinteractionprofile pages 1-8, liu2020theinteractionprofile pages 12-16).

## 1. Identity verification and domain architecture

### 1.1 Verification

The supplied identity is internally and externally consistent:

- **Gene/protein:** CDC20; cell division cycle protein 20 homolog/p55CDC.
- **Organism:** *Homo sapiens*.
- **UniProt accession:** Q12834.
- **Family:** CDC20/Fizzy WD-repeat family.
- **Architecture:** an N-terminal regulatory segment and a C-terminal WD40 β-propeller.

Recent human-cell work maps the regulatory N terminus approximately to residues **1–173**, containing BM1, KILR, C-box, KEN-box, and CRY-box elements, and the WD40 β-propeller to approximately residues **174–470**. The propeller contains substrate-degron-binding surfaces and APC/C-recognition determinants, directly aligning with the supplied InterPro WD40 and CDC20/Fizzy annotations (zhang2024functionalanalysisof pages 1-2).

This report does **not** conflate CDC20 with CDC20B or FZR1/CDH1. FZR1 is a related WD40 APC/C coactivator that predominates later in mitosis and G1, whereas CDC20 controls early mitotic APC/C activity and is the direct target of the spindle checkpoint (OpenTargets Search: -CDC20, yamano2019apcccurrentunderstanding pages 3-5).

## 2. Primary molecular function

### 2.1 CDC20 is an adaptor and coactivator, not a catalytic enzyme

APC/C contains the catalytic APC2–APC11 cullin–RING module. ATP-dependent E1 first activates ubiquitin; an E2 carries activated ubiquitin; APC/C positions E2~ubiquitin and the recruited substrate for ubiquitin transfer. CDC20 supplies substrate recognition and helps convert APC/C into its active mitotic conformation—it does not itself catalyze a standalone reaction (yamano2019apcccurrentunderstanding pages 3-5, liu2020theinteractionprofile pages 12-16, yamano2019apcccurrentunderstanding pages 1-3).

The principal biochemical reaction can therefore be written conceptually as:

**APC/C–CDC20 substrate + E2~ubiquitin → polyubiquitylated substrate → 26S-proteasomal degradation.**

UBE2C/UbcH10 or UBE2D/UbcH5 initiates substrate ubiquitylation, whereas UBE2S extends predominantly K11-linked chains; mixed or branched K11/K48 chains can also occur. CDC20's N-terminal C-box and C-terminal IR-tail help dock and activate APC/C, while its WD40 propeller recognizes substrate degrons (yamano2019apcccurrentunderstanding pages 3-5, yamano2019apcccurrentunderstanding pages 5-6).

### 2.2 Substrate specificity

CDC20 recognizes combinations of short linear degradation motifs rather than one chemically defined small-molecule substrate:

- **D-box:** commonly summarized as RxxLxxxxN.
- **KEN-box:** KENxxxN consensus in the cited synthesis.
- **ABBA motif:** Fx[ILV][FY]x[DE].

D-box and KEN-box motifs engage distinct surfaces of the CDC20 WD40 propeller. Motif multiplicity, affinity, accessibility, nearby phosphorylation, APC10 participation, and competition by checkpoint proteins determine whether and when a substrate is ubiquitylated. Human structural work supports multisite degron recognition rather than a single lock-and-key site (cosma2022biochemicalbiophysicaland pages 14-15, cosma2022biochemicalbiophysicaland pages 13-14, yamano2019apcccurrentunderstanding pages 5-6).

### 2.3 Principal physiological substrates and consequences

The decisive metaphase–anaphase substrates are:

1. **Securin/PTTG1.** Its degradation releases separase, which cleaves cohesin and permits sister-chromatid separation.
2. **Cyclin B1/CCNB1.** Its degradation inactivates cyclin-B–CDK1, allowing mitotic exit.

Earlier APC/C–CDC20 substrates include cyclin A and Nek2A, whose destruction can proceed in prometaphase under regulatory rules that differ from the checkpoint-sensitive destruction of securin and cyclin B1. CDC20 dominates from prometaphase through metaphase/anaphase; CDH1/FZR1 assumes broader APC/C substrate control in late anaphase and G1 (yamano2019apcccurrentunderstanding pages 3-5, liu2020theinteractionprofile pages 44-47, liu2020theinteractionprofile pages 1-8).

## 3. Spindle-assembly-checkpoint pathway

CDC20 has an unusual dual role. When chromosomes have achieved correct bipolar attachment, CDC20 activates APC/C. When an unattached or improperly attached kinetochore persists, checkpoint signaling generates the **MCC**, comprising CDC20, MAD2, BUBR1, and BUB3. The MCC binds APC/C–CDC20 and blocks productive degron recognition and ligase activation. Structural and biochemical models contain two CDC20 molecules: one associated with APC/C and another incorporated into MCC. This arrangement prevents premature securin and cyclin-B destruction (cosma2022biochemicalbiophysicaland pages 8-10).

Mitotic CDK1–cyclin-B–Cks phosphorylation of APC/C subunits facilitates CDC20 recruitment, but checkpoint-generated MCC restrains the resulting ligase until attachment is satisfactory. Checkpoint silencing then permits APC/C–CDC20 to cross the irreversible anaphase threshold. This explains why CDC20 depletion or failure to engage APC/C produces metaphase arrest, whereas defective MCC engagement can cause premature anaphase and chromosome mis-segregation (yamano2019apcccurrentunderstanding pages 5-6, zhang2024functionalanalysisof pages 1-2).

## 4. Cellular localization and site of action

CDC20 carries out its canonical function in the **mitotic chromosome–spindle/APC/C compartment**, particularly at or downstream of unattached kinetochores and on soluble or spatially organized APC/C assemblies after nuclear-envelope breakdown. A simple assignment as exclusively “nuclear” or “cytoplasmic” is misleading because mitosis dismantles the nuclear envelope and redistributes these components.

Direct human-cell evidence is strongest for two spatial features:

- In 2024 mutant-complementation experiments, tested CDC20 constructs localized to kinetochores except a mutant lacking the ABBA-binding region, indicating a requirement for that interface in kinetochore recruitment (zhang2024functionalanalysisof pages 3-5).
- Endogenous HeLa-cell proximity assays found APC8–CDC20 proximity highest in prometaphase and APC3–CDC20 proximity highest in metaphase, supporting temporally reorganized APC/C engagement (liu2020theinteractionprofile pages 47-50).

Claims that human CDC20 stably decorates spindle microtubules should be made more cautiously. Some direct spindle-localization evidence in the retrieved literature concerns plant CDC20, whereas human structural and interaction studies primarily establish kinetochore/MCC/APC/C engagement. Broad nuclear-versus-cytosolic partitioning also remains less precisely resolved than CDC20's mitotic interaction geography (liu2020theinteractionprofile pages 1-8, cosma2022biochemicalbiophysicaland pages 10-11).

## 5. Biological processes and physiological significance

The core biological processes are:

- spindle-assembly-checkpoint signaling;
- APC/C-dependent protein ubiquitylation;
- metaphase-to-anaphase transition;
- sister-chromatid separation;
- CDK1 inactivation and mitotic exit;
- maintenance of chromosome number and genome stability;
- analogous control of meiotic progression in oocytes.

The functional logic is highly constrained: too little APC/C–CDC20 activity causes prolonged metaphase arrest and often mitotic death; activity released too early permits segregation before correct attachment, producing aneuploidy. In human CRISPR-engineered cells, acute removal of residual CDC20 caused prolonged metaphase-to-anaphase timing, inefficient cyclin-B1 degradation, metaphase arrest, and cell death; RNAi-resistant CDC20 rescued the phenotype (zhang2024functionalanalysisof pages 1-2).

Human genetics supports an important reproductive role. Open Targets links CDC20 with **female infertility due to oocyte meiotic arrest** and **oocyte maturation defect 14**, consistent with the requirement for APC/C–CDC20 during meiotic chromosome segregation (OpenTargets Search: -CDC20). These genetic associations are stronger evidence of organism-level necessity than cancer-expression correlations, which can partly reflect the high mitotic fraction of tumors.

## 6. Important 2023–2024 developments

### 6.1 The CRY box is a checkpoint-signaling element

The most precise recent mechanistic advance is Zhang et al., published in *Communications Biology* in **September 2024**, DOI [10.1038/s42003-024-05859-6](https://doi.org/10.1038/s42003-024-05859-6). Using CRISPR-engineered, RNAi-sensitive HeLa cells complemented with mutant CDC20 proteins, live-cell analysis, immunoprecipitation, and reconstituted ubiquitylation assays, the authors showed that the conserved **CRY box** is required for efficient MCC assembly and stable MCC association with APC/C. It is therefore not merely a CDC20 degradation signal (zhang2024functionalanalysisof pages 11-11, zhang2024functionalanalysisof pages 1-2, zhang2024functionalanalysisof pages 7-8).

The biochemical assay used 50 nM APC/C, 50 nM CDC20, 200 nM cyclin B1, 30 nM UBA1, 20 μM ubiquitin, 5 mM ATP, 250 nM UbcH10, and 270 nM MCC constructs for 15 minutes at 25°C. These conditions provide direct mechanistic testing of how CRY-box or BUBR1-interface mutations change MCC inhibition of APC/C–CDC20-dependent cyclin-B1 ubiquitylation (zhang2024functionalanalysisof pages 11-11).

### 6.2 Structural refinement of APC/C regulation

High-resolution human APC/C cryo-EM work in 2023–2024 refined APC/C architecture and coactivator-dependent regulation, although the retrieved new structures emphasized apo-APC/C and APC/C–CDH1–EMI1 rather than a new CDC20 complex. They reinforce that CDC20 and CDH1 are sequential, structurally related coactivators whose N-terminal contacts are not identical. This is an evolving structural framework rather than a revision of CDC20's established primary function.

## 7. Disease relevance and recent quantitative studies

### 7.1 Cancer: strong association, emerging causality, no clinical validation yet

CDC20 is commonly elevated in proliferative tumors. Mechanistically this is plausible because excess or mistimed CDC20 can weaken checkpoint restraint, accelerate mitotic progression, or support survival of chromosome-unstable cells. Nevertheless, CDC20 expression also tracks cell proliferation; therefore, expression–outcome associations alone do not prove that CDC20 is an independent oncogenic driver.

**Breast cancer.** A July 2024 *Cancers* analysis, DOI [10.3390/cancers16142546](https://doi.org/10.3390/cancers16142546), integrated TCGA and several public resources. It reported tumor-associated CDC20 elevation, adverse clinical associations, and a median survival of **63.6 months** in the high-expression group versus **169.2 months** in the low-expression group. Preclinical evidence summarized in the same study found that CDC20 deficiency reduced growth and metastatic phenotypes, induced G2/M arrest, and increased paclitaxel cytotoxicity. However, some TNBC subgroup analyses did not find significant DMFS, OS, or DFS differences, illustrating cohort and endpoint dependence (messeha2024prognosticandtherapeutic pages 31-33, messeha2024prognosticandtherapeutic pages 29-31, messeha2024prognosticandtherapeutic pages 2-4).

**Lung adenocarcinoma.** Miao et al., *Frontiers in Oncology*, **July 2024**, DOI [10.3389/fonc.2024.1351393](https://doi.org/10.3389/fonc.2024.1351393), analyzed **279 samples** from GSE166722 and GSE31210, identified 91 recurrence/invasion/stemness-associated differentially expressed genes, and selected CDC45, CDC20, and TPX2 by machine-learning methods. CDC20 knockdown reduced migration, invasion, proliferation, cell-cycle progression, and mouse xenograft growth, potentially involving MAPK signaling. This combines retrospective biomarker discovery with preclinical functional validation, but it is not a prospective clinical test (miao2024basedonmachine pages 7-11).

**Bladder cancer.** Nalkiran et al., published online in **December 2024** in *Diagnostics* 15:59, DOI [10.3390/diagnostics15010059](https://doi.org/10.3390/diagnostics15010059), studied 54 tumors: 23 low-grade and 31 high-grade. Mean CDC20 fold change was **16.1** in high-grade versus **10.54** in low-grade tumors (**p=0.039**), with a grade correlation of **r=0.284, p=0.038**. CDC20 did not significantly associate with stage or overall survival in that cohort. This is a useful counterexample to overgeneralized claims that high CDC20 always predicts survival (nalkiran2024cdc20andccnb1 pages 15-16).

**Cervical cancer.** A November 2024 preprint examined 249 patients and reported CDC20 positivity in **61.85%** of tumors versus **7.5%** of adjacent tissues. Its xenograft study reported tumor volumes of 10.42 mm³ for control, 8.12 mm³ for Apcin, 6.21 mm³ for cisplatin, and 3.2 mm³ for the combination, with p<0.01. These unusually small reported volumes and the preprint status warrant cautious interpretation and independent validation (xian2024prognosticimpactand pages 14-17, xian2024prognosticimpactand pages 11-14).

### 7.2 Emerging noncanonical cancer mechanism

A 2024 study proposed a DNA-repair role in which nuclear CDC20 initially stabilizes RPA1 and supports ATR signaling, then later promotes RPA1 ubiquitylation and degradation, thereby contributing to homologous-recombination repair and radio/chemoresistance. This work used tumor cell lines and xenografts. It is intriguing but should be regarded as an emerging context-specific extension, not a replacement for the canonical APC/C–mitosis function.

## 8. Applications and real-world implementation

### 8.1 Current applications

Current practical use is predominantly **research-oriented**:

- CDC20 transcript or protein is evaluated in retrospective tumor cohorts as a proliferation/prognostic biomarker.
- CDC20 and MCC components are manipulated experimentally to study checkpoint fidelity, aneuploidy, drug sensitivity, and mitotic death.
- Human CDC20 variants can be considered in genetic investigation of recurrent oocyte maturation arrest or early embryonic failure, supported by disease-target and human genetic evidence (OpenTargets Search: -CDC20).

CDC20 immunostaining or expression profiling is **not established as a universal standard-of-care diagnostic or companion biomarker**. Cancer results vary by tumor type, cohort, endpoint, and adjustment for proliferation and stage.

### 8.2 Therapeutic strategies under investigation

Proposed strategies include:

- **Apcin**, which interferes with CDC20-dependent degron recognition;
- **TAME/proTAME**, APC/C inhibitors that mimic the coactivator IR motif and destabilize productive APC/C–coactivator engagement;
- CDC20 knockdown or genetic suppression;
- combining partial APC/C–CDC20 suppression with paclitaxel, cisplatin, radiation, KIF18A inhibition, or other spindle-directed treatments.

The mechanistic aim is generally to delay or block anaphase, increase mitotic stress, or prevent survival after abnormal spindle assembly. However, complete CDC20 suppression also threatens normal proliferating tissues and genome stability, creating a narrow therapeutic window. Selectivity, dosing, and whether tumor cells undergo durable death rather than mitotic slippage remain central unresolved issues (nalkiran2024cdc20andccnb1 pages 15-16, xian2024prognosticimpactand pages 11-14).

The clinical-trial search retrieved **no relevant CDC20-directed interventional trial**, and the literature reviewed here reports no approved CDC20-specific therapy. Thus, descriptions of CDC20 as “druggable” or “therapeutic” currently refer to **preclinical potential**, not established clinical implementation.

## 9. Expert interpretation and evidence-weighted conclusions

1. **The canonical annotation is exceptionally strong.** Human CDC20 Q12834 is a conserved CDC20/Fizzy-family WD40 protein whose primary role is APC/C activation and substrate recruitment. Its exact function is not generic “cell-cycle regulation” but temporal selection of proteins for APC/C-mediated polyubiquitylation at the metaphase–anaphase transition (yamano2019apcccurrentunderstanding pages 5-6, yamano2019apcccurrentunderstanding pages 3-5).

2. **CDC20 is both the accelerator and the checkpoint target.** The same molecule that activates APC/C is captured by MAD2–BUBR1–BUB3 when attachment is incomplete. This dual use creates a rapid, switch-like coupling between kinetochore status and anaphase (cosma2022biochemicalbiophysicaland pages 8-10).

3. **Localization is dynamic and interaction-defined.** The best-supported human localization is at kinetochores and in temporally changing APC/C/MCC assemblies during mitosis. Assertions of fixed nuclear, cytoplasmic, centrosomal, or spindle-wide localization should be qualified by cell-cycle stage and experimental system (zhang2024functionalanalysisof pages 3-5, liu2020theinteractionprofile pages 47-50).

4. **The 2024 CRY-box result materially improves annotation.** It assigns a positive checkpoint-signaling function to a motif previously discussed principally as a degron: the CRY box stabilizes MCC formation and APC/C inhibition (zhang2024functionalanalysisof pages 1-2, zhang2024functionalanalysisof pages 7-8).

5. **Human disease evidence is uneven.** Biallelic loss-of-function and oocyte maturation arrest directly fit the canonical meiotic function. Cancer overexpression is widespread and experimentally actionable, but many clinical claims remain retrospective and confounded by proliferation. The bladder study's absence of an overall-survival association underscores the need for tumor-specific validation (OpenTargets Search: -CDC20, nalkiran2024cdc20andccnb1 pages 15-16).
