Gene Description

BRAT1 is an ARM/HEAT-repeat assembly factor that binds the Integrator endonuclease INTS11 and its partner INTS9. Its conserved C-terminal motif occupies the INTS11 active site, inhibiting premature RNA cleavage while stabilizing an assembly intermediate. BRAT1 acts predominantly in the cytoplasm and can assist nuclear delivery of the cleavage module; it dissociates as the mature INTS4–INTS9–INTS11 module forms. Earlier studies also implicate BRAT1 in maintaining ATM and DNA-PK-associated damage signaling and in context-dependent cell survival, proliferation and mitochondrial homeostasis. Biallelic pathogenic variants cause a spectrum of neurological disease including neonatal rigidity with multifocal seizures and neurodevelopmental disease with cerebellar atrophy.

Core Functions

BRAT1 occupies the INTS11 active site through its conserved C-terminal motif, inhibiting premature endoribonuclease activity while stabilizing an INTS9–INTS11 assembly intermediate. This assembly-factor activity supports formation and nuclear delivery of the Integrator cleavage module; BRAT1 is released during maturation rather than retained as a catalytic subunit of mature Integrator.

Integrator assembly and nuclease inhibition

Sabath, Qiu and Jonas (2024), PMID:39032489 directly identify BRAT1 and WDR73 as assembly factors for the human INTS9–INTS11 cleavage module. The inspected abstract reports active-site blocking, prevention of premature INTS4 association, nuclear import assistance and IP6-dependent BRAT1 release. It supports specific endoribonuclease inhibition and Integrator assembly, not RNA cleavage by BRAT1. The complete original Methods and images were not inspected.

Lin and colleagues (2024), PMID:39032490 supplies complementary human and fly structural and functional evidence. The normal cache is abstract-only. An authenticated, separately retained full-text variant was read for all unique main Introduction, Results, Discussion and limitations paragraphs, plus the relevant construct, fractionation, imaging, structural, knockout, rescue and organoid Methods. These supplemental readings are paraphrased here; no snippet absent from the normal cache is used as a validated YAML quotation. Images, complete supplementary tables and every analytical Methods subsection were not independently audited.

The human ternary INTS9–INTS11–BRAT1 structure places the BRAT1 C terminus in the INTS11 active site. The detailed initial DCY metal-coordination account is in the fly complex; human BRAT1 is 821 amino acids and the fly Tyr974 numbering is not transferred to it. BRAT1-bound INTS9/INTS11 is incompatible with the mature INTS4-containing cleavage module. Knockout reduces INTS9/INTS11 protein levels, and wild-type BRAT1 rescues the processing phenotype more effectively than the DCY/CTE mutants. No UBE3D ligase or CPSF73 activity is assigned to BRAT1.

The two papers support one core assembly mechanism, represented by GO:0060698 endoribonuclease inhibitor activity coupled to GO:0160234 integrator complex assembly. The former is a child of the two seeded GO:0008428 assertions, so both are refined instead of adding a redundant NEW assertion. BRAT1 is an assembly factor and not a permanent holo-Integrator component; no mature Integrator in_complex assertion is introduced.

Import assistance is not universal transport dependence. Lin and colleagues found that residual INTS11 remained nuclear in BRAT1-null HCT116 cells. That observation qualifies, rather than erases, the import-assistance finding in the companion study. HCT116 and human embryonic stem-cell knockout cultures also lacked overt growth defects, whereas neural organoids developed transcriptional disruption. The latter experiments do not establish the activity of each patient allele or each BRAT1 product.

Earlier DNA-damage and trafficking evidence

Aglipay and colleagues (2006), PMID:16452482 was read from the normal abstract. It reports BRCA1 yeast two-hybrid association, ATM binding, double-strand-break localization and reduced ATM/CHK2 phosphorylation after BRAT1 depletion. It also reports that purified BAAT1 partially blocks PP2A-mediated ATM dephosphorylation. Thus the DNA-damage evidence is more than a generic knockdown phenotype. Substrate protection remains a possible explanation: this does not establish that BRAT1 is a kinase or a general PP2A inhibitor.

So and Ouchi (2011), PMID:22977523 remains abstract-only in the normal cache. The authenticated supplemental full main text was read separately, including Methods and Results. Human BAAT1 fragments and full-length FLAG-BAAT1 recover DNA-PKcs and ATM from 293T lysates; fragment assays also recover SMC1. The NCS time course alters these associations, and knockdown reduces DNA-PKcs and SMC1 phosphorylation in human MCF10A cells. Lysate recovery does not prove purified binary binding. SMC1A is a cohesin ATPase and phosphorylation substrate, not a protein kinase; the broad wording in the UniProt functional comment is not copied into the synthesis.
