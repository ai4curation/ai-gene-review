LMTK1/AATK construct identity in 35902728 and 17267545

Hypothesis slug: kgap-lmtk1-construct-isoform Gene: human LMTK1 (AATK), UniProt Q6ZMQ8, NCBITaxon:9606 Source file: genes/human/LMTK1/LMTK1-ai-review.yaml, free-text focus Iterations completed: 3. Findings recorded: 7. Papers read: 4.

Summary

The seed hypothesis asserts that the Q6ZMQ8 isoform of the human construct in PMID: 35902728 "should be decidable from the paper itself and from sequence". It is not. The 161 kB Europe PMC full text contains no sequence accession, no AATK residue number, no protein size, no isoform or transcript-variant statement, and no cloning or mutagenesis primers. The entire construct description is two sentences, and the first of them carries an internal citation conflict: the text credits "Ma and Rubin [16]" while the XML cross-reference rid="CR16" resolves to Haag et al. 2014 (PMID: 25352953), whose construct is the mouse clone BC080846. Ma and Rubin is reference 17. The two readings point to different species and to four different proteins, so the construct cannot be assigned.

The second half of the hypothesis resolves. PMID: 17267545 does not state the species of its AATYK1 clone in the abstract, and its full text could not be retrieved (journals.physiology.org returns HTTP 403, the record has no PMCID, and Unpaywall, OpenAlex and Europe PMC all report no open copy). The single residue number it gives settles the question by arithmetic: across 22 UniProt entries and 21 RefSeq proteins from human, mouse and rat, the valine of the only RVxF-type PP1 docking motif, KAVSF, sits at position 1175 only in the 1317-residue mouse AATYK1A protein (NP_031403.2, identical to the BC080846 coding sequence). The human positions are 1232 in Q6ZMQ8-1 and 1129 in NP_004911.2; Q6ZMQ8-2 (446 aa) and Q6ZMQ8-3 (879 aa) are too short to contain the motif at all.

A third result reframes the question for curation. The candidate set in the seed is Q6ZMQ8-1, -2 and -3. The human protein most likely to appear in a published AATK expression construct, the 1271-residue product of NM_004920.3 (NP_004911.2), is none of them. The UniProt flat file tags one RefSeq pair and not the other: DR RefSeq; NP_001073864.2; NM_001080395.3. [Q6ZMQ8-1] against DR RefSeq; NP_004911.2; NM_004920.3. with no isoform assignment. An isoform assignment restricted to the three UniProt isoforms can therefore be unsatisfiable. Separately, QuickGO shows that neither paper has yet been curated into GO (numberOfHits: 0 for both), so this is a prospective decision rather than a cleanup of an existing annotation.

Executive judgment

Partially supported, with the decisive half refuted.

The premise fails for 35902728. Nothing in the paper, its supplementary files, or its deposited dataset reports the species, the start codon, the length or the accession of the expressed protein. The kinase-dead allele is described only as "The ATP binding pocket of AATK was mutated (Ala>Lys)", written in reverse order (a kinase-dead ATP-pocket mutation is Lys to Ala) and with no position, so there is no arithmetic to run on it. If a position had been given, it would have separated NP_004911.2 (catalytic Lys K53) from Q6ZMQ8-1 and Q6ZMQ8-3 (both K156), but it would not have separated Q6ZMQ8-1 from Q6ZMQ8-3, which share residues 1-467 verbatim.

The hypothesis holds for 17267545 only in the weaker form the seed allows: the species is not stated, and the identity follows from sequence. The V1175 arithmetic is unambiguous, with no second RVxF-type candidate motif anywhere in the protein family to confuse it.

The most important caveats. First, the mouse assignment for 17267545 is a sequence inference, not a quotation; the methods section was never read because no accessible copy exists. Second, the V1175 inference assumes the "PP1 docking motif" of the abstract is the canonical KAVSF; that is the only consensus hit present, but the assumption is not independently confirmed. Third, for 35902728 the citation fork is irreducible from the published record, and both readings are internally plausible: Dammann is senior author of both 35902728 and 25352953, which makes reuse of their own mouse clone likely, while authors rarely thank themselves by name, which favours the "Ma and Rubin" wording being accurate.

Key findings

F1. 35902728 does not specify the construct well enough to assign an isoform

The complete construct description in the methods, section "Generation of AATK wt and AATK KD (kinase dead) stable cell lines", quoted verbatim from the Europe PMC full text:

"AATK coding sequence was kindly provided by Ma and Rubin [16] and cloned into, pEYFP-C2 (Clontech) and pCMVTag1 (Flag; Agilent). The ATP binding pocket of AATK was mutated (Ala>Lys) by site-directed mutagenesis (Agilent). DNA of AATK wt or AATK KD were cloned into pcDNATo/Myc/His vector (Thermo Fisher Scientific)."

and from the results:

"we generated an inducible clone pool for an AATK kinase-dead (KD) variant in the TREx system, in which the ATP binding pocket was mutated to obliterate ATP binding."

Programmatic string counts over the de-tagged full text:

Token Occurrences What it means
NM_, NP_, Q6ZMQ8 0 no sequence accession anywhere in the paper
kDa, amino acid, full-length 0 no protein size claim to work from
1374, 1271 0 neither candidate human length is stated
isoform 1 refers to the UniProt MaxQuant search database, not to AATK
variant 1 refers to the kinase-dead variant, not a transcript variant

The only residue number in the paper is TP53 Ser366, a substrate site. Supplementary Table S1, retrieved from Springer static content, lists bisulfite, CoBRA, pyrosequencing, CRISPR-guide and qPCR primers, with no cloning or mutagenesis primers. The AATK qPCR pair (TGGCCTGGCTCACTGCAAGTACAG / CCCAGATGGTCACGCCCAGG) gives a 170 nt amplicon in both human transcripts (NM_004920.3 nt 680-849, codon 169; NM_001080395.3 nt 1045-1214, codon 272, a 103-codon offset), so even the expression assay does not discriminate transcript variants.

The abstract gives no more than the methods do. The only construct description it offers is "The kinase activity of AATK in comparison to the kinase-dead mutant mediates a decreased expression of the key cell cycle regulators Cyclin D1 and WEE1" (PMID: 35902728), with no residue number, accession or isoform.

F2. The two candidate source constructs resolve to mouse Aatk or to human proteins that are not all UniProt isoforms

Haag et al. 2014, the reference linked by xref rid="CR16", states in its methods:

"The cDNA of Aatk was obtained as a full length cDNA vector IRAVp968F06121D (Accession Number: BC080846; 5277 bp in pYX-Asc; imaGenes GmbH, Berlin, Germany) and cloned into the NotI and EcoRI sites of pcDNA3.1+."

GenBank BC080846.1 is "Mus musculus apoptosis-associated tyrosine kinase, mRNA (cDNA clone MGC:90718 IMAGE:6856191), complete cds", 5277 bp, CDS 221..4174, protein AAH80846.1. Its translation is 1317 aa and byte-identical to mouse NP_031403.2. It is not identical to Q6ZMQ8-1 (1374 aa), Q6ZMQ8-2 (446 aa), Q6ZMQ8-3 (879 aa), Q80YE4 (1365 aa) or NP_004911.2 (1271 aa). That clone is 5'-incomplete: the first ATG lies at nt 115 while the annotated CDS starts at nt 221, and the in-frame upstream translation reads ...DGAPLSELSWSSSLAVVAVSFSGIFTVVIL, matching Q80YE4 residues 19-48. The annotated start is Met49 of Q80YE4, inside the transmembrane helix at 32-52, so the 1317 aa protein lacks the membrane anchor. Haag et al. report that their construct is functional in the relevant assay: "We further show that overexpression of Aatk significantly suppresses colony formation in cancer cell lines" (PMID: 25352953).

Ma and Rubin 2014, the reference named in the text, made four constructs:

"Full-length human AATKA-FLAG, human AATKB-FLAG, mouse AatkA-FLAG, and mouse AatkB-FLAG were inserted into the hygromycin-pBABE expression vector with FLAG at the C terminus."

Their Supplementary Table 1 primers, extracted from the deposited .doc files, fix the two human open reading frames. The human AATKA forward primer carries GCCACC ATG GCC AAG C (M-A-K-Q); MAKQPG occurs at residue 104 of Q6ZMQ8-1, and the string comparison Q6ZMQ8-1[103:] == NP_004911.2 returns True, so human AATKA is Q6ZMQ8-1 residues 104-1374, a 1271 aa protein. The human AATKB forward primer carries GCCACC ATG TCG TCG (M-S-S); MSSSFF is residue 1 of Q6ZMQ8-1, so human AATKB is the canonical 1374 aa form. Their mouse AatkA came from "pYX-Asc-MGC Mouse Aatk cDNA (MMM1013-202799199)", the same pYX-Asc MGC clone family as BC080846. Their own prose ("AATKA encodes a cytoplasmic protein with an open reading frame of 1317 amino acids, whereas AATKB contains an additional transmembrane domain at the N-terminus, which adds 57 amino acids") conflicts with their own primers, where the human difference is 103 residues. The primers are the more reliable record.

Five distinct proteins are therefore live candidates for the 35902728 construct, and only one of them, human AATKB, equals a current Q6ZMQ8 isoform.

F3. V1175 in 17267545 matches only the 1317 aa mouse AATYK1

The abstract reports one residue number:

"We identified a PP1 docking motif in AATYK1 and demonstrated interaction using yeast-2-hybrid analysis. Mutation of a key valine residue (V1175) within this motif prevented protein-protein interaction." (PMID: 17267545)

A regular-expression scan for the canonical PP1 docking consensus [RK]-x(0,1)-[VI]-x-[FW] returns exactly one hit, KAVSF, in every full-length LMTK1/AATK protein examined. The valine positions:

The two SPAK docking motifs mentioned in the same abstract, SRFTV and SRFSI, are present in both human and mouse, so they carry no species information. The only organism named in the abstract is the expression host: "Heterologous expression of AATYK1 in NKCC1-injected Xenopus laevis oocytes markedly inhibited cotransporter activity under isosmotic conditions" (PMID: 17267545). The species of the clone is not stated.

The earlier Delpire-lab screen that found the SPAK-AATYK interaction does not help either. Its abstract describes the interaction partners as cotransporters and never names AATYK or its clone source: "The interaction was identified using yeast two-hybrid assays and confirmed via glutathione S-transferase pull-down experiments" (PMID: 12386165).

F4. Five numbering frames, three of which are not UniProt isoforms

UniProt Q6ZMQ8 (entry version 171, sequence version 2, 1374 aa) annotates a transmembrane helix at 32-52, a protein kinase domain at 125-395, ATP binding at 131-139 and 156, and an active site at 253. The VAR_SEQ features are VSP_020225 (removes 1-433 in isoform 2), VSP_020226 (removes 468-503 in isoforms 2 and 3), VSP_020227 (replaces 897-915) and VSP_020228 (removes 916-1374 in isoforms 2 and 3), giving 446 aa for Q6ZMQ8-2 and 879 aa for Q6ZMQ8-3. The comparison Q6ZMQ8-1[0:467] == Q6ZMQ8-3[0:467] returns True, so no residue number inside the kinase domain can separate isoform 1 from isoform 3.

The catalytic lysine, located by the conserved VVVKEL motif, is K156 in Q6ZMQ8-1 and Q6ZMQ8-3, K53 in NP_004911.2, K157 in Q80YE4 and K109 in NP_031403.2. The two offsets most likely to produce silent curation errors are the 103-residue human shift (Q6ZMQ8-1 versus NP_004911.2) and the 57-residue human-to-mouse-clone shift (Q6ZMQ8-1 versus NP_031403.2).

On accession status, Q6ZMQ8 has absorbed three secondary accessions that a curator may meet in older records: O75136 (KIAA0641), Q6ZN31 and Q86X28. BC080846.1, NM_004920.3 / NP_004911.2 and NM_001080395.3 / NP_001073864.2 are all current; none is retired or split. The official RefSeq symbol is now LMTK1 ("lemur tail kinase 1") with AATK and AATYK as synonyms. 35902728 cites no sequence accession at all; its only accession is ArrayExpress E-MTAB-11198. Accessions cited in 17267545 could not be checked because the full text is not retrievable.

F5. The deposited dataset records the host cell line, not the construct

E-MTAB-11198 was retrieved live from the EBI BioStudies API (HTTP 200) with its IDF and SDRF files. The record is "Analysis of Apoptosis-associated Tyrosine Kinase (AATK) function in T-Rex-293 cells", with three samples on array design A-GEOD-23159 and Factor Value[genotype] of "wild type genotype", "AATK[WT] expression" and "AATK[kinase-dead] expression". Characteristics[organism] reads Homo sapiens for all three samples, but that field describes the T-REx-293 host (cell line T-REx-293, developmental stage embryo, sex female, organism part kidney), not the transgene. The five protocols (P-MTAB-116300 to P-MTAB-116304) cover Trizol lysis, RNA extraction, labelling, hybridization and scanning. No construct, vector, accession, plasmid or mutagenesis protocol appears anywhere in the IDF, SDRF or study attributes.

A re-scan of the full text for deposition accessions returned E-MTAB-11198 and nothing else: zero hits for PXD, SRA/ENA, GenBank and RefSeq patterns. The GSE numbers present (GSE16011, GSE3141, GSE363133, GSE7307) are public datasets the authors reanalysed. The GFP-Trap proteomics raw files are not deposited.

F6. No assay in the paper discriminates the construct species

The AATK qPCR primer pair was aligned to five transcripts by exhaustive sliding-window Hamming scan. Human: 0/24 forward and 0/20 reverse mismatches in both NM_004920.3 and NM_001080395.3. Mouse: 4/24 forward and 1/20 reverse mismatches in NM_007377.4, BC080846.1 and NM_001198787.1, with identical 170 nt spacing. The forward mismatches fall at primer positions 8, 10, 13 and 19 of 24 (TGGCCTGGCTCACTGCAAGTACAG against mouse TGGCCTGTCGCATTGCAAATACAG), so the pair is human-specific by design, though the matched 3' terminus leaves residual mouse amplification possible at high template load.

This does not settle anything, because the paper never applies qPCR to the transgene. A sentence-level scan shows that transgene expression is read only through the EYFP and Flag/Myc tags, through the pan-AATK antibody ab56625, and through doxycycline induction ("The induction of AATK wt or AATK KD was performed using doxycycline (2 µg/ml)"). The AATK qPCR assay belongs to the separate "RNA expression analysis" of endogenous AATK in cancer lines, where its human specificity is an asset.

In silico digestion quantifies what a retrospective proteomics check could settle. Of 41 fully tryptic peptides of 7-40 aa from human Q6ZMQ8-1, only 5 are shared with mouse NP_031403.2 (12.2 percent), leaving 36 human-specific and 43 mouse-specific peptides, so a combined human and mouse database search would separate the species decisively. The same digest gives 0 tryptic peptides of 7-40 aa that distinguish the 1374 aa start from the 1271 aa start: residues 1-103 yield only GGIGFK (6 aa) and a 43 aa peptide EFENAEGDEYAADLAQGSPATAAQNGPDVYVLPLTEVSLPMAK. GluC digestion of the same region yields GAPLSE, VYVLPLTE, LAQGSPATAAQNGPD and MSSSFFNPSFAFSSHFDPD, which are usable start-site diagnostics.

Mechanistic model and interpretation

The construct question is a provenance chain with one fork and two terminal states. The chain is reconstructed below from text, cross-references, accessions and primers.

The two papers sit at opposite ends of the same problem. 17267545 published one residue number and thereby fixed its numbering frame permanently, even though it never named a species. 35902728 published no residue number, no accession and no length, and so fixed nothing, even though its construct is the one a human LMTK1 curator needs. The lesson generalises: a single residue number in a stated frame is worth more to downstream curation than a paragraph of vector names.

The practical reading of each frame:

If the construct is Then the protein is Catalytic Lys PP1 Val Status for human LMTK1 curation
Haag's BC080846 mouse, 1317 aa, no TM anchor K109 V1175 Cross-species; human annotation needs ISS/ISO handling
Ma and Rubin human AATKA human, 1271 aa K53 V1129 No matching UniProt isoform
Ma and Rubin human AATKB human, 1374 aa K156 V1232 Equals Q6ZMQ8-1
Ma and Rubin mouse AatkA mouse, 1317 aa K109 V1175 Cross-species
Q6ZMQ8-3 human, 879 aa K156 absent Excluded only by size, not by any datum in the paper
Q6ZMQ8-2 human, 446 aa absent absent Cannot support any kinase assay

Q6ZMQ8-2 can be excluded on biology: it lacks residues 1-433 and therefore the entire kinase domain, so it cannot produce the in vitro kinase activity or the kinase-dead contrast reported in 35902728. Q6ZMQ8-3 cannot be excluded by any statement in the paper, only by the prior expectation that a "full-length" shared construct is not a truncated 879 aa form. That expectation is reasonable and is not evidence.

GO curation implications

All of the following are leads requiring curator verification.

Current state: no GO annotation cites either paper. Human Q6ZMQ8 has no experimental annotation of any kinase activity; its five experimental annotations are all GO:0005515 protein binding from IntAct. The construct ambiguity is therefore a prospective problem, not an existing error.

  1. Do not attach an isoform qualifier to annotations sourced from 35902728. The paper does not support one. If the review currently assigns the construct to a specific Q6ZMQ8 isoform, withdraw that assignment and record instead that the construct is unspecified, with the citation fork noted.

  2. Flag a possible organism mismatch. The only traceable construct in this lab's lineage is the mouse clone BC080846. Annotations made to human AATK from 35902728 may rest on a mouse coding sequence expressed in human cells. If that is confirmed, the correct handling is an ISS/ISO-style transfer from mouse rather than a direct human IDA, or an explicit annotation comment. The molecular claim is not invalidated by this, since the kinase domain is shared, which is exactly why the discrepancy is easy to miss.

  3. MF term from 35902728. The in vitro kinase assay on TP53 plus the loss of signal with the ATP-pocket mutant and the STK peptide array support a serine/threonine kinase term (GO:0004674, or GO:0106310 protein serine kinase activity) over a tyrosine kinase term. If curated, this would be the first experimental MF evidence on human LMTK1. It sits against GO:0004713 protein tyrosine kinase activity, which on the human protein comes only from InterPro (ECO:0000256) and PAINT (ECO:0000318); the experimental tyrosine evidence (GO:0004713 ECO:0000314, GO:0038083) is on the mouse protein from 11314040. A curator can treat GO:0004713 on human Q6ZMQ8 as an automatic inference pending the species question, and should not generalise it to a direct human claim. The kinase-domain residues are identical in Q6ZMQ8-1 and Q6ZMQ8-3 and absent from Q6ZMQ8-2, so any MF annotation is isoform-agnostic between isoforms 1 and 3 and must not propagate to isoform 2.

  4. Treat the TP53 Ser366 substrate claim as a weaker, separate lead. The construct ambiguity means the assay may have used a protein lacking the transmembrane anchor (the 1317 aa mouse form starts inside the 32-52 helix), which changes the subcellular context in which the kinase would meet TP53.

  5. Label 17267545-derived annotations as mouse-derived. The V1175 arithmetic places that clone in mouse AATYK1A. Any human LMTK1 annotation from it is a cross-species inference and should carry the matching evidence code. The claims in that paper (PP1 docking through an RVxF motif, SPAK scaffolding, NKCC1 inhibition independent of kinase activity) are interaction and scaffolding claims, so a direct human CC or BP assertion would over-reach from this paper alone.

  6. Raise the NP_004911.2 gap with UniProt. The 1271 aa human protein is a current RefSeq with its own transcript and is the form encoded by the Ma and Rubin human AATKA primers, yet it maps to no Q6ZMQ8 isoform. This is a database gap that affects every residue-level curation decision on this gene.

Mechanistic scope

The immediate molecular activity under test in 35902728 is ATP-dependent serine/threonine phosphotransferase activity of the AATK kinase domain, read out by an in vitro kinase assay on TP53 and by kinome-profiling peptide arrays, with the ATP-pocket mutant as the specificity control. Everything else in that paper is downstream: Cyclin D1 and WEE1 transcript levels, proliferation, viability, colony formation and the patient-survival correlations. The construct question bears only on the first of these.

In 17267545 the immediate activity tested is protein-protein scaffolding, not catalysis. The abstract states that "mutation of key residues in the catalytic domain of AATYK1 revealed that the kinase activity does not play a role in the suppression of NKCC1 function" and that mutating the two SPAK-binding motifs abolished the effect. The NKCC1 transport change is a downstream consequence measured in a heterologous Xenopus oocyte system. The V1175 mutation is the only direct structure-function handle in the paper, and it reports on PP1 docking.

Conflicts and alternatives

The risk here is ortholog and numbering-frame confusion, not paralog confusion. LMTK2 and LMTK3 are distinct genes and were not implicated in either paper. What circulates under the single label "AATK" is one mouse construct and three incompatible human numbering frames.

The citation fork in 35902728 cannot be reduced from the published record. Both readings are plausible for opposite reasons, as set out in the executive judgment.

The inverted mutation direction ("Ala>Lys") is most simply a writing error, but with no position given it cannot be checked, so the kinase-dead control is described but unverifiable.

The V1175 inference assumes the PP1 motif in question is the canonical KAVSF. If Gagnon et al. called a non-canonical motif a PP1 docking site, the arithmetic would be anchored to the wrong position. The abstract's phrase "a PP1 docking motif" with "a key valine residue" fits KAVSF, and no competing RVxF-type motif exists in any of these proteins, but the assumption stands.

Ma and Rubin's own length statement conflicts with their own primers: they write that AATKB "adds 57 amino acids" to a 1317 aa AATKA, while their primers give 1374 and 1271, a 103-residue difference. The 1317 and 57 figures are carried over from the mouse and rat AATYK1A/1B nomenclature.
