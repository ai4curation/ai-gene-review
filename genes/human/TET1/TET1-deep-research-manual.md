# TET1 manual deep research notes

Automated deep research could not run in this sparse runtime because the configured Falcon and Perplexity providers were unavailable, so this file records the literature synthesis used for the review.

TET1 is a nuclear methylcytosine dioxygenase. Tahiliani et al. identified mammalian TET proteins as 2-oxoglutarate/Fe(II)-dependent oxygenases and showed that recombinant TET1 catalytic domain directly converts 5-methylcytosine in double-stranded DNA to 5-hydroxymethylcytosine with an Fe(II) and 2-oxoglutarate requirement [PMID:19372391]. Guo et al. connected this chemistry to active DNA demethylation, showing that TET1-dependent oxidation of 5mC promotes 5hmC-dependent DNA demethylation and neuronal activity-induced locus-specific demethylation in the adult mouse brain [PMID:21496894].

TET1 acts on chromatin in the nucleus. The human protein carries an N-terminal CXXC zinc finger and a C-terminal cysteine-rich/DSBH catalytic core; the CXXC domain contributes CpG-DNA binding and the catalytic domain supplies the DNA 5-methylcytosine dioxygenase activity [PMID:29276034; genes/human/TET1/TET1-uniprot.txt]. QSER1 binds TET1, helps protect DNA methylation valleys from de novo methylation, and supports the view that TET1 catalytic activity and recruitment of chromatin regulators both shape chromatin methylation landscapes [PMID:33833093].

Several rows describe regulatory contexts rather than TET1's core chemistry. TET1 can participate in SIN3-HDAC complexes and has been connected to gene repression through SIN3A recruitment, but the ComplexPortal-derived process annotations around cell migration, TGF-beta signaling, and opposite directions of stem cell maintenance overreach the TET1-specific evidence [PMID:22865885; PMID:22984288; PMID:28554894]. TET1 is also recruited to oxidative-lesion-containing promoters by OGG1 during oxidative stress-induced demethylation, which is a stress-specific deployment of the same 5mC oxidation activity [PMID:27251462].
