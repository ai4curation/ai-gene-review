# EFEMP1 / fibulin-3

EFEMP1 is a secreted extracellular matrix glycoprotein. Human retinal-cell loss/rescue experiments establish a contribution to extracellular network integrity; a separate human pancreatic carcinoma study establishes EGFR association and receptor-dependent signaling. These mechanisms are represented as two qualified core activities. Neither supports EFEMP1 kinase/protease activity, and the matrix phenotype is not a purified collagen-binding assay. [PMID:35998264](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35998264/), [PMID:19804359](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19804359/).

## Matrix integrity and contextual signaling

In human ARPE-19 cells, EFEMP1 loss disrupts collagen VI network organization in decellularized matrix and reduces extracellular MMP2 activity. Wild-type EFEMP1 partly rescues the matrix phenotype, whereas the C55R variant fails despite secretion. The measured disulfide-linked dimers concern the mutant; they do not establish obligatory wild-type covalent dimers. Changes in MMP2 activity are an extracellular outcome, not evidence that EFEMP1 itself is a protease. [PMID:35998264](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35998264/).

Human aortic fractionation and human colon/liver matrisome experiments independently identify fibulin-3 with multiple peptides. The colon/liver workbook retains a mixed accession group, including several splice-product identifiers; shared peptide evidence cannot assign a natural isoform. RCA classification as a matrix structural constituent is supported by the term's scope and the separate human knockout/rescue evidence, rather than being presented as a mechanical-strength measurement. The [reproducible extract](EFEMP1-evidence/README.md) preserves the target measurements. [PMID:20551380](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20551380/), [PMID:25037231](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25037231/).

Human carcinoma-cell co-immunoprecipitation and EGF competition support EFEMP1–EGFR association. Purchased human EFEMP1 stimulates EGFR and downstream Akt/ERK phosphorylation in the measured system. The paper does not establish a purified binary interface, an identified natural splice product, or the same signaling mechanism in all normal tissues. EFEMP1 is the extracellular interacting factor; EGFR performs the phosphorylation. Accordingly, receptor activity is refined to receptor binding, and the direct phosphorylation-process assertion is over-annotated. The positive carcinoma signaling observation remains non-core. [PMID:19804359](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19804359/).

Human EFEMP1 expression and extracellular addition of purified tagged human protein inhibit chondrogenic differentiation in mouse ATDC5 cells. Conditioned-medium detection supports secretion. Reduced cartilage-like nodules, proteoglycan accumulation and marker expression support a contextual differentiation effect, not direct human cartilage-development measurements or an EFEMP1 DNA-binding mechanism. The human protein, mouse assay host and insect-cell production host are distinct. The suggested TIMP3/ADAM mechanism was not tested in this experiment; the separate TIMP3 interaction abstract does not close that mechanistic gap. [PMID:20005202](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20005202/), [PMID:15123717](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15123717/).

## Propagation and localization

Current PANTHER responses place EFEMP1 in PTHR24039, while the older machine record points to PTHR24050. The resolved 330-node tree and alignment contain the exact 493-residue target below the literal ancestral node PTN000620536. Both IBD assertions, experimental descendant annotations and the target path support the broad matrix activities. Current IBD and imported GOA donor lists differ; both snapshots are preserved. Donor number and evidence on a descendant are not circularity defects.

HPA reports mitochondrial staining with location-specific enhanced reliability, whereas its XML overall score is supported. This positive intracellular observation is retained as non-core. The secretion signal does not disprove an intracellular pool; no mitochondrial biochemical function or splice-product identity is inferred, and the images were not independently scored. The exact HPA location and antibody/cell-line context are linked in the source guide.

The elastic-fiber review explicitly includes fibulin-3 among associated proteins and discusses tissue-specific defects in fibulin-3-null mice. Detailed fibulin-4/5 tropoelastin/LOX mechanisms are not transferred. Reactome independently contains mature human EFEMP1 as a member of its fibulin set. Current typed ontology relationships place elastic fiber within the non-collagenous component of interstitial matrix. The narrower basement-membrane inference remains unresolved because the complete ARBA trigger and exact compartment were not independently established. [PMID:23962539](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23962539/).

## Disease, citation identity and unresolved assertions

The EFEMP1 Arg345Trp association with inherited drusen-associated retinal disease is supported by human family genetics. That disease relationship alone does not resolve the precise visual-perception process, which concerns reception and processing of visual stimuli; the original body supporting that assertion was unavailable. Broad matrix localization is independently established. [PMID:10369267](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10369267/).

Three eye-development expression assertions cite a study of FBN1, FBN2 and FBN3. Its full Methods specify fibrillin antibodies, and its Results concern fibrillins and MAGP1 in human eyes. Fibrillin-3 is FBN3, not EFEMP1/fibulin-3. These three assertions are removed on verified operative-source identity, while the original source objects and PMID remain intact. This is not an inference from an inaccessible title or abstract. [PMID:25406291](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25406291/).

The exosome assertion remains `UNDECIDED`: the original vesicle preparation and proteomics methods were read, but the EFEMP1 target row in Supplementary Table 2 was not recovered. A nominal workbook download returned a challenge document. Neither contamination nor exclusion from extracellular vesicles is asserted. [PMID:23533145](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23533145/).

## Natural products and review scope

The five official products reconstruct to 493, 485, 492, 492 and 355 residues. Product 5 lacks the canonical signal region and two central EGF modules. Equivalent secretion, stability, matrix function and receptor signaling have not been demonstrated. The older cloning paper reports 487 residues; without its construct sequence this is not equated with one of the current products. Its abstract's electron-microscopy result concerns fibulin-4. [PMID:10601734](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10601734/).
