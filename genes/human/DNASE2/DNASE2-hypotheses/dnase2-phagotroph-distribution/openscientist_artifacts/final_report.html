Summary

The seed hypothesis proposes that DNase II family enzymes — which digest the DNA of engulfed cells in animal phagolysosomes — are present in phagotrophic protists (Dictyostelium discoideum, Acanthamoeba, choanoflagellates), and that this distribution is "consistent with digestion of phagocytosed DNA being an ancestral eukaryotic function." The requested test was the phylogenetic distribution of DNase II homologs across eukaryotic reference proteomes.

I evaluated this by tabulating InterPro/UniProt protein counts for family IPR004947 (equivalently PANTHER PTHR10858 / Pfam PF03265) across major eukaryotic lineages, with protein-level identity audits of surprising hits and a proteome-coverage control for the key negative example. The verdict is partially supported. Two of the three named phagotroph examples are genuine: Dictyostelium discoideum encodes a real ~14-member family expansion and Acanthamoeba castellanii encodes 3 bona fide members. The family is indeed ancient and broadly eukaryotic (5,031 eukaryotic proteins across 1,423 reference proteomes), reaching Amoebozoa, land plants, ciliates and stramenopiles. However, the third named example — choanoflagellates — is refuted: both well-sequenced species (Monosiga brevicollis, Salpingoeca rosetta) lack the family entirely, and this is not a proteome-coverage artifact.

More importantly, the evolutionary interpretation is only weakly supported. Presence of the family does not track phagotrophy: non-phagotrophic land plants retain a robust complement (72 proteins) while several phagotrophic lineages (kinetoplastids, Giardia/Fornicata, and the choanoflagellates themselves) have lost it. The pattern is best explained as an ancient eukaryotic family subject to many independent lineage-specific losses, not as a clean marker of an ancestral phagocytic-DNA-digestion function. For curation, the evolutionary free-text should be narrowed and the choanoflagellate claim corrected; this observation is orientation/context only and should not by itself drive any GO term change on the human DNASE2 record.

The choanoflagellate absence was explicitly tested against a proteome-coverage control: Monosiga brevicollis (9,331 UniProt proteins) and Salpingoeca rosetta (12,235 UniProt proteins) both have well-populated proteomes, so a count of zero DNase II proteins reflects a genuine evolutionary loss, not missing sequence data.

This finding simultaneously supports the hypothesis for the two named amoebae and refutes it for choanoflagellates. It also undercuts the deeper inference: phagotrophy does not predict presence, because several phagotrophic lineages lack the family while non-phagotrophic land plants retain it.

The function the hypothesis attributes to the family is itself well established. The DNase II review states that "Its lysosomal localization and ubiquitous tissue distribution suggested that this enzyme played a role in the degradation of exogenous DNA encountered by phagocytosis" (PMID: 14644493). In an invertebrate gut, a DNase II homolog digests engulfed/dietary bacterial DNA — DNase II homologs "both fail to replace nuc-1 in degrading bacterial DNA in intestine" (PMID: 19809494). These confirm the engulfed-DNA digestion role beyond mammals but do not establish that this function is ancestral or phagotrophy-linked across the eukaryotic tree.

Finding 2 — Protein-level identity checks confirm genuine DNase II in amoebae and plants, and show the sole fungal "hit" is spurious — strengthening the loss pattern

Because InterPro signature matches can occasionally be incidental, each surprising hit was inspected at the protein-annotation level:

  • Dictyostelium discoideum hits are annotated "Deoxyribonuclease II" and "Deoxyribonuclease II family protein" (e.g., C7G058, Q54FV5) — confirming a genuine ~14-member family expansion rather than low-quality matches.
  • Viridiplantae hits include real, functionally named members: "Cell-death-related nuclease 7", "Plancitoxin-1", and "Deoxyribonuclease-2-alpha" — bona fide DNase II enzymes in a non-phagotrophic lineage.
  • The single Fungi hit (A0A2H9TI56) is annotated "Repressor of RNA polymerase III transcription" — an incidental/spurious signature match, not a true DNase II. Fungi are therefore effectively devoid of the family.

This audit removes the one apparent counter-example to fungal loss, confirms that plant retention is real, and verifies that the Dictyostelium expansion is authentic. Together these sharpen the conclusion that the presence/absence pattern is driven by repeated independent losses of an ancient family rather than by phagotrophic lifestyle.

Finding 3 — The catalytic domain is not strictly eukaryote-specific

An outgroup query of IPR004947 returned Bacteria 66, Archaea 0, Viruses 6 against Eukaryota 5,031. The family is overwhelmingly eukaryotic but not strictly eukaryote-restricted; sparse bacterial members suggest a deeper domain origin or horizontal transfer. This is a minor qualifier to the seed's phrase "ancestral eukaryotic function": the catalytic domain may predate or extend beyond eukaryotes even though the lysosomal/phagolysosomal DNA-digestion role is a eukaryotic elaboration. It does not change the core verdict.

Mechanistic Model / Interpretation

The molecular function under test is narrow and well-defined: DNase II–family enzymes are acid (lysosomal/phagolysosomal) endonucleases that cleave DNA at low pH without divalent-cation dependence, degrading DNA delivered into the lysosome — from apoptotic/engulfed cells, phagocytosed microbes, or dietary sources. The hypothesis takes this well-supported function and makes an evolutionary extrapolation: that the enzyme's distribution should mark phagotrophy as an ancestral eukaryotic activity.

Key logical points:

  1. Presence in phagotrophs is consistent with — but not diagnostic of — an ancestral phagocytic role. Dictyostelium and Acanthamoeba fit, but the inference is confounded by the exceptions below.
  2. Retention in non-phagotrophic plants shows the family is maintained for functions unrelated to phagocytosis (e.g., programmed-cell-death nucleases).
  3. Loss in multiple phagotrophs (choanoflagellates, kinetoplastids, Giardia) shows phagotrophy can proceed without this specific family — other acid nucleases can substitute.
  4. Sparse bacterial members indicate the catalytic domain's deep origin, offering an alternative to a strictly eukaryotic-phagotrophic framing.

The correct framing for curation is therefore evolutionary context, not mechanistic evidence for the human gene's GO annotations. Human DNASE2 function (lysosomal DNA catabolism, clearance of DNA from engulfed apoptotic cells and erythroid nuclei) is established by direct mammalian and invertebrate experiments; the cross-eukaryote distribution neither adds to nor subtracts from those annotations.

Mechanistic Scope

  • Direct gene-product activity (in scope, well-supported): an acid-optimum, divalent-cation-independent endonuclease that cleaves dsDNA within the lysosome/phagolysosome. This is the immediate, cell-autonomous molecular function, documented biochemically in mammals and genetically in C. elegans.
  • Downstream phenotypes (out of scope for this evolutionary hypothesis): failure of DNA clearance → undegraded cytosolic DNA → cGAS–STING/interferon activation → autoinflammation (human AIPCS, PMID: 42901880); innate-immune gene upregulation in C. elegans (PMID: 26251453). These are loss-of-function consequences, not evidence for the ancestral-function claim.
  • Evolutionary distribution is an orthology/inference layer, not a direct functional assay; it constrains ancestry but cannot assign the ancestral function.

Conflicts and Alternatives

  1. Choanoflagellate counter-evidence (direct conflict). The seed names choanoflagellates as a positive example, but Monosiga brevicollis and Salpingoeca rosetta have zero members in well-covered proteomes — refuting a named example.
  2. Phagotrophy does not predict presence (interpretive conflict). Phagotrophic kinetoplastids and Giardia lack the family; non-phagotrophic land plants retain it. The "ancestral phagotroph" framing is confounded.
  3. Paralog expansion / annotation inflation. Counts reflect family-signature hits, including paralogs; raw counts are not a phylogeny and can overstate independent acquisitions. The identity audit mitigated but did not eliminate this.
  4. Spurious signature matches (database carry-over). The lone fungal hit was a mis-annotated "Repressor of RNA Pol III," showing raw InterPro counts can contain false positives; single-hit "presence" needs identity checks.
  5. Deep (pre-eukaryotic) origin. Sparse bacterial members indicate the domain predates eukaryotic phagotrophy, an alternative to the "ancestral eukaryotic phagocytic function" story.
