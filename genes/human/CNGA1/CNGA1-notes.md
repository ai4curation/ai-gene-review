# CNGA1 review notes

CNGA1 forms the cGMP-dependent cation-conducting channel of the rod outer-segment membrane. cAMP binding and partial activation are secondary capabilities.

## Channel and transport evidence

Actual selected Methods and Results in PMID:33651975 and PMID:34699778 establish human CNGA1 homomeric and 3:1 CNGA1/CNGB1 channels. The structural constructs use historical NP_000078.2 residues 145–690, with full-length functional comparisons. This version has four leading residues relative to current 686-residue P29973; the paper coordinates remain explicit. The modelled structural residue extent is not a distinct biological isoform. CNGA1 contributes the pore-forming alpha subunits of the physiological heteromeric channel.

High cAMP partially activates the heteromer and barely activates the homomer. A cAMP-bound closed cryo-EM state does not cancel that functional result. Calcium occupancy and block in these human structural studies are distinct from direct calcium permeation. PMID:10352032 supplies the latter at donor scope: bovine alpha and alpha/beta channels expressed in Xenopus oocytes were studied under defined gradients, with reversal-potential permeability estimates and controls for endogenous chloride currents. Sodium and calcium membrane-crossing refinements therefore preserve a measured transport basis. Native salamander rod and fish cone assays in that paper are kept separate.
