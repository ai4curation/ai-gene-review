Gene Description

CNGA1 is the pore-forming alpha subunit of the rod photoreceptor cyclic-nucleotide-gated cation channel. Three CNGA1 subunits assemble with one CNGB1 subunit in the native channel at the rod outer-segment plasma membrane. Intracellular cGMP binding promotes pore opening and cation influx; the light-driven fall in cGMP closes the channel and reduces the rod dark current. CNGA1 can also form functional homomeric channels in expression systems. Human pathogenic variants can impair channel abundance, targeting or function and cause recessive retinitis pigmentosa.

Core Functions

CNGA1 contributes the cGMP-binding and pore-forming machinery of the rod CNGA1/CNGB1 channel, coupling intracellular ligand recognition to cation conduction. The physiological assembly has three alpha subunits and one beta subunit; experimentally expressed CNGA1 homotetramers also conduct. Human structural preparations use defined N-terminal truncations with full-length functional comparisons, and direct calcium-permeability support additionally comes from bovine recombinant channels. cAMP binding and partial activation remain secondary capabilities within this single channel mechanism.

CNGA1 review notes

CNGA1 forms the cGMP-dependent cation-conducting channel of the rod outer-segment membrane. cAMP binding and partial activation are secondary capabilities.

Channel and transport evidence

Actual selected Methods and Results in PMID:33651975 and PMID:34699778 establish human CNGA1 homomeric and 3:1 CNGA1/CNGB1 channels. The structural constructs use historical NP_000078.2 residues 145–690, with full-length functional comparisons. CNGA1 contributes the pore-forming alpha subunits of the physiological heteromeric channel.

High cAMP partially activates the heteromer and barely activates the homomer. A cAMP-bound closed cryo-EM state does not cancel that functional result. Calcium occupancy and block in these human structural studies are distinct from direct calcium permeation. PMID:10352032 supplies the latter at donor scope: bovine alpha and alpha/beta channels expressed in Xenopus oocytes were studied under defined gradients, with reversal-potential permeability estimates and controls for endogenous chloride currents.
