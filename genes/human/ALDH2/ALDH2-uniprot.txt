CC   -!- FUNCTION: Mitochondrial aldehyde dehydrogenase with broad substrate
CC       specificity which participates in the detoxification of ethanol-derived
CC       acetaldehyde and endogenous aldehydes, including those arising from
CC       lipid peroxidation (PubMed:16956664, PubMed:22339434, PubMed:33355142,
CC       PubMed:39075523). The reaction mechanism involves a nucleophilic attack
CC       on the aldehyde substrate, resulting in formation of a thiohemiacetal
CC       intermediate. Subsequent hydride transfer to NAD+, yields a thioester
CC       intermediate which is hydrolyzed to the carboxylic acid product through
CC       activation of a water molecule by the catalytic glutamate
CC       (PubMed:19506075). In vivo, ALDH2 is required for the clearance of
CC       formaldehyde, a cytotoxic and carcinogenic endogenous metabolite that
CC       induces DNA damage (PubMed:33355142). It also catalyzes the oxidation
CC       of 3,4-dihydroxyphenylacetaldehyde (DOPAL), a reactive intermediate of
CC       dopamine catabolism, to 3,4-dihydroxyphenylacetate (DOPAC)
CC       (PubMed:16956664). ALDH2 may also catalyze the oxidation of 4-
CC       aminobutanal (gamma-aminobutyraldehyde) to 4-aminobutanoate (GABA),
CC       representing an alternative route for GABA biosynthesis from polyamine
CC       catabolism (By similarity). In vitro, ALDH2 catalyzes the oxidation of
CC       acrolein, malondialdehyde, and 4-hydroxynonenal (4-HNE)
CC       (PubMed:22339434). It also exhibits esterase activity that is enhanced
CC       by NAD+ but does not require it, although the physiological relevance
CC       of this activity remains unclear (PubMed:19506075).
