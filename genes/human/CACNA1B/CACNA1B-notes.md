### Direct human channel evidence

The complete normal abstract of [PMID:1321501](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1321501/) describes human alpha1B-1 coexpression with human beta2 and alpha2b in HEK293 cells. The resulting current is omega-conotoxin-sensitive and dihydropyridine-insensitive. The experiment supports the pore function and channel-complex membership. Its requirement for beta2 and modulation by alpha2b concern that reconstitution; they do not establish one invariant auxiliary-subunit composition in every neuron. The abstract also identifies the two alpha1B sequence forms. The full paper, traces and all isoform comparisons were not inspected.

[PMID:34234349](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34234349/) provides complementary structural evidence. The preserved canonical cache contains only the abstract. Selected unique Results and Methods were also read in the authenticated fuller normal-fetch variant, without replacing that cache. Full-length human Q00975-1 was coexpressed with human alpha2delta1 and beta3 in HEK293F and purified for cryo-EM with or without ziconotide. The source resolves voltage-sensor/pore architecture and calcium coordination by the selectivity-filter glutamates. This supports the channel mechanism and broad calcium interaction; it does not establish a separate soluble calcium-sensor function, a particular EF-hand affinity, or endogenous localization from a detergent preparation. Most of the C-terminal cytosolic region was unresolved in the structure despite expression of the full-length construct. Images, maps and supplementary material were not independently examined. Every YAML quotation from this reference is restricted to its canonical abstract.

The complete cached [Reactome event](https://reactome.org/content/detail/R-HSA-210420) places calcium influx before transmitter-vesicle fusion. The GOA compartment assertion and independent target evidence support plasma membrane; the cached event summary itself supplies pathway context rather than a separate explicit compartment field. Alpha1B performs the calcium-conduction step. It does not perform every downstream fusion reaction. The promoter experiments in PMID:9030575 support neuronal expression; their synaptic-transmission TAS assertion is retained with independent functional evidence, without describing the promoter study as a direct release assay.

### Amyloid response and the unresolved binding assertion

The complete abstract and available indexed primary Results/conclusion of [PMID:23376566](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23376566/) describe synthetic amyloid-beta globulomer modulation of recombinant N/PQ currents, plus protection of slice-culture synaptic function by channel blockers. N-type current activation shifted toward more negative voltages; nonaggregated peptide had no effect. These findings support channel activity, synaptic modulation and a contextual non-core response to amyloid-beta.

[GO:0001540](https://amigo.geneontology.org/amigo/term/GO:0001540) requires binding to amyloid-beta. No target physical-binding assay was established in the inspected material, and the full article and supplements were inaccessible. The original IC assertion remains UNDECIDED rather than being treated as false. Functional current modulation is not by itself proof of direct binding, and an unidentified intermediary is not asserted as fact. The core therefore describes established calcium conduction rather than an endogenous amyloid-receptor role.

### Functional variant data and disease attribution

The complete normal abstract of PMID:25296916 reports WT/R1389H electrophysiology: lower open-channel current with preserved selectivity and voltage-dependent activation/inactivation. That remains direct evidence for the channel activity. The proposed R1389H myoclonus-dystonia association is a separate claim, now assessed against the normally cached [PMID:26157024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26157024/). Its complete abstract and unique Introduction, Results, Discussion and Materials and Methods were read. The study found the variant in one of 520 cases and one of 489 controls, and in none of 146 familial probands. Cases underwent exon-28 Sanger sequencing; the control comparison used whole-exome data. The authors did not support causal association of this specific variant with the disorder. The original disease finding is therefore explicitly DISPUTED, with the negative source attributed within its finding review. This does not invalidate the reported channel-current measurements, settle all CACNA1B-related disease mechanisms, or provide a current clinical classification. Supplementary chromatograms and images were not inspected.
