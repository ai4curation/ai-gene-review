Gene Description

CACNA1B encodes the pore-forming alpha1B subunit of the N-type, high-voltage-activated calcium channel CaV2.2. Its four homologous transmembrane repeats form voltage sensors and a calcium-selective pore. In neuronal plasma membranes, depolarization opens the channel and calcium entry couples electrical activity to neurotransmitter release. Beta and alpha2delta auxiliary subunits influence channel expression and gating, while presynaptic partners and phosphorylation pathways regulate the alpha1B subunit. The channel is sensitive to omega-conotoxins, including the pore blocker ziconotide. Biallelic variants predicted to cause loss of function underlie autosomal recessive neurodevelopmental disorder with seizures and non-epileptic hyperkinetic movements (NEDNEH); impaired calcium influx and synaptic transmission are the proposed disease mechanism.

Core Functions

Beta and alpha2delta subunits support channel expression and gating, and interaction partners regulate this same channel unit. Alpha1B conducts the ion; calcium sensing by release machinery and membrane fusion occur downstream.

CACNA1B functional evidence

2026-10-03: channel mechanism and annotation review

CACNA1B encodes human CaV2.2 alpha1B, the pore-forming subunit of an N-type, high-voltage-activated calcium channel. All 29 normal seeded assertions were assessed, preserving the 30 raw GOA records represented by them: the two assigner/date records for the PPM1A interaction have the same scientific source key and remain one seeded assertion. Both named products, Alpha-1B-1/Q00975-1 and Alpha-1B-2/Q00975-2, retain their exact identities and sequence note. No new annotation is proposed.

The decisions are 18 ACCEPT, 4 KEEP_AS_NON_CORE, 6 MODIFY and 1 UNDECIDED. The modifications refine broad channel activity to the existing high-voltage activity, two broad transport assignments to the existing calcium-import process, two broad membrane assignments to plasma membrane, and one generic interaction to protein-phosphatase binding. One integrated core combines the alpha1B pore, calcium import and synaptic transmission. Calcium binding, phosphorylation-dependent regulation and protein interactions are features of this channel mechanism rather than additional independent cores.

Direct human channel evidence

The complete normal abstract of PMID:1321501(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1321501/) describes human alpha1B-1 coexpression with human beta2 and alpha2b in HEK293 cells. The resulting current is omega-conotoxin-sensitive and dihydropyridine-insensitive. The experiment supports the pore function and channel-complex membership. Its requirement for beta2 and modulation by alpha2b concern that reconstitution; they do not establish one invariant auxiliary-subunit composition in every neuron. The abstract also identifies the two alpha1B sequence forms. The full paper, traces and all isoform comparisons were not inspected.

PMID:34234349(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34234349/) provides complementary structural evidence. The preserved canonical cache contains only the abstract. Selected unique Results and Methods were also read in the authenticated fuller normal-fetch variant, without replacing that cache. Full-length human Q00975-1 was coexpressed with human alpha2delta1 and beta3 in HEK293F and purified for cryo-EM with or without ziconotide. The source resolves voltage-sensor/pore architecture and calcium coordination by the selectivity-filter glutamates. This supports the channel mechanism and broad calcium interaction; it does not establish a separate soluble calcium-sensor function, a particular EF-hand affinity, or endogenous localization from a detergent preparation. Most of the C-terminal cytosolic region was unresolved in the structure despite expression of the full-length construct. Images, maps and supplementary material were not independently examined. Every YAML quotation from this reference is restricted to its canonical abstract.

The complete cached Reactome event places calcium influx before transmitter-vesicle fusion. The GOA compartment assertion and independent target evidence support plasma membrane; the cached event summary itself supplies pathway context rather than a separate explicit compartment field. Alpha1B performs the calcium-conduction step. It does not perform every downstream fusion reaction. The promoter experiments in PMID:9030575 support neuronal expression; their synaptic-transmission TAS assertion is retained with independent functional evidence, without describing the promoter study as a direct release assay.
