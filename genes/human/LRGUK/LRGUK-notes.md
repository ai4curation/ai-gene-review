# LRGUK evidence notes

## Direct human evidence

The strongest human evidence is a 2026 report of one infertile man homozygous for
LRGUK c.1063C>T (p.Arg355Ter). The variant is predicted to remove the C-terminal
GUK-like region: [PMID:42493466, abstract, "The variant, identified by whole-exome sequencing in a cohort of 168 MMAF patients and confirmed by Sanger sequencing, is predicted to result in loss of the C-terminal guanylate kinase-like domain."] Patient sperm lacked detectable LRGUK and showed impaired motility, complete
teratozoospermia, sperm-head and flagellar abnormalities, axonemal disorganization,
and central-pair defects: [PMID:42493466, abstract, "Immunofluorescence showed absence of LRGUK protein in patient spermatozoa, supporting a loss-of-function effect."] [PMID:42493466, abstract, "Morphological and ultrastructural analyzes demonstrated severe defects affecting both sperm head and flagellum, including disorganized axonemal architecture and central pair abnormalities."]

Altered central-apparatus-associated proteins support a human C1b-organization
role, but this is a single-case, abstract-only report and does not establish the
precise molecular activity of LRGUK: [PMID:42493466, abstract, "Immunostaining further indicated alterations of central apparatus-associated proteins, supporting a role for LRGUK in C1b projection organization."]

## Mouse spermiogenesis and cytoskeletal evidence

Mouse genetics directly establishes a requirement for Lrguk-1 across several
steps of sperm assembly. The cached abstract states that LRGUK-1 is required for
acrosome attachment, sperm-head shaping, basal-body attachment, subdistal
appendage formation, axoneme microtubule extension, and manchette organization:
[PMID:25781171, abstract, "Specifically, LRGUK-1 is required for basal body attachment to the plasma membrane, the appropriate formation of the sub-distal appendages, the extension of axoneme microtubules and for microtubule movement and organisation within the manchette."] The mutant males were sterile: [PMID:25781171, results, "Kaos/Kaos males were sterile as a consequence of a specific absence of the LRGUK-1 protein."]

Localization follows the relevant cytoskeletal route during spermiogenesis.
LRGUK moves from the acrosome/acroplaxome to the manchette and is also detected
at the basal body and sperm tail: [PMID:25781171, results, "LRGUK was also evident in the sperm basal body and the sperm tail (Fig. 3E)."] This is direct mouse localization evidence, not direct localization of endogenous human LRGUK.

## HOOK/cargo-adaptor complex and assay boundaries

Mouse LRGUK-1 and HOOK2 were supported by yeast two-hybrid and heterologous
co-immunoprecipitation. The Kaos R528Stop protein lost HOOK2 binding in the
yeast assay: [PMID:25781171, results, "Specific transfection of the full length HOOK2 with LRGUK-1 in a separate yeast two hybid assay confirmed this interaction and the introduction of the R528Stop mutation into the Lrguk-1 sequence completely abolished binding to HOOK2 (Fig. 6A)."] The co-IP used full-length mouse Lrguk-1-GFP expressed in HEK293T cells, so it is not an endogenous spermatid co-IP: [PMID:25781171, results, "An interaction between LRGUK-1 and endogenous HOOK2 was confirmed by co-immunoprecipitation, wherein full-length mouse Lrguk-1-GFP was transfected into HEK293T cells then the complex co-precipitated (Fig. 6B)."]

A follow-up mouse study placed LRGUK1 with HOOK1-3, RIMBP3, and KLC3 in a
manchette-associated transport complex: [PMID:28003339, abstract, "The data indicate that LRGUK1 likely achieves its function in partnership with members of the HOOK family of proteins (HOOK-1-3), Rab3-interacting molecule binding protein (RIMBP)-3 and kinesin light chain (KLC)-3, all of which are associated with intracellular protein transport as cargo adaptor proteins and are localized to the manchette."] The paper's locally cached record is abstract-only, so partner-specific assay details beyond the abstract are not assumed.

The human LRGUK-HOOK2 isoform-2 annotation comes from HuRI binary screening.
It is corroborative at most, not proof of a physiological sperm complex; the
HuRI authors explicitly caution that [PMID:32296183, discussion, "the cellular function of most individual PPIs remains to be elucidated."] The HOOK2 identifier in the annotation is Q96ED9-2, so the screen is also partner-isoform-specific.

## GUK-like domain and catalytic-status boundary

The GUK-like region has an experimentally supported interaction/scaffold role
in mouse LRGUK1: [PMID:28003339, abstract, "In the present study, we showed that the GUK-like domain is essential for binding to HOOK2 and RIMBP3, and the LRR domain is essential for binding to KLC3."] These experiments do not assay GMP phosphorylation. Accordingly, neither catalytic guanylate kinase activity nor a definitive pseudokinase designation is established; "GUK-like interaction domain, catalytic status unresolved" is the defensible boundary.

The human p.Arg355Ter report independently connects loss of the C-terminal
GUK-like region with absent sperm LRGUK and disease, but it does not separate
loss of domain-mediated binding from any possible catalytic contribution.

## Ciliate ortholog and ciliary-transfer boundary

Tetrahymena Cfap246 is a partial ortholog: it is homologous to an N-terminal
fragment of human LRGUK and lacks the C-terminal GUK-like region: [PMID:34083607, results, "In contrast to the much larger human ortholog (94 kDa), Tetrahymena Cfap246 protein lacks the guanylate kinase domain within its C-terminal region (Fig. S1)."] Cfap246 is implicated in the Tetrahymena C1b/C1f region and its knockdown causes only minor ciliary-motility changes: [PMID:34083607, abstract, "Loss of either Cfap246 or Adgb caused only minor alterations in ciliary motility."]

This ciliate result cannot by itself establish a mammalian somatic-cilium or
central-apparatus role. The authors explicitly state: [PMID:34083607, discussion, "It remains to be determined if LRGUK is indeed a C1b/C1f component in mammals."] Direct human sperm evidence published later supports a C1b-associated role in sperm, but it still does not justify unrestricted transfer to all human motile cilia.

## Species and isoform limits

- PMID:25781171 concerns mouse Lrguk-1. The Kaos mutation affects only the longest
  of three predicted mouse transcripts: [PMID:25781171, results, "Lrguk-1 is the longest transcript and the only isoform affected by the mutation (Fig. 1A)."] It does not prove functional equivalence of mouse Lrguk-2 or Lrguk-3.
- PMID:28003339 is also mouse LRGUK1 and is abstract-only in the local cache.
- PMID:34083607 studies Tetrahymena Cfap246, a shorter ortholog without the
  mammalian C-terminal GUK-like domain.
- PMID:42493466 is direct human evidence but a single affected individual, and
  the abstract does not resolve human transcript/isoform usage.
- PMID:32296183 is a human binary-interactome screen; it does not establish
  spermatid context, endogenous complex formation, or a physiological consequence.

## Curation synthesis boundary

The convergent human and mouse evidence supports a conserved core role in
spermatid morphogenesis, manchette/basal-body organization, and sperm-flagellum
axoneme/central-apparatus assembly. HOOK/RIMBP3/KLC3 binding supports a
cytoskeletal cargo-adaptor/scaffold model. Evidence does not currently support
generic protein-binding as an informative molecular function, confirmed GMP
kinase activity, a definitive pseudokinase label, a universal somatic-cilium
function, or unqualified transfer across paralogs and isoforms.
