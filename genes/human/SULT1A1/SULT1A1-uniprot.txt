CC   -!- FUNCTION: Sulfotransferase that utilizes 3'-phospho-5'-adenylyl sulfate
CC       (PAPS) as sulfonate donor to catalyze the sulfate conjugation of a wide
CC       variety of acceptor molecules bearing a hydroxyl or an amine group.
CC       Sulfonation increases the water solubility of most compounds, and
CC       therefore their renal excretion, but it can also result in
CC       bioactivation to form active metabolites. Displays broad substrate
CC       specificity for small phenolic compounds. Plays an important role in
CC       the sulfonation of endogenous molecules such as steroid hormones
CC       (PubMed:12471039, PubMed:16221673, PubMed:21723874, PubMed:22069470,
CC       PubMed:7834621). Mediates the sulfate conjugation of a variety of
CC       xenobiotics, including the drugs acetaminophen and minoxidil (By
CC       similarity). Mediates also the metabolic activation of carcinogenic N-
CC       hydroxyarylamines leading to highly reactive intermediates capable of
CC       forming DNA adducts, potentially resulting in mutagenesis
CC       (PubMed:7834621). May play a role in gut microbiota-host metabolic
CC       interaction. O-sulfonates 4-ethylphenol (4-EP), a dietary tyrosine-
CC       derived metabolite produced by gut bacteria. The product 4-EPS crosses
CC       the blood-brain barrier and may negatively regulate oligodendrocyte
CC       maturation and myelination, affecting the functional connectivity of
CC       different brain regions associated with the limbic system
CC       (PubMed:35165440). Catalyzes the sulfate conjugation of dopamine
CC       (PubMed:8093002). Catalyzes the sulfation of T4 (L-thyroxine/3,5,3',5'-
CC       tetraiodothyronine), T3 (3,5,3'-triiodothyronine), rT3 (3,3',5'-
CC       triiodothyronine) and 3,3'-T2 (3,3'-diiodothyronine), with a substrate
CC       preference of 3,3'-T2 > rT3 > T3 > T4 (PubMed:10199779).
