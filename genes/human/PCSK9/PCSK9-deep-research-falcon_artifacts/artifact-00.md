| Stage/site | Precise molecular role | Strongest experimental evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Endoplasmic reticulum: biosynthesis and maturation | The zymogen autocatalytically cleaves its own **VFAQ¹⁵²↓SIP¹⁵³** bond. Cleavage enables ER exit, but the prodomain remains tightly and noncovalently bound, occluding the catalytic site. Secreted PCSK9 is therefore not an ordinary active protease. The catalytic region begins at Ser153 and may be described as extending to approximately residue 421 or 454, depending on whether the hinge is included. | Pulse–chase experiments resolved precursor and cleaved secreted forms; biochemical and structural studies mapped the mature N terminus to Ser153 and showed the retained prodomain blocking the active site. **Direct biochemical and structural evidence.** (pqac-00000003, pqac-00000015, pqac-00000018) |
| Extracellular space and hepatocyte surface | Secreted PCSK9 functions chiefly as a **ligand and trafficking regulator**, not by proteolytically cleaving LDLR. A surface of its subtilisin-like catalytic domain, separate from the active site, binds the LDLR **EGF-A** repeat. Binding is calcium-dependent and strengthens as pH falls. | Purified-protein binding, receptor mutagenesis and a 2.4 Å PCSK9–EGF-A crystal structure localized the interface; catalytically inactive secreted PCSK9 retained LDLR-degrading activity. Extracellular PCSK9 reduced hepatocyte-surface LDLR, while D374Y was approximately tenfold more active in cells. **Direct structural, biochemical and cellular evidence.** (pqac-00000004, pqac-00000016, pqac-00000017, pqac-00000018) |
| Endosomes and lysosomes | The PCSK9–LDLR complex undergoes clathrin-mediated endocytosis. Acidic conditions favor persistent binding, impair normal LDLR recycling and redirect receptor and ligand to lysosomes. ARH dependence varies by cell type. The C-terminal cysteine/histidine-rich region promotes endocytosis and late-endosomal sorting rather than forming the primary EGF-A interface. | LDLR redistribution to lysosomes was visualized after extracellular PCSK9 treatment. Clathrin-heavy-chain knockdown blocked degradation, whereas inhibition of ubiquitination, proteasomes, macroautophagy or canonical ESCRT machinery did not. ARH loss blocked degradation in some cells but not fibroblasts; imaging and rescue experiments established the C-terminal region’s sorting role. **Direct cell-biological evidence.** (pqac-00000008, pqac-00000013, pqac-00000014, pqac-00000019) |
| Liver and circulation | Hepatocyte-derived PCSK9 lowers surface LDLR abundance, reducing plasma LDL-particle clearance and increasing LDL cholesterol. Hepatic transcription is induced by **SREBP2**, explaining in part why statins can increase PCSK9 while also inducing LDLR. | Liver-specific human-PCSK9 expression in mice produced severe hypercholesterolemia. Parabiosis transferred circulating PCSK9 to wild-type partners and reduced hepatic LDLR to nearly undetectable levels. Hepatic expression studies identified PCSK9 as SREBP regulated. **Direct in-vivo evidence for circulating function; transcriptional regulation supported by primary studies and later synthesis.** (pqac-00000018, pqac-00000019) |
| Ancillary LDLR-family targets | PCSK9 can also promote degradation of **VLDLR** and **ApoER2** experimentally, independently of LDLR and without requiring PCSK9 catalytic activity. These effects do not have evidence equivalent to the canonical human hepatic PCSK9–LDLR pathway. | Co-expression and uptake experiments showed that secreted PCSK9 reduced VLDLR and ApoER2; D374Y was more potent, and membrane tethering enhanced delivery to late endosomes and lysosomes. Evidence comes principally from cultured-cell and animal-expression systems. **Direct experimental evidence, but uncertain human physiological importance.** (pqac-00000012, pqac-00000036) |


*Table: Mechanistic evidence for human PCSK9/Q8NBP7 from autocatalytic maturation through LDLR binding, trafficking and systemic LDL regulation. Canonical functions are distinguished from ancillary receptor effects of less certain human significance.*
