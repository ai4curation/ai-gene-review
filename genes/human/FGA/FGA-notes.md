## 2026-10-26 — Structural participation and fibrinolytic cofactor activity

FGA supplies the alpha-chain component of fibrinogen, whose two alpha, two beta and two gamma chains form a secreted complex. The native chicken structure describes how the chains interlock at the central domain and extend through coiled coils; its alpha-C and amino-terminal peptide regions remain disordered rather than experimentally absent ([PMID:11601975](../../../publications/PMID_11601975.md), printed 12516–12517, purification Methods, central-domain Results and Figures 1–2 captions). The human structural preparation must be described separately because proteolysis during crystallization affects its alpha-C material. Neither structure demonstrates that released fibrinopeptide A is an independent structural member of fibrinogen.

The actual Reactome cleavage event separates intact fibrinogen, the released peptides and fibrin monomer ([R-HSA-140840](../../../reactome/R-HSA-140840.md), entities 114618/140841, 140867 and 140586/140583). Fibrin assembly and cross-linking are different steps: the FGA-derived chain provides structure and polymer contacts, while thrombin performs cleavage and Factor XIIIa supplies cross-linking catalysis ([R-HSA-140842](../../../reactome/R-HSA-140842.md); [R-HSA-140851](../../../reactome/R-HSA-140851.md)). Being cleaved alone is insufficient for process participation. Here structural incorporation supplies the positive participation evidence.

FGA also contributes to fibrinolysis through assembly-dependent cofactor activity. Human recombinant alpha-C oligomers bind both tPA and plasminogen and enhance plasmin generation ([PMID:21806028](../../../publications/PMID_21806028.md), binding and plasmin-generation Methods/Results and Figures 5–8). Soluble monomers are weaker, internally cross-linked monomers are intermediate, and immobilized-fragment assays must not be treated as equivalent to all these states. The activity supports a two-ligand organizing role; tPA performs the proteolytic activation. The authors explicitly contrast their monomer results with an earlier report ([PMID:11170397](../../../publications/PMID_11170397.md)). An untested difference in aggregation state is their proposed explanation, not an experimentally resolved reconciliation. The earlier paper's operative Methods remain inaccessible and that limit is retained.

A later comparison using human plasma material finds lower, but persistent, plasminogen activation after alpha-C truncation ([PMID:37082262](../../../publications/PMID_37082262.md), preparation/activation Methods, plasmin-generation Results and Figure 5). Thus alpha-C contributes to a cofactor function without accounting for every fibrin binding site or all activation. The experimental Aalpha221–610 fragment uses the paper's numbering and is not made into a natural product or silently mapped onto current precursor coordinates.
