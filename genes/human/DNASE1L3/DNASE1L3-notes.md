## 2026-10-17 — source-qualified review

DNASE1L3 hydrolyzes DNA rather than merely being required for DNA disposal. Human recombinant-enzyme work supports the DNase I chemistry, and human expression-supernatant and patient-plasma assays support digestion of nucleosomal and microparticle-associated DNA. The latter assays are not purified-enzyme kinetics. Engineered C-terminal truncations and tags help define substrate access but are not the natural internally deleted product. [PMID:9070308](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9070308/), [PMID:11141064](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11141064/), [PMID:9714828](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9714828/), [PMID:27293190, Results/Figure 5 and recombinant-DNase Methods](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5030815/).

## 2026-10-17 — NET term fit and intracellular DNA cleavage

This entry supersedes the earlier uncertainty-only NET verdicts and the extracellular-only core summary. The [2017 original, Results and Figures 1–4](https://www.sciencemagazinedigital.org/sciencemagazine/01_december_2017?pg=144) demonstrates digestion of already released NETs and protection from obstructive NET clots. G-CSF induces neutrophilia; circulating DNase clears the extracellular substrate. That step does not establish neutrophil activation or directed target-cell killing, so the four transferred annotations are now marked over-annotated. The inaccessible supplementary Methods remain an access limit, not a reason to ignore the demonstrated mechanism. The broad inflammatory-regulation rows remain non-core: less vascular injury during continued neutrophilia does not by itself resolve a narrower negative-regulation step.

Current GO searches found NET formation and symbiont-mediated NET disruption, but no matching host NET-disassembly term. A proposed ontology term records that gap without adding a NEW annotation. Neither formation nor suppression of formation is substituted for degradation. The extracellular core now explicitly includes released NET DNA alongside microparticle-associated chromatin.

A second catalytic context records cleavage of nuclear chromatin in dying cells, using existing GO:0004530 and GO:0006308. [PMID:24312463, Figures 1–4 and Cells and plasmid constructs](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0080223) supplies positive human-DNase transgene results in human cell lines, separately corroborated by mouse knockout and ischemia experiments. The four nucleus annotations now support this core location. Apoptosis-specific assertions remain non-core because CAD cooperation and DNASE1L3 dependence vary with the tested context. ## 2026-10-26 — cell-surface mitochondrial DNA (PMID:42838054)

Porat et al. 2026 (Mol Cell 86:4291–4309; doi:10.1016/j.molcel.2026.09.012) report that mitochondrial DNA is tethered to the surface of select mammalian cells through cell surface proteins and heparan sulfate proteoglycans, and that DNASE1L3 clears it [PMID:42838054 "human and murine B cell surfaces contain DNA that can be cleared by the secreted nuclease DNASE1L3"]. Patients carrying an autoimmunity-associated DNASE1L3 missense variant have more of this DNA on their cells [PMID:42838054 "patients with a DNASE1L3 missense variant associated with increased risk for autoimmune disease harbor increased levels of surface DNA on B and T cells"].

This adds a third extracellular substrate to the microparticle and NET chromatin already in the core function. The same GO:0004530 / GO:0006308 / GO:0005576 core covers it. No new GO annotation is proposed. DNASE1L3 acts on DNA at the cell surface, but the abstract does not show the protein itself residing there, so "cell surface" is not added as a location.

Only the abstract is available (no PMC record; cell.com returned HTTP 403 to automated fetch). The abstract does not name the variant (R206C is the commonly studied risk allele, but that is not stated) or describe the clearance assay, so neither is assumed in the review.
