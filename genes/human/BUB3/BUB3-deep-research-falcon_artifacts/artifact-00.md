| Aspect | Current conclusion | Strongest evidence/experiment | Key caveat |
|---|---|---|---|
| Target identity | The target is **human BUB3 mitotic checkpoint protein**, UniProt **O43684**, not the distinct checkpoint proteins BUB1 or BUB1B/BUBR1. | Protein identity, interaction profile, and spindle-assembly-checkpoint function consistently match the supplied UniProt record for *Homo sapiens*. | Some mechanistic experiments use conserved yeast or other model-organism homologues and must be distinguished from direct human evidence. |
| Structure and family | BUB3 is a **WD40-repeat protein** organized as a **seven-bladed β-propeller**, consistent with the WD-repeat BUB3 family and supplied InterPro/Pfam annotations. | Structural studies reveal a conserved β-propeller surface that binds phosphopeptides and GLEBS-containing partners. | A WD40 fold predicts interaction-scaffold capacity but does not, by itself, establish a specific cellular function. |
| Primary biochemical function | BUB3 is a **non-enzymatic phospho-MELT reader and adaptor**; it neither catalyzes a reaction nor transports a substrate. | Direct binding and structural assays show recognition of MPS1-phosphorylated KNL1 MELT motifs; mutations in the conserved binding interface disrupt kinetochore recruitment and checkpoint activity. | Affinity and recruitment strength vary among MELT repeats and are influenced by adjacent KNL1 sequence elements and partner proteins. |
| BUB1:BUB3 recruitment | Constitutive **BUB1:BUB3** complexes dock on phosphorylated KNL1 MELT motifs at signaling kinetochores, positioning BUB1 to recruit downstream checkpoint machinery. | Mutating BUB3’s phospho-MELT-binding surface abolishes or markedly reduces BUB3 and BUB1 kinetochore localization and produces checkpoint failure. | BUB1 enhances BUB3–phospho-MELT binding; therefore, isolated-BUB3 assays do not fully reproduce recruitment in cells. |
| BUBR1:BUB3 and MCC | A distinct **BUBR1:BUB3** heterodimer becomes part of the mitotic checkpoint complex (MCC), together with closed MAD2 and CDC20. | Biochemical, structural, and cell-based studies identify BUBR1, BUB3, MAD2, and CDC20 as the soluble MCC and distinguish BUBR1:BUB3 from the kinetochore-targeting BUB1:BUB3 complex. | BUBR1:BUB3 is recruited through BUB1-dependent interactions rather than functioning as an equivalent phospho-MELT receptor. |
| APC/C–CDC20 inhibition | In the MCC, BUB3 supports BUBR1-dependent inhibition of **APC/C–CDC20**, delaying securin and cyclin-B destruction and thereby preventing premature anaphase. | Reconstitution and perturbation experiments show that BUB3 promotes BUBR1–CDC20 engagement and efficient checkpoint-dependent APC/C inhibition. | BUB3 is a structural contributor, not the sole inhibitory element; direct APC/C blockade is principally executed through the integrated MCC architecture. |
| Localization and silencing | BUB3 becomes enriched with BUB1 and BUBR1 at unattached or improperly attached kinetochores. Correct microtubule attachment suppresses MPS1 signaling, promotes dephosphorylation and checkpoint-protein removal, and terminates new MCC production. | Imaging and perturbation studies show attachment-sensitive kinetochore accumulation; contemporary models implicate PP1/PP2A-B56, dynein-mediated stripping, and MCC disassembly in silencing. | BUB3-specific residence times, copy numbers, and the exact order of its removal remain less completely quantified than the pathway-level silencing mechanism. |
| 2023 expert consensus | BUB3 has two coordinated functions: **BUB1:BUB3 builds the kinetochore signaling platform**, whereas **BUBR1:BUB3 enters the diffusible MCC** that inhibits APC/C. Kinetochores act as catalytic scaffolds rather than passive docking sites. | Recent structural and cell-biological reviews integrate MPS1–KNL1 signaling, MAD2 conformational conversion, MCC assembly, and attachment-dependent silencing; scaffolded MAD2–CDC20 assembly was accelerated by roughly **35-fold** in vitro. | Several kinetic and spatial details remain unresolved, including quantitative BUB3 partitioning between complexes and how attached kinetochores retain limited MPS1 activity without maintaining a full checkpoint signal. |
| 2023 LUAD biomarker data | In **535** lung-adenocarcinoma cases, high- and low-BUB3 groups comprised **268** and **267** patients. High expression was associated with worse overall survival (**HR 1.7; log-rank p=0.00026**) and mortality of **21.5% versus 14.4%**; disease-specific deaths were **14.6% versus 9.4%**, and progression/failure events were **23.9% versus 18.3%**. | Public-cohort analyses were supplemented by qRT-PCR and immunoblotting in normal bronchial and LUAD cell lines, where BUB3 expression was variably increased. | The study was retrospective and largely database-driven; expression findings differed among platforms, causality was not established, and no BUB3-directed drug, prospective biomarker test, or clinical intervention was evaluated. |
