Gene Description

BUB3 is a 328-residue WD40-repeat protein that folds into a seven-bladed beta-propeller and functions as a non-catalytic adaptor in the spindle assembly checkpoint (SAC). It forms constitutive, mutually exclusive 1:1 complexes with the checkpoint kinases BUB1 and BUB1B (BubR1), each of which binds the top face of the propeller through a short GLEBS motif. During prometaphase the BUB1-BUB3 complex is recruited to unattached or tensionless kinetochores because BUB3 directly reads MPS1/TTK-phosphorylated MELT repeats of the outer-kinetochore protein KNL1; BUB3 is therefore required for kinetochore localisation of BUB1 and, downstream, of BUBR1, MAD1-MAD2 and CDC20, and it builds the platform on which the kinetochore catalyses conversion of MAD2 to its CDC20-bound closed form. A second BUB3 pool, bound to BUBR1, is incorporated with MAD2 and CDC20 into the soluble mitotic checkpoint complex (MCC), the diffusible inhibitor that binds APC/C-CDC20 and blocks ubiquitination of securin and cyclin B until every chromosome is bioriented; within the MCC, BUB3 is a peripheral, flexibly attached subunit that supports BUBR1 rather than contacting the APC/C directly. Through its BUB1 partnership BUB3 also promotes the establishment of stable end-on kinetochore-microtubule attachments and chromosome congression. Its abundance and kinetochore loading are supported by the GLEBS-motif protein ZNF207/BuGZ, it binds the dynein light chain DYNLT3, and it is ubiquitinated by UBR5 to release the MCC from the APC/C during checkpoint silencing. BUB3 is nuclear in interphase and is also required for SAC function and accurate homologue segregation in oocyte meiosis I.

Human BUB3 (UniProt O43684): functional-annotation research report

Executive summary

Identity is verified. The requested target is human BUB3 mitotic checkpoint protein (BUB3; UniProt O43684), not BUB1 or BUB1B/BUBR1. Its reported WD40-repeat, seven-bladed β-propeller architecture agrees with the supplied WD40/YVTN-repeat-like and WD40_PAC1 domain annotations and with membership in the WD-repeat BUB3 family. No conflicting human protein sharing this identity was found. Non-human results below are used only where explicitly identified as conserved mechanistic evidence. (primorac2013bub3readsphosphorylated pages 2-4, primorac2013bub3readsphosphorylated pages 1-2)

BUB3 is not an enzyme or transporter. Its primary function is that of a phosphopeptide-reading interaction adaptor in the spindle-assembly checkpoint (SAC). In a BUB1:BUB3 complex, it binds MPS1-phosphorylated MELT motifs in KNL1 at signaling kinetochores; this builds a platform that recruits additional SAC machinery. In a distinct BUBR1:BUB3 complex, BUB3 becomes part of the soluble mitotic checkpoint complex (MCC; BUBR1–BUB3–MAD2–CDC20), which inhibits APC/C–CDC20 and delays anaphase until chromosome attachment is satisfactory. (primorac2013bub3readsphosphorylated pages 1-2, mcainsh2023principlesanddynamics pages 3-5, fischer2023kinetochorecatalyzedmccformation pages 1-3)

1. Target verification and nomenclature

The supplied description—“mitotic checkpoint protein BUB3,” human, UniProt O43684—matches the literature in four independent respects:

  1. Gene/protein identity: the literature describes human BUB3 as a core SAC protein and constitutive interaction partner of both BUB1 and BUBR1. BUB1 and BUB1B/BUBR1 are separate proteins and should not be conflated with BUB3. (krenn2014insightsintothe pages 27-30, krenn2014insightsintothe pages 30-34)
  2. Organism: the principal annotation here is for Homo sapiens. Some foundational phospho-MELT mutagenesis was performed in budding yeast, but the relevant binding determinants are strongly conserved and later human/vertebrate work supports the same pathway architecture. (primorac2013bub3readsphosphorylated pages 2-4, primorac2013bub3readsphosphorylated pages 9-12)
  3. Protein family and fold: BUB3 is a WD40-repeat protein forming a seven-bladed β-propeller, consistent with the supplied WD40-family InterPro/Pfam assignments. (primorac2013bub3readsphosphorylated pages 2-4, primorac2013bub3readsphosphorylated pages 1-2)
  4. Expected molecular role: WD40 propellers generally organize protein interactions; experimentally, BUB3 uses a conserved propeller surface to recognize phosphorylated KNL1 and associates stoichiometrically with GLEBS/BUB3-binding motifs in BUB1 and BUBR1. (primorac2013bub3readsphosphorylated pages 2-4, krenn2014insightsintothe pages 30-34)

Thus, there is no indication that the symbol is ambiguous in this human context.

2. Primary molecular function

2.1 A non-catalytic phospho-MELT reader

BUB3’s defining biochemical activity is direct recognition of phosphorylated MELT motifs in KNL1, rather than catalysis. During mitosis, MPS1 phosphorylates KNL1 motifs centered on Met–Glu–Leu–Thr. The resulting MELT-P sequences dock onto a conserved hydrophobic/basic surface on the side of BUB3’s β-propeller. Unphosphorylated peptide did not bind in the reported isothermal-titration-calorimetry comparison. (primorac2013bub3readsphosphorylated pages 2-4, primorac2013bub3readsphosphorylated pages 1-2)

This interpretation is supported by loss-of-function experiments. Mutations in the conserved phospho-MELT-binding interface impair BUB3 and BUB1 kinetochore recruitment and cause SAC failure. In conserved yeast experiments, the BUB3 R217A/R239A mutant lost phospho-MELT binding and kinetochore localization; cells carrying this mutant or lacking BUB3 failed to maintain nocodazole-induced arrest and re-replicated DNA, whereas wild-type BUB3 restored checkpoint function. (primorac2013bub3readsphosphorylated pages 12-13, primorac2013bub3readsphosphorylated pages 9-12)

Accordingly, terms such as “substrate specificity” apply only analogically: BUB3’s ligand specificity is for phosphorylated KNL1 MELT-sequence elements, modulated by the neighboring sequence and by its bound partner BUB1. It has no demonstrated catalytic substrate or product.

2.2 Two functionally different BUB3-containing modules

A useful current distinction is:

  • BUB1:BUB3 is primarily the kinetochore-targeting and signaling-platform module. BUB1 binds BUB3 through a GLEBS/BUB3-binding region; BUB1 enhances BUB3 engagement with phospho-MELT KNL1. Individual phosphorylated MELT repeats can recruit BUB1:BUB3, permitting several checkpoint complexes to assemble on one KNL1 molecule when multiple repeats are phosphorylated. (primorac2013bub3readsphosphorylated pages 2-4, primorac2013bub3readsphosphorylated pages 13-14)
  • BUBR1:BUB3 is principally an effector module incorporated into the MCC. BUBR1:BUB3 is recruited downstream of BUB1 and joins MAD2-bound CDC20. BUB3 supports BUBR1–CDC20 engagement and efficient APC/C inhibition, but BUB3 is not itself the sole inhibitory element. (pereira2025germlinevariantsin pages 41-45, wang2022researchprogressof pages 1-2)

BUB1 and BUBR1 therefore are not interchangeable BUB3 partners. BUB1 is recruited first and helps establish the kinetochore catalytic platform; BUBR1:BUB3 acts downstream in the diffusible inhibitory complex. (krenn2014insightsintothe pages 30-34, mcainsh2023principlesanddynamics pages 3-5)

3. Pathway and mechanistic sequence

The best-supported pathway is:

  1. An unattached or improperly attached kinetochore exposes an active checkpoint platform. MPS1 preferentially engages outer-kinetochore NDC80 not occupied by microtubules and phosphorylates KNL1 MELT repeats. MPS1 activity begins approximately 10 minutes before nuclear-envelope breakdown, placing SAC preparation early in mitotic entry. (mcainsh2023principlesanddynamics pages 3-5)
  2. BUB1:BUB3 docks on KNL1. BUB3 directly reads MELT-P; BUB1 stabilizes the interaction and organizes recruitment of BUBR1:BUB3, MAD1:MAD2 and CDC20. In vertebrates, adjacent TxxΩ, SHT and KI elements tune recruitment, and MELT1 has been described as the strongest BUB1:BUB3-recruiting repeat and the only individual MELT repeat capable of sustaining the SAC alone. (fischer2023kinetochorecatalyzedmccformation pages 8-10)
  3. The kinetochore catalyzes MCC production. The BUB1–MAD1:C-MAD2 scaffold positions open MAD2 and CDC20 so that MAD2 converts to its closed CDC20-bound state. Reconstituted scaffold reactions accelerated MAD2–CDC20 association by approximately 35-fold, bringing the reaction into a minutes-scale regime. (fischer2023kinetochorecatalyzedmccformation pages 6-8)
  4. The soluble MCC forms. Closed MAD2–CDC20 associates with BUBR1:BUB3 to form the MCC. The MCC binds and inhibits APC/C–CDC20, preventing ubiquitin-dependent destruction of securin and cyclin B and thereby blocking separase activation, sister-chromatid separation and mitotic exit. (fischer2023kinetochorecatalyzedmccformation pages 1-3, wang2022researchprogressof pages 1-2)
  5. Attachment silences the checkpoint. Microtubule occupancy reduces MPS1 access/activity, KNL1 and other substrates are dephosphorylated, and dynein removes MAD1:MAD2 from attached kinetochores. PP1 and PP2A-B56 participate in reversal of checkpoint phosphorylation; TRIP13-mediated remodeling and APC/C-associated MCC ubiquitination help dismantle pre-existing MCC. APC/C–CDC20 is then released to degrade securin and cyclin B. (fischer2023kinetochorecatalyzedmccformation pages 1-3, mcainsh2023principlesanddynamics pages 8-9)

This mechanism explains BUB3’s broader structural role: it couples phosphorylation state at the outer kinetochore to assembly of a diffusible inhibitor of anaphase onset.

4. Cellular localization and dynamics

BUB3 has two relevant spatial pools:

  • A kinetochore-associated pool, most prominent at unattached, tensionless or improperly attached kinetochores during prometaphase. Here, BUB3 is present in BUB1:BUB3 and downstream BUBR1:BUB3 assemblies on the outer kinetochore/KMN–KNL1 platform. Mutation of the MELT-P-reading interface makes BUB3 diffuse and abolishes BUB1 kinetochore localization in conserved experimental systems. (primorac2013bub3readsphosphorylated pages 12-13, primorac2013bub3readsphosphorylated pages 9-12)
  • A soluble nucleoplasmic/cytosolic mitotic pool, including BUBR1:BUB3 in the diffusible MCC that reaches and inhibits APC/C. MCC production is catalytic: kinetochore components can be recycled to generate multiple soluble inhibitory complexes. (mcainsh2023principlesanddynamics pages 3-5, fischer2023kinetochorecatalyzedmccformation pages 1-3)

Correct kinetochore–microtubule attachment lowers, rather than necessarily instantaneously abolishes, MPS1-dependent signaling. Dephosphorylation and physical stripping reduce BUB-complex occupancy and stop production of new MCC. BUB3-specific residence times and absolute kinetochore copy numbers remain less securely defined than this pathway-level model. (fischer2023kinetochorecatalyzedmccformation pages 8-10, mcainsh2023principlesanddynamics pages 8-9)

5. Current expert understanding and recent developments

Two authoritative 2023 syntheses provide the clearest current framework:

  • McAinsh and Kops, Nature Reviews Molecular Cell Biology, published March 2023, DOI 10.1038/s41580-023-00593-z, emphasize SAC signaling as a dynamic catalytic network. Their synthesis assigns BUB3 coordinated roles in BUB1:BUB3 kinetochore docking and BUBR1:BUB3 MCC formation, rather than viewing it as a generic static checkpoint component. (mcainsh2023principlesanddynamics pages 3-5, mcainsh2023principlesanddynamics pages 8-9)
  • Fischer, IUBMB Life 75:289–310, published 1 April 2023, DOI 10.1002/iub.2697, integrates structural evidence showing how the kinetochore concentrates and repositions SAC components to overcome the kinetic barrier to MAD2–CDC20 assembly. The review defines the MCC as BUBR1:BUB3 plus MAD2:CDC20 and describes phosphatase-, dynein- and TRIP13-dependent silencing. (fischer2023kinetochorecatalyzedmccformation pages 1-3, fischer2023kinetochorecatalyzedmccformation pages 6-8)

A central expert interpretation is therefore that the kinetochore is not merely a BUB3 docking site. It is a catalytic assembly surface, and BUB3 is the molecular reader that links MPS1-generated KNL1 phosphorylation to that surface. Important unresolved questions include quantitative partitioning of BUB3 between BUB1 and BUBR1 complexes, the exact phosphatases acting on individual MPS1 substrates, the detailed role of PLK1, and how residual MPS1 at attached kinetochores participates in error correction without maintaining a full checkpoint signal. (fischer2023kinetochorecatalyzedmccformation pages 8-10, mcainsh2023principlesanddynamics pages 8-9)

6. Biological importance and experimental evidence hierarchy

The most precise evidence is not simply correlative expression data. It includes:

  • Direct biochemistry: selective binding of BUB3/BUB1:BUB3 to MELT-P but not the unphosphorylated counterpart. (primorac2013bub3readsphosphorylated pages 2-4)
  • Structural inference plus conservation: a seven-bladed WD40 propeller bearing a highly conserved phosphopeptide-recognition interface. (primorac2013bub3readsphosphorylated pages 1-2)
  • Structure-guided mutagenesis: disruption of conserved BUB3 interface residues eliminates phospho-MELT binding and kinetochore localization. (primorac2013bub3readsphosphorylated pages 12-13, primorac2013bub3readsphosphorylated pages 9-12)
  • Cellular checkpoint assays: mutant or absent BUB3 causes failure to arrest after spindle disruption and permits inappropriate cell-cycle progression. (primorac2013bub3readsphosphorylated pages 12-13, primorac2013bub3readsphosphorylated pages 9-12)
  • Pathway reconstitution: kinetochore-associated SAC scaffolds accelerate MAD2–CDC20 assembly by about 35-fold, explaining how BUB3-dependent recruitment contributes to physiologically rapid MCC production. (fischer2023kinetochorecatalyzedmccformation pages 6-8)

Collectively, these data establish causality for BUB3’s core SAC role far more strongly than transcriptomic associations alone.

7. Disease relevance and current applications

7.1 Chromosomal instability and congenital disease

Because BUB3 helps prevent premature anaphase, reduced or mistimed function is expected to weaken the SAC, increase chromosome mis-segregation and promote aneuploidy. Open Targets links human BUB3 (ENSG00000154473, approved name “BUB3 mitotic checkpoint protein”) to mosaic variegated aneuploidy syndrome, thyroid cancer and several complex traits. These are database-level target–disease associations, however, and their scores—approximately 0.370 for mosaic variegated aneuploidy syndrome and 0.169 for thyroid cancer in the retrieved results—should not be interpreted as proof that BUB3 is a validated causal or druggable target in each condition. (OpenTargets Search: -BUB3)

7.2 Cancer biomarker research

The strongest recent BUB3-specific quantitative application evidence retrieved was a 2023 lung-adenocarcinoma study. Li et al., published February 2023 in Aging, DOI 10.18632/aging.204517, analyzed 535 patients divided into low-BUB3 (n=267) and high-BUB3 (n=268) groups. High expression was associated with advanced T stage (p<0.001), pathological stage (p=0.016), smoking status (p=0.024), and shorter overall survival (HR 1.7; log-rank p=0.00026). Mortality was 21.5% versus 14.4%, disease-specific death 14.6% versus 9.4%, and progression/failure 23.9% versus 18.3% in high- versus low-expression groups. (li2023comprehensiveanalysisof pages 11-13)

The same study used qRT-PCR and immunoblotting in normal bronchial epithelial cells and LUAD lines including A549, H1299, PC9 and H460, finding variably increased BUB3 mRNA and protein. TIMER analysis associated BUB3 expression with CD8+ T-cell, B-cell and neutrophil estimates. Nevertheless, expression differences were platform-dependent: TIMER found a significant LUAD–normal difference whereas the reported TCGA/GTEx comparison did not, and one pan-cancer Oncomine analysis was also non-significant for lung cancer. (li2023comprehensiveanalysisof pages 1-2, li2023comprehensiveanalysisof pages 2-6)

Accordingly, BUB3 is currently best described as an exploratory prognostic and pathway biomarker, not a clinically implemented diagnostic or treatment-selection test. The LUAD work was retrospective and database-heavy, used cell lines rather than functional validation in patient-derived tumors, did not establish that BUB3 overexpression causes aggressive disease, and did not test a BUB3-directed therapy. (li2023comprehensiveanalysisof pages 15-17, li2023comprehensiveanalysisof pages 13-15)

7.3 Therapeutic status

No validated BUB3-selective drug, prospective biomarker implementation or BUB3-directed clinical trial was identified in the retrieved evidence. Pharmacologically disrupting a ubiquitous chromosome-segregation checkpoint also carries an obvious therapeutic-index concern in normal proliferating tissues. Current real-world use is therefore mainly as:

  • a research marker of SAC/kinetochore activity;
  • a component measured in studies of mitotic arrest and antimitotic-drug response;
  • a candidate prognostic marker in retrospective tumor cohorts; and
  • a mechanistic node in experimental strategies aimed at chromosome-instability vulnerabilities.

Claims that BUB3 is already an established cancer therapeutic target would exceed the evidence. One prominently indexed lncRNA–BUB3 cancer report was retracted and was therefore not used as affirmative support.

8. Conclusions for functional annotation

Human BUB3/O43684 should be annotated primarily as a WD40 β-propeller phosphopeptide reader and mitotic-checkpoint adaptor. Its central ligand is MPS1-phosphorylated KNL1 MELT motifs, recognized in the context of BUB1:BUB3. At unattached kinetochores this interaction organizes a catalytic SAC platform. A separate BUBR1:BUB3 module enters the MCC with MAD2 and CDC20, enabling inhibition of APC/C–CDC20 and delaying securin/cyclin-B degradation until chromosome attachment is complete. BUB3 acts principally at the outer kinetochore and in soluble mitotic checkpoint complexes; it has no established enzymatic reaction, catalytic substrate or transported substrate. (primorac2013bub3readsphosphorylated pages 1-2, mcainsh2023principlesanddynamics pages 3-5, fischer2023kinetochorecatalyzedmccformation pages 1-3)

The mechanistic annotation is supported by direct binding, structural, mutational, imaging, spindle-poison and reconstitution experiments. By contrast, disease-biomarker and therapeutic applications remain preliminary: recent LUAD data are quantitatively suggestive but observational, and clinical utility has not been demonstrated. (fischer2023kinetochorecatalyzedmccformation pages 6-8, li2023comprehensiveanalysisof pages 11-13, li2023comprehensiveanalysisof pages 13-15)

BUB3 (mitotic checkpoint protein BUB3, UniProt O43684) — review notes

Sources used: BUB3-uniprot.txt, BUB3-goa.tsv (68 seeded rows), the Edison/falcon deep
research report (BUB3-deep-research-falcon.md), the cached publications for every
original_reference_id, and QuickGO for term definitions. Exemplars followed:
genes/human/MAD2L1 and genes/SCHPO/bub1.

What the protein is

BUB3 is a 328-aa WD40-repeat protein (UniProt lists seven WD repeats; Taylor et al. counted
four by the 1998 criteria) that forms a seven-bladed beta-propeller. It has no catalytic
activity. Everything it does it does by binding two kinds of ligand on the top face of the
propeller:

  • GLEBS motifs of BUB1, BUB1B/BUBR1 and ZNF207/BuGZ, giving constitutive 1:1 complexes
    PMID:24462187 PMID:24462186.
  • MPS1-phosphorylated MELT repeats of KNL1, which is how BUB1-BUB3 is docked at
    unattached kinetochores (Primorac et al. 2013, eLife, summarised in the deep research:
    "In a BUB1:BUB3 complex, it binds MPS1-phosphorylated MELT motifs in KNL1 at signaling
    kinetochores; this builds a platform that recruits additional SAC machinery.").

The founding human paper is Taylor, Ha & McKeon 1998. GFP-BUB3 is nuclear in interphase
PMID:9660858 and concentrates on kinetochores before alignment PMID:9660858. The same paper showed that the BUB3-binding region of BUB1 is the kinetochore
targeting region PMID:9660858 and that BUBR1 reaches kinetochores only with excess BUB3
PMID:9660858. This is the direct basis of GO:0034501 protein localization to kinetochore and of my choice of GO:0140483 kinetochore adaptor activity as the
primary molecular function.

Two BUB3 modules

  1. BUB1-BUB3 = kinetochore targeting / signalling platform (GO:1990298 bub1-bub3 complex, IBA). In vitro BUB1 and BUB3 form a 1:1 complex that binds and phosphorylates
    MAD1 PMID:10198256. The kinase activity belongs to BUB1; UniProt's FUNCTION text about "the
    BUB1/BUB3 complex ... phosphorylating its activator CDC20" (PMID:15525512) should not be
