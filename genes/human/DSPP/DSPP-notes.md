# DSPP evidence notes

DSPP is a secreted precursor whose processed products contribute to the non-collagenous dentin matrix. The integrated matrix function includes DPP calcium/collagen interactions and dentin formation. The [source and assay guide](DSPP-source-guide.md) separates experimental species, processed products, original-paper access and repository cache availability.

## Product and timing boundaries

The [official Q9NZW4 record](https://www.uniprot.org/uniprotkb/Q9NZW4/entry) contains one displayed 1301-residue sequence and no alternative-products block. Signal peptide 1–15 is predicted; the annotated chains are 16–1301 (PRO_0000021120), DSP 16–462 (PRO_0000021121) and DPP 463–1301 (PRO_0000021122). These are processing annotations, not three splice isoforms. Cleavage by another enzyme does not confer protease activity on DSPP.

The historical [1997 precursor study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8995371/) combines a mouse full cDNA with a partial human mapping probe. The [1998 human sequence paper](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9879917/) supplies human molar sequence context; its complete original was not recovered. Neither title alone establishes a modern full-length human functional assay. Rat phosphophoryn biochemistry in [PMID:16046405](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16046405/) directly supports calcium/collagen association and mineral behavior. Its native-versus-fragment comparison changes both length and phosphorylation, so neither variable is isolated.

The [2025 mouse study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12246090/) compares wild type, Dspp null and a DSP-containing allele with truncated DPP. Initial mineral formation persists in the tested mutants; later circumpulpal dentin quantity and uniformity are impaired. The allele is not a clean isolated DSP reagent or a human splice product. The proposed DSP calcium-retention mechanism remains a model. These findings qualify obligatory initiation claims while supporting the established matrix role.

## Intracellular and developmental contexts

Positive nuclear staining in [mouse teeth](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2664930/) and [rat RUB1 cells](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3642346/) supports a contextual location, not a demonstrated transcriptional activity. The human developing-tooth localization source [PMID:11856645](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11856645/) remains supported by its authentic abstract and curator judgment; its full intracellular panel was not recovered. Intracellular pools are retained without calling them soluble cytosol or assigning matrix mineralization to them. No subcellularLocation record was present in the inspected exact HPA target entry; that absence is not negative localization evidence.

The rat [M2H4 study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2020511/) measures DSPP as a late differentiation marker. Independent [mouse-null evidence](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4235594/) supports a developmental context, with altered lineage behavior rather than a simple absence of initial odontoblast formation. The regulation ISS cites [PMID:15308669](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15308669/), whose inspected text concerns regulation of DSPP transcription; its exact protein-to-process attribution remains unresolved. Independent [DPP cellular signaling](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3285302/) supports the retained gene-level context. It does not retroactively validate an unseen assay in the earlier paper.

## Human genetics and source attribution follow-up (2026-10-18)

Human DSPP disease is supported independently of the rodent binding experiments. In [PMID:18521831](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5534847/), Methods (Kindreds and Genomic DNA; DSPP Mutation Analysis in Early Exons; Cloning and Sequencing of DSPP Repeat Domain) and Results (Patient Description and Mutational Analysis) describe families with autosomal dominant dentinogenesis imperfecta or dentin dysplasia, heterozygous variants, segregation in available relatives and control chromosomes. The paper treats the relationship between frameshift position and severity as a trend requiring more cases, not a universal rule. The reviewed [human UniProt record](https://www.uniprot.org/uniprotkb/Q9NZW4/entry) distinguishes DGI1/DFNA39, DGI2, DGI3 and DTDP2; the newly examined family study supports the dentin-disease spectrum without independently re-establishing every subtype or the hearing phenotype. Genetic disease association supports human dentin biology, not a direct human calcium-binding, collagen-binding or localization assay.

[PMID:22392858](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3390460/) provides a distinct mechanistic experiment. Methods (Expression Constructs; Cell culture and transfection; Quantitative ELISA; Immunocytochemistry) identify the first 31 human amino acids fused to mouse DSPP or GFP, additional engineered frameshift models and HEK293A host cells. Results and Figure 6 report reduced secretion of coexpressed normal DSPP when mutant constructs are added. This supports a dominant-negative trafficking model within the tested system. It is not an assay of the complete current natural human product, and calcium-mediated aggregation remains a mechanistic interpretation: the authors lacked sufficient purified material for the proposed calcium precipitation experiment. Mutant accumulation in membranes continuous with rough ER, including nuclear membrane in HEK293A, is distinct from the normal rodent nuclear DPP observations and does not establish a physiological human nuclear function.
