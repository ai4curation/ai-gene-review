CC   -!- FUNCTION: ATP-dependent transporter of the ATP-binding cassette (ABC)
CC       family that hydrolyzes ATP to enable active transport of substrates
CC       conjugated with glucuronate, glutathione or sulfate. Operates at
CC       basolateral membranes of polarized cells such as hepatocytes and
CC       intestinal epithelial cells, where it mediates the efflux of conjugated
CC       substrates from intestine and liver into the bloodstream
CC       (PubMed:10359813, PubMed:11581266, PubMed:15083066, PubMed:29175180,
CC       PubMed:30959153, PubMed:31704245, PubMed:39993711, PubMed:37485728).
CC       Involved in enterohepatic recirculation of steroid glucuronates. Shares
CC       substrate specificity with MRP2, displaying higher affinity for both
CC       estrogen and androgen glucuronates such as 17beta-estradiol, estrone,
CC       testosterone and 5alpha-dihydrotestosterone glucuronates
CC       (PubMed:29175180, PubMed:30959153, PubMed:31704245, PubMed:37485728).
CC       Involved in sinusoidal efflux of mono- and bis-glucuronidated bilirubin
CC       from hepatocytes into the blood for renal uptake and excretion
CC       (PubMed:15083066). May selectively transport steroid sulfate
CC       conjugates. Has transporter activity toward androgen sulfates such as
CC       androsterone and dehydroepiandrosterone sulfates but is inactive toward
CC       estrone 3-sulfate (PubMed:29175180, PubMed:39993711). Can transport
CC       various bile acid conjugates such as taurocholate, glycocholate,
CC       taurochenodeoxycholate-3-sulfate and taurolithocholate-3-sulfate. Does
CC       not contribute substantially to bile acids physiology, but provides an
CC       alternative route for the export of bile acids and glucuronate
CC       conjugates from cholestatic hepatocytes to avoid liver damage (By
CC       similarity). Can also transport glutathione conjugates such as
CC       leukotriene C4 (LTC4) (PubMed:11581266). Transports various
CC       xenobiotics. Confers resistance to anticancer drugs, methotrexate,
CC       tenoposide and etoposide, by decreasing accumulation of these drugs in
CC       cells (PubMed:10359813, PubMed:11581266). May contribute to the
CC       transport of organic compounds in testes across the blood-testis-
