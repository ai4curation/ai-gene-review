| **Identity and molecular class** | Human **CENP-F**, encoded by *CENPF*; UniProt **P49454**; 3,210-aa, elongated coiled-coil-rich, microtubule-binding protein | Accessions, sequence length and C-terminal interaction regions are explicitly mapped in biochemical studies and reviews (pqac-00000000, pqac-00000002) | **High.** CENP-F is a non-enzymatic microtubule-associated scaffold/adaptor and force transducer. | **Kinetochore corona** | Direct binding of a dimeric C-terminal CENP-F coiled coil to the **BUB1 kinase domain** recruits CENP-F independently of the RZZ corona complex; CENP-E can contribute under some conditions | Biochemical reconstitution, chromatography, mutagenesis and cell-localization assays mapped the interaction to approximately residues 2,866–2,990; BUB1 was stringently required (pqac-00000001, pqac-00000002) | **High** for direct BUB1 binding and corona localization. The relative contribution of CENP-E varies with experimental conditions and microtubule status |
| **Kinetochore–microtubule attachment and corona stripping** | Terminal microtubule-binding domains contact different lattice conformations; the **NDE1/NDEL1–LIS1–dynein** axis allows CENP-F to stabilize attachments and restrain dynein-mediated removal of CENP-E, Spindly, MAD2 and ZWILCH | CENP-F mutants produced unaligned chromosomes in about **60%** of cells versus **20%** of controls; an NDE1-binding fragment rescued congression to about **25%** unaligned versus **19%** in controls, whereas a fragment lacking that region did not (≥3 replicates; 100 cells/condition) (pqac-00000008, pqac-00000018) | **High** for attachment stabilization and limiting “over-stripping.” **Moderate/low** for CENP-F being a principal dynein recruiter: 2023 expert review notes dynein–dynactin recruitment is largely retained after CENP-F loss and mitotic fidelity can be minimally affected, implying redundancy and context dependence (pqac-00000016) |
| **Outer nuclear envelope / nuclear pores in late G2–prophase** | CENP-F binds the nucleoporin **NUP133** and participates in recruiting dynein to the outer nuclear envelope before nuclear-envelope breakdown | Cell-cycle localization and interaction mapping place CENP-F in the nucleus during S/G2, at NUP133-containing nuclear pores in late G2/prophase, and subsequently at prometaphase kinetochores (pqac-00000000, pqac-00000005, pqac-00000019) | **Moderate–high.** Localization and NUP133 dependence are well supported; the exact regulation by farnesylation, phosphorylation, nuclear export and pore remodeling remains incompletely resolved |
| **Mitochondria during late mitosis/cytokinesis** | Direct or selective association with mitochondrial GTPases **MIRO1/MIRO2** links mitochondria to microtubules and promotes mitochondrial-network spreading | A CRISPR-engineered Miro-binding-deficient CENP-F mutant lost mitochondrial localization and impaired mitochondrial spreading in cultured human cells (pqac-00000005, pqac-00000019) | **High** for the cultured-cell localization mechanism; **lower physiological indispensability** because mutant mice developed normally, even when a truncation reduced CENP-F expression by approximately **80%** (pqac-00000005, pqac-00000019) |
| **Centrosome, basal body and primary cilium** | CENP-F colocalizes and co-immunoprecipitates with **IFT88** and **KIF3B**; it co-elutes with IFT-B proteins, dynein intermediate chain and KIF3A, suggesting a ciliary transport complex | Microscopy, reciprocal co-immunoprecipitation and gel filtration showed centrosomal/axonemal localization and physical association; CENPF-mutant fetal kidneys had shorter cilia and failed to recruit IFT88 to residual axonemes (pqac-00000006, pqac-00000020) | **Moderate–high.** Strong association and patient-tissue evidence support a ciliary role, but direct molecular action and whether all interactions are direct remain unresolved |
| **Human developmental disease** | Biallelic loss-of-function variants disrupt mitotic, centrosomal and/or ciliary functions | Exome sequencing identified segregating truncating/splice variants in families with lethal ciliopathy or microcephaly; the recognized recessive phenotype includes **Strømme syndrome**, often featuring microcephaly, intestinal atresia and ciliopathy-related abnormalities (pqac-00000005, pqac-00000019, pqac-00000021) | **High** for gene–disease association. Phenotypic severity is variable, and the relative contributions of kinetochore versus ciliary dysfunction are not settled |
| **Cancer biomarker and target research, 2023–2024** | Elevated *CENPF* commonly tracks proliferative/G2–M programs; knockdown can induce G2 arrest and reduce proliferation, invasion or EMT-associated phenotypes | A 2024/2025 glioma study analyzed TCGA **1,097 tumors/5 controls**, GSE111260 **67/3**, and GSE16011 **276/8**, then reported knockdown effects in U87/U251 cells and mouse xenografts (pqac-00000012, pqac-00000013). A 2024 lung-adenocarcinoma analysis selected CENPF with BUB1B and PLK1 by random forest/LASSO; CENPF expression differed at **P=2.3×10⁻⁵**, but the authors acknowledged small samples, database dependence and no protein-level validation (pqac-00000014, pqac-00000015) | **Investigational only; low–moderate translational confidence.** No evidence establishes CENP-F as a clinically validated diagnostic, prognostic assay or approved drug target. Expression may chiefly report proliferation rather than a cancer-specific dependency, and current studies require prospective, independent validation |
