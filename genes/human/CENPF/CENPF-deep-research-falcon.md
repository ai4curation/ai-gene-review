# Human CENPF (CENP-F; UniProt P49454): Functional-Annotation Research Report

## Executive summary

**Identity is verified.** The target is human **CENPF**, encoding **centromere protein F (CENP-F)**, also called **mitosin**, **AH antigen**, or **kinetochore protein CENPF**. UniProt **P49454** corresponds to a 3,210-residue, highly elongated, coiled-coil-rich protein. This matches the supplied CENP-F-family and C-terminal retinoblastoma-protein-binding domain annotations. It is not CENP-E: CENP-E is a distinct ATP-hydrolysing kinesin motor (UniProt Q02224), whereas CENP-F is principally a **non-enzymatic microtubule-associated scaffold/adaptor and force-transducing protein** (berto2018regulationofcenpf pages 22-26, ciossani2018thekinetochoreproteins pages 7-8).

The best-supported primary function is at the **outer kinetochore/corona during mitosis**, where CENP-F binds microtubules, helps stabilize kinetochore–microtubule attachments, and regulates the dynein-dependent removal of corona proteins. Its direct recruitment receptor is chiefly **BUB1**. CENP-F also has cell-cycle-specific pools at nuclear pores/outer nuclear envelope, mitochondria, centrosomes, and primary cilia. These additional localizations connect it to nuclear positioning, mitochondrial redistribution, and ciliary transport, but several functions are redundant or context-dependent rather than universally essential (ciossani2018kinetochorerecruitmentof pages 1-4, auckland2020cenpfstabilizeskinetochoremicrotubule pages 18-19, peterka2019mirodependentmitochondrialpool pages 1-2, waters2015thekinetochoreprotein pages 8-9).

## 1. Identity, architecture, and molecular class

Human CENP-F/P49454 is a 3,210-amino-acid protein with extensive predicted coiled coils and comparatively disordered terminal regions. Electron microscopy of a C-terminal fragment showed an approximately 40-nm rod consistent with a parallel dimeric coiled coil. The protein contains terminal microtubule-binding regions, nuclear-localization and farnesylation determinants, a KEN-box-associated degradation region, and binding regions for BUB1, CENP-E, NDE1/NDEL1, NUP133, MIRO, and retinoblastoma protein (berto2018regulationofcenpf pages 22-26, ciossani2018thekinetochoreproteins pages 7-8).

CENP-F has no established catalytic reaction, substrate turnover, or intrinsic motor ATPase. Its functional analogy is therefore an elongated, multivalent **microtubule-binding adapter/scaffold**, not an enzyme or transporter. This distinction is important because the similarly named **CENP-E** is a plus-end-directed kinesin motor. The two proteins are probable structural paralogues, but CENP-F binds BUB1 while CENP-E preferentially binds BUBR1; CENP-E’s motor domain is absent from CENP-F (ciossani2018kinetochorerecruitmentof pages 1-4, ciossani2018thekinetochoreproteins pages 7-8).

The supplied InterPro/Pfam annotations—Cenp-F, CENP-F/LEK1 Rb-binding, leucine-rich repeat, CENP-F N-terminal, and C-terminal Rb-binding modules—are therefore compatible with the literature’s picture of a long, coiled-coil-rich interaction platform with specialized terminal modules. Most detailed interaction mapping has focused on the C-terminal third rather than on a complete atomic structure.

## 2. Primary function at kinetochores

### Recruitment and organization

CENP-F occupies the fibrous **kinetochore corona**, the outermost, transient kinetochore layer assembled in early mitosis before stable end-on microtubule attachment. Biochemical reconstitution showed that its C-terminal dimeric coiled coil binds directly to the kinase domain of **BUB1**. BUB1 is stringently required for kinetochore recruitment, whereas this recruitment is independent of the principal corona-forming ROD–ZWILCH–ZW10 complex. Interaction/localization determinants were narrowed to approximately residues 2,866–2,990; Cys2961 contributes to the interaction (ciossani2018kinetochorerecruitmentof pages 1-4, ciossani2018thekinetochoreproteins pages 7-8).

Some studies describe synergistic input from CENP-E, especially after microtubule depolymerization, but direct BUB1 binding is the clearest core recruitment mechanism. The mapped CENP-E-binding region, residues 1,804–2,104, is spatially distinct from the BUB1-binding coiled coil, allowing the interactions to coexist (ciossani2018thekinetochoreproteins pages 7-8, peterka2019mirodependentmitochondrialpool pages 1-2).

### Microtubule attachment and force transmission

CENP-F contains terminal microtubule-binding domains with preferences for different microtubule geometries: one favors curved structures and the other the straight lattice. This supports a model in which an elongated CENP-F dimer couples corona organization to microtubule attachment and transmits or buffers mechanical load. Depletion and domain-disruption experiments have produced unstable microtubule capture, reduced inter-kinetochore tension, chromosome misalignment, and spindle-checkpoint activation, although the severity varies among systems (berto2018regulationofcenpf pages 20-22, auckland2020cenpfstabilizeskinetochoremicrotubule pages 9-10).

A 2020 separation-of-function study provides useful quantitative evidence. Approximately **60%** of two CENP-F-mutant cell lines retained unaligned chromosomes versus approximately **20%** of controls. A fragment retaining the kinetochore-targeting and NDE1-binding regions rescued the phenotype to approximately **25%** unaligned cells, near the **19%** control value after monastrol release; a fragment lacking the NDE1-binding region did not rescue. These experiments used at least three replicates and 100 cells per condition (auckland2020cenpfstabilizeskinetochoremicrotubule pages 9-10).

### Dynein regulation and corona disassembly

CENP-F binds **NDE1/NDEL1**, which interact with LIS1 and cytoplasmic dynein. This places CENP-F in a pathway that coordinates microtubule attachment with dynein-dependent corona remodeling. After end-on attachment, kinetochore dynein strips corona cargoes—including CENP-E, MAD2, SPINDLY, and ZWILCH—toward spindle poles. CENP-F restrains excessive stripping and thereby helps retain an appropriate corona composition during error correction and chromosome alignment (auckland2020cenpfstabilizeskinetochoremicrotubule pages 18-19, auckland2020cenpfstabilizeskinetochoremicrotubule pages 9-10).

The current expert interpretation is more nuanced than a simple “CENP-F recruits dynein” model. A 2023 review concluded that dynein–dynactin recruitment remains largely intact after CENP-F loss; CENP-F-null kinetochores lose NDE1 but retain NDEL1 and some LIS1. CENP-F loss can also have negligible effects on overall mitotic duration and fidelity in some human and mouse cells. Thus, CENP-F is best viewed as a **modulator or tether/brake within a redundant dynein network**, rather than the sole kinetochore dynein receptor (gassmann2023dyneinatthe pages 9-10).

## 3. Cell-cycle expression and spatial itinerary

CENP-F abundance and localization are tightly cell-cycle regulated. It is generally undetectable in G1, accumulates in the nucleus during S/G2, appears at nuclear pores and the outer nuclear envelope in late G2/early prophase, relocates to kinetochores in prometaphase, and is rapidly degraded after mitosis. KEN-box/Cdc20-dependent proteolysis and C-terminal farnesylation have been implicated in its post-anaphase turnover and G2/M behavior (peterka2019mirodependentmitochondrialpool pages 1-2, berto2018regulationofcenpf pages 20-22).

At the **outer nuclear envelope**, CENP-F binds the NUP107–160-complex component **NUP133** and contributes to dynein recruitment before nuclear-envelope breakdown. Proposed regulatory inputs include CENP-F dimerization, farnesylation, CDK1-dependent control of nuclear import/export, and mitotic nucleoporin phosphorylation, although the relative contributions remain incompletely resolved (berto2018regulationofcenpf pages 22-26, peterka2019mirodependentmitochondrialpool pages 1-2).

This itinerary explains why CENP-F is often used experimentally as a G2/mitotic marker, but it should not be interpreted as a constitutive centromere component: unlike the constitutive centromere-associated network, CENP-F is transient and strongly cell-cycle dependent.

## 4. Additional functional pools

### Mitochondria

During late mitosis/cytokinesis, CENP-F associates with mitochondrial **MIRO1/MIRO2** GTPases and links mitochondria to the microtubule cytoskeleton, promoting redistribution and network spreading. In human cells, a CRISPR-engineered mutation that selectively blocked MIRO binding abolished mitochondrial CENP-F localization and produced a mitochondrial-spreading defect resembling MIRO depletion (peterka2019mirodependentmitochondrialpool pages 1-2).

Organism-level evidence indicates substantial redundancy. Mice carrying the MIRO-binding-deficient allele or C-terminal truncations developed normally; one truncation reduced CENP-F expression by approximately **80%** without an overt developmental phenotype. The mitochondrial function is therefore experimentally real in cultured cells but is not universally indispensable in vivo (peterka2019mirodependentmitochondrialpool pages 1-2).

### Centrosomes and primary cilia

CENP-F localizes to centrioles/subdistal appendages and ciliary axonemes. It colocalizes with **IFT88** and **KIF3B**, co-immunoprecipitates reciprocally with IFT88, and co-elutes with IFT-B proteins, dynein intermediate chain, and KIF3A. These findings support membership in, or close association with, a ciliary transport assembly rather than proving that every component binds CENP-F directly (waters2015thekinetochoreprotein pages 8-9).

In CENPF-mutant human fetal kidneys, remaining cilia were shorter and IFT88 failed to localize along GT335-positive axonemes. This combines patient-tissue pathology with biochemical and cell-localization evidence and supports a function in ciliogenesis or intraciliary cargo organization (waters2015thekinetochoreprotein pages 8-9).

## 5. Pathways and interaction network

CENP-F’s central pathway can be summarized as follows:

1. **Cell-cycle production and localization:** CENPF expression rises during S/G2; CENP-F accumulates in the nucleus and at NUP133-containing nuclear pores.
2. **Kinetochore recruitment:** the KNL1/BUB3/BUB1 checkpoint axis positions BUB1, whose kinase domain directly binds the CENP-F C-terminal coiled coil.
3. **Microtubule interface:** terminal microtubule-binding domains engage spindle microtubules and influence attachment stability and tension.
4. **Dynein/corona regulation:** CENP-F-associated NDE1/NDEL1–LIS1 interfaces with dynein; CENP-F limits premature or excessive stripping of checkpoint and congression factors.
5. **Attachment maturation:** as biorientation proceeds and BUB1/CENP-F decline, dynein removes corona material, coordinating attachment status with spindle-assembly-checkpoint silencing.
6. **Late-mitotic organelle redistribution and turnover:** MIRO-dependent mitochondrial recruitment occurs around cytokinesis, followed by postmitotic proteolysis.

A proposed CENP-F–ATR–CHK1–Aurora-B connection has also been reported, but its generality is less securely established than the BUB1, microtubule, and NDE1/dynein mechanisms; conflicting CENP-F-loss phenotypes argue for context-specific or redundant contributions (peterka2019mirodependentmitochondrialpool pages 1-2).

## 6. Human disease relevance

Biallelic CENPF variants cause a spectrum including **Strømme syndrome**, severe fetal ciliopathy, and primary microcephaly. Characteristic manifestations can include microcephaly, intestinal atresia, and renal or other ciliopathy-associated abnormalities. Exome studies identified segregating splice/truncating alleles in affected families, and functional studies connected these variants to shortened cilia and defective IFT88 localization (peterka2019mirodependentmitochondrialpool pages 1-2, waters2015thekinetochoreprotein pages 8-9, waters2015thekinetochoreprotein pages 1-2).

The mechanistic basis probably combines ciliary/centrosomal dysfunction with defects in microtubule organization and proliferative neural progenitors. It cannot yet be assigned exclusively to kinetochore malfunction: CENP-F loss often produces surprisingly mild mitotic phenotypes in some experimental models, whereas patient tissues show clear ciliary abnormalities (gassmann2023dyneinatthe pages 9-10, waters2015thekinetochoreprotein pages 8-9).

## 7. Recent developments and translational applications

### 2023 mechanistic reassessment

The 2023 authoritative review **“Dynein at the kinetochore”** refined the field’s model: CENP-F is not required for bulk kinetochore dynein–dynactin recruitment, and parallel NDE1/NDEL1/LIS1 routes persist without it. Its reproducible phenotype is instead excessive removal of corona components and impaired recovery from erroneous attachments. This shifts the interpretation from “essential dynein recruiter” to “redundant attachment/corona regulator” ([Gassmann, published March 2023](https://doi.org/10.1242/jcs.220269)) (gassmann2023dyneinatthe pages 9-10).

### 2024 cancer research

Recent studies repeatedly nominate CENPF as a cancer-associated proliferation marker, but the translational evidence remains preliminary.

* A glioma study received in April and accepted in October 2024, published in the 2025 volume, analyzed TCGA data from **1,097 gliomas and five adjacent controls**, GSE111260 (**67 tumors/three controls**), and GSE16011 (**276 tumors/eight controls**). CENPF knockdown in U87 and U251 cells reduced proliferation, migration, and invasion, induced G2 arrest, lowered vimentin, increased E-cadherin, and reduced xenograft size and mass. These results support a dependency in the tested models, but EMT-like marker changes do not establish that CENP-F directly signals through an EMT pathway ([Li et al., accepted October 2, 2024](https://doi.org/10.3892/ol.2024.14807)) (li2024knockdownofcenpf pages 10-12, li2024knockdownofcenpf pages 1-2).

* A July 2024 lung-adenocarcinoma study used random forest and LASSO analyses to select **BUB1B, CENPF, and PLK1** from public transcriptomic datasets. CENPF expression was higher in lung adenocarcinoma with reported **P=2.3×10⁻⁵**, and high expression associated with worse survival. However, the authors acknowledged a relatively small sample, heavy reliance on public data, limited independent validation, and absence of protein-level confirmation ([Li et al., published July 23, 2024](https://doi.org/10.7717/peerj.17746)) (li2024machinelearningand pages 1-2, li2024machinelearningand pages 18-20).

CENPF is therefore being explored as a prognostic or diagnostic marker and as a possible synthetic-dependency target in glioma, lung, prostate, breast, and other cancers. Nevertheless, elevated CENPF is tightly coupled to G2/M proliferation, creating a major confounding issue: it may report the fraction of cycling cells rather than a tumor-specific oncogenic mechanism. No evidence reviewed here establishes an approved CENP-F-directed drug, a clinically validated standalone CENPF assay, or routine therapeutic selection based on CENPF.

## 8. Evidence-weighted conclusions

1. **Primary molecular function:** CENP-F is a non-catalytic, elongated microtubule-binding scaffold/force transducer at the outer kinetochore. Its strongest demonstrated roles are stabilization/modulation of kinetochore–microtubule attachments and control of dynein-mediated corona disassembly.
2. **Principal cellular site:** its defining action occurs at prometaphase/metaphase kinetochores, although nuclear-envelope, mitochondrial, centrosomal, and ciliary pools are experimentally supported.
3. **Best-established direct pathway:** BUB1 → CENP-F recruitment → microtubule/NDE1–NDEL1–LIS1/dynein interface → controlled corona stripping and attachment maturation.
4. **Biological importance:** pathogenic biallelic variants establish an essential role in human development, especially in ciliary and neurodevelopmental biology. Conversely, mild knockout phenotypes in some cells and mice reveal extensive redundancy and context dependence.
5. **Current application:** CENP-F is useful as a research marker of G2/mitotic cells and as an investigational cancer biomarker. Clinical diagnostic or therapeutic implementation has not yet been demonstrated.
