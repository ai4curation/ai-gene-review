Gene Description

IRAK1 (interleukin-1 receptor-associated kinase 1) is a cytosolic Pelle-family serine/threonine protein kinase with an N-terminal death domain, a Pro/Ser/Thr-rich (ProST) linker, a kinase domain and a C-terminal region carrying TRAF6-binding motifs. In resting cells it is held inactive with TOLLIP. After IL-1 family receptor or Toll-like receptor engagement it is recruited by MYD88 into the receptor-proximal Myddosome, phosphorylated by IRAK4 (Thr209) and then autophosphorylates (Thr387 and the ProST region), which releases it from MYD88/TOLLIP while it keeps hold of TRAF6. IRAK1 thus acts as a scaffold that recruits TRAF6 and couples it to the TAK1-TAB complex; after Pellino- and TRAF6-dependent K63-linked polyubiquitination it also recruits NEMO and the IKK complex, driving NF-kappaB and MAP kinase activation, before K48 ubiquitination and degradation. Kinase-dead IRAK1 fully supports IL-1-induced NF-kappaB and JNK activation, and IRAK1 is partly redundant with IRAK2. Its catalytic activity phosphorylates Pellino E3 ligases, TIRAP/Mal and IRF7, and is essential for TLR7/TLR9-induced IRF7 activation and interferon-alpha production in plasmacytoid dendritic cells. Sumoylated IRAK1 can enter the nucleus. Several splice variants (IRAK1b, IRAK1c) are kinase-inactive.

IRAK1 is a catalytically active Ser/Thr kinase (Pelle subfamily of TKL kinases).

    and PMID:14625308

Conclusion: IRAK1 has two separable core activities — (1) a signalling scaffold that links the
Myddosome to TRAF6 (and, once K63-ubiquitinated, to NEMO), for which catalysis is largely
dispensable in IL-1/TLR NF-kappaB signalling and is redundant with IRAK4 and IRAK2; and (2)
protein Ser/Thr kinase activity that is required for IRF7 activation in pDCs and acts on Pellino,
Mal and itself. Both are kept as core. This refines the IL-1 module's knowledge gap: the data
argue the IL-1 step is scaffold-typed, while the kinase is essential in TLR7/9-IRF7.
