CC   -!- FUNCTION: Cytosolic aldehyde dehydrogenase that catalyzes the oxidation
CC       of aldehydes to their corresponding carboxylic acids, preferentially
CC       using NAD+ but also accepting NADP+ as cofactor, with a strong
CC       preference for medium- and long-chain saturated and unsaturated
CC       aliphatic aldehydes (PubMed:12943535, PubMed:1737758, PubMed:36966166).
CC       In the corneal epithelium, contributes to protection against UV-induced
CC       oxidative damage by metabolizing toxic lipid peroxidation-derived
CC       aldehydes, including 4-hydroxy-2-nonenal (4-HNE) and malondialdehyde
CC       (PubMed:12706498, PubMed:17158879). In addition to its catalytic
CC       activity, functions as a corneal crystallin and constitutes a major
CC       fraction of the water-soluble protein content of the mammalian corneal
CC       epithelium (PubMed:12943535). Independently of catalysis, acts as a
CC       direct UV absorber, reducing UV-induced inactivation and aggregation of
CC       other corneal proteins through a competitive UV-filtering mechanism
CC       (PubMed:17158879). Also exhibits molecular chaperone-like activity in
CC       vitro, protecting proteins from thermal- and stress-induced aggregation
CC       and inactivation (PubMed:28526614). May contribute to the maintenance
CC       of corneal epithelial homeostasis through modulation of epithelial
CC       proliferation and differentiation programs (PubMed:26751691). Expressed
CC       in the nasal mucosa, displays enzymatic activity toward a variety of
CC       aldehyde odorant substrates in vitro and may contribute to odorant
CC       metabolism (PubMed:36966166). {ECO:0000269|PubMed:12706498,
