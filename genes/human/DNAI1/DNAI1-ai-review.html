Gene Description

DNAI1 is a noncatalytic intermediate-chain subunit of axonemal outer dynein arms. It helps organize the dynein motor complex and supports ciliary beating and flagellated sperm movement. The mature protein is associated with axonemes, while a cytoplasmic pool occupies dynein axonemal particles during motor assembly and deployment. Biallelic loss-of-function variants cause primary ciliary dyskinesia with outer-arm defects, which can affect respiratory function, fertility and left-right body patterning.

DNAI1 forms an intermediate-chain interaction unit within outer dynein arms, with conserved contacts to dynein heavy and light chains. This guide distinguishes direct human evidence, ortholog evidence and curated inference. Normal publication-cache availability is separate from access to an independently recovered original. No failed publisher response is treated as an article.

Identity and products

HGNC:2954 identifies DNAI1; UniProt Q9UI46 identifies the human axonemal intermediate chain. DIC1 is a historical alias that must not be confused with cytoplasmic dynein intermediate chains DYNC1I1/DYNC1I2.

Official alternative-sequence features reconstruct Q9UI46-1 at 699 residues and Q9UI46-2 at 168 residues, matching their official FASTAs. Product 2 retains canonical residues 1–87, replaces 88–168 and lacks 169–699, including the seven current WD-repeat regions. The original 699-residue clone supports the reference product; clinical truncations and a possible longer-UTR transcript are not evidence for activity of the natural short product.

Mature outer arm and assembly-stage localization

  • PMID:10577904(https://doi.org/10.1086/302683), Methods and Results on cDNA cloning, genotype and respiratory ultrastructure: human identity and outer-arm contribution. Genuine full text was read. There is no isolated DNAI1 force-generation or ATPase assay.
  • PMID:11231901(https://doi.org/10.1086/319511): the verified abstract establishes human DNAI1-associated PCD with variable situs. Full-original attempts did not yield usable text. Full text and captions were read; figure pixels were not independently scored.

The core uses microtubule motor activity as a contribution of a noncatalytic complex subunit, with outer dynein arm in the complex slot. Axoneme describes the internal microtubule structure and does not itself preserve the motile or 9+2 cilium type. No separate chaperone or ATPase activity is asserted.
