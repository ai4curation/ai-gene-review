Gene Description

SREBF2 encodes sterol regulatory element-binding protein 2 (SREBP-2), a 1141-residue basic helix-loop-helix leucine zipper (bHLH-Zip) transcription factor made as an inactive precursor inserted in the endoplasmic reticulum membrane as a hairpin of two transmembrane segments. Its C-terminal regulatory domain binds the sterol sensor SCAP. When sterols are abundant, SCAP binds INSIG proteins and the SCAP-SREBP-2 complex is held in the ER. When cellular cholesterol falls, SCAP escorts SREBP-2 in COPII vesicles to the Golgi, where site-1 protease (MBTPS1) cuts the luminal loop and site-2 protease (MBTPS2) cuts within the first transmembrane segment. This releases the N-terminal bHLH-Zip domain, which dimerizes, is imported into the nucleus by importin-beta, and binds sterol regulatory elements (SRE-1). It also binds E-box sequences. With coactivators such as CBP/p300 and the Mediator subunit MED15, nuclear SREBP-2 activates transcription of the genes for cholesterol biosynthesis (HMGCS1, HMGCR and later pathway enzymes) and uptake (LDLR), and of feedback regulators such as INSIG1 and PCSK9. This makes it the main transcriptional controller of cellular cholesterol homeostasis. The nuclear form is rapidly ubiquitinated and degraded by the proteasome. In some contexts, such as ABCA1 in endothelial cells, nuclear SREBP-2 represses transcription.

Core Functions

Nuclear SREBF2 (the released bHLH-Zip N-terminal domain) is a sequence-specific RNA polymerase II transcription activator. It binds sterol regulatory elements and activates the genes for cholesterol biosynthesis and LDL receptor-mediated uptake, increasing cellular cholesterol synthesis.

Gene product overview

  • SREBP-2 is a 1141-residue bHLH-Zip transcription factor made as an ER membrane-bound
    precursor PMID:7903453.
  • Three domains: N-terminal bHLH-Zip transcription factor (~480 aa), a hairpin of two TM
    segments with a short luminal loop, and a C-terminal regulatory domain (~590 aa) that
    binds SCAP [PMID:11994399 "Each SREBP precursor of about 1150 amino acids is organized
    into three domains"; PMID:9242699 "Complex formation occurs when cells express only the
    COOH-terminal domain of either SREBP-2 or SCAP, indicating that the complex forms between
    the two COOH-terminal domains."].
  • Sterol-regulated two-step proteolysis releases the N-terminal domain: S1P in the luminal
    loop, then S2P inside TM1 [PMID:8674110 "The first, regulated by sterols, occurs in the
    lumenal loop. The second, not regulated by sterols, occurs within the first
    transmembrane domain."; "The liberated NH2-segment enters the nucleus and activates genes
    controlling cholesterol synthesis and uptake."].
  • SCAP escorts SREBP from ER to Golgi in COPII vesicles in sterol-depleted cells; sterols
    promote SCAP-INSIG binding and ER retention PMID:12202038; INSIG-2 behaves the same way
    PMID:12242332.
  • Golgi anchoring by PAQR3 promotes processing PMID:26311497. TRC8/RNF139 binds SREBP-2 and SCAP and hampers ER exit PMID:19706601.
  • Nuclear form is imported by importin-beta as a dimer (Reactome R-HSA-1655831, R-HSA-2065550)
    and is rapidly degraded by the ubiquitin-proteasome system PMID:11477106.

Transcriptional function and specificity

  • Binds SRE-1 like SREBP-1a and activates SRE reporters PMID:7903453.
  • Preferential activator of cholesterol synthesis genes and LDLR; weak on lipogenic
    SRE-like promoters; binds but does not activate an E-box reporter [PMID:12177166
    "cholesterogenic genes containing classic SREs in their promoters are strongly and
    efficiently activated by both SREBP-1a and SREBP-2, but not by SREBP-1c"; "SREBP-2 was
    inactive in spite of its ability to bind to the E-box"]; PMID:11994399.
  • In vivo, nSREBP-2 transgenic liver: 75-fold HMGCR mRNA increase, 28-fold cholesterol
    synthesis increase PMID:11994399.
  • Activates INSIG1 (feedback loop) PMID:12242332.
  • Coactivators: CBP/p300 KIX domain and Mediator MED15/ARC105 KIX-like domain
    PMID:16799563. HNF-4 binds SREBP-2 and potentiates it
    PMID:12855700.
  • Context-specific repression: nSREBP-2 represses ABCA1 via an E-box in endothelial cells
    PMID:15358760; it is also the proposed LRP1 repressor in vascular smooth muscle
    [PMID:19098903, indirect knockdown evidence]. These are non-core.

  helix-loop-helix leucine zipper (bHLH-Zip) transcription factor made as an inactive precursor inserted
  in the endoplasmic reticulum membrane as a hairpin of two transmembrane segments. Its C-terminal regulatory
  domain binds the sterol sensor SCAP. When sterols are abundant, SCAP binds INSIG proteins and the SCAP-SREBP-2
  complex is held in the ER. When cellular cholesterol falls, SCAP escorts SREBP-2 in COPII vesicles to
  the Golgi, where site-1 protease (MBTPS1) cuts the luminal loop and site-2 protease (MBTPS2) cuts within
  the first transmembrane segment. This releases the N-terminal bHLH-Zip domain, which dimerizes, is imported
  into the nucleus by importin-beta, and binds sterol regulatory elements (SRE-1). It also binds E-box
  sequences. With coactivators such as CBP/p300 and the Mediator subunit MED15, nuclear SREBP-2 activates
  transcription of the genes for cholesterol biosynthesis (HMGCS1, HMGCR and later pathway enzymes) and
  uptake (LDLR), and of feedback regulators such as INSIG1 and PCSK9. This makes it the main transcriptional
  controller of cellular cholesterol homeostasis. The nuclear form is rapidly ubiquitinated and degraded
  by the proteasome. In some contexts, such as ABCA1 in endothelial cells, nuclear SREBP-2 represses transcription.
