Gene Description

Small humanin-like peptide 2 (SHLP2) is a 26-residue peptide and a 16S-rRNA sORF peptide: its open reading frame lies inside MT-RNR2, the mitochondrial 16S ribosomal RNA gene, in the same stretch of mitochondrial DNA that encodes humanin. The six small humanin-like peptides SHLP1-SHLP6 were identified by an in silico search of this region and named by analogy with humanin, with which they share a genomic neighbourhood rather than sequence similarity. It is the best-characterised member of the group. SHLP2 binds and activates the atypical chemokine receptor ACKR3/CXCR7, recruiting beta-arrestin with sub-nanomolar potency and causing receptor internalisation; through this receptor it depolarises proopiomelanocortin neurons of the hypothalamic arcuate nucleus, and in rodents it suppresses food intake, activates thermogenesis and protects against diet-induced obesity. Intracerebral infusion increases glucose uptake and suppresses hepatic glucose production, and circulating levels fall in obesity, diabetes and ageing, so the peptide behaves as a secreted metabolic signal. Independently of this receptor arm, SHLP2 has chaperone-like activity in vitro: it binds misfolded, seeding-competent species of islet amyloid polypeptide - not IAPP monomers - and blocks amyloid seeding and fibril formation. A second, intracellular pool is biochemically well supported: endogenous SHLP2 fractionates with mitochondria rather than cytosol, resists carbonate extraction like an integral membrane protein, is accessible to proximity labelling of inner-membrane but not outer-membrane markers, and co-labels subunits of respiratory complex I. In cell models SHLP2 reduces apoptosis and reactive oxygen species, improves mitochondrial metabolism, stabilises OXPHOS subunits and protects against amyloid-beta-induced damage. How much of this is translated in vivo is unresolved. The peptides are read with the cytoplasmic genetic code, yet no route is known by which an mtDNA-encoded transcript reaches cytoplasmic ribosomes, nor by which a peptide made in the matrix would be exported; and the same region of mitochondrial DNA is present in the nuclear genome as MT-RNR2-like (NUMT-derived) copies, so antibody-based detection cannot be attributed to the mitochondrial locus with certainty.

Suggested Questions for Experts

Q: How does one peptide hold both reported locations? SHLP2 is an integral protein of the mitochondrial inner membrane by fractionation, carbonate extraction and proximity labelling (PMID:38167865), and a secreted ligand of a cell-surface GPCR (PMID:37468558). Are these two pools of the same peptide, two products of different loci (mitochondrial versus MT-RNR2-like nuclear), or does one assay report a synthetic-peptide artefact?

Q: Does endogenous SHLP2 reach ACKR3/CXCR7, and at what concentration? The receptor work used synthetic peptide; a physiological concentration range in plasma and cerebrospinal fluid is needed before the anorexigenic pathway can be treated as an endogenous signalling axis.

Q: Is the complex I association functional? Proximity labelling places SHLP2 next to complex I subunits, and SHLP2 treatment stabilises OXPHOS subunits in an AMD cell model (PMID:30310092), but no effect of SHLP2 on complex I assembly or activity has been measured directly. If one were shown, an assembly-factor or enzyme-regulator annotation would follow.

Q: Should the chaperone-like activity be annotated as a holdase? GO:0051082 unfolded protein binding was obsoleted without a direct replacement, and the IAPP data show seed binding rather than a demonstrated holdase cycle, so GO:0051787 misfolded protein binding is proposed here instead.

Q: Is the m.2158T>C (K4R) variant protective in Parkinson disease? PMID:38167865 reports increased peptide stability and protection, and PMID:38940474 finds no association in an independent cohort; nothing should be annotated from this until it is settled.

Suggested Experiments

Experiment: Submitochondrial localisation of endogenous SHLP2 by protease protection and swelling/shaving of mitoplasts, combined with a topology reporter, to establish sidedness in the inner membrane and whether a soluble pool exists.

Hypothesis: SHLP2 is an integral inner-membrane peptide with a defined orientation relative to complex I.

Experiment: Complex I activity and assembly (in-gel activity, complexome profiling) in cells depleted of SHLP2 by mitochondrial-ORF-targeted knockdown and in cells expressing the K4R variant.

Hypothesis: SHLP2 is required for normal complex I assembly or activity rather than merely adjacent to it.

Experiment: ACKR3/CXCR7 knockout or antagonist blockade in POMC neurons, with endogenous SHLP2 levels measured by targeted mass spectrometry, testing whether the metabolic phenotype requires the receptor at physiological peptide concentrations.

Hypothesis: The anorexigenic effect is an endogenous SHLP2-ACKR3 axis rather than a pharmacological effect of the synthetic peptide.

Experiment: Quantitative IAPP aggregation kinetics (seeded and unseeded) with SHLP2 titrated across the nanomolar range and in the presence of islet-derived seeds, to establish whether the chaperone-like activity operates at achievable concentrations.

Hypothesis: Seed binding inhibits IAPP fibril formation at concentrations comparable to circulating SHLP2.

SHLP2 (A0A3G1DIU6) - curation notes

Shared context: the MT-RNR2 sORF peptides

Humanin and SHLP1-6 are seven separate UniProt entries whose ORFs all lie inside
MT-RNR2, the mitochondrial 16S rRNA gene. UniProt files all seven under the host
symbol MT-RNR2, so this repo uses the <HOST>__<ACC> folder convention
(CLAUDE.md, "Alternative-ORF peptides"). The SHLPs were found by in silico search
of the humanin-containing region PMID:27070352 - they share a genomic
neighbourhood with humanin, not sequence similarity.

The founding study detected five of the six by immunoblot in mouse tissues
PMID:27070352 with organ-specific patterns PMID:27070352, and could not raise an antibody against SHLP5 PMID:27070352.
Phenotypes were assigned with synthetic peptides at 100 nM: SHLP2 and SHLP3 were
cytoprotective PMID:27070352, SHLP2 and SHLP4
proliferative PMID:27070352, and SHLP6 pro-apoptotic PMID:27070352. SHLP1 had no assigned phenotype.

Two caveats apply to all seven entries. The peptides are read with the
cytoplasmic genetic code although the locus is mitochondrial, and no export route
for an mtDNA-encoded transcript or a matrix-made peptide has been demonstrated.
And the same mtDNA region exists in the nuclear genome as MT-RNR2-like (NUMT)
copies, so antibody-based detection cannot be assigned to the mitochondrial locus
with certainty. All but SHLP2 are UniProt PE4 (predicted).

What is known about SHLP2 specifically

The only SHLP with mechanism. Three independent arms:

Receptor ligand (ACKR3/CXCR7). PMID:37468558; PMID:37468558; direct binding
PMID:37468558; and a physiological
output PMID:37468558.

Chaperone-like / misfolded-seed binding. PMID:28798389, with the conformer selectivity
controlled PMID:28798389.

Mitochondrial inner membrane and complex I. PMID:38167865; PMID:38167865;
PMID:38167865. Three orthogonal methods
(fractionation, Na2CO3 extraction, APEX2 topology), which is better localisation
evidence than most microproteins have.

Phenotypic context: AMD cell-model protection PMID:30310092. The m.2158T>C (K4R) Parkinson claim of PMID:38167865 is disputed by
PMID:38940474 and is not used for any annotation.

Decisions

  • All four GOA rows (extracellular region EXP+IEA, mitochondrial inner membrane
    EXP+IEA) ACCEPT.
  • Three NEW terms, each passing the participation test with SHLP2 itself doing the
    step: GO:0048018 receptor ligand activity (binds and activates CXCR7),
    GO:0051787 misfolded protein binding (binds the misfolded IAPP conformer, not
    the monomer), GO:1905907 negative regulation of amyloid fibril formation (the
    process counterpart; comparator check passes - humanin carries GO:1905907 twice
    by IDA for the equivalent amyloid-beta result).
  • Deliberately NOT proposed: the downstream cytoprotective phenotypes (apoptosis,
    ROS, OXPHOS subunit stabilisation, glucose handling, food intake), because for
    each of those the step is executed by something else and SHLP2's contribution is
    necessity, not participation. Also not proposed: a complex I assembly or
    enzyme-regulator term - proximity labelling places SHLP2 next to complex I but no
    effect on assembly or activity has been measured.
  • GO:0051082 unfolded protein binding was obsoleted without a direct replacement,
    so GO:0051787 misfolded protein binding is used rather than inventing a holdase
    term; no holdase cycle was demonstrated, only seed binding.
  • The secreted-versus-inner-membrane tension is the most interesting open question
    on this entry and is recorded as such rather than resolved.

suggested_questions:
- question: How does one peptide hold both reported locations? SHLP2 is an integral protein of the mitochondrial
    inner membrane by fractionation, carbonate extraction and proximity labelling (PMID:38167865), and
    a secreted ligand of a cell-surface GPCR (PMID:37468558). Are these two pools of the same peptide,
    two products of different loci (mitochondrial versus MT-RNR2-like nuclear), or does one assay report
    a synthetic-peptide artefact?
- question: Does endogenous SHLP2 reach ACKR3/CXCR7, and at what concentration? The receptor work used
    synthetic peptide; a physiological concentration range in plasma and cerebrospinal fluid is needed
    before the anorexigenic pathway can be treated as an endogenous signalling axis.
- question: Is the complex I association functional? Proximity labelling places SHLP2 next to complex
    I subunits, and SHLP2 treatment stabilises OXPHOS subunits in an AMD cell model (PMID:30310092), but
    no effect of SHLP2 on complex I assembly or activity has been measured directly. If one were shown,
    an assembly-factor or enzyme-regulator annotation would follow.
- question: Should the chaperone-like activity be annotated as a holdase? GO:0051082 unfolded protein
    binding was obsoleted without a direct replacement, and the IAPP data show seed binding rather than
    a demonstrated holdase cycle, so GO:0051787 misfolded protein binding is proposed here instead.
- question: Is the m.2158T>C (K4R) variant protective in Parkinson disease? PMID:38167865 reports increased
    peptide stability and protection, and PMID:38940474 finds no association in an independent cohort;
    nothing should be annotated from this until it is settled.
