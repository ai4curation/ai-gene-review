Gene Description

INSIG1 (insulin-induced gene 1) is a polytopic, six-transmembrane protein of the endoplasmic reticulum membrane and a sterol sensor in feedback control of cholesterol synthesis and uptake. When ER sterol levels rise, INSIG1 binds oxysterols such as 25-hydroxycholesterol (through a pocket formed with transmembrane helices 3 and 4), or engages cholesterol-bound SCAP. It binds the sterol-sensing domain of SCAP and holds the SCAP-SREBP complex in the ER, making SCAP's MELADL sorting signal inaccessible to the COPII proteins Sec23/24. SREBP-1 and SREBP-2 therefore are not carried to the Golgi for proteolytic activation. INSIG1 also binds the sterol-sensing domain of HMG-CoA reductase and acts as an adaptor that brings it to the ER ubiquitin ligases gp78/AMFR, TRC8/RNF139 and RNF145 for sterol-accelerated ER-associated degradation; it similarly recruits ACAT2/SOAT2 to gp78. INSIG1 is itself an SREBP target gene. When sterols are depleted, free INSIG1 is ubiquitinated (Lys156/Lys158) and degraded, which makes renewed feedback depend on new synthesis. A conserved juxtamembrane Asp205 is required for both SCAP and HMGCR functions. In hepatocellular carcinoma cells, phosphorylation at Ser207 by PCK1 has been reported to reduce sterol binding and release SCAP-SREBP.
