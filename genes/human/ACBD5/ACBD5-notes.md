# ACBD5 review notes

## 2026-11-15 — Primary-literature review

ACBD5 (HGNC:23338; UniProt Q5T8D3) is a tail-anchored peroxisomal membrane
protein with two experimentally separable activities: acyl-CoA binding and
VAP-mediated peroxisome–ER tethering. Native initialization supplied 18 annotation
contexts and four alternative products. All source fields and alternative products

### Acyl-CoA binding and metabolism

Human patient-derived fibroblasts and ACBD5-knockout HeLa cells establish impaired
VLCFA metabolism. Yagita et al. also used complementation, membrane targeting/domain
deletions and direct biochemical binding assays [PMID:27899449, *Deficiency of a
Retinal Dystrophy Protein, Acyl-CoA Binding Domain-containing 5 (ACBD5), Impairs
Peroxisomal β-Oxidation of Very-long-chain Fatty Acids*, “binds very-long-chain fatty
acyl-CoAs (VLC-CoAs).”]. The native cache contains the abstract; the primary
[PMC full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5241743/) was also inspected.
Fig. 8 tests C16-CoA binding and C24-CoA competition; the Discussion's C26 wording
must not replace the actual experimental substrate. The work labels the tested
human sequence isoform 2, NP_001035938.1.

Ferdinandusse et al. independently report defective peroxisomal VLCFA metabolism
in patient material and CRISPR ACBD5-deficient HeLa cells [PMID:27799409, *ACBD5
deficiency causes a defect in peroxisomal very long-chain fatty acid metabolism*].
Its cached abstract supports the metabolic conclusion. ACBD5 provides a
binding/recruitment contribution; it is neither assigned a β-oxidation catalytic
activity nor established as a transmembrane transporter. Its role is more than
being the substrate consumed by the pathway: the protein supplies acyl-CoA-binding
capacity on the cytosolic face of the peroxisomal membrane.

The HEK293 complementation study separates this role from tethering
[PMID:37414147, *Differential roles for ACBD4 and ACBD5 in peroxisome-ER interactions
and lipid metabolism*, “the tethering function of ACBD5 is not required to restore
VLCFA processing in an ACBD5 KO HEK293 cell line.”]. This is a system-specific
separation of functions, not proof that contacts never influence metabolism.

### Membrane tether activity

Direct recombinant ACBD5–VAPB binding, FFAT-motif mutants and quantitative
membrane-contact experiments support physical tethering [PMID:28108524, *ACBD5
and VAPB mediate membrane associations between peroxisomes and the ER*, “We conclude
that VAPB and ACBD5 interact directly via the ACBD5 FFAT-like motif”]. The discovery
and cellular experiments include human HepG2 and COS-7 systems; they are not all
human-cell experiments. Hua et al. independently establish VAPA/VAPB-mediated
tethering and associated membrane-growth/lipid-homeostasis effects
[PMID:28108526, *VAPs and ACBD5 tether peroxisomes to the ER for peroxisome
maintenance and lipid homeostasis*]. Both full texts are cached.

The one proposed NEW annotation is
[GO:0160229, peroxisome-endoplasmic reticulum membrane tether activity](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/QuickGO/term/GO:0160229).
QuickGO's official API verified the active MF term on 2026-11-15. Its definition
covers a molecule that bridges these membranes through lipid or membrane-protein
binding. Its is_a/part_of ancestor set includes GO:0170009, GO:0140177,
GO:0060090 and GO:0003674, not GO:0000062. The term is absent from all native
contexts; the live Q5T8D3/GO:0160229 query returned zero annotations, and the local
GO-CAM index has no ACBD5/Q5T8D3 entry. These absence checks establish nonredundancy;
the direct experiments establish the function. No new biological-process term is
proposed.

Phosphorylation near and within the FFAT motif modulates VAPB binding; different
sites have different effects [PMID:35019937, *Regulating peroxisome-ER contacts via
the ACBD5-VAPB tether by FFAT motif phosphorylation and GSK3β*]. ACBD5 is the
regulated substrate, not the kinase. Contact-dependent changes in lipid abundance
do not directly demonstrate transport or enzymatic lipid transfer by ACBD5.

### Pexophagy: preserve the disagreement

Nazarko et al. really tested human ACBD5 in HeLa and Ntera-2 cells, in addition to
yeast Atg37 [PMID:24535825, *Peroxisomal Atg37 binds Atg30 or palmitoyl-CoA to
regulate phagophore formation during pexophagy*, “ACBD5 is required for the
autophagic delivery of peroxisomes to the lysosomes.”]. Fig. 9 and supplementary
experiments support the tested human depletion phenotype. Their separate human
peroxisome-localization evidence is retained. The yeast Atg30/Atg11 mechanism is
not transferred to human ACBD5, and these experiments do not establish ACBD5 as a
mammalian cargo receptor.

Ferdinandusse et al. report “No effect on pexophagy was found.” [PMID:27799409].
The abstract is sufficient for this bounded statement; no detailed assay protocol
is inferred from it. Barone et al. additionally find that ACBD5 depletion does not
block induced pexophagy in human hTERT-RPE1 Keima-SKL cells, using MLN4924
(Fig. 2) or CMPD-39 (Fig. 4) [PMID:36810161, *Segregation of pathways leading to
pexophagy*, “The increased pexophagy after CMPD-39 is insensitive to BNIP3/NIX or
ACBD5 depletion”]. Basal versus induced conditions and cell context remain relevant
differences. Both native pexophagy rows therefore remain UNDECIDED. The narrow original
observation is preserved separately from the authors' generalized requirement
interpretation, which carries a DISPUTED assessment linked to the later evidence.

The 2024 cross-species study [PMID:39271061, *New insights into the functions of
ACBD4/5-like proteins using a combined phylogenetic and experimental approach
across model organisms*] provides context, not a new human ACBD5-knockout pexophagy
experiment. Its new mammalian comparison overexpresses human proteins in rat H9C2
myoblasts. The native cache is abstract-only; the primary PDF was inspected for
that assay boundary. Older negative studies cited in its discussion remain older
studies, not independent new experiments.
