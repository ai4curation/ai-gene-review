# ETV6 source guide

This guide separates native ETV6, experimental expression constructs, disease fusions and interaction partners. The source annotation objects and normal publication files are preserved. A full original can be recovered independently even when the normal cache contains only an abstract. No publication quotations were added.

## DNA recognition and transcription

- **[PMID:10514502](https://doi.org/10.1074/jbc.274.42.30132)** — [Actual author-repository PDF](https://orbi.uliege.be/bitstream/2268/23452/1/Lopez%20et%20al%201999.pdf), printed Methods pp. 30132–30133 and Results/Figures 1–6, pp. 30133–30137, read with the relevant Discussion. Human TEL represses EBS-containing reporters in human HeLa cells. Figure 5 uses a **mouse Fli1 promoter reporter with human TEL**, explaining the raw mouse target-gene input. The M1 construct has M43A to prevent internal initiation; M1 and M43 each repress the tested reporter. Deletions, ETS1 substitutions and EBV EB1/Gal4 fusions are experimental constructs. These results do not establish native promoter occupancy, every product's function or repression in every cellular context. Figure captions were read; no figure pixels were inspected.
- **[PMID:9018121](https://doi.org/10.1038/sj.onc.1200829)** — Actual author-uploaded original recovered through ResearchGate. Relevant Results, Methods and Figure 6 caption were read. Human TEL made in Sf9 cells binds a synthetic EBS probe with antibody and wild-type versus mutated competitor controls. Endogenous RAJI nuclear staining, negative Reh cells, and IVT/COS alternative-initiation comparisons are separate assays. The M43 construct lost its HA tag; M89 was not established as an endogenous product. The 1997 paper left functional equivalence of the two main products unresolved; the later 1999 reporter comparison supplies a bounded positive shared activity. HeLa CAT negative results in the earlier paper are reported as data not shown. No figure pixels were inspected.
- **[PMID:7761424](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.92.11.4917)** — PMID/title/DOI and the accessible abstract were verified. The scanned original body/PDF remained inaccessible through the attempted routes. Its fusion-focused title does not establish a wrong-gene annotation. Native ETV6 repressor specificity is independently supported by the two actual human originals above; no native assay is invented for this unread source.

Current GO:0000978 describes cis-regulatory sequence-specific DNA binding and is more specific than GO:0000977. GO:0001227 describes the demonstrated Pol II repressor activity. Contextual activation elsewhere is not excluded by retaining this principal activity. Synthetic-probe binding, promoter reporters and native chromatin occupancy remain distinct evidence types.

## Functional corepressor partners

- **[PMID:12588862](https://doi.org/10.1074/jbc.M300592200)** — [Actual author-uploaded original](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/10898254_The_Human_L3MBT_Polycomb_Group_Protein_Is_a_Transcriptional_Repressor_and_Interacts_Physically_and_Functionally_with_TEL_ETV6). Read plasmid/cell/co-IP/GST Methods and Results/Figures 4–6 captions. The source's human type-I L3MBTL1 is 772 residues. Endogenous human U2OS nuclear extracts support association; tagged human 293T constructs and GST-TEL with reticulocyte-translated L3MBTL1 establish SAM/SPM dependence. Human proteins are also tested in mouse NIH3T3 reporter cells, including a rat stromelysin-1 promoter. Positive corepressor binding does not require an invented purified two-protein reconstitution, native ChIP experiment or universal Polycomb-complex membership. GO:0001222 identifies the partner's role more informatively than domain-specific binding. No figure pixels were inspected.
- **[PMID:12590135](https://doi.org/10.1074/jbc.M212935200)** — [Actual author-uploaded original](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/10897610_The_Histone_Deacetylase_9_Gene_Encodes_Multiple_Protein_Isoforms). Read GST/co-IP Methods and Results/Figures 6–7 captions and the isoform/repression context. TEL binds HDAC9 variants, including catalytic-domain-lacking ΔCD/MITR. Bacterial GST proteins and reticulocyte-translated TEL, COS coexpression and endogenous human hematopoietic-cell co-IP are distinct assays. The endogenous C-terminal antibody does not identify the ΔCD product. [UniProt Q9UKV0](https://www.uniprot.org/uniprotkb/Q9UKV0/entry) explicitly identifies Q9UKV0-3 as inactive MITR/HDRP with a repressor role; no intrinsic deacetylase activity is assigned to that partner. The historical 593-residue construct and current 590-residue model are not asserted sequence-identical. No figure pixels were inspected.

Both publisher PDF routes returned blocked HTML; the operative evidence comes from the actual author-uploaded text, not those failed files. The exact observed text and read ranges are archived privately. No normal publication cache was replaced.

## Hematopoietic phenotype

**[PMID:25581430](https://doi.org/10.1038/ng.3177)** — Actual main Methods, Results and Figure 5 caption were read. Full-length human NM_001987.4 constructs in cord-blood CD34-positive HSPCs are distinct from recombinant ETS residues 335–430 used in DNA-binding assays. Mutant-expressing expansion cultures have fewer viable cells than wild-type/empty-vector controls, without reported increased apoptosis. This supports a contextual hematopoietic proliferative role, retained non-core. Mixed HSPCs and viable-cell counts do not separately establish long-term HSC self-renewal or a direct cell-cycle catalytic activity. No figure pixels were inspected.

The official canonical sequence is 452 residues. Absence of a UniProt alternative-products comment does not exclude other natural products. The independently captured transcript/RefSeq models and the historical M1/M43 translation evidence are preserved separately; pathological ETV6 kinase fusions are not normal splice products. No identical function or localization is assigned to every model.

The annotation proposals add no publication quotations. ## Core-function evidence and limits

One nuclear ETS-site-dependent repressor activity is represented by [GO:0001227](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/QuickGO/term/GO:0001227), acting in [GO:0000122](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/QuickGO/term/GO:0000122) at the [nucleus](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/QuickGO/term/GO:0005634). Current official definitions and typed ancestry were checked. Cis-DNA recognition and corepressor association support this mechanism; they are not split into redundant core activities. No speculative complex membership is asserted.

Additional synthesis reading used the observed author-upload text of [PMID:12588862](https://doi.org/10.1074/jbc.M300592200), Methods and Results/Figures 4–6, and [PMID:12590135](https://doi.org/10.1074/jbc.M212935200), Methods and Results/Figures 6–7. The first includes native U2OS nuclear co-immunoprecipitation, human-protein domain mapping, GST/reticulocyte pull-downs and the additive human-TEL/L3MBTL1 effect on a rat promoter in mouse NIH3T3 cells. The second distinguishes transfected HDAC9/ΔCD variants from an endogenous C-terminal antibody that cannot identify MITR. Neither establishes ETV6 deacetylase activity or one permanent multicomponent complex. No figure pixels or supplements were inspected during this synthesis.

The family segregation, variant and HSPC sections of [PMID:25581430](https://doi.org/10.1038/ng.3177), including Figures 1–5 captions and Methods, support familial thrombocytopenia with hematologic malignancy predisposition. They do not resolve the individual roles of every natural ETV6 product. The historical M1(M43A)/M43 reporter comparison and mouse S1A positive Fli1 context retain the species, promoter and construct limits described above. Questions and experiments address these boundaries without manufacturing new annotations.

The original publication and database reference assessments retain their access limits. This guide is cited as a local reference so that the Reactome participant routes and independent HPA observations are traceable from the annotation rationales. It does not turn the antibody signal into a product-resolved localization assay or establish a new core at the cytosol.
