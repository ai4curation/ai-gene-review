### Primary evidence and boundaries

- PMID:40203823: full-length wild-type human receptor reconstitution with delta/epsilon affinity tags, electrophysiology and resting/ACh-desensitized structures place beta1 in the pore. The structural snapshots are nonconducting; activity comes from separate electrophysiology. Classical transmitter sites are at alpha1–delta/epsilon interfaces. Model completeness and equivalence to both present-day products are not assumed.
- PMID:27375219: human beta1 Val289Ala (mature-chain V266A) alters opening/closing kinetics in HEK293 reconstitution. Apparent receptor affinity and model-derived ligand affinity are distinct; mutant necessity is not the sole evidence for normal channel work.
- PMID:8872460: the original receptor-affinity experiment uses mouse subunit cDNAs in HEK cells, alongside human patient endplates. The positive beta mutant apparent-affinity result is real, but reflects receptor state occupancy rather than direct beta1–acetylcholine contact. PMID:24194945 mouse beta1 gating and the human structure corroborate this distinction. Rows 41/42 are OVER, without asserting the impossibility of every noncanonical ligand interaction.
- PMID:7531341: all five original PMC page images 758–762 were read. The mutation is epsilon T264P; beta is a wild-type assembled subunit. Human endplate currents and mouse cDNA expression experiments are distinguished. The attempted PDF returned HTML and is excluded from scientific evidence.
- PMID:15701510: native Torpedo structural refinement is retained at its actual species scope, with independent human reconstruction supplying target corroboration.
- PMID:16527851: assembled adult human receptors tested in rat GH4C1 cells conduct measurable calcium; human fetal myotubes supply a separate comparison. Epsilon influences the adult human–mouse difference, so calcium conduction is not attributed to beta1 alone.
