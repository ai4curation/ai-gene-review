Gene Description

CHRNB1 encodes the beta1 subunit of the skeletal-muscle nicotinic acetylcholine receptor. Beta1 contributes a pore-lining transmembrane helix and allosteric gating elements to a heteropentamer containing two alpha1 subunits, delta and either fetal gamma or adult epsilon. Acetylcholine binding at alpha1 interfaces opens the assembled cation channel at the neuromuscular postsynaptic membrane, depolarizing muscle and initiating contraction. Beta1 pore mutations can prolong channel activation and cause slow-channel congenital myasthenic disease. The function of the N-terminally shortened alternative beta1 product remains unresolved.

Core Functions

Beta1 supplies a pore-lining helix and allosteric gating elements to the muscle nicotinic acetylcholine receptor. In a heteropentamer containing two alpha1 chains, delta and either fetal gamma or adult epsilon, this contribution supports ACh-evoked cation current, endplate depolarization and neuromuscular excitation. In the adult receptor, canonical ligand-binding sites lie at alpha1–delta and alpha1–epsilon interfaces; beta1 gating can change receptor apparent affinity without establishing a direct beta1–ACh contact. Full-length human receptors establish the channel contribution; the function of the N-terminally shortened alternative product remains unresolved.
