## Manual synthesis

IRF5 is a latent IRF-family transcriptional activator in innate immune cells. Virus and pattern-recognition-receptor pathways induce phosphorylation-dependent nuclear accumulation and target-gene transcription [PMID:12138184], and IRF5/IRF7 dimerization can change IFNA enhanceosome assembly and IFNA subtype expression [PMID:12600985].

Multiple papers support IRF5 as the transcriptional effector downstream of innate immune adapters. RIP2/NOD2 activation can drive IRF5-regulated IL12p40, IFNA14, and IFNB reporter transcription [PMID:22412986]. In TLR9-stimulated human plasmacytoid dendritic cells, IRF5 contributes to MyD88/TRAF6-dependent IFN-beta and IL-6 induction [PMID:23616277]. IKK-beta phosphorylation drives IRF5 dimerization and nuclear translocation [PMID:25326418], and TASL licenses IRF5 phosphorylation downstream of endolysosomal TLR7/8/9 signaling [PMID:32433612].

I treated nuclear DNA-binding transcriptional activation of inflammatory and type I IFN genes as the core function, cytoplasmic/cytosolic rows as the latent pre-activation state, and NOD2/TLR/cytokine-response process annotations as real pathway contexts in which IRF5 performs that core transcriptional work.
