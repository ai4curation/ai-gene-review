Gene Description

ACTL8 is a divergent, testis-enriched member of the actin family whose molecular function has not been determined. The 366-residue protein retains the actin fold but is only about a third identical to cytoplasmic beta-actin, and structural comparison shows the divergence is concentrated exactly where actin's identity resides: most of the ATP phosphate- and adenosine-binding residues survive, whereas the great majority of the protomer-protomer contact surface that builds a filament, including almost the whole DNase-I binding loop, does not, which makes canonical incorporation into a two-stranded filament unlikely. Whether it polymerises at all has never been tested, and no biochemical activity, ligand or cellular structure has been assigned to it experimentally. Expression is strongest in testis and pancreas with weak placental expression, and the protein is detected by proteomics and by immunohistochemistry in the cytoplasm of tumour cells. ACTL8 is also known as cancer/testis antigen 57, and this accounts for essentially all of its functional literature: it is transcriptionally silent in most somatic tissues but reactivated in breast, gastric, oral squamous, lung and head-and-neck carcinomas, and in several of these high expression has been associated with poor survival. In that ectopic setting, RNA-interference knockdown reproducibly reduces proliferation, migration and invasion, increases apoptosis, slows cell-cycle progression and lowers PI3K/AKT/mTOR phosphorylation, and shRNA knockdown suppresses xenograft growth; no direct interaction with any pathway component has been demonstrated, so the route is unresolved and may be indirect. Its physiological role in the testis, the one tissue where it is normally abundant, remains unstudied. The only reported binding partner is the ceramide transfer protein CERT1, from yeast two-hybrid screening alone.

Suggested Questions for Experts

Q: Should ACTL8 be moved out of PANTHER's cytoplasmic-actin subfamily? Nodes PTN002631586 and PTN007551913 each cover nine human genes - ACTB, ACTG1, ACTBL2, POTEE, POTEF, POTEI, POTEJ, POTEKP and ACTL8 - and the first eight are all at least 90.7 per cent identical to beta-actin because each POTE protein carries a C-terminal actin-family domain (UniProt SIMILARITY) that is nearly identical to beta-actin. ACTL8 joins them at 33.8 per cent, a 57-percentage-point gap. As a result ACTL8 has inherited ten annotations that are really beta-actin's cell biology, including synapse, axon, axonogenesis and membership of the NuA4 complex. The deeper node PTN002631484, whose clade runs from 33.7 to 100 per cent identity, donates only the generic GO:0015629 and is the level at which ACTL8 should be receiving inferences - and it is where every one of ACTL8's own divergent relatives sits. Of the eight human actin-like / actin-related-T proteins, ACTL8 is the only one under either narrow node; ACTL7A and ACTL7B use PTN001377938, PTN008986520 and PTN008986528, and ACTL9, ACTL10, ACTRT1, ACTRT2 and ACTRT3 use PTN000940351 plus PTN002631484. ACTL8 consequently carries 11 IBA rows against a median of 2 for those relatives. Is the tree placement wrong, or is it right and the ancestral-state calls at these two nodes need restricting?

Q: Should GO:0035267 NuA4 histone acetyltransferase complex be reviewed for the other eight human descendants of PTN007551913, not only for ACTL8? The node assigns membership to ACTB, ACTG1, ACTBL2, ACTL8 and the five POTE genes. ACTB's membership is not in question - it has its own IDA and is named in the term's definition, alongside BAF53/ACTL6A, as an actin subunit of the human complex. That is the point: the definition specifies which paralog, so the other eight rows are all similarity transfers of a purification result. Complex membership is established by purifying a particular subunit and does not descend a family, so this looks like a systematic rather than a gene-specific problem.

Q: Why does UniProt's ECO:0000250 subcellular location for ACTL8 cite no source entry? The line reads "SUBCELLULAR LOCATION: Cytoplasm, cytoskeleton {ECO:0000250}" with no UniProtKB accession after the code, so the similarity being invoked cannot be traced. GOA then converts it into an IEA (GO:0005856, GO_REF:0000044) that reads as independent of the phylogenetic annotations while resting on the same inference. Either the source entry should be added or the statement reconsidered - and given that the protein has been detected in the cytoplasm by immunohistochemistry, a plain Cytoplasm statement with ECO:0000269 would be better supported than the cytoskeletal one.

Q: Does ACTL8 bind ATP? Five of the 19 residues that contact ATP in beta-actin are non-conservatively substituted in ACTL8 (D157 to Tyr, K213 to Gln, E214 to Met, T303 to Asn, K336 to Asn), but the P1 phosphate loop, the Mg-coordinating Gln137 equivalent and the adenosine-contacting Gly301-Gly302 and Tyr306 equivalents are all intact. This is neither an intact site nor an ablated one, and no ATP-binding annotation exists for ACTL8 in either direction. A nucleotide-binding measurement would decide whether the gene deserves GO:0005524 or a NOT annotation, and either outcome would be more informative than the current silence.

Q: Should a gene whose evolved function is unknown carry a description-only core function? The schema permits it - molecular_function is not a required slot on CoreFunction - so an entry saying "unknown, actin fold retained, no activity assigned" would validate and would silence the "No core functions defined" warning. This review leaves core_functions empty instead, on the view that a core function should assert something and that the warning is the correct signal for an MF-dark gene. If the project would rather have an explicit placeholder for the 25 of 32 human PTHR11937 members with no informative experimental molecular function, that is a convention worth settling once rather than per gene.

Q: Is there any GO representation for a cancer/testis antigen's phenotype in a tissue where it is ectopically expressed? Every functional experiment on ACTL8 is a knockdown in a tumour line that has reactivated a normally testis-restricted gene. The resulting proliferation and migration phenotypes are real and reproducible, but describing them as the gene's biological process conflates what a protein does when misexpressed with what it evolved to do. GO has no qualifier for this distinction, and ACTL8 is a clean example of why one might be useful.

Q: What does ACTL8 do in the testis? It is testis-enriched in HPA and strongly expressed in testis and pancreas, and three of its closest human relatives are characterised sperm proteins: UniProt describes ACTL7A as "Essential for normal spermatogenesis and male fertility" and required for sperm head morphology, acroplaxome formation and acrosome attachment; ACTL9 as a testis-specific protein acting in proacrosomal vesicle fusion and perinuclear theca formation; and ACTL7B as detected only in testis and, to a lesser extent, prostate. The clade is not uniform - ACTRT1's characterised activity is negative regulation of Hedgehog signalling through the GLI1 promoter, and ACTRT3 is described as ubiquitously expressed - so this is a lead rather than a presumption. None of ACTL8's literature examines the testis at all. If it does belong with the sperm-structural members, the annotations it needs are spermatogenesis and sperm-structure terms, and none is present.

Q: Is the ACTL8-CERT1 interaction real? It is the gene's only reported partner, recorded with NbExp=3 in IntAct, but both supporting references are the same laboratory's yeast two-hybrid pipeline and the second is an expansion of the first, so the apparent replication is not independent. No orthogonal assay, no functional follow-up, and no mechanism relating a divergent actin-fold protein to non-vesicular ceramide transport. A single co-immunoprecipitation would either make this the one informative molecular-function annotation available for ACTL8 or retire it.

Q: Should the GO:0030855 epithelial cell differentiation IEP be reconsidered? It comes from a two-dimensional gel study of Caco-2 differentiation in which 53 spots changed and 34 were identified by MALDI-TOF. Peptide-mass fingerprinting within the actin family is intrinsically difficult, and ACTL8 is testis-enriched while Caco-2 is intestinal. The full text is not available here so the identification cannot be checked, but a curator with access could confirm whether the ACTL8 spot assignment was supported by ACTL8-specific peptides.

Current model (mechanistic narrative)

ACTL8 (CT57) is a cancer-testis antigen that functions as an oncogenic driver across multiple epithelial cancers, where it promotes proliferation, migration, invasion, and cell cycle progression while restraining apoptosis [PMID:33883901, PMID:31962007]. Loss-of-function studies in triple-negative breast cancer, gastric cancer, oral squamous cell carcinoma, lung adenocarcinoma, and endometrial cancer consistently show that ACTL8 silencing suppresses tumor cell growth and motility, an effect reproduced in nude-mouse xenografts [PMID:33883901, PMID:35051678, PMID:31962007, PMID:32125225]. Mechanistically, ACTL8 acts upstream of PI3K/AKT/mTOR signaling: knockdown reduces pathway phosphorylation and pharmacological pathway modulators reverse or mimic the ACTL8 phenotype in both breast and gastric cancer models [PMID:33883901, PMID:39322809]. ACTL8 sustains a cell-cycle and proliferation transcriptional program, supporting expression of FOXM1, STMN1, PLK1, BIRC5, CDK1, cyclin B2, cyclin E1, and c-Myc, while its loss derepresses p21 and shifts EMT markers toward an epithelial state [PMID:35051678, PMID:35116946, PMID:32125225]. ACTL8 additionally drives MYC-dependent glutamine metabolism through SLC1A5 and GLS1, maintaining redox homeostasis; MYC re-expression rescues the metabolic phenotype but not p-AKT, placing ACTL8 above both axes PMID:41621692. The small molecule Momordin Ic binds ACTL8 directly and destabilizes it via ubiquitin-proteasome degradation, establishing ACTL8 as a druggable target PMID:41621692. ACTL8 protein is detected in the cytoplasm of tumor cells PMID:41129177.

For comparison all four conventional actins score 19/19 compatible, Arp2 16 + 3, Drosophila
Arp53D 16 + 3.

So this is genuinely intermediate. It is not a case of "fold without residues": most of the
phosphate-binding and adenosine-binding machinery is intact, so ATP binding cannot be excluded and
would be worth testing. Equally it is not intact: five direct contacts, including the P2-loop
aspartate, are gone. The honest conclusion is untested, which is why no ATP-binding term is
proposed here — proposing one would repeat the fold-name-to-activity slip in the opposite
direction.

Filament protomer interface — 38 residues within 4.0 Å of a neighbouring protomer in PDB 6DJO
(four F-actin protomers, chain C analysed). ACTL8: 8 identical, 3 conservative, 24
non-conservative, 3 gaps — only 11 of 38 positions chemically compatible. β-actin scores 37 + 1;
Arp53D 29 + 4. Within the D-loop (structure residues 38–49), which makes the principal
longitudinal contact and is anchored in the alignment by an invariant proline, ACTL8 has 1
identical, 1 conservative and 8 non-conservative of 10 contacts: R39→C38, Q41→E40, G42→N41, V43→P42, M44→G43, V45→P44, M47→Y46, Q49→R48.

Both tallies are unchanged under a second scoring scheme (BLOSUM45 with harsher gaps), so they are
not an artefact of one gap model. The least reliable stretch of the alignment is the actin 60–67
loop, where ACTL8 carries a three-residue deletion — but the D-loop block, which carries the
argument, is independently anchored.

Consequence. Terms that assert ACTL8 is a structural part of an actin filament, or performs
β-actin's role in a postsynaptic actin network, are asserting exactly the property the sequence has
lost. Terms that merely place it in the actin cytoskeleton are not contradicted by this.

6. Family context: MF-dark is the rule for the divergent actin-like clade

32 human members of PTHR11937. 24 have at least one experimental-code MF annotation, but only
7 have an informative one once bare GO:0005515 protein binding is excluded — nearly every
human protein has that from interactome screens, so counting it would make the survey say nothing.

peculiarity; it is the normal state of this clade. And it is not because the family is
uncharacterisable — the conventional actins, Arp2/3 and the INO80/SRCAP actin-related subunits

    descendants of PTN007551913, not only for ACTL8? The node assigns membership to ACTB, ACTG1,
    ACTBL2, ACTL8 and the five POTE genes. ACTB's membership is not in question - it has its own IDA
    and is named in the term's definition, alongside BAF53/ACTL6A, as an actin subunit of the human
    complex. That is the point: the definition specifies which paralog, so the other eight rows are
    all similarity transfers of a purification result. Complex membership is established by
    purifying a particular subunit and does not descend a family, so this looks like a systematic
    rather than a gene-specific problem.
- question: >-
    Why does UniProt's ECO:0000250 subcellular location for ACTL8 cite no source entry? The line
    reads "SUBCELLULAR LOCATION: Cytoplasm, cytoskeleton {ECO:0000250}" with no UniProtKB accession
    after the code, so the similarity being invoked cannot be traced. GOA then converts it into an
    IEA (GO:0005856, GO_REF:0000044) that reads as independent of the phylogenetic annotations while
    resting on the same inference. Either the source entry should be added or the statement
    reconsidered - and given that the protein has been detected in the cytoplasm by
    immunohistochemistry, a plain Cytoplasm statement with ECO:0000269 would be better supported
    than the cytoskeletal one.
- question: >-
    Does ACTL8 bind ATP? Five of the 19 residues that contact ATP in beta-actin are
    non-conservatively substituted in ACTL8 (D157 to Tyr, K213 to Gln, E214 to Met, T303 to Asn,
    K336 to Asn), but the P1 phosphate loop, the Mg-coordinating Gln137 equivalent and the
    adenosine-contacting Gly301-Gly302 and Tyr306 equivalents are all intact. This is neither an
    intact site nor an ablated one, and no ATP-binding annotation exists for ACTL8 in either
    direction. A nucleotide-binding measurement would decide whether the gene deserves GO:0005524
    or a NOT annotation, and either outcome would be more informative than the current silence.
- question: >-
    Should a gene whose evolved function is unknown carry a description-only core function? The
    schema permits it - molecular_function is not a required slot on CoreFunction - so an entry
    saying "unknown, actin fold retained, no activity assigned" would validate and would silence the
    "No core functions defined" warning. This review leaves core_functions empty instead, on the view
    that a core function should assert something and that the warning is the correct signal for an
    MF-dark gene. If the project would rather have an explicit placeholder for the 25 of 32 human
    PTHR11937 members with no informative experimental molecular function, that is a convention worth
    settling once rather than per gene.
- question: >-
    Is there any GO representation for a cancer/testis antigen's phenotype in a tissue where it is
    ectopically expressed? Every functional experiment on ACTL8 is a knockdown in a tumour line
    that has reactivated a normally testis-restricted gene. The resulting proliferation and
    migration phenotypes are real and reproducible, but describing them as the gene's biological
    process conflates what a protein does when misexpressed with what it evolved to do. GO has no
    qualifier for this distinction, and ACTL8 is a clean example of why one might be useful.
- question: >-
    What does ACTL8 do in the testis? It is testis-enriched in HPA and strongly expressed in testis
    and pancreas, and three of its closest human relatives are characterised sperm proteins:
    UniProt describes ACTL7A as "Essential for normal spermatogenesis and male fertility" and
    required for sperm head morphology, acroplaxome formation and acrosome attachment; ACTL9 as a
    testis-specific protein acting in proacrosomal vesicle fusion and perinuclear theca formation;
    and ACTL7B as detected only in testis and, to a lesser extent, prostate. The clade is not
    uniform - ACTRT1's characterised activity is negative regulation of Hedgehog signalling through
    the GLI1 promoter, and ACTRT3 is described as ubiquitously expressed - so this is a lead rather
    than a presumption. None of ACTL8's literature examines the testis at all. If it does belong
    with the sperm-structural members, the annotations it needs are spermatogenesis and
    sperm-structure terms, and none is present.
- question: >-
    Is the ACTL8-CERT1 interaction real? It is the gene's only reported partner, recorded with
    NbExp=3 in IntAct, but both supporting references are the same laboratory's yeast two-hybrid
    pipeline and the second is an expansion of the first, so the apparent replication is not
    independent. No orthogonal assay, no functional follow-up, and no mechanism relating a divergent
    actin-fold protein to non-vesicular ceramide transport. A single co-immunoprecipitation would
    either make this the one informative molecular-function annotation available for ACTL8 or retire
    it.
- question: >-
    Should the GO:0030855 epithelial cell differentiation IEP be reconsidered? It comes from a
    two-dimensional gel study of Caco-2 differentiation in which 53 spots changed and 34 were
    identified by MALDI-TOF. Peptide-mass fingerprinting within the actin family is intrinsically
    difficult, and ACTL8 is testis-enriched while Caco-2 is intestinal. The full text is not
    available here so the identification cannot be checked, but a curator with access could confirm
    whether the ACTL8 spot assignment was supported by ACTL8-specific peptides.

- hypothesis: >-
    ACTL8 does not polymerise into filaments, consistent with the loss of 24 of 38 protomer-contact
    positions and almost the entire D-loop.
  description: >-
    Express and purify recombinant ACTL8 and test it in the standard actin assays: pyrene-actin
    fluorescence for spontaneous polymerisation, high-speed cosedimentation for filament formation,
    and electron microscopy of any polymer formed. Then test the two alternatives the interface data
    do not exclude - co-polymerisation with beta-actin at sub-stoichiometric ratios, and binding to
    preformed F-actin without incorporation. A protein that neither polymerises nor binds F-actin
    would settle GO:0005884 and GO:0015629; one that decorates filaments would need an
    actin-binding molecular function rather than a filament-component location.
  experiment_type: biochemistry
- hypothesis: >-
    ACTL8 retains nucleotide binding despite losing five direct ATP contacts, because the P1 loop
    and the adenosine pocket are intact.
  description: >-

    named after the actin fold is more useful than the current absence of any statement.
