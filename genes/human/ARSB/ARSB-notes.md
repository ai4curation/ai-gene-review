# ARSB functional review notes

ARSB is human N-acetylgalactosamine-4-sulfatase (P15848; HGNC:714; Gene:411), also called arylsulfatase B, ASB or G4S. Its principal work is removal of GalNAc 4-sulfate during lysosomal chondroitin sulfate and dermatan sulfate degradation. The catalytic calcium and Cys91-derived formylglycine chemistry belong to that same enzymatic function. This review distinguishes physiological glycan substrates from aromatic substrates used to measure arylsulfatase activity.

## Human enzyme and substrate evidence

The complete original abstract of [PMID:2303452](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2303452/) describes expression of human ASB in baby-hamster kidney cells, pulse-chase processing, dense-lysosome localization and mannose-6-phosphate-receptor-dependent delivery. This is a human protein expressed in a hamster cell system. The substrate is transported; the abstract does not establish a transport activity of ARSB itself. Complete original Methods/Results were not recovered. Precise signal-cleavage boundaries are omitted from the synthesis because the older abstract and current model use different chain boundaries.

[PMID:18285341](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18285341/) uses human MCF7 cells with ASB/GALNS silencing and overexpression. The complete cached abstract reports corresponding changes in C4S/CS pools and proteoglycan expression. Other sulfated GAGs, including DS, did not change in that experimental setting; this is not evidence against the independently established DS reaction. The study is not described here as a newly inspected purified-enzyme substrate assay: its full Methods remain unread. [PMID:19306108](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19306108/) explicitly identifies the 4-sulfate-removing activity and describes human NCM460/T84 colonic-cell perturbations. Its complete abstract supports the existing migration-related observations but not an ARSB RhoA catalytic activity.

The identity and complete primary abstract of [PMID:9032078](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9032078/), DOI 10.1016/S0969-2126(97)00185-8, were independently checked against the original structural citation and [RCSB 1FSU](https://www.rcsb.org/structure/1FSU). The human P15848 structure contains a calcium ion at the active site and identifies the modified catalytic residue. The recombinant expression system is distinguished from the organism of the protein. A full crystallographic Methods read is not claimed. The normal abstract-only publication record has now been recovered and checked against this primary identity. The review attaches its calcium statement with the full-text-unavailable flag; no source text has been manufactured.

## Rat donor experiments and contextual consequences

The original donor references were traced through the [official MGI comparative Arsb annotations](https://www.informatics.jax.org/homology/GOGraph/Arsb) and its older orthology display. Rat UniProt P50430 and the source Ensembl identifier are preserved. The following six already-existing normal caches were read and left unchanged.

- [PMID:8037](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8037/): the complete abstract measures rat hepatic arylsulfatase A/B activity ratios after starvation, glucagon and surgery, using electrophoretic separation and an aromatic substrate. That establishes activity changes under conditions promoting autophagy; full evidence that Arsb executes autophagy remains unresolved. The paper's historical suggestion that B could be degraded A is not adopted as modern gene/protein identity.
- [PMID:2290353](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2290353/): the complete abstract examines female rat liver cell populations at different ages and diets. Older restricted Kupffer/endothelial populations showed higher enzyme activities, whereas hepatocytes did not show a significant dietary effect. The nutrient-response annotation must retain these tissue, age and organism limits.
- [PMID:6137211](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/6137211/): the complete abstract concerns rat-enzyme pH-dependent kinetics and ionization. Its apparent full-text section repeats the abstract. The original full experiment supporting a cell/organism response-to-pH annotation remains unresolved; an enzyme's pH optimum alone does not settle it.
- [PMID:15040](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15040/): the complete abstract includes rat and hamster estrous-cycle observations and rat steroid interventions. Lower-dose estradiol restores cervical B activity after ovariectomy; a higher dose inhibits it, and cycloheximide prevents the increase. Response to estrogen is not hydrolysis of estrogen.
- [PMID:1682910](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1682910/): the complete abstract describes dose/time-dependent arylsulfatase inhibition in male rat CNS following methylmercuric chloride. This is a response-compatible observation, not evidence for a mercury-detoxifying function.
- [PMID:24311516](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24311516/): target Methods, Results, Figures 4 and 6–8 captions and selected Discussion were read in the normal full-text cache. Rat fetal astrocyte/neuron cocultures received ARSB siRNA or recombinant human ARSB. Knockdown reduced enzyme activity and neurite growth; human enzyme attenuated ethanol-associated growth defects. Two C4S antibodies and the unchanged GALNS control are distinguished from the inherited enzyme-assay method. Human recombinant protein in rat coculture is not a human-neuron experiment, and loss/rescue is not assigned as intrinsic growth-factor activity.

The positive contextual observations can support retaining existing non-core response or migration annotations. They do not warrant new hormone, mercury, growth-factor or RhoA molecular functions. The inaccessible full donor experiment remains explicit for the autophagy and pH assertions. Cell-surface assertions likewise remain unresolved because their precise source localization experiments were not inspected.

## Actual Reactome roles and compartments

The original cached summaries remain immutable. Their actual participant graphs were examined separately.

[R-HSA-1606789](https://reactome.org/content/detail/R-HSA-1606789) models human ARSB-catalyzed DS desulfation, with DS-chain input, desulfated-chain output and sulfate. The catalyst is [ARSB:Ca2+, R-HSA-1606792](https://reactome.org/content/detail/R-HSA-1606792), containing calcium and [OxA-ARSB, R-HSA-1606837](https://reactome.org/content/detail/R-HSA-1606837), explicitly linked to human P15848/HGNC:714 in lysosomal lumen. [R-HSA-1793207](https://reactome.org/content/detail/R-HSA-1793207) places the same catalyst in the CS pathway. Its C4S/C6S summary is broader than established 4-sulfatase specificity; the review does not adopt C6S hydrolysis from that wording.

The mutant sources R-HSA-2282889 and R-HSA-9036065 provide the original lysosomal-lumen annotations. Their full nested mutant sets were not independently reconstructed. The latter's DS title and cached CS wording are inconsistent. These limitations do not refute the compartment independently established for active human ARSB, and neither mutant summary is used to broaden normal substrate specificity.

[SUMF1 maturation, R-HSA-1614362](https://reactome.org/content/detail/R-HSA-1614362), has ARSB in its ER-lumen substrate set R-HSA-1614312 through entity R-HSA-1614331. SUMF1 performs the oxidation; ARSB is its substrate. ER transit is a non-core location, not an ARSB oxidoreductase function.

[Azurophil-granule release, R-HSA-6798751](https://reactome.org/content/detail/R-HSA-6798751), contains ARSB in input set R-HSA-6798742 and extracellular output set R-HSA-6806184, including ARSB granule entity R-HSA-6798774. [Ficolin-rich granule release, R-HSA-6800434](https://reactome.org/content/detail/R-HSA-6800434), similarly contains ARSB in input R-HSA-6800431 and output R-HSA-6806481, with granule entity R-HSA-6800887. These are model-supported cargo locations; being released does not establish that ARSB executes degranulation.

## Unresolved original assertions

[PMID:1718978](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1718978/) identifies the human G137V disease variant. The complete cached abstract and selected original Methods/Results, including precursor-stability and trafficking experiments, were read. The original text distinguishes patient fibroblasts and expression of human constructs in mouse LTK cells. A complete source adjudication for lysosome organization was not achieved; the term is left undecided, without claiming that the paper lacks every relevant experiment. [GO:0007040](https://amigo.geneontology.org/amigo/term/GO:0007040) concerns organelle assembly, arrangement and disassembly. [GO:0007041](https://amigo.geneontology.org/amigo/term/GO:0007041) concerns directed lysosomal transport; M6P-dependent transport of ARSB cargo alone does not establish performance of that process by ARSB.

[PMID:23533145](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23533145/) concerns human expressed-prostatic-secretion urine exosomes. The pooled sampling, isolation and proteomics workflow were inspected, but the exact ARSB supplementary protein/peptide row remains unread. The exosome annotation stays undecided. Omission from article prose is neither absence nor contamination evidence.

The PAINT source of the broad sulfatase IBA is PTN002233267. Tree/MSA/IBD placement remains unresolved. Target-specific human chemistry nevertheless supports the inherited function. Neither donor count nor the target's presence among descendant evidence is treated as a flaw.

## Process proposal and ontology checks

The pre-existing author-added DS catabolism proposal is supported by the actual human ARSB catalyst in R-HSA-1606789. The enzyme performs the sulfate-removal step; it is not the consumed glycan substrate. Comparator [IDUA](https://www.informatics.jax.org/homology/GOGraph/Idua) has a human GO:0030209 IDA assertion linked to PMID:24036510, while [human IDS reaction R-HSA-1793182](https://reactome.org/content/detail/R-HSA-1793182) explicitly links another catalytic DS-degradation step to this process. The local GO-CAM index has no ARSB/P15848 match; that is not a claim that no model exists elsewhere.

The indexed [GO:0030209](https://amigo.geneontology.org/amigo/term/GO:0030209) and [GO:0030207](https://amigo.geneontology.org/amigo/term/GO:0030207) pages have proteoglycan-catabolism/metabolism parents. Current comparative displays use shorter GAG labels. No unverified formal GO:0006027 ancestor relation is asserted. The prior broad author-added GAG-catabolism proposal is omitted because the named CS/DS processes already describe the established work; no original GOA row is deleted.

The actual [GO:0003943 definition and parent](https://flybase.org/reports/GO:0003943) identify GalNAc-4-sulfatase chemistry and sulfuric ester hydrolase parentage. [GO:0004065](https://amigo.geneontology.org/amigo/term/GO:0004065) describes aryl-sulfate hydrolysis and has the same parent; it is not the more general version of GO:0003943. [GO:0005509](https://amigo.geneontology.org/amigo/term/GO:0005509) refines metal-ion binding to calcium binding. [GO:0009268](https://amigo.geneontology.org/amigo/term/GO:0009268) concerns cell/organism changes responding to pH; it does not require direct sensing by ARSB.
