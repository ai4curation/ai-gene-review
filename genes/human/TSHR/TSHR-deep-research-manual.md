# TSHR manual literature synthesis

TSHR encodes the basolateral thyroid-stimulating hormone receptor, a leucine-rich-repeat class A GPCR that binds pituitary TSH and the GPHA2:GPHB5 heterodimer thyrostimulin. Its core molecular activity is Gs-coupled TSH/thyrostimulin receptor activity at the thyroid follicular cell surface, where ligand binding activates adenylate cyclase, raises cAMP, and drives thyroid hormone biosynthesis and secretion. UniProt summarizes this central role as: "Receptor for the thyroid-stimulating hormone (TSH) or thyrotropin" and "TSHR is coupled to G(s) proteins and mediates the activation of adenylate cyclase" [file:human/TSHR/TSHR-uniprot.txt].

Glycoprotein hormone recognition depends on the sulfated hinge region of this receptor subfamily. Costagliola et al. showed that the surface TSH receptor is tyrosine-sulfated and that the conserved sulfotyrosine motif is required for high-affinity TSH binding and receptor activation; the same abstract reports that the motif appears to play a similar role in LH/CG and FSH receptors [PMID:11847099, "Sulfation of one of the two tyrosines in the motif is mandatory for high-affinity binding of TSH and activation of the receptor."]. Nakabayashi et al. identified GPHA2 and GPHB5 as a heterodimeric ligand, thyrostimulin, that activated human TSHR but not LH or FSH receptors, stimulated cAMP production, and increased thymidine incorporation by cultured thyroid cells [PMID:12045258, "Recombinant A2/B5 heterodimeric glycoproteins, purified using cation exchange and size fractionation chromatography, activated human TSH receptors, but not LH and FSH receptors"].

Human TSHR is localized to the plasma membrane and specifically to the basolateral membrane in polarized thyroid cells. This fits its endocrine-receptor role: circulating TSH reaches the basolateral surface, not the thyroid follicle lumen. Reactome's human TSHR event likewise places TSH binding at the basolateral membrane of thyroid cells and connects receptor activation to G alpha s, adenylate cyclase, and intracellular cAMP [Reactome:R-HSA-391375, "Thyroid-stimulating hormone (TSH, thyrotropin) binds thyrotropin receptor (TSHR) on the basolateral membrane of thyroid cells"].

The 2022 TSHR structure captured the activated TSHR-Gs complex and supports the receptor's direct G protein-coupled receptor activity [PMID:35940205, "Autoantibody mimicry of hormone action at the thyrotropin receptor."]. TSHR also undergoes regulated cell-surface recycling. Lahuna et al. showed that SCRIB directly binds the TSHR PDZ-binding motif and participates in an hScrib-betaPIX-GIT1-ARF6 pathway required for TSHR trafficking and receptor function [PMID:15775968, "hScrib directly binds to TSHR, inhibits basal receptor endocytosis and promotes recycling, and thus TSHR signalling, at the cell membrane."].

Rows that describe the core TSH/adenylate-cyclase pathway should be retained as the main TSHR function. The brown-adipose thermogenesis row is a supported but peripheral orthology inference from mouse/rat work [PMID:18559984, "These results indicate that not only thyroid hormone but also thyroid-stimulating hormone (TSH)/TSHR are involved in the expression mechanism of UCP-1 in mouse brown adipose tissue."]. The old `cellular response to thyrotropin-releasing hormone` row is a term-choice error: thyrotropin-releasing hormone is TRH, not TSH/thyrotropin, and PMID:11847099 assays glycoprotein hormone receptor recognition rather than TRH signaling.
