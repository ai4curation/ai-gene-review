CC   -!- FUNCTION: Associates with the cytoplasmic domain of a variety of
CC       cadherins. The association of catenins to cadherins produces a complex
CC       which is linked to the actin filament network, and which seems to be of
CC       primary importance for cadherins cell-adhesion properties
CC       (PubMed:40983751). Can associate with both E- and N-cadherins.
CC       Originally believed to be a stable component of E-cadherin/catenin
CC       adhesion complexes and to mediate the linkage of cadherins to the actin
CC       cytoskeleton at adherens junctions. In contrast, cortical actin was
CC       found to be much more dynamic than E-cadherin/catenin complexes and
CC       CTNNA1 was shown not to bind to F-actin when assembled in the complex
CC       suggesting a different linkage between actin and adherens junctions
CC       components. The homodimeric form may regulate actin filament assembly
CC       and inhibit actin branching by competing with the Arp2/3 complex for
CC       binding to actin filaments. Involved in the regulation of WWTR1/TAZ,
