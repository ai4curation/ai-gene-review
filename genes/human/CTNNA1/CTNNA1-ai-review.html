Gene Description

Alpha-E-catenin is a cytoplasmic adhesion protein that couples cadherin–catenin complexes to the actin cortex at adherens junctions. Its N-terminal region associates with beta-catenin or plakoglobin, while actin binding, force-dependent conformational changes and recruitment of vinculin and other cytoskeletal partners stabilize junctional linkage. Cytosolic alpha-catenin can oligomerize and influence actin organization, and cytoplasmic and nuclear pools are also found outside junctions. The shorter CTNNA1b product lacks the N-terminal beta-catenin-binding region.

Core Functions

Full-length alpha-E-catenin anchors cadherin-associated complexes to the cortical actin cytoskeleton. Its N-terminal beta-catenin or plakoglobin binding recruits it to the junctional complex, and force-dependent F-actin engagement by its C-terminal region reinforces the linkage. Recruitment of vinculin and organization of EPLIN- and centralspindlin-associated assemblies support the apical adhesion belt; CAP350 recruitment provides a distinct connection to microtubules at basolateral adherens contacts. These interactions contribute to cell-cell adhesion and apical junction assembly.

Interpretation

The CTNNA1 IBAs are placed at a eumetazoan node, so they assert inheritance only
within animals, which is compatible with all the experimental donors. The
Dictyostelium alpha-catenin-like protein, which binds the beta-catenin-related
protein Aardvark and F-actin, sits in the PANTHER vinculin family. Its
beta-catenin-type binding therefore reaches human VCL (beta-catenin binding IBA at
PTN005285701), not CTNNA1. PANTHER does not put the animal alpha-catenin clade and
the amoebozoan protein in one family. Whether Dd ctnnA is orthologous to animal
alpha-catenin or to the vinculin/alpha-catenin ancestor is not settled by this
classification.
