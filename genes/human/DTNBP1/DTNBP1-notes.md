# DTNBP1 evidence notes

## 2026-10-18 — integrated annotation and source review

Dysbindin is a coiled-coil BLOC-1 subunit involved in endosomal cargo organization, lysosome-related organelle biogenesis and neuronal trafficking. Human genetics links its loss to Hermansky-Pudlak syndrome type 7. The biological synthesis distinguishes participation in an assembled membrane-remodeling complex from an autonomous enzyme, motor, fusion catalyst or tether. The 97 imported assertions are retained with 26 ACCEPT, 20 MODIFY, 48 KEEP_AS_NON_CORE and three UNDECIDED decisions; no new annotation is introduced.

Purified human BLOC-1 containing dysbindin residues 1–259 deforms negatively charged membranes, including initially flat giant vesicles. This supports a contribution to membrane bending, without identifying which dysbindin surfaces perform the membrane contact or showing independent activity of isolated dysbindin. PI3P as well as PI4P supports the measured system. Cellular kinase perturbations are distinct from dysbindin perturbations. The earlier recombinant architecture study uses residues 1–217 in the intact complex and reports an isolated-core start of 48 in Methods versus 47 in Results. AP-3 association there is measured for the complex. [PMID:36169638; PMID:22203680]

Endosomal carrier extension also involves KIF13A and actin-associated machinery. Human HeLa/MNT-1 experiments are distinct from mouse pallidin rescue, and the BLOC-1 siRNA mixture targets other subunits. A BLOC-1/KIF13A co-immunoprecipitation is positive, whereas attempted BLOC-1/Annexin A2 physical association was negative. Functional coordination does not establish a direct dysbindin bridge. Older endosomal tethering interpretations remain models. Endogenous human MNT-1 immunogold independently places dysbindin on endosomal tubules and a smaller melanosomal limiting-membrane pool. [PMID:26725201; PMID:16837549]

Neuronal effects are mechanistically differentiated. Acute mouse-protein rescue restores fusion-event kinetics and quantal size without immediately restoring every release parameter. Human neuronal-cell and rat-neuron experiments support D2 internalization/signaling, while separate mouse experiments emphasize receptor recycling. These findings justify a physiological trafficking/release unit without an invented autonomous MF. The WAVE2 literature similarly distinguishes a negative purified minimal-fragment system from a positive cell-derived complex assay. Dysbindin is also a DNA-PK substrate; that is not dysbindin kinase activity. [PMID:18504299; PMID:17989303; PMID:19887632; PMID:20531346; PMID:19142223]
