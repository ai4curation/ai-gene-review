# Comprehensive Research Report: FGF23 (Fibroblast Growth Factor 23)

## Gene Identity and Verification

FGF23 (UniProt accession Q9GZV9) encodes fibroblast growth factor 23, a bone-derived endocrine hormone that functions as the principal phosphaturic regulator of mineral homeostasis in humans (agoro2023regulationoffgf23 pages 3-4, edmonston2024fgf23andklotho pages 2-4). The protein belongs to the heparin-binding growth factors family and contains characteristic FGF domains (IPR002209, IPR008996), confirming the identity matches the provided UniProt annotation.

## Protein Structure and Post-Translational Regulation

FGF23 is synthesized as a 251-amino-acid precursor protein that, following signal peptide cleavage, yields a secreted 227-amino-acid glycoprotein with an approximate molecular mass of 32 kDa (agoro2023regulationoffgf23 pages 1-2, dastghaib2023newconceptsin pages 2-4). The intact, biologically active form (iFGF23) can be proteolytically processed at the R176HTR179↓S180AE motif by a furin-like proprotein convertase, generating an N-terminal fragment (~20 kDa) and a C-terminal fragment (~12 kDa) (agoro2023regulationoffgf23 pages 1-2, portalescastillo2022pthfgf23klotho pages 3-5).

Post-translational modifications critically regulate FGF23 bioactivity. GALNT3-mediated O-glycosylation—particularly at threonine 178 following prior glycosylation at threonine 171—protects the intact hormone from proteolytic cleavage and thereby increases circulating bioactive FGF23 (agoro2023regulationoffgf23 pages 1-2, agoro2023regulationoffgf23 pages 3-4). Conversely, FAM20C phosphorylates serine 180, which inhibits GALNT3 activity and promotes furin-mediated cleavage into inactive fragments (agoro2023regulationoffgf23 pages 1-2, portalescastillo2022pthfgf23klotho pages 3-5). This dynamic balance between glycosylation-mediated stabilization and phosphorylation-promoted cleavage determines the proportion of circulating intact hormone versus fragments. Mutations at arginine 176 or 179 that prevent cleavage cause accumulation of intact FGF23 and lead to autosomal-dominant hypophosphatemic rickets (agoro2023regulationoffgf23 pages 1-2, portalescastillo2022pthfgf23klotho pages 3-5).

## Cellular and Tissue Localization

### Sites of Production

FGF23 is produced primarily by osteocytes and osteoblast-lineage cells within bone tissue, which serve as the main endocrine source (agoro2023regulationoffgf23 pages 1-2, dastghaib2023newconceptsin pages 1-2, agoro2023regulationoffgf23 pages 3-4). These bone cells detect circulating metabolites, hormones, and minerals—particularly phosphate and calcium—and regulate FGF23 production accordingly through transcriptional, post-translational, and proteolytic mechanisms (agoro2023regulationoffgf23 pages 1-2, agoro2023regulationoffgf23 pages 2-3). Under specific physiological or pathological conditions, bone marrow stromal cells and erythroid cells can contribute to FGF23 production, particularly during anemia or in response to elevated erythropoietin (agoro2023regulationoffgf23 pages 1-2).

### Target Tissues and Localization of Action

The kidney represents the principal endocrine target organ where FGF23 exerts its canonical effects on mineral metabolism (latic2022interactionofvitamin pages 5-7, agoro2023regulationoffgf23 pages 3-4, latic2022interactionofvitamin pages 7-8). Within the kidney, FGF23 acts on both proximal and distal renal tubules through basolateral FGFR–α-Klotho receptor complexes. In the proximal tubule, FGF23 regulates phosphate reabsorption and vitamin D metabolism; in the distal tubule, it can modulate calcium and sodium reabsorption through TRPV5 and NCC channels, respectively (latic2022interactionofvitamin pages 5-7, latic2022interactionofvitamin pages 7-8).

The parathyroid glands represent another important physiological target tissue, where FGF23 can suppress parathyroid hormone (PTH) gene expression and secretion (dastghaib2023newconceptsin pages 1-2, martinezheredia2024nonclassicaleffectsof pages 2-4). Additional target tissues include bone itself, where FGF23 may exert local autocrine or paracrine effects on osteoblast activity and bone remodeling, although these effects are complex and context-dependent (martinezheredia2024nonclassicaleffectsof pages 2-4, martinezheredia2024nonclassicaleffectsof pages 4-5). Under pathological conditions—particularly in chronic kidney disease (CKD) with markedly elevated FGF23 levels—the heart becomes a significant target organ, with effects on cardiomyocytes and cardiac fibroblasts contributing to cardiovascular remodeling (diazhaaz2025effectsoffibroblast pages 4-5, edmonston2024fgf23andklotho pages 5-6).

## Molecular Function and Mechanism of Action

### Receptor Binding and Signaling Complex

FGF23 functions as a hormone rather than an enzyme, and therefore has no catalytic substrate or enzymatic activity. Its primary molecular function is to serve as a signaling ligand that binds to fibroblast growth factor receptors (FGFRs) in obligate association with the transmembrane co-receptor α-Klotho (edmonston2024fgf23andklotho pages 2-4, dastghaib2023newconceptsin pages 1-2).

The functional receptor complex consists of FGF23 bound to specific FGFR isoforms together with α-Klotho. Structural and biochemical studies have identified FGFR1c, FGFR3c, and FGFR4 as the principal receptor isoforms involved in FGF23 signaling (edmonston2024fgf23andklotho pages 2-4, chen2023structuralbasisfor pages 1-2). High-resolution cryo-electron microscopy structures reveal that FGF23's N-terminal region engages the FGFR, while its C-terminal tail interacts with α-Klotho (edmonston2024fgf23andklotho pages 2-4, chen2023structuralbasisfor pages 2-3). α-Klotho functions as a molecular scaffold that stabilizes the FGF23–FGFR complex, converting a canonical FGF receptor into a high-affinity FGF23-specific receptor (edmonston2024fgf23andklotho pages 2-4, chen2023structuralbasisfor pages 1-2).

Recent structural studies reveal an asymmetric 1:2:1:1 stoichiometry for the active signaling complex: FGF23–FGFR–α-Klotho–heparan sulfate (chen2023structuralbasisfor pages 2-3, chen2023structuralbasisfor pages 1-2). In this model, a single heparan sulfate chain enables the primary 1:1:1 FGF23–FGFR–α-Klotho ternary complex to recruit a second FGFR molecule, leading to asymmetric receptor dimerization that is mandatory for tyrosine kinase activation and downstream signaling (chen2023structuralbasisfor pages 2-3, chen2023structuralbasisfor pages 6-7). Notably, α-Klotho does not directly recruit the secondary receptor but rather stabilizes the primary complex, allowing heparan sulfate to mediate receptor dimerization (chen2023structuralbasisfor pages 1-2, chen2023structuralbasisfor pages 6-7).

### Signaling Pathways

Upon receptor binding, FGF23 activates FGFR substrate 2α (FRS2α) and downstream RAS–mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK) signaling, including ERK1 and ERK2 (edmonston2024fgf23andklotho pages 2-4, agoro2023regulationoffgf23 pages 3-4, latic2022interactionofvitamin pages 7-8). In renal proximal tubules, MAPK signaling through ERK1/2 leads to activation of early growth response protein 1 (EGR1) and serum/glucocorticoid-regulated kinase 1 (SGK1), which mediate effects on phosphate transporters and contribute to phosphaturia (agoro2023regulationoffgf23 pages 2-3, latic2022interactionofvitamin pages 14-15).

Under pathological conditions characterized by extremely high FGF23 concentrations and α-Klotho deficiency—such as advanced CKD—FGF23 can activate FGFR4 in cardiac tissue independently of α-Klotho (prudhomme2024antiinflammatoryroleof pages 3-5, diazhaaz2025effectsoffibroblast pages 4-5, edmonston2024fgf23andklotho pages 5-6). This klotho-independent pathway involves FGFR4-mediated recruitment and phosphorylation of phospholipase Cγ (PLCγ), which activates the calcineurin/nuclear factor of activated T-cells (NFAT) signaling cascade associated with pathological cardiac hypertrophy and remodeling (diazhaaz2025effectsoffibroblast pages 4-5, nakano2023directandindirect pages 2-3).

## Primary Physiological Functions

### Regulation of Phosphate Homeostasis

FGF23's primary physiological role is to function as a phosphaturic hormone that promotes urinary phosphate excretion and lowers serum phosphate concentrations (agoro2023regulationoffgf23 pages 3-4, edmonston2024fgf23andklotho pages 2-4). In the renal proximal tubule, FGF23 signaling through FGFR1–α-Klotho complexes and downstream MAPK activation promotes the internalization and degradation of apical membrane sodium-phosphate cotransporters, specifically NPT2a (NaPi-IIa, encoded by SLC34A1) and NPT2c (NaPi-IIc, encoded by SLC34A3) (portalescastillo2022pthfgf23klotho pages 3-5, agoro2023regulationoffgf23 pages 3-4, agoro2023regulationoffgf23 pages 2-3). The removal of these transporters from the brush border membrane decreases filtered phosphate reabsorption from tubular fluid, thereby increasing urinary phosphate excretion and reducing serum phosphate levels (agoro2023regulationoffgf23 pages 3-4, agoro2023regulationoffgf23 pages 7-8).

### Regulation of Vitamin D Metabolism

FGF23 exerts profound effects on vitamin D metabolism by regulating the expression of key vitamin D-metabolizing enzymes in the kidney (agoro2023regulationoffgf23 pages 3-4, latic2022interactionofvitamin pages 7-8, latic2022interactionofvitamin pages 5-7). FGF23 suppresses transcription of CYP27B1, the enzyme responsible for 1α-hydroxylation that converts 25-hydroxyvitamin D into the active metabolite 1,25-dihydroxyvitamin D (calcitriol) (agoro2023regulationoffgf23 pages 3-4, latic2022interactionofvitamin pages 7-8, latic2022interactionofvitamin pages 2-4). Simultaneously, FGF23 increases transcription of CYP24A1, the 24-hydroxylase enzyme that promotes vitamin D catabolism and inactivation (agoro2023regulationoffgf23 pages 3-4, latic2022interactionofvitamin pages 7-8, latic2022interactionofvitamin pages 2-4). Through these reciprocal effects—suppressing synthesis while enhancing degradation—FGF23 reduces circulating concentrations of active vitamin D (latic2022interactionofvitamin pages 7-8, martinezheredia2024nonclassicaleffectsof pages 1-2).

This regulation forms a critical negative feedback loop: circulating 1,25-dihydroxyvitamin D stimulates FGF23 transcription in bone through vitamin D receptor-dependent mechanisms, while FGF23 acts on the kidney to limit active vitamin D production (agoro2023regulationoffgf23 pages 3-4, latic2022interactionofvitamin pages 5-7, portalescastillo2022pthfgf23klotho pages 5-7). Lower calcitriol levels indirectly reduce intestinal phosphate and calcium absorption, complementing FGF23's direct renal phosphaturic effect and contributing to overall phosphate homeostasis (portalescastillo2022pthfgf23klotho pages 3-5, latic2022interactionofvitamin pages 5-7).

### Integration with Parathyroid Hormone Axis

FGF23 participates in a complex regulatory network with parathyroid hormone (PTH) and vitamin D. Under physiological conditions, FGF23 can suppress PTH secretion from parathyroid cells through both α-Klotho-dependent ERK1/2 signaling and proposed klotho-independent calcineurin-dependent pathways (martinezheredia2024nonclassicaleffectsof pages 2-4). However, in advanced CKD, prolonged exposure to high FGF23 levels downregulates parathyroid α-Klotho and FGFR1c expression, producing FGF23 resistance (martinezheredia2024nonclassicaleffectsof pages 2-4). Combined with FGF23-mediated suppression of calcitriol and consequent hypocalcemia, this resistance contributes to the development of secondary hyperparathyroidism in CKD (martinezheredia2024nonclassicaleffectsof pages 2-4, edmonston2024fgf23andklotho pages 4-5).

### Mineral and Endocrine Regulators

Elevated serum phosphate represents a major stimulus for FGF23 production (vervloet2022sheddinglighton pages 2-4, vervloet2022sheddinglighton pages 1-2, agoro2023regulationoffgf23 pages 2-3). Osteocyte phosphate sensing involves the sodium-phosphate cotransporter PiT2 and ligand-independent FGFR1 signaling (agoro2023regulationoffgf23 pages 2-3). Phosphate also increases GALNT3 expression, promoting protective O-glycosylation that stabilizes intact FGF23 and reduces its intracellular cleavage (vervloet2022sheddinglighton pages 2-4, vervloet2022sheddinglighton pages 1-2).

Calcium can directly stimulate FGF23 transcription and appears necessary for phosphate- or PTH-induced FGF23 increases (vervloet2022sheddinglighton pages 2-4, agoro2023regulationoffgf23 pages 2-3). Calcium-phosphate-protein complexes known as calciprotein particles (CPPs) can stimulate osteocyte FGF23 expression and secretion, potentially contributing to elevated FGF23 in CKD (vervloet2022sheddinglighton pages 2-4, agoro2023regulationoffgf23 pages 2-3).

Active vitamin D (1,25-dihydroxyvitamin D/calcitriol) strongly induces FGF23 transcription in osteoblast-lineage cells through vitamin D receptor-dependent mechanisms (agoro2023regulationoffgf23 pages 3-4, vervloet2022sheddinglighton pages 4-6, agoro2023regulationoffgf23 pages 4-5). This creates the negative feedback loop wherein FGF23 subsequently suppresses renal calcitriol synthesis.

Parathyroid hormone generally increases FGF23 during sustained exposure, signaling through PTH1R and the intracellular mediator Nurr1, and also by suppressing sclerostin to activate Wnt-associated FGF23 expression (vervloet2022sheddinglighton pages 10-11, vervloet2022sheddinglighton pages 4-6, agoro2023regulationoffgf23 pages 4-5). However, acute PTH effects can be biphasic, sometimes lowering intact FGF23 through phosphaturia or increased cleavage (vervloet2022sheddinglighton pages 10-11, agoro2023regulationoffgf23 pages 4-5).

### Kidney-Derived Factors

In CKD and acute kidney injury, several kidney-derived factors stimulate bone FGF23 production (agoro2023regulationoffgf23 pages 1-2, agoro2023regulationoffgf23 pages 4-5, agoro2023regulationoffgf23 pages 3-4). Erythropoietin (EPO) induces FGF23 in osteoblast/osteocyte-lineage and erythroid cells, particularly during anemia (agoro2023regulationoffgf23 pages 1-2). In CKD, renal EGF–EGFR–HIF1α signaling induces lipocalin-2 (LCN2), which then activates osteocyte LCN2R–cAMP–PKA–CREB signaling to stimulate FGF23 transcription (agoro2023regulationoffgf23 pages 4-5, agoro2023regulationoffgf23 pages 3-4). Additionally, glycerol-3-phosphate is converted to lysophosphatidic acid (LPA), which activates LPAR1 signaling in bone to promote FGF23 production (agoro2023regulationoffgf23 pages 4-5, agoro2023regulationoffgf23 pages 3-4).

### Local Bone Regulators

PHEX and DMP1 are local bone-derived proteins that normally restrain osteocytic FGF23 expression (vervloet2022sheddinglighton pages 10-11, vervloet2022sheddinglighton pages 4-6). Loss-of-function mutations in either gene cause excessive intact FGF23 production, resulting in renal phosphate wasting and hypophosphatemia characteristic of X-linked hypophosphatemia (PHEX deficiency) or autosomal recessive hypophosphatemic rickets (DMP1 deficiency) (vervloet2022sheddinglighton pages 10-11, vervloet2022sheddinglighton pages 4-6). In CKD, uremia can suppress osteocyte DMP1 expression, contributing to increased FGF23 production (vervloet2022sheddinglighton pages 4-6).

## Clinical Significance and Disease Associations

### Chronic Kidney Disease and Mineral-Bone Disorder

FGF23 plays a central but ultimately maladaptive role in CKD-mineral and bone disorder (CKD-MBD) (ponsbelda2023mineralmetabolismin pages 10-12, edmonston2024fgf23andklotho pages 4-5, vervloet2022sheddinglighton pages 4-6). As kidney function declines, circulating FGF23 rises early and progressively, often before the development of overt hyperphosphatemia or increased PTH (ponsbelda2023mineralmetabolismin pages 10-12, edmonston2024fgf23andklotho pages 4-5). Initially, elevated FGF23 represents an adaptive response that increases fractional phosphate excretion by remaining nephrons and suppresses calcitriol to limit intestinal phosphate absorption (edmonston2024fgf23andklotho pages 4-5, vervloet2022sheddinglighton pages 4-6).

However, in advanced CKD, nephron loss and declining kidney α-Klotho expression overwhelm this compensatory mechanism (diazhaaz2025effectsoffibroblast pages 2-4, edmonston2024fgf23andklotho pages 4-5). The result is markedly elevated FGF23 (often 100- to 1000-fold above normal), phosphate retention, profoundly suppressed calcitriol, hypocalcemia, and severe secondary hyperparathyroidism (edmonston2024fgf23andklotho pages 4-5, latic2022interactionofvitamin pages 7-8). FGF23 may also contribute to CKD-associated bone disease by impairing bone mineralization and is associated with increased fracture risk (martinezheredia2024nonclassicaleffectsof pages 2-4, martinezheredia2024nonclassicaleffectsof pages 4-5).

### Cardiovascular Disease

Elevated FGF23 is strongly associated with adverse cardiovascular outcomes, particularly in CKD populations but also in the general population (diazhaaz2025effectsoffibroblast pages 4-5, martinezheredia2024nonclassicaleffectsof pages 4-5, nakano2023directandindirect pages 1-2). Epidemiological studies link higher FGF23 levels with left ventricular hypertrophy (LVH), heart failure, atrial fibrillation, and increased cardiovascular and all-cause mortality (diazhaaz2025effectsoffibroblast pages 4-5, martinezheredia2024nonclassicaleffectsof pages 4-5, nakano2023directandindirect pages 2-3).

Mechanistically, when FGF23 levels are extremely elevated and cardiac α-Klotho is deficient—as occurs in CKD—FGF23 can activate cardiomyocyte FGFR4 independently of α-Klotho (diazhaaz2025effectsoffibroblast pages 4-5, edmonston2024fgf23andklotho pages 5-6, nakano2023directandindirect pages 2-3). This activates the PLCγ–calcineurin–NFAT signaling pathway, promoting pathological cardiac hypertrophy, contractile dysfunction, and arrhythmias (diazhaaz2025effectsoffibroblast pages 4-5, edmonston2024fgf23andklotho pages 5-6, nakano2023directandindirect pages 7-7). FGF23 may also promote cardiac fibrosis through WNT/β-catenin and TGF-β pathways in cardiac fibroblasts (martinezheredia2024nonclassicaleffectsof pages 4-5, edmonston2024fgf23andklotho pages 5-6, nakano2023directandindirect pages 3-5).

Indirect cardiovascular effects occur through FGF23's suppression of renal active vitamin D synthesis. Reduced vitamin D activity stimulates the renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system (RAAS), which contributes to hypertension, cardiac hypertrophy, and fibrosis (nakano2023directandindirect pages 1-2, nakano2023directandindirect pages 3-5, diazhaaz2025effectsoffibroblast pages 1-2). FGF23 is also associated with endothelial dysfunction, reduced nitric oxide availability, and increased vascular oxidative stress, particularly when α-Klotho is deficient (diazhaaz2025effectsoffibroblast pages 4-5, martinezheredia2024nonclassicaleffectsof pages 4-5).

### Hypophosphatemic Disorders

FGF23 excess causes a spectrum of hypophosphatemic disorders characterized by renal phosphate wasting, hypophosphatemia, and rickets or osteomalacia. X-linked hypophosphatemia results from loss-of-function mutations in PHEX, leading to increased FGF23 production (vervloet2022sheddinglighton pages 10-11, vervloet2022sheddinglighton pages 4-6). Autosomal-dominant hypophosphatemic rickets is caused by mutations at the FGF23 cleavage site (typically R176 or R179) that prevent proteolytic inactivation, resulting in excessive intact bioactive hormone (agoro2023regulationoffgf23 pages 1-2, portalescastillo2022pthfgf23klotho pages 3-5).

Tumor-induced osteomalacia occurs when mesenchymal tumors produce excessive FGF23, often driven by hypoxia-inducible factor pathways (vervloet2022sheddinglighton pages 10-11). Autosomal recessive hypophosphatemic rickets results from loss-of-function mutations in DMP1, derepressing FGF23 production (vervloet2022sheddinglighton pages 10-11, vervloet2022sheddinglighton pages 4-6).

### Clinical Applications

FGF23 immunoassays are clinically established for diagnosing hereditary hypophosphatemic disorders and for diagnosing and monitoring tumor-induced osteomalacia (portalescastillo2022pthfgf23klotho pages 3-5). The monoclonal antibody burosumab, which neutralizes FGF23, has been approved for treating X-linked hypophosphatemia and tumor-induced osteomalacia, representing a milestone in targeting FGF23-mediated diseases (martinezheredia2024nonclassicaleffectsof pages 1-2). However, FGF23 assays are not standardized across platforms, requiring assay-specific reference ranges (portalescastillo2022pthfgf23klotho pages 3-5). While FGF23 predicts adverse outcomes in CKD, its routine use for risk stratification or as a therapeutic target in CKD remains unproven and requires dedicated prospective trials (portalescastillo2022pthfgf23klotho pages 3-5, edmonston2024fgf23andklotho pages 4-5).

## Summary

FGF23 is a bone-derived endocrine hormone that serves as the principal phosphaturic regulator coordinating phosphate and vitamin D homeostasis. Produced primarily by osteocytes in response to elevated phosphate, calcium, vitamin D, and PTH, FGF23 acts through FGFR–α-Klotho complexes—predominantly FGFR1c, FGFR3c, and FGFR4—to activate MAPK/ERK signaling pathways. In the kidney, FGF23 downregulates sodium-phosphate cotransporters NPT2a and NPT2c to promote phosphaturia, while suppressing CYP27B1 and inducing CYP24A1 to reduce active vitamin D synthesis. FGF23 production is tightly regulated through transcriptional control, post-translational glycosylation by GALNT3 (which stabilizes intact hormone), and phosphorylation by FAM20C (which promotes cleavage). In CKD, progressively elevated FGF23 initially compensates for reduced phosphate excretion but ultimately becomes maladaptive, contributing to CKD-MBD and potentially to cardiovascular disease through klotho-independent FGFR4 signaling. FGF23 excess causes hypophosphatemic disorders, while FGF23-neutralizing antibodies represent an established therapeutic approach for these conditions.
