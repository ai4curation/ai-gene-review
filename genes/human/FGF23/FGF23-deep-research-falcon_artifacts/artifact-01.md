| Protein structure and processing | Human FGF23 is synthesized as a 251-amino-acid precursor; signal-peptide removal yields the secreted, biologically active 227-amino-acid glycoprotein of approximately 32 kDa. A furin-like convertase cleaves the R176–H177–T178–R179↓S180 motif to generate approximately 20-kDa N-terminal and 12-kDa C-terminal fragments. | (pqac-00000079, pqac-00000080, pqac-00000081) |
| Post-translational control | GALNT3-mediated O-glycosylation—particularly at Thr178 after modification of Thr171—protects FGF23 from convertase cleavage and preserves intact hormone. FAM20C phosphorylation of Ser180 opposes this glycosylation and favors cleavage. Cleavage-resistant substitutions at Arg176 or Arg179 cause accumulation of intact FGF23 in autosomal-dominant hypophosphataemic rickets. | (pqac-00000079, pqac-00000081, pqac-00000083) |
| Protein family and biochemical role | FGF23 is an endocrine member of the FGF/heparin-binding growth-factor family, but it is a signaling ligand rather than an enzyme or transporter; therefore, it has no catalytic substrate. Its principal physiological role is to function as a phosphaturic hormone coordinating phosphate and active-vitamin-D homeostasis. Its relatively weak heparan-sulfate affinity facilitates release from bone and endocrine circulation. | (pqac-00000000, pqac-00000001, pqac-00000079) |
| Canonical receptor complex | Physiological signaling is mediated chiefly by an FGFR1c–α-Klotho complex. Structural studies also support α-Klotho-dependent complexes with FGFR3c and FGFR4. FGF23’s N-terminal region engages FGFR, whereas its C-terminal tail engages α-Klotho; α-Klotho stabilizes the primary ligand–receptor complex rather than acting as the catalytic receptor. | (pqac-00000008, pqac-00000009, pqac-00000010) |
| Receptor activation mechanism | Cryo-EM structures reveal an asymmetric 1:2:1:1 FGF23–FGFR–α-Klotho–heparan-sulfate complex. Heparan sulfate enables the primary 1:1:1 complex to recruit a second FGFR, causing the receptor dimerization required for tyrosine-kinase activation; α-Klotho does not directly recruit the secondary receptor. | (pqac-00000009, pqac-00000010, pqac-00000013) |
| Primary tissue source and localization | FGF23 is synthesized and secreted primarily by osteocytes and osteoblast-lineage cells in bone and enters the extracellular fluid and circulation. Bone-marrow stromal and erythroid cells can contribute under conditions such as anaemia or elevated erythropoietin. | (pqac-00000016, pqac-00000018, pqac-00000020) |
| Principal target tissue | The kidney is the dominant endocrine target. In renal proximal tubules, FGF23 controls phosphate transport and vitamin-D metabolism; in distal tubules, it can enhance calcium and sodium reabsorption through TRPV5 and NCC, respectively. | (pqac-00000024, pqac-00000025, pqac-00000030) |
| Additional target tissues | FGF23 can suppress PTH transcription and secretion in the parathyroid gland. Proposed or pathological targets include cardiomyocytes and cardiac fibroblasts; bone may also experience local auto/paracrine effects, although several non-renal effects remain context-dependent or incompletely resolved. | (pqac-00000027, pqac-00000028, pqac-00000029) |
| Canonical intracellular signaling | FGFR–α-Klotho engagement activates FRS2α and RAS–MAPK signaling, including ERK1/2. In proximal tubules, MAPK-linked EGR1 and ERK1/2–SGK1 contribute to transporter regulation and phosphaturia, although downstream control of vitamin-D-metabolizing genes is not fully mapped. | (pqac-00000001, pqac-00000047, pqac-00000049, pqac-00000052) |
| Phosphate metabolism | In the proximal-tubule apical membrane, FGF23 promotes internalization and degradation or downregulation of NPT2a/NaPi-IIa (SLC34A1) and NPT2c/NaPi-IIc (SLC34A3). This decreases filtered-phosphate reabsorption and increases urinary phosphate excretion. | (pqac-00000032, pqac-00000033, pqac-00000034) |
| Vitamin-D metabolism | FGF23 suppresses renal CYP27B1/1α-hydroxylase, reducing conversion of 25-hydroxyvitamin D to active 1,25-dihydroxyvitamin D (calcitriol), while inducing CYP24A1/24-hydroxylase and increasing vitamin-D catabolism. Lower calcitriol also reduces intestinal phosphate absorption. Calcitriol reciprocally stimulates bone FGF23 expression, forming a negative-feedback loop. | (pqac-00000040, pqac-00000041, pqac-00000042) |
| Parathyroid-hormone axis | Under physiological conditions, FGF23 suppresses PTH through α-Klotho/ERK1/2-dependent and proposed calcineurin-dependent mechanisms. In advanced CKD, reduced parathyroid α-Klotho and FGFR1c expression can produce FGF23 resistance, while low calcitriol and calcium favor secondary hyperparathyroidism. | (pqac-00000028, pqac-00000051, pqac-00000073) |
| Mineral and endocrine regulation | Phosphate, calcium and calcium–phosphate calciprotein particles stimulate FGF23 production or increase intact-hormone stability. Calcitriol strongly induces transcription, and PTH generally increases FGF23 during sustained exposure, although acute effects can differ because PTH also changes phosphate balance and FGF23 cleavage. | (pqac-00000057, pqac-00000058, pqac-00000061) |
| Local and kidney-derived regulation | PHEX and DMP1 normally restrain osteocytic FGF23; loss of either causes FGF23 excess and renal phosphate wasting. Erythropoietin, kidney-derived lipocalin-2, and the glycerol-3-phosphate–lysophosphatidic-acid pathway can stimulate production, particularly during anaemia, CKD or acute kidney injury. | (pqac-00000055, pqac-00000057, pqac-00000060, pqac-00000062) |
| CKD and CKD–mineral and bone disorder | FGF23 rises early and progressively as kidney function declines, often before overt hyperphosphataemia or increased PTH. Initially it is adaptive, increasing fractional phosphate excretion and lowering calcitriol; in advanced disease, nephron loss and Klotho deficiency overwhelm this compensation, producing very high FGF23, phosphate retention, low calcitriol and secondary hyperparathyroidism. | (pqac-00000071, pqac-00000074, pqac-00000075) |
| Pathological cardiovascular signaling | At markedly elevated concentrations, especially with CKD-associated Klotho deficiency, FGF23 can activate cardiomyocyte FGFR4 independently of α-Klotho. The FGFR4–PLCγ–calcineurin–NFAT pathway has been linked experimentally to left-ventricular hypertrophy, while WNT/β-catenin and TGF-β pathways may contribute to fibrosis. Causality in humans remains debated. | (pqac-00000064, pqac-00000066, pqac-00000068) |
| Clinical significance and applications | FGF23 excess causes renal phosphate wasting, hypophosphataemic rickets/osteomalacia and tumor-induced osteomalacia; deficient signaling or excessive cleavage produces hyperphosphataemia and ectopic calcification. FGF23 immunoassays are clinically established for diagnosing hereditary hypophosphataemic disorders and diagnosing or monitoring tumor-induced osteomalacia, but assays are not standardized and routine CKD risk-guided use remains unproven. | (pqac-00000032, pqac-00000041, pqac-00000078) |
