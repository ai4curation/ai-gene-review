# USH1G/SANS (human; UniProt Q495M9): functional-annotation research report

## Executive summary

**Identity is verified.** The requested target is unambiguously the human **USH1G** gene product **SANS** (“scaffold protein containing ankyrin repeats and SAM domain”; UniProt **Q495M9**), not a similarly named protein from another organism. Open Targets identifies the human locus as **ENSG00000182040**, approved symbol **USH1G**, approved name “USH1 protein network component sans,” with curated associations to Usher syndrome type 1, deafness, and hearing impairment. Literature places the gene at **17q25.1** and describes a **461-residue, approximately 52-kDa** protein containing three N-terminal ankyrin repeats, a central interaction region, a sterile-alpha-motif (SAM) domain, and a C-terminal class-I PDZ-binding motif. This agrees with the supplied UniProt ankyrin-repeat and SAM-domain annotations for Q495M9. (OpenTargets Search: -USH1G, fritze2023pathogenicvariantsin pages 1-3, leith2025currentapproachesfor pages 3-4)

The best-supported primary annotation is **multifunctional non-enzymatic scaffold/adaptor and pre-mRNA-splicing regulator**. SANS does not catalyze a reaction and is not a transporter with a molecular substrate. Instead, it organizes protein complexes in auditory hair-cell stereocilia and the retinal ciliary apparatus, positions intraflagellar-transport-complex-B (IFT-B) proteins, and facilitates transfer of U4/U6.U5 tri-snRNP particles between nuclear Cajal bodies and speckles during spliceosome assembly. (yildirim2021sans(ush1g)regulates pages 19-20, sorusch2019sans(ush1g)molecularly pages 1-2, elamraoui2005usherisyndrome pages 3-4)

## 1. Identity, structure, and definition

USH1G/SANS is an autosomal-recessive Usher-syndrome protein whose architecture is optimized for multivalent binding rather than catalysis. Its three N-terminal ankyrin repeats and central **CENTn1–CENTn2–CENTc** region provide multiple interaction surfaces; the latter includes intrinsically disordered sequence capable of binding-induced structural change. A C-terminal SAM domain and terminal PDZ-binding motif connect SANS to additional scaffold proteins such as harmonin. (fritze2023pathogenicvariantsin pages 1-3, fritze2023pathogenicvariantsin pages 12-13, elamraoui2005usherisyndrome pages 3-4)

## 2. Primary molecular function

### 2.1 Structural adaptor in the auditory hair bundle

In cochlear and vestibular hair cells, SANS participates in the Usher type-1 protein network that organizes the actin-rich stereociliary bundle and its mechanosensitive linkages. The SANS central region binds the N-terminal MyTH4–FERM region of **MYO7A**, while its SAM/terminal region binds **harmonin/USH1C**. Through these interactions, SANS helps couple motor/cytoskeletal machinery to the protein complex associated with stereociliary tip links; it should therefore be annotated as an **assembly and positioning factor for the mechanotransduction apparatus**, not as the force-generating motor or ion channel itself. (sorusch2019sans(ush1g)molecularly pages 9-10, elamraoui2005usherisyndrome pages 3-4)

Genetic-model evidence is strong. Sans-mutant “Jackson shaker” mice have severe hearing and vestibular abnormalities, with disorganized and poorly cohesive hair bundles. These phenotypes support an essential role in bundle development and tip-link-complex integrity, although some fine-scale localization and mechanistic conclusions derive from animal models rather than direct experiments in living human hair cells. (elamraoui2005usherisyndrome pages 3-4)

### 2.2 Connector between the Usher network and ciliary transport

In the retinal ciliary/periciliary compartment, SANS directly or indirectly associates with IFT-B proteins. Sorusch et al. tested IFT20, IFT52, IFT57, and IFT74 by one-to-one yeast two-hybrid, in-vitro capture, and membrane-targeting assays. **IFT52** was the most reproducible direct partner; IFT52 and IFT57 bound the N-terminal ankyrin-repeat region, whereas the central SANS region binds MYO7A. The individual assays were not fully concordant—IFT20 failed one GFP-trap assay and IFT74 binding was weak—so “association with several IFT-B components” is more defensible than claiming equally strong direct binding to all four. (sorusch2019sans(ush1g)molecularly pages 1-2, sorusch2019sans(ush1g)molecularly pages 9-10)

Confocal immunofluorescence placed SANS with IFT20, IFT52, and IFT57 at the base of mouse photoreceptor sensory cilia. In *Ush1g* knockout photoreceptors, staining for all three IFT-B proteins was significantly reduced in the ciliary region. These data support a role in **recruiting or maintaining IFT-B at the ciliary base**, thereby linking the Usher network to cargo movement toward and through the connecting cilium. They do not show that SANS itself is a molecular motor. (sorusch2019sans(ush1g)molecularly pages 5-9)

SANS has also been associated with microtubules, receptor-mediated endocytosis through MAGI2, primary ciliogenesis, and stabilization of photoreceptor outer-segment architecture. These are plausible extensions of its scaffold/trafficking role, but their evidence base is less complete than that for IFT-B positioning and nuclear splicing. (fritze2023pathogenicvariantsin pages 1-3, sorusch2019sans(ush1g)molecularly pages 1-2)

### 2.3 Pre-mRNA-splicing regulator

A major update to the traditional “ciliary scaffold” annotation came from Yildirim et al. in **May 2021**. Endogenous SANS was detected in **Cajal bodies and nuclear speckles**, where it associates with spliceosomal components including SF3B1, SON, PRPF proteins, and snRNAs. SANS depletion impeded release/transfer of the **U4/U6.U5 tri-snRNP** from Cajal bodies to nuclear speckles, slowed spliceosome assembly, and caused accumulation of spliceosomal complex A. Wild-type SANS therefore acts as an intranuclear transfer or assembly factor and may also assist recycling of snRNPs to Cajal bodies. It does not catalyze transesterification of pre-mRNA. [Yildirim et al., *Nucleic Acids Research*, 18 May 2021; DOI/URL: https://doi.org/10.1093/nar/gkab386] (yildirim2021sans(ush1g)regulates pages 19-20)

SANS depletion altered constitutive and alternative splicing of proliferation- and Usher-related transcripts, including genes within the same disease network. This establishes a biochemical bridge between a ciliopathy protein and spliceosome function, while leaving unresolved how much retinal degeneration is attributable to global missplicing versus local ciliary defects. (yildirim2021sans(ush1g)regulates pages 19-20)

## 3. Subcellular localization—where SANS works

SANS is **not confined to one organelle**:

* **Auditory/vestibular hair cells:** stereociliary hair bundles and the upper tip-link-associated protein network, where it supports bundle cohesion and mechanotransduction-complex assembly. (fritze2024nuclear–cytoplasmicshuttlingof pages 20-22, elamraoui2005usherisyndrome pages 3-4)
* **Retina:** reported in the photoreceptor layer, ciliary apparatus/base, periciliary region, photoreceptor synapse, and retinal plexiform layers. More recent work cautions that retinal expression may be especially prominent in **Müller glia**, rather than being predominantly photoreceptor-autonomous. Thus, cell-type attribution in human retina remains an important unresolved issue. (fritze2023pathogenicvariantsin pages 1-3, leith2025currentapproachesfor pages 3-4)
* **Cytoplasm and primary-cilium base:** microtubule/IFT-associated trafficking and ciliogenesis-related functions. (sorusch2019sans(ush1g)molecularly pages 1-2, sorusch2019sans(ush1g)molecularly pages 5-9)
* **Nucleus:** Cajal bodies and nuclear speckles, where tri-snRNP transfer and spliceosome assembly occur. (yildirim2021sans(ush1g)regulates pages 19-20)

## 4. Recent developments, 2023–2024

### 4.1 Interaction mechanism with PRPF6 and PRPF31—2023

Fritze et al. mapped two conserved interaction surfaces in the SANS central region using cellular FRET and AlphaFold2-guided structural analysis. PRPF31 binds chiefly to **CENTn1**, whereas the C-terminal HAT-repeat region of PRPF6 engages **CENTn1/CENTn2**. PRPF6 binding was predicted to convert an intrinsically disordered CENTn2 “Janus” segment into a short α-helix. Competition/FRET findings support separate or sequential SANS–PRPF31 and SANS–PRPF6 states during tri-snRNP maturation, transfer, or recycling. [Fritze et al., *International Journal of Molecular Sciences*, 18 December 2023; DOI/URL: https://doi.org/10.3390/ijms242417608] (fritze2023pathogenicvariantsin pages 1-3, fritze2023pathogenicvariantsin pages 12-13)

Pathogenic frameshifts provided functional validation. **p.Val132Glyfs*3** disrupted PRPF6 binding, while **p.Ser278Profs*71** disturbed interactions with both PRPF6 and PRPF31. The residual PRPF31 interaction observed for p.Val132Glyfs*3 suggests a secondary contact in the ankyrin-repeat region and illustrates why simple deletion maps may understate multivalent binding. (fritze2023pathogenicvariantsin pages 12-13)

### 4.2 Regulated nuclear–cytoplasmic shuttling—2024

The most important 2024 USH1G-specific advance was demonstration that SANS localization is actively controlled. In HeLa cells, microscopy, automated CellProfiler analysis, and biochemical fractionation found both cytoplasmic and nuclear SANS; under the reported tagged-protein conditions, approximately **70%** was nuclear. A strong NLS was mapped to residues **213–224**, with a second candidate at **436–447**. Functional export signals were mapped to **181–195** in CENTn2 and **406–420** in the SAM domain. [Fritze et al., *Cells*, 8 November 2024; DOI/URL: https://doi.org/10.3390/cells13221855] (fritze2024nuclear–cytoplasmicshuttlingof pages 1-3, fritze2024nuclear–cytoplasmicshuttlingof pages 19-20, fritze2024nuclear–cytoplasmicshuttlingof pages 9-12, fritze2025decipheringtherole pages 42-43)

The experiments included NLS/NES mutagenesis in HeLa and HEK293T cells, **5 nM leptomycin B** to inhibit CRM1-dependent export, **40 μM importazole for four hours**, confocal imaging, nuclear/cytoplasmic fractionation, and FRAP. K213E significantly reduced nuclear localization; L195E increased nuclear enrichment; and truncating/frameshift variants lacking the 406–420 NES were significantly nuclear-enriched. Analyses used three independent experiments and at least 75 cells for relevant microscopy comparisons, with reported significance reaching **p ≤ 0.0009**. (fritze2024nuclear–cytoplasmicshuttlingof pages 12-14, fritze2024nuclear–cytoplasmicshuttlingof pages 9-12, fritze2024nuclear–cytoplasmicshuttlingof pages 5-6)

PRPF31 overexpression drove nearly complete nuclear localization of co-expressed SANS, but PRPF31 depletion did not measurably block import, arguing against an obligatory PRPF31 “piggyback” mechanism. The paralog ANKS4B, in contrast, was predominantly cytoplasmic and lacks the same NLS organization; CRM1 inhibition caused nuclear localization in more than 80% of tested ANKS4B cells/protein. This comparison reinforces that nuclear splicing is a specialized SANS function rather than a generic property of all related ankyrin/SAM proteins. (fritze2024nuclear–cytoplasmicshuttlingof pages 19-20)

SANS also forms mobile nuclear and cytoplasmic puncta with liquid–liquid-phase-separation-like behavior; harmonin or PRPF31 overexpression enlarges these condensates. This is an attractive mechanism for concentrating SANS interaction networks, but physiological necessity of condensation remains a **working model**, not yet as firmly established as NLS/NES-dependent shuttling. (fritze2025decipheringtherole pages 59-60, fritze2024nuclear–cytoplasmicshuttlingof pages 19-20)

## 5. Pathways and integrated mechanistic model

USH1G lies at the intersection of three coupled modules:

1. **Hair-cell mechanotransduction architecture:** SANS scaffolds MYO7A/harmonin-associated assemblies required for stereociliary cohesion and tip-link-complex organization. Loss causes defective bundle mechanics upstream of sensory transduction. (elamraoui2005usherisyndrome pages 3-4)
2. **Ciliary/periciliary trafficking:** ankyrin-repeat interactions recruit or stabilize IFT-B components at the ciliary base, while central-region interactions connect the Usher complex to MYO7A and microtubule-dependent transport. (sorusch2019sans(ush1g)molecularly pages 1-2, sorusch2019sans(ush1g)molecularly pages 5-9)
3. **Spliceosome assembly:** SANS coordinates PRPF31/PRPF6-containing tri-snRNP movement from Cajal bodies to nuclear speckles, enabling progression beyond spliceosomal complex A. (yildirim2021sans(ush1g)regulates pages 19-20, fritze2023pathogenicvariantsin pages 12-13)

The current expert model is therefore **dual-compartment disease biology**: pathogenic variants can simultaneously diminish cytoplasmic/ciliary availability and impair nuclear RNA processing. Variants that abolish export signals may accumulate in the nucleus yet remain functionally deficient there, while also depriving stereocilia and cilia of SANS. This model is experimentally plausible, but the relative contribution of each arm to human hearing versus retinal disease has not been quantified. (fritze2024nuclear–cytoplasmicshuttlingof pages 20-22, fritze2024nuclear–cytoplasmicshuttlingof pages 12-14)

## 6. Human disease relevance and statistics

Biallelic pathogenic USH1G variants cause **Usher syndrome type 1G**, generally characterized by congenital severe-to-profound sensorineural deafness, vestibular areflexia/delayed motor development, and childhood or prepubertal retinitis pigmentosa. USH1 overall accounts for approximately **25–44%** of Usher syndrome, with reported mean RP onset near nine years; these figures are subtype-wide and should not be interpreted as USH1G-specific natural-history estimates. (cuzzuol2024ushersyndromegenetic pages 2-4)

USH1G is rare: a recent review estimates it accounts for **up to approximately 7% of USH1 cases**, and recent molecular literature reports **more than 70 pathogenic USH1G variants**. Overall Usher-syndrome prevalence estimates vary from roughly **1:5,000–10,000** or 4–17 per 100,000, reflecting differences in ascertainment and population. These overall prevalence figures likewise are not specific to USH1G. (fritze2023pathogenicvariantsin pages 1-3, fritze2024nuclear–cytoplasmicshuttlingof pages 20-22, leith2025currentapproachesfor pages 3-4)

Open Targets’ integrated evidence gives the strongest listed association to Usher syndrome (score **0.784**) and Usher syndrome type 1 (score **0.724**), supported by curated literature and a Hearing Loss Gene Curation Expert Panel. Such scores summarize database evidence rather than penetrance or clinical effect size. (OpenTargets Search: -USH1G)

## 7. Current applications and real-world implementation

### Molecular diagnosis

USH1G is included in multigene hearing-loss/Usher and inherited-retinal-disease panels. Comprehensive sequencing is important because clinical classification alone can miss atypical presentations and because molecular confirmation establishes autosomal-recessive recurrence risk, enables carrier testing, and distinguishes USH1G from MYO7A-, USH1C-, CDH23-, PCDH15-, or USH2A-associated disease. Panel or exome findings should be interpreted with segregation, phenotype, population frequency, predicted loss of function, and—where feasible—RNA or protein-interaction assays. Complete exon sequencing across known Usher genes improves diagnostic yield compared with narrowly sequential testing. (OpenTargets Search: -USH1G, cuzzuol2024ushersyndromegenetic pages 12-13, busi2024navigatingtheusher pages 1-2)

The splicing and trafficking assays discussed above provide potential functional tests for variants of uncertain significance, particularly variants affecting CENTn PRPF-binding sites or mapped NLS/NES motifs. However, these cell-line assays are not yet routine accredited clinical diagnostics. (fritze2023pathogenicvariantsin pages 12-13, fritze2024nuclear–cytoplasmicshuttlingof pages 12-14)

### Clinical management

Management remains supportive and multidisciplinary: audiology and early cochlear implantation for profound congenital hearing loss, vestibular/developmental support, serial ophthalmic assessment, low-vision services, and genetic counseling. A 2024 review of 95 implanted patients across all Usher genes supported presurgical genetic testing for counseling, but did not provide a sufficiently powered USH1G-specific outcome estimate. [Busi and Castiglione, *Audiology Research*, 26 February 2024; DOI/URL: https://doi.org/10.3390/audiolres14020023] (busi2024navigatingtheusher pages 1-2)

The treatment literature must not be overgeneralized: retinal gene-therapy programs described in the 2024 review target **MYO7A/USH1B** or **USH2A**, not USH1G. The retrieved trial searches found no relevant USH1G/SANS-targeted interventional study, and the 2024 literature documented no approved gene-, RNA-, or drug therapy that restores SANS. Thus, cochlear implantation is a real-world treatment of the hearing phenotype, but not a molecular correction of USH1G. (cuzzuol2024ushersyndromegenetic pages 4-5, cuzzuol2024ushersyndromegenetic pages 12-13)

## 8. Evidence assessment and research priorities

**High-confidence annotations** are: human USH1G/SANS identity; non-enzymatic multivalent scaffold function; ankyrin/CENT/SAM-PDZ-binding architecture; hair-bundle requirement; IFT-B association and ciliary-base positioning; nuclear localization; PRPF6/PRPF31 binding; and tri-snRNP-transfer/splicing regulation. These are supported by convergent genetics, biochemical interaction assays, imaging, depletion/knockout experiments, and rescue or pathogenic-variant analyses. (fritze2023pathogenicvariantsin pages 1-3, yildirim2021sans(ush1g)regulates pages 19-20, sorusch2019sans(ush1g)molecularly pages 1-2, fritze2024nuclear–cytoplasmicshuttlingof pages 12-14)

**Moderate-confidence or unresolved areas** include the precise human retinal cell type in which SANS loss initiates degeneration, the physiological requirement for SANS condensates, and the relative contributions of ciliary versus spliceosomal dysfunction. Mouse photoreceptor data are mechanistically valuable, but rodent USH1 models incompletely reproduce human retinal degeneration. Quantitative natural-history cohorts and USH1G-specific organoid or induced-pluripotent-stem-cell models remain priorities. (fritze2023pathogenicvariantsin pages 1-3, fritze2025decipheringtherole pages 59-60)

## Conclusion

The most precise functional annotation for Q495M9 is: **USH1G/SANS is a human multivalent scaffold and pre-mRNA-splicing regulator that organizes stereociliary mechanotransduction-associated complexes, connects the Usher network to IFT-B-dependent ciliary trafficking, and mediates intranuclear tri-snRNP transfer for spliceosome assembly.** It acts in hair-cell stereocilia, the retinal ciliary/periciliary apparatus, cytoplasmic trafficking compartments, Cajal bodies, and nuclear speckles. The 2023–2024 work substantially strengthens the nuclear arm of this annotation by mapping PRPF6/PRPF31 contacts and functional NLS/NES elements. Clinically, biallelic loss causes rare autosomal-recessive USH1G; molecular testing and early auditory rehabilitation are implemented, whereas USH1G-targeted therapy remains preclinical or undeveloped.
