Gene Description

GLB1 encodes lysosomal beta-galactosidase, which removes terminal beta-linked galactose from glycoconjugates, including gangliosides, glycoprotein-derived glycans and keratan sulfate. The active enzyme undergoes lysosomal maturation and associates with a multienzyme system containing protective protein cathepsin A and neuraminidase. Deficiency causes GM1 gangliosidosis or Morquio B disease. Alternative splicing also produces the shorter, catalytically inactive S-Gal/elastin-binding protein, which binds extracellular-matrix ligands and supports elastic-fiber deposition in studied cell systems. These activities belong to distinct GLB1 products; the elastin-binding name does not identify the separate sterol-isomerase gene EBP.

Distinct products

UniProt P16278 currently describes products of 677, 546 and 647 residues. P16278-2 and legacy P16279-1 identify the same 546-residue sequence. Its canonical 83–244 replacement contains 31 residues; the historical 32-residue description includes the adjacent boundary glycine. The 647-residue product lacks canonical residues 1–30; that sequence fact alone establishes neither localization nor activity. The sterol-isomerase gene EBP is a separate locus.

PMID:2511208(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2511208/) distinguishes active long and inactive short cDNA products. PMID:8383699(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8383699/) supplies the splice-product/elastin-binding crosswalk. Its selected original text and figures were examined; full historical construct sequences were not independently reconstructed.

In PMID:9497360(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9497360/), human fibroblast S-Gal cDNA was expressed in reticulocyte and cell systems. Elastin retention, laminin recovery and collagen controls support matrix association. Figure 2D states that the product “binds to elastin and laminin but not to collagen type I.” These assays do not provide a purified two-component affinity constant. PMID:10841810(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10841810/) connects S-Gal deficiency and cellular rescue with elastic-fiber deposition; it does not isolate every proposed chaperone step.

Catalytic and structural evidence

PMID:22128166(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22128166/) studied processed recombinant human enzyme. Figure 2C describes material that “was eluted at a position corresponding to the dimer.” The preparation, proteolytic processing and unresolved physiological stoichiometry limit extrapolation to endogenous complexes. The original methods, results and discussion were read separately from search summaries.

PMID:31720227(https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6838976/) combines patient measurements with recombinant-enzyme experiments on deficient-mouse glycans. Its human keratan-sulfate assay uses keratanase II; it is not a purified GLB1 cleavage experiment. PMID:8200356(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8200356/) directly compares GLB1 and GALC in saposin-dependent lipid assays, whose substrate presentation matters.

The actual PTHR23421 tree places the exact 677-residue human sequence at AN156. The catalytic assertion is at AN0/PTN000602923; the vertebrate lysosome assertion is at AN146/PTN002765259. Selected catalytic alignment windows retain E188 and E268. The human target's experimental evidence legitimately contributes to the ancestral inference. No claim is made to have manually inspected every alignment column.

The original construct is distinct from GLB1; the historical GLB1 citation-target assignment therefore needs checking. GLB1 (human) — curation notes

UniProtKB: P16278 (BGAL_HUMAN). HGNC:4298. EC 3.2.1.23. Glycosyl hydrolase family 35 (CAZy GH35).

Core biology (grounded in UniProt + cached publications; falcon deep research unavailable, HTTP 402)

GLB1 encodes lysosomal acid beta-galactosidase (β-Gal), an exoglycosidase that
hydrolyses terminal non-reducing β-D-galactose residues from a broad range of
substrates.

  • UniProt CATALYTIC ACTIVITY: "Hydrolysis of terminal non-reducing beta-D-galactose
    residues in beta-D-galactosides.; EC=3.2.1.23" [file:human/GLB1/GLB1-uniprot.txt].
  • UniProt FUNCTION [Isoform 1]: "Cleaves beta-linked terminal galactosyl residues from
    gangliosides, glycoproteins, and glycosaminoglycans." [file:human/GLB1/GLB1-uniprot.txt].
  • pH optimum 4.5–5.5 (acid hydrolase) [file:human/GLB1/GLB1-uniprot.txt].

Two disease-relevant catabolic roles:
1. Ganglioside/glycosphingolipid catabolism — removes the terminal galactose of GM1
ganglioside (→ GM2) and of its asialo derivative GA1 (→ GA2). Deficiency → lysosomal
accumulation of GM1 and GA1 PMID:31720227. Also acts (with sap-B) on lactosylceramide/GM1 in vitro
PMID:8200356.
2. Keratan sulfate / glycosaminoglycan and glycoprotein catabolism — GM1
gangliosidosis / Morquio B accumulate keratan sulfate and β-galactose-terminated
N-/O-linked glycans. GLB1 deficiency is a "broad oligosaccharidosis" PMID:31720227.

Structure / mechanism

  • Crystal structure (homodimer): catalytic TIM barrel + β-domain 1 + β-domain 2; "β-Gal
    is an exoglycosidase that catalyzes the hydrolysis of terminal β-linked galactose
    residues" PMID:22128166. Active site: proton donor Glu188, nucleophile Glu268
    (UniProt ACT_SITE). Binds galactose product; homodimer PMID:22128166.

Lysosomal multienzyme complex

  • GLB1 forms a lysosomal complex with protective protein/cathepsin A (PPCA/CTSA),
    α-neuraminidase (NEU1)     and GALNS; PPCA stabilises β-Gal multimers
    PMID:15714521.
    Loss of PPCA (galactosialidosis) → combined β-Gal/NEU1 deficiency; PPCA restores
    high-MW β-Gal multimers PMID:3084261.

Localisation

  • Lysosome (isoform 1). Synthesised as precursor in RER → Golgi → mature high-MW multimer
    in lysosome PMID:3084261. UniProt SUBCELLULAR LOCATION [Isoform 1]: Lysosome
    [PMID:2511208, PMID:3084261].

Isoform 2 (EBP / elastin-binding protein / S-Gal)

  • Alternatively spliced, catalytically inactive (~67 kDa); functions in elastic-fibre
    assembly (elastogenesis) as a recycling chaperone for tropoelastin; localises to
    perinuclear cytoplasm / cell surface, NOT lysosome [UniProt FUNCTION [Isoform 2],
    SUBCELLULAR LOCATION [Isoform 2]; PMID:2511208]. The perinuclear-region (GO:0048471) and
    cytoplasm (GO:0005737) CC annotations pertain to this non-catalytic isoform.

Disease

  • Allelic disorders, all autosomal recessive lysosomal storage diseases:
    GM1-gangliosidosis types 1/2/3 (MIM 230500/230600/230650) and Mucopolysaccharidosis
    type IVB / Morquio B (MIM 253010) [UniProt DISEASE].
