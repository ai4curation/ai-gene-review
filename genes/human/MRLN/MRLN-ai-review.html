Gene Description

Myoregulin (MRLN, MLN) is a 46-amino-acid single-pass transmembrane micropeptide encoded by a short open reading frame in exon 3 of a skeletal-muscle transcript that was originally catalogued as a long noncoding RNA (LINC00948). It is a member of the regulin (phospholamban/sarcolipin) family of SERCA regulatory subunits: a type II membrane helix with a short cytosolic N-terminus and a few lumenal C-terminal residues, anchored in the sarcoplasmic reticulum membrane of skeletal muscle. Its transmembrane helix carries the hydrophobic motif shared with phospholamban and sarcolipin, through which it binds the SERCA calcium pump (SERCA1 predominantly, but also SERCA2a/2b in cells) as a monomer and inhibits pump activity, reducing calcium re-uptake into the sarcoplasmic reticulum. Whether it acts by lowering the pump's apparent calcium affinity or its maximal rate is debated. Myoregulin is expressed in all adult skeletal muscles and is transcriptionally controlled by MyoD/MEF2 and repressed by NR1D1. Loss of myoregulin in mice raises sarcoplasmic reticulum calcium and improves exercise performance. Knockdown in human myotubes and in a human cardiomyocyte line likewise increases SERCA activity or SR calcium content.

Core Functions

Myoregulin is a transmembrane regulatory subunit of the SERCA calcium pump in the skeletal-muscle sarcoplasmic reticulum membrane. Its single transmembrane helix binds SERCA as a monomer, in the same groove used by phospholamban and sarcolipin, and inhibits the pump. This slows calcium re-uptake into the sarcoplasmic reticulum and lowers SR calcium stores. In reconstitution the inhibition is a reduction in Vmax. In HEK293 cell homogenates it appeared as a reduction in apparent Ca2+ affinity.

Suggested Questions for Experts

Q: Does myoregulin inhibit SERCA by lowering its apparent Ca2+ affinity (KCa), as in the original HEK293 homogenate assays (PMID:25640239), or by lowering Vmax, as in proteoliposome co-reconstitution (PMID:34445594, PMID:37014032, PMID:37918638)? The UniProt FUNCTION text asserts the KCa mechanism, which the reconstitution data contradict.

Suggested experts: Howard S Young, L Michel Espinoza-Fonseca, Seth L Robia

Q: Should SERCA-regulin inhibition be captured as GO:0042030 ATPase inhibitor activity (as used for PLN by IBA and in GO-CAM) or as GO:0141110 transporter inhibitor activity (also carried by PLN, IDA)? In current GO, P-type calcium transporter activity is not a descendant of catalytic activity, so enzyme inhibitor activity is a poor parent for all regulins, and a consistent choice across PLN, SLN, MRLN, ERLN and ALN would help.

Q: Is myoregulin protein present at physiologically meaningful levels in human skeletal muscle fibre types, and in human heart (mRNA reported in PMID:38246425)? No human study has yet localised the endogenous peptide.

Suggested Experiments

Experiment: Co-reconstitute synthetic human MRLN (and mouse Mrln side by side) with purified SERCA1a and SERCA2a in proteoliposomes, and measure Ca2+-dependent ATPase activity and Ca2+ transport to determine KCa and Vmax. Include the L29A/F30A/F33A binding-motif mutant as a negative control.

Hypothesis: Human myoregulin inhibits human SERCA1a by the same kinetic mechanism as the mouse peptide.

Type: in vitro reconstitution enzyme kinetics

Experiment: Knock out MRLN in human iPSC-derived or primary myotubes (not only DMD lines). Measure SR Ca2+ content, Ca2+ transient decay kinetics and SERCA activity, and localise epitope-tagged endogenous MRLN by knock-in.

Hypothesis: Endogenous MRLN restrains SERCA activity in human skeletal muscle.

Type: CRISPR knockout and calcium imaging

GO annotation assessment

  • GOA human has 7 rows: enzyme inhibitor activity (ISS, IEA), ER membrane (IEA is_active_in),
    SR membrane (ISS, IEA), negative regulation of calcium ion import into SR (ISS).
  • Mouse Q9CV60 source annotations: GO:0004857 IDA (UniProt), GO:1902081 IDA/IMP, GO:1901895 IDA
    (PMID:27923914), GO:0033017 IDA, GO:0005789 IDA, protein binding IPI.
  • Comparators: human PLN (P26678) has GO:0042030 ATPase inhibitor activity (IDA+IBA) and
    GO:0141110 transporter inhibitor activity (IDA); SLN (O00631) has GO:0004857 ISS and
    GO:1901895 IDA. The mouse Pln GO-CAM (gocams 62f58d8800005094) types Pln's activity as
    GO:0042030 ATPase inhibitor activity in ER membrane.
  • Ontology note: GO:0005388 P-type calcium transporter activity is NOT a descendant of
    catalytic activity / ATP hydrolysis activity in current GO (is_a transporter + ATP-dependent
    activity). So GO:0004857 "enzyme inhibitor activity" (reduces a catalytic activity) is a
    loose fit; GO:0042030 ATPase inhibitor activity is what curators use for PLN (IBA, GO-CAM)
    and is directly supported by ATPase assays. MODIFY → GO:0042030.
  • GO:1901895 negative regulation of ATPase-coupled calcium transmembrane transporter activity:
    MLN performs the inhibition itself (direct binding to SERCA), passes the participation test;
    comparators PLN and SLN (same role) both carry it by IDA. Propose NEW (IMP, human AC16 data).
  • GO-CAM: no model in gocams/index.tsv contains MRLN/P0DMT0 (checked 2026-09-30).

core_functions:
- description: >-
    Myoregulin is a transmembrane regulatory subunit of the SERCA calcium pump in the
    skeletal-muscle sarcoplasmic reticulum membrane. Its single transmembrane helix binds
    SERCA as a monomer, in the same groove used by phospholamban and sarcolipin, and inhibits
    the pump. This slows calcium re-uptake into the sarcoplasmic reticulum and lowers SR
    calcium stores. In reconstitution the inhibition is a reduction in Vmax. In HEK293 cell
    homogenates it appeared as a reduction in apparent Ca2+ affinity.
