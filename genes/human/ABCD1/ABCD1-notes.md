## Transport and thioester hydrolysis

The stable biological conclusion is that ABCD1 is an ATP-utilizing peroxisomal membrane half-transporter, predominantly a homodimer, that recognizes fatty-acyl-CoA substrates and supplies the peroxisomal beta-oxidation pathway. Human ABCD1 complements yeast lacking Pxa1/Pxa2, with particularly effective rescue on saturated C24:0 and C26:0 substrates. ABCD2 has overlapping capacity and a different preferred spectrum. These experiments support transport participation but do not by themselves identify the chemical form that crosses the membrane. [PMID:18757502, *The human peroxisomal ABC half transporter ALDP functions as a homodimer and accepts acyl-CoA esters*; PMID:21145416, “rescue of beta-oxidation activity was best with C24:0 and C26:0 as substrates.”]

The patient-fibroblast/peroxisome study supports an ABCD1-dependent import limitation: blocking ABCD1 impairs C26:0-CoA oxidation and influences C22:0-CoA oxidation, with residual activity contributed by ABCD3. Its observation that added CoA or an additional intraperoxisomal synthetase was unnecessary supports an intact-ester model in that system, but oxidation is a coupled downstream readout, not direct tracing of both transported chemical moieties. The full text was unavailable in this session, so the interpretation is limited to the accessible abstract. [PMID:23671276, “the β-oxidation defect in X-ALD is directly caused by ABCD1 dysfunction”.]

The 2018 biochemical study reports ATPase and acyl-CoA thioesterase (ACOT) activity in reconstituted human ABCD-family preparations. The 2021 primary paper was read in full. Human His-ABCD1 was produced in a Pichia/Komagataella background lacking the endogenous Pxa1 transporter, purified and reconstituted, with a matched unrelated-protein preparation as a control. NBD-C16-CoA hydrolysis was separated by TLC, and substrate competition, covalent ABCD1 labeling, hydroxylamine treatment and chemical perturbations strengthened attribution to the ABCD1 preparation. ACOT activity was ATP-independent; the K513A ATPase mutant and ATP omission reduced transport while leaving residual signal. These results are meaningful biochemical evidence and are not dismissed merely because a later model uses intact acyl-CoA. [PMID:29397936; PMID:33500543, “The expressed ABCD1 possessed both ATPase and ACOT activities.”]

The direct hydrolysis/transport readouts in PMID:33500543 rely heavily on fluorescent substrate analogues. Native acyl-CoAs were assessed substantially through competition. Competitive inhibition demonstrates substrate recognition and is not a complete kinetic panel proving every native chain-length substrate is hydrolyzed at a physiological rate. The candidate catalytic cysteine was not mapped definitively, and the work does not establish obligatory cleavage and re-esterification in every native transport cycle. Existing hydrolase annotations are retained with those limits; the core synthesis uses generic fatty acyl-CoA hydrolase activity as a measured biochemical subactivity, without a separate asserted biosynthetic process.

The 2022 structural papers add substrate-binding and ATPase evidence. PMID:35013584 studied human ABCD1 in nanodiscs and explicitly noted the absence of a direct transport assay. C24:0-CoA, C26:0-CoA and C18:1-CoA stimulated ATPase, while the tested C22:6-CoA/acetyl-CoA conditions did not. PMID:35676282 used a chimera replacing the human N-terminal 63 residues with the corresponding 65 residues of the worm protein and resolved substrate-bound and ATP-bound conformations. A bound acyl-CoA molecule or substrate-stimulated ATPase does not establish the chemical form released on the opposite side. These structures neither independently demonstrate thioester hydrolysis nor refute the biochemical ACOT report. [PMID:35013584, “It is currently unclear whether the fatty acyl chain is separated from its CoA ester for subsequent re-esterification”; PMID:35676282.]

Accordingly, the core transporter term is GO:0015607, with explicit input recognition and chemical-form uncertainty. Broad long-chain-fatty-acid transporter and ABC-transporter annotations are refined to that term. The generic GO:0047617 hydrolase core entry records the measured subactivity and its limits. A decisive next experiment would independently label acyl and CoA moieties, measure thioester integrity and sidedness, and distinguish active translocation from lipid partitioning in an orientation-controlled reconstitution.

## Localization, targeting and partner specificity

Native peroxisomal membrane localization is strongly established. Broad peroxisome, membrane and cytoplasm terms are refined to that location for specificity; broad terms are not treated as biologically disproved. Targeting/mutant work supports internal targeting determinants and does not imply all mutant compartments are native functional locations. [PMID:14533738; PMID:17542813.]

The machine cache for PMID:16946495 is abstract-only, but the complete seven-page publisher PDF was read at <https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/bpb/29/9/29_9_1836/_pdf/-char/en>. Its source system is baculovirus overexpression of human ALDP-His in Sf21 insect cells. Fractionation and microscopy report multiple membranes, including ER, mitochondria and lysosomes; high expression also produced nuclear crystalloid inclusions. The authors detect an ALDP peroxisomal pool using established fractionation behavior for the catalase-less Sf21 peroxisomes. They attribute additional membrane distribution to the limited number of peroxisomes being unable to accommodate high heterologous expression. Thus the six ER/lysosome/mitochondrial-membrane annotations are marked over-annotated as generalized native human locations, not rejected as false observations. [PMID:16946495, “including mitochondria, lysosomes and ER”.]

The same study supports nucleotide binding and ATPase activity in crude membrane preparations (ALDP-expressing 77 ± 4 versus control 50 ± 4 nmol/min/mg under the reported assay conditions); purified detergent-solubilized protein lost ATPase activity. This should not be described as a purified soluble human enzyme assay. Direct structural and reconstitution studies independently support ATP utilization.

PEX19 binds ABCD1 as a membrane-protein client, with the internal targeting region implicated. Two PEX19 splice variants retain ABC-client interaction even though their peroxisome-rescue capacities differ. Client binding does not give ABCD1 PEX19's chaperone or insertion activity. The live definition of GO:0051087, protein-folding chaperone binding, includes chaperones involved in folding or transport; it provides a more informative client-binding term for the directly supported PEX19 interaction rows. [PMID:10777694; PMID:11883941, “Both interact with peroxisomal ABC transporters (ALDP, ALDRP, PMP70)”.]

The exact ABCD1-specific experiment behind PMID:20531392 remains unresolved because the accessible abstract describes PEX19's receptor domain more generally. The ABCD2-associated binding row tied to PMID:17609205 is likewise UNDECIDED: the abstract directly describes ABCD1/ABCD3 and homodimer FRET results, and the inaccessible full paper could contain the additional ABCD2 experiment. Neither case is called a wrong-gene citation. The human-mouse ABCD1 interaction tied to PMID:10551832 is also UNDECIDED because a xenolog pair does not alone establish homodimerization by identical proteins, and the exact constructs could not be inspected in full text. Independently supported native homo/heterodimer evidence receives the corresponding specific binding term, with predominant homodimerization distinguished from possible heterodimer formation. [PMID:10551832; PMID:17609205.]

Reactome cytosol annotations were audited against participants rather than harmonized mechanically. R-HSA-382613, R-HSA-9603784 and R-HSA-9603804 include nascent cytosolic PEX19/client complexes and are retained as non-core targeting intermediates. In R-HSA-9603775, the released cytosolic product is PEX19; ABCD1 remains in the peroxisomal membrane. That specific row is refined to peroxisomal membrane. The different actions explain one intentional validator warning.

## Mouse donor and pathway audit

Mouse UniProtKB:P48410 and Ensembl ENSMUSP00000002084 are the same Abcd1 donor chain. Their identity and current experimental GO annotations were traced rather than treating the two identifiers as independent experiments. ARBA identifiers are recorded, but uninspected rule internals are explicitly UNRESOLVED; annotation judgments rest on independent primary biology. Rhea reaction identities were checked against the fetched UniProt catalytic-activity records: 40783 docosanoyl-CoA, 40787 tetracosanoyl-CoA, 40791 hexacosanoyl-CoA and 67072 generic very-long-chain-acyl-CoA hydrolysis. These curated reaction mappings do not supply independent native-substrate kinetic experiments. PAINT IBA rows were assessed using ancestral nodes PANTHER:PTN004256010 and PANTHER:PTN008319914; target self-inclusion and a short extant donor list are not circularity or weak-evidence arguments.

The cached GO-CAM index also contains Abcd1 under MGI:MGI:1349215. Models `675b862c00001162` (alpha-linolenic acid metabolism) and `675b862c00001348` (linoleic acid metabolism) explicitly place its peroxisomal long-chain-fatty-acid transporter activity within fatty acid biosynthesis (GO:0006633), using IGI evidence PMID:16223892 with Abcd2/MGI:MGI:1349467. This is a curator's model of transport participation, not a claim that Abcd1 catalyzes elongation. The review acknowledges that placement. The exact Abcd1 genetic result could not be inspected in the inaccessible full paper, so the source-specific human biosynthesis transfer remains UNDECIDED. Its title foregrounding ABCD2 is not evidence of misattribution.

Other donor decisions distinguish the primary transport defect from consequences:

- Increased microsomal elongation in young Abcd1-deficient brain supports regulation of fatty-acid metabolism, not ABCD1's direct participation in two-carbon elongation chemistry. The elongation rows are refined to GO:0019217. [PMID:26108493, “microsomal fatty acid elongation activity is stimulated in abcd1-deficient mice”.]
- Macrophage single/double deficiency, glial oxidation studies and rescue experiments support substrate provision to beta-oxidation. Regulatory beta-oxidation terms are refined to the direct import-linked pathway role. [PMID:25255441; PMID:23123468.]
- Lipid homeostasis is close to the transported substrate and retained as core. Late axonal and myelin defects in mouse models are retained as downstream non-core functions, with ABCD2 compensation noted. [PMID:11875044; PMID:15489218.]
- Mitochondrial energetic/redox changes, ROS, inflammatory responses and cholesterol phenotypes are retained as non-core metabolic consequences. They do not establish ABCD1 as a mitochondrial membrane transporter or cytokine regulator acting directly in signaling. [PMID:23604518; PMID:18344354; PMID:18723473; PMID:22521832; PMID:25583114; PMID:16213491.]
- PMID:18854420 explicitly includes Abcd1-knockout brain-slice assays despite emphasizing ABCD2. Its exact Abcd1-specific positive PUFA-biosynthesis result was not resolved from the abstract; those two transfers remain UNDECIDED. Normal DHA synthesis in the human X-ALD fibroblast comparison is a tissue/system limit, not proof that ABCD1 never contributes. [PMID:11500517; PMID:18854420.]

Peroxisome organization is retained as non-core, based on complementation in Pex2-deficient cells rather than assigning ABCD1 a primary membrane insertion apparatus role. The older homology-based NAS source is distinguished from the later complementation experiment. [PMID:8441467; PMID:9425230.]
