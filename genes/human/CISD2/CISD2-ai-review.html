Gene Description

CISD2 (NAF-1/Miner1) is a small membrane-associated homodimeric protein that coordinates redox-active [2Fe-2S] clusters through its CDGSH domain. Its cytosolic domain can transfer a cluster to an apo-acceptor protein, and binding to BCL2 links it to restraint of Beclin 1-dependent autophagy at the endoplasmic reticulum. Human cell studies support endoplasmic reticulum localization; endogenous mouse tissue fractionation places Cisd2 predominantly in mitochondria, enriched at the outer membrane, with a smaller ER pool. CISD2 helps maintain organelle homeostasis, and loss of function causes Wolfram syndrome 2. Effects on calcium handling depend on the cellular context and the associated BCL2 complexes.

The Results interpret autophagy as clearance responding to damaged mitochondria. This is positive phenotype evidence, but it does not resolve a Cisd2-performed step in mitochondrial delivery or degradation. Increased autophagy after loss is also insufficient to assign a specific negative-mitophagy activity. This supersedes the earlier non-core retention rationale while preserving both original propagated source objects and the genuine mouse IMP record [PMID:19451219, Results: Mitochondrial degeneration and autophagy; Materials and methods: Western blotting and subcellular fractionation]. In human H1299 cells, NAF-1 depletion weakens BCL2–BECN1 association and prevents ER-targeted BCL2 from restraining starvation-induced autophagy. Bafilomycin/LC3 measurements, BECN1 codepletion and electron microscopy supply complementary controls. No obvious mitochondrial morphological change was observed in that four-hour system. This supports the defined regulatory contribution without making it a mitochondrial-selective clearance mechanism [PMID:20010695, Results on nutrient deprivation; Figure 4/5 descriptions; Autophagy assays].
