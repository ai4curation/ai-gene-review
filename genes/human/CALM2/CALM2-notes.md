# CALM2: calcium sensing and target regulation

CALM2 encodes calmodulin, whose canonical amino-acid sequence is identical to the proteins encoded by CALM1 and CALM3. Calcium binding and target association convert intracellular calcium signals into regulation of enzymes and ion channels. The three core units are CaMKII activation, calcineurin activation, and calcium-channel regulation. Calcium binding supplies the sensing mechanism within these units; it is not a fourth redundant core. A shared protein experiment supports the corresponding protein function without identifying which human CALM gene supplied an endogenous molecule.

Human CaMKII structures and interaction measurements show how calmodulin association relieves autoinhibition (PMID:20668654). The bacterial-effector study independently connects apo-calmodulin sequestration to loss of CaMKII activation in human cell systems (PMID:35568036). Its bacterial OspC enzymatic activity is separate from host calmodulin regulation. Human calmodulin conformational-lock experiments directly support calcineurin activation: disulfide locking impairs activation and chemical reduction restores it (PMID:8631777). Calcineurin performs dephosphorylation, while calmodulin supplies the activator function.

Calmodulin regulates calcium-channel gating through target association and calcium sensing. The long-QT study describes reduced calcium affinity and defective CaV1.2 inactivation for a characterized variant, with different effects on NaV1.5 and RyR2 (PMID:26969752). Mouse RyR2-R2474S cardiomyocyte work connects calmodulin association to calcium sparks and shows attenuation by added calmodulin (PMID:20226167). Other variant studies support target-dependent RyR2 mechanisms (PMID:26164367). These findings support a channel-regulator core rather than a universal claim that calmodulin inhibits every calcium channel in every condition.

CALM2 disease evidence must be separated from experiments on other CALM genes. The accessible long-QT source includes CALM1/2 genetic context and functional evidence for the shared protein (PMID:26969752). The 2012 ventricular-tachycardia paper reports CALM1 pedigrees, not CALM2 pedigrees (PMID:23040497). CALM3-A103V is likewise CALM3-specific genetic evidence (PMID:27516456). These distinctions preserve valid shared wild-type protein biology without transferring a clinical variant to the wrong gene. The available abstract and body of PMID:26969752 disagree in cohort counts; no prevalence estimate or disputed count is repeated here. Supplemental tables were not inspected, and exact variant-to-gene assignments are not guessed.

## Contextual functions and compartments

Potassium-channel regulation is a separate channel context. Calmodulin associates with KCNQ2/3 and is required for functional channel complexes in mouse-brain and CHO-cell experiments (PMID:12223552); SK2 regulation is supported by heterologous and distinct murine-heart experiments (PMID:27165696). These are not mislabeled as calcium-channel pore activities. In the NCKX4 study, calcium-competent calmodulin suppresses purinergic stimulation of exchanger activity, but does not suppress basal activity (PMID:33199372). Selected Methods use mouse NCKX4 and a chicken CALM2-encoded protein identical to the mammalian protein in HEK cells. The assay measures reverse-mode calcium entry; the calcium-export interpretation follows exchanger physiology and curation rather than a newly measured human export flux.

The mouse calmodulin-2 knockout study provides particularly useful gene-specific donor evidence for synaptic context. Wild-type calmodulin rescues endocytosis whereas a calcium-binding-defective mutant does not, supporting a calcium-sensor contribution rather than necessity alone (PMID:31628181). The calyx, presynaptic cytosol and endocytosis annotations are retained as contextual functions. Mouse gene-specific manipulation does not establish a unique human CALM2 expression pattern.

CP110 binding and a calmodulin-binding-defective CP110 mutant support regulatory cooperation in cytokinesis (PMID:16760425). Centrosomal and spindle locations are retained with curator and reviewed UniProt support. The available canonical extraction includes the abstract, selected cytokinesis Results and Discussion, but omits most Methods, Results and localization images. It therefore does not support a claim that every original localization assay was independently reanalyzed. Human sperm-nucleus proteomics provides an experimental nuclear annotation, although the target peptide table was not inspected (PMID:21630459). Independently curated human CaN:CaM:NFAT and NFAT:CaM nuclear events corroborate the broad nuclear pool (Reactome:R-HSA-2730867; Reactome:R-HSA-4551465). Their cached titles and summaries, rather than a reconstructed reaction graph or new imaging experiment, were inspected.

The three OspC3 reaction summaries supply interaction and reaction context (Reactome:R-HSA-9956602; Reactome:R-HSA-9956624; Reactome:R-HSA-9956667). They contain no explicit cytosol field. Cytosol attribution comes from the preserved Reactome-derived GOA assertions and independent broad cytoplasmic evidence.
