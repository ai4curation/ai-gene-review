# CSTB research notes

2026-10-13. Human CSTB is HGNC:2482/P04080, the 98-residue stefin B. The official displayed sequence agrees with the genuine fetched UniProt record and FASTA. The recorded CST6 alias must not be confused with the distinct approved CST6 gene. No alternative product is invented. The 1STF recombinant structure has C3S/E31Y relative to the current sequence; it does not establish a native oligomer or an alternative isoform.

CSTB binds and inhibits papain-like cysteine proteases. The original chemistry consultation distinguishes human and bovine inhibitor variants, mixed target-enzyme species and reducing assay conditions [PMID:11514663 “binding tightly to papain and cathepsin L”]. The full 1988 paper remained unavailable, but its complete abstract explicitly establishes recombinant human activity [PMID:3053245 “native recombinant stefin B were inhibitory to papain”]. The 1984 cystatin-C-centered paper really includes CSTB; its CSTB kinetic constants reproduce prior work, so this is not a wrong-gene citation and not a new CSTB kinetic experiment. One inhibitor core is paired with negative regulation of peptidase activity, accommodating the measured endo-/exopeptidase spectrum without asserting equal physiological control of every protease.

Human localization and nuclear functional studies support an intracellular core [PMID:20075068 “localized in the nucleus and the cytosol.”]. The actual 2001 main Methods/Results include nuclear and cytoplasmic imaging with antibody controls and differentiation-dependent distribution. Its immunogold Results also report mitochondrial labeling within the cytoplasmic pool; the original broad cytoplasm assertion is retained rather than narrowed to the independently supported cytosol pool. The separately recovered 2010 accepted manuscript distinguishes forced nuclear expression in human T98G, CUX1 processing in human MCF7, mouse knockout fibroblasts and human fluorescent constructs in CHO-K1 FRET. Histone potentiation of inhibition is an in-vitro result. Neither colocalization nor this experimental collection proves a uniform endogenous complex in every cell. HPA separately reports antibody-specific nucleolar and cytosolic locations, with no new ribosome-biogenesis claim.

Extracellular occurrence is positive, contextual evidence. The 1986 original identifies CSTB specifically [PMID:3488317 “one as cystatin B”], while its urinary isolation does not establish constitutive secretion. Exact supplementary rows identify CSTB in ECM-enriched colon/liver and omental fractions, parotid exosome preparations, and the authors’ urinary-vesicle database. These observations do not make CSTB a matrix structural protein or vesicle-biogenesis factor. The particular tear-screen and EPS-urine target-table attributions remain UNDECIDED because their CSTB rows were not recovered; no wrong-gene or biological-absence claim is made.
