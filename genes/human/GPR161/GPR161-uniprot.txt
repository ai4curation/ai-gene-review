CC   -!- FUNCTION: G protein-coupled receptor that acts as a key inhibitor of
CC       the smoothened signaling pathway in primary cilia (PubMed:38326651).
CC       Inhibits the smoothened signaling pathway in absence of hedgehog (DHH,
CC       IHH or SHH) by promoting formation of the transcription repressor form
CC       of GLI2 and GLI3 transcription factors (PubMed:38326651). GPR161
CC       activation triggers signaling via guanine nucleotide-binding protein
CC       G(s)/GNAS, mediating activation of adenylate cyclase activity and
CC       production of cAMP (PubMed:38326651). Increased cAMP levels activate
CC       PKA, promoting PKA-dependent processing of GLI2 and GLI3 transcription
CC       factors, leading to formation of transcription repressor GLI2R and
CC       GLI3R, which repress expression of target genes (PubMed:38326651).
CC       GPR161 also activates PKA by acting as a PKA-anchoring protein, which
CC       directly associates with type I PKA regulatory subunits (PRKAR1A and/or
CC       PRKAR1B), recruiting PKA followed by PKA-dependent processing of GLI2
CC       and GLI3 transcription factors (PubMed:38326651). In presence of
CC       hedgehog, GPR161 is removed from primary cilia by smoothened (SMO),
CC       preventing its activity and allowing activation of the smoothened
CC       signaling (By similarity). In contrast to the majority of G protein-
CC       coupled receptors, it is not activated by a ligand and it either acts
CC       as a constitutively active receptor or as a mechanosensitive receptor
CC       (PubMed:38326651). Involved in regulation of the smoothened signaling
CC       pathway during early neural tube development, forelimb formation,
CC       skeletal development, embryonic cerebellar development and forebrain
CC       development (By similarity). Involved in the saltatory migration of
CC       neurons during brain development by acting as a mechanosensitive
CC       receptor that senses mechanical forces generated by fluid shear stress
CC       (By similarity). {ECO:0000250|UniProtKB:B2RPY5,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:38326651}.
CC   -!- ACTIVITY REGULATION: In contrast to the majority of G protein-coupled
CC       receptors, it is not activated by a ligand and it either acts as a
CC       constitutively active receptor or as a mechanosensitive receptor
CC       (PubMed:38326651). Cholesterol promotes coupling to G(s)/GNAS and G
CC       protein-coupled receptor activity (PubMed:38326651).
CC       {ECO:0000269|PubMed:38326651}.
