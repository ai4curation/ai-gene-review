# GABRB3 biological reading

GABRB3 encodes the beta3 contribution to pentameric GABA-A receptors. Full-length human alpha1/beta3/gamma2 receptors provide direct structural and functional evidence for ligand interfaces and chloride conductance; engineered beta3 homomers are a separate experimental system. Native human brain preparations also contain beta3, including mixed beta2/beta3 assemblies, but alpha1-based purification and surgical sampling do not define every physiological receptor composition. These observations support a single receptor/channel core rather than separate universal functions for every recombinant composition ([PMID:30602789](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30602789/), [PMID:24909990](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24909990/), [PMID:39843743](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39843743/)).

Rat beta3-specific freeze-fracture labeling establishes postsynaptic and extrasynaptic membrane pools. The original authors explicitly leave the contribution of the extrasynaptic particles to pyramidal tonic currents unresolved and discuss transient membrane pools. Functional human alpha4/beta3/delta assemblies independently establish the relevant receptor capability. The extrasynaptic core process is therefore a synthesis of compartment and receptor-function evidence, not an assertion that the imaging study directly measured ambient-GABA responses ([PMID:21073549](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21073549/), [PMID:35355020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35355020/)). The older rat localization source uses beta2/3-reactive labeling and cannot independently isolate beta3 ([PMID:9464994](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9464994/)).

Receptor contact formation is context dependent. In the neuron/HEK coculture study, beta3-containing receptors increased inhibitory presynaptic contacts; the vesicle-release experiment presented for beta2 must not be reassigned to beta3. Mouse receptor-subunit work and physiological neuronal-current studies support additional developmental and transmission contexts, with construct/species distinctions retained ([PMID:25489750](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25489750/), [PMID:27129275](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27129275/), [PMID:12522165](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12522165/)). The beta3 knockout cochlear abstract reports reduced afferent density and a neuropathy, but does not resolve formation versus maintenance of innervation. Its four precise developmental/innervation assertions remain undecided. Beta2-specific early-development and later-degeneration timing cannot fill that gap ([PMID:17021187](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17021187/)).

The negative-regulation NAS claim is a source-specific regulator-versus-regulated-product mismatch. The complete main text of the cited review assigns negative receptor trafficking and transmission to CLPTM1 and changes in receptor kinetics to SHISA7; beta3-containing receptors are the regulated assemblies. This supports removing that particular assertion without claiming that beta3 can never have any regulatory role ([PMID:33234346](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33234346/)). Likewise, parent-of-origin regulation of GABRB3 expression does not establish the encoded receptor subunit as an imprinting effector; the broad biological claim is marked over-annotated with the original provenance retained ([PMID:15878204](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15878204/)).

Several historical sources support a correct biological claim without independently resolving the exact original beta3 assay. The epsilon-subunit paper describes human epsilon assembly with alpha and beta subunits, not a verified beta3 clone or baculovirus experiment. Its three original beta3 annotations remain accepted through independent human beta3 receptor evidence, with the source limit explicit ([PMID:9039914](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9039914/)). The alpha1/beta2/gamma2 structural paper likewise supplies receptor-family context, while independent beta3 evidence grounds the beta3-specific judgment ([PMID:29950725](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29950725/)). Unrecovered individual assays are not evidence of wrong-gene attribution. The dendritic-spine and cytoplasmic-vesicle assertions remain undecided where the exact beta3 grounding is unresolved ([PMID:25025157](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25025157/), [PMID:25172774](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25172774/)).

The human variant study measures expressed beta3 using a beta2/3-reactive antibody in controlled transfections with mock subtraction; the antibody itself is not beta3-specific. Gating changes and reduced surface abundance differ among variants. The paper's wild-type/heterozygous/homozygous terminology denotes expression conditions, not direct measurements in patient cells. Historical transcript-variant names are retained without assigning a modern UniProt suffix ([PMID:26950270](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26950270/)).

Four current products are preserved by exact sequence: P28472-1 and -2 are different 473-residue precursors, and -3/-4 are 402/388 residues. Engineered intracellular-loop replacements, BRIL insertions, tags, nanobody-bound structures and historical transcript names are not natural splice-product identities. PMID:35140402 calls its mature beta3 sequence 1–447, whereas the current 473-residue precursor minus the first 25 residues is 448 residues; PMID:24909990 explicitly uses precursor residues 26–473. That source-coordinate discrepancy is preserved rather than converted into a product change ([PMID:18514161](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18514161/), [PMID:22243422](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22243422/), [PMID:35140402](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35140402/)). The engineered alpha5/beta3 letter was read through the indexed original PMC main text and Figure 1 caption; its supplement-defined sequence was not inspected ([PMID:30140029](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6123483/)).
