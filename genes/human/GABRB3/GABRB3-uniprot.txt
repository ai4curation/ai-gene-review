CC   -!- FUNCTION: Beta subunit of the heteropentameric ligand-gated chloride
CC       channel gated by gamma-aminobutyric acid (GABA), a major inhibitory
CC       neurotransmitter in the brain (PubMed:14993607, PubMed:18514161,
CC       PubMed:22243422, PubMed:22303015, PubMed:24909990, PubMed:26950270,
CC       PubMed:30602789). GABA-gated chloride channels, also named GABA(A)
CC       receptors (GABAAR), consist of five subunits arranged around a central
CC       pore and contain GABA active binding site(s) located at the alpha and
CC       beta subunit interface(s) (PubMed:24909990, PubMed:30140029,
CC       PubMed:30602789). GABAARs containing beta-3/GABRB3 subunit are found at
CC       both synaptic and extrasynaptic sites (By similarity). When activated
CC       by GABA, GABAARs selectively allow the flow of chloride anions across
CC       the cell membrane down their electrochemical gradient (PubMed:14993607,
CC       PubMed:22303015, PubMed:26950270, PubMed:30602789). Chloride influx
CC       into the postsynaptic neuron following GABAAR opening decreases the
CC       neuron ability to generate a new action potential, thereby reducing
CC       nerve transmission (PubMed:22303015, PubMed:26950270). GABAARs
CC       containing alpha-1 and beta-3 subunits exhibit synaptogenic activity;
CC       the gamma-2 subunit being necessary but not sufficient to induce rapid
CC       synaptic contacts formation (PubMed:25489750). Extrasynaptic beta-3
CC       receptors contribute to the tonic GABAergic inhibition (By similarity).
CC       GABAARs containing alpha-1, beta-3 and epsilon subunits may also permit
CC       spontaneous chloride channel activity while preserving the structural
CC       information required for GABA-gated openings (By similarity). Beta-
CC       containing GABAARs can simultaneously bind GABA and histamine where
CC       histamine binds at the interface of two neighboring beta subunits,
CC       which may be involved in the regulation of sleep and wakefulness
CC       (PubMed:18281286, PubMed:24909990, PubMed:35355020). Plays an important
CC       role in somatosensation and in the production of antinociception (By
CC       similarity). {ECO:0000250|UniProtKB:P63079,
