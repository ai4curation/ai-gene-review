|---|---|---|---|
| Human COX18 is an integral mitochondrial membrane protein essential for complex IV biogenesis. | TALEN-generated **COX18**-knockout HEK293T clones lacked detectable complex IV, while other OXPHOS complexes were not detectably altered. COX18-FLAG localized to mitochondria, resisted alkaline-carbonate extraction, and restored complex IV abundance. (pqac-00000018, pqac-00000019) | Establishes mitochondrial membrane localization and an essential, relatively selective role in complex IV assembly or stability; COX18 is not a structural subunit or catalytic enzyme of mature complex IV. | [Bourens and Barrientos, JBC; published March 22, 2017](https://doi.org/10.1074/jbc.M117.778514) |
| COX18 translocates the C-terminal portion of MT-CO2 across the inner mitochondrial membrane. | In mitoplasts, MT-CO2 was proteinase-K sensitive when exposed to the intermembrane space but remained protected in COX18-knockout cells; sonication restored protease accessibility. The affected C-terminal region represents approximately half of MT-CO2 and contains the apo-CuA site. (pqac-00000020, pqac-00000046) | Direct evidence supports a **C-tail insertase/translocase** function. It does not establish COX18 as the insertase for the N-proximal transmembrane segment of MT-CO2. | [Bourens and Barrientos, JBC; 2017](https://doi.org/10.1074/jbc.M117.778514) |
| COX18 acts transiently between MT-CO2 stabilization and CuA-site maturation. | MT-CO2 was synthesized normally during a 30-minute pulse in knockout cells but became unstable during the chase. COX18-FLAG weakly but reproducibly co-immunoprecipitated MT-CO2 and associated with COX20 and a small SCO1 fraction. Without COX18, COX20 still bound some MT-CO2 but no longer co-precipitated SCO1, SCO2, or COA6. (pqac-00000019, pqac-00000020) | The interactions and post-translational instability are direct findings. The proposed sequence—COX20 binding, COX18-mediated translocation, COX18 release, and SCO1/SCO2/COA6-dependent metallation—is a mechanistic model rather than direct observation of copper transfer. | [Bourens and Barrientos, JBC; May 2017](https://doi.org/10.1074/jbc.M117.778514) |
| COX18 is distinct from the broader mitochondrial insertase OXA1L. | COX18 belongs to the Oxa1/YidC/Alb3 family, but the human study found no evidence that OXA1L substitutes for the MT-CO2 C-tail function of COX18. OXA1L knockdown had previously affected complexes I and V without reducing complexes III or IV. (pqac-00000017, pqac-00000021) | Family homology does not imply functional interchangeability. **COX18/OXA1L2 (Q8N8Q8) must not be conflated with OXA1L**; cross-species complementation is also limited by substrate-recognition specificity. | [Bourens and Barrientos, JBC; May 2017](https://doi.org/10.1074/jbc.M117.778514) |
| Homozygous **COX18 c.667G>C, p.Asp223His** causes an isolated complex IV assembly disorder. | Patient myoblasts had complex IV activity at **36% of control**; MT-CO2, MT-CO3, and COX4 steady-state levels were **36%, 46%, and 54%**, respectively. Fully assembled complex IV and in-gel activity were markedly reduced. COX18 knockdown reduced activity by about 60%, whereas wild-type COX18 complementation rescued the patient-cell biochemical defect. (pqac-00000028, pqac-00000000) | Direct evidence comes from one affected individual with neonatal encephalo-cardio-myopathy and axonal sensory neuropathy. It strongly supports pathogenicity but cannot establish broad genotype-phenotype correlations. | [Ronchi et al., European Journal of Human Genetics; published July 2023](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41431-023-01433-6) |
| Homozygous **COX18 c.598G>A** disrupts splicing and produces residual COX18Δ112-240 function. | Patient cDNA contained three abnormal splice products; a surviving transcript encoded COX18Δ112-240. Muscle complex IV activity was **18.38 nmol/min/mg**, versus a **34-180** reference range, while other respiratory-chain activities were normal. Fibroblast mitochondrial complex IV activity was **7.5-fold lower** than controls and rose **threefold** after full-length COX18 complementation, without reaching normal. (pqac-00000024, pqac-00000027, pqac-00000036) | This second single-patient report was associated with severe sensory-motor neuropathy and oculofacial apraxia. Expression of a residual truncated protein makes it a hypomorphic model rather than a complete loss-of-function state. | [Mavillard et al., BBA Molecular Basis of Disease; available July 1, 2024](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.bbadis.2024.167330) |
| COX18Δ112-240 permits gross complex IV and supercomplex formation but yields poorly functional enzyme. | Patient fibroblasts showed markedly reduced MT-CO2 but control-like COX1 and COX4 levels. BN-PAGE revealed no substantial change in gross complex IV or respiratory-supercomplex band patterns, and the truncated protein was detected in the complex IV band despite the severe activity deficit. (pqac-00000034) | Assembly detected by BN-PAGE does not demonstrate normal molecular composition, topology, CuA loading, or catalytic competence; retained gross assembly therefore does not contradict the MT-CO2 maturation role of COX18. | [Mavillard et al., BBA Molecular Basis of Disease; 2024](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.bbadis.2024.167330) |
