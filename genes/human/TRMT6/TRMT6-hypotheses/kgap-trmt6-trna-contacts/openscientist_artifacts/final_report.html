TRMT6 (Q9UJA5): what the subunit itself contributes, residue by residue

Hypothesis slug: kgap-trmt6-trna-contacts Source file: genes/human/TRMT6/TRMT6-ai-review.yaml Focus type: free_text Organism: Homo sapiens (NCBITaxon:9606)

Summary

Verdict: partially supported, with one component refuted.

Three of the four factual components of the seed hypothesis are confirmed by direct measurement on the deposited coordinates. The fourth, the proposal that TRMT6 contacts hold the splayed tRNA conformation and so make it a tRNA-remodelling subunit, is refuted.

TRMT6 contacts the tRNA through 37 residues and the catalytic subunit TRMT61A through 80 residues, and the two sets are almost disjoint (5 residues in common). The tRNA-contacting set touches 24 nucleotides across the T-stem (7 nt), T-loop (6 nt), variable loop (4 nt), anticodon stem (4 nt), D-stem (1 nt) and D-loop (1 nt), with zero contacts to the acceptor stem. The TRMT6-tRNA interface buries 1701 Å² per side, larger than the 1440 Å² that the catalytic subunit buries against the same tRNA. Across eight eukaryotic orthologs from human to budding yeast, the tRNA-contacting surface is conserved above background (67.6% mean identity against 50.7%, one-sided Mann-Whitney p = 1.8e-07, A = 0.75) and above the protein-protein interface (58.6%, p = 0.023, A = 0.63).

The enables-versus-contributes_to question is settled by distance. TRMT6 contributes no residue to the catalytic pocket and no residue to the S-adenosylmethionine site. The nearest TRMT6 heavy atom sits 4.14 Å from the cofactor and 8.46 Å from the N1 of nucleotide 58 that receives the methyl group in the substrate structure 5CCB, and 4.25 Å and 8.95 Å in the product structure 5CCX. TRMT61A reaches 2.53 Å and 3.15 Å over the same measurements. At a 4.0 Å heavy-atom criterion the count for TRMT6 is zero in both states. Profile-level evidence agrees: TRMT6 carries only IPR017423/PF04189 and lacks IPR029063, PF08704 and SSF53335, the SAM-dependent methyltransferase signatures carried by TRMT61A and by both archaeal and bacterial TrmI proteins.

The remodelling component fails its test. Superposing the bound tRNA (5CCB chain C) on free canonical tRNA (1EHZ chain A) over a 24-atom rigid core shows that TRMT6's exclusive footprint sits at the canonical-fold baseline (median C1' displacement 3.6 Å, n = 19), statistically indistinguishable from nucleotides that touch no protein at all (3.9 Å, n = 40, two-sided p = 0.98, A = 0.50 exactly). The nucleotides pulled out of position are D-loop 15-19, and TRMT61A holds those (median 13.4 Å, n = 17, one-sided p = 4.3e-03, A = 0.76). All three broken tertiary pairs of the elbow have their displaced partner contacted by TRMT61A and not by TRMT6. TRMT6 clamps the rigid body of the tRNA in register. TRMT61A opens the elbow.

The molecular-function wording the structural evidence supports for TRMT6 alone: it binds tRNA, it does not catalyse, it does not bind SAM. The existing term GO:0000049 tRNA binding already fits and no new term is needed. It is currently absent from the TRMT6 GO record, which carries only the generic GO:0003723 RNA binding (HDA, from an mRNA-interactome capture) and GO:0005515 protein binding as molecular functions.

Most important caveats. First, the one measurement that would settle enables versus contributes_to has never been reported: nobody has shown TRMT6 binding tRNA in the absence of TRMT61A. Yeast biochemistry (PMID: 10779558) shows the converse, that Gcd14p alone is impaired for tRNA binding, which is an argument for TRMT6 supplying affinity but not a demonstration of autonomous binding. Second, the remodelling refutation uses a cross-species reference (yeast tRNA-Phe against human tRNA3Lys) with a 2.92 Å rigid-core RMSD floor, so it supports the contrast between classes (13.4 Å against 3.6 Å) and not any absolute displacement near the floor. Third, the count of strictly invariant contact residues is unstable to the alignment gap model (1 under linear gaps, 9 under affine), so the enrichment should be cited and no single-residue claim should enter the review.

Key findings

F1. TRMT6 buries more tRNA surface than the catalytic subunit and grips a distinct half of the molecule

Shrake-Rupley SASA on 5CCB biological assembly 1 (probe 1.4 Å, 256 points) gives a TRMT6-tRNA interface of 1701 Å² per side, which is 12.0% of the isolated tRNA surface of 14191 Å². The catalytic subunit TRMT61A buries 1440 Å² of the same tRNA, 10.1%. The protein-protein interfaces are larger still: 2493 Å² per side within a heterodimer and 1462 Å² across the dimer-of-heterodimers interface. TRMT6's contact with the second tRNA in the assembly is 221 Å² over 7 residues (411-424) and is incidental.

At a 4.0 Å heavy-atom criterion in 5CCB, TRMT6 chain B uses 37 residues to touch 24 nucleotides, while TRMT61A uses 33 residues to touch 17. The two subunits grip non-overlapping halves of the substrate. TRMT6 holds the T-stem (49-53, 64-65), T-loop (54-59), variable loop (44-47), anticodon stem (30-31, 42-43), the D-stem/D-loop boundary (21-22) and the hinge nucleotide 9. TRMT61A holds the acceptor stem (3-4, 71-73), the D-loop (15-19), the T-loop (55-60) and T-stem 61. Only nucleotides 55-59 are shared, which is where the methylation target sits.

Across all three structures and four independent TRMT6 copies, 45 residues contact tRNA at 4.0 Å, of which 37 reproduce in two or more copies. The reproducible 37 are the set used throughout this report: 33, 34, 49, 94, 97, 99, 103, 104, 108, 115, 128, 134, 138, 141, 142, 145, 146, 149, 150, 153, 154, 174, 175, 176, 177, 179, 182, 210, 234, 237, 239, 350, 377, 434, 438, 439, 441.

The 80 residues contacting TRMT61A overlap this set by only 5 positions, so the subunit's protein-binding surface and its RNA-binding surface are separable at the sequence level, which is what a residue-grounded molecular-function statement needs.

F2. TRMT6 contributes nothing to the SAM pocket or to the methylation chemistry

The minimum heavy-atom distance from any TRMT6 atom to S-adenosyl-L-homocysteine is 4.14 Å in 5CCB, 4.25 Å in 5CCX and 4.47 Å in 5CD1. In every structure the closest approach is Asn94 side-chain OD1/ND2 to the SAH ribose 2'-hydroxyl. No TRMT6 atom falls within 4.0 Å of SAH in any copy, and none approaches the SAH sulfur or the methyl-transfer vector. TRMT61A contacts SAH through 21-24 residue pairs at the same criterion.

For the catalytic pocket the gap is wider. The closest TRMT6 atom to the base of nucleotide 58 is 6.17 Å (5CCB) and 6.63 Å (5CCX); to the N1 that receives the methyl group it is 8.46 Å and 8.95 Å. TRMT61A-trans reaches 3.09 Å and 3.15 Å over the same measurements in 5CCB, and 2.87 Å and 3.19 Å in 5CCX. The product structure 5CCX models residue 58 as 1MA, confirming the methylated state, so these are measurements on a genuine before-and-after pair.

TRMT6's only contribution at the active site is backbone capture of the flipped nucleotide. His441 NE2 reaches OP1 at 2.66 Å (5CCB), 2.71 Å (5CCX) and 2.95 Å (5CD1); Lys145 NZ reaches OP2 at 2.75-3.01 Å; Gln434 NE2 reaches OP1 at 2.88-3.25 Å; His441 NE2 reaches O5' at 3.03 Å. No TRMT6 atom reaches the adenine N1, C2, N6, N7 or C8 of residue 58. The same three contacts appear in the unmethylated substrate and in the methylated product, so they are a stable feature of the holoenzyme.

Yeast biochemistry reached the same conclusion from the other direction fifteen years before the structures: PMID: 10779558 reports that "binding of AdoMet and tRNA substrates depends on different subunits of the complex".

F3. The tRNA-contacting surface is conserved above background across eukaryotes

Eight orthologs were aligned to human TRMT6 (497 aa) by Needleman-Wunsch with BLOSUM62: mouse Q8CE96 (85.7% global identity), cow Q2T9V5 (93.4%), X. tropicalis Q6DF58 (68.1%), zebrafish Q32LR0 (62.2%), D. melanogaster Q7KTM0 (34.0%), Dictyostelium Q54UB1 (28.0%), S. pombe Q9HGL4 (25.2%) and S. cerevisiae P41814/Gcd10 (24.3%). Background mean identity over all 497 positions is 50.7%.

The tRNA set is more conserved than the TRMT61A set, two-sided p = 0.023, A = 0.63. Both interface classes sit above background, which is expected for any buried surface, and the tRNA class sits above both.

The count of strictly invariant positions is not stable to the gap model. Linear gaps (-8) give one invariant tRNA contact, K145. Affine gaps (-11 open, -1 extend) give nine. A surface held above background identity from human to budding yeast, roughly 1.5 billion years, is being maintained for a reason, which supports annotating it as a function of the protein rather than treating it as incidental surface. The mutational evidence agrees: PMID: 17932071 reports that substitutions in Trm6p destroy tRNA binding by the yeast complex.

F4. TRMT6 does not hold the splayed conformation; TRMT61A does

This is the component of the seed hypothesis that the data refute, and it was tested directly rather than assumed.

The bound tRNA (5CCB chain C, human tRNA3Lys, 77 nt) was superposed on free yeast tRNA-Phe (1EHZ chain A, 1.93 Å, canonical L-shape) using the 24 C1' atoms of the acceptor stem (1-7, 66-72) and anticodon stem (27-31, 39-43), the regions expected to be unchanged. The rigid-core RMSD is 2.92 Å, which sets the measurement floor because the two tRNAs differ in sequence and species. Per-nucleotide C1' displacement was then scored for the 76 shared positions and classified by which subunit contacts that nucleotide.

Every broken pair has its displaced D-loop partner (15, 18, 19) contacted by TRMT61A and not by TRMT6. Every intact pair that is contacted at all (22, 46, 44, 9) is contacted by TRMT6. The division of labour is that TRMT6 clamps the rigid body of the tRNA in register and TRMT61A opens the elbow and presents A58.

For curation this matters twice. It supports a tRNA-binding molecular-function term and it argues against any wording that credits TRMT6 with remodelling, unwinding or chaperone-like activity. It also reinforces that TRMT6 should not take enables with the catalytic term, since the subunit that acts on both the substrate geometry and the methyl transfer is TRMT61A.

Limits. The reference is a different tRNA species, so absolute displacements below about 3 Å carry no information and the conclusion rests on the class contrast, which is far above the floor for the TRMT61A set. Nucleotides 76 (23.4 Å) and 75 (14.5 Å) are the flexible 3' CCA end and are disregarded. A single bound structure cannot establish the order of events, so this describes the end state and not whether TRMT6 binds before or after the elbow opens.

F5. TRMT6 inherited its place in the complex and evolved its grip on the tRNA

The P. abyssi TrmI homotetramer (3MB5, biological assembly 1, four copies of entity 1) was superposed onto the human complex (5CCB biological assembly 1), anchoring TrmI subunit A onto TRMT61A chain A. Needleman-Wunsch gave 255 aligned pairs at 30.6% identity; iterative Kabsch with 4.0 Å outlier rejection converged on a 226 CA core at 1.92 Å RMSD.

That single anchor reproduces the whole quaternary arrangement. Centroid distances of the superposed archaeal subunits to their human counterparts: TrmI A-3 to TRMT61A-trans 3.2 Å, TrmI A-4 to TRMT6 6.8 Å, TrmI A-2 to the second TRMT6 copy 7.3 Å. TrmI A-4 covers 69.8% of TRMT6 heavy atoms within 5 Å, against 9.1%, 5.2% and 0.3% for the other three subunits. TRMT6's position in the eukaryotic heterotetramer is the position a second catalytic-fold protomer fills in the archaeal homotetramer. This matches PMID: 22168821: "All TrmIs are assembled as tetramers formed by dimers of tightly assembled dimers", and the P. abyssi tetramer specifically "is strengthened by four intersubunit disulfide bridges", so it is a genuine tetramer and not a crystallographic artefact.

Residue-level ancestry, scored as the distance from each TRMT6 CA to the nearest CA of the archaeal subunit occupying the same position, splits sharply by interface class.

Fisher exact test, tRNA-contacting against TRMT61A-contacting: OR = 0.053, p = 1.5e-10. Overall, 224 of 371 resolved TRMT6 residues (60.4%) lie within 4.0 Å of the ancestral fold.

The split is not a cutoff artefact. Sweeping the ancestral-core cutoff from 2.5 to 8.0 Å gives tRNA-contacting 18.9, 21.6, 24.3, 27.0, 32.4, 35.1 and 40.5 percent, and TRMT61A-contacting 65.0, 76.3, 81.3, 87.5, 91.3, 92.5 and 93.8 percent. OR stays between 0.044 and 0.126 and p between 1.5e-10 and 3.4e-06; the two curves never approach each other.

The archaeal subunit at the TRMT6 position does not reproduce the TRMT6 tRNA footprint. TRMT6 contacts 24 nucleotides including 7 in the T-stem; the superposed TrmI A-4 covers only 8 and none in the T-stem, Jaccard overlap 7/25 = 0.28.

This separates two claims that are easy to conflate. TRMT6 did not acquire its place in the complex by evolving a new protein-protein interaction; it inherited it. What it acquired is the grip on the T-stem, T-loop and variable loop, which sits in insertions and extensions absent from TrmI and is part of why TRMT6 is 497 residues against 255 modelled for TrmI. For the GO question this argues that the informative annotation for TRMT6 alone is tRNA binding, because the protein-protein surface is ancestral boilerplate while the tRNA surface is the eukaryote-specific feature of this subunit.

F6. What the archaeal comparison cannot show

An RCSB full-text search for TrmI and m1A58 methyltransferase structures returned 5C1I, 5C0O, 2PWY, 2YVL, 3MB5, 3LHD, 3LGA and 1I9G. Querying the RCSB data REST API entry by entry, all eight are annotated as homomeric protein with a single polymer entity and no RNA entity. No experimental archaeal or bacterial TrmI structure has tRNA bound, so a like-for-like archaeal contact map cannot be computed from experimental data. The seed hypothesis asks for conservation "in the archaeal homotetrameric homolog", and that part can be answered at the level of architecture and fold but not residue identity.

The modelled placement of TrmI A-4 also carries steric overlap with the human tRNA (36 tRNA atoms within 2.0 Å of A-4, 33 for A-3), meaning the apo/SAM archaeal tetramer would have to adjust to accept a splayed tRNA. PMID: 22168821 gives the independent reason for caution: "The monomers of the five enzymes are structurally highly similar, but the inter-monomer contacts differ strongly." No residue-level archaeal contact claim is made anywhere in this report.

The sequence-profile route is closed for the same reason at a different level. TRMT6 shares no InterPro or Pfam signature with TrmI, so there is no profile on which to align the contact residues. That absence is itself informative about catalysis (F2) but it means the archaeal half of the seed question has no sequence-level answer either.

F7. UniProt already commits to substrate binding; GO does not

UniProt Q9UJA5 carries seven "Binding site" features with ligand "substrate", at residues 94-104, 145-154, 175-182, 349, 377, 415-423 and 434-441, each evidenced by ECO:0000269 PubMed:26470919 together with ECO:0007744 PDB:5CD1. The FUNCTION line reads "Substrate-binding subunit of tRNA (adenine-N(1)-)-methyltransferase, which catalyzes the formation of N(1)-methyladenine at position 58 (m1A58) in initiator methionyl-tRNA (PubMed:16043508)". SUBUNIT reads "Heterotetramer; composed of two copies of TRMT6 and two copies of TRMT61A". SIMILARITY places TRMT6 in the TRM6/GCD10 family.

Roughly 21 of the 37 computed contact residues fall inside the seven UniProt ranges and 15 do not (33, 34, 49, 108, 115, 128, 134, 138, 141, 174, 210, 234, 237, 239). Conversely the UniProt ranges are spans rather than contacting residues, so they include positions that make no contact at 4.0 Å. The database evidence is attached to the 3.6 Å entry 5CD1, while the 2.0 Å entry 5CCB supports a residue-resolved set.

The GO record for Q9UJA5 (QuickGO, retrieved 2026-11-25, 21 annotation records) tells a different story.

Mechanistic model

The holoenzyme divides the substrate between its two subunit types. TRMT6 provides the larger of the two tRNA surfaces, 1701 Å² against 1440 Å², and places it on the T-arm, variable loop and anticodon stem, the parts of the tRNA that stay in their canonical positions when bound. TRMT61A takes the acceptor end and the D-loop, and it is the D-loop that moves, by a median of 13.4 Å, breaking three tertiary pairs of the elbow. Nucleotide 58 ends up at the boundary where the two footprints overlap, flipped into the TRMT61A active site of the opposing heterodimer, with TRMT6 holding its phosphate through His441, Lys145 and Gln434 while TRMT61A performs the chemistry from 2.53 Å away. PMID: 26470919 states the trans geometry directly: "tRNAs bind across the dimer interface such that Trm6 from the opposing heterodimer brings A58 into the active site of Trm61".

The evolutionary history explains why the two surfaces are asymmetric. TRMT6 occupies the slot of a second catalytic protomer in an ancestral homotetramer, and its interface with TRMT61A maps onto that ancestral contact almost completely (87.5% within 4.0 Å of the ancestral fold). Its tRNA surface does not (27.0%), and roughly three quarters of it sits in eukaryote-specific insertions. The protein kept the architecture and rebuilt the business end.

The qualifier question, stated plainly

GO's contributes_to exists for the case where an activity belongs to a complex and a subunit supplies part of it. The catalytic term GO:0160107 is exactly that case for TRMT6, and the answer is unambiguous from the distance measurements: TRMT6 is not in the active site, so enables is wrong and contributes_to is the ceiling.

GO:0000049 tRNA binding is a different question and it is the open one. The structural data show a 1701 Å² TRMT6-tRNA interface, larger than TRMT61A's 1440 Å² against the same tRNA, built from 37 residues conserved above background to budding yeast. That is a binding surface belonging to the TRMT6 polypeptide. Whether the isolated polypeptide binds tRNA with measurable affinity has never been tested. PMID: 10779558 shows that removing Gcd10p severely impairs tRNA binding, and PMID: 17932071 shows that substitutions in Trm6p destroy the complex's ability to bind tRNA, so both are evidence that TRMT6 supplies binding. Neither is evidence that it binds alone. A curator who requires demonstrated autonomous activity for enables should use contributes_to and record the gap.

What not to do

Do not add any wording that credits TRMT6 with remodelling, unwinding, chaperone or helicase-like activity. The measurement that would support such a term was run and came out negative: TRMT6's exclusive footprint sits on tRNA in its canonical conformation, indistinguishable from the uncontacted baseline (p = 0.98, A = 0.50).

Mechanistic scope

Direct gene-product activity

The immediate molecular function under test is tRNA binding by the TRMT6 polypeptide within the α2β2 holoenzyme. Measured directly on coordinates: a 1701 Å² interface per side, 37 residues, 24 nucleotides, concentrated on the T-arm and the variable loop, with no acceptor-stem contact and no active-site participation.

Complex-level activity, not TRMT6's own

m1A58 methyl transfer is the activity of TRMT61A acting in trans across the dimer interface. TRMT6's contribution to that event is holding the backbone of the flipped nucleotide 58 through three hydrogen bonds. That is a contribution to catalysis by the complex, measured at a distance from the chemistry itself.

Not TRMT6's direct activity

The conformational opening of the tRNA elbow is held by TRMT61A contacts on D-loop 15-19. mRNA m1A deposition (PMID: 29072297) is a complex-level activity on substrates that adopt tRNA T-loop-like folds, downstream of the same binding surface and the basis for the GO:0006397 BP annotation. Mitochondrial m1A58 runs through TRMT61B as a homo-oligomer with no TRMT6 involvement (PMID: 23097428), so it is outside TRMT6's scope entirely.

Evidence base

PMID: 26470919, Crystal Structure of the Human tRNA m(1)A58 Methyltransferase-tRNA(3)(Lys) Complex: Refolding of Substrate tRNA Allows Access to the Methylation Target. The source of 5CCB, 5CCX and 5CD1 and the basis for every structural measurement here. The abstract states that "three structures of human tRNA m(1)A58 MTase in complex with human tRNA3(Lys) and the product S-adenosyl-L-homocysteine show a dimer of heterodimers in which each heterodimer comprises a catalytic chain, Trm61, and a homologous but noncatalytic chain, Trm6, repurposed as a tRNA-binding subunit that acts in trans", and that "tRNAs bind across the dimer interface such that Trm6 from the opposing heterodimer brings A58 into the active site of Trm61". The authors describe Trm6 as a tRNA-binding subunit in their own words, which is the wording a GO:0000049 annotation would rest on. The computation here adds the residue-level detail the abstract does not give and tests the remodelling question the title's phrase "refolding of substrate tRNA" invites. The title is a statement about the complex and is correct at that level; it is not a statement that TRMT6 performs the refolding, and the displacement analysis assigns that to TRMT61A.

PMID: 10779558, The Gcd10p/Gcd14p complex is the essential two-subunit tRNA(1-methyladenosine) methyltransferase of Saccharomyces cerevisiae. Independent biochemical support for the subunit split, fifteen years before the structures. "Purified Flag-tagged Gcd14p alone had no enzymatic activity and was severely impaired for tRNA-binding compared with the wild-type complex, suggesting that Gcd10p is required for tight binding of the tRNA substrate", and "Our results provide a demonstration of a two-component tRNA MTase and suggest that binding of AdoMet and tRNA substrates depends on different subunits of the complex". Both statements match what the distance measurements show. The limit is that this establishes Gcd10p as required for tight binding, not that it binds tRNA on its own, which is exactly the gap that leaves enables against contributes_to open.

PMID: 17932071, Conserved amino acids in each subunit of the heteroligomeric tRNA m1A58 Mtase from Saccharomyces cerevisiae contribute to tRNA binding. Mutant-phenotype evidence that the conservation measured here is functional: "amino acid substitutions in either Trm6p or Trm61p destroy the ability of the yeast m1A58 tRNA Mtase to bind tRNA(Met)i, indicating that each subunit contributes to tRNA binding". The verb "contributes" in the authors' own sentence is worth weighing for the qualifier decision.

PMID: 22168821, Structural comparison of tRNA m(1)A58 methyltransferases revealed different molecular strategies to maintain their oligomeric architecture under extreme conditions. Supports the archaeal comparison in three ways and limits it in one. "All TrmIs are assembled as tetramers formed by dimers of tightly assembled dimers" establishes the architecture the superposition shows is preserved. "the tetramer of the archaeal P. abyssi enzyme is strengthened by four intersubunit disulfide bridges" confirms 3MB5 is a genuine tetramer rather than a lattice artefact. "The monomers of the five enzymes are structurally highly similar, but the inter-monomer contacts differ strongly" justifies using one TrmI structure as a protomer-fold proxy while warning against residue-level inter-subunit claims, which is why none are made. The paper also states that "tRNA m(1)A58 methyltransferases (TrmI) catalyze the transfer of a methyl group from S-adenosyl-L-methionine to nitrogen 1 of adenine 58 in the T-loop of tRNAs from all three domains of life", locating the target in the T-loop where the two footprints overlap.

PMID: 14739239, A primordial RNA modification enzyme: the case of tRNA (m1A) methyltransferase. Orientation for the evolutionary argument. It states that "in yeast, the formation of m(1)A58 is catalysed by the essential tRNA (m(1)A58) methyltransferase (MTase), a tetrameric enzyme that is composed of two types of subunits (Gcd14p and Gcd10p), whereas in the bacterium Thermus thermophilus the enzyme is a homotetramer of the TrmI polypeptide", and reports that the P. abyssi enzyme "is also a homotetramer". It also notes that the archaeal enzyme is region-specific, methylating positions 57 and 58, while the bacterial enzyme is site-specific. The homotetramer-to-heterotetramer transition is the event the superposition localises to a protomer slot.

PMID: 23097428, Trmt61B is a methyltransferase responsible for 1-methyladenosine at position 58 of human mitochondrial tRNAs. A boundary condition for the annotation. "Unlike the cytoplasmic tRNA m(1)A58 methyltransferase that consists of an α2β2 heterotetramer formed by Trmt61A and Trmt6, Trmt61B formed a homo-oligomer (presumably a homotetramer) that resembled the bacterial homotetrameric m(1)A58 methyltransferase." Mitochondrial m1A58 does not involve TRMT6, so mitochondrial localisation or process terms must not transfer to this gene.

PMID: 29072297, The m1A landscape on cytosolic and mitochondrial mRNA at single-base resolution. The one piece of competing evidence on substrate scope. "Within the cytosol, m1A is present in a low number of mRNAs, typically at low stoichiometries, and almost invariably in tRNA T-loop-like structures, where it is introduced by the TRMT6/TRMT61A complex." The phrase "tRNA T-loop-like structures" matters here: the mRNA substrates are recognised through the same T-loop fold that the contact map shows TRMT6 gripping, so the finding extends the complex's substrate range without changing what the binding surface is built to recognise. It is also the basis for the GO:0006397 mRNA processing annotation currently on TRMT6.

PMID: 24894648, Crystal structure of tRNA m(1)A58 methyltransferase TrmI from Aquifex aeolicus in complex with S-adenosyl-L-methionine. Orientation on the cofactor site in the bacterial homolog. It reports four molecules per asymmetric unit forming a tetramer and compares AdoMet binding across A. aeolicus, T. thermophilus and P. abyssi, noting that "although the binding modes to the N6 amino group of the adenine moiety of AdoMet are similar, using the side chains of acidic residues as well as hydrogen bonds, the positions of the amino acid residues involved in binding are diverse". The relevance here is that every TrmI protomer has an AdoMet site, while TRMT6 has lost both the site and the signature.

Conflicts and alternatives

The source paper's title invites the refuted reading. PMID: 26470919 is titled "Refolding of Substrate tRNA Allows Access to the Methylation Target", and it describes Trm6 as the subunit that "brings A58 into the active site". A reasonable reader can arrive at the seed hypothesis's remodelling proposal from that language. The displacement measurement separates the two claims: the complex refolds the tRNA, and the subunit whose contacts coincide with the refolded region is TRMT61A.

A single dominant crystal form. 5CCB and 5CCX are the same crystal form at 2.0 and 2.1 Å. 5CD1 is independent but at 3.6 Å, where side-chain placement is less reliable. Contacts at a 4.0 Å criterion can be influenced by lattice packing. The mitigation applied is to use only the 37 residues reproducible in two or more of four independent TRMT6 copies, but this does not fully replace an independent solution method.

Cross-species reference tRNA. Free human tRNA3Lys has not been solved in a canonical conformation, so yeast tRNA-Phe (1EHZ) stands in. The 2.92 Å rigid-core RMSD is a real limit on interpretation, and the sequences differ at many of the positions scored.

UniProt spans against computed residues. The database resolves TRMT6 substrate binding as seven sequence ranges and the computation resolves it as 37 contacting residues. Roughly 21 agree. The 15 computed contacts outside the UniProt ranges and the non-contacting positions inside them are a resolution difference between a 3.6 Å and a 2.0 Å entry, not a contradiction, but a curator citing either should say which they mean.

Invariant-residue instability. Reporting "9 invariant contact residues including K145, Q434 and H441" would be read as established, and under a different gap model the number is 1. The enrichment reproduces; the individual positions do not.

Paralog and isoform checks. TRMT61B is a distinct gene product acting as a homo-oligomer in mitochondria (PMID: 23097428), so there is a real paralog-confusion risk for m1A58 annotations that this report keeps separate. No isoform-specific findings were identified for TRMT6.

Substrate scope. The mRNA m1A claim (PMID: 29072297, with 29107537 cited alongside it on the GO record) means the complex is not tRNA-exclusive. The reported mRNA sites sit in tRNA T-loop-like structures, which is consistent with a T-loop-reading surface, so this qualifies the scope without undermining GO:0000049.

Limitations and knowledge gaps

Does TRMT6 bind tRNA without TRMT61A? What was checked: the structural interface (1701 Å², 37 residues), its conservation, and the yeast biochemistry. PMID: 10779558 tests the converse subunit (Gcd14p alone, impaired) and PMID: 17932071 tests substitutions within the complex. Neither reports isolated TRMT6 binding tRNA. Why it matters: GO's enables for a binding term is cleanest when the polypeptide shows the activity. Resolution: an EMSA, filter-binding or ITC titration of recombinant TRMT6 alone against tRNA3Lys or tRNAiMet.

One crystal form carries most of the weight. Resolution: a cryo-EM structure of the holoenzyme with a different tRNA, or crosslinking mass spectrometry on the solution complex.

The remodelling reference is cross-species, with a 2.92 Å floor. The 13.4 Å TRMT61A median is far above it; the 3.6 Å TRMT6 median cannot be distinguished from zero real displacement, which is itself the result but also means no displacement below about 3 Å should be interpreted. Resolution: a structure of free human tRNA3Lys, or an NMR or SAXS measurement of the unbound substrate.

The binding order is unknown. A single end-state structure cannot show whether TRMT6 engages the canonical tRNA first and TRMT61A then opens the elbow, or whether both happen together. It does not change the tRNA binding term, but it bears on any future attempt to describe TRMT6 as positioning or presenting the substrate. Resolution: stopped-flow or time-resolved measurements, or structures of a catalytically dead TRMT61A mutant with tRNA.

No archaeal residue-level conservation is obtainable. All eight TrmI structures are protein-only, and TRMT6 shares no InterPro or Pfam signature with TrmI, so neither the structural nor the sequence route can answer the seed's archaeal question at residue level. The modelled TrmI A-4 placement clashes with the tRNA (36 atoms within 2.0 Å), so even the modelled footprint is unreliable. Resolution: a TrmI-tRNA co-structure from any archaeon or bacterium.

The invariant-residue count is gap-model dependent (1 against 9). Resolution: a curated multiple-sequence alignment with structure-aware gap placement across a wider ortholog set.

The 26470919 abstract was not retrieved in full programmatically. The snippets quoted from it were verified against stored abstract text, but the UniProt evidence attribution (ECO:0000269 PubMed:26470919) was read from the database record rather than from the paper. A curator should confirm the binding-site assignments against the paper and the deposited coordinates directly.

Tooling that could not be used. The RCSB pairwise structure alignment service (alignment.rcsb.org/api/v1/structures/submit) could not be reached: POST with a JSON body returned 400 "Required parameter 'query' is not present", GET with params returned 415 "Unsupported Media Type", and POST form-encoded plus GET with an explicit Content-Type both returned 400 schema-validation errors. phenix.superpose_pdbs ran but fitted only 3 atoms with its default matrix and raised an internal AssertionError (['R','K'] at superpose_pdbs.py line 733) with blosum50. Biopython is listed as available but is blocked by the executor import allowlist. The superposition was therefore done with a hand-implemented Needleman-Wunsch plus iterative Kabsch, and its fit statistics (255 aligned pairs, 30.6% identity, 226 CA core, 1.92 Å) are reported alongside every result that depends on it so the quality of the fit can be judged.

Discriminating tests and proposed follow-up

1. Isolated TRMT6 tRNA binding. Express recombinant human TRMT6 without TRMT61A and measure binding to tRNA3Lys or tRNAiMet by EMSA, filter binding or ITC. This is the highest-value single experiment on the list because it is the only one that decides enables against contributes_to. Expected under the structural model: measurable but weaker binding than the holoenzyme, since TRMT6 supplies the larger surface but TRMT61A contributes the acceptor-end and D-loop grip. A negative result would make contributes_to correct and final.

2. Targeted mutagenesis of the T-arm clamp. Mutate the TRMT6 residues contacting the T-stem phosphate backbone (K49, Q104, Y146, K150, K153) to alanine, separately from the residues holding the nucleotide 58 phosphate (Q434, H441), and separately from TRMT61A-interface residues. Measure complex assembly, tRNA binding affinity and methylation rate for each class. This distinguishes a binding defect from an assembly defect, which the yeast mutant work (PMID: 17932071) could not fully separate. It also tests whether the conservation enrichment is functional at specific positions, independent of the alignment gap-model problem.

3. Structure of free human tRNA3Lys. Solve, or determine by SAXS, the unbound conformation of the exact substrate used in 5CCB. This removes the cross-species reference problem and would let the displacement analysis run with a sub-ångström floor instead of 2.92 Å, turning the 3.6 Å TRMT6-exclusive median into a quantitative statement rather than a baseline comparison.

4. Trapped-intermediate structures. Use a catalytically inactivated TRMT61A (an active-site acidic-residue mutant) in complex with tRNA to see whether the elbow is already splayed when catalysis cannot proceed, and whether TRMT6 engagement precedes it. This addresses the binding-order gap and tests whether elbow opening requires an intact TRMT61A active site.

5. A TrmI-tRNA co-structure. Any archaeal or bacterial TrmI solved with tRNA bound would convert the architectural correspondence reported here into a residue-level comparison, and would show whether the ancestral enzyme used a T-arm clamp at all or whether that is a eukaryotic invention. This is the only route to the part of the seed hypothesis that currently cannot be answered.

6. Substrate-scope test against the T-loop-like mRNA sites. PMID: 29072297 reports that the cytosolic mRNA m1A sites sit "almost invariably in tRNA T-loop-like structures". Testing TRMT6 binding to those mRNA fragments, with and without TRMT61A, would show whether the TRMT6 surface is what reads the T-loop fold, and would clarify whether GO:0003723 RNA binding on this gene reflects a genuine second substrate class or capture of the complex through its tRNA machinery.
