| **Ca²⁺-regulated acidic-phospholipid binding and membrane organization** — the annexin core has four approximately 70-residue, five-helix repeats; Ca²⁺ bridges type-II sites to phosphatidylserine, phosphatidic acid and phosphoinositides. [Gerke et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-024-45954-0) | Cytosol to Ca²⁺-dependent peripheral association with acidic membrane surfaces; the distinct N-terminus supplies ANXA1-specific interactions. | Structural synthesis across the annexin family defines the conserved disc-shaped core and Ca²⁺-dependent membrane-facing surface. (pqac-00000025, pqac-00000027) | Authoritative 2024 review integrating structural and biochemical evidence. This is **binding and scaffolding**, not enzymatic catalysis; individual annexins differ in Ca²⁺ sensitivity and membrane functions. |
| **Regulatory inhibition of cytosolic phospholipase A₂ (cPLA₂)** — ANXA1 limits phospholipid cleavage and arachidonate release but does not catalyze a reaction itself. [Solito et al., 1998](https://doi.org/10.1038/sj.bjp.0701991) | Intracellular, at the cPLA₂–phospholipid membrane interface in human U937 monocytic cells. | Stable ANXA1 antisense depletion increased PMA-induced radiolabeled arachidonate release from approximately **300% to 800%** of unstimulated levels. cPLA₂ and secretory PLA₂ abundance and cPLA₂ phosphorylation were unchanged; enhanced activity was preferentially sensitive to a cPLA₂ inhibitor. (pqac-00000031, pqac-00000033) | Direct human-cell perturbation supporting **negative regulation of cPLA₂ activity**. It does not make ANXA1 an enzyme, and older literature differs on direct protein interaction versus competition at phospholipid interfaces. |
| **Extracellular pro-resolving FPR signaling and efferocytosis** — full-length ANXA1 preferentially signals through FPR2/ALX, whereas the N-terminal mimetic **Ac2-26** can engage FPR1 and FPR2/ALX. [Scannell et al., 2007](https://doi.org/10.4049/jimmunol.178.7.4595); [Dalli et al., 2012](https://doi.org/10.3389/fphar.2012.00028) | Cell surface and extracellular milieu after non-classical externalization or release from apoptotic cells; the extreme N-terminus supplies receptor-interacting determinants. | ANXA1 was detected by mass spectrometry and immunoblot in apoptotic-cell supernatants; immunodepletion abolished their macrophage-prophagocytic activity, and an FPR-pathway antagonist blocked it. In primary human leukocytes, Ac2-26 caused ERK-dependent chemokinesis that either FPR1 or FPR2 could sustain. (pqac-00000002, pqac-00000024, pqac-00000030) | Strong cellular evidence for extracellular signaling and apoptotic-cell clearance. Receptor pharmacology depends on molecular form: findings for Ac2-26 should not automatically be assigned to intact ANXA1. |
| **Extracellular-vesicle ANXA1 suppresses epithelial NF-κB through FPR2**. [Fujita et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s42003-024-06197-3) | ANXA1 cargo of human bronchial epithelial-cell extracellular vesicles; recipient bronchial and type-II alveolar epithelial cells expressing FPR2. | Proteomics and immunoblotting identified EV ANXA1; recombinant ANXA1 reduced phosphorylated NF-κB, while the FPR2 antagonist WRW4 reversed EV-mediated NF-κB suppression in human epithelial cells. Intratracheal EVs reduced inflammatory cells, cytokines and injury in mouse acute-lung-injury models. (pqac-00000018, pqac-00000021, pqac-00000022) | Human-cell mechanism plus mouse efficacy, not human treatment evidence. EVs also contained multiple TLR–NF-κB-regulating miRNAs, so the total EV effect cannot be attributed exclusively to ANXA1. |
| **Intracellular anti-adipogenic adaptor function through PDLIM7–MYCBP2–SMAD4–PPARγ**. [Fang et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41392-024-01930-0) | Stromal vascular and adipose progenitor-cell cytoplasm; ANXA1 binds PDLIM7, altering access of the E3 ligase MYCBP2 to SMAD4. | ANXA1–PDLIM7 binding promoted MYCBP2-dependent K48-linked SMAD4 ubiquitination and proteasomal loss, reducing SMAD4 occupancy at the *Pparg* promoter and adipogenesis. Global or adipose ANXA1 loss worsened high-fat-diet phenotypes in mice; human adipose observations associated ANXA1 with obesity and cell composition. (pqac-00000010, pqac-00000011, pqac-00000012, pqac-00000016) | Detailed molecular and genetic evidence is predominantly cellular or murine; human tissue data are observational. Ac2-26 reproduced parts of the pathway preclinically, but this is not proof of efficacy in people. |
| **Disease-associated failure of ANXA1 redistribution and externalization in CKD neutrophils**. [Scharf et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.3389/fimmu.2024.1387566) | Intracellular, plasma-membrane-associated and secreted pools in circulating human neutrophils. | In **36 CKD patients versus 11 controls**, CKD neutrophils showed increased intracellular but decreased membrane-associated ANXA1; secretion after 18 hours was similar despite increased apoptosis. FPR2 was increased, and ANXA1-positive neutrophils were observed in five CKD kidney biopsies. (pqac-00000038, pqac-00000049, pqac-00000050) | Direct ex-vivo human evidence supporting compartment-specific dysregulation, not simple deficiency. Assay-specific sample sizes varied, the design was observational, and it does not establish causality or therapeutic benefit. |
| **Experimental blockade of extracellular or tumor-associated ANXA1 with MDX-124**, a humanized IgG1 antibody. [Al-Ali et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41388-023-02919-9) | Activated Ca²⁺-bound ANXA1; the antibody binds a discontinuous domain-III epitope and is proposed to disrupt ANXA1–FPR1/2 signaling. | MDX-124 caused dose-dependent G1 arrest in ANXA1-expressing human cancer-cell lines and produced **23% tumor-growth inhibition by day 15** in a 4T1-luc mouse model; efficacy was also reported in a Pan02 mouse model. (pqac-00000044, pqac-00000045, pqac-00000046) | Preclinical proof of concept only. Direct FPR ligand-displacement or receptor-occupancy evidence was not reported in the cited experiments, and these findings do not establish an approved therapy or human interventional efficacy. |
