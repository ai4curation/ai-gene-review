# Human ANXA1 (UniProt P04083): functional annotation and research report

## Identity and principal function

**Identity verified.** The supplied UniProt accession **P04083** identifies human **ANXA1**, encoding annexin A1, also called annexin I or lipocortin I. The literature agrees with this identity: annexin A1 is a 346-amino-acid annexin-family protein with a distinct N-terminal region and a conserved core of four homologous repeats. This report does **not** conflate it with annexin A2, A5, A6, or similarly named proteins from other organisms. (han2020annexina1involved pages 1-2, gerke2024annexins—afamilyof pages 1-2, kelly2022annexin‐a1theculprit pages 1-2)

**Best-supported primary molecular annotation:** ANXA1 is a **Ca²⁺-regulated phospholipid-binding, membrane-associated regulatory protein**, not a phospholipase or another catalytic enzyme. Its conserved core binds negatively charged membrane lipids when Ca²⁺ rises; its distinctive N terminus mediates protein interactions and, after externalization, formyl-peptide-receptor signaling. Intracellularly it can restrain cytosolic phospholipase A₂ (cPLA₂) activity. Extracellularly, full-length ANXA1 principally acts as a pro-resolving signal through **FPR2/ALX**, modulating leukocyte trafficking and macrophage clearance of dying cells. These are complementary, compartment-dependent functions rather than a single enzymatic reaction. (gerke2024annexins—afamilyof pages 1-2, gerke2024annexins—afamilyof pages 4-5, gerke2024annexins—afamilyof pages 8-9, solito1998u937cellsdeprived pages 3-4, scannell2007annexin1andpeptide pages 5-5)

## Molecular mechanism and biological processes

**Membrane binding and organization.** The annexin core comprises four approximately 70-residue, five-helix repeats arranged in a disc-shaped structure. Ca²⁺ coordinates between the membrane-facing core and acidic lipids, including phosphatidylserine, phosphatidic acid and phosphoinositides. This architecture explains reversible recruitment from the cytosol to membranes, but shared annexin architecture alone does not establish that ANXA1 performs every membrane-repair function attributed to other annexins. ANXA1’s N-terminal amphipathic region also binds S100A11; the complex has been implicated in cell-cortex organization and endosomal membrane contacts. (gerke2024annexins—afamilyof pages 1-2, gerke2024annexins—afamilyof pages 4-5, gerke2024annexins—afamilyof pages 5-6)

**Regulation of inflammatory lipid production—not phospholipid hydrolysis by ANXA1.** Phospholipase A₂ releases arachidonic acid from membrane phospholipids; ANXA1 acts as an *inhibitor/regulator* of this process, rather than catalyzing it. In human U937 monocytic cells, reducing endogenous annexin A1 increased PMA-stimulated radiolabeled arachidonate release from approximately **300% to 800% of unstimulated release** in the respective control and ANXA1-depleted clones. The increased activity was preferentially sensitive to a cPLA₂ inhibitor, whereas cPLA₂ protein abundance and phosphorylation did not increase. Biochemical accounts describe possible direct ANXA1–cPLA₂ interaction as well as interference at membrane substrates; the relative contribution of these mechanisms should not be treated as settled in every cell type. (solito1998u937cellsdeprived pages 3-4, solito1998u937cellsdeprived pages 1-2, han2020annexina1involved pages 1-2, walther2000anovelligand pages 1-2)

**Extracellular FPR signaling and resolution.** Upon inflammatory activation or cell death, ANXA1 can appear at the cell surface or outside the cell, where its N terminus participates in formyl peptide receptor signaling. An instructive human-cell experiment identified annexin A1 in apoptotic-neutrophil conditioned medium by mass spectrometry and immunoblotting: removing annexin A1 abolished the medium’s stimulation of macrophage phagocytosis of apoptotic neutrophils, and an FPR-pathway antagonist blocked the response. This provides causal evidence for an extracellular role in **efferocytosis**, not merely an association with dying cells. ANXA1 signaling also limits leukocyte recruitment and extravasation. (scannell2007annexin1andpeptide pages 5-5, han2020annexina1involved pages 3-4, gerke2024annexins—afamilyof pages 8-9)

**Distinguish intact protein from peptide mimetics.** Full-length extracellular ANXA1 is commonly associated with FPR2/ALX signaling, whereas the N-terminal peptide **Ac2-26** can engage **both FPR1 and FPR2/ALX**. In primary human leukocyte assays, Ac2-26 induced predominantly ERK-dependent *chemokinesis*—increased locomotion, not simply movement up a gradient. Consequently, an Ac2-26 response cannot automatically be assigned to intact ANXA1 or described as exclusively FPR2-dependent. FPR2 also recognizes other ligands and can produce context-dependent responses; “FPR2 activation” alone does not guarantee a uniformly anti-inflammatory outcome. (dalli2012annexina1nterminal pages 1-2, gerke2024annexins—afamilyof pages 8-9, scharf2024gpcrsoverexpressionand pages 7-10)

**Additional defined intracellular pathway.** A 2024 mechanistic study found that ANXA1 binds **PDLIM7**, competing with the E3 ligase **MYCBP2** for that partner. The resulting availability of MYCBP2 favors **K48-linked ubiquitination and proteasomal degradation of SMAD4**, reducing SMAD4-dependent *PPARγ* transcription and adipogenic differentiation. Perturbation and interaction studies support this pathway in cellular and mouse systems; elevated ANXA1 in some human obese adipose samples is an association, not evidence that the same pathway has been therapeutically validated in people. Importantly, this PDLIM7 pathway is a specific intracellular adaptor-like role, distinct from extracellular FPR signaling. (fang2024annexina1binds pages 12-13, fang2024annexina1binds pages 10-12, fang2024annexina1binds pages 8-10, fang2024annexina1binds pages 2-3)

## Where ANXA1 acts

ANXA1 is primarily **cytosolic** but functionally redistributes: Ca²⁺ promotes peripheral association with the **plasma membrane** and intracellular membranes; neutrophils contain an intracellular granular pool, and membrane-associated ANXA1 can subsequently be **externalized**. Outside cells, ANXA1 has been detected as released protein and in **extracellular vesicles**, placing it near receptors on leukocytes and epithelial cells. The 2024 annexin-family review describes serine-27 phosphorylation as important for ANXA1 secretion but emphasizes that its route across membranes is not fully resolved; degranulation, vesicles and transient membrane injury have been implicated, so it should not be annotated as an ordinary signal-peptide-secreted protein. (han2020annexina1involved pages 2-3, gerke2024annexins—afamilyof pages 8-9, gerke2024annexins—afamilyof pages 6-8, scannell2007annexin1andpeptide pages 5-5)

Localization is biologically consequential. In a 2024 human study of **36 people with nondialysis chronic kidney disease (CKD) and 11 controls**, circulating CKD neutrophils contained **more intracellular ANXA1 but less membrane-associated ANXA1**; ANXA1 released after 18 hours was similar between groups despite increased intracellular staining. FPR2 expression was higher, and ANXA1-positive neutrophils were seen in five CKD kidney biopsies. Thus, measuring total ANXA1 alone can obscure an altered, functionally relevant surface pool. The study is observational and does not establish that redistributing ANXA1 would improve CKD. The cropped **Figure 4** shows the separate receptor, intracellular, surface and secretion measurements; assay-specific sample sizes vary. (scharf2024gpcrsoverexpressionand pages 3-4, scharf2024gpcrsoverexpressionand pages 7-10, scharf2024gpcrsoverexpressionand media 57c819f3)

ANXA1 also reaches injured membranes. A 2024 expert review describes recruitment of ANXA1 to focal plasma-membrane wounds and an association with dysferlin in muscle; proposed membrane-fusion or organization roles differ from the more directly characterized wound-closure roles of certain other annexins. Notably, ANXA1 was described as dispensable for *immediate* myofiber membrane resealing while contributing to subsequent muscle regeneration. This evidence supports a context-specific membrane role, **not** assignment of universal ESCRT-mediated repair to ANXA1. (gerke2024annexins—afamilyof pages 6-8, gerke2024annexins—afamilyof pages 5-6)

## Developments in 2023–2024 and applications

**Lung epithelial extracellular vesicles.** Fujita and colleagues identified ANXA1 in extracellular vesicles from **human bronchial epithelial cells**. Recombinant ANXA1 reduced phosphorylated NF-κB in inflamed epithelial cells, and an FPR2 antagonist reversed vesicle-associated NF-κB suppression in bronchial and primary human type-II alveolar epithelial cells. Intratracheal administration of the vesicles reduced inflammatory-cell infiltration, bronchoalveolar IL-6 and TNF-α, and injury in induced **mouse** acute-lung-injury models. However, the vesicles also carried inflammation-modulating microRNAs; the entire vesicle benefit cannot be assigned to ANXA1, and this is not evidence of efficacy in human patients. Fujita et al., *Communications Biology*, **May 2024**, https://doi.org/10.1038/s42003-024-06197-3. (fujita2024mitigationofacute pages 7-9, fujita2024mitigationofacutea pages 1-3, fujita2024mitigationofacute pages 1-2)

**Metabolic targeting.** In the PDLIM7–MYCBP2–SMAD4 study, whole-body or adipose-specific *Anxa1* loss worsened high-fat-diet metabolic phenotypes in mice. The ANXA1-derived peptide Ac2-26 reproduced aspects of the anti-adipogenic pathway. A necessary qualification is that intraperitoneal Ac2-26 at **0.5–2 mg/kg every other day for 10 weeks** in high-fat-fed *db/db* mice did **not** significantly lower body weight or improve glucose or insulin tolerance versus controls; **2 mg/kg** did reduce blood glucose, total cholesterol and triglycerides. These results support experimental metabolic target discovery, not a demonstrated human weight-loss therapy. Fang et al., *Signal Transduction and Targeted Therapy*, **August 2024**, https://doi.org/10.1038/s41392-024-01930-0. (fang2024annexina1binds pages 1-2, fang2024annexina1binds pages 12-13)

**Cancer: an important opposite therapeutic direction.** ANXA1 activity need not be beneficial in tumors. A **2023** study found ANXA1 in human melanoma biopsies and reported that mouse neutrophil-conditioned medium increased melanoma-cell invasion; FPR antagonism or ANXA1-deficient neutrophils abolished that effect. Separately, the humanized anti-ANXA1 antibody **MDX-124** inhibited proliferation of ANXA1-expressing human cancer-cell lines and suppressed tumor growth in mouse breast- and pancreatic-cancer models. In one breast-cancer mouse experiment, reported tumor-growth inhibition was **23% at day 15**; the study reported dose-dependent cell-line effects (*p* < 0.013) and significant mouse tumor effects (*p* < 0.0001). The proposed interruption of ANXA1–FPR signaling should not be mistaken for a demonstrated receptor-occupancy or clinical-efficacy result. Sandri et al., *Cells*, **January 2023**, https://doi.org/10.3390/cells12030425; Al-Ali et al., *Oncogene*, **January 2024**, https://doi.org/10.1038/s41388-023-02919-9. (sandri2023roleofannexin pages 1-2, alali2024atherapeuticantibody pages 1-2, alali2024atherapeuticantibody pages 2-3, alali2024atherapeuticantibody pages 3-5)

**Biomarker investigation.** In a human periodontal study with **25 cases and 25 healthy controls**, salivary ANXA1 was lower in periodontitis (*p* = 0.0177), as was serum ANXA1 (*p* < 0.0001); ANXA1/ANXA2 ratios were also lower in saliva, serum and gingival crevicular fluid. This demonstrates measurable human extracellular ANXA1 and a disease association, **not** a validated diagnostic test or a causal role in periodontitis. Yilmaz et al., *Scientific Reports*, **November 2024**, https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-024-80418-x. (yilmaz2024annexinlevelsin pages 2-3, yilmaz2024annexinlevelsin pages 1-2)

## Interpretation and evidence boundaries

The most defensible functional annotation is **“Ca²⁺-dependent acidic-phospholipid-binding membrane organizer and context-dependent signaling regulator; extracellular ANXA1 promotes inflammation resolution principally through FPR2/ALX, while intracellular ANXA1 can inhibit cPLA₂-associated arachidonate release and participate in specific adaptor complexes.”** This formulation reflects the convergence of structural analysis, human-cell perturbations, apoptotic-cell immunodepletion and recent mechanistic studies. ANXA1 has **no established substrate-specific catalytic reaction**: arachidonate release is catalyzed by PLA₂, not ANXA1. Its functional location must be stated with the mechanism—cytosol/membrane for lipid and adaptor functions, versus cell surface/extracellular space for receptor signaling. (gerke2024annexins—afamilyof pages 1-2, gerke2024annexins—afamilyof pages 4-5, solito1998u937cellsdeprived pages 3-4, scannell2007annexin1andpeptide pages 5-5, fang2024annexina1binds pages 12-13, gerke2024annexins—afamilyof pages 8-9)

Finally, anti-inflammatory activation, peptide administration and anti-ANXA1 tumor blockade are **different, potentially opposing interventions**. Their apparent discrepancy reflects ligand form, receptor usage, cellular compartment and disease setting rather than uncertainty about which human protein is being studied. The strongest recent treatment results cited here remain **cellular or animal experiments**; human CKD and periodontal findings are observational, and the reviewed studies do not establish an approved ANXA1-directed treatment. (dalli2012annexina1nterminal pages 1-2, fujita2024mitigationofacute pages 7-9, alali2024atherapeuticantibody pages 1-2, scharf2024gpcrsoverexpressionand pages 3-4, yilmaz2024annexinlevelsin pages 1-2)
