## Ontology and decision boundaries

Current official QuickGO definitions were retrieved for every source term and the chromatin/nucleoplasm alternatives. Chromatin is the organized DNA–protein material forming a chromosome; the actual human chromatin recruitment experiments support refinement of chromosome to chromatin. Nucleoplasm excludes chromosomes and the nucleolus, so chromatin association alone is not used to assert that finer location. A separate whole-gene source can support that refinement.

CSA brings ligase components and substrate-associated repair factors together. This satisfies adaptor activity without attributing CSB motor chemistry or RBX/E2 ubiquitin transfer to CSA. The 2024 DNA–protein cross-link study contains a CSA-specific UV control and explicitly distinguishes its cross-link pathway from TC-NER. No new cross-link-process assertion is introduced.

## Whole-gene specificity reconciliation

Nucleus annotations are refined using the diffuse nucleoplasmic staining observed after UV and Triton extraction in PMID:11782547 and the exact human Reactome participant. The chromatin experiments alone are not used to infer nucleoplasm. Broad Cul4-complex annotations are refined to CUL4A using the defined human CSA/DDB1/CUL4A preparation in PMID:34526721. ## Core activity and unresolved mechanisms

The core represents CSA's own substrate-adaptor step in the CUL4A–DDB1–RBX1 ligase, supporting protein ubiquitination and transcription-coupled nucleotide-excision repair. The two process terms describe distinct supported outputs; they do not assign CSA the CSB motor or RING/E2 ubiquitin-transfer chemistry. The 396-residue full-length human CSA evidence does not establish equivalent activity of the 205-residue product.

[PMID:32355176](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-020-15903-8), Results Figures 1h and 3–6 and chromatin-IP/XR-seq Methods, establishes CSA-dependent DDB1/CRL4, UVSSA and TFIIH recruitment in human U2OS cells, with loss of transcribed-strand excision bias. The CSA-KO XR-seq comparison was performed once. Its purified CRL4CSA experiment uses Xenopus proteins, separate from the human cellular evidence.

[PMID:34526721](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-021-03906-4), Figures 1/3 and cloning, purification and ubiquitylation Methods, directly reconstitutes substrate modification with full-length human CSA and other human repair factors, mouse RBX1 and pig-thymus Pol II. The E2–ubiquitin geometry is modeled. [PMID:38316879](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41594-023-01207-0), Figure 4 and corresponding cellular/reconstitution Methods, tests the human CSA Y334A interface and ELOF1/UVSSA stabilization. Trabectedin gamma-H2AX is an incision proxy. Its E2 C85K–Ub preparation yields a dissociated ligase subcomplex, and positioning on complete Pol II is inferred by superposition. Neither observation alone proves CSA performs an adaptor step in its own ubiquitination.

Nucleoplasm is supported by [PMID:11782547](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.012473199), tagged-human construct/immunofluorescence Methods and printed pages 203–204: diffuse nucleoplasmic signal and foci occur after UV plus Triton extraction, while unextracted uniform nuclear signal is separately reported as data not shown. The main text reports complementation referring to unread supplementary Figure 6. No figure-pixel or separate supplementary-data inspection is claimed for this core synthesis.

The proposed end-joining experiment follows the distinction in [PMID:29545921](https://doi.org/10.18632/oncotarget.24342), Figures 3–5, Discussion and neutral-comet Methods, between persistent signaling and physical break resolution. The 3-Gy signaling and 10-Gy physical assays differ; unread supplementary material and those assay limits preclude a universal negative conclusion. CUL4B membership, self-ubiquitination and classical end joining remain unresolved contexts, and no additional core or annotation is introduced for them.
