# DST evidence notes

## Biological synthesis

Three coupled mechanisms distinguish the evidence: structural cytolinking at cellular attachment sites, microtubule binding/regulation, and neuronal association with dynactin-dependent retrograde transport. Human epithelial BP230 provides intracellular contacts with integrin beta4 and collagen XVII. In keratinocytes its depletion disrupts actin organization, polarity and directional persistence, without a significant change in average migration speed. These observations do not establish intrinsic exchange-factor activity. Muscle Z-disc evidence is positive but its complete original remains unavailable. [PMID:10637308](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10637308/), [PMID:11375975](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11375975/), [PMID:19403692](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19403692/), [PMID:19932097](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19932097/).

The BPAG1n4 tail experiments demonstrate calcium-sensitive EB1/plus-end versus lattice behavior. They do not establish identical regulation of every natural product, a direct calcium-affinity value, or a statistically significant calcium-dependent increase in microtubule cosedimentation. The neuronal transport experiments distinguish human-derived cDNA/library constructs from native mouse localization and transport assays. DST supplies a linker/adaptor role; dynein supplies motor force. [PMID:22995871](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22995871/), [PMID:14581450](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14581450/).

## Locations and interactions

The sperm-nuclear source is positive contextual detection: the author-thesis table lists Q03001 with 1.4% coverage and three peptides after human sperm-nucleus preparation. It does not resolve an isoform or a nuclear mechanism. Mouse neuronal ER/nuclear-envelope and N-terminal stress-fiber/nuclear evidence are retained as distinct non-core pools. The H-zone assignment remains unresolved because the accessible source does not settle that narrower compartment; positive Z-disc evidence does not exclude it. [PMID:21630459](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21630459/), [PMID:18638474](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18638474/), [PMID:14576348](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14576348/).

Supported partner-class refinements identify EB1/plectin as cytoskeletal partners, collagen XVII as a collagen partner through its cytoplasmic domain, and dengue NS5 as an RNA-polymerase partner in a fragment screen. None assigns the partner’s downstream function to DST. ERBIN, DISC1 and APPL1 associations remain positive non-core where no warranted narrower binding class was established. Co-complex recovery is distinguished from a purified binary interface. The explicit [CLINGEN project policy](../../../projects/CLINGEN_MENDELIAN.md) retains such supported generic binding, rather than removing it solely for low information content.

## Follow-up evidence clarification (2026-10-18)

The neuronal mechanism remains cytoskeletal adaptor activity (GO:0008093), the current official preferred label. Dynactin binding (GO:0034452) describes a complementary contact rather than replacing that transport-linking role. The new binding assertion uses human brain-derived interaction constructs, with native mouse p150/p50/dynein-complex association as corroboration. The human p150 partner is DCTN1/Q14203. The original Results and Figures 2–5 distinguish interaction mapping, native complexes and transport; the cloning Methods identify human cDNA and library provenance. Neither lysate overlay nor co-immunoprecipitation is represented as purified-complex reconstitution, and no complete current natural DST product is assigned. Existing broad cytoskeletal-partner refinements concern EB1/plectin; the added dynactin claim records a different partner and source context. [PMID:14581450](https://doi.org/10.1083/jcb.200306075), [GO:0008093](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/QuickGO/term/GO:0008093), [GO:0034452](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/QuickGO/term/GO:0034452).

The dengue NS5 refinement is retained on the identified partner class and controlled fragment interaction evidence. Four curated records include sufficient-binding-region features extending through NS5 residue 835; they are not all short N-terminal peptides. The original viral-library construction, interaction-screening and pairwise-retest Methods describe sequence checking, independent reporter selection and exclusion of promiscuous activators. These controls do not establish a DST-specific infection mechanism or an orthogonal cellular validation. The normal abstract-only cache is unchanged; the separately recovered original and the literal interaction records provide the additional scope. Reference-sequence checksums and sufficient-binding-region features remain distinct from complete clone sequence proof. [PMID:21911577](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3237087/).

The epithelial migration term is refined to keratinocyte migration (GO:0051546), both in the existing IMP review and the integrated structural core. The human keratinocyte Methods and Figure 6 Results report impaired persistence/turning after BPAG1e depletion, unchanged average speed and Rac1/cofilin bypass. This specifies the tested cell type without turning a migration phenotype into intrinsic motor or exchange-factor activity. Polarity remains separately represented, and the integrin/collagen contacts remain part of the same structural mechanism. [PMID:19403692](https://doi.org/10.1091/mbc.e09-01-0051), [GO:0051546](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/QuickGO/term/GO:0051546).

The 68 imported source dictionaries, nine official product records, historical notes and three existing core quotations are preserved. The sole appended annotation is the dynactin-binding MF; no new process assertion is added. The public parser has been reformatted for readability without changing its syntax tree, inputs, interface or reproduced results.
