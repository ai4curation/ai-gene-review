SCGB3A1 (HIN-1 / UGRP2): Functional Annotation Report

Gene: SCGB3A1 (synonyms: HIN1, PNSP2, UGRP2; ORF names UNQ629/PRO1245) Protein: Secretoglobin family 3A member 1 (Cytokine HIN-1 / High in Normal 1 / Pneumo secretory protein 2 / Uteroglobin-related protein 2) UniProt: Q96QR1 · NCBI Gene ID: 92304 · Ensembl: ENSG00000161055 Organism: Homo sapiens (Human) Family: Secretoglobin superfamily, UGRP (SCGB3A) subfamily Domains: Secretoglobin_3A (IPR040301); SCGB3A (PF20490)

Summary

SCGB3A1 encodes a small, secreted, disulfide-linked homodimeric secretoglobin that acts not as an enzyme or transporter, but as an extracellular, cytokine-like growth-inhibitory ligand. The gene symbol and the UniProt identity (Q96QR1) are concordant: all authoritative literature for "HIN-1," "SCGB3A1," and "UGRP2" describes the same human secretoglobin-superfamily protein originally discovered as a breast-epithelial tumor-suppressor cytokine. There is no evidence of gene-symbol ambiguity across organisms; the protein is well-characterized in human and mouse airway and glandular epithelium.

The protein is produced by terminally differentiated luminal and airway secretory epithelial cells — mammary luminal epithelium, large-airway bronchial epithelium, and submucosal gland cells — and is secreted apically into the lumen and body fluids, where it is recoverable in bronchial lavage, saliva, plasma, and serum. Its site of action is therefore extracellular. Functionally, HIN-1 binds a high-affinity, still-unidentified receptor on the surface of epithelial cells and suppresses mitogen-induced PI3K/AKT signaling. Through AKT inhibition it blocks anchorage-dependent and anchorage-independent growth, migration, and invasion; it inhibits Rb phosphorylation and cell-cycle re-entry; and it induces apoptosis in proliferating cells. Constitutively active AKT abolishes these effects, placing AKT downstream of HIN-1 as the critical mechanistic node.

Consistent with a tumor-suppressor role, SCGB3A1 expression is lost early in the development of breast, lung, prostate, esophageal, and multiple other epithelial cancers, predominantly through CpG-island promoter hypermethylation rather than mutation. Methylation is essentially absent in normal tissue, tightly correlated with loss of expression, and reversible by demethylating agents. At the structural level, SCGB3A1 shares the secreted, dimeric uteroglobin fold with a central hydrophobic cavity, which in family prototypes binds and sequesters hydrophobic ligands and confers anti-inflammatory activity — a capacity that is structurally plausible for SCGB3A1 but has not been directly demonstrated.

Key Findings

Finding 1 — SCGB3A1/HIN-1 is a small secreted secretoglobin acting as an epithelial tumor-suppressor cytokine

HIN-1 ("High in Normal 1") was originally identified by serial analysis of gene expression (SAGE) comparing normal versus ductal carcinoma in situ (DCIS) mammary epithelium. It emerged as one of the most strongly down-regulated transcripts in cancer: its expression is lost in approximately 94% of breast carcinomas and 95% of pre-invasive lesions (DCIS and LCIS). Re-introduction of HIN-1 into breast cancer cells inhibits their growth, establishing the first functional evidence of a tumor-suppressor role. The original report explicitly described HIN-1 as "a putative cytokine with no significant homology to known proteins" whose reintroduction "inhibits cell growth" (PMID: 11481438).

HIN-1 belongs to the secretoglobin/uteroglobin superfamily, whose members are "small, secretory, rarely glycosylated dimeric proteins with unclear physiological functions and are mainly expressed in mucosal tissues" (PMID: 11193762). This family assignment is the single most important structural clue to its function: it predicts a secreted, dimeric protein with a hydrophobic internal cavity rather than an enzyme or transporter.

Finding 2 — HIN-1/SCGB3A1 inhibits AKT activation to suppress growth, migration, and invasion

The definitive mechanistic study (Krop et al., Cancer Res 2005) established HIN-1 as "a potent inhibitor of anchorage-dependent and anchorage-independent cell growth, cell migration, and invasion" (PMID: 16266985). The mechanism operates through the PI3K/AKT pathway: "mitogen-induced phosphorylation and activation of AKT are inhibited in HIN-1-expressing cells. In addition, expression of constitutively activate AKT abrogates HIN-1-mediated growth arrest" (PMID: 16266985). This epistasis experiment is decisive — it places AKT downstream of HIN-1 and identifies AKT suppression as the proximate cause of growth arrest.

Downstream consequences include inhibition of retinoblastoma protein (Rb) phosphorylation and blockade of cell-cycle re-entry in synchronized cells, together with induction of apoptosis in exponentially growing cells. Critically, ligand-binding studies "indicate the presence of high-affinity cell surface HIN-1 binding on epithelial cells" (PMID: 16266985), demonstrating that HIN-1 acts as a secreted ligand engaging a cell-surface receptor — consistent with its cytokine-like classification. The receptor itself remains unidentified.

Finding 3 — SCGB3A1 is secreted by airway/glandular epithelium and detectable in body fluids; silenced by promoter hypermethylation in cancer

HIN-1 protein is highly expressed in mammary gland, trachea, lung (bronchial epithelial cells), prostate, pancreas, and salivary gland, with secreted protein detectable in bronchial lavage, saliva, plasma, and serum — "high levels of HIN-1 protein are detected in bronchial lavage, saliva, plasma, and serum" (PMID: 15383627). In the lung its expression is restricted to terminally differentiated airway epithelial cells and is up-regulated during retinoic-acid-induced bronchial epithelial differentiation: "The expression of HIN-1 is restricted to terminally differentiated airway epithelial cells in vivo and in vitro implicating HIN-1 in the acquisition or maintenance of terminally differentiated epithelial phenotype" (PMID: 12175512).

Across more than 800 tumors of 17 types, promoter methylation (≈73% breast, ≈67% NSCLC, high in prostate, retinoblastoma, and Wilms tumor) correlated completely with loss of expression and was reversed by the demethylating agent 5-aza-2'-deoxycytidine: "methylation was completely correlated with loss of expression. Expression negative cell lines restored HIN-1 expression after treatment with 5-aza-2'-deoxycytidine" (PMID: 15472908). Methylation is rare (~5%) in non-malignant tissue, underscoring that epigenetic silencing — not mutation — is the principal inactivation mechanism and that it is an early event in malignant transformation.

Finding 4 — SCGB3A1 is a 104-aa secreted precursor forming a disulfide-linked homodimer with a secretoglobin fold

UniProt Q96QR1 describes a 104-residue precursor with an N-terminal signal peptide (residues 1–20) cleaved to release the mature secreted chain (residues 21–104). The mature protein contains a single cysteine (position 78) that forms an interchain disulfide bond, assembling a disulfide-linked homodimer. Subcellular location is annotated as Secreted (extracellular region, inferred from direct assay; extracellular exosome, high-throughput). Curated GO terms include cytokine activity (molecular function) and negative regulation of cell growth (biological process).

This architecture is the hallmark of the secretoglobin fold. Family members "form antiparallel homodimers via two intermolecular disulfide bonds" (PMID: 11193762), and the fold contains a conserved central feature: "In the known uteroglobin structures, this lysine forms an exposed salt bridge with an aspartate side chain, which is conserved in almost all sequences" (PMID: 11193762). The dimeric, boomerang-shaped uteroglobin fold encloses a central hydrophobic cavity — the structural basis for the family's ligand-binding behavior.

Finding 5 — SCGB3A1 is produced by luminal/airway secretory epithelium and secreted into lumen and body fluids (extracellular site of action)

Curated tissue specificity (from PubMed 11481438 and 12406855) indicates SCGB3A1 is highly expressed in lung and prostate, and also in mammary gland, spleen, pancreas, testis, and liver. In the lung it is detected throughout the airway epithelium with highest expression in large airways and in submucosal gland acinar and ductile cells, but not in respiratory bronchioles, alveolar ducts, or alveolar epithelium. In the mammary gland it localizes specifically to luminal epithelial cells. The secreted protein is recoverable in bronchial lavage, saliva, plasma, and serum — "high levels of HIN-1 protein are detected in bronchial lavage, saliva, plasma, and serum" (PMID: 15383627) — confirming apical secretion into the lumen and the circulation. This defines the extracellular compartment as the location where SCGB3A1 carries out its function.

Finding 6 — SCGB3A1 maps to chromosome 5q35.3 in the SCGB3A subfamily, separate from the 11q12.2 secretoglobin cluster

NCBI/Ensembl annotation (Gene ID 92304, ENSG00000161055) places SCGB3A1 as a small protein-coding gene (~1.6 kb, minus strand) at chromosome 5q35.3. It belongs to the secretoglobin 3A (UGRP) subfamily together with its paralog SCGB3A2 (UGRP1 / HIN-2). This is distinct from the classical uteroglobin-family members (CC10/SCGB1A1, mammaglobin, lipophilins, lacryglobin), which "are localized on chromosome 11q12.2 in a dense cluster" (PMID: 11193762). SCGB3A1's separate chromosomal location establishes it as a distinct subfamily member rather than part of the ancestral 11q12.2 cluster.

Consolidated GO annotations assign molecular functions of cytokine activity (non-traceable author statement) and protein binding (inferred from physical interaction); cellular components of extracellular region and extracellular exosome; and biological processes of negative regulation of cell growth, regulation of cell population proliferation, and signal transduction. A "positive regulation of myoblast fusion" term is only an electronic/phylogenetic inference (IBA) with no direct experimental support and should be treated cautiously.

Finding 7 — Family-level inference: secretoglobins can sequester hydrophobic ligands and are anti-inflammatory

The founding secretoglobin, uteroglobin (SCGB1A1 prototype), is a secreted anti-inflammatory/immunomodulatory protein. It "inhibits soluble phospholipase A(2) activity and binds and perhaps sequesters hydrophobic ligands such as progesterone, retinols, polychlorinated biphenyls, phospholipids, and prostaglandins" (PMID: 17916741). It also displays antichemotactic, antiallergic, and antitumorigenic activities, and secretoglobins are broadly expressed by mucosal epithelia and present in blood, urine, and other body fluids.

Because SCGB3A1 shares the secreted, dimeric uteroglobin fold with its central hydrophobic cavity, an analogous small-molecule/lipid-binding and immunomodulatory capacity is structurally plausible. This remains a structure-/evolution-based inference and has not been directly demonstrated for SCGB3A1. It is nonetheless a valuable hypothesis: a sequestration or lipid-mediator-buffering activity could reconcile the "cytokine-like" ligand behavior with the absence of an identified classical receptor.

Primary function (answer to the core question). SCGB3A1 is not an enzyme or transporter. It is a signaling molecule — a secreted, cytokine-like ligand. Its primary role in the pathway is to antagonize mitogen-driven PI3K/AKT signaling by engaging a high-affinity cell-surface receptor on epithelial cells, thereby restraining proliferation, motility, and survival. The AKT epistasis experiment (PMID: 16266985) is the strongest direct mechanistic evidence and defines AKT as the critical downstream node.

Site of action. Extracellular. SCGB3A1 is synthesized in the secretory pathway (signal peptide), dimerizes via an interchain disulfide, and is secreted apically into the airway/glandular lumen and into the circulation. It exerts its effect from outside the cell by receptor engagement, not intracellularly.

Pathway context. The defined pathway is PI3K→AKT→(Rb/cell-cycle, survival, invasion). An additional, structurally-inferred but unproven layer is the family's capacity to sequester hydrophobic ligands (lipids, steroids, retinoids) and to be anti-inflammatory (PMID: 17916741); if operative for SCGB3A1, this could provide the molecular basis for receptor-independent or lipid-mediator-buffering effects.

Paralog contrast. SCGB3A1's closest paralog SCGB3A2/UGRP1 has an identified receptor (MARCO) and a role in innate pulmonary immunity/pathogen clearance (PMID: 12847263). This illustrates that UGRP-subfamily secretoglobins engage cell-surface receptors — supporting the plausibility of a dedicated SCGB3A1 receptor — while also showing that each paralog has a distinct biology, cautioning against assuming SCGB3A1 shares MARCO or an immune-clearance role.

Limitations and Knowledge Gaps

  1. The receptor is unidentified. High-affinity cell-surface binding is demonstrated (PMID: 16266985), but the receptor that transduces AKT suppression is unknown. This is the single largest gap in the mechanistic model. Unlike its paralog SCGB3A2 (receptor = MARCO), SCGB3A1 has no validated partner.

  2. Direct ligand-binding/sequestration is inferred, not shown. The hydrophobic-cavity ligand-binding and anti-inflammatory activities are documented for the family prototype uteroglobin (PMID: 17916741) but have not been experimentally confirmed for SCGB3A1. Whether SCGB3A1 binds steroids, retinoids, phospholipids, or prostaglandins is open.

  3. No experimental 3D structure for human SCGB3A1. The dimeric secretoglobin fold and interchain disulfide are inferred from sequence/family homology and UniProt annotation, not from a crystal or cryo-EM structure of SCGB3A1 itself.

  4. Causality vs. correlation in cancer. Promoter hypermethylation correlates tightly with loss of expression across many tumor types, but methylation studies are associative; the growth-suppressive causality rests primarily on the re-expression experiments in cell lines, not on in vivo genetic models.

  5. Physiological (non-cancer) function underexplored. The normal role in differentiated epithelium — possibly in surfactant/mucosal homeostasis, epithelial differentiation, or local immunomodulation — is not defined. Mouse knockouts show no gross airway developmental defect and do not alter other Scgb genes (PMID: 30768367), leaving the endogenous function unresolved.

  6. Some database annotations are weakly supported. The GO term "positive regulation of myoblast fusion" is only a phylogenetic/electronic inference (IBA) with no direct experimental backing and should not be treated as an established function.

Proposed Follow-up Experiments / Actions

  1. Receptor identification (highest priority). Use recombinant tagged HIN-1 dimer for affinity-capture/cross-linking proteomics or an expression-cloning screen (as was successfully used for the SCGB3A2–MARCO pair) on HIN-1-responsive epithelial cells. Validate candidates by loss-of-function (CRISPR) rescue of AKT suppression.

  2. Direct ligand-binding assays. Test whether purified SCGB3A1 dimer binds hydrophobic ligands (progesterone, retinoic acid, phospholipids, prostaglandins) by isothermal titration calorimetry, fluorescence displacement, or native mass spectrometry — directly testing the family-based inference in Finding 7.

  3. Structural determination. Solve the SCGB3A1 homodimer structure (X-ray/cryo-EM) and map the hydrophobic cavity; complement with AlphaFold-Multimer prediction and PAE/pLDDT confidence analysis of the dimer interface and disulfide geometry.

  4. Pathway dissection. Define how receptor engagement reaches PI3K/AKT (receptor tyrosine kinase inhibition? phosphatase activation, e.g., PTEN/PP2A?). Use phospho-proteomics after acute HIN-1 exposure to map the signaling cascade upstream of AKT.

  5. In vivo tumor-suppressor test. Generate conditional Scgb3a1 knockout mice and challenge with mammary/airway carcinogenesis models to establish whether loss accelerates tumorigenesis — moving from correlative methylation data to causal genetics.

  6. Biomarker development. Given secretion into saliva, plasma, serum, and lavage, evaluate circulating/secreted HIN-1 protein and promoter-methylation assays as early-detection or risk-stratification biomarkers for breast, lung, and esophageal cancer.

Conclusion

SCGB3A1 (HIN-1/UGRP2, Q96QR1) is a small, secreted, disulfide-linked homodimeric secretoglobin of the UGRP (SCGB3A) subfamily, produced by terminally differentiated luminal, airway, and glandular epithelial cells and secreted into the lumen and body fluids. It functions extracellularly as a cytokine-like growth-inhibitory ligand, not as an enzyme or transporter: by binding a high-affinity (still-unidentified) epithelial cell-surface receptor it suppresses mitogen-induced PI3K/AKT signaling, thereby inhibiting proliferation, migration, and invasion and promoting apoptosis. Its silencing by CpG-island promoter hypermethylation early in breast, lung, prostate, esophageal, and other epithelial cancers is consistent with a tumor-suppressor role. A family-based structural inference — that its uteroglobin fold could bind/sequester hydrophobic ligands and exert anti-inflammatory effects — remains an attractive but untested hypothesis.
