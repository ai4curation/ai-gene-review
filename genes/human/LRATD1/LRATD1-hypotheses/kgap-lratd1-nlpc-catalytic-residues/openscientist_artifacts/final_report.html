LRATD1 (FAM84A, Q96KN4): is the LRAT-like domain a pseudoenzyme?

Hypothesis slug: kgap-lratd1-nlpc-catalytic-residues Source: genes/human/LRATD1/LRATD1-ai-review.yaml, selector free-text Target: human LRATD1 / FAM84A, UniProt Q96KN4, NCBITaxon:9606 Focus type: free_text (computational prediction about catalytic competence)

Every number in this report comes from code executed during the investigation. The executed code, its output tables and the figures are in provenance/. Where a public resource refused a request, that failure is stated plainly instead of being replaced with an invented result.

Summary

The hypothesis is partially supported. Its conclusion is correct and its stated premise is wrong.

The seed hypothesis asserts that the LRAT-like domain of LRATD1 "has lost the catalytic Cys-His(-His/Asn) residues". That is not what the sequence and structure show. LRATD1 keeps both catalytic histidines in their exact structural positions and has lost only the nucleophilic cysteine. His143 of LRATD1 corresponds to LRAT His60 and PLAAT3 His23. His155 corresponds to LRAT His72 and PLAAT3 His35. Ser212 corresponds to LRAT Cys161 and PLAAT3 Cys113. A second, smaller loss sits beside it: the first-shell histidine of the NCEHFV motif (PLAAT3 His115, LRAT His163) is Ser214 in LRATD1. The family-defining motif reads KLTSENCEHFV in PLAAT3 and SLLWNNCEHFV in LRAT against EICWTNSESFA in LRATD1 and ELSWRNSESFA in LRATD2.

The conclusion the hypothesis draws from its premise survives. LRAT/PLAAT catalysis runs through a covalent acyl-thioester formed on that cysteine, captured crystallographically at 2.2 Å (PMID: 25383759). The exact substitution LRATD1 carries, Cys to Ser at the nucleophile, leaves LRAT with "only a few percent" of wild-type activity (PMID: 10819989). The substitution is fixed in 16 of 16 vertebrate orthologs spanning 63.0-99.3 percent identity, from human to Takifugu rubripes, so it is not a human quirk, an isoform artefact or a database error. In the AlphaFold model, superposed on the PLAAT3 crystal structure 4DOT at 1.34 Å over 89 CA pairs, no cysteine sulfur comes within 11.26 Å of the nucleophile position, so no nearby thiol can rescue the chemistry. The domain is predicted non-catalytic for the LRAT/PLAAT reaction set.

Two results change what a curator should do with this. First, there is nothing to remove: Q96KN4 carries no catalytic molecular function, and none of the three InterPro signatures that match it maps to any GO term, so the correct action is preventive (a review comment or a NOT annotation that blocks future carry-over from LRAT and PLAAT1-5), not a deletion. Second, the pocket has not decayed. Six of the eight first-shell positions of the PLAAT3 active site are conserved in LRATD1, the nucleophile position is open to solvent at 9.2 Å² (against 3.0 Å² for the buried PLAAT3 Cys113 SG), and AlphaMissense places Glu213, Phe215, Trp144 and His155 in the 83rd-99th percentile of predicted missense intolerance within the domain. That pattern is what a retained ligand-binding pseudoenzyme looks like, and it is the route to a molecular function term better than GO:0005515.

Key findings

F1. LRATD1 keeps both catalytic histidines and has serine where the catalytic cysteine belongs

Smith-Waterman local alignment (BLOSUM62, gap open 11, gap extend 1) of human LRATD1 (Q96KN4) against human LRAT (O95237) and PLAAT1-5 places the UniProt-annotated active-site residues of the targets on LRATD1 as follows: LRAT His60 and PLAAT3 His23 map to LRATD1 His143 (motif PHWAVYVG against PLAAT3 RHWAIYVG); LRAT His72 and PLAAT3 His35 map to LRATD1 His155 (motif GQIIHL against DGYVVHL); LRAT Cys161 and PLAAT3 Cys113 map to LRATD1 Ser212. Pairwise identity over the aligned LRAT domain is 27.6 percent against LRAT, 34.1 percent against PLAAT3 and 35.4 percent against PLAAT4, which is low enough that the register needed independent structural confirmation (see F3).

The result that matters for the curation text: the seed hypothesis is wrong that the histidines are gone. The seed hypothesis is right that the nucleophile is gone.

F3. Structural superposition confirms the register and rules out thiol rescue

The AlphaFold model AF-Q96KN4-F1-model_v6 was superposed on chain A of the PLAAT3/HRASLS3 crystal structure PDB 4DOT by iterative closest-CA Kabsch fitting seeded from the sequence alignment: 89 matched CA pairs, RMSD 1.34 Å. After refinement, LRATD1 His143 NE2 sits 0.44 Å from PLAAT3 His23 NE2, His155 NE2 sits 0.13 Å from His35 NE2, and Ser212 CB sits 1.76 Å from Cys113 CB (OG to SG 2.51 Å). The register derived from sequence is therefore confirmed by structure.

The His143 ND1 to Ser212 OG distance of 3.12 Å is a hydrogen bond, closer than the equivalent His23 ND1 to Cys113 SG contact in the crystal. Per-residue CA pLDDT from the same model: 64.3 for residues 1-132, 91.2 for the LRAT domain 133-228, 79.6 for residues 229-292. His143, His155 and Ser212 sit at 93.1, 97.4 and 96.1. The structural argument rests on the confidently modelled half of the protein.

phenix.superpose_pdbs was tried first and is reported here as having failed on this low-identity pair. With its default identity matrix it matched only 29 residues and least-squares fitted on 6 atoms; the blosum50/local retry raised AssertionError: ['I','V'] at superpose_pdbs.py:733. The in-executor Kabsch refinement was used instead, and its result is reproducible from the saved code.

F6. The only primary LRATD1 paper never tested the LRAT domain

PMID: 16820875 is the sole primary experimental paper on human FAM84A found in PubMed and in a 25-record Europe PMC sweep. It reports that FAM84A "was not expressed in any of the 23 normal tissues examined except the testis", that "immuno-cytochemical staining revealed localization of FAM84A protein in the subcellular membrane region, the staining was limited to the region lacking attachment with neighboring cells", that "exogenous FAM84A expression increased cell motility in NIH3T3 cells", and that "phosphorylation of serine 38 of FAM84A was associated with morphology of cells".

Ser38 sits at pLDDT 46.4, in the low-confidence N-terminal half (residues 1-132, mean 64.3), structurally separate from the LRAT domain (mean 91.2). The motility and morphology phenotypes therefore cannot be attributed to the pseudo-active site on current evidence. All three GO terms sourced from this paper (GO:0000902, GO:0048870, GO:0005737) rest on one overexpression study in a heterologous mouse fibroblast line.

The cytoplasm annotation deserves a curator check. The source paper describes staining in the subcellular membrane region restricted to cell edges lacking contact with neighbours, which reads as a membrane-proximal or leading-edge pool and not a diffuse cytoplasmic one.

F7. The serine-hydrolase alternative is disfavoured

Ser212 plus His143 plus His155 is formally a serine-histidine-histidine arrangement, and the His143 ND1 to Ser212 OG distance of 3.12 Å is a hydrogen bond. Reading this as a working serine hydrolase is disfavoured on three grounds.

The LRAT/PLAAT family is a circularly permuted branch of the papain-like NlpC/P60 superfamily, so the serine sits in a cysteine-protease geometry. Ab initio calculations on papain and S195C rat trypsin showed that "the spatial juxtapositions of the triad residues have been inverted in the serine and cysteine proteinases" (PMID: 8819168), which is why swapping the nucleophile between the two folds gives near-inert enzymes. In SARS 3C-like proteinase, "the Cys145 to Ser mutation resulted in an active enzyme with a 40-fold lower activity" (PMID: 15078103). In cathepsin L-like proteinases, "Active site mutants (pCAL2C25S and pCAL3C26S) were constructed by replacing the catalytic cysteine with serine to prevent autocatalytic processing" (PMID: 22659439), so the substitution is used experimentally as an inactivating one. Inside the LRAT family, C161S leaves only a few percent of wild-type activity (PMID: 10819989). LRATD1 also loses the first-shell His115 equivalent (Ser214), removing a second residue of the catalytic environment.

A Europe PMC sweep for FAM84A or LRATD1 (25 records) and an activity-based protein profiling search returned no report placing FAM84A or FAM84B in the serine hydrolase set. The alternative is disfavoured and not formally closed, because no one has assayed the purified protein.

Mechanistic model

The chain of inference runs from a single substitution to a curation action.

In words: the LRAT/PLAAT reaction requires a cysteine thiolate that attacks the sn-1 carbonyl and forms a covalent acyl-thioester intermediate. LRATD1 has a serine hydroxyl in that position in every vertebrate examined, has lost the adjacent first-shell histidine, and has no cysteine within 11.26 Å. The domain cannot run the family reaction. Because no GO pipeline has ever assigned that reaction to LRATD1, and no matching InterPro signature maps to GO, the output is a preventive record rather than a deletion.

What the domain still has is a folded, confidently modelled pocket (pLDDT 91.2) with the enzyme geometry intact, a His-His dyad at 2.86 Å, a hydrogen bond from His143 to Ser212, six of eight first-shell positions conserved, and the nucleophile position open to solvent. The dashed edges above mark the inference that is not yet tested: that this pocket is retained for ligand binding. That inference is the most useful lead in the report and the thing to test first.

The most useful curation output is a review comment, not a term change. No pipeline has ever attached an acyltransferase or phospholipase term to LRATD1, and no matching signature can introduce one through InterPro2GO. Recording the pseudoenzyme conclusion protects against future transfer from LRAT and PLAAT1-5 by sequence similarity, by orthology-based pipelines (IBA), or by paralog-based inference, which is the realistic carry-over risk for a protein whose only MF is GO:0005515.

On replacing "protein binding": no better MF is supported by evidence today. The defensible positive lead is a binding function, and the specific candidate worth testing is lipid or ligand binding in the retained pocket (GO:0008289 lipid binding or a child term). That candidate must not be asserted now, because no binding assay on LRATD1 exists. It belongs under Discriminating tests.

Mechanistic scope

Direct molecular activity being tested. Whether the LRAT domain of LRATD1 (residues 133-228) can perform acyl transfer or phospholipid hydrolysis. The chemistry requires a cysteine thiolate that attacks the sn-1 carbonyl and forms a covalent acyl-thioester intermediate, which was captured crystallographically for this family at 2.2 Å (PMID: 25383759). LRATD1 has a serine hydroxyl at that position in every vertebrate examined and no other cysteine within 11.26 Å. The predicted direct activity is none for the LRAT/PLAAT reaction set.

What the domain still has. A folded domain at pLDDT 91.2 with the PLAAT3 active-site geometry intact, a His-His dyad at 2.86 Å, a hydrogen bond from His143 ND1 to Ser212 OG at 3.12 Å, six of eight first-shell positions conserved, and the nucleophile position open to solvent at 9.2 Å². This is the profile of a pocket retained for something other than catalysis.

Downstream phenotypes, kept separate. Increased NIH3T3 motility on FAM84A overexpression and cell-morphology effects tied to phosphorylation of Ser38 (PMID: 16820875); upregulation in papillary thyroid carcinoma with knockdown effects on EMT and Wnt/beta-catenin (PMID: 33751775). These are overexpression, knockdown and correlation results at the whole-protein level. Ser38 sits at pLDDT 46.4 in the disordered N-terminal half, structurally separate from the LRAT domain. Nothing published links the motility phenotype to the pseudo-active site. For the paralog FAM84B, deleting the conserved H23/H35 motif abolishes oncogenic activity (PMID: 32183428, review-level), which is the only published hint that the LRAT domain matters functionally in this branch of the family, and it concerns the paralog.

Conflicts and alternatives

1. The seed hypothesis overstates the loss. Both catalytic histidines are present and correctly positioned. A curator copying the phrase "has lost the catalytic Cys-His(-His/Asn) residues" into a review comment would record a factual error. The accurate statement is that LRATD1 has lost the nucleophilic cysteine (Ser212) and the NCEHFV histidine (Ser214) while retaining His143, His155, Asn211, Glu213, Trp144 and Phe215.

2. The serine-hydrolase alternative. Covered in F7. Disfavoured on fold geometry, on measured activity costs of the equivalent substitution, and on the additional loss of the first-shell histidine. Not formally closed, because no one has assayed the protein.

3. Paralog confusion. FAM84A (LRATD1) and FAM84B (LRATD2) are routinely discussed together, and most published functional work is on FAM84B. Both carry the same substitution (EICWTNSESFA and ELSWRNSESFA), so the residue conclusion transfers, but phenotypic claims about FAM84B must not be imported into the LRATD1 review. The family is also confusable with HRASLS/PLAAT, where the enzymology is real and measured, which is exactly the carry-over the review comment should block.

4. Traceability of the FAM84B motif-deletion claim. PMID: 32183428 states that deleting the conserved H23/H35 motif abolishes FAM84B oncogenic activity. Repeated Europe PMC and PubMed searching returned only this review, not a separate primary report of that experiment. It is used here as review-level support and flagged for verification against the review's own reference list.

5. Model dependence. No experimental structure of LRATD1 exists. Every geometric number rests on AF-Q96KN4-F1-model_v6. The domain is well modelled (pLDDT 91.2) and the superposition onto an experimental crystal structure is tight (1.34 Å over 89 CA pairs), but a conformational change in the real protein could bring a cysteine closer than 11.26 Å. This is a low-probability alternative, not an excluded one.

6. Isoforms. UniProt Q96KN4 is 292 residues. No isoform that alters residues 133-228 was examined.

Discriminating tests
