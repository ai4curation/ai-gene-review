# LRATD1 literature notes

## Identity and naming boundaries

LRATD1 is also called FAM84A and neurological sensory protein 1 (NSE1). The historical
NSE1 alias is hazardous: it is not evidence that LRATD1 has a neural sensory function,
and LRATD1 must not be conflated with NSMCE1/Nse1, the RING-containing subunit of the
SMC5/6 genome-maintenance complex. One LRATD1 paper makes precisely this unsupported
cross-gene leap in its discussion, calling Nse1 an Smc5-Smc6 subunit; no LRATD1-specific
experiment in that paper establishes DNA repair, ubiquitin-ligase activity, or genomic
stability [PMID:21424122, "The function of this gene remains unknown, but recent findings
suggested that Nse1 (an alias of FAM84A) contributes to genomic stability (23). Nse1 is
one of subunit of the Smc5-Smc6 holocomplex, which facilitates DNA repair, and Nse1
contains a RING-like motif that often confers ubiquitin E3 ligase activity."].

LRATD2/FAM84B/NSE2 is a paralog, not an interchangeable name. A chemical-proteomics
study tested both proteins independently: [PMID:34956690, "Alk12 labeling signals
decreased with the treatment of NMT inhibitor in LRATD1, LRATD2, and ERICH5 and were
completely removed in G2A mutants, confirming LRATD1, LRATD2, and ERICH5 as substrates
of NMT1/2 (Figure 4C)."] LRATD2-specific tumor or signaling claims therefore should not
be transferred to LRATD1.

## Direct molecular and localization evidence

The clearest direct molecular result is that LRATD1 is an N-myristoyltransferase
substrate. Alkynyl-fatty-acid labeling in HEK293T cells was hydroxylamine-resistant,
reduced by an NMT inhibitor, and abolished by the LRATD1 G2A mutation
[PMID:34956690, "However, three proteins LRATD1 (LRAT domain-containing 1, also called
FAM84A or neurological sensory protein 1 NSE1), LRATD2 (FAM84B/NSE2), and ERICH5
(glutamate-rich protein 5) demonstrated clear fluorescence signals in the Alk12 treated
samples compared to the control without Alk12 treatment, and the signals were
hydroxylamine-resistant, suggesting that they are potentially N-myristoylated."]. This
is a post-translational modification of LRATD1; it does not show that LRATD1 catalyzes
lipid metabolism or retinoid acyl transfer. The LRAT-domain name alone is not catalytic
evidence.

The original colorectal-cancer study localized FAM84A to a peripheral region away from
cell-cell contacts [PMID:16820875, "Although immuno-cytochemical staining revealed
localization of FAM84A protein in the subcellular membrane region, the staining was
limited to the region lacking attachment with neighboring cells."]. A later papillary
thyroid cancer study described it more broadly as mainly cytoplasmic
[PMID:33751775, "Immunofluorescence analysis indicated that FAM84A was mainly located
in cytoplasm, β‐catenin was located both in cytoplasm and in nuclei, and that
downregulation of FAM84A inhibited expression of nuclear β‐catenin."]. These data
support a cytoplasmic/peripheral pool, not an integral-membrane assignment.

UniProtKB Q96KN4 records two splice isoforms. Isoform 2 replaces residues 146-292 with
a long alternative C terminus; the reviewed primary studies do not establish which
isoform was endogenous in the tested tumors or whether motility, localization, Ser38
phosphorylation, or myristoylation differs between isoforms. Gly2 and Ser38 lie before
the alternative segment, but that sequence fact alone does not prove equivalent
isoform behavior.

## Cell morphology, motility, and cancer context

The original paper reported exogenous FAM84A-dependent motility in mouse NIH3T3 cells
and linked Ser38 phosphorylation to morphology [PMID:16820875, "In addition, we found
that exogenous FAM84A expression increased cell motility in NIH3T3 cells, and that
phosphorylation of serine 38 of FAM84A was associated with morphology of cells."].
This supports broad morphology/motility processes but is heterologous overexpression,
not normal human physiology.

The phenotype is context dependent. Mouse Fam84a overexpression increased migration in
human HepG2 cells but not detectably in Huh7 cells [PMID:21424122, "The migration rates
in cells over-expressing FAM84A were significantly higher (P<0.01) than in control cells
(187±20.9 and 120±17.3, respectively, Fig. 7B). We also used Huh7 cells for the
migration experiment, but a clear difference was not observed (data not shown). These
data indicate that FAM84A plays an important role in acceleration of cell migration,
but this depends on the cell type."]. The same study did not detect Ser38
phosphorylation in its mouse liver or cancer-cell contexts, so that phosphosite should
not be assumed universally active.

In human papillary thyroid cancer models, FAM84A knockdown reduced motility and invasion
[PMID:33751775, "Collectively, these results indicated that knockdown of FAM84A
significantly suppressed the migration and invasion ability of PTC cells in vitro."].
Knockdown also altered EMT and beta-catenin readouts, while miR-874-3p directly targeted
the FAM84A 3' UTR. These results support disease-context cell behavior and upstream RNA
regulation; they do not demonstrate that LRATD1 directly binds beta-catenin or is an
enzyme in Wnt signaling.

## Expression, neural, metabolic, and interaction boundaries

The original survey found FAM84A frequently upregulated in colorectal cancer and absent
from 23 surveyed normal tissues except testis [PMID:16820875, "One of these was a gene
termed FAM84A that was not expressed in any of the 23 normal tissues examined except the
testis."]. Mouse brain cDNA was used as a cloning source in PMID:21424122, and an ATXN10
contact appears in a neurodegeneration-focused interaction screen, but neither fact
establishes a neural sensory function. No direct normal neural mechanism was found in
the reviewed primary literature.

The HuRI screen supplies candidate LRATD1 contacts with ARHGEF5 and CYGB
(PMID:32296183), and a separate systematic map supplies ATXN10 (PMID:32814053). These
are useful partner-level provenance but presently do not form a validated endogenous
complex or a coherent molecular mechanism. Likewise, the CAR-responsive mouse liver
tumor expression in PMID:21424122 does not make LRATD1 a xenobiotic- or energy-metabolic
enzyme. No direct metabolic catalytic activity was identified.

## Evidence-weighted conclusion

The best-supported picture is a cytoplasmic/peripheral, N-myristoylated protein whose
perturbation or overexpression affects morphology and motility in several cancer or
heterologous cell models. Normal-tissue physiological function, endogenous molecular
partners, isoform-specific behavior, direct neural function, and any metabolic
catalytic role remain unresolved.
