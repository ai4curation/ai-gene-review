| **Ciliary transition zone (TZ): membrane-protein gate.** Human B9D2/MKSR2 (Q9BPU9) is the central linker in a soluble, linear **MKS1–B9D2–B9D1** complex. Interdependent TZ localization of all three proteins supports selective ciliary membrane composition. | Interaction and three-hybrid assays showed that B9D2 binds MKS1 and B9D1, whereas MKS1 and B9D1 do not bind directly. In B9D2-knockout human RPE1 cells, ciliary GPR161, agonist-induced SMO, ARL13B and INPP5E were lost, while IFT88/IFT140 localization and IFT movement remained largely normal; wild-type B9D2 rescued the phenotype (2020). (pqac-00000011, pqac-00000010) | **High.** Direct human-cell knockout, rescue, interaction and localization evidence. “Gate” denotes a component of the larger MKS/TZ compartmentalization system—not a pore or independently sufficient barrier. Current reviews emphasize that passive diffusion restriction, lipid organization and active trafficking remain mechanistically intertwined. (pqac-00000032, pqac-00000035) |
| **TZ membrane anchoring and axonemal homeostasis.** The B9 complex recruits or stabilizes TMEM67 at the TZ and helps exclude inappropriate soluble enzymes, thereby maintaining axonemal tubulin acetylation and polyglutamylation. | Co-immunoprecipitation showed TMEM67 interaction with B9D2 and MKS1; B9D2 loss disrupted TMEM67—but not CEP290 or RPGRIP1L—localization. HDAC6 entered B9D2-deficient cilia, TTLL5 distribution changed and axonemal modifications declined without a corresponding global cellular decrease. After 6 hours of serum-induced disassembly, **77.8% of wild-type versus 41.5% of B9D2-knockout cells retained cilia**; HDAC6 inhibition or depletion restored acetylation. Published online **October 30, 2025** and assigned to JCI volume 136 (2026). (pqac-00000042, pqac-00000043) | **High for cultured cells; moderate for the complete in-vivo mechanism.** Supported across human and mouse cells and maternal–zygotic zebrafish mutants, but much of the mechanistic chain derives from engineered knockout and overexpression systems. |
| **Mother centriole before TZ assembly: initiation of ciliogenesis.** B9D2 and MKS1 localize to the mother centriole in non-ciliated proliferating cells and facilitate preciliary-vesicle docking and CP110 removal. | Electron microscopy found fewer mother centrioles with associated ciliary vesicles in B9D2-knockout RPE1 cells; CP110 removal after serum starvation was impaired. Structured-illumination microscopy placed B9D2 near the mother-centriole marker CEP164 before cilium formation. Published online in 2025 and assigned to JCI volume 136 (2026). (pqac-00000046) | **Moderate–high.** Direct imaging and knockout evidence establish an early ciliogenesis role, but the vesicle-recruitment mechanism and direct binding partners remain incompletely defined. |
| **Extraciliary epithelial tight junctions and lumen expansion.** In rat cholangiocyte and kidney epithelial models, B9D2 associates with ZO-1/claudin-4-positive junctions and supports junction maturation, barrier tightness and biliary-cyst lumen expansion. | Under conditions with fewer than 5% ciliated cells, B9D2 localized at cell contacts and colocalized with ZO-1 and claudin-4, but not adherens-junction or desmosomal markers. shRNA depletion broadened or disrupted ZO-1 and dispersed claudin-4 and E-cadherin. During days 8–10, control transepithelial resistance was approximately **2,000–2,500 Ω·cm²**, whereas all B9D2-depleted conditions remained **below 500 Ω·cm²**; 3D cyst diameter fell to approximately half of control without significantly preventing cyst formation or secretion (October 2024). (pqac-00000013, pqac-00000015, pqac-00000016) | **Moderate for rat epithelial models; low–moderate for normal human physiology.** Three shRNAs and low-ciliation conditions support an extraciliary interpretation, but no human-tissue confirmation or junctional rescue was reported; residual cilia and indirect polarity effects cannot be fully excluded. |
| **B9/C2-like domain and membrane association.** B9D2 is a small non-transmembrane B9-family protein whose domain belongs to the C2-domain superfamily; it may associate with phosphoinositide-rich membranes. No catalytic reaction is known. | Human-protein studies show that the B9 domain occupies nearly the entire B9D2 sequence and mediates B9-complex assembly. A 2024 lipid-overlay assay using extracts from B9D2–GFP-expressing HEK cells produced the strongest signals with PI4P and PI(3,4,5)P3 and a weaker signal with PI(4,5)P2. (pqac-00000012, pqac-00000019, pqac-00000020) | **High for domain-family assignment and complex assembly; low for lipid specificity.** The overlay used whole-cell extract and immobilized lipids—not purified B9D2—so it does not establish direct binding, native-membrane affinity or physiological specificity. The C2-like fold is not evidence of enzymatic activity. |
| **Human disease variants: recessive Meckel and Joubert syndromes.** Variant severity appears to reflect differential disruption of B9-complex stability, TZ localization and ciliogenesis. | In one consanguineous family, two Meckel-syndrome fetuses were homozygous for **p.Ser101Arg**; the variant was absent from 300 ancestry-matched controls, failed to rescue zebrafish b9d2 suppression and abolished MKS1—but not B9D1—binding (2011). (pqac-00000024, pqac-00000025) A later screen identified biallelic B9D2 variants in **2 of 151** Chinese Joubert patients: c.215-1G>T plus p.Asp47Val and p.Arg75Trp plus p.Asp53_Trp57del; parental segregation and aberrant splicing were confirmed. Joubert missense variants retained B9-complex binding and rescued ciliogenesis but impaired TZ function, whereas p.Ser101Arg was unstable and failed to rescue ciliogenesis. Published online October 30, 2025 and assigned to JCI volume 136 (2026). (pqac-00000044, pqac-00000046, pqac-00000047) | **High for recessive disease association; moderate for genotype–mechanism generalization.** Patient numbers are very small, and functional tests rely heavily on engineered cells and animal models. B9D2 should not be conflated with **B9D1/MKSR1** or **MKS1**, despite their shared domain and complex membership. |
