# Human B9D2 (MKSR2; UniProt Q9BPU9): functional annotation

**Identity verified.** The target is human **B9D2**, also called **MKSR2** or B9-domain-containing protein 2—not the related **B9D1/MKSR1** or **MKS1**. Its B9 domain occupies most of the small protein and belongs to the C2-like domain superfamily, consistent with the supplied B9-C2/PF07162 annotation. B9D2 is a non-transmembrane protein; there is no established catalytic reaction or transporter substrate. Its best-supported primary function is as a **structural and organizational component of the ciliary transition-zone MKS complex**. (caenenbraz2024newfunctionsof pages 1-2, okazaki2020formationofthe pages 5-7, okazaki2020formationofthe pages 1-5)

## Primary molecular function and location

The **transition zone (TZ)** lies at the base of the primary cilium, between the basal body and the axoneme. It helps maintain a ciliary membrane composition distinct from the surrounding plasma membrane while permitting regulated protein traffic. In human-cell interaction assays, B9D2 binds both MKS1 and B9D1; MKS1 and B9D1 do not bind one another detectably without B9D2. The supported arrangement is therefore **MKS1–B9D2–B9D1**, with B9D2 serving as the central molecular bridge. In human retinal pigment epithelial (RPE1) cells, loss of either MKS1 or B9D2 prevents normal TZ localization of the other B9-complex components. (okazaki2020formationofthe pages 7-11, okazaki2020formationofthe pages 5-7, okazaki2020formationofthe pages 1-5)

The clearest functional test is B9D2 knockout and rescue in human RPE1 cells. Knockout produces a moderate reduction in cilium formation and length, but a pronounced defect in **ciliary membrane-protein composition**: GPR161 is absent under basal conditions, Smoothened (SMO) fails to accumulate after agonist stimulation, and the membrane-associated proteins ARL13B and INPP5E are lost from cilia. Re-expression of B9D2 rescues the abnormal localization. By contrast, the examined IFT88/IFT140 localization and IFT88-particle movements remain largely intact. These observations identify B9D2 primarily as a contributor to TZ-dependent *selective retention or gating of membrane-associated cargo*, rather than an enzyme, a cargo transporter, or an indispensable component of intraflagellar transport. (okazaki2020formationofthe pages 7-11, okazaki2020formationofthe pages 5-7)

A subsequent study extended this mechanism. Co-immunoprecipitation detected an association of B9D2 and MKS1 with the TZ membrane protein **TMEM67**; B9D2-knockout cells lost normal TZ enrichment of TMEM67 while retaining the examined CEP290 and RPGRIP1L localization. The authors infer that the soluble B9 complex helps stabilize or anchor TMEM67 within the membrane-proximal MKS assembly. This is an experimentally supported organizational role, **not** evidence that B9D2 itself spans the membrane or forms a pore. The paper appeared online **30 October 2025**, although its journal assignment is *Journal of Clinical Investigation* **136(2), 2026**. (he2026ciliopathyrelatedb9protein pages 4-5, he2026ciliopathyrelatedb9protein pages 1-2)

B9D2 also acts **before the TZ forms**. Imaging placed B9D2 and MKS1 at the mother centriole in proliferating, predominantly non-ciliated RPE1 cells. Electron microscopy of B9D2 knockouts found fewer mother centrioles associated with preciliary vesicles, while immunostaining showed impaired removal of the centriole-capping protein CP110 after ciliogenesis induction. The data support a role in early preciliary-vesicle docking and initiation of cilium assembly; precisely how B9D2 recruits or docks those vesicles remains unresolved. (he2026ciliopathyrelatedb9protein pages 5-7)

## Pathways and recent mechanistic developments

**Hedgehog signaling** is the most directly interpretable signaling connection: SMO and GPR161 require appropriate ciliary localization for pathway regulation, and B9D2 knockout disrupts both proteins’ observed ciliary localization. This establishes a route by which B9D2 deficiency *can* perturb Hedgehog signaling; the receptor-localization experiments should not be mistaken for a measurement of every downstream Hedgehog response in B9D2-knockout cells. Hedgehog defects measured in **B9D1-mutant mice** provide complex-level support but are not B9D2-specific experiments. Reports linking the broader MKS system to Wnt signaling do not establish B9D2 as a direct Wnt receptor or dedicated Wnt-pathway effector. (okazaki2020formationofthe pages 5-7, dowdle2011disruptionofa pages 11-12, hartill2017meckel–grubersyndromean pages 5-6)

Recent experiments identify a second consequence of defective TZ organization: **axonemal microtubule homeostasis**. In B9D2-knockout human cells, ciliary tubulin acetylation and polyglutamylation fell despite no corresponding decrease in whole-cell levels of those modifications. HDAC6, a tubulin deacetylase, was detected in some mutant cilia where it was absent from controls, while ciliary distribution of the glutamylase TTLL5 was altered. HDAC6 inhibition or depletion restored axonemal acetylation. After **six hours** of serum-induced disassembly, **77.8% of wild-type versus 41.5% of B9D2-knockout cells** retained cilia. Maternal–zygotic *b9d2*-mutant zebrafish also had reduced axonemal modifications. Thus B9D2 affects the distribution of at least some *soluble* ciliary proteins as well as membrane cargo, although the disassembly findings do not fully account for its reduced ciliogenesis rate. (he2026ciliopathyrelatedb9protein pages 4-5, he2026ciliopathyrelatedb9protein pages 2-4)

This soluble-cargo result refines—not simply overturns—the 2020 observation of apparently normal IFT movement: intact movement of the tested IFT particles does **not** prove that every soluble protein is correctly compartmentalized. Authoritative TZ reviews likewise caution that a proposed diffusion barrier, lipid organization, and active cargo transport work together, and that their exact molecular contributions remain unresolved. (okazaki2020formationofthe pages 7-11, moran2024transportandbarrier pages 3-5, park2022compositionorganizationand pages 1-3, he2026ciliopathyrelatedb9protein pages 4-5)

**Domain-based biochemical inference requires restraint.** The B9/C2-like classification is compatible with a membrane-associated scaffold, but does not establish calcium-dependent lipid binding or enzymatic activity. A **2024** lipid-overlay experiment using extracts from B9D2–GFP-expressing HEK cells found its strongest signals with **PI4P** and **PI(3,4,5)P₃**, and a weaker signal with **PI(4,5)P₂**. Because the assay used immobilized lipids and cell extracts rather than purified B9D2, it does not determine direct binding, native-membrane specificity, affinity, or a physiological lipid substrate. (okazaki2020formationofthe pages 1-5, caenenbraz2024newfunctionsof pages 8-10, caenenbraz2024newfunctionsof pages 10-12)

## Extraciliary epithelial function: 2024 evidence

A **2024** study observed B9D2 at **ZO-1- and claudin-4-associated tight junctions** in normal rat cholangiocyte and kidney epithelial cultures, rather than exclusively at cilia. The authors used serum-maintained conditions in which **fewer than 5%** of cells were ciliated. Three B9D2-targeting shRNAs disrupted junctional ZO-1 organization and the distribution of claudin-4 and E-cadherin. In cholangiocyte monolayers, control transepithelial electrical resistance reached approximately **2,000–2,500 Ω·cm² on days 8–10**, whereas knockdown cultures remained **below 500 Ω·cm²**. In three-dimensional culture, depleted cells still formed cysts, but mean cyst diameter was approximately **half** that of controls. These results implicate B9D2 in epithelial junction maturation, barrier integrity, and lumen expansion, offering a plausible contributor to biliary abnormalities in ciliopathies. They do **not** yet establish the same extraciliary role in normal human tissue: the principal models were rat cells, residual cilia were possible, and a direct junctional molecular mechanism was not established. (caenenbraz2024newfunctionsof pages 8-10, caenenbraz2024newfunctionsof pages 1-2, caenenbraz2024newfunctionsof pages 2-3, caenenbraz2024newfunctionsof pages 10-12)

## Human disease evidence and present applications

**Biallelic B9D2 variants are implicated in Meckel syndrome and Joubert syndrome**, disorders consistent with loss or impairment of TZ-associated ciliary function. In one consanguineous Meckel-syndrome family, **two affected fetuses** carried homozygous **c.301A>C (p.Ser101Arg)**; the variant was absent from **300 ancestry-matched controls**. Wild-type human B9D2 partially rescued the studied *b9d2*-suppressed zebrafish phenotype, whereas p.Ser101Arg did not. Biochemical experiments found that p.Ser101Arg lost detectable MKS1 interaction while retaining B9D1 interaction. These observations connect a patient variant specifically to B9-complex disruption; the extensive mouse developmental and Hedgehog experiments in that publication examined **B9d1**, and should not be attributed to B9D2 knockout. (dowdle2011disruptionofa pages 1-2, dowdle2011disruptionofa pages 9-11, dowdle2011disruptionofa pages 8-9)

More recently, exome sequencing of **151 Chinese Joubert-syndrome patients** identified **two affected individuals** with biallelic B9D2 variants: one had **c.215-1G>T and p.Asp47Val**, and the other **p.Arg75Trp and p.Asp53_Trp57del**. Parental segregation was confirmed; the splice-site variant produced aberrant transcripts. In engineered B9D2-knockout cells, the tested Joubert-associated missense variants restored ciliogenesis but failed to restore normal TZ-associated cargo localization and axonemal modifications. In contrast, the Meckel-associated p.Ser101Arg variant was less stable under stable-expression conditions and failed to fully rescue ciliogenesis. This supports a **variant-dependent separation** between early cilium assembly and subsequent TZ function, but two newly identified patients cannot establish a general genotype–phenotype rule. Earlier *C. elegans* editing of Joubert-associated **P74S and G155S** likewise produced variant-dependent TZ phenotypes, providing model-organism, not direct human-cell, evidence. (lange2021interpretingthepathogenicity pages 1-4, he2026ciliopathyrelatedb9protein pages 7-9, he2026ciliopathyrelatedb9protein pages 5-7, he2026ciliopathyrelatedb9protein pages 9-11)

**Real-world use** is currently principally **molecular diagnosis and interpretation of suspected ciliopathies**, including evaluating recessive B9D2 variants alongside clinical features and family segregation. The cell and animal assays above provide experimental ways to assess variant effects; they do not constitute a validated B9D2-targeted treatment. Accordingly, the most defensible concise annotation is: *human B9D2/MKSR2 is a B9-domain scaffold in the MKS1–B9D2–B9D1 complex, acting at the ciliary TZ to organize TMEM67 and preserve ciliary protein compartmentalization; it also promotes early mother-centriole ciliogenesis and contributes to axonemal homeostasis. A tight-junction role is supported in rat epithelial models but remains less firmly established for human physiology.* (okazaki2020formationofthe pages 7-11, caenenbraz2024newfunctionsof pages 10-12, he2026ciliopathyrelatedb9protein pages 4-5, he2026ciliopathyrelatedb9protein pages 5-7)
