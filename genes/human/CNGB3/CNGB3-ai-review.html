Gene Description

CNGB3 is the beta subunit of the cone photoreceptor cyclic-nucleotide-gated cation channel. It assembles with CNGA3 in a heterotetramer containing three alpha subunits and one beta subunit. CNGB3 contributes to the ion-conduction pathway, selectivity filter and cyclic-nucleotide-binding machinery, shaping ligand sensitivity and pore gating. Intracellular cGMP controls the cone membrane current during visual transduction. Pathogenic CNGB3 variants can disrupt channel function and cause achromatopsia or related cone disorders.

Core Functions

CNGB3 contributes ligand-binding and pore-forming structures to the CNGA3/CNGB3 cone channel, coupling intracellular cGMP recognition to cation conduction. Direct human structures resolve three CNGA3 subunits and one CNGB3; beta3 alone is not asserted to form a functional channel. cAMP activation is a supported secondary capability. Full-length canonical human CNGB3 and engineered N-terminal truncations provide the structural evidence, whereas natural isoform 2, lacking residues 590–594 in the nucleotide-binding domain, remains functionally unresolved. The human cone outer-segment membrane location is an inference from mouse ortholog and alpha-partner studies together with human surface-channel experiments; direct human outer-segment immunolocalization is not established by those sources.
