CC   -!- FUNCTION: Non-catalytic subunit of the tRNA-specific adenosine-34
CC       deaminase complex, composed of the ADAT2 catalytic subunit and the
CC       ADAT3 regulatory subunit, which deaminates adenosine-34 (the first,
CC       also called wobble position of the anticodon) to inosine in many tRNAs.
CC       Inosine-34 allows the decoding of 3 different nucleotides at the third
CC       position of mRNA codons, as inosine is able to pair with U, C, and A.
CC       Required for binding of the ADAT2-ADAT3 complex to tRNA through its N-
CC       terminus, which rotates with respect to the catalytic domain of the
CC       complex, formed by ADAT2 and the ADAT3 C-terminal domain, to position
CC       the tRNA anticodon stem-loop correctly in the ADAT2 active site. The
CC       ADAT2-ADAT3 complex is required for radial migration of projection
CC       neurons in the developing brain cortex, and the catalytic activity of
CC       the complex is necessary for this function.
CC       {ECO:0000250|UniProtKB:Q6PAT0}.
CC   -!- SUBUNIT: Heterodimer with catalytic subunit ADAT2 to form the tRNA-
CC       specific adenosine-34 deaminase complex.
CC       {ECO:0000250|UniProtKB:Q6PAT0}.

CC   -!- DOMAIN: The N-terminal domain adopts a fold that shares similarity with
CC       the ferredoxin-like domains of other tRNA-modifying enzymes and is
CC       required for tRNA-binding and deamination activity of the ADAT2-ADAT3
CC       heterodimer. The domain can rotate with respect to the catalytic domain
CC       formed by ADAT2 and the ADAT3 C-terminal domain without affecting the
CC       structure of the catalytic domain. Rotation positions the tRNA
CC       anticodon stem-loop correctly in the ADAT2 active site.
CC       {ECO:0000250|UniProtKB:Q6PAT0}.
CC   -!- DISEASE: Neurodevelopmental disorder with brain abnormalities, poor
CC       growth, and dysmorphic facies (NEDBGF) [MIM:615286]: An autosomal
CC       recessive disorder characterized by global developmental delay,
CC       impaired intellectual development, and speech delay apparent from
CC       infancy or early childhood. Most patients have dysmorphic facial
CC       features, and white matter abnormalities on brain imaging. More
CC       variable features may include teeth anomalies, distal joint
CC       contractures, spasticity, peripheral neuropathy, and behavioral
CC       problems. {ECO:0000269|PubMed:23620220, ECO:0000269|PubMed:40120092}.
CC       Note=The disease is caused by variants affecting the gene represented
