## GO:0003924 GTPase activity — ACCEPT
GNAO1 is a guanine-nucleotide-binding G alpha subunit that switches between GDP- and GTP-bound states and hydrolyzes bound GTP. Receptor/effector biochemistry and nucleotide-state experiments support the intrinsic molecular switch. This is distinct from the GAP activity of RGS proteins; activated or nucleotide-defective mutants are controls, not substitutes for the wild-type function.
Sources: PMID:31073061, PMID:39103320, PMID:33535037

## GO:0005737 cytoplasm — ACCEPT

## GO:0005834 heterotrimeric G-protein complex — ACCEPT
The GDP-state G alpha subunit associates with a G beta/gamma dimer to form a functional heterotrimer. Receptor-coupling and structural/reconstitution experiments support this assembly and beta/gamma binding as integral functions. A particular experimental beta/gamma combination does not imply universal interchangeability.

## GO:0006936 muscle contraction — KEEP_AS_NON_CORE
Native mouse Go loss disrupts muscarinic regulation of ventricular L-type calcium currents, providing a signaling mechanism affecting cardiac contraction. Retain this regulatory contribution to the physiological process, not a claim that Go is part of the contractile filament.
Sources: PMID:9050846

## GO:0007188 adenylate cyclase-modulating G protein-coupled receptor signaling pathway — ACCEPT
Go modulates cellular cAMP signaling through receptor/cell-dependent mechanisms. Native neuronal and biochemical experiments separate effects on Gi/beta-gamma coupling from direct cyclase regulation; Go is not a universal direct cyclase inhibitor.
Sources: PMID:33535037, PMID:7961880

## GO:0007193 adenylate cyclase-inhibiting G protein-coupled receptor signaling pathway — ACCEPT
Receptor-coupled Go supports inhibition of cellular cAMP in defined systems, including wild-type Go/M4 reporter assays and neuronal dopamine signaling. Purified Go has limited isoform-specific effects, and the cellular phenotype does not establish direct Go inhibition of ADCY6. Structural receptor complexes establish reception, not the downstream enzyme mechanism by themselves.
Sources: PMID:7961880, PMID:33535037, PMID:8119955, PMID:39103320
