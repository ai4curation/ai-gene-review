Gene Description

CHRNB2 encodes the neuronal nicotinic acetylcholine receptor β2 subunit, an integral plasma-membrane component of heteropentameric ligand-gated cation channels. β2 contributes pore-lining helices and the complementary face of agonist-binding interfaces. In receptors such as alpha4β2, subunit composition and stoichiometry tune acetylcholine sensitivity, desensitization, conductance and calcium permeability. These receptors mediate neuronal excitation and modulate transmitter release, with circuit-dependent effects on attention, nicotine responses and development of visual connections. Pathogenic variants can alter channel gating and are associated with sleep-related hypermotor epilepsy.

Core Functions

β2 contributes pore-lining segments and the complementary acetylcholine-binding surface to assembled neuronal nicotinic receptors. Agonist binding gates transmembrane cation flow, including calcium with composition-dependent permeability, coupling cholinergic signaling to membrane depolarization and transmitter-release modulation. Reconstitution and engineered human structures establish the subunit contribution, not an autonomous β2 channel or a universal native stoichiometry.

2026-10-10. Human CHRNB2 (HGNC:1962; UniProt P17787) was reviewed from a genuine isolated normal intake. The original 122 source objects, their order, qualifiers, evidence, references, partners and negation/product fields are retained. Biological synthesis

β2 contributes pore-lining helices and the complementary orthosteric pocket surface to assembled neuronal nicotinic receptors. It does not form an autonomous channel. A single core therefore uses contributes_to GO:0022848, with plasma-membrane/acetylcholine-channel-complex location and the directly performed receptor-signaling and cation-transmembrane-transport processes. Actual ACh density at human alpha4–β2 interfaces supports the binding contribution; mutant sensitivity by itself would not distinguish affinity from gating (PMID:38454578; PMID:15964197). Human structures and reconstitution support the underlying channel capability (PMID:27698419; PMID:29720657; PMID:8906617).

Species, construct and stoichiometry limits are explicit. Structural constructs can contain loop deletions, tags or Bril insertions; functional full-length recordings are distinguished from those structures. The alpha7/beta2 source uses a defined concatemer with NACHO in Xenopus oocytes, not a universal native pentamer. Calcium permeability, calcium-only current and calcium-dependent potentiation are different measurements. Human concatemer experiments support calcium conduction with composition-dependent permeability (PMID:17132685; PMID:20881005). No separate calcium-binding MF or unsupported new process is proposed.

Process and location judgments

The exact current mouse Chrnb2 donor sources were inspected individually rather than treated as interchangeable orthology votes. Retinal waves, later visual mapping, transient dendritic refinement, adult dopamine circuitry, sleep and behavioral phenotypes are contextual contributions retained as non-core. They do not create additional autonomous molecular mechanisms. In PMID:15788760, normal young hearing followed by older spiral-ganglion loss supports auditory maintenance. It does not establish development of the vestibulocochlear nerve, so that developmental assertion is marked over-annotated while the hearing contribution remains.

The bladder study places the nicotinic step in autonomic ganglion neurons, with beta2/beta4 compensation. Intact muscarinic contraction and bypass by electrical stimulation distinguish neuronal regulation from smooth-muscle force generation. The smooth-muscle contraction row is refined to GO:0006940, and the micturition assertion remains non-core at autonomic circuit scope (PMID:10531434). No beta2 receptor on the final bladder smooth-muscle postsynaptic membrane is inferred.
