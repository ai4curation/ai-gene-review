CC   -!- FUNCTION: Mediates the Na(+)-independent high affinity transport of
CC       organic anions such as the thyroid hormones L-thyroxine (T4), L-
CC       thyroxine sulfate (T4S), and 3,3',5'-triiodo-L-thyronine (reverse T3,
CC       rT3) at the plasma membrane (PubMed:12351693, PubMed:18566113,
CC       PubMed:19129463, PubMed:40680733). Regulates T4 levels in different
CC       brain regions by transporting T4, and also by serving as an export pump
CC       for T4S, which is a source of T4 after hydrolysis by local sulfatases
CC       (PubMed:18566113). Increases the access of these substrates to the
CC       intracellular sites where they are metabolized by the deiodinases
CC       (PubMed:18566113). Other potential substrates, such as triiodothyronine
CC       (T3), 17-beta-glucuronosyl estradiol (17beta-estradiol 17-O-(beta-D-
CC       glucuronate)), estrone-3-sulfate (E1S) and sulfobromophthalein (BSP)
CC       are transported with much lower efficiency (PubMed:12351693,
CC       PubMed:19129463, PubMed:40680733). Transports T4 and E1S in a pH-
CC       insensitive manner (PubMed:19129463). Facilitates the transport of
CC       thyroid hormones across the blood-brain barrier and into glia and
CC       neuronal cells in the brain (PubMed:30296914).

CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:19129463, ECO:0000269|PubMed:30296914,
