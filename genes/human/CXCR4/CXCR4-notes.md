## Biological synthesis and finite review follow-up — 2026-10-15

CXCR4 converts extracellular CXCL12 gradients into a cellular migration signal. The original ligand-identification study and the active CXCL12–CXCR4–Gi structural report support receptor identity and coupling. An independently recovered functional study measures reduced cAMP after SDF-1alpha and inhibition of that response by AMD3100. Thus the ligand-specific pathway and the inhibitory G-protein branch describe complementary aspects of the same receptor unit; CXCR4 is upstream of the cyclase, rather than a cyclase enzyme itself [PMID:8752281; PMID:39093700; PMID:20228059, Methods and Figure 7/Results].

The axis also contributes to hematopoietic cell homing, discussed in the MIF/CD74 original. The preserved official UniProt record links pathogenic CXCR4 variants to WHIM syndrome and describes abundant mature myeloid cells in marrow despite peripheral neutropenia. These physiological and disease facts improve the standalone summary without adding a new developmental or disease-process annotation [PMID:19665027, Introduction/Discussion; genes/human/CXCR4/CXCR4-uniprot.txt, DISEASE: WHIMS1]. A separate claim about a specific hematopoietic progenitor retention assay is not manufactured from those descriptions.

Ligand binding, receptor-complex association and signaling are distinct measurements. Viral vMIP-II binds CXCR4 but is antagonistic; its engineered disulfide-trapped structure is not an agonist experiment. MIF-dependent AKT signaling requires CXCR4/CD74 in the tested human cells, and receptor co-immunoprecipitation supports the complex. The 2009 study cites earlier direct MIF-binding work rather than independently measuring that contact. The most specific directly inspected ligand class is therefore CXC chemokine binding, while positive MIF complex biology remains intact [PMID:25612609; PMID:19665027].

The intracellular tail connects CXCR4 to regulatory and cytoskeletal proteins. Actin was identified after receptor-tail pull-down, and myosin light chain co-precipitated with endogenous receptor. These observations support the existing contextual binding assertions; the precise binary interfaces remain unresolved. The relevant original text is available at https://www.researchgate.net/publication/11044228_Cutting_Edge_Association_of_the_Motor_Protein_Nonmuscle_Myosin_Heavy_Chain-IIA_with_the_C_Terminus_of_the_Chemokine_Receptor_CXCR4_in_T_Lymphocytes (GST pull-down Methods; Results and Figure 2 caption). The failed PDF request is not represented as full-PDF access [PMID:12421915].

RAMP3 has a positive target result in the original membrane-capture screen, including the independently inspected Tables S6/S7. This is useful association evidence, but it does not identify a particular RAMP-dependent transport or signaling activity of CXCR4. The standing project instruction retains such supported generic interactions as non-core; the review's request to remove the positive assertion solely because it is generic is not adopted [PMID:39083597, Tables S6/S7].

Antigen-dependent CXCR4 recruitment occurs at the immunological synapse. The explicit UniProt location note traces the electronic junction mapping to that same experiment, so both source tuples now use the specific location. A receptor need not perform structural adhesion to be located there [PMID:20215400]. Conversely, the dendritic-cell source is a review whose accessible abstract discusses CCR7. Its full discussion and underlying CXCR4-specific evidence remain unverified, so that assertion is now undecided; the general CXCL12 ligand paper does not independently establish a dendritic-cell endpoint [PMID:16621978].

Several remaining uncertainties are about experimental scope rather than absence of biology. Repair-associated OPC differentiation and remyelination do not separately demonstrate preservation of mature myelin. The exosome source requires a verified target-level detection, not receptor activity inside vesicles. The adhesion abstract separates pertussis-toxin sensitivity from chemokine-induced migration, and tumor vasculogenic mimicry does not automatically establish normal vasculature development. Short source-exact anchors now expose several of these boundaries; the quotes are confined to the review YAML and counted cumulatively [PMID:20534485; PMID:20458337; PMID:19703720; PMID:27693460].
