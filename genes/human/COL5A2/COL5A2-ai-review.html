Gene Description

COL5A2 encodes the alpha-2 chain of type-V collagen, a secreted fibrillar extracellular-matrix protein. Its principal assembly contains two alpha-1(V) chains and one alpha-2(V) chain. Type-V collagen participates in heterotypic fibrils with type-I collagen and helps organize their architecture. The alpha-2 chain supports efficient incorporation of type-V collagen into the matrix; disruption of its structure alters fibril assembly and connective-tissue integrity, causing classical Ehlers-Danlos syndrome. Its structural contribution is particularly important in skin and corneal connective tissue. The precursor passes through the secretory pathway before extracellular processing and matrix incorporation.

COL5A2 review notes

COL5A2 encodes a 1499-residue alpha-2(V) collagen precursor. The protein contributes to type-V heterotrimers and the organization of heterotypic fibrils. No alternative protein products are distinguished in the current reviewed record.

Structural assembly and complex specificity

COL5A2 supplies the alpha-2(V) chain of type-V collagen, principally an alpha1(V)2-alpha2(V) heterotrimer. Human mutation abstracts in PMID:9425231 and PMID:9783710 explicitly support alpha2(V)-associated matrix defects; their full Methods could not be recovered. Original full-text Methods, Results and Discussion in PMID:15199158 separate the metabolic-labeling experiments from purified collagen electrophoresis and densitometry. Mouse pN-mutant neonatal skin and fibroblasts show reduced alpha2(V) matrix incorporation, altered chain ratios and abnormal type-I/V matrix architecture. The WT versus mutant purified fibroblast collagen-V alpha1:alpha2 gel-densitometry ratios were 1.6:1 versus 15.5:1; these measure the reported assay, not every tissue. Sparse alpha2 remains. Secretion, degradation and assembly effects are not fully separable. The alpha1(V) homotrimer that predominates in mutant matrix is not an alpha2(V) homotrimer. No figure pixels were inspected.

These findings support one integrated matrix structural core: GO:0030020, type-V trimer GO:0005588, matrix location and collagen-fibril organization. The current structural-term comment explicitly permits ECM collagens; protein detection is not presented as a tensile-force experiment. COL5A2 performs structural assembly work rather than the proteolytic processing catalyzed by other proteins. No extra proteolysis, receptor or processing process is added.
