Gene Description

Atrophin-1 is a broadly expressed transcriptional coregulator with nuclear and cytoplasmic pools. Its transcriptional corepressor activity is enhanced by ETO/MTG8, and it can associate with nuclear-matrix structures containing corepressor machinery. Context-dependent coactivation is reported for the mouse ortholog. Atrophin proteins also associate with FAT1 and contribute to directional migration in vascular smooth muscle cells. Expansion of the ATN1 polyglutamine tract causes dentatorubral-pallidoluysian atrophy, with altered protein processing, localization and cellular toxicity.

Functional interpretation

Human ATN1 reporter repression and ETO association support the core. The GAL4 fusion supplies DNA binding; colocalized histone deacetylases supply their own catalytic activity. Neither activity is assigned intrinsically to ATN1. Nuclear-matrix association is experimentally contextual, not a claim that all ATN1 is permanently in an insoluble aggregate or a particular named N-CoR complex. Mouse reporter activation and rodent vascular migration/orientation remain correctly labeled contextual inferences. Species, reporter and cell-line differences are not proved causes of the different regulatory outputs.

The neuronal apoptosis annotation is over-specific for the cited non-neuronal assay. This does not deny the cytotoxicity result or exclude participation by a toxic fragment. The detailed external source boundaries are below. The CNS-development assertion and 14 interaction records remain uncertain. Positive curated interaction counts are acknowledged; lack of access to a particular pair table is not biological evidence against the pair.

IBA assertions agree with direct human evidence. The PAINT ancestral node and alignment were not replayed. Human ATN1 appearing among descendant evidence is legitimate, not circular. No new process, partner enzyme activity or family identifier is inferred from these associations.
