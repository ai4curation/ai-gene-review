Gene Description

CD79B (Igβ) is a type I transmembrane signaling subunit of the B-cell antigen receptor. It forms a disulfide-linked heterodimer with CD79A that associates with membrane immunoglobulin, supports receptor assembly and surface expression, and couples antigen recognition to intracellular signaling through immunoreceptor tyrosine-based activation motifs. The paired CD79 chains also support receptor-directed antigen trafficking to late endosomal processing compartments, facilitating MHC class II presentation. Ig-beta enhances Ig-alpha phosphorylation in chimeric-receptor experiments, although the underlying recruitment or protection mechanism is unresolved. Biallelic CD79B defects cause autosomal recessive agammaglobulinemia 6; the hypomorphic G137S allele impairs heterodimer assembly and receptor surface expression, with a leaky block in B-cell development.

Core Functions

CD79B forms the Ig-alpha/Ig-beta signaling module with CD79A, supporting surface assembly and transmembrane signal transmission by the B-cell antigen receptor. Cooperative Ig-beta-dependent enhancement of Ig-alpha phosphorylation is supported by chimeric-receptor experiments. The paired chains also facilitate receptor-directed antigen delivery to late endosomal processing compartments; this trafficking context is supported at the receptor-complex level.

Core activity and complex context

CD79B supplies the Ig-beta chain of the BCR signaling module with CD79A. The human IgM-BCR structure directly establishes its membership alongside membrane immunoglobulin PMID:35981043. Human B-cell experiments demonstrate loss of surface IgM/CD79A after CD79B knockout and stronger recovery with WT CD79B than with G137S PMID:36426942. Selected primary PMC Methods, rescue Results and Figure7 legend were subsequently inspected: sequence-verified WT/G137S human CD79B constructs were transduced into Granta-519 CD79B-knockout clone E4. Flow cytometry and Endo-H/western assays distinguish surface expression and protein maturation, with GFP and mRNA controls. G137S leaves low residual surface expression, so it is not treated as an absolute null in every expression system. Disease allele and expression context

UniProt P40259 identifies autosomal recessive agammaglobulinemia 6 (AGM6, MIM:612692) and links its G137S variant to the patient study. The primary report identifies a homozygous “glycine to serine at codon 137” [PMID:17675462, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17675462/]. Its journal PDF was independently inspected, including mutation analysis, transfected-cell methods, Results and Figure3/4 legends. The allele is hypomorphic: disulfide-linked CD79A/CD79B assembly is inefficient, and stable Jurkat reconstitution yields reduced surface BCR. The paper also reports that high transient expression can mask the surface-expression defect. The patient phenotype and the biochemical/cellular assays support receptor assembly as the link to impaired B-cell development; neither complete inactivity nor SGTA-dependent disease causation follows. The authentic publication cache remains unchanged and abstract-only.

The 2007 assembly study and the 2022 rescue experiment therefore complement each other while differing in expression system. The latter directly re-tests named G137S in human lymphoma cells, with low surface rescue and immature CD79B protein compared with WT [PMID:36426942, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9643646/]. The current developmental annotation remains non-core, now with direct human disease support in addition to the ancestral inference. Mouse deficient-progenitor evidence shows developmental arrest with intact V(D)J recombination; it does not assign CD79B recombinase activity [PMID:12097390, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12097390/; complete abstract inspected].

Cooperative signaling and antigen trafficking

Ig-beta enhances the speed and extent of Ig-alpha phosphorylation in the experimental receptor system of PMID:8617796 (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8617796/). The complete abstract explicitly describes human PDGFR extracellular/transmembrane domains fused to Ig cytoplasmic tails and expressed in murine A20 IIA1.6 cells. Full Methods and the exact Ig-tail species were not independently resolved. This supports cooperative signaling, without establishing that CD79B directly recruits a particular kinase or protects phosphorylated CD79A from dephosphorylation. Those mechanistic alternatives remain hypotheses.

PMID:9057631 (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9057631/; complete abstract inspected) compares engineered Ig-alpha/Ig-beta heterodimers and homodimers in WEHI-231 cells for apoptosis and associated Syk phosphorylation. It corroborates heterodimer cooperation. Its abstract is not evidence of an internalization assay, and no new apoptosis process assertion is added.

A separate primary source directly addresses trafficking: PMID:10352267 (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10352267/; complete abstract inspected). Single-chain chimeric receptors could internalize, yet failed to reach the MHC class II-enriched compartment efficiently under the conditions tested. The paired chains restored efficient late-endosomal targeting and presentation of temporally restricted antigen. This supports a contextual role for the receptor module in delivery to processing compartments. Full primary Methods and exact construct species were not independently inspected; the claim is not presented as a native human CD79B-only experiment.

No NEW process annotation is proposed. Current QuickGO definitions distinguish movement of activated receptors into the cell (GO:0031623) from expression of antigen in association with MHC at the cell surface (GO:0019882). A receptor being internalized is insufficient by itself to establish participation; an experimentally resolved sorting or adaptor role would be stronger evidence. The accessed chimeric evidence also separates uptake from later antigen trafficking. The question is retained for expert follow-up rather than turned into a direct human GO assertion. The local GO-CAM index had no CD79B/P40259 target entry at review time, which is not evidence that an annotation is missing. No comparator-based gap claim is made.
