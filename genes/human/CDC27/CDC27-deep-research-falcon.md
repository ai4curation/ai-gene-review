# Human CDC27/ANAPC3 Functional-Annotation Report

## Executive summary

**Identity is verified.** The requested protein is human **cell division cycle protein 27 homolog**, gene **CDC27**, also called **ANAPC3/APC3**, corresponding to **UniProt P30260**. The literature consistently places APC3/CDC27 in the tetratricopeptide-repeat (TPR) lobe of the human anaphase-promoting complex/cyclosome (APC/C), matching the supplied APC3/CDC27-family, TPR-repeat, and TPR-like helical-domain annotations. No conflicting same-symbol protein was encountered.

CDC27 is **not an enzyme, transporter, secreted ligand, or membrane receptor**. It is a large, intracellular, α-helical **structural/regulatory scaffold** within APC/C. Ubiquitin transfer is catalyzed by the APC2–APC11 cullin–RING module, whereas CDC20 or CDH1 together with APC10 supplies most substrate-degron recognition. CDC27 helps construct the TPR lobe, organize the holoenzyme, and provide interaction surfaces needed for coactivator-dependent substrate processing. Therefore, it has no independent catalytic reaction or intrinsic substrate specificity analogous to an enzyme. (alfieri2017visualizingthecomplex pages 4-4, alfieri2017visualizingthecomplex pages 2-3, zhou2016insightsintoapcc pages 1-2)

## 1. Identity, family, and domain verification

Human CDC27 is synonymous with **ANAPC3/APC3**. Structural literature describes APC3 as one of four canonical TPR proteins in the APC/C TPR lobe, alongside APC6, APC7, and APC8. Canonical APC/C TPR proteins are predominantly helical, contain approximately **14 TPR motifs**, and form homodimeric arrangements in the complex; a reported molecular mass for human CDC27 is approximately **91.9 kDa**. These observations are fully consistent with UniProt P30260 and the supplied InterPro/Pfam annotations **TPR-like helical domain**, **TPR repeats**, **ANAPC3**, **TPR_1**, and **TPR_8**. (chen2026thedysregulationof pages 58-61, chen2026thedysregulationof pages 19-25, alfieri2017visualizingthecomplex pages 2-3)

The APC/C itself is a roughly **1.2–1.5-MDa**, multisubunit cullin–RING E3 ubiquitin ligase. Cryo-EM resolves a triangular or bowl-like assembly containing a TPR lobe, platform, central cavity, substrate-recognition region, and peripheral catalytic module. APC3 occupies the TPR lobe, and APC7 associates specifically with APC3. This architecture supports a scaffold/organizing role rather than direct chemistry. (alfieri2017visualizingthecomplex pages 4-4, chen2026thedysregulationof pages 19-25, zhou2016insightsintoapcc pages 1-2)

## 2. Primary molecular function

### 2.1 Structural and regulatory role

CDC27/APC3 helps assemble and stabilize the APC/C TPR lobe and presents interaction surfaces that organize catalytic, coactivator, and substrate-recognition modules. The best functional description is therefore **APC/C structural-regulatory subunit and coactivator-docking scaffold**. Its repeated helix-turn-helix TPR architecture is well suited to protein–protein interaction and assembly of large macromolecular complexes. (alfieri2017visualizingthecomplex pages 4-4, alfieri2017visualizingthecomplex pages 2-3, zhou2016insightsintoapcc pages 1-2)

CDC27 should not be annotated as the APC/C catalytic center. APC2 is the cullin-like subunit and APC11 contains the RING domain that recruits ubiquitin-charged E2 enzymes. CDC20 or CDH1 and APC10 form the principal degron-recognition module: the coactivator WD40 β-propeller recognizes D-box, KEN-box, and ABBA motifs, while APC10 strengthens D-box recognition. CDC27 contributes indirectly by maintaining the geometry and docking surfaces that allow these modules to operate. (alfieri2017visualizingthecomplex pages 4-4, boer2016erratumtocontrolling pages 2-4, alfieri2017visualizingthecomplex pages 2-3)

### 2.2 Complex-level reaction and substrates

Although CDC27 is non-catalytic, the APC/C containing CDC27 catalyzes transfer of ubiquitin from E2 enzymes to lysines on selected cell-cycle proteins, promoting polyubiquitylation and usually 26S-proteasome-dependent degradation. Major physiological substrates include cyclin A, cyclin B, securin, Nek2A, and numerous spindle, kinetochore, and cell-cycle regulators. Substrate selection is temporally ordered: Nek2A and cyclin A can be degraded in prometaphase, followed by securin and cyclin B around metaphase–anaphase transition. (alfieri2017visualizingthecomplex pages 2-3, alfieri2017visualizingthecomplex pages 1-2)

Securin degradation releases separase, which cleaves cohesin and permits sister-chromatid separation. Cyclin-B destruction lowers CDK1 activity and drives mitotic exit and cytokinesis. Thus, CDC27’s broader structural role supports the irreversible proteolytic transitions underlying chromosome segregation and cell-cycle directionality. (alfieri2017visualizingthecomplex pages 1-2)

## 3. Pathways and regulation

### 3.1 APC/C–CDC20 and APC/C–CDH1 phases

APC/C activity is governed mainly by interchangeable coactivators. **APC/C–CDC20** operates during mitosis and promotes metaphase-to-anaphase transition and mitotic exit. **APC/C–CDH1** predominates from anaphase through late mitosis and G1, maintaining low abundance of mitotic regulators and helping establish the next cell-cycle state. (chen2026thedysregulationof pages 58-61, boer2016erratumtocontrolling pages 2-4, alfieri2017visualizingthecomplex pages 1-2)

Human-cell interaction imaging supports dynamic reorganization of CDC27-containing APC/C. APC3–APC10 interaction increased from prophase through metaphase and declined rapidly after metaphase–anaphase transition. That study quantified ten cells from two independent experiments at each cell-cycle stage, supporting stage-dependent complex organization, although it does not establish that CDC27 alone determines substrate specificity. (liu2020theinteractionprofile pages 44-47)

### 3.2 Spindle-assembly checkpoint

The spindle-assembly checkpoint prevents premature APC/C–CDC20 activation while unattached or improperly attached kinetochores remain. Its mitotic checkpoint complex restrains APC/C–CDC20 until chromosomes achieve correct bipolar attachment. Checkpoint silencing then allows securin and cyclin-B degradation, separase activation, anaphase onset, and mitotic exit. Structural and biochemical evidence indicates that this inhibition involves conformational and reciprocal regulation of the holoenzyme rather than an isolated inhibitory modification of CDC27. (alfieri2017visualizingthecomplex pages 2-3, alfieri2017visualizingthecomplex pages 1-2)

### 3.3 Phosphorylation

Multiple APC/C subunits are phosphorylated by mitotic kinases including CDK1 and PLK1. Holoenzyme phosphorylation promotes APC/C–CDC20 association and mitotic activation, whereas CDH1 phosphorylation prevents premature APC/C binding. CDC27 is widely used experimentally as a mitotic phosphoprotein/mobility-shift marker, but the strongest sources retrieved here support phosphorylation primarily at the **APC/C-complex level**; individual CDC27 phosphosites should not automatically be assigned a proven activating function. (boer2016erratumtocontrolling pages 2-4, alfieri2017visualizingthecomplex pages 2-3)

## 4. Cellular localization

CDC27 is an **intracellular APC/C component**, not a membrane or extracellular protein. Its function is carried out in the nuclear/chromatin and mitotic-apparatus environment, dynamically coupled to cell-cycle stage. APC/C is associated with mitotic chromosomes and apparatus structures, where it encounters substrates such as cyclin B1.

A 2024 study using live-cell imaging, fluorescence cross-correlation spectroscopy, reconstituted biochemistry, mutagenesis, and cryo-EM found that APC/C and cyclin B1 interact primarily at the mitotic apparatus. Both can bind nucleosomes; disrupting cyclin B1’s N-terminal nucleosome-binding arginine anchor reduced APC/C interaction, delayed degradation, and produced aneuploid cells. The result supports a spatial-concentration model in which mitotic chromosomes accelerate enzyme–substrate encounter. It establishes localization of the **CDC27-containing APC/C holoenzyme**, but does not mean that CDC27 alone dictates all APC/C targeting. Cirillo et al., published August 2024: https://doi.org/10.1038/s44318-024-00194-2.

## 5. Evidence hierarchy and current understanding

The strongest evidence for CDC27’s core annotation comes from reconstituted human APC/C, cryo-EM, crystallography, native mass spectrometry, interaction biochemistry, and cell-cycle perturbation. These data directly support TPR-lobe membership and a non-catalytic scaffold/regulatory role. The inference that CDC27 itself lacks ubiquitin-transfer chemistry is especially strong because the catalytic APC2–APC11 module is spatially and structurally distinct. (alfieri2017visualizingthecomplex pages 4-4, alfieri2017visualizingthecomplex pages 2-3)

By contrast, many disease studies measure CDC27 expression, mutation, or splicing without demonstrating that a particular phenotype results from a defined defect in APC/C assembly or catalysis. Such associations are valuable but should be classified below direct structural and biochemical evidence. Open Targets lists CDC27 associations with several phenotypes, but the retrieved scores are modest and often derive from overlapping evidence records; they do not establish validated clinical indications or therapeutic tractability. (OpenTargets Search: -CDC27)

## 6. Major 2024 developments

### 6.1 Hemifacial microsomia: candidate developmental-gene association

Song et al. performed trio whole-exome sequencing in **12 unrelated probands** and unaffected parents. Four de novo CDC27 variants occurred in three probands: **p.M475R, p.F26S, p.L27P, and p.L298\***. The p.M475R substitution lies in a TPR-region loop and was predicted to disrupt local structure; p.F26S and p.L27P occur in the annotated ANAPC3 region, while p.L298* is truncating. All 12 affected children had unilateral moderate-to-severe conductive hearing loss, with a mean air-conduction threshold of **80 ± 5 dBHL**. Song et al., published 26 April 2024: https://doi.org/10.3390/ijms25094707. (song2024functionalandgenetic pages 1-2, song2024functionalandgenetic pages 2-5)

CRISPR disruption of zebrafish cdc27 produced microcephaly, microphthalmia, spinal curvature, absent or hypoplastic pharyngeal cartilages, cardiac edema, disordered chondrocytes, increased apoptosis, and reduced cranial-neural-crest-cell proliferation. Injection of wild-type cdc27 mRNA rescued cartilage abnormalities; cdkn1a or tp53 disruption also produced substantial rescue, implicating inappropriate p53–CDKN1A signaling. (song2024functionalandgenetic pages 7-9, song2024functionalandgenetic pages 16-17)

This is substantial functional support for developmental relevance, but **not conclusive human causality**. The cohort was small, no mammalian validation was provided, heterozygous human variants were compared with a homozygous zebrafish loss-of-function model, and the authors explicitly state that disease-causing CDC27 variants had not been conclusively established. Hemifacial microsomia itself was reported to occur in approximately **1 in 3,500 to 1 in 45,000 births**. (song2024functionalandgenetic pages 1-2, song2024functionalandgenetic pages 16-17)

### 6.2 Ovarian failure and meiotic progression

A 2024 case–control WES study analyzed **118 Spanish women with ovarian failure**, **32 study controls**, and public pseudo-control populations. Eighteen genes showed an excess of prioritized variants at FDR <0.05. CDC27 had nine listed SNVs, with variants reported in **104 of 118 cases**, and a gene-level adjusted value of **5.35 × 10⁻⁵⁵**. Pathway analyses implicated oocyte meiosis, including metaphase-to-anaphase transition. Henarejos-Castillo et al., 2024: https://doi.org/10.1186/s12958-024-01325-4. (henarejoscastillo2024wholeexomesequencingand pages 1-2, henarejoscastillo2024wholeexomesequencingand pages 5-7)

Drosophila cdc27 knockout reduced fertility and significantly decreased the proportion of mature oocytes, providing cross-species functional support. However, the work remains a candidate association rather than a clinically validated biomarker: ovarian failure is genetically heterogeneous, the human signal was based on burden analysis, and Drosophila disruption does not establish pathogenicity of each human variant. The study reports that ovarian failure affects approximately **1% of women younger than 40**, that about **90%** of screened cases remain idiopathic, and that genetic factors may explain **10–25%** of cases. (henarejoscastillo2024wholeexomesequencingand pages 1-2, henarejoscastillo2024wholeexomesequencingand pages 5-7)

### 6.3 SF3B1-mutant myeloid disease and CHK1-inhibitor sensitivity

Sarchi et al. precisely engineered **SF3B1-K700E** in primary human CD34-positive hematopoietic stem/progenitor cells. Mutant SF3B1 caused alternative 3′ splice-site usage and reduced expression of mitotic genes, including **CDC27** and **BUBR1**. Among 186 conserved mis-spliced genes, **34 (18.3%)** were differentially expressed. Mutant cells showed delayed G2/M progression and delayed mitotic exit: they began leaving mitosis at about **90 minutes** after synchronization release, compared with **60 minutes** for wild-type cells. Sarchi et al., published September 2024: https://doi.org/10.1158/2643-3230.BCD-23-0230. (sarchi2024missplicingofmitotic pages 1-3, sarchi2024missplicingofmitotic pages 5-6)

CDC27 depletion or mis-splicing contributed functionally to the mitotic delay and sensitivity to CHK1 inhibition. The clinical-stage CHK1 inhibitor prexasertib preferentially depleted SF3B1-mutant immunophenotypic HSCs and impaired their engraftment in vivo. This is a promising **indirect vulnerability**, not evidence that prexasertib directly binds CDC27 and not yet a CDC27-targeted approved therapy. SF3B1 mutations occur in up to **30% of myelodysplastic-syndrome cases**, giving the mechanism potential translational relevance. (sarchi2024missplicingofmitotic pages 1-3, sarchi2024missplicingofmitotic pages 5-6)

## 7. Applications and translational relevance

Current practical uses of CDC27 are predominantly research-oriented:

1. **Mitotic and APC/C activity marker.** CDC27 phosphorylation-dependent mobility shifts and incorporation into APC/C are commonly used to monitor mitotic state and complex activity.
2. **Structural handle for APC/C biology.** CDC27 is useful for immunoprecipitating or visualizing the APC/C, mapping subunit contacts, and studying coactivator- and checkpoint-dependent conformations.
3. **Functional-genomics readout.** CDC27 depletion, knockout, or mis-splicing provides a way to test how weakened APC/C function affects chromosome segregation, development, fertility, and drug response.
4. **Candidate biomarker rather than validated clinical test.** The 2024 developmental, infertility, and myeloid-neoplasm studies support further validation, but none establishes routine diagnostic or therapeutic use.
5. **Indirect therapeutic stratification.** Defective CDC27 expression within broader APC/C or spliceosome dysfunction may help identify tumors vulnerable to mitotic-checkpoint or CHK1 perturbation. Direct APC/C inhibition is being explored preclinically, but systemic blockade risks harming normal proliferating tissues because APC/C is essential for chromosome segregation.

## 8. Expert assessment

The consensus structural interpretation is unusually clear: **CDC27 is an essential architectural and regulatory APC/C subunit, not its catalytic enzyme**. Assigning ubiquitin-ligase activity directly to CDC27 would be misleading. Its mechanistic importance lies in creating the TPR interaction framework through which coactivators, substrate receptors, checkpoint regulators, and catalytic subunits are coordinated. (alfieri2017visualizingthecomplex pages 4-4, alfieri2017visualizingthecomplex pages 2-3, zhou2016insightsintoapcc pages 1-2)

The most important conceptual advance in 2024 was that APC/C function is controlled not only by phosphorylation, coactivators, degrons, and checkpoint inhibition, but also by **spatial organization on mitotic chromatin**. Recent human genetics further suggests that partial CDC27 dysfunction may manifest in development or fertility, while acquired CDC27 mis-splicing can create drug vulnerabilities in malignant hematopoiesis. These disease links are biologically plausible because proliferating neural-crest cells, meiotic oocytes, and hematopoietic progenitors are highly dependent on accurate cell-cycle transitions; nevertheless, most remain candidate or preclinical findings requiring replication.

## Conclusion

Human CDC27/P30260 is a TPR-rich, non-catalytic APC/C scaffold that operates intracellularly within the nuclear/chromatin–mitotic apparatus. By organizing the APC/C TPR lobe and supporting coactivator-dependent substrate processing, it enables ordered ubiquitylation of securin, mitotic cyclins, and other regulators, thereby controlling anaphase onset, chromosome segregation, mitotic exit, and G1 maintenance. Cryo-EM and biochemical evidence strongly establish this core function. The leading 2024 developments concern spatial concentration of APC/C substrates on mitotic chromatin, candidate CDC27 variants in craniofacial development and ovarian failure, and CDC27 mis-splicing as part of a CHK1-inhibitor vulnerability in SF3B1-mutant hematopoietic cells. None yet constitutes an approved CDC27-specific diagnostic or therapy.
