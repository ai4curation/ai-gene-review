| Molecular action and localization | A 2.9 Å structure of human HNF4α residues 46–368 shows a homodimer bound head-to-tail to DR1 DNA and NCOA2-derived LXXLL peptides. Including the ligand-binding domain strengthened DNA affinity from approximately 6,000 nM for DBD–hinge to approximately 82 nM. The hinge contains a nuclear-localization signal; human variant assays generally detected HNF4A in nuclei, although R89W reduced nuclear abundance. (pqac-00000009, pqac-00000012, pqac-00000015, pqac-00000040) | HNF4A is primarily a nuclear, sequence-specific transcription factor—not an enzyme or transporter. Its ligand-binding domain contributes allosterically to DNA binding and co-regulator recruitment. |
| Liver | Direct or established targets include **APOA1, APOB, PCK1, PKLR**, and context-dependent **G6PC** regulation. In human hepatocyte-derived cells, HNF4A maintained G6PC1-promoter accessibility while repressing acute-phase loci **HP, SAA1/2,** and **FGG**; inflammation reduced HNF4A within 3–6 hours. Hepatic ChIP-seq datasets yielded 16,697 peaks in human hepatoblasts and 2,824 in HepG2 cells. (pqac-00000018, pqac-00000022, pqac-00000024, pqac-00000029) | HNF4A maintains differentiated hepatic programs spanning glucose, lipid, bile-acid, and xenobiotic metabolism while restraining selected inflammatory genes. A ChIP peak indicates occupancy, not automatically functional regulation. |
| Pancreatic β cells | Human HNF4A ChIP-seq produced 430 peaks in stem-cell-derived β-like cells, 1,186 in EndoC-βH1 cells, and 1,724 in human islets. Cross-dataset analysis yielded 63 shared β-cell targets and 44 high-confidence promoter-proximal targets; ChIP-qPCR plus knockdown/rescue supported direct regulation of **ACY3, HAAO, HNF1A, MAP3K11, USH1C,** and **VIL1**. (pqac-00000017, pqac-00000018, pqac-00000019) | HNF4A controls a β-cell-specific transcriptional network linked to insulin secretion, cAMP signaling, and cell architecture. Peak counts must not be confused with the smaller set of functionally validated direct targets. |
| Intestine | P1-HNF4A is enriched in differentiated luminal epithelium, whereas P2 isoforms are enriched in proliferative crypts. HNF4 factors promote enhancer accessibility and enhancer–promoter looping and cooperate with CDX2 in brush-border and lipid-absorption programs; HNF4G provides partial redundancy. (pqac-00000026, pqac-00000031, pqac-00000032) | Supports epithelial maturation, barrier function, and regeneration. Much causal evidence comes from mouse genetics; paralog and isoform compensation limits direct extrapolation to human HNF4A alone. |
| Kidney proximal tubule | HNF4A is expressed in proximal tubular epithelium, and damaging human variants can cause renal Fanconi syndrome. Developmental and loss-of-function studies associate HNF4A with solute-transport and metabolic programs, including proximal-tubule transporters. (pqac-00000003, pqac-00000006) | Human phenotype strongly supports renal relevance, but detailed target-network evidence is predominantly from mouse or organoid models; specific transporter regulation should therefore be annotated as inferred unless validated in human tissue. |
| MODY mechanism and diagnosis | Four variants in the HNF1A-binding region of the **HNF4A P2 promoter**, found in 63 individuals from 21 families, altered HNF1A binding or promoter activity. Coding variants can sharply reduce DNA binding—for example, R85W, S87N, and R89W retained 35%, 13%, and 9% binding at an HNF1A-promoter element. (pqac-00000015, pqac-00000021) | Molecular genetic testing is clinically implemented for diagnosing HNF4A-MODY/MODY1, distinguishing it from type 1 or type 2 diabetes and enabling family counseling. Promoter, isoform, and cell-context effects complicate variant classification. |
| MODY treatment evidence | A 2024 systematic review found separately extractable HNF4A treatment data in only three studies totaling 16 individuals. Some patients responded to sulfonylureas, but evidence was observational and markedly weaker than for HNF1A-diabetes. (pqac-00000033, pqac-00000035, pqac-00000039) | Genotype-informed treatment is used in practice, but HNF4A-specific comparative evidence is insufficient for strong efficacy estimates or universal treatment recommendations. |
| Direct HNF4A chemical modulation | A 480-fragment screen identified eight candidate activators and three inverse agonists; selected compounds modulated reporters or hepatocyte **FBP1**, and three bound the recombinant ligand-binding domain with micromolar affinity. Native linoleic-acid binding is reversible, yet no obligatory ligand-dependent transcriptional activation was detected. (pqac-00000011, pqac-00000014, pqac-00000034) | HNF4A is chemically tractable, but these compounds are preclinical starting points—not approved therapies or demonstrated clinical implementations. Linoleic-acid occupancy should not be equated with classical hormone-dependent activation. |
