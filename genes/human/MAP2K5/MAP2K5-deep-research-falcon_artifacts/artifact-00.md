|---|---|---|---|
| Identity | **MAP2K5** is the human gene encoding dual-specificity MAP kinase kinase 5 (**MEK5/MKK5**), corresponding to **UniProt Q13163**; the requested gene and protein are unambiguously matched. | Located at **15q23**; canonical protein reported as **448 aa**, approximately **50.1 kDa**. (pqac-00000002, pqac-00000003) | Strong database-supported identity. One secondary source reports inconsistent coordinates/length, so the UniProt-linked 448-aa annotation is preferred. (pqac-00000004) |
| Architecture | MEK5 is a STE-family MAP2K with an N-terminal **PB1 interaction domain** and a C-terminal dual-specificity protein-kinase domain. | PB1: approximately **residues 18–109**; kinase domain: approximately **166–409**. PB1 residues **18–25** support ERK5 binding, whereas **64–68** support MAP3K2/3 binding. (pqac-00000002, pqac-00000012) | Strong domain annotation plus interaction evidence; exact interaction boundaries summarized mainly from secondary literature. |
| Upstream activation | **MEKK2/MAP3K2** and **MEKK3/MAP3K3** bind MEK5 through PB1-domain interactions and activate it by phosphorylation; MEKK2 is generally considered the stronger MEK5-binding activator. | Activating sites are **Ser311 and Thr315**. PB1-complex disruption inhibits MEK5α activation. (pqac-00000001, pqac-00000006, pqac-00000016) | Strong biochemical/pathway evidence, although the dominant upstream kinase depends on stimulus and cell type. |
| Catalytic reaction and direct substrate | MEK5 transfers phosphate from ATP to both threonine and tyrosine in the ERK5 activation loop: **ATP + ERK5 → ADP + phospho-ERK5**. **ERK5/MAPK7** is the only firmly established direct physiological substrate. | MEK5 phosphorylates the ERK5 **TEY motif at Thr218 and Tyr220**; Thr218 is reported to be modified first, and dual phosphorylation is required for full ERK5 activation. (pqac-00000001, pqac-00000004) | Very strong and specific biochemical evidence. Claims about downstream transcription factors describe **ERK5 substrates**, not direct MEK5 substrates. |
| Localization | MEK5 is predominantly **cytosolic**, where it assembles with upstream MAP3Ks and ERK5. Activated ERK5—not necessarily MEK5—undergoes regulated nuclear translocation. | ERK5 contains its own reported NLS at **505–539** and NES at **771–816**; these should not be misattributed to MEK5. (pqac-00000012, pqac-00000014) | MEK5 localization evidence is less detailed than ERK5 trafficking evidence; many publications discuss the localization of the downstream kinase rather than MEK5 itself. |
| Isoforms | Alternative splicing produces multiple annotated forms; the best-studied are full-length **MEK5α** and an N-terminally truncated **MEK5β**. MEK5α efficiently activates ERK5, whereas MEK5β can behave as a dominant-negative competitor. | The shorter form lacks **89 N-terminal residues** and is approximately **40 kDa**. Both forms can co-immunoprecipitate with ERK5, but MEK5β interferes with MEK5α–ERK5 association. (pqac-00000003, pqac-00000015) | Supported by transfection and co-immunoprecipitation experiments. Historical α/β naming is inconsistent across sources and should be mapped carefully to current UniProt isoforms. |
| Physiological pathway | MEK5 is the central MAP2K in the **MEKK2/3–MEK5–ERK5** cascade, coupling mitogens and stresses to ERK5-dependent survival, differentiation and transcription. The best-established organismal context is endothelial mechanotransduction and vascular integrity. | Reported inputs include EGF, VEGF, PDGF, FGF-2, cytokines, oxidative/osmotic stress, hypoxia and fluid shear stress. (pqac-00000001, pqac-00000006, pqac-00000007) | Strong pathway-level evidence, but broad phenotypes are often inferred from ERK5 perturbation and are not always separable from direct MEK5 function. |
| Pharmacology | **BIX02188** and **BIX02189** are widely used research inhibitors of MEK5 catalytic activity and suppress ERK5 phosphorylation without inhibiting several tested MAPK-family controls. | Reported MEK5 IC50 values: **4.3 nM** for BIX02188 and **1.5 nM** for BIX02189; CSF1R is a reported off-target. (pqac-00000013, pqac-00000019) | Useful preclinical probes, not proof of clinical selectivity. Older agents such as U0126 and PD98059 are insufficiently selective; ERK5 inhibitors should not be described as MEK5 inhibitors. |
| Recent 2024 research | Current work mainly examines the broader MEK5–ERK5 pathway rather than discovering a new direct MEK5 reaction. A 2024 breast-cancer systems-pharmacology study prioritized genistein and drug combinations computationally; a major CAD Perturb-seq study recovered endothelial flow programs but did **not** report a MAP2K5-specific perturbation result. | The CAD study tested genes near GWAS loci in human aortic endothelial cells, achieved an average **40% knockdown**, identified **50 programs**, and linked **41 genes at 43 CAD signals** to endothelial programs. (pqac-00000009, pqac-00000010, pqac-00000011) | Recent findings reinforce pathway relevance but are indirect for MAP2K5. Computational drug prioritization and broader endothelial-flow signatures are hypothesis-generating, not evidence that a MEK5 therapy works in patients. |
| Clinical status | MEK5 remains a **research and preclinical therapeutic target**; no selective MEK5 inhibitor was identified as approved or as having demonstrated efficacy in a human interventional trial in the reviewed evidence. | Available disease associations include genetics, expression studies and experimental cancer models; Open Targets reports moderate association scores for several traits, approximately **0.31–0.42**, but these do not establish causality or therapeutic validation. (pqac-00000000) | Clinical evidence is weak. Cancer-expression associations, pathway inhibition in cells and genetic associations should not be equated with clinical validation, an approved indication or an actionable biomarker. |
