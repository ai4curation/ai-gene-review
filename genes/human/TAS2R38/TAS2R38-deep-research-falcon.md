# Human TAS2R38 (UniProt P59533): Functional Annotation Research Report

## Executive summary

**Identity is verified.** The requested gene is unambiguously the human **TAS2R38** gene, encoding **taste receptor type 2 member 38 (T2R38)**, also called the **PTC bitter-taste receptor**. The literature describes the same human receptor as a seven-transmembrane type-2 bitter G-protein-coupled receptor (GPCR) responsive principally to phenylthiocarbamide (PTC) and 6-n-propylthiouracil (PROP). This agrees with the supplied UniProt accession **P59533**, organism *Homo sapiens*, and TAS2R family/domain annotations (InterPro IPR007960/IPR030050; Pfam PF05296). No conflicting same-symbol protein or organism was identified. Authoritative target data likewise use the approved symbol **TAS2R38**, approved name “taste 2 receptor member 38,” and Ensembl identifier ENSG00000257138 (OpenTargets Search: -TAS2R38, lang2023activationprofileof pages 4-4, jeruzalswiatecka2020clinicalroleof pages 1-3).

T2R38 is **not an enzyme or transporter**. Its primary molecular role is ligand-activated signal transduction. In type-II taste cells, bitter ligand binding initiates a gustducin–PLCβ2–IP3/Ca²⁺–TRPM5 pathway culminating in ATP release and sensory-neuron activation. In human sinonasal ciliated epithelium, related Ca²⁺ signaling activates nitric-oxide synthase; nitric oxide directly damages bacteria and increases ciliary beating and mucociliary clearance. Thus, its best-established physiological functions are **thiourea-related bitter perception** and **rapid mucosal chemosurveillance/innate defense** (lee2015roleofthe pages 4-6, tuzim2021anupdateon pages 2-5, behrens2012immunohistochemicaldetectionof pages 1-2, jeruzalswiatecka2020clinicalroleof pages 6-8).

## 1. Gene and protein identity

The reported aliases—**T2R38**, **PTC receptor**, and historically **T2R61**—refer to the same human protein specified by P59533. Human TAS2R38 belongs to the approximately 25-member functional TAS2R bitter-receptor repertoire. TAS2Rs are compact seven-pass membrane receptors related to class-A-like GPCRs but are highly divergent from conventional aminergic GPCRs. The TAS2R/PF05296 and TAS2R38-specific InterPro domains therefore align with the receptor’s experimentally demonstrated membrane signaling function (jeruzalswiatecka2020clinicalroleof pages 1-3).

The protein is commonly discussed as a monomeric receptor, but the definitive composition and stoichiometry of bitter-receptor signaling complexes remain less firmly established than the downstream transduction pathway. Moreover, no retrieved study supplied an experimentally solved atomic structure of **TAS2R38 itself**. Residue-level binding models remain based on homology/computational modeling validated through mutagenesis and calcium signaling, rather than direct TAS2R38 cryo-EM.

## 2. Primary molecular function and ligand specificity

### 2.1 Core agonists

The most rigorously established agonists are the chemically related thiourea compounds **PTC** and **PROP**. TAS2R38 is the dominant receptor underlying sensitive human perception of both compounds. However, AVI/AVI individuals can perceive sufficiently high concentrations, and other receptors—including low-affinity TAS2R4 and population-restricted functional TAS2R2—may contribute at high stimulus levels. Accordingly, “nontaster” means markedly reduced sensitivity, not necessarily complete absence of perception (lang2023activationprofileof pages 4-4).

Additional reported agonists include thiourea- or isothiocyanate-containing dietary compounds, such as goitrin and allyl isothiocyanate. Bacterial metabolites have also been proposed as endogenous or ecological ligands. Pseudomonas acyl-homoserine lactones—including C4-HSL and 3-oxo-C12-HSL/AHL-12—activate T2R38-associated responses in primary airway cells and immune cells. Nevertheless, bacterial-ligand specificity is less settled than PTC/PROP pharmacology: one calcium-imaging study disputed direct HSL activation in its heterologous system and instead identified several volatile bacterial metabolites. Therefore, bacterial sensing should be described as strongly supported at the physiological pathway level but incompletely resolved at the direct receptor–ligand level (lee2015roleofthe pages 4-6, rudolph2025bittertastereceptors pages 3-5).

### 2.2 Structure–function evidence

Mutagenesis-supported models place the PTC/PROP pocket within the transmembrane bundle. Residues reported to contribute to recognition include **Asn103, Phe197, Trp201, and Phe264**, while residues near the pocket and activation microswitches—including Trp99, Met100, Ser259, Phe255, and Val296—affect cavity geometry or receptor activation. These conclusions derive from computational modeling tested with more than 20 mutants and functional calcium imaging; they are mechanistically informative but remain lower-resolution evidence than an experimental TAS2R38 structure.

## 3. Signal-transduction pathways

### 3.1 Canonical gustatory pathway

In type-II bitter-responsive taste cells, ligand-bound T2R38 activates heterotrimeric G proteins, prominently **gustducin**. Gβγ stimulates **PLCβ2**, which hydrolyzes PIP₂ and generates IP3. IP3 receptor activation releases Ca²⁺ from intracellular stores; Ca²⁺ activates **TRPM5**, producing membrane depolarization. ATP is then released through **CALHM1/CALHM3** channels to stimulate gustatory afferent fibers. This pathway converts extracellular bitter-compound recognition into neural perception (tuzim2021anupdateon pages 2-5, behrens2012immunohistochemicaldetectionof pages 1-2).

Direct human histology reinforces this assignment: TAS2R38-positive taste cells are a subset of PLCβ2-positive cells, consistent with type-II receptor-cell signaling. The antibody signal was distributed throughout the cell body rather than confined to the apical microvilli, indicating substantial intracellular receptor pools as well as membrane-associated protein (Behrens et al., published July 2012; https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0040304) (behrens2012immunohistochemicaldetectionof pages 1-2).

### 3.2 Sinonasal innate-defense pathway

In human sinonasal ciliated cells, receptor activation raises intracellular Ca²⁺ and activates nitric-oxide synthase. The resulting **NO** has two complementary effects:

1. direct antibacterial damage to microbial membranes, enzymes, and nucleic acids; and
2. increased ciliary beat frequency, accelerating mucociliary clearance.

Primary cultures carrying PAV/PAV generally produce more NO and exhibit stronger mucociliary and bactericidal responses than AVI/AVI or PAV/AVI cultures. Conditioned medium from wild-type *Pseudomonas aeruginosa*, but not an AHL-deficient strain, elicited the response in foundational experiments, supporting detection of bacterial population-density signals (lee2015roleofthe pages 4-6, jeruzalswiatecka2020clinicalroleof pages 6-8).

This is best viewed as a rapid chemosensory arm of innate immunity rather than a classical transcription-dependent pattern-recognition pathway. TAS2R38 can respond within minutes to microbial metabolites, complementing slower Toll-like-receptor responses. However, other airway TAS2Rs also detect bacterial metabolites, so not every bitter-evoked NO response can be assigned exclusively to T2R38.

## 4. Functional genetic variation

Three strongly linked nonsynonymous variants at amino-acid positions **49, 262, and 296** generate the common haplotypes:

- **PAV:** Pro49–Ala262–Val296; generally higher-functioning and strongly responsive to PTC/PROP.
- **AVI:** Ala49–Val262–Ile296; generally hypofunctional and associated with weak PTC/PROP sensitivity.

PAV/PAV individuals are often termed “supertasters,” PAV/AVI individuals have intermediate but broad sensitivity, and AVI/AVI individuals are conventionally called “nontasters.” Val296 appears especially important for activation, although the complete phenotype reflects all three variants, agonist concentration, receptor expression, age, sex, and ancestry (lee2015roleofthe pages 4-6, tuzim2021anupdateon pages 2-5, lang2023activationprofileof pages 4-4).

Historical estimates in Caucasian populations place AVI/AVI at approximately **30%** and PAV/PAV at approximately **20%**, but frequencies differ substantially among ancestries. In one study of 911 young adults, mean PTC bitterness ratings on a nine-point scale were 2.3, 4.9, and 5.9 for AA, AP, and PP genotypes at residue 49, respectively, illustrating the large but non-binary genotype–phenotype effect.

## 5. Cellular and tissue localization

### 5.1 Sites with direct, functionally persuasive evidence

**Taste buds.** Human TAS2R38 protein occurs in a subpopulation of PLCβ2-positive taste receptor cells. Its location is compatible with receiving luminal tastants and coupling to the type-II taste pathway, although immunostaining also detects intracellular receptor (behrens2012immunohistochemicaldetectionof pages 1-2).

**Sinonasal epithelium.** T2R38 is concentrated in motile cilia of differentiated epithelial cells. A 2023 human study localized it mainly to ciliated cells, with secretory goblet cells generally negative. This spatial organization places the receptor at the airway–environment interface, where bacterial metabolites are encountered (takemoto2023functionalalterationand pages 1-2).

**Leukocytes.** T2R38 protein and functional calcium responses have been reported in neutrophils, monocytes, and lymphocytes. In neutrophils, receptor staining occurs at the cell surface and intracellularly, including lipid-droplet-associated vesicles. Reported outputs include altered chemotaxis, CD11b, phagocytosis, bactericidal activity, and inflammatory cytokine production. These findings support an immunomodulatory role, but most evidence is ex vivo or in vitro and some antibody-based observations require caution.

### 5.2 Broader extraoral expression

Expression has been reported in gastrointestinal enteroendocrine cells, placenta, thyroid, skin, respiratory tissues, and several tumors. Human colonic TAS2R38 has been localized to subsets of CCK-, GLP-1-, and PYY-producing enteroendocrine cells, suggesting possible roles in nutrient sensing and hormone secretion (jeruzalswiatecka2020clinicalroleof pages 4-6). Placental cells respond to PTC with calcium influx that can be inhibited pharmacologically. These observations establish extraoral signaling capacity, but physiological ligands and organism-level importance are less certain than for taste and sinonasal defense.

## 6. Recent developments, 2023–2024

### 6.1 Chronic rhinosinusitis expression study, 2023

Takemoto et al. studied **36 eosinophilic CRS**, **56 non-eosinophilic CRS**, and **51 non-CRS controls**; the paper was published **24 February 2023** (https://doi.org/10.3390/ijms24054499). TAS2R38 mRNA was significantly reduced in ethmoid mucosa from non-eosinophilic CRS and in nasal polyps from eosinophilic CRS. Expression in inferior-turbinate mucosa did not differ significantly among groups. T2R38 immunoreactivity localized chiefly to ciliated cells, and non-eosinophilic CRS patients had lower oral and nasal fractional exhaled NO than controls. PAV/AVI and AVI/AVI genotypes showed only a trend toward higher CRS prevalence, so this study supports phenotype- and compartment-specific dysregulation but not a definitive genetic causal association (takemoto2023functionalalterationand pages 1-2).

### 6.2 CFTR–T2R interaction, 2023

Carey et al., published **January 2023** (https://doi.org/10.3389/fimmu.2023.1096242), found comparable bitter-receptor expression, localization, and Ca²⁺ signaling in cystic-fibrosis and non-CF nasal cells, but reduced receptor-stimulated NO, ciliary, and antibacterial responses when CFTR function was lost. VX-770/VX-809 treatment restored NO in relevant models; CFTR inhibition also reduced T2R-stimulated macrophage NO and phagocytosis. This indicates that intact CFTR supports signaling downstream of airway T2Rs. The study examined broader T2R responses and should not be interpreted as proving an exclusive TAS2R38–CFTR interaction.

### 6.3 Polish CRSwNP genetics study, 2024

A January 2024 study assessed **106 patients with CRS with nasal polyps** and **438 controls** (total **N=544**) using rs713598, rs1726866, and rs10246939 (https://doi.org/10.3390/biomedicines12010168). PAV frequency was **41% in cases versus 49% in controls**, whereas AVI was **59% versus 51%**. AVI was enriched in cases (**OR 1.43, p=0.0204**). PAV/PAV carriers were about twice as likely to have minor preoperative CT changes (**OR 2.1, p=0.0158**). AVI/AVI subjects also tended toward worse symptoms, SNOT-22 quality of life, and obstruction. These are clinically suggestive associations, but the study was preliminary and population-specific; it did not validate a diagnostic test or demonstrate causality (jeruzalswiatecka2024tas2r38bittertaste pages 2-3).

Earlier surgical cohorts similarly found depletion of PAV/PAV: **1/28 (3.6%)** and **6/70 (8.5%)** among surgical CRS patients versus approximately 20% in reference populations. In a 56-patient surgical cohort, none with Gram-negative or *P. aeruginosa* growth was PAV/PAV. Conversely, at least one Italian cohort failed to reproduce the CRS association, emphasizing population heterogeneity and the risk of small-study effects (lee2015roleofthe pages 4-6, tuzim2021anupdateon pages 8-9).

### 6.4 SARS-CoV-2/primary ciliary dyskinesia null result, 2024

Piatti et al., published **August 2024** (https://doi.org/10.3390/ijms25168635), studied **40** highly vaccinated patients with primary ciliary dyskinesia. Twelve (**30%**) reported SARS-CoV-2 infection: 10 mild and two moderate, with no severe cases or hospitalizations. Genotypes were approximately **28.6% PAV/PAV, 48.6% PAV/AVI, and 22.8% AVI/AVI**, and TAS2R38 genotype did **not** predict infection. BMI, rather than genotype, differed between infected and uninfected participants (**23.2 ± 3.3 versus 20.1 ± 4.1 kg/m²; p=0.03**). This well-defined null finding argues against overgeneralizing airway-mechanism data to COVID-19 susceptibility, although statistical power was low (piatti2024tas2r38genotypedoes pages 8-9, piatti2024tas2r38genotypedoes pages 2-4).

### 6.5 Structural progress in the broader family

In 2024, cryo-EM structures of other human TAS2Rs, particularly TAS2R14, revealed agonist-bound receptor–gustducin complexes and unusual ligand-binding modes. These structures strengthen general models of TAS2R activation and provide better templates for TAS2R38 modeling, but they are **not direct structures of P59533**. TAS2R38 pocket assignments therefore remain inferential pending an experimental structure.

## 7. Applications and real-world implementation

### Food and sensory science

PTC or PROP tasting is widely used in research and education as a low-cost phenotype reflecting TAS2R38 function. Potential food applications include predicting bitterness from cruciferous vegetables and designing bitterness blockers or formulations that improve acceptance of foods and medicines. However, food preference is polygenic and environmentally influenced; TAS2R38 genotype alone is not a reliable nutritional prescription.

### CRS risk stratification

The clearest proposed medical use is combining TAS2R38 genotype or PTC/PROP phenotype with CRS clinical data to estimate infection risk, biofilm burden, surgical requirement, or postoperative outcome. Mechanistic and association evidence is substantial, but inconsistent replication means that TAS2R38 testing is not yet a validated stand-alone diagnostic or standard-of-care prognostic test (jeruzalswiatecka2020clinicalroleof pages 6-8, lee2015roleofthe pages 4-6, tuzim2021anupdateon pages 8-9).

### Host-directed antimicrobial therapy

Topical T2R38 agonists could theoretically augment endogenous NO production, ciliary clearance, and bacterial killing without directly selecting for antibiotic resistance. This strategy is attractive for CRS, cystic fibrosis, and recurrent upper-airway infection. Major barriers include agonist selectivity, unpleasant bitterness, epithelial exposure and dosing, reduced-function AVI receptors, possible desensitization, and the absence of demonstrated clinical efficacy.

### Pharmacology and personalized medicine

Probenecid can inhibit TAS2R38 and related receptors and is useful experimentally, but it is not a selective clinical T2R38 antagonist. Dietary isothiocyanates have produced genotype-dependent immune-cell signaling at low-micromolar concentrations achievable after food exposure, suggesting a nutrition–immunity interface. These observations remain proof-of-mechanism rather than an approved personalized intervention.

## 8. Expert assessment and evidentiary limitations

The most defensible annotation is that TAS2R38 is a **ligand-gated chemosensory GPCR with two established contexts**: oral PTC/PROP detection and ciliated-airway NO-dependent defense. Leading reviews consider airway taste receptors potential alternatives or adjuncts to antibiotics, but consistently call for selective ligands, direct structural studies, larger multi-ancestry cohorts, and interventional trials (rudolph2025bittertastereceptors pages 3-5, jeruzalswiatecka2020clinicalroleof pages 6-8).

Several cautions are essential:

- **Association is not causation.** CRS genotype findings may be affected by ancestry, population structure, disease subtype, treatment history, and referral for surgery.
- **“AVI is nonfunctional” is an oversimplification.** It is hypofunctional and can support responses at high agonist concentrations (lang2023activationprofileof pages 4-4).
- **Bacterial ligands remain contested.** Direct AHL–T2R38 pharmacology varies across assay systems; solvent artifacts and activation of other TAS2Rs are possible.
- **Extraoral expression is not automatically physiological function.** Transcript or antibody signal alone is weaker evidence than receptor-specific perturbation plus downstream response.
- **Family-wide findings should not be assigned automatically to TAS2R38.** Bronchodilation, endocrine effects, and some antimicrobial-peptide pathways often involve other T2Rs.
- **No approved TAS2R38-directed therapy was identified.** Open Targets supports the receptor’s established bitter-perception association but does not establish a clinically validated disease indication (OpenTargets Search: -TAS2R38).

## Conclusion

Human **TAS2R38/P59533** is a TAS2R-family seven-transmembrane signaling receptor, not an enzyme or transporter. Its clearest agonists are PTC and PROP, with sensitivity governed strongly by the PAV/AVI coding haplotypes. In taste cells it couples to the gustducin–PLCβ2–IP3/Ca²⁺–TRPM5–CALHM pathway; in sinonasal ciliated cells it couples bitter or microbial chemical detection to NO production, enhanced ciliary beating, and antibacterial defense. Recent 2023–2024 studies reinforce ciliated-cell localization and associations with CRS while also supplying important null evidence for SARS-CoV-2 susceptibility. The receptor is a compelling host-defense and precision-medicine candidate, but clinical implementation remains investigational because ligand specificity, population reproducibility, and therapeutic efficacy are unresolved.
