| Identity and family | Human **TAS2R38** encodes T2R38, the PTC-responsive bitter-taste receptor corresponding to **UniProt P59533**. Its seven-transmembrane topology and TAS2R/PF05296 annotation agree with literature identifying it as a type-2 bitter GPCR. No gene-symbol or organism ambiguity was found. | Human receptor pharmacology, immunohistochemistry, and authoritative reviews (pqac-00000006, pqac-00000008) | **High.** The accession comes from the supplied UniProt record; literature independently confirms the matching human receptor, ligands, and family. |
| Primary ligands and specificity | The best-established synthetic agonists are **phenylthiocarbamide (PTC)** and **6-n-propylthiouracil (PROP)**. TAS2R38 is their dominant high-sensitivity receptor, although AVI homozygotes may perceive high concentrations and other receptors may contribute at low affinity. Mutagenesis-supported models implicate residues including N103, F197, W201, and F264 in recognition. | Heterologous calcium assays, psychophysics, molecular modeling, and site-directed mutagenesis (pqac-00000003) | **High for PTC/PROP agonism; moderate for atomic binding details.** The cited residue-level model is not an experimental TAS2R38 structure. |
| PAV and AVI haplotypes | Three linked coding variants at residues **49, 262, and 296** generate common **PAV** and **AVI** haplotypes. PAV/PAV usually confers strong PTC/PROP sensitivity, PAV/AVI an intermediate variable response, and AVI/AVI weak sensitivity. AVI is hypofunctional rather than invariably silent, and loss of Val296 is particularly disruptive. | Genotype–phenotype studies, recombinant receptor assays, and primary sinonasal cultures (pqac-00000001, pqac-00000002, pqac-00000003) | **High.** Concentration and non-genetic factors modify phenotype. Historical frequencies vary by ancestry; approximately 30% AVI/AVI and 20% PAV/PAV were reported in Caucasian populations. |
| Canonical taste signaling | Ligand-bound T2R38 activates gustducin-family G proteins. Gβγ activates **PLCβ2**, causing IP3-dependent Ca²⁺ release; Ca²⁺ activates **TRPM5**, depolarizing type-II taste cells, followed by ATP release through **CALHM1/CALHM3** to gustatory afferents. | Established TAS2R pathway biology plus TAS2R38 colocalization with PLCβ2-positive human taste cells (pqac-00000002, pqac-00000008) | **High for the type-II bitter-cell pathway.** Some steps are established across TAS2Rs rather than separately demonstrated for TAS2R38 in every experiment. |
| Gustatory localization | TAS2R38 protein occurs in a subpopulation of **PLCβ2-positive human taste-bud cells**. Immunostaining extended across the cell body rather than being restricted to the plasma membrane or strongly enriched in apical microvilli. | Antibody validation, Western blotting, and double-label immunohistochemistry; Behrens et al., July 2012 (pqac-00000008) | **High for tissue and cell-type localization.** Intracellular staining means that not all detected receptor can be assumed to be signaling-competent surface protein. |
| Sinonasal cilia and NO defense | T2R38 is localized mainly to **ciliated sinonasal epithelial cells**, generally not goblet cells. Activation raises intracellular Ca²⁺ and stimulates nitric-oxide synthase; NO has direct antibacterial activity and accelerates ciliary beating and mucociliary clearance. PAV/PAV cultures generally show stronger NO, clearance, and bacterial killing than AVI-containing cultures. | Human immunostaining, primary air–liquid-interface cultures, live-cell signaling, microbiology, and genotype comparisons (pqac-00000001, pqac-00000009, pqac-00000013) | **Moderate–high.** PTC responses are receptor-specific, but attribution of all bacterial acyl-homoserine-lactone responses to T2R38 remains debated; other T2Rs also contribute to airway defense. |
| Immune-cell evidence | Functional T2R38 has been reported in human leukocyte populations. Bitter or bacterial ligands can evoke PLCβ2-dependent Ca²⁺ signals and alter antimicrobial or inflammatory outputs; oral-cell evidence also links functional PAV/PAV receptors to stronger responses than AVI/AVI receptors. | Human immune- and oral-cell imaging, calcium-flux assays, ligand perturbation, and cytokine or antimicrobial measurements (pqac-00000005) | **Moderate.** Evidence is predominantly ex vivo or in vitro; outcomes vary by cell type and ligand, and antibody specificity or agonistic effects can complicate interpretation. |
| CRS expression, 2023 | Takemoto et al., published **24 February 2023**, studied 36 eosinophilic CRS cases, 56 non-eosinophilic CRS cases, and 51 controls. TAS2R38 mRNA was reduced in non-eosinophilic CRS ethmoid mucosa and eosinophilic CRS nasal polyps; inferior-turbinate expression did not differ. Non-eosinophilic CRS also showed lower oral and nasal FeNO. | Human case–control study using RT-PCR, immunostaining, genotyping, and FeNO measurements (pqac-00000009) | **Moderate.** Expression and localization were measured directly, but available results lack fold changes; genotype differences were trends, and causality cannot be inferred. |
| CFTR interaction, 2023 | Carey et al., **January 2023**, found similar T2R expression, localization, and Ca²⁺ signaling in CF and non-CF nasal cells, but lower T2R-stimulated NO and weaker ciliary and antibacterial responses in CF cells. VX-809/VX-770 restored NO, while CFTR inhibition reduced macrophage NO and phagocytosis. | Primary nasal cultures and macrophages, CRISPR-modified bronchial cells, live-cell imaging, and pharmacological rescue | **Moderate for the broader T2R pathway; limited for TAS2R38 specifically.** The work examined multiple T2R agonists or isoforms rather than an exclusive TAS2R38–CFTR interaction. |
| Polish CRSwNP study, 2024 | Jeruzal-Świątecka et al., **January 2024**, compared 106 CRSwNP cases with 438 controls. PAV frequency was 41% in cases versus 49% in controls, while AVI was 59% versus 51%. AVI was enriched among cases with **OR 1.43, p=0.0204**; PAV/PAV carriers were more likely to have minor preoperative CT changes with **OR 2.1, p=0.0158**. | Human genetic association study of rs713598, rs1726866, and rs10246939 with clinical, endoscopic, CT, VAS, and SNOT-22 phenotyping (pqac-00000018) | **Moderate–low for clinical prediction.** This was a preliminary, single-population association study; association does not prove receptor-mediated causality or establish a validated diagnostic test. |
| PCD/COVID null result, 2024 | Piatti et al., **August 2024**, studied 40 highly vaccinated patients with primary ciliary dyskinesia; 12, or 30%, reported SARS-CoV-2 infection, including 10 mild and 2 moderate cases with no severe outcomes. Diplotypes were 28.6% PAV/PAV, 48.6% PAV/AVI, and 22.8% AVI/AVI, but genotype did **not** predict infection. BMI differed between infected and uninfected groups: 23.2 ± 3.3 versus 20.1 ± 4.1 kg/m², **p=0.03**. | Small observational genotype–phenotype study using patient-reported infection and symptom outcomes (pqac-00000014, pqac-00000015) | **Low–moderate.** The negative result is informative, but power was limited by the small cohort, only 12 infections, extensive vaccination, and absence of severe disease. |
| Translational status | Proposed applications include TAS2R38 genotype or taste phenotype for CRS risk stratification and topical agonism to augment endogenous NO-dependent mucosal defense. No retrieved evidence establishes an approved TAS2R38-directed therapy or prospectively validated clinical test. | Clinical association cohorts, ex vivo airway models, expert reviews, and target-database evidence (pqac-00000000, pqac-00000011, pqac-00000013) | **Moderate biological rationale; low clinical readiness.** Population heterogeneity, non-replication, uncertain ligand selectivity, and absence of interventional efficacy trials remain major barriers. |
