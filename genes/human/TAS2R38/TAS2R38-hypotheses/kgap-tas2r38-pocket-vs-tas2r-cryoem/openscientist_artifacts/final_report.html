Summary

Verdict: partially supported.

The seed hypothesis makes three claims. The fold claim is confirmed. The pocket claim is right about location and wrong about membership. The haplotype claim is right for two residues out of three and refuted for the third.

The TAS2R38 AlphaFold model AF-P59533-F1-model_v6 superposes on the agonist-bound cryo-EM structures at TM-score 0.763 (TAS2R46, PDB 7XP6, strychnine), 0.708 (TAS2R14, PDB 8XQO, cholesterol plus aristolochic acid A) and 0.737 (TAS2R14, PDB 8RQL, two flufenamic acid copies), with core Cα RMSD 2.35-2.65 Å. Transferring the template orthosteric contact shells by structural equivalence gives a 24-residue TAS2R38 set that contains N103, F197, W201 and A263, and an independent cavity probe finds a buried pocket whose centroid lies 0.9 Å from the transferred agonist position, lined by nine residues of that same set. Two methods with no shared assumptions agree on where the pocket is.

Pocket membership is narrower than the hypothesis states. Only the TM3 arm of the published mutagenesis set actually reaches the mapped site: W99 comes within 2.44 Å of the superposed agonist, M100 within 3.75 Å and N103 within 4.83-7.41 Å. F197 and W201 sit 10.38-13.41 Å above it, in the extracellular vestibule. The six named residues do form one connected component at an 8 Å cutoff, but the component spans about 12 Å, which is more than a single phenylthiocarbamide molecule can bridge. "One contiguous pocket matching the template orthosteric site" overstates what the geometry shows.

The haplotype claim splits. A49 is 32.54-34.84 Å from all three superposed orthosteric ligands and 8.27 Å from the modelled Gα α5 helix. I296 is 15.89-22.59 Å from the orthosteric ligands, 5.12 Å from the largest intracellular cavity and 6.35 Å from α5. Both are at the coupling face, as the hypothesis proposed. A262 is not: it approaches the superposed orthosteric ligand to 2.12 Å in the 8XQO frame and 5.88 Å in the 7XP6 frame, and sits 23.41 Å from α5. An unrelated method reaches the same placement: PMID: 22656649 built SuperBiHelix models of the four haplotypes and found residue 262 inside an interhelical hydrogen-bond network present in tasters and absent in AVI, with PTC and PROP hydrogen-bonding to the 262 backbone.

Three caveats dominate the reading. The model's accuracy is inverted relative to what the hypothesis needs: its intracellular half matches the templates to 1.64-1.94 Å mean Cα deviation while the pocket-forming extracellular half deviates 4.01-6.16 Å and ECL2 (residues 145-174) by 8.86-14.32 Å at mean pLDDT 68.6. The templates undercut the premise that the extracellular site binds the tastant: in both TAS2R14 structures that pocket holds cholesterol while the bitter agonists bind intracellularly. And the apo model's cavity at the mapped site is 34 Å³, about one fifth the volume of the intracellular cavity in the same model and too small to hold PTC. The model supports the location of a pocket; it does not resolve the pocket.

Key findings

F001. The mutagenesis-defined pocket maps onto the cryo-EM orthosteric site

A TM-align-style structural superposition (iterative dynamic programming on a TM-score similarity matrix with Kabsch fitting and 5 Å outlier trimming, written from scratch because Biopython was unavailable) placed AF-P59533-F1-model_v6 on three cryo-EM receptor chains. TAS2R46 chain R of 7XP6: TM-score 0.763, 283 aligned pairs, core Cα RMSD 2.65 Å. TAS2R14 chain R of 8XQO: TM-score 0.707, 281 pairs, 2.35 Å. TAS2R14 chain R of 8RQL: TM-score 0.737, 278 pairs, 2.40 Å. Global Needleman-Wunsch identity is 32.2% (TAS2R46) and 32.0% (TAS2R14).

Taking every template residue with a heavy atom within 4.5 Å of the extracellular ligand and transferring it by structural equivalence gives a union of 24 TAS2R38 residues: L71, S74, E86, Y92, I95, I96, W99, N103, Q185, L189, S194, F197, C198, W201, F256, S260, A263, S266, L270, V279, C282, V283, I285, M286. This set contains three of the four residues named in the MM/CG study (PMID: 23741366, which states "Our calculations suggest that both agonists bind to Asn103, Phe197, Phe264 and Trp201") plus W99, and A263 is adjacent to F264.

Minimum heavy-atom distances from the superposed model to the bound agonist, in the order strychnine / cholesterol / flufenamic acid: W99 2.44 / 2.64 / 3.55 Å; M100 3.75 / 5.53 / 6.38 Å; N103 7.41 / 4.83 / 8.60 Å; F264 6.68 / 3.84 / 9.22 Å; F197 10.38 / 12.94 / 14.53 Å; W201 13.41 / 13.24 / 16.50 Å. W99 is the structural equivalent of the TAS2R46 W88 and TAS2R14 W89 toggle tryptophan and makes the closest approach of any residue tested. Independent mutagenesis agrees that W99 matters functionally: PMID: 20811630 reports "W99 and M100, along with F255 and V296, play a key role for receptor activation".

F002 and F008. A262 is inside the orthosteric pocket region

The hypothesis treats all three haplotype residues as non-pocket. The measurements separate them. A49 is 34.07 / 32.54 / 34.84 Å from the three superposed orthosteric ligands, 19.10 Å from the intracellular ligand site and 8.27 Å from the Gα α5 C-terminal six residues. I296 is 21.92 / 15.89 / 22.59 Å from the orthosteric ligands, 5.44 Å from the intracellular ligand and 6.35 Å from α5. A262 is 5.88 / 2.12 / 7.91 Å from the orthosteric ligands, 15.42 Å from the intracellular ligand and 23.41 Å from α5, with pLDDT 94.9, the highest of the eleven residues measured. It sits on TM6 between the two pocket-proximal residues S259 (8.80 / 1.70 / 9.93 Å) and F264 (6.68 / 3.84 / 9.22 Å), and its neighbour A263 is in the 24-residue orthosteric union.

Two independent lines agree. The SuperBiHelix prediction in PMID: 22656649 states "We find that residue 262 (2nd position in the polymorphism) is involved in the interhelical hydrogen bond network stabilizing the GPCR structure in tasters (hTAS2R38PAV, hTAS2R38AAI, and hTAS2R38PVV), while it is not in the nontaster (hTAS2R38AVI)". That method is template-free Monte Carlo helix packing from 2012; this one is cryo-EM-templated superposition from 2026. They share no assumptions and place 262 at the same site. Functionally, all three SNPs carry independent effects: PMID: 23632915 reports "In vitro assays revealed a continuous range of response across alleles, and associations with threshold were significant for all single nucleotide polymorphisms (P < 0.002)", so 262 is not functionally silent.

The two residues the hypothesis placed correctly are also the two with independent functional support for an indirect mechanism. PMID: 19675003 finds "Amino acid 49 has the major effect on the distinction between tasters and non-tasters of all three variants", yet 49 sits 32-35 Å from the orthosteric ligand, so its large phenotypic effect has to be indirect. PMID: 20811630 assigns V296 an activation role rather than a binding role, matching its measured position 6.35 Å from α5.

F003. The largest cavity in the apo model is the intracellular site

Grid-based cavity detection on the model (1 Å grid, residues with pLDDT ≥ 70, probe-accessible points more than 3.3 Å from any heavy atom, buriedness ≥ 24 of 26 ray directions, single-linkage clustering at 1.5 Å) found 22856 interior candidate points, 361 buried points and 28 clusters, the three largest at 176, 54 and 27 points. The largest cavity, about 176 Å³, is lined by S51, S53, D54, L113, Y117, C118, K120, L216, S219, L220, E240, A241, H242, K244, A245, S248, L249, F252, H293, L297, G300, N301, A302, K303 and R305. Its closest approach to the superposed orthosteric ligands is 17.9 Å (cholesterol), 23.0 Å (strychnine) and 23.9 Å (flufenamic acid). It overlaps the TAS2R38 equivalents of the TAS2R14 intracellular ligand site, and I296 is 5.12 Å from it.

This matters because it means cavity detection on the apo model alone would mislocate the ligand site. The orthosteric assignment in this report holds only because the model was superposed on agonist-bound structures and each ligand copy was classified by its distance to Gα. In 8XQO the aristolochic acid A copy is 3.4 Å from Gα and the cholesterol copy 23.7 Å; in 8RQL the two flufenamic acid copies are 3.1 Å and 30.7 Å from Gα. Superposing 7XP6 chain R on 8XQO chain R (TM-score 0.839, 268 pairs, RMSD 1.90 Å) puts the strychnine centroid 25.2 Å from the aristolochic acid centroid with zero contact-residue overlap, and instead coincident with the cholesterol site. A curator mapping pocket residues from a TAS2R14 entry without checking which ligand copy is extracellular would transfer the wrong site. PMID: 42906028 confirms the two-site arrangement independently: "Recent cryo-EM structures revealed that the human bitter taste receptor TAS2R14 contains not only the canonical extracellular binding site, but also a novel intracellular site".

F004. Residue-level transfers carry a register uncertainty of one to eight residues

Comparing the sequence-derived residue mapping with the structure-derived mapping shows agreement of 205/278 (74%) for 7XP6, 201/272 (74%) for 8XQO and 214/271 (79%) for 8RQL. Among the disagreements the commonest offsets are +5 (28 residues) and +3 (14) against 7XP6, and +1 (36) and +8 (19) against 8XQO, concentrated in loops and helix ends. At 32% identity, specific residue numbers quoted from a pre-structure homology model should be treated as approximate.

The geometry adds a second constraint. W99 to W201 is 11.6 Å in the model, which exceeds the longest dimension of phenylthiocarbamide. One small thiourea agonist cannot contact W99, N103, F197, W201 and F264 at the same time. The mutagenesis set more plausibly reports a binding plus activation pathway spanning the pocket and its vestibule than a single contact shell. The MM/CG paper itself notes the problem it was working around: PMID: 23741366 states "The lack of atomic resolution structure for any of them is complicating an in depth understanding of the molecular mechanisms underlying bitter taste perception".

F005. The orthosteric cavity exists in the model but is partly collapsed

Re-running the cavity detection with the search box centred on the transferred agonist position found a cluster of 34 Å³ whose centroid is 0.9 Å from the transferred agonist centroid. The result was identical at buriedness cutoffs of 20, 22 and 24 of 26 rays, so it is not a threshold artefact. Lining residues: L70, L71, S74, Y92, I95, I96, W99, S266, V267, L270, V279, V283, M286. Nine of these 13 are in the independently derived 24-residue orthosteric union, so the template transfer and the cavity detection converge without being told to.

Of the residues named in the hypothesis, only W99 lines this cavity, at 3.03 Å. Distances from the cavity: M100 5.50, A262 6.02, N103 7.65, F264 7.67, F197 12.73, W201 15.55, I296 20.67 and A49 33.73 Å. At 34 Å³ the pocket is too small for PTC, which is the expected signature of a ligand-free, non-activated model whose transmembrane bundle has relaxed onto the agonist site. The conservative reading: the model supports the location of the pocket but cannot by itself enumerate pocket-lining residues. N103, A262 and F264 at 6-8 Å are consistent with second-shell or pocket-wall positions that would be exposed on pocket opening; F197 and W201 at 12.7 and 15.6 Å are too far to line the agonist site under any plausible opening.

The two primary TAS2R14 papers explain why the unrestricted scan returns the intracellular site. PMID: 38776963 reports "Uniquely, a cholesterol molecule is observed occupying what is typically an orthosteric site in class A G-protein-coupled receptors. The three potent agonists bind, individually, to the intracellular pockets, suggesting a distinct activation mechanism for this receptor". PMID: 38600377 adds that "the orthosteric pocket is connected to the allosteric site via an elongated cavity", which matches the single large interior cavity found here.

The two sites carry opposite evolutionary signals. The intracellular pocket that contacts the Gα α5 helix is the conserved, shared part of the family, which is what a surface every TAS2R must use to couple gustducin should look like. PMID: 38600377 reports the physical basis for this, "including a direct interaction with the α5 helix of Ggust and Gi1". The extracellular orthosteric pocket is as variable as an average receptor position, which is what ligand-specificity divergence across 25 receptors with different agonist repertoires predicts.

Two orthosteric positions escape the variability: W99 (identity 0.81, W in 22 of 27 paralogs) and N103 (0.81, N in 22 of 27). Their conservation marks them as family-level activation machinery, which weakens the inference from mutagenesis at N103. Loss of signal at a family-conserved toggle position is as consistent with a broken activation switch as with lost PTC contact. F197 (0.22) and W201 (0.15) are variable and therefore better candidates for specificity determinants, but the model places them 10-16 Å above the bound agonist.

Position 296 is well fixed across the family at I (21 of 27), and the taster allele V296 appears in 1 of 27. The non-taster I296 is the family consensus. Since 296 sits 5.12 Å from the intracellular cavity and 6.35 Å from the modelled α5 helix, the taster allele introduces a non-consensus residue into the conserved coupling region. Position 49 is unusual in a different way: the family carries a basic residue (K or R) in 20 of 26 paralogs, and both TAS2R38 alleles are foreign to it, so no family-level functional inference is available. Position 262 is permissive, which is consistent with a pocket-wall position that tolerates substitution.

The UniProt canonical P59533 sequence is A49/A262/I296, the AAI haplotype, so the AlphaFold model carries one taster-associated residue and two non-taster-associated residues. No haplotype-specific structural conclusion can be drawn from it without that caveat.

The part of the model the hypothesis relies on most is the part the model gets least right. The haplotype assignment at the intracellular face rests on the best-determined region: A49 and I296 are placed within 1.76-4.15 Å of their template equivalents and both templates agree. The pocket mapping rests on the worst region: F197 and W201 move 9.70 and 10.11 Å between template frames, next to an ECL2 misplaced by 8.86-14.32 Å at pLDDT 68.6. Mechanistic model and interpretation

The picture that holds all nine findings together is a receptor with two functionally distinct faces, where the three haplotype substitutions do not all act on the same one.

Reading the diagram: solid arrows are supported by direct measurement in this work or by the cited primary structures; dashed arrows are inferences or observations made in TAS2R14 that have no TAS2R38 equivalent.

The conservation result gives the two faces opposite evolutionary signatures. The intracellular surface is the shared, constrained machinery (identity 0.673 versus 0.254 background, p = 2.7e-08) because every TAS2R has to couple gustducin through it. The orthosteric pocket is unconstrained at family level (0.261, p = 0.39 versus background) because 25 receptors with different agonist repertoires need different pockets. Within that variable pocket, W99 and N103 stay fixed at 0.81, which marks them as activation machinery rather than specificity determinants. That distinction changes how a mutagenesis phenotype at N103 should be read.

Placed on this map, the three haplotype substitutions are mechanistically heterogeneous. A49, which has "the major effect on the distinction between tasters and non-tasters of all three variants" (PMID: 19675003), sits 32-35 Å from the agonist and 8.27 Å from α5, so its effect has to be transmitted through coupling, folding or surface delivery. V296I sits 6.35 Å from α5 at a position the family fixes at I, so the taster allele is the divergent state at a conserved coupling position, and mutagenesis already assigns V296 an activation role (PMID: 20811630). A262V sits on the pocket wall, 23.41 Å from α5, so it is positioned to change agonist binding directly.

The one premise that remains unsettled is which site binds PTC. In TAS2R46 the agonist strychnine occupies the extracellular pocket. In TAS2R14 the same pocket holds cholesterol and the tastants bind intracellularly. TAS2R38 could follow either. Nothing available settles it.

How the literature divides

Six papers support the mapping or the method: the TAS2R46 structure (PMID: 36108005), the MM/CG pocket prediction (PMID: 23741366), the W99 and M100 mutagenesis (PMID: 20811630), the haplotype functional assays (PMID: 15723792, PMID: 23632915) and the Neanderthal analysis identifying position 49 as dominant (PMID: 19675003).

One paper refutes a specific claim in the seed hypothesis (PMID: 22656649), and a second, weaker computational study reaches a concordant conclusion about position 262 (PMID: 39970077) while disagreeing about the pocket location.

Four papers qualify the premise. The two TAS2R14 cryo-EM papers (PMID: 38776963, PMID: 38600377) and the antagonist mutagenesis (PMID: 41912874) show that at least one TAS2R binds its tastants intracellularly, and the earlier predicted TAS2R14 structure (PMID: 37096045) is a worked example of a confidently predicted single pocket that the later structures did not confirm.

Two papers point to mechanisms outside the receptor's pocket entirely. The second-receptor result (PMID: 38732607) shows TAS2R38 haplotype explains about 29% of PROP bitterness variation in 243 people and TAS2R4 another 7-8%. The expression result (PMID: 24025627) ties allele-specific transcript abundance to bitterness in 18-22 heterozygotes.

Two comparative papers (PMID: 16612383, PMID: 20665225) establish that non-taster status arose independently in chimpanzee and macaque by different mechanisms, so non-human PTC data cannot be used to argue about these residues.

Mechanistic scope

The direct gene-product activity at issue is agonist recognition by a single seven-transmembrane receptor: which TAS2R38 side chains contact PTC and PROP, and whether the three haplotype substitutions change that contact or change something downstream of it.

What this analysis addresses directly is geometry in a predicted structure: distances between modelled TAS2R38 atoms and ligand atoms borrowed from other receptors by superposition, cavity volumes in an apo model, and per-position conservation across paralogs. These are computational measurements on coordinates, not measurements on TAS2R38.

What must not be read into it: the PAV versus AVI difference in PTC and PROP response measured in heterologous cells and human panels (PMID: 15723792) is a receptor-level phenotype whose mechanism could be agonist affinity, G-protein coupling efficiency, receptor folding, surface delivery or transcript abundance. PMID: 24025627 shows allele-specific PAV-TAS2R38 mRNA abundance in human fungiform papillae correlates with PROP bitterness, an expression-level contribution no structural model can capture. PMID: 38732607 shows the TAS2R38 haplotype accounts for about 29% of PROP bitterness variation and TAS2R4 for another 7-8%, so even the sensory phenotype is not a pure readout of this receptor. Everything further downstream (vegetable intake, postprandial glycaemia, rhinosinusitis susceptibility, Alzheimer biomarkers) is several steps removed and should not be used to argue about pocket residues.

Conflicts and alternatives

The premise is the strongest conflict. The hypothesis assumes the extracellular pocket of a TAS2R is the tastant site. Both TAS2R14 cryo-EM papers found cholesterol in that pocket and the bitter tastants at an intracellular site contacting the Gα α5 helix. The measurement here agrees: in 8XQO, aristolochic acid A is 3.4 Å from Gα and 25.2 Å from where strychnine sits in the superposed TAS2R46; in 8RQL one flufenamic acid copy is 3.1 Å from Gα and the other 30.7 Å. If TAS2R38 follows TAS2R14, the site mapped here is the wrong site for PTC. If it follows TAS2R46, where strychnine occupies the extracellular pocket, it is the right one. Nothing available settles which.

A competing computational result exists on the TAS2R14 side. PMID: 37096045 predicted a single binding pocket accommodating five diverse TAS2R14 agonists, which the later cryo-EM work does not support. That history is a direct caution about trusting any single predicted pocket, including this one.

A competing TAS2R38 model reaches a different pocket. PMID: 39970077 used I-TASSER threading plus 100 ns MD and reported PROP binding "to the allosteric hydrophobic pocket of hTAS2R38" with a hydrogen bond to ASN190, a position that is not in the 24-residue orthosteric union derived here. That study does agree with this work on 262, stating that a triple mutation makes "the 262nd position critical to the binding pocket". Two models disagreeing about the pocket while agreeing about 262 is informative in both directions.

For residue 262 the seed hypothesis is the outlier. Two unrelated methods, the 2012 Monte Carlo helix-packing prediction and the cryo-EM-templated superposition done here, independently place 262 at the agonist site.

Template dependence is a real alternative explanation for parts of the mapping. F197, W201, F255, S259 and A262 all shift by 5.88-10.11 Å between the 7XP6 and 8XQO frames. Any statement about these residues that holds in one frame and not the other should be treated as unresolved.

The AlphaFold model is not either common haplotype. UniProt canonical P59533 is A49/A262/I296, the AAI haplotype, so the model carries one taster-associated residue (A262 of PAV) and two non-taster-associated residues. Any haplotype-specific structural conclusion drawn from this single model inherits that mismatch. The AAI haplotype is itself rare in population data: in 2047 Japanese ToMMo subjects, AAI appears only in the AAI/PAV and AVI/AAI combinations at 0.10% and 0.05% (PMID: 37940575).

Species carry-over is a further risk. PMID: 16612383 shows chimpanzee PTC non-taster status arises from an initiation-codon mutation, not from the human three-SNP haplotype, and PMID: 20665225 shows Japanese macaque non-tasters arose independently again. Non-human PTC data cannot be used to argue about these residue positions.

Limitations and knowledge gaps

No experimental structure of TAS2R38 exists. The RCSB Search API v2 query on UniProt P59533 returned HTTP 204 with an empty body. Every structural statement in this report derives from one AlphaFold model superposed on three cryo-EM entries.

The tooling is bespoke. Biopython was not available in the execution environment, so the PDB parser, the Needleman-Wunsch alignment and the TM-align-style superposition were written from scratch for this job. They approximate the standard tools. TM-scores, RMSDs and mapped residue sets should be regenerated with TM-align and MAFFT before any of this enters a curation record. The cavity thresholds (probe radius, buriedness cutoff, clustering distance) are mine, and while the focused-probe result was stable across three buriedness cutoffs, the absolute volumes should not be compared to values from other software. The conservation analysis used pairwise rather than multiple alignment, which is adequate for per-position identity counts but not for detecting coevolution. No molecular dynamics, no docking and no experimental validation were performed.

Which site binds PTC in TAS2R38. Checked: ligand positions in 7XP6, 8XQO and 8RQL measured directly against Gα, plus both primary TAS2R14 papers. Matters because the entire pocket mapping is anchored to the extracellular site, and in TAS2R14 that site holds cholesterol. Resolved by a TAS2R38 structure with PTC or PROP bound, or by mutagenesis at intracellular-site positions testing whether they alter PTC response.

Whether the modelled pocket opens. Checked: cavity detection at three buriedness cutoffs, focused and unrestricted. The apo cavity is 34 Å³, too small for PTC. Matters because residue membership of a closed pocket is unreliable, which is why N103, A262 and F264 at 6-8 Å cannot be classified as lining or not. Resolved by molecular dynamics with PTC docked, or by a holo structure.

Whether F197 and W201 contact the agonist at all. Checked: both are 10.38-13.41 Å from the superposed agonists, deviate 9.70-10.11 Å between template frames, sit next to an ECL2 misplaced by 8.86-14.32 Å, and are poorly conserved (0.22 and 0.15). Matters because they are named in the hypothesis as pocket residues. Resolved by testing whether F197 and W201 mutants lose PTC response specifically rather than losing expression or general signalling.

What A262V actually does. Checked: structural placement at the pocket wall, family permissiveness at the position, and the concordant predictions in PMID: 22656649 and PMID: 39970077. Matters because the mechanistic story for the PAV/AVI difference differs completely depending on whether 262 is a binding or a coupling residue. Resolved by comparing agonist EC50 and maximal response between A262V single mutants on a fixed background with surface expression controlled.

Haplotype background of the model. Checked: UniProt canonical is AAI. Matters because no haplotype-specific structural claim can be made from a model of a third haplotype. Resolved by predicting or determining PAV and AVI structures separately.

Contribution of expression versus protein function. Checked: PMID: 24025627 reports allele-specific transcript abundance correlating with bitterness in 18-22 heterozygotes. Matters because a transcript-level effect would make the residue-level debate partly moot for the sensory phenotype. Resolved by measuring surface receptor density per haplotype alongside response amplitude.

Proposed follow-up experiments and actions

  1. Cryo-EM or crystallography of TAS2R38-PAV with PTC or PROP bound, in complex with mini-Ggust. This settles the site question, the pocket membership question and the A262 question in one experiment, and it is the only item that would convert any of these leads into experimental evidence.
  2. Mutagenesis with surface expression controlled. Alanine substitution at W99, M100, N103, A262 and F264 (predicted pocket) against F197 and W201 (predicted vestibule), measuring PTC and PROP EC50 and maximal response in a calcium assay. Prediction from this work: the TM3 set and 262 shift affinity, while 197 and 201 do not, or affect maximal response instead.
  3. A262 swap experiments on a fixed haplotype background. A262V and A262S (S is a common family residue at this position, 9 of 26 paralogs) on both PAV and AVI backgrounds. If 262 is a binding residue, the two substitutions should differ in agonist potency; if it is a packing residue, they should differ mainly in expression or constitutive activity.
  4. Test whether TAS2R38 has a functional intracellular site. Assay PTC and PROP against TAS2R38 mutants at the intracellular-cavity positions identified here (S51, S53, D54, Y117, K120, E240, H242, K244, H293, N301, K303, R305), and test whether known TAS2R14 intracellular ligands modulate TAS2R38. PMID: 41912874 provides the mutational template for this design in TAS2R14.
  5. Chimeric receptors. Transplant the TAS2R38 TM3 segment (residues 95-105) into TAS2R46 and ask whether PTC responsiveness transfers. This tests whether the TM3 arm of the mapped pocket is sufficient for thiourea recognition.
  6. Repeat the structural mapping with AlphaFold3 or Boltz predictions of TAS2R38-PAV with PTC supplied as a ligand, and with a proper TM-align and MAFFT run, as an inexpensive check on the geometry reported here.
