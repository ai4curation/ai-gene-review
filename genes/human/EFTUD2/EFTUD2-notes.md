# EFTUD2 evidence notes

EFTUD2/Snu114 is the GTP-binding U5 snRNP subunit that helps organize major and minor spliceosomal machinery. Two complementary binding activities are represented: human GTP binding and conserved U5 snRNA binding. The protein is predominantly nuclear, with a smaller cytoplasmic pool during U5 assembly. The reviewed UniProt record associates pathogenic variants with mandibulofacial dysostosis with microcephaly. The three current products are 972, 937 and 962 residues; their official identifiers and sequence notes are retained without assuming equivalent experimental activity.

Native human U5 photo-crosslinking, nucleotide competition and dissociating immunoprecipitation establish GTP binding (PMID:9233818). The same study's yeast experiments are distinct from its human antibody-inhibition and localization results. Native human tri-snRNP architecture places EFTUD2 with PRPF8 and other U5 proteins (PMID:26912367). Neither paper measures human EFTUD2 GTP hydrolysis. Purified yeast Snu114 72–1008 does have weak activity, with reported Km 578.4 micromolar and kcat 1.012 × 10^-3 s^-1, suppressed by the Prp8 binding region (PMID:32196113). That finding supports retained inherited capacity while leaving an operative human turnover cycle unresolved. It is not evidence for an autonomous human motor or universal absence of activity.

The literal PAINT path distinguishes ancestral PTN000562745 from the spliceosomal PTN000562846 branch. Translation-function losses do not negate the inherited GTPase term. Corrected-and-republished PMID:9848662 supports yeast U5 RNA contact; human RNA-interactome capture provides independent broad RNA-binding evidence (PMID:22658674; PMID:22681889). The latter's separately available target evidence is an author thesis, not a publisher supplement.

Three source-specific structural refinements preserve the imported citations and identify the measured stages: PMID:29361316 Bact corresponds to U2-type catalytic step1, PMID:35705093 pre-C*/C* to U2-type catalytic step2, and PMID:33509932 minor Bact to U12-type catalytic step1. Current definitions and deposited human Q15029 entities support these distinctions. The three sources were assessed from complete abstracts plus deposited model metadata, without a full-body read claim. Deposited sequence identity is not proof that every residue was modeled or every modern product tested.

The interaction review distinguishes Y2H fragments, endogenous/tagged affinity purifications, aggregate cap-binding-complex association and proximity labeling. Positive associations with independently established enzymes, DNA-binding transcription factors, HSP27 or a protein complex support the corresponding partner-class terms. These refinements do not confer the partner's activity on EFTUD2. PRPF8's spliceosomal scaffold role does not satisfy the signaling-specific scaffold-binding definition. The SNRNP40 association in PMID:9774689 is complex-mediated; its negative overlay result is preserved alongside the positive assembly. Under the explicit CLINGEN_MENDELIAN project instruction, supported generic binding without an informative justified replacement is retained as non-core. No new native-physiology or purified-interface prerequisite is imposed.

HPA's per-location reliability supports nucleoplasm; cytosol and vesicles are uncertain. Independent human assembly experiments support a minor cytoplasmic pool (PMID:28515276), distinct from artificial P-body recruitment. The Reactome nucleoplasm assertions have literal paths to EFTUD2 and are not new experiments. A modest Cajal-body signal is retained as a secondary pool.

The AAR2–PRPF8 study does not demonstrate an EFTUD2-specific inhibitory mechanism; its negative-splicing assignment is marked over-annotated, without asserting that EFTUD2 lacks regulatory capacity (PMID:36322420). Narrow minor-snRNP release, membrane-proteomics target evidence and two rat drug-expression transfers remain unresolved. Missing tables or incomplete access are not treated as contamination, paralog confusion or evidence that curators deliberately rejected an observation.
