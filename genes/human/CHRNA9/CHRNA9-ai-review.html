Gene Description

CHRNA9 encodes the alpha9 subunit of an acetylcholine-gated cation channel associated with efferent signaling to inner-ear hair cells. Human alpha10 strongly promotes functional alpha9-containing receptor expression; functional properties of isolated domains or chimeras are not equivalent to a native pentamer. Calcium entry through the receptor recruits calcium-dependent potassium channels, allowing a cation channel to mediate net inhibition of hair cells. Rat/chicken comparisons establish species differences in calcium permeability and choline efficacy; those numerical results are not direct human measurements. Nicotine antagonizes the ordinary alpha9/alpha10 channel rather than serving as its canonical activating agonist.

Core Functions

Human alpha9 contributes to the alpha9/alpha10 receptor pore and ACh-dependent gating. Native auditory inhibition results from coupling this cation current to a separate potassium conductance; the receptor is not itself a potassium channel or primary mechanical sound sensor. Human alpha10 strongly promotes functional alpha9-containing receptor expression; functional properties of isolated domains or chimeras are not equivalent to a native pentamer.
