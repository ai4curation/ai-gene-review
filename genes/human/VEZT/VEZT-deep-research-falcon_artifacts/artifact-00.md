| VEZT is a junctional, two-pass integral-membrane scaffold with intracellular N- and C-termini | MDCKII epithelial cells and mouse cochlear hair-cell/supporting-cell junctions | Two endogenous isoforms migrated at approximately 90 and 70 kDa; vezatin was recruited after E-cadherin during junction maturation and was enriched at mechanically stressed adherens junctions (pqac-00000001, pqac-00000007) | Strong evidence for membrane topology and a role in junction stabilization, but derived from canine cells and mouse tissue rather than direct topology experiments on human Q9HBM0. |
| Vezatin couples myosin VIIA to E-cadherin-associated cortical machinery and supports internalization | Human Caco-2 epithelial cells challenged with *Listeria monocytogenes* or InlA-coated beads | Myosin-VIIA tail was recruited around 72 ± 5% of InlA-coated beads versus 7 ± 2.4% of InlB-coated beads; dominant-negative myosin-VIIA or vezatin C-terminal constructs reduced InlA-dependent uptake approximately threefold (pqac-00000008, pqac-00000010) | Direct human-cell functional evidence supports a myosin-binding adaptor role. The α-catenin connection is probably indirect because two-hybrid assays did not detect direct VEZT–α-catenin binding. Pathogen uptake is an experimental application, not necessarily VEZT’s physiological primary function. |
| Vezatin directly associates with acetylcholine receptors and stabilizes the mature postsynaptic membrane | Muscle-conditional *Vezt*-deficient mice and cultured mouse myotubes | At postnatal day 60, 48.6% of mutant synapses remained immature versus 6.5% of controls; AChR density was 80.7% of control, postjunctional folding fell 15%, and AChR-cluster half-life after agrin withdrawal fell from 12 to 5 hours (pqac-00000000, pqac-00000005) | Strong vertebrate evidence for postsynaptic maturation and AChR stabilization, but the experiments do not establish the same role in human neuromuscular junctions. |
| Vezatin regulates cargo-selective retrograde transport of Rab7-positive late endosomes | CRISPR *vezt*-mutant zebrafish sensory axons, with supporting Drosophila genetics | Mutant axon-terminal volume increased to 638 ± 49 µm³ versus 417 ± 63 µm³; Rab7-positive endosomes accumulated and showed impaired retrograde movement, whereas Rab5 and Rab11 compartments were largely unaffected (pqac-00000002, pqac-00000003) | Supports a conserved, cargo-specific transport role, potentially involving dynein–dynactin. This is ortholog-based inference: tagged human VEZT did not rescue the fly phenotype, and a direct human dynein/dynactin mechanism has not been demonstrated. |
| The endometriosis-associated 12q22 locus regulates human VEZT expression | Human eutopic endometrium, blood eQTL datasets, and endometriosis case-control meta-analysis | rs10859871 lies about 17 kb upstream of VEZT; the C risk allele was associated with endometriosis (OR 1.19, P = 7.9 × 10⁻²⁰) and increased endometrial VEZT expression (effect estimate 0.20; eQTL P = 7.19 × 10⁻²⁶). Protein-level differences by genotype or disease status were not significant overall (pqac-00000015, pqac-00000017, pqac-00000018) | Direct human genetic and expression evidence, but association and colocalized eQTL signals do not prove that VEZT mediates disease risk. Menstrual-cycle and cell-type effects complicate interpretation. |
| No validated VEZT-directed diagnostic, prognostic, or therapeutic implementation is established | Human translational literature | The principal human study used 122 endometrial samples for eQTL analysis, 56 for western blotting, and 42 for immunohistochemistry; it reported no validated biomarker-performance assay or VEZT-targeted intervention (pqac-00000020, pqac-00000021) | VEZT currently remains a research-level functional-annotation and disease-association candidate. Clinical use would require causal validation, standardized assays, independent cohorts, and evidence that manipulating VEZT is safe and beneficial. |
