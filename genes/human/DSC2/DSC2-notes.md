# DSC2 evidence and annotation review

DSC2 is a desmosomal cadherin with one integrated adhesive and plaque-anchoring role. Its calcium-binding ectodomain engages cadherins across adjacent cells, while its alternative cytoplasmic tails differ in plaque associations. The final core uses [GO:0098641](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/QuickGO/term/GO:0098641), a child of both cadherin binding and cell-cell adhesion mediator activity, at the desmosome. Calcium binding, the adhesive process and tail-dependent anchoring are components of this mechanism; the intestinal Rap1, traction and wound-repair observations do not establish a second catalytic activity.

## Source and product boundaries

The official DSC2 record is Q02487, with 901-residue 2A/DGII and 847-residue 2B/DGIII products. The short product has the specified alternative terminal sequence and lacks the final 54 residues of 2A. Existing product identifiers and sequence notes are preserved. Human DSC2a/b tail constructs associate with plakophilin-2 in rat-heart lysate assays; the longer tail additionally supports plakoglobin and desmoplakin-fragment association in the reported experiments. Neither those lysate assays nor human constructs expressed in monkey/rodent hosts are described as purified human binary measurements. See [PMID:21062920](https://doi.org/10.1093/cvr/cvq353) and the historical splicing study [PMID:2037591](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2037591/).

Historical DSC3 naming in the DSC2 record does not identify the current DSC3 gene, Q14574. Experimental DSC3 reagents are not relabeled from this alias alone. Disease truncations, proteolytic maturation products and experimental fragments are not new natural splice products.

## Adhesive specificity and binding provenance

The human ectodomain study [PMID:27298358](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1606272113) supports strong desmoglein–desmocollin adhesion. Its weak DSC2 self-association and EC1-containing chimera crystal contact do not establish an exclusively homophilic native junction. Conversely, positive self-binding in the single-molecule experiments [PMID:24610950](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24610950/) and [PMID:29999492](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29999492/) is not erased by a negative bulk bead assay. Protein preparation, calcium, force and membrane context can distinguish these experiments. The exact construct species in the inspected single-molecule Methods is not inferred merely from a human expression host.

The 2022 surface-protein network [PMID:35922511](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-022-05028-x) contains DSC2–DSG2, but the 29,000 nM affinity in Supplementary Table 3 and the Figure 2 source-data network is explicitly a previously published result attributed to the 2016 study. It is not a new 2022 high-affinity validation. The library DSC2 sequence matches canonical residues 1–693; this shared ectodomain does not resolve the two different natural tails. IntAct [EBI-6901957](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/intact/interaction/EBI-6901957), also IM-20978-13, instead records the 2011 anti-tag co-IP association and its mutant/sufficient-region context. Reference ROGIDs authenticate the curated reference sequences, not every experimental insert. Reproducible checks and the public workbook/record locators are linked in the source-evidence sidecar.

The complete [2024 correction](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-07928-6) was inspected. It addresses a missing CLC4M figure label, a concentration-unit correction in Figure 2c and four missing single-cell references. It does not identify a change to the DSC2–DSG2 affinity record used here. The preserved original cache is unchanged.

## Localization and contextual processes

Measured and modeled adhesive pools support desmosome refinement. Broader plasma-membrane observations from HPA and the 2013 cell-surface study remain nonexclusive contextual observations. [HPA](https://www.proteinatlas.org/ENSG00000134755-DSC2/subcellular) identifies supported plasma-membrane staining in HaCaT, SuSa and U-2 OS and additional ER staining in HaCaT/SuSa; these are metadata/caption observations, not newly interpreted pixels. A membrane prediction is refined to the complementary plasma-membrane and ER pools without asserting that every molecule is an assembled desmosome component.

The Reactome paths explicitly place Q02487 residues 136–901 in desmocollin/desmosome networks, including a cornified-envelope component. Membership is not evidence that DSC2 executes filaggrin cleavage, phosphorylation or envelope cross-linking. Intestinal adhesion and timely DSG2 recruitment are experimentally supported; the specific positive protein-localization refinement is [GO:0150107](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/QuickGO/term/GO:0150107). Wound healing remains a contextual process. The tension sensor in the 2021 study is DSG2, not DSC2.

Nine decisions remain UNDECIDED because the exact source assertion cannot be resolved: the transferred adherens-junction pool, two unrecovered exosome target tables, specific His–Purkinje binding/adhesion, heart-rate regulation, ventricular action-potential regulation and two p38 source/transfer claims. The genuine primary text that was recovered, including clinical and original keratinocyte experiments, is documented separately from inaccessible supplements. No negative annotation, wrong-gene accusation or absence claim follows from these limits. The urinary proteomic target was independently found in the author laboratory database, but its one-peptide entry does not establish a unique natural product.
