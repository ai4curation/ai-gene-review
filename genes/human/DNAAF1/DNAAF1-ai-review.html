Gene Description

DNAAF1 is a leucine-rich-repeat protein that supports cytoplasmic assembly of the outer and inner dynein arms needed for motile cilia. Loss of DNAAF1 disrupts dynein-arm incorporation and causes primary ciliary dyskinesia with respiratory and left–right patterning abnormalities. Human protein has also been observed in cilia and at mitotic spindle poles, and depletion in cultured renal epithelial cells impairs primary-cilium formation. Alternative transcripts encode three predicted products with different repeat or C-terminal regions.

Core Functions

Supports cytoplasmic preassembly and incorporation of the outer and inner dynein arms during motile-cilium assembly. Human genetic and component-localization evidence establishes both arm classes; ortholog fractionation and rescue experiments support cytoplasmic preassembly. Human DNAAF1 associates with IFT88 and RUVBL1/RUVBL2, linking the assembly factor to ciliary trafficking machinery. The proposed coupling of dynein assembly to IFT delivery remains mechanistic inference: the direct dynein-client interfaces and a reconstituted transfer reaction are unresolved.

2026-10-17 — assembly partners and the motile-cilium claim

DNAAF1 contributes both dynein-arm classes to the motile axoneme. The existing motile-cilium assembly assertion is retained: absence of arms within recognizable cilia is an assembly defect, not evidence that the process is irrelevant. Fresh QuickGO is_a/part_of closures place outer- and inner-arm assembly under axoneme assembly, but do not place them under GO:0044458. The specific arm terms therefore refine the axoneme and dynein-complex assertions without replacing the distinct motile-cilium claim.

Human interaction experiments identify more than an unspecified binding possibility. PMID:29228333, DNAAF1 links heart laterality with the AAA+ ATPase RUVBL1 and ciliary intraflagellar transport reports “DNAAF1 interacted with both full length and N’-terminally truncated IFT88”. The retinal-library screen was followed by one-to-one Y2H and human HEK293 co-immunoprecipitation (Figure 3A–C; Yeast two-hybrid and Co-immunoprecipitation Methods). IFT88 residues 442–833 retained interaction while 1–449 did not. SF-TAP and endogenous-partner co-IP also support RUVBL1/RUVBL2 association (Figure 3D, Table 1 and accompanying Results). Reduced p.Leu191Phe protein abundance prevents attributing every diminished signal to a selective affinity defect.

Endogenous DNAAF1 localizes at the basal body in human hTERT-RPE1 cells. RUVBL1 depletion perturbs DNAAF1/IFT88 colocalization (Figure 4), supporting a connection between the assembly factor and transport machinery. The authors' R2TP-like assembly-to-IFT handoff is a model, rather than a demonstrated stoichiometric complex or reconstituted transfer reaction. These findings narrow the open question to direct dynein-client interfaces and the coupling mechanism. They do not establish an autonomous ATPase/chaperone activity or the functions of the shorter predicted products.

The Discussion of PMID:29601588(https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5895051/) provides related preassembly context, but its DNAAF1 statements cite prior studies; they are not additional direct human DNAAF1 assays. Its cited 2012 original distinguishes ODA7/LRRC50 and PF13/KTU despite historical DNAAF1/2 labels differing from current nomenclature. The cited 2016 C21ORF59 original was not recovered by the attempted XML route. Separately, PMID:29959317(https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6026120/) Figure 5D–E shows murine Dnaaf1 co-precipitation with Ruvbl1 and Dpcd; its Methods specify a murine clone. These observations corroborate mammalian assembly-factor association without supplying a purified human interface.

The original notes, source records, products and quotations remain unchanged. The single action correction is motile-cilium assembly MODIFY to ACCEPT; no NEW annotation is added. The axoneme IBA retains the curator's inherited activity context, with the distinction between localization and a directly measured axonemal reaction made explicit.
