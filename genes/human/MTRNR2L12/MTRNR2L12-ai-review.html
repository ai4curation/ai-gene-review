Gene Description

MTRNR2L12 is a nuclear human locus on chromosome 3 (3q11.2) that resembles the humanin small open reading frame of the mitochondrial large-subunit rRNA gene MT-RNR2, one of thirteen such MT-RNR2-like sequences in the human genome that are generally interpreted as nuclear insertions of mitochondrial DNA and are classed by HGNC as pseudogenes. Its predicted product, humanin-like 12 (HN12), is a 24-residue peptide (MAPRGFSCLLLSTSEIDLPVKRRA) that matches humanin at 23 of 24 aligned positions, differing only by Leu12->Ser, and is identical in sequence to the predicted product of MTRNR2L8; a coding polymorphism at this position would make it identical to humanin itself. Every residue humanin is known to require for activity or secretion is retained. The locus is the most frequently named of the thirteen in transcriptomic studies, appearing as a differentially expressed transcript or co-expression hub in blood mononuclear cells of adults with Down syndrome and in platelets during COVID-19, where it groups with the mitochondrially encoded transcripts MT-ND1 and MT-ND5. UniProt lists it at protein existence level 3 (inferred from homology), and whether a peptide is made from it, and what it would do, is unestablished: no HN12 peptide has been detected in human tissue or fluid, no molecular activity has been measured for it, and no binding partner or receptor interaction has been reported. The humanin peptide from which it is named is cytoprotective and antiapoptotic, binds BAX, BID, BIM and IGFBP3 intracellularly and signals through FPRL1/FPR2 and a CNTFR-alpha/WSX-1/gp130 complex when secreted, but those properties were measured on the mitochondrially encoded peptide and on synthetic analogues of it, not on this gene product.

Suggested Questions for Experts

Q: Is a peptide produced from the MTRNR2L12 locus? This is the locus where the question matters most, because its predicted peptide differs from humanin at a single position and a common polymorphism would erase even that difference [PMID:25720405]. One paper asserts that the peptide has been confirmed in brain tissue, but gives no source for the claim and I could find no primary report of it. Targeted mass spectrometry cannot distinguish HN12 from humanin on sequence alone, so the experiment has to be genetic: look for the Ser12-containing tryptic peptide in a donor homozygous for the serine allele.

Suggested experts: Alan Saghatelian, Pinchas Cohen

Q: Why does MTRNR2L12 carry no IBA rows when MTRNR2L8, whose predicted peptide is identical in sequence, carries both the receptor antagonist activity and the apoptosis rows from PANTHER:PTN002141596, as do the far more diverged MTRNR2L11 and MTRNR2L13? The difference tracks PANTHER subfamily assignment rather than sequence, which makes the family's IBA coverage inconsistent in a way visible from the alignment alone. The resolution should be to narrow the node to the sequences with functional data, not to extend the rows to the remaining copies.

Q: How should GO and UniProt treat a nuclear pseudogene whose transcript is repeatedly reported as differentially expressed, when the transcript cannot be distinguished by short reads from twelve near-identical copies and from an extremely abundant mitochondrial rRNA? MTRNR2L12 is the most frequently named locus of the thirteen in transcriptomic studies, and in at least one it clusters with mitochondrially encoded transcripts [PMID:35421970]. A locus-resolved quantification standard for this family would settle how all of that literature should be read.

Q: If HN12 is translated, is it functionally distinguishable from humanin at all? With a single conservative difference at Leu12, and with synthetic HN5 carrying the same Leu12->Ser still cytoprotective [PMID:19477263], the interesting possibility is not that this locus has a distinct function but that some of the activity attributed to mitochondrial humanin is contributed by nuclear copies.

Suggested experts: Pinchas Cohen

Suggested Experiments

Experiment: Allele-resolved mass spectrometry for the HN12 peptide. Because HN12 differs from humanin only at position 12, use donors or cell lines genotyped at rs6484338 and search for the serine-containing peptide; the leucine allele produces a species indistinguishable from mitochondrial humanin, so genotype is the only available discriminator.

Experiment: Locus-resolved transcript quantification for MTRNR2L12 by long-read sequencing or by amplicon sequencing designed across the positions that differ between the nuclear copies, applied to the sample types where the gene has been reported as differentially expressed (Down syndrome blood mononuclear cells, COVID-19 platelets). This tests whether the reported signal is this locus rather than a sibling copy or mitochondrial rRNA.

Experiment: Ribosome profiling of the MTRNR2L12 transcript in a cell type where it is reported to be expressed, to test whether the predicted open reading frame is translated rather than merely transcribed.

Experiment: CRISPR deletion of the MTRNR2L12 locus in a human cell line, with apoptosis challenge and with measurement of total circulating humanin-like immunoreactivity, to test whether this copy contributes to the pool of humanin-like peptide. The thirteen near-identical loci make guide design the hard part, and off-target cutting at MTRNR2L8, which has the identical coding sequence, is the control that matters.

MTRNR2L12 (P0DMP1, humanin-like 12, HN12) - locus-specific notes

  • HGNC:37169, MT-RNR2 like 12 (pseudogene), locus_type pseudogene, 3q11.2 (REST lookup at
    rest.genenames.org, 2026-10-04). GRCh37 position chr3:96336030-96337067 PMID:31753007.
    UniProt P0DMP1 is PE3, "Inferred from homology".
  • 24 aa MAPRGFSCLLLSTSEIDLPVKRRA; 23/24 vs humanin MAPRGFSCLLLLTSEIDLPVKRRA - the single
    difference is Leu12->Ser. This is the closest of the thirteen nuclear copies to humanin, and its
    sequence is identical to that of MTRNR2L8 (both computed in this session from the UniProt SQ
    records). All of the humanin determinants are intact: Pro3, Ser7, Cys8, Leu9-Leu11, Thr13, Ser14,
    and both secretion segments Leu9-Leu11 and Pro19-Val20.
  • Leu12 is a position where humanin Leu12->Ala abolishes neuroprotective activity
    (file:human/MT-RNR2__Q8IVG9/MT-RNR2__Q8IVG9-uniprot.txt), but synthetic HN5 carries the same
    Leu12->Ser and is still cytoprotective PMID:19477263, so this is a caution, not a refutation -
    the same reading as for MTRNR2L1.
  • The Down-syndrome paper notes a coding polymorphism that would erase even that one difference
    PMID:25720405.
  • GOA inconsistency worth recording. MTRNR2L12 has only four rows (extracellular and cytoplasm,
    each IEA + ISS). It carries no IBA rows, while MTRNR2L8 - whose peptide sequence is identical -
    carries both GO:0048019 and GO:1900118 from PANTHER:PTN002141596, as do L11 and L13, which are
    far more diverged (verified from the per-gene GOA files in this repository). The difference tracks
    PANTHER subfamily/node membership, not sequence: UniProt lists L12 under PTHR33895:SF5 (as L8 does),
    while L11 and L13 have their own subfamilies SF16 and SF17. So the family's IBA coverage is
    inconsistent in a way visible from the alignment alone. This is a defect of coverage, not a reason
    to add the terms here: the right fix is to restrict the node, not to extend it (see the shared
    background below).

Literature: the most-cited of the thirteen loci, and why that is misleading

A PubMed search for "MTRNR2L12" (E-utilities, 2026-10-04) returns 15 records - far more than any
other nuclear humanin-like locus (L11 returns 0, L13 returns 2). Nearly all are transcriptomic:
the gene surfaces as a differentially expressed transcript, a co-expression hub, or a lncRNA in
studies of periodontitis, nasal polyps, narcolepsy CSF, focal cortical dysplasia, PM2.5 exposure,
liver-cancer extracellular vesicles, and so on. Two are worth reading for this review.

  1. PMID:25720405 (J Alzheimers Dis 2015), the one paper named after the locus. Microarray plus
    RQ-PCR in blood mononuclear cells of 48 adults with Down syndrome; MTRNR2L12 was the single gene
    distinguishing younger severely-affected from older unaffected participants
    PMID:25720405. Important caveats:
  2. This is transcript evidence in blood, in a small sample, offered as a candidate biomarker.
  3. It designed its own primers from ENST00000600213, acknowledging that no commercial assay
    exists; locus specificity against the other twelve near-identical copies and against the
    mitochondrial rRNA is not demonstrated in the paper.
  4. It asserts, with no citation, that a peptide exists
    PMID:25720405.
    I could find no primary report of an HN12 peptide; this sentence should not be propagated, and
    I flag it in reference_review rather than using it as support for anything.
  5. PMID:35421970 (BMC Med Genomics 2022), platelet co-expression in COVID-19. MTRNR2L12 is a hub
    gene of the magenta module
    PMID:35421970, and the paper itself notes
    PMID:35421970.
    The company it keeps is the tell: MT-ND1 and MT-ND5 are mitochondrially encoded. A nuclear copy of
    a mitochondrial rRNA segment clustering with mitochondrial transcripts in a short-read
    co-expression analysis is exactly what read mis-assignment looks like. This does not prove
    mis-assignment, but it means the module membership cannot be read as evidence about a nuclear gene
    product, and the same caution applies to the other transcriptomic hits above.
  6. Genetics: the CAD lookup included the L12 region (rs78077066 P = 0.015, rs12106821 P = 0.52), and
    PMID:31753007
  7. Net: despite being the most frequently named locus of the thirteen, MTRNR2L12 has no
    peptide-level evidence, no activity assay, no interaction data, and no locus-resolved
    quantification that excludes the other copies. Expression-level notoriety is not functional
    evidence.
  8. Actions: 4 MARK_AS_OVER_ANNOTATED (extracellular IEA + ISS, cytoplasm IEA + ISS). The extracellular
    rows are not removed here, unlike L3/L4/L7/L11/L13, because L12 retains both humanin secretion
    segments intact - the same reasoning as MTRNR2L1. core_functions: [], no NEW terms.

The family, and why these reviews look alike

Humanin is a 24-residue peptide encoded by a small open reading frame inside the mitochondrial 16S
rRNA gene MT-RNR2, and it is the member of this family with all the experimental evidence:
cytoprotection, BAX/BID/BIM binding, FPRL1/FPR2 engagement, the CNTFR-alpha/WSX-1/gp130 receptor
complex. Bodzioch et al. 2009 (PMID:19477263, PubMed-verified, Genomics 94:247-256) found thirteen
MT-RNR2-like sequences in the nuclear genome whose reading frames would encode humanin-like peptides
HN1-HN13
PMID:19477263
and showed that at least ten are transcribed and respond to staurosporine and beta-carotene
PMID:19477263.
The later lookup paper gives the range as
PMID:31753007, so L11-L13 are the three loci left out - consistent with their PE3 status, and in tension
with the volume of transcriptomic literature naming L12.

UniProt states the position in a CAUTION line on each entry: the active peptide is the product of the
mitochondrial gene, and whether any nuclear locus makes a physiologically active peptide is open. A
2022 review restates it
PMID:35372353.
HGNC classes all thirteen as pseudogenes.

Residues

Humanin has an unusually complete mutagenesis map (UniProt Q8IVG9). Single substitutions that
abolish neuroprotective activity: Pro3, Ser7, Cys8, Leu9, Leu12, Thr13, Ser14 (S14->A,R,W,E,P);
secretion requires Leu9-Leu11 and Pro19-Val20. HN12 differs from humanin at Leu12 only, and the
synthetic HN5 peptide carrying the same Leu12->Ser is cytoprotective PMID:19477263. So for this
locus sequence divergence is not the argument - the missing link is simply that no peptide has ever
been shown to exist.

Methodological caution

RNA-seq and microarray signals over a nuclear copy that is 23/24 identical to a peptide encoded
inside an extremely abundant mitochondrial rRNA are not locus-resolved unless the analysis
demonstrates it, and serum ELISAs for "humanin-like" species have not been shown to be
locus-specific for any member of this family. No antibody- or short-read-based measurement is used
here as evidence that MTRNR2L12 makes a peptide.

suggested_questions:
- question: 'Is a peptide produced from the MTRNR2L12 locus? This is the locus where the question matters
    most, because its predicted peptide differs from humanin at a single position and a common polymorphism
    would erase even that difference [PMID:25720405]. One paper asserts that the peptide has been confirmed
    in brain tissue, but gives no source for the claim and I could find no primary report of it. Targeted
    mass spectrometry cannot distinguish HN12 from humanin on sequence alone, so the experiment has to
    be genetic: look for the Ser12-containing tryptic peptide in a donor homozygous for the serine allele.'

- question: Why does MTRNR2L12 carry no IBA rows when MTRNR2L8, whose predicted peptide is identical
    in sequence, carries both the receptor antagonist activity and the apoptosis rows from PANTHER:PTN002141596,
    as do the far more diverged MTRNR2L11 and MTRNR2L13? The difference tracks PANTHER subfamily assignment
    rather than sequence, which makes the family's IBA coverage inconsistent in a way visible from the
    alignment alone. The resolution should be to narrow the node to the sequences with functional data,
    not to extend the rows to the remaining copies.
- question: How should GO and UniProt treat a nuclear pseudogene whose transcript is repeatedly reported
    as differentially expressed, when the transcript cannot be distinguished by short reads from twelve
    near-identical copies and from an extremely abundant mitochondrial rRNA? MTRNR2L12 is the most frequently
    named locus of the thirteen in transcriptomic studies, and in at least one it clusters with mitochondrially
    encoded transcripts [PMID:35421970]. A locus-resolved quantification standard for this family would
    settle how all of that literature should be read.
- question: If HN12 is translated, is it functionally distinguishable from humanin at all? With a single
    conservative difference at Leu12, and with synthetic HN5 carrying the same Leu12->Ser still cytoprotective
    [PMID:19477263], the interesting possibility is not that this locus has a distinct function but that
    some of the activity attributed to mitochondrial humanin is contributed by nuclear copies.
