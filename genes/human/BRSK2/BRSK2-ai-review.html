Gene Description

BRSK2 (SAD-A) is an AMPK-related protein serine/threonine kinase with an N-terminal catalytic domain and regulatory UBA and KA1 regions. LKB1-dependent activation enables phosphorylation of protein substrates, including tau, and conserved SAD-kinase signaling helps establish neuronal axon-dendrite polarity and organize neuronal projections. BRSK2 is cytoplasmic, with mitotic centrosomal localization and stress-dependent recruitment to the endoplasmic reticulum. In pancreatic beta-cell models, SAD-A binds and phosphorylates PAK1 and influences cytoskeletal remodeling and glucose-stimulated insulin secretion. Its abundance is controlled by ubiquitin-dependent turnover, and it interacts with VCP/p97 in an ER-associated degradation context.

Core Functions

LKB1-activated protein serine/threonine phosphorylation supports conserved SAD-kinase signaling in neuronal polarity. Tau is a demonstrated substrate, and combined SAD A/B loss disrupts axonal polarization in mouse neurons; those combined-loss assays do not resolve the individual contribution of BRSK2. Context-specific PAK1, cell-cycle and ERAD effects are reviewed separately. ATP binding and the tau-kinase subactivity are parts of this single catalytic unit.
