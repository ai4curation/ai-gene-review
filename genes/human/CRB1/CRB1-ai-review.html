Gene Description

CRB1 is a Crumbs-family membrane protein that helps organize the apical polarity architecture of retinal photoreceptors and Müller glia. Its canonical C-terminal PDZ-binding motif engages PALS1/MPP5, linking CRB1 to a larger polarity assembly at the outer limiting membrane. CRB1 supports epithelial apicobasal organization and maintenance of retinal cell contacts; biallelic defects cause a spectrum of inherited retinal dystrophies. Alternative products differ in extracellular sequence, membrane topology and cytoplasmic tail, and tissue-specific Crb1 forms have distinct functions in experimental models. Mouse work also implicates Crb1 in intestinal and retinal epithelial barriers, while human CRB1–CRB2 association has been demonstrated without resolving whether the interaction acts between or within cells.

2026-10-13: polarity, products and source-specific annotation assessment

CRB1 is best understood as a membrane component of apical polarity assemblies. Its canonical ERLI tail binds the PDZ domain of PALS1/MPP5. PALS1 links CRB1 to PATJ and can recruit MPP4; CRB1 also binds EPB41L5 through a different tail motif. These interactions and their epithelial/retinal context support one PDZ-binding and apicobasal-polarity core. They do not make every associated protein a direct PDZ ligand, establish a fixed native stoichiometry, or demonstrate that CRB1 is itself a generic membrane adaptor.

The original human-tail experiments and the modern human tissue work matter more than a broad family description. In the full PALS1 study (PMID:11927608), the deletion controls resolve the PALS1 bridge to PATJ; a short anchor is “mediating the indirect interaction between CRB1 and PATJ”. The MPP4 study (PMID:15914641) separates direct MPP5 binding from MPP4 recruitment. The EPB41L5 study (PMID:17920587) separates GTY-dependent FERM binding from ERLI-dependent PALS1 binding. Its calcium-switch perturbation is an EPB41L5 experiment, and the authors do not identify an EPB41L5 actin-binding domain. These distinctions preserve positive interactions without adding an untested anchoring mechanism.

The human-tail rescue study (PMID:11734541) used a human cytoplasmic tail grafted into a fly Crumbs construct carrying the fly signal and transmembrane regions. Partial rescue and overexpression phenotypes support a conserved tail function; they are not rescue by full-length human CRB1. The more recent human CRB1 peptide experiment (PMID:33758649) directly measures binding to purified PALS1 PDZ with a mutant-domain comparison. Its viral-protein title does not erase this genuine CRB1 control experiment.

Localization requires a distinction between neighboring compartments

The authentic original PMID:15316081(https://doi.org/10.1242/jcs.01301), Results on knockout generation and Figure 3, uses three antibodies and knockout controls to place Crb1 in the subapical inner segment, explicitly contrasting earlier cone outer-segment staining. The short source anchor is “not in the outer segments of the cones”. This identifies a genuine conflict in the older source, but it does not settle all products. The later PMID:32620864 delB-controlled, isoform-specific antibody and tangential-section Western experiment positively places mouse CRB1-B in outer and inner segments. Together with conserved human retinal B transcripts, that evidence supports retaining the existing gene-level outer-segment inference. It does not directly locate human CRB1-B protein or identify B with any of the five official products. The earlier PMID:11850625(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11850625/) remains abstract-only here; its abstract itself names mammalian inner segments.

Both the mouse/human Figure 1 in PMID:15316081 and the human retina images in PMID:15914641 distinguish CRB1-positive subapical region from the more basal beta-catenin-positive adherens junction. The broader apical junction complex is appropriate for these source rows. In addition, the current definition of GO:0035003 explicitly names an invertebrate structure; a vertebrate retinal region called subapical is not automatically that named complex. No donor organism or positive localization is discarded merely because of this terminology difference.

Human fetal retina and patient-derived organoids (PMID:30956116) show CRB1 at photoreceptor inner segments and apical Müller villi, with altered outer-limiting-membrane organization in patient material. These direct target observations support microvillus and glial-projection locations. The mouse source underlying one broad apical-cell inference, PMID:25636444(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25636444/), used a pan-Crb antibody shared by Crb1, Crb2 and Crb3; that staining is not independently CRB1-specific. Another literal donor record cites PMID:129154, which is retained as written and is not silently repaired to PMID:12915475. Independent human localization supports the target without resolving that citation defect.

Products 1, 3 and 5 retain the canonical C-terminal membrane/tail region; products 2 and 4 alter or remove that region. The historical 1,376-residue form was discussed as potentially having an alternative membrane segment. Loss of the canonical segment therefore does not by itself prove secretion, and the secretion assertion remains unresolved without a product-specific release assay.
