# EZH2 source and evidence guide

This guide distinguishes target experiments, ortholog evidence and database propagation for all 186 imported annotation contexts. The five source-described products remain separate; cell species, expression host and historical construct names do not establish equivalent natural-product functions. The [campaign curation instructions](../../../projects/CLINGEN_MENDELIAN.md#curation-instructions) govern supported generic binding and reference-only support without new publication quotations.

The annotation checkpoint contains 81 ACCEPT, 69 KEEP_AS_NON_CORE, 21 MODIFY and 15 UNDECIDED decisions. These are evidence assessments, not a claim that every biochemical or product-specific question is resolved.

## Database and conserved-function evidence

EZH2 is a catalytic component of PRC2. The experiments discussed here distinguish its contacts with chromatin and RNA from the targeting contributions of accessory proteins, and distinguish enzymatic chemistry from the downstream developmental contexts in which that chemistry acts.

### Human substrate contacts and chemistry

[PMID:33479123](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33479123/) was read in its operative structural Results and expression, purification, nucleosome and activity Methods. The preparation contains full-length human EZH2, engineered human EED/SUZ12 and JARID2 components, and additional PRC2 partners expressed in High Five insect cells. EZH2 SET/CXC and bridge-helix regions contact nucleosomal DNA and the histone tail. These are direct target contacts, not evidence that EZH2 independently recognizes a promoter nucleotide motif. The study uses Xenopus histone preparations and separate commercial human modified nucleosomes; the cellular origin of the enzyme and substrate should not be conflated. Its SAH assay integrates methyl-transfer events rather than independently resolving each methylation state.

Broader substrate terms remain meaningful. In [PMID:19144645](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19144645/), anti-EZH2 immunoprecipitates from a HeLa nuclear-pellet fraction methylate H1.4, with WT/K26A substrate comparisons. The paper also studies G9a, but the EZH2 experiment is separately positive. The preparation is not isolated EZH2. [PMID:25620564](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25620564/) demonstrates PRC2 methylation of nonhistone JARID2 K116 and cellular dependence on Ezh2/Eed. Its peptide-binding and structural experiments identify EED as the methyllysine reader; that binding role is not reassigned to EZH2. Mammalian and fly PRC2 preparations and mouse ES-cell experiments are distinguished; all construct details delegated to supplements were not independently inspected.

The official [EC 2.1.1.356 reaction](https://iubmb.qmul.ac.uk/enzyme/EC2/1/1/356.html) and the target UniProt RHEA:60292 mapping support H3K27 trimethylation. The current ARBA00098061 endpoint returned 404, and its unavailable condition sets are not reconstructed. A source-specific H3K27 refinement does not erase independent H1.4 or JARID2 evidence.

### Chromatin occupancy is not automatically sequence recognition

The mouse donor experiments in [PMID:24137001](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24137001/), [23942234](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23942234/) and [20956546](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20956546/) contain positive Ezh2 chromatin occupancy. However, the inspected synthetic RNA–DNA hybrid assay in the first does not contain EZH2 protein; motif experiments in the second concern YY1; and motif/EMSA experiments in the third concern STAT6. Figure 2D in [PMID:20798045](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20798045/) measures H3K27me3 ChIP. None of those observations alone demonstrates EZH2 sequence discrimination.

[PMID:18483221](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18483221/) includes mouse ES-cell chromatin measurements and separate human 293T association/artificial Gal4 recruitment experiments. [PMID:20064375](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20064375/) includes mouse ES-cell work and a separate positive human ES-cell ChIP-qPCR experiment. Its direct JARID2 interaction maps to SUZ12, not EZH2. Selected publisher Results from [PMID:26658965](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26658965/) support Linc-YY1-dependent changes in PRC2 occupancy in mouse C2C12 cells; the motif/EMSA experiments concern YY1. Full operative Methods, underlying supplementary datasets and figure pixels from that paper were not read.

GO:0000976 deliberately permits non-sequence-specific regulatory-region binding, and GO:1990841 describes promoter-associated chromatin binding. These are distinct from the sequence-specific terms. The positive evidence supports chromatin DNA binding where the original assertion implied EZH2 nucleotide-sequence recognition.

### RNA ligands and PRC2 targeting

[PMID:21172659](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21172659/) explicitly tests human FLAG-EZH2 and human PRC2 purified from Sf9 cells, including heparin and size-exclusion steps, with RNA EMSA. Cellular mouse RIP and UV-crosslink experiments are separate. The direct ligands include RepA, Hes1 antisense and Gtl2-derived RNAs, supporting the more specific lncRNA-binding activity. The purified ligand is RNA, not an assembled RNA–protein complex; no lncRNA sequence exclusivity is inferred.

In [PMID:27618650](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27618650/), the EMSA uses a mouse Ezh2 insert in a human in-vitro translation system. The host system does not make the product human. Rat/mouse Chaer experiments, reported human iPSC-cardiomyocyte RIP, and purified human RNA binding from the independent paper are kept separate. The human supplementary RIP figure was not viewed. [PMID:21874018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21874018/) uses mouse ES-cell formaldehyde RIP for lincRNA associations; this is not an isolated-protein binding assay for every recovered RNA. The pri-miR-208b result in PMID:24137001 is a particular mouse cardiac RNA-ChIP context, retained as non-core.

### Conserved repression and developmental settings

[PMID:19026781](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19026781/) distinguishes EZH2-associated histone methylation from stronger EZH1 SAM-independent compaction. The latter is not transferred to EZH2. [PMID:16717091](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16717091/) contains positive human EZH2 constructs: enhancement of CRY-dependent repression of mouse promoter reporters in HeLa and rescue of mouse-specific siRNA effects in NIH3T3 cells. Mouse liver ChIP/co-IP and mouse fibroblast oscillations remain distinct from those human-construct results. No native human circadian time course is claimed.

[PMID:23531880](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23531880/) perturbs YY1 and rescues with WT versus deltaREPO; it does not knock out Ezh2. Independent actual [PMID:12496962](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12496962/) deletes the mouse Ezh2 SET region and demonstrates a cell-autonomous pro-B to pre-B defect with H3K27 changes. EZH2 is not thereby assigned the recombinase step. The inspected author original for [PMID:12900441](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12900441/) provides mouse pronuclear Ezh2 localization and maternal-depletion controls. [PMID:27892467](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27892467/) shows conditional pericentric recruitment in Dnmt1-deficient mouse ES cells and separate engineered MBD–Ezh2 targeting. These do not establish constitutive native human pericentric occupancy.

Operative originals remain unavailable for the specific rat contexts in PMIDs [22821416](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22821416/), [23932971](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23932971/), [25088689](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25088689/), [27690696](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27690696/) and [18467665](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18467665/). Registry/abstract access is explicitly distinguished from actual experiment reading. Current rat GOA assigns IEP to hippocampus development, estradiol response and kidney regulation; other contexts use IMP. Expression can be a valid response readout, but it does not automatically establish participation in a developmental step. The unusual title of PMID:27690696 is not grounds to reject its curator-supported GABAergic or inflammatory assertions.

Actual PANTHER v19 PTHR45747 tree and MSA place human Q15910 at PTN002505151; its ungapped 746-residue sequence exactly matches the displayed target. The complete HPA029131 antigen matches all five source products; the CAB009589 antigen sequence is not supplied. The images therefore do not identify a particular natural product.

Representative target routes are verified; unrelated branches with retained access failures are not claimed to form a complete graph survey.

The five UniProt products remain distinct. Supported generic interactions are retained as non-core under the campaign instruction; seven partner-specific molecular-function refinements use current ontology definitions. ## Core-function synthesis

Two activities summarize the accepted evidence: H3K27 methylation by the catalytic EZH2 subunit of PRC2, and direct binding to tested long noncoding RNA ligands. The current definition of GO:0140951 includes successive methyl addition to form H3K27me3; it does not mean that EZH2 acts only on a dimethylated substrate. WT PRC2 acts on unmethylated, mono- and dimethylated H3K27 substrates with different efficiencies (PMID:22323599, Results accompanying Figure 3 and Materials and Methods). Human PRC2 reconstitution and native human-cell biochemical evidence establish the complex requirement (PMID:15385962, Figures 6–7 and Biochemical and enzymatic analyses). The direct molecular-function slot identifies EZH2's catalytic site within that complex; it does not assert an active isolated enzyme. A separate broad protein-lysine methyltransferase unit would duplicate an ancestor of this core activity. H1.4 methylation by human anti-EZH2 immunoprecipitates (PMID:19144645, Figure 1 and Experimental Procedures) and PRC2-mediated JARID2 K116 methylation (PMID:25620564, Figure 1) remain positive substrate-breadth evidence in the biological description and consistently retained protein-lysine methyltransferase reviews.

EZH2 contacts nucleosomal DNA and the H3 tail, making chromatin the location of the catalytic unit (PMID:33479123, nucleosome-engagement Results and Protein purification/Nucleosome reconstitution). The experiment uses full-length human EZH2 with engineered PRC2/accessory constructs; Xenopus histones and separate purchased human modified nucleosomes are distinguished. Total SAH production is cumulative methyltransferase output, while Western blots and reported mass spectrometry separately address H3K27 methylation states. EED reads methylated ligands; JARID2/AEBP2 and Polycomb-like proteins add distinct targeting functions. These are not transferred to EZH2. The two selected process facets, facultative heterochromatin formation and negative regulation of Pol II transcription, are not ancestors of one another in the captured ontology. EZH2 performs the methyl-transfer step; the process attribution is supported by chemistry together with human catalytic-mutant rescue, repression and chromatin-accessibility/FISH experiments (PMID:26581166, Results accompanying Figures 4–6 and the corresponding main Methods), rather than necessity alone. Detailed supplemental construct protocols and new figure-pixel inspection are not claimed.

The lncRNA-binding unit rests on purified human EZH2 and human PRC2 EMSAs (PMID:21172659, Figure 5 and protein-purification/RNA-EMSA Methods) and the independent isolated-loop experiment (PMID:35682829, Results 2.4 and Methods 4.1–4.3). Human recombinant binding is distinct from the mouse ES-cell RIP and locus-regulation experiments. The tested direct ligands are lncRNA-derived; the refinement specifies those ligands without asserting exclusive recognition of lncRNAs. The isolated loop binds the HOTAIR fragment weakly with low specificity; its phosphomimetic mutant is not a measurement of phosphorylation. Neither experiment establishes that every PRC2-associated transcript directly binds EZH2, that an RNA G-quadruplex is universally required, or that RNA binding always recruits, activates or inhibits PRC2. Accordingly, this unit has no separately asserted process term. Nucleoplasm is a supported broad compartment, not a claim that the in-vitro binding assay localizes the interaction. ## Mechanistic clarification after external review

**Ras signaling and hydrolysis.** [PMID:20154697](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20154697/), Figure 5 and the corresponding Results/Methods, tests human prostate epithelial cells, promoter-associated repression of DAB2IP, Ras-GTP and downstream signaling, and reversal by DAB2IP reconstitution. Suppression of a Ras GAP does not increase GTPase catalytic activity. GO:0046579 captures the positive signaling outcome; EZH2 is the upstream transcriptional regulator. The [2024 correction](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38383797/) concerns a Figure 1g Ki-67 control micrograph, not these Figure 5 assays.

**Chromatin and substrate breadth.** [PMID:26581166](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26581166/), Figures 4–6 and chromatin-accessibility/FISH Methods, supports a regulated H3K27-dependent facultative process in human MCF7 cells. The four broad heterochromatin contexts are refined consistently without assigning H1.2 reader/structural activity to EZH2. The broad protein-lysine methyltransferase contexts are retained because the H1.4 and JARID2 experiments supply real additional substrate classes. [Reactome R-HSA-212263](https://reactome.org/content/detail/R-HSA-212263) supplies narrower H3K27-trimethylation evidence for one of those broad assertions. [R-HSA-5638332](https://reactome.org/content/detail/R-HSA-5638332) instead describes mono-, di- and trimethylation and the MeK28-histone H3 output set; the H3K27-site refinement does not assert that this event is mono-only. The source's histone K28 numbering is distinguished from the conventional H3K27 name.

**Catalytic granularity and disease.** [GO:0140951](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/QuickGO/term/GO:0140951) explicitly describes three successive methyl transfers, so retaining it includes intermediate methylation steps. Adding its broader ancestor as another core would duplicate that activity. [PMID:22323599](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22323599/), Figures 3–4 and main Materials and Methods, directly compares wild-type and lymphoma-associated variant preferences. [PMID:26694085](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26694085/), Results and methyltransferase-assay Methods, tests Weaver-associated variants in reconstituted human PRC2. Several tested variants reduce activity; neither this experiment nor the separate EED/SUZ12 variant study establishes a uniform mechanism for every EZH2 allele.

**Evidence limits.** No new original figure-pixel inspection or inaccessible supplement recovery is claimed by this feedback pass. PMID:9214638 remains abstract-only. The lncRNA refinement rests on actual human biochemical assays, not on mouse RNA-ChIP or a generic claim that every PRC2-associated RNA is a ligand. Existing historical construct and natural-product boundaries remain unchanged.


## Consistent breadth and localization

Nuclear fractionation and promoter ChIP retain their actual resolutions; neither is relabeled as a new nucleoplasm imaging assay. Nucleoplasm is a finer nuclear compartment, not an assertion that EZH2 is absent from chromosomes or other supported pools.
