# SUCLG1 (P53597) notes

## 2026-11-11 first review

Written as part of the evolutionary review of the succinyl-CoA synthetase families (PTHR11117 alpha, PTHR11815 beta); see genes/yeast/LSC2/LSC2-bioinformatics/RESULTS.md.

- SUCLG1 is the single alpha subunit shared by the two human enzymes [PMID:30470562 "Succinate-CoA ligase (SUCL) is a heterodimer consisting of an alpha subunit encoded by SUCLG1, and a beta subunit encoded by either SUCLA2 or SUCLG2 catalyzing an ATP- or GTP-forming reaction, respectively, in the mitochondrial matrix."].
- The alpha subunit does not set nucleotide specificity [PMID:9765290 "Because the two succinyl-CoA synthetases incorporate the same alpha-subunit, the determinants of nucleotide specificity must reside within the beta-subunit"]. CoA binds the alpha subunit [PMID:32627745 "The CoA portion binds in the CoA-binding site located in the amino-terminal domain of the α-subunit"].
- Loss of SUCLG1 removes both beta subunits and all activity, with rescue [PMID:27484306 "The patient's cells lack the SUCLG1 protein, with significantly reduced levels of SUCLA2 and SUCLG2 protein."].
- Evolutionary reading of the GDP-forming rows: accepted here. Human SUCLG1 is the case the PAINT root placement was built from; the same node over-propagates to the fungal alpha subunits in the PANTHER tree (LSC1 removes it, S. pombe marks it over-annotated). The difference is the beta repertoire of the genome, not anything in the alpha subunit. (Corrected 2026-11-12: PTN000122012 is the LUCA node of the alpha tree, not its root, and the loss of the G-type beta gene is within fungi, not kingdom-wide.)
- GO:0004774 succinate-CoA ligase activity (IMP) marked TOO_GENERAL. The paper assayed ADP-dependent and GDP-dependent activity separately, and both were lost and rescued, so the two children are supported and the parent adds nothing [PMID:27484306 "Both ADP and GDP-specific SCS activities are completely restored in the patient’s cells with ectopic expression of SUCLG1 (Figure 1B)"]. (Corrected 2026-11-12: this bullet used to say the row was accepted at the parent level because the assay did not separate the two enzymes. Both statements were wrong.)
- Side activities (malate-CoA ligase, acid-thiol ligase, malate metabolic process) kept as non-core, matching SUCLA2. Toxic metabolite repair was marked over-annotated in this round and changed to UNDECIDED on 2026-11-12 (see below) [PMID:40108300 "Here we discover that CLYBL additionally functions as a metabolite repair enzyme for malyl-CoA, a side product of promiscuous citric acid cycle enzymes."]. PMID:40108300 is abstract-only in the cache.
- RNA binding (HDA, mRNA interactome capture) marked over-annotated.
- Protein binding IPI rows with SUCLG2 removed under the protein-binding policy; the interaction is real and is the GDP-forming complex.

## Retrospective audit corrections (2026-11-12)

A retrospective audit of the succinyl-CoA synthetase reviews found errors in this review. Each was checked against the source before it was changed.

- Itaconate. PMID:34492704 was cited as support for itaconate-CoA ligase activity, but it reports the opposite [PMID:34492704 "neither ATP- nor GTP-specific SCS is able to use itaconate under the same conditions as succinate in vitro"] and that itaconate inhibits the enzyme. The quote was removed from the proposed new term. The negative result is now a finding of that reference, marked DISPUTED against PMID:40108300. The description says that itaconyl-CoA formation is reported but contested. The GO:0004775 EXP row from this paper stays ACCEPT: recombinant SCS-A did convert succinate [PMID:34492704 "Succinate but not itaconate at 10 mM concentrations is metabolized by pig heart SCS-GTP (SCS-G) (A) and recombinant SCS-ATP (SCS-A) (B)."].
- PMID:40108300. Only the abstract is cached, and the published full text is not open. The bioRxiv preprint (doi 10.1101/2024.09.25.614871, version 1) was read. It assays recombinant human SUCLG1/SUCLA2 and SUCLG1/SUCLG2 with succinate, malate and itaconate; catalytic efficiencies are 30-fold (ADP-forming) and 15-fold (GDP-forming) lower with itaconate, and 340-fold and 540-fold lower with malate, than with succinate. The proposed term itaconate-CoA ligase activity is kept on that basis, with the conflict stated. The CLYBL abstract sentence was removed from the GO:0004775 and GO:0004776 EXP rows because it says nothing about succinate-CoA ligase activity. The reference stays UNVERIFIED because the published version was not read.
- GO:0110052 toxic metabolite repair (IDA, PMID:40108300): MARK_AS_OVER_ANNOTATED changed to UNDECIDED, the same as SUCLA2. The abstract and the preprint present succinyl-CoA synthetase as a source of malyl-CoA and CLYBL as the repair enzyme, which argues for over-annotation. The preprint also names succinyl-CoA synthetase with AUH and CLYBL as an enzyme of itaconate catabolism, so the curator may have annotated the activating step of a disposal route. The published paper was not read, so the experimental row is not overruled. A suggested question records what would settle it.
- GO:0004774 (IMP): action unchanged (TOO_GENERAL). The quote showing separate ADP- and GDP-dependent assays was added to this row, to the GO:0004776 EXP and IBA rows, to the GO:0004775 IBA row and to both core functions.
- GO:0016878 acid-thiol ligase (IDA, contributes_to): action unchanged (KEEP_AS_NON_CORE), reason rewritten. Malyl-CoA formation is covered by the descendant GO:0050074, which is on this gene. The row is retained only because itaconate-CoA ligase activity has no GO term. The proposed term's parent is GO:0016878 and not GO:0016405, which QuickGO defines as forming AMP and diphosphate.
- GO:0003723 RNA binding (HDA): action unchanged. The argument that the enzyme acts in the mitochondrial matrix was dropped from the reason. Table 1 of the paper (author-hosted PDF of the published article, not cached) lists SUCLG1 in the mRNA interactome class with 16 other metabolic enzymes.
- Phylogeny wording. The GO:0004776 IBA reason said fungal genomes lost the G-type beta paralog. The reworked family analysis shows the loss is within fungi [file:yeast/LSC2/LSC2-bioinformatics/RESULTS.md "The 15 fungal genomes in the PANTHER tree (14 Dikarya and the chytrid Batrachochytrium dendrobatidis) have an A-type beta gene and no G-type beta gene."], so the reason now speaks of the fungal alpha subunits in the tree. PTN000122012 is now called the LUCA node of the alpha-subunit family, not its root, on the four IBA rows that name it. Quotes of RESULTS.md sentences that no longer exist were replaced.
- Mitochondrion rows on IBA, IEA, NAS and TAS evidence: the sentence about an assay not resolving the sub-compartment was replaced by what each source rests on (the PAINT node and its seeds, the UniProt line from HAMAP rule MF_03222, an author statement). Actions unchanged.
- References. PMID:20693550: the note said the loss of SUCLA2 protein was not in the abstract. It is [PMID:20693550 "in the absence of SUCLG1 protein, no SUCLA2 protein is found in fibroblasts by western blot analysis"]; note corrected and finding added. Affinage record: correctness changed from DISPUTED to LOW_QUALITY, and the DISPUTED status moved to its GO grounding finding. Its one supporting quote on the GO:0004774 row was kept, beside the primary paper it was verified against. (Removed later on 2026-11-12: a record rated LOW_QUALITY should not support an annotation row; the row keeps its primary support. The validator's advisory warning that no annotation cites the deep research file is accepted.)
- Status changed from INITIALIZED to DRAFT.
- Cross-file consistency pass (2026-11-12). GO:0003824 catalytic activity (IEA) stays TOO_GENERAL; its reason is now worded the same way in all eleven subunit reviews of the family.
