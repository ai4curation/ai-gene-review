Gene Description

Humanin (HN) is a 24-residue peptide translated from a short open reading frame inside MT-RNR2, the mitochondrial 16S ribosomal RNA gene, and is the founding member of the mitochondrial-derived peptide family that also includes MOTS-c and the small humanin-like peptides SHLP1-6. It behaves as a secreted, cytoprotective signalling peptide: it is released from cells, circulates in plasma at levels that decline with age, and engages cell-surface receptors - the G protein-coupled formyl peptide receptors FPR2/FPRL1 and FPR3/FPRL2, and a gp130-family cytokine receptor complex containing CNTFR and WSX-1/IL27RA - triggering JAK/STAT3 signalling that protects neurons, retinal pigment epithelium, endothelium and pancreatic beta-cells from apoptotic insults. Humanin also has an intracellular arm: in the cytosol it binds the BCL-2-family proteins BAX, BID and the BimEL isoform of BIM, co-assembling with them into fibers that sequester them and prevent mitochondrial outer-membrane permeabilization, and it binds IGFBP3, blocking IGFBP3-dependent cell death and competing with importin-beta1 for IGFBP3. It binds amyloid-beta oligomers directly and delays amyloid fibril formation. Humanin homodimerizes through Ser7, and the determinants of its secretion and of its neuroprotective activity have been mapped residue by residue. It is detected in testis, sperm (concentrated in the flagellar midpiece), retina, heart, skeletal muscle, kidney and liver, and intracellularly in cytoplasm and mitochondria. Two features of the peptide remain unresolved: whether the active peptide is translated from the mitochondrial transcript (which, read with the mitochondrial genetic code, would yield a 21-residue product lacking the three C-terminal residues) or from one of the thirteen nuclear MT-RNR2-like loci that derive from mitochondrial DNA inserted into the nuclear genome; and how a peptide encoded in the mitochondrial genome reaches the extracellular space. Most functional data come from synthetic peptide applied exogenously, often as the more potent S14G analogue.

Proposed New Ontology Terms

insulin-like growth factor binding protein binding

Definition: Binding to an insulin-like growth factor binding protein (IGFBP), a secreted carrier protein of the IGFBP family.

Justification: Humanin binds IGFBP3 with high affinity and specificity, the interaction has been mapped to the IGFBP3 heparin-binding segment (residues 215-232), is abolished by the humanin F6A substitution, and has defined consequences: humanin blocks IGFBP3-induced cell death and competes with importin-beta1 for IGFBP3, preventing its nuclear import. Three independent GOA rows record this interaction as bare GO:0005515 protein binding, which conveys none of it. No existing GO term fits: GO:0005520 insulin-like growth factor binding and its children describe binding to IGF itself, not to the carrier, and GO:0019838 growth factor binding is wrong for the same reason. The term would also serve other IGFBP-binding proteins (for example the humanin-competitive hyaluronan-CD44 system described in PMID:30184438) rather than being gene-specific.

Parent term: protein binding

Suggested Questions for Experts

Q: Is the biologically active humanin peptide the product of the mitochondrial MT-RNR2 ORF or of one of the thirteen nuclear MT-RNR2-like (NUMT-derived) loci? Every antibody-based detection of humanin in tissue, and therefore several of the localization annotations on this entry, cannot distinguish the two. GO curation of this accession would benefit from an explicit convention for peptides whose coding assignment is unresolved.

Q: If humanin is translated from the mitochondrial transcript inside the organelle, the mitochondrial genetic code yields a 21-residue peptide lacking Lys-Arg-Arg-Ala. Which length is the physiological species, and do the published structure-activity data (all obtained with the 24-mer or the S14G 24-mer) apply to it?

Q: How does a peptide encoded in the mitochondrial genome reach the extracellular space? Secretion is well documented and its sequence determinants are mapped, but no export route from the matrix, and no cytoplasmic translation mechanism for an mtDNA-encoded mRNA, has been demonstrated.

Q: Do the humanin-BAX and humanin-BID fibers characterised in vitro with purified recombinant proteins exist in cells, and at what stoichiometry? This is the mechanistic basis of the anti-apoptotic annotations and has so far only been shown cell-free.

Q: Which receptor system accounts for which phenotype? FPR2 knockdown does not abolish humanin neuroprotection in F11 cells (PMID:16005025), whereas the CNTFR/WSX-1/gp130 complex is required (PMID:19386761); several of the cytoprotective process annotations are not attributed to either receptor.

Q: Does humanin have a function in sperm? Localization to the flagellar midpiece is replicated and seminal-plasma levels track sperm concentration and motility, but no causal experiment exists, and the sperm annotations are purely locational.

Q: GO has no term for binding an insulin-like growth factor binding protein, so the three humanin-IGFBP3 rows sit as bare protein binding. Should such a term be created (see proposed_new_terms), or should the interaction be captured on IGFBP3 instead?

Humanin (Q8IVG9, HUNIN_HUMAN) — curation notes

Folder follows the <HOST>__<ACC> convention for alternative-ORF peptides
(CLAUDE.md, "Alternative-ORF peptides"): UniProt files humanin under the host
symbol MT-RNR2, the mitochondrial 16S rRNA gene, and the peptide has its own
accession and its own GOA rows (76).

1. What the gene is

Humanin (HN) is a 24-residue peptide (MAPRGFSCLL LLTSEIDLPV KRRA) whose ORF
lies inside the mitochondrial 16S rRNA gene MT-RNR2. It was found by functional
screening of an Alzheimer brain cDNA library for a factor protecting neurons
from familial AD genes PMID:11371646.

2. The coding question is genuinely unresolved

UniProt itself carries a CAUTION on this entry. Three distinct uncertainties:

  1. Genetic code / peptide length. The ORF read with the cytoplasmic code
    gives 24 aa; read with the mitochondrial code the last three residues are
    lost (21 aa). UniProt: "If translation of the mRNA occurs in the
    mitochondrion rather than in the cytoplasm, then the usage of the
    mitochondrial genetic code would lead to the production of a shorter peptide
    lacking the last three C-terminal residues."
  2. Where translation happens. No mechanism has been shown for export of an
    mtDNA-encoded mRNA to the cytoplasm, nor for secretion of a peptide made in
    the matrix. UniProt: "The mechanisms allowing the production and the
    secretion of humanin remain unclear."
  3. Which locus actually makes the active peptide. 13 nuclear MT-RNR2-like
    loci (NUMT-derived MTRNR2L1-13) are predicted to encode 15 full-length
    HN-like peptides, and at least ten are expressed PMID:19477263. Guo et al. made the same point from the other
    direction PMID:12732850. In-vivo peptide detection used an anti-HN antibody
    that cannot distinguish the mitochondrial product from the nuclear paralogs
    PMID:12009529.

Consequence for curation: every immunodetection-based localization row on this
entry (nucleus, mitochondrion, sperm compartments) is in principle a statement
about "HN immunoreactivity", not about this accession specifically. I have not
used this to remove well-replicated locations, but it is the reason I treat the
single-study nuclear and perinuclear rows as over-annotations.

A second, pervasive caveat: most functional work uses synthetic peptide added
exogenously, frequently at micromolar concentrations, and frequently the
S14G ("HNG") analogue, which is 2-3 orders of magnitude more potent than HN
PMID:12787071.

3. Direct molecular activities (what HN itself does)

Receptor ligand. Two receptor systems, both with direct binding data.
- GPCRs FPR2/FPRL1 and FPR3/FPRL2: PMID:15465011, with direct membrane binding ("We demonstrated by binding
experiments using [(125)I]-W peptide that HN and fHN directly interacted with
hFPRL1 on the membrane") and EC50 3.5 nM for HN at FPRL1. Functional output:
chemotaxis of mononuclear phagocytes PMID:15153530.
HN also competes with Abeta42 for the same receptor, which is the basis of the
receptor antagonist activity annotation.
- A gp130-family cytokine receptor complex: PMID:19386761. Downstream signalling
is JAK/STAT3 PMID:16005025, confirmed in RPE cells
PMID:26990160.
Note PMID:16005025 explicitly dissociates the two systems: FPR2 knockdown did
not abolish HN rescue in F11 cells.

BH3-domain / BCL-2-family binding. The intracellular arm.
- BAX: PMID:12732850.
- BID/tBID: PMID:15661737.
- BimEL only: PMID:15661735.
- Mechanism: co-fibrillation and sequestration PMID:31690630; PMID:33106313. Both are in-vitro recombinant studies.

Amyloid-beta binding / anti-fibrillization.
PMID:28282805;
PMID:27349871.

IGFBP3 binding. High-affinity, functionally consequential, but GO has no
term for it (no "insulin-like growth factor binding protein binding").
PMID:14561895; Kd 5.05 uM and competition with importin-beta1
PMID:26216267. Mapped to the IGFBP3 heparin-binding
domain, and competitive with hyaluronan for the same segment
PMID:30184438.

Homodimerization. Required for neuroprotection, Ser7-dependent
PMID:12787071.

Low-value interactions. TRIM11 (Y2H; destabilizes HN, so TRIM11's substrate

4. Localization

Secretion is well supported PMID:11371646 and residues required for it were mapped PMID:12860203. HN circulates and declines with
age PMID:19623253. Cytoplasmic pool is needed for the BAX arm. Mitochondrial signal is
reported by EM-immunohistochemistry PMID:15567815 and by uptake of labelled
peptide PMID:26990160 — i.e. exogenous peptide entering mitochondria, not evidence of
matrix synthesis. Sperm midpiece/flagellum localization is replicated by two

the spermatozoa"].

5. Downstream phenotypes (the over-annotation zone)

A single HUVEC study generates five GOA rows (IL-1 production, IL-18 production,
inflammatory response, NLRP3 inflammasome assembly, response to oxidative
stress) PMID:32923762. Others: astrocyte neuroinflammation PMID:23277413, PGC1A-driven
mitochondrial biogenesis in MIN6 beta-cells PMID:29432738, ER-stress
protection via mitochondrial GSH PMID:27783653, insulin sensitivity PMID:19623253. These are consequences of receptor signalling in whichever cell
type was assayed; they are not separate activities of the peptide, and the
IL-1/IL-18-production and oxidative-stress rows in particular are two steps
downstream of anything HN touches.

Two rows are flatly unsupported:
- GO:0006879 intracellular iron ion homeostasis (NAS) rests only on humanin
immunoreactivity being seen near iron deposits in villonodular synovitis
PMID:15567815. Proximity to iron
is not iron homeostasis. REMOVE.
- GO:0048471 perinuclear region of cytoplasm rests on GFP-HN overexpression
colocalizing with endogenous actinin in HEK293 PMID:15619032.

    informative term where one exists (BH3 domain binding for BAX/BID/BimEL,

    CLAUDE.md excludes as redundancy rather than coverage.

suggested_questions:
- question: Is the biologically active humanin peptide the product of the mitochondrial MT-RNR2 ORF or
    of one of the thirteen nuclear MT-RNR2-like (NUMT-derived) loci? Every antibody-based detection of
    humanin in tissue, and therefore several of the localization annotations on this entry, cannot distinguish
    the two. GO curation of this accession would benefit from an explicit convention for peptides whose
    coding assignment is unresolved.
- question: If humanin is translated from the mitochondrial transcript inside the organelle, the mitochondrial
    genetic code yields a 21-residue peptide lacking Lys-Arg-Arg-Ala. Which length is the physiological
    species, and do the published structure-activity data (all obtained with the 24-mer or the S14G 24-mer)
    apply to it?
- question: How does a peptide encoded in the mitochondrial genome reach the extracellular space? Secretion
    is well documented and its sequence determinants are mapped, but no export route from the matrix,
    and no cytoplasmic translation mechanism for an mtDNA-encoded mRNA, has been demonstrated.
- question: Do the humanin-BAX and humanin-BID fibers characterised in vitro with purified recombinant
    proteins exist in cells, and at what stoichiometry? This is the mechanistic basis of the anti-apoptotic
    annotations and has so far only been shown cell-free.
- question: Which receptor system accounts for which phenotype? FPR2 knockdown does not abolish humanin
    neuroprotection in F11 cells (PMID:16005025), whereas the CNTFR/WSX-1/gp130 complex is required (PMID:19386761);
    several of the cytoprotective process annotations are not attributed to either receptor.
- question: Does humanin have a function in sperm? Localization to the flagellar midpiece is replicated
    and seminal-plasma levels track sperm concentration and motility, but no causal experiment exists,
    and the sperm annotations are purely locational.
- question: GO has no term for binding an insulin-like growth factor binding protein, so the three humanin-IGFBP3
    rows sit as bare protein binding. Should such a term be created (see proposed_new_terms), or should
    the interaction be captured on IGFBP3 instead?
