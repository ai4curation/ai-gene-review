CC   -!- FUNCTION: A component of desmosome cell-cell junctions which are
CC       required for positive regulation of cellular adhesion
CC       (PubMed:31835537). Required for adherens and desmosome junction
CC       assembly in response to mechanical force in keratinocytes
CC       (PubMed:31835537). Required for desmosome-mediated cell-cell adhesion
CC       of cells surrounding the telogen hair club and the basal layer of the
CC       outer root sheath epithelium, consequently is essential for the
CC       anchoring of telogen hairs in the hair follicle (PubMed:9701552).
CC       Required for the maintenance of the epithelial barrier via promoting
CC       desmosome-mediated intercellular attachment of suprabasal epithelium to
CC       basal cells (By similarity). May play a role in the protein stability
CC       of the desmosome plaque components DSP, JUP, PKP1, PKP2 and PKP3
CC       (PubMed:22294297). Required for YAP1 localization at the plasma
CC       membrane in keratinocytes in response to mechanical strain, via the
CC       formation of an interaction complex composed of DSG3, PKP1 and YWHAG
CC       (PubMed:31835537). May also be involved in the positive regulation of
CC       YAP1 target gene transcription and as a result cell proliferation
CC       (PubMed:31835537). Positively regulates cellular contractility and cell
CC       junction formation via organization of cortical F-actin bundles and
CC       anchoring of actin to tight junctions, in conjunction with RAC1
CC       (PubMed:22796473). The cytoplasmic pool of DSG3 is required for the
CC       localization of CDH1 and CTNNB1 at developing adherens junctions,
CC       potentially via modulation of SRC activity (PubMed:22294297). Inhibits
CC       keratinocyte migration via suppression of p38MAPK signaling, may
CC       therefore play a role in moderating wound healing (PubMed:26763450).
CC       {ECO:0000250|UniProtKB:O35902, ECO:0000269|PubMed:22294297,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:22796473, ECO:0000269|PubMed:26763450,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:31835537, ECO:0000269|PubMed:9701552}.
CC   -!- SUBUNIT: Homodimer (PubMed:19717567). Part of a complex that contains
CC       DSG3, PKP1, YAP1 and YWHAG; the complex is required for localization of
CC       DSG3 and YAP1 to the cell membrane in keratinocytes (PubMed:31835537).
CC       Interacts with PKP2 (PubMed:11790773). Interacts with CTNND1; the
CC       interaction facilitates DSG3 localization and retention at cell-cell
CC       junctions (PubMed:18343367). Interacts with CDH1; the interaction is
CC       required for CDH1 localization to developing adherens junctions
CC       (PubMed:22294297). Interacts with RAC1; the interaction is required for
CC       DSG3 translocation to cell-cell junctions, organization of cortical F-
CC       actin bundles and actin anchoring at cell-cell junctions
CC       (PubMed:22796473). Interacts with DSC3; the interaction may limit the
CC       interaction of DSC3 with p38MAPK family members and therefore repress
CC       p38MAPK signaling activation (By similarity).
CC       {ECO:0000250|UniProtKB:O35902, ECO:0000269|PubMed:11790773,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:18343367, ECO:0000269|PubMed:19717567,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:22294297, ECO:0000269|PubMed:22796473,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:31835537}.
CC   -!- SUBCELLULAR LOCATION: Cell membrane {ECO:0000269|PubMed:19717567,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:30949721, ECO:0000269|PubMed:31835537}; Single-pass
CC       type I membrane protein {ECO:0000255}. Cell junction, desmosome
CC       {ECO:0000250|UniProtKB:O35902}. Cytoplasm {ECO:0000269|PubMed:22796473,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:31835537}. Cell junction, tight junction
CC       {ECO:0000269|PubMed:22796473}. Cell junction
CC       {ECO:0000269|PubMed:18343367}.
CC   -!- TISSUE SPECIFICITY: Expressed throughout the basal and spinous layer of
CC       the epidermis with weak expression in the granular layer (at protein
CC       level) (PubMed:19717567). Expressed in skin and mucosa (at protein
CC       level) (PubMed:22294297, PubMed:30528827). Expressed throughout the
CC       esophageal mucosa (at protein level) (PubMed:24220297). Expressed in
CC       the basal layer of the outer root sheath of the telogen hair club,
CC       specifically at the cell membrane between the apex of the cells and the
CC       surrounding hair club (at protein level) (PubMed:9701552). Expression
CC       is less abundant between the lateral margins of the outer root sheath
CC       basal cells (at protein level) (PubMed:9701552). Also expressed in the
CC       tongue, tonsil and esophagus (PubMed:16740002).
CC       {ECO:0000269|PubMed:16740002, ECO:0000269|PubMed:19717567,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:22294297, ECO:0000269|PubMed:24220297,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:30528827, ECO:0000269|PubMed:9701552}.
CC   -!- INDUCTION: Induced in the hours following cyclic mechanical strain in
CC       keratinocytes. {ECO:0000269|PubMed:31835537}.
CC   -!- DOMAIN: Three calcium ions are usually bound at the interface of each
CC       cadherin domain and rigidify the connections, imparting a strong
CC       curvature to the full-length ectodomain. {ECO:0000250}.
CC   -!- DISEASE: Blistering, acantholytic, of oral and laryngeal mucosa (ABOLM)
CC       [MIM:619226]: An autosomal recessive disorder characterized by
CC       recurrent, suprabasal acantholytic blisters in the oral and laryngeal
CC       mucosa. Skin, conjunctival and genital mucosa, nail folds, and nails
CC       are unaffected. Normal structure is observed in the scalp epidermis and
CC       hair follicle. {ECO:0000269|PubMed:30528827}. Note=The disease may be
CC       caused by variants affecting the gene represented in this entry.
CC   -!- MISCELLANEOUS: Pemphigus vulgaris (PV) is a potentially lethal skin
CC       disease in which epidermal blisters occur as the result of the loss of
CC       cell-cell adhesion caused by the action of autoantibodies against
CC       desmoglein 3 (PubMed:1720352). Loss of expression at points of cell-
CC       cell contact between the basal and suprabasal layers in blisters and
CC       immediately adjacent to blisters in PV (PubMed:22294297). In a PV
CC       patient acantholysis extends from the epidermis of the scalp down the
CC       entire outer root sheath of mostly empty dilated hair follicles that
CC       contain only small residual unanchored hair shafts and necrotic
CC       detached cells (PubMed:9701552). Although hair loss is not a major
CC       phenotype in PV due to the lower number of hair follicles in telogen
CC       phase in humans, this would suggest DSG3 disruption results in hair
CC       follicle abnormalities (PubMed:9701552). {ECO:0000269|PubMed:1720352,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:22294297, ECO:0000269|PubMed:9701552}.
