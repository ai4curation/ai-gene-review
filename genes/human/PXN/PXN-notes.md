# PXN mechanobiology review notes

PXN encodes paxillin, a focal-adhesion LIM/LD adaptor that binds multiple focal
adhesion proteins and couples integrins to downstream FAK/Src/Crk/Rac
signalling. The original human cloning paper described paxillin as a
"68-kDa focal adhesion protein" that is tyrosine phosphorylated after Src
transformation, PDGF treatment, or integrin cross-linking, has candidate Src SH3
and Crk SH2 docking motifs, and coprecipitates with vinculin and FAK
[PMID:7534286].

The core FAK connection is direct rather than a generic complex
co-purification: Tachibana et al. mapped the paxillin-binding region of FAK to
the FAK C-terminal domain and concluded that FAK targets to focal adhesions by
direct association with paxillin [PMID:7561682]. Paxillin beta and gamma
isoforms have distinct biochemical preferences: beta binds FAK strongly and
vinculin weakly, gamma binds vinculin strongly and FAK weakly, and both bind
talin [PMID:9054445].

The alpha4 integrin cytoplasmic tail binds paxillin and changes the biological
output of alpha4-containing integrins: a tail mutation that disrupts paxillin
binding reversed alpha4-tail effects on FAK phosphorylation, migration,
spreading, and focal adhesion and stress-fiber formation [PMID:10604475]. PXN
therefore belongs in the integrin adhesome force-coupling layer, but the GOA
set has no direct `detection of mechanical stimulus` or
`response to mechanical stimulus` row. The NRP1/ABL1 study demonstrates a
fibronectin-induced endothelial phosphorylation and stress-fiber/migration axis
through paxillin, not a primary force-detection function [PMID:24863063].

Rows mapped to focal adhesion are central. PXN was found in the myosin-II
responsive focal adhesion proteome [PMID:21423176]; pY31-PXN was localized to
matrix adhesions at the ends of TGF-beta-induced ventral stress fibers
[PMID:24036928]; gamma-actin knockdown enlarged paxillin-marked focal
adhesions [PMID:25705373]; and phosphorylation of focal-adhesion paxillin was
used as a readout of focal-adhesion signalling in NRP1/ABL1 and VHR/DUSP3
studies [PMID:24863063; PMID:28759036].

Several rows are disease- or ligand-specific contexts rather than core
function. Paxillin/beta-catenin interaction in oxidized phospholipid-treated
endothelium links focal adhesions to adherens junctions [PMID:17513457], and
PXN knockdown suppresses colorectal or gastric cancer cell migration in
miR-137/miR-212 studies [PMID:23275153; PMID:26693054]. Growth-hormone-induced
phosphorylation of paxillin in neutrophils and H2O2-induced paxillin
mislocalization in endothelial cells are downstream readouts, not evidence that
PXN is a growth hormone receptor signalling component or a reactive-oxygen
sensor [PMID:10925297; PMID:12686598].

The Reactome focal-adhesion events that generate cytosol and plasma-membrane
component rows are modeled with both `cytosol` and `plasma membrane`
compartments in the Reactome ContentService. The component calls are retained
as non-core locations because paxillin is a cytosolic adaptor recruited to
plasma-membrane-proximal focal adhesions.

The Human Protein Atlas PXN subcellular profile
(https://www.proteinatlas.org/ENSG00000089159-PXN/subcellular) reports that
paxillin is "Mainly localized to the focal adhesion sites. In addition
localized to the cytosol and centrosome."
