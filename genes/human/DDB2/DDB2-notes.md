# DDB2 review notes

## 2026-10-17 — complex contribution in the core summary

The second core now records GO:0004842 ubiquitin-protein transferase activity under `contributes_to_molecular_function`. The existing annotation (zero-based index 72; IDA, PMID:12732143) already uses `contributes_to` and remains accepted. DDB2 recognizes damaged DNA and helps target the CRL4 complex; the correction does not assign independent ubiquitin-transfer catalysis to DDB2 or add a new annotation. All source qualifiers, reference identifiers, product records and annotation decisions are preserved.

The [PMID:12732143 abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12732143/) reports ubiquitin ligase activity in assembled DDB2/DDB1/CUL4A/ROC1 complexes. Its original assay text was not accessible during this focused correction. In the original [PMID:16473935](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1413840/) Methods and Results, human normal and XP-E cell comparisons show DDB2-dependent complex recruitment to UV-damaged chromatin and changes in H2A ubiquitination. Native-chromatin co-immunoprecipitation establishes association in that context, not isolated DDB2-catalyzed transfer or purified binary DDB2–H2A binding.

The original [PMID:19109893](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2676164/) structure study distinguishes human and zebrafish DDB2 complexes. DDB2 makes the damaged-DNA contacts; the relationship to the ubiquitination zone is explicitly a structural model. The human construct comprises residues 10–427 and was expressed with DDB1 in insect cells. That experiment does not establish equivalent function of all five natural products. The [PMID:14751237 abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14751237/) specifically reports dominant-negative repair effects for D1/D2, while the full paper remained inaccessible. No new function is assigned to D3/D4. Original publication cache bytes and availability flags remain unchanged despite additive access to the two PMC originals.

The actual current [GO:0004842 definition](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/QuickGO/term/GO:0004842) describes ubiquitin transfer; the schema provides the contribution slot for a subunit's role in a complex-level activity. [GO:1990756](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/QuickGO/term/GO:1990756), ubiquitin-like ligase-substrate adaptor activity, was considered but not newly assigned in this focused change: its complete binding assertion needs its own source assessment. The existing 18 generic protein-binding annotations remain non-core under the standing [project curation instruction](https://github.com/ai4curation/ai-gene-review/blob/a87ae568676c4012ea4abe5393967aeff1b52ae7/projects/CLINGEN_MENDELIAN.md#curation-instructions).
