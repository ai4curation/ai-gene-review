# G6PD evidence and source guide

The review centers on cytosolic NADP-dependent glucose-6-phosphate oxidation. G6PD performs the entry reaction of the oxidative pentose-phosphate pathway and directly reduces NADP+. Downstream glutathione recycling, lipid synthesis and redox-sensitive signaling depend on its products but are carried out by other enzymes. The annotation rationales distinguish direct catalysis from those contextual contributions.

The review YAML contains the current judgments and 39 manual reference assessments. The source-evidence archive retains the 66 original contexts and their current joins, three natural-product sequences, inherited review and journal, source-access records, and quotation locators. Its compact PAINT section includes the four literal ancestral assertions, target lineage, retained negative assertions and original book URLs/hashes. The single urine extension remains in its original context; it is not reconstructed from the older auxiliary projection.

[PMID:38066190](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38066190/) has genuine full text in the retained normal cache. Selected Results, Table 1, size-exclusion chromatography descriptions, figure captions and Methods were read. His-tagged human wild type and R219G were expressed in bacteria; NADPH formation was measured with G6P and NADP+. Wild type predominated as a dimer under the reported conditions, whereas the mutant had dimeric and monomeric populations. Michaelis constants are kinetic parameters rather than direct binding dissociation constants. Transcript Arg249 and canonical Arg219 numbering are distinct. Figure captions were read; this is not a new image audit.

The canonical P11413-1 product is 515 residues. P11413-2 is 561 residues and P11413-3 is 545 residues, with all three sequences preserved from direct official FASTA responses. Engineered deletions, substitutions and tags do not establish equal activity or localization of these natural products. The core describes the established enzyme, not experimentally demonstrated equivalence of all products.
