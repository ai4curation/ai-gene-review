Gene Description

STK36 (Fused homolog) is a 1315-residue serine/threonine protein kinase (EC 2.7.11.1) and the vertebrate orthologue of Drosophila Fused, with which it shares PANTHER subfamily PTHR22983:SF6. Its N-terminal kinase domain is activated by trans-autophosphorylation of the activation loop on Thr158 and Ser159 in response to Hedgehog stimulation, and is followed by a long C-terminal region of unknown structure. STK36 has two distinct roles. In Hedgehog signalling it is one of two vertebrate Fused-family kinases, with ULK3, that convert the latent GLI transcription factors into their activator form: at the tip of the primary cilium, in a complex with the pseudokinase ULK4 that bridges kinase to substrate, STK36 phosphorylates GLI2 on Ser230 and Ser232 - sites conserved with the Fused sites on Cubitus interruptus - which weakens the GLI-SUFU interaction and permits GLI nuclear entry and target-gene transcription. STK36 also phosphorylates ULK4 itself on Thr1021 and Thr1023, and the two proteins depend on each other to accumulate at the ciliary tip. Independently of Hedgehog signalling, STK36 is required to build the central pair apparatus of motile 9+2 cilia, where it sits between the radial spokes and the central pair and works with the kinesin KIF27 and central-pair structural proteins. Because ULK3 can substitute for it in Hedgehog signalling, mouse knockouts develop normally and respond normally to Hedgehog, but die postnatally of progressive hydrocephalus from defective ependymal ciliary beating and disrupted cerebrospinal fluid flow. In humans, biallelic loss of function causes primary ciliary dyskinesia 46, with a central-pair axonemal defect, disordered ciliary orientation and no situs abnormality. STK36 is cytoplasmic and axonemal, with a low-level nuclear pool, and is expressed most highly in adult testis germ cells and in respiratory epithelium.

Core Functions

Fused-family serine/threonine kinase activity that converts GLI into its activator form at the ciliary tip. On Hedgehog stimulation STK36 autophosphorylates its activation loop on Thr158 and Ser159 and accumulates at the tip of the primary cilium together with the pseudokinase ULK4 and GLI2, where it phosphorylates GLI2 on Ser230 and Ser232 - the sites corresponding to the Fused sites on Drosophila Ci. The phosphorylation weakens the GLI-SUFU interaction and licenses GLI nuclear entry and target-gene transcription. STK36 also phosphorylates ULK4 on Thr1021/Thr1023, which is what recruits ULK4 to the tip, so the two proteins are mutually dependent for the localisation this step requires. ULK3 performs the same phosphorylation in parallel, which is why mouse knockouts of STK36 alone show no Hedgehog phenotype while the biochemical activity is fully conserved from Drosophila Fused.

Suggested Questions for Experts

Q: UniProt records, from the original Murone paper, that GLI2 requires a function of STK36 that does not need an active catalytic site. What is that non-catalytic activity? If it is a scaffolding or adaptor role it would deserve a molecular function of its own, but no mechanism has been proposed in twenty-five years and the claim currently rests on a kinase-dead rescue in overexpression.

Q: Do STK36 and ULK3 phosphorylate GLI2 in the same cells, or does each dominate in a particular tissue? Epistasis places them in parallel pathways, and the difference decides whether the absence of a Hedgehog phenotype in Stk36-null mice reflects true redundancy or a division of labour that the assayed tissues happened not to reveal.

Q: Is the central-pair function of STK36 catalytic? The PCD alleles are loss-of-function rather than kinase-dead point mutants, and no axonemal substrate has been identified, so it remains open whether STK36 phosphorylates a central-pair component or contributes structurally through its interactions with KIF27 and SPAG16.

Q: STK36 partners with a Costal-2-family kinesin in both of its roles - KIF7 in the ciliary tip GLI complex and KIF27 in central-pair construction. Is that a conserved ancestral Fused-kinesin module that the two vertebrate paralogue pairs inherited and specialised, or convergence? A reconstruction across the PTHR22983 and PTHR24115 trees would answer it.

Q: Human STK36 has two isoforms differing by a 21-residue deletion (VSP_051983, residues 838-858) in the uncharacterised C-terminal region, and isoform 2 was cloned from brain. No GOA row for STK36 is isoform-qualified. Do the isoforms differ in ULK4 binding, ciliary targeting, or between motile and primary cilia?

1. What the protein is

STK36 (Fused homolog, EC 2.7.11.1) is a 1315-residue serine/threonine protein
kinase with an N-terminal kinase domain (UniProt DOMAIN 4..254, catalytic
proton acceptor Asp-125, ATP-binding Lys-33) followed by a long, largely

This is the part of STK36 biology a reviewer has to get right, because the
literature looks contradictory until the redundancy is taken into account.

  • GO:0090660 cerebrospinal fluid circulation. Two mouse studies tie STK36
    loss to hydrocephalus and defective CSF flow, and one of them puts the phrase
    in its title. It fails the participation test: STK36 does not move
    cerebrospinal fluid. The ependymal motile cilia do, and STK36's contribution is
    to build them. Annotating the fluid-flow process would be annotating a

    motile-cilium-assembly replacement is where this evidence belongs.
