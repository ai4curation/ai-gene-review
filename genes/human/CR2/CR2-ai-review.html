Gene Description

CR2 (CD21) is a complement-fragment receptor expressed at the cell surface, with an extracellular array of complement-control repeats, a single membrane-spanning segment and a short cytoplasmic tail. It recognizes C3-derived ligands and participates in B-cell co-receptor complexes that amplify antigen responses. CR2 also binds CD23 and Epstein–Barr virus gp350/220; the latter interaction supports viral attachment and entry. Interferon-alpha and DNA interactions have been reported in defined experimental contexts. Four protein products differ within the extracellular region, while the N-terminal ligand-binding domains and membrane anchor are retained.

2026-10-12: identity, receptor function and source scope

Human CR2 (CD21), HGNC:2336, UniProt P20023 is a complement-fragment receptor with extracellular complement-control repeats, one membrane anchor and a short cytoplasmic tail. The central function is recognition of C3-derived ligands in a B-cell co-receptor system that amplifies activation. The review retains one complement-receptor/B-cell activation core. EBV entry, CD23 binding, interferon-associated responses and the reported DNA interaction are positive contexts with their own experimental limits.

The source evidence file contains the complete 39 current source objects and literal GOA records, product reconstruction, exact rule predicates, actual PAINT path/IBDs, donor records, pathway component paths and public primary-source locators. No historical review was present, so no annotation has been retired or merged away. Original source fields and ordinary qualifiers are unchanged. The assessment adds no NEW annotation.

Complement recognition and B-cell signaling

The C3d mutagenesis study actually assayed “mutant iC3b molecules”, so its complement-binding assertion spans a C3d-region interface in a larger complement product. This supports retaining GO:0001848 for that source. The two CR2–C3d structural assertions can be refined to the specific C3d-binding child GO:0001854. (PMID:11034390(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11034390/); 1GHQ; 3OED.)

The primary deposits authenticate human CR2 canonical residues 21–153, with an A prefix in 1GHQ and an MG prefix in 3OED. They are isolated N-terminal domain complexes, not intact receptor or four-product comparisons. The later study disputes the earlier interface geometry, including crystallization conditions, without disputing C3d recognition. Both Science full articles remained unavailable after bounded retrieval attempts; the complete abstracts and authentic deposits provide the actual accessible evidence. (PMID:11387479(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11387479/); PMID:21527715(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21527715/).)

The receptor-complex literature describes CR2 as a “ligand-binding subunit” associated with CD19. Reciprocal immunoprecipitation and K562 reconstitution distinguish the CR2/CD19 association from proteins primarily associated with CD19. The later CD19/CD21/TAPA-1/Leu-13 work provides additional surface-complex and signaling evidence. These papers' complete abstracts were read, but their full Methods were not recovered; the experimental curator assertions are retained at the stated resolution. (PMID:1702139(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1702139/); PMID:1383329(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1383329/).)

The human CD21/FCRL5 study reports physical association and stronger calcium responses following joint CD21/FCRL5/BCR engagement, including tonsil B cells. Its conclusion that these cells could “robustly respond” to C3-fragment-loaded immune complexes supports B-cell activation without making CR2 a kinase. This is also why the broad immune-response NAS assertion is refined to B-cell activation. (PMID:30107486(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30107486/).)

GO:0004875 is retained in the compact core because C3d, C3dg and iC3b are genuine ligand contexts of the shared receptor activity. It does not imply C3a or C5a recognition. The broad existing signaling-receptor-complex class GO:0043235 fits the independently established assembly. Its plasma-membrane child GO:0098802 explicitly discourages direct annotation, and GO:0019815 defines the antigen-binding immunoglobulin/Ig-alpha/Ig-beta BCR, not automatically the CD21/CD19/CD81 co-receptor complex. A finer complex identifier is therefore not invented.

CD23 binding and the direction of the functional experiment

The original 1992 article was recovered as its three-page publisher PDF. Human CD21 cDNA expressed in BHK-21 cells binds CD23 liposomes, with mock-transfected cells, control liposomes and antibody competition. Its functional experiment is captioned “Engagement of CD21”: soluble CD23 fusion protein or anti-CD21 is combined with IL-4, and IgE output is measured. This is not an independently demonstrated CR2-triggered change in CD23 receptor activity. Refining the existing receptor-ligand assertion to immunoglobulin-receptor binding preserves the actual positive interaction without denying reciprocal biology or the authors' ligand terminology. (PMID:1386409, original pages 505–507 and Figure 4.)

The later full JEM study directly measures human CD21 D1–2 binding to human CD23 by SPR and NMR mapping. The reported affinity is 0.87 ± 0.09 µM for the tested fragments; calcium removal does not change the measured CD21 interaction. The IgE and CD21 sites on CD23 are distinct. A ternary arrangement bridged by CD23 does not establish direct CR2–IgE binding, and the Fc-gamma-RI-binding child of GO:0034987 does not fit CD23. (PMID:16172256, binding Results/Figure 1, mapping/Figure 4, construct and SPR Methods.)

Additional experimentally positive contexts

The original DNA study was read in full main-text scope. Human soluble SCR1–4 and SCR1–15 preparations bind DNA in ELISA/SPR assays with competition controls. However, high nonspecific cell binding “did not allow the reproducible detection of CR2-dependent binding”. The study used 50 mM NaCl and reported no high-affinity DNA binding at 150 mM NaCl. The positive molecular interaction is retained as non-core; it is not a transcription-factor assay, a demonstration of nuclear activity, or proof of physiological surface affinity for every product. (PMID:22885687, Methods, Results and Discussion.)

Original 1991 scans establish IFN-alpha/CR2 binding, competition, Raji-cell binding and proliferation effects. Later human B-cell work reports that anti-CR2 antibody “diminishes the induction of IFN-alpha-responsive genes.” This supports a contextual interferon contribution, with the relationship to IFNAR1/IFNAR2 still unresolved. The 2006 full Methods were not recovered. The protected GO-CAM names MX1 and EIF2AK2 downstream of CR2 and cites the 1991 article for both links, but the original 1991 assays did not measure those named gene-induction readouts. This is a source-scope question, not a reason to erase the positive receptor-associated response. (PMID:1849076, original EMBO Journal pages 919–926; PMID:16785534(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16785534/); GO-CAM 66187e4700002881.)

Selected anti-CR2 antibodies stimulate human B-cell DNA synthesis and later polyclonal immunoglobulin secretion, with T-cell help required. The differentiation row's review excerpt anchors the secretion endpoint; the proliferation and differentiation are retained as contextual downstream outcomes, without a direct cell-cycle or lineage-commitment claim. A B-cell response requiring T-cell help does not itself settle GO:0002456, which refers to an immune response carried out by T cells. (PMID:2995485(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2995485/).)

The EBV gp350/220 literature reports receptor-mediated adsorption, capping and endocytosis. These positive virus-receptor and entry annotations remain non-core host-exploitation functions; they do not require a second normal-host core.
