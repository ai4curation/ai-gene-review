Gene Description

CLCN5 encodes ClC-5, a voltage-dependent chloride:proton exchanger concentrated in renal epithelial endosomal membranes. Coupled ion transport supports proximal-tubule handling of filtered proteins and solutes, and pathogenic loss of function causes Dent disease. ClC-5 also occupies Golgi and plasma membrane pools whose abundance depends on cell context and trafficking partners, including KIF3B and TMEM9-family proteins. Its cytoplasmic CBS-domain region recognizes ATP, ADP and AMP. The physiological consequences of nucleotide occupancy and the distribution of the two human protein products remain incompletely resolved.

Core Functions

ClC-5 exchanges chloride and protons across endosomal membranes, supporting proximal-tubule absorption of filtered proteins and solutes. Its proton-transport activity can acidify endosomes under suitable ion gradients; renal uptake also depends on coupled exchange when bulk ATP-driven acidification remains normal. Cytoplasmic adenyl-ribonucleotide recognition and trafficking interactions accompany this transport function.

Coupled transport and renal function

The early human study links CLCN5 variants with renal stone disorders and reduced oocyte currents. Its complete abstract was read, but the original full article was not recovered; a repository route also returned an access challenge. Those observations are preserved as positive current and renal-function evidence, not represented as proton-flux measurements PMID:8559248. Later original ClC-4/ClC-5 experiments directly measure proton transport and distinguish coupled exchange using gradient, channel and uncoupling controls. Their stoichiometric estimate is approximate, not a precise universal 2:1 measurement PMID:16034422. This supports refining the historical channel, generic transporter and antiporter labels to chloride:proton antiporter activity (GO:0062158), without rewriting the source assertions.

Mouse E211A knock-in experiments separate exchange from chloride conduction: uptake defects persist despite preserved bulk ATP-driven vesicular acidification. The original article and selected supplementary Methods include littermate, localization and uptake controls. The chloride-accumulation interpretation is a model rather than a directly measured endosomal chloride result in that study PMID:20430975. A separate human-expression study measures a bafilomycin-insensitive acidification component in HEK293 vesicles, with transport mutants as controls; its opossum-cell knockdown and proton-entry interpretation remain distinct PMID:20421284. Human ClC-5 oocyte measurements provide additional direct transport evidence, with apparent stoichiometry calculated from electrochemical energetics and epithelial localization evaluated separately PMID:31852738. These context-dependent findings motivate a question about native voltage and ion gradients, not a categorical claim that ClC-5 always acidifies or always alkalinizes an endosome.

The existing renal process is retained because ClC-5 performs the ion-transport step supporting renal epithelial function. No new endocytosis, developmental or disease process is added solely from loss-of-function and rescue phenotypes. The single organizing core is endosomal chloride/proton exchange with the established renal role.
