## Scaffold and contributed nuclease activity

CNOT1 supplies the physical framework of CCR4–NOT and recruitment interfaces for factors targeting mRNAs. The human NOT1–CAF1 structure and direct binding experiments establish scaffold work; the functional reporter in that particular study uses Drosophila S2 cells (PMID:22977175, Results and Materials and Methods). Reconstitution of human complexes independently establishes the noncatalytic modules’ contribution to efficient deadenylation (PMID:31320642, Results and Methods). GO:0140378 is therefore the single core molecular function and replaces the two broader adaptor annotations. The GO:0030674 comment expressly distinguishes complex scaffolds from other adaptors. The historical PAINT node was not inspected; this refinement is based on target experiments, not a reconstructed ancestral error.

The preserved GO:0004535 assertion already has `contributes_to`. The original 2011 study measures reduced poly(A) degradation by CNOT6L immunoprecipitates after CNOT1 depletion, even when exogenous CNOT6L abundance is unchanged. It also shows human FLAG-CNOT1 in cytoplasmic foci and reduced AGO2-positive P-body counts without reduced AGO2 abundance (PMID:21976065, Results and deadenylase-assay Methods; [original publisher paper](https://doi.org/10.1007/s13238-011-1092-4)). These data support the complex’s contributed nuclease activity and a contextual P-body assembly role, without establishing intrinsic CNOT1 hydrolysis or direct condensate nucleation. The RNase contribution is integrated into the scaffold core rather than repeated as a second core.

TTP recruitment experiments use human CNOT1 fragments named with historical isoform-C/2371-residue coordinates, mouse Krebs extracts for deadenylation and a human HEK293 reporter for cellular output (PMID:23644599, Results). Those settings are kept distinct. Current alternative-product reconstruction gives lengths 2376, 2371, 2150 and 1551 residues from the authentic UniProt VAR_SEQ features. Neither those lengths nor engineered structural fragments establish equivalent activity of all four natural products.

## Nuclear receptors, localization and developmental context

The complete original receptor study separates ligand-dependent ER/RXR interaction from the absence of the corresponding tested RARα interaction, while RAR-dependent reporter repression is still positive (PMID:16778766, Results and Discussion). The obsolete estrogen-receptor-binding term is refined to current nuclear-receptor binding. The original RAR-binding assertion remains intact but is marked over-annotated at this assay scope; the contextual RAR-signaling repression annotation is retained non-core. This does not assert universal inability to contact a retinoic-acid receptor under all conditions.

P-body and cytosolic localization are supported by human imaging, the mouse donor study and the exact Reactome physical-entity chain, respectively (PMID:21976065; PMID:20133598; Reactome:R-HSA-429955 and R-HSA-6798044). The mouse donor experiments concern gonadal tissue and NANOS2-associated recruitment, not human germ cells. Nuclear localization is consistent with direct promoter/receptor experiments. The tear-proteomics original Table II identifies CNOT1, supporting extracellular detection as non-core without establishing secretion or an extracellular molecular function (PMID:22664934). The exact membrane-proteomics target table for PMID:19946888 was not recovered; that source-specific annotation remains UNDECIDED.

Stem-cell and trophectoderm effects remain contextual. The original study includes mouse ESC and human H1 experiments, with separate CNOT2/3 rescue and H9 scopes; no human embryo experiment is inferred (PMID:22367759). Current exact ortholog and same-term GOA records support the retained transfers where experimental grounding was found. Historical PAINT tree, alignment and IBD placement were not reconstructed, and donor count is not used to reject an inference. Human CNOT1 among its own experimental sources is legitimate grounding.

This assessment preserves every original annotation, source qualifier, product, reference identity, selected cache and earlier note. The human NOT1 study separately establishes coimmunoprecipitation of CCR4a/b and direct recombinant binding/structure for CAF1/POP2 (PMID:22977175, Methods, Figures 1–3). CAF1/POP2 connect the CCR4 enzymes to the NOT module. The later exact IntAct records are spoke-expanded anti-tag coimmunoprecipitation associations (PMID:26496610 and PMID:33961781). These positive records do not distinguish a direct CNOT1–CNOT6/6L contact. The original PMID:21984185 full assay remains uninspected; its positive annotation and complex context are retained without manufacturing a negative result. This is an evidence-specific limit on the added partner-class specificity, not a rule that all coimmunoprecipitation evidence is invalid. CNOT7/CNOT8 refinements remain supported by direct recombinant assays.

The single scaffold core now explicitly contributes to poly(A)-specific ribonuclease activity (GO:0004535), consistent with the original contributes_to source qualifier. CNOT1 depletion reduces the activity of CNOT6L-containing immunoprecipitates without reducing the exogenous CNOT6L level (PMID:21976065, Figure 3 and Deadenylase assay Methods). Human reconstitution shows enhanced and more selective poly(A) degradation by the assembled complex (PMID:31320642, Results and Methods). The molecular activity of CNOT1 itself remains protein complex scaffold activity; this does not assign hydrolysis to CNOT1 or create a second core.

The RNA-capture refinements remain. In the original Castello article, UV crosslinking does “not promote protein-protein crosslinking”; the subsequent LiCl/LiDS purification dissociates most noncovalent protein interactions (PMID:22658674, printed pages 1393–1394 and Technical Aspects of the Interactome Capture Protocol). The exact Table S1 CNOT1 entry supports a high-probability mRNA-contact hit, with individual validation still appropriate. This method does not simply capture every member of an RNA-associated complex. The Baltz thesis independently reproduces the CNOT1 target row and describes irradiated/nonirradiated HEK293 controls with 500 mM LiCl and 1% lithium dodecyl sulfate in Methods section 3.5 (PMID:22681889; thesis printed pages 34–35, target table PDF page 144). This extends access to the author’s original thesis methods, not to the full journal article. Neither experiment establishes a cognate sequence, binding domain, equal activity of all products, or a separate RNA-binding core.

The tear annotation remains KEEP_AS_NON_CORE. The original Table II identifies CNOT1 in the merged tear-proteome list (PMID:22664934). Schirmer-strip sampling and detergent extraction leave cellular carryover possible; the study does not identify the cellular source or release mechanism of CNOT1. Detection does not establish secretion or extracellular function, but absence of a signal peptide does not refute detection in an extracellular sample. The current GO:0005576 definition is spatial.
