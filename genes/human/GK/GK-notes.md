## 2026-11-06 — biological synthesis and review follow-up

GK uses ATP to phosphorylate glycerol, producing sn-glycerol-3-phosphate. This catalytic entry supplies glycerol utilization and lipid synthesis. [PMID:37021775](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37021775/)

Exon-18 inclusion changes kinetic behavior and distribution in the reported comparison; the imaging ambiguity is recorded for curator follow-up. [PMID:15845384](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15845384/)

The predicted membrane helix falls inside the alternatively spliced exon-18 segment, supporting product-dependent membrane association. This sequence evidence does not establish membrane orientation. [UniProt:P32189](https://www.uniprot.org/uniprotkb/P32189/entry)

Glycerol kinase deficiency elevates free glycerol. A glycerol-sensitive triglyceride assay can consequently report pseudohypertriglyceridemia; this is distinct from increased triglyceride synthesis. [PMID:8651297](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8651297/)

GK contact with NR4A1 accompanies altered receptor DNA binding and reporter activity, supporting a secondary receptor-binding function. [PMID:21988832](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21988832/)

MitoCoP supports mitochondrial membership for a GK protein group, without independently assigning every splice product or resolving membrane topology. [PMID:34800366](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34800366/)

The specificity criterion is biological scope: retain the established glycerol-catabolic, glycerol-3-phosphate biosynthetic and triglyceride-biosynthetic roles. Mark their broader parents too general when they introduce no additional supported reaction or pathway direction. Apply that criterion to IBA, IMP and IEA alike; phylogenetic evidence remains valid.
