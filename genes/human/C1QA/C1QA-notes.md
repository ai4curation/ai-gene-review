### Recognition, assembly and catalysis

Human C1q consists of six A/B/C heterotrimers. C1QA contributes structural collagen-like and globular recognition elements; the C1r/C1s proteases carry out complement proteolysis. The human recombinant ghA abstract directly reports immunoglobulin binding: [PMID:12847249](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12847249/), “the MBP-ghA bound to heat-aggregated IgG and IgM”. The complete abstract, rather than full Methods, was read. PMID:19006321 also reports A-chain substitutions affecting IgG/IgM recognition. Physiological avidity and activation belong to the assembled C1q/C1 system, as supported by recombinant C1q assembly/activation (PMID:23650384), IgG-hexamer experiments (PMID:24626930), C1-IgG1 structures (PMID:29449492), and surface-binding/reconstitution work (PMID:34155115).

The core uses GO:0019865 immunoglobulin binding to synthesize the existing IgG- and IgM-binding siblings; it does not add a redundant broad NEW annotation. Official [AmiGO GO:0019865](https://amigo.geneontology.org/amigo/term/GO%3A0019865) identifies IgG binding GO:0019864 and IgM binding GO:0001791 as direct children. The direct record initially timed out; the successful official indexed term record, with definition and parent/child relations, is retained separately. New core identifiers are checked by normal term validation.

The actual Methods/Results of [PMID:18250442](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18250442/) test human serum C1q and its globular region with apoptotic HeLa cells, phosphatidylserine-containing surfaces, cosedimentation, SPR and phosphoserine crystals. Its Discussion states “phosphoserine is bound to subunit C”. An A-chain contact is contributed by a neighboring crystal molecule and is packing-constrained. Thus the core describes C1QA as contributing to phosphatidylserine recognition within C1q, not possessing an independently established A-chain phosphatidylserine site. No NEW phagocytosis annotation is manufactured from a clearance or knockout phenotype.

The annotation consultant read actual human C1q/CD33 binding and ITIM-phosphorylation experiments in PMID:28325905. Only the existing CD33-specific protein-binding assertion is refined to GO:0048018 receptor ligand activity, already present as a separate IBA source row. The C1QB partner row from the same paper is not repurposed. Whole-C1q or heterotrimeric-fragment experiments do not establish isolated A-chain sufficiency. Activation of an inhibitory receptor is compatible with receptor ligand activity; it is not evidence of ligand activity with the wrong regulatory sign.

Direct ghA binding to mature gC1qR/C1QBP is supported in PMID:28018340 and as the actual positive control in PMID:34804054. The latter's viral title is not a reason to reject the C1QA interaction. The processed partner Q07021-PRO_0000018590 remains exact. C1QB association has independent heterotrimer-interface evidence from PMID:12960167. PMID:7240 establishes a covalent A–B disulfide linkage, which supports assembly but is not by itself a noncovalent protein-binding assay; that generic binding row is retained through curator deference and the independent interface evidence. The three UBQLN2/SGTB screen assertions retain their exact curated edges with framework-level assay reading and explicit uninspected individual target-table limits; no native ubiquitin-related activity is inferred. The 2017 erratum listed in the PMID:21988832 cache was not adjudicated.

## 2026-10-03 — Primary evidence anchors and classical-pathway specificity

C1QA supplies a recognition chain of C1q rather than a complement protease. Human recombinant ghA binds immunoglobulins (PMID:12847249); the globular heterotrimer has noncovalent interfaces (PMID:12960167), and assembled human C1q binds and activates the C1r/C1s protease assembly (PMID:23650384; PMID:34155115). The broad complement-activation assertion is correct, but its supported classical-pathway child GO:0006958 gives the more specific description. The official ontology places GO:0006958 directly under GO:0006956. The original TAS source and all machine annotation fields remain intact.

Other recognition roles have different experimental scopes. Phosphoserine occupies a chain-C site in the C1q head (PMID:18250442), so the A-chain contribution is expressed as part of the assembled recognition unit. Whole C1q and its globular region engage CD33 and activate its inhibitory signaling motifs in human monocytes (PMID:28325905); this does not establish isolated A-chain sufficiency. Recombinant ghA also binds mature gC1qR in the reported assays (PMID:28018340), and is the positive control in the Figure 5 binding assay of PMID:34804054. Ten short, exact cached-text anchors now document these claims and the principal neural observations in the review's reference findings; quotes are not repeated here.

The neural evidence remains explicitly rodent and contextual. The complete PMID:18083105 abstract describes postnatal synaptic localization and failed retinogeniculate refinement in complement-deficient mice; the full Methods are not available in that cache. PMID:27033548 directly reports C1q colocalization with PSD95-positive puncta in mouse hippocampus. These findings support the retained broad synaptic and postsynaptic annotations. An extrinsic membrane component need only associate with a membrane surface, so leaflet-level resolution is not a requirement. The exact donor experiments for presynaptic-membrane association, postsynaptic-membrane association and the glutamatergic-synapse transfer were not exposed in the inspected donor records. Those three source-specific decisions remain UNDECIDED, without rejecting synaptic C1q or treating the curator's inference as disproved.

Ontology access: [classical complement activation](https://amigo.geneontology.org/amigo/term/GO:0006958), [extrinsic presynaptic membrane component](https://amigo.geneontology.org/amigo/term/GO:0098888), and [glutamatergic synapse](https://www.informatics.jax.org/vocab/gene_ontology/GO:0098978). The direct postsynaptic-term page timed out; no successful reading of that page is claimed. Earlier dated journal entries are retained as historical records.
