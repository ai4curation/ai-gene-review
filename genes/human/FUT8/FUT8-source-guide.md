# FUT8 evidence guide

This is an authored guide to selected source evidence. It is not provider-generated deep research. Original GOA, UniProt, publication and pathway records must retain their machine-fetched contents.

## Catalytic evidence

[PMID:9133635](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9133635/) describes purification and cloning of human FUT8 and fucose transfer to an N-linked glycopeptide. The accessible original abstract supports enzyme chemistry; supernatant purification does not define every endogenous location. [PMID:17172260](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17172260/) describes the human catalytic structure. Its full text was inaccessible in this reading, so individual mutant results are not reconstructed. [PMID:10764839](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10764839/) describes recombinant human enzyme and donor-binding mutagenesis in its abstract; no new residue-level assertion is supplied here.

[PMID:36280670](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-022-34029-7), selected original Results/Figure 4 and Methods, tests an engineered soluble human FUT8 fusion made in bacterial hosts. Glycan-product measurements support core fucosylation. The Results/Methods substrate-number discrepancy is preserved; this engineered protein is not a natural isoform. [PMID:29304374, full original](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5777984/) is available as full HTML-derived Main Text in the immutable native publication cache. The complete main text was read: individual 1–3 case descriptions establish prenatal growth restriction; the fibroblast protein, serum LC-MS/MS and fibroblast LC-MS/lectin passages establish deficient core fucosylation. The serum paragraph reports O-glycans unaffected, with data not shown. The introduction distinguishes FUCA1-mediated glycan degradation from FUT8 synthesis. The earlier private NCBI XML response contained no body; that historical failure does not describe the later native cache. No genetic rescue, original image audit or independent supplementary analysis is claimed.

## Products and location

Current UniProt declares four products with verified sequences of 575, 308, 412 and 169 residues. Product Q9BYC5-2 (308 residues; synonym Retinal; sequence evidence [PMID:10814706](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10814706/)) is distinct from Q9BYC5-4 (169 residues; synonyms Retina-1 and Retina-2; sequence evidence [PMID:14514715](https://doi.org/10.1093/glycob/cwh006) and [PMID:15489334](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15489334/)). UniProt's inactivity comment is specifically attached to product 4. In [the author-deposited original PMID:14514715](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/5784859_Activity_and_tissue_distribution_of_splice_variants_of_6-fucosyltransferase_in_human_embryogenesis), selected Results, Tables I–II and Figure 6 support construct-dependent activity and Golgi localization of recombinant human FUT8 in COS7 cells; tag placement affects retention. The Discussion reports inactivity of retinal splice forms, without a retinal construct row in the displayed activity tables, and leaves their proposed GDP-fucose regulation unresolved. That report must not be transferred by the shared retinal naming to product 2, and exact sequence identity alone does not establish equivalent activity or expression of the four products.

[PMID:10814706](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10814706/) concerns genomic organization and promoter activity, not a transcriptional function of the FUT8 protein. Large-scale sequence references [PMID:12508121](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12508121/), [PMID:14702039](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14702039/) and [PMID:15489334](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15489334/) remain sequence provenance; their specific target clones were not independently joined.

## Detection and interactions

[PMID:19199708](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19199708/): [authentic supplementary Table S2](https://ndownloader.figshare.com/files/4571341), sheet Paratid Exosomes, row 397 identifies historical FUT8 accession IPI00302523.2 with two peptides; [Table S1](https://ndownloader.figshare.com/files/4571323), sheet Saliva-exosome-proteins, rows 3993–3994 provide the matching peptide records. Current-sequence joins support detection in the human parotid exosome preparation, while neither establishes a unique current product. The original parotid context must remain attached. This is not evidence of vesicle biogenesis or a membrane orientation.

[PMID:19946888](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19946888/) studies membranes from the human NK-like YTS line. Its abstract is accessible, but the FUT8-specific identification table was not recovered. Preserve the uncertainty of that experimental context rather than inferring a wrong target from incomplete evidence.

[PMID:32296183](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32296183/): authentic IntAct records EBI-24951701, EBI-23083633 and EBI-24447815 identify FUT8–ZBTB10 isoform3 physical association in yeast two-hybrid assays. The partner suffix is not a FUT8 isoform. Full Supplementary Table 9 and exact construct boundaries were not independently examined. The result does not establish a physiological Golgi/nuclear mechanism or a specific adaptor function.

[PMID:25171405](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25171405/), original Results and Figure 4/S4A caption, explicitly names FUT8 as a substrate phosphorylated following VLK coexpression. It does not give FUT8 kinase activity. [PMID:30237576](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30237576/) names the FUT8 disease case in the original abstract and Figure 1 caption; its exact variant supplement was not inspected.

## Mapping and evolutionary context

[PMID:11698403, original DOI](https://doi.org/10.1074/jbc.M107927200) and [indexed original article](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/11656847_Composition_of_Drosophila_melanogaster_Proteome_Involved_in_Fucosylated_Glycan_Metabolism): selected Results, “Fucosyltransferases Using Glycan Acceptors,” explicitly discusses human FUT8 N-glycan core fucosylation; the Introduction distinguishes fucosidase-mediated degradation, and the separate protein-O-fucosyltransferase section concerns serine/threonine acceptors. The publisher direct-open route returned 403, but those selected original sections were actually read from indexed text. The paper's fetal-expression observation is not a direct human embryogenesis assay. Later human prenatal findings in PMID:29304374 support retaining the original developmental context as non-core while preserving its NAS provenance. The current GO link from GO:0008424 to protein O-linked fucosylation is recorded as an ontology mapping question; FUT8's measured acceptor is the N-glycan GlcNAc.

Human self is expected experimental grounding, not circularity. This tree read does not independently certify every donor paper or activity of each shorter human product.
