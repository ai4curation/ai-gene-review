## Catalysis and products

CHAT transfers an acetyl group from acetyl-CoA to choline. Human structures and purified-enzyme kinetics support one choline O-acetyltransferase core and the existing acetylcholine-biosynthesis/neuromuscular-transmission annotations. The broad acyltransferase mapping is refined to GO:0004102. This is intrinsic performed enzyme work, not an inference from disease severity alone. The human disease study includes endplate physiology and direct recombinant kinetic measurements [PMID:11172068]; the structural paper reports binary complexes and a modeled ternary complex, not an experimentally solved covalent enzyme intermediate [PMID:17144655].

The unchanged products are M/83 kDa (P28329-1), S/74 kDa (P28329-2) and R/70 kDa (P28329-3). Older papers call the larger and common smaller forms 82/69 kDa. Translation starts and transcript identity must be distinguished: a transcript can yield more than one protein. A tagged 70-kDa construct can migrate near 74 kDa; it is not thereby the distinct S product. The separately expressed 74-kDa enzyme's comparison is reported as data not shown in PMID:11172068.

Nuclear localization is retained as non-core, rather than rejected because the principal enzyme role is cytosolic. Human recombinant enzyme in insect cells changes location with expression time [PMID:10861222]; human-cell localization/shuttling and native human neuronal observations independently corroborate a nuclear pool [PMID:10383456; PMID:12637523; PMID:16797789]. The physiological nuclear mechanism is unresolved. Human 69-kDa ChAT-GFP expression in SH-SY5Y cells increases SLC5A7 expression, requiring nuclear targeting and catalytic competence, whereas HEK293 lacks that response [PMID:21163949]. Neither direct DNA binding, a coactivator interface, histone acetylation nor another catalytic specificity was demonstrated. No new transcription process or molecular function is manufactured from this endpoint.
