Gene Description

MLKL (mixed lineage kinase domain-like) is a cytosolic pseudokinase that executes necroptosis, a lytic, caspase-independent form of programmed cell death. It has an N-terminal four-helix bundle (4HB) executioner domain, a two-helix brace and a C-terminal pseudokinase domain. The pseudokinase domain binds ATP without divalent cations but has no protein kinase activity; it acts as a phosphorylation-controlled molecular switch. During necroptosis RIPK3, within the RIPK1-RIPK3 necrosome, phosphorylates the MLKL activation loop (T357/S358 in human). This releases the 4HB domain, and activated MLKL self-associates into oligomers, a step that also depends on highly phosphorylated inositol phosphates. The oligomers move to the plasma membrane, where a basic patch on the 4HB binds phosphatidylinositol phosphates, preferentially PI(4,5)P2, and MLKL compromises membrane integrity, leading to ion influx and cell lysis. In mouse cells MLKL can also act in the nucleus during ZBP1-dependent responses to influenza virus.

Core Functions

MLKL is the terminal effector of necroptosis. Once RIPK3 phosphorylates its pseudokinase domain, MLKL oligomerizes, and its N-terminal four-helix bundle engages plasma-membrane phosphatidylinositol phosphates, preferentially PI(4,5)P2, then permeabilizes the membrane. The pseudokinase domain binds ATP but has no catalytic activity; it acts as a phosphorylation-controlled switch.
