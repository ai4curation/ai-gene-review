# PRPSAP1 notes

PRPSAP1 encodes PAP39, one of the two non-catalytic PRPP synthase-associated proteins in the PRPS family. The original rat liver work purified PRPP synthetase aggregates containing the 34-kDa catalytic subunits plus 39- and 41-kDa associated proteins; PAP39 cross-linked to the catalytic subunits, and removal of the PAPs increased the activity of the remaining catalytic subunits, supporting an inhibitory/regulatory role for PAP39 [PMID:8132556, "Covalent cross-linking experiments gave definitive evidence for molecular interaction of PAP39 with the catalytic subunits."]. The human cDNA encodes a 356 aa homolog that is 98% identical to rat PAP39 and is expressed in all examined human tissues [PMID:8611620, "A human cDNA encoding a human homologue of the rat phosphoribosylpyrophosphate synthetase-associated protein of 39 kDa was isolated."].

PTHR10210 places PRPSAP1 in `PTHR10210:SF28`, together with PRPSAP2/SF29 below PAINT node `PTN008303105`. That node has a supported IRD blocking `GO:0004749 ribose phosphate diphosphokinase activity` and an IBD for `GO:0030234 enzyme regulator activity`; this matches the biology: PRPSAP1 keeps the PRPS fold and binds the cytosolic PRPS1/PRPS2 machinery but is a regulatory associated subunit, not a PRPP-forming enzyme.

The current GOA rows still include older PRPP-synthesis biological-process IBAs from the catalytic ancestor and InterPro2GO rows from the family-wide ribose-phosphate diphosphokinase signature. Those are catalytic-family projections and should be removed from PRPSAP1. Bare `GO:0005515 protein binding` rows from proteome-scale interactome screens are also uninformative even when the reported partner is PRPS1, PRPS2 or PRPSAP2; the mechanistically useful assertion is PRPSAP1 membership in the ribose phosphate diphosphokinase complex.
