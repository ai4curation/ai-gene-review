Gene Description

HHAT (Hedgehog acyltransferase) is a polytopic endoplasmic-reticulum membrane enzyme of the membrane-bound O-acyltransferase (MBOAT) superfamily, and the enzyme that lipidates the Hedgehog morphogens. It transfers palmitate from palmitoyl-CoA onto the alpha-amino group of the N-terminal cysteine exposed by autoprocessing of the Sonic, Desert and Indian Hedgehog precursors, forming an amide rather than a thioester bond, and it recognises the conserved Hedgehog N-terminal CGPGR motif with such selectivity that Hedgehog proteins are thought to be its only substrates. The protein comprises ten transmembrane helices and two reentrant loops, with the catalytic aspartate and histidine on opposite faces of the bilayer and a bound heme b that is required for activity; the active site is embedded in the membrane and receives palmitoyl-CoA from the cytosolic leaflet through an internal archway while the Hedgehog peptide enters from the ER lumen, so the enzyme brings together two chemically dissimilar substrates from opposite sides of the membrane. HHAT has also been reported to promote net movement of palmitoyl-CoA to the luminal side of the ER membrane, an activity separable from catalysis in reconstituted liposomes. The palmitate HHAT installs is not decoration: together with the C-terminal cholesterol added by Hedgehog autoprocessing it makes the dually lipidated ligand, and the palmitoyl chain is read directly by the receptor Patched, so palmitoylation is required for both short- and long-range Hedgehog signalling. HHAT is therefore a positive regulator of the pathway that acts entirely upstream of it, on the ligand rather than within the signalling cascade. Loss-of-function variants cause Nivelon-Nivelon-Mabille syndrome, an autosomal recessive disorder with progressive microcephaly, cerebellar vermis hypoplasia, skeletal dysplasia and, in some individuals, 46,XY gonadal dysgenesis; the enzyme is also a drug target in Hedgehog-dependent cancers, and small molecules such as IMP-1575 inhibit it by occluding the substrate site. Its closest paralog, HHATL, retains the fold but has lost acyltransferase activity, and its Drosophila counterpart is Rasp.

Proposed New Ontology Terms

protein-cysteine N-palmitoyltransferase activity

Definition: Catalysis of the transfer of a palmitoyl (systematic name, hexadecanoyl) group to the nitrogen atom of the alpha-amino group of an N-terminal cysteine residue of a protein, in the reaction hexadecanoyl-CoA + N-terminal L-cysteinyl-[protein] = CoA + N-terminal N-hexadecanoyl-L-cysteinyl-[protein] + H(+).

Justification: GO has no molecular-function term for N-terminal-cysteine N-palmitoylation, although it distinguishes the linkage on the process side (GO:0018009 N-terminal peptidyl-L-cysteine N-palmitoylation versus GO:0018230 peptidyl-L-cysteine S-palmitoylation). The only available MF term, GO:0019706 protein-cysteine S-palmitoyltransferase activity, is defined as transfer to a sulfur atom, which is not the reaction HHAT catalyses: HHAT forms an amide bond to the alpha-amino group, shown biochemically and resolved as density for the amide linkage in the product cryo-EM structure. The existing N-palmitoyltransferase terms (GO:0018031, GO:0140771, GO:0140772) are all peptidyl-lysine N6, and GO:0140186 protein N-acyltransferase activity is likewise defined on lysine N6. The proposed name is the one both UniProt and PANTHER already use for this protein - UniProt's recommended name is Protein-cysteine N-palmitoyltransferase HHAT and PANTHER's subfamily PTHR13285:SF20 is PROTEIN-CYSTEINE N-PALMITOYLTRANSFERASE HHAT - and the reaction is already encoded as RHEA:59528, with RHEA:59580 for the cholesterylated substrate. Adding the term would let HHAT, mouse Hhat and Drosophila Rasp be annotated to their actual chemistry, and would give GOAT and Porcupine, the other protein-acylating MBOATs, a correctly-typed sibling branch. Until it exists, GO:0019706 is retained on this record as the closest available term.

Core Functions

N-terminal palmitoylation of the Hedgehog morphogens. HHAT transfers palmitate from palmitoyl-CoA onto the alpha-amino group of the N-terminal cysteine of autoprocessed Sonic, Desert and Indian Hedgehog, forming an amide bond, with Asp339 and His379 as the catalytic residues and a bound heme b required for activity. Catalysis happens inside the ER membrane: palmitoyl-CoA reaches the reaction chamber from the cytosolic leaflet and the Hedgehog peptide from the lumen, and the enzyme also promotes movement of palmitoyl-CoA to the luminal side. The reaction is the last covalent step of Hedgehog ligand maturation, after autocleavage and autocholesterylation, and the palmitate it installs is what the receptor Patched reads - which is why the modification is required for Hedgehog signalling at both ranges and why HHAT, which never touches the receptor, counts as a positive regulator of the pathway rather than a component of it. Substrate selectivity is essentially absolute for the Hedgehog N-terminal CGPGR motif.

Suggested Questions for Experts

Q: Is HHAT's reported palmitoyl-CoA uptake activity a distinct transport function or simply substrate access to the membrane-embedded active site? Liposome reconstitution showed uptake is intrinsic to HHAT and separable from catalysis, yet the cryo-EM structures describe an internal archway feeding the reaction chamber, and no MBOAT in PANTHER family PTHR13285 carries a transport annotation in any species. Resolving this would determine whether a transporter-activity annotation is warranted, and whether GO needs a term for it - the nearest existing MF term, GO:0015607, is ABC-type and does not apply.

Q: Should GO gain a molecular-function term for N-terminal-cysteine N-palmitoylation? The process side already distinguishes GO:0018009 from GO:0018230, and the reaction is encoded as RHEA:59528, but the only available MF term (GO:0019706) specifies transfer to a sulfur atom. The same gap affects mouse Hhat, Drosophila Rasp and, by analogy, the other protein-acylating MBOATs GOAT and Porcupine.

Q: Does HHAT have any function in the Golgi, or is the Golgi pool simply enzyme in transit or following its substrate? The Golgi assignment rests on a curator-inferred co-localization with SHH (ECO:0000305 on PubMed:18534984), while all catalytic characterisation places the active site in the ER membrane.

Q: HHATL sits in the same PANTHER family and retains the fold, and the PAINT slice records an explicit IRD negating acyltransferase activity in its branch. What does HHATL actually do, and does it regulate HHAT - for instance by competing for palmitoyl-CoA or for Hedgehog substrate in the ER membrane?

1. What the protein is

HHAT (Hedgehog acyltransferase, UniProt Q5VTY9, HGNC:18023, 493 aa, chromosome
1) is a polytopic endoplasmic-reticulum membrane enzyme of the membrane-bound
O-acyltransferase (MBOAT) superfamily. UniProt's recommended name is
Protein-cysteine N-palmitoyltransferase HHAT, and UniProt assigns it to the
"membrane-bound acyltransferase family, HHAT subfamily"
[file:human/HHAT/HHAT-uniprot.txt "Belongs to the membrane-bound acyltransferase family. HHAT"].
PANTHER places it in family PTHR13285 (official name ACYLTRANSFERASE),
subfamily PTHR13285:SF20 PROTEIN-CYSTEINE N-PALMITOYLTRANSFERASE HHAT
(interpro/panther/panther.obo,
interpro/panther/PTHR13285/PTHR13285-entries.csv). The same family holds
Drosophila Rasp (Q9VZU2, subfamily SF18), the HHAT paralog HHATL (Q9HCP6,
SF19), the yeast MBOATs GUP1/GUP2, and the bacterial DltB/AlgI acyltransferases.

Topology: ten transmembrane domains plus two reentrant loops, with the catalytic
His and Asp on opposite faces of the ER membrane
PMID:25505265, and the protein is itself palmitoylated on
several cytosolic cysteines. Mutating the conserved catalytic His abolishes
activity PMID:25505265. A bound heme b is required for function
PMID:34890564.

Seven isoforms are annotated (Q5VTY9-1 … Q5VTY9-7); no GOA row is
isoform-specific, so no isoform field is set.

2. The reaction, and why the GO molecular-function term is chemically off

HHAT transfers palmitate from palmitoyl-CoA onto the alpha-amino group of the
N-terminal cysteine of Hedgehog proteins, forming an amide (N-linked) bond -
not a thioester on the cysteine sulfur. This was established biochemically
PMID:18534984 and then visualised directly in the product complex
PMID:34112694. UniProt encodes it as RHEA:59528,
N-terminal L-cysteinyl-[protein] + hexadecanoyl-CoA = N-terminal N-hexadecanoyl-L-cysteinyl-[protein] + CoA + H(+), and a second reaction
(RHEA:59580) for the cholesterylated substrate.

The problem. GOA annotates this with GO:0019706
protein-cysteine S-palmitoyltransferase activity, whose definition is explicit
about the acceptor atom: "Catalysis of the transfer of a palmitoyl (systematic
name, hexadecanoyl) group to a sulfur atom on the cysteine of a protein
molecule" (OLS). That is the wrong linkage for HHAT.

3. Where it acts

The ER membrane is the site of catalysis. Every structural and biochemical study
agrees:
PMID:34112694;
PMID:31875564;
PMID:25505265.
The active site is membrane-embedded, reached by palmitoyl-CoA from the cytosolic
leaflet and by the Hedgehog peptide from the lumen
PMID:34890564.

Golgi localisation is a weaker, secondary claim. The abstract of the primary
paper says only that the modification happens en route through the secretory
system PMID:18534984, and UniProt records the Golgi assignment as a curator
inference rather than a direct observation
(Golgi apparatus membrane {ECO:0000305|PubMed:18534984}, and
Note=Co-localizes with SHH in the ER and Golgi membrane. {ECO:0000305|PubMed:18534984}). The cache holds only the abstract of
PMID:18534984, so I have not tried to adjudicate the imaging; the two Golgi rows
(GO:0005794 IDA, GO:0000139 IEA) are kept and marked non-core, because the ER
membrane is where all the characterised chemistry happens.

4. The palmitoyl-CoA conduit — a second activity, deliberately not annotated

Asciolla and Resh reported that HHAT also moves its own acyl donor across the ER
membrane, and that this is separable from catalysis:
PMID:31875564
and PMID:31875564 UniProt states it as a FUNCTION with ECO:0000269 from three papers:
Promotes the transfer of palmitoyl-CoA from the cytoplasmic to the luminal side of the endoplasmic reticulum membrane, where hedgehog palmitoylation occurs (PubMed:31875564, PubMed:34112694, PubMed:34890564).

I considered proposing a transport annotation and decided against it, on two
grounds.

  1. Comparator check. No MBOAT in this family carries a transport term
    anywhere. QuickGO for mouse Hhat (Q8BMT9) and Drosophila rasp (Q9VZU2)
    returns only acyltransferase, localisation, palmitoylation and Hedgehog
    pathway terms - no transporter activity, no lipid transport process. A
    systematic absence across the family is a convention, not an oversight.
  2. The 2021 structures reinterpret the phenomenon as part of catalysis. They
    show palmitoyl-CoA entering an "archway" and a membrane-embedded reaction
    chamber within the enzyme, i.e. substrate access to the active site rather
    than net transport of a cargo released on the far side
    PMID:34112694 is the ligand-side
    claim; the mechanism claim is
    PMID:34890564

ABC transporter). I raise the question in suggested_questions instead of

5. The one gap worth filling: GO:0018009

GO:0018009 N-terminal peptidyl-L-cysteine N-palmitoylation is the biological

not carry it. Both of its closest relatives do:

The mouse row is the decisive detail: GO_Central transferred GO:0018009 to
mouse Hhat from human HHAT by ISO. A curator therefore already judged that
human HHAT has this function, and the human row is missing from the GOA
snapshot rather than deliberately withheld. (Source: QuickGO
downloadSearch?goId=GO:0018009.)

Applying the two NEW tests from CLAUDE.md:

  • Participation. HHAT catalyses the step. The term's ancestors are
    GO:0018345 protein palmitoylation, GO:0006498 N-terminal protein lipidation,
    GO:0006497 protein lipidation and GO:0043543 protein acylation - all
    attachment processes, which name the enzyme that does the attaching, not the
    substrate. This is the mirror image of the Drosophila hh case, where the
    ligand is the substrate and the acyltransferase activity is not the ligand's
    function: here the acyltransferase is the actor.
  • Comparator. rasp carries the term with three experimental rows, and mouse
    Hhat carries it by transfer from human. This is a gap, not a convention.
  • Non-redundancy. GO:0018009 is neither an ancestor nor a descendant of
    GO:0051604 protein maturation (protein maturation is not among its ancestors),
    nor of GO:0045880, nor of the molecular-function terms on the record.

So GO:0018009 is added as the single NEW row, with IDA on PMID:34112694 (the
product complex resolving the amide bond) and PMID:18534984 / PMID:31875564 as
additional references.

6. Pathway role, and the substrate-versus-actor line

HHAT's product is what licenses Hedgehog reception. The palmitate is not
decoration: it is structurally read by Patched
PMID:34112694, and palmitoylation
is required for signalling at both ranges
PMID:18534984 and
PMID:31875564. Reactome makes the loss-of-function
side of the same point [Reactome:R-HSA-5358343 "Mutation or depletion of the HHAT
enzyme and mutation of the palmitoyl acceptor cysteine in Hh itself abrogates
palmitoylation of the ligand and reduces Hh signaling"].

GOA expresses this as GO:0045880 positive regulation of smoothened signaling
pathway (three human IDA rows plus an Ensembl-Compara IEA; mouse Hhat has two
IDA rows for the same term). I accepted it, and the reasoning matters because it
is where the substrate/actor distinction bites:

  • HHAT is not a component of the smoothened signaling pathway - it never
    touches the receptor, and it acts before the ligand is even released. A bare
    GO:0007224 smoothened signaling pathway row on HHAT would be an
    over-annotation. (Mouse Hhat does carry GO:0007224 by IMP, and Drosophila rasp
    carries GO:0007225 patched signaling pathway by IMP; the human record, which
    uses the regulation term instead, has this better.)
  • But HHAT does perform the act that raises pathway output: it covalently
    modifies the ligand into its high-activity form. "Positive regulation of"
    is precisely GO's way of saying that something acts on a pathway from outside
    it, and the work of that regulation is the palmitoyl transfer HHAT catalyses.

The narrower, mechanistically direct processes are GO:0018009 (the chemistry) and
GO:0051604 protein maturation (the ligand-maturation step the GO-CAM uses). All
three are treated as core; nothing further downstream is.

10. Disease

Biallelic HHAT variants cause Nivelon-Nivelon-Mabille syndrome
(NNMS, MIM:600092): progressive microcephaly, cerebellar vermis hypoplasia and
skeletal dysplasia, with variable early seizures, growth retardation,
chondrodysplasia and micromelia, and 46,XY gonadal dysgenesis
(file:human/HHAT/HHAT-uniprot.txt, DISEASE block; PubMed:24784881,
PubMed:30912300, PubMed:34890564, PubMed:35045414, PubMed:36303863,
PubMed:40326711 - only PMID:34890564 is cached). The G287V allele is the one
Reactome models
[Reactome:R-HSA-5483229 "A G287V loss-of-function mutation in HHAT was
identified in a rare case of Syndromic 46, XY Disorder of Sex Development, which
results in testis dysgenesis."]. HHAT is also a cancer drug target; the inhibitor
IMP-1575 occludes the active site
PMID:34890564. Disease terms are not GO annotations and no action follows.

Worth recording because it bounds the annotation: HHAT acylates Hedgehog proteins
and apparently nothing else.
PMID:18534984 and
PMID:34112694. The acceptor consensus is the
Hedgehog N-terminal CGPGR motif. This is why the MF and BP terms can be tied
to the Hedgehog pathway without the enzyme being a pathway component.
