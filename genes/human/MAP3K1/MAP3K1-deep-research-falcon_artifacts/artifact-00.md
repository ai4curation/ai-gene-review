| Feature | Best-supported annotation | Principal evidence | Confidence / caveat |
|---|---|---|---|
| Identity and size | Human **MAP3K1** encodes **MEKK1** (UniProt **Q13233**), a signaling protein of **1,512 aa** and approximately **196 kDa**. | Literature equates MAP3K1 with MEKK1 and reports matching length, mass, and architecture (pqac-00000001, pqac-00000003). | **High.** Identity matches the supplied UniProt record; it is not MAP3K2/MEKK2 or MAP3K3/MEKK3. |
| Domain architecture | N-terminal regulatory region containing **SWIM**, a six-HEAT-repeat **TOG** domain, a **RING/PHD-like E3-ligase** domain, and tandem **ubiquitin-interacting motifs (UIMs)**; a caspase-3 site at **Asp878** separates regulatory and catalytic regions; the C-terminal kinase domain spans **1243–1508**. | Structural, biochemical, and sequence analyses; the isolated TOG domain binds curved tubulin (pqac-00000001, pqac-00000003, pqac-00000004, pqac-00000014). | **High** for the overall architecture, TOG structure, Asp878, and kinase coordinates. “PHD” is the historical name for the RING-like E3 domain. |
| Protein-kinase reaction | ATP-dependent phosphorylation of MAP2Ks: **ATP + MAP2K → ADP + phospho-MAP2K**. Oligomerization supports trans-autophosphorylation and activation. | Biochemical assays and mechanistic reviews identify MAP2Ks as direct substrates and support oligomerization-dependent autophosphorylation (pqac-00000001, pqac-00000002, pqac-00000025). | **High** for protein Ser/Thr kinase activity; detailed catalytic constants and a strict peptide consensus are not established here. |
| Kinase substrate specificity | **MKK4/MAP2K4 is the best-supported and preferred substrate**, coupling MAP3K1 principally to JNK. MAP3K1 can also phosphorylate **MKK1/MAP2K1** and signal through **MKK7/MAP2K7**; MKK1/2–ERK output is more context-dependent. | Strongest kinase activity toward MKK4; direct MKK1 and MKK4 phosphorylation; MKK4/MKK7 activation-loop phosphorylation supports JNK signaling (pqac-00000025, pqac-00000027, pqac-00000029). | **High** for MKK4; **moderate** for the physiological importance of MKK1/2 and MKK7 in each context. |
| E3 ubiquitin-ligase activity | The RING/PHD-like domain recruits ubiquitin-charged E2 enzymes and catalyzes substrate and self-ubiquitylation, integrating phosphorylation and ubiquitin signaling. | PHD/RING mutations impair ubiquitylation; kinase activity and intact full-length architecture can be required for efficient cellular ubiquitylation (pqac-00000026, pqac-00000030). | **High.** Chain topology and outcome depend on substrate, stimulus, and E2 enzyme. |
| ERK1/2 ubiquitylation | Under hyperosmotic stress, MAP3K1 associates with and polyubiquitylates **ERK1/2**, promoting proteasome-dependent degradation; endogenous proteins colocalize perinuclearly. | Purified-protein, NIH-3T3, and 293T experiments; MG132 blocked ERK loss, while PHD or kinase mutants impaired ubiquitylation (pqac-00000025, pqac-00000026). | **High mechanistic confidence**, although the effect is stress- and context-dependent. |
| c-Jun ubiquitylation | MAP3K1 promotes **c-Jun** ubiquitylation and proteasomal degradation during osmotic stress, limiting sustained c-Jun signaling and influencing apoptosis. | In-vitro and cellular assays; c-Jun loss occurred by **6 h** after sorbitol exposure and was blocked by MAP3K1 deficiency (pqac-00000028). | **High** in osmotic-stress models; other E3 ligases regulate c-Jun in other contexts. |
| TAB1 ubiquitylation | MAP3K1 catalyzes predominantly **Lys63-linked**, non-degradative polyubiquitylation of **TAB1** through UBE2N–UBE2V1, promoting TAB1–TAK1 coupling and cytokine-induced JNK/p38 activation. | Protein-array discovery, biochemical assays, and an E3-defective *Map3k1* knock-in model (pqac-00000029, pqac-00000032). | **High mechanistic confidence**, but much of the physiological evidence comes from mouse embryonic stem cells. |
| Localization | Dynamic intracellular localization in the **perinuclear cytoplasm**, at the **plasma membrane**, **tight junctions**, **actin stress fibers**, **focal adhesions**, and the leading edge of migrating cells; its TOG domain targets soluble curved tubulin and microtubule assembly/disassembly sites. | Imaging, interaction studies, and TOG-domain structural and biochemical analyses (pqac-00000014, pqac-00000025, pqac-00000026). | **High** that localization is stimulus- and partner-dependent; MAP3K1 is not a constitutive resident of one organelle. |
| Pathway outputs and cell-fate switch | Principal cascade: **MAP3K1 → MKK4/MKK7 → JNK → c-Jun**. Contextual outputs include ERK1/2, p38, TAK1, NF-κB, cytoskeletal remodeling, migration, survival, and apoptosis. Caspase cleavage at Asp878 releases a C-terminal kinase fragment associated with pro-apoptotic signaling. | Biochemical, genetic, and cell-biological evidence supports JNK predominance and distinct effects of full-length versus cleaved MAP3K1 (pqac-00000001, pqac-00000003, pqac-00000029). | **High** for JNK–c-Jun and cleavage-dependent switching; broader outputs are cell- and stimulus-dependent. |
| Cancer | MAP3K1 is commonly altered as a putative **tumor suppressor** in luminal breast cancer: reported alterations were predominantly frameshift (**59%**) or nonsense (**19%**), and about **50%** of tumors altered in MAP3K1 or MAP2K4 also carried activating **PIK3CA** mutations. Conversely, amplification or activated fragments can be pro-growth in other cancers. | Human tumor genomics and pathway genetics; experimental JNK deficiency accelerates HER2-driven tumors (pqac-00000017, pqac-00000022, pqac-00000024). | **High** for context dependence and loss-of-function enrichment in luminal breast cancer. JNK-model findings are not direct proof of MAP3K1-specific tumor suppression in HER2-positive disease. |
| 46,XY DSD | Heterozygous, sex-limited **gain-of-function MAP3K1 variants** cause 46,XY gonadal dysgenesis/sex reversal, commonly increasing ERK and, to a lesser extent, p38 signaling and perturbing testis-versus-ovary developmental signaling. | Patient-derived cells and human genetic studies; MAP3K1 variants were reported in approximately **18% of sporadic 46,XY gonadal-dysgenesis cases** in an influential cohort (pqac-00000018, pqac-00000019). | **High** disease association; signaling effects vary by variant, and the 18% estimate is not generalizable to every DSD population. |
| 2024 DFNB128 deafness | Homozygous kinase-domain variant **c.4460G>A, p.Arg1487His** segregated with severe-to-profound nonsyndromic sensorineural deafness in two siblings from one consanguineous family. The allele occurred in **3/1,613,810** population chromosomes and **0/348** ethnically matched control chromosomes. | Homozygosity mapping, exome sequencing, Sanger segregation, conservation/modeling, cochlear single-cell expression, and concordant deaf *Map3k1* mouse models (pqac-00000009, pqac-00000010, pqac-00000011, pqac-00000012). | **Moderate.** Strong candidate association, but only one human family was reported; direct functional testing of Arg1487His and independent replication were lacking. |
