Gene Description

SMIM26 is a 95-amino-acid mitochondrial microprotein encoded by a transcript that was originally annotated as the long non-coding RNA LINC00493. It has a single predicted transmembrane helix near its N terminus and is anchored in the mitochondrial outer membrane, with the bulk of its soluble sequence reported to span the intermembrane space. SMIM26 has no catalytic or transport activity of its own; it acts as a tether that holds mitochondrial metabolite carriers of the two membranes in proximity. Reported partners include the outer-membrane porins VDAC1 and VDAC2, the inner-membrane ADP/ATP translocase SLC25A6 (ANT3), the 2-oxoglutarate/malate carrier SLC25A11, the serine transporters SFXN1 and SFXN2, and the TIM22-complex lipid kinase AGK. Loss of SMIM26 leaves cultured cells viable but strongly reduces respiratory chain activity; knockout mice die at early developmental stages and homozygous zebrafish mutants show reduced fitness, survival and respiration. A separate line of work places SMIM26 in a complex with the sideroflexin serine transporters and the mitoribosome, where it is proposed to support localized translation of the complex I subunit mt-ND5; whether SMIM26 also has an inner-membrane, matrix-facing pool that could account for direct mitoribosome contact is unresolved, because a single-pass outer-membrane protein spanning the intermembrane space cannot reach the matrix face of the inner membrane. The protein is phosphorylated at several residues of unassigned function, and its loss has been linked to progression of clear cell renal cell carcinoma.

Core Functions

SMIM26 tethers mitochondrial metabolite carriers of the outer and inner membranes to one another. Anchored in the outer membrane by a single transmembrane helix, its soluble region reaches across the intermembrane space so that it can contact outer-membrane porins (VDAC1/2) on one side and inner-membrane carriers (SLC25A6, SLC25A11, SFXN1/2) on the other. It has no catalytic or transport activity itself; the function is to hold these transporters in proximity so that metabolite flux across the two membranes is coordinated, which is required for normal respiratory chain activity. Loss of SMIM26 leaves cultured cells viable with strongly reduced respiration, and is lethal in mouse embryos.

Suggested Questions for Experts

Q: Is there an inner-membrane-integral, matrix-facing pool of SMIM26? This is the single question that would reconcile the outer-membrane/intermembrane-space topology of PMID:41991342 with the inner-membrane localisation and direct mitoribosome binding reported in PMID:40578345, and it decides whether GO:0005743 and GO:0140978 should be retained.

Suggested experts: Gunter Meister, Lena Ho, David A Stroud

Q: Are the three reported partner sets - AGK plus SLC25A11, SFXN1/2 plus the mitoribosome, and VDAC1/2 plus SLC25A6 - alternative occupancies of one promiscuous tether, or genuinely distinct cell-type-specific complexes? The answer determines whether transmembrane transporter binding should be annotated once as a general property or separately per carrier.

Suggested experts: Gunter Meister, Qing-Yu He

Q: What is the function of the phosphorylation sites reported on SMIM26? PMID:41991342 states that the protein is phosphorylated at distinct residues but assigns no role to the modification.

Suggested experts: Gunter Meister, Andrea Pauli

Suggested Experiments

Experiment: Determine SMIM26 topology directly by protease protection of intact mitochondria, mitoplasts and detergent-solubilised membranes, using antibodies against the N and C termini and against an internal epitope tag. Combine with alkaline carbonate extraction to distinguish integral from peripheral association, and with separation of outer membrane, intermembrane space, inner membrane and matrix fractions. A single-pass outer-membrane anchor with an intermembrane-space-facing C terminus predicts complete loss of the C-terminal epitope on mitoplasting.

Hypothesis: SMIM26 is anchored only in the outer membrane, and its association with the mitoribosome is indirect.

Type: subcellular fractionation and protease protection

Experiment: Repeat the mitoribosome co-purification in SFXN1/SFXN2 double-knockout cells and with recombinant SMIM26 and purified mitoribosomes in vitro. Direct binding should survive loss of the sideroflexins and should be reconstitutable from purified components; indirect association should not.

Hypothesis: The mitoribosome association reported for SMIM26 is bridged by SFXN1/2 rather than being a direct contact.

Type: co-immunoprecipitation in knockout background plus in vitro reconstitution

Experiment: Measure ADP/ATP exchange and serine import in mitochondria isolated from SMIM26 knockout cells and from knockout cells rescued with wild-type SMIM26 or with separation -of-function mutants that retain the outer-membrane anchor but lose the intermembrane-space region. If the tether coordinates transport, the anchor-only mutant should fail to restore flux despite correct localisation.

Hypothesis: The adaptor function of SMIM26 is required for coordinated metabolite flux, not merely correlated with it.

Type: isolated-mitochondria transport assay with structure-guided rescue

SMIM26 review notes

Identity and why this gene is interesting

SMIM26 (HGNC:43430, UniProt A0A096LP01) is a 95-amino-acid microprotein. Its locus was
originally annotated as the long non-coding RNA LINC00493 (still the HGNC previous
symbol), so this is a reannotated lncRNA that turned out to encode a protein. UniProt
records a single predicted transmembrane helix (residues 13-35) and calls it a
single-pass mitochondrial outer membrane protein. Two isoforms are recorded
(A0A096LP01-1 and -2); isoform 2 simply lacks residue 40 (VSP_062288) and no
isoform-specific function has been reported, so none of the annotations below are
isoform-scoped.

Translation of the LINC00493 ORF and mitochondrial localisation of its product were
established first: PMID:33386847

Three papers, three interactomes

2023, EMBO Rep (ccRCC). SMIM26 binds AGK and SLC25A11:
PMID:37009826 and
PMID:37009826
Both partners are inner-membrane-associated: SLC25A11 is the mitochondrial
2-oxoglutarate/malate carrier of the inner membrane, and AGK (UniProt Q53H12) is a
lipid kinase (EC 2.7.1.94/2.7.1.107) that is also a subunit of the TIM22 inner-membrane
translocase. This matters for GOA: SMIM26 carries GO:0019901 protein kinase binding with
AGK as the WITH/FROM partner, but AGK is not a protein kinase.

2025, Mol Cell (one-carbon metabolism). A different interactome — sideroflexins and
the mitoribosome:
PMID:40578345 with the loss-of-function phenotype
PMID:40578345
This is the paper behind GOA's GO:0005743 mitochondrial inner membrane (IDA) and
GO:0140978 mitochondrial large ribosomal subunit binding (IDA).

2026, Genes Dev (respiratory chain). A third interactome, and an explicit topology:
PMID:41991342, PMID:41991342,
PMID:41991342, PMID:41991342, and the summary claim
PMID:41991342

SLC25A6 is ADP/ATP translocase 3 (ANT3), the inner-membrane adenine nucleotide carrier —
see this repository's genes/human/SLC25A6 review, which describes it as an
alternating-access ADP/ATP antiporter of the inner membrane. An OMM-anchored protein
reaching SLC25A6 must do so from the intermembrane-space face, which is exactly what the
Genes Dev topology asserts.

The tension GOA now carries

GOA holds, all as IDA/IPI on the same 95-residue protein:

  • GO:0005741 mitochondrial outer membrane (three independent papers)
  • GO:0005743 mitochondrial inner membrane (one paper)
  • GO:0030674 protein-macromolecule adaptor activity (two papers)
  • GO:0044325 transmembrane transporter binding (three papers, four different partners:
    SLC25A11, SFXN1, SLC25A6, VDAC1)
  • GO:0140978 mitochondrial large ribosomal subunit binding (one paper)

The adaptor and transporter-binding calls are mutually reinforcing: every paper finds
SMIM26 bridging metabolite carriers, and the partner set differs between papers rather than
contradicting between them. A microprotein with one TM helix and ~60 residues of soluble
sequence is a plausible tether for several carriers, and different cell backgrounds and
baits will pull out different ones.

The mitoribosome claim is the one that does not sit comfortably. The mitochondrial large
ribosomal subunit is on the matrix face of the inner membrane. A single-pass protein
anchored in the outer membrane and spanning the intermembrane space cannot reach it. Either

  1. there is a second, inner-membrane-integral pool of SMIM26 whose soluble portion faces the
    matrix (which would also explain the GO:0005743 IDA), or
  2. the mitoribosome association is indirect — captured through SFXN1/2 or through the
    co-translational insertion machinery — and the direct-binding reading of GO:0140978 is
    too strong.

I cannot distinguish these from the abstracts available (both PMID:40578345 and
PMID:41991342 are abstract-only in this repository's cache). Per project rules an IDA is not
overruled from an abstract, so GO:0005743 and GO:0140978 are marked UNDECIDED rather
than removed or demoted, with the topological objection recorded. These two annotations
stand or fall together; they are the two claims of the inner-membrane/mitoribosome model.

Curation position taken

  • GO:0030674 protein-macromolecule adaptor activity → core. Two independent IDA
    papers, and it is the one molecular description all three papers agree on.
  • GO:0044325 transmembrane transporter binding → ACCEPT, core-adjacent. It is an
    informative binding term (unlike bare protein binding) and names the actual mechanism.
  • GO:0005741 mitochondrial outer membrane → ACCEPT (core location, three papers).
  • GO:0005739 mitochondrion → ACCEPT but less informative than the membrane terms.
  • GO:0005743 mitochondrial inner membrane and GO:0140978 mitochondrial large ribosomal
    subunit binding → UNDECIDED (see above).
  • GO:0019901 protein kinase binding → MODIFY to GO:0019900 kinase binding. The
    interaction with AGK is real, but AGK is a lipid kinase, not a protein kinase.
  • GO:0005515 protein binding → MARK_AS_OVER_ANNOTATED per project guidance.

Proposed additions (reviewer proposals, single-source)

  • GO:0005758 mitochondrial intermembrane space as a location, from the explicit
    IMS-spanning topology in PMID:41991342.
  • No molecular function is proposed beyond the adaptor term. SMIM26 has no catalytic or
    transport activity of its own; what it does is hold carriers together.

Open questions

  • Is there an inner-membrane pool of SMIM26, and if so what is its topology? This is the
    single question that would reconcile the two 2025/2026 models.
  • Are the reported partner sets (AGK/SLC25A11; SFXN1/2 + mitoribosome; VDAC1/2 + SLC25A6)
    alternative states of one bridging complex, or cell-type-specific interactomes?
  • What do the reported phosphorylation sites do? PMID:41991342 reports phosphorylation at
    distinct residues but the abstract does not assign a function to them.

core_functions:
- description: >-
    SMIM26 tethers mitochondrial metabolite carriers of the outer and inner membranes to
    one another. Anchored in the outer membrane by a single transmembrane helix, its
    soluble region reaches across the intermembrane space so that it can contact
    outer-membrane porins (VDAC1/2) on one side and inner-membrane carriers (SLC25A6,
    SLC25A11, SFXN1/2) on the other. It has no catalytic or transport activity itself; the
    function is to hold these transporters in proximity so that metabolite flux across the
    two membranes is coordinated, which is required for normal respiratory chain activity.
    Loss of SMIM26 leaves cultured cells viable with strongly reduced respiration, and is
    lethal in mouse embryos.
