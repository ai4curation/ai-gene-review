Gene Description

Ornithine aminotransferase (OAT; EC 2.6.1.13) is a nuclear-encoded, mitochondrial matrix, pyridoxal 5'-phosphate-dependent enzyme that reversibly transfers the delta-amino group of L-ornithine to 2-oxoglutarate, forming L-glutamate 5-semialdehyde and L-glutamate. The semialdehyde interconverts spontaneously with pyrroline-5-carboxylate, linking ornithine and arginine degradation to glutamate and proline metabolism. Its functional catalytic unit is a homodimer; recombinant human protein exhibits a tetramer–dimer equilibrium, while crystal structures also show hexameric assemblies. Net reaction direction depends on tissue, developmental stage and substrate supply. Ornithine degradation is prominent in many adult tissues, whereas intestinal ornithine synthesis supports citrulline and arginine production, particularly during early development. Biallelic loss of function causes gyrate atrophy of the choroid and retina, usually with hyperornithinemia after infancy.

Core Functions

PLP-dependent ornithine delta-aminotransferase in the mitochondrial matrix. It reversibly converts L-ornithine plus 2-oxoglutarate to L-glutamate 5-semialdehyde plus glutamate; the semialdehyde/P5C pool can feed proline synthesis or ALDH4A1-mediated oxidation to glutamate. Forward flux contributes to arginine/ornithine catabolism, while intestinal reverse flux supports ornithine and citrulline synthesis in a tissue- and development-dependent manner. The catalytic unit is a dimer; a fixed hexamer is not required by the direct human biochemical evidence.

OAT (Ornithine aminotransferase) — review notes

UniProt: P04181 (OAT_HUMAN). HGNC:8091. Gene on chromosome 10. EC 2.6.1.13.

Core biology (from local files)

OAT is the nuclear-encoded, mitochondrial-matrix, pyridoxal-5'-phosphate (PLP)-dependent
aminotransferase (class-III PLP-dependent / aminotransferase-3 family) that reversibly
transfers the delta-amino group of L-ornithine to 2-oxoglutarate (alpha-ketoglutarate),
producing L-glutamate 5-semialdehyde (which spontaneously cyclizes to
delta-1-pyrroline-5-carboxylate, P5C) and L-glutamate.

  • Reaction (UniProt CATALYTIC ACTIVITY, Rhea:RHEA:25160): "L-ornithine + 2-oxoglutarate =
    L-glutamate 5-semialdehyde + L-glutamate; EC=2.6.1.13"
    [file:human/OAT/OAT-uniprot.txt].
  • FUNCTION: [file:human/OAT/OAT-uniprot.txt "Catalyzes the reversible interconversion of L-ornithine and"]
    (continues "2-oxoglutarate to L-glutamate semialdehyde and L-glutamate.").
  • Cofactor: pyridoxal 5'-phosphate, covalently bound as N6-(pyridoxal phosphate)lysine at
    Lys-292 (active-site Schiff base) [file:human/OAT/OAT-uniprot.txt MOD_RES 292;
    PMID:3754226 identified PLP at Lys-292].
  • Pathway: "Amino-acid biosynthesis; L-proline biosynthesis; L-glutamate 5-semialdehyde
    from L-ornithine: step 1/1" (UniPathway UPA00098 / UER00358)
    [file:human/OAT/OAT-uniprot.txt].

Metabolic role / directionality

OAT links the ornithine pool (urea cycle / arginine metabolism) to the glutamate/proline/P5C
pool. In most tissues it operates catabolically (net ornithine -> P5C/glutamate); in the
small intestine it runs biosynthetically toward ornithine/citrulline/arginine. Physiological
directionality is annotated in both directions in UniProt (RHEA:25161 left-to-right;
RHEA:25162 right-to-left) and captured as two Reactome reactions R-HSA-70654 (forward) and
R-HSA-70666 (reverse). The tissue-enhanced expression in intestine (UniProt HPA:
"Tissue enhanced (intestine)") is consistent with the gut biosynthetic role.

Disease

Deficiency causes gyrate atrophy of the choroid and retina (GACR; MIM:258870), an autosomal
recessive chorioretinal degeneration with hyperornithinemia, early cataract, and type II
muscle-fiber atrophy, leading to blindness. Many missense alleles characterized.

  • PMID:1737786 (Brody et al. 1992): surveyed OAT alleles; "ornithine delta-aminotransferase
    is a nuclear-encoded mitochondrial matrix enzyme which catalyzes the reversible
    interconversion of ornithine and alpha-ketoglutarate to glutamate semialdehyde and
    glutamate"; site-directed mutagenesis + CHO expression "confirms that several of these
    mutations inactivate ornithine delta-aminotransferase and cause gyrate atrophy". This is
    the basis for the IMP -> GO:0004587 annotation (loss-of-function mutants abolish activity).
  • PMID:23076989 (Doimo et al. 2013): functional analysis of missense mutants expressed in a
    yeast OAT-ortholog-deletion strain; "All mutations markedly reduced enzymatic activity";
    mutants "failed to assemble to form the active OAT hexamer". Basis for IMP -> GO:0004587
    and IDA -> mitochondrial matrix (subcellular location determined).
  • PMID:2793865 (Inana et al. 1989): H319Y processing mutant; supports OAT as mitochondrial
    enzyme whose deficiency causes gyrate atrophy; used for the TAS visual perception
    annotation (disease context).
  • PMID:3456579 (Inana et al. 1986): cloned human OAT cDNA; "a nonabundant mitochondrial
    matrix enzyme that is severely deficient in a hereditary chorioretinal degenerative
    disease (gyrate atrophy)". Basis for NAS mitochondrial matrix location and TAS
    GO:0004587.

Reviewed all 21 existing assertions, all original reference sources and the core summary. Original term/evidence/reference/qualifier payloads remain unchanged; no NEW row is added. This session applies the current protein-binding and general-term policies: the OAT–APP interaction is retained as reported interaction evidence (PMID:32814053; curated UniProt CC:INTERACTION records three experiments), but bare GO:0005515 is REMOVE as uninformative. Broad mitochondrial locations and transaminase activity become MODIFY to the already supported specific terms. These decisions supersede historical action conventions in earlier notes, not the measured interaction or localization observations.

Reaction, direction and assembly

PMID:1737786 explicitly describes the mitochondrial matrix reaction: "enzyme which catalyzes the reversible interconversion of ornithine and alpha-ketoglutarate to glutamate semialdehyde and glutamate". Direct mutation/expression evidence supports the native reaction; the historical PMID:23076989 mutant study corroborates lost activity and assembly defects. Matrix targeting is also supported by human precursor/cloning studies PMID:3456579 and PMID:2793865. The two cached Reactome reactions R-HSA-70654/R-HSA-70666 represent forward and reverse chemistry, not a universal net direction.

The prior obligatory-homohexamer statement is too strong. PMID:9514741 observes a crystallographic hexamer constructed from dimers, and directly identifies PLP: "The cofactor pyridoxal-5'-phosphate is bound through a Schiff base to Lys292". In contrast, the native-sequence recombinant human enzyme in the full primary PMID:28345116 exhibits a tetramer–dimer equilibrium; engineered R217A dimers retain catalytic properties. Its conclusion is explicit: "This finding indicates that the catalytic unit of hOAT is the dimer." Retain homomeric binding as a core assembly property, while distinguishing crystallographic assembly, solution equilibrium and minimal catalytic unit. PMID:36834788 is additional primary interface/stability evidence, not evidence for obligatory hexamers. PMID:3754226 is historical active-site/processing context; its abstract alone is not used to assign human-specific residue chemistry beyond the direct human structural evidence.

The comparative synthesis PMID:28272331 and recent human synthesis PMID:41887919 explain developmental and tissue dependence. Intestinal reverse OAT flux can support ornithine/citrulline/arginine synthesis, especially in early life; adult forward catabolism is prominent elsewhere. Human neonatal intestinal expression and the neonatal hypoornithinemia versus later hyperornithinemia distinction prevent extrapolating one direction to every age. These are reviews integrating multiple organisms, not all new human flux experiments. The module's catabolic branch is legitimate because OAT itself performs the ornithine-to-semialdehyde step; no claim is made that all ornithine must come from hepatic ARG1 or even from arginase in every context. The semialdehyde can enter ALDH4A1 oxidation or proline synthesis. No extra process annotation was needed merely for pathway coverage.

Visual-perception GO:0007601 remains MARK_AS_OVER_ANNOTATED: the original PMID:2793865 establishes metabolic disease and a precursor-processing defect, but that does not identify a visual sensory step executed by OAT. This is not an assertion that visual perception is synonymous with phototransduction or that retinal metabolic support is unimportant.
