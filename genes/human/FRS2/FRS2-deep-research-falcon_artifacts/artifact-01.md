| Biological Process | Cell Type/Tissue | Developmental Stage | Key Findings |
|---|---|---|---|
| Oligodendrocyte progenitor specification | Embryonic forebrain/telencephalon; oligodendrocyte-lineage cells | Embryonic development, including approximately E12.5 in mouse studies | FRS2 is a critical mediator of FGFR1 signaling: loss of FRS2 or disruption of FGFR1–FRS2 coupling severely reduces PDGFRA- and OLIG2-positive oligodendrocyte progenitor cells, demonstrating a strong requirement for early lineage specification (pqac-00000037, pqac-00000041). |
| Myelination and myelin-sheath growth | Mature oligodendrocytes in the central nervous system | Postnatal active myelination | Unlike its essential early role, FRS2—together with FRS3—is largely dispensable for FGFR2-dependent myelin growth; FRS2/3 disruption produces only minor changes in myelin-gene expression and sheath thickness, indicating that FGFR2 and TrkB also use other effectors in mature oligodendrocytes (pqac-00000024, pqac-00000037). |
| Hippocampal neurogenesis and neuronal maturation | Dentate-gyrus neural stem cells and dentate granule cells | Postnatal brain development | Combined FRS2/FRS3 loss impairs dentate-gyrus formation, neural-stem-cell maintenance or proliferation, and granule-cell maturation. Because major abnormalities occur mainly in double mutants, these results support overlapping or compensatory FRS2 and FRS3 functions rather than an exclusively FRS2-specific effect (pqac-00000038, pqac-00000043). |
| Dendritogenesis and excitatory synaptogenesis | Hippocampal dentate granule cells | Postnatal neuronal maturation | FRS2/FRS3-dependent signaling supports dendritic complexity, dendrite length, spine formation, and functional excitatory synapses. Double mutants exhibit lower spine density and reduced miniature excitatory postsynaptic-current frequency without corresponding changes in amplitude or paired-pulse ratio (pqac-00000038, pqac-00000043). |
| Cell proliferation and survival | Broadly distributed FGF-responsive cells, including neural precursors | Development and adult tissue homeostasis | Phosphorylated FRS2 recruits GRB2–SOS to activate RAS–RAF–MEK–ERK-dependent mitogenic transcription and recruits GAB1-linked PI3K–AKT signaling that promotes survival and suppresses apoptosis (pqac-00000015, pqac-00000039, pqac-00000044). |
| Cancer invasion, proliferation, and tumor progression | FGFR-dependent cancer models, including ovarian cancer, prostate tumor models, breast and gastric cancer cells, medulloblastoma, and FRS2-amplified liposarcoma | Adult pathological state | Aberrant FGFR–FRS2 signaling supports proliferation, migration, matrix invasion, and tumor growth. Preclinical disruption of the FRS2 PTB-domain interaction or N-terminal myristoylation reduces FRS2/ERK/AKT signaling and tumor phenotypes; however, direct FRS2 ligands remain low-affinity research compounds, and FRS2 amplification alone may not reliably predict response to FGFR inhibitors (pqac-00000053, pqac-00000055, pqac-00000056, pqac-00000060). |


*Table: This table summarizes experimentally supported roles of FRS2 in neural development, intracellular growth and survival signaling, and cancer. It distinguishes FRS2-specific evidence from findings based on combined FRS2/FRS3 perturbation and preclinical disease models.*
