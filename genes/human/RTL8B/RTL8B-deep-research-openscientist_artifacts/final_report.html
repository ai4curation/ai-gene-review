Functional Annotation Report: RTL8B (Retrotransposon Gag-like protein 8B)

Gene: RTL8B (synonyms: CXX1C, FAM127C, MAR8B / MART8B) UniProt: Q17RB0 · Organism: Homo sapiens · Locus: X chromosome (Xq23) Family: FAM127 / Mart / SIRH (Sushi-ichi-related retrotransposon homolog) family Domain: RTL1/LDOC capsid-like (InterPro IPR032549; Pfam PF16297) Date: 2026-10-24

1. Summary (Answer to the Research Question)

RTL8B is a small (~113 aa, ~13 kDa) domesticated retrotransposon Gag/capsid-derived protein. It is not an enzyme, transporter, or classical signaling molecule; rather it is a capsid-homology adaptor/modulator consisting essentially of a single N-terminal Gag "capsid-like" domain. Its established primary function is protein–protein interaction with two other retroelement-derived human proteins — the Gag-like protein PEG10 (RTL2) and the ubiquitin-proteasome shuttle factor UBQLN2 — to control their subcellular localization and activity. Specifically, RTL8(B):

  1. binds the N-terminal capsid domain of PEG10 and acts as a dominant-negative "capsid poison," reducing assembly/release of PEG10 virus-like particles (VLPs) and retaining PEG10 intracellularly;
  2. promotes nuclear/nucleolar translocation of UBQLN2 into ubiquitin-rich sub-nuclear protein-quality-control (PQC) compartments; and
  3. is the obligatory bridging factor that enables PEG10 to recruit UBQLN2 into cytoplasmic stress granules, thereby shaping stress-granule dynamics and proteostasis.

Thus RTL8B operates at the intersection of endogenous retroelement capsid biology and cellular protein quality control, acting in both the nucleolus/nucleus and the cytoplasm (stress granules).

Identity caveat (addressed): "RTL8B" (Q17RB0) is unambiguously the human FAM127/Mart Gag-like gene described below. The published functional literature refers to the protein collectively as "RTL8" because humans carry three X-linked paralogs — RTL8A, RTL8B, RTL8C — encoding near-identical proteins. Sequence comparison performed in this analysis shows RTL8B is 96.5% identical (109/113 residues) to RTL8A (Q9BWD3) and nearly identical to RTL8C (A6ZKI3). The paralogs cannot be distinguished by the antibodies or knockdown/overexpression reagents used in the primary studies, so the functional conclusions apply to RTL8B.

  • Domain architecture: a single RTL1/LDOC capsid-like domain (PF16297) spanning essentially the whole protein. It corresponds to the N-terminal Gag capsid (CA) region of a Sushi-ichi/LTR retrotransposon, and resembles a truncated PEG10 that retains only the capsid-homology portion — it lacks any Pol/protease, reverse-transcriptase, or nucleocapsid (zinc-knuckle) region (Campodonico et al. 2024, 39775359).
  • Paralog set (this analysis): RTL8B vs RTL8A = 96.5% identity; RTL8C near-identical. All three are small single-exon-like Gag capsid genes clustered on the X chromosome.
  • Family placement: member of the SIRH/Mart/RTL family of genes domesticated from LTR retrotransposons in mammals; related members include PEG10 (SIRH1/RTL2), RTL1/PEG11 (SIRH2), and LDOC1 (RTL7/SIRH7) (Kaneko-Ishino & Ishino 2012, 22866050).

Functional inference from structure/evolution: Possessing only a capsid-homology domain and no catalytic module, RTL8B is predicted to act through homotypic/heterotypic capsid–capsid interactions rather than catalysis — consistent with all experimental data showing it works by binding and modulating partner proteins.

Bioinformatic corroboration (this analysis): A BLOSUM62 Smith-Waterman alignment of RTL8B against PEG10 shows RTL8B residues 26–105 align to PEG10 residues ~79–158 at 35% identity / 54% similarity (80-aa window), with conserved capsid motifs (…PE·FDG…, and a C-terminal …MK·VF block: RTL8B AEMKRVF vs PEG10 MMEMKHVF). The alignment covers ~70% of RTL8B — i.e. essentially the entire protein is one copy of PEG10's N-terminal Gag capsid domain — and the homologous stretch coincides with the experimentally-mapped RTL8-binding region of PEG10 capsid (39775359). This independently supports a capsid–capsid heterodimerization mechanism.

3. Primary Function and Mechanism

3.1 Antagonist of PEG10 capsid / virus-like-particle assembly

PEG10 is a Gag-Pol–like, retroelement-derived protein that self-assembles into virus-like particles (VLPs) which package mRNA and are secreted in extracellular vesicles (Segel et al. 2021, 34413232). RTL8 shares homology with the PEG10 N-terminal capsid domain and binds there. On its own RTL8 cannot form VLPs, but it is incorporated into PEG10 particles; when co-expressed, RTL8 decreases PEG10 VLP abundance and raises intracellular PEG10, i.e., it inhibits PEG10 VLP formation/release (Campodonico et al. 2024, 39775359).

Mechanistic model: RTL8B behaves like a capsid "chain terminator"/dominant-negative subunit — it co-assembles with PEG10 capsid via the shared Gag interface but, lacking the full assembly determinants, caps or poisons higher-order particle formation, thereby limiting VLP output and retaining PEG10 in the cell.

3.2 Adaptor controlling UBQLN2 localization (nuclear PQC)

RTL8 was discovered as a UBQLN2 interactor by mass spectrometry and validated with recombinant proteins and in mouse brain (Mohan et al. 2022, 35247097). Endogenous and overexpressed RTL8 localizes to nucleoli; co-expressed RTL8 promotes nuclear translocation of UBQLN2, including under heat-shock stress, and the two colocalize in ubiquitin-enriched sub-nuclear structures containing protein-quality-control components. The effect is selective for UBQLN2 (not UBQLN1 or UBQLN4), and UBQLN2 reciprocally stabilizes RTL8 protein levels.

3.3 Obligatory bridge in the UBQLN2–PEG10 stress-granule axis

RTL8 "confers on UBQLN2 the ability to complex with and regulate PEG10." PEG10 is a core stress-granule component and recruits UBQLN2 to stress granules under stress, but only when RTL8 is present. Perturbing RTL8 (or UBQLN2/PEG10) levels remodels stress-granule disassembly kinetics and translation recovery; within stress granules PEG10 forms VLPs (Mohan et al. 2025, 40680123; preprint 39484508).

Synthesis of mechanism: RTL8B is a molecular linchpin that uses its single capsid interface to simultaneously engage PEG10 (another capsid protein) and the UBQLN2 proteostasis shuttle, thereby (i) tuning PEG10 capsid/VLP assembly and (ii) routing UBQLN2 between cytoplasmic stress granules and nuclear/nucleolar PQC compartments.

4. Subcellular Localization

Compartment Evidence
Nucleolus (steady state) RTL8 localizes to nucleoli under endogenous and overexpressed conditions (35247097); rationalized by an N-terminal arginine-rich NoLS-like motif (see below)
Nucleus / sub-nuclear PQC foci RTL8 + UBQLN2 colocalize in ubiquitin-enriched sub-nuclear structures (35247097)
Cytoplasm / stress granules RTL8 required for PEG10-mediated UBQLN2 recruitment to stress granules (40680123)
Incorporated into PEG10 VLPs RTL8 packaged into PEG10-derived particles (39775359)

RTL8B therefore acts in multiple compartments, shuttling/partitioning between the nucleolus/nucleus and cytoplasmic condensates depending on its partners and cellular stress state.

Sequence rationale for nucleolar targeting (this analysis): RTL8B's N-terminus is strongly basic — 6 arginines in the first 25 residues, with a maximal positive-charge window at residues ~13–26 (RPLRPAARRWRN, net +4), conserved across RTL8A/B/C and lying just upstream of the capsid-homology region. This architecture matches a canonical arginine-rich nucleolar-localization signal (NoLS)/nucleic-acid-binding motif, providing a mechanistic explanation for RTL8B's steady-state nucleolar localization. Notably, the PEG10 N-terminus lacks a comparable arginine cluster, consistent with PEG10 not being intrinsically nucleolar. (Bioinformatic inference; not yet confirmed by mutagenesis.)

5. Pathways and Biological Context

  • Endogenous retroelement capsid pathway: regulator of PEG10 VLP biogenesis (39775359 34413232).
  • Protein quality control / ubiquitin-proteasome system (proteostasis): governs localization of the UBQLN2 shuttle factor and the kinetics of stress-granule assembly/disassembly (35247097 40680123).
  • Tissue/expression context: RTL8 is broadly expressed and co-expressed with PEG10 in many tissues including human brain (39775359). Because UBQLN2 and PEG10 are implicated in ALS/FTD and other neurodegenerative disease, RTL8B's control of their localization and assembly is of likely neurobiological relevance.
  • Evolutionary pathway: domesticated from a Sushi-ichi/LTR retrotransposon Gag during mammalian evolution; part of the SIRH/Mart family whose founding members (PEG10, RTL1) are essential for placentation, situating RTL8 within a retroelement-derived regulatory network (22866050).

Supported (experimental evidence): - H1 — RTL8B is a capsid-homology adaptor, not an enzyme. ✔ (domain structure; truncated-Gag homology, 39775359) - H2 — RTL8 directs UBQLN2 to the nucleus/nucleolus and PQC foci. ✔ (35247097) - H3 — RTL8 binds PEG10 capsid and antagonizes VLP formation. ✔ (39775359) - H4 — RTL8 is required to bridge PEG10 and UBQLN2 at stress granules. ✔ (40680123)

7. Limitations and Future Directions

  • Paralog resolution: reagents do not distinguish RTL8A/B/C; the specific, non-redundant contribution of RTL8B versus its paralogs has not been isolated. Paralog-specific CRISPR/degron studies are needed. This near-identity (~96–97%) also defeats high-throughput resources: in this analysis GTEx v8 returned no median expression for RTL8B/A/C and the AlphaFold DB has no deposited model for Q17RB0 (HTTP 404) — multi-mapping reads and redundant sequences prevent unique assignment. Expression context therefore rests on the primary-literature statement that RTL8 is broadly expressed, including in human brain (39775359).
  • In-vivo physiology: no dedicated RTL8B knockout phenotype is reported; organismal function is inferred from in-vitro/cell and mouse-brain interaction data.
  • Structure: no experimental 3D structure of RTL8B–PEG10 or RTL8B–UBQLN2 complexes; the predicted capsid fold and binding interface merit structural confirmation (cryo-EM/AlphaFold-Multimer).
  • Directionality: whether RTL8B's nucleolar role and its cytoplasmic stress-granule role are competing or sequential activities remains open.
