Gene Description

CIC encodes capicua, a nuclear sequence-specific transcriptional repressor that links RTK/Ras–MAPK signaling to gene expression. Its HMG-box and C1 domains jointly recognize TGAATGAA-like octamers in regulatory DNA, including ETV-family promoters. RTK activation can downregulate CIC and release its target genes from repression. CIC associates with ATXN1 and ATXN1L in repressive complexes and controls developmental gene-expression programs. CIC also functions as a tumor suppressor; somatic mutations, including missense changes in its DNA-recognition domains, recur in oligodendroglioma. In contrast, oncogenic CIC–DUX4 fusions activate CIC target genes in a subset of sarcomas. Loss of CIC disrupts neural maturation and behavior, and human heterozygous loss-of-function variants cause neurodevelopmental disease. Short and long CIC products share the DNA-recognition domains but differ in their N-terminal regions.

Core Functions

CIC recognizes regulatory DNA through its HMG-box and C1 domains and represses RNA polymerase II transcription. Human GFP-tagged CIC-S links promoter occupancy to reduced reporter expression; human HMG–C1 fragments independently establish direct motif recognition. The human construct accession NP_055940.3 maps to Q96RK0-2. Engineered min18/min31 structures do not establish equivalent regulation or affinity of both complete products.

Molecular synthesis and products

The core is sequence-specific DNA-binding transcriptional repression, represented by GO:0001227 with negative regulation of Pol II transcription. Human GFP-CIC reporter and ChIP assays in PMID:28278156 connect promoter occupancy to repression; purified or translated HMG–C1 modules establish direct motif recognition. The paper explicitly names NP_055940.3, which the authenticated UniProt record maps to Q96RK0-2. Its 1608-aa sequence is reconstructed by removing canonical residues 1–909 and applying the equal-length alternative N-terminal segment at 910–931. Q96RK0-1 remains the 2517-aa displayed product. Neither is silently removed or marked inactive.

The 2025 structure (PMID:40967212) uses engineered min18/min31 modules rather than either complete protein. Assay and linker context matter: R201W can increase fragment binding, and R215W differs between EMSA and MST. These results do not justify a blanket statement that pathogenic variants invariably abolish DNA binding. The full-length regulatory consequences and the distinct N termini remain explicit questions. CIC-DUX4 activation is not transferred to normal CIC.
