Gene Description

CA5A encodes carbonic anhydrase VA, a zinc-dependent enzyme imported into the mitochondrial matrix after cleavage of an N-terminal targeting peptide. It catalyzes reversible interconversion of carbon dioxide and bicarbonate. Its bicarbonate production supplies mitochondrial carbamoyl-phosphate synthetase 1 and biotin-dependent carboxylases, linking this reaction to nitrogen disposal and intermediary metabolism without catalyzing their downstream reactions. Human loss-of-function variants can impair enzyme activity or stability and cause episodes of hyperammonemia and associated metabolic disturbances, particularly early in life.

Core Functions

CA5A review notes

2026-10-03 — mitochondrial carbonic anhydrase VA

CA5A (HGNC:1377; UniProt P35218, secondary B2RPF2; previous symbol CA5) encodes a 305-residue precursor. Human liver cDNA expression, mitochondrial fractions and N-terminal sequencing support a 38-residue targeting peptide and the mature chain at residues 39–305. The mature chain is a processing product, not an alternatively spliced isoform. The normal seed contains 20 annotations, 16 references and no alternative-product records; all original source fields are preserved.

The review centers on one activity: reversible carbon dioxide/bicarbonate interconversion in the mitochondrial matrix. Zinc coordination is part of that catalytic apparatus. It is retained as a non-core binding annotation, not made into a second core function. The source seed has no biological-process annotation. This review adds none: supplying bicarbonate is mechanistically distinct from catalyzing the reactions of CPS1, pyruvate carboxylase, propionyl-CoA carboxylase or methylcrotonyl-CoA carboxylase. The clinical metabolite profile supports that physiological connection without assigning CA5A those enzymes' activities.
