Gene Description

CDHR1 (photoreceptor cadherin, formerly PCDH21) is an atypical cadherin with six extracellular cadherin repeats and a single transmembrane segment in its canonical 859-amino-acid product. It is concentrated near the base of photoreceptor outer segments and contributes to outer-segment membrane organization and photoreceptor survival. Human biallelic variants cause retinal dystrophy. Mouse ultrastructure places its ortholog at contacts between nascent outer-segment membranes and the inner segment of the same photoreceptor. Zebrafish Cdhr1a associates with Pcdh15b and supports heterotypic adhesion in cell assays, suggesting a conserved cadherin-based connection between outer segments and calyceal processes; the precise human molecular complex remains unresolved. Ectodomain shedding and association with prominin-1 have been demonstrated in mammalian ortholog systems.

Suggested Questions for Experts

Q: Does native human CDHR1 directly bind PCDH15, and what stoichiometry and geometry connect the outer-segment membrane to calyceal processes in rods versus cones?

Q: Which calcium sites and affinities govern human CDHR1 ectodomain structure and partner binding? The inspected ortholog aggregation study did not measure calcium dependence.

Q: How does the preserved alternative product Q96JP9-2, whose sequence differs around the canonical transmembrane region and lacks the canonical distal tail, localize and function relative to Q96JP9-1?

Q: Which protease cleaves CDHR1, and how is ectodomain shedding coupled to disc formation and release in rods versus cones? CDHR1 is the cleaved substrate, not an established protease.

Q: Is the CDHR1-PROM1 association a direct molecular interaction, and how does it relate to the PCDH15-associated outer-segment connection? Existing mouse PCDH21 co-immunoprecipitation does not resolve a purified binary interface or the native human complex.

CDHR1 functional review

CDHR1 is a photoreceptor membrane cadherin whose best-supported physiological role is outer-segment organization and maintenance. The molecular core is cadherin binding, inferred from a directly tested ortholog interaction and conserved human localization. This is not an assignment of phototransduction activity, calcium transport, protease activity, or a native human intercellular junction.

Evidence and scope

  • PMID:23044944(https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3799916/) was read in the normal full XML cache, including genetic analysis, SD-OCT/AOSLO methods, clinical results and Comment. The segregating human nonsense allele is associated with outer-retinal structural disruption and cone loss. The authors report clinical anatomy, not a purified molecular activity or a direct developmental cell-fate assay. The morphogenesis row is refined to outer-segment organization for anatomical precision; its existing IMP provenance remains unchanged.
  • PMID:20805371(https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2976051/) was read in the normal full cache: the human family, segregation and patient-fibroblast cDNA support frameshift-associated cone-rod degeneration. This is direct human disease evidence for maintenance. Mouse knockout findings in its Discussion are earlier work, not new mouse experiments in this paper.
  • PMID:11738025(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11738025/) was available as an authentic abstract only. It reports mouse basal outer-segment localization, disorganized outer segments and progressive photoreceptor loss. No detailed full-text conditions or human construct are inferred from that abstract.
  • PMID:15284225(https://doi.org/10.1074/jbc.M407928200) has an abstract-only normal cache; selected full original article text was also accessed through its author-shared copy. Methods, developmental localization, Fig.4 cleavage/fractionation, the rds comparison and Discussion were inspected. Mouse and bovine assays distinguish soluble ectodomain from membrane-associated C-terminal material. Binding partners and irreversible assembly coupling were hypotheses. No negative self-association assay was located, and none is claimed here. CDHR1 is the substrate of cleavage; this supplies no protease or proteolysis-process annotation.
  • PMID:18654668(https://www.jci.org/articles/view/35891) also has an abstract-only normal cache, but authentic publisher HTML was recovered and selected Results/Figs.4–6 and Methods were read. The PCDH21 construct was mouse; human WT/R373C PROM1 was tested in cells and transgenic mouse retina. Co-immunoprecipitation supports complex association, not a purified binary interface. The paper supports the mouse donor's outer-segment location. It does not constitute a human CDHR1 binding assay or transfer PROM1's actin behavior to CDHR1.
  • PMID:26668363(https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4702997/) was read in its normal full HTML cache, especially Fig.6 immunogold tomography and the relevant Discussion and Methods. The contacts are between nascent outer-segment membranes and the inner segment of the same mouse photoreceptor. The original Discussion describes “different parts of the same cell”; this is a brief provenance anchor for the geometry. Their partner was unresolved in that study. This anatomy should not be relabeled as native cell-cell adhesion.
  • PMID:41995082(https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.102258) was read in the exact existing-main full XML cache. Selected Figs.1–6 and 8–9, the resource table, cloning, co-IP and K562 methods identify zebrafish Cdhr1a-FLAG and Pcdh15b-MYC, with human host cells. Reciprocal co-IP and aggregation of mixed singly transfected cultures support a heterotypic extracellular interaction; either alone produced minimal aggregation. Human Fig.2 is a separate localization experiment. No human-protein affinity, calcium titration or resolved native human complex was measured. The fish phenotype already includes a slight but significant increase in cone outer-segment length at 5 dpf (4.79 to 5.34 micrometers), with disorganization by 15 dpf and later degeneration; early morphology was not completely normal.

Annotation decisions

All 14 machine assertions, their original evidence codes, references, qualifiers and supporting entities, and both alternative products are preserved. Seven annotations are ACCEPT, four KEEP_AS_NON_CORE, two MODIFY, and one MARK_AS_OVER_ANNOTATED; no NEW row is proposed.

The broad inherited cell/cell-cell adhesion processes remain non-core because the ortholog mixed-cell assay establishes trans-adhesive capability. The principal proposed retinal function instead connects membrane regions of one cell. These are different scopes, not contradictory observations. The homophilic term specifically requires identical molecules on adjacent cells; a general cadherin domain plus the observed heterotypic assay does not establish that specificity. The over-annotation decision is not a universal claim that every human product lacks homophilic binding.

Cell-adhesion-molecule binding is refined to GO:0045296 cadherin binding. GO:0098641 would additionally constrain the activity to a cell-cell process, which would misdescribe the proposed physiological intraphotoreceptor connection. The broad morphogenesis term is refined to the already represented outer-segment-organization process, retaining the human evidence and acknowledging the independent early fish phenotype. No new developmental process or redundant binding assertion is added.

Calcium binding remains a plausible domain-supported structural property, retained as non-core. The selected papers do not measure a CDHR1 calcium affinity. Plasma membrane, membrane and outer-segment membrane describe the canonical product's functional setting. For the plasma-membrane IBA, surface-mediated activity is supported by the ortholog assays in addition to localization; no historical PAINT node was reconstructed. The original PTN identifiers are retained, and the structured source status for the binding refinement is UNRESOLVED at the historical-node level.

The bounded source extract records the exact source assertions, relevant current QuickGO term metadata, and experimental mouse-donor annotation fields with retrieval URLs and hashes. Mouse Q8VHP6 has IDA plasma membrane from PMID:11738025 and IDA outer-segment membrane from PMID:18654668. The current donor records corroborate the preserved transfer; they do not reconstruct the full historical Ensembl/PAINT state.

Q96JP9-1 and Q96JP9-2 remain exactly as seeded. The canonical product has a transmembrane span at 701–721; the preserved alternative sequence changes the corresponding region and removes the distal canonical tail. The cited assays do not establish equivalent localization or activity for both products. Their equivalence is left as a biological question rather than inferred from a shared gene symbol.
