Gene Description

BRPF1 is a multivalent chromatin reader and scaffold that assembles and activates histone acetyltransferase complexes containing KAT6A/MOZ, KAT6B/MORF or KAT7/HBO1. It connects catalytic subunits with ING and MEAF6 cofactors and helps direct their activity toward nucleosomal histone H3, including H3K14 and H3K23. Its PZP module recognizes the unmodified H3 amino terminus and supports chromatin engagement, while its bromodomain recognizes acetylated histone ligands. BRPF1 acts principally in the nucleus, with partner-dependent localization observed in expression assays, and supports context-dependent transcriptional regulation. Monoallelic pathogenic variants cause an intellectual developmental disorder associated with deficient histone H3 acetylation.

Core Functions

Assembles and activates MOZ/MORF acetyltransferase complexes by bridging KAT6A or KAT6B with ING5 and MEAF6, promoting nucleosomal H3 acetylation and context-dependent transcriptional coactivation. BRPF1 also forms alternative KAT7/HBO1 complexes and helps determine their H3 substrate preference; the KAT subunits perform acetyl transfer. Through complex assembly and activation, BRPF1 contributes to the histone-modification component of chromatin remodeling; this does not imply ATP-dependent nucleosome repositioning.

Scientific synthesis

BRPF1 supplies a chromatin-reader and scaffold/activator role in alternative KAT6A/MOZ, KAT6B/MORF and KAT7/HBO1 assemblies. KAT6A and KAT6B are related alternative catalytic partners; the complex name does not require both enzymes in every particle. BRPF1 bridges the KAT proteins to ING and MEAF6 factors, increases activity on nucleosomal substrates, and helps select substrate context. Acetyl transfer is performed by the KAT subunits.
