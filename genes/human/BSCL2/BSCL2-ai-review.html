Gene Description

Seipin, encoded by BSCL2, is an oligomeric integral endoplasmic reticulum membrane protein that organizes lipid droplet biogenesis. Its conserved lumenal domain binds anionic phospholipids, and seipin associates with LDAF1 to define sites where neutral lipids assemble into droplets. Seipin remains at ER–lipid droplet contacts as droplets grow, supporting delivery of lipids and proteins and maintaining an organized droplet population. These functions contribute to cellular lipid storage and adipose tissue physiology. Biallelic loss causes congenital generalized lipodystrophy, whereas specific dominant variants that affect folding or glycosylation are associated with motor neuropathy.

Core Functions

Anionic-phospholipid binding by oligomeric seipin is part of a physical ER scaffold that, together with LDAF1, organizes lipid-droplet assembly sites and supports lipid and protein delivery during growth. Seipin remains at ER–LD contacts while LDAF1 can move onto the growing droplet. Formation, subsequent organization and lipid storage are represented as one coupled mechanistic unit; chemical triglyceride synthesis, desaturation and lipase catalysis are not assigned to seipin.

Mechanistic synthesis

Seipin is an oligomeric ER membrane protein that contributes physical structure to lipid-droplet assembly and growth. Human structural work reports an anionic-phospholipid-binding lumenal domain and an undecameric assembly (PMID:30293840(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30293840/), complete cached abstract). Human live-cell work places the seipin–LDAF1 machinery at droplet initiation sites and shows that recruited machinery can redirect those sites (PMID:31708432(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31708432/), relevant Results/Methods read). Seipin remains ER-associated while LDAF1 can move onto the growing droplet. Human knockout, rescue, endogenous tagging and cargo-exchange experiments connect seipin to functional ER–LD contacts (PMID:27879284(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27879284/)).
