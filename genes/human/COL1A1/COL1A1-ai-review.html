Gene Description

COL1A1 encodes the pro-alpha-1 chain of type I collagen, a major fibrillar component of extracellular matrices in bone, skin, tendon and many other connective tissues. Its C-terminal propeptide helps select and assemble collagen chains in the endoplasmic reticulum. Two alpha-1(I) chains usually associate with one alpha-2(I) chain, although alpha-1(I) homotrimers also form. Following secretion and propeptide removal by other proteins, mature collagen molecules assemble into fibrils that provide tensile support and organize the matrix. Collagen-I matrices also interact with extracellular proteins and influence cellular adhesion, migration and signaling. Pathogenic COL1A1 variants disrupt matrix quantity or architecture and cause osteogenesis imperfecta and other connective-tissue disorders.

Structural core, assembly and processing

COL1A1 has one integrated extracellular-matrix structural core: GO:0030020, in the type I collagen trimer and extracellular matrix, contributing to collagen fibril organization. The current GO definition expressly encompasses extracellular collagens. This is the structural role of an assembled matrix constituent, not a claim that every cited proteomic paper measured tensile force in an isolated alpha-1 chain. Source rows with broad structural-constituent activity are refined to this already represented function rather than supplemented with a redundant new annotation.

PMID:28281531 directly studies recombinant human C-terminal propeptides. The homotrimer crystal and biochemical assembly experiments establish selective chain association; the heterotrimer was assessed biochemically and by SAXS, with residue interfaces modeled rather than determined in a heterotrimer crystal. These isolated precursor-domain experiments are not themselves a mature-fibril mechanics assay. The assembly information is integrated with established fibrillar collagen structure, clinical structural perturbations and direct extracellular-matrix observations. Calcium-dependent features of a construct are not promoted to a new COL1A1 calcium-binding assertion in this review.

PMID:18375391 studies patient fibroblasts carrying specified COL1A2 C-propeptide mutations, not engineered COL1A1 mutations.
