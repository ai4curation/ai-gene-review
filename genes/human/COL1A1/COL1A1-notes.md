# COL1A1 evidence review

## Scope and preserved sources

This assessment concerns human COL1A1 (HGNC:2197; UniProt P02452), the precursor of the alpha-1 chain of type I collagen. The genuine isolated fetch produced 149 distinct source assertions, 93 reference pairs and no `alternative_products` section. All source assertions, qualifiers, supporting entities and original reference identifiers are preserved. The 149 raw GOA data rows are reconciled one for one to the 149 review assertions by literal tuple comparison. No new annotation is proposed.

The selected publication and Reactome caches preserve exact available main bytes at the pinned source snapshot. Freshly fetched full variants are archived separately where an existing main cache was shorter. In particular, the fresh full texts of PMID:18375391 and PMID:26848503 were inspected without replacing their shared selected caches. A `full_text_available` metadata value is not treated as proof that the entire article body is present. Actual access is described in each reference assessment and in the compact source index.

## Structural core, assembly and processing

COL1A1 has one integrated extracellular-matrix structural core: GO:0030020, in the type I collagen trimer and extracellular matrix, contributing to collagen fibril organization. The current GO definition expressly encompasses extracellular collagens. This is the structural role of an assembled matrix constituent, not a claim that every cited proteomic paper measured tensile force in an isolated alpha-1 chain. Source rows with broad structural-constituent activity are refined to this already represented function rather than supplemented with a redundant new annotation.

PMID:28281531 directly studies recombinant human C-terminal propeptides. The homotrimer crystal and biochemical assembly experiments establish selective chain association; the heterotrimer was assessed biochemically and by SAXS, with residue interfaces modeled rather than determined in a heterotrimer crystal. These isolated precursor-domain experiments are not themselves a mature-fibril mechanics assay. The assembly information is integrated with established fibrillar collagen structure, clinical structural perturbations and direct extracellular-matrix observations. Calcium-dependent features of a construct are not promoted to a new COL1A1 calcium-binding assertion in this review.

PMID:18375391 studies patient fibroblasts carrying specified COL1A2 C-propeptide mutations, not engineered COL1A1 mutations. It directly measures alpha-1/alpha-2 association and alpha-1 homotrimer formation. The positive source association is retained. Its immediate functional readouts concern intracellular procollagen assembly and processing; extracellular fibril organization is a downstream structural role, supported holistically rather than described as a directly imaged endpoint of that paper. PMID:17211858 supplies a different human context: mutant COL1A1 cysteine-containing chains and altered dermal fibrils. Mutant covalent dimers are not generalized to an unmodified wild-type assay. Wild-type C-propeptide self-assembly is independently supported by PMID:28281531.

Procollagen secretion, propeptide cleavage, hydroxylation and glycosylation require other proteins. COL1A1 is often their substrate or cargo. The reviewed Reactome records are compartment assertions and do not establish that collagen catalyzes its own maturation. No peptidase, synthase or additional processing-process activity is manufactured. The question about substrate representation belongs in expert questions rather than a new process assertion.

PMID:15095409 is not dismissed because its title emphasizes COL5A1. Its complete abstract explicitly includes COL1A1-haploinsufficient osteogenesis-imperfecta fibroblasts as a comparator. Bounded publisher and indexed retrieval attempts did not recover its complete original text. The exact contribution asserted by the original collagen-biosynthesis annotation remains unresolved: reduced synthesis or secretion of collagen does not alone distinguish performed biosynthetic work from availability of the substrate/product. Row 122 is therefore source-scoped UNDECIDED, without denying normal collagen assembly or claiming a wrong gene.

## Core synthesis and remaining questions

The single core integrates the assembled collagen matrix’s structural contribution with chain association and fibril organization. It does not duplicate secondary binding, assign the activities of processing enzymes to their substrate, or imply that an isolated propeptide has the mechanics of a mature fibril. The major outstanding questions concern the exact source-specific biosynthesis assertion, substrate-versus-performed-step representation, physiological interpretation of high-throughput pairs, and precursor/fragment-specific extracellular signaling. Proposed experiments distinguish direct pair binding or matrix function from expression, secretion and secondary phenotypes.

## Tranche 4B Addendum — Category C Receptor Ligand Role in Collagen–DDR Signaling (`GO:0038063`)

While COL1A1 does not possess intrinsic receptor or catalytic activity (and therefore Reactome IEA transfers of `GO:0005178` *integrin binding* and `GO:0016176` *superoxide-generating NADPH oxidase activator activity* remain removed), native triple-helical type I collagen fibrils (`[alpha-1(I)]2[alpha-2(I)]`, `GO:0005584`) act directly as insoluble extracellular matrix receptor ligands (`GO:0048018`) that bind the discoidin domains of preformed DDR1 and DDR2 receptor tyrosine kinase dimers (`GO:0038062`) to trigger sustained receptor autophosphorylation and initiate the collagen-activated tyrosine kinase receptor signaling pathway (`GO:0038063`) ([Reactome:R-HSA-2327738](file:///Users/cjm/orca/workspaces/ai-gene-review/aigr-ligands/reactome/R-HSA-2327738.md), `"DDR1 binds several of the major fibrillar collagens (types I, II, III, and V) and the non-fibrillar collagen IV (Shrivastava et al. 1997, Vogel et al. 1997, Hou et al. 2001)."`; [Reactome:R-HSA-2465890](file:///Users/cjm/orca/workspaces/ai-gene-review/aigr-ligands/reactome/R-HSA-2465890.md), `"DDR2 binds the major fibrillar collagens types I, II, III, and V) and the non-fibrillar collagen X (Shrivastava et al. 1997, Vogel et al. 1997, Leitinger & Kwan 2006)."`).

In PANTHER family `PTHR24023` (`COLLAGEN ALPHA`), PAINT placed `GO:0038063` only on the `COL4A3` (`PTN000615596`) and `COL4A6` (`PTN000615545`) subfamilies from mouse seeds, omitting the major fibrillar collagen clade (`PTN009074843`: `COL1A1`, `COL1A2`, `COL2A1`, `COL3A1`) and omitting `GO:0048018` (*receptor ligand activity*). We therefore add `NEW` `IC` annotations for `GO:0048018` (*receptor ligand activity*) and `GO:0038063` (*collagen-activated tyrosine kinase receptor signaling pathway*) and a corresponding second `core_functions` entry to `COL1A1-ai-review.yaml`, paired with [PTHR24023-review.yaml](file:///Users/cjm/orca/workspaces/ai-gene-review/aigr-ligands/interpro/panther/PTHR24023/PTHR24023-review.yaml) and [collagen_ddr_signaling.yaml](file:///Users/cjm/orca/workspaces/ai-gene-review/aigr-ligands/modules/collagen_ddr_signaling.yaml).
