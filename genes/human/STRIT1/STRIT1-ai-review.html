Gene Description

STRIT1 encodes DWORF (dwarf open reading frame), a 35-residue tail-anchored transmembrane micropeptide translated from a muscle-specific transcript formerly annotated as a long noncoding RNA. It resides in the sarcoplasmic reticulum membrane of cardiac and slow-twitch skeletal muscle, where its single C-terminal helix binds the SERCA Ca2+-ATPase at the same transmembrane groove used by the inhibitory regulins phospholamban, sarcolipin and myoregulin. DWORF displaces these inhibitors and, in studies with the human peptide, also raises SERCA activity directly, increasing the apparent Ca2+ affinity and turnover of the pump. The result is faster Ca2+ reuptake into the sarcoplasmic reticulum, larger SR Ca2+ stores and faster muscle relaxation. DWORF binds preferentially to the Ca2+-bound, phosphorylated conformations of SERCA and forms homo- and hetero-oligomers with the other regulins. Loss of Dworf in mouse slows relaxation of slow-twitch muscle, and its overexpression protects mouse hearts in models of dilated cardiomyopathy.

description: STRIT1 encodes DWORF (dwarf open reading frame), a 35-residue tail-anchored transmembrane
  micropeptide translated from a muscle-specific transcript formerly annotated as a long noncoding RNA.
  It resides in the sarcoplasmic reticulum membrane of cardiac and slow-twitch skeletal muscle, where
  its single C-terminal helix binds the SERCA Ca2+-ATPase at the same transmembrane groove used by the
  inhibitory regulins phospholamban, sarcolipin and myoregulin. DWORF displaces these inhibitors and,
  in studies with the human peptide, also raises SERCA activity directly, increasing the apparent Ca2+
  affinity and turnover of the pump. The result is faster Ca2+ reuptake into the sarcoplasmic reticulum,
  larger SR Ca2+ stores and faster muscle relaxation. DWORF binds preferentially to the Ca2+-bound, phosphorylated
  conformations of SERCA and forms homo- and hetero-oligomers with the other regulins. Loss of Dworf in
  mouse slows relaxation of slow-twitch muscle, and its overexpression protects mouse hearts in models
  of dilated cardiomyopathy.
