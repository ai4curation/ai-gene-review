Gene Description

APELA (ELABELA, ELA, Toddler) encodes a small secreted peptide hormone that is the second endogenous agonist of the apelin receptor APLNR (APJ), a Gi-coupled G-protein-coupled receptor. The gene lies in a transcript that was first annotated as non-coding; it encodes a 54-residue precursor whose 22-residue signal peptide is removed to release the mature 32-residue peptide ELA-32. Shorter C-terminal forms (ELA-21, ELA-14, ELA-11) are also active, and the C-terminal residues are essential for receptor binding and signalling, while the positively charged N-terminal region increases affinity. ELA binds APLNR in human heart, inhibits forskolin-stimulated cAMP production through a pertussis-toxin-sensitive Gi pathway, stimulates ERK1/2 phosphorylation, recruits beta-arrestin and drives receptor internalization. It circulates in human plasma at subnanomolar concentrations and is expressed in vascular endothelium, kidney, placental trophoblasts and pluripotent stem cells. In zebrafish it is a motogen required for mesendoderm migration during gastrulation, endoderm differentiation and heart formation; in mouse it is required for normal cardiovascular development, sinus venosus-derived coronary vessel growth and placental angiogenesis, and its loss causes preeclampsia-like disease in pregnancy. In the adult it increases cardiac contractility and output and causes vasodilatation. In human embryonic stem cells, which lack APLNR, secreted ELA supports self-renewal through PI3K/AKT/mTORC1 signalling via an unidentified alternative receptor.

Core Functions

Secreted peptide hormone that is an endogenous agonist of the Gi-coupled apelin receptor APLNR. The mature peptide (ELA-32, and shorter C-terminal forms) binds APLNR in the extracellular space and activates it, inhibiting adenylyl cyclase to lower cAMP, stimulating ERK1/2, recruiting beta-arrestin and driving receptor internalization. Developmental and cardiovascular roles (mesendoderm migration, heart and coronary vessel formation, placental angiogenesis, cardiac contractility) are downstream of this activity.

  endoderm differentiation and heart formation; in mouse it is required for normal
  cardiovascular development, sinus venosus-derived coronary vessel growth and placental
  angiogenesis, and its loss causes preeclampsia-like disease in pregnancy. In the adult it
  increases cardiac contractility and output and causes vasodilatation. In human embryonic
  stem cells, which lack APLNR, secreted ELA supports self-renewal through PI3K/AKT/mTORC1
  signalling via an unidentified alternative receptor.

core_functions:
- description: >-
    Secreted peptide hormone that is an endogenous agonist of the Gi-coupled apelin receptor
    APLNR. The mature peptide (ELA-32, and shorter C-terminal forms) binds APLNR in the
    extracellular space and activates it, inhibiting adenylyl cyclase to lower cAMP,
    stimulating ERK1/2, recruiting beta-arrestin and driving receptor internalization.
    Developmental and cardiovascular roles (mesendoderm migration, heart and coronary vessel
    formation, placental angiogenesis, cardiac contractility) are downstream of this activity.
