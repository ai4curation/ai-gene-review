Gene Description

CHST14 encodes dermatan 4-O-sulfotransferase 1, a Golgi-associated carbohydrate sulfotransferase with a predicted type-II membrane topology. It transfers sulfate from PAPS to the C4 hydroxyl of N-acetylgalactosamine next to iduronic acid in dermatan chains. Its catalytic activity cooperates with dermatan sulfate epimerase 1 to generate extended sulfated iduronic-acid regions of dermatan sulfate proteoglycans. Biallelic loss-of-function variants cause musculocontractural Ehlers-Danlos syndrome, with congenital contractures and connective-tissue fragility; affected fibroblasts show deficient dermatan sulfate and increased chondroitin sulfate.

Core Functions

CHST14 catalyzes PAPS-dependent GalNAc 4-O-sulfation during dermatan sulfate proteoglycan biosynthesis. Human full-length recombinant enzyme assays establish the chemical specificity, and a purified human 61–376 construct cooperates with DS-epi1 to form extended sulfated iduronic-acid blocks. Endogenous human-cell microscopy supports a Golgi location; type-II membrane topology remains sequence-based. Cellular FRET/co-immunoprecipitation supports enzyme association, without assigning epimerase activity or a defined complex stoichiometry to CHST14.

2026-10-10 follow-up: what the source establishes and what is refined

The broad sulfate-transfer annotations are sound. Human CHST14 nevertheless has a directly measured, more specific reaction: PAPS-dependent GalNAc C4 sulfation next to iduronic acid. The original CHO/Tag expression assays used extracts and medium, with controls and product analysis (PMID:11470797); the later human 61–376 preparation cooperates with DS-epi1 in a reconstituted reaction (PMID:29976758). The retained MODIFY recommendations express this target-specific chemistry. They do not assert dermatan specificity for every member of the family or alter the historical PAINT node or electronic rule.

A family membership table and the list of extant WITH/FROM sources cannot establish historical IBD placement. In particular, a single listed CHST14 source at PTN002637560 does not demonstrate a CHST14-specific ancestral node. Its legitimate self-evidence supports the existing assertion; the biosynthetic refinement is grounded in measured chemistry. The broad membrane review and the more informative MF recommendation address different annotation properties. Existing specific MF rows provide corroboration, and no duplicate NEW assertion is proposed.

The original biosynthesis qualifier acts_upstream_of_or_within includes work within the process. CHST14 actually performs the sulfate-transfer step, so involved_in is the more precise biological interpretation; the original qualifier is retained rather than silently rewritten. The substrate/product experiments establish this performed role independently of disease necessity.

The phosphate-ion assertion remains over-annotated at the source's actual scope. The original alignment labels putative PAPS contact regions, and the Discussion explicitly treats phosphate-group contacts as hypotheses (PMID:11470797, sequence alignment and Discussion). A phosphate group within PAPS is not evidence for a separate phosphate-ion ligand activity. This conclusion does not require a categorical claim that free-ion interaction is impossible, and the paper supplies no negative free-ion binding assay.

The CFTR association retains a defined transporter-binding refinement. Its three literal records comprise traditional MaMTH and WT/F508del screening observations, with the original tags and variant features visible (PMID:35156780, Figure EV5 and Dataset EV2 records). The term identifies the tested partner class; it does not assert endogenous channel regulation, purified direct contact, or a native encounter between the Golgi enzyme and channel. Generic non-core retention remains appropriate for other pairs without an established useful class. These choices use the same evidence standard.

Five primary-supported annotations cite the publications directly; provenance pointers remain for literal interaction, rule, PAINT and Reactome records where needed.
