Gene Description

DOCK6 is a DOCK-family guanine-nucleotide exchange factor that activates Rac1 and CDC42 signaling and helps regulate actin organization and cell morphology. It occupies cytosolic and perinuclear pools, and association with LRCH proteins can redistribute it toward the cell periphery. DOCK6-dependent signaling contributes to neuronal outgrowth, while biallelic human DOCK6 disruption causes Adams–Oliver syndrome with vascular and limb abnormalities. Its signaling output is sensitive to cellular regulatory context.

Core Functions

DOCK6 promotes nucleotide exchange on Rho-family small GTPases, coupling their activation to cytoskeletal remodeling. Rac1/CDC42 exchange was reported in neuronal-cell studies, while human patient fibroblasts show altered actin organization. Human DOCK6 has a cytosolic pool and associates with LRCH proteins that can redistribute it. Activation depends on experimental context: a later human-cell FRET screen detected no Rac1/CDC42 activation under its tested conditions.

DOCK6 evidence notes

DOCK6 has one integrated GEF role connecting Rho-family activation to actin organization. The review retains ten imported source assertions and proposes no new annotation. The supported specific refinements are cytoplasm to cytosol, small-GTPase signaling to Rho signaling, and Rho regulation to positive regulation. The latter reflects the performed exchange/activation step, not developmental necessity. Positive generic association is retained as non-core under the scoped project instruction.

Activity, species and product scope

PMID:17196961(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17196961/) reports Rac1/CDC42 exchange and explicitly identifies mouse N1E-115 cells. Its full Methods remained inaccessible, so the introduced construct species and purification cannot be reconstructed from the abstract. PMID:23462102(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23462102/) describes mouse Dock6 phosphorylation/dephosphorylation during neuronal development; the complete abstract was read, not its full Methods. PMID:27693507(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27693507/) describes compensatory human-cell responses after chronic DOCK6 loss, distinct from acute depletion. Human patient-fibroblast actin and morphology phenotypes in PMID:21820096(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21820096/), Results and Figure 4, corroborate the biological role but are not a direct human exchange assay. Its mouse expression experiments remain separate.

The 2020 human-cell screen in PMID:32203420(https://doi.org/10.1038/s41556-020-0488-x) detected no RhoA/Rac1/CDC42 activation by full-length DOCK6 or its engineered DHR2 fragment under its tested conditions. Figure 2a source data give full-length mean ratios 1.025313, 0.995138 and 0.988886, respectively, below the stated thresholds 1.0619, 1.0121 and 1.016. The DHR2 fragment means are likewise below threshold. These conditional negatives are preserved alongside positive earlier studies; they do not establish universal inactivity. The paper discusses activation discrepancies and autoinhibition generally without demonstrating a specific DOCK6 release mechanism.

The official Q96HP0 record and FASTA identify one displayed 2047-residue product. Supplementary Table 1 row 78 identifies the human DOCK6 library construct; translating its coding sequence reproduces the current protein exactly. Tags and the 1586–2047 DHR2 fragment are engineered constructs, not natural products. Core substrate identifiers RAC1/P63000 and CDC42/P60953 are verified human identities; they do not transform the older neuronal study into a purified human-protein experiment.

LRCH association and localization

The genuine original PDF, journal page 506/Figure 6f, states: “LRCH4 binds all C-Dock RhoGEFs, but not DOCK9.” The targeted HEK293T FLAG-LRCH4/mCherry-DOCK6 co-immunoprecipitation corroborates the supplementary AP-MS association. Two exact IntAct pair records were sequence-authenticated against Q96HP0 and O75427. The targeted record's Figure 6E locator differs from the original Figure 6f. Neither co-IP nor the spoke-expanded AP-MS record is represented as purified binary affinity. The DOCK7 DHR2 sufficiency experiment and DOCK8 ER recruitment are not assigned to DOCK6. Figure 6d supports DOCK6 redistribution toward the periphery with LRCH2.

HPA target data identify supported cytosol ICC/IF. The data and target identifiers were inspected, not the microscopy pixels. Reactome R-HSA-1011598 contains input set R-HSA-1012978 with explicit member R-HSA-1012966, human Q96HP0 residues 1–2047 in cytosol. This positive path corroborates localization. The event title's RhoGEFs wording differs from its Rho-GTPase substrate description. Perinuclear enrichment attributed to the inaccessible 2007 experiment remains UNDECIDED; diffuse cytosol does not reproduce or refute that specific distribution.
