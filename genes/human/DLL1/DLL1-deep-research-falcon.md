# Comprehensive Research Report: DLL1 (Delta-like Protein 1) - Gene Functional Annotation

## Gene Identity Verification

The gene DLL1 (UniProt accession O00548) encodes Delta-like protein 1 in humans, a canonical member of the Delta/Serrate/LAG-2 (DSL) family of Notch pathway ligands (vazquezulloa2022reversibleandbidirectional pages 1-3, katoh2020precisionmedicinefor pages 1-2). This protein is the human ortholog of Drosophila Delta and should be distinguished from the non-canonical ligand DLK1 and the atypical ligand DLL3 (katoh2020precisionmedicinefor pages 1-2). All literature retrieved is consistent with the UniProt annotation, confirming that DLL1 is an unambiguous gene symbol in humans encoding Delta-like protein 1.

## Protein Structure and Molecular Organization

DLL1 is synthesized as a precursor protein that matures into a type I single-pass transmembrane glycoprotein (vazquezulloa2022reversibleandbidirectional pages 8-11, vazquezulloa2022reversibleandbidirectional pages 1-3). The extracellular region comprises an N-terminal module at the N-terminus of Notch ligands (MNNL), the highly conserved Delta/Serrate/LAG-2 (DSL) domain, and multiple cysteine-rich epidermal growth factor (EGF)-like repeats (you2023targetingthedllnotch pages 1-2, vazquezulloa2022reversibleandbidirectional pages 8-11). The DSL domain and MNNL module are critical structural elements for Notch receptor binding, with the DSL domain containing a conserved positively charged surface loop bordered by aromatic residues that mediates receptor interaction (feng2024thestructuraland pages 49-52). The protein also contains a single transmembrane domain and a cytoplasmic intracellular domain (ICD) that is intrinsically disordered and terminates in a C-terminal PDZ-binding motif (vazquezulloa2022reversibleandbidirectional pages 8-11, vazquezulloa2022reversibleandbidirectional pages 18-19). This PDZ-binding motif enables interactions with scaffold proteins including MAGI proteins, MPDZ/MUPP1, and syntenin, which regulate DLL1 membrane localization and stability (vazquezulloa2022reversibleandbidirectional pages 15-16).

## Primary Molecular Function as a Notch Ligand

DLL1 functions as a **contact-dependent cell-to-cell signaling ligand** rather than as an enzyme, transporter, or structural protein. It operates through direct physical interaction with Notch receptors on adjacent cells—a process termed trans-activation (vazquezulloa2022reversibleandbidirectional pages 1-3, you2023targetingthedllnotch pages 1-1). The molecular mechanism involves DLL1 on a signal-sending cell binding to Notch receptors on a neighboring signal-receiving cell. This ligand-receptor engagement induces a conformational change in Notch that exposes the S2 cleavage site in the juxtamembrane region (vazquezulloa2022reversibleandbidirectional pages 1-3). ADAM-family disintegrin metalloproteases then cleave Notch at S2, releasing the Notch extracellular domain, which is trans-endocytosed together with the ligand into the signal-sending cell (vazquezulloa2022reversibleandbidirectional pages 1-3, vazquezulloa2022reversibleandbidirectional pages 11-13). The remaining membrane-tethered Notch fragment undergoes subsequent cleavage at the S3 site by the γ-secretase complex, liberating the Notch intracellular domain (NICD) (vazquezulloa2022reversibleandbidirectional pages 1-3, you2023targetingthedllnotch pages 1-2).

### Trans-Activation and Cis-Inhibition

DLL1 exhibits dual signaling modalities. In trans-activation, DLL1 expressed by one cell activates Notch signaling in neighboring cells, inducing canonical Notch target genes such as HEY1 and HEYL (zhang2021feedbackregulationof pages 6-8, zhang2021feedbackregulationof pages 4-6). In contrast, DLL1 can also mediate cis-inhibition when expressed in the same cell as Notch receptors, preventing that cell from responding to DLL1 presented by neighboring signal-sending cells (zhang2021feedbackregulationof pages 6-8, zhang2021feedbackregulationof pages 4-6). Cis-inhibition occurs upstream of NICD generation and helps establish signaling directionality by creating distinct sender and receiver cell populations (zhang2021feedbackregulationof pages 6-8, vazquezulloa2022reversibleandbidirectional pages 5-7). The mechanism likely involves receptor titration or mutual degradation of ligand and receptor within the same cell (vazquezulloa2022reversibleandbidirectional pages 5-7, feng2024thestructuraland pages 25-28).

### Receptor Specificity and Glycosylation Modulation

DLL1 functionally interacts with Notch1 and Notch2 receptors, with recent quantitative studies demonstrating that DLL1 activates Notch2 more strongly than Notch1 overall (kuintzle2025diversityinnotch pages 6-7). However, the glycosyltransferase Lunatic Fringe (Lfng) significantly enhances DLL1-mediated activation of both receptors, with approximately threefold enhancement observed for DLL1-Notch1 signaling (kuintzle2025diversityinnotch pages 6-7). The extracellular EGF-like repeats 1–13 of NOTCH1 are involved in DLL1 binding (katoh2020precisionmedicinefor pages 1-2). This receptor selectivity and Fringe-dependent modulation contribute to the fine-tuning of Notch pathway activity in different cellular contexts.

## Subcellular Localization and Trafficking

DLL1 is primarily localized to the **plasma membrane and adherens junctions**, where it carries out its function as a transmembrane signaling ligand (vazquezulloa2022reversibleandbidirectional pages 11-13, vazquezulloa2022reversibleandbidirectional pages 15-16). The protein is not constitutively secreted; rather, full-length DLL1 is membrane-tethered and functions through cell-cell contact (vazquezulloa2022reversibleandbidirectional pages 14-15, vazquezulloa2022reversibleandbidirectional pages 8-11). However, DLL1 undergoes dynamic membrane trafficking that is critical for its signaling activity. Active DLL1 is monoubiquitinated and internalized into Epsin-dependent recycling compartments, and Rab11-mediated recycling returns the ligand to the plasma membrane (vazquezulloa2022reversibleandbidirectional pages 5-7, vazquezulloa2022reversibleandbidirectional pages 11-13). This endocytosis is not merely degradative but is actually required for productive Notch activation, potentially by generating the mechanical or molecular conditions necessary for receptor cleavage (vazquezulloa2022reversibleandbidirectional pages 11-13, vazquezulloa2022reversibleandbidirectional pages 19-20).

PDZ domain-containing scaffold proteins regulate DLL1 localization and stability at the membrane. MAGI proteins recruit DLL1 to adherens junctions, MPDZ/MUPP1 also directs ligands to these junctions, and syntenin promotes DLL1 retention at the cell membrane (vazquezulloa2022reversibleandbidirectional pages 15-16). SYNJBP2/ARIP2 increases DLL1 half-life by limiting lysosomal degradation, thereby promoting membrane accumulation and greater ligand availability for Notch signaling (vazquezulloa2022reversibleandbidirectional pages 15-16). DLL1 can also undergo proteolytic processing via ADAM-mediated ectodomain shedding, releasing a soluble extracellular domain, while the remaining membrane-bound C-terminal fragment may undergo γ-secretase cleavage to liberate an intracellular domain (vazquezulloa2022reversibleandbidirectional pages 11-13, vazquezulloa2022reversibleandbidirectional pages 8-11).

## Notch Pathway Signaling and Downstream Targets

Upon trans-activation of Notch receptors, the released NICD translocates to the nucleus and forms a transcriptional activation complex with the DNA-binding protein RBPJ/CSL and Mastermind-like (MAML) coactivators (vazquezulloa2022reversibleandbidirectional pages 1-3, feng2024thestructuraland pages 25-28). This complex recruits p300 and the mediator complex, enabling RNA polymerase II loading and transcription of Notch target genes (feng2024thestructuraland pages 25-28, feng2024thestructuraland pages 21-25). The primary downstream transcriptional targets include members of the HES (Hairy and Enhancer of Split) and HEY (Hairy/Enhancer of Split related with YRPW motif) gene families (zhang2021feedbackregulationof pages 4-6, feng2024thestructuraland pages 25-28, anusewicz2021notchsignalingpathway pages 18-19). Direct experimental evidence from human myoblasts demonstrates DLL1-dependent regulation of HES1, HEY1, and HEYL (zhang2021feedbackregulationof pages 4-6, zhang2021feedbackregulationof pages 6-8). These transcription factors then regulate cell-fate decisions, differentiation programs, and proliferation in a context-dependent manner.

## Biological Processes and Developmental Roles

### Skeletal Muscle Development and Regeneration

DLL1 plays a critical role in skeletal muscle development and tissue homeostasis. The myogenic transcription factor MyoD directly induces DLL1 transcription as myoblasts enter the differentiation program, binding to conserved E-box motifs in the Dll1 gene (zhang2021feedbackregulationof pages 14-15, zhang2021feedbackregulationof pages 2-4). DLL1 is the most strongly induced Notch ligand during early human myoblast differentiation, peaking around 36 hours (zhang2021feedbackregulationof pages 2-4). Through trans-activation, DLL1-expressing differentiating myoblasts activate Notch in neighboring cells to suppress MyoD and preserve muscle stem cell quiescence and progenitor states, increasing expression of Pax7 and preventing premature differentiation (zhang2021feedbackregulationof pages 4-6, zhang2021feedbackregulationof pages 2-4). Conversely, through cis-inhibition, DLL1 permits the DLL1-expressing cell itself to proceed with differentiation (zhang2021feedbackregulationof pages 6-8, zhang2021feedbackregulationof pages 4-6). This establishes a feedback loop that coordinates differentiation timing across the muscle cell population. Loss of Dll1 in mice depletes muscle progenitors, causes severe muscle hypotrophy, and impairs regeneration (zhang2021feedbackregulationof pages 1-2, zhang2021feedbackregulationof pages 2-4).

### Embryonic Development and Somitogenesis

DLL1 is essential for embryonic viability in mammals. It plays an indispensable role in **somitogenesis**, the segmentation process that generates somites during embryonic development (nian2022evolvingrolesof pages 7-9). DLL1 and DLL3 are expressed in different regions of newly formed somites, and both are required for normal somite formation; critically, Dll3 cDNA cannot replace Dll1, demonstrating DLL1's unique and non-redundant developmental function (nian2022evolvingrolesof pages 7-9). Human DLL1 mutations are associated with neurodevelopmental disorders involving brain abnormalities, underscoring its importance in neural development (nian2022evolvingrolesof pages 7-9).

### Neural Development and Corticogenesis

In the developing nervous system, DLL1 is expressed in an oscillatory pattern in forebrain neural progenitors during mammalian neocortical development (nian2022evolvingrolesof pages 7-9, nian2022evolvingrolesof pages 6-7). This expression supports proper dorsal telencephalic and neocortical development by regulating Notch signaling that maintains neural and cortical progenitor pools and controls the transition from apical radial glial cells to intermediate progenitor cells (nian2022evolvingrolesof pages 6-7). The balance between DLL1 expression and Robo signaling regulates whether progenitors undergo direct or indirect neurogenesis, with this reciprocal DLL1-Robo balance proposed as an important evolutionary factor in amniote brain size and cortical organization (nian2022evolvingrolesof pages 6-7).

### Pancreatic Islet Homeostasis and Glucose Regulation

During pancreatic development, DLL1 regulates the decision between endocrine and exocrine cell fates in multipotent progenitors; loss of Dll1 causes premature endocrine differentiation (rubey2020dll1anddll4mediated pages 1-2). In adult pancreatic islets, DLL1 is specifically expressed in β cells and contributes to maintaining islet homeostasis (rubey2020dll1anddll4mediated pages 1-2, rubey2020dll1anddll4mediated pages 3-3). Combined deletion of DLL1 and DLL4 in adult mouse β cells improves glucose tolerance and increases glucose-stimulated insulin secretion approximately fourfold, though this also produces hyperglucagonemia (rubey2020dll1anddll4mediated pages 1-2, rubey2020dll1anddll4mediated pages 5-6). Conversely, overexpression of the DLL1 intracellular domain in β cells impairs glucose tolerance and reduces insulin secretion both in vitro and in vivo (rubey2020dll1anddll4mediated pages 1-2, rubey2020dll1anddll4mediated pages 9-10). These findings indicate that DLL1-mediated Notch signaling regulates β-cell insulin secretory function and systemic glucose homeostasis, potentially through cAMP-related signaling pathways involving RAPGEF2/EPAC2 (rubey2020dll1anddll4mediated pages 9-10, rubey2020dll1anddll4mediated pages 5-6).

### Immune Cell Development

DLL1-positive stromal microenvironments in the fetal liver support hematopoietic and lymphoid cell differentiation (golub2021thenotchsignaling pages 1-2). DLL1 is broadly expressed in murine fetal liver during embryonic stages E12–E17, and DLL1-positive stromal niches are present there, suggesting roles in fetal immune-cell development (golub2021thenotchsignaling pages 1-2). Studies in mice demonstrate that DLL1 can function as a ligand for both Notch1 and Notch2 in thymic epithelium, supporting T-cell development, although DLL4 is normally the dominant ligand in mammalian thymus (kuintzle2025diversityinnotch pages 6-7).

## Recent Research Developments (2023–2025)

Recent research has expanded understanding of DLL1 in both basic mechanisms and clinical contexts. Key developments include:

**Mechanistic Insights:** A 2025 study systematically characterized the diversity in Notch ligand-receptor signaling interactions, demonstrating that DLL1 activates Notch2 more strongly than Notch1 and that Lunatic Fringe enhances DLL1-mediated activation of both receptors (kuintzle2025diversityinnotch pages 6-7). This work revealed unique interaction profiles for each ligand-receptor combination, highlighting the complexity of Notch pathway regulation.

**Clinical Biomarker Applications:** DLL1 has emerged as a biomarker in infectious diseases and inflammation. Elevated soluble DLL1 (sDLL1) levels are associated with poor outcomes in COVID-19 patients, including higher rates of secondary infections, organ dysfunction, and 90-day mortality (vazquezulloa2022reversibleandbidirectional pages 1-3). Studies in sepsis demonstrate that DLL1 can differentiate sepsis from other infectious and inflammatory diseases, though its diagnostic performance in early emergency department presentation is moderate (you2023targetingthedllnotch pages 1-2).

**Therapeutic Antibody Development:** Novel monoclonal antibodies targeting DLL1 have been developed, including Dl1.72, a human IgG1 antibody that exhibits DLL1 specificity with affinity in the low nanomolar range and significantly impairs DLL1-Notch signaling in estrogen receptor-positive breast cancer cells (you2023targetingthedllnotch pages 1-1). In vivo, Dl1.72 inhibited tumor growth and reduced metastases in xenograft models without apparent toxicity.

**Disease Associations:** Recent studies have implicated DLL1 in diverse pathologies. Upregulated Notch1/4 and JAG-1/DLL-1 expression was detected in allergic rhinitis patients (zhang2021feedbackregulationof pages 2-4), and DLL1 blockade was shown to inhibit atherosclerotic lesion formation and attenuate plaque vulnerability in animal models (zhang2021feedbackregulationof pages 6-8). These findings suggest DLL1 contributes to inflammatory and vascular pathologies beyond its developmental roles.

## Expert Analysis and Current Understanding

The Notch signaling pathway, and DLL1 specifically, presents a complex picture characterized by context-dependent outcomes. As emphasized in recent comprehensive reviews (you2023targetingthedllnotch pages 1-2, katoh2020precisionmedicinefor pages 1-2), Notch signaling can be either oncogenic or tumor-suppressive depending on cancer type, stage, and cellular context. This duality has complicated clinical translation of Notch-targeted therapies. Broad-spectrum γ-secretase inhibitors have shown limited efficacy and significant toxicity in clinical trials, leading experts to advocate for more selective approaches such as ligand-specific antibodies, biomarker-guided patient stratification, and combination therapies (you2023targetingthedllnotch pages 1-1, you2023targetingthedllnotch pages 1-2).

The concept of signaling strength is increasingly recognized as critical to understanding DLL1 function. Different levels of Notch activation—not just on/off states—can lead to distinct developmental outcomes, and disruption of signaling strength contributes to various developmental disorders (kuintzle2025diversityinnotch pages 6-7). The balance between cis-inhibition and trans-activation, modulation by Fringe glycosyltransferases, receptor selectivity, and endocytic trafficking all contribute to determining the effective signaling strength in any given cellular context.

## Summary and Functional Annotation

**Primary Function:** DLL1 functions as a membrane-presented canonical Notch agonist that converts direct cell-cell contact into proteolytic activation of Notch receptors and subsequent NICD-dependent transcriptional regulation. As a signaling molecule, DLL1 does not catalyze biochemical reactions, transport substrates, or serve structural roles; rather, it mediates juxtacrine communication between neighboring cells.

**Substrate Specificity:** In the context of a signaling ligand, DLL1's "substrates" are Notch receptors—primarily Notch1 and Notch2—on adjacent cells. The interaction is modulated by receptor glycosylation state and occurs through the DSL domain and MNNL module of DLL1 binding to EGF-like repeats on Notch.

**Localization:** DLL1 carries out its function at the plasma membrane and adherens junctions, where it engages Notch receptors through cell-cell contact. Dynamic trafficking through endosomal compartments and Rab11-mediated recycling are integral to its signaling activity.

**Pathway Role:** DLL1 operates within the Notch signaling pathway, activating the canonical NICD-RBPJ-MAML transcriptional axis to induce HES and HEY family target genes. Its specific contribution depends on expression pattern, receptor and Fringe combination, cis versus trans configuration, and the broader cellular and developmental context.

**Biological Significance:** DLL1 is essential for embryonic development (somitogenesis, neural development), tissue homeostasis (muscle regeneration, pancreatic β-cell function), and cell-fate decisions across multiple systems. Its dysregulation is implicated in developmental disorders, metabolic dysfunction, cancer, and inflammatory diseases, making it both a fundamental developmental regulator and a potential therapeutic target.
