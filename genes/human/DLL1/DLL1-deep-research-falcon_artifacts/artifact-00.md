| Category | Key functional characteristics of human DLL1 |
|---|---|
| Verified identity | **DLL1 (Delta-like canonical Notch ligand 1; UniProt O00548)** is the human ortholog of Drosophila Delta and a canonical agonistic DSL-family Notch ligand. It is distinct from similarly named **DLK1** and the atypical ligand **DLL3**. (pqac-00000003, pqac-00000028) |
| Protein class and topology | DLL1 is synthesized as a precursor and matures into a **type-I single-pass transmembrane glycoprotein** with a large extracellular ligand-binding region, one transmembrane segment, and a cytoplasmic tail. Intact DLL1 is primarily membrane tethered rather than freely secreted. (pqac-00000001, pqac-00000003, pqac-00000011) |
| Domain architecture | The ectodomain contains an N-terminal **MNNL module**, the conserved **DSL domain**, and multiple cysteine-rich **EGF-like repeats**. The intracellular region is intrinsically disordered and has a C-terminal PDZ-binding motif. (pqac-00000000, pqac-00000001, pqac-00000002) |
| Primary molecular function | DLL1 is a **contact-dependent signaling ligand**, not an enzyme or transporter. DLL1 on a sender cell binds Notch on an adjacent receiver cell and initiates receptor activation; it therefore has no catalytic reaction, biochemical substrate, or transported solute. (pqac-00000003, pqac-00000005) |
| Canonical activation mechanism | Productive trans binding and ligand endocytosis promote a conformational change that exposes the Notch S2 cleavage site. ADAM-family cleavage is followed by γ-secretase cleavage at S3, releasing NICD; NICD enters the nucleus and forms a transcriptional complex with RBPJ/CSL and MAML. (pqac-00000003, pqac-00000008, pqac-00000009) |
| Trans and cis activity | In **trans**, DLL1 activates Notch on an adjacent cell. In **cis**, DLL1 can inhibit Notch in the same cell upstream of NICD production, probably through receptor sequestration or mutual internalization and degradation. This duality helps establish sender–receiver polarity. (pqac-00000042, pqac-00000043, pqac-00000044, pqac-00000046) |
| Receptor interactions | DLL1 functionally activates **NOTCH1 and NOTCH2**. Quantitative cell assays found stronger overall DLL1 signaling through NOTCH2, while Lunatic Fringe strongly enhanced DLL1–NOTCH1 signaling. Receptor-specific affinity evidence is less complete for NOTCH3 and NOTCH4. (pqac-00000027, pqac-00000028) |
| Glycosylation-dependent modulation | Fringe-mediated glycosylation of Notch alters ligand–receptor output. Lunatic Fringe strengthens DLL1-mediated trans activation of NOTCH1 and NOTCH2, showing that receptor glycan state helps determine signaling strength and specificity. (pqac-00000027) |
| Principal subcellular localization | Full-length DLL1 acts at the **plasma membrane**, including adherens-junction-associated regions. MAGI proteins, MPDZ/MUPP1, syntenin, and SYNJBP2/ARIP2 can stabilize or concentrate DLL1 at the membrane through interactions involving its cytoplasmic tail. (pqac-00000013, pqac-00000015) |
| Trafficking and turnover | Ubiquitination and Epsin-dependent endocytosis contribute to signaling competence. Rab11-mediated recycling, polarized transcytosis, and Sec15-associated delivery regulate where DLL1 is presented, while lysosomal degradation limits membrane availability. (pqac-00000029, pqac-00000030, pqac-00000031) |
| Proteolytic processing | ADAM-family proteases can shed the DLL1 ectodomain, leaving a membrane-bound C-terminal fragment that may undergo γ-secretase cleavage. Soluble extracellular and intracellular fragments can therefore occur, but intact surface DLL1 is the best-established canonical signaling form. (pqac-00000011, pqac-00000013, pqac-00000014, pqac-00000016) |
| Downstream transcriptional output | The NICD–RBPJ–MAML complex activates context-dependent Notch targets, prominently members of the **HES and HEY families**. Human myoblast experiments directly demonstrated DLL1-dependent regulation of HES1, HEY1, and HEYL. (pqac-00000034, pqac-00000039) |
| Skeletal-muscle biology | MyoD induces DLL1 in differentiating myoblasts. DLL1 activates Notch in neighboring cells to preserve progenitor states and delay differentiation, while cis inhibition in DLL1-expressing cells permits their differentiation. Loss of DLL1 reduces Pax7, increases differentiation markers, and impairs progenitor maintenance and regeneration in model systems. (pqac-00000019, pqac-00000020, pqac-00000023, pqac-00000024) |
| Embryonic and neural development | DLL1 is required for embryonic viability and normal **somitogenesis**. Oscillatory expression in neural progenitors regulates Notch-dependent progenitor maintenance, neurogenesis, dorsal telencephalic development, and corticogenesis; human DLL1 variants are associated with neurodevelopmental abnormalities. (pqac-00000057, pqac-00000058) |
| Pancreatic expression and homeostasis | DLL1 regulates endocrine-versus-exocrine fate during pancreatic development and is expressed in adult β cells. Mouse perturbation studies implicate DLL1 and DLL4 in glucose-stimulated insulin secretion, glucagon balance, and systemic glucose tolerance. Combined deletion increased stimulated insulin release by approximately fourfold in one experiment, although DLL1-specific and DLL4-specific contributions were not fully separable. (pqac-00000049, pqac-00000050, pqac-00000055) |
| Immune and stromal niches | DLL1-positive stromal microenvironments can support fetal hematopoietic and peripheral lymphoid differentiation. In mice, Dll1 can signal through Notch1 or Notch2 sufficiently to support thymic T-cell development, although DLL4 is normally the dominant mammalian thymic ligand. (pqac-00000026, pqac-00000027) |
| Representative expression contexts | Experimentally supported contexts include differentiating skeletal myoblasts, neural progenitors, pancreatic β cells, and fetal-liver and splenic stromal cells. Expression is dynamic and cell-state dependent rather than uniformly distributed across tissues. (pqac-00000023, pqac-00000026, pqac-00000049) |
| Cancer relevance | DLL1-driven Notch signaling can influence proliferation, survival, differentiation, stem-like states, and tumor–stroma communication. Its net effect is highly context dependent because Notch can be oncogenic or tumor suppressive according to cancer type, cell state, receptor, and signaling strength. (pqac-00000005, pqac-00000028) |
| Therapeutic relevance | DLL/Notch inhibitors include γ-secretase inhibitors and ligand- or receptor-directed antibodies, but broad pathway inhibition has generally shown a narrow therapeutic window, on-target toxicity, or modest efficacy. Experts therefore favor more selective, biomarker-guided approaches over indiscriminate Notch blockade. (pqac-00000005, pqac-00000009) |
| Functional-annotation conclusion | DLL1’s best-supported primary role is a **membrane-presented canonical Notch agonist** that converts direct cell contact into receptor proteolysis and NICD-dependent transcription. Functional specificity arises from expression pattern, receptor and Fringe combination, cis–trans configuration, trafficking, and signaling strength. (pqac-00000003, pqac-00000027, pqac-00000030, pqac-00000042) |


*Table: This table consolidates the verified identity, molecular mechanism, localization, receptor interactions, biological roles, expression contexts, and translational relevance of human DLL1. It distinguishes well-established canonical functions from context-dependent findings.*
