# EGR2 review notes

EGR2 has two supported molecular mechanisms: sequence-specific transcriptional activation and SUMO E3 activity toward NAB coregulators. The direct human promoter and nuclear-extract evidence in PMID:12687019 and the defined mouse reconstitution in PMID:21836637 support different activities. The latter used mouse Krox20, E1, UBC9 and NAB2 with human SUMO1; it does not establish purified human EGR2 activity or equivalent function of the two natural human products. The Long and Short products are 476 and 426 residues, respectively; Short lacks Long residues 1–50.

The HCFC1 study, PMID:14532282, explicitly used murine Krox20. Its positive cellular recruitment and motif-control experiments support coactivator binding. The authors' unsuccessful stable-binding attempts should not be converted into a positive purified-pair assay. HCFC1 is a transcription coactivator, whereas the independently supported DNA-binding transcription-factor partner in the mouse donor record is KLF4, from PMID:18396140. WWP2 binding in PMID:19651900 is a positive interaction regulating EGR2 degradation, not evidence that EGR2 acquires WWP2's ubiquitin-ligase activity.

PMID:17938205 reports a positive SFN-dependent cytoplasmic EGR2 pool. Its Figure 5 redistribution experiment makes EGR2 the relocated protein; it does not establish EGR2 as the machinery that exports proteins. The cytoplasmic location and the proposed rejection of the export-process role therefore concern different claims. The current mouse donor annotation is preserved and explicitly acknowledged.

The complete authentic abstract and publisher preview of PMID:17717711 were available, but the operative functional-assay body was not recovered. Retaining clearly correct DNA-binding/activation annotations does not require inventing the unavailable variant-specific results. The exact EGR2 supplement row in PMID:28473536 was also not recovered. The separately assessed human EGR2 promoter evidence supports the general DNA-binding function without establishing a specific methylation preference.
