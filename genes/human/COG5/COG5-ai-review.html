Gene Description

COG5 is a noncatalytic subunit of the conserved oligomeric Golgi complex. It helps assemble and stabilize the complex's lobe B, cooperating with COG7 to incorporate COG6. Cytosolic and peripheral Golgi-associated COG5 pools support the organization of trafficking machinery that recycles Golgi components and maintains Golgi architecture. Disruption of this machinery alters glycosylation indirectly by disturbing the distribution and stability of Golgi proteins. Biallelic COG5 variants cause a congenital disorder of glycosylation with variable neurological and systemic manifestations.

The single scaffold core reflects COG5-specific assembly evidence, rather than transfer of COG4's recruitment mechanism. COG5 cooperates with COG7 to build a COG6-containing subcomplex. Clinical and later mechanistic context

The first COG5-CDG abstract explicitly reports a patient fibroblast trafficking defect and wild-type cDNA rescue. Later full patient studies show loss of COG5/COG7 abundance and delayed GalT-GFP redistribution. These findings support transport and Golgi organization, without manufacturing neurological-development or glycosyltransferase activities. The 2026 study provides positive cellular COG5–ATP7A association, protein-turnover and rescue data.
