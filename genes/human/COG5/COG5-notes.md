# COG5 review notes

## Molecular role and scope

The single scaffold core reflects COG5-specific assembly evidence, rather than transfer of COG4's recruitment mechanism. COG5 cooperates with COG7 to build a COG6-containing subcomplex. The original experiments use human cDNAs in rabbit reticulocyte lysate and explicitly qualify binary directness pending purified-protein tests. The COG7-source binding assertion is refined to this structural activity; other positive pairs remain distinct. No NEW annotation is added. [PMID:15047703]

The historical GTC-90 experiments use bovine complex and CHO donor/acceptor Golgi, with rat microscopy. Antibody inhibition supports complex function but does not isolate COG5 tethering or fusion. The human cDNA work is a separate experimental component. The subsequent complex-characterization paper primarily perturbs COG1/COG2 and images COG1/COG7; these assays must not be reassigned to COG5. [PMID:9792665; PMID:11980916]

In the human knockout panel, COG5 loss destabilizes COG7 and reduces COG6. The Golgi retains dilated mini-stacks and is less severely affected than several other COG knockouts. The integrated toxin-transport readout is interpreted with its compartment and cell-panel limits. COG5 supplies assembly activity to trafficking machinery, rather than catalyzing the glycan reactions whose distribution changes. [PMID:27066481]

## Clinical and later mechanistic context

The first COG5-CDG abstract explicitly reports a patient fibroblast trafficking defect and wild-type cDNA rescue. Later full patient studies show loss of COG5/COG7 abundance and delayed GalT-GFP redistribution. These findings support transport and Golgi organization, without manufacturing neurological-development or glycosyltransferase activities. The human variant study distinguishes Golgi/stress assays from separate mouse retinal PERK experiments. [PMID:19690088; PMID:23228021; PMID:33277529]

The 2026 study provides positive cellular COG5–ATP7A association, protein-turnover and rescue data. It proposes an ATP7A recycling/localization mechanism; that trafficking step is not directly resolved. Copper accumulation and respiratory phenotypes therefore remain mechanistic context and a question for separation-of-function experiments, not a new COG5 copper-transporter or OXPHOS annotation. Its patient-cell and variant interpretation limits are retained. [PMID:41824529]
