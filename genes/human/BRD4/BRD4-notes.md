## Mechanistic synthesis

BRD4 recognizes acetylated chromatin and selected acetylated transcription factors, and recruits transcriptional regulators. The long product recruits P-TEFb; CDK9 supplies the kinase activity attributed to P-TEFb in these experiments. The ET-domain route recruits additional partners and can support transcription independently of P-TEFb. Chromatin reading and transcription-factor coactivation are represented as two cooperating core units. A single histone-reader core captures the coupled multi-mark mechanism; the specific accepted source annotations retain mark-level detail without duplicating one mechanism as a parent and six child cores.

The paper on late-mitotic P-TEFb recruitment supports transcriptional regulation and elongation, while antibody perturbation in HeLa cells supports contextual G2/M regulation. The latter is retained as non-core, without assigning BRD4 a cell-cycle kinase activity. The chromatin-remodeling IBA is retained as a noncatalytic recruitment/organization contribution: BRD4 associates with remodeling partners and the studied isoform B recruits condensin II. Neither observation makes BRD4 an ATPase. The source's mouse Th17 ISS donor is preserved, but its exact lineage experiment remains unresolved.

Primary sources: [late-mitotic recruitment](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2223388/), [ET-domain effectors](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3133372/), [chromatin insulation](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3683358/), [anti-pause enhancers](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3886918/).

## Regulatory chromatin versus direct DNA recognition

The human CD4+ T-cell source uses ChIP-seq to identify BRD4 occupancy at promoters and enhancers, relating occupancy to acetylated histones and Pol II transcription. The exact GO definition of transcription cis-regulatory region binding requires DNA-sequence binding. The source establishes chromatin association rather than direct sequence recognition by BRD4, so that source row is refined to chromatin binding, which is already supported independently. This does not assert that BRD4 can never contact DNA. The p53 study assigns sequence recognition to p53 and distinguishes it from regulated BRD4-p53 interaction.

Sources: [human T-cell occupancy](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3522308/), [GO:0000976](https://amigo.geneontology.org/amigo/term/GO:0000976), [phospho-switch study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23317504/). The official local GO mirror used for definitions is dated 2026-03-25; successful live AmiGO confirmation was obtained for NF-kappaB binding, not for every term.

## Intrinsic catalysis remains unresolved

The 2012 BRD4 kinase report contains substantive positive biochemical evidence, including recombinant/in-gel and bacterial-expression controls. The 2021 follow-up adds positive mouse-protein assays; it shares investigators with the earlier work and is not independent-laboratory replication. An independent 2014 study found no phosphorylation using human BRD4 residues 1–698 expressed in E. coli or Sf21, while allowing preparation or activation requirements. Thus the source kinase IBA and CTD-kinase IMP remain UNDECIDED. The disputed catalysis is not dismissed merely because BRD4 lacks a conventional kinase domain or recruits CDK9. Physical CTD binding remains supported separately. The PAINT node and donor experiments were not reconstructed, and a self-source is not labeled circular.

The reported 2016 HAT activity also has substantive human/bacterial/in-gel controls. It is recorded as an unresolved question rather than a new HAT annotation or settled catalytic core. Recruitment or activation of p300, and BRD4-NUT fusion effects, must not be confused with intrinsic native BRD4 catalysis. A 2024 GEO update corrects sample/data associations; this is not a retraction or automatic biochemical refutation.
