Gene Description

BRD4 is a nuclear BET-family chromatin reader and transcriptional coregulator. Its tandem bromodomains recognize acetylated histones and selected acetylated transcription factors, while its extraterminal region recruits regulatory partners. The long product links regulatory chromatin and transcription factors to P-TEFb, promoting RNA polymerase II elongation, and BRD4 can also recruit effectors through a P-TEFb-independent pathway. These interactions support stimulus-responsive gene expression and the maintenance of transcriptional programs across cell division. Alternative products differ in their C-terminal regions; the studied isoform B recruits condensin II and restricts DNA-damage signaling through chromatin compaction. BRD4 also binds papillomavirus E2 proteins, and BRD4-NUTM1 fusions have oncogenic activities distinct from those of the native protein.

Mechanistic synthesis

BRD4 recognizes acetylated chromatin and selected acetylated transcription factors, and recruits transcriptional regulators. The long product recruits P-TEFb; CDK9 supplies the kinase activity attributed to P-TEFb in these experiments. The ET-domain route recruits additional partners and can support transcription independently of P-TEFb. Chromatin reading and transcription-factor coactivation are represented as two cooperating core units. A single histone-reader core captures the coupled multi-mark mechanism; the specific accepted source annotations retain mark-level detail without duplicating one mechanism as a parent and six child cores.

The paper on late-mitotic P-TEFb recruitment supports transcriptional regulation and elongation, while antibody perturbation in HeLa cells supports contextual G2/M regulation.
