# ABHD14B — review notes

Family-coordinated review of human `ABHD14B` (`UniProtKB:Q96IU4`, `PANTHER:PTHR46197:SF2`), alongside paralog `ABHD14A` (`Q9BUJ0`, `PTHR46197:SF1`), rodent orthologs (`Abhd14b`, `Abhd14a`), *C. elegans* `abhd-14` (`Q9N366`, `PTHR46197:SF3`), and the `PANTHER:PTHR46197` family review.

## 1. Biochemical mechanism: CoA-dependent protein-lysine deacetylase (KDAC), not hydrolytic esterase or KAT

Human ABHD14B (210 aa; formerly CCG1/TAF(II)250-interacting factor B, CIB) adopts a compact canonical alpha/beta-hydrolase fold resolved at 2.2 Å (PDB: `1IMJ`) that lacks the canonical lid/cap excursion domain typical of lipid-acting alpha/beta-hydrolases and carries a classical Ser111-Asp162-His188 charge-relay triad within a non-canonical `SxSxS` (`SPSLS`, residues 109–113) nucleophile elbow [PMID:14672934, "CIB possesses a conserved catalytic triad that is found in other alpha/beta-hydrolases, and our in vitro studies confirmed that it bears hydrolase activity. However, CIB differs from other alpha/beta-hydrolases in that it lacks a binding site excursion, which facilitates the substrate selectivity of the other alpha/beta-hydrolases."].

Rajendran et al. 2020 (`PMID:31478652`) de-orphanized ABHD14B as the founding member of a third mechanistic class of mammalian protein-lysine deacetylases (KDACs), distinct from Zn2+-dependent hydrolytic HDACs (`RHEA:58108`) and NAD+-dependent sirtuins (`GO:0034979`):
- Rather than using water to hydrolyze acetyl-lysine to free acetate, ABHD14B uses a ping-pong acyl-transfer mechanism in which Ser111 attacks the N6-acetyl-L-lysyl residue to form a covalent acetyl-enzyme intermediate and transfers the acetyl group onto coenzyme A (CoA) to generate acetyl-CoA (`RHEA:45950`) [PMID:31478652, "In this paper, we annotate ABHD14B as a lysine deacetylase (KDAC), showing this enzyme's ability to transfer an acetyl group from a post-translationally acetylated lysine to coenzyme A (CoA), to yield acetyl-CoA, while regenerating the free amine of protein lysine residues."].
- Mutating the catalytic serine (`S111A`) abolishes fluorophosphonate-rhodamine (FP-Rh) labeling and acetyl-CoA generation from acetylated lysine peptides and calf thymus histones [PMID:31478652, "We found that WT human ABHD14B, but not S111A human ABHD14B, was indeed able to make acetyl-CoA from this acetylated peptide (Figure 7A)."].
- Crucially, ABHD14B does **not** turn over acetyl-CoA in the reverse (protein-lysine acetyltransferase / KAT) direction, and knocking down `ABHD14B` in HEK293T cells elevates cellular protein lysine acetylation while lowering cellular acetyl-CoA [PMID:31478652, "Third, we show that genetically knocking down ABHD14B in human HEK293T cells, results in increased levels of protein lysine acetylation, and decreased cellular concentrations of acetyl-CoA, thereby confirming in cell physiological settings, that ABHD14B indeed functions as a novel lysine deacetylase (Figure 6)."].

### Ontology gap and UniProt in vivo caution for CoA-dependent KDAC chemistry
In QuickGO / current Gene Ontology:
- `GO:0034979` (`NAD-dependent protein lysine deacetylase activity`, sirtuin branch) is **not** `is_a GO:0033558` (`protein lysine deacetylase activity`). Instead, `GO:0034979 is_a GO:0141218` (`NAD-dependent protein lysine deacylase activity`), which has dual `is_a` parents **`GO:0160215` (`deacylase activity`)** and **`GO:0016747` (`acyltransferase activity, transferring groups other than amino-acyl groups`)**.
- Meanwhile, `GO:0033558` (`protein lysine deacetylase activity`, *"Catalysis of the reaction: Removal of an acetyl group from a lysine residue in a protein"*) `is_a GO:0019213` (`deacetylase activity`, *"Catalysis of the hydrolysis of an acetyl group from a substrate molecule"*, `RHEA:58108`), which `is_a GO:0160215` (`deacylase activity`) -> `GO:0140096` (`catalytic activity, acting on a protein`). Neither `GO:0033558` nor `GO:0160215` sits under `GO:0016787` (`hydrolase activity`), though `GO:0019213`'s definition restricts deacetylase activity to hydrolysis.
- `GO:0016407` (`acetyltransferase activity`, *"Catalysis of the transfer of an acetyl group to an acceptor molecule"*) captures the EC `2.3.1.-` acyl-transfer chemistry onto CoA (`RHEA:45950`) without implying water hydrolysis (`GO:0019213`), while losing the protein-lysine deacylase/KDAC grouping.
- Therefore, we propose `GO:0016407` (`acetyltransferase activity`) as a conservative `NEW` annotation (matching UniProt's EC `2.3.1.-` assignment), and in `proposed_new_terms` set `proposed_parent` to `GO:0160215` (`deacylase activity`) while explicitly requesting GOC **dual-parent** **`CoA-dependent protein-lysine deacetylase activity`** (`RHEA:45950`) under BOTH `GO:0160215` (`deacylase activity` — or `GO:0033558` if `GO:0019213` is generalized beyond hydrolysis) AND `GO:0016407` (`acetyltransferase activity`), directly mirroring GO's sirtuin precedent (`GO:0141218` -> `GO:0160215` + `GO:0016747`).
- **UniProtKB `Putative` / `in vivo` caution (`knowledge_gaps`)**: `Q96IU4` (`ABHD14B-uniprot.txt`, curated before `PMID:35700823` and `PMID:40658808`) retains `RecName: Full=Putative protein-lysine deacylase ABHD14B {ECO:0000305}`, notes `"Additional experiments are however required to confirm the protein-lysine deacetylase activity in vivo (Probable)"`, and carries a `CAUTION` noting that non-hydrolytic CoA-dependent lysine deacetylation by a serine-hydrolase-fold protein requires in vivo confirmation. Cellular perturbation studies (`PMID:31478652`, `PMID:35700823`, `PMID:40658808`) support cellular/chromatin KDAC function, while the identity and stoichiometry of direct endogenous human protein-lysine substrates in vivo remain open (`MF_DARK`, `BIOLOGY`, `status: NARROWING`).

## 2. Two severe upstream pipeline defects in GOA (`REMOVE` + `source_defects`)

1. **Inverted RHEA2GO directionality (`GO:0061733` `protein-lysine-acetyltransferase activity`, `IEA` `GO_REF:0000116`, `RHEA:45948`)**:
   - UniProt curates `CC -!- CATALYTIC ACTIVITY` using undirected master reaction `RHEA:45948` (`L-lysyl-[protein] + acetyl-CoA = N(6)-acetyl-L-lysyl-[protein] + CoA + H(+)`) paired with explicit `PhysiologicalDirection=right-to-left; Xref=Rhea:RHEA:45950` (deacetylation, `PMID:31478652`).
   - Because RHEA's undirected `RHEA:45948` maps to `GO:0061733` (`protein-lysine-acetyltransferase activity`, EC 2.3.1.48, KAT/HAT direction) without respecting `PhysiologicalDirection=right-to-left`, the `GO_REF:0000116` IEA pipeline (and downstream UniProt `ISS` transfers to mouse `Q8VCR7` and rat `Q6DGG1`) assigns ABHD14B the exact opposite enzymatic direction (`KAT` instead of `KDAC`), directly contradicted by `PMID:31478652`. Action: `REMOVE` with `source_defects` (`WRONG_TERM`, `resource: UNIPROT` / RHEA2GO pipeline).

2. **Surrogate-substrate Reactome pathway artifact (`GO:0050427` `3'-phosphoadenosine 5'-phosphosulfate metabolic process`, `TAS` `Reactome:R-HSA-156584`)**:
   - Reactome reaction `R-HSA-5695964` (*ABHD14B hydrolyses PNPB*) curates Padmanabhan et al. 2004's artificial chromogenic esterase assay (`p-nitrophenyl butyrate -> p-nitrophenol + butyrate`). Because *p*-nitrophenol (PNP) is also a classic xenobiotic test substrate for cytosolic SULT1A sulfotransferases that consume PAPS in pathway `R-HSA-156584` (*Cytosolic sulfonation of small molecules*), Reactome placed `R-HSA-5695964` inside `R-HSA-156584`, causing GO export to emit `GO:0050427` (`3'-phosphoadenosine 5'-phosphosulfate metabolic process`) for `ABHD14B`! ABHD14B neither binds nor metabolizes PAPS or sulfonates small molecules. Action: `REMOVE` with `source_defects` (`WRONG_TERM`, `resource: REACTOME`).

3. **Surrogate *p*-nitrophenyl esterase annotations (`GO:0016787` `hydrolase activity`, `IDA` `PMID:14672934`, `TAS` `Reactome:R-HSA-5695964`, `IBA` `GO_REF:0000033` from `PANTHER:PTN002920773`)**:
   - Following `projects/ENZYME_SPECIFICITY.md`, slow in vitro cleavage of activated synthetic *p*-nitrophenyl esters (`kcat = 2.2 min^-1` for pNp-acetate, `0.6 min^-1` for pNp-butyrate) reflects nucleophilic attack by Ser111 with slow hydrolytic deacylation in the absence of CoA; CoA accelerates turnover 4.5-fold via acetyl-transfer and acetyl-lysine peptides yield ~3.5-fold more acetyl-CoA than equimolar pNp-acetate [PMID:31478652]. Thus `GO:0016787` represents real in vitro surrogate esterase activity rather than physiological hydrolytic function: `MARK_AS_OVER_ANNOTATED` across all three rows (and `propagation_review` on the `PTN002920773` IBA).

## 3. Biological roles, localization, and paralog boundary (`ABHD14B` vs `ABHD14A`)

- **Metabolic regulation (`PMID:35700823`)**: Transcriptomic and metabolomic profiling of `ABHD14B`-depleted HEK293T cells demonstrated broad transcriptional dysregulation of glycolytic and TCA cycle enzymes and an ~85–95% drop in intracellular pyruvate alongside 40–60% reductions in 2-ketoglutarate, succinyl-CoA, fumarate, and malate. Liver-specific `Abhd14b` knockdown in mice disrupts systemic glucose homeostasis and exacerbates hepatic ATP depletion during fasting.
- **Transcriptional / chromatin roles (`PMID:14672934`, `PMID:40658808`)**: Originally isolated as a TAF1 (`UniProtKB:P21675`) interactor (`GO:0005515` `IPI` -> `MODIFY` to `GO:0140296` `general transcription initiation factor binding`) that activated reporter transcription (`GO:0045944`, `KEEP_AS_NON_CORE` on `IDA` and `IBA` because transcriptional activation in reporter assays vs transcriptional repression at viral minichromosomes is context-dependent). In HBV-infected hepatocytes (`PMID:40658808`), host ABHD14B is recruited via TFII-I (`GTF2I`) to HBV cccDNA minichromosomes, reduces histone H3K27ac/acetylation on cccDNA, and suppresses HBV RNA transcription (`NEW` `GO:0032897` `negative regulation of viral transcription`).
- **Subcellular localization**: Endogenous ABHD14B is a soluble protein distributed across both `cytosol` (`GO:0005829`, `ACCEPT`) and `nucleus` / `nucleoplasm` (`GO:0005634` / `GO:0005654`, `ACCEPT`), with broader `cytoplasm` (`GO:0005737`) marked `TOO_GENERAL` (entailed by accepted `GO:0005829`), HPA `nucleolus` (`GO:0005730`) `KEEP_AS_NON_CORE`, and urinary/vesicle proteomics `extracellular exosome` (`GO:0070062`, `HDA`) `KEEP_AS_NON_CORE`.
- **Paralog boundary (`PMID:37974539`, `PMID:42469586`)**: Unlike soluble cytosolic/nuclear `ABHD14B` (`SF2`), vertebrate `ABHD14A` (`SF1`) carries an N-terminal type II transmembrane signal-anchor and lumenal N-glycosylation sites, localizing to the Golgi apparatus upon heterologous expression (`PMID:42469586`), and lacks established protein-lysine deacetylase physiological substrates (`PMID:37974539`).
