## Identity and sequence versions

Current [UniProt Q9P0K9](https://www.uniprot.org/uniprotkb/Q9P0K9/entry) and HGNC:1362 identify human FRRS1L, formerly C9orf4/CG-6. The current reviewed reference is 293 residues and exactly matches the human leaf in the retained PTHR46902 tree/alignment. The native record declares no alternative products. Names suggesting a reductase do not establish an enzymatic activity.

The official [UniSave history](https://rest.uniprot.org/unisave/Q9P0K9?format=json), entry 100 (2017; sequence version 2) and entry 128 (2022; sequence version 3), was compared with [NM_014334.2](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/nuccore/NM_014334.2), [NM_014334.3](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/nuccore/NM_014334.3) and [NM_014334.4](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/nuccore/NM_014334.4). The older NP_055149.2 sequence has 344 residues; the current NP_055149.3 sequence has 293. Old positions 52–344 match current positions 1–293 exactly. Historical K155/V195/W282/S317/Q321 therefore map to current K104/V144/W231/S266/Q270. Published labels remain tied to their original numbering. This is a reference-sequence revision, not evidence of a natural alternative product. The unversioned NM_014334 construct reported in PMID:28675162 was not resequenced here.

Postsynapse and broad synapse remain unresolved because the relevant later localization experiments were not accessible. Contextual plasma-membrane evidence is retained separately from the ER-centered core.

## Review clarification: localization and receptor association

For the two GO:0045202 synapse rows, the human IEA from GO_REF:0000044 and the human ISS from GO_REF:0000024 are different evidence routes. ISS is a curator-mediated sequence transfer, even though the current same-term mouse B1AXV0 row is itself electronic. Both remain UNDECIDED. The exact original/native indices are defined by `index_scheme` and the complete joins in the public [evidence map](FRRS1L-source-evidence.yaml).

The negative result in PMID:28675162 must be stated explicitly: its Results section “FRRS1l-containing AMPARs in brain neurons localize to the ER” and Figure 5 report 815/848 immunoparticles at ER membranes across 29 sampled rat neurons, with 33 at or near plasma membrane and no observed staining in the sampled synaptic compartment. The described distribution is mainly somatic/proximal-dendritic. Its immunohistochemistry Methods specify 3–4-week-old Wistar rats; electron-microscopy Methods separately name two Wistar rats without giving age in that sentence. These observations strongly favor ER localization in the inspected preparations. PMID:29276473 Figure 1F, neuronal immunocytochemistry Methods and Discussion report minor rather than absent PSD-95 overlap for expressed mouse HA-FRRS1L. Neither assay is a human endogenous localization experiment. The broad located_in annotation is nonexclusive and does not require dominant or constitutive residence; conversely, MARK_AS_OVER_ANNOTATED does not require proof of universal absence. The sampled negative and distinct partial-positive contexts leave the transferred scope unresolved. The still-uninspected PMID:31604597 donor experiment consequently matters to both postsynapse and its broader synapse location. The positive plasma-membrane observations are retained in their specific experimental contexts and do not imply that lack of signal in one preparation settles every other compartment.

The GO:0044877→GO:0035255 MODIFY names the identified receptor partner rather than asserting that the replacement is an is_a child of complex binding. The current UniProt SUBUNIT statement is by similarity to B1AXV0, including its direct-binding language; it is not a new human binary-binding experiment. Its separate FUNCTION statement has experimental provenance to PMID:27236917. That original was inspected externally at the selected Figure 4/Methods/Results scope recorded above, while its normal cache remains abstract-only. An optional in_complex addition of GO:0032281 is outside this focused amendment. The existing core already describes nascent ER receptor association; this does not imply that GO:0032281 excludes transient assemblies.
