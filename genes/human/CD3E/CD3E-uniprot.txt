CC   -!- FUNCTION: Part of the TCR-CD3 complex present on T-lymphocyte cell
CC       surface that plays an essential role in adaptive immune response
CC       (PubMed:15294938, PubMed:15546002, PubMed:2470098, PubMed:40592325,
CC       PubMed:8490660). When antigen presenting cells (APCs) activate T-cell
CC       receptor (TCR), TCR-mediated signals are transmitted across the cell
CC       membrane by the CD3 chains CD3D, CD3E, CD3G and CD247/CD3Z
CC       (PubMed:2470098, PubMed:40592325). All CD3 chains contain
CC       immunoreceptor tyrosine-based activation motifs (ITAMs) in their
CC       cytoplasmic domain (PubMed:2470098, PubMed:40592325). Upon TCR
CC       engagement, these motifs become phosphorylated by Src family protein
CC       tyrosine kinases LCK and FYN, resulting in the activation of downstream
CC       signaling pathways (PubMed:2470098, PubMed:40592325). CD3E ITAM
CC       phosphorylation creates docking sites for the protein kinase ZAP70
CC       leading to ZAP70 phosphorylation and its conversion into a
CC       catalytically active enzyme (By similarity). In addition of this role
CC       of signal transduction in T-cell activation, CD3E plays an essential
CC       role in correct T-cell development (By similarity). Also participates
CC       in internalization and cell surface down-regulation of TCR-CD3
CC       complexes via endocytosis sequences present in CD3E cytosolic region
CC       (PubMed:10384095, PubMed:26507128). In addition to its role as a TCR
CC       coreceptor, it serves as a receptor for ITPRIPL1 (PubMed:38614099).
CC       Ligand recognition inhibits T-cell activation by promoting interaction
CC       with NCK1, which prevents CD3E-ZAP70 interaction and blocks the ERK-
CC       NFkB signaling cascade and calcium influx (PubMed:12110186,
CC       PubMed:38614099). {ECO:0000250|UniProtKB:P22646,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:10384095, ECO:0000269|PubMed:12110186,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:15294938, ECO:0000269|PubMed:15546002,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:2470098, ECO:0000269|PubMed:26507128,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:38614099, ECO:0000269|PubMed:40592325,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:8490660}.
CC   -!- SUBUNIT: The TCR-CD3 complex is composed of a CD3D-CD3E and a CD3G-CD3E
CC       heterodimers that preferentially associate with TCRalpha and TCRbeta,
CC       respectively, to form TCRalpha-CD3E-CD3G and TCRbeta/CD3G-CD3E trimers
CC       (PubMed:15136729, PubMed:15534202, PubMed:40592325). In turn, the
CC       hexamer interacts with CD247/CD3Z homodimer to form the TCR-CD3 complex
CC       (PubMed:15136729, PubMed:15534202). Alternatively, TCRalpha and TCRbeta
CC       can be replaced by TCRgamma and TCRdelta. Interacts with CD6
CC       (PubMed:15294938). Interacts (via Proline-rich sequence) with NCK1; the
CC       interaction is ligand dependent but independent of tyrosine kinase
CC       activation (PubMed:12110186, PubMed:15972658, PubMed:38614099).
CC       Interacts with NUMB; this interaction is important for TCR-CD3
CC       internalization and subsequent degradation (PubMed:26507128). Interacts
CC       (when tyrosine phosphorylated) with LAG3; disrupting the association
CC       between CD3E and LCK and preventing TCR activation (PubMed:15534202).
CC       {ECO:0000269|PubMed:12110186, ECO:0000269|PubMed:15136729,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:15294938, ECO:0000269|PubMed:15534202,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:15972658, ECO:0000269|PubMed:26507128,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:38614099, ECO:0000269|PubMed:40592325}.

CC   -!- DISEASE: Immunodeficiency 18 (IMD18) [MIM:615615]: An autosomal
CC       recessive primary immunodeficiency characterized by onset in infancy or
CC       early childhood of recurrent infections. The severity is variable,
CC       encompassing both a mild immunodeficiency and severe combined
CC       immunodeficiency (SCID), resulting in early death without bone marrow
CC       transplantation in some patients. Immunologic work-up of the IMD18 SCID
CC       patients shows a T cell-negative, B cell-positive, natural killer (NK)
CC       cell-positive phenotype, whereas T-cell development is not impaired in
CC       the mild form of IMD18. {ECO:0000269|PubMed:15546002,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:8490660}. Note=The disease is caused by variants
CC       affecting the gene represented in this entry.
