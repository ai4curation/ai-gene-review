# CRPPA: nucleotide-donor synthesis and dystroglycan glycosylation

## 2026-10-13

CRPPA (previously ISPD) synthesizes CDP-ribitol from CTP and ribitol phosphate. FKTN and FKRP subsequently transfer ribitol phosphate to the alpha-dystroglycan glycan. CRPPA therefore performs an upstream chemical step in glycan biosynthesis; it does not itself transfer the ribitol phosphate onto the protein. CDP-ribitol supplementation can rescue glycosylation in deficient cells [PMID:26923585 "CDP-Rbo to ISPD-deficient cells restored α-DG glycosylation."]. The [original human-enzyme study](https://da.lib.kobe-u.ac.jp/da/kernel/90003339/90003339.pdf) identifies the product by chromatography and mass spectrometry, with substrate controls and matched mutant protein amounts.

The principal physiological synthesis is represented by D-ribitol-5-phosphate cytidylyltransferase activity, protein O-linked glycosylation via mannose, and cytosol. The process definition includes elongation of the mannose-initiated glycan. This is donor synthesis rather than a claim that CRPPA catalyzes the first mannose-transfer reaction. Human patient fibroblasts show wild-type complementation of functional glycosylation [PMID:22522420 "restored functional glycosylation"]. The early POMT-activity interpretation in that study is qualified by later experiments showing retained POMT activity in other deficient-cell contexts. The positive patient-cell rescue is preserved. The [2012 original](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3371168/) includes the actual constructs and POMT assay Methods.

Human CRPPA also acts on ribose and ribulose phosphates in vitro [PMID:26687144 "activity toward pentose phosphates"]. Accordingly, the broader existing cytidylyltransferase assertions retain measured substrate breadth. The [human biochemical and structural original](https://hdl.handle.net/2066/152310) uses full-length 1–451 protein expressed in bacteria and a distinct 43–451 construct produced in insect cells. It reports low basal MEP turnover; this is not an absence-of-catalysis claim. Two HAP1 knockout clones nevertheless retained the measured dolichol species and levels while losing functional dystroglycan glycosylation. Together with the identified nucleotide-sugar products, these results distinguish CRPPA from the bacterial isoprenoid-pathway role assigned through the conserved IspD site.

Constructs differ across studies. The human 43–451 protein in [PMID:27130732](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4924997/) was expressed in HEK293F with an N-terminal His8/TEV tag. The purified enzyme in [PMID:27194101](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4873967/) was mouse Ispd expressed in Sf9 cells; human knockout/rescue cells and rat-muscle metabolites are separate experiments. Bacterial TarI complementation supports the importance of the nucleotide donor rather than an obligatory additional mammalian activity. Differences in low-level alternative-substrate turnover remain dependent on species and assay conditions.

The human crystal construct forms a homodimer; the same study reports consistent solution gel filtration without displaying those data. This supports the existing homodimerization assertion but does not establish endogenous cellular stoichiometry. Tagged full-length human protein partitions to the cytosolic fraction with organelle controls [PMID:26687144 "cytosolic localization of hISPD"]. The [Reactome reaction](https://reactome.org/content/detail/R-HSA-9940844) independently models the A4D126 dimer in cytosol.

The official displayed product has 451 residues. The second listed product has 401 residues and lacks residues 179–228, including the region containing the structural dimer arm. Its sequence is reconstructed from the recorded VSP edit and matches the official product FASTA. Its catalytic activity and oligomerization have not been established here. Neither the engineered 43–451 construct nor the patient exon-3 deletion is automatically assigned to this numbered splice product.

Human recessive variants and zebrafish knockdown support the same glycan pathway, but retain their experimental distinctions. Two splice-blocking zebrafish morpholinos give concordant structural and sarcolemma defects; the direct glycosylated-dystroglycan measurement uses MO1 [PMID:22522421 "glycosylated αDG was reduced"]. The [original main article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3378661/) and its linked five-page supplementary Methods document the human cohort, fish splice controls, morpholino controls and muscle assays. Figure 4 was inspected directly. The phenotype is not converted into an additional autonomous muscle-development function.

## 2026-10-13: process specificity and structural association

The broad glycoprotein-biosynthesis annotation is refined to protein O-linked glycosylation via mannose. The current ontology places this process beneath glycoprotein biosynthesis and includes elaboration of the mannose-initiated glycan. Human enzyme assays and donor rescue support CRPPA doing the upstream chemistry that supplies CDP-ribitol for that pathway. The original ARBA output and its matching branch 26 remain positive source evidence; they are not rewritten as a more specific rule output. This refinement supersedes the earlier choice to retain the broad process alongside the specific assertion ([GO:0035269](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/QuickGO/term/GO:0035269); [PMID:26923585](https://da.lib.kobe-u.ac.jp/da/kernel/90003339/90003339.pdf), human enzyme assays and donor rescue).

Homodimerization remains experimentally supported, but is retained as a non-core structural property. The human 43–451 crystal and reported gel filtration establish association with their stated limits; they do not establish an additional primary activity or endogenous stoichiometry for every splice product. This refines the earlier core-status choice without rejecting the positive association ([PMID:26687144](https://hdl.handle.net/2066/152310), printed page1645, structural Results and gel-filtration statement).

Product interpretation belongs with these biological evidence limits. The following descriptions are preserved from the preceding assessment, while the listed product identifiers, names and sequence note remain the original machine-derived fields:

This sequence change does not by itself establish its catalytic activity, dimerization or physiological contribution. The tested 43–451 truncation is a different construct. CRPPA itself catalyzes donor formation, so a donor-biosynthesis process is a meaningful question rather than a substrate-necessity inference. The current nucleotide-sugar definition specifies a glycosidic nucleotide–carbohydrate linkage; its application to the exact CDP-ribitol chemistry merits explicit clarification. A bounded current comparison found no GO:0009226 or descendant annotations for human CRPPA, GALT or UGP2. This limited absence does not establish a universal convention or prove a missed annotation. No additional process assertion is made here ([GO:0009226](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/QuickGO/term/GO:0009226)).
