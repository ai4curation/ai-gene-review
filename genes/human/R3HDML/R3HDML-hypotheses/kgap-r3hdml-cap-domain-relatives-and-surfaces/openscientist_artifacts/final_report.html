R3HDML (Q9H3Y0): CAP-domain relatives and surfaces

Hypothesis slug: kgap-r3hdml-cap-domain-relatives-and-surfaces Source: genes/human/R3HDML/R3HDML-ai-review.yaml, free-text focus Organism: Homo sapiens (NCBITaxon:9606) Gene: R3HDML, UniProt Q9H3Y0

Summary

Of the three molecular activity classes named in the seed hypothesis, metal-dependent ligand binding at a conserved CAP cavity is the best supported, and protease inhibition is the least supported. The structural comparison the hypothesis proposed was carried out in full, and it discriminates. R3HDML is a PI15/PI16-type CAP protein by structure (TM-score 0.988 to the PI15 CAP domain, 66.0% structure-based identity), with CRISP2, CRISP3, natrin, stecrisp and triflin ranking last of 16 CAP structures at TM 0.682-0.702. It has no CRISP cysteine-rich ion-channel-regulatory domain: its 46-residue C-terminal tail scores TM 0.640 against the PI15 tail and 0.058-0.120 against CRISP CRDs and ShKT domains. Its CAP cavity is 78 Å³, ordinary for a vertebrate CAP protein and 3.5 times smaller than the 275 Å³ sterol-binding cavity of yeast Pry1, so a Pry1-style bulk lipid carrier role is not indicated.

The CAP tetrad of R3HDML is His108-Gln119-Glu141-His168, with glutamine at position 2 in 142 of 143 vertebrate orthologs. That substitution does not disable the divalent-cation site. The only cation bound at a CAP tetrad anywhere in the eight downloaded CAP crystal structures is triflin (PDB 1WVR) Cd301, held by His60 NE2 at 2.10 Å and His115 NE2 at 2.11 Å. Transferring that experimentally observed ion onto R3HDML by structural superposition places it 2.70 Å from His168 NE2 and 2.92 Å from His108 NE2. Tetrad position 2 sits 7.7-8.0 Å from the same ion in natrin, GAPR-1, PI15 and R3HDML alike, so it is never a first-shell ligand at this subsite. What holds the cation is the His1/His4 clamp, and that clamp is intact in R3HDML. The cavity around it is under purifying selection: its 23 lining residues have mean ortholog identity 0.925 against 0.752 elsewhere, and they remain more conserved than burial-matched control residues (mean Shannon entropy 0.197 against a matched null of 0.503, permutation p = 0.0004 over 5000 draws).

The "peptidase inhibitor" label carries no evidentiary weight. It descends from a single 1996 reverse-zymography band on p25TI/PI15, described by its own authors as weak (PMID: 8882727), in a clade whose cloning paper states that the sequence "has no homology to other proteinase inhibitors" (PMID: 9473672). No Ki or IC50 has ever been published for PI15, PI16 or R3HDML against any protease. Q9H3Y0 carries no Kazal, Kunitz, serpin, TIL, WAP or cystatin module, only PF00188 CAP/SCP. GO has never accepted an inhibitor term for this protein: its entire molecular function annotation is GO:0060090 molecular adaptor activity by IBA, with zero experimental evidence. Verdict: partially supported, with the protease-inhibition branch over-annotated.

Executive judgment

Partially supported. The method works and discriminates; the protease-inhibition premise embedded in the gene name is over-annotated.

The seed hypothesis is correct that structural comparison with characterised CAP proteins narrows the molecular activity of R3HDML, and the comparison was run as specified: the AlphaFold model against CRISP, GAPR-1, Pry1/tablysin and PI15/PI16-type structures, with the tetrad, the cavity and the C-terminal region all scored, and all features scored across vertebrate orthologs.

Three of the hypothesis's structural questions got clean answers:

  • Does it retain the CAP tetrad His/Glu site? Partly. Two of four canonical positions are the His pair that actually coordinates the ion, and both are intact and correctly positioned. Position 2 is a lineage-fixed glutamine, and that position is not a first-shell ligand in any CAP structure examined.
  • Does it retain the CAP cavity? Yes, at ordinary vertebrate size (78 Å³), and the lining is under measurable purifying selection.
  • Does it have a C-terminal cysteine-rich or ion-channel-regulatory region? No. The tail is a PI15-type tail with 4 cysteines over 46 residues, against 8-10 cysteines over 49-61 residues in CRISP CRDs, and it packs back onto the CAP domain rather than projecting as an independent module.

One question could not be answered by the route attempted. The test for a canonical protease-inhibitor P1 loop failed its positive controls: the crystallographically defined P1 residues of BPTI, OMTKY3 and eglin c span 11.6-52.8 Å² main-chain SASA and 210-84 protrusion in opposite directions, so no threshold separates true inhibitors from anything else. This is reported as uninformative. The argument against protease inhibition rests instead on evidence that holds: no inhibitor module in the sequence, no documented canonical-inhibitor mechanism for the CAP fold, and no kinetic measurement anywhere in the clade.

Caveats that bound the verdict

Every structural result here is computational and rests on an apo AlphaFold model. The cation transfer uses a single Cd ion from a single crystal structure, and Cd in crystals is frequently adventitious and stands in here as a Zn surrogate. The distances in R3HDML (2.70, 2.92 Å) are longer than ideal M²⁺-N coordination of about 2.1 Å and longer than the crystal controls (2.10-2.44 Å); the symmetric offset in PI15 (2.86, 2.99 Å) points to apo side-chain placement as the cause, but that is an inference. No ligand has been identified for the conserved cavity, so "metal-dependent ligand binding" names a class, not an activity.

Key findings

F1. R3HDML is a PI15-type CAP protein, not a CRISP

TM-align was reimplemented in numpy because Biopython was unavailable in the execution environment, and it was validated on a positive control before use: the GAPR-1 crystal structure 1SMB chain A against the AlphaFold model AF-Q9H4G4 gave TM = 0.966 with 147 of 147 residues aligned, core RMSD 0.63 Å and 100% sequence identity. On that footing, mature human R3HDML (AF-Q9H3Y0-F1-v6, residues 57-253, mean pLDDT 93.5; CAP domain 67-207, mean pLDDT 96.4) was compared against 16 CAP structures.

F2. The CAP tetrad is degenerate at one position, and that degeneracy is fixed across vertebrates

Unsupervised geometric clustering of His and Glu side chains on a 7.5 Å side-chain contact graph recovered four published CAP tetrads without being told where to look: GAPR-1 1SMB His54/Glu65/Glu86/His103, Na-ASP-2 1U53 His69/Glu80/Glu106/His129, Pry1 5ETE His208/Glu215/Glu233/His250, and stecrisp 1RC9 His60/Glu75/Glu96/His115. That recovery validates the tetrad-detection step. The same search on R3HDML returned only His108, Glu141, His144 and His168. Structure-based mapping anchored on Na-ASP-2 gives the R3HDML tetrad as His108, Gln119, Glu141, His168, a 3-of-4 score. PI15 (His112/Gln123/Glu145/His172) and CRISPLD2 (His101/Gln112/Glu134/His161) also score 3 of 4, while PI16, GLIPR1, CRISP2, CRISP3 and stecrisp score 4 of 4.

The substituted residue sits in the CAP4 motif: R3HDML has G118-Q119-N120-L121 where Na-ASP-2 has G79-E80-N81-I82. The rest of the site geometry is intact in the model. His108 N to His168 N is 5.09 Å, against 5.22 Å in PI15, 5.44 Å in GAPR-1, 4.52 Å in CRISP2 and 4.93 Å in Na-ASP-2. His168 N to Glu141 O is 2.65 Å, against 2.67, 2.89 and 2.58 Å in the same three comparators.

Across 143 vertebrate orthologs retained after filtering (length 180-320 aa, more than 40% identity to human R3HDML, and closer to R3HDML than to PI15 or PI16; mammals, birds, reptiles, one amphibian, teleosts), conservation is His108 99.3%, Gln119 99.3% (glutamine in 142 of 143, the single exception being arginine), Glu141 97.9% and His168 98.6%. The glutamine is a lineage feature, not a human quirk.

F3. No CRISP cysteine-rich domain and no ion-channel-regulatory region

R3HDML residues 208-253 form a 46-residue tail with four cysteines (C225, C228, C236, C241). Structurally it matches the PI15 tail (residues 212-258) at TM = 0.640, core RMSD 1.60 Å, 60.9% identity:

PI15   212-258  APKGNWIGEAPYKVGVPCSSCPPSYGGSCTDNLCFPGVTSNYLYWFK

Against CRISP modules it matches nothing: human CRISP2 ShKT (195-243) TM = 0.058, CRISP3 ShKT (197-245) TM = 0.060, triflin CRD (1WVR) TM = 0.103, stecrisp CRD (1RC9) TM = 0.120. The CRISP CRD and ion-channel-regulatory regions carry 8-10 cysteines over 49-61 residues, roughly twice the cysteine density of the R3HDML tail. UniProt annotates a ShKT domain in CRISP2 (205-238) and CRISP3 (207-240) and none in R3HDML or PI15.

The tail is nonetheless conserved: C225 98.6%, C228 98.6%, C236 98.6%, C241 97.2% across the 143 orthologs, as are the CAP-domain cysteines (C182, C187, C205 all at 100%).

Probe-accessible cavity volumes around the tetrad site (1.6 Å probe) place R3HDML in the ordinary vertebrate range: R3HDML 78 Å³, PI15 89, GAPR-1 77, CRISP2 69, stecrisp 70, PI16 99, GLIPR1 96, tablysin-15 92, Na-ASP-2 48, Pry1 275. The 3.5-fold gap to Pry1 is the structural reason a bulk sterol-carrier role is not indicated for R3HDML.

F4. The conserved cavity is under purifying selection, and the tail folds back to line it

Shrake-Rupley solvent accessibility (240-point Fibonacci sphere, 1.4 Å probe, Tien maximum-ACC normalisation) and Shannon entropy across the 143 orthologs were computed for all 197 mature residues. A cKDTree grid search around the tetrad identified 23 cavity-lining residues: H168, N120, Q119, L121, S167, H108, W138, Q171, Y169, N212, E141, T165, Y207, C166, C205, G118, M172, S122, T170, W106, L203, S137, S139.

Cavity lining 23 0.925 0.060 0.111
All other mature residues 174 0.752 0.717 0.314

The raw comparison gives Mann-Whitney U = 859, p = 4.4 × 10⁻⁶ (one-sided). Cavity residues are more buried than the rest, and burial alone predicts conservation, so a burial-matched permutation test was run: 5000 draws, each cavity residue matched to one of its 5 nearest non-cavity residues by rSASA. Observed mean entropy 0.197 against a matched null of 0.503 (sd 0.100, 95% range 0.313-0.701), p = 0.0004. The cavity is more conserved than burial explains.

Two spatially clustered conserved surface patches, both built on the C-terminal tail, exceed their own burial-matched nulls: patch 1 (P150, C153, P155, P158, S167, N212, W213, E216; entropy 0.142 against null 0.881, p = 0.0004) and patch 2 (P80, P224, S226, G234, C236, N239; 0.182 against 1.048, p = 0.0002).

AlphaFold predicted aligned error separates the three regions of the molecule cleanly: CAP internal 2.62 Å, tail internal 7.43 Å, CAP to tail 5.07 Å, tail to CAP 8.49 Å, propeptide to CAP 28.87 Å. 145 of 356 tail heavy atoms (41%) lie within 4.5 Å of the CAP domain, contributed by 30 tail residues spanning A208 to F253. Asn212, from the tail, lines the CAP cavity. The functional unit is the CAP domain with its packed tail. The propeptide has no confident placement, consistent with cleavage.

F5. An experimentally bound cation lands in an intact bi-histidine site in R3HDML

This is the result that reverses the naive reading of the degenerate tetrad. Scanning the eight downloaded CAP crystal structures for bound divalent cations found exactly one ion at a CAP tetrad: triflin, PDB 1WVR, Cd301, coordinated by His60 NE2 at 2.10 Å and His115 NE2 at 2.11 Å, which are tetrad positions 1 and 4. Superposing triflin onto each of 11 CAP structures (transfer RMSD over the matched core 1.6-4.5 Å) and carrying the ion across places it on a histidine NE2 in every CAP protein tested.

F6. The peptidase inhibitor name traces to one 1996 gel band

The documentary chain was traced by PubMed search. In 1996, p25TI was purified from glioblastoma-conditioned medium and reported to show "weak inhibitory activity against trypsin in reverse zymography as compared with the other inhibitors" (PMID: 8882727). In 1998 the same protein was cloned, and the authors wrote that "The P25TI sequence has no homology to other proteinase inhibitors, but has similarity to insect venom allergens, mammalian testis-specific proteins and plant pathogenesis-related proteins", which is the CAP superfamily (PMID: 9473672). In 1999, LGL1 from late-gestation lung mesenchyme was found to share 81% homology with P25TI (PMID: 10362728).

p25TI is PI15. The UniProt names "peptidase inhibitor 15", "peptidase inhibitor 16" and "peptidase inhibitor R3HDML" all descend from that one weak semi-quantitative gel result. R3HDML's own name is an ECO:0000250 sequence-similarity transfer from PI15/PI16, with no R3HDML inhibition assay in the literature. No Ki or IC50 exists for any of the three proteins against any protease. A domain scan of Q9H3Y0 finds PF00188 CAP/SCP only (with SMART SM00198, Gene3D 3.40.33.10, SUPFAM SSF55797, InterPro IPR014044/IPR001283/IPR047899) and no Kazal, Kunitz, serpin, TIL, WAP or cystatin module. UniProt itself marks the FUNCTION line "Putative serine protease inhibitor" with ECO:0000250 and carries a CAUTION note that the protein does not contain an R3H domain despite its symbol.

F8. A negative methodological result, reported as such

The intended test for a canonical protease-inhibitor P1 loop failed its positive controls. Main-chain SASA and a loop-protrusion score were computed for three crystallographically defined P1 residues: BPTI 5PTI Lys15 at 11.6 Å² and protrusion 210; OMTKY3 1CSO chain I Ile18 at 40.1 Å² and 108; eglin c 1ACB chain I Leu45 at 52.8 Å² and 84. The three true inhibitors span a 4.6-fold range in main-chain exposure and a 2.5-fold range in protrusion, in opposite directions, so no threshold on either metric separates canonical inhibitors from anything else. For reference only, the most main-chain-exposed Arg or Lys in each CAP protein was R3HDML R129 (44.9 Å²), PI15 K258 (56.1 Å²) and PI16 K80 (46.4 Å²), values inside the inhibitor range and therefore carrying no information.

A separate caveolin-binding-motif scan ([WFY].[WFY].{4}[WFY].{2}[WFY]) returned zero hits in every protein tested, including the Pry1 positive control, so that scan is likewise uninformative rather than evidence of absence.

Reporting these failures matters. Presenting "no inhibitor loop found" as a positive structural result would be an unsupported claim.

Mechanistic model

The immediate molecular question is what the R3HDML polypeptide does after secretion. The structure answers part of it.

R3HDML is a secreted single-CAP-domain protein of the PI15 type: a signal peptide, a propeptide with no confident placement against the CAP domain (PAE 28.87 Å, consistent with cleavage), an SCP/CAP fold carrying a His108-Gln119-Glu141-His168 site, and a 46-residue cysteine-containing tail that folds back onto the CAP domain instead of projecting away from it. The functional unit is the CAP domain plus its packed tail, with Asn212 from the tail contributing to the cavity rim and both conserved surface patches sitting at the CAP/tail junction. Any ligand-binding surface should be modelled on that composite.

What the structure identifies is a binding site: a conserved cavity with a geometrically viable divalent-cation clamp at its mouth. It does not identify a ligand, and it demonstrates no catalysis, no inhibition and no channel modulation. The two dashed links at the bottom of the diagram mark the step the data do not cross.

The two functional papers on R3HDML report downstream phenotypes. PMID: 31524320 describes satellite cell proliferation and muscle regeneration, and explicitly calls the protein "previously uncharacterized". PMID: 33620517 reports that R3hdml "inhibited p38 mitogen-activated protein kinase phosphorylation, which was induced by TGF-β". Both are cellular and organismal consequences several steps removed from the protein's own chemistry, so neither can be used to assign a molecular function term. The PI16 interaction annotations (PMID: 32296183, PMID: 35922511) are the closest experimental handle in the clade, and they belong to PI16.

Evidence base

The naming chain. PMID: 8882727 is the sole source of inhibitory activity in this clade, and the activity it reports is a weak reverse-zymography band with no inhibition constant. PMID: 9473672 is the paper that should have stopped the name propagating: its authors recorded that the sequence has no homology to proteinase inhibitors and belongs to the CAP superfamily. PMID: 10362728 extended the clade to lung mesenchyme at 81% homology, and placed it in the CRISP family of cysteine-rich extracellular proteins. These three papers together explain how a weak gel band became three gene names.

The CAP cation site. PMID: 29501266 states that the BmVAL-1 cavity "is a prototypical CRISP cavity with two histidines required to bind divalent cations", which is the direct literature anchor for the finding that R3HDML's two retained histidines are the functionally relevant pair. The structural transfer experiment here is consistent with that statement: the only cation found at a CAP tetrad in the structure set was held by two histidine NE2 atoms, and the glutamates were 6-8 Å away.

Tetrad degeneracy and function. PMID: 24598142 reports that in yeast Pry1 "mutations in highly conserved putative catalytic residues had no effect on export of cholesteryl acetate or on lipid binding". This paper challenges any simple reading in which tetrad degeneracy equals lost function, and supports the conclusion drawn here that a 3-of-4 tetrad score does not retire the cavity as a binding site.

Where ion-channel regulation lives. PMID: 20972450 states that CRISPs have "a CAP domain, which has been implicated in cell-cell adhesion, and a CRISP domain, which has been shown to regulate several classes of ion channels". This is review-level evidence, and it is used here only to locate the activity in the domain that R3HDML lacks.

R3HDML's own literature. PubMed returns 8 records for R3HDML. Two are functional: PMID: 31524320 on satellite cells and PMID: 33620517 on podocytes. The remainder are genetic associations at the FITM2-R3HDML-HNF4A type 2 diabetes locus (PMID: 22158537, PMID: 24086726, PMID: 27862917), a gene-based association with depressive symptoms in psychotic illness (PMID: 22220189), a fine-mapped locus in a burning mouth syndrome genomic SEM study (PMID: 41067425), and a liver transcriptomics screen in which R3hdml was one of 16 differentially expressed genes (PMID: 37866487). None of these assays protease inhibition or any direct molecular activity. The GWAS signals sit in a gene-dense locus adjacent to HNF4A, so they do not implicate the R3HDML protein product specifically.

Family context. PMID: 32378701 and PMID: 32178374 are reviews covering CAP superfamily function and snake venom CRISPs respectively, and they are used here for orientation only. PMID: 29505764 reports that HpVAL-4 "lacks the two histidines required to coordinate divalent cations" while still binding palmitate and sterol, which is a useful contrast case: that protein lost the histidines and kept lipid binding, while R3HDML kept the histidines and has an ordinary-sized cavity.

Conflicts and alternatives

The name conflicts with the structure. UniProt calls Q9H3Y0 a peptidase inhibitor and carries the keyword "Protease inhibitor"; GO carries no inhibitor term; the sequence has no inhibitor module. This is database carry-over from a 1996 naming decision, propagated across PI15, PI16 and R3HDML by homology. Curation that treats the name as evidence will be circular.

Paralog confusion runs in two directions. PI15 is the only family member with any inhibition data, and that data is one weak gel band. PI16 is the only family member with interaction data. R3HDML has neither, and transferring either would be inference presented as evidence.

An alternative reading of the conserved cavity. Conservation of the lining could reflect fold stability rather than ligand binding. The burial-matched permutation test was run specifically to address this, and the lining remains more conserved than control residues at the same burial (p = 0.0004), which weakens but does not eliminate the structural-stability explanation. Residues can be conserved for folding reasons that correlate with burial only imperfectly.

The cation result has a narrow base. It rests on one Cd ion in triflin 1WVR. Cd ions in crystal structures are frequently adventitious, so both the site's solution occupancy and its physiological ion are unknown. The distances in R3HDML (2.70, 2.92 Å) exceed ideal M²⁺-N coordination of about 2.1 Å and exceed the crystal controls. The symmetric offset in PI15 points to apo side-chain placement, but that remains an inference.

No lipid-carrier alternative. The Pry1/tablysin reading, a large hydrophobic cavity for sterol or fatty-acid binding, is not supported: 78 Å³ against 275 Å³ for Pry1. PMID: 24598142 nonetheless shows that Pry1 lipid export depends on a caveolin-binding motif rather than on the tetrad, and the motif scan run here was uninformative because its positive control failed, so this alternative is reduced by cavity size and not formally excluded by motif analysis.

Limitations and knowledge gaps

Everything structural here is computational. No wet-lab measurement on R3HDML protein was performed or exists in the literature. The model is an apo AlphaFold prediction. Side-chain rotamers at the cation site are not optimised for a bound metal, which is the most likely cause of the 2.7-3.0 Å distances against 2.1-2.4 Å in the crystal controls.

Biopython was unavailable in the execution environment. TM-align, Kabsch superposition, Needleman-Wunsch alignment and Shrake-Rupley SASA were reimplemented in numpy and scipy. The TM-align implementation was validated against a known-identical pair (GAPR-1 crystal against AlphaFold model: TM 0.966, core RMSD 0.63 Å, 100% identity over 147 residues), and the tetrad detector was validated by recovering four published tetrads without supervision. The SASA implementation's validation was the P1-loop control, which failed, so SASA-derived conclusions are limited to relative comparisons within this dataset.

Ortholog entropy is approximate. The 143 vertebrate orthologs were aligned pairwise to the human sequence rather than through a full multiple sequence alignment, so entropy values are slightly conservative and indel-rich regions are under-sampled.

Gaps that matter for curation

No ligand for the conserved cavity. Checked: cavity volume (78 Å³), lining composition (23 residues, polar and aromatic: His, Trp, Tyr, Gln, Asn), lining conservation (p = 0.0004 against a burial-matched null), and cation site geometry. This gap is the difference between GO:0046872 metal ion binding, which the structure supports as a lead, and a specific binding term, which it cannot. Resolution: a thermal-shift or ITC screen of recombinant R3HDML against divalent cations, then against small-molecule libraries biased by the cavity's lining.

No binding measurement of any kind for R3HDML. Checked: the GO annotation state (zero experimental MF evidence) and the primary literature for the clade (no Ki or IC50 for PI15, PI16 or R3HDML). Any direct biophysical assay on recombinant protein would move this gene out of pure inference.

Physiological ion unknown. Checked: all eight CAP crystal structures in the set for bound metals, which yielded one Cd site in triflin. Resolution: ICP-MS on recombinant R3HDML, or a Zn/Ca/Mg competition titration against the His108/His168 pair.

Function of the Gln119 substitution. Checked: it is fixed as glutamine in 142 of 143 vertebrate orthologs, and it lies 7.95 Å from the cation subsite, matching the 7.79-7.90 Å seen for the corresponding glutamate in GAPR-1 and natrin. A substitution that conserved is under selection, but what for is unknown. Resolution: a Q119E reversion mutant compared with wild type in any binding assay that gives signal.

Whether the tail is a binding element. Checked: PAE (CAP to tail 5.07 Å), packing (41% of tail heavy atoms within 4.5 Å of the CAP domain, 30 contacting residues from A208 to F253), and two conserved surface patches at the CAP/tail junction. Resolution: a tail-truncated construct compared with full-length protein in the same assay.

Whether R3HDML is cleaved to a mature form in vivo. Checked: PAE places the propeptide with no confidence against the CAP domain (28.87 Å), which is consistent with cleavage without demonstrating it. Resolution: N-terminal sequencing or intact-mass measurement of secreted R3HDML.

Discriminating tests

Ranked by how much they separate the three activity classes per unit of effort.

  1. Divalent-cation binding by thermal shift or ITC on recombinant mature R3HDML, with Zn²⁺, Ca²⁺, Mg²⁺ and Cd²⁺, against an H108A/H168A double mutant. This is the single most informative experiment: it converts the strongest structural result into evidence and directly tests the metal-dependent class.
  2. A quantitative protease-inhibition panel on recombinant R3HDML and PI15 side by side (trypsin, chymotrypsin, elastase, cathepsins, continuous fluorogenic substrates, reporting Ki or an explicit limit of detection). Either this retires the family name with a number or it rehabilitates it. The 1996 result has never been repeated quantitatively.
  3. Pull-down or BioID from a tissue that expresses R3HDML (skeletal muscle satellite cells or kidney podocytes, following PMID: 31524320 and PMID: 33620517), to test the GO:0060090 adaptor inference and to find out whether PI16-like interaction behaviour extends to R3HDML.
  4. Crystallography or cryo-EM of R3HDML with and without added Zn²⁺. A holo structure would settle the ion identity and whether the 2.7-2.9 Å distances are an artefact of apo side-chain placement.
  5. A tail-truncation series (full length, CAP only, CAP plus tail) in whichever binding assay from test 1 or 3 gives signal, testing whether the packed tail and its two conserved surface patches contribute to the binding surface.
  6. Q119E reversion, to ask what the clade-defining substitution does. Lowest priority, since it needs a working assay first.
