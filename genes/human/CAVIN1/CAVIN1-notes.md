## 2026-10-04 — final nuclear-source integration

The two references were recovered by the normal source workflow and imported without changing earlier caches. PMID:11139612, The transcript release factor PTRF augments ribosomal gene transcription by facilitating reinitiation of RNA polymerase I, remains abstract-only despite its PMC identifier. Separately indexed official Methods and selected Results/captions were read. Recombinant mouse PTRF and murine templates establish release-dependent recycling: increased output occurs over repeated rounds, while single-round conditions do not show that increase. Dissociation of isolated paused complexes releases transcript without increasing the amount already synthesized. This resolves the earlier checkpoint's pending recycling question but does not establish a first-round promoter-initiation activity or the provenance of a direct human IDA assay.

PMID:27528195, PTRF/Cavin-1 promotes efficient ribosomal RNA transcription in response to metabolic challenges, has a normal XML body. The unique Results, Discussion, Methods and captions were read, including primary mouse adipocytes, CRISPR-edited mouse 3T3-L1 cells, primary MEFs and rescue controls. Nutrient/insulin changes regulate nuclear PTRF, TTF1 association, termination-region ChIP signal and pre-rRNA output. PTRF rescue differs from caveolin-1 rescue. A 3C assay connects PTRF loss with reduced promoter–terminator proximity, but does not establish direct DNA binding or prove that PTRF itself forms a physical bridge. Mouse PTRF constructs in HEK293 and separate rat-adipocyte experiments address modification/localization; HEK293 host cells do not turn these into human-protein assays. The article's proposed explanation of human lipodystrophy is not treated as a direct human nuclear-function experiment. Caveolar signaling and nuclear activity can be coupled, so the account does not assert complete independence from caveolae.

The final synthesis adds a distinct nuclear core using the existing pre-rRNA-binding molecular function and existing Pol I termination process.
