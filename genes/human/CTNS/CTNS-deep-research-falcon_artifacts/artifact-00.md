| **Identity and isoforms** | Human **CTNS UniProt O60931-1** is canonical 367-aa cystinosin, a seven-transmembrane cystine/H⁺ symporter located predominantly in the lysosomal membrane. Alternative exon-12 splicing produces 400-aa **O60931-2/cystinosin-LKG**; its C-terminal SSLKG motif promotes plasma-membrane sorting, whereas canonical GYDQL and YFPQA motifs mediate lysosomal targeting. | **Strong:** human isoform localization, mutagenesis and transport experiments. | **Primary substrate and proton coupling** | Plasma-membrane-redirected cystinosin transported **L-cystine** with Michaelis–Menten kinetics (**Kₘ 278 ± 49 µM**, three transfections). Acidic pH stimulated uptake, and nigericin-mediated collapse of the proton gradient inhibited it by **more than 85%**. L-cystine, but not D-cystine, competed effectively; direct cystinosin-dependent cysteine translocation was not detected. | **Very strong:** direct radiotracer transport, kinetics, stereoselectivity and ionophore experiments. Kalatzis et al., published **1 November 2001**, [DOI: 10.1093/emboj/20.21.5940](https://doi.org/10.1093/emboj/20.21.5940) (pqac-00000027, pqac-00000043) |
| **Structure and alternating-access mechanism** | Human cystinosin structures captured lumen-open, cytosol-open and cystine-bound states. The binding pocket includes **K273, K280, Y281, D305, N166, N301, W138, F142 and F170**; mutating K273, K280 or D305 abolished transport. DEER analysis identified **D346 as the proposed proton sensor**. The paper cites preceding functional work supporting **1 cystine:1 H⁺ symport**. | **Very strong for structure and residues; moderate for stoichiometry because it derives from preceding work:** crystallography, cryo-EM, mutagenesis and DEER. | **Lysosomal nutrient signaling** | In proximal-tubule models, CTNS loss and lysosomal cystine storage promoted Ragulator–Rag-dependent lysosomal recruitment and persistent **mTORC1** activity. Wild-type CTNS, but not transport-defective G339R, restored nutrient responsiveness; cystine dimethyl ester was sufficient to induce starvation-resistant signaling. Low-dose rapamycin rescued lysosomal, differentiation and tubular phenotypes in **preclinical** cell, rat and zebrafish models only. | **Strong preclinical evidence, not clinical evidence:** genetic rescue, metabolite loading, co-immunoprecipitation and cross-species intervention. Berquez et al., **2023**, [DOI: 10.1038/s41467-023-39261-3](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-023-39261-3) (pqac-00000029, pqac-00000030, pqac-00000033) |
