# F5: cofactors, stored pools and secretory transit

Factor V is a secreted procofactor. Its processed Va chains assist factor Xa in prothrombin activation on phospholipid surfaces; Xa performs the proteolysis. Cleavage of FV by thrombin, Xa or activated protein C makes FV the substrate of those particular reactions. Neither a catalyst complex containing FV nor a strong coagulation phenotype makes FV a protease.

## Human biochemical and platelet evidence

The original human reconstitution in [PMID:9252393](https://doi.org/10.1074/jbc.272.33.20721), printed pages 20722–20723, uses normal plasma FV and homozygous-patient Leiden FV with human coagulation factors, PC/PS membranes, thrombin-generation readout and prothrombin immunoblots. The inspected Methods/Results support a cofactor step in fibrin clot formation and a membrane-associated context. They do not establish an F5 catalytic active site. The complete PDF is available, but this database assessment read those operative pages without inspecting figure pixels.

[PMID:9556620](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/13722970_Protein_C_Inhibitor_Secreted_from_Activated_Platelets_Efficiently_Inhibits_Activated_Protein_C_on_Phosphatidylethanolamine_of_Platelet_Membrane_and_Microvesicles), JBC 273:11281–11287, contains a useful F5 control within a PCI study. Its immunoelectron-microscopy Methods use anti-factor-V antibody as a control. Results report 267 of 330 F5 gold particles inside alpha granules; activated-platelet membrane/canalicular F5 is reported as data not shown. These positive locations are retained. The measured inhibitory complex is APC–PCI, not an experimentally demonstrated F5–PCI heterodimer in the inspected passages. Only selected original text was recovered here; no figure pixels were read. The direct-evidence assessment separately addresses the unresolved imported F5–SERPINA5 interaction.

[PMID:6490642](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/16704949_Coagulation_Factor_V_contains_copper_ion), JBC 259:12949–12951, directly measures copper in three bovine and one human FV preparation by atomic absorption and emission spectroscopy after extensive dialysis, with buffer and prothrombin controls. Approximately one copper ion per FV supports copper binding even though the generic InterPro cupredoxin description calls FV/FVIII non-copper-binding. The paper does not establish a copper-dependent assembly or cofactor mechanism. Its negative oxidase assay is bovine FV/Va with a ceruloplasmin control. Complete author-transcribed main text was available; purification, measurement, controls and the functional-limit discussion were read, without pixels.

## Core synthesis: distinct cofactor states

The principal procoagulant activity is represented by [GO:0061133 endopeptidase activator activity](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/QuickGO/term/GO:0061133). Its current definition requires binding to and increasing an endopeptidase's activity, matching Va enhancement of Xa. The accepted [human reconstitution study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9252393/) (Materials and Methods, pp. 20722–20723; Results/Figure 1 and Discussion pp. 20727–20728) distinguishes the cofactor from the protease and its APC-mediated inactivation. The experiment measures thrombin generation and protein cleavage; fibrin-clot participation follows the established cofactor step, not an assertion that the experiment directly measured an in vivo clot. [Reactome 140664](https://reactome.org/content/detail/R-HSA-140664) and [140686](https://reactome.org/content/detail/R-HSA-140686) supply the explicit human Xa/Va assembly and external membrane context. Plasma and granule residence remain valid accepted locations; the active prothrombinase unit uses the external membrane face.

A separately read original, [PMID:30046712, Factor V-short and protein S as synergistic TFPIalpha cofactors](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6055574/), provides direct evidence for an anticoagulant contribution. Materials and Methods describes human plasma-derived FV and coagulation reagents, BHK-expressed recombinant full-length FV/FV-short, and the author-numbered R709Q/R1545Q FV-short QQ mutant. Purified-component S2765 assays and Figures 1–3 compare TFPIalpha with FV-short and protein S; Figures 4–9 and the corresponding Results use deficient/normal plasma, antibody blocking and thrombin-cleavage controls. FV-short enhances TFPIalpha inhibition of Xa in synergy with protein S; intact FV is less efficient and the synergistic FV-short activity is lost after thrombin cleavage. Full XML and its complete normal cache are available. Methods, Results and captions were read, but figure pixels and the cited earlier construct-generation articles were not. The paper's introductory schematic chain-domain wording is not used to define the Va domain architecture.

The inhibitor unit uses **contributes_to** [GO:0004867 serine-type endopeptidase inhibitor activity](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/QuickGO/term/GO:0004867): TFPIalpha supplies the direct protease-inhibitory contact, while F5 contributes to the inhibitory assembly. This is supported by functional component-omission, titration and cleavage experiments, not only association or loss-of-function necessity. The synthetic phospholipid assays do not independently establish a native endothelial or platelet location, so none is assigned to this unit. [GO:0030195 negative regulation of blood coagulation](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/QuickGO/term/GO:0030195) and the procoagulant [GO:0072378 fibrin clot formation](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/QuickGO/term/GO:0072378) are non-ancestral process facets of distinct activities. No new annotation rows were proposed at the original core-synthesis checkpoint; the focused feedback review below now proposes two supported rows.

The study's recombinant splice-derived FV-short, engineered QQ variant, full-length FV and thrombin-cleaved preparations remain distinct. No UniProt isoform identifier or functional equivalence across current transcript models was inferred. The accepted [Reactome 9931624](https://reactome.org/content/detail/R-HSA-9931624) and [9931628](https://reactome.org/content/detail/R-HSA-9931628) instead model a proteolytic FV intermediate: their actual P12259 chains are 29–737 and 1047–2224. Their discussion of FV-short does not turn that modeled intermediate into a splice-product record. The second core is grounded in the newly read experiment; the earlier database assessment established modeled assembly/localization only.

The two NEW rows describe performed work already represented in the core: Va enhancement of Xa-dependent prothrombin activation, and FV-short-dependent enhancement of TFPIalpha/protein-S anticoagulation. The former uses the human reconstitution and the explicit human Xa/Va assembly; the latter uses the purified-component assays, plasma experiments, blocking antibodies and cleavage controls in PMID:30046712.
