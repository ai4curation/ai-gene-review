# Human CDC25C (UniProt P30307): Functional-Annotation Research Report

## Executive summary

The requested target is unambiguously **human CDC25C**, UniProt **P30307**, also called **M-phase inducer phosphatase 3** or **dual-specificity phosphatase CDC25C**. The literature describes the same 473-amino-acid human protein, one of the three mammalian CDC25 paralogues, with a regulatory N-terminal region and a C-terminal MPI/rhodanese-like phosphatase domain. This agrees with the supplied UniProt family and InterPro/Pfam assignments; no similarly named non-human protein was substituted. (alsohaibani2023rhodanesefoldcontainingproteins pages 9-11, liu2020theroleof pages 1-2)

Its best-established primary function is to activate **CDK1–cyclin B** at the G2-to-M transition by hydrolyzing the inhibitory phosphates on **CDK1 Thr14 and Tyr15**. CDC25C is therefore not a broad housekeeping phosphatase: its physiological specificity is generated by recognition of a phosphorylated CDK–cyclin complex, cellular localization, docking interactions, and tightly controlled phosphorylation. Biochemical comparisons show high efficiency toward doubly phosphorylated CDK1–cyclin B but weak or negligible activity toward CDK2-pThr14/pTyr15–cyclin A. (moura2019phosphatasesinmitosis pages 6-8, liu2020theroleof pages 2-3, rudolph2001specificityofnatural pages 1-2)

CDC25C is predominantly cytoplasmic during interphase and shuttles through the nucleus. Its cytoplasmic bias reflects both active CRM1-dependent nuclear export and Ser216-dependent binding to 14-3-3, which impedes nuclear import. At mitotic onset, release from these inhibitory mechanisms allows CDC25C to activate nuclear CDK1–cyclin B. DNA-damage pathways reverse this process through ATR–CHK1 and ATM–CHK2 signaling, maintaining CDK1 inhibition and G2 arrest. (moura2019phosphatasesinmitosis pages 6-8, graves2001localizationofhuman pages 9-10, graves2001localizationofhuman pages 2-5)

The canonical biochemical function is well established, but CDC25C should not be described as uniquely essential for mitosis. Mouse knockout and double-knockout studies demonstrate extensive compensation by other CDC25 proteins. Likewise, cancer associations are often correlative. A 2023 human lung-adenocarcinoma cohort supports prognostic potential, but no selective CDC25C inhibitor has become an established clinical therapy. (stern2023cdc25cproteinexpression pages 1-2, kiyokawa2008invivoroles pages 11-11)

## 1. Identity verification and nomenclature

The gene symbol **CDC25C** matches the supplied protein description. Human CDC25C is encoded by **ENSG00000158402**, is also termed *cell division cycle 25C*, and belongs to the three-member human CDC25 family—CDC25A, CDC25B, and CDC25C. The canonical protein is reported as **473 residues**, with alternative splice variants also described. Its reported chromosomal location is 5q31.2. (OpenTargets Search: -CDC25C, alsohaibani2023rhodanesefoldcontainingproteins pages 9-11)

The domain architecture also matches the supplied UniProt record. CDC25 proteins contain a poorly conserved, regulatory N-terminal region and a conserved C-terminal catalytic region; the latter adopts a **rhodanese-like fold** and contains the characteristic protein-tyrosine-phosphatase loop **HCX₅R**. The three human paralogues share approximately 60% identity in their C-terminal catalytic domains but diverge substantially in their regulatory N-termini. Thus, the UniProt annotations MPI_Phosphatase, Rhodanese-like domain, M-inducer phosphatase, and Rhodanese are mutually consistent rather than evidence for separate enzymatic functions. (alsohaibani2023rhodanesefoldcontainingproteins pages 9-11, moura2019phosphatasesinmitosis pages 6-8, moura2019phosphatasesinmitosis pages 5-6)

A caution concerns residue numbering in secondary sources: one 2023 rhodanese-fold review gives a catalytic-cysteine number incompatible with the 473-residue canonical CDC25C sequence. The reliable annotation is therefore the conserved **HCX₅R catalytic cysteine**, not that unreconciled residue number. (alsohaibani2023rhodanesefoldcontainingproteins pages 9-11)

## 2. Primary biochemical function

### 2.1 Catalyzed reaction

CDC25C is a cysteine-dependent **dual-specificity protein phosphatase**. Its principal reaction can be summarized as:

**CDK1–cyclin B–pThr14/pTyr15 + 2 H₂O → active CDK1–cyclin B + 2 inorganic phosphate**

CDC25C can hydrolyze phosphothreonine and phosphotyrosine ester bonds—hence “dual specificity.” The catalytic cysteine in the HCX₅R loop attacks substrate phosphate to produce a transient phosphocysteine intermediate. The conserved arginine supports phosphate binding and stabilizes the intermediate; water then hydrolyzes that intermediate and regenerates active enzyme. The wider, shallower active-site pocket of dual-specificity phosphatases accommodates phosphorylated threonine as well as tyrosine. (moura2019phosphatasesinmitosis pages 6-8, moura2019phosphatasesinmitosis pages 5-6)

Removal of CDK1 Thr14/Tyr15 phosphorylation reverses the inhibitory action of **WEE1** and **PKMYT1/MYT1**, converting preassembled cyclin-B–CDK1 into an active mitotic kinase. The direct consequence is not merely dephosphorylation of an isolated peptide: it is switch-like activation of a CDK–cyclin complex that phosphorylates many substrates required for chromosome condensation, nuclear-envelope breakdown, spindle assembly, and entry into M phase. (moura2019phosphatasesinmitosis pages 6-8, liu2020theroleof pages 2-3)

### 2.2 Substrate specificity

The strongest physiological substrate assignment is **CDK1-pThr14/pTyr15 bound to cyclin B**. Comparative biochemical evidence indicates that CDC25C acts efficiently on this complex but has weak or essentially absent activity toward doubly phosphorylated **CDK2–cyclin A**. This contrasts with CDC25A, which efficiently processes CDK2–cyclin A; reported CDC25A kinetic constants must not be transferred to CDC25C. These observations support paralogue-specific CDK–cyclin recognition despite highly similar catalytic domains. (moura2019phosphatasesinmitosis pages 6-8, rudolph2001specificityofnatural pages 1-2)

Specificity consequently resides in more than the HCX₅R pocket. Protein–protein docking surfaces, cyclin identity, CDK sequence context, the divergent N-terminal region, phosphorylation state, and compartmentalization collectively determine which CDK complex encounters active CDC25C. A 2024 cryo-EM study of CDC25A–CDK2–cyclin A advanced family-level understanding of CDC25–CDK docking, but it did not directly solve a CDC25C complex; extrapolation to P30307 should therefore remain conservative.

### 2.3 Noncanonical in-vitro activity

Purified CDC25B and CDC25C catalytic domains can also reduce inorganic arsenate in vitro with glutathione/glutaredoxin. This illustrates catalytic versatility of the rhodanese-like active site but is generally regarded as **adventitious biochemical activity**, not CDC25C’s established physiological role. (alsohaibani2023rhodanesefoldcontainingproteins pages 9-11)

## 3. Cellular function and pathway placement

### 3.1 Mitotic-entry switch

During G2, cyclin B accumulates in complexes with CDK1, while WEE1/MYT1 maintain CDK1 in an inhibited Thr14/Tyr15-phosphorylated state. CDC25C removes these inhibitory phosphates near mitotic onset. Activated CDK1–cyclin B can then promote additional CDC25C activation and suppress opposing inhibitory kinases, generating positive feedback and an abrupt, effectively irreversible transition into mitosis. (moura2019phosphatasesinmitosis pages 6-8, liu2020theroleof pages 2-3)

Current models assign overlapping spatial and temporal contributions to the three CDC25 paralogues. CDC25B is often placed early at centrosomes, CDC25A contributes to CDK1 activation and chromosome condensation, and CDC25C helps produce or maintain strong nuclear CDK1–cyclin-B activity at mitotic onset. CDC25C is therefore a central component of the network, but not necessarily its sole trigger. (moura2019phosphatasesinmitosis pages 6-8)

### 3.2 DNA-damage and replication checkpoints

CDC25C is an important output of the G2 DNA-damage checkpoint. Following DNA damage or replication stress, **ATR–CHK1** and **ATM–CHK2** signaling promotes inhibitory CDC25C phosphorylation, especially at **Ser216**, and supports association with 14-3-3. This keeps CDC25C away from nuclear CDK1–cyclin B. In parallel, WEE1/MYT1 preserve inhibitory CDK1 phosphorylation, so cells delay mitosis while lesions are processed. p38–MK2/JNK pathways may also regulate CDC25C under particular stresses, but the exact kinase contribution is context-dependent. (liu2020theroleof pages 2-3, liu2020theroleof pages 9-11, moura2019phosphatasesinmitosis pages 32-34)

The checkpoint should not be represented as a simple linear pathway unique to CDC25C. CDC25A and CDC25B are also checkpoint substrates, and inhibition of CDK1 reflects simultaneous regulation of CDC25 phosphatases and WEE1-family kinases. Recent authoritative reviews of replication stress and DNA-damage targeting retain CDC25C as a recognized CHK1-regulated node, but most clinically advanced strategies target upstream ATR, CHK1, WEE1, or PKMYT1 rather than CDC25C itself.

### 3.3 Activating regulation

Mitotic activation entails dissociation from 14-3-3 and phosphorylation at several sites, including reported Thr48, Thr67, Ser122, Thr130, and Ser214. PLK1, Aurora-A-associated pathways, and CDK1 feedback participate in the broader activation network. Protein phosphatases such as PP1/PP2A-family complexes also contribute to switching CDC25C between interphase and mitotic phosphorylation states. Exact phosphosite assignments and their hierarchy can vary with cell system, so the robust conclusion is that multisite phosphorylation converts CDC25C from an interphase-inhibited state into a mitotically active state. (liu2020theroleof pages 2-3, liu2020theroleof pages 9-11)

## 4. Subcellular localization

Human CDC25C is a **nucleocytoplasmic shuttling protein**, predominantly cytoplasmic during interphase but functionally engaged with nuclear CDK1–cyclin B around mitotic entry. Direct HeLa-cell experiments showed that wild-type GFP–CDC25C was mainly cytoplasmic and accumulated rapidly in nuclei after inhibition of CRM1/exportin-1 with leptomycin B; one experiment used **10 ng/mL for 3 hours**, and accumulation was detectable within 15 minutes. (graves2001localizationofhuman pages 2-5, graves2001localizationofhuman pages 1-2)

Mutagenesis and nuclear-microinjection experiments mapped a functional intrinsic nuclear-export region to approximately residues **177–204**. Leu194 and Leu199 were required: L194A/L199A disrupted export and promoted nuclear accumulation while preserving 14-3-3 binding. A basic sequence involving **Lys242–Lys244** contributes to nuclear import; alanine substitution reduced nuclear accumulation. (graves2001localizationofhuman pages 9-10, graves2001localizationofhuman pages 5-6, graves2001localizationofhuman pages 10-11)

Ser216 phosphorylation creates a 14-3-3-binding site. An S216A mutant that cannot bind 14-3-3 became more nuclear but remained sensitive to leptomycin B, demonstrating that 14-3-3 is not itself required for export. Conversely, simultaneous disruption of the export signal and 14-3-3 binding produced stronger nuclear accumulation than either perturbation alone. The best-supported mechanism is therefore two-layered: **CRM1-dependent export removes CDC25C from the nucleus, while phospho-Ser216-bound 14-3-3 suppresses efficient nuclear import**. (graves2001localizationofhuman pages 9-10, graves2001localizationofhuman pages 2-5, graves2001localizationofhuman pages 1-2)

These localization studies used tagged constructs extensively, although their mutational and pharmacological convergence provides strong mechanistic evidence. Localization is dynamic and should not be reduced to “cytoplasmic protein” or “nuclear protein.”

## 5. Biological necessity and paralogue redundancy

Genetic evidence substantially qualifies older descriptions of CDC25C as an indispensable mitotic inducer. **Cdc25c-null mice are viable and show no major developmental defect**. Their embryonic fibroblasts display broadly normal cell-cycle progression and G1, S, and G2 checkpoint responses, and lymphocyte development and proliferation are largely normal. Cdc25b/Cdc25c double-null mice are also viable; females show sterility resembling the Cdc25b-null phenotype, while somatic cell-cycle and irradiation-checkpoint phenotypes remain comparatively mild. (kiyokawa2008invivoroles pages 11-11)

These are mouse data, not proof of universal dispensability in human cells. Nevertheless, they demonstrate substantial compensation among CDC25A, CDC25B, and CDC25C and argue against annotations claiming that CDC25C alone is required for every mammalian mitosis. A more precise annotation is: **CDC25C is a biochemically competent and regulated CDK1 activator whose organism-level requirement is buffered by paralogous CDC25 pathways**.

## 6. Disease relevance and recent research

### 6.1 Cancer expression and prognosis

Many tumors show elevated CDC25C expression, consistent with increased proliferative fraction, altered checkpoint control, or both. However, overexpression does not by itself establish that CDC25C is the initiating oncogenic driver. The strongest retrieved recent clinical evidence came from a study published **26 January 2023** in *Biomedicines* (DOI: [10.3390/biomedicines11020362](https://doi.org/10.3390/biomedicines11020362)). In **61 human lung adenocarcinoma specimens**, high CDC25C immunohistochemical expression was enriched in poorly differentiated tumors (**p < 0.001**) and tumors larger than 1 cm (**p < 0.05**). High expression correlated with shorter disease-free survival (**p = 0.03; median follow-up 39 months**); overall survival showed only a nonsignificant adverse trend (**p = 0.08**). The authors appropriately called for validation in larger cohorts. (stern2023cdc25cproteinexpression pages 1-2, stern2023cdc25cproteinexpression pages 10-11)

A 2020 TCGA-based hepatocellular-carcinoma analysis reported higher CDC25C expression, poorer survival associations, and four candidate alterations—V41A, L87H, N222K, and an X309 splice-site event. Its interaction/enrichment analysis recovered CDK1, cyclins B1/B2, CHEK1/2, PLK1, WEE1, TP53, and 14-3-3ζ, coherently placing CDC25C in G2/M and p53/checkpoint networks. Because this was primarily bioinformatic, the mutations and biomarker claims require functional and prospective validation. (xun2020identificationofcdc25c pages 5-6)

Open Targets lists disease associations for CDC25C, but several have modest association scores and mixed evidence types. Such database links—particularly those to neurodegenerative, metabolic, or reproductive phenotypes—should be treated as hypotheses rather than established P30307 functions. (OpenTargets Search: -CDC25C)

### 6.2 2023–2024 developments

Recent work has mostly refined CDC25C’s disease context rather than overturning its core functional annotation. The 2023 lung-adenocarcinoma study supplied protein-level clinical evidence with outcome data. A separate 2023 report implicated PUF60-dependent alternative splicing of CDC25C in lung-cancer progression, highlighting isoform regulation as an emerging layer; however, full quantitative evidence from that paper was not available in the retrieved corpus, so detailed mechanistic claims are not made here.

A **January 2024** review in *Frontiers in Pharmacology* (DOI: [10.3389/fphar.2024.1324001](https://doi.org/10.3389/fphar.2024.1324001)) assessed CDC25 phosphatases as precision-oncology targets. It catalogued pan-family inhibitors such as NSC-95397, menadione and other quinones; CDC25B/C-directed SV37; and putative direct or indirect CDC25C agents such as shikonin, NS1′ protein, and Annurca-apple polyphenol extract. The evidence was principally biochemical, cell-line, or preclinical, not evidence of a validated selective CDC25C drug in patients. (dakilah2024potentialofcdc25 pages 8-9)

A **March 2023** review of human rhodanese-fold proteins reinforced the structural classification of CDC25C and emphasized the catalytic versatility of the fold, while a 2024 redox review highlighted CDC25 enzymes as redox-sensitive cell-cycle regulators. Because the catalytic cysteine is chemically reactive, oxidation can inhibit phosphatase activity; that feature is biologically plausible but also complicates inhibitor interpretation, as redox-active compounds may appear to inhibit CDC25C nonspecifically. (alsohaibani2023rhodanesefoldcontainingproteins pages 9-11)

## 7. Applications and translational status

### Biomarker applications

CDC25C protein or transcript abundance is being investigated as a proliferation-associated prognostic marker, particularly in lung adenocarcinoma and hepatocellular carcinoma. The 2023 LUAD cohort offers the clearest recent quantitative support, but its size and retrospective design do not justify routine clinical deployment. CDC25C staining may also partly report mitotic fraction rather than a tumor-specific dependency. (stern2023cdc25cproteinexpression pages 1-2, stern2023cdc25cproteinexpression pages 10-11)

### Experimental readout

Phospho-CDC25C, total CDC25C, inhibitory CDK1 Tyr15 phosphorylation, cyclin B1, and mitotic markers are commonly measured together to assess G2 arrest, checkpoint activation, and mitotic entry in cultured cells. This is a useful pathway readout, but changes in phospho-CDC25C alone do not prove direct drug binding or direct pathway causality.

### Therapeutic targeting

Direct targeting remains challenging for four reasons:

1. The HCX₅R catalytic pocket is shallow and highly conserved across CDC25 paralogues.
2. The catalytic cysteine is vulnerable to nonspecific oxidation and covalent reactivity.
3. Many reported inhibitors are quinones, redox-active compounds, or indirect pathway modulators.
4. CDC25A/B/C compensation may reduce the efficacy of an isoform-selective inhibitor.

Accordingly, **no selective CDC25C-directed therapy is clinically established** in the reviewed evidence. Current real-world oncology implementation is instead indirect: inhibitors of ATR, CHK1, WEE1, PKMYT1, PLK1, or CDKs manipulate the checkpoint network in which CDC25C operates. Candidate CDC25 inhibitors remain useful chemical probes and lead structures, but claims of clinical readiness are premature. (dakilah2024potentialofcdc25 pages 8-9, kiyokawa2008invivoroles pages 11-11)

## 8. Evidence-weighted functional annotation

The recommended concise annotation is:

> **Human CDC25C/P30307 is a regulated, nucleocytoplasmic, cysteine-dependent dual-specificity phosphatase of the MPI/rhodanese-like family. Its principal physiological function is to activate cyclin-B-bound CDK1 at mitotic entry by dephosphorylating inhibitory CDK1 Thr14 and Tyr15. Its activity and access to substrate are controlled by multisite phosphorylation, Ser216-dependent 14-3-3 binding, CRM1-dependent nuclear export, and DNA-damage checkpoint signaling. CDC25C contributes to the switch-like G2/M transition but overlaps functionally with CDC25A and CDC25B.**

Confidence is **high** for identity, catalytic mechanism, CDK1–cyclin-B substrate preference, G2/M function, and dynamic localization; **moderate** for isoform-specific pathway hierarchy because paralogues overlap; and **low-to-moderate** for disease causality and direct therapeutic tractability because most human evidence is associative and inhibitor development remains preclinical.
