| Aspect | Best-supported annotation | Evidence type | Key caveat |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Identity and domains | Human **CDC25C/P30307** is the canonical **473-aa M-phase inducer phosphatase 3**. It has a divergent regulatory N-terminus and a conserved C-terminal MPI/rhodanese-like phosphatase domain with an **HCX₅R** catalytic loop. (pqac-00000001, pqac-00000003) | Human sequence-aligned annotation and structural/family reviews | Fold and catalytic-mechanism evidence is partly family-level; residue numbering must be verified against the P30307 sequence. |
| Catalytic reaction | CDC25C removes the inhibitory phosphates from CDK1 Thr14 and Tyr15, activating cyclin-B–CDK1. Catalysis uses the HCX₅R cysteine, a transient phosphocysteine intermediate, and water-mediated hydrolysis. (pqac-00000003, pqac-00000004, pqac-00000006) | CDC25C substrate evidence supported by family mechanistic studies | Dual specificity denotes activity toward phosphothreonine and phosphotyrosine, not indiscriminate protein dephosphorylation. |
| Substrate specificity | CDC25C is efficient toward **CDK1-pThr14/pTyr15–cyclin B**, whereas its catalytic domain has weak or negligible activity toward **CDK2-pThr14/pTyr15–cyclin A** in comparative assays. (pqac-00000003, pqac-00000008) | Comparative biochemical assays using defined CDK–cyclin substrates | CDC25A kinetic values cannot be assigned to CDC25C; full-length regulation and docking context can alter specificity. |
| Localization | Human CDC25C shuttles between cytoplasm and nucleus and is predominantly cytoplasmic during interphase. In HeLa cells, **10 ng/mL leptomycin B for 3 hours** caused nuclear accumulation. Residues **177–204** contain a CRM1-sensitive export signal, **L194/L199** are critical for export, and **K242–K244** contribute to import. (pqac-00000014, pqac-00000015, pqac-00000017) | Direct human-cell localization, mutagenesis, microinjection, and export-inhibition experiments | Much of the mapping used ectopically expressed constructs; localization is dynamic and cell-cycle dependent. |
| Checkpoint regulation | Ser216 phosphorylation promotes 14-3-3 binding and cytoplasmic exclusion. After DNA damage or replication stress, ATR–CHK1 and ATM–CHK2 signaling inhibits CDC25C, allowing WEE1/MYT1-dependent CDK1 inhibition; PLK1/CDK1 feedback promotes activation during normal mitotic entry. (pqac-00000003, pqac-00000004, pqac-00000005) | Human molecular evidence synthesized in mitosis and CDC25C reviews | Kinase assignments and additional phosphosites can vary by stimulus and model; CDC25A/B also contribute to these pathways. |
| Redundancy and knockout | **Mouse** Cdc25c-null animals are viable, and derived fibroblasts have largely normal cell-cycle and checkpoint function. Cdc25b/c double-null mice are also viable, although females are sterile similarly to Cdc25b-null mice, indicating substantial family compensation. (pqac-00000012) | In-vivo mouse genetics and derived-cell phenotyping | Mouse findings do not prove that human CDC25C is dispensable in every tissue or tumor. |
| 2023 clinical biomarker evidence | In **61 human lung adenocarcinomas**, high CDC25C protein expression was associated with poor differentiation (**p < 0.001**), tumor size greater than 1 cm (**p < 0.05**), and shorter disease-free survival (**p = 0.03; median follow-up 39 months**). Overall survival showed only a nonsignificant adverse trend (**p = 0.08**). (pqac-00000010, pqac-00000011) | Retrospective human tissue cohort with clinical follow-up | The small, heterogeneous cohort establishes association, not causality or validated clinical utility. |
| Therapeutic status | CDC25 inhibition remains preclinical. Candidates include pan-CDC25 compound **NSC-95397**, menadione and related quinones, CDC25B/C inhibitor **SV37**, and direct or indirect CDC25C-directed agents such as shikonin, NS1′ protein, and Annurca-apple polyphenol extract. No selective CDC25C therapy is clinically established. (pqac-00000009) | 2024 pharmacology review of biochemical and cell-line studies | Many candidates are reactive, redox-active, indirect, or pan-isoform inhibitors; selectivity, toxicity, and family redundancy hinder translation. |
