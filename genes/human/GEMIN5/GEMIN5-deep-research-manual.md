# Selected primary evidence for GEMIN5

This manual source account distinguishes selected original experiments from abstract-only caches and inferred mechanisms. It is not an automated provider report. Reference assessments in the review give the access scope for each source.

## RNA recognition and assembly

[PMID:11714716](https://www.med.upenn.edu/dreyfusslab/publications/2002GubitzJBC5691.pdf) used full-length cellular GEMIN5 and a bacterial 43–1508 construct. Purified SMN and SmB interactions are distinct from reticulocyte-translation and cellular association assays. The six-page main text and captions were read; Figures 3–5 on page 5634 were inspected. The 694–1508 immunogen is an experimental reagent.

[PMID:16857593](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16857593/) supports snRNA recognition and Sm-core assembly in human-cell experiments. Reduced assembly after depletion is not a direct spliceosomal splicing assay. [PMID:20513430](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2901871/) extends the substrate-delivery model to pre-snRNAs. Selected original Methods and Results were read for both.

The original [PMID:19377484 article](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/24308421_Gemin5-snRNA_interaction_reveals_an_RNA_binding_function_for_WD_repeat_domains) was accessed as an author-article transcription, with the official supplement separately inspected. Figure 5a reports positive binding to recombinant SMN–Gemin2. The 1–720 and 1–844 fragments differ in U4 versus U1 recognition; they are not natural isoforms. Native subcomplex stoichiometry remains unresolved.

[PMID:17178713](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/6620949_A_Comprehensive_Interaction_Map_of_the_Human_Survival_of_Motor_Neuron_SMN_Complex) reports conditional recruitment and interactions in several assay systems. [PMID:18984161](https://pure.mpg.de/rest/items/item_641696_5/component/file_641695/content) includes native human SMN complexes containing GEMIN5, while simplified complexes retain assembly activity without it. Selected Methods, Results and captions were read; Figure 3 of the latter was inspected. These observations do not establish universal GEMIN5 dispensability or autonomous assembly catalysis.

## Cap recognition and translation

[PMID:19750007](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2736588/) establishes cap binding, but its proposed W286 stacking mechanism was a model. The human 1–740 structure in [PMID:27834343](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27834343/) resolves two seven-bladed propellers and distinct RNA and cap sites. W286 is buried; W286A was insoluble in that study. The original main text, supplementary Methods and captions were read, with selected Figure 1/S1G images. The other 2016 structural papers, [27881600](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27881600/) and [27881601](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27881601/), provide independent construct-specific evidence.

The direct bacterial-protein interaction with eIF4E in [PMID:16739988](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16739988/) is retained alongside the tagged-293T no-coimmunoprecipitation result in PMID:19750007. Different conditions need not yield the same association. The exact historical GST construct boundary was not independently re-established; cap-resin recruitment alone is not a direct contact map.

[PMID:25911097](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25911097/) combines direct SMN-mRNA recognition with transcript-specific translation effects. [PMID:27507887](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27507887/) instead measures ribosome interactions and broader translational repression under its tested conditions. Selected original sections were read; elongation stalling remains a mechanistic proposal. [PMID:34424823](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34424823/) examines engineered C-terminal fragments and viral IRES recognition, which does not make those fragments natural products.

## Variants, model participants and access limits

[PMID:33963192](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33963192/) distinguishes L1068P patient-derived neurons from engineered H913R iPSC clones. Assembly-system abundance changes and extract-level assembly defects do not isolate a catalytic activity of GEMIN5. Selected original Results, Methods, Discussion and captions were read; images and supplements were not included in that reading.

The six current Reactome contexts have explicit human Q8TEQ6 participant paths. R-HSA-191763 is snRNP–Snurportin complex formation; cap hypermethylation is R-HSA-191784. The nuclear-envelope location of import event R-HSA-191830 is not assigned to GEMIN5: its input and output target leaves are cytosolic and nucleoplasmic, respectively. TGS1 and methylosome catalytic roles remain separate.

For large-scale RNA-binding and interaction screens, source-level assay evidence is distinguished from exact target-table verification and from orthogonal physiological validation. In particular, the PMID:22658674 target-table subset was not independently inspected. Historical generated research and old quote associations remain provenance, not fresh verification of their claims.

## Viral IRES substrate identity

The official [RNAcentral URS0002318D25 record](https://rnacentral.org/api/v1/rna/URS0002318D25/?format=json) identifies a 462-nucleotide FMDV type-C IRES in taxon 12116. In [PMID:23221641](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3553989/), the final Introduction paragraph places the recognized IRES domain upstream of the initiator codon. The Constructs and RNA synthesis Methods, Figure 1 caption and Results on domain-5/C-terminal binding distinguish the RNA segments and purified-protein assays from BHK-21 extract photocrosslinking. Those selected original sections were read from NCBI BioC; figure pixels and supplements were not inspected.

Together with the later d5/d5ss experiments in PMID:34424823, this supports [mRNA 5′-UTR binding, GO:0048027](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/QuickGO/term/GO:0048027). It does not establish that the full RNAcentral sequence equals either short reagent, nor does it convert the engineered protein fragments into natural products.
