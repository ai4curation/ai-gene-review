Gene Description

CHD2 is a nuclear ATP-dependent chromatin remodeler with tandem chromodomains and an SNF2-family motor. It binds DNA and histones, uses ATP hydrolysis to organize nucleosomes, and regulates chromatin accessibility and histone H3.3 occupancy. Its recruitment can depend on lineage-specific transcription factors or, at DNA breaks, PARP1-generated poly(ADP-ribose). These chromatin activities contribute to gene-expression programs during development and to an accessible environment for nonhomologous end joining. Human CHD2 haploinsufficiency alters cortical interneuron development and is associated with developmental disorders and epilepsy. Mouse myogenic studies link Chd2 to MyoD-dependent chromatin marking before differentiation; human glioma studies show a distinct FOSL1-associated transcriptional context.

Core Functions

Uses ATP hydrolysis to reorganize histone–DNA contacts and assemble or remodel nucleosomes on nuclear chromatin. Purified human CHD2 acts with histones and NAP1 in biochemical reconstitution; cellular chromatin expansion and H3.3 occupancy connect the motor to transcriptional and DNA-repair contexts without assigning autonomous histone-chaperone activity.
