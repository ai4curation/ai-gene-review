## Molecular synthesis

BMP4 is a secreted TGF-beta-superfamily growth factor. Its precursor has an N-terminal signal peptide and is proteolytically processed to a disulfide-linked mature ligand. The ligand binds type I/type II BMP receptor complexes and initiates receptor-mediated SMAD1/5/8 signaling. The receptors phosphorylate signaling proteins, and downstream SMAD complexes perform nuclear regulatory work; BMP4 is neither a kinase nor a DNA- or RNA-processing enzyme. Its extracellular ligand function unifies many context-dependent outcomes in proliferation, survival, migration and differentiation.

The single core-function synthesis records extracellular growth-factor activity in BMP signaling. BMP4's experimentally supported chemoattractant action in periureteral mesenchyme is retained as a specific non-core activity rather than a second universal function. Tissue-development terms remain contextual outcomes rather than separate core molecular activities. No NEW assertion was needed or proposed.

In the engineered reporter experiments of [PMID:9389648](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9389648/), BMP4 stimulation does not activate GAL4-SMAD constructs in SMAD4-deficient SW480.7 cells without rescue by full-length SMAD4 or its C domain. The positive evidence is that rescue, not the preceding statement of reporter inactivity. Separate nuclear-localization assays also use activated receptor cotransfection; these requirements remain explicit in the corresponding annotation reasons.

Both inspected production GO-CAMs, `66e382fb00001232` and `646ff70100002308`, place BMP4 cytokine activity in the extracellular region and BMP signaling. They distinguish BMP4 from receptor catalytic activities and downstream SMAD transcription-factor activities. The source models and index were not changed. PAINT assertions were treated as ancestral functional-inheritance judgments; BMP4 appearing among supporting experimental descendants is expected, not circular.

## Binding decisions and evidence limits

- **BMPR2:** [PMID:12045205](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12045205/) measures radiolabeled BMP4 binding with receptor constructs. The generic P12644–Q13873 assertion is refined to the existing BMP receptor binding term. [PMID:7811286](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7811286/) independently supports BMP4 binding to a mammalian BMP receptor.
- **Fibrillins:** Selected primary Table 3 in [PMID:18339631](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2376219/) reports BMP4-prodomain binding to FBN1 rF23 and FBN2 rF86, with 34 and 25 nM affinities. The refinement is extracellular matrix binding. Distinguish BMP7 rotary-shadowed molecular complexes from separate BMP4 tissue co-immunolocalization in the Figure 10 caption; neither the caption nor purified-fragment binding proves direct binding to assembled microfibrils. Later [PMID:36825302](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36825302/) found no FBN1-dependent BMP4 stability/signaling effect in its examined mouse kidney/lung settings. This limits physiological extrapolation rather than refuting biochemical binding. Only selected indexed primary content was inspected for that later study; it supplies a contextual limitation, not a new GO assertion.
- **Agrin:** [PMID:20505824](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20505824/) reports SPR and reporter assays using the N-terminal part of rat agrin. Extracellular matrix binding is the specific refinement; the ligand does not acquire agrin's receptor-clustering activity.
- **SCUBE3:** [PMID:33308444](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33308444/) directly reports co-precipitation of tagged SCUBE3 and BMP4 after HEK293T co-overexpression, with separate BMP4-stimulated ALP/SMAD experiments. Co-receptor binding fits the auxiliary receptor-associated interaction. The CUB-domain mapping was BMP2-specific, and the tagged overexpression assay is not an endogenous tissue-complex measurement.
- **Antagonists:** The Cerberus-like and noggin interactions in [PMID:10748465](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10748465/) and [PMID:8752214](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8752214/) are retained as supported non-core generic interactions. No more specific BMP4 activity was established for these pairs. Broadness alone does not make an interaction false, and binding an antagonist does not make BMP4 itself an antagonist.
- **DAF-4:** [PMID:8413626](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8413626/) explicitly tests human BMP4 binding to a nematode receptor expressed in COS cells. [NCBI Gene 175781](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/gene/175781) maps all three source partners G8JY02, P50488 and Q8ITX8 to daf-4 products. The exact tested construct was not mapped to each accession, so all three distinct source rows remain UNDECIDED. The species difference is not evidence of misattribution.
- **Other receptor pairs:** The exact P36894 assays behind PMIDs:20860622 and 21054789 and the Q7TQ32 HJV pair behind PMID:16604073 remain unverified. Neither a paper's emphasis on another protein nor absence of reporter inhibition disproves physical receptor binding. All partners and source tuples remain intact.

The official AmiGO definitions were checked for [extracellular matrix binding](https://amigo.geneontology.org/amigo/term/GO:0050840), [co-receptor binding](https://amigo.geneontology.org/amigo/term/GO:0039706) and [growth factor activity](https://amigo.geneontology.org/amigo/term/GO:0008083). The first two refinements describe BMP4 binding its partners, not the partner's own activity.

## Developmental and assay distinctions

[PMID:14749725](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14749725/) tests recombinant human BMP4 in mouse C2C12 cells and human mesenchymal stem cells. Myotube inhibition supports negative regulation of myoblast differentiation; the broader striated-muscle-tissue term is refined to that measured endpoint. Osteogenic markers and calcium deposition depend on culture conditions and are enhanced by SB431542. The two distinct mineralization source assertions are retained with their co-treatment context. [PMID:18436533](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18436533/) independently reports BMP4-dose-dependent calcium deposits in primary human mesenchymal cultures. BMP2-specific SCUBE3 cartilage experiments were not silently reused as BMP4 cartilage evidence.

[PMID:18823971](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18823971/) explicitly uses recombinant human BMP4 in frog animal-cap experiments, supporting the epidermal/fate-choice effects in that assay. [PMID:12631064](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12631064/) includes a modified Boyden-chamber assay and bead-induced periureteral mesenchymal accumulation, which directly support chemoattractant activity. Its metanephric and periureteral effects differ. [PMID:12141440](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12141440/) directly tests BMP4 inhibition of branching and nephrogenesis in mouse explants, but the abstract does not distinguish every S-shaped/comma-shaped-body endpoint; those exact assignments remain undecided.

BMP4-stimulated endothelial proliferation and migration in [PMID:17992660](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17992660/) are compatible with inhibitory pulmonary artery smooth-muscle responses in [PMIDs:11502704](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11502704/) and [17600318](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17600318/). The latter's positive-proliferation source row remains UNDECIDED because its precise condition was inaccessible; an abstract-level discrepancy is insufficient for removal. Pancreatic epithelial-colony formation in [PMID:11865031](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11865031/) supports a developmental response but does not by itself resolve the separate proliferation endpoint. Likewise, endothelial sprouting is not automatically proof of the precise cell-division term.

[PMID:17850284](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17850284/) includes BMP4 immunostaining despite emphasizing SHH in its abstract. The IEP smooth-muscle annotation is retained as expression-based support, with correlation rather than BMP4 perturbation explicit. The accessible [PMID:22529382](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22529382/) Discussion reports BMP4-induced TEAD4 nuclear localization but leaves the mechanism unresolved; import-specific regulation remains UNDECIDED. BMP4-dependent downstream protein association in [PMID:23610558](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23610558/) is retained without assigning BMP4 a nuclear bridging function.

The many specialized mouse transfers were considered individually. Broad pathway or organ-development evidence was not used to automatically accept exact valve, coronary-vessel, tendon, deltoid-tuberosity, glomerular-capillary or extrinsic-apoptotic-pathway claims. Conversely, an unresolved donor lookup was not used to remove an assertion. Relevant target-specific corroboration supports retention where the same biological process is demonstrated, with exact donor-edge limits stated.

## Six NOT assertions

The five cardiac mouse-transfer NOT assertions concern muscular septum, right ventricle, cushion EMT, cushion apoptosis and cardiac jelly. All five preserve their NOT flags, ISS evidence, GO_REF:0000024 and P21275 supporting entity, and remain UNDECIDED. Independent bounded lookups did not verify their exact mouse GO-to-PMID edges.

Selected Results from [PMID:18924235](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2728547/) are concrete candidate evidence: a Mef2c-AHF conditional model showed no apparent muscular-septum/right-ventricle phenotype, comparable E10.5 cardiac jelly, no initial EMT difference in the reported E9.5 explant assay, and no proximal-outflow TUNEL difference at E10.5/E12.5. These are stage-, tissue- and assay-limited observations, not universal absence of BMP4 function. The EMT result was reported without displayed data. No donor edge was inferred or original source reference replaced merely because this paper is relevant.

The sixth NOT, [PMID:18548003](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18548003/), is retained as non-core in the tested miR-21 context. Selected full-text experiments use time courses, alpha-amanitin, promoter reporters and CMV-driven pri-miR-21 to distinguish processing from transcription. SMAD1/5, p68 and DROSHA-associated experiments support increased pri-to-pre processing; DROSHA performs the RNA-processing step. This does not mean BMP4 cannot regulate any miRNA transcription: [PMID:22988237](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22988237/) reports repression of the miR-302–367 cluster. Reduced miR-494 abundance in [PMID:28124060](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28124060/) alone does not resolve transcription versus processing/stability, so its transcription-specific row remains undecided.
