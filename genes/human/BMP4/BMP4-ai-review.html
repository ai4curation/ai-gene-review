Gene Description

BMP4 is a secreted, disulfide-linked growth factor of the TGF-beta superfamily. Proteolytic maturation of its precursor produces a ligand that engages type I and type II BMP receptor complexes, initiating receptor-mediated SMAD1/5/8 signaling and context-dependent noncanonical responses. Co-receptors and extracellular antagonists modulate receptor use and signal strength, while the prodomain can bind fibrillin matrix components. BMP4 directs cell-fate choices and tissue patterning in many embryonic organs and regulates proliferation, survival, migration and differentiation according to cell type, developmental stage and signaling environment.

Molecular synthesis

BMP4 is a secreted TGF-beta-superfamily growth factor. Its precursor has an N-terminal signal peptide and is proteolytically processed to a disulfide-linked mature ligand. The ligand binds type I/type II BMP receptor complexes and initiates receptor-mediated SMAD1/5/8 signaling. The receptors phosphorylate signaling proteins, and downstream SMAD complexes perform nuclear regulatory work; BMP4 is neither a kinase nor a DNA- or RNA-processing enzyme. Its extracellular ligand function unifies many context-dependent outcomes in proliferation, survival, migration and differentiation.

The single core-function synthesis records extracellular growth-factor activity in BMP signaling. BMP4's experimentally supported chemoattractant action in periureteral mesenchyme is retained as a specific non-core activity rather than a second universal function. Tissue-development terms remain contextual outcomes rather than separate core molecular activities. No NEW assertion was needed or proposed.

In the engineered reporter experiments of PMID:9389648(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9389648/), BMP4 stimulation does not activate GAL4-SMAD constructs in SMAD4-deficient SW480.7 cells without rescue by full-length SMAD4 or its C domain. The positive evidence is that rescue, not the preceding statement of reporter inactivity. Separate nuclear-localization assays also use activated receptor cotransfection; these requirements remain explicit in the corresponding annotation reasons.

Both inspected production GO-CAMs, 66e382fb00001232 and 646ff70100002308, place BMP4 cytokine activity in the extracellular region and BMP signaling. They distinguish BMP4 from receptor catalytic activities and downstream SMAD transcription-factor activities. The source models and index were not changed. PAINT assertions were treated as ancestral functional-inheritance judgments; BMP4 appearing among supporting experimental descendants is expected, not circular.
