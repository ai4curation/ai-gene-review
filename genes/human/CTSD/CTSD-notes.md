# CTSD evidence review

Cathepsin D is an aspartic endopeptidase whose central performed role is endolysosomal proteolysis. The official human P07339 record has one 412-aa precursor and no alternative-product listing. Signal 1–20 and propeptide 21–64 precede the active region 65–412; current light/heavy chains are 65–162 and 169–412. These are proteolytic products, not splice isoforms or evidence of precursor homodimerization. The native/pepstatin structural abstract supports the human aspartic mechanism, while the human mutant study shows that a stable single chain remains active in rodent cells [PMID:8393577; PMID:17188016].

All seven processed granulin pairs remain distinct. Co-immunoprecipitation from cells or media is not relabeled purified binary binding [PMID:16997486; PMID:17112520; PMID:28493053].

The apoptotic context is upstream regulation. Actual human-fibroblast experiments show cytochrome-c redistribution before caspase activity after active-enzyme microinjection; later endogenous lysosomal leakage is distinct. Sigma C8696 reagent species is unspecified. The work does not establish direct BID cleavage or CTSD execution-phase dismantling. GO:0090200 explicitly requires DIRECT positive regulation and is therefore not substituted for the supported broader protein-localization regulation [PMID:12107093].

The insulin-source chain is now explicit. The 2016 mouse study measures endosomal enzyme activity kits and receptor distribution/phosphorylation. Its reference 34 is Authier et al. 2002: rat-endosome immunodepletion and bovine purified enzyme directly demonstrate insulin cleavage; matching human digestion is stated as data not shown. Neither the kit signal nor receptor phosphorylation resolves return to a signal-ready plasma membrane, so the recycling assertion remains undecided [PMID:27703010; PMID:11779865].
