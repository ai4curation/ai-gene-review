# SUCLA2 notes

Literature review was done manually from cached PubMed records; the falcon deep-research report (SUCLA2-deep-research-falcon.md) completed afterwards and was skimmed for consistency with the review decisions; no conflicts were found.

- SUCLA2 is the ATP-forming beta subunit of the Krebs cycle succinyl-CoA ligase [PMID:17287286 "The SUCLA2 gene encodes the ATP-forming beta subunit of the Krebs cycle enzyme succinyl-CoA ligase."].
- Disease mechanism hypotheses: NDPK association / dNTP salvage [PMID:15877282 "Succinyl-CoA synthetase is invariably in a complex with mitochondrial nucleotide diphosphate kinase"], and succinyl-CoA accumulation explaining MMA [PMID:17287286].
- Promiscuous malyl-CoA formation by TCA enzymes, repaired by CLYBL [PMID:40108300]; 'toxic metabolite repair' was therefore marked as over-annotation for SUCLA2 (changed to UNDECIDED on 2026-11-12, see below).
- 'succinyl-CoA pathway' (heme) rests on the speculative ALAS2 interaction [PMID:10727444]; marked as over-annotation.

## The organelle-level localization rows

The ten `GO:0005739` mitochondrion rows are `ACCEPT`, and that is a decision rather than an
oversight. SUCLA2 is a matrix enzyme and `GO:0005759` is accepted here on two rows and is the core
location, so a reader applying the "correct but general terms are MODIFY" rule mechanically, or
re-running an ancestor-based scan, will stop on these ten. They are not reached by that rule: none
of the ten reasons says the term is less specific than the evidence supports — they give the
localization and proteome evidence and the gene's function. Each does describe SUCLA2 as a matrix
TCA-cycle enzyme, which names a narrower compartment than the row annotates; that is background on
the protein rather than an argument that the row's term should be refined, which is the distinction
the rule turns on. Nothing else in this file refines a mitochondrial-location ancestor, so the file
does not treat one argument two ways. Whether GOA's
organelle-level rows should be refined wherever a compartment row exists is a real question, and it
is a slice-wide one rather than anything specific to this gene.

## Retrospective audit corrections (2026-11-12)

A retrospective audit of the succinyl-CoA synthetase reviews found errors in this review. Each was checked against the source before it was changed.

- GO:0110052 toxic metabolite repair (IDA, PMID:40108300): MARK_AS_OVER_ANNOTATED changed to UNDECIDED, the same as SUCLG1. Only the abstract of the published paper is cached and the published full text is not open. The abstract and the bioRxiv preprint (doi 10.1101/2024.09.25.614871, version 1) present succinyl-CoA synthetase as a source of malyl-CoA and CLYBL as the repair enzyme, which argues for over-annotation. The preprint also names succinyl-CoA synthetase with AUH and CLYBL as an enzyme of itaconate catabolism, so the curator may have annotated the activating step of a disposal route. The experimental row is not overruled on an unread paper. A suggested question records what would settle it.
- GO:0046872 metal ion binding (IEA): KEEP_AS_NON_CORE changed to MODIFY with replacement GO:0000287 magnesium ion binding, which SUCLA2 already carries. A correct but general term is a MODIFY, not a non-core keep.
- core_functions: GO:0004775 moved from molecular_function to contributes_to_molecular_function. The enzyme is an obligate heterodimer, with the phosphohistidine and CoA site on the alpha subunit and the nucleotide and succinate site on the beta subunit. SUCLG1 and SUCLG2 already used contributes_to.
- Itaconate. The description said the enzyme forms itaconyl-CoA. One study found no conversion [PMID:34492704 "neither ATP- nor GTP-specific SCS is able to use itaconate under the same conditions as succinate in vitro"]; the preprint of PMID:40108300 reports a catalytic efficiency 30-fold lower than with succinate for the ADP-forming enzyme. The description now says the activity is reported but contested, and the negative result is a DISPUTED finding of PMID:34492704. The GO:0004775 EXP row from that paper stays ACCEPT and now quotes the result that recombinant SCS-A converted succinate.
- GO:0016878 acid-thiol ligase (IDA, contributes_to): action unchanged, reason rewritten as in SUCLG1. Malyl-CoA formation is covered by GO:0050074 on this gene; the row is retained only because itaconate-CoA ligase activity has no GO term.
- GO:0004775 EXP row from PMID:40108300: the CLYBL abstract sentence was replaced by the curated UniProt reaction, because the sentence says nothing about succinate-CoA ligase activity.
- GO:0006104 rows: the reason now says why the term is kept beside its child GO:1901289. The child is defined by breakdown of succinyl-CoA, so it names only the direction that consumes succinyl-CoA (the forward direction of the TCA cycle).
- Status changed from COMPLETE to DRAFT. The GO:0110052 row is UNDECIDED until the published text of PMID:40108300 is read, as in SUCLG1 and SUCLG2.
- Affinage record. The note listed PMID:33991485 and PMID:39482887 as not verified because they were not cached. Both are cached and support the claims [PMID:33991485 "at S79 dissociates from GLS, resulting in enhanced GLS K311 succinylation"]; they were moved to the verified list. Correctness changed from DISPUTED to LOW_QUALITY, with the DISPUTED status moved to the GO grounding finding. Its one supporting quote on the GO:0004775 IBA row was kept beside the primary paper it was verified against. (Removed later on 2026-11-12: a record rated LOW_QUALITY should not support an annotation row; the row keeps its primary support. The validator's advisory warning that no annotation cites the deep research file is accepted.)
- Cross-file consistency pass (2026-11-12). GO:0003824 catalytic activity (IEA) stays TOO_GENERAL; its reason is now worded the same way in all eleven subunit reviews of the family.

## GO:0110052 follow-up (2026-11-18)

The task was to settle the GO:0110052 toxic metabolite repair row (IDA, PMID:40108300). It is not settled: the published full text could not be obtained, so the row stays UNDECIDED. The reason was rewritten.

- Routes tried for the published full text of PMID:40108300 (doi 10.1038/s41589-025-01857-9): PubMed ID conversion (no PMCID), Europe PMC (not in Europe PMC, not open access), the publisher page (paywalled), Unpaywall, OpenAlex and Semantic Scholar (only the bioRxiv submitted version is open), and the ORBilu repository copy (not reachable). `just fetch-pmid 40108300` reports the paper as already cached; the cache is abstract-only (`full_text_available: false`), and the fetcher takes full text only from PMC, so it cannot improve on it.
- Read outside the cache, and therefore not quoted in the review:
  - Supplementary Information of the published article (publisher site, open). Supplementary Fig. 5 legend: recombinant human succinyl-CoA ligase was produced in E. coli by co-expressing SUCLG1 (residues 41-346) with SUCLA2 (residues 53-463) or with SUCLG2 (residues 38-432). Both heterodimers were assayed.
  - Source data for Table 1 of the published article (spreadsheet, open). Mean of three replicates, kcat/Km in per s per mM, with Km in mM in brackets. SUCLG1/SUCLA2: succinate 1.12 (6.28), itaconate 0.033 (20.0), malate 0.0030 (21.9). SUCLG1/SUCLG2: succinate 52.2 (0.26), itaconate 3.73 (1.45), malate 0.097 (1.85). The ratios to succinate were computed here, not taken from the paper: itaconate about 34-fold lower (ADP-forming) and 14-fold lower (GDP-forming); malate about 370-fold and 540-fold lower.
  - bioRxiv preprint version 2 (2024-10-01, doi 10.1101/2024.09.25.614871), read as excerpts returned by a web fetch tool that summarises the page; the raw text could not be downloaded, so no wording was checked. Earlier notes cite version 1; version 2 is the latest. As excerpted, it gives folds of 30 and 15 (itaconate) and 340 and 540 (malate), calls malyl-CoA formation a side activity of citric acid cycle enzymes, names succinyl-CoA ligase with AUH and CLYBL as the enzymes of itaconate catabolism, and assigns the repair role to CLYBL. The excerpts contain no statement that succinyl-CoA ligase takes part in metabolite repair.
  - Not read: the main text, Methods and Table 1 of the published article.
- What the curator annotated (QuickGO, annotations by reference PMID:40108300, UniProt, dated 2025-09-17). SUCLG1 and SUCLA2 each carry two GO:0016878 acid-thiol ligase rows (IDA, contributes_to). One has the extension part_of(GO:0110052), has_input(CHEBI:17240 itaconate), has_input(CHEBI:57287 CoA). The other has part_of(GO:0006108 malate metabolic process), has_input(CHEBI:15589 (S)-malate), has_input(CoA), has_output(CHEBI:57317 malyl-CoA). CLYBL carries GO:0016289 with has_input(CHEBI:57317), has_output(CHEBI:15589), part_of(GO:0110052). So the curator attached toxic metabolite repair to the itaconate-activating step, not to malyl-CoA formation. This is a reading of the annotation record, not of the paper.
- Human GO:0110052 annotations (QuickGO by term id, 14 rows, so the query can return positives): AUH (TAS) and CLYBL (IDA) from PMID:29056341, the itaconate route; CLYBL, SUCLG1 and SUCLA2 (IDA) from PMID:40108300; PGP, ADK and MAPDA for other repair reactions. Annotating the enzymes of itaconate catabolism to this term is an existing practice.
- SUCLG2 has only the GO:0004776 EXP row from PMID:40108300: no GO:0016878, GO:0050074, GO:0006108 or GO:0110052 row, although the GDP-forming enzyme was assayed with malate and itaconate and is the more efficient with both, and although its UniProt FUNCTION text records the activity. This is a possible omission in the annotation set, recorded here as a query for UniProt and not as a source defect: the main text was not read, and a missing row is a curation gap.
- GO:0110052 row: action unchanged (UNDECIDED). The earlier reason leaned to over-annotation on the reading that the enzyme is only the source of malyl-CoA. That is not what the curator asserted. The reason now says that the term was attached to itaconyl-CoA formation, that this step was measured, that on this reading the gene product does part of the work, and that what remains open is term fit: GO:0110052 is defined for [QuickGO GO:0110052 "toxic endogenous compounds, formed as by-products of primary metabolism"], itaconate is a deliberately made immunometabolite, and itaconyl-CoA is itself the mutase inhibitor [PMID:40108300 "result from inhibition of the B12-dependent enzyme methylmalonyl-CoA mutase by itaconyl-CoA"]. AUH and CLYBL share the first point, so it is a question for the term. The cached abstract and the preprint excerpts assign the repair role to CLYBL acting on malyl-CoA and treat itaconate catabolism as a separate role; no statement that succinyl-CoA synthetase takes part in repair was found. If the published text agrees, the row rests on the existing practice of annotating the itaconate route to this term (AUH and CLYBL from PMID:29056341), and the choice is between KEEP_AS_NON_CORE and MARK_AS_OVER_ANNOTATED. The same text is used for SUCLG1.
- supported_by on that row: the abstract sentence about CLYBL repairing malyl-CoA was replaced, because it supports the reading the curator did not make. The row now quotes the curated UniProt FUNCTION sentence on itaconyl-CoA synthetase activity and the abstract sentence on itaconyl-CoA and methylmalonyl-CoA mutase.
- GO:0050074 malate-CoA ligase rows: unchanged (KEEP_AS_NON_CORE). Under the tiers of projects/ENZYME_SPECIFICITY.md this is not REMOVE: human has no dedicated malate-CoA ligase and the gap to succinate is a few hundred fold. MARK_AS_OVER_ANNOTATED could be argued because the product is a damage metabolite, but that is a judgement the unread main text should inform.
- Suggested questions: the question about what the published paper says was replaced by the term-fit question. The itaconyl-CoA question now carries the Km values, which fit the earlier negative result at 10 mM itaconate for SCS-A but not for SCS-G [PMID:34492704 "neither ATP- nor GTP-specific SCS is able to use itaconate under the same conditions as succinate in vitro"].
- PMID:40108300 reference note rewritten to list what was read; correctness stays UNVERIFIED.
- Status: the review file is COMPLETE, set on 2026-11-13 when the tca_cycle module was completed, with this one justified UNDECIDED row. It was not changed here. The line above from 2026-11-12 that says the status was changed from COMPLETE to DRAFT describes the earlier state; the 2026-11-13 promotion superseded it.
