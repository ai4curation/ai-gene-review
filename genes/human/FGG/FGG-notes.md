## 2026-10-27 — Gamma-chain structure and evidence boundaries

Gamma is not merely consumed during clotting. Human gamma-containing intermediates assemble into fibrinogen (PMID:8910396), and gamma-derived polymerization-site experiments plus the a-pocket structure demonstrate molecular contacts used in fibrin assembly (PMID:6451630, Results/Figures 3–4/6; PMID:9207064, pocket Results). The structural core therefore has direct participation evidence. It does not assign the thrombin cleavage or factor-XIII crosslinking reaction to gamma. The broader structural-molecule term also covers assembly of the soluble complex; matrix-specific annotations are retained without a duplicate core based only on proteomic classification.

Human gamma preparations support platelet aggregation (PMID:6281794, Tables 1–2/Figure 1), but the isolated chains form multimers. The assays do not compare natural gamma-A and gamma-B or establish a soluble monomer as a platelet agonist. Official product identities are retained exactly: displayed gamma-B/gamma-prime is 453 residues; the alternative gamma-A C terminus yields 437 residues. Historical mature-chain numbering, precursor numbering, experimental fragments and pathological splice constructs must remain distinct.

Gamma117–133 recognition of ICAM-1 is supported by the positive PMID:7822297 abstract and independently read adhesion/survival experiments in PMID:10903502. This helps interpret receptor-triggered effects of intact fibrinogen. Human fibrinogen or multichain fragment D in rat vascular preparations (PMID:15739255) and rat heart endothelial cells (PMID:19193866) support contextual constriction, ERK and secretory responses. These are not demonstrations of isolated gamma hormone activity, gamma kinase activity or a gamma vesicle-fusion mechanism.

Fibrin-chain surface labeling/calcium-dependent retention is real in human platelets (PMID:6777381, Figure 3/Results). Granule cargo wording is not a gamma-specific intragranular image. Exact human tissue proteomic joins support ECM association; a urinary fraction contains a low-count FGG identification. Neither enrichment nor database protein naming supplies new mechanical, topology or splice-specific claims.
