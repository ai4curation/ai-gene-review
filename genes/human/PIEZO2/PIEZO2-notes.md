# PIEZO2 curation notes

## 2026-11-27

Deep research via Falcon with the Perplexity Lite fallback was attempted, but
both providers were unavailable in this checkout. The review was assembled
manually from GOA, UniProt, the mouse UniProt record that donates the ISS
rows, and primary cached papers.

PIEZO2 is a direct mechanotransduction channel. Coste et al. 2010 cloned mouse
Piezo2 from dorsal-root-ganglion neurons and found that heterologous Piezo2
drives mechanically activated nonselective cation currents in N2A and HEK293T
cells [PMID:20813920 "N2A and HEK293T cells transfected with Piezo2 and gene
encoding GFP showed large MA currents"]. The same paper placed endogenous
Piezo2 in mouse DRG neurons and showed that Piezo2 siRNA selectively reduced
rapidly adapting mechanically activated currents [PMID:20813920 "specifically
and significantly reduced in neurons transfected with Piezo2 siRNA"].

The transferred mouse sensory rows are supported by several native-cell
experiments:

- Woo et al. 2014 found that Piezo2 is expressed in mouse Merkel cells and that
  epithelial Piezo2 loss abolishes their poking-evoked mechanically activated
  currents [PMID:24717433 "none of Piezo2-deficient Merkel cells (n=13)
  showed MA currents"].
- Ranade et al. 2014 localized Piezo2 to a broad set of low-threshold
  mechanoreceptor endings and showed that deleting Piezo2 from adult sensory
  neurons and Merkel cells removes most rapidly adapting DRG mechanically
  activated currents and severely impairs light-touch behavior
  [PMID:25471886 "mice lacking Piezo2 in both adult sensory neurons and
  Merkel cells exhibit a profound loss of touch sensation"].
- Woo et al. 2015 found Piezo2 at muscle-spindle and Golgi-tendon-organ
  proprioceptive endings and showed that Piezo2-deficient proprioceptive
  neurons mostly lose rapidly adapting mechanically activated currents and
  stretch-evoked muscle-afferent activity [PMID:26551544 "a dramatic decrease
  in stretch-sensitive neuronal activity in proprioceptive muscle afferents ex
  vivo"].

The renal blood-flow row is newer and context-specific. Hill et al. 2025
localized Piezo2 to mouse juxtaglomerular and mesangial cells, showed that
cells of the renin lineage have mechanically activated currents, and found that
Piezo2 loss dysregulates JG-cell calcium oscillations and renin
[PMID:41349545 "PIEZO2 is expressed in renin-producing juxtaglomerular granular
cells"].
This supports a kidney baroreceptor context, but it is not the conserved core
somatosensory function.

The hair-cell stereocilium and cuticular-plate rows come from Lee et al. 2024,
which reported Pz1/Pz2 signal at stereocilia and cuticular plate membranes and
argued that Piezo channels may be components of the hair-cell MET complex
[PMID:38228630 "co-localize at the tips and sides of stereocilia and cuticular
plate membranes"]. This is retained as a non-core compartment because mouse
Piezo2's dominant force-sensing biology remains somatosensory and
proprioceptive.
