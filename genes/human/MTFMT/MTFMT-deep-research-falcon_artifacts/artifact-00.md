| Feature | Experimental finding | Evidence/interpretive limit |
|---|---|---|
| Enzyme identity and reaction | Human MTFMT (Q96DP5) is the mitochondrial methionyl-tRNA formyltransferase. Purified recombinant enzyme catalysed **Met-tRNA^Met + 10-formyl-THF → fMet-tRNA^Met + THF**, transferring the formyl group to the aminoacylated tRNA—not to an existing protein. [Sinha et al., 2014](https://doi.org/10.1074/jbc.M114.610626) (pqac-00000004, pqac-00000009) | **Direct human-enzyme biochemistry.** Fmt-family assignment is also supported by conserved catalytic/FTHF-binding regions and homology to structurally characterized bacterial MTF; the detailed structural model is homology-based rather than a solved human MTFMT structure. (pqac-00000029, pqac-00000035) |
| Substrate specificity | Metazoan mitochondria use a **single mt-tRNA^Met** for initiation and elongation. MTFMT preferentially recognizes methionine attached to that tRNA plus the **purine-11:pyrimidine-24 base pair in its D stem**; formylated fMet-tRNA^Met is directed toward initiation, whereas unformylated Met-tRNA^Met can enter elongation. [Sinha et al., 2014](https://doi.org/10.1074/jbc.M114.610626) (pqac-00000002, pqac-00000015, pqac-00000035) | **Biochemistry plus comparative structure/function evidence.** The human enzyme relies on fewer tRNA identity elements than bacterial MTF, so bacterial substrate-recognition rules should not be transferred wholesale to human MTFMT. (pqac-00000035) |
| Cellular phenotype and causality | In fibroblasts from two index cases (P1/P2), northern analysis found **no detectable steady-state fMet-tRNA^Met** and predominantly unformylated Met-tRNA^Met, yet mass spectrometry showed formylated COX1 remained the dominant product, indicating low residual MTFMT activity. Wild-type MTFMT cDNA restored COX2 and NDUFB8 abundance, supporting causality. [Tucker et al., 2011](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cmet.2011.07.010) (pqac-00000012, pqac-00000013, pqac-00000014, pqac-00000020, pqac-00000049) | **Direct patient-cell evidence with genetic complementation.** “Undetectable” means below the northern-blot detection limit, not complete absence; dominant formyl-COX1 demonstrates that residual formylation persisted. The original mechanistic cohort comprised two unrelated index patients, so it does not define population prevalence. |
| Newer exploratory extension | In 2024, CRISPR MTFMT knockout in murine tumor cells reduced in-vivo growth in immunocompetent mice without a significant intrinsic in-vitro growth or respiration defect. PMN-MDSCs comprised **10.3 ± 0.81%** of WT versus **1.7 ± 0.33%** of knockout tumors (**p = 0.003**), suggesting mitochondrial formyl peptides can modify antitumor immunity. [Waki et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1111/cas.16266) (pqac-00000022, pqac-00000030, pqac-00000037, pqac-00000039) | **Exploratory murine cancer evidence only.** Experiments used edited mouse cell lines, small groups (typically n = 6), and pooled tissues in some immune assays; clone effects and tumor-size normalization complicate interpretation. No established human cancer relevance, clinical application, or validated MTFMT-targeted therapy follows from this study. |
