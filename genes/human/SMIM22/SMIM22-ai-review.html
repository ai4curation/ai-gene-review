Gene Description

SMIM22, also called CASIMO1 (cancer-associated small integral membrane open reading frame 1), is an 83-amino-acid microprotein (about 10 kDa) encoded by a short open reading frame (sORF). It has a single predicted transmembrane helix (residues 32-52), followed by a cysteine-rich juxtamembrane stretch and a short, charged, disordered C-terminal tail. A splice isoform inserts five residues into this tail. The protein is membrane-associated; it partly overlaps with LAMP1-positive late endosomes and co-purifies with the ER membrane proteins calnexin and DDOST. Its best supported partner is squalene epoxidase (SQLE), the ER enzyme that catalyses the first oxygenation step of sterol synthesis. Raising SMIM22 levels increases SQLE protein without changing SQLE mRNA and causes lipid droplets to cluster. Lowering SMIM22 reduces SQLE protein and ERK phosphorylation. SMIM22 RNA is elevated in hormone receptor-positive breast tumours. In breast cancer cell lines, knockdown slows proliferation with a G0/G1 arrest, disorganises the actin cytoskeleton and reduces cell motility. SQLE knockdown reproduces this phenotype and SQLE overexpression rescues it, which places SQLE downstream of SMIM22. A non-translatable mutant does not reproduce the overexpression phenotype, so these effects are attributed to the protein rather than to the RNA. A single later study in lung cancer cells linked SMIM22 to glycolysis and proliferation. Whether SMIM22 has a role in non-transformed cells is unknown.

Core Functions

SMIM22/CASIMO1 is a single-pass membrane microprotein that binds the sterol-synthesis enzyme squalene epoxidase (SQLE) and maintains SQLE protein levels. Higher SMIM22 increases SQLE protein without changing SQLE mRNA; lower SMIM22 decreases SQLE protein and downstream ERK phosphorylation. In breast cancer cells this SQLE-dependent effect supports proliferation, cell motility and lipid droplet organisation. Whether SMIM22 stabilises SQLE against degradation or acts by another post-transcriptional route has not been resolved.

Suggested Questions for Experts

Q: Does SMIM22 stabilise SQLE against degradation? SQLE turnover is controlled by cholesterol-induced, MARCHF6-dependent ER-associated degradation. If SMIM22 shields SQLE from MARCHF6, protein stabilization (GO:0050821) or a negative regulation of ERAD-type term would be justified. The abstract shows only steady-state protein accumulation.

Suggested experts: Sven Diederichs, Andrew J Brown

Q: Where does SMIM22 reside at steady state: ER, lipid droplet surface, or late endosome? Its partners (SQLE, CANX, DDOST) are ER proteins, yet the only localisation recorded in UniProt is a partial late-endosome overlap.

Suggested experts: Sven Diederichs

Q: Does SMIM22 have a function outside transformed cells, for example in normal mammary epithelium or in tissues where it is highly expressed?

Suggested Experiments

Experiment: Measure SQLE half-life by cycloheximide chase in SMIM22-knockdown and SMIM22-overexpressing cells, under low and high sterol conditions and with or without MARCHF6 depletion. Test whether a SQLE-binding-deficient SMIM22 mutant (for example, with the TM helix or cysteine-rich juxtamembrane motif altered) loses the effect.

Hypothesis: SMIM22 extends the half-life of SQLE by protecting it from MARCHF6-mediated ERAD.

Experiment: Map the subcellular distribution of endogenous or endogenously tagged SMIM22 by co-staining with ER, lipid droplet and LAMP1 markers, and determine its membrane topology by protease protection.

Hypothesis: SMIM22 is an ER-resident single-pass membrane protein.

SMIM22 (CASIMO1) review notes

2026-10-03 — initial review (claude-code, MICROPROTEINS Tier 2)

Identity

  • UniProt K7EJ46, 83 aa (isoform 3, displayed); isoform 2 (VSP_060203) inserts 5 residues
    after R69 (R -> RKVSPW). PE1. One predicted helical TM segment (residues 32-52,
    ECO:0000255); C-terminal disordered, basic/acidic region 60-83. Cys-rich juxtamembrane
    stretch (VAHCCCCSS) just after the TM helix.
  • Canonical gene symbol SMIM22 (HGNC:48329), not an alternative ORF of another protein-coding
    host, so the standard genes/human/SMIM22/ folder is correct.
  • Alias CASIMO1, "Cancer-Associated Small Integral Membrane Open reading frame 1"
    PMID:29765154.

Literature

PubMed search "CASIMO1 OR SMIM22" (2026-10-03) returns 7 records. Only two study the protein:

  1. PMID:29765154 (Polycarpou-Schwarz et al., Oncogene 2018) — discovery and sole source of all
    GOA experimental rows. Abstract only in cache; not OA (Unpaywall/EuropePMC: no free copy).
  2. Overexpressed in hormone-receptor-positive breast tumours PMID:29765154.
  3. Knockdown reduces proliferation PMID:29765154.
  4. Loss disturbs actin, motility, causes G0/G1 arrest PMID:29765154.
  5. Protein (not RNA) responsible PMID:29765154.
  6. Interacts with SQLE PMID:29765154.
  7. Raises SQLE protein, not mRNA; LD clustering; knockdown lowers SQLE and pERK PMID:29765154.
  8. SQLE epistasis PMID:29765154.
  9. UniProt (from full text, which I cannot see): interacts with CANX and DDOST; partially
    co-localises with LAMP1 in late endosome. The IPI row lists CANX (P27824), DDOST (P39656)
    and SQLE (Q14534). CANX and DDOST are abundant ER-membrane proteins (calnexin, OST
    subunit) — common co-purifying partners of any ER-inserted TM protein.
  10. PMID:39215037 (Zhao et al., NPJ Precis Oncol 2024) — NSCLC, NORAD/miR-520g-3p/SMIM22/GALE
    axis. Full text. Claims SMIM22 overexpression raises glycolysis and proliferation, co-IP with
    GALE, and nuclear co-localisation PMID:39215037.
    The nuclear localisation of a single-pass TM protein is implausible and not explained;
    the paper never tests the protein vs RNA distinction. Treated as LOW_QUALITY, used only as
    corroboration of a proliferation phenotype.

Other hits (ALDH7A1 methylation, Li-7 HCC gene set, TiO2 lncRNA profile, prostate cancer
recurrence gene set, a 2026 microprotein review) are expression-list mentions only; not cached.

Assessment

  • Mechanistic picture is thin: one study, one interaction (SQLE) with epistasis, protein-level
    effect on SQLE (stabilisation vs translation not distinguished in abstract).
  • All BP rows are cancer-cell-line knockdown phenotypes; plausibly downstream of SQLE /
    cholesterol synthesis / ERK signalling. Keep as non-core.
  • No GO "regulation of lipid droplet formation" term exists (QuickGO search 2026-10-03), so the
    lipid droplet formation row cannot be refined to a regulation term; keep as non-core.
  • protein binding IPI: modify to enzyme binding (GO:0019899) on the strength of SQLE, which is
    the functionally validated partner (epistasis). CANX/DDOST hits are not informative.
  • No NEW annotation: protein stabilisation (GO:0050821) toward SQLE is tempting but the
    abstract does not show a half-life experiment; left as a question.
  • Subcellular localisation: UniProt says late endosome (partial LAMP1 overlap); ER is
    predicted by CANX/DDOST association and by SQLE partner (SQLE is ER-resident), but no
    ER location row exists and I cannot verify from the abstract — not added.

    enzyme squalene epoxidase (SQLE) and maintains SQLE protein levels. Higher SMIM22
    increases SQLE protein without changing SQLE mRNA; lower SMIM22 decreases SQLE protein
    and downstream ERK phosphorylation. In breast cancer cells this SQLE-dependent effect
    supports proliferation, cell motility and lipid droplet organisation. Whether SMIM22
    stabilises SQLE against degradation or acts by another post-transcriptional route has
    not been resolved.

suggested_questions:
- question: >-
    Does SMIM22 stabilise SQLE against degradation? SQLE turnover is controlled by
    cholesterol-induced, MARCHF6-dependent ER-associated degradation. If SMIM22 shields SQLE
    from MARCHF6, protein stabilization (GO:0050821) or a negative regulation of
    ERAD-type term would be justified. The abstract shows only steady-state protein
    accumulation.
