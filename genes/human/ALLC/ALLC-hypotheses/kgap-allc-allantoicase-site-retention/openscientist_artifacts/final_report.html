ALLC (Q8N6M5): does human allantoicase retain catalytic machinery?

Hypothesis slug: kgap-allc-allantoicase-site-retention Source: genes/human/ALLC/ALLC-ai-review.yaml, selector free-text Gene: human ALLC, ENSG00000151360, ENSP00000252505, UniProt Q8N6M5, 391 aa Focus type: free_text Iterations completed: 3. Findings recorded: 6. Papers reviewed: 23.

Every number in this report comes from an analysis run in this job or from a cited abstract. Executed code and raw outputs are saved under provenance/. Resources that could not be reached are named as such, and no value was substituted for them.

Summary

Human ALLC keeps the allantoicase active site. Of the 42 residues that form the two conserved surface patches of the yeast enzyme, 40 are identical in human, and after per-domain superposition of the AlphaFold model onto the yeast crystal structure their side chains sit a median of 0.49 Å from the yeast positions. Those positions evolve about 4.4-fold slower than the rest of the protein across 84 mammalian orthologues (pooled dN/dS 0.043 against 0.190 in a divergence-matched window, Fisher OR 0.262). The depletion survives matching on solvent exposure, so fold stability alone does not account for it. Mammalian ALLC is not a freely drifting relic at the level of the pocket.

The comparison the seed hypothesis asked for, mammals against species that still degrade urate, returns a null for the pocket as a whole. In a matched divergence window the pocket is strongly constrained in both clades: pooled dN/dS 0.043 in 627 mammal pairs and 0.028 in 193 ray-finned fish pairs, against backgrounds of 0.190 and 0.238. Relative constraint is nominally weaker in mammals (0.23 against 0.13), but a bootstrap over phylogenetically independent species pairs puts the difference at 0.103 with 95 % CI -0.061 to 0.260 (p = 0.224). That difference cannot be claimed. What does survive multiple testing is local: exactly 4 of the 42 pocket positions are less retained in mammals than in fish and none in the reverse direction (yeast T102, N108, E72, M230), with human carrying the derived state at two of them, V79 and D85.

Catalytic competence still cannot be asserted, for three reasons that emerged during the investigation. The yeast active site is composite: 20 of the 42 patch residues contact a neighbouring subunit in the D3 hexamer, and patch 1 of one chain packs against patch 2 of its neighbour, so a monomeric AlphaFold model cannot show that the site is assembled. In mammals the non-patch hexamer interface evolves 2.3-fold faster than an exposure-matched control set (0.355 against 0.156, Fisher OR 1.83, p = 3.3e-29), so the surface that would have to bring two pockets together is the part of the protein that is least conserved. And human ALLC is loss-of-function tolerant: gnomAD v4 gives oe_lof 0.889 (90 % CI 0.691-1.157) with five homozygous truncation carriers, and transcription is confined to testis, with the strongest signal in early spermatids. No mammalian ALLC protein has ever been assayed against allantoate or anything else.

Executive judgment

Verdict: partially supported.

The seed hypothesis has two halves and they resolve differently.

Supported. The structural and evolutionary half. The pocket is present, geometrically assembled in the model, and under strong purifying selection across mammals. The hypothesis framed this as the discriminator between "retained or repurposed" and "relic", and on that framing the data choose retention.

Not supported. The inference from pocket conservation to predicted catalytic competence. The hypothesis assumed a monomeric pocket could be assessed for assembly. The allantoicase site is formed across a subunit interface, that interface is the fastest evolving functional surface in the mammalian protein, and the human gene is dispensable in human populations.

Null. The specific cross-clade test the hypothesis prescribed. Mammals do not show statistically supported relaxation of the pocket relative to urate-degrading fish once divergence is matched and phylogenetic non-independence is removed.

The honest summary for a curator is that ALLC in mammals is a conserved pocket with four eroded positions, built into a protein whose oligomerisation surface is decaying, expressed only in spermatids, and dispensable in humans. "Relic" is too strong, because relics drift and this one does not. "Catalytically competent allantoicase" is also too strong, because no assay exists, the upstream substrate supply is gone, and the site needs an oligomer that mammals appear to be dismantling.

Most important caveats. No enzyme assay of any mammalian ALLC protein exists in the literature. The active site was defined computationally in this job, because neither PDB 1SG3 nor 1O59 carries SITE records or a bound ligand, so the residue set is inferred from invariance plus 3D clustering rather than read from a curated annotation. All dN/dS here is pairwise Nei-Gojobori, not a branch-site or RELAX model; HyPhy was not available in this executor. The all-pairs p-values are inflated by phylogenetic non-independence and should be read alongside the independent-pairs bootstrap.

Key findings

F1. Human ALLC retains both conserved allantoicase surface patches with intact geometry

The only structural work on the allantoicase family is the yeast enzyme (Crystal structure of yeast allantoicase reveals a repeated jelly roll motif, PMID: 15020593, PDB 1SG3 at 2.6 Å and 1O59 at 2.4 Å). Neither entry carries SITE records and neither has a bound ligand, both verified by parsing the files, so there was no curated active site to transfer. The site was reconstructed de novo.

Aligning 12 allantoicases from organisms that degrade urate (S. cerevisiae P25335, N. crassa P18407, S. pombe Q09913, D. discoideum Q54VL5, D. rerio Q6DGA6, X. laevis Q9W6S5, X. tropicalis Q640T1 and five bacterial enzymes) gave 51 positions invariant across all 12. Single-linkage clustering of their side-chain centroids in 1SG3 chain A at a 9 Å cutoff yields exactly two clusters and nine scattered singletons or pairs. Cluster 1 holds 19 residues on jelly-roll 1 (D39, F42, D69, G70, W71, E72, R75, D82, D101, T102, F105, G107, N108, P171, D172, G173, G174, A176, R177). Cluster 2 holds 23 on jelly-roll 2 (D197, G225, M230, D232, G233, W234, E235, T236, R238, G243, L252, D265, T266, G271, N272, W297, H324, P331, D332, G333, G334, R337, R339). This reproduces, from conservation and geometry alone, the description in the crystallography paper of "two totally conserved surface patches, one on each jelly roll motif".

Human ALLC matches 40 of the 42 patch residues. The two substitutions are T102V, buried with 0.6 Å² of solvent-accessible surface in the human model, and N108D, a surface position with 61.6 Å² and a conservative charge change. Superposing AF-Q8N6M5-F1 v6 (mean pLDDT 91.9, 85 % of residues above 90) on 1SG3 chain A domain by domain gives CA RMSD 1.17 Å over 118 core residues for jelly-roll 1 and 0.91 Å over 129 for jelly-roll 2. Across the 42 patch residues the median CA deviation is 0.42 Å and the median side-chain centroid deviation 0.49 Å. Only two patch residues deviate materially, G243/G258 at 3.2 Å and W297/W310 at 10.7 Å, both in loops where the alignment contains a vertebrate-specific insertion, so these are alignment-assignment artefacts rather than structural loss.

F2. The pocket is under strong purifying selection across mammals, beyond the rest of the protein

Nei-Gojobori dN/dS was computed from Ensembl Compara codon alignments of human ALLC against 84 one-to-one mammalian orthologues. Ensembl's own dn_ds field is null for this gene tree, so NG86 with Jukes-Cantor correction was implemented locally.

Gene-wide mammalian dN/dS has median 0.143 (IQR 0.129-0.176, n = 83 with finite estimates), which is strong purifying selection on the whole protein, not pseudogene-like drift. Partitioning codons into the 42 pocket positions and the other 349 gives pooled dN/dS 0.042 for the pocket against 0.161 for the rest, on essentially identical synonymous divergence (pS 0.422 against 0.465). Fisher exact test on pooled nonsynonymous and synonymous difference counts, [[206, 930], [5527, 7140]], gives OR 0.29 and p = 1.9e-64. A paired Wilcoxon across the 67 deep comparisons with dS > 0.15 gives statistic 0 and p = 1.1e-12, with median 0.035 against 0.152 and the pocket value lower in all 67 pairs.

Burial does not explain this. Restricting to solvent-exposed residues (Shrake-Rupley SASA ≥ 40 Å² on the AlphaFold model), exposed pocket residues (n = 12) have dN/dS 0.076 against 0.170 for exposed non-pocket residues (n = 192), Fisher OR 0.38, p = 3.9e-15. Buried pocket residues (n = 24) give 0.030 against 0.140 for buried non-pocket residues (n = 117), OR 0.33, p = 5.9e-27. Exposed pocket residues have no packing role, and they are constrained 2.2-fold more than other exposed residues.

F3. The cross-clade comparison is a null for the pocket as a whole, with four mammal-specific eroded positions

The seed hypothesis prescribed a comparison with species that retain urate degradation. The first attempt, using zebrafish allc as a single anchor, yielded only 5 usable teleost pairs after filtering for unsaturated dS, which was too few to conclude anything. Iteration 3 replaced it with a symmetric design.

Codon-coordinate matrices were built from Ensembl Compara pairwise cDNA alignments anchored on human ALLC (391 codons, 164 orthologues) and on zebrafish allc (395 codons, 164 orthologues). The Ensembl zebrafish CDS translates exactly to UniProt Q6DGA6, 0/395 mismatches. All 42 pocket positions map to zebrafish and all 42 carry the yeast residue there, against 40/42 in human. All within-clade species pairs were then computed: 3394 mammal pairs across 84 taxa and 1538 ray-finned fish pairs across 63 taxa.

Restricting both clades to a matched unsaturated window of dS 0.05-0.60 leaves 627 mammal and 193 fish pairs with median dS 0.464 against 0.496 (Mann-Whitney p = 0.082), so divergence is comparable.

F4. The active site is a composite inter-subunit pocket, and the oligomer interface is not conserved in mammals

This finding is what blocks the inference from F1 and F2 to catalytic competence.

The author-defined hexameric assembly of 1SG3 was parsed: D3 symmetry, homo 6-mer, stoichiometry A6, three MODEL records of two chains. In chain A, 60 residues contact another subunit within 4.5 Å, and 20 of the 42 conserved patch residues are among them. The contacts are reciprocal between the two patches: patch-1 residues of one subunit (F42, E72, R75, G107, N108, P171, D172, G173) contact patch-2 residues of a neighbouring subunit (E235, T236, R237, R238, G271, N272, D332, G333). The conserved pocket is completed only on assembly, which is what the crystallography paper meant by hexameric packing concentrating the patches into pockets.

Mapping the 60 interface positions onto human ALLC by structure-guided alignment gives good correspondence: median CA deviation 0.58 Å after per-domain superposition for the 40 non-patch interface residues, with only 7 % exceeding 3 Å.

The pattern holds separately in primates (interface 0.421 against control 0.194, pocket 0.073) and in non-primate mammals (interface 0.331 against control 0.138, pocket 0.035).

Two readings fit. The mammalian protein may assemble through a different, lineage-specific interface, in which case the yeast-transferred interface definition is wrong and the elevated dN/dS is an artefact of that transfer. Or the composite catalytic site is no longer maintained and the conserved patches are preserved for a monomeric binding role. These are not distinguishable from the data available here. Either way, predicted catalytic competence cannot be asserted from pocket residue retention alone.

F5. Human ALLC is loss-of-function tolerant and transcribed almost only in spermatids

gnomAD v4 constraint for ALLC gives observed/expected LoF 0.889 with 90 % CI 0.691-1.157, 40 observed against 45.0 expected LoF alleles, and pLI 9.2e-13. Missense is equally unconstrained: oe_mis 0.990, mis_z 0.099. The upper CI bound of 1.16 excludes any appreciable LoF depletion.

Querying variants directly returned 128 pLoF-class alleles. High-confidence examples segregate at appreciable frequency with homozygotes present: p.Trp161Ter at AF 1.11e-3 with 2 homozygotes, p.Arg89Ter at AF 1.66e-4 with 1 homozygote, p.Glu194AsnfsTer5 at AF 1.31e-4 with 2 homozygotes. p.Trp234Ter also segregates and truncates at W234, one of the invariant pocket residues.

Human Protein Atlas gives ALLC as tissue enriched, detected in a single tissue, with testis at 15.6 nTPM and single-cell enrichment in early spermatids (155.0 nCPM) and late primary spermatocytes (95.9 nCPM). HPA's own protein evidence level is "Evidence at transcript level". UniProt's PE = 1 rests on the keyword "Proteomics identification" with PeptideAtlas, MassIVE, ProteomicsDB and PaxDb cross-references, and none of the six references in the entry is a targeted protein or antibody study. A GTEx median-expression query for ENSG00000151360 returned no rows from either the v10 or v8 endpoint, so GTEx was not used and no substitute value was invented.

The testis-restricted expression has a direct consequence for the historical record: classical mammalian allantoicase activity assays were done on liver and kidney, which is not where the human transcript is.

F6. The UniProt "probable inactive allantoicase" call is an inference, not a measurement

UniProt Q8N6M5 is titled "Probable inactive allantoicase" with the FUNCTION comment "The function of this enzyme is unclear as allantoicase activity is not known to exist in mammals" (ECO:0000305, cited to PMID: 12036579). The same designation is applied to mouse Q9JHX6, rat Q6AYP0, bovine Q2KIG4 and macaque Q4R964, so one inference propagates across the mammalian clade and a curator reading five entries may see five assessments where there is one.

12036579 is a gene-structure paper reporting 11 exons in mouse and human ALLC. Its only functional statement is the background assertion that allantoicase activity is absent in mammals, birds and reptiles, with no assay of the cloned product. The earlier cDNA paper (PMID: 11054555) reported a human transcript whose 5' end lacked a continuous ORF and attributed this to incorrect splicing; the current reference sequence is a complete 391-residue ORF.

Activity has been measured directly only for the Xenopus enzyme (PMID: 10864446: Km 8.44 mM, Vmax 6.94 µmol/min/mg in kidney) and for fungal enzymes. No published assay of recombinant human or mouse ALLC against allantoate or any other substrate was found in PubMed. The neighbouring pathway gene URAD has now been expressed and assayed (PMID: 41956358), giving a well-folded protein with reduced but non-zero activity, which shows the experiment is tractable and that "pathway lost" does not imply "protein non-functional".

Mechanistic model and interpretation

The direct molecular activity at issue is hydrolysis of allantoate to (S)-ureidoglycolate plus urea, EC 3.5.3.4, by a protein built from two jelly-roll repeats whose active site is formed where a conserved patch on jelly-roll 1 of one subunit meets a conserved patch on jelly-roll 2 of a neighbouring subunit in a D3 hexamer.

The findings assemble into a sequence of events with two timescales that disagree.

Over roughly 90 to 100 Myr of mammalian evolution the pocket has been held at dN/dS 0.042, about 4.4-fold below the rest of the protein, while the surface that assembles it into a functional site has run at 0.355, about 2.3-fold above exposure-matched controls. Within human populations, over roughly 10⁵ years, there is no detectable constraint at all: oe_lof 0.889, oe_mis 0.990, five homozygous truncation carriers. The straightforward reconciliation is lineage-specific relaxation, with the pocket maintained through most of mammalian history and the constraint weakening recently. The primate against non-primate split is consistent in direction (whole protein 0.218 against 0.140; pocket 0.073 against 0.035) but is a descriptive split, not a fitted relaxation test.

The pattern of a conserved pocket with a small number of eroded positions on a decaying oligomer surface is what a pseudoenzyme with a retained binding role looks like, as in CA VIII (PMID: 42268453) and c-FLIP (PMID: 42237682). It is also what an enzyme with altered substrate specificity looks like. Nothing in this analysis distinguishes those two, and both are compatible with the 42-residue constraint signal.

Review free text none Candidate note: the human protein retains the Pfam PF03561 allantoicase repeat fold and 40 of 42 residues of the conserved pocket, with side chains superposing on the yeast enzyme to a median of 0.49 Å, and those positions are under purifying selection across mammals (dN/dS 0.042 against 0.161 for the rest of the protein). The site is composite across the hexamer interface, and that interface is relaxed in mammals (0.355 against 0.156 matched control). This is the defensible claim. It is structural and evolutionary, it is computed and reproducible, and it does not assert an activity.

Net recommendation to the curator. ALLC should carry no asserted molecular function for human at present. The review text should state that the allantoicase fold and pocket are retained under purifying selection in mammals, that the ancestral activity has never been assayed in any mammal, and that the composite inter-subunit nature of the site means a monomer model cannot settle the question.

Mechanistic scope

Direct molecular activity being tested. Hydrolysis of allantoate to (S)-ureidoglycolate plus urea, EC 3.5.3.4, by a protein built from two jelly-roll repeats whose active site forms where a conserved patch on jelly-roll 1 of one subunit meets a conserved patch on jelly-roll 2 of a neighbouring subunit in a D3 hexamer.

What was tested here. Residue identity against 12 active allantoicases, side-chain geometry in an AlphaFold monomer against the yeast crystal structure, per-site selective constraint across 84 mammals and 63 ray-finned fish, the subunit-contact status of each pocket residue in the yeast hexamer, the selective constraint on that interface in mammals, human population constraint, and tissue and single-cell expression.

What was not tested and is not claimed. Catalysis. No substrate turnover, no binding measurement, no thermal stability, no oligomeric state determination for any mammalian ALLC. Nothing in this report measures an enzyme.

Separated from the direct activity. The testis and spermatid expression pattern is a context, not a function. The gnomAD LoF tolerance is a population-level statement about dispensability in humans and does not bear on whether the purified protein can turn over allantoate. The FEV₁ association (24792382) and the stroke-outcome DMR (38845005) are downstream statistical associations with no mechanistic link and should not enter the review as functional support. The loss of urate oxidase upstream is a pathway fact about substrate supply, not a property of the ALLC protein.

Conflicts and alternatives

Two timescales disagree. Across mammals the pocket is strongly constrained (dN/dS 0.042, about 4.4-fold below background, over roughly 100 Myr). Within humans there is no detectable constraint at all (oe_lof 0.889, oe_mis 0.990), with five homozygous truncation carriers in gnomAD. The straightforward reconciliation is lineage-specific relaxation, with the pocket maintained through most of mammalian history and the constraint weakening recently. The primate against non-primate split is consistent in direction (whole protein 0.218 against 0.140; pocket 0.073 against 0.035) but is descriptive, not a fitted relaxation test.

The clade comparison the hypothesis proposed returns a null. The pocket is as constrained in mammals as in fish once divergence is matched and non-independence removed. A curator should not be told that mammals show relaxed selection on the active site. The supported statement is narrower: four specific positions eroded in mammals and not in fish.

The interface result cuts against the structural result. The same analysis that shows the pocket is conserved shows the surface that assembles two pockets into one site is evolving 2.3-fold faster than matched controls. One reading is that mammals retain the pocket for a monomeric or non-catalytic reason while the oligomer decays. Another is that the mammalian oligomer uses a different interface, in which case the yeast-transferred interface definition is wrong and the elevated dN/dS is an artefact of transferring it. These are not distinguishable from the data available here.

Database carry-over. UniProt titles Q8N6M5 "Probable inactive allantoicase" and applies the same title to mouse, rat, bovine and macaque orthologues, all tracing to the single background sentence in 12036579. The same propagation risk runs in the opposite direction for any IEA allantoicase-activity annotation derived from PF03561.

Isoform and protein-existence uncertainty. 12036579 describes a second transcript lacking exon 1, and 11054555 reported a 5' region without a continuous ORF that the authors attributed to incorrect splicing. Whatever protein is detected in testis proteomics has not been shown to be the full 391-residue form. UniProt PE = 1 rests only on the "Proteomics identification" keyword.

Paralog and model-organism confusion. The Dictyostelium allC RNAi work (PMID: 26662428) reports developmental and cell-cycle phenotypes and a secondary zinc protease, in an organism that still runs the full urate pathway. It should not be used as evidence for a human ALLC function.

Alternative hypothesis worth stating. ALLC may be a spermatid pseudoenzyme that keeps its pocket for a non-catalytic reason such as small-molecule binding or scaffolding, which is the pattern in CA VIII (42268453) and c-FLIP (42237682). The data here are compatible with that and do not test it.

Discriminating tests and proposed follow-up

Ranked by how much they separate the alternatives per unit of effort.

  1. Recombinant human ALLC activity assay against allantoate. This directly decides "catalytically competent" against "pseudoenzyme". Include Xenopus or zebrafish allantoicase as a positive control and measure at high substrate concentration, since the Xenopus Km is 8.44 mM (10864446). A negative result at saturating allantoate converts the recommendation from "unmeasured" to "supported absence" and would justify a NOT qualifier.
  2. Oligomeric state by SEC-MALS or native MS, plus an AlphaFold-Multimer or AF3 prediction of the human hexamer. If human ALLC is monomeric, or hexameric on a different interface, the composite site is not assembled and the conservation result in F2 must be explained by something other than catalysis.
  3. RELAX (HyPhy) with mammals as test and ray-finned fish as reference, on the 42-codon pocket partition against the rest. This is the properly fitted version of the comparison attempted in F3, and it would convert the null bootstrap result into either a supported relaxation or a supported absence of relaxation.
  4. Back-to-ancestor mutagenesis. Revert human V79 and D85 to the ancestral T and N (yeast T102 and N108) and assay. These are the two positions where human carries the derived state at sites invariant in fish, and T102 is buried in the pocket with 0.6 Å² of accessible surface. If reversion restores activity, the mammalian protein is one or two substitutions from the ancestral chemistry.
  5. Broader substrate panel. Assay human ALLC against related linear amidines and ureido compounds, not only allantoate. A pocket kept under selection with four eroded positions fits altered specificity as well as lost activity.
  6. Spermatid-stage proteomics and mouse knockout fertility phenotyping. This establishes whether a full-length protein exists where the mRNA is, and whether its loss costs anything, which is the biological counterpart of the gnomAD result.
  7. Amphibian codon set. Assemble ALLC CDSs from amphibian genomes outside Ensembl and repeat the matched-window analysis, since amphibians are the vertebrates with directly measured allantoicase activity.
