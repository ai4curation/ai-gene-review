# TEAD2 manual research notes

TEAD2 is one of the four human TEA-domain transcription factors that bind MCAT/GT-IIC-related cis-regulatory DNA through an N-terminal TEA domain and recruit Hippo-regulated transcriptional coactivators through a C-terminal YAP-binding domain. The original TEAD2/TEF4 cloning paper identified a developmentally regulated mammalian TEF family member [PMID:8702974]. Genome-scale and reporter work later showed that TEAD2, TEAD3, and TEAD4 are strongly activated by YAP, that YAP S94 is needed for TEAD binding and target-gene induction, and that TEAD factors are the DNA-binding partners that mediate YAP-dependent Hippo transcriptional output [PMID:18579750]. TAZ/WWTR1 likewise binds TEAD factors and depends on them for CTGF induction, cell growth, and epithelial-mesenchymal transition [PMID:19324877].

TEAD2 has direct structural evidence for YAP binding. The crystal/NMR study of the human TEAD2 YAP-binding domain showed an immunoglobulin-like beta-sandwich fold, a natively unfolded YAP TEAD-binding region that changes conformation on binding, and a conserved TEAD2 surface required for YAP binding and transcriptional activity [PMID:20368466]. These data support TEAD2 as part of a nuclear TEAD-YAP complex and support transcription coactivator binding or disordered-domain binding for YAP-specific rows.

The PTHR11834 PAINT root supports RNA polymerase II cis-regulatory DNA binding, RNA polymerase II-specific DNA-binding transcription factor activity, transcription regulator complex membership, and regulation of Pol II transcription across the TEAD family. The narrower PTN000931330 node propagates Hippo signaling to all four human TEADs from mouse/human TEAD/YAP/TAZ evidence. The PTN000931329 embryonic-organ-development row is a plausible vertebrate developmental use of TEAD transcription factors, but it is downstream of the molecular transcription-factor/coactivator mechanism.
