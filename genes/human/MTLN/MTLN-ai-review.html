Gene Description

Mitoregulin (MTLN; also MOXI, MPM, LEMP, SMIM37) is a 56-amino-acid, vertebrate-conserved microprotein translated from a short ORF in a transcript long annotated as the non-coding RNA LINC00116. It is most abundant in heart, skeletal muscle and adipose tissue and is induced during myogenic and adipogenic differentiation. The peptide has a single transmembrane helix followed by a basic C-terminal tail and resides in mitochondrial membranes, most studies placing it in the inner membrane (one study places it mainly in the outer membrane). MTLN binds cardiolipin, self-associates into a ~66 kDa, probably hexameric, complex, and co-migrates with respiratory supercomplexes, complex I assembly intermediates and ATP synthase. It associates with the mitochondrial trifunctional protein (HADHA/HADHB), CPT1B and cytochrome b5 reductase CYB5R3. Loss of MTLN reduces respiratory supercomplex levels and complex I-linked respiration, lowers fatty acid beta-oxidation, destabilises cristae and cardiolipin, and leads to triglyceride accumulation, reduced exercise capacity and impaired muscle differentiation, whereas overexpression raises respiration, membrane potential, mitochondrial Ca2+ retention and fatty acid oxidation. It has no known catalytic activity and is thought to act as a membrane-organising factor that supports respiratory supercomplex assembly or stability and enhances fatty acid oxidation.

Core Functions

MTLN is a cardiolipin-binding single-pass membrane microprotein of mitochondria that supports the assembly and/or stability of respiratory chain supercomplexes (respirasomes), thereby sustaining complex I-linked respiration. It acts structurally at the membrane level rather than as a subunit or catalytic component of the complexes.

Synthesis

The most direct molecular observation is lipid (cardiolipin) binding and membrane modulation, plus
physical association with several IMM/OMM enzyme complexes (MTP, ATP synthase, CYB5R3, CPT1B).
There is no catalytic activity. Respiratory supercomplex (respirasome) assembly/stability and
enhanced FAO are the best-supported processes; both plausibly follow from a membrane-organising,
CL-binding role. Downstream phenotypes (TG accumulation, obesity on HFD, myogenic
differentiation defects, exercise capacity) are consequences of reduced oxidative capacity.
