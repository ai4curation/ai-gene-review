Gene Description

MEF2C (myocyte enhancer factor 2C) is a MADS-box transcription factor of the MEF2 family. Its N-terminal MADS and MEF2 domains mediate homo- and heterodimerization with other MEF2 proteins and binding to A/T-rich MEF2 sites (CTA(A/T)4TAG) in promoters and enhancers of muscle, cardiac, neuronal, lymphoid and skeletal genes. The C-terminal transactivation domains activate RNA polymerase II transcription with coactivators such as p300 and MAML1. MEF2C binds partner DNA-binding factors through its DNA-binding domain: myogenic bHLH proteins (MyoD, myogenin), HAND1/HAND2, TBX5, NKX2-5, SOX10 and SMADs. These partners synergistically activate targets such as NPPA (ANF) and MYH6. Class II histone deacetylases (HDAC4/5/7/9) bind MEF2C and repress it; calcium/calmodulin-dependent kinase signalling releases them. p38 MAPK, ERK5 and skMLCK phosphorylation and SUMOylation modulate its activity. Mef2c is expressed in cardiac precursors and the second heart field, where it is a direct ISL1/GATA target. Mef2c-null mice fail to undergo heart looping, lack the right ventricle and do not express a subset of cardiac genes. MEF2C and NKX2-5 up-regulate each other. MEF2C also amplifies MyoD-driven skeletal myogenesis. Outside muscle it controls chondrocyte hypertrophy and endochondral ossification, vascular development, megakaryopoiesis and B-cell proliferation/survival after BCR stimulation. In the brain it governs cortical neuron differentiation and limits excitatory synapse number. Heterozygous loss of MEF2C causes a severe neurodevelopmental disorder (MEF2C haploinsufficiency syndrome) with intellectual disability, absent speech, hypotonia, stereotypic movements and seizures.

Molecular function

  • MADS-box/MEF2 transcription factor. It binds A/T-rich MEF2 sites as a dimer and transactivates PMID:8455629; PMID:9858528.
  • Partner DNA-binding TFs bind it through the DNA-binding domain:
  • MyoD/myogenin PMID:8548800
  • HAND1 PMID:16043483
  • HAND2 PMID:15486975
  • TBX5 PMID:19204083
  • NKX2-5 PMID:19035347
  • SOX10 PMID:21610032
  • Repression by class II HDACs PMID:10983972.
  • Coactivators: MAML1 PMID:16510869; p300, recruited after skMLCK phosphorylates T80 PMID:21556048.
  • Activated by p38 PMID:9069290. MEF2C is the kinase's substrate, not a component of the MAPK cascade.

Heart

  • Mef2c-null mice PMID:9162005.
  • Second heart field: direct ISL1/GATA target PMID:15253934.
  • Positive loop with NKX2-5 PMID:9857019. In double mutants the ventricular defect reflects differentiation, not proliferation PMID:19035347.
  • The linear heart tube still forms in nulls, so primary heart field specification is marked as an over-annotation.

description: 'MEF2C (myocyte enhancer factor 2C) is a MADS-box transcription factor of the MEF2 family. Its N-terminal
  MADS and MEF2 domains mediate homo- and heterodimerization with other MEF2 proteins and binding to A/T-rich MEF2
  sites (CTA(A/T)4TAG) in promoters and enhancers of muscle, cardiac, neuronal, lymphoid and skeletal genes. The
  C-terminal transactivation domains activate RNA polymerase II transcription with coactivators such as p300 and
  MAML1. MEF2C binds partner DNA-binding factors through its DNA-binding domain: myogenic bHLH proteins (MyoD, myogenin),
  HAND1/HAND2, TBX5, NKX2-5, SOX10 and SMADs. These partners synergistically activate targets such as NPPA (ANF)
  and MYH6. Class II histone deacetylases (HDAC4/5/7/9) bind MEF2C and repress it; calcium/calmodulin-dependent
  kinase signalling releases them. p38 MAPK, ERK5 and skMLCK phosphorylation and SUMOylation modulate its activity.
  Mef2c is expressed in cardiac precursors and the second heart field, where it is a direct ISL1/GATA target. Mef2c-null
  mice fail to undergo heart looping, lack the right ventricle and do not express a subset of cardiac genes. MEF2C
  and NKX2-5 up-regulate each other. MEF2C also amplifies MyoD-driven skeletal myogenesis. Outside muscle it controls
  chondrocyte hypertrophy and endochondral ossification, vascular development, megakaryopoiesis and B-cell proliferation/survival
  after BCR stimulation. In the brain it governs cortical neuron differentiation and limits excitatory synapse number.
  Heterozygous loss of MEF2C causes a severe neurodevelopmental disorder (MEF2C haploinsufficiency syndrome) with
  intellectual disability, absent speech, hypotonia, stereotypic movements and seizures.'
