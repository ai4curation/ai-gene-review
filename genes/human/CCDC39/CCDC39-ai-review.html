Gene Description

CCDC39 is a coiled-coil structural protein of motile ciliary and flagellar axonemes. Together with CCDC40, it forms an extended scaffold associated with the 96-nm repeat of the outer microtubule doublets, organizing the positioning and assembly of inner dynein arms, the nexin-dynein regulatory complex and radial-spoke-associated structures. Human CCDC39 deficiency disrupts this periodic organization while largely preserving outer dynein arms, producing immotile or dyskinetic respiratory cilia and impaired sperm motility. Its function in motile cilia also supports embryonic left-right patterning. Biallelic loss-of-function variants cause primary ciliary dyskinesia type 14. CCDC39 provides structural organization rather than the ATP-driven motor activity of dynein.

What the human studies establish

PMID:21131972(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21131972/) combines human respiratory-cell localization and ultrastructure with dog genetics and zebrafish perturbation. Its complete cached abstract, unique target Results/Discussion paragraphs and available Methods container were read. Human CCDC39 deficiency removes the axonemal signal, DNALI1-containing inner dynein arms and GAS8/DRC localization while preserving tested outer-arm markers. Affected males have oligoasthenospermia. Two zebrafish morpholinos disrupt laterality, southpaw expression and heart looping; human CCDC39 mRNA rescues that phenotype. The human rescue construct was amplified from HEK293T cDNA, which is distinct from directly perturbing the developing human heart. No raw microscopy, supplementary video or patient-table reanalysis was performed.

PMID:21131974(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21131974/) mainly studies CCDC40 but directly measures CCDC39 localization. Complete selected Results/Discussion and Methods were read. CCDC39 is present along control human respiratory axonemes but accumulates in the apical cytoplasm/ciliary base of CCDC40-deficient cells. Omission of primary antibodies is documented. Mouse and fish Ccdc40 experiments are not reattributed to CCDC39. The cytoplasmic staining does not resolve whether the particular mouse cytosol transfer represents a soluble pool or another associated compartment.

PMID:23255504(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23255504/) supplies direct human CCDC39 ultrastructural and high-speed video measurements. Ontology and inference boundaries

The extracellular-region IEA is traceable to GO_REF:0000108 and the left-right epithelial-cilium movement source. Official ontology links connect that process through epithelial extracellular-fluid movement and extracellular transport to an extracellular location. This explains the logical route, not a secretion experiment. The actual target scaffold is inside the membrane-enclosed axoneme.
