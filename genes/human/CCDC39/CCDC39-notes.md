## What the human studies establish

[PMID:21131972](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21131972/) combines human respiratory-cell localization and ultrastructure with dog genetics and zebrafish perturbation. Its complete cached abstract, unique target Results/Discussion paragraphs and available Methods container were read. Human CCDC39 deficiency removes the axonemal signal, DNALI1-containing inner dynein arms and GAS8/DRC localization while preserving tested outer-arm markers. Affected males have oligoasthenospermia. Two zebrafish morpholinos disrupt laterality, southpaw expression and heart looping; human CCDC39 mRNA rescues that phenotype. The human rescue construct was amplified from HEK293T cDNA, which is distinct from directly perturbing the developing human heart. No raw microscopy, supplementary video or patient-table reanalysis was performed.

[PMID:21131974](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21131974/) mainly studies CCDC40 but directly measures CCDC39 localization. Complete selected Results/Discussion and Methods were read. CCDC39 is present along control human respiratory axonemes but accumulates in the apical cytoplasm/ciliary base of CCDC40-deficient cells. Omission of primary antibodies is documented. Mouse and fish Ccdc40 experiments are not reattributed to CCDC39. The cytoplasmic staining does not resolve whether the particular mouse cytosol transfer represents a soluble pool or another associated compartment.

[PMID:23255504](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23255504/) supplies direct human CCDC39 ultrastructural and high-speed video measurements. Complete nonduplicated Methods, Results and Discussion containers were read. Inner dynein arms are depleted while outer arms remain apparent, and many cilia are immotile; motile subsets have altered beat pattern and frequency. The sperm finding is explicitly data not shown. The detailed radial-spoke immunofluorescence example is a CCDC40 patient. It establishes that presence of radial-spoke components need not mean their correct positioning, but is not a separately performed CCDC39 experiment.

[PMID:22499950](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22499950/) is not simply an incidental heterotaxy citation. The unchanged canonical cache is abstract-only. Its independently authenticated normal artifact contains a richer XML body, from which relevant complete Methods, Results and Discussion paragraphs were separately read. The body identifies CCDC39-homozygous PCD controls 9028 and 9098; it links 9028 to characteristic ultrastructure and an abnormal-motion video. This supports the existing cilium-movement annotation alongside independent CCDC39 studies. The artifact body is an external evidence projection, not a replacement for the canonical cache, and no artifact-only text is quoted in the review. Supplementary tables and videos were not independently inspected. The primary full-text location is [PMC3770728](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3770728/).

[PMID:26387594](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26387594/) contains actual anti-CCDC39 Methods and human diagnostic localization evidence despite foregrounding GAS8. Selected complete respiratory-cell Methods, Results and Discussion paragraphs were read. The authors distinguish CCDC39/CCDC40-associated loss of GAS8 from GAS8-mutant cases retaining CCDC39 and DNALI1. This supports the location and assembly relationship without turning CCDC39 into an intraflagellar-transport motor. [PMID:27120127](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27120127/) independently identifies CCDC39/CCDC40 immunofluorescence in human respiratory cilia in its verified abstract. Its detailed Methods were not accessed; the location is accepted with corroboration from the directly read original studies, not with a false claim of full access.

## Structural synthesis and the proposed molecular function

Cryo-EM places the CCDC39–CCDC40 coiled coil within the human 96-nm repeat; deficient axonemes lack recognizable 96-nm periodicity, while the 24-nm outer-arm repeat remains. Mutant and control reconstructions were matched for particle numbers and resolution. Radial spokes can enter deficient cilia and bind sporadically, separating entry from proper periodic docking. ## Ontology and inference boundaries

The extracellular-region IEA is traceable to GO_REF:0000108 and the left-right epithelial-cilium movement source. Official ontology links connect that process through epithelial extracellular-fluid movement and extracellular transport to an extracellular location. This explains the logical route, not a secretion experiment. The actual target scaffold is inside the membrane-enclosed axoneme. Removal of this particular inferred location follows the mismatch between process geography and molecule location; it does not assert that every possible extracellular release of CCDC39 has been experimentally excluded.

GO:0060285 denotes movement of a cell, whereas airway epithelial cilia ordinarily move fluid. The existing human sperm observations provide the appropriate cell-motility context. They support retention of the IMP and PAINT assertions without equating fluid movement with locomotion. PAINT annotations are ancestral functional judgments; inclusion of human CCDC39 in their descendant evidence is legitimate and not circular. The exact family tree and ancestral placement were not reconstructed here.

The measured beat-frequency change is retained as a non-core regulatory phenotype of the organized apparatus, not as evidence of an acute signaling switch. Motile-cilium assembly is retained even though deficient cells still make cilia: assembly includes arranging their internal parts. Protein localization refers to selected components reaching or remaining at an axonemal destination; the data do not show universal import control.
