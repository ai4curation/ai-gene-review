| Cancer immunotherapy—poly(I:C) | Breast, gastric, colorectal, oral squamous-cell, hepatocellular, and other cancers; combinations with paclitaxel, cisplatin, sorafenib, cetuximab, checkpoint blockade, or cancer vaccines | Primarily preclinical and early translational; 2023–2025 reviews describe investigational use rather than an approved standard therapy | Activates TLR3–TRIF signaling, type I interferons, dendritic-cell maturation, NK and CD8 T-cell responses, and sometimes tumor-cell apoptosis. Clinical translation is limited by heterogeneous molecular size, instability, short half-life, systemic inflammation, and toxicity; context-dependent protumor signaling is also possible. | Cancer immunotherapy—poly A:U | Historical adjuvant therapy in breast and locally advanced gastric cancer; gastric regimens included 5-fluorouracil and doxorubicin | Evaluated in clinical studies, including adjuvant breast-cancer studies and a phase III gastric-cancer trial; evidence is historical and has not established routine contemporary use | Some studies reported improved relapse-free or overall survival and reduced recurrence, but the evidence has not produced broadly adopted TLR3-directed standard care. (pqac-00000034, pqac-00000038) |
| Cancer immunotherapy—rintatolimod (Ampligen) | Investigated with HER2/neu peptide vaccination and with IFN-α2b plus celecoxib | Clinical investigation/combination immunotherapy; no definitive efficacy or routine oncology approval established in the cited 2025 review | Designed to stimulate dsRNA-sensing immune pathways while improving the tolerability or formulation limitations of unmodified poly(I:C); current use remains investigational. (pqac-00000037, pqac-00000038) |
| Cancer immunotherapy—poly ICLC (Hiltonol) | Glioblastoma with radiotherapy; head-and-neck cancer, melanoma, leukemia, and solid tumors; combinations with immune-checkpoint inhibitors or vaccines | Phase I–II experience and a phase II glioblastoma/radiation study are reported; additional trials were ongoing or under investigation in the 2025 review | Stabilized poly(I:C) derivative intended to enhance innate and adaptive antitumor immunity. Potential benefits must be balanced against systemic inflammatory toxicity, delivery constraints, and tumor-specific protumor effects. (pqac-00000034, pqac-00000038) |
| Cancer prognostic or predictive biomarker | Hepatocellular carcinoma, melanoma, non-small-cell lung cancer, neuroblastoma, colorectal, esophageal, gastric, breast, renal, oral squamous-cell, and prostate cancers | Observational tumor-expression and outcome studies; not a validated pan-cancer clinical biomarker | High TLR3 expression has correlated with favorable prognosis or immune-active tumors in several cancers, but with worse outcomes in oral squamous-cell and prostate cancers and mixed results elsewhere. Interpretation must account for tumor type, receptor localization, immune context, and treatment. (pqac-00000034, pqac-00000041) |
| Vaccine adjuvants | Poly(I:C), poly ICLC, poly A:U, and defined dsRNA agonists; infectious-disease and cancer vaccines | Preclinical development plus phase I–II evaluation of selected TLR3-agonist approaches; no general-purpose TLR3 adjuvant is established as routine care in the cited evidence | TLR3 agonism can promote dendritic-cell maturation, antigen presentation, Th1-skewed antibodies, NK-cell activity, and cytotoxic CD8 T-cell responses. Defined or targeted formulations aim to preserve adjuvanticity while reducing systemic cytokine toxicity. (pqac-00000033, pqac-00000036) |
| Antiviral immunity—HBV and HCV | Hepatitis B and C; experimental stimulation with poly(I:C) | Mechanistic human association studies and preclinical/translational evidence; not an established antiviral treatment | TLR3 activation can induce type I/III interferons and suppress HBV replication. HCV proteins can impair the pathway by cleaving or downregulating TRIF, while TLR3 expression or variation may influence viral clearance, chronicity, fibrosis, cirrhosis, and hepatocellular carcinoma risk. (pqac-00000032, pqac-00000046) |
| Antiviral immunity—other viruses | Influenza A, SARS-CoV-2, HSV-1, chikungunya, Ebola, respiratory syncytial virus, rhinovirus, vaccinia, and West Nile virus | Predominantly experimental, observational, or preclinical evidence; effects differ by virus and tissue | TLR3-mediated interferon responses can restrict viral replication and support clearance, but excessive signaling can increase cytokine production, inflammatory-cell accumulation, viral-associated tissue injury, or mortality. Protective associations were reported for HSV-1, HBV, chikungunya, Ebola, and SARS-CoV-2, whereas detrimental effects occurred in some influenza, RSV, rhinovirus, vaccinia, and West Nile models. (pqac-00000040, pqac-00000043) |
| Autoimmune and inflammatory disease | Rheumatoid arthritis, lupus nephritis, type 1 diabetes, psoriasis, Sjögren’s syndrome, and celiac disease | Disease-association and mechanistic evidence; therapeutic targeting remains investigational | Endogenous or damage-associated RNA can drive TLR3-dependent type I interferon, NF-κB/IRF3, cytokine, chemokine, and immune-cell recruitment programs. Excessive activation may promote tissue injury, including epithelial or pancreatic β-cell apoptosis, but disease-specific causality and therapeutic windows remain unresolved. (pqac-00000040, pqac-00000042) |
| Allergic disease | Seasonal allergic rhinitis, asthma, atopic dermatitis, and contact dermatitis | Mechanistic and disease-association evidence; no established TLR3-directed standard therapy | Dysregulated TLR3 signaling has been linked to airway inflammation and obstruction, leukocyte and eosinophil recruitment, edema, and pruritus. Both antiviral protection and exacerbation of allergic inflammation are biologically plausible, making context-selective modulation necessary. (pqac-00000039, pqac-00000042) |
| Cardiovascular and calcific disease | Aortic stenosis/calcific aortic-valve disease; endogenous dsRNA-like or damage-associated signaling | Recent preclinical and human-genetic association evidence; pharmacological inhibition remains experimental | TLR3 can promote inflammatory and osteogenic programs in valvular cells. This expands therapeutic interest beyond agonism: inhibition may be useful where endogenous-RNA sensing drives chronic inflammation or tissue calcification, but clinical benefit is unproven. (pqac-00000044, pqac-00000045) |
| Overall translational assessment | Agonism for antiviral immunity, vaccines, and selected tumors; inhibition for inflammatory or tissue-injury states | No broadly accepted, routine TLR3-directed therapy is established by the cited 2023–2025 literature | Authoritative reviews characterize TLR3 as a “double-edged sword”: benefit depends on ligand formulation, dose, delivery route, receptor localization, cell type, tumor or pathogen context, and control of systemic inflammation. Biomarker-guided patient selection and localized delivery are major development priorities. (pqac-00000037, pqac-00000039, pqac-00000040) |
