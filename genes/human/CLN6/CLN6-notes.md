# CLN6 review notes

CLN6 is an ER membrane protein that cooperates with CLN8 during lysosomal enzyme recruitment. The original 54 annotations, two alternative-product objects and 17 reference identity pairs are retained. The draft has no new GO assertion. Positive generic interaction evidence is kept or refined by a verified partner class; a missing native mechanism is not treated as evidence against binding.

## Recruitment and molecular-function boundary

Selected actual Methods and Results of [PMID:32597833](https://www.jci.org/articles/view/130955) establish CLN6-dependent enzyme recruitment, while the budding assay excludes CLN6 from COPII carriers. The core is consequently descriptive, with ER membrane location. Pairwise association and mutual dependence do not resolve an autonomous cargo-receptor activity or a particular adaptor geometry. No process term is added from deficiency alone.

The [corrigendum](https://www.jci.org/articles/view/202081) replaces Figure 2A, 5A and 7A images. Corrected pixels and two supplement pages were inspected in the independent bounded consultation. Results and Methods disagree about the exact loop-deletion interval; the inspected supplement does not settle it. Figure 6 caption/image labels supersede reversed body cross-references. These limitations remain explicit rather than being silently repaired.

## Original localization and degradation evidence

The selected normal cache for [PMID:15010453](https://doi.org/10.1074/jbc.M400643200) remains an abstract. Authentic author-uploaded original Methods and Results were recovered separately. Human CLN6 expressed in BHK21 cells shows glycosylation at an engineered luminal site and cross-link-dependent dimer formation. Its domain-level lumen assertion is compatible with multipass ER-membrane residence. Patient fibroblasts show impaired degradation of endocytosed arylsulfatase A, while cathepsin D processing is preserved in those experiments. The later recruitment study uses different models and assays; neither source is rewritten to match the other.

Actual publisher text of [PMID:11722572](https://febs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1046/j.0014-2956.2001.02530.x) supplies the pH experiment behind the selected abstract. One control and a CLN6-locus-linked patient fibroblast line were tested before sequence-based CLN6 identification. This supports secondary pH context, not a CLN6 proton pump. Current HPA XML independently corroborates ER localization; original HPA image pixels were not viewed. Secondary early-endosome and raft observations in [PMID:17237713](https://doi.org/10.1203/pdr.0b013e31802d8a4a) are retained at their marker-colocalization scope.

## Binding and products

The full original CLN6–CRMP-2 and CLN5 studies distinguish a human fragment library bait, tagged-cell associations, and a mouse GST-Cln5 reagent. No returned IntAct record for these older pairs is treated as a negative experiment. The later screens have exact accession/reference joins: HuRI is PMID:32296183, while the WWP2 and EPHB2 pairs belong to PMID:32814053. Viral physical-association records are separated from one colocalization observation. Features documenting mutations, tags and sufficient binding regions are retained in the index.

Partner-class refinements describe what CLN6 binds. They do not transfer protease, kinase, transporter, GTPase or transcription-factor activities to CLN6. TMPRSS2 isoform 2 has a 37-residue N-terminal extension under the current UniProt sequence model; its recorded 38–529 region retains the protease region. EPHB2 isoform 3 retains the receptor kinase domain. The exact RIC3 product is kept broad because current sequence-feature/product labeling is not fully reconciled. SLC30A4 transporter identity is family-supported; its precise substrate flux is not established by the screen.

CLN6 lipid binding in [PMID:18317235](https://doi.org/10.1203/PDR.0b013e31816fdc17) is genuine target evidence despite the CLN3-focused title. Actual lysate overlays support the LPA and sulfatide assertions as secondary capacities. They do not establish purified affinity or soluble lipid-carrier chemistry. Current CLN6 isoform 2 replaces the canonical N-terminal 28 residues with 60 residues, yielding a reconstructed 343-residue product; equivalent recruitment or binding has not been demonstrated for both products.
