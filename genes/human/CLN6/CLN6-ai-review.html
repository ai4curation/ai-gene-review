Gene Description

CLN6 is a multipass endoplasmic-reticulum membrane protein involved in the delivery of newly synthesized lysosomal enzymes. Together with CLN8, it recruits enzyme precursors at the ER, while CLN8 subsequently accompanies cargo toward the Golgi. CLN6 forms homodimers and exposes luminal regions involved in recruitment. Its deficiency impairs lysosomal degradative capacity and causes neuronal ceroid lipofuscinosis, with progressive neurological and visual dysfunction. Secondary cellular observations include altered lipid distribution and lysosomal pH.

Proposed New Ontology Terms

lysosomal enzyme recruitment activity at the endoplasmic reticulum

Definition: Selective association with newly synthesized soluble lysosomal enzyme precursors that recruits them to an endoplasmic-reticulum resident complex for subsequent Golgi delivery.

Justification: CLN6 and CLN8 cooperate in enzyme recruitment, while CLN8 mediates subsequent COPII export. A recruitment activity describes the demonstrated contribution without asserting that CLN6 directly binds the coat, independently escorts cargo, or structurally holds the complex together. Ontology parent placement remains to be assessed. The current GO:0038024 cargo receptor activity definition requires a membrane-spanning receptor to engage cargo and coat adaptors together for vesicle delivery. CLN6 remains ER-resident in the tested recruitment system, and simultaneous CLN6 cargo/coat-adaptor engagement is not established; CLN8 supplies the demonstrated export role. GO:0038024 is therefore not asserted for CLN6 from these experiments.
