CC   -!- FUNCTION: Catalyzes the transfer of a gamma-phosphoryl group from a
CC       nucleoside triphosphate, mainly ATP, to a nucleoside diphosphate via a
CC       ping-pong mechanism involving a phosphohistidine intermediate,
CC       therefore contributing to the nucleoside triphosphate homeostasis
CC       (PubMed:10952986, PubMed:14960567, PubMed:16313181, PubMed:1851158,
CC       PubMed:23519676, PubMed:33903070, PubMed:8810265, PubMed:9038158). Also
CC       phosphorylates geranyl pyrophosphate (GPP) and farnesyl pyrophosphate
CC       (FPP), linking it to isoprenoid metabolism (PubMed:10952986).
CC       Additionally, functions as a non-specific serine/threonine kinase and
CC       histidine protein kinase, transferring phosphoryl groups from its
CC       active site to target proteins (PubMed:8529641, PubMed:9038158). May
CC       function as a Mg(2+)-dependent single-stranded DNA endonuclease as part
CC       of the SET complex, cooperating with the 3'-5' exonuclease TREX1 to
CC       mediate apoptotic DNA fragmentation in cytotoxic T lymphocytes
CC       (PubMed:12628186, PubMed:16818237). Reported to nick one DNA strand,
CC       enabling TREX1 to remove nucleotides from the free 3' end, enhancing
CC       DNA damage and suppressing DNA end reannealing and repair
CC       (PubMed:16818237). Has been shown to cleave double strands DNA within
CC       the 3'-portions of both 5'-SHS silencer and NHE basal promoter element
CC       of the PDGFA gene, potentially repressing its transcription
CC       (PubMed:11694515). May also function as a Mg(2+)-dependent 3'-5' DNA
CC       exonuclease, excising nucleotides from 3' single-stranded DNA or DNA
CC       with 3' single strand overhangs, suggesting a role in DNA nucleolytic
CC       processing (PubMed:14960567, PubMed:16313181). Involved in the
CC       regulation of tumor metastasis and cellular differentiation (By
CC       similarity). Also required for cell motility (PubMed:8270257,
CC       PubMed:25582197). May control, with NME2, AcCoA usage between histone
CC       acetylation and fatty acid synthesis, possibly by binding and releasing
CC       AcCoA at transcriptionally active chromatin regions in proximity to
CC       histone acetyltransferase (HAT) (By similarity).
