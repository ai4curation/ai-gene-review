## 2026-10-17 — microtubule-binding original and biological synthesis

DNAAF19 couples an axonemal attachment role with dynein-arm assembly. The human-protein rescue experiments in [Panizzi et al. (2012), PMID:22581229](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3371652/) restore both arm classes in zebrafish, while the selected human respiratory samples show variable outer-arm defects and can retain DNALI1. Thus the conserved inner-arm role is not a claim that every human allele eliminates inner arms. The original human respiratory material also contains apparent dimers; zebrafish microscopy and Chlamydomonas fractionation provide distinct evidence for cytoplasmic and axonemal pools.

The newly inspected [King and Patel-King (2015), PMID:25572396](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4367249/) provides direct human-protein evidence for microtubule binding. Its protein-expression and microtubule-assay Methods use His10-tagged human CCDC103 and purified bovine-brain tubulin. Figure 7A shows microtubule-dependent sedimentation with a no-microtubule control; Figure 7B distinguishes binding to polymerized microtubules from free tubulin, and Figure 7C measures protection against cold depolymerization. Figure 8 examines the two-component reconstruction by negative-stain EM. Figure 7D instead uses Chlamydomonas protein and axoneme extracts. The reconstruction supports periodic association under those conditions, not a solved native human polymer or direct dynein-contact interface. Figures 5A and 6 further characterize human full-length and truncated oligomers. The genuine new publication cache is abstract-only; these Methods and Results were read in separately recovered original PMC HTML, not inferred from that cache flag.

[King and Patel-King (2019), PMID:31858719](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7002230/) supplies complementary human-fragment evidence: residues 68–94 fused to Chlamydomonas LC1 support dimerization, and refolded human 93–242 forms monomeric and oligomeric populations. The predicted 98-residue alternative product lacks both the complete tested 68–94 segment and the 93–242 region. No functional equivalence or inactivity is inferred for it. These findings support one integrated microtubule-binding/self-association/arm-attachment core, not separate duplicated assembly processes.

The 13 specific partner-class refinements remain bounded to verified binary pairs and independently established classes. [Rolland et al. (2014), PMID:25416956](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4266588/) reports repeated pairwise retesting and identity confirmation; [Chen et al. (2018), PMID:29892012](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6314957/) explicitly tests positive wild-type pairs against mutant counterparts and excludes autoactivators; [Luck et al. (2020), PMID:32296183](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7169983/) describes the binary HuRI resource. The exact target records and partner definitions are linked in the guide. Binding a verified transcription factor, enzyme, kinase, ATPase or scaffold does not transfer that partner's activity to DNAAF19. It also does not demonstrate native co-localization, endogenous stoichiometry, transcriptional regulation or pathway membership. The GRIPAP1 sufficient-region annotation does not locate the contact within its activated scaffold chain; its separate AP-MS record is co-capture. RCN3 remains only a probable chaperone. Those distinctions support non-core generic binding in the unresolved cases rather than blanket specificity or blanket rejection.
