# CANT1 evidence notes

## 2026-10-04 — Human enzyme, products and substrate preference

CANT1 (Q8WVQ1; HGNC:19721) is a calcium-dependent nucleoside diphosphatase. The current reviewed human sequence has 401 residues. Its full-length form has a type-II membrane anchor and a lumen-facing catalytic region; soluble processed forms can be released. Preserve the three machine-fetched products Q8WVQ1-1, Q8WVQ1-2 and Q8WVQ1-3. Product 2 changes residues 220–245 and lacks 246–401; product 3 lacks 41–91, overlapping the anchor. Neither catalytic competence nor identical localization is established for every product. Historical studies use a 371-residue precursor and different residue numbering, so their constructs and mutation labels must not be silently equated with the current sequence. The description's EDM7 association is clinical context from the reviewed UniProt DISEASE block; the directly read human patient study discussed below concerns Desbuquois dysplasia type 1, and separate EDM7 patient studies were not read.

PMID:12234496, *Cloning, expression, and characterization of a soluble calcium-activated nucleotidase*, describes human enzyme expression and preference for UDP and GDP. PMID:15248776, the hSCAN-1 site-directed mutagenesis study, links calcium-dependent conformational changes to activity. The reviewed UniProt reaction assignments also support IDP hydrolysis. These two caches are abstract-only; original kinetic tables were not recovered. The four short anchors in the review are exact cache excerpts, with fewer than 25 quoted words per source. The broad nucleoside-diphosphatase class remains appropriate for this multisubstrate enzyme. Low ADP activity is a secondary capacity; efficient ADP hydrolysis and platelet inhibition by engineered proteins or insect homologues must not be assigned to wild-type human CANT1.

PMID:16835225, *Calcium-dependent dimerization of human soluble calcium activated nucleotidase*, supports calcium-dependent self-association by solution and structural approaches, including a membrane-associated form. Its abstract and the reviewed target record were read; the original full article was not accessible. Dimerization and calcium binding are properties of the enzyme, not separate independent core functions.

Additional primary reading of PMID:18067325, [the dimer-interface follow-up](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2538946/), covered Methods, Results, Discussion and captions. Both monomer and dimer are catalytically active; dimerization particularly enhances ADPase activity. Preferred UDP/GDP/IDP substrates were assayed at lower enzyme concentration than ADP/ATP/CDP, so those experiments do not provide an equal-concentration comparison of oligomeric effects. COS-cell membrane assays used a C30S construct background. This paper is corroboration, not a source for a new surface-localization claim. Its normal reference cache was subsequently authenticated and imported unchanged; it is abstract-only despite the PMC identifier. The separate official PMC reading supplies the full-text evidence and does not alter the cache.

The normal cached and PubMed abstracts of PMID:15006348 describe a human structure and engineered enhancement of ADPase activity. The complete indexed [publisher correction](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0092867404004088) supplies two omitted insect-apyrase references in the Figure 2 caption; it states no change to human structure or biochemical results. The correction's direct live page failed to open, and the original full structure article was not read. No platelet-inhibition annotation is proposed for the native protein.

## Human proteoglycan evidence and its mechanistic limits

PMID:22539336, *Further delineation of CANT1 phenotypic spectrum and demonstration of its role in proteoglycan synthesis*, contains the main direct human process evidence. Unique Methods, Results, Discussion and figure-caption text were read from the full cache. Functional experiments used two previously reported patient fibroblast lines and four age-matched controls, not all 38 subjects in the clinical cohort. Reduced GAG synthesis reached significance under beta-D-xyloside stimulation; basal synthesis varied substantially. Separately, the chain-size chromatography used material made **without xyloside** and found smaller GAG chains. Hyaluronan synthesis and the indirect sulfate/glucosamine ratio did not show a defect within the methods' limits.

The paper proposes that luminal CANT1 hydrolyzes UDP released by glycosyltransferases, preventing product inhibition and providing UMP for nucleotide-sugar exchange. That is a mechanistic model supported by the enzyme chemistry and patient-cell phenotype, not a direct measurement of Golgi nucleotide concentrations, antiporter flux or feedback inhibition. CANT1 catalyzes nucleotide hydrolysis; it does not itself transfer sugar to the chain. The existing proteoglycan-biosynthesis assertions are retained on this combined basis. No new developmental or glycosyltransferase activity is inferred simply because CANT1 deficiency causes skeletal disease.

The [2019 mouse cartilage study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6598859/) provides supporting animal context for altered GAG production and chain properties. Its findings must not be relabelled as human assays or collapsed into the human fibroblast sulfation result. Mouse tissue phenotypes do not justify adding every affected developmental process.

## Localization, interaction and signaling assessment

The full-length secretory-pathway membrane form and the human proteoglycan work support ER/Golgi localization. The [Human Protein Atlas target record](https://www.proteinatlas.org/ENSG00000171302-CANT1/subcellular) reports supported Golgi localization in U2OS cells with HPA019639; A-431 and U-251MG are listed without staining. This is antibody-based localization, not RNA expression, but does not resolve individual Golgi cisternae. UniProt supplies the Golgi stack membrane mapping. The source experimental plasma-membrane annotation and its electronic derivative remain UNDECIDED because the original target-specific surface assay in PMID:16835225 could not be inspected. Broad membrane association does not settle the precise membrane compartment.

The underlying peptides were not reanalysed; being released as cargo does not establish active participation in exocytosis.
