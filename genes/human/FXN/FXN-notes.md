## Scientific follow-up to the first review

The initial results above remain the dated publication record. The proposed current actions are 33 ACCEPT, 40 KEEP_AS_NON_CORE, 8 TOO_GENERAL, 10 UNDECIDED and 1 MODIFY. Human holo-frataxin can bind ferrochelatase and deliver iron in the reported reconstitution. The [original abstract (PMID:15123683)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15123683/) supports replacing the generic FECH-binding context with iron chaperone activity; full Methods were inaccessible, and the result does not establish an obligatory physiological donor. The [authentic PMID:15961414 abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15961414/) supports association with human succinate dehydrogenase subunits. Current reasons omit the earlier fragment, antibody and host details because the successful indexed full response was not retained.

The ferric-binding disagreement is substantive. [PMID:12785837](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12785837/) reports positive measurements, whereas [PMID:29576242](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29576242/) challenged human FXN81–210 with five-fold FeCl3, checked precipitation, and found loss of the ferrous-bound response upon oxidation. A discrepancy due to assay conditions is possible but has not been established. Four ferric-binding rows therefore remain UNDECIDED, preserving the valid PAINT node and experimental donor evidence. The finding-specific conflict is recorded without declaring either identifier invalid or every older finding overturned. The XAS study cited by the latter paper is described there as yeast evidence, not a second independently checked human experiment.

The mouse donor has direct experimental grounding. [PMID:25597503](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25597503/) is the exact O35943 IDA source and reports persulfide transfer; its cited [PMID:23265191](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23265191/) has an authentic supplement identifying murine components and measuring [4Fe-4S] species in reconstituted complexes. The varying Table S2 conditions are not replicates and do not show a uniform FXN-dependent increase. The original abstract separately reports cluster transfer to aconitase. These observations support the bounded ortholog-based non-core calls; they do not rewrite the human PMID:29576242 acts_upstream_of assertion or demonstrate the final physiological human step.

The core remains FXN-assisted initial cluster assembly. [PMID:38627381](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38627381/) links FXN binding to rearrangement of the ISCU site and faster persulfide transfer; [PMID:31101807](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31101807/) resolves contacts with NFS1 and ISCU in the assembled complex. Short exact-cache excerpts anchor selected direct annotations, while the original two core quotations remain intact. This does not assign enzyme chemistry to the general interaction survey rows. NDUFAB1 is the human mitochondrial acyl-carrier protein; that nomenclature clarification is retained here rather than as a standalone sentence in the description.

All 68 inherited quotation occurrences remain recoverable in their prior 20 active and 48 historical placements.
