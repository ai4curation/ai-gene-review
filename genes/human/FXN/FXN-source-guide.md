# FXN source guide

This guide separates the biological evidence from the material actually accessed. A native publication cache may contain only an abstract even when selected original sections were read elsewhere. The table below records the actual native cache and the scoped original reading separately. Genuine acquisition refreshed the target UniProt and GOA files without reseeding the inherited review. Forty existing normal sources remain byte-identical; 23 previously absent sources were fetched with native functions.

## Assembly mechanism and iron chemistry

[PMID:24971490](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24971490/) supports human FXN-assisted sulfur transfer and initial [2Fe-2S] formation through radiolabel and CD measurements. [PMID:38627381](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-024-47310-8) connects FXN binding with rearrangement of the ISCU site and faster persulfide transfer; exact constructs delegated to its unrecovered Supplementary Table 3 remain unspecified. [PMID:31101807](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-019-09989-y) resolves contacts to NFS1/ISCU using human FXN 81-210 and bacterial ACP-containing preparations. BLI measures the assembled complex; the thermal-stability experiment is not an isolated binary affinity assay. [PMID:29097656](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-017-01497-1) distinguishes human crystallographic components from fungal FXN-containing SAXS assemblies. [PMID:29491838](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29491838/) explicitly adds human FXN to a fly core complex and therefore supplies direct target evidence within that mixed-species experiment.

[PMID:12785837](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12785837/) reports both ferrous and ferric binding and assembly on ISU; an ISU-bound cluster is not itself proof of an FXN-bound cluster. [PMID:29576242](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29576242/) reports condition-dependent ferrous binding and release while leaving ISCU uptake and alternative physiological iron sources unresolved. Its ferric-negative assay cannot erase earlier ferric measurements. [PMID:15123683](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15123683/) positively supports human holoFXN-human FECH association and reconstituted iron delivery. [PMID:15641778](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15641778/) supports ferroxidase activity of assembled human protein, separately from the unresolved cellular peroxide-response assertion.

## Interactions, locations and protein forms

The exact positive HuRI and Haenig partner contexts are retained as yeast-assay evidence. Original supplementary joins identify all ten FXN Haenig partners, but none occurs in the retained mammalian-validation subset. Study-wide validation rates are not assigned to those pairs. [PMID:15961414](https://doi.org/10.1093/hmg/ddi214) tests human FXN and SDHA/SDHB fragments in yeast; the successful indexed original was read contemporaneously, but its complete response body was not retained. [PMID:26702583](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26702583/) explicitly names FXN among HSPA9-associated proteins in its abstract; inaccessible Methods are not filled in from interaction databases.

Human-cell studies distinguish mitochondrial and contextual cytosolic pools. Processing, mutant targeting, rescue and fractionation experiments do not establish that every tested fragment is a natural splice product. Official [UniProt Q16595](https://www.uniprot.org/uniprotkb/Q16595/entry) declares three products whose 210/171/196-residue sequences were checked against the official FASTAs. The retained [HPA location record](https://www.proteinatlas.org/ENSG00000165060-FXN/subcellular) reports mitochondrial and cytosolic labels for CAB022164 samples; individual images were not independently scored.
