## Receptor activity and human controls

CD27's central activity is recognition of CD70 and transduction of a costimulatory signal through intracellular partners. Human CD27-Fc binding and the ligand-expression controls in [PMID:28011863](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28011863/) complement the original CD27 extracellular-domain–IgG expression-cloning assay in [PMID:8387892](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8387892/). Although the 2017 title emphasizes CD70 deficiency, it directly tests T cells from a CD27 p.W110X patient. CD27 surface expression is absent, CD70-dependent proliferation with anti-CD3 is impaired, and anti-CD3/CD28 stimulation remains effective. Anti-CD27 blockade and CD70 rescue/CRISPR controls support a receptor mechanism rather than necessity inferred from a clinical phenotype alone.

The full cached GO-CAM model 666b894f00000352 and exact P26842 index entry were read. CD27 appears as transmembrane signaling receptor activity at the plasma membrane, in CD27 signaling and T-cell activation, with CD70 ligand activity upstream and TRAF2 adaptor activity downstream. A single receptor core captures this role. No new process annotation is proposed, and the broad receptor-signaling process is not duplicated alongside its existing specific CD27-signaling child in the core. The cytokine-family relationship does not license GO:0005031, whose actual definition specifies TNF binding: CD70 is not TNF itself. ## Apoptosis and the inhibitor boundary

The original 2002 experiments use EBV-negative human Ramos cells and human CD70 or CD154 expressed on murine 300-19 feeder cells, with vector feeders, BCR ligation, antibody blockade and p53-mutant controls. They examine Bcl-2/Bcl-XL/Bik, mitochondrial potential, cytochrome-c redistribution and caspase/PARP cleavage. These support context-dependent suppression of BCR-induced mitochondrial apoptosis. The peptide caspase inhibitors are separate reagents; CD27 was not thereby shown to be a caspase-binding inhibitor.

The official [GO:0043027 definition](https://www.informatics.jax.org/vocab/gene_ontology/GO%3A0043027) requires binding to and reducing the activity of the relevant endopeptidase. The distinction between this direct molecular activity and upstream receptor signaling supports bounded MARK_AS_OVER_ANNOTATED for row28. The supported negative-regulation-of-apoptosis rows remain non-core. This does not imply that a noncatalytic regulator must itself catalyze a reaction; the missing criterion is the demonstrated direct inhibitory activity, not enzyme chemistry.

The 1997 [CD27/Siva study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9177220/) contains ligand-triggered apoptotic responses in human B-cell lines, human CD27-expressing murine pre-B cells and activated human lymphocytes, as well as human CD27/GFP-Siva coimmunoprecipitation in transfected 293 cells. These are distinct from Siva-overexpression effects. The extrinsic-apoptotic-signaling assertion is retained non-core with context limits. The 2002 authors discuss the difference from the earlier result; their proposed explanations are not treated as a resolved universal mechanism. Neither a CD27 death domain nor a universal prosurvival or prodeath behavior is asserted.
