## Biological evidence and review follow-up — 2026-11-01

The human cloning study establishes GABA-gated chloride channels, and later human structural and functional studies explain receptor assembly and gating [PMID:2465923](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2465923/), [PMID:29950725](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6220708/). The full-length human nanodisc study reports [PMID:30602789, "We isolated the full-length human α1β3γ2L receptor"](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6364807/). Its ligand-binding and electrophysiological measurements support receptor function alongside the desensitized structural state. Tags, megabodies and engineered loop replacements are assay constructs, not additional natural GABRA1 products.

In the neuron–HEK co-culture preparation, GABA-evoked currents are pharmacologically consistent with alpha1-containing receptors: [PMID:23909897, "This response was enhanced by zolpidem"](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4028986/). Spontaneous and evoked currents show postsynaptic transmission, while receptor-dependent contacts support a contextual structural contribution to inhibitory synapse assembly. The latter is non-core and does not establish a universal alpha1-only organizing mechanism. The JoVE protocol includes surface staining and contact assays but reuses earlier figures, so it is not independent replication of every earlier measurement [PMID:25489750](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4354098/).

The alpha1/beta3 preparation contains [PMID:35140402, "two α1 subunits and three β3 subunits"](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8850191/), with engineered constructs compared against wild-type recordings.
