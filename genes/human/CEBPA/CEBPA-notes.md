# CEBPA research notes

## 2026-10-10 — substantive whole-gene review

C/EBPalpha is a bZIP DNA-binding transcription factor whose differentiation programs are coupled to proliferative restraint. The review retains all 143 normal source assertions and all four UniProt products. No new annotation row is proposed. There was no prior CEBPA notes file at the checked main snapshot, so this is the first journal entry.

The central activity is sequence-specific transcriptional regulation. Human CEBPA binds and activates granulocyte targets, and AML-associated mutations alter that output ([PMID:11242107](https://doi.org/10.1038/85820), Methods and Figures 3–5; genuine full publisher PDF inspected). Human CYP3A4 promoter-site mutations, reporters and endogenous expression provide independent evidence ([PMID:12695546](https://doi.org/10.1124/mol.63.5.1180), abstract). EVI2B promoter occupancy and mutant reporters directly connect human CEBPA to a myeloid target ([PMID:28186500](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28186500/), full publisher Methods, Figure 2 and supplemental Methods). Human U937 endogenous ChIP and human CEBPA/K562 reporters are distinct from the rat p42/p30 reporter comparison in Figure 3a; the separate ER-induction constructs are human. The [published correction](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41418-018-0146-z) changes only an acknowledged grant number. Short verbatim anchors appear at the relevant YAML decisions, with distinct excerpts and at most 25 newly quoted words per paper across the whole packet.

### Products and assay scope

The normal UniProt record displays P49715-1. P49715-2 is named p42 and lacks the displayed sequence's first 14 residues; P49715-3 is p30 and lacks its first 119 residues. P49715-4 has an extended N terminus. These source names, identifiers and sequence notes are preserved exactly. An untagged GOA assertion is not silently converted to a product-specific assertion.

The original p30/WT work directly tests human CEBPA, but its reduced p30 DNA binding is specific to the tested probe and conditions. It does not mean p30 cannot bind any DNA. SWI/SNF-dependent proliferation arrest in [PMID:14660596](https://doi.org/10.1074/jbc.M312709200) was read in the selected original author-uploaded Methods/Results and uses rat p42/p30 constructs. Cell-count outcomes are not cell-size measurements.

The extended-product work ([PMID:20075868](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2810377/), full normal Methods/Results) links nucleolar retention to rDNA regulation and cell-size changes. Many engineered localization and functional assays use rat constructs in human cells. Human endogenous localization and translation-initiation experiments are separate evidence. Both p42 and extended products associate with UBF1; this is not an extended-only interaction or intrinsic RNA-polymerase activity. The nucleolar location remains non-core at the gene level.

### Cell-cycle control and contextual processes

Direct CDK inhibition is reported in the authentic abstract of [PMID:11684017](https://doi.org/10.1016/S1097-2765(01)00366-5). Its full construct-level Methods were not recovered. The reported inhibitory core is explicitly context dependent, and the reference assessment states the construct-access limit. The original source-specific kinase-binding rows are refined only to protein kinase binding, preserving their IPI event. Selected full indexed [PMID:15107404](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC395850/) Methods/Results and Figure 8 contain genuine human normal/tumor liver associations with CDK2, CDK4 and BRM; separate phosphorylation mutagenesis uses mouse constructs. The existing short normal cache is partial despite its full-text flag. Official PubMed XML checked on 2026-10-10 has no linked correction/retraction record or retraction publication type for either paper; the direct publisher status requests were blocked. This is a dated database-status observation, not a guarantee against every possible concern. E2F repression in mouse alleles ([PMID:11672531](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0092-8674(01)00516-5)) supplies a distinct mechanistic route to arrest and differentiation, not the direct kinase-inhibition assay.

A separate primary test limits the physiological generalization. In [PMID:16428455](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1347024/), the genuine full Methods, Results/Figures 1–2 and 5–7 and Discussion were read externally. Rat expression constructs and a mouse/rat hybrid knock-in retain growth repression after deleting the proposed PHR kinase-interaction region. Fetal-liver BrdU incorporation and CDK2 histone-H1 kinase activity, myeloid replating, and the measured response to partial hepatectomy show no corresponding growth defect in the tested systems. The authors could not recover CDK2 co-IP under their extraction conditions and explicitly discuss possible high-salt disruption; that does not disprove the separate binding evidence. Thus direct CDK inhibition remains a reported context-dependent mechanism, not an obligatory explanation of all CEBPA growth repression. The normal cache is abstract-only, whereas the externally inspected full source is available at the linked PMCID.

The CHOP-focused review [PMID:14685163](https://doi.org/10.1038/sj.cdd.4401373) actually discusses CEBPA-mediated CDK turnover and arrest in its original full author-uploaded text. Independent [PMID:11867521](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11867521/) abstract and primary Figure 2/6 legends support CDK4 ubiquitination/turnover and MG132 sensitivity. This is a regulatory process, not evidence that CEBPA is an E3 enzyme; it is not generalized to CDK2 degradation.

The white-fat proliferation assertion is refined to fat-cell differentiation. In [PMID:36228616](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cmet.2022.09.018), selected full Figure 6 Results and Methods show CEBPA DNA binding, reporter activation and adipogenic promoter occupancy. Ovol2 perturbations alter Oil Red O and mature markers during differentiation; tissue hyperplasia is not a demonstrated CEBPA-dependent division assay. Human OVOL2 experiments do not establish an independently manipulated human CEBPA result. The leptin-promoter source [PMID:8643605](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.93.11.5507) positively establishes transcriptional activation; its broad energy-generation term is refined to that performed step, not interpreted as CEBPA catalyzing energy-producing chemistry. Full scanned body access remains incomplete, and no claim excludes all possible metabolic regulatory roles.

The STAT3-focused [PMID:16946298](https://doi.org/10.1182/blood-2006-07-033969) explicitly contains CEBPA-siRNA and promoter-deletion experiments in human Huh7 cells, Figure 3. The original indexed full Results were inspected; the title/abstract alone would miss them. Human IL6-dependent CEBPA/PU.1/STAT3 complexes, promoter occupancy and reporter cooperation were read in [PMID:24429361](https://doi.org/10.1128/JVI.03060-13), selected publisher Methods/Results and Figures 3–4. These co-IP, ChIP and reporter experiments use primary human hepatocytes; the separate nuclear-colocalization experiment uses HepG2. Other organ/metabolic effects retain their rodent transfer scope and remain non-core.
