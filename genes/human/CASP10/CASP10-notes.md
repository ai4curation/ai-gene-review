# CASP10 apoptosis review notes

## 2026-09-30

- CASP10 is an active tandem-DED initiator caspase rather than a CFLAR-like pseudoenzyme. UniProt records Asp-directed catalytic activity for the enzyme, two catalytic residues, recruitment to FAS/FADD/CASP8/CASP10 complexes, interaction with FADD/CASP8, interaction with RFFL/RNF34 CARP E3 ligases, and interaction with RIOK3.
- Wang et al. support the direct death-receptor branch: CASP10 can initiate Fas- and TRAIL-receptor apoptosis independently of CASP8 in the tested system, is recruited and activated after Fas crosslinking in primary human T cells, and uses its death effector domains to interact with the FADD death effector domain [PMID:11717445, "Fas crosslinking in primary human T cells leads to the recruitment and activation of caspase-10"].
- The original Mch4 paper established CASP10 as an active cysteine protease that can activate effector procaspases CPP32/CASP3 and Mch3/CASP7 in vitro, so InterPro/PAINT catalytic-caspase propagation is appropriate for CASP10 even though the same family-level mappings were wrong for c-FLIP [PMID:8755496, "Expression of Mch4 in Escherichia coli produced an active protease"].
- Several generic `GO:0005515 protein binding` imports do reflect real interactions but not useful CASP10 molecular functions. RIPK1 and RIOK3 rows were removed as bare partner edges while retaining the mechanistic NF-kappaB branch [PMID:12884866, "roles in a non- or anti-apoptotic signaling pathway leading to NF-kappaB activation through RIP, NIK and IKKalpha"; PMID:19557502, "RIOK3 interacted with caspase-10"].
- CARP/RNF34 ubiquitin-ligase binding is real but peripheral: CARP1/2 interact with CASP8 and CASP10 prodomains and can regulate DED caspases, so the imported `ubiquitin protein ligase binding` row was retained as non-core [PMID:15069192, "experiments with caspase-10"].
- CASP10 has a noncanonical c-FLIP(L)-associated activity in myeloma cells. The generic CFLAR row from Lamy et al. was narrowed to `GO:1904093 negative regulation of autophagic cell death`, based on the CASP10-c-FLIP(L) protease that cleaves BCLAF1 and restrains lethal autophagy, but this was not promoted into the core death-receptor summary [PMID:23541952, "Caspase-10 inhibits autophagy by cleaving the BCL2-interacting protein BCLAF1"].
- Feoktistova et al. directly place CASP10 in the RIP1/FADD/CASP8/c-FLIP ripoptosome, but the functional switch in that paper is controlled mostly by cIAPs, RIP1/RIP3, CASP8 activity, and c-FLIP isoform balance, so CASP10 should get a ripoptosome component row but not a new CASP10-specific necroptosis process assertion [PMID:21737330, "It contains RIP1, FADD, caspase-8, caspase-10, and caspase inhibitor cFLIP isoforms"].
