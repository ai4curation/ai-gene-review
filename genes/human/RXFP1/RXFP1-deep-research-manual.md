# RXFP1 manual literature synthesis

RXFP1 encodes relaxin family peptide receptor 1, a leucine-rich-repeat GPCR for relaxin peptides. UniProt describes it as a "Receptor for relaxins" whose activity is "mediated by G proteins leading to stimulation of adenylate cyclase and an increase of cAMP" [file:human/RXFP1/RXFP1-uniprot.txt]. Hsu et al. identified LGR7/RXFP1 and LGR8/RXFP2 as relaxin-responsive GPCRs and showed relaxin-dependent cAMP signaling through these receptors [PMID:11809971, "two orphan heterotrimeric guanine nucleotide binding protein (G protein)-coupled receptors, LGR7 and LGR8, are capable of mediating the action of relaxin through an adenosine 3',5'-monophosphate (cAMP)-dependent pathway"].

RXFP1 acts at the plasma membrane. Reactome places human RXFP1 in the relaxin receptor branch of Gs signaling and records that RXFP1/LGR7 binds human relaxin-2 and relaxin-3 [Reactome:R-HSA-444838, "Relaxin receptor 1 (RXFP1 or LGR7) was identified as a receptor for porcine relaxin and subsequently shown to bind human relaxin-2 and relaxin-3."].

RXFP1 also nucleates receptor-associated signaling complexes. The AKAP79/adenylyl cyclase 2/beta-arrestin/PDE4D3 work mapped a pre-assembled RXFP1 signalosome that underlies sub-picomolar relaxin signaling [PMID:20664520, "Sub-picomolar relaxin signalling by a pre-assembled RXFP1, AKAP79, AC2, beta-arrestin 2, PDE4D3 complex."]; this interaction evidence remains bare `protein binding` in GOA and is removed under the project policy rather than turned into a separate molecular function. CTRP8 can also bind and activate RXFP1 in glioblastoma cells [PMID:24014093, "Finally, co-immunoprecipitation studies revealed the direct interaction of human CTRP8 with RXFP1."], but that cancer-cell ligand is peripheral to the conserved relaxin receptor function.

RAMP2 and RAMP3 were detected as RXFP1 interactors in a systematic GPCR-RAMP interactome map [PMID:39083597, "To elucidate a comprehensive GPCR-RAMP interactome, we created a library of 215 dual epitope-tagged (DuET) GPCRs representing all GPCR subfamilies and coexpressed each GPCR with each of the three RAMPs."]. Those rows are also bare binding rows and should be removed under the generic protein-binding policy.
