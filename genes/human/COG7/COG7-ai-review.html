Gene Description

COG7 is a peripheral Golgi-associated subunit of the conserved oligomeric Golgi complex. Its interaction with COG5 helps organize lobe B and incorporate other COG subunits. This structural role supports recycling and localization of Golgi-resident proteins, maintains Golgi architecture and enables normal glycan processing. Human COG7 deficiency causes a severe congenital disorder of glycosylation with disrupted trafficking and glycosylation in patient cells. COG7 is not a glycosyltransferase; its effects on glycan synthesis arise through the organization of Golgi machinery.

Core Functions

Provides a COG5-contacting structural interface that assembles lobe B of the COG complex at Golgi membranes and supports resident-protein recycling, Golgi organization and protein maintenance. Human COG7 interface mutations impair COG5 association and recovered lobe-B assembly; COG7 loss reduces lobe-B partner abundance, and COG7 correction restores GS15 in patient cells. Stabilization is complex mediated, with the resident-protein turnover route unresolved. Yeast fragment structures and human COG5 rescue experiments have distinct scope; isolated COG7 tethering, SNARE bridging and enzymatic activity are not established.
