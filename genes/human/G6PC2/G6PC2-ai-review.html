Gene Description

Glucose-6-phosphatase catalytic subunit 2 (G6PC2, EC 3.1.3.9), historically called IGRP (islet-specific glucose-6-phosphatase catalytic subunit-related protein), is the pancreatic-islet-restricted paralog of the liver/kidney enzyme G6PC1. It is a multi-pass integral endoplasmic reticulum membrane glycoprotein of nine transmembrane helices, with a luminal active site built on a type 2 phosphatidic acid phosphatase (PAP2) motif in which His-174 is the phosphoryl-accepting nucleophile and His-115 the proton donor. G6PC2 hydrolyzes D-glucose 6-phosphate to D-glucose and inorganic phosphate, but its specific activity is far below that of G6PC1 — it eluded detection in the original expression assays and became measurable only in high-level heterologous systems, with the gap traced to divergent residues lining the substrate cavity. Because the islet is not a gluconeogenic tissue, this hydrolysis does not release glucose into the circulation; instead it runs against the beta-cell glucose sensor glucokinase to form a glucose/glucose-6-phosphate substrate cycle that lowers glycolytic flux and thereby raises the glucose threshold for insulin release. Loss of the gene in mice shifts the glucose-stimulated insulin secretion dose-response curve leftward and lowers fasting blood glucose without changing fasting plasma insulin, while predisposing to hypoglycemia during prolonged fasting or ketogenic feeding. The same set-point logic operates in pancreatic alpha cells, where G6PC2 governs glucose suppression of glucagon secretion. Common variants at the human G6PC2 locus are among the most reproducible determinants of fasting plasma glucose and beta-cell function. As a beta-cell-restricted, proteasomally degraded protein, G6PC2 is also a major source of MHC class I-presented peptides and a prominent CD8+ T-cell autoantigen in type 1 diabetes, though this antigenicity is a consequence of its expression pattern rather than of its enzymatic role.

Proposed New Ontology Terms

glucose-glucose 6-phosphate substrate cycle

Definition: A glucose 6-phosphate metabolic process in which glucose is phosphorylated to glucose 6-phosphate and glucose 6-phosphate is concurrently hydrolyzed back to glucose within the same cell, with net consumption of ATP and no net change in the concentration of either metabolite, such that the ratio of the two opposing activities sets the rate of glycolytic flux.

Justification: G6PC2 and glucokinase together form exactly such a cycle in pancreatic islet cells, and the cycle rather than either enzyme alone is what determines the glucose set point for insulin and glucagon secretion. GO currently offers no term for a substrate (futile) cycle in carbohydrate metabolism, so the biology has to be split across the generic glucose 6-phosphate metabolic process term and a separate hormone-secretion regulation term, losing the fact that the two are mechanistically the same event. A dedicated term would also serve GCK, G6PC1 and hepatic glucose cycling.

Parent term: glucose 6-phosphate metabolic process

Core Functions

Hydrolyzes D-glucose 6-phosphate to D-glucose and inorganic phosphate at the luminal face of the endoplasmic reticulum membrane of pancreatic beta cells. Because beta cells are not gluconeogenic and do not export glucose, this activity does not contribute free glucose to the circulation; instead it opposes glucokinase to create a glucose/glucose-6-phosphate substrate cycle that lowers glycolytic flux and so raises the glucose threshold for insulin release. Its specific activity is markedly lower than that of the liver enzyme G6PC1, consistent with a rate-setting rather than a bulk-flux role.

    "Glucose-6-phosphatase activity was reduced, whereas basal cytoplasmic calcium levels were elevated in islets isolated from G6pc2 KO mice."
  • Articulates the glucokinase/G6PC2 futile substrate cycle model that explains why an islet glucose-6-phosphatase lowers rather than raises glycemia.
    "This glucokinase/G6pc2 futile substrate cycle is predicted to reduce glycolytic flux and hence insulin secretion."
PMID:32213654
Pancreatic islet beta cell-specific deletion of G6pc2 reduces fasting blood glucose.
  • Beta-cell-specific deletion alone reproduces the fasting-glucose phenotype, establishing that the effect is islet-autonomous rather than a consequence of trace expression elsewhere.
    "In adult mice, beta cell-specific deletion of G6pc2 was sufficient to reduce FBG without changing FPI."
  • In islets, both glucose-6-phosphatase activity and glucose cycling are abolished by G6pc2 deletion, and glucose-stimulated insulin secretion is enhanced specifically at submaximal glucose, which is the signature of a set-point change.
    "in isolated islets, glucose-6-phosphatase activity and glucose cycling are abolished and glucose-stimulated insulin secretion (GSIS) is enhanced at submaximal but not high glucose"
  • States the field consensus that G6PC1 and G6PC3 both have much higher catalytic activity than G6PC2, which is why trace G6PC2 expression outside islets is judged metabolically irrelevant.
    "because both G6PC1 (Lei, et al. 1993) and G6PC3 (Shieh et al. 2003) have much higher catalytic activity than G6PC2 (Petrolonis, et al. 2004)"
PMID:32569842
G6PC2 confers protection against hypoglycemia upon ketogenic diet feeding and prolonged fasting.

G6PC2 (Q9NQR9) — research notes

Human glucose-6-phosphatase catalytic subunit 2, historically IGRP (islet-specific
glucose-6-phosphatase catalytic subunit-related protein). HGNC:28906, chr 2q31.1,
355 aa, EC 3.1.3.9.

Journal-style notes, appended as research proceeded. Every assertion carries inline
provenance in the form [PMID:xxxxxxx "verbatim supporting text"]. Quotes were
checked as verbatim substrings of the cached publications/PMID_*.md records.

1. Identity, paralogy and tissue restriction

G6PC2 is one of three human glucose-6-phosphatase catalytic subunits. G6PC1 is the
liver/kidney gluconeogenic enzyme, G6PC3 is ubiquitous, G6PC2 is islet-restricted
PMID:32569842.

It was cloned from mouse insulinoma by subtractive hybridisation and shown to be ~50%
identical to the liver enzyme with the catalytic residues conserved
PMID:10078553. Expression is islet/beta-cell dominated
PMID:10078553, confirmed later at the protein level in
human and mouse PMID:32569842. Human and mouse promoters are islet-active; the rat orthologue is a pseudogene
PMID:11297555. Note the rat exception when
reading rodent literature.

Isoform note: three UniProt isoforms (Q9NQR9-1/-2/-3); isoforms 2 and 3 truncate at
residues 103 and 155 respectively and so lose the His-174 nucleophile. Alternative
splicing differs between pancreas and lymphoid tissue (PMID:16520917, title-level only —
not used to support any annotation here).

2. Subcellular location and topology

Experimentally an ER-membrane multipass glycoprotein
PMID:15044018.
UniProt records SUBCELLULAR LOCATION: Endoplasmic reticulum membrane {ECO:0000269|PubMed:15044018}; Multi-pass membrane protein, N-glycosylation at Asn-92,
and a C-terminal (352-355) "Prevents secretion from ER" motif. Active site residues are
annotated at His-115 (proton donor) and His-174 (nucleophile) — the latter is the
phosphohistidine acceptor shared with G6PC1.

So GO:0005789 (ER membrane) is the informative location; GO:0005783 (ER) and GO:0016020
(membrane) are true but generic parents.

3. The catalytic-activity question — this is the contested part

Round 1 (1999-2001): no activity detectable. The cloning paper explicitly failed to
demonstrate catalysis PMID:10078553, concluding
PMID:10078553. Two years
later the same group still wrote PMID:11297555. As late as 2004 the topology paper opened with
PMID:15044018.

Round 2 (2004): activity demonstrated on overexpression. Petrolonis et al. expressed
IGRP in insect cells and measured G6P hydrolysis
PMID:14722102, with
PMID:14722102 and a selective
inhibitor that did not touch the liver enzyme
PMID:14722102, concluding
PMID:14722102. This is the annotation source for GOA's EXP
GO:0004346 and for UniProt's CATALYTIC ACTIVITY ... ECO:0000269|PubMed:14722102.

Round 3: activity is real but weak, and that weakness is the recurring technical
obstacle. The activity is genuine but far below G6PC1's, which is exactly why the early
assays failed and why later work needed engineered overexpression systems:
PMID:34954144;
side-by-side at matched protein levels PMID:34954144; and as a general statement of the field
PMID:32213654.

Round 4: mechanism confirmed genetically and structurally. The His-174 nucleophile is
required PMID:34954144. The PAP2 phosphatase motif is required
PMID:38095063. And the structural basis for the G6PC1/G6PC2
activity gap has been localised to the substrate cavity
PMID:38095063. A
selective small-molecule inhibitor now exists PMID:38431189.

Round 5: activity is required in vivo. Islets from G6pc2-null mice lose the activity
PMID:23274894 and lose glucose
cycling PMID:32213654.

Net reading for curation. GO:0004346 is correct for G6PC2 — the annotation should be
kept, not removed. What should not be asserted is that its activity is comparable to
G6PC1's, or that the earlier negative reports were simply wrong: they reflect a genuinely
low specific activity that only became measurable with overexpression systems. The claim
"weak/largely absent" is half right — largely undetectable in the original assays, weak
but physiologically decisive in vivo.

4. What the enzyme actually does: a futile cycle, not gluconeogenesis

The islet is not a gluconeogenic tissue and G6PC2 does not release free glucose to the
blood. Its hydrolysis of G6P runs against glucokinase inside the beta cell:
PMID:32569842 and
PMID:23274894. The consequence is a shift in the glucose
set point of secretion PMID:32213654.

Directionality: G6PC2 restrains insulin secretion
PMID:23274894. Loss of the gene
lowers, not raises, fasting glucose — the opposite of what a gluconeogenic enzyme would
give PMID:17265032, and the effect is islet-autonomous
PMID:32213654.

There may be a physiological reason for keeping a futile cycle: it buffers against
hypoglycaemia PMID:32569842.

Curation consequence: the three GO:0006094 (gluconeogenesis) rows in GOA are inherited
from the G6PC1-like ancestral/pathway context, not from G6PC2 biology. Reactome's own
pathway summary states gluconeogenesis "is confined to cells of the liver and kidney"
(reactome/R-HSA-70263.md), while the G6PC2 reaction it assigns to that pathway is
explicitly the islet one (R-HSA-3266566 "G6PC2 hydrolyzes G6P to form Glc and Pi
(islet)"). GO:0051156 (glucose 6-phosphate metabolic process) is the honest replacement —
and is also what PAN-GO asserts for Q9NQR9 (UniProt cross-reference "PAN-GO; Q9NQR9; 4 GO
annotations based on evolutionary models"; the UniProt GO cross-reference block lists
GO:0051156; P:glucose 6-phosphate metabolic process; IBA:GO_Central).

G6PC2 is not purely a beta-cell story: an inducible alpha-cell-specific knockout shows

The reason the protein is famous outside metabolism
PMID:12815107, with the human relevance flagged in the same

PMID:15044018.

degraded protein happens to present, not an evolved activity.

8. Things G6PC2 does not do

  • It does not supply blood glucose by gluconeogenesis or glycogenolysis (§4).
  • It does not detectably influence 11beta-HSD1/glucocorticoid signalling despite sharing
    the ER G6P pool with H6PD PMID:37855366.
  • It has no established role outside islets: PMID:32213654. (UniProt records lower-level testis expression and HPA
    calls the gene "Group enriched (pancreas, retina)"; no function is attached to either.)
