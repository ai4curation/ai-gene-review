# KASH5/CCDC155 (human; UniProt Q8N6L0): functional-annotation report

## Executive conclusion

KASH5 is **not an enzyme or transporter**. It is a meiosis-restricted, tail-anchored outer-nuclear-membrane protein that performs two linked functions: it is the cytoplasmic half of a meiotic **LINC (linker of nucleoskeleton and cytoskeleton) complex**, and it is a direct **activating adaptor for cytoplasmic dynein-1**. By binding SUN1—or, in some mouse contexts, SUN2—across the nuclear envelope while recruiting dynein–dynactin on the cytoplasmic side, KASH5 transmits microtubule-generated forces to chromosome telomeres. These forces drive telomere-led chromosome movements required for efficient homolog search, pairing, synapsis, recombination, and progression through meiotic prophase I (garner2023themeioticlinc pages 12-12, park2024cargospecificityregulation pages 5-5, kmonickova2020theroleof pages 6-9).

Human biallelic KASH5 variants are associated with non-obstructive azoospermia (NOA), meiotic arrest, diminished ovarian reserve or premature ovarian insufficiency, and recurrent miscarriage. A 2024 study extended the phenotype to recurrent androgenetic hydatidiform mole, although this last association rests on very limited case evidence (hou2023ahomozygouskash5 pages 1-2, rezaei2024defectsinmeiosis pages 1-2).

## 2. Protein architecture and membrane topology

KASH5 has an N-terminal cytoplasmic region containing tandem EF-hand-like domains, an extended coiled-coil region, and a C-terminal tail-anchor comprising a transmembrane segment followed by a short KASH sequence in the perinuclear space. Early comparative annotation placed the EF-hand-like region approximately at residues 18–109 and estimated a roughly 190-residue central coiled coil. The human and mouse proteins were estimated at approximately 62.7 and 64.4 kDa, respectively (horn2013amammaliankash pages 2-3).

The KASH terminus binds SUN-domain proteins in the lumen between the inner and outer nuclear membranes. Consequently, KASH5’s N-terminal EF-hand/coiled-coil region projects into the cytoplasm, where it engages dynein. In cultured human cells, KASH5 and its isolated KASH region localized to the nuclear envelope; dominant-negative SUN1 or combined SUN1/SUN2 depletion disrupted this localization, demonstrating SUN-dependent nuclear-envelope retention (horn2013amammaliankash pages 2-3).

The EF-hand pair is structurally and functionally important but should not be annotated simply as a conventional calcium sensor. Substitution of predicted calcium-coordinating positions impaired or abolished dynein binding, yet purified KASH5 did not detectably bind calcium and cellular calcium changes did not control the interaction. Current evidence therefore favors an EF-hand-derived structural interface for dynein LIC recognition rather than calcium-regulated signaling (garner2023themeioticlinc pages 13-14).

## 3. Cellular localization

KASH5 operates principally at the **outer nuclear membrane of meiotic germ cells during prophase I**. Its KASH tail contacts SUN1/SUN2 in the perinuclear space, while its large N-terminal region remains cytoplasmic. On the nucleoplasmic side, the SUN-containing assembly connects indirectly to telomeric proteins; on the cytoplasmic side, KASH5 recruits dynein, dynactin, and associated regulators (park2024cargospecificityregulation pages 5-5, kmonickova2020theroleof pages 6-9).

Mouse spermatocyte imaging showed KASH5 colocalization with the telomere protein TRF2 and SUN1 at chromosome ends. It appears from leptotene, persists through pachytene and diplotene, and disappears by metaphase I as telomeres detach from the nuclear envelope. Expression was detected mainly in testis and ovary during early oogenesis rather than broadly across somatic tissues, consistent with a meiosis-specialized protein (morimoto2012aconservedkash pages 2-3).

SUN1 is the principal partner, but SUN2 can form residual or alternative KASH5-containing meiotic LINC complexes in mouse spermatocytes. Thus, “SUN1–KASH5 complex” is the best primary annotation, while SUN2 provides partially redundant context-dependent support rather than an entirely separate pathway (kmonickova2020theroleof pages 6-9).

## 4. Primary molecular function: transmembrane dynein-activating adaptor

The decisive mechanistic advance was recognition that KASH5 does more than tether an already active motor: it directly **activates cytoplasmic dynein-1**. Purified-component and cell-recruitment studies showed that KASH5 engages either dynein light intermediate chain LIC1 or LIC2 and supports formation of motile dynein–dynactin complexes. It is therefore regarded as the first established transmembrane dynein-activating adaptor (garner2023themeioticlinc pages 12-12, park2024cargospecificityregulation pages 5-5, garner2023themeioticlinc pages 13-14).

The binding interface follows a mechanism shared with other dynein activating adaptors. A conserved helix in the C-terminal region of LIC1—helix 1, residues 440–456—binds the KASH5 EF-hand region. Deleting both LIC helices reduced KASH5 association to 8.0% of the full-length reference, while helix-1 mutations or deletion reduced pull-down to approximately 1.9–3.9% of wild type. A construct lacking only helix 2 retained 177% of reference pull-down, identifying helix 1 rather than helix 2 as the crucial element (garner2023themeioticlinc pages 11-12).

Complex assembly appears hierarchical. KASH5 first captures dynein through LIC; LIS1 promotes or stabilizes dynein’s open conformation; dynactin is then incorporated through dynein intermediate-chain/p150 interactions and broader contacts with the adaptor. Experimentally, disrupting dynactin did not prevent initial dynein/LIS1 recruitment to KASH5, whereas LIS1 depletion disproportionately impaired dynactin incorporation. A candidate Spindly-like motif around KASH5 residues 371–376 may contribute to dynactin engagement, but that specific interaction remains less firmly established (garner2023themeioticlinc pages 12-12, garner2023themeioticlinc pages 5-6).

KASH5 therefore has no catalytic substrate of its own. Its relevant “cargo” is the telomere-attached meiotic chromosome, coupled across the nuclear envelope by the SUN–KASH bridge. Dynein hydrolyzes ATP and walks along microtubules; KASH5 specifies, assembles, and activates that transport machinery at the nuclear envelope.

## 5. Biological process and pathway

The functional pathway can be summarized as:

**meiotic telomere complex → SUN1/SUN2 at the inner nuclear membrane → KASH5 across the outer nuclear membrane → LIC-bound dynein + LIS1 + dynactin → microtubule-dependent telomere/chromosome movement.**

During meiotic prophase I, telomeres attach to the nuclear envelope and undergo rapid movements, including transient clustering or “bouquet” organization. Restricting chromosome motion to the nuclear surface facilitates a two-dimensional homology search and helps align homologous chromosomes for synaptonemal-complex formation and crossover recombination. KASH5 is consequently upstream of normal homolog pairing and synapsis, although it is not itself a recombinase or structural component of the synaptonemal complex (park2024cargospecificityregulation pages 5-5, morimoto2012aconservedkash pages 2-3).

Mouse loss-of-function evidence shows that telomere attachment can persist in some contexts while effective homolog movement and pairing fail, followed by arrest before pachytene and infertility. This distinction indicates that KASH5’s central role is force coupling and motor activation, not merely passive attachment of telomeres to the envelope (hou2023ahomozygouskash5 pages 2-3).

## 6. Human genetics and reproductive phenotypes

### Male infertility

Human biallelic variants consistently implicate KASH5 in severe spermatogenic failure, particularly meiotic arrest and NOA. The transmembrane variant **p.Leu535Gln (L535Q)** was reported homozygously in two azoospermic brothers. Functional studies showed that reduced transmembrane-segment hydrophobicity redirects L535Q KASH5 from the endoplasmic reticulum/outer nuclear membrane to mitochondria. The most plausible pathogenic mechanism is therefore loss of KASH5 from the meiotic nuclear envelope—not major mitochondrial toxicity—preventing assembly of the telomere–LINC–dynein apparatus (bentebbal2022ahumaninfertilityassociated pages 8-9, bentebbal2022ahumaninfertilityassociated pages 1-2).

In a 2023 consanguineous Pakistani family, homozygous **c.1270_1273del, p.Arg424Thrfs*20** occurred in five siblings. The affected brother had absent testicular KASH5 protein, NOA, and meiotic arrest before pachytene. This human cellular phenotype closely matches the predicted consequence of failed telomere-driven chromosome movement and the mouse knockout phenotype (hou2023ahomozygouskash5 pages 1-2).

### Female infertility and pregnancy loss

The same p.Arg424Thrfs*20 family showed marked sex-dependent expressivity. Four affected sisters had diminished ovarian reserve rather than complete ovarian failure. Three experienced at least three miscarriages each, all during the third month; another had never conceived but retained a dominant follicle at age 35. In cultured cells, the truncated protein still encircled the nucleus but interacted more weakly with SUN1. The authors proposed that residual function may permit folliculogenesis and fertilization while increasing production of chromosomally abnormal oocytes and subsequent miscarriage (hou2023ahomozygouskash5 pages 8-9, hou2023ahomozygouskash5 pages 1-2).

Other 2022 reports linked homozygous KASH5 variants to premature ovarian insufficiency through disordered homolog pairing and linked biallelic variants to male meiotic arrest/NOA. Collectively, the evidence supports an autosomal-recessive KASH5-related reproductive disorder with variable female severity, but the number of independently characterized families remains small (hou2023ahomozygouskash5 pages 11-11).

### Hydatidiform mole: 2024 development

A November 2024 JCI study of meiosis-I genes identified a woman with three hydatidiform moles and two miscarriages carrying KASH5 **p.Arg519*** and **p.Leu535Gln** variants. Their CADD scores were 36 and 25.6, respectively; ACMG interpretation remained cautious, even though L535Q already had functional and infertility evidence. This observation plausibly links defective maternal meiosis I to androgenetic mole formation, but it represents an emerging association rather than a population-level estimate or definitive expansion of the KASH5 phenotype (rezaei2024defectsinmeiosis pages 1-2, rezaei2024defectsinmeiosis pages 14-15).

## 7. Recent developments, 2023–2024

The major 2023 molecular development was detailed biochemical placement of KASH5 within the activating-adaptor family. Garner and colleagues resolved the dependence on LIC helix 1, showed that either LIC paralog can support recruitment, defined roles for LIS1 and dynactin, and demonstrated that the EF hands function structurally rather than as a conventional calcium switch (garner2023themeioticlinc pages 11-12, garner2023themeioticlinc pages 12-12, garner2023themeioticlinc pages 13-14).

The major 2023 clinical development was expansion from complete meiotic failure to a sex-dimorphic spectrum comprising male NOA, female diminished ovarian reserve, and recurrent miscarriage within one family. This finding argues against assuming that the same biallelic variant necessarily produces equivalent germ-cell depletion in both sexes (hou2023ahomozygouskash5 pages 8-9, hou2023ahomozygouskash5 pages 1-2).

In 2024, authoritative dynein reviews incorporated KASH5 as a newly established cargo-specific activating adaptor, confirming that the field now regards active motor assembly—not passive tethering—as its core molecular function. The recurrent-mole study further suggested that partial or variant-specific disruption may permit conception but predispose to abnormal meiotic products and pregnancy pathology (park2024cargospecificityregulation pages 5-5, rezaei2024defectsinmeiosis pages 1-2).

## 8. Current and potential applications

The immediate real-world application is **genetic diagnosis and counseling**, not targeted therapy. KASH5 is a rational gene-panel or exome-analysis candidate in:

- NOA with spermatocyte arrest, especially zygotene/pre-pachytene arrest;
- unexplained premature ovarian insufficiency or diminished ovarian reserve;
- familial reproductive failure affecting both sexes;
- recurrent miscarriage where a meiotic defect is suspected; and
- possibly recurrent androgenetic hydatidiform mole.

Variant interpretation should incorporate domain location and functional assays. Transmembrane substitutions may alter organelle targeting, truncations may weaken SUN binding or remove the KASH tail, and N-terminal EF-hand/coiled-coil variants may impair dynein recruitment. Because reported families are few, pathogenic classification should not rely on gene plausibility alone; segregation, rarity, reproductive histology, protein localization, SUN interaction, and dynein-binding assays are particularly valuable (bentebbal2022ahumaninfertilityassociated pages 8-9, hou2023ahomozygouskash5 pages 1-2, rezaei2024defectsinmeiosis pages 1-2).

There is no established KASH5-specific drug, replacement strategy, or clinical trial. Assisted-reproduction decisions remain phenotype dependent. Complete male meiotic arrest predicts poor prospects for recovering mature sperm, whereas partially affected women may still ovulate but face diminished reserve, miscarriage, or abnormal conceptions. These implications require individualized reproductive-genetics counseling rather than a uniform prognosis.

## 9. Evidence quality and unresolved questions

The molecular annotation is strong because multiple independent approaches converge: meiotic colocalization, SUN-dependent envelope targeting, protein interaction mapping, purified-motor reconstitution, cell-based recruitment, mutagenesis, and mouse loss-of-function phenotypes. The conclusion that KASH5 is a dynein-activating LINC adaptor is considerably firmer than any individual disease association (garner2023themeioticlinc pages 11-12, garner2023themeioticlinc pages 12-12, horn2013amammaliankash pages 2-3, morimoto2012aconservedkash pages 2-3).

The human disease evidence is compelling but numerically limited. Most reports involve single consanguineous families or a few affected individuals; penetrance and genotype–phenotype relationships are therefore not yet reliably quantified. In particular, the determinants of female phenotypic variation—complete ovarian insufficiency versus diminished reserve, miscarriage, or molar pregnancy—remain unresolved.

Additional priorities include structural determination of a full KASH5–dynein–dynactin complex, clarification of SUN1 versus SUN2 usage in human meiocytes, definition of how KASH5 motor activity is temporally switched on and off, and larger genetic cohorts with detailed meiotic histology. The apparent calcium independence of its EF hands and the proposed dynactin-contact motif also merit direct high-resolution testing.

## Final functional annotation

**KASH5/CCDC155 (UniProt Q8N6L0) is a meiosis-specific, single-pass outer-nuclear-membrane KASH protein and dynein-1 activating adaptor. Its luminal C-terminal KASH tail binds SUN1/SUN2, while its cytoplasmic EF-hand-like and coiled-coil regions recruit dynein through LIC1/LIC2 and support LIS1/dynactin-dependent motor activation. This LINC–motor assembly transmits microtubule forces to telomeres during meiotic prophase I, enabling chromosome movement needed for homolog pairing, synapsis, recombination, and gametogenesis. Human biallelic loss-of-function or mistargeting variants cause a recessive reproductive phenotype encompassing meiotic-arrest NOA and variable female ovarian insufficiency, diminished reserve, miscarriage, and potentially recurrent androgenetic hydatidiform mole.**
