CC   -!- FUNCTION: Core component of the complement C1 complex, a multiprotein
CC       complex that initiates the classical pathway of the complement system,
CC       a cascade of proteins that leads to phagocytosis and breakdown of
CC       pathogens and signaling that strengthens the adaptive immune system
CC       (PubMed:12847249, PubMed:19006321, PubMed:24626930, PubMed:29449492,
CC       PubMed:3258649, PubMed:34155115, PubMed:6249812, PubMed:6776418). The
CC       classical complement pathway is initiated by the C1Q subcomplex of the
CC       C1 complex, which specifically binds IgG or IgM immunoglobulins
CC       complexed with antigens, forming antigen-antibody complexes on the
CC       surface of pathogens: C1QA, together with C1QB and C1QC, specifically
CC       recognizes and binds the Fc regions of IgG or IgM via its C1q domain
CC       (PubMed:12847249, PubMed:19006321, PubMed:24626930, PubMed:29449492,
CC       PubMed:3258649, PubMed:6776418). Immunoglobulin-binding activates the
CC       proenzyme C1R, which cleaves C1S, initiating the proteolytic cascade of
CC       the complement system (PubMed:29449492). The C1Q subcomplex is
CC       activated by a hexamer of IgG complexed with antigens, while it is
CC       activated by a pentameric IgM (PubMed:19706439, PubMed:24626930,
CC       PubMed:29449492). The C1Q subcomplex also recognizes and binds
CC       phosphatidylserine exposed on the surface of cells undergoing
CC       programmed cell death, possibly promoting activation of the complement
CC       system (PubMed:18250442). {ECO:0000269|PubMed:12847249,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:18250442, ECO:0000269|PubMed:19006321,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:19706439, ECO:0000269|PubMed:24626930,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:29449492, ECO:0000269|PubMed:3258649,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:34155115, ECO:0000269|PubMed:6249812,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:6776418}.
CC   -!- ACTIVITY REGULATION: The C1Q subcomplex is inhibited by sulfated
CC       molecules, such as triterpenoid sulfates, heparan sulfate, or
CC       chondroitin sulfates. {ECO:0000269|PubMed:20548024,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:6218163}.
CC   -!- SUBUNIT: Core component of the complement C1 complex, a calcium-
CC       dependent complex composed of 1 molecule of the C1Q subcomplex, 2
CC       molecules of C1R and 2 molecules of C1S (PubMed:23650384,
CC       PubMed:29449492, PubMed:34155115, PubMed:6249812). The C1Q subcomplex
CC       is composed 18 subunits: 3 chains of C1QA, C1QB, and C1QC trimerize to
CC       form 6 collagen-like triple helices connected to six globular ligand-
CC       recognition modules (C1q domain) (PubMed:12960167, PubMed:18250442,
CC       PubMed:20548024, PubMed:29449492, PubMed:2988513, PubMed:6952210).
CC       {ECO:0000269|PubMed:12960167, ECO:0000269|PubMed:18250442,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:20548024, ECO:0000269|PubMed:23650384,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:29449492, ECO:0000269|PubMed:2988513,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:34155115, ECO:0000269|PubMed:6249812,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:6952210}.

CC   -!- DOMAIN: The collagen-like domain interacts with C1R and C1S proenzymes.
CC       {ECO:0000269|PubMed:29449492}.
CC   -!- PTM: Hydroxylated on lysine and proline residues. Hydroxylated lysine
CC       residues can be glycosylated. Human C1Q contains up to 68.3
CC       hydroxylysine-galactosylglucose residues and up to 2.5 hydroxylysine-
CC       galactose per molecule. Total percentage hydroxylysine residues
CC       glycosylated is 86.4%. {ECO:0000269|PubMed:486087,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:6286235, ECO:0000269|PubMed:708376}.
CC   -!- DISEASE: C1q deficiency 2 (C1QD2) [MIM:620321]: An autosomal recessive
CC       disorder caused by impaired activation of the complement classical
CC       pathway. It generally leads to severe immune complex disease
CC       characterized by recurrent skin lesions, chronic infections, an
CC       increased risk of systemic lupus erythematosus, and glomerulonephritis.
CC       {ECO:0000269|PubMed:9476130}. Note=The disease is caused by variants
CC       affecting the gene represented in this entry.
