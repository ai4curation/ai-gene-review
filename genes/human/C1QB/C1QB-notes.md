## Recognition and assembly

Human C1q consists of six A/B/C heterotrimers, each with a collagen-like stem and globular head. Recombinant human B-chain globular modules recognize immunoglobulins, with greater IgG than IgM binding in the reported assays. The independently folded MBP modules were produced in E. coli; the intact protein retains multivalent heterotrimeric organization. Thus B-chain-specific recognition is supported, but isolated-module preferences should not be equated with the complete serum molecule's affinity or geometry ([PMID:12847249](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12847249/)). The human C1q scFv study implicates B-chain Arg108/Arg109 while mutagenizing A/C modules; it is not described here as an isolated B-chain mutation experiment ([PMID:19006321](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19006321/)).

The first core uses immunoglobulin binding with classical complement activation. Full-length human C1q assembled in HEK293-F cells provides direct evidence for structural participation: mutation of mature B-chain Lys61 interferes with recruitment of the C1r2C1s2 tetramer and activation, without abolishing the tested ligand-recognition properties. This is work performed by the B chain, not a downstream necessity phenotype. Lys61 is mature-chain numbering, corresponding to position 88 in the precursor. C1r and C1s perform proteolysis; C1QB does not acquire their catalytic activity by being in C1 ([PMID:23650384](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23650384/)).

Antibody hexamerization, C1–IgG structures and human C1q/C1 binding to antibody-coated bacterial and model surfaces corroborate recognition and activation at the assembled-complex level. The 1988 ligand-mutagenesis paper tests mouse IgG2b, not a human B-chain mutant. These distinctions are preserved in individual reasons ([PMID:24626930](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24626930/), [PMID:29449492](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29449492/), [PMID:34155115](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34155115/), [PMID:3258649](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3258649/)). Extracellular cell-surface association means deposited ligand-bound C1q, not a transmembrane B chain. The microbial-surface annotation is supported by actual S. aureus experiments.

## Activities contributed by the assembled complex

The second core represents a contribution to phosphatidylserine binding. Human serum C1q and its heterotrimeric globular region directly recognize PS in biochemical and apoptotic-HeLa-cell experiments. The inspected crystal Results place the principal phosphoserine-binding pocket on the C chain; an A-chain contact involves a neighboring crystal molecule. These experiments do not establish autonomous B-chain PS recognition. The original GOA row is retained unchanged, including its qualifier, while the authored core makes the complex contribution explicit ([PMID:18250442](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18250442/)). No new apoptotic-clearance process is added merely because C1q binding accompanies cell removal.

The third core represents a contribution to receptor-ligand activity. Human C1q and globular fragments bind CD33; monocyte experiments demonstrate CD33 ITIM phosphorylation, and whole C1q is required for coordinated CD33/LAIR-1 engagement. This supports signaling by a ligand assembly. It does not show that a purified B chain alone activates either receptor, that C1QB is a kinase, or that activating an inhibitory receptor makes the ligand an antagonist ([PMID:28325905](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28325905/)). The distinct human ghB–gC1qR interaction is directly supported by purified-module assays, but gC1qR binding and PBMC proliferation alone do not identify an equivalent receptor-signaling mechanism ([PMID:28018340](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28018340/)).

The three cores separate B-module immunoglobulin recognition, contributed lipid recognition, and contributed inhibitory-receptor ligand activity. They describe cooperating functions of C1q rather than three independent B-chain enzymes. The two contribution cores omit an unsupported autonomous molecular function. Complex membership is recorded in `in_complex`, not anatomical locations.
