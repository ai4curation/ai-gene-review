Gene Description

C1QB encodes the B chain of the secreted complement recognition protein C1q. Six copies each of the A, B and C chains assemble into six collagen-like stems ending in heterotrimeric globular heads. The B-chain globular module recognizes immunoglobulins, while its collagen-like region helps recruit the C1r/C1s protease tetramer. These recognition and assembly functions initiate the classical complement pathway without conferring protease activity on C1QB. As part of intact C1q, the B chain also contributes to phosphatidylserine recognition and inhibitory immunoreceptor signaling. Complement-dependent synaptic remodeling has been demonstrated in rodents; these studies generally test the assembled C1q system rather than an autonomous B-chain activity.

Recognition and assembly

Human C1q consists of six A/B/C heterotrimers, each with a collagen-like stem and globular head. Recombinant human B-chain globular modules recognize immunoglobulins, with greater IgG than IgM binding in the reported assays. The independently folded MBP modules were produced in E. coli; the intact protein retains multivalent heterotrimeric organization. Thus B-chain-specific recognition is supported, but isolated-module preferences should not be equated with the complete serum molecule's affinity or geometry (PMID:12847249(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12847249/)). The human C1q scFv study implicates B-chain Arg108/Arg109 while mutagenizing A/C modules; it is not described here as an isolated B-chain mutation experiment (PMID:19006321(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19006321/)).

The first core uses immunoglobulin binding with classical complement activation. Full-length human C1q assembled in HEK293-F cells provides direct evidence for structural participation: mutation of mature B-chain Lys61 interferes with recruitment of the C1r2C1s2 tetramer and activation, without abolishing the tested ligand-recognition properties. This is work performed by the B chain, not a downstream necessity phenotype. Lys61 is mature-chain numbering, corresponding to position 88 in the precursor. C1r and C1s perform proteolysis; C1QB does not acquire their catalytic activity by being in C1 (PMID:23650384(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23650384/)).

Antibody hexamerization, C1–IgG structures and human C1q/C1 binding to antibody-coated bacterial and model surfaces corroborate recognition and activation at the assembled-complex level. The 1988 ligand-mutagenesis paper tests mouse IgG2b, not a human B-chain mutant. These distinctions are preserved in individual reasons (PMID:24626930(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24626930/), PMID:29449492(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29449492/), PMID:34155115(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34155115/), PMID:3258649(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3258649/)).
