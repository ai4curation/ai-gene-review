# GABRG2 source guide

GABRG2 contributes the gamma2 subunit to heteromeric GABA-A receptors. The review separates human receptor function, ortholog localization, specific partner assertions and recombinant construct evidence. It contains 74 existing contexts and 31 manually assessed references, with no proposed NEW annotation.

## Identity and receptor composition

The reviewed human entry is [UniProt P18507](https://www.uniprot.org/uniprotkb/P18507/entry). Direct sequences and independent variation reconstruction agree on the displayed long product P18507-2 (475 residues), short P18507-1 (467) and inserted-segment P18507-3 (515). The eight-residue deletion distinguishes the short form. Purification tags and engineered M3–M4 loop replacements are experimental constructs, not natural product definitions. PMID:30602789 Methods explicitly identifies the long P18507-2 backbone despite the UniProt reference-position range 1–467. Native human mass spectrometry reports short and long gamma2, without assigning a splice product to every structural map.

The core is contribution to a GABA-gated chloride channel in a heteromeric GABA-A receptor complex. Gamma2 contributes pore structure and the alpha/gamma benzodiazepine interface. The orthosteric GABA interfaces are principally beta/alpha interfaces; neither an autonomous gamma2 channel nor gamma2-specific GABA binding is asserted. Receptor composition and the chloride gradient constrain physiological interpretation.

Histamine responsiveness is a receptor-composition context, not proof of gamma2 histamine binding. Axonal, vesicular and extrasynaptic pools remain distinct from the principal postsynaptic role. PMID:25172774 directly concerns gamma2 transport-vesicle delivery; it is not used to manufacture matching alpha1 or beta3 evidence.
