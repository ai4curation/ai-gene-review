# DKC1 biological notes

Dyskerin performs two complementary kinds of work. Its conserved catalytic domain converts uridine to pseudouridine in RNA within H/ACA guide particles. Human DKC1 perturbation reduces rRNA modification and selected mRNA sites; the guide-dependent rRNA mechanism differs from the guide-independent term originally attached to one source. The conserved snRNA activity has direct yeast U2 guide-RNA, nuclease/rescue and catalytic-mutant evidence, with human H/ACA scaRNP membership providing a compatible target context. The yeast experiments are not human enzyme assays. [PMID:25219674](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25219674/), [PMID:15962000](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15962000/), [PMID:19179534](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19179534/).

In telomerase, dyskerin binds the H/ACA region of hTR and contributes its structure and stability. Human active-holoenzyme purification and cryo-EM distinguish the dyskerin-containing H/ACA units from the TERT catalytic lobe. Dyskerin depletion accelerates hTR loss, and inhibition of decay pathways can restore RNA and telomerase activity. Thus whole-complex activity and a contributes_to molecular-function claim are justified, while autonomous reverse-transcriptase activity is not. The older antisense paper remains abstract-only; the distinction rests on the independently established mechanism, not on silence in that abstract. [PMID:29695869](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29695869/), [PMID:26950371](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26950371/), [PMID:12135483](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12135483/).

The canonical official product is 514 residues. O60832-2 is named isoform 3 and is 420 residues, sharing residues 1–419 then ending in Arg420. The reported cytoplasmic variant is retained without assuming that preserved Asp125/PUA sequence proves catalytic equivalence or inactivity. Historical endogenous and tagged constructs are not automatically assigned to a current natural isoform. Nuclear dyskerin occupies nucleolar, nucleoplasmic and Cajal-body pools; the nucleolar snoRNP and Cajal-body scaRNP contexts belong to one RNA-isomerization mechanism, not to a claim that every substrate is modified in every listed compartment. [PMID:21820037](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21820037/), [PMID:10556300](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10556300/), [PMID:23726835](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23726835/).

Literal PAINT reconstruction places all six ancestral assertions on the human lineage. The target sequence matches the 514-residue official record within the 277-member tree/alignment. Experimental grounding includes human, yeast, rat and mouse contexts; a target appearing among descendant sources is valid grounding. The yeast snoRNA maturation phenotype is a structural assembly/quality-control role and is not a claim that dyskerin performs RNA cleavage. [PMID:15964797](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15964797/), [PMID:9472021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9472021/).

Protein associations are judged by their actual evidence. RUVBL1 has recombinant dyskerin-binding support, permitting an ATPase partner-class refinement without assigning ATP hydrolysis to dyskerin. In contrast, the DHX36 association depends on TERC and the Npro capture is an RNP-proteome observation with an explicit indirect-association possibility. Both remain positive non-core associations. NAF1/SHQ1 assembly functions and TCAB1 RNA-chaperone activity do not automatically establish the protein-folding-chaperone definition. [PMID:18358808](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18358808/), [PMID:21846770](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21846770/), [PMID:24965446](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24965446/), [PMID:19383767](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19383767/).

The human UV-capture study supports mRNA binding through its exact DKC1 Table S1 row and stringent RNA-protein crosslinking protocol. This does not identify the poly(A) tail as the contact site. The imported long RNAcentral telomerase-RNA record contains the canonical 451-nt sequence, but no historical construct endpoint is inferred from that database record. [PMID:22658674](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22658674/), [PMID:18082603](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18082603/), [PMID:22527283](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22527283/).
