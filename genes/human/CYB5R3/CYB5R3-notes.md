## Catalysis and products

The core reaction is FAD-dependent electron transfer from NADH to cytochrome b5. Human kinetic and structural studies support the specific NADH term, while the D239G variant illustrates that mutant cofactor specificity can change. The 301-residue membrane precursor, official 278-residue soluble product, and 334-residue alternative N terminus were reconstructed independently from official sequence changes and FASTAs. A 275-residue crystal or ΔN26 construct is not the 278-residue splice product. The 334-residue product is not assigned equivalent function by sequence resemblance alone.

PMID24500710 explicitly uses His-tagged human isoform2 in a reconstituted NADH–CYB5R3–cytochrome-b5–mARC system. CYB5R3 performs electron delivery; mARC molybdenum performs terminal nitrite reduction. This supports contextual NO biosynthesis but does not make the system an arginine/NADPH/oxygen-dependent nitric-oxide-synthase complex. The source’s contributes_to qualifier remains intact; GO0050421’s cytochrome-c donor does not match the actual b5 system, and no defective broad-parent wording is forced as a replacement. PMID23123858 supports Hb-alpha association, rather than oxygen-carrying hemoglobin tetramer membership.

## Primary evidence and functional products: follow-up

CYB5R3 performs the NADH-to-cytochrome-b5 reductase step. The human Ser127 study directly assayed the proteins [PMID:1898726 "with cytochrome b5 as an electron acceptor"]. The 1992 kinetic study and the later Cys203Tyr, D239G and F298-deletion studies separate catalytic/cofactor effects from protein instability; the D239G effect on NADPH specificity belongs to that variant and is not generalized to wild type. Short exact anchors and access limits are now attached to the individual references.

Membrane anchoring and soluble erythroid function are distinct biological contexts for the shared reductase domain. The 1998 human study reports that [PMID:9639531 "the S-transcript generates soluble b5R"], with late erythroid expression and soluble product formation in transfected HeLa cells. Internal initiation remains proposed in its accessible abstract. The 2014 reconstitution explicitly used the human 278-residue His-tagged product; the older 275-residue crystal fragment is not that exact isoform. Curated functional classes map the M and S forms, while the three original product records and every imported source isoform field remain unchanged. No equivalent function is assigned to the 334-residue alternative N terminus.

In the nitrite-reducing system, CYB5R3 delivers electrons through CYB5B to mARC. The original active-site, component-omission and dithionite experiments support the conclusion that [PMID:24500710 "mARC is the catalytic subunit"]. This is a real contribution to the measured NO-producing system; it does not establish the distinct cytochrome-c-defined GO:0050421 reaction. Row93 is therefore now over-annotated for that exact class, superseding its earlier unresolved classification. The broader ontology wording question and contextual NO-biosynthesis role remain separate.

The vascular source used [PMID:23123858 "co-immunoprecipitation from cell lysates and purified proteins"] to support the Hb-alpha association. This positive result and the reductase's heme-redox maintenance do not assign CYB5R3 as a globin subunit of the oxygen-carrying hemoglobin complex. Row85 is now over-annotated on that class distinction, while the unavailable 1992 full text and positive Hb-alpha binding remain explicit. This does not claim that the earlier source contained no association assay.

HuRI reports physical interactions in the recorded yeast assay context. A supported binding-class refinement names the established class of the actual matched partner; it does not give CYB5R3 that partner's catalysis, transport, fusion or signaling activity. Thus lack of demonstrated native-human receptor activation is not itself a reason to erase a binding-class term. TNFRSF10C remains a contextual generic positive at its decoy-receptor scope. ASGR1 also has reported JAK2–STAT3 signaling; its positive pair is conservatively left unrefined here, without excluding the defensible signaling-receptor-binding option or requiring CYB5R3 agonism. P23763-3 is the literal VAMP1 partner accession, officially named isoform 2; its accession suffix is not the product name.

The YAML source guide provides public DOI, dataset and interaction locators, retaining exact assay and product limits. It is navigation rather than independent experimental evidence and is no longer cited as annotation/core support. Original notes above remain an unchanged historical prefix. Broad membrane observations and the unresolved granule-lumen/release claims retain their distinct evidence scope; no compartment conclusion is inferred merely from the existence of a more specific annotation elsewhere.
