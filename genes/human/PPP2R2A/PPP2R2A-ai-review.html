Gene Description

PPP2R2A encodes B55-alpha (PR55-alpha/B-alpha/R2-alpha), the alpha isoform of the B/B55/PR55 family of regulatory subunits of protein phosphatase 2A. It is a seven-bladed WD40 beta-propeller that latches onto the PP2A HEAT-repeat scaffold subunit (PPP2R1A/B) through a protruding beta-hairpin arm, forming a heterotrimeric holoenzyme with a catalytic subunit (PPP2CA/B). B55-alpha has no catalytic centre of its own; its function is to select, recruit and position phosphoproteins on an acidic groove on the top face of the propeller so that the catalytic subunit can hydrolyse phospho-serine and phospho-threonine, and thereby to set the substrate specificity, localisation and timing of that holoenzyme's activity. Substrates are recognised through basic patches flanking a proline-directed phosphosite and through structured alpha-helices that dock onto conserved surfaces of the propeller; phospho-threonine sites are preferred over the equivalent phospho-serine sites. PP2A-B55 is the principal phosphatase that reverses cyclin B-CDK1 phosphorylation: it is held inactive at mitotic entry by the endosulfines ENSA and ARPP19 once these are phosphorylated by the Greatwall kinase MASTL, and by the inhibitor PABIR1/FAM122A, both of which bind B55-alpha itself and occlude the same platform that substrates use; as cyclin B is destroyed and the inhibitors are released the holoenzyme dephosphorylates CDK1 substrates in an ordered sequence, driving spindle disassembly, chromatin decondensation, nuclear envelope reformation (through BANF1, with ANKLE2 as a targeting factor) and Golgi reassembly, and counteracting Ki-67-dependent chromosome individualisation. Outside mitosis the same holoenzyme dephosphorylates WEE1 and so stabilises it, reinforcing the G2/M checkpoint under replication stress, and acts on pRB-family proteins p107 and p130, hyperphosphorylated tau, FOXM1, HSF1, the CREB coactivator CRTC3, DAPK1 and AKT. B55-alpha is a broadly expressed, soluble protein found in both cytoplasm and nucleus, with a synaptic pool in brain; loss of the mouse orthologue is embryonic lethal, and the human gene is recurrently deleted in prostate and other epithelial cancers.

PPP2R2A / PP2A-B55α Functional Annotation Report

Executive summary

Identity is verified. Human PPP2R2A encodes the approximately 55-kDa PP2A regulatory subunit B55α (also Bα, PR55α, or R2α), corresponding to UniProt P63151. The literature consistently assigns PPP2R2A to the B/B55 regulatory-subunit family; its seven-bladed WD40 β-propeller matches the supplied PP2A_PR55 and WD40-domain annotations. No conflicting gene-symbol usage was encountered. (goguetrubio2020pp2ab55holoenzymeregulation pages 1-3, lacroix2022structuralenzymaticand pages 1-2, fowle2019pp2aholoenzymessubstrate pages 29-31)

PPP2R2A is not itself an enzyme active site. It assembles with a PP2A A scaffold subunit and a PP2A-C catalytic subunit to create a heterotrimeric PP2A-B55α holoenzyme. B55α recruits and positions selected phosphoproteins, while PP2A-C hydrolyzes phosphoserine/phosphothreonine ester bonds. Its best-established primary role is therefore substrate selection, spatial targeting, and temporal control of PP2A phosphatase activity, especially reversal of cyclin-B–CDK1 phosphorylation during mitotic exit. (lacroix2022structuralenzymaticand pages 2-4, lacroix2022structuralenzymaticand pages 1-2, fowle2019pp2aholoenzymessubstrate pages 29-31)

The major recent advance is a 2024 revision of the substrate-recognition model: rather than relying only on diffuse charge or a short linear motif, many partners bind through structured α-helices engaging conserved hydrophobic and electrostatic patches on B55α. This work also connected PP2A-B55 to RBM7 and the nuclear-exosome-targeting complex and produced a designed competitive B55 inhibitor as a research and therapeutic-proof-of-concept tool. (kruse2024substraterecognitionprinciples pages 7-8, kruse2024substraterecognitionprinciples pages 1-2)

Zhao et al., Dec 2019; Fowle et al., Jan 2019 (fowle2019pp2aholoenzymessubstrate pages 15-17, zhao2019ppp2r2aprostatecancer pages 13-14)

1. Molecular identity and architecture

The canonical PP2A holoenzyme contains:

  1. a catalytic C subunit, PPP2CA or PPP2CB;
  2. a HEAT-repeat A scaffold, PPP2R1A or PPP2R1B; and
  3. one regulatory B subunit, here PPP2R2A/B55α.

The regulatory subunit is a major determinant of substrate specificity and localization. More than 90 combinatorial PP2A assemblies are possible, making “PP2A activity” an aggregate of biochemically distinct holoenzymes rather than one uniform enzyme. (goguetrubio2020pp2ab55holoenzymeregulation pages 1-3)

B55-family proteins form seven-bladed WD40-repeat β-propellers with an acidic central groove. B55α contacts approximately HEAT repeats 1–7 of the A scaffold and makes comparatively few contacts with PP2A-C. The active-site-facing surface is acidic and lies beside the catalytic center, creating a composite recruitment-and-catalysis platform. Assembly is promoted by methylation of PP2A-C Leu309 and inhibited by PP2A-C phosphorylation at Thr304 or Tyr308. (goguetrubio2020pp2ab55holoenzymeregulation pages 1-3, lacroix2022structuralenzymaticand pages 1-2, fowle2019pp2aholoenzymessubstrate pages 29-31)

2. Primary biochemical function and reaction

The holoenzyme catalyzes:

phosphoprotein–O–PO₃²⁻ + H₂O → dephosphorylated protein–OH + inorganic phosphate.

The two-metal-ion catalytic center resides in PP2A-C, not PPP2R2A. B55α supplies substrate docking and helps orient the phosphosite near that center. Accordingly, PPP2R2A is best annotated as a regulatory/adaptor subunit of a serine/threonine protein phosphatase, rather than as an independently active phosphatase. (lacroix2022structuralenzymaticand pages 1-2, fowle2019pp2aholoenzymessubstrate pages 29-31)

Substrate specificity

Specificity is multilayered:

  • Phospho-amino-acid preference: PP2A-B55 generally dephosphorylates phosphothreonine-proline sites faster than equivalent phosphoserine-proline sites. Short TP peptides exhibited at least 34-fold greater turnover than matched SP peptides. This preference helps order mitotic exit, with many TP sites being removed before SP sites. This property is primarily attributed to the catalytic/holoenzyme context and should not be considered unique to B55α. (fowle2019pp2aholoenzymessubstrate pages 7-9)
  • Electrostatic recognition: mitotic substrates frequently contain a bipartite polybasic recognition determinant flanking a CDK1 site. Cooperative basic residues engage the acidic B55 surface, with stronger basicity affecting residence time and dephosphorylation kinetics. (cundell2016app2ab55recognition pages 1-2)
  • Structured α-helical docking: Kruse et al. used AlphaFold2, mutational scanning, binding assays, and protein design to show that diverse α-helices contact conserved B55α patches. One patch includes Y178, D197, M222, L225, and V228; another includes I284 and S287. Hydrophobic residues spaced along one helix face combine with charged contacts to confer specificity. This framework reconciles earlier “polybasic” and motif models with a structured docking mechanism. (kruse2024substraterecognitionprinciples pages 4-5, kruse2024substraterecognitionprinciples pages 1-2)

Direct or strongly supported B55α substrates/partners include Tau, PRC1, p107, pRB-family proteins, ATM, RPA2, and centrosomal proteins. Evidence strength varies: some were validated with purified holoenzyme and mutagenesis, whereas others were identified by interaction or phosphoproteomic changes. Tau contains highly basic Bα-binding regions—one reported region includes 11 lysines in 63 residues—consistent with electrostatic recruitment. (amin2022pp2ab55substratesand pages 8-8, fowle2019pp2aholoenzymessubstrate pages 6-7, zhao2019ppp2r2aprostatecancer pages 13-14)

3. Cellular localization

B55α is an intracellular, soluble holoenzyme component rather than a secreted or transmembrane protein. The evidence supports dynamic nuclear and cytoplasmic pools whose effective localization is imposed by holoenzyme assembly, substrates, and cell-cycle state. Functional PP2A-B55 pools act at mitotic chromosomes/spindle-associated structures, the anaphase midzone, Golgi and nuclear-envelope reassembly machinery, centrosomes, and nuclear DNA/RNA regulatory complexes. (fowle2019pp2aholoenzymessubstrate pages 7-9, zhao2019ppp2r2aprostatecancer pages 13-14, kruse2024substraterecognitionprinciples pages 7-8)

PPP2R2A is broadly expressed. Mouse-expression evidence cited in the knockout study reports particularly high levels in embryonic central nervous system and limbs and in adult brain, bladder, adrenal gland, ovary, and placenta. That distribution supports broad physiological relevance but does not by itself identify the active subcellular pool in each tissue. (panicker2020ppp2r2aknockoutmice pages 1-2)

4. Core pathways and biological processes

4.1 Mitotic entry and ordered mitotic exit

PP2A-B55 is a principal phosphatase opposing cyclin-B–CDK1. During mitosis, MASTL/Greatwall phosphorylates ENSA and ARPP19, converting them into high-affinity PP2A-B55 inhibitors. Cyclin-B destruction lowers CDK1 activity; Greatwall is then inactivated, phosphorylated ENSA is slowly dephosphorylated, and PP2A-B55 activity re-emerges. This delayed release permits ordered rather than simultaneous reversal of mitotic phosphorylation. (goguetrubio2020pp2ab55holoenzymeregulation pages 1-3, cundell2016app2ab55recognition pages 1-2)

Cundell et al. combined phosphoproteomics and kinetic modeling in human cells. Of 7,390 phosphopeptides modeled, 2,960 had a positive B55-dependent rate constant. PRC1 pThr481, a validated substrate required for anaphase spindle formation and cytokinesis, had a modeled B55-dependent rate constant of 0.121 min⁻¹. The analysis linked substrate basicity and phosphothreonine content to dephosphorylation order. These are PP2A-B55-family findings; individual peptides were not all shown to depend specifically on B55α rather than another B55 isoform. Published 22 August 2016; https://doi.org/10.1083/jcb.201606033. (cundell2016app2ab55recognition pages 1-2)

Validated pathway outputs include PRC1 activation, APC/C regulation through Cdc20/Cdh1 dephosphorylation, chromosome-passenger-complex relocation, spindle/cytokinesis control, and reassembly of Golgi and nuclear-envelope structures. Direct B55α evidence identifies it as the principal holoenzyme dephosphorylating PRC1 Thr481, while Mps1 Ser820, Thr675, and Thr685 are direct PP2A-B55 substrates. (amin2022pp2ab55substratesand pages 8-8, fowle2019pp2aholoenzymessubstrate pages 7-9)

4.2 Centrosome maintenance

In B55α-low human prostate-cancer cells, reconstitution of B55α decreased phosphorylation of centrosomal proteins including HAUS6, NEDD1, and CEP170, caused centrosome destabilization or collapse, prolonged the metaphase/anaphase checkpoint, disrupted chromosome segregation, and led to cell death. The enriched regulated sites were predominantly proline-directed serines, consistent with reversal of mitotic kinase signaling. This is strong α-isoform-specific evidence, although obtained in cancer cells adapted to reduced B55α. Published December 2019; https://doi.org/10.1038/s41389-019-0180-9. (zhao2019ppp2r2aprostatecancer pages 13-14)

4.3 Interphase and DNA-damage checkpoints

Reported α-specific functions include direct dephosphorylation of ATM Ser1981 and interaction with RPA2, influencing replication-stress-associated RPA2 phosphorylation. These findings place B55α in DNA-damage signaling, but its effect can depend on timing and substrate context rather than being uniformly activating or inhibitory. (amin2022pp2ab55substratesand pages 8-8)

A 2024 study established FAM122A as a competitive B55α inhibitor. Its conserved [RK]-V-x-x-[VI]-R element forms a helix that docks the B55α substrate-binding surface; an adjacent helix constrains access to the catalytic subunit. FAM122A therefore suppresses substrate binding and dephosphorylation of CDK substrates. FAM122A knockout in human cells slowed proliferation and cell-cycle progression, impaired G1/S and intra-S checkpoints, and attenuated CHK1/CHK2 activation after replication stress. Published July 2024; https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-024-50015-7. This work shows that B55α remains actively restrained during interphase, not merely switched on at mitotic exit. (kruse2024substraterecognitionprinciples pages 1-2)

4.4 RNA metabolism: a 2024 expansion of function

Kruse et al. identified an α-helical B55-binding element in RBM7, a component of the nuclear exosome targeting (NEXT) complex. RBM7 R143A selectively abolished PP2A-B55 binding without disrupting association with ZCCHC8 and MTR4/MTREX. Phosphorylation of RBM7 S136—or an S136D phosphomimetic—reduced binding, probably by repulsion from acidic B55 residues. (kruse2024substraterecognitionprinciples pages 7-8)

After dTAG-mediated removal of endogenous RBM7 in mouse embryonic stem cells, wild-type RBM7 restored suppression of NEXT-target RNAs, whereas S136 and R143 mutants did not. The authors infer that PP2A-B55 recruitment regulates RBM7/NEXT phosphorylation and RNA turnover. The binding work used B55α, but complementation was performed in mouse cells; therefore the physiological result supports the B55 family and conserved B55–RBM7 interface more strongly than exclusive human B55α dependence. Published 2 October 2024; https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.adp5491. (kruse2024substraterecognitionprinciples pages 7-8, kruse2024substraterecognitionprinciples pages 1-2)

5. Developmental evidence

The first constitutive Ppp2r2a knockout mouse demonstrated a nonredundant developmental requirement. Homozygous embryos occurred at the expected 25% at E10.5, but declined to 18% at E14.5 and 10% at E18.5; no homozygous pups were born alive. Mutants were smaller and showed exencephaly, spina bifida, cranial-vault collapse, syndactyly, and severe epidermal-barrier defects. Basal keratinocytes were disorganized, integrin and basement-membrane labeling was reduced, and focal-adhesion, hemidesmosome, adherens-junction, and tight-junction architecture was abnormal. Published 5 June 2020; https://doi.org/10.3389/fcell.2020.00358. (panicker2020ppp2r2aknockoutmice pages 1-2)

This is precise genetic evidence for the ortholog and establishes that B55α cannot be completely substituted by other B55 isoforms during mouse ectodermal development. It should not, however, be interpreted as a quantified human congenital phenotype without corresponding human genetic data.

6. Disease relevance and translational applications

Cancer

PPP2R2A alteration is common in several tumor types, but its biological effect is context-dependent. Deletions have been reported in prostate, ovarian, and breast epithelial cancers. Rare truncating mutations G247T and delG85 were reported in three de novo acute-myeloid-leukemia patients and associated with loss of B55α, increased AKT Thr308 phosphorylation, and sensitivity to AKT inhibition. Conversely, B55α depletion suppressed growth in some pancreatic models, illustrating that “PPP2R2A is always a tumor suppressor” is too simple. (fowle2019pp2aholoenzymessubstrate pages 15-17)

The most quantitative α-specific study reported hemizygous PPP2R2A loss in approximately 42% of prostate adenocarcinomas, rising above 75% in metastatic disease; homozygous loss was approximately 5% and did not rise in later stages. Lower expression correlated with poorer prognosis. Re-expression in B55α-low prostate-cancer cells reduced proliferation and transformation and blocked xenograft tumorigenicity, while inducing centrosomal failure. These findings nominate PPP2R2A copy loss as a possible biomarker for sensitivity to agents that restore PP2A-B55α signaling, but that strategy remains preclinical. (zhao2019ppp2r2aprostatecancer pages 13-14)

Neurological relevance

B55α can recruit and dephosphorylate Tau, linking PPP2R2A mechanistically to neuronal microtubule regulation and tau hyperphosphorylation. Nevertheless, broad database associations with “neurodegenerative disease” or other disease categories can derive from pooled functional screens rather than causal human genetics. Open Targets, for example, lists neurodegenerative and metabolic associations supported largely by glutamatergic-neuron CRISPR screens; these are hypothesis-generating rather than evidence that PPP2R2A is an established disease driver. (OpenTargets Search: -PPP2R2A, fowle2019pp2aholoenzymessubstrate pages 6-7)

Current implementation status

There is no established PPP2R2A-specific approved therapy, diagnostic test, or clinical implementation in the retrieved evidence. Current applications are experimental:

  • PPP2R2A deletion/expression as a candidate stratification biomarker for PP2A-reactivation strategies;
  • engineered B55-binding inhibitors to perturb substrate recruitment;
  • phosphoproteomic signatures for identifying B55-regulated pathways;
  • targeted manipulation of FAM122A–B55α or MASTL–ENSA/ARPP19 control as research strategies.

The 2024 deep-learning-designed inhibitor was described as potent and highly specific and competed with substrate recruitment, including at 2 μM in an immunoprecipitation/proteomics workflow. It is a mechanistic probe and therapeutic-proof-of-principle construct, not a clinical drug. (kruse2024substraterecognitionprinciples pages 7-8, kruse2024substraterecognitionprinciples pages 1-2)

7. Expert interpretation and annotation confidence

Authoritative reviews converge on three principles. First, PP2A holoenzymes possess real substrate specificity; B55α is not an interchangeable accessory subunit. Second, PP2A-B55 is a central temporal controller of mitotic exit. Third, substrate recognition reflects a combination of B55 docking, local charge, phosphosite chemistry, and endogenous inhibitors rather than one universal sequence motif. (lacroix2022structuralenzymaticand pages 2-4, goguetrubio2020pp2ab55holoenzymeregulation pages 1-3, lacroix2022structuralenzymaticand pages 1-2)

The strongest functional annotation is therefore:

PPP2R2A encodes the human PP2A B55α regulatory subunit, a WD40 β-propeller that assembles with PP2A-A and PP2A-C, recruits selected phosphoserine/threonine proteins through electrostatic and α-helical docking interfaces, and spatially and temporally directs their dephosphorylation. Its best-established pathway role is ordered reversal of CDK1 phosphorylation during mitotic exit, with additional direct functions in interphase checkpoints, DNA-damage signaling, centrosome maintenance, Tau regulation, and RBM7/NEXT-associated RNA metabolism.

Confidence is high for identity, holoenzyme role, WD40 architecture, and mitotic function; moderate-to-high for individual validated substrates and checkpoint functions; and preliminary/preclinical for disease targeting and biomarker use. Some prominent mechanistic results apply to PP2A-B55 as a family and cannot yet be assigned exclusively to B55α in every cell type.

PPP2R2A (PP2A B55-alpha, P63151) — curation notes

Identity and architecture

PPP2R2A encodes the ~55 kDa B-type (B/B55/PR55) regulatory subunit alpha of protein
phosphatase 2A. It was cloned from PR55 peptide sequences obtained by purifying the
trimeric PP2A1 holoenzyme from rabbit skeletal muscle
PMID:1849734,
and the gene family comprises alpha and beta paralogues
PMID:1849734
(PR55-beta = PPP2R2B, the neuronal-enriched isoform of that paper).

The holoenzyme is a heterotrimer of a catalytic C subunit (PPP2CA/CB), a HEAT-repeat A
scaffold (PPP2R1A/B) and one variable B subunit
PMID:30595372.
B55-alpha is a seven-bladed WD40 beta-propeller carrying an acidic substrate-binding
groove on its top face, latched onto the A scaffold by a protruding beta-hairpin arm
PMID:18922469.
UniProt records seven WD repeats (11-446) and notes that the extended WD 2 repeat mediates
the PPP2R1A contact (ECO:0000269|PubMed:38123684).

Critically for grading MF rows: the catalytic centre is in the C subunit, not in B55-alpha.
B55-alpha recruits, positions and selects substrates; PP2A-C hydrolyses the phosphoester.
This is why contributes_to (not enables) is the correct qualifier for phosphatase
activity on this gene product, and why GO:0019888 protein phosphatase regulator activity
and GO:0140767 enzyme-substrate adaptor activity are the informative MF terms for it
PMID:20711181.

acting downstream of CDK1 inactivation
PMID:20711181,

PMID:38123684,
and PP2A:B55 has hundreds of substrates
PMID:38123684.

  1. GO:0004721 IDA contributes_to (PMID:30595372) → MODIFY to GO:0004722. The
    qualifier is already the weaker, correct claim; the essence is right but PP2A is
    specifically a Ser/Thr phosphatase, which is the term both repository modules use for
    this holoenzyme. Not a demotion of the annotation, a sharpening of it.
  2. 43 bare GO:0005515 IPI rows. Repository policy: resolve to an informative MF where
    the cited paper supports one, otherwise REMOVE as uninformative without asserting the
    interaction is false. Four rows are resolved to GO:0140767 enzyme-substrate adaptor activity (HSF1, CRTC3, DAPK1, p107/RBL1 — each a paper that shows B55 binding and

    PPP2R1A/PPP2R1B/PPP2CA rows are holoenzyme assembly already captured by the GO:0000159
    part_of rows, and the rest are high-throughput interactome or inhibitor/regulator
    co-precipitations that carry no functional information about B55-alpha itself.
  3. GO:0044877 protein-containing complex binding (IEA from rat P36876, PMID:11032905).
    REMOVE — uninformative and redundant: the same rat experiment already yields the specific
    GO:0048156 tau protein binding row, which is what the assay actually measured.
  4. GO:0051721 protein phosphatase 2A binding (IEA from rat). MARK_AS_OVER_ANNOTATED.
    Binding the PP2A core dimer is real (the A-subunit mutagenesis of PMID:9847399 maps it),
    but B55-alpha is a PP2A subunit; annotating a constituent subunit as "binds PP2A"
    conflates complex membership (already asserted five times over via GO:0000159) with
    binding an external complex. Not false, so not REMOVE.
  5. Synapse / glutamatergic synapse (IEA from mouse Q6P1F6). The mouse source is
    PMID:24413018, a fluorescence-sorted glutamatergic synaptosome proteomics screen. Presence
    in a synaptosome preparation is decent evidence of a synaptic pool, poor evidence of
    is_active_in. KEEP_AS_NON_CORE rather than REMOVE — B55-alpha is a ubiquitous subunit
    with real neuronal substrates (Tau, DARPP-32 via ARPP-16), but the synapse is not where
    its core function is defined.
  6. Reactome TAS cytosol rows. All ACCEPTed on location grounds. Two (BANF1, ANKLE2)
    are explicitly B55-alpha-specific reactions; the NMD/UPF1 and YAP1 rows use "PP2A"
    generically, so the cytosolic location is right even though the reaction attribution to
    B55-alpha specifically is Reactome's inference rather than a B55-alpha experiment. The
    annotated term is the compartment, not the reaction, so this does not warrant a downgrade.
  7. PMID:17245430 rows. The paper's headline is B56-gamma
    PMID:17245430,
    and B55-alpha appears in it as the comparator B subunit. Per repository rules this is NOT
    grounds to call the experimental rows mis-attributed: the curator read the full text, and
    B55-alpha's membership in a PP2A holoenzyme (the GO:0000159 IDA) is independently
    established many times over. The TP53 GO:0005515 row is REMOVEd on the generic-binding
    policy, not because the interaction is doubted.
  8. No NEW terms proposed, and no cell-cycle-transition term in core_functions either. The obvious candidate would be a mitotic-exit process term
    (e.g. GO:0010458 exit from mitosis), and B55-alpha would pass the participation test
    — it is the subunit that performs substrate selection for the dephosphorylations that
    constitute mitotic exit, not merely a substrate of the process. But the comparator check
    argues against adding it here: the human catalytic and scaffold subunits (PPP2CA,
    PPP2R1A) do not carry GO:0010458 either, and GOA's chosen representation for this
    holoenzyme's mitotic role is GO:0051983 regulation of chromosome segregation plus
    GO:0006470, both of which PPP2R2A already has. The mitotic-exit role is captured in
    core_functions and in the two repository modules rather than asserted as a new
    annotation.

should be phrased. QuickGO returns zero annotations to any of GO:0010389 regulation of G2/M transition, GO:0000086 G2/M transition, GO:0010458 exit from mitosis or
GO:0007096 regulation of exit from mitosis for the catalytic subunit PPP2CA (P67775), the

the cell-cycle-transition terms go to the kinase/regulatory switch, and the phosphatase
holoenzyme's contribution is represented by GO:0006470 protein dephosphorylation plus
GO:0051983 regulation of chromosome segregation. That is a convention across all three
subunits of the same complex, not an oversight on one gene, so core_functions follows it:
the WEE1/G2/M-checkpoint role is stated in prose and in review.reason on the
GO:0006470 IMP row, not as a directly_involved_in term the holoenzyme's other subunits
are all missing.

Open questions carried into the review

  • Which mitotic-exit substrates are B55-alpha-specific versus shared with B55-delta
    (PPP2R2D)? B55-delta depletion did not delay mitotic exit in HeLa cells even though it is
    the Xenopus-extract isoform, which the authors attribute to expression-level or technical
    differences PMID:20711181.
  • Is the GO:0019888 "regulator activity" framing or the GO:0140767 "adaptor" framing the
    better primary MF for a B subunit? They describe the same physical fact from two directions
    and GOA asserts both; the review accepts both and uses GO:0140767 as the primary MF in
    core_functions because substrate selection is the mechanistically specific claim.
