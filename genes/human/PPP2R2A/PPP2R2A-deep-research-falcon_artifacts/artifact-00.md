| Identity | **PPP2R2A** encodes PP2A regulatory subunit **B55α/Bα** in humans; the literature identity agrees with UniProt **P63151** and the supplied B55-family annotation. | Human α-specific nomenclature; no symbol ambiguity identified. | Goguet-Rubio et al., Nov 2020 (pqac-00000003) |
| Holoenzyme role and reaction | B55α is **not catalytic**. It joins an A-subunit scaffold and PP2A-C catalytic subunit to form a heterotrimer, recruiting/positioning substrates for PP2A-C–mediated hydrolysis of phosphoserine/phosphothreonine. Catalysis occurs at the PP2A-C two-metal active site. | Holoenzyme architecture is PP2A/B55-family evidence; B55α assembly and substrate-specifying role are human α-specific. | Fowle et al., Jan 2019; Lacroix et al., Aug 2022 (pqac-00000006, pqac-00000007) |
| WD40 structure | B55 proteins form a **seven-bladed WD40-repeat β-propeller** with an acidic central/active-site-facing groove. B55α contacts A-subunit HEAT repeats 1–7 and makes relatively few contacts with PP2A-C. | Structural B55-family evidence, including B55α surface and assembly features; matches supplied PP2A_PR55 and WD40-domain annotations. | Fowle et al., Jan 2019; Goguet-Rubio et al., Nov 2020 (pqac-00000003, pqac-00000007) |
| Substrate recognition | B55 uses complementary electrostatics to recognize basic substrate regions and preferentially processes phosphothreonine over phosphoserine; equivalent TP peptides showed **≥34-fold** faster turnover than SP peptides. Newer work shows diverse substrate **α-helices** dock conserved hydrophobic/electrostatic patches on B55α, including residues Y178, D197, M222, L225 and V228. | TP/SP selectivity and bipartite polybasic recognition are B55-family findings; α-helical docking surfaces were mapped on human B55α by modeling, mutational scanning and binding assays. | Cundell et al., Aug 22, 2016; Kruse et al., Oct 2, 2024 (pqac-00000012, pqac-00000016, pqac-00000017) |
| Localization | PPP2R2A helps determine where holoenzyme activity is delivered; evidence supports functional pools at mitotic structures, Golgi/nuclear-envelope reassembly machinery, centrosomes, nucleus and cytoplasm. Localization is dynamic and substrate-dependent rather than confined to one organelle. | Mostly functional localization inferred from B55-family substrates; centrosomal effects are human B55α-specific. No evidence supports extracellular or membrane-spanning localization. | Fowle et al., Jan 2019; Zhao et al., Dec 2019 (pqac-00000005, pqac-00000009) |
| Mitotic pathway | PP2A-B55 reverses cyclin-B–CDK1 phosphorylation during mitotic exit. MASTL/Greatwall-phosphorylated ENSA/ARPP19 inhibits B55 until cyclin B destruction and pathway reset permit ordered dephosphorylation. PRC1 Thr481 had a modeled B55-dependent rate constant of **0.121 min⁻¹**; 2,960 of 7,390 modeled phosphopeptides had a positive B55-dependent rate. | Primarily B55-family phosphoproteomics and kinetic modeling; B55α-specific direct targets include PRC1 and centrosomal proteins such as HAUS6, NEDD1 and CEP170. | Cundell et al., Aug 22, 2016; Zhao et al., Dec 2019 (pqac-00000009, pqac-00000017) |
| DNA damage and interphase | B55α directly dephosphorylates ATM Ser1981 and interacts with RPA2. FAM122A competitively inhibits B55α substrate docking through an `[RK]-V-x-x-[VI]-R` helical element; FAM122A loss slows proliferation and cell-cycle progression and weakens G1/S, intra-S, CHK1 and CHK2 checkpoint responses to replication stress. | Human B55α-specific biochemical and knockout-cell evidence; some substrate classes remain pathway-level rather than exhaustively validated. | Amin et al., Oct 2022; Wasserman et al., Jul 2024 (pqac-00000002) |
| RNA/NEXT advance | PP2A-B55 binds an α-helical module in **RBM7**. RBM7 R143A uncoupled B55 binding without disrupting ZCCHC8–MTR4/MTREX assembly; S136 phosphorylation or S136D inhibited binding. Wild-type—but not S136 or R143 mutant—RBM7 restored control of NEXT-target RNAs after endogenous RBM7 degradation. | Human-cell binding/complex evidence plus functional complementation in mouse embryonic stem cells; the exact relevant PP2A-B55 isoform contribution in every setting is not fully resolved. | Kruse et al., Oct 2, 2024 (pqac-00000011, pqac-00000015) |
| Development | Complete **Ppp2r2a** loss is embryonic lethal in mice: homozygotes fell from **25% at E10.5** to **18% at E14.5** and **10% at E18.5**, with none born alive. Defects included reduced size, exencephaly, spina bifida, cranial-vault collapse, syndactyly and severe epidermal-barrier/adhesion abnormalities. | Mouse ortholog knockout evidence; demonstrates nonredundant developmental function but should not be treated as a quantified human phenotype. | Panicker et al., Jun 5, 2020 (pqac-00000013) |
| Cancer and translational status | PPP2R2A was reported hemizygously lost in about **42%** of prostate adenocarcinomas and in **>75%** of metastatic disease, whereas homozygous loss was about **5%**. Restoring B55α in B55α-low prostate-cancer models caused centrosome collapse, chromosome-segregation failure and loss of tumorigenicity, suggesting deletion as a possible PP2A-reactivation biomarker. Evidence is context-dependent, and no PPP2R2A-specific approved therapy or validated clinical implementation was identified. | Human tumor-genomic association plus cell/xenograft preclinical evidence; therapeutic interpretation remains hypothesis-generating. | Zhao et al., Dec 2019; Fowle et al., Jan 2019 (pqac-00000004, pqac-00000009) |
