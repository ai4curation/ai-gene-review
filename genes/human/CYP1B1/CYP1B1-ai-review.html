Gene Description

CYP1B1 is a 543-residue, heme-dependent cytochrome P450 associated with the endoplasmic-reticulum membrane. With reducing equivalents supplied by NADPH-cytochrome P450 reductase, it oxidizes estrogens, retinoids, arachidonic acid and diverse xenobiotics. Human CYP1B1 preferentially forms 4-hydroxylated estrogens in studied enzyme systems and generates both hydroxylated and epoxidized fatty-acid products. It also converts fatty-acid hydroperoxides to oxo products through a distinct dehydration reaction demonstrated without added NADPH. These metabolic activities influence local redox balance and vascular and extracellular-matrix behavior; loss of function is associated with primary congenital glaucoma. Mitochondrial association has been demonstrated under induced conditions in mouse tissues, while its human physiological substrate and compartment contributions remain context dependent.

Definition: Catalysis of the reaction: 17beta-estradiol + reduced [NADPH--hemoprotein reductase] + O2 = 4-hydroxy-17beta-estradiol + oxidized [NADPH--hemoprotein reductase] + H2O + H(+).

Justification: Human CYP1B1 product-resolved assays directly establish this reaction and distinguish it from the existing estrogen 2-hydroxylase activity GO:0101021. The official RHEA:47280 reaction supplies the chemical mapping. Broad keyword results are not treated as an exhaustive search of all GO; ontology maintainers should check an equivalent term or mapping elsewhere before creating this proposed term. The reduced-flavoprotein donor class GO:0016712 is also relevant. This proposal assigns no new GO identifier and creates no NEW annotation.

Biological synthesis

Human CYP1B1 is a 543-residue heme P450 associated principally with the endoplasmic-reticulum membrane. Its oxygenation chemistry uses reducing equivalents delivered by P450 reductase. Recombinant human enzyme directly oxidizes estrogens, retinoids, arachidonic acid and xenobiotics. The physiological contribution of each substrate depends on tissue, substrate availability and competing enzymes; activity in a reconstituted preparation does not establish systemic dominance. Human estrogen 4-hydroxylation is favored over 2-hydroxylation in the directly inspected comparison, whereas rodent regioselectivity differs. The description therefore distinguishes human enzyme chemistry from inherited or cellular-context effects. PMID:15258110(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15258110/), PMID:23821647(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23821647/), PMID:10681376(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10681376/).

A second catalytic mode converts fatty-acid hydroperoxides to oxo products. The original study whose title foregrounds CYP2S1 also directly assays CYP1B1. Its commercial CYP1B1 microsomes contain POR, but the assays receive no added NADPH. This supports the distinct dehydratase function without transferring the paper's CYP2S1-specific anaerobic and reductase-removal controls to CYP1B1. The two core functions describe complementary chemistry and do not imply mutually exclusive products or locations. PMID:21068195(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21068195/), Methods and Table 2.

The human arachidonate source resolves EET positions but groups terminal HETE peaks: 19 and 20 can coelute, as can the assigned 16/17/18 group. It does not establish a separately quantified flux to every terminal product. A different chiral analysis supplies the stereochemical evidence: human CYP1B1 Supersomes predominantly form 8R,9S and 14R,15S EET and produce both 11,12 enantiomers. Its comparator/column procedure is retained as the assignment boundary. PMID:15258110(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15258110/), Table 3; PMID:20972997(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20972997/), Table 2 and Methods.

The official record specifies one displayed 543-aa product and no alternative-product comment. All three official sequence representations inspected agree. The heme ligand is Cys470. Experimental reagents include a human deletion of residues 2–4, polymorphic alleles, and the crystallographic CYP1B1*2 construct replacing residues 1–50 with MAKKTSSKGK, retaining A119S and adding a His4 tag. These are not official splice products. The ANF-bound structure supports the heme/domain architecture without establishing intact-protein localization. UniProt Q16678, PMID:21147782(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21147782/).

Contextual and unresolved functions

Tagged human TEK/CYP1B1 co-immunoprecipitation, including the actual TEK domain comparisons, supports receptor protein tyrosine kinase binding. Six exact interaction records agree with the curated pair. This is an association assay; the data do not establish ligand agonism or an isolated binary interface. The five other retrieved interaction records remain context and do not generate new annotations. PMID:28620713(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28620713/).

Mouse retinal pericyte experiments support context-dependent effects on population expansion, VEGF output, migration, matrix adhesion, apoptosis and endothelial coculture morphogenesis. Increased population does not mean increased DNA-synthesis rate in this experiment. Basal/staurosporine caspase assays and peroxide viability/ROS assays are separate, so the precise oxidative-stress intrinsic-apoptosis route remains unresolved. The reported STAT3 Ser727 result alone does not establish receptor/JAK–STAT signaling. Methods and Results also differ in their peroxide dose. These limits accompany the literal mouse-to-human transfers rather than changing their source identities. PMID:23568032(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23568032/), Methods, Results and Figures 3, 4 and 10.

Endothelial experiments distinguish chronic Cyp1b1 loss, eNOS restoration and acute CYP1B1 rescue. NO bioavailability can improve without restored eNOS protein, and the authors discuss synthesis and bioavailability together. The exact NO-biosynthetic claim remains uncertain despite positive vascular-regulatory evidence. Migration direction differs between endothelial and pericyte contexts. The analysis does not impose a universal direct-sensor requirement or erase positive regulatory annotations because their biochemical intermediate is unresolved. PMID:20032512(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20032512/), Methods, Results and Discussion.

Mouse trabecular-meshwork/periostin experiments and complementary human TM1 knockdown support matrix, adhesion and redox effects. They do not demonstrate direct CYP1B1 collagen chemistry. Lipid peroxidation is not automatically a resolved lipid-catabolic step. The nine UNDECIDED source objects concern steroid catabolism, NO biosynthesis, oxidative-stress intrinsic apoptosis, lipid catabolism and receptor/JAK–STAT signaling; their individual reasons retain the positive surrounding evidence. PMID:23979599(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23979599/).

Mitochondrial fraction association has been demonstrated after TCDD induction in female mouse tissues with knockout and fraction-marker controls, while carryover is acknowledged. This supports contextual inheritance rather than an unqualified human constitutive-localization claim. The proposed targeting residues do not define an experimentally demonstrated cleavage site.
