Gene Description

C2CD3 is a C2-domain-rich protein that organizes the distal region of centrioles and supports their elongation and maturation into ciliary basal bodies. Its radially arranged molecules link the distal luminal protein network to the machinery that recruits appendages and other ciliogenesis factors. C2CD3 associates with OFD1 and helps coordinate centriole length with distal architecture. Loss of C2CD3 disrupts appendage recruitment and primary-cilium formation, while initial procentriole formation can persist. Pathogenic variants cause an oral-facial-digital ciliopathy with variable brain and skeletal abnormalities.

Distal-centriole organization and length

PMID:24997988(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24997988/) connects C2CD3 loss to shortened centrioles and gain of C2CD3 to long centriolar extensions. The inspected Results and relevant Methods distinguish low-level GFP-C2cd3 in mouse IMCD3 cells, mutant mouse fibroblasts, endogenous human RPE co-immunoprecipitation, and human-cell overexpression with correlative microscopy. The endogenous C2CD3–OFD1 association and capture of cell-expressed C2CD3 by OFD1 fragments support the exact OFD1 partner. They do not establish purified binary affinity. OFD1 antagonism is consistent with regulation of length; it does not make C2CD3 a tubulin polymerase or protein kinase.

The centriolar-satellite pool is retained as non-core. The mouse tagged-protein PCM1 colocalization is explicitly distinguished from a human localization image. Satellite localization does not imply that C2CD3 is needed for satellite integrity: the inspected perturbations did not establish that requirement. C2-domain calcium or lipid binding remains a mechanistic question, not an activity assigned from the domain name.

PMID:24469809(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24469809/) independently summarizes C2cd3-dependent distal-appendage assembly, ciliogenic protein recruitment and ciliary-vesicle docking. Its normal cache is abstract-only despite an available PMCID. The complete abstract supports the stated mammalian mechanism; exact full-text constructs, Methods and images were not inspected. Recruitment of TTBK2, IFT factors and vesicle-associated proteins does not transfer their catalytic or transport activities to C2CD3.

Human spatial and perturbation evidence

PMID:41364719(https://journals.plos.org/plosbiology/article?id=10.1371/journal.pbio.3003519) supplies newer primary evidence beyond a disease or knockout phenotype. Human U2OS expansion-microscopy epitope mapping places C2CD3 in a radial distal-centriolar organization, with different regions positioned toward the lumen and microtubule wall. Depletion disrupts the luminal protein network, distal-cap components and recruitment of the MNR/CEP90/OFD1 machinery. Initial procentriole formation persists; the result is not a universal absence of centriole duplication.

Mouse photoreceptor and tracheal cryo-electron tomography provides complementary architectural context. It does not identify each ring node or hook as a particular C2CD3 domain. The Discussion explicitly leaves the molecular composition of these structures open, including possible NA14 contributions. The MNR experiment is a cellular displacement/recruitment assay using expressed fluorescent fusions, not purified binary binding. C2CD3 alone did not decorate microtubules under those conditions; the paper does not claim this disproves every earlier construct-dependent observation.

Complete main Results and Discussion and relevant Methods were read from the primary publisher, and the matching normal XML cache supplies the exact quotation anchors. Original images, raw data and supplementary files were not independently analyzed. One C2CD3 siRNA was used in the inspected depletion experiments; centriolar fluorescence verified depletion, whereas a western-blot depletion measurement was unavailable because the control protein signal was not detectable. These limits constrain molecular detail without erasing the observed architectural phenotype.

One cytoskeletal structural function, with existing process coverage

One new molecular-function assertion is proposed: GO:0005200 structural constituent of cytoskeleton. Its official definition concerns contribution to the structural integrity of a cytoskeletal structure; it is a child of GO:0005198 structural molecule activity, part_of cytoskeleton organization and occurs_in cytoskeleton. The distal centriole is the mapped cytoskeletal structure, so this is more precise than the generic parent without requiring direct tubulin affinity. The basis here is the combination of physical C2CD3 occupancy within the distal architecture and disruption of that architecture after its loss. This is more specific evidence of structural work than ciliogenesis necessity alone. It does not assert an atomically identified C2CD3 ring, a finer adapter mechanism, direct microtubule affinity or calcium-dependent lipid binding.

The same function anchors one core unit covering the existing centriole-elongation, centrosomal protein-localization and non-motile-cilium-assembly assertions. GO:0071539 includes maintenance at the centrosome, so this role does not require a transport motor. GO:0061511 places elongation within centriole replication; the annotation does not imply that C2CD3 initiates every step of procentriole formation. No new process term is proposed, and no additional parent/child structural-function rows or redundant core units are added.
