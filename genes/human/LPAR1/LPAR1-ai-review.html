Gene Description

LPAR1 is a class-A seven-transmembrane G protein-coupled receptor that binds extracellular lysophosphatidic acid at the plasma membrane and converts ligand occupancy into heterotrimeric G-protein signaling. Integrated human evidence supports coupling to Gi/Go, Gq/11, and G12/13; direct recombinant-human studies particularly establish a pertussis-toxin-sensitive Gi route to phospholipase-C-dependent inositol-phosphate production and cytosolic calcium elevation. Gi signaling also inhibits adenylyl cyclase, lowering cAMP and PKA activity, while Gq- and G12/13-linked branches connect the receptor to calcium and Rho-family cytoskeletal responses. LPAR1 is delivered to the cell surface through regulated biosynthetic trafficking and undergoes agonist-induced internalization to endosomes; RalA and GRK2 regulate signaling output and desensitization. Downstream LPAR1-dependent migration, actin remodeling, ciliogenesis inhibition, chemotaxis, and nervous-system development are tissue-, disease-, or organism-specific outcomes of this receptor mechanism rather than separate core molecular activities. UniProt records two splice products, Q92633-1 and Q92633-2; Q92633-2 replaces the first 15 residues, but no direct study has established isoform-specific ligand binding, coupling, trafficking, or tissue function.

Core Functions

Acts at the plasma membrane as an LPA-activated GPCR, coupling ligand occupancy to Gi/Go, Gq/11, and G12/13 heterotrimeric G proteins. Proximal outputs include Gi-dependent inhibition of adenylyl cyclase and cAMP/PKA signaling and phospholipase-C-dependent inositol-phosphate production and cytosolic calcium elevation. G12/13-linked Rho activation is a third proximal core signaling branch; the resulting cytoskeletal, migratory, developmental, and disease phenotypes are context-dependent outputs rather than additional core molecular functions.

The founding human paper directly connected EDG2/LPAR1 expression to selective
LPA signaling and physical ligand binding:

"HEK293 cells expressing the human Edg2 protein showed an elevated response to
lysophosphatidic acid (LPA) in a serum response element reporter gene assay,

increases in specific binding of [3H]-LPA."

[PMID:9070858, abstract; full text unavailable]

This is the decisive human evidence for LPA binding and LPA receptor activity.
The same study found broad human mRNA distribution with highest abundance in
brain; expression does not by itself establish a tissue-specific function.

G-protein coupling and proximal signaling

Recombinant human EDG2/LPAR1 couples LPA to PLC/IP3-dependent calcium signaling:

"The coincident production of inositol phosphates and the inhibition of Ca2+
mobilization by the phospholipase C inhibitor U73122 strongly suggested that
Edg2 and Edg4 mobilize Ca2+ through inositol trisphosphate generated by
phospholipase C activation."

"Pertussis toxin almost completely blocked LPA-induced Ca2+ mobilization by
Edg2 but only partially blocked that by Edg4, which suggests that Edg2
transduces Ca2+ mobilization largely through pertussis toxin-sensitive Gi
proteins, whereas Edg4 requires both Gi and Gq."

[PMID:9804623, abstract; full text unavailable]

This directly supports a Gi-sensitive PLC/IP3/Ca2+ route. Broader coupling to
Gi/Go, G12/G13, and Gq is summarized by UniProt and Reactome, but those
integrated sources should not be mistaken for equal-strength direct human
evidence for every G-alpha family.

"We show that the small GTPase RalA associates with both LPA(1) and LPA(2) in
human embryonic kidney (HEK 293) cells and that stimulation of LPA(1) receptors

[PMID:19306925, abstract; full text unavailable]

The paper further reports Ral-dependent PLC activity and agonist-triggered
internalization/colocalization in endocytic vesicles, making it the key source
for signaling regulation and endosomal trafficking.

Biosynthetic and agonist-regulated trafficking

The cell-surface trafficking study defines receptor-intrinsic folding/export
determinants:

"Further, site-directed mutagenesis analysis of the LPA1 revealed that Ile325,
Tyr85, and Leu87 within these two fragments regulate LPA1 maturation and
trafficking to the cell surface."

"The mutants of LPA1 are majorly localized in the endoplasmic reticulum (ER)
and exhibit a higher binding affinity to heat shock protein 70 (Hsp70), when
compared to the LPA1 wild type."

[PMID:25025571, abstract; cached full text available]

Sar1/COPII enhances wild-type LPA1 glycosylation, and the trafficking-defective
mutants fail LPA-induced ERK phosphorylation and cytokine release. These are
overexpression/mutagenesis results defining biosynthetic requirements, distinct
from the agonist-induced internalization described by PMID:19306925.

Antagonist-bound structure and pharmacology

The human receptor was solved with three antagonist tool compounds:

"Herein, we present three crystal structures of LPA1 in complex with antagonist
tool compounds selected and designed through structural and stability analyses."

"Structural analysis combined with molecular dynamics identified a basis for
ligand access to the LPA1 binding pocket from the extracellular space
contrasting with the proposed access for the sphingosine 1-phosphate receptor."

[PMID:26091040, abstract; cached full text available]

This is strong structural support for antagonist recognition and an extracellular
ligand-entry route. The paper's proposed recognition of phosphorylated
endocannabinoids is intriguing but broader and more speculative than the firmly
established LPA receptor function, so it should not define the core annotation.

Antagonist effects are context- and receptor-dependent. Ki16425 blocks LPA1 and
LPA3 and therefore requires receptor-specific depletion as corroboration. The
human-receptor comparison also found that compounds previously described as
Xenopus LPA-receptor antagonists were agonists or partial agonists at human
EDG2/EDG4 [PMID:9804623, abstract]. Pharmacological labels should therefore not
be transferred across receptor subtype or species without direct testing.

[file:human/LPAR1/LPAR1-uniprot.txt]

No audited source compares ligand binding, G-protein coupling, trafficking, or
tissue distribution between the two isoforms. The existence of a sequence
variant must not be converted into an isoform-specific functional assertion.

Development and species boundaries

The founding Vzg-1 developmental work is mouse, not human. Its biochemical
results remain important:

"Vzg-1 overexpression decreased the EC50 of both cell rounding and Gi activation
in response to LPA. Pertussis toxin treatment inhibited vzg-1-dependent
LPA-mediated Gi activation, but had no effect on cell rounding. Membrane binding
studies indicated that vzg-1 overexpression increased specific LPA binding."

[PMID:8922387, abstract; full text unavailable in project cache]

The full article explicitly labels its developmental result, Vzg-1 is expressed in the embryonic mouse brain and in neocortical neuroblast cell lines.
[PMID:8922387 / PMCID:PMC2133395, full text checked through Europe PMC on
2026-08-11]

This supports conserved receptor identity and a mouse cortical-neurogenesis
context. It does not directly establish human corpus-callosum, optic-nerve,
cerebellar, oligodendrocyte, synaptic, pain, or neuronal-maturation roles. The
many detailed developmental and neuronal GOA rows transferred from mouse/rat by
Ensembl Compara or PAINT must be reviewed as orthology inferences, not human
experimental annotations.

Core-function boundary for subsequent curation

  • Direct core: plasma-membrane class-A GPCR that binds extracellular LPA and
    activates heterotrimeric-G-protein signaling, with strong human evidence for a
    Gi-sensitive PLC/IP3/Ca2+ route.
  • Regulatory/trafficking machinery: receptor folding and COPII export determine
    surface delivery; LPA stimulation triggers internalization; RalA and GRK2
    regulate PLC output and desensitization.
  • Contextual downstream outcomes include calcium/CaMKII-dependent migration,
    PI3K/Akt-dependent inhibition of ciliogenesis, and FLNA/MRTF-A/actin signaling.
    These should not all be elevated to independent molecular functions.
  • Mouse developmental phenotypes, high-throughput PDZ/RAMP contacts, and generic
    Reactome G-protein-cycle steps require explicit evidence/source boundaries.
