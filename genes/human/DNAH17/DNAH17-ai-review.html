Gene Description

DNAH17 (dynein axonemal heavy chain 17; also DNEL2, DNAHL1) is a beta-type outer dynein arm (ODA) heavy chain, the sperm-specific paralog of DNAH9 and DNAH11. It is a 4462-residue motor protein with an N-terminal tail that assembles with other ODA subunits, a ring of six AAA+ domains with the hydrolytic ATP site in AAA1, and a coiled-coil stalk with a microtubule-binding domain, and it generates force towards microtubule minus ends. DNAH17 is expressed in testicular germ cells from early spermatocytes to late spermatids and lies along the sperm flagellar axoneme (midpiece, principal piece and end piece). In human sperm the ODA heavy chains are DNAH17 (beta) and DNAH8 (gamma), whereas respiratory cilia use DNAH5, DNAH9 and DNAH11 and lack DNAH17. Loss of DNAH17 removes the outer arms from sperm, together with DNAH8, DNAI1 and the docking-complex proteins ODAD2 and ODAD4, but leaves the inner arms in place. Bi-allelic variants cause isolated male infertility with asthenozoospermia or asthenoteratozoospermia with multiple morphological abnormalities of the sperm flagella (MMAF; spermatogenic failure 39), without respiratory disease. Dnah17-/- mice show MMAF with disorganized axonemes and impaired flagellar assembly in spermatids; Dnah17 knockout rats are infertile, and an in-frame DNAH17 splice deletion in pigs causes MMAF. ICSI outcomes are variable and sometimes require oocyte activation. Candidate DNAH17 variants have also been reported in two families with left-right asymmetry disorders, which is not yet confirmed.

Core Functions

Force-generating beta heavy chain of the sperm outer dynein arm. Together with the gamma chain DNAH8, DNAH17 hydrolyses ATP in its AAA1 site and moves towards microtubule minus ends, sliding doublet microtubules to power sperm flagellar beating. It is an essential structural subunit of the sperm ODA: without it the other ODA subunits do not accumulate, and men and mice lacking it have immotile, malformed sperm while motile cilia elsewhere are spared.

  tail that assembles with other ODA subunits, a ring of six AAA+ domains with the hydrolytic ATP site
  in AAA1, and a coiled-coil stalk with a microtubule-binding domain, and it generates force towards microtubule
  minus ends. DNAH17 is expressed in testicular germ cells from early spermatocytes to late spermatids
  and lies along the sperm flagellar axoneme (midpiece, principal piece and end piece). In human sperm
  the ODA heavy chains are DNAH17 (beta) and DNAH8 (gamma), whereas respiratory cilia use DNAH5, DNAH9
  and DNAH11 and lack DNAH17. Loss of DNAH17 removes the outer arms from sperm, together with DNAH8, DNAI1
  and the docking-complex proteins ODAD2 and ODAD4, but leaves the inner arms in place. Bi-allelic variants
  cause isolated male infertility with asthenozoospermia or asthenoteratozoospermia with multiple morphological
  abnormalities of the sperm flagella (MMAF; spermatogenic failure 39), without respiratory disease. Dnah17-/-
  mice show MMAF with disorganized axonemes and impaired flagellar assembly in spermatids; Dnah17 knockout
  rats are infertile, and an in-frame DNAH17 splice deletion in pigs causes MMAF. ICSI outcomes are variable
  and sometimes require oocyte activation. Candidate DNAH17 variants have also been reported in two families
  with left-right asymmetry disorders, which is not yet confirmed.
