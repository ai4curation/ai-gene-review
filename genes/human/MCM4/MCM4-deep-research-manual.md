# MCM4 manual deep research

Automated Falcon and Perplexity deep-research generation was unavailable in this workstream, so this file records the manual synthesis used for the review.

MCM4 is the human CDC21 ortholog and a motor subunit of the MCM2-7/CMG replicative helicase. The MCM4-MCM6-MCM7 subcomplex purified from HeLa cells has ATP-dependent DNA helicase activity [PMID:9305914], and a human cancer-derived MCM4 G364R mutant forms MCM4/6/7 and retains ssDNA-binding and ATPase activity while reducing helicase activity [PMID:25661590]. These studies support MCM4 as a direct contributor to the assembled helicase motor.

The intact human CMG complex supplies the physiological frame for those subcomplex assays: purified human CMG binds ssDNA, hydrolyses ATP, unwinds duplex DNA, translocates on the leading strand in the 3-prime to 5-prime direction, and cooperates with DNA polymerase epsilon in rolling-circle synthesis [PMID:22474384]. Human CMG cryoEM structures show MCM4 contacting the ssDNA spiral and participating in the AAA+ motor ring [PMID:32453425].

The membrane HDA row from the NK-cell membrane proteome was treated as over-annotation because MCM4 is a nuclear MCM2-7 subunit without a membrane anchor and the source experiment recovered many proteins likely to be transiently associated with membranes [PMID:19946888]. A promoter-mapping paper supports MCM4 as an essential component of the rereplication-prevention complex only at the level of an author statement, but that broad `DNA replication` NAS row is consistent with stronger MCM2-7 evidence [PMID:9465298].

No PAINT IRD or IKR loss applies to the human MCM4 lineage in PTHR11630.
