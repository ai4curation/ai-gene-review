CC   -!- FUNCTION: Na(+)-independent transporter that mediates the cellular
CC       uptake of a broad range of organic anions such as the endogenous bile
CC       salts cholate and deoxycholate, either in their unconjugated or
CC       conjugated forms (taurocholate and glycocholate), at the plasmam
CC       membrane (PubMed:19129463, PubMed:7557095). Responsible for intestinal
CC       absorption of bile acids (By similarity). Transports
CC       dehydroepiandrosterone 3-sulfate (DHEAS), a major circulating steroid
CC       secreted by the adrenal cortex, as well as estrone 3-sulfate and
CC       17beta-estradiol 17-O-(beta-D-glucuronate) (PubMed:11159893,
CC       PubMed:12568656, PubMed:19129463, PubMed:23918469, PubMed:25560245,
CC       PubMed:9539145). Mediates apical uptake of all-trans-retinol (atROL)
CC       across human retinal pigment epithelium, which is essential to
CC       maintaining the integrity of the visual cycle and thus vision
CC       (PubMed:25560245). Involved in the uptake of clinically used drugs
CC       (PubMed:17301733, PubMed:20686826, PubMed:27777271). Capable of thyroid
CC       hormone transport (both T3 or 3,3',5'-triiodo-L-thyronine, and T4 or L-
CC       tyroxine) (PubMed:19129463, PubMed:20358049). Also transports
CC       prostaglandin E2 (PubMed:19129463). Plays roles in blood-brain and
CC       -cerebrospinal fluid barrier transport of organic anions and signal
CC       mediators, and in hormone uptake by neural cells (By similarity). May
CC       also play a role in the reuptake of neuropeptides such as substance
CC       P/TAC1 and vasoactive intestinal peptide/VIP released from retinal
CC       neurons (PubMed:25132355). May play an important role in plasma and
CC       tissue distribution of the structurally diverse chemotherapeutic drugs
CC       methotrexate and paclitaxel (PubMed:23243220). Shows a pH-sensitive
CC       substrate specificity which may be ascribed to the protonation state of
CC       the binding site and leads to a stimulation of substrate transport in
CC       an acidic microenvironment (PubMed:19129463). Hydrogencarbonate/HCO3(-)
CC       acts as the probable counteranion that exchanges for organic anions
CC       (PubMed:19129463). May contribute to regulate the transport of organic
CC       compounds in testis across the blood-testis-barrier (Probable).
