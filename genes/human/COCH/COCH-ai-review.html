Gene Description

Cochlin is a secreted extracellular-matrix glycoprotein with an N-terminal LCCL domain and two von Willebrand factor A domains. Its collagen-binding and sulfated-glycan-binding capacities connect it to matrix organization and maintenance, especially in the inner ear. Pathogenic COCH variants can cause dominant progressive audiovestibular disease, while biallelic loss of function also impairs hearing. Proteolytic release of the LCCL region produces a distinct immune-active fragment that can aggregate bacteria and attract immune cells. Cochlin also influences cell shape in cultured human trabecular-meshwork cells.

Core Functions

Binds collagen through the vWFA2 region and associates with extracellular matrix. Sulfated-glycan recognition provides a complementary matrix-binding capability. Human domain and engineered-reporter assays establish these interactions; mouse tendon phenotypes support matrix maintenance without resolving a separate direct tensile or collagen-assembly activity. The alternative product replaces part of vWFA2, so equal full-product activity is untested.

Matrix-associated activities

Human cochlin secretion and vWFA2 collagen binding are directly supported. The original PMID:19013156 abstract reports collagen I/II/IV binding and is corroborated by literal MINT SPR records. Full recovery of that paper failed. PMID:22610276 supplies actual human construct and purified-domain experiments: F527C is unstable and aggregation-prone but retains collagen-II binding. Its cited wild-type affinity originates in the earlier paper. Mutant aggregation alone does not establish the normal matrix mechanical function.

PMID:35901072 tests an engineered human cochlin-CD8alpha-CD3zeta reporter and purified mouse cochlin-Fc. The human clone is sequence checked, with a reported natural T-to-S variant; the cochlin-free reporter is a control. Sulfation-dependent heparin recognition supports one scoped heparin-binding proposal. Human reporter-negative collagen results concern that assay geometry and do not overturn purified-domain SPR. PMID:36511224 uses mouse Fc/Protein-A fusions and domain deletions: vWA1 is required for GAG binding in those constructs, and isolated LCCL is negative. The earlier proposed LCCL-only glycan mechanism is therefore not adopted. This secondary matrix-binding capability is integrated with the matrix narrative rather than turned into a redundant extra core or process.

PMID:40094079 tests constitutive mouse Coch loss. It measures altered tendon fibrils, reduced stiffness at six months and reduced peak load at six/nine months; its Results report no significant elastic-modulus difference at either age.
