Gene Description

CSPP1 is a microtubule-associated protein that stabilizes microtubule ends and damaged lattices. The long CSPP-L product can act within the microtubule lumen, suppressing catastrophe and shrinkage and promoting pauses followed by renewed growth. CSPP1 occupies centrosomes, spindle poles, the central spindle and primary cilia in defined cellular contexts. It associates with ciliary microtubule regulators and promotes MyoGEF recruitment during cytokinesis. Biallelic loss-of-function variants cause Joubert-spectrum ciliopathy. The long and short products differ in their effects on microtubule dynamics.

Short CSPP-S SPLICE VARIANT
ID: CSPP1_SHORT

The 876-aa short product is directly compared with CSPP-L in the 2023 study: it binds microtubules but has weaker shrinkage suppression and differs in pausing and outgrowth behavior. Two existing binary-screen annotations explicitly identify this product. These observations do not establish that all binding partners or cellular roles are unique to it.

Core Functions

Binds and stabilizes microtubule ends and damaged lattices, limiting catastrophe and depolymerization and promoting pauses followed by regrowth. Reconstitution establishes luminal action of CSPP-L, while cellular experiments support control of microtubule-end dynamics. CSPP-L also localizes to primary cilia in human cells; the purified singlet-microtubule assays establish activity without directly reconstituting human axonemes.

The 26 raw GOA records reconcile to 25 distinct seeded assertions. The duplicated TOGARAM1 interaction retains its two literal raw rows in the source index. All original IDs, qualifiers, omissions and two explicitly short-product screen assignments are preserved. One new molecular-function assertion is proposed from direct purified-protein experiments; no process is added from disease necessity or a missing annotation.

CSPP-L stabilizes microtubule ends and damaged lattices. The 2023 reconstitution/cellular study reports [PMID:36752787 “prevents their shrinkage, and induces pausing”]; the independent 2024 reconstitution reports [PMID:38389254 “complete suppression of MT catastrophe”]. These are direct activities rather than a catalytic or generic scaffold inference. The compact core uses GO:0140778 microtubule stabilizing activity. It is not duplicated by a new microtubule-binding or biological-process term. The current parent wording about preventing interaction with destabilizers is narrower than its own SSNA1 defining experiment, which permits spastin binding while blocking severing. Likewise CSPP1 permits MCAK association while stabilizing microtubules. This wording issue is raised explicitly rather than asserting an unobserved exclusion mechanism.

The official displayed product is Q1MSJ5-3 (1,256 aa). Exact VSP reconstruction yields Q1MSJ5-1/CSPP-L (1,221 aa; deletion138–172) and Q1MSJ5-2/CSPP-S (876 aa; deletion1–329 and replacement689–740 by S). Modern experiments establish long/short differences: the short product binds microtubules but differs in shrinkage suppression, pausing and outgrowth. The 2024 full-length construct is 1,221 aa, expressed in Sf9, while fragments were bacterially expressed. The 2009 human antibody/depletion is CSPP-L-specific; its separately described mouse isoforms must not be relabeled as the three official human products. Curated functional classes describe these measured long/short differences without assigning equal activity to the displayed product.

The mitotic evidence remains positive. Human CSPP-L depletion disrupts [PMID:19129481 “MyoGEF localization”] at the central spindle and produces cytokinesis failure in a subset of HeLa cells. This is a performed recruitment/organization role, not RhoA exchange catalysis. The broad cell-division assertion is refined to the existing cytokinesis term. Patient fibroblasts in PMID:24360807 proliferate comparably to controls, so the result is not generalized into an indispensable proliferation requirement in every cell type. The single distilled molecular core describes direct stabilization; the independently established mitotic recruitment context is retained in the description and reviewed biological-process assertions without claiming that stabilization alone explains recruitment.

All three actual PAINT IBDs sit at PTN001091588 on the human PTHR21616 path. The displayed 1,256-aa target sequence matches the ungapped alignment. Target-derived human experimental evidence is legitimate descendant grounding, not circularity; the short donor list is not a weakness. No target-path loss record was found. Current extant donor records are separated from the saved IBD snapshot and from historical curator evidence.
