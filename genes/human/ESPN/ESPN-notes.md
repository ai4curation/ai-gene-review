## 2026-10-24 — Direct-source review

Seven source contexts were examined individually. The proposal retains six as ACCEPT and keeps SH3-domain binding as a supported contextual interaction. No annotation or publication quotation is added. All imported fields, the other eleven reviews and the two natural product records remain unchanged in this phase.

PMID:29572253 provides direct human-protein evidence in heterologous systems: GFP-human espin 3A targets apical microvilli in LLC-PK1-CL4 cells and stereocilia in mouse cochlear explants; purified human 3A binds and bundles F-actin. The deletion and charge-pattern controls separate residual binding from bundling/elongation. The precise current-product correspondence of the reported 3A reagent remains unresolved; these experiments do not establish equal function of the 854- and 288-aa source products.

PMID:9763424 supports the rat donor's brush-border location by biochemical isolation, antibody labeling and immunogold localization along microvillar actin bundles. Its 253-aa small espin and recombinant affinity values must remain distinct from human products. PMID:12598619 supplies rat PC-espin binding to a human IRSp53 SH3-containing fusion, with appropriate GST and proline-region deletion controls. The corresponding human long sequence contains a proline-rich region, but the paper does not assay human ESPN and does not establish short-product SH3 binding.

The full original PMID:8799813 remains unavailable. Its positive abstract is not grounds for rejection or a wrong-gene claim. Independent actual human PMID:29572253 and rat PMID:9763424 experiments establish the general F-actin association and binding claims, without implying that the 1996 Methods were inspected. Its reference assessment therefore retains the explicit original-access limitation while the annotation decisions cite independent primary support.

## Core synthesis — 2026-10-24

One actin-filament cross-linking core captures the independently measured bundling activity. Filament binding is a distinct assay and is retained in the biological explanation and existing annotations; it is not duplicated as a second core for the same bundle-organizing mechanism. The microvillar assembly process describes espin's structural contribution, while microvillus and stereocilium record two supported locations. The more general bundle-assembly process and the nested stereocilium-tip location are not added as redundant core coverage.
