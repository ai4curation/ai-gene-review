# MGAT4A notes

MGAT4A/GnT-IVa is an experimentally assayed type II Golgi glycosyltransferase. The 1999 cloning
paper reports a 535-aa human cDNA whose transient expression increased total cellular GnT-IV
activity 25-fold (PMID:10024668), and Oguri et al. 2006 measured recombinant human GnT-IVa and
GnT-IVb kinetics on a series of pyridylaminated N-glycan acceptors (PMID:17006639).

The direct function is EC 2.4.1.145, transfer of GlcNAc from UDP-GlcNAc to the
GlcNAc-beta1,2-Man-alpha1,3 arm of complex and hybrid N-glycans, forming the
beta1,4 GlcNAc branch that contributes to tri- and tetra-antennary N-glycans. MGAT4A
has lower Km values than MGAT4B in the 2006 enzyme comparison, so the review treats it
as a core GnT-IV isozyme rather than as a marginal paralog.

Four 2021 UniProt ISS rows are clearly imported from UniProtKB:P21549, the human AGXT
alanine:glyoxylate aminotransferase, and should not be on MGAT4A: peroxisome,
L-alanine:glyoxylate transaminase activity, glyoxylate metabolic process, and
protein homodimerization activity.

Reactome places MGAT4A in a FAM20C-substrate event. That supports phosphorylation of the
luminal/soluble part in the secretory pathway at most; it is not evidence that the mature
GnT-IVa enzyme acts in the ER lumen.

The SARS-CoV-2 Spike Reactome event is context rather than a separate evolved role:
the host GnT-IV enzymes branch viral Spike N-glycans by the same Golgi chemistry they use
on host glycoproteins.
