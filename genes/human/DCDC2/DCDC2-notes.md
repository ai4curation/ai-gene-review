# DCDC2 owner evidence notes

This review integrates all 62 imported source annotations, independent assessment of the four core papers, and the raw interaction, PAINT and product evidence described below. All source objects and the initial 15 reference identifiers/titles are preserved; five additional references provide donor or partner-class context.

## Identity and products

The human gene is HGNC:18141, official symbol DCDC2, UniProt Q9UHG0. Verified synonyms include DCDC2A, DFNB66, KIAA1154, NPHP19 and RU2. ClinGen association strengths remain disease-specific: the project records definitive ciliopathy and limited hearing-loss evidence; neither a dyslexia association nor a cell assay is used to upgrade those clinical classifications.

Official products are Q9UHG0-1 (476 residues) and Q9UHG0-2 (229 residues). The short form is reconstructed from two changes: deletion of canonical residues 1–247 (VSP_014670), and replacement of residues 248–307 by a different 60-residue segment (VSP_014671). It is not simply the C-terminal tail of the long form. Both products exactly match their official FASTA sequences. The long product has canonical doublecortin domains at residues 17–100 and 139–221; these regions are absent from the short product. This architecture does not by itself prove that the short form is inactive. No equivalent ciliary or microtubule activity is assigned to it without an assay.

The 81 joined interaction records use the long DCDC2 sequence, with exact sequence checksum matches. Participant checksum matches identify the underlying entry sequence, while experimental tags and binding fragments must still be interpreted separately.

## Interaction evidence and role limits

PMID:25416956 contributes two target pairs, with NIF3L1 and MRFAP1. PMID:32296183 contributes twenty-five target pairs. All twenty-seven source rows join three deposited IntAct records each: array, prey-pooling, and validated two-hybrid stages in yeast. This is positive binary-interaction evidence with human protein sequences; it is not twenty-seven demonstrated physiological pathways or three independent in-vivo observations per pair. MINT returned zero rows in the saved query. Four additional IntAct records concern other publications and were not substituted for these sources.

Eight high-throughput pairs support narrower binding terms: TRMT11/MGME1/B3GNT5/MYG1 enzyme binding; DYNLT2 dynein light-chain binding; DYNC2LI1 isoform2 dynein light-intermediate-chain binding; SLC38A2 transmembrane-transporter binding; and C5AR2 signaling-receptor binding. The observed pair plus the verified identity of a catalytic enzyme, receptor or named dynein chain supports that binding class. It does not assign the partner's activity, regulation or pathway to DCDC2. The other nineteen high-throughput pairs remain non-core generic binding. C5AR2 signaling capacity is supported by the separate original PMID:34582793: its selective agonist elicits p90RSK phosphorylation in native human macrophages during C5AR1 antagonism. This establishes the partner class, not a DCDC2 signaling pathway. CYTH4 remains a positive GEF-partner interaction; current GO searches and the retrieved GO release do not establish a bare GEF-binding term, while GAP binding, GEF adaptor activity and GEF modulation describe unshown roles.

Two construct cases matter. The LMO3 isoform4 binding region is 19–163. CYTH4 has a sufficient binding region of 1–118, covering only part of its SEC7 domain at 54–241. Neither assay establishes an intact transcriptional or GEF-regulatory assembly. OXT and NMU were assayed as precursor sequences, so mature-peptide recognition is not inferred. CKS1B and POP5 are noncatalytic components, PTPRCAP is not the phosphatase PTPRC, MICALL2 is not a Rab GTPase, KRTAP proteins are not assembled intermediate filaments, and CENPO alone is not the kinetochore. Q9NWS6 is currently SIRAL2, also known as FAM118A/C22orf8.

The HuRI original text distinguishes high-quality biophysical interactions from the still-undetermined cellular function of most individual pairs. Its original Supplementary Table9 was not independently downloaded; the exact target rows are established by the curator-deposited MITAB. The older HI-II-14 cache exposes abstract/introduction/discussion text rather than a complete Methods section. No original supplementary figure pixels were inspected for these pairs.

## PAINT and ortholog propagation

The PTHR23004 raw tree and MSA both contain 190 nodes/sequences. The human target is PTN002506770; its ungapped MSA sequence exactly matches all 476 canonical residues. Seven current IBD rows reproduce the seven target IBA source annotations. Human DCDC2 descends from both relevant ancestral nodes, PTN002744418 and the deeper PTN000543880.

At PTN002744418, neuronal migration is supported by rat RGD:1310227/D3ZR10 and mouse MGI:2652818/Q5DU00. Kinocilium localization uses the rat donor. Axoneme, cilium assembly and regulation of cilium assembly use human Q9UHG0 experimental descendants. The target appearing in its own IBD evidence is ordinary PAINT reasoning, not circular support.

At PTN000543880, microtubule-organizing-center localization uses Dictyostelium dcx/DDB_G0277115/Q550F6; microtubule localization also uses worm rpi-1/WBGene00012332/Q69Z08. The worm paper PMID:27930654 explicitly studies RPI-1 in its Results despite focusing on RAB-28 in its title. RPI-1::GFP localizes to ciliary and cytoplasmic microtubules; the source GO:0005879 and GO:0005881 annotations are typed descendants of GO:0005874. Thus an exact-term-only join would miss real experimental donor evidence.

The Dictyostelium paper PMID:21034843 is classified as a review but explicitly reports original DdDCX characterization. Its full original localization experiments were not recovered. That limitation alone is not a reason to reject an IBA, and a single donor is not weak evidence. The decisive target-specific issue for the MTOC row comes from PMID:25557784: DCDC2 is observed on axoneme/spindle fibers and excluded from the basal body and spindle poles in the reported cell-cycle states. PMID:21698230 reports microtubule networks near the centrosome, not localization to the centrosome itself. The review marks the ancestral MTOC assertion over-annotated for DCDC2, without claiming universal exclusion from every MTOC in all cell states.

The rat ortholog D3ZR10 has direct migration annotations to PMID:16278297 and PMID:18313856, kinesin binding to PMID:21698230, and kinocilium localization to PMID:25601850. Combined IEA/ISS routes can repeat the same underlying experiment and should not be counted as independent replication.

## Neuronal migration is context dependent

The original PMID:16278297 abstract reports altered migration following RNA interference but does not name the organism. The rat attribution is supported by the verified rat donor record and the later original PMID:21689730, whose Introduction and Discussion identify those prior experiments as rat neocortical RNAi studies. The initial immutable cache and later efetch recovery contain the abstract, not a complete original body. PMID:18313856 similarly reports rat embryonic knockdown, partial rescue of heterotopia, and unresolved overmigration; its recovered body is abstract-only.

The genuinely recovered full text of PMID:21689730 supplies the contrasting mouse experiment. Both constitutive and mosaic conditional Dcdc2 deletion gave grossly normal neocortical migration in the reported assays. Dcx shRNA caused more severe migration defects in the Dcdc2 mutant background than in wild type, supporting a context-dependent contribution rather than an absolute requirement. The study established the exon2 deletion, aberrant splicing and transcript decay, while noting that the tested antibodies did not verify the protein-level knockout. These findings support retaining all four neuronal-migration source rows as non-core developmental context. They do not establish a general human neuronal-migration mechanism or a Mendelian dyslexia diagnosis.

## RU2AS antigen is not DCDC2 immune-effector activity

The full Results of PMID:10601354 distinguish the sense RU2S/DCDC2 message from an oppositely transcribed RU2AS message, represented by cDNA4.1 and encoding an 84-residue antigenic product. A peptide from that antisense product sensitized cells to HLA-B7-restricted CTL recognition. The study does not establish a cellular defense effector role for the long or short DCDC2 product. Moreover, being recognized as an antigen is not itself performing the defense response. The review removes GO:0006968 on this explicit product/role distinction, rather than title-based suspicion or inability to read the source.

## Electronic mapping and human cell localization

The official InterPro mappings IPR003533 and IPR036572 genuinely assign intracellular signal transduction and match DCDC2's doublecortin architecture. The domain description spans structural doublecortin proteins and kinase-containing family members. Those common domains do not establish downstream signal propagation by DCDC2. Specific Wnt/Smoothened regulatory effects remain separate source-level assessments: their GO regulation terms are not is_a/part_of descendants of GO:0035556. The review marks the broad mapping over-annotated, while the independently assessed target experiments support a separate canonical Wnt-inhibition unit.

UniProt's SL-0090 cytoskeleton mapping is true but subsumed by retained microtubule and axoneme terms. SL-0066 cilium is retained as TOO_GENERAL beside accepted axoneme/kinocilium terms, consistently with the positive source-specific cilium rows. This does not restrict all primary cilia to kinocilia. SL-0304 maps axoneme; SL-0460 kinocilium traces to the rat ortholog.

The HPA record and human-cell antibody table report microtubules/cytosol as main locations and mitotic spindle as a supported additional location. HPA031582 and HPA031584 report spindle localization in RPTEC/TERT1 cells. This supports retaining the HPA mitotic-spindle annotation as non-core context. No individual HPA image pixels were read, and no spindle-assembly or chromosome-segregation function is manufactured from localization alone.

## Core source assessment

This source note is an authored synthesis of selected original passages, not a replacement publication cache. No new verbatim quotations are proposed. The original annotation source objects and both official product objects are preserved.

**PMID:21698230 — Massinen et al.** The original Results, Figure 3 caption and co-immunoprecipitation Methods distinguish two experiments: tagged human DCDC2 expressed in rat neurons or cultured cells, and endogenous rat Dcdc2–Kif3a association measured by proximity ligation and co-IP. The SH-SY5Y lane is a protein-input sample. Figure S1's caption reports human DCDC2 co-pelleting with polymerized microtubules from NIH 3T3 lysates, with microtubule-omission and LacZ controls. This supports microtubule binding beyond localization, while leaving a purified interface or binding affinity unmeasured. Human DCDC2 overexpression increases ciliary length and a Shh-responsive reporter, but the deletion/knockdown comparisons show context dependence. Proposed Smoothened transport is a mechanistic hypothesis. Wnt reporter elevation after knockdown is rescued by human DCDC2, whereas overexpression alone does not suppress that neuronal reporter. Public locator: [original article](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0020580), Results on ciliary localization, Kif3a interaction and signaling; Figure 3; Figure S1 caption; Materials and Methods, immunoprecipitation and reporter assays. Figures themselves and the complete supplementary file were not visually assessed.

**PMID:25557784 — Schueler et al.** The original identifies human DCDC2 and DVL1/2/3 constructs. Human renal-tubule and cholangiocyte DCDC2 localizes to the axoneme; cell-cycle experiments distinguish spindle/axonemal fibers from basal bodies, spindle poles and the midbody. Co-IP establishes DVL associations. Engineered patient mutants can retain those associations while losing suppression of beta-catenin/TCF output, so binding alone does not establish a DCDC2 adaptor mechanism. Two mouse Dcdc2 siRNAs reduce ciliation in IMCD3 spheroids, and human wild-type DCDC2 rescues the phenotype; tested truncations do not. A Wnt inhibitor restores cilia numbers in the knockdown model, supporting a connection to canonical Wnt regulation without establishing the exact molecular inhibitory step. Public locator: [original article](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ajhg.2014.12.002), Methods on constructs, co-IP, Wnt reporter and IMCD3 spheroids; Results and Figures 1G, 2 and 3. The actual cached text and captions were read; no full SI or figure-pixel reading is claimed.

**PMID:25601850 — Grati et al.** A separate [genuine original PMC HTML](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4383862/) was recovered while the normal publication cache remains abstract-only. Results and Figure 3 describe human variant-1 DCDC2-GFP expressed in rat sensory epithelia: hair-cell kinocilia and supporting-cell primary cilia are both positive locations. Wild-type and p.Gln424Pro affect cilium length, with stronger and more irregular mutant effects; these ciliary effects were not reproduced in MDCK-II or COS7 cells. Figure 3 reports 20 hair cells and 20 supporting cells per wild-type/mutant comparison. Relative length values are 123 ± 15% versus 223 ± 45% in hair cells, and 350 ± 110% versus 907 ± 322% in supporting cells; these are reported comparisons, not measurements re-extracted from images. Results/Figure 4 and auditory-assay Methods describe zebrafish dcdc2b morpholino effects on hair cells and microphonic responses, with developmental/dose limitations. Human family genetics, rat cell experiments and zebrafish physiology are separate evidence streams. Methods identify human NP_001182539.1 and OriGene variant-1 construct RG208721, supporting the long Q9UHG0-1 product. One Methods sentence spells the variant p.Glu424Pro, but mutagenesis, Results and the identified Gln424 residue consistently establish p.Gln424Pro. The original bytes are not corrected. Public locators: accession numbers, construct preparation, hair-cell transfection and auditory function Methods; Results and Figures 1–4 captions. No supplementary-material or figure-pixel claim is made.

**PMID:27319779 — Girard et al.** The complete normal-cache abstract reports four affected children with DCDC2 variants and normal cholangiocyte cytoplasmic/ciliary localization, contrasted with mutant cytoplasmic accumulation, ciliary absence and a ciliogenesis defect. Original Methods and figures remain inaccessible after genuine publisher attempts. Source-specific axoneme and assembly assertions are retained with curator deference because independent recovered target experiments support those functions; the precise 2016 experiments are not falsely described as recovered. Public locator: [original identifier and abstract](https://doi.org/10.1002/humu.23031). This source is not the different Grammatikopoulos cholangitis article available as PMC5116266.

The current official long product has 476 residues and doublecortin domains at 17–100 and 139–221. The 229-residue alternative product removes canonical 1–247 and replaces canonical 248–307 with its alternative sequence before continuing at 308. It lacks both annotated domains and is not the engineered 222–476 deletion used in the DVL study. No reviewed experiment establishes equivalent ciliary, microtubule or Wnt function for this short product. This product knowledge must not be retrofitted into the original GOA isoform fields.

Dishevelled partner class is assessed separately from DCDC2's own activity. The curated FUNCTION/SUBUNIT and DSH-family/domain records for [DVL1](https://www.uniprot.org/uniprotkb/O14640/entry), [DVL2](https://www.uniprot.org/uniprotkb/O14641/entry) and [DVL3](https://www.uniprot.org/uniprotkb/Q92997/entry) establish multivalent signaling-complex organization. Together with the direct target-paper co-IP evidence, this supports the binding-to-scaffold class GO:0097110. It does not establish that DCDC2 itself is a scaffold or that the associations are purified binary interactions. Some curated partner details are transferred by similarity; no partner-specific primary papers beyond the four scoped DCDC2 sources were claimed as independently read.

# DCDC2 follow-up evidence

The controlled microtubule co-pelleting experiment in PMID:21698230 supports microtubule binding beyond localization: expressed human DCDC2 in NIH/3T3 lysates co-pellets with polymerized microtubules, with microtubule-omission and LacZ controls. This is not purified binary reconstitution and does not establish binding kinetics, stabilization or activity of the natural short product. GO:0008017 is recorded as a NEW/IDA annotation and remains the molecular function of the first core. The generic protein-binding rows concern other protein partners, so none is repurposed as a microtubule assay. The user instruction rejecting ancestor/descendant additions is introduced specifically for NEW process terms. Applying it as a blanket prohibition on this missing molecular function was too broad; the source-supported addition corrects that interpretation.

The HuRI pairs identify measured binding capacity in a heterologous assay. PMID:32296183 reports sequence confirmation and pairwise quadruplicate retesting, with orthogonal validation of samples of the dataset. The six DCDC2 pairs each have array, pooling and validated two-hybrid source records; these are stages of the same mapping study, not independent physiological replications. Their complete partner sequences were verified. [HuRI's own methods explanation](https://interactome-atlas.org/faq/) distinguishes biophysical binding quality from physiological relevance and discusses membrane/folding limitations. No pair-specific orthogonal validation is established here. The molecular-function refinement describes binding to the verified partner class; it does not assign the partner's catalysis, transport, signaling or localization to DCDC2. Native membrane topology and cellular co-localization remain unresolved for the receptor, transporter and organellar enzyme pairs. The [project instruction](https://github.com/ai4curation/ai-gene-review/blob/a87ae568676c4012ea4abe5393967aeff1b52ae7/projects/CLINGEN_MENDELIAN.md#curation-instructions) preserves generic binding when no supported informative refinement exists; it does not equate unknown physiological relevance with absent binding-class evidence. This is a user task instruction, not a repository-wide policy or a human sign-off.

Current direct GO relations establish axoneme part_of ciliary plasm (GO:0097014) is_a cytoplasm. The broad cytoplasm judgments retain the observed non-ciliary microtubule pool. The cilium judgment relies on both axoneme and kinocilium: the supporting-cell primary-cilium result in PMID:25601850 is retained and is not converted into a sensory kinocilium result.

PMID:21698230 shows that full-length DCDC2 overexpression increases a Shh-responsive reporter in rat cortical neurons, whereas deletion constructs and knockdown do not. The positive gain-of-function result supports a non-core, experimental-context annotation. It does not establish that normal DCDC2 abundance is required for the pathway, that DCDC2 directly regulates Smoothened, or that the proposed kinesin-mediated trafficking mechanism was measured. This limitation differs from actual negative localization evidence at basal bodies and spindle poles in PMID:25557784.

The molecular step connecting DCDC2 to canonical Wnt suppression and the activity of the short 229-residue product remain unresolved biological questions. More binding observations alone would not settle either question.
