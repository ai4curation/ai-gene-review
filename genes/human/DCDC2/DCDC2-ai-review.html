Gene Description

DCDC2 is a microtubule-associated protein whose long isoform contains tandem doublecortin domains. It binds microtubules, associates with the kinesin-2 machinery and localizes along ciliary axonemes. DCDC2 supports epithelial cilium assembly and controls ciliary growth in sensory-cell contexts. It restrains canonical Wnt/beta-catenin signaling and associates with Dishevelled proteins, although the molecular link between those interactions and Wnt inhibition remains unresolved. Biallelic variants are associated with renal-hepatic ciliopathy and neonatal sclerosing cholangitis. The shorter annotated product lacks both doublecortin domains, and its equivalent ciliary or Wnt-regulatory activity has not been established.

Identity and products

The human gene is HGNC:18141, official symbol DCDC2, UniProt Q9UHG0. Verified synonyms include DCDC2A, DFNB66, KIAA1154, NPHP19 and RU2. ClinGen association strengths remain disease-specific: the project records definitive ciliopathy and limited hearing-loss evidence; neither a dyslexia association nor a cell assay is used to upgrade those clinical classifications.

Official products are Q9UHG0-1 (476 residues) and Q9UHG0-2 (229 residues). The short form is reconstructed from two changes: deletion of canonical residues 1–247 (VSP_014670), and replacement of residues 248–307 by a different 60-residue segment (VSP_014671). It is not simply the C-terminal tail of the long form. Both products exactly match their official FASTA sequences. The long product has canonical doublecortin domains at residues 17–100 and 139–221; these regions are absent from the short product. This architecture does not by itself prove that the short form is inactive. No equivalent ciliary or microtubule activity is assigned to it without an assay.

The 81 joined interaction records use the long DCDC2 sequence, with exact sequence checksum matches. Participant checksum matches identify the underlying entry sequence, while experimental tags and binding fragments must still be interpreted separately.

Interaction evidence and role limits

PMID:25416956 contributes two target pairs, with NIF3L1 and MRFAP1. PMID:32296183 contributes twenty-five target pairs. All twenty-seven source rows join three deposited IntAct records each: array, prey-pooling, and validated two-hybrid stages in yeast. This is positive binary-interaction evidence with human protein sequences; it is not twenty-seven demonstrated physiological pathways or three independent in-vivo observations per pair. MINT returned zero rows in the saved query. Four additional IntAct records concern other publications and were not substituted for these sources.

Eight high-throughput pairs support narrower binding terms: TRMT11/MGME1/B3GNT5/MYG1 enzyme binding; DYNLT2 dynein light-chain binding; DYNC2LI1 isoform2 dynein light-intermediate-chain binding; SLC38A2 transmembrane-transporter binding; and C5AR2 signaling-receptor binding. The observed pair plus the verified identity of a catalytic enzyme, receptor or named dynein chain supports that binding class. It does not assign the partner's activity, regulation or pathway to DCDC2. The other nineteen high-throughput pairs remain non-core generic binding. Neuronal migration is context dependent

The original PMID:16278297 abstract reports altered migration following RNA interference but does not name the organism. The rat attribution is supported by the verified rat donor record and the later original PMID:21689730, whose Introduction and Discussion identify those prior experiments as rat neocortical RNAi studies. The initial immutable cache and later efetch recovery contain the abstract, not a complete original body. PMID:18313856 similarly reports rat embryonic knockdown, partial rescue of heterotopia, and unresolved overmigration; its recovered body is abstract-only.

The genuinely recovered full text of PMID:21689730 supplies the contrasting mouse experiment. Both constitutive and mosaic conditional Dcdc2 deletion gave grossly normal neocortical migration in the reported assays. Dcx shRNA caused more severe migration defects in the Dcdc2 mutant background than in wild type, supporting a context-dependent contribution rather than an absolute requirement. The study established the exon2 deletion, aberrant splicing and transcript decay, while noting that the tested antibodies did not verify the protein-level knockout. These findings support retaining all four neuronal-migration source rows as non-core developmental context. They do not establish a general human neuronal-migration mechanism or a Mendelian dyslexia diagnosis.

RU2AS antigen is not DCDC2 immune-effector activity

The full Results of PMID:10601354 distinguish the sense RU2S/DCDC2 message from an oppositely transcribed RU2AS message, represented by cDNA4.1 and encoding an 84-residue antigenic product. A peptide from that antisense product sensitized cells to HLA-B7-restricted CTL recognition. The study does not establish a cellular defense effector role for the long or short DCDC2 product. Moreover, being recognized as an antigen is not itself performing the defense response. The review removes GO:0006968 on this explicit product/role distinction, rather than title-based suspicion or inability to read the source.

Core source assessment

This source note is an authored synthesis of selected original passages, not a replacement publication cache. No new verbatim quotations are proposed. The original annotation source objects and both official product objects are preserved.

PMID:21698230 — Massinen et al. The original Results, Figure 3 caption and co-immunoprecipitation Methods distinguish two experiments: tagged human DCDC2 expressed in rat neurons or cultured cells, and endogenous rat Dcdc2–Kif3a association measured by proximity ligation and co-IP. The SH-SY5Y lane is a protein-input sample. Figure S1's caption reports human DCDC2 co-pelleting with polymerized microtubules from NIH 3T3 lysates, with microtubule-omission and LacZ controls. This supports microtubule binding beyond localization, while leaving a purified interface or binding affinity unmeasured. Human DCDC2 overexpression increases ciliary length and a Shh-responsive reporter, but the deletion/knockdown comparisons show context dependence. Proposed Smoothened transport is a mechanistic hypothesis. Wnt reporter elevation after knockdown is rescued by human DCDC2, whereas overexpression alone does not suppress that neuronal reporter. Public locator: original article, Results on ciliary localization, Kif3a interaction and signaling; Figure 3; Figure S1 caption; Materials and Methods, immunoprecipitation and reporter assays. Figures themselves and the complete supplementary file were not visually assessed.

PMID:25557784 — Schueler et al. The original identifies human DCDC2 and DVL1/2/3 constructs. Human renal-tubule and cholangiocyte DCDC2 localizes to the axoneme; cell-cycle experiments distinguish spindle/axonemal fibers from basal bodies, spindle poles and the midbody. Co-IP establishes DVL associations. Engineered patient mutants can retain those associations while losing suppression of beta-catenin/TCF output, so binding alone does not establish a DCDC2 adaptor mechanism. Two mouse Dcdc2 siRNAs reduce ciliation in IMCD3 spheroids, and human wild-type DCDC2 rescues the phenotype; tested truncations do not. A Wnt inhibitor restores cilia numbers in the knockdown model, supporting a connection to canonical Wnt regulation without establishing the exact molecular inhibitory step. Public locator: original article, Methods on constructs, co-IP, Wnt reporter and IMCD3 spheroids; Results and Figures 1G, 2 and 3. The actual cached text and captions were read; no full SI or figure-pixel reading is claimed.

PMID:25601850 — Grati et al. A separate genuine original PMC HTML was recovered while the normal publication cache remains abstract-only. Results and Figure 3 describe human variant-1 DCDC2-GFP expressed in rat sensory epithelia: hair-cell kinocilia and supporting-cell primary cilia are both positive locations. Wild-type and p.Gln424Pro affect cilium length, with stronger and more irregular mutant effects; these ciliary effects were not reproduced in MDCK-II or COS7 cells. Figure 3 reports 20 hair cells and 20 supporting cells per wild-type/mutant comparison. Relative length values are 123 ± 15% versus 223 ± 45% in hair cells, and 350 ± 110% versus 907 ± 322% in supporting cells; these are reported comparisons, not measurements re-extracted from images. Results/Figure 4 and auditory-assay Methods describe zebrafish dcdc2b morpholino effects on hair cells and microphonic responses, with developmental/dose limitations. Human family genetics, rat cell experiments and zebrafish physiology are separate evidence streams. Methods identify human NP_001182539.1 and OriGene variant-1 construct RG208721, supporting the long Q9UHG0-1 product. One Methods sentence spells the variant p.Glu424Pro, but mutagenesis, Results and the identified Gln424 residue consistently establish p.Gln424Pro. The original bytes are not corrected. Public locators: accession numbers, construct preparation, hair-cell transfection and auditory function Methods; Results and Figures 1–4 captions. No supplementary-material or figure-pixel claim is made.

PMID:27319779 — Girard et al. The complete normal-cache abstract reports four affected children with DCDC2 variants and normal cholangiocyte cytoplasmic/ciliary localization, contrasted with mutant cytoplasmic accumulation, ciliary absence and a ciliogenesis defect. Original Methods and figures remain inaccessible after genuine publisher attempts. Source-specific axoneme and assembly assertions are retained with curator deference because independent recovered target experiments support those functions; the precise 2016 experiments are not falsely described as recovered. Public locator: original identifier and abstract. This source is not the different Grammatikopoulos cholangitis article available as PMC5116266.

DCDC2 follow-up evidence

The controlled microtubule co-pelleting experiment in PMID:21698230 supports microtubule binding beyond localization: expressed human DCDC2 in NIH/3T3 lysates co-pellets with polymerized microtubules, with microtubule-omission and LacZ controls. This is not purified binary reconstitution and does not establish binding kinetics, stabilization or activity of the natural short product. GO:0008017 is recorded as a NEW/IDA annotation and remains the molecular function of the first core. The generic protein-binding rows concern other protein partners, so none is repurposed as a microtubule assay. The user instruction rejecting ancestor/descendant additions is introduced specifically for NEW process terms. Applying it as a blanket prohibition on this missing molecular function was too broad; the source-supported addition corrects that interpretation.

The HuRI pairs identify measured binding capacity in a heterologous assay. PMID:32296183 reports sequence confirmation and pairwise quadruplicate retesting, with orthogonal validation of samples of the dataset. The six DCDC2 pairs each have array, pooling and validated two-hybrid source records; these are stages of the same mapping study, not independent physiological replications. Their complete partner sequences were verified. HuRI's own methods explanation distinguishes biophysical binding quality from physiological relevance and discusses membrane/folding limitations. No pair-specific orthogonal validation is established here. The molecular-function refinement describes binding to the verified partner class; it does not assign the partner's catalysis, transport, signaling or localization to DCDC2. Native membrane topology and cellular co-localization remain unresolved for the receptor, transporter and organellar enzyme pairs. The project instruction preserves generic binding when no supported informative refinement exists; it does not equate unknown physiological relevance with absent binding-class evidence. This is a user task instruction, not a repository-wide policy or a human sign-off.

Current direct GO relations establish axoneme part_of ciliary plasm (GO:0097014) is_a cytoplasm. The broad cytoplasm judgments retain the observed non-ciliary microtubule pool. The cilium judgment relies on both axoneme and kinocilium: the supporting-cell primary-cilium result in PMID:25601850 is retained and is not converted into a sensory kinocilium result.

PMID:21698230 shows that full-length DCDC2 overexpression increases a Shh-responsive reporter in rat cortical neurons, whereas deletion constructs and knockdown do not. The positive gain-of-function result supports a non-core, experimental-context annotation. It does not establish that normal DCDC2 abundance is required for the pathway, that DCDC2 directly regulates Smoothened, or that the proposed kinesin-mediated trafficking mechanism was measured. This limitation differs from actual negative localization evidence at basal bodies and spindle poles in PMID:25557784.

The molecular step connecting DCDC2 to canonical Wnt suppression and the activity of the short 229-residue product remain unresolved biological questions. More binding observations alone would not settle either question.
