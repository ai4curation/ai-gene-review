Gene Description

COL9A1 encodes the alpha1 chain of collagen IX, a fibril-associated collagen with interrupted triple helices in cartilage and vitreous extracellular matrix. Alpha1(IX) assembles with alpha2(IX) and alpha3(IX), and the resulting heterotrimer associates with type II collagen fibrils and other matrix components. The long alpha1 chain exposes an N-terminal NC4 domain that binds heparin and cartilage oligomeric matrix protein. Recombinant NC4 can also inhibit terminal complement assembly. Alternative transcripts encode a shorter N-terminal form and a truncated product. Biallelic pathogenic variants cause recessive Stickler syndrome with ocular, auditory and skeletal manifestations.

Alpha1(IX) supplies structural elements of the alpha1/alpha2/alpha3 type IX heterotrimer at the surface of type II collagen fibrils. Chain assembly and covalent matrix connections support this fibril-associated matrix role. The NC2 structure is an engineered assembly-domain assay, and the chain does not autonomously form the major fibrillar scaffold or catalyze its own processing. Long/short precursor architecture and the severely truncated product are kept distinct.

COL9A1 evidence notes

COL9A1 supplies the alpha1 chain of fibril-associated collagen IX. The structural interpretation combines native human cartilage cross-links, chain assembly and matrix localization. It does not treat the chain as an enzyme or as an autonomous major fibrillar scaffold. A GO:0030020 structural core is retained with type IX trimer membership and extracellular matrix organization; the GO definition expressly permits extracellular collagen proteins under this structural term.

Products and assembly

The three official products reconstruct exactly from the recorded sequence changes: long P20849-1 is 921 residues, Short P20849-2 is 678 residues, and truncated P20849-3 is 328 residues. The short product lacks NC4 but retains the main downstream assembly regions. The truncated product retains NC4 and part of COL3, but lacks the remaining collagenous and noncollagenous assembly regions. A retained domain establishes sequence coverage, not secretion, folding or equivalent intact-product activity.

Original human cartilage analysis maps alpha1(IX) collagenous-region cross-links to alpha1(II) and alpha3(IX) NC1. These are positive network contacts, not an independently established alpha1–alpha1 homodimer. The separate original recombinant study reports predominantly monomers and dimers when human full-length alpha1(IX) is individually expressed in insect cells. That supports non-core contextual self-association while preserving the original source assertion. Its engineered shortened constructs must not be renamed as the natural Short product. The source-evidence index, followup_source_clarifications, provides a short exact Results excerpt and Figure 3 caption locator, distinguishing original full-text evidence from the selected abstract cache. PMID:8660302(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8660302/), original indexed source, PMID:17880280 accepted postprint.

The NC2 structure uses the three IX chains attached to engineered type-I host–guest collagen sequences. It resolves a genuine assembly interface and stagger, without being an intact IX structure or testing every natural product. The gene/exon study's complete abstract corroborates identity and transcript architecture; full original assembly passages were unavailable. PMID:27897211(https://doi.org/10.1038/srep37831), PMID:9707347(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9707347/).

NC4 ligands and complement

Original PMID:15047691 directly tests human NC4 residues 24–268 with rat COMP (P35444, including its 266–755 fragment), heparin, competition and a folding-controlled basic-region mutation. Thus extracellular matrix binding and heparin binding are justified source-preserving replacements. They are semantic ligand refinements, not claims of current typed descendant edges from the original generic terms. The 17553797 abstract independently supports the heparin-tetrasaccharide interaction and reports positive zinc measurements. Full Methods/control access remained unavailable, so no zinc NEW or physiological zinc-regulatory core is proposed. Heparin binding is also represented as a distinct core activity of alpha1 NC4 in extracellular matrix. The original 2004 SPR and solid-state assays tested both isolated NC4 and full-length recombinant human collagen IX. The apparent full-molecule affinity was 3.6 nM, versus approximately 0.6 µM for isolated NC4; this is not a sub-nanomolar measurement. Additional heterotrimer binding sites are not all assigned to alpha1, and a native matrix-bridging mechanism or natural splice-product equivalence is not inferred. 15047691 original Methods/Results, 17553797 primary abstract.

Recombinant human NC4 performs direct inhibitory work in the terminal-complement assays: purified C9 polymerization and membrane-associated polymeric C9 decrease with controls. This supports one experimental, NC4-scoped NEW GO:0001971. The domain is not itself a factor-I cofactor or protease. It enhances reactions containing FH/C4BP, and a poorly NC4-binding C4BP mutant retains enhancement; an obligatory cofactor-bridging model is therefore not asserted. Effects of intact collagen IX and quantitative endogenous fragment activity remain unresolved. Human pAM104-2 identity is established by the original indexed Methods of reference16, PMID:19700767, without assuming equivalence to the earlier pGAT2-NC23 construct or exact residue endpoints. 21659506 full source, 19700767.

Current GO:0001971 includes inhibition of MAC assembly/formation and has no narrower returned descendant. Final is_a/part_of coverage against all original, replacement and core terms finds no ancestor/descendant redundancy. CLU has two exact human IDA records for this term; VTN and COMP have no matching records in complete queries, which is retained as an absence rather than a universal exclusion. These are annotation comparators, not newly read comparator experiments. The broader parent and a redundant complement-binding NEW are omitted. The saved GO-CAM index has no COL9A1/P20849 hit.
