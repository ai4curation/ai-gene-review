| Target identity | **GLUD1** encodes human mitochondrial glutamate dehydrogenase 1 (**hGDH1**; target accession **P00367** as specified). It is the broadly expressed chromosome-10 housekeeping paralog, not the X-linked hominoid-specific **GLUD2/hGDH2**. | 2023–2024 structural and evolutionary literature | Litso et al., published 23 Dec 2023, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/biom14010022) (pqac-00000022); Aleshina and Aleshin, 14 Apr 2024, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/ijms25084341) (pqac-00000017) |
| Primary reaction and specificity | EC 1.4.1.3 catalyzes **L-glutamate + NAD(P)+ + H2O ⇌ 2-oxoglutarate + NH4+ + NAD(P)H + H+**. Both NAD+ and NADP+ can serve as cofactors. **L-leucine is principally an activator, not the amino-acid substrate deaminated in this reaction.** | Direct human-enzyme biochemical description and 2024 review | Fang et al., 1 Apr 2002, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1042/0264-6021:3630081) (pqac-00000040); Aleshina and Aleshin, 14 Apr 2024, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/ijms25084341) (pqac-00000017) |
| Localization and maturation | GLUD1 is synthesized as a **558-residue precursor** with a **53-residue N-terminal mitochondrial targeting peptide**; cleavage after import yields a **505-residue mature mitochondrial-matrix enzyme**. Mitochondria are the principal location, although full-length extramitochondrial forms have been reported. | Structural and targeting evidence | Litso et al., published 23 Dec 2023, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/biom14010022) (pqac-00000022); Aleshina and Aleshin, 14 Apr 2024, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/ijms25084341) (pqac-00000038) |
| Oligomer and domains | Mature hGDH1 forms a symmetric **homohexamer composed of two trimers**. Each subunit contains glutamate- and NAD(P)-binding regions, the regulatory antenna and a pivot helix, consistent with the supplied InterPro amino-acid-dehydrogenase, catalytic, dimerization and active-site assignments. | Human X-ray structure and structural review | Plaitakis et al., Feb 2017, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/biology6010011) (pqac-00000020, pqac-00000025); Litso et al., published 23 Dec 2023, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/biom14010022) (pqac-00000022) |
| Energy-state regulation | **GTP inhibits** hGDH1 by stabilizing a closed or product-bound state; **ADP activates** by facilitating catalytic-cleft opening and product release. At 1 mM, ADP increased hGDH1 activity by **235%**; the reported ADP SC50 was **25 micromolar**. | Recombinant kinetics, crystallography and mutagenesis | Plaitakis et al., Feb 2017, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/biology6010011) (pqac-00000019) |
| Leucine regulation | L-leucine activates hGDH1 over approximately **0.5–5 mM**, with a reported SC50 of **1.0 mM**, and can counter GTP inhibition together with ADP. Evidence favors facilitation of product release, possibly through active-site occupancy rather than a conventional remote allosteric site. | Recombinant kinetics and structure–function analysis | Plaitakis et al., Feb 2017, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/biology6010011) (pqac-00000019) |
| Regulatory-site validation | GTP half-inhibition shifted from **42 nM for wild-type human GDH** to **210 micromolar for H454Y** and **3.1 micromolar for S448P**. These disease variants selectively impaired GTP inhibition while retaining ADP and leucine responsiveness. Engineered **R463A abolished ADP activation** with little effect on GTP inhibition or leucine activation. | Primary human recombinant-protein mutagenesis | Fang et al., 1 Apr 2002, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1042/0264-6021:3630081) (pqac-00000028, pqac-00000040, pqac-00000021) |
| Physiological direction and pH | Although equilibrium can favor glutamate synthesis, low tissue ammonia and GLUD1's high ammonia requirement generally favor **oxidative deamination** in relevant settings. Recombinant hGDH1 ammonia Km increased from **12.8 mM at pH 8.0** to **35.0 mM at pH 7.5** and **57.5 mM at pH 7.0**, making reductive amination progressively less favorable during acidification. | Recombinant human-enzyme kinetics and physiological interpretation | Plaitakis et al., Feb 2017, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/biology6010011) (pqac-00000009, pqac-00000020) |
| Disease mechanism | Dominant activating GLUD1 variants causing hyperinsulinism–hyperammonemia (**HI/HA**) affect the GTP-contact, antenna or pivot-helix network. Reduced nucleotide inhibition permits excessive glutamate oxidation and TCA-cycle ATP production in beta cells, driving inappropriate insulin release, especially after leucine or protein exposure. | Human genetics, recombinant mutagenesis and 2024 structural analysis | Fang et al., 1 Apr 2002, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1042/0264-6021:3630081) (pqac-00000040); Aleshina and Aleshin, 14 Apr 2024, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/ijms25084341) (pqac-00000037) |
| Recent clinical statistics | A 2023 two-center cohort included **25** children with activating GLUD1 variants: **24/25 (96%)** presented with hypoglycemic seizures, **24/25 responded to diazoxide plus protein restriction**, and **13/25 (52%)** developed neurodevelopmental manifestations. Epilepsy occurred in **9/25 (36%)**, learning difficulties in **8/25 (32%)**, and speech delay in **8/25 (32%)**. | Retrospective human genotype–phenotype cohort | Aftab et al., Apr 2023, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1530/ec-22-0008) (pqac-00000023, pqac-00000029) |
| Current clinical implementation | International guidance recommends **diazoxide as first-line therapy for established hyperinsulinism**, generally **5–15 mg/kg/day in children**, with safety monitoring. Diazoxide suppresses insulin secretion through beta-cell KATP channels; it is not a selective GLUD1 inhibitor. The guideline was **published online in July 2023** and appears in **volume 97 (2024)**. | International consensus guideline | De Leon et al., online 6 Jul 2023, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1159/000531766) (pqac-00000024, pqac-00000042) |
| 2024 targeting-sequence development | A 2024 evolutionary analysis proposes that ape-GLUD1 **A23S** slightly reduced mitochondrial-import efficiency and may create a phosphorylation-sensitive site near Ser21. This is a **sequence- and structure-based inference**, not direct proof that A23S alters endogenous human P00367 import. | Evolutionary inference with explicit caveat | Aleshina and Aleshin, 14 Apr 2024, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/ijms25084341) (pqac-00000032, pqac-00000037) |
