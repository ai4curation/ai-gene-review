Gene Description

AJM1 (apical junction component 1 homolog, formerly C9orf172) is a 976-residue human protein carrying the diagnostic domains of the AJM-1 family (Ajm-1, AJM1_C and a shared apical-junction signature) and no catalytic or transporter domain, so it is expected to act structurally rather than enzymatically. It is named for, and inferred from, the Caenorhabditis elegans protein AJM-1, a large coiled-coil component of the nematode apical junction that occupies a domain basal to the cadherin-catenin complex, is recruited there by the MAGUK scaffold DLG-1 together with LET-413/Scribble, and is required for the integrity of that junctional domain. On that basis human AJM1 is expected to localise to the apical plasma membrane and to cell-cell junctions and to contribute to organising them, most plausibly as a scaffolding or adapter component rather than as a signalling or catalytic one. This expectation is untested: the protein has no experimental characterisation of its own, no reported binding partners, no endogenous localisation study, and no established physiological or disease role in humans, and nematode and vertebrate apical junctions are not assembled from the same components, so conservation of the specific structural role remains an open question rather than an established fact.

Core Functions

Inferred structural component of epithelial cell-cell junctions. This entry is recorded as an inference and should be read as one: human AJM1 has no experimental annotation, no reported interaction partner and no endogenous localisation study, so everything below is transferred from C. elegans ajm-1 or read off domain architecture. On that basis AJM1 is expected to sit at the apical plasma membrane and at cell-cell junctions and to help organise them, acting as a scaffolding or adapter component - it carries the AJM-1 family domains and no catalytic or transporter domain, so a non-enzymatic structural role is the only reading the sequence supports. No molecular_function term is asserted here, deliberately. The worm donor's only molecular function annotation is cytoskeletal protein binding with TAS evidence [file:genes/human/AJM1/AJM1-donor-goa-check.json "worm ajm-1's only molecular function annotation is GO:0008092 with TAS evidence"], which is too weak and too generic to transfer, and inventing a structural-constituent term for a protein with zero human data would be the kind of over-annotation this review set exists to remove. The honest position is a located, process-level inference with the molecular activity left open. The largest open question is not whether AJM1 is junctional but whether the specific nematode role transfers at all, since the nematode apical junction and the vertebrate apical junction are not built from the same parts.

Suggested Questions for Experts

Q: Does human AJM1 localise to epithelial junctions at all? Every location annotation on this gene is a by-similarity transfer from one worm protein, and a single endogenous-tagging or validated-antibody experiment in a polarised human epithelial line would either ground the whole set or collapse it.

Q: Does human AJM1 bind DLG1? The worm interaction with the DLG-1 L27 domain is the mechanistic core of the ortholog's function, and its presence or absence in human cells is the sharpest single test of whether the nematode role transferred.

Q: Should the cilium annotation be removed at UniProt as well as in GOA? The human IEA derives from UniProt's by-similarity cilium statement, whose worm source is itself only IEA. Fixing it only in GOA leaves the generator in place.

Q: Does mouse Ajm1 (A2AJA9), in the same PTHR21517:SF3 subfamily, have any experimental data? A mammalian ortholog with even one localisation experiment would be far stronger ground for the human transfers than the nematode is, and it was not examined in this review.

Q: Is the 'no catalytic domain' reading safe? It rests on InterPro/Pfam returning only Ajm-1, AJM1_C and the Apical_junction signature. The cysteine-rich stretch in the human sequence has not been scanned against structural or metal-binding motif sets, and should be before the non-enzymatic claim is treated as settled.

Q: Is the apparent independence of this gene's evidence codes misleading in general? Here three IEAs, an ISS and three IBAs reduce to one worm protein plus one InterPro family. A curator scanning the evidence-code column would read this as far better supported than it is.

Suggested Experiments

Experiment: Endogenous tagging of AJM1 in a polarised human epithelial line (Caco-2 or MDCK-equivalent) with apical, tight-junction and adherens-junction markers, to test the apical plasma membrane and apical junction complex annotations directly rather than by similarity.

Experiment: Proximity labelling from tagged endogenous AJM1 in the same line, specifically asking whether DLG1, SCRIB or catenins are recovered - i.e. whether the worm DAC architecture has any human counterpart.

Experiment: AJM1 knockout in a polarised epithelial line followed by transepithelial electrical resistance and permeability assays, which would convert the cell-cell junction organization term from an IMP transfer into direct human evidence.

1. Identity and domain verification

The supplied UniProt record identifies C9J069 as human AJM1/C9orf172. Its listed domains—Ajm-1 (IPR058586/PF26649), AJM1_C (IPR060583/PF27982), and Apical_junction (IPR038825)—align with an AJM-1-like junction-associated protein rather than an enzyme or transporter. These are sequence/classification annotations, not demonstrations of human protein activity or localization.

The domain interpretation is compatible with model-organism literature describing worm AJM-1 as a large protein with a shared coiled-coil region. Some worm isoforms may also possess a predicted transmembrane segment, but that prediction cannot be transferred automatically to the human protein. (xiong2026anapicaljunction pages 6-10, xiong2026anapicaljunction pages 50-53)

A major source of potential confusion is that mechanistic papers commonly use ajm-1/AJM-1 for the nematode gene and protein. These are relevant ortholog studies, not studies of human C9J069. No evidence retrieved indicated that the requested human symbol refers to a different human protein.

2. Probable molecular function

Best current model

Human AJM1 is most plausibly a non-catalytic structural or adapter/scaffold protein. This interpretation rests on:

  1. its conserved Ajm-1/apical-junction domain architecture;
  2. the absence of a recognized catalytic or transporter domain;
  3. the junctional localization and structural phenotypes of C. elegans AJM-1; and
  4. the physical and functional relationship between worm AJM-1 and the MAGUK scaffold DLG-1.

Accordingly, there is no known enzymatic reaction, substrate, cofactor, or transport cargo to report. Its presumed role is organizational: assembling or stabilizing a protein complex at cell–cell contacts.

Ortholog evidence supporting the model

In C. elegans, AJM-1 belongs to the DLG-1/AJM-1 complex, a junctional domain positioned basal to the classical cadherin–catenin complex. DLG-1 is required for efficient AJM-1 recruitment and spreading along junctions; AJM-1 then appears to stabilize the complex. AJM-1 was reported to associate in vivo with the N-terminal/L27 region of DLG-1. (lynch2009theassemblyand pages 2-6, lynch2009theassemblyand pages 7-8, lynch2009theassemblyand pages 8-9)

Loss of worm AJM-1 or DLG-1 disrupts epithelial architecture. Reported phenotypes include loss or alteration of electron-dense junctional structures, bubble-like separations between cells, swollen or ruptured epithelia, and developmental arrest near the two-fold elongation stage. Genetic enhancement of cadherin–catenin or VAB-9 defects further indicates that this complex cooperates with other adhesion systems to preserve epithelial integrity. (lynch2009theassemblyand pages 7-8, lynch2009theassemblyand pages 8-9, armenti2012adherensjunctionsin pages 8-10)

This is strong evidence for the worm protein, but only low-to-moderate-confidence functional inference for human AJM1. Mammalian apical junctions are not identical to the nematode CeAJ, and no direct human DLG1–AJM1 interaction was identified.

3. Cellular localization

Human protein

A decisive endogenous localization study for human C9J069 was not identified. Therefore, it is not presently justified to state as fact that human AJM1 resides at tight junctions, adherens junctions, the plasma membrane, or any other compartment.

Ortholog-based prediction

Worm AJM-1 localizes to apical epithelial junctions, including digestive-tract junctions and electron-dense interfaces between amphid neurons, sheath glia, socket glia, and hypodermal cells. DLG-1 and LET-413 regulate localization of the DLG-1/AJM-1 complex. (markovich2024decipheringinternaland pages 4-6, lynch2009theassemblyand pages 2-6, xiong2026anapicaljunction pages 6-10)

Thus, the leading human hypothesis is cell–cell-junction-associated localization on the cytoplasmic side of epithelial contacts, potentially in a DLG-family scaffold complex. This remains a prediction requiring endogenous tagging, validated antibodies, super-resolution microscopy, and biochemical fractionation in human epithelial cells.

4. Biological processes and pathways

Junction organization and epithelial integrity

The most credible process assignment is cell–cell junction organization or stabilization. In worms, AJM-1 is necessary for normal junction ultrastructure and cooperates genetically with cadherin–catenin and other junctional systems. A 2024 review of intestinal junctional complexes summarizes AJM-1 as a coiled-coil component of the worm DLG-1/AJM-1 complex, with DLG-1 recruiting AJM-1 and LET-413 positioning the complex. The review does not supply direct evidence that human AJM1 has the same role. Published August 2024; DOI: https://doi.org/10.1080/19490976.2024.2389320. (markovich2024decipheringinternaland pages 4-6)

Mechanosensitive calcium signaling: recent ortholog development

A March 2026 preprint—not a 2023–2024 human study—extended worm AJM-1 biology beyond epithelial structure. Genetic and tissue-specific degradation experiments suggested that AJM-1 promotes the AWC^ON olfactory-neuron fate by supporting SLO-1 BK-channel expression and antagonizing DEL-1-dependent mechanosensitive calcium signaling through UNC-2/EGL-19 channels. The authors explicitly treated the force-sensing model as inferential because mechanical forces were not measured directly. DOI: https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.03.12.710951. (xiong2026anapicaljunction pages 46-50, xiong2026anapicaljunction pages 26-31)

Quantitatively, the temperature-sensitive worm allele produced the abnormal 2AWC^OFF phenotype in 32%, 75%, and 92% of animals at 15, 20, and 25°C, respectively. Tissue-specific experiments reported 22–40% penetrance after depletion of DLG-1 or LET-413 in selected tissues, whereas AWC-specific SLO-1 depletion produced 96–100% penetrance. These results establish a genetically tractable worm pathway, not a human AJM1 signaling pathway. (xiong2026anapicaljunction pages 18-22, xiong2026anapicaljunction pages 6-10)

5. Human experimental and disease evidence

Preliminary small-cell lung cancer study

A 2024 report proposed a BCYRN1–miR-378c–AJM1 regulatory axis in small-cell lung cancer. In human SBC-2 cells, luciferase-targeting experiments and siRNA knockdown/rescue were interpreted to place AJM1 downstream of miR-378c; AJM1 depletion reportedly reversed effects of miR-378c depletion on proliferation, migration, invasion, and epithelial–mesenchymal-transition-related behavior. The study also described serum cohorts of 10 patients with distant metastasis, 20 without distant metastasis, and 10 healthy controls, plus a nude-mouse model. (wentao2024hyperexpressedbcyrn1promotes pages 1-2)

This is the most direct retrieved human mechanistic lead, but confidence should remain limited. The available record lacked a journal, DOI, animal sample size, independent replication, and detailed confirmation that endogenous AJM1 protein—not merely a reporter or transcript-level relationship—mediated the phenotype. It also concerns a disease context and does not establish AJM1’s normal physiological function.

At present, there is no compelling evidence that germline or somatic AJM1 variation causes a Mendelian disorder, that AJM1 is a validated cancer driver, or that its abundance predicts clinical outcome independently.

The most relevant 2023–2024 development is the August 2024 junctional-complex review, which reinforces the evolutionary rationale for studying AJM-1 proteins but discusses functional evidence predominantly from C. elegans. The preliminary 2024 SCLC report raises a human disease hypothesis, but it is not sufficiently mature for clinical interpretation. (wentao2024hyperexpressedbcyrn1promotes pages 1-2, markovich2024decipheringinternaland pages 4-6)

No approved drug, clinical trial, diagnostic test, companion biomarker, or therapeutic program directed at AJM1 was identified. Open Targets' very weak, evidence-poor disease scores support the conclusion that AJM1 is currently a research target rather than a clinically actionable target. (OpenTargets Search: -AJM1)

Potential near-term applications are experimental rather than clinical:

  • endogenous fluorescent tagging to establish human subcellular localization;
  • AJM1 knockout and rescue in polarized epithelial monolayers, followed by transepithelial resistance and permeability assays;
  • co-immunoprecipitation, proximity labeling, and quantitative proteomics to test DLG1 and other junctional partners;
  • deletion of Ajm-1 and AJM1_C domains to map structural requirements;
  • replication of the miR-378c targeting result with endogenous AJM1 protein measurements and 3′-UTR editing;
  • organoid and animal studies assessing epithelial integrity, polarity, and tissue-specific phenotypes.

7. Expert assessment and evidence gaps

The authoritative interpretation is conservative. The identity and domain annotations are secure, and the worm ortholog has a well-supported structural role at apical junctions. Nevertheless, the evidence chain from worm AJM-1 to human C9J069 remains incomplete. In particular, the following are unknown or unvalidated for human AJM1:

  • endogenous subcellular localization;
  • membrane topology;
  • direct binding partners;
  • whether it binds human DLG1;
  • tissue- and cell-type-specific physiological function;
  • consequences of human loss of function;
  • structural architecture at atomic or domain resolution;
  • causal involvement in cancer or other disease.

The protein should therefore be annotated as “probable junction-associated structural/scaffolding protein, inferred from conserved domains and ortholog evidence”, not as an established human tight-junction component. The worm mechanosensitive-calcium pathway is an interesting hypothesis-generating result but should not yet be assigned to human AJM1. Likewise, the cancer RNA-axis study is preliminary and insufficient to justify biomarker or therapeutic claims.

Bottom line

Human AJM1/C9orf172/C9J069 is the correct target, but it is poorly characterized. Its strongest functional model is a non-enzymatic role in the organization or stabilization of cell–cell junctions. That model derives mainly from C. elegans, where AJM-1 forms a DLG-1-dependent junctional complex required for epithelial architecture. Direct human evidence is presently limited to preliminary disease-context observations, with no established biochemical activity, definitive localization, validated physiological pathway, or clinical application.

suggested_questions:
- question: Does human AJM1 localise to epithelial junctions at all? Every location annotation on this
    gene is a by-similarity transfer from one worm protein, and a single endogenous-tagging or validated-antibody
    experiment in a polarised human epithelial line would either ground the whole set or collapse it.
- question: Does human AJM1 bind DLG1? The worm interaction with the DLG-1 L27 domain is the mechanistic
    core of the ortholog's function, and its presence or absence in human cells is the sharpest single
    test of whether the nematode role transferred.
- question: Should the cilium annotation be removed at UniProt as well as in GOA? The human IEA derives
    from UniProt's by-similarity cilium statement, whose worm source is itself only IEA. Fixing it only
    in GOA leaves the generator in place.
- question: Does mouse Ajm1 (A2AJA9), in the same PTHR21517:SF3 subfamily, have any experimental data?
    A mammalian ortholog with even one localisation experiment would be far stronger ground for the human
    transfers than the nematode is, and it was not examined in this review.
- question: Is the 'no catalytic domain' reading safe? It rests on InterPro/Pfam returning only Ajm-1,
    AJM1_C and the Apical_junction signature. The cysteine-rich stretch in the human sequence has not
    been scanned against structural or metal-binding motif sets, and should be before the non-enzymatic
    claim is treated as settled.
- question: Is the apparent independence of this gene's evidence codes misleading in general? Here three
    IEAs, an ISS and three IBAs reduce to one worm protein plus one InterPro family. A curator scanning
    the evidence-code column would read this as far better supported than it is.
