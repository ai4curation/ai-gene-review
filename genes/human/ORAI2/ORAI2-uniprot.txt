CC   -!- FUNCTION: Pore-forming subunit of inward rectifying Ca(2+) release-
CC       activated Ca(2+) (CRAC) channels. Assembles with ORAI1 and ORAI3 to
CC       form hexameric CRAC channels that mediate Ca(2+) influx upon depletion
CC       of endoplasmic reticulum Ca(2+) store and channel activation by Ca(2+)
CC       sensor STIM1, a process known as store-operated Ca(2+) entry (SOCE).
CC       Various pore subunit combinations may account for distinct CRAC channel
CC       spatiotemporal and cell-type specific dynamics. ORAI1 mainly
CC       contributes to the generation of Ca(2+) plateaus involved in sustained
CC       Ca(2+) entry and is dispensable for cytosolic Ca(2+) oscillations,
CC       whereas ORAI2 and ORAI3 generate oscillatory patterns. CRAC channels
CC       assemble in Ca(2+) signaling microdomains where Ca(2+) influx is
CC       coupled to calmodulin and calcineurin signaling and activation of NFAT
CC       transcription factors recruited to ORAI1 via AKAP5. CRAC channels are
CC       the main pathway for Ca(2+) influx in T cells and promote the immune
CC       response to pathogens by activating NFAT-dependent cytokine and
CC       chemokine transcription. {ECO:0000269|PubMed:16807233,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:17442569, ECO:0000269|PubMed:17452328,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:19182790, ECO:0000269|PubMed:19706554,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:32415068, ECO:0000269|PubMed:33941685}.

CC   -!- ACTIVITY REGULATION: CRAC channels are regulated by fast Ca(2+)-
CC       dependent inactivation (FCDI), a mechanism that limits Ca(2+) influx
CC       and cell toxicity. ORAI2 channels display prominent FCDI
CC       (PubMed:19706554). Inhibited by lanthanides such as Gd(3+) ions
CC       (PubMed:16807233). {ECO:0000269|PubMed:16807233,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:19706554}.
CC   -!- SUBUNIT: Oligomerizes in homomeric and heteromeric ORAI complexes.
CC       Native CRAC channels most likely consist of hexameric ORAI heteromers,
CC       implying that diverse ORAI1, ORAI2 and ORAI3 subunit combinations with
CC       distinct biophysical properties can operate in a cell-type specific way
CC       (PubMed:17442569, PubMed:32415068). Interacts with STIM1; this
CC       regulates channel activity (PubMed:19706554, PubMed:32415068).
CC       Interacts with CRACR2A/EFCAB4B. {ECO:0000269|PubMed:17442569,
