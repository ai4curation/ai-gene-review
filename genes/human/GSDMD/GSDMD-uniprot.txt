CC   -!- FUNCTION: [Gasdermin-D]: Precursor of a pore-forming protein that plays
CC       a key role in host defense against pathogen infection and danger
CC       signals (PubMed:26375003, PubMed:26375259, PubMed:27281216). This form
CC       constitutes the precursor of the pore-forming protein: upon cleavage,
CC       the released N-terminal moiety (Gasdermin-D, N-terminal) binds to
CC       membranes and forms pores, triggering pyroptosis (PubMed:26375003,
CC       PubMed:26375259, PubMed:27281216, PubMed:39673710, PubMed:40118151).
CC       {ECO:0000269|PubMed:26375003, ECO:0000269|PubMed:26375259,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:27281216, ECO:0000269|PubMed:39673710,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:40118151}.
CC   -!- FUNCTION: [Gasdermin-D, N-terminal]: Promotes pyroptosis in response to
CC       microbial infection and danger signals (PubMed:26375003,
CC       PubMed:26375259, PubMed:27418190, PubMed:28392147, PubMed:32820063,
CC       PubMed:34289345, PubMed:38040708, PubMed:38530158, PubMed:38599239).
CC       Produced by the cleavage of gasdermin-D by inflammatory caspases CASP1,
CC       CASP4 or CASP5 in response to canonical, as well as non-canonical (such
CC       as cytosolic LPS) inflammasome activators (PubMed:26375003,
CC       PubMed:26375259, PubMed:27418190). After cleavage, moves to the plasma
CC       membrane where it strongly binds to inner leaflet lipids, including
CC       monophosphorylated phosphatidylinositols, such as phosphatidylinositol
CC       4-phosphate, bisphosphorylated phosphatidylinositols, such as
CC       phosphatidylinositol (4,5)-bisphosphate, as well as
CC       phosphatidylinositol (3,4,5)-bisphosphate, and more weakly to
CC       phosphatidic acid and phosphatidylserine (PubMed:27281216,
CC       PubMed:29898893, PubMed:36227980). Homooligomerizes within the membrane
CC       and forms pores of 10-15 nanometers (nm) of inner diameter, allowing
CC       the release of mature interleukin-1 (IL1B and IL18) and triggering
CC       pyroptosis (PubMed:27281216, PubMed:27418190, PubMed:29898893,
CC       PubMed:33883744, PubMed:38040708, PubMed:38530158, PubMed:38599239).
CC       Gasdermin pores also allow the release of mature caspase-7 (CASP7) (By
CC       similarity). In some, but not all, cells types, pyroptosis is followed
CC       by pyroptotic cell death, which is caused by downstream activation of
CC       ninjurin-1 (NINJ1), which mediates membrane rupture (cytolysis)
CC       (PubMed:33472215, PubMed:37198476). Also forms pores in the
CC       mitochondrial membrane, resulting in release of mitochondrial DNA
CC       (mtDNA) into the cytosol (By similarity). Gasdermin-D, N-terminal
CC       released from pyroptotic cells into the extracellular milieu rapidly
CC       binds to and kills both Gram-negative and Gram-positive bacteria,
CC       without harming neighboring mammalian cells, as it does not disrupt the
CC       plasma membrane from the outside due to lipid-binding specificity
CC       (PubMed:27281216). Under cell culture conditions, also active against
CC       intracellular bacteria, such as Listeria monocytogenes (By similarity).
CC       Also active in response to MAP3K7/TAK1 inactivation by Yersinia toxin
CC       YopJ, which triggers cleavage by CASP8 and subsequent activation (By
CC       similarity). Required for mucosal tissue defense against enteric
CC       pathogens (By similarity). Activation of the non-canonical inflammasome
CC       in brain endothelial cells can lead to excessive pyroptosis, leading to
CC       blood-brain barrier breakdown (By similarity). Strongly binds to
CC       bacterial and mitochondrial lipids, including cardiolipin
CC       (PubMed:27281216). Does not bind to unphosphorylated
CC       phosphatidylinositol, phosphatidylethanolamine nor phosphatidylcholine
CC       (PubMed:27281216). {ECO:0000250|UniProtKB:Q9D8T2,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:26375003, ECO:0000269|PubMed:26375259,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:27281216, ECO:0000269|PubMed:27418190,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:28392147, ECO:0000269|PubMed:29898893,

CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:36227980, ECO:0000269|PubMed:37198476,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:38040708, ECO:0000269|PubMed:38530158,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:38599239, ECO:0000269|PubMed:40097387}.
CC   -!- FUNCTION: [Gasdermin-D, p13]: Transcription coactivator produced by the
CC       cleavage by CASP3 or CASP7 in the upper small intestine in response to
CC       dietary antigens (By similarity). Required to maintain food tolerance
