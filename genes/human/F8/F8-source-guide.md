# F8: cofactor activities, locations and product boundaries

The displayed human FVIII precursor has 2351 residues. Its signal peptide and processed heavy, B and light chains are proteolytic forms, distinct from the naturally spliced 216-residue F8B product. Historical mature-FVIII residue numbering differs from the displayed precursor by the 19-residue signal peptide. Engineered B-domain deletions and furin-site variants must not be equated with natural F8B. The original articles do not establish equivalent function of both natural products.

## VWF association and enzyme activation

FVIII binds its VWF carrier. All three imported binding contexts name VWF (P04275), including PMID:18492805, whose Figure 5/ELISA Methods directly test that association. These rows retain the supported interaction as contextual binding; they do not mean that FVIII transports or stabilizes VWF. The same ADAMTS13 study separately reports enhanced VWF cleavage under shear and a smaller direct enzyme/peptide-substrate effect. Those results are retained with their physiological and construct limits, but do not replace the imported VWF partner by ADAMTS13. The independently tested FVIIIa–FIXa reaction grounds the proposed [endopeptidase activator activity](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/QuickGO/term/GO:0061133) annotation; FIXa remains the protease.

The intrinsic-tenase experiments measure activated FVIII as a cofactor for FIXa-mediated FX cleavage. [Intrinsic coagulation](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/QuickGO/term/GO:0007597) includes this performed step; the term does not require every physiological initiation route to begin with FXII. The genetic inversion study is supporting disease context, not the operative biochemical experiment.

## Human cofactor chemistry and the membrane face

The complete [Hultin 1982 original, PMID:6804496](https://www.jci.org/articles/view/110534) was recovered. Printed pages 950–958 contain the operative purification, reaction, activation-control and platelet/lipid experiments. Actual page images 955–956 were inspected, including Tables II/III and Figures 4/5. The experiments use human FVIII preparations and human FIXa; the bovine-factor-X series and the human-factor-X series are distinct. Human factor X activation was measured through released radiolabeled activation peptide. Activation and contamination controls distinguish the FVIII cofactor from the proteases. The FVIII preparations contain VWF, an explicit author limitation rather than proof of a partner-free molecule. Externally supplied FVIII supports tenase on washed activated human platelets and phospholipid surfaces. This supports peripheral external-face association, not an integral membrane chain or every proposed binding mechanism.

The official supplement was recovered for [PMID:27749002](https://doi.org/10.1111/jth.13543). Its Figures S1/S2 assay descriptions were independently read in the database assessment: human B-domain-deleted or Delta3 FVIII is activated by thrombin, thrombin is quenched by hirudin, and FVIIIa is added to human FIXa/FX and PC/PS vesicles. Chromogenic generated FXa reports the cofactor-supported reaction. The engineered constructs, BHK expression host and natural 216-residue F8B product are not interchangeable. No supplementary figure pixels were inspected in this consultation.

[GO:0009897](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/QuickGO/term/GO:0009897) includes proteins attached to or peripherally associated with the external plasma-membrane face. It is more precise than the seven imported broad membrane contexts. All seven are refined consistently. The Reactome disease events are not interchangeable: R-HSA-9668253 and R-HSA-9670874 alter F9 while their FVIII chains are native; R-HSA-9668365 contains FVIII A2 variants. Refining the positive compartment does not assign wild-type activity to each variant.

This directly supports copper binding, while retaining the preparation and occupancy qualifications. IPR011706 also currently maps to [GO:0016491 oxidoreductase activity](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/QuickGO/term/GO:0016491). That term requires catalytic oxidation-state change. FVIII's coordinated copper and its enhancement of FIXa-mediated proteolysis do not establish such chemistry. The electronic oxidoreductase propagation is removed while the actual domain and copper-binding observations remain. This is a domain-function overgeneralization, not rejection of an unread experimental oxidoreductase paper.
