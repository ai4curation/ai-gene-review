| Feature | Description | Evidence/Citations |
|---|---|---|
| Identity | Human **FRS3** encodes fibroblast growth factor receptor substrate 3, also called **FRS2β** or **SNT2**. It is a nonenzymatic intracellular docking/adaptor protein related to FRS2α. | FRS3 and FRS2 are the two members of the FRS adaptor family and have overlapping but context-dependent activities (pqac-00000027, pqac-00000029) |
| Primary molecular function | FRS3 connects activated receptor tyrosine kinases to cytoplasmic signaling proteins. Receptor-associated phosphorylation converts it into a docking scaffold. It is not an enzyme and therefore has no catalytic substrate specificity. | FRS3 is characterized as an intracellular adaptor linking activated FGFRs to selected downstream pathways (pqac-00000000, pqac-00000001) |
| N-terminal targeting region | An N-terminal myristoylation signal can promote membrane association and proximity to receptor tyrosine kinases. Endogenous brain FRS3 is nevertheless substantially cytosolic, indicating regulated localization. | Comparative biochemical studies describe the shared FRS-family motif and predominantly soluble endogenous FRS3 (pqac-00000012, pqac-00000027) |
| PTB domain | The phosphotyrosine-binding (**PTB**) domain is the principal receptor-docking module. FRS-family PTB domains bind FGFR juxtamembrane regions; exact binding determinants are better established for FRS2 than for human FRS3. | Receptor docking through the FRS PTB domain is supported, but residue-level evidence is predominantly from FRS2 (pqac-00000007, pqac-00000011, pqac-00000014) |
| PH-like fold annotation | The PTB region adopts a pleckstrin-homology-like fold, consistent with the FRS2_PTB, IRS_PTB, and PH-like superfamily annotations. This does not establish a separate canonical PH domain. | FRS proteins are consistently described as PTB-domain-containing adaptors (pqac-00000005, pqac-00000027) |
| C-terminal signaling region | The C-terminal region contains phosphorylatable tyrosines that generate binding sites for SH2-domain signaling proteins. A reliable residue-by-residue FRS3 phosphosite map remains limited. | Phosphorylated FRS3 recruits downstream signaling proteins, although exact site assignments are uncertain (pqac-00000000, pqac-00000014) |
| FGFR interaction | FRS3 acts downstream of fibroblast growth factor receptors. Its contribution varies by receptor, developmental stage, and cell type and frequently overlaps with FRS2. | Genetic studies support context-dependent FRS2/FRS3 mediation of FGFR signaling in telencephalic and oligodendrocyte-lineage cells (pqac-00000001, pqac-00000028, pqac-00000048) |
| Neurotrophin-receptor interaction | FRS3 has been connected to Trk-family signaling, and BDNF stimulation can induce FRS3 phosphorylation. Its necessity in BDNF–TrkB signaling and full mechanism remain incompletely defined. | BDNF-responsive phosphorylation supports pathway participation, but cortical-neuron experiments show that FRS3 does not reproduce all FRS2-mediated responses (pqac-00000022, pqac-00000024, pqac-00000025) |
| GRB2 and SHP2 recruitment | Tyrosine-phosphorylated FRS3 can recruit **GRB2** and **SHP2/PTPN11**, assembling signaling complexes. FRS2-derived site assignments should not be transferred residue-for-residue to FRS3. | Protein-level recruitment is supported, while the detailed FRS3 site map remains uncertain (pqac-00000000, pqac-00000014, pqac-00000027) |
| MAPK/ERK pathway | FRS3 can support Ras–MAPK/ERK signaling. In PC12 cells, FRS3 depletion impaired FGF-induced differentiation and strongly reduced ERK activation during combined EGF/FGF stimulation. | Knockdown evidence connects FRS3 to ERK activation, growth-factor crosstalk, and neuronal differentiation (pqac-00000002, pqac-00000016, pqac-00000030) |
| PI3K–AKT pathway | FRS adaptors can support PI3K–AKT signaling in a context-dependent manner. Combined FRS loss in the embryonic medial ganglionic eminence reduced AKT activation without eliminating ERK activation. | Genetic evidence supports an FRS2/FRS3-dependent AKT branch but does not isolate FRS3's individual contribution (pqac-00000001, pqac-00000028, pqac-00000029) |
| EGF–FGF crosstalk | FRS3 may preserve or reshape FGF signaling when EGF signaling modifies FRS2 activity, suggesting a context-dependent signal-integrating function. | FRS3 knockdown altered ERK activation and differentiation under combined EGF/FGF stimulation (pqac-00000002, pqac-00000016) |
| Plasma-membrane localization | Myristoylated FRS3 can associate with the plasma membrane in expression systems, consistent with receptor-proximal signaling, but endogenous brain FRS3 is less lipid-raft-associated than FRS2. | Fractionation distinguishes lipid-raft-associated FRS2 from predominantly soluble FRS3 (pqac-00000012, pqac-00000027) |
| Cytosolic localization | Endogenous brain FRS3 occurs mainly in the soluble/cytosolic fraction, supporting functions beyond receptor-proximal membrane signaling. | Brain-fractionation evidence indicates predominantly cytosolic localization (pqac-00000012, pqac-00000013) |
| Microtubule association | FRS3 associates with acetylated α-tubulin, βIII-tubulin, Doublecortin, MAP2, and Tau. It may stabilize microtubules directly or recruit stabilizing proteins; these alternatives remain unresolved. | Co-immunoprecipitation, colocalization, and perturbation studies support a microtubule-associated role (pqac-00000040, pqac-00000043, pqac-00000047) |
| Molecular-motor association | FRS3 associates with kinesin heavy and light chains more clearly than with dynein, suggesting a possible adaptor role in anterograde microtubule-based transport. | Protein-association and localization studies connect FRS3 with kinesin machinery (pqac-00000042, pqac-00000047) |
| Synaptic localization | FRS3 occurs in adult mouse-brain postsynaptic-density fractions and associates with synaptic proteins, suggesting functions in cargo transport or synaptic structural organization. Direct human-neuron validation is limited. | Postsynaptic-density enrichment and synaptic-protein interactions distinguish FRS3 from FRS2 (pqac-00000042, pqac-00000052) |
| Tissue expression | FRS3 is nervous-system enriched, whereas FRS2 is more broadly expressed. During embryogenesis, FRS3 is concentrated in ventral neural regions where it overlaps with FRS2. | In situ and protein-expression evidence supports restricted neural and ventral-telencephalic expression (pqac-00000053, pqac-00000055) |
| Cellular expression | FRS3 is detected in migrating and differentiated neurons, embryonic hippocampal and cortical neurons, and mature synaptic compartments. A comprehensive functionally validated human cell-type map is lacking. | Developmental and neuronal-culture evidence supports expression during neuronal migration, differentiation, and maturation (pqac-00000052, pqac-00000054, pqac-00000056) |
| Neural precursor differentiation | FRS3 cooperates with FRS2 in FGFR-dependent differentiation of selected medial ganglionic eminence-derived precursors. FRS2 is generally dominant, with FRS3 providing partial redundancy. | Combined mouse genetics demonstrate overlapping, context-dependent functions in the early telencephalon (pqac-00000001, pqac-00000028, pqac-00000057) |
| Neurite formation | FRS3 depletion increases free tubulin, reduces polymerized tubulin, increases nocodazole sensitivity, and markedly reduces neurite number and length. Comparable FRS2 depletion did not reproduce these effects. | Loss-of-function studies support a specialized role in microtubule stability and neurite extension (pqac-00000040, pqac-00000041, pqac-00000044) |
| Oligodendrocyte lineage | FRS3 is not independently essential for embryonic oligodendrocyte-progenitor specification; FRS2 has the dominant role. Combined loss modestly affects selected ERK signals but does not reproduce major FGFR-loss myelination defects. | Conditional mouse genetics indicate redundancy and signaling through additional FGFR effectors (pqac-00000048, pqac-00000049, pqac-00000051) |
| Human BMI genetics | A 2025 multi-ancestry study of **2,005,180 participants** associated several FRS3 missense variants with BMI. Finnish p.Glu115Lys was associated with **1.09 kg/m² lower BMI per allele**; other alleles had smaller positive or negative effects. | Large-scale human genetics provides recent evidence connecting FRS3 to body-weight regulation (pqac-00000033, pqac-00000034, pqac-00000035, pqac-00000037) |
| Proposed metabolic mechanism | FRS3 is proposed as a downstream component of BDNF–TrkB signaling relevant to appetite, food intake, and energy expenditure. The causal cell type and biochemical effects of the associated variants remain unresolved. | The BMI study advances this model while acknowledging mechanistic uncertainty (pqac-00000033, pqac-00000038, pqac-00000039) |
| Overall biological model | FRS3 appears to have a dual role: a receptor-proximal adaptor in selected FGFR/Trk contexts and a neural, cytosolic microtubule-associated scaffold involved in cytoskeletal stability and transport. The balance between these roles is cell- and stage-dependent. | This model integrates receptor-signaling, localization, developmental-genetic, and cytoskeletal evidence (pqac-00000001, pqac-00000012, pqac-00000040, pqac-00000048) |


*Table: This table consolidates the verified structural features, localization, signaling partners, expression patterns, and biological roles of human FRS3. It distinguishes direct FRS3 evidence from conclusions inferred from the better-characterized paralog FRS2.*
