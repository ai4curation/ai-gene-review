## Evidence synthesis

- LPP is a proline-rich, three-LIM-domain zyxin-family adaptor. The original human cloning paper identified it as the recurrent chromosome-3 partner of HMGIC/HMGA2 in lipoma and described the domain architecture [PMID:8812423, "Nucleotide sequence analysis of a composite cDNA of LPP revealed an open reading frame of 1836 nucleotides encoding a proline-rich protein containing a leucine-zipper motif in its amino-terminal region and three LIM domains in its carboxy-terminal region."].
- Direct localization and interaction work places intact human LPP at focal adhesions and cell-cell contacts and shows VASP binding [PMID:10637295, "LPP localizes in focal adhesions as well as in cell-to-cell contacts, and it binds VASP, a protein implicated in the control of actin organization."]. The three LIM domains cooperate for robust focal-adhesion targeting, whereas the proline-rich region has weaker, separable focal-adhesion/stress-fiber targeting information [PMID:12441356, "All of the LIM domains of LPP cooperate in order to provide robust targeting to focal adhesions, and the linker between LIM domains 1 and 2 plays a pivotal role in this targeting."].
- LPP binds α-actinin directly. The α-actinin interface maps to central spectrin-like repeats 2/3 and the LPP determinant to a conserved N-terminal motif [PMID:12615977, "This site was mapped to the central rod of alpha-actinin, which contains spectrin-like repeats 2 and 3. In the case of LPP, a conserved motif present at the N-terminus was shown to be responsible for the interaction."]. Quantitative hybrid assays found lower LPP than zyxin affinity, so zyxin behavior should not be transferred wholesale to LPP [PMID:12615977, "Quantitative data obtained with the two-hybrid and the three-hybrid system suggest that LPP has a lower affinity for alpha-actinin than zyxin."].
- Palladin is another direct cytoskeletal partner: its N terminus interacts with the first and second LPP LIM domains in vitro and in vivo [PMID:17322171, "The palladin interacting region of LPP was mapped to the first and second LIM domains. The N-terminus of palladin interacted with LPP both in vitro and in vivo, but not solely through its FPLPPP and FPPPP motifs."]. In smooth-muscle systems, both proteins enhance migration and spreading, and their abundance responds to FAK/PRX1, angiotensin II, actin dynamics, matrix rigidity, oxidative stress, and vascular injury [PMID:17322171, "Both LPP and palladin enhanced cell migration and spreading."; PMID:19205907, "In conclusion, expression of LPP and palladin are modulated by a mix of mechanical cues, oxidative stress and substrate composition which translate into their up or down regulation in vessel wall injury and early atherogenesis."].
- Scrib binds the extreme LPP C terminus through its PDZ domains [PMID:15649318, "The binding between Scrib and LPP is mediated by the PDZ domains of Scrib and the carboxy-terminus of LPP."]. This interaction does not itself target either protein to cell-cell contacts, and Scrib is dispensable for LPP focal-adhesion targeting in the tested MDCKII/CV-1 systems [PMID:15649318, "Furthermore, our investigations indicate that Scrib is dispensable for targeting LPP to focal adhesions and to cell-cell contacts, and that LPP is not necessary for localizing Scrib in cell-cell contacts."].
- LPP undergoes nucleo-cytoplasmic shuttling: CRM1 inhibition traps it in nuclei, and export depends on an N-terminal leucine-rich signal [PMID:10637295, "The nuclear export of LPP depends on an N-terminally located leucine-rich sequence that shares sequence homology with well-defined nuclear export signals."]. GAL4 reporter transactivation demonstrates latent transcriptional activation capacity, but does not identify an endogenous transcriptional target or establish that intact LPP is a sequence-specific transcription factor [PMID:10637295, "Moreover, LPP displays transcriptional activation capacity, as measured by GAL4-based assays."].
- Mechanotransduction evidence is context-dependent. Human vascular smooth-muscle cells on denatured collagen or rigid substrate up-regulate LPP/palladin, pFAK and stress fibers [PMID:19205907, "SMCs cultured on denatured collagen or on a rigid substrate, up regulated expression of LPP, its partner palladin, tenascin C (TN-C), phosphorylated focal adhesion kinase (pFAK) and exhibited robust stress fibers."]. A mouse knockout study found stretch-dependent movement of LPP toward stress fibers and nuclei and an attenuated pressure-induced myogenic response in mesenteric arteries [PMID:37759537, "In response to stretching, LPP redistributed towards stress fibres and the nucleus."; PMID:37759537, "In contrast, the myogenic response of LPP-KO MA segments was significantly attenuated while zyxin-deficient MA segments displayed a normal myogenic response."]. These mouse vascular findings support a mechanosensory role but are not direct human physiological evidence.
- In an ErbB2/TGFβ breast-cancer context, LPP moves to focal adhesions and is needed for rapid adhesion turnover [PMID:23447672, "We show that LPP re-localizes to focal adhesion complexes upon TGFβ stimulation and is a critical determinant in TGFβ-mediated focal adhesion turnover."]. The migration/invasion phenotype requires LPP–α-actinin binding [PMID:23447672, "Taken together, these data demonstrate that the ability of LPP to promote TGFβ-induced migration and invasion requires its interaction with α-Actinin at focal adhesions."]. This is strong context-specific mechanism, not evidence that invasion is a constitutive core function in normal tissue.
- Fusion-oncogene biology must remain separate from intact LPP function. Human lipomas express HMGA2/HMGIC-LPP products retaining two or three LPP LIM domains [PMID:8812423, "Two alternative HMGIC/LPP hybrid transcripts have been detected; the difference between them is mainly the presence of either two or three LIM domains in the predicted HMGI-C/LPP fusion proteins."]. A secondary AML case instead produced MLL/KMT2A-LPP and reciprocal LPP-MLL transcripts [PMID:11433529, "The predicted MLL-LPP fusion protein includes the A/T hook motifs and methyltransferase domain of MLL joined to the two last LIM domains of LPP."]. These chimeric, mainly nuclear proteins cannot establish normal LPP molecular function.

## Expression, transcript, isoform, and species boundaries

- The original human study detected an LPP transcript broadly but not in brain or peripheral blood leukocytes [PMID:8812423, "In Northern blot analysis, an mRNA of over 10 kb was detected by these ectopic sequences in a variety of human tissues but not in brain and peripheral blood leukocytes."]. This older assay should not be read as absolute absence in every brain or immune cell state.
- An intron-2 promoter directs smooth-muscle-enriched transcription and is SRF/myocardin dependent [PMID:18511849, "Quantitative RT-PCR shows that this alternative promoter directs transcription specifically to smooth muscle containing tissues in vivo."]. This supports promoter/transcript regulation; the accessible evidence does not establish a distinct protein isoform with a different biochemical function.
- Reviewed UniProt does not curate alternative normal protein isoforms for Q93052. The “alternative transcripts” in PMID:8812423 are tumor-derived HMGA2-LPP fusion transcripts, not native LPP isoforms.
- PMID:15649318 mixes human LPP constructs with mouse Scrib screening and MDCKII/CV-1 cell localization. PMID:19205907 combines cultured human vascular smooth-muscle experiments with mouse injury/atherosclerosis models. PMID:23447672 includes human and mouse breast-cancer systems. PMID:37759537 is a mouse knockout study. Species and heterologous-cell boundaries must be retained in synthesis.

## Curation implications

- Best-supported normal functions are adhesion-site adaptor/scaffold roles involving VASP, α-actinin, palladin, and Scrib, with focal-adhesion/stress-fiber organization and regulated nucleo-cytoplasmic trafficking.
- Avoid a generic “protein binding” core statement when partner-specific binding or adaptor/scaffold language is possible.
- Treat transcriptional activation as reporter-based potential unless a direct endogenous target and mechanism are cited.
- Treat migration, invasion, vascular injury, and myogenic phenotypes as downstream/contextual roles rather than the universal molecular function of LPP.
