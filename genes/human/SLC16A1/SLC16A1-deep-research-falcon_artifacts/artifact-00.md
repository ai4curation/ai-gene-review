| Proton-coupled L-lactate transport | Geistlinger et al., 2023, https://doi.org/10.1093/pnasnexus/pgad007 | Recombinant human MCT1 had lactate Km 2.32±0.36 mM pH 6.8. (pqac-00000013, pqac-00000015) | Kinetics were measured in engineered yeast; the complete proton-transfer sequence is a data-supported mechanistic model rather than direct visualization of every transfer step. |
| Plasma-membrane trafficking by CD147/basigin | Seka et al., 2024, https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jbc.2024.107333 | In human-cell expression experiments, MCT1 required CD147 for efficient plasma-membrane localization. Co-expression with CD147 stabilized human MCT1 by approximately +7 °C; G19C and W20A impaired complex stability, surface localization and substrate flux. (pqac-00000025, pqac-00000026, pqac-00000027) | Overexpression and mutagenesis establish trafficking requirements but do not quantify endogenous tissue-specific complex abundance. Embigin/GP70 can substitute in some cellular contexts. |
| Ketone-body delivery and inherited MCT1 deficiency | van Hasselt et al., 2014, https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1407778 | Among 96 patients investigated for unexplained ketoacidosis, the study identified 9 patients—3 biallelic and 6 monoallelic—with damaging SLC16A1 variants. Protein abundance and erythrocyte lactate transport were lowest in biallelic cases, supporting dosage-sensitive MCT1 deficiency and impaired extrahepatic ketone delivery. (pqac-00000033, pqac-00000035, pqac-00000042, pqac-00000043) | This rare-disease cohort was small, and the functional assay measured erythrocyte lactate efflux rather than ketone transport directly in every affected tissue. |
| Pharmacological MCT1 inhibition in cancer | Halford et al., 2023, https://doi.org/10.1158/1078-0432.CCR-22-2263; trial NCT01791595 | In phase-I dose escalation, 40 patients received AZD3965. Seven patients at ≥20 mg/day had dose-limiting toxicities; among 39 patients with baseline assessment, 9/39 had stable disease and no objective response was reported. The recommended phase-II dose was 10 mg twice daily. (pqac-00000036, pqac-00000045, pqac-00000058) | Non-randomized dose escalation was designed primarily for safety, not efficacy. Ocular, cardiac and acid–base effects were consistent with on-target inhibition; MCT4 co-expression may confer resistance. |
| Skeletal-muscle lactate flux and metabolic adaptation | Zhang et al., 2024, https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.adn4508 | In muscle-specific Slc16a1-knockout mice, loss of MCT1 altered circulating-to-muscle lactate contribution, shifted fibers toward oxidative types and increased running endurance. Genetic loss or AZD3965 treatment increased mitochondrial proteins, respiration and PGC-1α stability; in C2C12 cells, PGC-1α half-life increased from 10.6 to 19.6 hours. (pqac-00000020, pqac-00000021, pqac-00000050, pqac-00000055) | These are mouse and murine-cell results, not human intervention data. Because MCT1 is bidirectional, the adaptive phenotype should not be attributed exclusively to impaired lactate uptake or efflux. |
| Intestinal lactate and short-chain-fatty-acid transport | Wang et al., 2024, https://doi.org/10.1093/lifemeta/load041 | Intestinal-epithelial Slc16a1 deletion in male mice reduced intestinal interstitial and portal-vein lactate and reduced portal butyrate and pentanoate after substrate challenge; glucose tolerance, inflammation and microbiota changed in a sex-dependent manner. (pqac-00000023, pqac-00000057) | This was a conditional mouse study. Strong male–female divergence cautions against direct extrapolation to human intestinal physiology or treatment. |
| Proposed cardiac mitochondrial lactate import | Cluntun et al., 2024 bioRxiv, https://doi.org/10.1101/2024.10.07.617073 | MCT1 was detected in purified human cardiac mitochondrial preparations; mouse cardiomyocyte localization, protease protection, knockout and isotope-tracing experiments supported a proposed inner-mitochondrial-membrane role in lactate oxidation. (pqac-00000056, pqac-00000059) | Preliminary, non-peer-reviewed evidence; mitochondrial MCT1 localization and mitochondrial lactate oxidation remain controversial and should not replace the established plasma-membrane annotation. |
