# FBN2 evidence and product scope

Fibrillin-2 has three retained core molecular activities: extracellular matrix structure, calcium binding in its EGF-like domain architecture, and hormone activity of the released C-terminal product placensin. Matrix-chain and hormone functions are distinct. The two original splice records remain unchanged; the shorter 1,473-aa product lacks the mapped placensin interval.

## Matrix organization and regulatory context

Human FBN2 microfibril and coassembly evidence supports a broad structural role, including the scaffold for early elastic-fiber assembly and nonelastic matrix organization. UniProt annotates early assembly with ortholog evidence, while PMID8120105 provides target sequence and preferential elastic-matrix localization. The calcium-binding core now cites the target primary-sequence analysis in PMID8120105 alongside the mapping reference. The exact UniProt calcium-binding EGF-like domain features and their evidence are located in the direct source guide; no calcium signaling or measured affinity at every repeat is asserted. In mouse osteoblasts, Fbn2-dependent matrix sequestration of latent TGF-β complexes limits signaling. This provides a scaffold mechanism for the retained regulatory process without assigning receptor catalysis or making fibrillin-2 an obligatory mineralization substrate. Bone and eye outcomes remain contextual non-core biology.

The generic basement-membrane location is supported by actual human renal GBM and Descemet/anterior lens-capsule evidence. The separate Bruch membrane study places FBN2 particularly in its central elastic layer; that measurement is not relabeled as RPE or choriocapillaris basal-lamina incorporation. Original source and tissue limits remain visible in each complete reference assessment.

## Placensin and processing limits

The two applied classes use reviewed UniProt chain mappings: matrix fibrillin-2 at 78–2779 and placensin at 2780–2912. These curated coordinates are not newly measured endogenous termini. The placensin paper's Methods use 2780–2912, whereas its Figure 2 caption says 2779–2912; both statements are preserved. Human trophoblast secretion and serum IP-MS support the released product, while recombinant human ligand was tested in mouse metabolic systems and human trophoblast cells. Neither an endogenous human glucose intervention nor hormone output from the truncated splice product is inferred.

Placensin positively regulates gluconeogenesis in the tested mouse hepatocyte systems; those cells execute the glucose-conversion reactions. Figure 3 and the hepatocyte Methods in PMID:32329225 report glucose output in glucose-free lactate/pyruvate medium with gluconeogenic transcript induction. This supports GO:0045722 without claiming tracer-resolved flux or a human metabolic intervention. FBN1/asprosin's raw glucose-metabolism annotation is recognized, and its reviewed record likewise uses a regulatory-role correction. A shared cAMP/PKA response does not establish a placensin GPCR class. UniProt explicitly assigns linked secretion and subsequent furin separation with ECO:0000269/PMID32329225. The processing model and positive product-release evidence are retained. Its original site prediction, colocalization and internal peptides after proteolytic digestion leave the precise intact endogenous terminus and quantitative furin dependence unresolved. The proposed experiment addresses this bounded biological gap with intact-product mapping and controlled furin perturbation. No processing activity is assigned to the FBN2 substrate.
