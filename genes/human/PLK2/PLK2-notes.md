# PLK2 manual review notes

PLK2 is an active polo-like serine/threonine kinase with a core centrosomal
role in centriole duplication and a separate neuronal substrate branch.

Key evidence reviewed:

- PMID:15242618 supports PLK2 as a physiological centrosomal protein whose
  kinase activity is required for centriole duplication near G1/S.
- PMID:19001868 shows that PLK2 localization to centrioles and an intact
  polo-box domain are required for PLK2-mediated centriole duplication; the
  abnormal spindle phenotype after PLK2 knockdown is interpreted as downstream
  of defective centriole reproduction.
- PMID:20352051 and PMID:22349705 support PLK2 phosphorylation of NPM1/B23 and
  CENPJ/CPAP as centriole-duplication substrates.
- The reviewed neuronal rows support PLK2 phosphorylation of synaptic and
  alpha-synuclein substrates and non-core regulation of synaptic homeostasis,
  dendritic-spine remodeling and selective autophagic clearance of
  alpha-synuclein.

PAINT review:

- PTN008613833 is the PLK2 branch. Its centriole IBD is supported for human
  PLK2, but its G1/S-transition IBD overstates the human seed: PLK2 is
  activated near G1/S to promote centriole duplication rather than to execute
  the G1-to-S transition itself.
- The PTN000679870 kinetochore, spindle-pole and mitotic-spindle-organization
  IBDs reflect active PLK1/CDC5/Plo1-like mitotic biology. They over-transfer
  to PLK2, whose best-supported cell-cycle role is procentriole formation at
  centrosomes rather than kinetochore, spindle-pole or mitotic-spindle control.
