Gene Description

ADO (2-aminoethanethiol dioxygenase, also called cysteamine dioxygenase; EC 1.13.11.19) is a cytosolic non-heme mononuclear Fe(II) thiol dioxygenase of the cupin superfamily and one of only two mammalian thiol dioxygenases (the other being cysteine dioxygenase, CDO1). It has two distinct, well-established catalytic activities that act on chemically different substrate classes. First, as a cysteamine dioxygenase it oxidizes the sulfur of the small molecule cysteamine (2-aminoethanethiol) to hypotaurine using molecular oxygen, providing a second route of hypotaurine and taurine biosynthesis that complements the cysteine-sulfinate route through CDO1 and CSAD. Second, ADO is the animal N-terminal cysteine dioxygenase of the Cys/N-degron pathway: it oxidizes the thiol of N-terminal cysteine residues of substrate proteins (e.g. RGS4, RGS5 and interleukin-32) to cysteine-sulfinic acid, priming them for arginylation by ATE1 and subsequent ubiquitin-mediated degradation. Because its oxygen affinity is low (a high apparent KmO2, above the physiological range), this protein-modifying activity couples substrate stability to oxygen availability, making ADO an enzymatic cellular oxygen sensor analogous to the plant cysteine oxidases. The active site is a 3-His-ligated ferrous iron center in a jelly-roll beta-barrel; ADO can autocatalytically form a Cys220-Tyr222 thioether cross-linked cofactor that amplifies catalysis.

Proposed New Ontology Terms

oxygen-dependent protein N-terminal cysteine dioxygenase activity

Definition: Catalysis of the reaction: N-terminal L-cysteinyl-[protein] + O2 = N-terminal S-hydroxy-S-oxy-L-cysteinyl-[protein] (N-terminal cysteine-sulfinic acid) + H(+). This oxygen-dependent dioxygenation of a protein N-terminal cysteine thiol primes the substrate for the Cys/N-degron pathway. Distinct from cysteamine dioxygenase activity (GO:0047800), which acts on the free small molecule cysteamine, and from cysteine dioxygenase activity (GO:0017172), which acts on free L-cysteine.

Justification: ADO (and plant cysteine oxidases) catalyze dioxygenation of the N-terminal cysteine of protein substrates (RGS4, RGS5, IL-32) as the committed enzymatic step of the Cys/N-degron oxygen-sensing pathway. This is a distinct molecular function from the small-molecule cysteamine dioxygenase activity, corresponds to a defined Rhea reaction (RHEA:70895) already used by UniProt, but has no dedicated GO MF term. A specific term is needed so that ADO's second, physiologically central catalytic activity can be annotated precisely rather than approximated by GO:0047800 plus process terms.

Parent term: oxidoreductase activity, acting on single donors with incorporation of molecular oxygen, incorporation of two atoms of oxygen

Supporting Evidence:

  • PMID:31273118 : Thus, human ADO catalyzes the dioxygenation of N-Cys residues in RGS4 and RGS5 to cysteine sulfinic acid.

ADO (2-aminoethanethiol / cysteamine dioxygenase) — curation notes

Overview / core biology

ADO is a cytosolic non-heme mononuclear Fe(II) thiol dioxygenase of the cupin
superfamily. It is one of only two mammalian thiol dioxygenases (the other is
cysteine dioxygenase, CDO1), and is unusual in having two well-established
activities acting on chemically distinct substrate classes:

  1. Cysteamine (2-aminoethanethiol) dioxygenase — oxidizes the sulfur of the
    small molecule cysteamine to hypotaurine (a sulfinic acid), the second route of
    hypotaurine/taurine biosynthesis complementing the CDO1/CSAD cysteinesulfinate
    route. This was the founding assigned activity.
    [file:human/ADO/ADO-uniprot.txt "Catalyzes the oxidation of cysteamine (2-aminoethanethiol) to hypotaurine"]
    PMID:17581819

  2. N-terminal cysteine dioxygenase of the Cys/N-degron (oxygen-sensing) pathway
    — oxidizes the thiol of N-terminal cysteine residues of substrate proteins to
    Cys-sulfinic acid, priming them for arginylation (by ATE1) and downstream
    ubiquitin-mediated degradation. This makes ADO an enzymatic cellular O2 sensor.
    Verified substrates: RGS4, RGS5, IL-32 (and, in reporter assays, the plant
    ERF-VII RAP2.12 N-terminus).
    PMID:31273118
    PMID:31273118

Both activities use O2 as a co-substrate and incorporate both oxygen atoms
(true dioxygenase). ADO has a high KmO2 (>500 uM for peptide substrates),
significantly above the physiological range, which underpins its oxygen-sensing role.

Missing GO term (proposed)

GO currently has no dedicated molecular-function term for "oxygen-dependent
protein/peptidyl N-terminal cysteine dioxygenase activity" (the RHEA:70895 reaction:
N-terminal L-cysteinyl-[protein] + O2 = N-terminal S-hydroxy-S-oxy-L-cysteinyl-[protein]).
The nearest existing terms are GO:0047800 (cysteamine dioxygenase activity, the
small-molecule reaction), GO:0017172 (cysteine dioxygenase activity, the CDO1
reaction), and the BP term GO:0018171 (peptidyl-cysteine oxidation). This second
core catalytic activity of ADO is therefore proposed as a new MF term and, in the
interim, modeled through the process terms GO:0018171 (peptidyl-cysteine oxidation)
and GO:0031648 (protein destabilization) plus GO:0071456 (cellular response to hypoxia).
