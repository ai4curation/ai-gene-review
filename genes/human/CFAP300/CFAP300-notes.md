## Primary findings and assay limits

**PMID:29727692.** The genuine UCL accepted manuscript was read at the localization/fractionation, RNAi and IFT Methods/Results and Figures 3–5 descriptions. Patient nasal-cell TEM and dynein-marker imaging show combined outer/inner-arm defects. The model experiments express each species' own tagged ortholog: Paramecium C-terminal GFP and Chlamydomonas FBB5 with a C-terminal triple-HA tag. Approximately 98% is cytoplasmic, with a small ciliary pool released into the detergent-soluble membrane/matrix fraction. IFT perturbation and regeneration experiments establish the distribution of CFAP300 itself; the paper calls that distribution [PMID:29727692, "IFT dependent"]. They do not show a CFAP300 molecule carrying a dynein complex. IFT46 N-terminal deletion reduces ODA16/ODA abundance without reducing FBB5. The ortholog data are not direct human localization experiments.

**PMID:29727693.** The actual publisher article-plus-supplement and Figure S12/Table S2 were inspected. A DNAAF2 yeast-two-hybrid signal is corroborated by recovery of DNAAF2-3xFlag with C11ORF70-HA. PIH1D3 and IFT46 yeast candidates were excluded because of autoactivation and were not recovered in the co-IP. The selected assay descriptions state sequence confirmation against RefSeq but do not identify the exact accession/version or co-IP host. These results support an accessory role in assembly machinery while leaving the performed molecular step unresolved. The authors themselves describe a [PMID:29727693, "preassembly factor"] interpretation and also leave a possible delivery role open. The preserved GOA IPI cites the neighboring PMID:29727692; its precise historical linkage remains unresolved. Keeping the scientifically supported binding assertion uses independent PMID:29727693 evidence without changing that original field.

**PMID:36246608.** Full Methods, Results and Discussion, and the actual Figure 4 image, were read. Endogenous antibody staining in normal human nasal epithelial cells supports a predominantly cytoplasmic distribution and puncta along motile cilia, with substantially depleted staining in patient cells. The antibody epitope spans residues 113–188; the paper discusses possible residual signal from a shorter transcript. Airyscan images are endpoint localization data, not trajectories or a dynein-cargo binding assay. The Discussion describes a [PMID:36246608, "potential role in transport"]. Basal-foot disorientation could be secondary to immotility, so it does not establish a new polarity function.

**PMID:30916986.** The exact-main cache remains abstract-only, but author-uploaded indexed full Methods, Results and Discussion were accessible. Patient ODA/IDA markers accumulate abnormally near the apical cell region, with DNALI1 and DNAH5 differing relative to centrin. This motivates a trafficking model but does not assay whether the retained material is a preassembled complex. Planarian RNAi addresses an ortholog, not human CFAP300. The supplemental methods and figure pixels were not independently recovered. No transport-process proposal is based on these endpoints.

## Curation decisions and process gate

Both cytoplasm assertions, the axoneme assertion and the motile-cilium assertion remain ACCEPT, supported by actual localization rather than disease necessity alone. The axoneme IEA source is curator-backed and human Airyscan microscopy detects a small CFAP300 pool along the ciliary axoneme; this minor ciliary pool does not make axonemal localization the protein's core activity, but it is enough to keep the existing cellular-component assertion without duplicating the accepted motile-cilium row.

The generic binding assertion remains KEEP_AS_NON_CORE under the [pinned ClinGen instruction](https://github.com/ai4curation/ai-gene-review/blob/3fbffb26407414459e21ed806ebfa28885e8d809/projects/CLINGEN_MENDELIAN.md#L2976). DNAAF2 association is positive, but no more specific chaperone, motor, dynein-binding or cargo-adaptor MF is established. The current IntAct whole-target query returned six unrelated-source records and no exact 2018 pair; this does not negate the primary Figure S12 experiment.

Retain the two prior authored assembly proposals, GO:0036158 and GO:0036159. CFAP300's inferred participation as an accessory assembly factor rests on the combination of selective conserved arm defects and association with DNAAF2, not on necessity alone or on a demand for purified catalysis. The precise preassembly, stabilization, delivery or incorporation step remains open. Live same-role comparator queries show human DNAAF1, DNAAF2 and DNAAF3 carry dynein-assembly annotations, with DNAAF1 carrying both specific child processes. These comparators support the applicability of assembly processes to accessory factors; they do not prove an identical mechanism. The fixed-main GO-CAM index has no CFAP300/accession match. Current typed parents show that both child terms descend from GO:0070286. Withdraw only that redundant prior authored parent proposal, preserving its full old object in the audit checkpoint and its immutable source URL/hash in the evidence record. This is not a claim that the broader biological description is false.

The assembly-centred core deliberately leaves the molecular activity unresolved. The structured knowledge gap makes the same distinction. The proposed MF term is therefore mechanism-neutral: it requests an activity term for DNAAF-like cytoplasmic preassembly factors without asserting that CFAP300 is specifically a scaffold, co-chaperone or transport adaptor.
