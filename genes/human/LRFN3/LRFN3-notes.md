# LRFN3 / SALM4 literature notes

## Identity and family boundaries

LRFN3 is SALM4. The family mapping used throughout this review is
SALM1/LRFN2, SALM2/LRFN1, SALM3/LRFN4, SALM4/LRFN3, and SALM5/LRFN5
[PMID:27480238 "Synaptic cell adhesion-like molecules (SALMs; also known as Lrfns) represent a family of leucine-rich repeat (LRR)-containing synaptic cell adhesion molecules, with five known members: SALM1/Lrfn2, SALM2/Lrfn1, SALM3/Lrfn4, SALM4/Lrfn3 and SALM5/Lrfn5 (refs 12, 13, 14, 15)."]

SALM4 is a single-pass membrane adhesion-like protein with extracellular LRR,
Ig, and FNIII domains. It lacks the C-terminal PDZ-binding element found in
SALM1-SALM3 [PMID:16495444 "The family members, SALM1-SALM4, have a single transmembrane (TM) domain and contain extracellular leucine-rich repeats, an Ig C2 type domain, a fibronectin type III domain, and an intracellular postsynaptic density-95 (PSD-95)/Discs large/zona occludens-1 (PDZ) binding domain, which is present on all members except SALM4."]. Therefore, PSD-95 recruitment and direct NMDAR association shown for SALM1 in this founding paper cannot be assigned to LRFN3.

A decisive paralog boundary is that SALM3 and SALM5 directly induce
presynaptic differentiation through LAR-family receptor phosphatases, whereas
SALM4 does not [PMID:27480238 "SALM3 and SALM5 (but not the other SALMs) have synaptogenic activities, promoting both excitatory and inhibitory presynaptic differentiation in contacting axons18 via trans-synaptic interactions with presynaptic LAR family receptor tyrosine phosphatases19."]. SALM4's key synaptic role is inhibitory regulation of these positive organizers.

## Homophilic adhesion and localization

In rat brain, SALM1-SALM3 strongly co-immunoprecipitated, whereas SALM4 and
SALM5 did not, leading the authors to infer that SALM4 and SALM5 mainly form
homomeric complexes in brain [PMID:18227064 "In brain, we found that SALMs 1-3 strongly co-immunoprecipitated with each other, whereas SALMs 4 and 5 did not, suggesting that SALMs 4 and 5 mainly form homomeric complexes."]. Heterologous-cell assays showed a distinct trans-adhesion property: SALM4 and SALM5 formed homophilic but not heterophilic associations, and the SALM4 extracellular N terminus was necessary for this behavior [PMID:18227064 "Both SALMs 4 and 5 formed homophilic, but not heterophilic associations, whereas no trans associations were formed by the other SALMs. The ability of SALM4 to form trans interactions is due to its extracellular N terminus because chimeras of SALM4 N terminus and SALM2 C terminus can form trans interactions, whereas chimeras of SALM2 N terminus and SALM4 C terminus cannot."].

In HeLa-neuron coculture, SALM4 accumulated at contact sites with both axons and
dendrites [PMID:18227064 "Co-culture experiments using HeLa cells and rat hippocampal neurons expressing the SALMs showed that SALM4 is recruited to points of contact between the cells. In neurons, these points of contact were seen in both axons and dendrites."]. In biochemical fractionation, SALM4 was detected in crude synaptosomes, synaptic membranes, and postsynaptic-density fractions [PMID:27480238 "SALM4 proteins were detected in synaptic brain fractions, including crude synaptosomes, synaptic membranes and PSD fractions (Fig. 1e,f and Supplementary Fig. 1e,f), consistent with the previously reported ultrastructural localization of SALM4 proteins around cell junctions, including neuronal synapses20."]. These results support synaptic and pre-/postsynaptic membrane localizations, but they do not turn SALM4 into a PSD-95-binding protein.

## Negative regulation of excitatory synapse development

The principal gene-specific mechanism comes from Salm4-null mice and mixed-cell
assays. SALM4 cis-interacts with SALM3, reduces the trans-synaptic interaction of
SALM3 with presynaptic LAR-family phosphatases, and suppresses SALM3-dependent
presynaptic differentiation [PMID:27480238 "SALM4 cis-interacts with SALM3, inhibits trans-synaptic SALM3 interaction with presynaptic LAR family receptor tyrosine phosphatases and suppresses SALM3-dependent presynaptic differentiation."].

Loss of Salm4 increased both excitatory and inhibitory synapse numbers in the
mouse hippocampus [PMID:27480238 "These results suggest that SALM4 deletion increases the number of excitatory and inhibitory synapses in the hippocampus."]. Re-expression of SALM4 in Salm4-null CA1 pyramidal neurons restored the elevated mEPSC frequency to the normal range [PMID:27480238 "We found that SALM4 re-expression, confirmed by immunoblot analysis (Fig. 3e and Supplementary Figs 1i and 3b), rescued the increased mEPSC frequency in Salm4−/−neurons to a normal range without affecting the amplitude (Fig. 3f)."]. Genetic removal of Salm3 also normalized the excess excitatory synapse phenotype in Salm4-null mice [PMID:27480238 "Importantly, deletion of Salm3 in Salm4(-/-) mice (Salm3(-/-); Salm4(-/-)) normalizes the increased excitatory synapse number."]. Together, these experiments strongly support negative regulation of presynapse assembly and excitatory synapse development as a conserved SALM4 core role.

The homophilic trans-adhesion property is real, but the knockout rescue argues
that postsynaptic cis inhibition—not SALM4 homophilic adhesion—is the key
mechanism for suppressing excitatory synapse number. The authors state that
postsynaptic SALM4 re-expression was sufficient to rescue the elevated mEPSC
frequency [PMID:27480238 "This homophilic adhesion, however, is unlikely to contribute to the SALM4-dependent negative regulation of SALM3 because we herein showed that HSV-mediated re-expression of SALM4 in Salm4−/− CA1 pyramidal neurons (postsynaptic side) was sufficient to rescue the increased mEPSC frequency in a cell-autonomous manner."].

## NMDAR signaling and behavior

SALM4 also restrains synaptic NMDAR function in vivo. Lrfn3-null mice showed
increased NMDAR-mediated, but not AMPAR-mediated, hippocampal transmission
involving GluN2B-containing receptors and presynaptic PTPsigma [PMID:34588597 "In the present study, we found that SALM4-knockout (KO) (Lrfn3−/−) mice displayed abnormally increased NMDAR-mediated, but not AMPAR-mediated, synaptic transmission involving the NMDAR GluN2B subunit and the presynaptic receptor tyrosine phosphatase PTPσ."]. The authors concluded that SALM4 suppresses excessive GluN2B-NMDAR function and contextual fear-memory consolidation, not acquisition [PMID:34588597 "These results suggest that SALM4 suppresses excessive GluN2B-NMDAR (not AMPAR) function and fear memory consolidation (not acquisition)."]. This is functional regulation, not evidence that LRFN3 directly binds NMDARs.

## Neurite outgrowth and flotillin-1

Cultured hippocampal-neuron experiments distributed tagged SALMs across axons,
dendrites, and growth cones and found that overexpression of every family member
enhanced neurite outgrowth with distinct phenotypes [PMID:18585462 "Expression of epitope-tagged proteins in cultured hippocampal neurons showed that SALMs are distributed throughout neurons, including axons, dendrites, and growth cones. Over-expression of each SALM resulted in enhanced neurite outgrowth, but with different phenotypes."]. SALM2/SALM4 chimera experiments implicated the extracellular N terminus in outgrowth [PMID:18585462 "In addition, by using a chimera of SALMs 2 and 4, we found that the N-terminus is also involved in neurite outgrowth."].

Flotillin-1 is required for SALM4-induced neurite branching: flotillin-1 siRNA
blocked branching when SALM4 was overexpressed [PMID:20600927 "When each of SALMs 1-5 was overexpressed, siRNA knockdown of flot-1 prevented neurite branching by SALM4."]. The flotillin pathway depends on lipid rafts, SoHo proteins, actin regulation, and the exocyst [PMID:20600927 "We show that induction of neurite outgrowth by flot-1 depends on amino acids 134-151 as well as lipid raft microdomains, SoHo proteins to regulate the actin cytoskeleton, and the exocyst complex to deliver new membrane proteins to growing neurites."]. This supports the Reactome SALM4-flotillin event but remains rodent-neuron evidence transferred to human by orthology.

## Reticulon-3 interaction

The RTN3 evidence is family-level and weaker functionally. The common SALM LRR
region mediated interaction in yeast two-hybrid assays, and a 19-kDa RTN3C band
bound SALM1-SALM4 in brain immunoprecipitations [PMID:19681166 "This interaction was shown to occur through the LRR domain, which is found on all SALMs. To determine whether this relationship also occurs in brain, we performed immunoprecipitation using antibodies to SALMs 1-4. A 19-kDa band, identified as reticulon 3C, bound to all four SALMs, whereas a 90-kDa band, which did not comigrate with any known reticulon 3 variant, bound to SALMs 2 and 3."]. The proposed trafficking consequence was explicitly tentative [PMID:19681166 "These results show that reticulon 3 may play a role in the trafficking of the SALM family of adhesion molecules."]. It should not be promoted to a core LRFN3 function without further evidence.

## Direct human endothelial evidence

The clearest direct human perturbation evidence is outside the nervous system.
In HUVECs, SALM4 knockdown selectively reduced VEGF-A-induced VEGFR2 Tyr1175
phosphorylation and downstream signaling while leaving Tyr951 and Tyr1214
phosphorylation unchanged [PMID:31170000 "SALM4 silencing in HUVECs and Salm4 knockout (KO) in lung endothelial cells (ECs) of Salm4-/- mice suppressed phosphorylation of VEGFR2 tyrosine (Y) 1175 (Y1173 in mice) and downstream signaling upon VEGF-A stimulation. However, VEGFR2 phosphorylation at Y951 (Y949 in mice) and Y1214 (Y1212 in mice) remained unchanged."]. SALM4 depletion also reduced endothelial leakage, sprouting, and migration [PMID:31170000 "Knockdown and KO of SALM4 inhibited VEGF-A-induced angiogenic functions of ECs. SALM4 depletion reduced endothelial leakage, sprouting, and migratory activities."]. The paper did not identify a direct SALM4-VEGFR2 interaction or the intervening mediator, so this vascular signaling role is credible but mechanistically incomplete and secondary to the synaptic core.

## Curation synthesis and open questions

- Strongest conserved core: synaptic membrane adhesion/regulation, with SALM4 acting in cis to inhibit SALM3-LAR-dependent presynaptic differentiation and thereby negatively regulate excitatory synapse development.
- Separate direct activity: SALM4 homophilic trans-cellular adhesion through its extracellular N terminus.
- Supported associated processes: flotillin-1-dependent neurite branching and negative regulation of GluN2B-NMDAR responses.
- Important exclusions: do not transfer SALM1/SALM2 direct NMDAR/AMPAR binding or PSD-95-dependent postsynaptic organizing functions; do not assign SALM3/SALM5 direct presynapse-inducing activity to SALM4.
- Direct human evidence is currently endothelial rather than neuronal. Human-neuron perturbation studies are needed to test conservation of the SALM4-SALM3-LAR mechanism.
