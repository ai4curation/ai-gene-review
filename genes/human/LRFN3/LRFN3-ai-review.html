Gene Description

LRFN3, also known as SALM4, is a type-I single-pass cell-surface adhesion protein with extracellular leucine-rich-repeat, immunoglobulin-like, and fibronectin type III domains and a short cytoplasmic tail that lacks the C-terminal PDZ-binding motif found in SALM1-SALM3. At neuronal synapses, SALM4 forms homophilic trans-cellular adhesions and also acts postsynaptically in cis with SALM3 to limit SALM3 engagement of presynaptic LAR-family receptor phosphatases, thereby restraining presynapse and excitatory-synapse development. Rodent studies additionally link SALM4 to neurite branching and suppression of excessive GluN2B-containing NMDA-receptor signaling, without demonstrating direct SALM4 binding to NMDA receptors. In primary human endothelial cells, LRFN3 supports VEGF-A-induced VEGFR2 Tyr1175 phosphorylation and angiogenic responses through an as-yet unidentified membrane-associated mediator.

Core Functions

Uses its extracellular adhesion domains at the neuronal plasma membrane to bind SALM4 on an apposed cell and mediate homophilic trans-cellular adhesion at synaptic contacts. This adhesion activity is distinct from the SALM3/SALM5 interaction with presynaptic LAR-family receptor phosphatases.

LRFN3 / SALM4 literature notes

Identity and family boundaries

LRFN3 is SALM4. The family mapping used throughout this review is
SALM1/LRFN2, SALM2/LRFN1, SALM3/LRFN4, SALM4/LRFN3, and SALM5/LRFN5
PMID:27480238

SALM4 is a single-pass membrane adhesion-like protein with extracellular LRR,
Ig, and FNIII domains. It lacks the C-terminal PDZ-binding element found in
SALM1-SALM3 PMID:16495444. Therefore, PSD-95 recruitment and direct NMDAR association shown for SALM1 in this founding paper cannot be assigned to LRFN3.

A decisive paralog boundary is that SALM3 and SALM5 directly induce
presynaptic differentiation through LAR-family receptor phosphatases, whereas
SALM4 does not PMID:27480238. SALM4's key synaptic role is inhibitory regulation of these positive organizers.

Homophilic adhesion and localization

In rat brain, SALM1-SALM3 strongly co-immunoprecipitated, whereas SALM4 and
SALM5 did not, leading the authors to infer that SALM4 and SALM5 mainly form
homomeric complexes in brain PMID:18227064. Heterologous-cell assays showed a distinct trans-adhesion property: SALM4 and SALM5 formed homophilic but not heterophilic associations, and the SALM4 extracellular N terminus was necessary for this behavior PMID:18227064.

In HeLa-neuron coculture, SALM4 accumulated at contact sites with both axons and
dendrites PMID:18227064. In biochemical fractionation, SALM4 was detected in crude synaptosomes, synaptic membranes, and postsynaptic-density fractions PMID:27480238. These results support synaptic and pre-/postsynaptic membrane localizations, but they do not turn SALM4 into a PSD-95-binding protein.

Negative regulation of excitatory synapse development

The principal gene-specific mechanism comes from Salm4-null mice and mixed-cell
assays. SALM4 cis-interacts with SALM3, reduces the trans-synaptic interaction of
SALM3 with presynaptic LAR-family phosphatases, and suppresses SALM3-dependent
presynaptic differentiation PMID:27480238.

Loss of Salm4 increased both excitatory and inhibitory synapse numbers in the
mouse hippocampus PMID:27480238. Re-expression of SALM4 in Salm4-null CA1 pyramidal neurons restored the elevated mEPSC frequency to the normal range PMID:27480238. Genetic removal of Salm3 also normalized the excess excitatory synapse phenotype in Salm4-null mice PMID:27480238. Together, these experiments strongly support negative regulation of presynapse assembly and excitatory synapse development as a conserved SALM4 core role.

The homophilic trans-adhesion property is real, but the knockout rescue argues
that postsynaptic cis inhibition—not SALM4 homophilic adhesion—is the key
mechanism for suppressing excitatory synapse number. The authors state that
postsynaptic SALM4 re-expression was sufficient to rescue the elevated mEPSC
frequency PMID:27480238.

NMDAR signaling and behavior

SALM4 also restrains synaptic NMDAR function in vivo. Lrfn3-null mice showed
increased NMDAR-mediated, but not AMPAR-mediated, hippocampal transmission
involving GluN2B-containing receptors and presynaptic PTPsigma PMID:34588597. The authors concluded that SALM4 suppresses excessive GluN2B-NMDAR function and contextual fear-memory consolidation, not acquisition PMID:34588597. This is functional regulation, not evidence that LRFN3 directly binds NMDARs.

Neurite outgrowth and flotillin-1

Cultured hippocampal-neuron experiments distributed tagged SALMs across axons,
dendrites, and growth cones and found that overexpression of every family member
enhanced neurite outgrowth with distinct phenotypes PMID:18585462. SALM2/SALM4 chimera experiments implicated the extracellular N terminus in outgrowth PMID:18585462.

Flotillin-1 is required for SALM4-induced neurite branching: flotillin-1 siRNA
blocked branching when SALM4 was overexpressed PMID:20600927. The flotillin pathway depends on lipid rafts, SoHo proteins, actin regulation, and the exocyst PMID:20600927. This supports the Reactome SALM4-flotillin event but remains rodent-neuron evidence transferred to human by orthology.

Reticulon-3 interaction

The RTN3 evidence is family-level and weaker functionally. The common SALM LRR
region mediated interaction in yeast two-hybrid assays, and a 19-kDa RTN3C band
bound SALM1-SALM4 in brain immunoprecipitations PMID:19681166. The proposed trafficking consequence was explicitly tentative PMID:19681166. It should not be promoted to a core LRFN3 function without further evidence.

Direct human endothelial evidence

The clearest direct human perturbation evidence is outside the nervous system.
In HUVECs, SALM4 knockdown selectively reduced VEGF-A-induced VEGFR2 Tyr1175
phosphorylation and downstream signaling while leaving Tyr951 and Tyr1214
phosphorylation unchanged PMID:31170000. SALM4 depletion also reduced endothelial leakage, sprouting, and migration PMID:31170000. The paper did not identify a direct SALM4-VEGFR2 interaction or the intervening mediator, so this vascular signaling role is credible but mechanistically incomplete and secondary to the synaptic core.

Curation synthesis and open questions

  • Strongest conserved core: synaptic membrane adhesion/regulation, with SALM4 acting in cis to inhibit SALM3-LAR-dependent presynaptic differentiation and thereby negatively regulate excitatory synapse development.
  • Separate direct activity: SALM4 homophilic trans-cellular adhesion through its extracellular N terminus.
  • Supported associated processes: flotillin-1-dependent neurite branching and negative regulation of GluN2B-NMDAR responses.
  • Important exclusions: do not transfer SALM1/SALM2 direct NMDAR/AMPAR binding or PSD-95-dependent postsynaptic organizing functions; do not assign SALM3/SALM5 direct presynapse-inducing activity to SALM4.
  • Direct human evidence is currently endothelial rather than neuronal. Human-neuron perturbation studies are needed to test conservation of the SALM4-SALM3-LAR mechanism.
