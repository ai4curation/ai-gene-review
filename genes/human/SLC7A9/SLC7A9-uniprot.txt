CC   -!- FUNCTION: Associates with SLC3A1 to form a functional transporter
CC       complex that mediates the electrogenic exchange between cationic amino
CC       acids and neutral amino acids, with a stoichiometry of 1:1
CC       (PubMed:16825196, PubMed:32494597, PubMed:32817565, PubMed:8663357).
CC       Has system b(0,+)-like activity with high affinity for extracellular
CC       cationic amino acids and L-cystine and lower affinity for intracellular
CC       neutral amino acids (PubMed:16825196, PubMed:32494597, PubMed:8663357).
CC       Substrate exchange is driven by high concentration of intracellular
CC       neutral amino acids and the intracellular reduction of L-cystine to L-
CC       cysteine (PubMed:8663357). Required for reabsorption of L-cystine and
CC       dibasic amino acids across the brush border membrane in renal proximal
CC       tubules. {ECO:0000269|PubMed:10471498, ECO:0000269|PubMed:10588648,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:16609684, ECO:0000269|PubMed:16825196,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:32494597, ECO:0000269|PubMed:32817565,

CC   -!- DISEASE: Cystinuria (CSNU) [MIM:220100]: An autosomal disorder
CC       characterized by impaired epithelial cell transport of cystine and
CC       dibasic amino acids (lysine, ornithine, and arginine) in the proximal
CC       renal tubule and gastrointestinal tract. The impaired renal
CC       reabsorption of cystine and its low solubility causes the formation of
CC       calculi in the urinary tract, resulting in obstructive uropathy,
CC       pyelonephritis, and, rarely, renal failure.
