Gene Description

ARMC9 is a Joubert syndrome-associated ciliary protein that coordinates a network of microtubule regulators, including TOGARAM1 and CSPP1. Distinct regions mediate partner interactions, and its central H region supports dimerization. Reconstitution shows partner-dependent recruitment and modulation of microtubule dynamics, without autonomous ARMC9 microtubule binding or polymerase activity. Endogenous human ARMC9 localizes near basal bodies and daughter centrioles; tagged ARMC9 also shows stimulus-dependent ciliary-tip enrichment in mouse cells. Disruption of ARMC9 impairs ciliary formation and stability, and biallelic variants cause Joubert syndrome. The location and composition of the active partner-coordinating network in cells remain incompletely resolved.

Core Functions

ARMC9 coordinates TOGARAM1 and CSPP1 through distinct partner-binding regions, contributing to cilium formation and stability. Its H region supports dimerization, although dimerization is not established as a requirement for every interaction. Partner recruitment and modulation of microtubule dynamics support adaptor activity in a flexible protein network. The precise cellular site of this activity remains unresolved: basal-body, daughter-centriole and conditional ciliary-tip distributions establish protein localization without locating each biochemical interaction. ARMC9 does not autonomously bind microtubules or supply polymerase activity in the tested reconstitution.

ARMC9 is an 818-residue LisH/armadillo-repeat protein that organizes a ciliary protein interaction network. Human cell localization places it at the basal body and daughter centriole; mammalian ciliary localization is dynamic, with Hedgehog-dependent tip enrichment in the mouse cell systems studied. The strongest mechanistic evidence now distinguishes its structural contribution to a complex from autonomous microtubule binding or polymerase activity.
