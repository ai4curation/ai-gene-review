CC   -!- FUNCTION: Functions as a binuclear Fe(III)-Fe(II) acid phosphatase that
CC       hydrolyzes phosphate esters to alcohols and phosphate under acidic
CC       conditions (PubMed:1477968, PubMed:2775236). Dephosphorylates a broad
CC       range of substrates, including adenosine 5'-diphosphate (ADP),
CC       adenosine 5'-triphosphate (ATP), as well as larger phosphoproteins such
CC       as osteopontin (SPP1), a highly phosphorylated bone matrix protein,
CC       bone sialoprotein (IBSP) and synthetic compounds such as para-
CC       nitrophenyl phosphate (p-NPP) (By similarity). Through the
CC       dephosphorylation of bone matrix proteins, plays a crucial role in
CC       normal cartilage mineralization during skeletal development and in
CC       maintaining and remodeling bone integrity in adults (By similarity).
CC       Promotes osteoclast migration by dephosphorylating SPP1, which binds
CC       osteoclasts to the bone matrix (By similarity). Also involved in immune
CC       regulation and macrophage function, contributes to the breakdown of
CC       internalized pathogens within lysosomes (By similarity). Regulates
CC       immune cell recruitment during infection, partly via interaction with
CC       SPP1 (By similarity). The uncleaved form (isoform 5a) functions
CC       primarily in immune regulation rather than bone resorption (By
CC       similarity). May contribute to the removal of mannose-6-phosphate
CC       (Man6P) from lysosomal proteins after their delivery to lysosomes,
CC       acting in concert with ACP2 (By similarity).

CC       predisposition to autoimmune diseases. The skeletal dysplasia is
CC       characterized by radiolucent and irregular spondylar and metaphyseal
CC       lesions that represent islands of chondroid tissue within bone.
