Gene Description

CCN6, also called WISP3, is a secreted, cysteine-rich CCN-family protein involved in cartilage homeostasis and extracellular regulation of growth-factor responses. Human CCN6 binds IGF1 and associates with BMP4 in experimental systems, modifying their cellular effects. An intracellular pool localizes to mitochondria and influences respiration and reactive oxygen species in a cell-context-dependent manner. Biallelic pathogenic variants cause progressive pseudorheumatoid dysplasia.

Core Functions

Secreted human CCN6 binds IGF-I and modulates its availability and cellular responses. Recombinant-protein assays establish weak binding, approximately 30 micromolar in the reported SPR system, with more than the IGFBP-like domain required for full interaction. Human conditioned-medium experiments independently show reduced IGF1R-pathway activation. Together these support an extracellular binding and negative-regulation role, while the conditioned-medium data do not isolate sequestration as the sole causal mechanism. This does not assign receptor kinase activity or a universal response direction across other ligands and tissues.

Direct extracellular activity and the proposed molecular-function addition

WISP3–IGF1 interaction, PMID:23424195 supplies the strongest own-activity evidence. Selected original Results, captions and Methods were read on the publisher page, including human WISP3 from a synovial-fibroblast cDNA, human IGF1, C-28/I2 chondrocytes, transfected HEK293 cells, recombinant coimmunoprecipitation and surface plasmon resonance. The cell line is derived from human juvenile costal cartilage and immortalized with SV40; it is not primary adult articular cartilage. Mouse ATDC5 positive-control material is separate. SPR used micromolar IGF1 concentrations and reported approximately 30 μM affinity for immobilized WISP3; this must not be described as the high affinity of canonical IGFBPs. The full multidomain protein is important, and the IGFBP-like domain alone is insufficient. Poor expression of a deletion construct cannot be interpreted as a clean binding-negative mutant. The cellular experiments link WISP3 to IGF1 secretion and IGF1-induced hypertrophic markers, without showing proteolytic cleavage of IGF1 or an intrinsic enzyme activity.

The original Wisp3 BMP/Wnt study, PMID:17823661 provides an independent ligand context. Selected original Methods, Results and figure captions distinguish human WISP3-containing conditioned medium inhibiting human BMP2-driven alkaline-phosphatase responses in mouse 10T1/2 cells from human WISP3/mouse BMP4 coassociation. The Wnt receptor binding and TopFlash experiments principally use zebrafish Wisp3, even when carried out in mammalian cells.
