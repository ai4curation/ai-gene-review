Gene Description

CCN6, also called WISP3, is a secreted, cysteine-rich CCN-family protein involved in cartilage homeostasis and extracellular regulation of growth-factor responses. Human CCN6 binds IGF1 and associates with BMP4 in experimental systems, modifying their cellular effects. An intracellular pool localizes to mitochondria and influences respiration and reactive oxygen species in a cell-context-dependent manner. Biallelic pathogenic variants cause progressive pseudorheumatoid dysplasia.

Core Functions

Recombinant human WISP3–IGF1 interaction establishes an own binding activity; human WISP3/mouse BMP4 conditioned-medium assays provide a separate, explicitly cross-species ligand context. This does not establish receptor kinase activity or transfer zebrafish Wisp3 Wnt-coreceptor binding to human CCN6.

Direct extracellular activity and the proposed molecular-function addition

WISP3–IGF1 interaction, PMID:23424195 supplies the strongest own-activity evidence. Selected original Results, captions and Methods were read on the publisher page, including human WISP3 from a synovial-fibroblast cDNA, human IGF1, C-28/I2 chondrocytes, transfected HEK293 cells, recombinant coimmunoprecipitation and surface plasmon resonance. The cell line is derived from human juvenile costal cartilage and immortalized with SV40; it is not primary adult articular cartilage. Mouse ATDC5 positive-control material is separate. SPR used micromolar IGF1 concentrations and reported approximately 30 μM affinity for immobilized WISP3; this must not be described as the high affinity of canonical IGFBPs. The full multidomain protein is important, and the IGFBP-like domain alone is insufficient. Poor expression of a deletion construct cannot be interpreted as a clean binding-negative mutant. The cellular experiments link WISP3 to IGF1 secretion and IGF1-induced hypertrophic markers, without showing proteolytic cleavage of IGF1 or an intrinsic enzyme activity.

The original Wisp3 BMP/Wnt study, PMID:17823661 provides an independent ligand context. Selected original Methods, Results and figure captions distinguish human WISP3-containing conditioned medium inhibiting human BMP2-driven alkaline-phosphatase responses in mouse 10T1/2 cells from human WISP3/mouse BMP4 coassociation. Reciprocal immunoprecipitation, conditioned-medium depletion and disease-variant comparisons are informative. Mixing conditioned media is not a purified two-protein binding assay. The Wnt receptor binding and TopFlash experiments principally use zebrafish Wisp3, even when carried out in mammalian cells. They are not relabeled as direct human CCN6–LRP6 or human CCN6–Frizzled measurements. The zebrafish cartilage phenotype and the absence of an obvious mouse phenotype remain distinct from human recessive disease.

The single provisional NEW is GO:0019838 growth factor binding. Its official definition and protein-binding parent were checked in AmiGO. It describes CCN6's own interaction with growth-factor ligands, not merely a response to added growth factor. The existing integrin- and heparin-binding terms do not cover IGF1/BMP ligand binding. Only this broad activity is proposed; its IGF-binding child is not added redundantly. Dated local IGFBP3 annotations provide positive IGF-binding/growth-factor-binding comparator coverage, and the official MGI Nog record provides positive predicted growth-factor-binding coverage for a separate extracellular antagonist. These are explicit, limited positive precedents, not a complete current QuickGO survey or proof that curators missed a gene. The inspected local GO-CAM index has no O95389/CCN6/WISP3 match; that literal result is not proof of global model absence. No NEW biological process is proposed.

Intracellular and mitochondrial evidence

The canonical 2016 CCN6 study, PMID:27252383 is abstract-only. Selected original Results and Methods were read separately from an author-uploaded primary reproduction. The available evidence includes endogenous and expressed-protein microscopy, fractionation, mitoplast protease protection and siRNA depletion in human C-28/I2 cells. Carbonate extraction of membrane-associated protein used CCN6-Myc-transfected HEK293 cells, not the chondrocyte line. Isotype/empty-vector and compartment-marker controls were considered; figure pixels and all supplements were not independently analyzed. These experiments support a mitochondrial pool without uniquely fixing its membrane topology.

In the chondrocyte experiment, partial CCN6 depletion increases respiration, mitochondrial ROS, ATP-associated measures and potential-sensitive dye signals. Mitochondrial mass also changes, so a dye response should not be described as an independently calibrated voltage measurement unaffected by organelle abundance. The primary experiments do not measure CCN6 catalyzing an electron-transfer reaction. The nondirectional membrane-potential and ROS regulatory assertions are retained as non-core cellular processes.

For PMID:32686858(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32686858/), the complete official abstract has been read; the complete original Methods and Results remain pending. Size-exclusion/native-gel coassociation and perturbation-linked assembly effects do not by themselves make CCN6 a stoichiometric respiratory-complex subunit. The different effects of partial depletion and disease-variant contexts should not be compressed into one universal direction.

For PMID:34811632(https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9411309/), the official abstract and selected original PMC Results were read. Human mammary-cell depletion and ectopic-expression experiments show respiration/ROS responses that differ from the chondrocyte depletion direction. Clinical expression correlations are distinct from the cellular interventions. The description therefore says context-dependent mitochondrial regulation, not a universal mitochondrial brake. A second mitochondrial molecular-function core is not invented merely to accommodate a well-supported compartment and perturbation phenotype.

The current Human Protein Atlas CCN6 page reports supported mitochondrial staining with HPA064196 in RT4, SK-MEL-30 and U2OS. This independently corroborates the broad compartment.
