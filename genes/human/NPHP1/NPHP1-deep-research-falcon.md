# Human NPHP1 (nephrocystin-1): functional annotation research report

## Identity and principal function

**Identity check.** The specified human gene **NPHP1** (synonym **NPH1**; supplied UniProt accession **O15259**) encodes **nephrocystin-1**, also called nephrocystin—not nephrocystin-4 or another similarly named ciliopathy protein. Primary studies describe a 733-amino-acid protein with a coiled-coil region, an **SH3 protein-interaction domain**, acidic clusters and a C-terminal nephrocystin-homology region. This agrees with the supplied nephrocystin-1-family and SH3-domain annotations. The accession is supplied as the identification anchor; the cited experiments independently establish the human gene–protein identity and domain-based function. (fliegauf2006nephrocystinspecificallylocalizes pages 1-2, wodarczyk2010nephrocystin1formsa pages 1-2)

**Best-supported primary function:** nephrocystin-1 is a **non-enzymatic interaction scaffold/adaptor**, principally associated with the **transition zone at the base of cilia** and also found in epithelial adhesion complexes. It helps organize a nephrocystin protein module and is required for normal ciliary morphology and renal epithelial organization. Its location and complex membership support a role in maintaining the composition of the ciliary signaling compartment, but a particular molecule transported specifically by NPHP1 has **not** been established. It is neither characterized as a catalyst nor as a substrate-specific transporter. (fliegauf2006nephrocystinspecificallylocalizes pages 1-2, moran2024transportandbarrier pages 5-6, sang2011mappingthenphpjbtsmks pages 5-7, sang2011mappingthenphpjbtsmks pages 7-9, arai2024patientderivedandgeneedited pages 4-6)

## Where nephrocystin-1 acts

The **ciliary transition zone** is the short region between the basal body and the ciliary axoneme. In human respiratory epithelium, nephrocystin-1 moves from a diffuse cytoplasmic distribution during ciliogenesis to a concentrated signal at this zone; it has also been observed at renal cilia and the photoreceptor **connecting cilium**, the specialized link to the light-sensing outer segment. Respiratory cells from people with homozygous NPHP1 deletions lack the nephrocystin-1 signal yet can still assemble motile cilia. Thus, localization and patient-cell evidence support a role in **ciliary organization or function rather than an absolute requirement for cilium formation** in that tissue. (fliegauf2006nephrocystinspecificallylocalizes pages 1-2, fliegauf2006nephrocystinspecificallylocalizes pages 2-3)

Nephrocystin-1 also occurs at epithelial **cell–cell contacts and cell–matrix adhesion sites**. Reported partners include the adhesion-associated proteins p130CAS, tensin, filamin and Pyk2. These findings make it plausible that NPHP1 links ciliary organization with epithelial junctional architecture, although junctional interactions alone do not establish which site drives kidney disease. In three-dimensional renal epithelial cultures, NPHP1 depletion impaired organized spheroid formation despite preservation of cilia numbers, providing functional evidence for an epithelial-architecture role separable from simply making a cilium. (gupta2021nephronophthisispathobiologyandmolecular pages 4-6, sang2011mappingthenphpjbtsmks pages 7-9)

## Molecular complexes, trafficking and signaling

**Transition-zone assembly.** Affinity purification and reciprocal binding experiments identify an **NPHP1–NPHP4–NPHP8 module**, where NPHP8 is **RPGRIP1L**. Importantly, direct-binding experiments found that **NPHP4 binds NPHP1 and NPHP8 and can bridge them**; they did not establish a direct NPHP1–NPHP8 interaction. The proteins were observed at the ciliary-base/transition-zone region and epithelial cell contacts. Recent expert reviews place this NPHP module alongside other, partly interconnected transition-zone modules that help establish a selective boundary between ciliary and non-ciliary compartments. That is a **module-level gating model**, not proof that NPHP1 directly recognizes a defined cargo. (sang2011mappingthenphpjbtsmks pages 3-5, park2022compositionorganizationand pages 6-8, moran2024transportandbarrier pages 5-6, sang2011mappingthenphpjbtsmks pages 5-7)

**Control of NPHP1 targeting.** A particularly well-resolved biochemical pathway is **CK2 → nephrocystin-1 phosphorylation → PACS-1 binding → ciliary-base localization**. CK2 phosphorylates nephrocystin-1 at **Ser121, Ser123 and Ser126** in an acidic cluster adjacent to the SH3 domain. Phosphosite mutagenesis and biochemical binding assays support phosphorylation-dependent association with the sorting protein **PACS-1**; CK2 inhibition or interference with PACS-1 removes nephrocystin-1 from the ciliary base and redistributes it to intracellular vesicle-like structures without visibly abolishing cilia. This evidence identifies **NPHP1 as a CK2 substrate, not a kinase**. (schermer2005phosphorylationbycasein pages 5-7, schermer2005phosphorylationbycasein pages 3-5, schermer2005phosphorylationbycasein pages 7-8)

An additional regulatory axis involves **Pyk2 and NPHP4**. Pyk2-mediated phosphorylation of three NPHP1 tyrosines—**Tyr46, Tyr349 and Tyr721**—is counteracted by NPHP4 and influences PACS-1 association. NPHP4 depletion causes NPHP1 accumulation near the trans-Golgi in ciliated retinal epithelial cells. Together, these experiments support phosphorylation-regulated positioning of the NPHP1 complex between intracellular sorting compartments and the ciliary base; the complete direction and physiological dynamics of trafficking remain a model rather than a fully measured transport cycle. (liebau2011nephrocystin4regulatespyk2induced pages 1-2, liebau2011nephrocystin4regulatespyk2induced pages 6-8)

**A defined SH3 ligand and pathway connection.** Biochemical and NMR experiments show that the NPHP1 SH3 domain binds the **PP2 proline-rich motif of polycystin-1**, the PKD1 protein, whereas the tested PP1 motif did not bind detectably. The isolated peptide–SH3 interaction had an estimated dissociation constant of **0.30 ± 0.02 mM**; mutation of polycystin-1 **Pro4270** disrupted binding, including in full-length-protein experiments. In renal epithelial cell models, NPHP1 depletion impaired polycystin-1-associated resistance to an apoptotic challenge but did not abolish its tested cell-cycle or ERK effects; increased apoptosis was also observed in tissue from **four** NPHP1-mutant patients. This establishes a physical and experimentally supported **apoptosis-response connection**, not that apoptosis regulation is nephrocystin-1’s sole or principal physiological task. Polycystin-1 was not required for NPHP1 localization to cilia. (wodarczyk2010nephrocystin1formsa pages 6-8, wodarczyk2010nephrocystin1formsa pages 3-5)

The transition zone more generally regulates which membrane and soluble signaling proteins occupy cilia. Reviews discuss consequences for pathways such as **Hedgehog signaling** and for renal cystoprotein localization, but evidence for the broader ciliary gate should not be recast as proof that NPHP1 directly transduces Hedgehog signals or selects a specific receptor. Similarly, transcriptomic changes in cell-cycle, cholesterol-biosynthesis or EGFR-associated programs after NPHP1 loss do not, by themselves, identify direct NPHP1-binding partners in those pathways. (moran2024transportandbarrier pages 14-15, moran2024transportandbarrier pages 5-6, moran2024transportandbarrier pages 3-5, arai2024patientderivedandgeneedited pages 6-8)

## Recent human evidence, disease relevance and applications

Biallelic loss of NPHP1 is an established cause of **autosomal-recessive juvenile nephronophthisis**, a progressive renal tubulointerstitial ciliopathy; retinal degeneration can accompany kidney disease. Estimates cited by recent experimental authors attribute approximately **20–25% of nephronophthisis cases** to NPHP1, but the fraction depends on cohort and case definition. Homozygous whole-gene deletion is a common pathogenic mechanism. Disease association, transition-zone localization and patient-cell phenotypes together make NPHP1 a strong functional disease annotation, without implying that all nephronophthisis is NPHP1-related. (wolf2011nephronophthisis pages 4-6, arai2024patientderivedandgeneedited pages 1-2, hellmann2024immunofluorescenceanalysesof pages 5-7)

**Human disease modeling, June 2024.** Arai and colleagues compared patient-derived, gene-edited and NPHP1-re-expressing human pluripotent stem cells. Loss of NPHP1 produced **longer primary cilia and fewer ciliated cells**. After transfer to rotating suspension culture, approximately **80%** of NPHP1-deficient kidney organoids developed epithelial-lined cysts by day 32, compared with **20–30%** of control or NPHP1-overexpressing organoids. Re-expression reduced cyst formation; nephron-like structures initially formed even without NPHP1. The matched genetic manipulation and rescue provide unusually strong cellular evidence that NPHP1 loss drives the phenotype. Rotation is an experimental stress condition, however, and an organoid rescue is **not clinical gene-therapy efficacy**. Study: Arai *et al.*, *Frontiers in Cell and Developmental Biology*, **June 2024**, https://doi.org/10.3389/fcell.2024.1370723. (arai2024patientderivedandgeneedited pages 6-8, arai2024patientderivedandgeneedited pages 4-6)

**Patient-cell diagnostic implementation, August 2024.** Hellmann and colleagues tested whether minimally invasive nasal epithelial sampling could complement genetic diagnosis. Their abstract reports **86 genetically characterized individuals**, with **35** also assessed for NPHP4; the detailed staining analysis describes **111 samples**, of which **22 were unevaluable** because of specimen quality. NPHP1 staining was absent in **14** evaluable samples, including **10** previously recognized homozygous NPHP1 deletions and one homozygous **p.Gly343Arg** variant previously classified as uncertain. Abnormal staining prompted further testing that diagnosed two previously unsolved individuals. In NPHP1-genotype cases, mean NPHP4 transition-zone signal-to-noise ratio was **2.57 versus 9.21** in controls (**p = 0.008**), consistent with functional interdependence. This is a promising **adjunct to molecular testing**, not a validated stand-alone screen with a generalizable sensitivity estimate. Study: Hellmann *et al.*, *Pediatric Nephrology*, **August 2024**, https://doi.org/10.1007/s00467-024-06443-0. (hellmann2024immunofluorescenceanalysesof pages 1-2, hellmann2024immunofluorescenceanalysesof pages 5-7)

**More recent exploratory therapy work, September 2025.** Urine-derived renal epithelial cells from an affected member of a family with homozygous NPHP1 deletion were used to compare candidate treatments. **Alprostadil** increased ciliation but worsened the already elongated-cilium phenotype; the EGFR inhibitor **AG556** improved ciliary length and shape and more effectively reversed aspects of the measured gene-expression signature. This motivates testing EGFR-dependent mechanisms but does **not** establish that nephrocystin-1 directly signals through EGFR, or that AG556 is effective or safe for patients. The authors note the single-patient cell model and the need for in-vivo validation. Study: Sudhindar *et al.*, *Journal of Cell Science*, **September 2025**, https://doi.org/10.1242/jcs.264141. (sudhindar2025urinaryrenalepithelial pages 12-13, sudhindar2025urinaryrenalepithelial pages 1-2)

## Evidence-weighted conclusion

The most defensible functional annotation for **human NPHP1/O15259** is **“phosphorylation-regulated, SH3-containing nephrocystin scaffold at the ciliary transition zone and epithelial adhesion sites.”** Direct experiments establish its location, NPHP4-bridged module membership, CK2/PACS-1-dependent targeting, a polycystin-1 SH3 interaction and the consequences of human NPHP1 loss and re-expression. Selective ciliary gating is a strong **transition-zone-complex-level interpretation**, whereas the identity of an **NPHP1-specific transported cargo**, the relative contribution of its ciliary versus junctional pools to human kidney disease, and an effective NPHP1-directed treatment remain unresolved. (schermer2005phosphorylationbycasein pages 5-7, moran2024transportandbarrier pages 5-6, sang2011mappingthenphpjbtsmks pages 5-7, wodarczyk2010nephrocystin1formsa pages 3-5, sang2011mappingthenphpjbtsmks pages 7-9, arai2024patientderivedandgeneedited pages 4-6)
