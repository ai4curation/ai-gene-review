| Evidence tier | Finding for human NPHP1/nephrocystin-1 | Experimental basis and interpretation | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Identity/domain validation | Human **NPHP1** encodes the 733-aa nephrocystin-1 protein, containing an N-terminal coiled-coil region, an SH3 domain, acidic clusters and a C-terminal nephrocystin-homology region. This matches UniProt **O15259** and supports classification as a non-enzymatic interaction scaffold. | Protein characterization and human mutation/localization studies; no catalytic activity or transported substrate has been demonstrated. | Fliegauf et al., 2006, DOI: [10.1681/ASN.2005121351](https://doi.org/10.1681/ASN.2005121351) (pqac-00000001) |
| Direct trafficking mechanism | CK2 phosphorylates nephrocystin-1 at **Ser121, Ser123 and Ser126** in its first acidic cluster, enhancing PACS-1 binding. CK2 inhibition or dominant-negative PACS-1 displaces nephrocystin-1 from the ciliary base into vesicle-like structures without destroying cilia. | In-vitro phosphorylation, phosphosite mutagenesis, binding assays, kinase inhibition and cellular localization establish a phosphorylation-dependent targeting mechanism. | Schermer et al., 2005, DOI: [10.1038/sj.emboj.7600885](https://doi.org/10.1038/sj.emboj.7600885) (pqac-00000020, pqac-00000022) |
| Direct human localization | Nephrocystin-1 localizes specifically to the transition zone of human renal and respiratory cilia and to photoreceptor connecting cilia; during ciliogenesis it moves from diffuse cytoplasm to the transition zone. Homozygous deletion removes the signal but does not prevent respiratory ciliogenesis. | Immunofluorescence in human tissues and patient respiratory cells supports a transition-zone structural/adaptor role, not an obligatory role in axoneme assembly. | Fliegauf et al., 2006, DOI: [10.1681/ASN.2005121351](https://doi.org/10.1681/ASN.2005121351) (pqac-00000001, pqac-00000047) |
| Direct complex architecture | NPHP1, NPHP4 and NPHP8/RPGRIP1L form a transition-zone module. In-vitro binding showed that **NPHP4 directly binds both NPHP1 and NPHP8 and bridges them**; NPHP1 and NPHP8 were not shown to bind directly. | Reciprocal affinity purification, mass spectrometry, co-immunoprecipitation, direct-binding assays and localization define complex organization. This supports general gate organization, but not an NPHP1-specific cargo. | Sang et al., 2011, DOI: [10.1016/j.cell.2011.04.019](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2011.04.019) (pqac-00000014, pqac-00000031) |
| Direct SH3 interaction and functional association | The NPHP1 SH3 domain binds the **PP2 polyproline motif** in the PKD1 product polycystin-1, with peptide affinity of approximately **0.30 ± 0.02 mM**; PC-1 P4270L abolishes binding. NPHP1 depletion increased TNFα-induced apoptosis, and increased apoptosis was found in kidney tissue from four NPHP1-mutant patients. | NMR, ITC, pull-down and co-immunoprecipitation establish binding; cell and patient-tissue assays associate the complex with apoptosis resistance, but do not prove that apoptosis is NPHP1’s primary physiological function. | Wodarczyk et al., 2010, DOI: [10.1371/journal.pone.0012719](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0012719) (pqac-00000034, pqac-00000050) |
| Human diagnostic/complex evidence | In genetically characterized renal-ciliopathy cases, pathogenic NPHP1 variants eliminated NPHP1 staining and markedly reduced NPHP4 transition-zone signal (**mean signal-to-noise ratio 2.57 versus 9.21 in controls; p=0.008**). Reciprocal disruption occurred with NPHP4 genotypes. | Nasal epithelial immunofluorescence, immunoblotting and follow-up genetics support in-vivo NPHP1–NPHP4 interdependence and a potential diagnostic assay; they do not alone establish the molecular direction of dependency. | Hellmann et al., 2024, DOI: [10.1007/s00467-024-06443-0](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00467-024-06443-0) (pqac-00000010, pqac-00000015) |
| Human disease-model causality | NPHP1-deficient patient-derived and CRISPR-edited hiPSCs had longer cilia and fewer ciliated cells. Under rotating suspension culture, about **80%** of deficient kidney organoids formed cysts by day 32 versus **20–30%** of isogenic controls or NPHP1-overexpressing organoids; re-expression rescued cyst formation. | Isogenic editing, patient cells, organoid phenotyping and rescue provide strong causal cellular evidence. Fluid-stress culture is a model, so the result is preclinical and does not establish therapeutic efficacy in patients. | Arai et al., 2024, DOI: [10.3389/fcell.2024.1370723](https://doi.org/10.3389/fcell.2024.1370723) (pqac-00000013, pqac-00000048, pqac-00000049) |


*Table: Evidence supporting nephrocystin-1 as a phosphorylation-regulated transition-zone and junctional interaction scaffold. The table separates direct mechanistic findings from functional interpretations and avoids assigning catalytic activity or a uniquely NPHP1-dependent ciliary cargo.*
