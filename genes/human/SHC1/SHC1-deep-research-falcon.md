# SHC1 (human; UniProt P29353): functional-annotation research report

## Executive summary

The requested target is correctly identified as **human SHC1**, also called **ShcA/SHCA**, encoding SHC-transforming protein 1 (UniProt **P29353**). The literature’s three ShcA isoforms—**p46Shc, p52Shc, and p66Shc**—and its PTB/CH1/SH2 domain organization match the supplied UniProt annotation. This target is distinct from human SHC2/ShcB, SHC3/ShcC, and SHC4/ShcD and from similarly named nonhuman genes such as *C. elegans shc-3*. Instead, it recognizes phosphorylated receptors and signaling proteins through its PTB and SH2 domains, is itself phosphorylated on CH1 tyrosines, and creates docking sites for GRB2 and other effectors. (wills2016…shcdeviance pages 17-21, ahmed2017insightsintothe pages 4-5, wills2016…shcdeviance pages 14-17)

## 1. Identity, isoforms, and domain organization

### Identity verification

The supplied identity is internally consistent:

- **Gene:** SHC1, aliases SHC and SHCA.
- **Organism:** *Homo sapiens*.
- **Protein:** SHC-transforming protein 1/ShcA.
- **UniProt:** P29353.
- **Functional class:** phosphotyrosine-binding adaptor rather than enzyme, transporter, or structural polymer.

The literature recognizes four vertebrate Shc paralogs: ShcA/SHC1, ShcB/SHC2, ShcC/SHC3, and ShcD/SHC4. SHC1 is the broadly expressed, canonical RTK adaptor discussed here; results concerning SHC2–4 or nonhuman *shc* genes were not treated as evidence for human P29353. (ahmed2017insightsintothe pages 1-4, wills2016…shcdeviance pages 14-17, wills2016…shcdeviance pages 149-152)

### Isoforms

SHC1 produces three major protein forms. **p46Shc and p52Shc** arise through alternative translation initiation, whereas **p66Shc** is produced from a distinct transcript and has an additional approximately **110-amino-acid N-terminal region**. The shorter proteins principally mediate receptor-proximal mitogenic signaling, while p66Shc has additional oxidative-stress functions and may oppose mitogenic signaling in some contexts. (ahmed2017insightsintothe pages 4-5, ahmed2017insightsintothe pages 1-4, wills2016…shcdeviance pages 14-17)

### Domains and recognition specificity

The shared core is **PTB–CH1–SH2**; p66Shc additionally contains an N-terminal **CH2** region. This agrees with the supplied InterPro annotations for a Shc-like PID/PTB domain, PH-like fold, and SH2 domain. The “CEP152/SHC-transforming” label should not be interpreted as making SHC1 a centrosomal CEP152-family protein; the experimentally informative modules are its Shc-like PTB/PID, CH regions, and SH2 domain.

The PTB domain preferentially recognizes phosphorylated **NPXpY**-type motifs and can also interact with phosphoinositides, supporting membrane recruitment. The SH2 domain binds a different class of phosphotyrosine-containing sequences. This dual recognition system enables SHC1 to bind many activated RTKs and associated phosphoproteins. The central CH1 region contains conserved Tyr239/Tyr240 and Tyr317 in p52 numbering; phosphorylation converts these sites into docking motifs, especially for GRB2. (wills2016…shcdeviance pages 17-21, ahmed2017insightsintothe pages 4-5, wills2016…shcdeviance pages 14-17)

## 2. Primary molecular function

SHC1 is not known to catalyze a chemical reaction. Consequently, enzyme substrate specificity and transporter cargo are not applicable. Its biochemical specificity is instead **interaction specificity**:

1. PTB or SH2 domains recognize activated, tyrosine-phosphorylated receptors or receptor-associated proteins.
2. Receptor and/or Src-family kinases phosphorylate SHC1’s CH1 tyrosines.
3. These phosphotyrosines recruit downstream proteins, particularly GRB2.
4. SHC1 thereby increases the local concentration, lifetime, and ordering of signaling complexes while also contributing to signal termination and receptor trafficking.

This is better described as a dynamic molecular scaffold than as a passive bridge. Quantitative EGF-network work showed successive SHC1 interaction waves: an early GRB2-dependent mitogenic/survival network, followed by AKT-dependent Ser29 phosphorylation and PTPN12 recruitment, and then delayed complexes implicated in cytoskeletal remodeling, trafficking, morphogenesis, and termination. Thus SHC1 helps route signaling information over time rather than merely switching a pathway on. (yoshizawa2021biphasicspatiotemporalregulation pages 1-2, wills2016…shcdeviance pages 21-24)

## 3. Canonical signaling mechanism

### RTK–SHC1–GRB2–SOS–RAS–ERK

The most firmly established pathway is:

**growth factor → RTK dimerization/autophosphorylation → SHC1 PTB/SH2 recruitment → SHC1 Tyr239/Tyr240/Tyr317 phosphorylation → GRB2 binding → SOS recruitment → RAS-GDP to RAS-GTP exchange → RAF → MEK → ERK.**

GRB2’s SH2 domain recognizes phosphorylated SHC1, while its SH3 domains associate with the SOS guanine-nucleotide exchange factor. SOS activates membrane-bound RAS, which initiates the RAF–MEK–ERK kinase cascade controlling proliferation, differentiation, survival, and cell-state transitions. SHC1 can therefore amplify receptor output by adding GRB2–SOS docking sites beyond those present on the receptor itself. (wills2016…shcdeviance pages 17-21, ahmed2017insightsintothe pages 4-5, yoshizawa2021biphasicspatiotemporalregulation pages 1-2)

Live-cell single-molecule work in heregulin-stimulated MCF7 cells revealed a biphasic mechanism. During the first **<10 minutes**, p52Shc increased membrane docking sites for GRB2–SOS and amplified RAS activity. Later, cytoplasmic SHC–GRB2 complexes sequestered GRB2 away from the membrane and helped terminate RAS activation despite persistent receptor/SHC behavior. This supports an expert interpretation of SHC1 as a controller of signaling amplitude and duration, not simply a positive pathway component. The study was published online January 8, 2021: https://doi.org/10.2142/biophysico.bppb-v18.001. (yoshizawa2021biphasicspatiotemporalregulation pages 1-2)

### PI3K–AKT and other outputs

SHC1-dependent receptor complexes can also engage PI3K–AKT, directly or through proteins such as GAB adaptors, but this connection is more receptor- and cell-context-dependent than the canonical GRB2–SOS–RAS pathway. Other reported outputs include cytoskeletal remodeling, migration, receptor trafficking, STAT signaling, and PLCγ/PKC-related responses. Phosphatases including PTPN12/PTP-PEST, PTEN, PP2A, and PTPε can reduce SHC1 phosphorylation or SHC1–GRB2 signaling and contribute to pathway termination. (wills2016…shcdeviance pages 21-24, wills2016…shcdeviance pages 40-44)

### Integrin and immune-receptor signaling

SHC1 also integrates extracellular-matrix adhesion with growth-factor signaling. Several integrins recruit SHC1 through associated Src-family kinases; SHC1 Tyr317 phosphorylation then recruits GRB2 and activates RAS–MAPK. In T-cell signaling, p52Shc can associate with phospho-CD3 and ZAP-70, linking antigen-receptor activation to RAS/MAPK and Rac responses. These mechanisms explain how SHC1 coordinates biochemical signals with adhesion and cellular context. (wills2016…shcdeviance pages 44-47, wills2016…shcdeviance pages 40-44)

## 4. Cellular localization

### Basal and receptor-stimulated localization

SHC1 is primarily **cytosolic and ER-associated** under basal conditions. Only a small membrane-associated pool is expected without stimulation; growth-factor exposure recruits p46/p52Shc to activated receptors at the plasma membrane and subsequently to receptor-containing endosomal complexes. One literature synthesis estimated that approximately **5% of p52Shc** is membrane-associated basally, followed by redistribution toward the cell periphery after stimulation. (wills2016…shcdeviance pages 17-21, lebiedzinskaarciszewska2024distributionofthe pages 1-2)

p46Shc has also been reported in the mitochondrial matrix, where it may regulate ACAA2 and lipid oxidation, and in hepatocyte nuclei during proliferation and hepatocellular-carcinoma development. These isoform-specific localizations indicate that “SHC1 localization” cannot be represented by one fixed compartment. (ahmed2017insightsintothe pages 4-5, lebiedzinskaarciszewska2024distributionofthe pages 1-2)

### p66Shc, mitochondria, and mitochondria-associated membranes

The classical stress model proposes that oxidative stress induces p66Shc Ser36 phosphorylation by PKCβ or JNK, followed by conformational regulation, Pin1-dependent targeting, and redistribution to mitochondria. In the mitochondrial intermembrane-space model, p66Shc interacts with cytochrome *c* through a redox region containing residues 132-EEW-134, promotes hydrogen-peroxide production, increases mitochondrial permeability, and favors apoptosis. Selective p66Shc deletion was historically reported to extend mouse lifespan by about **30%**, although that mouse result does not establish an equivalent effect in humans. (wills2016…shcdeviance pages 44-47, lebiedzinskaarciszewska2024distributionofthe pages 1-2)

A 2024 study refined this localization model using differential fractionation and controlled proteolysis in HepG2 cells and mouse liver. The authors found that a substantial p66Shc pool assigned to “crude mitochondria” was actually associated with **mitochondria-associated ER membranes (MAM)**. In control HepG2 cells, p46 and p66 represented **25.175% and 24.054%** of total ShcA, respectively, while p52 was dominant. In crude mitochondrial fractions, p66Shc signal fell by approximately **50% after 0.5 µg/mL trypsin** and was undetectable after **2 µg/mL**, before protected mitochondrial markers were disrupted. Even after H₂O₂ treatment, most recovered p66Shc behaved more like a MAM-associated pool than an intramitochondrial protein. Published November 29, 2024: https://doi.org/10.3390/ijms252312835. (lebiedzinskaarciszewska2024distributionofthe pages 9-11, lebiedzinskaarciszewska2024distributionofthe pages 1-2)

The most defensible current interpretation is therefore that p66Shc is mainly cytosolic/ER/MAM-associated, with a smaller or condition-dependent mitochondrial pool. Fractionation sensitivity, soluble versus membrane-protein susceptibility to protease, cell type, and stress protocol can explain some discrepancies across studies. (lebiedzinskaarciszewska2024distributionofthe pages 9-11)

## 5. Biological processes most directly attributable to SHC1

The highest-confidence functions are:

- transduction of RTK signals into RAS–ERK signaling;
- modulation of signal amplitude and duration through GRB2 redistribution and phosphatase-dependent feedback;
- integration of growth-factor and integrin/adhesion signals;
- regulation of receptor internalization and trafficking;
- p66Shc-dependent oxidative-stress, ROS, mitochondrial-permeability, and apoptosis responses;
- context-dependent coupling to PI3K–AKT, cytoskeletal, and immune-receptor pathways. (wills2016…shcdeviance pages 44-47, wills2016…shcdeviance pages 21-24, yoshizawa2021biphasicspatiotemporalregulation pages 1-2)

Broad associations with cancer, aging, inflammation, metabolism, and neurodegeneration are plausible consequences of these proximal mechanisms, but they should not replace the more precise functional annotation above. Open Targets, for example, lists SHC1 associations with cancer, inflammatory bowel disease/Crohn disease, neurodegenerative disease, and leukocyte-count phenotypes; these are evidence aggregations rather than proof that SHC1 is a clinically actionable driver in every listed condition. (OpenTargets Search: -SHC1)

## 6. Recent developments and translational applications

### Glioblastoma

A May 2024 *Scientific Reports* study developed a nine-gene mitochondria-related prognostic model and examined SHC1/p66Shc in glioblastoma. TCGA-based analyses associated higher SHC1/p66Shc expression with shorter overall survival, progression-free interval, and disease-specific survival. The multivariable analysis included **111 patients**. In U87 cells, **72-hour p66Shc siRNA** treatment reduced proliferation and migration, increased apoptosis, and altered mitochondrial ROS and membrane-potential-related readouts. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-024-62083-2. (peng2024themitochondriarelatedgene pages 2-3, peng2024themitochondriarelatedgene pages 9-10)

This is mechanistically suggestive but not clinical validation. The work relies substantially on retrospective datasets and a limited cell-line system; the prognostic model, SHC1 assay, and p66Shc targeting strategy require independent prospective cohorts, isoform-specific human-tissue validation, and in-vivo efficacy and safety studies.

### Clear-cell renal-cell carcinoma

An August 2024 *Scientific Reports* study reported higher SHC1 RNA and protein in clear-cell renal-cell carcinoma, associations with poorer survival, and correlations with immune infiltration, immune escape, and predicted immunotherapy response. Experimental protein validation used paired tissues from only **nine patients**, plus renal cell lines. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-024-70897-3.

The study supports SHC1 as a candidate prognostic/immunological biomarker, but not as a validated clinical test. Most evidence was retrospective and bioinformatic, and the tissue-validation cohort was too small to establish generalizable diagnostic thresholds or treatment-selection utility.

### Non-obstructive azoospermia

A study published September 26, 2024 combined GEO datasets, WGCNA, machine learning, protein-interaction analysis, single-cell RNA sequencing, RT-PCR, and immunoblotting. SHC1 and FGFR1 emerged as immune/oxidative-stress-associated candidate biomarkers; SHC1 was enriched in testicular endothelial cells and elevated in non-obstructive azoospermia tissue. ROC analyses yielded **AUCs >0.8**. The computational network contained **24 transcription factors, 82 miRNAs, and 111 predicted drugs**. DOI: https://doi.org/10.3389/fendo.2024.1356959. (pan2024identificationandvalidation pages 1-2, pan2024identificationandvalidation pages 10-13)

These findings remain exploratory. The predicted drugs are not SHC1 therapies, and the AUCs derive from selected retrospective expression datasets rather than prospective clinical diagnosis. The authors’ finding is best viewed as a hypothesis about endothelial oxidative/immune dysregulation in impaired spermatogenesis.

### Current real-world implementation

No evidence retrieved here establishes an approved SHC1-directed drug, a routine clinical SHC1 assay, or a validated SHC1-based treatment-selection algorithm. Present applications are primarily research uses: pathway-state measurement, phosphoproteomics, receptor-signaling models, cancer biomarker discovery, oxidative-stress studies, and experimental perturbation of p66Shc. Quercetin and other compounds have been investigated as indirect modulators of Rac1/p66Shc-associated ROS signaling, but such results do not demonstrate selective SHC1 engagement or clinical efficacy.

## 7. Expert assessment and unresolved issues

1. **SHC1’s core annotation is secure.** It is a phosphotyrosine adaptor whose principal pathway role is receptor-to-GRB2/SOS/RAS–ERK coupling.
2. **Isoform resolution is essential.** Total SHC1 measurements conflate p46, p52, and p66, which differ in localization and may produce opposing mitogenic or stress responses.
3. **SHC1 is temporally active, not merely permissive.** Early p52Shc amplifies membrane RAS activation; later complexes can sequester GRB2 and redirect or terminate signaling. (yoshizawa2021biphasicspatiotemporalregulation pages 1-2)
4. **p66Shc localization remains conditional.** The traditional mitochondrial-intermembrane-space model is supported mechanistically, but 2024 fractionation indicates that much of the apparent mitochondrial pool resides at MAM/ER–mitochondria contact sites. (lebiedzinskaarciszewska2024distributionofthe pages 9-11, lebiedzinskaarciszewska2024distributionofthe pages 1-2)
5. **Disease associations exceed clinical evidence.** Recent cancer and infertility studies identify correlations and candidate biomarkers, but none yet supports routine implementation or direct pharmacological targeting.
6. **Direct adaptor targeting is difficult.** SHC1 lacks a conventional catalytic pocket, and systemic inhibition could disrupt many normal RTK, immune, and adhesion pathways. More plausible strategies may involve isoform-specific interfaces, inducible phosphotyrosine interactions, upstream receptor dependence, or p66-specific redox/localization mechanisms.

## Conclusion

Human SHC1/P29353 is best annotated as a **multidomain, noncatalytic phosphotyrosine adaptor and temporal signaling scaffold**. Its central physiological role is to translate activated receptor and adhesion signals into organized protein complexes, especially the GRB2–SOS–RAS–RAF–MEK–ERK pathway. p46/p52 primarily perform receptor-proximal signal relay, whereas p66 adds an oxidative-stress module involving ER/MAM and condition-dependent mitochondrial localization, ROS generation, and apoptosis. Recent 2024 research has sharpened the p66 localization model and identified candidate disease-biomarker roles, but SHC1 currently remains a research target rather than an established clinical biomarker or directly actionable therapeutic target.
