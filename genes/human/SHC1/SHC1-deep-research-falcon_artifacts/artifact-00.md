| Identity | **Human SHC1 (ShcA/SHCA), UniProt P29353** is the nonenzymatic phosphotyrosine-signaling adaptor specified in the query. It is distinct from human **SHC2/ShcB, SHC3/ShcC, and SHC4/ShcD**, and from similarly named nonhuman *shc* genes. The literature’s ShcA architecture and three isoforms align with the supplied UniProt identity. (pqac-00000002, pqac-00000003, pqac-00000004) | **Verified target match**; no conflicting same-symbol human protein was identified. |
| Isoforms | SHC1 produces **p46Shc, p52Shc, and p66Shc**. p46 and p52 arise through alternative translation initiation, whereas p66 is produced from a distinct transcript and carries an additional **110-aa N-terminal extension** containing CH2. (pqac-00000002, pqac-00000003, pqac-00000004) | Established molecular biology; isoforms can have opposing biological effects. |
| Architecture | Shared organization is **PTB–CH1–SH2**; p66 additionally has an N-terminal **CH2** region. The PTB domain preferentially recognizes receptor **NPXpY** contexts, while SH2 recognizes other phosphotyrosine motifs. CH1 contains the principal phosphorylated docking residues **Tyr239/Tyr240 and Tyr317** (p52 numbering), which recruit GRB2. (pqac-00000001, pqac-00000002, pqac-00000004) | Strong structural and biochemical support; the supplied InterPro PTB/PID, PH-like, and SH2 annotations are concordant. |
| Primary biochemical role | SHC1 is an **adaptor/scaffold, not an enzyme**: it has no demonstrated catalytic reaction or substrate turnover. Its primary role is to bind activated receptors or other phosphotyrosine-containing proteins, become phosphorylated, and assemble signaling complexes. (pqac-00000002, pqac-00000003, pqac-00000004) | Core functional annotation; “substrate specificity” is therefore inapplicable except as phosphopeptide-binding specificity. |
| Canonical RTK–RAS mechanism | Following activation of EGFR/ERBB or another RTK, SHC1 is recruited through PTB/SH2 interactions and phosphorylated in CH1. **GRB2’s SH2 domain binds phosphorylated SHC1; GRB2-associated SOS activates RAS-GDP→RAS-GTP, initiating RAF–MEK–ERK signaling.** Live-cell analysis found a biphasic role for p52Shc: during the first **<10 min**, it increased membrane docking sites for GRB2–SOS and amplified RAS activity; later, cytoplasmic SHC–GRB2 complexes sequestered GRB2 and helped terminate RAS activation. DOI: https://doi.org/10.2142/biophysico.bppb-v18.001 (pqac-00000012) | Strong mechanistic evidence in cultured cells; timing and output depend on receptor, ligand, cell type, and feedback state. |
| p66Shc redox role | Oxidative stress induces **Ser36** phosphorylation by PKCβ/JNK, conformational regulation and mitochondrial redistribution. In the mitochondrial intermembrane-space model, p66Shc interacts with cytochrome *c* through a redox region containing **132-EEW-134**, promotes H₂O₂ production and mitochondrial permeability, and favors apoptosis. Historical p66Shc-knockout mice reportedly lived about **30% longer** than wild-type littermates, although this should not be interpreted as a demonstrated human longevity effect. (pqac-00000014, pqac-00000017) | Established stress-response model, but localization and whether p66Shc should be classified as a direct redox enzyme remain context-dependent. |
| Localization | Basally, SHC1 isoforms are predominantly **cytosolic and ER-associated**; receptor stimulation recruits p46/p52 to the plasma membrane and receptor/endosomal complexes. p46 also has reported mitochondrial-matrix localization. A 2024 fractionation study found substantial p66Shc at **mitochondria-associated ER membranes (MAM)** rather than exclusively inside mitochondria: in control HepG2 cells, p46 and p66 contributed **25.175%** and **24.054%** of total ShcA, respectively; p66 signal fell about **50% at 0.5 µg/mL trypsin** and became undetectable at **2 µg/mL**, before mitochondrial markers were disrupted. Published **29 November 2024**; DOI: https://doi.org/10.3390/ijms252312835 (pqac-00000016, pqac-00000017) | Direct biochemical fractionation in HepG2 cells and mouse liver; results refine—not fully negate—the smaller stress-induced mitochondrial pool. |
| 2024 glioblastoma findings | TCGA/GTEx-based analyses associated elevated SHC1/p66Shc with worse survival, while **72-h p66Shc siRNA** in U87 cells reduced proliferation and migration, induced apoptosis, and altered mitochondrial ROS/potential readouts. The prognostic model used **nine mitochondria-related genes**, and multivariable analysis included **111 patients**. Published May 2024; DOI: https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-024-62083-2 (pqac-00000009, pqac-00000011) | **Preliminary biomarker/target evidence**, not a clinically validated diagnostic, prognostic assay, or approved SHC1-directed therapy; substantial reliance on retrospective datasets and cell lines. |
| 2024 clear-cell renal carcinoma findings | A multi-database study reported higher SHC1 expression in ccRCC and associations with poorer survival, immune infiltration/escape, and predicted immunotherapy response; protein findings were checked in paired material from only **nine patients** and in renal cell lines. Published August 2024; DOI: https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-024-70897-3 | **Preliminary translational association**, not clinical implementation; small experimental validation set and mainly retrospective/bioinformatic evidence. |
| 2024 non-obstructive azoospermia findings | SHC1 and FGFR1 were selected as immune/oxidative-stress hub genes; SHC1 was enriched in testicular endothelial cells and was elevated in NOA tissue by RT-PCR and immunoblotting. ROC analyses reported **AUCs >0.8**; the two-gene analysis predicted **24 transcription factors, 82 miRNAs, and 111 potential drugs**. Published **26 September 2024**; DOI: https://doi.org/10.3389/fendo.2024.1356959 (pqac-00000018, pqac-00000019) | **Exploratory biomarker discovery only**—not a validated NOA diagnostic or treatment. Dataset reuse, feature-selection procedures, and limited tissue validation require prospective replication. |
