Gene Description

AP3S1 encodes sigma3A, the small subunit of adaptor protein complex 3 (AP-3), the heterotetrameric coat adaptor that selects transmembrane cargo out of tubular sorting endosomes and delivers it to lysosomes and to lysosome-related organelles such as melanosomes, platelet dense granules and synaptic vesicles. AP-3 is built from two large chains (delta, encoded by AP3D1, plus beta3A or the neuron-enriched beta3B), a medium chain (mu3A or mu3B) and a small chain (sigma3A or sigma3B), so a cell can assemble several isoform combinations; sigma3A and sigma3B are both expressed in a wide range of tissues. The complex is recruited to membranes by Arf1-GTP through its delta subunit, associates with clathrin through the beta3 appendage, and is released again after the vesicle buds. Within the complex, sigma3A is not an inert filler. Cargo is selected through two classes of cytosolic sorting signal that bind different parts of AP-3: tyrosine-based YxxO motifs bind the mu3 subunit, while acidic dileucine motifs bind no single subunit but a composite site formed by the delta and sigma3 chains together. sigma3A supplies the hydrophobic pockets of that site and one of the two basic residues that grip the acidic position of the motif; substituting the pocket residues abolishes signal binding, and sigma3A is one of the four chains of the solved human AP-3 core, where the same pocket is found occupied by the N-terminal extension of beta3 in the cargo-free state. sigma3A pairs with delta as a stable hemicomplex that survives loss of the other two subunits. Cargoes routed this way include the lysosomal membrane proteins LAMP-1, LAMP-2, CD63 and LIMP-II, the melanosomal enzymes tyrosinase and OCA2, the zinc transporter ZnT3 and the lipid kinase PI4KIIalpha; HIV-1 Nef subverts the same site. Loss of AP-3 function is a disease mechanism in its own right. Mutations in the beta3A subunit cause Hermansky-Pudlak syndrome type 2, with oculocutaneous albinism, a bleeding diathesis from platelet dense-granule deficiency and immune defects, and the mouse mutants mocha and pearl, null for the delta and beta3A subunits, combine coat-colour dilution, platelet storage-pool deficiency and neurological deficits. No human disease has yet been attributed to AP3S1 itself, and the functional division of labour between sigma3A and sigma3B remains unresolved.

Proposed New Ontology Terms

dileucine sorting signal binding

Definition: Binding to an acidic dileucine sorting signal, a short peptide motif of the form acidic residue - X - X - X - Leu - Leu/Ile in the cytosolic tail of a transmembrane protein, which directs that protein into coated carriers at the trans-Golgi network, endosomes or the plasma membrane.

Justification: GO expresses the tyrosine-based branch of adaptor cargo recognition through GO:0089710 endocytic targeting sequence binding, whose definition requires an essential tyrosine and internalization by clathrin-coated pits, but it has no counterpart for the dileucine branch. Checked independently for this review: a QuickGO ontology search for 'dileucine' returns zero hits; searches for 'sorting signal binding' and 'targeting sequence binding' return only the tyrosine, signal- and transit-peptide receptor terms; and GO:0140312 cargo adaptor activity has exactly one child, GO:0035615. The consequence is that a well-characterised, subunit-attributable activity -- demonstrated for sigma3A by yeast three-hybrid, GST pull-down and point mutagenesis (V94D, L109S) across four independent signals -- can currently only be annotated through a complex-level coat-binding term. The term would apply to the sigma and large subunits of AP-1, AP-2 and AP-3 across eukaryotes, with contributes_to, since the site straddles the two subunits. On the choice of parent: GO:0005515 protein binding is normally a term to avoid and is used here deliberately, because GO:0089710 -- the tyrosine counterpart this proposal mirrors -- is itself is_a GO:0005515 (is_a ancestors GO:0005515, GO:0005488, GO:0003674, checked on QuickGO), so mirroring it places the two motif-binding siblings in one genus. The same term was proposed independently in this repository's AP1S1 review; this is the AP-3 half of the same gap, not a second request.

Core Functions

As the small subunit of AP-3, sigma3A forms the delta-sigma3A hemicomplex that recognises acidic dileucine sorting signals in the cytosolic tails of transmembrane cargo, capturing that cargo into AP-3-coated carriers budding from tubular sorting endosomes. The activity belongs to the hemicomplex, not to sigma3A alone -- these signals bind no single AP subunit -- but the hydrophobic pockets are on sigma3A itself and substituting them abolishes binding, and in AP-3 (unlike AP-1) sigma3A also supplies one of the two basic residues that grip the acidic position of the motif. The molecular_function slot records what sigma3A enables on its own: it is an obligate structural chain of the AP-3 core, pairing with delta as a stable hemicomplex. GO:0140312 is used rather than its only child GO:0035615, whose definition restricts it to the formation of endocytic vesicles -- which AP-3 does not do.

Suggested Questions for Experts

Q: Is there any cargo, tissue or trafficking step that requires sigma3A specifically and cannot be served by sigma3B? Both isoforms are broadly expressed, both delta-sigma3A and delta-sigma3B bind the LIMP-II dileucine tail, and the two proteins are identical at every residue named for the cargo-signal site, so nothing currently distinguishes them functionally.

Suggested experts: Juan S. Bonifacino, Esteban C. Dell'Angelica

Q: In the cargo-free human AP-3 structure the sigma3 dileucine pocket is occupied by the N-terminal extension of beta3. Is that an autoinhibited state that a dileucine cargo has to displace, and is displacement coupled to the tyrosine-motif site on mu3?

Suggested experts: Richard W. Baker

Q: The delta ear binds sigma3 and blocks Arf binding without affecting dileucine-signal binding. Is that interaction regulated in cells -- by phosphorylation, by cargo occupancy, or by a partner -- and does the reported phosphoserine at position 191 of sigma3A play any part in AP-3 recruitment?

Suggested experts: Juan S. Bonifacino

Q: Given that AP3S1 is ubiquitously expressed and essential to a complex whose loss causes Hermansky-Pudlak syndrome through other subunits, why has no human AP3S1 disease allele been reported? Is the gene intolerant of loss of function, or is sigma3B able to compensate?

Knowledge Gaps

What is not known — curated, literature-grounded statements of the open unknowns (the inverse of core functions).

Gap: Nothing is known that functionally separates sigma3A from sigma3B. The two paralogs are both broadly expressed, both delta-sigma3A and delta-sigma3B bind the LIMP-II dileucine tail, and no cargo, tissue or trafficking step has been shown to require one and not the other.

OPEN BIOLOGY RESIDUAL_SUBGAP

What is known: The shared properties are firmly established: both were identified together as AP-3 subunits and both are expressed in a wide variety of tissues and cell lines; the GO definition of the complex admits either; and the yeast three-hybrid comparison that tested them side by side found both hemicomplexes binding the same signal. The bioinformatics analysis in this review adds that the two proteins, 83.9% identical overall, are identical at every residue the literature names for the cargo-signal site. What has never been done is a side-by-side loss-of-function comparison in cells, or a test of sigma3B against the Nef signal, which does discriminate between the AP-1 and AP-3 large subunits.

Significance: GOA gives AP3S1 and AP3S2 near-identical annotation sets from the same references. That is currently an honest reflection of the evidence rather than a curation shortcut, but it means neither gene's annotation can be improved without new experiments, and any isoform-specific role -- including a possible explanation for why no human AP3S1 disease allele has been reported -- is invisible.

What would resolve it: Single and double AP3S1/AP3S2 knockouts in the same cell background, with isoform swap rescue, scored on complex assembly, membrane recruitment and cargo sorting.

Provenance (the field's own admissions):

  • "yeast three-hybrid analyses showed that the cytosolic tail of LIMP-II interacted with γ1–σ1A, δ–σ3A, and δ–σ3B (σ3A and σ3B are two isoforms of σ3), but not with αC–σ2, ɛ–σ4, μ3A, or the VHS domain of GGA1" — PMID:14691137
  • "Together, these experiments demonstrate that the LIMP-II cytosolic tail binds to the AP-1 and AP-3 complexes through γ1–σ1A and δ–σ3 (A or B isoforms)." — PMID:14691137
  • "Northern and Western blot analyses demonstrate that the products of both the sigma3A and sigma3B genes are expressed in a wide variety of tissues and cell lines." — PMID:9118953
  • "No sequence feature of the cargo-signal site separates sigma3A from sigma3B." — file:human/AP3S1/AP3S1-bioinformatics/RESULTS.md

Gap: It is not known what displaces the beta3 N-terminal extension from the sigma3 dileucine pocket, or whether that occupancy is a genuine autoinhibited state of human AP-3 rather than a feature of the reconstituted, cargo-free complex.

OPEN BIOLOGY MF_DARK

What is known: The structural observation is solid and unexpected: in the cargo-engaged human AP-3 structure the tyrosine motif is in the mu3 pocket while the dileucine pocket on sigma3 is filled by the N-terminal extension of beta3, and the authors offer the alternative that full activation requires ejection of that tail. AP-1 and AP-2 have the equivalent extensions and release them on activation. What is missing is any measurement of dileucine-peptide binding to the assembled complex with and without the beta3 extension, and any test of whether the two cargo sites are coupled.

Significance: If the beta3 tail is autoinhibitory, then sigma3A's cargo site is a regulated switch rather than a constitutively available pocket, and AP-3 cargo selection would be ordered rather than simultaneous. That changes what a contributes_to cargo-adaptor-activity annotation on sigma3A actually means in the assembled complex.

What would resolve it: Quantitative dileucine-peptide binding to reconstituted AP-3 with and without the beta3 N-terminal extension, plus a structure of the complex with a dileucine cargo bound.

Provenance (the field's own admissions):

  • "Surprisingly, the dileucine cargo-binding site on σ3 is occupied by the N-terminal extension of β3" — PMID:39705307
  • "Alternatively, this structure may represent a partially activated state, whereby full activation requires ejection on the β3 tail, potentially by engagement of a dileucine cargo motif." — PMID:39705307

Gap: Whether AP3S1 has any role outside AP-3 is undetermined. The one published claim of an independent activity -- that sigma3A acts as a membrane receptor for IRS-1 in adipocytes -- has never been tested again.

OPEN BIOLOGYCURATION BP_DARK

What is known: The original observation is specific: a sigma3A cDNA came out of an expression-library screen with IRS-1, GST-sigma3A bound IRS-1 in vitro, and adding GST-sigma3A released IRS-1 from low-density membranes. The authors framed the functional interpretation as a hypothesis. Since then the paper has accumulated 5 PMC-indexed citations, UniProt's SUBUNIT line for Q92572 records AGAP1 and BLOC-1 but not IRS1, and no AP-3-independent pool of sigma3A has been described. Against that, the assembly genetics show that sigma3 is left monomeric when delta is absent, so a free monomeric pool does exist in at least one genetic background.

Significance: GOA carries an insulin-receptor-signalling annotation on this gene that rests entirely on that single study. Either it should be supported by modern evidence or it should be retired; at present it is the only claim on the gene that does not connect to AP-3.

What would resolve it: Test IRS-1 association with endogenous sigma3A in adipocytes by proximity labelling or co-IP, in wild type and in AP3D1-null cells where sigma3 is monomeric, and ask whether insulin signalling output changes on AP3S1 knockout.

Provenance (the field's own admissions):

  • "These results are consistent with the hypothesis that sigma3A serves as an IRS-1 receptor that may dictate the subcellular localization and the signaling functions of IRS-1." — PMID:9792713
  • "A cDNA clone that encodes sigma3A, a small subunit of the AP-3 adaptor protein complex, was demonstrated to bind IRS-1 utilizing this cloning strategy." — PMID:9792713
  • "In mocha cells, the beta3 and mu3 subunits coassemble into a heterodimer, whereas the sigma3 subunit remains monomeric." — PMID:11807095

Gap: What decides whether a given acidic dileucine signal is read by the AP-1 gamma1-sigma1A hemicomplex or by the AP-3 delta-sigma3 hemicomplex is not understood, and the sigma-side contribution to that choice has not been isolated.

OPEN BIOLOGY MF_DARK

What is known: The phenomenon is well documented. Several cargoes bind both adaptors -- tyrosinase binds gamma1-sigma1A and delta-sigma3 -- while single-residue changes in an OCA2 signal can be engineered to favour one adaptor over the other, and only AP-3 binding gives steady-state melanosome localisation. Discrimination also runs in the other direction: of five Nef variants tested, all bound gamma1-sigma1A but only two bound delta-sigma3A. Mutagenesis has mapped the shared site onto all three complexes and shown which residues matter for which signal, but the pockets are nearly identical between sigma1A and sigma3A, so the determinant is most likely in the large subunit or in accessibility rather than in the sigma pocket itself. That has not been tested with chimeric hemicomplexes.

Significance: Adaptor choice is what routes a membrane protein to a lysosome rather than to a melanosome or back to the surface. Until the determinant is known, the specificity of a sigma subunit cannot be predicted from sequence -- which is exactly the inference an automated pipeline would attempt -- and AP-1 and AP-3 cargo assignments have to be made one cargo at a time.

What would resolve it: Test chimeric hemicomplexes pairing gamma1 with sigma3A and delta with sigma1A against a panel of signals with known adaptor preference, and ask which subunit carries the preference.

Provenance (the field's own admissions):

  • "Mutants that preferentially bound either AP-1 or AP-3 each trafficked toward melanosomes and functionally complemented OCA2 deficiency, but AP-3 binding was necessary for steady-state melanosome localization." — PMID:22718909
  • "We show that binding to AP-1 or AP-3 depends on the primary sequence of the signal and not its position within the cytoplasmic domain." — PMID:22718909
  • "All five Nefs bound to γ1–σ1A, whereas only the NLA4-3 and 248 Nefs bound to δ–σ3A" — PMID:14691137
  • "Both adaptors recognize the tyrosinase dileucine-based melanosome sorting signal, and tyrosinase largely colocalizes with each adaptor on endosomes." — PMID:16162817

Gap: The delta-ear/sigma3 interaction that gates AP-3 membrane recruitment has no known regulator: it is not known what relieves it in cells, nor where on sigma3A the ear binds.

OPEN BIOLOGY RESIDUAL_SUBGAP

What is known: The interaction itself is characterised and is unusually specific in its effect: the ear domain of delta binds sigma3, this blocks AP-3 binding to Arf while leaving dileucine-signal binding intact, and the isolated delta ear inhibits AP-3 recruitment to membranes in vitro and in vivo and impairs lysosomal membrane protein sorting. So the switch acts on the recruitment step, not on cargo recognition. What is missing is the binding surface on sigma3A, any physiological input that toggles it, and whether the phosphoserine UniProt records at sigma3A position 191 is involved.

Significance: If this is a regulated switch, sigma3A is not only a cargo-binding and structural subunit but also the target of AP-3's recruitment control, which would be a third, currently unannotatable role for the protein. It also matters for interpreting AP-3's constitutively open conformation: an open complex still has to be kept off membranes somehow.

What would resolve it: Map the delta-ear footprint on sigma3A, then make separation-of-function sigma3A mutants that lose ear binding but keep the cargo pocket, and test whether AP-3 becomes constitutively membrane-associated.

  • "This interaction interferes with the binding of AP-3 to Arf but not to dileucine-based sorting signals." — PMID:15469849

Current model (mechanistic narrative)

AP3S1 (CLAPS3) encodes a small clathrin-adaptor chain that functions as a subunit of the AP-3 adaptor complex in intracellular vesicular trafficking, and is expressed ubiquitously across human tissues PMID:8697810. 1. What the protein is

sigma3A is the small subunit of AP-3, the adaptor that sorts transmembrane cargo out of
tubular sorting endosomes towards lysosomes and lysosome-related organelles (LROs). The GO
definition of GO:0030123 names the subunit and both of its isoforms explicitly, which
settles the complex-membership rows without argument: "A heterotetrameric AP-type membrane
coat adaptor complex that consists of beta3, delta, mu3 and sigma3 subunits and is found
associated with endosomal membranes. AP-3 does not appear to associate with clathrin in all
organisms. In at least humans, the AP-3 complex can be heterogeneric due to the existence
of multiple subunit isoforms encoded by different genes (beta3A and beta3B, mu3A and mu3B,
and sigma3A and sigma3B)."

UniProt's FUNCTION is complex-level and reflects the 1997 literature:
CC -!- FUNCTION: Part of the AP-3 complex, an adaptor-related complex which is …
"It facilitates the budding of vesicles from the Golgi membrane and may be directly
involved in trafficking to lysosomes." The SUBUNIT line gives the composition and
partners: two large adaptins (AP3D1 plus AP3B1 or AP3B2), a medium adaptin (AP3M1 or
AP3M2), a small adaptin (AP3S1 or AP3S2), interaction with AGAP1, and association with
BLOC-1. Localisation is Golgi apparatus and cytoplasmic vesicle membrane, peripheral, on
the cytoplasmic side.

sigma3A and sigma3B were identified together in the paper that named AP-3
PMID:9118953,
and independently as subunits of the same complex in
PMID:9151686.

Where the complex acts has moved since 1997. Simpson's immunofluorescence put AP-3 "in the
Golgi region of the cell as well as with more peripheral structures"; the compartment that
survived is endosomal
PMID:15051738
and, in pigment cells,
PMID:16162817.

2. The molecular function: sigma3A is half of the AP-3 dileucine cargo-signal site

2.1 What transfers unchanged

Cargo signals come in two flavours and bind different parts of an AP complex. Tyrosine
YXXØ motifs bind the mu subunit. Acidic dileucine motifs bind no single subunit — they
bind the large-subunit/sigma hemicomplex:

  • PMID:21097499

The "not any single subunit" clause is what the contributes_to qualifier exists for, and
it is why the core function below uses contributes_to_molecular_function.

2.3 Where the AP-1 reasoning does not transfer

Three places:

  1. The GO term to use. The AP1S1 review annotated the hemicomplex activity through
    GO:0035615 clathrin-cargo adaptor activity, noting that the definition — "Bringing
    together a cargo protein with clathrin, responsible for the formation of endocytic
    vesicles" — names the wrong compartment for AP-1. For AP-3 that mismatch is worse, not
    equal: AP-3 is not an endocytic coat at all, it acts on endosomal exit
    PMID:15051738, and AP-3's clathrin association is itself conditional (the GO:0030123
    definition says AP-3 "does not appear to associate with clathrin in all organisms", and
    a beta3A point mutant with a dead clathrin box gives full functional rescue
    PMID:11807095).
    So this review uses the parent, GO:0140312 cargo adaptor activity, instead. I checked
    that GO:0035615 is GO:0140312's only child via the QuickGO children endpoint, so
    the parent is not a lazy generalisation — it is the most specific term in that lineage
    that does not assert endocytosis.
  2. The acidic-residue contact. In AP-1 it is mainly on the large subunit; in AP-3 both
    delta R26 and sigma3A R15 contribute (§2.2). The sigma subunit carries proportionally
    more of the AP-3 site than of the AP-1 site.
  3. A structure of the assembled complex exists for AP-3 too, and it says something the
    AP-1 structure did not.     In the human AP-3 cryo-EM structures the pocket is not empty
    and not occupied by cargo: PMID:39705307,
    with the authors proposing that "full activation requires ejection on the β3 tail,
    potentially by engagement of a dileucine cargo motif". The AP-1 structure
    (PDB 4P6Z) had a dileucine mimic bound in the sigma1 pocket; the AP-3 structure has an
    autoinhibitory occupant in the sigma3 pocket. Same site, opposite snapshot.

2.4 There is still no GO term for it

Checked independently rather than inherited from the AP1S1 review: QuickGO's ontology
search returns zero hits for dileucine; targeting sequence binding returns
GO:0089710 endocytic targeting sequence binding, whose definition requires "an essential
tyrosine (Y)" and internalization by clathrin-coated pits, i.e. the other motif class; and
the molecular-function children of GO:0140312 cargo adaptor activity are exactly one
term, GO:0035615. There is no dileucine counterpart. Hence a proposed_new_terms entry
and an ONTOLOGY knowledge gap, consistent with the one AP1S1 raised — the same term would
serve both genes and their large-subunit partners.

AP3S1 (sigma3A): the AP-3 dileucine pocket, and whether sigma3A differs from sigma3B

Reproduce with uv run python sigma3_pocket.py in this directory. Every sequence is
fetched live from the UniProt REST API; nothing is hardcoded, and each expected sequence
length is asserted so that a re-released canonical sequence aborts the run instead of
silently shifting every position. The unedited console output is kept in
RESULTS_raw.txt and the machine-readable form in results.json.

Questions

Human AP3S1 encodes sigma3A, the small subunit of the AP-3 adaptor. Two separable things
are tested here.

Q1. Does sigma3A carry the residues the primary literature names for it?
Acidic dileucine sorting signals — the (D/E)XXXL(L/I) motif — bind no single AP subunit.
They bind the gamma1-sigma1A, alphaC-sigma2 and delta-sigma3 hemicomplexes and not the
homologous epsilon-sigma4 hemicomplex of AP-4. The site was defined structurally on AP-2,
and Mattera et al. (PMID:21097499) transferred it onto AP-3 by mutating sigma3A itself:
V94D and L109S abolish signal binding, and Arg 15 and Leu 107 contribute. This asks
whether those positions really carry those residues in the current Q92572 sequence, and
whether an alignment computed here independently reproduces the sigma2-to-sigma3A
correspondences the paper asserts.

Q2. Can sigma3A be told apart from sigma3B (AP3S2) at this site?
GOA gives the two paralogs near-identical annotation sets, and PMID:14691137 found that
both delta-sigma3A and delta-sigma3B bind the LIMP-II dileucine tail. If the site is
identical between them, that interchangeability is a property of the proteins, not a
shortcut in curation.

Q3 additionally reports the identity between human AP3S1 and the mouse ortholog that
serves as the Ensembl-Compara and ISS donor for four of this gene's annotations.

Design

The anchor is human sigma2 (AP2S1, P53680), whose pocket is structurally defined and whose
residues were mutated in PMID:21097499. Anchor positions are mapped onto each paralog by
global pairwise alignment (Biopython PairwiseAligner, BLOSUM62, gap open -11 /
extend -1), and the mapping is cross-checked against the homologous positions the paper
states independently.

The design includes a verified negative control: sigma4 (AP4S1, Q9Y587) is in the same
PANTHER family, PTHR11753, but the epsilon-sigma4 hemicomplex of AP-4 was tested and does
not bind these signals. A conservation argument that cannot separate the verified
binders from the verified non-binder is not evidence, and including sigma4 is what makes
that testable rather than assumed.

Ten sequences: sigma3A (Q92572, target), sigma3B (P59780), sigma2 (P53680, anchor),
sigma1A/1B/1C (P61966, P56377, Q96PC3), sigma4 (Q9Y587, negative control), mouse Ap3s1
(Q9DCR2) and two PAINT IBD donors from the AP-3 clade — S. cerevisiae Aps3 (P47064) and
C. albicans Aps3 (Q59QC5).

retained fold — is as common as inferring loss from a missing residue, and this family is
a case where the fold genuinely does not predict the function.

The two fungal Aps3 sequences, which are PAINT IBD seeds for the family node that gives
AP3S1 its only IBA, also score 5/5, so the site is old.

Result 3 — sigma3A and sigma3B are indistinguishable at this site

Global identity between AP3S1 and AP3S2 is 83.9% over 193 aa (31 differing positions), and
all five literature-named pocket positions are identical between them (R15, V94, D98,
L107, L109 in both). No sequence feature of the cargo-signal site separates sigma3A from
sigma3B.

This is the sequence-level counterpart of the experimental result that both delta-sigma3A
and delta-sigma3B bind the LIMP-II dileucine tail (PMID:14691137), and it means that the
near-identical GOA annotation sets carried by AP3S1 and AP3S2 are a fair representation of
what is known rather than a failure to distinguish them. Whatever separates the two
paralogs functionally, it is not this site.

Result 4 — the mouse ortholog is sequence-identical

Human AP3S1 (Q92572) and mouse Ap3s1 (Q9DCR2) are both 193 aa and 100% identical, so
the four annotations this gene receives by Ensembl-Compara transfer or by curator ISS from
Q9DCR2 rest on an unusually safe orthology relation: there is no divergence anywhere in
the protein for the transfer to have to bridge.

Limits

  • Identity at a mapped position is not a structural argument. The pocket geometry was
    solved on AP-2, not on AP-3, and the AP-3 cryo-EM structures (PMID:39705307) show the
    sigma3 dileucine pocket occupied by the N-terminal extension of beta3 rather than by
    cargo, so the site's accessible state in the assembled complex is not settled here.
  • The global pairwise alignment is computed ad hoc rather than taken from a versioned
    resource. That is deliberate: the claims state both positions and both residues, so the
    alignment is only how the correspondence was discovered, and each residue is read
    directly from its own sequence.
  • Nothing here tests binding. The script cannot and does not assert that sigma3A binds a
    dileucine signal; it tests whether the residues the literature names are where the
    literature says they are, and whether conservation at that site is informative. The
    answer to the second is no.

  sorting signal that bind different parts of AP-3: tyrosine-based YxxO motifs bind the mu3 subunit, while
