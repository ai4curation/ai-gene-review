The core activity is low-voltage-activated, T-type calcium conductance across the plasma membrane. Calcium entry contributes to depolarization and firing behavior. Cardiac nodal functions are specialized physiological contexts of this conductance, rather than additional molecular mechanisms. The description distinguishes adult inherited SCA42 gating changes from particular severe childhood de novo gain-of-function variants. Disease associations are not interpreted as clinical recommendations.

## Channel experiments and the cloning correction

[PMID:10648811](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10648811/) characterizes a human brain T-type channel in an expression system. Its historical title names CaV3.1c. The [publisher's corrigendum](https://febs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1016/S0014-5793%2800%2901326-0) explicitly corrects the expressed cDNA to human CaV3.1a: an 18-residue exon in the original sequence figure was erroneous and absent from the expressed clone; the corrected accession is AF190860. The source PMID and fetched title remain unchanged. This correction prevents a false isoform inference without invalidating the calcium-channel electrophysiology. Historical clone nomenclature is not used to relabel the current 35-product UniProt inventory.

[PMID:9495342](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9495342/) describes a rat brain clone expressed in Xenopus oocytes and identifies/maps the human gene. The available abstract supports the T-type channel discovery but does not show a native human multiprotein preparation. The source TAS channel-complex assertion is retained with curator deference. A rat-focused abstract is not evidence that a human annotation was misattributed.

[PMID:26715324](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26715324/) tests a short human transcript, NM_198382.2/BC110995.1. Its c.5075G>A substitution corresponds to c.5144G>A in the longer NM_018896.4 transcript. The construct contains an IRES-EGFP reporter; it is not described here as a direct CaV3.1-GFP fusion. The whole-cell recordings use extracellular calcium and potassium-channel blockers. Wild-type channel activation occurs at negative potentials; the recurrent ataxia substitution shifts activation and steady-state inactivation positively. Current amplitude is reduced at the smaller depolarizations but not at the higher tested potentials. These results do not justify calling the mutant a null channel or the wild-type channel high-voltage activated.

That study observed both cytoplasmic and plasma-membrane immunoreactivity for wild-type and mutant channels, using NaK-ATPase as a membrane marker. Cytoplasmic localization is retained as an observed non-core pool; it is not converted to soluble cytosolic calcium-channel activity. The tested iPSC-derived Purkinje-cell differentiation and morphology showed no obvious difference. This result does not establish normal neuronal firing, which was not the endpoint of that differentiation analysis.

[PMID:26456284](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26456284/) independently identifies R1715H in inherited adult ataxia families and reports a positive activation shift in recombinant channels with modeling of altered neuronal excitability. Its available abstract and PMID:26715324 support altered gating in SCA42. In contrast, [PMID:29878067](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29878067/) studies A961T/M1531V in four children with severe cerebellar disease and finds slower inactivation and greater window current. Those findings support variant-specific gain of function in the childhood phenotype; they must not be generalized to all CACNA1G disease alleles.

## Caveolin association: the title is not the whole experiment

[PMID:21084288](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21084288/) foregrounds CaV3.2 but explicitly includes CaV3.1 co-immunoprecipitation with caveolin-3 and CaV3.1 current measurements. Its Figure 1 caption describes neonatal mouse ventricular-cell and transfected HEK293-cell assays, including negative controls. The scaffold-protein-binding IPI assertion is therefore retained as non-core association evidence. Direct GST-pulldown binding to caveolin's N terminus and the strong caveolin-dependent current/PKA modulation concern CaV3.2 in the visible evidence. Caveolin-3 coexpression did not alter peak CaV3.1 current density. Neither the paralog-specific regulatory effect nor a claim of absent CaV3.1 assays is transferred from the title.

## Cardiac context and remaining uncertainty

[PMID:16690884](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16690884/) reports that mouse Cacna1g disruption removes T-type current from isolated SAN and AV-node cells and slows pacemaking and AV conduction. The channel contributes the inward current, so this is direct electrical participation rather than necessity alone. The study also reports no altered right-atrial excitability or detectable T-type current in the sampled atrial myocytes. It supports nodal physiology while limiting an unqualified claim about working-myocyte contraction.

[PMID:21406960](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21406960/) further separates the contributions of CaV3.1 and CaV1.3 in mouse AV-node cells. Loss of CaV3.1 destabilizes pacing and limits high stimulated rates; loss of CaV1.3 abolishes spontaneous automaticity under the tested conditions. CaV3.1 is therefore not described as the sole generator of every AV-node action potential. The corresponding human rows retain their ISS provenance.

The checked ontology places SA-node action potential within [SA-node-to-atrial-cell signaling, GO:0086018](https://amigo.geneontology.org/amigo/term/GO%3A0086018), and AV-node action potential within [AV-node-to-His-cell signaling, GO:0086027](https://flybase.org/cgi-bin/cvreport.pl?childdepth=2&cvterm=GO%3A0086027&rel=is_a). A nodal calcium current can therefore contribute to these electrical-signaling processes without acting as a gap-junction pore or being expressed in the receiving cell. These tissue-specific assertions are retained as non-core. Local ontology definitions/OWL restrictions came from the 2026-03-25 OAK snapshot, cross-checked where accessible against official current term pages; the local snapshot is not claimed current.

Two decisions remain UNDECIDED. The [PMID:26786210 publisher full text](https://journals.biologists.com/dev/article/143/2/197/47400/The-formation-and-function-of-the-cardiac) was subsequently accessed, resolving the earlier access limitation. Its developmental section distinguishes regulated channel expression from morphogenesis. GO:0003163 remains unresolved. The ISS assertion for a cardiac-muscle action potential involved in contraction (GO:0086002) also needs its precise donor/developmental or working-myocyte context; nodal electrical function alone does not settle it. Neither uncertainty implies that CACNA1G lacks a cardiac role.
