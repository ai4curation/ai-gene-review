Gene Description

MTRNR2L1 is a nuclear human locus on chromosome 17 that resembles the humanin small open reading frame of the mitochondrial large-subunit rRNA gene MT-RNR2, one of thirteen such MT-RNR2-like sequences in the human genome that are generally interpreted as nuclear insertions of mitochondrial DNA. Its predicted product, humanin-like 1 (HN1), is a 24-residue peptide (MAPRGFSCLLLSTSEIDLPVKRRT) that matches humanin at 22 of 24 aligned positions, differing by Leu12->Ser and Ala24->Thr. The locus is transcribed: RT-PCR detects it in human tissues, most strongly in kidney, heart muscle and testis, and its transcript level changes after staurosporine and beta-carotene treatment of cultured cells (down-regulated 6 h and up-regulated 24 h after staurosporine, and down-regulated 6 h after beta-carotene). Whether a peptide is actually made from this transcript, and what it does, is unestablished: no HN1 peptide has been detected in human tissue or fluid, no molecular activity has been measured for it, and no binding partner or receptor interaction has been reported. The humanin peptide from which it is named is cytoprotective and antiapoptotic, binds BAX, BID, BIM and IGFBP3 intracellularly and signals through FPRL1/FPR2 and a CNTFR-alpha/WSX-1/gp130 complex when secreted, but those properties were measured on the mitochondrially encoded peptide and on synthetic analogues of it, not on this gene product.

Suggested Questions for Experts

Q: Is a peptide produced from the MTRNR2L1 locus at all? Everything annotated to this gene follows from the assumption that it is, and the assumption rests on an intact open reading frame plus RT-PCR detection of the transcript [PMID:19477263]. Targeted mass spectrometry of kidney for the HN1-specific tryptic peptides would settle it.

Suggested experts: Alan Saghatelian, Pinchas Cohen, Jonathan Wren

Q: How should GO treat a family in which one member has all the experimental evidence and twelve nuclear copies have none? The present solution propagates humanin's activity to every copy through PANTHER:PTN002141596, which produces thirteen gene products annotated as receptor antagonists and apoptosis regulators on the strength of one mitochondrial peptide. A node restricted to MT-RNR2 plus MTRNR2L5, the two sequences with functional data, would say what is actually known.

Q: Can any assay distinguish HN1 from humanin and from the other nuclear humanin-like peptides? HN1 matches humanin at 22 of 24 positions, so ELISA and antibody-based measurements of circulating humanin-like peptides - which exist in the clinical literature for other members of this family - cannot be assumed to be locus-specific. This question governs how all biomarker reports about this family should be read.

Suggested experts: Pinchas Cohen

Suggested Experiments

Experiment: Targeted (PRM) mass spectrometry of human kidney and of plasma for peptides unique to HN1, using the positions at which it differs from humanin (Leu12->Ser and Ala24->Thr) to design discriminating transitions. This is the one experiment that would convert the entire annotation set on this gene from inference to observation.

Experiment: Ribosome profiling or ORF-capture of the MTRNR2L1 transcript in a tissue where it is expressed, to test whether the predicted ORF is translated rather than merely transcribed.

Experiment: Synthesise the HN1 peptide and run it side by side with humanin in the assays that define humanin activity - staurosporine- and amyloid-beta-induced death, FPRL1 competition, BAX binding - as was done for HN5 alone [PMID:19477263]. Side-by-side potencies would show whether the shared annotations are warranted and would put numbers on the contribution of the substituted residues.

Experiment: CRISPR deletion of the MTRNR2L1 locus in a human cell line that expresses it, with apoptosis challenge, to test whether the locus contributes to cytoprotection as a gene rather than as a synthetic peptide. The thirteen near-identical loci make guide design the hard part, and off-target cutting at the paralogs is the control that matters.

The family, and why these seven reviews look alike

Humanin is a 24-residue peptide encoded by a small open reading frame inside the
mitochondrial 16S rRNA gene MT-RNR2, and it is the member of this family with all the
experimental evidence: cytoprotection, BAX/BID/BIM binding, FPRL1/FPR2 engagement, the
CNTFR-alpha/WSX-1/gp130 receptor complex. Bodzioch et al. 2009 (PMID:19477263, verified -
Genomics 94:247-256) found thirteen MT-RNR2-like sequences in the nuclear genome whose
reading frames would encode humanin-like peptides HN1-HN13
PMID:19477263, and showed that at least ten are transcribed and respond to
staurosporine and beta-carotene PMID:19477263.

That paper is the single primary source behind every UniProt MTRNR2L entry, and its own
title calls the functionality potential. UniProt agrees, and says so in a CAUTION line on
each entry: the active peptide is the product of the mitochondrial gene, and whether any of
the nuclear loci makes a physiologically active peptide is open. A 2022 review restates the
position PMID:35372353.

humanin, by sequence similarity or by phylogenetic descent, and none to an observation made on
the locus itself. The exception is MTRNR2L5, where synthetic HN5 peptide was assayed
PMID:19477263.

Residues: where similarity stops being an argument

Humanin has an unusually complete mutagenesis map (UniProt Q8IVG9). Positions where a single
substitution abolishes neuroprotective activity: Pro3, Ser7, Cys8, Leu9, Leu12, Thr13, Ser14,
Pro19. Secretion needs Leu9-Leu11 and Pro19-Val20
PMID:35372353.
Bodzioch et al. reached the same two positions from the alignment
PMID:19477263.

This is what makes the reviews differ from one another:

  • Pro19 lost (L3, L4, L7): the secretion claim is actively doubtful, by humanin's own secretion
    determinants and by Bodzioch's position-19 criterion. The two extracellular rows are REMOVEd.
  • Thr13/Ser14 lost (L4: T13V; L7: T13I + S14R + S7G): the functional rows lose their premise.
    Ser14->Arg is literally one of the humanin substitutions annotated as abolishing activity, so L7's
    two functional rows are REMOVEd. L4 keeps Ser14 and its T13 change parallels the HN5 Thr13->Ile
    variant, which reduces rather than abolishes protection, so its functional rows are marked
    over-annotated instead.
  • Everything else: MARK_AS_OVER_ANNOTATED. Not wrong, not demonstrated, and asserted of a gene
    product that has never been shown to exist.

Note the counterweight that stops this becoming a blanket purge: synthetic HN5, which carries the
same Leu12->Ser substitution as most of the set, is cytoprotective. So Leu12 divergence is a
caution, not a refutation, and the family-wide antiapoptotic claim is plausible - just untested
per locus.

Negative and orthogonal evidence

A lookup across all thirteen loci in CARDIoGRAMplusC4D found no significant association with
coronary artery disease and no significant expression QTL
PMID:31753007. That does not show the loci are inert, but it is the only genetics-scale
evidence covering them.

Methodological caution that applies to every clinical report on this family

Serum "humanin-like 3" and "humanin-like 10" ELISAs exist and are used
PMID:41777515, and
antibody-based detection of MTRNR2L2 has been reported
PMID:38221658. These peptides are 24-28 residues long
and match humanin at 16-22 of 24 positions. Locus-specific immunodetection has not been demonstrated,
so none of these measurements is used here as evidence that a particular locus makes a peptide.

- question: Is a peptide produced from the MTRNR2L1 locus at all? Everything annotated to this gene
    follows from the assumption that it is, and the assumption rests on an intact open reading frame
    plus RT-PCR detection of the transcript [PMID:19477263]. Targeted mass spectrometry of kidney

- question: How should GO treat a family in which one member has all the experimental evidence and
    twelve nuclear copies have none? The present solution propagates humanin's activity to every
    copy through PANTHER:PTN002141596, which produces thirteen gene products annotated as receptor
    antagonists and apoptosis regulators on the strength of one mitochondrial peptide. A node
    restricted to MT-RNR2 plus MTRNR2L5, the two sequences with functional data, would say what is
    actually known.
- question: Can any assay distinguish HN1 from humanin and from the other nuclear humanin-like
    peptides? HN1 matches humanin at 22 of 24 positions, so ELISA and antibody-based measurements of
    circulating humanin-like peptides - which exist in the clinical literature for other members of
    this family - cannot be assumed to be locus-specific. This question governs how all biomarker
    reports about this family should be read.

  - Pinchas Cohen
