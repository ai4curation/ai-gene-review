## Biological evidence and assay limits

Direct human evidence places FOXP2 in transcriptional regulation. In [PMID:18987363](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2756409/), selected Methods/Results paragraphs 29–48 and Figures 1–2 captions describe CNTNAP2 intron-1 occupancy, sequence-specific binding in nuclear-extract EMSA and reduced CNTNAP2 RNA. The human 503–584 forkhead fragment in [PMID:16407075](https://pure.mpg.de/rest/items/item_1555024/component/file_2261109/content), Results/Figures 1–5 and sample-preparation/MALS Methods, printed pp. 160–165, provides structural DNA-recognition and fragment domain-swapping evidence. It does not establish DNA looping by native full-length FOXP2.

FOXP2 regulation depends on target and cellular context. [PMID:25027557](https://www.yorku.ca/dakc/Dorota%20-%20RESEARCH%20ARTICLES/Sin%20Li%20and%20Crawford%202014-Transcriptional%20Regulaton%20by%20FOXP1%20FOXP2%20and%20FOXP4%20Dimerization.pdf), Methods pp. 1–3, Results pp. 3–4, 6, 8 and Figures 1–2 captions, reports tagged family association, nuclear colocalization and target RNA changes in both directions. Its BC143867 construct is described as 714 residues and is not assigned to the current 715-aa product. Human WT FOXP2 in [PMID:27933109](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5126810/), construct/BRET/luciferase Methods and Results/Figures 1–3, represses SV40/SRPX2 reporters and associates with FOXP2, FOXP1, FOXP4 and CTBP1/2 in HEK293 assays. These observations support the retained repressor core while allowing target-dependent activation; they do not measure isolated binding affinity or establish natural-product-specific functions. Selected RNA-seq/ATAC-seq Methods and Results in [PMID:31067457](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6794152/) likewise show activation and repression after exogenous FOXP2 expression in human neuronal cultures, without establishing endogenous cortical necessity.

Developmental findings are interpreted at their measured scope. Human KE-family MRI in [PMID:11872605](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/11489179_MRI_analysis_of_an_inherited_speech_and_language_disorder_Structural_brain_abnormalities), Subjects/statistical Methods, Tables 1–2 and Results, printed pp. 467–471, identifies caudate reduction and bilateral putamen increase; the cohort and statistical thresholds limit mechanistic inference. Mouse cortical mosaic knockdown with human rescue in [PMID:23283338](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6618635/), selected cortical electroporation/precursor Methods and Results, differs from the early Emx1 and later Ntsr1 conditional-deletion models in [PMID:31099752](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6561705/), selected Methods/Results/Discussion and Figures 2–4 captions. Their different outcomes support context-dependent cortical roles rather than a universal requirement for the measured histogenesis endpoints.

The esophageal skeletal/smooth-muscle observations in [PMID:17428829](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/6399310_Foxp2_and_Foxp1_cooperatively_regulate_lung_and_esophagus_development), Methods lines 251–295 and esophageal Results/Figures 7–8 lines 960–1110, concern compound Foxp2-null/Foxp1-heterozygous mice. They do not isolate a human or FOXP2-only mechanism. [PMID:22133810](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22133810/) remains limited to its authentic abstract and current mouse experimental annotation: R552H knock-in ultrasonic-vocalization and cerebellar findings are not a direct human language assay, and the original Methods remain unavailable in the selected reading.
