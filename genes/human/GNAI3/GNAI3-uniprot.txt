CC   -!- FUNCTION: Heterotrimeric guanine nucleotide-binding proteins (G
CC       proteins) function as transducers downstream of G protein-coupled
CC       receptors (GPCRs) in numerous signaling cascades. The alpha chain
CC       contains the guanine nucleotide binding site and alternates between an
CC       active, GTP-bound state and an inactive, GDP-bound state. Signaling by
CC       an activated GPCR promotes GDP release and GTP binding. The alpha
CC       subunit has a low GTPase activity that converts bound GTP to GDP,
CC       thereby terminating the signal (By similarity). Both GDP release and
CC       GTP hydrolysis are modulated by numerous regulatory proteins
CC       (PubMed:18434541, PubMed:19478087, PubMed:8774883). Signaling is
CC       mediated via effector proteins, such as adenylate cyclase. Inhibits
CC       adenylate cyclase activity, leading to decreased intracellular cAMP
CC       levels (PubMed:19478087). Stimulates the activity of receptor-regulated
CC       K(+) channels (PubMed:2535845). The active GTP-bound form prevents the
CC       association of RGS14 with centrosomes and is required for the
CC       translocation of RGS14 from the cytoplasm to the plasma membrane. May
CC       play a role in cell division (PubMed:17635935). The active GTP-bound
CC       form activates the calcium permeant TRPC5 ion channels
CC       (PubMed:37137991). {ECO:0000250|UniProtKB:P08753,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:17635935, ECO:0000269|PubMed:18434541,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:2535845, ECO:0000269|PubMed:37137991,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:8774883}.
CC   -!- SUBUNIT: Heterotrimeric G proteins are composed of 3 units; alpha, beta
CC       and gamma. The alpha subunit contains the guanine nucleotide binding
CC       site (By similarity). GTP binding causes dissociation of the
CC       heterotrimer, liberating the individual subunits so that they can
CC       interact with downstream effector proteins. Forms a complex with
CC       CCDC88A/GIV and EGFR which leads to enhanced EGFR signaling and
CC       triggering of cell migration; ligand stimulation is required for
CC       recruitment of GNAI3 to the complex (PubMed:20462955). Interacts
CC       (inactive GDP-bound form) with CCDC88A/GIV (via GBA motif); the
CC       interaction leads to activation of GNAI3 (PubMed:19211784,
CC       PubMed:20462955). Interacts (inactive GDP-bound form) with
CC       CCDC88C/DAPLE (via GBA motif); the interaction leads to activation of
CC       GNAI3 (PubMed:26126266). Interacts (inactive GDP-bound form) with NUCB1
CC       (via GBA motif) and NUCB2 (via GBA motif); the interaction leads to
CC       activation of GNAI3 (By similarity). Interacts (inactive GDP-bound
CC       form) with PLCD4 (via GBA motif); the interaction leads to activation
CC       of GNAI3 (PubMed:30194280). Interacts with INSR; the interaction is
CC       probably mediated by CCDC88A/GIV (PubMed:25187647). Interacts with
CC       GPSM1 (By similarity). Interacts (GDP-bound form) with GPSM2 (via
CC       GoLoco domains) (PubMed:22952234). Does not interact with RGS2
CC       (PubMed:19478087). Interacts with RGS8 and RGS10; this strongly
CC       enhances the intrinsic GTPase activity (PubMed:18434541,
CC       PubMed:8774883). Interacts with RGS16; this strongly enhances the
CC       intrinsic GTPase activity (PubMed:19478087). Interacts with RGS12 (By
