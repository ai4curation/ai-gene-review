Gene Description

AKAP10, also called dual-specific A-kinase anchoring protein 2 (D-AKAP2), is a 662-residue scaffold protein with no catalytic domain, no nucleotide- or metal-binding site and no EC number. Its chain is built from three interaction modules separated by disordered linkers: two tandem regulator-of-G-protein-signalling (RGS) domains in the N-terminal half (residues 125-369 and 379-505), an amphipathic A-kinase-binding helix near the C-terminus (residues 629-653), and a class-I PDZ-binding motif at the extreme C-terminus. Its defining activity is anchoring cAMP-dependent protein kinase. The C-terminal helix docks into the dimerization/docking domain of the protein kinase A regulatory subunit, and it does so for both type I and type II regulatory subunits, which is what makes it dual-specific; the two complexes differ in the register with which the same helix packs against the R-subunit dimer. Only the regulatory subunit has ever been assayed, never the catalytic one. Immediately downstream of that helix, the PDZ-binding motif engages PDZ-domain scaffolds, PDZK1 most strongly, and the two motifs become ordered only on binding, so a single short segment presents an alpha-helix to the kinase and a beta-strand to the PDZ domain. Because formation of the kinase complex precedes high-affinity PDZ engagement, AKAP10 nucleates a polyvalent assembly that reaches membrane transporters indirectly, through the PDZ scaffold, rather than by contacting a membrane protein itself. The tandem RGS domains do not act as GTPase activators on heterotrimeric G-protein alpha subunits: the Galpha transition-state contact residues of a canonical RGS box are absent from both domains, and no activation of a heterotrimeric G protein has ever been measured. What the two domains do, acting as a single functional unit, is bind the small GTPases Rab4 and Rab11 - Rab4 preferentially in its GTP-bound state. Through this surface AKAP10 associates with Rab4/Rab11-positive recycling endosomes and retains cargo there: depleting it redistributes Rab11 and the transferrin receptor toward the cell periphery and accelerates transferrin recycling, so the protein normally slows traffic out of the endocytic recycling compartment. Where the protein sits is genuinely contested. Endogenous AKAP10 is reported as highly enriched in mitochondria in human, mouse and rat tissue, and it appears in high-confidence human mitochondrial proteomes; a later study in HeLa cells found the endogenous protein almost entirely cytosolic with very little mitochondrial colocalisation, and attributed the discrepancy to antibody cross-reactivity or cell type. The N-terminal mitochondrial transit peptide that gives the protein its name is a sequence-based prediction, and the entry contains no transmembrane segment and no lipid-binding domain, so the mechanism of membrane attachment is unresolved. A common coding variant, Ile646Val in the A-kinase-binding helix, is carried by roughly 40 percent of human alleles and is associated in population data with a higher basal heart rate and reduced heart-rate variability; the molecular basis of that association is not known.

Core Functions

Anchors cAMP-dependent protein kinase by binding its regulatory subunit. An amphipathic helix at residues 629-653 docks into the dimerization/docking domain of the PKA regulatory subunit, and it does so for both type I and type II subunits, which is the property the D-AKAP name records. The dual specificity is not a loose one: the same helix binds RI-alpha and RII-alpha in different helical registers, and the original description found the active fragment bound RI-alpha, RII-alpha and RII-beta but not RI-beta. Only the regulatory subunit has ever been assayed, which is why the molecular function is the regulatory-subunit child term rather than the parent. The consequence, and the reason an AKAP exists, is that the bound kinase is placed somewhere. For this protein that is recorded at the level of intracellular protein localization and of protein-membrane adaptor activity, transferred from the mouse orthologue's own direct evidence for in vivo PKA association.

Current model (mechanistic narrative)

AKAP10 (D-AKAP2) is a dual-specific A-kinase anchoring protein that organizes compartmentalized PKA signaling at mitochondria and the endocytic recycling system [PMID:11248059, PMID:19797056]. It anchors both type I (RIα) and type II (RIIα) PKA regulatory subunits through a C-terminal amphipathic helix that engages the dimerization/docking domains of each subunit, with dual specificity arising from a register shift in how the AKAP helix packs against RIα versus RIIα [PMID:9326583, PMID:20159461]. Beyond PKA, AKAP10 nucleates a polyvalent membrane scaffold: a C-terminal PDZ-binding motif recruits PDZK1 (and to a lesser extent NHERF-1), and high-affinity PDZK1 engagement requires prior formation of the AKAP10:PKA complex, coupling kinase anchoring to transporter regulation [PMID:14531807, PMID:25348485]. Its N-terminal tandem RGS domains bind GTP-loaded Rab4 and Rab11 and promote accumulation of recycling cargo such as the transferrin receptor in the Rab4/Rab11-positive recycling compartment PMID:19797056. In vivo, disruption of the AKAP10 C-terminus in mice heightens cholinergic cardiac responses and produces arrhythmias, establishing AKAP10 as a regulator of cardiac rhythm through the autonomic signaling pathway PMID:17485678.

AKAP10 RGS domains: are the Galpha GAP contact residues present?

Generated by rgs_gap_residues.py (run python rgs_gap_residues.py to regenerate;
a fresh run must reproduce this file and results.json exactly).

Question

UniProt annotates two RGS domains in AKAP10 (FT DOMAIN 125..369 "RGS 1",
379..505 "RGS 2", both ECO:0000255). An RGS domain name implies
GTPase-activator activity on heterotrimeric Galpha subunits, which is a catalytic
claim. This tests whether the residues that make an RGS box a Galpha GAP are
present in AKAP10.

Method

Each domain is aligned (BLOSUM62, global, gap -11/-1) to the RGS box of human
RGS4 (P49798, residues 68-184). Five RGS4 positions that the
RGS4:Gi-alpha-1 structure and mutagenesis identify as Galpha transition-state
contacts are scored: S85, E87, N88, N128, R167.
RGS4 Asn128 is decisive — N128A abolishes GAP activity without unfolding the
domain. A site counts as retained only when the alignment column is a true
aligned pair (no gap on either side) and the residue is identical.

Controls run in the same pass: RGS16 and RGS9 (established Galpha GAPs, must
score 5/5 or the script aborts) and AXIN1 (an RGS fold that is not a Galpha
GAP).

Result

domain role identity vs RGS4 box contacts retained residue aligned to RGS4 N128 N128 retained
AKAP10 RGS-A subject 30.3% 0/5 P315 no
AKAP10 RGS-B subject 24.3% 0/5 G452 no
RGS16 positive control (Galpha GAP) 56.6% 5/5 N131 yes
RGS9 positive control (Galpha GAP) 38.0% 5/5 N367 yes
AXIN1 negative control (RGS fold, no Galpha GAP) 31.5% 0/5 gap no

Reading

Both AKAP10 RGS domains score 0/5, exactly like the AXIN1 negative control
and unlike both positive controls, and the decisive asparagine is replaced by
proline in RGS-A and glycine in RGS-B. The fold is present; the Galpha catalytic
machinery is not.

Caveat stated rather than hidden: RGS-B sits at 24.3% identity, below the range
where a residue-level call is safe on alignment alone. RGS-A at 30.3% is directly
comparable to the AXIN1 control at 31.5%, and the RGS-A N128->Pro substitution is
independently disruptive. The computational result therefore corroborates, and
does not replace, the two published statements: that not all of the charged
residues conserved in RGS proteins are conserved in D-AKAP2 (PMID:9326583), and
that "D-AKAP2 has so far demonstrated no specific interactions with heterotrimeric
G proteins, and no ability to activate them" (PMID:19797056).

Conclusion

No GTPase-activator (GO:0005096) or Galpha-binding annotation is warranted for
AKAP10 from its RGS domains. GOA carries none, so this is recorded as a
hypothesis that did not confirm rather than as a defect found.
