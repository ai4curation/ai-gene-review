| Identity/topology | Human TAS2R16 is a 291-aa TAS2R-family bitter-sensing GPCR with characteristic seven-transmembrane architecture; heterologously expressed receptor reaches the cell surface. (pqac-00000008, pqac-00000010, pqac-00000013, pqac-00000014) | Direct human TAS2R16 mutagenesis and trafficking assays; topology also supported by TAS2R-family analysis | **High.** Identity and length are direct; complete topology is partly family-level inference in the cited excerpts. |
| Primary ligands | TAS2R16 preferentially detects β-glycosides, including salicin, arbutin, and aromatic, aliphatic, charged, or sulfur-linked analogues; glucose alone is inactive. Salicin had an EC50 of 1.2 mM, while selected agonists ranged from approximately 0.65 to 2.5 mM in one assay. (pqac-00000000, pqac-00000004, pqac-00000005) | Direct human-receptor calcium-flux concentration-response assays | **High.** Potencies depend on the assay and engineered G-protein coupling. |
| Binding pocket | A broad, two-faced pocket formed mainly by TM3 and TM7 accommodates a conserved carbohydrate group and diverse aglycones. Screening 573 mutants identified 13 ligand-selectivity residues and 38 signaling-critical residues; W261 and E262 are especially important. (pqac-00000000, pqac-00000001, pqac-00000003) | Direct mutagenesis plus homology-model interpretation | **High** for residue effects; **moderate** for atomic contacts because no experimental TAS2R16 structure was available. |
| Canonical oral signaling | Bitter-receptor signaling generally couples gustducin or GNAT3 to PLCβ2, IP3-dependent Ca2+ release, and TRPM5 activation. TAS2R16 directly produces calcium responses and couples to a gustducin-tail chimera, but the complete native cascade has not been demonstrated specifically for this receptor in the cited experiments. (pqac-00000011, pqac-00000013, pqac-00000014) | Direct human TAS2R16 heterologous signaling plus TAS2R-family and taste-cell pathway inference | **Moderate-high.** Several downstream steps are family-level inference rather than direct endogenous TAS2R16 evidence. |
| Oral localization/function | TAS2R16 belongs to the bitter-receptor repertoire of taste cells and detects dietary β-glycosides, contributing to bitter perception, avoidance, and food choice. (pqac-00000004, pqac-00000009) | Human receptor pharmacology plus TAS2R-family taste-cell expression | **High** for β-glycoside detection; **moderate** for TAS2R16-specific apical localization because direct endogenous imaging was not supplied. |
| Sinonasal localization/innate immunity | TAS2R16 is expressed by human sinonasal epithelial cells. Studies place it in epithelial cilia and report salicin-evoked, dose-dependent nitric-oxide production through taste-signaling machinery, supporting ciliary and antimicrobial defense. (pqac-00000018) | Direct human primary-cell localization and agonist-response evidence summarized by an authoritative review | **Moderate-high.** Patient therapeutic benefit and receptor-specific genetic effects remain unproven. |
| Gingival anti-inflammatory signaling | Human gingival fibroblasts express TAS2R16 with gustducin and PLCβ2. Salicin evokes cytosolic Ca2+ and suppresses LPS-induced cytokine release, cAMP elevation, and NF-κB p65 nuclear translocation. (pqac-00000021) | Direct human cell-culture pharmacology | **Moderate.** Mechanistic in vitro evidence exists, but clinical efficacy in periodontitis has not been demonstrated. |
| Neuroblastoma neurite outgrowth | In human SH-SY5Y neuroblastoma cells, salicin promoted neurite outgrowth through a mechanism reported to involve TAS2R16 and ERK signaling. (pqac-00000017, pqac-00000020) | Direct human cell-line perturbation evidence summarized secondarily | **Moderate-low.** Confirmation in primary neurons or in vivo tissue is needed; receptor-independent salicin effects remain possible. |
| Genetics | The N172/K172 difference affects trafficking and β-glycoside sensitivity: N172 showed approximately twofold greater surface expression and sensitivity than K172. Other variants and haplotypes have observational associations with longevity, periodontitis, and other disorders. (pqac-00000006, pqac-00000016, pqac-00000019) | Direct variant-function assays plus observational human association studies | **High** for the N172/K172 cellular phenotype; **low-moderate** for disease associations because replication and causal evidence are limited. |
| Translational status | Proposed uses include bitter masking, sinonasal innate-immune modulation, and topical control of gingival inflammation. Antagonists provide tool-compound starting points, while serum and genetic studies explore possible biomarkers. (pqac-00000016, pqac-00000019) | Preclinical pharmacology and exploratory observational studies | **Early/preclinical.** No TAS2R16-selective therapy, validated biomarker, or receptor-directed clinical implementation is established. |
