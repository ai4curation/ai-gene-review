Gene Description

Six-pass transmembrane protein of the trans-Golgi network, encoded at Xq28 and formerly known as CXorf12 or ITBA1. It is ubiquitously expressed, has no recognisable catalytic, transport or adaptor domain, and defines a protein family of its own (Pfam PF15100). TMEM187 binds the small GTPase RAB11A, preferentially in its GDP-bound form, and acts as a negative regulator of endosomal recycling: in its presence RAB11A is less available to its guanine-nucleotide exchange factor GRAB, recycling of transferrin receptor 1 back to the plasma membrane is slowed, and cellular iron uptake is held down. In erythroid cells this sets a brake on differentiation. Loss of TMEM187 in human erythroleukaemia lines accelerates iron uptake and triggers erythroid differentiation without inducing agents, leading to premature maturation with phosphatidylserine exposure, membrane fragility and increased macrophage clearance; zebrafish lacking tmem187 show enhanced early erythropoiesis, and forced expression of human TMEM187 in mouse haematopoietic stem cells, in an organism with no endogenous orthologue, compromises erythropoiesis and produces moderate anaemia. The molecular activity through which TMEM187 achieves this is not established: binding to RAB11A is demonstrated, but whether it acts by occluding the GEF, by sequestering RAB11A in a membrane subdomain, or by some other means has not been determined.

Core Functions

TMEM187 sits in the trans-Golgi network membrane as a six-pass integral membrane protein and acts as a negative regulator of RAB11A-dependent endosomal recycling, and through it of erythroid differentiation. It binds RAB11A, preferentially in the GDP-bound state, and in its presence less transferrin receptor 1 returns to the plasma membrane and less iron is taken up; removing TMEM187 releases that brake and drives premature erythroid differentiation and maturation. No molecular_function is asserted here, deliberately. What is demonstrated at the molecular level is binding to RAB11A and nothing more; that is recorded as a small GTPase binding annotation in existing_annotations, but binding is not an activity. The published model - that TMEM187 occupies GDP-RAB11A and thereby competes with the exchange factor GRAB - is consistent with the data but has not been tested with a nucleotide-exchange assay, and the paper itself notes that the nucleotide-state preference it observes runs opposite to earlier reports of GRAB's own preference. Nothing supports an adaptor, cargo-receptor, transporter or channel assignment either: no cargo binds TMEM187, TfR1 is affected downstream of RAB11A rather than by contact, and no permeation or transport assay has been performed despite the six transmembrane helices. An omitted molecular function states that it is not established; a plausible-sounding one would state something false.

Suggested Questions for Experts

Q: Does TMEM187 inhibit GRAB-catalysed nucleotide exchange on RAB11A directly, or does it restrain recycling some other way? This is the single experiment that would convert TMEM187's GO molecular function from a binding term into an activity. The competition model currently rests on co-immunoprecipitation, colocalisation and expression changes, not on a measured exchange rate.

Suggested experts: Kai Li, Yang Liu

Q: Is the observed preference of TMEM187 for GDP-bound RAB11A (S25N) reconcilable with the reported behaviour of GRAB? The authors note that their nucleotide-state preference for GRAB is the opposite of what earlier axon-outgrowth studies found, which matters because the whole competition model depends on both proteins contending for the same RAB11A conformer.

Suggested experts: Kai Li

Q: Should any molecular-function term be asserted for TMEM187 at this stage, or is small GTPase binding (GO:0031267) the correct ceiling? The cellular component and biological process are now reasonably clear, which creates pressure to supply a matching MF - but adaptor, cargo receptor and transporter are all unsupported.

Q: Does TMEM187 act outside the erythroid lineage? It is ubiquitously expressed and X-linked, yet mice have lost the gene entirely and all functional data come from erythroid systems.

Suggested experts: Kai Li, Yvette Y Yien

Suggested Experiments

Experiment: Purify RAB11A and the GRAB (RAB3IL1) GEF domain, and reconstitute TMEM187 into detergent micelles or nanodiscs. Measure GDP release and GTP loading on RAB11A (mant-nucleotide fluorescence or filter-binding) with and without TMEM187, over a TMEM187 concentration series, and test whether a TMEM187 mutant that fails to co-immunoprecipitate RAB11A also fails to inhibit exchange.

Hypothesis: TMEM187 restrains RAB11A activation by occluding the surface that its GEF GRAB engages, so TMEM187 should slow GRAB-catalysed GDP release from RAB11A in a reconstituted system.

Type: Reconstituted guanine-nucleotide exchange assay

Experiment: Perform proximity-dependent biotinylation from TMEM187 in K562 cells before and after erythroid induction and ask whether TfR1 or other recycling cargoes appear in the proximitome; in parallel, test whether the TfR1 surface phenotype of TMEM187 knockout cells is abolished by a RAB11A mutant locked in the GDP state. If cargo never appears and the RAB11A epistasis is complete, the adaptor and cargo-receptor hypotheses can be formally excluded rather than merely left unasserted.

Hypothesis: TMEM187's effect on TfR1 is entirely indirect, via RAB11A, with no TMEM187-cargo contact.

Type: Proximity labelling plus epistasis with RAB11A nucleotide-state mutants

Experiment: Reconstitute purified TMEM187 into proteoliposomes and screen for transport or conductance using a broad panel (ions including iron species, protons, small metabolites) with electrophysiology and radiotracer flux. A clean negative result across a wide panel would be worth publishing, because it would justify treating the transmembrane region as structural and redirect effort to the cytosolic surfaces that contact RAB11A.

Hypothesis: The six transmembrane helices are functional rather than merely anchoring, and TMEM187 may move a substrate across the TGN membrane.

Type: Proteoliposome transport and conductance screen

TMEM187 review notes

Why this gene was selected

TMEM187 (formerly CXorf12/ITBA1) is a gene whose name says only that it is a membrane protein, and
until 2026 that was the whole of what was known. GOA carries three CC rows and a GO:0005515 protein binding from a single binary-interactome screen - no biological process, no molecular function beyond
bare binding. A 2026 Blood paper supplies a cellular localisation and a biological role, but the
molecular function is still not determined, and the temptation here is to invent one.

Why I did not assert a molecular function

The CC and BP claims are reasonably clear. The MF is not, and the right response is to say so rather than
to reach for a plausible-sounding term.

What is actually demonstrated at the molecular level is one thing: TMEM187 binds RAB11A, preferentially in
its GDP-bound state, by co-immunoprecipitation in HEK293 and K562 cells. That supports GO:0031267 small GTPase binding and nothing beyond it. Everything past that point is inference:

  • Not an adaptor. No cargo has been shown to bind TMEM187, and TfR1 is affected downstream of RAB11A,
    not by contact with TMEM187.
  • Not a cargo receptor. TfR1's behaviour changes because RAB11A activation changes; there is no
    evidence of TMEM187-TfR1 binding.
  • Not demonstrated to be a GEF inhibitor. The competition model (TMEM187 occupying GDP-RAB11A so that
    the GEF GRAB cannot reach it) fits the data, but the paper itself flags that this preference runs
    opposite to what was reported for GRAB in axon outgrowth, no reconstituted nucleotide-exchange assay is
    presented, and the competition is inferred from colocalisation and expression changes rather than
    measured directly.
  • Not a transporter or channel, despite six TM helices: there is no permeation or transport assay, and
    the protein has no recognisable transporter signature - Pfam PF15100 is a family of its own, and the
    PANTHER family PTHR15066 contains only TMEM187 itself.

So core_functions asserts locations and processes and deliberately asserts no molecular_function.
Per CLAUDE.md, an omitted id says "not established"; a wrong id says something false in a machine-readable
field that other tooling will believe. The RAB11A binding is instead recorded where it belongs, as a
curatable GO:0031267 annotation in existing_annotations, and the missing activity is written up as a
MF_DARK knowledge gap.

A caution about the rest of the TMEM187 literature

Searching PubMed for TMEM187 returns mostly two things that are not about TMEM187's function: Xq28
fine-mapping studies of systemic lupus erythematosus, where TMEM187 is one of several genes in a linkage
block around IRAK1/MECP2 and is not itself implicated (e.g. PMID:22904263), and machine-learning
"biomarker" papers that list TMEM187 among five genes correlated with osteoporosis. Neither supports any
GO annotation, and neither is cited here.

Open question for experts

Does TMEM187 inhibit GRAB-catalysed nucleotide exchange on RAB11A directly? That is the single experiment
that would convert the current binding annotation into a real molecular function - and, if it failed,
would send the field looking for a different activity for a six-pass Golgi protein.
