Gene Description

miPEP155 (P155) is a 17-amino-acid human micropeptide translated from a 54-bp sense short open reading frame inside MIR155HG, the miR-155 host transcript (BIC), which is otherwise annotated as non-coding; it therefore has its own UniProt entry while sharing the host locus symbol. The peptide is made endogenously, as shown by a CRISPR knock-in EGFP reporter at the ORF stop codon, a peptide-specific antibody and mass spectrometry of immunoprecipitates, and the host transcript is strongly induced in antigen-presenting cells of inflamed skin. miPEP155 is found in the cytoplasm and nucleus and binds the nucleotide-binding (ATPase) domain, but not the substrate-binding domain, of the constitutive chaperone HSC70/HSPA8, lowering its ATP hydrolysis rate. In dendritic cells this weakens the HSC70-HSP90 co-chaperone machinery and HSC70's association with LAMP2A, so that internalised antigen is delivered less efficiently to lysosomes, surface MHC class II is reduced and priming of CD4+ T cells is dampened; synthetic peptide given to mice alleviates MOG-induced experimental autoimmune encephalomyelitis and imiquimod-induced psoriasis-like skin inflammation. The peptide does not regulate the level of its own primary miRNA transcript or the activity of miR-155, and appears functionally antagonistic to the microRNA it is co-transcribed with. Its coding sequence is well conserved in primates but is reported as absent or only partially conserved in mouse, whereas HSC70 itself is essentially identical between human and mouse.

Core Functions

miPEP155 is an inhibitor of the constitutive chaperone HSC70/HSPA8. It binds the nucleotide-binding (ATPase) domain and not the substrate-binding domain, and lowers the ATP hydrolysis activity of the full-length chaperone, which is the step that drives HSC70's client-loading and release cycle. In dendritic cells this blunts assembly of the HSC70-HSP90 co-chaperone machinery and HSC70's engagement with LAMP2A, so internalised and chaperone-delivered antigen reaches lysosomes less efficiently, surface MHC class II falls, and CD4+ T cell priming is reduced. The peptide thus acts as an endogenous, inflammation-induced brake on dendritic-cell antigen presentation; it carries out this role itself, by occupying the chaperone's nucleotide-binding site, rather than by being a client or substrate of the pathway it restrains.

Suggested Questions for Experts

Q: Does the endogenous peptide, as opposed to synthetic peptide supplied at micromolar concentration, restrain antigen presentation? Every functional result for this entry comes from adding peptide from outside, and the experiment that would settle whether the annotations describe a physiological function or a pharmacological one has not been done.

Suggested experts: Hua Wang, Arnaud Besson

Q: Is the coding ORF genuinely absent from the mouse genome? The primary paper states it is and uses the human peptide on mouse cells for that reason, while a second group describes the peptide as partially conserved in mouse. The answer decides whether any mouse ortholog annotation is legitimate and whether mouse disease models are testing a conserved physiological axis or a human-peptide intervention.

Suggested experts: Jean-Philippe Combier, Serge Plaza

Q: Should the MHC class II route be annotated explicitly, with negative regulation of antigen processing and presentation of peptide antigen via MHC class II, alongside the dendritic-cell term already present? The mechanism runs through LAMP2A and surface MHC class II, but the two terms overlap heavily and the evidence comes from a single study, so this was left as a question rather than proposed.

Suggested experts: Hua Wang

Q: What does the peptide do in the nucleus? Nuclear residence is documented, host-pri-miRNA autoregulation has been excluded, and no nuclear partner is known.

Suggested experts: Arnaud Besson

Suggested Experiments

Experiment: Disrupt the ORF without disturbing miR-155 production: introduce a start-codon or frameshift mutation in the sORF by base editing in human monocyte-derived or iPSC-derived dendritic cells, confirm by peptide-specific immunoblot and mass spectrometry that the peptide is lost while pri-miR-155 and mature miR-155 levels are unchanged, then measure antigen delivery to LAMP2A-positive compartments, surface MHC class II and autologous CD4+ T cell proliferation. The complementary control is to re-express the ORF from a heterologous promoter.

Hypothesis: The endogenous peptide, not just exogenously supplied peptide, limits MHC class II-restricted antigen presentation in human dendritic cells.

Experiment: Characterise the inhibition kinetically with purified human HSPA8: steady-state ATP hydrolysis across an ATP concentration series with and without peptide to establish the mode of inhibition and an IC50, isothermal titration calorimetry or surface plasmon resonance against the isolated nucleotide-binding domain to obtain an affinity, and a scrambled-sequence and alanine-scan series to identify the residues required. Pair this with a luciferase-refolding or client-release assay to show that the chaperone cycle itself, not only hydrolysis, is slowed.

Hypothesis: The peptide is a competitive inhibitor of nucleotide binding or of a nucleotide-cycle step of HSPA8, rather than an unspecific aggregation-prone peptide effect.

Experiment: In a non-immune human cell line, use a KFERQ-motif reporter to quantify chaperone-mediated autophagy flux with and without the peptide, and test whether the peptide alters HSPA8-LAMP2A co-immunoprecipitation and lysosomal translocation of a canonical substrate. A positive result would indicate that the dendritic-cell phenotype is one consequence of a broader effect on HSC70-dependent delivery, and would change which process terms are appropriate.

Hypothesis: The peptide acts on chaperone-mediated autophagy substrate delivery generally, not only on the dendritic-cell antigen route.

miPEP155 / P155 (UniProt C0HMA1, gene symbol MIR155HG) — curation notes

Folder uses the repo's <HOST>__<ACC> convention for alternative-ORF peptides: the peptide
has its own UniProt entry (C0HMA1, P155_HUMAN) but UniProt files it under the host gene
symbol MIR155HG (HGNC:35460), the miR-155 host gene / BIC lncRNA. It must not be merged
with the host locus folder genes/human/MIR155HG/.

What it is

  • 17 aa peptide (MEMALMVAQTRKGKSVV), PE1 (evidence at protein level), no domains, no TM,
    1879 Da. UniProt feature PRO_0000459474 covers residues 1..17 (the whole chain).
  • Encoded by a 54-bp sense sORF ("ORF1") inside the human MIR155HG transcript, which is
    annotated as non-coding and is the primary transcript for miR-155
    PMID:32671205.
  • Endogenous existence established three ways in the primary paper: CRISPR/Cas9 C-terminal
    EGFP knock-in at the ORF1 stop codon in HEK293T
    PMID:32671205,
    a peptide-specific antibody, and LC-MS of antibody immunoprecipitates
    PMID:32671205.
    Independently reproduced: the spliced pri-miR-155 transcript expresses miPEP155 in HeLa
    PMID:33810468.

Function (Niu et al. 2020, Sci Adv, PMID:32671205 — verified via PubMed/UniProt; journal is

Sci Adv, not Nat Commun)

  • MIR155HG is induced in antigen-presenting cells in inflamed (psoriatic) dermis, not at
    steady state PMID:32671205.
  • Target identification in human monocyte-derived DCs: biotin-P155 pull-down + silver
    stain + LC-MS + immunoblot identified a ~73 kDa partner as HSC70 (HSPA8)
    PMID:32671205;
    competition with free peptide established specificity. Also confirmed in human
    THP-1-derived DCs PMID:32671205.
  • Binding is domain-selective (ATPase/NBD, not the SBD) and inhibits HSC70 ATPase activity
    PMID:32671205.
    ATPase assay used recombinant human HSPA8 protein plus synthetic peptide
    PMID:32671205.
  • Downstream consequences were measured in mouse bone marrow-derived DCs: weakened
    HSC70–HSP90 and HSC70–LAMP2A co-IP, less OVA delivery to LAMP2A+ lysosomes, reduced
    MHC class II, reduced OT-II CD4+ T cell proliferation
    PMID:32671205,
    PMID:32671205.
  • Therapeutic effect of exogenous synthetic peptide in two mouse models (MOG-EAE,
    imiquimod psoriasis-like skin inflammation)
    PMID:32671205.

Species caveat (important)

The peptide-coding sequence is not present in the mouse genome per the primary paper
PMID:32671205.
Mouse BMDC and in vivo work therefore tests the human peptide acting on mouse HSC70,
which is essentially identical to human HSC70 in both domains. A second group describes the
peptide as "extremely well conserved in primates and partially conserved in mice"
PMID:33810468,
so conservation outside primates is contested. Practical consequence for GO: the molecular
interaction (HSPA8 binding, ATPase inhibition) is demonstrated with human proteins, while
the cell-biological outcome (antigen trafficking, MHC-II, T cell priming) rests mainly on
mouse cells treated with the human peptide. The UniProt/GOA IDA rows are defensible, but
all of them derive from exogenously supplied synthetic peptide at 25 µM, not from
loss of function of the endogenous peptide. No peptide-specific knockout or knockdown
(which is hard here: the ORF sits inside the miR-155 primary transcript) has been reported.

What it does NOT do

Unlike plant miPEPs and human miPEP133, miPEP155 does not autoregulate its own host
pri-miRNA or the activity of miR-155
PMID:33810468;
instead it appears functionally antagonistic to miR-155
PMID:33810468.
This is a useful negative result: no transcription-regulation GO terms should be inferred
for this peptide. A third paper reports that MIR155HG promotes NK-cell proliferation
"without reliance on its derived miR-155 and micropeptide P155" (PMID:40486832, Acta Pharm
Sin B 2025) — i.e. a host-RNA function explicitly separable from the peptide; not cached and
not used as support for any annotation here.

Annotation decisions

  • GO:0030544 Hsp70 protein binding (IPI, WITH UniProtKB:P11142) — ACCEPT. Informative MF,
    not bare protein binding; HSPA8 is the correct partner and the pull-down/LC-MS/PLA
    evidence is in human cells.
  • GO:0002605 negative regulation of dendritic cell antigen processing and presentation
    (IDA) — ACCEPT, core. Direction and cell type are right; caveat recorded that readouts
    were in mouse BMDCs with exogenous human peptide.
  • GO:0005737 cytoplasm / GO:0005634 nucleus (IDA, and the GO_REF:0000044 SubCell
    mappings SL-0086/SL-0191) — ACCEPT for cytoplasm (where HSC70 and the CMA/MHC-II
    trafficking machinery operate); nucleus kept but flagged as non-core, since it rests on
    immunofluorescence of FITC-synthetic peptide colocalising with antibody-detected
    endogenous peptide in HEK293T and no nuclear activity is known
    PMID:32671205.
    The two IEA rows are UniProt SubCell mappings of the same primary evidence — correct, and
    not circular in the sense CLAUDE.md reserves for IBA chains, but redundant with the IDA rows.
  • NEW GO:0042030 ATPase inhibitor activity — proposed. Participation test is satisfied at
    the molecular level: the peptide itself binds the HSC70 nucleotide-binding domain and the
    measured ATP hydrolysis of recombinant human HSPA8 falls in its presence. This is the
    mechanistically informative MF, and it is what makes the BP term intelligible; the
    existing Hsp70 protein binding row records only the physical interaction.
  • Not proposed: GO:0002587 negative regulation of antigen processing and presentation of peptide antigen via MHC class II. The MHC-II specificity is real but the GOA row
    GO:0002605 already captures the regulated process in the right cell type, and adding an
    overlapping sibling would be over-annotation on evidence from one paper. Raised as a
    suggested question instead.
  • Not proposed: any chaperone-mediated autophagy term. The peptide inhibits a chaperone
    that performs that process; impairing someone else's machine is regulation of it, and the
    dedicated negative-regulation BP term already annotated covers the demonstrated outcome.

- description: >-
    miPEP155 is an inhibitor of the constitutive chaperone HSC70/HSPA8. It binds the
    nucleotide-binding (ATPase) domain and not the substrate-binding domain, and lowers the
    ATP hydrolysis activity of the full-length chaperone, which is the step that drives
    HSC70's client-loading and release cycle. In dendritic cells this blunts assembly of the

    falls, and CD4+ T cell priming is reduced. The peptide thus acts as an endogenous,
    inflammation-induced brake on dendritic-cell antigen presentation; it carries out this
    role itself, by occupying the chaperone's nucleotide-binding site, rather than by being
    a client or substrate of the pathway it restrains.
