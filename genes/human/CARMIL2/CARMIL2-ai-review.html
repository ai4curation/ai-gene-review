Gene Description

CARMIL2 is a multidomain cytoskeletal regulator and signaling adaptor. It binds the actin-capping-protein heterodimer and modulates barbed-end capping, helping organize peripheral actin networks. Distinct regions support peripheral membrane association and localization along vimentin intermediate filaments. In human cultured cells, these activities contribute to polarized protrusions, collective migration and invadopodia. Signaling-competent splice forms in lymphocytes also organize CD28-dependent signaling to CARD11 and NF-kappaB through a function separable from capping-protein binding. The predominant leukocyte splice form differs from the longest reported product. Biallelic loss of CARMIL2 causes combined immunodeficiency with defects extending beyond CD28 responses.

Proposed New Ontology Terms

actin filament capping protein inhibitor activity

Definition: Binding to an actin filament capping protein or capping-protein complex and directly decreasing its ability to cap actin filament ends, without covalently modifying the target.

Justification: Human CARMIL2 binds the CP heterodimer and reduces its capping activity in purified reconstitution; CBR added after capping promotes uncapping, and CPI-motif mutation loses both effects. Protein-containing complex binding omits this regulatory output. Molecular function inhibitor activity is an applicable existing parent but lacks CP specificity. The proposed activity belongs to the regulator, not to the CP target and not to a motor or enzyme by implication. GO:2000813 already records the associated biological process; no redundant NEW capping-process annotation is proposed.

Core Functions

Directly inhibits the actin-capping-protein heterodimer through noncovalent binding, reducing CP-mediated barbed-end capping and promoting uncapping in purified CBR assays. Membrane and vimentin-associated localization coordinates this regulation with polarized protrusions; direct binary binding to vimentin is not established. The existing molecular-function inhibitor term captures the demonstrated activity, while the proposed CP-specific child term supplies its missing target specificity. CARMIL2 modulates CP activity rather than eliminating every CP function, and collective migration can be rescued independently of CP binding.

CARMIL2 biological review notes

2026-10-04 — cytoskeletal organization and immune signaling

CARMIL2 (RLTPR/LRRC16C; human UniProt Q6F5E8) is a multidomain cytoskeletal regulator with a separable immune-signaling role. The normal UniProt record contains a 1,435-residue canonical sequence and two alternative products, CARMIL2a and CARMIL2b. The human-cell cytoskeletal papers studied the shorter CARMIL2b construct; their residue numbering must not be silently substituted for canonical-sequence numbering. The source annotations do not specify an isoform, and none has been added by inference.

The manually reviewed set contains 45 source assertions: 34 accepted, eight retained as non-core and three undecided. All source identifiers, evidence codes, qualifiers and supporting entities are retained exactly. There are no new annotation rows. Two core functions distinguish regulation of the actin-capping-protein complex from noncatalytic immune-signal organization.     resolving the reported splice forms against authoritative protein records?
suggested_experiments:
- description: Compare endogenous CARMIL2 rescue with matched CP-binding-deficient and membrane-targeting-deficient
    variants in human immune and migratory cells, measuring local actin organization and CD28-to-CARD11/NF-kappaB
    signaling separately.
- description: Reconstitute the candidate CARMIL2-vimentin interaction with purified components while
    testing whether it changes delivery or regulation of the actin-capping-protein heterodimer.

proposed_new_terms:
- proposed_name: actin filament capping protein inhibitor activity
  proposed_definition: Binding to an actin filament capping protein or capping-protein complex and directly
    decreasing its ability to cap actin filament ends, without covalently modifying the target.
  justification: Human CARMIL2 binds the CP heterodimer and reduces its capping activity in purified reconstitution;
    CBR added after capping promotes uncapping, and CPI-motif mutation loses both effects. Protein-containing
    complex binding omits this regulatory output. Molecular function inhibitor activity is an applicable
    existing parent but lacks CP specificity. The proposed activity belongs to the regulator, not to the
    CP target and not to a motor or enzyme by implication. GO:2000813 already records the associated biological
    process; no redundant NEW capping-process annotation is proposed.

    id: GO:0140678
