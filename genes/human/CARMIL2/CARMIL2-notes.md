# CARMIL2 biological review notes

## 2026-10-04 — cytoskeletal organization and immune signaling

CARMIL2 (RLTPR/LRRC16C; human UniProt Q6F5E8) is a multidomain cytoskeletal regulator with a separable immune-signaling role. The normal UniProt record contains a 1,435-residue canonical sequence and two alternative products, CARMIL2a and CARMIL2b. The human-cell cytoskeletal papers studied the shorter CARMIL2b construct; their residue numbering must not be silently substituted for canonical-sequence numbering. The source annotations do not specify an isoform, and none has been added by inference.

The manually reviewed set contains 45 source assertions: 34 accepted, eight retained as non-core and three undecided. All source identifiers, evidence codes, qualifiers and supporting entities are retained exactly. There are no new annotation rows. Two core functions distinguish regulation of the actin-capping-protein complex from noncatalytic immune-signal organization. Four additional normal immune-reference caches were integrated after their identities and access metadata were checked; the source annotations and two product records were preserved.

### Direct capping-protein regulation and localization

PMID:26466680, *CARMIL2 is a novel molecular connection between vimentin and actin essential for cell migration and invadopodia formation*, contains the most direct mechanistic evidence. The normal cache includes the full XML-derived body. Its repeated aggregate extraction blocks were separated from unique paragraphs for reading; the Results, Discussion, Methods and relevant captions were read. The experiments combine human HT1080 depletion/rescue, localization and domain swaps with purified CARMIL2 capping-protein-binding-region assays. CARMIL2 reduces CP-mediated barbed-end capping and promotes uncapping in the reconstituted assay. The molecular-function annotation for protein-containing complex binding therefore refers specifically to the heterodimeric CP complex, rather than an unspecified association.

The biochemical effect should not be reduced to elimination of CP function. CARMIL2 alters CP affinity and dynamics while localizing this regulator to peripheral actin networks. The study connects CP-binding activity to lamellipodial and ruffle actin organization, cell polarity and invadopodia. These effects have a molecular contribution from CARMIL2 itself, beyond a generic loss-of-function requirement.

The same paper maps intermediate-filament localization to the CARMIL2 LRR region using CARMIL1/CARMIL2 chimeras. Imaging shows association with dynamic vimentin filaments; the assays do not establish purified binary CARMIL2–vimentin binding. The localization annotations are accepted without converting them into an untested direct-binding claim. Intermediate-filament organization itself remained largely intact after depletion.

PMID:26578515, *Cell Migration and Invadopodia Formation Require a Membrane-binding Domain of CARMIL2*, supplies distinct membrane evidence. Its normal cache is abstract-only; selected primary Results, Discussion and Methods were additionally read through indexed PMC text after the direct page challenged access. A short basic/hydrophobic C-terminal segment targets membranes, and a purified fusion containing it binds lipid vesicles. This supports broad phospholipid binding, peripheral plasma-membrane location and the cytoplasmic-facing topology. It does not establish a particular lipid-headgroup preference. The measured membrane binding belongs to this segment, not the PH domain. Imaging also supports lamellipodia, ruffles and macropinosomal localization. Mutation of membrane targeting retains intermediate-filament localization but impairs several peripheral actin phenotypes.

PMID:19846667, *Distinct roles for CARMIL isoforms in cell migration*, was read as a complete cached abstract plus selected indexed primary Results and Discussion. Human HT1080 CARMIL2 depletion altered cell polarity, MTOC/Golgi orientation and collective migration; microscopy identified its vimentin-associated cytoplasmic pool. CARMIL1-specific myosin-IE, Trio and Rac observations were not transferred to CARMIL2. Differences between the 2009 and later peripheral-localization observations are not treated as proof that the later location is wrong.

### Cellular outcomes and assay boundaries

Collective migration is retained as non-core. In the 2015 study, a CP-binding-deficient CARMIL2 mutant rescued collective movement into a scratch gap even though it failed to rescue several actin-protrusion and invadopodial phenotypes. Random single-cell migration was not reduced under that study's conditions. Accordingly, the review does not say that every CARMIL2 migration effect requires CP binding or that the protein universally increases movement speed.

The official definition of GO:0044319, wound healing, spreading of cells, includes migration across a wound gap to restore a continuous surface. The scratch-gap experiments fit this definition despite the potentially narrower everyday meaning of “spreading.” These two assertions remain non-core cellular outcomes; they are not rejected on a label-only interpretation, and they are not generalized to physiological tissue repair.

The two extracellular-matrix-disassembly annotations reflect invadopodia and gelatin-degradation assays in human tumor cells. CARMIL2 contributes by organizing the relevant cytoskeletal machinery. It is not an extracellular protease. The actin-network, lamellipodium, ruffle and cellular-polarity annotations are supported more directly by localization, perturbation/rescue and the demonstrated CP-regulatory mechanism. GO:0061339 describes a single cellular polarity axis, consistent with the observed unipolar versus multipolar/apolar organization; it does not require an organism-specific developmental interpretation.

### Immune-adaptor function

The existing canonical abstract of PMID:23793062 describes a mouse Rltpr mutation that disrupts CD28 co-stimulation and recruitment of PKC-theta and CARMA1. This supplies orthologous immune context, not a claim that the mouse experiment was performed on human CARMIL2.

PMID:27647348, *The scaffolding function of the RLTPR protein explains its essential role for CD28 co-stimulation in mouse and human T cells*, supports a noncatalytic signaling role with several distinct experimental systems. Mouse thymocyte affinity purification and mass spectrometry identify a CARMIL2-associated signaling assembly after stimulation. Endogenous human Jurkat PH-domain and proline-rich-region deletions impair co-stimulation even while retaining CP association. The rescue constructs in that earlier study were murine, as the later human isoform paper explicitly clarifies. They must not be described as demonstrating functional rescue by the longest human product. Loss of CP-binding-motif function separates capping-protein binding from the tested immune response in both systems.

These recruitment and functional observations support signaling-adaptor activity, whose official GO:0035591 definition concerns noncatalytic binding that brings signaling molecules together. The evidence exceeds a generic immunodeficiency phenotype. It does not establish that every interaction is direct, that CARMIL2 simultaneously contacts CD28 and CARD11 without other adaptors, or that CARMIL2 is a subunit of the CARD11–BCL10–MALT1 complex. Mouse proteomics and human endogenous-cell evidence are kept distinct.

PMID:27647349, *Dual T cell- and B cell-intrinsic deficiency in humans with biallelic RLTPR mutations*, supplies human patient evidence for defective CD28-induced p65 activation in CD4 T cells and defective BCR-induced NF-kappaB activation in B cells. The tested B-cell CD40 response remains intact, and much ERK signaling is retained. This human BCR result is not negated by the absence of a detectable corresponding phenotype in the earlier mouse experiments. It also argues against describing every immune consequence as a downstream effect of T-cell deficiency.

PMID:28112205, *A human immunodeficiency syndrome caused by mutations in CARMIL2*, adds primary human cytoskeletal evidence. Patient T lymphoblasts show altered actin/microtubule distribution and impaired directional migration or chemotaxis. Increased speed in some conditions coexists with reduced directional persistence. These results reinforce the distinction between coordinated movement and a universal increase in velocity; they do not change the existing migration decisions. The broad clinical phenotype supports disease context, without turning every affected immune lineage into a new process annotation.

PMID:36515678, *Human CARMIL2 deficiency underlies a broader immunological and clinical phenotype than CD28 deficiency*, is essential for interpreting splice forms. The paper's isoform1 contains 1435 residues, isoform2 1372 residues, and isoform3 1399 residues. Leukocyte RNA evidence supports isoform3 as the predominant form, with an exon14 segment removed and exon36 retained. In complementation experiments, both exogenous isoform2 and isoform3 restore CD28-to-NF-kappaB responses; isoform1 does not. Human isoform3 also restores deficient primary T-cell signaling. Thus, the predominance of isoform3 must not be misreported as an inability of isoform2 to function.

The gene-level immune-adaptor core is restricted to signaling-competent splice forms. The current normal UniProt record still contains two products and a 1435-residue canonical sequence. Those source records are preserved exactly; no guessed third UniProt isoform accession or source-row isoform qualifier is added. The difference between the literature's nomenclature and current authoritative protein/transcript records is raised explicitly for reconciliation. It does not justify deleting the human gene-level function or assuming every reported product shares it.

The 2023 patient/rescue and transcript analyses also delimit the signaling claim. Canonical NF-kappaB responses are impaired, whereas the measured NFAT/AP-1 target responses are retained. CARMIL2 deficiency has effects beyond CD28 deficiency alone; PMA-driven signaling and patient comparisons do not establish every additional receptor linkage. Selected mechanistic Results, captions, Discussion and Methods from each of the four new XML-derived caches were read; this is not a claim that every supplementary experiment was independently inspected.

### Three unresolved source assertions

1. PMID:19946888, *Defining the membrane proteome of NK cells*, describes membrane enrichment and mass spectrometry in the YTS NK-like cell line. Its complete canonical abstract and publisher abstract were read. The official supplementary XLS was located, but download returned HTTP 403. The CARMIL2-specific protein/peptide entry therefore remains uninspected. Independent membrane-targeting work makes the broad location plausible but cannot authenticate this original high-throughput entry. The HDA annotation is undecided, with no accusation of misattribution.
2. GO:0034315, regulation of Arp2/3 complex-mediated actin nucleation, is an IBA with a Dictyostelium descendant and a PAINT node. An IBA records an ancestral judgment, not a pairwise similarity transfer. The number of listed donors and the absence of demonstrated direct human Arp2/3 binding do not refute a regulatory process. However, the actual IBD and the load-bearing donor experiment were not inspected, and direct human CP regulation alone does not settle the exact nucleation-regulatory assertion. It remains undecided. A later reconstitution study located during research uses human CARMIL1 rather than CARMIL2; it is relevant family context, not an experimentally validated human CARMIL2 replacement for the uninspected PAINT evidence.
3. GO:0160108, animal gross anatomical part developmental process, derives from an uninspected ARBA rule. Cell-polarity phenotypes and immune-cell differentiation do not, by themselves, identify a role in the development of a gross anatomical part. The review retains uncertainty rather than asserting that the rule is wrong or turning the broad developmental label into a core function.

The plasma-membrane, lamellipodium and migration IBAs have direct human evidence consistent with their asserted roles. Where the target itself appears in WITH/FROM, this is legitimate descendant experimental grounding; it is not circular support. The exact PTN and all donor identifiers remain unchanged, with no invented family label or reconstructed node placement.

## 2026-10-04 — response to the first scientific review

This entry supersedes the earlier zero-NEW count and the complex-binding headline for the first core. The 45 original assertions, all of their machine-sourced fields, two product records, thirteen reference entries and three unresolved source assertions remain intact. The source CP-complex-binding row now proposes molecular function inhibitor activity; one additional row proposes signaling adaptor activity. The resulting 46 decisions comprise 33 ACCEPT, eight KEEP_AS_NON_CORE, three UNDECIDED, one MODIFY and one NEW. DRAFT still reflects the three unresolved assertions.

### Molecular activity and participation

The immune proposal is GO:0035591 signaling adaptor activity, supported by PMID:27647348 with PMID:27647349 and PMID:36515678. CARMIL2 contributes the noncatalytic organization of signaling molecules. Mouse AP-MS/recruitment supplies physical-assembly evidence; endogenous human domain deletions, patient phenotypes and human isoform complementation support its conserved functional role. Human perturbation/rescue is not silently recast as a purified human three-protein bridge. The longstanding core qualification remains: the tested signaling-competent isoforms function, while the longest isoform did not rescue in the reported system. The proposal therefore does not imply universal isoform activity or invent a third UniProt product accession.

The official signaling-adaptor definition and parent chain distinguish this MF from the existing phospholipid- and CP-complex-binding assertions. No ancestor/descendant duplicate MF or NEW process is added. GO:0031295 describes provision of the additional T-cell activation signal through surface receptor–ligand pairs; GO:0043123 describes increasing canonical NF-kappaB signaling. These are plausible contextual follow-ups, not prerequisites for recording the demonstrated molecular activity. A live same-role comparator request for CARD11, LAT and LCP2 could not resolve the QuickGO host; the local CARD11 source contains costimulation and canonical-NF-kappaB assertions, but that one example cannot establish a convention for all intracellular adaptors. No presence/absence conclusion is drawn for LAT or LCP2, and no NEW BP is proposed from an incomplete comparator check. The local GO-CAM index has no CARMIL2/Q6F5E8 hit. A question records the receptor, cell and splice-form mapping still needed for a process-annotation extension.

### Direct CP inhibition and ontology specificity

PMID:26466680 Figure 3 and its associated Results distinguish direct regulation from binding alone. Purified human CBR relieves CP inhibition of actin polymerization; addition after CP capping increases polymerization consistent with uncapping. The RR985/987AA CBR mutant loses both effects, while full-length cell-lysate coimmunoprecipitation supports the corresponding CP association. These results justify refining the existing row to GO:0140678 molecular function inhibitor activity and using that same supported activity in the core. This parent explicitly describes noncovalent inhibition without covalent target modification. CP need not be an enzyme, receptor or motor, so those narrower inhibitor terms would be misleading.

An actin filament capping protein inhibitor activity is proposed beneath that parent. The bounded official search did not identify a CP-specific MF; it did identify the broader inhibitor parent and separate capping/uncapping biological processes. This is a proposed ontology refinement, not a claim that search proves exhaustive absence. The capping target performs capping, whereas CARMIL2 directly regulates it; existing GO:2000813 already captures the negative regulation process. The proposal is not an additional NEW BP. The reviewed local schema has no proposed_molecular_function slot, so the existing supported MF is paired with the schema-supported proposed_new_terms entry. Purified CBR inhibition is kept separate from intact-protein and collective-migration behavior.

## 2026-10-04 — Clarification of the core-function schema choice

The earlier schema statement describes the older local checkout. The current main schema includes `proposed_molecular_function` for a core activity lacking an applicable GO term, linked to an entry in `proposed_new_terms`. Its absence locally is not the reason for the biological representation chosen here. GO:0140678 molecular function inhibitor activity applies to the demonstrated noncovalent inhibition of capping protein, while the proposed actin filament capping protein inhibitor activity supplies the desired target specificity. The existing MF and proposed term therefore remain appropriate with the current main schema; the annotation decisions, core activities and source evidence are unchanged.
