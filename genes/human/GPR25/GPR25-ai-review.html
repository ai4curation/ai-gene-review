Gene Description

Class A G protein-coupled receptor of the lymphocyte plasma membrane, and the receptor for the chemoattractant cytokine CXCL17. GPR25 was cloned in 1997 and remained an orphan for nearly three decades before three independent laboratories, using in vivo homing assays, beta-arrestin recruitment and TGF-alpha shedding in transfected cells, and chemotaxis of transfected pre-B cells, converged on CXCL17 as its agonist; site-directed mutagenesis in two of those studies identified the same orthosteric pocket residues, Trp95 and Arg178, as essential, and activation depends on the conserved C-terminal fragment of CXCL17. Because CXCL17 is produced by the epithelia of the airways, oral cavity, stomach, and biliary and genitourinary tracts - the non-intestinal mucosae - the GPR25-CXCL17 axis provides the tissue-selective chemoattractant pathway for those barrier surfaces, complementing the separately known homing systems for intestine and skin. GPR25 is induced on innate lymphocytes before they leave the bone marrow and is imprinted in secondary lymphoid tissue on activated B and T cells; it marks tissue-resident memory B and T cells and regulatory T cells in non-intestinal mucosae and lung, and is also expressed on T cells in cerebrospinal fluid. Adoptive transfer shows that GPR25 drives homing into mucosal barrier tissues but not into intestine, peripheral lymph nodes or spleen. Independently of chemotaxis, GPR25 is induced by TGF-beta signalling and is required for efficient formation of lung and liver tissue-resident memory CD8 T cells. CXCL17 itself is promiscuous - it also activates MRGPRX1, MRGPRX2 and MAS1 at micromolar concentrations, through a mechanism that does not require its C-terminal fragment - and whether CXCL17 should be classified as a chemokine at all is debated; neither question undermines GPR25 as one of its receptors.

Core Functions

GPR25 is the lymphocyte receptor for CXCL17. The conserved C-terminal fragment of CXCL17 inserts into an orthosteric pocket whose Trp95 and Arg178 are required for activation, with the receptor N-terminus providing an initial orientation step; occupancy triggers G protein signalling and beta-arrestin recruitment and drives RhoA-dependent cytoskeletal and integrin changes. The functional output is chemotaxis, and in vivo the axis delivers lymphocytes from blood to the barrier epithelia of the airways, oral cavity, stomach, and biliary and genitourinary tracts, where CXCL17 is made - and specifically not to intestine, peripheral lymph nodes or spleen. Independently, GPR25 is induced by TGF-beta and is required for efficient formation of lung and liver tissue-resident memory CD8 T cells.

Suggested Questions for Experts

Q: Does CXCL17 signal through MRGPRX1, MRGPRX2 and MAS1 at physiologically attainable concentrations, or are those micromolar responses an in vitro property of a cationic peptide? The answer decides how much of CXCL17 biology is GPR25 biology, without bearing on whether GPR25 is a CXCL17 receptor.

Suggested experts: Zhan-Yun Guo, James E Pease

Q: Is the TGF-beta-induced role of GPR25 in lung and liver tissue-resident memory CD8 T cell formation downstream of CXCL17, or a ligand-independent output? Gpr25-deficient cells entered tissues normally and failed at a later step, which is not obviously a chemotaxis phenotype.

Suggested experts: Pandurangan Vijayanand, Hayley Feng

Q: Should GO acquire a child term for lymphocyte homing to mucosal barrier epithelia? GPR25 is currently annotated with lymphocyte migration into lymphoid organs, which the primary data explicitly exclude, and the accurate alternative is the uninformative general migration term.

Q: Does GPR25 have additional endogenous agonists, and is CXCL17 its only ligand? The receptor was assayed against a panel of one ligand family; no counter-screen of orphan ligands has been reported.

Suggested experts: Eugene C Butcher, Borja Ocón

Suggested Experiments

Experiment: Transfer wild-type, Gpr25-null and Gpr25 W95A/R178A-reconstituted CD8 T cells into wild-type and Cxcl17-null recipients, infect, and score tissue entry and subsequent TRM differentiation including TCF1 acquisition, separating the entry step from the residency step.

Hypothesis: The tissue-resident memory phenotype of GPR25 loss requires CXCL17 binding.

Type: Adoptive transfer with receptor pocket mutants in ligand-null hosts

Experiment: Quantify CXCL17 protein in airway, gastric and biliary mucosal lavage and tissue by targeted mass spectrometry, and compare with the measured EC50 values at GPR25 and at the three MRGPRs determined side by side in one laboratory with one ligand preparation.

Hypothesis: CXCL17 does not reach concentrations at mucosal surfaces sufficient to activate MRGPRX1, MRGPRX2 or MAS1.

Type: Quantitative ligand measurement plus matched pharmacology

Experiment: Determine a cryo-EM structure of the CXCL17-GPR25-G protein complex and test the predicted N-terminal orientation contacts and the Trp95, Arg178 and Arg264 pocket contacts by mutagenesis in both chemotaxis and beta-arrestin assays.

Hypothesis: GPR25 has a structurally defined CXCL17 binding mode consistent with the two-step model.

Type: Structural biology with structure-guided mutagenesis

Experiment: Screen GPR25 against a broad orphan-ligand and chemoattractant library in a beta-arrestin recruitment assay, with the W95A pocket mutant as a counter-screen to distinguish orthosteric from allosteric or assay-artefact hits.

Hypothesis: GPR25 responds to ligands other than CXCL17.

Type: Deorphanization counter-screen

The residual controversy is about CXCL17, not about GPR25

PMID:41167449 — the same Tongji group reports that CXCL17 also
activates MRGPRX1, MRGPRX2 and MAS1. But the mechanistic dissection points the other way
from what that sentence suggests:
PMID:41167449

In other words, the MRGPR responses require micromolar CXCL17 and a different
structural determinant, whereas the GPR25 response is nanomolar-to-~100 nM, requires the
conserved C-terminal fragment, and maps to a defined orthosteric pocket. A promiscuous
ligand does not make one of its receptors wrong. The direction of residual doubt is
"which receptor carries CXCL17's physiological signal in which tissue", not "is GPR25 a
CXCL17 receptor".

There is also a live taxonomic argument about CXCL17 itself — the Imperial group
"advocate the reclassification of CXCL17 as a chemoattractant distinct from the chemokine
family" (PMID:42207165). That is a question about the ligand's nomenclature; it does not
disturb GO:0016494, whose scope is C-X-C-named chemokine ligands, and CXCL17 is one.

A mis-scoped GO term found in review

GO:0097021 lymphocyte migration into lymphoid organs is annotated twice with IDA
(PMID:39293486, PMID:40279398). Its definition is "The movement of a lymphocyte within
the lymphatic system into lymphoid organs such as lymph nodes, spleen or Peyer's
patches...". The full text of PMID:39293486 states the opposite for GPR25: homing was
enhanced to mucosal barrier tissues "but not to the intestines or control peripheral
lymph nodes (PLNs) or spleen". This is a term-scoping error, not a gene error — the
underlying biology (lymphocyte migration to peripheral tissue) is right. Action:
MODIFY both rows to GO:0072676 lymphocyte migration, which is true and does not
assert lymphoid-organ entry. A mucosal-homing child term would be better still and is
raised in suggested_questions.

  • GO:0016494 C-X-C chemokine receptor activity (IDA ×2) → ACCEPT, core. This is
    about as well-replicated as a 2024-2026 deorphanization gets: three labs, three assay
    platforms, converging site-directed mutagenesis, plus in vivo genetics in two labs.
  • GO:0070098 chemokine-mediated signaling pathway (IDA ×2) → ACCEPT, core.
  • GO:0048247 lymphocyte chemotaxis (IDA ×2) → ACCEPT, core.
  • GO:0030595 leukocyte chemotaxis (IDA) → ACCEPT (correct parent of the above).
  • GO:0097021 lymphocyte migration into lymphoid organs (IDA ×2) → MODIFY to
    GO:0072676, see above.
  • GO:0004930 GPCR activity (IBA, IEA, IDA ×2, TAS) → ACCEPT. Correct, now subsumed
    by the ligand-specific child, and the IBA is a sound family-level statement.
  • GO:0007186, GO:0005886, GO:0016020 → ACCEPT.
  • GO:0005515 protein binding (IPI ×20, from three interactome screens and one
    GPCR-RAMP mapping) → MARK_AS_OVER_ANNOTATED per project guidance; bare protein
    binding carries no functional information. Most partners are unrelated membrane
    proteins recovered in Y2H-style binary screens.

Unresolved

  • Is CXCL17 the only endogenous GPR25 agonist, and does GPR25 have additional ligands?
  • Does CXCL17 signal through MRGPRX1/X2/MAS1 at physiological concentrations, or are
    those micromolar responses in vitro artefacts of a cationic peptide?
  • GPR25's TGF-β-induced role in TRM formation (PMID:41270189) is phenotypically distinct
    from chemotaxis; whether it is downstream of CXCL17 binding or a separate output is
    not established.

  description: GPR25 is the lymphocyte receptor for CXCL17. The conserved C-terminal fragment of CXCL17
    inserts into an orthosteric pocket whose Trp95 and Arg178 are required for activation, with the

    is made - and specifically not to intestine, peripheral lymph nodes or spleen. Independently, GPR25
    is induced by TGF-beta and is required for efficient formation of lung and liver tissue-resident
    memory CD8 T cells.
