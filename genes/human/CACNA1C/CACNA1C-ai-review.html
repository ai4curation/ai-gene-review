Gene Description

CACNA1C encodes the pore-forming alpha1C subunit of CaV1.2, an L-type high-voltage-activated calcium channel. Its voltage sensors and calcium-selective pore convert plasma-membrane depolarization into calcium entry. In cardiac muscle this current contributes to the action potential and triggers ryanodine-receptor-mediated calcium release for contraction; in smooth muscle and neurons it links electrical activity to calcium-dependent responses. Beta and alpha2delta subunits regulate channel expression and gating, while calmodulin and other intracellular partners control inactivation and local signaling. Extensive alternative splicing changes gating, surface expression and pharmacology. Pathogenic variants can alter current density, selectivity or inactivation in different directions, producing cardiac and neurodevelopmental phenotypes.

Core Functions

The alpha1C pore couples membrane depolarization to calcium entry through L-type CaV1.2 channels. In cardiac muscle, this current contributes to electrical excitation and triggers RyR-mediated stored-calcium release for contraction. Auxiliary subunits and calmodulin regulate this same pore unit, and splice variation changes its gating and surface expression. The channel supplies trigger calcium; the ryanodine receptor performs sarcoplasmic-reticulum release.

CACNA1C functional review

CACNA1C encodes the alpha1C pore of the L-type calcium channel CaV1.2. Human channel expression, pore mutagenesis and electrophysiology establish voltage-dependent calcium conductance. Cardiac calcium entry contributes to electrical excitation and triggers RyR2-mediated release from the sarcoplasmic reticulum. This synthesis separates the calcium-conducting pore from its regulators and from the downstream release pore.
