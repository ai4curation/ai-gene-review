## 2026-10-10 substantive review

The defining function is ATP-dependent chromatin remodeling. Purified human CHD7 assays directly measure ATP hydrolysis, DNA/nucleosome engagement, nucleosome repositioning and altered restriction accessibility (PMID:23134727; PMID:28533432). The WT/K999R control supports intrinsic catalytic coupling. The 2017 Methods pair a correct CHD7 CCDS with a CHD9 UniProt accession; official CCDS and UniProt mapping resolve the intended long CHD7 sequence, not an independently resequenced experimental plasmid. No equivalent motor activity is assigned to all current products. The core combines remodeling with transcription regulation rather than duplicating its broader gene-expression ancestor.

Regulatory-region binding is refined from motif-specific binding to GO:0000976. The exact mouse source PMID:23319608 provides occupancy/recruitment evidence; the official term comment permits nonspecific regulatory binding. Independent human enhancer assays support the biological context. The mouse Sema3c promoter ChIP and DNase-sensitivity experiments in PMID:26102480 support the separate existing promoter-chromatin assertion. Direct DNA EMSA supports the GO:0003690 refinement. Histone recognition remains broad: GST-CHD7 tandem chromodomains bind methylated H3K4 in vitro, but isolated-domain capture is not histone modification catalysis (PMID:19251738).

Nucleolar localization is positive and functionally relevant. Endogenous human DLD1 knock-in imaging and untagged fractionation support it, with rDNA occupancy and rRNA effects (PMID:20591827). Human coding-region occupancy and mouse promoter/3-prime occupancy differ. Promoter nucleosome repositioning remains the authors' model. The paper-defined human short CHD7S and long constructs both show nucleolar signal, but the current Q9P2D1-3 sequence description does not straightforwardly reproduce the paper's intron-6-stop product (PMID:22646239). All authenticated product descriptors are preserved without silently reconciling sequences.

The four protein-binding assertions have positive source support despite empty exact-tuple results from the bounded IntAct queries. Reciprocal PBAF co-IP supports PBRM1 and SMARCC2 association in human neural-crest-like cells, but those two broad protein-binding rows are removed as uninformative because the co-IP does not support a more precise CHD7 molecular-function term (PMID:20130577). Human CHD7/CHD8 fragment Y2H, reciprocal HeLa co-IP and BiFC support binding; the exact CHD8 NP_065971.2 maps to Q9HCK8-2. These two assertions are refined to ATPase binding without a regulation claim (PMID:20453063; PMID:28533432). In the separate FAM124B study, co-IP is positive but the tested CHD7 fragment is Y2H-negative, unlike CHD8; the Reactome complex is retained as context rather than proof of a native full-length ternary particle (PMID:23285124).

Broad developmental annotations remain non-core. The actual human discovery article/Table 1 and genotyped cohort establish congenital phenotypes across affected organs (PMID:15300250; PMID:16155193). Human neural crest and neuroepithelial experiments add mechanistic transcriptional context; frog morpholino/rescue observations remain explicitly frog (PMID:20130577; PMID:29440260). Cognitive outcomes vary and are not a direct cognitive effector mechanism. The immunological source documents two CHD7-genotyped cases and thymic/T-cell production defects, not an isolated precursor-intrinsic step (PMID:17684005). The limb report is abstract-limited but explicitly describes three human mutation carriers (PMID:17937444).

That source is a 1998 pre-gene clinical series whose full body was not recovered; general growth impairment does not resolve regulated hormone release (PMID:9556299). The live ARBA00027251 output is genuine regulation of transport, but no reviewed evidence identifies a CHD7-dependent transported substrate or regulatory step, so the row is marked over-annotated rather than left unresolved.
