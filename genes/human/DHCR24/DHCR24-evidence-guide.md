# DHCR24 evidence guide

This guide distinguishes the original experiments from database summaries and records the boundaries of the annotation decisions. The historical 50 annotation identities are retained. A current QuickGO snapshot is useful comparison data, but it does not replace those imported rows.

## Chemistry and products

[Waterham et al., PMID11519011](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1226055/) tested sequence-verified human full-length DHCR24 in yeast. Methods on expression and enzyme assays and the conversion Results establish NADPH-dependent desmosterol reduction, enhanced by FAD. The assays used yeast homogenates, not purified DHCR24. The 516-residue coding sequence also corrects the much shorter historical KIAA0018 prediction. The core contains one short literal quotation from this source; other annotations cite the same evidence without repeating it.

Current [GO:0000246](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/QuickGO/term/GO:0000246) specifies the EC 1.3.1.71 delta-24(28) reaction involving ergosterol. [GO:0050614](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/QuickGO/term/GO:0050614) specifies EC 1.3.1.72 delta-24 sterol reduction. They are not interchangeable synonyms. Both historical 0000246 source rows are preserved, with proposed replacements to 0050614. The three obsolete pathway-specific rows retain their previously approved replacement to cholesterol biosynthesis.

The official [UniProt Q15392 record](https://www.uniprot.org/uniprotkb/Q15392/entry) describes two splice products. Q15392-1 has 516 residues. VSP_056479 replaces its first 76 residues with a 35-residue segment in Q15392-2, producing 475 residues; independent sequence reconstruction matches the downloaded isoform sequence. Historical full-length expression experiments are not evidence that the shorter natural product has the same activity or localization. Reactome's retained 23–516 entity coordinates are a database model, not adopted as proof of physiological signal-peptide cleavage.

## Membrane location and enzyme association

[PMID11007892](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6772756/) was read as a genuine original, including human H4 construct Methods, microscopy and endogenous organelle-fractionation Results. ER and a lesser Golgi pool were detected; mitochondria were not detected in those conditions. This does not prove exclusive ER residence in every cell or stress state.

The existing [PMID22010141](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22010141/) cache remains abstract-only; the missing reference-list entry is restored. Its topology interpretation must be considered alongside [PMID27919032](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3958128/), whose original Methods and Results were read and whose cache was fetched normally. In CHO-7 experiments, deletion of the first 56 or 240 residues did not abolish membrane association, and the latter construct remained ER-associated. The C terminus was protease-accessible, while the N terminus remained protected even after partial detergent solubilization. Exact membrane-spanning architecture remains unresolved. Neither an obligatory cleaved signal peptide nor an unequivocal luminal N terminus is asserted.

[PMID25637936](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4373745/) uses human DHCR24 and DHCR7 constructs in CHO cells, reciprocal co-immunoprecipitation, hamster-selective depletion and labelled-sterol conversion. DHCR7 is an enzyme, supporting refinement of the existing pair to enzyme binding. Changes in DHCR7 protein abundance, pathway flux and allostery are explicitly left unresolved by the authors. These data do not justify a second duplicate sterol-reductase core or a claim of established direct allosteric activation.

## Stress-dependent p53 mechanism

The main [PMID15577914 article](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature03173) remains available here only as a primary abstract. Its genuine [publisher supplementary Methods and Data](https://media.springernature.com/original/springer-static/esm/art%3A10.1038%2Fnature03173/MediaObjects/41586_2004_BFnature03173_MOESM1_ESM.doc) were recovered and read. Supplementary Methods describe human p53, MDM2 and DHCR24 constructs, bacterial protein expression, p53 competition and ubiquitination assay designs. Supplementary Data distinguish catalytically inactive N294T/K306N from mutants with impaired p53 association and stress response. Human U2OS experiments also compare MDM2 association of wild-type and L210Q/F211S DHCR24.

This supports a distinct, narrowly described p53-binding core, and specific p53-binding and ubiquitin-protein-ligase-binding replacements for the existing pair annotations. It does not give DHCR24 a ubiquitin-ligase or ROS-consuming activity. One short primary-abstract quotation appears in the core; the supplementary assay evidence is cited without invented quotations. The nuclear annotation is retained as context-dependent: the supplement describes human WI38 fractionation and immunostaining with stress controls, but main-article figure pixels were not inspected. Strong ER attachment in later work makes trafficking a question to resolve, not a sufficient basis to erase a curated stress-specific observation.

## PGRMC1 interactions

[PMID30021884](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30021884/) was read in its genuine original. The curated IntAct record EBI-20911008 / IM-26653-2289 identifies human DHCR24–PGRMC1 crosslinking in the Triton-soluble portion of a U2OS nuclear preparation. Both participant sequence identifiers match the canonical proteins. The paper explains that ER can remain with nuclei; the pair therefore does not establish DHCR24 nuclear localization. Crosslinking supports a physical association, not purified binary binding.

[OpenCell, PMID35271311](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.abi6983) was read from a genuine author manuscript. Its Methods use endogenous tagging in HEK293T cells, digitonin extraction and biological-triplicate affinity proteomics. The [author dataset](https://opencell.czbiohub.org/) contains a PGRMC1-bait/DHCR24-prey record (CSV line 15878). The prey group includes Q15392, Q15392-2 and A0A0A0MTI1, so the natural isoform is unresolved. Low interaction stoichiometry is not evidence that the detected association is false, and the CSV column labelled pval is not interpreted here as a raw probability.

Official PGRMC1 functions include heme handling and participation in membrane complexes; these do not establish a protein-folding-chaperone or nuclear-receptor partner class for the pair. Both supported generic-binding assertions remain non-core under the [explicit project instruction](https://github.com/ai4curation/ai-gene-review/blob/1b47f6653c7fa96e61b6a6ac5fe76b989961b59c/projects/CLINGEN_MENDELIAN.md#curation-instructions). This instruction is scoped to the ClinGen project. Genericity alone is not contrary evidence.

## Membrane effects and developmental limits

The genuine [PMID16407971 original](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1383521/) was read through the mouse and human Results, membrane/plasmin assays, APP-cleavage assays and corresponding Methods. It compares mouse loss of function with full-length human Q15392-HA expression in SH-SY5Y cells. Cholesterol-dependent membrane organization alters protein partitioning, plasminogen recruitment/activation and BACE1 access to APP. The existing plasminogen and APP process assertions are refined to positive regulation of plasminogen activation and negative regulation of APP catabolism, respectively. DHCR24 is not assigned the proteolytic reactions. These are source-specific refinements of existing rows, not new process assertions.

[PMID16513830](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16513830/) is abstract-only: mouse cholesterol-depleted fibroblasts show altered insulin-receptor/caveolin partitioning. [PMID17210742](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17210742/) is also abstract-only and reports rat ACTH responsiveness. Its expression/distribution evidence does not settle the human process transfer, which is undecided. [PMID14684813](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14684813/) is title-only, so the tissue- and male-genitalia-development transfers remain undecided. The clinical [PMID12457401](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12457401/) abstract supports developmental disease and biochemical deficiency but does not settle process allocation; that row is undecided too.

The skin/barrier phenotype is explicit in the [PMID16410790](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16410790/) abstract. The existing mouse-to-human skin assertions remain undecided because their original developmental allocation and controls could not be read. For the membrane-proteomics row from [PMID19946888](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19946888/), the target peptide assignment and exact fraction remain unavailable. Independent localization experiments nevertheless establish membrane association, so the broad row proposes the supported ER-membrane term. This is a gene-level refinement using independent evidence; the YTS HDA fraction itself is not claimed to resolve ER. [PMID16321981](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16321981/) provides abstract-level mouse sterol context, while direct human chemistry independently establishes cholesterol-synthesis participation. Failed original-recovery attempts are recorded as access limits, not negative biological results.

## 2026-10-17 follow-up source anchors

The topology and APP/plasminogen rows now contain short literal excerpts from their cached originals. The two core excerpts remain single-use anchors. References elsewhere point to the same evidence without repeating quotations.

For [Greeve et al., original Results on intracellular localization](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6772756/), the endogenous human H4 fractionation result is: “Seladin-1 was clearly present in the Golgi and ER fractions”. The membrane row separately uses a five-word cached Discussion excerpt. Although its cache is marked `full_text_available: true`, the quoted fractionation Results passage is absent from that extraction; it was read in the genuine original article. Tagged imaging and endogenous fractionation are distinct observations, and neither establishes exclusive ER residence.

For [Luu et al., original Results on reciprocal immunoprecipitation](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4373745/), the authors state “we used a more stringent RIPA buffer” and “DHCR7 immunoprecipitation also pulled down DHCR24”. The binding row uses a short cached abstract excerpt. This cache also has a true full-text flag but omits these Results passages; the exact RIPA and reverse-pull-down details come from the recovered original. Human constructs were expressed in CHO cells. Cellular regulation is demonstrated, while altered DHCR7 abundance, sterol flux and direct allostery remain alternatives in the Discussion.

The separate p53-binding core is a molecular interaction mechanism supported by human recombinant assays and separation-of-function data in the genuine publisher supplement. The broader proliferation/Ras phenotypes and reported nuclear pool remain contextual observations. No nuclear or ER location is assigned to the binding core: its knowledge gap explicitly records that endogenous interaction localization and trafficking are unresolved. The main article remains abstract-only here; no unseen main-article figures are claimed.
