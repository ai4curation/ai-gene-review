GLO1 isoform 2 (UniProt Q04760-2, del 105-119): is it catalytically incompetent, and why?

Gene: human GLO1, UniProt Q04760, NCBITaxon:9606 Hypothesis slug: kgap-glo1-isoform2-catalytic-region Source file: genes/human/GLO1/GLO1-ai-review.yaml, focus type free_text Verdict: partially supported

Provenance for every computed number is in provenance/: glo1_isoform2_analysis.py (the executed code with its outputs annotated inline), table1_structural_mapping_105_119.csv, table2_insdc_cdna_survey.csv, table3_evidence_matrix.csv, table4_go_decision_table.csv, and the four figures embedded below.

Summary

The seed hypothesis has two parts, a conclusion and a mechanism. The conclusion, that human GLO1 isoform 2 would be catalytically incompetent, holds. The stated mechanism, that the 105-119 deletion removes active-site or zinc-coordinating residues or disrupts the domain-swapped dimer interface, is refuted by the structural mapping that the hypothesis itself requested. Mapping the segment onto PDB 1QIN, the human GLO1 dimer with Zn²⁺ and the transition-state analogue HIPC-GSH bound, places every annotated zinc ligand and every annotated substrate contact outside 105-119. The zinc coordination shell measured at under 2.8 Å is Gln33 OE1 at 2.05 Å and Glu99 OE1 at 1.91 Å from one chain, His126 NE2 at 2.05 Å from the partner chain, plus inhibitor oxygens. In UniProt numbering these are Gln34, Glu100, His127 and Glu173. The nearest deleted atom to the zinc is Asn119 at 9.51 Å; Glu111 is 25.37 Å away. The deletion also fails to touch the lid: superposing 1QIN onto 1FRO shows the ligand-dependent loop closure is residues 156-160 plus the C-terminal tail, while 105-119 does not move at all.

The dimer-interface clause is weak rather than false. The segment buries 157 Å² of a 3657 Å² chain-A interface, 4.3%, concentrated in His116 (56 Å²), Trp105 (46 Å²), Tyr115 (24 Å²) and Asn119 (17 Å²). Trp105, Tyr115, His116 and Asn119 are genuine cross-subunit contacts, and Tyr115 and Asn119 sit in the second shell around the bound transition-state analogue at 5.53 Å and 5.23 Å. A 4.3% loss of a large interface does not by itself predict loss of catalysis.

What does predict it is a different structural argument that the hypothesis did not make. Residues 105-119 are the crossover connector between the two VOC modules of the monomer. They run from β-strand 98-104 in sheet A, which carries zinc ligand Glu100 and substrate contact Asn104, to β-strand 128-131 in sheet B, which follows zinc ligand His127. Excising them requires a direct peptide bond between Asn104 and Ser120, whose Cα atoms are 22.87 Å apart in every chain of 1QIN, 1FRO and 3W0T, against the 3.81 Å mean observed for consecutive Cα pairs in the same structure. The spacing between metal ligand 2 and ligand 3 would fall from 27 residues in human and mouse GLO1 to 12, below the 18 of the 135-residue E. coli Ni-GloA. The two halves of the β-sandwich cannot be rejoined without refolding the whole VOC domain, and the 3D domain swap that places the active site in the dimer interface depends on that connector.

A third result sits upstream of all of this and matters more for curation than either clause of the hypothesis. Isoform 2 is probably not a real transcript. The 45 nt encoding residues 105-119 sit entirely inside exon 4, and no canonical or known minor-class splice-site pair can excise them at any register that yields the annotated 169-residue protein. One of twelve INSDC GLO1 cDNAs carries the deletion (AK293345, cDNA FLJ51791, from a project that deliberately enriched for splicing variants, reference status Unpublished); the other eleven, including the sole GLO1 RefSeq mRNA NM_006708, encode the canonical 184-residue protein. No RefSeq, CCDS or Ensembl transcript encodes 169 residues. The UniProt alternative-sequence feature VSP_041632 carries a single ECO:0000303 tag from a bulk cDNA catalogue (PMID: 14702039), the weakest isoform evidence class. A 4 nt direct repeat, CAAT, flanks the deleted block at mRNA level, which is what reverse-transcriptase slippage leaves behind. Public mass spectrometry observes the canonical peptide spanning the whole segment and reports no isoform 2 junction peptide.

The net recommendation for the curator is editorial. Isoform 2 should not be annotated as an inactive isoform, because that statement implies the isoform exists. It should be flagged as unverified, and any mechanistic statement about it should cite connector excision rather than loss of zinc ligands.

Key findings

F1. No zinc ligand and no annotated substrate contact lies inside 105-119

The numbering offset was fixed first, by exact match of the modelled 1QIN chain-A sequence to Q04760: UniProt = PDB + 1. With that established, the zinc coordination shell in 1QIN contains Gln33 OE1 at 2.05 Å and Glu99 OE1 at 1.91 Å from the same chain, His126 NE2 at 2.05 Å from the partner chain, and two oxygens of the GIP inhibitor. Translated to UniProt numbering these are Gln34, Glu100, His127 and Glu173, matching the UniProt binding-site features for Zn²⁺ at 34, 100, 127 (other chain) and 173 (other chain). The substrate and glutathione contacts annotated at 34, 38, 104, 123, 127, 157-158 and 173 are likewise all outside the deletion.

Distances confirm the segment is peripheral to chemistry. Minimum heavy-atom distance from each deleted residue to the zinc runs from 9.51 Å (Asn119) to 25.37 Å (Glu111). Distance to the bound transition-state analogue runs from 5.23 Å (Asn119) and 5.53 Å (Tyr115) to 16.65 Å (Glu111). Only Tyr115 and Asn119 reach the second shell around the ligand, and neither is an annotated contact.

The identity of the catalytic residues is not an inference from the structure alone. In Involvement of an active-site Zn2+ ligand in the catalytic mechanism of human glyoxalase I (PMID: 9705294), "The Zn2+ ligands glutamate 99 and glutamate 172 in the active site of human glyoxalase I were replaced, each in turn, by glutamines by site-directed mutagenesis", fixing ligand identity in mature numbering and therefore placing UniProt 100 and 173 outside 105-119. The architecture that assigns ligands to chains comes from Crystal structure of human glyoxalase I (PMID: 9218781): "3D domain swapping of the N- and C-terminal domains has resulted in the active site being situated in the dimer interface, with the inhibitor and essential zinc ion interacting with side chains from both subunits."

F2. The segment is the crossover connector, and removing it breaks the fold

In 1QIN chain A, the Cα of Asn104 and the Cα of Ser120 are 22.87 Å apart. Chain B gives 22.9 Å, and chains of 1FRO and 3W0T agree within rounding. A peptide bond constrains consecutive Cα atoms to about 3.8 Å; the observed consecutive Cα-Cα distances in this structure average 3.81 Å with a range of 2.83 to 3.85 Å. Joining 104 to 120 directly is therefore not a local adjustment but a demand that the chain cover 22.9 Å in one step.

Secondary-structure records explain why the gap is so large. The segment sits immediately after β-strand 98-104 in sheet A, which carries zinc ligand Glu100 and substrate contact Asn104, and immediately before β-strand 128-131 in sheet B, which follows zinc ligand His127. It lies inside the annotated VOC domain (UniProt 31-177) and contains only a three-residue 3₁₀ helix at 107-109; the rest is an extended connector. SASA shows it is partly packed against its own subunit even in the isolated monomer: 1034 Å² exposed out of roughly 2250 Å² expected for an extended 15-mer, with Thr107 (19 Å²), Gly118 (9 Å²), Asn117 (30 Å²) and Gln113 (37 Å²) largely buried.

The gene-duplication architecture that this connector serves is described in PMID: 9218781: "Each monomer consists of two domains. Despite only low sequence homology between them, these domains are structurally equivalent and appear to have arisen by a gene duplication." Removing 105-119 removes the link between those two structurally equivalent modules.

An orthologue check gives the same answer from a different direction. The spacing between metal ligand 2 and metal ligand 3 is 27 residues in human and mouse GLO1 and 18 residues in the 135-residue E. coli Ni-GloA (UniProt P0AC81, ligands at 5, 56, 74, 122). Isoform 2 would shorten it to 12, below anything seen in a characterised glyoxalase I.

F3. The catalytic lid is residues 156-160 and the C-terminus, not 105-119

The last route by which the stated mechanism could have been right was that 105-119 is the loop that closes over the active site on substrate binding. Kabsch superposition of 1QIN chain A (transition-state analogue complex) onto 1FRO chain A (S-benzylglutathione complex) over 176 common Cα atoms gives a global RMSD of 0.52 Å. The largest per-residue displacements are at UniProt 157 (2.48 Å), 183 and 184 (2.36 Å each), 182 (2.23 Å), 156 (1.96 Å) and 158 (1.35 Å). Across 105-119 the mean displacement is 0.36 Å, with a maximum of 1.28 Å at Thr112, indistinguishable from the whole-chain mean of 0.34 Å.

The closure itself is reported in Reaction mechanism of glyoxalase I explored by an X-ray crystallographic analysis of the human enzyme in complex with a transition state analogue (PMID: 10521255): "Coordination of the transition state analogue to the metal enables a loop to close down over the active site, relative to its position in the product-like structure, allowing the glycine residue of the glutathione moiety to hydrogen bond with the protein." The same paper identifies the structures used here: "The structures of human glyoxalase I in complexes with S-(N-hydroxy-N-p-iodophenylcarbamoyl) glutathione (HIPC-GSH) and S-p-nitrobenzyloxycarbonylglutathione (NBC-GSH) have been determined at 2.0 and 1.72 A resolution, respectively." The superposition performed here localises that loop to 156-160 plus the C-terminal tail, both annotated as partner-chain substrate contacts.

F4. No splice-site pair can produce the deletion, and no transcript carries it

The 45 nt encoding residues 105-119 map entirely inside GLO1 exon 4 (Ensembl ENST00000373365, exon ENSE00000750246, chr6:38,682,808-38,682,875, minus strand, 68 nt of CDS covering codons 103.67-125.33). The removed block is chr6:38,682,827-38,682,871. All six 45 nt windows inside exon 4 whose removal reproduces the same 169-residue protein were enumerated, and their flanking dinucleotides are CA/GG, AA/GC, AT/CA, TT/AA, TG/AT and GG/TT. None is GT..AG, GC..AG or AT..AC, so no canonical or known minor-class spliceosomal pair can generate the deletion. An alternative-acceptor model using the intron 3 donor would require AG at exon-4 nt 44-45, which is GG.

Transcript databases agree. Ensembl lists four GLO1 transcripts, GLO1-201 (184 aa), GLO1-203 (179 aa), GLO1-204 (178 aa) and GLO1-202 (non-coding); none encodes 169 aa. NCBI RefSeq lists a single GLO1 mRNA, NM_006708. UniProt VSP_041632 carries one evidence tag, ECO:0000303 from PMID: 14702039, an author statement from a bulk cDNA catalogue with no functional characterisation.

An earlier count of 5 nt was corrected: the leading G belongs to the last base of intron 3 and is not present in the mature mRNA.

An incidental control shows the clone panel is informative. The rs4746 E111A polymorphism, which lies inside the deleted segment, appears in 4 of the 12 clones, so the panel does sample real population variation at this locus and the absence of a second isoform 2 clone is not a coverage artefact at the level of the region.

F6. Public MS sees the canonical segment and no isoform 2 junction peptide

The EBI Proteins API proteomics-ptm endpoint for Q04760 returned 22 distinct observed peptides covering 137 of 184 residues. Every residue of 105-119 is covered twice, including the tryptic peptide KATLELTHNWGTEDDETQSYHNGNSDPR (residues 96-123), which spans the whole deleted segment and both flanks. Two of the 22 peptides span residues 104 and 120 contiguously.

In-silico tryptic digestion at up to one missed cleavage, peptide length 6 to 45, identifies three peptides unique to the canonical protein (KATLELTHNWGTEDDETQSYHNGNSDPR 96-123, ATLELTHNWGTEDDETQSYHNGNSDPR 97-123, ATLELTHNWGTEDDETQSYHNGNSDPRGFGHIGIAVPDVYSACK 97-140) and three unique to isoform 2 (KATLELTHNSDPR, ATLELTHNSDPR, ATLELTHNSDPRGFGHIGIAVPDVYSACK). None of the isoform 2 junction peptides appears in the returned set.

This is not a clean negative. Most public searches use a canonical-only or UniProt-with-isoform database, and this endpoint maps peptides onto the canonical sequence, so a junction peptide would surface only if the search space contained it. A targeted PRM assay for ATLELTHNSDPR is the clean test.

F7. The segment carries two regulatory features of the canonical protein

UniProt Q04760 annotates phosphothreonine at position 107 and a natural variant at position 111 (dbSNP rs4746), both inside 105-119. In 1QIN these are 16.74 Å and 25.37 Å from the nearest zinc and 8.18 Å and 16.65 Å from the bound analogue. Glu111 is fully solvent-facing, 163 Å² exposed in the monomer with 0 Å² buried on dimerisation. Thr107 is partly buried within its own subunit (19 Å² exposed) and contributes nothing to the interface. Residues 100-125 are identical in human (Q04760), mouse (Q9CPU0) and rat (Q6P7Q4) GLO1, all 184 residues.

The segment is therefore not a dispensable surface insertion. It is invariant across mammalian orthologues and hosts both a regulatory phosphosite and a common population polymorphism site. These are features of the canonical protein; they are relevant here because they would be lost in any isoform 2 polypeptide, and because they show the region is under selection.

Mechanistic model

The hypothesis proposed one causal chain. The data support a different one. Both are drawn below, with the refuted links dashed.

The synthesis reads as follows. Glyoxalase I catalyses isomerisation of the hemithioacetal of methylglyoxal and glutathione to S-D-lactoylglutathione, with one catalytic Zn²⁺ per active site. The zinc is ligated by Gln34 and Glu100 from one subunit and His127 and Glu173 from the other, and Glu173 abstracts the proton from the hemithioacetal (PMID: 9705294). Because of the 3D domain swap, a monomer has no complete active site (PMID: 9218781). Residues 105-119 participate in none of that chemistry. They hold the two VOC modules of the monomer together, which is a structural requirement for everything downstream. Their deletion therefore abolishes activity, through fold failure instead of loss of chemistry.

The prediction stands one step removed from measurement. No laboratory has expressed Q04760-2, so there is no activity assay, no metal content, no gel filtration and no structure. The verdict rests on geometry measured in crystal structures of the canonical protein plus a connector-length comparison across orthologues.

Mechanistic scope

Direct gene-product activity under test. Glyoxalase I isomerises the non-enzymatically formed hemithioacetal of methylglyoxal and glutathione to S-D-lactoylglutathione, using one catalytic Zn²⁺ per active site. The question asked is whether a 169-residue polypeptide lacking 105-119 can assemble that site.

What is inference, separate from measurement. The verdict of incompetence rests on a geometric argument about crystal structures of the canonical protein plus the connector-length constraint across orthologues. No structure prediction, molecular dynamics or folding simulation of the 169-mer was run, and none was attempted.

Explicitly outside scope. Downstream GLO1 biology, including dicarbonyl stress, advanced glycation end-product formation, GLO1 copy-number variation in anxiety phenotypes and GLO1 as an anticancer target, is unaffected by this question and should not be used as support either way. The Thr107 phosphosite and the rs4746 variant lie inside the deleted segment, but both are surface-facing and 16.7 Å and 25.4 Å from the zinc. They are regulatory and population-genetic features of the canonical protein, outside the catalytic machinery.

Evidence base

Four papers carry the literature weight of this report, and one is orientation only.

Crystal structure of human glyoxalase I: evidence for gene duplication and 3D domain swapping (PMID: 9218781) supplies the architecture: the active site lies in the dimer interface with zinc and inhibitor contacting side chains from both subunits, and each monomer is two structurally equivalent domains from a gene duplication. This paper makes the cross-subunit ligand assignment legitimate and makes the obligate-dimer statement in the GO table defensible. It does not address isoform 2.

Involvement of an active-site Zn2+ ligand in the catalytic mechanism of human glyoxalase I (PMID: 9705294) fixes ligand identity by site-directed mutagenesis of Glu99 and Glu172 in mature numbering, that is UniProt 100 and 173. This is the single most direct refutation of the hypothesis's first clause: the residues whose loss would abolish catalysis sit outside the deletion.

Reaction mechanism of glyoxalase I explored by an X-ray crystallographic analysis of the human enzyme in complex with a transition state analogue (PMID: 10521255) identifies 1QIN as the HIPC-GSH complex at 2.0 Å and reports that metal coordination of the analogue enables a loop to close over the active site. The superposition performed here localises that loop to 156-160 and the C-terminal tail, which the abstract does not specify. This paper both supplies the structure used for all distance measurements and, through the localisation, removes the lid-disruption variant of the hypothesis.

PMID: 14702039 is cited only through UniProt's ECO:0000303 tag on VSP_041632. It is the bulk full-length cDNA catalogue behind the isoform annotation, treated as database-level provenance and not as functional evidence.

One paper returned by the literature search, Effect of chronic hypoxia on RAGE and its soluble forms in lungs and plasma of mice (PMID: 25703138), refers to GLO1 as "the AGE detoxifying enzyme GLO1" in a mouse hypoxia model. It bears on downstream GLO1 physiology and contributes nothing to the isoform question. It is listed here so it is not mistaken for supporting evidence.

Two PubMed queries returned nothing usable: one for glyoxalase I dimer-interface mutagenesis testing whether both subunits are required, and one for the full-length cDNA catalogue by title. No substitute was fabricated, and the corresponding claims rest on the computed analyses instead of on literature.

Conflicts and alternatives

  1. The stated mechanism is contradicted by its own test. The mapping the hypothesis requested shows no zinc ligand, no substrate contact and no lid residue in 105-119. A curator taking the hypothesis at face value would record a mechanistic claim the structure refutes.
  2. The dimer-interface clause is weak, not false. Trp105, Tyr115, His116 and Asn119 make 48, 28, 35 and 13 heavy-atom contacts under 5 Å with the partner chain, but together they account for 4.3% of the interface. The hypothesis treats this as sufficient; it is not.
  3. Existence of the isoform is the dominant alternative. Exon-internal deletion, no usable splice-site pair, one of twelve cDNAs, a 4 nt direct repeat at the junction, and no RefSeq, CCDS or Ensembl transcript. The parsimonious reading is a reverse-transcription or cloning artefact in AK293345.
  4. Database carry-over risk. ProteomicsDB has an identifier for Q04760-2 (58282). That is a mapping stub, not evidence of detection. Treating such identifiers as support would be circular.
  5. A residual alternative worth naming. If a 169-residue polypeptide were somehow produced, a misfolded or aggregation-prone product could in principle sequester canonical subunits and act as a dominant negative. No evidence for this exists; it is listed so it is not mistaken for a tested possibility.
  6. No paralogue confusion. GLO1 has no close human paralogue that could contaminate the sequence analysis, and all 12 cDNAs aligned unambiguously to the GLO1 ORF.

Limitations and knowledge gaps

Every structural number comes from crystal structures of the canonical protein (1QIN, 1FRO, 3W0T). The fold verdict for isoform 2 is a geometric inference, not a modelling result. The proteomics check uses an aggregated resource that maps peptides onto the canonical sequence, so the missing junction peptide is a weak negative. The cDNA survey covers the 12 entries cross-referenced by UniProt plus RefSeq, not every GLO1 sequence in INSDC. The direct-repeat observation is 4 nt with a 3.9 × 10⁻³ chance expectation and is reported as suggestive only. Splice-site analysis excludes canonical and known minor-class pairs; it does not formally exclude recursive or trans-splicing, for which there is no supporting evidence here.
