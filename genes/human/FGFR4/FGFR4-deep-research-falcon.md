# Comprehensive Research Report: FGFR4 (Fibroblast Growth Factor Receptor 4)

## Gene Identity Verification

The gene FGFR4 (UniProt accession P22455) encodes Fibroblast Growth Factor Receptor 4 in *Homo sapiens* (human), a receptor tyrosine kinase (EC 2.7.10.1) belonging to the protein kinase superfamily and specifically to the Tyr protein kinase family (nguyen2024thecomplexityand pages 6-8, he2025fgfraberrationsin pages 2-4). This receptor is correctly identified and matches the UniProt annotation, confirming it contains immunoglobulin-like domains (Ig-like_dom, Ig_I-set) and FGF receptor family domains (FGF_rcpt_fam), consistent with the provided protein family information.

## Structural Organization and Key Domains

FGFR4 is a single-pass transmembrane receptor tyrosine kinase with a highly conserved modular architecture shared across the FGFR family (he2025fgfraberrationsin pages 2-4, chioni2021biologicalsignificanceand pages 2-5). The receptor consists of three principal structural regions: an extracellular ligand-binding region, a single transmembrane helix, and an intracellular tyrosine kinase domain (nguyen2024thecomplexityand pages 6-8, ferguson2021fibroblastgrowthfactor pages 2-3, astolfi2020theemergingrole pages 1-3).

The **extracellular region** comprises three immunoglobulin-like domains (D1, D2, and D3) and an acid box, an acidic serine-rich sequence positioned between D1 and D2 (he2025fgfraberrationsin pages 2-4, nguyen2024thecomplexityand pages 4-6). The D1 domain and acid box contribute to receptor autoinhibition, maintaining FGFR4 in a quiescent state prior to ligand binding (he2025fgfraberrationsin pages 2-4, astolfi2020theemergingrole pages 1-3). The D2 and D3 domains form the principal FGF-binding interface and are essential for ligand recognition and coreceptor interactions (he2025fgfraberrationsin pages 2-4, chioni2021biologicalsignificanceand pages 2-5). Unlike FGFR1–3, which undergo alternative splicing to generate "b" and "c" isoforms of the D3/IgIII domain, FGFR4 exists predominantly as a single c-type isoform, providing it with distinct ligand-binding specificity (nguyen2024thecomplexityand pages 6-8, chioni2021biologicalsignificanceand pages 2-5).

A distinctive structural feature of FGFR4 is the presence of **cysteine 552 (Cys552)** in the kinase-domain hinge region, which is not conserved in FGFR1–3 (nguyen2024thecomplexityand pages 6-8, miranda2020developmentofa pages 1-2). This residue has proven invaluable for therapeutic development, as it can be covalently targeted by highly selective FGFR4 inhibitors, enabling exquisite receptor selectivity over other FGFR family members (nguyen2024thecomplexityand pages 6-8, miranda2020developmentofa pages 1-2, schwarz2024developmentofhighly pages 52-55). The extracellular domains also contain multiple N-glycosylation sites (Asn112 in D1, Asn258 in D2, and Asn290, Asn311, Asn322 in D3) that influence receptor folding, trafficking, processing, and signaling (nguyen2024thecomplexityand pages 6-8, phillips2022glycosylationoffgfr4 pages 6-8).

The **intracellular region** contains a juxtamembrane segment that provides binding sites for adaptor proteins such as FRS2 and CRK/CRKL, followed by a split tyrosine kinase domain (TK1 and TK2 lobes) and a C-terminal regulatory tail (ferguson2021fibroblastgrowthfactor pages 2-3, chioni2021biologicalsignificanceand pages 2-5). Upon ligand-induced receptor dimerization, the kinase domains undergo trans-autophosphorylation, creating docking sites for downstream signaling effectors and activating the catalytic activity required for substrate phosphorylation (xie2020fibroblastgrowthfactor pages 3-4, xie2020fibroblastgrowthfactor pages 4-5, ferguson2021fibroblastgrowthfactor pages 3-5).

## Primary Molecular Function: Ligand Binding and Enzymatic Activity

As a receptor tyrosine kinase, FGFR4's primary molecular function is to bind extracellular FGF ligands and transduce this signal across the plasma membrane through tyrosine phosphorylation of intracellular substrates (he2025fgfraberrationsin pages 2-4, liu2026fgffamilyin pages 3-4, xie2020fibroblastgrowthfactor pages 3-4).

### Ligand Specificity and Substrate Preference

FGFR4 exhibits broad ligand-binding capacity, interacting with multiple FGF family members, but shows particularly high affinity and specificity for **FGF19** (FGF15 in mice) (ferguson2021fibroblastgrowthfactor pages 2-3, edirisinghe2024decodingfgffgfrsignaling pages 14-15). This specificity is structurally encoded by complementary features at the FGF19–FGFR4 interface, including the extended β1 strand of FGF19 and the longer βC′–βE loop of FGFR4 (edirisinghe2024decodingfgffgfrsignaling pages 14-15).

### Catalytic Mechanism and Substrate Phosphorylation

Upon FGF ligand binding, assisted by appropriate coreceptors, FGFR4 undergoes receptor dimerization and conformational changes that bring the intracellular kinase domains into proximity (he2025fgfraberrationsin pages 2-4, xie2020fibroblastgrowthfactor pages 3-4, xie2020fibroblastgrowthfactor pages 4-5). This enables trans-autophosphorylation on tyrosine residues within the activation loop and other regulatory sites, fully activating the kinase (xie2020fibroblastgrowthfactor pages 3-4, xie2020fibroblastgrowthfactor pages 4-5, edirisinghe2024decodingfgffgfrsignaling pages 3-4). The activated FGFR4 kinase catalyzes ATP-dependent phosphorylation of tyrosine residues on two principal intracellular substrates:

1. **FRS2α (FGFR substrate 2α)**: A membrane-associated adaptor protein phosphorylated on multiple tyrosine residues (Tyr-196, Tyr-306, Tyr-349, Tyr-392, Tyr-436, Tyr-471), creating docking sites for GRB2, SOS, and GAB1, thereby initiating RAS/MAPK and PI3K/AKT signaling cascades (liu2026fgffamilyin pages 3-4, xie2020fibroblastgrowthfactor pages 3-4, xie2020fibroblastgrowthfactor pages 4-5, edirisinghe2024decodingfgffgfrsignaling pages 3-4).

2. **PLCγ (Phospholipase C gamma)**: Recruited to phosphorylated FGFR4 docking sites and activated through tyrosine phosphorylation (including Tyr-783), enabling it to hydrolyze PIP2 into IP3 and DAG, thereby controlling calcium mobilization and PKC activation (liu2026fgffamilyin pages 3-4, xie2020fibroblastgrowthfactor pages 3-4, xie2020fibroblastgrowthfactor pages 4-5, edirisinghe2024decodingfgffgfrsignaling pages 3-4).

These phosphorylation events convert FGFR4 activation into a coordinated intracellular signaling response regulating proliferation, survival, differentiation, metabolism, and calcium signaling (liu2026fgffamilyin pages 3-4, xie2020fibroblastgrowthfactor pages 3-4, ferguson2021fibroblastgrowthfactor pages 3-5).

## Cellular Localization and Trafficking

FGFR4 is primarily localized at the **plasma membrane**, where it functions as a cell-surface receptor capable of binding circulating or locally presented FGF ligands (szybowska2021negativeregulationof pages 6-7, szybowska2021negativeregulationof pages 2-4, szybowska2021negativeregulationof pages 4-6). Immunofluorescence studies have detected FGFR4 both at the plasma membrane and in intracellular vesicular compartments, indicating active receptor trafficking (phillips2022glycosylationoffgfr4 pages 6-8).

Following ligand binding and activation, FGFR4 undergoes **clathrin-mediated endocytosis (CME)**, a distinct mechanism compared to FGFR3, which can utilize both CME and clathrin-independent pathways (szybowska2021negativeregulationof pages 2-4, szybowska2021negativeregulationof pages 4-6). A key distinguishing feature of FGFR4 trafficking is its preferential sorting to **recycling compartments** rather than lysosomal degradation pathways (szybowska2021negativeregulationof pages 6-7, szybowska2021negativeregulationof pages 2-4, szybowska2021negativeregulationof pages 4-6, ferguson2021fibroblastgrowthfactor pages 5-7). This recycling route, mediated by Rab11, returns FGFR4 to the plasma membrane and is associated with sustained signaling (szybowska2021negativeregulationof pages 6-7, szybowska2021negativeregulationof pages 4-6). In contrast, FGFR1–3 are predominantly directed to late endosomes and lysosomes for degradation after FGF1 stimulation (szybowska2021negativeregulationof pages 6-7, ferguson2021fibroblastgrowthfactor pages 5-7).

Internalized FGF1–FGFR4 complexes may also traffic through or associate with trans-Golgi network structures (szybowska2021negativeregulationof pages 6-7). Importantly, FGFR signaling can continue from endosomal compartments, and the subcellular localization of FGFR4 influences its signaling output—impaired recycling leads to intracellular FGFR4 accumulation, sustained PLCγ signaling, but reduced AKT pathway activation (szybowska2021negativeregulationof pages 4-6). While FGF and FGFR family members can traffic to the nucleus, the extent and functional significance of nuclear FGFR4 localization remain incompletely defined (szybowska2021negativeregulationof pages 6-7, szybowska2021negativeregulationof pages 2-4).

## Downstream Signaling Pathways

FGFR4 activation initiates four major intracellular signaling cascades that coordinately regulate cellular responses (liu2023fgfrfamiliesbiological pages 9-11, liu2026fgffamilyin pages 3-4, ferguson2021fibroblastgrowthfactor pages 3-5, edirisinghe2024decodingfgffgfrsignaling pages 1-3).

### 1. RAS–RAF–MEK–ERK (MAPK) Pathway

The RAS/MAPK/ERK pathway is a central regulator of proliferation and differentiation downstream of FGFR4 (liu2026fgffamilyin pages 3-4, ferguson2021fibroblastgrowthfactor pages 3-5, edirisinghe2024decodingfgffgfrsignaling pages 1-3, he2025fgfraberrationsin pages 7-9). Phosphorylated FRS2α recruits GRB2 and SOS, leading to RAS activation, followed by sequential activation of RAF, MEK, and ERK1/2 (ferguson2021fibroblastgrowthfactor pages 3-5, edirisinghe2024decodingfgffgfrsignaling pages 1-3, he2025fgfraberrationsin pages 7-9). Activated ERK1/2 translocates to the nucleus and phosphorylates transcription factors including ELK1, FOS, JUN, and ETS-family members, driving cell-cycle gene expression and proliferative responses (ferguson2021fibroblastgrowthfactor pages 3-5, he2025fgfraberrationsin pages 7-9). Persistent MAPK activation contributes to oncogenic signaling in FGFR4-driven cancers (liu2023fgfrfamiliesbiological pages 9-11, ferguson2021fibroblastgrowthfactor pages 3-5, hu2026fgfrsignalingand pages 3-4).

### 2. PI3K–AKT–mTOR Pathway

The PI3K/AKT pathway promotes cell survival, growth, metabolism, and resistance to apoptosis (liu2026fgffamilyin pages 3-4, edirisinghe2024decodingfgffgfrsignaling pages 1-3, he2025fgfraberrationsin pages 7-9). FRS2α-recruited GRB2 and GAB1 activate PI3K, generating PIP3 and activating AKT and PDK1 (ferguson2021fibroblastgrowthfactor pages 3-5, edirisinghe2024decodingfgffgfrsignaling pages 1-3, he2025fgfraberrationsin pages 7-9). AKT phosphorylates numerous substrates, including TSC2, FOXO transcription factors, and apoptosis regulators such as BAD, BAX, and caspase-9, thereby supporting survival and metabolic reprogramming (liu2026fgffamilyin pages 3-4, edirisinghe2024decodingfgffgfrsignaling pages 1-3). Downstream mTORC1 activation regulates protein synthesis and cell growth (liu2026fgffamilyin pages 3-4, he2025fgfraberrationsin pages 7-9). In FGFR4-driven hepatocellular carcinoma, the PI3K/AKT/HIF1α axis can establish positive feedback loops promoting metastasis (liu2023fgfrfamiliesbiological pages 9-11).

### 3. PLCγ–PKC–Ca²⁺ Pathway

PLCγ binds to phosphorylated FGFR4 docking sites and, upon activation, hydrolyzes PIP2 into IP3 and DAG (liu2026fgffamilyin pages 3-4, ferguson2021fibroblastgrowthfactor pages 3-5, edirisinghe2024decodingfgffgfrsignaling pages 1-3, he2025fgfraberrationsin pages 7-9). IP3 triggers intracellular calcium release from the endoplasmic reticulum, while DAG and calcium together activate protein kinase C (PKC) (liu2026fgffamilyin pages 3-4, ferguson2021fibroblastgrowthfactor pages 3-5, he2025fgfraberrationsin pages 7-9, he2025fgfraberrationsin pages 9-10). This pathway regulates calcium-dependent transcription factors like NFAT, influences differentiation and migration, and can support cancer stem-cell self-renewal through store-operated calcium entry in FGFR4-expressing hepatocellular carcinoma (liu2023fgfrfamiliesbiological pages 9-11, ferguson2021fibroblastgrowthfactor pages 3-5).

### 4. SRC–STAT3 and JAK–STAT Pathway

FGFR4 can activate STAT transcription factors, particularly STAT3, through both direct and SRC-mediated mechanisms (liu2023fgfrfamiliesbiological pages 9-11, ferguson2021fibroblastgrowthfactor pages 3-5, edirisinghe2024decodingfgffgfrsignaling pages 1-3). In FGF19-expressing hepatocellular carcinoma cells, FGFR4 forms an endosomal signaling complex with SRC and STAT3 that traffics to the nucleus, where STAT3 functions as a transcriptional regulator (liu2023fgfrfamiliesbiological pages 9-11). SRC is essential for endosomal delivery of this complex and nuclear STAT3 signaling (liu2023fgfrfamiliesbiological pages 9-11). This pathway regulates survival, proliferation, and oncogenic transcription programs (liu2023fgfrfamiliesbiological pages 9-11, ferguson2021fibroblastgrowthfactor pages 3-5, edirisinghe2024decodingfgffgfrsignaling pages 1-3).

## Biological Functions and Tissue-Specific Roles

### Bile Acid Metabolism and Hepatic Homeostasis

A defining physiological role of FGFR4 is its central function in the **enterohepatic regulation of bile acid synthesis** through the FGF19–FGFR4–β-Klotho signaling axis (li2024targetingthefgf19–fgfr4 pages 3-3, li2024targetingthefgf19–fgfr4 pages 2-3, li2024targetingthefgf19–fgfr4 pages 2-2, edirisinghe2024decodingfgffgfrsignaling pages 14-15). This endocrine feedback loop operates as follows:

Following a meal, bile acids reach the ileum and activate the nuclear receptor FXR (farnesoid X receptor), which induces transcription and secretion of FGF19 (or FGF15 in mice) (li2024targetingthefgf19–fgfr4 pages 3-3, li2024targetingthefgf19–fgfr4 pages 2-3, li2024targetingthefgf19–fgfr4 pages 2-2, xu2026fgf19insolid pages 6-7). Circulating FGF19 then binds to the hepatic FGFR4–β-Klotho receptor complex on hepatocytes, activating downstream ERK1/2 and JNK signaling (li2024targetingthefgf19–fgfr4 pages 3-3, li2024targetingthefgf19–fgfr4 pages 2-3, li2024targetingthefgf19–fgfr4 pages 2-2). This signal transcriptionally suppresses **CYP7A1** (cholesterol 7α-hydroxylase), the rate-limiting enzyme in the classical bile acid synthesis pathway, thereby reducing hepatic conversion of cholesterol into bile acids and maintaining bile acid homeostasis (li2024targetingthefgf19–fgfr4 pages 3-3, li2024targetingthefgf19–fgfr4 pages 2-3, li2024targetingthefgf19–fgfr4 pages 2-2, xu2026fgf19insolid pages 6-7, edirisinghe2024decodingfgffgfrsignaling pages 14-15, li2024targetingthefgf19–fgfr4 pages 16-16).

Experimental evidence strongly supports this model: liver-specific FGFR4 knockdown in mice fed a high-fat diet increased bile acid synthesis, elevated serum and hepatic bile acids, and upregulated Cyp7a1 expression, demonstrating that hepatic FGFR4 normally restrains bile acid production (moreau2023liverspecificfgfr4knockdown pages 5-8). Conversely, FGFR4-deficient mice exhibit elevated cholesterol metabolism and bile acid synthesis (li2024targetingthefgf19–fgfr4 pages 16-16). This pathway is therapeutically relevant for cholestatic diseases such as primary biliary cholangitis (PBC) and primary sclerosing cholangitis (PSC), as well as metabolic disorders including nonalcoholic steatohepatitis (NASH) (li2024targetingthefgf19–fgfr4 pages 3-3, li2024targetingthefgf19–fgfr4 pages 14-14).

### Liver Regeneration and Hepatocyte Function

FGFR4 plays an essential role in **liver regeneration** following injury (li2024targetingthefgf19–fgfr4 pages 14-14, ornitz2022newdevelopmentsin pages 59-60). Loss of FGFR4 severely impairs liver regeneration after partial hepatectomy, and combined deletion of FGFR1, FGFR2, and FGFR4 in hepatocytes causes liver failure, demonstrating the critical and partially redundant functions of FGFR signaling in hepatic tissue (ornitz2022newdevelopmentsin pages 59-60). FGF19 signaling through hepatic FGFR4 accelerates restoration of liver mass in wild-type mice but not in FGFR4-knockdown animals (li2024targetingthefgf19–fgfr4 pages 14-14). FGFR4 is predominantly expressed in liver and lung tissues, with particularly important hepatic functions (liu2023fgfrfamiliesbiological pages 9-11, ornitz2022newdevelopmentsin pages 59-60, xu2026fgf19insolid pages 6-7, li2024targetingthefgf19–fgfr4 pages 14-14).

### Cancer Biology: Oncogenic Signaling and Therapeutic Targeting

Aberrant FGFR4 signaling, particularly through the FGF19–FGFR4 axis, is a critical oncogenic driver in multiple cancer types, most notably **hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC)** (li2024targetingthefgf19–fgfr4 pages 12-13, miranda2020developmentofa pages 1-2, li2024targetingthefgf19–fgfr4 pages 11-12, xu2026fgf19insolid pages 31-33, xu2026fgf19insolid pages 30-31). Approximately 30% of HCC patients have tumors with increased FGFR4 expression, and FGF19 amplification occurs in a substantial subset (miranda2020developmentofa pages 1-2). FGF19/FGFR4 pathway activation promotes tumor-cell proliferation, survival, invasion, metastasis, angiogenesis, altered metabolism, and treatment resistance (miranda2020developmentofa pages 1-2, li2024targetingthefgf19–fgfr4 pages 11-12, xu2026fgf19insolid pages 31-33, xu2026fgf19insolid pages 30-31).

In HCC models, FGF19 transgenic mice develop liver tumors, and this tumorigenesis is prevented by FGFR4 knockout, establishing FGFR4 as the critical mediator of FGF19-driven hepatocarcinogenesis (miranda2020developmentofa pages 1-2). Beyond HCC, FGFR4 overexpression has been reported in colorectal, breast, gastric, thyroid, nasopharyngeal, and other cancers, where it contributes to oncogenic signaling, epithelial–mesenchymal transition, metastasis, and resistance to chemotherapy (liu2023fgfrfamiliesbiological pages 9-11, xu2026fgf19insolid pages 30-31, nguyen2024thecomplexityand pages 14-16).

## Recent Developments (2023–2024)

### Novel FGFR4 Inhibitors and Clinical Translation

Significant progress has been made in developing highly selective FGFR4 inhibitors that exploit the unique Cys552 residue for covalent targeting (li2024targetingthefgf19–fgfr4 pages 12-13, miranda2020developmentofa pages 1-2, li2024targetingthefgf19–fgfr4 pages 11-12, xu2026fgf19insolid pages 31-33, schwarz2024developmentofhighly pages 52-55). In 2024, a new class of **SNAr-based covalent FGFR4 inhibitors** was reported, demonstrating low to subnanomolar potency, efficient covalent inactivation kinetics, excellent selectivity against FGFR1–3, good microsomal stability, and nanomolar cellular potency (schwarz2024developmentofhighly pages 52-55).

Several selective FGFR4 inhibitors have advanced into clinical trials for FGF19-positive HCC and intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma (li2024targetingthefgf19–fgfr4 pages 12-13, li2024targetingthefgf19–fgfr4 pages 11-12, xu2026fgf19insolid pages 31-33, nguyen2024thecomplexityand pages 12-14):

- **H3B-6527**: A highly selective, irreversible FGFR4 inhibitor that received FDA orphan drug designation. In phase I trials in advanced HCC, it produced a 16.7% overall response rate, 10.6-month overall survival, and 4.1-month progression-free survival (li2024targetingthefgf19–fgfr4 pages 12-13).

- **Roblitinib (FGF401)**: A reversible-covalent FGFR4 inhibitor evaluated alone and in combination with the PD-1 inhibitor spartalizumab. While responses were observed, activity was modest, potentially because patients were not preselected for FGF19–FGFR4 pathway abnormalities (li2024targetingthefgf19–fgfr4 pages 12-13, li2024targetingthefgf19–fgfr4 pages 11-12).

- **Fisogatinib (BLU-554)**: A selective covalent FGFR4 inhibitor evaluated in phase Ib/II trials, including combination with the anti-PD-L1 antibody CS1001 in advanced or metastatic HCC (li2024targetingthefgf19–fgfr4 pages 12-13, li2024targetingthefgf19–fgfr4 pages 11-12, nguyen2024thecomplexityand pages 12-14, schwarz2024developmentofhighly pages 52-55).

- **Irpagratinib (ABSK011)**: An irreversible FGFR4 inhibitor targeting Cys552 that, in previously treated advanced HCC patients with FGF19 overexpression, produced a 44.8% objective response rate, 7.4-month median response duration, and 5.5-month median progression-free survival (xu2026fgf19insolid pages 31-33, nguyen2024thecomplexityand pages 12-14).

These clinical data underscore the importance of patient selection based on FGF19 overexpression or FGFR4 positivity for optimal therapeutic benefit (li2024targetingthefgf19–fgfr4 pages 12-13, nguyen2024thecomplexityand pages 12-14).

### Metabolic Disease and Hepatic Steatosis

A 2023 study demonstrated that **liver-specific FGFR4 knockdown** in mice on a high-fat diet increased bile acid synthesis and **improved hepatic steatosis**, suggesting that reducing hepatic FGFR4 signaling may have therapeutic potential for nonalcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD) (moreau2023liverspecificfgfr4knockdown pages 5-8, moreau2023liverspecificfgfr4knockdown pages 15-15). This finding expands the therapeutic scope of FGFR4 modulation beyond cancer to include metabolic disorders (li2024targetingthefgf19–fgfr4 pages 12-13, fan2025structurebasedidentificationof pages 22-23).

### Expanded Understanding of FGF19–FGFR4 in Cholestatic and Metabolic Diseases

Recent reviews have synthesized emerging evidence that the FGF19–FGFR4 pathway is relevant not only for cancer but also for cholestatic diseases (PBC, PSC), metabolic abnormalities (NASH), and bile acid homeostasis disorders (li2024targetingthefgf19–fgfr4 pages 3-3, li2024targetingthefgf19–fgfr4 pages 12-13, fan2025structurebasedidentificationof pages 22-23). This expanded pathophysiological framework positions FGFR4 as a therapeutic target spanning oncology, hepatology, and metabolism.

### Combination Strategies and Resistance Mechanisms

Emerging therapeutic strategies include combinations of FGFR4 inhibitors with immune checkpoint inhibitors (anti-PD-1, anti-PD-L1) and CDK4/6 inhibitors (li2024targetingthefgf19–fgfr4 pages 12-13, li2024targetingthefgf19–fgfr4 pages 11-12, xu2026fgf19insolid pages 31-33, schwarz2024developmentofhighly pages 52-55). Preclinical evidence suggests that pan-FGFR inhibitors such as LY2874455 can overcome resistance to selective FGFR4 inhibitors like fisogatinib, highlighting the need for next-generation agents and rational combination approaches (li2024targetingthefgf19–fgfr4 pages 12-13).

## Conclusions

FGFR4 is a receptor tyrosine kinase with distinctive structural features, including a unique Cys552 residue exploited for selective therapeutic targeting and a single c-type isoform conferring specific ligand-binding properties. Its primary molecular function is to bind FGF ligands—particularly FGF19—and transduce signals through tyrosine phosphorylation of FRS2α and PLCγ, activating RAS/MAPK, PI3K/AKT, PLCγ/PKC/Ca²⁺, and SRC/STAT3 pathways. FGFR4 is localized at the plasma membrane and undergoes clathrin-mediated endocytosis with preferential recycling that sustains signaling. Its most well-defined physiological role is in the enterohepatic regulation of bile acid synthesis through the FGF19–FGFR4–β-Klotho axis, which suppresses CYP7A1 and maintains bile acid homeostasis. FGFR4 is also critical for liver regeneration and, when dysregulated, drives oncogenic signaling in hepatocellular carcinoma and other cancers. Recent developments (2023–2024) include advanced selective covalent inhibitors in clinical trials, expanded therapeutic applications in metabolic and cholestatic diseases, and evidence that modulating hepatic FGFR4 can improve hepatic steatosis, establishing FGFR4 as a multifaceted target in precision medicine.
