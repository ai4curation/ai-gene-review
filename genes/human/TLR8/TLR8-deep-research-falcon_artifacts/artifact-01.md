| Category | Human TLR8 characteristics | Functional significance and evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Identity | **TLR8 (CD288)**; human Toll-like receptor 8; UniProt **Q9NR97**; type I transmembrane pattern-recognition receptor in the Toll-like receptor family. | Literature identifies human TLR8 as an intracellular RNA sensor, matching the supplied UniProt identity. (pqac-00000001, pqac-00000011) |
| Domain architecture | N-terminal signal peptide; large luminal ectodomain with approximately **26 leucine-rich repeats (LRRs)**, a cysteine-rich region, and a characteristic **Z-loop** between LRR14 and LRR15; one transmembrane helix; cytosolic **Toll/IL-1 receptor (TIR) domain**. | LRRs form the ligand-binding ectodomain, whereas the TIR domain recruits signaling adaptors. Z-loop processing and receptor rearrangement contribute to activation. (pqac-00000011, pqac-00000028) |
| Biochemical class | Signaling receptor, **not an enzyme or transporter**; it has ligands rather than catalytic substrates and does not catalyze a chemical reaction. | Its primary molecular function is to convert detection of endosomal RNA degradation products into MyD88-dependent inflammatory and antimicrobial signaling. (pqac-00000002, pqac-00000016) |
| Natural ligands | Pathogen-derived and, under pathological conditions, self-derived **single-stranded RNA (ssRNA)** and RNA degradation products. Viral, bacterial, mitochondrial, microRNA, and ribonucleoprotein-associated RNA can stimulate TLR8. | Endosomal sequestration focuses recognition on RNA delivered through infection, uptake, or phagocytosis, although access of self-RNA can drive inflammation. (pqac-00000001, pqac-00000007, pqac-00000057) |
| Ligand specificity | Responds to **AU-rich and GU-rich ssRNA**, RNA secondary structures, free **uridine**, and short guanosine-rich oligoribonucleotides. Unmodified RNA is generally more stimulatory than host RNA containing immunosuppressive nucleoside modifications. | Current structural understanding indicates that TLR8 detects combinations of RNA catabolites rather than merely binding intact long ssRNA as one undivided ligand. (pqac-00000000, pqac-00000024, pqac-00000040) |
| First binding site | Conserved site at the dimer interface binds **uridine** and synthetic small-molecule agonists; important contacts include Phe405 and Asp543, with contributions from Tyr348, Val520, and Thr574. | Ligand binding stabilizes the two protomers in the active dimer configuration. (pqac-00000025, pqac-00000029) |
| Second binding site | Distinct oligonucleotide-binding site on the concave LRR surface, principally involving **LRR9–13** and part of the Z-loop. | Occupancy synergizes with first-site ligands by reducing electrostatic repulsion and lowering the energetic barrier to receptor activation. (pqac-00000024, pqac-00000029) |
| Synthetic agonists | **Resiquimod/R848**, CL075, and CL097 activate TLR8 or TLR7/8; more selective TLR8 agonists include **motolimod/VTX-2337**, **selgantolimod/GS-9688**, and **TL8-506**. | These compounds reproduce ligand-induced receptor rearrangement and are used to stimulate inflammatory, antiviral, or antitumor immunity. (pqac-00000047, pqac-00000048, pqac-00000049) |
| Antagonists | TLR8-selective **CU-CPT compounds**, including CU-CPT9b, and TH1027; dual TLR7/8 antagonist programs include M5049-related compounds. | Antagonists occupy interprotomer pockets and stabilize the inactive dimer, blocking the activating conformational transition; development remains investigational. (pqac-00000027, pqac-00000047, pqac-00000053) |
| Activation mechanism | TLR8 exists as a **preformed inactive homodimer**. Agonist binding reorganizes the dimer and reduces the separation between ectodomain C termini from approximately **50 Å to 30 Å**. | C-terminal approximation permits productive association of cytosolic TIR domains and recruitment of downstream adaptors. (pqac-00000024, pqac-00000025, pqac-00000027) |
| Subcellular localization | Predominantly an **endosomal/endolysosomal membrane receptor**; ligand recognition occurs on the luminal side, while signaling begins through cytosolic TIR domains. | This topology enables TLR8 to inspect internalized nucleic acids while limiting indiscriminate recognition of extracellular or cytosolic self-RNA. (pqac-00000008, pqac-00000009, pqac-00000012) |
| Trafficking | Synthesized through the endoplasmic reticulum; its signal peptide and the chaperone **UNC93B1** support transport to endosomes. | Loss of UNC93B1 or disruption of the signal peptide impairs endosomal localization, showing that localization is actively regulated. (pqac-00000008, pqac-00000010, pqac-00000014) |
| Cellular expression | Strongly associated with **CD14-positive monocytes, macrophages, neutrophils, myeloid dendritic cells**, and subsets of T cells, including regulatory T cells; functional activity also occurs in NK-cell contexts. | TLR8 is primarily a myeloid inflammatory sensor. In human monocytes it frequently dominates responses to dual TLR7/8 agonists. (pqac-00000031, pqac-00000036, pqac-00000039) |
| Proximal signaling | Ligand-induced TIR-domain juxtaposition recruits **MyD88**; evidence supports participation of **TIRAP/MAL**, followed by **IRAK4** and **IRAK1** in a Myddosome-like complex. | TLR8 uses a MyD88-dependent pathway rather than the TRIF-dominant pathway of TLR3; TIRAP requirements may vary by cell type and signaling branch. (pqac-00000018, pqac-00000019, pqac-00000020) |
| Core inflammatory pathway | **TLR8 → TIRAP/MyD88 → IRAK4/IRAK1 → TRAF6 → TAK1–TAB1/TAB2/3 → IKK and MAPKs**. | IKK releases **NF-κB** from IκB inhibition, while MAPKs support AP-1 activity; together they drive inflammatory gene expression. (pqac-00000017, pqac-00000021, pqac-00000022) |
| Interferon branch | Context-dependent activation of **IRF3, IRF5, and IRF7**, with possible participation of Akt and IKK-family signaling. | This supports IFN-β and antiviral responses, although human TLR8 is more strongly associated with inflammatory cytokines than the TLR7-driven IFN-α response of plasmacytoid dendritic cells. (pqac-00000017, pqac-00000023, pqac-00000043) |
| Major outputs | **TNF-α, IL-1α/β, IL-6, IL-8/CXCL8, IL-12, IL-27, MIP-1α**, and context-dependent type I interferons, especially **IFN-β**. | Output varies with cell type, ligand, dose, and infection; excessive signaling can cause harmful inflammation as well as antimicrobial protection. (pqac-00000017, pqac-00000033, pqac-00000060) |
| Primary functions | Detects microbial RNA and initiates **innate antibacterial and antiviral immunity**, including inflammatory transcription, chemokine release, leukocyte activation, and enhanced antigen presentation. | TLR8 connects phagosomal or endosomal microbial degradation to immune activation and helps recognize viable bacteria through bacterial RNA. (pqac-00000001, pqac-00000031, pqac-00000057) |
| Innate–adaptive linkage | Promotes dendritic-cell maturation, costimulatory and MHC-II expression, Th1 and T-follicular-helper differentiation, antigen-specific CD8-positive T-cell priming, and NK-cell antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity; it can reduce regulatory T-cell suppression. | These functions underpin development of TLR8 agonists as vaccine adjuvants and cancer immunotherapies. (pqac-00000020, pqac-00000031, pqac-00000037) |
| Infection examples | Studied in **HIV-1, influenza A, SARS-CoV-2, hepatitis B and C, Zika virus, respiratory syncytial virus, and Coxsackie B virus** infections. | Effects are context-dependent: TLR8 can improve antiviral defense but may also promote immunopathology, neutrophil activation, or HIV latency reversal and replication-supporting cytokines. (pqac-00000032, pqac-00000033, pqac-00000034) |
| Disease relevance | Human **TLR8 gain-of-function variants** are associated with neutropenia or other cytopenias, low IgG, hepatosplenomegaly, elevated inflammatory cytokines, and hemophagocytosis; links to systemic juvenile idiopathic arthritis and adult-onset Still’s disease are under investigation. | Human genetics supports a causal role for dysregulated TLR8 signaling in inflammatory disease, although its contribution to common autoimmune disorders is less established than TLR7’s. (pqac-00000055, pqac-00000058, pqac-00000062) |
| Therapeutic applications | Agonists are being explored as **vaccine adjuvants, antiviral agents, HIV latency-reversing agents, and cancer immunotherapies**; antagonists are being developed for autoimmune and autoinflammatory disease. | Selgantolimod underwent phase II evaluation in chronic hepatitis B, while motolimod and localized TLR7/8 formulations have been studied in oncology. Systemic agonism can cause cytokine-mediated toxicity. (pqac-00000048, pqac-00000049, pqac-00000051) |
| Translational caveat | Human TLR8 findings should not be inferred uncritically from conventional mouse models because ligand sensing and function differ substantially between species. | Human primary cells, human genetics, and appropriate humanized models are particularly important for functional annotation and therapeutic development. (pqac-00000036, pqac-00000062) |


*Table: This table consolidates the identity, architecture, ligand recognition, localization, signaling, cellular functions, disease relevance, and therapeutic targeting of human TLR8 (UniProt Q9NR97). It emphasizes evidence supporting TLR8 as an endosomal RNA-degradation-product sensor that drives predominantly MyD88-dependent myeloid inflammatory responses.*
