### Two performed catalytic activities

CTPS1 catalyzes ATP-dependent amination of UTP, using glutamine as the nitrogen source. Human protein expressed in yeast has been purified and tested directly; human patient deficiency, knockdown and reconstitution provide complementary cellular evidence (PMID:17189248, PMID:17463002, PMID:28459447, PMID:24870241). The 2005 complete abstract supports human complementation of the yeast ura7/ura8 double mutant, but its full original remains unavailable. Its glucose-associated phosphorylation observation does not establish direct PKA activation. The recovered 2007 purified-enzyme experiment instead measures PKA inhibition, and PKC effects depend on the tested phosphorylation sites and preparation.

Human CTPS1 forms homotetramers and regulated stacked-tetramer filaments. In the substrate-associated preparations, polymerization enhances activity; H355A retains tetramers and residual activity. This does not make every cellular filament active or polymerization obligatory for every catalytic event. The bacterial filament state differs from the human state tested in PMID:28459447. PMID:34583994 compares the CTPS1 and CTPS2 paralogs; its use of “isoforms” is not evidence about the official CTPS1 splice product. The compact nucleotide-synthesis core uses the existing de novo CTP biosynthesis term, without a redundant additional biosynthesis core.

A separate catalytic role is protein asparagine deamidation. PMID:40240600 uses human HeLa-derived CTPS1 cDNA, purified HEK293T Flag-CTPS1 and bacterial H1.4, with catalytic mutants and direct biochemical endpoints. H1 N76/N77 deamidation permits subsequent EP300 acetylation and chromatin relaxation during DNA repair. CTPS1 performs deamidation; EP300 performs acetylation. PMID:39719712 establishes an IRF3 substrate context, including human endogenous HEK293T/THP-1 perturbations and mouse genetic experiments. The expression-plasmid accession is not specified sufficiently to relabel every recombinant experiment as human IDA. The mouse donor evidence and human cellular corroboration remain distinct. These two substrate contexts support one broad deamidase core with explicit H1 and IRF3 mechanisms, alongside the already present H1-specific annotations.
