# EPHB4 functional review

## Receptor mechanism and vascular role

EPHB4 encodes an active ephrin-B receptor tyrosine kinase. The displayed human product is a 987-residue membrane receptor with an extracellular ephrin-binding region and a cytoplasmic kinase domain. Human receptor activation by ephrin-B2 is supported by the operative experiments in [PMID:27400125](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4966301/), while purified human EPHB4 residues 588–987 phosphorylate protein substrate in [PMID:34431498](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8454701/), Figure 4. The kinase reagent is not a full-length receptor. These complementary assays support the specific transmembrane-ephrin receptor activity, GO:0005005.

EphB4–ephrin-B2 contact is bidirectional. Receptor activation and ephrin-B2 reverse signaling both affect endothelial behavior. In [PMID:16424904](https://www.embopress.org/doi/full/10.1038/sj.emboj.7600949), endothelial full-length and artificial kinase-truncated EphB4 change vessel architecture and sprouting. The results support angiogenesis without reducing every effect to receptor forward catalysis. Human HUVEC and MCF-7 experiments in [PMID:22555806](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.4161/cbt.20080) show opposite ERK responses to the same receptor stimulus, with different downstream dependencies. A single universal positive or negative ERK-regulation claim would lose this context.

Human vascular and lymphatic variants can impair receptor activation, abundance or localization. [PMID:30578106](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10292091/), Figure 4 and STAR Methods, tests HA-tagged **mouse** Ephb4 in human HEK293T, including Ras-GAP association. This supports a conserved GAP-binding role with an explicit preparation boundary. It does not establish that EPHB4 itself catalyzes GTP hydrolysis. Clinical vascular phenotypes are not interchangeable with direct measurements of every angiogenic step.

## Interaction specificity

Generic binding rows are refined only where a supported partner role exists: ephrin-B2 binding contributes to the receptor activity; RASA1 association is GAP binding; the EPHA5/EPHA6/EPHA7 AP-MS associations fit Eph-receptor binding. The latter assays establish complex association, not a purified binary interface, trans-cell orientation or ligand agonism. Actual target rows and methods are listed in the [source guide](EPHB4-source-guide.md).

[PMID:26481148](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4643364/) also reports erythropoietin binding and receptor-dependent activation in selected human tumor-cell models. Its recombinant binding and receptor-knockdown controls justify a contextual receptor-activity refinement. EPO–EphB4 apparent affinity is weaker than the EPOR comparison, recombinant boundaries are incompletely specified in the main Methods, and these experiments do not establish a general physiological erythropoiesis role. This activity is not promoted into a second core function. Current PubMed metadata had no linked correction or retraction; that check does not establish agreement throughout subsequent literature.

## Natural products and processing

All four source product identifiers remain distinct. Independent official FASTA sequences exactly match reconstruction from the UniProt VSP edits: P54760-1, 987 residues; P54760-2, 516; P54760-3, 306; P54760-4, 414. The shorter products lack the displayed transmembrane helix and kinase region. [PMID:18593464](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2575619/), Table 1 and Additional file 1, identifies their cloned transcripts, but the ten functionally tested ASVs in Table 2 exclude these EPHB4 variants. Their secretion, ligand affinity and physiological roles remain unestablished. Engineered soluble EphB4 constructs do not supply that missing product-specific evidence.

Reactome explicitly models human EPHB4 extracellular and cytosolic cleavage fragments. Those are separate from the natural splice products. The recovered original cleavage papers [PMID:18713744](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2570862/) and [PMID:17428795](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4005067/) directly test EphB2, with variable family susceptibility discussed. The EPHB4 fragment annotations remain undecided pending target-specific support. This is an experimental-transfer uncertainty, not a claim that the target is absent from the model.
