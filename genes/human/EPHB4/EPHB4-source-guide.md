# EPHB4 source guide

This guide distinguishes original experiments, database assertions and access limits. It provides locators, not an independent experimental citation. Publication caches retain their fetched bytes and metadata; a cached `full_text_available` flag does not guarantee that a required figure, method or supplementary target table is present. No publication quotations were added.

## Natural products and modeled cleavage fragments

[PMID:18593464](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2575619/), Methods and Table 1, plus Additional file 1 entries **EU826608/012D11**, **EU826609/012C08** and **EU826610/012E11**, identifies EPHB4 products of 516, 306 and 414 residues. Table 2's ten functionally tested variants do **not** include these EPHB4 products. Reconstructing the current UniProt VSP edits gives sequences identical to the independently downloaded official P54760-1/-2/-3/-4 FASTAs (987/516/306/414 residues). This proves source sequence consistency, not secretion, ligand trapping, or equal function.

The source [UniProt P54760](https://www.uniprot.org/uniprotkb/P54760/entry) describes the displayed receptor's extracellular region, transmembrane helix at 540–560 and cytoplasmic kinase. Each shorter product lacks the displayed transmembrane and kinase regions. Proteolytic fragments in Reactome are a separate product category from these splice products.

[PMID:18713744](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2570862/), Experimental Procedures, Results and the cleavage-site discussion, directly examines EphB2 with metalloproteinases and notes variable family susceptibility. Its double-site construct designation must not be mistaken for EPHB4. [PMID:17428795](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4005067/), Experimental Procedures and Results/Figures 1–2 captions, examines murine EphB2 processing, with HEK293T as a host and separate mouse/rat material. No EPHB4 cleavage-site measurement was recovered in these originals.

Current [Reactome R-HSA-3928657](https://reactome.org/content/detail/R-HSA-3928657) and [R-HSA-3928656](https://reactome.org/content/detail/R-HSA-3928656) explicitly contain human EPHB4 fragment members: extracellular set 3928488→member 3928420 and cytosolic set 3928494→member 3928406, each linked to P54760 reference entity 54382. The uncertainty concerns experimental transfer of the cleavage mechanism to EPHB4, **not** missing target membership or the compartment label of an unrelated event.

[PMID:23533145](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3773505/), main Methods/Results, describes expressed-prostatic-secretion urine pools, vesicle isolation and LC-MS/MS. EPHB4-specific peptides in Supplemental Table 2 were not independently recovered. Vesicle detection and exosome biogenesis are different assertions.

## Computational source chains

The actual PANTHER v19 human leaf **PTN002521330** descends from both imported IBD nodes **PTN001230349** and **PTN000700012**. The target path contains the relevant positive assertions and no inspected NOT assertion. Official human/mouse sequences match the MSA leaves. The current UniProt family label and archived v19 family label differ by release; this is not evidence that the target lies outside the IBD clade. Mouse P54761 has experimental PMID:10518221 assertions, so its pathway transfer is not merely reciprocal electronic propagation.

The actual [InterPro2GO mapping](https://ftp.ebi.ac.uk/pub/databases/interpro/current_release/interpro2go), [Rhea 10596](https://www.rhea-db.org/rhea/10596) and [UniProt SL-0039](https://www.uniprot.org/locations/SL-0039) support protein-tyrosine kinase chemistry, ATP binding and plasma membrane. Current [ARBA00090097](https://www.uniprot.org/arba/ARBA00090097) and [ARBA00086040](https://www.uniprot.org/arba/ARBA00086040) emit angiogenesis and ephrin receptor activity, respectively. Target-specific FunFam eligibility was not fully replayed because those assignments were absent from the inspected target JSON; their biological conclusions were assessed against independent evidence.

The specific receptor replacement [GO:0005005](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/QuickGO/term/GO:0005005) is **transmembrane-ephrin receptor activity**, not the GPI-linked ephrin receptor term. Its current ancestry includes the broader kinase/receptor terms it replaces. Partner refinements [GO:0032794](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/QuickGO/term/GO:0032794) and [GO:0046875](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/QuickGO/term/GO:0046875) describe GAP and Eph-receptor binding without asserting their partners' catalytic activities.

### Follow-up assay locators

For PMID:26481148, Figure 1 and the MST/SPR Methods establish recombinant EPO–EphB4 binding; Figure 2 and its Results separate A2780 EPOR knockdown from combined EPOR/EPHB4 knockdown and receptor addition in COS-1 cells. Figure 3 separates EPHB4-dependent SRC/STAT3 from EPOR-dependent JAK2/STAT5 signaling. The positive binding and signal-transmission evidence supports the contextual receptor activity. The exact commercial-protein boundaries and general physiological erythropoiesis are not established by these observations.

For PMID:16424904, Results “Generation of recombinant retrovirus-producing cell lines,” “Interference with endothelial EphB4 signaling,” “Expression of endothelial EphB4 results in circumferential blood vessel growth,” and the retinal-vascularization section were read with Methods on retroviral generation, mouse models, intravital microscopy, immunohistochemistry and retinal preparation. The historical wording about caption access did not mean that the main Results or Methods were unavailable. These data identify remodeling and branching effects with both full-length and kinase-truncated reagents; initial vessel formation and subsequent patterning are distinguished.
