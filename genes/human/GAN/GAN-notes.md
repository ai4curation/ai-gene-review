## 2026-11-04 — Substrate recognition and cytoskeletal homeostasis

Human gigaxonin is a 597-residue BTB/Kelch protein. Its principal molecular role is substrate recognition within a CUL3–RBX1 ubiquitin ligase. Purified human reconstitution provides stronger mechanistic grounding than a loss-of-function phenotype alone: gigaxonin participates in the reaction by recruiting substrates, while the RING/E2 machinery transfers ubiquitin. Neurofilament and actin-associated substrate turnover helps explain the wider cytoskeletal phenotype of giant axonal neuropathy (PMID:37903270).

The earlier MAP1B and TBCB studies support particular recognition and degradation relationships. MAP1B-mediated microtubule association is indirect and is distinct from the later turnover experiments. Intermediate-filament studies show Kelch-dependent associations and proteasome-sensitive clearance; disease and cellular phenotypes do not make gigaxonin a protease, a microtubule motor or a general chaperone (PMID:12147674, PMID:16227972, PMID:16303566, PMID:23585478, PMID:26460568).

The selected original reconstitution study tests NEFL, INA, TPM1, TAGLN and CNN2 with purified components. TPM2 has broader cellular evidence but is not silently added to the listed purified assay. NEFM was not affected in the tested systems. Human GST-gigaxonin reagent identity does not establish its complete purchased sequence. The 1–258 GAN fragment described in the selected structural original is also kept distinct from the 1–254 UniProt bibliography coordinate and from the current full-length protein (PMID:37903270; PMID:19818708).

Loss of gigaxonin increases soluble intermediate-filament oligomers and impairs filament transport. Rescue by direct kinesin–filament tethering is evidence about the resulting transport defect, not identification of gigaxonin as the missing motor adaptor. Mitochondrial motility changes remain downstream or system-specific. Glycosylation of gigaxonin and its NUDCD3 associations provide regulatory context without assigning the modifying enzyme's or chaperone's activity to gigaxonin (PMID:37043392, PMID:27000625, PMID:31944090, PMID:40749357).
