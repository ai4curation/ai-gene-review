# GATA1 source and interpretation guide

GATA1 is a nuclear GATA-motif transcription factor that controls erythroid and megakaryocytic gene expression and contributes to eosinophil lineage commitment. The review distinguishes DNA recognition, recruitment of named transcriptional partners, and downstream cell phenotypes. It does not treat all GATA1-associated proteins as a single constitutive complex.

The proposal contains 120 original contexts, one core function, 54 assessed references and three sequence-verified products. Actions are 57 ACCEPT, 33 MODIFY, 10 KEEP_AS_NON_CORE, 1 TOO_GENERAL, 8 REMOVE, 8 UNDECIDED and 3 MARK_AS_OVER_ANNOTATED. No NEW annotation is proposed. Every original context has readable evidence links; the few exact excerpts are centralized in the relevant row, core or reference finding, while other claims link to the same assessed references.

## DNA recognition and regulatory complexes

Human gamma-globin promoter experiments identify the historical erythroid factor GF-1/GATA1 and connect a defined recognition motif to promoter activity ([PMID:2467208](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2467208/)). The original ZNF268 study adds EMSA competition and supershift, GATA1/FOG chromatin occupancy and mutant-site reporter tests in K562 cells ([PMID:22235304](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3250450/), Results paragraphs 19 and 21 and Methods). Its cell-growth experiments perturb ZNF268, not GATA1. These experiments support the specific PolII regulatory-DNA replacements without claiming that GATA1 performs every downstream phenotype.

FOG1, MED1 and CCAR1 have different partner roles. The V205M and D218G studies separate impaired FOG recruitment from retained DNA recognition; the R216Q study instead impairs palindromic-site recognition while preserving FOG interaction ([PMID:10700180](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10700180/), [PMID:11418466](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11418466/), [PMID:12200364](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12200364/)). The complex study resolves TAL1/LDB1, FOG1/MeCP1, GFI1B and ACF/WCRF contexts, with FOG1 bridging GATA1 to MeCP1 ([PMID:15920471](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15920471/)). Its construct provenance explicitly traces to murine tagged GATA1 in the cited original; a human beta-globin cassette does not change that identity.

MED1 interactions and CCAR1/CoCoA recruitment are supported by selected original two-hybrid, pulldown, occupancy and reporter experiments ([PMID:17132730](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17132730/), [PMID:24245781](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24245781/)). The N-finger and C-finger mappings belong to their respective constructs. A cofactor's own catalytic or bridging activity is not assigned to GATA1. For partner-directed binding terms, the identity and role of the protein being bound determine specificity: FOG1 is a coregulator, MED1/CCAR1 are coactivating partners, and NRF2/HOXA1/PU1/CEBPE are DNA-binding transcription factors.

## Lineage processes and uncertainty

Erythroid and megakaryocytic programs are supported by direct regulatory assays, human variants and conserved functional experiments. Eosinophil commitment is supported by selected original human-progenitor recipient and mouse deficiency/rescue experiments using explicitly murine GATA cDNAs ([PMID:12045236](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2193540/)). GATA1 performs transcriptional regulation in these programs; it is not merely a substrate or a passive marker.

The broad anatomical-morphogenesis context is true but classified TOO_GENERAL because accepted platelet formation already entails it through megakaryocyte development and cell morphogenesis. That specific path does not establish every gross anatomical or system-development assertion. Liver-regeneration over-annotation retains the genuine rat nuclear-extract GATA activity assay but does not convert a tissue-response profile into a direct Gata1 perturbation mechanism ([PMID:18990226](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2613928/)).

Eight source-specific uncertainties remain: Q8TES7-6 identity, FOG-domain orientation, male-gonad development, two glycoprotein-biosynthesis claims, basophil differentiation, Sertoli-cell development and the exact absent-ligand apoptotic process. The original cryptorchidism report itself leaves causality unclear. Abstract-access limits do not establish wrong-gene attribution, and phenotype or surface abundance does not by itself distinguish synthesis from trafficking or turnover. Rat and mouse donor evidence remains provenance for the human transfer; donor extension contexts are not imported into human rows.
