## 2026-10-17 — dynein maturation contributes to motile-cilium assembly

DNAAF3 helps produce assembly-competent dynein components of motile cilia. Human patient respiratory cilia lack outer and inner arms, while zebrafish morphants can retain ciliary projections with largely normal number and morphology but defective arms and motility. In Chlamydomonas, genomic rescue restores assembly, and heavy/intermediate-chain immunoprecipitation shows abnormal complexes that cannot complete productive axonemal assembly. The proposed later maturation step generates an “assembly-competent complex”; chaperone release or transport competence remain mechanistic hypotheses rather than measured human activities [PMID:22387996, Results/Figures 3–5 and 7, Discussion/Figure 8](https://doi.org/10.1038/ng.1106).

This constituent-assembly work supports the original motile-cilium assembly assertions as core biology. It does not imply that DNAAF3 builds every ciliary structure or that losing it must eliminate ciliary projections. The current [motile cilium assembly definition](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/QuickGO/term/GO:0044458) covers aggregation and arrangement of the components. Checked is_a/part_of ancestry places [outer-arm assembly](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/QuickGO/term/GO:0036158) and [inner-arm assembly](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/QuickGO/term/GO:0036159) beneath axonemal dynein complex assembly, but not beneath GO:0044458. The two motile-cilium assertions are therefore retained as ACCEPT; the three axonemal-dynein assertions retain their specific two-term replacements. This supersedes the earlier five-MODIFY count: the effective eight decisions are five ACCEPT and three MODIFY, with the three assembly processes represented together in one core and no NEW assertion.

The compartment and mechanism boundaries remain distinct. Direct tagged-DNAAF3 DynAP imaging in the 2018 study is Xenopus evidence supporting the human ortholog inference, not imaging of endogenous human DNAAF3 [PMID:30561330, Figure 2C and cloning/imaging Methods](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.38497). Structural predictions and loss of detectable arginine-methylated dynein peptides in algal pf22 mutants support the methyltransferase model, while later comparative data leave catalytic and noncatalytic assembly contributions unresolved [PMID:38261620, structural comparisons and mutant proteomics](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2318522121), [PMID:39969973, Discussion](https://doi.org/10.1091/mbc.E25-02-0055). The existing purified-protein and catalytic-mutant experiment question remains appropriate; no intrinsic human catalytic activity or equivalence of the five natural products is added.

The two human/ortholog IMP decisions now carry separate short cache-exact assembly anchors, with the prior patient-arm anchor moved once from the core. The biological notes add only the two-word excerpt above, keeping the aggregate for PMID:22387996 at 22 words. The other three existing source excerpts are retained once. The 2012 Methods remain separately recovered original evidence because the genuine normal cache omits them; the historical notes and publication caches remain unchanged.
