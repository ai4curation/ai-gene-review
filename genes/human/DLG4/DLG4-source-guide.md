# DLG4 evidence and source guide

DLG4 (human P78352; HGNC:2903) has cooperating synaptic receptor-adaptor and structural postsynaptic-density roles. PDZ receptor recognition, SH3–GK recruitment and postsynaptic structural support are cooperating mechanisms. Binding a catalytic partner does not give PSD-95 that partner's activity. The official products are P78352-1 (724 residues), P78352-2 (767) and P78352-3 (721); historical constructs and domain fragments are not assumed to test all three products.

## Reproducible interaction measurements

The [fragmentomics results and executable analysis](DLG4-bioinformatics/RESULTS.md) expose the original measurement values, exact source partner identities, peptide modifications and sequence-selection rules. The [reproduction instructions](DLG4-bioinformatics/README.md) use the published PMID:36115835 workbook and the saved official sequence inputs.

The workbook has 1,344 DLG4 measurements. Accession matching yields 1,197 candidate crosswalk records: 1,179 natural-sequence matches and 18 unmatched natural-sequence candidates. Three explicit AXIN1 internal peptides are additionally sequence-supported, giving 1,182 selected records. Of 388 source pairs, 248 have a nonzero selected composite and 140 have only zero selected composites. Zero is neither zero affinity nor proof of noninteraction. Unmatched candidates, including particular viral phosphopeptides, are not silently used as identity support.

Most positive isolated-domain interfaces remain non-core binding observations because their physiological role is not resolved. The exact [project curation instruction](https://github.com/ai4curation/ai-gene-review/blob/a87ae568676c4012ea4abe5393967aeff1b52ae7/projects/CLINGEN_MENDELIAN.md#curation-instructions) supports retaining positive generic binding when an informative narrower function is not justified. Peptide reagents do not by themselves establish a physiological peptide-binding function. Supported specificity uses MODIFY, with the imported term and supporting entities retained.

Repeated partners were compared across sources. DLGAP1 scaffold recognition and its targeted phosphoprotein assay describe compatible aspects; SPAR's GAP class and phosphorylation-dependent recognition are likewise compatible. ERBB4 has independent receptor-tyrosine-kinase binding evidence despite the expression of concern on one later paper. APBA1 has independent BioPlex co-complex evidence, while its isolated PDZ-tail composites remain zero. DLGAP4 has independent GK-fragment recognition at residues 546–724; that does not convert its zero PDZ-tail measurements into positive results. Source-specific uncertainty is retained where no independent positive evidence resolves a zero-only fragment assertion.

## Remaining boundaries

The specific endoplasmic-reticulum, synaptic-vesicle, cortical-cytoskeleton, mossy-fiber-rosette, dendritic-arborization and epithelial-polarity assertions remain uncertain where the available target-resolved evidence does not settle them. These are source/term-specific limits, not denials of established synaptic biology. No NEW annotation is proposed. The follow-up explicitly represents the complementary structural molecular role alongside receptor-adaptor activity.

## Follow-up: quantitative thresholds and structural contribution

The fragmentomics Methods distinguish DAPF (double affinity-purified PDZ protein with fluorescence readout) normalized binding-intensity threshold 0.05 from LYSC/SAPF threshold 0.1. Effective Kd limits vary with peptide concentrations (30–790 µM, mean 320 µM); the smallest nonzero workbook composite is not a universal biological cutoff. The composite chooses a holdup method and does not replace independent fluorescence polarization. Of 729 zero composites across 1344 measurements, 380 have negative raw DAPF intensities. Four zero composites have positive FP measurements elsewhere in the grid, but none belongs to the selected measurements of the 138 uncertain source rows. All 414 selected measurements for those 138 rows use DAPF and remain below 0.05. Negative intensity is an assay estimate relative to a control; it is not negative affinity or a declaration of nonbinding. See [PMID:36115835](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9482650/), Methods “Estimation of binding intensities”/holdup affinity calculations and Discussion on below-quantification pairs. The unchanged public workbook artifact exposes the values and exact sequence joins.

The structural core is grounded in [PMID:21525273](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21525273/), Materials and Methods (rat hippocampal cultures, PSD95-EYFP constructs, immunogold/electron tomography), Results on PSD95-containing vertical filaments and their loss after RNAi, and Discussion on structural maintenance. These are E20 rat neurons; only a subset of filaments is immunolabeled, and receptor-type classifications are morphological/putative. The source does not imply universal loss of NMDA-receptor clusters. It supports physical structural participation, not simply a gene-knockdown phenotype. Current [GO:0098919](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/QuickGO/term/GO:0098919) has a part_of edge to [GO:0099562](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/QuickGO/term/GO:0099562). The latter's current definition contains a presynaptic/postsynaptic wording discrepancy; the proposal uses its postsynaptic label, linked structural activity and actual source, not a presynaptic claim.

The [PMID:26609151 original](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6705472/), Results “Enhanced activity-dependent degradation” and Figure 7A–C, reports: “Our results confirmed that PSD-95 is degraded in an activity-dependent manner”. This eleven-word original-only anchor is distinct from the abstract-only normal cache. The actual experiment uses mouse hippocampal neurons, 50 mM KCl for 15 min and proteasome inhibition; the significant additional genotype effect is on PSD-93. Accordingly the two potassium-response judgments retain the real abundance observation but do not infer a performed response step from being degraded. No original-only quotation is placed in cache-validated YAML.
