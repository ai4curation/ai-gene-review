# Comprehensive Research Report: Human TLR6 (Toll-like Receptor 6)

**Gene:** TLR6 | **UniProt:** Q9Y2C9 | **Organism:** *Homo sapiens* (Human)

## Executive Summary

Human TLR6 (Toll-like receptor 6, also known as CD286) is a cell surface pattern recognition receptor of the innate immune system that plays a critical role in detecting bacterial lipoproteins and initiating inflammatory responses (colleselli2023anupdateon pages 1-2, behzadi2021tolllikereceptorsgeneral pages 9-10). This report provides a comprehensive analysis of TLR6 function, including its molecular mechanism, substrate specificity, signaling pathways, cellular localization, and clinical relevance based on current scientific literature with emphasis on recent (2023-2025) developments.

> **Primary function:** Human TLR6 (UniProt Q9Y2C9; CD286) is an innate immune pattern-recognition receptor whose best-established functional form is a heterodimer with TLR2. Ligand-induced TLR2–TLR6 assembly brings their intracellular TIR domains together to initiate signaling. (colleselli2023anupdateon pages 2-4, asami2021structuralandfunctional pages 3-5)
>
> **Ligand specificity and structural basis:** TLR2–TLR6 preferentially recognizes diacylated microbial lipopeptides and lipoproteins, including Pam2CSK4, FSL-1, and MALP-2. TLR6 residues Phe343 and Phe365 obstruct the hydrophobic channel that, in TLR1, accommodates the third acyl chain of triacylated ligands; this steric restriction helps confer diacyl-ligand selectivity. (kaur2020tlr2agonisticsmall pages 4-6, kaur2020tlr2agonisticsmall pages 1-2, umar2025lipoproteinsignalpeptide pages 5-8, kaur2020tlr2agonisticsmall pages 38-39)
>
> **Functional location:** TLR6 is principally a plasma-membrane receptor, positioning its extracellular leucine-rich-repeat ectodomain to detect lipidated microbial components outside the cell. (sameer2021tolllikereceptors(tlrs) pages 2-3, jin2023regulationoftolllike pages 3-4, behzadi2021tolllikereceptorsgeneral pages 5-5)
>
> **Signaling mechanism:** Activated TLR2–TLR6 recruits the sorting adaptor TIRAP/MAL and MyD88, assembling an IRAK4–IRAK1/2 signaling complex. TRAF6 and TAK1 then activate the canonical IKK–NF-κB pathway and the JNK, p38, and ERK MAPK pathways, driving NF-κB-, AP-1-, and CREB-dependent gene expression. (liu2021tolllikereceptorsregulate pages 3-4, duan2022tolllikereceptorsignaling pages 2-3, duan2022tolllikereceptorsignaling pages 3-4)
>
> **Biological significance:** TLR6 enables recognition of microbial lipidation patterns and promotes cytokine and chemokine production—including TNF, IL-1β, IL-6, and CXCL8/IL-8—to recruit and activate immune cells. This response supports antimicrobial defense and links innate sensing to adaptive immunity, but persistent or excessive activation can contribute to inflammatory tissue pathology. (sameer2021tolllikereceptors(tlrs) pages 5-6, dhakad2025toll‐likereceptorexpression pages 8-8, colleselli2023anupdateon pages 6-7)

## 1. Gene Identity and Protein Structure

### 1.1 Molecular Identity
TLR6 (UniProt Q9Y2C9) encodes Toll-like receptor 6, a transmembrane glycoprotein belonging to the Toll-like receptor family. The protein is also designated as CD286 and is synthesized as a precursor requiring post-translational processing (colleselli2023anupdateon pages 1-2, sameer2021tolllikereceptors(tlrs) pages 2-3). TLR6 is evolutionarily related to TLR1 and TLR10, forming a subfamily within the TLR family that arose through gene duplication events (behzadi2021tolllikereceptorsgeneral pages 9-10).

### 1.2 Structural Organization
TLR6 exhibits the characteristic architecture of Toll-like receptors: an extracellular ligand-recognition domain containing leucine-rich repeats (LRRs) flanked by cysteine-rich regions, a single-pass transmembrane helix, and an intracellular Toll/interleukin-1 receptor (TIR) signaling domain (behzadi2021tolllikereceptorsgeneral pages 12-13, sameer2021tolllikereceptors(tlrs) pages 3-5). The extracellular LRR domain (IPR001611, IPR003591, IPR032675) mediates ligand binding and receptor dimerization, while the cytoplasmic TIR domain (IPR000157) recruits downstream signaling adaptors (xia2021researchprogresson pages 2-4, asami2021structuralandfunctional pages 3-5).

## 2. Primary Molecular Function and Substrate Specificity

### 2.1 Pattern Recognition Receptor Function
TLR6 functions primarily as a co-receptor that heterodimerizes with TLR2 to form the TLR2/TLR6 complex, a critical pattern recognition receptor for innate immunity (umar2025lipoproteinsignalpeptide pages 5-8, behzadi2021tolllikereceptorsgeneral pages 12-13, colleselli2023anupdateon pages 2-4). Unlike enzymatic proteins, TLR6 does not catalyze chemical reactions; instead, it serves as a ligand-sensing receptor that couples extracellular microbial detection to intracellular signaling cascades (xia2021researchprogresson pages 2-4).

### 2.2 Substrate Specificity: Diacylated Lipopeptides
The TLR2/TLR6 heterodimer specifically recognizes **diacylated lipopeptides and lipoproteins**, distinguishing them from the triacylated lipopeptides recognized by the TLR2/TLR1 complex (xia2021researchprogresson pages 4-5, xia2021researchprogresson pages 2-2, behzadi2021tolllikereceptorsgeneral pages 12-13). Key substrates include:

**Synthetic ligands:**
- **Pam2CSK4** (S-[2,3-bis(palmitoyloxy)propyl]-cysteine-SK4): A widely used experimental agonist (xia2021researchprogresson pages 2-2, kaur2020tlr2agonisticsmall pages 1-2, behzadi2021tolllikereceptorsgeneral pages 5-5)
- **FSL-1** (fibroblast-stimulating lipopeptide-1): A synthetic diacylated lipopeptide derived from *Mycoplasma salivarium* (kaur2020tlr2agonisticsmall pages 4-6, kaur2020tlr2agonisticsmall pages 2-4, xia2021researchprogresson pages 2-2)
- **MALP-2** (macrophage-activating lipopeptide-2): A diacylated lipopeptide from *Mycoplasma fermentans* (kaur2020tlr2agonisticsmall pages 4-6, kaur2020tlr2agonisticsmall pages 1-2)

**Natural microbial ligands:**
- Diacylated bacterial lipoproteins from Gram-positive bacteria (behzadi2021tolllikereceptorsgeneral pages 9-10)
- Lipoproteins from *Mycoplasma* species (behzadi2021tolllikereceptorsgeneral pages 10-12, behzadi2021tolllikereceptorsgeneral pages 9-10)
- Additional pathogen-associated molecular patterns including lipoteichoic acid, fungal zymosan, and viral glycoproteins when complexed with appropriate co-receptors (behzadi2021tolllikereceptorsgeneral pages 10-12)

### 2.3 Structural Basis of Ligand Discrimination
The molecular mechanism underlying TLR6's specificity for diacylated lipopeptides has been elucidated through structural and mutagenesis studies. TLR6 contains two key phenylalanine residues, **Phe343 and Phe365**, that project into and obstruct the hydrophobic binding channel (umar2025lipoproteinsignalpeptide pages 5-8, kaur2020tlr2agonisticsmall pages 38-39). This structural constraint prevents accommodation of the third (amide-linked) acyl chain characteristic of triacylated lipopeptides, which is instead recognized by TLR1 in the TLR2/TLR1 complex (behzadi2021tolllikereceptorsgeneral pages 12-13, kaur2020tlr2agonisticsmall pages 38-39).

In the TLR2/TLR6 heterodimer, the two ester-linked fatty acid chains of diacylated ligands insert primarily into the hydrophobic pocket of TLR2, while the glycerol moiety and peptide head group form stabilizing contacts with both receptors, including hydrogen bonds with LRR11 region residues (umar2025lipoproteinsignalpeptide pages 5-8). Experimental evidence demonstrates that replacing TLR6's obstructing phenylalanines with corresponding TLR1 residues broadens the receptor's responsiveness to include triacylated lipopeptides, confirming the structural basis of ligand selectivity (kaur2020tlr2agonisticsmall pages 38-39).

## 3. Heterodimerization and Protein Interactions

### 3.1 TLR2/TLR6 Heterodimer Formation
The primary functional form of TLR6 is the TLR2/TLR6 heterodimer (colleselli2023anupdateon pages 2-4, asami2021structuralandfunctional pages 3-5, colleselli2023anupdateon pages 1-2). Ligand binding induces conformational changes that stabilize receptor dimerization, bringing the extracellular LRR domains into an activated "m"-shaped configuration and positioning the intracellular TIR domains in close proximity to enable adaptor protein recruitment (colleselli2023anupdateon pages 2-4, asami2021structuralandfunctional pages 3-5). The mechanism is ligand-dependent: in the absence of appropriate diacylated lipopeptides, the TLR ectodomains remain separated and signaling-incompetent (behzadi2021tolllikereceptorsgeneral pages 9-10).

### 3.2 Co-receptors and Accessory Proteins
TLR2/TLR6 signaling is modulated by several co-receptors and accessory proteins that enhance ligand recognition and receptor activation:

- **CD36**: A scavenger receptor that associates with TLR2/TLR6 complexes and promotes immune responses to certain ligands (behzadi2021tolllikereceptorsgeneral pages 10-12, behzadi2021tolllikereceptorsgeneral pages 12-13, dhakad2025toll‐likereceptorexpression pages 5-6)
- **CD14**: A myeloid cell surface protein and soluble blood protein that binds diverse microbial ligands and facilitates their presentation to TLR complexes (behzadi2021tolllikereceptorsgeneral pages 10-12, behzadi2021tolllikereceptorsgeneral pages 12-13)
- **LBP (lipopolysaccharide-binding protein)**: Works with CD14 to transfer lipopeptides and other microbial components to TLR2/TLR6 (behzadi2021tolllikereceptorsgeneral pages 12-13)
- **Additional co-receptors**: RP105, mannose-binding lectin (MBL), and dectin-1 participate in ligand- and cell-type-specific contexts (behzadi2021tolllikereceptorsgeneral pages 10-12)

These accessory proteins enable TLR2/TLR6 to recognize structurally diverse microbial molecules beyond simple diacylated lipopeptides, including combinations such as CD14/CD36 for mycoplasmal lipopeptides, CD14/CD36/MBL for lipoteichoic acid, and CD14/dectin-1 for fungal zymosan (behzadi2021tolllikereceptorsgeneral pages 10-12).

## 4. Cellular Localization

TLR6 is predominantly localized to the **cell surface (plasma membrane)**, where its extracellular domain is positioned to detect lipidated microbial components in the extracellular environment (sameer2021tolllikereceptors(tlrs) pages 2-3, jin2023regulationoftolllike pages 3-4, behzadi2021tolllikereceptorsgeneral pages 5-5). This cell surface localization distinguishes TLR6 from intracellular TLRs such as TLR3, TLR7, TLR8, and TLR9, which reside primarily in endosomal compartments and recognize nucleic acids (jin2023regulationoftolllike pages 3-4, behzadi2021tolllikereceptorsgeneral pages 5-5, behzadi2021tolllikereceptorsgeneral pages 9-10). The plasma membrane localization of TLR6 is functionally appropriate for its role in detecting bacterial cell-envelope lipoproteins released during infection or present on intact bacteria contacting host cells.

## 5. Signaling Pathways

### 5.1 MyD88-Dependent Signaling Cascade
TLR6 signals exclusively through the **MyD88-dependent pathway**, in contrast to TLR3 and TLR4 which can utilize TRIF-dependent signaling (liu2021tolllikereceptorsregulate pages 3-4, duan2022tolllikereceptorsignaling pages 2-3, jin2023regulationoftolllike pages 3-4, xia2021researchprogresson pages 8-9). The signaling cascade proceeds as follows:

**Step 1: Adaptor Recruitment**
Following ligand-induced TLR2/TLR6 dimerization, the membrane-proximal sorting adaptor **TIRAP (also called MAL)** is recruited to the TIR domains, which in turn recruits the central adaptor protein **MyD88** (liu2021tolllikereceptorsregulate pages 3-4, duan2022tolllikereceptorsignaling pages 2-3, sameer2021tolllikereceptors(tlrs) pages 3-5, behzadi2021tolllikereceptorsgeneral pages 12-13).

**Step 2: Myddosome Formation**
MyD88 nucleates formation of the "Myddosome," a signaling complex containing **IRAK4** (IL-1 receptor-associated kinase 4) and **IRAK1/IRAK2** (liu2021tolllikereceptorsregulate pages 3-4, duan2022tolllikereceptorsignaling pages 2-3, xia2021researchprogresson pages 8-9). IRAK4 phosphorylates and activates IRAK1 and IRAK2, initiating the downstream kinase cascade.

**Step 3: TRAF6-TAK1 Activation**
Activated IRAK1 associates with **TRAF6** (TNF receptor-associated factor 6), an E3 ubiquitin ligase that catalyzes K63-linked polyubiquitination. This ubiquitin signaling recruits and activates the **TAK1 (TGF-β-activated kinase 1) complex**, which includes TAB1, TAB2, and TAB3 adaptor proteins (liu2021tolllikereceptorsregulate pages 3-4, duan2022tolllikereceptorsignaling pages 2-3, jin2023regulationoftolllike pages 3-4, xia2021researchprogresson pages 8-9).

**Step 4: Dual Pathway Activation**
TAK1 activates two major downstream branches:

1. **NF-κB pathway**: TAK1 phosphorylates the IKK (IκB kinase) complex, leading to IκBα phosphorylation and degradation. This releases NF-κB transcription factors (p50/p65 heterodimers), which translocate to the nucleus and activate inflammatory gene expression (duan2022tolllikereceptorsignaling pages 2-3, jin2023regulationoftolllike pages 3-4, sameer2021tolllikereceptors(tlrs) pages 5-6, duan2022tolllikereceptorsignaling pages 3-4).

2. **MAPK pathways**: TAK1 activates three parallel MAPK cascades:
   - **JNK pathway**: MKK4/7 → JNK
   - **p38 pathway**: MKK3/6 → p38
   - **ERK pathway**: MKK1/2 → ERK1/2
   
These MAPKs activate transcription factors including **AP-1** (c-Fos/c-Jun) and **CREB** (duan2022tolllikereceptorsignaling pages 2-3, duan2022tolllikereceptorsignaling pages 3-4).

### 5.2 Transcriptional Outputs
The coordinated action of NF-κB, AP-1, and CREB drives expression of numerous inflammatory genes, including:

- **Pro-inflammatory cytokines**: TNF-α, IL-1β, IL-6, IL-12 (liu2021tolllikereceptorsregulate pages 3-4, sameer2021tolllikereceptors(tlrs) pages 5-6, colleselli2023anupdateon pages 6-7)
- **Chemokines**: CXCL8 (IL-8), CCL2 (sameer2021tolllikereceptors(tlrs) pages 5-6)
- **Growth factors**: GM-CSF (styczen2025theroleof pages 10-12)
- **Adhesion molecules**: E-selectin, ICAM-1, VCAM-1 (sameer2021tolllikereceptors(tlrs) pages 5-6)
- **Costimulatory molecules**: CD80, CD86 (sameer2021tolllikereceptors(tlrs) pages 5-6)
- **Additional mediators**: In certain contexts, type I interferons (IFN-α/β) through IRF5 and IRF7 activation (liu2021tolllikereceptorsregulate pages 3-4)

## 6. Biological Functions

### 6.1 Innate Immune Recognition
TLR6's primary biological function is to detect bacterial lipoproteins and lipopeptides as pathogen-associated molecular patterns (PAMPs), initiating rapid innate immune responses (xia2021researchprogresson pages 4-5, colleselli2023anupdateon pages 1-2, dhakad2025toll‐likereceptorexpression pages 8-8). By forming heterodimers with TLR2, TLR6 expands the repertoire of microbial patterns that can be recognized, enabling discrimination between diacylated and triacylated lipid structures that characterize different bacterial species (behzadi2021tolllikereceptorsgeneral pages 12-13, kaur2020tlr2agonisticsmall pages 38-39).

### 6.2 Linking Innate and Adaptive Immunity
TLR6 signaling promotes activation of antigen-presenting cells (macrophages and dendritic cells), enhancing their expression of MHC molecules and costimulatory molecules (CD80, CD86) necessary for T cell activation (sameer2021tolllikereceptors(tlrs) pages 5-6). This bridges innate pathogen recognition to adaptive immune responses, enabling development of pathogen-specific immunity (xia2021researchprogresson pages 2-4).

### 6.3 Cytokine Production and Inflammation
Activated TLR2/TLR6 complexes induce production of pro-inflammatory cytokines that recruit neutrophils, monocytes, and other immune cells to sites of infection (sameer2021tolllikereceptors(tlrs) pages 5-6, colleselli2023anupdateon pages 6-7). While this inflammatory response is protective during acute infection, excessive or chronic TLR6 activation can contribute to tissue damage and pathological inflammation (styczen2025theroleof pages 10-12, colleselli2023anupdateon pages 6-7).

## 7. Disease Associations and Pathological Roles

### 7.1 Infectious Diseases
TLR6 plays important roles in host defense against diverse pathogens:

- **Bacterial infections**: Recognition of Gram-positive bacteria and *Mycoplasma* species through their diacylated lipoproteins (behzadi2021tolllikereceptorsgeneral pages 9-10, dhakad2025toll‐likereceptorexpression pages 8-8)
- ***Legionella pneumophila***: TLR6 has been implicated in immune responses to this respiratory pathogen, with the TLR6 A359T>C polymorphism associated with increased susceptibility to Legionnaires' disease (styczen2025theroleof pages 10-12, duan2022tolllikereceptorsignaling pages 10-11)
- **Fungal infections**: TLR2/TLR6 contributes to recognition of *Candida albicans*, *Cryptococcus neoformans*, and *Aspergillus fumigatus*, with TLR6 variants affecting susceptibility (dhakad2025toll‐likereceptorexpression pages 8-8)
- **Viral infections**: TLR2/TLR6 has been reported to recognize dengue virus and participate in responses to other viral pathogens (duan2022tolllikereceptorsignaling pages 10-11)

### 7.2 Inflammatory and Autoimmune Conditions
Recent research (2023-2025) has highlighted TLR6's involvement in various inflammatory pathologies:

- **Periodontal disease**: TLR2/TLR6 ligands including Pam2CSK4 induce osteoclast differentiation and bone resorption through PGE2 and RANKL production, contributing to alveolar bone loss in periodontal disease (styczen2025theroleof pages 10-12)

- **Pulmonary arterial hypertension (PAH)**: Emerging evidence from transcriptomic studies suggests TLR2/TLR6 activation in pulmonary arterial smooth muscle cells promotes vascular proliferation, remodeling, endothelin-1 production, and inflammation, though direct clinical evidence remains limited (styczen2025theroleof pages 10-12)

- **Helicobacter pylori infection**: A 2024 study demonstrated that sustained exposure to *H. pylori* induces immune tolerance through TLR6 desensitization in gastric epithelial cells, with TLR6 expression initially increasing then decreasing during chronic infection (dhakad2025toll‐likereceptorexpression pages 8-8, colleselli2023anupdateon pages 6-7)

- **Ischemia/reperfusion injury**: TLR2/TLR6 activation has been associated with inflammatory responses in ischemia/reperfusion pathology (dhakad2025toll‐likereceptorexpression pages 10-10)

### 7.3 Cancer
The role of TLR6 in cancer is context-dependent and incompletely understood. TLR2/TLR6 agonists have been explored as immunostimulants in melanoma and other cancers, with the hypothesis that enhanced immune activation could promote anti-tumor immunity (dhakad2025toll‐likereceptorexpression pages 10-10). However, chronic inflammatory signaling through TLRs can also create a tumor-promoting microenvironment in some contexts (colleselli2023anupdateon pages 6-7).

### 7.4 Genetic Polymorphisms
Several TLR6 polymorphisms have been associated with disease susceptibility:
- **A359T>C polymorphism**: Associated with increased risk of Legionnaires' disease (styczen2025theroleof pages 10-12)
- **Ser249Pro polymorphism**: Suggested association with bronchial asthma susceptibility in some populations (styczen2025theroleof pages 10-12)
- **rs3775073**: Identified as a risk-modifying variant for rheumatic heart disease in gender- and age-dependent manner (styczen2025theroleof pages 10-12)

## 8. Recent Developments and Therapeutic Applications (2023-2025)

### 8.1 Emerging Therapeutic Strategies
Recent research has identified several potential therapeutic approaches targeting TLR6:

**TLR2/6 Agonists as Vaccine Adjuvants:**
Diacylated lipopeptides based on Pam2CSK4 and MALP-2 structures are being developed as vaccine adjuvants to enhance immune responses (kaur2020tlr2agonisticsmall pages 4-6, umar2025lipoproteinsignalpeptide pages 5-8). These agonists directly activate innate immune pathways, leading to stronger, more specific, and longer-lasting protective immunity. Structure-activity relationship studies have optimized stereochemistry, acyl chain length, and peptide components to maximize TLR2/6 activity while minimizing toxicity (kaur2020tlr2agonisticsmall pages 4-6, kaur2020tlr2agonisticsmall pages 6-8).

**TLR2/6 Antagonists for Inflammatory Diseases:**
Inhibition of TLR2/TLR6 signaling is being explored for conditions with excessive inflammation. For example, GIT2 has been investigated as a TLR2/6 antagonist in diabetes, reportedly reducing NOX4 and NF-κB activity (dhakad2025toll‐likereceptorexpression pages 10-10). Similarly, antagonism of TLR2/TLR6-mediated NF-κB signaling is being considered for pulmonary arterial hypertension, though direct clinical evidence remains limited (styczen2025theroleof pages 10-12).

**TLR6 Agonists for Helicobacter pylori:**
A 2024 study identified TLR6 as a potential therapeutic target for *H. pylori* infection, demonstrating that TLR6 agonists can restore inflammatory responses in desensitized gastric epithelial cells and reduce bacterial colonization (dhakad2025toll‐likereceptorexpression pages 8-8).

### 8.2 Current Limitations
Despite promising preclinical data, no TLR6-selective therapeutics have been approved for clinical use as of 2025. Most therapeutic approaches target the TLR2/TLR6 heterodimer rather than TLR6 alone, and effects cannot always be attributed specifically to TLR6 versus TLR2 or associated co-receptors (styczen2025theroleof pages 10-12, dhakad2025toll‐likereceptorexpression pages 10-10). Direct clinical trials specifically evaluating TLR6-targeted interventions remain sparse.

### 8.3 Recent Research Highlights (2023-2025)

- **Trained immunity**: A 2025 review highlighted TLR6's role in mediating trained immunity—long-term functional adaptation of innate immune cells—through epigenetic and metabolic reprogramming following TLR2/6 agonist exposure (colleselli2023anupdateon pages 6-7)

- **Blood-brain barrier**: TLR6 expression in cerebral endothelial cells at the blood-brain barrier has been implicated in neuroinflammation and CNS pathology in traumatic brain injury and infections (dhakad2025toll‐likereceptorexpression pages 8-8)

- **Sex differences in immunity**: A 2024 study examined sex-dependent differences in TLR expression and activity, noting that TLR genes including TLR6 are encoded on autosomes but their expression and downstream effects can be influenced by sex hormones and X-chromosome-linked factors (colleselli2023anupdateon pages 6-7)

## 9. Key Research Methodologies and Experimental Evidence

The functional characterization of TLR6 has been achieved through multiple complementary approaches:

**Structural studies**: X-ray crystallography of TLR2/TLR6 complexes with diacylated lipopeptides (Pam2CSK4) has revealed the molecular basis of ligand recognition and receptor dimerization (asami2021structuralandfunctional pages 3-5, kaur2020tlr2agonisticsmall pages 38-39).

**Reporter cell systems**: HEK-Blue and similar reporter cell lines expressing specific TLR combinations have enabled precise dissection of TLR2/TLR1 versus TLR2/TLR6 signaling specificity (kaur2020tlr2agonisticsmall pages 4-6, kaur2020tlr2agonisticsmall pages 2-4).

**Knockout and knockdown studies**: TLR6-deficient mice and cells with siRNA-mediated TLR6 knockdown have demonstrated the specific requirement for TLR6 in diacylated lipopeptide responses (xia2021researchprogresson pages 4-5, dhakad2025toll‐likereceptorexpression pages 8-8).

**Mutagenesis studies**: Site-directed mutagenesis of TLR6 residues, including the critical Phe343 and Phe365, has defined the structural determinants of ligand selectivity (kaur2020tlr2agonisticsmall pages 38-39).

Human TLR6 (UniProt Q9Y2C9) is a plasma membrane-localized pattern recognition receptor that functions primarily through heterodimerization with TLR2 to detect diacylated bacterial lipoproteins and lipopeptides. The TLR2/TLR6 complex couples ligand recognition to MyD88-dependent signaling pathways that activate NF-κB and MAPK cascades, resulting in production of pro-inflammatory cytokines and chemokines essential for innate immunity and host defense against bacterial and fungal pathogens.

The substrate specificity of TLR6 for diacylated versus triacylated lipopeptides is determined by specific structural features, particularly phenylalanine residues that obstruct the hydrophobic channel required for binding the third acyl chain. This structural constraint enables discrimination between different classes of bacterial lipoproteins and contributes to the remarkable ligand diversity recognized by the TLR2 subfamily.

Recent research (2023-2025) has expanded understanding of TLR6's roles in diverse pathological conditions including chronic infections, inflammatory diseases, and metabolic disorders. While TLR2/6 agonists show promise as vaccine adjuvants and immunostimulants, and antagonists are being explored for inflammatory conditions, translation to approved therapeutics remains limited. Future research directions include development of TLR6-selective modulators, investigation of TLR6 polymorphisms in personalized medicine, and elucidation of TLR6's role in trained immunity and tissue-specific inflammatory responses.
