> **Primary function:** Human TLR6 (UniProt Q9Y2C9; CD286) is an innate immune pattern-recognition receptor whose best-established functional form is a heterodimer with TLR2. Ligand-induced TLR2–TLR6 assembly brings their intracellular TIR domains together to initiate signaling. (pqac-00000008, pqac-00000011)
>
> **Ligand specificity and structural basis:** TLR2–TLR6 preferentially recognizes diacylated microbial lipopeptides and lipoproteins, including Pam2CSK4, FSL-1, and MALP-2. TLR6 residues Phe343 and Phe365 obstruct the hydrophobic channel that, in TLR1, accommodates the third acyl chain of triacylated ligands; this steric restriction helps confer diacyl-ligand selectivity. (pqac-00000028, pqac-00000030, pqac-00000036, pqac-00000037)
>
> **Functional location:** TLR6 is principally a plasma-membrane receptor, positioning its extracellular leucine-rich-repeat ectodomain to detect lipidated microbial components outside the cell. (pqac-00000014, pqac-00000015, pqac-00000016)
>
> **Signaling mechanism:** Activated TLR2–TLR6 recruits the sorting adaptor TIRAP/MAL and MyD88, assembling an IRAK4–IRAK1/2 signaling complex. TRAF6 and TAK1 then activate the canonical IKK–NF-κB pathway and the JNK, p38, and ERK MAPK pathways, driving NF-κB-, AP-1-, and CREB-dependent gene expression. (pqac-00000020, pqac-00000021, pqac-00000027)
>
> **Biological significance:** TLR6 enables recognition of microbial lipidation patterns and promotes cytokine and chemokine production—including TNF, IL-1β, IL-6, and CXCL8/IL-8—to recruit and activate immune cells. This response supports antimicrobial defense and links innate sensing to adaptive immunity, but persistent or excessive activation can contribute to inflammatory tissue pathology. (pqac-00000023, pqac-00000045, pqac-00000046)
