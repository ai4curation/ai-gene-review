| Category | TLR6 characteristic | Evidence and interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Gene identity | **Symbol:** *TLR6*; **organism:** *Homo sapiens*; **UniProt:** Q9Y2C9; **protein:** Toll-like receptor 6; **alternative designation:** CD286; precursor protein | Identity matches the supplied UniProt record and literature describing human TLR6/CD286; no conflicting same-symbol protein was identified. |
| Protein class and topology | Type I single-pass transmembrane member of the Toll-like receptor family, comprising an extracellular ligand-recognition region, one transmembrane helix, and a cytoplasmic Toll/interleukin-1 receptor (**TIR**) signaling domain | TLR6 is a signaling receptor rather than an enzyme or transporter: extracellular recognition is coupled to intracellular adaptor recruitment (pqac-00000011, pqac-00000025). |
| Annotated domains | Extracellular cysteine-rich LRR-flanking region (**Cys-rich_flank_reg_C; IPR000483**), leucine-rich repeats (**IPR001611; IPR003591**), LRR superfamily domain (**IPR032675**), and intracellular **TIR domain (IPR000157)** | The LRR ectodomain mediates ligand-dependent receptor assembly, whereas the TIR domain forms the cytosolic signaling platform. |
| Primary molecular function | Cell-surface innate immune pattern-recognition receptor that functions principally as the ligand-discriminating partner of TLR2 | Its primary role is to form a ligand-responsive **TLR2–TLR6 heterodimer**, detect microbial lipid patterns, and initiate inflammatory signaling (pqac-00000000, pqac-00000004, pqac-00000008). |
| Principal heterodimer | **TLR2–TLR6** is the best-established functional complex | Ligand binding stabilizes an activated, approximately m-shaped ectodomain dimer and brings the intracellular TIR domains together, permitting adaptor assembly (pqac-00000008, pqac-00000011, pqac-00000013). |
| Other proposed receptor association | TLR4–TLR6 complexes have been reported in CD36-dependent contexts but are less canonical and less extensively established than TLR2–TLR6 | Functional annotation should center on TLR2–TLR6; TLR4–TLR6 should be considered context-dependent rather than TLR6’s primary receptor form (pqac-00000009, pqac-00000010, pqac-00000050). |
| Core ligand specificity | Preferential recognition of **diacylated lipopeptides and lipoproteins**, in contrast to triacylated ligands preferentially sensed by TLR2–TLR1 | The principal recognized feature is a diacylated N-terminal lipidated-cysteine architecture, although peptide sequence, stereochemistry, chain length, and hydrophobicity modulate potency (pqac-00000028, pqac-00000034, pqac-00000039). |
| Natural microbial ligand examples | Diacylated lipoproteins and lipopeptides from **Mycoplasma** and Gram-positive bacteria; MALP-2 from *Mycoplasma fermentans*; lipoteichoic-acid-, zymosan-, and viral-glycoprotein-associated responses have also been assigned to accessory-protein-containing TLR2–TLR6 complexes | Evidence is strongest for lipidated bacterial or mycoplasmal molecules. Claims involving chemically different preparations require caution because contaminating lipoproteins can cause apparent TLR2–TLR6 activity (pqac-00000006, pqac-00000030, pqac-00000034). |
| Synthetic or experimental agonists | **Pam2CSK4**, **FSL-1**, and synthetic MALP-2 or Pam2Cys derivatives | These reagents are widely used to activate and experimentally define TLR2–TLR6 signaling. Intact lipid-plus-peptide architecture can be necessary; separated Pam2Cys and peptide components were inactive up to 100 nM in one structure–activity analysis (pqac-00000002, pqac-00000028, pqac-00000031). |
| Structural basis of diacyl selectivity | TLR6 residues **Phe343 and Phe365** obstruct the hydrophobic channel corresponding to the TLR1 pocket that accepts a third acyl chain | In TLR2–TLR6, the two ester-linked lipid chains primarily insert into TLR2, while TLR6 cannot accommodate the additional amide-linked chain of triacylated ligands. Replacing the obstructing TLR6 phenylalanines with corresponding TLR1 residues broadens responsiveness to triacylated ligands (pqac-00000036, pqac-00000037). |
| Additional ligand contacts | Glycerol and peptide-head-group contacts help bridge and stabilize the TLR2–TLR6 interface; contacts near LRR11 contribute to activation | Acyl-chain number alone does not fully determine potency: peptide composition, lipid dimensions, stereochemistry, and interfacial hydrogen bonding also matter (pqac-00000028, pqac-00000033, pqac-00000036). |
| Cellular localization | Predominantly a **plasma-membrane/cell-surface** receptor | This distinguishes TLR6 from nucleic-acid-sensing TLR3 and TLR7–TLR9, which function principally in endosomal compartments. Surface localization positions TLR6 to survey extracellular microbial-envelope lipoproteins (pqac-00000014, pqac-00000015, pqac-00000016). |
| Accessory ligand-handling proteins | **CD36**, **CD14**, LBP, RP105, mannose-binding lectin, and dectin-1 can participate in ligand- and cell-type-dependent complexes | CD36 can associate with TLR2–TLR6; CD14 and LBP can capture or transfer microbial ligands. Reported combinations include CD14/CD36 for mycoplasmal lipopeptides, CD14/CD36/MBL for lipoteichoic acid, CD14/dectin-1 for zymosan, and CD14 for viral glycoprotein B (pqac-00000049, pqac-00000050). |
| Proximal signaling adaptors | **TIRAP/MAL → MyD88** | After ligand-induced dimerization, TIRAP acts as a membrane-proximal sorting adaptor and recruits MyD88 to the paired receptor TIR domains. TLR6 therefore principally uses the canonical MyD88-dependent rather than TRIF-dependent pathway (pqac-00000020, pqac-00000021, pqac-00000022). |
| Myddosome and kinase cascade | **MyD88 → IRAK4 → IRAK1/IRAK2 → TRAF6 → TAK1–TAB complex** | MyD88 nucleates the Myddosome; IRAK activation and TRAF6-dependent ubiquitin signaling engage TAK1 and its TAB partners (pqac-00000020, pqac-00000021, pqac-00000024). |
| Distal signaling branches | **IKK–NF-κB** and MAPKs: **JNK, p38, and ERK1/2** | TAK1 activates the canonical IKK complex and MAPK modules, including MKK4/7–JNK, MKK3/6–p38, and MKK1/2–ERK signaling (pqac-00000021, pqac-00000027). |
| Principal transcription factors | **NF-κB**, **AP-1**, and **CREB** | These factors cooperate to induce inflammatory and immune-response genes. An IRF/type-I-interferon branch may occur in particular contexts but is not the defining canonical output of surface TLR2–TLR6 signaling (pqac-00000021, pqac-00000022, pqac-00000027). |
| Major outputs | Inflammatory cytokines and chemokines including **TNF, IL-1β, IL-6, and CXCL8/IL-8**, plus context-dependent IL-12, GM-CSF, interferons, adhesion molecules, and antigen-presenting-cell costimulatory molecules | These outputs recruit and activate leukocytes, support microbial clearance, promote antigen-presenting-cell activation, and connect innate recognition to adaptive immunity (pqac-00000023, pqac-00000046). |
| Biological role | Detection of microbial-envelope lipidation states, initiation of antibacterial and antifungal inflammation, and modulation of immune-cell and tissue responses | TLR6 broadens TLR2’s ligand repertoire. Its activity is protective when promoting pathogen clearance but can become pathogenic when signaling is excessive, persistent, or misdirected (pqac-00000001, pqac-00000003, pqac-00000045). |
| Infectious-disease associations | TLR6-containing responses have been implicated in recognition or control of *Legionella pneumophila*, fungal pathogens including *Candida albicans*, *Cryptococcus neoformans*, and *Aspergillus fumigatus*, and selected viral responses | Much of this evidence concerns the TLR2–TLR6 complex or genetic associations rather than isolated TLR6 action; attribution should remain heterodimer-specific (pqac-00000043, pqac-00000044, pqac-00000045). |
| Inflammatory and tissue pathology | TLR2–TLR6 signaling can promote cytokine production, endothelial or vascular activation, ischemia/reperfusion-associated signaling, and other inflammatory tissue responses | These effects reflect the same MyD88–NF-κB/MAPK mechanism that is beneficial in host defense but potentially damaging during sterile or chronic inflammation (pqac-00000042, pqac-00000043, pqac-00000046). |
| Cancer-related evidence | TLR2–TLR6 agonism has been explored as an immunostimulatory strategy, including melanoma-related experimental settings, but direct TLR6-selective clinical evidence remains limited | Cancer effects are context-dependent because inflammatory signaling may enhance antitumor immunity while also supporting tumor-promoting inflammation (pqac-00000042, pqac-00000047). |
| Therapeutic applications | Pam2Cys- and MALP-2-like agonists are being investigated as **vaccine adjuvants and immunostimulants**; antagonism of TLR2–TLR6 or downstream NF-κB signaling is being explored for excessive inflammation | The cell-surface location and chemically tractable lipopeptide ligands make the complex druggable in principle, but most programs target shared TLR2 signaling rather than TLR6 alone (pqac-00000028, pqac-00000033, pqac-00000047, pqac-00000048). |
| Translational status and limitation | No established approved therapy selectively targeting human TLR6 was identified; direct TLR6-specific clinical-trial evidence is sparse | Applications remain predominantly experimental or preclinical. Because TLR6 acts with TLR2 and accessory receptors, pharmacologic effects cannot automatically be attributed to TLR6 alone (pqac-00000047, pqac-00000048). |
| Functional-annotation conclusion | **TLR6 is a plasma-membrane signaling receptor, not an enzyme, whose primary role is to confer diacylated-lipopeptide specificity on TLR2 and couple recognition to TIRAP/MyD88-dependent NF-κB and MAPK activation.** | This annotation is supported by convergent structural, biochemical, reporter-cell, and signaling-pathway evidence (pqac-00000004, pqac-00000008, pqac-00000021, pqac-00000037). |
