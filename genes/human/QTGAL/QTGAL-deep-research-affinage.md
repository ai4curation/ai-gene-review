# Affinage mechanistic annotation for QTGAL (human)

## Current model (mechanistic narrative)

QTGAL (also known as B3GNTL1) is an RNA glycosyltransferase that installs a galactose moiety onto the queuosine (Q) base in the anticodon of tRNA-Tyr, generating galactosyl-queuosine (galQ) [PMID:37992713]. Using nucleotide diphosphate sugars as the glycosyl donor, this Q-glycosylation activity was reconstituted in vitro, establishing QTGAL as the direct catalytic enzyme [PMID:37992713]. Functionally, the galQ modification slows ribosomal elongation at cognate UAC (Tyr) codons and suppresses stop codon readthrough, defining a role in tuning translational speed and decoding fidelity [PMID:37992713]; cryo-EM of the human ribosome-tRNA complex provides the structural basis for how Q-glycosylation modulates codon recognition [PMID:37992713]. Loss of this modification in QTGAL knockout cells increases protein aggregation, linking the enzyme to proteostasis [PMID:37992713], and zebrafish qtgal knockouts show shortened body length, demonstrating a requirement for post-embryonic growth in vertebrates [PMID:37992713].
