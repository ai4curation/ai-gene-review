Gene Description

C11orf98 encodes a 123-amino-acid nuclear microprotein, translated from an alternative open reading frame of the LBHD1 locus and therefore distinct from the LBHD1 protein made from the same genomic region. UniProt names it RSMC, for 28S rRNA/ribosome and sororin micro-cofactor. The protein is basic and disordered at both termini, with a short helical core, and two lines of work assign it two nuclear roles. In the nucleolus it is a component of nucleoplasmic pre-60S ribosomal particles, resolved by cryo-electron microscopy within the assembling large subunit and proposed to be a distal functional counterpart of yeast Alb1; consistent with this, its most prominent proximity partners are the nucleolar chaperones nucleolin and nucleophosmin. In the nucleoplasm during S phase it binds sororin/CDCA5 directly through two short sororin-interacting motifs, and this interaction is strengthened when PARP1 poly(ADP-ribosyl)ates the microprotein. Through that interaction it promotes recruitment of sororin to cohesin on replicated chromatin and stimulates the anti-WAPL activity of sororin, acting alongside ESCO1/ESCO2-dependent acetylation of the cohesin subunit SMC3 to establish sister chromatid cohesion. Depletion causes precocious sister chromatid separation and increased inter-sister distance, and its overexpression rescues cohesion defects caused by PARP inhibition. Whether the ribosome-assembly and cohesion roles are mechanistically connected is not known.

Core Functions

In S phase, the C11orf98/RSMC microprotein binds sororin/CDCA5 directly through two short sororin-interacting motifs and delivers it to cohesin on replicated chromatin. PARP1 poly(ADP-ribosyl)ates the microprotein during S phase, which strengthens the sororin interaction, so that the adaptor works in parallel with ESCO1/ESCO2-dependent SMC3 acetylation. The bound sororin then antagonises WAPL, locking cohesin onto chromatin and establishing sister chromatid cohesion. The microprotein carries no catalytic activity of its own; its contribution is the selective, regulated bringing-together of sororin and cohesin.

Suggested Questions for Experts

Q: Are the two assigned roles of this microprotein - as a component of nucleoplasmic pre-60S ribosomal particles and as a sororin cofactor in sister chromatid cohesion - mechanistically connected, or does one protein simply do two unrelated jobs in two nuclear compartments? Both come from single papers that did not test the other role.

Suggested experts: Wenya Hou, Huiqiang Lou, Ning Gao

Q: Is C11orf98 required for pre-60S maturation, or only present in the particle? The cryo-EM work identifies it as a component; no depletion phenotype for ribosome biogenesis has been published, and UniProt's wording remains "may be involved".

Suggested experts: Ning Gao, Yunyang Zhang

Q: Is homozygous loss of the alternative ORF viable? Only heterozygous CRISPR knockout clones were recovered, which hints at essentiality but is not stated as such, and a knockout that also disrupts the host LBHD1 reading frame would confound the interpretation.

Suggested experts: Wenya Hou, Xingzhi Xu

Q: Should the molecular function be modelled as an adaptor that brings sororin to cohesin, or as an activator that increases sororin's anti-WAPL activity? The reported data are compatible with both, and the distinction determines which GO molecular function term is correct.

Suggested experts: Wenya Hou

Suggested Experiments

Experiment: Separate the two reading frames genetically: introduce a start-codon mutation that abolishes only the alternative ORF while leaving the LBHD1 coding sequence intact, and conversely a frameshift that removes LBHD1 but preserves the alternative ORF. Score sister chromatid cohesion by chromosome spreads in each background. This is the control that keeps annotations on E9PRG8 from leaking onto Q9BQE6.

Hypothesis: The cohesion function belongs specifically to the alternative-ORF product and not to LBHD1 translated from the same locus.

Type: allele-specific genome editing of overlapping reading frames

Experiment: Follow endogenously tagged C11orf98 through the cell cycle by live imaging and by fractionation into nucleolar, nucleoplasmic and chromatin compartments, quantifying co-purification with pre-60S particles and with sororin at each stage. Test whether PARP1 inhibition shifts the partition.

Hypothesis: The nucleolar pre-60S pool and the nucleoplasmic cohesion pool are the same protein partitioning between compartments, and the partition is cell-cycle regulated.

Type: cell-cycle-resolved fractionation with quantitative proteomics

Experiment: Reconstitute cohesin, sororin, WAPL, PDS5 and recombinant C11orf98 and ask whether the microprotein increases sororin occupancy on cohesin (adaptor) or increases the anti-WAPL effect per bound sororin molecule (activator), using PARylated and non-PARylated microprotein and the 16-lysine-and-arginine PARylation-site mutant as controls.

Hypothesis: The microprotein acts as an adaptor rather than as an allosteric activator of sororin.

Type: in vitro reconstitution with purified components

C11orf98 / RSMC review notes

Symbol and ORF situation — read this first

Two things about this gene's identity matter for curation and are easy to get wrong.

1. The symbol. HGNC still uses C11orf98 (HGNC:51238, previous symbol C11orf48).
UniProt has adopted the gene name RSMC ("28S rRNA/ribosome and sororin micro-cofactor")
from PMID:41261216, and the GOA file downloaded for this gene already shows RSMC in its
SYMBOL column. This repository's directory convention is the HGNC symbol, so the directory
and gene_symbol stay C11orf98, with RSMC recorded as an alias.

2. The ORF. The RSMC microprotein is an alternative ORF, not a splice isoform:
PMID:41261216 — the
sentence is truncated by a figure callout, but the claim is unambiguous, and the authors
confirmed that their RSMC siRNAs leave LBHD1 mRNA unchanged. HGNC records C11orf48 as the
previous symbol of both C11orf98 (E9PRG8) and LBHD1 (HGNC:28351, UniProt Q9BQE6),
which is the fingerprint of an alt-ORF locus that was later split into two gene records.

The practical consequence: E9PRG8 is itself the alt-ORF product. The whole 123-residue
UniProt entry is the microprotein, there are no alternative_products on the entry, and no
annotation below needs an isoform: scope. What must not happen is transfer of any of these
annotations to LBHD1/Q9BQE6, which is a different protein from a different reading frame of
the same locus. That is the ORF-blurring risk here, and it lives between UniProt entries,
not within one.

What the 2026 paper shows

PMID:41261216

The chain of evidence:

  • Direct, reconstituted interaction with Sororin/CDCA5: PMID:41261216 from recombinant GST-RSMC and
    6xHis-Flag-Sororin pulldowns.
  • Loss of function causes cohesion defects: PMID:41261216 Knockdown gave ~40% defective cells versus ~80% for
    Sororin, and combined siRNA plus heterozygous knockout reached ~67%.
  • Mechanism, part 1 — Sororin recruitment: PMID:41261216
  • Mechanism, part 2 — stimulating Sororin's anti-Wapl activity: PMID:41261216
  • Regulation: PMID:41261216
  • Localisation: PMID:41261216

The other half of the name: ribosome biogenesis

The "R" in RSMC is not from this paper. C11orf98 was independently found as a component of
human nucleoplasmic pre-60S particles: PMID:37491604 UniProt models it in PDB 8INF at 3.0 A in complex with
the pre-60S ribosome and describes it as a possible distal functional ortholog of yeast
Alb1. The cohesion paper acknowledges this and notes a difference from the yeast case:
PMID:41261216

The earlier APEX proximity-labelling study is consistent: the top partners were the two
major nucleolar chaperones, PMID:28589727, and the microprotein
localises to nucleoli, PMID:28589727

So this gene has two proposed functions from two non-overlapping lines of work: a
nucleolar/pre-60S ribosome assembly factor, and a nucleoplasmic cofactor for Sororin in
sister chromatid cohesion. Unlike SMIM26, these are not topologically incompatible — both
are nuclear, and the protein is small and disordered at both termini (UniProt: residues
1-21 and 82-123 disordered), which is typical of a factor that is recycled between
complexes. But nothing in either paper tests whether the two roles are connected, and the
composite UniProt protein name asserts both.

Curation position taken

  • GO:0045876 positive regulation of sister chromatid cohesion (IMP) → ACCEPT, core.
    It is the best-supported claim in the record: direct binding, knockdown, heterozygous
    knockout, rescue, and an inter-sister-distance assay.
  • GO:0005634 nucleus and GO:0005730 nucleolus → ACCEPT both. They are not in
    conflict; the nucleolar pool is where the pre-60S work puts the protein and the
    nucleoplasmic pool is where the cohesion work puts it.
  • GO:0005515 protein binding (both IPI rows) → MARK_AS_OVER_ANNOTATED per project
    guidance. The Sororin row (WITH UniProtKB:Q96FF9) is the most informative interaction this
    gene has and deserves a real molecular function term rather than bare protein binding.
  • Proposed as reviewer additions, all single-source and flagged as such:
    GO:0030674 protein-macromolecule adaptor activity (RSMC brings Sororin to cohesin on
    chromatin), GO:0120187 positive regulation of protein localization to chromatin (the
    Sororin recruitment result), and GO:0042273 ribosomal large subunit biogenesis (the
    pre-60S structural identification).

What is not established

  • Whether the ribosome-assembly role and the cohesion role are mechanistically linked, or
    whether the protein simply has two jobs.
  • Whether RSMC is required for pre-60S maturation. PMID:37491604 identifies it as a
    component of the particle; it does not report a depletion phenotype, and UniProt's
    wording is "may be involved".
  • Whether homozygous loss is viable. Only heterozygous knockouts were obtained
    (PMID:41261216), which is suggestive of essentiality but is not stated as such.
