Gene Description

LPXN (leupaxin) is a paxillin-family LD/LIM adaptor enriched in hematopoietic cells and vascular smooth muscle. It cycles among the cytoplasm, focal adhesions or podosomes, and the nucleus. At adhesion structures, its LD motifs bind focal-adhesion-family tyrosine kinases including PYK2/PTK2B and FAK1/PTK2, while regulated contacts with Src-family kinases, PTP-PEST, and p95PKL connect integrin-associated signaling to cytoskeletal organization. LIM3 directs focal-adhesion targeting, and adhesion-site recruitment precedes stimulus-dependent LPXN tyrosine phosphorylation. In murine osteoclasts, podosomal LPXN promotes adhesion and bone-resorptive activity. B-cell-receptor stimulation recruits LPXN to the plasma membrane and induces Lyn binding in human BJAB lymphoma cells; gain-of-function experiments in mouse A20 cells show selective inhibition of B-cell-receptor outputs. Nuclear LPXN acts as a transcriptional coactivator with androgen receptor in prostate-cancer cells and as a serum-response-factor cofactor in vascular smooth-muscle systems; beta-catenin coactivation has also been observed in hepatocellular-carcinoma models. These transcriptional and migration effects depend on cell state and experimental context. Two human isoforms differ at their extreme N termini, but no isoform-specific activity has been established.

Core Functions

LPXN acts through its LD and LIM interaction motifs as a regulated protein-macromolecule adaptor at focal adhesions and podosomes. LD1 and LD3 bind the two sites of the PYK2/PTK2B FAT domain, while cellular studies place LPXN with PYK2/PTK2B or FAK1/PTK2, Src-family kinases, PTP-PEST, and p95PKL. These contacts couple adhesion-site organization to integrin-dependent adhesion and cytoskeletal signaling. In human MDA-MB-231 breast-cancer cells, LPXN negatively regulates collagen-I adhesion, whereas murine osteoclast studies show a positive contribution to adhesion and resorption; these opposing directions support a context-dependent integrin-adhesion role. Human prostate-cancer-cell experiments additionally establish context-specific migration effects. Upon B-cell-receptor stimulation, human BJAB cells recruit phosphorylated LPXN to the plasma membrane and induce its binding to Lyn, whereas selective pathway inhibition was demonstrated by LPXN overexpression in mouse A20 cells. The activity is therefore a dynamic adaptor mechanism, not evidence for one obligate stable LPXN complex.

LPXN literature notes

Literature was searched directly in PubMed with user permission to skip provider deep research. No provider-named deep-research file was created. Exact quotations below come from repository publication caches fetched through project tooling.

Molecular identity and interaction architecture

  • LPXN is a paxillin-family LIM adaptor originally identified in human hematopoietic cells. Its N terminus contains three LD-like interaction motifs and its C terminus contains four LIM domains [PMID:9565592, "We have identified a novel cytoplasmic protein, leupaxin, that is preferentially expressed in hematopoietic cells and is most homologous to the focal adhesion protein, paxillin."]. The discovery paper directly showed association with PYK2 [PMID:9565592, "We demonstrate here that leupaxin associates with a second FAK family member, PYK2."].
  • Structural/biochemical work resolves the PYK2 interface: LPXN LD1 and LD3 preferentially engage the two sites of the PYK2 FAT domain [PMID:26866573, "The first and third LD motifs of leupaxin preferably target the two LD-binding sites on the Pyk2-FAT domain, respectively."], and purified full-length LPXN binds PYK2 FAT in a stable 1:1 complex [PMID:26866573, "Moreover, the full-length leupaxin binds to Pyk2-FAT as a stable one-to-one complex."]. This in-vitro stoichiometry should not be converted into a claim that LPXN and PYK2 form an obligate constitutive complex in every cell.
  • UniProt records two human protein isoforms. Isoform 2 changes only the four-residue N terminus (MEEL to MSTLLISSS; VSP_042655); none of the cited functional papers establishes an isoform-specific interaction or phenotype. Screen annotations involving isoforms of partners likewise do not establish LPXN isoform specificity.

Osteoclast podosomes and adhesion signaling

  • In murine osteoclasts, LPXN localizes to the podosomal/sealing-zone signaling complex and associates with PYK2, FAK, PTP-PEST, and p95PKL [PMID:12674328, "We have identified leupaxin to be a component of the osteoclast podosomal signaling complex. We have found that leupaxin in murine osteoclasts is associated with both PYK2 and pp125FAK in the osteoclast."]. LPXN depletion decreases in-vitro resorption [PMID:12674328, "Finally, in vitro inhibition of leupaxin expression in the osteoclast led to a decrease in resorptive capacity."]. These are direct mouse osteoclast results, not direct evidence for human bone physiology.
  • A second murine osteoclast study found that LPXN overexpression increases cell adhesion and resorptive activity [PMID:16914530, "In the current study, overexpression of LPXN in murine osteoclasts resulted in both enhanced resorptive activity and cell adhesion, as assessed by in vitro resorption assays."]. Src co-immunoprecipitates with LPXN, with more association after TNF-alpha exposure [PMID:16914530, "After exposure to the pro-inflammatory and osteoclastogenic cytokine TNF-alpha, there was an increase in the level of Src that coimmunoprecipitated with LPXN."]. Together with PYK2/PTP-PEST, this supports a regulated podosomal scaffold rather than an LPXN enzymatic activity.

Hematopoietic and B-cell signaling boundary

  • BCR stimulation in human BJAB lymphoma cells recruits tyrosine-phosphorylated LPXN to the plasma membrane and induces binding to Lyn [PMID:17640867, "We show here that leupaxin (LPXN), a member of this family, was tyrosine-phosphorylated and recruited to the plasma membrane of human BJAB lymphoma cells upon BCR stimulation and that it interacted with Lyn (a critical Src family tyrosine kinase in BCR signaling) in a BCR-induced manner."].
  • The functional pathway-inhibition experiments were performed in mouse A20 B lymphoma cells: LPXN overexpression selectively suppressed BCR-induced JNK, p38 and, more weakly, Akt activation [PMID:17640867, "The overexpression of LPXN in mouse A20 B lymphoma cells led to the suppression of BCR-induced activation of JNK, p38 MAPK, and, to a lesser extent, Akt, but not ERK and NFkappaB, suggesting that LPXN can selectively repress BCR signaling."]. This species and gain-of-function boundary matters when evaluating a human negative-regulation annotation.

Focal adhesions, migration, and cancer contexts

  • In MDA-MB-231 breast-cancer cells, LIM3 targets LPXN to focal adhesions and recruitment precedes bombesin-induced phosphorylation at Tyr22/62/72 [PMID:20543562, "Using mutagenesis, we identified LIM3 as the primary FA targeting domain for LPXN and showed BN-induced LPXN tyrosine phosphorylation on residues 22, 62 and 72."]. LPXN depletion increases collagen-I adhesion, opposite to paxillin depletion [PMID:20543562, "LPXN siRNA stimulated whereas paxillin siRNA inhibited cell adhesion."]. The effect is substrate- and cell-context-specific, not evidence that LPXN universally inhibits adhesion.
  • Human PC-3 prostate-cancer cells contain an LPXN–PYK2–Src–PTP-PEST complex [PMID:17329398, "In PC-3 cells, LPXN was also found to associate with Pyk2, c-Src, and PTP-PEST."]. LPXN depletion reduces PC-3 migration [PMID:17329398, "A siRNA-mediated inhibition of LPXN resulted in decreased in vitro PC-3 cell migration."], whereas LPXN overexpression increases migration and Rho activity and PTP-PEST overexpression has the opposite effect. These results establish a disease-cell signaling context, not a general developmental migration program.
  • In prostate-cancer cells LPXN also shuttles between cytoplasm/focal adhesions and nucleus, binds androgen receptor in a ligand-dependent manner, and activates AR-dependent transcription [PMID:18451096, "We could also demonstrate for the first time that leupaxin interacts with the androgen receptor in a ligand-dependent manner and serves as a transcriptional activator of this hormone receptor in PCa cells."]. LPXN depletion reduces PC-3 and DU145 motility/invasion [PMID:18451096, "In contrast, knockdown of leupaxin expression in androgen-independent PC-3 and DU 145 cells induced a significant decrease of both the invasive capacity and motility."]. AR coactivation should remain bounded to ligand-responsive prostate-cancer assays.
  • In hepatocellular-carcinoma models, LPXN binds beta-catenin and enhances transcription through SRC-1 and p300 recruitment [PMID:26361959, "We show that leupaxin could interact with β-catenin and enhance its transcriptional activity through recruitment of coactivator complex, including steroid receptor coactivator 1 (SRC-1) and P300."]. This is an additional cancer-context nuclear coadapter role and should not be assumed to be the normal function of LPXN in all tissues.

Smooth-muscle nuclear cofactor and trafficking boundary

  • LPXN is enriched in human and mouse vascular smooth muscle [PMID:18497331, "Herein, we identified leupaxin in a screen for focal adhesion kinase binding partners in aortic smooth muscle, and we show that leupaxin is enriched in human and mouse vascular smooth muscle and that leupaxin expression is dynamically regulated during development."]. It forms a complex with SRF and associates with CArG-containing smooth-muscle promoters, while ectopic expression promotes marker transcription in mouse 10T1/2 and rat aortic smooth-muscle cells [PMID:18497331, "We found that leupaxin forms a complex with serum response factor and associates with CArG-containing regions of smooth muscle promoters and that ectopic expression of leupaxin induces smooth muscle marker gene expression in both 10T1/2 cells and rat aortic smooth muscle cells."]. Human promoter occupancy and animal-cell transcriptional perturbations must not be conflated.
  • FAK activity regulates the focal-adhesion/nuclear balance: increased FAK activity reduces nuclear LPXN and its SRF-dependent transcriptional effect [PMID:18497331, "Subsequent studies indicated that enhanced focal adhesion kinase activity (induced by fibronectin or expression of constitutively active focal adhesion kinase) attenuates the nuclear accumulation of leupaxin and limits the ability of leupaxin to enhance serum response factor-dependent gene transcription."]. This supports a shuttling adaptor/cofactor model, not sequence-specific DNA binding by LPXN.

High-throughput interaction and curator-deference boundary

  • The seeded IPI set includes directed and proteome-scale interaction maps. These establish assay-level partner provenance but do not by themselves show that every pair is endogenous, stable, simultaneous, or central to LPXN function. Fragment-level interactions (PMID:36115835), isoform-tagged partner constructs (PMID:32296183), and disease-network screens (PMID:32814053) require especially cautious interpretation.
  • PMID:10604475 is a paxillin–alpha4-integrin paper whose cached abstract does not mention LPXN. UniProt cites it for an LPXN interaction, presumably from evidence outside the abstract; without accessible LPXN-specific text, no independent LPXN claim was drawn from it.
