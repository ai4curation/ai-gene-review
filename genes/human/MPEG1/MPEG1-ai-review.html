Gene Description

Macrophage-expressed gene 1 protein (perforin-2) is a pore-forming protein of the MACPF/CDC superfamily and the founding member of the MPEG1 family. It is constitutively expressed in macrophages, dendritic cells, neutrophils, natural killer cells and keratinocytes. The full-length isoform is a membrane protein of cytoplasmic vesicles, anchored by a C-terminal transmembrane helix, and an N-terminal MACPF domain is followed by a P2 region and an MVB12-associated beta-prism (MABP) membrane-binding domain. Vesicles carrying perforin-2 traffic to and fuse with bacterium-containing phagosomes, where the protein oligomerises into ring- and arc-shaped prepores that convert, on acidification, into giant beta-barrel transmembrane pores; the MABP domain holds the assembly on the phagolysosome membrane with the pore-forming machinery directed at the engulfed target. Perforin-2 is required for phagocyte killing of a broad range of Gram-positive, Gram-negative and acid-fast bacteria and for the bactericidal action of reactive oxygen and nitrogen species; loss-of-function variants in humans cause immunodeficiency 77, with recurrent skin and soft-tissue infection. In cross-presenting dendritic cells the same pore-forming activity permeabilises antigen-containing endocytic compartments so that internalised antigen escapes to the cytosol for proteasomal processing and presentation on MHC class I, and perforin-2-deficient mice prime CD8+ T cells poorly against cell-associated antigen. How pore formation differs between these two settings is unsettled: the protein is extensively processed by endocytic proteases, and it is disputed whether the species that perforates endocytic membranes is the released ectodomain or the full-length membrane-anchored protein, and whether acidification is required in dendritic cells, which do not acidify their phagosomes. A second, alternatively spliced isoform lacks the transmembrane domain, does not reach vesicle membranes, and is secreted.

Core Functions

MPEG1 assembles into a homo-oligomeric MACPF pore. Soluble or membrane-tethered prepore rings and arcs convert to a giant transmembrane beta-barrel when two alpha-helical segments of each protomer refold into amphipathic beta-hairpins. The resulting large-diameter pore is used in two settings. In phagocytes it perforates the surface of engulfed bacteria from the phagolysosome membrane, killing them and permitting access of reactive oxygen and nitrogen species and hydrolases; acidification of the maturing phagolysosome triggers the prepore-to-pore transition. In cross-presenting dendritic cells the same activity breaches the antigen-containing endocytic compartment so that internalised antigen reaches the cytosol for proteasomal processing and MHC class I loading. These are treated here as one molecular function in two contexts rather than two activities: the reported differences concern which molecular species oligomerises (full-length membrane-anchored protein versus proteolytically released ectodomain), which membrane is perforated, and whether low pH is required - regulation, topology and target, none of which the molecular-function branch of GO distinguishes.

Suggested Questions for Experts

Q: Does MPEG1 have one pore-forming molecular function deployed in two settings, or two mechanistically distinct pore-forming activities? PMID:42618800 reports that endocytic escape uses full-length membrane-anchored perforin-2 and does not require low pH, whereas the antibacterial pore characterised structurally in PMID:31537793 is acid-triggered. Is the difference one of regulation and topology only, or of the assembly pathway itself?

Suggested experts: GO molecular-function curators, MACPF/CDC structural biologists

Q: If the full-length, transmembrane-anchored protein makes the pore during cross-presentation, what is the function of the extensive proteolytic processing by endocytic proteases and of the released ectodomain? Is the ectodomain the antibacterial effector, an inactivation product, or something else?

Q: If acidification is not the trigger in cross-presenting dendritic cells, what is? Candidate triggers include lipid composition of the target membrane, redox state, a protease-generated conformational change, or local concentration reached on oligomerisation.

Q: How does the cell avoid catastrophic self-perforation if the pore-forming machinery is deployed from an anchor in the host compartment membrane? The MABP-orientation model of PMID:31537793 was the previous answer; PMID:42618800 argues that in dendritic cells the host membrane is in fact the perforated one.

Q: Should MPEG1 also carry GO:0140911 pore-forming activity? GO:0022829 wide pore channel activity is defined in terms of un-gated facilitated diffusion through gap junctions and porins, which does not describe a gated lytic MACPF beta-barrel, yet it is the term used consistently across PRF1, GSDMD and MPEG1.

Suggested experts: GO ontology developers

Q: Do the mechanisms proposed by PMID:42618800 and PMID:41951589 describe the same cell biology? One places the critical event at the endocytic membrane (anchored pore, pH-independent escape) and the other at the plasma membrane and at endosomal pH control (macropinocytic uptake, restraint of acidification). Both agree perforin-2 is required for cross-presentation.

Why this gene was selected

MPEG1/perforin-2 is a MACPF-superfamily pore former with a single, well-supported molecular
function (GO:0022829 wide pore channel activity, IDA) that is deployed in two very different
biological settings: killing of bacteria inside phagolysosomes, and rupture of antigen-containing
endocytic compartments so that antigen reaches the cytosol for cross-presentation. A 2026 EMBO J
paper (PMID:42618800) argues that the form of the protein that makes the pore differs between the
two settings, which raises the curation question of whether this is one molecular function used
twice, or two mechanistically distinct activities.

Status of the evidence: this is abstract-only in the cache (full_text_available: false), it is a
single paper, and it has no published rebuttal. It does not dispute that MPEG1 forms pores, nor
that it is required for cross-presentation; both of those are independently replicated. What it
disputes is (a) the topological model in which the TM anchor merely aims the pore at bacteria, and
(b) the assumption - inherited from the acid-activation result of PMID:31537793 - that acidification
is required for pore formation in every setting.

Note that the two claims pull in the same direction and are mutually consistent: if the protein
stays anchored, its pore is necessarily made in the host compartment membrane, and a DC phagosome
that is deliberately kept non-acidic cannot supply the acid trigger, so a different trigger must
exist. UniProt already records the released-ectodomain model as an inference from the mouse ortholog
("cleaved by trypsin in proximity of the helical transmembrane domain releases the ectodomain into
the lysosomal lumen to orient the pore-forming domain toward the endogenous membranes",
ECO:0000250|UniProtKB:A1L314) and as a separate "processed form" chain; PMID:42618800 argues this is
not the species that does the work in cross-presentation.

Curation position taken

One core molecular function, not two. GO:0022829 covers the act of building an oligomeric
transmembrane pore; that act is the same chemistry (MACPF prepore -> beta-barrel) in both settings.
What PMID:42618800 changes is which molecular species oligomerises (full-length anchored vs
released ectodomain), which membrane is perforated (host endocytic membrane vs engulfed bacterium)
and what triggers it (pH-independent vs acid-triggered). Those are differences in regulation,
topology and target - none of which GO's molecular-function branch distinguishes, and none of which
would be captured by splitting GO:0022829 into two entries. Splitting on a single, abstract-only
paper would also assert more confidence than the evidence carries. The two deployments are instead
separated in directly_involved_in (antibacterial defence terms vs the cross-presentation terms)
and the divergence is recorded in the reason fields of GO:0022829 and GO:0061474 and in
suggested_questions.

No annotation is removed on the strength of PMID:42618800. GO:0061474 phagolysosome membrane
(is_active_in, IDA) is if anything strengthened by it: a pore made by anchored full-length
protein is unambiguously active in the compartment membrane. GO:0005576 extracellular region is
kept as non-core - it reflects the secreted, TM-less splice isoform perforin-2b
PMID:28705375 and PMID:28705375 - not the pore-forming species.

Ontology observation (not acted on). GO:0022829 is defined as "the energy-independent
facilitated diffusion of propanediol through a large pore, un-gated channel. Examples include gap
junctions ... and porins", which is a poor fit for an acid-gated lytic MACPF beta-barrel; GO:0140911
pore-forming activity ("a protein is inserted into the membrane of another cell where it forms
transmembrane pores") fits the antibacterial deployment far better. GO:0022829 is nonetheless the
term GO uses consistently across this protein class (PRF1, GSDMD and MPEG1 all carry it), so it is
accepted here rather than replaced for MPEG1 alone; adding GO:0140911 - as PRF1 already has via
InterPro - is raised as a recommendation instead. Neither term covers pore formation in the cell's
own endomembrane, which is what the cross-presentation deployment does.

  • GO:0055085 transmembrane transport is an automatic inter-ontology inference from GO:0022829

    over-annotated.
  • GO:0002478 is the parent of GO:0042590; both are carried by ISS from the mouse ortholog. Kept,
    but the specific child is the informative one.
