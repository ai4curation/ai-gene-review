CC   -!- FUNCTION: May act as a GTPase-activating protein for Rab family
CC       protein(s) (PubMed:20727515, PubMed:20797691). Involved in neuronal
CC       projection development, probably through negative modulation of ARF6
CC       function (PubMed:20727515). Required for the maintenance of
CC       postsynaptic neuron dendritic spines in the adult by suppressing ARF6
CC       activity (PubMed:32004315). Involved in the regulation of synaptic
CC       vesicle trafficking (PubMed:31257402). Promotes axonal outgrowth and
CC       membrane trafficking at the growth cone (By similarity). Positively
CC       regulates radial migration and morphological maturation of pyramidal
CC       neurons during cortical development by preventing ARF6 activation (By
CC       similarity). In one study, has been shown to inhibit the activity of
CC       the vacuolar-type ATPase (V-ATPase) by inducing disassembly of the V-
CC       ATPase complex (PubMed:38593795). In another study, has been shown to
CC       positively regulate V-ATPase assembly in neurons which promotes
CC       acidification of lysosomes and synaptic vesicles (By similarity). This
CC       supports neuronal autophagy and also regulates synaptic vesicle
CC       reacidification and the maintenance of the synaptic vesicle reserve
CC       pool (By similarity). Protects neuronal cells against oxidative stress
CC       (PubMed:30335140). Involved in the maintainenance of endosomal-mediated
CC       vesicle recycling and sustained exocytosis of hair cell ribbon synapses
CC       (By similarity). Facilitates formation of tubular recycling endosomes
CC       which promotes recycling of clathrin-independent endocytosis cargo
CC       proteins back to the plasma membrane (PubMed:32475639).

CC   -!- DISEASE: Developmental and epileptic encephalopathy 16 (DEE16)
CC       [MIM:615338]: A severe autosomal recessive neurologic disorder
CC       characterized by onset of seizures in the first weeks or months of
CC       life. Seizures can be of various types, are unresponsive to medication,
CC       last for long periods of time, and occur frequently. Affected infants
CC       show psychomotor regression or lack of psychomotor development, as well
CC       as other neurologic features such as extrapyramidal signs and
CC       hypotonia. Most die in childhood. {ECO:0000269|PubMed:23526554,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:27541164}. Note=The disease is caused by variants
CC       affecting the gene represented in this entry.
CC   -!- DISEASE: Deafness, autosomal dominant, 65 (DFNA65) [MIM:616044]: A form
CC       of non-syndromic sensorineural hearing loss. Sensorineural deafness
CC       results from damage to the neural receptors of the inner ear, the nerve
CC       pathways to the brain, or the area of the brain that receives sound
CC       information. DFNA65 is characterized by postlingual onset of slowly
CC       progressive hearing loss in the third decade. Initially affecting the
CC       high frequencies, the hearing loss eventually affects all frequencies
CC       and results in severe to profound deafness in the seventh decade.
CC       Vestibular function is normal. {ECO:0000269|PubMed:24729539,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:24729547, ECO:0000269|PubMed:38413761}. Note=The
CC       disease is caused by variants affecting the gene represented in this
CC       entry.
CC   -!- DISEASE: Deafness, onychodystrophy, osteodystrophy, impaired
CC       intellectual development, and seizures syndrome (DOORS) [MIM:220500]: A
CC       syndrome characterized by sensorineural deafness, intellectual
CC       disability, hypoplastic or absent nails, small or absent distal
CC       phalanges of hands and feet. Additional features include coarse facies,
CC       a large nose with wide nasal bridge, bulbous tip and anteverted nares,
CC       a long prominent philtrum and downturned corners of the mouth.
CC       Progressive neurological manifestations include seizures from infancy,
CC       optic atrophy, and peripheral polyneuropathy.
