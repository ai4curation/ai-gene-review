CC   -!- FUNCTION: G protein-coupled receptor that binds melanocyte-stimulating
CC       hormones (alpha- and beta-MSH) and corticotropin/ACTH, which are
CC       peptide products of the POMC precursor (PubMed:12646665,
CC       PubMed:14764818, PubMed:25163632, PubMed:32327598, PubMed:33858992,
CC       PubMed:8392067). Functions as a central component of the leptin-
CC       melanocortin pathway, which is essential for maintaining energy
CC       homeostasis (PubMed:32327598, PubMed:33858992). Upon activation,
CC       couples to G(s) protein, stimulating adenylate cyclase and the cAMP-
CC       dependent signaling pathway, which promotes anorexogenic signaling in
CC       the hypothalamus and contributes to a negative energy balance
CC       (PubMed:12588803, PubMed:14764818, PubMed:25163632, PubMed:33858992).
CC       Regulates food intake: activation by agonists suppresses appetite,
CC       whereas the antagonist Agouti-related protein/AGRP precludes agonist-
CC       induced signaling, thereby stimulating appetite (PubMed:9311920,
CC       PubMed:29311635). Modulates the firing activity of neurons in
CC       paraventricular nucleus (PVN) of the hypothalamus via alpha-MSH and
CC       AGRP regulation of inwardly rectifying potassium channel KCNJ13
CC       closure, independently of G(s) signaling (PubMed:32327598). In the PVN,
CC       also interacts with opsin 3/OPN3, which couples to G(i/o) proteins to
CC       inhibit MC4R-mediated cAMP signaling, thereby promoting food intake
CC       (PubMed:39951488). In intestinal epithelial cells, contributes to
CC       inhibition of hepatic glucose production via nesfatin-1/NUCB2, leading
CC       to increased cAMP levels and glucagon-like peptide 1 (GLP-1) secretion
CC       (PubMed:39562740). Interaction with MGRN1 displaces the G(s) protein,
CC       further decreasing MC4R signaling activity (PubMed:19737927). Also
CC       activated by gamma-MSH, though with low potency (PubMed:8392067).
