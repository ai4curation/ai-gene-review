Gene Description

C18orf32 encodes a 76-residue protein of the UPF0729 family (DUF4512) with an N-terminal hydrophobic segment and a disordered C-terminal tail. The N-terminal region anchors the protein in the endoplasmic reticulum, and when cells are loaded with fatty acids a fraction moves onto nascent and mature lipid droplets. The protein turns over constitutively by ER-associated degradation through the E3 ligase gp78 (AMFR), derlin-1, VCP and the proteasome. In a genetic screen, C18orf32 was one of a small set of ER factors required for efficient removal of the inositol acyl chain from newly made GPI-anchored proteins by the deacylase PGAP1, and a homozygous frameshift variant causes an autosomal recessive GPI-biosynthesis-defect neurodevelopmental disorder (GPIBD25) with loss of this activity. How C18orf32 supports GPI-inositol deacylation is unknown, and it has no known enzymatic activity. A single recent study also places it on SEC22B-positive secretory autophagosomes with a role in lipid droplet secretion. An early overexpression screen reported that it can activate an NF-kappaB reporter, but this has not been followed up.

Core Functions

ER-anchored small membrane protein that also moves onto lipid droplets. It is required for efficient PGAP1-dependent removal of the inositol acyl chain from GPI-anchored proteins in the ER, but the molecular basis of this requirement is unknown. No molecular function term is assigned.

Suggested Questions for Experts

Q: How does C18orf32 support GPI-inositol deacylation by PGAP1? PMID:29255114 found PGAP1 expression, stability and localisation unchanged in knockout cells. Does C18orf32 bind PGAP1 or GPI-anchored protein precursors, or act indirectly, for example through ER lipid or calnexin-cycle homeostasis? The answer decides whether a GPI-anchor processing process term would describe participation or only necessity.

Suggested experts: Morihisa Fujita, Taroh Kinoshita

Q: Is the lipid droplet pool of C18orf32 functional, or is it a by-product of ER partitioning of a hairpin-like protein that is normally removed by ERAD? Can the secretory autophagy and lipid droplet secretion role proposed in PMID:41351175 be reproduced independently?

Suggested experts: James A Olzmann

Suggested Experiments

Experiment: In C18orf32 knockout cells, test proximity labelling or co-immunoprecipitation of tagged C18orf32 with PGAP1 and with inositol-acylated GPI-AP precursors. Measure PGAP1 activity in vitro on microsomes from wild-type and knockout cells, with and without recombinant C18orf32. Ask whether the N-terminal ER-anchoring region or the disordered C-terminal tail is needed to rescue PIPLC sensitivity.

Hypothesis: C18orf32 acts directly on the PGAP1 deacylation step rather than indirectly through ER homeostasis.

Type: biochemical reconstitution and genetic rescue

C18orf32 (Q8TCD1) notes

2026-10-08 review session (MICROPROTEINS Tier 2)

Gene: C18orf32, UPF0729 protein, 76 aa, PE1 (protein level), HGNC protein-coding, MANE
NM_001035005.4. Canonical-ORF small protein (not an alternative-ORF peptide), so the
standard genes/human/C18orf32/ folder is correct. Domain: Pfam DUF4512 (PF14975),
PANTHER PTHR13456 (UPF0729). N-terminal hydrophobic region (1-37), C-terminal disordered
region (46-76).

Literature found (PubMed esearch "C18orf32": 5 hits; plus GPI screen)

  • PMID:12761501 (Matsuda 2003, abstract only): genome-scale overexpression screen of
    150,000 cDNAs in HEK293 with an NF-kB luciferase reporter; source of the "putative
    NF-kappa-B-activating protein" name and the HMP row. No follow-up on C18orf32 in the
    abstract PMID:12761501.
  • PMID:23864651 (Huang 2013): MYTH screen with GLP-1R bait; C18orf32 not named in the
    cached text (likely in a supplementary interactor table). Source of IPI GLP1R row.
  • PMID:29275994 (Bersuker 2018, full text): APEX2 proximity labelling of LD proteome.
    Key points:
  • ER under basal conditions, redistributes to LDs with oleate PMID:29275994.
  • Endogenous protein on LDs, antibody validated by KO PMID:29275994.
  • N-terminal hydrophobic domain necessary and sufficient for ER/LD targeting.
  • Constitutively degraded by gp78 (AMFR)/derlin-1 ERAD, VCP- and proteasome-dependent;
    DERL1 and AMFR co-purify PMID:29275994. These are the IPI rows: they reflect
    C18orf32 being an ERAD substrate, not an MF of C18orf32.
  • KO had no LD phenotype PMID:29275994; minor lipidomic changes.
  • PMID:29255114 (Liu 2018, JCB, full text; found via PubMed): haploid screen for factors
    needed for GPI-inositol deacylation by PGAP1 identified C18orf32 among 7 genes; KO gives
    partial PIPLC resistance PMID:29255114. Molecular function unknown PMID:29255114. PGAP1
    expression/stability/localization unchanged in KO cells (Fig 3).
  • PMID:35107634 (Salian 2022, abstract only): homozygous c.90dupC (p.Phe31Leufs*3) in two
    siblings with neurodevelopmental disorder (GPIBD25); KO HEK293 PIPLC-resistance rescued by
    WT but not mutant cDNA; mutant protein in ER and nuclear aggregates. Source of the EXP ER
    row (abstract says the mutant was in ER; WT localization presumably in full text).
  • PMID:41351175 (Kumar 2025, Cell Commun Signal, full text): single-group study claiming
    C18orf32 on SEC22B-positive secretory autophagosomes, binding SEC22B via its C-terminus,
    and required for LD secretion; liver knockdown causes steatosis. Interesting but
    unreplicated; much of the binding evidence is docking/in vitro affinity; Fig 1E is a GO
    enrichment of interactors. Not used for NEW annotations.
  • PMID:41218492 (chimeric RNA RPL17-C18orf32) and PMID:37438770 (blood transcriptome),
    PMID:27833855 (KLHL23 readthrough): not about the C18orf32 protein's function; not cited.

Assessment

  • Locations ER and LD: well supported by two independent groups (IDA, EXP); ACCEPT all
    five CC rows.
  • Protein binding x3: REMOVE (bare protein binding). DERL1/AMFR binding reflects the
    protein being an ERAD client; GLP1R is a MYTH hit with a hydrophobic segment.
  • NF-kB HMP: overexpression reporter hit from a genome-scale screen; no follow-up in
    23 years; MARK_AS_OVER_ANNOTATED.
  • GPI anchor: the KO phenotype (partial PIPLC resistance; rescue) shows necessity for
    efficient PGAP1-mediated inositol deacylation, but nothing says what C18orf32 does.
    PGAP1 performs the deacylation. Per the participation test, no NEW BP is proposed;
    raised as a question. No MF term fits.
