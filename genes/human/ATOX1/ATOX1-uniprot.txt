CC   -!- FUNCTION: Multifunctional copper-sensing protein that operates both as
CC       a cytosolic copper chaperone and as a copper-dependent transcription
CC       factor. Essential for maintaining cellular copper homeostasis and
CC       linking copper distribution to redox balance, signal transduction, and
CC       gene regulation. In the cytosol, transfers copper to the copper-
CC       transporting ATPase ATP7A at the trans-Golgi network, which is required
CC       for activation of secretory copper enzymes including lysyl oxidase
CC       (LOX) and SOD3. This chaperone function is essential for full SOD3 and
CC       LOX enzymatic activity. Acts as a copper-dependent transcription
CC       factor. Upon copper binding, translocates to the nucleus where it binds
CC       specific promoter elements and regulates transcription of genes
CC       involved in cell proliferation, oxidative stress defense, and
CC       extracellular matrix remodeling. Activates expression of target genes
CC       including CCND1, SOD3 and NCF1, thereby linking cellular copper status
CC       to gene expression programs controlling growth and redox homeostasis.
CC       {ECO:0000250|UniProtKB:O08997}.
CC   -!- SUBUNIT: Homodimer (PubMed:24837030). Interacts with ATP7B
CC       (PubMed:10966647). Interacts with ATP7A (PubMed:19453293,
CC       PubMed:21667063). Interacts (via dimer form) with SLC31A1 (via C-
CC       terminal domain); this interaction improves ATOX1 stability and
CC       controls intracellular Cu(I) levels (PubMed:24837030, PubMed:26745413).
CC       {ECO:0000269|PubMed:10966647, ECO:0000269|PubMed:19453293,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:21667063, ECO:0000269|PubMed:24837030,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:26745413}.

CC   -!- TISSUE SPECIFICITY: Ubiquitous.
CC   -!- DOMAIN: The heavy-metal-associated domain (HMA) coordinates a Cu(+) ion
CC       via the cysteine residues within the CXXC motif. The transfer of Cu(+)
CC       ion from ATOX1 to ATP7A involves the formation of a three-coordinate
CC       Cu(+)-bridged heterodimer where the metal is shared between the two
CC       metal binding sites of ATOX1 and ATP7A. The Cu(+) ion appears to switch
CC       between two coordination modes, forming two links with one protein and
CC       one with the other. Cisplatin, a chemotherapeutic drug, can bind the
CC       CXXC motif and hinder the release of Cu(+) ion.
CC       {ECO:0000269|PubMed:19453293, ECO:0000269|PubMed:31283225}.
