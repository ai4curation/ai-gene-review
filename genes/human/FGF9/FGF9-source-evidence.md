## Secretion and growth-factor activity

[PMID:8321227](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8321227/) explicitly reports secretion from human-FGF9-cDNA-transfected COS cells in its abstract. The recovered [author-upload original](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/14878527_Molecular_cloning_of_a_novel_cytokine_cDNA_encoding_the_ninth_member_of_the_Fibroblast_Growth_Factor_Family_which_has_a_unique_secretion_property) provides Results around Figure 5, printed page 4255: three CHO-derived product peaks have mitogenic activity and react with the C-terminal antibody. The 30/27-kDa products start at Ala2; a minor 21-kDa product starts at residue 33. These preparation-dependent products do not establish uniform processing of every FGF9 molecule. The Figure 4 operative passage was not recovered; the COS secretion statement is explicitly abstract-level. The immutable normal cache remains abstract-only. [PMID:8428960](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8428960/) supplies corroborating human glia-activating-factor purification/mitogenesis at abstract level only.

[PMID:10506195](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0021925819519810), Results and Figures 1, 4 and 5, was read through indexed publisher text. Figure 4 identifies **rat FGF9**. Microsomal protection/glycosylation and COS-cell secretion support ER translocation despite lack of a conventional cleaved signal peptide. Central hydrophobic and amino-terminal contributions must not be reduced to a claim of cytosolic confinement. [PMID:10713129](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10713129/) reports ER/Golgi transit and a non-cleaved amino-terminal signal in its abstract; its operative body and construct species were not independently established here. Broad GO:0005737 cytoplasm includes organelles, so secretory trafficking does not refute the inherited broad location or establish a separate soluble-cytosolic function.

## Receptor specificity, dimerization and distribution

The actual [PMID:8663044 author PDF](https://bpb-us-e2.wpmucdn.com/sites.wustl.edu/dist/8/2092/files/2019/08/Ornitz_FGF_specificity_JBC.pdf), Methods and Tables I–III (pages 15293–15296), was read; Table III pixels were inspected. Table II specifies **mouse FGF9**. The endpoint is receptor-dependent BaF3 mitogenesis, not direct affinity. Receptor species differ, and FGFR3/4 extracellular domains were joined to FGFR1 signaling domains. Responses depend on splice form and include FGFR3b; the experiment does not establish absolute c-isoform exclusivity.

[PMID:28757146](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5587394/), Results, Figures 1 and 4–6 captions, and STAR Methods, supplies direct binding and signaling evidence. Human FGF9 constructs include residues 35–208, an amino-terminal truncation, and engineered monomeric mutants. SPR and receptor-dependent ERK responses distinguish receptor recognition from downstream activation. The crystal complex uses engineered FGFR1c P252R. These constructs are not equivalent to an unmodified 208-residue ligand or natural isoforms.

[PMID:19219044](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2676118/), Results, Figures 3–8 captions and relevant Methods, separates two effects of mouse N143T: weaker dimerization/heparin binding with broader tissue distribution, but reduced mitogenic potency for several receptor forms. The 2017 study also finds an intrinsic receptor-potency effect. Thus increased signaling range need not mean greater receptor potency. Human multiple-synostoses association in [PMID:19589401](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19589401/) was read at abstract level; inconsistent S99D/S99N labels in that record and later narrative are not converted into a confident variant assertion. The separate structural/dimer abstracts PMID:11060292, PMID:11223514 and PMID:19564416 are corroboration, not claimed full experimental reads.

## Developmental and smooth-muscle contexts

[PMID:17848411](https://doi.org/10.1210/jc.2007-1690), author-upload Methods, FGF9 Results and Figure 4 caption, measures human second-trimester fetal-testis transcripts. Expression increases per testis but not per Sertoli cell. The retained IEP context is observational, not an FGF9 perturbation or proof of a human sex-determination mechanism.

[PMID:22926577](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11064-012-0874-2), author-upload Methods and Tables 1–2, explicitly identifies P31371/glia-activating factor in adult postmortem human substantia-nigra disease/control proteomics. Table 2 is a positive target join, but its functional category does not demonstrate formation or maturation of the substantia nigra. This is the basis for marking the developmental HEP interpretation over-annotated, without alleging a wrong-gene result.

[PMID:29118903 publisher PDF](https://e-century.us/files/ajtr/9/10/ajtr0053894.pdf), Methods and Figure 3E (page 4407, pixels inspected), shows FGF9 expression rescuing a miR-665-associated CCK-8 decrease in a human aortic smooth-muscle cell line. The purchased construct is not sequence-resolved. This does not assign every miRNA-dependent migration/Wnt phenotype to FGF9. Figure 2 caption directions disagree with Results, and Figure 3A labels 5′ UTR while Methods/caption state 3′ UTR; neither changes the visible rescue direction in Figure 3E.

[PMID:28259995](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5360430/), rat aortic SMC Methods and Results/Figures 5–6, supports the P36364 donor annotations: FGF9 knockdown reduces proliferation/migration and increases SMA/SM22α contractile markers. Interpretation follows these readouts despite inconsistent differentiation wording. PDGFRβ expression changes do not demonstrate FGF9–PDGFRβ binding. [PMID:21858205](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3157460/), Results/Figures 8–9 and ocular Methods, supports mouse donor eye-development scope; a neomorphic Y162C phenotype is not assumed to be simple loss of function or a direct human experiment.
