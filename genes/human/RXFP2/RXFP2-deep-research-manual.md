# RXFP2 manual literature synthesis

RXFP2 encodes relaxin family peptide receptor 2, also known as LGR8. UniProt describes RXFP2 as a "Receptor for relaxin" whose activity is "mediated by G proteins leading to stimulation of adenylate cyclase and an increase of cAMP" and notes that it may also act as a receptor for INSL3 [file:human/RXFP2/RXFP2-uniprot.txt]. Hsu et al. originally identified LGR7/RXFP1 and LGR8/RXFP2 as relaxin-responsive GPCRs that signal through cAMP [PMID:11809971, "Here, we demonstrate that two orphan heterotrimeric guanine nucleotide binding protein (G protein)-coupled receptors, LGR7 and LGR8, are capable of mediating the action of relaxin through an adenosine 3',5'-monophosphate (cAMP)-dependent pathway"].

The human Reactome RXFP2 event records both relaxin-2 and INSL3 as activating ligands, with lower relaxin affinity than RXFP1 and a likely physiological role as the INSL3 receptor [Reactome:R-HSA-444879, "Relaxin receptor 2 (RXFP2 or LGR8) is activated by porcine relaxin, human relaxin-2  and insulin-like peptide-3 (INSL3)."]. The reviewed human entry likewise places RXFP2 at the cell membrane as a multi-pass receptor [file:human/RXFP2/RXFP2-uniprot.txt, "CC   -!- SUBCELLULAR LOCATION: Cell membrane; Multi-pass membrane protein."].

The directly supported core of the human record is therefore plasma-membrane peptide-hormone GPCR activity through adenylate cyclase. The rat-derived electronic annotations to oocyte maturation, adenylate-cyclase-inhibiting signaling, negative regulation of proliferation, and negative regulation of apoptosis are not exposed as human RXFP2 functions in UniProt or Reactome and appear to over-propagate downstream rat biology onto the human receptor.
