# FANCD2: biological source guide

FANCD2 binds DNA and functions with FANCI in the ID2 repair complex. DNA binding, regulated clamp formation and recruitment of other repair proteins support interstrand-crosslink repair and additional genome-maintenance roles. The biochemical performer remains explicit: FANCL/UBE2T transfer ubiquitin, nucleases cut DNA and polymerases synthesize it; FANCD2 is not assigned those activities from complex membership.

## One DNA-associated functional unit

The core uses [GO:0003677 DNA binding](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/QuickGO/term/GO:0003677) to encompass two directly distinguished states: purified human FANCD2 preferentially binding ssDNA in PMID:19609304 and human ubiquitinated ID2 encircling duplex DNA in PMID:32269332. The latter structure places much of the DNA-contact groove on FANCI but also identifies FANCD2 DNA contacts. It is not evidence that isolated FANCD2 has every property of the heterodimer. The more specific ssDNA annotation remains intact; the core's broader activity preserves the experimentally supported substrate/state breadth rather than creating a redundant second unit.

[GO:0003689 DNA clamp loader activity](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/QuickGO/term/GO:0003689) requires ATP-hydrolysis-driven opening and closing of a clamp. It is not inferred from ID2 ring closure or from ATP present in the ubiquitination reaction. No independently established FANCD2 ATPase, nuclease, polymerase or polymerase-processivity reaction is assigned. The finite official term searches did not establish a more suitable dedicated ID2-clamp activity or complex term; the supported [DNA repair complex](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/QuickGO/term/GO:1990391) term is retained.

The three core process facets are [interstrand cross-link repair](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/QuickGO/term/GO:0036297), [double-strand break repair via homologous recombination](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/QuickGO/term/GO:0000724) and [replication fork processing](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/QuickGO/term/GO:0031297). Current official is_a/part_of closures show that none is an ancestor of another. They summarize the DNA-associated repair role, not three independently measured catalytic reactions. The chicken DT40 HR inference, Xenopus ICL experiments and human reconstitution/restart experiments remain distinct below. Aphidicolin restart fits the restoration/restart definition; hydroxyurea fork restraint is retained separately without asserting a demonstrated physical reversal mechanism.

The previous core's checkpoint process link is omitted only from this activity-to-process grouping. Its accepted annotation remains unchanged. Human transformed-cell complementation supports a checkpoint contribution, whereas primary mouse fibroblasts can retain an IR/PUVA checkpoint; these data do not establish that the same DNA-contact or clamp state directly performs every checkpoint response. Chromatin and nucleoplasm remain distinct locations: the latter excludes chromosomes and nucleolus. Neither location is used to erase the other.

## ID2, chromatin and repair

[PMID:19965384](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19965384/) directly tests **Xenopus** ID2 depletion and add-back in replication-coupled interstrand-crosslink repair. Wild-type versus ubiquitination-site mutant experiments support chromatin recruitment and repair participation. They do not make FANCD2 the incision enzyme or polymerase. Human [PMID:19995904](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2815579/) independently supplies chromatin-associated FANCD2/POLN evidence; selected Methods and Results distinguish benzonase-treated association from a resolved binary interface.

[PMID:32269332](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32269332/) reconstitutes full-length human ID2 and resolves the FANCI–FANCD2 heterodimer and its ubiquitinated DNA-clamp state. FANCL and UBE2T catalyze ubiquitin transfer. The resolved interface independently supports the heterodimerization refinement of the older FANCI interaction records; it does not retrospectively turn the older coimmunoprecipitations into structural assays. The selected original Methods, Results and captions were read; no new cryo-EM images or coordinates were analyzed.

Human [PMID:30335751](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30335751/) follows recruitment to nuclear psoralen/UVA lesions, while [PMID:26323318](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26323318/) separates nuclear/chromatin fractions. The encompassing nucleus term is retained across these pools. An undetectable untreated chromatin signal is an assay limit, not proof of absence. No inference of identical localization of all natural products follows.

The interaction studies distinguish individual partners and assay resolutions. Positive association is retained without assigning the partner’s catalytic activity to FANCD2.

Men1-null mouse phenotypes are not FANCD2 perturbations. The supported generic interaction and gamma-response endpoint remain non-core.

## Additional DNA, RNA and organelle contexts

These additional functional observations have distinct experimental and inference scopes.

1. **Single-stranded DNA binding:** PMID:19609304 directly tests purified human FANCD2 with alternative DNA substrates and antibody controls. The cDNA contains exon 44. This does not establish universal natural-product equivalence or FANCD2 nuclease activity.
2. **Homologous-recombination repair:** [PMID:15601828](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC538764/) tests chicken DT40 Fancd2 disruption and rescue, SCneo/I-SceI repair and gene conversion. Increased spontaneous SCE and retained RAD51 foci limit any universal recombination-loss claim. The inherited human inference remains ISS, not human IMP.
3. **Fork processing:** [PMID:25659033](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4614542/) directly tests aphidicolin-stalled fork restart in human PD20 cells. Higher K561R expression limits physiological equivalence. [PMID:26797144](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26797144/) separately establishes FANCD2–FAN1-dependent restraint of hydroxyurea-stalled fork progression; it does not identify a physical reversal mechanism or make the two endpoints interchangeable.
4. **R-loop processing:** [GO:0062176](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/QuickGO/term/GO:0062176) explicitly denotes disassembly. Actual publisher Methods/Results in [PMID:30431240](https://febs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/febs.14700) and the full cached original of [PMID:38165804](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38165804/) support suppression of R-loop accumulation through RNA handling and export, with SRSF1 bridging ID2 to NXF1. They do not resolve FANCD2-mediated disassembly of pre-existing R-loops. The inherited proposal is therefore UNDECIDED, while its positive phenotype and all original quotation anchors remain intact. The source identity and historical quotations remain preserved while its assertion lifecycle is assessed separately.
5. **Mitochondrial pool:** [PMID:28378742](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5381226/) uses an endogenous tagged **mouse** Fancd2 knock-in, functional controls, MEFs/tissues and mitochondrial fractionation/imaging. The official reviewed mouse identifier is **Q80V62**, not the earlier authored Q80V54. The inherited ISO proposal remains contextual and does not imply direct human imaging, all-product localization or an established mtDNA-repair role.


## PAINT placement and experimental descendants

The actual PANTHER version 19 PTHR32086 tree and alignment place the human node PTN002528859 below PTN000788426. Its ungapped 1,451-residue sequence matches the displayed Q9BXW9 sequence. Seven non-negated IBD assertions occur on that ancestral node: nucleus, condensed chromosome, DNA polymerase binding, interstrand cross-link repair, intra-S DNA-damage checkpoint, meiotic chromosome pairing and meiotic double-strand-break repair. These are ancestral assertions, not independent similarity transfers. Human FANCD2 appearing among the descendant evidence for nucleus and polymerase binding is expected and is not circular support.

The donor identities were checked against current official records: mouse Fancd2/Q80V62/MGI:2448480, fly Fancd2/Q8MYL1/FBgn0038827, Arabidopsis FANCD2/F4JIJ1/AT4G14970 and worm fcd-2/Q9U2H4/WBGene00012767. Other protein products were not silently equated with these records.
