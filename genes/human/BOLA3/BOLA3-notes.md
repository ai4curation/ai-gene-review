# BOLA3 annotation audit — 2026-10-02

This audit concerns the existing human Q53S33 review. The reviewed UniProt record describes a 107-residue canonical protein and two named isoforms. Q8N338 is separately indexed and is not treated as synonymous with the canonical protein or sequence-identical to Q53S33-2. All 15 machine-source annotation objects, source reference identifiers/titles, raw UniProt and GOA bytes, and the existing provider report are preserved. The 22 raw GOA rows collapse to those 15 source objects; none has an isoform or NOT field in the review.

The annotation-reviewer and core-function-synthesizer skills were consulted. The task-specific standing instruction retains supported generic protein interactions as non-core when no justified specific protein-function replacement is established. This is a scoped exception to the skill’s general informational-exclusion recommendation; it is not a global policy change. Unverified interactions remain undecided.

## Primary source boundaries

All seven cached abstracts were read. A true `full_text_available` cache flag does not ensure that the particular experimental section or supplementary record is present.

- **PMID:27532772:** The human biochemical Results, yeast genetic Results and Discussion were read. Yeast Bol1/Bol3 deletion and rescue place these proteins in late Fe-S protein maturation, especially the LIAS-linked lipoylation phenotype; the study distinguishes this from the broad defects of early ISC factors. Direct human assays establish GLRX5 heterocomplexes and report NFU1 interactions, preferentially holo-NFU1 for BOLA3; the later conflicting NFU1 reports are addressed below. The paper leaves the precise physiological mechanism unresolved. Its yeast iron-regulon experiments separate mitochondrial Bol1/Bol3 from cytosolic Bol2. This does not justify a positive human BOLA3 iron-homeostasis annotation solely from participation in Fe-S assembly.
- **PMID:34063696:** Results 2.1–2.3 and Discussion were read, together with the beginning of Methods 4.1. Human wild-type and C59Y BOLA3 comparisons support GLRX5 association, altered interaction geometry, retained cluster binding and reduced NFU1 cluster-assembly efficiency in the mutant. C59Y does not abolish the protein fold or all complex function. The proposed explanation of one compound-heterozygous patient’s recovery is an interpretation, not a demonstrated developmental rescue mechanism. The experiments do not establish that the reconstituted route is the sole cellular transfer pathway.
- **PMID:22746225:** The whole available cached body contains the Introduction and Discussion but omits Results and Methods. It primarily describes BOLA1, while specifically discussing BOLA3 in mitochondrial Fe-S biogenesis. The attempted PMC page returned reCAPTCHA. Its BOLA1-localization and redox sentences must not be quoted as direct BOLA3 measurements. The curated BOLA3 mitochondrial IDA is retained with explicit curator deference because the location is biologically established; no misattribution is alleged.
- **PMID:27499296:** The available cache contains the abstract and Introduction, not the target interaction table. Its source BOLA3–GLRX5 assertion is corroborated independently by the two biochemical papers above, but the original target record was not independently re-read.
- **PMID:32296183:** The abstract, HuRI generation/verification Results and Discussion were read. The BOLA3 supplementary rows are not in the cached body. The source aggregate names seven partners, not just GLRX5: GLRX3, KRT34, MAGEA6, INCA1, GLRX5, RAB17 and PICK1. Support for GLRX5 cannot resolve every other record; the aggregate remains undecided.
- **PMID:33961781:** The available cache contains Introduction and Discussion but no target-specific record. The BOLA3 source partner is FAAP100, not GLRX5. The cell context, orientation and target experiment remain unverified, so this IPI remains undecided.
- **PMID:34800366:** The abstract and initial Results describing quantitative mitochondrial mapping were read. These describe human-cell subtractive proteomics, spatial profiling and importomics. The specific BOLA3 supplementary measurement was not available; its curated mitochondrial annotation is retained consistently with independent biology, without claiming a re-read of that measurement.

The falcon report was read as secondary orientation and preserved unchanged. Its later mitoribosome and H96R discussion does not substitute for primary reading. The previous description conflated a [4Fe-4S] client route with mitoribosomal claims; that composite claim is omitted from the bounded replacement description. This is not evidence that later BOLA3 functions are false. No new source, provider report, or annotation assertion was manufactured.

## Annotation decisions and synthesis

The source IBA protein-maturation annotation at PANTHER:PTN001763701 is accepted: the role is biologically supported, and no target-specific loss or ancestral-placement defect was established. The mitochondrial matrix IBA and mitochondrial annotations are retained with the source-specific access limits above. No donor-count or self-source objection is introduced.

Direct binding of isolated BOLA3 to NFU1 is not an unqualified consensus finding. PMID:27532772 reports it by microscale thermophoresis. The Introduction of PMID:34063696 instead summarizes later experiments with no detected apo/holo BOLA3–NFU1 binding and notes that the interaction was unclear in vivo. That paragraph was independently read after ROOT identified the disagreement; the underlying cited studies were not newly accessed. The original NFU1 finding is recorded as DISPUTED, with explicit attribution to the later paper and no claim of definitive refutation. The source IPI remains non-core. Direct binding of isolated proteins and cluster-transfer-associated activity of the GLRX5–BOLA3 complex are kept distinct in the description, findings and suggested question.

The three corroborated generic-interaction annotations are retained as non-core. PMID:27532772 covers both processed GLRX5 and NFU1 records, which must both remain visible. Replacing these IPIs with metal-cluster binding would change the assertion rather than refine the protein interaction. The two unresolved screen-derived assertions are undecided, not removed or called over-annotated because of genericity.

The NAS iron-homeostasis and redox-homeostasis assertions remain undecided. Yeast Bol2 iron regulation, BOLA1 redox experiments, and BOLA3 binding to a glutaredoxin do not by themselves establish these specific BOLA3 processes. The audit does not claim that a human BOLA3 role is impossible.

Two existing authored proposals are retained: 2Fe-2S binding and membership of the GLRX5–BOLA3 Fe-S assembly complex. The immutable UniProt record lists ComplexPortal CPX-6863 and GO:1990229; its live Complex Portal page was inaccessible. Human protein assays independently support a heterocomplex, without a claim of a permanent ternary complex with NFU1.

The existing authored `NEW` proposal for GO:0044572 is withdrawn because it is a descendant of source GO:0016226 and therefore does not add nonredundant coverage. This withdraws an earlier proposal, not a GOA annotation. No new process term is proposed. The broad assembly process remains the single core biological-process term, avoiding an ancestor/descendant pair in the core.

The participation test is satisfied at the broad assembly level by BOLA3’s cluster-binding and transfer-associated contribution; it is not inferred solely from a knockout phenotype. LIAS performs lipoic-acid synthesis. BOLA3 helps mature Fe-S proteins and is not assigned the enzymatic step performed by LIAS.

## Ontology and comparator check

The existing local official OAK ontology snapshot defines GO:0051537 as binding a 2Fe-2S cluster, GO:0044572 as assembling a four-iron/four-sulfur cluster, and GO:1990229 as an Fe-S assembly protein complex. It confirms GO:0044572 is a subclass of GO:0016226 and GO:1990229 is a protein-containing complex. The indexed official [AmiGO assembly term](https://amigo.geneontology.org/amigo/term/GO%3A0044572) and [complex term](https://amigo.geneontology.org/amigo/term/GO%3A1990229) independently agree. Live QuickGO API access failed; that failure is not treated as a negative annotation result.

The four existing cached official UniProt records for GLRX5/Q86SX6, NFU1/Q9UMS0, ISCA1/Q9BUE6 and ISCA2/Q86U28 were inspected. All are entry snapshots dated 18 June 2025 and contain broad GO:0016226; none lists exact GO:0044572. This limited comparison does not prove a universal convention or current absence. It does show that an assertion that all analogous assembly factors carry the finer term was not established. The core synthesis therefore stays at the supported broad process, and the redundant `NEW` proposal is not retained as a purported missing annotation. No Q53S33/BOLA3 entry was found in the local GO-CAM index; that local result is not a claim about every current model.

The proposed review has 17 objects: all 15 source objects plus two retained authored proposals, with eight ACCEPT, three KEEP_AS_NON_CORE, four UNDECIDED and two NEW actions. It has one core unit and no REMOVE or MODIFY actions. The original third authored proposal is preserved in the audit record for review. This proposal awaits distinct scientific peer review before canonical application.
