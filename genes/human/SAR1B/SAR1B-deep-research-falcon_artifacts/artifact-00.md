| **Human identity and patient intestine** | Human **SAR1B**, formerly **SARA2**, UniProt **Q9Y6B6**, was identified as a Sar1/ARF-family small GTPase. Patient duodenal biopsies showed reduced SAR1B expression and lipid-laden enterocytes; increased SAR1A did not compensate. | Confirms the requested human protein identity and connects SAR1B deficiency to intestinal lipoprotein retention. Only three patients were studied, so the findings do not establish prevalence or universal genotype–phenotype relationships. (pqac-00000002, pqac-00000036, pqac-00000048) | Georges et al., 2011. *Orphanet Journal of Rare Diseases* 6:1. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1186/1750-1172-6-1) |
| **Purified human SAR1 paralogs; rat hepatoma secretion model** | Purified SAR1B recruited SEC23A or SEC23B to membranes approximately **fivefold more strongly** than SAR1A. SAR1A exchanged nucleotide faster, but GTP-hydrolysis activity was broadly similar in vitro. In SAR1B-deficient McArdle RH7777 rat hepatoma cells, SAR1B—but not SAR1A—restored secretion of APOB100-containing lipoproteins. | Direct biochemical evidence that small sequence differences affect COPII assembly and lipoprotein export. The secretion experiment used rat hepatoma cells rather than human enterocytes; faster nucleotide exchange must not be interpreted as faster GTP hydrolysis. (pqac-00000022, pqac-00000023, pqac-00000038) | Melville, Studer & Schekman, 2020. *Journal of Biological Chemistry* 295:8401–8412. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1074/jbc.RA120.012964) |
| **Human Caco-2/15 intestinal epithelial cells** | Complete SAR1B knockout retained approximately **40% of control triglyceride secretion, 57% of control APOB48 secretion, and 34.5% of control chylomicron output**. Combined SAR1A/SAR1B disruption produced near-complete inhibition. | Demonstrates a causal role in intestinal triglyceride-rich lipoprotein secretion while revealing partial paralog compensation. These are **percent-of-control outputs**, not percentage decreases; an immortalized cell line does not fully reproduce severe human disease. (pqac-00000027, pqac-00000035) | Sané et al., 2017. *Arteriosclerosis, Thrombosis, and Vascular Biology* 37:2243–2251. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1161/ATVBAHA.117.310121) |
| **Cell-free intestinal prechylomicron transport-vesicle assay** | Prechylomicron transport vesicles averaged approximately **250 nm**. Budding from intestinal ER membranes did not require GTP or COPII proteins, but Golgi docking did: SEC23 depletion reduced docking by **25%**, whereas SEC24C depletion nearly abolished it and recombinant SEC24C restored activity. | COPII/SAR1 involvement may be strongest at targeting or docking rather than strictly required for budding in this reconstituted system. The assay does not prove that all intact human enterocytes use wholly COPII-independent budding. (pqac-00000039) | Siddiqi, Siddiqi & Mansbach, 2010. *Journal of Lipid Research* 51:1093–1100. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1194/jlr.M002758) |
| **Heterozygous Sar1b mice under dietary challenge** | Five-week-old male and female **Sar1b** deletion or D137N heterozygotes received chow or high-fat diet for **8 weeks**. The models showed genotype- and sex-dependent changes in weight gain, adiposity, insulin resistance, plasma lipids, hepatic steatosis, and fatty-acid composition; male mutants on high-fat diet had lower hepatic triglycerides and lipid accumulation than controls. | Supports a role for Sar1b dosage in whole-body lipid handling and dietary response. These were heterozygous mice, many effects depended on sex and allele, and the study does not directly reproduce recessive human SAR1B deficiency. (pqac-00000040, pqac-00000044, pqac-00000046) | Auclair et al., 2023. *Journal of Lipid Research* 64:100423. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jlr.2023.100423) |
| **Conditional and coding-sequence-replacement mouse genetics** | Hepatocyte-specific Sar1b loss lowered plasma cholesterol to approximately **20 mg/dL versus 75 mg/dL** in controls. Adenoviral expression of either mouse SAR1A or SAR1B restored cholesterol to wild-type levels by day 3. Replacing endogenous Sar1b coding sequence with Sar1a rescued Sar1b-null perinatal lethality; homozygous replacement mice survived with normal growth, fertility, histology, and plasma cholesterol. | Strong in-vivo evidence for extensive biochemical overlap and transcriptional subfunctionalization of mouse paralogs. Mouse Sar1b-null lethality differs from viable human chylomicron-retention disease, so rescue by mouse SAR1A does not establish that SAR1A upregulation will treat patients. (pqac-00000014, pqac-00000015, pqac-00000056) | Tang et al., 2024. *Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences* 121:e2322164121. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2322164121) |
