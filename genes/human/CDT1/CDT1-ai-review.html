Gene Description

CDT1 is a nuclear replication licensing factor that recruits MCM2–7 to DNA with ORC and CDC6, enabling formation of pre-replicative complexes. Its DNA/chromatin contacts and MCM-binding domains support helicase loading; geminin binding and regulated turnover constrain licensing. CDT1 also restrains early-S-phase DNA synthesis through inhibition of CMG-dependent fork progression. During mitosis it binds microtubules and associates with Hec1/NDC80 at kinetochores to support stable spindle attachments. Human hypomorphic variants impair replication licensing and cause Meier-Gorlin syndrome.

Licensing, DNA contacts and disease alleles

CDT1 cooperates with ORC and CDC6 to load MCM2–7 onto DNA. Purified human reconstitution separates recruitment from high-salt-resistant loading, and omission of CDT1 disrupts loading. Full-length and later truncated constructs in the 2024 study are distinct preparations. This directly performed co-loader role supports the pre-RC assembly core; it does not give CDT1 an intrinsic ATPase or helicase activity. PMID:39604729(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39604729/), selected full Results/Fig.1 and expression/loading Methods.

The 2026 human reconstitution tests WT CDT1 and the MGS variants R462Q/E468K; R453W was not successfully purified for the assay. R210C is derived from a fly mutant and is not identified here as a human MGS allele. Recruitment/loading deficits are measured, whereas suggested interface/salt-bridge explanations are structural interpretations. [PMID:41448435 Molecular impacts of Meier-Gorlin syndrome mutations on human origin licensing, "MGS variants of CDT1 specifically reduce MCM recruitment"].

Direct DNA binding is grounded in mouse biochemical experiments and separately corroborated by human recombinant-protein DNA-cellulose assays. Mouse full-length 557-aa Cdt1 and mapped deletions were tested by DNA-cellulose and mobility shift; this does not establish sequence-specific origin recognition. Human 2026 experiments use tagged WT/mutant CDT1, with the DNA-binding control distinguished from cellular synthesis inhibition. PMID:12192004(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12192004/), indexed full primary Methods/Figs.2,4–6; PMID:41004347(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41004347/), full Materials/Methods and selected Results/Fig.4. PMID:17318181(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17318181/).

These are distinct branches, so this is a functional-stage refinement rather than a claimed direct-parent replacement. The original IEA/source is unchanged and the existing experimental pre-RC assertion is retained.

Inhibition of synthesis versus checkpoint signaling

CDT1 itself can restrain DNA synthesis. Human-cell experiments and purified human replication systems in PMID:36608667 distinguish this effect from checkpoint signaling and rereplication. CDT1 inhibits established forks and a minimal CMG/Pol-epsilon/RPA system, while Pol-epsilon-only primer extension is unaffected; MCM-contacting domains are required. The purified CDT1 preparation is 2×FLAG human protein from insect cells. ATR/WEE1 inhibition and CDC6-degron experiments provide separate cellular controls. These support an enzyme-inhibitor core and retention of the existing negative replication assertion. They do not provide a CMG-alone unwinding assay or a solved inhibitory structure; the paper explicitly leaves altered helicase–polymerase coupling as an alternative. PMID:36608667(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36608667/), selected full Results and STAR protein/replication Methods.

The additional human MCF-7 study uses engineered nondegradable, licensing-impaired and geminin-nonbinding CDT1 constructs, with MCM-contacting mutants as controls. EdU/flow-cytometry and binding experiments corroborate synthesis restraint. This engineered overexpression context is not substituted for a measurement of its unperturbed physiological magnitude. PMID:41004347(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41004347/).

The original 2009 source combines a human truncated CDT1–geminin structure, Xenopus licensing assays and separately described human U2OS overexpression-induced G2 arrest. Its supplementary Fig.S9 pixels and detailed SI Methods were not independently recovered; the explicit main Results description was read. The cell-cycle annotation remains contextual. New human licensing reconstitution shows inhibition with a CDT1–geminin heterotrimer, so an obligatory inhibitory hexamer is not asserted. This qualifies the older mechanism without denying binding or CDT1’s independently demonstrated fork-inhibitory function. PMID:19906994(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19906994/), indexed full main text; PMID:41365879(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41365879/), full selected Results/Figs.1–3.
