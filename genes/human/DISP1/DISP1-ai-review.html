Gene Description

DISP1 is a multipass plasma-membrane protein that releases lipid-modified Sonic hedgehog from producing cells. It binds the membrane-tethered ligand and cooperates with extracellular SCUBE acceptors to make SHH available for signaling to other cells. Proteolytic processing of its extracellular region facilitates efficient ligand binding and release. By supplying SHH to responding cells, DISP1 supports Hedgehog-dependent embryonic brain and midline craniofacial patterning. Human DISP1 variants are associated with a variably expressed spectrum of midline craniofacial malformations, including microforms of holoprosencephaly.

DISP1 evidence guide

This review distinguishes direct human experiments, ortholog experiments, curated propagation and candidate-gene observations. Source annotation identities and evidence codes are retained; the review actions describe their biological interpretation.

Core originals

PMID:34845226(https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8630017/), Results/Figures 1–4 and Methods on expression, purification and SHH release, directly assays human DISP1 and human SHH in human cells with mouse SCUBE2. Wild-type and engineered cleavage-site constructs support ligand release; transport-inactive NNN material supplies the principal structures. Direct protein recognition is supported, but SHH lipid termini were unresolved. The exact lipid route and transient SCUBE interface remain models, and a release defect for SHH-N81A is partly confounded by reduced cell-surface delivery. No stable DISP1–SCUBE–SHH complex is established.

PMID:34707294(https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8785653/), Results/Figures 2–4 and construct/release Methods, concerns mouse DISP1, including the engineered Disp1-A deletion. Sodium coordination and extracellular-gradient manipulation support coupling to SHH release. The structural SHH fragment is unlipidated mouse protein; functional release assays use lipid-modified ligand. These data support a mechanistic comparison, not a direct human sodium-flux measurement or a demonstrated exact transport stoichiometry.

PMID:22902404(https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3682496/), Results/Figures 1–5 and the main photocrosslinking/secretion Methods, distinguishes mouse DispA from its human SHH cargo and human host cells. Controlled secretion assays and sterol-dependent interactions support cooperation with SCUBE2. Some SCUBE-only release and nonspecific serum-mediated release occur, so efficient DISP1-dependent secretion is not an assertion of absolute dependence under every condition.

The human official sequence is 1524 residues. Experimental point mutants, artificial protease sites and terminal deletions are not natural isoforms. Protease-mediated maturation facilitates DISP1 function but does not make DISP1 a protease. Protein transporter activity describes ligand relocation from the cell surface; neither passage of the SHH protein through the bilayer nor a carrier that travels with its cargo is asserted.

Hedgehog pathway participation and human patterning

Ligand release is the performed DISP1 step in Hedgehog signaling, so the existing GO:0007224 assertion is retained as core alongside protein secretion. Its definition names the signals generated after Smoothened activation; curated pathway use also includes ligand-side participants. The valid PAINT assignment and the human release experiments above support this classification without assigning downstream Smoothened transduction to DISP1. Human SHH, UniProtKB:Q15465, is recorded as the cargo; the relevant transported species is the lipid-modified N-terminal signaling product. No separate positive-regulation or NEW process annotation is introduced.

PMID:19184110(https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2774849/), functional-analysis Methods, truncation Results and family descriptions, links human DISP1 truncations to microform craniofacial features. The clinically unaffected carrier mothers and normal CT/MRI in the affected daughters limit a simple severe-HPE interpretation. The functional assay is specifically murine Disp1 carrying human-equivalent alterations in Drosophila S2R+ cells; human wild-type DISP1 did not enhance export in that system.

PMID:38529886(https://doi.org/10.1016/j.gim.2024.101126) was assessed from its complete accessible abstract; the cohort findings are recorded once in the review reference entry. The full original, detailed pedigrees and functional assays were not independently read, so no uniform inheritance model or variant-specific pathogenicity judgment is assigned.

description: DISP1 is a multipass plasma-membrane protein that releases lipid-modified Sonic hedgehog
  from producing cells. It binds the membrane-tethered ligand and cooperates with extracellular SCUBE
  acceptors to make SHH available for signaling to other cells. Proteolytic processing of its extracellular
  region facilitates efficient ligand binding and release. By supplying SHH to responding cells, DISP1
  supports Hedgehog-dependent embryonic brain and midline craniofacial patterning. Human DISP1 variants
  are associated with a variably expressed spectrum of midline craniofacial malformations, including
  microforms of holoprosencephaly.
