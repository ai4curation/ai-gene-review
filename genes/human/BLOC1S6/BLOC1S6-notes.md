# BLOC1S6 / pallidin — research and annotation notes

## 2026-10-01: initial manual review

Human pallidin is a small coiled-coil BLOC-1 subunit with a cytoplasmic/peripheral membrane distribution. The strongest mechanistic evidence is its contribution to the BLOC-1 assembly and to endosomal delivery of membrane cargo. Human pallidin forms a stable subassembly with Cappuccino and BLOS1; human PaHA complementation of pallid mouse melanocytes restores pigmentation and Tyrp1 sorting. These findings support an assembly-mediated cargo-trafficking role, not an autonomous motor, membrane-spanning transporter or catalytic enzyme.

All 177 imported source annotations, including qualifiers and supporting entities, are retained. The three `alternative_products` are also retained; `products` is absent, and Q9UL45-3 remains **Not described**. No isoform-specific function or new GO annotation is proposed. There are 26 ACCEPT, 101 KEEP_AS_NON_CORE and 50 UNDECIDED decisions. The one core represents the BLOC-1 structural adaptor and lysosome-related-organelle cargo pathway.

### Assembly, binding and localization

- [PMID:10610180](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10610180/) describes the pallid mouse gene, platelet storage-pool deficiency, pigmentation and otolith phenotypes, and syntaxin-13 association by yeast two-hybrid and co-immunoprecipitation. The complete cached abstract was read; the complete article and every construct/species detail were not. This is positive target evidence for the existing syntaxin-binding and otolith annotations.
- [PMID:12019270](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12019270/) establishes pallidin-containing BLOC-1, a soluble/peripheral membrane distribution and self-association. Besides its complete cached abstract, selected indexed original Methods, Results and Discussion were read, including human constructs, HeLa localization and F-actin sedimentation. Cytosolic pallidin-containing material associates with F-actin, but the authors leave direct binding versus mediation by other proteins unresolved. The tagged overexpression localization preferentially marks perinuclear actin and does not establish a general actin-crosslinking mechanism. Actin associations are retained non-core. The stress-fiber, focal-adhesion and cell-contact assignments remain U pending their exact mouse assays and ancestral context, not because an uninspected donor is assumed wrong.
- [PMID:12191018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12191018/) and [PMID:15102850](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15102850/) independently establish human pallidin in a heterooligomer and the eight-component BLOC-1 assembly. Both canonical caches are abstract-only; pair-level supplementary matrices were not independently read.
- [PMID:22203680](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22203680/) reconstitutes a human octamer with one copy of each component and identifies a stable pallidin–Cappuccino–BLOS1 trimer. Selected Methods, Results and Discussion of the authenticated full-text variant were read separately; the canonical abstract cache was preserved. Isolated pallidin–BLOS1 and pallidin–Cappuccino pairs were tested. Whole-octamer AP-3 pull-down is distinguished from an isolated pallidin–AP-3 interface. The one-copy octamer does not refute self-association in isolated-protein assays; those self-binding and homodimer observations are retained non-core.
- The normal [Reactome R-HSA-429815](https://reactome.org/content/detail/R-HSA-429815) summary describes BLOC-1 formation. The cytosolic annotation is also grounded in pallidin fractionation, not inferred from the event title alone.

Generic protein-binding rows retain their exact experimental source and partner identifiers. The 69 generic IPI annotations are non-core physical associations, with different evidence limits for small-scale assembly studies, binary screens and AP-MS. An interaction does not transfer the partner's function to pallidin, and AP-MS does not necessarily establish direct binary contact. The user-defined action rules take precedence over a blanket policy to remove generic binding. No experimental association is rejected merely because its cache is abstract-only.

### Cargo sorting, pigment and neuronal context

[PMID:17182842](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17182842/) directly uses human PaHA to complement pallid mouse melanocytes. Selected authenticated full-text Methods, Results and Discussion establish restoration of pigmentation and Tyrp1 routing from early endosomes to melanosomes. The original canonical cache remains abstract-only; this distinction is retained in `reference_review`. The inspected body separately maps MuHA-reconstituted BLOC-1 to tubulovesicular endosomes. No pallidin-specific supplementary localization image was independently inspected. Thus the precise transport-vesicle localization remains U, despite strong evidence for membership of the functioning complex.

Whole-melanosome movement (GO:0032402) and melanosome positioning are different from Tyrp1 cargo delivery (GO:0035646). The former annotations remain U; the latter are accepted. Likewise, restoration of pigment in an established melanocyte line does not by itself resolve positive regulation of pigment-cell differentiation. These are evidence limits, not declarations that experimental annotations are erroneous.

The [2017 correction, PMID:28137951](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5341732/), invalidates the original transferrin-FITC control. Repeat experiments using corrected reagent show a mild early recycling defect in the mutant contexts. The review does not repeat the original claim of unchanged transferrin recycling or generalize the control failure to all Tyrp1 experiments. The notice was read through official primary content; figure images and supplementary datasets were not independently assessed.

[PMID:19546860](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19546860/) directly tests primary pallid mouse hippocampal neurons, despite its title foregrounding dysbindin. Selected complete relevant Methods, Results and Discussion show modest neurite-number/length changes. Native brain BLOC-1 detected by anti-pallidin associates selectively with SNAP25 isoforms and open syntaxin conformations; those assays are not isolated-pallidin contact maps.

[PMID:21998198](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21998198/) includes actual pallid fibroblasts and dentate-gyrus cargo measurements, plus human SH-SY5Y complexes containing endogenous pallidin. Selected Results and Methods were read. Other experiments also use dysbindin, muted or AP-3 mutants. The neuronal cargo delivery annotations are retained non-core without a motor claim. A preparation of small intracellular vesicles from homogenized PC12 cells does not establish localization to extracellular microvesicles (GO:1990742); that transferred location remains U.

For the fly-grounded IBA annotations, independently inspected primary [PMID:28317021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5356223/) describes Pldn-mutant high-activity recycling-pool defects and endosomal changes, with relatively preserved basal transmission. The selected text also places the donor protein in postsynaptic muscle as well as neuronal structures. This supports non-core retention of synaptic-vesicle recycling and neuromuscular postsynapse context. It is not a claim of direct human localization, full figure coverage, or reconstruction of the complete PAINT tree. Target self-entries in other IBA donor sets are legitimate descendant experimental grounding, not circularity; donor count is not used to judge validity.

### Mouse metabolic, immune and unresolved donor effects

ROOT's bounded independent consultation identified and read the full official abstract and selected indexed Methods, Results and Table 1 of [PMID:28701731 / PMC5507893](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5507893/). It directly compares pallid and control postnatal day 1–2 mouse hippocampi and reports 28 changed metabolites, including amino acids, adenosine and phospholipid derivatives. Rows 87–91 and 150 are retained non-core as downstream consequences. This does not identify pallidin as an amino-acid, nucleoside or lipid enzyme. The exact current donor GO-row/reference edges were not recovered, so this study is explicitly corroborating evidence, not an asserted reconstruction of every transfer.

Science's separate seven-row consultation read direct pallid-mouse neutrophil experiments in [PMID:17785837](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17785837/), including selected Methods, Results and Figure 3 legend. Deficient inflammatory neutrophils release less elastase and provide less support for parasite control. Rows 92/93 are retained non-core. ROOT's independent whole-review check identified that the measured elastase release also supplies positive evidence for row 101, protein secretion; this existing assertion is likewise retained non-core. Pallidin is not the elastase, TLR4 or oxidant-generating enzyme used elsewhere in the paper. The exact current mouse annotation-source edges remain unresolved. No full figure-image or supplement claim is made.

Positive redox evidence is acknowledged: selected indexed Results and Figure 7 caption in [PMID:22423091 / PMC3313842](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3313842/) describe increased basal oxidant signal and decreased peroxiredoxins after pallidin shRNA in human SH-SY5Y cells. This is not automatically a demonstrated *response to oxidative stress*, so row 94 remains U rather than denying the phenotype or assigning peroxidase activity.

A [2005 primary Regensburg thesis](https://epub.uni-regensburg.de/10341/1/Salim%20Maa%20Bared_Dr_Arbeit.pdf), selected Results pages 87–88 and Figure 40 caption, reports pooled sera from four pallidin-knockout and four control mice with altered HDL lipids and VLDL triglycerides. Lipid composition, homeostatic maintenance and lipoprotein-particle numbers are distinct claims. Rows 162/166/171 remain U because the exact current donor sources and adequate process-level interpretation remain unresolved, not because there is no positive lipid evidence. Proposed LCAT or secretion explanations are not pallidin catalytic results. The consultant's attempted screenshot failed; no figure-image reading is claimed.

The recovered [MGI Bloc1s6 graph](https://www.informatics.jax.org/marker/gograph/MGI%3A1927580) is explicitly generated in 2023. It and the current summary identify the legitimate mouse Bloc1s6/Pldn donor. Its missing rows do not prove current annotation absence, a wrong donor, or confusion with another protein. Spatial/developmental/behavioral assignments whose exact assay remains inaccessible are left U, including ER lumen. Cytosolic and peripheral membrane localization do not independently establish an intraluminal pool or rule out every additional pool.
