| **Catalytic hydrolysis** | Human FUCA1 is an EC 3.2.1.51 GH29 α-L-fucosidase that releases terminal α-L-fucose from glycoconjugates. Literature supports cleavage of α1,2-, α1,3-, α1,4-, and α1,6-linked substrates; recombinant human FUCA1 hydrolyzed 4-MUF and pNPF in mammalian-cell assays. (pqac-00000012, pqac-00000013) | These data establish broad linkage capability, **not a quantitative kinetic ranking** among natural linkages. FUCA2 is a separate, lysosome-targeted homolog but lacked detectable activity toward these standard pseudo-substrates. Bäumges et al., 2025: [10.3390/cells14171355](https://doi.org/10.3390/cells14171355). (pqac-00000011, pqac-00000013) |
| **Lysosomal localization and M6P targeting** | FUCA1 partially colocalized with LAMP1; endogenous FUCA1 was detected in lysosome-enriched human-cell fractions. GNPTAB knockout reduced lysosomal FUCA1 and increased missorting/secretion, directly supporting mannose-6-phosphate-dependent targeting. (pqac-00000014, pqac-00000017) | FUCA1 is synthesized through the secretory pathway as an N-glycosylated precursor, so ER, Golgi, endosomal, extracellular, or other pools can occur. FUCA2 is also M6P-dependent and lysosomal—not exclusively secreted. Bäumges et al., 2025: [10.3390/cells14171355](https://doi.org/10.3390/cells14171355). (pqac-00000011, pqac-00000014) |
| **Storage product and human genetics** | FUCA1 deficiency causes fucosidosis, with accumulation of fucosylated glycoconjugates—particularly **Fuc(α1,6)GlcNAcβ1-Asn**. Sequencing of three Tunisian patients identified four novel variants: p.K57Sfs*75, p.F77Sfs*55, p.G332E, and c.662+5G>C; p.G332E was modeled near the catalytic region. (pqac-00000012, pqac-00000043) | Accumulation in genetically deficient humans is strong evidence for the physiological lysosomal substrate class, while the modeled effect of p.G332E is inferential rather than a purified-mutant kinetic result. Chkioua et al., 2021: [10.1186/s12920-021-01061-3](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12920-021-01061-3); Bäumges et al., 2025: [10.3390/cells14171355](https://doi.org/10.3390/cells14171355). |
| **p53–FUCA1–EGFR signaling** | A p53-responsive element in FUCA1 intron 1 drove p53-dependent reporter activation. Ectopic FUCA1 reduced α1,6-fucosylation of immunoprecipitated EGFR and attenuated EGF-induced Akt phosphorylation; inactive fucosidosis variants did not reproduce wild-type enzyme activity. (pqac-00000023, pqac-00000024, pqac-00000045) | This is a proposed context-dependent, partly extra-lysosomal signaling function based mainly on cultured-cell overexpression and lectin assays; it does not supersede the established lysosomal catabolic role, and the precise EGFR glycosite was not identified. Ezawa et al., 2016: [10.1111/cas.12933](https://doi.org/10.1111/cas.12933). |
