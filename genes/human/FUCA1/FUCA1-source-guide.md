# FUCA1 source guide

Human FUCA1 (P04066; HGNC:4006) is a 466-residue tissue alpha-L-fucosidase. Its 31-residue signal peptide and mature chain are processing features. The common Arg281/Gln281 alleles and experimental residues 6–466 construct are not alternate splice products.

## Catalytic and substrate evidence

[PMID:19666478](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19666478/) explicitly characterizes human FUCA1 alongside FUCA2. Its infection-associated secretion and adhesion conclusions concern FUCA2. [PMID:4074382](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/4074382/) supplies human fibroblast activity/processing evidence through the accessible abstract; it does not establish a cytosolic enzyme.

[PMID:9741689](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9741689/) separates bovine kidney purified-enzyme assays from human fibroblast glycolipid turnover. The existing ISS donor Q2KIM0 is bovine FUCA1 and has an IDA annotation to this paper. NAGA acts before efficient defucosylation of the tested linear intermediate. FUCA1 is not described as efficiently hydrolysing every branched blood-group substrate.

[PMID:27491075](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27491075/) is a mouse knockout study. Its storage data support conserved core-defucosylation biology; AGA steric inhibition is cited background. The inherited human IMP/NEW row is archived rather than repeated as a human experiment. The existing core retains glycoprotein catabolism with species scope explicit. Comparator queries found human MAN2B1 and GLB1 annotations to this process; FUCA1 catalyses a step rather than merely being a substrate. No local GO-CAM index hit was found for P04066/FUCA1.

## Actual propagation and ontology

All three IBA rows follow the actual PTHR10030 AN0/PTN000003123 ancestor to human AN252/PTN002467369, whose sequence exactly matches P04066. Self-donor evidence is legitimate grounding. A fly NOT/IRD is outside that path; two other-species sequence discrepancies are retained explicitly. No human loss is inferred from them.

Fucose metabolism reaches carbohydrate metabolism through hexose and monosaccharide metabolism, all by is_a. The broad carbohydrate metabolic process annotation (GO:0005975; IEA; GO_REF:0000002) is TOO_GENERAL; its former glycoprotein replacement is not retained. Lysosomal lumen part_of lysosome supports the location refinement; the longer path to cytoplasm also contains part_of. Glycoprotein, glycoside and glycolipid catabolism are not treated as ancestors of one another.

## Contextual associations and locations

The exact [HuRI](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32296183/) records support three CIB1, three MED10 and five UBQLN2 two-hybrid records using FUCA1 residues 6–466. Positive assay associations remain non-core; no native lysosomal complex or orthogonal mammalian validation is inferred.

The original [lysosomal reaction](https://reactome.org/content/detail/R-HSA-5693807) identifies FUCA1 tetramer as catalyst. The [granule event](https://reactome.org/content/detail/R-HSA-6798751) contains exact human P04066 input/output members, distinct from FUCA2. These establish modeled cargo locations, not an exocytosis role. Human lymphoid-cell secretion in [PMID:1873910](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1873910/) remains culture-specific.

Original [PMID:19056867](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19056867/) Table 1 lists FUCA1 with Pep 1 and ID 1. Retain its original urine extension and non-core detection. The recovered original supplementary Table 2 of [PMID:23533145](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23533145/) identifies P04066/FUCA1 at row 197 of SI Table 2, separate from FUCA2, with five unique peptides in each of four analysis columns. These are duplicate analyses of two pooled clinical groups. Retain the existing EPS-urine exosome annotation as non-core preparation-level detection. The article's soluble-protein/contaminant caveat limits interpretation: cell of origin, vesicle topology, disease-specific expression and a role in exosome biogenesis are not established. The exact workbook was recovered through the official [BioStudies article record](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/biostudies/studies/S-EPMC3773505); its target cells and headers were independently replayed.
