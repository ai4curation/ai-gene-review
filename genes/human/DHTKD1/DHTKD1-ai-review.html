Gene Description

DHTKD1 is the thiamine diphosphate-dependent E1 component of the mitochondrial oxoadipate dehydrogenase complex. It decarboxylates 2-oxoadipate and transfers the resulting glutaryl intermediate to the lipoyl group on DLST. DLST then transfers glutaryl to CoA, and DLD reoxidizes the reduced lipoyl group. This sequence participates in the convergent lysine, hydroxylysine and tryptophan degradation pathway. DHTKD1 forms a functional homodimer and strongly prefers 2-oxoadipate over 2-oxoglutarate, although lower oxoglutarate activity and association with OGDH-containing assemblies have been demonstrated. Loss of DHTKD1 activity can cause accumulation of 2-aminoadipate and 2-oxoadipate.

Core Functions

DHTKD1 supplies the ThDP-dependent E1 reaction of mitochondrial oxoadipate dehydrogenase: decarboxylation of 2-oxoadipate and reductive transfer of its glutaryl intermediate to the lipoyl group on DLST. This catalytic step participates in the convergent lysine and tryptophan catabolic pathway. DLST and DLD carry out the subsequent CoA transfer and lipoamide reoxidation; cofactor binding and homodimer formation are features of this one catalytic core.

DHTKD1 (human UniProt Q96HY7, HGNC:23537) supplies the E1 reaction of the mitochondrial oxoadipate dehydrogenase complex. Its ThDP-dependent decarboxylation and transfer to DLST-bound lipoamide form one catalytic function. DLST subsequently transfers glutaryl to CoA, and DLD reoxidizes the lipoyl group. The official product is 919 residues; no alternative-product set is supplied in the reviewed UniProt record.

Localization and Reactome roles

PMID:23141294(https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3516600/) Results/Figure S3 reports endogenous DHTKD1 colocalization with MitoTracker in human HepG2 cells. The separately recovered original is more extensive than the unchanged abstract-only cache. HPA reports supported mitochondrial localization with HPA037949 in A-431, MCF7 and U2OS cells. These fluorescence observations resolve mitochondria, not the matrix.

The original MitoCoP study, PMID:34800366 combines mitochondrial fractionation, spatial proteomics, importomics and curated evidence. Its original methods and Table S1 were read. Sheet (A), row 305 identifies Q96HY7 as the majority and simplified protein ID and marks experimental identification and MitoCoP inclusion. It scores Class 1 in subtractive proteomics (85.2% mean sequence coverage), Cluster 1 in spatial proteomics, and Class 1 in importomics; sheet (B), row 278 confirms DHTKD1 in the gene list. The importomics entry is a TOMM40-depletion response, not a purified DHTKD1 import assay. The membrane-association category is Ambiguous. The matrix entry is a compiled GO annotation, so it is not used as independent direct matrix localization. A fresh download failed, but a previously authenticated copy of the same publisher supplement supplied the actual table evidence.

The source mouse ortholog A2ATU0 carries a curator-assigned matrix IDA and amino-acid/lysine IMP from PMID:28545977(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28545977/). The abstract describes mouse Dhtkd1/Gcdh perturbation; the full localization assay was not recovered. The annotation is therefore recognized as experimental donor grounding without claiming a personally verified mouse localization experiment. It is not a transfer chain consisting solely of human-derived similarity statements.

Human Reactome supplies an explicit target-specific matrix path:

  • DHTKD1 protein R-HSA-6792669 identifies Q96HY7 and its matrix compartment. It belongs to DHTKD1:ThDP:Mg2+ R-HSA-9858584, within DHTKD1 dimer R-HSA-9858579 and OADH complex R-HSA-9858580.
  • E1 event R-HSA-9858321 uses catalyst 9853486, whose active unit is the DHTKD1 dimer.
  • DLD event R-HSA-9858589 uses catalyst 9858592; its active unit is DLD, while its physical entity includes the DHTKD1-containing OADH complex.
  • DLST event R-HSA-9858590 uses catalyst 9858591; its active unit is DLST within that complex.

Thus the DLD- and DLST-associated rows support location through complex membership, not transfer of their catalytic activities. The more precise matrix replacements use this human curated evidence and the mouse experimental annotation together; they are not presented as finer resolution of the original human fluorescence or HTP assays.

Energy phenotype and review conventions

PMID:23141294(https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3516600/) Results/Figure 4 reports reduced ATP and NAD(H) after DHTKD1 silencing in HEK293T cells. Those pool measurements do not identify a direct reaction. Independent support for GO:0006091 comes from DHTKD1 performing the E1 step in a precursor- and redox-generating sequence: it decarboxylates 2-oxoadipate and reductively glutarylates DLST-bound lipoamide. The PMID:32633484 publisher supplement, S7–S8, separately assays isolated human E1 through DCPIP reduction and NADH production by reconstituted DHTKD1/DLST/DLD. The overall OADH event and the individual E1, DLST and DLD events above preserve the same role separation: DLST transfers glutaryl to CoA and DLD reduces NAD+. Retention is therefore grounded in a performed catalytic step, not knockdown necessity alone. No autonomous DHTKD1 NADH production or ATP synthesis is asserted. The broad annotation remains contextual to the specific catabolic core.

The project curation instructions govern the non-core retention of positive generic interactions. Supported specificity changes use MODIFY even when a specific term is already represented by another source. Original GOA identities, evidence codes and qualifiers remain intact. Shared PANTHER and pending module content were not replaced. This guide distinguishes public originals from unchanged abstract-only publication caches; cache availability is not treated as the boundary of actual original access.
