# DHTKD1 evidence and source guide

DHTKD1 (human UniProt Q96HY7, HGNC:23537) supplies the E1 reaction of the mitochondrial oxoadipate dehydrogenase complex. Its ThDP-dependent decarboxylation and transfer to DLST-bound lipoamide form one catalytic function. DLST subsequently transfers glutaryl to CoA, and DLD reoxidizes the lipoyl group. The official product is 919 residues; no alternative-product set is supplied in the reviewed UniProt record.

The current [GO:0160166](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/QuickGO/term/GO:0160166) definition is the specific E1 reaction and is a child of [GO:0016624](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/QuickGO/term/GO:0016624). Consequently, the broad oxidoreductase annotation is valid but merits refinement; the earlier claim that it applies only to the complete complex was incorrect. ThDP binding is retained as a mechanism-associated annotation within the same core.

The lysine and tryptophan replacements of the existing amino-acid-catabolism row describe DHTKD1's own catalytic participation. Row 8 already includes [GO:0006569](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/QuickGO/term/GO:0006569) as a supported replacement of the broad term, and the same pathway role is retained in the catalytic core. The separate historical NEW object is retired as duplicate coverage, with its source identity preserved. This is deduplication, not withdrawal of tryptophan-pathway convergence. No new process is proposed, no comparator-absence argument is used, and no target activity was found in the fixed GO-CAM index.

## Localization and Reactome roles

[PMID:23141294](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3516600/) Results/Figure S3 reports endogenous DHTKD1 colocalization with MitoTracker in human HepG2 cells. The separately recovered original is more extensive than the unchanged abstract-only cache. [HPA](https://www.proteinatlas.org/ENSG00000181192-DHTKD1/subcellular) reports supported mitochondrial localization with HPA037949 in A-431, MCF7 and U2OS cells. These fluorescence observations resolve mitochondria, not the matrix.

The original [MitoCoP study, PMID:34800366](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8664129/) combines mitochondrial fractionation, spatial proteomics, importomics and curated evidence. Its original methods and [Table S1](https://ars.els-cdn.com/content/image/1-s2.0-S1550413121005295-mmc2.xlsx) were read. Sheet (A), row 305 identifies Q96HY7 as the majority and simplified protein ID and marks experimental identification and MitoCoP inclusion. It scores Class 1 in subtractive proteomics (85.2% mean sequence coverage), Cluster 1 in spatial proteomics, and Class 1 in importomics; sheet (B), row 278 confirms DHTKD1 in the gene list. The importomics entry is a TOMM40-depletion response, not a purified DHTKD1 import assay. The membrane-association category is Ambiguous. The matrix entry is a compiled GO annotation, so it is not used as independent direct matrix localization. A fresh download failed, but a previously authenticated copy of the same publisher supplement supplied the actual table evidence.

The source mouse ortholog [A2ATU0](https://www.uniprot.org/uniprotkb/A2ATU0/entry) carries a curator-assigned matrix IDA and amino-acid/lysine IMP from [PMID:28545977](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28545977/). The abstract describes mouse Dhtkd1/Gcdh perturbation; the full localization assay was not recovered. The annotation is therefore recognized as experimental donor grounding without claiming a personally verified mouse localization experiment. It is not a transfer chain consisting solely of human-derived similarity statements.

Human Reactome supplies an explicit target-specific matrix path:

- [DHTKD1 protein R-HSA-6792669](https://reactome.org/content/detail/R-HSA-6792669) identifies Q96HY7 and its matrix compartment. It belongs to [DHTKD1:ThDP:Mg2+ R-HSA-9858584](https://reactome.org/content/detail/R-HSA-9858584), within [DHTKD1 dimer R-HSA-9858579](https://reactome.org/content/detail/R-HSA-9858579) and [OADH complex R-HSA-9858580](https://reactome.org/content/detail/R-HSA-9858580).
- [E1 event R-HSA-9858321](https://reactome.org/content/detail/R-HSA-9858321) uses [catalyst 9853486](https://reactome.org/ContentService/data/query/9853486), whose active unit is the DHTKD1 dimer.
- [DLD event R-HSA-9858589](https://reactome.org/content/detail/R-HSA-9858589) uses [catalyst 9858592](https://reactome.org/ContentService/data/query/9858592); its active unit is DLD, while its physical entity includes the DHTKD1-containing OADH complex.
- [DLST event R-HSA-9858590](https://reactome.org/content/detail/R-HSA-9858590) uses [catalyst 9858591](https://reactome.org/ContentService/data/query/9858591); its active unit is DLST within that complex.

Thus the DLD- and DLST-associated rows support **location through complex membership**, not transfer of their catalytic activities. The more precise matrix replacements use this human curated evidence and the mouse experimental annotation together; they are not presented as finer resolution of the original human fluorescence or HTP assays.

[GO:0140297](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/QuickGO/term/GO:0140297) describes binding to a DNA-binding transcription factor. The official [BMAL1 O00327 record](https://www.uniprot.org/uniprotkb/O00327/entry) describes sequence-specific E-box recognition and transcriptional activation; its isoform-8 deletion at canonical residue 274 leaves the bHLH domain at residues 72–125 intact. Together with the positive [validated pair EBI-24412093](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/intact/interaction/EBI-24412093), this supports a known partner-class refinement as qualitative tagged-Y2H binding capacity. The term does not require DHTKD1 to regulate transcription or circadian timing. HuRI Results describes quadruplicate retesting and sequence confirmation, while orthogonal validation is study-wide, not established for this individual pair. Native colocalization, endogenous complex formation and affinity remain unresolved. PGLYRP3 lacks an established applicable finer class here; its non-core treatment does not rank human-cell AP-MS below Y2H.

## Energy phenotype and review conventions

[PMID:23141294](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3516600/) Results/Figure 4 reports reduced ATP and NAD(H) after DHTKD1 silencing in HEK293T cells. Those pool measurements do not identify a direct reaction. Independent support for [GO:0006091](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/QuickGO/term/GO:0006091) comes from DHTKD1 performing the E1 step in a precursor- and redox-generating sequence: it decarboxylates 2-oxoadipate and reductively glutarylates DLST-bound lipoamide. The [PMID:32633484 publisher supplement](https://acs.figshare.com/articles/journal_contribution/Inhibition_and_Crystal_Structure_of_the_Human_DHTKD1-Thiamin_Diphosphate_Complex/12636756), S7–S8, separately assays isolated human E1 through DCPIP reduction and NADH production by reconstituted DHTKD1/DLST/DLD. The [overall OADH event](https://reactome.org/content/detail/R-HSA-9858328) and the individual E1, DLST and DLD events above preserve the same role separation: DLST transfers glutaryl to CoA and DLD reduces NAD+. Retention is therefore grounded in a performed catalytic step, not knockdown necessity alone. No autonomous DHTKD1 NADH production or ATP synthesis is asserted. The broad annotation remains contextual to the specific catabolic core.

Supported specificity changes use MODIFY even when a specific term is already represented by another source. Original GOA identities, evidence codes and qualifiers remain intact. Shared PANTHER and pending module content were not replaced.
