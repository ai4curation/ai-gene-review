Gene Description

CHRNG encodes the gamma subunit of the fetal skeletal-muscle nicotinic acetylcholine receptor. Gamma assembles with two alpha1 subunits, beta1 and delta to form a postsynaptic cation channel that couples acetylcholine release to muscle excitation and prenatal movement. Gamma contributes to the pore, gating architecture and the complementary side of an acetylcholine-binding interface; fetal receptors have greater agonist sensitivity and longer openings than adult receptors in which epsilon replaces gamma. The human gamma-containing receptor conducts calcium as a small fraction of its inward current. Biallelic loss-of-function variants cause fetal akinesia and lethal or nonlethal multiple pterygium phenotypes. The developmental replacement by epsilon explains why surviving individuals may lack ongoing postnatal myasthenia. Functional effects of the alternative product replacing residues 117–169 with lysine remain unresolved.
