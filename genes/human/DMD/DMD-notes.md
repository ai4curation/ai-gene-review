# DMD biological and evidence notes

Long dystrophin connects cytoskeletal networks to the sarcolemmal dystrophin-associated glycoprotein complex (DGC) and helps organize membrane-associated proteins. Human dystrophin fragment binding to a rabbit beta-dystroglycan tail and direct keratin-19 binding support complementary sides of this linkage [PMID:7592992](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7592992/), [PMID:16000376](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16000376/). The compact core describes this integrated anchor and organization mechanism without assigning one core to every recovered partner.

The historical human WW/EF-like fragment bound to a dystroglycan peptide is distinct from newer native complexes. Native rabbit and mouse cryo-EM structures place beta-dystroglycan near the dystrophin ZZ region and include additional cytoplasmic partners; unresolved flexible regions and fitted models limit atomic claims. Neither native ortholog structure measures all human natural products [PMID:10932245](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10932245/), [PMID:39663457](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39663457/), [PMID:39663450](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39663450/). The human 1DXX N-terminal construct is an apo 246-residue fragment carrying C10S/C188S relative to current canonical sequence, not a full-length wild-type actin-bound complex [PMID:10801490](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10801490/).

Seventeen official human sequences were reconstructed independently. Accessions and displayed product names are not ordinal equivalents. Long Dp427 products contain the N-terminal actin-binding region; Dp260, Dp140, Dp116, Dp71-related and Dp40 products have different retained regions. Sequence shortening alone establishes neither equivalence nor inactivity. Historical molecular-mass bands, splice names and engineered deletions cannot be assigned indiscriminately to current accessions. Controlled human mitochondrial fractions support a Dp71-related pool but do not resolve a matrix-facing inner-membrane location or every natural product [PMID:34575126](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34575126/).

The source of a localization observation is retained when selecting a more precise term. Rat brain postsynaptic-density fractionation supports a PSD pool, not a synaptic-vesicle pool; the original journal pages are 11642–11644 [PMID:1454857](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1454857/). Glial material can complicate brain PSD interpretation [PMID:10545507](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10545507/). Unavailable platelet originals leave specific filopodium and binding assertions unresolved rather than disproved [PMID:16803572](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16803572/).

Targeted mouse transgene experiments restore and co-isolate dystrobrevin/syntrophin assemblies with dystrophin lacking exons 71–74. This supports performed organization of the DGC, while the exact stoichiometric arrangement remains a model [PMID:9214383](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9214383/). In contrast, RyR/calstabin destabilization and its pharmacological rescue demonstrate downstream calcium-handling consequences of dystrophin deficiency, not a direct DMD-catalyzed assembly reaction [PMID:19198614](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19198614/), [PMID:20080623](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20080623/). Cardiac sodium-current density and additional utrophin-dependent gating effects are distinguished [PMID:21677768](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21677768/).

nNOS association has both direct-fragment and mediated-recruitment evidence. A purified R16–17/nNOS-PDZ BLI assay reports an equilibrium dissociation constant near 50 micromolar; kinetics are multiphasic and atomic contacts are modeled [PMID:26378238](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4705953/). Human repeat peptides capture syntrophins from mouse muscle, and microdystrophin restores nNOS at the mouse sarcolemma only in the presence of alpha-syntrophin [PMID:29790927](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6097012/). The latter study does not repeat the BLI assay and therefore does not directly invalidate it. Human atrial co-immunoprecipitation separately establishes native association [PMID:27225184](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27225184/). The unresolved question is how much direct contact contributes within the native human complex. Kir-channel-tail experiments attribute their PDZ recognition to syntrophin, so they do not independently establish DMD–PDZ recognition [PMID:15024025](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15024025/).
