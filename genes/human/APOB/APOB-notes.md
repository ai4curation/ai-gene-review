## Compartments and process participation

Reactome represents apoB-containing particles during extracellular circulation, receptor engagement, internalization and degradation. An apoB molecule can be present in an endosomal or lysosomal lumen as transported cargo without carrying out the receptor's uptake machinery or the lysosomal hydrolase's reaction. Conversely, apoB's physical lipid-binding scaffold contributes directly to particle assembly and lipid transport; it is not merely an inert substrate in those processes.

The LDLR and chylomicron-remnant summaries specify different recognition routes: apoB100 binds LDLR, whereas remnant uptake can use apoE on apoB48-containing particles. MTP lipidates nascent apoB in the ER, LPL hydrolyzes particle triglyceride, CETP transfers neutral lipid between particles, and LIPA hydrolyzes internalized ester. None of those activities is assigned to apoB simply from membership in the reaction. The late coated-vesicle/uncoating summaries require source-specific care: a general AP2/HSPA8 event does not by itself resolve an APOB plasma-membrane location at that stage. The two coarse cytosol summaries involving chylomicron secretion or retinyl-ester packaging likewise do not establish which side of a membrane the apoB polypeptide occupies.

For the foam-cell and lipid-storage annotations, the available abstracts describe macrophages exposed to LDL, enzymatically modified LDL or infection plus LDL. They do not yet distinguish apoB-specific regulatory work from provision of a lipid-rich particle. These experimental assertions will remain unresolved where the relevant full assay cannot be checked; the evidence is not rejected because lipid delivery is necessary for the outcome. No new biological-process assertion is proposed at this stage.
