CC   -!- FUNCTION: High-affinity sodium-dicarboxylate cotransporter that accepts
CC       a range of substrates with 4-6 carbon atoms, such as the citric acid
CC       cycle intermediates succinate and alpha-ketoglutarate (2-oxoglutarate),
CC       as well as other compounds including N-acetyl-L-aspartate
CC       (PubMed:10794676, PubMed:10992006, PubMed:15561973, PubMed:17356845,
CC       PubMed:17426067, PubMed:24247155, PubMed:30635937, PubMed:39622972).
CC       Transports the dicarboxylate into the cell with a probable
CC       stoichiometry of 3 Na(+) for 1 divalent dicarboxylate, rendering the
CC       process electrogenic (PubMed:10794676, PubMed:10992006). Can transport
CC       citrate in a Na(+)-dependent manner, recognizing the divalent form of
CC       citrate rather than the trivalent form which is normally found in blood
CC       (PubMed:10794676). Imports itaconate in hepatocytes leading to
CC       activation of TFEB-dependent lysosomal biogenesis involved in
CC       antibacterial innate immune response (PubMed:39116875).
CC       {ECO:0000269|PubMed:10794676, ECO:0000269|PubMed:10992006,
