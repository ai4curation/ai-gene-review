# ENPP1 review notes

ENPP1 is reviewed as an extracellular nucleotide-hydrolyzing enzyme. The most clearly established reactions are ATP-to-AMP/PPi hydrolysis and cGAMP destruction. ATP-derived PPi regulates mineral deposition; the immune effects of cGAMP hydrolysis are a separate substrate consequence. Human cell experiments, mouse genetics, purified mouse extracellular protein and engineered commercial reagents provide complementary evidence with distinct limits. Exact original URLs, sections, figures and access limits are recorded in [the source guide](ENPP1-source-guide.md).

## Annotation decisions

All 97 imported annotation contexts have individual decisions. Imported terms, evidence codes, citations, supporting entities and flags remain unchanged. Supported generic protein interactions are retained as non-core; no new annotation or publication quotation is added.

The broad membrane, hydrolase and metal-binding rows are refined to existing supported specific locations, reactions and metals. Cell counts with unchanged viability support population-proliferation regulation, rather than individual-cell-size growth. Bone rescue supports mineralization regulation rather than direct mineral deposition. Phosphomonoesterase and nucleotide-diphosphatase chemistry are distinguished. The intracellular and systemic phosphate-homeostasis source claims are assessed against the papers' actual PPi versus Pi measurements; these findings do not exclude ENPP1 effects on phosphate physiology in other settings.

PPi generation is distinguished from directed PPi movement. Read mouse donor originals measure uptake of administered PPi, genetic effects on its abundance, or rescue by supplementation, without establishing ENPP1 as the transport agent. This is a process-scope judgment, not an inference from donor count or a claim that PAINT placed the ancestral node incorrectly. The unread human transport paper remains explicitly unresolved.

Uncertainty remains for exact oligonucleotide cleavage, dinucleotide-pyrophosphate cleavage, cyclic-AMP hydrolysis, PAPS claims, selected source-specific substrate assays, lysosomal enrichment, melanocyte differentiation and the developmental scope of a rule-derived organ annotation. The cGAMP reaction does not independently establish dinucleotide phosphatase under its P–O–P-bond ancestry. ENPP1 regulation can contribute to a developmental process; lack of a role as developmental machinery is not a reason to reject the broad organ-development annotation. The unresolved distinction is between organ formation/progression and mineral balance in an existing organ.

## Literature follow-up, 2026-10-24

ENPP1 supplies ATP-to-AMP/PPi chemistry and also hydrolyzes cGAMP; the [original-source guide](ENPP1-source-guide.md) distinguishes the enzyme preparations and the roles of CD73, TNAP and receptors. The [INSR study](https://doi.org/10.2337/diabetes.50.4.831) identifies the partner in receptor-capture/co-immunoprecipitation assays (Methods and Figs. 2–3), supporting a specific binding refinement. The [patient-fibroblast study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5606141/) separates ENPP1 transcript measurement (Fig. 2D), CD73 transcript/activity (Fig. 2E–F), and a proposed downstream TNAP-expression model (Discussion/Fig. 7). These inspected readouts raise a process-specificity concern, but the unread detailed supplementary methods preclude rejecting the experimental annotation. Its support remains UNDECIDED pending clarification, without ruling out downstream expression regulation. The broad SMB mapping is judged separately for polysaccharide recognition and scavenger-ligand internalization.

The [2017 substrate study](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.bbagen.2016.12.019) combines biochemistry and modeling. Its externally recovered indexed abstract/previews and its bibliography-only normal cache are distinct evidence objects. Complete Methods and Table 1 remain unavailable. The follow-up makes three action changes: insulin-receptor binding refinement, unresolved experimental gene-expression support and polysaccharide mapping over-annotation.
