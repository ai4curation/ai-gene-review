# FOXG1 source guide

This guide distinguishes the material actually inspected from the scientific inference drawn from it. Article access below describes selected original material and is separate from the actual normal-cache availability listed below. No new supporting-text quotations are added. The accompanying [evidence record](FOXG1-source-evidence.yaml) retains the original source objects, reviewed judgments and input identities.

## Direct human functional evidence

### PMID:12657635 — BF-1 and PLU-1/KDM5B

[PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12657635/) and [the original article DOI](https://doi.org/10.1074/jbc.M301994200) identify the JBC study. Selected original text was read from an [author-uploaded article replica](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/10837197_Tan_K_Shaw_AL_Madsen_B_Jensen_K_Taylor-Papadimitriou_J_Freemont_PSHuman_PLU-1_Has_transcriptional_repression_properties_and_interacts_with_the_developmental_transcription_factors_BF-1_and_PAX9_J_Biol_).

Locators: article pages 20507–20512; Experimental Procedures sections on constructs, yeast two-hybrid, co-immunoprecipitation and repression assays; Results on the VP motif, interaction and BF-1/PLU-1 repression; Figures 3–7 captions. Figure pixels were not independently inspected.

Human fetal-brain BF-1 NM_005249 and sequence-checked constructs were used. Yeast two-hybrid and reciprocal tagged co-immunoprecipitation support PLU-1 association. HEK293 assays tether GAL4DBD–BF-1 to five GAL4 sites and an adenovirus major late promoter. PLU-1 enhances repression; VP-motif mutants retain basal repression but lose the added effect. The HT1080 BF-1 co-immunoprecipitation is transfected/tagged; the endogenous PAX9 experiment concerns PAX9. GAL4 tethering does not test native FOXG1 recognition, and these assays do not measure purified affinity. PLU-1's corepressor or demethylase activity is not assigned to FOXG1.

### PMID:24356439 — endogenous FOXG1/TLE1 in human BTICs

The [original PMC article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3984242/) is also available as [original full XML](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC3984242/fullTextXML). Full XML was recovered; selected Results, Methods and Figures 6–8 captions were read. Figure pixels and supplementary data were not independently audited.

Locators: Results describing FOXG1/TLE association, TLE silencing and GRG6 antagonism; Figures 6e–g, 7 and 8; Methods on cell culture, co-immunoprecipitation, lentiviral knockdown and ChIP. The retained zero-based body-paragraph extraction locates these at Results 11–16 and Methods 29, 36, 38 and 39.

Human patient-derived BT048 cells provide reciprocal endogenous FOXG1/TLE1 co-immunoprecipitation and sequential ChIP at the p21 promoter. FOXG1/TLE perturbations and GRG6 antagonism supply functional context. Cross-linked chromatin fragments were 300–500 bp, so co-occupancy is not base-resolution contact. Complex recovery is not purified binary affinity or stoichiometry. Tagged GRG6/FOXG1 assays in U87MG cells are separate. Human tumor-cell experiments and mouse xenografts remain distinct, and tumor propagation is not converted into a new general developmental annotation.

### PMID:28473536 — target-specific HT-SELEX construct and motifs

Use the [original paper](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28473536/) and its [original sequence/PWM supplementary workbook](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/biostudies/files/S-EPMC8009048/NIHMS1670407-supplement-Supplemental_tables_S1-6.xlsx). In the original workbook, worksheet `S1 sequence information`, row 266, identifies FOXG1B, ENSG00000176165 and a 135-amino-acid eDBD; the S2 motif entries for FOXG1 are rows 5118 and 5123. The retained workbook SHA256 is `4994eb7b3e33eb301541cf7d53d8be0936d5bdb6b504f5d63719e133f40004a6`.

The best ungapped alignment is current P55316 residues 163–297, with R281P and R291A. Both match documented historical reference-sequence conflicts outside the annotated 181–275 forkhead domain. Authentic target-specific motif selection supports sequence-specific double-stranded DNA binding with these construct limits. It does not establish an exact wild-type fragment, natural alternative product, the effects of either difference, full-length cellular affinity or endogenous promoter specificity.

### PMID:21280142 — human clinical evidence, localization and CDKN1A

The [original publisher PDF](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/pdfdirect/10.1002/humu.21422) was accessed through a text reader. Selected Methods, Results and Figures 3–4 captions were read. Direct local PDF download returned 403; no local PDF or figure-pixel inspection is claimed.

Locators: Methods E2028–E2030, PDF pages 2–4, covering cloning/mutagenesis, microscopy and the CDKN1A assay; functional Results E2032–E2033, PDF pages 6–7, and Figures 3–4 captions. The human clinical Results are at retained text lines 194–215, before the functional localization section; they provide independent clinical developmental evidence, distinct from expression and construct assays. The retained Methods and functional Results excerpts are lines 108–187 and 225–264 respectively.

Human BC035020/IMAGE4823883 was expressed as an N-terminal EGFP fusion. A reported Pro-to-Arg codon 82 difference was repaired before generating R244C, and constructs were sequence-checked. This does not independently map every residue to current P55316 or establish a natural product. HeLa and DAOY cells show dispersed nuclear WT signal excluding nucleoli; R244C forms foci partly overlapping SC35. Retinoic-acid-treated SH-SY5Y cells were transfected, GFP-sorted and assessed for CDKN1A mRNA by qRT-PCR normalized to HPRT. The WT decrease is reported as data not shown, while Figure 4 compares mutant and WT. These are localization and expression endpoints, not a direct DNA-binding assay or proof of altered splicing. The codon 82 repair does not imply repair of the unrelated HT-SELEX construct.
