## Channel and metal binding

PMID:22779914 explicitly reports purified human CIB2 binding calcium, magnesium and integrin-tail peptides. Only its complete abstract was recovered; no original affinity constants are reconstructed. PMID:40000792 directly tests human CIB2 with purified human TMC1 domains and resolves a fusion domain complex containing Mg at EF3/EF4. Its calcium-driven closing model is a proposal. The paper disagrees about fusion orientation between Results and Methods, so that detail is omitted.

PMID:34089643 supplies actual mouse electrophysiology. The R186W resting-open-probability analysis isolates TMC2 currents on a Tmc1-deficient background, and the tested adaptation measures are unchanged. Mouse co-IP constructs and a human CIB3 crystal must not be presented as a human CIB2 channel structure. PMID:28663585 includes distinct human-protein FRET and mouse Y2H experiments. The combined physical and functional evidence supports an ion-channel regulator MF, without asserting that CIB2 alone forms a pore.

The actual PMID:39773557 human experiments use recombinant proteins and TMC1 fragments for SEC/NMR. Ion conduction is an AF2/molecular-dynamics result, not direct current recording from a reconstituted human channel. Its solution data favor monomeric CIB2 under tested calcium conditions while explicitly discussing earlier positive cellular self-association. Identical protein binding therefore preserves the original association with less stoichiometric commitment than homodimerization. PMID:42427029 uses mouse Cib2 and, separately, human CIB3 for mouse rescue; it is not a human-CIB2 treatment experiment.

## Retinal and RHEB context

PMID:34162842 links CIB2 to RHEB and Raptor by human-protein interaction experiments and to reduced mTORC1 substrate phosphorylation. The nucleotide-loaded RHEB assay meets CIB2-containing lysate, not purified CIB2; the kinase assay uses Raptor immunoprecipitates from mouse brain. The proposed small-GTPase binding annotation captures the performed molecular interaction. Direct GDI, GAP, nucleotide-exchange and catalytic mTOR inhibition remain unestablished.

Mouse RPE-specific loss, but not rod-specific loss, causes the reported photoreceptor functional decline. The existing photoreceptor-maintenance annotations are retained as context-dependent, potentially non-cell-autonomous outcomes. The older PMID:23023331 retinal perturbation is in Drosophila, whereas mouse experiments establish localization. Current donor annotations and the later mouse genetics are recorded separately. No new retinal process, autophagy or hearing-process annotation is proposed.
