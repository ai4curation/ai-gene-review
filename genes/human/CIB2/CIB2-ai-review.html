Gene Description

CIB2 is a small intracellular EF-hand protein that binds calcium and magnesium and engages several protein partners. In sensory hair cells it associates with cytoplasmic regions of TMC channels and regulates the mechanotransduction apparatus at stereocilia. CIB2 also binds integrin cytoplasmic tails, and its C-terminal peptide recognizes PDZ domains. In a distinct signaling context, CIB2 associates with RHEB and Raptor and restrains mTORC1 signaling; loss in the retinal pigment epithelium impairs phagolysosomal processing and can secondarily compromise photoreceptor function. Calcium-dependent conformational changes influence partner interactions, while the precise connection between metal occupancy and channel gating remains unresolved. Alternative products differ in their N-terminal regions or C-terminal tail.

Source and product scope

The review preserves all 61 seeded assertions, all four fetched products, and all 12 original reference identities. The normal seed and raw GOA remain separate from authored judgments. The displayed O75838-1 protein has 187 residues. The canonical C-terminal 178–187 peptide, DFLSTFHIRI, is the substrate of the PDZ holdup assays. O75838-2 replaces residues 181–187 with a longer sequence and does not carry that tested tail. Products 3 and 4 retain the terminal sequence but have N-terminal deletions; matching a short peptide does not show that either complete product has the same affinity, localization or channel function.

Channel and metal binding

PMID:22779914 explicitly reports purified human CIB2 binding calcium, magnesium and integrin-tail peptides. Only its complete abstract was recovered; no original affinity constants are reconstructed. PMID:40000792 directly tests human CIB2 with purified human TMC1 domains and resolves a fusion domain complex containing Mg at EF3/EF4. Its calcium-driven closing model is a proposal. The paper disagrees about fusion orientation between Results and Methods, so that detail is omitted.

PMID:34089643 supplies actual mouse electrophysiology. The R186W resting-open-probability analysis isolates TMC2 currents on a Tmc1-deficient background, and the tested adaptation measures are unchanged. Mouse co-IP constructs and a human CIB3 crystal must not be presented as a human CIB2 channel structure. PMID:28663585 includes distinct human-protein FRET and mouse Y2H experiments. The combined physical and functional evidence supports an ion-channel regulator MF, without asserting that CIB2 alone forms a pore.

The actual PMID:39773557 human experiments use recombinant proteins and TMC1 fragments for SEC/NMR. Ion conduction is an AF2/molecular-dynamics result, not direct current recording from a reconstituted human channel. Its solution data favor monomeric CIB2 under tested calcium conditions while explicitly discussing earlier positive cellular self-association. Identical protein binding therefore preserves the original association with less stoichiometric commitment than homodimerization. PMID:42427029 uses mouse Cib2 and, separately, human CIB3 for mouse rescue; it is not a human-CIB2 treatment experiment.

Binding and calcium direction

PMID:36115835 establishes direct fragment-level PDZ binding through holdup affinity measurements. Exact IntAct entries retain the target peptide and each partner-domain boundary, tags, method, host and positive status. The campaign policy retains supported generic binding as non-core.

The broad calcium-homeostasis annotations remain valid; response to ATP is a contextual perturbation, not ATP recognition by CIB2. PMID:26426422 names HEK293 in calcium Methods/Results but COS7 in the figure legend, so that internal host ambiguity is not silently resolved.

Retinal and RHEB context

PMID:34162842 links CIB2 to RHEB and Raptor by human-protein interaction experiments and to reduced mTORC1 substrate phosphorylation. The nucleotide-loaded RHEB assay meets CIB2-containing lysate, not purified CIB2; the kinase assay uses Raptor immunoprecipitates from mouse brain.
