# Human ARAF (UniProt P10398): functional-annotation research report

## Executive conclusion

The requested target is unambiguously **human ARAF**, encoding **A-Raf**, UniProt **P10398**—not an ARAF pseudogene or a similarly named non-human protein. The supplied description, aliases (**ARAF1, PKS, PKS2**), serine/threonine-protein-kinase classification, and C1-like/DAG-binding plus protein-kinase domains agree with the literature on the ARAF member of the three-protein RAF family. Open Targets likewise maps approved symbol **ARAF** to human gene ENSG00000078061 and distinguishes it from **ARAFP2**, an ARAF pseudogene (OpenTargets Search: -ARAF).

ARAF’s primary molecular function is to act as a RAS-regulated MAP kinase kinase kinase. It transfers the γ-phosphate of ATP to serine/threonine residues in **MEK1/MAP2K1 and MEK2/MAP2K2**, promoting MEK-dependent activation of ERK1/2. Wild-type ARAF is the least catalytically active RAF paralog under many assay conditions, but activated dimers and disease mutants can signal strongly. ARAF also has important non-catalytic functions, particularly sequestration of the pro-apoptotic kinase MST2 at mitochondria. Its location and function are consequently state-dependent: inactive cytosolic complexes, RAS-associated plasma-membrane signalosomes, trafficking compartments, and mitochondria are all relevant.

## 1. Identity, family, and architecture

ARAF is one of the three mammalian RAF paralogs—**ARAF, BRAF, and RAF1/CRAF**—and belongs to the TKL branch of the protein-kinase superfamily. RAF-family architecture comprises three conserved regions: **CR1**, containing the RAS-binding domain and cysteine-rich/C1-like membrane-binding domain; **CR2**, a serine/threonine-rich regulatory segment with an inhibitory 14-3-3 site; and **CR3**, containing the catalytic protein-kinase domain, RAF dimer interface, and MEK-binding determinants. ARAF and CRAF lack the BRAF-specific N-terminal region, but otherwise share the core RAF regulatory organization (degirmenci2020targetingaberrantrasrafmekerk pages 3-5, jeon2024signalingfromras pages 4-6).

This architecture aligns with the user-supplied InterPro annotations: C1-like/DAG-phorbol-ester-binding folds correspond to the cysteine-rich portion of CR1, while the kinase-like and protein-kinase domains correspond to CR3. The C1-like annotation should not be interpreted to mean that ARAF functions principally as a conventional PKC-like DAG receptor; in RAF, this region contributes to RAS and membrane interactions within the larger regulatory apparatus.

## 2. Primary biochemical function and substrate specificity

### Catalytic reaction

The generic enzymatic reaction is:

**ATP + protein serine/threonine-OH → ADP + phosphoprotein serine/threonine-O-phosphate.**

The best-established physiologically relevant substrates are **MEK1 and MEK2**. Activated RAF phosphorylates their activation-loop serines, enabling MEK1/2 to phosphorylate ERK1/2. Contemporary RAF literature treats MEK1/2 as the principal, and often the only clearly established, physiological catalytic substrates of RAF proteins (cook2024functionalassessmentof pages 27-31).

ARAF-specific experiments support this assignment. Wild-type ARAF immunoprecipitates generated phosphorylated MEK1, whereas kinase-dead **K336M** and **D447A** proteins produced little or no p-MEK1. A dimer-deficient **R362H** protein also produced very little p-MEK1 and lacked associated BRAF/CRAF, demonstrating that productive ARAF signaling requires both an intact catalytic site and dimer assembly in those models (monaco2021lxh254apotent pages 17-21).

Wild-type ARAF generally has the lowest activity of the RAF paralogs—commonly ranked **BRAF > CRAF > ARAF**—in part because of sequence differences around its noncanonical APE-region motif. This is a relative statement, not evidence that ARAF is a pseudokinase: activated ARAF and oncogenic mutants can phosphorylate MEK strongly (degirmenci2020targetingaberrantrasrafmekerk pages 3-5, nelson2014somaticactivatingaraf pages 2-3).

Other reported ARAF substrates or outputs, such as SMAD2 linker phosphorylation, are substantially less established than MEK1/2 and appear context-dependent. They should not replace MEK1/2 in the primary annotation (yang2026a‑rafaserinethreonine pages 2-4).

## 3. Activation mechanism and regulation

### Autoinhibition and RAS recruitment

The accepted RAF-family model—supported most extensively by recent BRAF/CRAF structures but applicable to conserved ARAF architecture—places inactive RAF in the cytosol as a compact, autoinhibited complex stabilized by 14-3-3. RAS-GTP binds the RAS-binding and cysteine-rich domains, recruits RAF to the inner plasma membrane, and promotes release of autoinhibition and side-to-side RAF dimerization (cook2024functionalassessmentof pages 27-31, jeon2024signalingfromras pages 4-6).

For ARAF, **Ser214** is the inhibitory CR2 phosphorylation/14-3-3 site. Dephosphorylation of the homologous RAF regulatory site—mediated in the current family model by phosphatase machinery that includes the SHOC2–MRAS–PP1 complex—facilitates conversion to the active state. However, much of the highest-resolution SHOC2 and 14-3-3 structural work uses BRAF or CRAF; residue-level extrapolation beyond conserved ARAF Ser214 should therefore be regarded as family-based inference rather than direct ARAF structural observation (cook2024functionalassessmentof pages 27-31, jeon2024signalingfromras pages 4-6).

ARAF’s N-terminal acidic regulatory segment includes phosphorylation-sensitive residues around **Ser299/Tyr301/Tyr302**, and its activation loop includes **Thr452/Thr455**. Phosphorylation and dimerization cooperate to establish the active kinase conformation (cook2024functionalassessmentof pages 27-31, berlinska2025unravellingkrasbiology pages 10-13).

### Dimerization is functionally essential

ARAF can form homodimers and heterodimers with BRAF or CRAF. In RAS-mutant cells, wild-type ARAF restored resistance to the BRAF/CRAF-biased inhibitor LXH254, whereas kinase-dead K336M/D447A and dimer-defective R362H did not. These rescue experiments provide unusually direct evidence that both catalytic activity and RAF heterodimerization are required for ARAF-dependent MAPK output in those settings (monaco2021lxh254apotent pages 17-21).

## 4. Cellular location

ARAF localization is dynamic rather than confined to one organelle:

1. **Cytosol:** inactive RAF-family complexes are predominantly cytosolic and autoinhibited.
2. **Plasma membrane:** growth-factor-generated RAS-GTP recruits ARAF to the inner plasma membrane, where RAF dimers phosphorylate MEK.
3. **Endosomal/trafficking compartments:** ARF6-associated trafficking and KSR-dependent localization have been reported, suggesting spatial control of ARAF–MEK signaling (yang2026a‑rafaserinethreonine pages 2-4).
4. **Mitochondria:** ARAF is imported/anchored through mitochondrial translocase machinery and has been observed at mitochondria in proliferating epithelial and tumor cells (yang2026a‑rafaserinethreonine pages 1-2).

The answer to “where does ARAF carry out its function?” is therefore function-specific: canonical MEK phosphorylation is chiefly associated with RAS-organized membrane signalosomes and RAF–MEK complexes, whereas the best-characterized kinase-independent anti-apoptotic activity occurs in cytoplasmic/mitochondrial compartments.

## 5. Pathways and biological processes

### Canonical RAS–RAF–MEK–ERK signaling

ARAF functions at the RAF/MAP3K tier of the pathway:

**growth-factor receptor → RAS-GTP → ARAF-containing RAF dimer → MEK1/2 → ERK1/2 → context-dependent proliferation, differentiation, survival, and migration.**

The 2024 Annual Review emphasizes that RAF operates not as a free binary switch but as an integrated regulatory assembly containing RAS, RAF dimers, MEK, 14-3-3, phosphatases, and chaperones. For ARAF, this framework explains how a comparatively weak isolated kinase can make substantial contributions in membrane-localized dimers and mutant-RAS signalosomes (jeon2024signalingfromras pages 4-6).

### Kinase-independent apoptosis regulation

ARAF also binds and sequesters **MST2/STK3**, limiting MST2-dependent apoptotic signaling. During epithelial differentiation, redistribution of ARAF away from mitochondria changes access to MST2, linking intracellular location to cell fate. This activity is substantially kinase-independent and should be annotated as a scaffold/sequestration function rather than as phosphorylation of MST2 (yang2026a‑rafaserinethreonine pages 5-6, yang2026a‑rafaserinethreonine pages 1-2).

Older RAF literature also implicates ARAF in suppression of ASK1-dependent apoptosis, but the MST2 interaction has more precise ARAF-specific localization evidence. These noncanonical functions broaden ARAF biology without changing its primary enzymatic annotation.

## 6. Human disease evidence

### Lung adenocarcinoma: ARAF p.S214C

The strongest exceptional-responder evidence comes from a 66-year-old woman with stage-IV lung adenocarcinoma. Oral sorafenib produced a near-complete radiographic response within two months, followed by approximately **five years of progression-free, asymptomatic treatment**. Tumor/normal whole-genome sequencing identified somatic **ARAF p.S214C**: 11/23 tumor-DNA reads versus 0/18 normal reads, with 48/56 tumor-RNA reads supporting expression. Engineered ARAF variants transformed immortalized human airway epithelial cells in a sorafenib-sensitive manner. Same-site ARAF variants and a nearby RAF1 variant collectively occurred in approximately **1%** of an independent lung-adenocarcinoma cohort (Imielinski et al., published April 2014; https://doi.org/10.1172/JCI72763) (imielinski2014oncogenicandsorafenibsensitive pages 1-2).

This is compelling mechanistic and individual-patient evidence, but not a prospective biomarker trial. Sorafenib is multikinase-directed; therefore, the response cannot prove that isolated ARAF inhibition alone caused the clinical outcome.

### Langerhans-cell histiocytosis

In purified CD1a-positive lesional cells from a BRAF-wild-type LCH patient, Nelson et al. identified same-allele somatic **ARAF p.F351L plus p.Q347_A348del**. The compound mutant robustly phosphorylated MEK and activated ERK; its kinase activity was only slightly below BRAF V600E and was abolished by adding kinase-dead K336M. In soft agar it produced **102.5 ± 8.5 colonies per 4 cm²**, versus **0 ± 0** for wild-type ARAF after 22 days. Its in-vitro activity was inhibited by vemurafenib (published May 15, 2014; https://doi.org/10.1182/blood-2013-06-511139) (nelson2014somaticactivatingaraf pages 3-4, nelson2014somaticactivatingaraf pages 2-3).

The discovery cohort comprised exomes from three patients, followed by targeted testing of 52 LCH samples. Thus, the study establishes biological plausibility and oncogenicity but also demonstrates that activating ARAF lesions are uncommon relative to BRAF-driven LCH (nelson2014somaticactivatingaraf pages 1-2).

### Central conducting lymphatic anomaly

A recurrent ARAF mutation has been reported as a cause of central conducting lymphatic anomaly, with clinical responsiveness to a MEK inhibitor. The primary report appeared in *Nature Medicine* in 2019, volume 25, pages 1116–1122; DOI: https://doi.org/10.1038/s41591-019-0479-2 (yang2026a‑rafaserinethreonine pages 8-8). This is an important precision-medicine precedent because it targets the downstream MAPK output of activating ARAF rather than requiring an ARAF-selective drug. Detailed patient-level quantitative results were not available in the retrieved full text and are therefore not reproduced here.

Open Targets aggregates ARAF associations with cancer, hypertrophic cardiomyopathy, Noonan syndrome, Costello syndrome, and low-grade glioma. These scores are useful for hypothesis generation but should not be interpreted as equivalent to variant-level causal evidence; the same small literature set contributes to several disease labels (OpenTargets Search: -ARAF).

## 7. Recent developments, 2023–2024

### Structural understanding in 2024

Two authoritative 2024 reviews consolidated a model in which RAF activation is governed by coordinated autoinhibition, membrane recruitment, regulatory-site dephosphorylation, 14-3-3 rearrangement, dimerization, and assembly with MEK. They also stress that the strongest structural data concern BRAF and CRAF; ARAF-specific structural coverage remains comparatively sparse (Jeon, Tkacik & Eck, August 2024, https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-biochem-052521-040754; Spencer-Smith & Morrison, May 2024, https://doi.org/10.1042/BST20230552) (jeon2024signalingfromras pages 4-6, degirmenci2020targetingaberrantrasrafmekerk pages 3-5).

### ARAF protomer engagement in RAF signalosomes

The 2023 peer-reviewed Cell Chemical Biology work, first available as a 2022 preprint, showed that inhibitor potency measured against isolated kinase domains can misrepresent engagement in intact RAS–RAF complexes. Across 13 type-II RAF inhibitors, live-cell BRAF-versus-ARAF engagement ratios ranged from **9- to 75-fold** in KRAS-driven heterodimers. LY3009120 and RAF709 had ARAF cellular IC50 values of **200 and 220 nM**, whereas RAF265 and TAK-580 were much weaker at **6,400 and 4,000 nM**. ARAF engagement tracked p-ERK suppression in KRAS-mutant DLD-1 and MIA PaCa-2 cells, identifying insufficient ARAF occupancy as a potency-limiting property of nominally pan-RAF inhibitors (peer-reviewed DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.chembiol.2023.07.019; preprint evidence retrieved at https://doi.org/10.21203/rs.3.rs-2175742/v1) (vasta2022protomerselectivityof pages 6-9, vasta2022protomerselectivityof pages 9-11).

This changes the therapeutic interpretation of ARAF: it is not merely the weakest RAF kinase but can be the least effectively drug-engaged protomer sustaining signaling in mutant-RAS complexes.

## 8. Current applications and therapeutic status

No ARAF-selective drug is currently established as an approved therapy, and the trial search did not identify a validated ARAF-genotype-specific interventional program. Present applications are instead:

- **Variant-guided downstream inhibition:** MEK inhibition in activating ARAF-associated lymphatic disease.
- **Exceptional-responder interpretation:** ARAF p.S214C as a plausible marker of sorafenib-sensitive lung adenocarcinoma, requiring prospective validation.
- **Resistance prediction:** ARAF can sustain RAS–MAPK signaling when inhibitors preferentially occupy BRAF and CRAF.
- **Pan-RAF drug design:** cellular engagement of the ARAF protomer should be measured directly rather than inferred from purified-kinase potency.

In five RAS-mutant cancer lines, ARAF deletion increased LXH254 sensitivity **3–11-fold**. In RAF-isogenic cells, BRAF- or CRAF-only signaling was inhibited below 100 nM LXH254, whereas ARAF-only cells required more than **1,500 nM**; ARAF-knockout xenografts showed complete or near-complete regressions, although some later escaped through pathway reactivation or bypass. These are strong preclinical data, not proof of patient benefit (monaco2021lxh254apotent pages 17-21, monaco2021lxh254apotent pages 21-24).

## 9. Expert interpretation and evidence limitations

The most defensible functional annotation is:

> **ARAF is a RAS-regulated RAF-family serine/threonine MAP3K whose primary catalytic substrates are MEK1/2. Its activity depends strongly on membrane recruitment, phosphorylation state, 14-3-3 remodeling, and RAF dimerization; it also executes kinase-independent mitochondrial/cytoplasmic anti-apoptotic functions through MST2 sequestration.**

Three cautions are important. First, many regulatory details are inferred from the more extensively characterized BRAF and CRAF paralogs; they should be labeled RAF-family mechanisms unless directly tested in ARAF. Second, rare activating ARAF variants can be much more active than wild-type ARAF, so the phrase “weak kinase” does not predict mutant behavior. Third, therapeutic evidence is heterogeneous: the lung-cancer result is an exceptional case, CCLA supports downstream MEK inhibition, and inhibitor-engagement studies are primarily cellular or preclinical. None establishes a broadly validated, ARAF-selective standard of care.
