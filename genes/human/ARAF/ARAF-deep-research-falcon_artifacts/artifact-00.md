| Identity and domains | Human **ARAF** encodes A-Raf, one of three RAF-family serine/threonine kinases. Conserved regions comprise CR1 (RAS-binding and cysteine-rich/C1-like membrane-binding domains), regulatory CR2, and CR3 (protein-kinase domain); unlike BRAF, ARAF lacks the BRAF-specific region. This matches UniProt **P10398** and the stated TKL kinase, C1-like/DAG-binding, and kinase-domain annotations. | Curated target identity plus comparative sequence/structural analysis | Jeon, Tkacik & Eck, *Annual Review of Biochemistry*, August 2024 (pqac-00000007); Degirmenci et al., *Cells*, January 2020 (pqac-00000003) |
| Catalytic reaction and substrate | ARAF catalyzes ATP-dependent transfer of phosphate to serine/threonine residues. Its best-established physiological substrates are **MEK1/MAP2K1 and MEK2/MAP2K2**, phosphorylated on their activation loops, thereby enabling MEK-dependent ERK1/2 activation. ARAF-associated MEK1 phosphorylation is lost or strongly reduced with kinase-dead ARAF variants. | RAF-family biochemical consensus plus ARAF immunoprecipitation kinase assays | Cook, 2024 (pqac-00000005); Monaco et al., *Clinical Cancer Research*, December 2021 (pqac-00000002) |
| Relative catalytic activity | Wild-type ARAF generally has the **lowest basal and inducible MEK-kinase activity** of the three RAF paralogs (BRAF > CRAF > ARAF), partly attributed to its noncanonical APE-region sequence. Low activity does not mean catalytically inactive: activated or mutant ARAF can robustly phosphorylate MEK. | Comparative biochemical and sequence evidence | Degirmenci et al., *Cells*, January 2020 (pqac-00000003); Nelson et al., *Blood*, May 2014 (pqac-00000019) |
| Activation and localization | In the canonical model, inactive RAF is predominantly a cytosolic, 14-3-3-stabilized monomer. RAS-GTP recruits ARAF through CR1 to the plasma membrane; regulatory dephosphorylation and side-to-side RAF homo/heterodimerization promote kinase activation. ARAF Ser214 is the inhibitory CR2 14-3-3 site; dimerization and catalytic activity are experimentally required for strong ARAF-dependent MEK signaling in RAS-mutant models. | RAF-family structural mechanism with ARAF-specific mutagenesis | Jeon, Tkacik & Eck, August 2024 (pqac-00000007); Cook, 2024 (pqac-00000005); Monaco et al., December 2021 (pqac-00000002) |
| Mitochondrial MST2 role | ARAF also localizes to mitochondria in proliferating epithelial and tumor cells, where it binds and sequesters the pro-apoptotic kinase **MST2/STK3**. This anti-apoptotic function is substantially kinase-independent and changes with ARAF’s compartmental redistribution during epithelial differentiation. | Cell fractionation, microscopy, interaction, depletion, and differentiation studies | Rauch et al., *Cell Death & Differentiation*, 2016; summarized in ARAF-specific evidence (pqac-00000001, pqac-00000004) |
| Lung adenocarcinoma variant | Somatic **ARAF p.S214C** was identified in a stage-IV lung adenocarcinoma from a 66-year-old patient who had a near-complete response to sorafenib within two months and remained progression-free for five years. Tumor/normal sequencing showed 11/23 versus 0/18 variant reads and tumor RNA showed 48/56; engineered S214 ARAF mutants transformed human airway epithelial cells in a sorafenib-sensitive manner. Same-site ARAF and nearby RAF1 variants occurred in approximately **1%** of an independent cohort. | Tumor/normal genome and transcriptome sequencing, cellular transformation assays, exceptional-responder observation | Imielinski et al., *Journal of Clinical Investigation*, April 2014 (pqac-00000018) |
| Langerhans-cell histiocytosis variant | One BRAF-wild-type LCH lesion carried same-allele somatic **ARAF p.F351L plus p.Q347_A348del**. The compound mutant strongly phosphorylated MEK and activated ERK, had activity only slightly below BRAF V600E, and produced **102.5 ± 8.5** soft-agar colonies per 4 cm² versus **0 ± 0** for wild-type ARAF; kinase-dead K336M abolished activity. | Purified lesional-cell sequencing, biochemical kinase assays, mutagenesis, and transformation assays | Nelson et al., *Blood*, May 15, 2014 (pqac-00000017, pqac-00000019, pqac-00000020) |
| LXH254 resistance | In five RAS-mutant cancer lines, ARAF loss increased sensitivity to the BRAF/CRAF-selective inhibitor LXH254 by **3–11-fold**. Wild-type ARAF restored resistance, whereas kinase-dead K336M/D447A or dimer-deficient R362H did not. ARAF-only cells required >1,500 nM LXH254 for pathway inhibition versus <100 nM in BRAF- or CRAF-only cells. | CRISPR/isogenic-cell pharmacology, rescue experiments, kinase assays, and xenografts | Monaco et al., *Clinical Cancer Research*, December 2021 (pqac-00000012, pqac-00000015) |
| Live-cell ARAF engagement | Conditional NanoBRET assays showed that 13 type-II RAF inhibitors preferentially engaged BRAF over ARAF by **9–75-fold** in KRAS-driven RAF complexes. LY3009120 and RAF709 had ARAF cellular IC50 values of **200 and 220 nM**, whereas RAF265 and TAK-580 measured **6,400 and 4,000 nM**. ARAF engagement—not merely BRAF/CRAF engagement—tracked with p-ERK suppression in tested KRAS-mutant cells. | Live-cell target-engagement and pathway pharmacology | Vasta et al., preprint October 2022; peer-reviewed *Cell Chemical Biology*, 2023 (pqac-00000009, pqac-00000014) |
