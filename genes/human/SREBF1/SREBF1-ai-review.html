Gene Description

SREBF1 encodes sterol regulatory element-binding protein 1 (SREBP-1), a basic helix-loop-helix leucine zipper (bHLH-ZIP) transcription factor made as an inactive ~125 kDa precursor anchored in the endoplasmic reticulum membrane by two transmembrane segments. Its C-terminal regulatory domain binds the sterol-sensing escort protein SCAP. When ER sterols are high, SCAP binds INSIG proteins and the SCAP-SREBP complex is held in the ER. When sterols fall, SCAP carries SREBP-1 in COPII vesicles to the Golgi, where site-1 protease (MBTPS1) cuts the luminal loop and site-2 protease (MBTPS2) cuts within the first transmembrane segment. This releases the N-terminal transactivation/bHLH-ZIP fragment (~68 kDa), which dimerizes, enters the nucleus via importin-beta, and binds sterol regulatory elements (direct-repeat SRE-1, ATCACCCCAC) and E-boxes in target promoters, often with NF-Y or SP1. The nuclear form is short-lived and is degraded by the ubiquitin-proteasome system. Alternative first exons give two main isoforms. SREBP-1a has a long acidic transactivation domain and strongly activates both cholesterogenic (HMGCR, HMGCS1, LDLR) and lipogenic genes. SREBP-1c, the predominant form in liver and adipose tissue, has a short activation domain and preferentially activates fatty acid and triglyceride synthesis genes (ACLY, ACACA, FASN, SCD, ELOVL6). SREBP-1c transcription is induced by insulin (via mTORC1) and LXR, and repressed by glucagon/cAMP and polyunsaturated fatty acids, making it the main transcriptional link between feeding/insulin and hepatic lipogenesis. Truncated isoforms lacking the membrane anchor (SREBP-1aDelta, -1cDelta) are constitutively nuclear. Heterozygous variants at the S1P cleavage site cause autosomal dominant IFAP syndrome 2, and SREBF1 variants also cause hereditary mucoepithelial dysplasia.

Functional Isoforms

Curated functional classes representing distinct biological activities. These may be splice variants, cleavage products, or other forms with different functions.

SREBP-1a SPLICE VARIANT
ID: SREBF1_1A
UNIPROT ISOFORM: P36956-1
Product of the upstream exon 1a promoter, with a long acidic N-terminal transactivation domain. It is a potent activator of all SREBP targets, both cholesterogenic (LDLR, HMGCR, HMGCS1) and lipogenic. Predominant in cultured cell lines and expressed at low, fairly constant levels in tissues; its sterol-regulated processing resembles SREBP-2. Most cholesterol-biosynthesis regulation attributed to SREBF1 reflects this isoform. The functional differences between SREBF1 forms are preferences of target choice and regulation, not exclusive activities, so the related GO terms are annotated to the gene as a whole.
SREBP-1c SPLICE VARIANT
ID: SREBF1_1C
UNIPROT ISOFORM: P36956-3
Product of the downstream exon 1c promoter, with a short activation domain. It is a weaker activator, biased toward fatty acid and triglyceride synthesis genes (ACLY, ACACA, FASN, ELOVL6, SCD) and weak on classic cholesterogenic SREs. Predominant in liver and adipose tissue. Its transcription is induced by insulin and LXR agonists and repressed by fasting, glucagon/cAMP and PUFA, which makes it the mediator of insulin-stimulated hepatic lipogenesis. The functional differences between SREBF1 forms are preferences of target choice and regulation, not exclusive activities, so the related GO terms are annotated to the gene as a whole.
SREBP-1aDelta / SREBP-1cDelta SPLICE CLASS
ID: SREBF1_DELTA
UNIPROT ISOFORM: P36956-5, P36956-6
Splice variants that end at residue 470, before the membrane anchor. They need no SCAP transport or Golgi proteolysis, so they are constitutively nuclear and active on lipogenic promoters (UniProt; PMID:18267114, not cached).
Processed nuclear SREBP-1 (nSREBP-1) CLEAVAGE PRODUCT
ID: SREBF1_NUCLEAR
UNIPROT CHAIN: PRO_0000314029 (residues 1-490)
N-terminal fragment released by sequential S1P and S2P cleavage. It carries the transactivation and bHLH-ZIP domains, dimerizes, is imported into the nucleus, binds SRE and E-box elements and activates transcription. It is rapidly ubiquitinated and degraded by the proteasome. The full-length precursor (PRO_0000127447) is the ER/Golgi membrane form in the SREBP-SCAP complex. The functional differences between SREBF1 forms are preferences of target choice and regulation, not exclusive activities, so the related GO terms are annotated to the gene as a whole.

description: SREBF1 encodes sterol regulatory element-binding protein 1 (SREBP-1), a basic helix-loop-helix leucine
  zipper (bHLH-ZIP) transcription factor made as an inactive ~125 kDa precursor anchored in the endoplasmic reticulum
  membrane by two transmembrane segments. Its C-terminal regulatory domain binds the sterol-sensing escort protein
  SCAP. When ER sterols are high, SCAP binds INSIG proteins and the SCAP-SREBP complex is held in the ER. When sterols
  fall, SCAP carries SREBP-1 in COPII vesicles to the Golgi, where site-1 protease (MBTPS1) cuts the luminal loop
  and site-2 protease (MBTPS2) cuts within the first transmembrane segment. This releases the N-terminal transactivation/bHLH-ZIP
  fragment (~68 kDa), which dimerizes, enters the nucleus via importin-beta, and binds sterol regulatory elements
  (direct-repeat SRE-1, ATCACCCCAC) and E-boxes in target promoters, often with NF-Y or SP1. The nuclear form is
  short-lived and is degraded by the ubiquitin-proteasome system. Alternative first exons give two main isoforms.
  SREBP-1a has a long acidic transactivation domain and strongly activates both cholesterogenic (HMGCR, HMGCS1,
  LDLR) and lipogenic genes. SREBP-1c, the predominant form in liver and adipose tissue, has a short activation
  domain and preferentially activates fatty acid and triglyceride synthesis genes (ACLY, ACACA, FASN, SCD, ELOVL6).
  SREBP-1c transcription is induced by insulin (via mTORC1) and LXR, and repressed by glucagon/cAMP and polyunsaturated
  fatty acids, making it the main transcriptional link between feeding/insulin and hepatic lipogenesis. Truncated
  isoforms lacking the membrane anchor (SREBP-1aDelta, -1cDelta) are constitutively nuclear. Heterozygous variants
  at the S1P cleavage site cause autosomal dominant IFAP syndrome 2, and SREBF1 variants also cause hereditary mucoepithelial
  dysplasia.
