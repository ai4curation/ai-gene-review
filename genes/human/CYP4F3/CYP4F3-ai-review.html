Gene Description

Cytochrome P450 4F3 is a microsomal, heme-thiolate cytochrome P450 monooxygenase of the endoplasmic reticulum membrane that omega-hydroxylates fatty acids and their oxygenated derivatives (oxylipins), using molecular oxygen and electrons delivered from NADPH by cytochrome P450 reductase. Alternative promoter usage and splicing of mutually exclusive 48-codon exons produce two 520-residue isoforms with different substrate preferences and tissue distributions, which is the defining feature of the gene. CYP4F3A, the only CYP4F in blood neutrophils and bone marrow, is the leukotriene B4 omega-hydroxylase: it inactivates LTB4 with submicromolar affinity and carries the oxidation through three successive steps to 20-hydroxy-, 20-oxo- and finally 20-carboxy-LTB4, terminating a potent chemoattractant signal during the resolution of inflammation. CYP4F3B, expressed in liver, kidney, trachea and gastrointestinal tissues, instead prefers polyunsaturated fatty acids: it converts arachidonate to 20-HETE, a vasoconstrictive and natriuretic mediator of arterial blood pressure, and omega-hydroxylates eicosapentaenoate and docosahexaenoate, so omega-3 fatty acids act as competing substrates that lower 20-HETE output. Both isoforms also omega-hydroxylate monohydroxy polyunsaturated fatty acids and fatty acid epoxides, and both initiate the omega-oxidation of saturated very-long-chain fatty acids to dicarboxylic acids, an extra-peroxisomal route for shortening chains that would otherwise require peroxisomal beta-oxidation.

CYP4F3A (exon 4, myeloid) SPLICE VARIANT
ID: CYP4F3_A
UNIPROT ISOFORM: Q08477-1
Incorporates exon 4. The only CYP4F isoform in blood neutrophils and bone marrow. Leukotriene B4 omega-hydroxylase (Km 0.64-0.71 uM), carrying LTB4 through three successive oxidations to 20-carboxy-LTB4. Essentially inactive on arachidonate: the purified neutrophil enzyme showed no detectable activity on it, and Km is ~185 uM.
Isoform-specific terms: leukotriene-B4 20-monooxygenase activity 20-hydroxy-leukotriene B4 omega oxidase activity 20-aldehyde-leukotriene B4 20-monooxygenase activity leukotriene B4 catabolic process
CYP4F3B (exon 3, hepatic/renal) SPLICE VARIANT
ID: CYP4F3_B
UNIPROT ISOFORM: Q08477-2
Incorporates exon 3 (VSP_047193). Selectively expressed in liver and kidney, and the predominant CYP4F in trachea and gastrointestinal tissues. Omega-hydroxylates arachidonate to 20-HETE (Km 22 uM) and other PUFAs including EPA and DHA; 30-fold higher Km for LTB4 than CYP4F3A. One of two P450s initiating very-long-chain fatty acid omega-oxidation.
Isoform-specific terms: arachidonate metabolic process

omega-hydroxy fatty acid omega-oxidase activity

Definition: Catalysis of the reaction: an omega-hydroxy fatty acid + reduced [NADPH--hemoprotein reductase] + O2 = an omega-oxo fatty acid + oxidized [NADPH--hemoprotein reductase] + 2 H2O + H(+).

Justification: Step 2 of fatty acid omega-oxidation has no GO molecular function term, although GO holds the identical step for leukotriene B4 as GO:0097258 '20-hydroxy-leukotriene B4 omega oxidase activity' -- annotated to this very protein and mapped in rhea2go. CYP4F3A and CYP4F3B are both experimentally credited with the NADPH-dependent oxidation of omega-hydroxy-VLCFAs. Anchored to RHEA:39055 (C22) and RHEA:39059 (C26). No chain-length qualifier: docosanoate is exactly 22 carbons, so GO:0102033 (13-22 C) covers it and GO:0140692 (>22 C) does not, and GO:0120250 shows GO keeps an unqualified parent tier for this chemistry. Proposed in SSSOM batch 8 of the RHEA project; this review supplies the CYP4F3-specific evidence.

Parent term: monooxygenase activity

omega-oxo fatty acid omega-monooxygenase activity

Definition: Catalysis of the reaction: an omega-oxo fatty acid + reduced [NADPH--hemoprotein reductase] + O2 = a fatty acid dicarboxylate + oxidized [NADPH--hemoprotein reductase] + H2O + 2 H(+).

Justification: Step 3 of fatty acid omega-oxidation, yielding the dicarboxylate that enters beta-oxidation, likewise has no GO term while its leukotriene B4 equivalent does (GO:0097259 '20-aldehyde-leukotriene B4 20-monooxygenase activity'). The suffix follows that template, which is a monooxygenase rather than the 'omega oxidase' of the step-2 term. Anchored to RHEA:39043 (C22) and RHEA:39047 (C26). Suggested Questions for Experts

Q: GO:0097265 '5(S)-hydroxyeicosatetraenoic acid dehydrogenase activity' appears to conflate two different reactions and should probably be fixed or obsoleted. Its definition formula is an O2-consuming omega-hydroxylation (xref RHEA:48656, product 5,20-dihydroxy-eicosatetraenoate) yet its name says 'dehydrogenase', its definition glosses the product as '(5-oxo-ETE)' -- the product of a genuine 5-hydroxyeicosanoid dehydrogenase, not of a hydroxylase -- and its is_a path runs through GO:0016616 'oxidoreductase activity, acting on the CH-OH group of donors, NAD or NADP as acceptor', where an O2-consuming monooxygenation does not belong. Should the term be renamed and re-parented under GO:0004497, or obsoleted as a per-substrate term in favour of GO:0102033, following the precedent of GO:0052869 and GO:0018777?

Q: Should GO represent steps 2 and 3 of fatty acid omega-oxidation, given that it already represents both steps for leukotriene B4 (GO:0097258, GO:0097259) on this same protein? CYP4F3 is the clearest case of the asymmetry: all three LTB4 steps have terms and are annotated here, while the chemically identical C22/C26 route has a term only for step 1. Two terms are proposed in this review.

Q: UniProt's CYP4F3B FUNCTION line states that it omega-hydroxylates long-chain 3-hydroxy fatty acids, citing PMID:18065749 -- but that paper reports CYP4F3b as having negligible activity and attributes the function to CYP4F11. Can UniProt confirm whether this line is a mis-citation, or whether it rests on data elsewhere in the paper that the abstract does not state?

Q: Is there any direct evidence that CYP4F3 (either isoform) omega-hydroxylates vitamin K? The menaquinone and phylloquinone catabolic process annotations here are IBA only; PMID:24138531 assayed CYP4F2 and CYP4F11 and did not test CYP4F3, and its authors note that other liver enzymes may contribute. A negative result for CYP4F3B would justify removing these two annotations rather than keeping them as non-core.

Q: GOA carries both the LTB4 and the arachidonate activities on the single accession Q08477 with no isoform qualifier, yet the two are carried by different, mutually-exclusive splice isoforms and the purified myeloid isoform has no arachidonate activity at all. Should these annotations carry isoform qualifiers (Q08477-1 / Q08477-2), and is that representable in the GAF the curators work from?

suggested_questions:
- question: GO:0097265 '5(S)-hydroxyeicosatetraenoic acid dehydrogenase activity' appears to conflate
    two different reactions and should probably be fixed or obsoleted. Its definition formula is an O2-consuming
    omega-hydroxylation (xref RHEA:48656, product 5,20-dihydroxy-eicosatetraenoate) yet its name says
    'dehydrogenase', its definition glosses the product as '(5-oxo-ETE)' -- the product of a genuine 5-hydroxyeicosanoid
    dehydrogenase, not of a hydroxylase -- and its is_a path runs through GO:0016616 'oxidoreductase activity,
    acting on the CH-OH group of donors, NAD or NADP as acceptor', where an O2-consuming monooxygenation
    does not belong. Should the term be renamed and re-parented under GO:0004497, or obsoleted as a per-substrate
    term in favour of GO:0102033, following the precedent of GO:0052869 and GO:0018777?
- question: 'Should GO represent steps 2 and 3 of fatty acid omega-oxidation, given that it already represents
    both steps for leukotriene B4 (GO:0097258, GO:0097259) on this same protein? CYP4F3 is the clearest
    case of the asymmetry: all three LTB4 steps have terms and are annotated here, while the chemically
    identical C22/C26 route has a term only for step 1. Two terms are proposed in this review.'
- question: UniProt's CYP4F3B FUNCTION line states that it omega-hydroxylates long-chain 3-hydroxy fatty
    acids, citing PMID:18065749 -- but that paper reports CYP4F3b as having negligible activity and attributes
    the function to CYP4F11. Can UniProt confirm whether this line is a mis-citation, or whether it rests
    on data elsewhere in the paper that the abstract does not state?
- question: Is there any direct evidence that CYP4F3 (either isoform) omega-hydroxylates vitamin K? The
    menaquinone and phylloquinone catabolic process annotations here are IBA only; PMID:24138531 assayed
    CYP4F2 and CYP4F11 and did not test CYP4F3, and its authors note that other liver enzymes may contribute.
    A negative result for CYP4F3B would justify removing these two annotations rather than keeping them
    as non-core.
- question: GOA carries both the LTB4 and the arachidonate activities on the single accession Q08477 with
    no isoform qualifier, yet the two are carried by different, mutually-exclusive splice isoforms and
    the purified myeloid isoform has no arachidonate activity at all. Should these annotations carry isoform
    qualifiers (Q08477-1 / Q08477-2), and is that representable in the GAF the curators work from?
