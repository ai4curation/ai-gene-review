| **Identity, accession, and domains** | Human **SIM1** was studied using full-length human cDNA and explicitly identified as **UniProt P81133**. It encodes a bHLH–PAS transcription factor; the N-terminal basic region mediates sequence-specific DNA binding, while the HLH, PAS-A/PAS-B, and PAC-containing region supports heterodimerization and complex architecture (Ramachandrappa et al., 2013; Mohammed et al., 2026). (pqac-00000009, pqac-00000010, pqac-00000019) | Confirms that the target is human **Single-minded homolog 1**, not SIM2 or another similarly named protein. PAC is structurally associated with the PAS region; its independent biochemical function in SIM1 remains poorly resolved. |
| **Molecular partner and cellular location** | SIM1 forms transcriptionally active heterodimers with ARNT or the neuronally enriched physiological candidate **ARNT2**. Immunocytochemistry showed wild-type SIM1 to be predominantly **nuclear**; reporter assays demonstrated ARNT2-dependent transcriptional activity (Ramachandrappa et al., 2013). (pqac-00000001, pqac-00000009) | SIM1 functions principally as a nuclear DNA-binding transcriptional regulator rather than an enzyme, receptor, transporter, or secreted protein. Most localization evidence comes from transfected HEK293 cells rather than native human hypothalamic neurons. |
| **DNA recognition and R171H mechanism** | Purified SIM1–ARNT2 preferentially recognized **TACGTG-containing** response elements, with additional specificity encoded by flanking sequence and DNA shape. The obesity-associated R171H PAS-A-loop variant impaired a DNA-responsive reporter but not a Gal4 dimerization reporter; ARNT/ARNT2 recovery was unchanged. However, purified WT and R171H complexes showed no difference by EMSA on either 22- or 79-bp probes, despite stronger binding to longer DNA (Bersten et al., 2026; received August 7, 2024). (pqac-00000032, pqac-00000044, pqac-00000045, pqac-00000049) | The best interpretation is defective **chromatin-context or DNA-shape-dependent transcription**, not loss of ARNT2 dimerization. The unchanged purified-DNA EMSA means that a simple intrinsic affinity defect is not established; structural simulations suggesting loss of an R171–DNA contact remain mechanistic models. |
| **Developmental downstream genes** | In mouse hypothalamus, **Brn2/POU3F2** expression is lost in prospective neuroendocrine cells lacking Sim1 or Arnt2. An inducible neuronal-cell screen identified **268 candidates induced more than 1.7-fold**; Jak2 and Thrb/TRβ2 expression was subsequently absent from the embryonic PVN of Sim1 mutants (Michaud et al., 1998; Liu et al., 2003). (pqac-00000012, pqac-00000015, pqac-00000022, pqac-00000042) | These results establish Sim1 dependence and place POU3F2, Jak2, and Thrb downstream developmentally. They **do not prove direct SIM1 occupancy** of their endogenous promoters or enhancers; indirect regulation through secondary transcription factors remains possible. |
| **Adult Mc4r and Oxt expression** | Postnatal CNS deletion of Sim1 caused dosage-dependent hyperphagic obesity without global PVN hypocellularity or abnormal major PVN projections, while markedly reducing hypothalamic **Oxt** and PVN **Mc4r** mRNA (Tolson et al., 2010). (pqac-00000025) | Demonstrates an ongoing postdevelopmental requirement for Sim1 and supports a leptin–melanocortin–oxytocin network. Neither **MC4R** nor **OXT** is established as a direct SIM1-bound transcriptional target; altered neuronal state or cell-subtype composition could contribute. |
| **Human loss-of-function genetics** | Sequencing **2,100** people with severe early-onset obesity and **1,680 controls** identified 13 heterozygous SIM1 variants in 28 unrelated affected individuals; **9 of 13** significantly reduced ARNT2-supported reporter activity. Carriers showed increased test-meal intake, normal basal metabolic rate, and variable neurobehavioral findings (Ramachandrappa et al., July 2013). (pqac-00000031, pqac-00000036, pqac-00000048) | Strong human genetic-plus-functional evidence links reduced SIM1 activity to dominant, variably penetrant hyperphagic obesity. A synthetic reporter cannot classify every variant or reproduce native hypothalamic chromatin; some variants also occurred in controls. |
| **2024 noncoding enhancer development** | The SIM1-associated VISTA enhancer **hs576** contained two rare recessive ASD-case variants. Introducing both variants into an H11-integrated reporter reduced or abolished E11.5 mouse enhancer activity in several cranial nerves, including trigeminal, facial, vagal/nodose, and accessory nerves; one variant was predicted to remove a MEIS1 site and create a BACH2 site (Shin et al., August 14, 2024). (pqac-00000028, pqac-00000053, pqac-00000063) | This extends SIM1-relevant variation beyond coding sequence, but endogenous **SIM1 expression was not measured or edited**. The reporter therefore shows altered hs576 activity, not definitive causal regulation of SIM1, obesity, or autism in patients; patient weight data were unavailable. |
| **Preclinical dosage rescue** | CRISPR activation of the intact Sim1 allele—targeting either its promoter or a hypothalamus-specific enhancer approximately **270 kb** away—raised endogenous Sim1 expression, reduced food intake and adiposity, and rescued obesity in haploinsufficient mice. Postnatal hypothalamic rAAV delivery also produced sustained rescue (Matharu et al., January 18, 2019). (pqac-00000035) | Provides causal evidence that insufficient Sim1 dosage drives the mouse phenotype and that endogenous upregulation can reverse it postnatally. This remains **preclinical**: no human SIM1-directed CRISPRa therapy or established targeted pharmacotherapy has been demonstrated. |
