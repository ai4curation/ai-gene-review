# AGFG2 primary, PAINT and ontology scope check (2026-09-20)

The actual PTHR46134 tree places O95081 leaf PTN002509056 below PTN002919572 and root PTN002353603. The former carries the positive IBDs for cytoplasmic vesicle, acrosome assembly, spermatid nucleus differentiation and intermediate filament organization; the root supplies cytoplasm. The exact target path, current source rows, ancestral IBDs and raw-response hash are saved in AGFG2-O95081-paint-lineage.json. No loss on this target path was established. Mouse Agfg1 is experimental descendant evidence for the ancestral assertion, not an arbitrary pairwise donor. The unused mouse Agfg2 ortholog and a shorter source list are not negative controls for biological function. Salivary enrichment does not exclude germ-cell expression or activity.

PMID:11711676 and PMID:14724135 are abstract-only in the central cache. They describe mouse Hrb-dependent vesicle docking/fusion and keratin-5 distribution in the acroplaxome. PMID:16765935 is also abstract-only, but explicitly includes hrb-null spermatids; its title naming H1T2 does not establish misattribution. Its unchanged early nuclear-polarity marker is not a demonstration that Hrb has no role in later nuclear differentiation. Retaining the PAINT capacity does not convert mouse evidence into direct human AGFG2 experimentation.

The stored MAFFT domain alignment maps ASAP3 Arg469 to AGFG2 Arg75 and ASAP3 Asp484 to AGFG2 Thr89. The canonical target sequence is O95081 sequence version 2. These are coordinate/residue observations, not enzyme assays. PMID:23433073 predicts reduced AGFG GAP activity from several divergent sites, but also reports that the Asp-homologous residue is absent from all 18 GIT sequences analyzed. Primary PMID:10788515 directly reports GIT1/GIT2 ARF GAP activity, including ARF6 hydrolysis. Thus the selected four positive-control proteins retaining Asp do not establish a universal requirement across all ArfGAP mechanisms. ASAP3 structural/mutational PMID:20510928 is an ASAP-family result, with calcium-dependent catalytic regulation. The exact AGFG2 catalytic inference remains open. PMID:34369554 calls R75Q GAP-dead and reports preserved secretion as data not shown; that phenotype is not a direct GAP measurement.

Full primary Results, Discussion and relevant Methods of PMID:34369554 were read. AGFG2 depletion reduces stimulated VWF release; siRNA-resistant overexpression reduces retained cigar-shaped granules and promotes release even without stimulation. AGFG2 is not the released VWF cargo: it performs a trafficking-cofactor/regulatory step. The experiments favor a late release step but do not directly isolate membrane fusion kinetics. Granule morphology and cargo delivery are largely preserved, while secreted high-molecular-weight VWF and propeptide cleavage show modest quantitative reductions; describing all cargo maturation as unchanged is too strong. The precise localization of AGFG2 itself was not imaged in these experiments.

Full PMID:10613896 measures human Hrbl in CV-1 cells: the Rev-dependent reporter and synergism with Eps15/Eps15R involve Hrbl, but the detailed NPF mutation, imaging and fractionation experiments use Hrb. Increased steady-state Rev levels leave protein stability, transcription and translation mechanisms unresolved; reporter activity is not direct measurement of RNA transport by AGFG2. Full PMID:25496667 demonstrates HRBL-dependent Nef-induced CD4 removal, but HRBL depletion did not significantly reduce viral replication. The abstract of PMID:26701340 independently distinguishes HRBL's dual Nef/Vpu cofactor activity from HRB's Nef-only activity. CD4 is the removed receptor; AGFG2 contributes host trafficking machinery rather than being the substrate of removal.

The entire Affinage report was read. Its narrative correctly describes dual Nef/Vpu support, while its dated table contradicts that narrative by claiming no Nef effect. Its detailed target binding/localization statements overextend paralog experiments, its mammal-only evolutionary statement conflicts with PMID:23433073, and its corpus omits the direct endothelial secretion work. The useful viral cofactor synthesis is retained with these limitations; provider coverage is not a negative assay.

AGFG2-ontology-comparators.json records exact QuickGO term definitions, direct human annotations and ancestry checks. Regulated exocytosis is annotated to trafficking effectors such as RAB11FIP1/RAB11FIP2/RAB11FIP5, not only the fusion catalyst. Host-mediated activation of viral process means promoting a viral process, not necessarily increasing viral replication; human CAV2 and CFL1 have this annotation from PMID:25556234. These are role comparators for host trafficking/cytoskeletal cofactors. The two existing authored process proposals do not duplicate each other or an ancestor/descendant of the inherited developmental processes. No O95081/AGFG2 entry was found in gocams/index.tsv. The proposed viral annotation is retained at the broad host-cofactor level, without a separate redundant Rev-process proposal.

The membrane HDA is from PMID:19946888, a membrane-fraction proteomics experiment. Its abstract explicitly discusses transient membrane association. High throughput and absence of a transmembrane helix do not refute peripheral membrane localization. This source does not locate AGFG2 specifically to plasma membrane or a particular endosome.
