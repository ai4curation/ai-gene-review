Gene Description

LRP3 encodes a poorly characterized 770-amino-acid member of the low-density lipoprotein receptor family. Its type-I membrane architecture comprises a signal peptide, an extracellular ectodomain predicted to contain two CUB domains and four LDL-receptor class-A domains in the order CUB1–LDLRA1–LDLRA2–CUB2–LDLRA3–LDLRA4, one transmembrane helix, and a long proline-rich cytoplasmic region containing a tyrosine-based internalization signal. LRP3 transcript is broadly detected in human tissues. In immortalized human bone-marrow stromal cells, LRP3 expression and perturbation support a context-dependent role that favors osteoblast differentiation and opposes adipocyte differentiation, with LRP3 mRNA regulated by miR-4739. Its physiological ligand, transported cargo, proximal signaling mechanism, and general relevance outside this stromal-cell model remain unknown; transfected CHO cells did not bind beta-migrating VLDL or receptor-associated protein under the reported conditions.

Core Functions

LRP3 is a type-I single-pass LDLR-family membrane protein whose extracellular ectodomain is predicted to contain CUB1–LDLRA1–LDLRA2–CUB2–LDLRA3–LDLRA4 and whose cytoplasmic tyrosine-based internalization signal is compatible with receptor trafficking. Phylogenetic inference places LRP3 at the plasma membrane, whereas the reviewed UniProt record supports only generic membrane localization; plasma membrane is therefore an IBA-level location rather than a directly demonstrated human LRP3 location. No physiological ligand, cargo-uptake activity, or proximal signaling reaction has yet been established, so no specific molecular-function term is assigned.

Suggested Experiments

Experiment: Use purified human LRP3 ectodomain for unbiased cell-surface and secreted-protein ligand capture, then test candidate binding and uptake in matched LRP3-null cells rescued with wild-type LRP3 or a tyrosine-signal mutant. Quantify surface loss, cargo internalization, endosomal delivery, and competition with soluble ectodomain.

Hypothesis: Human LRP3 recognizes a ligand distinct from beta-migrating VLDL and LRPAP1 and internalizes it through the cytoplasmic tyrosine-based signal.

Type: ligand discovery and internalization-rescue assay

Experiment: Compare endogenous or equivalently expressed wild-type and tyrosine-signal-mutant LRP3 by surface biotinylation pulse-chase and live-cell imaging, coupled to proximity labeling or immunoprecipitation of adaptor proteins. Include expression, topology, and plasma-membrane delivery controls so a trafficking defect is not confused with misfolding.

Hypothesis: The cytoplasmic tyrosine-based signal recruits endocytic adaptors and is required for LRP3 movement from the plasma membrane into coated vesicles.

Type: motif separation-of-function trafficking assay

Experiment: Generate independent LRP3 knockout clones in primary human bone-marrow stromal cells from multiple donors, rescue them with wild-type LRP3, and profile early phosphoproteomic and transcriptional responses before lineage markers diverge. Compare osteogenic and adipogenic differentiation with matched proliferation and viability controls, and test whether miR-4739 effects are rescued by an LRP3 cDNA lacking its 3' UTR.

Hypothesis: The stromal differentiation phenotype is caused by a proximal LRP3-dependent signaling pathway rather than nonspecific effects of the original knockdown.

Type: primary-cell knockout-rescue and pathway-identification study
