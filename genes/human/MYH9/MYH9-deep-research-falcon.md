# Human MYH9 (UniProt P35579): Functional Annotation Research Report

## Executive summary

**Identity is verified.** Human **MYH9** (chromosome 22q12.3; UniProt **P35579**) encodes the 1,960-residue, approximately 230-kDa **non-muscle myosin heavy chain IIA** (NMHC-IIA; also NMMHC-IIA). Two MYH9-encoded heavy chains associate with two essential and two regulatory light chains to form the functional **non-muscle myosin IIA/NM2A hexamer**. This matches the supplied organism, nomenclature, TRAFAC myosin–kinesin ATPase family, motor-head, IQ-motif and myosin-tail annotations; no gene-symbol ambiguity was found. The “kinesin motor-domain superfamily” annotation denotes remote structural-fold homology and does **not** make MYH9 a microtubule motor. (li2024unveilingtheenigmatic pages 1-3, feroz2024nonmusclemyosinii pages 2-5, llorentegonzalez2021targetingcytoskeletalphosphorylation pages 3-4)

MYH9’s primary function is to encode the force-generating heavy chain of an **actin-activated Mg²⁺-ATPase**. NMIIA converts ATP hydrolysis into tension and relative actin-filament movement. Its bipolar minifilaments organize and contract cortical and bundled actin networks, thereby controlling cell shape, adhesion, migration, polarity, cytokinesis and tissue mechanics. Its best-established human disease relationship is autosomal-dominant **MYH9-related disease (MYH9-RD)**, characterized by congenital macrothrombocytopenia and variable leukocyte inclusions, nephropathy, sensorineural hearing loss and cataract. (asensiojuarez2020linkingthelandscape pages 1-3, brito2020nonmusclemyosin2a pages 1-4, asensiojuarez2020linkingthelandscape pages 3-6)

## 1. Molecular identity and architecture

The nomenclature needs careful separation:

- **MYH9** is the gene.
- **NMHC-IIA/NMMHC-IIA** is the MYH9-encoded heavy-chain polypeptide.
- **NMIIA/NM2A** is the assembled motor complex containing a heavy-chain homodimer, two essential light chains—commonly MYL6—and two regulatory light chains—MYL12A/B. (asensiojuarez2020linkingthelandscape pages 1-3, feroz2024nonmusclemyosinii pages 2-5)

Each heavy chain has three functional regions. The N-terminal globular head contains ATP- and actin-binding sites; the neck contains two IQ motifs that bind the essential and regulatory light chains and acts as a lever arm; and the long C-terminal coiled coil drives heavy-chain dimerization. The non-helical tailpiece and adjacent assembly regions control higher-order oligomerization into bipolar minifilaments. Reported NMIIA minifilaments are approximately 300 nm long and contain roughly 20–30 hexamers. (asensiojuarez2020linkingthelandscape pages 1-3, llorentegonzalez2021targetingcytoskeletalphosphorylation pages 3-4)

This organization aligns with the supplied InterPro domains: the **Myosin_S1_N** and myosin-head-like annotations correspond to the catalytic motor; **IQ_motif_EF-hand-BS** corresponds to light-chain binding; and **Myosin_tail** corresponds to dimerization and filament assembly. The literature therefore supports—not merely predicts—the domain-based functional assignment.

## 2. Primary biochemical function

### Catalyzed reaction and substrate specificity

The core reaction is:

**ATP + H₂O → ADP + inorganic phosphate**, requiring Mg²⁺ and strongly coupled to binding of **filamentous actin (F-actin)**.

Thus, ATP is the chemical substrate and F-actin is the physiological track and activity-stimulating binding partner. ATP binding, hydrolysis, phosphate release and ADP release produce conformational changes that are transmitted through the neck/lever arm. In bipolar filaments, motor heads at opposite ends engage oppositely oriented actin, causing filament sliding, network contraction and tension generation. MYH9 is therefore best annotated as a **cytoskeletal chemomechanical motor and force generator**, not principally as a transporter of a discrete soluble cargo. (feroz2024nonmusclemyosinii pages 2-5, llorentegonzalez2021targetingcytoskeletalphosphorylation pages 3-4, brito2020nonmusclemyosin2a pages 1-4)

NMIIA has a relatively low duty ratio—reported around 5–20%—and moves actin two- to three-fold faster than NMII-B or NMII-C in comparative assays. Its individual heads spend comparatively little of each cycle strongly actin-bound, but collective operation in minifilaments supports sustained network-level force. This kinetic profile favors rapid contraction, remodeling and motor exchange; NMII-B is comparatively specialized for prolonged force holding. (juarez2024newmechanismsof pages 39-42, brito2020nonmusclemyosin2a pages 6-9, juarez2024newmechanismsof pages 36-39)

## 3. Activation and signaling pathways

NMIIA is regulated at the levels of conformation, ATPase activity and filament assembly.

### Regulatory-light-chain pathway

Unphosphorylated myosin can adopt a compact **10S** conformation in which head–tail interactions inhibit actin binding and assembly. Phosphorylation of regulatory light-chain **Ser19** promotes an extended **6S**, assembly-competent conformation, increases actin-activated ATPase activity and enables bipolar-filament formation. Additional phosphorylation at **Thr18** further increases activity and filament stability. One review reports an approximately 1,000-fold rise in ATPase activity upon activating RLC phosphorylation, although this is a pan-NMII biochemical estimate rather than a universally applicable cellular fold-change for MYH9. (brito2020nonmusclemyosin2a pages 6-9, asensiojuarez2020linkingthelandscape pages 6-8)

Principal upstream routes include:

- **Ca²⁺–calmodulin–MLCK**, linking calcium signals to myosin activation.
- **RhoA–ROCK**, which phosphorylates RLC and suppresses myosin phosphatase through MYPT1, coupling Rho-family signaling to contractility.
- **MRCK**, downstream of Cdc42 in appropriate cellular contexts.
- **MYPT1-containing phosphatase**, which removes activating Thr18/Ser19 phosphorylation and favors relaxation. (juarez2024newmechanismsof pages 39-42, llorentegonzalez2021targetingcytoskeletalphosphorylation pages 3-4, asensiojuarez2020linkingthelandscape pages 6-8)

These kinase mechanisms are broadly conserved across non-muscle myosin II. Evidence suggests ROCK preferentially acts on NMIIA-associated RLC in some settings, but RLC is a separate subunit and should not be described as part of the MYH9 polypeptide. (asensiojuarez2020linkingthelandscape pages 6-8)

### Heavy-chain and filament regulation

MYH9 heavy-chain phosphorylation provides an additional assembly-control layer. Reported sites include Tyr158 and tail-region Thr1800, Ser1803, Ser1808, Ser1916 and Ser1943. Depending on kinase and site, phosphorylation can reduce filament stability, increase binding of disassembly factors such as S100A4, or inhibit filament formation. PKC phosphorylation of RLC Ser1/Ser2 is inhibitory and contributes to NMIIA disassembly/reorganization in platelets. (llorentegonzalez2021targetingcytoskeletalphosphorylation pages 3-4, asensiojuarez2020linkingthelandscape pages 6-8)

## 4. Cellular localization and precise structural role

MYH9’s product is an **intracellular cytoplasmic cytoskeletal motor**. It is not a secreted or integral-membrane protein. Its distribution is dynamic rather than uniformly cytosolic and depends on actin architecture, phosphorylation and cell state.

Direct or strongly supported locations include:

- **Cortical actomyosin:** NMIIA accumulates on newly formed cortical actin during membrane-bleb retraction and supplies the contractile force that restores membrane shape. It can reorganize into cortical bundles during plasma-membrane repair. (brito2020nonmusclemyosin2a pages 11-14)
- **Stress fibers and adhesion-linked bundles:** tropomyosin-dependent recruitment places NMIIA on contractile actin filaments; its tension promotes focal-adhesion maturation and force transmission. Some focal-adhesion conclusions derive from combined actin-fiber and pan-NMII perturbations rather than MYH9-only imaging. (feroz2024nonmusclemyosinii pages 9-11)
- **Cell migration machinery:** rapid NMIIA turnover supports leading-edge/lamellar remodeling, while activated myosin also contributes to rear contraction and polarized traction. Bisphosphorylated RLC is enriched in high-tension regions such as the trailing edge. (juarez2024newmechanismsof pages 39-42, feroz2024nonmusclemyosinii pages 9-11)
- **Cell–cell junctions:** NMIIA-dependent actomyosin tension supports E-cadherin adherens-junction formation and stabilizes apical junctional complexes. (brito2020nonmusclemyosin2a pages 6-9, feroz2024nonmusclemyosinii pages 9-11)
- **Division apparatus:** NMII-rich contractile rings generate cleavage-furrow tension; MYH9 loss can produce multinucleation and division defects. Because other NMII paralogs can support cytokinesis, not every contractile-ring observation is MYH9-specific. (feroz2024nonmusclemyosinii pages 9-11, feroz2024nonmusclemyosinii pages 2-5)
- **Megakaryocytes and platelets:** NMIIA is reported to be the only NMII paralog expressed in these cells, making platelet production especially sensitive to MYH9 variants. (brito2020nonmusclemyosin2a pages 11-14, asensiojuarez2020linkingthelandscape pages 3-6)

## 5. Biological processes and physiological function

The common mechanistic denominator is **generation, organization and transmission of actomyosin tension**.

1. **Adhesion and mechanosensing.** NMIIA contracts stress fibers and loads integrin-associated adhesions. This supports adhesion maturation, extracellular-matrix rigidity sensing and coupling between substrate mechanics and intracellular signaling.
2. **Migration and polarity.** Spatially controlled contraction coordinates protrusion, adhesion turnover, rear retraction and nuclear/cell-body translocation. NMIIA’s rapid kinetics favor dynamic remodeling.
3. **Cytokinesis.** NMIIA contributes to constriction of the actomyosin ring and maintenance of mitotic mechanical integrity.
4. **Epithelial and endothelial barriers.** Junctional actomyosin tension helps organize adherens and tight-junction systems. In 2024 mouse work, endothelial myosin-IIA deficiency increased blood–brain-barrier permeability; β-catenin restoration improved barrier integrity, suggesting a mechanistic link between MYH9-dependent force organization and junctional transcription/interaction networks. This remains preclinical evidence rather than a human therapy. 
5. **Morphogenesis.** Myosin-generated stromal forces can instruct epithelial patterning. A 2024 study found that fibroblast-specific Myh9 loss, ROCK inhibition or NMII inhibition abrogated fibroblast-induced mammary epithelial branching, connecting MYH9-dependent fibroblast contractility to YAP/ERK-patterned morphogenesis.
6. **Hematopoiesis.** In megakaryocytes, localized NMIIA constriction and blood-flow shear partition proplatelet extensions into platelets. NMIIA also controls megakaryocyte migration toward marrow sinusoids. (feroz2024nonmusclemyosinii pages 1-2, brito2020nonmusclemyosin2a pages 6-9, asensiojuarez2020linkingthelandscape pages 13-14)

The first four broad processes are well established, but exact paralog attribution must be handled carefully: many pharmacologic studies inhibit all NMII isoforms, whereas MYH9-specific knockout, knockdown or mutation gives stronger isoform-level evidence.

## 6. Human disease: MYH9-related disease

Pathogenic heterozygous MYH9 variants cause an autosomal-dominant spectrum historically divided into May–Hegglin anomaly, Sebastian, Fechtner and Epstein syndromes. Modern usage groups these as **MYH9-RD**. Open Targets independently shows strong genetic support for MYH9 association with macrothrombocytopenia plus granulocyte inclusions—with or without nephritis or hearing loss—and with dominant hearing-loss phenotypes. (OpenTargets Search: -MYH9)

### Hematologic mechanism

Congenital macrothrombocytopenia is nearly universal. Motor-domain variants can reduce ATPase activity, actin binding or force generation; rod/tail variants can destabilize dimer or filament assembly. Defective megakaryocyte chemotaxis and incomplete constriction at proplatelet necks produce fewer, oversized platelets. Because megakaryocytes lack another NMII paralog, functional compensation is limited. Abnormal NMIIA can also precipitate as Döhle-like inclusions in granulocytes. (asensiojuarez2020linkingthelandscape pages 3-6, asensiojuarez2020linkingthelandscape pages 13-14)

### Quantitative clinical data

Published estimates vary with genotype and ascertainment:

- Severe mucosal, nasal, gingival or menstrual bleeding was reported in approximately **28%**; bleeding is otherwise frequently mild despite low platelet counts.
- NMIIA-positive leukocyte inclusions occur in approximately **42–84%**.
- Approximately **60%** develop later hearing impairment.
- About **25%** develop progressive nephritis, beginning with proteinuria or microhematuria and potentially advancing to chronic kidney disease.
- One review cites high-tone hearing loss in **83%** and cataracts in **23%** of a particular cohort, illustrating cohort dependence rather than universal penetrance.
- Prevalence estimates range from approximately **3 per million** in older clinically ascertained series to **1 in 20,000–25,000** from population-variant estimates; an Italian registry estimated at least **2.7 per 100,000**. The disparity strongly suggests underdiagnosis and methodological differences. (feroz2024nonmusclemyosinii pages 21-22, asensiojuarez2020linkingthelandscape pages 3-6, asensiojuarez2020linkingthelandscape pages 1-3, khor2024myh9relateddisorder(myh9rd) pages 1-2)

Variants cluster at recurrent residues including motor-domain Ser96 and Arg702 and rod/tail residues Arg1165, Asp1424 and Glu1841; 20–35% may arise de novo. Genotype influences renal, auditory and cataract risk, so molecular diagnosis has prognostic value beyond confirming thrombocytopenia. (asensiojuarez2020linkingthelandscape pages 3-6, khor2024myh9relateddisorder(myh9rd) pages 6-7)

### Clinical implementation

Current real-world use is diagnostic and preventive rather than MYH9-directed pharmacology. Persistent thrombocytopenia, giant platelets, mean platelet volume above roughly 12 fL, broad platelet-size distribution, Döhle-like leukocyte inclusions and dominant family history should trigger MYH9 testing. Automated counters may misclassify giant platelets, and MYH9-RD is often mistaken for immune thrombocytopenia. A 2024 family report documented platelet counts around **30–90 × 10³/µL**, absent steroid response and a pathogenic p.Arg1933* variant. Correct diagnosis can prevent ineffective corticosteroids, immunosuppression or splenectomy. (khor2024myh9relateddisorder(myh9rd) pages 6-7, khor2024myh9relateddisorder(myh9rd) pages 2-6)

Management is individualized: observation if bleeding is minimal; avoidance of aspirin and NSAIDs where possible; antifibrinolytics such as tranexamic acid and, in selected procedures, desmopressin; and genotype-informed surveillance of renal function/proteinuria, hearing and vision. There is **no approved therapy that repairs or selectively normalizes MYH9/NMIIA itself**. (khor2024myh9relateddisorder(myh9rd) pages 6-7)

## 7. Recent research and emerging applications, 2023–2024

### Cancer biology

Two 2024 reviews emphasize that MYH9 is not uniformly oncogenic or tumor suppressive. Increased MYH9 can drive adhesion signaling, migration, stemness and drug resistance in some tumors; loss can impair mitotic stability or p53-associated tumor suppression in others. In glioblastoma, MYH9 has been linked to invasion, temozolomide resistance and β-catenin signaling; a reported **94-patient** cohort associated the S100A4–NMIIA axis with aggressive disease. In renal and breast cancer models, MYH9-associated AKT, HER-family, integrin/FAK and mechanotransduction pathways have been implicated in growth or treatment resistance. (feroz2024nonmusclemyosinii pages 19-21, feroz2024nonmusclemyosinii pages 15-16, feroz2024nonmusclemyosinii pages 21-22)

A 2024 hepatocellular-carcinoma study identified phosphorylated MYH9 Ser1943 as a scaffold recruiting USP22, stabilizing HIF-1α and promoting cancer stemness and lenvatinib resistance; CK2 or USP22 inhibition reversed resistance in experimental models. A separate breast-cancer study reported that TMEM120B stabilized MYH9, promoted focal-adhesion assembly and activated β1-integrin–FAK–TAZ–mTOR signaling, increasing stemness and resistance to docetaxel/doxorubicin. These are promising biomarker and pathway hypotheses, not validated MYH9-targeted standards of care.

The expert interpretation from recent reviews is appropriately cautious: tumor effects depend on tissue, p53 status, matrix mechanics and treatment context. Systemic MYH9 inhibition could also disrupt platelets, barriers and normal tissue mechanics. (li2024unveilingtheenigmatic pages 1-3, feroz2024nonmusclemyosinii pages 2-5, feroz2024nonmusclemyosinii pages 15-16)

### Immunomechanics

A November 2024 *Science Advances* study reported that partial NMII inhibition or MYH9 knockdown increased T-cell traction and tumor cytotoxicity through an NMII–NKX3-2–ADGRB3–F-actin pathway, improving checkpoint-immunotherapy efficacy in animal models. This counterintuitive result shows that reducing one contractile regulator can reorganize actin to increase net traction. It is preclinical and should not be interpreted as evidence for systemic myosin inhibition in patients.

### Tissue engineering and regenerative biology

Recent organoid, kidney-repair and mammary-branching studies use MYH9 manipulation to dissect how cellular force controls morphogenesis. These applications are presently experimental: they make MYH9 a mechanobiology handle for organoid design, barrier models and regeneration research, not yet a deployed therapeutic target.

## 8. Evidence limitations and overall assessment

The central annotation is high confidence: **MYH9/P35579 is the heavy-chain motor of NMIIA, an actin-activated MgATPase whose regulated bipolar filaments generate intracellular force and tension.** Human genetics, biochemistry, structural studies and cell biology converge on this conclusion.

Three interpretive cautions are important:

1. Results from blebbistatin, ROCK inhibitors or RLC phosphorylation frequently apply to **all NMII paralogs**, not uniquely MYH9.
2. MYH9 abundance, phosphorylation and filament assembly are distinct variables; expression alone does not establish motor activity.
3. Cancer associations are highly context dependent. MYH9 is currently more mature as a mechanistic biomarker and experimental pathway node than as a safe, validated drug target. (feroz2024nonmusclemyosinii pages 2-5, llorentegonzalez2021targetingcytoskeletalphosphorylation pages 3-4, feroz2024nonmusclemyosinii pages 21-22)
