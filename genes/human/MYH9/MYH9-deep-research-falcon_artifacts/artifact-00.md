| Identity and architecture | Human **MYH9** at 22q12.3 encodes the 1,960-aa, ~230-kDa non-muscle myosin heavy chain IIA (**NMHC-IIA; UniProt P35579**). Two heavy chains, two essential light chains and two regulatory light chains form the NMIIA hexamer. Each heavy chain contains an N-terminal motor/head, an IQ-motif neck that binds light chains, and a coiled-coil/C-terminal tail that mediates dimerization and filament assembly. (pqac-00000002, pqac-00000003, pqac-00000005) | Cytoplasmic actomyosin cytoskeleton | **Strong, direct MYH9/NMIIA evidence** from protein annotation and convergent structural reviews | The InterPro “kinesin motor-domain superfamily” label reflects remote TRAFAC motor-fold homology; MYH9 is functionally a **myosin**, not a kinesin or microtubule motor. |
| Biochemical reaction | The motor is an **actin-activated Mg²⁺-dependent ATPase**: ATP + H₂O → ADP + Pi. Nucleotide-driven conformational changes move the light-chain-bound neck/lever arm, converting chemical energy into force and relative sliding of actin filaments. Its physiological protein substrate/track is filamentous actin rather than a transported small molecule. (pqac-00000003, pqac-00000005, pqac-00000006) | Motor heads engaging F-actin | **Strong direct biochemical/structural consensus**; exact catalytic-cycle details are also supported by broader class-II myosin work | “ATPase” alone understates its role: useful cellular force generally requires regulated assembly of multiple motors into bipolar filaments. |
| Filament mechanics | Coiled-coil heavy-chain dimers laterally assemble into ~300-nm bipolar minifilaments containing roughly 20–30 hexamers. Oppositely oriented motor heads pull on actin to generate contraction, tension and force transmission. NMIIA has a relatively low duty ratio (~5–20%), rapid turnover and a 2–3-fold higher actin-sliding velocity than NMII-B/C, favoring dynamic remodeling over prolonged force holding. (pqac-00000001, pqac-00000010, pqac-00000016) | Contractile actomyosin bundles and networks | **Strong for NMIIA assembly and kinetic specialization**; minifilament principles combine direct NMIIA and pan-NMII evidence | NMIIA can copolymerize with other NMII paralogs, so cellular filament behavior cannot always be attributed exclusively to MYH9. |
| Regulation | RLC Ser19 phosphorylation promotes conversion from compact, assembly-incompetent **10S** myosin to extended **6S**, increasing actin-activated ATPase activity and filament assembly; additional Thr18 phosphorylation further stabilizes and activates filaments. MLCK, ROCK and MRCK can phosphorylate RLC, while MYPT1-mediated dephosphorylation favors inactivity. MYH9-tail phosphorylation—including Thr1800/Ser1803/Ser1808, Ser1916 and Ser1943—can reduce filament stability or formation. (pqac-00000009, pqac-00000013, pqac-00000014) | Spatially regulated actomyosin networks downstream of Ca²⁺–calmodulin and Rho-family signaling | **Strong pan-NMII mechanism with NMIIA-specific support**; ROCK reportedly favors NMIIA-associated RLC, and MYH9 heavy-chain sites are isoform-specific | RLC is a separate subunit, not the MYH9 polypeptide. Kinase effects depend on cell type, localization and phosphosite; they should not all be described as direct MYH9 phosphorylation. |
| Localization | NMIIA is intracellular and dynamically recruited to F-actin-rich stress fibers, cortical actomyosin and retracting blebs; it contributes to adhesion-associated and apical-junctional bundles and NMII-rich cytokinetic structures. In motile cells, its rapid dynamics support leading-edge/lamellar remodeling, while phosphorylated NMII also accumulates in tensile rear regions. NMIIA is the only NMII paralog reported in platelets and megakaryocytes. (pqac-00000011, pqac-00000012, pqac-00000014) | Cell cortex, stress fibers, junctional actomyosin, migratory protrusion/rear and division apparatus; platelet/megakaryocyte cytoskeleton | **Moderate-to-strong**, combining direct NMIIA imaging/function with some pan-NMII localization inference | Localization is transient and cell-state dependent. “Cytoplasmic” does not imply diffuse distribution, and some cytokinetic or junctional observations do not distinguish NMIIA from NMII-B/C. |
| Core biological processes | By producing and transmitting actomyosin tension, NMIIA supports cell shape and polarity, adhesion maturation, migration, cortical retraction, epithelial junction integrity, cytokinesis and tissue morphogenesis. Direct evidence includes NMIIA-dependent E-cadherin junction formation, bleb retraction and cell-division integrity. (pqac-00000007, pqac-00000010, pqac-00000011) | Broadly expressed non-muscle cells; particularly mechanically active epithelia, endothelia and migratory cells | **Strong functional evidence**, including genetic loss-of-function and pharmacological perturbation | Many processes use multiple NMII paralogs. A phenotype after broad myosin-II inhibition is not automatically specific to MYH9. |
| MYH9-related disease | Heterozygous pathogenic variants cause autosomal-dominant **MYH9-related disease**: congenital macrothrombocytopenia, giant platelets and NMIIA-positive leukocyte inclusions, with variable later nephropathy, sensorineural hearing loss and cataract. Motor mutations can impair ATPase/actin interaction; rod/tail mutations can destabilize filaments. Defective megakaryocyte migration and neck constriction provide a mechanistic explanation for fewer, oversized platelets. Reported figures include severe bleeding in ~28%, leukocyte inclusions in 42–84%, hearing impairment in ~60% and nephritis in ~25%; prevalence estimates vary from ~3 per million to at least 2.7 per 100,000, consistent with underdiagnosis. (pqac-00000023, pqac-00000025, pqac-00000028, pqac-00000029) | Megakaryocytes/platelets and granulocytes; variably kidney glomeruli, inner ear and lens | **Strong human genetic and clinical evidence**, supported by mutation-specific biochemical mechanisms | Automated counters may miss giant platelets, and MYH9-RD is often mistaken for immune thrombocytopenia. Frequency and complication estimates vary by ascertainment and genotype. |
| 2023–2024 translational developments | Current implementation is primarily **molecular diagnosis and risk-aware care**: blood-smear recognition of giant platelets/Döhle-like inclusions plus MYH9 testing can prevent ineffective corticosteroids, immunosuppression or splenectomy. Cancer studies nominate MYH9 abundance or phosphorylation as biomarkers and implicate MYH9-associated mechanics in drug resistance, but MYH9 can promote or restrain tumors depending on tissue, p53 status and mechanical context. No clinically approved MYH9-specific disease-modifying therapy or validated MYH9-directed oncology treatment was identified. (pqac-00000018, pqac-00000020, pqac-00000021, pqac-00000024, pqac-00000027) | Diagnostic hematology and genetic medicine; preclinical oncology and mechanobiology | **Established for diagnosis; exploratory/preclinical for therapeutic targeting** | Blebbistatin and pathway inhibitors are not selective clinical MYH9 drugs. Context-dependent tumor effects and essential physiological functions make systemic inhibition potentially hazardous. |
