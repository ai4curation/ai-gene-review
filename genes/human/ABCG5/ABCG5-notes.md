## Primary evidence to cache and inspect after seeding

The public literature-search tool exposed the primary abstracts and selected indexed article passages below despite shell DNS failures. Several direct PMC page opens returned a browser challenge, so this readiness pass does not claim exhaustive full-text review. The cited papers are not yet in the local publication cache.

- [PMID:12208867 — Coexpression of ATP-binding cassette proteins ABCG5 and ABCG8 permits their transport to the apical surface](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12208867/). Recombinant epitope-tagged **mouse** proteins in cultured cells associate and undergo glycan maturation when coexpressed. In polarized WIF-B cells, ABCG5 reaches the apical/canalicular membrane with ABCG8; expression alone retains immature protein in the ER. Indexed discussion distinguishes the mature heterodimer from ER-retained homo-oligomeric material. Trafficking and assembly evidence should not be called proof of functional ABCG5 homodimer transport.
- [PMID:16867993 — Sterol transfer by ABCG5 and ABCG8: in vitro assay and reconstitution](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16867993/). Recombinant **mouse** G5/G8 in Sf9 vesicles and reconstituted preparations support ATP-dependent, vanadate-sensitive transfer of cholesterol and sitosterol. The abstract reports minimal corresponding transfer of cholesteryl oleate, phosphatidylcholine or enantiomeric cholesterol. Free-sterol transport should not be broadened into efficient sterol-ester or general phospholipid transport on the strength of this assay.
- [PMID:18402465 — Purification and reconstitution of sterol transfer by native mouse ABCG5 and ABCG8](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18402465/). Purified endogenous **mouse liver** G5/G8 reconstitutes selective, stereospecific sterol transfer. Native electrophoresis, sedimentation and crosslinking support a functional heterodimer, with no higher oligomer detected across the tested catalytic states. G5 palmitoylation was not essential for the tested dimerization, trafficking or biliary secretion endpoints.
- [PMID:21209088 — Sequences in the nonconsensus nucleotide-binding domain of ABCG5/ABCG8 required for sterol transport](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21209088/). Mutagenesis and mouse biliary-sterol assays distinguish the two composite nucleotide-binding sites. A catalytically nonessential site can still be structurally necessary, and selected mutations discriminate cholesterol versus plant-sterol transport. The future review must map the paper's NBD/site nomenclature to each protein before assigning residue- or subunit-specific ATPase claims.
- [PMID:27144356 — Crystal structure of the human sterol transporter ABCG5/ABCG8](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27144356/). A nucleotide-free **human** heterodimer structure establishes the architecture and asymmetric coupling of nucleotide-binding and membrane domains. Structural interpretation should be separated from direct flux measurements.
- [PMID:34404721 — Molecular basis of cholesterol efflux via ABCG subfamily transporters](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34404721/). Human G5/G8 sterol-bound structures identify a cytosolic-leaflet-accessible site and a second transmembrane site. The paper also examines ABCG1; its macrophage-to-HDL pathway must not be assigned to G5/G8 merely because both proteins occur in the article. G5/G8 supports secretion toward bile and gut lumen.
