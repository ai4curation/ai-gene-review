Gene Description

AK2 is a mitochondrial intermembrane-space adenylate kinase that reversibly transfers a phosphate between ATP and AMP to form two ADP molecules. This reaction balances adenine nucleotides and supplies ADP without hydrolyzing ATP to free phosphate. Human AK2A and AK2B are catalytically active isoforms; the activities and localization of all six sequence products are less fully resolved. Biallelic deficiency causes reticular dysgenesis, with severe myeloid and lymphoid defects and sensorineural deafness. Human hematopoietic studies connect the differentiation defect to disrupted nucleotide and metabolic homeostasis. AK2 also has a distinct, experimentally demonstrated interaction with DUSP26 that increases protein-phosphatase activity independently of adenylate-kinase catalysis in recombinant assays and cultured human cells.

Core Functions

Catalyzes reversible phosphate transfer between ATP and AMP, forming two ADP molecules in the forward direction and ATP plus AMP in the reverse direction. The mitochondrial intermembrane-space enzyme thereby balances adenine nucleotides; ATP binding, AMP/ATP metabolism and ADP formation are aspects of this single catalytic unit. Both tested human AK2A and AK2B are active. The compartment assignment includes conserved mammalian localization evidence, whereas human enzyme purification directly establishes catalysis.
