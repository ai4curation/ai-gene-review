Gene Description

BLM is a RecQ-family ATP-dependent DNA helicase that moves with 3-prime-to-5-prime polarity and remodels branched DNA structures during genome maintenance. Its helicase, RecQ C-terminal and HRDC regions recognize and process duplex overhangs, replication forks, Holliday junctions, D-loops and G-quadruplex DNA. In the BLM–TOP3A–RMI1–RMI2 complex, BLM drives convergent branch migration and TOP3A removes the resulting DNA entanglement, promoting non-crossover recombination products. BLM also cooperates with DNA2 and EXO1 during DNA-end resection and with shelterin-associated proteins during telomeric DNA processing. It can anneal complementary DNA strands, an activity that cooperates with unwinding during fork remodeling. Nuclear BLM is enriched at repair-associated chromatin and PML nuclear bodies, with localization changing through the cell cycle and after DNA damage. Loss of BLM causes Bloom syndrome and a marked increase in sister-chromatid exchange and chromosome instability.

Core Functions

BLM couples ATP hydrolysis to 3-prime-to-5-prime DNA strand separation and branched-DNA remodeling. In the BTR complex it supplies the helicase step of double-Holliday-junction dissolution, while TOP3A supplies topoisomerase chemistry. The same DNA motor participates in DNA-end processing, stalled-fork remodeling and telomeric DNA transactions. The recombination-intermediate resolution term denotes participation in BTR dissolution: BLM supplies DNA remodeling and TOP3A supplies transient strand cleavage and rejoining, yielding non-crossover products; it does not assign junction endonuclease activity to BLM.
