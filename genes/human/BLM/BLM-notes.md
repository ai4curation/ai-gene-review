## Scientific assessment, 2026-10-01 UTC

This proposal reviews all 188 seeded source annotations from 192 raw GOA rows. Four raw rows duplicate complete source tuples; the temporary projection accounts for every raw row including evidence, reference, qualifier and WITH/FROM. The 188 source objects are unchanged. No alternative-product list was seeded, and none has been invented. The normal UniProt record, raw GOA and 86 publication/Reactome caches remain byte-identical to the preparation baseline.

BLM is an ATP-dependent RecQ DNA motor with established 3-prime-to-5-prime polarity. The first core describes that motor and its DNA work in homologous recombination repair, fork processing and telomere maintenance. [The original human helicase study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9388193/) establishes polarity, and [selected full-length biochemical and complementation results](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25901030/) directly test ATPase activity, fork unwinding, junction migration, double-Holliday-junction dissolution and sister-chromatid-exchange rescue. TOP3A supplies the topoisomerase chemistry in the BLM–TOP3A–RMI1–RMI2 complex; that chemistry is not assigned to BLM. The second core is DNA/DNA annealing, supported by [the purified catalytic-fragment experiment](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17878217/) and [fork-regression biochemistry](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17115688/). Concentration and construct limits are explicit. These cores summarize existing annotations; no NEW assertion is proposed.

The draft distinguishes enzyme-partner binding from the partner enzyme's catalytic activity. TOP3A binding is refined to DNA topoisomerase binding; RAD51, FANCJ and SUV3 contacts support ATPase binding; the PLK1 contact supports protein kinase binding. [FEN1 biochemistry](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14688284/) explicitly reports direct interaction and helicase-independent stimulation of FEN1 endo- and exonuclease activities, supporting an endodeoxyribonuclease-activator refinement. [The WRN study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11919194/) explicitly reports direct binding and inhibition of WRN exonuclease activity, supporting deoxyribonuclease inhibitor activity. DNA2 is refined to enzyme binding: substrate generation by BLM unwinding is not by itself direct activation of the nuclease.

Other generic interactions remain individually justified non-core records or unresolved source assertions. RPA1, RMI1, shelterin partners and SPIDR are not assigned an invented catalytic class. SPIDR is the scaffold in its study; BLM is not made an adaptor by reversing that relationship. [Selected NABP2/hSSB1 Results](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28506294/) support cellular association but did not detect binding between the purified recombinant proteins. The draft preserves that directness limit. The literal P42677 partner in [PMID16864798](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16864798/) is RPS27, not the abstract's MPS1/TTK. Its exact pair is unresolved, while the separate P53350/PLK1 association is directly described by the abstract. MitoCheck, the HeLa interaction survey and OpenCell pair-level datasets were not inspected and remain undecided rather than being removed.

[Selected irradiation Results](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11309417/) distinguish BLM accumulation during a G2 delay from induction of the checkpoint itself. The checkpoint-signaling assertion is therefore refined to the supported cellular DNA-damage response. The CDC2-regulation assertion remains undecided because its abstract treats BLM phosphorylation as a readout and the complete experiment was unavailable. The precise meiotic lateral-element localization, the partial PRIMPOL-cache interaction record, and the camptothecin-specific BLM/SPIDR experiment also remain unresolved; no wrong-gene claim is inferred from an abstract or incomplete extraction.

[The stem-cell telomere study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27918544/) distinguishes C-circles, T-circles and C-rich overhangs. Its BLM depletion result concerns C-circle abundance; the positive T-circle assay concerns NBS1, and separate XRCC3 experiments concern overhangs. This does not establish universal absence of a BLM role in t-circle formation. The existing TAS assertion remains undecided. [The damaged telomeric D-loop paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2781507/) was abstract-only in the normal cache; selected primary-paper search excerpts were accessible, but direct page access failed and a separate telomeric-G4 binding panel was not recovered. The exact G4 assertion remains undecided, while DNA engagement and D-loop unwinding retain the support actually available.

## Generic protein-binding policy migration, 2026-10-05 UTC

Fifteen `GO:0005515 protein binding` rows were migrated from `KEEP_AS_NON_CORE` to `REMOVE` to follow the repository policy for new and re-reviewed generic binding annotations. Their partner evidence remains recorded in `review.reason`, but no retained GO annotation now keeps the literal generic term solely because the interaction itself is source-supported.

The RPA1, RMI1, shelterin, RAD51D, SPIDR, UIMC1/RAP80 and NABP2/hSSB1 rows still deliberately avoid unsupported partner-class refinements. RPA1 is a DNA-binding cofactor rather than an ATPase or kinase. RMI1 is represented through BTR complex membership rather than an invented BLM adaptor function. TRF1, TRF2, POT1 and SPIDR regulate or recruit BLM in the cited assays rather than showing that BLM activates those nonenzymatic partners. UIMC1/RAP80 binds ubiquitylated BLM via RAP80 UIMs, and the BLM-NABP2 association is cellular but not detectably direct with the purified proteins tested. Removing `GO:0005515` is therefore a specificity decision; it does not assert that those interactions are false.
