# GGCX notes

## 2026-11-05 — Catalysis, products and physiological scope

GGCX directly modifies protein glutamate residues and oxidizes vitamin K hydroquinone; both chemical aspects belong to one catalytic function. Human enzyme assays and recent substrate-bound structures support this function at the ER membrane, with the protein-modifying machinery facing the lumen. A lumenal catalytic domain does not make the whole membrane protein a freely soluble lumen resident ([PMID1749935](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1749935/), [PMID10910912](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10910912/), [PMID39880037](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39880037/)).

Substrate-recognition structures clarify how propeptides and other recognition sequences position glutamate-rich regions. Proposed bicarbonate/proton-transfer mechanisms remain distinct from direct measurement of every step, and epoxidation-only mutant readouts are not automatically site-specific carboxylation assays ([PMID41290650](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41290650/), [PMID41888148](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41888148/)).

The two official natural sequences have 758 and 701 residues; the shorter product deletes positions 15–71, overlapping the first annotated membrane helix. Its activity and physiological contribution remain unresolved. Initiator removal, experimental tags and engineered fusions are separate issues. The historical two-transcript report concerns 3-prime untranslated sequence rather than demonstrating these two protein forms ([PMID9166845](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9166845/)).

Mouse Ggcx deletion directly removes precursor-modifying activity, while bleeding and endocrine consequences depend on which substrates and tissues are affected. The accessible evidence leaves five specific neurotransmitter/bone/testosterone contexts unresolved. These are source-specific uncertainties, not claims that the curated experiments were absent or performed on the wrong gene ([PMID17327402](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17327402/), [PMID25264202](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25264202/), [PMID17073445](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17073445/)).
