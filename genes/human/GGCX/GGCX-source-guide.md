# GGCX source and context guide

GGCX performs protein glutamate carboxylation and vitamin K hydroquinone epoxidation as coupled chemistry at the endoplasmic-reticulum membrane. The [evidence archive](GGCX-evidence.yaml) preserves original source contexts and the distinct sequence and assay records; it is provenance, not an independent experiment.

## Catalysis, recognition and topology

[PMID1749935](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1749935/) links expression of cloned human GGCX to increased microsomal carboxylase activity. Its three-helix prediction and lipoxygenase similarity are historical observations, not additional enzyme functions. [PMID10910912](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10910912/) reports terminal tags in 293 cells and reporter segments in canine microsomes; the authentic abstract supports amino-terminal cytoplasmic and carboxyl-terminal lumenal orientation. Neither original Methods section was recovered.

Selected full text of [PMID39880037](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39880037/) separates full-length human enzyme from an engineered membrane-domain/BRIL fusion and directly tests carboxylation and vitamin K epoxidation. The nine-helix fold includes membrane-associated/reentrant structure; it should not be simplified into nine independent full bilayer crossings. [PMID41290650](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41290650/) supplies human recognition and biochemical evidence for a bicarbonate-linked model. [PMID41888148](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41888148/) examines five human substrates with separate carboxylation and epoxidation assays. An interface mutant's epoxide readout is not automatically a direct measurement of every carboxylated substrate residue. Proton transfer, substrate order, gas entry and unidentified peptide-like densities retain the papers' stated inferential limits.

The selected original [PMID12963724](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12963724/) supports a structural Cys99–Cys450 disulfide, not free catalytic thiols; its topology diagram derives from earlier work. [PMID11570873](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11570873/) remains abstract-only, with enzyme species/construct unverified. [PMID39880952](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39880952/) has partial primary access: publisher abstract/extended captions, supplement and reporting summary, but not main Methods/Results. Modeled propeptides or a C-terminal peptide are distinct from solved complexes and demonstrated self-carboxylation.
