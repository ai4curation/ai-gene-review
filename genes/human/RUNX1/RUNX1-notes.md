# RUNX1 review notes

Deep research could not be generated in this worktree: Falcon was unavailable, the Perplexity fallback was unavailable, and the Claude Code provider was not logged in. I therefore used the cached UniProt record, GOA-seeded publications, Reactome records, and the PTHR11950 PAINT slice directly.

RUNX1 is a Runt-domain sequence-specific DNA-binding transcription factor. UniProt summarizes the canonical mechanism as heterodimerization with CBFB/core-binding factor, Runt-domain recognition of TGTGGT/TGCGGT regulatory motifs, and CBFB allosteric enhancement of RUNX DNA binding. The structural RUNX1:CBFB paper reports the AML1 Runt-domain:CBFB crystal structure [PMID:10856244, "We report the 2.6 A resolution crystal structure of the complex between the AML1 Runt domain and CBFbeta"], and the GM-CSF enhancer paper shows a human enhancer occupied by a RUNX1/CBFB core-binding-factor complex [PMID:20483917, "Runx1 binds as a dimeric complex to overlapping Runx1 sites within a palindromic element in the human GM-CSF enhancer."].

The most specific core molecular terms are `GO:0000978` RNA polymerase II cis-regulatory region sequence-specific DNA binding and `GO:0000981` DNA-binding transcription factor activity, RNA polymerase II-specific. Generic InterPro `DNA binding`, `DNA-binding transcription factor activity`, and `regulation of DNA-templated transcription` rows should be modified to these Pol II-specific terms. The InterPro `ATP binding` row is a domain-mapping artifact: the Runt domain is not an ATP-dependent catalytic or motor domain.

RUNX1 regulatory outputs are broad and context-dependent. The CD34 study directly supports RUNX1 binding to a distal regulatory element that is required for human CD34 expression in LT-HSCs [PMID:21873977, "Targeted mutagenesis of RUNX binding sites leads to perturbation of this interaction and decreased human CD34 expression in LT-HSCs."]. T-cell rows are also plausible downstream transcriptional programs: the Th-POK paper reports that a Runx-binding sequence in the Th-POK locus is needed for repression and CD8 lineage development [PMID:18258917, "A Runx-binding sequence within the Th-POK locus acts as a transcriptional silencer that is essential for Th-POK repression and for development of CD8+ T cells."]. These outputs remain non-core relative to the shared core DNA-binding transcription-factor activity.

The PTHR11950 IBA rows for ossification, chondrocyte differentiation, neuron differentiation, hemopoiesis, and regulation of cell differentiation all trace to PAINT node PTN000237028. They are kept as non-core inherited RUNX developmental programs rather than promoted into the RUNX1 core function, whose direct biochemical role is transcriptional regulation at chromatin.

The 23 bare `GO:0005515` protein-binding rows are all uninformative interaction capture. Even where the interactions themselves are biologically real, they do not identify a more specific RUNX1 molecular activity and should be removed under the current protein-binding policy.
