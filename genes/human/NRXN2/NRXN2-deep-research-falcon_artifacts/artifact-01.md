| Category | NRXN2 annotation | Evidence and qualification |
|---|---|---|
| Gene and protein identity | **Gene:** *NRXN2* (synonym *KIAA0921*); **protein:** neurexin-2/neurexin-2α; **UniProt:** **Q9P2S2**; **organism:** *Homo sapiens*. | The identity is concordant with literature describing human *NRXN2* as one of the three mammalian neurexin genes; no symbol ambiguity was detected. (pqac-00000000, pqac-00000046) |
| Protein family | Neurexin-family neuronal cell-adhesion and synapse-organizing protein; a type-I, single-pass transmembrane glycoprotein. | Neurexins connect extracellular synaptic-recognition complexes to intracellular presynaptic scaffolds. (pqac-00000002, pqac-00000005) |
| Primary molecular function | A predominantly presynaptic, nonenzymatic adhesion receptor and molecular scaffold. Its extracellular region recognizes ligands across the synaptic cleft, while its cytoplasmic tail couples those interactions to presynaptic structural and signaling machinery. It therefore specifies and regulates synapse number, identity, release properties, and connectivity rather than catalyzing a reaction or transporting a substrate. (pqac-00000002, pqac-00000005, pqac-00000009) | The strongest NRXN2-specific physiological evidence indicates that it continuously restrains excitatory hippocampal synapse assembly and presynaptic release probability. (pqac-00000024, pqac-00000035) |
| Isoforms and structural organization | Separate promoters generate long **α** and shorter **β** isoforms. NRXN2α has six extracellular laminin/neurexin/sex-hormone-binding-globulin (**LNS/LG**) domains and three interspersed **EGF-like** domains; NRXN2β has a short isoform-specific N terminus followed principally by the shared LNS6 module. Both contain a glycosylated juxtamembrane stalk, one transmembrane helix, and a short cytoplasmic tail with Protein 4.1 interaction sites and a C-terminal PDZ-binding motif. (pqac-00000046, pqac-00000048) | This architecture agrees with the supplied InterPro annotations: ConA-like/Laminin-G, EGF, neurexin-like, and neurexin-related C-terminal regions. LNS domains provide calcium- and ligand-binding surfaces; the EGF modules contribute flexible hinges. (pqac-00000002, pqac-00000052) |
| Molecular diversification | Alternative promoter use, alternative transcription, and extensive splicing create context-specific ligand-binding repertoires. NRXN2α is described as using five principal splice sites (SS1–SS5) and lacks the SS6 element found in NRXN1/3; splice variation at SS4 modifies ligand preferences, including β-NRXN2 affinity for neuroligins. (pqac-00000022, pqac-00000046, pqac-00000048) | Splice-state effects should not automatically be extrapolated among paralogs: unlike NRXN1/3, experimentally forcing NRXN2 SS4 inclusion did not reproduce changes in AMPAR- or NMDAR-mediated currents. (pqac-00000022, pqac-00000036) |
| Subcellular localization and functional site | Predominantly localized to the **presynaptic plasma membrane** of neuronal terminals at excitatory and inhibitory synapses. Its ectodomain projects into the synaptic cleft to bind postsynaptic ligands; its short tail remains cytoplasmic and associates with presynaptic adaptors. A smaller postsynaptic α-neurexin pool is possible at the family level but is not firmly resolved for human NRXN2. (pqac-00000008, pqac-00000009, pqac-00000011) | Developmental human-brain observations associate NRXN2 with axon-growth and presynaptic-terminal markers in early cortical layers, although much mechanistic localization evidence comes from rodent neurons. (pqac-00000001, pqac-00000022) |
| Key extracellular binding partners | **IgSF21:** highly selective for NRXN2α; postsynaptic IgSF21 Ig1 binds presynaptic NRXN2α LNS1 with approximately **5.7 nM** affinity and 1:1 stoichiometry, promoting dendritic GABAergic presynaptic differentiation. **Neuroligins:** β-NRXN2 binds neuroligins with nanomolar affinity through LNS6, modulated by SS4 and calcium-dependent surfaces. **LRRTM1/2:** bind the LNS6/heparan-sulfate region in a splice-dependent manner. **Cerebellins:** favor SS4-positive neurexin states. Other family-level candidate ligands include neurexophilins, dystroglycan, calsyntenin-3, latrophilins, and GABA-receptor-related molecules, but these should not all be assumed to be NRXN2-specific. (pqac-00000014, pqac-00000016, pqac-00000062, pqac-00000065, pqac-00000068) | The IgSF21 interaction is the most clearly established NRXN2α-selective ligand relationship: the modeled contact involves the IgSF21 Ig1 β-sheet and NRXN2α LNS1 β11–β12 loop, is calcium-independent, and is supported by interface mutagenesis. (pqac-00000062, pqac-00000063) |
| Intracellular partners | The conserved neurexin tail can bind **CASK, Mint proteins, syntenin, Protein 4.1**, and reported synaptic-vesicle-associated proteins, linking trans-synaptic recognition to cytoskeletal organization and release machinery. (pqac-00000005, pqac-00000018) | These intracellular interactions are well established for the neurexin family, but the degree to which each is specifically required for human NRXN2 signaling remains incompletely resolved. |
| Unique role relative to NRXN1/NRXN3 | In mouse hippocampus, NRXN2 acts as an **anti-synaptogenic or restrictive organizer**, whereas NRXN1 and NRXN3 are more commonly synapse-supporting. Constitutive or neuron-selective *Nrxn2* loss nearly doubled excitatory CA3→CA1 connection strength and excitatory CA1 synapse density and increased release probability, without a detected effect on inhibitory synapses. Adult deletion also increased connectivity, indicating an ongoing role beyond development. (pqac-00000021, pqac-00000024, pqac-00000035) | The exact molecular mechanism producing this opposing output remains unresolved. Sequence divergence—especially in LNS1—and the smaller gene/splice architecture are plausible contributors, but are not yet a complete mechanistic explanation. (pqac-00000025, pqac-00000027) |
| Excitatory-synapse function | Restricts hippocampal excitatory synapse number and strength, controls release probability, and contributes to glutamatergic receptor regulation. Earlier Nrxn2α models also reported altered NMDAR-mediated transmission and facilitation, demonstrating that phenotypes depend on isoform, region, developmental timing, and deletion strategy. (pqac-00000035, pqac-00000036, pqac-00000059) | The most direct quantitative findings are an approximately twofold increase in CA1 excitatory synapse density and nearly doubled CA3→CA1 strength after deletion. (pqac-00000024, pqac-00000035) |
| Inhibitory-synapse function | NRXN2α serves as the presynaptic receptor for postsynaptic IgSF21 and is required for IgSF21-induced **dendritic GABAergic presynaptic differentiation**. This is compartment-specific: neuroligin-2–neurexin signaling acts more strongly at perisomatic inhibitory synapses. NRXN2 loss alone does not uniformly disrupt basal inhibitory transmission, so its GABAergic role is ligand-, compartment-, and circuit-dependent. (pqac-00000058, pqac-00000059, pqac-00000065) | IgSF21 presented by contacting cells induced VGAT accumulation on wild-type but not Nrxn2α-null axons, providing direct functional evidence for this trans-synaptic pathway. (pqac-00000065) |
| Major signaling pathways | **IgSF21–NRXN2α–JNK axis:** c-Jun N-terminal kinase activity is required for formation of new IgSF21-induced presynaptic specializations but not maintenance of established synapses; Nrxn2α loss alters synaptic JNK activity in a sex-dependent manner. **Neuroligin-2–neurexin signaling:** also requires JNK and, unlike IgSF21 signaling, additionally depends on CaMKII and Src. **Presynaptic organization:** family-level mechanisms connect neurexins to calcium-channel positioning, calcium-triggered vesicle release, cytoskeletal adaptors, and postsynaptic receptor/scaffold organization. (pqac-00000028, pqac-00000031) | JNK coupling is experimentally supported in cultured/rodent systems, but the direction and intervening molecular components between presynaptic NRXN2 and postsynaptic pathway activation are not completely defined. |
| Biological-process and circuit consequences | Maintains appropriate excitation/inhibition and synaptic connectivity; influences hippocampal circuit excitability, cognitive flexibility, social behavior, and action–outcome updating. Conditional cortical/hippocampal loss increased network activity and produced spontaneous recurrent seizures in mice; targeted deletion also impaired reversal learning and adaptation to changed action–outcome contingencies. (pqac-00000037, pqac-00000038, pqac-00000054) | These are mechanistic animal-model outcomes rather than proof that identical behavioral effects follow every human NRXN2 variant. |
| Human clinical associations | Rare deletions, truncating variants, and other NRXN2 alterations have been reported in autism spectrum disorder, developmental delay/intellectual disability, epilepsy, and occasionally schizophrenia-related cohorts. A truncating variant impaired membrane anchoring and abolished LRRTM2/NLGN2 binding. A severe early epileptic-encephalopathy case carried variants in both NRXN1α and NRXN2α, preventing attribution to NRXN2 alone. (pqac-00000027, pqac-00000040, pqac-00000043, pqac-00000044) | Clinical evidence remains sparse relative to NRXN1, with variable inheritance and limited penetrance data. Associations support NRXN2 as a candidate neurodevelopmental-risk gene, not a single-gene diagnostic explanation in most patients. (pqac-00000043, pqac-00000045) |
| Current applications and translational status | Present applications are primarily **research and genomic interpretation**: conditional knockout animals map circuit-specific functions; ligand-binding and mutagenesis assays test molecular mechanisms; and patient sequencing identifies candidate variants. NRXN2/IgSF21 and splice-specific complexes may eventually provide therapeutic or biomarker hypotheses, but no validated NRXN2-directed drug, clinical biomarker, or approved therapy was identified in the reviewed evidence. (pqac-00000054, pqac-00000062, pqac-00000065) | Expert reviews emphasize the need for standardized animal and human iPSC models and more direct study of NRXN2/NRXN3 because they remain substantially less characterized than NRXN1. |
