# Comprehensive Research Report: NRXN2 (Neurexin-2) Gene Function and Annotation

## Gene Identity Verification

The human NRXN2 gene (UniProt: Q9P2S2) encodes neurexin-2, a member of the neurexin family of presynaptic cell adhesion molecules (cuttler2021emergingevidenceimplicating pages 2-3, khoja2023advancesinneurexin pages 3-4). The gene is also known by the synonym KIAA0921 (chofflet2025alphaneurexinsinhealth pages 2-3). All retrieved scientific literature confirms that NRXN2 refers specifically to human neurexin-2, with consistent descriptions matching the UniProt annotation: a protein belonging to the neurexin family, containing laminin-G (LNS) and EGF-like domains, and functioning in synaptic organization (gomez2021neurexinsmolecularcodes pages 4-6, cuttler2021emergingevidenceimplicating pages 2-3). There is no ambiguity with other genes sharing this symbol.

## Primary Molecular Function

### Core Function: A Unique Anti-Synaptogenic Organizer

NRXN2 encodes a presynaptic transmembrane cell-adhesion molecule that functions fundamentally differently from its paralogues NRXN1 and NRXN3 (lin2023neurexin2aninhibitory pages 1-2). Rather than being a substrate-specific enzyme or transporter, NRXN2 acts as a **nonenzymatic molecular scaffold and synaptic organizer** whose primary function is to **restrict and regulate synapse formation** (gomez2021neurexinsmolecularcodes pages 4-6, li2022modulationoftranssynaptic pages 2-4).

A landmark 2023 study published in Science Advances by Lin et al. established that NRXN2 functions as an "inhibitory neurexin" that actively restricts excitatory synapse formation in the hippocampus (lin2023neurexin2aninhibitory pages 1-2). This represents a unique and opposing functional role compared to NRXN1 and NRXN3, which generally promote synapse assembly (chofflet2024structuralandfunctional pages 16-17). Specifically, constitutive or neuron-specific deletion of Nrxn2 in mice nearly doubled the strength of excitatory CA3→CA1 synaptic connections, markedly increased presynaptic release probability, and approximately doubled the density of excitatory synapses in the hippocampal CA1 region (lin2023neurexin2aninhibitory pages 1-2). Critically, these effects were selective for excitatory synapses, with no detectable changes in inhibitory synapses (lin2023neurexin2aninhibitory pages 1-2).

This restrictive function persists throughout life: hippocampal NRXN2 deletion in mature adult mice also increased synaptic connectivity, demonstrating that NRXN2 continuously controls synaptic circuit dynamics rather than functioning only during development (lin2023neurexin2aninhibitory pages 1-2). The behavioral consequence of this dysregulation includes impaired cognitive flexibility in spatial memory tasks (lin2023neurexin2aninhibitory pages 1-2, khoja2024conditionaldeletionof pages 11-12).

### Mechanism of Action

NRXN2's molecular function is mediated through trans-synaptic adhesion complexes. Its extracellular domain recognizes and binds specific postsynaptic ligands across the synaptic cleft, while its short cytoplasmic tail couples these extracellular interactions to presynaptic structural and signaling machinery (gomez2021neurexinsmolecularcodes pages 4-6, li2022modulationoftranssynaptic pages 2-4, cuttler2021emergingevidenceimplicating pages 2-3). Through these bidirectional connections, NRXN2 specifies synapse number, identity, release properties, and overall circuit connectivity (gomez2021neurexinsmolecularcodes pages 4-6, chofflet2024structuralandfunctional pages 1-2).

Unlike enzymes with defined substrates, NRXN2's "substrate specificity" is determined by its binding partner repertoire, which is extensively regulated by alternative splicing (gomez2021neurexinsmolecularcodes pages 4-6). The molecular basis for NRXN2's unique anti-synaptogenic activity likely relates to its substantial sequence divergence from NRXN1 and NRXN3, particularly in the LNS1 domain, combined with its smaller gene size and distinct splice architecture (chofflet2024structuralandfunctional pages 16-17, khoja2023advancesinneurexin pages 3-4).

## Structural Organization and Domains

### Gene and Isoform Architecture

The human NRXN2 gene spans approximately 106.4 kilobases and contains 23 exons (chofflet2025alphaneurexinsinhealth pages 2-3). Like other neurexin genes, NRXN2 employs two independent promoters to generate structurally distinct protein isoforms (chofflet2025alphaneurexinsinhealth pages 2-3, cuttler2021emergingevidenceimplicating pages 2-3):

1. **α-Neurexin-2 (NRXN2α)**: The longer isoform transcribed from an upstream promoter
2. **β-Neurexin-2 (NRXN2β)**: The shorter isoform transcribed from a downstream promoter located after exon 17 (liu2026rolesofnrxn1 pages 1-2)

Both isoforms are type I single-pass transmembrane proteins but differ dramatically in their extracellular architecture (chofflet2025alphaneurexinsinhealth pages 2-3, chofflet2025alphaneurexinsinhealth pages 1-2).

### Domain Organization

**NRXN2α Structure:**
The alpha isoform contains an elaborate extracellular region comprising six laminin-neurexin-sex hormone-binding globulin (LNS) domains (LNS1–LNS6) interspersed with three epidermal growth factor-like (EGF) domains (EGF1–EGF3) (chofflet2025alphaneurexinsinhealth pages 2-3, gomez2021neurexinsmolecularcodes pages 4-6, chofflet2025alphaneurexinsinhealth pages 6-7). The LNS domains form β-sandwich structures containing calcium- and ligand-binding sites (gomez2021neurexinsmolecularcodes pages 4-6). The EGF-like domains provide flexible hinges between LNS modules, creating a structurally adaptable extracellular scaffold capable of engaging multiple synaptic partners (chofflet2025alphaneurexinsinhealth pages 2-3, chofflet2025alphaneurexinsinhealth pages 1-2).

**NRXN2β Structure:**
The beta isoform has a short, unique N-terminal histidine-rich domain followed by only the LNS6 module (corresponding to the sixth LNS domain of α-neurexin), thus lacking the upstream LNS1–5 and EGF domains (chofflet2025alphaneurexinsinhealth pages 2-3, cuttler2021emergingevidenceimplicating pages 3-3).

**Conserved Regions:**
Both isoforms share:
- An extensively O-glycosylated juxtamembrane stalk region (gomez2021neurexinsmolecularcodes pages 4-6)
- A single transmembrane helix (chofflet2025alphaneurexinsinhealth pages 2-3)
- A short cytoplasmic tail containing protein 4.1 interaction sites and a C-terminal PDZ-binding motif (chofflet2025alphaneurexinsinhealth pages 2-3, gomez2021neurexinsmolecularcodes pages 4-6, chofflet2025alphaneurexinsinhealth pages 6-7)

### Alternative Splicing and Molecular Diversity

NRXN2 undergoes extensive alternative splicing at five principal splice sites (SS1–SS5) in the alpha isoform (chofflet2025alphaneurexinsinhealth pages 2-3, gomez2021neurexinsmolecularcodes pages 4-6). Notably, NRXN2 lacks the SS6 splice element found in NRXN1 and NRXN3, representing a key structural distinction (khoja2023advancesinneurexin pages 3-4, chofflet2025alphaneurexinsinhealth pages 6-7). Alternative splicing at SS4 (also termed AS4) is particularly important for modulating ligand-binding preferences: inclusion of the SS4 insert diminishes β-NRXN2's affinity for neuroligins while enabling binding to cerebellins (gomez2021neurexinsmolecularcodes pages 4-6).

Importantly, the functional consequences of splicing are not identical across neurexin family members. Unlike NRXN1 and NRXN3, where SS4 inclusion/exclusion affects AMPA and NMDA receptor regulation, the same splice variation in NRXN2 does not alter AMPAR or NMDAR function in the same way (khoja2023advancesinneurexin pages 3-4, khoja2023advancesinneurexin pages 4-5). This splice-isoform specificity is also activity-dependent: neuronal activation and calcium signaling promote exclusion of the Nrxn2 SS3 insert (liu2026rolesofnrxn1 pages 3-4).

## Subcellular Localization and Functional Sites

NRXN2 is predominantly localized to the **presynaptic plasma membrane** at neuronal terminals (cuttler2021emergingevidenceimplicating pages 3-3, cuttler2021emergingevidenceimplicating pages 2-3). As a type I transmembrane protein, NRXN2 spans the neuronal membrane with its large extracellular domain projecting into the synaptic cleft to mediate trans-synaptic interactions, while its cytoplasmic tail remains intracellular and associates with presynaptic scaffolding proteins (cuttler2021emergingevidenceimplicating pages 2-3, chofflet2025alphaneurexinsinhealth pages 2-3).

NRXN2 carries out its function at both **excitatory and inhibitory synapses**, though with distinct roles at each (cuttler2021emergingevidenceimplicating pages 3-3, chofflet2024structuralandfunctional pages 1-2). During early human cortical development, NRXN2 is associated with axon-growth markers and presynaptic-terminal markers in proliferative cortical layers, suggesting a particularly important developmental role in early cortical synaptogenesis (khoja2023advancesinneurexin pages 3-4).

A minor postsynaptic pool of α-neurexins has been detected at the neurexin family level, particularly for regulating postsynaptic NMDA receptor function (chofflet2025alphaneurexinsinhealth pages 3-4), though this has not been definitively established for human NRXN2 specifically.

### Key Binding Partner: IgSF21

The most clearly established NRXN2-specific interaction is with **IgSF21 (Immunoglobulin Superfamily Member 21)**, a postsynaptic adhesion molecule (chofflet2024structuralandfunctional pages 16-17, chofflet2024structuralandfunctional pages 7-9). This interaction is highly selective: IgSF21 binds NRXN2α but not NRXN1α, NRXN3α, or tested β-neurexin isoforms (chofflet2024structuralandfunctional pages 7-9, chofflet2024structuralandfunctional pages 6-7).

**Structural Basis:**
Recent structural modeling (2024) using AlphaFold2 combined with extensive mutagenesis revealed that the IgSF21 Ig1 immunoglobulin domain binds to the NRXN2α LNS1 domain (chofflet2024structuralandfunctional pages 16-17, chofflet2024structuralandfunctional pages 9-10). The predicted interface involves the IgSF21 Ig1 β-sheet region (residues R53–D76) contacting the NRXN2α LNS1 β11–β12 loop (residues L161–Y171) through an extensive hydrogen-bonding network and hydrophobic contacts (chofflet2024structuralandfunctional pages 9-10). The interaction is calcium-independent and occurs with high affinity (KD ≈ 5.7 nM) in a 1:1 stoichiometry (chofflet2024structuralandfunctional pages 16-17, chofflet2024structuralandfunctional pages 6-7).

**Functional Consequence:**
The IgSF21–NRXN2α complex is required for IgSF21-induced GABAergic presynaptic differentiation, specifically at dendritic (rather than perisomatic) inhibitory synapses (chofflet2024structuralandfunctional pages 1-2, chofflet2024structuralandfunctional pages 7-9). When IgSF21 is presented on contacting cells, it induces accumulation of the inhibitory vesicle marker VGAT on wild-type axons but not on Nrxn2α-knockout axons, demonstrating that NRXN2α is absolutely required for this synaptogenic activity (chofflet2024structuralandfunctional pages 7-9).

### Neuroligins

β-NRXN2 binds all neuroligins with nanomolar affinity through its LNS6 domain (gomez2021neurexinsmolecularcodes pages 4-6). This binding is modulated by alternative splicing at SS4 and involves calcium-dependent surfaces on the neurexin hypervariable region (gomez2021neurexinsmolecularcodes pages 4-6). Neuroligin-2 (NLGN2) is particularly important for organizing inhibitory postsynaptic scaffolds, primarily at perisomatic inhibitory synapses (chofflet2024structuralandfunctional pages 1-2, boxer2022neurexinsandtheir pages 9-10). A pathogenic NRXN2 truncation that prevented membrane anchoring also abolished binding to both NLGN2 and LRRTM2, demonstrating the functional importance of these interactions (khoja2023advancesinneurexin pages 3-4).

### Other Ligands

Additional binding partners include **LRRTM1/2** (leucine-rich repeat transmembrane proteins), which bind the LNS6 region in a splice-dependent manner favoring SS4-negative forms (gomez2021neurexinsmolecularcodes pages 4-6, li2022modulationoftranssynaptic pages 2-4); **cerebellins**, which bind SS4-positive neurexin states (gomez2021neurexinsmolecularcodes pages 4-6); and family-level ligands including **neurexophilins**, **dystroglycan**, **calsyntenin-3**, and **latrophilins** (li2022modulationoftranssynaptic pages 2-4, chofflet2025alphaneurexinsinhealth pages 6-7, boxer2022neurexinsandtheir pages 8-9), though NRXN2-specific evidence for these remains limited.

## Signaling Pathways and Biochemical Mechanisms

### JNK-Mediated Signaling Pathway

A critical recent discovery (2024) identified the **c-Jun N-terminal kinase (JNK) pathway** as essential for NRXN2α-mediated inhibitory synapse organization (chofflet2024structuralandfunctional pages 1-2). Both the IgSF21–NRXN2α complex and the neuroligin-2–neurexin complex require JNK activity for their synaptogenic effects (chofflet2024structuralandfunctional pages 1-2). Pharmacological JNK inhibition reduces formation of new presynaptic specializations without disrupting already-established synapses, indicating that JNK functions specifically in synapse development rather than maintenance (chofflet2024structuralandfunctional pages 1-2).

Importantly, loss of NRXN2α alters synaptic JNK activity in a sex-specific manner, supporting functional linkage between neurexin signaling and JNK pathways (chofflet2024structuralandfunctional pages 1-2). However, different neurexin ligands engage distinct downstream requirements: while both IgSF21–NRXN2α and neuroligin-2–neurexin signaling require JNK, neuroligin-2 additionally depends on CaMKII and Src kinase activity, whereas IgSF21 does not (chofflet2024structuralandfunctional pages 1-2). These differential requirements may contribute to the distinct synaptic compartment specificities of these complexes.

### Regulation of Neurotransmitter Release and Calcium Signaling

At the presynaptic level, NRXN2 regulates calcium-dependent neurotransmitter release mechanisms (khoja2023advancesinneurexin pages 5-6, juanes2026neurexin3βis pages 18-21, tromp2021neurexinsinautism pages 5-6). The neurexin family, including NRXN2, helps organize voltage-gated calcium channels at the presynaptic active zone, coupling calcium entry to synaptic vesicle exocytosis (juanes2026neurexin3βis pages 18-21, li2022modulationoftranssynaptic pages 2-4). NRXN2 deletion leads to altered release probability at hippocampal excitatory synapses, with neuron-specific deletion producing increased release probability in CA1 (khoja2023advancesinneurexin pages 5-6, lin2023neurexin2aninhibitory pages 1-2).

### Postsynaptic Receptor Regulation

NRXN2α plays a particularly important role in regulating **NMDA receptor (NMDAR) function** at excitatory synapses (chofflet2025alphaneurexinsinhealth pages 5-6). Deletion of NRXN2α selectively reduces NMDAR-mediated transmission, shortens NMDAR-EPSC decay, and alters paired-pulse facilitation (khoja2023advancesinneurexin pages 4-5, chofflet2025alphaneurexinsinhealth pages 5-6). Importantly, additional deletion of NRXN2β does not substantially worsen these phenotypes, identifying NRXN2α as the principal isoform for NMDAR regulation (chofflet2025alphaneurexinsinhealth pages 5-6).

### Intracellular Scaffolding

The NRXN2 cytoplasmic tail couples extracellular adhesion to intracellular machinery through interactions with **CASK, Mint proteins, protein 4.1, and syntenin** (li2022modulationoftranssynaptic pages 2-4, boxer2022neurexinsandtheir pages 8-9). These interactions link NRXN2 to cytoskeletal elements, presynaptic scaffolds, synaptic vesicles, and the neurotransmitter release machinery (li2022modulationoftranssynaptic pages 2-4, cuttler2021emergingevidenceimplicating pages 2-3).

## Circuit-Level Functions and Behavioral Consequences

### Regulation of Hippocampal Circuits

NRXN2 plays a critical role in maintaining appropriate hippocampal circuit connectivity and excitation/inhibition balance. Conditional deletion in the hippocampus and cortex (Emx1-Nrxn2 cKO) increased hippocampal network activity, measured by higher frequencies of spontaneous excitatory and inhibitory postsynaptic currents (khoja2023advancesinneurexin pages 5-6). This network hyperexcitability occurred without detectable changes in neurotransmitter release probability or the relative contributions of AMPA and NMDA receptors, suggesting circuit-level reorganization (khoja2023advancesinneurexin pages 5-6).

Most strikingly, these circuit alterations manifested as **spontaneous recurrent seizures** in Nrxn2 conditional knockout mice, representing the first direct evidence that NRXN2 loss can produce epileptiform activity (lin2023neurexin2aninhibitory pages 1-2, khoja2023advancesinneurexin pages 5-6).

### Behavioral Flexibility and Habit Formation

Recent studies (2023-2024) have established that NRXN2 is essential for **behavioral flexibility** and adaptive updating of action–outcome contingencies (khoja2024conditionaldeletionof pages 11-12). Conditional deletion of Nrxn2 impaired reversal learning and the ability to disengage from previously established behavioral patterns (khoja2024conditionaldeletionof pages 11-12). These mice showed difficulty updating their behavior when action–outcome relationships changed, manifesting as:

1. Impaired goal-directed action control in instrumental learning paradigms (khoja2024conditionaldeletionof pages 11-12)
2. Enhanced habitual responding and reduced sensitivity to outcome devaluation (khoja2024conditionaldeletionof pages 11-12)
3. Deficits in reversal learning in water T-maze tasks (khoja2024conditionaldeletionof pages 11-12)

The neural basis appears to involve altered cortical-striatal circuits, particularly the cingulate cortex (Cg1/M2) projections to dorsomedial striatum, which shift behavior toward habitual systems (khoja2024conditionaldeletionof pages 11-12).

### Social Behavior

Both homozygous and heterozygous Nrxn2α knockout mice show diminished social approach, reduced responses to social novelty, and impaired recognition of novel objects (gomez2021neurexinsmolecularcodes pages 10-12). These behavioral phenotypes align with the gene's association with autism spectrum disorder.

## Clinical Relevance and Disease Associations

### Human Genetic Associations

NRXN2 mutations and variants have been identified in multiple neurodevelopmental and neuropsychiatric disorders, though the clinical evidence base remains more limited than for NRXN1 (chofflet2025alphaneurexinsinhealth pages 11-12, tromp2021neurexinsinautism pages 11-11):

**Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD):** Truncating variants in NRXN2 have been reported in ASD patients (tromp2021neurexinsinautism pages 11-11). A functionally characterized truncating mutation prevented NRXN2 from anchoring in the plasma membrane and abolished its binding to the postsynaptic partners LRRTM2 and NLGN2 (khoja2023advancesinneurexin pages 3-4). This demonstrates how loss-of-function mutations disrupt NRXN2's critical trans-synaptic adhesion role.

**Epilepsy:** A severe case of early-onset epileptic encephalopathy with fatal respiratory failure involved heterozygous missense variants in both NRXN1α and NRXN2α (digenic inheritance) (chofflet2025alphaneurexinsinhealth pages 11-12, tromp2021neurexinsinautism pages 11-11, khoja2023advancesinneurexin pages 10-10). The NRXN2α variant was inherited from a father with febrile seizures, suggesting potential pathogenicity, though the digenic nature prevents clear attribution to NRXN2 alone.

**Intellectual Disability and Developmental Delay:** NRXN2 deletions and mutations are associated with intellectual disability and neurodevelopmental delay (khoja2023advancesinneurexin pages 3-4, tromp2021neurexinsinautism pages 11-11).

**Schizophrenia:** While NRXN1 has strong schizophrenia associations, current evidence does not firmly establish NRXN2 as an independent schizophrenia risk gene, though some studies have reported associations (chofflet2025alphaneurexinsinhealth pages 11-12, tromp2021neurexinsinautism pages 11-11).

### Mechanistic Insights from Human Variants

The mechanistic consequences of NRXN2 disruption include:
- Loss of membrane localization (khoja2023advancesinneurexin pages 3-4)
- Abolished trans-synaptic ligand binding (khoja2023advancesinneurexin pages 3-4)
- Disrupted excitation/inhibition balance (lin2023neurexin2aninhibitory pages 1-2, khoja2023advancesinneurexin pages 5-6)
- Altered synaptic connectivity and density (lin2023neurexin2aninhibitory pages 1-2, khoja2023advancesinneurexin pages 5-6)
- Network hyperexcitability and seizure susceptibility (khoja2023advancesinneurexin pages 5-6)

## Recent Developments (2023-2024)

### Major Recent Discoveries

1. **Anti-Synaptogenic Function** (Lin et al., Science Advances, 2023): Established NRXN2 as uniquely restrictive for excitatory synapse formation, with nearly 2-fold increases in hippocampal synapse density and strength upon deletion (lin2023neurexin2aninhibitory pages 1-2).

2. **IgSF21–NRXN2α Structural Complex** (Chofflet et al., Frontiers in Molecular Neuroscience, 2024): First detailed structural characterization of the NRXN2α–IgSF21 binding interface using AlphaFold2 modeling and extensive mutagenesis (chofflet2024structuralandfunctional pages 16-17, chofflet2024structuralandfunctional pages 9-10). Identified the LNS1–Ig1 domain interaction as the structural basis for selective GABAergic synapse organization.

3. **JNK Signaling Pathway** (Chofflet et al., 2024): Discovery that IgSF21–NRXN2α synaptogenesis requires JNK-mediated signaling, with sex-specific alterations in synaptic JNK activity following NRXN2 loss (chofflet2024structuralandfunctional pages 1-2).

4. **Behavioral Flexibility Deficits** (Khoja & Chen, Scientific Reports, 2024): Demonstrated that NRXN2 deletion impairs behavioral flexibility and action–outcome updating, linking molecular mechanisms to complex cognitive functions (khoja2024conditionaldeletionof pages 11-12).

5. **Seizure Susceptibility** (Haile et al., 2023): First direct evidence that conditional NRXN2 deletion produces spontaneous recurrent seizures, establishing a causal link to epilepsy (khoja2023advancesinneurexin pages 5-6).

### Emerging Research Directions

Recent reviews emphasize several critical gaps and opportunities (chofflet2025alphaneurexinsinhealth pages 11-12, khoja2024conditionaldeletionof pages 11-12):
- Need for standardized animal and human iPSC models
- Expansion of research beyond NRXN1 to better characterize NRXN2 and NRXN3
- Investigation of sex-specific effects and mechanisms
- Development of splice-isoform-specific therapeutic strategies
- Understanding cell-type-specific NRXN2 functions across brain regions

## Current Applications and Translational Potential

### Research Applications

NRXN2 is currently employed as:
1. **Disease Model Systems:** Conditional knockout mice map circuit-specific functions and model neurodevelopmental disorders (khoja2024conditionaldeletionof pages 11-12)
2. **Molecular Probes:** Ligand-binding assays and mutagenesis experiments elucidate trans-synaptic organizing mechanisms (chofflet2024structuralandfunctional pages 16-17, chofflet2024structuralandfunctional pages 7-9)
3. **Genomic Interpretation:** Patient sequencing identifies candidate pathogenic variants for diagnostic purposes (khoja2023advancesinneurexin pages 3-4)

### Therapeutic Potential

While no validated NRXN2-directed therapies currently exist, potential future applications include:
- **Splice-modulating therapeutics:** Targeting specific NRXN2 splice isoforms to restore balanced synaptic function
- **Synapse-stabilizing agents:** Modulating NRXN2–ligand interactions to correct circuit hyperexcitability
- **Precision medicine:** Genotype-guided interventions for NRXN2 mutation carriers
- **Biomarker development:** NRXN2 splice variants or IgSF21 complex levels as potential disease biomarkers

## Conclusions

NRXN2 encodes a presynaptic cell-adhesion molecule with a unique anti-synaptogenic function that distinguishes it from other neurexin family members. As a molecular scaffold rather than an enzyme or transporter, NRXN2 continuously restricts excitatory synapse formation throughout life while organizing specific inhibitory synaptic contacts through selective ligand interactions. The protein's elaborate domain architecture—featuring six LNS domains and three EGF-like domains in the alpha isoform—enables diverse trans-synaptic interactions that are extensively regulated by alternative splicing and ligand availability.

Recent structural and functional studies (2023-2024) have revolutionized understanding of NRXN2, revealing its selective high-affinity interaction with IgSF21 through a precisely defined LNS1–Ig1 interface, its requirement for JNK-mediated signaling in inhibitory synapse organization, and its critical role in behavioral flexibility and seizure susceptibility. Human genetic studies implicate NRXN2 in autism, epilepsy, and intellectual disability, though the clinical evidence base remains more limited than for NRXN1.

The primary function of NRXN2 is to act as a context-dependent specifier of synaptic properties, maintaining appropriate circuit connectivity through trans-synaptic adhesion, intracellular scaffolding, and regulation of neurotransmitter release and receptor function. Its unique restrictive role at excitatory synapses—nearly doubling synapse density when deleted—positions NRXN2 as an essential brake on excitatory connectivity that prevents circuit hyperexcitability and enables adaptive behavioral flexibility.
