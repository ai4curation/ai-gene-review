# LRIG1 evidence notes

## Biological synthesis

Human LRIG1 is a single-pass cell-surface protein whose extracellular leucine-rich-repeat and immunoglobulin-like domains participate in receptor regulation. Direct human studies support receptor-specific negative regulation rather than an intrinsic catalytic activity. For EGFR/ERBB receptors, LRIG1 can promote receptor ubiquitination and degradation through c-Cbl recruitment [PMID:15282549 "The underlying mechanism involves recruitment of c-Cbl, an E3 ubiquitin ligase that simultaneously ubiquitylates EGFR and LRIG1 and sorts them for degradation."]. A second human study found complexes with all four ERBB receptors in a transfection system [PMID:15345710 "LRIG1 forms a complex with each of the ErbB receptors"]. This is strong positive evidence, but not assay-universal: purified extracellular fragments did not show detectable EGFR binding in another study [PMID:25765764 "We could not detect any binding of the LRIG1 domains"].

MET regulation is mechanistically distinct from the c-Cbl model established for EGFR. LRIG1 interacts with MET without HGF stimulation and destabilizes MET independently of cbl [PMID:17178829 "LRIG1 destabilizes the Met receptor in a cbl-independent manner."]. In the therapeutic-antibody SAIT301 setting, ubiquitinated LRIG1 recruits Hrs and carries the MET-LRIG1 complex toward lysosomal degradation [PMID:24828152 "These results suggest that SAIT301-induced ubiquitination of LRIG1 promotes recruitment of Met-LRIG1 complex to the lysosome through its interaction with Hrs, resulting in lysosomal degradation of Met and LRIG1."]. USP8 deubiquitinates LRIG1 and thereby modulates this route; this makes LRIG1 a regulated substrate/adaptor in the pathway, not an ubiquitin ligase or deubiquitinase.

RET regulation uses yet another experimentally described mode. Lrig1 associates with RET and suppresses GDNF binding, lipid-raft recruitment, receptor autophosphorylation, MAPK activation, and neuronal differentiation [PMID:18171921 "physically interacting with Ret"]. With oncogenic RET2A and RET2B, LRIG1 reduces receptor phosphorylation without significantly changing total receptor abundance [PMID:29436694 "LRIG1 co-expression induced the downregulation of the phosphorylation levels of RET2A and RET2B without significantly affecting the corresponding total protein levels"]. Thus RET inhibition should not automatically be described as receptor degradation.

## Ectodomain shedding and localization

The membrane-anchored and shed forms must be kept distinct. Full-length LRIG1 is a transmembrane protein [PMID:15282549 "Their structural relative in mammals, LRIG1, is a transmembrane protein"]. Cells also constitutively shed LRIG1 ectodomains [PMID:21087604 "Cells constitutively shed LRIG1 ectodomains in vitro"], and purified ectodomain inhibited EGF signaling without apparent EGFR downregulation [PMID:21087604 "Purified LRIG1 ectodomains suppressed EGF signaling without"]. ADAM17-sensitive modulation supports metalloprotease-dependent shedding in the tested systems, but the abstract does not establish one obligatory protease or a functionally unique UniProt isoform.

## Cancer and tissue context

LRIG1 can act as a tumor suppressor in defined settings, but a universal human tumor-suppressor claim is not licensed. In mouse intestine, loss of Lrig1 increased ErbB/ERK signaling and 14 of 16 homozygous mice developed duodenal tumors [PMID:22464327 "Notably, at five to six months of age, 14/16 (88%) Lrig1-CreERT2/CreERT2 mice developed duodenal tumors."]. In human thyroid tumor cohorts, LRIG1 expression was reduced, but no significant association with survival was found; moreover, Lrig1 loss did not significantly change the RET2B mouse-model outcome [PMID:29436694 "However, in our mouse model of RET2B-driven MTC, no significant effect of Lrig1 gene ablation was observed."]. EGFRvIII-positive glioblastoma cells provide another specific setting in which ectopic LRIG1 opposes proliferation, survival, motility, and invasion [PMID:18542056 "Ectopic expression of LRIG1 in EGFRvIII(+) glioblastoma cells opposes EGFRvIII-driven tumor cell proliferation, survival, motility and invasion."].

## Evidence-transfer boundaries

- **Species:** PMID:22464327 and PMID:34338291 provide mouse in vivo evidence. They inform conserved biology but do not directly establish the same tissue roles in humans.
- **Paralogs:** LRIG3 opposes LRIG1 in the ERBB setting [PMID:23723069 "Lrig3 opposes Lrig1 negative regulatory"], while mouse Lrig1 and Lrig3 act redundantly in RET-positive sensory neurons [PMID:34338291 "Lrig1 and Lrig3 act redundantly"]. These results are context-dependent and do not license blanket transfer among LRIG1, LRIG2, and LRIG3.
- **Assay:** PMID:15345710, PMID:18171921, and PMID:25765764 are abstract-only in the local cache. The curated experimental annotations should receive curator deference where the abstract does not expose interaction-level details. Overexpression/co-transfection results and SAIT301-induced trafficking are not automatically basal physiology.
- **Screens:** Proteome-scale affinity-purification data are cell-line-specific [PMID:33961781 "mass spectrometry, we have created two proteome-scale, cell-line-specific"]. The LRIG1-centered yeast two-hybrid/BioPlex study begins with screen candidates [PMID:29317492 "performed a yeast two-hybrid screen to identify novel LRIG1-interacting proteins"]. Such interactions are hypothesis-generating unless supported by receptor-specific functional experiments.
- **Isoforms:** UniProt lists Q96JA1-1 and Q96JA1-2, but the reviewed primary studies do not demonstrate distinct receptor-regulatory or shedding functions for those isoforms. Tested constructs or products should not be converted into isoform-specific biological claims without direct evidence.

## Curation implications

The defensible core is that membrane LRIG1 restrains multiple receptor tyrosine kinase pathways through receptor- and context-dependent mechanisms, including c-Cbl-linked ERBB ubiquitination/degradation, CBL-independent MET destabilization, and inhibition of RET activation. Ectodomain shedding adds a soluble, non-cell-autonomous mode of EGF-pathway inhibition. Generic protein-binding annotations do not express these mechanisms, and screen interactions should remain non-core unless functionally resolved.
