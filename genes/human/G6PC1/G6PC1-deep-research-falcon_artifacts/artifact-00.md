| Aspect | Human G6PC1/P35575 findings | Evidence and interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Identity and nomenclature | **G6PC1**, formerly **G6PC**, encodes glucose-6-phosphatase catalytic subunit 1, also called G6Pase-α. It is distinct from **G6PC2**, **G6PC3**, and the separate transporter **G6PT/SLC37A4**. | G6PC1 is the membrane-embedded catalytic enzyme, whereas SLC37A4 transports G6P. Accordingly, G6PC1 itself should not be annotated as G6PT. (pqac-00000000, pqac-00000003) |
| Primary reaction | **D-glucose 6-phosphate + H₂O → D-glucose + inorganic phosphate**; EC 3.1.3.9. | Human structural evidence places G6P in the active-site cavity and identifies His176 as the catalytic nucleophile, with His119 assisting phosphoester cleavage. (pqac-00000008, pqac-00000010) |
| Phosphosugar specificity | G6P is the physiological substrate. Purified **mouse** G6PC1, an 89%-identical ortholog rather than human P35575, hydrolyzed G6P and M6P at similar rates. F6P activity was approximately **26–30%** of G6P activity at 1.5 mM, and catalytic efficiency for G6P was **more than tenfold** higher than for F6P. | These results demonstrate intrinsic phosphosugar promiscuity but do not establish M6P or F6P as major human physiological substrates. Mouse WT G6PC1 in LMNG had a G6P Kₘ of **0.431 ± 0.08 mM**, kcat of **41 s⁻¹**, and kcat/Kₘ of **9.6 × 10⁴ M⁻¹ s⁻¹**; purified human G6PC1 had an apparent G6P Kₘ of approximately **2 mM**. (pqac-00000008, pqac-00000015, pqac-00000016, pqac-00000017) |
| Family and localization | G6PC1 is a PAP2-family, multispanning integral membrane glycoprotein of the **endoplasmic-reticulum membrane**. It has **nine transmembrane helices**, an N-luminal and C-cytosolic orientation, and a positively charged catalytic pocket opening toward the ER lumen. | Cryo-EM and purified-protein evidence support a luminal active site rather than a cytosolic catalytic site. (pqac-00000008, pqac-00000009, pqac-00000015) |
| Coupled ER system | **SLC37A4/G6PT** imports cytosolic G6P into the ER. G6PC1 hydrolyzes it within the lumen, after which glucose and phosphate leave the ER. | Transport and catalysis are performed by different proteins. Transport-dependent substrate access means that intrinsic enzyme activity alone does not determine physiological substrate use. (pqac-00000000, pqac-00000002, pqac-00000011) |
| Physiological pathway | G6PC1 performs the terminal common step of **gluconeogenesis and glycogenolysis**, enabling fasting glucose output principally from liver and kidney, with expression also reported in intestine. | Its position at the shared endpoint of these pathways makes G6PC1 a gatekeeper of interprandial blood-glucose homeostasis. (pqac-00000003, pqac-00000015) |
| Loss of function | Biallelic pathogenic G6PC1 variants cause **glycogen storage disease type Ia**. Failed G6P hydrolysis produces fasting hypoglycemia, glycogen and lipid accumulation, hepatomegaly, nephromegaly, hyperlipidemia, hyperuricemia, and lactic acidemia. | In a 2024 humanized G6PC1-p.R83C mouse model, hepatic activity was **2.2 ± 0.3 units**, compared with **330.4 ± 36.3 units** in wild-type animals, and only **39%** of untreated animals survived to three weeks. (pqac-00000020) |


*Table: Compact annotation of human G6PC1/P35575 covering identity, catalytic function, specificity, ER topology, pathway role, and loss-of-function disease. It distinguishes the catalytic enzyme from SLC37A4/G6PT and explicitly labels mouse-derived kinetic evidence.*
