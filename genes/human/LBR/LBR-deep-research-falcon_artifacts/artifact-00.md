| **Sterol C14 reduction — high confidence** | NADPH-dependent reduction of the C14=C15 bond in Δ14 sterol intermediates; chiefly at the **inner nuclear membrane (INM)**, with a context-dependent LBR pool in the contiguous ER | Human LBR complemented fungal **ERG24/erg-3 C14-reductase** defects but not the ERG4 C24-reductase defect. Greenberg-dysplasia fibroblasts accumulated **cholesta-8,14-dien-3β-ol** and carried a truncating *LBR* mutation ([2010 review](https://doi.org/10.4161/nucl.1.1.10515)) (pqac-00000002, pqac-00000020). LBR also co-localized with the ER marker calnexin in fibroblasts and HeLa cells ([Clayton et al., 2010](https://doi.org/10.4161/nucl.1.4.12435)) (pqac-00000044). | Strong genetic, complementation and cellular evidence establishes the reaction class. However, these studies do **not** provide purified-LBR substrate-to-product turnover kinetics for a specific human sterol; substrate breadth and exact physiological flux remain incompletely defined. |
| **Cholesterol biosynthesis — high confidence in tested human cells** | Δ14 sterol intermediates and NADPH at the INM; related enzyme **TM7SF2** resides mainly in the ER | CRISPR *LBR*-null HeLa cells had approximately **40% less total cholesterol** under restrictive conditions, showed near-complete loss of newly synthesized radiolabeled cholesterol, and died or detached by days **5–7**; free cholesterol or LDL rescued viability. Wild-type LBR and its isolated reductase domain rescued the defect, whereas endogenous TM7SF2 did not compensate ([Tsai et al., 2016](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.16011)) (pqac-00000008, pqac-00000012, pqac-00000013, pqac-00000036, pqac-00000040). | Establishes LBR as the dominant functional C14 reductase under these human-cell conditions. Earlier mouse genetics suggested greater LBR–TM7SF2 redundancy, so “essential” should not be generalized to every tissue, species or nutrient condition. |
| **NADPH binding and disease-variant mechanism — high confidence** | NADPH-binding pocket in the membrane-embedded C-terminal sterol-reductase domain | Purified wild-type LBR bound NADPH with a reported dissociation constant of **24.2 mM**; Greenberg-associated **N547D** and **R583Q** markedly weakened binding and failed to restore cholesterol synthesis. C-terminal disease truncations had **10–15-minute half-lives** and underwent ubiquitin–proteasome-dependent degradation ([Tsai et al., 2016](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.16011)) (pqac-00000018, pqac-00000019). | Supports defective cofactor binding and protein instability as pathogenic mechanisms, but it is not a complete catalytic-kinetics analysis. The reported millimolar affinity should be interpreted exactly as reported rather than as a turnover constant. |
| **Recognition and compaction of heterochromatin — high-confidence biochemistry; context-dependent in vivo** | N-terminal Tudor domain, especially **W16**, recognizes histone **H4K20me2** on nucleoplasmic chromatin at the INM; RS and globular regions also contact histones, HP1, MeCP2 and lamin B | Peptide arrays, recombinant-H4 pull-downs and ChIP supported H4K20me2 recognition; **W16A** abolished selective binding. Tudor-pocket mutants diffused about **sevenfold faster** than wild-type LBR, and the full nucleoplasmic domain compacted reconstituted chromatin and repressed a reporter ([Hirano et al., 2012](https://doi.org/10.1074/jbc.M112.397950)) (pqac-00000023, pqac-00000038). | Direct binding and compaction are well supported, but Tudor binding alone does not explain all repression: the entire nucleoplasmic region is required. HP1 and methylated histones also occur away from the nuclear periphery, so binding does not prove universal locus anchoring or silencing. |
| **Peripheral heterochromatin tethering and nuclear architecture — high confidence, developmentally conditional** | LBR links peripheral heterochromatin to the INM; lamin A/C supplies a later or alternative tether | Loss of both LBR and lamin A/C caused peripheral heterochromatin loss and “inverted” nuclear architecture; ectopic LBR restored peripheral positioning. The study proposed sequential use of LBR early and lamin A/C later during differentiation ([Solovei et al., 2013](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2013.01.009)) (pqac-00000017, pqac-00000027). | LBR is a major architectural tether, not necessarily the sole tether. Cell lineage, differentiation state, lamins and other INM proteins determine whether loss of LBR alone produces a strong phenotype. |
| **Granulocyte nuclear segmentation / Pelger–Huët mechanism — moderate-to-high confidence** | N-terminal Tudor/RS structural regions interacting with heterochromatin and the lamina at the INM | A mouse **236-bp N-terminal deletion** predicted to remove Tudor/RS domains while retaining the transmembrane reductase region reproduced Pelger–Huët-like blood-cell nuclear defects without severe skin or skeletal dysplasia; X-inactivation effects were minor, although homozygous crosses showed approximately **35% perinatal mortality** ([Young et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.1038/s42003-021-01944-2)) (pqac-00000024, pqac-00000045, pqac-00000046). | Supports domain-level separation of nuclear architecture from sterol metabolism, but preserved reductase activity was inferred from retention of the domain rather than established by a complete biochemical assay. Mouse findings do not fully define human genotype–phenotype relationships. |
| **Allelic separation of metabolic and structural disease — high confidence** | C-terminal reductase variants **N547D/R583Q** versus truncating alleles affecting broader LBR structure | N547D and R583Q impaired rescue of yeast C14-reductase deficiency. A heterozygous R583Q carrier had normally segmented neutrophils, whereas a carrier of a truncating allele showed Pelger morphology ([Clayton et al., 2010](https://doi.org/10.4161/nucl.1.4.12435)) (pqac-00000041, pqac-00000042). | Demonstrates that lethal Greenberg dysplasia can arise from loss of enzymatic activity without an overt heterozygous nuclear-shape phenotype. Dosage, residual activity and protein stability nevertheless complicate simple domain-based predictions. |
| **Recent sterol-pathway evidence — moderate and indirect for substrate specificity** | **14-Dehydrozymostenol** was monitored as a DHCR14-pathway intermediate in human Huh-7 cells | High-dose AY9944 increased 14-dehydrozymostenol, and *LBR*-, *TM7SF2*- and *EBP*-ablated cells were used to exclude upstream-enzyme inhibition as the principal ferroptosis-protective mechanism ([Yamada et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-024-46386-6)) (pqac-00000039). | This peer-reviewed study supports pathway placement but does **not** directly demonstrate purified human LBR converting 14-dehydrozymostenol into a specific product. It is not definitive substrate-specificity or catalytic-kinetics evidence. |
| **Recent nuclear-envelope dynamics — moderate confidence** | LBR and nesprin-1 during human neutrophil extracellular-trap formation | Microscopy indicated early LBR disintegration; calcium-dependent calpain-1 degraded nesprin-1, weakening the envelope before later actin collapse ([Singh et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12964-024-01785-6)) (pqac-00000029). | Extends LBR biology to dynamic envelope breakdown during NET release but does not redefine its primary enzymatic or chromatin-tethering functions; numerical effect sizes were unavailable in the retrieved excerpt. |
| **Exploratory transcriptomic effects — preliminary** | Golgi-, ribosome- and nucleolus-associated processes in LBR-depleted human HL-60/S4 myeloid cells | An **8–10-fold LBR mRNA reduction** prevented retinoic-acid-induced nuclear lobulation. The analysis identified **638** knockdown-specific downregulated and **696** upregulated genes; approximately **70%** of control versus **45%** of knockdown nuclei contained more than two nucleoli ([Welch et al., bioRxiv, 2024](https://doi.org/10.1101/2024.06.19.598074)) (pqac-00000031, pqac-00000032, pqac-00000033). | This is an unreviewed preprint using one leukemia-derived cell model. Golgi, ribosome and nucleolar associations are hypothesis-generating and should not yet be annotated as primary LBR molecular functions. |
