ADAMTSL1 (Q8N6G6) domain structure: what Foldseek supports and what it does not

Hypothesis slug: kgap-adamtsl1-domain-structure Source: genes/human/ADAMTSL1/ADAMTSL1-ai-review.yaml, selector free-text Target: human ADAMTSL1, UniProt Q8N6G6, NCBITaxon:9606 Analysis performed: Foldseek 3Di+AA web API on 11 AlphaFold-derived domain segments against pdb100 and afdb-swissprot, plus exosite residue mapping, TSR charge and motif scanning, GO molecular-function transfer testing, and interaction and annotation database queries.

Summary

The seed hypothesis has two parts. The structural part is correct and is now quantified. The functional part does not follow from it.

Foldseek searches of 11 AlphaFold model segments of Q8N6G6 against pdb100 return a confident homolog (E < 1e-3) for 8 of 11 segments, with 3 segments below E = 1e-5, and 9 of 11 segments have mean pLDDT above 70. The architecture the hypothesis proposed is confirmed at fold level: thrombospondin type-1 repeats matching thrombospondin-1 (1lsl_A, E = 1.94e-4 to 6.16e-4), an ADAMTS cysteine-rich plus spacer module matching the ADAMTS13 DTCS fragment (3vn4_A, E = 1.18e-10, 25.1% identity over 219 residues), an immunoglobulin-superfamily array matching DCC Ig1-4 (5z5k_A, E = 1.38e-21), and a PLAC domain. The hypothesis named the right homolog families before the search was run. Two segments return nothing above noise: the ADAMTSL1-specific domain (Pfam PF24484, residues 1040-1120, best E = 5.01) and the PLAC domain (1715-1762, best E = 2.10), so PLAC has no experimental structural representative in the PDB.

Zero of 11 segments yield a transferable molecular function. Three independent checks explain why. Of the 22 GO molecular-function terms carried by the 18 UniProt proteins behind the 25 confident PDB hits, 19 (86%) are unique to a single segment's homolog set, and the three recurring terms (calcium ion binding 3/3 sets, metalloendopeptidase activity 2/3, integrin binding 2/3) all trace to domains ADAMTSL1 does not have. Mapping the published ADAMTS13 exosite-3 residues through the structural alignment shows that the fold is retained without the functional surface: of 8 exosite residues with a structural equivalent, only R636 to K258 is conserved, R639 to D261 is a charge reversal, and R568, K608, R660 and Y661 map to A198, E238, S282 and G283. The Ig-array homologs (DCC, Robo1, sidekick-1 and -2, titin, obscurin, UNC-5, contactin-4) are transmembrane or intracellular proteins whose netrin receptor, axon guidance receptor, kinase and phosphatase activities require regions a secreted protein does not possess.

The two functions the hypothesis named by example fare differently. Fibrillin/LTBP binding is weakly supported and partly contradicted. The only extracellular matrix interaction record for ADAMTSL1 is FBN2, from two BioPlex datasets that share method, platform and consortium, scored at MI 0.35 with spoke expansion, and ADAMTSL1 is absent from the list of ADAMTSL proteins shown to use the fibrillin-1 N-terminal binding site. Heparin binding is the one structurally motivated candidate that survives, concentrated in TSR10 (net charge +5), TSR11 (+6), TSR13 (+4) and TSR4 (+3), but it has never been measured for ADAMTSL1 and should not be annotated on homology. The single curation action the evidence supports is a cellular component term, GO:0031012 extracellular matrix, which is currently absent from the gene's four GO annotations.

Executive judgment

Verdict: partially supported, and over-reaching on molecular function.

The hypothesis is right that ADAMTSL1 contains the stated structured domains and right that their closest structural homologs are ADAMTS ancillary modules, thrombospondin TSRs and immunoglobulin folds. It is wrong in the inference that this "suggests a matrix-binding function" in any sense a curator can act on. The analysis was designed to test that inference directly, and the inference fails at every segment.

The reasoning runs as follows. GO molecular-function terms are assigned to whole proteins, not to domains. Transferring a term across a domain-level structural alignment is valid only when the homolog's annotated function is carried by the aligned domain. For ADAMTSL1 that condition fails in every case. The strongest biologically interpretable hit in the entire run, residues 96-300 against ADAMTS13 at E = 1.18e-10, is to a module whose documented job is docking unfolded von Willebrand factor A2 and positioning it for a catalytic domain. ADAMTSL1 has no catalytic domain, and the residues that do the docking are not conserved. The strongest hit by E-value, the four-Ig array against DCC, is to a set of cell-surface receptors. ADAMTSL1 is secreted. The TSR hits are to thrombospondin-1, whose ligand-binding GO terms are properties of an intact trimeric molecule with N-terminal and C-terminal domains that ADAMTSL1 lacks.

Three caveats matter for how a curator reads this.

The strongest structural signal in the protein lies in a region no experiment has ever touched. Residues 1160-1490 (Ig2-Ig4) give E = 1.38e-21 against DCC Ig1-4 with seven further hits below E = 1e-19, and they fall entirely outside residues 29-525, the 525 aa punctin-1 form that is the only ADAMTSL1 protein ever purified and characterised (PMID: 11805097). The Ig array is conserved: mouse Adamtsl1 (Q8BLI0, 1745 aa) has 4 Ig-like domains, human ADAMTSL3 (P82987, 1691 aa) has 3, and Ensembl reports 201 orthologues with one2one calls to lamprey, elephant shark, Xenopus and chicken. It is a real subfamily feature and an unexamined one.

Negative structural results for PLAC and the PF24484 ADAMTSL1-specific domain reflect both low signal from small modules and the absence of experimental structures for those families. They are not evidence that those regions are disordered.

Three evidence streams describe three different molecules. The biochemistry is on the 525 aa isoform, the structural analysis and the Ensembl canonical transcript are the 1762 aa isoform, and the BioPlex bait in PMID: 28514442 is Q8N6G6-6 (463 aa).

Key findings

F001: The N-terminal cysteine-rich plus spacer module is an ADAMTS ancillary fold with ADAMTS13 as the closest experimental structure

Segment 96-300, comprising the Pfam PF19236 cysteine-rich domain and the PF05986 spacer, returns the ADAMTS13 DTCS fragment 3VN4_A at E = 1.18e-10 with 25.1% sequence identity over 219 aligned residues, followed by 3GHM_A (E = 3.76e-10), 3GHN_A (E = 4.41e-10) and the full-length ADAMTS13 structure 6QIG_A (E = 9.85e-9). Searching the submodules alone gives weaker but concordant results (cysteine-rich 96-187 against 3GHM at E = 5.1e-4, spacer 188-300 against 3VN4 at E = 8.3e-4), which is expected because each submodule is roughly 100 residues.

Model quality supports the assignment. Mean pLDDT is 84.3 for the cysteine-rich domain and 83.8 for the spacer, with 97.6% and 89.0% of residues above 70. Predicted aligned error within the N-terminal unit is low (TSR1 to cysteine-rich 10 Å, cysteine-rich to spacer 15 Å, spacer to TSR2 13.5 Å), so these domains form one semi-rigid block. PAE to every downstream domain is 28-30 Å, which is the beads-on-a-string pattern of an extended modular protein. The best non-paralog AFDB-SwissProt matches for the same segment are ADAMTSL3 (63.1% identity) and mouse Adamtsl1, confirming the subfamily assignment from a second database.

In ADAMTS13, this module carries the exosites that engage the substrate. Petri and colleagues report that "in binding VWF, the ADAMTS13 cysteine-rich and spacer domain exosites bring enzyme and substrate into proximity" (PMID: 31439947). The fold is one used for macromolecular docking, which is why the hypothesis expected a binding function from it. Hirohata and colleagues confirm the architectural premise directly: punctin "lacks the pro-metalloprotease and the disintegrin-like domain typical of this family but contains other ADAMTS domains in precise order including four thrombospondin type I repeats" (PMID: 11805097).

F002: The ADAMTS13 exosite surface is not conserved, so the fold match transfers no ligand

This is the test that separates fold from function, and it is negative.

The Foldseek alignment of ADAMTSL1 96-300 to 3GHM_A yielded 185 aligned residue pairs. These were mapped to ADAMTS13 UniProt numbering (Q76LX8) by exact 12-mer anchoring of the PDB observed sequence, with 368 of 369 residues mapped unambiguously. Of the 12 published exosite-3 residues, 8 have a structural equivalent in ADAMTSL1 and only 1 is conserved.

The cysteine-rich exosite-2 patch G471-A472-A473-V474 maps to V111-S112-N113-D114. Bulk electrostatics are similar between the two spacers (ADAMTSL1 188-296 net +2, 11.9% K+R; ADAMTS13 556-682 net +3, 13.4% K+R), so the difference lies in the specific exosite residues and not in overall charge.

Ercig and colleagues define the residues that were mapped: "residues R568, F592, R660, Y661, and Y665 within exosite-3 of the spacer domain provide an immunodominant region of ADAMTS13 for pathogenic autoantibodies that develop in patients with iTTP" (PMID: 33544829). Four of those five are among the positions that are lost or unaligned in ADAMTSL1. The curation consequence is direct: any action should stay at the level of the fold, and must not import ADAMTS13-specific or protease-substrate-specific molecular function terms.

Best non-paralog AFDB-SwissProt hits are human DCC (P43146, E = 1.2e-22) and human hemicentin-1 (Q96RW7, E = 1.9e-22). The isolated Ig1 (855-965) matches obscurin Ig (2EO1, E = 1.24e-6), Robo1 Ig5, contactin-4, and the heparin-bound C. elegans UNC-5 Ig1+2 structure 8EDI (E = 1.93e-5). Mean pLDDT is 84.4 (Ig1), 77.8 (Ig2), 82.4 (Ig3) and 82.3 (Ig4). Inter-domain PAE within the array is 13-19 Å (Ig3 to Ig4 is 13.4 Å) against 28-30 Å to every other domain, so the four Ig domains form a coupled unit.

All four Ig domains and the C-terminal TSR10-TSR13 plus PLAC block lie outside residues 29-525. That region is the 60 kDa punctin-1 protein that was purified, mass-measured and rotary-shadowed by Hirohata and colleagues, who report that "rotary shadowing demonstrates that punctin is hatchet-shaped having a globular region attached to a short stem" (PMID: 11805097). The only experimental shape data on ADAMTSL1 therefore concerns a molecule that does not contain the Ig array.

This is a competing interpretation to the seed hypothesis, which framed ADAMTSL1 purely as a fibrillin/LTBP/heparin-binding ADAMTS-like module. Taye and Hubmacher give the standard family framing that the Ig result qualifies: ADAMTSL proteins "constitute a family of regulatory ECM proteins that are related to ADAMTS proteases but lack their protease domains" (PMID: 38284126). The Ig array is not part of that framing and has no place in it yet.

F004: The TSRs match generic TSP-1-type repeats with no heparin-binding groove signature, and PLAC is structurally uninformative

Best pdb100 hits for the TSR blocks are weak and spread across unrelated TSR-containing proteins: TSR1 (29-95) against 1LSL_A thrombospondin-1 TSR2-3 at E = 3.81e-4; TSR2-TSR4 (301-500) against 1LSL_A at E = 6.16e-4; TSR5-TSR9 (520-855) against 1LSL_A at E = 1.94e-4; TSR10-TSR13 (1490-1730) against 3T5O_A complement C6 TSR at E = 1.36e-3, with properdin, complement C5b6/C7, BAI1 TSR3 and CCN3 filling the rest of the hit lists. This is fold-level recognition of a small disulfide-stapled repeat, not recognition of a specific ligand-binding variant.

Sequence-level analysis of all 13 TSRs sharpens this. Only TSR1 carries the W-x-x-W-x-x-W tryptophan ladder (36-WDAWGPW-42) that forms the charged groove face in thrombospondin-1 TSR2 and TSR3. Only TSR7 carries a CSVTCG-like motif (CSLTCG). Net charge (K+R minus D+E) ranges from -3 (TSR3, TSR6) to +6 (TSR11), with the C-terminal TSR10 (+5), TSR11 (+6) and TSR13 (+4) the most basic. The heparin-binding thrombospondin-1 TSR2 and TSR3 controls are only +2 and 0, and properdin TSR4 is +7 without a ladder, so charge alone does not discriminate heparin binders from non-binders.

The one conserved TSR motif in ADAMTSL1 is experimentally a glycosylation site, not a binding site. Wang and colleagues identified "a peptide derived from TSR1 containing the (36)WDAWGPWSECSRTC(49) sequence of interest modified with two mannose residues and a Glc-Fuc disaccharide (O-fucosylation)" (PMID: 19671700). The same group showed that "mutation of the putative modified Ser/Thr residues in TSR2, TSR3, and TSR4 led to significantly decreased levels of secreted punctin-1" (PMID: 17395588). The characterised function of ADAMTSL1 TSR surface residues is secretion-linked glycosylation.

The ADAMTSL1-specific domain (PF24484, 1071-1110) returned no pdb100 hit better than E = 5.01 and no AFDB-SwissProt hit better than E = 8.4. The PLAC domain (1726-1762) returned no pdb100 hit better than E = 2.10 and no non-paralog AFDB hit better than E = 0.12 (ADAMTSL3 E = 0.21, THSD4 E = 0.12 are paralogs). No experimental PLAC structure exists to compare against.

F006: ADAMTSL1 has four GO annotations, none molecular function, none extracellular

QuickGO returns 4 annotations for Q8N6G6: GO:0030198 extracellular matrix organization (BP, IEA, from InterPro IPR013273, GO_REF:0000002) and GO:0005788 endoplasmic reticulum lumen (CC, TAS) three times from Reactome R-HSA-5173005, R-HSA-5173192 and R-HSA-6785565. There is no molecular function annotation and no extracellular region or extracellular matrix cellular component annotation, although UniProt Q8N6G6 records SUBCELLULAR LOCATION "Secreted, extracellular space, extracellular matrix" and SUBUNIT "Monomer".

UniProt lists 6 isoforms: Q8N6G6-1 (525 aa), -2 (439), -3 (1762, displayed canonical), -4 (683), -5 (258) and -6 (463). Ensembl ENSG00000178031 has 20 transcripts, with canonical ENST00000380548 encoding 1762 aa and the longest transcript encoding 1810 aa. The 525 aa form is the punctin-1 protein of PMID: 11805097: secreted, about 60 kDa, hatchet-shaped, four TSRs, and "expressed primarily in adult skeletal muscle".

The sparse GO record is itself a curation deficit, independent of the structural question. A gene with a documented secreted ECM localization and no extracellular CC annotation has a gap that can be filled now.

F007: The matrix-binding claim rests on one non-independent FBN2 association

IntAct returns 16 interaction records for Q8N6G6. Every protein-protein record is anti-tag co-immunoprecipitation with spoke expansion at MI score 0.35, from two BioPlex datasets: PMID: 28514442 (bait Q8N6G6-6) and PMID: 33961781 (BioPlex 3.0). The same seven partners appear in both: ACOX1, B3GLCT, WDCP, FBN2, RSPRY1, GRN and FHL2. FBN2 is the only ECM protein among them.

Huttlin and colleagues describe the single method behind all of these records: "Through affinity-purification mass spectrometry, we have created two proteome-scale, cell-line-specific interaction networks" (PMID: 33961781). The two papers share method, platform and consortium, so they are one assay type, not two independent lines of evidence. Spoke expansion means the FBN2 record does not assert a direct binary contact.

STRING gives B3GLCT 0.773, WDCP 0.754 (experimental subscore 0.754), POFUT2 0.732, FBN2 0.522 (experimental subscore 0.307) and FBN1 0.431 (text mining only). The two highest-confidence partners are the TSR-modifying glycosyltransferases, consistent with substrate-enzyme contact in the secretory pathway. That interpretation is supported directly by the demonstration that POFUT2 modifies ADAMTSL1 TSRs and that this modification regulates secretion (PMID: 17395588).

Against this, Sengle and colleagues map the fibrillin-1 N-terminal binding site used by the ADAMTSL family and name ADAMTSLIKE-2, -3, -6 and "papilin" (PMID: 22242013). ADAMTSL1 does not appear in that list. Direct fibrillin binding has been measured for ADAMTSL-6, which "binds to the N-terminal half of fibrillin-1 with a dissociation constant of approximately 80 nm" (PMID: 19940141), and localised to its third TSP1 domain (PMID: 32141660). No equivalent measurement exists for ADAMTSL1.

F008: Consolidated verdict

Across all 11 segments: 8 of 11 reach E < 1e-3, 3 of 11 reach E < 1e-5, 9 of 11 have mean pLDDT above 70, and 0 of 11 yield a transferable molecular function. Current GO content is 4 annotations with no MF and no extracellular CC term, although secretion is shown in PMID: 11805097 and PMID: 17395588 and ADAMTSL1 protein is placed at the human myotendinous junction in PMID: 36924352.

Existing annotations

GO:0030198 extracellular matrix organization (BP, IEA from InterPro IPR013273): retain and flag for experimental support. The term is plausible from family context but rests on a family-level signature, and no vertebrate ADAMTSL1 loss-of-function matrix phenotype has been published.

GO:0005788 endoplasmic reticulum lumen (CC, TAS, three Reactome sources): retain and treat as non-core. It reflects secretory transit and TSR glycosylation, consistent with B3GLCT (STRING 0.773) and POFUT2 (0.732) being the highest-confidence partners, and it is not the site of action.

Mechanistic model and interpretation

The immediate molecular event under test is whether a folded ADAMTSL1 domain makes a direct, specific contact with a named extracellular ligand. The structural run establishes the domain inventory and its confidence, which is a statement about fold topology, not about contact.

Direct gene-product properties established by experiment are narrow. The 525 aa punctin-1 form is secreted as a monomeric, roughly 60 kDa, hatchet-shaped particle (PMID: 11805097). TSR2, TSR3 and TSR4 carry O-fucose whose loss reduces secretion (PMID: 17395588). TSR1 W39 and W42 are C-mannosylated (PMID: 19671700). These are properties of the protein itself and they all concern biosynthesis.

Everything else in the ADAMTSL1 literature is downstream or contextual: the myotendinous junction localization (PMID: 36924352), the Adamtsl1-positive Schwann cell populations (PMID: 35115729, PMID: 39880678), the GWAS association with myocardial T1 time and fibrosis (PMID: 37081215), and the C. elegans madd-4B receptor-clustering phenotype (PMID: 39190555). None identifies a binding partner or a catalytic activity for human ADAMTSL1.

The ADAMTS13 match is the sharpest illustration of the separation between fold and function. In ADAMTS13 the cysteine-rich and spacer domains do defined molecular work: they grip unfolded VWF A2 through exosites and position it for cleavage (PMID: 31439947, PMID: 33544829). That work is defined by the catalytic domain downstream of it. Remove the protease and the module has a fold without a defined job, which is the position ADAMTSL1 occupies.

Conflicts and alternatives

Paralog confusion is the main risk. The fibrillin-binding story for the ADAMTSL family is built on ADAMTSL-2, -3, -6 and papilin (PMID: 22242013), with the quantitative measurements on ADAMTSL-6 (PMID: 19940141, PMID: 32141660). ADAMTSL1 is absent from the Sengle list and differs architecturally: 13 TSRs and a four-Ig array against ADAMTSL6, which has neither. Transferring the family function to ADAMTSL1 is the specific error this report advises against.

Isoform mismatch. Six UniProt isoforms exist. The biochemistry is on the 525 aa form, the structural analysis and Ensembl canonical transcript are the 1762 aa form, and the BioPlex bait is the 463 aa Q8N6G6-6. The three evidence streams are not describing the same molecule, and a review that merges them silently will create an annotation that no single experiment supports.

Assay non-independence. The two BioPlex papers share method, platform and consortium. Counting them as two independent supports for the FBN2 contact overstates the evidence. Spoke expansion means the record does not assert a direct binary contact.

An alternative to matrix binding. The best non-paralog AFDB-SwissProt hit to the TSR5-TSR9 block is C. elegans MADD-4 / punctin at E = 3.8e-26. Animals lacking madd-4B show severe GABA-A receptor clustering defects in postsynaptic muscle cells (PMID: 39190555). A diffusible organizing cue is a different molecular model from a structural matrix component, and the ADAMTSL1 myotendinous junction and Schwann cell localizations are compatible with it. The original name "punctin" came from exactly this punctate, junction-associated distribution.

Database carry-over. The one BP annotation, GO:0030198, derives from InterPro IPR013273, a family signature. If a curator treats it as independent support for a matrix function, the reasoning becomes circular with the structural homology argument.

Limitations and knowledge gaps

Limitations of this analysis

The Foldseek run used the public web API against pdb100 and afdb-swissprot with 3Di+AA mode and default settings, executed once per segment. Segment boundaries came from UniProt and InterPro domain limits and were not optimised. E-values for isolated TSRs are intrinsically weak because these repeats are about 55 residues and structurally defined by disulfides, so the negative results for PF24484 and PLAC reflect both low signal and the absence of experimental structures for those families. The exosite mapping relies on a structural alignment at 25% identity, so individual residue correspondences carry uncertainty even though the overall pattern of non-conservation is clear. GO terms were taken at the protein level from UniProt with no attempt to resolve which domain of each homolog carries which term, which is the limitation the transfer analysis is designed to expose. No binding measurement was performed in this work, so every conclusion about ADAMTSL1 molecular function is negative or conditional.

Knowledge gaps that matter for curation

No measured ligand. Checked: IntAct (16 records, one assay type), STRING, PubMed. ADAMTSL1 has no binding partner with a measured affinity. This is the gap that blocks every MF term. Resolution: solid-phase or SPR binding of recombinant ADAMTSL1 against fibrillin-1 and fibrillin-2 fragments, heparin, and a matrix panel.

No biochemistry on the Ig array. Checked: PubMed, UniProt feature table, structural run. Residues 1160-1490 give the strongest hit in the protein (E = 1.38e-21 to DCC Ig1-4) and are conserved to lamprey, with no experiment in any species. A curator reading only the literature would never know the domain exists in the expressed protein. Resolution: expression of the long isoform and detection of the Ig region by isoform-specific antibody or targeted proteomics in skeletal muscle.

Which isoform is the physiological product. Checked: UniProt (6 isoforms), Ensembl (20 transcripts, canonical 1762 aa, longest 1810 aa). The 525 aa form is the only one with protein-level data. Resolution: long-read transcript quantification in adult skeletal muscle plus western blot sizing of endogenous ADAMTSL1.

Whether the basic TSRs bind glycosaminoglycan. Checked: charge and motif scan across all 13 TSRs with four reference TSRs. TSR10, TSR11, TSR13 and TSR4 are the candidates. Resolution: heparin-Sepharose salt elution of the recombinant C-terminal TSR block, with charge-reversal mutants of the basic residues.

Whether PLAC has any structural representative. Checked: Foldseek against pdb100 and afdb-swissprot. The best pdb100 hit is noise (E = 2.10) and all AFDB hits are paralogs (ADAMTSL3 E = 0.21, THSD4 E = 0.12). No experimental PLAC structure exists. Resolution: experimental structure determination of a PLAC domain from any family member.

No vertebrate loss-of-function phenotype. Checked: PubMed. The only in vivo mechanistic data come from C. elegans madd-4. The retained BP term GO:0030198 therefore has no direct support. Resolution: Adamtsl1 knockout mouse with myotendinous junction and microfibril analysis.

Proposed follow-up experiments and discriminating tests

Ranked by how efficiently each separates the live hypotheses.

  1. Heparin-Sepharose chromatography of the recombinant C-terminal TSR10-TSR13 block against the N-terminal TSR1-TSR4 block. Separates "basic TSRs bind glycosaminoglycan" from "charge is incidental". The internal control is built in: TSR10, TSR11 and TSR13 are +5, +6 and +4 while TSR2, TSR3 and TSR6 are -2, -3 and -3, so the two blocks predict opposite behaviour from the same protein. Follow with charge-reversal mutants of the basic residues.

  2. Solid-phase binding of full-length recombinant ADAMTSL1 to the fibrillin-1 N-terminal fragment used in PMID: 22242013, run alongside ADAMTSL-6 as the positive control. Closes the gap created by ADAMTSL1 being absent from that study's list and tests the BioPlex FBN2 lead. Include fibrillin-2, since FBN2 and not FBN1 is the BioPlex partner.

  3. Reciprocal co-immunoprecipitation of endogenous ADAMTSL1 and FBN2 from a tissue or primary cell expressing both, with an isotype control. Converts the single spoke-expanded AP-MS record into either a validated interaction or a discarded one, using a method genuinely independent of BioPlex.

  4. Isoform-resolved detection in adult skeletal muscle: long-read RNA sequencing plus western blot with antibodies raised against the Ig region (residues 1160-1490) and against the N-terminal TSR block. Determines whether the molecule carrying the strongest structural signal is actually expressed, which currently gates any annotation on the Ig array.

  5. Experimental structure of the ADAMTSL1 cysteine-rich plus spacer module (residues 96-300), which the AlphaFold model places at mean pLDDT 84 and which has a well-defined homolog to compare against. Would show whether the surface replacing the ADAMTS13 exosite is a distinct binding site or a featureless patch.

  6. Comparative test of the MADD-4 alternative: rescue of a C. elegans madd-4 null with human ADAMTSL1 constructs, full-length against TSR-block only. Discriminates the synapse-organizer model from the structural matrix model and tests which domains carry the activity.

Evidence base

The 35 papers reviewed divide into five groups by what they contribute.

Direct biochemistry on ADAMTSL1 protein (3 papers). PMID: 11805097 is the original punctin-1 characterisation and the source of the architecture premise, the rotary-shadowing shape and the skeletal muscle expression. PMID: 17395588 and PMID: 19671700 establish TSR O-fucosylation and C-mannosylation and their effect on secretion. All three concern the 525 aa isoform. Together they support the architecture and the secretion claims and they qualify the TSR motif analysis by showing that the one conserved tryptophan ladder is a glycosylation acceptor.

ADAMTS13 structure and exosite function (6 papers). PMID: 31439947 and PMID: 33544829 define what the closest structural homolog's cysteine-rich and spacer domains do and which residues do it. PMID: 32196558, PMID: 28209710, PMID: 34946607, PMID: 34517169 and PMID: 42173289 describe the autoinhibition and conformational activation of ADAMTS13 through the same spacer exosites. These papers challenge rather than support the seed hypothesis: they show that the module's documented function is inseparable from the catalytic domain.

ADAMTSL paralog function (6 papers). PMID: 19940141 and PMID: 32141660 measure ADAMTSL-6 binding to fibrillin-1 and localise it to the third TSP1 domain. PMID: 22242013 maps the fibrillin-1 binding site used by the family and omits ADAMTSL1. PMID: 25957949, PMID: 31463913, PMID: 38284126 and PMID: 38324186 provide review-level family framing. PMID: 32855533 shows that THSD4 (ADAMTSL6) haploinsufficiency causes thoracic aortic aneurysm through impaired fibrillin-1 microfibril assembly. These establish the family precedent that makes the seed hypothesis plausible, and the one that specifically excludes ADAMTSL1 from it.

ADAMTSL1 in tissue and disease contexts (6 papers). PMID: 36924352 places the protein at the human myotendinous junction, which is the evidence behind the recommended CC term. PMID: 35115729 and PMID: 39880678 identify Adamtsl1-expressing Schwann cells that myelinate large-caliber motor axons. PMID: 37081215 associates the ADAMTSL1 locus with myocardial T1 time and tissue repair in 41,505 UK Biobank participants. PMID: 39190555 supplies the C. elegans synapse-organizer alternative. PMID: 34790768 reports pan-cancer prognostic associations across the ADAMTSL family. None of these identifies a molecular function; all are downstream or correlative.

High-throughput interaction and expression data (several papers). PMID: 33961781 and PMID: 28514442 are the source of every ADAMTSL1 interaction record in IntAct, including FBN2. The remaining expression-level papers (PMID: 41534528, PMID: 39769211, PMID: 37061115, PMID: 34499774, PMID: 40401006, PMID: 40869199, PMID: 42413983, PMID: 42175658, PMID: 36339449) report ADAMTSL1 transcript changes in adipose fatty acid genetics, mucopolysaccharidosis, pseudoexfoliation glaucoma, surgical wound healing, breast cancer, egg albumen quality, urothelial carcinoma, gastric cancer and type 2 diabetes. They establish that the gene is transcriptionally responsive across many tissues and contribute nothing to the molecular function question.
