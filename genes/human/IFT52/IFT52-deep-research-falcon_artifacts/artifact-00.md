| Evidence setting | Finding relevant to IFT52 | Interpretation or limitation |
|---|---|---|
| **2014 — algal and protist structural studies** | Crystal structures of IFT70–IFT52 and IFT52–IFT46 supported an architecture comprising an N-terminal GIFT domain, central region and small C-terminal region. The conserved central region binds IFT70, whereas the C-terminal region forms an α-helical interface with IFT46 and helps connect IFT-B assemblies. (pqac-00000010, pqac-00000011, pqac-00000014, pqac-00000040) | Strong structural evidence for a scaffold function. Residue indices and structures came principally from *Chlamydomonas* and *Tetrahymena* proteins; conservation supports, but does not prove, identical residue-level contacts in human Q9Y366. |
| **2016 — human SRPS fibroblasts and chondrocytes** | Biallelic IFT52 variants markedly reduced IFT52 and destabilized other IFT-B components, including IFT74, IFT81 and IFT88. Patient cells showed an approximately **60% reduction in ciliation**, abnormal ciliary-length regulation and loss of detectable ciliary IFT88 above background. (pqac-00000001, pqac-00000034, pqac-00000035, pqac-00000038) | Direct patient-cell evidence that IFT52 is required for IFT-B stability and ciliogenesis; the compound-allelic state does not isolate the effect of each allele. |
| **2019 — human genetics and mouse IMCD3 cells** | Human biallelic variants were associated with short-rib thoracic dysplasia, Sensenbrenner syndrome or CAKUT, with residual-function alleles helping explain phenotypic severity. In Ift52-null mouse IMCD3 cells, IFT52 loss altered centrin organization, centrosomal microtubule anchorage and dynamics, and centriole cohesion; wild-type rescue was more effective than N98S or D259H rescue. (pqac-00000031, pqac-00000032, pqac-00000042, pqac-00000043) | Human genetics supports disease relevance, but the detailed centrin and centrosome mechanism was established mainly in a murine kidney-cell model and is less independently replicated than the canonical IFT-B function. |
| **2022 — human hTERT-RPE1 knockout and rescue** | IFT52 knockout abolished ciliogenesis and basal-body IFT88 localization. Wild-type IFT52 rescued these defects; A199T produced partial rescue with reduced IFT-B–kinesin-II coupling, whereas L293Afs* failed to restore cilia and behaved as a functional null. A199T and Δ139–162 did not visibly alter agonist-induced SMO entry or GPR161 exit, although KIF17 and ICK/CILK1 tip localization was impaired. (pqac-00000012, pqac-00000019, pqac-00000027, pqac-00000041) | Direct human-cell evidence for IFT52 as an IFT-B assembly and motor-coupling hub. The receptor-trafficking assay does not exclude defects in downstream GLI processing, transcription or other signaling contexts. |
| **2023 — in situ cryo-electron tomography in *Chlamydomonas*** | Molecular modeling of anterograde trains placed IFT52 centrally within the repeating IFT-B1 architecture. Its central region associates with IFT88 and contributes with IFT57 and IFT38 to the IFT-B1–IFT-B2 interface; IFT-B repeats occur at approximately 6-nm spacing. (pqac-00000015, pqac-00000023, pqac-00000025) | High-resolution native-context evidence for a structural train-organizing role rather than catalytic activity. The structure is from algal flagella and is not a human IFT-train structure. |
| **2025 — human hTERT-RPE1 knockout studies** | IFT52 knockout eliminated detectable mother-centriole recruitment of IFT38 and IFT88. IFT-B recruitment persisted in non-ciliated KIF3B-knockout cells, showing that recruitment to the mother centriole can precede and occur independently of ciliogenesis. (pqac-00000020, pqac-00000021, pqac-00000039, pqac-00000048) | Direct human-cell evidence that the IFT52-containing IFT-B1b module is required for IFT-B assembly or recruitment at the ciliary base; it does not distinguish defective preassembly from defective centriole docking. |


*Table: Evidence progresses from conserved model-organism structures to human patient cells and targeted human-cell knockouts. The table distinguishes direct human findings from conclusions based on non-human systems.*
