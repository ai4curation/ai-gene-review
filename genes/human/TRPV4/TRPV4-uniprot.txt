CC   -!- FUNCTION: Non-selective calcium permeant cation channel involved in
CC       osmotic sensitivity and mechanosensitivity (PubMed:16293632,
CC       PubMed:18695040, PubMed:18826956, PubMed:22526352, PubMed:23136043,
CC       PubMed:29899501). Activation by exposure to hypotonicity within the
CC       physiological range exhibits an outward rectification (PubMed:18695040,
CC       PubMed:18826956, PubMed:29899501). Also activated by heat, low pH,
CC       citrate and phorbol esters (PubMed:16293632, PubMed:18695040,
CC       PubMed:18826956, PubMed:20037586, PubMed:21964574, PubMed:25256292).
CC       Increase of intracellular Ca(2+) potentiates currents. Channel activity
CC       seems to be regulated by a calmodulin-dependent mechanism with a
CC       negative feedback mechanism (PubMed:12724311, PubMed:18826956).
CC       Promotes cell-cell junction formation in skin keratinocytes and plays
CC       an important role in the formation and/or maintenance of functional
CC       intercellular barriers (By similarity). Acts as a regulator of
CC       intracellular Ca(2+) in synoviocytes (PubMed:19759329). Plays an
CC       obligatory role as a molecular component in the nonselective cation
CC       channel activation induced by 4-alpha-phorbol 12,13-didecanoate and
CC       hypotonic stimulation in synoviocytes and also regulates production of
CC       IL-8 (PubMed:19759329). Together with PKD2, forms mechano- and
CC       thermosensitive channels in cilium (PubMed:18695040). Negatively
CC       regulates expression of PPARGC1A, UCP1, oxidative metabolism and
CC       respiration in adipocytes (By similarity). Regulates expression of
CC       chemokines and cytokines related to pro-inflammatory pathway in
CC       adipocytes (By similarity). Together with AQP5, controls regulatory
CC       volume decrease in salivary epithelial cells (By similarity). Required
CC       for normal development and maintenance of bone and cartilage
CC       (PubMed:26249260). In its inactive state, may sequester DDX3X at the
CC       plasma membrane. When activated, the interaction between both proteins
CC       is affected and DDX3X relocalizes to the nucleus (PubMed:29899501). In
CC       neurons of the central nervous system, could play a role in triggering
CC       voluntary water intake in response to increased sodium concentration in
