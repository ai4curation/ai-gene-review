# TRPV4 human manual literature synthesis

No deep-research provider was available for this session; this file was written
from the cached publications, UniProt, the merged mechanosensation guide, and
the merged rat Trpv4 review.

## Identity and channel activity

- Human TRPV4 is a TRP vanilloid-family channel with an N-terminal ankyrin-repeat
  region, six transmembrane helices, a pore loop between S5 and S6, and cytosolic
  N- and C-termini, matching the topology curated in UniProt and the human splice
  isoform paper [PMID:16293632 "The TRPV4 cation channel exhibits a topology
  consisting of six predicted transmembrane domains and a pore region between the
  fifth and the sixth transmembrane domains"].
- The independent OTRPC4 cloning paper identified a Ca2+-permeable nonselective
  cation channel whose activity increases and decreases with extracellular
  osmolarity [PMID:11025659 "OTRPC4 forms Ca2+-permeable, nonselective cation
  channels that exhibit spontaneous activity in isotonic media and are rapidly
  activated by decreases in, and are inhibited by increases in, extracellular
  osmolarity"].
- Human TRPV4 channel activity is potentiated by intracellular Ca2+ through a
  C-terminal calmodulin-binding site [PMID:12724311 "During TRPV4 activation by
  hypotonic solutions or phorbol esters, Ca2+ entry through the channel increased
  the rate and extent of channel activation"].
- The human isoform study showed that isoforms with intact oligomerization were
  processed through the ER to the plasma membrane and retained TRPV4 stimulus
  responses, whereas splice variants defective for oligomerization were retained
  in the ER [PMID:16293632 "TRPV4 biogenesis involves core glycosylation and
  oligomerization in the endoplasmic reticulum"].

## Osmotic and Mechanical Stimuli

- TRPV4 is best treated as an osmotic/mechano-osmotic channel in this review:
  hypotonic stimulation gates a Ca2+-permeable channel, but hypotonic swelling
  should not be inflated to every detection-of-mechanical-stimulus BP.
- Mouse loss of TRPV4 impairs central osmoregulation, including water intake and
  antidiuretic hormone response after osmotic challenge, supporting the inherited
  osmosensor claim [PMID:14581612 "TRPV4 is necessary for the normal response to
  changes in osmotic pressure and functions as an osmotic sensor in the CNS"].
- A high-viscosity assay in ciliated cells provided the direct `response to
  mechanical stimulus` evidence: human TRPV4 conferred high-viscosity-evoked
  currents on HeLa cells, and hamster oviduct ciliated cells required TRPV4-like
  activity for the immediate high-viscosity current [PMID:15753126
  "TRPV4-transfected HeLa cells also increased their cationic currents in
  response to high viscous load"].
- Human TRPV4 localizes to cilia and interacts with PKD2/TRPP2 in a ciliary
  channel complex [PMID:18695040 "TRPP2 utilizes TRPV4 to form a mechano- and
  thermosensitive molecular sensor in the cilium"]. That supports cilium as a
  non-core location; PKD1/PKD2 should be reviewed separately.

## Peripheral Signaling and Localization Contexts

- AQP4 and TRPV4 form an astrocyte complex required for hypotonicity-elicited
  Ca2+ entry and regulatory volume decrease in rat astrocytes, supporting
  non-core cell-volume rows [PMID:21262839 "TRPV4 is necessary for
  hypotonicity-elicited [Ca 2+ ] o influx"].
- The DDX3X paper showed a human-cell interaction in which TRPV4-mediated Ca2+
  influx releases DDX3X and promotes its nuclear translocation
  [PMID:29899501 "TRPV4-mediated Ca2+ influx releases DDX3X from the channel
  and drives DDX3X nuclear translocation"].
- The rat cytoskeleton paper showed that the TRPV4 C-terminus binds tubulin and
  actin and that channel expression/activation remodels lamellipodia, filopodia,
  growth cones and neurites [PMID:20657843 "Both expression and activation of
  TRPV4 induces striking morphological changes affecting lamellipodial,
  filopodial, growth cone, and neurite structures"].
- TRPV4 controls mouse adipocyte ERK/JNK signaling, chemokine expression and
  Pgc1a/Ucp1-linked respiration; those rows are context-specific or downstream
  organismal consequences, not the core TRPV4 activity [PMID:23021218 "TRPV4
  negatively regulated the expression of PGC1α, UCP1, and cellular
  respiration"].

## Curation Edges

- Bare AQP4 and DDX3X `protein binding` rows are uninformative and were removed
  even though the interactions themselves are real.
- `blood vessel endothelial cell delamination` from PMID:23136043 was left
  undecided. The abstract supports vascular permeability and edema after high
  pulmonary venous pressure, but it does not establish that exact developmental
  process.
- `positive regulation of striated muscle contraction` was left undecided
  because the automatic mouse transfer could not be tied to accessible evidence
  for striated muscle specifically.
