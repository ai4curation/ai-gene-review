# Human CFAP410 review notes

## 2026-09-12 curation session

CFAP410 is the current human symbol for C21ORF2/LRRC76 (UniProt O43822). The seeded review contained 19 annotations, predominantly localizations and generic protein binding. All original annotation records, including their evidence codes, identifiers and supporting entities, were preserved; two missing process annotations were added. No custom sequence analysis was needed because the question is best resolved by experimental literature and published structures.

### Evidence synthesis and limits

- The principal activity supported at molecular-function level is association with the serine/threonine kinase NEK1, linked to primary-cilium assembly and homologous recombination. Generic NEK1 protein-binding rows were modified to GO:0120283, verified through the QuickGO API on 2026-09-12 (definition: “Binding to a protein serine/threonine kinase.”). This is not an assertion that CFAP410 is a kinase or an independently proven kinase activator. [PMID:26167768, *An siRNA-based functional genomics screen for the identification of regulators of ciliogenesis and ciliopathy genes*, “Importantly, both the p.Arg73Pro and p.Leu224Pro mutations in C21orf2 abolished the interaction with NEK1”].
- Endogenous human-cell data strengthen the complex and ciliogenesis evidence. The 2023 paper explicitly treats direct interaction as unresolved; a co-immunoprecipitated complex is not proof of direct contact. [PMID:37188479, *Functional characterization of C21ORF2 association with the NEK1 kinase mutated in human in diseases*, “Here, we show that ARPE-19 cells lacking C21ORF2, like cells lacking NEK1, also show major defects in ciliogenesis.”]. The NEW GO:1905515 non-motile cilium assembly annotation is based on this human knockout/rescue evidence, not on disease association alone. GO identifier, label and definition were verified via QuickGO.
- HR is experimentally supported and distinct from a claimed repair-enzyme activity. [PMID:26290490, *The NEK1 interactor, C21ORF2, is required for efficient DNA damage repair*, “Knockdown of both C21ORF2 and NEK1 resulted in defective HR repair, as shown by significantly decreased percentages of GFP-positive (HR-repaired) cells”]. This paper also distinguishes HR from NHEJ and G2 checkpoint activation, and finds CFAP410 in the cytoplasm without recruitment to irradiation-induced foci. The 2023 study independently reproduces HR impairment. NEW GO:0000724 was verified against QuickGO; no nuclease, recombinase or nuclear-lesion localization is inferred.
- Recent human neuronal work reinforces ciliary dysfunction but does not establish an IFT motor or cargo-adapter activity. [PMID:39703094, *C21ORF2 mutations point towards primary cilia dysfunction in amyotrophic lateral sclerosis*, “In cells with reduced levels of C21ORF2, IFT88 accumulates primarily at the tip of the cilium and forms a ‘bulb’ phenotype”]. IFT distribution and Hedgehog responsiveness may depend on ciliary architecture; the direction of causation warrants mechanistic testing.
- Basal-body and photoreceptor connecting-cilium locations are well supported. [PMID:26167768, “C21orf2 localises to the basal body in mIMCD3 and hTERT-RPE1 cells and to the base of the connecting cilium in mouse photoreceptors”]; [PMID:26294103, *C21orf2 is mutated in recessive early-onset retinal dystrophy with macular staphyloma and encodes a protein that localises to the photoreceptor primary cilium*, “Immunohistochemical studies in human, pig and mouse retinas localised C21orf2 protein to the ciliary structures of the photoreceptor cell”]. The latter cache is abstract-only, but the abstract directly addresses the localization.
- The Suga retinal paper is abstract-only in the cache. It supports photoreceptor cilia, while the finer outer-segment assignment is retained on the original curator's assessment rather than falsely claimed as independently resolved by the abstract. [PMID:27548899, *Identification of Novel Mutations in the LRR-Cap Domain of C21orf2 in Japanese Patients With Retinitis Pigmentosa and Cone-Rod Dystrophy*, “the protein localized to the photoreceptor cilia in the adult retina.”].
- Mitochondrial localization remains non-core, not rejected: the early experiment used antibody staining and a mitochondrial dye. Its physiological role and isoform dependence remain uncertain. [PMID:9325172, *Immunochemical characterization of a novel mitochondrially located protein encoded by a nuclear gene within the DFNB8/10 critical region on 21q22.3*, “Immunofluorescence studies and co-staining with a mitochondrial-specific dye suggest the subcellular localization of the protein to mitochondria.”]. The cache is abstract-only but explicitly describes the experiment.
- PMID:21834987 full text identifies a CFAP410 knockdown phenotype in PC3 cells (Table 2: “C21orf2cortical actin 55%NPNPNPNP”). This is not a CFAP410-protein localization assay. The specific primary evidence behind its plasma-membrane IDA row was not recovered, so that annotation is UNDECIDED. Its cytoplasm row is independently supported by Fang et al. No misattribution is alleged. The later [author correction (PMID:39390519)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11465620/), checked online because its cache lacks full text, concerns other genes' Figure 6 panels, not CFAP410 localization.
- The ATOX1 interaction from the systematic interactome survey is not disputed; the generic protein-binding term was removed as uninformative. Neither copper handling nor an adapter function can be inferred from that pair. [PMID:25416956, *A proteome-scale map of the human interactome network*, “By systematically screening half of the interactome space with minimal inspection bias, we more than doubled the number of high-quality binary PPIs available from the literature.”].

### Structural context

Published studies explain the domain architecture without requiring a new computational analysis. The CTD structures include the human protein, but the localization-disruption experiment was in *T. brucei*, which should not be described as a human localization assay. [PMID:39255848, *The C-terminus of CFAP410 forms a tetrameric helical bundle that is essential for its localization to the basal body*, “The crystal structures reveal that the three proteins all adopt the same conformation as a tetrameric helical bundle.”; “Our work further demonstrates that the tetrameric assembly of the CTD is essential for the correct localization of CFAP410 in T. brucei”].

The 2025 N-terminal crystal structure is also from *T. brucei*. Its human CFAP410–NEK1 interface is modeled, not experimentally solved. [PMID:40018707, *CFAP410 has a bimodular architecture with a conserved surface patch on its N-terminal leucine-rich repeat motif for binding interaction partners*, “We carried out structural modelling using AlphaFold-multimer for the complex of HsCFAP410-NTD (aa1-150) and HsNEK1-CTD (aa1208-1286).”]. These structures support a modular interaction protein but do not warrant inventing a specific scaffold or kinase-activator function.

## 2026-09-13 targeted localization audit (primary-source work)

### Plasma membrane: stronger evidence of a source mismatch

Live [QuickGO annotation search](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/QuickGO/services/annotation/search?geneProductId=UniProtKB%3AO43822&goId=GO%3A0005886&limit=100), retrieved 2026-09-13, identifies the CFAP410 plasma-membrane annotation as UniProt-assigned IDA, PMID:21834987, date 20110926, annotation ID `UniProtKB:O43822!178120408`, with no extension. A second query restricting that PMID and GO term returned only CFAP410 and ARC, both dated 20110926. This does not demonstrate how the error arose, but argues against assuming that every gene in the screen was assigned the term.

The complete Bai paper distinguishes its screen from its localization experiment. The localization results explicitly concern other named proteins:

- [PMID:21834987, Results, Fig. 8] “To investigate how overexpression of selected PMMs affected cell morphology and cytoskeletal organisation and to determine their localization, cDNAs for ARC, FAM40A, FAM40B, FMNL3 and ZRANB1 were cloned downstream of GFP and transfected into PC3 cells (Figure 8A).”
- [PMID:21834987] “GFP-ARC, FAM40A, FAM40B and FMNL3 proteins showed cytoplasmic localization with some enrichment on the plasma membrane compared to GFP alone (Figure 8B).”

The actual Fig. 8 image was inspected and agrees with this explicit list: no CFAP410 localization panel. CFAP410 instead appears in the knockdown morphology screen. Table 2 defines its cortical-actin phenotype; this observes actin after CFAP410 depletion, not localization of CFAP410 itself.

**Supplement audit and reproducibility.** The [Europe PMC full-text XML](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC3201212/fullTextXML) lists 14 supplemental files. The [supplementaryFiles ZIP endpoint](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC3201212/supplementaryFiles) was downloaded, its filenames inventoried, the Word tables extracted using macOS `textutil -convert txt -stdout`, and Fig. 8 inspected as `1741-7007-9-54-8.jpg`. Temporary originals reside under `/tmp/cfap410-supplements/`; they were not inserted into the cached publication text. The two tables relevant to hidden localization evidence are:

- `1741-7007-9-54-S1.DOC` (Table S1): CFAP410/C21orf2 row lists CG14995, Entrez 755, prior Down-syndrome association, domains, and interaction partners; it contains no localization experiment. Its “Functional data” entry reads “Linked with Down syndrome (Shim et al., 2003).” Word-field XML was removed only from the temporary extraction for readability.
- `1741-7007-9-54-S4.DOC` (Table S2): validates siRNA phenotypes for ARC, FAM40A, FAM40B, FMNL3, FNBP3, LIMD1 and ZRANB1; CFAP410 is absent. ### Mitochondria: cell context and isoform uncertainty

The original PMID:9325172 remains abstract-only after publisher/author-repository access checks. It explicitly reports polyclonal-antiserum staining, a 25-kDa immunoreactive band and co-staining with a mitochondrial dye. No isoform-resolved localization or validated molecular-weight-to-isoform mapping was recovered. RefSeq's inherited “nuclear encoded mitochondrial protein” product names are not independent isoform-specific experiments.

A newly retrieved full-text primary paper provides a context-specific counterpoint: [PMID:26974433, *Axial Spondylometaphyseal Dysplasia Is Caused by C21orf2 Mutations*, Fig. 6O–Q] “We stained mitochondria by Kusabira-Orange fused with mitochondria localizing signal and found that subcellular distribution of the C21orf2-EGFP fusion protein was complementary to that of mitochondria (Fig 6O–6Q).” These are mouse photoreceptor experiments. They argue against universal mitochondrial localization but do not refute a human immune-cell pool. The old assay's EBV-transformed-B-cell context is stated retrospectively in Wang et al.; it has not been independently recovered from the original full text. The 2024 V58L respiratory phenotype also does not demonstrate where CFAP410 resides.

### Photoreceptor outer segment versus connecting cilium

The Suga PMID:27548899 publisher page remained inaccessible (HTTP 403) through the DOI route. The publicly exposed [ResearchGate item](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/306395326_Identification_of_Novel_Mutations_in_the_LRR-Cap_Domain_ofC21orf2in_Japanese_Patients_With_Retinitis_Pigmentosa_and_Cone-Rod_Dystrophy), despite its “full text” label, exposes supplementary tables/figures, not the main paper text. Its supplementary negative-control legend explicitly names mouse and monkey retina. The fine outer-segment assignment therefore remains unverified from this source; it should not be presented as established in human tissue merely because GOA uses human CFAP410.

Wang PMID:26974433 Fig. 6 separately shows a connecting-cilium-localized fusion signal extending within PNA-positive cone outer segments. This illustrates why “within an outer-segment region” and a resolved outer-segment subcompartment need careful distinction; it is not a replacement for reading Suga's actual images and methods.

### HPA evidence strength

Current [HPA subcellular data](https://www.proteinatlas.org/ENSG00000160226-CFAP410/subcellular) call cytosol and basal body “supported,” and Golgi/primary-cilium transition zone “approved.” Cell-specific calls are cytosol in A-431/U-251MG/U2OS; Golgi and basal body in ASC52telo; nucleoplasm, transition zone and cytosol in RPTEC/TERT1; Golgi and transition zone in serum-starved hTERT-RPE1. There is no current plasma-membrane call. Older HPA pages listing GO:0005886 reproduce GO annotations and are not a distinct experimental observation.

The [antibody page](https://www.proteinatlas.org/ENSG00000160226-CFAP410/summary/antibody) was checked as an HTML table with each antibody column preserved. The HPA030284 column reports rabbit polyclonal antibody, standard ICC “Supported” with the explanation “The subcellular location is supported by literature.” It does not show enhanced knockdown/tag-based validation. The same antibody's Western-blot column is “Uncertain,” explaining “Only bands not corresponding to the predicted size.” Its 87-aa antigen matches CFAP410-202/ENSP00000344566 at 100% and CFAP410-201/ENSP00000317302 at 99%; it does not distinguish those isoforms. This warrants caution for HPA-only compartments, while cytosol/basal body have independent literature support.

**Construct and GO-definition refinement:** Wang et al. methods identify the fusion as human `NM_004928.2` CDS fused in-frame to EGFP, expressed by AAV in six-week-old C57BL6 mouse retina. Thus the negative mitochondrial comparison concerns human protein in mouse photoreceptors, rather than a demonstrated endogenous human-retina result. Its Results further state that the signal “appeared strictly confined to the cilia without dispersing into the surrounding outer segment structures”; its Figure 6E–H legend states that the fusion was not detected in outer segments. The experiment distinguishes the connecting cilium from surrounding outer-segment structures even when signal extends within the cone-outer-segment region.

QuickGO term definitions and `is_a,part_of` ancestor closures were queried for GO:0032391 and GO:0001750 on 2026-09-13. Connecting cilium (the portion linking inner and outer segments, equivalent to transition zone) is not a subclass or part-of descendant of outer segment (the disc-containing compartment). A connecting-cilium observation therefore does not automatically validate an outer-segment annotation. QuickGO also defines plasma membrane to include associated proteins; absence of a transmembrane helix is not grounds to exclude peripheral membrane association.

The independently verified source audit has now been reflected in the YAML: PMID:21834987 is MISCITED specifically for localization, plasma membrane remains UNDECIDED, the cytoplasm row retains acceptance on independent evidence, and mitochondrial rows retain non-core status with the human-protein/mouse-photoreceptor counterpoint from PMID:26974433. No original annotation identifiers/evidence codes were changed. OpenScientist reconciliation is still pending.

### OpenScientist reconciliation and final decisions

The targeted [OpenScientist report](CFAP410-deep-research-openscientist.md), generated in 1096.17 seconds over five iterations, and its HTML report were reviewed after completion. The generated Markdown and HTML/PDF sidecars are preserved unchanged. The report independently agrees with the primary-source finding that Bai2011 did not localize CFAP410, including the Figure 8 protein list and supplement inventory. It is cited as a secondary audit; primary snippets remain the basis for the annotation decision.

Several provider conclusions are **not adopted**:

1. Calling the plasma-membrane localization “CONTRADICTED” conflates an unsupported source with an experimentally established negative localization. The actual internal curation error mechanism—phenotype/localization confusion versus copying another protein's result—is speculative. Missing plasma membrane from UniProt's prose is a consistency clue, not decisive biological counterevidence. **PM remains UNDECIDED; the particular PMID is MISCITED for localization.**
2. Suga outer-segment remapping relies on abstract wording. The main article/figures were not recovered in our independent check, and the report provides no decisive fine-compartment primary evidence that resolves this. Its statement that Khan “explicitly” excludes outer segment is also stronger than the accessible abstract's list of observed ciliary compartments. **The original outer-segment annotations are retained with the existing access caveat**, pending main-article adjudication. Wang's distinct fusion-protein experiment establishes a relevant compartment-specific comparison, not an automatic global override of Suga.
3. Fang2015's inaccessibility is a provider access limitation, not a repository limitation. Our cached PMID:26290490 contains the full text, including the direct observation of cytoplasmic HA-tagged CFAP410 in HeLa cells and its lack of recruitment to irradiation-induced foci. **Cytoplasm remains accepted on this independently read evidence.**
4. The report's broad “Supported” HPA characterization does not capture the individual Golgi/transition-zone “approved” versus basal-body/cytosol “supported” grades, the antibody's standard literature-based ICC validation, or its uncertain Western blot. **No new HPA-only compartment is promoted to a core function.**
5. PMID:37188479 is not merely an association-only study: the full text includes endogenous complex and localization data, ciliogenesis knockout/rescue experiments and HR reporters. Its failure to demonstrate a specific catalytic/activator/scaffold mechanism must not erase those actual process-level experiments. **The ciliary and HR core functions remain supported, with specific kinase association rather than an invented activator activity.**
6. The report does not establish an isoform-resolved mitochondrial assay or an experimental absence of mitochondrial localization. **Historical mitochondrial annotations remain non-core**, supplemented by the human-CFAP410/mouse-photoreceptor comparison and a proposal for controlled endogenous localization studies. The report labels PMID:39227882 as a 2025 neuronal study in its table; its cached publication year is 2024.

The OpenScientist reference is marked DISPUTED to make these partial disagreements machine-readable while retaining its useful source-audit finding. Original GOA fields remain intact; suggested localization experiments now distinguish native peripheral plasma-membrane association from basal-body/ciliary signal using functional endogenous tags, knockout controls and orthogonal fractionation in retinal, neuronal and immune-cell contexts. No external annotation-quality report was sent.

### Final adjudication: remove the unsupported IDA, retain the biological question

After discussion of the distinction between an annotation record and a biological possibility, the PM action was changed from UNDECIDED to **REMOVE**. The initial uncertainty was appropriate when the specific localization experiment had not been fully checked. The completed audit now establishes that Bai2011's localization experiment assayed a named set of other proteins; full text, the actual Fig. 8, relevant supplemental Word tables and the complete supplement inventory establish that this citation does not support a CFAP410 IDA. OpenScientist independently corroborates that finding.

The removal is therefore **evidence-specific**: the asserted direct-assay support is wrong. It does **not** mean CFAP410 is experimentally proven absent from the plasma membrane, does not create a NOT annotation, and does not exclude a peripheral membrane pool. This preserves the useful part of OpenScientist's removal recommendation while rejecting its biological “CONTRADICTED” framing and speculative reconstruction of the internal curation error. The original annotation fields are preserved; only its review action/rationale change. The cytoplasm term remains accepted because it has independent positive evidence, and its Bai source mismatch remains explicit. The targeted localization experiment remains in the YAML to resolve the biological question.
