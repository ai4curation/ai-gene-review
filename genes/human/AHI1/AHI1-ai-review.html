Gene Description

AHI1 encodes Jouberin, a 1196-residue modular protein with no catalytic domain, no nucleotide- or metal-binding site and no assigned EC number. Its architecture is entirely built from interaction modules: an N-terminal coiled coil (residues 13-45), a long disordered region, seven WD40 repeats (607-926) and a C-terminal SH3 domain (1051-1111). The coiled coil is present in the human protein but absent from the mouse orthologue. Jouberin is concentrated at the base of the primary cilium. It occupies a ring-shaped domain at the distal end of the mother centriole and is a subunit of the MKS (tectonic-like) transition-zone complex, the assembly that forms the Y-shaped links between the axonemal microtubules and the ciliary membrane and acts as a diffusion barrier separating the ciliary compartment from the rest of the cell. It is also found at the centrosome and at epithelial adherens junctions, and a non-ciliary cytoplasmic pool exists. The molecular activities that have been measured are all protein-protein associations, each through a different part of the chain. The WD40 repeats bind nephrocystin-1, and the two proteins co-migrate as species whose masses are read as a heterotetramer and a heterodimer; a region between the coiled coil and the WD40 repeats (141-434) binds huntingtin-associated protein 1, likewise co-migrating as a species read as a heterotetramer; the SH3 domain binds the large GTPase dynamin-2; and Jouberin binds itself. Both heterotetramer assignments rest on the same kind of evidence and are hedged to the same degree by the source and by UniProt: they are inferred from migration behaviour in cell lysates, so they describe the reported architecture rather than a measured one. In the interferon pathway it acts as a genuine adaptor, holding the deubiquitinase OTUD1 against the kinase TYK2 so that TYK2 is deubiquitinated and stabilised - the association of OTUD1 with TYK2 is lost when Jouberin is absent, while the Jouberin-TYK2 association survives loss of OTUD1. Jouberin also binds beta-catenin and carries it into the nucleus through its own nuclear-localisation signals, amplifying an already-activated canonical Wnt response rather than initiating one; the primary cilium opposes this by sequestering Jouberin away from the nucleus. Loss of Jouberin disrupts ciliogenesis and ciliary cargo delivery: cilia are fewer, ARL13B fails to accumulate in the remaining cilia, RAB8A is destabilised and lost from the basal body, and photoreceptor outer segments fail to form with opsin mislocalised throughout the cell. Biallelic AHI1 mutations cause Joubert syndrome type 3, a cerebello-oculo-renal ciliopathy defined by cerebellar vermis hypoplasia and the molar tooth sign, with variable retinal dystrophy and nephronophthisis, and missense alleles in the WD40 repeats also cause non-syndromic retinitis pigmentosa.

Core Functions

Structural subunit of the MKS (tectonic-like) transition-zone complex, held in a ring-shaped domain at the distal end of the mother centriole. Through this position AHI1 is required for primary cilia to form and for ciliary cargo to be delivered and retained: cells lacking it make fewer cilia, and the cilia they do make fail to accumulate ARL13B, while RAB8A is destabilised and lost from the basal body. The binding activities that place it there are its own - the WD40 beta-propeller binds nephrocystin-1 into a complex reported as an AHI1(2):NPHP1(2) heterotetramer, and the protein binds itself. Both stoichiometries are read off migration behaviour in cell lysates and are hedged in the source, so they are reported rather than measured. The molecular_function slot should be read as inferred by transfer, not as demonstrated at this location. The only bridging assay in the AHI1 literature - AHI1 holding OTUD1 against TYK2, with the reciprocal control - was done in mouse macrophages in an interferon pathway, and no experiment has tested whether AHI1 is required for any two transition-zone proteins to associate with each other. What is established at the ciliary base is the position, the partner binding and the requirement; the adaptor mechanism there is an extrapolation from a different compartment.

Current model (mechanistic narrative)

AHI1 (Jouberin) is a modular SH3- and WD40-repeat scaffolding/adaptor protein that operates at the primary cilium transition zone and in neuronal cytoplasmic signaling, with loss-of-function mutations causing Joubert syndrome featuring cerebellar malformation and abnormal axonal decussation [PMID:15322546, PMID:15467982]. At the ciliary base, AHI1 localizes to the mother centriole/basal body, where its recruitment to a ring-shaped transition-zone domain depends on Cby1, and where it is required for ciliogenesis and polarized vesicular trafficking by stabilizing the small GTPase Rab8a [PMID:19625297, PMID:25103236]. AHI1 governs ciliary cargo: it sustains Arl13b ciliary membrane localization and proteasomal stability to support Sonic hedgehog signaling and proper axoneme length, mediates outer-segment protein transport in photoreceptors, and targets the GPCRs MCHR1 to the ciliary membrane for downstream cAMP/ERK signaling [PMID:20592197, PMID:31391239, PMID:33741721]. Its WD40 region binds NPHP1, and Joubert/retinitis pigmentosa mutations in this region destabilize AHI1 and impair its ciliary-base enrichment, whereas the C-terminal SH3 domain is dispensable for normal development [PMID:23532844, PMID:25616960, PMID:28442542]. In neurons AHI1 forms a stable complex with HAP1 that regulates TrkB receptor endocytic sorting and signaling, and AHI1 controls additional partners including 5-HT2CR, Cend1, GR, and APP to influence neurite outgrowth, feeding behavior, and stress-related/depressive phenotypes [PMID:18636121, PMID:20956301, PMID:22123816, PMID:23658157, PMID:31062249, PMID:33782379]. Beyond ciliary and neuronal roles, AHI1 has hematopoietic and immune functions: an AHI-1/BCR-ABL/JAK2 complex assembled via its SH3 domain modulates JAK2-STAT5 phosphorylation and tyrosine kinase inhibitor resistance in leukemia, and AHI1 recruits the deubiquitinase OTUD1 to stabilize Tyk2 and maintain basal type-I interferon signaling [PMID:18936234, PMID:22623184, PMID:35821088].
