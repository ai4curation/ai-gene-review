# GCH1 notes

## 2026-11-04 — Catalysis, regulation and evidence boundaries

GCH1 supplies a pteridine precursor; the triphosphate stays attached and formate is released. The primary reaction does not release pyrophosphate. Regulation must distinguish GCH1 from its GFRP partner ([PMID:33229582](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33229582/)). The current product descriptions separate natural sequences from engineered proteins ([PMID:8068008](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8068008/)).

Cofactor supply can regulate nitric oxide synthase activity without making GCH1 the enzyme that synthesizes nitric oxide or dopamine. Similarly, the superoxide study measures production through enzyme uncoupling, which differs from removal ([PMID:12176133](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12176133/), [PMID:19666465](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19666465/)). The human tetrahydrofolate mapping is not justified by a family whose shared catalytic step feeds different downstream pathways in bacteria and vertebrates ([InterPro IPR001474](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/interpro/entry/InterPro/IPR001474/), [IPR018234](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/interpro/entry/InterPro/IPR018234/)).

The positive GTPase and unresolved vesicle/neuronal-terminal claims retain uncertainty where full supporting assays were not accessible. A verified PAX6 citation mismatch is recorded without guessing a replacement identifier; independent genuine GCH1 evidence supports the complex function. [Original-reading scope](GCH1-external-evidence.md) gives the exact distinctions.

Current decisions were assessed individually.
