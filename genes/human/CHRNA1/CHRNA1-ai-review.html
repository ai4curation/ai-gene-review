Gene Description

CHRNA1 encodes the alpha1 subunit of the skeletal-muscle nicotinic acetylcholine receptor. Two alpha1 chains join beta1, delta and either fetal gamma or adult epsilon to form a ligand-gated cation channel at the postsynaptic neuromuscular junction. Alpha1 contributes acetylcholine-recognition interfaces and pore-gating elements that convert motor-neuron transmitter release into endplate depolarization and muscle contraction. The assembled receptor also conducts calcium, with permeability influenced strongly by the complementary subunits. Pathogenic variants can impair receptor expression or alter gating and agonist affinity, causing congenital myasthenic syndromes; severe biallelic defects can cause fetal akinesia. Human splice products differ: the P3A-containing alpha+ product was not incorporated into functional receptors in the tested heterologous system.

Core Functions

Alpha1 contributes ligand-recognition and pore-gating elements to the muscle acetylcholine receptor heteropentamer, directly converting acetylcholine binding into postsynaptic cation current. Human receptor reconstitution and patient endplate recordings establish the transmission mechanism; two alpha chains act with beta/delta and gamma or epsilon. Calcium conduction is real but the adult calcium fraction is strongly influenced by epsilon, not established as an alpha1-only property. The current displayed P02708-2 functional product must be distinguished from P3A-containing P02708-1; the latter was not incorporated into functional receptors in the original human-product expression study. Existing annotation relations are preserved, while this synthesis explicitly represents subunit contribution.

Calcium and synaptic compartment

Actual full PMID:16527851 Methods and Results directly establish calcium entry through human alpha-containing muscle receptors. Adult alpha/beta/delta/epsilon combinations were expressed in rat GH4C1 cells, giving a fractional calcium current near 7%; fetal combinations were around 2–3% and were corroborated in human myotubes. Intracellular-store controls and nontransfected controls support the measured conductance. Adult native-muscle fractional current was not directly measured. Epsilon largely explains the human–mouse adult difference, and the tested alpha mutations do not alter the calcium fraction. Alpha1 participation in transport is therefore supported, without assigning autonomous calcium selectivity. This same paper directly measures nicotine-evoked current, retained as a non-core pharmacological response. PMID:16527851
