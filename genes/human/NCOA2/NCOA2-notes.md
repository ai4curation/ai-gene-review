# NCOA2 evidence and annotation notes

## 2026-10-26: Human nuclear-receptor coregulator review

NCOA2/SRC2/TIF2 (Q15596) is a p160 nuclear-receptor coactivator. [PMID:8670870](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8670870/) establishes human nuclear TIF2, direct ligand/AF2-dependent receptor binding and transcriptional activation. [PMID:9430642](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9430642/) dissects its three LXXLL modules and CBP-dependent and independent activation regions. [PMID:11265755](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11265755/) shows TIF2 can bridge ERalpha AF1 and AF2. Thus the UniProt summary's AF2-versus-AF1 wording should not be generalized to every receptor, and an AR signaling module must not make NCOA2 universal or interchangeable with every SRC paralog.

The human AR study [PMID:15563469](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15563469/) supports recruitment of selected SRC LXXLL motifs by AR LBD at the family/experimental-system level. The available abstract does not reconstruct the exact seeded NCOA2 construct; that original pair remains uncertain. It complements, rather than erases, SRC1 AF1 evidence from PMID10567563. No NEW AR process annotation was added just to fill a module.

Targeted cached Results and Methods of [PMID:22504882](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22504882/) explicitly identify the human TIF2 NR-box-3 peptide in the LRH1–DLPC structure. This supports the nuclear-receptor-binding refinement, with peptide/full-length scope retained. Other structural or interactome pairs whose exact target construct/table was not resolved remain uncertain. AhR-domain association is explicit in [PMID:15641800](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15641800/), permitting replacement of the now-obsolete receptor-specific binding term by the transcription-factor-binding class.

Targeted full-text Results, Figure 7 and Methods in [PMID:39531503](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39531503/) show that human NCOA2, unlike NCOA1/NCOA3, suppresses Ci/GLI activity in the tested systems. Human transgenes were tested in flies; 293T co-IP/reporter assays, mouse 3T3 endogenous associations and human lung-cell perturbations were distinguished. NCOA2 reduced GLI-mediated output; depletion increased GLI2 target-promoter binding. The existing inhibitor/Hedgehog annotations are retained as contextual functions, not a generic rule that all SRC proteins inhibit GLI or a universal tumor-suppressor label.

CLOCK–BMAL1 coactivation is a curated by-similarity role. Circadian rhythm is refined to the already present circadian regulation of gene expression, while a precise human locomotor phenotype remains unresolved. Metabolic transfer annotations require the original ortholog causal experiments; the coactivation mechanism alone cannot establish all downstream glucose/lipid physiology. PMID16373399's target-specific PPAR comparator and physiology claims remain uncertain because the accessible abstract concentrates on PGC1alpha and ligand effects.

## Evidence access and review method

Normal `fetch-gene` seeded the review and immutable UniProt/GOA sources. Default Falcon research with Perplexity-lite fallback was attempted concurrently with publication caching; both providers failed (authentication/quota errors in `.cache/module-audit-logs/NCOA2-research.log`). No manual text was written under a provider-generated filename. Primary PubMed/PMC caches were fetched through the normal tools; source files were not manually edited. The normal cache frequently contains only abstracts. Positive target-specific abstract statements can support a claim; missing target detail is recorded as UNDECIDED, never as a wrong-gene conclusion based solely on the title. Full-text inspection was targeted to the passages specified below, rather than a claim to have read every source in full.

All original annotation IDs, labels, evidence codes, qualifiers, supporting entities and isoform fields are preserved. Only review judgments and author-supplied synthesis are changed. No NEW annotations are proposed. Current GO term definitions/obsolescence were checked through QuickGO (2026-10-26; diagnostic JSON in `.cache/module-audit-logs/coregulator-terms.json`). Obsolete original source IDs remain intact; author-proposed replacements use current terms. The PAINT assertions are not treated as pairwise transfers or rejected based on donor count. No independent reconstruction of their whole phylogeny is claimed.

Bare protein binding is refined when the cited evidence resolves a useful partner class. Uninformative but verified cofactor associations are removed under the current GO:0005515 policy, without denying the interaction. Unresolved pair tables, constructs or electronic source claims remain UNDECIDED. Chromatin occupancy does not by itself establish direct or sequence-specific DNA recognition. The original bile-transport pathway mappings are flagged as overextended: GO:0015721 explicitly describes directed movement by a transporter/pore or equivalent, whereas SRC proteins supply transcriptional regulation.

The review by Sun and Xu ([PMID:32449767](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32449767/)) supplies broader context: animal models establish many endocrine, neural and behavioral roles, and coregulator functions overlap without being identical. Its expression maps are not direct evidence that every human ortholog executes every brain-development process. The review's target-specific mechanisms and animal-model scope were inspected; no cancer association is promoted to an intrinsic core molecular function.

72 source assertions reviewed; 34 ACCEPT, 5 KEEP_AS_NON_CORE, 2 MARK_AS_OVER_ANNOTATED, 11 MODIFY, 20 UNDECIDED. The status is COMPLETE with explicit unresolved claims, not a statement that all evidence gaps have been closed.
