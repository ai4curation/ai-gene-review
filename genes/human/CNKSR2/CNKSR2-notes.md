Removing the terminal motif or the appropriate self-association region disrupted the relevant contacts; a MAGUIN C-terminal fragment assembled the three-protein complex. ### Postsynaptic organization and spatial signaling

Rat-neuron experiments connect CNK2–Vilse binding to localization and spatial Rac signaling, with wild-type rescue, interaction-site mutants and competitive fragments. The isolated binding fragment did not alter Vilse catalytic activity; Vilse supplies the GAP chemistry. These performed organizing actions support the already-existing postsynapse terms without adding new Rac or spine-development processes [PMID:24656827].

The TNIK study uses mouse Cnk2 constructs and rat neuronal preparations. Full-length protein recruits TNIK to membranes in heterologous cells; loss of the C-terminal membrane-association region redistributes the bound kinase. In neurons the effect is smaller, and CNK2 knockdown did not significantly alter dendritic TNIK content. The authors did not establish that altered TNIK explains the PSD-size effect. Endogenous rat imaging separately supports postsynaptic location [PMID:32235845].

Mouse Cnksr2 loss reduces postsynaptic PSD-95 signal and AMPA-receptor cluster intensity while leaving cluster density and total surface-receptor abundance distinct from those changes. The mouse MAGUIN/rat afadin construct experiments support a partner-assembly mechanism; they are not human endogenous assays. These results support the existing organization annotations and one integrated scaffold core [PMID:36803960]. The older rat MAGUIN abstract positively reports cell bodies, neurites, membrane association and brain coimmunoprecipitation [PMID:10207009]. The mouse phosphoproteomics table for PMID:17114649 remains uninspected, so that exact supporting relationship is UNVERIFIED even though independent neuronal evidence supports the location.
