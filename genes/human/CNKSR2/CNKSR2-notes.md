### Organizing activity and products

The original human-library experiment identified CNKSR2 as a Densin interactor. Subsequent mechanistic assays used rat MAGUIN-1 fragments with human Densin and PSD-95. Removing the terminal motif or the appropriate self-association region disrupted the relevant contacts; a MAGUIN C-terminal fragment assembled the three-protein complex. The original assay does not resolve a fixed dimer versus a larger oligomer, nor does it demonstrate a full-length human endogenous ternary complex. Current GO:0140378 describes the integral organizing role more precisely than the broad adaptor assertion. The scaffold is the component that performs the assembly work, not an enzyme or a passive substrate [PMID:12390249].

A literal MINT metadata discrepancy is retained: positive pair EBI-8548334 assigns Densin residues 1242–1442, which the original paper identifies as the negative PDZ-deleted construct. The actual positive human-library bait spans 1242–1537. The positive interaction and PDZ refinement remain supported by the original experiment and independent target peptide assays; the raw source record is not repaired by hand.

Official UniProt features reconstruct the four products to 1034, 898, 849 and 1004 residues. Products Q8WXI2-2 and -4 omit the terminal 899–1034 segment; -4 also lacks 271–319. Product -5 lacks 435–464 but retains the terminal motif. Exact official ENA translations for the original CNK2A/B cDNAs match current -1/-2. Rat experimental coordinates are preserved as rat coordinates, with an independently recorded alignment. Retaining a sequence motif in -5 does not demonstrate its whole-protein activity. These differences belong in interpretation, while all four machine product objects remain exact.

### Binding evidence

The human terminal 1025–1034 peptide binds isolated PDZ domains in the fragmentomics study. The 22 source-specific partner groups are refined to GO:0030165, with precise partner ranges and tags in the index. The source measures peptide/domain affinity; it does not prove that every pair forms a native full-length complex. APBA1 includes a residue-655 feature; a separate NHERF4 isoform-2 record is preserved as such, alongside the two exact canonical-partner records [PMID:36115835].

The EGFR interaction is independently positive in human-library MaMTH and tagged coimmunoprecipitation, including wild-type EGFR. CNK2 showed similar binding patterns for wild-type and tested oncogenic EGFR variants. The detailed TACC3 cancer mechanism in that paper is not transferred to CNKSR2 [PMID:27956147]. Human CNK2 binds Raf and Rlf in the positive original abstract, supporting membrane localization and kinase-partner specificity. Full Methods/Results of that 2003 report remained unavailable after bounded legitimate retrieval attempts; neither pathway direction nor a new catalytic role is inferred [PMID:14597674].

Human CNK2 and fly HYP SAM fragments form a stable 1:1 complex in the separately preserved full text. This supports heterodimerization at that precise fragment and cross-species scope. The functional fly-cell RAF experiments and the human/fly complex that did not bind fly KSR are distinguished from a native human signaling complex [PMID:18287031].

### Postsynaptic organization and spatial signaling

Rat-neuron experiments connect CNK2–Vilse binding to localization and spatial Rac signaling, with wild-type rescue, interaction-site mutants and competitive fragments. The isolated binding fragment did not alter Vilse catalytic activity; Vilse supplies the GAP chemistry. These performed organizing actions support the already-existing postsynapse terms without adding new Rac or spine-development processes [PMID:24656827].

The TNIK study uses mouse Cnk2 constructs and rat neuronal preparations. Full-length protein recruits TNIK to membranes in heterologous cells; loss of the C-terminal membrane-association region redistributes the bound kinase. In neurons the effect is smaller, and CNK2 knockdown did not significantly alter dendritic TNIK content. The authors did not establish that altered TNIK explains the PSD-size effect. Endogenous rat imaging separately supports postsynaptic location [PMID:32235845].

Mouse Cnksr2 loss reduces postsynaptic PSD-95 signal and AMPA-receptor cluster intensity while leaving cluster density and total surface-receptor abundance distinct from those changes. The mouse MAGUIN/rat afadin construct experiments support a partner-assembly mechanism; they are not human endogenous assays. These results support the existing organization annotations and one integrated scaffold core [PMID:36803960]. The older rat MAGUIN abstract positively reports cell bodies, neurites, membrane association and brain coimmunoprecipitation [PMID:10207009]. The mouse phosphoproteomics table for PMID:17114649 remains uninspected, so that exact supporting relationship is UNVERIFIED even though independent neuronal evidence supports the location.
