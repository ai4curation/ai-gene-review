Gene Description

DCLRE1C encodes Artemis, a metal-dependent DNA nuclease with an N-terminal metallo-β-lactamase/β-CASP catalytic region and a regulatory C-terminal region. Artemis cleaves exposed single-stranded DNA and DNA hairpin or overhang structures during nuclear DNA end processing. In cooperation with DNA-PK, it opens coding-end hairpins generated during V(D)J recombination and processes a subset of complex double-strand breaks for nonhomologous end joining. These activities support antigen-receptor assembly and genome integrity. Purified full-length Artemis also exhibits weaker 5′ exonuclease activity on single-stranded DNA, whose physiological contribution is less well established.

Core Functions

Catalyzes metal-dependent endonucleolytic cleavage of exposed single-stranded regions in DNA hairpins and overhangs. In nuclear repair assemblies, Artemis opens coding-end hairpins for V(D)J recombination and processes complex DNA breaks before ligation during nonhomologous end joining. DNA-PKcs and the DNA/Ku context regulate access to these substrates.

DCLRE1C biological notes

DCLRE1C encodes Artemis, a metal-dependent DNA nuclease with an N-terminal metallo-β-lactamase/β-CASP region and a regulatory C-terminal region. Its central performed role is internal cleavage of exposed DNA regions, including coding-end hairpins and overhangs, before DNA ligase IV joins the processed ends. Purified human-protein experiments distinguish ssDNA endonucleolysis from terminal trimming, and human patient-derived chromosomal recombination assays link the catalytic region to coding-joint formation [PMID:20117966; PMID:14744996; PMID:11955432]. One integrated core joins this chemistry to NHEJ and V(D)J recombination. The new V(D)J assertion rests on a performed incision step plus direct cellular assays, rather than immune deficiency alone.

DNA-PKcs helps license Artemis end processing in a substrate-, salt- and Ku-dependent manner. DNA-PKcs autophosphorylation must be distinguished from phosphorylation of Artemis itself: the latter is not a universal obligatory catalytic switch. Historical Reactome summaries retain earlier phosphorylation language; the positive Artemis location and end-processing paths remain usable without adopting every mechanistic statement [PMID:16874298; PMID:20117966; Reactome:R-HSA-5693533]. Artemis is the nuclease; PRKDC is the protein kinase and LIG4 is the joining enzyme.

The weaker exonuclease activity has a substantive experimental history. Hydroxyapatite fractionation separated most exonuclease from Artemis in one study. A later study used WT/H115A, cofractionation, circular-dichroism, association, poly(dA), end-label and inhibition controls to support intrinsic nonprocessive ssDNA exonuclease. An independent full-length preparation also showed weak activity, whereas its engineered catalytic-domain construct did not. These results support retention of the measured biochemical capability, with physiological importance unresolved, rather than removal as an assumed contaminant or promotion to a second core [PMID:20347402; PMID:24500713; PMID:34387696]. The corresponding PAINT DNA-exonuclease assertion is a valid ancestor-node inference from experimentally characterized DCLRE1A; it is not a direct Artemis experiment PMID:22692201.

Artemis has distinct binding interfaces for LIG4 and PRKDC. Human cellular co-immunoprecipitation is complemented by direct LIG4-fragment binding, calorimetry and structures with the Artemis 485–495 peptide. These support enzyme-binding and protein-serine/threonine-kinase-binding refinements, without transferring the partners’ catalytic functions to Artemis. The peptide structures do not represent intact Artemis, and positive AP-MS association is not automatically a purified binary interaction [PMID:22529269; PMID:23219551; PMID:23523427; PMID:33961781]. The original nuclear DNA-PKcs purification also explicitly detects Artemis, despite the paper’s focus on XLS PMID:25941166.

The major functional setting is nucleoplasm and the NHEJ repair assembly. Tagged human Artemis was observed in nuclei outside nucleoli, and the HPA data record independently identifies supported nucleoplasmic localization. The data record, not its microscopy images, was inspected; its additional Golgi entry is preserved as context. The broad nucleus assertions remain true but less informative than the accepted nucleoplasm location [PMID:12055248; PMID:15071507; GO_REF:0000052].

Four official products are retained: 692, 572, 577 and 434 residues. Products 2 and 3 alter the N-terminal catalytic region; product 4 changes the tail after residue 385 and loses the remaining canonical C terminus. These sequence differences do not prove inactivity or equivalent activity. The 1–385 engineered construct that restores chromosomal recombination is not the natural 434-residue product, and its radiation-growth rescue differed from full length under conditions with lower protein abundance PMID:14744996. The cDNA papers support sequence provenance rather than interchangeable repair functions [PMID:14702039; PMID:15489334]. No product assignment is inserted into an imported source row that lacks it.

Telomere maintenance is a contextual contribution. Mouse Artemis evidence is present among the actual PAINT descendants, and human Artemis-defective fibroblasts display mild telomeric fusions and population-doubling-dependent TIF changes. Younger cells did not have the same TIF phenotype; no direct telomere-specific Artemis incision reaction was established [PMID:12615897; PMID:20678254].

Interstrand-cross-link repair remains unresolved for the human target despite a legitimate ancestral PAINT placement. Chicken DT40 Artemis loss did not increase the tested cisplatin/MMC sensitivity, while a single Artemis-null human HCT116 clone did. The human background is hMLH1-defective, and drug survival does not identify whether Artemis directly removes a cross-link or processes downstream DNA damage. Neither experiment establishes universal absence or a specific human cross-link incision mechanism [PMID:15572677; PMID:21785230]. The experimental contrast is retained explicitly rather than turning a valid inherited annotation into a wrong-paralog assertion.
