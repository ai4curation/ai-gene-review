Gene Description

C7orf25 encodes a 421-amino-acid nuclear protein of the otherwise uncharacterised UPF0415 family, also known as PANAD (proliferation-associated NXF1 adaptor). It acts as an adaptor for nuclear RNA export factor 1 (NXF1), the principal carrier that moves mature mRNA through the nuclear pore. NXF1 is autoinhibited: its arginine-rich RNA-binding domain folds back onto its NTF2-like domain, so that NXF1 cannot hold mRNA stably until an adaptor breaks that intramolecular contact. C7orf25 binds the RNA-binding domain of NXF1 through a small set of residues on each side, opens the autoinhibited conformation, and at the same time binds cargo transcripts directly, so that mRNA is handed to the activated carrier. Its effect on export is selective rather than global: the transcripts whose nuclear export depends on it are enriched for cell-proliferation functions, and CDK6 mRNA is a directly bound target. Through this route the protein raises cytoplasmic levels of proliferation-stimulating proteins, accelerates the G1-to-S transition, and promotes growth of hepatocellular carcinoma cells in culture and as xenografts; it is elevated in hepatocellular carcinoma tissue and its level tracks tumour-cell sensitivity to CDK4/6 inhibition. Two adaptor classes were known before this - the TREX-associated ALYREF and UIF, and the SR proteins - and C7orf25 belongs to neither. The assignment rests on a single study.

Core Functions

C7orf25/PANAD is an adaptor for the mRNA export receptor NXF1. It binds the arginine-rich RNA-binding domain of NXF1 through a small interface (its R122, E125, G133 and D139 contacting NXF1 Q20, K22 and K23), which displaces the autoinhibitory contact between that domain and the NTF2-like domain and licenses NXF1 to hold mRNA stably. At the same time it binds cargo transcripts directly, so it delivers mRNA to the carrier it has just activated. The consequence is selective rather than bulk export: the transcripts whose nuclear export depends on this protein are enriched for cell-proliferation functions, and CDK6 mRNA is a directly bound example. It belongs to neither of the two previously known adaptor classes (TREX-associated ALYREF and UIF, and the SR proteins). The assignment rests on one 2026 study.

Suggested Questions for Experts

Q: Is the export selectivity a property of this protein, or of the transcripts? Put another way, does it recognise a shared element in the mRNAs it binds, or does it simply act on whatever NXF1 is loading in a proliferating cell? No sequence or structural determinant in the cargo has been identified, and the answer decides whether an export-cargo-selectivity concept is needed in GO at all.

Suggested experts: Shi-Mei Zhuang, Jin-E Yang

Q: Does C7orf25 function outside tumour cells? The entire characterisation is in hepatocellular carcinoma models, yet the protein is broadly expressed, so it is unclear whether the mRNA-export role is a normal housekeeping function that tumours exploit or something specific to the transformed state.

Suggested experts: Shi-Mei Zhuang, Qi Zhang

Q: Does the adaptor act alone or as part of a complex? Canonical NXF1 adaptors work with the TREX complex or as SR proteins, and this one belongs to neither class; whether it requires cofactors, and whether it competes with ALYREF for the same NXF1 surface, is unaddressed.

Suggested experts: Shi-Mei Zhuang

Q: The UPF0415 family is otherwise uncharacterised and this gene carries no phylogenetic (IBA) annotation. Is the adaptor function conserved in the family, and does a non-vertebrate ortholog exist that could be tested genetically?

Suggested Experiments

Experiment: Determine a structure of the NXF1 N-terminal region bound to C7orf25, by cryo-electron microscopy or crystallography, and compare it with the autoinhibited NXF1 conformation. The paper's own conclusion is that structural work is still needed; the mutations identified (PANAD R122/E125/G133/D139 and NXF1 Q20/K22/K23) give a defined interface to target and validate.

Hypothesis: C7orf25 activates NXF1 by physically opening the RBD-NTF2L autoinhibitory contact.

Type: structural biology

Experiment: Map the transcriptome-wide binding sites of endogenous C7orf25 by crosslinking and immunoprecipitation, and compare the bound set with the transcripts whose cytoplasmic-to-nuclear ratio falls on knockdown. If selectivity is intrinsic, the two sets should overlap and should share a motif or structural feature; if it is not, bound transcripts should simply track abundance in proliferating cells.

Hypothesis: The set of transcripts exported by this adaptor is defined by direct binding, and shares a recognisable element.

Type: CLIP-seq with nuclear/cytoplasmic fractionation RNA-seq

Experiment: Repeat the knockdown and export measurements in untransformed, non-proliferating primary hepatocytes and in the same cells stimulated to re-enter the cycle, and ask whether the dependent transcript set is the same. Include a conditional knockout mouse to test whether loss has any phenotype in normal tissue.

Hypothesis: C7orf25 is a general NXF1 adaptor whose apparent tumour specificity reflects which transcripts are abundant in proliferating cells, not a tumour-specific mechanism.

Type: comparative knockdown in primary versus transformed cells

C7orf25 / PANAD review notes

Identity

C7orf25 (HGNC:21703, UniProt Q9BPX7) is a 421-amino-acid protein of the UPF0415 family. It
is a genuine dark-genome entry: the symbol is still an open-reading-frame placeholder,
UniProt's recommended name is "UPF0415 protein C7orf25", Pharos classes it Tdark, and until
2026 GOA held exactly one molecular function annotation for it - bare GO:0005515 protein binding from three high-throughput binary interactome screens - plus a Human Protein Atlas
nucleoplasm localisation.

HGNC has since registered the alias PANAD ("proliferation-associated NXF1 adaptor"),
citing PMID:42449115. UniProt has not yet adopted it (entry version 152, 10-JUN-2026, still
Name=C7orf25 with no function block). Two isoforms are recorded, differing only in an
N-terminal extension in isoform 2 (VSP_047243); the residue numbering used in the functional
paper (R122/E125/G133/D139) is that of isoform 1, so the work is on the canonical form.

What the 2026 paper shows

PMID:42449115

The mechanism is specific and is the reason bare protein binding is inadequate here. NXF1 is
autoinhibited: its arginine-rich RNA-binding domain (RBD) folds back onto its NTF2-like
domain, and an adaptor must break that contact before NXF1 can hold mRNA stably. PANAD does
exactly this, and the interface is mapped to single residues on both sides:

activator: PMID:42449115 The binding was mapped with purified GST-PANAD
to a defined region of the CDK6 3' UTR, and the authors state the dual mechanism explicitly:

The export consequence is selective rather than global:

Why bare protein binding is not adequate, and what replaces it

The three existing GO:0005515 rows come from proteome-scale binary interaction screens and
carry no functional content on their own. One of them is worth a second look, though: the
partner in PMID:37219487 is RANBP2/Nup358 (UniProtKB:P49792), the cytoplasmic-filament
nucleoporin at which NXF1-bound mRNP is remodelled on arrival. That is not evidence of the
adaptor function by itself - it is a motif-based screen hit - but it is at least consistent
with a protein that works in the NXF1 export pathway, and it was sitting unexploited in GOA.

The claimed molecular function is "NXF1 adaptor conferring mRNA-export selectivity". The GO
term whose definition matches is GO:0030674 protein-macromolecule adaptor activity: "An
adaptor activity that brings together two or more macromolecules in contact... The adaptor
can bring together two proteins, or a protein and another macromolecule such as a lipid or a
nucleic acid." PANAD brings together a protein (NXF1) and a nucleic acid (the cargo mRNA),
which is the nucleic-acid clause of that definition almost verbatim. GO:0003729 mRNA
binding is independently supported by the GST-PANAD RNA pulldown. GO:0006406 mRNA export
from nucleus is the process.

The selectivity claim - that PANAD biases export towards proliferation-stimulating
transcripts rather than acting globally - is the part GO cannot express in a single term.
core_functions can carry it in two ways used here: substrates records the cargo class
(CHEBI:33699 messenger RNA), and the free-text description states which transcripts are
affected. There is no "export-cargo-selectivity" molecular function term and I am not
proposing one: the selectivity is a property of which mRNAs PANAD binds, and the right way
to capture it in GO would be an annotation extension naming the cargo, not a new MF term.

Curation position taken

  • All three GO:0005515 protein binding rows -> MARK_AS_OVER_ANNOTATED.
  • GO:0005654 nucleoplasm (HPA, IDA) -> ACCEPT. Independently corroborated by the
    BiFC signal inside the lamin B1 boundary in PMID:42449115.
  • Proposed as reviewer additions, all from one paper and flagged as such: GO:0030674
    protein-macromolecule adaptor activity (core MF), GO:0003729 mRNA binding,
    GO:0006406 mRNA export from nucleus (core BP), and GO:1902808 positive regulation of
    cell cycle G1/S phase transition (downstream, non-core).

What is not established

  • This is a single paper from a single laboratory, and the function has not been reproduced
    elsewhere. Nothing in the older literature on C7orf25 anticipates it.
  • The structural basis is inferred from mutagenesis and FRET, not from a structure. The
    authors say so themselves.
  • Whether PANAD has a role outside proliferating and tumour cells is untested; the entire
    study is in hepatocellular carcinoma models.
  • The UPF0415 family is otherwise uncharacterised, so there is no orthology-based support
    for or against the adaptor assignment. No PAINT/IBA annotation exists for this gene.

suggested_questions:
- question: >-
    Is the export selectivity a property of this protein, or of the transcripts? Put another
    way, does it recognise a shared element in the mRNAs it binds, or does it simply act on
    whatever NXF1 is loading in a proliferating cell? No sequence or structural determinant
    in the cargo has been identified, and the answer decides whether an
    export-cargo-selectivity concept is needed in GO at all.

    whether the mRNA-export role is a normal housekeeping function that tumours exploit or
    something specific to the transformed state.
