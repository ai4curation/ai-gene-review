CC   -!- FUNCTION: Flippase that catalyzes in an ATP-dependent manner the
CC       transport of retinal-phosphatidylethanolamine conjugates like 11-cis
CC       and all-trans isomers of N-retinylidene-phosphatidylethanolamine (N-
CC       Ret-PE) from the lumen to the cytoplasmic leaflet of photoreceptor
CC       outer segment disk membranes, where 11-cis-retinylidene-
CC       phosphatidylethanolamine is then isomerized to its all-trans isomer and
CC       reduced by RDH8 to produce all-trans-retinol. This transport activity
CC       ensures that all-trans-retinal generated from photoexcitation and 11-
CC       cis-retinal not needed for the regeneration of rhodopsin and cone
CC       opsins are effectively cleared from the photoreceptors, therefore
CC       preventing their accumulation and the formation of toxic bisretinoid
CC       (PubMed:10075733, PubMed:20404325, PubMed:22735453, PubMed:23144455,
CC       PubMed:24097981, PubMed:29847635, PubMed:33375396). Displays ATPase
CC       activity in vitro in absence of retinal substrate (PubMed:33605212,
CC       PubMed:39128720, PubMed:29847635, PubMed:33375396). May display GTPase
CC       activity that is strongly influenced by the lipid environment and the
CC       presence of retinoid compounds (PubMed:22735453). Binds the
CC       unprotonated form of N-retinylidene-phosphatidylethanolamine with high
CC       affinity in the absence of ATP, and ATP binding and hydrolysis induce a
CC       protein conformational change that causes N-retinylidene-
CC       phosphatidylethanolamine release (By similarity).
