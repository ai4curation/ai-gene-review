Gene Description

CNNM4 is a plasma-membrane magnesium transporter with an extracellular domain, a transmembrane transport region, tandem CBS domains and a cyclic nucleotide-binding homology domain. It couples magnesium export to sodium entry and also associates with TRPM7 to promote divalent-cation channel activity. MgATP binding organizes its cytoplasmic domains, and full-length human CNNM4 forms an asymmetric dimer-of-dimers assembly. Basolateral epithelial localization supports directional magnesium movement and systemic magnesium balance. Biallelic pathogenic variants cause Jalili syndrome, combining cone-rod dystrophy with defective enamel formation.

Core Functions

CNNM4 couples magnesium export to sodium entry at the plasma membrane, supporting magnesium balance and directional transport in polarized epithelia. MgATP binding to its CBS region helps organize the full-length dimer-of-dimers assembly. Cellular exchange measurements and human structures support antiporter activity; exact cycle stoichiometry and the inward-facing human conformation remain unresolved. This function is established for full-length CNNM4, not the short alternative product.

CNNM4 evidence review

CNNM4 has two separable molecular roles: sodium-coupled magnesium export and regulation of TRPM7-mediated divalent-cation entry. The transporter role is supported by explicitly human CNNM4 constructs, including the 2013 study whose title emphasizes its mouse model. The later full-length human structures add direct architecture and mutational evidence. Clinical variants cause Jalili syndrome, but clinical necessity does not by itself identify an enamel-development or visual-process step performed by CNNM4.

Original binding and homeostasis assertions

PMID:15840172 contains a human fetal-brain library screen. Its first positive clone contained two plasmids, but COX11 and RBM30 were retested separately with the ACDP4 bait and negative controls. RBM30 maps to RBM4B/Q9BQ04, not LIN7B. These positive interactions remain non-core. COX11 is a copper metallochaperone/assembly factor; protein-folding chaperone binding would be an inappropriate class refinement. The selected IntAct and MINT target queries contain no exact records for this old publication; absence from those current services is not contrary evidence against the inspected original experiments.

The same paper measures sensitivity to metal-induced cell death, with copper, manganese and cobalt effects and stronger toxicity on COX11 coexpression. These are indirect intracellular-homeostasis observations. Its authors explicitly leave direct metal accumulation and the proposed handoff mechanism unresolved. We retain the broad homeostasis and complex assertions as contextual, supported independently by later magnesium measurements and structural assembly, without declaring native COX11 stoichiometry or copper transport. Rat neuron staining is not a native human localization assay.

Direct transport, ATP and channel regulation

PMID:24339795 Methods identifies human CNNM4 IMAGE30340626. Human CNNM4-FLAG in HEK293 cells lowers magnesium while increasing sodium; sodium withdrawal/restoration controls the efflux response. Ratiometric sodium and magnesium measurements give an approximately 2.14:1 change and a sodium-dependence Hill coefficient near 1.90. These cellular results support magnesium:sodium antiporter activity without fixing the molecular stoichiometry. Mouse knockout experiments independently demonstrate magnesium malabsorption and altered serum/urine/fecal magnesium. Human CNNM4 in MDCK cells and native mouse intestine/ameloblasts support a basolateral epithelial context. The broad inherited magnesium-transport process is retained alongside the already-present route-specific process.

PMID:42497866 studies full-length human Q6P4Q7, including purified protein in GDN for cryo-EM and mCherry-tagged variants in HEK293T efflux assays with surface controls. It resolves a MgATP-associated tetramer composed of asymmetric dimers and a mutant occluded conformation. The inward-facing comparison is bacterial CorC, and ion densities near E284 are tentatively attributed to sodium. These limits remain explicit. An entire reconstituted transport cycle, native tissue stoichiometry and physiological sensing thresholds were not measured.

PMID:24706765 directly tests human CNNM4 CBS residues 359–511 using radiolabeled ATP and a GST background control. Magnesium-dependent nucleotide recognition is also visible in full-length human CNNM4 in PMID:42497866. This warrants a new ATP-binding assertion and belongs within the transport-core explanation; ATP hydrolysis is not inferred. The more extensive mouse CNNM2 SPR and AMP-competition experiments remain distinct.

PMID:34928937 provides both physical association and functional human CNNM4 evidence. Modest sustained expression of WT CNNM4 restores TRPM7 currents after CNNM3/4 loss, avoiding the nonspecific effects of high overexpression on protein abundance. Channel surface levels, SLC41A1 comparison, isotope uptake, inhibitor and knockout experiments distinguish regulation from simply lowering magnesium. The explicit Fig. 3E caption describes normal-to-sodium-free buffer exchange; the adjoining prose reverses that order. The mouse CNNM2 pore-mutant experiment is not transferred to CNNM4. PMID:40962059 corroborates the separation with human CNNM4 S196P and CBS-deletion variants, while its detailed kinase/domain experiments largely use mouse CNNM2. We add ion channel regulator activity, not the TRPM7 pore or kinase function.

PMID:37449820 directly maps ARL15 binding to human CNNM4 fragments 356–511/356–726 and tests interface mutants. Its full-length transport experiments use mouse CNNM2. PMID:25347473 supplies human CNNM4/PRL interaction and magnesium-efflux inhibition in selected external JCI Methods/Results. The selected normal cache stays abstract-only; no full-text cache was fabricated. The tumor context is not converted into a new direct process annotation.
