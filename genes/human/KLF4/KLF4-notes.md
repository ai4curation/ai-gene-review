# KLF4 manual notes

Automated deep research failed for Falcon and the configured Perplexity-lite fallback, so this review uses the seeded GOA references and targeted manual source checks.

KLF4 is a C2H2 zinc-finger KLF transcription factor with three C-terminal zinc fingers. The early human EZF paper showed that recombinant hEZF/KLF4 bound a CACCC-containing DNA probe and that hEZF carried both a repression domain and an activation region (PMID:9422764). This is the core evidence for treating the DNA-binding and Pol II transcription-regulator rows as the conserved molecular role.

KLF4 is strongly pleiotropic. The cached literature supports endothelial anti-inflammatory and anti-angiogenic programs (PMID:20551324; PMID:22267480; PMID:23867820), telomerase maintenance in cancer and stem-cell contexts (PMID:20629177), epidermal barrier formation in mouse (PMID:10431239), and HBG promoter activation in erythroid cells (PMID:21539536). These are kept as context-specific outputs of a DNA-binding transcription factor rather than elevated above the DNA-binding activity.

The high-throughput interactome rows from PMID:32296183, PMID:32814053, and PMID:24048479 were stripped only when they were bare GO:0005515 protein-binding rows. Specific TF/cofactor binding rows were kept where a small-scale paper or the row term itself captured a transcriptional-regulatory complex.
