Gene Description

ATP1A3 encodes the catalytic alpha3 subunit of the sodium-potassium pump, a multipass plasma-membrane P-type ATPase that couples ATP hydrolysis to sodium export and potassium import. Alpha3 assembles with a beta subunit and can associate with an FXYD regulatory protein. Its activity sustains ion gradients and membrane potential in neurons and other expressing cells. Disease-associated variants can impair pump activity or stability, producing neurological disorders. Cardiac glycosides inhibit the pump, and pathological amyloid assemblies can bind alpha3 and disrupt neuronal ion homeostasis.

Sodium-potassium transport

Alpha3 is the catalytic component of one plasma-membrane sodium-potassium pump core. Its own ATP-dependent cycle exports sodium and imports potassium; the two direct transport processes are already represented in the machine annotations. No NEW term or redundant electrochemical-gradient ancestor is added to the core. Beta subunits support alpha3 assembly and stability, and FXYD proteins can modulate the complex. Binding these partners does not make alpha3 an autonomous chaperone.

Human alpha3/beta1/FXYD6 expressed in HEK293F cells produces pump current, reaches the cell surface and supports ATP turnover in purified preparations PMID:36075933(https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9458724/).
