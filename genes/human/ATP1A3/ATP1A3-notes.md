# ATP1A3 notes

## Sodium-potassium transport

Alpha3 is the catalytic component of one plasma-membrane sodium-potassium pump core. Its own ATP-dependent cycle exports sodium and imports potassium; the two direct transport processes are already represented in the machine annotations. No NEW term or redundant electrochemical-gradient ancestor is added to the core. Beta subunits support alpha3 assembly and stability, and FXYD proteins can modulate the complex. Binding these partners does not make alpha3 an autonomous chaperone.

Human alpha3/beta1/FXYD6 expressed in HEK293F cells produces pump current, reaches the cell surface and supports ATP turnover in purified preparations [PMID:36075933](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9458724/). The nominal E1•ATP structural state uses AMPPCP according to Methods, so it is described here as ATP-like rather than literal ATP-bound. Separate phosphorylation and ATP-hydrolysis assays use ATP. Results and Methods disagree over E324 versus E327A as the first member of a four-residue mutant; that discrepancy is preserved without guessed renumbering. The selected reading establishes human catalytic function but is not a complete figure-pixel or supplemental-data audit.

Human alpha3 coassembles with human beta1, beta2 and beta3 in Xenopus oocytes, with stabilization demonstrated by pulse-chase and immunoprecipitation [PMID:10636900](https://doi.org/10.1074/jbc.275.3.1976). Donor species and assay host are separate facts: these are human proteins in a frog host. The three UniProt alternative products remain unchanged, and the experiments are not assigned invented isoform identifiers or presumed to assay all products.

## Ouabain binding and functional response

[PMID:11546672](https://doi.org/10.1152/ajpcell.2001.281.4.C1336) provides ligand-binding evidence. Official [ChEBI:472805](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/chebi/searchId.do?chebiId=CHEBI:472805) classifies ouabain as a steroid hormone and cardenolide glycoside. Classification plus binding alone does not establish a response process. Independently inspected original PMID:10636900 Methods and Fig. 6 measure intact-oocyte currents before and after 100 micromolar ouabain for human alpha3/beta1, subtracting uninjected-oocyte currents. This functional endpoint corroborates the two broad response terms as non-core pharmacological context. The original PMID:11546672 NAS provenance remains unchanged, with PMID:10636900 recorded as additional evidence. No endocrine transcriptional pathway or native-human-cell assay is inferred.

## Localization and pathological context

Human cardiomyocyte staining places alpha3 at plasma and intracellular membranes, but not at the T-tubules reported for alpha1 and alpha2 [PMID:19751721](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4948948/). PMID:26373354 uses Xenopus alpha3 constructs in transgenic rods; the human construct in its inspected Methods is alpha4. Its inner-segment result remains valid orthology evidence, not a new human alpha3 IDA experiment. The existing ER/Golgi assertions from PMID:15260953 remain undecided because the full localization panels and wild-type/mutant distinction were not available in the bounded reading.

Original [PMID:26224839](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4538662/) Results report direct human alpha3 binding to pathological amyloid assemblies by SPR (Kd 28.6 nM), alongside rat/mouse recombinant measurements, human cortical staining and rat-neuron perturbation experiments. Selected supplementary text identifies cell-free wheat-germ/asolectin production. Rat neuronal toxicity or neurite experiments are not relabeled as human assays. These contextual binding/response/maintenance assertions remain outside the transport core. The normal cached record is abstract-only despite separate access to indexed original sections.
