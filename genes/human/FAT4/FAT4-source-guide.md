# FAT4 evidence guide

The characterized full-length FAT4 protein is an atypical cadherin that mediates heterophilic contacts with DCHS1. Human purified N-terminal fragments establish a calcium-containing cadherin interface and direct binding (PMID:36797229, Results on binding and crystal structures; Methods on protein expression and ITC). Full-length human fluorescent fusion proteins support heterotypic boundary accumulation, with no homotypic accumulation in the tested cultures (PMID:28826487, Results on development of the experimental system; cloning and cell-culture Methods). These are direct human protein experiments; they do not establish that DCHS1 is FAT4's only partner.

PMID:19506035 requires a species distinction. The full-length transfected aggregation constructs are rat Fat4/Dachsous1; endogenous tissue localization, in-utero knockdown, co-immunoprecipitation and electron microscopy are mouse embryonic cortex. The original Methods explicitly identify both. The experiments support conserved adhesion and apical organization without relabeling the transfected protein as mouse or human.

In that cortex, Fat4 is apical to beta-catenin-defined adherens junctions and maintains a separate subapical membrane apposition. PMID:25355906 independently distinguishes these structures by electron tomography in mouse cortex and characterizes heterotypic contacts in transfected MDCK cells. The latter main text does not independently resolve the construct species. These observations support an apical-compartment refinement of the broad classical adherens-junction inference; they do not deny all FAT4 junctional interactions in other tissues.

The original PMID:24056717 main body remains inaccessible. Its genuine publisher supplement was inspected directly: Figure S4 supplies knockdown controls and cortical distributions/rescue; S7 shows reduced phospho-Yap after Fat4 knockdown in P19 cells, with total Yap and actin controls; S8 shows cortical proliferation/differentiation rescue after concurrent Yap knockdown. The schematic in S9 is an interpretation, not an independently measured MST/LATS chain. These positive experiments support the bounded neuronal-generation and Hippo/YAP contexts. Neuronal generation is not interchangeable with axon extension.

The YAP mechanism is context dependent. Original mouse kidney experiments in PMID:23974041 associate Fat4 with Yap phosphorylation/localization and epithelial differentiation. The extracellular Fat4 co-culture effect contributes a signal to neighboring progenitors; their exact receptor and all kinase intermediates were unresolved. Supplementary Methods downloads failed, so co-culture construct species was not independently resolved. PMID:28239148 separates mouse hearts, rat primary cardiomyocytes and transfected HEK293 experiments; its AMOTL1-dependent route changes YAP localization without the measured change in Hippo-kinase/YAP phosphorylation. PMID:23563179 provides mouse liver and fly motif/rescue constraints. Neither study warrants a universal claim that mammalian FAT4 lacks Hippo/YAP participation or uses the fly Fat–Dachs mechanism.

The actual PAINT ancestry is PANTHER v19 PTHR24026, target leaf PTN002513196; its ungapped 4,981-residue sequence matches canonical Q6V0I7. All five source IBD nodes lie on that path. Current UniProt/InterPro's PTHR24027 assignment is preserved separately. Two later IBD donor lists add Dachsous relative to frozen GOA; neither source is rewritten. The axonogenesis donor is fly shotgun/DE-cadherin Q24298, with a genuine IMP source PMID:12716940. Only discontinuous original passages and the abstract were accessible. The axon annotation remains an ancestral, non-core inference, not a measured human FAT4 axon function; developmental emphasis elsewhere is not evidence of evolutionary loss.

Actual InterPro matches and all four ARBA records were checked. A real cadherin-domain match does not establish homophilic adhesion. Complete target eligibility through every ARBA FunFam condition was not reproduced, and isolated matching domains in taxon-specific clauses are not treated as complete matching rules. Primary evidence independently supports the retained compartment/developmental contexts. Broad membrane and adhesion/development parent terms are marked TOO_GENERAL where retained descendants already supply the supported scope.

The three protected natural products are retained. Isoform 2 deletes the canonical N-terminal region, including the tested EC1–4 binding region and predicted signal peptide. Neither the full-length construct experiments nor artificial tag/ICD deletions establish its localization or function. No natural cleavage product is inferred from purification constructs. No supporting-text quotations were added.

PMID:39955614 adds a later human-construct context: engineered FAT4 intracellular-domain deletion reduces DCHS1 trans-endocytosis and boundary accumulation, without a detectable change in the measured minute-scale FRAP stability. ROOT read its full original Methods, Results, Discussion, captions and Figures 1–3 pixels. A Figure 1 caption sentence conflicts with the actual Figure 1I groups and Results; the reduced trans-endocytosis interpretation follows the plotted experiment and Results. No formal erratum is asserted. Figure 2 supports the measured eight-minute FRAP scope, and Figure 3B directly measures boundary persistence, with altered internalization an interpretation. Videos and supplemental sequences remain unread. The artificial deletion retains nine cytoplasmic residues and is not a natural splice product. Actin perturbation does not demonstrate direct FAT4–actin binding, and tissue-level PCP plasticity remains an implication of the cell system. This source adds context without a new annotation or enzyme/scaffold assertion.

## Direct interaction and unresolved proteomics evidence

PMID:22153077 supports direct recognition of two human FAT4 cytoplasmic peptides by the substrate-recognition ARC4 domain of tankyrase-2 (TNKS2; UniProtKB:Q9H2K2). The publisher-hosted complete article plus supplement was read, including the original Figure 6 and S7A pixels, Extended Methods and Tables S5/S6. Fluorescence-polarization assays used three experiments in technical duplicate. Table S6 peptide sequences match the pinned human FAT4 canonical sequence exactly. The original figures give peptide 1 a Kd of 36.7 ± 11.0 μM and peptide 2 a Kd of 31.0 ± 5.6 μM; these are fit standard errors. Current IntAct records reverse the approximate affinity-to-region association, so numerical attribution follows the primary figures.

The cellular co-immunoprecipitation and anti-PAR experiments in Figure 6 used an overexpressed mouse Fat4 fragment (4367–4981)-FLAG with human TNKS2 in HEK293T cells. Motif mutants and catalytically inactive TNKS2 distinguish recognition from modification; dominant-negative PARG was used to limit PAR hydrolysis. These experiments do not establish endogenous full-length human FAT4 PARylation. The paper reports that endogenous FAT4 could not be detected in these cells. Binding to an enzyme does not make FAT4 that enzyme or establish an adaptor activity. The imported generic binding observation is retained as non-core under the explicit project exception.

An erratum is bibliographically established at Cell 2012;148:376 (DOI:10.1016/j.cell.2012.01.009). Its full content was not recovered. Its scope remains unresolved; no retraction or invalidation of the interaction is inferred.

PMID:23533145 was read in its original main-article form. It describes proteomics of exosome preparations from pooled expressed prostatic secretions in urine, differential ultracentrifugation with a DTT/sucrose wash, duplicate MS runs and a peptide/protein FDR threshold below 1%. The authors discuss mixed urine/prostate contributions and potential contaminants. However, the FAT4-specific entry in Supplemental Table 2 and supplementary Methods remain inaccessible after bounded attempts. Nominal spreadsheet/document links returned HTML gates or access errors; no target accession, peptides, intensity, sample-pool assignment or isoform was inferred. The HDA localization row is UNDECIDED pending the original target-bearing evidence. This is an access limit, not a finding that the source or curator was wrong.


## Integrated molecular activity and remaining limits

Human FAT4 itself binds DCHS1 across a cell boundary. The human purified-fragment ITC, L379R interface perturbation and full-length human cell assays support GO:0098641 cadherin binding involved in cell-cell adhesion. This single added molecular-function annotation is distinct from the imported TNKS2 interaction and the retained adhesion process annotations. GO:0086080 requires different cell types and is not inferred from different cadherin partners. No new biological-process annotation is proposed. DCHS1 is an experimentally established partner, without a claim that it is the only partner.

Integration reading of the actual original Figure 1I pixels independently confirms lower DCHS1 trans-endocytosis in both FAT4 intracellular-deletion groups, despite an oppositely worded sentence in the Figure 1 caption. This is an internal caption inconsistency, not a verified formal erratum. ROOT also inspected Figures 2 and 3: no significant recovery difference over the measured minutes and slower boundary loss after calcium withdrawal with the deletion. Videos and supplemental construct sequences were not independently read. The engineered deletion is not a natural splice-product assay.

The concise core is this adhesion activity at the plasma membrane. Calcium binding supports its extracellular architecture; tissue-dependent Hippo/YAP and developmental effects remain subsidiary. The questions distinguish engineered intracellular deletion, native cellular context and the N-terminally shortened natural product. None assumes a universal downstream kinase route or product equivalence.
