# FAT4 biological notes

FAT4 is a large atypical cadherin whose extracellular region binds DCHS1 to create heterophilic cell contacts. Direct human ITC and interface-mutation/cell-boundary experiments support the adhesion activity (PMID:36797229); the original full-length human cloning and co-culture experiments are in PMID:28826487. The concise core is cadherin binding involved in cell-cell adhesion at the plasma membrane. The retained calcium-binding annotation describes the extracellular architecture, not a second independent signaling mechanism.

The intracellular region contributes to boundary-complex accumulation and internalization in engineered human-cell assays (PMID:39955614). This cellular result is not a new tissue-level planar-polarity annotation. YAP phosphorylation/localization and developmental effects differ across cortex, kidney, heart and liver contexts; the evidence guide distinguishes these rather than imposing a universal fly Fat–Dachs or mammalian Hippo-kinase chain.

## Natural products and construct boundaries

The three inherited product objects remain exact. Official sequence reconstruction gives Q6V0I7-1 4,981 residues, Q6V0I7-2 3,222 residues, and Q6V0I7-3 4,980 residues. Product 2 removes canonical residues 1–1702 and therefore lacks both the canonical predicted signal peptide and the N-terminal EC1–4 region used in the direct binding experiments. Its additional sequence edits do not rescue a demonstrated localization or binding function. Product 3 removes canonical residue 4360; no functional equivalence is inferred from this small sequence difference. The canonical signal peptide at 1–38 is computationally annotated, not a measured universal cleavage boundary.

The human N-terminal constructs used for structure/ITC carry heterologous leaders and purification tags; full-length fluorescent constructs and intracellular deletion constructs are experimental reagents. Neither is silently assigned to a natural isoform. The 2025 intracellular deletion retains nine cytoplasmic residues and differs from all three inherited natural products.

## Source decisions and open questions

Human FAT4 peptides are recognized by TNKS2; the separate modification assay used a mouse Fat4 fragment. This interaction is retained as non-core and does not establish FAT4 PARP or adaptor activity. The exact exosome proteomics target entry remains inaccessible and UNDECIDED. The verified tankyrase-paper erratum has unresolved content; no scope is guessed.

The source guide and two YAML tables connect every decision to its source and assay limits. The specific questions concern intracellular partners in a native cell context and the localization/adhesion of the N-terminally shortened product, without declaring a comprehensive literature gap. No new syndrome or clinical-mechanism assertion is introduced.
