| Disease / clinical setting | MYD88 alteration and reported frequency | Biological or diagnostic significance | Prognostic / therapeutic implications |
|---|---|---|---|
| Waldenström macroglobulinemia (WM) / lymphoplasmacytic lymphoma | **L265P in >90%** of patients; approximately **91%** in one review, leaving **5–10%** MYD88-wild-type (WT) (pqac-00000055, pqac-00000056) | Hallmark driver and useful diagnostic biomarker, although not disease-specific. Constitutive MYD88 signaling promotes IRAK/BTK, NF-κB, BCL2, and tumor-cell survival pathways (pqac-00000052, pqac-00000053) | Genotype informs treatment selection, particularly expected response to BTK-pathway inhibition. WT disease has been associated with reduced response to some BTK inhibitors, greater transformation risk, and shorter overall survival (pqac-00000055) |
| WM: mutant versus WT molecular phenotype | A 2024 single-cell study analyzed **five L265P**, **two WT**, and **two healthy-donor** samples; L265P occurs in >90% of WM overall (pqac-00000055) | L265P and WT tumors exhibit distinct transcriptional programs involving NF-κB, BCL2, and BTK, with substantial intratumoral and interpatient heterogeneity | Molecular heterogeneity may explain variable prognosis and treatment response even among L265P-positive patients; genotype alone does not capture all relevant tumor biology |
| IgM monoclonal gammopathy of undetermined significance (IgM-MGUS) | L265P reported in approximately **50–80%** of cases; one 2024 cohort found **60%** (pqac-00000056) | May arise early in clonal evolution and helps distinguish IgM-MGUS/WM biology from IgM myeloma. It is associated with increased progression risk, although most carriers do not progress (pqac-00000056) | Potential risk-stratification biomarker, but insufficient alone to predict progression; additional genomic and clinical variables are required |
| IgM multiple myeloma and non-IgM MGUS | L265P is generally **absent** from IgM myeloma and IgG/IgA MGUS (pqac-00000056) | Absence helps distinguish plasma-cell neoplasia from WM/LPL when clinicopathologic findings overlap; IgM myeloma is more often associated with `t(11;14)` | Useful primarily for differential diagnosis rather than prognosis |
| Activated B-cell-like diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (ABC-DLBCL) | Recurrent activating MYD88 mutations, especially L265P; uncommon in germinal-center B-cell-like DLBCL, but no single frequency applies across cohorts (pqac-00000053) | Constitutive MYD88–IRAK–NF-κB signaling supports lymphoma-cell growth and survival; some tumors are functionally dependent on mutant MYD88 (pqac-00000059) | Establishes a targetable dependency involving the myddosome, IRAK kinases, BTK, and NF-κB. Prognostic effects vary by anatomic site, molecular subtype, and treatment context |
| Primary CNS, primary testicular, and other immune-privileged-site DLBCL | MYD88 mutations, including L265P, are enriched, although frequencies were not quantified in the reviewed evidence (pqac-00000053, pqac-00000059) | Supports a shared oncogenic program centered on chronic MYD88 signaling and helps classify these extranodal lymphomas | May provide diagnostic support and identify patients for pathway-directed therapy; clinical value remains context-dependent |
| Primary cutaneous DLBCL, leg type | L265P is described as frequent and clinically relevant, without a numerical frequency in the reviewed evidence (pqac-00000052, pqac-00000058) | Supports classification as a MYD88-driven lymphoma and may help distinguish it from other cutaneous B-cell neoplasms | Reported to carry prognostic value, but its direction and magnitude require interpretation within disease-specific cohorts |
| Marginal-zone lymphoma (MZL), particularly IgM-secreting cases | L265P occurs in a **minority** of cases; prevalence varies by MZL subtype (pqac-00000056) | Demonstrates that L265P is not specific to WM; mutation results must be integrated with morphology, immunophenotype, paraprotein, and clinical distribution | Limited standalone prognostic value; used principally in differential diagnosis with other disease features |
| Other mature B-cell neoplasms | Lower-frequency MYD88 mutations occur in chronic lymphocytic leukemia and splenic MZL; they are rare in hairy-cell leukemia, Burkitt lymphoma, and follicular lymphoma and appear absent from some entities such as primary mediastinal B-cell lymphoma (pqac-00000055, pqac-00000056) | MYD88 activation is concentrated in particular B-cell differentiation states and lymphoma subtypes rather than being a universal cancer event | Clinical significance is disease-specific; low prevalence limits use as a universal prognostic biomarker |
| Non-L265P MYD88-mutant IgM gammopathies / lymphomas | Reported variants include **M232T, S243N, S222R, T294P, and S219C**; frequencies are low or incompletely defined (pqac-00000053) | Some variants may activate MYD88 signaling, but their functional strength and diagnostic specificity are less established than those of L265P | These variants should not automatically be interpreted as equivalent to L265P; functional annotation and disease context are necessary |
| MYD88-WT WM / IgM gammopathy | Approximately **5–10% of WM** lacks L265P or another detectable MYD88 mutation (pqac-00000055, pqac-00000056) | WT status does **not** exclude WM but may define a biologically distinct subgroup; exclusion of mimics and confirmation with sensitive testing are important | Associated in several studies with greater transformation risk, resistance to some therapies, reduced BTK-inhibitor responsiveness, and shorter survival relative to mutant disease (pqac-00000055) |
