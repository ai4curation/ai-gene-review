# Comprehensive Research Report: Human MYD88 (Myeloid Differentiation Primary Response Protein 88)

## Gene Identity Verification

The research target has been confirmed as human MYD88 (UniProt: Q99836), encoding the myeloid differentiation primary response protein MyD88. The protein contains the expected domain architecture including a DEATH-like domain superfamily region, Death domain (IPR000488), MyD88 Death domain (IPR034249), and TIR domain (IPR000157), consistent with the UniProt annotation for *Homo sapiens* (song2024myd88andits pages 1-2, pustelny2022mechanismofmyd88s pages 1-2).

## 1. Primary Function and Molecular Mechanism

### 1.1 Core Adaptor Function

Human MYD88 functions as a critical cytoplasmic adaptor protein that links activated innate immune receptors—particularly Toll-like receptors (TLRs) and interleukin-1 receptor family members (IL-1R, IL-18R)—to downstream signaling kinases and transcription factors (song2024myd88andits pages 2-4, song2024myd88andits pages 1-2, rughetti2024imperativeroleof pages 5-6). Unlike enzymes that catalyze biochemical reactions with specific substrates, MYD88 operates as a non-enzymatic scaffold protein that organizes proximity-driven signaling complexes (song2024myd88andits pages 2-4, pustelny2022mechanismofmyd88s pages 1-2).

MYD88 mediates signaling from nearly all TLRs except TLR3, which uses the alternative TRIF adaptor (song2024myd88andits pages 2-4, rughetti2024imperativeroleof pages 3-5, rughetti2024imperativeroleof pages 5-6). Following receptor engagement by pathogen-associated or damage-associated molecular patterns, MYD88 is recruited to the receptor's intracellular TIR domain and nucleates formation of a supramolecular organizing center called the "myddosome" (pustelny2022mechanismofmyd88s pages 1-2, pereira2023regulationofinnate pages 2-4).

**N-terminal Death Domain (DD; residues ~1-109):** The death domain mediates homotypic protein-protein interactions with other MYD88 molecules and heterotypic interactions with the death domains of IL-1 receptor-associated kinases (IRAKs), particularly IRAK4 and IRAK1/2 (pustelny2022mechanismofmyd88s pages 1-2, gurkan2024theirakmdeath pages 1-2). Through these interactions, the DD constructs the core oligomeric scaffold of the myddosome. Structural studies reveal that the myddosome assembles as a layered, left-handed helical complex typically containing approximately 6-8 MyD88 molecules associating with four IRAK4 molecules (pereira2023regulationofinnate pages 2-4, gurkan2024theirakmdeath pages 8-10).

**Intermediate Domain (ID; residues ~110-155):** Initially thought to be merely a passive linker, the 46-amino-acid intermediate domain is largely intrinsically disordered yet plays an essential regulatory role in myddosome nucleation and signal propagation (pustelny2022mechanismofmyd88s pages 1-2, pustelny2022mechanismofmyd88s pages 2-4). Residues Gln114-Leu118, particularly Tyr116, are critical for productive myddosome assembly and IRAK4 activation (pustelny2022mechanismofmyd88s pages 2-4, pustelny2022mechanismofmyd88s pages 12-14, pustelny2022mechanismofmyd88s pages 9-11). The alternatively spliced isoform MyD88S, which lacks the intermediate domain, can be incorporated into nascent signaling complexes but prevents their maturation, thereby acting as a dominant-negative regulator that terminates inflammatory signaling (pustelny2022mechanismofmyd88s pages 1-2, pustelny2022mechanismofmyd88s pages 9-11).

**C-terminal TIR Domain (residues ~156-296):** The Toll/interleukin-1 receptor (TIR) domain adopts a flavodoxin-like fold and engages the TIR domains of activated receptors, either directly or through bridging adaptors such as TIRAP/MAL (saikh2024targetingmyeloiddifferentiation pages 2-4, bhatt2024structuralcharacterizationof pages 2-3, rughetti2024imperativeroleof pages 5-6). The TIR domain's BB loop is functionally critical for TIR-TIR homotypic interactions that drive MyD88 oligomerization (saikh2024targetingmyeloiddifferentiation pages 2-4).

### 1.3 Mechanism of Myddosome Assembly

Recent structural and mechanistic studies have elucidated the stepwise process of myddosome formation:

1. **Receptor Nucleation:** Ligand-induced receptor dimerization or oligomerization triggers conformational changes in receptor cytoplasmic TIR domains, creating high-affinity binding sites for MYD88 TIR domains (pustelny2022mechanismofmyd88s pages 1-2, pereira2023regulationofinnate pages 2-4, song2024myd88andits pages 2-4).

2. **TIR-Domain Assembly:** MYD88 TIR domains assemble into two-stranded, parallel, head-to-tail filamentous structures. A landmark 2024 microcrystal electron diffraction (MicroED) study resolved the TLR2-nucleated MyD88 TIR assembly at 2.85 Å resolution, revealing that assembly is stabilized by asymmetric intrastrand BE interfaces and lateral interstrand BCD interfaces (li2024microcrystalelectrondiffraction pages 8-11, bhatt2024structuralcharacterizationof pages 7-8, li2024microcrystalelectrondiffraction pages 1-2). The EE surface of incoming MyD88 molecules binds the BB surface of already-oligomerized subunits, driving conformational rearrangement of the B helix and BB loop and supporting unidirectional elongation (li2024microcrystalelectrondiffraction pages 8-11).

3. **Progressive Oligomerization:** MyD88 molecules initially form small, unstable oligomers that grow progressively with continued receptor stimulation (pereira2023regulationofinnate pages 2-4, song2024myd88andits pages 2-4). Once the oligomer reaches a critical size threshold, it establishes stable interactions with IRAK4 (song2024myd88andits pages 2-4).

4. **IRAK Recruitment and Activation:** The assembled MyD88 death domains recruit four IRAK4 molecules, followed by IRAK1 and/or IRAK2, creating a three-layered helical signaling complex (gurkan2024theirakmdeath pages 1-2, pereira2023regulationofinnate pages 2-4, gurkan2024theirakmdeath pages 8-10). Proximity within this structure enables IRAK4 molecules to trans-autophosphorylate and subsequently phosphorylate and activate IRAK1/2 (pereira2023regulationofinnate pages 2-4, vegivinti2023roleofinnate pages 1-3).

5. **Downstream Signaling Initiation:** Activated IRAK1 dissociates from the myddosome and engages the E3 ubiquitin ligase TRAF6, initiating the downstream TAK1-IKK-NF-κB and MAPK cascades (vegivinti2023roleofinnate pages 1-3, pereira2023regulationofinnate pages 6-7).

## 2. Subcellular Localization

MYD88 is a cytoplasmic/cytosolic protein that carries out its signaling function at receptor-associated sites (song2024myd88andits pages 1-2, rughetti2024imperativeroleof pages 5-6, pustelny2022mechanismofmyd88s pages 1-2). In its basal state, MYD88 resides in the cytoplasm. Upon receptor activation, it is recruited to the intracellular domains of TLRs and IL-1R family members at cellular membranes, where it assembles into functional myddosome signaling complexes (rughetti2024imperativeroleof pages 5-6, pustelny2022mechanismofmyd88s pages 1-2, pustelny2022mechanismofmyd88s pages 11-12). The myddosome forms at membrane-proximal sites for plasma-membrane receptors (TLR2, TLR4, TLR5, IL-1R) or at endosomal membranes for intracellular TLRs (TLR7, TLR8, TLR9) that detect nucleic acids (rughetti2024imperativeroleof pages 5-6).

MYD88 functions downstream of most TLRs and the IL-1 receptor family:

**Direct MYD88 Recruitment:** TLR5, TLR7, TLR8, TLR9, IL-1R, and IL-18R directly recruit MYD88 through TIR-domain interactions (rughetti2024imperativeroleof pages 5-6, rughetti2024imperativeroleof pages 3-5, song2024myd88andits pages 1-2).

**TIRAP/MAL-Mediated Recruitment:** TLR1/2, TLR2/6, and TLR4 require the bridging adaptor TIRAP (also called MAL) to facilitate MYD88 recruitment (rughetti2024imperativeroleof pages 5-6, rughetti2024imperativeroleof pages 3-5).

**TLR4 Dual Signaling:** TLR4 is unique in engaging both MYD88-dependent and MYD88-independent pathways. At the plasma membrane, TLR4 signals through TIRAP-MYD88 to activate early NF-κB and inflammatory cytokines. After endocytosis, TLR4 switches to a TRAM-TRIF pathway that activates IRF3, type I interferons, and late NF-κB signaling (rughetti2024imperativeroleof pages 5-6, rughetti2024imperativeroleof pages 3-5).

**TLR3 Exception:** TLR3 is the only TLR that does not use MYD88, instead recruiting TRIF directly to activate IRF3-dependent interferon responses (rughetti2024imperativeroleof pages 3-5).

### 3.2 Downstream Transcriptional Programs

MYD88-dependent signaling activates multiple transcription factor families:

**NF-κB Pathway:** IRAK1 activation leads to TRAF6-mediated K63-linked ubiquitination and activation of TAK1. TAK1 phosphorylates the IKK complex, which phosphorylates IκBα, triggering its proteasomal degradation and releasing NF-κB dimers (p50/p65) to translocate to the nucleus and induce inflammatory gene expression (vegivinti2023roleofinnate pages 1-3, song2024myd88andits pages 1-2, rughetti2024imperativeroleof pages 2-3).

**MAPK/AP-1 Pathways:** TAK1 also activates mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK) cascades including ERK, JNK, and p38, leading to AP-1 transcription factor activation and expression of inflammatory mediators (song2024myd88andits pages 1-2, rughetti2024imperativeroleof pages 5-6, rughetti2024imperativeroleof pages 2-3, pereira2023regulationofinnate pages 6-7).

**Interferon Regulatory Factor Signaling:** MYD88 can modulate interferon responses through multiple mechanisms. In plasmacytoid dendritic cells, TLR7 and TLR9 signaling recruits IRF7 to the myddosome, promoting type I interferon production (rughetti2024imperativeroleof pages 5-6, song2024myd88andits pages 1-2). MYD88 also regulates IRF5 activation in certain cell types (rughetti2024imperativeroleof pages 5-6, pereira2023regulationofinnate pages 6-7). Additionally, the MyD88 intermediate domain can mediate IRF-7 activity independently of death-domain folding (song2024myd88andits pages 2-4, song2024myd88andits pages 1-2).

**Functional Outputs:** These transcriptional programs induce production of pro-inflammatory cytokines (TNF-α, IL-6, IL-1β), chemokines, type I and type III interferons, acute-phase proteins, and enzymes such as inducible nitric oxide synthase (iNOS) and cyclooxygenase-2 (COX-2) (song2024myd88andits pages 2-4, rughetti2024imperativeroleof pages 5-6).

## 4. Recent Structural and Mechanistic Advances (2023-2024)

### 4.1 TIR Domain Higher-Order Assembly

A major advance in 2024 came from combined microcrystal electron diffraction (MicroED) and serial femtosecond crystallography studies that resolved MyD88 TIR-domain assemblies at near-atomic resolution (bhatt2024structuralcharacterizationof pages 7-8, bhatt2024structuralcharacterizationof pages 5-7, li2024microcrystalelectrondiffraction pages 1-2). These structures revealed that:

- MyD88 TIR domains assemble into two-stranded parallel filaments with head-to-tail contacts (bhatt2024structuralcharacterizationof pages 7-8, li2024microcrystalelectrondiffraction pages 8-11)
- TLR2 TIR domains can directly nucleate MyD88 assembly at 2.85 Å resolution, whereas TLR1 and TLR6 TIR domains cannot do so independently (li2024microcrystalelectrondiffraction pages 1-2, li2024microcrystalelectrondiffraction pages 8-11, li2024microcrystalelectrondiffraction pages 5-8)
- Assembly involves conformational changes in the BB loop, CD loop, and B helix relative to monomeric structures (li2024microcrystalelectrondiffraction pages 8-11, li2024microcrystalelectrondiffraction pages 5-8)
- Structure-guided mutagenesis confirmed that the BE and BCD interfaces observed crystallographically correspond to functionally relevant signaling interfaces in cells (bhatt2024structuralcharacterizationof pages 7-8)

These findings support a nucleated hierarchical assembly model termed "signaling by cooperative assembly formation" (SCAF), in which receptor clustering overcomes the energetic barrier to forming open-ended TIR-domain assemblies that recruit downstream effectors (bhatt2024structuralcharacterizationof pages 8-10).

### 4.2 Post-Translational Regulation

Recent studies (2023-2024) have identified regulatory mechanisms that fine-tune MyD88 activity:

**Phosphorylation:** A 2023 study demonstrated that MyD88 serine 257 (S257) phosphorylation promotes MyD88 aggregation, IRAK1 phosphorylation, and NF-κB activation. The oncogenic L265P mutation genocopies this phosphorylation-induced activation (minderman2023theoncogenichuman pages 8-9).

**Acetylation:** A 2024 study showed that upon LPS stimulation, the lysine acetyltransferase CBP acetylates TIR domains in the TLR4 signalosome, including MyD88, enhancing NF-κB signaling while having minimal effect on IRF3 pathways (saikh2024targetingmyeloiddifferentiation pages 4-5).

**Ubiquitination:** OTUD5 deubiquitinase cleaves K11-linked polyubiquitin chains on MyD88 at Lys95, Lys231, and Lys250, enhancing MyD88 oligomerization and myddosome formation after LPS stimulation (li2024microcrystalelectrondiffraction pages 8-11).

The most clinically significant MYD88 alteration is the somatic L265P mutation (leucine to proline at position 265 in the TIR domain), which drives several B-cell malignancies:

**Waldenström Macroglobulinemia (WM):** The L265P mutation occurs in >90% of WM cases, a lymphoplasmacytic lymphoma characterized by IgM monoclonal protein secretion (bagratuni2023myd88wildtype pages 2-4, bagratuni2023myd88wildtype pages 1-2, bagratuni2023myd88wildtype pages 4-5, lemasson2023bcellspecificexpression pages 1-6). This mutation serves as a key diagnostic biomarker and driver of disease pathogenesis (tu2024lymphoplasmacyticlymphomawaldenströmmacroglobulinemia pages 7-9, bagratuni2023myd88wildtype pages 2-4). The mutation promotes constitutive MYD88 aggregation, IRAK/BTK pathway activation, NF-κB signaling, and B-cell survival (tu2024lymphoplasmacyticlymphomawaldenströmmacroglobulinemia pages 7-9, bagratuni2023myd88wildtype pages 2-4, minderman2023theoncogenichuman pages 8-9).

**Diffuse Large B-Cell Lymphoma (DLBCL):** L265P is enriched in activated B-cell-like (ABC) DLBCL and is particularly frequent in primary CNS, primary testicular, and primary cutaneous DLBCL leg-type (bagratuni2023myd88wildtype pages 2-4, song2024myd88andits pages 2-4, tu2024lymphoplasmacyticlymphomawaldenströmmacroglobulinemia pages 9-11, minderman2023theoncogenichuman pages 8-9). The mutation creates oncogenic addiction to MYD88-IRAK-BTK-NF-κB signaling (minderman2023theoncogenichuman pages 8-9).

**IgM Monoclonal Gammopathy of Undetermined Significance (IgM-MGUS):** L265P is present in 50-80% of IgM-MGUS cases and is associated with increased risk of progression to WM, although most carriers do not progress (bagratuni2023myd88wildtype pages 4-5).

### 5.2 Clinical and Therapeutic Implications

**MYD88 Wild-Type Disease:** Approximately 5-10% of WM patients lack the L265P mutation. MYD88 wild-type (WT) WM is associated with higher risk of transformation to aggressive lymphoma, reduced responsiveness to BTK inhibitors, and shorter overall survival compared to L265P-positive disease (bagratuni2023myd88wildtype pages 1-2, bagratuni2023myd88wildtype pages 4-5).

**Molecular Heterogeneity:** A 2024 single-cell RNA sequencing study of WM patients revealed substantial intra-tumor and inter-patient transcriptional heterogeneity even among L265P-positive cases, with distinct gene expression signatures affecting NF-κB, BCL2, and BTK pathways (bagratuni2023myd88wildtype pages 2-4).

**Therapeutic Targeting:** The central role of MYD88 in lymphoma cell survival has prompted development of targeted therapies, including:
- Small molecule inhibitors of MYD88 dimerization (e.g., ST2825) that disrupt myddosome assembly (song2024myd88andits pages 2-4)
- BTK inhibitors (e.g., ibrutinib) that target downstream signaling in MYD88-mutant tumors
- Compounds targeting the TIR domain BB-loop interactions to reduce inflammatory signaling (saikh2024targetingmyeloiddifferentiation pages 2-4, saikh2024targetingmyeloiddifferentiation pages 4-5)

### 5.3 Role in Inflammatory Diseases

Beyond cancer, excessive MYD88 signaling contributes to various inflammatory conditions. Targeting MYD88 has shown promise in preclinical models of:
- Rheumatoid arthritis, where ST2825 reduced synovial fibroblast proliferation and invasiveness (song2024myd88andits pages 2-4)
- Sepsis and acute lung injury, where MYD88 inhibition modulated cytokine storm responses (saikh2024targetingmyeloiddifferentiation pages 2-4)
- Viral infections including SARS-CoV-2, where MYD88 upregulation may impair type I interferon responses (saikh2024targetingmyeloiddifferentiation pages 2-4, saikh2024targetingmyeloiddifferentiation pages 4-5)

## 6. Summary and Current Understanding

Human MYD88 functions as a pivotal cytoplasmic adaptor protein in innate immunity, linking activated pattern-recognition receptors to inflammatory signaling cascades. Its primary role is to scaffold the myddosome, a supramolecular signaling complex that amplifies receptor-derived signals through IRAK kinases, TRAF6, and downstream NF-κB, MAPK, and IRF pathways. MYD88 does not possess enzymatic activity or substrate specificity in the traditional sense; rather, it organizes proximity-driven protein interactions that enable signal transduction.

The protein's modular domain architecture—comprising an N-terminal death domain, intermediate domain, and C-terminal TIR domain—supports sequential receptor engagement, oligomerization, and kinase recruitment. Recent structural advances in 2024 have provided near-atomic resolution views of TIR-domain assembly, revealing the molecular basis for cooperative signaling complex formation.

Clinically, the oncogenic L265P mutation in the TIR domain drives >90% of Waldenström macroglobulinemia cases and substantial fractions of aggressive B-cell lymphomas, establishing MYD88 as both a diagnostic biomarker and therapeutic target. Current research focuses on developing selective inhibitors of MYD88 dimerization and downstream signaling to treat both malignant and inflammatory diseases.

The evidence presented here derives primarily from peer-reviewed publications from 2022-2024, with particular emphasis on recent structural, mechanistic, and clinical studies that have advanced understanding of MYD88 biology and its role in human disease.
