# FAN1 evidence and core-function guide

This guide combines the accepted review of 17 database-provenance and 34 literature-provenance contexts. All 51 imported contexts are reviewed; the historical lane checkpoints remain in the notes. The imported terms, evidence codes, reference identities, qualifiers and supporting entities remain unchanged. Recommendations synthesize the source trace with independently read target evidence; they do not convert an electronic or ancestral assertion into a new experimental assertion. [Campaign instructions](../../../projects/CLINGEN_MENDELIAN.md#curation-instructions) require supported broad terms to be refined with `MODIFY` and prohibit new publication quotations for this pass.

## Catalysis, binding and repair

The database lane independently read the main text of four papers at the Methods, Results and caption passages below. Its original-article scope does not include figure pixels or supplements. The direct lane additionally read other main sections and inspected selected original figures, as separately listed below. Complete availability is not a claim that every paragraph or figure was read.

* [MacKay et al., PMID:20603015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3710700/): the Results on purified human FAN1 and the DR catalytic mutant establish 5′-flap incision and 5′-to-3′ shortening of DNA. The Experimental Procedures specify bacterial expression, purification, synthetic substrates and manganese-containing assays. The recruitment Results distinguish endogenous or tagged human FAN1 foci, UBZ mutations and FANCD2 dependence from worm deletion phenotypes. Human crosslink sensitivity and repair endpoints support a repair role; the proposed late recombination-intermediate mechanism remains a model.
* [Smogorzewska et al., PMID:20603073](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2919743/): human U2OS damage-track recruitment and 293-cell FAN1 immunoprecipitates were read alongside the bacterial human 373–1017 construct, catalytic mutants and nuclease Methods. The paper distinguishes intrinsic 5′ exonuclease/flap-endonuclease activity from some contaminating 3′ exonuclease in the bacterial preparation. Worm assays remain a separate organism context.
* [Pennell et al., PMID:24981866](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4103454/): Results and Methods compare bacterially expressed human and mouse FAN1 on asymmetric DNA substrates. They supply actual experimental mouse-donor grounding for 5′ exonuclease activity. The crystallographic structures of bacterial/phage nuclease domains and their Holliday-junction specificities are separate from the human assays. Mouse analytical ultracentrifugation and a human proteolytic truncation do not establish one oligomeric state for every FAN1 preparation.
* [Wang et al., PMID:25430771](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25430771/): the main Results and captions describe quantitative binding to short phosphorylated flaps, the human 364–1017 DNA-bound structure, successive distributive cleavage and synthetic-crosslink processing checked by mass spectrometry. The predominantly three-nucleotide products matter for terminology. Calcium in the crystallographic complex is not a demonstrated catalytic cofactor.

Current [GO:0008409](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/QuickGO/term/GO:0008409) allows removal of nucleotide residues from the 5′ end. The DNA-specific child [GO:0035312](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/QuickGO/term/GO:0035312) explicitly requires sequential mononucleotides and is therefore not a faithful narrowing of the inspected trimer-product assay. Conversely, [GO:0004528](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/QuickGO/term/GO:0004528) and [EC:3.1.4.1](https://enzyme.expasy.org/EC/3.1.4.1) define removal from a 3′-hydroxy terminus. Their alternative names do not override that reaction definition. The imported phosphodiesterase-I context is refined to the experimentally established 5′-to-3′ exonuclease activity.

[GO:0017108](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/QuickGO/term/GO:0017108) remains the imported, experimentally grounded flap-incision facet. Its literal definition includes substrate exclusivity and Okazaki-fragment wording that merit ontology clarification. The revised core uses [GO:0004520 DNA endonuclease activity](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/QuickGO/term/GO:0004520) to encompass both established flap incision and independently measured non-flap extrusion incision; it does not add a redundant ancestor core alongside a flap core. The endonuclease and retained 5′ exonuclease core terms are non-ancestral. Quantitative DNA binding and structural contacts support [GO:0070336](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/QuickGO/term/GO:0070336), rather than inferring binding only from cleavage. Human crosslink processing supports [GO:0036297](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/QuickGO/term/GO:0036297); ordinary nucleotide excision repair is not treated as a synonym.

## Expanded catalytic and replication-stress evidence

[PMID:26797144, Lachaud et al. 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4770513/) separates recruitment-dependent fork restraint from crosslink resistance. The complete cached main narrative was read, including Results referring to Figures 1–4 and the Discussion. Human U2OS FAN1 knockout/rescue experiments show that UBZ C44A/C47A restores MMC resistance but fails to restore HU-dependent fork restraint; a nuclease-dead preparation also fails the fork endpoint. Mouse MEF fiber/epistasis experiments and aged-mouse cancer phenotypes are distinct evidence. The proposed fork-reversal mechanism remains untested in this study. No supplementary Methods or figure pixels were read in this feedback pass. The physical cleavage substrate and endonuclease-versus-exonuclease contributions to restraint remain unresolved.

[PMID:37549289, Phadte et al. 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10438374/) directly tests full-length human FAN1 and D960A on extrahelical DNA. Actual reading covered Introduction, individual main Results paragraphs, Figures 1–5 captions, Discussion and the main Methods summary; no SI Appendix or figure pixels were inspected. At 125 mM KCl, RFC, PCNA and ATP support incision of nicked circular DNA when the nick and extrusion lie on the same strand. PCNA alone stimulates the corresponding linear substrate, consistent with RFC providing clamp loading; ATP is not required for intrinsic FAN1 catalysis. Random six-base extrusions are also cleaved, so no CAG-exclusive sequence recognition is assigned.

The same study links FAN1 to short-patch extrusion removal through partial depletion/add-back in MLH1/MSH3-deficient HCT116 nuclear extracts. MutSβ competes with FAN1 for extrusion occupancy. MutLα association is a separate interaction: up to fourfold excess did not significantly change FAN1 activity in the tested reaction. Neither result turns FAN1 into a canonical mismatch-repair enzyme. Complete extrusion removal has not been reconstituted from purified components; DNA synthesis and ligation belong to unresolved additional activities, not FAN1.

Biallelic FAN1 deficiency causing karyomegalic interstitial nephritis, human Huntington disease modifier associations and animal repeat-expansion effects are cited background in these papers. This feedback pass did not independently reread the original clinical genetics or animal-repeat studies. They provide disease context, separate from the executed fork and biochemical experiments.

## Core synthesis and ontology decisions

Two coordinated catalytic units describe one enzyme: 5′ exonucleolysis and DNA endonucleolysis. The latter now explicitly covers both flap incision and extrusion incision. The retained crosslink-repair process and [GO:0035753 maintenance of DNA trinucleotide repeats](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/QuickGO/term/GO:0035753) are non-ancestral in the retrieved `is_a`/`part_of` closure. The repeat role is grounded in FAN1 performing DNA incision plus extract depletion/rescue, rather than solely in a genetic modifier association. Flap-structured DNA recognition remains explicit in substrate positioning and existing accepted binding annotations.

The new process facet underwent a performer/parent/comparator/model check. FAN1 itself performs incision; it is not the consumed DNA substrate or the polymerase/ligase completing repair. The term concerns maintenance of repeat fidelity/copy number under chromosome organization and DNA metabolism. Finite current human queries of the same repair-nuclease role (FEN1, EXO1 and ERCC1) returned no annotations under repeat maintenance; this is not evidence that curators overlooked a universal nuclease function. The FAN1-specific biochemical/extract result supplies the positive basis here. The fixed published GO-CAM index has no FAN1 entry. No NEW annotation row is manufactured, and no ancestor/descendant process is added to the same core unit.

Whole-enzyme fork restraint is explicit in the description and catalytic prose but is not assigned independently to either catalytic mode. GO:2000104 negative regulation of DNA-templated DNA replication was considered; no mode-specific assignment is made. GO:0031297 replication fork processing expressly requires restoration and restart, and the new 2016 restraint experiment does not itself establish those endpoints. Fork reversal is not asserted. Questions ask which cleavage reaction accounts for the observed restraint and which additional activities complete extrusion removal.

Damage-site localization remains the demonstrated crosslink-repair context in the cores. Broader nucleoplasmic detection is retained without assuming that all imaged protein is catalytically active or that every extrusion reaction occupies an induced focus. The two existing HR/NER UNDECIDED contexts remain source-access specific; the two reader/adaptor UNDECIDED contexts remain definition specific. Thirteen original reference objects are unchanged; the GO_REF:0000044 manual assessment has one stale replacement sentence corrected to retain nucleus and the independent damage-site core. Every original identity, title, finding and flag is unchanged. Natural products remain exact, and two correctly identified full-main references are added with separate actual-read scopes. The absence of legacy availability metadata for PMID:25430771 remains exact. No new publication quotations or source-cache edits were made.
