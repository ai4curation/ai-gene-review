Gene Description

Adipogenin (ADIG, also called SMAF1) is an 80-residue microprotein of the endoplasmic reticulum membrane, anchored by a single conserved N-terminal hydrophobic helix (residues 14-38) with its short C-terminal tail facing the cytosol. Its expression is essentially restricted to adipocytes and is a direct transcriptional output of PPAR-gamma, rising sharply during adipogenesis and falling when the adipocyte phenotype is lost; it is also induced in steatotic liver. ADIG's molecular activity is to bind seipin, the ER protein that organizes lipid droplet biogenesis at ER-droplet junctions. Seipin assembles into both undecameric and dodecameric rings, and ADIG selectively binds the dodecamer, bridging neighbouring subunits to stabilize the ring and promote its assembly; the resulting rigid seipin-ADIG particle favours droplet expansion over new droplet nucleation, redirecting triacylglycerol flux toward growing droplets and assisting the relocation of droplet-targeted proteins such as ACSL3 and GPAT4. Through this single structural role ADIG sets how much lipid an adipocyte stores: loss of adipogenin impairs adipocyte differentiation, yields leaner animals on a high-fat diet and in the leptin-deficient background, disrupts triglyceride accumulation in brown adipose tissue, while adipocyte-specific overexpression enlarges droplets and increases fat mass. Adipogenin deficiency additionally lowers fat-mass-adjusted circulating leptin and impairs leptin secretion from adipose explants, and human genetic variation at the ADIG locus associates with BMI-adjusted leptin levels.

Proposed New Ontology Terms

seipin-adipogenin complex

Definition: A protein-containing complex of the endoplasmic reticulum membrane consisting of a dodecameric ring of seipin (BSCL2) together with adipogenin subunits bound between adjacent seipin protomers. The complex is rigid relative to adipogenin-free seipin oligomers and promotes the expansion of nascent lipid droplets.

Justification: GO has no term for any seipin-containing complex, so the defining assembly of this gene product cannot be recorded in the in_complex slot of its core function. The honest difficulty is that the stoichiometry is NOT fixed: the authors state plainly that [PMID:41196993 "the complex might be heterogeneous with respect to seipin:Adig stoichiometry."] and enumerate a set of coexisting species [PMID:41196993 "There may thus be several different types of seipin complexes: seipin-alone undecamers, seipin-alone dodecamers, Adig-poor seipin dodecamers, and Adig-rich seipin dodecamers"], with Adig exchanging on and off on a ~10 s timescale by FRAP, and the C12 cryo-EM map being a symmetrised average over many particles. A variable-occupancy, dynamically exchanging subunit is a harder case for minting a discrete complex term, not an easier one, and the proposal should be judged knowing that. The case for the term anyway: the Adig-bound dodecamer is compositionally and functionally distinct from the Adig-free undecamer and dodecamer - only it is selected by Adig, only it is rigidified, and it behaves differently toward nascent droplets - so it is a real biological entity that curation currently cannot name. A parent term for the seipin oligomer itself would also be useful, and would let the Adig-bound form be a child.

Parent term: endoplasmic reticulum protein-containing complex

Core Functions

Oligomer-state-selective binding partner of seipin that licenses lipid droplet expansion. ADIG is anchored in the ER membrane by a single conserved N-terminal helix with its C-terminus in the cytosol, placing it in the same bilayer as seipin, the ring-shaped organizer of droplet biogenesis. Mammalian seipin populates two oligomeric states, undecameric and dodecameric; ADIG binds only the dodecamer and bridges neighbouring subunits, so it both selects and stabilizes one assembly state, and in its absence seipin is prone to aggregate rather than form functional rings. The consequence is a change in what the seipin machine does: the ADIG-loaded, rigid complex is mobile rather than stably docked at nascent droplets, and it shifts triacylglycerol flux away from nucleating new droplets toward expanding existing ones, while assisting relocation of droplet-targeted enzymes such as ACSL3 and GPAT4. Because ADIG transcription is a direct PPAR-gamma output confined to adipocytes, this structural switch is the means by which the adipogenic program sets droplet size and therefore tissue lipid storage capacity - gain of ADIG enlarges droplets and fat mass, while loss impairs adipogenesis and brown-fat triglyceride accumulation. ADIG contributes no catalytic activity; its entire function is structural. The adipocyte differentiation terms are grouped here rather than split into a second core function because they are the organismal reading of this same activity, not a separate one: ADIG is a terminal PPAR-gamma output whose loss impairs differentiation and yields leaner animals, which is what a droplet-expansion defect looks like at tissue scale. One point of framing worth stating explicitly: ADIG is annotated as BINDING a complex (GO:0044877) while also being proposed as a CONSTITUENT of one. Both are correct, and the reason is that the association is dynamic - FRAP shows Adig exchanging on and off the seipin ring on a ~10 s timescale, and Adig-poor and Adig-rich dodecamers coexist - so ADIG is a reversibly associating subunit rather than a permanently incorporated one.

Suggested Questions for Experts

Q: Does human ADIG bind human seipin with the same dodecamer selectivity shown for the mouse protein? Every human ADIG annotation is ISS or IBA, and the structural work used mouse Adig, so the human orthology assumption is untested even though it is very likely.

Q: The preprint of PMID:41196993 reported that Adig overexpression elevates thermogenesis during cold exposure and that adipocyte-specific knockout impairs cold tolerance, but neither claim appears anywhere in the published Science paper. Was the thermogenic phenotype withdrawn, or simply cut for space? It is not annotated here because the published version does not support it.

Q: Is the reduced leptin secretion in Adig null mice a direct consequence of altered droplet architecture, or a separate role for the protein at the ER? The GWAS association of the ADIG locus with BMI-adjusted leptin suggests the link also holds in humans.

Q: ADIG is induced in steatotic liver by hepatic PPAR-gamma. Does it drive hepatocyte droplet enlargement there through the same seipin mechanism, and is that a tractable target in fatty liver disease?

Current model (mechanistic narrative)

ADIG (adipogenin/SMAF1) is an adipocyte-enriched ~10 kDa microprotein that functions in lipid droplet biogenesis as a transcriptional output of the adipogenic program and a regulator of fat mass [PMID:26427354, PMID:33691105]. Its expression is tightly coupled to the adipocyte phenotype, induced during adipogenesis, and wholly dependent on PPARγ, which directly binds a functional PPRE in the Adig promoter; ADIG transcription is further modulated by insulin, glucose, and Srebp1c [PMID:26427354, PMID:27766294, PMID:37249025]. ADIG localizes to lipid droplets in adipocytes, and genetic loss of ADIG impairs adipogenesis, produces leaner mice on a high-fat diet and in the ob/ob background, and reduces leptin secretion from adipose tissue [PMID:26427354, PMID:33691105]. Mechanistically, ADIG directly binds seipin and selectively stabilizes its dodecameric oligomer, bridging adjacent subunits to promote seipin assembly and support lipid droplet formation, with bidirectional genetic perturbation altering lipid droplet morphology, fat mass, and thermogenic cold tolerance. PPARγ-driven ADIG expression is not restricted to adipose tissue but is also induced in hepatic steatosis PMID:37249025.

core_functions:
- description: 'Oligomer-state-selective binding partner of seipin that licenses lipid droplet expansion.
    ADIG is anchored in the ER membrane by a single conserved N-terminal helix with its C-terminus in
    the cytosol, placing it in the same bilayer as seipin, the ring-shaped organizer of droplet biogenesis.
    Mammalian seipin populates two oligomeric states, undecameric and dodecameric; ADIG binds only the
    dodecamer and bridges neighbouring subunits, so it both selects and stabilizes one assembly state,
    and in its absence seipin is prone to aggregate rather than form functional rings. The consequence
    is a change in what the seipin machine does: the ADIG-loaded, rigid complex is mobile rather than
    stably docked at nascent droplets, and it shifts triacylglycerol flux away from nucleating new droplets
    toward expanding existing ones, while assisting relocation of droplet-targeted enzymes such as ACSL3
    and GPAT4. Because ADIG transcription is a direct PPAR-gamma output confined to adipocytes, this structural
    switch is the means by which the adipogenic program sets droplet size and therefore tissue lipid storage
    capacity - gain of ADIG enlarges droplets and fat mass, while loss impairs adipogenesis and brown-fat
    triglyceride accumulation. ADIG contributes no catalytic activity; its entire function is structural.

    because they are the organismal reading of this same activity, not a separate one: ADIG is a terminal
