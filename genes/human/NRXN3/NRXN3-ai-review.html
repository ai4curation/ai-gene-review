Gene Description

Neurexin-3 (NRXN3) is a presynaptic single-pass type I membrane protein of the neurexin family that organizes synapses through trans-synaptic adhesion complexes. Like NRXN1 and NRXN2, the very large NRXN3 gene uses two promoters: the upstream promoter produces the long neurexin-3-alpha isoforms described by this entry, with six laminin/neurexin/sex-hormone-binding globulin (LNS) domains interspersed with three EGF-like repeats, and an internal promoter produces short neurexin-3-beta isoforms (separate UniProt entry Q9HDB5) with only the sixth LNS domain; both share a heparan-sulfate-modified stalk, a transmembrane helix and a short cytoplasmic tail ending in a PDZ-binding motif that binds CASK and Mint scaffolds. Extensive alternative splicing diversifies the protein: splice site 4 (SS4) in LNS6 selects between ligands, splicing at SS5 yields soluble or membrane-anchored forms, and an interneuron-enriched splice variant is GPI-anchored and targeted to dendrite-inhibiting GABAergic terminals. Through its LNS domains NRXN3 binds postsynaptic neuroligins, LRRTM2, latrophilin-1 and, via LNS2 of the alpha isoforms, dystroglycan, and SS4-containing forms bind secreted cerebellins and C1q-like proteins that bridge to postsynaptic receptors. NRXN3 acts mainly as a specifier of synaptic properties rather than of synapse number: at hippocampal excitatory synapses presynaptic neurexin-3, through its extracellular domain and SS4 splicing (via a neurexin-3-cerebellin-2-GluD1 complex), controls postsynaptic AMPA-receptor levels and LTP, whereas at inhibitory synapses in olfactory bulb and prefrontal cortex it is required for normal GABA release probability through a trans-synaptic loop with dystroglycan; its effects differ between brain regions, synapse types and sexes. Neurexin-3 is also required for maturation of hair-cell ribbon synapses. Constitutive loss of both alpha and beta neurexin-3 is largely lethal at birth in mice, and Nrxn3 variants alter socially transmitted fear. NRXN3 is expressed outside the brain as well, including cardiac-specific splice forms and vascular expression. In humans, rare exonic NRXN3 deletions with incomplete penetrance are found in autism spectrum disorder, common variants have been associated with substance dependence and waist circumference, and neurexin-3-alpha is the target of autoantibodies in an autoimmune encephalitis.

Beta isoforms from the internal promoter are a separate UniProt entry, Q9HDB5 (GOA rows in NRXN3-bioinformatics/NRXN3-beta-isoform-entry-goa.tsv; reference only, not reviewed here).
  • UniProt function text is by similarity to mouse Nrxn1 [file:human/NRXN3/NRXN3-uniprot.txt "Neuronal cell surface protein that may be involved in cell"] (continuing "recognition and cell adhesion"); location [file:human/NRXN3/NRXN3-uniprot.txt "SUBCELLULAR LOCATION: Presynaptic cell membrane"]. Interactions with APBA1 (Mint1), GRIP2, LNX1 and PATJ (all PDZ proteins) come from the fragmentomics PBM screen (PMID:36115835).
  • Gene structure: PMID:12036300. SS5 produces membrane-anchored vs soluble NRXN3 PMID:17804423. A GPI-anchored NRXN3 proteoform (AS5, exon 24) exists in interneurons PMID:35550065
  • Non-neuronal expression: heart-specific splicing PMID:12379233; vascular neurexins (family-level, beta-neurexin antibody) PMID:19926856.

  • Dystroglycan via LNS2 PMID:11470830
  • Nanoscale organization PMID:37543682
  • Trans-synaptic control of postsynaptic AMPA receptors (the best-defined NRXN3-specific function):
  • PMID:23827676
  • PMID:26030848; this is independent of the neurexin tail and of synapse number PMID:26030848
  • Non-overlapping with NRXN1 (NMDAR) and NRXN2 (no effect) PMID:31005376
  • Mechanism: Nrxn3SS4+–Cbln2–GluD1 PMID:34135511; circuit dependence PMID:36205393
  • Mossy fibre: C1ql2 acts via a specific Nrxn3 variant PMID:38358390
  • Presynaptic release, region/synapse specific:
  • Olfactory bulb, needs intracellular sequences PMID:26030848
  • Inhibitory release probability via dystroglycan loop (OB, PFC) PMID:36997523
  • Dendrite-targeting interneuron synapses PMID:35550065
  • Sex- and synapse-dependent effects in ventral subiculum PMID:34879268
  • Human A687T missense (profound ID + epilepsy patient) is a presynaptic gain of function PMID:31578233
  • Pan-neurexin (not NRXN3-specific) presynaptic organizer roles: Ca2+ channel coupling PMID:32134527 and GABA-B receptor signalling PMID:33888718. These are triple-neurexin deletions; they do not isolate NRXN3 and are not used for NRXN3-specific claims.
  • Ribbon synapses: PMID:39254120
  • Knockout: constitutive Nrxn3 alpha/beta KO is largely perinatal lethal PMID:26030848; synapse density normal in hippocampal cultures PMID:26030848.

Behaviour and human genetics (weighed critically)

  • Mouse: Nrxn3 R498W (129S1 strain) enhances observational fear; SST+ ACC deletion reproduces it PMID:29681532 (abstract only). OB deletion impairs olfaction (Aoto 2015).
  • ASD CNVs (source of the BHF-UCL IGI rows): four ASD probands with NRXN3 deletions, several inherited from carrier parents without ASD PMID:22209245 The IGI "with" Q9HDB5 reflects deletions removing both alpha and beta exons PMID:22209245. One proband was "gifted" PMID:22209245; language findings are a clinical diagnosis of "speech language delays" in one proband who later had near-normal speech.
  • Addiction: SNP association with alcohol dependence near SS5 PMID:17804423; suggestive linkage of opioid dependence to 14q that is ancestry-specific and spans a large region PMID:17409192. Numerous smaller candidate-SNP studies (smoking, impulsivity, borderline traits) exist. Assessment: modest-sized candidate-gene associations and a linkage peak, not genome-wide significant, not consistently replicated, and without a variant-to-function mechanism; they support no GO process annotation (e.g. no "response to drug" or "behavioral response to ethanol" term is warranted).
  • Waist circumference GWAS PMID:19557197 — genome-wide significant only in the combined analysis; mechanism unknown; not GO-annotatable.
  • Autoimmunity: neurexin-3-alpha autoantibody encephalitis PMID:27170573

Divergences from NRXN1 / NRXN2

  • Signal transduction replacement: NRXN1/NRXN2 used GO:0099545; for NRXN3 the child GO:0099557 (…modulating synaptic transmission) is used, because the NRXN3-specific trans-synaptic signal is a modulation of postsynaptic AMPA receptor levels without changes in synapse number (Aoto 2013/2015, Dai 2019/2021), and mouse Nrxn3 already has GO:0099557 by IDA (PMID:34135511, UniProt). GO:0099557 is_a GO:0099545, so it remains consistent with the module.
  • Release role: like NRXN2 (and unlike NRXN1's core neurotransmitter secretion), NRXN3 gets NEW GO:0099171; its effect is synapse- and region-specific (required for GABA release in OB, inhibitory release probability in OB/PFC; sex-dependent opposite effects in vSUB). NRXN3 has no neurotransmitter secretion row in GOA.
  • PDZ: as for NRXN1, protein binding -> PDZ domain binding, used as the MF of the release core function (intracellular sequences are required for OB GABA release; Aoto 2015). NRXN2 had no such rows.
  • Learning IGI: kept non-core like NRXN1, but for a different reason - NRXN1 has biallelic-loss severe ID, whereas the NRXN3 CNV paper reports no learning phenotype; the row is retained only because the term is supported for the gene by mouse data and the IBA (validator flags inconsistent actions per term).
  • Social/adult behaviour IGI: kept non-core as for NRXN1/2; penetrance is incomplete (carrier parents without ASD).

Items for follow-up

  • GOA has no NRXN3 row for the cerebellin/GluD1 signalling complex (mouse Nrxn3 has GO:0030545 contributes_to, IDA) or for GO:0099072 regulation of postsynaptic membrane neurotransmitter receptor levels (SynGO, mouse). Not proposed as NEW here; GO:0099557 captures the process.

description: 'Neurexin-3 (NRXN3) is a presynaptic single-pass type I membrane protein of the neurexin family that
  organizes synapses through trans-synaptic adhesion complexes. Like NRXN1 and NRXN2, the very large NRXN3 gene
  uses two promoters: the upstream promoter produces the long neurexin-3-alpha isoforms described by this entry,
  with six laminin/neurexin/sex-hormone-binding globulin (LNS) domains interspersed with three EGF-like repeats,
  and an internal promoter produces short neurexin-3-beta isoforms (separate UniProt entry Q9HDB5) with only the
  sixth LNS domain; both share a heparan-sulfate-modified stalk, a transmembrane helix and a short cytoplasmic tail
  ending in a PDZ-binding motif that binds CASK and Mint scaffolds. Extensive alternative splicing diversifies the
  protein: splice site 4 (SS4) in LNS6 selects between ligands, splicing at SS5 yields soluble or membrane-anchored
  forms, and an interneuron-enriched splice variant is GPI-anchored and targeted to dendrite-inhibiting GABAergic
  terminals. Through its LNS domains NRXN3 binds postsynaptic neuroligins, LRRTM2, latrophilin-1 and, via LNS2 of
  the alpha isoforms, dystroglycan, and SS4-containing forms bind secreted cerebellins and C1q-like proteins that
  bridge to postsynaptic receptors. NRXN3 acts mainly as a specifier of synaptic properties rather than of synapse
  number: at hippocampal excitatory synapses presynaptic neurexin-3, through its extracellular domain and SS4 splicing
  (via a neurexin-3-cerebellin-2-GluD1 complex), controls postsynaptic AMPA-receptor levels and LTP, whereas at
  inhibitory synapses in olfactory bulb and prefrontal cortex it is required for normal GABA release probability
  through a trans-synaptic loop with dystroglycan; its effects differ between brain regions, synapse types and sexes.
  Neurexin-3 is also required for maturation of hair-cell ribbon synapses. Constitutive loss of both alpha and beta
  neurexin-3 is largely lethal at birth in mice, and Nrxn3 variants alter socially transmitted fear. NRXN3 is expressed
  outside the brain as well, including cardiac-specific splice forms and vascular expression. In humans, rare exonic
  NRXN3 deletions with incomplete penetrance are found in autism spectrum disorder, common variants have been associated
  with substance dependence and waist circumference, and neurexin-3-alpha is the target of autoantibodies in an
  autoimmune encephalitis.'
