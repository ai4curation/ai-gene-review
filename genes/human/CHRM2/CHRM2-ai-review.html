Gene Description

CHRM2 encodes the M2 muscarinic acetylcholine receptor, a seven-transmembrane receptor that preferentially activates Gi/o proteins. It mediates inhibitory cyclic-AMP signaling, regulates cardiac rate and excitability, and acts as a neuronal autoreceptor controlling transmitter release. G-protein beta-gamma signaling is an important component of its physiological responses. Phosphorylation and arrestin recruitment regulate receptor desensitization and internalization. Human M2 can also engage Gs and Gq/11 in expression systems, depending on agonist and concentration.

CHRM2 evidence notes

2026-10-26: complete source-specific acetylcholine panel audit

Human M2–Go structures and G-protein activation assays (full PMID:36690613) do not directly measure adenylyl cyclase or cAMP. Retain true inhibitory signaling using independent human CHO-cell experiments (PMID:17065363), which also distinguish Gs-dependent cAMP stimulation at high carbachol and Gq/11-dependent IP accumulation. Hence M2 PLC signaling is a demonstrated non-core capacity rather than a false claim simply because Gi/o is preferred.

Native mouse cardiac regulation (PMID:20884879, PMID:24318880) supports conserved cardiac physiology without identifying every human Galpha effector from the whole-cell response. Presynaptic autoreceptor scope and neuronal locations are bounded by subtype-resolved native experiments, including PMID:8182478 and neuromuscular studies. Arrestin binding is supported by wild-type/mutant phosphorylation studies (PMID:10952973), curated ARRB1/2 interaction and later recruitment experiments. The original PMID:11566136 has bovine-arrestin/construct limits and is not the sole proof of native human binding. Nervous-system developmental and virus-response claims remain source-specific unresolved; they are not removed from titles or predominance of other functions.
