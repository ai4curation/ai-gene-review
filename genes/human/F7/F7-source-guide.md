# F7 evidence and biological scope

F7 encodes the circulating factor VII precursor. Activated FVIIa is a serine endopeptidase that works with tissue factor (TF) and calcium at extracellular membrane surfaces. Its cleavage of factors IX and X is distinct from the processing of FVII itself by other enzymes. The precursor also passes through ER and Golgi compartments during secretion; those transit pools do not make F7 a carboxylase, trafficking factor or furin-like processing enzyme.

## TF receptor binding and performed chemistry

The specific TF association is represented by signaling receptor binding (GO:0005102). TF is both receptor and coagulation cofactor. This term identifies binding to that partner; it does not require an inhibited crystal construct to signal. The independent human-FVIIa signaling study supplies a separate cellular context. No exact TF-specific binding term was established by the saved current searches, and generic heterodimerization would add a less informative stoichiometric description. Extrinsic coagulation, serine endopeptidase activity and zymogen activation describe the pathway and step performed by FVIIa; processing of FVII by Xa is a separate substrate relationship.

## Rhythmic activity and response-context boundaries

FVII rhythmic activity persisted in constant darkness, distinguishing an endogenous rhythm from a light/dark-associated daily fluctuation. The assay measured generated FXa in FVII-depleted plasma, not FVIIa hydrolysis of the fluorescent reporter. No figure pixels or online supplement were inspected. Human two-time-point sampling alone does not establish an endogenous circadian oscillator. These results corroborate a non-core rhythmic-activity context, while the original rat PMID:2607889 remains available only as an abstract.
