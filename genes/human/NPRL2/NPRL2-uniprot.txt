CC   -!- FUNCTION: Catalytic component of the GATOR1 complex, a multiprotein
CC       complex that functions as an inhibitor of the amino acid-sensing branch
CC       of the mTORC1 pathway (PubMed:23723238, PubMed:29590090,
CC       PubMed:35338845, PubMed:38006878). In response to amino acid depletion,
CC       the GATOR1 complex has GTPase activating protein (GAP) activity and
CC       strongly increases GTP hydrolysis by RagA/RRAGA (or RagB/RRAGB) within
CC       heterodimeric Rag complexes, thereby turning them into their inactive
CC       GDP-bound form, releasing mTORC1 from lysosomal surface and inhibiting
CC       mTORC1 signaling (PubMed:23723238, PubMed:29590090, PubMed:35338845).
CC       In the presence of abundant amino acids, the GATOR1 complex is
CC       ubiquitinated and inhibited by GATOR2 (PubMed:23723238,
CC       PubMed:36528027). Within the GATOR1 complex, NPRL2 constitutes the
CC       catalytic subunit that mediates the GTPase activator activity and under
CC       methionine-sufficient conditions, the GTPase activator activity is
CC       inhibited by PRMT1 through methylation and consequently inducing timely
CC       mTORC1 activation (PubMed:27173016, PubMed:30651352, PubMed:35338845).
CC       {ECO:0000269|PubMed:23723238, ECO:0000269|PubMed:27173016,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:29590090, ECO:0000269|PubMed:30651352,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:35338845, ECO:0000269|PubMed:36528027,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:38006878}.
CC   -!- FUNCTION: Suppresses Src-dependent tyrosine phosphorylation and
CC       activation of PDPK1 and its downstream signaling (PubMed:18616680).
CC       Down-regulates PDPK1 kinase activity by interfering with tyrosine
CC       phosphorylation at 'Tyr-9', 'Tyr-373' and 'Tyr-376' residues
CC       (PubMed:18616680). May act as a tumor suppressor (PubMed:18616680).
CC       Suppresses cell growth and enhances sensitivity to various anticancer
CC       drugs (PubMed:18616680). {ECO:0000269|PubMed:18616680}.
CC   -!- SUBUNIT: Within the GATOR complex, component of the GATOR1 subcomplex,
CC       made of DEPDC5, NPRL2 and NPRL3 (PubMed:19521502, PubMed:23723238,
CC       PubMed:29590090, PubMed:35338845). GATOR1 mediates the strong
CC       interaction of the GATOR complex with small GTPases Rag (RagA/RRAGA,
CC       RagB/RRAGB, RagC/RRAGC and/or RagD/RRAGD) heterodimers
CC       (PubMed:23723238, PubMed:29590090). GATOR1 interacts with
CC       GPR155/LYCHOS; interaction takes place in presence of cholesterol and
CC       prevents interaction between GATOR1 and KICSTOR (PubMed:36007018).
CC       Interacts with PDPK1 (PubMed:18616680). {ECO:0000269|PubMed:18616680,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:19521502, ECO:0000269|PubMed:23723238,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:29590090, ECO:0000269|PubMed:35338845,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:36007018}.

CC   -!- DISEASE: Note=Inactivating mutations and truncating deletions in the
CC       genes encoding GATOR1 proteins, including NPRL2, are detected in
CC       glioblastoma and ovarian tumors and are associated with loss of
CC       heterozygosity events. Inactivation of GATOR1 proteins promotes
CC       constitutive localization of mTORC1 to the lysosomal membrane and
CC       blocks mTORC1 inactivation following amino acid withdrawal
CC       (PubMed:23723238). {ECO:0000269|PubMed:23723238}.
CC   -!- DISEASE: Epilepsy, familial focal, with variable foci 2 (FFEVF2)
CC       [MIM:617116]: An autosomal dominant form of epilepsy characterized by
CC       focal seizures arising from different cortical regions, including the
CC       temporal, frontal, parietal, and occipital lobes. Seizure types
CC       commonly include temporal lobe epilepsy, frontal lobe epilepsy, and
CC       nocturnal frontal lobe epilepsy. Some patients may have intellectual
CC       disability or autism spectrum disorders. Seizure onset usually occurs
CC       in the first or second decades, although later onset has been reported,
CC       and there is phenotypic variability within families. A subset of
CC       patients have structural brain abnormalities. Penetrance of the
CC       disorder is incomplete. {ECO:0000269|PubMed:26505888,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:27173016}. Note=The disease is caused by variants
CC       affecting the gene represented in this entry.
