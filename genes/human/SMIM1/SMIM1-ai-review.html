Gene Description

SMIM1 (small integral membrane protein 1) is a 78-amino-acid single-pass membrane protein of the erythrocyte plasma membrane that carries the Vel blood group antigen. It is expressed mainly in the erythroid lineage, with highest levels in bone marrow and induction during erythroid differentiation of CD34+ progenitors. The protein has a cytoplasmic N-terminal region bearing phosphoserines (Ser6, Ser17, Ser22, Ser27) and an acetylated initiator methionine, a single transmembrane helix near the C-terminus, and a very short extracellular C-terminal segment that displays the Vel epitope (type II / tail-anchored topology). SMIM1 forms disulfide-linked homodimers, through Cys77 and a transmembrane GxxxG motif, and dimerization promotes surface display of the Vel antigen. Homozygosity for a 17-bp frameshift deletion in exon 3 abolishes the protein and causes the Vel-negative phenotype, in which anti-Vel alloantibodies can cause haemolytic transfusion reactions. Its molecular function is unknown. Indirect evidence links it to red cell formation: an intronic eQTL that lowers SMIM1 expression is associated with lower mean corpuscular haemoglobin concentration, and smim1 morpholino knockdown in zebrafish mildly reduces red cell numbers. Humans lacking SMIM1 are healthy but show higher body weight, lower circulating thyroid hormone and reduced resting energy expenditure.

  of the erythrocyte plasma membrane that carries the Vel blood group antigen. It is expressed mainly
  in the erythroid lineage, with highest levels in bone marrow and induction during erythroid differentiation
  of CD34+ progenitors. The protein has a cytoplasmic N-terminal region bearing phosphoserines (Ser6,
  Ser17, Ser22, Ser27) and an acetylated initiator methionine, a single transmembrane helix near the C-terminus,
  and a very short extracellular C-terminal segment that displays the Vel epitope (type II / tail-anchored
  topology). SMIM1 forms disulfide-linked homodimers, through Cys77 and a transmembrane GxxxG motif, and
  dimerization promotes surface display of the Vel antigen. Homozygosity for a 17-bp frameshift deletion
  in exon 3 abolishes the protein and causes the Vel-negative phenotype, in which anti-Vel alloantibodies
  can cause haemolytic transfusion reactions. Its molecular function is unknown. Indirect evidence links
  it to red cell formation: an intronic eQTL that lowers SMIM1 expression is associated with lower mean
  corpuscular haemoglobin concentration, and smim1 morpholino knockdown in zebrafish mildly reduces red
  cell numbers. Humans lacking SMIM1 are healthy but show higher body weight, lower circulating thyroid
  hormone and reduced resting energy expenditure.'
