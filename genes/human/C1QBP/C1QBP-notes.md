### Molecular synthesis

Four distinct, existing molecular activities organize the review. They are not four names for one interaction.

1. **Mitochondrial mRNA binding and translation support.** The normal cache for [PMID:22904065](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22904065/) contains actual full text. Mouse p32 K89A/K93A constructs, recombinant RNA binding, loss/rescue and translation assays connect RNA engagement to protein synthesis. Human HeLa p32 immunoprecipitation identifies mitoribosomal associations. Selected Methods and Results were read; the human association experiment is not described as a purified human translation system. The human patient and mouse-cell rescue observations in [PMID:28942965](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28942965/) corroborate mitochondrial function. [PMID:9305894](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9305894/) supplies human matrix localization and separate yeast loss/human-cDNA rescue. Necessity alone is not the mechanistic basis of this core: RNA and ribosome engagement provide the direct work.
2. **m5C-associated RNA recognition and clearance.** [PMID:39019044](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39019044/) assigns methylation to NSUN4, recognition/recruitment to C1QBP and degradation to PNPT1. The normal cache is abstract-only. Selected indexed primary Results and Methods were additionally inspected: methylated-versus-unmodified RNA pulldown uses precleared total cell lysate, and cellular CLIP/partner association supports the reader model. These experiments do not establish isolated C1QBP affinity or an intrinsic recognition site. Initial publisher/PMC access attempts did not expose the correction. ROOT subsequently recovered its complete text from the [primary author institution](https://pure.kaist.ac.kr/en/publications/erratum-rna-5-methylcytosine-marks-mitochondrial-double-stranded-/). It corrects the IMT1 reagent citation from Spahr to Bonekamp, removes an extraneous prefix before 2-CM and corrects the expansion of IMT1 to inhibitor of mitochondrial transcription 1. It does not alter the C1QBP assays. The earlier inaccessible-content assessment is preserved as historical; no normal correction cache was fabricated. The reader is an adaptor activity, not C1QBP nuclease catalysis. Its marked noncoding-RNA clearance context is distinct from the mRNA/translation core; an additional generic RNA-binding or adaptor ancestor is not added.
3. **C1q recognition and complement regulation.** Cell-derived and recombinant mature human protein bind the globular C1q region and inhibit hemolysis in [PMID:8195709](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8195709/). The available abstract is itself truncated at 400 words; no complete-paper read is claimed. This is recognition/regulation, not a C1q subunit or protease function. The keyword-derived classical activation row is refined to the already represented regulation term. The official [activation definition](https://amigo.geneontology.org/amigo/term/GO:0006958) describes the cascade steps, whereas [GO:0030449](https://amigo.geneontology.org/amigo/term/GO:0030449) describes their regulation.
4. **Kininogen binding and surface presentation.** [PMID:8662673](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8662673/) tests endothelial p33 and recombinant protein, distinguishing high- from low-molecular-weight kininogen and its D5H region. [PMID:8710908](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8710908/) separately supports kininogen and factor XII interactions. These binding activities supply an extrinsic surface assembly site; kallikrein, factor XII and prolylcarboxypeptidase perform the proteolytic reactions. The core uses the verified high-molecular-weight kininogen-binding child, GO:0030985, supported by the refined existing partner-specific row and the direct ligand-class row. The inherited ligand-class row is also refined using independent human specificity evidence, without claiming inspection or correction of the original IBD node; no extra parent core or NEW annotation is added. No new kinin-generation process annotation is proposed. Historical apparent tetramer language in old papers/Reactome is not used as a new oligomeric-stoichiometry assertion.

C1QBP is predominantly mitochondrial but not universally mitochondria-only. Later cell/tissue localization, surface antibody detection and purified endothelial p33 reconcile the matrix and surface pools. Rat tissue observations are not renamed as human assays. Nuclear/cytoplasmic transcription, splicing, preribosome remodeling, DNA-damage and apoptosis functions remain contextual. The Hrk perturbation evidence supports an apoptotic role without establishing an autonomous p32 pore. The MRE11 paper explicitly supports deoxyribonuclease inhibition before ATM-dependent complex release; its repair-promoting title does not negate this inhibitory phase. GO:0060703 is a verified refinement of generic enzyme inhibition, not nuclease activity assigned to C1QBP.
