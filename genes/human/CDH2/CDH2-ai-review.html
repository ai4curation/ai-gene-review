Gene Description

CDH2 encodes N-cadherin, a classical single-pass transmembrane cadherin. Its extracellular cadherin domains bind cadherins on neighboring cells to mediate calcium-dependent adhesion, while its cytoplasmic tail binds catenins that organize and stabilize adherens junctions. This adhesive architecture supports tissue integrity and the development and organization of neural and cardiac tissues. Neural stem-cell anchorage, synaptic organization and axon growth are specialized contexts of this adhesion system. De novo variants affecting the extracellular or cytoplasmic domains are associated with a multisystem developmental disorder that includes callosal, cardiac, ocular and genital abnormalities.

CDH2 evidence and annotation review

Reviewed 2026-10-10. Human CDH2 encodes N-cadherin (UniProt P19022), a classical transmembrane cadherin whose extracellular region mediates calcium-dependent adhesion and whose cytoplasmic tail binds catenins. The review preserves all 114 seeded assertions, their original evidence, qualifiers, partners and isoform fields, and both seeded products. There are no NEW annotations. The two core molecular activities are cadherin binding involved in cell-cell adhesion (GO:0098641) and beta-catenin binding (GO:0008013).

Direct adhesion and catenin evidence

The adhesion refinement replaces the existing generic cadherin-binding annotation; it does not add an overlapping NEW row. GO:0098641 captures the cadherin-mediated trans interaction more precisely than the broader cell-cell adhesion mediator term. The primary developmental-disease study tests wild-type and variant N-cadherin in L-cell aggregation and mixed-cell trans-adhesion assays, with expression and cell-surface controls. Its abstract reports that variants “impair the adhesive activity of N-cadherin” (PMID:31585109(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31585109/)). The selected full Results and Figure 3 were also inspected through the primary PMC article. Calcium was present in the assay; changes to calcium-binding pockets were modelled, rather than established by direct variant calcium-affinity measurements. The main text does not establish construct species, and supplementary methods were not accessible. The phenotype is therefore evidence for N-cadherin-mediated adhesion, with this construct limitation retained. Human leukemia-cell adhesion to N-cadherin-coated wells provides independent human-cell support at abstract scope (PMID:19101069(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19101069/)).

Beta-catenin binding is independently grounded in endogenous N-cadherin association in human HEK293 cells and the effects of beta-catenin armadillo-domain deletion, read in selected full Results and Methods (PMID:17679699(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17679699/)). Human 293T co-immunoprecipitation in PMID:18948590 and U373 cadherin/catenin complexes in PMID:17098867 corroborate that role. The latter study used pan-cadherin antibodies and estimated that most cadherin in that system was N-cadherin; it is not a purified binary interaction assay. N-cadherin cytoplasmic GST fusions were actually included in the beta-catenin controls of the otherwise VE-cadherin-focused PMID:17057644. Selected full figures were inspected, avoiding a conclusion based only on the article title. Precise construct species for that experiment was not independently resolved.

Plakoglobin association has separate support from human HT-1080 co-immunoprecipitation and deletion mapping at abstract scope (PMID:7650039(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7650039/)). That abstract maps plakoglobin regions that associate with N-cadherin and alpha-catenin separately; it does not settle direct CDH2–alpha-catenin binding. The original alpha-catenin IPI is consequently UNDECIDED, without alleging an erroneous curator attribution.
