The best-supported core functions are choline and ethanolamine transmembrane transport by characterized full-length human FLVCR2. Human HEK293 uptake, separate choline release, transport-site mutants and human-protein yeast complementation support choline movement. CHKA/CHAT-dependent metabolic trapping affects measured accumulation; it does not make FLVCR2 a synthesis enzyme or turn the cellular experiment into purified-protein reconstitution. Human FLVCR2 choline-bound structural evidence supports substrate recognition separately from transport. [PMID:38302740](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38302740/), [PMID:38778100](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38778100/)

Ethanolamine uptake is condition dependent. PMID:38302740 reports kinase-assisted uptake after a negative unassisted condition, while PMID:38778100 independently measures FLVCR2-dependent uptake and finds no substantial enhancement by ETNK1. The ethanolamine-bound structure and specifically reported ethanolamine washout in the latter paper concern FLVCR1 and are not transferred to FLVCR2. The two proposed cores therefore pair GO:0015220 with GO:0015871 and GO:0034228 with GO:0034229, both at GO:0005886 plasma membrane. Separate kinases perform precursor phosphorylation.

The blood–brain barrier contexts retain their physiological transport interpretation with mouse in vivo limits. Endothelial knockout/tracer experiments include reduced brain uptake after a high intravenous choline dose and a separate clearance interpretation involving LPC-derived choline. Export into plasma versus accumulation in endothelium remains unresolved. Human protein transport supports the ortholog inference, but this is not a direct human in vivo flux measurement. PMID:38693257 contributes official abstract/figure-caption evidence only; full Methods/Results were not recovered. [PMID:38302740](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38302740/), [PMID:38693257](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38693257/)

Historical heme uptake and binding remain non-core. Human FLVCR2 expressed in CHO cells and Xenopus oocytes, together with endogenous human TE671 knockdown, provides contextual uptake evidence; these assays do not isolate purified transporter flux. Hemin-agarose binding from cell lysates and purified human N-terminal-domain binding are distinct experiments. New amine-transport findings alone do not refute these observations. [PMID:20823265](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20823265/), [PMID:32973183](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32973183/)

Only raw18, the heme-export IBA GO:0097037, is removed. The ancestral placement is genuinely on the human FLVCR2 path. This challenges functional inheritance across the paralog split under the tested conditions. It is not an argument from donor count, new substrate discovery, or the assumption that import universally excludes export. [PMID:15369674](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15369674/), [PMID:20823265](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20823265/)

Endogenous human THP-1 imaging supports contextual mitochondrial and ER-contact pools. These are retained outside the plasma-membrane core. ER-marker overlap at a contact does not resolve membrane insertion, and inner versus outer mitochondrial membrane remains unknown. Human imaging and the purified human 1–84 fragment are distinguished from mouse tissues and murine tagged/deletion constructs. The two official natural products are Q9UPI3-1 (526 residues) and Q9UPI3-2 (321 residues); the latter replaces native residues 1–18 and lacks 19–223. Neither fragment nor deletion experiments assign transport, heme binding or a location to that natural short product. [PMID:32973183](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32973183/)
