## Transport and substrate recognition

**PMID:38302740 — selected original Results/Methods available.** Human FLVCR2 was tested in HEK293 cells with empty-vector, CHT1 and S203Y comparisons, and human-protein complementation was tested in yeast lacking Hnm1. Dose/time dependence, sodium replacement and release from preloaded cells support facilitative choline transport. CHKA/CHAT co-expression increases metabolic trapping. Kinase-assisted ethanolamine uptake must be considered alongside the earlier negative unassisted condition. Surface-localization controls separate the S203Y uptake defect from simple loss of abundance or surface targeting. Mouse endothelial knockout, tracer and metabolite experiments are separate from these human-protein assays. They support a blood–brain barrier role while leaving physiological net direction and final plasma-versus-endothelial destination unresolved. Relevant existing rows: raw1/13/21 directly, with corroboration for other transport/orthology rows. [Original reference](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38302740/)

**PMID:38778100 — selected original Results/Methods available.** Human FLVCR2 choline-bound structures, ligand-dependent fluorescence and transport-site mutations support substrate recognition. HEK293 radiotracer uptake, dose/time curves and separate choline washout support cellular movement; membrane-targeted GFP colocalization supports plasma-membrane localization. FLVCR2 ethanolamine uptake is positive, but ETNK1 does not substantially enhance it in this study. The ethanolamine-bound structure and specifically reported ethanolamine washout concern FLVCR1. No human FLVCR2 proteoliposome flux experiment is claimed from these cellular data. Relevant direct rows: raw2/11/14/22; corroboration supports raw10 and the electronic activity/process assignments. [Original reference](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38778100/)

**PMID:38693257 — selected original Results/Methods now available through the genuine normal fetch.** Adult endothelial-knockout mice show reduced brain radiotracer uptake; human brain immunostaining and human iHBEC siRNA-associated uptake reduction are separate observations. Methods identify mouse FLVCR2 in the HEK293 overexpression, purified binding, proteoliposome and structural experiments. Protein-free liposomes control background uptake; inward proton gradients enhance transport, whereas sodium gradients do not in the tested mouse system. The authors leave proton coupling versus facilitative uniport unresolved; pH enhancement alone is not a coupling mechanism assignment. They report robust transport without a proton gradient. These data strengthen the existing barrier-role interpretation without transferring purified mouse kinetics or coupling to human FLVCR2. No figure pixels, supplement-table values or source data were independently reanalysed. Relevant corroborated rows remain raw10/19/20. [Original reference](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38693257/)

**PMID:40972734 — authentic metadata/abstract only.** Exact FLVCR2 experimental support remains UNVERIFIED. Raw3 and raw15 retain independently established choline activity/process terms using PMID:38302740 and PMID:38778100, while preserving the original citation. No kinetics or assay parameters of another SLC are assigned to FLVCR2, and limited abstract coverage is not treated as misattribution. [Original reference](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40972734/)

## Heme evidence and the export IBA

**PMID:20823265 — original Methods/Results read in the accepted consultation.** Human FLVCR2 expression increases ZnMP uptake in hamster CHO cells and radiolabeled-hemin accumulation in Xenopus oocytes; two siRNAs reduce analog uptake in human TE671 cells. HA-tagged human protein from CHO lysates binds hemin-agarose with soluble-hemin competition and a feline thiamine-transporter comparison. Uptake, perturbation and binding provide different kinds of evidence. These are not an isolated full-length-protein affinity or reconstituted-flux measurement. Raw4/5 heme activity and raw6/7 binding remain non-core. The paper also explicitly identifies the chromosome-14q construct in PMID:15369674 as human FLVCR2. [Original reference](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20823265/)

**PMID:15369674 — retained original publisher PDF and selected comparative result/caption read.** In rat NRK cells expressing the human constructs, radiolabeled-heme washout distinguished FLVCR1 from the chromosome-14q paralog. Together with the explicit identity link in PMID:20823265, the negative result for the latter supports removing the propagated FLVCR2 export assertion raw18. The review challenges the functional scope of the ancestral placement, not its mechanical descent or donor count. It does not claim that every export condition was tested, that import logically prevents export, or that choline release establishes heme release. The normal source is shared with FLVCR1 and was reused byte-for-byte from its accepted actual main merge after fresh ownership qualification. [Original reference](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15369674/)

## Organellar pools and product boundaries

**PMID:32973183 — original main body plus selected supplementary Figure 10/caption available.** Purified human FLVCR2 residues 1–84 bind heme by co-elution and optical titration, with histidine substitutions affecting the response. This supports raw8 without transferring murine full-length cellular experiments to the human fragment. Endogenous human THP-1 imaging, including knockout staining controls, supports a mitochondrial pool; selected supplementary imaging also supports a fraction at ER–mitochondrial contacts. Mouse-brain fractionation and murine tagged-protein studies are separate corroboration. Retain raw24/25 ER-membrane and raw28/29 mitochondrial-membrane contexts as non-core, with unresolved ER-bilayer insertion and inner-versus-outer mitochondrial assignment. Figure 6 alternatives are models, not localization measurements. The engineered deletion and human fragment are not natural product Q9UPI3-2. Only the selected supplementary figure/caption was inspected; no source-data analysis is claimed. [Original reference](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32973183/)

**PMID:14729055 — verified identity and abstract only.** Exact source-specific localization evidence for raw27 remains UNVERIFIED.
