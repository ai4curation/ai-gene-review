# A1BG focused report assessment, 2026-09-20

The complete OpenScientist report, its HTML/PDF renderings, hydropathy code/provenance JSON, plot and citation list were reviewed. The three standing decisions remain plasma membrane KEEP_AS_NON_CORE, transmembrane signaling receptor activity UNDECIDED, and growth-hormone receptor signaling pathway UNDECIDED. No source annotation or qualifier changed. The report is incorporated with disagreement, not pending another duplicate adjudication.

## What the report establishes

The canonical hydropathy result independently reproduces: mature-chain 19-residue maximum 0.9842105263; only residues 1–19 exceed the chosen 1.6 threshold. This supports absence of a conventional hydrophobic transmembrane helix, not exclusion of peripheral membrane association or all receptor-complex functions. A minimal reproducible check, exact input hashes, full output and unrelated-protein control are in A1BG-bioinformatics. The provider's code fetched the canonical sequence; the source isoform-2 record deletes residues 1–122, introducing no new sequence, but neither calculation measures isoform localization or function.

The report correctly distinguishes GH-induced expression from work done by the A1BG protein in signaling. That is a concrete source-scope concern, not proof that the complete experimentally curated mouse record is wrong. Its discussion of CRISP3 complex formation is compatible with binding evidence; it does not demonstrate the physiological outcome of that plasma complex.

## Specific corrections

1. The report's claim that structure closes the membrane-pool route is contradicted by the primary evidence it missed. PMID:40270023 Results and Fig. 4D describe A1BG/tropomyosin co-immunostaining at mouse atrial cardiomyocyte surfaces. Methods use fixed cryosections and confocal imaging. This supports ortholog surface association; it is not a purified membrane insertion experiment, a direct human IDA assay, or proof of ligand-dependent receptor activity. The cardiac interactome contains sex-dependent protein sets, but association and downstream cardiac phenotypes do not identify an A1BG receptor mechanism.
2. GO:0004888 defines ligand-dependent transmission of a signal across a membrane. The definition does not require each component of a receptor assembly to independently span the membrane. Lack of a canonical helix is a relevant divergence from intrinsic transmembrane receptors, but does not itself demonstrate loss of every enabling receptor-complex role. Surface localization and NAMPT association also do not establish receptor activity, leaving the inherited assertion unresolved.
3. The report acknowledges that it did not trace the PANTHER topology and treated family membership as provenance. The saved actual P04217 path descends from receptor/membrane IBD PTN002621170 and the A1BG-specific GH IBD PTN000200788 (project artifact a1bg-paint-and-term-checks.json). The GH source is mouse A1bg, not an unrelated LILR/KIR donor. IBA-only status, absence of another experimental row and the family label do not refute an ancestral assertion.
4. The report says it read the complete PMID:16723264 paper, but its cited passages and assay description are also in the public abstract; no full-text copy, URL, figure or additional assay provenance was supplied. Independent DOI and primary-source searches still provided only the abstract. This does not prove the provider lacked access, but its claim of access cannot be independently verified and does not close the source-review gap. Retain UNDECIDED pending primary-full-text or curator review. No new expression-response annotation is manufactured.
5. PMID:40560034 Fig. 5 uses A1BG-overexpressing osteosarcoma cell lysates for co-IP/MS, cellular colocalization, and cycloheximide/lysosome-inhibitor experiments for NAMPT stability. These findings support cell-associated NAMPT regulation, not a specifically secreted NAMPT complex or purified binary interaction. Recombinant extracellular A1BG and adipocyte-conditioned-medium effects elsewhere in the paper do not determine the compartment of that complex.
6. PMID:39433128 was independently rechecked because it supplies the existing NEW MF. Purified A1BG binds CRISP2 and prevents cholesterol-sulfate binding while preserving palmitic-acid binding; reconstructed yeast sterol export falls by more than half. The paper also includes CRISP3 in yeast export experiments, but this is not proof of inhibition of CRISP3 in normal human plasma. The selective protein-sequestering capacity remains supported independently of the disputed receptor inference, without claiming sterol binding by A1BG itself or a sterol-process annotation.

The earlier full-gene review's remaining source actions, standalone description and qualified core remain consistent with these findings. The report's categorical REMOVE/NOT leads are not adopted. Outstanding mechanistic questions are carried in suggested_questions rather than counted as pending provider jobs.
