CC   -!- FUNCTION: Component of the mechanistic target of rapamycin complex 1
CC       (mTORC1), an evolutionarily conserved central nutrient sensor that
CC       stimulates anabolic reactions and macromolecule biosynthesis to promote
CC       cellular biomass generation and growth (PubMed:12150925,
CC       PubMed:12150926, PubMed:12747827, PubMed:24403073, PubMed:26588989,
CC       PubMed:32561715, PubMed:37541260). In response to nutrients, growth
CC       factors or amino acids, mTORC1 is recruited to the lysosome membrane
CC       and promotes protein, lipid and nucleotide synthesis by phosphorylating
CC       several substrates, such as ribosomal protein S6 kinase (RPS6KB1 and
CC       RPS6KB2) and EIF4EBP1 (4E-BP1) (PubMed:12150925, PubMed:12150926,
CC       PubMed:12747827, PubMed:24403073, PubMed:26588989, PubMed:37541260). In
CC       the same time, it inhibits catabolic pathways by phosphorylating the
CC       autophagy initiation components ULK1 and ATG13, as well as
CC       transcription factor TFEB, a master regulators of lysosomal biogenesis
CC       and autophagy (PubMed:12150925, PubMed:12150926, PubMed:12747827,
CC       PubMed:24403073, PubMed:32561715, PubMed:37541260). The mTORC1 complex
CC       is inhibited in response to starvation and amino acid depletion
CC       (PubMed:12150925, PubMed:12150926, PubMed:12747827, PubMed:24403073,
CC       PubMed:37541260). Within the mTORC1 complex, RPTOR acts both as a
CC       molecular adapter, which (1) mediates recruitment of mTORC1 to
CC       lysosomal membranes via interaction with small GTPases Rag (RagA/RRAGA,
CC       RagB/RRAGB, RagC/RRAGC and/or RagD/RRAGD), and a (2) substrate-specific
CC       adapter, which promotes substrate specificity by binding to TOS motif-
CC       containing proteins and direct them towards the active site of the MTOR
CC       kinase domain for phosphorylation (PubMed:12747827, PubMed:24403073,
CC       PubMed:26588989, PubMed:37541260). mTORC1 complex regulates many
CC       cellular processes, such as odontoblast and osteoclast differentiation
CC       or neuronal transmission (By similarity). mTORC1 complex in excitatory
CC       neuronal transmission is required for the prosocial behavior induced by
