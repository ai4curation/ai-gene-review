# Human MAP2K4 (MKK4; UniProt P45985): functional-annotation research report

## Executive conclusion

The requested identity is unambiguous: **human MAP2K4 encodes dual-specificity mitogen-activated protein kinase kinase 4 (MKK4)**, also called **MEK4, JNKK1, SEK1, SAPKK1, and JNK-activating kinase 1**. UniProt **P45985** is a 399-amino-acid intracellular MAPK kinase belonging to the STE/MAP2K branch of the protein-kinase superfamily. This identity, organism, length, architecture, and substrate profile agree with the supplied UniProt record; the literature does not indicate that the requested symbol refers here to another organism or protein. MAP2K4 must not be confused with **MAP2K7/MKK7**, its cooperating JNK kinase, or **MAP4K4**, an upstream MAP4K-family enzyme. (katzengruber2023mkk4inhibitors—recentdevelopment pages 1-2, gehi2022intrinsicdisorderin pages 15-17, gehi2022intrinsicdisorderin pages 17-18)

Its primary biochemical function is to use ATP to phosphorylate activation-loop threonine and tyrosine residues in stress-activated MAPKs. Its best-supported direct physiological substrates are **JNK1–3** and members of the **p38 MAPK family**. For JNK, MKK4 preferentially phosphorylates the tyrosine—Tyr185 in the canonical JNK1 numbering—whereas MKK7 preferentially phosphorylates Thr183. MKK4 can also phosphorylate the p38 Thr180-Gly-Tyr182 motif. Thus, MKK4 is a stress-responsive signaling relay positioned between several MAP3Ks and the JNK/p38 effector systems, rather than a constitutively active metabolic enzyme. (ho2003adockingsite pages 1-1, katzengruber2023mkk4inhibitors—recentdevelopment pages 2-4, gehi2022intrinsicdisorderin pages 15-17)

The most important recent translational development is **HRX215**, an orally active, selective MKK4 inhibitor. A 2024 *Cell* study showed enhanced liver regeneration in mice and pigs, survival after otherwise lethal porcine hepatectomy, and favorable safety and pharmacokinetics in 48 healthy volunteers. These results constitute strong preclinical and phase-I safety evidence, but not yet proof of therapeutic efficacy in patients with liver failure. (zwirner2024firstinclassmkk4inhibitors pages 11-13, zwirner2024firstinclassmkk4inhibitors pages 10-11, zwirner2024firstinclassmkk4inhibitors pages 3-5, zwirner2024firstinclassmkk4inhibitors pages 1-3)

### Verification against the supplied record

* **Gene/protein:** MAP2K4/MKK4, matching “dual-specificity mitogen-activated protein kinase kinase 4.”
* **Accession and organism:** UniProt **P45985**, *Homo sapiens*.
* **Protein length:** 399 amino acids.
* **Aliases supported in the literature:** MKK4, MEK4, JNKK1, and SEK1; the additional supplied names are historical synonyms for the same stress-activated MAP2K.
* **Family:** MAPK kinase/MAP2K family within the STE protein-kinase group.
* **Domain concordance:** the literature identifies a canonical protein-kinase domain and ATP-binding catalytic fold, consistent with InterPro IPR011009, IPR000719, IPR017441, IPR008271 and Pfam PF00069 in the supplied annotation. (katzengruber2023mkk4inhibitors—recentdevelopment pages 1-2, gehi2022intrinsicdisorderin pages 15-17, gehi2022intrinsicdisorderin pages 17-18)

MAP2K4 is distinct from **MAP2K7**, although both activate JNK. MKK7 is comparatively JNK selective and favors phosphorylation of JNK Thr183, while MKK4 favors Tyr185 and additionally activates p38. MAP4K4 is a different kinase tier and family; evidence retrieved for MAP2K4 was not conflated with MAP4K4 literature. (ho2003adockingsite pages 1-1, katzengruber2023mkk4inhibitors—recentdevelopment pages 2-4, gehi2022intrinsicdisorderin pages 15-17)

## 2. Primary enzymatic function

### Catalyzed reaction

The reaction can be summarized as:

**ATP + MAPK protein–OH → ADP + MAPK protein–O-phosphate**

MKK4 is termed “dual specificity” because its kinase domain can phosphorylate both **threonine and tyrosine** residues in MAPK activation loops. This does not mean that it broadly phosphorylates arbitrary Ser/Thr and Tyr proteins; recognition is strongly constrained by MAPK docking interactions and activation-loop sequence. (ho2003adockingsite pages 1-1, katzengruber2023mkk4inhibitors—recentdevelopment pages 2-4)

### Direct substrates and residue preference

1. **JNK1, JNK2 and JNK3:** MKK4 binds all three through an N-terminal MAPK-docking site. In the JNK Thr-Pro-Tyr activation motif, MKK4 preferentially supplies the tyrosine phosphorylation, whereas MKK7 preferentially supplies the threonine phosphorylation. Dual phosphorylation produces full JNK activation in many cellular settings. (ho2003adockingsite pages 1-1, gehi2022intrinsicdisorderin pages 15-17, ho2003adockingsite pages 1-2)
2. **p38 MAPKs:** MKK4 binds at least p38α and p38β and can phosphorylate the p38 Thr180-Gly-Tyr182 activation motif. This p38 activity is a major functional distinction from MKK7. (ho2003adockingsite pages 1-1, katzengruber2023mkk4inhibitors—recentdevelopment pages 2-4)

The evidence includes direct binding, peptide competition and in-vitro kinase experiments, not merely pathway co-occurrence. Mutation of the MKK4 docking site reduces JNK/p38 binding, while docking peptides inhibit MKK4-dependent phosphorylation of JNK1. (ho2003adockingsite pages 1-1, ho2003adockingsite pages 3-4)

## 3. Activation and structural basis of specificity

MKK4 has the canonical bilobed protein-kinase fold: a smaller N-terminal lobe, larger predominantly α-helical C-terminal lobe, intervening hinge, ATP-binding cleft, glycine-rich loop, HRD catalytic loop, DFG magnesium-binding segment and flexible activation loop. Public structures include PDB **3ALN, 3ALO and 3VUT**; 3ALN resolves residues 80–399 at 2.30 Å. (katzengruber2023mkk4inhibitors—recentdevelopment pages 1-2, katzengruber2023mkk4inhibitors—recentdevelopment pages 2-4, gehi2022intrinsicdisorderin pages 17-18)

Three regions organize signal transfer:

* an N-terminal MAPK-docking **D-domain**, approximately residues 37–57, with a core docking motif around residues 40–46;
* the catalytic protein-kinase domain, approximately residues 102–367;
* a C-terminal **DVD domain**, approximately residues 364–387, used for MAP3K interactions. (gehi2022intrinsicdisorderin pages 17-18, ho2003adockingsite pages 3-4, ho2003adockingsite pages 1-2)

Upstream kinases phosphorylate the MKK4 activation-loop **Ser257-X-Ala-Lys-Thr261** motif. Ser257 is essential for activation, while Thr261 is needed for full activity. Relevant upstream kinase groups include the **ASK, MEKK, MLK and TAK** families. This modular architecture—MAP3K recruitment at the C terminus, catalysis in the central domain and substrate recruitment at the N terminus—helps preserve signaling specificity despite the conserved ATP pockets of MAPK-family enzymes. (katzengruber2023mkk4inhibitors—recentdevelopment pages 1-2, katzengruber2023mkk4inhibitors—recentdevelopment pages 2-4)

## 4. Pathway position and biological processes

A concise pathway model is:

**cellular stress/cytokines → MAP3K → MKK4 → JNK and/or p38 → transcriptional and cytoplasmic effectors**.

Reported activating contexts include oxidative and osmotic stress, heat shock, UV/genotoxic stress, inflammatory cytokines and some growth-factor signals. Activated JNK subsequently regulates substrates including c-Jun/AP-1 components, ATF2 and other nuclear or cytoplasmic proteins; p38 supplies an overlapping but distinct stress-response output. Signal amplitude, duration, scaffold usage, cell lineage and cooperation with MKK7 determine whether the outcome is adaptation, proliferation, differentiation, arrest or apoptosis. (katzengruber2023mkk4inhibitors—recentdevelopment pages 2-4, zhao2026advancesinsmallmolecule pages 3-5)

MKK4 consequently participates in:

* **stress adaptation and apoptosis**, through JNK/p38 signaling;
* **cell proliferation and differentiation**, including context-specific growth restraint or promotion;
* **embryonic liver and nervous-system development**, neuronal positioning, neurite/axon responses and retinal biology;
* **inflammatory signaling**, including reported communication with NF-κB pathways;
* **liver regeneration**, where MKK4 acts as a stress-dependent brake on hepatocyte proliferation. (katzengruber2023mkk4inhibitors—recentdevelopment pages 2-4, katzengruber2023mkk4inhibitors—recentdevelopment pages 1-2)

Some broad biological annotations remain context dependent. For example, older work assigned MKK4 major T-cell survival/proliferation functions, whereas newer loss-of-function studies found little effect on some T-cell proliferative and antiviral responses. It is therefore safer to annotate MKK4 as a stress-activated JNK/p38 relay than to assign it an invariant immune-cell phenotype. (katzengruber2023mkk4inhibitors—recentdevelopment pages 2-4)

## 5. Subcellular localization

MKK4 is a soluble intracellular kinase rather than a secreted, transmembrane or organelle-resident protein. Cellular studies report both **cytoplasmic and nuclear** MKK4, but the available evidence does not justify assigning one universal compartment. Its working localization is best annotated as **cytoplasmic and nuclear, dynamic and cell-context dependent**. Activated downstream JNK and p38 can translocate to the nucleus, so nuclear pathway output does not necessarily imply that all MKK4 catalysis occurs there. (katzengruber2023mkk4inhibitors—recentdevelopment pages 2-4, ahn2011map2k4functionsas pages 1-2)

Accordingly, the most defensible functional-localization statement is: MKK4 carries out substrate phosphorylation within intracellular stress-signaling complexes, with substantial cytoplasmic signaling and reported nuclear presence, rather than at the plasma membrane or outside the cell.

## 6. Disease relevance and expert interpretation

### Cancer: genuine context dependence

MAP2K4 should not be annotated simply as either an oncogene or a tumor-suppressor gene. A 2023 expert review concluded that its cancer role is tissue- and state-dependent. MAP2K4 loss has been reported in approximately **7–10% of epithelial cancers**, and loss-of-function mutations were identified in 11 of 356 tumors in an early sequencing series. Conversely, elevated MKK4 signaling has been associated with aggressive or metastatic prostate, ovarian and some breast-cancer contexts. (katzengruber2023mkk4inhibitors—recentdevelopment pages 2-4, katzengruber2023mkk4inhibitors—recentdevelopment pages 1-2)

Direct lung-adenocarcinoma experiments support tumor suppression: conditional Map2k4 loss increased multiplicity and accelerated growth of KRAS-driven mouse lung tumors, while MKK4 suppressed invasion in KRAS/TP53-mutant cells. Eight of 11 tested cancer-associated variants reduced protein stability or kinase activity. One proposed mechanism involved restraint of PPARγ2 expression. (ahn2011map2k4functionsas pages 1-2, ahn2011map2k4functionsas pages 4-5, ahn2011map2k4functionsas pages 2-4)

These apparently opposing findings are biologically plausible because MKK4 feeds two pleiotropic systems, JNK and p38. Different MAP3Ks, scaffolds, MKK7 availability, downstream isoforms and oncogenic backgrounds can redirect the same kinase toward apoptosis, growth restraint, survival or motility. Open Targets likewise associates MAP2K4 with breast, colorectal and pancreatic adenocarcinoma and neurodegenerative disease, but such aggregate associations establish relevance rather than causal direction. (OpenTargets Search: -MAP2K4)

### Practical implication

MAP2K4 genotype or abundance alone is unlikely to be a universal therapeutic biomarker. Functional pathway state—including MKK7/JNK and p38 activity, tissue type, stress context and tumor genotype—should be measured before inferring whether inhibition or restoration would be beneficial.

## 7. Recent developments: MKK4 inhibition and liver regeneration

The major 2023–2024 advance was movement from tool inhibitors toward a selective clinical candidate. The 2023 inhibitor review identified MKK4 as a promising but challenging target because its ATP pocket resembles MKK7 and because chronic pathway inhibition could have opposing effects across tissues and cancers. (katzengruber2023mkk4inhibitors—recentdevelopment pages 1-2, katzengruber2023mkk4inhibitors—recentdevelopment pages 2-4)

In March 2024, Zwirner and colleagues reported **HRX215** in *Cell*. The compound had two-digit-nanomolar MKK4 potency and greater than 100-fold selectivity over JNK1, BRAF and MKK7. NMR-guided studies supported direct kinase-pocket binding, and human PBMC assays demonstrated cellular target engagement. (zwirner2024firstinclassmkk4inhibitors pages 7-8)

Mechanistically, MKK4 inhibition in injured hepatocytes did not simply switch off all stress signaling. It redirected signaling toward **MKK7–JNK1–ATF2/ELK1**, producing a pro-survival and pro-regenerative transcriptional state. Little proliferation was induced in uninjured liver, suggesting dependence on an existing regenerative stress signal. In carcinoma models, by contrast, HRX215 reduced rather than stimulated proliferation, illustrating the pathway’s context dependence. (zwirner2024firstinclassmkk4inhibitors pages 10-11, zwirner2024firstinclassmkk4inhibitors pages 3-5, tchorz2024prometheus2.0druginduced pages 1-2)

### Quantitative preclinical results

* After mouse partial hepatectomy, proliferating hepatocytes increased from **4.93% with vehicle** to **15.38%, 15.15% and 26.3%** at 0.4, 2 and 10 mg/kg HRX215, respectively. Benefit was also observed in fibrotic and toxic-injury models. (zwirner2024firstinclassmkk4inhibitors pages 7-8)
* After 80% hepatectomy in pigs, regenerated liver volume at 43 hours was **251.8 ± 13.6 mL** with HRX215 versus **160.0 ± 23.5 mL** with placebo; regeneration indices were **2.57 ± 0.11 versus 2.12 ± 0.10**. (zwirner2024firstinclassmkk4inhibitors pages 10-11)
* In an otherwise lethal 85% porcine hepatectomy model, **4/6 preoperatively treated and 5/6 postoperatively treated animals survived**; five of six placebo animals required euthanasia. HRX215 also attenuated hyperammonemia, intracranial-pressure elevation and abnormalities in bilirubin, INR and AST. (zwirner2024firstinclassmkk4inhibitors pages 11-13, zwirner2024firstinclassmkk4inhibitors pages 10-11)
* Long-term genetic suppression in mice did not produce reported organ histopathology or increased spontaneous tumorigenesis, and HRX215 did not increase tumor frequency in a stringent NASH–HCC model. These observations reduce—but do not eliminate—oncogenic-safety concerns. (zwirner2024firstinclassmkk4inhibitors pages 32-38, zwirner2024firstinclassmkk4inhibitors pages 3-5)

### First-in-human findings and present implementation status

The placebo-controlled phase-I study enrolled **48 healthy men across six treatment groups**. There were no severe or serious adverse events; mild-to-moderate adverse events occurred in **36.1% of HRX215 recipients versus 41.7% with placebo**. Exposure increased dose proportionally. Food delayed median time to maximum concentration from 1.2 to 3.9 hours while increasing Cmax 1.76-fold and AUC 2.09-fold. These findings support tolerability and oral development but do not demonstrate regeneration or survival benefit in patients. (zwirner2024firstinclassmkk4inhibitors pages 11-13, zwirner2024firstinclassmkk4inhibitors pages 1-3)

The intended real-world applications are prevention or treatment of post-hepatectomy liver failure, enabling more extensive oncological resections, and improving regeneration of small-for-size liver grafts. Registered development includes a recruiting phase-I/II study after minor or major liver resection (**NCT06638502**, planned enrollment 80) and a planned phase-II study after portal-vein embolization for colorectal liver metastasis (**NCT07612007**, planned enrollment 80). Trial status and enrollment targets should be checked in the registry before clinical use; HRX215 remains investigational.

## 9. Confidence assessment and knowledge gaps

**High-confidence annotations** are the identity of P45985 as human MKK4, its MAP2K/STE kinase architecture, activation at Ser257/Thr261, direct JNK/p38 phosphorylation, JNK Tyr185 preference, N-terminal MAPK docking and its position between stress-responsive MAP3Ks and JNK/p38. These conclusions are supported by structural, biochemical and mutational evidence. (ho2003adockingsite pages 1-1, katzengruber2023mkk4inhibitors—recentdevelopment pages 1-2, katzengruber2023mkk4inhibitors—recentdevelopment pages 2-4, gehi2022intrinsicdisorderin pages 17-18)

**Moderate-confidence, context-dependent annotations** include exact compartmental dominance, immune-cell phenotypes and whether MAP2K4 is tumor suppressive or tumor promoting in a particular cancer. These require cell-type- and stimulus-resolved interpretation. (katzengruber2023mkk4inhibitors—recentdevelopment pages 2-4, ahn2011map2k4functionsas pages 1-2, katzengruber2023mkk4inhibitors—recentdevelopment pages 1-2)

**Promising but not clinically established** is MKK4 inhibition for liver regeneration. The 2024 mouse, pig and phase-I results are unusually strong translational evidence, but controlled patient efficacy, perioperative safety, infection/inflammation effects and long-term oncological safety remain to be established. (zwirner2024firstinclassmkk4inhibitors pages 11-13, zwirner2024firstinclassmkk4inhibitors pages 10-11, zwirner2024firstinclassmkk4inhibitors pages 3-5, tchorz2024prometheus2.0druginduced pages 1-2)
