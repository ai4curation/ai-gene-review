|---|---|---|---|
| Identity | **MAP2K4** encodes human **MKK4** (MEK4/JNKK1/SEK1), a **399-aa dual-specificity MAP kinase kinase**; UniProt **P45985**. It is distinct from MKK7/MAP2K7 and MAP4K4. | Protein annotation and biochemical literature consistently identify P45985 as the 399-aa human MAP2K that activates stress-responsive MAPKs. | Gehi et al., 2022, https://doi.org/10.1007/s00018-022-04230-4; Katzengruber et al., 2023, https://doi.org/10.3390/ijms24087495 (pqac-00000002, pqac-00000004) |
| Family and domains | MKK4 has a canonical bilobed protein-kinase fold, an **N-terminal MAPK-docking D-domain (residues 37–57)**, a **protein-kinase domain (102–367)**, and a **C-terminal MAP3K-binding DVD domain (364–387)**. | The N-terminal docking site is necessary and sufficient for high-affinity binding to JNK1–3 and p38α/β; PDB **3ALN** resolves residues 80–399 at 2.30 Å. | Ho et al., 2003, https://doi.org/10.1074/jbc.M304229200; Gehi et al., 2022 (pqac-00000001, pqac-00000005, pqac-00000006) |
| Activation mechanism | Upstream MAP3Ks activate MKK4 by phosphorylating its **Ser257-X-Ala-Lys-Thr261** activation-loop motif. **Ser257 is essential**, while Thr261 is required for full activity. | Mutational, structural, and kinase studies support phosphorylation-dependent activation of the flexible kinase activation loop. | Katzengruber et al., 2023, https://doi.org/10.3390/ijms24087495 (pqac-00000002, pqac-00000003) |
| Catalytic function | MKK4 transfers phosphate from ATP to both threonine and tyrosine residues in MAPK activation loops; its direct physiological substrates are principally **JNK1–3 and p38 MAPKs**. | In-vitro kinase and docking experiments demonstrate MKK4-dependent phosphorylation of JNK1 and p38, consistent with EC 2.7.12.2 dual-specificity activity. | Ho et al., 2003; Krishna et al., 2013; Katzengruber et al., 2023 (pqac-00000001, pqac-00000003) |
| JNK specificity | MKK4 preferentially phosphorylates **Tyr185** in the JNK **Thr183-Pro-Tyr185** motif; MKK7 preferentially phosphorylates Thr183. Efficient cellular JNK activation commonly integrates both inputs. | Direct docking and phosphorylation studies show MKK4 binding to JNK1–3 and a relative preference for the tyrosine site, distinguishing it from MKK7. | Ho et al., 2003; Gehi et al., 2022; Itah et al., 2023, https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2218373120 (pqac-00000001, pqac-00000004) |
| p38 specificity | Unlike JNK-selective MKK7, MKK4 can phosphorylate the p38 **Thr180-Gly-Tyr182** activation motif; substrate docking is documented for p38α and p38β. | The MKK4 D-site binds p38α/β, and biochemical kinase assays use p38 as a direct MKK4 substrate. | Ho et al., 2003; Katzengruber et al., 2023 (pqac-00000001, pqac-00000003) |
| Upstream inputs and pathway position | MKK4 is a second-tier MAP2K activated by MAP3Ks in the **ASK, MEKK, MLK, and TAK** families during oxidative, osmotic, genotoxic, heat, cytokine, and related stress signaling. MAP3Ks dock through the C-terminal DVD region. | Biochemical and pathway studies place MKK4 between stress-responsive MAP3Ks and JNK/p38; its N-terminal D-site recruits downstream MAPKs. | Katzengruber et al., 2023 (pqac-00000003, pqac-00000017) |
| Cellular location | MKK4 is an **intracellular, non-membrane kinase** reported in both cytoplasmic and nuclear fractions; however, strong MKK4-specific evidence defining a fixed compartment is limited. Localization should therefore be treated as **dynamic and context-dependent**, with signaling output also propagated by activated JNK/p38 translocation. | Available experimental literature supports nuclear and cytoplasmic staining in cellular models, but recent authoritative reviews do not establish a single dominant compartment or a universal stimulus-dependent trafficking mechanism for MKK4 itself. | Ahn et al., 2011, https://doi.org/10.1128/MCB.05562-11; Katzengruber et al., 2023 (pqac-00000017, pqac-00000018) |
| Cancer duality | MKK4 is **not uniformly oncogenic or tumor suppressive**. MAP2K4 loss can promote lung and other epithelial cancers, whereas elevated MKK4 signaling can support invasion/metastasis in prostate, ovarian, and some breast-cancer contexts. | Conditional Map2k4 loss accelerated KRAS-driven lung tumors; 8 of 11 tested cancer-associated variants impaired stability or activity. Reviews report MAP2K4 loss in **7–10% of epithelial cancers**, but also summarize prometastatic settings. | Ahn et al., 2011; Katzengruber et al., 2023 (pqac-00000017, pqac-00000018, pqac-00000020) |
| HRX215 potency and mechanism | **HRX215** is an orally active, first-in-class MKK4 inhibitor with two-digit-nanomolar potency and **>100-fold selectivity** over JNK1, BRAF, and MKK7. In injured hepatocytes, MKK4 inhibition redirects signaling toward a pro-regenerative MKK7–JNK1–ATF2/ELK1 program. | NMR-guided binding studies, kinase selectivity panels, human-PBMC target-engagement assays, and genetic MKK4 suppression support on-target activity and pathway rewiring. | Zwirner et al., 2024, https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2024.02.023 (pqac-00000013, pqac-00000014) |
| Mouse liver regeneration | After partial hepatectomy, hepatocyte proliferation increased from **4.93% with vehicle to 15.38%, 15.15%, and 26.3%** at 0.4, 2, and 10 mg/kg HRX215, respectively; benefit was also observed in fibrotic and toxic-injury models. | Dose-response experiments measured proliferating hepatocytes and showed protection from CCl4-associated hepatocyte death. | Zwirner et al., 2024 (pqac-00000014) |
| Pig liver regeneration and survival | After **80% hepatectomy**, regenerated volume at 43 h was **251.8 ± 13.6 mL** with HRX215 versus **160.0 ± 23.5 mL** with placebo. After otherwise lethal **85% hepatectomy**, **4/6 pretreated and 5/6 post-treated pigs survived**, whereas no placebo animal completed the experiment without euthanasia. | Large-animal studies documented greater regeneration and attenuation of hyperammonemia, intracranial-pressure elevation, bilirubin/INR abnormalities, and liver injury. | Zwirner et al., 2024 (pqac-00000009, pqac-00000010) |
| Phase I safety and pharmacokinetics | A placebo-controlled first-in-human study enrolled **48 healthy men**. No severe or serious adverse events occurred; mild-to-moderate adverse-event rates were **36.1% with HRX215 versus 41.7% with placebo**. Exposure was dose proportional; food increased Cmax **1.76-fold** and AUC **2.09-fold**. | Six treatment groups were assessed for safety and pharmacokinetics. HRX215 was rapidly absorbed and showed biphasic elimination without significant blood-count differences. These are early safety data, not proof of clinical efficacy. | Zwirner et al., 2024; EudraCT 2021-000193-28, https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2024.02.023 (pqac-00000009, pqac-00000016) |
