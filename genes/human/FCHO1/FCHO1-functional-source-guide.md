# FCHO1 functional evidence

The [functional decision table](FCHO1-functional-decisions.yaml) preserves each complete imported source object beside its review. Positive plasma-membrane, clathrin-coated-pit, coat-assembly and endocytosis decisions rely on convergent human FCHO1 evidence. They do not require FCHO1 to be indispensable for every cargo or cell type. T-cell signaling and activation are retained as contextual, non-core roles.

## The 2010 report and editorial notices

[PMID:20448150](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20448150/) has a genuine [author-hosted original and supplement](https://www2.mrc-lmb.cam.ac.uk/groups/hmm/publica/Papers/HenneScience2010.pdf). The supplement identifies human FCHO1 and mouse FCHo2 constructs. FCHO1 appears explicitly in Figure S1D localization, S2B coat dynamics, S3 knockdown comparisons and S5 expression comparisons; many main-paper experiments instead test FCHo2. The selected supplement Methods and captions were read, and actual PDF pages 15 and 20 (Figures S1 and S6) were viewed. This was a bounded read, not a claim that all supplemental movies or every panel were inspected.

The original reports FCHo1/2 depletion from coated-vesicle fractions. Figure S6E shows a combined FCHo1/2 signal, while the antibody Methods identify anti-FCHo2 Ra103. The FCHO1-specific scope of this negative result is unresolved here. Its imported **NOT** flag is preserved and its review is UNDECIDED; positive pit localization is not evidence of universal absence from detached vesicles. This is not a finding of curator misattribution.

PubMed explicitly links the [2025 Expression of Concern](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39847650/) and [subsequent Erratum](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41232007/) to this report. Genuine notice metadata were recovered, but the bodies were inaccessible. The affected panels, correction scope and resolution are unknown; neither retraction nor resolution is inferred. The report is therefore marked DISPUTED. The notice caches’ automatic PRIMARY_RESEARCH classification does not make them experimental evidence. Independent 2012 and 2020 target evidence supports the positive annotations while these source-specific limits remain visible.

## Independent human FCHO1 evidence

[PMID:22484487](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3354769/) provides human FCHO1 surface-coat localization and maps AP-2 binding to its central linker. The Results sections “The linker region” and “The μHD,” Figure 1 and construct Methods distinguish human FCHO1 from mouse/rat AP-2 appendage reagents. The α-appendage platform dependence supports AP-2-complex binding. It does not turn the same experiment into a direct clathrin-binding or AP-2-activation assay. The Discussion and Figure 8 account of coat persistence after FCHO1/2 depletion limit universal obligatory-nucleator language. Zebrafish developmental findings are not presented as direct human developmental experiments.

[PMID:32098969](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7042371/) supplies human wild-type/variant localization and coat-dynamics evidence (Figures 2 and 3), receptor-internalization and signaling results (Figure 4), and patient T-cell response observations (Figure 6). These support coat organization and a positive T-cell context. Direct FCHO1–CD3 binding was not established, and the detailed relationship between trafficking and signal strength remains unresolved. Preserved transferrin uptake in patient fibroblasts is an explicit counterpoint to universal dependence. The [2020 Author Correction](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-020-15946-x) corrects the Discussion’s account of prior work: Calzoni and colleagues had functional evidence, and their attributed defect concerned transferrin-receptor endocytosis in activated T-cell blasts, not all endocytosis. The correction contrasts that context with the study’s own preserved transferrin uptake and subtler effects. It states no experimental figure or data correction. The unmodified cache retains the old wording; that wording is not adopted here.

The 2012/2020 assessments also incorporate the database lane’s independently bounded main-text readings once. Neither lane claims a complete review of their supplemental images or movies. Paper clone names, “isoform b” wording and disease variants are not mapped to a unique natural splice-product function. All three imported alternative-product objects remain unchanged.

## Scope of the synthesis

The resolved molecular anchor is AP-2 adaptor-complex binding, within a membrane-associated coat-organizing role involving endocytic partners. Coat assembly and clathrin-dependent endocytosis are supported contributions. The contextual T-cell findings add physiological scope without implying a new receptor-proximal catalytic activity. This account adds no new annotation, molecular-function identifier, product or supporting quotation.
