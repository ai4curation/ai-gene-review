# DGKE review notes

## 2026-10-17: catalytic activity, products and source scope

DGKE performs ATP-dependent phosphorylation of DAG to PA. Human recombinant experiments support this chemistry directly; mouse knockout lipidomics supply additional pathway context. One catalytic core combines ATP-dependent DAG kinase activity with PA production and upstream participation in PI recycling. The enzyme is not a PI synthase. DAG metabolism and lipid phosphorylation describe the same chemistry, rather than additional catalytic units. The current typed ontology does not make lipid phosphorylation an ancestor of PA biosynthesis ([PMID:18004883](https://doi.org/10.1021/bi701584v), [PMID:19744926](https://doi.org/10.1074/jbc.M109.050617), [PMID:21477596](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3095698/), [PMID:22108654](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3237191/)).

The four-source independent chemistry assessment follows. The two abstract-only normal caches retain that flag even though original PDFs were recovered separately. Original-only quotations remain in these notes; YAML anchors use literal text in the normal caches.

[PMID:8626589](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8626589/) — Tang et al., *J Biol Chem* 271, 10237–10241 (1996). The [original PDF](https://pdfs.semanticscholar.org/bd1e/0234222901a14e918f7737746a4ab1a4e057.pdf) identifies an open reading frame of “567 residues” (p. 10237). Human endothelial-cell cDNA was expressed in COS-7 cells, and vector-controlled lysate assays measured radiolabeled PA production with a preference for arachidonoyl-containing DAG (Methods p. 10238; Results and Figure 3 caption pp. 10239–10240). The cDNA truncation removed untranslated regions, not the protein's catalytic domain. The reported supernatant activity is qualified by poor total activity recovery and does not establish exclusively soluble localization.

[PMID:19744926](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19744926/) — Lung et al., [doi:10.1074/jbc.M109.050617](https://doi.org/10.1074/jbc.M109.050617). The [original PDF](https://pdfs.semanticscholar.org/ad7d/caa5c2d9be340b42f11efdc7feced6beadd4.pdf) describes human full-length/engineered Δ58 proteins in COS-7 cells and His6-DGKE in Sf21 cells. Enriched detergent lysates, mock controls and ATP-to-PA assays support human catalytic activity and acyl-chain selectivity (Methods pp. 31064–31065; Results/Figures 4 and 7, pp. 31067–31071). Separate knockout mouse-fibroblast lipidomics found 29% less stearoyl-containing PI; the method “does not determine their position on the glycerol backbone” (p. 31065). This supports PI-cycle participation without proving direct human PI synthesis, positional lipid flux or a physiological feedback circuit. Δ58 is not the natural short isoform.

[PMID:21477596](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21477596/) — Shulga et al., [doi:10.1016/j.jmb.2011.03.071](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jmb.2011.03.071), [PMC3095698](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3095698/). The Materials and Methods subsection on DGKε constructs states: “All correspond to human forms of the respective enzymes.” FLAG-DGKE wild type and mutants were expressed in COS-7 cells; detergent-lysate assays measured ATP-dependent PA formation with mock subtraction, lipid dependence and a DGKalpha comparator (Materials and Methods, Enzyme Preparation and Enzymatic Activity). Mutations near the proposed selectivity motif alter activity and acyl-chain preference. This is functional mutagenesis, not a solved substrate-binding structure. PIP5Kalpha experiments in the paper do not establish that activity for DGKE.

[PMID:22108654](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22108654/) — Shulga et al., [doi:10.1016/j.febslet.2011.11.016](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.febslet.2011.11.016), [PMC3237191](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3237191/). Human His6-DGKE expressed in Sf21 cells was assayed in detergent extracts and mixed micelles (Methods §§2.1, 2.2 and 2.4). Results and Discussion, including the Figure 1/Table 1 discussion, show that arachidonoyl chains at both positions can support activity similar to stearoyl/arachidonoyl DAG: “18:0/20:4-DAG is not unique in being a good substrate for DGKε”. Selectivity is stricter at sn-2 than at sn-1; linoleoyl at sn-2 is a poorer substrate. The membrane-penetration explanation is a hypothesis. None of these studies tests the natural 177-residue product.

## Other original evidence and localization

The separately recovered [PMID:18004883 original](https://nrc-publications.canada.ca/eng/view/ft/?id=1aac8f58-0d85-486d-857c-db0baa900726), published pp. 14225–14231, was read at Methods/Results pp. 14227–14230, Table 1 and Figure 5. Human C-terminal His6-DGKE in Sf21-cell detergent extracts phosphorylates exogenous DAG with radiolabeled ATP. No-enzyme/mock controls and substrate comparisons support the reaction and stereoselectivity. The small 2,3-DAG signal has a possible 1,2-isomer contamination explanation; the extracts are not a purified native membrane system. The normal cache remains abstract-only.

[PMID:23542698](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3719402/), Methods and Figure 4b, identifies endogenous DGKE in both membrane and cytoplasmic fractions of unstimulated human platelets, with Na,K-ATPase and β-tubulin controls. Human endothelial and renal expression are distinct from the rat kidney experiments. The platelet fractionation does not identify a unique membrane compartment. The official [Human Protein Atlas record](https://www.proteinatlas.org/ENSG00000153933-DGKE/subcellular) reports approved cytosol/nucleoplasm staining with antibody-based ICC/IF in A-431, U-251MG and U2OS. This supports the existing cytosol annotation and refinement of broad cytoplasm; it does not justify a new nucleoplasm assertion or product-specific localization. The general membrane location remains appropriate for the catalytic core.

[PMID:23274426](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3582208/), Methods and Results, tests full-length human myc-DGKE, c.610delA and siRNA in HEK293T cells with M3-receptor-driven TRPC6 currents. This supports intracellular lipid signaling. The current is a DAG-sensitive functional readout, not direct DAG quantification or an endogenous podocyte recording. Platelet activation remains non-core pathway context: the human disease study proposes a thrombosis mechanism, and nonselective DGK inhibitor observations do not establish a selective DGKE platelet mechanism.

The full [mouse original PMID:11287665](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC31904/) shows weaker high-frequency-stimulation-induced EPSP potentiation in knockout perforant path–dentate granule-cell synapses, with basic membrane properties preserved. It does not image DGKE in a pre- or postsynaptic compartment. The exact Q9R1C6/ENSMUSP00000000285 donor and SynGO annotations ground the non-core orthology annotations; their `is_active_in` relation is preserved. Mouse results are not presented as a direct human synaptic assay.

The abstract of [PMID:15544348](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15544348/) explicitly includes epsilon activity despite its alpha-focused title. [PMID:23949095](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23949095/) explicitly includes epsilon in a ten-isozyme comparison. Both full originals remain inaccessible. Their established catalytic annotations are retained with the target-positive abstracts and independently read human DGKE assays; no inaccessible construct detail is invented. In contrast, the [PMID:19946888 NK-cell membrane screen](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19946888/) could not be checked at the DGKE peptide/table level, so that source-specific membrane annotation remains UNDECIDED rather than being rejected as a wrong target.

## 2026-10-17: membrane decision after independent review

This access limitation does not negate established DGKE membrane association and does not justify a specific membrane subcompartment. This supersedes the earlier UNDECIDED proposal above.
