Gene Description

DGKE is an ATP-dependent lipid kinase that converts diacylglycerol to phosphatidic acid. Its preference for substrates bearing an arachidonoyl chain at sn-2 connects diacylglycerol signaling with phosphatidylinositol recycling. DGKE acts at membrane interfaces and is found in membrane-associated and cytosolic pools. The sn-1 chain preference is more flexible, so stearoyl-arachidonoyl diacylglycerol is not its only efficiently used substrate.

Core Functions

Phosphorylates membrane diacylglycerol to phosphatidic acid using ATP, preferentially recycling arachidonoyl-containing diacylglycerol into the phosphatidylinositol cycle. Selectivity is strongest for the sn-2 acyl chain; stearoyl at sn-1 is favored among tested saturated chains, but other sn-1 chains can support substantial activity.

2026-10-17: catalytic activity, products and source scope

DGKE performs ATP-dependent phosphorylation of DAG to PA. Human recombinant experiments support this chemistry directly; mouse knockout lipidomics supply additional pathway context. One catalytic core combines ATP-dependent DAG kinase activity with PA production and upstream participation in PI recycling. The enzyme is not a PI synthase. DAG metabolism and lipid phosphorylation describe the same chemistry, rather than additional catalytic units. The current typed ontology does not make lipid phosphorylation an ancestor of PA biosynthesis (PMID:18004883(https://doi.org/10.1021/bi701584v), PMID:19744926(https://doi.org/10.1074/jbc.M109.050617), PMID:21477596(https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3095698/), PMID:22108654(https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3237191/)).

The four-source independent chemistry assessment follows. The two abstract-only normal caches retain that flag even though original PDFs were recovered separately. Original-only quotations remain in these notes; YAML anchors use literal text in the normal caches.

PMID:8626589(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8626589/) — Tang et al., J Biol Chem 271, 10237–10241 (1996). The original PDF identifies an open reading frame of “567 residues” (p. 10237). Human endothelial-cell cDNA was expressed in COS-7 cells, and vector-controlled lysate assays measured radiolabeled PA production with a preference for arachidonoyl-containing DAG (Methods p. 10238; Results and Figure 3 caption pp. 10239–10240). The cDNA truncation removed untranslated regions, not the protein's catalytic domain. The reported supernatant activity is qualified by poor total activity recovery and does not establish exclusively soluble localization.

PMID:19744926(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19744926/) — Lung et al., doi:10.1074/jbc.M109.050617. The original PDF describes human full-length/engineered Δ58 proteins in COS-7 cells and His6-DGKE in Sf21 cells. Enriched detergent lysates, mock controls and ATP-to-PA assays support human catalytic activity and acyl-chain selectivity (Methods pp. 31064–31065; Results/Figures 4 and 7, pp. 31067–31071). Separate knockout mouse-fibroblast lipidomics found 29% less stearoyl-containing PI; the method “does not determine their position on the glycerol backbone” (p. 31065). This supports PI-cycle participation without proving direct human PI synthesis, positional lipid flux or a physiological feedback circuit. Δ58 is not the natural short isoform.

PMID:21477596(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21477596/) — Shulga et al., doi:10.1016/j.jmb.2011.03.071, PMC3095698. The Materials and Methods subsection on DGKε constructs states: “All correspond to human forms of the respective enzymes.” FLAG-DGKE wild type and mutants were expressed in COS-7 cells; detergent-lysate assays measured ATP-dependent PA formation with mock subtraction, lipid dependence and a DGKalpha comparator (Materials and Methods, Enzyme Preparation and Enzymatic Activity). Mutations near the proposed selectivity motif alter activity and acyl-chain preference. This is functional mutagenesis, not a solved substrate-binding structure. PIP5Kalpha experiments in the paper do not establish that activity for DGKE.

PMID:22108654(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22108654/) — Shulga et al., doi:10.1016/j.febslet.2011.11.016, PMC3237191. Human His6-DGKE expressed in Sf21 cells was assayed in detergent extracts and mixed micelles (Methods §§2.1, 2.2 and 2.4). Results and Discussion, including the Figure 1/Table 1 discussion, show that arachidonoyl chains at both positions can support activity similar to stearoyl/arachidonoyl DAG: “18:0/20:4-DAG is not unique in being a good substrate for DGKε”. Selectivity is stricter at sn-2 than at sn-1; linoleoyl at sn-2 is a poorer substrate. The membrane-penetration explanation is a hypothesis. None of these studies tests the natural 177-residue product.

Other original evidence and localization
