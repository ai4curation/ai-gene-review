Gene Description

ACSM4 (acyl-CoA synthetase medium-chain family member 4) encodes a mitochondrial AMP-forming acyl-CoA synthetase of the medium-chain (ACSM) subfamily of the ANL adenylate-forming enzyme superfamily, one of the xenobiotic/medium-chain fatty acid:CoA ligases. It carries an N-terminal mitochondrial transit peptide and the AMP-binding and AMP-binding C domains. By orthology with rat O-MACS, the experimentally characterised olfactory epithelium-specific ortholog, it is expected to catalyse the ATP-dependent ligation of free medium-chain fatty acids (C6-C12) to coenzyme A, forming the acyl-CoA thioester with release of AMP and diphosphate, the activation step that precedes their use in downstream metabolism. The human protein was detected in a high-confidence mitochondrial proteome. Its enzymatic activity, substrates and physiological role in human have not been characterised directly.

GO:0006633 fatty acid biosynthetic process
IBA
GO_REF:0000033 		MARK AS OVER ANNOTATED
Summary: Over-annotation. An acyl-CoA synthetase activates existing fatty acids to their CoA thioesters; it does not perform a step in the synthesis of fatty acids. The seed paper points to oxidation, not synthesis.
Reason: The IBD on PTHR43605 root node PTN001944787 is seeded by mouse Acsm1 (MGI:2152200) and Acsm3 (MGI:99538), annotated by MGI from PMID:11470804. That paper characterises both as medium-chain acyl-CoA synthetases whose acyl-CoA products are "utilized mainly for oxidation", which argues against a fatty acid biosynthetic role. The ligase reaction forms a fatty-acyl-CoA, not a fatty acid, so the intended concept was probably fatty-acyl-CoA biosynthetic process (GO:0046949), proposed here as the replacement for the acyl-CoA metabolic process row. Nothing places ACSM4 in de novo or mitochondrial fatty acid synthesis. Not removed because only the seed paper's abstract was read.
