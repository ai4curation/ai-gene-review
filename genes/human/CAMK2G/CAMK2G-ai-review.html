Gene Description

CAMK2G encodes the gamma subunit of calcium/calmodulin-dependent protein kinase II (CaMKII), a serine/threonine kinase built from an N-terminal kinase domain, an autoinhibitory Ca2+/calmodulin-binding regulatory segment, a variable linker and a C-terminal association domain that assembles twelve subunits into a homo- or heteromeric holoenzyme with the alpha, beta and delta paralogs. Ca2+/calmodulin binding relieves autoinhibition and allows Thr287 autophosphorylation, which makes the kinase Ca2+-independent. Unlike the neuron-specific alpha and beta isoforms, gamma is widely expressed, including in skeletal muscle and pancreatic islets, and it binds calmodulin with the highest affinity of the four paralogs. In the developing brain it is the dominant hippocampal isoform and regulates neurite outgrowth and neuronal maturation; in neurons gamma-CaMKII also acts as a carrier that sequesters Ca2+/calmodulin and shuttles it to the nucleus to drive CREB-dependent transcription needed for persistent synaptic plasticity and memory. Nucleus-targeted splice isoforms carry a nuclear localization signal. With the delta isoform it binds the STAT1 transcription activation domain and phosphorylates STAT1 Ser727 during interferon-gamma signalling. In skeletal muscle it associates with the sarcoplasmic reticulum and contributes to Ca2+ handling. The de novo gain-of-function variant p.Arg292Pro causes severe intellectual disability.
