CC   -!- FUNCTION: Bifunctional enzyme that catalyzes amidation of the C-
CC       terminus of proteins (PubMed:12699694, PubMed:2357221). Alpha-amidation
CC       is present at the C-terminus of many endocrine hormones and
CC       neuropeptides and is required for their activity (PubMed:1575450). C-
CC       terminal amidation also takes place in response to protein
CC       fragmentation triggered by oxidative stress, promoting degradation of
CC       amidated protein fragments by the proteasome (PubMed:2207077). Alpha-
CC       amidation involves two sequential reactions, both of which are
CC       catalyzed by separate catalytic domains of the enzyme
CC       (PubMed:12699694). The first step, catalyzed by peptidyl alpha-
CC       hydroxylating monooxygenase (PHM) domain, is the copper-, ascorbate-,
CC       and O2- dependent stereospecific hydroxylation (with S stereochemistry)
CC       at the alpha-carbon (C-alpha) of the C-terminal glycine of the
CC       peptidylglycine substrate (PubMed:12699694). The second step, catalyzed
CC       by the peptidylglycine amidoglycolate lyase (PAL) domain, is the zinc-
CC       dependent cleavage of the N-C-alpha bond, producing the alpha-amidated
CC       peptide and glyoxylate (PubMed:12699694). Similarly, catalyzes the two-
CC       step conversion of an N-fatty acylglycine to a primary fatty acid amide
CC       and glyoxylate (By similarity). {ECO:0000250|UniProtKB:P14925,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:12699694, ECO:0000269|PubMed:2357221,
CC       ECO:0000303|PubMed:1575450, ECO:0000303|PubMed:2207077}.
