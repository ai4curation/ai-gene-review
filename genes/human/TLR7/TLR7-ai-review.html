Gene Description

TLR7 (Toll-like receptor 7) is an endolysosomal pattern recognition receptor of the innate immune system that detects RNA degradation products, chiefly those of viral single-stranded RNA genomes. It is a single-pass type I membrane glycoprotein: a large leucine-rich-repeat ectodomain faces the lumen of the compartment, a single transmembrane helix crosses the membrane, and a cytoplasmic TIR domain carries the signal. Unlike a simple ssRNA sensor, TLR7 is a dual receptor with two spatially distinct ligand-binding sites on its horseshoe-shaped ectodomain. The first site, at the dimerisation interface and conserved with TLR8, binds a guanosine moiety - guanosine itself, deoxyguanosine, synthetic guanosine analogues and imidazoquinolines such as imiquimod and R-848, and physiologically the guanosine 2',3'-cyclic monophosphate released from RNA. The second site, on the concave surface and unique to TLR7, binds short uridine-containing single-stranded RNA fragments, which raise the affinity of the first site. Both ligands are made in situ by sequential RNA catabolism: the lysosomal endonuclease RNase T2 cuts RNA between purine and uridine residues, and the exonucleases PLD3 and PLD4 release the terminal 2',3'-cyclic GMP and generate the pyrimidine-rich fragments. Occupancy of both sites locks TLR7 into an activated m-shaped homodimer that brings the two TIR domains together, recruiting MyD88 and assembling the myddosome with IRAK4, IRAK1/IRAK2 and TRAF6; this activates canonical NF-kappaB and MAP kinases, driving IL-6, IL-8, TNF and chemokine production, and activates IRF7, driving type I interferon. Newly made TLR7 folds in the endoplasmic reticulum membrane and is delivered to endosomes and lysosomes by the chaperone UNC93B1, which engages its transmembrane and juxtamembrane regions; signalling requires endosomal acidification, and this intracellular confinement is what restrains responses to self RNA. TLR7 is expressed most strongly in plasmacytoid dendritic cells and B cells, and also in monocytes and various epithelial and pigment cells. Loss-of-function variants cause an X-linked immunodeficiency presenting as severe COVID-19 with impaired interferon responses, while gain-of-function variants that increase guanosine sensing cause X-linked systemic lupus erythematosus, driving survival of B-cell-receptor-activated B cells and accumulation of CD11c-positive age-associated B cells in a MyD88-dependent manner.

Core Functions

TLR7 is the recognition receptor of the endolysosomal TLR7 signalling pathway. Its leucine-rich-repeat ectodomain binds RNA catabolites in the lumen of the endolysosome and its cytoplasmic TIR domain transduces the event: occupancy of both ligand sites locks two protomers into an activated m-shaped dimer whose apposed TIR domains recruit MyD88, assembling the IRAK4-IRAK1/2-TRAF6 myddosome that activates canonical NF-kappaB and IRF7. Because the ligands are generic RNA degradation products rather than a foreign-specific chemistry, discrimination of self from non-self rests on where the receptor is: it signals only from acidified endolysosomes reached via UNC93B1.

1. Key Concepts and Definitions

TLR7 (Toll-like receptor 7) is a pattern recognition receptor (PRR) located in the endosomal/lysosomal compartments of human immune cells. Its primary role is sensing viral and endogenous single-stranded RNA (ssRNA) and initiating innate and adaptive immune responses. TLR7 belongs to the toll-like receptor family, contains multiple leucine-rich repeats (LRRs), a transmembrane segment, and a cytoplasmic TIR (Toll/IL-1 receptor) signaling domain. It is encoded by the TLR7 gene, with the canonical human protein reviewed under UniProt accession Q9NYK1 (pal2022integrationofligandbased pages 1-2, zheng2023recentadvanceson pages 2-4, hamerman2024thepathahead pages 1-3, zheng2023recentadvanceson pages 1-2).

Major domains and features:
- N-terminal ectodomain (with LRRs for ligand binding)
- Z-loop, which must be proteolytically cleaved for activation
- C-terminal TIR domain (mediates interactions with signaling adaptors)
- Anchored in endosomal membranes (not at plasma membrane)

2. Primary Molecular Function: Ligand Recognition and Specificity

TLR7 acts as a sensor (not an enzyme) for pathogen- and damage-associated molecular patterns (PAMPs and DAMPs) within endosomal compartments. Its canonical ligands are single-stranded RNA containing guanosine and uridine, typically from viral genomes. TLR7 uses a dual-site recognition mechanism: binding a guanosine or related small molecule in one site (site 1), and a short uridine-rich ssRNA fragment (site 2), enabling precise detection of viral or aberrant endogenous RNA (zheng2023recentadvanceson pages 1-2, hamerman2024thepathahead pages 3-4, luan2024innateimmuneresponses pages 5-7, hofsten2024humanandmurine pages 8-10, kong2023understandingnucleicacid pages 2-3, zheng2023recentadvanceson pages 2-4, lind2022regulationofthe pages 8-9).

Processed RNA ligands are required for activation—extracellular and endosomal nucleases (including RNase T2) generate agonistic RNA fragments. Proteolytic maturation of TLR7 (Z-loop cleavage) also restricts sensitive RNA recognition to the endolysosomal compartment, preventing inappropriate activation (zheng2023recentadvanceson pages 2-4, zheng2023recentadvanceson pages 1-2, hamerman2024thepathahead pages 11-14).

3. TLR7 Signaling Pathways and Mechanisms

Following ligand-induced dimerization, TLR7 initiates intracellular signal transduction predominantly via the MyD88 adaptor. The pathway involves the assembly of the Myddosome (MyD88-IRAK4-IRAK1), recruitment of TRAF6, and activation of TAK1, leading to nuclear translocation of NF-κB and AP-1, and the induction of IRF7 for robust type I interferon expression—critical in anti-viral defense and autoimmunity (zheng2023recentadvanceson pages 2-4, lannoy2023tiraptramand pages 5-6, luan2024innateimmuneresponses pages 5-7, jin2023regulationoftolllike pages 3-4, zheng2023recentadvanceson pages 14-16, hamerman2024thepathahead pages 3-4).

Some evidence supports involvement of bridging adaptors (TIRAP/MAL and TRAM) in specific contexts or cell types, adding complexity to the interferon-response branch.

4. Subcellular Localization and Trafficking

TLR7 is synthesized in the endoplasmic reticulum (ER) and trafficked to endosomal compartments via the chaperone UNC93B1. Within endosomes, further trafficking to lysosome-related organelles occurs via AP3/AP4 and SLC15A4-dependent pathways (hofsten2024humanandmurine pages 10-11, hamerman2024thepathahead pages 1-3, kong2023understandingnucleicacid pages 2-3, hamerman2024thepathahead pages 3-4, zheng2023recentadvanceson pages 1-2, hofsten2024humanandmurine pages 8-10, zheng2023recentadvanceson pages 2-4, zheng2023recentadvanceson pages 4-6, hamerman2024thepathahead pages 11-14).

TLR7 protein is not localized at the plasma membrane. Its RNA-sensing function is strictly compartmentalized to endosomal/lysosomal membranes, where proteolytic maturation permits ligand recognition and prevents inappropriate activation by cytosolic or extracellular self-RNA (hofsten2024humanandmurine pages 10-11, hamerman2024thepathahead pages 1-3, zheng2023recentadvanceson pages 1-2, hofsten2024humanandmurine pages 8-10, zheng2023recentadvanceson pages 2-4, hamerman2024thepathahead pages 11-14).

5. Biological Processes and Immune Functions

TLR7 is prominently expressed in:
- Plasmacytoid dendritic cells (pDCs): potent type I interferon producers
- B cells: involved in antibody production and B-cell activation
- Monocytes and select dendritic cell subtypes

Major roles include:
- Induction of type I interferon and proinflammatory cytokines in response to viral ssRNA
- Promoting differentiation of B cells (including plasma cell formation and autoantibody production)
- Amplification and regulation of antiviral and autoimmune immune responses
- Pathogenesis of autoimmune disorders, notably systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE), where overactivation or dysregulation leads to loss of immune tolerance (hamerman2024thepathahead pages 1-3, hamerman2024thepathahead pages 3-4, hofsten2024humanandmurine pages 10-11, wen2023tolllikereceptors7 pages 2-4, zheng2023recentadvanceson pages 14-16, hofsten2024humanandmurine pages 8-10, zheng2023recentadvanceson pages 2-4, wen2023tolllikereceptors7 pages 6-7).

6. Recent Developments (2023–2024) & Disease Associations

  • Genetic findings: A gain-of-function variant (TLR7Y264H) was discovered in a young girl with SLE, demonstrating that enhanced TLR7 RNA sensing/activation is sufficient to cause human lupus. Increased TLR7 copy number or expression, or impaired X-chromosome inactivation, also raise disease risk (hofsten2024humanandmurine pages 10-11, hofsten2024humanandmurine pages 8-10, hamerman2024thepathahead pages 1-3).
  • Trafficking mutations: New studies identified UNC93B1 mutations and endosomal sorting defects (e.g., BLOC-1–Arl8b–Unc93B1 axis) as causes of TLR7 overactivation and childhood-onset lupus (hofsten2024humanandmurine pages 10-11, hamerman2024thepathahead pages 1-3).
  • Therapeutics: Small-molecule TLR7 antagonists (e.g., MHV370, enpatoran) are in development or clinical testing, aiming to modulate excessive interferon responses in SLE, rheumatoid arthritis, and severe viral disease https://doi.org/10.1016/j.xcrm.2023.101036 (hofsten2024humanandmurine pages 8-10, hamerman2024thepathahead pages 3-4, kalliolias2024targetingtlrsignaling pages 8-10).
  • Application in infection and cancer: TLR7 is implicated in antiviral immunity (e.g., COVID-19, HIV) and in responses to cancer immunotherapies targeting innate immune checkpoints (kalliolias2024targetingtlrsignaling pages 8-10, zheng2023recentadvanceson pages 4-6, zheng2023recentadvanceson pages 14-16).

Statistics/Data:
- In SLE, TLR7 activity correlates with disease severity; gene duplication and activating mutations worsen autoimmune phenotypes and interferon signatures (hofsten2024humanandmurine pages 10-11, hofsten2024humanandmurine pages 8-10, kalliolias2024targetingtlrsignaling pages 8-10).
- Inhibition of TLR7 in mice and human cells can ameliorate lupus pathology and cytokine storm in viral infections (zheng2023recentadvanceson pages 4-6, zheng2023recentadvanceson pages 2-4).

7. Expert Analysis and Authoritative Opinions

  • Current consensus is that TLR7 is the principal endosomal RNA sensor driving SLE and type I IFN-related autoimmune pathology. TLR7 activity is tightly regulated by trafficking, maturation, and ligand processing; failure in any regulation can lead to autoinflammation (hamerman2024thepathahead pages 1-3, hofsten2024humanandmurine pages 10-11, hofsten2024humanandmurine pages 8-10).
  • Sex bias in SLE may derive from TLR7 escape from X-inactivation, with higher TLR7 gene dosage in females.
  • Therapeutically targeting TLR7 or its pathway shows promise for stratified intervention in autoimmune disease and severe viral immunopathology (kalliolias2024targetingtlrsignaling pages 8-10, zheng2023recentadvanceson pages 4-6).

What TLR7 is

Endolysosomal single-pass type I membrane pattern-recognition receptor: a
leucine-rich-repeat ectodomain, one transmembrane helix, and a cytoplasmic TIR
domain. UniProt Q9NYK1: signal 1–26, chain 27–1049, extracellular 27–839,
transmembrane 840–860.

crystal structures resolve two spatially distinct sites:

Annotation-level findings

  1. GO:0070305 response to cGMP (IMP, PMID:35477763) is chemically wrong.
    GO:0070305 is defined as a response to "cGMP (cyclic GMP, guanosine
    3',5'-cyclophosphate)" (QuickGO). The TLR7 site-1 ligand released by
    RNase T2/PLD3/PLD4 is guanosine 2',3'-cyclic monophosphate
    PMID:38697119, and the cited lupus paper says exactly that
    ("guanosine and 2',3'-cGMP"). MODIFY to GO:1905108 guanosine binding, which
    is what the structures and the deoxyguanosine work establish directly. GO has
    no term for 2',3'-cGMP binding or for a response to it — raised as a question.

  2. GO:0071260 cellular response to mechanical stimulus (IEP, PMID:19593445)
    cites an unrelated paper.     PMID:19593445 is "Expression of the Bcl-2 protein
    BAD promotes prostate cancer growth" (PLoS ONE 2009); the cached record has
    full text and contains zero occurrences of "TLR" or "toll". The same row
    exists on TLR8 and on TLR3 (where it was also left UNDECIDED). Marked
    UNDECIDED, with reference_review.correctness: WRONG_IDENTIFIER.

  3. GO:0034158 toll-like receptor 8 signaling pathway on TLR7. An Ensembl
    Compara transfer from mouse Tlr7 (UniProtKB:P58681). GO:0034158 is defined as
    "The series of molecular signals initiated by a ligand binding to the
    endolysosomal toll-like receptor 8" (QuickGO) — by definition it cannot be a
    process TLR7 carries out. TLR7 already has GO:0034154 four times over.
    REMOVE.

  4. GO:0043235 signaling receptor complex (IDA, PMID:23382219) cites the SNX17
    PX-FERM structure paper.     I retrieved the open PMC copy (PMC3581954, 244 kB of
    text) and it contains no mention of TLR7 or of Toll-like receptors. The claim
    itself is true — TLR7 forms an activated m-shaped homodimer PMID:27742543 and
    associates with UNC93B1 PMID:33432245 — so the row is kept (ACCEPT) with the
    real support attached and the reference flagged as a wrong identifier.

  5. GO:0003725 double-stranded RNA binding (IDA, PMID:16111635). The cached
    record is abstract-only and says "RNA signals through human TLR3, TLR7, and
    TLR8"; dsRNA is TLR3's ligand, while TLR7's second site binds single-stranded
    uridine-containing RNA PMID:27742543. MODIFY to GO:0003727.

  6. GO:0035197 siRNA binding (IEA from mouse Tlr7 + ISS). siRNA is a duplex;
    the structurally defined TLR7 sites bind guanosine and single-stranded
    uridine-containing RNA. Immunostimulatory siRNA plausibly acts through
    single-stranded regions or degradation products rather than duplex binding.
    MARK_AS_OVER_ANNOTATED.

  7. Type II interferon. Two rows. The IDA PMID:16286015 row rests on a paper
    that measured IFN-α/β and IFN-λ only — "Human TLR-7-, -8-, and -9-mediated
    induction of IFN-alpha/beta and -lambda Is IRAK-4 dependent" — so it is
    MODIFY→GO:0034346 positive regulation of type III interferon production
    (matching the decision already taken in the TLR9 review). The ARBA IEA row is
    independently supported for TLR7 by PMID:32706371 (IFN-γ abolished in TLR7
    loss-of-function patients' PBMCs), but that is a 7-day PBMC readout and the
    IFN-γ producers are not the TLR7-expressing cells, so it is kept as non-core.

  8. Plasma membrane. The is_active_in GO:0005886 IBA sits at the TLR7/8/9
    PAINT node and there is a melanocyte surface IDA PMID:19740627. TLR7's
    intracellular confinement is functionally essential, and the endolysosome is
    where it signals, so both rows are kept as non-core rather than accepted —
    consistent with the TLR9 review. Whether is_active_in plasma membrane should
    stand at that node is raised as a question.

  9. GO:0005515 protein binding (IPI, PMID:33432245, UNC93B1). Uninformative;
    TLR7 is the client of UNC93B1 and the biology is trafficking, not an activity
    of TLR7. REMOVE (mirrors the TLR3 review's handling of the same paper).
    Removal does not dispute the interaction.

  10. Reactome bulk rows. 19 GO:0010008 endosome membrane and 19

  ligand-binding sites on its horseshoe-shaped ectodomain. The first site, at the dimerisation interface

  analogues and imidazoquinolines such as imiquimod and R-848, and physiologically the guanosine 2'',3''-cyclic
  monophosphate released from RNA. The second site, on the concave surface and unique to TLR7, binds short
  uridine-containing single-stranded RNA fragments, which raise the affinity of the first site. Both ligands
  are made in situ by sequential RNA catabolism: the lysosomal endonuclease RNase T2 cuts RNA between
  purine and uridine residues, and the exonucleases PLD3 and PLD4 release the terminal 2'',3''-cyclic
