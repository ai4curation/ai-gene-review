### Complement chemistry, assembly and opsonization

C3 does some of the work of complement activation. The complete cached abstract of [PMID:6903192](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/6903192/) reports chemical modification of human C3, factor binding, convertase formation with B/D, and the thioester-dependent target-attachment model. C3b supplies its own covalent surface attachment and the nonenzymatic assembly platform; the partner proteases perform precursor cleavage. Thus the broad complement-activation term is valid across several pathways and is not simply a substrate annotation. The GZMK source [PMID:39914456](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39914456/) was checked in actual human reconstitution, C3a signaling, cell-surface deposition and relevant Methods passages. GZMK acts upstream through C2 and C4; it does not directly cleave C3. Neither GZMK, Bb nor activated-C2 protease activity is transferred to C3.

The six factor B/H/H-related binding rows are refined to GO:0001848 complement binding. The inverse activity, C3b binding by those partners, is not assigned to C3 merely because the paper describes it in that direction. The first core combines C3b complement assembly with its established opsonic contribution to phagocytic engulfment. Human C3b-IgG target experiments in PMID:3653927 and apoptotic-cell uptake in PMID:19302245 support the latter directly; the phagocyte executes engulfment while C3-derived material supplies recognition ligands. No protease or endocytic machinery activity is implied.

The C3d–CR2 core is a distinct processed-fragment receptor function. PMID:21527715 confirms the interaction while disputing the earlier PMID:11387479 structural interface. The earlier source is preserved with that finding-level limit. The human CD21/FCRL5 paper PMID:30107486 includes receptor co-engagement and calcium assays; its proposed physiological C3-IgG immune-complex arrangement is not treated as demonstrated in every configuration. No receptor activity is assigned to C3d itself.

### C3a activity, original negative source and intentional GO-CAM placement

The modern C3a experiments establish receptor ligand activity and G protein-coupled receptor signaling. Selected complete Methods and Results of PMID:37852260 were read, including recombinant human C3a, human C3AR1 expressed in insect cells, purified receptor complexes, HEK293 transducer assays and human macrophage responses. These are human constructs even when an expression host is bacterial or insect. The complete PMID:37169960 abstract distinguishes complement-anaphylatoxin recognition from chemokine-receptor recognition. Structures and receptor activation do not by themselves assay directional migration.

The official definitions matter. [GO:0008009 chemokine activity](https://amigo.geneontology.org/amigo/term/GO:0008009) specifies the chemokine family and has chemokine receptor binding as a parent. [GO:0048018 receptor ligand activity](https://amigo.geneontology.org/amigo/term/GO:0048018) describes a ligand effect on its receptor. [GO:0042056 chemoattractant activity](https://amigo.geneontology.org/amigo/term/GO:0042056) requires directed movement toward higher concentration. The modern C3a source rows are refined to receptor ligand activity, not automatically to chemoattractant activity. The broad complement-activation row explicitly on processed C3a is refined to the directly assayed GPCR pathway; the other C3/C3b complement rows remain valid.

The immutable PMID:342601 cache contains bibliographic metadata without an abstract. The complete [original publisher abstract](https://academic.oup.com/jimmunol/article-abstract/120/1/109/8081003) was read independently, as was the complete [published correction](https://academic.oup.com/jimmunol/article/120/4/1445/8080999). Human C3a was reported inactive in the tested human-PMN migration and rabbit-neutrophil skin-window conditions. The correction changes the trypsin-treated human-C3 result from active to inactive and fixes a C5a-desArg printing error. It is not a retraction. These are direct contrary source statements, not rejection from a title or lack of access. Accordingly the two positive source-linked chemokine/leukocyte-chemotaxis assertions are REMOVE. No universal absence of C3a migration effects on other cell types is asserted, and full original Methods remain unread.

The old PMID:5778786 abstract reports chemotactic activity, but the later original source fails to reproduce the relevant short-trypsin-C3/C3a activity and raises possible C5 contamination. Because neither full old assay was inspected, that source remains UNDECIDED rather than being confidently replaced by a positive chemoattractant assertion. C3AR1 ligand activity is independently established.

Three inspected GO-CAM models (67c10cc400005671, 67e5e74400000671 and 67f85f2b00004288) intentionally place C3a chemokine activity under leukocyte chemotaxis and complement activation using these exact sources. This was an explicit curator choice, not an overlooked product or missing annotation. ## 2026-10-03 — Inhibitor mapping and complement receptor specificity

The published review's electronic endopeptidase-inhibitor decision is revised from UNDECIDED to REMOVE. The domain matches themselves are real; the problem is transferring inhibitor specificity across functionally distinct branches of the macroglobulin family. Human C3 experiments in PMID:6903192 establish thioester-dependent attachment to a target surface and complement-convertase formation. Primary structural descriptions distinguish this complement mechanism from the protease-cleavable bait sequence and protease-trapping mechanism of macroglobulin inhibitors. The [2022 human A2ML1 structural study](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-022-30758-x) describes the complement anaphylatoxin segment replacing the functional bait region and distinguishes covalent attachment to a complement-activating surface from attachment to the attacking protease. The [2021 structural mechanics study](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0022283621006501) likewise distinguishes human C3's anaphylatoxin/alpha-prime N-terminal segments from the inhibitor bait-region arrangement. Access was limited to the indexed primary-publisher paragraphs; full publisher opens failed, and no full-paper or figure reading is claimed. No new publication cache or quotation has been manufactured.

This is a functional-divergence argument about the electronic mapping, not a universal claim that C3 cannot influence any protease. Historical names on homologous domains, including a bait-region-domain label, do not independently establish the functional bait sequence or trapping activity. The exact GOA PAINT node remains unavailable. The earlier journal statement that the available inhibitor IBD was not transferred in this review must not be read as proof that PAINT explicitly placed that historical node outside C3. The existing IBA decisions and node-access limits are unchanged. The retracted Nature paper with DOI 10.1038/nature05258 is not used as evidence for this correction.

The two existing C3d–CR2 binding annotations from PMID:11387479 and PMID:21527715 are refined to GO:0048018 receptor ligand activity. That activity already has an accepted source row and the C3d core; the change makes the binding-row refinements consistent with the supported receptor-ligand role. The early structural interface remains disputed, and the isolated structure is not described as a measurement of every downstream immune response. The [official indexed receptor-ligand term page](https://amigo.geneontology.org/amigo/term/GO:0048018) currently displays a has-part relationship to signaling receptor binding, so this decision does not repeat the review comment's unsupported is-a assertion.

Its [official definition](https://amigo.geneontology.org/amigo/term/GO:0002430) includes signaling triggered by complement fragments binding complement receptors. The core still describes G-protein/arrestin mechanism, but does not retain a second generic GPCR process entry. No C3aR-specific binding term was verified, so no identifier is guessed.
