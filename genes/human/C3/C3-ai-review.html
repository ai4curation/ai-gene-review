Gene Description

C3 is a secreted central component of the complement system whose proteolytic and chemical processing creates functionally distinct effectors. Activated C3b uses an internal thioester to attach covalently to nearby surfaces, provides a nonenzymatic platform for complement convertases, and marks targets for recognition by complement receptors. Further processing produces iC3b and C3d-containing fragments that connect opsonic recognition to phagocytes and B-cell co-receptors. Soluble C3a activates the G-protein-coupled receptor C3AR1 and elicits context-dependent inflammatory responses. These activities support microbial defense and removal or remodeling of tagged material; complement-dependent neural pruning and metabolic effects of the distinct C3a-desArg product depend on experimental and tissue context.

Core Functions

C3b attaches to target surfaces through its exposed thioester and binds complement partners to assemble and regulate convertases. C3b-containing fragments also present recognition ligands that promote phagocytic engulfment. This nonenzymatic complement-effector unit serves several activation pathways, including the GZMK pathway; Bb or activated C2 supplies convertase proteolysis.
