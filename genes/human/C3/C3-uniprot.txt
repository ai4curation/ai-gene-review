CC   -!- FUNCTION: Precursor of non-enzymatic components of the classical,
CC       alternative, lectin and GZMK complement pathways, which consist in a
CC       cascade of proteins that leads to phagocytosis and breakdown of
CC       pathogens and signaling that strengthens the adaptive immune system.
CC       {ECO:0000269|PubMed:12878586, ECO:0000269|PubMed:18204047,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:28264884, ECO:0000269|PubMed:31507604,

CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:624565, ECO:0000269|PubMed:6554279}.
CC   -!- FUNCTION: [Complement C3b]: Non-enzymatic component of C5 convertase
CC       (PubMed:28264884, PubMed:31507604, PubMed:3653927, PubMed:3897448).
CC       Generated following cleavage by C3 convertase, it covalently attaches
CC       to the surface of pathogens, where it acts as an opsonin that marks the
CC       surface of antigens for removal (PubMed:28264884, PubMed:31507604,
CC       PubMed:3653927, PubMed:3897448, PubMed:833545, PubMed:8349625).
CC       Complement C3b binds covalently via its reactive thioester, to cell
CC       surface carbohydrates or immune aggregates (PubMed:6903192). Together
CC       with complement C4b, it then recruits the serine protease complement
CC       C2b to form the C5 convertase, which cleaves and activate C5, the next
CC       component of the complement pathways (PubMed:12878586, PubMed:18204047,
CC       PubMed:2387864). In the alternative complement pathway, recruits the
CC       serine protease CFB to form the C5 convertase that cleaves and
CC       activates C5 (PubMed:624565, PubMed:6554279).
CC       {ECO:0000269|PubMed:12878586, ECO:0000269|PubMed:18204047,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:2387864, ECO:0000269|PubMed:28264884,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:31507604, ECO:0000269|PubMed:3653927,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:3897448, ECO:0000269|PubMed:624565,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:6554279, ECO:0000269|PubMed:6903192,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:833545, ECO:0000269|PubMed:8349625}.
CC   -!- FUNCTION: [C3a anaphylatoxin]: Mediator of local inflammatory process
CC       released following cleavage by C3 convertase (PubMed:6968751,
CC       PubMed:37169960, PubMed:37852260). Acts by binding to its receptor,
CC       C3AR1, activating G protein-coupled receptor signaling, promoting the
CC       phosphorylation, ARRB2-mediated internalization and endocytosis of
CC       C3AR1 (PubMed:8702752, PubMed:37169960, PubMed:37852260). C3a
CC       anaphylatoxin stimulates the activation of immune cells such as mast
CC       cells and basophilic leukocytes to release inflammation agents, such as
CC       cytokines, chemokines and histamine, which promote inflammation
CC       development (PubMed:23383423). Also acts as potent chemoattractant for
CC       the migration of macrophages and neutrophils to the inflamed tissues,
CC       resulting in neutralization of the inflammatory triggers by multiple
CC       ways, such as phagocytosis and generation of reactive oxidants
CC       (PubMed:23383423, PubMed:342601, PubMed:5778786).
CC       {ECO:0000269|PubMed:342601, ECO:0000269|PubMed:37169960,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:37852260, ECO:0000269|PubMed:5778786,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:6968751, ECO:0000269|PubMed:8702752,
CC       ECO:0000303|PubMed:23383423}.
CC   -!- FUNCTION: [Acylation stimulating protein]: Adipogenic hormone that
CC       stimulates triglyceride synthesis and glucose transport in adipocytes,
CC       regulating fat storage and playing a role in postprandial triglyceride
CC       clearance (PubMed:10432298, PubMed:15833747, PubMed:16333141,
CC       PubMed:19615750, PubMed:2909530, PubMed:8376604, PubMed:9059512).
CC       Appears to stimulate triglyceride synthesis via activation of the PLC,
CC       MAPK and AKT signaling pathways (PubMed:16333141). Acts by binding to
CC       its receptor, C5AR2, activating G protein-coupled receptor signaling,
CC       promoting the phosphorylation, ARRB2-mediated internalization and
CC       endocytosis of C5AR2 (PubMed:11773063, PubMed:12540846,
CC       PubMed:19615750). In contrast to C3a anaphylatoxin peptide, does not
CC       show pro-inflammatory activity (PubMed:37852260).
