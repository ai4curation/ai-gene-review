Gene Description

AHNAK, also called desmoyokin, is a 5,890-residue (~700 kDa) intrinsically disordered scaffolding phosphoprotein with three parts: an N-terminal PDZ domain, a very large central region built from roughly 26 to 30 tandem 128-residue repeats, and a ~1,000-residue C-terminal regulatory domain carrying several nuclear localisation signals. It has no catalytic activity and no folded interaction module other than the PDZ domain; its role is to hold other proteins together at the cytoplasmic face of membranes. The C-terminal domain binds the annexin A2/S100A10 heterotetramer through a short motif resolved crystallographically at about 30 nM affinity, binds monomeric actin, cosediments with and bundles actin filaments, and binds the CACNB2 (Cav-beta2) regulatory subunit of L-type voltage-gated calcium channels with 55-60 nM affinity through a proline-rich motif. Through these contacts AHNAK couples the L-type channel to the cortical actin cytoskeleton and acts as a brake on L-type calcium current that beta-adrenergic PKA signalling relieves; it is also required for surface delivery of L-type channels in T lymphocytes and neurons. The central repeats bind and activate protein kinase C-alpha by displacing it from an inhibitory complex with protein phosphatase 2A, and bind and activate phospholipase C-gamma1 in the presence of arachidonic acid. In striated muscle AHNAK is a sarcolemmal and costameric protein and a component of the dysferlin complex, binding the dysferlin C2A domain directly; it is lost from the sarcolemma in dysferlinopathy and appears in calcium-stimulated shed vesicles. In the nucleus it binds the 53BP1 oligomerization domain and restrains 53BP1 multimerisation and phase separation, damping the p53 DNA-damage response. Its distribution between nucleus, cytoplasm and plasma membrane is set by phosphorylation: PKB/Akt phosphorylation drives nuclear export through an adjacent nuclear export signal, while protein kinase C activation and raised calcium drive it toward the membrane. A short alternatively spliced isoform of about 17 kDa, comprising essentially the PDZ domain, shuttles to the nucleus and feeds back on splicing at the AHNAK locus; the same arrangement occurs at the Periaxin locus, with which AHNAK and AHNAK2 share a common ancestor.

Proposed New Ontology Terms

protein oligomerization limiting activity

Definition: A molecular function in which a protein binds the self-association interface of another protein and limits the size of the oligomer or condensate that partner forms, without preventing its binding to other partners, displacing it from its site of action, or targeting it for degradation.

Justification: AHNAK's nuclear activity has no MF term. It binds the 53BP1 oligomerization domain and caps 53BP1 multimerisation and phase separation; in its absence 53BP1 nuclear bodies enlarge and the p53 response is amplified (PMID:33961796). The two candidate existing terms both fail on their differentia. GO:0140311 protein sequestering activity is defined as binding a protein 'to prevent it from interacting with other partners or to inhibit its localization to the area of the cell or complex where it is active' - but AHNAK does neither: 53BP1 stays on chromatin and stays active, it simply assembles less far. GO:0060090 molecular adaptor activity is the opposite direction, bringing molecules together. This is not an AHNAK-specific gap: the same shape recurs wherever a chaperone-like binder tunes the extent of a biomolecular condensate rather than blocking its formation, and that class of regulator is growing faster than the ontology's vocabulary for it. A biological-process parallel already exists (GO:0032460 negative regulation of protein oligomerization), which this review uses; what is missing is the activity that carries it out. Parenting is the hard part, and the difficulty is itself the gap. The obvious homes do not hold: GO:0140678 molecular function inhibitor activity and GO:0098772 molecular function regulator activity both presume the thing being regulated is a molecular function, and 53BP1 oligomerization is not one - GO models it as a biological process (GO:0051260 protein homooligomerization). GO:0140313 molecular sequestering activity fails on the same differentia as its child GO:0140311. So the term is proposed under GO:0005515 protein binding, which is where it structurally belongs and which is deliberately uninformative, to make the point: GO's molecular-function regulator vocabulary is built to regulate molecular functions, and has no way to say that an activity modulates a partner's self-assembly. If GO were willing to read limiting a partner's self-association as regulating its function, GO:0098772 would become the natural parent; that is a decision for an ontology editor rather than something this review should assume.

Core Functions

Cortical membrane scaffold. Through a defined 20-residue motif in its C-terminal domain, AHNAK is recruited to the inner leaflet of the plasma membrane by the annexin A2/S100A10 heterotetramer, and through the same C-terminal region it binds monomeric actin and cosediments with filaments. Holding those two otherwise unconnected partners together is what organises the cortical actin layer beneath the membrane: depleting annexin A2/S100A10 removes AHNAK from the membrane, while depleting AHNAK leaves that complex in place and blocks the cortical actin reorganisation that sets epithelial cell height. The complex concentrates at calcium-dependent cell-cell contacts and partitions into detergent-resistant membrane microdomains.

Current model (mechanistic narrative)

AHNAK is a giant (~700 kDa) scaffolding phosphoprotein built from highly conserved central repeated units whose subcellular distribution between nucleus, cytoplasm, and plasma membrane is dynamically controlled by phosphorylation: PKB/Akt phosphorylation of Ser5535 drives nuclear export through an adjacent NES PMID:11535620, while PKC activation and Ca2+-dependent signals translocate it toward the cell membrane via determinants in its C-terminal domain [PMID:7698224, PMID:10771490]. At the cytosolic face of the plasma membrane AHNAK assembles a cortical actin-organizing platform: its C-terminal domain binds G- and F-actin and the annexin A2/S100A10 (A2t) heterotetramer through a defined 20-residue motif (resolved at 2.5 Å), and these interactions are required for cortical actin reorganization and cell architecture [PMID:12153988, PMID:14699089, PMID:16984913, PMID:23275167]. Through its central repeated units AHNAK functions as a signaling scaffold, binding and activating PLC-γ1 in an arachidonic-acid-dependent manner and recruiting PKC-α—disrupting the inhibitory PKC-α/PP2A complex—to amplify Ca2+ mobilization and Raf/MEK/Erk and Rac signaling [PMID:10318799, PMID:15033986, PMID:18174170, PMID:23042471]. A second major role is regulation of L-type voltage-gated Ca2+ channels: AHNAK binds the Cavβ2 subunit (Kd ~55–60 nM via a C-terminal PxxP motif) to act as a brake on Cav1.2 current that is relieved by PKA phosphorylation of either partner, and is required for surface expression and Ca2+ influx of L-type channels in T cells, neurons, and other cell types [PMID:10593863, PMID:12153988, PMID:14722071, PMID:16319140, PMID:22497893, PMID:20607281, PMID:30760886, PMID:18191595, PMID:19497879]. AHNAK is a structural component of muscle membrane complexes including the dysferlin complex and the costamere/β-dystroglycan network, linking these to the actin cytoskeleton and influencing membrane mechanics and Schwann cell morphology [PMID:17185750, PMID:20833135, PMID:24796807]. In the nucleus AHNAK shapes growth and stress responses: it potentiates TGFβ/Smad3 signaling by promoting Smad3 nuclear accumulation and drives BMP2/Smad1-dependent adipogenesis at the PPARγ promoter [PMID:24662814, PMID:26466345, PMID:30258109], and it restrains 53BP1 oligomerization and phase separation to tune the p53 DNA-damage response and senescence PMID:33961796. Its protein levels are controlled by the UBE3C and RNF38 E3 ubiquitin ligases, providing post-translational control over its context-dependent tumor-suppressive and pro-metastatic activities [PMID:30503554, PMID:30836988].

AHNAK (Q09666) — review notes

Working journal for the GO annotation review. Provenance is given inline as
[PMID:xxxx "verbatim text"] or as the query that produced a number.

What AHNAK is

5,890 aa, ~629 kDa by UniProt's own calculation (the literature says "700 kDa", which is the
apparent mass on a gel). Architecture from the UniProt feature table:

  • FT DOMAIN 9..90 /note="PDZ" /evidence="ECO:0000255|PROSITE-ProRule:PRU00143" — predicted, not
    experimentally solved, but see the holdup finding below, which is functional evidence that it
    really is a PDZ.
  • No FT REPEAT features at all, despite KW Repeat. The tandem 128-residue central repeat
    units that the entire literature describes are not modelled in the entry. The published counts
    differ — PMID:14699089 for the 5,643-aa sequence, against PMID:10318799 — so this review says "26 to 30" rather than picking one. The
    22 FT REGION features are all MobiDB-lite "Disordered".
  • Five predicted NLS motifs, all in the C-terminal region (4971-4979, 5019-5027, 5034-5039,
    5706-5716, 5772-5779).
  • 13 VARIANTs, all dbSNP; the I5236T variant that PMID:16319140 characterises functionally is
    not among them.
  • CC -!- FUNCTION: May be required for neuronal cell differentiation. — one hedged sentence
    for a protein with a thirty-year literature. Raised as a UniProt suggestion.
  • CC -!- SUBCELLULAR LOCATION: Nucleus. — also a single word, against four plasma-membrane
    IDAs on the same protein.
  • Isoform 2 (Q09666-2) = residues 1-149 with 115-149 replaced by a 22-residue alternative, i.e.
    ~136 aa = the PDZ domain alone. This is almost certainly the "small 17 kDa AHNAK" of
    PMID:21940993. Corroborating: mouse has TrEMBL entries Q8CEX7 and A0A494B8Y7 (176 aa),
    both named "PDZ domain-containing protein" / "AHNAK nucleoprotein".

description: 'AHNAK, also called desmoyokin, is a 5,890-residue (~700 kDa) intrinsically disordered
  scaffolding phosphoprotein with three parts: an N-terminal PDZ domain, a very large central region
  built from roughly 26 to 30 tandem 128-residue repeats, and a ~1,000-residue C-terminal regulatory
  domain carrying several nuclear localisation signals. It has no catalytic activity and no folded
  interaction module other than the PDZ domain; its role is to hold other proteins together at the
  cytoplasmic face of membranes. The C-terminal domain binds the annexin A2/S100A10 heterotetramer
  through a short motif resolved crystallographically at about 30 nM affinity, binds monomeric actin,
  cosediments with and bundles actin filaments, and binds the CACNB2 (Cav-beta2) regulatory subunit
  of L-type voltage-gated calcium channels with 55-60 nM affinity through a proline-rich motif. Through
  these contacts AHNAK couples the L-type channel to the cortical actin cytoskeleton and acts as a
  brake on L-type calcium current that beta-adrenergic PKA signalling relieves; it is also required
  for surface delivery of L-type channels in T lymphocytes and neurons. The central repeats bind and
  activate protein kinase C-alpha by displacing it from an inhibitory complex with protein phosphatase
  2A, and bind and activate phospholipase C-gamma1 in the presence of arachidonic acid. In striated
  muscle AHNAK is a sarcolemmal and costameric protein and a component of the dysferlin complex, binding
  the dysferlin C2A domain directly; it is lost from the sarcolemma in dysferlinopathy and appears
  in calcium-stimulated shed vesicles. In the nucleus it binds the 53BP1 oligomerization domain and
  restrains 53BP1 multimerisation and phase separation, damping the p53 DNA-damage response. Its distribution
  between nucleus, cytoplasm and plasma membrane is set by phosphorylation: PKB/Akt phosphorylation
  drives nuclear export through an adjacent nuclear export signal, while protein kinase C activation
  and raised calcium drive it toward the membrane. A short alternatively spliced isoform of about
  17 kDa, comprising essentially the PDZ domain, shuttles to the nucleus and feeds back on splicing
  at the AHNAK locus; the same arrangement occurs at the Periaxin locus, with which AHNAK and AHNAK2
  share a common ancestor.'
