# CBS: catalytic sulfur metabolism, regulation, and evidence limits

## 2026-10-04 — Whole-review reassessment

CBS is a PLP-dependent enzyme at the entry to transsulfuration. It condenses serine and homocysteine to cystathionine; CTH subsequently cleaves cystathionine to release cysteine. CBS therefore performs a chemical step in cysteine biosynthesis rather than merely being necessary for the pathway. It can also use cysteine with homocysteine to produce cystathionine and H2S. The two reactions, cofactor interactions, assembly and allosteric regulation belong to one metabolic enzyme unit. The core summary does not assign separate occupations to heme or SAM binding.

The original human biochemical sources establish the reaction and its substrate kinetics (PMID:7929220; PMID:18776696; PMID:19010420). PMID:15520012 directly compares cysteine/homocysteine beta-replacement with less efficient cysteine-only hydrolysis under its tested conditions. These measurements support H2S production, without establishing how much CBS contributes to H2S flux in every human tissue. The same study reports no stimulation by calcium/calmodulin in its experiments; a universal calcium-dependent mechanism is not inferred.

The reviewed human sequence is P35520, 551 residues, sequence version 2, CRC64 F89E69C67BDE6701, from the preserved 18 June 2025 UniProt entry. The normal parser recovers two machine products: P35520-1, `1 (Major)`, and P35520-2, `2 (Minor)` with VSP_001217. These are retained exactly, without equating product labels with untested functional specialization. The record includes both NCBI Gene 875 and 102724560. Current official NCBI information explains that the CBSL scaffold may be a false duplication/redundancy with CBS; that does not authorize rewriting the archived cross-references or asserting a second independently functional human gene. The frozen ClinGen project association is autosomal recessive classical homocystinuria, Definitive. This review does not change its validity classification.

## Assembly, SAM and heme

The human structural study PMID:11483494 used a 1–413 construct without the C-terminal regulatory region. Its dimer supports a dimeric assembly unit, not exclusive dimerization of intact CBS. Historical tetramer language in UniProt, SAM studies and Reactome remains part of those sources but is not treated as an immutable stoichiometric rule. The normal full XML source PMID:38575566 establishes filaments of recombinant full-length human CBS, built from dimeric units. The basal and SAM-bound structures differ in regulatory-domain arrangement and catalytic-domain accessibility. Both a construct with its N-terminal affinity tag removed and one with a permanent C-terminal His tag were examined; the tag affects stability and measured thermodynamics. Loop-deleted and isolated catalytic-domain constructs remain distinct controls. The proposed cooperativity increase was not statistically significant, and the study does not finally exclude a cryptic SAM-binding site. Its oligomerization-based disease-rescue model remains a hypothesis.

PMID:22985361 directly investigates SAM binding, stability and activation of human CBS. SAM is an allosteric ligand of the regulatory region, not a second enzyme activity. Heme association is established by native-enzyme biochemistry and gas spectroscopy. PMID:29410458 additionally studies the disordered human 1–40 peptide and its heme interaction; a peptide experiment is not an assay of the whole enzyme. The older interpretation that heme is required for transsulfuration is qualified by subsequent work separating its structural/regulatory effects from PLP chemistry. No unsupported universal heme requirement is retained.

PMID:24416422 demonstrates anaerobic nitrite reduction by recombinant human CBS and formation of an inhibitory ferrous-nitrosyl state. Both chemical reduction and an NADPH/methionine-synthase-reductase system were examined. The biochemical side activity is retained outside the defining core. The selected complete Results, captions, Discussion and Methods paragraphs were read in the normal cache; high experimental nitrite/reducing conditions and the absence of a cellular CBS-dependent NO-flux experiment limit physiological extrapolation. Hypoxic signaling in that paper is a proposed implication.

PMID:24515102 is abstract-only in the immutable cache. Separately accessed primary Results and Discussion distinguish NO/CO ligation from oxygen-driven oxidation of ferrous CBS to ferric CBS. The oxygen experiment monitors spectrum recovery and superoxide-sensitive kinetics; it does not establish an O2-occupied protein state. The oxygen-binding IDA is therefore marked as over-annotated on this specific mechanistic evidence. This is neither an abstract-only rejection nor a claim that no fleeting interaction with oxygen is possible. NO and CO binding remain genuine conditional regulatory properties, with the primary study’s quantitative affinity interpretation kept separate from earlier apparent values.

## Interaction and localization evidence

The historical review treated several generic interaction records as uninformative overannotations. That blanket treatment is withdrawn. Exact human CBS partners that are corroborated in reviewed UniProt are retained as non-core physical associations, with their original partner accessions, source isoforms, evidence codes and PMIDs intact. Complete abstracts establish the screen contexts; pair-level target tables and construct controls were not independently checked for every edge. These records do not by themselves establish CBS regulation of the partner, a physiological complex, a neurological function, or a more specific domain-binding activity.

The seven CBS–yeast assertions from PMID:27107014 remain unresolved. Selected original screening Methods and Results show that cross-species yeast-two-hybrid and pair-retesting experiments were intentional. A yeast partner is not a reason to reject an experimental result. The CBS-specific supplementary rows and controls were not inspected, and the exact edges lack the reviewed human-partner corroboration available for the other records. No wrong-gene, curator-error or physiological interaction claim is made.

PMID:17087506 explicitly names Ubc9, PIAS1, PIAS3, Pc2 and RanBPM among CBS-binding proteins and reports SUMO modification and nuclear-scaffold association. Only its complete primary abstract was accessible; publisher full-text access failed. The original ubiquitin-protein-ligase-binding row has P63279, Ubc9/UBE2I, as its exact partner. It is not a PIAS or Pc2 edge. The experimentally stated context is SUMO conjugation, whereas GO:0031625 refers specifically to ubiquitin protein ligase binding. The existing broader enzyme-binding term preserves the supported association without manufacturing a finer activity. CBS is the SUMO-modified substrate, and is not assigned the conjugation machinery’s catalytic role. RanBPM’s name likewise does not establish GTPase-binding activity by CBS.

Human wild-type localization is corroborated by PMID:23981774 and the preserved Reactome-derived GOA cytosol assignment. The cached Reactome event summary describes the reaction but does not itself state a compartment; its beta-lyase wording is not adopted as the CBS enzyme identity. Mutant puncta or aggregation are not generalized to the wild-type enzyme. The chromosome-21 survey PMID:16780588 supplies a curated HDA cytosol assignment; its CBS-specific image/table was not independently inspected. The assignment is nevertheless retained in light of convergent target localization and reaction evidence. The sumoylated nuclear fraction is retained as a non-core localization, without inferring a nuclear transcription or DNA-repair mechanism.

## Unresolved process and binding assertions

The available body of PMID:20031578 contains the population study’s Introduction, Results and Discussion, but lacks complete original Methods and target supplementary material. It supports CBS-region association with homocysteine concentration and is consistent with CBS metabolism. The specific modified-amino-acid-binding IDA assay remains unidentified. The metabolic annotation can be retained on independent direct human chemistry while the binding annotation remains undecided. No PMID remapping or miscitation is asserted.

Official dated donor records link the rat nutrient annotation to PMID:19352060 and hypoxia/anti-apoptosis annotations to PMID:20149843. Their complete primary abstracts were subsequently read separately from the normal cache collection. The casein study measures dietary responses in rats. The hypoxia paper reports SH-SY5Y cells, H2S-donor treatment and protection associated with CBS overexpression; its abstract does not resolve the species and sequence of the expression construct. A human cell line is not proof of a human construct. The hypoxia and anti-apoptosis transfers remain unresolved pending the necessary original-assay/transfer details; they are not rejected because they describe regulated expression, downstream protection or an electronic source. The nutrient-response assertion is now independently corroborated by the direct human experiment in PMID:38575566 and retained as non-core. Its original rat source fields remain unchanged.

PMID:24534463 reports DNA damage in treated CBS-deficient patients and homocysteine-induced damage in white blood cells. Its complete abstract was read, but full original experiments were inaccessible. The existing DNA-protection IMP is retained as undecided, without converting a metabolic disease phenotype into a direct DNA-binding or repair mechanism.

## Human nutrient-responsive assembly: experiment and limits

The cellular experiments in PMID:38575566 transiently expressed human CBS with a C-terminal Flag-mKO2 tag, as specified in Methods, although the figure names use mKO2-CBS. In PC-3 cells, eight-hour methionine depletion changed the tagged-protein fluorescence from a predominantly filament-like pattern toward dispersed/punctate morphology. SAM supplementation at 320 micromolar restored the filament-like pattern under deprivation, with a matched solvent control; adding SAM to complete medium did not produce a significant change. Loop-deletion and SAM-site mutants provide additional assembly controls. These are measurements of fluorescence morphology, not direct endogenous-filament ultrastructure or metabolic flux. The existing broad nutrient-response annotation is retained as a conditional non-core property of the enzyme, without creating a new process assertion or silently replacing its rat donor metadata.

The complete normal abstract and selected full primary paragraphs covering structural Results, cell imaging, biochemical assays, Discussion and Methods were read. Figure captions were read as text; figure pixels, deposited maps and numerical source-data files were not independently reanalysed. The H2S assay measures the cysteine/homocysteine side reaction under defined substrate concentrations, not direct cellular transsulfuration flux. The oxygen-overannotation anchor was separately read in the external primary Results of PMC3961681; PMID:24515102 remains abstract-only in the immutable cache. Short catalytic anchors now accompany both broad-to-specific molecular-function refinements.
