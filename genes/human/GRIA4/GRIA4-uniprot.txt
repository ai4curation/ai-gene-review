CC   -!- FUNCTION: Ionotropic glutamate receptor that functions as a ligand-
CC       gated cation channel, gated by L-glutamate and glutamatergic agonists
CC       such as alpha-amino-3-hydroxy-5-methyl-4-isoxazolepropionic acid
CC       (AMPA), quisqualic acid, and kainic acid (By similarity). L-glutamate
CC       acts as an excitatory neurotransmitter at many synapses in the central
CC       nervous system and plays an important role in fast excitatory synaptic
CC       transmission (By similarity). Binding of the excitatory
CC       neurotransmitter L-glutamate induces a conformation change, leading to
CC       the opening of the cation channel, and thereby converts the chemical
CC       signal to an electrical impulse upon entry of monovalent and divalent
CC       cations such as sodium and calcium. The receptor then desensitizes
CC       rapidly and enters a transient inactive state, characterized by the
CC       presence of bound agonist (By similarity). In the presence of CACNG8,
CC       shows resensitization which is characterized by a delayed accumulation
CC       of current flux upon continued application of L-glutamate
CC       (PubMed:21172611). {ECO:0000250|UniProtKB:P19493,
CC       ECO:0000250|UniProtKB:P42262, ECO:0000269|PubMed:21172611}.

CC   -!- SUBUNIT: Homotetramer or heterotetramer of pore-forming glutamate
CC       receptor subunits. Tetramers may be formed by the dimerization of
CC       dimers. Interacts with EPB41L1 via its C-terminus. Isoform 3 interacts
CC       with PICK1. Found in a complex with GRIA1, GRIA2, GRIA3, CNIH2, CNIH3,
CC       CACNG2, CACNG3, CACNG4, CACNG5, CACNG7 and CACNG8. Interacts with
CC       CACNG5 and PRKCG (By similarity). Found in a complex with GRIA1, GRIA2,
CC       GRIA3, DLG4, CACNG8 and CNIH2 (By similarity).
CC       {ECO:0000250|UniProtKB:P19493, ECO:0000250|UniProtKB:Q9Z2W8}.

CC   -!- DISEASE: Neurodevelopmental disorder with or without seizures and gait
CC       abnormalities (NEDSGA) [MIM:617864]: An autosomal dominant
CC       neurodevelopmental disorder characterized by global developmental delay
CC       apparent from infancy or early childhood, mild to profound intellectual
CC       disability, hypertonia early in life, which progresses to spasticity
CC       and impaired gait later, and behavioral abnormalities. Some patients
CC       may develop seizures of variable severity early in life.
CC       {ECO:0000269|PubMed:29220673}. Note=The disease is caused by variants
CC       affecting the gene represented in this entry.
