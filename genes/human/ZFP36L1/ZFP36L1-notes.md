ZFP36L1/BRF1/TIS11B is a TTP-family CCCH zinc-finger protein that binds AU-rich elements in 3'-UTRs and promotes post-transcriptional repression, usually by recruiting decay enzymes. Stoecklin et al. identified BRF1 as the missing regulator in an ARE-decay-defective cell line and found that BRF1 rescue restored rapid mRNA decay [PMID:12198173, "The cDNA was identified as butyrate response factor-1 (BRF1), encoding a zinc finger protein homologous to tristetraprolin"]. Lykke-Andersen and Wagner showed that BRF-1, like TTP, recruits decay enzymes through decay-activation domains [PMID:15687258, "TTP and BRF-1 each contain two activation domains that can activate mRNA decay"], and Adachi et al. showed that ZFP36L1 and ZFP36L2 bind LDLR mRNA 3'-UTR and recruit CCR4-NOT [PMID:25106868, "proteins ZFP36L1 and ZFP36L2 specifically bind to the 3'-UTR of LDLR mRNA and recruit the CCR4-NOT-deadenylase complex"].

Several human papers annotate particular ZFP36L1 target transcripts rather than a new molecular activity. Examples include VEGFA mRNA in adrenocortical cells [PMID:15467755, "TIS11b and its homologs TIS11 and TIS11d interact with the 3'-untranslated region of VEGF mRNA and decrease its stability"], BCL2 mRNA in malignant B cells [PMID:25014217, "ZFP36L1 interacted with the BCL2 adenine uridine rich element"], CDK6 mRNA during monocyte/macrophage differentiation [PMID:26542173, "ZFP36L1 binds to the CDK6 mRNA 3'untranslated region bearing adenine-uridine rich elements"], and DLL4 mRNA 3'-end processing in endothelial cells [PMID:21832157, "Tis11b silencing in endothelial cells leads to up-regulation of Dll4 protein and mRNA expressions"].

ZFP36L1 is itself regulated by AKT, p38/MK2, ERK/RSK, cAMP, osmotic, hypoxic, hormone, and growth-factor contexts. Rows that merely say an upstream stimulus changes ZFP36L1 abundance or phosphorylation are marked as over-annotations; rows in which ZFP36L1 binds a target mRNA and destabilizes, cleaves, or physiologically tunes that mRNA are kept as direct or non-core functions.

The PAINT `PTN002648882` rows for ARE binding, adaptor activity, cytosol, poly(A)-tail shortening, and `GO:0000900` mRNA regulatory element binding translation repressor activity are consistent with the conserved mammalian TTP-family mechanism. The `GO:0000900` IBA row comes through the shared eumetazoan TTP-family node and is kept as non-core for human ZFP36L1 because human BRF1 and TTP deliver ARE-mRNAs to P-bodies and silence their translation.
