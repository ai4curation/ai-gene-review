Gene Description

ENDOG encodes mitochondrial endonuclease G, a nuclear-encoded Mg-dependent DNA/RNA nuclease of the DRGH/His-Me finger family with an N-terminal mitochondrial transit peptide. It acts as a homodimer. Mature ENDOG resides mainly in mitochondria, can enter the nucleus, and catalyzes endonucleolytic cleavage of DNA substrates, with a strong preference for oxidatively damaged, nicked or gapped DNA and for 5-hydroxymethylcytosine-modified double-stranded DNA. Where it sits inside mitochondria is unresolved. Fractionation places it in the intermembrane space, but it colocalizes with matrix nucleoids in human cells and acts on mitochondrial DNA. Its nuclease activity supports mitochondrial genome turnover and compensatory replication, and nuclear ENDOG-mediated DNA breaks can promote conservative recombination. During starvation, released and phosphorylated ENDOG also binds 14-3-3 proteins to suppress mTOR signaling and promote autophagy, with an additional endonuclease-dependent DNA damage-response arm. After release from mitochondria during cell death, ENDOG cleaves nuclear chromatin into nucleosomal fragments independently of caspases, in a role partly redundant with CAD/DFFB. KU80 binds ENDOG and restrains its nuclear activity.

Subfractionation alone cannot separate them, because those membrane fractions co-stained for COX-IV, TOM20 and cytochrome c together; but the immunogold electron microscopy in the next panel can, and its legend reports both tagged proteins delineating the cristae, which are inner membrane. One honest discrepancy inside that paper, recorded rather than smoothed over. The running text describes the EM only for EXOG and cites earlier work for mammalian EndoG, so the legend's claim about both proteins is the stronger of the two statements; and the whole experiment used tagged, expressed protein. That is enough to keep an inner-membrane row with its own support, and not enough to make the location core, so the row stays non-core. Sub-compartment check under the organelle policy, which is a separate question from whether this row stands. Fractionation places mammalian EndoG in the intermembrane space and not the matrix (PMID:12444964), consistent with its release on outer-membrane permeabilization. In human HeLa cells it instead colocalizes with the matrix nucleoid marker TFAM (PMID:36394256), and its cleavage of mtDNA (PMID:29719607) and, in mouse, of mitochondrial transcripts (PMID:39752519) requires matrix access. The worm ortholog CPS-6 moves from the intermembrane space to the matrix after fertilization (PMID:27338704), so both may hold under different conditions. A sequence analysis narrows the import route without settling the compartment. ENDOG has a cleavable arginine-rich presequence and only three cysteines with no twin CX9C or CX3C motif, so it enters by the TIM23 presequence route and is not a MIA40 disulfide-relay intermembrane-space protein. That route delivers proteins to both compartments, the same signature is shared with the EXOG paralog, and the classifier scored only 60 percent on reference proteins of known sub-compartment, so it does not discriminate. Because those lines disagree, no soluble intermembrane-space or matrix row is added; the membrane-fraction result above is consistent with that, since it finds ENDOG in neither soluble compartment. The root mitochondrion rows carry the overall location.

IBA

Summary: ENDOG-family nucleases can contribute to apoptotic DNA fragmentation, but this is not human ENDOGs principal function.
Reason: ENDOG is catalytically capable of fragmenting chromosomal DNA after mitochondrial release, so the PAINT process transfer is plausible. Recent direct human data instead center ENDOG on 5hmC-modified DNA cleavage, mitochondrial genome maintenance, and autophagy, making apoptotic DNA fragmentation a retained non-core branch rather than the defining function. Checked against the APOPTOSIS project's assay-readout rule. This is not an endpoint-readout inference, because ENDOG is itself the nuclease that performs the step. Recombinant human EndoG reproduces the defining two-stage pattern on isolated nuclei, large fragments then nucleosomal cleavage (PMID:11606588). EndoG fragments chromatin in DFF-deficient mouse cells (PMID:11452314). In worms, cps-6 loss impairs apoptotic DNA degradation, and the nuclease activity of CPS-6 mutants tracks their killing activity (PMID:11452313, PMID:22223640). Necessity in mammals is weak. A null mouse allele that spares the overlapping gene shows no obvious apoptotic DNA-degradation defect (PMID:15601850), so the role is redundant with CAD/DFFB. Non-core is the right weight. The apparent conflict between the two mouse lines does not change that. The PMID:14663139 allele was embryonic lethal, but the PMID:15601850 authors built a null that spares the gene lying tail to tail with Endog and attribute the lethality to disrupting that neighbour, which is why PMID:14663139 is marked DISPUTED in the reference list. The cleaner allele is therefore the better test of necessity. The activity itself does not rest on either mouse, but on the purified human enzyme cleaving isolated nuclei (PMID:11606588) and on fragmentation in CAD-deficient cells (PMID:11452314), so the activity is real and the requirement is not.

ENDOG cleaves damaged mitochondrial DNA, promoting removal of mtDNA molecules that are not repaired efficiently and stimulating compensatory mitochondrial DNA replication under oxidative stress. Executive Judgment

Verdict: PARTIALLY SUPPORTED, and over-interpreted as stated — the NUMT arm is REFUTED and the
deletion arm is a direct-repeat confound rather than evidence of ENDOG causation.

  • mtDNA deletions: breakpoints are statistically enriched at G4-forming / C-rich motifs,
    even after matching local GC composition (local max|G4Hunter| fold ≈ 1.17, z = +13, p < 1e-4;
    robust across G4Hunter windows and a stringent ±1 kb local null). However, a direct-repeat
    stratification shows the entire signal comes from repeat-mediated deletions (DR ≥ 5 bp:
    fold 1.24, z = +16) and disappears in non-repeat deletions (DR < 5 bp: fold 0.97, p = 0.91).
    mtDNA direct repeats are themselves G/C-rich, so the "motif" enrichment is collinear with the
    classical slipped-replication / direct-repeat mechanism     and does not isolate ENDOG cleavage.
  • NUMTs: No enrichment at G4/C-rich motifs over GC-matched controls, in three independent
    datasets     — the exact 66,083-genome catalogue named in the seed (PMID:36198798 germline, z = +0.3,
    p = 0.39; somatic, p = 0.10) and the UCSC NumtS set (p = 0.59). The seed's NUMT claim is refuted.

Curation bottom line: This computational result should not be used to assign ENDOG a
mtDNA-breakpoint-determining function. It demonstrates co-localization of deletion breakpoints with
G/C-rich, repeat-prone sequence — a long-known property of mtDNA deletions — not a specific,
ENDOG-attributable cleavage signature. The enrichment is real but non-discriminating; the NUMT
prediction is wrong.

Mechanistic Scope

  • Immediate molecular activity under test: sequence/structure-specific DNA cleavage by ENDOG
    (G4-forming / C-rich motif preference). The analysis probes whether where breakpoints sit in the
    mtDNA sequence     matches ENDOG's reported preference.
  • What the data actually show: a statistical association between mtDNA deletion breakpoints
    and G/C-rich, G4-prone, direct-repeat sequence. This is a property of the breakpoints' genomic
    neighborhood, not a demonstration that ENDOG cut there.
  • Downstream / not tested here: actual deletion/NUMT formation, replication-slippage vs
    nuclease-cut causation, ENDOG loss-of-function effects on breakpoint spectra, disease phenotypes.
    The enrichment is agnostic to which nuclease (if any) acts; direct-repeat replication slippage
    explains the same localization without invoking ENDOG.

Conflicts and Alternatives

  1. Direct-repeat mechanism (strongest competing explanation). 70 % of deletions are flanked by
    ≥5 bp repeats; the motif enrichment is entirely within this class. mtDNA deletion hotspots have
    long been attributed to direct/imperfect repeats and replication slippage — a mechanism that does
    not require ENDOG and fully accounts for the observed G/C-rich localization.
  2. NUMT arm contradicted by its own source. The 66,083-genome catalogue shows no G4/C-rich
    enrichment after composition matching; its trinucleotide table is dominated by low-complexity
    microhomology (AT- and C-rich), consistent with non-homologous end joining at the nuclear insertion
    site rather than ENDOG motif cleavage in mtDNA.
  3. Substrate determinant may be damage, not sequence (PMID:41854021). If ENDOG's site selection is
    driven by oxidative lesions / nicks / gaps, a static reference-sequence motif scan cannot capture it —
    the negative/confounded motif result is expected even if ENDOG is biologically involved.
  4. Organism / substrate specificity. The C-rich cleavage evidence is mouse (PMID:39752519) and in an
    RNA/transcript context; the yeast transfer-limiting role (PMID:39227600) is species-specific. Neither
    licenses a human DNA-breakpoint-placement claim.
  5. Composition collinearity. G4 propensity, C-richness, and GC content are mutually correlated in the
    GC-skewed major arc where deletions cluster; GC-decile matching mitigates but cannot fully disentangle
    these from repeat content.

    association ≠ cleavage. Resolve with ENDOG-knockout vs WT mtDNA breakpoint spectra (ChIP/cleavage-seq,

  • Damage-conditioned motif preference. Checked: static rCRS scan only. Why it matters: ENDOG prefers

Sub-mitochondrial Localization of Human ENDOG (Q14249): Matrix vs. Intermembrane Space

Gene: ENDOG · Organism: Homo sapiens (NCBITaxon:9606) · UniProt: Q14249 (NUCG_HUMAN)
Hypothesis slug: submitochondrial-localization-ims-vs-matrix
Seed hypothesis: Human ENDOG's targeting and sequence features place it with mitochondrial matrix proteins rather than with intermembrane space (IMS) proteins.

Summary

Two independent, self-contained sequence-feature classifiers — benchmarked on a reference set of 15 mitochondrial proteins whose sub-compartment is already settled — agree that human ENDOG carries a matrix / presequence-type import signature and is definitively not a MIA40 twin-cysteine IMS protein. ENDOG has a cleavable, arginine-rich, positively charged N-terminal transit peptide (UniProt residues 1–48, net charge ~+5, 16.7% Arg), a basic mature protein (computed pI ~9.0, net charge ~+4), only 3 total cysteines with no twin CX9C/CX3C motif, and no strong N-terminal hydrophobic sorting (stop-transfer) signal. Its nearest reference neighbors in standardized feature space are all matrix proteins — TFAM, SSBP1, and SOD2 — and calibrated classifiers assign it a matrix lean of P(matrix) ≈ 0.67–0.72.

The verdict is partially supported, with a decisive caveat: the sequence cannot, on its own, cleanly discriminate matrix from IMS. The single feature driving the "matrix" call — a cleavable, positively charged presequence with no hydrophobic sorting signal — is also carried by a subclass of genuine IMS residents (DIABLO/SMAC, HTRA2), which both classifiers misclassify as matrix. An abstention-free leave-one-out cross-validation reached only 60% accuracy (barely above the 53% majority-class baseline), and the errors fell precisely on the presequence-bearing IMS proteins that ENDOG most resembles. The robust conclusion is therefore that ENDOG is a presequence-imported, matrix-competent protein that is not a cysteine-shuttle IMS protein — but sequence features alone cannot exclude an IMS or dual localization.

This computational picture reconciles the conflicting experimental biology the curator must weigh: classical fractionation places mammalian EndoG in the IMS (PMID:12444964), yet colocalization with the matrix nucleoid marker TFAM (PMID:36394256) and the substrate logic of mtDNA/transcript cleavage (PMID:39752519) argue for matrix access, while the worm ortholog is known to relocate between compartments (PMID:27338704). Curation recommendation: do not change the sub-mitochondrial cellular-component (CC) term on sequence grounds alone; record the matrix lean as weak computational support compatible with a dual or relocating localization.

Executive Judgment

Verdict: Partially supported — the sequence is matrix-compatible and non-MIA, but cannot cleanly discriminate matrix from IMS.

  • Matrix-compatible targeting = medium confidence. ENDOG's nearest reference neighbors are the matrix proteins TFAM, SSBP1, and SOD2, and its mean distance to the matrix class (2.58) is smaller than to the IMS class (3.63); calibrated models give P(matrix) ≈ 0.67–0.72.
  • "Matrix, not IMS" = low confidence. The classifier's presequence rule misclassifies the genuine IMS proteins DIABLO and HTRA2 as matrix — the exact failure mode that applies to ENDOG.
  • "Not a MIA40/twin-cysteine IMS protein" = high confidence. With only 3 cysteines and no twin CX9C/CX3C motif, ENDOG is unambiguously outside the disulfide-relay IMS import class.

The method is blind to the matrix/presequence-IMS distinction, which is why the hypothesis is only partially supported rather than confirmed.

Mechanistic Model / Interpretation

ENDOG is a nuclear-encoded, mitochondrially imported ββα-Me endonuclease. Its import logic can be read directly from two independent signals in the primary sequence:

   N-terminus ──────────────────────────────── C-terminus
  [ transit peptide 1–48 ]────[ mature nuclease domain ]
   Arg-rich, +5 net charge        basic, pI ~9, +4 net charge
   amphipathic presequence        only 3 Cys, NO twin CX9C/CX3C
          │                              │
          ▼                              ▼
   TOM → TIM23 presequence         NOT a MIA40/disulfide-relay
   import pathway (MPP cleaves)    (rules out cysteine-motif IMS route)

Two mutually compatible conclusions follow:

  1. ENDOG is imported via the cleavable-presequence (TOM/TIM23) pathway, not the MIA40 twin-cysteine pathway. This is a robust, high-confidence negative the sequence supports directly, removing one competing hypothesis — that ENDOG is a classical cysteine-motif IMS resident like CHCHD4 or TIMM13.
  2. The presequence pathway is itself ambiguous between matrix and IMS. Many IMS proteins (DIABLO/SMAC, HTRA2) are delivered by the same presequence machinery, then arrested or sorted into the IMS. Because ENDOG lacks a strong hydrophobic sorting/stop-transfer signal, the default read is matrix — but the classifiers prove this default is unreliable, failing on DIABLO and HTRA2.

The result therefore narrows the question rather than answering it. ENDOG belongs to the presequence-import class; within that class its features lean matrix, but the benchmark explicitly fails on the IMS members of the same class. This is the computational counterpart of the experimental conflict in the literature (IMS fractionation vs. matrix/nucleoid colocalization). A dual or regulated/relocating localization — as documented for the worm ortholog — reconciles all observations and is not excluded by any sequence feature computed here.

Mechanistic Scope

The immediate property tested is the protein import / sub-compartment targeting signal, a sequence-intrinsic feature, not a phenotype. ENDOG carries a canonical cleavable presequence (direct gene-product feature) and lacks the cysteine-shuttle code. "Needs matrix access to cut mtDNA" (PMID:39752519) is a downstream functional inference, and the historical IMS placement (PMID:12444964) is a biochemical localization, not a sequence property — these are kept separate from the computed targeting call. Apoptotic nuclear translocation of EndoG is a distinct phenomenon and not part of this sub-mitochondrial question.

Conflicts and Alternatives

ENDOG is a nuclear-encoded, mitochondrial DNA/RNA endonuclease with a mitochondrial
transit peptide and catalytic Mg-dependent nuclease fold. The human protein is in the
same PANTHER PTHR13966:SF5 subfamily as mouse Endog, yeast NUC1, and worm cps-6,
which supports the broad DNA/RNA nonspecific endonuclease PAINT transfers.

The best direct human and mammalian process evidence splits into three branches:

  • Modified nuclear DNA cleavage and recombination. Robertson et al. purified EndoG
    as the activity in mouse liver extracts that preferentially cleaves
    5-hydroxymethylcytosine-modified DNA, showed recombinant EndoG cleavage, and showed
    that 5hmC stimulates conservative recombination in an EndoG-dependent manner
    [PMID:25355512 Endonuclease G preferentially cleaves 5-hydroxymethylcytosine-modified
    DNA creating a substrate for recombination., "recombinant EndoG preferentially
    recognizes and cleaves a core sequence"; "promote conservative recombination in an
    EndoG-dependent manner"].
  • Mitochondrial DNA turnover. Wiehe et al. used human-cell knockdown, knockout,
    re-expression, and nuclease-mutant assays to show that ENDOG stimulates mitochondrial
    DNA depletion and compensatory mitochondrial DNA replication in a nuclease-dependent
    manner, especially under oxidative stress [PMID:29719607 Endonuclease G promotes
    mitochondrial genome cleavage and replication., "EndoG stimulates both mtDNA
    replication"].
  • Starvation autophagy. Wang et al. showed that starvation-released, GSK3beta-
    phosphorylated ENDOG binds YWHAG/14-3-3gamma, freeing TSC2 and VPS34 from YWHAG to
    suppress mTORC1 and initiate autophagy; they also support an endonuclease-dependent
    DNA damage-response branch of the same autophagy phenotype [PMID:33473107
    Endonuclease G promotes autophagy by suppressing mTOR signaling and activating the DNA
    damage response., "mTOR pathway suppression and autophagy initiation"; "enhances its
    interaction with 14-3-3"].

The inherited apoptotic DNA-fragmentation row is plausible and should stay specific:
ENDOG can itself cleave DNA after mitochondrial release, so GO:0006309 apoptotic DNA fragmentation is preferable to any broad GO:0006915 apoptotic process row. It is not
the central CAD/DFF40 nuclease, though, and the more recent human papers point toward
mtDNA maintenance, 5hmC-biased cleavage/recombination, and starvation autophagy as the
core ENDOG axes.

The rat-derived stimulus rows for mechanical stimulus, calcium, glucose, hypoxia,
estradiol, oxidative stress, and hydrogen-peroxide cell death look over-propagated into
human. They describe conditions or cell-death outcomes in which rat Endog was mobilized
rather than a molecular step performed by human ENDOG. The DNAJA4 and ITLN2 protein
binding rows from high-throughput interactome/chaperone studies were removed as generic
edges with no ENDOG-specific functional interpretation.

2026-10-26 — family, orthologs, human disease, and the dataset landscape

Family and evolution

ENDOG sits in PANTHER PTHR13966 subfamily SF5 with worm CPS-6, yeast NUC1, fission
yeast pnu1 and the secreted bacterial nucleases NucA (Serratia, Nostoc). Two
sibling subfamilies matter for interpretation. SF19 is EXOG, a metazoan paralog
produced by duplication of an ancestral mitochondrial endo/exonuclease
PMID:18187503; a Wing domain converts it into a 5' exonuclease for mtDNA base-excision
repair PMID:28466855. SF17 is secreted insect salivary DNases, one of which
destroys neutrophil extracellular traps to help Leishmania survive
PMID:24516388. So the same His-Me finger fold is used for mtDNA housekeeping, for
mtDNA repair, and — on a secreted branch — for host-DNA destruction.

Regulation by a dedicated inhibitor is conserved in outline. Flies have EndoGI
PMID:19129189, and two 2026 papers independently identify KU80 as the
mammalian equivalent PMID:41837724.

Ortholog phenotypes do not transfer cleanly. Worm CPS-6 and fly EndoG degrade
paternal mtDNA [PMID:27338704, PMID:22421049], but mouse Endog knockouts have normal
spermatogenesis and sperm mtDNA PMID:39373150.

Human disease: mitochondrial, not immune

One genetically defined patient is reported: biallelic ENDOG variants with absent
protein, progressive external ophthalmoplegia, mitochondrial myopathy and multiple
mtDNA deletions in muscle PMID:35326425, plus low-level
heteroplasmic point mutations in fibroblasts PMID:35326425. dismech's Gene Table of Neuromuscular Disorders transcription
classifies ENDOG under mitochondrial_myopathies. MONDO has no ENDOG-specific term;
such a disease would sit under MONDO:0000090 progressive external ophthalmoplegia
with mitochondrial DNA deletions.

The other established mammalian phenotype is cardiac. Rat systems genetics mapped a
blood-pressure-independent left-ventricular-mass locus to Endog PMID:21979051, with depleted mitochondria and elevated ROS in the
knockout heart PMID:21979051; the hypertrophy is ROS-dependent PMID:29502044.

No immune phenotype is reported for ENDOG loss in any organism. Human LoF is

cytosolic DNA sensing. Nuc1 degrades mtDNA destined to leave the organelle

vacuole. Separately, Nuc1 limits transfer of long mtDNA to the nucleus
PMID:39227600,
in a paper that frames the metazoan question explicitly PMID:39227600.

Whether human ENDOG does either job is untested. That is the gap worth an
OpenScientist run, and it is a mechanistic question rather than a disease-gene one.

  • mtDNA deletions. Breakpoints are enriched at G-quadruplex and C-rich motifs over

    repeat-free deletions (fold 0.97, p 0.91). mtDNA direct repeats are themselves G/C-rich,
    so the motif enrichment is collinear with the classical mechanism
    [file:human/ENDOG/ENDOG-hypotheses/mtdna-breakpoint-motif-enrichment/openscientist.md
    "a mechanism that does not require ENDOG and fully accounts for the observed G/C-rich
    localization"].
  • NUMTs. No enrichment in the 66,083-genome catalogue (germline p 0.39, somatic p 0.10)

report quotes is right (C 31.3%, G 13.1%, 16,569 bp), and the 9-bp deletion hotspot it
leans on really is a C-rich direct repeat, ACCCCCTCTAC at 8271-8281 — which is the
report's own central point, that at the hotspot the "motif" and the repeat are the same
sequence.

What cannot be checked: only an HTML and a PDF report were saved, with no code or
intermediate tables, so the test statistics are not replayable. Treat the numbers as
indicative.
