ENDOG mtDNA-breakpoint motif-enrichment hypothesis — Deep Research Report

Gene: ENDOG (Q14249), Homo sapiens (NCBITaxon:9606) Hypothesis slug: mtdna-breakpoint-motif-enrichment Focus type: free_text (computational analysis on sequence + coordinate data) Date: 2026-10-26

Seed hypothesis. Human ENDOG's cleavage-site preferences explain where mitochondrial DNA breakpoints fall: breakpoints of nuclear mitochondrial insertions (NUMTs) and of mtDNA deletions are enriched at G-quadruplex-forming and C-rich motifs in the mitochondrial genome, relative to position- and composition-matched control sites.

This was executed as a computation on sequence and coordinate data, not a literature review. All breakpoint coordinate sources named in the seed context were obtained programmatically.

Executive Judgment

Verdict: PARTIALLY SUPPORTED, and over-interpreted as stated — the NUMT arm is REFUTED and the deletion arm is a direct-repeat confound rather than evidence of ENDOG causation.

  • mtDNA deletions: breakpoints are statistically enriched at G4-forming / C-rich motifs, even after matching local GC composition (local max|G4Hunter| fold ≈ 1.17, z = +13, p < 1e-4; robust across G4Hunter windows and a stringent ±1 kb local null). However, a direct-repeat stratification shows the entire signal comes from repeat-mediated deletions (DR ≥ 5 bp: fold 1.24, z = +16) and disappears in non-repeat deletions (DR < 5 bp: fold 0.97, p = 0.91). mtDNA direct repeats are themselves G/C-rich, so the "motif" enrichment is collinear with the classical slipped-replication / direct-repeat mechanism and does not isolate ENDOG cleavage.
  • NUMTs: No enrichment at G4/C-rich motifs over GC-matched controls, in three independent datasets — the exact 66,083-genome catalogue named in the seed (36198798 germline, z = +0.3, p = 0.39; somatic, p = 0.10) and the UCSC NumtS set (p = 0.59). The seed's NUMT claim is refuted.

Curation bottom line: This computational result should not be used to assign ENDOG a mtDNA-breakpoint-determining function. It demonstrates co-localization of deletion breakpoints with G/C-rich, repeat-prone sequence — a long-known property of mtDNA deletions — not a specific, ENDOG-attributable cleavage signature. The enrichment is real but non-discriminating; the NUMT prediction is wrong.

Mechanistic Scope

  • Immediate molecular activity under test: sequence/structure-specific DNA cleavage by ENDOG (G4-forming / C-rich motif preference). The analysis probes whether where breakpoints sit in the mtDNA sequence matches ENDOG's reported preference.
  • What the data actually show: a statistical association between mtDNA deletion breakpoints and G/C-rich, G4-prone, direct-repeat sequence. This is a property of the breakpoints' genomic neighborhood, not a demonstration that ENDOG cut there.
  • Downstream / not tested here: actual deletion/NUMT formation, replication-slippage vs nuclease-cut causation, ENDOG loss-of-function effects on breakpoint spectra, disease phenotypes. The enrichment is agnostic to which nuclease (if any) acts; direct-repeat replication slippage explains the same localization without invoking ENDOG.

Conflicts and Alternatives

  1. Direct-repeat mechanism (strongest competing explanation). 70 % of deletions are flanked by ≥5 bp repeats; the motif enrichment is entirely within this class. mtDNA deletion hotspots have long been attributed to direct/imperfect repeats and replication slippage — a mechanism that does not require ENDOG and fully accounts for the observed G/C-rich localization.
  2. NUMT arm contradicted by its own source. The 66,083-genome catalogue shows no G4/C-rich enrichment after composition matching; its trinucleotide table is dominated by low-complexity microhomology (AT- and C-rich), consistent with non-homologous end joining at the nuclear insertion site rather than ENDOG motif cleavage in mtDNA.
  3. Substrate determinant may be damage, not sequence (41854021). If ENDOG's site selection is driven by oxidative lesions / nicks / gaps, a static reference-sequence motif scan cannot capture it — the negative/confounded motif result is expected even if ENDOG is biologically involved.
  4. Organism / substrate specificity. The C-rich cleavage evidence is mouse (39752519) and in an RNA/transcript context; the yeast transfer-limiting role (39227600) is species-specific. Neither licenses a human DNA-breakpoint-placement claim.
  5. Composition collinearity. G4 propensity, C-richness, and GC content are mutually correlated in the GC-skewed major arc where deletions cluster; GC-decile matching mitigates but cannot fully disentangle these from repeat content.

Discriminating Tests

  1. ENDOG-null vs WT breakpoint spectra (cells/mouse): if ENDOG drives breakpoint placement, its loss should shift/deplete breakpoints at G4/C-rich sites — most informative single experiment.
  2. In-vitro ENDOG cleavage-site mapping on mtDNA (undamaged vs oxidatively-damaged) → define the true human cleavage motif, then test breakpoint overlap (replaces the assumed motif with a measured one).
  3. Repeat-controlled re-analysis at scale: restrict to repeat-free deletions and NUMTs and ask whether any residual ENDOG-specific motif survives (this run: none).
  4. Experimental G4 maps (G4-seq / BG4) on mtDNA to replace G4Hunter predictions and re-test overlap.
  5. Cross-nuclease comparison: test whether other mitochondrial nucleases' known motifs fit the breakpoints equally well — if so, the signal is non-specific.

Limitations

  • Reference-sequence motif scan cannot capture damage-conditioned or structure-conditioned cleavage.
  • Direct-repeat detection used a longest-common-substring proxy (±15 bp); imperfect repeats and microhomology thresholds could shift the exact DR≥5 split, though the qualitative confound is robust.
  • G4Hunter predicts, but does not prove, G4 folding.
  • MitoBreak aggregates deletions of heterogeneous ascertainment; recurrent hotspots were collapsed to unique positions to avoid double-counting (results unchanged).
  • No ENDOG perturbation data were available; causation cannot be tested from coordinates alone.

Conclusion

The seed hypothesis is partially supported for the descriptive claim (deletion breakpoints co-localize with G4/C-rich motifs) but over-interpreted as an ENDOG function: the deletion signal is a direct-repeat confound (absent in non-repeat deletions), and the NUMT arm is refuted in the exact 66,083-genome catalogue and a second independent dataset. The analysis should inform the review as a negative/qualifying computational result, not as support for a new ENDOG breakpoint-placement annotation.
