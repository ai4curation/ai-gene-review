CC   -!- FUNCTION: Electrogenic voltage-dependent transporter that mediates the
CC       transport of a variety of endogenous bioactive amines, cationic
CC       xenobiotics and drugs (PubMed:15448143, PubMed:16099839,
CC       PubMed:16873718, PubMed:17018840, PubMed:17121826, PubMed:20592246,
CC       PubMed:20858707, PubMed:22396231, PubMed:31537831). Utilizes the
CC       physiologic inside-negative membrane potential as a driving force to
CC       facilitate cellular uptake of organic cations (PubMed:15448143,
CC       PubMed:20592246, PubMed:22396231). Functions as a Na(+)- and Cl(-)-
CC       independent bidirectional transporter (PubMed:15448143,
CC       PubMed:16099839, PubMed:22396231, PubMed:31537831). Substrate transport
CC       is pH-dependent and enhanced under acidic condition, which is most
CC       likely the result of allosteric changes in the transporter structure
CC       (PubMed:16873718, PubMed:17018840, PubMed:20592246, PubMed:22396231,
CC       PubMed:31537831). Implicated in monoamine neurotransmitters uptake such
CC       as serotonin, dopamine, adrenaline/epinephrine,
CC       noradrenaline/norepinephrine, histamine and tyramine, thereby
CC       supporting a role in homeostatic regulation of aminergic
CC       neurotransmission in the central nervous system (PubMed:15448143,
CC       PubMed:16099839, PubMed:17018840, PubMed:17121826, PubMed:20858707,
CC       PubMed:22396231). Also responsible for the uptake of bioactive amines
CC       and drugs through the blood-cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) barrier, from the
CC       CSF into choroid plexus epithelial cells, thereby playing a significant
CC       role in the clearance of cationic neurotoxins, xenobiotics and
CC       metabolic waste in the brain (By similarity). Involved in bidirectional
CC       transport of the purine nucleoside adenosine and plays a role in the
CC       regulation of extracellular adenosine concentrations in cardiac
CC       tissues, in particular during ischemia (PubMed:16873718,
CC       PubMed:20592246, PubMed:31537831). May be involved in organic cation
CC       uptake from the tubular lumen into renal tubular cells, thereby
CC       contributing to organic cation reabsorption in the kidney
CC       (PubMed:17018840). Also transports guanidine (PubMed:16099839).
