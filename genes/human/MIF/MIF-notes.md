# MIF (Macrophage Migration Inhibitory Factor) — Curation Notes

## Overview

- **Gene symbol**: `MIF` (HGNC:7097)
- **UniProtKB**: `P14174` (`MIF_HUMAN`, 115 aa; initiator Met-1 is cleaved to expose the N-terminal catalytic Pro-2 residue)
- **PANTHER family**: `PANTHER:PTHR11954` (`D-DOPACHROME DECARBOXYLASE`), subfamily `PANTHER:PTHR11954:SF6` (`MACROPHAGE MIGRATION INHIBITORY FACTOR`), metazoan ancestor `PANTHER:PTN000237513`, eukaryotic root `PANTHER:PTN000237510`
- **Vertebrate paralog**: `DDT` (`UniProtKB:P30046`, D-dopachrome decarboxylase / D-dopachrome tautomerase, also designated MIF-2; `PANTHER:PTHR11954:SF22`, 118 aa, ~34% sequence identity to MIF)

## Biological Summary

1. **CD74–CD44 receptor complex and canonical MIF signaling (`GO:0035691`)**:
   - MIF is a leaderless pro-inflammatory cytokine exported by a non-classical route; its regulated release from monocytes/macrophages depends on the Golgi-associated protein p115 (`USO1`), with which it interacts in the cytoplasm [PMID:19454686, "The depletion of p115 from monocytes/macrophages decreases the release of MIF but not other cytokines following inflammatory stimulation or intracellular bacterial infection"].
   - Extracellular MIF binds the extracellular domain of the cell-surface form of the MHC class II invariant chain `CD74` (`UniProtKB:P04233`), and CD74 is required for MIF-induced ERK1/2 (`MAPK3`/`MAPK1`) activation, cell proliferation and PGE2 production [PMID:12782713, "MIF binds to the extracellular domain of CD74, and CD74 is required for MIF-induced activation of the extracellular signal-regulated kinase-1/2 MAP kinase cascade, cell proliferation, and PGE2 production"]. Soluble CD74 binds MIF with a dissociation constant of about 9 nM by surface plasmon resonance [PMID:12782713, "A recombinant, soluble form of CD74 binds MIF with a dissociation constant of approximately 9 x 10-9 Kd, as defined by surface plasmon resonance"].
   - CD74 alone binds MIF but cannot transduce the signal: MIF-induced ERK1/2 phosphorylation requires co-expression of full-length `CD44` (`UniProtKB:P16070`), and MIF binding is accompanied by serine phosphorylation of CD74 and CD44 and Src-dependent ERK activation; CD74 and CD44 are both needed for MIF protection from apoptosis [PMID:17045821, "CD74 alone mediated MIF binding; however, MIF-induced ERK1 and ERK2 kinase phosphorylation required the coexpression of full-length CD44"; PMID:17045821, "These data establish CD44 as an integral member of the CD74 receptor complex leading to MIF signal transduction"]. (This paper is abstract-only in the cache; the kinase responsible for the serine phosphorylation is not named there.)
   - The paralog `DDT` (MIF-2) also binds the CD74 ectodomain with high affinity, reduces MIF binding to CD74 in a competition assay, and activates ERK1/2 and downstream proinflammatory pathways [PMID:21817065, "We show that recombinant D-DT protein binds CD74 with high affinity, leading to activation of ERK1/2 MAP kinase and downstream proinflammatory pathways"; PMID:21817065, "D-DT reduced MIF binding to the CD74 ectodomain in a dose-dependent manner, with a maximal effect of ∼50% compared with MIF"]. The measured dissociation constant was 5.42 × 10−9 M for D-DT against 1.40 × 10−9 M for MIF in the same assay [PMID:21817065, "revealed a high-affinity binding interaction between D-DT and the MIF receptor ( K D of 5.42 × 10 −9 M) ( Fig. 3 B ), albeit 60% lower than for MIF ( K D = 1.40"].

2. **Non-cognate chemokine receptor agonism (`CXCR2` and `CXCR4`)**:
   - MIF also acts as a non-cognate ligand of the CXC chemokine receptor `CXCR2` (`UniProtKB:P25025`), with an ELR-like motif, and drives leukocyte recruitment through it; a MIF/CXCR4 (`UniProtKB:P61073`) pathway is also invoked [PMID:19602265, "MIF acts as a non-cognate ligand of CXCR2 and drives leukocyte recruitment through CXCR2"]. This is a review statement in a breast cancer paper citing earlier primary work, not a primary demonstration. Antibody blocking identified CXCR2 as the major receptor for MIF-mediated chemotaxis of peripheral blood mononuclear cells [PMID:20534506, "CXCR2 is the major receptor for MIF-mediated chemotaxis of peripheral blood mononuclear cells"].

3. **Tautomerase / isomerase catalytic activity (`GO:0050178`, `GO:0004167`)**:
   - MIF is a homotrimer [UniProt P14174, "Homotrimer (PubMed:23776208, PubMed:8610159)"]. The initiator methionine is removed, exposing Pro-2, which UniProt annotates (by similarity to mouse) as the catalytic proton acceptor; Lys-33, Ile-65 and Asn-98 are annotated substrate-binding residues from crystal structures (UniProt FT ACT_SITE 2 and BINDING 33, 65, 98).
   - MIF has phenylpyruvate tautomerase (EC 5.3.2.1) and dopachrome tautomerase (EC 5.3.3.12) activity in vitro, but its physiological substrate is unknown [UniProt P14174, "Has phenylpyruvate tautomerase and dopachrome tautomerase activity (in vitro), but the physiological substrate is not known"]. Inhibitors binding the catalytic site, or an allosteric site, also block MIF biological activities [PMID:17526494, "to validate that inhibition of a catalytic site could produce therapeutic benefits"; PMID:20534506, "inhibit the catalytic and chemotactic functions of the proinflammatory protein, macrophage migration inhibitory factor (MIF)"].

4. **Intracellular interactions**:
   - Cytoplasmic MIF interacts with `COPS5`/`JAB1` (`UniProtKB:Q92905`) and inhibits Jab1- and stimulus-enhanced AP-1 activity and Jab1-dependent cell-cycle regulation [PMID:11089976, "MIF inhibits Jab1- and stimulus-enhanced AP-1 activity"]; MIF treatment overcomes p53 activity and suppresses its transcriptional activator function [PMID:10562313, "Treatment with MIF overcame p53 activity in three different biological assays"]; and MIF binds `BCL2L11` (Bim) and partially inhibits Bim-induced apoptosis [PMID:18677096, "exogenous expression of MIF partially inhibited Bim-induced apoptosis in HEK293 cells"].

  - Human `MIF` (`UniProtKB:P14174`, `genes/human/MIF/MIF-goa.tsv`) was annotated in 2003 (`PMID:12782713`) to the parent term `GO:0007166` (*cell surface receptor signaling pathway*, `IDA`) before `GO:0035691` was created in the ontology, and that annotation was never updated to the specific pathway term.
