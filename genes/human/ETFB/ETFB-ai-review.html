Gene Description

ETFB is the beta subunit of electron-transfer flavoprotein (ETF), a soluble mitochondrial-matrix heterodimer with ETFA. The complex receives electrons from flavoprotein dehydrogenases, including medium-chain acyl-CoA dehydrogenase (MCAD), glutaryl-CoA dehydrogenase (GCDH) and sarcosine dehydrogenase (SARDH), linking fatty-acid oxidation, amino-acid catabolism and choline degradation to respiration. ETF transfers these electrons to ETFDH, which reduces ubiquinone in the inner mitochondrial membrane; this route supplies the ubiquinone pool alongside respiratory complexes I and II. ETFB binds AMP and contributes to the architecture surrounding the complex’s single redox-active FAD. Its recognition-loop surface anchors MCAD in the ETF–MCAD complex, helping couple partner engagement to movement of the FAD-bearing domain. AMP is thought to stabilize the beta-subunit fold. Pathogenic ETFB variants cause multiple acyl-CoA dehydrogenase deficiency, also called glutaric acidemia type II.

ETFB: electron transfer, partner recognition and source limits

ETFB contributes to the soluble ETFA–ETFB electron carrier in the mitochondrial matrix. The canonical human beta product is 255 residues; the 346-residue product has a different N terminus. Initiator-methionine removal gives the modeled canonical chain 2–255 and is distinct from ETFA's 19-residue transit-peptide cleavage. The experiments below do not establish equivalent import, assembly or activity of the longer ETFB product.

Human carrier structure and chemistry

PMID8962055, human ETF structure: Experimental Procedures describes recombinant human alpha/beta ETF expressed in E. coli and its crystallographic analysis. Results, the overall-structure and cofactor-site sections, resolve beta density from Leu4 to Ile255. The FAD-containing cleft spans both subunits, with most FAD contacts in alpha and beta Phe41/Tyr16 near the flavin. AMP is buried within beta domain III; a role in stabilizing the fold is proposed, rather than AMP catalysis. The Introduction identifies fatty-acid, amino-acid and choline-catabolic donor classes, including glutaryl-, isovaleryl-, short/branched-chain, sarcosine and dimethylglycine dehydrogenases. That passage is pathway synthesis, not a new assay of every donor. The actual body and captions were read; figure pixels and raw coordinates were not reanalyzed. The paper's introductory alpha Q21 differs from the current alpha sequence at that particular position; no universal residue offset is inferred, and beta positions retain the paper's numbering.

PMID25416781, human ETF methylation and activity: Experimental Procedures, cloning and protein production, specifies human ORFs, mature untagged Δ19-alpha and full-length N-His6-beta coexpressed in bacteria. The electron-transfer assay and Figure 5 use MCAD/octanoyl-CoA or GCDH/glutaryl-CoA with DCIP as the final acceptor. Beta Lys200/Lys203 methylation and site mutations change electron transfer by assembled ETF. METTL20/ETFBKMT performs methylation; ETFB is its substrate. Isolated beta had stability problems and was not the preparation used to establish independent carrier turnover. The actual Methods, Results and captions were read, without figure-pixel inspection. The assay establishes the donor–ETF redox step, not complete fatty-acid flux or direct ETFDH/ubiquinone reduction. The proposed explanation linking methylation to partner recognition is mechanistic interpretation.

The human ETF–MCAD structure, PMID15159392, reports a recognition-loop anchor coupled to a mobile FAD domain. PMID15975918 reports human beta-E165A structural and kinetic effects with MCAD and dimethylglycine dehydrogenase. Their identifiers and titles were checked against primary registry records and their original abstracts were read. Publisher body and PDF routes returned access failures; detailed Methods, figure pixels and historical construct sequences remain uninspected. These positive reports support a beta contribution to donor recognition and conformational dynamics, without establishing identical docking for every donor or activity of the 346-residue product.
