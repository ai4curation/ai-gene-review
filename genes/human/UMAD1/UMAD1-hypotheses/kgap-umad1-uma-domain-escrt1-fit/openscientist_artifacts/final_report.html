UMAD1 (human, UniProt C9J7I0): can the UMA domain complete an ESCRT-I complex?

Hypothesis slug: kgap-umad1-uma-domain-escrt1-fit Source: genes/human/UMAD1/UMAD1-ai-review.yaml, focus type free_text Analyses run 2026-11-24. Provenance: provenance/plot_1.png to provenance/plot_4.png, provenance/evidence_matrix.tsv, provenance/go_decision_table.tsv, with the executed code and its printed output recorded in the iteration log.

Summary

Verdict: weakly supported. Human UMAD1 carries a genuine UMA domain (PROSITE PS51497, residues 86-134), the same module that MVB12A, MVB12B and UBAP1 use to complete ESCRT-I. The residues that MVB12A buries against the ESCRT-I headpiece in PDB 6VME are preferentially conserved in UMAD1: identity at the 19 contact positions is 47 percent against a 24 percent background in the same region (Mann-Whitney one-sided p = 0.0231, CLES 0.65; Fisher OR 2.92, p = 0.052). The five most deeply buried MVB12A residues, V218-P219-F220-T221-L222, are identical in UMAD1 as V89-P90-F91-T92-L93. Across 150 vertebrate orthologs, P90 and F91 are the only two positions invariant in every species, and they map onto P219 and F220, the two MVB12A residues with the largest buried contact area. On sequence and interface-projection grounds, UMAD1 is a credible candidate fourth ESCRT-I subunit.

Three results keep it from being more than a candidate. First, there is no direct binding evidence. IntAct PSICQUIC returns 16 binary evidences for C9J7I0 and none of them involve TSG101, VPS37A-D, MVB12A or MVB12B. The single ESCRT-relevant record is an affinity-capture hit in VPS28, PTPN23, CEP55 and TFG pulldowns from one proteome-scale survey (PMID: 26496610), whose authors state that their network "is dominated by weak, substoichiometric interactions". Second, the evolutionary signal is domain-wide and not interface-specific: the projected contact face is more conserved than the rest of the protein (0.723 vs 0.592, p = 0.019) but indistinguishable from the rest of the UMA domain (0.781, p = 0.590), so purifying selection on a folded module explains the data as well as selection on an ESCRT-I interface. Third, the modelling clause of the hypothesis could not be executed. AlphaFold-Multimer is not runnable in this environment, and the named fallback, rigid structural superposition, fails at chance level: the AlphaFold C9J7I0 monomer fits MVB12A chain A at 6.29 Å CA RMSD against a random-window null of 6.42 ± 1.10 Å (empirical p = 0.526), and placing the predicted UMA domain into the site by that transform creates 216 heavy-atom pairs below 2.2 Å against 0 for the experimental MVB12A chain.

The VPS37 paralog clause of the hypothesis has a clean negative answer. The MVB12A contact surface on VPS37B (L103, L106, R153, V154, K155, E157, K158, L159, E161, M162) is 60, 70 and 50 percent identical in VPS37A, VPS37C and VPS37D against full-length identities of 35, 44 and 40 percent, and the charged core R153/K155/E157/K158 plus L159 is identical in all four. No paralog preference is structurally predictable. Paralog selectivity does exist in cells: UBAP1-containing ESCRT-I holds VPS37A and not VPS37C (PMID: 21757351), which locates the determinant outside the headpiece contact residues this analysis can read.

Key findings

F1. UMAD1 conserves the MVB12A ESCRT-I contact face above its local background

Contact mapping was done directly on PDB 6VME, the human ESCRT-I heterotetramer headpiece at 2.19 Å, which contains six copies of TSG101 313-388, VPS37B 99-165, VPS28 16-117 and MVB12A 204-223 in the asymmetric unit. Heavy-atom contacts under 4.5 Å define 19 MVB12A interface positions (204-212, 214-223). In the copy built around MVB12A chain A, the partners are TSG101 chain I (263 heavy-atom pairs under 4.5 Å), VPS28 chain Y (60) and VPS37B chain N (56). F220 alone accounts for 69 contact atoms, the largest of any residue.

Needleman-Wunsch alignment with BLOSUM62 and affine gaps (open -11, extend -1) of MVB12A 196-273 against UMAD1 66-137, UBAP1 1-75 and MVB12B 235-312 gives UMAD1 an identity of 9 of 19 at the interface positions against 12 of 51 at non-interface positions in the same region. Mean BLOSUM62 score is +1.63 at the interface and -0.92 at the background. The one-sided Mann-Whitney test on BLOSUM62 scores gives p = 0.0231 with a common-language effect size of 0.65. UBAP1, which is an experimentally confirmed ESCRT-I subunit and so works as a positive control for how much interface identity a real subunit needs, scores lower than UMAD1 (26 percent interface identity, p = 0.201). That comparison cuts both ways: it shows the UMAD1 score is not unusually weak for a genuine subunit, and it shows that high identity to MVB12A is not a requirement for subunit status, which reduces how much the UMAD1 score can carry on its own.

The AlphaFold monomer places the best-ordered part of the protein exactly over the UMA domain: mean pLDDT in 86-134 is 74.9 ± 8.4 against a whole-protein mean of 61.4.

F2. The VPS37 contact surface is the same in all four paralogs, so no paralog preference can be predicted

The seed hypothesis asks which VPS37 paralogs UMAD1 is most compatible with. The structural answer is that the question cannot be settled from the headpiece. MVB12A contacts ten VPS37B residues (L103, L106, R153, V154, K155, E157, K158, L159, E161, M162) and twenty-six TSG101 residues (316-321, 325, 328, 329, 331, 332, 335, 336, 339, 370, 374, 377, 381, 382, 386-388). Identity at the ten VPS37B contact positions is 60 percent for VPS37A, 70 percent for VPS37C and 50 percent for VPS37D, each above the corresponding full-length identity of 35, 44 and 40 percent. The charged core R153, K155, E157, K158 and the hydrophobic L159 are identical in all four paralogs.

This is a negative result with a concrete consequence for curation: any statement of the form "UMAD1 prefers VPS37X" is unsupported by structure. The determinant of paralog pairing lies elsewhere, in the VPS37 N-terminal regions outside the headpiece, in expression overlap, or in accessory partners. PMID: 21757351 shows that the pairing is nonetheless real in cells: "UBAP1 is part of a complex that contains a fraction of total cellular TSG101 and that also contains VPS37A but not VPS37C."

F3. The experimental evidence base for UMAD1 is one high-throughput pulldown

UniProt C9J7I0 is Swiss-Prot reviewed with protein existence level 1 ("evidence at protein level"), 137 residues, zero GO cross-references and four references, all genome or cDNA sequencing papers. IntAct PSICQUIC returns 16 binary evidences for C9J7I0 against 62 for MVB12A, 25 for MVB12B and 19 for UBAP1. Four of the sixteen are anti-tag co-immunoprecipitation records from a single study, the BAC GFP-tagged bait library in HeLa (PMID: 26496610): UMAD1 is detected in pulldowns of VPS28, PTPN23 (HD-PTP), CEP55 and TFG. The remaining records are GABARAPL1 and TH isoform 3 by yeast two-hybrid (PMID: 32296183) and RBCK1, TNIP1, BIRC2 and TRAF2 by Virotrap (PMID: 30561431).

No co-immunoprecipitation, two-hybrid or structural record links UMAD1 to TSG101, VPS37A-D, MVB12A or MVB12B. PubMed returns no primary paper on UMAD1 protein function. The gene appears in genetic association and methylation studies only: Alzheimer age at onset (PMID: 36738287), Lewy body pathology (PMID: 39424714), rheumatoid arthritis interstitial lung disease at the RPA3-UMAD1 locus (PMID: 32737115), primary biliary cholangitis (PMID: 29773854) and placental methylation in fetal overgrowth (PMID: 36585686).

The VPS28, PTPN23 and CEP55 co-occurrence is the pattern expected for an ESCRT-associated protein, since all three operate in ESCRT-dependent sorting and abscission, and PTPN23 is the same HD-PTP that binds UBAP1 in PMID: 21757351. It is bait-side only, from one dataset, with no reciprocal pulldown and no reported stoichiometry.

F4. The whole UMA domain is under selection, not the contact face specifically

150 species-deduplicated vertebrate UMAD1 orthologs were retrieved from UniProtKB with a length filter of 110-175 aa and aligned to human C9J7I0 with a hand-implemented affine Needleman-Wunsch aligner (biopython is unavailable in this executor). Mean global identity to human is 60.9 percent. Per-residue identity fractions are 0.723 ± 0.325 at the 18 projected contact positions (n = 18), 0.592 ± 0.345 over the rest of the protein (n = 119, Mann-Whitney one-sided p = 1.89e-02, CLES 0.65) and 0.781 ± 0.319 over the rest of the UMA domain 86-134 (n = 40, p = 0.590, CLES 0.48).

The contact face beats the rest of the protein and loses to the rest of the domain. The unit under purifying selection is the whole UMA module. That is the expected signature if the domain folds and binds as one piece, and it is equally the expected signature if the domain binds something other than ESCRT-I. Individual anchor identities are L81 0.207, M82 0.820, L86 0.993, S87 0.793, D88 0.987, V89 0.900, P90 1.000, F91 1.000, T92 0.340, L93 0.993. Only P90 and F91 are invariant in all 150 species, and they correspond to MVB12A P219 and F220, the two deepest contacts in 6VME. That coincidence is the single strongest sequence-level argument in favour of the hypothesis. It shows the UMAD1 UMA retains a functional binding face of the MVB12/UBAP1 type. It does not identify the partner.

F5. Orthogonal cell-biology and taxonomy checks do not separate UMAD1 from real ESCRT-I subunits

Three checks were run that could have refuted ESCRT-I membership independently of the structural projection. None did.

UMAD1 is the only reviewed human UMA-domain protein with zero GO annotations. Every other human ESCRT-I component carries 10 to 42 GO terms: MVB12A 21, MVB12B 18, UBAP1 10, VPS37A 12, VPS37B 19, VPS37C 14, VPS37D 12, TSG101 42, VPS28 19. The zero-GO state is a curation gap, since UniProt already accepts protein-level existence for C9J7I0.

Human Protein Atlas antibody immunofluorescence reports UMAD1 as nucleoplasm only, with no vesicle term, while MVB12A, MVB12B, UBAP1, VPS37A, VPS37C, VPS37D and VPS28 all report vesicles. The check is weak, because TSG101 itself reports nucleoli, plasma membrane and cytosol with no vesicle term, so the assay does not separate ESCRT-I subunits from non-subunits, and UMAD1 has one unvalidated antibody.

HPA RNA tissue specificity for UMAD1 is "low tissue specificity", the same as MVB12A, UBAP1 and VPS37A-D, so UMAD1 is not testis-restricted and is co-expressed widely enough for complex formation to be possible.

UniProtKB entry counts by gene name and clade give UMAD1 593 vertebrate entries and zero in arthropods, nematodes, fungi or plants. MVB12A, a confirmed subunit, shows the same pattern (366/0/0/0/0). MVB12B is 731/8/3/0, UBAP1 601/9/0/0, VPS37A 742/11/0/6, TSG101 676/30/7/17 and VPS28 482/56/10/194. Vertebrate restriction is therefore not evidence against subunit status. These are gene-name counts and not a phylogenetically reconciled orthology search.

An independent protein-existence check at neXtProt could not be run: https://api.nextprot.org/entry/NX_C9J7I0/protein-existence.json returns an ExPASy archive HTML page, so the service appears retired.

F6. The superposition fallback fails at chance level and cannot settle the modelling clause

AlphaFold-Multimer cannot be run here: no GPU pipeline is installed and no prediction service is reachable from the executor. The hypothesis names rigid structural superposition as the fallback, so that test was run and reported as executed.

Needleman-Wunsch mapping of MVB12A onto C9J7I0 gives 18 contact-residue pairs, including the identities V218/V89, P219/P90, F220/F91, T221/T92 and L222/L93. Kabsch superposition of AF-C9J7I0-F1-model_v6 onto MVB12A chain A over those 18 CA pairs gives RMSD 6.29 Å. A null built from 500 random equal-length CA windows of the same model gives 6.42 ± 1.10 Å with a minimum of 4.81 Å, so the empirical p value is 0.526. Transforming the whole predicted UMA domain 86-134 into the complex by that superposition produces 216 heavy-atom pairs below 2.2 Å with TSG101, VPS37B and VPS28, spread over 23 residues (86, 90-105, 109-114), against 0 such clashes for the experimental MVB12A chain A in the same site. Mean pLDDT is 61.2 for UMAD1 66-85, 74.8 for 86-134 and 61.4 over the whole protein.

The failure is geometric and does not refute binding. The AlphaFold monomer predicts the UMAD1 UMA as a compact fold, while the MVB12A UMA contributes to the headpiece as an extended chain whose conformation is created by the complex. PMID: 17442384 establishes the architecture that makes this clear: "The core complex is nearly 18 nm long and consists of a headpiece attached to a 13 nm stalk." MVB12A would fail the same test if modelled alone. What the result shows is that monomer-model superposition cannot answer the question, and that a conformational change on binding would be required.

Mechanistic model and interpretation

The immediate molecular claim under test is narrow. It is that residues 86-134 of UMAD1 bind the TSG101 steadiness box, VPS37 and VPS28 in the same register MVB12A 204-223 uses in PDB 6VME, completing a 1:1:1:1 ESCRT-I heterotetramer. The UMA domain has no catalytic activity, so the claim is purely about assembly, over a stretch of roughly 20 to 50 residues.

Everything else attributed to ESCRT-I is downstream and would follow only if assembly were demonstrated: ubiquitinated cargo sorting into multivesicular bodies, EGFR degradation, HIV-1 budding, cytokinetic abscission, and the division of labour between subcomplexes described for UBAP1 against MVB12 in PMID: 21757351. None of that has been tested for UMAD1. The genetic association and methylation hits at the RPA3-UMAD1 locus sit several steps further out, and the locus contains a second gene.

The dashed links are the ones the evidence does not close. Two of them compete: the conserved UMA face binds ESCRT-I, or it binds something else that is equally conserved. The data assembled here cannot choose between them, because the selection signal covers the whole domain and because the only ESCRT-relevant interaction record is a single bait-side hit.

Conflicts and alternatives

The conservation signal is not interface-specific. The ortholog test shows the projected contact face is no more conserved than the rest of the UMA domain (p = 0.590). A UMA domain conserved as a folded module, with P90 and F91 as structural rather than interface anchors, explains the data as well as the ESCRT-I hypothesis.

Paralog comparison cuts both ways. The high contact-face identity to MVB12A is measured against MVB12A, but UBAP1, a confirmed subunit, scores only 26 percent at the same positions. Identity to MVB12A is therefore not a requirement for subunit status, which reduces how much the UMAD1 score establishes.

The locus is shared. UMAD1 aliases include RPA3-AS1 and RPA3OS, and the gene overlaps the RPA3 locus antisense. Every genetic association reported there is attributable to RPA3 regulation as readily as to the UMAD1 protein. PMID: 30146317 shows the mouse Umad1 locus is a long-range target of the Evf2 ultraconserved enhancer lncRNA from 1.6 Mb away, which is a reason to treat locus-level signals here as regulatory until shown otherwise.

The interaction set is thin and could be an artifact. The four UMAD1 hits in PMID: 26496610 come from one BAC GFP-tagged bait study in HeLa with no reciprocal pulldown. Small, low-abundance proteins are common contaminants of GFP pulldowns. The coherence of the set (VPS28, PTPN23, CEP55 and TFG are all ESCRT or ESCRT-adjacent) argues against pure contamination without establishing stoichiometric binding.

ESCRT-I is not one complex. PMID: 21757351 shows UBAP1-containing ESCRT-I holds VPS37A and not VPS37C, and functions in EGFR sorting and not cytokinesis. A curation statement of the form "UMAD1 is an ESCRT-I subunit" is underspecified, and this analysis cannot specify the subcomplex because the VPS37 contact surface is conserved in all four paralogs.

The failed superposition is not a refutation. The 216 clashes and the chance-level RMSD arise from docking an unbound compact monomer model into a site occupied by an extended chain. MVB12A would fail the same test if modelled alone.

Limitations and knowledge gaps

Does UMAD1 bind TSG101 or any VPS37 at all? Checked: IntAct PSICQUIC for C9J7I0 (16 binary evidences) and for MVB12A, MVB12B and UBAP1. No record links UMAD1 to TSG101, VPS37A-D or the MVB12 paralogs. This gap determines the verdict. A reciprocal endogenous co-immunoprecipitation, or in vitro reconstitution with recombinant TSG101 coiled coil, VPS28 and VPS37B, would resolve it.

What does AlphaFold-Multimer predict for the four-chain complex with UMAD1 in place of MVB12A? Checked: no GPU pipeline and no reachable prediction service from this executor, so the prediction was not run. The monomer-superposition fallback is uninformative for the geometric reason given in F6. ipTM and interface PAE for TSG101/VPS28/VPS37B/UMAD1, compared against the same calculation with MVB12A and with a scrambled UMAD1 UMA, would resolve it at prediction level.

Is the C-terminal half of the UMA domain (UMAD1 95-134) an interface at all? Checked: PDB 6VME resolves MVB12A only to residue 223, and PMID: 17442384 shows the yeast Mvb12 chain runs the full 13 nm stalk. No human structure covers the equivalent region, so half the domain has no experimental interface to project onto. A longer human ESCRT-I structure, or crosslinking mass spectrometry, would resolve it.

Is UMAD1 protein present where ESCRT-I operates? Checked: HPA reports nucleoplasm by antibody immunofluorescence and low tissue specificity by RNA. The antibody is unvalidated and TSG101 fails the same vesicle test, so the result is unusable either way. The independent protein-existence check at neXtProt could not be run because the API returns an ExPASy archive page and the service appears retired. Endogenous tagging with imaging, or targeted mass spectrometry in a cell line with measured UMAD1 transcript, would resolve it.

Does the UMAD1 UMA domain fold in isolation? Checked: AlphaFold mean pLDDT is 74.8 over 86-134 and 61.4 over the whole protein, which is modest confidence rather than confident order. Circular dichroism or NMR on the recombinant domain, alone and with the TSG101 coiled coil, would resolve it.

Which VPS37 paralog, if any? Checked: the MVB12A contact surface is conserved in VPS37A, VPS37B, VPS37C and VPS37D, so no preference is predictable from the headpiece contacts, while PMID: 21757351 shows selectivity exists in cells. Pulldowns of each VPS37 paralog probed for UMAD1 would resolve it.

Method limitations. Biopython was unavailable in the execution environment, so the pairwise aligner and the PDB parser were written by hand and validated by exact substring matching of each chain against its UniProt sequence. The alignment register around MVB12A 211-217 is ambiguous (two registers score 35 and 34), affecting the D216 to S87 assignment and not the P219/F220 anchors, which lie in an unambiguously aligned block. The ortholog set is 150 vertebrate UniProtKB entries selected by gene name and length, not a phylogenetically reconciled orthology set, so paralog contamination cannot be excluded. Proposed follow-up experiments and actions

Ranked by how much each separates the hypothesis from the alternative that UMAD1 is a UMA-fold protein with a different partner.

  1. AlphaFold-Multimer or AlphaFold 3 on the four-chain assembly. Run UMAD1 against TSG101/VPS28 with each of VPS37A, VPS37B, VPS37C and VPS37D in turn. Report ipTM and interface PAE, with MVB12A and UBAP1 as positive controls and a shuffled UMAD1 UMA as negative. This is the cheapest test, it is the one the seed hypothesis asked for first, and it is the only one that also addresses the paralog clause.
  2. Reconstitution. Co-express recombinant TSG101 (steadiness box plus coiled coil, as in the 6VME construct), VPS28, VPS37B and UMAD1 86-134, then run size-exclusion chromatography with multi-angle light scattering. A 1:1:1:1 species is decisive for subunit status; failure with MVB12A run in parallel as the positive control is decisive against.
  3. Reciprocal endogenous co-immunoprecipitation. Pull down TSG101 and each VPS37 paralog from a cell line with measurable UMAD1 expression and probe for UMAD1, then pull UMAD1 and probe the reverse. This also tests the single VPS28 hit from PMID: 26496610.
  4. Anchor mutagenesis. P90A/F91A in UMAD1, tested in whichever binding assay turns positive. These are the only two residues invariant across 150 vertebrate orthologs and the two deepest contacts in MVB12A, so loss of binding would tie the evolutionary signal to the interface directly.
  5. Crosslinking mass spectrometry of endogenous ESCRT-I, which reports which UMAD1 residues sit near TSG101 and VPS37 without needing a stable purified complex, and which covers UMAD1 95-134, the region no structure reaches.
  6. Functional readout, only if binding is confirmed: UMAD1 depletion followed by EGFR degradation kinetics and multivesicular body morphology, with UBAP1 and MVB12A depletion as comparators, since PMID: 21757351 shows these subunits have separable requirements.
