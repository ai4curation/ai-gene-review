## Caveolar organization and muscle disease

The muscle membrane role is supported by CAV3-dependent caveola abundance, human sarcolemmal localization and cavin interactions. The cardiac overexpression study uses mouse transgenics and rodent cardiomyocytes, with blinded ultrastructural measurements; its caveola measurements are separate from infarct protection, Akt/GSK signaling and pharmacological inhibition. The human GOA source evidence code remains untouched, while the experimental species is explicit. Increased expression is not assumed to be uniformly beneficial [PMID:18936328].

The original dominant muscle-disease report describes eight patients from two families with severe muscle CAV3 deficiency. Its abstract proposes altered oligomerization/caveola formation as a mechanism; that proposal is not presented as a completed oligomer-structure experiment [PMID:9537420]. The rippling-muscle-disease family report supplies clinical context but its accessible abstract does not resolve the exact contraction mechanism [PMID:17524427]. Human myopathy-associated CAV3 constructs show variant-dependent surface/Golgi localization and different effects on growth-factor signaling, with an important distinction between wild type and retained mutants [PMID:20472890].

The PTRF study establishes secondary caveolin deficiency in human muscle and uses sequence-verified human CAV3 in reciprocal association experiments. PTRF disease is not attributed to CAV3 merely because CAV3 protein is affected [PMID:19726876]. CAVIN4 interaction and trafficking experiments likewise separate binding, localization dependency and downstream ERK/hypertrophy effects; a deletion can preserve CAV3 association while changing its trafficking [PMID:24567387; PMID:26497963].

The broad adaptor rows are refined to protein-macromolecule adaptor activity because the hERG/NEDD4-2 experiments establish coordinated recruitment of protein partners. This is not a claim that every pairwise interaction is an adaptor mechanism. A stable caveolar structural scaffold is conceptually distinct; an additional scaffold annotation is left as a question pending a complete target-specific and same-role comparator assessment. No NEW assertion is introduced merely to fill a core-coverage advisory.

## Channel mechanisms and physiological limits

The calcium-channel study uses human CAV3 and human CaV3.1/CaV3.2 constructs in HEK293 cells and mouse cardiomyocytes. Both channels associate with CAV3, but the functional decrease is selective for CaV3.2 in the tested conditions. Surface abundance was not reduced, and the N-terminal CAV3 region participates in association. CAV3 depletion removes cAMP-dependent augmentation without simply lowering basal T-type current. These results support calcium-channel regulator activity, without equating CAV3 with the pore, a calcium sensor or an inhibitor of every calcium channel [PMID:21084288].

The potassium-channel study combines heterologous hERG experiments with native rat/rabbit association and neonatal rat depletion. CAV3 recruits NEDD4-2 and promotes turnover of mature surface hERG; channel PY-motif mutations and ligase depletion limit the proposed mechanism. Current changes reflect surface-channel abundance rather than a demonstrated direct pore block or altered voltage-dependent gating. CAV3 is not the ubiquitin ligase or a protease. The inhibitor term permits a binding-dependent reduction in channel function and does not require physical pore occlusion [PMID:22879586].

The sodium studies were evaluated using the canonical caches and the accessible original primary article. Human CAV3 and human SCN5A/Nav1.5 were tested in HEK293 cells. F97C/S141R increased persistent late current in the first study, but wild-type CAV3 did not measurably alter current versus vector. The second study used tagged human CAV3 variants with SCN5A Q1077del; the variant increases were significant, the smaller wild-type increase was not, and peak-current/tested gating differences were not significant. Controlled coimmunoprecipitation supports association, with mutants retaining it; it does not prove a purified direct interface [PMID:17060380; PMID:17275750].

Human right-ventricular sections in the first study directly support sarcolemmal localization. Its heart-rate discussion, however, extrapolates an untested T78M variant from a separate SCN5A pacemaking mechanism. That specific heart-rate extension is marked over-annotated. This does not dispute the measured late-current result or assert that CAV3 can never influence heart rate [PMID:17060380].

Ventricular repolarization, depolarization and contraction-linked annotations remain undecided at the source-specific physiological level. A native cardiomyocyte or whole-heart assay is not an absolute prerequisite: a sufficiently demonstrated mechanism could support a process inference. Here the exact wild-type/native mechanism and the connection from mutant late current to those particular processes remain unresolved. The anonymous SIDS cohort and the clinical limitations do not erase the molecular result, but neither do they establish every downstream physiological process [PMID:17060380; PMID:17275750].

## Dystrophin association and membrane repair

The beta-dystroglycan study supports association with the cytoplasmic tail and competition involving dystrophin. A separate biochemical study finds that residual CAV3 separates from the stable purified dystrophin complex, and that CAV3 sarcolemmal localization persists independently of dystrophin/sarcoglycan defects. Stable integral membership and transient association are different claims. The current GO complex definition explicitly includes caveolin-3; the existing component and complex-binding annotations are retained non-core with the distinction stated, rather than treating the purification result as universal absence of interaction [PMID:10988290; PMID:9607328].

Dysferlin association is independently reported in normal human muscle biopsies, with abnormal localization in affected muscle. The historical WW-domain/motif proposal is not accepted as verified CAV3 architecture [PMID:11532985]. The complete repair paper uses mouse Cav3 constructs, C2C12 cells and mouse muscle. Controlled association, MG53/dysferlin mislocalization, transgenic and acute P104L perturbations, and R26Q/C71W comparisons support coordinated trafficking and repair effects. MG53 is the injury-responsive recruiter in those experiments; CAV3 is not thereby shown to catalyze membrane fusion or sense oxidation. Wild-type overexpression also has effects, so mutant comparisons do not justify a simple universal dosage rule [PMID:19380584]. The complete Reactome event provides a human pathway context for the DYSF–CAV3–TRIM72 assembly, with its underlying experimental species kept explicit [Reactome:R-HSA-5263633].
