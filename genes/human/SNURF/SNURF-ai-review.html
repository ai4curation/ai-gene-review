Gene Description

SNURF (SNRPN upstream reading frame protein) is a highly basic 71-amino-acid nuclear protein encoded by the upstream open reading frame of the bicistronic SNURF-SNRPN transcript on chromosome 15q11-q13; the downstream ORF of the same mRNA encodes the spliceosomal SmN protein (SNRPN). The locus lies in the Prader-Willi syndrome imprinting centre and is expressed only from the paternal allele, and both proteins are made in normal but not Prader-Willi cells. The ORF is conserved across eutherian mammals with substitutions concentrated at wobble positions, which indicates selection for coding function, and the protein carries a conserved basic nuclear localization motif. A GFP-tagged protein localizes to the nucleus and antibody staining places it in nuclear speckles. Its molecular function and biological role are unknown. It is unrelated to the RING finger protein RNF4, which was originally also named SNURF.

Core Functions

Small basic nuclear protein of unknown molecular function, translated from the upstream ORF of the imprinted SNURF-SNRPN bicistronic mRNA and localized to nuclear speckles. No molecular activity or process has been demonstrated, so only its location is stated.

Suggested Questions for Experts

Q: Does the SNURF protein itself (as opposed to the SNURF-SNRPN transcript and the imprinting centre) contribute to any Prader-Willi syndrome phenotype?

Q: Does the arginine-rich 71-aa protein bind RNA or speckle components (SR proteins, snRNPs), consistent with its nuclear-speckle localization?

Suggested Experiments

Experiment: Introduce a start-codon or frameshift mutation into the mouse Snurf ORF that leaves the imprinting centre and Snrpn translation intact, and phenotype the mice. Combine with proximity labelling or RNA crosslinking of tagged endogenous SNURF in neurons.

Hypothesis: The SNURF protein has a function separate from the imprinting-centre DNA and the SNRPN product.

SNURF (Q9Y675) review notes

2026-10-08 — MICROPROTEINS Tier 2 review (claude-code)

Identity

  • HGNC SNURF (HGNC:11171), UniProt Q9Y675 (Swiss-Prot, PE1), 71 aa, highly basic
    (~17% Arg), conserved nuclear localization motif (KRRR). Encoded by the upstream ORF of
    the bicistronic SNURF-SNRPN transcript in the Prader-Willi imprinting centre (15q11-q13);
    the downstream ORF encodes SmN (SNRPN). Separate UniProt entry and HGNC symbol, so it has
    its own folder (CLAUDE.md alt-ORF convention).
  • Symbol collision: "SNURF" was also the original name of RNF4 (small nuclear RING
    finger protein, 194 aa) PMID:9710597. Most PubMed hits for "SNURF" are RNF4 papers
    (PMID:10617653, 10934038, 11319220, 15707587 ...). None of them concern Q9Y675.

Evidence for the SNRPN-uORF protein

  • Only primary paper: Gray et al. 1999 PMID:10318933; GFP fusion PMID:10318933; protein
    detected in normal but not PWS cells. Function unknown PMID:10318933. Wobble-position-biased substitution pattern argues
    for coding selection.
  • HPA immunofluorescence: nuclear speckles, reliability "Supported" (proteinatlas.org JSON for
    ENSG00000273173, queried 2026-10-08).
  • PubMed search (2026-10-08) for "SNRPN upstream reading frame" found no later study of the
    protein's function; later papers address the locus/imprinting centre or RNF4.

ATPase binding (IEA, Ensembl Compara from mouse Q9WU12)

  • Mouse source is MGI IPI to Snurf, WITH Rad54l2/ARIP4 (Q99NG0), PMID:12058073 (Rouleau 2002,
    ARIP4 paper; cache is abstract-only). QuickGO shows MGI also gave ARIP4 "protein binding"
    IPI WITH Q9WU12 from the same paper.
  • Full text read through PMC (WebFetch, PMC117628): the paper defines "small nuclear RING
    finger protein (SNURF)" and uses a "SNURF residues 20–177" VP16 fusion as an AR-binding
    control in two-hybrid. Residues 20-177 cannot exist in a 71-aa protein, so the SNURF in the
    paper is RNF4. The MGI rows are a symbol-collision mapping error (should be Rnf4, O88846,
    if an ARIP4 interaction was shown at all). Removed here; upstream fix is at MGI.

Decisions

  • nucleus (IEA, NAS): ACCEPT. nuclear speck (IDA HPA, IBA): ACCEPT.
  • ATPase binding: REMOVE (wrong gene product at source).
  • No MF or BP proposed: nothing known.
