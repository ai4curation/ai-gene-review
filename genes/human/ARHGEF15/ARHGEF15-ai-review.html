Gene Description

ARHGEF15 (Ephexin-5, Vsm-RhoGEF) is a Dbl-family guanine nucleotide exchange factor for Rho-family GTPases, built from a long disordered N-terminal region, a catalytic DH domain at residues 417 to 601 and an adjacent ephexin-type PH domain. It couples Eph receptor tyrosine kinases to actin remodelling: EphA4 associates with it in vascular smooth muscle and, on ephrin-A1 stimulation, tyrosine-phosphorylates it and raises its exchange activity toward RhoA, driving stress-fibre assembly and smooth-muscle contractility; in neurons it binds EphB2 instead, and EphB2-dependent phosphorylation at Tyr-353 triggers UBE3A-mediated ubiquitination and degradation. Exchange activity has been measured directly for RhoA and, in a family-wide screen and in brain tissue, for CDC42 as well, while RAC1 is negative wherever it has been tested. Which of RhoA and CDC42 dominates appears to be set by context and by the phosphorylation state of that same tyrosine rather than by the catalytic site. Expression is largely vascular, in smooth muscle and endothelium, with a separate developmental role in hippocampal dendrites where the protein restrains excitatory synapse formation. Heterozygous loss-of-function variants cause autosomal dominant brain small vessel disease with osteoporosis, through RhoA/ROCK2 inactivation and F-actin disorganisation in vascular cells and impaired Wnt signalling in osteoblasts.

Core Functions

Receptor-coupled Dbl-family guanine nucleotide exchange factor. Through the DH domain at residues 417 to 601, assisted by the adjacent ephexin-type PH domain that targets it to the membrane, it loads GTP onto Rho-family GTPases and thereby drives actin remodelling. RHOA is the substrate every system agrees on: exchange activity toward RhoA is measured directly on the human protein three times, by EphA4-stimulated assay in vascular smooth muscle, by comparison of wild type with the R604C variant, and in a family-wide screen of all 145 human RhoGEFs and RhoGAPs whose library entry for this gene is the human protein at its full 841 residues. CDC42 is the contested half, positive in that same screen, in human endothelial cells by knockdown and in mouse brain, negative in mouse neurons and in the disease paper. RAC1 is negative wherever it has been tested. The exchange machinery is intact in the human protein, which retains the alpha5 residues L554/Q558/R559 whose triple-alanine substitution abolishes activity in the mouse ortholog, but those residues do not predict which GTPase is used: in a twelve-protein panel they are absent from all three canonical RhoA-specific GEFs and present in two of three CDC42-specific ones, and the same triple mutant is the published GEF-dead control for CDC42 as well as for RhoA. What does set selectivity, experimentally, is the phosphorylation state of the EphB2 site at human Y353. The substrate cannot be named in the term, because GO merged every substrate-specific GEF molecular function into GO:0005085, so it is carried in substrates and in has_input extensions instead.

Suggested Questions for Experts

Q: Does human ARHGEF15 activate CDC42 in vascular smooth muscle, where its RhoA activity was established, or is the CDC42 arm restricted to endothelium and neurons? The three CDC42-positive results come from endothelial cells, a family-wide screen and mouse brain, while both CDC42-negative results come from systems where the RhoA arm dominates. If selectivity is set by the phosphorylation state of Y353 as PMID:40138406 reports, the answer should depend on receptor input rather than on cell type as such, and the two literatures would stop looking contradictory.

Q: Should GO restore a way to state which GTPase a GEF or GAP acts on, given that both the GEF and the GAP molecular-function branches have now been flattened to a single term? GO:0005089 and its four siblings merged into GO:0005085, exactly as GO:0005097, GO:0005099, GO:0005100 and GO:0008060 merged into GO:0005096. The information is not recoverable from the biological-process branch either: Cdc42 protein signal transduction is an is_a descendant of Rho protein signal transduction, and there is no positive regulation of Cdc42 protein signal transduction term at all. For a family of 145 human proteins whose entire functional distinction is substrate choice, that leaves the molecular-function annotation uninformative.

Q: Is the PRKG1 interaction real, and does cGMP-dependent kinase signalling regulate this exchange factor in vascular smooth muscle? Seven bare protein-binding rows in this record, covering five distinct partners, are recommended for removal because the generic term is uninformative, not because the interactions are disputed; the partners are PIN1, LASP1, CEP55, PRKG1 and GORASP2 and they are named here so that removing the annotations does not lose them. PRKG1 is the one whose partner is a central regulator of the tissue the protein was discovered in, and it opposes RhoA/ROCK signalling there. A confirmed interaction would place a known vasodilatory pathway directly onto the exchange factor. It currently rests on one two-hybrid observation against one isoform.

Q: Do the mouse neuronal functions of Ephexin5 operate in human at all? Seven of the thirty-one GOA rows are neuronal or dendritic and every one of them is a projection from mouse. The human expression evidence is vascular and adipose, the human disease is cerebrovascular and skeletal rather than synaptic, and the one human neurological association is a single de novo variant of uncertain significance. The rows are kept because the mouse evidence is strong, but nothing currently distinguishes a conserved function from a rodent-specific one.

Current model (mechanistic narrative)

ARHGEF15 (Vsm-RhoGEF/Ephexin5) is a Rho guanine nucleotide exchange factor that couples receptor signaling to actin cytoskeletal remodeling and is required for vascular development [PMID:12775584, PMID:23029280]. In vascular smooth muscle cells it associates with the EphA4 receptor; ephrin-A1 stimulation drives EphA4-mediated tyrosine phosphorylation of ARHGEF15, enhancing its exchange activity toward RhoA and promoting actin stress fiber assembly PMID:12775584. In endothelial cells it acts downstream of VEGF to activate Cdc42 while potentiating inactivation of RhoJ, driving actin polymerization and cell motility; its genetic disruption in mice delays postnatal retinal vascular network extension [PMID:23029280, PMID:24397187], and it operates upstream of STAT3 phosphorylation to promote endothelial migration PMID:41359253. Loss-of-function ARHGEF15 mutations produce RhoA/ROCK2 inactivation and F-actin disorganization in vascular cells and impair Wnt/β-catenin signaling in osteoblasts, with a knock-in transgenic mouse recapitulating cerebral small vessel disease and severe osteoporosis PMID:36929019. Beyond the vasculature, amyloid-β acutely upregulates Ephexin5 in hippocampal neurons to drive dendritic spine loss and cognitive impairment, effects reversed by genetic or shRNA-mediated Ephexin5 reduction PMID:28346227, and Sertoli-cell-specific deletion disrupts blood-testis barrier integrity and testicular immune privilege PMID:36001358.

PMID:12775584 (Ogita et al. 2003, Circ Res) named the protein Vsm-RhoGEF and reported it as
VSMC-specific:
"a novel VSMC-specific guanine nucleotide exchange factor (GEF) for Rho (Vsm-RhoGEF/KIAA0915) was expressed specifically in VSMCs of several organs including the heart, aorta, liver, kidney, and spleen"

"tyrosine phosphorylation of Vsm-RhoGEF induced by EphA4 upon ephrin-A1 stimulation enhanced the Vsm-RhoGEF activity for RhoA"

"The requirement of Vsm-RhoGEF for ephrin-A1-induced assembly of actin stress fibers in VSMCs was shown by the overexpression of a dominant-negative form of VSM-RhoGEF and by the depletion of Vsm-RhoGEF using RNA interference."
PMID:12775584. The cache is abstract-only; the GOA IDA rests on the full text.

1. The exchange machinery is intact in the human protein — and says nothing about substrate

What was tested

Margolis et al. 2010 (PMID:21029865) identified three residues "within the α5 helix of E5's
DH domain" — L562, Q566, R567, numbered in mouse Ephexin5 — and built the triple-alanine
mutant E5-LQR as a predicted GEF-dead control. They introduced those residues as a
specificity signature: the three "are conserved in other GEFs that, like E5, activate RhoA
but not Rac1 and Cdc42".

Two separate questions follow, and they are answered separately below:

  1. Mapping. Does the human protein carry those residues? Human ARHGEF15 is 841 aa and
    mouse Ephexin5 is 849 aa, so the human positions must come from an alignment. Subtracting
    8 would be a guess that happens to be right.
  2. Discrimination. Is the triad actually a RhoA-vs-Rac/Cdc42 signature? That is a claim
    about a comparator panel and is falsifiable.

What this does and does not license

It does not refute Margolis et al. Their assignment was made against a structure-based
set of RhoA-specific GEFs (Snyder et al. 2002); the alignment here is sequence-based over a
panel they did not choose, so a disagreement is a failure to reproduce the discrimination,
not a demonstration that no such determinant exists.

What it does establish is that the triad cannot be used to infer human substrate
specificity, and the functional literature agrees with that reading rather than with the
specificity reading. Petshow et al. 2025 (PMID:40138406) use the very same triple mutant as
their GEF-dead control for Cdc42 — "either E5WT, E5Y361F, or GEF-dead E5 (E5LQR)" — so
the triad is required for exchange onto Cdc42 as well as onto RhoA. A residue set required
for both activities is exchange machinery, not a substrate selector.

Hence the conclusion runs in both directions, and neither direction is decisive on its own:

  • Presence is not activity. Human ARHGEF15 retains the full triad and the phosphosite,
    which is consistent with an intact exchange site; it is not evidence that the human
    protein is active, and no measurement of human ARHGEF15 exchange activity exists outside
    the three assays on the human protein cited in the review.
  • Absence would not have been inactivity, and presence is not specificity. The residues
    do not separate RhoA-GEFs from Cdc42-GEFs here, and the one substrate-selectivity switch
    that is experimentally established for this protein is not a DH residue at all but the
    phosphorylation state of Y361/Y353, which sits 60 residues N-terminal of the DH domain.

One tempting over-read has to be named and refused. Human Ephexin5 shares the discriminating
first residue (L) with FGD1 and ITSN1, and section 0 shows Müller et al. measured Cdc42
activity for ARHGEF15 and for FGD1. That is a single coincidence on a single column across
twelve proteins, in the opposite direction to the published interpretation of the same
residues, and the panel contains no design that would have detected it as signal. It is
recorded here only so that nobody later mistakes it for a prediction; nothing in the review
rests on it.
