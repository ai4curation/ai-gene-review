CC   -!- FUNCTION: G protein-coupled receptor for catecholamines that couples to
CC       both G(s) and G(i) proteins, leading to either activation or inhibition
CC       of adenylate cyclase and cAMP-dependent pathway, respectively
CC       (PubMed:10188996, PubMed:2570461, PubMed:8641219). The rank order of
CC       potency for physiological agonists is norepinephrine > epinephrine
CC       (PubMed:10188996, PubMed:2570461, PubMed:8641219). Involved in the
CC       regulation of thermogenesis and lipolysis in brown and white adipose
CC       tissue, after coupling to G(s) proteins and stimulation of the cAMP-PKA
CC       axis (By similarity). Also activates lipolytic process by coupling to
CC       G(i) proteins and consequent initiation of the ERK1/2 MAP kinase
CC       cascade (PubMed:10207024). Participates in relaxation of the blood
CC       vessels and the urinary bladder (PubMed:10188996). Also mediates
CC       negative inotropic effects in cardiomyocytes through activation of an
CC       NO synthase pathway and subsequent increase in cGMP levels, possibly
CC       involving G(i/o) protein-mediated coupling (PubMed:9769330).
CC       {ECO:0000250|UniProtKB:P25962, ECO:0000269|PubMed:10188996,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:10207024, ECO:0000269|PubMed:2570461,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:8641219, ECO:0000269|PubMed:9769330}.
CC   -!- SUBUNIT: Interacts with ARRDC3. {ECO:0000269|PubMed:21982743}.

CC   -!- TISSUE SPECIFICITY: Expressed mainly in adipose tissues.
CC   -!- POLYMORPHISM: The variant Arg-64 seems to be associated with weight
CC       gain (obesity) and is also associated with susceptibility to non-
CC       insulin-dependent diabetes mellitus (NIDDM).
