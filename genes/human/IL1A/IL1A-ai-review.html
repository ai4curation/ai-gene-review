Gene Description

Interleukin-1 alpha (IL-1alpha) is a leaderless IL-1 family cytokine and one of the two agonists of the type I IL-1 receptor. It is made as a 271-residue precursor that is constitutively present in the cytosol of many non-haematopoietic cells and induced in activated myeloid cells. Unlike pro-IL-1beta, the precursor already binds IL1R1; cleavage of the N-terminal propiece (residues 1-112) by calpain and other proteases increases its activity. Extracellular IL-1alpha binds IL1R1, recruits the co-receptor IL1RAP, and the paired receptor TIR domains assemble MYD88-IRAK signalling that activates NF-kappaB and the p38, ERK and JNK MAPK pathways, driving inflammation, cytokine and chemokine induction, and lymphocyte activation. IL-1alpha acts as an alarmin: it is released passively by necrotic cells and also exported by unconventional secretion routes (copper/S100A13-dependent stress export, caspase-1-dependent secretion, and TMED10-mediated entry into the ERGIC). A nuclear localisation signal in the propiece gives a nuclear pool that binds HAX1 and relocates to DNA damage sites, coupling genotoxic stress to IL-1alpha secretion. The decoy receptor IL1R2 captures IL-1alpha extracellularly and, as a cytosolic soluble form, binds the precursor and blocks its activity.

Core Functions

Extracellular agonist cytokine of the type I IL-1 receptor. Precursor and mature IL-1alpha bind IL1R1, which recruits IL1RAP to form the signalling receptor complex that triggers MYD88/IRAK-dependent NF-kappaB and MAPK activation and inflammatory responses. Acts as an alarmin when released from damaged or stressed cells.

Key findings

  • IL-1alpha is one of two IL-1 agonists. Unlike pro-IL-1beta, the IL-1alpha precursor binds IL1R1
    without processing PMID:2950091.
  • Same receptor and outputs as IL-1beta PMID:33482337.
  • Alarmin: constitutive intracellular precursor released on necrosis PMID:33482337.
  • Leaderless; unconventional secretion routes: S100A13/Cu2+-dependent stress export
    PMID:12746488, caspase-1-dependent secretion PMID:18329368, TMED10 channel into ERGIC
    PMID:32272059.
  • Nuclear pool via NLS in the N-terminal propiece, binds HAX1 [PMID:11554782, PMID:16971486];
    DNA damage sensing and secretion without loss of integrity PMID:26439902.
  • Decoy receptor IL1R2 captures IL-1alpha, including pro-IL-1alpha in the cytosol PMID:23847621.
  • Processing by calpain increases activity ~50-fold affinity PMID:33391283.

Curation observations

  • The NAS rows from PMID:23847621 (assigned by ComplexPortal, ECO:0005547 inferred from complex
    membership) on negative regulation of IL-1 signalling, IL-1alpha production, inflammation and
    monocyte differentiation describe the IL1R2 decoy complex. IL-1alpha is the captured ligand in
    that complex and performs none of the inhibition; those rows were removed.
  • GO:0045323 interleukin-1 receptor complex is defined as the receptor (alpha and beta subunits)
    that binds IL-1; the ligand is not a subunit.
  • No IL1A-specific evidence for apoptotic process participation (IL-1 is processed/released during
    apoptosis, PMID:1924307, it does not execute it).

  in the cytosol of many non-haematopoietic cells and induced in activated myeloid cells. Unlike pro-IL-1beta,
  the precursor already binds IL1R1; cleavage of the N-terminal propiece (residues 1-112) by calpain and
  other proteases increases its activity. Extracellular IL-1alpha binds IL1R1, recruits the co-receptor
  IL1RAP, and the paired receptor TIR domains assemble MYD88-IRAK signalling that activates NF-kappaB
  and the p38, ERK and JNK MAPK pathways, driving inflammation, cytokine and chemokine induction, and
  lymphocyte activation. IL-1alpha acts as an alarmin: it is released passively by necrotic cells and
  also exported by unconventional secretion routes (copper/S100A13-dependent stress export, caspase-1-dependent
  secretion, and TMED10-mediated entry into the ERGIC). A nuclear localisation signal in the propiece
  gives a nuclear pool that binds HAX1 and relocates to DNA damage sites, coupling genotoxic stress to
  IL-1alpha secretion. The decoy receptor IL1R2 captures IL-1alpha extracellularly and, as a cytosolic
  soluble form, binds the precursor and blocks its activity.'
