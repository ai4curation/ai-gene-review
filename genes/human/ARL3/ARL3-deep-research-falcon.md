# Human ARL3 (UniProt P36405): functional annotation research report

## Identity and principal function

**Identity verified.** Human **ARL3** (also called **ARFL3**) encodes ADP-ribosylation factor-like protein 3, a small GTPase of the Arf family. An experimental interaction study explicitly identifies ARL3 as **UniProt P36405** and distinguishes it from its paralog ARL2 (**P36404**); human genetic and biochemical studies examine the same ARL3 protein. Its annotated P-loop nucleotide-binding and Arf-family domains accord with this identification. Despite its name, ARL3 is **not an ADP-ribosylating enzyme**: its relevant catalytic cycle is binding and hydrolysis of **GTP to GDP**, with partner proteins controlling nucleotide exchange and hydrolysis. (veltel2008specificityofarl2arl3 pages 1-2, alkanderi2018arl3mutationscause pages 1-2, fansa2016sortingoflipidated pages 1-3)

**Primary molecular role:** GTP-bound ARL3 is a spatially regulated **release factor for lipid-modified protein cargo**. The soluble carriers **PDE6D/PDEδ** and **UNC119A/B** shield hydrophobic lipid anchors while transporting proteins through the cytoplasm. ARL3-GTP binds these carriers allosterically and promotes cargo release at the cilium; it is neither the lipid-modifying enzyme nor the cargo’s membrane transporter. This mechanism helps establish the protein composition of primary cilia and the photoreceptor outer segment, a specialized cilium. (fisher2020arffamilygtpases pages 11-15, fansa2016pde6δmediatedsortingof pages 1-2, jaiswal2016novelbiochemicaland pages 2-4, hankegogokhia2016arflikeprotein3 pages 1-2)

## Biochemical specificity and pathway

**Prenylated cargo.** PDE6D carries proteins bearing C-terminal prenyl groups, particularly farnesylated proteins. In purified-component experiments, farnesylated **INPP5E** bound PDE6D with a dissociation constant of **3.7 ± 0.2 nM**, versus **445 ± 83 nM** for farnesylated **RHEB**—approximately a 120-fold difference. Under the tested conditions, activated ARL3 released the tightly bound INPP5E cargo, whereas either ARL3 or ARL2 could release the more weakly bound RHEB. Structural analysis and substitutions near the prenylated cysteine showed that cargo sequence, particularly residues at positions −1 and −3, determines carrier affinity; weakening INPP5E binding reduced its exclusive ciliary localization. These results support *affinity-dependent sorting*, rather than an assertion that ARL3 directly recognizes only one named protein. **INPP5E** is itself a phosphoinositide phosphatase; that enzymatic activity should not be attributed to ARL3. Fansa and colleagues, *Nature Communications* (April 2016), https://doi.org/10.1038/ncomms11366. (fansa2016pde6δmediatedsortingof pages 1-2, fansa2016pde6δmediatedsortingof pages 2-3)

**N-myristoylated cargo.** UNC119A/B instead carry N-terminally myristoylated proteins. Biochemical experiments identified **NPHP3, cystin-1 and GNAT1** as relatively high-affinity UNC119 cargoes, **RP2** as intermediate, and **SRC** as lower affinity. High-affinity cargo was released effectively by ARL3-GTP, whereas both ARL2-GTP and ARL3-GTP could release lower-affinity cargo in the assays. A UNC119A–myristoylated NPHP3 crystal structure and peptide substitutions linked affinity to the size of residues at positions **+2 and +3 after the modified glycine**. Structural work further found that ARL3, unlike ARL2, accelerates release of myristoylated cargo from UNC119 by approximately **three orders of magnitude**, through rearrangement of the carrier’s lipid-binding pocket. These are measurements of **cargo–carrier binding and unloading**, not of ARL3 binding individually to each intact cargo protein. Jaiswal and colleagues, *Journal of Biological Chemistry* (September 2016), https://doi.org/10.1074/jbc.M116.741827; Ismail and colleagues, *EMBO Journal* (October 2012), https://doi.org/10.1038/emboj.2012.257. (jaiswal2016novelbiochemicaland pages 7-8, jaiswal2016novelbiochemicaland pages 2-4, fisher2020arffamilygtpases pages 11-15)

**The nucleotide cycle creates spatial specificity.** Ciliary **ARL13B** promotes GDP release and GTP loading on ARL3; biochemical and cellular studies identify ARL13B as its guanine-nucleotide exchange factor. Subsequent work found that **BART/ARL2BP** cooperates as a co-GEF: in the reported reconstitution, adding BART and ARL13B increased exchange to more than **500 times** intrinsic GDP dissociation, and ARL3 effectors helped displace BART after activation. **RP2** is an ARL3 GTPase-activating protein that stimulates GTP hydrolysis, terminating the active state. Thus the overall reaction cycle is *ARL3–GDP → ARL3–GTP → ARL3–GDP + phosphate*, with ARL3-GTP-driven unloading between activation and termination. Gotthardt and colleagues, *eLife* (November 2015), https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.11859; ElMaghloob and colleagues, *eLife* (January 2021), https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.64624; Veltel and colleagues, *FEBS Letters* (June 2008), https://doi.org/10.1016/j.febslet.2008.05.053. (gotthardt2015agproteinactivation pages 2-4, elmaghloob2021arl3activationrequires pages 5-6, elmaghloob2021arl3activationrequires pages 1-2, veltel2008specificityofarl2arl3 pages 5-6)

## Where ARL3 acts and what it accomplishes

ARL3 is a soluble, cilia-associated protein that can occur in the cell body as well as the ciliary compartment. Its particularly consequential **active pool is within cilia**: ciliary ARL13B and BART favor activation, while RP2 is enriched outside the cilium or near its base. This arrangement favors an **ARL3-GTP activity gradient**, enabling lipidated proteins to travel shielded by their carriers and then be unloaded near their ciliary destination. The gradient is a mechanistic model supported by regulator localization, biochemistry and perturbation experiments; it should not be interpreted as proof that all ARL3 activity is confined to cilia. A 2024 authoritative review likewise describes ARL3-dependent unloading from PDE6D and UNC119 within cilia. Hilgendorf, Myers and Reiter, *Nature Reviews Molecular Cell Biology* (2024), https://doi.org/10.1038/s41580-023-00698-5. (fansa2016sortingoflipidated pages 7-8, elmaghloob2021arl3activationrequires pages 1-2, hilgendorf2024emergingmechanisticunderstanding pages 6-8, travis2023disruptingtheciliary pages 1-2)

**Photoreceptors provide strong physiological evidence.** In mouse rods, removal of Arl3 after initial development selectively impaired trafficking of lipidated phototransduction proteins; earlier retina-wide deletion prevented formation of connecting cilia and outer segments. Constitutively active ARL3-Q71L likewise caused abnormal accumulation or loss of prenylated **PDE6** and **GRK1**, disturbed transducin distribution, and progressive photoreceptor degeneration. Thus both insufficient and excessive activity disrupt retinal cell biology, although the earliest embryonic deletion also demonstrates a broader requirement for normal ciliogenesis. Hanke-Gogokhia and colleagues, *Journal of Biological Chemistry* (March 2016), https://doi.org/10.1074/jbc.M115.710954; Wright and colleagues, *Human Molecular Genetics* (2016), https://doi.org/10.1093/hmg/ddw077. (hankegogokhia2016arflikeprotein3 pages 5-6, hankegogokhia2016arflikeprotein3 pages 1-2, wright2016arl3regulatestrafficking pages 13-15, wright2016arl3regulatestrafficking pages 1-3)

**Ciliary signaling is chiefly a consequence of cargo composition, not a demonstrated direct receptor pathway for ARL3.** For example, ARL3-dependent targeting of INPP5E connects this GTPase to the ciliary phosphoinositide environment. Importantly, affected ARL3-mutant fibroblasts could retain morphologically normal cilia and showed no detectable defect in the tested ciliary **GLI3 response to Hedgehog-pathway stimulation**, despite reduced ciliary INPP5E and NPHP3. It would therefore overstate the evidence to label human ARL3 a universal direct Hedgehog-signaling effector. Alkanderi and colleagues, *American Journal of Human Genetics* (October 2018), https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ajhg.2018.08.015. (alkanderi2018arl3mutationscause pages 4-5, alkanderi2018arl3mutationscause pages 1-2)

## Human disease, 2023–2024 advances and practical relevance

**Recessive ciliopathy.** In two unrelated families with **four affected individuals** altogether, substitutions of conserved **Arg149** in ARL3 were linked to Joubert syndrome with prominent brain and retinal involvement and variable kidney findings. Functional experiments showed defective interaction or exchange with ARL13B and reduced ciliary **INPP5E and NPHP3** in patient-derived fibroblasts. This connects the human phenotype to failure of the specific cargo-release pathway rather than merely to a generic cilium defect. Alkanderi and colleagues, *American Journal of Human Genetics* (October 2018), https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ajhg.2018.08.015. (alkanderi2018arl3mutationscause pages 1-2, alkanderi2018arl3mutationscause pages 5-7, alkanderi2018arl3mutationscause pages 7-8, alkanderi2018arl3mutationscause pages 4-5)

**Dominant retinal disease need not be loss of function.** Travis and colleagues found that disease-associated **D67V** was constitutively active and **Y90C** fast-cycling. Expressing these variants in mouse rods disturbed the normal ciliary ARL3-GTP distribution and displaced photoreceptor nuclei; experimentally removing the abnormal gradient or restoring the appropriate one rescued nuclear positioning. This is important when interpreting variants or designing interventions: simply increasing ARL3 activity could worsen some phenotypes. *eLife* (published January 4, 2023), https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.80533. The nuclear-migration rescue was demonstrated in an experimental mouse system, not a patient treatment. (travis2023disruptingtheciliary pages 1-2)

**Other recent observations require careful attribution.** A 2024 study of ARL3 **T31A** and compound **T31A/C118F** patient fibroblasts reported reduced mitochondrial oxygen consumption and membrane potential, increased mitochondrial reactive oxygen species and apoptosis; effects of **C118F alone** differed in ARPE-19 cells. These findings establish variant-associated cellular phenotypes, **not** that mitochondrial respiration is ARL3’s primary biochemical function. Zhang and colleagues, *FASEB BioAdvances* (2024), https://doi.org/10.1096/fba.2023-00138. Separately, a 2024 retinal-genetics study nominated the noncoding **ARL3 c.-88G>A** variant, but could **not confirm its predicted effect** in accessible patient material; it should not be treated as a functionally established pathogenic allele. Dueñas Rey and colleagues, *Genome Medicine* (January 2024), https://doi.org/10.1186/s13073-023-01277-1. (zhang2024mitochondrialfunctionalimpairment pages 8-10, zhang2024mitochondrialfunctionalimpairment pages 4-5, rey2024combiningaprioritization pages 15-17)

**An emerging additional role concerns motile cilia.** A 2024 preprint reported that GTP-loaded ARL3 binds **DNAAF9/Shulin**, the packaging factor that inhibits outer dynein-arm motors, and displaces Shulin from inhibited complexes **in vitro**. Its authors propose that this permits motor activation after entry into motile cilia. The same line of work appeared as a peer-reviewed *Nature Structural & Molecular Biology* article in **2025**, https://doi.org/10.1038/s41594-025-01680-9. The displacement experiment supports a second biochemical interaction; spatially restricted dynein activation in living human cilia remains a mechanistic interpretation rather than the established primary human ARL3 function. 2024 preprint: https://doi.org/10.21203/rs.3.rs-5233849/v1. (issa2024dnaaf9(shulin)and pages 1-3, mali2024molecularbasisfor pages 1-4)

**Current implementation.** ARL3’s established practical use is in **molecular interpretation of inherited retinal disease and Joubert syndrome**, supported by segregation, patient-fibroblast cargo-localization assays and variant-specific biochemical testing. Subretinal delivery of ARL3-EGFP rescued ciliogenesis and partly reversed degeneration in a **mouse** knockout experiment; this is a preclinical proof of concept, **not an established human ARL3 gene therapy**. The best-supported functional annotation remains: **a GTP-dependent, spatially regulated allosteric release factor for PDE6D-transported prenylated and UNC119-transported myristoylated ciliary cargo**, with ARL13B/BART-dependent activation and RP2-dependent termination. (alkanderi2018arl3mutationscause pages 1-2, elmaghloob2021arl3activationrequires pages 1-2, hankegogokhia2016arflikeprotein3 pages 1-2, travis2023disruptingtheciliary pages 1-2)
