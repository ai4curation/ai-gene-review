# TEAD4 manual research notes

TEAD4 is the human RTEF-1/TEF-3 paralog and a TEA-domain sequence-specific transcription factor. The original human RTEF-1 paper cloned a TEF-1-related human gene, mapped it to chromosome 12p13, showed related tissue distribution for TEF-1 and RTEF-1, and argued that the four vertebrate TEF genes arose from an ancient multigene family [PMID:8921372]. YAP reporter assays identified TEAD4 together with TEAD2 and TEAD3 as a strong YAP-activated transcription factor, and a YAP S94A mutation prevented TEAD4 binding and activation [PMID:18579750]. TAZ/WWTR1 also binds TEAD factors and needs TEAD binding for CTGF induction, cell growth, and epithelial-mesenchymal transition [PMID:19324877].

TEAD4 has multiple literature and Reactome rows for YAP/TAZ-regulated transcription. VGLL4 competes with YAP for TEAD binding and represses YAP-TEAD transcriptional output [PMID:24525233]. Aerobic-glycolysis work detected endogenous nuclear TEAD4 and linked PFK1 to the YAP/TAZ-TEAD transcriptional module [PMID:25796446]. WBP2 enhances TAZ-driven transcription in a TEAD-dependent way [PMID:20972459].

The PTHR11834 PAINT root supports RNA polymerase II cis-regulatory DNA binding, RNA polymerase II-specific DNA-binding transcription factor activity, transcription regulator complex membership, and regulation of Pol II transcription across the TEAD family. The PTN000931330 Hippo-signaling row is supported by mammalian YAP/TAZ-TEAD evidence for all four human paralogs. Older skeletal and muscle rows are much weaker: PMID:8662936 cloned a mouse TEF-1-related FGF-inducible gene expressed during C2C12 myoblast differentiation, and PMID:8921372 reported high RTEF-1 expression in skeletal muscle rather than a direct muscle-development assay.
