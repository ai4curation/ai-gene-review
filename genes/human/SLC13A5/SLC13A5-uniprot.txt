CC   -!- FUNCTION: High-affinity sodium/citrate cotransporter that mediates the
CC       entry of citrate into cells, which is a critical participant of
CC       biochemical pathways (PubMed:12445824, PubMed:12826022,
CC       PubMed:26324167, PubMed:26384929, PubMed:30054523, PubMed:33597751,
CC       PubMed:39622972). May function in various metabolic processes in which
CC       citrate has a critical role such as energy production (Krebs cycle),
CC       fatty acid synthesis, cholesterol synthesis, glycolysis, and
CC       gluconeogenesis (PubMed:12826022). Transports citrate into the cell in
CC       a Na(+)-dependent manner, recognizing the trivalent form of citrate
CC       (physiological pH) rather than the divalent form (PubMed:12445824,
CC       PubMed:12826022, PubMed:26324167, PubMed:26384929, PubMed:30054523,
CC       PubMed:33597751, PubMed:39622972). Can recognize succinate as a
CC       substrate, but its affinity for succinate is several fold lower than
CC       for citrate (PubMed:26324167). The stoichiometry is probably 4 Na(+)
CC       for each carboxylate, irrespective of whether the translocated
CC       substrate is divalent or trivalent, rendering the process electrogenic
CC       (PubMed:12445824, PubMed:12826022, PubMed:39622972). Involved in the
CC       regulation of citrate levels in the brain (By similarity).
CC       {ECO:0000250|UniProtKB:Q67BT3, ECO:0000269|PubMed:12445824,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:12826022, ECO:0000269|PubMed:26324167,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:26384929, ECO:0000269|PubMed:30054523,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:33597751, ECO:0000269|PubMed:39622972}.
