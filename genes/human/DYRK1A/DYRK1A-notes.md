# DYRK1A evidence notes

## Biological synthesis

DYRK1A is a protein kinase with serine/threonine substrate activity and tyrosine autophosphorylation. Human catalytic-fragment experiments establish that weak tyrosine activity can persist after maturation; substrate preferences are not absolute sequence requirements. A single catalytic core therefore uses GO:0004712, with CTD, histone, Tau and splicing-factor specificity explained in its description and retained in the individual source annotations. Separate ancestor and descendant kinase terms are not duplicated as artificial core units. [PMID:23665168](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23665168/)

Human DYRK1A phosphorylates the RNA polymerase II CTD at Ser2 and Ser5, with weaker Ser7 signal. Kinase-dead DYRK1A can restore polymerase occupancy without restoring local CTD phosphorylation. Direct motif-DNA binding was not detected, so chromatin recruitment is not treated as DNA-binding transcription-factor activity. The histidine-rich region contributes to CTD association and hyperphosphorylation, but experimental IDR droplets and cyclin-T1 experiments do not establish that all endogenous DYRK1A forms assemble equivalent condensates. [PMID:25620562](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25620562/), [PMID:29849146](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29849146/)

Histone H3 phosphorylation and the measured effects on HP1/Brg1 occupancy support chromatin and transcription regulation. This does not imply that DYRK1A is an ATP-dependent chromatin-remodeling motor. Splicing-factor phosphorylation changes Tau exon-10 choice in a substrate- and cell-dependent manner: both increased inclusion and increased exclusion occur in the experiments. Accordingly, two overly directional RNA-splicing assertions are refined to regulation of alternative splicing, preserving their original source identities and the actual rodent construct scopes. The coactivator IBA is retained with literal PAINT grounding and independent target-specific transcription evidence; it does not force a second core or a DNA-binding claim. [PMID:24820035](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24820035/), [PMID:21215488](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21215488/), [PMID:22767602](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22767602/), [PMID:28377597](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28377597/)

Human pathogenic variants cause an autosomal dominant neurodevelopmental disorder commonly including microcephaly and intellectual disability. That clinical relationship is distinct from evidence for a particular purified biochemical reaction or substrate. The official target record provides the disease identity and linked genetic literature. [UniProt Q13627](https://www.uniprot.org/uniprotkb/Q13627/entry)

## Contextual and over-extended assertions

The amyloid-output and Tau-insolubility experiments are kept at their measured scope. Human cell kinase perturbations change APP-derived amyloid output, while the sarkosyl-insoluble Tau experiment uses rat cortical neurons. Insoluble Tau is not itself a demonstrated human neurofibrillary-tangle-assembly endpoint, so that specific assembly assertion is marked over-annotated while retaining the positive aggregation-related evidence. [PMID:25556849](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25556849/)

DYRK1A's DNA-repair effects depend on perturbation and context. RNF169 phosphorylation and overexpression-associated changes in 53BP1 are real, but knockout increases the reported homologous-recombination endpoint, and early recruitment phenotypes are not fully explained by RNF169. The curated positive RNF169 branch and GO-CAM context are preserved as non-core, without claiming all double-strand-break repair is positively regulated. Harmine effects persisting in knockout cells also prevent inhibitor phenotypes from serving as uniquely DYRK1A-specific evidence. [PMID:30773093](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30773093/), [PMID:31024071](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31024071/)
