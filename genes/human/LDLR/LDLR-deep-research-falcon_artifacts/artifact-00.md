| **Identity and architecture — secretory pathway/plasma membrane** | Verified target is **human LDLR, UniProt P01130**, a single-pass LDLR-family receptor with seven cysteine-rich LA repeats, EGF-like modules, a six-bladed YWTD β-propeller, an O-glycosylated juxtamembrane stalk, transmembrane helix, and cytosolic NPxY-containing tail. It is not LRP1. | Direct accession use in the 2024 OptiMo-LDLr study; architecture independently supported by structural and functional literature and a 2024 domain/regulation review. | (pqac-00000008, pqac-00000001, pqac-00000027, pqac-00000056) |
| **Lipoprotein capture — extracellular face of plasma membrane** | Extracellular LA repeats recognize **apoB100 on LDL** and **apoE-containing lipoprotein remnants**. Recent cryo-EM resolved LDLR-bound apoB100 through two interfaces: LA modules contact basic regions of the apoB100 β-belt, while the LDLR β-propeller contacts the apoB100 N-terminal domain. | Established biochemical annotation plus high-quality cryo-EM primary study by Reimund *et al.*: online 11 December 2024, *Nature* 2025; maps were moderate resolution, so broad interfaces are stronger than residue-level assignments. | (pqac-00000027, pqac-00000017, pqac-00000018) |
| **Internalization — clathrin-coated pits and vesicles** | The cytosolic **NPxY motif** recruits the LDLR adaptor **LDLRAP1/ARH**, coupling ligand-bound LDLR to clathrin-mediated endocytosis. Defects in this step constitute an LDLR internalization phenotype. | Supported by established receptor biology and functional-variant assay literature; direct binding and uptake are commonly tested by surface expression, 4 °C LDL binding, and 37 °C DiI-LDL uptake. | (pqac-00000014, pqac-00000025, pqac-00000013) |
| **Ligand release and receptor recycling — acidified endosome to plasma membrane** | Acidification drives LDLR from an open ligand-binding state toward a closed conformation in which LA repeats engage the EGF/YWTD β-propeller region, displacing lipoprotein cargo. LDLR can then recycle to the surface; WASH and the COMMD–CCDC22–CCDC93 **CCC complex** support efficient endosomal recycling. | Structural and cellular evidence supports acid-dependent release and recycling. The 2024 Commander study directly established cargo-regulated SNX17–Retriever coupling using **LRP1 and other cargoes, not LDLR**; it therefore informs the general pathway but is not direct LDLR-specific proof. | (pqac-00000003, pqac-00000013, pqac-00000027, pqac-00000030) |
| **Cargo degradation — late endosome/lysosome** | Released LDL or remnant particles proceed to lysosomes, where apolipoproteins and lipids are degraded and cholesterol is liberated; LDLR normally avoids this fate when successfully recycled. | Canonical pathway supported by targeted LDLR trafficking and functional studies; disease variants that prevent release can cause receptor–cargo co-degradation and defective recycling. | (pqac-00000013, pqac-00000027) |
| **Sterol-sensitive transcription — ER, Golgi and nucleus** | Low cellular cholesterol permits **SCAP–SREBP2** trafficking to the Golgi, SREBP2 activation, and increased *LDLR* transcription. High cholesterol retains SCAP–SREBP2 in the ER, reducing receptor synthesis. | Authoritative 2024 mechanistic review integrating established sterol-sensing experiments. | (pqac-00000022, pqac-00000027) |
| **PCSK9 regulation — cell surface/endosome and trans-Golgi network** | Secreted PCSK9 binds LDLR **EGF-A**, enters through clathrin-coated vesicles, binds more strongly under acidic conditions, blocks the recycling conformation, and redirects LDLR to lysosomes. An intracellular route can also divert nascent LDLR from the trans-Golgi network. | Strong structural, cellular, genetic, and clinical evidence. A 2024 primary study further implicated the PCSK9 M2 region and MHC-I proteins in lysosomal sorting, while acknowledging that the complete sorting machinery remains unresolved. | (pqac-00000028, pqac-00000046, pqac-00000024) |
| **IDOL/MYLIP regulation — plasma membrane/endosome/lysosome** | Oxysterol-activated **LXR** induces the E3 ligase **IDOL/MYLIP**. IDOL binds the LDLR NPxY region through its FERM domain and, with UBE2D, ubiquitinates the cytosolic tail, promoting epsin/ESCRT-dependent multivesicular-body sorting and lysosomal degradation. | Strong pathway evidence summarized in a 2024 review; the precise role of USP8-mediated deubiquitination remains contradictory. Species differences caution against directly extrapolating mouse hepatic LXR–IDOL responses to humans. | (pqac-00000021, pqac-00000046) |
| **Functional variant assays — cell surface, binding, uptake and recycling** | Sixteen EGF-precursor-homology-domain variants were tested in LDLR-deficient CHO-ldlA7 cells using Western blotting, surface expression, LDL binding, uptake, and recycling. Ten were functionally pathogenic and six non-pathogenic, resolving defects in processing, binding, or recycling. | Direct targeted experimental evidence: Galicia-Garcia *et al.*, *Scientific Reports*, 2020. More informative for individual tested variants than prediction alone, though based on a heterologous cell system. | (pqac-00000004, pqac-00000013, pqac-00000037) |
| **Variant prediction and clinical annotation — sequence/structure interpretation** | **OptiMo-LDLr** integrates six prediction tools using human P01130. It was trained/validated against ClinVar and independently checked against 93 later expert-panel classifications, correctly predicting 95.7% overall; it maps predicted pathogenic hotspots across functional domains. | Peer-reviewed computational study: Larrea-Sebal *et al.*, *Advanced Science*, 2024. Reported optimized-model sensitivity and specificity were high, but predictions remain supporting evidence and should not replace functional assays, segregation, phenotype, or expert curation. | (pqac-00000044, pqac-00000045) |
