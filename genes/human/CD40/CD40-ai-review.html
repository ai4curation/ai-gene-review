Gene Description

CD40 is a type I membrane receptor for CD40 ligand (CD154/TNFSF5). Its extracellular region recognizes ligand, while cytoplasmic motifs recruit TRAF proteins and organize signaling that activates NF-kappaB and MAP kinase pathways. This receptor activity helps antigen-presenting cells coordinate immune responses and enables B-cell activation, proliferation and differentiation together with other inputs such as IL4. CD40 also mediates context-dependent inflammatory, endothelial and tumor-cell responses. It has no intrinsic protein kinase or ubiquitin ligase activity. The membrane isoform supplies transmembrane signaling, whereas an alternatively spliced product is secreted. Biallelic loss of functional CD40 causes immunodeficiency with hyper-IgM type 3.

Core Functions

Recognizes CD40L at the cell surface and couples ligand engagement to cytoplasmic TRAF recruitment, enabling immune activation and B-cell responses. Ligand recognition and receptor-tail docking form one signal-transduction unit; recruited TRAF proteins and kinases perform the enzymatic steps. The membrane receptor, not the secreted alternative product, supplies this transmembrane activity.
