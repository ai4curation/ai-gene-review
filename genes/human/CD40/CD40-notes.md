### Receptor mechanism and core synthesis

CD40 recognizes CD40L/CD154 and connects extracellular ligand engagement to cytoplasmic TRAF recruitment. The receptor does the signal-reception and docking work; recruited proteins perform ubiquitination, kinase reactions and downstream transcriptional control. The reviewed transmembrane product supplies one coherent receptor-signaling unit. No intrinsic protein kinase or ubiquitin ligase activity is inferred for CD40. Human CD40L mutant studies distinguish receptor recognition from integrin co-receptor recognition and from downstream activation; CD40L's integrin-binding activity is not attributed to CD40 (PMID:31331973, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31331973/). Human B-cell responses to uncleavable versus wild-type CD40L, in conjunction with IL4, directly ground activation and proliferation (PMID:8605945). Human IL4/CD40 synergy in AID expression is upstream receptor regulation, not direct transcription-factor or JAK activity (PMID:12574355).

Official GO:0004888 describes transmembrane signaling receptor activity, a more informative refinement of the existing broad receptor assertions. GO:0005031 specifically describes recognition of TNF and is not assigned merely because CD40 belongs to the TNF-receptor superfamily (https://www.flybase.org/reports/GO:0004888; https://www.flybase.org/reports/GO:0005031). Cached GO-CAM models 663d668500001599 and 663d668500001704 already place CD40 in the receptor role, upstream of distal TRAF6 and proximal TRAF2/5 signaling. This supports a receptor core without manufacturing a new process annotation. The existing CD40 signaling, B-cell activation and B-cell proliferation terms describe steps to which receptor activity contributes; broad parent immune/developmental terms remain non-core rather than duplicate core units.

### Calcium and conditional downstream processes

Selected complete Methods/Results from PMID:21410936 distinguish human U87 glioblastoma/HMC-1 mast-cell cocultures from primary mouse astrocytes/BMMCs and mouse EAE experiments. CD40 antibody blockade and U87 CD40 siRNA reduce a measured Fluo-3 calcium rise, as well as selected Rho/PKC/MAPK and transcription-factor readouts. Some PKC isoforms were unaffected. The positive calcium-regulation term GO:0007204 matches an increase in cytosolic calcium; GO:0006874 describes steady-state homeostasis and is not established by a transient rise alone (https://www.flybase.org/reports/GO:0007204; https://www.flybase.org/reports/GO:0006874). The experimental homeostasis row is therefore refined, while the separate combined electronic donor/rule assertion remains UNDECIDED until its original evidence is resolved.

The same source's later TNF/TNFR1 feedback and anti-TNFR1 controls matter for JAK-STAT interpretation. CD40 participates upstream in this conditional coculture response; it is not described as directly recruiting or catalyzing JAK. Canonical NF-kappaB, MAPK, PKC, transcription and JAK-STAT outputs remain non-core context-specific processes, while the receptor step itself remains core (PMID:21410936, https://doi.org/10.1186/1742-2094-8-25). Independent CD40-tail deletion and signaling studies support canonical/noncanonical pathway participation without receptor enzyme activity (PMID:8910514; PMID:15708970).

Selected target Results/Methods in PMID:28566713 establish human HUVEC CD40 knockdown effects on LPS-induced migration/tube formation and CD40 overexpression rescue of miRNA-dependent inhibition. Mouse miRNA knockout and Matrigel experiments manipulate a different entity and are not presented as human CD40 knockout evidence. Human coronary endothelial-cell apoptosis under proinflammatory conditions remains a separate conditional outcome with A20 protection (PMID:12885753).

Selected original tumor-virus Methods/Results in PMID:38494863 distinguish human CD40-positive lines, CD40L-bearing versus control viruses, CD46-mediated viral entry, and effects also seen in other ligand/cell contexts. The study supports receptor activity without attributing every death marker or all viral oncolysis to CD40. No universal tumor-cell death function is made core.
