CC   -!- FUNCTION: Non-enzymatic adapter protein that plays a pivotal role in
CC       precisely regulated signaling cascades from cell surface receptors to
CC       cellular responses, including signaling transduction and gene
CC       expression (PubMed:11016927, PubMed:11726515, PubMed:37626338). Thus,
CC       participates in many biological processes including regulation of
CC       innate and adaptive immunity, autophagy, DNA repair or necroptosis
CC       (PubMed:35831301, PubMed:37626338, PubMed:38182563). Controls signaling
CC       complexes at the T-cell antigen receptor to facilitate the activation,
CC       differentiation, and function of T-cells (PubMed:36864087,
CC       PubMed:9489702). Mechanistically, engagement of the TCR leads to
CC       phosphorylation of the adapter protein LAT, which serves as docking
CC       site for GRB2 (PubMed:9489702). In turn, GRB2 establishes a a
CC       connection with SOS1 that acts as a guanine nucleotide exchange factor
CC       and serves as a critical regulator of KRAS/RAF1 leading to MAPKs
CC       translocation to the nucleus and activation (PubMed:12171928,
CC       PubMed:25870599). Functions also a role in B-cell activation by
CC       amplifying Ca(2+) mobilization and activation of the ERK MAP kinase
CC       pathway upon recruitment to the phosphorylated B-cell antigen receptor
CC       (BCR) (PubMed:25413232, PubMed:29523808). Plays a role in switching
CC       between autophagy and programmed necrosis upstream of EGFR by
CC       interacting with components of necrosomes including RIPK1 and with
CC       autophagy regulators SQSTM1 and BECN1 (PubMed:35831301,
CC       PubMed:38182563). Regulates miRNA biogenesis by forming a functional
CC       ternary complex with AGO2 and DICER1 (PubMed:37328606). Functions in
CC       the replication stress response by protecting DNA at stalled
CC       replication forks from MRE11-mediated degradation. Mechanistically,
CC       inhibits RAD51 ATPase activity to stabilize RAD51 on stalled
CC       replication forks (PubMed:38459011). Additionally, directly recruits
CC       and later releases MRE11 at DNA damage sites during the homology-
CC       directed repair (HDR) process (PubMed:34348893).
