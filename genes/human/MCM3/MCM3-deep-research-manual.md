# MCM3 manual deep research

Automated Falcon and Perplexity deep-research generation was unavailable in this workstream, so this file records the manual synthesis used for the review.

MCM3 is a conserved MCM2-7 subunit. The original P1/MCM3 cloning paper showed that P1 is a mammalian MCM3 homolog, nuclear, serum-induced, and associated with complex DNA polymerase alpha-primase preparations [PMID:1549468]. That association was not treated as evidence that MCM3 is a stable component of the alpha DNA polymerase:primase complex; the contemporary complex assignment is the MCM2-7 helicase.

Human MCM2-7/CMG biochemical work demonstrates the complex-level activities that MCM3 contributes to: CMG is chromatin-localized in human cells, binds ssDNA, hydrolyses ATP, unwinds duplex substrates, and works with DNA polymerase epsilon to synthesize long products [PMID:22474384]. The 2020 human CMG structure places MCM3 as one of the subunits contacting ssDNA in the AAA+ spiral [PMID:32453425]. O-GlcNAc transferase work reported that MCM3, like the other MCM2-7 subunits, is O-GlcNAcylated and participates in MCM-MCM interactions in the chromatin-bound fraction [PMID:30069701].

The membrane row from the Natural Killer cell membrane-proteomics paper was treated as over-annotation: MCM3 has no membrane anchor, and the source explicitly notes that many non-integral proteins were identified in the preparation [PMID:19946888]. The perinuclear row from the B-cell interactome study was retained as non-core because it derives from imaging of MCM3 with mitotic proteins rather than from the canonical replication-helicase location [PMID:20531406].

No PAINT IRD or IKR loss applies to the human MCM3 lineage in PTHR11630.
