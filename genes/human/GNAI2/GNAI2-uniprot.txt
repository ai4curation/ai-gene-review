CC   -!- FUNCTION: Guanine nucleotide-binding proteins (G proteins) function as
CC       transducers downstream of G protein-coupled receptors (GPCRs) in
CC       numerous signaling cascades (PubMed:38505600, PubMed:29925945,
CC       PubMed:35637350, PubMed:35365641). The alpha chain contains the guanine
CC       nucleotide binding site and alternates between an active, GTP-bound
CC       state and an inactive, GDP-bound state (PubMed:12359238). Signaling by
CC       an activated GPCR promotes GDP release and GTP binding
CC       (PubMed:29925945). Examples of interacting GPCRs include the adenosine
CC       A1 receptor/ADORA1, CNR1, and FPR2 (PubMed:29925945, PubMed:35637350,
CC       PubMed:35365641). The alpha subunit has a low GTPase activity that
CC       converts bound GTP to GDP, thereby terminating the signal. Both GDP
CC       release and GTP hydrolysis are modulated by numerous regulatory
CC       proteins (PubMed:12359238). Signaling is mediated via effector
CC       proteins, such as adenylate cyclase: inhibits adenylate cyclase
CC       activity of ADCY1, ADCY5 and ADCY6, leading to decreased intracellular
CC       cAMP levels. Plays an important role in the activation of the
CC       transcription factor NFAT in endothelial cells to promote angiogenesis
CC       (PubMed:38505600). The G(i) proteins are involved in hormonal
CC       regulation of adenylate cyclase: they inhibit the cyclase in response
CC       to beta-adrenergic stimuli. Plays an essential role for neutrophil
CC       recruitment in the context of acute inflammation suggesting a linked
CC       function of both GNAI2 in neutrophils and endothelial cells for
CC       polymorphonuclear neutrophils transmigration (By similarity).
