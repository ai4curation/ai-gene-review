# GAA: evidence and source scope

The proposal reviews all 45 current annotation contexts individually: 18 ACCEPT, 19 KEEP_AS_NON_CORE, six MODIFY, one UNDECIDED and one MARK_AS_OVER_ANNOTATED. It adds no annotation. The 45 original raw objects, their literal donor order, the actual exporter/parser forecast and the single urine extension remain unchanged. A genuine no-seed target fetch produced the same 45-context, 16-column export and 952-residue sequence. The inherited review was not regenerated. The one urine atom omitted by the parser is restored explicitly at raw41/native38; all original fields remain in the evidence archive.

Two catalytic cores describe the work GAA performs in lysosomal glycogen degradation: alpha-1,4 hydrolysis and complementary alpha-1,6 cleavage. The slower isomaltose reaction does not make branch cleavage incidental. The reported 32-fold specificity-constant comparison is between maltose and isomaltose under particular assay conditions, not a universal rate ratio for glycogen linkages. No independent role in exocytosis, membrane organization or extracellular-vesicle function is assigned.

## Primary catalytic evidence

[PMID:29061980](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5653652/) is available as an immutable full-text cache. The selected Results, kinetic Methods and captions establish CHO-derived recombinant human GAA chemistry. The structural preparation was treated with alpha-chymotrypsin and retained comparable activity. Maltose and isomaltose assays measured glucose release at acidic pH; glycogen assays measured reducing equivalents. The enzyme is human even when the glycogen substrate is bovine or rabbit. Isomaltose accommodation was modeled by superposition with another enzyme, rather than observed in a human isomaltose co-crystal. A second acarbose site was observed near the experimentally removed G116–M122 loop, but its endogenous processivity role remains a hypothesis. The supplementary kinetic table and structural images were not independently inspected for this proposal.

[PMID:1856189](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1856189/) is abstract-only in the normal cache. Its original abstract reports active-site-directed conduritol-B-epoxide inhibition, glycogen/artificial-substrate competition, covalent labeling, and human GAA cDNA mutagenesis in COS cells. It distinguishes demonstrated catalytic importance from the initial mechanistic assignment of the catalytic carboxylate. [PMID:18429042](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18429042/) and [PMID:7717400](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7717400/) are also abstract-only; their human variant/function and residual-enzyme observations are retained without inventing unavailable assay conditions.

[PMID:9505277](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9505277/) explicitly reports CHO-produced human GAA uptake, lysosomal immunofluorescence, precursor processing and enzyme/glycogen correction in deficient human fibroblasts. Quail recipient experiments are stated separately. The abstract does not establish maltose or sucrose flux in those cells. Maltose retention instead combines the existing curator inference, direct GAA substrate chemistry and the official GO:0000023 definition, which explicitly includes maltose as an intermediate in glycogen/starch catabolism. The broad disaccharide context is non-core. Sucrose metabolism remains over-annotated because alpha-1,4 activity does not establish the distinct sucrose chemistry; no negative sucrose assay is invented.

[PMID:24417](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24417/) and [PMID:5264799](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/5264799/) remain metadata/title-only after targeted access attempts. Their historical source identifiers remain literal. The two linkage activities and broad alpha-glucosidase classification can be supported independently by the later human biochemical study. The particular glycophagy experiment attributed to 5264799 cannot be reconstructed from its title. Its action nevertheless retains the combined gene-level glycophagy claim: GAA catalyzes the terminal degradation of autophagically delivered glycogen. The lysosome-organization claim is similarly retained from combined glycogen-clearance and verified ancestral evidence while the precise 7717400 endpoint remains unverified. The diaphragm-contraction annotation attributed to [PMID:16917947](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16917947/) remains UNDECIDED: the accessible abstract neither establishes direct participation in force generation nor exposes the underlying diaphragm measurements. No unavailable experiment is represented as read.

## Membranes and extracellular-vesicle preparations

The normal [PMID:17897319](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1600-0854.2007.00643.x) cache is abstract-only. Separately retained original publisher text and selected Methods/Results/Table 1 identify human placental lysosomal alpha-glucosidase IPI00293088 with 24 peptides, 36.2% coverage and 112 spectral counts. The enriched lysosomal-membrane fraction was compared with dense-pool material. Peripheral proteins were not stripped. This supports non-core membrane association; the historical 957-residue database entry and predicted transmembrane count do not prove an integral topology or a present-day natural isoform. This external reading does not upgrade the normal cache's access flag.

The normal [PMID:23533145](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3773505/) cache contains original main text. Its Methods and Results, together with the retained authentic [Supplemental Table 2](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/biostudies/files/S-EPMC3773505/NIHMS502285-supplement-Supplemental_Table_2.xlsx), establish P10253/GAA in SI Table 2 row 440, with 15, 14, 29 and 28 unique peptides across the pooled measurements. Protein-group labels mentioning processed forms do not establish form-specific peptide localization. Differential ultracentrifugation, a DTT/sucrose wash and an identification FDR below 1% were used. The paper explicitly notes soluble material and possible contamination.

The normal [PMID:19056867](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19056867/) cache contains only the abstract, despite a Full Text heading. The separately retained original [NHLBI urinary-exosome table](https://esbl.nhlbi.nih.gov/Databases/UrinaryExosomes/) lists GAA NP_000143 and NP_001073272, with four and five peptides respectively and an explicit reference-2 link to PMID:19056867. Its imported GO columns are not additional experiments. Historical accessions do not determine current UniProt products.

These positive target observations support non-core retention of the two exosome-fraction annotations, while purity, intravesicular topology and biogenesis remain unresolved. The complete October 10 manual decision is retained in the inherited archive: it confirmed MO using the soluble-protein/EV-surface-receptor criterion. The new proposal explicitly reassesses that criterion. A demonstrated cognate EV-surface receptor is not a prerequisite for retaining positive preparation membership, and solubility does not negate the identified peptides. This does not assign GAA a receptor function or a physiological role in extracellular vesicles.

[PMID:19946888](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19946888/) remains abstract-only. The YTS membrane methods are described, but no retained authentic GAA-specific row or peptides were found. The general membrane claim is refined to the already-supported lysosomal-membrane descendant on independent human placental evidence. That gene-level refinement does not verify the unavailable YTS target observation or claim lysosomal resolution for that assay.
