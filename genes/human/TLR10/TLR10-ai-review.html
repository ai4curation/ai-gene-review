Gene Description

TLR10 is a type I transmembrane receptor of the Toll-like receptor family, most closely related to TLR1 and TLR6 and encoded in the same TLR6-TLR1-TLR10 cluster; it is functional in humans but is a disrupted pseudogene in mice. It is expressed mainly in B cells, plasmacytoid dendritic cells and monocytes, on the cell surface and in endosomes. Like TLR1 it heterodimerises with TLR2, senses triacylated lipopeptides through TLR2 and recruits MYD88 through its TIR domain, but unlike other TLRs it largely fails to activate NF-kappaB. Instead TLR10 acts mainly as an inhibitory receptor: it dampens proinflammatory and type I interferon responses to other TLR agonists (MyD88- and TRIF-dependent), in part by competing for TLR2 or ligand and by PI3K/Akt-driven IL-1 receptor antagonist production; it suppresses B cell proliferation and antibody responses cell-intrinsically, restrains monocyte-to-dendritic-cell differentiation, and in pDCs induces STAT3/SOCS3 and blocks IRF7 nuclear translocation. Proposed ligands include lipopeptides, double-stranded RNA and HIV-1 gp41, and some studies report activating roles in viral and Listeria infection.

Core Functions

TLR10 is an inhibitory Toll-like receptor: engagement of this transmembrane receptor (or its heterodimerisation with TLR2) suppresses MyD88- and TRIF-dependent TLR signaling and downstream cytokine and type I interferon production in monocytes, pDCs and B cells.

Comprehensive Research Report: TLR10 (Toll-like Receptor 10)

Gene and Protein Identity Confirmation

The research target TLR10 (UniProt accession Q9BXR5) encodes human Toll-like receptor 10, a transmembrane glycoprotein belonging to the Toll-like receptor family (rodrigues2024tlr10anintriguing pages 1-2). TLR10 is the most recently identified member of the human TLR family and is unique among TLRs as it remains an "orphan receptor" with incompletely defined ligands and biological functions, making it the subject of ongoing investigation (rodrigues2024tlr10anintriguing pages 3-4, rodrigues2024tlr10anintriguing pages 2-3, rodrigues2024tlr10anintriguing pages 1-2). The gene is located on the same chromosomal cluster as TLR1 and TLR6, and shares substantial sequence homology with these receptors, indicating evolutionary relatedness within the TLR1 subfamily (su2021tlr10insightscontroversies pages 9-13, rodrigues2024tlr10anintriguing pages 3-4).

Primary Function and Pattern Recognition

Immunomodulatory Role

TLR10 functions primarily as an immunomodulatory pattern recognition receptor with context-dependent effects that can be either anti-inflammatory or, less commonly, pro-inflammatory (su2021tlr10insightscontroversies pages 27-30, fore2020tlr10andits pages 4-6, rodrigues2024tlr10anintriguing pages 2-3). Unlike most TLRs that predominantly activate inflammatory responses, TLR10 is characterized as a negative regulator of innate immune signaling, particularly of TLR2-driven responses (su2021tlr10insightscontroversies pages 27-30, su2021tlr10insightscontroversies pages 13-17). A landmark 2024 study by Deb and colleagues provided the first definitive evidence that TLR10 functions as an inhibitory receptor in human plasmacytoid dendritic cells (pDCs), where antibody-mediated engagement suppressed virus-induced production of type I and type III interferons as well as TNF-α without impairing viral uptake or pDC maturation (deb2024tlr10(cd290)is pages 15-20, deb2024tlr10(cd290)is pages 1-3).

Proposed Ligand Specificity

As an orphan receptor, TLR10's natural ligand repertoire has not been definitively established, though multiple candidates have been proposed based on experimental evidence (su2021tlr10insightscontroversies pages 5-9, rodrigues2024tlr10anintriguing pages 3-4). The most extensively studied proposed ligands include:

Nucleic acids: TLR10 has been reported to bind double-stranded RNA (dsRNA) in endosomal compartments at acidic pH, recruiting MyD88 and suppressing IRF7-dependent type I interferon production (su2021tlr10insightscontroversies pages 5-9, rodrigues2024tlr10anintriguing pages 2-3). This endosomal nucleic acid recognition suggests a role in antiviral immunity regulation.

Bacterial lipopeptides: TLR10 appears to recognize or modulate responses to synthetic diacylated lipopeptides such as FSL-1 (typically a TLR2/TLR6 ligand) and triacylated lipopeptides like Pam3Cys and Pam3CSK4 (typically TLR2/TLR1 ligands), likely through formation of heterodimers with TLR2 (su2021tlr10insightscontroversies pages 27-30, rodrigues2024tlr10anintriguing pages 3-4, su2021tlr10insightscontroversies pages 13-17, su2021tlr10insightscontroversies pages 9-13). These ligands can enhance TLR2-TLR10 association and promote MyD88 recruitment.

Viral components: HIV-1 envelope glycoprotein gp41 and influenza virus-associated RNA-protein complexes have been identified as potential TLR10 activators, triggering NF-κB activation and inflammatory cytokine production in some experimental systems (su2021tlr10insightscontroversies pages 27-30, su2021tlr10insightscontroversies pages 5-9).

Bacterial pathogens: TLR10 has been implicated in responses to several bacterial species including Listeria monocytogenes, Helicobacter pylori, Borrelia burgdorferi, and Salmonella typhimurium, though the specific molecular patterns recognized remain undefined (su2021tlr10insightscontroversies pages 5-9, fore2020tlr10andits pages 4-6).

Cellular Localization and Subcellular Distribution

TLR10 exhibits dual subcellular localization, functioning at both the plasma membrane and in endosomal compartments (rodrigues2024tlr10anintriguing pages 2-3, su2021tlr10insightscontroversies pages 5-9, rodrigues2024tlr10anintriguing pages 3-4). This distinguishes it from the strictly cell-surface TLRs (TLR1, TLR2, TLR4, TLR5, TLR6) and the exclusively endosomal TLRs (TLR3, TLR7, TLR8, TLR9) (fore2020tlr10andits pages 1-2, rodrigues2024tlr10anintriguing pages 1-2).

At the plasma membrane, TLR10 is constitutively expressed as a type I transmembrane protein with its leucine-rich repeat ectodomain exposed extracellularly for ligand recognition and its Toll/interleukin-1 receptor (TIR) domain positioned intracellularly for signaling (rodrigues2024tlr10anintriguing pages 1-2, su2021tlr10insightscontroversies pages 13-17, fore2020tlr10andits pages 2-4). Studies in human neutrophils have demonstrated dynamic trafficking: following LPS stimulation, TLR10 undergoes ligand-induced endocytosis, moving from the plasma membrane into cytoplasmic vesicles within approximately 60-90 minutes, colocalizing with flotillin-1 (a lipid raft marker) and EEA-1 (an early endosomal marker), before returning to the cell surface after approximately 2 hours (rodrigues2024tlr10anintriguing pages 5-5).

In endosomal compartments, TLR10 has been shown to recognize pathogen nucleic acids, particularly dsRNA, and recruit MyD88 for signal transduction (rodrigues2024tlr10anintriguing pages 2-3, su2021tlr10insightscontroversies pages 5-9, rodrigues2024tlr10anintriguing pages 3-4). This endosomal localization may enable TLR10 to regulate responses to internalized viral particles or nucleic acids derived from phagocytosed pathogens.

Cell Type Expression Pattern

TLR10 expression exhibits remarkable cell-type specificity within the immune system. Among human immune cells, expression is highest in B cells and plasmacytoid dendritic cells (pDCs), with detectable expression also in eosinophils, neutrophils, monocytes, and regulatory T cells (Tregs) (su2021tlr10insightscontroversies pages 5-9, fore2020tlr10andits pages 2-4, rodrigues2024tlr10anintriguing pages 3-4). Notably, monocytes, natural killer cells, and conventional T cells reportedly lack detectable TLR10 expression (su2021tlr10insightscontroversies pages 5-9). TLR10 is also expressed in non-immune cells including trophoblasts and various epithelial cell types (fore2020tlr10andits pages 2-4). At the tissue level, TLR10 is found primarily in lymphoid organs including lymph nodes, thymus, tonsils, and spleen (rodrigues2024tlr10anintriguing pages 3-4).

Signaling Pathways and Molecular Mechanisms

TLR10 engages a complex, context-dependent network of signaling pathways that can result in either immune suppression or activation depending on the cellular environment, receptor dimerization partner, and ligand (su2021tlr10insightscontroversies pages 9-13, su2021tlr10insightscontroversies pages 5-9). Recent mechanistic advances, particularly the 2024 study by Deb et al., have clarified several key pathways.

Inhibitory Signaling Mechanisms

STAT3-SOCS3-IRF7 Axis (Primary Inhibitory Pathway in pDCs): The most clearly defined inhibitory mechanism was established in 2024 through studies of primary human plasmacytoid dendritic cells (deb2024tlr10(cd290)is pages 15-20, deb2024tlr10(cd290)is pages 8-10, deb2024tlr10(cd290)is pages 1-3, deb2024tlr10(cd290)is pages 10-12). Antibody-mediated engagement of surface TLR10 induces rapid phosphorylation of STAT3 (within approximately 1 hour) through an as-yet-unidentified kinase, without affecting phosphorylation of NF-κB, STAT1, or Akt (deb2024tlr10(cd290)is pages 8-10). This STAT3 activation leads to selective induction of the suppressor of cytokine signaling 3 (SOCS3), a negative feedback regulator (deb2024tlr10(cd290)is pages 8-10, deb2024tlr10(cd290)is pages 10-12, deb2024tlr10(cd290)is pages 20-27). The functional consequence is potent inhibition of IRF7 nuclear translocation, without preventing IRF7 phosphorylation, thereby selectively suppressing type I interferon (IFN-α) and type III interferon (IFN-λ) production as well as TNF-α secretion in response to viral stimulation (deb2024tlr10(cd290)is pages 15-20, deb2024tlr10(cd290)is pages 8-10, deb2024tlr10(cd290)is pages 10-12). Importantly, this inhibitory effect does not impair viral uptake, pDC maturation marker expression (CD40, CD80, CD83, CD86), NF-κB nuclear translocation, or the capacity of pDCs to stimulate allogeneic T cells, indicating pathway-selective rather than global immunosuppression (deb2024tlr10(cd290)is pages 8-10, deb2024tlr10(cd290)is pages 20-27).

MyD88-Dependent Pathways: TLR10 homodimers and TLR2/TLR10 heterodimers can recruit the adaptor protein MyD88 and its partner TIRAP/MAL through interactions involving the TIR domain BB loop (su2021tlr10insightscontroversies pages 9-13, fore2020tlr10andits pages 2-4). However, unlike canonical activating TLR complexes, TLR10-MyD88 signaling can result in inhibition of downstream NF-κB and MAPK pathways rather than their activation (fore2020tlr10andits pages 4-6, fore2020tlr10andits pages 2-4). The mechanism involves either direct inhibition of MyD88 signaling, competitive interference with TLR2 dimerization and ligand binding, or recruitment of distinct adaptor proteins that redirect signaling outcomes (su2021tlr10insightscontroversies pages 13-17).

NF-κB and MAPK Modulation: TLR10 can suppress NF-κB activation by inhibiting IκB degradation and reducing phosphorylation of the p65 subunit (fore2020tlr10andits pages 2-4, su2021tlr10insightscontroversies pages 13-17, rodrigues2024tlr10anintriguing pages 5-5). Similarly, TLR10 engagement can reduce phosphorylation of MAPK family members including p38, ERK, and JNK, thereby limiting transcription of pro-inflammatory genes (fore2020tlr10andits pages 4-6, fore2020tlr10andits pages 2-4, su2021tlr10insightscontroversies pages 27-30). Functional evidence comes from experiments showing that TLR10 blockade or knockdown increases inflammatory cytokine production, while TLR10 engagement or overexpression suppresses these outputs in multiple cell types (su2021tlr10insightscontroversies pages 13-17, rodrigues2024tlr10anintriguing pages 5-5).

PI3K/Akt-IL-1Ra Pathway: TLR10 activation can engage PI3K/Akt signaling to induce production of interleukin-1 receptor antagonist (IL-1Ra), an endogenous anti-inflammatory cytokine that blocks IL-1β activity (su2021tlr10insightscontroversies pages 13-17, su2021tlr10insightscontroversies pages 27-30). This mechanism provides an additional layer of anti-inflammatory control by shifting the cytokine balance toward immune suppression.

Context-Dependent Activating Functions

While TLR10 functions predominantly as an inhibitory receptor, certain experimental contexts reveal pro-inflammatory capabilities (su2021tlr10insightscontroversies pages 5-9, su2021tlr10insightscontroversies pages 17-21). Engineered chimeric receptors containing TLR10 cytoplasmic domains can activate NF-κB and induce TNF-α, IL-6, AP-1, and IL-4 expression (su2021tlr10insightscontroversies pages 5-9). In infection models, TLR10 knockdown reduced pro-inflammatory cytokine expression in response to TLR ligands and influenza virus, suggesting that endogenous TLR10 can contribute to inflammatory responses in specific cellular and pathogen contexts (su2021tlr10insightscontroversies pages 5-9, su2021tlr10insightscontroversies pages 17-21). During Listeria monocytogenes infection, TLR10 increased production of CCL20 and IL-8, supporting host defense (su2021tlr10insightscontroversies pages 5-9).

Receptor Dimerization and Structural Basis of Function

TLR10 functions through formation of both homodimers and heterodimers, particularly with TLR1 and TLR2 (su2021tlr10insightscontroversies pages 13-17, rodrigues2024tlr10anintriguing pages 7-7, su2021tlr10insightscontroversies pages 9-13, rodrigues2024tlr10anintriguing pages 3-4). This dimerization capacity is critical for ligand recognition and determines signaling outcomes.

Structural Organization: TLR10 contains three functional domains: (1) an extracellular leucine-rich repeat (LRR) domain responsible for ligand recognition and receptor dimerization, (2) a single-pass transmembrane region that mediates homodimerization and heterodimerization through extensive interactions with corresponding domains of TLR1 and TLR6, and (3) an intracellular TIR domain consisting of five parallel β-sheets surrounded by five α-helical segments that recruits adaptor proteins (rodrigues2024tlr10anintriguing pages 3-4, su2021tlr10insightscontroversies pages 13-17). Structural studies have demonstrated that the isolated TLR10 TIR domain forms symmetric homodimers, and the full ectodomain also assembles into stable homodimers (su2021tlr10insightscontroversies pages 13-17).

Heterodimerization: TLR10 exhibits approximately 87% of its homodimer affinity for TLR1, while the TLR1/TLR2 heterodimer shows approximately 80% relative affinity, indicating that TLR10 can compete effectively for TLR1 and potentially disrupt TLR1/TLR2 signaling (su2021tlr10insightscontroversies pages 9-13). TLR2/TLR10 association is enhanced by lipidated ligands such as Pam3CSK4 and depends primarily on the TLR10 ectodomain (su2021tlr10insightscontroversies pages 9-13). Domain-swap experiments have shown that the TLR10 ectodomain and transmembrane region support interaction with TLR2, while the TIR domain determines downstream signaling: replacing the TLR1 TIR domain with the TLR10 TIR domain abolishes strong inflammatory responses, demonstrating that TIR-domain-specific adaptor recruitment is critical for functional outcomes (su2021tlr10insightscontroversies pages 13-17).

Functional Consequences: The nature of the receptor complex—TLR10 homodimer versus TLR10/TLR1 or TLR10/TLR2 heterodimer—determines biological outcomes. TLR10 homodimers and TLR10/TLR1 heterodimers are generally associated with pro-inflammatory activity, whereas TLR2/TLR10 heterodimers show variable effects ranging from enhanced to inhibited TLR2 signaling depending on experimental conditions (rodrigues2024tlr10anintriguing pages 7-7, rodrigues2024tlr10anintriguing pages 3-4). This heterogeneity reflects competition for ligands, differential adaptor recruitment, and cell-type-specific co-receptor availability.

Biological Processes and Disease Associations

Role in Immune Cell Function

Plasmacytoid Dendritic Cells: The 2024 study by Deb et al. established that TLR10 regulates pDC antiviral responses by suppressing type I and type III interferon production through the STAT3-SOCS3-IRF7 pathway, while preserving the ability of pDCs to stimulate T cells (deb2024tlr10(cd290)is pages 15-20, deb2024tlr10(cd290)is pages 1-3, deb2024tlr10(cd290)is pages 10-12). This selective inhibition positions TLR10 as a critical brake on pDC-mediated interferon responses, which is significant because excessive pDC activation and interferon production are implicated in autoimmune diseases such as systemic lupus erythematosus and type I interferon-driven inflammatory conditions.

B Cells: TLR10 expression in B cells is dynamic, increasing during activation and maturation, and may function to restrain B cell activation and autoantibody production (rodrigues2024tlr10anintriguing pages 3-4, rodrigues2024tlr10anintriguing pages 4-5). In rheumatoid arthritis, pro-inflammatory B cell subsets express lower TLR10 than anti-inflammatory subsets, consistent with a protective, inflammation-limiting role (su2021tlr10insightscontroversies pages 13-17). Conversely, reduced TLR10 expression in B cell subsets from patients with primary Sjögren's syndrome correlates with high disease activity, potentially indicating loss of inhibitory control (rodrigues2024tlr10anintriguing pages 4-5).

Neutrophils: In human neutrophils, TLR10 expression is regulated by reactive oxygen species, TLR4 signaling, and NF-κB, with evidence that TLR10 undergoes dynamic trafficking in response to inflammatory stimuli and may regulate neutrophil chemotaxis (rodrigues2024tlr10anintriguing pages 5-5).

Regulatory T Cells: TLR10 expression on regulatory T cells (Tregs) is controlled by FOXP3, a master regulator of Treg function, suggesting specialized immunoregulatory roles distinct from other TLRs (rodrigues2024tlr10anintriguing pages 4-5, fore2020tlr10andits pages 2-4).

Disease Associations and Genetic Polymorphisms

TLR10 genetic polymorphisms have been associated with susceptibility to, or protection from, a broad spectrum of human diseases (fore2020tlr10andits pages 6-7, su2021tlr10insightscontroversies pages 17-21, fore2020tlr10andits pages 9-10, su2021tlr10insightscontroversies pages 30-31):

Autoimmune Diseases: Variants including rs4129009, rs11096955, rs11096957, and rs10004195 are associated with rheumatoid arthritis, Crohn's disease, autoimmune thyroid disease (including Graves' disease and Hashimoto's thyroiditis), primary Sjögren's syndrome, and IgA nephropathy (fore2020tlr10andits pages 6-7, su2021tlr10insightscontroversies pages 17-21, rodrigues2024tlr10anintriguing pages 4-5, fore2020tlr10andits pages 9-10). The functional variant I437T alters TNF-α-induced NF-κB transcription and may predict worse prognosis in rheumatoid arthritis (fore2020tlr10andits pages 6-7).

Infectious Diseases: TLR10 polymorphisms influence susceptibility to tuberculosis (rs11096957), complicated skin and skin-structure infections (I775L and I369L variants show protective effects), Helicobacter pylori-associated gastritis (rs10004195), Crimean-Congo hemorrhagic fever, asthma following bronchiolitis (rs4129009), and responses to influenza and HIV infection (fore2020tlr10andits pages 6-7, su2021tlr10insightscontroversies pages 17-21, su2021tlr10insightscontroversies pages 13-17, su2021tlr10insightscontroversies pages 30-31).

Cancer: TLR10 genetic variants are associated with urothelial bladder cancer, nasopharyngeal carcinoma, prostate cancer (rs11096955 and rs11096957 associated with reduced risk), papillary thyroid carcinoma, and non-Hodgkin lymphoma (fore2020tlr10andits pages 6-7, su2021tlr10insightscontroversies pages 17-21).

Metabolic Disease: Human TLR10 transgenic mice fed a high-fat diet showed smaller adipocytes, reduced adipose tissue mass, and marginally lower plasma insulin, implicating TLR10 in obesity-related inflammation and metabolic regulation (su2021tlr10insightscontroversies pages 13-17).

Current Research Applications and Therapeutic Potential

TLR10 is emerging as a promising therapeutic target for immune-mediated and inflammatory diseases (deb2024tlr10(cd290)is pages 1-3, fore2020tlr10andits pages 6-7, fore2020tlr10andits pages 4-6, rodrigues2024tlr10anintriguing pages 4-5):

Antibody-Mediated Targeting: Monoclonal antibodies that engage TLR10 can suppress pro-inflammatory cytokine production, reduce dendritic cell capacity to activate T cells, and modulate monocyte differentiation (rodrigues2024tlr10anintriguing pages 5-5, rodrigues2024tlr10anintriguing pages 4-5). The 2024 pDC study proposed antibody-mediated TLR10 engagement as a selective therapeutic strategy for autoimmune diseases characterized by aberrant pDC activation and type I interferon production, potentially avoiding generalized immunosuppression due to TLR10's restricted expression in B cells, monocytes, and pDCs (deb2024tlr10(cd290)is pages 1-3, deb2024tlr10(cd290)is pages 12-13).

Experimental Models: Human TLR10 knock-in/transgenic mice have been developed by inserting the human TLR10 open reading frame into the ROSA26 locus or using CMV-FLAG-TLR10 constructs, providing in vivo platforms for studying TLR10 function and evaluating therapeutic interventions (su2021tlr10insightscontroversies pages 21-23, su2021tlr10insightscontroversies pages 17-21, rodrigues2024tlr10anintriguing pages 6-7). These models demonstrate that TLR10 functions primarily as an inhibitor of TLR2-driven signaling in vivo, although engagement can also up-regulate distinct gene sets (su2021tlr10insightscontroversies pages 21-23, su2021tlr10insightscontroversies pages 17-21).

Mechanistic Approaches: Experimental strategies to study TLR10 include antibody-mediated receptor engagement, siRNA-mediated knockdown in human cell lines, expression analysis in patient samples, in vitro ligand-binding assays, receptor chimera construction, and functional assays measuring cytokine production and signaling pathway activation (rodrigues2024tlr10anintriguing pages 5-5, fore2020tlr10andits pages 2-4, su2021tlr10insightscontroversies pages 21-23, su2021tlr10insightscontroversies pages 17-21, su2021tlr10insightscontroversies pages 13-17, rodrigues2024tlr10anintriguing pages 4-5).

Clinical Applications Under Investigation: Potential therapeutic approaches include: (1) enhancing TLR10 anti-inflammatory activity to suppress NF-κB and inflammatory cytokine production in rheumatoid arthritis and other hyperinflammatory autoimmune conditions, (2) promoting M2 macrophage/microglial polarization for neuroprotective applications, (3) using TLR10 polymorphisms for disease risk stratification and prognostic assessment, and (4) modulating TLR10 expression or signaling pathways through vitamin D or other upstream regulators (fore2020tlr10andits pages 6-7, fore2020tlr10andits pages 4-6, fore2020tlr10andits pages 9-10).

Evidence Quality and Current Understanding

The characterization of TLR10 function represents an evolving field with several important caveats. TLR10 remains an orphan receptor with no definitively validated endogenous ligand, and many proposed ligand-receptor interactions are based on indirect evidence from knockdown, overexpression, or antibody-engagement experiments rather than direct biochemical binding studies (su2021tlr10insightscontroversies pages 5-9, rodrigues2024tlr10anintriguing pages 3-4, rodrigues2024tlr10anintriguing pages 2-3, rodrigues2024tlr10anintriguing pages 1-2). The conflicting reports of pro-inflammatory versus anti-inflammatory activity reflect genuine context-dependence—varying by cell type, ligand, receptor dimerization partner, and subcellular compartment—rather than experimental artifact (fore2020tlr10andits pages 4-6, rodrigues2024tlr10anintriguing pages 2-3, su2021tlr10insightscontroversies pages 1-5).

The strongest mechanistic evidence comes from the 2024 study by Deb et al. in primary human plasmacytoid dendritic cells, which used antibody-mediated engagement, phospho-flow cytometry, gene expression analysis, nuclear translocation assays, and functional cytokine measurements to establish the STAT3-SOCS3-IRF7 inhibitory axis (deb2024tlr10(cd290)is pages 15-20, deb2024tlr10(cd290)is pages 8-10, deb2024tlr10(cd290)is pages 1-3, deb2024tlr10(cd290)is pages 10-12). This represents the first definitive characterization of a TLR10-specific signaling mechanism in primary human immune cells. Earlier evidence derives largely from cell-line overexpression systems, knockdown experiments, and TLR10 transgenic mice, which provide valuable but more indirect support (su2021tlr10insightscontroversies pages 21-23, su2021tlr10insightscontroversies pages 17-21, su2021tlr10insightscontroversies pages 13-17).

A major impediment to TLR10 research is the absence of a functional TLR10 gene in mice—the most widely used model organism for immunology research—necessitating reliance on human cell systems, TLR10 knock-in mice, or alternative species such as rats that naturally express TLR10 (fore2020tlr10andits pages 2-4, rodrigues2024tlr10anintriguing pages 6-7).

Summary and Future Directions

TLR10 (CD290) is a unique member of the human Toll-like receptor family that functions primarily as an immunomodulatory pattern recognition receptor with predominantly anti-inflammatory effects. It is localized at both the plasma membrane and in endosomal compartments, with highest expression in B cells and plasmacytoid dendritic cells. TLR10 can form homodimers or heterodimers with TLR1 and TLR2, and while its natural ligand repertoire remains incompletely defined, proposed ligands include double-stranded RNA, bacterial lipopeptides, viral envelope proteins, and components of various bacterial pathogens.

The primary signaling mechanism involves recruitment of MyD88 adaptor proteins, with a recently elucidated inhibitory pathway in plasmacytoid dendritic cells operating through STAT3 phosphorylation, SOCS3 induction, and suppression of IRF7 nuclear translocation to limit type I and type III interferon production. Additional regulatory pathways include modulation of NF-κB, MAPK (p38, ERK, JNK), and PI3K/Akt signaling. TLR10 genetic polymorphisms are associated with susceptibility to autoimmune diseases, infections, and cancers, and the receptor represents a promising therapeutic target for conditions characterized by excessive inflammation or aberrant interferon production.

Future research priorities include: (1) definitive identification of endogenous TLR10 ligands through biochemical binding studies, (2) clarification of the molecular mechanisms governing pro- versus anti-inflammatory signaling, (3) structural determination of TLR10 homodimers and heterodimers with ligands bound, (4) preclinical evaluation of TLR10-targeting therapeutics in disease models, and (5) clinical translation of TLR10 genetic variants as biomarkers and TLR10-directed therapies for autoimmune and inflammatory conditions.
