| Signaling component or pathway | Mechanism of action | Cellular outcome | Key evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| MyD88 recruitment | TLR10 homodimers and proposed TLR2/TLR10 heterodimers recruit MyD88 through cytoplasmic TIR-domain interactions. Endosomal dsRNA-associated TLR10 reportedly recruits MyD88 rather than TRIF. | Context-dependent: may support IRAK–TRAF6 inflammatory signaling or inhibit NF-κB and MAPK activity. | Direct association with MyD88 and TIRAP has been reported; dominant-negative MyD88 blocks TLR10-associated ENA-78 expression. Signaling downstream of endogenous TLR10 remains incompletely resolved. (pqac-00000006, pqac-00000016, pqac-00000018) |
| STAT3–SOCS3 inhibitory axis | Antibody-mediated engagement of surface TLR10 in human plasmacytoid dendritic cells increases STAT3 phosphorylation and selectively induces SOCS3; the upstream kinase is unknown. | Suppresses IFN-α, IFN-λ, and TNF-α without impairing viral uptake, maturation markers, or T-cell stimulation. | Primary human pDC experiments detected STAT3 phosphorylation and SOCS3 induction within approximately one hour; NF-κB, STAT1, and Akt phosphorylation were unaffected. (pqac-00000033, pqac-00000035, pqac-00000038) |
| IRF7 inhibition | TLR10 engagement inhibits IRF7 nuclear translocation after viral stimulation without substantially preventing IRF7 phosphorylation. | Selectively suppresses IRF7-dependent type-I and type-III interferon responses in pDCs. | Anti-TLR10 engagement reduced virus-induced cytokines and IRF7 nuclear entry while leaving NF-κB nuclear translocation and viral uptake intact. The proposed STAT3/SOCS3-to-IRF7 causal chain is not yet fully proven. (pqac-00000032, pqac-00000033, pqac-00000035, pqac-00000037) |
| NF-κB modulation | TLR10 can inhibit IκB degradation and NF-κB activation, potentially through competition for TLR2 ligands or partners or recruitment of inhibitory adaptors. Engineered receptors and selected infection models instead show activation. | In inhibitory settings, lowers IL-1β, IL-6, IL-8, and TNF-α; in activating settings, increases IL-8, CCL20, and other inflammatory mediators. | TLR10 blockade or depletion increases phospho-p65 and cytokines in some systems but decreases ligand-induced cytokines in others, demonstrating dependence on cell type, ligand, and receptor configuration. (pqac-00000017, pqac-00000018, pqac-00000020, pqac-00000023) |
| MAPK pathways: p38, ERK, and JNK | TLR10-containing complexes can reduce p38, ERK, and JNK phosphorylation downstream of TLR2/MyD88; other contexts permit MAPK-associated activation. | Predominantly limits transcription of proinflammatory mediators, although activating effects occur in some pathogen and cell contexts. | Antibody, receptor-expression, and ligand-response experiments support MAPK suppression, but the direct biochemical target and relative contribution of each kinase remain unresolved. (pqac-00000001, pqac-00000018, pqac-00000019) |
| PI3K/Akt | TLR10 engagement is linked to PI3K/Akt-dependent induction of IL-1 receptor antagonist; some inhibitory models also show reduced Akt phosphorylation. | Promotes an anti-inflammatory cytokine balance by increasing IL-1Ra and opposing IL-1β activity. | Pharmacologic and cellular evidence supports PI3K/Akt involvement in IL-1Ra induction, although pathway direction varies among experimental systems. (pqac-00000005, pqac-00000019, pqac-00000020) |
| IRAK–TRAF6–AP-1 cascade | In an activating configuration, MyD88/TIRAP recruitment may engage IRAK1, IRAK4, and TRAF6, followed by AP-1 and NF-κB activation. | Potentially induces TNF-α, IL-1, IL-6, IL-12, IL-18, ENA-78, and related inflammatory genes. | Much of the activating evidence comes from chimeric or engineered TLR10 constructs and selected infection models; support is less definitive for endogenous TLR10. (pqac-00000016, pqac-00000017) |
| Cytokine and chemokine regulation | TLR10 integrates MyD88, STAT3/SOCS3, IRF7, NF-κB, MAPK, and PI3K/Akt signaling. Output depends on ligand, dimer partner, compartment, and cell type. | Inhibitory profile: lower IFN-α, IFN-λ, TNF-α, IL-1β, IL-6, and IL-8 with higher IL-1Ra or IL-10. Activating profile: increased IL-8, CCL20, IL-6, or TNF-α. | Primary pDC evidence strongly supports selective antiviral suppression, whereas monocyte, epithelial-cell, overexpression, knockdown, and antibody studies show mixed pro- and anti-inflammatory effects. (pqac-00000019, pqac-00000028, pqac-00000032, pqac-00000040) |


*Table: Summary of the principal activating and inhibitory pathways attributed to human TLR10. The table distinguishes strong primary-cell evidence from context-dependent or incompletely resolved signaling models.*
