| Feature | Annotation | Evidence/interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Identity and isoforms | Human **LYN** encodes the Src-family non-receptor tyrosine kinase Lyn (**UniProt P07948**). Alternative splicing produces **LynA/p56Lyn** (512 aa) and **LynB/p53Lyn**, which lacks a 21-aa segment in the N-terminal unique region. | Identity, organism, accession, and isoform structures are directly established. Major isoform-specific functional differences remain incompletely defined. |
| Domain architecture | N-terminal **SH4 membrane-targeting region → intrinsically disordered unique region → SH3 domain → SH2 domain → SH1 protein-tyrosine-kinase domain → C-terminal regulatory tail**. | Direct sequence and structural evidence; this matches canonical Src-family kinase organization. |
| Catalytic reaction | **ATP + protein-L-tyrosine → ADP + protein-L-tyrosine phosphate + H⁺** (EC 2.7.10.2). Lyn transfers ATP’s γ-phosphoryl group to tyrosine residues in protein substrates. | Direct enzymatic and structural evidence. Lyn is a protein tyrosine kinase, not a receptor, transporter, or structural protein. |
| Substrate recognition | Specificity is determined jointly by kinase-domain sequence preference, SH2 binding to phosphotyrosine motifs, SH3 binding to proline-rich motifs, receptor-associated complexes, and membrane colocalization. Major substrates include ITAM- and ITIM-bearing receptors and associated signaling proteins. | Direct evidence supports numerous substrates, but Lyn has no single exclusive consensus substrate; physiological specificity strongly depends on cell type, receptor complex, and localization. |
| Activating regulation: Y397 | Trans-autophosphorylation of activation-loop **Tyr397** stabilizes the active kinase conformation and increases catalytic activity. | Direct biochemical, phosphoproteomic, structural, and disease-variant evidence; phospho-Y397 is commonly used as an activation-associated readout. |
| Inhibitory regulation: Y508 | **CSK/CHK phosphorylation of Tyr508** creates an intramolecular SH2-binding site; together with SH3–linker engagement, this produces a compact autoinhibited conformation. CD45/CD148-mediated dephosphorylation favors opening and activation. | Direct structural and functional evidence. Human variants that remove Y508 or prevent its phosphorylation cause constitutive activity. |
| Membrane localization and acylation | Gly2 **N-myristoylation** and Cys3 **S-palmitoylation** target Lyn to the cytoplasmic face of the plasma membrane and promote lipid-raft partitioning. Lyn also traffics through the Golgi and has reported endosomal and nuclear pools. | Mutating Gly2 abolishes normal membrane targeting; Cys3 mutation strongly reduces raft association. Plasma-membrane and Golgi localization are well supported, whereas other pools are more context-dependent. |
| Principal activating ITAM pathway | After BCR or Fc-receptor clustering, Lyn phosphorylates receptor ITAMs—such as those in **CD79A/Igα, CD79B/Igβ, or Fc-receptor subunits**—creating docking sites for SYK. Downstream signaling engages PI3K–AKT, BTK–PLCγ, Ca²⁺, MAPK, and NF-κB pathways. | Strong receptor-proximal evidence. Lyn’s initiating role is partly redundant with other Src-family kinases, so its necessity varies by receptor and cell type. |
| Principal inhibitory ITIM pathway | Lyn phosphorylates ITIMs in **CD22, FcγRIIB, PIR-B, SIRPα, CD5, CD72, and CEACAM1**. Phospho-ITIMs recruit **SHP-1/SHP-2** or **SHIP-1/SHIP-2**, which remove activating protein or lipid phosphates and restrain BCR, Fc-receptor, integrin, and innate-receptor signaling. | Strong genetic and biochemical evidence, especially in B and myeloid cells. This inhibitory function is comparatively nonredundant and explains why Lyn deficiency can cause hyperactivation and autoimmunity. |
| Recent human disease evidence: LAVLI | De novo **p.Q507\***, **p.Y508\***, and **p.Y508F** gain-of-function variants were reported in three unrelated boys with perinatal neutrophilic cutaneous small-vessel vasculitis and systemic inflammation; two developed early liver fibrosis. Mutant signaling increased endothelial ICAM-1, neutrophil β2-integrins, adhesion, and transendothelial migration. | Direct human genetic, patient-cell, induced-endothelial-cell, isogenic-correction, and biochemical evidence establishes causal constitutive Lyn activation. The syndrome is termed **Lyn kinase-associated vasculopathy and liver fibrosis (LAVLI)**. |
| Translational status | Multi-kinase drugs such as **dasatinib** inhibit Lyn and are approved for other kinase-driven diseases; dasatinib produced mechanistically consistent improvement, including reported resolution of liver fibrosis, in an individual LAVLI case. Lyn is also indirectly affected by therapies targeting BCR-pathway components. | Precision-treatment evidence is compelling but anecdotal. No selective Lyn inhibitor or activator is established as an approved LYN-specific therapy, and indiscriminate inhibition is risky because both deficient and excessive Lyn signaling can be pathogenic. |


*Table: Concise annotation of human LYN/P07948 covering molecular identity, catalytic and regulatory mechanisms, localization, immune-signaling pathways, recent human disease evidence, and translational status.*
