# Human LYN (UniProt P07948): Functional-Annotation Research Report

## Executive summary

The target is unambiguous: **human LYN**, officially *LYN proto-oncogene, Src family tyrosine kinase*, corresponding to **UniProt P07948**. It is the same protein historically called Lck/Yes-related novel tyrosine kinase, p56Lyn or p53Lyn—not another similarly named gene. Human LYN encodes a membrane-associated, non-receptor protein-tyrosine kinase of the Src family, with the expected SH4–unique–SH3–SH2–SH1 architecture and two principal splice isoforms, LynA and LynB. Thus, the identity, organism, family and domains all match the supplied target specification. (weerawarna2023lynkinasestructure pages 1-3, l’estrangestranieri2024thedualisticrole pages 2-4)

LYN’s primary biochemical function is ATP-dependent phosphorylation of protein tyrosine residues. Its defining physiological role is not simply to “turn immune signaling on”: it is a receptor-proximal signaling switch that phosphorylates both activating **ITAMs** and inhibitory **ITIMs**. In B cells and myeloid cells, its activating function is often partly redundant with other Src-family kinases, whereas its recruitment of SHP and SHIP phosphatases through inhibitory receptors is comparatively difficult to replace. This explains the apparently paradoxical observation that both too little and too much Lyn signaling can cause inflammatory or autoimmune disease. (l’estrangestranieri2024thedualisticrole pages 4-6, l’estrangestranieri2024thedualisticrole pages 6-7, l’estrangestranieri2024thedualisticrole pages 1-2)

## 1. Identity verification and protein organization

The reviewed human sequence is explicitly identified as **UniProt P07948** and encodes a 512-amino-acid long isoform, **LynA/p56Lyn**. Alternative use of exon 2 produces **LynB/p53Lyn**, lacking 21 residues in the N-terminal unique region. LynA’s insert includes Tyr32, reported as an EGFR phosphorylation site, but a settled, universal functional distinction between LynA and LynB has not been demonstrated. (weerawarna2023lynkinasestructure pages 1-3, weerawarna2023lynkinasestructure pages 3-4)

The N-to-C-terminal organization is:

1. **SH4 region**, containing the lipidation signals that attach Lyn to membranes;
2. an intrinsically disordered **unique region**, including the 21-residue isoform difference;
3. an **SH3 domain**, which recognizes proline-rich motifs and participates in autoinhibition;
4. an **SH2 domain**, which recognizes phosphotyrosine-containing motifs;
5. the **SH1/protein-tyrosine-kinase domain**; and
6. a short C-terminal regulatory tail containing Tyr508. (weerawarna2023lynkinasestructure pages 1-3, weerawarna2023lynkinasestructure pages 3-4)

This agrees with the supplied InterPro assignments for an SH3 domain, SH2 domain and protein-kinase domain and confirms membership in the non-receptor Src-family branch of the tyrosine-kinase superfamily.

## 2. Primary molecular function

### 2.1 Catalytic reaction

LYN is an EC 2.7.10.2 protein-tyrosine kinase. Its reaction can be written as:

**ATP + protein-L-tyrosine → ADP + protein-L-tyrosine phosphate + H⁺.**

The kinase domain binds ATP in the cleft between its N- and C-terminal lobes and transfers ATP’s γ-phosphoryl group to a substrate tyrosine hydroxyl. The domain contains the canonical glycine-rich phosphate-binding loop, αC helix, activation loop and DFG motif found in eukaryotic protein kinases. (weerawarna2023lynkinasestructure pages 3-4, weerawarna2023lynkinasestructure pages 4-7)

### 2.2 Substrate specificity

LYN is not adequately described by one exclusive linear substrate motif. Physiological specificity emerges from four cooperating mechanisms:

* recognition of local peptide sequence by the catalytic domain;
* SH2 binding to phosphotyrosine motifs;
* SH3 binding to proline-rich motifs; and
* colocalization with receptor complexes in membrane nanodomains.

Its best-established substrates are tyrosines in receptor or adaptor **ITAMs** and **ITIMs**, together with signaling proteins such as CD19, PLCγ2, SHP/SHIP phosphatases, c-CBL-associated complexes and context-specific receptors such as ROR1. Consequently, substrate use depends strongly on cell type, receptor ligation and membrane organization. (weerawarna2023lynkinasestructure pages 3-4, l’estrangestranieri2024thedualisticrole pages 4-6, jesus2023constitutivelyactivelyn pages 3-6)

### 2.3 Activity switch

Two tyrosines provide the core regulatory switch:

* **Tyr397**, in the activation loop, undergoes trans-autophosphorylation and stabilizes the active kinase conformation. Its phosphorylation is widely used as an activation-associated biochemical readout and has been repeatedly detected by phosphoproteomics. (weerawarna2023lynkinasestructure pages 4-7, smolnigUnknownyearphosphorylationactivityand pages 61-64)
* **Tyr508**, in the C-terminal tail, is phosphorylated by CSK or CHK. Phospho-Tyr508 binds Lyn’s own SH2 domain, while the SH3 domain engages the SH2–kinase linker, producing a compact autoinhibited structure. CD45 or CD148 dephosphorylation favors opening and activation. (l’estrangestranieri2024thedualisticrole pages 2-4, weerawarna2023lynkinasestructure pages 4-7)

This is a conformational equilibrium rather than a binary switch: receptor recruitment, ligand valency, phosphatases, protein partners and membrane partitioning influence which substrates active Lyn encounters.

## 3. Cellular localization

### 3.1 Principal functional location

LYN primarily acts on the **cytoplasmic face of the plasma membrane**, especially in receptor-associated cholesterol/sphingolipid-rich nanodomains conventionally termed lipid rafts. Gly2 is cotranslationally N-myristoylated and Cys3 is reversibly S-palmitoylated. Myristoylation provides basal membrane affinity; palmitoylation strongly promotes stable raft partitioning and receptor-proximal signaling. (weerawarna2023lynkinasestructure pages 3-4, kovarova2001structurefunctionanalysisof pages 1-2, kovarova2001structurefunctionanalysisof pages 3-4)

Mutational experiments establish causality. A Gly2 mutation that prevents myristoylation delocalizes Lyn to the cytosol, while combined loss of myristoylation and palmitoylation abolishes plasma-membrane anchoring and FcεRI signaling. A Cys3 palmitoylation mutant can remain membrane-associated but has much weaker raft partitioning. In mast-cell-like cells, approximately 31% of wild-type Lyn was in raft fractions at rest versus 29% after receptor activation, whereas only 5.3% of the palmitoylation mutant was raft-associated at rest, rising to 10.3% after aggregation. (kovarova2001structurefunctionanalysisof pages 4-5, kinoshitakikuta2020proteinnmyristoylationdependentphosphorylationof pages 6-8, kovarova2001structurefunctionanalysisof pages 3-4)

### 3.2 Trafficking and secondary pools

Newly synthesized, N-myristoylated Lyn traffics through the **Golgi**, where membrane-associated CK1γ phosphorylates Lyn Ser13. The non-palmitoylated C3A mutant remains at the Golgi, whereas the non-myristoylated G2A mutant becomes cytosolic, supporting a model in which Lyn reaches the Golgi, is palmitoylated, and travels through secretory vesicles to the plasma membrane. (kinoshitakikuta2020proteinnmyristoylationdependentphosphorylationof pages 1-2, kinoshitakikuta2020proteinnmyristoylationdependentphosphorylationof pages 3-5)

Golgi and plasma-membrane localization are supported directly by imaging and mutation studies. Endosomal and nuclear Lyn pools have also been reported, including activation after DNA damage, but these appear context-dependent and are less central to its established receptor-proximal immune function. (l’estrangestranieri2024thedualisticrole pages 2-4)

## 4. Signaling pathways and biological processes

### 4.1 Activating immunoreceptor signaling

Following clustering of the B-cell receptor, Lyn phosphorylates ITAM tyrosines in **CD79A/Igα and CD79B/Igβ**. Doubly phosphorylated ITAMs recruit SYK, which propagates signaling through PI3K–AKT, BTK–PLCγ, calcium, MAPK and NF-κB pathways. These pathways regulate B-cell activation, survival, proliferation and differentiation. Lyn performs an analogous initiating function at Fc receptors; in mast cells, it phosphorylates FcεRI β- and γ-chain ITAMs, providing docking sites for SYK. (weerawarna2023lynkinasestructure pages 8-10, l’estrangestranieri2024thedualisticrole pages 2-4)

The activating role is often partly redundant: Fyn or other Src-family members can initiate some of the same pathways. Therefore, loss of Lyn does not merely cause immunodeficiency; in many settings, the more important phenotype is failure of negative feedback. (weerawarna2023lynkinasestructure pages 8-10, l’estrangestranieri2024thedualisticrole pages 6-7)

### 4.2 Inhibitory immunoreceptor signaling

LYN phosphorylates ITIMs in **CD22, FcγRIIB, CD5, CD72, PIR-B, SIRPα and CEACAM1**, creating binding sites for SH2-containing phosphatases. CD22 principally recruits SHP-1, which dephosphorylates signaling proteins and raises the BCR activation threshold. FcγRIIB recruits SHIP-1/SHIP-2; these convert PIP3 to PI(3,4)P2, reducing membrane recruitment of BTK and AKT and thereby limiting PLCγ activation, IP3/DAG production and calcium mobilization. (weerawarna2023lynkinasestructure pages 7-8, l’estrangestranieri2024thedualisticrole pages 7-8, l’estrangestranieri2024thedualisticrole pages 6-7)

In myeloid cells, Lyn-dependent SIRPα phosphorylation supports CD47-mediated inhibitory “don’t-eat-me” signaling through SHP-1/SHP-2. Lyn also phosphorylates NLRP3, promoting its ubiquitination and degradation; Lyn-deficient macrophages consequently produce excess IL-1β. PIR-B and CEACAM1 provide additional restraints on integrin, chemokine and innate-receptor signaling. (l’estrangestranieri2024thedualisticrole pages 7-8, l’estrangestranieri2024thedualisticrole pages 8-9)

The resulting model is a **signal-balancing circuit**: Lyn can initiate receptor signaling, then limit its magnitude and duration through inhibitory receptors, lipid phosphatases, protein phosphatases and ubiquitin-linked downregulation.

### 4.3 Other cell systems

Lyn is abundant in B-lineage and myeloid cells and is also functional in mast cells, platelets, endothelial cells and selected neural cells. In platelets and endothelial contexts it contributes to PECAM-1 phosphorylation and recruitment of SHP-1/SHP-2 and PLCγ1. In macrophages, raft-localized Lyn suppresses parts of TLR4/LPS signaling; mutation of its palmitoylation, SH2, SH3 or kinase functions reduces this inhibition and increases NF-κB/IRF3-associated signaling. (l’estrangestranieri2024thedualisticrole pages 7-8, borzeckasolarz2017associationoflyn pages 1-5, borzeckasolarz2017associationoflyn pages 43-46)

## 5. Recent developments, 2023–2024

### 5.1 Human gain-of-function disease establishes causal biology

The strongest recent advance was the March 2023 *Nature Communications* report of three unrelated boys with de novo **p.Q507\***, **p.Y508\*** or **p.Y508F** variants. All developed perinatal systemic inflammation and neutrophilic cutaneous small-vessel vasculitis; two developed early liver fibrosis. The variants delete Tyr508 or prevent its regulatory phosphorylation, causing constitutive Tyr397 phosphorylation and increased phosphorylation of Lyn-associated substrates. The syndrome was termed **Lyn kinase-associated vasculopathy and liver fibrosis (LAVLI)**. [Publication: March 2023; DOI URL: https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-023-36941-y.] (jesus2023constitutivelyactivelyn pages 2-3, jesus2023constitutivelyactivelyn pages 7-9)

Patient neutrophils had increased β2-integrins, and patient-derived endothelial cells had increased ICAM-1, enhanced neutrophil adhesion and transendothelial migration, reduced VE-cadherin at adhesion sites and reduced electrical barrier resistance. Isogenic gene correction normalized the phenotype more completely than pharmacological inhibition, providing unusually strong human-genetic and cellular evidence that constitutive LYN signaling was causal. (jesus2023constitutivelyactivelyn pages 1-2, jesus2023constitutivelyactivelyn pages 7-9, jesus2023constitutivelyactivelyn pages 9-10)

TNF inhibition improved systemic inflammation. Compassionate/off-label dasatinib, a broad Src-family inhibitor, reduced cellular abnormalities and was associated with resolution of liver fibrosis in one patient. This is compelling mechanism-guided precision medicine, but it remains evidence from an ultra-rare case series—not a controlled trial or an approved LYN-specific treatment. (jesus2023constitutivelyactivelyn pages 1-2, jesus2023constitutivelyactivelyn pages 9-10)

### 5.2 2024 interpretation in lupus

A June 2024 expert review emphasized that LYN is **dualistic**, not uniformly pro- or anti-inflammatory. Both Lyn-null mice and animals with constitutively active Lyn develop lupus-like inflammatory disease. Human SLE studies likewise conflict: some report reduced LYN RNA/protein, increased ubiquitination or increased CSK-mediated suppression in B cells, whereas transcriptomic studies report increased LYN in whole blood or antibody-secreting cells in some cohorts. [Publication: June 2024; DOI URL: https://doi.org/10.3389/fimmu.2024.1395427.] (l’estrangestranieri2024thedualisticrole pages 9-10, l’estrangestranieri2024thedualisticrole pages 1-2)

Reported SLE-associated LYN SNPs are non-coding, incompletely annotated and not consistently reproduced across ancestry groups. Moreover, most human work has focused on B cells and may miss opposing effects in myeloid cells. The authoritative interpretation is therefore that LYN state could help define mechanistic patient subsets, but neither global inhibition nor global activation is presently justified for unselected SLE. (l’estrangestranieri2024thedualisticrole pages 9-10, l’estrangestranieri2024thedualisticrole pages 8-9)

## 6. Disease and translational evidence

### Hematologic malignancy

LYN can act as a receptor-proximal survival kinase in B-cell malignancy, but its importance varies by disease and molecular context. Open Targets links LYN to cancer, acute lymphoblastic leukemia and chronic myeloid leukemia; these aggregated associations support relevance but do not by themselves prove that LYN is a disease driver or independently druggable target. (OpenTargets Search: -LYN)

In primary CLL cells, a 2022 study found that Lyn phosphorylates ROR1—especially Tyr645/Tyr646—and promotes c-CBL recruitment, linking BCR and Wnt5a–ROR1 signaling. In paired primary-cell migration experiments, 25 nM dasatinib reduced CCL19-directed chemotaxis by about **25%** and blunted migration-associated ROR1 surface increases; ROR1 was evaluated in 15 samples and CCR7 in 13. These are mechanistic ex vivo data, not clinical-response evidence. [Publication: February 2022; DOI URL: https://doi.org/10.3389/fcell.2022.838871.] (dave2022lynphosphorylatesand pages 6-9, dave2022lynphosphorylatesand pages 5-6)

In DLBCL, SHP-1 was positive in **114/150 tumors (76%)**, with high expression in 57/150 (38%). Pharmacological SHP-1 activation reduced p-Lyn, p-BTK and p-STAT3, induced apoptosis, and suppressed U2932 xenografts; however, the mouse treatment groups were only N=4, so this remains preclinical. The related clinical observation that ibrutinib produced responses in 20/80 relapsed/refractory DLBCL patients—37% in ABC versus 5% in GCB-like disease—supports BCR-pathway dependence, but ibrutinib targets BTK rather than proving clinical efficacy of direct Lyn inhibition. [Publication: August 2022; DOI URL: https://doi.org/10.1186/s10020-022-00518-0.] (chen2022interferingbcell pages 4-7, chen2022interferingbcell pages 1-2, chen2022interferingbcell pages 2-4)

### Autoimmune and autoinflammatory disease

Loss of Lyn-mediated ITIM signaling lowers activation thresholds and can expand autoreactive B cells, while gain-of-function variants increase activating receptor, endothelial and neutrophil signaling. LYN-related inflammation is therefore best understood as failure to maintain an optimal signaling range rather than a simple excess-or-deficiency disorder. The LAVLI cases provide direct human evidence for the gain-of-function side; the loss-of-function side is supported most strongly by mouse genetics and heterogeneous SLE patient observations. (jesus2023constitutivelyactivelyn pages 3-6, l’estrangestranieri2024thedualisticrole pages 9-10, l’estrangestranieri2024thedualisticrole pages 12-13)

## 7. Clinical implementation and trial status

No highly selective LYN inhibitor or activator is established as an approved LYN-specific therapy. Existing translational interventions use multi-kinase drugs or modulate downstream BCR components.

* **NCT00438854**, Phase II dasatinib in relapsed CLL: completed, 15 participants. LYN activity in blood and marrow was an explicit mechanistic objective, but no numerical outcomes were available in the retrieved registry excerpt. ClinicalTrials.gov URL: https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT00438854. (NCT00438854 chunk 1)
* **NCT01234740**, Phase I bafetinib in recurrent high-grade glioma or brain metastases: completed, seven participants. Bafetinib was described as a dual BCR-ABL/LYN inhibitor, but the study’s primary focus was brain pharmacokinetics; Lyn/Fyn phosphorylation was correlative. ClinicalTrials.gov URL: https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT01234740. (NCT01234740 chunk 1)
* **NCT01538342**, psoriasis/atopic-dermatitis basic-science study: completed, 170 participants. It measured Lyn expression, phosphorylation and caspase-dependent cleavage in biopsies; it did not administer a Lyn inhibitor. ClinicalTrials.gov URL: https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT01538342. (NCT01538342 chunk 1)

These records demonstrate clinical interest in Lyn biology but do not establish selective target validation. Dasatinib and bafetinib inhibit several kinases, so efficacy or toxicity cannot be attributed to Lyn alone.

## 8. Evidence assessment and expert interpretation

**Highest-confidence annotations** are the target identity, Src-family domain organization, ATP-dependent tyrosine phosphorylation, Tyr397/Tyr508 regulatory switch, membrane targeting by myristoylation/palmitoylation, and dual ITAM/ITIM function. These are supported by sequence, structural, biochemical, mutational and cellular evidence. (weerawarna2023lynkinasestructure pages 1-3, weerawarna2023lynkinasestructure pages 4-7, kovarova2001structurefunctionanalysisof pages 4-5)

**Strong but context-dependent annotations** include individual receptor substrates and whether Lyn’s net effect is activating or inhibitory. The same enzyme can produce opposite outputs depending on receptor ligation, ligand valency, membrane compartment, cell lineage and availability of SHP/SHIP phosphatases. (l’estrangestranieri2024thedualisticrole pages 4-6, weerawarna2023lynkinasestructure pages 7-8)

**Emerging evidence** includes nuclear and neurodegenerative functions, isoform-specific biology and broad therapeutic targeting. These areas have plausible mechanistic support but are less mature than Lyn’s immune-receptor function. (weerawarna2023lynkinasestructure pages 1-3, l’estrangestranieri2024thedualisticrole pages 2-4)

**Clinical conclusion:** LYN is biologically validated but pharmacologically difficult. A useful therapy would ideally normalize the disease-relevant branch of signaling in the relevant cell type. Global kinase inhibition risks suppressing both pathological activation and physiologically important inhibitory feedback. The 2023–2024 literature therefore favors molecular stratification and pathway-aware precision treatment over indiscriminate LYN inhibition. (jesus2023constitutivelyactivelyn pages 9-10, l’estrangestranieri2024thedualisticrole pages 9-10, l’estrangestranieri2024thedualisticrole pages 1-2)
