# DOK7 evidence notes

## 2026-10-18: initial review

DOK7 has two complementary signaling functions. Its N-terminal PH–PTB region binds phosphorylated MUSK and promotes receptor pairing and activation. Its phosphorylated tail recruits CRK and CRKL, coordinating downstream synaptic differentiation. MUSK supplies kinase catalysis; DOK7 is an activator and adaptor. The two core entries therefore represent distinct mechanisms rather than two copies of generic kinase binding.

The structural study separates its reagents carefully: human DOK7 residues 1–220 were used in solution binding and oligomerization measurements, whereas mouse PH–PTB was used for the ligand-bound crystal, lipid binding and direct kinase-activation measurements with rat MUSK. Mouse full-length Dok7 was expressed in NIH3T3 cells. The dimer-interface mutant distinguishes receptor binding from efficient activation. See [Bergamin et al., 2010](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2917201/), Results on the Dok7–MuSK complex and stimulation of kinase activity, and Experimental Procedures.

The second mechanism is supported by the separately recovered [Hallock et al., 2010 original](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2964755/). Figures 1–5 and the DNA constructs, cell culture, phosphopeptide affinity purification and biochemical Methods establish murine Dok7 phosphorylation, CRK/CRKL recruitment and AChR-clustering effects. The cDNA was cloned from C2 mouse muscle cells. HEK293 is an expression host, not evidence of a human construct. Phosphopeptide capture was performed from lysate; it is not a purified two-protein reconstitution. The current normal publication cache contains only the abstract, despite the successful separate original recovery. Discussion proposes Rac/Rho exchange-factor linkage; it does not directly measure that linkage.
