# ENG review notes

## Biological synthesis

Endoglin is an extracellular ligand-binding coreceptor anchored at the plasma membrane. Human receptor-domain and reporter experiments place ENG in TGF-beta receptor systems, while direct BMP9/BMP10 binding and human endothelial signaling studies support a mechanistic role in BMP receptor signaling. The signaling receptors supply kinase activity; ENG does not become a kinase because receptor phosphorylation or SMAD activation depends on it. [PMID:12015308](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12015308/), [PMID:21737454](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21737454/), [PMID:23300529](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23300529/), [PMID:28564608](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28564608/)

The orphan region provides the directly resolved BMP9 interface, and the ZP region participates in covalent dimerization. A solved soluble ENG–BMP9 complex does not establish the complete membrane receptor assembly or demonstrate the proposed ligand-handoff step. Earlier truncation-based Cys350 interpretations must remain distinct from the later C350–C382 intramolecular disulfide structure. Soluble engineered ectodomains can sequester ligand and inhibit signaling; this does not reverse the function of membrane ENG. [PMID:21737454](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21737454/), [PMID:22347366](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22347366/), [PMID:28564608](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28564608/)

TGF-beta and activin/BMP7 experiments often examine receptor systems using affinity labeling, chemical crosslinking and immunoprecipitation. They should not be silently represented as isolated binary affinities. Three imported annotations explicitly state `contributes_to`, and that contribution is evaluated on its own terms. [PMID:1326540](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1326540/), [PMID:9872992](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9872992/)

## Natural products and preparations

The official natural products are P17813-1/Long, 658 residues, and P17813-2/Short, 625 residues. Short replaces long-product residues 619–658 with EYPRPPQ through VSP_004233. Their shared extracellular region does not establish equivalent cytoplasmic signaling. Signal peptide 1–25, processing of the mature protein, soluble shed forms, Fc/His fusions and engineered deletions are not additional natural splice products. Historical cDNAs and constructs remain unassigned to a current product where exact sequences were unavailable. [UniProt P17813](https://www.uniprot.org/uniprotkb/P17813/entry), [PMID:8370410](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8370410/), [PMID:18974388](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18974388/)

The porcine donor is P37176. Its historical 643-residue clone is not assumed to be identical to the current 653-residue record. Human ENG expressed in monkey, mouse or rat hosts remains a human construct in a different cellular system; native mouse knockout and human transgene experiments are recorded separately. [PMID:8294451](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8294451/), [PMID:12015308](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12015308/), [PMID:17628518](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17628518/)

## Annotation decisions and unresolved evidence

All 104 frozen source objects have proposed decisions: 29 ACCEPT, 62 KEEP_AS_NON_CORE, one MODIFY, two MARK_AS_OVER_ANNOTATED and ten UNDECIDED. The generic receptor activity is refined to coreceptor activity, GO:0015026. All fourteen supported generic protein-binding rows remain non-core under the campaign policy. No new GO annotation or publication quotation is introduced.

The two glycan-recognition assignments from the 44G4-antigen study exceed its inspected experiment. Lectin affinity, galactose competition and glycosidase treatment characterize sugars carried by ENG; the lectin is the recognizing molecule. This preserves the positive glycosylation findings. The separate porcine glycosaminoglycan claim remains unresolved. [PMID:3262645](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3262645/), [PMID:8294451](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8294451/)

The three PAINT annotations have authentic ancestral IBD matches on the human target path, with a target MSA sequence matching all 658 official residues. A short donor list or the target's occurrence in its own evidence list is not a reason to reject them. The vasculogenesis uncertainty instead concerns the developmental step: two inspected mouse originals preserve early endothelial differentiation and primitive vascular networks while disrupting subsequent remodeling and branching. The human HHT original needed to settle its particular process annotations remains inaccessible. This supports uncertainty about the exact claim, not universal absence of ENG participation or a false ancestral placement. [PMID:10348742](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10348742/), [PMID:10562296](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10562296/), [PMID:7894484](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7894484/)

Five imported NOT annotations remain literal. Four have bounded early-development or gain-of-function evidence; they do not assert a negative in every tissue, developmental stage or ligand context. The negative cushion-transition assertion remains unresolved because its precise original assay was not recovered. The related positive cushion-formation transition term has a different definition and is not mechanically treated as its logical opposite. [PMID:17628518](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17628518/), [PMID:19703439](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19703439/), [PMID:12941632](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12941632/)

Most vascular, smooth-muscle and cardiac developmental outcomes are retained as non-core contexts. Trabeculation may include secondary hypoxia. Outflow septation failure occurred in combined human-ENG overexpression and Smad4 loss in mouse neural crest. Neither creates a new autonomous ENG molecular activity. Precise repression, dorsal-aorta and negative cushion-transition assertions from the inaccessible 2003 original remain uncertain. [PMID:17628518](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17628518/), [PMID:19703439](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19703439/), [PMID:12941632](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12941632/)
