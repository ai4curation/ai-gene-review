CC   -!- FUNCTION: Kisspeptins are ligands for the G protein-coupled receptor
CC       KISS1R/GPR54 (PubMed:11385580, PubMed:11457843, PubMed:11527393,
CC       PubMed:12879005, PubMed:15020672, PubMed:15596153). The hypothalamic
CC       KISS1/KISS1R signaling system plays a central role in the regulation of
CC       the hypothalamic-pituitary-gonadal reproductive axis by modulating the
CC       secretion of gonadotropin-releasing hormone (GnRH) from GnRH neurons
CC       (PubMed:15219839, PubMed:15598687, PubMed:22335740). In these neurons,
CC       kisspeptin binding to its receptor activates G(q)-dependent signaling,
CC       leading to phospholipase C (PLC) activation, and hydrolysis of
CC       phosphatidylinositol 4,5-bisphosphate (PIP2) (PubMed:15598687,
CC       PubMed:22335740). The subsequent rise in intracellular calcium levels
CC       results in the inhibition of inward rectifier potassium channels and
CC       activation of TRPC-like cation channels, leading to GnRH neurons
CC       depolarization and stimulation (By similarity). In addition to this
CC       pathway, kisspeptin also triggers G(q)-independent signaling via beta-
CC       arrestin, leading to MAPK cascade activation and ERK1/ERK2
CC       phosphorylation (PubMed:25147978). Kisspeptins are also involved in the
CC       regulation of other processes including cell growth, cell proliferation
CC       and cell migration (PubMed:11385580, PubMed:11457843, PubMed:11527393,
CC       PubMed:12879005, PubMed:15020672, PubMed:15596153).
CC       {ECO:0000250|UniProtKB:Q6Y4S4, ECO:0000269|PubMed:11385580,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:11457843, ECO:0000269|PubMed:11527393,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:12879005, ECO:0000269|PubMed:15020672,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:15219839, ECO:0000269|PubMed:15596153,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:15598687, ECO:0000269|PubMed:22335740,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:25147978}.
CC   -!- FUNCTION: [Kisspeptin-10]: Binds the G protein-coupled receptor
CC       KISS1R/GPR54 and triggers G protein-coupled receptor signaling via
CC       activation of G(q) and phosphatidylinositol 4,5-bisphosphate (PIP2)
CC       hydrolysis by phospholipase C (PubMed:11457843, PubMed:22335740).
CC       Binding to the receptor also activates beta-arrestin-dependent
CC       signaling resulting in ERK1/2 phosphorylation (PubMed:11457843,
CC       PubMed:25147978). Activation of the receptor inhibits cell
CC       proliferation and cell migration, and is involved in the regulation of
CC       trophoblast invasion during early stages of pregnancy. Is also involved
CC       in the modulation of airway smooth muscle cells migration
CC       (PubMed:11457843, PubMed:15020672, PubMed:38512807). In bone tissue,
CC       activation of KISS1R by kisspeptin-10 recruits phosphatase DUSP18 and
CC       SRC to the KISS1R C-terminus through a G(q)-dependent signaling
CC       pathway. This leads to DUSP18-mediated dephosphorylation of SRC, down-
CC       regulation of osteoclast differentiation and activity, and consequently
CC       suppression of bone resorption (By similarity).
CC       {ECO:0000250|UniProtKB:Q6Y4S4, ECO:0000269|PubMed:11457843,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:15020672, ECO:0000269|PubMed:22335740,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:25147978, ECO:0000269|PubMed:38512807}.
CC   -!- FUNCTION: [Metastin]: Receptor binding triggers G protein-coupled
CC       receptor signaling via activation of G(q) and phosphatidylinositol 4,5-
CC       bisphosphate (PIP2) hydrolysis by phospholipase C.
CC       {ECO:0000269|PubMed:39151001}.

CC   -!- DISEASE: Hypogonadotropic hypogonadism 13 with or without anosmia
CC       (HH13) [MIM:614842]: A disorder characterized by absent or incomplete
CC       sexual maturation by the age of 18 years, in conjunction with low
CC       levels of circulating gonadotropins and testosterone and no other
CC       abnormalities of the hypothalamic-pituitary axis. In some cases, it is
CC       associated with non-reproductive phenotypes, such as anosmia, cleft
CC       palate, and sensorineural hearing loss. Anosmia or hyposmia is related
CC       to the absence or hypoplasia of the olfactory bulbs and tracts.
CC       Hypogonadism is due to deficiency in gonadotropin-releasing hormone and
CC       probably results from a failure of embryonic migration of gonadotropin-
CC       releasing hormone-synthesizing neurons. In the presence of anosmia,
CC       idiopathic hypogonadotropic hypogonadism is referred to as Kallmann
CC       syndrome, whereas in the presence of a normal sense of smell, it has
CC       been termed normosmic idiopathic hypogonadotropic hypogonadism (nIHH).
CC       {ECO:0000269|PubMed:22335740}. Note=The disease is caused by variants
CC       affecting the gene represented in this entry.
