VOPP1 (Q96AW1): scaffolding determinants beyond the PPPY motif

Summary

VOPP1 does have determinants besides the PPPY motif, and the topology question the seed hypothesis asks can be answered cleanly from sequence and the AlphaFold model. VOPP1 is a type I single-pass membrane protein: a cleaved signal peptide at 1-22, a cysteine-rich lumenal domain at 23-60 that AlphaFold models as folded (mean pLDDT 82.0) and stapled by two disulfides, a transmembrane helix at 61-81 (mean pLDDT 83.6, Kyte-Doolittle maximum 2.24 at residue 72), and a 91-residue cytoplasmic tail at 82-172 that is 27.5% proline and largely disordered (mean pLDDT 46.7 across the proline-rich core, 58.1% of all residues below pLDDT 70). Every candidate interaction motif except one lies on the cytoplasmic face. The four determinants beyond PPPY 162-165, ranked by the support behind them, are a second PPxY at 154-157 (PPAY in human, PPSY in most orthologs, PPxY class in 27/27 aligned vertebrates), a 100% conserved RRRM juxtamembrane basic patch at 82-85, a disulfide-stapled lumenal module at 23-60 with no N-glycosylation sequon, and a YxxPhi at 165-168 (YEQV, 94.4% conserved).

The hypothesis fails in two specific ways, which is why the verdict is partial. First, the YxxPhi is not an independent determinant: YEQV 165-168 overlaps PPPY 162-165 and shares the single tyrosine Y165, so WW-domain occupancy and AP mu-subunit recognition are mutually exclusive uses of one residue. A positional benchmark computed in iteration 3 weakens it further. In LAMP1 (GYQTI), LAMP2 (GYEQF), CD63 (GYEVM), LAPTM5 (PPPYSEV) and TSPAN6 (QYEIV) the signal tyrosine sits exactly 3 residues from the C-terminus, and the three classical LAMP-type signals carry glycine at the -1 position. VOPP1's Y165 has 7 residues after it and is preceded by proline. It fails both positional requirements of the canonical lysosomal tyrosine signal. Second, the only mutagenesis available says single-motif adaptor: in PMID: 30285739, reaching UniProt as a curated mutagenesis series, Y119A and Y157A leave WWOX binding unchanged and only Y165A reduces it.

The multivalent-scaffold reading survives in a weaker, architecture-level form. VOPP1 presents a conserved two-PPxY array on a membrane-anchored, disordered, proline-rich tail, which is the construction of a WW-domain display platform. That construction is rare and compartment-specific: the overlapping C-terminal PPxY+YxxPhi module occurs in only 33 of 20,414 reviewed human proteins (0.16%, binomial one-sided p = 7.3e-04 for C-terminal placement), and that set is 6.10-fold enriched for endosome, lysosome and cytoplasmic-vesicle annotation (Fisher one-sided p = 1.96e-05) and 3.95-fold enriched for transmembrane proteins (p = 9.63e-05), with LAPTM5, ARRDC3, ARRDC4, TMEM207 and ATP9A among its members. Multivalency is a prediction from architecture and conservation. It is not yet an experimental observation.

Key findings

F1. VOPP1 is a type I membrane protein with one folded lumenal module and a fully disordered cytoplasmic tail

Per-residue pLDDT across all 172 residues of AF-Q96AW1-F1 (model v6) gives a mean of 65.6, with 58.1% of residues below 70 and 24.4% below 50. The segment means separate the protein cleanly: signal peptide 1-22 at 74.5, cysteine-rich lumenal domain 23-60 at 82.0, TM helix 61-81 at 83.6, juxtamembrane 82-101 at 56.2, proline-rich region 102-153 at 46.7, and the C-terminal PY region 154-172 at 64.0. Kyte-Doolittle hydropathy with a window of 19 produces exactly two hydrophobic peaks: 1.03 centred at residue 13 (signal-peptide h-region, core ALLLGL at 10-15) and 2.24 centred at residue 72 (TM core LLMMGVLFCCGA at 66-77). The cytoplasmic region 86-172 peaks at only 0.25, so there is no second membrane-embedded segment. The cytoplasmic tail 82-172 contains 27.5% proline with a single leucine and a single isoleucine.

The topology assignment is supported by a juxtamembrane basic cluster RRRM at 82-85, 100% identical across all 27 aligned vertebrate orthologs, which satisfies the positive-inside rule for a cytoplasmic C-terminus. Six of the eleven cysteines (C26, C39, C45, C46, C50, C51) lie in the lumenal domain 23-60, which InterPro and Pfam define as a distinct module (PF27964 VOPP1_N, IPR060429).

The consequence for the hypothesis is that the only folded part of VOPP1 is the lumenal module plus the TM helix. Every candidate scaffolding motif sits in an intrinsically disordered cytoplasmic tail, which is the architecture expected of a short-linear-motif hub and not of a globular adaptor. The single strong hydrophobic peak at 66-77, with a weaker N-terminal peak, supports a cleaved signal peptide plus one TM helix (type I), not a signal anchor with two TMs.

F2. Three PPxY motifs, two deeply conserved, but only the C-terminal PPPY is required for WWOX binding

A regex scan of Q96AW1 finds three PPxY matches, all cytoplasmic: PPYY at 116-119, PPAY at 154-157 and PPPY at 162-165. Global alignment of human VOPP1 to 26 vertebrate orthologs (mean identity 78.0%, range 58.1-100%) gives mean per-residue identity of 95.4% for PPPY 162-165, with the exact sequence PPPY in 22/27 and the remainder PPSY, so the PPxY class is retained in 27/27. PPAY 154-157 scores 77.8% per-residue identity (PPSY in 22/27), again with the PPxY class retained in 27/27. PPYY at 116-119 scores only 64.8%, and the first proline is lost in 22/27 orthologs (MPYY, VPYY, LPYY, MQYY).

A wider census of 218 vertebrate VOPP1 entries shows 213/218 (97.7%) have at least one PPxY in the C-terminal 25 residues and 212/218 (97.2%) have two or more PPxY motifs in the full sequence. Only 27/218 (12.4%) have three or more, so the human PPYY is an uncommon extra match rather than a conserved third site.

Against this, the UniProt-curated mutagenesis from PMID: 30285739 reports that Y119A does not affect WWOX binding, Y157A does not affect WWOX binding, and Y165A reduces, without abolishing, WWOX binding. Conservation and mutagenesis agree on PPYY 116-119: it is both the least conserved and the one shown not to matter. They disagree on PPAY 154-157, which is PPxY-class in 27/27 orthologs yet dispensable for WWOX. The resolution the data support is that VOPP1 is a single-motif adaptor for WWOX specifically, and that the conserved but WWOX-dispensable PPAY at 154-157 is a candidate docking site for a different WW-domain protein.

F3. The C-terminal PPPY overlaps a conserved YxxPhi, which is the protein's only classical endolysosomal sorting motif

The tyrosine of the PPPY motif (Y165) is also the tyrosine of a YxxPhi signal: Y165-E166-Q167-V168, embedded in the C-terminus PPPPYEQVVKAK. Across 27 aligned orthologs the YEQV window scores 94.4% mean per-residue identity, with variants YEQI, YEQL, YDQV and YEQA. In the 218-entry census, 205/218 (94.0%) carry a YxxPhi in the C-terminal 25 residues and 204/218 (93.6%) carry the overlapping dual module PP.Y..[LIVMF].

A full motif scan of human VOPP1 returned zero dileucine signals ([DE]xxxL[LIM] or DxxLL), zero NPxY, zero NPF, zero FYVE-type PI(3)P cores, zero class-II WW or SH3 ligands (PPLP, PPxR) and no R/KxxPxxP or PxxPxR SH3 consensus. The only other YxxPhi in the protein, YFWF at 62-65, lies inside the TM helix and is not exposed on the cytoplasmic face. Dileucine motifs occur in 4/218 orthologs and NPF in 1/218, so their absence in human VOPP1 is the vertebrate norm.

VOPP1 therefore has exactly one candidate adaptor-protein sorting signal, and it is superimposed on the WW-domain ligand. Because WW-domain engagement of PPxY buries the same tyrosine that mu-subunit recognition of YxxPhi requires, the two uses are mutually exclusive, and phosphorylation of Y165 would block both. This is a concrete, testable alternative to the seed hypothesis's expectation of separable determinants.

F4. The cytoplasmic tail is a poor ubiquitin acceptor, and the empty phosphosite record is a detection artifact

Human VOPP1 has five lysines in total (K7, K23, K24, K170, K172). Only two (K170, K172) are cytoplasmic, and both sit in the last three residues. The KAK window is exact in only 29.6% of 27 aligned orthologs (61.7% mean per-residue identity). Across 218 vertebrate orthologs the cytoplasmic tail contains a median of 1 lysine, distributed as 0 in 2 entries, 1 in 111, 2 in 89, 3 in 8 and 4 or more in 2.

Trypsin cleavage sites in Q96AW1 fall at residues 2, 3, 7, 23, 24, 40, 49, 53, 59, 82, 83, 84, 102, 170 and 172. The entire motif-bearing region therefore forms a single 68-residue tryptic peptide from R102 to K170, 27.5% proline, with no internal K or R. EBI Proteins API large-scale proteomics coverage for Q96AW1 is 43/172 residues (25%) with zero coverage of positions 150-172, and the proteomics-PTM endpoint returns HTTP 404 with no PTM features.

Two conclusions follow. VOPP1 is unlikely to act as a ubiquitinated cargo through its own tail, which favours a model where it recruits a ubiquitin ligase to act on partners rather than on itself. The empty phosphosite record for the PPxY/YxxPhi region is explained by an undetectable tryptic peptide, so the absence of annotated phosphorylation at Y157, Y165, T160 or S148 is not evidence against regulation. Resolving it needs a targeted assay, not a database lookup.

F5. VOPP1 co-purifies with NEDD4 as well as WWOX

IntAct via PSICQUIC returns 24 interactions for Q96AW1 from two sources. Eight are affinity-purification mass spectrometry associations from PMID: 33961781: NEDD4, WWOX, EPHA2, TMEM259, ZER1, LRRC15, GBF1 and MLST8. Sixteen are yeast two-hybrid physical associations from PMID: 16169070, including the endolysosomal proteins SNAPIN (BLOC-1/BORC) and LAMTOR5 (Ragulator), plus MTF2, CRIP1, MAP7D1, GLYAT, HMOX2, STMN2, TFG, SMPD3, PBK, OFD1, PFDN1 and TAC3.

NEDD4 is a HECT E3 ligase whose three to four WW domains bind PPxY ligands, the same motif class as VOPP1's PPAY 154-157 and PPPY 162-165, which lie 8 residues apart (Y157 to P162 separated by C-N-T-P). Two independent high-throughput datasets therefore place a second WW-domain protein and lysosome/endosome machinery in the VOPP1 interactome. The evidence is association-level, not direct binding, and NEDD4 binding to VOPP1 has not been mapped to a motif.

Current GO annotation for human VOPP1 (QuickGO, 14 annotations) carries no biological process term at all. MF is limited to protein binding (IPI, 30285739 and 33961781), enzyme binding (IPI, 30285739) and protein sequestering activity GO:0140311 (IDA, MGI, 30285739).

F6. The overlapping C-terminal module occurs in 33 of 20,414 human proteins and that set is 6-fold enriched for endolysosomal membranes

A scan of the full reviewed human proteome (UP000005640, 20,414 sequences) finds PPxY anywhere in 1,303 proteins (6.38%), the overlapping module PP.Y..[LIVMF] anywhere in 403 (1.97%), and the same module inside the C-terminal 25 residues in only 33 (0.16%), VOPP1 among them. With a mean protein length of 559 aa, uniform placement predicts 18.0 C-terminal occurrences against 33 observed, a 1.8-fold positional enrichment at binomial one-sided p = 7.3e-04.

Of those 33 proteins, 11 carry an endosome, lysosome or cytoplasmic-vesicle subcellular location in UniProt, against 1,556/20,414 in the background: Fisher exact one-sided OR = 6.10, p = 1.96e-05. Nineteen of 33 are transmembrane proteins against 25.6% background: OR = 3.95, p = 9.63e-05. The endolysosomal members are LAPTM5, ARRDC3, ARRDC4, TMEM207, ATP9A, ACP2, FLT1, NTRK1, SCNN1B, RNASE2 and VOPP1.

The architecture is not a chance coincidence of two common motifs. A PPxY immediately followed by a YxxPhi at the extreme C-terminus of a single-pass membrane protein is a rare and compartment-specific design shared with established NEDD4-family adaptors. This is convergent-architecture evidence that VOPP1's C-terminal module is a functional endolysosomal targeting and WW-recruitment unit, independent of the WWOX experiments. The limitation is that compartment labels come from UniProt subcellular-location annotation, so the enrichment inherits any bias in that annotation, and architecture similarity is not a functional test.

F7. LAPTM5 shows PY-dependent NEDD4 sorting without cargo ubiquitination, resolving the lysine-scarcity problem

LAPTM5 (Q13571) carries three PY motifs at 117 (LPAY), 236 (LPSY) and 256 (PPPY), the last ending the protein as GPAPPPYSEV, 7 residues from the C-terminus. VOPP1 carries three PPxY motifs at 116, 154 and 162, the last ending the protein as PPPPYEQVVKAK, 8 residues from the C-terminus. PMID: 17116753 states that "LAPTM5 contains three PY motifs (L/PPxY), which bind Nedd4-WW domains", that PY-mutant or Nedd4-knockdown LAPTM5 is retained in the Golgi, and that "ubiquitination-impaired LAPTM5 can still traffic to the lysosome, suggesting that Nedd4 binding to LAPTM5, not LAPTM5 ubiquitination, is required for targeting". PMID: 22096579 extends the PY-motif dependence of Nedd4-mediated lysosomal sorting to LAPTM4a and LAPTM4b, reporting "an interaction that is dependent on LAPTM4 PY motifs and plays a role in membrane sorting".

The scarcity of ubiquitin-acceptor lysines in VOPP1 therefore does not argue against a NEDD4-family mechanism. The precedent shows PY-dependent ligase recruitment can direct lysosomal delivery with the cargo itself unubiquitinated. For VOPP1 this predicts that the PPxY motifs act as a ligase-recruitment surface, with the ligase acting in trans on captured partners such as WWOX.

VOPP1 differs from LAPTM5 in its acidic content: the 91-residue tail has D=1 and E=3, which argues against the acidic UIM block that LAPTM5 uses to recruit ubiquitinated GGA3. A UIM-consensus regex scan returned no match in VOPP1, but the same regex also failed on LAPTM5, which is annotated to contain a UIM. The regex test is uninformative and is reported as such; only the acidic-residue scarcity argues against a UIM in VOPP1.

F8. AlphaFold predicts a disulfide-stapled lumenal module, and the signal-peptide-less isoforms leave tail topology unresolved

In AF-Q96AW1-F1 (model v6) the SG-SG distances among the eleven cysteines give two disulfide-compatible pairs: C26-C45 at 2.02 Å and C39-C50 at 2.03 Å. The remaining lumenal pair C46-C51 is 3.60 Å apart and is not modelled as bonded. The intramembrane pair C74-C75 is 6.87 Å apart. The lumenal domain 23-60 has mean pLDDT 82.0 and matches Pfam PF27964 (VOPP1_N, IPR060429). It contains no NxS/NxT sequon. The only sequon in the protein, NVS at 97-99, is cytoplasmic and so cannot be glycosylated.

The lumenal face carries a small folded module stabilised by two predicted disulfides, a second candidate interaction surface distinct from the cytoplasmic motifs, though no lumenal partner is known. The absence of N-glycosylation in a 38-residue lumenal domain is unusual for a lysosomal membrane protein, where the LAMPs use a heavily glycosylated lumenal domain for protection against lumenal hydrolases. VOPP1 is probably not a structural component of the lysosomal glycocalyx.

Isoforms 2, 3 and 4 delete residues within 1-18 and lose the hydrophobic h-region: the maximum Kyte-Doolittle value (window 11) in the first 25 residues falls from 2.43 in isoform 1 to 0.32, 1.37 and 0.32 respectively, while all four isoforms retain all three PPxY motifs and the identical C-terminus PPPPYEQVVKAK. A positive-inside charge test around TM 61-81 gives net +2 on both the 30-residue N-flank and the 30-residue C-flank in every isoform, so charge bias does not predict the orientation of the signal-anchor-only isoforms. Every scaffolding conclusion in this report is anchored to isoform 1.

In all five validated or annotated signals the tyrosine sits exactly 3 residues from the C-terminus, so the motif occupies the final four positions, and all three classical LAMP-type signals carry glycine at -1. VOPP1's Y165 has 7 residues after it, with VKAK following the motif, and is preceded by proline. Ten control proteins (SCARB2/LIMP2, CPE, ELOVL1, STOM, VPS37B, ITGB1, CD74, AXL, MRC2, CD14) carry no YxxPhi within their last 30 residues, confirming the scan is not matching indiscriminately.

This weakens the claim that YEQV 165-168 is a second independent sorting determinant acting by the LAMP mechanism. PMID: 11139587 reports that "mu4 recognized the tyrosine signal from the human lysosomal protein LAMP-2, HTGYEQF", so the YEQ-Phi sequence class is genuinely read by an AP medium subunit, but VOPP1 matches the sequence without matching the position. Internal YxxPhi motifs can still bind AP mu subunits (TGN38's YQRL is internal), so the motif is weakened, not excluded. The conservation of YEQV at 94.4% is then better explained by the overlap with PPPY 162-165, since Y165 is shared between the two motifs and the PY motif is what mutagenesis implicates.

F10. VOPP1 has no human paralog

A UniProt search for the VOPP1_N lumenal domain family (InterPro IPR060429, Pfam PF27964) returns only three Homo sapiens entries: Q96AW1 (reviewed, 172 aa) plus A0ACI8T482 (171 aa) and C9JVH1, which are unreviewed TrEMBL entries of the same locus rather than distinct genes. Reviewed entries carrying the VOPP1 gene name number 7 and are all one-to-one vertebrate orthologs: Q96AW1 human, Q8R1C3 mouse, B5DEK8 rat, A6QNZ8 bovine, Q5RDG5 orangutan, Q5XGS4 Xenopus laevis and Q0V9A8 Xenopus tropicalis, all 167-172 aa. The family is single-copy across vertebrates with no detected human expansion.

Sequence-similarity-based transfer of function from a paralog cannot explain any VOPP1 annotation, so the main overannotation risk raised in the research objective is removed. Mouse and rat orthologs are the same length as the human protein, so the MGI-sourced GO:0140311 protein sequestering activity annotation can be treated as a legitimate ortholog transfer and not a cross-family artifact. The remaining carry-over risk is vertical, from the UniProt FUNCTION line about NF-kB citing PMID: 15735698, not horizontal from paralogs.

Mechanistic model

The findings assemble into a single chain. A membrane anchor places a disordered, proline-rich tail on the cytosolic face of the late endosome and lysosome. That tail displays two conserved PPxY motifs. One of them, the C-terminal PPPY, captures the WW domain of WWOX and holds it on the membrane, away from the nuclear pool where it would meet p73α. The second, PPAY 154-157, is conserved but has no assigned partner, and NEDD4 is the leading candidate by co-purification and by analogy with LAPTM5. The YEQV overlapping the C-terminal PY motif is a sequence match to the LAMP-2 class, but it sits in the wrong position for that mechanism and shares its tyrosine with the PY motif, so it is better treated as a contested residue than as a second determinant.

Dashed arrows mark links that are predicted and not experimentally established for VOPP1. The strongest untested link is NEDD4 recruitment through PPAY 154-157, which is where the multivalency question will be settled.

Conflicts and alternatives

The two determinants compete for one residue. YEQV 165-168 and PPPY 162-165 share Y165. A WW domain bound at the PY motif and an AP mu subunit bound at the YxxPhi cannot occupy the site at once. This turns the hypothesis from "two determinants specify compartment and complex" into "one tyrosine is contested between two readers", which the available data cannot settle.

The YxxPhi is positionally wrong for the LAMP mechanism. Validated lysosomal tyrosine signals sit at the extreme C-terminus with glycine at -1. VOPP1's sits 7 residues in with proline at -1. The 94.4% conservation of YEQV is therefore better explained by the overlap with PPPY than by independent selection on a sorting signal.

Isoform-specific topology is unresolved. Isoforms 2, 3 and 4 lose the hydrophobic signal-peptide core (maximum Kyte-Doolittle window 11 falls from 2.43 to 0.32, 1.37 and 0.32) while keeping all three PPxY motifs and the identical C-terminus. The positive-inside test gives net +2 on both TM flanks in every isoform, so charge bias does not resolve orientation. All conclusions here are anchored to isoform 1.

Lysine scarcity, apparently a conflict, resolved by precedent. Two cytoplasmic lysines in the last three residues, median 1 across 212 orthologs, and 4 acidic residues in 91 look incompatible with being a NEDD4 substrate. 17116753 shows ubiquitination-impaired LAPTM5 still reaching the lysosome, so PY-dependent ligase recruitment works with the cargo unubiquitinated. The scarcity is compatible with VOPP1 acting as a recruitment platform for a ligase that works in trans.

Paralog confusion is ruled out. The VOPP1_N family has one reviewed human member, and the MGI-sourced GO:0140311 annotation is a one-to-one ortholog transfer from a 172-residue mouse protein.

A competing reading of the lumenal domain. The 38-residue lumenal module is disulfide-stapled but carries no N-glycosylation sequon, unlike the heavily glycosylated LAMP lumenal domains. VOPP1 is probably not a structural component of the lysosomal glycocalyx, and whether the module has a lumenal partner is unknown.

The cancer literature does not test the molecular model. Of the 26 papers reviewed, most treat VOPP1 as a prosurvival gene in a disease context: BET inhibitor resistance through BCL-2 (PMID: 36001806), NAE inhibitor sensitivity through a VOPP1-E2F1-CDT1 axis (PMID: 40651787), EMT and paclitaxel resistance in ovarian cancer (PMID: 40949794), p38 MAPK signalling in rheumatoid arthritis synoviocytes (PMID: 40771899), and TKI resistance in lung adenocarcinoma (PMID: 41605901). None maps a motif or tests the topology. They are phenotype-level and should not be used to assign a core molecular function.

Limitations and knowledge gaps

The seed hypothesis asked for a single analysis from sequence and structure. That analysis can map topology, enumerate motifs and score conservation, and it did. It cannot determine which motif is used in a cell, whether motifs are used at the same time, or whether a predicted disulfide forms. Three of the computations rest on predictions: the AlphaFold model, the signal peptide and TM boundaries, and disorder inferred from pLDDT. The conservation analysis used a Needleman-Wunsch aligner written for this work because Biopython was not importable in the execution environment; it was validated only by the identity distribution it produced (mean 78.0%, range 58.1-100%). Compartment labels in the proteome enrichment come from UniProt subcellular-location annotation, so the 6.10-fold enrichment inherits any bias in that annotation. No wet-lab evidence was generated.

Does NEDD4 bind VOPP1 directly, and through which motif? Checked: IntAct lists NEDD4 from AP-MS (33961781) with no motif mapping; PubMed searches returned no VOPP1-NEDD4 binding study. Why it matters: this determines whether PPAY 154-157 has a function distinct from the C-terminal motif, which is the central multivalency question. Resolution: pulldown of recombinant NEDD4 WW1-4 against VOPP1 tail peptides with PPAY and PPPY mutated singly and together.

Is Y165 phosphorylated? Checked: the EBI proteomics-ptm endpoint returns 404 for Q96AW1; MS coverage is 25% with zero peptides over residues 150-172; the motif region 102-170 is one 68-residue tryptic peptide with no internal K or R. Why it matters: phosphorylation of Y165 blocks WW binding and could switch the site to a phosphotyrosine reader, and EPHA2 appears in the VOPP1 interactome. Resolution: targeted PRM with GluC or chymotrypsin, or a phospho-Y165 antibody with and without EPHA2 activation.

Does a WW domain and an AP complex actually compete at Y165? Checked: no publication found addresses this. Why it matters: it distinguishes "second determinant" from "contested residue". Resolution: competition binding of a WWOX WW domain and an AP mu subunit for the same VOPP1 C-terminal peptide, with separation-of-function mutants (Q167A/V168A should kill the YxxPhi without touching the PY core).

What is the orientation of isoforms 2-4? Checked: hydropathy and positive-inside tests, both uninformative. Why it matters: if those tails face the lumen, their PPxY motifs cannot engage cytosolic WW proteins and the annotation becomes isoform-restricted. Resolution: protease protection or glycosylation-site insertion on tagged isoform constructs.

Does VOPP1 have a UIM? Checked: a UIM consensus regex returned nothing in VOPP1, but the same regex also failed on LAPTM5, which is annotated to carry a UIM, so the test is uninformative and is reported as such. Only the scarcity of acidic residues (D=1, E=3 in 91) argues against a UIM. Resolution: a profile-based UIM search or a ubiquitin binding assay.

Does the lumenal module bind anything? Checked: no lumenal partner in IntAct; the AP-MS interactors are all cytosolic or membrane proteins. Why it matters: a second interaction surface would be a multivalency argument independent of the PY motifs. Resolution: lumenal-domain pulldown from lysosomal lumen fractions.

Proposed follow-up experiments

  1. Motif-swap WWOX binding panel. Co-IP WWOX with VOPP1 carrying PPAY154AAAA, PPPY162AAAA, both, and the YxxPhi-only mutant Q167A/V168A. If only the PPPY mutant loses binding and the double mutant loses no more, VOPP1 is a single-motif adaptor for WWOX. If the double mutant is worse than either single, the motifs are additive and the scaffold reading gains support.
  2. NEDD4 WW-domain array against VOPP1 tail peptides. Peptide arrays spanning 150-172 and 148-160 against individual WW1-WW4 domains of NEDD4 and of WWOX. This tests directly whether different WW proteins read different motifs, which is the mechanistic core of multivalency.
  3. Localization of the PY-mutant protein. If the PPxY motifs are a sorting determinant and not only a capture surface, PPxY-mutant VOPP1 should mislocalize, as PY-mutant LAPTM5 does to the Golgi (17116753). If VOPP1 reaches the lysosome normally without its PY motifs, the motifs are a capture surface only and the compartment is specified elsewhere, probably by the TM helix and the RRRM patch.
  4. AP subunit knockdown. Deplete AP1M1, AP2M1, AP3M1 or AP4M1 and score VOPP1 localization. A positional outlier YxxPhi should give no effect; an effect would rescue the YxxPhi determinant despite its non-canonical position.
  5. Targeted PRM over the C-terminal region. Non-tryptic digestion with GluC or chymotrypsin to obtain coverage of 150-172, with and without EPHA2 stimulation, to settle whether Y165 is phosphorylated.
  6. Isoform topology by glycosylation-site insertion. Place an N-glycosylation reporter in the tail of isoforms 1 and 3. Glycosylation reports lumenal exposure and would make the annotation isoform-specific.
