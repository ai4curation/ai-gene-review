# EIF2B2 evidence notes

EIF2B2 is the beta regulatory subunit of cytosolic eIF2B. Native human complex purification, human subunit assembly and variant-bearing patient extracts support its contribution to nucleotide exchange and translation initiation. The exchange reaction belongs to the assembled machinery; these observations do not establish beta-only GEF activity. In the patient assay, the extracts are human and the GDP-bound eIF2 substrate is from rat liver. [PMID:11323413; PMID:15054402; PMID:15060152]

Native beta ATP/GTP cross-linking is a positive observation. Recombinant beta also binds GTP, but the inspected historical cloning passage does not establish its exact current-human insert sequence. The physiological role of nucleotide binding remains unresolved, and no beta GTPase activity or second catalytic mechanism follows from it. [PMID:11323413]

Six EIF2B4 associations support translation initiation factor binding. The older human-subunit paper recovered beta in larger assemblies while finding no stable isolated beta–individual-subunit association in its pairwise assay. Later Y2H and AP-MS records retain their own scope. C9orf72 association is retained as non-core generic binding because GEF/GAP labels do not by themselves establish a narrower catalytic or scaffold partner class. This follows the explicit ClinGen project instruction for supported generic associations; no pair-specific activity is invented. [PMID:15060152; PMID:25910212; PMID:31515488; PMID:32296183; PMID:33961781]

Human EIF2B2 variants are associated with vanishing-white-matter disease and severe myelination abnormalities. The original infant/autopsy evidence includes beta-associated oligodendrocyte pathology, but altered survival and apoptosis do not resolve the developmental progression defined by the imported oligodendrocyte-development term. The ovarian screen tested known variants in isolated ovarian failure and does not establish a follicle-building role. The review preserves clinical relevance while separating these observations from specific developmental mechanisms. [PMID:14566705; PMID:15217090; PMID:15507143; PMID:15776425]

Rat-ortholog heat, glucose and peptide-hormone responses concern regulated whole-factor activity; they are retained as contextual non-core functions without assigning beta a sensor or receptor role. The clinical heat-response citation and human T-cell signaling assertion retain source-specific uncertainty. Early whole-factor activity and later beta abundance after T-cell activation are distinct from a demonstrated antigen-receptor signaling step. [PMID:9341116; PMID:8567668; PMID:8430778; PMID:12499492; PMID:8626696]
