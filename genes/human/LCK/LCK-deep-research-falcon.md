# Comprehensive Research Report: LCK (Lymphocyte-specific protein tyrosine kinase)

## Gene Identity Verification

The gene LCK (UniProt accession P06239) encodes the lymphocyte-specific protein tyrosine kinase, also known as Leukocyte C-terminal Src kinase (LSK), p56-LCK, or proto-oncogene Lck. This protein belongs to the Src family of non-receptor tyrosine kinases and is correctly identified as the human tyrosine-protein kinase with EC number 2.7.10.2 (sanctis2024lckfunctionand pages 2-4, fernandezaguilar2023astoryof pages 2-4).

## 1. Protein Structure and Domain Organization

### 1.1 Overview of Domain Architecture

LCK is a modular, multi-domain protein organized from N-terminus to C-terminus as follows: an SH4 membrane-targeting domain, a unique domain (UD), an SH3 regulatory domain, an SH2 regulatory domain, a short proline-rich linker, the SH1 catalytic tyrosine kinase domain, and a short C-terminal regulatory tail (sanctis2024lckfunctionand pages 2-4, fernandezaguilar2023astoryof pages 5-7). This architecture is characteristic of Src family kinases but includes LCK-specific features that determine its localization and function in T lymphocytes (ortiz2021srcfamilykinases pages 2-5).

### 1.2 Membrane Targeting and Lipid Modifications

The SH4 domain is essential for membrane anchoring and contains constitutive N-myristoylation at glycine 2, along with reversible S-palmitoylation at cysteines 3 and 5 (sanctis2024lckfunctionand pages 2-4). Palmitoylation is dynamically regulated by palmitoyl acyltransferases ZDHHC2 and ZDHHC21, and this modification is crucial for targeting LCK to plasma membrane lipid rafts—cholesterol- and glycosphingolipid-enriched membrane microdomains where T-cell receptor signaling occurs (sanctis2024lckfunctionand pages 2-4, zhang2023newinsightsinto pages 1-2). Disruption of palmitoylation, inhibition of DHHC enzymes, or depletion of membrane cholesterol prevents proper lipid-raft localization and diminishes T-cell activation (sanctis2024lckfunctionand pages 2-4).

### 1.3 Regulatory and Interaction Domains

The unique domain (residues ~16–63) is an intrinsically disordered region that confers LCK-specific protein interactions, particularly binding to the cytoplasmic tails of CD4 and CD8α coreceptors through cysteines 20 and 23 in a zinc-dependent manner (fernandezaguilar2023astoryof pages 5-7, ortiz2021srcfamilykinases pages 2-5, fernandezaguilar2023astoryof pages 14-16). The SH3 domain recognizes proline-rich PXXP motifs and participates in intramolecular autoinhibition by engaging the linker region between SH2 and the kinase domain (sanctis2024lckfunctionand pages 2-4, fernandezaguilar2023astoryof pages 5-7). The SH2 domain binds phosphotyrosine-containing sequences and plays a central regulatory role by engaging phosphorylated tyrosine 505 in the C-terminal tail, thereby stabilizing the closed, inactive conformation (fernandezaguilar2023astoryof pages 5-7, sanctis2024lckfunctionand pages 4-6).

### 1.4 Catalytic Domain and Activation Mechanism

The SH1 catalytic domain (residues ~246–498) contains the bilobal kinase fold with an ATP-binding cleft and activation loop (karpouzou2026moleculardeterminantsof pages 1-2). The activation loop contains tyrosine 394, which upon autophosphorylation stabilizes the active conformation and dramatically increases catalytic activity (sanctis2024lckfunctionand pages 2-4, fernandezaguilar2023astoryof pages 5-7). Recent molecular dynamics and structural studies demonstrate that the LCK kinase domain has intrinsic substrate selectivity: it accommodates TCR immunoreceptor tyrosine-based activation motif (ITAM) peptides with better shape complementarity, deeper burial, and more favorable electrostatic interactions than the closely related kinase Src (karpouzou2026moleculardeterminantsof pages 2-4, karpouzou2026moleculardeterminantsof pages 4-6, karpouzou2026moleculardeterminantsof pages 1-2).

### 1.5 Regulation by Phosphorylation

LCK activity is primarily controlled by two tyrosine phosphorylation sites with opposing functions. Phosphorylation of Y394 in the activation loop is activating and occurs through cis- or trans-autophosphorylation (sanctis2024lckfunctionand pages 2-4, fernandezaguilar2023astoryof pages 5-7). In contrast, phosphorylation of Y505 in the C-terminal tail by C-terminal Src kinase (Csk) is inhibitory, promoting intramolecular SH2-domain binding and stabilizing the closed conformation (sanctis2024lckfunctionand pages 4-6). The CD45 phosphatase preferentially dephosphorylates Y505, allowing LCK to open and become primed for activation (fernandezaguilar2023astoryof pages 12-14, fernandezaguilar2023astoryof pages 5-7). An additional regulatory site, Y192 in the SH2 domain, can reduce LCK binding to CD45 when phosphorylated, creating a negative-feedback loop during TCR signaling (sanctis2024lckfunctionand pages 4-6, fernandezaguilar2023astoryof pages 12-14, fernandezaguilar2023astoryof pages 14-16). Importantly, the regulation of LCK is not a simple binary on/off switch: doubly phosphorylated LCK (Y394+Y505) can retain significant kinase activity, and the balance between CD45 and Csk activities establishes a dynamic equilibrium rather than discrete activation states (fernandezaguilar2023astoryof pages 11-12, weerawarna2023lynkinasestructure pages 4-7).

## 2. Primary Enzymatic Function and Substrate Specificity

### 2.1 Catalyzed Reaction

LCK is a tyrosine-protein kinase that catalyzes the transfer of the γ-phosphate from ATP to tyrosine residues in protein substrates (EC 2.7.10.2). The general reaction is:

**Protein-Tyr + ATP → Protein-Tyr-PO₃²⁻ + ADP + H⁺**

### 2.2 Principal Substrates

The best-characterized and most important LCK substrates are the immunoreceptor tyrosine-based activation motifs (ITAMs) in the cytoplasmic tails of the CD3 and TCR ζ-chain subunits of the T-cell receptor complex (karpouzou2026moleculardeterminantsof pages 1-2, fernandezaguilar2023astoryof pages 2-4). A 2026 study specifically identified CD3ζ tyrosine 142 (Y142) as a well-established direct LCK substrate (karpouzou2026moleculardeterminantsof pages 2-4, karpouzou2026moleculardeterminantsof pages 1-2). Following ITAM phosphorylation, LCK also phosphorylates ZAP-70 (particularly at Y319) after this kinase is recruited to phosphorylated ITAMs, thereby activating ZAP-70 for downstream signal propagation (karpouzou2026moleculardeterminantsof pages 2-4, karpouzou2026moleculardeterminantsof pages 4-6, fernandezaguilar2023astoryof pages 2-4). Additional substrates include the adaptor protein LAT and the receptor CD5, although much of LAT phosphorylation may occur indirectly through activated ZAP-70 (sanctis2024lckfunctionand pages 6-8, sanctis2024lckfunctionand pages 8-10, fernandezaguilar2023astoryof pages 2-4).

### 2.3 Substrate Recognition and Specificity

Despite sharing highly conserved catalytic domains with other Src family kinases, LCK displays remarkable substrate selectivity for TCR-associated signaling proteins (karpouzou2026moleculardeterminantsof pages 1-2). Recent domain-swap experiments demonstrated that when Src is expressed in T cells, it cannot phosphorylate TCR ITAMs, whereas substituting Src's kinase domain with LCK's kinase domain restores ITAM phosphorylation and downstream signaling (karpouzou2026moleculardeterminantsof pages 2-4, karpouzou2026moleculardeterminantsof pages 1-2). This selectivity is determined by subtle structural features within the kinase domain rather than differences in adaptor domains or membrane localization (karpouzou2026moleculardeterminantsof pages 4-6).

Molecular dynamics simulations and ensemble docking revealed that LCK accommodates ITAM peptides with superior shape complementarity compared to Src, allowing deeper burial of the substrate, denser atomic packing, and increased solvent shielding (karpouzou2026moleculardeterminantsof pages 6-7, karpouzou2026moleculardeterminantsof pages 4-6). Furthermore, LCK preferentially recognizes tyrosine-containing peptide contexts enriched in positively charged residues, reflecting both electrostatic preferences and structural compatibility of its kinase-domain active-site architecture (karpouzou2026moleculardeterminantsof pages 6-7). Thus, LCK substrate specificity arises from an integration of kinase-domain structure, electrostatic complementarity, and cellular localization near TCR signaling complexes (karpouzou2026moleculardeterminantsof pages 1-2, sanctis2024lckfunctionand pages 2-4).

## 3. Subcellular Localization

### 3.1 Plasma Membrane Lipid Raft Localization

LCK functions primarily at the plasma membrane, where it is concentrated in cholesterol- and glycosphingolipid-enriched membrane microdomains known as lipid rafts or glycolipid-enriched microdomains (GEMs) (sanctis2024lckfunctionand pages 2-4, zhang2023newinsightsinto pages 1-2, fernandezaguilar2023astoryof pages 23-25). This specialized localization positions LCK alongside the TCR/CD3 signaling machinery and enables rapid initiation of signaling upon TCR engagement (fernandezaguilar2023astoryof pages 2-4, zhang2023newinsightsinto pages 1-2). The lipidation-dependent membrane association is essential: myristoylation at Gly2 provides constitutive membrane anchoring, while reversible palmitoylation at Cys3 and Cys5 dynamically regulates raft partitioning (sanctis2024lckfunctionand pages 2-4, wei2020lckboundto pages 5-6, zhang2023newinsightsinto pages 1-2).

### 3.2 Coreceptor-Bound and Free LCK Pools

LCK exists in at least two functionally distinct pools in T cells: coreceptor-bound LCK associated with CD4 or CD8, and free LCK not bound to coreceptors (wei2020lckboundto pages 5-6, wei2020lckboundto pages 8-9). A landmark 2020 study demonstrated that free LCK exhibits greater lateral mobility, higher Y394 phosphorylation, and significantly greater kinase activity than coreceptor-bound LCK (wei2020lckboundto pages 5-6). CD4 has higher affinity for LCK than CD8, so more LCK is sequestered by CD4; however, a substantial free pool remains available for signaling (wei2020lckboundto pages 8-9). During thymocyte development, downregulation of one coreceptor can release previously bound LCK, and activated LCK can dissociate from CD4, dynamically regulating the functional pool sizes (wei2020lckboundto pages 8-9). This partitioning provides a mechanism for modulating T-cell sensitivity and signaling strength (wei2020lckboundto pages 5-6).

### 3.3 Membrane Dynamics and Trafficking

LCK does not undergo directed transport toward TCR clusters; instead, single-molecule imaging shows that during early TCR signaling, LCK undergoes Brownian diffusion and encounters signaling complexes stochastically (wei2020lckboundto pages 5-6). Its membrane association is regulated by reversible palmitoylation and can be modulated by membrane lipid composition, receptor interactions, and stimulus-dependent recruitment (sanctis2024lckfunctionand pages 8-10, sanctis2024lckfunctionand pages 6-8). While LCK is predominantly membrane-associated, it can also exist in cytosolic and potentially nuclear pools, with different interaction partners and regulatory states in each compartment (sanctis2024lckfunctionand pages 8-10, sanctis2024lckfunctionand pages 6-8).

## 4. Signaling Pathways and Biochemical Function

### 4.1 Central Role in T-Cell Receptor Signaling

LCK is the essential initiating tyrosine kinase in T-cell receptor signaling (fernandezaguilar2023astoryof pages 2-4, zhang2023newinsightsinto pages 1-2, fernandezaguilar2023astoryof pages 4-5). When the TCR/CD3 complex recognizes antigen presented by MHC molecules, LCK phosphorylates the ITAMs located in the cytoplasmic tails of CD3ε, CD3δ, CD3γ, and the TCR ζ-chain (fernandezaguilar2023astoryof pages 14-16, fernandezaguilar2023astoryof pages 2-4, fernandezaguilar2023astoryof pages 4-5). These phosphorylated ITAMs create docking sites for the tandem SH2 domains of ZAP-70, recruiting this Syk-family kinase to the membrane (fernandezaguilar2023astoryof pages 14-16, fernandezaguilar2023astoryof pages 2-4). LCK then phosphorylates and activates ZAP-70, which in turn phosphorylates the transmembrane adaptor LAT (linker for activation of T cells) and other downstream targets (fernandezaguilar2023astoryof pages 2-4, fernandezaguilar2023astoryof pages 12-14).

The phosphorylated LAT adaptor nucleates assembly of a large signaling complex (the LAT signalosome) that includes SLP-76, PLCγ1, and numerous other effectors, ultimately leading to calcium flux, MAPK activation, cytoskeletal reorganization, and transcriptional programs required for T-cell activation, proliferation, and differentiation (fernandezaguilar2023astoryof pages 14-16, fernandezaguilar2023astoryof pages 2-4, fernandezaguilar2023astoryof pages 11-12).

### 4.2 Regulation of LCK Activity in the TCR Pathway

LCK activity is tightly controlled through multiple mechanisms. Resting T cells contain a pre-existing pool of LCK phosphorylated at the activating Y394 residue, which allows rapid ITAM phosphorylation when LCK encounters the TCR upon antigen recognition (fernandezaguilar2023astoryof pages 11-12). The opposing activities of CD45 phosphatase and Csk kinase establish a dynamic signaling threshold: CD45 dephosphorylates the inhibitory Y505 and can also remove Y394 phosphate, while Csk phosphorylates Y505 to promote the closed conformation (fernandezaguilar2023astoryof pages 12-14, sanctis2024lckfunctionand pages 4-6). 

The transmembrane adaptor PAG (phosphoprotein associated with glycosphingolipid-enriched microdomains) recruits Csk to the membrane in resting cells; TCR stimulation causes PAG dephosphorylation and Csk dissociation, reducing LCK inhibition (fernandezaguilar2023astoryof pages 12-14). Conversely, immune checkpoint receptors such as PD-1 can promote PAG phosphorylation and Csk recruitment, suppressing LCK activity and attenuating T-cell responses (fernandezaguilar2023astoryof pages 12-14).

A negative-feedback loop limits excessive signaling: activated ZAP-70 phosphorylates LCK at Y192, which reduces LCK binding to CD45, allowing Y505 phosphorylation and favoring the closed conformation (fernandezaguilar2023astoryof pages 14-16, fernandezaguilar2023astoryof pages 12-14). This regulatory circuit helps prevent uncontrolled T-cell activation while maintaining sensitivity to antigen (fernandezaguilar2023astoryof pages 12-14, fernandezaguilar2023astoryof pages 11-12).

### 4.3 Role in T-Cell Development

LCK is essential for normal T-cell development in the thymus. Lck-deficient mice display severe thymic atrophy, markedly reduced numbers of CD4⁺CD8⁺ double-positive thymocytes, and decreased peripheral CD4⁺ and CD8⁺ T cells (fernandezaguilar2023astoryof pages 4-5). While Fyn can partially compensate in the absence of LCK, combined Lck and Fyn deficiency blocks thymic maturation at the double-negative stage (fernandezaguilar2023astoryof pages 4-5). The CD45-Csk-LCK regulatory axis controls signaling strength during thymic selection, helping T cells distinguish between self and non-self antigens and establishing the threshold for positive and negative selection (fernandezaguilar2023astoryof pages 12-14, fernandezaguilar2023astoryof pages 11-12).

### 4.4 Integration with Other Signaling Pathways

Beyond the canonical TCR pathway, LCK contributes to downstream activation of ERK through reactive oxygen species-dependent mechanisms and calcium/calmodulin-mediated pathways (sanctis2024lckfunctionand pages 6-8). LCK activity also links TCR signaling to NF-κB activation, supporting transcriptional programs involved in immunity, inflammation, cell survival, and proliferation (zhang2023newinsightsinto pages 1-2). Recent work has revealed that LCK participates in signaling through surface receptors including CD2, CD5, CD44, CD146, and CD160, forming lipid-raft signaling platforms and contributing to T-cell adhesion, migration, and cytotoxic responses (sanctis2024lckfunctionand pages 8-10).

## 5. Recent Developments and Current Research (2023–2024)

### 5.1 Human LCK Deficiency Disease

A major 2023 study reported the first detailed characterization of human partial LCK deficiency caused by a homozygous P440S missense mutation in the catalytic domain (lui2023apartialhuman pages 2-4, lui2023apartialhuman pages 1-2). Affected siblings presented with combined immunodeficiency (CID) characterized by severe T-cell lymphopenia, absent naive T cells, infant-onset recurrent viral and fungal infections, failure to thrive, chronic diarrhea, and intestinal inflammation (lui2023apartialhuman pages 2-4, lui2023apartialhuman pages 14-15, lui2023apartialhuman pages 1-2). The P440S variant causes protein instability and defective TCR signal transduction but permits residual LCK function (lui2023apartialhuman pages 2-4).

Importantly, the study established that while complete LCK loss-of-function causes severe combined immunodeficiency (SCID)-like phenotypes, partial LCK deficiency permits development of some conventional T cells but insufficient regulatory T cells, leading to immune dysregulation and autoimmune/inflammatory manifestations including intestinal inflammation resembling monogenic inflammatory bowel disease (lui2023apartialhuman pages 14-15, lui2023apartialhuman pages 1-2). Other reported LCK variants include L341P (associated with inflammatory skin lesions) and splice variants, and one family with LCK mutations developed atypical epidermodysplasia verruciformis with virus-induced squamous cell carcinoma (lui2023apartialhuman pages 22-22, lui2023apartialhuman pages 14-15).

### 5.2 Molecular Determinants of Substrate Specificity

A breakthrough 2026 study combined cellular experiments with molecular dynamics simulations to define how LCK achieves substrate selectivity despite sharing ~90% kinase-domain sequence identity with Src (karpouzou2026moleculardeterminantsof pages 1-2, karpouzou2026moleculardeterminantsof pages 2-4). In-cell assays demonstrated that Src cannot phosphorylate TCR ITAMs when ectopically expressed in T cells, whereas swapping Src's kinase domain with LCK's kinase domain was sufficient to restore ITAM phosphorylation and downstream signaling (karpouzou2026moleculardeterminantsof pages 2-4, karpouzou2026moleculardeterminantsof pages 1-2). 

Computational analyses revealed that LCK consistently accommodates ITAM peptides with higher shape complementarity than Src, supporting a more favorable structural environment for substrate recognition (karpouzou2026moleculardeterminantsof pages 1-2, karpouzou2026moleculardeterminantsof pages 6-7, karpouzou2026moleculardeterminantsof pages 4-6). This work provides mechanistic insight into how closely related kinases achieve distinct biological functions and has implications for understanding signaling specificity more broadly.

### 5.3 LCK in CAR-T Cell Therapy

Recent studies have revealed complex and context-dependent roles for LCK in chimeric antigen receptor (CAR) T-cell signaling. A 2023 report demonstrated that CD28-containing CARs can signal through the Src-family kinase FYN rather than LCK, and that LCK-disrupted CAR-T cells show reduced exhaustion, enhanced memory-like characteristics, improved proliferation and persistence, and significantly better anti-tumor efficacy in vivo (wu2023cd28cartcellactivation pages 1-3). This occurs because FYN-mediated signaling produces less intense activation and reduced exhaustion compared to LCK-driven signaling (wu2023cd28cartcellactivation pages 1-3). LCK disruption also selectively prevents endogenous TCR signaling while preserving CAR signaling, potentially enabling allogeneic CAR-T approaches without complete TCR knockout (wu2023cd28cartcellactivation pages 1-3).

Other studies have shown that incorporating LCK or modulating LCK-recruiting motifs in CAR constructs can enhance IL-2 production and modulate responses to suppressive tumor microenvironments (wei2020lckboundto pages 8-9, wei2020lckboundto pages 6-8). Additionally, dasatinib (an LCK-targeting kinase inhibitor) can serve as a pharmacologic "on/off switch" for CAR-T cells, potentially providing control over excessive activation or cytokine release (wei2020lckboundto pages 8-9, zhang2023newinsightsinto pages 11-11, wei2020lckboundto pages 6-8).

Multiple therapeutic strategies targeting LCK are under investigation for cancer, autoimmune diseases, and transplantation medicine. In hematologic malignancies, LCK supports leukemic cell survival, BCR-SYK-NF-κB signaling in chronic lymphocytic leukemia (CLL), and glucocorticoid resistance (zhang2023newinsightsinto pages 7-8, zhang2023newinsightsinto pages 8-9, sanctis2024lckfunctionand pages 1-2). Dasatinib, an ATP-competitive multikinase inhibitor with potent LCK activity, is clinically approved for Philadelphia chromosome–positive chronic myeloid leukemia (CML) and potently suppresses T-cell activation, although its therapeutic effects in CML are primarily due to BCR-ABL inhibition rather than LCK inhibition (zhang2023newinsightsinto pages 7-8, zhang2023newinsightsinto pages 8-9).

In solid tumors, preclinical studies report that LCK inhibition can reduce invasion, metastasis, and chemoresistance in breast, pancreatic, endometrioid, oral, and ovarian cancers (sanctis2024lckfunctionand pages 10-13, zhang2023newinsightsinto pages 8-9, sanctis2024lckfunctionand pages 21-22). Clinical investigations include masitinib plus gemcitabine in advanced pancreatic cancer (showing improved overall survival in selected patients), nilotinib in KIT-driven melanoma, and various other combinations (sanctis2024lckfunctionand pages 14-16, sanctis2024lckfunctionand pages 13-14). In glioblastoma, experimental LCK inhibitors (A-770041, WH-4-23) reduced tumor growth, migration, and stem-cell properties (sanctis2024lckfunctionand pages 14-16, zhang2023newinsightsinto pages 7-8).

For autoimmune and inflammatory diseases, LCK inhibition has shown promise in preclinical psoriasis models, where it reduced inflammatory cytokine production by CD4⁺ T cells (sanctis2024lckfunctionand pages 10-13, sanctis2024lckfunctionand pages 21-22). Selective inhibitors (A-770041, BMS-243117) suppress T-cell proliferation, and A-770041 prevented heart-allograft rejection in animal models (zhang2023newinsightsinto pages 7-8, zhang2023newinsightsinto pages 12-12). Nilotinib has demonstrated clinical activity in steroid-dependent or refractory chronic graft-versus-host disease, though this is a multikinase effect (sanctis2024lckfunctionand pages 14-16).

### 5.5 Checkpoint Immunotherapy and LCK Regulation

Multiple immune checkpoint receptors negatively regulate LCK-dependent signaling. PD-1 engagement promotes recruitment of SHP-2 phosphatase, which dephosphorylates LCK at Y394 and suppresses proximal TCR signaling (sanctis2024lckfunctionand pages 8-10, sanctis2024lckfunctionand pages 6-8). PD-1 also induces PAG phosphorylation, recruiting Csk and further inhibiting LCK (fernandezaguilar2023astoryof pages 12-14). LAG-3 suppresses signaling by promoting dissociation of LCK from CD4/CD8 coreceptors (sanctis2024lckfunctionand pages 8-10, sanctis2024lckfunctionand pages 21-22). Conversely, checkpoint blockade can restore LCK-dependent signaling in dysfunctional or exhausted T cells, providing a mechanistic basis for combining checkpoint immunotherapy with approaches that modulate LCK activity (sanctis2024lckfunctionand pages 8-10, sanctis2024lckfunctionand pages 21-22).

### 5.6 Expert Analysis and Future Directions

Recent comprehensive reviews emphasize that LCK function is "more than a simple event," reflecting its context-dependent roles in different T-cell populations, varying activity levels among T-cell subsets (higher in CD8⁺ cytotoxic T cells than CD4⁺ helper cells, lower in regulatory T cells), and complex regulatory networks (sanctis2024lckfunctionand pages 2-4, sanctis2024lckfunctionand pages 1-2). LCK is now recognized as functioning not only in TCR-dependent signaling but also in antigen-independent cytotoxic responses and in non-lymphoid tissues including lung, intestine, brain, endometrium, and prostate (sanctis2024lckfunctionand pages 2-4).

New therapeutic approaches are focusing on more selective LCK inhibitors targeting not only the catalytic domain but also membrane-anchor and SH4-domain elements (sanctis2024lckfunctionand pages 13-14). Drug repurposing of existing multikinase inhibitors for LCK-directed applications continues, supported by bioinformatics and systems-biology approaches (sanctis2024lckfunctionand pages 1-2, zhang2023newinsightsinto pages 12-12). LCK expression is being evaluated as a prognostic or predictive biomarker in multiple cancers, though careful distinction must be made between tumor-cell-intrinsic LCK and LCK in tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes (sanctis2024lckfunctionand pages 21-22, sanctis2024lckfunctionand pages 1-2).

## 6. Summary and Conclusions

LCK (lymphocyte-specific protein tyrosine kinase; UniProt P06239) is a 56-kDa Src-family non-receptor tyrosine kinase that serves as the essential initiating enzyme for T-cell receptor signaling in human T lymphocytes. The protein contains an N-terminal SH4 membrane-targeting domain (myristoylated and palmitoylated), a unique domain that binds CD4/CD8 coreceptors, regulatory SH3 and SH2 domains, a catalytic tyrosine kinase domain, and a C-terminal regulatory tail. Its primary enzymatic function is to catalyze phosphorylation of tyrosine residues in TCR-associated immunoreceptor tyrosine-based activation motifs (ITAMs), particularly in CD3 and TCR ζ-chain subunits, as well as in the downstream kinase ZAP-70. Substrate specificity arises from intrinsic structural features of the kinase domain that favor ITAM peptides enriched in positively charged residues.

LCK localizes to cholesterol-enriched lipid rafts at the plasma membrane through lipid modifications and exists in functionally distinct coreceptor-bound and free pools. Its activity is dynamically regulated by opposing phosphorylation at Y394 (activating, via autophosphorylation) and Y505 (inhibitory, via Csk), with CD45 phosphatase controlling the balance. LCK initiates TCR signaling by phosphorylating ITAMs, recruiting and activating ZAP-70, and enabling assembly of the LAT signalosome, ultimately triggering calcium flux, MAPK activation, and T-cell activation.

Recent research (2023–2024) has provided major advances including: (1) identification of human LCK deficiency as a cause of combined immunodeficiency with intestinal inflammation; (2) molecular-level definition of substrate selectivity mechanisms; (3) discovery of LCK-independent CAR-T signaling pathways that can enhance therapeutic efficacy; and (4) expanding therapeutic applications in cancer, autoimmune disease, and cellular immunotherapy. LCK remains an active area of investigation for both mechanistic immunology and translational medicine.
