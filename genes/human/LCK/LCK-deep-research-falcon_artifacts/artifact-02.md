(pqac-00000043) |
| Melanoma | **Nilotinib** | KIT-driven melanoma | **Clinical investigation / repurposing** | Clinical activity has been reported in molecularly selected KIT-driven melanoma, but nilotinib is a multikinase drug and the contribution of LCK inhibition is uncertain. (pqac-00000039) |
| Pancreatic cancer | **Masitinib plus gemcitabine** | Advanced pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma, including an ACOX1-overexpressing subgroup | **Clinical investigation** | A masitinib-based regimen was associated with better overall survival than gemcitabine plus placebo in a selected population. Masitinib is multikinase, so this is not proof that LCK inhibition caused the benefit. (pqac-00000039, pqac-00000041) |

| Psoriatic inflammation | Pharmacologic **LCK inhibition** | Psoriasis-like inflammatory disease | **Preclinical mouse models** | Inhibition reduced inflammatory cytokine production by CD4-positive T cells and improved disease manifestations in an imiquimod-induced psoriasis model. | Chronic graft-versus-host disease | **Nilotinib** and broader kinase-modulating regimens | Steroid-dependent or refractory chronic GVHD | **Clinical use/investigation, but not an LCK-specific therapy** | Nilotinib has shown clinical activity associated with reduced inflammatory cytokines and improved transplant outcomes; attribution specifically to LCK is not established because the drug has multiple targets. (pqac-00000039) |
| CAR-T safety control | **Dasatinib as a reversible pharmacologic on/off switch** | Management of excessive CAR-T activation and potential toxicity | **Preclinical/translational** | Dasatinib rapidly suppresses Src-family kinase signaling and CAR-T function; withdrawal can restore activity, making temporary kinase inhibition a potential safety-control strategy. (pqac-00000049, pqac-00000050, pqac-00000051) |
| CAR-T engineering | Disruption of **LCK** in CD28-containing CAR-T cells to redirect signaling toward **FYN** | Improved persistence, reduced exhaustion, and possible allogeneic CAR-T development | **Preclinical, including in-vivo tumor models** | CD28-CAR signaling persisted through FYN after LCK disruption. Engineered cells showed less exhaustion, more memory-like features, improved proliferation and persistence, and enhanced antitumor efficacy; endogenous TCR signaling was simultaneously impaired. (pqac-00000047) |
| CAR-T receptor design | Incorporation or alteration of **LCK-recruiting motifs** in CD28- or CD3ζ-based CARs | Optimization of cytokine output, activation, and resistance to suppressive tumor environments | **Preclinical engineering** | Adding LCK to a CD3ζ CAR enhanced IL-2 production, while deleting the CD28 LCK-binding motif altered cytokine production and responses to TGF-β or regulatory T cells. These context-dependent results show that maximal LCK signaling is not always therapeutically optimal. (pqac-00000049, pqac-00000051) |
| Checkpoint immunotherapy | Modulation of **PD-1–PAG–CSK–LCK**, **LAG-3–coreceptor–LCK**, or phosphatase pathways | Restoring dysfunctional or exhausted antitumor T-cell signaling | **Mechanistic/preclinical; checkpoint drugs themselves are clinically established or under clinical study** | PD-1, LAG-3, SHP-1, and SHP-2 restrain LCK-dependent proximal signaling. Checkpoint blockade or manipulation of PAG/CSK and phosphatase circuits could restore signaling, but these are indirect—not LCK-specific—therapeutic strategies. (pqac-00000026, pqac-00000044, pqac-00000052, pqac-00000053) |
| Biomarker development | Tumor **LCK expression or activation state** | Melanoma, ovarian cancer, colorectal cancer, thymoma, and other tumors | **Exploratory/retrospective** | LCK is being evaluated as a prognostic or predictive biomarker and as a marker of tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes versus tumor-intrinsic signaling. Cell-type resolution is essential because high LCK may reflect antitumor immune infiltration rather than oncogenic activity in malignant cells. (pqac-00000044, pqac-00000045) |
