Gene Description

ACLY encodes ATP-citrate synthase (also called ATP-citrate lyase; EC 2.3.3.8), the cytosolic homotetrameric enzyme that produces the acetyl-CoA used throughout the cell for de novo lipogenesis, cholesterol/isoprenoid synthesis and protein acetylation. Citrate exported from the mitochondria to the cytosol is cleaved by ACLY in an ATP- and CoA-dependent reaction, citrate + ATP + CoA -> acetyl-CoA + oxaloacetate + ADP + Pi, with Mg(2+) as a required cofactor. Each protomer combines an N-terminal acyl-CoA synthetase homology (ASH) superdomain (containing an ATP-grasp module and the catalytic His-760 that forms a tele-phosphohistidine intermediate) and a C-terminal citrate synthase homology (CSH) domain; four protomers assemble into the active homotetramer. ACLY is the predominant source of nucleocytosolic acetyl-CoA and thereby links carbohydrate-derived carbon (via mitochondrial citrate) to lipid biosynthesis and to acetyl-CoA-dependent processes including histone acetylation. Its activity is regulated by phosphorylation (by PKA, GSK3, AKT and BCKDK), by acetylation/ubiquitination competition at Lys-540/546/554, and allosterically by phosphorylated sugars such as fructose 6-phosphate. ACLY is a validated drug target; the prodrug bempedoic acid lowers LDL cholesterol by inhibiting it.

ACLY (ATP-citrate synthase / ATP-citrate lyase) — review notes

UniProt: P53396 (ACLY_HUMAN), 1101 aa, Homo sapiens (9606). HGNC:115.

Identity and catalytic function

ACLY is the cytosolic homotetrameric enzyme that produces the acetyl-CoA used
for de novo lipogenesis, cholesterol synthesis and protein acetylation. Citrate
exported from mitochondria is cleaved in the cytosol.

  • RecName in UniProt: "ATP-citrate synthase"; AltName "ATP-citrate (pro-S-)-lyase";
    "Citrate cleavage enzyme"; EC 2.3.3.8
    [file:human/ACLY/ACLY-uniprot.txt "RecName: Full=ATP-citrate synthase"].
  • CATALYTIC ACTIVITY (UniProt / Rhea RHEA:21160): "oxaloacetate + acetyl-CoA + ADP +
    phosphate = citrate + ATP + CoA"; physiological direction right-to-left (i.e.
    citrate + ATP + CoA -> acetyl-CoA + oxaloacetate + ADP + Pi)
    [file:human/ACLY/ACLY-uniprot.txt "Reaction=oxaloacetate + acetyl-CoA + ADP +"].
  • FUNCTION: "Catalyzes the cleavage of citrate into oxaloacetate and acetyl-CoA, the
    latter serving as common substrate in multiple biochemical reactions in protein,
    carbohydrate and lipid metabolism."
    [file:human/ACLY/ACLY-uniprot.txt "Catalyzes the cleavage of citrate into oxaloacetate and"].
  • COFACTOR: Mg(2+) [file:human/ACLY/ACLY-uniprot.txt "Name=Mg(2+); Xref=ChEBI:CHEBI:18420"].
  • SUBUNIT: Homotetramer [file:human/ACLY/ACLY-uniprot.txt "SUBUNIT: Homotetramer."].
  • SUBCELLULAR LOCATION: Cytoplasm, cytosol
    [file:human/ACLY/ACLY-uniprot.txt "SUBCELLULAR LOCATION: Cytoplasm, cytosol"].
  • Active site His-760, tele-phosphohistidine intermediate; the H760A mutant has reduced
    activity [file:human/ACLY/ACLY-uniprot.txt "Tele-phosphohistidine intermediate"].

  predominant source of nucleocytosolic acetyl-CoA and thereby links carbohydrate-derived
  carbon (via mitochondrial citrate) to lipid biosynthesis and to acetyl-CoA-dependent
  processes including histone acetylation. Its activity is regulated by phosphorylation
  (by PKA, GSK3, AKT and BCKDK), by acetylation/ubiquitination competition at Lys-540/546/554,
  and allosterically by phosphorylated sugars such as fructose 6-phosphate. ACLY is a
  validated drug target; the prodrug bempedoic acid lowers LDL cholesterol by inhibiting it.
