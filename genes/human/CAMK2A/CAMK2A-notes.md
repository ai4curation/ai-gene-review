## Manual reassessment (2026-10-04)

CAMK2A's principal activity is calcium/calmodulin-regulated serine/threonine phosphorylation. Human alpha-domain structures, biochemical assays and E183V experiments support this activity, with target association and holoenzyme assembly organizing the kinase rather than creating multiple redundant catalytic cores [PMID:20668654; PMID:21884935; PMID:28130356]. Thr286 autophosphorylation can sustain autonomous activity after calcium falls. The single synthesized core joins kinase activity to synaptic potentiation, spine development and regulatory autophosphorylation; it does not equate activation, ATP binding and oligomerization with three independent core functions. Human disease variants can increase or decrease regulatory activity and alter target association; alpha and beta variants are not interchangeable [PMID:29100089].

The immutable UniProt record names two actual splice products: Q9UQM7-1 (A, displayed) and Q9UQM7-2 (B, VSP_004766). The standard repository parser restores those literal product records in the candidate. The sequence feature replaces the displayed Lys328 with KKRKSSSSVQLM in B. No functional isoform class, universal compartment difference or new experimental product claim is inferred. The prior absence of a structured products slot did not mean the gene had no alternate products.

### Source-specific biochemical and cellular evidence

PMID:20668654 examines all four human CaMKII kinase domains. Its calmodulin-bound crystal is delta, not alpha; alpha residues 13–301 were among the isolated-domain constructs and weak domain association was measured in solution. PMID:21884935 provides an engineered human-alpha holoenzyme crystal: residues 7–444, a beta7 linker, K42M/D135N/T306V substitutions and bosutinib. The accessible primary Methods also describe chicken calmodulin. These sources support assembly and regulation without requiring every native holoenzyme to adopt the same compact conformation. An erratum (DOI:10.1016/j.cell.2011.10.013) was identified through the primary-author institutional bibliography, but its contents were inaccessible. Fine crystallographic claims are therefore bounded; independent WT/E183V coassembly supports the retained assembly conclusion.

The E183V study reports reduced catalytic/regulatory phosphorylation, changed partner association, neuronal spine targeting and model phenotypes [PMID:28130356]. Its cache contains the abstract, Introduction and Discussion but not detailed Results/Methods. The CAMK2A/CAMK2B developmental study likewise contains partial narrative, and distinguishes alpha-mutant migration effects from the lack of a migration effect of wild-type alpha overexpression/knockdown at the tested developmental stage [PMID:29100089]. These limits do not justify rejecting curated experimental annotations from the title or from a paralog's appearance in the paper.

The STAT1 paper includes a real rat-brain alpha/beta kinase control, but its cellular STAT1 interaction experiments chiefly use gamma/delta [PMID:11972023]. Its alpha-related kinase annotation is therefore supported with the mixed-preparation boundary; the contextual interferon/JAK-STAT annotations defer to the explicit human-Q9UQM7 GO-CAM assignment without claiming an independently verified endogenous human-alpha assay. In contrast, the bacterial-effector study explicitly uses activated CaMKII-alpha T286D/T305A/T306A to rescue an interferon-response phenotype [PMID:35568036]. The paper's pathogen framing does not invalidate its target experiment. That engineered rescue is still distinct from normal alpha signaling in every cell type.

The PSAT1 study's complete abstract reports CaMKII-dependent PSAT1 phosphorylation upstream of GPX4 stabilization [PMID:40281343]. Cached GO-CAM 680ad14200007201 explicitly includes CAMK2A kinase activity in the ferroptosis-resistance pathway. Both distinct current GOA relations, acts_upstream_of and involved_in, survive as separate source objects. CAMK2A does kinase work; PSAT1 enzymology, PHD3 hydroxylation and GPX4 peroxidase activity are not transferred to it. This supersedes the older journal entry's historical source-absence rationale: the current authenticated GOA now contains the assertions. That older entry remains unchanged above as history, not current evidence.

Mouse alpha kinase directly phosphorylates DAGLA and modulates 2-AG signaling; selected full Results and Methods establish striatal association, purified recombinant alpha kinase, phosphosite analysis and the limits of phosphomimetic assays [PMID:23502535]. This resolves the previous uncertainty about negative regulation of hydrolase activity without assigning the lipase's own activity to CAMK2A. Presynaptic alpha deletion and complementary activation-state experiments distinguish activity-dependent release regulation from basal release and from kinase-dependent mechanisms [PMID:12629219; PMID:17660813].
