Gene Description

AKAP12 (gravin / SSeCKS / AKAP250) is a 1782-residue, largely intrinsically disordered multivalent scaffold that concentrates cyclic-AMP and diacylglycerol signalling machinery at the inner face of the plasma membrane. It is N-myristoylated at Gly2 and carries an A-kinase-anchoring region near its C-terminus that binds the dimeric RII regulatory subunit of protein kinase A with high affinity, a separate central region that binds protein kinase C, and three copies of the AKAP calmodulin-binding (WSK) motif. Through these modules it assembles a subplasmalemmal signalling module containing PKA, PKC, PDE4D and adenylate cyclases AC4/AC6, so that cAMP produced at the membrane is both sensed and rapidly degraded within a restricted microdomain. Ligand binding at the scaffold appears to be phosphorylation-gated, although this is established only for the rodent orthologue SSeCKS, where PKC phosphorylation of the central serine cluster antagonises calmodulin binding and releases bound cyclin D; whether the human protein is regulated the same way has not been tested. Functionally, AKAP12 is required for resensitisation and recycling of the internalised beta2-adrenergic receptor, contributes to cAMP-dependent stabilisation of the endothelial barrier, and is concentrated at the cell periphery, filopodia, cell cortex and focal adhesions. Its relationship to the actin cytoskeleton is unsettled. Tet-regulated induction of SSeCKS, on removal of tetracycline, in mouse NIH3T3 fibroblasts elaborates a detergent-resistant cortical cytoskeletal matrix, and bacterially expressed unphosphorylated SSeCKS co-sediments with F-actin in ultracentrifugation assays; in fibroblasts, cortical-edge localisation and costaining along F-actin stress fibres behave as alternative states that track FAK-dependent tyrosine phosphorylation, while in mesangial cells the SSeCKS network is reported to be distinct from F-actin. This work is on the rodent orthologue or on purified protein, and none of it has been repeated on the human protein. Three promoter-specified isoforms (alpha, beta, gamma) differ in their N-termini and in subcellular targeting. The protein is strongly expressed in endothelium and fibroblasts, is induced by phorbol ester and lysophosphatidylcholine, and is the autoantigen recognised by a subset of myasthenia gravis sera.

Proposed New Ontology Terms

cyclic-nucleotide phosphodiesterase binding

Definition: Binding to a cyclic-nucleotide phosphodiesterase, an enzyme that hydrolyses the 3',5'-cyclic phosphate bond of cAMP or cGMP.

Justification: GO can express binding to the enzyme that makes cAMP but not to the enzyme that destroys it. GO:0019899 enzyme binding has 28 direct children, including GO:0008179 adenylate cyclase binding, GO:0019902 phosphatase binding, GO:0035473 lipase binding and GO:0043274 phospholipase binding, and none of them covers a phosphodiesterase; text searches for "phosphodiesterase binding" and "cyclic nucleotide phosphodiesterase binding" return only catalysis and regulation terms (GO:0004112, GO:0170005, GO:0051344), so the absence was checked by enumerating the parent's children and not only by keyword search. The asymmetry has a direct cost here: the single best-evidenced human experiment on AKAP12 shows that it maintains a signalling complex containing PKA and PDE4D, and GOA can record the PKA half but has no term for the PDE4D half. The gap is general to cAMP-microdomain biology - other AKAPs and beta-arrestins anchor PDE4 isoforms too - so the term would be used well beyond this gene. GO already models the analogous regulatory relationship with GO:0170005 cyclic nucleotide phosphodiesterase activator activity, so a binding term completes a pattern rather than introducing a new one.

Parent term: enzyme binding

Suggested Questions for Experts

Q: Should RGD re-examine the Akap12 annotation to GO:0043116 negative regulation of vascular permeability from PMID:20454828? That paper reports that depleting SSeCKS attenuated the cytokine-induced fall in transendothelial resistance and rise in dextran flux, and concludes that SSeCKS is involved in endothelial hyperpermeability - i.e. the experiment supports GO:0043117 positive regulation of vascular permeability, the opposite sign. Because Ensembl Compara transfers the rat annotation to human, the inversion is currently propagated to AKAP12 as well.

Suggested experts: RGD curators, GO Consortium

Q: Does the full text of PMID:23925424 contain a hepatic stellate cell migration assay or any perturbation of Akap12? The abstract describes only expression measurement, yet RGD records both an IMP to GO:0061870 positive regulation of hepatic stellate cell migration and an IEP to GO:0035733 hepatic stellate cell activation from it. If no perturbation exists, both rows and their human Compara transfers should be revisited.

Suggested experts: RGD curators

Q: Should PAINT consider placing a Rho-axis term on PTHR23209? The zebrafish orthologue akap12b carries GO:0035024 negative regulation of Rho protein signal transduction by its own IMP, and the mammalian literature independently reports AKAP12 reducing RhoA and Cdc42 activity more than fivefold (PMID:16547152) and restraining the Rho kinase pathway in cerebral endothelium (PMID:33260683); at present the node propagates only GO:0007165 signal transduction, and the specific Rho observation stops at the zebrafish gene.

Suggested experts: GO Central / PAINT curators

Q: Should UniProt add the two PKC-binding motifs mapped in PMID:21903576 as FT MOTIF features on Q02952? The published consensus matches the human sequence exactly at residues 605-633 and 754-782, overlapping the annotated AKAP CaM-binding motifs 1 (607-627) and 2 (756-776). UniProt currently annotates only the region 266-557 from PMID:9000000, so the entry represents one of two mapped PKC determinants and does not record that the second coincides with the calmodulin sites.

Suggested experts: UniProt curators

Q: Should UniProt correct the SUBUNIT line on Q02952? It states that the protein binds the dimeric RII-alpha regulatory subunit of PKC, but RII-alpha (PRKAR2A) is a regulatory subunit of protein kinase A, not protein kinase C. The line as written contradicts the entry's own FUNCTION text and its RII-binding region feature.

Suggested experts: UniProt curators

Q: Does human AKAP12 bind F-actin directly? Bacterially expressed unphosphorylated rodent SSeCKS co-sediments with F-actin (PMID:12083796), which would license GO:0051015, but the same laboratory reports SSeCKS decorating a cytoskeletal network explicitly distinct from F-actin in mesangial cells (PMID:9885289). The evidence is currently self-opposed and no actin-binding term was proposed here for that reason.

Suggested experts: Cytoskeleton researchers

Q: Is AKAP12 a molecular sequestering agent for cyclin D1 in human cells? Bacterially expressed rodent SSeCKS CY motifs bind cyclins D1 and E, and PKC phosphorylation releases cyclin D1 for nuclear translocation (PMID:10982843). If the CY motifs are conserved and functional in human AKAP12, GO:0140313 molecular sequestering activity would be appropriate, but no human experiment tests it.

Suggested experts: Cell cycle researchers

Current model (mechanistic narrative)

AKAP12 (SSeCKS/Gravin) is a large multivalent scaffold protein that spatiotemporally compartmentalizes kinases, cytoskeletal regulators, and signaling lipids to govern cytoskeletal architecture, cell-cycle progression, receptor recycling, and barriergenesis [PMID:9744295, PMID:10982843, PMID:14657015, PMID:12808449]. Its scaffolding logic is built from defined, phosphorylation-gated binding modules: it carries a PKA RII-binding domain establishing it as a bona fide AKAP [PMID:9885289, PMID:10469144], cyclin-binding (CY) motifs that overlap PKC phosphorylation sites (Ser507/515) so that PKC phosphorylation releases cytoplasmically sequestered cyclin D1 for nuclear translocation [PMID:10982843, PMID:22249313], calmodulin-binding sites antagonized by PKC phosphorylation PMID:11820772, and direct PKCα-binding motifs through which AKAP12 binds and dampens PKC activity to restrain Rb-mediated senescence and cytoskeletal remodeling [PMID:21099353, PMID:21903576]. Through this architecture AKAP12 organizes actin-based cytoskeleton and adhesion, sequestering Src to caveolin-rich lipid rafts to disengage FAK and suppressing RhoA/Cdc42 to block podosome formation, and scaffolding phosphoinositides via MARCKS-like polybasic domains to direct lamellipodial chemotaxis [PMID:9744295, PMID:16547152, PMID:22710722, PMID:25356636]. It functions as a tumor and metastasis suppressor by attenuating Raf/MEK/ERK signaling away from a sequestered PKC pool, reducing MMP-2, VEGF, and invasion [PMID:9187136, PMID:16740695, PMID:20018890]. In endothelial and astrocytic compartments AKAP12 drives blood-brain/blood-retinal barrier formation by suppressing HIF-1α/VEGF and inducing angiopoietin-1, and by compartmentalizing PKA-VASP/Rac1 signaling at endothelial junctions and leading edges while restraining the Rho kinase pathway [PMID:12808449, PMID:17442832, PMID:31162891, PMID:33260683]. As an AKAP it phosphorylation-dependently re-associates with the β2-adrenergic receptor to enable resensitization and recycling [PMID:14657015, PMID:19055733], and in cardiomyocytes couples to PDE8 to limit intracellular cAMP and contractility PMID:38506047; it also scaffolds PKA-mediated phosphorylation of ATR-S435 to recruit XPA during nucleotide excision repair PMID:27683220. AKAP12 expression and stability are tightly controlled, undergoing epigenetic silencing by HDAC3/HDAC7 and Src-driven HDAC1 recruitment, and HDAC6-mediated deacetylation that triggers ubiquitin-proteasomal degradation [PMID:17626016, PMID:20568114, PMID:22584896, PMID:36122629, PMID:29484387].

The decisive paper the provider missed: PMID:9000000

The affinage record (gates_passed: True, 44 citations, all numeric PMIDs) is rich and

The sign inversion: RGD's GO:0043116 contradicts its own source paper

The highest-value finding after PMID:9000000. GO:0043116 negative regulation of vascular permeability reaches human AKAP12 by Ensembl Compara from rat Q5QD51, where RGD holds it
as IMP from PMID:20454828. That paper reports the opposite sign:

Depletion of endogenous SSeCKS in RPMVEC significantly attenuated cytokine-induced
decrease in TER and increase in P (d), but not to the basal levels.

TER is transendothelial electrical resistance: cytokines lower it (leakier barrier),
and removing SSeCKS blunted that, i.e. the protein promotes permeability here. The
authors' own CONCLUSIONS sentence says so outright:

SSeCKS is involved in the endothelial hyperpermeability induced by IL-1β and TNF-α in
inflammatory process.

On its own evidence the rat annotation should be GO:0043117 positive regulation of vascular permeability.

But the term survives on the human row, because a different body of evidence supports
it: mouse-specific siRNA against Akap12 in MyEnd endothelial cells impairs barrier function
(PMID:25188285), and Akap12-knockout mice have leakier vessels. So the action is
KEEP_AS_NON_CORE with propagation_review: root_cause: SOURCE_BAD,
failure_modes: [REGULATORY_SIGN_INVERSION] — the term is right, the provenance is not,
and both facts are recorded rather than one silently inheriting the other.

The two results are probably reconcilable: barrier-protective basally, permeability-promoting
under inflammatory cytokine stimulation. That is the suggested_experiments entry with
matched stimulation arms.

Assessed from the abstract only (full text unavailable), so the note to RGD is phrased as a
re-examination request, not a verdict.

Sequence analysis done for this review: the PKC motifs are the CaM motifs

PMID:21903576 (Gelman lab) publishes a PKC-binding consensus for rodent SSeCKS,
EG(I/V)(T/S)XWXSFK(K/R)(M/L)VTP(K/R)K(K/R)X(K/R)XXXEXXXE(E/D), at aa 592-620 and 741-769.
Those coordinates are rodent, so they cannot simply be read onto human AKAP12.

Rather than transfer the numbers, the consensus was run as a regex against the human Q02952
sequence parsed from AKAP12-uniprot.txt (script:
AKAP12-bioinformatics/akap12_motifs.py; length asserted == 1782 before use). Exactly two matches:

605-633  EGVTPWASFKKMVTPKKRVRRPSESDKED
754-782  EGVSTWESFKRLVTPRKKSKSKLEEKSED

UniProt annotates AKAP CaM-binding (WSK) motifs at 607-627, 756-776 and 801-821.
So matches 1 and 2 coincide with CaM motifs 1 and 2; the shared W.S.K cores are WASFK
and WESFK. The third CaM motif (WVSIK, 801-821) does not match the PKC consensus.

Two consequences:

  1. The human protein carries both published PKC determinants at conserved positions, which
    is what makes GO:0005080 defensible with the human PMID:9000000 evidence as primary
    and the rodent mapping as corroboration.
  2. It gives a mechanism for PMID:11820772's observation that PKC phosphorylation
    antagonises CaM binding — the two ligands compete for one element. That is the
    suggested_experiments ITC competition experiment.

Note what this is not: it is not evidence that UniProt's REGION 266..557 PKC-binding
annotation is misplaced. That region comes from PMID:9000000's human fragment mapping and
is a genuinely separate determinant. AKAP12 has two mapped PKC-binding regions from two
labs; UniProt records one of them. Filed as an additive UniProt suggestion, not a correction.

The PKC-binding motifs coincide with two of the three CaM-binding motifs

akap12_motifs.py searched human Q02952 (1782 aa) with the PKC-binding consensus
published for rodent SSeCKS (PMID:21903576), without assuming the rodent
coordinates transfer. Two matches, and both fall inside a UniProt-annotated AKAP
CaM-binding (WSK) motif:

PKC consensus match UniProt CaM motif WSK core
605-633 607-627 WASFK (610-614)
754-782 756-776 WESFK (759-763)
(no match) 801-821 WVSIK (804-808)

So the human protein has three WSK motifs, of which two also match the
PKC-binding consensus, and the third (801-821) does not.

This is a structural rationale for an observation the review records as
rodent-only: PKC phosphorylation antagonises calmodulin binding
(PMID:11820772). If the PKC-binding determinant physically overlaps the
CaM-binding motif, the two ligands compete for the same segment, and
phosphorylation within it would be expected to disfavour CaM. The overlap is a
sequence fact about the human protein; the antagonism itself has still only been
measured in rodent, and the review hedges it accordingly.

Caveats. These are regex matches to a published consensus, not binding
measurements — they establish that the motif is present and where, not that it is
used. The UniProt motif annotations are PROSITE-ProRule (PRU01241) inferences,
not experimental. Nothing here demonstrates human PKC binding or human CaM
binding.

Open discrepancy: three human motifs vs four rodent sites

PMID:11820772 reports four 1-5-10 CaM-binding sites in rodent SSeCKS;
human UniProt annotates three WSK motifs, and this search finds three WSK
cores. Whether the fourth rodent site is genuinely absent from human or merely
unannotated is unresolved, and is recorded as a knowledge_gaps entry on the
calmodulin core_functions entry rather than silently reconciled.
