# ETFA integrated source guide

This guide combines the actual direct and database evidence assessments for 36 current source contexts. One absent historical location record is retained separately, preserving its source and quotation context. Source retirement describes the observed GOA boundary, not biological loss. The canonical 333-residue precursor, its 314-residue mature chain after removal of 19 residues, and the 284-residue alternative product are distinct. Individual assays do not establish equivalent function for both natural products.

## Core synthesis: human carrier chemistry and attribution

The four ordered core summaries retain electron transfer, alpha-subunit FAD binding, fatty-acid oxidation context and amino-acid catabolic context. The two catabolic entries are contexts of the same heterodimeric carrier activity. Electron transfer uses `contributes_to_molecular_function` because the assayed carrier is ETFA–ETFB. FAD binding remains a directly supported alpha-subunit activity. The seven existing core quotations remain historical evidence anchors; no new publication quotation has been inserted.

* **Human structure — PMID:8962055.** [Original article, DOI:10.1073/pnas.93.25.14355](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.93.25.14355), accessed through the [PMC original](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC26136/). The recombinant-protein/crystallographic Methods and Results describing the overall structure, FAD-binding site and AMP site establish alpha/beta assembly and direct alpha contacts to FAD. Figure 4 caption and the associated structural Results were read; no new figure-pixel inspection was performed during core synthesis. AMP is primarily beta-associated. The isolated first-residue numbering discrepancy is not evidence for shifting every reported contact.
* **Human coupled assay — PMID:25416781.** [Original article, DOI:10.1074/jbc.M114.614115](https://doi.org/10.1074/jbc.M114.614115), accessed through the [PMC original](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4281744/). The recombinant ETF-preparation and DCIP-assay subsections and Results/Figure 5A–D caption were read. Human mature Δ19-ETFalpha was coexpressed with full-length His6-ETFbeta. MCAD with octanoyl-CoA and GCDH with glutaryl-CoA each drive ETF-dependent DCIP reduction. This directly supports the carrier’s redox contribution in both metabolic settings. DCIP is an artificial final acceptor; the assay does not reconstitute ETFDH/ubiquinone reduction, complete respiratory flux or the entire catabolic pathways. Figure 5 caption was read, without new pixel or supplementary-material inspection in this phase.
* **Human reaction roles.** [Reactome R-HSA-169260](https://reactome.org/content/detail/R-HSA-169260) assigns the electron-transfer activity to ETF:FAD complex 169267 through catalyst 169261. [Reactome R-HSA-169270](https://reactome.org/content/detail/R-HSA-169270) contains reduced and oxidized ETF, but its catalyst 169271 is ETFDH entity 448210/Q16134 at the inner membrane. Both ETF forms contain ETFA entity 169262/P13804 in the matrix. Thus the ETF complex participates in redox transfer, while ETFDH performs ubiquinone reduction. The unspecified ETFA endpoint in the reaction model is not an experimentally resolved product boundary.
