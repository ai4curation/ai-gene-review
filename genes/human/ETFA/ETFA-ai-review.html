Gene Description

ETFA is the alpha subunit of electron-transfer flavoprotein (ETF), a soluble mitochondrial-matrix heterodimer with ETFB. Each heterodimer contains one FAD and one AMP: ETFA supplies most of the FAD-binding site, while AMP is embedded in ETFB. ETF accepts electrons from fatty-acid acyl-CoA dehydrogenases, including MCAD and very-long-chain acyl-CoA dehydrogenase, and from dehydrogenases involved in amino-acid and choline catabolism, including GCDH, isovaleryl-CoA dehydrogenase, dimethylglycine dehydrogenase and sarcosine dehydrogenase. It passes these electrons to the inner-membrane enzyme ETFDH, which reduces ubiquinone. This route couples fatty-acid, amino-acid and choline oxidation to respiratory electron transport alongside electron entry through complexes I and II. The canonical 333-residue precursor is imported into mitochondria and processed to a 314-residue mature chain. Biallelic pathogenic ETFA variants cause multiple acyl-CoA dehydrogenase deficiency, also called glutaric aciduria type II.

Core Functions

ETFA contributes to electron transfer by the soluble mitochondrial ETFA–ETFB heterodimer, whose FAD accepts reducing equivalents from matrix dehydrogenases and passes them to ETFDH. ETFA supplies much of the FAD-binding site within this composite carrier; ETFDH separately catalyzes transfer to ubiquinone at the inner membrane. Human heterodimer assays establish electron transfer to an artificial acceptor, not isolated-alpha activity or complete respiratory-chain flux.
