Gene Description

RAD51D (RAD51L3, TRAD) is one of the five human RAD51 paralogs, RecA/RAD51-family proteins that act as accessory factors to the RAD51 recombinase in homologous recombination. RAD51D binds XRCC2 directly to form the DX2 subcomplex. DX2 is part of the heterotetrameric BCDX2 complex (RAD51B-RAD51C-RAD51D-XRCC2) and can also join RAD51C-XRCC3 to form the X3CDX2 complex. In BCDX2, RAD51C-RAD51D-XRCC2 forms a RAD51-filament-like arrangement, and the complex stimulates nucleation and extension of RAD51 filaments on RPA-coated single-stranded DNA, downstream of BRCA2 and upstream of RAD51 loading. X3CDX2 caps the 5' ends of RAD51 filaments and promotes homologous pairing. RAD51D binds ssDNA through its N-terminal domain and binds ATP through its Walker A motif. Unlike RAD51B and RAD51C it lacks the catalytic glutamate and is not an active ATPase within these complexes; it may instead regulate the ATPase of its partners. RAD51D is needed for homologous recombination repair of double-strand breaks and interstrand crosslinks, for slowing and reversing replication forks under stress, and for telomere maintenance, where it localises to telomeres and prevents telomere erosion and end-to-end fusions. The XRCC2-RAD51D subcomplex interacts with BLM and stimulates BLM-mediated disruption of Holliday junction-like structures in vitro. Rad51d loss is embryonic lethal in mice and causes chromosome instability. Germline loss-of-function variants in RAD51D predispose to ovarian cancer and confer PARP-inhibitor sensitivity.

Core Functions

As a subunit of BCDX2, RAD51D forms the DX2 half with XRCC2. RAD51C-RAD51D-XRCC2 mimics a short RAD51 filament, and the complex binds ssDNA through the N-terminal domains and stimulates nucleation and extension of RAD51 filaments on RPA-coated ssDNA. This happens downstream of BRCA2 and upstream of RAD51 loading, in homologous recombination repair of DNA double-strand breaks and interstrand crosslinks. RAD51D binds but does not hydrolyse ATP, and may regulate the RAD51B-RAD51C ATPase.

  recombination repair of double-strand breaks and interstrand crosslinks, for slowing and reversing replication
