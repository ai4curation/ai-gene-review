## 2026-11-01 — biological research synthesis

GABRB2 supplies a beta subunit to the pentameric GABA-A receptor. Human alpha1/beta2/gamma2 structures place beta2 at the GABA-binding interfaces and in the shared ion-channel architecture. Patch-clamp measurements of the engineered receptor, with wild-type comparisons, connect that architecture to GABA-activated currents and bicuculline sensitivity. These observations support the specific GABA-A receptor and GABA-gated chloride-channel refinements; they do not require an independently functioning native beta2 pore. The intracellular-loop engineering also prevents assigning the structural construct to a natural splice accession ([PMID:29950725](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29950725/)). Human Thr287Pro experiments separately distinguish impaired surface delivery from impaired currents in mutant-containing receptors, while retaining wild-type and mixed-receptor comparators ([PMID:27789573](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27789573/)).

Native human-brain assemblies provide an independent setting for beta2 participation. The alpha1-selected receptors came from neurosurgical material outside the epileptic foci and include beta2-containing and mixed beta2/beta3 assemblies. Selection of alpha1-containing receptors, unresolved mixed positions and missing splice identities limit generalization. Those data do not directly establish beta2 binding to neuroligin-2 or a beta2/beta2 lamotrigine site ([PMID:39843743](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39843743/)). Postsynaptic membrane localization describes an important deployment rather than an exclusive compartment: beta2/3-reactive rat labeling also supplies extrasynaptic context, with antibody and species limits ([PMID:9464994](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9464994/)).

Natural-product identity and experimental construct identity require separate treatment. Current UniProt records and the four verified FASTAs give 512, 474, 313 and 372 residues, with P47870-2 displayed. The original splice study reports short experimental forms of 314 and 373 residues, recovered through human-brain transcript work and investigated using engineered expression systems. Those historical lengths are retained without inventing an explanation for the one-residue differences. The short experimental forms lacked TM4 but generated currents with alpha1, so the architecture alone cannot establish loss of channel contribution. The brain transcripts, minigene variants, C-terminal tags and T365A comparison are distinct observations ([PMID:19763268](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19763268/); [UniProt P47870](https://www.uniprot.org/uniprotkb/P47870/entry)).

Synapse organization is supported in defined experimental settings. Beta2-containing receptors expressed in HEK cells supported functional contacts with rat medium spiny neurons; that co-culture demonstrates a receptor-assembly contribution without isolating a beta2-only adhesive mechanism ([PMID:23909897](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23909897/)). In a separate mouse-subunit/neuronal study, beta2 extracellular-domain competition reduced VGAT-positive contact co-localization. Activity was demonstrated in other receptor co-culture experiments, not in that competition arm. Bicuculline produced no statistically significant difference in contact counts; this is not proof of an exactly zero effect. The presynaptic ligand and direct in-vivo mechanism remain unresolved ([PMID:27129275](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27129275/)).

The remaining uncertainty is specific rather than a rejection of experimental curation. Mouse beta2 cochlear evidence concerns progressive loss after initially normal efferent development, and the accessible abstract cannot resolve all four distinct developmental transfers ([PMID:17021187](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17021187/)). Beta2/3-reactive surface labeling does not resolve the beta2-specific dendritic-spine claim ([PMID:25025157](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25025157/)). The accessed vesicle-transport abstract emphasizes gamma2 without resolving the beta2 donor assertion ([PMID:25172774](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25172774/)). None of these access limits establishes that the full original lacks the curator's evidence. The urinary-fraction row supplies one peptide without a recovered sequence, limiting conclusions about modern splice identity, vesicle topology or biogenesis ([PMID:19056867](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19056867/)).
