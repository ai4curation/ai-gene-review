# SLC16A3 (MCT4, UniProt O15427) — curation notes

## Identity and family
- SLC16A3 encodes Monocarboxylate transporter 4 (MCT4; also historically mis-named MCT3) [file:human/SLC16A3/SLC16A3-uniprot.txt "Was initially assigned as monocarboxylate transporter 3 (MCT3)"]. It belongs to the major facilitator superfamily, monocarboxylate porter (TC 2.A.1.13) family [file:human/SLC16A3/SLC16A3-uniprot.txt "Belongs to the major facilitator superfamily."].
- 465 aa, multi-pass membrane protein with 12 transmembrane helices and cytoplasmic N- and C-termini [file:human/SLC16A3/SLC16A3-uniprot.txt "Multi-pass membrane protein"]; [PMID:23935841 "predicted to contain 12 transmembrane-spanning domains (TMDs) with amino and carboxy termini facing the cytosol"].
- PANTHER family PTHR11360 (MONOCARBOXYLATE TRANSPORTER), subfamily SF27 (MCT4) [file:human/SLC16A3/SLC16A3-uniprot.txt "PTHR11360:SF27; MONOCARBOXYLATE TRANSPORTER 4"].

## Core molecular function: proton-coupled L-lactate efflux transporter
- MCT4 is a proton-dependent (H+-linked) symporter of monocarboxylates such as L-lactate and pyruvate [file:human/SLC16A3/SLC16A3-uniprot.txt "Proton-dependent transporter of monocarboxylates such as L-lactate and pyruvate"].
- Catalytic reaction is electroneutral 1:1 lactate:H+ symport: (S)-lactate(in) + H+(in) = (S)-lactate(out) + H+(out) [file:human/SLC16A3/SLC16A3-uniprot.txt "Reaction=(S)-lactate(in) + H(+)(in) = (S)-lactate(out) + H(+)(out)"]. ChEBI: (S)-lactate CHEBI:16651, H+ CHEBI:15378, pyruvate CHEBI:15361.
- Direct experimental demonstration of proton-linked L-lactate transport in Xenopus oocytes; the transport is strongly pH-dependent, confirming the proton-coupled mechanism [PMID:23935841 "indicating that hMCT4 expressed in oocytes preserved the original characteristics of the proton-linked transport system"].
- MCT4 is the major MCT isoform of white (glycolytic) skeletal muscle and mediates efflux of glycolytically produced lactic acid [PMID:11101640 "is the major monocarboxylate transporter isoform present in white skeletal muscle and is responsible for the efflux of lactic acid produced by glycolysis"].
- Arg-278 in TMD8 is a critical substrate-recognition residue: R278Q/K abolish L-lactate transport without affecting membrane localization, whereas R198Q is tolerated [PMID:23935841 "In hMCT4-R278 mutants, the uptake of L-lactate is void of any transport activity without the alteration of hMCT4 localization"]; [file:human/SLC16A3/SLC16A3-uniprot.txt "Abolishes lactate transmembrane transporter activity. Does not affect cell membrane localization."].

### Affinity: the "low-affinity" view was revised
- Early oocyte characterization reported very high Km values (L-lactate 28 mM, D-lactate 519 mM, pyruvate 153 mM) and concluded MCT4 was a low-affinity transporter that effluxes lactate but "not pyruvate" [PMID:11101640 "Km values of MCT4 for these substrates were found to be 28, 519 and 153 mM, respectively"].
- This was later shown to be an artifact of pH-based assays. Using FRET sensors, the true Km for lactate is ~0.7-1.7 mM and for pyruvate ~4.2 mM; MCT4 is a high-affinity lactate transporter with physiologically relevant pyruvate affinity [PMID:31719150 "MCT4 is a high-affinity lactate transporter with physiologically relevant affinity for pyruvate"]; [PMID:31719150 "previous Km estimates based on substrate-induced acidification were severely biased by confounding pH-regulatory mechanisms"].
- UniProt records this revision as a CAUTION [file:human/SLC16A3/SLC16A3-uniprot.txt "it was later shown that SLC16A3 is a high affinity lactate transporter with physiologically relevant affinity for pyruvate"].
- Functionally, MCT4 (unlike MCT1/MCT2) is kinetically adapted to EXPORT lactate even in high-lactate microenvironments [PMID:31719150 "MCT4, but not the related transporters MCT1 and MCT2, endows cells with the ability to export lactate in high-lactate microenvironments"]. Substrate scope includes lactate, pyruvate and ketone bodies [PMID:23935841 "mediates transport of monocarboxylates, such as lactate, pyruvate and ketone bodies, across the plasma membrane"].

## Obligate partner: BSG (basigin / CD147)
- MCT4 (like MCT1 and MCT3, but not MCT2) requires the single-span glycoprotein BSG/CD147 as an ancillary subunit for plasma-membrane trafficking and activity [PMID:10921872 "CD147 specifically interacts with MCT1 and MCT4, two members of the proton-linked monocarboxylate (lactate) transporter family"]; [PMID:10921872 "co-transfection with CD147 enabled expression of active MCT1 or MCT4, but not MCT2, in the plasma membrane"].
- Without BSG, MCT4 is retained in a perinuclear (ER/Golgi) compartment; co-expression of BSG delivers both proteins to the cell surface where they remain tightly bound [PMID:10921872 "CD147 facilitates proper expression of MCT1 and MCT4 at the cell surface, where they remain tightly bound to each other"].
- UniProt: "Interacts with BSG; interaction mediates SLC16A3 targeting to the plasma membrane" [file:human/SLC16A3/SLC16A3-uniprot.txt "interaction mediates SLC16A3 targeting to the plasma membrane"]. The MCT4-BSG assembly is a heterodimeric plasma-membrane transporter complex; the complex is assembled in the ER and loss of either subunit degrades the other [PMID:21199217 "MCT1, MCT3, and MCT4 form a heterodimeric complex with CD147"].
- This is the exact analogue of the B0AT1/ACE2 (collectrin) partnership modeled in batch 10 (SLC6A19). Modeling decision: the bare GO:0005515 protein-binding row against BSG (P26453) is uninformative and is REMOVEd per the protein-binding policy, while the functional partnership is captured by a NEW complex-membership row (part_of GO:1902495 transmembrane transporter complex). BSG is not MCT4's enzymatic function, so it is not a core molecular function, but complex membership is.

## Localization
- Plasma membrane (cell membrane); multi-pass membrane protein. Core location [file:human/SLC16A3/SLC16A3-uniprot.txt "Cell membrane"]. Direct experimental PM localization in oocytes, leukocytes, and cell lines [PMID:11101640 "The protein was correctly targeted to the plasma membrane"]; [PMID:15505343 "Plasmalemmal staining occurred with all MCTs in all leukocyte fractions"].
- In polarized epithelia MCT4 is basolateral: it carries strong, redundant basolateral sorting signals (BLSS) in its C-terminal cytoplasmic tail [PMID:21199217 "MCT3 and MCT4 harbor strong redundant basolateral sorting signals (BLSS) in their C-terminal cytoplasmic tails"]; [PMID:21199217 "MCT3 (SLC16A8) and MCT4 (SLC16A3) are localized basolaterally in all epithelia"]. Mutation of the acidic cluster E425/E426/E427 redirects MCT4 apically [file:human/SLC16A3/SLC16A3-uniprot.txt "Affects subcellular localization leading to apical localization"].
- Detected in the lateral plasma membrane of Caco-2 enterocytes, acting as a basolateral efflux route [PMID:26854723 "MCT4 was only detected in the lateral plasma membrane sections of Caco-2 cells"].
- In retinal pigment epithelium quantitative proteomics MCT4 is detected in both basolateral and apical membrane fractions; basolateral is its canonical polarity, so the apical detection is treated as a context-specific/non-core location.

## Physiology and regulation
- MCT4 exports lactate from highly glycolytic cells: white muscle, astrocytes, white blood cells, chondrocytes, placenta and many tumors (Warburg effect) [PMID:23935841 "expressed strongly in glycolytic tissues such as white skeletal muscle fibers, astrocytes, white blood cells, chondrocytes and some mammalian cell lines"]; [PMID:31719150 "highly expressed in metastatic tumors and at inflammatory sites undergoing hypoxia or the Warburg effect"].
- Highly expressed in skeletal muscle [file:human/SLC16A3/SLC16A3-uniprot.txt "Highly expressed in skeletal muscle."].
- Hypoxia-inducible: up-regulated by hypoxia through a HIF1A (HIF-1alpha)-dependent mechanism, distinguishing it from the non-hypoxia-regulated MCT1 [file:human/SLC16A3/SLC16A3-uniprot.txt "Up-regulated by hypoxia through a HIF1A-mediated mechanism."] (PMID:16452478, not cached; UniProt-curated).

## RNA binding (GO:0003723)
- A single HDA row (PMID:22658674) from a global mRNA-interactome capture atlas. MCT4 is a polytopic membrane transporter with no described RNA-binding function; such HDA RNA-binding hits on membrane transporters are common non-specific captures. Treated as over-annotation.

## Process-term assessment (participation test)
- MCT4 performs the lactate/pyruvate efflux step itself, so lactate transmembrane transport (GO:0035873) and plasma membrane lactate transport (GO:0035879) are genuine. Pyruvate transmembrane transport (GO:1901475) is a real secondary (non-core) substrate activity.
- "pyruvate catabolic process" (GO:0042867, IEA from ortholog): fails the participation test. MCT4 transports pyruvate across the membrane; it does not catalyze any step of pyruvate breakdown. Over-annotation from electronic orthology transfer.
- Downstream glycolysis/tumor/pH-homeostasis roles are consequences of lactate export (necessity), not steps MCT4 performs, so no process term for those is proposed.

## Summary of core function
Secondary-active, proton-coupled (H+-symport, electroneutral 1:1) low-Km L-lactate efflux transporter of the plasma membrane, exporting lactate (and, secondarily, pyruvate and ketone bodies) from highly glycolytic cells; HIF-1alpha-induced under hypoxia; functions as a heterodimeric plasma-membrane transporter complex with its obligate ancillary subunit BSG/CD147, and is targeted basolaterally in polarized epithelia via C-terminal sorting signals.
