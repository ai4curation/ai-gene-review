CC   -!- FUNCTION: Component of neuronal acetylcholine receptors (nAChRs) that
CC       function as pentameric, ligand-gated cation channels with high calcium
CC       permeability among other activities. nAChRs are excitatory
CC       neurotrasnmitter receptors formed by a collection of nAChR subunits
CC       known to mediate synaptic transmission in the nervous system and the
CC       neuromuscular junction. Each nAchR subunit confers differential
CC       attributes to channel properties, including activation, deactivation
CC       and desensitization kinetics, pH sensitivity, cation permeability, and
CC       binding to allosteric modulators (PubMed:20881005, PubMed:31488329,
CC       PubMed:8663494, PubMed:8906617, PubMed:9203638). CHRNB4 forms
CC       heteropentameric neuronal acetylcholine receptors with CHRNA2, CHRNA3
CC       and CHRNA4, as well as CHRNA5 and CHRNB3 as accesory subunits
CC       (PubMed:11118490, PubMed:20881005, PubMed:8663494). CHRNA3:CHRNB4 being
CC       predominant in neurons of the autonomic ganglia, it is known as
CC       ganglionic nicotinic receptor (PubMed:31488329). CHRNA3:CHRNB4 or
CC       CHRNA3:CHRNA5:CHRNB4 play also an important role in the habenulo-
CC       interpeduncular tract, modulating the mesolimbic dopamine system and
CC       affecting reward circuits and addiction (By similarity). Hypothalamic
CC       CHRNA3:CHRNB4 nAChR activation by nicotine leads to activation of POMC
CC       neurons and a decrease in food intake (By similarity).
