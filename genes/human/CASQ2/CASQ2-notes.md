# CASQ2 evidence notes

CASQ2 is an acidic, high-capacity calcium buffer in the sarcoplasmic-reticulum lumen. Calcium-dependent polymerization and association with the release complex couple this reservoir to calcium availability and release during cardiac contraction. The mechanistic synthesis has one calcium-sequestering core. It includes the existing cardiac release-regulation process and calcium-channel-complex membership while keeping the conducting pore assigned to RYR2. Calcium binding and polymer assembly are components of this unit, not additional independent cores.

The complete cached human-protein abstracts support calcium binding, storage and release-complex regulation. PMID:17881003 compares wild-type human CASQ2 with disease-associated variants and relates polymerization to binding capacity. PMID:16601229 combines human genetics with adult-rat-myocyte expression and planar-bilayer assays; its R33Q result separates preserved bulk binding from impaired release regulation. PMID:16908766 likewise separates two mutant mechanisms: the truncation loses calcium binding, while L167H preserves binding in that assay but still alters storage and release in rat myocytes. The later biochemical study reports decreased capacity for L167H and R33Q under its own conditions. These observations should not be flattened into one universal mutant mechanism without comparing complete protocols.

PMID:21416293 identifies human CASQ2 phosphorylation sites and tests phosphomimetic substitutions. The double substitution affects calcium-binding capacity above 6 mM calcium. Its proposed trafficking interpretation is not treated as an independently demonstrated localization or transport activity. Phosphorylation of CASQ2 does not make CASQ2 a kinase.
