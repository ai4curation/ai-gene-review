Gene Description

CSF2RB encodes the shared transmembrane beta chain of the human GM-CSF, interleukin-3 and interleukin-5 receptors. It cooperates with ligand-specific alpha chains to form cytokine-binding receptor assemblies, stabilize high-affinity interactions and couple extracellular engagement to associated JAKs and intracellular signaling proteins. This supports cytokine-dependent survival, proliferation and activation of hematopoietic cells. The beta chain has no intrinsic kinase domain. Disrupted receptor function can impair pulmonary surfactant homeostasis and is associated with pulmonary alveolar proteinosis. Two sequence-defined products differ by a six-residue insertion in the extracellular region; their functional equivalence has not been established.

Proposed New Ontology Terms

granulocyte-macrophage colony-stimulating factor binding

Definition: Binding to granulocyte-macrophage colony-stimulating factor (GM-CSF).

Justification: Preserve the distinct GM-CSF ligand identity of an existing experimentally supported binding assertion. The current cytokine-binding child audit finds IL-3, IL-5 and G-CSF binding terms, whereas GO:0042021 explicitly names a GM-CSF complex. Resolve whether that existing term should be clarified or a separate ligand-binding term is needed; no autonomous beta-only binding or new process is asserted.

2026-10-13 — source-specific review

CSF2RB is the shared beta chain of the GM-CSF, IL-3 and IL-5 receptors. Its core role combines cooperative ligand/receptor assembly with recruitment of signaling machinery. The compact core records coreceptor activity and contribution to cytokine-receptor activity, the three ligand-specific pathways, and plasma-membrane location. This does not assign intrinsic kinase activity to the beta chain or make the private alpha chains dispensable. Original receptor reconstitution establishes PMID:1702217, and beta-chain mutagenesis supports PMID:7957082. Both originals were read in the bounded core consultation; the former directly measures binding, not downstream phosphorylation.

The 2024 structural and cellular study qualifies a universal requirement for a dodecamer. It reports signaling through paired private-alpha/beta intracellular domains, PMID:38614096. The actual assays use extracellular zipper/GFP constructs, tagged receptors in HeLa cells, JAK FERM–SH2 recruitment constructs and engineered HEK signaling chimeras. These do not establish one compulsory native stoichiometry in every cell, nor exclude minor higher-order populations or complexes elsewhere in the cell. The older 2008/2016 structures and mutagenesis remain positive evidence for cooperative receptor function. The 2008 supplement and selected 2016 original windows/supplement were read; access is not described as a complete read of both main papers.

The two official products reconstruct exactly as 897 and 903 amino acids. VSP_032798 replaces canonical Gly285 with GSAVLLR. This extracellular difference is recorded without claiming that the longer product has equivalent ligand affinity or signaling output, or mapping legacy constructs to a numbered product without sequence evidence. The existing annotation source objects retain their original product omissions and ordinary qualifiers.

Specific binding judgments preserve assay scope. JAK2 association is supported by independent receptor co-immunoprecipitation and newer recruitment experiments; the 2006 Figure 2D phosphorylated-JAK2 lysate panel is not itself a receptor–JAK2 pull-down. IL-5-receptor binding is a binding-class assertion, not an assertion that CSF2RB is an IL-5 agonist. The integrin source used human EPC co-immunoprecipitation and extracellular-domain Fc pull-down from lysates, not purified binary proteins. Blocking the interaction altered IL-3 responses PMID:20802515, so ordinary integrin binding is supported without assigning the more specific cell–matrix-adhesion binding class. The positive CBAP/TMEM102 interaction remains non-core generic binding where a defensible narrower molecular activity has not been established. This follows the standing campaign policy for supported generic binding.

The rat lipopolysaccharide response is retained as non-core at its actual expression-pattern scope. The available abstract reports PMID:7643220 in rat microglia/brain.
