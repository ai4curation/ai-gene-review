# CAMTA1 evidence and functional review

CAMTA1 is a nuclear DNA-dependent transcriptional regulator with a conserved CG-1 domain, a TIG domain, ankyrin repeats and IQ motifs. Human reporter experiments establish transcriptional activation capacity. Human loss-of-function rearrangements connect CAMTA1 to congenital cerebellar ataxia with or without intellectual disability; mouse studies provide a mechanistic context for neuronal maintenance. A separate positive influence on NFAT signaling is supported in cultured human cells, with its intervening mechanism unresolved.

## Evidence boundaries

- PMID:11925432, A novel family of calmodulin-binding transcription activators in multicellular organisms: the abstract explicitly includes human CAMTA1/2 activation in LexA-tethered yeast reporters. That demonstrates activation capacity; LexA supplies the DNA tether. The specified nuclear fractionation concerns Arabidopsis, and the high-affinity calmodulin measurement is not assigned to human CAMTA1. Full article access failed.
- PMID:15138581, Cell cycle-dependent transcriptional regulation of calmodulin-binding transcription activator 1 in neuroblastoma cells: the accessible human abstract measures cell-cycle-dependent abundance. It does not resolve the cytoplasmic distribution cited by UniProt. Expression changes alone do not establish a CAMTA1 role in cell-cycle progression. Full article access failed.
- PMID:25049392, Ataxia and Purkinje cell degeneration in mice lacking the CAMTA1 transcription factor: the normal cache remains abstract-only. Selected primary Results, Discussion and Methods were read separately at [PMC4128133](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4128133/). Detailed motif selection used mouse CAMTA2, but the main text also reports CAMTA1 K141E loss of DNA binding and activation. Both CAMTAs activate motif reporters. Supplementary figures were not independently inspected. Mouse neuronal loss follows initially preserved cerebellar architecture; this is not evidence of a direct human embryonic-morphogenesis activity.
- PMID:22693284, Intragenic CAMTA1 rearrangements cause non-progressive congenital ataxia with or without intellectual disability: the human disease association is supported by the abstract and verified PubMed identity. Clinical non-progression must not be equated with static imaging: the abstract reports progressive regional atrophy. The clinical results do not themselves establish a molecular binding mechanism. Full article access failed.
- PMID:23853098, MicroRNA-124 suppresses the transactivation of nuclear factor of activated T cells by targeting multiple genes and inhibits the proliferation of pulmonary artery smooth muscle cells: the cached CAMTA1-targeted Methods, Results and Discussion were read. CAMTA1 knockdown reduces stimulated NFAT reporter output in 293A cells and c-Myc-tagged NFATc2 dephosphorylation in 293T cells, as distinguished in the separately accessed primary Figure 4 caption. Human CAMTA1 3-prime-UTR experiments identify it as a miR-124 target. The broader smooth-muscle miRNA phenotypes involve multiple targets and are not assigned wholesale to CAMTA1.

## Annotation and core decisions

All ten original assertion objects and all four products remain exact. Six assertions are accepted, generic DNA binding is refined to GO:0003700, two assertions remain undecided, and the experimental NFAT-regulatory process is retained as non-core. No NEW annotation is proposed.

The official definitions of [GO:0003700](https://amigo.geneontology.org/amigo/term/GO:0003700) and [GO:0003712](https://amigo.geneontology.org/amigo/term/GO:0003712) distinguish intrinsic DNA-dependent regulation from regulation through a DNA-binding transcription-factor partner. Human activation evidence, conserved domains and mammalian molecular experiments support one DNA-binding transcription-factor core in the nucleus, linked to positive RNA polymerase II transcriptional regulation. The core does not assert a characterized human promoter repertoire, direct calcium sensing, calmodulin binding, a particular protein complex, or NFAT catalytic activity.

The coregulator IBA is plausible and remains UNDECIDED because its distinct partner mechanism and precise PTN000596526 IBD placement were not recovered. DNA binding does not disprove coregulation. The donor count and mixture of descendant species are not arguments against an ancestral assertion, and no target-specific loss is claimed. Broader IBA DNA-binding, nuclear and transcriptional assertions are retained as coherent inherited functions. The cytoplasm IEA remains UNDECIDED because the relevant localization details could not be accessed; a nuclear activity does not exclude a cytoplasmic pool.

## Follow-up: native CAMTA promoter evidence

PMID:34499028, Neuronal calmodulin levels are controlled by CAMTA transcription factors, is now used as experimental family evidence. This supersedes the earlier access note that set this existing cache aside. Its full cached Introduction, Results and figure captions, Discussion, and Methods were read for this follow-up; the source cache itself remains unchanged.

In C. elegans, a CRISPR knock-in CAMT-1a::GFP strain was used for ChIP-seq with input and mock-pulldown controls. The experiments identify three enriched regions approximately 6.3, 4.8 and 2.2 kb upstream of the cmd-1 translation start. The tested single or double promoter-region deletions leave aggregation and O2 responses similar to controls. Deleting all three regions produces locomotory and chemotaxis defects resembling camt-1 loss; extra neuronal CMD-1 rescues these defects. The promoter experiment therefore connects native occupancy with cis-regulatory function. ChIP alone does not distinguish direct DNA contacts from tethered occupancy, and the Discussion reports that de novo motif analysis did not find coincident motifs, while noting overlap with mouse CAMTA motifs. The integrated CG-1, human transactivation and mammalian DNA-recognition evidence remains the basis for the human DNA-binding transcription-factor refinement; the worm result strengthens it without being mislabeled a human experiment.

The official GO definitions distinguish DNA-binding transcription-factor activity (GO:0003700) from regulation through a DNA-binding transcription-factor partner (GO:0003712). These mechanisms can coexist. Neither native promoter occupancy nor the new worm experiments prove that human CAMTA1 lacks coregulator activity. The exact frozen coregulator row contains PTN000596526, PomBase:SPAC26H5.05 and UniProtKB:O94983. Contrary to one premise of the external review, WB:WBGene00020251 is present in other CAMTA1 IBA rows but not this coregulator row. Reading experimental family evidence does not reconstruct the PAINT node placement.

A bounded check of the literal source identities confirms PomBase SPAC26H5.05 as Mga2 and UniProt O94983 as CAMTA2. The primary abstract of Song et al., The transcriptional coactivator CAMTA2 stimulates cardiac growth by opposing class II histone deacetylases ([PubMed 16678093](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16678093/)), reports CAMTA-associated activation through the DNA-binding partner Nkx2-5. This supports the plausibility of a separate family coregulator mechanism; its full paper and exact PAINT placement were not reconstructed here. It is not used to manufacture a human CAMTA1 partner annotation. The two existing actions therefore remain MODIFY and UNDECIDED, with the uncertainty now specifying the unresolved human mechanism and ancestral placement rather than overlooking the available worm study.

The same worm paper studies potential IQ-dependent feedback at elevated calmodulin levels, but explicitly treats the binding model and physiological feedback as unresolved. It does not establish direct human CAMTA1 calcium or calmodulin binding. No new molecular-function or process assertion is introduced. The [2024 eLife correction](https://elifesciences.org/articles/104114) was read in full: it adds Saliha E Sönmez as an author for Drosophila retinal imaging and makes no change to the CAMT-1 promoter experiments or their interpretation.

Two optional review concerns were checked against external primary material already retained with the original review. The published [Figure 4 caption](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3757204/#F4) in PMID:23853098 explicitly assigns the reporter assay to 293A and the CAMTA1/PTBP1 knockdown dephosphorylation assay in panel B to 293T; that accurate distinction is retained. The primary Results of PMID:25049392 explicitly report CAMTA1 K141E loss of DNA binding, so the statement is retained with its existing external-access provenance despite the canonical cache being abstract-only. No claim is weakened solely because it was checked outside the cache.
