Gene Description

CAMTA1 is a nuclear transcriptional regulator containing a CG-1 DNA-binding domain, a TIG domain, ankyrin repeats and C-terminal IQ motifs. Its conserved DNA-recognition and transcription-activation functions support regulation of neuronal gene expression. Human CAMTA1 has transcriptional activation capacity, and loss-of-function rearrangements cause congenital cerebellar ataxia with or without intellectual disability. Mouse studies connect Camta1 deficiency to impaired Purkinje-cell maintenance and cerebellar degeneration. Human cell experiments also implicate CAMTA1 in positive regulation of calcineurin-NFAT signaling, although the intervening molecular mechanism remains unresolved.

CAMTA1 evidence and functional review

CAMTA1 is a nuclear DNA-dependent transcriptional regulator with a conserved CG-1 domain, a TIG domain, ankyrin repeats and IQ motifs. Human reporter experiments establish transcriptional activation capacity. Human loss-of-function rearrangements connect CAMTA1 to congenital cerebellar ataxia with or without intellectual disability; mouse studies provide a mechanistic context for neuronal maintenance. A separate positive influence on NFAT signaling is supported in cultured human cells, with its intervening mechanism unresolved.

Identity and products

The normal UniProt record is human Q9Y6Y1, 1,673 amino acids, sequence version 4, CRC64 008AA6A906374C00. Its four machine-fetched products are preserved: Q9Y6Y1-1; Q9Y6Y1-2 (VSP_035936, VSP_035937); Q9Y6Y1-3 (VSP_043842, VSP_043843); and Q9Y6Y1-4 (VSP_046358, VSP_046359). Products 3 and 4 lack most of the canonical sequence. The full-length domain architecture and activity are not asserted independently for every short product.

Evidence boundaries

  • PMID:11925432, A novel family of calmodulin-binding transcription activators in multicellular organisms: the abstract explicitly includes human CAMTA1/2 activation in LexA-tethered yeast reporters. That demonstrates activation capacity; LexA supplies the DNA tether. The specified nuclear fractionation concerns Arabidopsis, and the high-affinity calmodulin measurement is not assigned to human CAMTA1. Full article access failed.
  • PMID:15138581, Cell cycle-dependent transcriptional regulation of calmodulin-binding transcription activator 1 in neuroblastoma cells: the accessible human abstract measures cell-cycle-dependent abundance. It does not resolve the cytoplasmic distribution cited by UniProt. Expression changes alone do not establish a CAMTA1 role in cell-cycle progression. Full article access failed.
  • PMID:25049392, Ataxia and Purkinje cell degeneration in mice lacking the CAMTA1 transcription factor: the normal cache remains abstract-only. Selected primary Results, Discussion and Methods were read separately at PMC4128133. Detailed motif selection used mouse CAMTA2, but the main text also reports CAMTA1 K141E loss of DNA binding and activation. Both CAMTAs activate motif reporters. Supplementary figures were not independently inspected. Mouse neuronal loss follows initially preserved cerebellar architecture; this is not evidence of a direct human embryonic-morphogenesis activity.
  • PMID:22693284, Intragenic CAMTA1 rearrangements cause non-progressive congenital ataxia with or without intellectual disability: the human disease association is supported by the abstract and verified PubMed identity. Clinical non-progression must not be equated with static imaging: the abstract reports progressive regional atrophy. The clinical results do not themselves establish a molecular binding mechanism. Full article access failed.
  • PMID:23853098, MicroRNA-124 suppresses the transactivation of nuclear factor of activated T cells by targeting multiple genes and inhibits the proliferation of pulmonary artery smooth muscle cells: the cached CAMTA1-targeted Methods, Results and Discussion were read. CAMTA1 knockdown reduces stimulated NFAT reporter output in 293A cells and c-Myc-tagged NFATc2 dephosphorylation in 293T cells, as distinguished in the separately accessed primary Figure 4 caption. Human CAMTA1 3-prime-UTR experiments identify it as a miR-124 target. The broader smooth-muscle miRNA phenotypes involve multiple targets and are not assigned wholesale to CAMTA1.

Annotation and core decisions

All ten original assertion objects and all four products remain exact. Six assertions are accepted, generic DNA binding is refined to GO:0003700, two assertions remain undecided, and the experimental NFAT-regulatory process is retained as non-core. No NEW annotation is proposed.

The official definitions of GO:0003700 and GO:0003712 distinguish intrinsic DNA-dependent regulation from regulation through a DNA-binding transcription-factor partner. Human activation evidence, conserved domains and mammalian molecular experiments support one DNA-binding transcription-factor core in the nucleus, linked to positive RNA polymerase II transcriptional regulation. The core does not assert a characterized human promoter repertoire, direct calcium sensing, calmodulin binding, a particular protein complex, or NFAT catalytic activity.

The coregulator IBA is plausible and remains UNDECIDED because its distinct partner mechanism and precise PTN000596526 IBD placement were not recovered. DNA binding does not disprove coregulation. The donor count and mixture of descendant species are not arguments against an ancestral assertion, and no target-specific loss is claimed. Broader IBA DNA-binding, nuclear and transcriptional assertions are retained as coherent inherited functions. The cytoplasm IEA remains UNDECIDED because the relevant localization details could not be accessed; a nuclear activity does not exclude a cytoplasmic pool.
