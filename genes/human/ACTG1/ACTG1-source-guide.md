## Primary evidence

### PMID:19497859 — Gamma-actin is required for cytoskeletal maintenance but not development.

[Primary article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2701000/). Native abstract only; primary PMC full Results, Methods and figures inspected externally.

Mouse Actg1 gene-null stereocilia form, then deteriorate with progressive adult hearing loss despite beta-actin compensation. Gamma-actin normally occupies the stereocilia core; this is structural participation, not motor chemistry.

Limits: The null-mouse developmental/maintenance comparison and noise-damage redistribution experiment use different species: mouse knockout versus noise-exposed guinea pig. Beta-actin also accumulates at core gaps; redistribution alone does not demonstrate gamma-exclusive repair.
Limits: Whole-gene loss does not isolate an indispensable gamma protein sequence function; interpret alongside the 2025 Actg1 coding-beta replacement.

Cached locator: Abstract, line 60. Exact excerpt: “gamma(cyto)-actin localizes along the length of stereocilia”.

### PMID:20308063 — Ion-dependent polymerization differences between mammalian beta- and gamma-nonmuscle actin isoforms.

[Primary article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2871477/). Native abstract only; primary PMC Methods, Table 1 and polymerization/phosphate-release Results inspected externally.

Recombinant human beta/gamma actin can copolymerize. Calcium-bound gamma-actin has slower nucleotide exchange, nucleation and filament turnover; magnesium-bound differences are much smaller. Gamma-actin itself participates in ATP turnover/phosphate release associated with polymerization and treadmilling.

Limits: Baculovirus/Sf21 preparations contain 20–25% insect actin; not chemically pure human isoforms. Authors tested mixture controls. The phosphate-release assay does not by itself separate hydrolysis chemistry from product-release kinetics.
Limits: Cation-dependent in vitro differences do not establish universal intracellular isoform segregation or an indispensable gamma-specific role. Actin ATP chemistry is distinct from myosin motor ATPase chemistry.
Limits: Human coding constructs are described without a purification affinity tag. The abstract uses Asp1–3/Glu1–3 and Val10/Ile10 numbering; other papers use precursor positions 2–4, so do not silently merge residue-number conventions.

Cached locator: Abstract, line 54. Exact excerpt: “the two will readily co-polymerize”.
Cached locator: Abstract, line 57. Exact excerpt: “Phosphate release during treadmilling is twice as fast with beta- as with gamma-actin”.

### PMID:20976199 — β-actin and γ-actin are each dispensable for auditory hair cell development but required for Stereocilia maintenance.

[Primary article](https://journals.plos.org/plosgenetics/article?id=10.1371/journal.pgen.1001158). Full native XML-derived text; primary PLOS article inspected.

Mouse conditional deletion experiments distinguish early stereocilia formation from later maintenance. Both cytoplasmic actins colocalize in hair-cell structures. Combined Actb/Actg1 deletion largely eliminates stereocilia, whereas individual loss permits development but causes distinct progressive maintenance defects.
The actin filament is the substrate/track for myosin motors. Actin assembly can generate protrusive force without conferring the motor protein molecular function.

Limits: Conditional mouse gene deletions, not direct human ACTG1 motor assays. Antibody staining methods affect apparent isoform distribution; no observed hair-cell structure contained only one isoform.
Limits: Do not interpret the gene-null maintenance phenotypes as disproving protein interchangeability tested by endogenous-locus coding replacement in 2025.

Cached locator: Results heading and Figure 2, line 81. Exact excerpt: “β-actin and γ-actin colocalize”.
Cached locator: Introduction; general mechanistic distinction, line 66. Exact excerpt: “Actin filaments are substrates for myosin motors”.
Cached locator: Results: double knockout; Figure 3, line 81. Exact excerpt: “devoid of phalloidin-stained stereocilia”.

### PMID:40109120 — The complete absence of cytoplasmic γ-actin results in no discernible phenotype in mice or primary fibroblasts.

[Primary article](https://febs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/febs.70075). Full native XML-derived text and primary publisher Results/Methods/Figures inspected.

Endogenous mouse Actg1 coding-beta replacement produces beta-actin instead of gamma-actin while retaining the Actg1 locus. No gamma protein was detected; the tested survival, growth, activity, contractility, fibroblast motility and auditory outcomes showed no detrimental measurable phenotype.
Auditory brainstem responses at 4–32 kHz and outer-hair-cell stereocilia morphology were normal or slightly improved at 16 weeks (Figure 8; n>=3). This directly limits claims that the gamma amino-acid sequence is uniquely required for maintenance under these tested conditions.

Limits: Coding replacement is not Actg1 gene deletion. It does not refute the earlier gene-null phenotype or negate normal gamma-actin structural participation.
Limits: No lifetime, noise-injury, human, or every-neuronal-cell-type equivalence was established. Most other phenotypes were examined at 3 months; survival followed to P180.
Limits: G:F actin ratio was not newly measured in these replacement MEFs; the authors inferred no need to measure it from prior null data and SRF/MRTF/YAP results. Do not report a new direct G:F assay.

Cached locator: Results: replacement mice; Figure 2, line 153. Exact excerpt: “no γ‐actin protein”.
Cached locator: Results: auditory function; Figure 8, line 320. Exact excerpt: “mutants had similar or even slightly improved ABR thresholds”.
Cached locator: Results: auditory function conclusion, line 323. Exact excerpt: “for at least 16 weeks in mice”.

### PMID:28493397 — A recurrent de novo mutation in ACTG1 causes isolated ocular coloboma.

[Primary article](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28493397/). Full native XML-derived text, Figure 2 legend and Results; primary PubMed title/identifier/figure checked.

Human HEK293 cells express N-terminal eGFP-tagged ACTG1 WT or P70L; separate CRISPR mouse embryonic fibroblasts carry homozygous P70L. Imaging shows impaired mutant incorporation into cellular actin structures.
GFP pull-down/MS followed by western confirmation supports association with twinfilin-1, CAPz beta, cofilin and profilin. This is association evidence, not purified binary binding affinity.

Limits: Abstract wording that mutant is incapable of F-actin incorporation overstates Fig 2D: mutant G fraction is reduced but F fraction is comparable to WT. Avoid repeating an absolute incorporation defect.
Limits: Enhanced mutant CCT association concerns actin as a folding substrate, not ACTG1 chaperone activity. Validate any specific interaction partner against the actual table/figure rather than assuming every screen row is confirmed.

Cached locator: Figure 2D legend, line 91. Exact excerpt: “levels were comparable to WT in the F‐actin phase”.
Cached locator: Figure 2E legend, line 91. Exact excerpt: “Western blotting confirmed these for twinfilin‐1, CAPzβ, cofilin, and profilin.”.

### PMID:22855531 — Nonredundant roles of cytoplasmic β- and γ-actin isoforms in regulation of epithelial apical junctions.

[Primary article](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22855531/). Full native XML-derived Results/Methods; primary PubMed identifier/title checked.

Human intestinal SK-CO15/Caco-2 epithelial models, isoform-specific siRNAs and inhibitory N-terminal peptides connect gamma-actin to tight-junction organization and rapid junction reassembly. Both isoforms contribute to barrier function and 3D epithelial cyst formation.
Gamma depletion affects 3D cyst polarity even though the steady-state 2D monolayer polarity effect is beta-selective. The abstract alone misses this context relevant to the native morphogenesis annotation.

Limits: Culture-specific depletion phenotypes do not establish universal protein-sequence-specific indispensability. Distinguish epithelial junction/barrier function from dedicated transport, transcription or motor catalysis.

Cached locator: Discussion: reassembly and 3D cyst formation; Figure 11, line 144. Exact excerpt: “γ-CYA knockdown caused polarity defects only in the 3D system”.
Cached locator: Discussion/conclusion, line 144. Exact excerpt: “γ-CYA controls normal TJ architecture”.

### PMID:25705373 — γ-Actin plays a key role in endothelial cell motility and neovessel maintenance.

[Primary article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4335457/). Full native XML-derived Methods/Results, Figures 2–6; primary PMC identifier/title checked.

Human immortalized HMEC-1 and BMH29L endothelial models show reduced migration and rapid regression of initially formed Matrigel tubes after partial gamma depletion. Increased stress fibres/focal adhesions/pMLC accompany depletion; ROCK inhibitors rescue migration and partly rescue vascular-network maintenance.
VE-cadherin protein up-regulation during Matrigel differentiation depends on gamma expression; no steady-state difference on plastic, and VEGFR2 induction is unaffected.

Limits: These are culture models, not in vivo angiogenesis or wound repair. The wound assay measures closure of a cell-free culture gap.
Limits: VE-cadherin was assayed by western blot; no direct transcription-factor function was demonstrated. Gamma-actin is not the ROCK kinase or myosin motor. Beta/gamma staining overlaps despite spatial preferences.

Cached locator: Results/Figure 5, line 255. Exact excerpt: “γ-actin knockdown significantly impaired VE-cadherin up-regulation”.
Cached locator: Methods: Wound healing assay, line 121. Exact excerpt: “leaving a definite cell-free gap of approximately 400 μm”.
Cached locator: Abstract; Matrigel networks, line 73. Exact excerpt: “but were unable to maintain these structures”.
