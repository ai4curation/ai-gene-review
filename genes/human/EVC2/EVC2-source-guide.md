# EVC2 source guide

# Human sperm-nucleus source

PMID:21630459, [Proteomic characterization of the human sperm nucleus](https://doi.org/10.1002/pmic.201000799), reports a human sperm nuclear proteomics study. Its complete journal text and original publisher supplement were not recovered. The normal publication cache remains abstract-only and unchanged; the proposed reference retains `full_text_unavailable: true`.

Positive target evidence is available in coauthor **Judit Castillo Corullón's 2014 University of Barcelona thesis**, *Caracterització del contingut proteic del nucli de l’espermatozoide humà i el seu potencial epigenètic*: [author-deposited PDF](https://diposit.ub.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/748602ae-3122-4398-88b1-5043df608fcb/content). Results 4.2.1, printed page 130, explicitly identifies its 403-protein list as part of the 2011 article's Supporting Information. Annex 8.2, page 191, identifies that article. Supplementary Table 8.1 begins on page 193 and lists **EVC2, Limbin, Q86UK5, 3.7% coverage and three peptides** on page 199. These four pages were read as text and visually inspected; the table identity and target row were also independently checked by ROOT.

The author-restated experiment uses nuclei from four pooled normozoospermic semen samples. Thesis Methods 3.5.1, pages 97–100, describe CTAB isolation, washes and nuclear extraction; Methods 3.5.5, pages 112–113, describe LC-MS/MS and the thesis's search criteria. These are accessible author Methods, not a claim that the complete journal protocol was recovered or that every later thesis method is identical to it.

This positive target entry supports retaining the imported nucleus HDA assertion as **KEEP_AS_NON_CORE**, scoped to the sperm nuclear preparation. Neither a nuclear molecular function nor localization in every cell type follows from this observation. The recovered table reports a peptide count; no individual EVC2 sequences, spectra or target-specific uniqueness check was recovered. Q86UK5 has no isoform suffix in that table, so none of the three natural UniProt products is assigned to the experiment. No EVC2-specific imaging is claimed.

The correct ResearchGate article page is [51182225](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/51182225_Proteomic_characterization_of_the_human_sperm_nucleus), which was request-only. The similarly encountered 2012 *Proteomics of the Spermatozoon* review is a different publication and was not used as target evidence. No inference of contamination, wrong-gene assignment or false nuclear localization is made from the remaining access limits.

## Nuclear context

PMID:21356043 distinguishes Evc2 from Evc: Evc2, but not Evc, was detected in the mouse nuclear fraction. The authors also report that nuclear Evc2 was not observed by their immunofluorescence or overexpression experiments. The retained nuclear annotation therefore describes a contextual pool; it does not establish a nuclear transport pathway, proteolytic release, DNA binding or transcriptional activity. The human sperm-nucleus source, PMID:21630459, is independently reviewed in the preceding section; its positive target table and access limits are distinct from the mouse fractionation evidence.

## Complex and product boundaries

The EVC–EVC2 assembly contributes to ciliary signaling, but the mapped adaptor connection to IQCE belongs to EFCAB7. In PMID:24582806, purified GST-EVC2 C-terminal tail was tested with wheat-germ translated EFCAB7 or IQCE; this is not an assay using two independently homogeneous proteins. The stable subcomplexes and assay-dependent assemblies do not establish an invariant four-protein stoichiometry or an isolated EVC2 scaffold activity.

The mouse Weyers-related EVC2ΔW construct (residues 1–1176) and engineered motif substitutions are mechanistic reagents. They are distinct from human Q86UK5-1 (1,308 residues), Q86UK5-2 (1,228 residues; loss of residues 1–80) and Q86UK5-3 (1,246 residues; replacement of the canonical C terminus). The second product loses the region containing the canonical predicted signal peptide. Neither identical processing nor functional equivalence is established. Reactome’s modeled chain 27–1308 is not an independent processing experiment on these products.

Current [GO:0060170](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/QuickGO/term/GO:0060170) specifies the membrane around a cilium. [GO:0045880](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/QuickGO/term/GO:0045880) describes positive regulation of Smoothened signaling; its relationship to the pathway is regulatory, not an asserted is_a relationship. [GO:0098797](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/QuickGO/term/GO:0098797) covers the membrane protein-complex context without inventing a named EVC2 complex term.


## Integrated core and remaining molecular question

The core combines positive Smoothened signaling, ciliary membrane location and membrane-complex membership. The operative EVC2-specific evidence is PMID:21356043 Figures 1, 3–4 and 7 with Methods, and PMID:24582806 Figures 1–3 and 6 with its explicit mouse-construct Methods. The 2014 Discussion presents the EVC–EVC2 scaffold mechanism as a possibility; the mapped EFCAB7–IQCE adaptor connection is a different protein's activity. No isolated EVC2 scaffold, adaptor or catalytic function is asserted.

The [genuine human UniProt record Q86UK5](https://www.uniprot.org/uniprotkb/Q86UK5/entry) supplies the product sequence boundaries and curated Ellis–van Creveld/Weyers disease association in the standalone description. That association does not establish a new developmental GO annotation or functional equivalence of the three products. The mouse W-peptide experiments are retained with their construct boundaries, rather than used as direct assays of the human C-terminal splice replacement.

The nuclear evidence remains a contextual annotation supported by independent human sperm-proteomics and mouse fractionation observations. Neither is treated as a second molecular-function core. The suggested experiments address localization and molecular form before proposing a nuclear mechanism.
