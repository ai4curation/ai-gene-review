Gene Description

COL7A1 encodes the alpha1 chain of collagen VII, the principal structural protein of anchoring fibrils at epithelial basement membranes. Three alpha1(VII) chains form a long interrupted triple helix flanked by NC1 and NC2 regions. NC1 binds matrix ligands, including fibrillar collagen I, collagen IV and laminin 332; C-terminal interactions organize antiparallel assemblies that mature into anchoring fibrils linking the basement membrane to underlying connective tissue. Human skin and reconstituted matrix experiments support this mechanical attachment function. Pathogenic variants cause dystrophic epidermolysis bullosa, with fragile dermal–epidermal adhesion. The two annotated splice products differ by a 32-residue segment in the collagenous region; their individual assembly and binding properties have not been comprehensively compared.

COL7A1 supplies the alpha1 chain of collagen VII anchoring fibrils, which join epithelial basement membranes to underlying connective tissue. Human skin preparations and sequence-defined recombinant experiments support one integrated structural function. Structural mechanism and molecular specificity

The 2008 experiments combine authentic human dermal suprastructures with reconstitution using human full-length 2,944-residue collagen VII and mini-collagens.
