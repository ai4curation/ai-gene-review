Gene Description

UQCC6 (BRAWNIN, C12orf73) is a 71-amino-acid microprotein encoded by a small open reading frame and conserved across vertebrates. It is an integral protein of the mitochondrial inner membrane, anchored by a single N-terminal signal-anchor helix with its short N terminus in the matrix and its C-terminal region in the intermembrane space. UQCC6 is a non-catalytic assembly factor for respiratory chain complex III (ubiquinol-cytochrome c reductase, cytochrome bc1). Together with the microprotein UQCC5 (SMIM4) and the cytochrome b translation/assembly factors UQCC1, UQCC2 and UQCC4 (C16orf91) it forms an early assembly intermediate (termed the COMB complex) that binds newly synthesised mitochondrially encoded cytochrome b (MT-CYB) and stabilises it before later subunits join. Loss of the protein in zebrafish or mouse cells causes near-complete loss of assembled complex III dimers with little effect on the other respiratory complexes, leading in zebrafish to lactic acidosis, growth retardation and early death; partial knockdown in human cells has much milder effects. Its expression is highest in cardiac and skeletal muscle and is induced by nutrient stress and AMPK activation, linking complex III biogenesis to cellular energy demand.

Core Functions

Non-catalytic complex III (cytochrome bc1) assembly factor of the mitochondrial inner membrane. As part of the early COMB assembly intermediate with UQCC1, UQCC2, UQCC4 and UQCC5/SMIM4, UQCC6 binds and stabilises newly synthesised mitochondrially encoded cytochrome b before later complex III subunits are added. No molecular function term is assigned because no GO activity term captures this stabilising role and the only available molecular function annotation is bare protein binding.

Suggested Questions for Experts

Q: Should GO add cellular component terms for the mammalian cytochrome b assembly intermediates (COMA, COMB, COMC), so that UQCC6 and UQCC5 can be annotated to COMB membership rather than only to the complex III assembly process?

Suggested experts: Lena Ho, David A Stroud, Peter Rehling

Q: Does UQCC6 have an intrinsic molecular activity (for example a chaperone-like activity toward nascent cytochrome b), or does it act solely as a structural component of the COMB intermediate? Its C terminus faces the intermembrane space, whereas cytochrome b is synthesised at the matrix face.

Q: Why is UQCC6 knockdown in human cells so much milder than knockout, and is this because only small amounts of the protein are needed or because it acts catalytically in successive assembly cycles?

  • Family: InterPro IPR027858 "Protein BRAWNIN"; Pfam PF14990 (DUF4516); PANTHER PTHR28492.
    Vertebrate-conserved; IBA donors include fly (FBgn0259204), mouse and zebrafish, so the

    axon branching downstream of NUAK1. Contextual, not a direct GO annotation candidate

Decisions

  • protein binding (IPI, UQCRC1) → REMOVE (uninformative; interaction with late subunits also
    not reproduced by PMID:34969438). No informative MF term: the protein's activity (stabilising
    nascent CYTB within COMB) has no clean GO MF; follow UQCC2 precedent of no MF.
  • mitochondrion (HTP, IBA) → ACCEPT; mitochondrial inner membrane (IDA x2, IEA) → ACCEPT.
  • complex III assembly (IBA, IEA, IMP, ISS) → ACCEPT (core).
  • complex IV assembly (IDA) → MARK_AS_OVER_ANNOTATED.
  • Comparators: UQCC2 review (genes/human/UQCC2) uses directly_involved_in GO:0034551 + IMM,
    no MF — same pattern applied here. UQCC3 review exists (cardiolipin binding MF).
  • No NEW annotations proposed. Complex III assembly is a process in which UQCC6 does part of the
    work (chaperoning/stabilising nascent CYTB within COMB), so it passes the participation test,
    but it is already annotated.
