Gene Description

CCM2 (malcavernin) is a nonenzymatic intracellular signaling adaptor that coordinates proteins controlling endothelial organization and vascular integrity. Its N-terminal PTB domain binds KRIT1, while a region following that domain binds PDCD10/CCM3; its C-terminal harmonin-homology domain forms a separate helical fold. CCM2 associates with signaling components including MAP3K3/MEKK3 and helps organize their interactions and localization. In human endothelial cells it is required for formation of lumenized tubes. Pathogenic loss of CCM2 causes cerebral cavernous malformations.

The imported normal seed has 34 assertions, four UniProt products and 18 references. All machine assertions, source qualifiers and products are preserved. The proposed decisions are eight ACCEPT, 24 KEEP_AS_NON_CORE, one MODIFY and one UNDECIDED, with no NEW assertion. The completed review remains DRAFT because the validator reports 19 generic-binding advisories. These are acknowledged under the project's instruction to preserve positive interaction evidence as non-core when no supported specific function follows from an individual row.

CCM2 is an intracellular noncatalytic adaptor. The strongest molecular evidence combines PTB-mediated KRIT1 binding and localization effects with a KRIT1–CCM2–MEKK3 ternary complex (PMID:16037064(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16037064/)). The source generic binding row is refined to GO:0035591 signaling adaptor activity, whose definition requires coordination of multiple signaling participants. Direct binding alone would not establish that function; the ternary-complex and localization results supply the additional rationale. The narrower MAP kinase scaffold term is not selected solely because MEKK3 is a partner. No kinase, phosphatase, GTPase or ubiquitin-ligase activity is assigned to CCM2.

The best human process experiment is the CCM2-specific versus scrambled-control shRNA comparison in HUVEC fibrin-bead assays: visible lumens decline while branched cord length, migration, proliferation and size are retained (PMID:19151727(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19151727/)). The original selected Results and Methods were read, not every figure or supplement. Mouse tagged CCM2/KRIT1 constructs used for separate biochemical assays in that paper remain explicitly distinct from its human endothelial experiments. HEG1 recruitment through KRIT1 does not establish direct CCM2–HEG1 contact. The existing endothelial-tube-morphogenesis annotation supplies the core process; broader development terms are not added redundantly.

The CCM3 structural paper contains relevant CCM2 experiments despite its title (PMID:20489202(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20489202/)). Its broad CCM2 51–251 construct includes the region after the narrower PTB fold. In the CCM2 HHD study, full-length CCM2 binds CCM3 while the isolated HHD does not; the relevant linker lies after the PTB domain (PMID:23266514(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23266514/)). Weak crystal packing and solution monomer measurements do not justify a stable physiological homodimer claim. The separate CCM2–KRIT1 structural study supports the PTB interface (PMID:25525273(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25525273/)); the domain name alone does not establish a phosphorylation requirement.

The original PMID:16037064 full text was unavailable. Its abstract draws an analogy involving ICAP1 and integrin interactions; it does not independently settle CCM2 participation in integrin-initiated signaling. That TAS row is UNDECIDED. The abstract-only human genetics paper PMID:14740320 likewise is not relabeled as a direct vasculogenesis experiment; its curator-supported assertion is retained because the biology is independently supported, without accusing the curator of an error on incomplete evidence.

The 19 remaining generic binding rows preserve exact partner accessions. Binary-interaction evidence in PMID:32296183 is distinguished from cell-specific network association in PMID:33961781 and the multimodal resource PMID:40205054. Study-wide methods do not identify every individual edge's physiological role. Target-specific supplementary tables were not inspected. In particular, the phosphotyrosine screen PMID:25814554 does not automatically make every CCM2 edge phosphorylation dependent. The PMID:17657516 abstract's STK25 and FAP-1 enzymatic claims concern CCM3, not CCM2.

The official Human Protein Atlas CCM2 subcellular page reports enhanced mitochondrial localization with HPA020273 and HPA065815. The existing IDA location is retained as non-core, with no respiratory enzyme, obligate mitochondrial function or respiratory-complex membership inferred. Cytoplasm remains the broad core location.
