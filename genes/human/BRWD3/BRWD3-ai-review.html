Gene Description

BRWD3 is a nuclear protein containing eight WD repeats and tandem bromodomains that participates in regulation of RNA polymerase II transcription. Mammalian studies connect its WD-repeat region to DDB1 binding and recruitment of transcriptional machinery to active promoters during early adipogenesis. The bromodomains are consistent with chromatin recognition, while the physiological histone-mark preferences and effector repertoire of the human protein remain incompletely resolved. Depletion of human BRWD3 alters cell morphology, and loss-of-function variants cause X-linked intellectual disability frequently accompanied by macrocephaly. The single Drosophila family homolog regulates histone deposition and KDM5 abundance; the conservation of its particular ubiquitin-ligase substrates among human BRWD3, BRWD1, and PHIP remains uncertain.

Core Functions

BRWD3 participates in nuclear chromatin-associated regulation of RNA polymerase II transcription. In mouse preadipocytes, BRWD3 binds DDB1 through its WD-repeat region and recruits it to immediate-early gene promoters, supporting transcriptional activation. This mammalian mechanism connects chromatin association to recruitment of transcription-regulatory machinery.

OPEN BIOLOGY RESIDUAL_SUBGAP

What would resolve it: Resolve human BRWD3 constructs and endogenous rescue separately from PHIP/BRWD1; directly measure nucleosome recognition and recruitment of DDB1 or a defined ligase-substrate pair.
