Gene Description

AHDC1 encodes Gibbin, a large (1,603-residue), largely intrinsically disordered nuclear protein of metazoans with no catalytic domain and no recognised fold beyond a family signature and a domain of unknown function. In human embryonic stem cells induced to differentiate with retinoic acid and BMP4 it occupies active promoters and enhancers, and it behaves as a chromatin-associated regulator of lineage gene expression rather than as a classical sequence-specific transcription factor: its occupancy is not restricted to the genes whose expression depends on it, and the sequence specificity in the system is supplied by the dozens of zinc-finger transcription factors, the entire GATA family among them, and methyl-CpG-binding proteins with which it associates. Loss of the protein alters roughly 1,100 transcripts, hypermethylates the cis-regulatory elements that would normally lose methylation during lineage commitment, raises DNA-methyltransferase activity, lowers CTCF occupancy and weakens the promoter-enhancer chromatin contacts anchored at the genes it controls, while leaving global chromatin accessibility, compartmentalisation and constitutive heterochromatin essentially untouched. The developmental consequence is a failure of mesoderm maturation: mesodermal progenitors are depleted, the dermal compartment of skin organoids does not mature, and the signalling it would normally send to the overlying epidermis is lost, so keratinocytes stratify poorly even though they are themselves genetically unperturbed. Mouse embryos mosaic for Gibbin mutations show craniofacial defects, open ventral walls and unstratified, poorly attached skin, and heterozygous or homozygous mutants do not survive birth. Two AT-hook motifs are annotated in the sequence, one of them canonical, and disease-associated missense variants cluster in and around the second; the protein has never been purified and no direct DNA-binding measurement on it has been reported. Heterozygous de novo truncating variants cause Xia-Gibbs syndrome, an intellectual-disability syndrome with hypotonia, expressive-language delay, sleep apnoea and variable craniofacial, skeletal and skin features.

The term requires selective binding to a specific DNA sequence motif; nothing in the cited paper measures sequence-specific binding, and the paper's own data and model place the sequence specificity in AHDC1's zinc-finger partners. Reason: GO:0003700 is defined as a transcription regulator activity that modulates transcription of gene sets via selective and non-covalent binding to a specific double-stranded genomic DNA sequence, sometimes referred to as a motif, within a cis-regulatory region. Four things argue against it here. First, the only DNA-side experiment in the cited paper is ChIP-seq of a stably introduced, inducible, tagged transgene; the protein has never been purified and no gel shift, anisotropy, SELEX or de novo motif call appears anywhere in the paper. Second, the authors state outright that occupancy is not restricted to the loci the protein regulates, which is the opposite of what a sequence-specific regulator's binding profile looks like. Third, the paper's own model puts the motif recognition in the partners: enhancer- and promoter-bound Gibbin interacts with dozens of sequence-specific zinc-finger transcription factors, and consistent with this only about ten percent of Gibbin sites overlap GATA3 sites while the two proteins sit at typical promoter-enhancer separations. Fourth, the sequence offers no basis for the stronger claim: the two UniProt-annotated AT-hooks are the only DNA-binding elements, an AT-hook engages the minor groove of AT-rich DNA without sequence specificity, and only one of the two is canonical - KRKRGRPPKNLLL at 544-556 carries the invariant RGRP core, which occurs exactly once in the whole 1603-residue sequence, while RRKAGRGRKADAG at 396-408 has GRGRK and no proline. Neither DNA_BIND feature carries an evidence tag, and InterPro detects no AT-hook signature on this protein at all: the only two entries that match are IPR032757 (a domain of unknown function) and IPR039225 (the family), and neither carries any GO term, so the absence of a GO_REF:0000002 InterPro2GO row on this gene was checked rather than overlooked - there is no domain- or family-level inference here for the manual IDA to be confused with. GO:0003712 transcription coregulator activity is defined as modulating transcription via binding to a DNA-binding transcription factor at a specific genomic locus, either alone or as part of a complex, which is precisely what the interactome and the GATA3 epistasis demonstrate. It is a sibling of GO:0003700 under GO:0140110, so this is a lateral correction within the transcription-regulator branch rather than a demotion to a parent. Chromatin occupancy is not lost by this change; it is recorded separately as GO:0003682 below. For the record, the evidence code on the replacement should be IMP or IPI rather than IDA, since the supporting experiments are a knockout and a proximity-labelling interactome. On the transgene: the methods describe a doxycycline-inducible, HA-tagged Gibbin cDNA delivered on a PiggyBac vector and induced for 24 hours before crosslinking, and the paper nowhere compares the resulting protein level to endogenous AHDC1. So the accurate description is ectopic, epitope-tagged expression from a heterologous promoter at an unmeasured level - not "over-expression", which would be an inference rather than a reported property of the experiment. That limitation is deliberately weighted differently here than on the GO:0003682 row below, and the asymmetry is principled rather than convenient. Ectopic expression from a heterologous promoter bears heavily on WHICH sites are occupied, because a chromatin protein present at a level its own promoter does not set will sample sites the endogenous protein need not - and site specificity is exactly what GO:0003700 asserts. It bears much less on WHETHER the protein associates with chromatin at all, which is all GO:0003682 asserts. So the same experimental limitation is load-bearing against the specificity claim and secondary against the association claim. It is in any case not the load-bearing objection on this row: the four arguments above stand even if the transgene were endogenous, since no purified protein was ever assayed, no motif was called, the occupancy is explicitly unrestricted, and the sequence offers only AT-hooks. A fifth argument belongs here and is recorded on the GO:0003682 row as well, since it bears on both: the Gibbin ChIP-seq alone among the ChIPs in that paper pre-treated cells with 5 percent 1,6-hexanediol for 60 seconds before crosslinking, so the peak set is a filtered one - whatever occupancy depended on the weak multivalent interactions that hexanediol disrupts was removed before anything was crosslinked. Whichever mechanism one attributes to the reagent, a map collected after a disruptive pre-treatment is not a map from which site specificity can be read.

The Gibbin ChIP-seq, alone among the ChIPs in that paper, pre-treated cells with 5 percent 1,6-hexanediol for 60 seconds before crosslinking. Hexanediol disrupts the weak multivalent interactions that hold biomolecular condensates together, so what was crosslinked and sequenced is the hexanediol-resistant fraction of AHDC1 on chromatin. For a protein with ten annotated disordered regions the intent of that step is clear - condensate co-precipitation is a real alternative explanation for apparent chromatin binding by such proteins, and surviving a condensate-disrupting pre-treatment would exclude it. An earlier version of this row took that at face value and counted it as strengthening the binding call. It does not, and the reference that supplies the caveat is the one that refutes it. PMID:33814344 reports "at least 7.5% 1,6-hexanediol is required to dissolve phase-separated GST-CTD" in vitro, and describes a regime "where the agent inhibits kinase activity while condensates are not yet dissolved". The Gibbin ChIP used 5 percent - below the only dissolution threshold that paper reports and inside the range where it finds kinases and phosphatases virtually inactive. Two further details point the same way rather than rescuing the control. That 7.5 percent figure is specific to one construct in a cell-free system, and the same Discussion notes that "the concentration required to dissolve LLPS probably varies with respect to protein and experimental condition", so it is a floor established in vitro rather than a value that can be assumed to transfer. Duration is an independent problem: the Gibbin pre-treatment ran for 60 seconds, while the cellular studies that paper cites used 1.5 percent for 30 minutes and 1 percent for 2 hours. So the pre-treatment cannot be relied on to have dissolved anything, and "survived a condensate-disrupting pre-treatment" may partly mean the condensates were never disrupted. The condensate-control reading is therefore withdrawn rather than merely hedged, and this row does not rest on it. What survives is the other half, which does not depend on the mechanism at all: the map is filtered, because whatever occupancy did depend on the interactions the reagent disrupts was removed before crosslinking, and a pre-treatment that perturbs kinases and phosphatases at this concentration is not inert either way. That is a further reason not to read site specificity off these peaks, alongside the ectopic promoter and the authors' own statement that occupancy is unrestricted, and it is recorded on the GO:0003700 row as well, where those other two arguments live and where it is counted. The binding call rests instead on the conditional occupancy and the endogenous-locus ENCODE replication above, neither of which involves the reagent.

Core Functions

The partner-facing arm: a transcription coregulator that works through sequence-specific DNA-binding transcription factors. AHDC1 is recruited to active promoters and enhancers only after differentiation is triggered, and there it associates with dozens of zinc-finger transcription factors - the whole GATA family among them - and with methyl-CpG-binding proteins. The sequence specificity belongs to those partners: AHDC1's own occupancy is not restricted to the genes it regulates, and its only DNA-binding elements are two AT-hook motifs, which read the minor groove of AT-rich DNA without sequence preference and have never been assayed. Its transcriptional output is large, roughly 1,100 genes in differentiating human embryonic stem cells, and it acts as an effector of GATA3 rather than as a modifier of GATA3 itself, since GATA3's own expression, accessibility and DNA binding are unchanged when AHDC1 is lost. This entry is deliberately confined to the coregulator activity and its immediate transcriptional output; the chromatin-state arm and the developmental outcomes are the second entry.

The two developmental terms are recorded here as the organism-level outcomes of that chromatin role, and their causal distance differs: mesoderm formation is the direct readout, since mesodermal progenitors are depleted in the knockout, whereas skin morphogenesis is reached non-cell-autonomously - the dermis fails to mature, dermal-to-epidermal signalling is lost, and keratinocytes stratify poorly even though they are themselves unperturbed, which is why this review declines to annotate keratinocyte differentiation.

Current model (mechanistic narrative)

AHDC1 encodes a nuclear protein whose loss of function causes Xia-Gibbs syndrome, a syndromic neurodevelopmental disorder marked by hypotonia, intellectual disability, developmental delay, and sleep apnea, arising via haploinsufficiency from de novo truncating mutations PMID:24791903. Wild-type AHDC1 localizes to the nucleus, and the C-terminal portion is required for correct nuclear targeting: patient-derived truncated proteins mislocalize, with short truncations distributing diffusely throughout the nucleus and mid-protein truncations aggregating in the nucleolus PMID:33644933. Beyond its neurodevelopmental role, AHDC1 acts as a regulator of energy metabolism, since Ahdc1-deficient mice develop obesity with reduced energy expenditure, hyperleptinemia, insulin resistance, and fatty liver PMID:37819197. The direct molecular mechanism by which nuclear AHDC1 influences gene regulation, development, or metabolism has not been characterized in the available corpus.

3. What the AT-hooks actually look like

not a DNA-binding measurement. The same paper says only that "AHDC1 likely has a
function in the nucleus mediated by its AT-hook binding motifs", and the Nature paper's
Discussion says "We speculate that this regulation occurs through Gibbin DNA
binding".

16. The hexanediol pre-treatment, which I had missed entirely

Raised in the pass-5 review and confirmed verbatim in the methods:

"For Gibbin ChIP-seq, freshly collected cells were treated with 5% 1,6-hexanediol in 5mL
PBS in suspension for 60 seconds, upon which the solution was immediately diluted with
25mL PBS and 2mL 16% formaldehyde for crosslinking."

Note "For Gibbin ChIP-seq" — this step is applied to that ChIP and not to the GATA3 or
CTCF ChIPs in the same paper. 1,6-hexanediol disrupts the weak multivalent interactions
that hold biomolecular condensates together, so what was crosslinked and sequenced is the
hexanediol-resistant fraction of AHDC1 on chromatin.

The retraction lint could not have worked, and a reviewer proved it

The sharpest finding on this PR, and it is about this script rather than about AHDC1.

A paraphrase of the retracted IntAct claim survived nine review passes in the
reference_review for PMID:32814053 — "IntAct logs its single AHDC1-HTT experiment
(EBI-25827495) twelve times … a twelvefold overstatement of replication" — while the
GO:0005515 row two hundred lines below said the opposite. The correction commit had
rewritten the row and the notes and never touched the references block.

The lint did not catch it, and the reason is structural, not an oversight in one string:

  1. RETRACTED was seeded with the exact wordings the fix had deleted. Such a list can
    only ever confirm that the fix happened. It cannot find the claim restated in
    different words    , which is the failure mode that actually occurs. The patterns now
    anchor on the numbers and the semantic core — single\s+AHDC1-HTT\s+experiment,
    twelve-?fold\s+overstatement — which any paraphrase must also carry.
  2. Matching re.escape(phrase) against raw text made every wrapped claim invisible.
    Everything in the review YAML lives in block scalars and wraps. Matching is now done on
    whitespace-normalised text, which is what REQUIRED already did via its \s+
    patterns. Of the seven phrasings in the previous version, zero could match anything
    in the review file at all.

Proof, not assertion: splicing the historical sentence back into a copy now fails on two
independent patterns, and that splice is committed as the regression_stale_reference_review
self-test guard — the only case here taken from a real defect rather than from a mutation I
invented.

The general lesson: a regression lint seeded from the diff of a fix inherits the fix's
vocabulary, not the claim's meaning. Seed it from what the claim asserts — preferably a
number — and it will still fire after someone rewrites the sentence.

seeded from the diff of this fix exactly as the original four were seeded from the diff
of af3276a2. The proof was in the same commit: my replacement note restates the retracted
reading as "one experiment, twelvefold" and none of the eight patterns matched it.
Added, together with pre-correction / rewritten / earlier version as
RETRACTION_MARKERS — the paired half, since a note may legitimately quote the reading
it replaced, and adding the pattern without the marker would red-flag that quotation.

So the seeding basis is now itself auditable:

replays every past revision of the review and the notes on this branch and classifies each
pattern as HISTORIC (matches text that really existed — fires on a literal revert) or

was removed. All nine surviving patterns are HISTORIC, over 24 replayed revisions.

A lint that guards imagined phrasings while presenting itself as evidence-based is this
file's own failure mode, one level up — so it is now checked by machine rather than claimed
in prose.

Markers only ever widen, so they now have a shrinking force

A reviewer's closing note: every RETRACTION_MARKER is a standing licence to keep a
retracted claim within ±400 characters of it, adding one is always the quickest way to
turn a red run green, and the list only ever widens.

uv run python genes/human/AHDC1/AHDC1-bioinformatics/audit_ahdc1_claims.py --markers

computes a greedy minimal cover — remove a marker only if the audit still passes with
it already removed — and exits non-zero if anything can be dropped. It took the list from
eight markers to three (refuted, superseded, pre-correction).

The obvious version of this test is wrong, and I wrote it that way first. Dropping each
marker in turn and asking whether the audit goes red reports "inert" for markers that are
jointly necessary: two markers covering the same window each test inert while together
being the only cover. Measured — six of eight looked individually inert, and removing all
six turned the audit red on three counts. withdrawn is droppable only because
superseded covers the same windows; drop both and the struck bullet in the notes is
uncovered.

Greedy gives a minimal set, not necessarily the minimum one. That is the honest claim
and it is sufficient: it certifies that nothing can be deleted one at a time from what is
committed.

  • the "no InterPro2GO mapping exists" non-confirmation was in the notes but not in the
    review, so a curator reading only the YAML could not tell the absence had been checked.
    A sentence was added to the GO:0003700 row rather than the requirement lowered.
  • the claim that GO:0003712 is a sibling of GO:0003700 under GO:0140110 — the
    crux of why the correction is lateral rather than a retreat to a parent — was stated in
    exactly one place. It is now in the notes as well.

That third one is the reason this file exists: the check was written to stop a claim
coming back, and what it actually caught was a claim that was barely there.

A guard that did not enforce its own docstring

Worth recording, because it is the failure mode this script exists to catch and it
happened inside the script. The first version of the paired check counted two matches
anywhere in the review file, while its docstring claimed to enforce "stated on both
sides of a comparison". Both statements sitting in the same row would have passed — the
exact case it existed to prevent. A reviewer caught it.

It now parses the YAML, resolves each named term to its row, and requires the pattern in
every row's review.reason, with a missing row treated as an error rather than a skip.
Assert presence; do not validate only on match — otherwise the guard is defeated by
deleting the thing it guards.

The self-test earned its keep: a guard that had been passing by luck

Recorded because it is the one thing a self-test can do that reading cannot. After an
unrelated edit elsewhere in the notes, --self-test went red on retracted_phrasing
while the ordinary run stayed green.

Cause: the retraction-context window reached backwards to the last blank line. In
markdown that is a paragraph; in a YAML file, where blank lines are rare, it can reach
the start of the document — so a retraction marker anywhere above silently excused every
later match. The guard had been firing only because no marker happened to sit above the
mutation point, and my edit put one there.

Fixed with a bounded ±400-character window, which is predictable and cannot swallow the
file. The general form: a scoping heuristic tuned on one file format will behave
differently on another, and an unbounded reach in either direction is the version that
fails silently — it makes the guard more permissive, so nothing goes red.

Caveat on the method, stated because it applies to this file too: a passing self-test
proves the guards I thought of fire. It cannot tell me which guard I failed to write, and
the required-claim patterns encode my framing of each claim — they check that the
sentence is present, not that it is true.
