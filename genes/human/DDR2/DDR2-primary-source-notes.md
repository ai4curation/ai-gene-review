# DDR2 primary-source notes

The collagen-recognition experiments use defined receptor fragments and ligands. In PMID:17703188, Methods, “Sample preparation”, the human DDR2 discoidin domain comprises residues 26–186 and is expressed in Pichia. The collagen preparation is described as **“collagen type II from bovine nasal septum”**. Results, the collagen-binding subsection, and Figure 1 report a monomeric-domain dissociation constant of 2.5 × 10^-5 M, equivalent to 25 micromolar. The source is [the original article](https://doi.org/10.1038/sj.emboj.7601833) (DOI:10.1038/sj.emboj.7601833). The two MINT records IM-11002-1 and IM-11002-2 map the ligand to human P13942 and one curated comment reports 2.5 micromolar. Neither the human partner mapping nor that decimal discrepancy overrides the original bovine collagen-II assay and value. NMR transferred cross-saturation identifies a domain contact surface; it does not assay an intact native human receptor–COL11A2 complex.

PMID:20004161, Methods “Protein Expression Vectors”, Results and Figure 6, distinguishes human DS 26–190, soluble ectodomain 22–398 and full-length wild-type/W52A constructs. The crystal contains a synthetic collagen-like triple helix with norleucine replacing methionine. Figure 6A tests collagen-I binding; Figure 6B tests collagen-triggered phosphotyrosine in HEK293 cells. The latter readout is not a site-by-site assignment of kinase performer. The separate PMID:16186108 abstract distinguishes Src activation-loop phosphorylation from DDR2 cis-autophosphorylation. The [2009 structural original](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.str.2009.10.012) was recovered; the full 1997 PMID:9659899 and 2005 PMID:16186108 originals remained inaccessible. Their available abstracts and curator assertions are retained honestly.

PMID:21423176 is source-specific evidence for focal-adhesion-associated DDR2. The original [NHLBI author-hosted Supplementary Table 1](https://dir.nhlbi.nih.gov/papers/lctm/focaladhesion/supplemental%20table/supplemental%20table%201.htm) contains this literal row, under columns Gene Name, Common Protein Name, Detectable List and Reproducible List:

| Gene Name | Common Protein Name | Detectable List | Reproducible List |
|---|---|---|---|
| DDR2 | Discoidin domain-containing receptor 2 | V | V |

The table reports detection and reproducibility in the HFF1 adhesion proteome. Its DDR2 row is the 173rd zero-based HTML table row, including the title and header rows. [Original Methods and Results](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3279191/) describe hypotonic isolation/trituration of human HFF1 adhesion material and proteomic reproducibility criteria. This establishes detection in the preparation, not direct focal-adhesion structural activity, a specific interaction interface, or absence of associated cytoskeletal material. Failed PMC supplement downloads returned challenge HTML; the actual source used here is the linked author table.

PMID:20564243, Methods “Cell culture” and “DDR2 knockdown”, Results on osteogenic differentiation and mineralization, combines human MG63 marker assays with mouse C2C12/C3H10T1/2/MC3T3-E1 experiments. The mineralization readouts are from mouse cultures; the p38/Runx2 mechanism is not a direct DNA-binding function of DDR2. The publisher-indexed original at [DOI:10.1002/jbmr.159](https://doi.org/10.1002/jbmr.159) was read despite direct HTML/PDF access failures. The preserved abstract-only publication cache is not silently expanded with those original-only details.

PMID:19900459, Methods “Plasmid Constructs” and “Matrix Mineralization”, and Results/Figure 7, tests an engineered mouse DDR2 Met1–Ile400 V5/His ectodomain in MC3T3-E1 cultures. It changes fibrillogenesis and mineral deposition. The [original article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2813395/) explicitly does not establish endogenous DDR2 ectodomain shedding. This experiment therefore informs contextual annotation but does not create a natural soluble human isoform.

PMID:11375938, Results/Figures 4–6 and Methods, uses Ddr2-null mice and mutant fibroblast rescue. Reduced growth-plate proliferation, rather than increased apoptosis, accompanies shortened bones; wild-type receptor rescues fibroblast proliferation whereas kinase-dead receptor does not. [Original article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1083888/). PMID:23409069, Methods and Results on DDR2 suppression, uses rat HSC-T6 cells and an alcoholic-liver-disease rat model; the proliferation, activation and apoptosis effects are retained within those contexts. [Original article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3567027/).

The binding papers are evaluated at the actual assay level. PMID:22939624 uses tagged-cell LUMIER/co-immunoprecipitation; PMID:25036637 supplies the CDC37 LUMIER association; PMID:33961781 supplies the DDR1-bait BioPlex association; PMID:35384245 uses MAC-tagged RTKs, pervanadate, AP-MS/BioID and selected co-immunoprecipitations. The accompanying evidence guide gives literal EBI/IMEx records and partner identities. Partner-class binding refinements do not imply purified direct interfaces, physiological ligand-dependent complexes, or phosphorylation/activation of the partner by DDR2.
