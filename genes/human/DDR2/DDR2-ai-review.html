Gene Description

DDR2 is a single-pass cell-surface receptor tyrosine kinase that recognizes triple-helical collagen through an extracellular discoidin domain. Its cytoplasmic kinase converts collagen recognition into tyrosine phosphorylation and downstream signaling that regulates cellular responses to the extracellular matrix, including matrix remodeling and mesenchymal cell behavior. DDR2 signaling contributes to skeletal growth and tissue remodeling; inherited loss-of-function and activating variants produce distinct skeletal and connective-tissue disorders.

DDR2 biological review notes

DDR2 senses extracellular triple-helical collagen through its discoidin domain and transmits that signal through an intracellular tyrosine kinase. Collagen recognition, Src-dependent activation-loop phosphorylation and subsequent DDR2 autophosphorylation are related but distinct steps. A single collagen-receptor core captures this mechanism; ATP binding and collagen recognition are components rather than independent physiological cores. [PMID:9659899; PMID:16186108; PMID:17703188; PMID:20004161]

The official Q16832 record contains one 855-residue precursor, with a predicted signal peptide at 1–21 and mature chain at 22–855. Extracellular 22–399, transmembrane 400–421 and cytoplasmic 422–855 features describe its architecture. These cleavage/topology coordinates are prediction-supported, not newly measured processing sites. No alternative products were seeded. Isolated discoidin domains, Fc ectodomains, V5/His fragments, kinase-dead constructs and disease variants are experimental states rather than demonstrated natural splice products. The mouse secreted ectodomain experiment does not establish an endogenous human shed receptor. [PMID:17703188; PMID:20004161; PMID:19900459]

The original collagen-II experiment and current human-mapped interaction records differ in ligand species/type and reported affinity. The source objects are preserved, while the original bovine ligand and 25 micromolar value govern the scientific interpretation. Exact source locators and the independently recovered focal-adhesion table are in DDR2 primary-source notes. [PMID:17703188; PMID:21423176]

HSP90AB1, CDC37, DDR1 and NTRK3 associations support binding-class refinements. The target is a client or associated receptor, not thereby a chaperone, neurotrophin receptor, or kinase acting on each partner. C-terminal tags, cellular co-complex capture, proximity labeling and pervanadate stimulation limit claims about native interfaces and physiological activation. [PMID:22939624; PMID:25036637; PMID:33961781; PMID:35384245]

Plasma-membrane localization is central to collagen sensing. The HPA textual record supports that location, with a separate uncertain actin-filament signal; no microscopy images were independently reinterpreted. The actual human HFF1 focal-adhesion proteome contains DDR2 in its reproducible set, supporting a contextual localization without a new structural-adaptor claim. Rat nephron apical staining is retained as a tissue-specific ortholog inference. [PMID:20004161; PMID:21423176; PMID:15218324]

Mouse knockout and rescue experiments connect DDR2 to chondrocyte and fibroblast proliferation, endochondral growth and matrix-dependent migration. They distinguish impaired Matrigel migration from unchanged simple motility. Those outputs, as well as osteogenic differentiation and matrix remodeling, remain contextual consequences of receptor signaling. Human MG63 marker knockdown is distinguished from mouse mineralization/p38 experiments. Activating human disease variants implicate DDR2 in connective-tissue remodeling but did not increase every downstream signaling marker in the examined fibroblasts. [PMID:11375938; PMID:11723120; PMID:20564243; PMID:30449416]

Rat donor annotations describe cardiac fibroblast, vascular smooth-muscle, nephron and hepatic-stellate contexts. Expression responses to stretch, TGF-beta, hypoxia or angiotensin do not make DDR2 the primary sensor for each stimulus. Downstream survival and cell-cycle responses are not constitutive molecular activities.
