RGR (P47804) — Function-Assignment Review: GPCR coupling, phototransduction, and plasma-membrane activity

Focus: function_assignment · Hypothesis slug: g-protein-coupling-phototransduction-and-active-membrane-scope Gene: human RGR (RPE-retinal G protein-coupled receptor), UniProt P47804 · Iteration: 1 of 3

Executive Judgment

Verdict: Over-annotated (refuted for the G-protein/signaling claims; partially/weakly supported only in a narrow, isoform-specific sense for plasma membrane).

The seed hypothesis proposes that human RGR enables GPCR/photoreceptor activity, participates in GPCR signaling and phototransduction, and is active in the plasma membrane. Evaluating each claim separately against primary literature, exact GO scope, and a direct sequence check:

  1. GPCR activity (GO:0004930) / G-protein-coupled photoreceptor activity (GO:0008020) — NOT SUPPORTED. These MF terms require GDP/GTP exchange on a Gα subunit. Thirty years of research have produced no coupled G protein and no positive coupling assay for RGR (39961453). RGR's demonstrated molecular function is light-driven retinaldehyde photoisomerase activity (atRAL→11cRAL) (32493732 37585292 33187985 37057907). A direct scan of P47804 shows it lacks both canonical class-A activation motifs (no DRY/ERY — it has GRY at 112; no NPxxY — it has NAINY at 261), i.e., the structural determinants of canonical G-protein engagement are absent. A comparative scan (Iteration 3; see table below) shows RGR is the only human opsin sampled to have lost both — rhodopsin, red/blue cone opsins, and even peropsin all retain DRY and NPxxY — establishing this as target-specific divergence rather than a shared opsin-family trait. These are IBA terms propagated from the opsin ancestral node (PTN008398221) and represent paralog/ancestral over-propagation, not a target-specific property.

  2. GPCR signaling pathway (GO:0007186) — NOT SUPPORTED for the same reason; no downstream heterotrimeric G-protein cascade has been demonstrated for RGR.

  3. Phototransduction (GO:0007602) — QUALIFIED / non-core. GO:0007602 ("conversion of absorbed photons into a molecular signal") does not itself demand a heterotrimeric G protein. RGR does perform a light-driven molecular event (photoisomerization producing 11-cis-retinal, the chromophore that enables photoreceptor phototransduction). However, RGR acts in the visual/retinoid cycle in RPE and Müller glia, upstream of and physically separate from the photoreceptor phototransduction cascade — it supplies chromophore rather than transducing the light signal that leads to neuronal excitation. Assigning canonical "phototransduction" to RGR conflates chromophore regeneration with signal transduction; a visual-cycle / chromophore-biosynthesis framing is more accurate than GO:0007602 as a core BP.

  4. Plasma membrane (GO:0005886) — WEAKLY / ISOFORM-SPECIFICALLY SUPPORTED, not for the canonical product. Full-length (normal) RGR is ER/microsomal-membrane associated (8639565; RPE microsomes). Plasma-membrane localization is documented only for the human exon-VI-skipping splice isoform RGR-d (27011730), which escapes ER-associated degradation, traffics to the RPE basolateral membrane, and co-localizes with terminal complement complex C5b-9/vitronectin in Bruch's-membrane deposits (an aging/AMD, likely pathological context). No functional signaling activity has been shown at that membrane. This supports a gene-level CC of plasma membrane at most as isoform-specific, and does not justify an is_active_in plasma membrane (active MF at that location).

Most important caveats: (a) Absence of a positive coupling assay plus loss of DRY/NPxxY is strong convergent inference but is not a formal, controlled negative G-protein assay; (b) the non-ocular lead (RGR in human skin keratinocytes, 35445026 / PMC9014095) was read in full (Iteration 2) and does not rescue the signaling claim: the authors themselves call RGR "a photoisomerase," RGR localizes predominantly to nucleus, mitochondria and cytoplasm (immunoEM) rather than a canonical plasma-membrane GPCR pattern, and the only functional data are an siRNA-knockdown phenotype (proliferation/migration/apoptosis) with no G-protein coupling assay. This is a loss-of-function cellular phenotype, not evidence of G-protein activation.

Mechanistic Scope

  • Direct gene-product activity: light absorption by RGR-bound all-trans-retinal (blue/near-UV photopigment) → photoisomerization to 11-cis-retinal (photoisomerase; MF). Also retinaldehyde (all-trans-retinal) binding via a Schiff base.
  • Immediate cellular role: supplies 11-cis-retinal chromophore for opsin regeneration under photopic conditions in RPE and specialized Müller glia (auxiliary/photic visual cycle).
  • Downstream / not direct RGR MF: regeneration of photoreceptor light sensitivity, phototransduction in rods/cones, and the whole-visual-cycle flux are consequences of chromophore supply, not RGR's own signal-transduction activity.
  • Regulatory phenotype (18474598): light-dependent release of RGR's dark inhibition of LRAT and atRE hydrolase — a genuine light-regulated regulatory effect, but no G-protein mediator identified; mechanism explicitly left open.
  • Disease/pleiotropic: RGR mutations → retinitis pigmentosa / peripapillary choroidal atrophy / AMD associations; RGR-d in Bruch's-membrane complement deposits — these are phenotypes, not evidence of a signaling MF.

Conflicts and Alternatives

  • Paralog/ancestral over-propagation: the GPCR/phototransduction terms trace to opsin ancestral node PTN008398221 (IBD). RGR is a retinochrome-type photoisomerase, a functional divergence from signaling opsins; the target descends from the ancestor but has diverged in function (motif loss). Inheritance should not be assumed.
  • Historical naming artifact: "RPE-retinal G protein-coupled receptor" is a homology-based name from the 1990s, repeatedly flagged in reviews as not reflecting a demonstrated G-protein function (39961453).
  • Isoform-specific localization: plasma-membrane data are for RGR-d (exon-VI skip), which the canonical UniProt entry/annotation may not represent; using it to support gene-level PM is_active_in conflates isoforms.
  • Organism caveat: foundational biochemistry (retinal binding, spectra) is bovine RGR (8639565) with human sequence context — not a direct human coupling experiment.
  • Non-ocular lead resolved (Iteration 2): the keratinocyte study (35445026) was read in full. It does not support a G-protein/GPCR signaling capacity — RGR is again described as a photoisomerase, localizes mainly to nucleus/mitochondria/cytoplasm, and the functional readout is an siRNA-knockdown phenotype (proliferation/migration/apoptosis) with no coupling assay. If anything it suggests a non-canonical intracellular role, not plasma-membrane GPCR signaling.
  • Evolutionary divergence (7947717): RGR is the earliest-diverging branch of the vertebrate opsin family with a proposed retinochrome-like mechanism — direct support that opsin-ancestor (PTN008398221) G-protein signaling functions should not be assumed to propagate to RGR (target divergence, not mere donor-count).
