# RGR primary evidence checked during the IBA re-review

This is a reviewer evidence note, not a generated deep-research report. Checked 2026-09-21 UTC.

PMID:27011730 ([primary full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4783578/)):
the DE21 antibody recognizes the exon5/7 splice junction rather than full-length RGR.
Human donor RPE and polarized fetal RPE experiments place RGR-d at the plasma membrane.
The observed junction sequence matches removal of residues211–248 from P47804 sequence
version1, the deletion annotated for P47804-3. Syntaxin-4 provides a membrane comparison;
juxtaposition is not direct protein binding. The study establishes location, not a
specific activity at that membrane. Its suggestion that surface delivery proves proper
folding is not needed for the curation decision.

PMID:31694912 ([author-deposited full article](https://escholarship.org/content/qt2n44z0rz/qt2n44z0rz.pdf)):
the accessible Results and Figure9 legend describe human RGR constructs in HEK293S
GnTI-negative cells. Expression controls accompanied retinal photoisomerization assays.
Canonical RGR produced 11-cis-retinal; the insertion variant had lower activity and the
exon6-deletion variant was comparable to the K255A control. The paper's “variant2” is
the exon6 deletion, corresponding to UniProt isoform3, not UniProt isoform2. Thus,
RGR-d surface localization does not establish retained photoisomerase activity there.
The local publication cache is abstract-only because PMC fetching was rate-limited;
the full-text Results were accessible through indexed author-deposited PDF text.
An independent download/render attempt failed, so the figure pixels were not inspected
and no new quantitative estimate was extracted from the graph.

PMID:37585292 ([primary record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37585292/)):
the abstract and indexed Results/Discussion describe cell-specific mouse reactivation
and electroretinography supporting visual-chromophore recycling. This supplies
physiological ortholog evidence, not a human in-vivo assay. The local cache is
abstract-only. Its identification of a subset of RGR-positive mouse Müller glia also
qualifies the earlier categorical claim that mouse Müller glia do not express RGR.

PMID:18474598 ([primary full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2443657/)):
light-dependent retinoid handling is experimentally supported. The Discussion explicitly
leaves G-protein coupling or another signaling mechanism open. The motif discussion is
not a negative coupling assay. This re-review does not use the motifs as a mechanistic
loss assertion or treat unestablished coupling as exclusion of all photic processes.
