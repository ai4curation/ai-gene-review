| Function/Process | Mechanism/Pathway | Target Tissue/Receptor | Key Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bile-acid feedback regulation | Postprandial bile acids activate ileal **FXR–RXRα**, inducing **FGF19** transcription and secretion. Circulating FGF19 activates **FGFR4–β-Klotho (KLB)** and downstream **FRS2–ERK1/2** and **JNK** signaling to repress **CYP7A1**; SHP-mediated inhibition of **HNF4α/LRH-1** can contribute. | Distal-ileal enterocytes to portal circulation to hepatocyte **FGFR4–KLB** | Human FGF19 is the endocrine counterpart of rodent FGF15. Loss of FGFR4 or KLB prevents effective FGF19-mediated CYP7A1 repression, supporting this gut–liver axis as its primary homeostatic function. (pqac-00000017, pqac-00000019, pqac-00000022) |
| Gallbladder filling and enterohepatic coordination | FGF19 promotes gallbladder smooth-muscle relaxation and refilling after bile release, coordinating bile storage with inhibition of new bile-acid synthesis. | Gallbladder **FGFR4–KLB** | Physiological and experimental evidence identifies gallbladder relaxation as part of the FXR–FGF19 enterohepatic program. (pqac-00000019, pqac-00000021) |
| Hepatic glycogen synthesis | FGF19 activates **ERK–p90RSK**, which phosphorylates and inhibits **GSK3α/β**. This relieves inhibition of glycogen synthase and increases glycogen formation through a pathway distinct from insulin-mediated **PI3K–AKT** signaling. | Primarily liver; hepatic FGFR signaling, especially **FGFR4–KLB** | Acute FGF19 treatment increased hepatic glycogen synthase activity and glycogen content by approximately **30%**. FGF19 also restored glycogen in Fgf15-deficient and insulin-deficient diabetic mice, supporting an insulin-independent mechanism. (pqac-00000025, pqac-00000027) |
| Suppression of hepatic gluconeogenesis | FGF19 promotes dephosphorylation and inactivation of **CREB**, reducing **PGC-1α** induction and transcription of gluconeogenic genes including **G6PC** and **PCK1/PEPCK**. | Liver; principally FGFR4-associated endocrine signaling | Fgf15 deficiency increases gluconeogenic flux after pyruvate or lactate challenge, whereas FGF15/19 signaling reduces it independently of insulin and AKT. (pqac-00000024, pqac-00000025, pqac-00000026) |
| Hepatic lipogenesis and steatosis | FGF19 can activate **STAT3**, reduce **PGC-1β/SREBP-1c** signaling, increase **SHP**, and decrease lipogenic genes including **FASN, SCD1, ACC2, ACSL3**, and **FABP5**. Increased **ACOX1** is consistent with enhanced peroxisomal fatty-acid oxidation. | Liver; mainly **FGFR4–KLB**, with possible indirect **FGFR1–KLB** inter-organ effects | FGF19 or nonmitogenic analogs reduced hepatic triglycerides, free fatty acids, and steatosis in preclinical models. Lipid-oxidation results vary with model, dose, and receptor context. (pqac-00000033, pqac-00000039) |
| Adipose remodeling and energy expenditure | FGF19 can activate adipocyte **FGFR1c–KLB**, increase glucose uptake in vitro, and promote white-adipose browning, mitochondrial and thermogenic programs, lipid oxidation, and energy expenditure. Reduced bile-acid synthesis and lipid absorption may also indirectly reduce adiposity. | White and brown adipose tissue; **FGFR1c–KLB** and context-dependent FGFR signaling | Gain-of-function models show reduced adiposity and body weight with increased energy expenditure; Fgf15 deficiency reduces UCP1 and beige-adipocyte markers. The necessity of brown-fat thermogenesis remains uncertain. (pqac-00000033, pqac-00000035, pqac-00000036) |
| Skeletal-muscle metabolism | FGF19 activates **AMPK, ERK1/2, mTORC1**, and **AMPK–SIRT1–PGC-1α** programs, supporting mitochondrial function and reducing palmitate-induced lipid-droplet accumulation; effects on β-oxidation are context dependent. | Skeletal muscle; FGFR–KLB complexes | Cell and animal studies support effects on muscle mass, mitochondrial biogenesis, oxidative stress, and lipid handling, but these are less firmly established as human physiological functions. (pqac-00000031, pqac-00000035) |
| Central control of appetite and energy balance | Circulating FGF19 can engage central **FGFR1–KLB**, reduce orexigenic **AgRP/NPY** neuronal activity, and increase energy expenditure through corticotropin-releasing-factor and sympathetic pathways. | Central nervous system and hypothalamus; **FGFR1–KLB** | Preclinical evidence links central KLB-dependent signaling to reduced food intake, body weight, and hepatic steatosis; its quantitative contribution in humans remains unresolved. (pqac-00000028, pqac-00000038) |
| Mitogenic and regenerative signaling | Strong FGFR4-complex assembly and dimerization activate **FRS2–GRB2–RAS–RAF–ERK1/2** and **PI3K–AKT**, promoting hepatocyte proliferation and regeneration; chronic excessive signaling can be oncogenic. CYP7A1 repression can persist in variants with reduced receptor-dimerization capacity. | Liver and FGF19-driven tumors; **FGFR4**, enhanced by **KLB** | Structure–function studies distinguish high-threshold mitogenic signaling from lower-threshold metabolic signaling: weakened FGFR, KLB, or heparan-sulfate interactions reduce proliferation while retaining bile-acid and glycemic actions. (pqac-00000002, pqac-00000043, pqac-00000045) |
| Receptor selectivity and signal initiation | Weak extracellular-matrix heparan-sulfate affinity permits endocrine circulation. FGF19 activates **FGFR1c/2c/3c only with KLB** and activates **FGFR4 most effectively with KLB**. Receptor dimerization and tyrosine autophosphorylation initiate **ERK1/2, PI3K–AKT, JNK, STAT3**, and **mTORC1** signaling. | KLB-expressing liver, adipose tissue, muscle, and CNS; **FGFR1c–4** | Tissue-restricted FGFR and KLB co-expression determines responsiveness, helping explain why hepatic FGFR4 primarily controls bile acids while FGFR1c-associated signaling contributes to broader metabolic effects. (pqac-00000004, pqac-00000040, pqac-00000042) |


*Table: This table summarizes the principal and secondary functions of human FGF19, connecting receptor complexes and downstream pathways to tissue-specific biological outputs. It distinguishes the established FXR–FGF19–FGFR4/KLB bile-acid feedback loop from broader, mainly preclinical metabolic and mitogenic effects.*
