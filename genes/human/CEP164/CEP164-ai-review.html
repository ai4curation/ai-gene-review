Gene Description

CEP164 is a coiled-coil and WW-domain protein of mother-centriole distal appendages, extending toward their outer membrane-docking region. Its N-terminal region directly binds TTBK2 and recruits the kinase to the mother centriole, enabling early events in primary-cilium assembly. The C-terminal region targets CEP164 to appendages and binds Rabin8; CEP164 also supports ciliary membrane organization and centrosomal Rab8 accumulation; recent ultrastructure places its requirement after initial small-vesicle docking, in vesicle enlargement. Rab8 association in cell extracts is distinct from the direct Rabin8 interaction. Biallelic CEP164 variants cause a ciliopathy that can include nephronophthisis and retinal degeneration. Reported nuclear localization and DNA-damage-response functions remain contextually disputed.

Human CEP164 (UniProt Q9UPV0): functional annotation and research report

Identity and primary function. CEP164 (also called KIAA1052 or NPHP15) encodes the human centrosomal protein of approximately 164 kDa. The characterized protein has 1,460 amino acids, an N-terminal WW interaction domain and three predicted coiled-coil regions, consistent with the supplied UniProt domain annotations. Its experimentally established primary role is a structural and molecular-adaptor role at the distal appendages of the mature mother centriole: it couples that centriole to membrane-vesicle docking and recruits factors that initiate primary-cilium assembly. It is not known as an enzyme or a transporter, so a catalytic reaction or transported substrate should not be assigned to CEP164. The literature examined concerns this human CEP164 protein, not a similarly named centrosomal protein. (susanne2007cep164anovel pages 3-5, mansour2021theroleof pages 10-12, cajanek2014cep164triggersciliogenesis pages 1-2)

Localization and mechanism

Immunogold electron microscopy places CEP164 on the distal appendages of the mature mother centriole, which becomes the basal body beneath a developing primary cilium. It is not an axonemal protein. In interphase cells it marks the mature rather than the daughter centriole; it remains centriole-associated during mitosis. This location distinguishes it from ninein and CEP170, proteins associated with subdistal appendages. Graser and colleagues also established its functional importance in serum-starved human RPE1 cells: 3.6% of effectively CEP164-depleted cells formed a primary cilium, versus 95% of controls. Graser et al., Journal of Cell Biology, October 2007. (susanne2007cep164anovel pages 7-8, susanne2007cep164anovel pages 2-3, susanne2007cep164anovel pages 5-6, susanne2007cep164anovel pages 3-5)

Two experimentally supported activities explain how this distal-appendage scaffold initiates ciliogenesis:

  1. Membrane capture and organization. CEP164 supports docking of the early ciliary vesicle at the distal mother centriole. Its C-terminal region binds purified RABIN8, the guanine-nucleotide exchange factor for RAB8; cellular assays additionally detect association with RAB8. Importantly, the original purified-protein experiments did not establish direct binding of RAB8 to the isolated CEP164 C-terminal fragment. CEP164 depletion disrupts early vesicle docking and reduces centrosomal NPHP1 and IFT88 without reducing their total cellular amounts; an siRNA-resistant CEP164 construct restores the latter localization defects. These results support a physical link between distal appendages and the RABIN8–RAB8 ciliary-membrane trafficking machinery, rather than a claim that CEP164 itself transports vesicles. Schmidt et al., Journal of Cell Biology, December 2012. (schmidt2012cep164mediatesvesicular pages 5-6, schmidt2012cep164mediatesvesicular pages 7-8, schmidt2012cep164mediatesvesicular pages 10-11, schmidt2012cep164mediatesvesicular pages 8-9)

  2. Recruitment of a ciliogenesis kinase. The CEP164 N-terminal WW-containing region binds the noncatalytic, proline-rich C-terminal region of tau-tubulin kinase 2 (TTBK2). Mutating CEP164 WW-domain residues Y74/Y75 impairs binding and kinase recruitment. When an otherwise CEP164-depleted centriole was supplied with an engineered CEP164-targeted TTBK2 chimera, ciliogenesis could be restored: an especially strong test that localizing TTBK2 is a major CEP164 function. At the mother centriole, TTBK2 promotes removal of the inhibitory CP110–CEP97 cap and recruitment of intraflagellar-transport machinery, permitting ciliary axoneme growth. TTBK2 also phosphorylates CEP164; CEP164 should therefore be annotated as a kinase-binding scaffold and substrate, not as the kinase. Čajánek and Nigg, PNAS, June 2014; Bernatik et al., Molecular Biology of the Cell, May 2020. (bernatik2020phosphorylationofmultiple pages 16-19, cajanek2014cep164triggersciliogenesis pages 3-4, cajanek2014cep164triggersciliogenesis pages 1-2)

The broader assembly pathway places CEP164 downstream of distal-appendage organizers CEP83 and SCLT1. Structural and biochemical analysis refined the interaction model: the CEP164 WW domain sits within an N-terminal α-helical bundle; disease-associated Q11P and R93W substitutions compromise the CEP164–TTBK2 complex. Binding positions the kinase rather than simply activating its catalytic activity. Centrosomal phosphatidylinositol 4-phosphate can bind CEP164 and TTBK2 and weaken their association, indicating a lipid-dependent timing mechanism. These findings describe regulation of cilium formation; they do not make CEP164 a direct receptor for an extracellular signaling ligand. Rosa e Silva et al., Structure, January 2022; Xu et al., Nature Communications, February 2016. (mansour2021theroleof pages 10-12, xu2016phosphatidylinositolphosphatekinase pages 1-2, silva2022molecularmechanismsunderlying pages 1-3)

Pathway interpretation. CEP164 acts upstream of cilium-dependent signaling by establishing the organelle and its membrane/transport interface. Primary cilia are sites of developmental signaling, including Hedgehog signaling, but it would overstate the evidence to describe CEP164 as a Hedgehog enzyme or a dedicated Hedgehog-pathway transducer. Its best-resolved biochemical pathway is distal-appendage assembly → RABIN8-associated membrane docking and CEP164-dependent TTBK2 localization → CP110-cap removal/IFT recruitment → ciliary growth. (cajanek2014cep164triggersciliogenesis pages 1-2, silva2022molecularmechanismsunderlying pages 1-3, schmidt2012cep164mediatesvesicular pages 8-9)

Tissue context and recent research

CEP164's requirement depends on the kind and developmental stage of the cilium. In a mouse conditional-deletion study, loss of Cep164 markedly reduced airway, brain-ependymal and oviduct multicilia. Tracheal-cell experiments implicated small-vesicle recruitment, ciliary-vesicle formation and basal-body docking, including recruitment of CBY1 and FAM92A/B. Unlike the primary-cilium setting, recruitment of IFT components to multicilia was not obligatorily lost. In mouse photoreceptors, deletion before basal-body docking prevented connecting-cilium and outer-segment formation; deletion after cilium formation initially preserved these structures but progressively depleted IFT88, IFT57 and IFT140 from the ciliary base and connecting cilium. These results support a later, tissue-specific role in ciliary transport/maintenance, not just one-time initiation. Siller et al., PLOS Genetics, December 2017; Reed et al., PLOS Genetics, September 2022. (reed2022deletionofcep164 pages 1-2, siller2017conditionalknockoutmice pages 1-2, siller2017conditionalknockoutmice pages 14-17)

2023–2024 developments. A 2024 primary study found that depletion of SMYD3 reduced CEP164 protein abundance and recruitment to the basal body in mouse collecting-duct-derived cells; its CEP164-localization assay examined more than 275 centrioles. The authors also observed reduced Cep164 transcript abundance and SMYD3 association with its promoter. This identifies a candidate upstream regulator but does not replace the direct CEP164 loss-and-rescue evidence defining CEP164's own function. Agborbesong et al., International Journal of Molecular Sciences, May 2024. (agborbesong2024smyd3controlsciliogenesis pages 7-9, agborbesong2024smyd3controlsciliogenesis pages 9-12)

An important 2023 correction to functional interpretation concerns DNA repair: work identifying a centrosomal contribution to homologous recombination implicated CEP170 and subdistal appendages, while explicitly noting that an earlier proposed CEP164 repair role had been disputed. CEP170's experimentally defined repair activity must not be transferred to CEP164 because their symbols and centriole locations are similar. Rodríguez-Real et al., EMBO Reports, September 2023. (rodriguez‐real2023centriolarsubdistalappendages pages 1-2, susanne2007cep164anovel pages 7-8)

Human disease, applications and limits of the evidence

Human genetics establishes CEP164 as a ciliopathy gene. Recessive pathogenic variants are associated with nephronophthisis-related disease, frequently with retinal involvement, and CEP164 is designated NPHP15. The original exome study identified CEP164 variants in four families, including a child with Leber congenital amaurosis and a homozygous stop-loss allele extending the protein by 57 residues; screening of 856 nephronophthisis-related families identified three further families with two affected CEP164 alleles. Genotype and phenotype are variable, so renal disease should not be presumed in every individual presenting with retinal degeneration. CEP164 belongs in the differential diagnosis and molecular-testing assessment of compatible renal–retinal ciliopathy; its experimentally supported role is diagnostic interpretation, not an established drug target. Chaki et al., Cell, August 2012. The Open Targets CEP164 entry independently lists associations with nephronophthisis 15, Senior–Løken syndrome and inherited retinal dystrophy; database associations are corroboration, not a substitute for variant-level clinical interpretation. (OpenTargets Search: -CEP164, chaki2012exomecapturereveals pages 5-6, maria2016geneticandclinical pages 5-6)

Animal studies provide mechanistic disease evidence and one preclinical, not clinical, intervention. Collecting-duct-specific Cep164 deletion in mice reduced the proportion of ciliated collecting-duct cells at postnatal day 7 from 52.95 ± 3.58% in controls to 0.36 ± 0.20%, followed by cystic growth. In these mice, the CDK inhibitor roscovitine reduced kidney enlargement and cortical cyst growth; the reported kidney-weight/body-weight measure fell from 2.76 ± 0.10 with vehicle to 1.79 ± 0.15 with treatment at the assessed time point. Medullary cysts were not comparably inhibited. This is proof of principle in a mouse model, not evidence of an approved or effective treatment for people with CEP164 variants. Airik et al., Kidney International, August 2019. (airik2019roscovitineblockscollecting pages 1-2, airik2019roscovitineblockscollecting pages 2-4, airik2019roscovitineblockscollecting pages 4-6)

Finally, CEP164 has been reported near nuclear DNA-damage-response proteins, and depletion-based studies described altered damage signaling and cell-cycle behavior. The strength of that annotation is lower than for its distal-appendage/ciliogenesis role: a subsequent genome-editing study reported CEP164-null cells with defective ciliation but intact DNA-repair capacity, and the 2023 centrosomal repair study instead pinpointed CEP170. Thus, a direct, indispensable DNA-repair-enzyme function should not be assigned to human CEP164; potential context-specific checkpoint effects remain distinguishable from repair catalysis. Chaki et al., 2012; Daly et al., Journal of Cell Science, May 2016; Rodríguez-Real et al., 2023. (chaki2012exomecapturereveals pages 1-2, devlin2020embryonicandfoetal pages 19-20, rodriguez‐real2023centriolarsubdistalappendages pages 1-2)

Functional annotation in one sentence: Human CEP164 is a mother-centriole distal-appendage scaffold that enables ciliary membrane docking through the RABIN8-associated trafficking machinery and licenses cilium initiation by recruiting TTBK2; its loss causes defective ciliogenesis and can produce inherited renal–retinal ciliopathy. (susanne2007cep164anovel pages 2-3, cajanek2014cep164triggersciliogenesis pages 1-2, schmidt2012cep164mediatesvesicular pages 8-9, chaki2012exomecapturereveals pages 5-6)
