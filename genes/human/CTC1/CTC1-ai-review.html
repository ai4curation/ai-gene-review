Gene Description

CTC1 is the large DNA-binding subunit of the CTC1–STN1–TEN1 (CST) complex. Its oligonucleotide-binding domains contact single-stranded DNA and organize the template with polymerase alpha–primase, supporting initiation of telomeric C-strand synthesis and replication recovery. CST also limits telomerase initiation by sequestering DNA substrate. CTC1 acts in the nucleus, including chromosome ends, and defects in its replication-support functions cause telomere instability. Biallelic pathogenic variants cause cerebroretinal microangiopathy with calcifications and cysts 1 (CRMCC1, Coats plus syndrome). The characterized full-length product contains 1,217 residues; a shorter alternatively spliced product lacks part of the C-terminal DNA-binding and assembly region, and its functional activity has not been established.

CTC1 research notes

CTC1 is a DNA-binding subunit of CST that presents the template and contacts polymerase alpha–primase. The existing annotations already cover ssDNA binding, telomeric DNA binding, CST membership, telomere maintenance and positive replication regulation. One compact core integrates these functions; no additional process assertion is needed merely because depletion disrupts an outcome.

Direct DNA binding and replication support

Human CST structure maps a DNA anchor on CTC1, and contact-patch mutations impair DNA binding while leaving the heterotrimer assembled. The original structural paper distinguishes the STN1-bridged TEN1 association within one heterotrimer from contacts between neighboring complexes in a DNA-induced decamer. Neither observation establishes one obligatory native oligomer size. The source has an R978/R987 mutant-list discrepancy, which is not silently normalized. PMID:32499435, original Results, “ssDNA anchor patch”. The original main text is available in the selected cache; supplementary Methods were not recovered.

Modern experiments strengthen performed participation: CTC1 DNA-binding mutants retain polymerase association but impair template engagement and C-strand synthesis. Recruitment interfaces and an initiation-ready template scaffold are additional mechanistic evidence, while PRIM1 and POLA1 perform the catalytic reactions. Initiation stimulation does not demonstrate the topologically bound moving-polymerase mechanism required by GO:0030337. ROOT independently read the selected Methods/Results/Discussion of these four papers, and that scoped consultation is incorporated with attribution: PMID:35578024(https://doi.org/10.1038/s41594-022-00766-y), PMID:35831508(https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-022-04930-8), PMID:35830881(https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-022-05040-1), and PMID:38838667(https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2024.05.002).
