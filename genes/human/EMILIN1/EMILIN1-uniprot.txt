CC   -!- FUNCTION: Involved in elastic and collagen fibers formation. It is
CC       required for EFEMP2 deposition into the extracellular matrix, and
CC       collagen network assembly and cross-linking via protein-lysine 6-
CC       oxidase/LOX activity (PubMed:36351433). May be responsible for
CC       anchoring smooth muscle cells to elastic fibers, and may be involved in
CC       the processes that regulate vessel assembly. Has cell adhesive
CC       capacity. {ECO:0000269|PubMed:36351433}.
CC   -!- SUBUNIT: Homotrimer associated through a moderately stable interaction
CC       of the C-terminal globular C1q domains, allowing the nucleation of the
CC       triple helix and then a further quaternary assembly to higher-order
CC       polymers via intermolecular disulfide bonds. Interacts with EMILIN2.
CC       Interacts with EFEMP2; this interaction promotes the incorporation of
CC       EFEMP2 into the extracellular matrix (By similarity).
CC       {ECO:0000250|UniProtKB:Q99K41, ECO:0000269|PubMed:19023665}.

CC   -!- DISEASE: Neuronopathy, distal hereditary motor, autosomal dominant 10
CC       (HMND10) [MIM:620080]: A form of distal hereditary motor neuronopathy,
CC       a heterogeneous group of neuromuscular diseases caused by selective
CC       degeneration of motor neurons in the anterior horn of the spinal cord,
CC       without sensory deficit in the posterior horn. HMND10 is characterized
CC       by length-dependent motor neuropathy primarily affecting the lower
CC       limbs, and onset of distal muscle weakness and atrophy in early
CC       childhood resulting in walking difficulties and gait abnormalities.
CC       Some affected individuals have pyramidal signs, including
CC       hyperreflexia. More variable features may include mild intellectual
CC       disability, minor gyration defects on brain imaging, foot deformities,
CC       and connective tissue defects. {ECO:0000269|PubMed:26462740,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:31978608}. Note=The disease is caused by variants
CC       affecting the gene represented in this entry.
CC   -!- DISEASE: Arterial tortuosity-bone fragility syndrome (ATBFS)
CC       [MIM:620908]: An autosomal recessive disorder characterized by
CC       congenital arterial tortuosity, mainly affecting the aorta and main
CC       aortic side branches, aneurysm of the aortic root, and bone fragility
CC       resulting in fractures of the ribs, clavicle, acromion, metatarsal, or
CC       long bones. Tortuosity of the retinal arteries is present in some
CC       patients. Connective tissue features include blue sclerae, mild cutis
CC       laxa with a thin and velvety skin, joint hyperlaxity, and
CC       arachnodactyly. {ECO:0000269|PubMed:36351433}. Note=The disease is
CC       caused by variants affecting the gene represented in this entry.
