CC   -!- FUNCTION: Hormone that acts as a blood lipid regulator by regulating
CC       serum triglyceride levels (PubMed:22569073, PubMed:22809513,
CC       PubMed:23150577). May be involved in the metabolic transition between
CC       fasting and refeeding: required to direct fatty acids to adipose tissue
CC       for storage in the fed state (By similarity).
CC       {ECO:0000250|UniProtKB:Q8R1L8, ECO:0000269|PubMed:22569073,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:22809513, ECO:0000269|PubMed:23150577}.

CC   -!- DISEASE: Type 1 diabetes mellitus (T1D) [MIM:222100]: A multifactorial
CC       disorder of glucose homeostasis that is characterized by susceptibility
CC       to ketoacidosis in the absence of insulin therapy. Clinical features
CC       are polydipsia, polyphagia and polyuria which result from
CC       hyperglycemia-induced osmotic diuresis and secondary thirst. These
CC       derangements result in long-term complications that affect the eyes,
CC       kidneys, nerves, and blood vessels. {ECO:0000305|PubMed:24078058}.
CC       Note=The gene represented in this entry may be involved in disease
CC       pathogenesis. Increased protein levels are observed in the serum of
CC       patients. This result should however be reinvestigated in light of
CC       recent advances that suggest that this protein is not promoting
CC       pancreatic beta cell proliferation. {ECO:0000305|PubMed:24078058}.
CC   -!- DISEASE: Type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2D) [MIM:125853]: A multifactorial
CC       disorder of glucose homeostasis caused by a lack of sensitivity to
CC       insulin. Affected individuals usually have an obese body habitus and
CC       manifestations of a metabolic syndrome characterized by diabetes,
CC       insulin resistance, hypertension and hypertriglyceridemia. The disease
CC       results in long-term complications that affect the eyes, kidneys,
CC       nerves, and blood vessels. {ECO:0000305|PubMed:24852694,
CC       ECO:0000305|PubMed:24963292, ECO:0000305|PubMed:25024395,
CC       ECO:0000305|PubMed:25303484}. Note=The gene represented in this entry
CC       may be involved in disease pathogenesis. Increased protein levels are
CC       observed in the serum of patients and are associated with insulin
CC       resistance (PubMed:24852694, PubMed:24963292, PubMed:25024395,
CC       PubMed:25303484). According to another report, protein levels are
CC       decreased in the serum of patients (PubMed:25050901). Discrepancies
CC       between increased and decreased levels of proteins levels in T2D
CC       patients may be explained by the use of different kits developed on the
CC       market that either use antibodies recognizing the N-terminal or the C-
CC       terminal part of the protein (PubMed:25099942). These results should
CC       however be reinvestigated in light of recent advances that suggest that
CC       this protein is not promoting pancreatic beta cell proliferation.
CC       {ECO:0000305|PubMed:24852694, ECO:0000305|PubMed:24963292,
CC       ECO:0000305|PubMed:25024395, ECO:0000305|PubMed:25050901,
CC       ECO:0000305|PubMed:25099942, ECO:0000305|PubMed:25303484}.
CC   -!- SIMILARITY: Belongs to the ANGPTL8 family. {ECO:0000305}.
CC   -!- CAUTION: Initially reported to specifically promote pancreatic beta
CC       cell proliferation without insulin resistance and to promote beta cell
CC       mass expansion, thereby improving glucose tolerance (PubMed:23623304).
CC       However, this result could not be confirmed by further studies and the
CC       original paper was later retracted (PubMed:28038792). The lack of a
CC       role in beta cell proliferation was also confirmed in another study
CC       (PubMed:25417115). {ECO:0000269|PubMed:25417115,
CC       ECO:0000305|PubMed:23623304, ECO:0000305|PubMed:28038792}.
