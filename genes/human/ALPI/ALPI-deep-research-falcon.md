# Human **ALPI** Functional Annotation Report

## Executive summary

The requested target is correctly identified: **human ALPI encodes intestinal-type alkaline phosphatase (IAP), UniProt P09923**, not tissue-nonspecific alkaline phosphatase (**ALPL/TNAP**), placental alkaline phosphatase, or a nonhuman *alpi* ortholog. The supplied InterPro/Pfam assignments—alkaline-phosphatase catalytic domain, active-site signature, and conserved alkaline-phosphatase core—agree with the biochemical literature. ALPI is therefore not ambiguous in this context.

ALPI’s best-established physiological function is as an **apical intestinal metallo-phosphomonoesterase**. It removes phosphate from microbial and host-derived molecules in the brush-border/luminal compartment. The strongest human mechanistic evidence concerns dephosphorylation of the **lipid A moiety of bacterial lipopolysaccharide (LPS)**, converting strongly TLR4-stimulatory bisphosphorylated LPS toward a much less inflammatory monophosphorylated form. ALPI also hydrolyzes extracellular nucleotides, including ATP, ADP, AMP and UDP, thereby reducing pro-inflammatory purinergic signals and favoring anti-inflammatory adenosine formation. These reactions position ALPI at the interface of intestinal epithelium, diet, microbiota and innate immunity. (balabanova2024insightsintoalkaline pages 4-5, balabanova2024insightsintoalkaline pages 9-11, parlato2018humanalpideficiency pages 1-2, parlato2018humanalpideficiency pages 3-5)

The clearest causal evidence comes from rare human biallelic loss-of-function variants: two unrelated patients developed severe, treatment-refractory inflammatory bowel disease, while their variants impaired ALPI stability, dimerization, catalytic activity, surface expression or GPI anchoring; intestinal staining and stool activity were markedly reduced or absent. This establishes that ALPI-mediated luminal detoxification is biologically nonredundant in at least some humans. (parlato2018humanalpideficiency pages 2-3, parlato2018humanalpideficiency pages 9-10, parlato2018humanalpideficiency pages 5-8)

## 1. Identity verification and nomenclature

**Verification result:** the gene symbol, organism and protein description are mutually consistent.

- **Gene:** *ALPI*
- **Organism:** *Homo sapiens*
- **Protein:** intestinal-type alkaline phosphatase, commonly IAP or intestinal ALP
- **UniProt accession supplied:** P09923
- **Enzyme class:** EC 3.1.3.1, alkaline phosphatase
- **Family/domain:** alkaline-phosphatase family with the conserved catalytic core and active-site signature described in the supplied InterPro/Pfam annotations.

The essential distinction is from **ALPL**, which encodes tissue-nonspecific alkaline phosphatase/TNAP. ALPL is central to extracellular pyrophosphate metabolism and skeletal mineralization; ALPI is principally an intestinal brush-border enzyme whose strongest validated role is control of luminal microbial and nucleotide inflammatory signals. Upregulation of TNAP in ALPI-deficient intestine did not restore luminal ALPI function, supporting functional non-equivalence in this compartment. (kiffermoreira2014catalyticsignatureof pages 6-8, parlato2018humanalpideficiency pages 5-8)

## 2. Protein organization, maturation and localization

Human ALPI is synthesized as a **528-amino-acid precursor** containing an N-terminal signal peptide at approximately residues 1–19, a large extracellular phosphatase domain, and a C-terminal GPI-anchor signal at approximately residues 504–528. Mature enzymatic activity is associated with a homodimer of two GPI-anchored subunits on the apical brush border of intestinal epithelial cells. A patient truncation, p.Glu439Ter/Q439X, removes the GPI-anchor region and produces a null or near-null phenotype, directly supporting the importance of membrane targeting and normal maturation. (parlato2018humanalpideficiency pages 2-3, parlato2018humanalpideficiency pages 3-5)

The principal site of action is the **extracellular apical surface of differentiated enterocytes**, particularly the microvillar brush border, and the immediately adjacent intestinal lumen. ALPI can be released from brush-border microvilli in vesicular material, is measurable in stool, and a fraction may enter the circulation. Thus, ALPI is an ectoenzyme rather than a cytosolic signaling phosphatase. (balabanova2024insightsintoalkaline pages 4-5, parlato2018humanalpideficiency pages 5-8, parlato2018humanalpideficiency pages 1-2)

ALPI is also widely used experimentally as a marker of mature absorptive-enterocyte differentiation. That marker role should not be confused with its molecular function: its primary activity is extracellular phosphate-ester hydrolysis.

## 3. Catalytic reaction and mechanism

### 3.1 General reaction

As an alkaline phosphomonoesterase, ALPI catalyzes:

**phosphate monoester + H₂O → dephosphorylated alcohol/product + inorganic phosphate**

For LPS, the physiologically important reaction is removal of a phosphate from lipid A. Wild-type ALPI converts highly stimulatory bisphosphorylated lipid A toward a less stimulatory monophosphorylated species, substantially reducing TLR4 agonist activity. (parlato2018humanalpideficiency pages 2-3, parlato2018humanalpideficiency pages 1-2, parlato2018humanalpideficiency pages 3-5)

### 3.2 Metalloenzyme properties

ALPI belongs to the conserved alkaline-phosphatase metalloenzyme family. Catalysis depends on the family’s metal-containing active site, including zinc coordination; comparative kinetic and engineered-enzyme studies show that disruption of metal-site stability impairs activity. The catalytic fold and metal dependence are strongly supported by alkaline-phosphatase family structural studies, although some residue-level structural assignments for native human ALPI are inferred from homologous mammalian enzymes and modeling rather than a dedicated high-resolution structure of every ALPI state. (kiffermoreira2014catalyticsignatureof pages 6-8, kiffermoreira2014catalyticsignatureofa pages 6-8)

### 3.3 Substrate specificity

“Alkaline phosphatase” is often described as nonspecific because it hydrolyzes many phosphate monoesters in vitro. Physiologically, however, substrate access, luminal concentration, membrane localization and catalytic efficiency impose meaningful selectivity.

The best-supported ALPI substrates are:

1. **Bacterial LPS/lipid A.** This is the substrate with the strongest direct connection to the human ALPI-deficiency phenotype. ALPI-mediated dephosphorylation lowers TLR4-driven cytokine signaling. (parlato2018humanalpideficiency pages 2-3, parlato2018humanalpideficiency pages 1-2, parlato2018humanalpideficiency pages 3-5)
2. **Extracellular ATP, ADP and AMP.** Sequential dephosphorylation reduces danger-associated purinergic signaling and contributes to formation of anti-inflammatory adenosine. (balabanova2024insightsintoalkaline pages 4-5, balabanova2024insightsintoalkaline pages 9-11)
3. **UDP and other nucleotides.** UDP dephosphorylation suppresses a pro-inflammatory P2Y-receptor ligand; broader biochemical surveys report activity toward purine and pyrimidine nucleotides. (balabanova2024insightsintoalkaline pages 9-11)
4. **PLP and PPi.** Activity is measurable in comparative assays, but these should not be treated as equally established primary intestinal substrates. In particular, PPi hydrolysis is much more clearly central to ALPL/TNAP-mediated mineralization than to the validated human ALPI phenotype. One comparative study ranked tested IAP efficiencies approximately **LPS > PLP > ATP > PPi**, emphasizing that activity depends on the enzyme preparation and assay. (kiffermoreira2014catalyticsignatureofa pages 6-8)

A significant qualification from recent expert analysis is that ALP activity is not uniform across all LPS structures. Commercial preparations may show little activity against fully acylated enteric LPS unless the molecule is first remodeled or deacylated. Claims that ALPI universally “neutralizes endotoxin” are therefore too broad; lipid-A chemotype and accessibility matter. (balabanova2024insightsintoalkaline pages 4-5)

## 4. Biological processes and pathways

### 4.1 LPS–TLR4 innate-immune regulation

ALPI’s most precise pathway assignment is upstream control of **TLR4–NF-κB inflammatory signaling**. By dephosphorylating luminal LPS, ALPI reduces the agonist potency of lipid A before or while microbial material interacts with the epithelium and immune system. A 2024 review estimated that dephosphorylation can reduce LPS toxicity by approximately **100-fold** in relevant experimental settings, while the modified molecule may retain TLR4 binding and behave as a weaker agonist or antagonist. This magnitude is context- and chemotype-dependent rather than a universal constant. (balabanova2024insightsintoalkaline pages 4-5)

Human mutant ALPI proteins failed to suppress LPS-dependent IL-8 responses in functional assays. This links catalytic failure directly to inflammatory signaling rather than merely associating low expression with disease. (parlato2018humanalpideficiency pages 5-8, parlato2018humanalpideficiency pages 3-5)

### 4.2 Purinergic signaling

Cell stress, tissue injury and microbes elevate extracellular nucleotides. ATP, ADP and UDP can signal through P2 receptors and promote inflammation. ALPI removes phosphate groups from these nucleotides; degradation through AMP favors extracellular adenosine, which generally acts through P1 receptors to restrain inflammatory activity. ALPI thus acts as a biochemical switch from nucleotide danger signals toward an adenosine-dominant anti-inflammatory environment. (balabanova2024insightsintoalkaline pages 4-5, balabanova2024insightsintoalkaline pages 9-11)

### 4.3 Epithelial barrier and host–microbiota homeostasis

Through LPS and nucleotide control, ALPI limits inflammatory damage that would otherwise weaken tight-junction and barrier function. Experimental supplementation also changes barrier-protein expression, oxidative-stress markers and microbiota composition. These effects are biologically plausible downstream consequences of its catalytic activity, but not all have been proven to result from one direct ALPI substrate. (balabanova2024insightsintoalkaline pages 4-5)

ALPI also shapes the ecological interface with intestinal microorganisms. Reduced inflammatory nucleotide concentrations may favor commensal growth, while lower LPS signaling reduces inappropriate host responses to the resident microbiota. Human ALPI deficiency, with absent stool activity despite compensatory TNAP expression, provides direct evidence that local luminal activity is important to host–microbiota tolerance. (parlato2018humanalpideficiency pages 9-10, parlato2018humanalpideficiency pages 5-8)

### 4.4 Additional proposed functions

Literature attributes roles in maintenance of duodenal pH/bicarbonate secretion, long-chain fatty-acid absorption, oxidative-stress control and autophagy-associated anti-inflammatory responses to intestinal or exogenously administered alkaline phosphatase. These are credible secondary functions, but the evidence is less ALPI-specific than for LPS and nucleotide dephosphorylation; some studies use bovine IAP or engineered recombinant ALP rather than native human ALPI. (balabanova2024insightsintoalkaline pages 4-5)

## 5. Direct human genetic and experimental evidence

Parlato and colleagues, published **March 2018** in *EMBO Molecular Medicine*, identified four biallelic variants in two unrelated patients: p.Ala97Thr and p.Ala350Val in one patient, and p.Ala360Val and p.Glu439Ter in the other. The patients had severe intestinal inflammation; one presented at age two with diarrhea and weight loss and subsequently developed medically refractory pancolitis, while the other had ileocolonic Crohn’s disease. Both ultimately required intestinal surgery. [DOI URL](https://doi.org/10.15252/emmm.201708483). (parlato2018humanalpideficiency pages 2-3, parlato2018humanalpideficiency pages 1-2, parlato2018humanalpideficiency pages 3-5)

Functional dissection showed that:

- p.Ala97Thr impaired stability and dimerization, reducing cell-surface protein despite preserved transcript abundance.
- p.Glu439Ter removed the GPI-anchor signal and was also subject to substantial transcript/protein loss.
- p.Ala350Val and p.Ala360Val retained only approximately **10–30% of wild-type phosphatase activity** in the reported assays.
- Mutant proteins failed to suppress LPS-induced IL-8 adequately.
- Patient intestinal biopsies showed markedly reduced ALPI staining, and stool ALPI activity was reduced or undetectable. (parlato2018humanalpideficiency pages 2-3, parlato2018humanalpideficiency pages 9-10, parlato2018humanalpideficiency pages 3-5)

This evidence chain—genotype, altered protein biogenesis/activity, defective LPS detoxification, absent local enzyme output and severe intestinal disease—is the strongest basis for annotating ALPI as a nonredundant intestinal innate-defense enzyme. The principal limitation is sample size: only two unrelated affected patients were reported, so penetrance, modifier genes and the full clinical spectrum remain uncertain.

## 6. Regulation and transcript diversity

Human *ALPI* produces at least two reported alternatively spliced transcripts. A 2022 expression study found that one encoded form produced readily detectable phosphatase activity after transfection, whereas another lacked detectable activity and was modeled to lack an N-terminal α-helix and a residue near the active site. The in-vivo abundance and physiological significance of the less active transcript remain unresolved.

Vitamin D has been reported to increase both ALPI splice transcripts in Caco-2 cells, consistent with nutritional regulation, but transformed cell lines do not fully reproduce the mature human intestinal epithelium. Diet, microbial products and enterocyte differentiation also influence intestinal ALP expression, making enzyme abundance responsive to mucosal state rather than constitutive.

## 7. Recent developments, 2023–2024

### 7.1 Mechanistic synthesis

A review published **November 2024** integrated classical LPS detoxification with extracellular nucleotide/adenosine metabolism, barrier protection, oxidative-stress regulation, endocytosis and autophagy. Its expert conclusion was that ALP’s anti-inflammatory activity is probably multi-mechanistic and context-dependent, rather than explained solely by direct LPS dephosphorylation. [DOI URL](https://doi.org/10.3390/biomedicines12112502). (balabanova2024insightsintoalkaline pages 4-5, balabanova2024insightsintoalkaline pages 9-11)

That review also stressed a translationally important limitation: biochemical efficacy varies with LPS chemotype and remodeling state. This helps explain why strong activity in selected experimental models does not necessarily translate into uniform clinical benefit. (balabanova2024insightsintoalkaline pages 4-5)

### 7.2 Preclinical intestinal disease research

A **January 2024** mouse study found that exogenous IAP ameliorated TNBS colitis worsened by high-fat feeding and forced exercise. Treatment reduced inflammatory and oxidative-stress readouts, increased barrier-protein expression, increased Verrucomicrobiota and reduced Clostridia and *Odoribacter*. These findings support microbiota/barrier applications but remain preclinical and do not show that native human ALPI supplementation treats ulcerative colitis.

A **September 2024** mouse study linked reduced colonic *Alpi* to constipation arising in complement-C3 deficiency and reported that acetate-associated restoration of *Alpi* expression improved mucin-related genes and constipation phenotypes. This is a new proposed ALPI-associated process, but it is mechanistically downstream, mouse-specific and much less established than LPS detoxification.

Human stem-cell-derived small-intestinal organoid studies published in 2024 continued to use ALPI as a key enterocyte maturation and nutritional-response marker. Such models are valuable for studying regulation and epithelial differentiation, but changes in ALPI expression alone do not establish a causal pathway.

### 7.3 Human observational statistics

A five-year cohort reported in 2022 followed 574 adults without diabetes, classifying stool IAP deficiency as less than 65 U/g. Type 2 diabetes incidence was **8.0%** with persistently normal IAP, **11.7%** with remittent deficiency, **25.6%** with persistent deficiency and **33.3%** with incident deficiency. Relative to persistently normal activity, incident deficiency had RR **3.69** (95% CI 1.76–7.71), and persistent deficiency RR **3.27** (95% CI 1.64–6.50). These observations are quantitatively notable but do not prove ALPI causality: stool activity may reflect diet, microbiota, enterocyte mass, inflammation or other intestinal phosphatases.

## 8. Applications and clinical translation

### 8.1 Biomarker and model applications

Current real-world or near-real-world uses include:

- **Enterocyte differentiation marker:** ALPI transcript/activity is routinely used to assess maturation of Caco-2 cultures, intestinal organoids and intestine-on-chip models.
- **Luminal enzyme-output biomarker:** Stool intestinal-ALP activity has been studied in IBD and metabolic disease. Isozyme specificity and assay standardization remain important limitations.
- **Monogenic IBD testing:** Biallelic *ALPI* variants are a plausible target in sequencing panels for severe early-onset IBD, particularly where functional testing shows defective LPS detoxification. (parlato2018humanalpideficiency pages 2-3, parlato2018humanalpideficiency pages 9-10)

### 8.2 Therapeutic enzyme studies

Clinical programs have evaluated bovine IAP and recombinant alkaline-phosphatase products in ulcerative colitis, sepsis and sepsis-associated AKI. These products must not be equated with endogenous P09923:

- **NCT00727324:** completed phase 2 bovine intestinal alkaline phosphatase study in moderate-to-severe ulcerative colitis; registry enrollment 22. [ClinicalTrials.gov](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT00727324)
- **NCT02182440:** completed phase 2 recombinant alkaline-phosphatase study in sepsis-associated AKI; registry enrollment 301. [ClinicalTrials.gov](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT02182440)
- **NCT04411472, REVIVAL:** randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled phase 3 recombinant alkaline-phosphatase study in sepsis-associated AKI; registry enrollment 676 and status terminated. Results were reported by Pickkers and colleagues in *Intensive Care Medicine* in **January 2024**. [ClinicalTrials.gov](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04411472). (NCT04411472 chunk 5, NCT04411472 chunk 4)
- **NCT06168799:** phase 2 ilofotase-alfa study for prevention of renal damage after cardiac surgery; registry enrollment 271 and listed as completed. [ClinicalTrials.gov](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06168799)

Ilofotase alfa/recAP is an engineered recombinant alkaline phosphatase, not unmodified native intestinal ALPI. Chimeric designs were developed to retain useful substrate activity while improving stability, zinc binding and catalytic performance. Consequently, systemic AKI trials test the pharmacology of an engineered enzyme platform, not the normal physiological function of brush-border P09923. (kiffermoreira2014catalyticsignatureof pages 6-8)

The phase 3 program’s termination and the 2024 nephrology assessment that efficacy in AKI remains unproven argue against describing alkaline-phosphatase therapy as an established clinical implementation. The most defensible expert position is that the pathway is biologically compelling but therapeutic translation remains experimental, with target compartment, timing, substrate availability and patient heterogeneity likely determining efficacy. (NCT04411472 chunk 5, NCT04411472 chunk 4)

## 9. Evidence grading and unresolved questions

### High-confidence annotations

- Human ALPI is intestinal-type alkaline phosphatase and is distinct from ALPL/TNAP.
- It is a secretory-pathway, GPI-anchored, apical brush-border metalloenzyme functioning as a dimer.
- Its principal validated reaction is extracellular phosphate-monoester hydrolysis.
- LPS lipid-A dephosphorylation and suppression of TLR4 inflammatory activity are central physiological functions.
- Extracellular nucleotide dephosphorylation connects ALPI to purinergic signaling.
- Rare biallelic ALPI loss-of-function variants can cause severe human IBD. (balabanova2024insightsintoalkaline pages 9-11, parlato2018humanalpideficiency pages 2-3, parlato2018humanalpideficiency pages 5-8, parlato2018humanalpideficiency pages 3-5)

### Moderate-confidence annotations

- ALPI helps maintain epithelial barrier integrity and shapes microbiota composition.
- Nutritional and microbial regulation of ALPI contributes to metabolic homeostasis.
- Stool activity may have biomarker value.

### Emerging or uncertain claims

- Autophagy, endocytosis, oxidative metabolism and constipation pathways may contribute to ALPI-associated protection but are not yet as directly established.
- The clinically relevant spectrum of LPS chemotypes susceptible to human ALPI remains incompletely defined.
- Whether oral native/recombinant ALPI can effectively treat common polygenic IBD is unresolved.
- Systemically administered engineered alkaline phosphatases cannot be assumed to reproduce native apical ALPI biology.

## Conclusion

Human ALPI/P09923 is best annotated as an **intestinal apical ectophosphatase that maintains mucosal homeostasis by dephosphorylating pro-inflammatory luminal substrates**, foremost bacterial LPS and extracellular nucleotides. Its location at the GPI-anchored brush border places catalytic activity exactly where enterocytes encounter microbiota-derived lipid A and injury-associated nucleotide signals. The resulting suppression of TLR4 and purinergic inflammatory pathways provides the most coherent explanation for downstream barrier and microbiome effects. Human biallelic loss-of-function disease supplies unusually strong causal validation for this annotation. Recent 2024 work broadens the mechanistic framework and continues translational development, but clinical alkaline-phosphatase products remain investigational and often differ substantially from native ALPI in sequence, formulation, compartment and pharmacology.
