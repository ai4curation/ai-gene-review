Gene Description

DNAJC30 is a mitochondrial inner-membrane member of the DnaJ/HSP40 family that is enriched in neurons, including large cortical pyramidal neurons. It helps maintain respiratory-chain function through turnover and replacement of N-module subunits in assembled complex I. DNAJC30 also binds mitochondrial ATP synthase and contributes to regulation of mitochondrial ATP production, with the contribution varying across cellular contexts. The gene lies in the chromosome 7q11.23 Williams-Beuren syndrome interval; its individual contribution does not explain the full multigene-deletion phenotype. Biallelic pathogenic variants cause autosomal-recessive Leber hereditary optic neuropathy.

DNAJC30 (WBSCR18) research notes

Identity

  • UniProt Q96LL9 (DJC30_HUMAN), 226 aa precursor. HGNC:16410. Synonym WBSCR18 (Williams-Beuren syndrome chromosomal region 18 protein). Located in 7q11.23 WBS critical region.
  • Mitochondrial J-domain (HSP40/DnaJ subfamily C) protein. N-terminal mitochondrial transit peptide (1-38); J domain 49-114; single-pass transmembrane helix 208-225. Inner mitochondrial membrane.

Function (well-supported)

  • Mitochondrial inner-membrane protein; auxiliary component of the ATP synthase machinery; facilitates ATP synthesis.
    [PMID:30318146 "we identify DNAJC30 as an auxiliary component of ATP-synthase machinery"; "the 7q11.23 protein DNAJC30, which interacts with mitochondrial ATP-synthase machinery."]
    [UniProt FUNCTION "Associates with the ATP synthase complex and facilitates ATP synthesis (By similarity)."]
  • Chaperone in complex I (NADH:ubiquinone oxidoreductase) "repair": facilitates the efficient exchange/turnover of complex I N-module subunits damaged by reactive oxygen species, integral to a complex I repair mechanism; contributes to complex I functional efficiency.
    PMID:33465056
    [UniProt FUNCTION "May be a chaperone protein involved in the turnover of the subunits of mitochondrial complex I N-module. It facilitates the degradation of N-module subunits damaged by oxidative stress, and contributes to complex I functional efficiency (PubMed:33465056)."]

Interactions

  • Direct interaction with MT-ATP6 (P00846) and ATP5MC2 (Q06055); associates with ATP synthase complex (PMID:30318146).
  • Large set of binary HuRI interactions (PMID:32296183) with mostly membrane proteins (AQP6, BEST2, EBP, MGST2, MRM1, etc.) — likely membrane-protein Y2H artifacts, uninformative bare protein binding.

Localization

  • Mitochondrion inner membrane (IDA, PMID:30318146); single-pass membrane protein.
  • HTP mitochondrion (PMID:34800366, FlyBase high-throughput).
  • Highly expressed in brain; enriched in pyramidal neurons.

Disease

  • Autosomal-recessive Leber hereditary optic neuropathy (LHONAR1/arLHON, MIM:619382): biallelic DNAJC30 mutations (e.g. Y51C, P78S, L101Q) recapitulate all hallmarks of mtDNA LHON (incomplete penetrance, male predominance, idebenone responsivity). Variants reduce turnover of complex I N-module subunits and impair complex I function.
    PMID:33465056
  • DNAJC30 deletion contributes to mitochondrial dysfunction in Williams-Beuren syndrome neurodevelopmental phenotypes (PMID:30318146).

  • mitochondrial inner membrane (IDA, IEA) — ACCEPT (core localization, experimentally demonstrated).
  • mitochondrion (HTP) — ACCEPT (consistent, less specific).

Best MF terms: protein-folding chaperone binding / unfolded protein binding not directly shown; the gene acts as a holdase/chaperone facilitating subunit exchange. The clearest experimentally-grounded MF for the ATP-synthase role is enzyme/structural association; for complex I it's chaperone activity. Use GO:0051082 unfolded protein binding? Not demonstrated. AIGR Review Snapshot

  • Description: DNAJC30 (WBSCR18) is a mitochondrial inner-membrane DnaJ/HSP40 (J-domain) protein encoded in the 7q11.23 Williams-Beuren syndrome critical region. Imported via an N-terminal transit peptide and anchored by a single transmembrane helix, it has two intertwined roles in oxidative phosphorylation. It is an auxiliary component of the ATP synthase machinery, interacting directly with MT-ATP6 and ATP5MC2 and facilitating ATP synthesis. It also acts as a chaperone in a mitochondrial complex I (NADH:ubiquinone oxidoreductase) repair mechanism, promoting the efficient exchange/turnover of N-module subunits damaged by reactive oxygen species and thereby maintaining complex I efficiency. DNAJC30 is highly expressed in brain (pyramidal neurons). Biallelic loss-of-function variants cause autosomal-recessive Leber hereditary optic neuropathy (LHONAR1), and hemizygous loss contributes to the mitochondrial dysfunction underlying neurodevelopmental features of Williams-Beuren syndrome.
  • Existing/core annotation action counts: ACCEPT: 5; KEEP_AS_NON_CORE: 3; MODIFY: 1

DNAJC30 evidence guide

DNAJC30 has two supported mitochondrial roles: participation in repair of assembled complex I and association with ATP synthase. The repair mechanism remains unresolved at the biochemical-step level. These roles do not establish intrinsic ATPase activity, a stable respiratory-complex subunit or universal ATP-synthase dimer stabilization.

Primary evidence and assay scope

PMID:30318146, complete primary article — Results on ATP-synthase interaction and the corresponding immunoprecipitation, yeast two-hybrid, localization and mitochondrial-function Methods distinguish the following experiments:

  • Full human DNAJC30 bait selected MT-ATP6 and ATP5G2 (now ATP5MC2) in an adult human brain library. ATP-synthase F1 immunoprecipitation from adult human superior temporal cortex mitochondria recovered DNAJC30 and Fo/F1 subunits, with an IgG control.
  • The tagged MT-ATP6 co-immunoprecipitation used a codon-optimized nuclear-expression construct with a COXVIII targeting sequence and a C-terminal tag. It is distinct from native complex precipitation.
  • Inner-membrane immunogold used macaque dorsolateral frontal cortex. Human brain and cell observations provide complementary mitochondrial evidence; mouse knockout neurons/cortical mitochondria supplied respiration and ATP-production phenotypes.
  • ATP-synthase dimer/supercomplex abnormalities in multigene Williams-Beuren samples were not reproduced as that pattern in isolated Dnajc30 knockout. Those samples do not establish a DNAJC30-specific dimer-maintenance function.

The MINT records IM-29041 resolve MT-ATP6 two-hybrid (IM-29041-1; EBI-27023768), MT-ATP6 anti-tag co-immunoprecipitation (IM-29041-13; EBI-27023813), and ATP5MC2 two-hybrid (IM-29041-7; EBI-27023780). IM-29041-15/EBI-27023834 is confocal colocalization and is not counted as a binary binding assay. Target and partner sequence/taxon identifiers match the official products. The native-complex and subunit assays together support GO:0140260, mitochondrial proton-transporting ATP synthase complex binding.

PMID:33465056, complete primary article — Results on complex-I repair, complexome profiles and pulse-SILAC turnover, together with the blue-native/MS and cell-culture Methods, establish selective N-module turnover defects in patient fibroblasts and knockout HEK293 cells. Assembled complex I remains present without the expected assembly intermediates. DNAJC30 co-migration at low abundance is consistent with transient association; highlighted N-module interaction links originate in BioPlex. Selective turnover plus physical association supports participation in remodeling an existing complex. Direct extraction of damaged subunits, delivery to CLPXP or HSP70 activation was not reconstituted. The same study found no complex-V activity, abundance or turnover defect in its tested human systems; this limits a universal regulatory mechanism while preserving the earlier binding evidence.

Both complete primary articles were recovered and read separately from the normal publication caches. The cached 2018 representation is incomplete despite its availability metadata; that metadata and all original cache bytes remain unchanged. Source-specific claims above follow the original Results/Methods, not an assumption that every experiment occurs in the cache.
