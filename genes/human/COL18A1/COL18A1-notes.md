## Structural core and processed-product boundaries

The single molecular core is the existing extracellular-matrix structural activity conferring tensile strength, in the collagen-XVIII homotrimer at basement membranes. The official term explicitly includes ECM collagens; nonfibrillar collagen XVIII is not thereby excluded. Same-role collagen XV carries the term and network-forming collagen IV has experimental support. Direct human isolated-domain structure and folding demonstrate a trimerization module; the full-NC1 ultracentrifugation assay is mouse. Mouse matrix/retinal experiments connect collagen XVIII to basement-membrane thickness and vitreoretinal anchoring. No isolated whole-human force measurement is claimed. [PMID:19631658; PMID:11927538; PMID:15254016; PMID:21421911]

Human oligomeric NC1 and engineered endostatin dimers promote matrix-dependent motility/morphogenesis, whereas monomeric preparations differ and can antagonize the oligomers. Downstream MAPK/Rac/Cdc42 dependence does not establish that collagen is a receptor, kinase or adaptor. Original inhibitory endostatin preparations, including nonrefolded bacterial material, are positive evidence, while purified native circulating human endostatin lacked the tested antiproliferative effect. These are preparation-specific results, not uniform fragment activities or blanket contradiction. [PMID:11257123; PMID:9008168; PMID:9459295]

The broad angiogenesis/morphogenesis annotations remain non-core. Mouse developmental endostatin assays can show positive proliferation and tube formation, so assigning a universal inhibitory sign would be misleading. No NEW annotation is proposed. A substrate of proteolytic processing is not the enzyme performing that processing, and no precise new molecular activity is inferred from necessity or downstream signaling alone. The complete selected GO-CAM index has no target match; this is not proof that a missing activity should be added. [PMID:15188432; PMID:10626789]
