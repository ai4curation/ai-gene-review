Gene Description

TERF2 (TRF2) is a core subunit of shelterin, the six-protein complex that coats the duplex TTAGGG repeats of mammalian telomeres. A TRFH-domain homodimer, it binds double-stranded telomeric DNA through a C-terminal Myb/homeodomain, and its N-terminal basic domain engages branched DNA. TRF2 remodels telomeres into t-loops, in which the 3' G-strand overhang invades the duplex repeat array, and it is the shelterin subunit chiefly responsible for hiding chromosome ends from the ATM/MRN DNA damage response and from classical non-homologous end joining; loss of TRF2 causes ligase IV-dependent end-to-end fusions, while loss of its basic domain causes XRCC3-dependent excision of t-loops as telomeric circles. Beyond DNA binding, TRF2 acts as a protein docking hub: its TRFH domain binds short Y/F/H-x-L-x-P motifs in Apollo/SNM1B, dephosphorylated NBS1, SLX4, PNUTS and MCPH1, and other regions bind RAP1, TIN2 and RTEL1, recruiting these factors to telomeres to process leading-end overhangs, relieve topological stress during replication and open t-loops in S phase. TRF2 dosage also influences telomere length by telomerase-independent, XPF-dependent shortening.

Core Functions

Binds duplex TTAGGG telomeric repeats as a TRFH-domain homodimer within shelterin, and remodels telomere ends into t-loops. This DNA-bound architecture caps chromosome ends: it represses ATM/MRN signalling and classical NHEJ (preventing end fusions) and, through the basic domain, prevents homologous recombination from excising t-loops.

Suggested Questions for Experts

Q: Should the TRF2 docking-hub function be modelled in GO-CAM as TRF2 chromatin-protein adaptor activity with has_input NBN/Apollo (rather than NBN adaptor activity with has_input TERF2, as in gomodel:65a1f4f800002884)?

Q: Does human TERF2 produce a neuronal short isoform equivalent to rat TRF2-S with RNA-binding and axonal mRNA transport activity?

Q: Is TRF2 repression of ATM at telomeres mediated mainly by t-loop formation, by direct inhibition of ATM S1981 autophosphorylation, or by sequestering dephosphorylated NBS1, and do these contributions differ by cell-cycle phase?

Suggested Experiments

Experiment: Long-read RNA-seq and isoform-specific immunoblotting in human iPSC-derived cortical neurons, followed by RIP-seq and compartmentalized-culture axonal RNA quantification after isoform-specific knockdown.

Hypothesis: A human neuronal TERF2 isoform lacking the Myb domain exists and transports G-rich mRNAs into axons, as rat TRF2-S does.

Experiment: Express TRF2 TRFH mutants that lose NBS1 but retain Apollo and RTEL1 binding in TRF2-null cells, and measure telomeric ATM activation, TIFs and fusions, combined with super-resolution measurement of t-loop frequency.

Hypothesis: TRFH-domain docking of dephosphorylated NBS1, independent of t-loops, is sufficient to suppress ATM activation at telomeres.

Key findings

  • Duplex TTAGGG binding via C-terminal Myb/homeodomain, as a TRFH-domain homodimer
    PMID:9326950.
  • End protection: dominant-negative TRF2 causes end-to-end fusions
    PMID:9476899.
  • NHEJ repression requires TRF2, and ERCC1/XPF removes the overhang in its absence
    PMID:14690602.
  • T-loop formation PMID:10338214.
  • Basic domain represses HR-mediated t-loop excision PMID:15507207.
  • TRFH docking hub: YxLxP/[Y/F]xL motifs of Apollo, NBS1, SLX4, PNUTS, MCPH1
    PMID:19287395;
    RTEL1 recruitment in S phase PMID:25620558.
  • ATM/MRN repression and NBS1 docking PMID:28216226.
  • Length regulation is telomerase-independent in the targeting assay PMID:11971978.
  • Telomerase co-IP in the HOT1 paper is attributed to TPP1 PMID:23685356 -> contributes_to telomerase activity removed.
  • Neuronal TRF2-S (rat) lacks the DNA-binding domain and transports axonal mRNAs PMID:26586091; not shown for human, so axon / axonal mRNP transport IEA rows marked over-annotated.

Curation decisions to remember

  • GO:0140463 chromatin-protein adaptor activity chosen as the docking-hub MF (TRF2 on telomeric
    chromatin recruits Apollo, RAP1, SLX4, RTEL1). PMID:19287395 calls TRF2 a protein hub, and PMID:28216226
    shows NBS1, Apollo and PNUTS competing for the same TRFH surface, so the adaptor role is placed on TRF2;
    gomodel:65a1f4f800002884 instead draws NBN adaptor activity with has_input TERF2 (see suggested_questions).
  • Positive regulation of telomere maintenance and negative regulation via telomerase marked over-annotated.

description: 'TERF2 (TRF2) is a core subunit of shelterin, the six-protein complex that coats the duplex
  TTAGGG repeats of mammalian telomeres. A TRFH-domain homodimer, it binds double-stranded telomeric DNA
  through a C-terminal Myb/homeodomain, and its N-terminal basic domain engages branched DNA. TRF2 remodels
  telomeres into t-loops, in which the 3'' G-strand overhang invades the duplex repeat array, and it is
  the shelterin subunit chiefly responsible for hiding chromosome ends from the ATM/MRN DNA damage response
  and from classical non-homologous end joining; loss of TRF2 causes ligase IV-dependent end-to-end fusions,
  while loss of its basic domain causes XRCC3-dependent excision of t-loops as telomeric circles. Beyond
  DNA binding, TRF2 acts as a protein docking hub: its TRFH domain binds short Y/F/H-x-L-x-P motifs in
  Apollo/SNM1B, dephosphorylated NBS1, SLX4, PNUTS and MCPH1, and other regions bind RAP1, TIN2 and RTEL1,
  recruiting these factors to telomeres to process leading-end overhangs, relieve topological stress during
  replication and open t-loops in S phase. TRF2 dosage also influences telomere length by telomerase-independent,
  XPF-dependent shortening.'
