# CDC73 / parafibromin

Parafibromin participates in the nuclear PAF1 transcription machinery. Its central role is to connect transcription with the factors that handle the emerging RNA. Human purification and reconstitution establish complex membership and association with RNA polymerase II; they do not isolate a purified human CDC73–PolII interface. The early primary report describes parafibromin as “part of a human Paf1 complex” ([PMID:15632063](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15632063/)). In the later reconstitution study, PAF1 and LEO1 provide the strongest measured PolII contacts; yeast CDC73 results are distinct from human subunit experiments ([PMID:20178742](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20178742/)).

CDC73 associates with CPSF/CstF and promotes their recruitment at a tested endogenous locus. Immunodepletion also removes processing factors, so that experiment alone cannot identify CDC73 as a core cleavage factor ([PMID:19136632](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19136632/)). The follow-up distinguishes transcription-coupled processing from an uncoupled RNA-substrate assay; direct VP16 binding belongs to PAF1 ([PMID:21329879](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21329879/)). The core therefore records binding and positive regulation of elongation/processing, without assigning RNA cleavage, poly(A) polymerase, ubiquitin-ligase or histone-methyltransferase activity to CDC73. No NEW annotation was needed.

Beta-catenin binding supports a more informative refinement of its corresponding generic interaction ([PMID:16630820](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16630820/)). KMT2A binding is likewise refined using the enzyme partner’s identity and the inspected interaction experiments; KMT2A catalysis remains distinct from CDC73 binding ([PMID:20541477](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20541477/)). PAF1C effects on myeloid differentiation include complex overexpression in human THP-1 cells, rather than a demonstrated isolated-subunit developmental mechanism.

Growth effects depend on context. The original G1/S study reports increased S phase after CDC73 depletion, so its positive-regulation source annotation is refined to negative regulation ([PMID:15923622](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15923622/)). MYC promoter and proliferation assays support secondary regulatory effects in HeLa and primary human dermal fibroblasts; the CDC73-knockdown MYC half-life increase is a nonsignificant trend, and depletion can also reduce other PAF1C components ([PMID:18987311](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18987311/)). Mouse stem-cell observations remain explicitly orthology-based. The LPS-transfer assertions are marked over-annotated after separating expression induction from functional participation ([PMID:19345177](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19345177/), [PMID:17911113](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17911113/)). The precise endodermal-fate experiment remains unresolved.

RTF1 association depends on preparation and assay. PMID:16024656 reports substoichiometric coimmunoprecipitation despite its absence from the stable purified core; PMID:20178742 supplies later complex evidence and documents partial dissociation during gel filtration. These source-specific results are distinguished below. The exact telomeric PICh target entry and unqualified SHP2 source assignment remain open. The exact Q06124-2 association is retained only at the demonstrated substrate-trapping mutant scope. Negative recovery in one assay does not prove absence of a transient physiological interaction.
