Gene Description

CHN1 encodes alpha-chimaerin Rac GTPase-activating proteins that promote GTP hydrolysis on Rac and restrain Rac-dependent cytoskeletal signaling. The alpha2 product contains an N-terminal SH2 domain in addition to a lipid-responsive C1 region and a C-terminal GAP domain; alpha1 has a different N terminus. Alpha2 associates with Eph receptors and couples guidance cues to spatially regulated Rac signaling in developing axons. Mouse and chick studies establish roles in growth-cone responses and motor-axon trajectory selection, while human dominant gain-of-function variants cause Duane retraction syndrome and strengthen Rac inactivation. Cytosolic alpha2 can redistribute toward membranes after stimulation. In human alpha2, C1-dependent diacylglycerol recognition helps control membrane recruitment, while intramolecular contacts restrain ligand access.

Core Functions

Alpha-chimaerin accelerates GTP hydrolysis on Rac, providing a regulated signaling step that couples guidance-receptor input to axonal cytoskeletal responses. Original purified human alpha2 experiments establish GAP activity; Rho and CDC42 were negative only under the tested assay conditions. Mouse/chick receptor association and GAP/SH2 perturbations establish performed regulation of axon extension and EphA-linked motor-axon guidance. Human-cell Rac-GTP measurements corroborate activity, while WT and mutant overexpression effects are dosage-sensitive. The older constructs were not sequence-aligned to all current products; receptor binding and alpha2 developmental assays do not establish universal all-product function. Human alpha2 C1-dependent diacylglycerol recognition helps position the GAP at membranes; intramolecular autoinhibition and additional inputs make ligand binding distinct from sufficient GAP activation.
