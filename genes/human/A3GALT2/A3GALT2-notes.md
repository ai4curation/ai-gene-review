# A3GALT2 review notes

## 2026-08-08 — evidence synthesis

A3GALT2 is a difficult human-function case rather than a straightforward active enzyme or a straightforward pseudogene. UniProt U3KPV4 is reviewed but remains at protein-evidence level 2 (transcript evidence). It predicts a type-II membrane topology (cytoplasmic residues 1–12, transmembrane signal anchor 13–32, lumenal catalytic region 33–340) and assigns Golgi localization only by similarity to rat A3galt2. The sequence retains a GT6-family domain and the predicted DXD metal-binding motif, but those features establish structural capacity, not catalytic activity [file:human/A3GALT2/A3GALT2-uniprot.txt, "PE   2: Evidence at transcript level;"] [file:human/A3GALT2/A3GALT2-uniprot.txt, "FT   TRANSMEM        13..32"] [file:human/A3GALT2/A3GALT2-uniprot.txt, "FT                   /note=\"Helical; Signal-anchor for type II membrane protein\""]

The 2008 primary study could not detect correctly spliced A3GALT2/iGb3S RNA in the adult human tissues and dendritic-cell samples it tested. It then expressed rat/human chimeras containing the human catalytic domain; the constructs were translated and showed perinuclear, Golgi-like trafficking, but did not generate detectable Gal-alpha(1,3)-Gal or downstream isoForssman product. Mutating rat Tyr252 to the human-equivalent Asn abolished rat activity, while reverting two human-like residues in the chimera did not rescue it, indicating that several substitutions may impair the human catalytic domain. The authors concluded: "Collectively, our data suggest that iGb3S is not expressed in humans, and even if it were expressed, this enzyme would be inactive." [PMID:18630988, "Despite using numerous primer combinations, including forward primers from exons 2, 3, or 4 with a reverse primer from exon 5 (Figure 2C), we were unable to detect any products of the correct size to suggest spliced iGb3S mRNA in any human tissue examined"] [PMID:18630988, "Detection of the FLAG epitope in both chimeric enzymes confirmed that the absence of Galα(1,3)Gal synthesis was not due to impaired translation or expression"]

The 2013 study is genuine contrary evidence and prevents accepting that absolute negation without qualification. It assembled a full-length human ORF from pediatric-thymus cDNA fragments (with exon 1 and part of exon 2 introduced by a long PCR primer), expressed it in CHO cells, and detected iGb3 and iGb4 by linear ion-trap mass spectrometry. The authors explicitly characterized the activity as weak: "However, the human iGb3 synthase showed very weak activity even in the CHO cell transfection experiment and could not further elongate iGb3 to B4, B5, and B6 GSLs (Table 1)." The same study detected iGb3/iGb4 signatures in monocyte-derived dendritic-cell lipids from five donors, but did not causally connect those endogenous lipids to A3GALT2 protein [PMID:23378701, "The full-length human iGb3 synthase gene was transfected into Chinese hamster ovary (CHO) cells by a pIRES2-EGFP plasmid"] [PMID:23378701, "Our results showed that human iGb3 synthase initiated the synthesis of both iGb3 (Fig. 6A) and iGb4 (Fig. 6B), as measured by LIT-MSn technology."] [PMID:23378701, "However, the human iGb3 synthase showed very weak activity even in the CHO cell transfection experiment and could not further elongate iGb3 to B4, B5, and B6 GSLs (Table 1)."]

Earlier mass spectrometry detected iGb4 in human thymus, but this is product-presence evidence and does not identify the responsible enzyme [PMID:18056651, "report the identification of isoglobotetraglycosylceramide in a CHO cell line transfected by iGb3 synthase, as well as in human thymus."]. A later human-podocyte study found A3GALT2 expression undetectable in that specific immortalized cell model; it cannot establish organism-wide absence [PMID:32915357, "gene expression level of A3GALT2 was undetectable in whole-transcriptome analysis generated in human immortalized podocytes"]. A human-knockout cohort reported two homozygotes for a predicted truncating allele with low fasting insulin and C-peptide, but expressly stated that "the biological role of this enzyme in humans is uncertain" and did not establish an A3GALT2 mechanism [PMID:28406212, "the biological role of this enzyme in humans is uncertain"]. PMID:30696937 does not test human A3GALT2; its iGb3-synthase constructs are rat proteins and therefore cannot resolve the human controversy.

## GO implications

QuickGO was checked directly on 2026-08-08. GO:0001962 is current and defined broadly as alpha-1,3-galactosyltransferase activity; it explicitly carries "isoglobotriaosylceramide synthase" as a narrow synonym. The negated IDA row from PMID:18630988 should therefore be `UNDECIDED`, because full-text direct evidence now exists on both sides and the later positive experiment is weak but real.

GO:0047276 is also current, but its defined acceptor is beta-D-Gal-(1,4)-beta-D-GlcNAc-R (N-acetyllactosaminide; RHEA:13013/EC 2.4.1.87). The human overexpression study instead tested formation of iGb3 from lactosylceramide, beta-D-Gal-(1,4)-D-Glc-Cer. Thus the GO:0047276 rows are not supported by the human evidence and appear to derive from an inappropriate EC/Rhea or ortholog transfer. The broader GO:0001962 is the appropriate ontology concept for the disputed activity; no positive `NEW` row should be added while endogenous function remains unresolved [QuickGO GO:0047276, "Catalysis of the reaction: beta-D-galactosyl-(1,4)-beta-N-acetyl-D-glucosaminyl-R + UDP-galactose = alpha-D-galactosyl-(1,3)-beta-D-galactosyl-(1,4)-beta-N-acetyl-D-glucosaminyl-R + UDP."] [QuickGO GO:0001962, "Catalysis of the transfer of a galactose residue from a donor molecule, such as GDP-galactose or UDP-galactose, to an oligosaccharide, forming an alpha-(1->3) linkage."]

The defensible standalone synthesis is: A3GALT2 encodes a predicted GT6-family type-II membrane protein whose human iGb3-synthase activity is disputed and, where detected in overexpression, very weak. Endogenous protein expression, physiological catalytic flux, and human subcellular localization have not been established. Keep `core_functions` empty rather than converting a contested overexpression capacity into a positive core function.

## 2026-08-08 — PR review follow-up

PR review correctly noted that the initial GO:0047276 removal reasons did not explicitly rebut two contrary statements already preserved in the review. UniProt assigns RHEA:13013/EC 2.4.1.87 to U3KPV4 only by similarity to rat A3galt2, while PMID:28406212 repeats the Gal-alpha(1,3)-Gal-beta(1,4)-GlcNAc-R reaction as background in a genotype-phenotype study that did not assay A3GALT2. Both removal reasons now distinguish the GGTA1 reaction on LacNAc-containing glycoconjugates from iGb3S-family use of lactosylceramide, identify the rat ISS source explicitly, and explain why MODIFY to a positive GO:0001962 would conflict with the existing contested NOT row. The PMID:28406212 reference review now records that its functional gloss is not accepted. Structured knowledge gaps were added for endogenous expression, causal catalysis/flux, and native localization; the PANTHER cache is cited explicitly. Status remains `COMPLETE` because the curation workflow is complete (no PENDING records), while the biological uncertainty is represented by `UNDECIDED` actions, `core_functions: []`, and open knowledge gaps.
