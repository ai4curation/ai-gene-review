Gene Description

DAG1 encodes dystroglycan, a cell-surface receptor that links extracellular matrix to the membrane-associated cytoskeleton. The 895-residue precursor enters the secretory pathway and undergoes SEA-domain self-processing into extracellular alpha-dystroglycan and transmembrane beta-dystroglycan, which remain noncovalently associated. Glycosylated alpha-dystroglycan binds laminins and other matrix ligands, including agrin and perlecan. Beta-dystroglycan anchors this ligand-binding surface in the membrane and engages cytoplasmic partners such as dystrophin, utrophin, ankyrin and CLASPs. This linkage supports basement-membrane organization and tissue integrity, with specialized roles at muscle membranes, glial interfaces and synapses. Glycan-dependent ligand binding, cytoplasmic-tail phosphorylation and proteolytic processing modify receptor behavior. Released alpha-derived material and intracellular beta-chain pools occur in specific contexts, including nuclear trafficking in cultured human cells.

Functional Isoforms

Curated functional classes representing distinct biological activities. These may be splice variants, cleavage products, or other forms with different functions.

Alpha-dystroglycan CLEAVAGE PRODUCT
ID: DAG1_ALPHA
UNIPROT CHAIN: PRO_0000021065 (residues 30-653)
Extracellular chain produced from the secretory precursor after removal of the 1–29 signal peptide and SEA processing. Its glycosylated receptor surface binds matrix ligands and associates noncovalently with beta-dystroglycan. Further release of N-terminal alpha-derived material is reported; the complete 30–653 chain is not assumed to be freely secreted in every context. Glycosylation states can differ in ligand competence without changing this chain identity.
Beta-dystroglycan CLEAVAGE PRODUCT
ID: DAG1_BETA
UNIPROT CHAIN: PRO_0000021066 (residues 654-895)
Membrane-spanning chain with an extracellular alpha-chain association region, a transmembrane segment at residues 750–775 and a cytoplasmic tail at 776–895. The tail binds cytoskeletal and signaling partners in context-dependent assays. Intrinsic precursor cleavage is distinct from MMP-dependent shedding of the extracellular beta-chain region. Nuclear and cytoplasmic pools have been observed in cultured human cells; these do not assign the same localization or functions to the extracellular alpha chain.

Core Functions

Glycosylated alpha-dystroglycan (DAG1_ALPHA; PRO_0000021065, residues 30–653) binds laminin as the extracellular ligand-binding chain of the alpha/beta dystroglycan receptor. Together with the membrane-anchoring beta chain, it organizes and supports assembly of extracellular matrix and basement membrane. Agrin and perlecan are additional matrix ligands.

Processed beta-dystroglycan (DAG1_BETA; PRO_0000021066, residues 654–895) is the transmembrane cytoskeletal adaptor connecting extracellular alpha-dystroglycan to intracellular dystrophin or utrophin. These contacts provide the intracellular side of the matrix–membrane–cytoskeleton bridge in muscle and non-muscle tissues. In muscle, this linkage contributes to the structural integrity of the sarcolemma. Association with CLASP proteins also connects dystroglycan to microtubule-associated partners.

DAG1 biological evidence notes

Dystroglycan is a processed receptor system. The official Q14118 sequence is an 895-residue precursor, with signal peptide 1–29, alpha chain 30–653 and beta chain 654–895. These chains have complementary functions and are recorded as cleavage products, not splice isoforms. (UniProt Q14118) Glycosylated alpha-DG presents the matrix-ligand-binding surface; beta-DG anchors it in the membrane and engages intracellular partners. One integrated laminin-receptor core captures this physical linkage and its contribution to matrix organization. (PMID:20044576(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20044576/)) (PMID:23940118(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23940118/)) Developmental, synaptic and signaling effects retain their tissue and experimental contexts rather than becoming universal core activities.

The distinction between intrinsic maturation and cleavage by another enzyme is decisive. The 2008 SEA study supports intramolecular precursor cleavage but does not establish the catalytic triad in the serine-endopeptidase definition. Cleavage mutants retain or enhance laminin binding and assembly, so cleavage is not described as universally necessary for ligand competence. (PMID:17905726(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17905726/)) In the separate MMP-9 study, murine beta-DG is the cleaved substrate and human MMP-9 performs proteolysis. (PMID:19946898(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19946898/)) Neither experiment assigns glycan-transfer activity to DAG1. Public original locators and precise read limits are in the evidence guide.

Beta-chain partner evidence has different levels of resolution. Utrophin and dystrophin are established cytoskeletal partners. (PMID:10767429(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10767429/)) (PMID:11495720(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11495720/)) Human DAG1–CLASP co-immunoprecipitation is distinguished from chick epiblast perturbation, and complex association is not described as a purified binary interface. (PMID:23940118(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23940118/)) The NCK1 screen tests a synthetic peptide corresponding to alpha-chain residues 300–313; its interaction with intracellular SH3 domains does not establish accessibility in the native mature receptor. (PMID:17474147(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17474147/)) The ankyrin records test a human beta tail against rat ankyrin-G. (PMID:19109891(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19109891/)) Positive AGR2/AGR3 yeast interactions and the caveolin-3 tail interaction are retained with their assay limits. (PMID:12592373(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12592373/)) (PMID:10988290(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10988290/)) No partner enzyme activity is transferred to DAG1.

The platelet original remains inaccessible. (PMID:18341635(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18341635/)) General cytoskeletal interplay in its abstract is insufficient to reconstruct individual actin, tubulin, MAP2 or vinculin assays and the fine localization/anchoring endpoints. These assertions remain unresolved. A separately inspected human ACTN1–DAG1 curated BioGRID record supports that specific interaction, and plasma-membrane localization is independently established. (BioGRID 616031) (PMID:8017170(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8017170/)) Neither a missing current interaction record nor an unavailable original is evidence of nonbinding.

The human 8UF4 structure supplies direct evidence for calcium coordination. One modeled ion is contacted by five native DAG1 residues in a recombinant 491–748 fragment. Refolding and purification used calcium-rich buffers. This supports contextual calcium binding while leaving native occupancy, affinity and physiological regulation unresolved. It is independent of calcium dependence on the ligand side of an adhesion assay; the paper’s wording about both molecules is kept distinct from the single calcium modeled in the current deposit. (PMID:39305901(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39305901/)) (8UF4 coordinates)

Complementary mature-chain functions and focal-adhesion context — 2026-10-16

The alpha and beta chains now have separate, complementary core representations. Alpha-dystroglycan presents the glycan-dependent laminin receptor surface; beta-dystroglycan spans the membrane and connects alpha to dystrophin or utrophin. This makes the performed cytoskeletal adaptor activity explicit without changing the underlying receptor architecture or asserting direct beta-chain binding to laminin or actin. The complete utrophin and dystrophin study abstracts support the cytoplasmic pair interactions, while human muscle immunogold supports the membrane linkage. The dystrophin phosphorylation experiment uses mouse myotubes and peptide assays, not a human full-length ternary-complex measurement. These two cores supersede the earlier single-core representation described above. (PMID:10767429(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10767429/), PMID:11495720(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11495720/), PMID:8017170(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8017170/))

Human DAG1 from an MGC cDNA and human CLASP constructs associate in HEK293 co-immunoprecipitation. The corresponding functional basement-membrane experiments were performed in chick epiblast; they support the conserved structural linkage without becoming direct human embryonic assays. Co-immunoprecipitation establishes association, not an isolated molecular interface. The receptor core uses the already supported basement-membrane organization and extracellular-matrix assembly processes, whose current ontology paths are separate descendants of matrix organization. DAG1 contributes the receptor/scaffold rather than synthesizing matrix ligands. (PMID:23940118(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23940118/), PMID:10366602(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10366602/), PMID:17905726(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17905726/))

The complete PMID9117347 abstract explicitly reports focal-adhesion recruitment in human aortic smooth-muscle cells and rat REF52 fibroblasts. Recruitment depends on cell type and extracellular ligand; some other cell lines do not recruit dystroglycan even on laminin. This independent positive human observation supports contextual retention of the focal-adhesion annotations. It does not recover the separate PMID21423176 DAG1 proteomics assignment or the PMID18341635 platelet localization panel, both of which remain unresolved at the source-assay level. The original rat donor is preserved rather than relabeled as human.
