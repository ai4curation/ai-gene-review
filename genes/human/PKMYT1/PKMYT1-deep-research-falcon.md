# PKMYT1 (UniProt Q99640): functional-annotation research report

## Executive summary

**Identity is verified.** The requested protein is human **PKMYT1**—also called **Myt1/MYT1** or human Myt1 (Myt1Hu)—not the similarly named **PKMYT1AR** long noncoding RNA. PKMYT1 is a 499-amino-acid, approximately 55-kDa, membrane-associated member of the WEE-family protein kinases. Its experimentally defined kinase domain and C-terminal membrane-targeting region agree with the supplied UniProt Q99640 annotation and InterPro protein-kinase domains. Open Targets separately assigns PKMYT1 and PKMYT1AR to different human genes, further excluding an identity mix-up. (OpenTargets Search: -PKMYT1, liu1997thehumanmyt1 pages 4-6, schmidt2017regulationofg2m pages 5-9)

PKMYT1’s primary molecular function is to use ATP to phosphorylate **CDK1/Cdc2**, especially cyclin-B-bound CDK1, at the inhibitory **Thr14** residue and, less efficiently, **Tyr15**. It thereby restrains CDK1–cyclin-B activity and prevents premature mitotic entry. Its distinguishing cell-biological feature is localization to cytoplasmic membranes of the **endoplasmic reticulum and Golgi apparatus**, where it can both phosphorylate and sequester CDK1–cyclin-B complexes. (liu1997thehumanmyt1 pages 10-11, liu1997thehumanmyt1 pages 3-4, schmidt2017regulationofg2m pages 3-5)

The principal current application is investigational oncology. Tumors with excessive replication stress—especially those with **CCNE1 amplification/overexpression**, low-molecular-weight cyclin E, deficient p53 signaling, or selected **FBXW7/PPP2R1A** alterations—can become dependent on PKMYT1 to prevent S-phase cells from entering mitosis. Inhibiting PKMYT1 removes that safeguard and can produce premature mitosis, DNA damage, mitotic catastrophe and apoptosis. However, lunresertib/RP-6306 remains an experimental drug: there is no approved PKMYT1-directed therapy and mature clinical efficacy has not yet been established in the cited peer-reviewed evidence. (NCT04855656 chunk 1, NCT04855656 chunk 2, wang2024genomewidecrisprscreens pages 9-10, xu2025targetingccne1amplified pages 1-2)

## 1. Target verification and nomenclature

The foundational human study characterized **Myt1Hu** as a 499-residue protein with an expected molecular mass of approximately 55 kDa. It is most closely related to *Xenopus* Myt1—about 46% identical over the full protein and approximately 64% identical in the predicted catalytic domain—and belongs to the WEE/Myt1 kinase family. These findings align with Q99640’s annotation as a membrane-associated tyrosine- and threonine-specific Cdc2-inhibitory kinase. (liu1997thehumanmyt1 pages 10-11, liu1997thehumanmyt1 pages 4-6)

PKMYT1 contains a conventional bilobal protein-kinase fold despite belonging to an atypical WEE-family branch. Reported structural features include catalytic Asp233, hinge residue Cys190 and gatekeeper Thr178. The smaller threonine gatekeeper provides access to a hydrophobic back pocket relevant to selective inhibitor design. A structural comparison found 36.5% kinase-domain identity and 1.87 Å backbone RMSD between PKMYT1 and WEE1, while their ATP-pocket residues are more conserved. (schmidt2017regulationofg2m pages 5-9)

The similarly named **PKMYT1AR** is a distinct PKMYT1-associated lncRNA, not an alias or protein isoform of PKMYT1. Literature or database associations assigned to PKMYT1AR therefore cannot be used to annotate Q99640. (OpenTargets Search: -PKMYT1)

## 2. Primary biochemical function

### Catalytic reaction

PKMYT1 catalyzes the canonical protein-kinase reaction:

**ATP + CDK1 residue-OH → ADP + phospho-CDK1 residue + H⁺**

The physiological inhibitory sites are **Thr14** and **Tyr15** in CDK1’s glycine-rich loop. Recombinant Myt1Hu underwent serine and tyrosine autophosphorylation and phosphorylated kinase-dead CDK1 substrates, demonstrating that phosphate transfer came from PKMYT1 rather than CDK1 autophosphorylation. Radiometric assays used ATP or [γ-³²P]ATP with Mg²⁺, purified GST-PKMYT1, cyclin-B1–CDK1 complexes and site-mutant substrates. (liu1997thehumanmyt1 pages 10-11, liu1997thehumanmyt1 pages 3-4)

### Substrate and phosphosite specificity

PKMYT1 is formally dual-specific because it can modify both threonine and tyrosine, but the human enzyme **strongly prefers CDK1 Thr14**. Thr14 had a substantially lower apparent ATP *K*m than Tyr15, and the experiments supported an ordered tendency in which Thr14 phosphorylation precedes Tyr15 phosphorylation. PKMYT1 also strongly preferred **cyclin-B1-bound CDK1** over monomeric CDK1. (liu1997thehumanmyt1 pages 10-11)

The two inhibitory sites have cumulative functional effects. Phosphorylation at either Thr14 or Tyr15 reduced CDK1–cyclin-B histone-H1 kinase activity by approximately **10-fold**, whereas phosphorylation at both sites reduced activity by approximately **100-fold**. Thus, Tyr15 is a real PKMYT1 substrate in vitro, but Thr14 phosphorylation is the most characteristic biochemical and pharmacodynamic readout of PKMYT1 activity. (liu1997thehumanmyt1 pages 10-11)

PKMYT1 is substantially more substrate-restricted than a general cell-cycle kinase. The reviewed evidence indicates activity toward CDK1 complexes but not CDK2 complexes. This distinction is important because WEE1 is often described as acting more broadly in nuclear S/G2 control, whereas PKMYT1 principally regulates a membrane-associated cytoplasmic CDK1 pool. (schmidt2017regulationofg2m pages 3-5)

## 3. Cellular localization and topology

Cell fractionation and immunofluorescence placed endogenous or expressed human PKMYT1 on **ER and Golgi membranes**, including colocalization with the Golgi-associated marker γ-adaptin and ER marker PTP1B. A largely hydrophobic/uncharged segment at residues **378–399**, C-terminal to the catalytic domain, is required for membrane targeting; deleting this segment redirected PKMYT1 predominantly to the nucleus in HeLa cells. (liu1997thehumanmyt1 pages 10-11, liu1997thehumanmyt1 pages 4-6)

Functionally, this localization is not merely descriptive. PKMYT1’s C-terminal region binds CDK1 complexes, retaining CDK1–cyclin-B in the cytoplasm and limiting nuclear access. Kinase-inactive PKMYT1 can still delay mitotic entry to some extent, supporting a second, noncatalytic mechanism based on binding and sequestration. Accordingly, PKMYT1 acts at the cytoplasmic face of internal membranes rather than as a secreted enzyme, cell-surface receptor or luminal ER kinase. (schmidt2017regulationofg2m pages 3-5)

## 4. Pathway role: control of the G2/M transition

Mitotic entry depends on switch-like activation of CDK1–cyclin B. PKMYT1 and WEE1 impose inhibitory phosphorylation, while CDC25 phosphatases remove Thr14/Tyr15 phosphate; CDK-activating kinase phosphorylation at Thr161 supports the active CDK1 state. Activated CDK1–cyclin B reinforces mitotic commitment by activating CDC25 and contributing, with PLK1, to inactivation of inhibitory WEE-family signaling. (schmidt2017regulationofg2m pages 3-5, schmidt2017regulationofg2m pages 1-3)

PKMYT1 is therefore best viewed as a **brake on premature mitosis**, not as the sole G2/M checkpoint. Its catalytic and sequestration functions maintain inactive cytoplasmic CDK1–cyclin-B complexes until replication and checkpoint conditions permit mitotic entry. PLK1-mediated phosphorylation negatively regulates PKMYT1, helping dismantle this brake during checkpoint recovery and mitotic commitment. (schmidt2017regulationofg2m pages 3-5)

This model explains why PKMYT1 inhibition is particularly damaging under replication stress: active CDK1 appears while DNA synthesis or repair is incomplete, forcing cells into mitosis with under-replicated or damaged chromosomes. The resulting phenotypes include pan-γH2AX accumulation, chromosome breakage, mitotic catastrophe and apoptosis. (wang2024genomewidecrisprscreens pages 9-10, xu2025targetingccne1amplified pages 1-2)

## 5. Recent developments, 2023–2024

### 5.1 Resistance to checkpoint-directed drugs—2023

Sokhi and colleagues reported that Myt1 overexpression can compensate when WEE1, ATR or CHK1 is inhibited. Increased Myt1 maintained inhibitory CDK1 phosphorylation, reduced premature mitotic entry and improved cancer-cell survival. This work identifies PKMYT1 abundance as a potential resistance biomarker and cautions that inhibiting one checkpoint branch can select for reliance on another. The study was published in November 2023: [DOI 10.3389/fcell.2023.1270542](https://doi.org/10.3389/fcell.2023.1270542).

### 5.2 Pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma—April 2024

Genome-wide CRISPR screens identified PKMYT1 as a non-mutant dependency in pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma. Dependency occurred in KRAS-mutant and KRAS-wild-type backgrounds, while TP53-mutant KPC models were more sensitive than TP53-intact KC models. Genetic ablation or RP-6306 disrupted cell-cycle progression, increased γH2AX, promoted premature mitosis and induced apoptosis or mitotic catastrophe. (wang2024genomewidecrisprscreens pages 9-10)

Oral twice-daily RP-6306 suppressed Pa-Tu-8988T xenografts and patient-derived PDAC xenografts. The reported experiments used six mice per cell-line xenograft group and eight per PDX group; body weight and serum ALT/AST did not show overt toxicity in those models. These are useful preclinical safety signals but cannot establish human tolerability. Published April 2024: [DOI 10.1038/s44321-024-00060-y](https://doi.org/10.1038/s44321-024-00060-y). (wang2024genomewidecrisprscreens pages 9-10)

### 5.3 Endocrine/CDK4/6-inhibitor-resistant ER-positive breast cancer—May 2024

In ER-positive breast cancer, PKMYT1 expression tracked E2F/proliferation programs and incomplete response to endocrine or CDK4/6-inhibitor therapy. In the NeoPalAna cohort, palbociclib reduced PKMYT1 mRNA in Ki67 responders, whereas two nonresponders retained high expression; the post-treatment comparison approached significance (*p*=0.056) but is underpowered and should be considered hypothesis-generating. (chen2024pkmyt1isa pages 17-18)

Preclinically, lunresertib plus gemcitabine was synergistic in p53-deficient, palbociclib-resistant cells and produced greater tumor reduction in resistant organoid/PDX systems than either agent alone. TP53 alteration is proposed as a stratifier because it was reported in up to 58.5% of CDK4/6-inhibitor-resistant metastatic ER-positive cancers, but this is not yet a validated companion diagnostic for lunresertib. Published May 2024: [DOI 10.1158/1535-7163.MCT-23-0564](https://doi.org/10.1158/1535-7163.MCT-23-0564). (chen2024pkmyt1isa pages 24-26, chen2024pkmyt1isa pages 14-15)

### 5.4 Low-molecular-weight cyclin E in triple-negative breast cancer—August 2024

Low-molecular-weight cyclin E (LMW-E) was reported in approximately 70% of TNBC and was associated with increased PKMYT1 expression/stability and CDK1 inhibitory phosphorylation. Lunresertib produced preferential antitumor effects in LMW-E-high engineered tumors, transgenic mammary tumors and PDXs, while LMW-E-null comparators were less responsive. Mechanistically, treatment accelerated mitotic entry, impaired replication-fork restart, increased chromosome damage and apoptosis, and induced interferon/T-cell-associated responses. Published August 2024: [DOI 10.1158/0008-5472.CAN-23-4130](https://doi.org/10.1158/0008-5472.CAN-23-4130).

## 6. Translational application and clinical implementation

The leading inhibitor is **lunresertib (RP-6306)**, an orally administered, selective PKMYT1 inhibitor. The strongest biomarker rationale originated with **CCNE1 amplification**, which increases cyclin-E/CDK2-driven origin firing and replication stress. PKMYT1 inhibition then activates CDK1 prematurely, causing mitosis during ongoing DNA synthesis. Preclinical studies also support combinations with gemcitabine or ATR inhibition because these further increase replication stress or weaken compensatory checkpoint signaling. (xu2025targetingccne1amplified pages 10-10, xu2025targetingccne1amplified pages 1-2)

The main registered implementation is **MYTHIC, NCT04855656**, an open-label phase 1/1b study of lunresertib alone or with the ATR inhibitor camonsertib/RP-3500 or WEE1 inhibitor Debio 0123. Registry eligibility includes **CCNE1 amplification** and deleterious **FBXW7** or **PPP2R1A** alterations. The retrieved registry version listed recruiting status and estimated enrollment of 464, with pharmacokinetic, pharmacodynamic, ctDNA, safety and preliminary antitumor endpoints. [ClinicalTrials.gov: NCT04855656](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04855656). (NCT04855656 chunk 1, NCT04855656 chunk 2)

A nonrandomized phase 2 study, **NCT05605509**, enrolled 28 patients and evaluated RP-6306 with gemcitabine, FOLFIRI, trastuzumab or camonsertib in selected advanced cancers. Its registry description explicitly defines the mechanism as inhibition of PKMYT1-mediated CDK1 Thr14 phosphorylation and cytoplasmic sequestration. [ClinicalTrials.gov: NCT05605509](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05605509). (NCT05605509 chunk 1)

The phase 1 **MINOTAUR** study of RP-6306 plus FOLFIRI, **NCT05147350**, enrolled 38 participants and was subsequently terminated by the sponsor; the registry does not state that termination resulted from a demonstrated mechanistic or safety failure. [ClinicalTrials.gov: NCT05147350](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05147350). (NCT05147350 chunk 1)

These studies represent genuine real-world clinical implementation, but they are early-stage trials rather than routine clinical practice. Enrollment and trial status are not evidence of efficacy. The available cited sources do not justify a definitive human response rate, survival benefit or approved indication.

## 7. Expert assessment and remaining uncertainties

1. **Confidence in core annotation is high.** CDK1 is supported by direct recombinant assays, phosphosite-mutant experiments and functional kinase measurements. Thr14 preference, cyclin-B dependence and ER/Golgi localization are experimentally established rather than inferred only from homology. (liu1997thehumanmyt1 pages 10-11, liu1997thehumanmyt1 pages 3-4)

2. **PKMYT1 and WEE1 overlap but are not interchangeable.** WEE1 is principally Tyr15-directed and associated with nuclear CDK control; PKMYT1 is dual-specific, Thr14-preferring and membrane-associated. PKMYT1 also has a noncatalytic sequestration function. This division helps explain different therapeutic windows and resistance relationships, although cellular context can blur a simple one-kinase/one-compartment model. (schmidt2017regulationofg2m pages 3-5, schmidt2017regulationofg2m pages 5-9, lang2025myt1kinasean pages 7-11)

3. **The strongest precision-oncology rationale is replication-stress dependency, not PKMYT1 mutation.** CCNE1 amplification/overexpression has the clearest genetic basis, while TP53 loss, LMW-E, FBXW7 and PPP2R1A are promising but less uniformly validated biomarkers. Biomarker combinations may ultimately outperform any single alteration. (chen2024pkmyt1isa pages 24-26, wang2024genomewidecrisprscreens pages 9-10, xu2025targetingccne1amplified pages 10-10, xu2025targetingccne1amplified pages 1-2)

4. **Therapeutic selectivity remains an open clinical question.** Preclinical models suggest that stressed cancer cells are more dependent on PKMYT1 than normal cells and that selective inhibition may be less myelosuppressive than broader checkpoint blockade. Nevertheless, PKMYT1 regulates an essential cell-cycle transition, so intermittent schedules, biomarker selection and combination dosing will be central to tolerability. Human safety and efficacy must be judged from completed trial datasets, not xenograft tolerability. (NCT04855656 chunk 1, wang2024genomewidecrisprscreens pages 9-10, xu2025targetingccne1amplified pages 10-10)

## Conclusion

Human PKMYT1/Q99640 is a membrane-associated WEE-family kinase whose primary physiological role is inhibitory control of cyclin-B-bound CDK1. It preferentially phosphorylates CDK1 Thr14, can also phosphorylate Tyr15, and acts at ER/Golgi membranes through both catalysis and cytoplasmic sequestration. These activities restrain premature G2/M transition and create a druggable dependency in cancers with severe replication stress. Research in 2023–2024 expanded the biomarker landscape from CCNE1-amplified tumors to checkpoint-inhibitor resistance, PDAC, CDK4/6-inhibitor-resistant ER-positive breast cancer and LMW-E-high TNBC. Lunresertib has reached biomarker-guided phase 1/2 testing, but PKMYT1-directed treatment remains investigational and its ultimate clinical efficacy, optimal biomarkers and therapeutic window are not yet settled.
