Gene Description

PKMYT1 (Myt1) is a membrane-associated protein kinase of the WEE1 subfamily that restrains entry into mitosis by phosphorylating the cyclin-dependent kinase CDK1 (Cdc2) on the inhibitory residue Thr14, and, to a lesser and less firmly established degree, on Tyr15; phosphorylation of either site lowers cyclin B-CDK1 activity and phosphorylation of both nearly abolishes it. PKMYT1 acts preferentially on cyclin-bound CDK1 and does not phosphorylate CDK2- or CDK4-containing complexes, making it a substrate-restricted counterpart of the nuclear kinase WEE1, which is directed mainly at CDK1 Tyr15. Unlike WEE1, PKMYT1 is a peripheral protein of the endoplasmic reticulum and Golgi membranes: a 20-residue hydrophobic motif C-terminal to the kinase domain (arginine 378 to histidine 399) anchors it there, and both the kinase domain and the C-terminal substrate-docking region face the cytosol. The C-terminal 63 residues contain an RXL motif (Arg486-Asn487-Leu488) that docks cyclin B1-CDK1; this docking both promotes efficient Thr14 phosphorylation and tethers the complex to the cytoplasmic face of these membranes, limiting the nuclear-cytoplasmic shuttling of cyclin B1-CDK1. PKMYT1 is therefore a brake on premature mitosis, opposed by the CDC25 phosphatases; at mitotic entry it becomes hyperphosphorylated (CDK1 and PLK1 have both been implicated, with Ser426 phosphorylated by PLK1), loses affinity for cyclin B1-CDK1 and releases the brake. Myt1-type kinases perform the equivalent CDK1-inhibitory role in the prophase arrest of oocytes in other animals. Because PKMYT1 sustains inhibitory CDK1 phosphorylation when replication is incomplete, tumour cells under high replication stress, such as those with CCNE1 amplification, depend on it, and selective PKMYT1 inhibitors (lunresertib/RP-6306) force such cells into mitosis with under-replicated DNA.

Suggested Questions for Experts

Q: Is human PKMYT1 a genuine dual-specificity kinase in cells, i.e. does it phosphorylate CDK1 Tyr15 directly, or is the Tyr15 signal attributable to WEE1 and to PKMYT1 autophosphorylation? UniProt currently flags the tyrosine activity as unclear and possibly indirect, and no GO annotation asserts protein tyrosine kinase activity for PKMYT1.

Q: Which kinase actually inactivates PKMYT1 at mitotic entry, and is PLK1 phosphorylation of Ser426 sufficient to abolish cyclin B1-CDK1 docking, or is CDK1-dependent hyperphosphorylation of the C-terminal region required as well?

Q: Does human PKMYT1 maintain the prophase-I arrest of human oocytes, as Myt1 orthologues do in Xenopus and mouse? The meiotic annotations on human PKMYT1 rest on phylogenetic inference alone.

Suggested Experiments

Experiment: Assay purified recombinant PKMYT1 against cyclin B1-CDK1 complexes carrying T14A or Y15F substitutions, reading out site occupancy by phosphosite-specific antibodies and quantitative mass spectrometry; repeat in cells depleted of WEE1 or treated with a WEE1 inhibitor to separate the two kinases' contributions to each site.

Hypothesis: PKMYT1 phosphorylates CDK1 Thr14 directly but contributes little or no direct Tyr15 phosphorylation in cells.

Type: biochemical and cell-based kinase assay

Experiment: Generate isogenic knock-ins of kinase-dead (Asp233 or Asn238 substituted) and RXL-motif (R486A/N487A/L488A) PKMYT1 at the endogenous locus, and compare CDK1-pThr14 levels, cyclin B1 nuclear-cytoplasmic shuttling by live imaging, and mitotic-entry timing; previous work used overproduced protein, which may exaggerate the sequestration effect.

Hypothesis: The catalytic and the docking/sequestration arms of PKMYT1 make separable contributions to the G2 brake at endogenous expression levels.

Type: CRISPR knock-in with live-cell imaging

Experiment: Replace endogenous PKMYT1 with variants lacking the membrane-targeting motif (delta 378-399), with the motif replaced by a heterologous ER anchor, or with an added nuclear-localisation signal, and test which variants restore CDK1-pThr14 and normal G2 length, and whether a nuclear PKMYT1 can substitute for WEE1.

Hypothesis: ER/Golgi membrane anchoring is required for PKMYT1 to restrain mitotic entry, rather than being incidental to its kinase activity.

Type: structure-function complementation

PKMYT1 (UniProt Q99640): functional-annotation research report

Executive summary

Identity is verified. The requested protein is human PKMYT1—also called Myt1/MYT1 or human Myt1 (Myt1Hu)—not the similarly named PKMYT1AR long noncoding RNA. PKMYT1 is a 499-amino-acid, approximately 55-kDa, membrane-associated member of the WEE-family protein kinases. Its experimentally defined kinase domain and C-terminal membrane-targeting region agree with the supplied UniProt Q99640 annotation and InterPro protein-kinase domains. Open Targets separately assigns PKMYT1 and PKMYT1AR to different human genes, further excluding an identity mix-up. (OpenTargets Search: -PKMYT1, liu1997thehumanmyt1 pages 4-6, schmidt2017regulationofg2m pages 5-9)

PKMYT1’s primary molecular function is to use ATP to phosphorylate CDK1/Cdc2, especially cyclin-B-bound CDK1, at the inhibitory Thr14 residue and, less efficiently, Tyr15. It thereby restrains CDK1–cyclin-B activity and prevents premature mitotic entry. Its distinguishing cell-biological feature is localization to cytoplasmic membranes of the endoplasmic reticulum and Golgi apparatus, where it can both phosphorylate and sequester CDK1–cyclin-B complexes. (liu1997thehumanmyt1 pages 10-11, liu1997thehumanmyt1 pages 3-4, schmidt2017regulationofg2m pages 3-5)

The principal current application is investigational oncology. Tumors with excessive replication stress—especially those with CCNE1 amplification/overexpression, low-molecular-weight cyclin E, deficient p53 signaling, or selected FBXW7/PPP2R1A alterations—can become dependent on PKMYT1 to prevent S-phase cells from entering mitosis. Inhibiting PKMYT1 removes that safeguard and can produce premature mitosis, DNA damage, mitotic catastrophe and apoptosis. However, lunresertib/RP-6306 remains an experimental drug: there is no approved PKMYT1-directed therapy and mature clinical efficacy has not yet been established in the cited peer-reviewed evidence. (NCT04855656 chunk 1, NCT04855656 chunk 2, wang2024genomewidecrisprscreens pages 9-10, xu2025targetingccne1amplified pages 1-2)

1. Target verification and nomenclature

The foundational human study characterized Myt1Hu as a 499-residue protein with an expected molecular mass of approximately 55 kDa. It is most closely related to Xenopus Myt1—about 46% identical over the full protein and approximately 64% identical in the predicted catalytic domain—and belongs to the WEE/Myt1 kinase family. These findings align with Q99640’s annotation as a membrane-associated tyrosine- and threonine-specific Cdc2-inhibitory kinase. (liu1997thehumanmyt1 pages 10-11, liu1997thehumanmyt1 pages 4-6)

PKMYT1 contains a conventional bilobal protein-kinase fold despite belonging to an atypical WEE-family branch. Reported structural features include catalytic Asp233, hinge residue Cys190 and gatekeeper Thr178. The smaller threonine gatekeeper provides access to a hydrophobic back pocket relevant to selective inhibitor design. A structural comparison found 36.5% kinase-domain identity and 1.87 Å backbone RMSD between PKMYT1 and WEE1, while their ATP-pocket residues are more conserved. (schmidt2017regulationofg2m pages 5-9)

The similarly named PKMYT1AR is a distinct PKMYT1-associated lncRNA, not an alias or protein isoform of PKMYT1. Literature or database associations assigned to PKMYT1AR therefore cannot be used to annotate Q99640. (OpenTargets Search: -PKMYT1)

2. Primary biochemical function

Catalytic reaction

PKMYT1 catalyzes the canonical protein-kinase reaction:

ATP + CDK1 residue-OH → ADP + phospho-CDK1 residue + H⁺

The physiological inhibitory sites are Thr14 and Tyr15 in CDK1’s glycine-rich loop. Recombinant Myt1Hu underwent serine and tyrosine autophosphorylation and phosphorylated kinase-dead CDK1 substrates, demonstrating that phosphate transfer came from PKMYT1 rather than CDK1 autophosphorylation. Radiometric assays used ATP or [γ-³²P]ATP with Mg²⁺, purified GST-PKMYT1, cyclin-B1–CDK1 complexes and site-mutant substrates. (liu1997thehumanmyt1 pages 10-11, liu1997thehumanmyt1 pages 3-4)

Substrate and phosphosite specificity

PKMYT1 is formally dual-specific because it can modify both threonine and tyrosine, but the human enzyme strongly prefers CDK1 Thr14. Thr14 had a substantially lower apparent ATP Km than Tyr15, and the experiments supported an ordered tendency in which Thr14 phosphorylation precedes Tyr15 phosphorylation. PKMYT1 also strongly preferred cyclin-B1-bound CDK1 over monomeric CDK1. (liu1997thehumanmyt1 pages 10-11)

The two inhibitory sites have cumulative functional effects. Phosphorylation at either Thr14 or Tyr15 reduced CDK1–cyclin-B histone-H1 kinase activity by approximately 10-fold, whereas phosphorylation at both sites reduced activity by approximately 100-fold. Thus, Tyr15 is a real PKMYT1 substrate in vitro, but Thr14 phosphorylation is the most characteristic biochemical and pharmacodynamic readout of PKMYT1 activity. (liu1997thehumanmyt1 pages 10-11)

PKMYT1 is substantially more substrate-restricted than a general cell-cycle kinase. The reviewed evidence indicates activity toward CDK1 complexes but not CDK2 complexes. This distinction is important because WEE1 is often described as acting more broadly in nuclear S/G2 control, whereas PKMYT1 principally regulates a membrane-associated cytoplasmic CDK1 pool. (schmidt2017regulationofg2m pages 3-5)

3. Cellular localization and topology

Cell fractionation and immunofluorescence placed endogenous or expressed human PKMYT1 on ER and Golgi membranes, including colocalization with the Golgi-associated marker γ-adaptin and ER marker PTP1B. A largely hydrophobic/uncharged segment at residues 378–399, C-terminal to the catalytic domain, is required for membrane targeting; deleting this segment redirected PKMYT1 predominantly to the nucleus in HeLa cells. (liu1997thehumanmyt1 pages 10-11, liu1997thehumanmyt1 pages 4-6)

Functionally, this localization is not merely descriptive. PKMYT1’s C-terminal region binds CDK1 complexes, retaining CDK1–cyclin-B in the cytoplasm and limiting nuclear access. Kinase-inactive PKMYT1 can still delay mitotic entry to some extent, supporting a second, noncatalytic mechanism based on binding and sequestration. Accordingly, PKMYT1 acts at the cytoplasmic face of internal membranes rather than as a secreted enzyme, cell-surface receptor or luminal ER kinase. (schmidt2017regulationofg2m pages 3-5)

4. Pathway role: control of the G2/M transition

Mitotic entry depends on switch-like activation of CDK1–cyclin B. PKMYT1 and WEE1 impose inhibitory phosphorylation, while CDC25 phosphatases remove Thr14/Tyr15 phosphate; CDK-activating kinase phosphorylation at Thr161 supports the active CDK1 state. Activated CDK1–cyclin B reinforces mitotic commitment by activating CDC25 and contributing, with PLK1, to inactivation of inhibitory WEE-family signaling. (schmidt2017regulationofg2m pages 3-5, schmidt2017regulationofg2m pages 1-3)

PKMYT1 is therefore best viewed as a brake on premature mitosis, not as the sole G2/M checkpoint. Its catalytic and sequestration functions maintain inactive cytoplasmic CDK1–cyclin-B complexes until replication and checkpoint conditions permit mitotic entry. PLK1-mediated phosphorylation negatively regulates PKMYT1, helping dismantle this brake during checkpoint recovery and mitotic commitment. (schmidt2017regulationofg2m pages 3-5)

This model explains why PKMYT1 inhibition is particularly damaging under replication stress: active CDK1 appears while DNA synthesis or repair is incomplete, forcing cells into mitosis with under-replicated or damaged chromosomes. The resulting phenotypes include pan-γH2AX accumulation, chromosome breakage, mitotic catastrophe and apoptosis. (wang2024genomewidecrisprscreens pages 9-10, xu2025targetingccne1amplified pages 1-2)

5. Recent developments, 2023–2024

5.1 Resistance to checkpoint-directed drugs—2023

Sokhi and colleagues reported that Myt1 overexpression can compensate when WEE1, ATR or CHK1 is inhibited. Increased Myt1 maintained inhibitory CDK1 phosphorylation, reduced premature mitotic entry and improved cancer-cell survival. This work identifies PKMYT1 abundance as a potential resistance biomarker and cautions that inhibiting one checkpoint branch can select for reliance on another. The study was published in November 2023: DOI 10.3389/fcell.2023.1270542.

5.2 Pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma—April 2024

Genome-wide CRISPR screens identified PKMYT1 as a non-mutant dependency in pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma. Dependency occurred in KRAS-mutant and KRAS-wild-type backgrounds, while TP53-mutant KPC models were more sensitive than TP53-intact KC models. Genetic ablation or RP-6306 disrupted cell-cycle progression, increased γH2AX, promoted premature mitosis and induced apoptosis or mitotic catastrophe. (wang2024genomewidecrisprscreens pages 9-10)

Oral twice-daily RP-6306 suppressed Pa-Tu-8988T xenografts and patient-derived PDAC xenografts. The reported experiments used six mice per cell-line xenograft group and eight per PDX group; body weight and serum ALT/AST did not show overt toxicity in those models. These are useful preclinical safety signals but cannot establish human tolerability. Published April 2024: DOI 10.1038/s44321-024-00060-y. (wang2024genomewidecrisprscreens pages 9-10)

5.3 Endocrine/CDK4/6-inhibitor-resistant ER-positive breast cancer—May 2024

In ER-positive breast cancer, PKMYT1 expression tracked E2F/proliferation programs and incomplete response to endocrine or CDK4/6-inhibitor therapy. In the NeoPalAna cohort, palbociclib reduced PKMYT1 mRNA in Ki67 responders, whereas two nonresponders retained high expression; the post-treatment comparison approached significance (p=0.056) but is underpowered and should be considered hypothesis-generating. (chen2024pkmyt1isa pages 17-18)

Preclinically, lunresertib plus gemcitabine was synergistic in p53-deficient, palbociclib-resistant cells and produced greater tumor reduction in resistant organoid/PDX systems than either agent alone. TP53 alteration is proposed as a stratifier because it was reported in up to 58.5% of CDK4/6-inhibitor-resistant metastatic ER-positive cancers, but this is not yet a validated companion diagnostic for lunresertib. Published May 2024: DOI 10.1158/1535-7163.MCT-23-0564. (chen2024pkmyt1isa pages 24-26, chen2024pkmyt1isa pages 14-15)

5.4 Low-molecular-weight cyclin E in triple-negative breast cancer—August 2024

Low-molecular-weight cyclin E (LMW-E) was reported in approximately 70% of TNBC and was associated with increased PKMYT1 expression/stability and CDK1 inhibitory phosphorylation. Lunresertib produced preferential antitumor effects in LMW-E-high engineered tumors, transgenic mammary tumors and PDXs, while LMW-E-null comparators were less responsive. Mechanistically, treatment accelerated mitotic entry, impaired replication-fork restart, increased chromosome damage and apoptosis, and induced interferon/T-cell-associated responses. Published August 2024: DOI 10.1158/0008-5472.CAN-23-4130.

6. Translational application and clinical implementation

The leading inhibitor is lunresertib (RP-6306), an orally administered, selective PKMYT1 inhibitor. The strongest biomarker rationale originated with CCNE1 amplification, which increases cyclin-E/CDK2-driven origin firing and replication stress. PKMYT1 inhibition then activates CDK1 prematurely, causing mitosis during ongoing DNA synthesis. Preclinical studies also support combinations with gemcitabine or ATR inhibition because these further increase replication stress or weaken compensatory checkpoint signaling. (xu2025targetingccne1amplified pages 10-10, xu2025targetingccne1amplified pages 1-2)

The main registered implementation is MYTHIC, NCT04855656, an open-label phase 1/1b study of lunresertib alone or with the ATR inhibitor camonsertib/RP-3500 or WEE1 inhibitor Debio 0123. Registry eligibility includes CCNE1 amplification and deleterious FBXW7 or PPP2R1A alterations. The retrieved registry version listed recruiting status and estimated enrollment of 464, with pharmacokinetic, pharmacodynamic, ctDNA, safety and preliminary antitumor endpoints. ClinicalTrials.gov: NCT04855656. (NCT04855656 chunk 1, NCT04855656 chunk 2)

A nonrandomized phase 2 study, NCT05605509, enrolled 28 patients and evaluated RP-6306 with gemcitabine, FOLFIRI, trastuzumab or camonsertib in selected advanced cancers. Its registry description explicitly defines the mechanism as inhibition of PKMYT1-mediated CDK1 Thr14 phosphorylation and cytoplasmic sequestration. ClinicalTrials.gov: NCT05605509. (NCT05605509 chunk 1)

The phase 1 MINOTAUR study of RP-6306 plus FOLFIRI, NCT05147350, enrolled 38 participants and was subsequently terminated by the sponsor; the registry does not state that termination resulted from a demonstrated mechanistic or safety failure. ClinicalTrials.gov: NCT05147350. (NCT05147350 chunk 1)

These studies represent genuine real-world clinical implementation, but they are early-stage trials rather than routine clinical practice. Enrollment and trial status are not evidence of efficacy. The available cited sources do not justify a definitive human response rate, survival benefit or approved indication.

7. Expert assessment and remaining uncertainties

  1. Confidence in core annotation is high. CDK1 is supported by direct recombinant assays, phosphosite-mutant experiments and functional kinase measurements. Thr14 preference, cyclin-B dependence and ER/Golgi localization are experimentally established rather than inferred only from homology. (liu1997thehumanmyt1 pages 10-11, liu1997thehumanmyt1 pages 3-4)

  2. PKMYT1 and WEE1 overlap but are not interchangeable. WEE1 is principally Tyr15-directed and associated with nuclear CDK control; PKMYT1 is dual-specific, Thr14-preferring and membrane-associated. PKMYT1 also has a noncatalytic sequestration function. This division helps explain different therapeutic windows and resistance relationships, although cellular context can blur a simple one-kinase/one-compartment model. (schmidt2017regulationofg2m pages 3-5, schmidt2017regulationofg2m pages 5-9, lang2025myt1kinasean pages 7-11)

  3. The strongest precision-oncology rationale is replication-stress dependency, not PKMYT1 mutation. CCNE1 amplification/overexpression has the clearest genetic basis, while TP53 loss, LMW-E, FBXW7 and PPP2R1A are promising but less uniformly validated biomarkers. Biomarker combinations may ultimately outperform any single alteration. (chen2024pkmyt1isa pages 24-26, wang2024genomewidecrisprscreens pages 9-10, xu2025targetingccne1amplified pages 10-10, xu2025targetingccne1amplified pages 1-2)

  4. Therapeutic selectivity remains an open clinical question. Preclinical models suggest that stressed cancer cells are more dependent on PKMYT1 than normal cells and that selective inhibition may be less myelosuppressive than broader checkpoint blockade. Nevertheless, PKMYT1 regulates an essential cell-cycle transition, so intermittent schedules, biomarker selection and combination dosing will be central to tolerability. Human safety and efficacy must be judged from completed trial datasets, not xenograft tolerability. (NCT04855656 chunk 1, wang2024genomewidecrisprscreens pages 9-10, xu2025targetingccne1amplified pages 10-10)

Conclusion

Human PKMYT1/Q99640 is a membrane-associated WEE-family kinase whose primary physiological role is inhibitory control of cyclin-B-bound CDK1. It preferentially phosphorylates CDK1 Thr14, can also phosphorylate Tyr15, and acts at ER/Golgi membranes through both catalysis and cytoplasmic sequestration. These activities restrain premature G2/M transition and create a druggable dependency in cancers with severe replication stress. Research in 2023–2024 expanded the biomarker landscape from CCNE1-amplified tumors to checkpoint-inhibitor resistance, PDAC, CDK4/6-inhibitor-resistant ER-positive breast cancer and LMW-E-high TNBC. Lunresertib has reached biomarker-guided phase 1/2 testing, but PKMYT1-directed treatment remains investigational and its ultimate clinical efficacy, optimal biomarkers and therapeutic window are not yet settled.

1. Identity and gestalt

  • UniProt Q99640, PMYT1_HUMAN, 499 aa. RecName: "Membrane-associated tyrosine- and
    threonine-specific cdc2-inhibitory kinase"; EC 2.7.11.1. Family: protein kinase
    superfamily, Ser/Thr protein kinase family, WEE1 subfamily.
  • Domain architecture from the UniProt feature table: protein kinase domain 110–359;
    active site (proton acceptor) Asp233; ATP binding 116–124 and 139; Mg(2+) binding
    238/251/253; membrane-association motif 382–398; interaction with CDC2-CCNB1
    437–499    ; interaction with PIN1 398–499; Ser426 phosphorylated by PLK1
    (UniProt cites Nakajima et al. 2003, PubMed 12738781).
  • Not to be confused with the lncRNA PKMYT1AR, which the deep-research report
    explicitly separates from this gene.

The picture that all three evidence streams agree on: PKMYT1 is the membrane-anchored
WEE1-family brake on mitotic entry, acting on cyclin-bound CDK1 at Thr14, with a
second, non-catalytic arm — docking/sequestration of cyclin B1–CDK1 — that WEE1 does
not have.

3. Annotation decisions and why

48 GOA rows. Actions: ACCEPT 15, REMOVE 14, MODIFY 11, KEEP_AS_NON_CORE 8.

Molecular function

  • GO:0004674 protein serine/threonine kinase activity (IDA, IEA, 2× Reactome TAS) —
    ACCEPT. This is the core activity; UniProt's threonine-kinase reaction (RHEA:46608) is
    the one carrying experimental evidence.
  • GO:0004672 protein kinase activity (IBA, IEA, TAS) and GO:0016301 kinase
    activity     (IDA) — MODIFY → GO:0004674. Pure granularity corrections; GO:0016301 does
    not even state that the substrate is a protein.
  • GO:0106310 protein serine kinase activity (IEA, RHEA:17989) — MODIFY → GO:0004674.
    The term's own usage note restricts it to kinases that specifically phosphorylate
    serine and sends dual-specificity Ser/Thr enzymes to the Ser/Thr term. PKMYT1's
    characterised residue is Thr14; UniProt's serine reaction is an ECO:0000305 curator
    inference, and serine phosphorylation by PKMYT1 is reported as autophosphorylation.
  • GO:0005524 ATP binding (IEA) — ACCEPT; ATP site 116–124/139 plus Mg(2+) residues.
  • GO:0004713 protein tyrosine kinase activity deliberately NOT proposed. The obvious
    temptation, given the protein's own RecName ("tyrosine- and threonine-specific") and
    the "dual-specificity" phrasing in PMID:9001210. But UniProt states the Tyr-15 activity
    "is unclear and may be indirect", GOA carries no such row, and the human data cannot
    separate direct PKMYT1 Tyr15 phosphorylation from WEE1 activity plus PKMYT1
    autophosphorylation. Raised as suggested_questions[0] and
    suggested_experiments[0] instead of asserted.

The 13 bare GO:0005515 protein binding rows

Every partner is either CDK1 (P06493) or cyclin B1 (P14635) — i.e. the
physiological substrate complex. Policy (annotation-reviewer SKILL, "Quality
Standards"): MODIFY where the cited paper supports an informative MF, otherwise REMOVE as
uninformative, never asserting the interaction is false.

  • The two rows anchored to PMID:10373560 (the only mechanistic primary study) → MODIFY, because that paper genuinely
    resolves the interaction:
  • P06493 → GO:0019901 protein kinase binding (co-IP + C-terminal deletion mapping;
    docking required for efficient Cdc2 phosphorylation).
  • P14635 → GO:0030332 cyclin binding (RXL motif R486/N487/L488 mapped by alanine
    substitution).
  • The remaining 11 rows come from high-throughput surveys (PMID:23397142, PMID:23602568,
    PMID:25852190, PMID:26496610, PMID:28514442, PMID:32707033 ×2, PMID:33961781 ×2,
    PMID:35271311 ×2) → REMOVE as uninformative. The interactions themselves are the
    well-supported physiological ones; the informative molecular functions now live on the
    PMID:10373560 rows. PMID:32707033 is the only screen whose cached full text names
    PKMYT1: PMID:32707033.
  • A protein-kinase-inhibitor-activity term (GO:0004860/GO:0030291) was considered for
    the CDK1 row and rejected: PKMYT1's inhibition of CDK1 is principally catalytic, and the
    binding-only component rests on overproduction experiments.

Cellular component

  • ER membrane (GO:0005789), Golgi membrane (GO:0000139), ER (GO:0005783),
    Golgi apparatus (GO:0005794) — ACCEPT, all four. The parent/child pairs are
    simultaneously true of a peripheral membrane protein, and this mirrors how nucleus and
    nucleoplasm are both accepted on WEE1. The HPA IDA Golgi row (GO_REF:0000052) is an
    independent reproduction of the 1997 immunofluorescence; antibody imaging cannot resolve
    membrane from lumen, so the organelle-level term is right for that evidence.
  • membrane (GO:0016020) ×3 (HDA, IEA, TAS) — KEEP_AS_NON_CORE. True and characteristic
    of PKMYT1, but the uninformative ancestor of the two specific membrane terms. The HDA row
    is from an NK-cell membrane-proteome survey that explicitly expected peripherally
    associated proteins: PMID:19946888.
  • cytoplasm (GO:0005737) ×2 (IBA is_active_in, IEA) — KEEP_AS_NON_CORE. Correct in
    the broad sense (the catalytic and docking faces are cytosolic; the sequestered cyclin
    B1–CDK1 pool is cytoplasmic) but superseded by the membrane terms.
  • nucleus (GO:0005634) ×2 and nucleoplasm (GO:0005654) — REMOVE, the most
    substantive negative calls in this review. Grounds:
  • Endogenous Myt1 "remains membrane-bound throughout the cell cycle" (PMID:9268380).
  • Nuclear accumulation is the phenotype of the membrane-anchor deletion mutant
    (PMID:9001210), not of the wild-type protein.
  • UniProt records only ER membrane and Golgi apparatus membrane for Q99640.
  • The functional contrast with WEE1 — "whereas Wee1 localizes to the nucleus"
    (PMID:10373560) — is the point of the gene.
    The IBA row is a is_active_in nucleus propagation from PTHR11042 node PTN000113601,
    whose characterised descendants include the nuclear WEE1-type kinases and the
    eIF2-alpha kinases; the argument recorded in propagation_review is about node
    placement for the Myt1 sub-branch    , which acquired the C-terminal membrane anchor, not
    about donor count. The IEA row is an Ensembl Compara transfer from mouse Pkmyt1 with no
    human support. The nucleoplasm row is a Reactome compartment assignment on the
    "Inactivation of Myt1 kinase" reaction — the reaction is real, the compartment is not.
  • cytosol (GO:0005829) ×2 (Reactome TAS) — MODIFY → GO:0005789 + GO:0000139. Cytosol
    denotes the aqueous compartment excluding membranes and organelles; PKMYT1 faces the
    cytosol but is attached to membranes.

Biological process

  • GO:0010972 negative regulation of G2/M transition of mitotic cell cycle (IBA + IDA)
    — ACCEPT; this is the central process claim, and both mechanistic arms feed it. Q99640
    appears in its own IBA WITH/FROM list, which is the expected marker of experimental
    grounding on the target, not circularity (IBA_REVIEW guidance).
  • GO:0000079 regulation of cyclin-dependent protein serine/threonine kinase activity
    (TAS) — MODIFY → GO:0045736 (negative regulation …). The direction is known and
    invariant.
  • GO:0007088 regulation of mitotic nuclear division (TAS) — MODIFY → GO:0010972. The
    assays behind the reference are inhibitory Thr14/Tyr15 phosphorylation and a G2 delay;
    effects on nuclear division are downstream consequences of gating CDK1 activation.
  • GO:0000086 G2/M transition (Reactome TAS) — ACCEPT (as on WEE1).
    GO:0000278 mitotic cell cycle (TAS) — KEEP_AS_NON_CORE (top-level parent).
  • GO:0051321 meiotic cell cycle and GO:0110031 negative regulation of G2/MI
    transition of meiotic cell cycle     (both IBA) — KEEP_AS_NON_CORE. Myt1 orthologues do
    hold oocytes in prophase arrest in Xenopus and mouse, and the mechanism is the same
    inhibitory phosphorylation, so the node placements are credible and the rows are kept.
    But there is no human oocyte experiment, and the roles are germline-restricted — so
    non-core rather than ACCEPT. Turned into suggested_questions[2].

4. NEW annotations considered and declined

Per CLAUDE.md's "do not add what curators deliberately declined to add", nothing was added.
Candidates weighed:

  • GO:0004713 protein tyrosine kinase activity — declined; see above. The comparator
    check is instructive: WEE1, the paralogue for which Tyr15 activity is established,
    carries GO:0004713 across many evidence codes, while PKMYT1 carries none in any species.
    That is a curation convention tracking a real evidential difference, not an oversight.
  • A protein-kinase-inhibitor MF for the sequestration arm — declined; captured instead
    as the cyclin binding core function, where the evidence (RXL mapping) actually sits.
  • Anything about the CCNE1-amplification dependency — declined. Synthetic lethality
    establishes that PKMYT1 is necessary in those cells, which is not participation in a
    process; the relevant GO content is already negative regulation of G2/M transition.

5. Core functions

Two activity units, deliberately kept separate because they are separable by mutation:

  1. Catalytic: GO:0004674 on cyclin-bound CDK1 (substrate UniProtKB:P06493), at
    GO:0005789 / GO:0000139, driving GO:0010972 and GO:0045736.
  2. Non-catalytic docking: GO:0030332 cyclin binding via the RXL motif, at the same
    locations, contributing to GO:0010972. Kinase-dead Myt1 retains a partial G2 delay;
    the RXL mutant loses both binding and the delay.

All core-function term ids are either ACCEPTed rows or proposed replacement terms, so
nothing is asserted that the annotation review does not already sanction.
