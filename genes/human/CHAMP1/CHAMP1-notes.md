### Mitotic function and direct molecular evidence

PMID:21063390 was read in genuine full primary text from the newly fetched normal variant while its published abstract-only cache remains untouched. Human endogenous and tagged CHAMP1 localize to chromosomes, spindle and kinetochores. Depletion and rescue establish unstable kinetochore attachments under tension, impaired biorientation and reduced CENP-E/F kinetochore localization without loss of their total protein. Monopolar K-fibre maturation is possible. MAD2L2 spindle localization also depends on CHAMP1 in the original depletion experiment, but that interaction is separable from chromosome alignment.

The FPE region supports mitotic targeting and rescue; the C-terminal region contributes chromosome localization. The primary domain map describes two N-terminal and three C-terminal predicted zinc fingers, not five domains at the C terminus. Broad metal binding is refined to structural zinc binding, acknowledging that neither domain inference nor the present UniProt feature set is a zinc titration assay for every predicted site.

The study reports an unpublished negative microtubule-binding experiment and no recovered direct CENP-E/F association. Those observations do not establish a universal negative, but they prevent an invented physical scaffold or autonomous microtubule-binding MF. The mitotic core therefore retains the supported processes and locations without forcing a molecular-function label. The kinase-inhibitor and phosphosite experiments in this paper support phosphorylation-sensitive mitotic function; they are not attributed to the distinct 2022 HR paper or converted into a direct CDK1 catalytic assay.

### Distinct chromatin mechanism and variant scope

PMID:39962076 describes a CHAMP1–POGZ–HP1α complex separable from REV7 binding. The proposed POGZ interface is predicted and tested by mutant co-IP, not an experimentally measured atomic complex. CHAMP1–HP1α contact remains direct or indirect. Human ALT-cell telomere-damage experiments link the complex to heterochromatin and repair-protein recruitment; mouse NIH3T3 chromatin observations remain a separate context. Reduced foci are not described as global loss of protein expression or all chromatin binding. The participation test does not yet resolve a CHAMP1 molecular function: the paper assigns SETDB1 recruitment to the POGZ subunit, and SETDB1 is the methyltransferase that performs H3K9 methylation. No additional heterochromatin process, GO:0000792 heterochromatin location or autonomous scaffold MF is proposed until the direct CHAMP1 step in this complex is clearer.

PMID:41481470 extends this role to replication stress in human RPE1 and U2OS systems. HU-induced proximity to EdU-labeled nascent DNA, stress-enhanced complex association, SETDB1-dependent H3K9 methylation and DNA-fiber restart/protection assays support a transient chromatin response at stalled forks. The actor mapping still points away from a new CHAMP1 process term: POGZ recruits SETDB1, SETDB1 methylates H3K9 and MRE11 is the nuclease whose excessive fork degradation is restrained. This establishes the complex as necessary for fork protection, but not a CHAMP1-catalyzed or CHAMP1-scaffolded step precise enough for a fourth core function. The protection from excessive processing is distinct from pro-resection REV7 competition at double-strand breaks. The abstract mentions ORC2 recruitment, but the inspected final full main text did not supply that result; it is not propagated here.

PMID:39738383 distinguishes patient cells and engineered U2OS/DLD-1 systems. Truncating alleles fail rescue after endogenous depletion, whereas tested N-terminal missense alleles can retain repair-associated activity. pRPA2 and 53BP1 endpoints are not direct strand-exchange measurements, and not every clinical allele is declared HR-null. The clinical PMID:26340335 abstract supports the neurodevelopmental phenotype without resolving all molecular causes.

### Human myoblast transcriptional coactivation

PMID:41540007 is a genuine January 2026 primary study recovered through the normal fetch workflow. Human CHAMP1-deficient myoblasts retain myogenic differentiation markers but lose fusion; isogenic CHAMP1 rescue or MYMK expression restores fusion. MyoD and CHAMP1 associate by reported co-IP and controlled endogenous PLA. They co-occupy the MYMK enhancer and depend on each other for occupancy without losing nuclear protein expression. The fourth zinc-finger mutant impairs association and fusion after normalization for expression. Human myoblast transplantation is into a mouse host, not evidence that the donor constructs are mouse.

These combined data support a distinct nuclear GO:0003713 transcription coactivator activity, represented by one NEW MF and one core. This is performed transcriptional work rather than necessity alone. Association and reciprocal chromatin occupancy do not establish purified binary contact or intrinsic sequence-specific DNA recognition by CHAMP1; GO:0001228 would overstate the latter. CHAMP1 enables expression of the fusogen MYMK, not membrane fusion itself. No NEW fusion or muscle-development process is added. The official coactivator definition/parent guidance and independent ROOT peer are pinned in the source artifact.

The additional molecular-function proposal is distinct from the conservative NEW process rule and its hierarchy/comparator gate. The HR regulatory process remains the single revised process proposal. All historical machine objects remain unchanged through this new literature addition.
