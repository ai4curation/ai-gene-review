Gene Description

Alpha-2/delta-1, the founding member of the four-gene mammalian alpha-2/delta family and an auxiliary subunit of high-voltage-activated calcium channels. The single precursor is cleaved and disulphide-linked into an extracellular alpha-2 chain and a membrane-attached delta chain, and the mature protein is tethered to the outer face of the plasma membrane through a GPI anchor; its extracellular region carries VWA (with a MIDAS motif) and Cache domains. Assembled with a pore-forming CaV1 or CaV2 alpha-1 subunit and a cytoplasmic CaV-beta subunit, it raises calcium-current density and shifts voltage-dependent activation to more negative potentials, largely by increasing the number of channels at the cell surface rather than by conducting ions itself. It is expressed in skeletal and cardiac muscle T-tubules, in vascular smooth muscle, and broadly in neurons, where it concentrates at presynaptic terminals. It is the high-affinity binding site for the gabapentinoid drugs gabapentin and pregabalin. Biallelic loss-of-function variants cause an early-onset developmental and epileptic encephalopathy, and missense variants have been associated with short-QT and Brugada-like cardiac syndromes. Two further roles are proposed and contested. The first is that alpha-2/delta-1 is the neuronal thrombospondin receptor that drives excitatory synaptogenesis; a conditional triple knockout of presynaptic alpha-2/delta-1, -2 and -3 found synapse development, active-zone ultrastructure and transsynaptic alignment intact and reassigned presynaptic alpha-2/delta to setting synaptic gain through Munc13-1, while astrocyte-conditioned-medium experiments continue to support a postsynaptic thrombospondin-1 receptor role. The second is that alpha-2/delta-1 forms a gabapentin-sensitive complex with NMDA receptors, independently of calcium channels, and promotes their synaptic trafficking in chronic pain; this model rests almost entirely on work from a single laboratory and has not been independently replicated.

Core Functions

Alpha-2/delta-1 is a constitutive auxiliary subunit of high-voltage-activated calcium channel complexes. Assembled with a CaV1 or CaV2 pore-forming alpha-1 subunit and a cytoplasmic CaV-beta subunit, it is required for the complex to produce a normal calcium current. It contributes no pore of its own - the mature protein is a disulphide-linked extracellular alpha-2 chain plus a membrane-attached delta chain, GPI-anchored on the outer face of the plasma membrane - so its participation in conduction is recorded as a contribution to a complex-level activity rather than an activity it independently enables. Deployed in skeletal and cardiac muscle T-tubules and at neuronal presynaptic terminals.

Separately from being counted as part of the conducting complex, alpha-2/delta-1 modulates that complex. It raises peak calcium-current density and shifts voltage-dependent activation to more negative potentials, and it does so principally by increasing the number of channels delivered to and retained at the plasma membrane rather than by changing the unitary behaviour of the alpha-1 subunit. This trafficking-and-gating modulation is the subunit's own contribution, it is what disease variants perturb, and it is the property the 2025 presynaptic triple knockout confirmed while overturning the synaptogenic claims. It is also the reason gabapentinoids, which bind alpha-2/delta-1, act on neuronal excitability despite having little direct effect on calcium current.

Suggested Questions for Experts

Q: Is alpha-2/delta-1 a synaptogenic receptor, and if so on which side of the synapse? The 2009 thrombospondin-receptor claim was specifically postsynaptic, and astrocyte-conditioned-medium experiments published in 2026 still depend on it. The 2025 triple conditional knockout that found synapse development unaffected ablated alpha-2/delta-1, -2 and -3 together and only presynaptically. These two results are compatible, and the unanswered question is what a postsynaptic conditional knockout of alpha-2/delta-1 alone would show.

Q: Does alpha-2/delta-1 form a functional complex with NMDA receptors independently of calcium channels? Essentially all of the primary evidence comes from one laboratory, using two shared reagents (gabapentin and an alpha-2/delta-1 C-terminal interfering peptide) across pain, hypertension and HIV models. No independent replication and no explicit rebuttal was found in the 2025-2026 literature. What is needed is a structural or proximity-labelling demonstration of the interaction from a different group, and a test in Cacna2d1 knock-in mice carrying a C-terminal deletion that spares channel function.

Q: Should the three `enables GO:0005245` rows be harmonised to `contributes_to`? The gene carries the same term with both qualifiers, and the source paper behind one of the enables rows describes the alpha-2 subunit as accessory. Mixed qualifiers on one term make the annotation set internally inconsistent about whether alpha-2/delta-1 is pore-forming.

Q: What observation underlies the `is_active_in GO:0098992 neuronal dense core vesicle` annotation? A GPI-anchored extracellular subunit of a surface channel acting in a secretory vesicle is difficult to interpret, and the qualifier asserts activity rather than mere presence.

Q: Do gabapentinoids act therapeutically by inhibiting alpha-2/delta-1 trafficking of calcium channels, by disrupting a proposed alpha-2/delta-1-NMDAR complex, or by blocking thrombospondin binding? All three mechanisms have been proposed for the same drug-target pair and they imply different molecular functions for the protein.

Suggested Experiments

Experiment: Generate a postsynaptic-restricted, single-gene conditional knockout of Cacna2d1 in cortical or hippocampal neurons and assay excitatory synapse number with and without astrocyte-conditioned medium or purified thrombospondin-1. This is the experiment that the 2025 presynaptic triple knockout explicitly does not perform, and it would test the original 2009 claim on its own terms.

Experiment: Knock in a Cacna2d1 allele lacking the intrinsically disordered C-terminal region proposed to mediate NMDA receptor binding, while leaving the VWA/MIDAS and Cache domains and the GPI anchor intact. If calcium-channel trafficking is normal but NMDAR synaptic trafficking and neuropathic pain behaviour are lost, the two functions are separable; if channel function is also lost, the NMDAR phenotypes reported to date cannot be attributed to a channel-independent complex.

Experiment: Attempt independent replication of the alpha-2/delta-1-NMDAR interaction outside the originating laboratory, using a method that does not rely on co-immunoprecipitation or the C-terminal peptide - for example proximity labelling in spinal dorsal horn tissue, or single-molecule pull-down from native membranes - and quantify what fraction of synaptic GluN1 is associated with alpha-2/delta-1.

Experiment: Measure calcium-channel surface density and current density in parallel for each of the disease-associated CACNA2D1 variants (epileptic encephalopathy and short-QT alleles) in the same cell background, to test whether all pathogenic variants act through the trafficking/regulator function or whether some act only through a proposed non-channel partner.

- question: Is alpha-2/delta-1 a synaptogenic receptor, and if so on which side of the synapse? The
    2009 thrombospondin-receptor claim was specifically postsynaptic, and astrocyte-conditioned-medium
    experiments published in 2026 still depend on it. The 2025 triple conditional knockout that found
    synapse development unaffected ablated alpha-2/delta-1, -2 and -3 together and only presynaptically.

    channels? Essentially all of the primary evidence comes from one laboratory, using two shared
    reagents (gabapentin and an alpha-2/delta-1 C-terminal interfering peptide) across pain, hypertension
    and HIV models. No independent replication and no explicit rebuttal was found in the 2025-2026
    literature. What is needed is a structural or proximity-labelling demonstration of the interaction
    from a different group, and a test in Cacna2d1 knock-in mice carrying a C-terminal deletion that
    spares channel function.
