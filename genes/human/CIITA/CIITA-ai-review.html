Gene Description

CIITA is a nuclear transcriptional coactivator that drives MHC class II and associated antigen-presentation gene expression and can also enhance MHC class I transcription. It associates with promoter-bound regulatory factors, including RFX, and basal transcription machinery rather than recognizing promoter DNA directly. Nucleotide binding and regulated acetylation, phosphorylation and protein stability shape its activity. CIITA has measured protein kinase and histone acetyltransferase capacities. Context-dependent collagen repression and induction of host-defense genes broaden its transcriptional effects, while loss of functional CIITA causes MHC class II deficiency.

Core Functions

CIITA coactivates transcription of MHC and antigen-presentation genes by engaging promoter-bound regulatory factors and basal transcription machinery. Its activity integrates nucleotide binding, protein interactions and regulated acetylation and phosphorylation. CIITA also phosphorylates protein substrates and acetylates histones in vitro; the allocation of these catalytic activities during cellular initiation remains unresolved.

CIITA review notes

Biological synthesis

CIITA supplies transcriptional coactivation rather than sequence-specific promoter recognition. Its interactions with RFX and the basal factor TAF9 link promoter-bound regulatory machinery to MHC transcription. Human complementation, promoter assays and interaction experiments support MHC class II biosynthesis as the dominant role, with positive MHC class I regulation also directly tested in the NLRC5 study. A single organizing coactivator core retains both contexts (PMID:8402893(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8402893/), PMID:9171108(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9171108/), PMID:10938133(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10938133/), PMID:20639463(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20639463/)).

CIITA also has directly measured biochemical capacities. The 2013 study tests phosphorylation of H2B Ser36, TAF7 and RAP74, with matched extract controls, in-gel denaturation/renaturation, a human-cell preparation, contaminant assessment and a TAF1-negative immunoblot. Its Methods, Results and supplementary legends also describe acetyl-CoA-dependent H3/H4 acetylation. These observations support the existing kinase annotations and one new histone acetyltransferase annotation. They do not demonstrate the complete proposed sequence of TAF7 release and transcription initiation in living cells. The original 2001 acetyltransferase article remains abstract-only in available access; the later actual assay supplies independent support (PMID:24036077(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24036077/), PMID:11172716(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11172716/)).

The original GTP-binding study was recovered only as journal page 1402 after three pages of an unrelated article. The page has the matching title, authors and complete Figure 1 legend. In-vitro-translated and immunoprecipitated CIITA is tested for GTP binding with RasQ61L, NF-YA, motif-mutant and protein-expression controls; COS7 nucleotide labeling is a separate assay. Independent review inspected the actual figure pixels. This supports one new GTP-binding MF, without claiming that the uninspected Methods footnotes or later nuclear-import Results were read. Nucleotide labeling alone does not establish GTP-versus-GDP selectivity, and no GTPase or new import-process annotation is proposed (PMID:10464099(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10464099/)).

Both new molecular functions were compared with the original terms using typed ontology ancestry. Neither adds an ancestor or descendant of existing coverage, and the two proposed MFs are not ancestors of one another. No new process is proposed. The exact selected GO-CAM index has no CIITA activity hit; absence is not used as a reason to manufacture process coverage.

Full-length 1130-residue constructs and tested fragments are described at the source’s scope; they are not silently mapped to every current product. The TAF9 refinement does not assert that TAF9 can never contact DNA. ZXDA–ZXDC experiments include complex-dependent association rather than uniformly purified binary affinity, and their zinc fingers or promoter occupancy do not by themselves establish sequence-specific DNA recognition (PMID:16600381(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16600381/), PMID:17493635(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17493635/), PMID:21890893(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21890893/)).

The abstract positively reports CIITA–HDAC2 co-immunoprecipitation, deacetylation, altered promoter recruitment, reporter activity, quantitative PCR and RFX5 association. Those observations support the retained context-dependent repression and binding annotations, with exact product-to-cell assignments left unspecified. HDAC2 antagonizes CIITA; it is not demonstrated to be a recruited enzyme executing CIITA-mediated collagen repression (PMID:19041327(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19041327/)).

PML-body localization is retained as a contextual pool alongside diffuse nuclear CIITA. The PML partner isoform II remains explicit. Selected HPA HEL and U251MG images independently support nuclear enrichment, with an additional HEL cytosolic pool. The antiviral study distinguishes CIITA-dependent transcription from downstream CD74 p41 protease-related effects. The newer HCMV study independently corroborates CIITA-dependent HLA-II expression and shows viral pUS28 antagonism, including reciprocal association after lysate mixing; neither source makes CIITA a direct antiviral protease inhibitor (PMID:32855215(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32855215/), PMID:40608405(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40608405/)).
