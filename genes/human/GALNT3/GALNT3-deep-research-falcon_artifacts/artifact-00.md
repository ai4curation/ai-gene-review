Its short N terminus is cytosolic, while the catalytic and lectin regions face the secretory-pathway lumen; nuclear localization has also been reported in some contexts. (pqac-00000003, pqac-00000029) | Golgi-luminal O-glycosylation is the canonical functional annotation. Non-Golgi observations are context-dependent and do not establish an equally general catalytic role. |
| **General substrate recognition** | de las Rivas et al., *Nature Chemical Biology* (2020), [doi:10.1038/s41589-019-0444-x](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41589-019-0444-x) | GalNAc-T3 has peptide- and glycopeptide-context preferences rather than one strict consensus: highly active test peptides frequently contained Tyr at −3, while the lectin domain recognizes pre-existing GalNAc and promotes modification of neighboring sites. (pqac-00000009, pqac-00000017) | In-vitro peptide preferences do not define the complete cellular substrate repertoire; expression, Golgi access, prior glycosylation, and competing GALNT isoforms also matter. |
| **FGF23 site specificity and priming** | de las Rivas et al., *Nature Chemical Biology* (2020), [doi:10.1038/s41589-019-0444-x](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41589-019-0444-x) | Structural, NMR, and kinetic experiments show recognition of the FGF23 cleavage-site region. A Thr171-glycosylated peptide was modified near Thr178 with catalytic efficiency about 21.3 versus 0.29 for the corresponding unmodified peptide, an approximately 73-fold difference under the assay conditions. (pqac-00000011, pqac-00000012, pqac-00000013) | Some crystallographic work used a highly similar nonhuman GalNAc-T3 ortholog, but comparative binding and kinetics showed closely matching behavior for human GalNAc-T3. |
| **FGF23 stabilization and protease protection** | Tagliabracci et al., *PNAS* (March 2014), [doi:10.1073/pnas.1402218111](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1402218111) | Purified GalNAc-T3 transferred GalNAc specifically to FGF23 Thr178. This modification protects the adjacent R176XXR179–S180 convertase site, favoring intact, biologically active FGF23 rather than inactive fragments. (pqac-00000033, pqac-00000036) | FGF23 is the best-established physiologically decisive substrate, but it is not GALNT3’s only substrate. |
| **Competition with FAM20C phosphorylation** | Tagliabracci et al., *PNAS* (2014), [doi:10.1073/pnas.1402218111](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1402218111) | FAM20C phosphorylates adjacent FGF23 Ser180; phosphorylated FGF23(172–190) showed no detectable GalNAc-T3 glycosylation by MALDI-TOF. Loss of Thr178 glycosylation permits furin cleavage, whereas furin-knockout cells produced only intact FGF23. (pqac-00000032, pqac-00000033, pqac-00000034) | This is reciprocal post-translational control in the secretory pathway, not evidence that GALNT3 directly signals through FAM20C or furin. |
| **Phosphate-sensing pathway** | Takashi et al., *PNAS* (16 May 2019), [doi:10.1073/pnas.1815166116](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1815166116) | High extracellular phosphate activated unliganded FGFR1c–ERK signaling and induced skeletal Galnt3, increasing the intact-to-cleaved FGF23 ratio. Mice received 1.2% versus 0.6% phosphate diets for two weeks with **n=19 per group**. (pqac-00000020) | Evidence combines mouse and osteoblastic-cell experiments. Whether FGFR1 is the sole or universal mammalian phosphate sensor remains unresolved. |
| **Physiological phosphate homeostasis** | Jiang et al., *International Journal of Molecular Sciences* (14 February 2024), [doi:10.3390/ijms25042275](https://doi.org/10.3390/ijms25042275) | By stabilizing osteocyte-derived FGF23, Galnt3 supports renal phosphate excretion and suppression of 1,25-dihydroxyvitamin-D production. Galnt3-null mice had intact FGF23 at about **40% of wild type**, despite approximately threefold higher bone Fgf23 mRNA and approximately 16-fold higher total circulating FGF23 and fragments. (pqac-00000019, pqac-00000021) | Quantitative results are from mice, not human Q14435-null subjects, but support the conserved mechanism established by human genetics and biochemistry. |
| **Transcriptional control in bone** | Jiang et al., *International Journal of Molecular Sciences* (2024), [doi:10.3390/ijms25042275](https://doi.org/10.3390/ijms25042275) | Runx2 overexpression and knockdown respectively increased and decreased Galnt3; promoter-reporter and ChIP experiments supported direct regulation. Osteoblast-lineage Runx2 knockout reduced Galnt3 and Fgf23 expression to approximately half of control. (pqac-00000019, pqac-00000022) | Evidence is predominantly from mice and primary osteoblasts; direct quantitative confirmation in human osteocytes remains limited. |
| **Recent bone-mass genetics** | Hassan et al., *Journal of Bone and Mineral Research* (February 2023), [doi:10.1002/jbmr.4795](https://doi.org/10.1002/jbmr.4795) | Rare heterozygous p.Arg553Trp and p.Asp277Glu variants occurred in two independent high-bone-mass kindreds; modelling predicted protein destabilization and impaired Mn²⁺-dependent catalysis. Common GALNT3 variants were significantly associated with lumbar-spine, femoral-neck, and total-body BMD. (pqac-00000025, pqac-00000026, pqac-00000027) | The proposed FGF23-independent bone-mass role is not mechanistically resolved; rare-variant evidence rests on very few kindreds and structural predictions. |
| **Emerging antiviral substrate** | Wang et al., *Nature Communications* (May 2024), [doi:10.1038/s41467-024-48503-x](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-024-48503-x) | GalNAc-T3 first modified SARS-CoV-2 spike T678, enabling GalNAc-T7 modification at S686 near the S1/S2 furin site. Clustered glycosylation reduced cleavage and particle incorporation; T3/T7 expression reduced Omicron BA.1 titer by **76%** in Calu-3 cells. (pqac-00000040, pqac-00000041) | This is an experimental host–virus mechanism, not an approved therapy. Evidence includes purified peptides, engineered expression, virus-like particles, and cultured lung cells; variant susceptibility differs. |
| **Clinical implementation and management** | Iwasaki, *Endocrinology, Diabetes & Metabolism Case Reports* (October 2023), [doi:10.1530/edm-23-0071](https://doi.org/10.1530/edm-23-0071) | Genome sequencing identified a novel GALNT3 lectin-domain frameshift in a 73-year-old with hyperphosphatemia, low FGF23, and calcinosis. Diet and phosphate-lowering drugs reduced serum phosphate from **6.5 to 5.5 mg/dL after 10 months**, but calcinosis did not improve after more than one year. (pqac-00000028) | Current implementation is molecular diagnosis and phosphate-lowering management; no approved therapy directly restores or selectively modulates GALNT3. Treatment evidence is largely observational. |
