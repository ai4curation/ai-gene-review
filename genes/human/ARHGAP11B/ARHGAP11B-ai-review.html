Gene Description

ARHGAP11B is a 267-residue human-specific protein that arose by partial duplication of the RhoGAP-encoding gene ARHGAP11A on the human lineage after the split from chimpanzee. Its first 220 residues are 98% identical to ARHGAP11A and carry most of a Rho-GAP fold, including the canonical catalytic arginine finger at position 87, but a single C-to-G substitution creates a novel splice donor site that removes 55 nucleotides, shifts the reading frame, and replaces the C-terminal end of the GAP domain with 47 residues found in no other known protein. The result is a protein that keeps the catalytic residue yet has no Rho GTPase-activating activity in cells, and the loss of that activity is not incidental damage: the reconstructed ancestral version, which retains GAP activity, does not do what the modern protein does. Instead of acting on Rho GTPases, ARHGAP11B carries an N-terminal targeting sequence into the mitochondrial matrix, where it binds the inner-membrane ADP/ATP translocases SLC25A4 and SLC25A5 and delays opening of the mitochondrial permeability transition pore. The resulting rise in mitochondrial calcium drives glutaminolysis, the routing of glutamine through glutamate into the tricarboxylic acid cycle, which is the metabolic state that proliferating cells require. In the fetal human neocortex the protein is expressed specifically in apical and basal radial glia and is absent from cortical neurons and cortical plate, and its effect there is to amplify basal progenitors in the subventricular zone, so that more upper-layer neurons are produced. Expressing it in mouse, ferret and marmoset neocortex increases basal progenitor proliferation, enlarges the neocortex and can induce cortical folding, while blocking or deleting it in human cerebral organoids drops basal progenitor levels to those of chimpanzee organoids. ARHGAP11B is therefore best understood not as a Rho regulator but as a mitochondrial metabolic modulator of neural progenitor proliferation, and the substitution that made it one is shared with Neanderthals and Denisovans and fixed in present-day humans.

Core Functions

Mitochondrial restraint of the permeability transition pore. ARHGAP11B is imported into the mitochondrial matrix, where it binds the inner-membrane ADP/ATP translocases SLC25A4 and SLC25A5 and delays opening of the mitochondrial permeability transition pore. The consequence is a raised mitochondrial calcium concentration and induction of glutaminolysis, the routing of glutamine through glutamate into the TCA cycle, which is the metabolic state characteristic of highly proliferative cells. Pharmacological inhibition of ANT function or of mPTP opening reproduces the downstream effect, and removing the protein from mitochondria abolishes it, so the localisation is functionally required rather than incidental. The molecular function is recorded as transmembrane transporter binding because that is what the cited experiment measures; whether ARHGAP11B additionally inhibits ANT-mediated transport, which would license GO:0141110, is raised as a question rather than asserted, since the cached record is abstract-only.

Suggested Questions for Experts

Q: GOA currently ships a direct contradiction for ARHGAP11B: two IDA rows asserting NOT|enables GO:0005096 and one Reactome TAS row asserting enables GO:0005096, plus a Reactome TAS row for GO:0051056 that the NOT|involved_in GO:0043547 row contradicts. And separately, should the GOA pipeline refuse to emit a positive annotation for a term that the same gene already carries as a curated NOT from experimental evidence? The second question generalises well beyond this gene.

Q: Does ARHGAP11B inhibit ANT-mediated adenine nucleotide transport, or does it inhibit mPTP opening without inhibiting transport? PMID:31883789 reports that pharmacological inhibition of ANT function mimics the ARHGAP11B effect, which suggests the former, but the cached record is abstract-only and the two claims are distinct. If transport inhibition is shown in the full text, GO:0141110 transporter inhibitor activity would be a better molecular function than the GO:0044325 transmembrane transporter binding proposed here.

Q: UniProt's Rho-GAP DOMAIN feature on ARHGAP11B is annotated as 49..250, which extends 30 residues past residue 220, the last position homologous to ARHGAP11A, and therefore into the human-specific frameshift C-terminus that is homologous to nothing. The effect is that the annotated domain on the inactive truncated paralog (202 aa) is longer than the annotated domain on the catalytically active parent ARHGAP11A (49..239, 191 aa). Should the ARHGAP11B feature be truncated at the end of homology, and should the interpro2go line from IPR000198 to GO:0007165 be gated on something other than a profile match for family members curated as inactive?

Q: Is there a GO-sanctioned way to state that a protein has retained a catalytic residue while losing the activity? ARHGAP11B keeps the canonical arginine finger at position 87 - UniProt annotates the Site, and it aligns reciprocally to both ARHGAP11A's and ARHGAP1's own annotated fingers - yet has no GAP activity. Pipelines and reviewers that gate catalytic terms on residue presence will get this gene wrong in the permissive direction.

Q: Should ARHGAP36 (Q6ZRI8) be re-examined? It is the reciprocal of ARHGAP11B within the same family: it has a threonine at its own UniProt-annotated arginine-finger position, yet UniProt asserts "GTPase activator for the Rho-type GTPases" for it on ECO:0000250, by similarity. Of the six reviewed human RhoGAP-profile proteins whose annotated finger is not an arginine, only OCRL carries an experimentally supported statement of catalytic inactivity; three are silent. So residue identity and curated activity are decoupled in both directions in this family, and a by-similarity activity claim over a substituted catalytic residue is the same class of defect this review corrects, pointing the other way.

Q: PAINT gave ARHGAP11B nothing - UniProt records zero PAN-GO annotations and GOA contains no IBA row for this gene - even though PANTHER places it in PTHR15670:SF4, a subfamily named "RHO GTPASE-ACTIVATING PROTEIN 11A" that it shares with its active paralog, which does receive GO:0005096 by IBA. That restraint is correct and is worth recording as a positive example: the paralog leak that PAINT avoided arrived through Reactome instead. Is the ARHGAP11B exclusion documented as a deliberate PAINT decision, so that a future re-run of the family does not reinstate it?

Q: Should the neurobehavioural phenotypes of ARHGAP11B-transgenic mice - increased memory flexibility and reduced anxiety (PMID:33938018) - support any annotation on the human gene product? They are several steps downstream of the molecular activity, were measured in mouse, and reflect an enlarged neocortex rather than a direct function, so no annotation is proposed here. Current model (mechanistic narrative)

ARHGAP11B is a human-specific gene that drives evolutionary expansion of the neocortex by amplifying basal progenitor populations during corticogenesis PMID:25721503. It arose from a partial duplication of ARHGAP11A on the human lineage, and a single C→G substitution created a novel splice donor site that truncates the RhoGAP domain—abolishing GTPase-activating activity—and appends a unique human-specific C-terminus; both the loss of RhoGAP activity and the gain of the novel C-terminal sequence are required for its progenitor-amplifying function PMID:27957544. Mechanistically, ARHGAP11B is imported into mitochondria, where it binds the adenine nucleotide translocase (ANT) and inhibits the mitochondrial permeability transition pore (mPTP), establishing a glutaminolysis-dependent metabolic state required for basal progenitor expansion; pharmacological inhibition of ANT or mPTP phenocopies its effect PMID:31883789. Expression of ARHGAP11B increases proliferative basal radial glia, extends neurogenesis, and produces supernumerary upper-layer neurons with neocortical enlargement and folding across mouse, ferret, marmoset, and primate organoid systems [PMID:30484771, PMID:32554627, PMID:36098218], and at physiological levels it enlarges the neocortex with persistent cognitive-behavioural consequences PMID:33938018. ARHGAP11B is both necessary and sufficient for the elevated basal progenitor levels that distinguish human from chimpanzee cortex, a role its ancestral paralog ARHGAP11A cannot fulfill PMID:36098218.

4. What the truncation actually removes

The GAP:GTPase interface is computed from PDB 1TX4 (chain A = RHG01_HUMAN, chain B = RHOA_HUMAN), a transition-state mimic containing ALF, GDP, MG. Chain roles are proved, not assumed: chain A is 99.5% identical to Q07960 and chain B is 99.4% identical to P61586. Contacts are every GAP residue with an atom within 4.5 A of RhoA or of the nucleotide/metal/fluoride.

Sanity check on the geometry: the calculation recovers the control's own annotated arginine finger [282] among its contacts, as it must.
