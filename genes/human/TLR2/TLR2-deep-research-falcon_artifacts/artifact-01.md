| Signaling component/step | Molecular players | Function/role | Downstream effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Extracellular ligand recognition | TLR2 extracellular leucine-rich-repeat domain; microbial lipoproteins/lipopeptides; accessory receptors CD14, CD36, CD44, and integrins | Detects extracellular pathogen-associated molecular patterns; accessory receptors can capture or present ligands to TLR2 complexes. TLR2 is a pattern-recognition signaling receptor, not an enzyme, so ligands are recognized rather than chemically converted. (pqac-00000006, pqac-00000024) | Initiates ligand-dependent receptor assembly and determines signaling strength and specificity. |
| 2. Partner-specific recognition | TLR2–TLR1; TLR2–TLR6 | TLR2–TLR1 preferentially recognizes triacylated lipopeptides such as Pam3CSK4, whereas TLR2–TLR6 recognizes diacylated lipopeptides such as Pam2CSK4, FSL-1, and MALP-2. (pqac-00000003, pqac-00000025, pqac-00000027) | Broadens microbial recognition while discriminating lipid-acylation patterns. |
| 3. Receptor dimerization | TLR2 with TLR1 or TLR6; context-dependent TLR2–TLR10, TLR2–TLR4, CLEC2D–TLR2, and possible TLR2 homodimers | Ligand binding stabilizes extracellular receptor association and brings cytoplasmic TIR domains into proximity. TLR2–TLR1 and TLR2–TLR6 are best established; physiological TLR2 homodimer signaling remains controversial. (pqac-00000024, pqac-00000025, pqac-00000030) | Creates an intracellular docking platform for signaling adaptors. |
| 4. Sorting-adaptor recruitment | TIRAP/MAL; phosphoinositide-rich plasma-membrane regions | TIRAP binds the activated receptor complex and facilitates recruitment of MyD88. TIRAP loss impairs cytokine production and delays, rather than necessarily abolishes, NF-κB activation. (pqac-00000017, pqac-00000018) | Enables efficient spatial assembly of the proximal TLR2 signaling complex. |
| 5. Myddosome assembly | MyD88, IRAK4, IRAK1, IRAK2 | MyD88 provides a death-domain scaffold that recruits IRAK kinases; IRAK4 activates IRAK1/2 within the myddosome. TLR2 signaling is principally MyD88-dependent rather than TRIF-dependent. (pqac-00000017, pqac-00000021, pqac-00000022) | Converts receptor engagement into an amplified kinase and ubiquitin-signaling cascade. |
| 6. TRAF6 activation and ubiquitin scaffolding | IRAK1/2, TRAF6, K63-linked polyubiquitin, NEMO/IKKγ | Activated IRAKs engage TRAF6; TRAF6-dependent K63-linked ubiquitination creates non-degradative signaling scaffolds and promotes NEMO ubiquitination. (pqac-00000017, pqac-00000020) | Recruits and activates TAK1 and the IKK/MAPK signaling modules. |
| 7. TAK1 activation | TAK1/MAP3K7, TAB2, TAB3 | TAB proteins recognize ubiquitin scaffolds and support TAK1 activation, forming a branch point between NF-κB and MAPK signaling. (pqac-00000017, pqac-00000021) | Activates the IKK complex and MAPK cascades. |
| 8. Canonical NF-κB pathway | IKKα, IKKβ, NEMO, IκB, NF-κB RelA/p50 | IKK-mediated phosphorylation targets IκB for degradation, releasing NF-κB for nuclear translocation. (pqac-00000017, pqac-00000019) | Induces inflammatory genes, including TNF, IL6, IL1B, chemokines, adhesion molecules, and other innate-defense mediators. |
| 9. MAPK and AP-1 pathways | p38, JNK, ERK1/2, AP-1, MEK1/2, TPL2/MAP3K8 | TAK1 and TPL2 activate MAPK branches; MAPKs regulate AP-1 and cooperate with NF-κB in inflammatory transcription, cellular activation, survival, and proliferation. (pqac-00000016, pqac-00000018, pqac-00000021) | Promotes cytokine and chemokine production and alters activation, survival, migration, and inflammatory phenotype. |
| 10. Alternative PI3K–AKT signaling | PI3K, AKT, RAC1, ERK1/2 | Particularly reported downstream of TLR2–TLR6; intersects with RAC1 and ERK signaling and can modify inflammatory, survival, migration, and cell-type-specific responses. (pqac-00000016) | Context-dependent modulation of NF-κB/MAPK output, cell survival, cytoskeletal behavior, adhesion, and migration. |
| 11. Compartment-dependent signaling | Internalized TLR2; early and late endosomes, lysosomes, Rab11-positive compartments | Although TLR2 functions primarily at the plasma membrane, intracellular pools can generate qualitatively different responses. Endosomal TLR2 has been associated with regulatory signaling and, in some infection contexts, interferon-related responses. (pqac-00000013, pqac-00000020) | Produces context-dependent antimicrobial, regulatory, or anti-inflammatory outputs, including TLR2-dependent IL-10 in some infections. |
| 12. Principal inflammatory outputs | TNF-α, IL-1α/β, IL-6, IL-8/CXCL8, IL-12, CXCL10, and other chemokines | NF-κB, AP-1, and MAPKs drive a coordinated inflammatory transcriptional program. The exact output depends on ligand, dimer partner, cell type, co-receptors, and cellular compartment. (pqac-00000023, pqac-00000033, pqac-00000038) | Supports leukocyte recruitment and activation, antimicrobial defense, dendritic-cell maturation, endothelial activation, and coordination of adaptive immunity. |
| 13. Regulatory and anti-inflammatory outputs | IL-10, IFN-γ; soluble or extracellular-vesicle-associated TLR2 | Certain ligands and compartments produce regulatory rather than classically inflammatory signaling; soluble TLR2 may sequester ligand or act as a decoy. (pqac-00000013, pqac-00000033, pqac-00000045) | Promotes immune tolerance or suppression, limits excessive inflammation, and modulates host–microbiota interactions. |
| 14. Functional cellular consequences | Monocytes, macrophages, dendritic cells, epithelial and endothelial cells, platelets | TLR2 signaling influences cytokine production, antimicrobial peptide expression, antigen presentation, monocyte adhesion and migration, endothelial-barrier disruption, coagulation, and platelet–leukocyte interactions. (pqac-00000039, pqac-00000045, pqac-00000049) | Supports pathogen defense when controlled but can cause chronic inflammation, vascular injury, thrombosis, or tissue damage when excessive or persistent. |


*Table: Stepwise summary of canonical and alternative TLR2 signaling from ligand recognition and receptor dimerization through MyD88–IRAK–TRAF6 signaling, transcription-factor activation, and inflammatory or regulatory outputs.*
