Gene Description

CLDN14 encodes claudin-14, a four-pass membrane protein that forms structural strands of epithelial tight junctions. Its self-association and interactions with other claudins restrict paracellular permeability, supporting the inner-ear barrier and regulated renal calcium handling. In model junctions, claudin-14 can form a barrier on its own and replace claudin-16 within claudin-19-containing strands. Its cytoplasmic C-terminal motif binds PDZ domains. Human loss-of-function variants cause recessive nonsyndromic deafness, while renal expression is responsive to calcium-sensing pathways.

Core Functions

Claudin-14 supplies a polymerizing membrane component of bicellular tight junctions. Homomeric strands and context-dependent copolymers form a barrier to paracellular passage; changes in strand composition couple its abundance to epithelial permeability.

Biological synthesis

CLDN14 is a structural claudin of epithelial tight junctions. Its membrane strands restrict paracellular permeability, and its C-terminal motif recognizes PDZ domains. Human deafness genetics establishes clinical relevance, while mechanistic studies distinguish inner-ear barrier organization from regulated renal claudin composition. One structural molecular-function core covers the performed junctional work. No new annotation is proposed.

The older renal study uses mouse CLDN14 with human CLDN16 and CLDN19. Membrane two-hybrid and extract coimmunoprecipitation show self-association and CLDN16 association under those conditions; transepithelial measurements show reduced cation permeability. This is not a purified human CLDN14 binding or ion-channel assay PMID:22373575. The newer study resolves CLDN14-containing strands, copolymerization with CLDN19 and replacement of CLDN16. In a septuple-claudin knockout MDCKII background, CLDN14 expression restores a restrictive barrier. These observations establish structural participation in assembly. The actual supplement describes synthetic tagged CLDN14 constructs but does not specify their species or accession, so host-cell identity is not used to claim human transgene identity PMID:41325524.

The two studies probe different geometries and expression contexts. The 2012 association model is retained as an actual observation rather than declared false because the 2025 study resolves a different strand arrangement. Native mouse renal observations support changing claudin composition; the tested dynamin perturbation does not exclude every endocytic mechanism. A barrier that restricts permeability is not assigned the selective paracellular channel activity term merely because it regulates flux. Current structural descendants contain no appropriate barrier-specific molecular-function child.

Selected original cochlear Methods and Results distinguish mouse cDNA-derived GFP constructs from endogenous mouse, rat and guinea-pig localization. CLDN14 occupies an apical junctional subdomain and forms parallel strands in COS-7 cells. The native partner across the opposing cell membrane was unresolved; CLDN14 did not recruit the tested catenins in that model PMID:17130295. Human deafness and mouse-loss phenotypes contextualize this structural role without generating additional developmental or hearing-process assertions [PMID:11163249; PMID:12913076]. The authentic 2005 variant abstract reports distinct assembly defects for V85D and G101R, but its full Methods were not recovered PMID:15880785.
