# GNB5: signaling-regulator evidence review

All original annotation payloads, evidence codes, references and qualifiers were preserved. No new annotations were manufactured to populate a module. Actual Falcon and fallback research attempts failed authentication/quota; this is manual literature research, not provider-generated prose.

The whole record was reviewed, including secondary physiology and localization. Existing assertion specificity is judged independently of evidence code. Native, heterologous, mutant and orthology-based results are distinguished below.

## GO:0005096 GTPase activator activity — ACCEPT
Gbeta5 contributes to GAP activity by stabilizing and organizing its R7 RGS partner. Purified RGS6–Gbeta5 complexes and functional analysis of human GNB5 variants support this complex-dependent activity; Gbeta5 is not assigned an independent GTP-hydrolyzing catalytic site.
Sources: PMID:10521509, PMID:27677260

## GO:0005515 protein binding — REMOVE
The physical interactions are not denied; remove uninformative bare protein binding under the project policy while retaining the specific gamma-subunit, chaperone and GAP-complex functions.
Source handling: original payload retained; no fabricated supporting quote.

## GO:0005634 nucleus — KEEP_AS_NON_CORE
R7BP-dependent trafficking experiments support a nuclear R7–Gbeta5 pool, conditional on partner localization and palmitoylation. The mouse evidence supports a conserved contextual human assignment; it does not imply constitutive nuclear residence in every cell.
Sources: PMID:15897264

## GO:0005737 cytoplasm — ACCEPT
Gbeta5 is an intracellular soluble protein whose RGS complexes have a cytoplasmic/cytosolic pool before or alongside recruitment to membranes. Purification and partner-dependent localization experiments support these compartments.
Sources: PMID:10521509, PMID:15897264

## GO:0005829 cytosol — ACCEPT

## GO:0005834 heterotrimeric G-protein complex — KEEP_AS_NON_CORE
Retain the ancestral heterotrimer membership assertion as bounded biochemical capacity. PMID9417126 reconstitutes Gbeta5–gamma2HF with Gq, whereas PMID25733868 finds no beta5 assemblies with Gi1 or GsL in its screen. The latter is not a universal negative. Neither result makes Gbeta5 interchangeable with beta1–4 in native signaling or redefines an RGS–Gbeta5 dimer as an alpha–beta–gamma heterotrimer.
Sources: PMID:9417126, PMID:25733868

## GO:0005886 plasma membrane — ACCEPT
R7-complex recruitment and immunolocalization establish a plasma-membrane-associated Gbeta5 pool. Its association depends on partners and does not require Gbeta5 itself to have a membrane-spanning domain.
Sources: PMID:15897264, PMID:23804514

## GO:0007165 signal transduction — MODIFY
Gbeta5 has a defined role in GPCR signal regulation through R7 RGS complexes. Refine the generic signal-transduction term to the existing GPCR process rather than retain it merely because it is broad.
Sources: PMID:10521509, PMID:27677260

## GO:0007186 G protein-coupled receptor signaling pathway — ACCEPT
Gbeta5 contributes structural and regulatory work to R7 GAP complexes that terminate GPCR signaling. This is direct participation despite not itself being the guanine-nucleotide-hydrolyzing subunit.
Sources: PMID:10521509, PMID:27677260, PMID:20884879

## GO:0007212 G protein-coupled dopamine receptor signaling pathway — KEEP_AS_NON_CORE
The RGS9/Gbeta5 dopamine-receptor assay in PMID27677260 demonstrates defective termination with a human disease-associated GNB5 variant. Retain dopamine signaling as a specific implementation of the broader R7-complex function; the target itself among PAINT descendant evidence is legitimate experimental grounding.
Sources: PMID:27677260

## GO:0016020 membrane — MODIFY
Partner-mediated plasma-membrane recruitment is experimentally established and already annotated. Refine the broad membrane term to plasma membrane; this does not assert that all Gbeta5 is membrane-bound or that other membrane pools are absent.
Sources: PMID:15897264, PMID:23804514

## GO:0030159 signaling receptor complex adaptor activity — ACCEPT
Gbeta5 supports assembly and stabilization of R7 RGS signaling complexes. Structural and biochemical evidence for receptor-associated RGS7–Gbeta5 supports the adaptor/scaffold role without claiming Gbeta5 is an autonomous receptor or enzymatic G alpha subunit.
Sources: PMID:34815401, PMID:10521509

## GO:0030425 dendrite — KEEP_AS_NON_CORE
Mouse retinal immunofluorescence in PMID25997951 localizes Gbeta5-containing signaling machinery to ON-bipolar dendritic tips, with cell-type-dependent loss in Lrit3 mutants. Retain this specific neuronal localization and its conserved human inference; it is not cone-photoreceptor outer-segment evidence.
Sources: PMID:25997951

## GO:0031682 G-protein gamma-subunit binding — KEEP_AS_NON_CORE
Direct FRET experiments support Gbeta5 association with gamma2 and gamma13. The same study finds that a CFP tag creates gamma3 binding absent for untagged gamma3, so retain gamma-subunit binding as a selective capability rather than universal affinity for every gamma isoform.
Sources: PMID:12606627

## GO:0045202 synapse — KEEP_AS_NON_CORE
Independent immunolocalization studies place Gbeta5 complexes at neuronal synaptic sites. This supports synapse localization even though the abstract of the separate SNARE proteomics paper does not identify Gbeta5-specific results; no specific SNARE-binding mechanism is inferred from that abstract.
Sources: PMID:21766168, PMID:27965545

## GO:0051087 protein-folding chaperone binding — KEEP_AS_NON_CORE
Gbeta5 interacts with the folding/assembly machinery during RGS-complex biogenesis. The chaperone-binding assignment describes Gbeta5 as a client/assembly partner, not as a chaperone that catalyzes folding itself.
Sources: PMID:19376773

## GO:0120199 cone photoreceptor outer segment — KEEP_AS_NON_CORE
Publisher full text of PMID11262419 was recovered and inspected. Methods explicitly stain Gb5 with an antibody recognizing the long/short products, and Fig. 2 compares Gb5 in PNA-labeled cones of wild-type and Rgs9-null mouse retina. This directly supports the mouse cone outer-segment assignment and a qualified conserved human inference; it is not inferred solely from rod or RGS9 localization.
Sources: PMID:11262419, url:https://www.molvis.org/molvis/v7/a11/

## GO:0003924 GTPase activity — MODIFY
Gbeta5 supports the RGS GAP complex but is not itself a guanine-nucleotide-binding GTPase. The original cloning citation cannot convert a beta subunit into the G alpha catalytic component; refine to the independently demonstrated GAP contribution.
Sources: PMID:10521509, PMID:27677260

## GO:1902773 GTPase activator complex — ACCEPT
Gbeta5 is a physical and functional component of R7 RGS GAP complexes, including the RGS9 complex tested in the human variant study. This specific complex role is central to its molecular biology.
Sources: PMID:10521509, PMID:27677260
