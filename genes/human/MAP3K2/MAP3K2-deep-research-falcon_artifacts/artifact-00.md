| Identity and synonyms | Human **MAP3K2** encodes mitogen-activated protein kinase kinase kinase 2, also called **MEKK2**; UniProt **Q9Y2U5**. It is a STE11-family Ser/Thr MAP3K. MAP3K3/MEKK3 and MAP3K20 are distinct proteins. | Concordant human protein annotation and peer-reviewed literature (pqac-00000001, pqac-00000004) | **High.** Symbol, protein name, organism, and family are concordant. |
| Architecture | The canonical protein is **619 aa** and contains an **N-terminal PB1 interaction domain**, an intervening regulatory region, and a **C-terminal STE-family protein-kinase domain**. | Sequence/domain annotation plus structural and biochemical studies (pqac-00000001, pqac-00000003) | **High** for length and PB1–kinase architecture. Exact regulatory-region boundaries may vary among resources or isoforms. |
| Catalytic function | ATP-dependent protein kinase that transfers the γ-phosphate of ATP to Ser/Thr residues on protein substrates: **ATP + protein-OH → ADP + phosphoprotein**. | Kinase-family annotation supported by purified-protein and human-cell phosphorylation assays (pqac-00000002, pqac-00000006) | **High.** Robust catalytic constants were not available in the retrieved evidence. |
| Direct substrate: MEK5/MAP2K5 | MEKK2 binds and phosphorylates MEK5, activating the **MEK5–ERK5** signaling module. | PB1 mutagenesis, co-immunoprecipitation, pathway inhibition, and human HEK293T phosphorylation assays (pqac-00000002, pqac-00000004) | **High.** MEK5 is one of the best-established physiological MAP2K substrates. |
| Direct substrate: MKK6/MAP2K6 | MEKK2 directly phosphorylates MKK6, including activation-loop **Ser207**, supporting downstream p38 signaling. | Purified-protein kinase assays and HEK293T experiments; assays used 10 nM MEKK2, 500 nM MKK6, 1 mM ATP, and a 10-minute incubation at 30 °C (pqac-00000002, pqac-00000006) | **High** for biochemical and cellular phosphorylation. Its physiological importance is likely context dependent. |
| Substrate recognition | **MEK5 recruitment is PB1–PB1 dependent**. MKK6 lacks a PB1 domain and relies more strongly on the kinase-domain **αG-helix interface**. Both substrates require αG-mediated productive engagement. | Domain deletion and point-mutant studies, split-luciferase interaction assays, phosphorylation assays, and structural analysis (pqac-00000001, pqac-00000002, pqac-00000004) | **High.** MKK6 phosphorylation experiments used *N*=5 and gave ANOVA *p*≤0.0001; interaction assays used *N*=25 and gave *p*≤0.0001 (pqac-00000002). |
| Activation mechanism | Kinase-domain dimerization promotes **trans-autophosphorylation** and activation. The αG helix and C-terminal activation segment contribute to the dimerization interface. | Human HEK293T mutagenesis and trans-phosphorylation experiments plus structural and biochemical analysis (pqac-00000001, pqac-00000004) | **High.** Activation-loop numbering is reported as Ser519 or Ser520 in different sources, so sequence and isoform conventions must be checked. |
| SMYD3 regulation | SMYD3-mediated methylation of **Lys260** can enhance oncogenic MAP3K2 signaling, reportedly by opposing PP2A-mediated negative control in Ras-driven cancer models. | Cancer-cell and animal-model findings summarized in recent mechanistic sources (pqac-00000010, pqac-00000013) | **Moderate to high** mechanistic confidence. Disease dependence and therapeutic generalizability remain preclinical. |
| 14-3-3 regulation | Phosphorylated **Thr283** recruits 14-3-3, restricting MEKK2 homodimerization and activation-loop phosphorylation. T283A disrupts binding and alters JNK, IL-6, ERK, and proliferation outputs. | Site-directed mutagenesis and cellular-signaling studies summarized in mechanistic literature (pqac-00000010, pqac-00000013) | **Moderate.** Effects are context dependent and do not establish uniform inhibition of every downstream branch. |
| Ubiquitin-dependent turnover | **NEDD4L** directly binds, polyubiquitinates, and promotes degradation of MEKK2. This supplies negative feedback following IL-17-induced activation-loop phosphorylation and limits p38 and NF-κB inflammatory signaling. | Cell-based assays and Nedd4l-deficient mouse inflammation and EAE models (pqac-00000016) | **High** for pathway regulation. Much of the organism-level evidence is murine rather than human clinical evidence. |
| Localization | MAP3K2 is predominantly described as a **cytoplasmic intracellular signaling kinase**. Activation-dependent redistribution or nuclear access has been reported, but localization appears dynamic and context dependent. | Cellular annotation and secondary synthesis rather than definitive endogenous spatial mapping (pqac-00000003, pqac-00000008) | **Moderate to low.** An exclusive compartment should not be assigned without stronger endogenous imaging or fractionation evidence. |
| Principal signaling pathways | The best-supported axis is **MEKK2 → MEK5 → ERK5**. Direct MKK6 phosphorylation supports **p38** signaling. Context-dependent evidence also connects MEKK2 to **MKK7/JNK** and inflammatory **NF-κB** outputs. | Direct substrate assays for ERK5 and p38 branches; older biochemical, genetic, and inflammatory models for JNK and NF-κB (pqac-00000002, pqac-00000005, pqac-00000016) | **High** for MEK5–ERK5 and direct MKK6 phosphorylation; **moderate and context dependent** for JNK and NF-κB. Other proposed substrates are not equally established. |
| Translational status | MAP3K2 is an experimental target in cancer, inflammation, cardiovascular stress, and immune signaling. A **2.4 Å** ponatinib-bound kinase-domain structure demonstrates inhibitor-binding feasibility, but no validated MAP3K2-selective clinical therapy was identified. | Preclinical cell and animal studies, structural pharmacology, and target–disease database evidence (pqac-00000000, pqac-00000001, pqac-00000004) | **Preclinical.** Ponatinib is not MAP3K2-selective; database associations are hypothesis-generating and do not establish clinical efficacy or causality. |
