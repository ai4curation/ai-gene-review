## 1. Identity verification

Literature consistently equates **MAP3K2 with MEKK2** and classifies it in the STE11 subfamily of serine/threonine MAP3Ks. Recent structural work describes a 619-amino-acid protein with an N-terminal PB1 domain and C-terminal catalytic domain separated by roughly 200 residues, fully concordant with Q9Y2U5 and the supplied PB1/kinase-domain annotations. MAP3K3/MEKK3 is a close paralogue, but it is a different gene and was not treated as the target here. (vish2025structuralbasisfor pages 1-2, vish2025structuralbasisfor pages 2-3)

Some secondary sources report inconsistent apparent molecular masses, including approximately 69.7 or 94 kDa. Sequence-defined length and accession are therefore safer identifiers than apparent gel mobility or older tabular mass estimates. (ibrahim2025thefunctionalrole pages 31-34, keshet2010themapkinase pages 5-7)

## 2. Primary molecular function and catalytic reaction

MAP3K2 is an **ATP-dependent protein serine/threonine kinase**. Its generic reaction is:

**ATP + protein–OH → ADP + protein–O–phosphate**

As a MAP3K, its physiologically relevant acceptors are principally activation-loop serine/threonine residues in downstream MAP2Ks. The best-supported direct substrates in the retrieved evidence are **MEK5** and **MKK6**. In purified-protein assays, 10 nM MEKK2 phosphorylated 500 nM MKK6 at Ser207 in the presence of 1 mM ATP during a 10-minute reaction at 30 °C; a D483N kinase-inactive mutant served as a catalytic control. Human HEK293T experiments independently detected MEKK2-dependent MEK5 Ser311/Thr315 and MKK6 Ser207 phosphorylation. (vish2025structuralbasisfor pages 7-8, vish2025structuralbasisfor pages 10-11)

Older literature also assigns MKK4/MKK7, MEK1/2, IKK and other proteins to MAP3K2-dependent signaling. These should not all be annotated as equally established direct physiological substrates: some relationships derive from overexpression, in-vitro assays, pathway readouts, or secondary reviews rather than endogenous substrate validation. The most defensible core annotation is therefore **MEK5 and MKK6 kinase**, with context-dependent participation in JNK and NF-κB branches. (ibrahim2025thefunctionalrole pages 31-34, thiriet2013mitogenactivatedproteinkinase pages 25-29)

## 3. Substrate specificity and structural mechanism

MAP3K2 specificity depends substantially on docking and assembly rather than solely on catalytic-site peptide preference.

* **MEK5 recognition:** the PB1 domain of MAP3K2 heterodimerizes with the MEK5 PB1 domain. Deletion or mutation of the MAP3K2 PB1 domain disrupts the complex, and isolated competing PB1 domains selectively inhibit ERK5 activation. This creates an organized MEKK2–MEK5–ERK5 module.
* **MKK6 recognition:** MKK6 lacks a PB1 domain and is engaged mainly through kinase-domain surfaces, especially the **αG helix**.
* **Shared productive interface:** αG-helix mutations in MAP3K2, MEK5, or MKK6 impair phosphorylation. For MKK6, phosphorylation experiments used five independent replicates and gave ANOVA *p*≤0.0001 (*F*=40.8); interaction assays used 25 measurements and gave *p*≤0.0001 (*F*=15.12). (vish2025structuralbasisfor pages 1-2, vish2025structuralbasisfor pages 7-8, vish2025structuralbasisfor pages 2-3)

Thus, PB1-mediated recruitment confers particularly strong pathway specificity for MEK5–ERK5, whereas αG-mediated contacts support catalytic engagement of both MEK5 and MKK6.

## 4. Activation and regulatory mechanisms

### Dimerization and autophosphorylation

Kinase-domain dimerization promotes MAP3K2 trans-autophosphorylation. Mutations affecting the αG-helix/activation-segment interface eliminate phosphorylation-dependent mobility shifts in HEK293T cells, while co-expression experiments support phosphorylation in trans. Recent structural analysis identified a conserved dimerization surface centered on the αG helix and C-terminal activation segment. Activation-loop numbering appears as Ser519 or Ser520 among sources, probably reflecting sequence/isoform conventions; annotations should preserve the source-specific numbering. (vish2025structuralbasisfor pages 1-2, vish2025structuralbasisfor pages 2-3)

### Methylation and phosphatase control

In Ras-driven cancer models, the methyltransferase **SMYD3 methylates MAP3K2 Lys260**. This modification has been proposed to prevent PP2A-mediated negative regulation, sustaining MAP3K2-to-MEK1/2 signaling. The mechanism is experimentally important but remains context dependent rather than a universal basal function of MAP3K2. (corridore2024roleofmekk2 pages 23-28, corridore2024roleofmekk2a pages 23-28)

### 14-3-3 restraint

Phosphorylated **Thr283** recruits 14-3-3 proteins. Binding limits MAP3K2 homodimerization and activation-loop phosphorylation. A T283A substitution disrupts 14-3-3 interaction and changes JNK, IL-6, ERK and proliferation outputs, indicating that the site regulates pathway allocation as well as overall activity. (corridore2024roleofmekk2 pages 23-28, corridore2024roleofmekk2a pages 23-28)

### Ubiquitin-dependent shutdown

A 2022 EMBO Reports study identified **NEDD4L** as a negative regulator that directly binds, polyubiquitinates and promotes degradation of MEKK2. In IL-17 receptor signaling, activation-loop phosphorylation was required both for downstream p38/NF-κB activation and NEDD4L-dependent signal termination. Nedd4l deficiency enhanced inflammatory signaling and aggravated experimental autoimmune encephalomyelitis in mice. This is strong mechanistic evidence, although organism-level disease evidence is predominantly murine. Publication: September 2022; DOI: https://doi.org/10.15252/embr.202254603. (li2022e3ubiquitinligase pages 17-17)

## 5. Cellular location

MAP3K2 is best described as a **predominantly cytoplasmic intracellular signaling kinase** rather than a secreted, membrane-spanning, or structural protein. Its PB1-dependent complexes and kinase cascades are assembled in the cytoplasmic signaling compartment. Secondary synthesis also reports activation-dependent nuclear entry, but the retrieved literature did not provide equally strong endogenous imaging or fractionation evidence. Accordingly, “cytoplasm, with potentially dynamic stimulus-dependent redistribution” is more defensible than an exclusive nuclear or organellar assignment. (ibrahim2025thefunctionalrole pages 31-34, thiriet2013mitogenactivatedproteinkinase pages 14-17)

## 6. Principal pathways and biological processes

### MEK5–ERK5

The highest-confidence signaling route is:

**MAP3K2/MEKK2 → MAP2K5/MEK5 → MAPK7/ERK5**

PB1-domain assembly makes this a particularly coherent module. ERK5 signaling responds to mitogens and stressors such as cytokines, shear stress, hyperosmolarity and oxidative stress and contributes to survival, differentiation and mechanoadaptation. However, MEKK3 and other upstream kinases can also activate MEK5; downstream ERK5 activity should not automatically be attributed exclusively to MAP3K2. (vish2025structuralbasisfor pages 1-2, thiriet2013mitogenactivatedproteinkinase pages 25-29)

### MKK6–p38

Direct phosphorylation of MKK6 provides a mechanistic route from MAP3K2 to p38 MAPKs. This branch is prominent in inflammatory and stress responses, including IL-17 signaling, where MAP3K2 contributes to p38 and NF-κB activation before NEDD4L-dependent termination. (vish2025structuralbasisfor pages 7-8, vish2025structuralbasisfor pages 10-11, li2022e3ubiquitinligase pages 17-17)

### MKK7–JNK and NF-κB

MAP3K2 has context-dependent links to MKK7/JNK and IKK/NF-κB signaling, including cytokine, immune-cell and stress models. Evidence supports biological participation, but direct-substrate and pathway dominance vary by cell type. These branches should therefore be annotated as supported secondary pathways, not as invariant outputs. (ibrahim2025thefunctionalrole pages 31-34, thiriet2013mitogenactivatedproteinkinase pages 25-29, li2022e3ubiquitinligase pages 17-17)

## 7. Recent research and disease relevance, 2023–2024

Recent work has focused less on redefining catalytic function and more on regulatory RNAs, cancer dependencies and inflammatory control. Studies reported MAP3K2-associated regulatory axes in hypoxic human AC16 cardiomyocytes and esophageal squamous-cell carcinoma, while ovarian-cancer work used MAP3K2 knockdown as part of investigating MEK5–ERK5 pathway dependence. These are preclinical model systems, not evidence of an approved biomarker or therapy.

A 2023 review emphasized MAP3K-family control of interferon and NF-κB signaling in antiviral immunity, but family-level conclusions should not be converted automatically into MAP3K2-specific mechanisms. A 2024 study of SMYD3 phosphorylation/methylation regulation further explored Thr263, Thr283 and Lys260, although the retrieved source was a thesis-like document with incomplete journal metadata and should be weighted below fully peer-reviewed primary studies. (corridore2024roleofmekk2a pages 106-109, corridore2024roleofmekk2 pages 23-28)

The most consequential recent mechanistic advance actually retrieved was later structural work: a **2.4 Å ponatinib-bound MAP3K2 kinase-domain structure** and direct dissection of MEK5/MKK6 recognition. It was accepted in November 2025 and therefore lies outside the requested 2023–2024 priority window, but it materially updates current understanding. PDB **9P6A**; DOI: https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-025-66884-5. (vish2025structuralbasisfor pages 1-2, vish2025structuralbasisfor pages 2-3, vish2025structuralbasisfor pages 10-11)

## 8. Applications and translational status

MAP3K2 currently has three principal research applications:

1. **Pathway dissection:** PB1 and αG mutants are used to distinguish MEK5–ERK5 from MKK6–p38 signaling.
2. **Disease-model intervention:** knockdown, CRISPR and regulatory-miRNA experiments test roles in cancer, inflammatory signaling and cellular stress.
3. **Drug discovery:** the ponatinib-bound structure demonstrates that the catalytic pocket is ligandable, while the SMYD3–MAP3K2 interface offers an indirect intervention strategy. Ponatinib is not a selective MAP3K2 inhibitor, however, and inhibition of SMYD3 affects many chromatin and nonhistone substrates. (vish2025structuralbasisfor pages 1-2, vish2025structuralbasisfor pages 2-3, corridore2024roleofmekk2 pages 23-28)

No validated MAP3K2-selective clinical therapy or established diagnostic use was identified. Open Targets lists comparatively modest associations with venous thromboembolism (score 0.322), neurodegenerative disease (0.303), deep-vein thrombosis (0.271), thrombophilia (0.253) and cutaneous lupus erythematosus (0.229). These are evidence-aggregation scores—not effect sizes, prevalence estimates, or proof of causality—and are best treated as hypothesis-generating. (OpenTargets Search: -MAP3K2)

## 9. Expert interpretation and recommended annotation

The evidence supports the following conservative functional statement:

> **MAP3K2/MEKK2 is a predominantly cytoplasmic STE-family serine/threonine MAP kinase kinase kinase that undergoes dimerization-dependent autophosphorylation and phosphorylates MAP2Ks. Its best-established substrates are MEK5, recruited through PB1-domain interactions to activate ERK5, and MKK6, engaged through kinase-domain αG surfaces to activate p38. Its activity is tuned by phosphorylation, 14-3-3 binding, SMYD3-mediated Lys260 methylation and ubiquitin-dependent turnover, enabling context-dependent ERK5, p38, JNK and NF-κB signaling.**

Confidence is **high** for identity, domain architecture, kinase activity, MEK5/MKK6 phosphorylation, PB1-dependent MEK5 assembly and dimerization/autophosphorylation. Confidence is **moderate and context dependent** for broad substrate lists, nuclear localization, pathway dominance in specific diseases and therapeutic tractability. Most disease applications remain preclinical, and the absence of selective clinical inhibitors limits causal pharmacological validation.
