Gene Description

CDC45 is a nuclear replication factor that forms the CMG helicase with the MCM2–7 motor and the GINS complex. It contributes the architecture needed for productive DNA unwinding and remains associated with the replication machinery during DNA synthesis. Purified human CDC45 also binds single-stranded and branched DNA. Its RecJ-related fold has lost the conserved nuclease active site, and ATP-driven unwinding is a property of the assembled CMG complex. Biallelic hypomorphic CDC45 variants cause Meier-Gorlin syndrome and craniosynostosis, linking impaired replication capacity to developmental growth defects.

Core Functions

CDC45 binds single-stranded DNA and supplies structural interfaces that organize the nuclear CMG replication helicase. Together with GINS it supports the MCM2–7 motor during genome duplication; ATP-driven unwinding belongs to the assembled complex.

CDC45 manual research and annotation review

CDC45 is the structural replication factor in the human CDC45–MCM2–7–GINS (CMG) helicase. The MCM ring supplies ATP-driven translocation/unwinding; CDC45 and GINS organize its active architecture. The core therefore combines CDC45's experimentally supported ssDNA binding with a contributes_to DNA-helicase activity and CMG membership. This does not assign independent helicase, ATPase or nuclease catalysis to CDC45. All 43 source assertions, their qualifiers and partners, the three alternative products and the original 25 reference identities/titles are preserved. Six normally fetched primary sources provide corroboration; no NEW annotation is proposed.

Primary evidence and scope

  • PMID:22474384, human CMG biochemistry: the normal publication cache is abstract-only, but authentic external full HTML was inspected for Results/Figures 1–3 and 6 and Methods. Eleven human subunits were expressed in Sf9 cells and purified as CMG. ATPase/helicase assays and rolling-circle replication with human polymerase epsilon test the complex. HeLa chromatin co-immunoprecipitation and active preparations from 293 cells corroborate human complex membership. These are neither CDC45-alone catalysis nor yeast proteins mislabeled as human.
  • PMID:24293646, purified human CDC45 DNA binding: selected full Results/Figures 4–7 and expression/EMSA methods establish binding by full-length, C-terminal His6-tagged human CDC45 expressed in bacteria and assayed purified in vitro. A brief provenance anchor is “hCdc45 displayed a preference for 3' protruding strands”. The study also tests ssDNA length dependence and branched substrates. Its passive, ATP-independent strand destabilization is distinct from GO:0003678, whose definition requires ATP-driven unwinding. The tested substrates do not independently establish origin-sequence specificity.
  • PMID:27189187, CDC45 structure: selected full structural Results and Methods distinguish the human crystal construct (internal deletion 154–164), docking into fly CMG, and Xenopus interface-mutant replication/chromatin experiments in egg extracts. Conserved MCM/GINS interfaces support an architectural role. Loss of the conserved RecJ catalytic motifs prevents transferring nuclease activity from the ancestral fold.
  • PMID:32453425, human CMG/CMGA structures: selected full Results and HEK293-F expression/purification, fork-DNA and cryoEM methods show human CMG bound to DNA/ATPγS and the AND-1 interface. DNA and nucleotide contacts in MCM2–7 distinguish the motor from CDC45/GINS architecture. Structural recruitment of AND-1 does not make CDC45 a polymerase or a general signaling adaptor.
  • PMID:35585232, human replisome reconstitution: selected full minimal-replisome Results/Figure 1 and replication-reaction Methods show CMG-dependent leading-strand DNA synthesis. This establishes direct participation of the replication assembly, rather than interpreting a knockout phenotype as the biochemical mechanism.
  • PMID:27374770, Mendelian disease: the authentic abstract describes biallelic splice/substitution variants and reduced full-length transcript/protein in patient cells. This supports hypomorphic Meier-Gorlin syndrome/craniosynostosis. Full experimental Methods were not recovered; no exact mutant rescue, numerical activity deficit or alternative-product equivalence is asserted.
