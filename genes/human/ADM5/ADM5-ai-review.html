Gene Description

ADM5 is the human member of the adrenomedullin-5 branch of the calcitonin gene-related peptide (CGRP)/adrenomedullin peptide family, and in humans it is almost certainly a non-functional remnant rather than a working hormone gene. In vertebrates that retain it, AM5 is a secreted prohormone whose mature peptide sits in the middle of the precursor, is closed by an intramolecular disulfide ring and is C-terminally alpha-amidated; it lowers arterial pressure and total peripheral resistance and raises cardiac output and plasma renin activity when given peripherally, while central administration is vasopressor and drives oxytocin release. In teleost fish and amphibians AM5 signals through the calcitonin receptor-like receptor with RAMP3, but the mammalian peptide activates no CLR or CTR/RAMP combination, though plasma cAMP does rise on AM5 infusion in vivo and one study argues it may after all signal through the same receptors as AM and AM2, so the mammalian receptor assignment is unsettled rather than simply unknown. The human gene is the exception within the family: a nucleotide deletion in the mature AM5 coding sequence occurred in anthropoids after divergence from the rhesus monkey, and the human protein, while retaining an N-terminal signal peptide and the disulfide ring cysteines, has lost the glycine-dibasic amidation signal that every active peptide of this family requires - it contains no such motif anywhere, whereas the functional pig orthologue has two. Human ADM5 is therefore best understood as a degenerate prohormone locus: still transcribed and still carrying a secretion signal, but unable to yield an amidated adrenomedullin-family peptide. Its functional paralogues ADM and ADM2 carry out the corresponding physiology in humans.

Suggested Questions for Experts

Q: Should human ADM5 be reclassified as a pseudogene or unitary pseudogene? UniProt already calls it a probable non-functional remnant and gives it protein existence at transcript level only, but HGNC and Ensembl still treat it as protein-coding, and it retains seven GO annotations describing hormone physiology.

Q: Is the ADM5 transcript translated at all in human tissues, and is any product detectable by mass spectrometry? The signal peptide and disulfide ring are intact, so a secreted but inactive polypeptide is possible, and that is the only scenario in which the extracellular-region annotations retained here have real content.

Q: What is the mammalian AM5 receptor, and is the adrenomedullin receptor pathway term right for AM5 at all? The evidence genuinely conflicts: AM5 activated no CLR or CTR/RAMP combination in transfected cells, yet plasma cAMP rises on infusion and the heart-failure study argues AM5 may signal through the same receptors as AM and AM2. This review takes no position on it, because the human removal does not depend on one.

Q: Should the IBD be moved down from PANTHER node PTN000602075 to the existing ADM, ADM2 and ADM5 subfamily nodes? The family already resolves into subfamilies (ADM5 is SF6 alone in the PTHR23414 entries), so the fix is probably re-placing the ancestral assertion at the subfamily level rather than splitting the family. Propagating ADM and ADM2 physiology across a node that also contains a degenerate ADM5 is what produced six unsupportable annotations here, and it will recur for ADM5 in other anthropoids.

Current model (mechanistic narrative)

ADM5 (adrenomedullin 5) is an evolutionarily ancient member of the calcitonin gene-related peptide (CGRP)/adrenomedullin peptide family that functions as a secreted regulator of cardiovascular, fluid, and neuroendocrine homeostasis [PMID:17092606, PMID:21436721]. The mature peptide is encoded in the middle of its prohormone and carries signals for intramolecular ring formation and C-terminal amidation characteristic of this peptide family PMID:18434369. In non-mammalian vertebrates, AM5 signals through the calcitonin receptor-like receptor complexed with RAMP3 (CLR-RAMP3): this complex generates cAMP in response to AM5 in teleost fish PMID:16195494 and drives CRE-luciferase reporter activity in amphibian receptor-expressing cells PMID:33711314. In mammals, AM5 produces a vasodepressor response and reduced peripheral resistance with increased heart rate, cardiac output, renin activity, and natriuresis when infused peripherally, including in heart failure models, whereas central (intracerebroventricular) administration is vasopressor and activates oxytocin-secreting neurons of the hypothalamic supraoptic and paraventricular nuclei to raise plasma oxytocin [PMID:18434369, PMID:19420012, PMID:21436721, PMID:22087608]. The mammalian receptor for AM5 remains unidentified: no combination of CLR or CTR with RAMP1–3 responds to AM5 in cell-based cAMP assays, and central oxytocin induction is only partially blocked by CGRP and AM receptor antagonists, indicating signaling through an as-yet-undefined receptor [PMID:18434369, PMID:19420012]. AM5 is differentially expressed across tissues including spleen, thymus, liver, and kidney, and its expression in fish is modulated by salinity, consistent with a role in osmoregulation [PMID:18434369, PMID:26605057].

Does human ADM5 retain what a functional adrenomedullin-family peptide needs?

Run 2026-09-26. Script: analyze_adm5.py (no dependencies; fetches live from the UniProt
REST API and computes the alignment — nothing is hardcoded). Raw output: run-output.txt.

Why this was asked

Human ADM5 (C9JUS6) carries seven IBA annotations asserting hormone activity, adrenomedullin
receptor signalling, and regulation of blood pressure, heart rate and urine volume — all
propagated from the ancestral AM5 node. But UniProt's own function line for the human entry reads
"Probable non-functional remnant of adrenomedullin-5", and every functional AM5 experiment in the
literature is in pufferfish, medaka, Xenopus, pig, rat or sheep. The question is whether the human
protein could execute the propagated functions at all.

Reference: pig ADM5 (A5LHG2), the reviewed mammalian ortholog with an experimentally
characterised, feature-annotated mature peptide.

What a functional peptide of this family requires

  1. The intramolecular disulfide ring (C-x-x-x-x-C).
  2. C-terminal alpha-amidation — encoded in the precursor as the amidated residue followed by
    a glycine donor and a dibasic cleavage site (X-G-[KR][KR]). Amidation is required for
    receptor activation across the CGRP/adrenomedullin family, so losing the signal abolishes
    hormone activity no matter what else survives.

Reading

The ring survived; the amidation signal did not. That combination is the informative one. A
retained disulfide ring is the kind of feature that keeps a degenerate sequence looking plausible
to similarity- and phylogeny-based methods — it is why the IBA node reaches this gene at all —
while the feature that actually licenses receptor activation is gone.

The single most robust line in the table is the last one, because it does not depend on the
alignment: scanning each sequence independently, pig contains two G[KR][KR] motifs and human
contains none. The projected position of pig Tyr77 onto human Pro92 is alignment-dependent and
should be treated as indicative only — the offset is large, and the two proteins have clearly
diverged in that region (consistent with the 45-residue C-terminal extension in human, the shape
a frameshift leaves).

This was reached independently of UniProt's curated judgment and agrees with it.

Limitations, stated plainly

  • Two sequences only. This is a targeted feature check against one functional reference, not a
    phylogenetic reconstruction of when AM5 was lost in the primate lineage.
  • Absence of a G[KR][KR] motif is strong evidence against amidation but is a motif argument,
    not a measurement; nobody has assayed the human peptide.
  • The analysis cannot exclude that human ADM5 acquired some unrelated function, only that it
    cannot act as an amidated adrenomedullin-family hormone.
  • It says nothing about whether the locus is transcribed. UniProt lists human ADM5 at protein
    existence level PE=2 (evidence at transcript level).
