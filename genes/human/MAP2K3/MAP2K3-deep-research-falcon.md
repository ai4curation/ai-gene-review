# Human MAP2K3 (MKK3; UniProt P46734): Functional Annotation Report

## Executive summary

The requested target is correctly identified. **MAP2K3** is the approved human gene symbol for **mitogen-activated protein kinase kinase 3**, commonly called **MKK3** or **MEK3**; the target corresponds to human **UniProt P46734** and Ensembl **ENSG00000034152**. Its aliases and classification as a STE-family dual-specificity protein kinase agree with the supplied record. No evidence was used from the unrelated plant gene sometimes also called *MAP2K3*.

MAP2K3 is an intracellular, ATP-dependent signaling enzyme whose principal established function is to phosphorylate the threonine and tyrosine residues in the **TGY activation loop of p38-family MAP kinases**. The clearest substrate-level evidence concerns p38α/MAPK14, phosphorylated at **Thr180 and Tyr182**, with additional evidence for p38β. MKK3 therefore occupies the middle tier of a canonical **MAP3K → MKK3 → p38** stress-signaling module. It acts in both cytoplasm and nucleus and converts oxidative, osmotic, inflammatory, genotoxic, hypoxic and ischemic inputs into context-dependent stress-adaptation, inflammatory, differentiation, apoptotic and proliferation responses. MKK6 overlaps substantially with MKK3, so neither kinase should be treated as universally indispensable or absolutely isoform-specific. (roux2004erkandp38 pages 2-3, maikrachline2021alternativesplicingof pages 5-6, moralesmartinez2024p38moleculartargeting pages 2-4)

### Family and domain consistency

MAP2K3 belongs to the MAP kinase kinase branch of the **STE protein-kinase group**. The supplied InterPro/Pfam annotations—protein-kinase domain, kinase-like fold, ATP-binding site and serine/threonine-kinase active-site signature—are exactly what is expected for an MAP2K. “Dual specificity” refers to its ability to phosphorylate both a threonine and a tyrosine in the activation loop of its MAPK substrate; it does not mean that MKK3 broadly phosphorylates arbitrary serine/threonine and tyrosine substrates.

MKK3 and MKK6 are closely related—approximately 80% similar at the primary-sequence level in one review—and both recognize p38 TGY motifs, although stimulus responses and p38-isoform preferences differ. Two substantially expressed MKK3 splice isoforms have been described, with the principal isoform generally more abundant than MKK3b and relative expression varying by cell type. (maikrachline2021alternativesplicingof pages 5-6)

## 2. Primary biochemical function

### Catalyzed reaction

The functional reaction can be represented as:

**ATP + p38-MAPK → ADP + dual-phosphorylated, activated p38-MAPK**

More specifically, MKK3 transfers ATP-derived phosphate to the threonine and tyrosine of the p38-family **TGY activation motif**. In p38α/MAPK14 these sites are **Thr180 and Tyr182**. Phosphorylation remodels the p38 activation loop and increases its catalytic activity toward downstream protein substrates. (maikrachline2021alternativesplicingof pages 5-6, moralesmartinez2024p38moleculartargeting pages 2-4)

This is a phosphorylation reaction, not a transport or structural function. MKK3 is therefore best annotated as a **stress-responsive p38 MAPK activator**.

### Substrate specificity

The strongest conclusion is that MKK3 is selective at the MAPK-family level for **p38 MAPKs**, rather than ERK1/2 or JNK. Established reviews place MKK3 and MKK6 as the principal p38-directed MAP2Ks and report high specificity for p38 over those other MAPK branches. (roux2004erkandp38 pages 2-3)

Within the p38 family, the evidence supports:

- **p38α/MAPK14:** strong evidence; activation-loop Thr180/Tyr182 are phosphorylated.
- **p38β/MAPK11:** supported as an MKK3 substrate in pathway reviews.
- **p38γ/MAPK12 and p38δ/MAPK13:** literature commonly reports differential activation by MKK3 and MKK6, but isoform preference is stimulus- and cell-dependent. The evidence retrieved here is insufficient to assign an absolute rule.

Consequently, annotations such as “MKK3 activates only p38α” or “MKK3 activates every p38 isoform equivalently” would both be too strong. Docking interactions, scaffold proteins, relative MKK3/MKK6 abundance, compartment and stimulus help determine effective specificity. MKK3 and MKK6 can preferentially—but not exclusively—activate different p38 isoforms. (maikrachline2021alternativesplicingof pages 5-6)

### Activation of MKK3

MKK3 itself is activated by upstream MAP3Ks through activation-loop phosphorylation. In human-cell experiments, phosphorylation at **Thr222** was used as an activation-associated readout. Leukemia-derived MEK3 mutants displayed reduced pThr222 together with reduced phosphorylation of p38 Thr180/Tyr182, supporting a functional connection between MKK3 activation state and downstream p38 activation. These assays establish Thr222 as an experimentally informative regulatory site, although they do not imply that it is the only regulatory event affecting MKK3. (ayalamarin2021quadrupleandtruncated pages 8-10, ayalamarin2021quadrupleandtruncated pages 10-12)

### Recognition and structural basis

The catalytic domain provides the conventional bilobal protein-kinase architecture and ATP-binding machinery. Specificity additionally depends on non-catalytic docking contacts. Recent proteome-scale work on p38α docking showed that p38-selective interactions favor Lys-rich basic motifs that complement acidic residues in the p38 docking groove; MAP2K3 and MAP2K6 contain N-terminal basic docking regions consistent with this recognition principle. This 2023 result refines understanding of how MAPK cascades preserve signaling fidelity despite structurally similar kinase active sites. However, it explains p38 docking generally rather than supplying a complete atomic structure of full-length MKK3 bound to p38.

## 3. Position in signaling pathways

### Canonical pathway architecture

MAP2K3 occupies the central kinase tier:

**stress/cytokine receptor or sensor → MAP3K → MKK3 → p38 MAPK → downstream kinases, transcription factors and other substrates**

Reported upstream MAP3Ks include **MEKK1–4, MLK2/3, DLK, ASK1, Tpl2/Cot and TAK1**. Which MAP3K engages MKK3 depends on cell type, scaffold organization and initiating signal. Established inputs include oxidative stress, UV irradiation, hypoxia, ischemia, physical or chemical stress, and inflammatory cytokines such as **IL-1 and TNF-α**. (roux2004erkandp38 pages 2-3)

The pathway should not be viewed as a simple isolated linear chain. MKK6 provides overlapping p38 activation; MKK4 can contribute in some contexts; scaffolds spatially organize complexes; phosphatases terminate signaling; and cross-talk occurs with ERK, JNK, PI3K–AKT, NF-κB and other networks.

### Downstream biological processes

Through p38, MAP2K3 contributes to:

- cellular adaptation to oxidative, osmotic, hypoxic and genotoxic stress;
- inflammatory cytokine production and innate-immune signaling;
- cell-cycle arrest, survival or apoptosis, depending on context;
- differentiation programs, including musculoskeletal lineages;
- migration, cytoskeletal responses and tissue remodeling;
- transcriptional and post-transcriptional regulation through downstream p38 substrates.

These are pathway outcomes rather than independent catalytic activities of MKK3. Their direction is context dependent: p38 signaling can suppress proliferation in one cellular state yet support survival, invasion or therapy resistance in another. Expert reviews emphasize this signal-, duration-, isoform- and cell-type dependence rather than assigning p38 signaling a universally pro- or anti-apoptotic role. (roux2004erkandp38 pages 2-3, maikrachline2021alternativesplicingof pages 5-6)

## 4. Cellular localization

MAP2K3 is a soluble intracellular kinase and has been detected in **both cytoplasm and nucleus**. This permits it to connect cytoplasmic stress-sensing complexes with p38 pools in either compartment. Localization is dynamic rather than fixed; protein interactions and stimulation can alter compartmental distribution.

Context-specific observations include predominantly cytoplasmic MAP2K3 staining in glioma-related cells and tissues, while earlier cell-biological work and mutant-expression experiments found MKK3/MEK3 in both nuclear and cytoplasmic compartments. The correct annotation is therefore “cytoplasm and nucleus, context dependent,” not an exclusively nuclear or exclusively cytosolic location. In the 2024 glioma analysis, Human Protein Atlas staining placed MAP2K3 in the cytoplasm of the examined SH-SY5Y cell model. (pu2024exploringmap2k3as pages 3-4)

MKK3’s location must also be distinguished from that of its substrate. Activated p38 can redistribute between compartments and can be exported through complexes with downstream proteins such as MK2; p38 localization should not automatically be assigned to MKK3.

## 5. Experimental evidence beyond pathway annotation

### Disease-associated mutant experiments

A 2021 study investigated four substitutions—**P11T, R26T, R65L and R67W**—and a truncating **Q73\*** variant identified in an acute-lymphoblastic-leukemia sequence set. R65L and R67W were near the ATP-binding region, whereas P11T and R26T were in the amino-terminal region. Expressed in HEK293 cells, the quadruple mutant was unstable and underwent enhanced degradation; the Q73-truncated protein underwent ubiquitin-independent proteasomal degradation. Both mutant forms showed reduced MKK3 activation and reduced downstream p38 phosphorylation. (ayalamarin2021quadrupleandtruncated pages 1-2)

Phospho-specific assays measured MKK3 pThr222 and p38 pThr180/pTyr182. Mutants showed weaker enzymatic signaling and less suppression of HEK293-cell viability than wild-type MKK3, consistent with loss of a growth-restraining MKK3–p38 output in that experimental system. This is mechanistic evidence from engineered cell expression, not proof that these rare variants are recurrent clinical drivers or actionable biomarkers in leukemia. (ayalamarin2021quadrupleandtruncated pages 8-10, ayalamarin2021quadrupleandtruncated pages 10-12)

### Non-canonical interactions

An experimentally reported extension beyond canonical p38 signaling is a direct MKK3–MYC protein interaction. The cited study mapped a short MKK3 MYC-binding motif distinct from known p38/upstream-kinase interaction sites and reported stabilization and increased transcriptional activity of MYC. This proposes that MAP2K3 may act as a protein-interaction regulator in addition to phosphorylating p38. Because the non-canonical model has a smaller validation base than the MKK3–p38 reaction, it should be regarded as a supported but less-established function rather than the primary annotation.

## 6. Recent developments, 2023–2024

### 2023: signaling specificity through docking motifs

A 2023 PNAS study screened thousands of human-proteome-derived sequences against p38α and ERK2. It found distinct motifs for selective versus promiscuous docking; p38α selectivity correlated with increased net positive charge and Lys enrichment, mediated by acidic residues unique to the p38α docking groove. The N-terminal basic regions of MAP2K3 and MAP2K6 fit this emerging specificity model. This work strengthens the mechanistic view that MAP2K-to-MAPK fidelity is determined by docking interfaces in addition to active-site chemistry.

### 2024: glioma biomarker study

A June 2024 Frontiers in Neurology study integrated TCGA, CGGA, GEO, Human Protein Atlas and single-cell data with limited functional assays. MAP2K3 transcript and protein abundance increased with glioma grade, and higher expression was associated with shorter overall and progression-free survival. Multivariable analyses treated MAP2K3 as an independent prognostic predictor. A reported prognostic model yielded time-dependent AUCs of **0.89, 0.92 and 0.92 at 1, 3 and 5 years**, respectively. (pu2024exploringmap2k3as pages 14-15, pu2024exploringmap2k3as pages 3-4)

High-MAP2K3 tumors had higher immune and stromal scores, lower estimated tumor purity and increased expression of seven reported checkpoint-associated molecules, including **PD-L1/CD274, PDCD1, PDCD1LG2 and CTLA4**. They also showed increased immunosuppressive populations such as T regulatory cells, MDSCs, Th2 cells and M2 macrophages, alongside some effector populations. (pu2024exploringmap2k3as pages 6-10)

Interpretation requires caution. Most evidence was retrospective and computational, and associations between MAP2K3 expression, immune infiltration and outcome do not establish that MKK3 drives glioma progression. Although high expression was associated with possible benefit in several anti-PD-1 cohorts, results were not uniformly significant and were not derived from prospective glioma trials. Functional invasion assays used three replicates and reported p < 0.001, but that limited experiment does not validate clinical utility. (pu2024exploringmap2k3as pages 14-15)

### 2024: therapeutic interest in the p38 pathway

A 2024 review of p38 targeting in multiple myeloma described p38 signaling as relevant to inflammatory cytokines, tumor survival and treatment resistance and identified MKK3 as one upstream activator of p38α/β. The translational activity is primarily directed at p38 or downstream pathway outputs rather than clinically validated MKK3-selective inhibition. The review is useful for pathway context but does not provide direct MAP2K3-specific clinical evidence. (moralesmartinez2024p38moleculartargeting pages 2-4)

## 7. Current and potential applications

### Research applications

MAP2K3 is currently useful as:

1. **A pathway-activity node:** MKK3 pThr222 and p38 pThr180/Tyr182 can be measured to test stress-pathway engagement.
2. **A perturbation target:** knockout, knockdown, constitutively active constructs and kinase-impaired mutants distinguish the MKK3 arm from MKK6 and other MAP2Ks.
3. **A mechanistic bridge:** it connects particular MAP3Ks and stress sensors to p38-dependent transcription, inflammatory output or cell-fate decisions.
4. **A candidate biomarker:** expression and phospho-state are being explored in cancers, inflammatory conditions and stress-related pathologies.

### Therapeutic status

No MAP2K3-selective drug, approved therapy or validated companion diagnostic was identified in the reviewed evidence. Therapeutic work has more commonly targeted p38α itself, upstream MAP3Ks or downstream effectors. This has practical limitations: ATP sites are conserved across kinases, MKK3 and MKK6 are partially redundant, and systemic p38-pathway inhibition can disrupt both pathological and homeostatic stress responses.

Open Targets lists MAP2K3 associations with broad phenotypes including skeletal, neurodegenerative, immune and infectious conditions, but the displayed scores are moderate—approximately **0.31–0.46**—and several associations share the same small evidence sets. These database statistics are hypothesis-generating, not evidence of approved use or causal disease involvement. (OpenTargets Search: -MAP2K3)

More selective future strategies may target a disease-specific docking interface or scaffolded complex rather than the conserved ATP pocket. The reported MKK3–MYC interface is one experimental example of this concept, but it remains preclinical.

## 8. Evidence-weighted interpretation

**High-confidence annotation:** MAP2K3/P46734 is human MKK3, a STE-family dual-specificity MAP2K whose primary biochemical role is ATP-dependent activation of p38 MAPKs through dual phosphorylation of their TGY activation loop. It functions intracellularly in cytoplasmic and nuclear stress-signaling complexes. (roux2004erkandp38 pages 2-3, maikrachline2021alternativesplicingof pages 5-6, moralesmartinez2024p38moleculartargeting pages 2-4)

**Moderate-confidence refinements:** MKK3 and MKK6 have overlapping but non-identical stimulus and p38-isoform preferences; MKK3 localization and biological output vary by cell state; Thr222 is a useful activation-associated phosphosite. **Emerging or context-specific conclusions:** MAP2K3 expression may have prognostic and immunological relevance in glioma, and MKK3 may regulate MYC through direct binding. ## Conclusion

The most defensible functional annotation is: **human MAP2K3 encodes MKK3, an intracellular dual-specificity MAP kinase kinase that receives stress- and cytokine-dependent signals from MAP3Ks and activates p38-family MAPKs by phosphorylating the threonine and tyrosine of their TGY activation motif.** Its clearest reaction is activation of p38α at Thr180/Tyr182. It acts in cytoplasm and nucleus, with localization, isoform preference and biological consequences determined by stimulus, scaffold, cell type and competition or redundancy with MKK6. Recent research has refined MAPK docking specificity and identified potentially useful cancer associations, especially in glioma, but MAP2K3 remains a preclinical research target rather than a clinically established drug target or biomarker.
