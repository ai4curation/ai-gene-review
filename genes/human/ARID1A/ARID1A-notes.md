## PR 3367 follow-up: functional synthesis and source boundaries — 2026-09-28

The preceding map and validation counts describe the initial published review. The follow-up preserves all 86 machine-sourced assertions, three products, raw inputs, normal reference caches and published history. Its decisions use the individual biological claims, not a target percentage of uncertainty. The published file contains 19 generic protein-binding rows: six with inspected positive association evidence and 13 with unresolved source-specific pair evidence.

Human p270/ARID1A contacts DNA through an ARID domain without an established AT-rich preference. The direct fragment experiments distinguish this property from sequence-selective ARID proteins; DNA association alone does not make ARID1A a sequence-specific transcription factor. Human p270 purification and hOsa1 peptide/immunoblot identification place the protein within SWI/SNF-A, while the related PBAF preparation is distinct ([PMID:15640446](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15640446/), [PMID:9584200](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9584200/), [PMID:12200431](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12200431/)).

The original PMID:8895581 Results and Figure 7 caption report ATP-dependent nucleosome disruption and improved GAL4-VP16 access by purified human YT complexes. The authors state: “Both activities require the presence of hydrolyzable ATP”. This establishes activity of the complex; identified human p270/hOsa1 membership in later studies supplies the ARID1A attribution, without assigning ATP hydrolysis to ARID1A. The source was read through an [original author copy](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/14310576_Purification_and_biochemical_heterogeneity_of_the_mammalian_SWI-SNF_complex), specifically the purification/remodeling Results, Figure 7 caption and targeted nuclear-extract/immunoaffinity Methods. Figure pixels and complete Methods were not inspected. The normal cache remains abstract-only. ARID1A supplies DNA, structural and recruitment contacts; SMARCA2/4 provides the ATPase. The original ordinary `enables` qualifier is preserved, and the core retains explicit contribution to the assembled remodeling activity.

The original PMID:12200431 human-clone Methods and Figures 3–5 and 8 distinguish native HeLa association, reciprocal C33A coprecipitation and GST/translated-fragment binding to BRG1 or BRM. Figures 9 and 11 show receptor-dependent activation by human hOsa1 in T47D cells; its isolated C terminus does not reproduce the activation. Rat GR and human ER/AR reagents are distinguished. These experiments support coactivator function within SWI/SNF rather than sequence-specific DNA recognition by ARID1A. These targeted Methods, Results and captions were read in the [original study](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/11188855_Largest_Subunits_of_the_Human_SWISNF_Chromatin-remodeling_Complex_Promote_Transcriptional_Activation_by_Steroid_Hormone_Receptors); this does not mean all paper sections or image pixels were inspected. The unchanged normal record is abstract-only. The historical human clone is not automatically equated with a present-day isoform. DNA contact and receptor-dependent coactivation are represented as distinct, cooperating molecular functions, using existing annotations and no new process assertion.

Transcriptional recruitment is not invariably activating. Endogenous HIC1/ARID1A association and promoter ChIP-reChIP support recruitment of an ARID1A-containing repressive complex at E2F1. HIC1 is a sequence-specific repressor, so the inspected interaction can be refined to DNA-binding transcription-factor binding; the co-IP result does not establish a purified binary interface. In human DIV24 neural cultures, reciprocal ASCL1/ARID1A co-IP supports the more specific bHLH transcription-factor-binding term. Other-subunit fetal PLA panels and complex-wide pioneer experiments are not silently transferred to ARID1A ([PMID:19486893](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19486893/), [PMID:36931659](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36931659/)).

The TOP2A study maps positive BAF250a association to the full-length and N-terminal constructs and reports loss of BAF–TOP2A association after BAF250a depletion. These observations justify enzyme-binding refinement while retaining the unresolved species/accession of the particular heterologous BAF250a construct and the mouse MEF scope of the perturbation work ([PMID:23698369](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23698369/)). The current [GO:0044547 page](https://amigo.geneontology.org/amigo/term/GO:0044547) has a general topoisomerase-binding definition but also lists RO:0004009 to type 1B topoisomerase PR:000016542. That unresolved text/axiom mismatch motivates the unambiguous enzyme-binding parent for TOP2A; it is not evidence against the observed association.

Broad chromatin, DNA-binding, remodeling, coactivation, transcription-regulation and complex-membership annotations are accepted where independent human evidence establishes the same function. Their reasons explicitly retain the original reference and any unrecovered historical assay or target derivation. This differs from declaring an individual uninspected pair table or a specialized neuronal assembly verified. The 13 source-specific interaction uncertainties remain unresolved; known BRG1/BRM associations are acknowledged separately. Genericity alone does not satisfy the user's REMOVE criterion, and no such removals are made. Specialized assembly, repair and developmental assertions remain bounded by their actual evidence.

The unrelated PMID:17340523 remains WRONG_IDENTIFIER and its original annotation remains UNDECIDED. No replacement is guessed. This is a citation error, not a denial of ARID1A transcriptional regulation. The research questions now focus on locus selection, activation versus repression, and the TOP2A interface; unresolved citation and assay provenance remain documented in their reference reviews and annotation reasons.
