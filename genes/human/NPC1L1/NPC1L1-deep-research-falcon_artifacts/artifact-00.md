| **Identity and topology** | Human NPC1L1 is a **1,332-aa integral membrane protein**. Protease-protection and selective-permeabilization experiments support **13 transmembrane helices**, a luminal N-terminus, cytosolic C-terminus, three large luminal domains, and TM3–TM7 forming the sterol-sensing domain (SSD). | Confirms the supplied Q9UHC9 annotation as human **NPC1L1**, a patched/NPC1-related protein distinct from lysosomal NPC1. The experimental papers do not themselves print accession Q9UHC9. | Wang et al., **2009-08**, *J Lipid Res*, [doi:10.1194/jlr.M800669-JLR200](https://doi.org/10.1194/jlr.M800669-JLR200); Huang et al., **2020-06-19**, *Sci Adv*, [doi:10.1126/sciadv.abb1989](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.abb1989) (pqac-00000008, pqac-00000018) |
| **Primary substrate: cholesterol** | Npc1l1-null mice exhibit about a **70% reduction in net intestinal cholesterol absorption** and no further response to ezetimibe. Purified-protein structures identify an approximately **21-Å N-terminal sterol cavity** and a roughly **63-Å internal tunnel** toward the SSD; cholesterol binds the N-terminal domain and SSD-associated regions. | Strong genetic, pharmacological, biochemical, and structural evidence supports **unesterified cholesterol** as the principal physiological substrate. Binding plus tunnel architecture supports transport, but a static structure alone does not measure transmembrane flux. | Huang et al., **2020-06-19**, [doi:10.1126/sciadv.abb1989](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.abb1989) (pqac-00000018, pqac-00000032) |
| **Plant sterols** | NPC1L1 deletion reduces phytosterol absorption and prevents plasma/liver phytosterol accumulation in Abcg5/Abcg8-null mice. Cholesterol, β-sitosterol, and stigmasterol compete for purified NPC1L1 sterol binding. | Supports sitosterol and related phytosterols as physiological NPC1L1-associated substrates, generally handled less efficiently than cholesterol. Competitive binding demonstrates recognition, not by itself complete membrane translocation; much physiological evidence is from mice. | Tang et al., **2009-02**, *J Lipid Res*, [doi:10.1194/jlr.M800439-JLR200](https://doi.org/10.1194/jlr.M800439-JLR200); Huang et al., **2020-06-19**, [doi:10.1126/sciadv.abb1989](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.abb1989) (pqac-00000002, pqac-00000032) |
| **Intestinal site of action** | NPC1L1 is concentrated at the **apical brush-border membrane of small-intestinal enterocytes**, especially proximal jejunum. In endogenous Npc1l1-EGFP knock-in mice, cholesterol gavage produced subapical NPC1L1-positive vesicles within **5 min**, peaking at **15 min**; vesicles colocalized with EEA1 and filipin-detectable cholesterol and were suppressed by ezetimibe. | Establishes uptake from the intestinal lumen into enterocytes as the dominant physiological role. The real-time trafficking experiment is in mice, although apical human intestinal localization and clinical ezetimibe action are independently supported. | Wu et al., **2023-03-16**, *Life Metab*, [doi:10.1093/lifemeta/load011](https://doi.org/10.1093/lifemeta/load011) (pqac-00000017, pqac-00000027) |
| **Human hepatic canalicular role** | Human and nonhuman-primate NPC1L1 localizes to the **hepatocyte canalicular membrane**. Liver-specific human-NPC1L1 expression in mice reduced biliary cholesterol excretion by about **90%**, reversible by ezetimibe, consistent with retrieval of cholesterol from bile into hepatocytes. | Supports canalicular cholesterol reabsorption and enterohepatic recycling, but the strongest directional intervention is a transgenic-mouse model because normal mice express little hepatic Npc1l1. Long-term gallstone protection was not reproduced in a 2023 AAV model because hepatic NPC1L1 declined during prolonged lithogenic feeding. | Tang et al., **2011-09**, [doi:10.1016/j.bbalip.2011.05.013](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.bbalip.2011.05.013); Mo et al., **2023-05**, [doi:10.1016/j.heliyon.2023.e15757](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.heliyon.2023.e15757) (pqac-00000012, pqac-00000029) |
| **Molecular entry and endocytosis—qualified model** | Cryo-EM shows ezetimibe-induced N-terminal rotation of **57.8°** and displacement of **17.5 Å**, with drug occlusion of the NTD-to-SSD tunnel without competing for cholesterol binding. Clathrin/AP2 perturbation and 2023 mouse imaging support sterol-triggered endocytosis. Conversely, hepatoma-cell assays found ezetimibe-sensitive cholesterol uptake despite blocked NPC1L1 endocytosis; about **45%** of surface NPC1L1 internalized in 20 min, but ezetimibe did not inhibit that internalization. | Best current model: NPC1L1 captures sterol and transfers it toward or into the apical membrane; vesicular trafficking occurs physiologically and may amplify delivery, but whether endocytosis is strictly required for the elementary transport step remains unresolved and may depend on model and assay. | Ge et al., **2008-06**, [doi:10.1016/j.cmet.2008.04.001](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cmet.2008.04.001); Johnson & Pfeffer, **2016-06**, [doi:10.1091/mbc.E16-03-0154](https://doi.org/10.1091/mbc.E16-03-0154); Huang et al., **2020-06-19**, [doi:10.1126/sciadv.abb1989](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.abb1989) (pqac-00000010, pqac-00000014, pqac-00000019) |
