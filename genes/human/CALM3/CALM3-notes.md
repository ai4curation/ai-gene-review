# CALM3 notes

## 2026-03-19

- Review scope: the `CALM3` GOA file fetched on 2026-03-19 contains no literal `ISO` evidence-code rows, but it does contain multiple transferred annotations (`IBA` and `IEA`). I reviewed those in the spirit of [projects/ISO.md] by asking whether a term is safe to transfer across the human calmodulin paralogs.
- Family identity: humans have three calmodulin genes, `CALM1`, `CALM2`, and `CALM3`, and all three encode an identical 149 aa calmodulin polypeptide [PMID:27516456 "Humans have 3 CaM genes ( CALM1 , CALM2 , and CALM3 ) encoding for a perfectly conserved sequence of amino acids"] [PMID:31454269 "three separate CaM genes ( CALM1 , CALM2 , and CALM3 ) each encoding an identical 149 amino acid polypeptide"].
- Family regulation differs even when protein sequence does not: transcription of the three genes is not equivalent, and in teratoma cells `CALM3` was reported to be more highly transcribed than `CALM1` or `CALM2` [PMID:9681195 "CALM3 was at least 5-fold more actively transcribed than CALM1 or CALM2"].
- Curation consequence: core protein-level functions that follow directly from the conserved calmodulin sequence are safe to treat as family-wide, especially calcium binding, calcium sensing, kinase/phosphatase activation, and ion-channel regulation [PMID:27516456 "CaM is an essential Ca-binding protein with multiple cellular targets"] [PMID:23040497 "Both CALM1 substitutions demonstrated compromised calcium binding"].
- Cardiac core function is strong for the protein family and directly relevant to `CALM3`: defective calmodulin regulation of RyR2 increases spontaneous calcium release events [PMID:20226167 "the addition of a high concentration of CaM attenuated the aberrant increase of Ca(2+) sparks"] and the `CALM3` A103V mutation promotes arrhythmogenic calcium waves and sparks [PMID:27516456 "A103V-CaM (100 nmol/L) promoted spontaneous Ca wave and spark activity"].
- `CALM3` also has direct human disease evidence for impaired L-type calcium channel regulation and action potential phenotypes in calmodulinopathy [PMID:31454269 "both mutants impaired Ca 2+-dependent inactivation (CDI) of L-type Ca 2+ channels and prolonged action potential duration"].
- Cell-division localization/function is supported independently of cardiac biology: calmodulin binds CP110 and loss of that interaction disrupts late cytokinesis [PMID:16760425 "its depletion leads to a failure at a late stage of cytokinesis and the formation of binucleate cells"].
- Microbial hijacking papers support genuine calmodulin biochemistry but not necessarily core host biological-process terms. OspC-family effectors bind calmodulin and block CaMKII / IFN signaling [PMID:35568036 "IFN inhibition was mediated by the binding of OspC1 and OspC3 to the Ca2+ sensor calmodulin (CaM), blocking CaM kinase II and downstream JAK/STAT signaling"], but those infection-context processes should stay non-core.
- Several GOA terms are too context-specific to distinguish `CALM3` from `CALM1` / `CALM2`: synaptic localizations, myelin-associated terms, sperm terms, and specialized membrane-trafficking terms likely reflect cell-type context layered on top of the same calmodulin protein sequence rather than a `CALM3`-specific biochemical innovation [PMID:9681195 "the CALM gene family is differentially active at the transcriptional level"].
- Some annotations should be removed or down-weighted on evidence quality grounds. `substantia nigra development` is based on proteomic detection in diseased tissue rather than developmental perturbation [PMID:22926577 "Using a quantitative proteomic approach, we investigated protein expressions in the substantia nigra"].
- Generic `protein binding` annotations are not informative for calmodulin and should not be favored when more specific regulator/binding terms are available.
- Current UniProt entries for `CALM1`, `CALM2`, and `CALM3` all show the same 149 aa sequence summary and I found no `ALTERNATIVE PRODUCTS` block in these local records, so there is no isoform-specific caveat comparable to the splice-isoform project genes [file:human/CALM1/CALM1-uniprot.txt "SQ   SEQUENCE   149 AA"] [file:human/CALM2/CALM2-uniprot.txt "SQ   SEQUENCE   149 AA"] [file:human/CALM3/CALM3-uniprot.txt "SQ   SEQUENCE   149 AA"].

### Shared protein and gene-specific disease

CALM1, CALM2 and CALM3 share the canonical calmodulin sequence. This supports transfer of its intrinsic calcium-sensing and target-regulatory biochemistry, but does not establish identical transcription, tissue distribution, dosage or disease variants. PMID:9681195 directly compares transcription in proliferating human teratoma cells; its CALM3 advantage is not a general expression ranking across tissues. The abstract is complete, while the full experimental paper was unavailable.

PMID:23040497 concerns CALM1 variants. Its mature-protein N53I/N97S notation corresponds to UniProt precursor N54I/N98S because the initiator methionine is removed. These patient variants are not relabeled CALM3. PMID:27516456 directly identifies CALM3-A103V and compares its channel/calcium effects with CALM1 variants. PMID:31454269 distinguishes CALM1-E141V from CALM3-E141K; both mosaic pedigrees involve CALM3, including D130G. The recombinant affinity and overexpression cardiomyocyte experiments are distinguished from clinical gene attribution. These caches contain complete abstracts but only selected full-text narrative; detailed supplemental Methods, figures and target tables were not claimed as read.

### Regulatory activities and participation

The kinase unit is supported by human CaMKII/calmodulin structures and solution-binding experiments in PMID:20668654. Complete unique Results and Methods were read, alongside the complete abstract. Calmodulin binding relieves autoinhibition; CaMKII performs phosphate transfer. The construct numbering reported by the paper is not a new human product. The structural study is not a direct neuronal long-term-potentiation experiment, so the existing pathway row is retained as a contextual assertion.

The calcineurin unit is supported by PMID:8631777: engineered human calmodulin mutants restrict calcium-induced opening, lose target activation and regain regulatory activity after reduction. This establishes calmodulin's own phosphatase-activator work, not phosphatase catalysis. Only the complete abstract was available.

The channel unit combines binding and calcium sensing with calcium-dependent channel regulation. The mouse RyR2-R2474S experiment in PMID:20226167 mutates the channel, not a human CALM gene. Human CALM3 variant experiments independently support its cardiac relevance. PMID:22067155 provides RyR2-directed FRET and Z-line association in ventricular myocytes; all available cached Introduction/Discussion were read, with detailed Methods/Results absent. Channel-associated calmodulin is a regulator rather than a pore or sarcomeric structural protein.

The existing nitric-oxide-synthase regulator assertion is supported independently of its rodent transfer by the complete cached human event Reactome:R-HSA-202110, in which calmodulin relieves caveolin inhibition of eNOS. The official GO:0030235 definition describes binding and modulation of NOS and includes NOS binding. Its own regulatory activity is distinct from kinase, phosphatase and channel regulation. The linked primary papers and complete structured Reactome participant/compartment graph were not inspected; no new process or CALM3-specific endothelial expression assertion is proposed.
