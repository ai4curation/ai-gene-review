Gene Description

CSDE1 (UNR) is a cytoplasmic RNA-binding protein with multiple cold-shock domains that coordinates RNA with regulatory protein partners. It modulates translation and mRNA stability through transcript- and ribonucleoprotein-specific interactions, including the c-FOS coding-region determinant/poly(A)-binding-protein assembly, internal ribosome entry sites, and miRNA-induced silencing complexes. Its effects on stabilization and turnover depend on translation and complex state. CSDE1 also associates with stress granules and selected lncRNAs in defined cellular contexts. Alternative splicing changes N-terminal and internal protein sequences.

CSDE1 / UNR

2026-10-13 — RNA binding, partner coordination and transcript fate

CSDE1 binds RNA and connects it to regulatory proteins. Direct UNR–PABP assays and coding-region RNA binding support a protein–RNA adaptor role: PMID:15314026(https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC514181/). This does not make CSDE1 a nuclease. The c-FOS mCRD experiments identify CCR4 as the poly(A) nuclease and show that blocking initiation or ribosome transit stabilizes the reporter. Deadenylation-dependent turnover is therefore the appropriate source-specific process; these experiments do not demonstrate surveillance of elongation-stalled transcripts by no-go decay.

The c-FOS mCRD assembly is distinct from the c-MYC coding-region stability complex. Human HeLa complex isolation and mouse NIH3T3 reporter experiments establish complementary aspects of its biology. Overexpression PMID:11051545(https://doi.org/10.1016/S0092-8674(00)00102-1), whereas subsequent experiments show that UNR is required for rapid translation-coupled turnover. These results describe different ribonucleoprotein states. They do not require a universal stabilizer-only or destabilizer-only role.

Positive translation is independently demonstrated by recombinant human-derived UNR in reticulocyte assays, where it PMID:10049359(https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC316477/). The 2000 mCRD paper proposes enhanced initiation in its model and cites earlier work; it is not treated as a new direct translation-rate measurement. HRV-2 RNA mapping identifies PMID:17947529(https://doi.org/10.1099/vir.0.82463-0) in two structured domains, including exposed bulges and loops. This supports structured-RNA recognition without establishing duplex specificity or a solved universal RNA-chaperone mechanism. Viral IRES initiation remains a contextual activity rather than a separate primary core.

Cellular interactions and localization

CSDE1 is predominantly cytosolic, with cell-state-dependent associations. Human UNR–4E-T coimmunoprecipitation, RNase treatment and motif deletion support the PMID:27342281(https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5291280/). These assays do not establish purified pairwise contact, and the combined deletion phenotypes leave alternative explanations for repression. The HID1 and FAM9B binary-screen positives are also retained at their measured scope. Partner compartment expectations do not invalidate those experiments; no informative binding class is asserted solely from a partner's general cellular role.

The miRNA study reports that CSDE1 PMID:32161113(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32161113/). Human interaction experiments are distinguished from mouse and fly perturbations. Association and domain dependence do not imply that CSDE1 assembles the core RISC or performs intrinsic decapping. The HCC source identifies CSDE1 as an PMID:36354136(https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9647857/), ARHGAP5-AS1. Its VIM/RAC1 protein-output results support a translation-regulatory context but are not direct translation-rate measurements. TRIM28 supplies the proposed ubiquitin-ligase activity.

The stress-granule source abstract explicitly names CSDE1 among proteins PMID:29395067(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29395067/) granules. The positive IMP and localization are retained as context-dependent, with full target-level Methods and supplementary data still unavailable. In contrast, the P-body assignment remains unresolved: inspected PMID:32354837 main and supplementary material predominantly localizes 4E-T, while another study reports UNR absence in one condition.
