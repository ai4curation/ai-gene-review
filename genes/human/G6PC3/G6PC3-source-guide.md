## Catalysis and cellular experiments

[PMID:30626647 / PMC6347702](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6347702/) provides the principal direct mechanism. Selected Results, Discussion and construct Methods establish human G6PC3-mediated 1,5-anhydroglucitol 6-phosphate hydrolysis, a catalytic H167A control, and metabolite accumulation in deficient human cells. Human HEK293T membrane preparations were used, not purified protein. Human HAP1 cells, mouse neutrophil models, rat microsomal transport assays and patient neutrophils are separate experimental systems; the human G6PC3 patient sample was one individual. G6PC3 performs the hydrolysis and SLC37A4 supplies transport. Neutrophil consequences do not establish a separate apoptosis-regulator activity. The exact seven-word core excerpt comes from the Results concluding paragraph on joint dephosphorylation; its original XML and passage offsets are recorded in the source archive. The identical excerpt is verified at byte offsets 20670, 54518 and 59733 of the native publication cache, whose hash is recorded in the source archive.

[PMID:39548727 / PMC11788743](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11788743/) reports additional sugar-phosphate hydrolysis using recombinant human GFP-G6PC3 in HEK-cell lysates with empty-vector background. Rat microsomal transport and HL-60 differentiation are distinct experiments. The Table 1 and Discussion assignments of fructose-6-phosphate versus mannose-6-phosphate Km values disagree; those numbers do not support a physiological substrate ranking here. Selected main-text passages were read, not every figure image or supplementary assay.

[PMID:14718531](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14718531/) was read as an authentic complete abstract. It identifies G6Pase-beta's covalent His167 intermediate and luminal catalytic residues in a nine-transmembrane ER protein. The abstract does not specify construct species; human attribution comes from the current human GOA and target identity. Original Methods were not recovered. [PMID:25492228 / PMC4794745](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4794745/) was read in selected original Methods and Results, including human wild-type and variant expression in COS-1 cells, supplied glucose-6-phosphate, mock controls and protein measurements. These experiments establish catalytic capability and variant effects without identifying the dominant physiological substrate.

The single authored cellular-detoxification assertion is recorded separately from the 17 incoming annotations. Its rationale and the one core function remain in the review YAML. The accepted participation, same-role comparator and typed-ontology checks distinguish direct detoxifying hydrolysis from indirect neutrophil consequences; narrower metabolite-repair terminology remains a question because a useful role for the dephosphorylated product is unresolved.
