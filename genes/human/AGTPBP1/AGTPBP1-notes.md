### Direct chemistry and core function

- **PMID:22170066**, *Cytosolic carboxypeptidase 1 is involved in processing α- and β-tubulin.* The local cache is abstract-only. The [original full PMC article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3307270/) was read externally. Methods identify human CCP1-His6 expressed in Sf9; wild-type-baculovirus eluates provide preparation controls. Human HEK293T perturbations, purified porcine tubulin and polymerized HEK293T microtubules are distinct experimental components. Mass spectrometry and chelator sensitivity support terminal-glutamate processing by a metallopeptidase. The preparation is affinity-enriched, not described here as proven homogeneous. Cellular peptide-level measurements argue against bulk intracellular peptide turnover as its predominant assayed role.
- **PMID:24022482**, *Cytosolic carboxypeptidase 5 removes α- and γ-linked glutamates from tubulin.* [Full PMC3798508](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3798508/) Methods and comparative assays were read. The title does not identify the complete experimental scope: human CCP1 and mouse CCP5 are directly compared. Synthetic glutamylated tails and tubulin distinguish terminal alpha-linked trimming by CCP1 from CCP5-mediated gamma-linked branch-point removal. The branch-point limitation is bounded to the tested tubulin substrates.
- **PMID:21074048**, *A family of protein-deglutamylating enzymes associated with neurodegeneration.* The [primary PubMed abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21074048/) and DOI 10.1016/j.cell.2010.10.014 identify the Cell paper and the CCP-family chemistry. Its full body was not read here. It is a traced mouse donor source, not a substitute for the independently read human assays.
- **PMID:29593216**, *Klf4 glutamylation is required for cell reprogramming and early embryonic development in mice.* The [full publisher article](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-018-03008-2) was read. Mouse knockout MEFs/ESCs provide most mechanistic work; human reprogramming and human CCP shRNA methods are separately described. In-vitro Klf4 deglutamylation and protein-stability experiments support non-tubulin substrate scope. The mouse embryogenesis findings do not become a new human process assertion, and the paper's discussion of possible substrate-dependent branch-point behavior is not treated as demonstrated tubulin chemistry.

The broad InterPro proteolysis row is refined to **GO:0035609 C-terminal protein deglutamylation** and **GO:0035610 protein side chain deglutamylation**. CCP1 itself performs the hydrolysis, so this is participation evidence rather than loss-of-function necessity alone. These two processes distinguish gene-encoded terminal residues from post-translational side chains. They are sibling terms under protein deglutamylation; they are not described as formal children of proteolysis. No NEW row is needed. The core keeps **GO:0004181 metallocarboxypeptidase activity** with the two measured processes. The current child **GO:0106423 tubulin-tyrosine carboxypeptidase activity** removes terminal tyrosine and must not be substituted for the CCP1 glutamate reaction.

Primary ontology checks used [AmiGO GO:0004181](https://amigo.geneontology.org/amigo/term/GO:0004181), [GO:0106423](https://amigo.geneontology.org/amigo/term/GO:0106423), [GO:0035609](https://amigo.geneontology.org/amigo/term/GO:0035609), and the [GO:0035608 definition and child list](https://amigo.geneontology.org/amigo/term/GO:0035608).
