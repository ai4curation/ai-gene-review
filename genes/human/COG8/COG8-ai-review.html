Gene Description

COG8 is a peripheral Golgi-associated subunit of the eight-subunit conserved oligomeric Golgi complex. It binds Golgi SNARE proteins and couples their recognition to complex-dependent carrier recruitment and SNARE-complex organization. These activities help maintain Golgi architecture and the recycling environment needed for normal glycosylation. COG8 also supports endosome-to-trans-Golgi traffic; its loss disrupts COG subassemblies and the localization or assembly of selected SNAREs.

Core Functions

Binds Golgi SNAREs, including STX16, as a subunit of the COG complex, coupling recognition to complex-dependent carrier capture and SNARE-complex organization. Human experiments establish COG8-dependent endosome-to-TGN transport and ectopic recruitment of STX16-bearing carriers, with lobe B retained in the retargeting context. Binding alone is insufficient for recruitment; COG8 is not itself a fusogenic SNARE or a demonstrated autonomous tether. Complex assembly and maintenance of Golgi organization are integrated aspects of this trafficking role.

Integrated activity

Actual human COG8 experiments support one SNARE-binding core in COG at Golgi/TGN membranes. Directed binary tests and co-immunoprecipitation establish contacts with selected SNAREs, and ectopically anchored COG8 redirects STX16-bearing carriers. Binding-retaining truncations fail to redirect carriers; lobe B partners remain in this context. Binding, carrier capture and fusion therefore remain distinct endpoints. The experiments do not establish autonomous COG8 membrane fusion or sufficiency of an isolated purified COG8 molecule as a complete tether (PMID:23462996, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3595136/).

Human COG8 depletion separates COG subassemblies, changes selected SNARE-complex associations, and impairs TGN38 return and CI-MPR localization. Patient fibroblasts and HeLa cells are distinguished from the CHO comparison. These endosome-to-TGN experiments do not themselves verify the narrower COPI intra-Golgi route (PMID:23865579, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4084554/).
