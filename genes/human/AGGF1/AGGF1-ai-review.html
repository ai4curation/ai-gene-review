Gene Description

AGGF1 is a 714-residue human protein that leads two separable lives. Inside the nucleus it behaves as an RNA-processing factor: it carries an OCRE domain, an FHA domain (residues 434-487) and a G-patch domain (619-665) - the architecture of splicing regulators such as RBM5/RBM10 and of the G-patch cofactors of DEAH-box RNA helicases - it binds the long non-coding RNA NEAT1, coats paraspeckles as an outer rim around their NONO/PSF/PSPC1 core, and regulates alternative splicing of hundreds of transcripts, among them SRSF6, whose exon-3 skipping it promotes. Outside the cell it acts as a secreted ligand: AGGF1 is released from endothelial cells as capillary tube formation begins, engages integrin alpha5beta1 through a short RDD-containing motif at residues 604-613 that lies outside both named domains, and activates FAK, Src and AKT to drive endothelial cell adhesion, proliferation, migration, autophagy and capillary tube formation. It also binds the secreted cytokine TNFSF12/TWEAK. The protein shuttles between these two compartments under the control of its FHA domain and an internal nuclear localisation region, and it lacks a signal peptide, so its export is non-classical. Homozygous loss of the mouse orthologue is embryonic lethal, and in zebrafish aggf1 acts near the top of the hierarchy specifying hemangioblasts and venous identity. Although the gene was discovered through a reported association with Klippel-Trenaunay syndrome, the specific variant on which that association rested, E133K, has since been shown to be a benign polymorphism: its gnomAD v4 allele frequency is about 1.4% overall, i.e. roughly 2.8% of people carry a copy, and it was found in 3.3% of healthy population controls.

Proposed New Ontology Terms

TNFSF12 binding

Definition: Binding to tumor necrosis factor ligand superfamily member 12 (TNFSF12, also known as TWEAK), a secreted member of the tumor necrosis factor cytokine family that signals through the receptor TNFRSF12A/Fn14.

Justification: GO already contains GO:0038057 TNFSF11 binding, defined in exactly this shape for another TNF-superfamily ligand, so the pattern is established and this is a missing sibling rather than a new modelling decision. TNFSF12 binding is needed by more than one gene product: AGGF1 binds TNFSF12 by yeast two-hybrid, GST pull-down and co-immunoprecipitation (PMID:14961121) with independent confirmation by co-immunoprecipitation in human retinal endothelial cells (PMID:39905000), and TNFRSF12A/Fn14 is TNFSF12's receptor. At present all of this can only be recorded as bare GO:0005515 protein binding or as the generic GO:0019955 cytokine binding. Note a related inconsistency that should be settled at the same time: GO:0038057 is currently placed under GO:0043120 tumor necrosis factor binding, whose definition names TNF specifically as a proinflammatory cytokine produced by monocytes and macrophages and so does not cover superfamily ligands. Either GO:0043120's definition should be broadened to the superfamily, or the superfamily-member terms should hang directly from GO:0019955; this review used GO:0019955 because it is definitionally correct today.

Suggested Questions for Experts

Q: Does the AGGF1 G-patch activate DHX15, and is that what underlies AGGF1's splicing phenotype? This is the single question that would settle what two thirds of the gene's name means.

Q: Has anyone ever tested whether AGGF1's FHA domain binds a phosphothreonine peptide? The domain retains the complete canonical recognition set - G437-R438, S454 and N478 all transfer with identity from both CHEK2 and Rad53 FHA1 and land inside AGGF1's own annotated domain - and the cancer somatic mutations that impair function include G437E, on the conserved glycine of the phosphothreonine-anchoring G-R pair, while the two that do not impair function (R447Q, V497I) lie off that surface. Yet both published FHA functions, p53 binding (PMID:33069768) and 14-3-3 alpha/beta binding (PMID:33471274), were mapped by plain co-immunoprecipitation with deletion constructs, with no phosphatase control, no phospho-site mutant and no peptide competition. An FHA domain binding 14-3-3 - itself the canonical phospho-serine/threonine reader - is a mechanistically odd claim in exactly the direction a phosphatase control would test.

Q: A correction to report to UniProt. The FT VARIANT 133 line reads E -> K (displays a stronger angiogenic activity; dbSNP:rs34203073), presenting the variant as a functional Klippel-Trenaunay mutation. That attribution has been refuted: 3.3 percent of 275 healthy controls carry E133K, the single affected carrier in that study inherited it from a clinically normal parent (PMID:16443853), a second group reported it as a polymorphism in the Spanish population (PMID:17103452), and the originating laboratory's own larger study conceded that further genetic studies were needed to establish any AGGF1-KTS relationship (PMID:18564129). ClinVar classifies NM_018046.5(AGGF1):c.397G>A as Benign/Likely benign with multiple submitters and no conflicts. The in vitro CAM-assay difference may be real; the disease attribution is not, and no GO annotation should be propagated from it.

Q: Should GO:0043120 tumor necrosis factor binding be redefined? Its definition names TNF specifically as a proinflammatory cytokine produced by monocytes and macrophages, but GO:0038057 TNFSF11 binding is placed beneath it - so the term's placement already treats it as covering the superfamily while its text does not. This review used GO:0019955 cytokine binding for the AGGF1-TNFSF12 interaction for that reason.

Q: Why has none of AGGF1's mechanistic literature reached GO? Querying QuickGO by reference for all 35 papers relevant to this gene returns 22 that produced no GO annotation on any gene product anywhere, and 3 more that annotate other genes but nothing on AGGF1. Only two papers ever produced an AGGF1 annotation: the 2004 discovery paper and a 2005 review. Everything since - the receptor, the signalling pathway, the paraspeckles, the splicing, the FHA-dependent p53 stabilisation, the nucleocytoplasmic transport, the autophagy - is invisible to GO. This is a coverage defect, not an over-annotation defect, and it is unusually stark for a gene with this much literature.

Q: Do the four HuRI two-hybrid partners of AGGF1 - DHX15, MCRS1 and FBXO28, all three experimentally nuclear, plus the entirely unlocalised MAB21L3 - deserve orthogonal testing as a set? They are the only evidence in AGGF1's own GO record that points at the nucleus, and a single co-immunoprecipitation panel in a human cell line would either corroborate the nuclear picture or remove four rows at once.

Current model (mechanistic narrative)

AGGF1 is a secreted angiogenic factor that drives vascular development, vessel integrity, and tissue protection by engaging integrin receptors on the vascular wall while also performing distinct nuclear functions in RNA processing and tumor suppression [PMID:34551592, PMID:27522498, PMID:29885663]. On endothelial cells it signals through integrin α5β1 via an angiogenic motif mapped to residues 604–613 (FQRDDAPAS), activating FAK–Src–AKT and the PI3K p110α/p85α–AKT–GSK3β–p70S6K axis, and it stabilizes endothelial junctions by suppressing VE-cadherin phosphorylation [PMID:34551592, PMID:27522498]. AGGF1-driven angiogenesis additionally requires JNK-dependent assembly of the Becn1–Vps34–Atg14 autophagy complex PMID:27513923. On vascular smooth muscle cells AGGF1 acts through integrin α7 (ITGA7) via the same RDDAPAS motif to inhibit MEK1/2–ERK1/2–Elk signaling, sustain the myocardin/SRF contractile program, and suppress neointimal formation; it further enhances integrin α7 binding to LAP–TGF-β1, blocking maturation of TGF-β1 and limiting Smad2/3 activation to attenuate aortic aneurysm [PMID:35202649, PMID:28649088, PMID:37081014]. In developmental settings AGGF1 specifies venous identity and acts high in the hemangioblast hierarchy upstream of scl/fli1/etsrp and of npas4l and mTOR–S6K–Emp2 signaling [PMID:23197652, PMID:24277077, PMID:37881938]. In the nucleus, AGGF1 is a structural component of paraspeckles that nucleates the NONO/PSF/PSPC1/NEAT1 core and functions as a broad alternative-splicing regulator, promoting SRSF6 exon-3 skipping to generate the angiogenically active full-length isoform [PMID:29885663, PMID:40035560]. As a tumor suppressor, AGGF1 binds p53 and MDM2 through its FHA domain to inhibit p53 ubiquitination and stabilize p53, an activity abolished by somatic FHA-domain variants PMID:33069768. AGGF1 expression is transcriptionally activated by GATA1 and by NF-κB p65 downstream of AngII/AT1R and HIF-1α, and is post-transcriptionally repressed by miR-27a under hypoxia [PMID:19556247, PMID:29641288, PMID:39905000, PMID:24462738]. Across fibrotic, inflammatory, and atrophic contexts AGGF1 dampens NF-κB and TGF-β/SMAD signaling, forming a complex with SMAD7 and interacting with NF-κB and with TWEAK to restrain pathological responses [PMID:26850475, PMID:23628701, PMID:28958996, PMID:36696895].

12. The self-test checks WHICH guard fired, and is itself tested by breaking it

audit_claims.py --self-test mutates each claim in turn and requires a guard to
fire. That is necessary and was not sufficient: a mutation passed if any
problem appeared, so a guard that had silently stopped matching would still report
ok whenever the inserted text happened to offend a different guard. Each
mutation now declares a marker — a substring the intended guard's message
contains — and the newly-appearing problems must include it.

Installing the markers immediately exposed three mutations whose labels described
the wrong guard. Two of them fire the vague-plural pattern rather than the derived
count check, because three is a legal value in the derived ok-set (it equals
independent_cited_n) — a concrete instance of the ok-set limitation documented
in the code.

break_selftest.py then tests the tester, on a copy, restoring the real file
byte-for-byte:

breakage required behaviour result
a marker pointing at a string no guard emits exactly that mutation fails 1 failure, the right one
one guard disabled outright exactly its own mutation fails 1 failure, the right one
control: unmodified file clean exit 0

A self-test that reports "ok" is only worth anything if it can report "not ok" for
the right reason, and that has to be demonstrated rather than assumed.
