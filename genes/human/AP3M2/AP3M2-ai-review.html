Gene Description

AP3M2 is the mu-3B medium subunit of the heterotetrameric AP-3 adaptor complex, one of two interchangeable mu subunits that define the complex's two forms. With the ubiquitous beta3A subunit and mu3A the complex is AP-3A; with the neuron-enriched beta3B subunit and mu3B it is AP-3B, and the two beta/mu pairings are obligate, so AP3M2 is the medium subunit of the neuronal complex. The shared delta and sigma3 subunits complete either form. AP-3 is a vesicle coat. It is recruited from the cytosol onto the cytoplasmic face of endosomal membranes by the small GTPase Arf1, selects transmembrane cargo through sorting signals in cargo cytoplasmic tails, and buds coated carriers that deliver that cargo to lysosomes and to lysosome-related organelles such as melanosomes and platelet dense granules. The complex reads two kinds of signal through two different subunits: acidic dileucine motifs through the sigma3 subunit, and tyrosine-based YxxPhi motifs through the medium subunit, which is the arm AP3M2 provides. Cargo engagement rigidifies the complex and licenses coat polymerisation, so cargo selection and vesicle formation are mechanically coupled. Unlike the AP-1 and AP-2 adaptors, AP-3 is not a clathrin coat, and its site of action is the tubular sorting endosome rather than the trans-Golgi network or the plasma membrane. In neurons the mu3B-containing complex has a distinct job: it generates a subset of synaptic vesicles by budding them from endosomal membranes, a route parallel to the dominant AP-2-dependent retrieval of vesicles from the presynaptic membrane, and it loads particular membrane proteins into those vesicles. Loss of mu3B in mouse reduces the number and size of synaptic vesicles, depletes the vesicular GABA transporter from hippocampal terminals while leaving the glutamate transporters and the general synaptic vesicle proteins untouched, selectively impairs evoked GABA release, lowers the threshold for hippocampal long-term potentiation, and produces spontaneous epileptic seizures. The same sorting machinery, acting with the BLOC-1 complex, is also required for membrane cargo to leave neuronal cell bodies for neurites and nerve terminals. Although the rodent transcript is confined to brain and spinal cord, the human gene is broadly expressed, with its strongest single-cell signal in melanocytes and spermatogenic cells, and the zebrafish gene named ap3m2 is required for the lysosome-related organelles that build guanine crystals in pigment cells - so the protein's role outside the nervous system is likely wider than the historical neuron-specific description implies.

Proposed New Ontology Terms

tyrosine-based sorting signal binding

Definition: Binding to a tyrosine-based sorting signal, a short peptide motif of the form YxxPhi (where Y is tyrosine, x is any amino acid and Phi is a bulky hydrophobic residue) located in the cytoplasmic tail of a transmembrane protein, which directs that protein into a vesicular transport pathway.

Justification: A note on form: this entry is a request for a term GO does not have, but its companion entry below is a rename-and-re-parent request for an existing term, and ProposedOntologyTerm has no field that distinguishes the two - so read proposed_name there as the proposed new name of GO:0035654, not as a term that is missing. This is the activity of the medium (mu) subunits of the heterotetrameric adaptor protein complexes, and it is not confined to endocytosis: mu1 of AP-1 reads such signals at the trans-Golgi network, mu3 of AP-3 reads them on endosomes en route to lysosomes, and a mu4-specific signal routes a reporter into the endosomal-lysosomal system without being internalised from the plasma membrane at all. GO's only molecular function term for the motif, GO:0089710 endocytic targeting sequence binding, is defined as directing 'internalization by clathrin-coated pits' and its only ancestors are GO:0005515, GO:0005488 and GO:0003674, so there is no non-endocytic sibling available. The consequence is visible in this gene's annotations: lacking any subunit-level term, the PAINT family-root node propagates the clathrin- and endocytosis-committed GO:0035615 to AP-3 and AP-4 medium subunits, and the review of AP3M2 can only record a contributes_to at the complex level. GO:0089710 would become a child of the new term.

vesicle cargo loading, AP-3-mediated

Definition: Formation of a macromolecular complex between proteins of the AP-3 adaptor complex and the proteins and/or lipoproteins that are to be transported by the vesicle the complex coats.

Justification: This is a rename-and-re-parent request for an existing term, not a request for an additional one; ProposedOntologyTerm has no field to say so, so proposed_name here is the proposed new name of GO:0035654. GO:0035654 'clathrin-coated vesicle cargo loading, AP-3-mediated' is the only AP-3-specific cargo-loading term in GO, and this review accepts it as a core process for AP3M2 - but both its name and its definition's clause 'transported by a clathrin-coated vesicle' commit AP-3 to a coat it is not. UniProt describes AP-3 as 'an adaptor-related complex which is not clathrin-associated'; GO's own definition of the AP-3 complex says 'AP-3 does not appear to associate with clathrin in all organisms'; and the structural work shows AP-3 recruited by Arf1 and polymerising through a second Arf1 molecule rather than onto a clathrin lattice. The commitment is not harmless: it is the same legacy framing that lets GO:0035615 and GO:0030131 reach AP-3 subunits, and this review modifies both of those for that reason. The cleanest fix is to drop 'clathrin-coated' from GO:0035654 and re-parent it directly under GO:0035459 vesicle cargo loading, leaving its current parent GO:0035652 for the AP-1 and AP-2 adaptors. Both of those ids and labels were checked against QuickGO rather than written from memory: GO:0035652 is 'clathrin-coated vesicle cargo loading', not obsolete, and is_a GO:0035459 'vesicle cargo loading'; and QuickGO lists GO:0035652's children as exactly GO:0035653 and GO:0035654, both is_a, which is what establishes that GO:0035654 currently sits beneath GO:0035652 rather than beside it. So the proposal moves GO:0035654 up one level to sit alongside GO:0035652. Generalising annotations to GO:0035459 instead would discard the AP-3 identity in order to remove an adjective, which is why this gene's row is accepted rather than modified.

Current model (mechanistic narrative)

AP3M2 is the mu-2 subunit of the AP-3 adaptor complex and functions in endolysosomal protein trafficking and the biogenesis of lysosome-related organelles (LROs) PMID:41950095. Its clearest mechanistic role is in pigment cells: CRISPR-Cas9 disruption of ap3m2 in zebrafish iridophores reduces guanine crystal number, alters crystal morphology, and produces distinct iridosome maturation defects, establishing AP3M2 as essential for LRO formation and crystal growth via endolysosomal trafficking PMID:41950095. In the nervous system, Ap3m2 knockout mice develop spontaneous epileptic seizures and show altered alcohol-related behaviors, linking the protein to GABAergic transmission, with expression controlled by an upstream H3K4me3 regulatory site PMID:24923803. Beyond its role in LRO biogenesis and neuronal function, AP3M2 has been linked to cytokine (IL-6) secretion in astrocytes PMID:30371777 and to autophagy and ROS pathways in colorectal cancer cells PMID:39488930, but these connections have not been mechanistically reconstructed in the available corpus.

1. What the protein is

adaptor. UniProt states the composition explicitly:

  • PMID:15492041

lost cargo recognition — encoded as residue_claims on the molecular-function row.

A second, independent AP-3 cargo complex. The PANTHER family metadata names
PDB 4IKN as PTHR10529's representative structure: the rat mu3A C-terminal domain
bound to the TGN38 tail (DYQRL) — a different species and a different cargo from 9C5B.

in 9C5B. Human AP3M2 carries the same residue as rat mu3A at 9 of the 10. So the site is
not an artefact of one structure, one cargo or one species, and mu3B matches mu3A across

the outgroup comparison below — the other is mu2 V401, which maps to V389 and is itself a
contact in both AP-3 structures. analyze.py computes that count rather than my asserting
it; every other mu2 contact position has at least one paralogue that diverges.)

Outgroup and alignment check. The classical AP-2 pocket from PDB 1BXX (rat mu2 +
TGN38 DYQRLN) gives 13 contact residues. Both AP-3 mu subunits have diverged sharply
from it — AP3M2 matches mu2 at 4/13 and AP3M1 at 5/13, versus AP1M1 10/13, AP1M2 9/13
and AP4M1 11/13 — most strikingly mu2 W421 → G404 in both mu3 proteins. Yet all 13
positions still align with no deletion, and projecting them onto AP3M1 lands on 9 of
the 9 positions 9C5B observes contacting LAMP1. The two routes agree, so the
cross-family alignment is not drifting, and the reading is that AP-3 recognises
tyrosine cargo through the structurally equivalent site with a diverged residue
complement — which is precisely what Ohno et al. reported functionally
PMID:9748267.

mu3 linker amphipathic helix. Begley et al. report a membrane-inserting amphipathic
helix in the mu3 linker PMID:39705307.

7. Open questions

  • Is human AP3M2 functionally neuronal at all? The rodent restriction (PMID:8076832) is
    not reproduced by human expression atlases, which put its highest single-cell signal
    in melanocytes and spermatogenic cells. Either the paralogue's tissue distribution
    changed in the primate lineage, or the protein has an unrecognised role in
    lysosome-related-organelle biogenesis in pigment cells, as the zebrafish knockout
    suggests.
  • Does mu3B-containing AP-3B select different cargo from mu3A? Ohno's library screen
    says the YxxPhi preferences of mu3A and mu3B differ, but no mu3B-specific cargo has
    been identified; VGAT, the best candidate, is proposed to use a dileucine signal,
    which is read by sigma3/delta rather than by mu3.
  • Is the AP-3B complex ever assembled without beta3B? Nakatsu's immunoblot says beta3B
    is destabilised in mu3B-null brain, implying strict interdependence, but the reverse
    experiment (mu3B stability in AP3B2-null neurons) was not found here — and it matters
    for interpreting DEE48.
  • Which complex does the alcohol-preference QTL act through? PMID:24923803 maps
    regulatory variation upstream of Ap3m2 rather than coding change, so the phenotype may
    be a dosage effect on AP-3B assembly.
