### Own activity and centriole construction

[PMID:23857771, "CEP120 cooperates with CPAP to regulate centriole elongation"] reports human CEP120 constructs amplified from HEK293T RNA. Selected Results, Figure4/5 descriptions and Methods distinguish purified GST-tagged N-terminal fragments from cellular full-length constructs. The N-terminal 1–209 region binds polymerized microtubules; K76A impairs this interaction and reduces overexpression-induced centriole elongation. The C-terminal regions contribute localization and self-association. The microtubule-binding molecular-function core is grounded in CEP120's own assay, rather than inferred from a partner's role. This study does not by itself establish a full-length single-molecule polymerase mechanism.

PMID:20956381 resolves endogenous CEP120 enrichment on daughter centrioles in human RPE1 cells. Its depletion/rescue study includes human RPE1 cells rescued with mouse Cep120; detailed multiciliated tracheal-epithelium and many cycling-cell experiments are mouse. Recruitment of SAS6 can persist despite the loss of normal centriole duplication, distinguishing early cartwheel assembly from later structural construction. Human loss/rescue and direct binding studies support the centrosome is_active_in assertion beyond mere localization.

The current mouse Q7TSG1 source chain links centrosome-cycle, positive-duplication and positive-cilium-assembly annotations to PMID:25251415. The complete current donor query and selected literal records are linked in the evidence JSON. ### Recruitment: preserve the function, distinguish the source claim

PMID:27185865 examines CEP120-induced overextension in the CEP295 pathway, but its Figure7D and Results explicitly report [PMID:27185865, "depletion of CPAP, but not CEP120, centrobin or SPICE, substantially blocked the targeting of CEP295 to centrioles"]. The relevant Methods use human U2OS PLK4-inducible cells and siRNA. The study's Figure5 also shows that a CEP295 N-terminal fragment disrupts CEP120 localization; that reverse direction does not demonstrate CEP120-dependent CEP295 recruitment. The source therefore supports an elongation context while leaving a claim-specific problem for the original positive-protein-localization citation. Its IMP tuple is preserved, not silently reassigned.

Independent human PMID:30988386 resolves the broader functional question. Selected Figures1–5 show shortened centrioles and deficient appendages in CEP120/p53-null RPE1 cells, with p53-null controls and wild-type-p53 depletion experiments. CEP295 and RTTN recruitment remain detectable; SPICE, centrobin, POC1B, POC5, C2CD3 and TALPID3 recruitment are affected. Tagged co-IP and GST-fragment pull-down of in-vitro-translated partners map C2CD3 and TALPID3 associations. These are not all purified two-component affinity measurements or a proven single simultaneous complex. The positive-protein-localization process is retained on this independent evidence, expressly excluding the unsupported CEP295 recruitment paraphrase.

### Ciliogenesis and cell-state boundaries

PMID:30988386 Figure6 reports loss of ciliation in the human CEP120 knockout models. Wild-type re-expression restores appendage markers but only partially rescues ciliation; excess CEP120 can also impair ciliation and overextend centrioles. Disease variants can retain overexpression-induced elongation while differing in ciliation rescue and partner recruitment. Thus the positive assembly role is conditional on proper protein level and architecture, not a universal monotonic effect of overexpression.

PMID:29847808 independently examines genome-engineered human RPE1 V194A/A199P variants, protein stability, distal markers and ciliation. Reduced ciliation need not imply abolished centriole duplication. Its domain structures have distinct species: C2A from zebrafish, C2B from tilapia and C2C from mouse; human mutant biochemical and cellular measurements are separate. No calcium-binding or membrane-binding activity is inferred merely from the C2 fold.

PMID:29741480 adds a different quiescent-cell role. Selected mouse fibroblast depletion, rescue, domain and MT-regrowth assays show excess PCM accumulation and altered satellite distribution after Cep120 loss. Its 988-residue mouse constructs are distinguished from the human RPE1 cells also listed in its Methods. Restraint of premature PCM maturation is compatible with a positive role in centriole construction during cycling. The direction-neutral protein-localization assertion can encompass both contexts.
