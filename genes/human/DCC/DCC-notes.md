# DCC functional review

## Receptor biology and products

DCC is a netrin receptor whose extracellular immunoglobulin and fibronectin domains connect guidance cues to intracellular signaling. The official human record contains one displayed 1,447-residue sequence and no alternative-products declaration. The predicted signal peptide, transmembrane segment and cytoplasmic tail are distinguished from experimentally expressed fragments and from the modeled cleavage products. The current model assigns residues 26–1290 to a membrane-retained product and 1291–1447 to the released distal tail; these are not separately encoded isoforms. The proximal apoptotic region is not the released inactive tail. [UniProt P43146](https://www.uniprot.org/uniprotkb/P43146/entry), [Reactome R-HSA-373705](https://reactome.org/content/detail/R-HSA-373705).

The original receptor study establishes binding and antibody-sensitive axon outgrowth, with rat DCC, chick netrin and rat neurons; the human 293 cells are the expression host. Its author dissertation was recovered when the journal PDF was blocked. The particular turning assay inside explants was not blocked by the antibody, so outgrowth is not silently converted into a direct chemoattraction assay. The ligand-specific netrin receptor term is appropriate, and the axon-guidance synthesis also uses the actual PAINT placement and subsequent neuronal evidence. [PMID:8861902](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8861902/).

A second functional unit organizes ligand-absence apoptotic signaling. Human DCC expression, D1290 mutation and intracellular truncations support a proximal signaling role; later work connects DCC to APPL1 and state-dependent caspase association. DCC plus caspases alone does not reconstitute activation: additional S100 components are required. The signaling-adaptor interpretation therefore represents functional recruitment and coordination, not autonomous protease activity, a purified ternary complex, or a demonstrated direct caspase-9 interface. The early studies did not directly detect cleavage of native full-length DCC. [PMID:9796814](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9796814/), [PMID:12011067](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12011067/), [PMID:11248093](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11248093/), [PMID:16537496](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16537496/).

## Binding evidence and limits

The interaction assessments distinguish the assay from the partner class. MAZ supports transcription-factor binding, CASP9 caspase binding, MYO10 myosin binding and TRIM9 ligase binding. Direct DCC P3–MYO10 contact is demonstrated with recombinant fragments and a deliberately engineered MYO10 deletion. Several database records are mutant perturbations rather than independent confirmations of wild-type binding. Human TRIM9 association is explicitly stated in the original main text even though the exact supplementary assay remained inaccessible. This is not a reason to dismiss the human result because most of that paper studies a fly homolog. [PMID:11527412](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11527412/), [PMID:21642953](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21642953/), [PMID:22084112](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22084112/).

The translation study establishes DCC association with ribosomal particles and independently demonstrates purified RPL5 contact. RPL28/RPS23 complex records are not presented as purified binary assays, and binding to a named ribosomal protein alone would not establish ribosome binding. Here the broader fractionation evidence supplies that functional context. DCC remains a receptor-associated regulator rather than a structural ribosomal subunit or constitutive translation inhibitor. [PMID:20434207](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20434207/).

Positive netrin-binding assertions remain contextual generic associations where the inspected ontology does not supply an appropriate binding-only class. Netrin receptor activity adds signal transmission, while netrin receptor binding points in the opposite direction. PSG7 and IGFBPL1 binding/SPR results are retained with extracellular-construct and avidity limits; they do not establish physiological receptor activation. Supported generic associations are retained under the standing campaign policy. [PMID:19721007](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19721007/), [PMID:26190107](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26190107/), [PMID:32822567](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32822567/).

## Inherited and contextual neuronal functions

The actual PTHR44170 tree, target alignment and five on-path IBDs were reconstructed. The deep cell-adhesion node is not judged by donor count or pairwise similarity. Its current sources include positive experimental annotations to verified children of cell-cell adhesion; an exact-term-only query would miss these. The ancestral adhesion assertion is retained as contextual, without inventing direct human homophilic adhesion. The current guidance IBD includes an additional zebrafish donor absent from the old seeded source; the imported source fields remain literal.

Mouse spinal accessory neuron experiments quantify Dcc-dependent cell-body migration and axon trajectories. The synaptic study combines mouse conditional deletion and Schaffer-collateral physiology with adult-rat PSD fractionation and immunoelectron microscopy. Its genuine author thesis Chapter 3 was recovered and selected Methods/Results were read; neither this nor the mouse ortholog annotation is represented as a direct human synapse experiment. [PMID:16267219](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16267219/), [PMID:23291093](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23291093/), [PMID:9331350](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9331350/). The Neogenin/Lrig2 paper is used only as PAINT donor context. [PMID:26651291](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26651291/).

Three precise negative developmental assertions remain undecided. The BCL11A paper explicitly reports DCC-overexpression rescue, so there is positive target evidence and no wrong-gene claim. Its accessible abstract does not identify which DCC rescue measurements support the separate neuron-projection, collateral-sprouting and dendrite-development terms. Bounded original-access attempts did not resolve this. [PMID:19616629](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19616629/).
