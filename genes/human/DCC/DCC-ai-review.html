Gene Description

DCC (deleted in colorectal cancer) is a single-pass transmembrane netrin receptor with extracellular immunoglobulin-like and fibronectin type III domains and a cytoplasmic signaling tail. Netrin-responsive DCC assemblies recruit intracellular effectors that regulate neuronal migration, axon extension and guidance. The receptor also associates with translation machinery and supports synaptic signaling in mature neuronal circuits. In the absence of netrin, its intracellular region can organize a caspase-dependent apoptotic signaling platform. This dependence-receptor behavior underlies its proposed role as a candidate tumor suppressor. Proteolytic processing distinguishes a membrane-retained proximal signaling region from a released distal tail.

DCC functional review

Receptor biology and products

DCC is a netrin receptor whose extracellular immunoglobulin and fibronectin domains connect guidance cues to intracellular signaling. The official human record contains one displayed 1,447-residue sequence and no alternative-products declaration. The predicted signal peptide, transmembrane segment and cytoplasmic tail are distinguished from experimentally expressed fragments and from the modeled cleavage products. The current model assigns residues 26–1290 to a membrane-retained product and 1291–1447 to the released distal tail; these are not separately encoded isoforms. The proximal apoptotic region is not the released inactive tail. UniProt P43146, Reactome R-HSA-373705.

The original receptor study establishes binding and antibody-sensitive axon outgrowth, with rat DCC, chick netrin and rat neurons; the human 293 cells are the expression host. Its author dissertation was recovered when the journal PDF was blocked. The particular turning assay inside explants was not blocked by the antibody, so outgrowth is not silently converted into a direct chemoattraction assay. The ligand-specific netrin receptor term is appropriate, and the axon-guidance synthesis also uses the actual PAINT placement and subsequent neuronal evidence. PMID:8861902(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8861902/).

A second functional unit organizes ligand-absence apoptotic signaling. Human DCC expression, D1290 mutation and intracellular truncations support a proximal signaling role; later work connects DCC to APPL1 and state-dependent caspase association. DCC plus caspases alone does not reconstitute activation: additional S100 components are required. The signaling-adaptor interpretation therefore represents functional recruitment and coordination, not autonomous protease activity, a purified ternary complex, or a demonstrated direct caspase-9 interface. The early studies did not directly detect cleavage of native full-length DCC. PMID:9796814(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9796814/), PMID:12011067(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12011067/), PMID:11248093(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11248093/), PMID:16537496(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16537496/).

Binding evidence and limits

The interaction assessments distinguish the assay from the partner class. MAZ supports transcription-factor binding, CASP9 caspase binding, MYO10 myosin binding and TRIM9 ligase binding. This is not a reason to dismiss the human result because most of that paper studies a fly homolog.
