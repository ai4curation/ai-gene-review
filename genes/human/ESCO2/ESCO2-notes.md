## 2026-10-24: Evidence clarification after PR review

[PMID:15958495](https://doi.org/10.1091/mbc.e04-12-1063), RNA-interference Methods and Results/Figure 3, reports cohesion defects after depletion of either human EFO1/ESCO1 or EFO2/ESCO2. This is positive evidence that the proteins are not generally interchangeable. [PMID:22101327](https://doi.org/10.1038/emboj.2011.381), Results/Figures 3–4 and 6–7, resolves a nonredundant mouse Esco2 requirement: pericentric enrichment, altered cohesion, and reduced Smc3 K105/K106 acetylation and Sororin association after Esco2 loss. The mouse results are not a direct human short-product assay.

[PMID:21111234](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2010.10.031), printed pp. 741–742 and Figure 3A/B, shows that only combined ESCO1/ESCO2 depletion substantially reduced the measured chromatin acetyl-SMC3 endpoint in that S-phase HeLa assay. This narrower observation is retained without generalizing to all cohesion functions. Its separate purified-enzyme experiment uses ESCO1 and Xenopus chromatin. Human ESCO2-fragment autoacetylation in PMID:15958495 Figure 2A remains separate.

[PMID:22699483](https://doi.org/10.1095/biolreprod.112.099978), immunofluorescence Methods and Results/Figures 4–5, supports ESCO2/phospho-H2AFX-positive chromosomal clumps in E18.5 female pachytene oocytes. This differs from the male XY-body experiment in Figure 2. GO:0035861 names a chromosome region, so direct DNA-end binding and repair catalysis are not prerequisites. The specialized localization remains non-core with explicit marker-based resolution; it is not converted into a repair-function assertion. PMID:22614755 remains unavailable beyond metadata/abstract.

The positive HPA nucleoplasmic staining and actual Reactome target compartments are retained as non-core contexts. The principal acetylation/cohesion core remains on chromatin. [PMID:19907496](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature08550), Figures 3d/e and 4e, supplies independent human chromatin and replication-fork velocity evidence. GO:0045740 includes regulation of rate; the precise fork-velocity endpoint remains in the biological rationale and open question. The broader cohesion annotation is refined to the already-supported mitotic term GO:0007064.
