# SMAD1 literature and BMP9 GO-CAM notes

## Core BMP-SMAD transcriptional function

SMAD1 is a receptor-regulated SMAD in the BMP branch of TGF-beta superfamily signaling. BMP receptor activation rapidly phosphorylates human MADR1/SMAD1, but TGF-beta and activin do not, and BMP2 induces MADR1 accumulation in the nucleus [PMID:8653785, "BMP2 treatment results in accumulation of MADR1 in the nucleus"]. A parallel human SMAD1 cloning paper showed that SMAD1 mimics BMP4 ventralizing activity in Xenopus animal caps, moves into the nucleus in response to BMP4, and gains transcriptional activity when fused to a heterologous DNA-binding domain downstream of BMP type I and II receptors [PMID:8637600].

The activated SMAD1 MH2 domain is a receptor-regulated assembly module. Receptor phosphorylation of the C-terminal SVS motif activates BMP signaling and uses the phosphorylated tail as a ligand for the L3 loop of another SMAD1 or SMAD4 MH2 domain, explaining phospho-SMAD1 dissociation from receptor kinases and formation of heteromeric SMAD1-SMAD4 complexes [PMID:11779505]. SMAD6 antagonizes this step by binding BMP-activated SMAD1 and competing with SMAD4, yielding an inactive SMAD1-SMAD6 complex [PMID:9436979].

SMAD1's MH1 domain binds cis-regulatory DNA directly. The crystal structure of mouse Smad1 MH1 bound to a palindromic Smad-binding element and accompanying EMSA assays show specific Smad1-DNA binding and altered cooperativity relative to TGF-beta effector Smad3 [PMID:20147459]. BMP-response elements in the Hex promoter bind SMAD4 and SMAD1-SMAD4 complexes in vitro, respond to SMAD1 plus SMAD4 in transfection assays, and use GC-rich elements that are conserved in other BMP-responsive promoters [PMID:12270938]. SMAD4 is not needed for receptor-activated SMAD1 nuclear import, but its C-terminal activation function is required for SMAD1-dependent transcriptional activation [PMID:9389648].

Inactive or post-signaling SMAD1 also shuttles through the cytosol. EGF/ERK linker phosphorylation antagonizes BMP signaling by blocking BMP-induced SMAD1 nuclear accumulation [PMID:9335504]. RanBP3L binds dephosphorylated SMAD1/5/8 and exports them from the nucleus, thereby terminating BMP-SMAD transcription and tuning mesenchymal differentiation [PMID:25755279]. The inner nuclear membrane protein MAN1/LEMD3 binds receptor-regulated SMADs at the nuclear periphery and represses BMP, TGF-beta, and activin reporter responses [PMID:15647271].

## Contextual and noncanonical outputs

The core biochemical pathway is BMP receptor phosphorylation of SMAD1, SMAD1-SMAD4 complex formation, nuclear accumulation, DNA binding, and Pol II transcriptional activation. Additional annotations are real but contextual. SMAD1/SMAD5 mediate anti-Mullerian hormone signaling downstream of BMPR1A in mouse granulosa cells [PMID:24312319]. BMP/SMAD1 signaling supports osteoblast differentiation in human preosteoblasts through an activin A/ALK1 context [PMID:34948289] and is regulated by miR-205 in vascular smooth-muscle calcification assays [PMID:25012146]. Hepatic BMP6-SMAD1/5/8 signaling drives hepcidin in the iron-homeostasis pathway [PMID:31800957]. Endothelial SMAD1 is a miR-26a target in angiogenic responses: lowering miR-26a increases SMAD1, Id1 signaling, endothelial growth, tube formation, and angiogenesis in the cited human-cell and mouse assays [PMID:24047927].

SMAD1 also has a SMAD4-independent RNA-processing role. In human pulmonary artery smooth-muscle cells, BMP4 requires SMAD1/5 to increase pre-miR-21 and mature miR-21 without increasing pri-miR-21; SMAD1 binds DDX5/p68 and is recruited to the DROSHA microprocessor on pri-miR-21 [PMID:18548003]. This supports primary miRNA binding, DDX5 binding, and primary miRNA processing as noncanonical, non-core activities.

## Protein-interaction rows

Many IntAct rows assert only bare `GO:0005515 protein binding`. Those are intentionally uninformative in GO. The rows supported by mechanistic papers are represented better by specific terms already present in GOA: `co-SMAD binding` for SMAD4, `I-SMAD binding` for SMAD6, `DEAD/H-box RNA helicase binding` for DDX5, `ubiquitin protein ligase binding` for SMURF1/SMURF2, `protein kinase binding` for HIPK2, and `identical protein binding` for the SMAD1 homomer. Several remaining partners, including PSMB4, LEMD3, CTDSP1/2/L, IPO8, YAP1, FAM83G/PAWS1, RANBP3L, UCHL3, and USP15, are biologically plausible SMAD1 regulators but lack a more specific existing GO molecular-function term in this review.

## BMP9 GO-CAM 600ced8500002343

The SMAD1 activity in `gomodel:600ced8500002343/6446bfcb00001798` correctly types SMAD1 as `GO:0000981 DNA-binding transcription factor activity, RNA polymerase II-specific` in `GO:0030509 BMP signaling pathway`. However, the activity occurs in `GO:0000785 chromatin`. The gene review accepts chromatin as the active DNA-bound state of SMAD1, but the model's occurs_in evidence, PMID:8653785, only reports nuclear accumulation of MADR1 after BMP2, and GO's chromatin term does not place the activity in the nucleus for GO-CAM compartment reasoning. The final GO-CAM consistency PR should add an explicit nuclear location or otherwise resolve the chromatin-to-nucleus gap; the model also lacks the SMAD4 cofactor shown by PMID:9748228 to form a complex with phosphorylated SMAD1/5 after ALK1 or ALK2 signaling.
