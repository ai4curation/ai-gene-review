# COL4A5 review notes

## Matrix structure and exact products

COL4A5 supplies the alpha5 chain of type-IV collagen protomers and basement-membrane networks. Current GO:0030020 explicitly permits ECM collagens, so the tensile-strength class does not require an isolated-chain force experiment. Human alpha5 NC1 coding sequence from HEK293 was expressed in Sf9 cells in PMID:30443360. The alpha3/4/5 mixture yielded alpha5 homohexamer crystals, and alpha5 self-assembly was also observed in solution. This supports performed structural assembly but is not reconstruction of a native alpha345 network. No sulfilimine bond was observed in these recombinant structures; peroxidasin supplies the crosslinking chemistry. The independently inspected publisher corrigendum 32695424 concerns author name/affiliation, not these experiments.

The complete available PMID:12193605 abstract supports alpha345 protomer/NC1 organization; exact original Methods, species and constructs remain unrecovered. Human chain-specific localization in PMID:7657706 and PMID:9671947 supports basement membranes, including distinct alpha5/alpha6 tissue patterns. Their colocalization and suggested heterotrimers are not treated as measured stoichiometry. Human Alport mutations in PMID:2349482 provide clinical/GBM context. The broader structural and trimer rows are refined to existing GO:0030020 and GO:0005587. GO:0030198 describes the chain's own structural assembly work. The NAS fibril-organization row from PMID:29853175 remains UNDECIDED because its available abstract does not settle the alpha5-specific process and bounded full recovery failed; network architecture alone neither establishes that exact process nor excludes effects on other collagen fibrils.

Official P29400 and P29400-1 reconstruct at 1685 residues, P29400-2 at 1691. VSP_001173 replaces canonical residue 1264 with GPTGFQG, a net six-residue insertion. All three official FASTAs match exactly, and both original product objects remain unchanged. The signal peptide spans 1–26 and the mature chain 27–1685 in the displayed record. Sequence preservation of an NC1 region does not prove equivalent assembly, processing or ligand activity of both intact products.

## The additional fragment-binding evidence

PMID:17239819 tests chemically synthesized short peptides in HUVEC WST-1 metabolic/viability and Boyden migration assays. It provides positive experimental effects, but no direct integrin assay or demonstration of natural fragment release. The original Table 1 sequences were independently mapped: the three alpha5 sequences map to canonical 1510–1529, 1520–1539 and 1626–1644, with all positions six residues later in isoform 2. Historical AAF66217 coordinates must not be copied onto the displayed UniProt product.

The later genuine full PMID:37694286 changes the molecular-function assessment. Its actual Methods use recombinant human NC1, reported Gly1461–Thr1685, and synthetic LRRFSTMPFMFCNINNVCNF. In the solid-phase assay, alpha1beta1 integrin reagent CC1012 binds coated NC1 or peptide; beta1 antibody provides detection. Random peptide and BSA are controls. This is not a purified beta1-monomer assay. Crosslinked cellular immunoprecipitation corroborates association but does not alone establish a purified binary complex. The direct solid-phase experiment supports GO:0005178 at the tested fragment scope. It does not by itself establish an endogenous fragment role as a primary conserved function. The core synthesis therefore describes the established structural role; the positive fragment-binding annotation remains.

The peptide maps to canonical 1510–1529 and isoform 2 1516–1535. The paper's peptide coordinates match the latter, while its NC1 endpoints match the canonical chain. That coordinate difference is not evidence of a wrong reagent or proof that a particular whole isoform was assayed. Receptor blockade only partially limits peptide-induced barrier disruption. NC1-reactive staining and plasma ELISA do not identify the exact naturally released 20-residue species. Natural cleavage, exposure and chronic physiological relevance remain biological questions. No apoptosis, angiogenesis, Rho-regulation or pulmonary-disease process is added from these endpoints.

Current GO:0005178 has no is_a/part_of ancestor or descendant overlap with the original 35 terms or structural replacements/core terms. Its child GO:0098640 requires integrin binding as part of cell-matrix adhesion, which the isolated-fragment binding/barrier experiments do not establish. The exact protected GO-CAM index has no target entry; absence establishes no positive biological fact and is used only in the coverage audit. The binding experiment supplies the positive MF evidence.

## Interpretation of the isolated-domain activity

A distinct measured molecular activity and a primary conserved core role are different judgments. The human NC1 solid-phase experiment establishes integrin binding. Its endogenous release, sequence-defined circulating product and physiological contribution remain unresolved, so it is retained as experimentally supported fragment biology without a separate formal core. This does not deny binding by intact collagen, require an in-vivo assay for GO binding, or transfer a decision from another collagen chain. The alpha4 comparison also involves defined recombinant NC1 activity without resolved endogenous-fragment identity; the same primary-versus-context-specific criterion applies. Arresten has a separately curated cleavage product, which is a different evidence state.

Reactome:R-HSA-2327695 summarizes integrin binding by several collagen types and named alpha1/alpha2/alpha3 NC1 ligands. It is compatible family context, but supplies neither an alpha5-specific assay nor evidence that the isolated and assembled forms have identical or mutually exclusive activities. PMID:17239819 remains highly relevant to experimentally tested alpha5-derived peptides: WST-1 and Boyden endpoints are positive, while receptor binding and natural release were not tested there.

The source-specific GO:0030199 assertion remains unresolved. Its definition encompasses determining the size and arrangement of collagen fibrils; it does not require the participant to be a fibrillar collagen. The available PMID:29853175 abstract and alpha5 type-IV network evidence do not settle the exact asserted participation. A different collagen review cannot substitute for that missing source-specific support.
