CC   -!- FUNCTION: Probable Polycomb group (PcG) protein involved in
CC       transcriptional regulation mediated by ligand-bound nuclear hormone
CC       receptors, such as retinoic acid receptors (RARs) and peroxisome
CC       proliferator-activated receptor gamma (PPARG) (PubMed:16606617). Acts
CC       as a coactivator of RARA and RXRA through association with NCOA1
CC       (PubMed:16606617). Acts as a corepressor for PPARG and suppresses its
CC       adipocyte differentiation-inducing activity (By similarity). Non-
CC       catalytic component of the PR-DUB complex, a complex that specifically
CC       mediates deubiquitination of histone H2A monoubiquitinated at 'Lys-119'
CC       (H2AK119ub1) (PubMed:20436459, PubMed:30664650, PubMed:36180891). Acts
CC       as a sensor of N(6)-methyladenine methylation on DNA (6mA): recognizes
CC       and binds 6mA DNA, leading to its ubiquitination and degradation by
CC       TRIP12, thereby inactivating the PR-DUB complex and regulating Polycomb
CC       silencing (PubMed:30982744). The PR-DUB complex is an epigenetic
CC       regulator of gene expression and acts as a transcriptional coactivator,
CC       affecting genes involved in development, cell communication, signaling,
CC       cell proliferation and cell viability (PubMed:30664650,
CC       PubMed:36180891). ASXL1, ASXL2 and ASXL3 function redundantly in the
CC       PR-DUB complex (By similarity) (PubMed:30664650). The ASXL proteins are
CC       essential for chromatin recruitment and transcriptional activation of
CC       associated genes (By similarity). ASXL1 and ASXL2 are important for
CC       BAP1 protein stability (PubMed:30664650). Together with BAP1,
CC       negatively regulates epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT) of
CC       trophoblast stem cells during placental development by regulating genes
CC       involved in epithelial cell integrity, cell adhesion and cytoskeletal
CC       organization (PubMed:34170818). {ECO:0000250|UniProtKB:P59598,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:16606617, ECO:0000269|PubMed:20436459,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:30664650, ECO:0000269|PubMed:30982744,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:34170818, ECO:0000269|PubMed:36180891}.
CC   -!- SUBUNIT: Core component of the polycomb repressive deubiquitinase (PR-
CC       DUB) complex, at least composed of BAP1, one of ASXL1, ASXL2 or
CC       (probably) ASXL3, and one of MBD5 or MBD6 (PubMed:20436459,
CC       PubMed:24634419, PubMed:30664650, PubMed:36180891). Distinct
CC       combinations of ASXL and MBD proteins may preferentially bind specific
CC       histone modification marks (PubMed:36180891). The PR-DUB core
CC       associates with a number of accessory proteins, including FOXK1, FOXK2,
CC       KDM1B, HCFC1 and OGT; KDM1B specifically associates with ASXL2 PR-DUB
CC       complexes (Probable) (PubMed:30664650). Interacts (via DEUBAD domain)
CC       with BAP1 (via ULD domain); the interaction is direct and forms a
CC       ubiquitin binding cleft (PubMed:36180891, PubMed:36991118,
CC       PubMed:37556531). The interaction with BAP1 is important for
CC       maintaining BAP1 stability (PubMed:36180891). Together with BAP1,
CC       associates (via DEUBAD domain) with nucleosomes; interacts with
CC       nucleosomal DNA and stabilizes the orientation of the nucleosome to
CC       line up the PR-DUB complex active site with its H2AK118ub1 substrate
CC       (PubMed:36991118, PubMed:37556531). Interacts (via PHD domain) with
CC       MBD5 and MBD6 (via MBD domain); the interaction is probably direct and
CC       mediates association of MBD proteins with the PR-DUB core
CC       (PubMed:36180891). Interacts with RARA, RXRA and NCOA1
CC       (PubMed:16606617). Interacts with PPARA and PPARG (By similarity).
CC       {ECO:0000250|UniProtKB:P59598, ECO:0000269|PubMed:16606617,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:20436459, ECO:0000269|PubMed:24634419,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:30664650, ECO:0000269|PubMed:36180891,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:36991118, ECO:0000269|PubMed:37556531,
CC       ECO:0000305|PubMed:24634419}.

CC   -!- DISEASE: Bohring-Opitz syndrome (BOPS) [MIM:605039]: A syndrome
CC       characterized by severe intrauterine growth retardation, poor feeding,
CC       profound intellectual disability, trigonocephaly, prominent metopic
CC       suture, exophthalmos, nevus flammeus of the face, upslanting palpebral
CC       fissures, hirsutism, and flexion of the elbows and wrists with
CC       deviation of the wrists and metacarpophalangeal joints.
CC       {ECO:0000269|PubMed:21706002}. Note=The disease is caused by variants
CC       affecting the gene represented in this entry.
CC   -!- DISEASE: Myelodysplastic syndrome (MDS) [MIM:614286]: A heterogeneous
CC       group of closely related clonal hematopoietic disorders. All are
CC       characterized by a hypercellular or hypocellular bone marrow with
CC       impaired morphology and maturation, dysplasia of the myeloid,
CC       megakaryocytic and/or erythroid lineages, and peripheral blood
CC       cytopenias resulting from ineffective blood cell production. Included
CC       diseases are: refractory anemia (RA), refractory anemia with ringed
CC       sideroblasts (RARS), refractory anemia with excess blasts (RAEB),
CC       refractory cytopenia with multilineage dysplasia and ringed
CC       sideroblasts (RCMD-RS); chronic myelomonocytic leukemia (CMML) is a
CC       myelodysplastic/myeloproliferative disease. MDS is considered a
CC       premalignant condition in a subgroup of patients that often progresses
CC       to acute myeloid leukemia (AML). {ECO:0000269|PubMed:19388938,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:20182461}. Note=The disease is caused by variants
CC       affecting the gene represented in this entry.
