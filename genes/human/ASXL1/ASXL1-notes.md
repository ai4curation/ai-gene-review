### Molecular roles

ASXL1 is a noncatalytic regulator of PR-DUB. [PMID:20436459](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3182123/) reconstitutes human BAP1 with human ASXL1 residues 2–365 and demonstrates ASXL1-dependent removal of nucleosomal H2A ubiquitin. The human experiments are distinct from the paper's Drosophila genetics and Calypso catalytic mutant. Targeted human-complex Results and purification/activity Methods were read. This supports refining the corresponding generic BAP1-binding annotation to deubiquitinase activator activity, GO:0035800.

The final structural study [PMID:37556531](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10411902/) examines human BAP1 and the ASXL1 DEUBAD fragment, with structural and turnover substrates distinguished. ASXL1 contacts nucleosomal DNA and positions the complex for BAP1-catalyzed hydrolysis. Selected Results, construct/activity/binding Methods and captions were independently assessed; supplementary data and figure pixels were not inspected. DNA contact mutations affect catalysis without necessarily changing equilibrium affinity. This is evidence of a regulatory contribution, not an autonomous ASXL1 ubiquitin-hydrolase active site. The earlier preprint is not substituted for the final paper.

The second proposed core is transcriptional coactivation, GO:0003713. [PMID:16606617](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16606617/) supports receptor association and cooperation with SRC-1. An independent read of original Figure 5A identifies human ASXL1 residues 1096–1541 tested against human ESR1, mouse RXRalpha and chicken THRB. Figure 5B uses full-length mouse ASXL1. The three GOA partner accessions remain unchanged. Increased promoter histone acetylation does not establish ASXL1 acetyltransferase activity. Broad human chromatin/transcriptional evidence in [PMID:30664650](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6341105/) also informs this core; later double-knockout experiments and full Methods require further reading.

### Context-dependent associations and processes

[PMID:22897849](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3422511/) contains human BAP1, EED and EZH2 co-association evidence. Targeted Results, immunoprecipitation Methods and Figure 2/6 captions were read. Cellular co-immunoprecipitation supports the reported associations without proving purified binary affinity. The differing HOXA effects of BAP1 and ASXL1 depletion also caution against treating PRC2-associated repression as identical to PR-DUB catalysis. These three measured generic associations are provisionally NON_CORE under the user-supplied action definitions; the standing generic-binding policy clarification remains unanswered.

[PMID:21047783](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3020743/) supports contextual PPAR regulation and adipocyte phenotypes. Its expression constructs are mouse ASXL1 and human ASXL2. Figure 1C instead detects endogenous ASXL1 in human HEK293 cells with transfected FLAG-PPARgamma. The species and expression boundaries are retained. Methods and selected Results/captions were read; figure pixels and supplements were not.

### Later reading: human ASXL redundancy and transcription

The indexed primary text of [PMID:30664650](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6341105/) subsequently exposed Results and legends for Figures 1, 2 and 5, plus the Discussion. Human HAP1 single knockouts have modest effects; combined ASXL1/ASXL2 loss reduces BAP1 protein and increases H2A ubiquitination, with predominantly reduced transcript levels. ASXL3 is not expressed in this model. This supports context-specific redundancy, not universal interchangeability. HeLa affinity purification distinguishes the stable BAP1–ASXL core from associated factors; KDM1B is preferentially associated with ASXL2. The transcriptional coactivator interpretation also uses human ASXL1/PolII chromatin profiles, separately from mouse BAP1/PolII data. These findings strengthen the proposed coactivation role without making every transcriptional effect independent of PR-DUB. Full Methods, supplementary datasets and figure pixels remain unread. Direct publisher and PMC opens were challenged; the readable evidence was the indexed original text.
