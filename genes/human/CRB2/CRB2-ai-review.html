Gene Description

CRB2 is a Crumbs-family cell-surface protein with a large extracellular EGF-repeat and laminin-G-domain region and a short cytoplasmic tail. Its terminal PDZ-binding motif and separate FERM-binding region connect it to epithelial polarity proteins. Membrane-associated CRB2 supports apical junction organization, maintenance and recovery, with prominent retinal and renal epithelial roles. In vertebrate models, Crb2 also coordinates epithelial cell behavior during gastrulation and supports photoreceptor organization and survival. A separate transmembrane-dependent interaction modulates gamma-secretase activity in human cell systems. Alternative products differ substantially in their extracellular regions and retention of the membrane anchor and cytoplasmic binding motifs.

CRB2 biological notes

2026-10-13: epithelial polarity and distinct experimental contexts

CRB2 is a membrane-associated Crumbs protein whose cytoplasmic tail interacts with polarity proteins. Its main supported role is organizing and maintaining the apical epithelial junction region. Human fetal RPE depletion impairs junction ultrastructure and recovery after a calcium switch, even when baseline transepithelial resistance is unchanged. This establishes maintenance and reestablishment in that assay; it does not establish a universal requirement for initial junction formation. PMID:34262913

The terminal CRB2 peptide has direct, measured PDZ-domain binding. The fragmentomics experiment tests the biotin-tagged final ten residues, 1276–1285, against isolated PDZ constructs. Its 21 source-specific partner annotations map to 66 positive curator records, including the actual domains, tags and construct features. These are valid domain-binding observations; they do not establish that every partner contacts intact CRB2 in its native compartment. The separate EPB41L5 interaction concerns a FERM domain, rather than a PDZ domain. Detailed motif-deletion controls in that earlier paper predominantly concern CRB1 and are not reassigned to CRB2. PMID:36115835 PMID:17920587(https://doi.org/10.1016/j.yexcr.2007.08.025)

The fragmentomics author correction PMID:36477203(https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9729566/) restores missing figure labels and a column, and corrects Figure 5 panel ordering. Its operative text was read independently of the metadata-only normal cache. It does not state that the CRB2 interaction records were withdrawn. Supplementary Data 1 was not recovered; the literal interaction records are retained, and a malformed affinity exponent is not converted into an invented numerical value.

Mouse RPE CRB2 is apicolateral, including the tight-junction region. Human RPE experiments independently corroborate peripheral junction localization; the mouse antibody and HEK construct controls in the earlier paper are not human retinal microscopy. The anatomical apical junction complex must also be distinguished from a molecular complex of fixed stoichiometry. The GO subapical-complex term explicitly describes an invertebrate epithelial compartment, so the mammalian location is represented by the broader anatomical apical junction unit. PMID:26404741

Developmental and retinal evidence

Mouse Crb2 deletion disrupts gastrulation, mesoderm production and associated axial and cardiac morphogenesis. Later live imaging directly connects Crb2 loss with cells that fail to complete apical detachment and ingression after initiating apical constriction. These are positive source-specific developmental roles, retained outside the distilled epithelial polarity core. The later study separates these events from a universal primary loss of all classical polarity markers and leaves the biochemical connection to Myosin IIB incompletely resolved. PMID:22072575 PMID:27870829

The independent Poglut1 study directly compares Crb2-null embryos and shows a wider primitive streak, retained E-cadherin-positive cells and deficient mesoderm. It also shows that Crb2 is a glycosylation substrate whose surface trafficking depends on Poglut1. That does not make CRB2 a glycosyltransferase, and the separate Notch defect of Poglut1-null embryos is not assigned to CRB2. PMID:26496195

The earlier Crb2 knockout study reports reduced Bmp4 expression during E7–E7.75, with expression retained in amnion/allantois and Brachyury/Fgf8 unchanged. Gross abnormalities become apparent later in that interval. This stage- and region-selective response supports the existing indirect positive BMP-regulation inference as non-core. A downstream BMP-output assay, direct molecular mechanism and the contribution of altered cell composition remain unresolved; the observation is not described as a pSMAD assay or universal CRB2-to-BMP4 pathway.

The retinal conditional-knockout paper genuinely assays Crb2, despite its title discussing human CRB1 disease. Its original Methods and Results support junction disorganization, photoreceptor orientation defects, progressive degeneration and shorter developing cone outer segments. These findings support retinal homeostasis, photoreceptor maintenance and cone-development annotations at mouse source scope. The human RPE maintenance assay does not negate separate developmental polarity evidence. PMID:23001562

Human renal disease variants and rescue with human CRB2 in zebrafish support a conserved renal epithelial role, with species and construct scope kept explicit. Recent podocyte experiments connect CRB2 depletion with contractility and increased YAP-associated readouts. Culture Methods and Results identify human immortalized cells, while the immunoblot Methods say murine; that internal source ambiguity is unresolved. Twenty-four-hour YAP or rapamycin inhibition did not reverse the force phenotype, so the data do not establish a sole CRB2–Hippo–YAP–contractility chain. No additional YAP-signaling activity is inferred. PMID:25557779(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25557779/) PMID:40062402(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40062402/)

Gamma-secretase modulation

Human CRB2 expression reduces APP cleavage products, whereas endogenous SH-SY5Y knockdown increases them. CHAPSO-solubilized membrane reactions with recombinant substrate provide cell-free corroboration; they are not purified binary CRB2–protease experiments. The association depends on the CRB2 transmembrane region, while disrupting the PDZ- and FERM-binding motifs does not remove the measured inhibition. PMID:20299451

The original also co-immunoprecipitates native CRB2 with the four gamma-secretase components from cynomolgus monkey brain and observes corresponding complexes in human cell membrane preparations. Reciprocal immunoprecipitation was limited by background. The current gamma-secretase-complex definition includes regulatory subunits, making a CRB2-associated regulatory pool a defensible source-specific refinement. This does not make CRB2 a universal catalytic-core subunit, establish fixed stoichiometry or identify its directly contacted partner. The inhibitory/complex-binding activities remain positive contextual functions outside the primary polarity core; physiological human-brain relevance is unresolved.
