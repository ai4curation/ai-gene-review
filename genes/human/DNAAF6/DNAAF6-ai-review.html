Gene Description

DNAAF6 is a 214-residue cytoplasmic assembly factor required for formation of outer and inner axonemal dynein arms. It associates with the dynein intermediate chain DNAI2 and other assembly factors, and is enriched at the trans-Golgi network in human ciliated airway cells. It belongs to the PIH/CS dynein-assembly-factor family and contains a predicted CS-like domain, rather than a distinct PIH domain. Loss of DNAAF6 disrupts ciliary and sperm flagellar motility and causes X-linked primary ciliary dyskinesia, with variable residual dynein arms and laterality phenotypes.

Identity and protein scope

HGNC:28570 identifies DNAAF6, formerly PIH1D3/CXorf41. The reviewed Q9NQM4 record and independent FASTA both describe a 214-residue protein. No natural alternative products are recorded. Human assembly, binding and localization

Paff et al., 2017 establish human respiratory-ciliary dynein-arm defects and impaired sperm motility. The Results and Figure 4 Methods identify human sequence-confirmed DNAAF2 and DNAAF4 constructs in a binary Y2H experiment, followed by reciprocal tagged co-immunoprecipitation in HEK293T cells. HSP90 was tested with fragments. These positive associations support the assembly context; they do not establish that DNAAF2/DNAAF4 are themselves folding enzymes or that DNAAF6 has a separately resolved adaptor activity. Dynein intermediate-chain stabilization is a mechanistic proposal, not a purified folding assay.

Olcese et al., 2017 provide the specific DNAI2 and TGN evidence. Figure 5c,d and the accompanying Results use human tagged DNAAF6 and DNAI2 in HEK293 cells, with swapped tags and empty-vector controls. Wild type associates; p.Asp133Tyr and three truncating DNAAF6 variants lose this signal. This supports the existing curated intermediate-chain-binding function, without resolving purified affinity or attributing every mutant effect exclusively to a contact residue. Figure 5a compares endogenous human nasal-cell staining with TGN46 and GM130, supporting late-Golgi enrichment; supplementary overexpression staining corroborates it. Neither Golgi-luminal topology nor an independent secretory function follows from these experiments.

Human arm loss varies between individuals: some retain residual outer and inner arms and dysmotile cilia. The zebrafish mutant loses arms and motility; fish mRNA rescue is strong, whereas low-dose human mRNA produces partial body-curvature rescue and higher doses are detrimental. The core therefore combines DNAI2 association, outer/inner arm assembly, observed chaperone associations and the cytoplasmic/TGN locations without implying a universal all-or-none phenotype or a known complete complex at that location.
