Gene Description

FH (fumarate hydratase, fumarase) is a class II, iron-independent fumarase that forms a homotetramer and catalyses the reversible stereospecific hydration of fumarate to L-malate (EC 4.2.1.2). Alternative transcription initiation produces two products from one gene. The longer product carries an N-terminal mitochondrial targeting sequence; it is imported into the mitochondrial matrix and catalyses the fumarate-to-malate step of the tricarboxylic acid (TCA) cycle. The shorter product has no targeting sequence and stays in the cytosol, where it metabolises fumarate produced by argininosuccinate lyase in the urea cycle and by amino acid catabolism. After DNA damage, part of the cytosolic pool enters the nucleus. There DNA-PK phosphorylates FH at Thr236, and FH binds histone H2A.Z at double-strand breaks. The fumarate it makes there inhibits the KDM2B demethylase, which raises H3K36me2 and promotes Ku70/DNA-PK recruitment and non-homologous end joining. FH is a tumour suppressor. Germline heterozygous loss-of-function mutations with second-hit inactivation cause hereditary leiomyomatosis and renal cell cancer (HLRCC). In these tumours fumarate accumulates and acts as an oncometabolite: it inhibits 2-oxoglutarate-dependent dioxygenases such as the HIF prolyl hydroxylases, and it succinates cysteines on proteins such as KEAP1. Biallelic hypomorphic mutations cause fumarase deficiency, a severe encephalopathy. Eukaryotes inherited two unrelated fumarase families: an O2-sensitive [4Fe-4S] class I enzyme and the class II enzyme. Humans kept only the class II form.

Summary: Ensembl Compara transfer of urea cycle from mouse Fh1 (P97807).
Reason: In the cytosol, argininosuccinate lyase releases fumarate as a co-product of arginine formation in the urea cycle; cytosolic FH hydrates this fumarate to malate, linking the urea cycle to the TCA cycle ("Krebs bicycle") and returning carbon toward aspartate. FH thus catalyses a reaction on the fumarate branch rather than a step of the ornithine-citrulline-argininosuccinate-arginine sequence itself. It does contribute a step (removing ASL product); when FH is lost fumarate accumulates and ASL runs in reverse to make argininosuccinate (PMID:24280230), showing FH activity is coupled to the cycle. This is a genuine but peripheral, isoform-specific (cytosolic) role, best kept as non-core.

  • Homotetramer. Each active site is built from residues of three subunits, so disease
    variants that break oligomerisation lose activity PMID:29456767.
  • Class II (FumC-type). The human cDNA is most similar to B. subtilis and E. coli FumC fumarases PMID:3828494. These are the iron-independent class II enzymes.

    without the mitochondrial targeting sequence PMID:27037871.
