# FA2H: free-fatty-acid hydroxylation and lipid-precursor roles

The displayed human product is Q7L5A8-1 (372 residues); Q7L5A8-2 is the natural 159-residue product lacking residues 1–213. The experimental Met93-start construct and the NeuroNet region 93–372 are different from that natural product. Historical accession labels, expression hosts and domain similarity do not establish that both natural products share activity, localization or partners.

## One enzyme, distinct lipid-biosynthetic roles

The core reaction is [free-fatty-acid 2-hydroxylation](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/QuickGO/term/GO:0120520) in the ER membrane. Its hydroxylated acyl products feed the [GalCer](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/QuickGO/term/GO:0006682) and [GlcCer](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/QuickGO/term/GO:0006679) biosynthetic branches, both descendants of ceramide biosynthesis. These are precursor contributions, not downstream sugar-transfer reactions. Fatty-acid metabolism and [CNS myelin maintenance](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/QuickGO/term/GO:0032286) describe distinct supported aspects of the same enzyme's work; none is an ancestor of another selected process. No separate core is introduced solely for heme binding. The cytochrome-b5 domain/signature and human deletion experiment support this enzyme facet without direct human heme-occupancy measurements or a complete physiological electron-transfer mechanism.

## Free-fatty-acid chemistry and membrane localization

[Alderson et al., 2004, PMID:15337768](https://doi.org/10.1074/jbc.M406649200), Experimental Procedures and Results/Figures 2–6, uses human FA2H expressed in COS7 cells. The original full-length construct was assembled with its N-terminal cytochrome b5 region and sequence-verified; a Met93-start form and a FLAG-tagged form were tested separately. Membrane fractionation places the expressed protein in the membrane pellet. Microsomes convert deuterated free tetracosanoic acid into the corresponding 2-hydroxy acid in an NADPH/reductase-supported assay. Empty-vector, deletion and electron-supply comparisons bound the attribution. Precise ER localization and topology were predictions in this paper.

[Alderson et al., 2005, PMID:15863841](https://doi.org/10.1194/jlr.D500013-JLR200), Methods and Results/Figures 2–7, specifies the human construct in COS7 microsomes and identifies the free hydroxy-acid product by retention time and mass fragments. The source distinguishes this direct assay from older assays that measured label in ceramide after multiple reactions. Neither paper is a purified isolated-protein assay. The assay does not by itself establish all physiological electron donors or identical activity of the shorter natural product.

For both papers the immutable normal cache is abstract-only. Selected operative author-upload main text and captions were actually read; direct PDF routes remained inaccessible. No figure pixels were inspected. Public author copies are [the 2004 article](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/8374285_The_Human_FA2H_Gene_Encodes_a_Fatty_Acid_2-Hydroxylase) and [the 2005 assay article](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/7874026_A_novel_method_for_the_measurement_of_in_vitro_fatty_acid_2-hydroxylase_activity_by_gas_chromatography-mass_spectrometry).

[Eckhardt et al., PMID:15658937](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1186713/), Experimental sections and Results/Figure 4, provides independent mouse localization evidence. A sequenced FVB/N 372-residue ORF, expressed as an N-terminal EGFP fusion in CHO-CGT cells, colocalizes with calnexin and retains lipid-product formation. Human membrane fractionation plus this conserved localization supports the ER-membrane refinement; it does not become a human marker-imaging experiment. [Reactome's human FA2H entity](https://reactome.org/content/detail/R-HSA-5693771) explicitly places residues 1–372 in that compartment. The mouse paper's Discussion calls ceramide usage probable while acknowledging that the immediate substrate was not established.

## Stereochemistry, ceramide production and skin

[PMID:22517924](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3371244/), Methods and Figure 1, demonstrates R-2-hydroxypalmitate production in FLAG-human-FA2H CHO cells. The complete main was read. Its separate mouse 3T3-L1 experiments address enantiomer rescue, raft mobility, GLUT4/glucose uptake and lipid abundance (Figures 2–6). Those outcomes do not isolate turnover of a ceramide-bound acyl substrate. [GO:0120520](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/QuickGO/term/GO:0120520) therefore captures the directly established free-substrate reaction. The more specific existing [GO:0120521](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/QuickGO/term/GO:0120521) assertion remains undecided; this is not a claim that such activity has been disproved. Hydroxylated acyl precursors and increased 2-hydroxyceramides support participation in ceramide biosynthesis without assigning ceramide-synthase activity.

[PMID:17355976](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17355976/) remains available only as metadata and a positive abstract in this review. It reports human-keratinocyte enzyme/lipid changes and lamellar-body/extracellular-membrane phenotypes after FA2H knockdown. Operative Methods, controls and microscopy were not recovered. The skin-barrier assertion remains undecided. Independent human biochemical originals support the specific enzyme refinement without being attributed to the unread keratinocyte assay.

## Conserved lipid and myelin roles

The original main of [PMID:17901466](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2662131/) was recovered and selected Methods/Results/captions/Discussion read. Primary rat Schwann cells and D6P2T cells, knockdown, free deuterated C24 assays and lipid measurements link FA2H activity to hydroxylated GalCer. This supports a conserved precursor-supply step, not galactosyltransferase chemistry or a direct human Schwann-cell experiment.

Selected original Methods and the Results section “Analysis of Skin and Epidermal Sphingolipids,” including the Figure 5 caption, were read for [PMID:21628453](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3138264/). Complete export was not established and its normal cache remains abstract-only. In mouse whole skin, selected 2-hydroxy GlcCer species decreased; isolated epidermis retained the measured hydroxylated lipids. The MS analysis selected a d18:1 backbone, and the effects differed by acyl-chain length: C20 was absent, C22–24 decreased, and C16/C26 were not significantly changed. The transferred GlcCer process therefore retains tissue and species limits. It does not assert that every glucosylceramide or every tissue depends on FA2H. No Figure 5 pixels were inspected. An unrelated initial search hit was excluded from the target evidence.

Complete originals were recovered for [PMID:18815260](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6671223/) and [PMID:21491498](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3094470/). Selected knockout, lipid-analysis and morphology Methods, Results and captions distinguish loss of hydroxylated myelin lipids from initially formed myelin and later degeneration. The second paper includes glial conditional deletion. These support conserved CNS myelin maintenance through FA2H's executed lipid-precursor chemistry; they are not human perturbation experiments. No microscopy pixels or supplements were read.
