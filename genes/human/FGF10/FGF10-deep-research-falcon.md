# Comprehensive Research Report: FGF10 Gene Function and Annotation

## Gene Identity Verification

The research target FGF10 (UniProt accession O15520) encodes fibroblast growth factor 10, also known as keratinocyte growth factor 2 (KGF-2), in humans (Homo sapiens). This protein belongs to the heparin-binding growth factors family and is specifically classified within the FGF7 subfamily, which also includes FGF3, FGF7, and FGF22 (edirisinghe2024decodingfgffgfrsignaling pages 7-8, bzdega2022phenotypicspectrumof pages 3-5, edirisinghe2024decodingfgffgfrsignaling pages 5-7). The human FGF10 gene maps to chromosome 5p12-p13, spans approximately 89 kb, and contains three coding exons (bzdega2022phenotypicspectrumof pages 3-5, edirisinghe2025pathologyandtherapeutic pages 8-10). This confirms that the research is focused on the correct human protein as specified.

## Protein Structure and Molecular Characteristics

FGF10 is synthesized as a 208-amino-acid precursor protein with an approximate molecular weight of 23.4 kDa (edirisinghe2024decodingfgffgfrsignaling pages 7-8, bzdega2022phenotypicspectrumof pages 3-5, edirisinghe2025pathologyandtherapeutic pages 8-10). The protein architecture consists of an N-terminal signal peptide spanning residues 1-37, which directs the protein into the endoplasmic reticulum-Golgi secretory pathway, and a mature FGF domain comprising residues 38-208 (edirisinghe2024decodingfgffgfrsignaling pages 7-8, bzdega2022phenotypicspectrumof pages 3-5). Two glycosylation sites have been identified at residues 51 and 196 (bzdega2022phenotypicspectrumof pages 3-5).

Structurally, the mature FGF10 protein adopts the characteristic FGF β-trefoil fold composed of 12 antiparallel β-strands (edirisinghe2024decodingfgffgfrsignaling pages 7-8). Compared to FGF1 and FGF2, FGF10 exhibits a longer β1 strand and less-organized β10 and β11 strands. Additionally, the first five N-terminal residues form a short helix termed gN, which is linked to β1 by a short loop, making the receptor-bound N-terminus more structurally defined (edirisinghe2024decodingfgffgfrsignaling pages 7-8). These structural features place FGF10 within the Fibroblast_GF_fam domain (IPR002209) and IL1/FGF structural superfamily (IPR008996), consistent with the UniProt annotation provided.

## Primary Molecular Function: Paracrine Signaling

FGF10 functions as a secreted paracrine signaling molecule rather than as an enzyme or transporter (bzdega2022phenotypicspectrumof pages 1-3, edirisinghe2024decodingfgffgfrsignaling pages 7-8). Its primary molecular role is to transmit extracellular growth and developmental signals from mesenchymal or stromal cells to adjacent epithelial cells expressing cognate fibroblast growth factor receptors (bzdega2022phenotypicspectrumof pages 3-5, baran2025fibroblastgrowthfactors pages 5-7, baran2025fibroblastgrowthfactors pages 7-8). The N-terminal signal peptide directs FGF10 through the classical ER-Golgi secretory pathway, resulting in protein secretion into the extracellular space where it functions (edirisinghe2024decodingfgffgfrsignaling pages 7-8, bzdega2022phenotypicspectrumof pages 3-5).

FGF10 is predominantly produced by mesenchymal cells and acts locally on neighboring epithelial cells in a paracrine manner (baran2025fibroblastgrowthfactors pages 5-7, baran2025fibroblastgrowthfactors pages 7-8, ornitz2022newdevelopmentsin pages 59-60, yin2020fgf9andfgf10 pages 8-9). This spatially organized signaling is critical for coordinating epithelial-mesenchymal interactions during organogenesis and tissue repair. The protein's activity is physiologically restricted to the local microenvironment through binding to cell-surface heparan sulfate proteoglycans, which stabilize the molecule near its source and prevent long-range diffusion (chen2025fgfbaseddrugdiscovery pages 2-4, baran2025fibroblastgrowthfactors pages 7-8).

## Receptor Specificity and Binding Mechanism

### Primary Receptor: FGFR2b

FGF10 signals predominantly through FGFR2b, the epithelial IgIIIb splice isoform of fibroblast growth factor receptor 2 (edirisinghe2024decodingfgffgfrsignaling pages 7-8, bzdega2022phenotypicspectrumof pages 3-5, edirisinghe2024decodingfgffgfrsignaling pages 5-7). This receptor specificity is a defining characteristic of the FGF7 subfamily, whose members signal exclusively through the "b" isoforms of FGFRs rather than the mesenchymal "c" isoforms (edirisinghe2024decodingfgffgfrsignaling pages 5-7). The interaction between FGF10 and FGFR2b involves an extensive network of contacts with the receptor's D3 domain. Specific FGF10 regions involved in receptor binding include the βB′ strand, βB′-βC loop, N-terminus, β1 and β4 strands, and β7-β8 loop (edirisinghe2024decodingfgffgfrsignaling pages 7-8). Mutagenesis studies have identified Asp76, Arg78, and Thr114 as critical residues for FGF10-mediated cell proliferation (edirisinghe2024decodingfgffgfrsignaling pages 7-8).

### Additional Receptor: FGFR1b

While FGFR2b is the principal and best-characterized receptor, FGF10 can also bind to FGFR1b, the epithelial IgIIIb splice isoform of FGFR1 (ferguson2021fibroblastgrowthfactor pages 2-3, francavilla2022fibroblastgrowthfactor pages 3-4). This broader receptor usage within the "b" isoform family allows FGF10 to signal in multiple epithelial contexts. However, FGFR2b remains the dominant developmental receptor and the most extensively studied (baran2025fibroblastgrowthfactors pages 5-7, edirisinghe2024decodingfgffgfrsignaling pages 8-10). There is no substantial evidence that FGF10 productively binds FGFR1c, FGFR2c, FGFR3, or FGFR4 under physiological conditions (bzdega2022phenotypicspectrumof pages 3-5).

### Heparan Sulfate Proteoglycan Cofactor Requirement

Heparan sulfate proteoglycans (HSPGs) function as essential cofactors for FGF10 signaling (bzdega2022phenotypicspectrumof pages 3-5, francavilla2022fibroblastgrowthfactor pages 3-4, chen2025fgfbaseddrugdiscovery pages 2-4). These include membrane-anchored syndecans and glypicans, as well as extracellular matrix perlecans. HSPGs contain heparan sulfate (HS) chains with sulfated regions that interact with positively charged residues in FGF10's heparan-sulfate-binding site (chen2025fgfbaseddrugdiscovery pages 2-4, farooq2021theroleof pages 5-6). The HS-FGF10-FGFR interaction serves multiple critical functions: it stabilizes the ligand-receptor complex, promotes productive FGFR dimerization and activation, and retains FGF10 within the pericellular matrix near its site of production, thereby restricting signaling to nearby cells (bzdega2022phenotypicspectrumof pages 3-5, francavilla2022fibroblastgrowthfactor pages 3-4, chen2025fgfbaseddrugdiscovery pages 2-4, chen2022rolesofthe pages 3-4, edirisinghe2024decodingfgffgfrsignaling pages 5-7, ferguson2021fibroblastgrowthfactor pages 3-5).

The canonical FGF signaling mechanism requires formation of a ternary FGF-FGFR-HSPG complex at the cell surface, which enables receptor dimerization and trans-autophosphorylation of intracellular tyrosine residues (bzdega2022phenotypicspectrumof pages 3-5, bzdega2022phenotypicspectrumof pages 1-3, farooq2021theroleof pages 5-6, chen2022rolesofthe pages 3-4).

## Downstream Signaling Pathways

FGF10 binding to FGFR2b (or FGFR1b) initiates multiple intracellular signaling cascades that regulate cellular proliferation, survival, migration, differentiation, and metabolism.

### MAPK/ERK Pathway

The RAS-MAPK pathway is a major downstream effector of FGF10-FGFR2b signaling. Following receptor autophosphorylation, the adaptor protein FRS2α is recruited and phosphorylated, which in turn recruits GRB2 and SOS to activate the RAS-RAF-MEK-ERK cascade (francavilla2022fibroblastgrowthfactor pages 2-3, ferguson2021fibroblastgrowthfactor pages 3-5, liu2021fgfmechanismof pages 4-5, liu2026fgffamilyin pages 3-4). In the developing lung epithelium during the pseudoglandular stage, FGF10-FGFR2b signaling preferentially activates ERK phosphorylation and promotes epithelial proliferation and bud outgrowth (yin2020fgf9andfgf10 pages 9-9, yin2020fgf9andfgf10 pages 1-2, yin2020fgf9andfgf10 pages 7-8). This MAPK/ERK activation is the dominant experimentally demonstrated output in lung branching morphogenesis, though pathway weighting can vary depending on tissue and developmental context (yin2020fgf9andfgf10 pages 9-9).

### PI3K/AKT Pathway

FGF10-FGFR2b activation can directly recruit the PI3K p85 regulatory subunit to phosphorylated FGFR2 tyrosine residue Y734 (ferguson2021fibroblastgrowthfactor pages 3-5, ferguson2021fibroblastgrowthfactor pages 5-7). Additionally, FRS2α recruits GAB1, which activates the PI3K-AKT pathway (francavilla2022fibroblastgrowthfactor pages 2-3, ferguson2021fibroblastgrowthfactor pages 3-5, liu2026fgffamilyin pages 3-4). AKT activation leads to downstream effects including mTOR complex 1 activation, phosphorylation of FOXO1 transcription factor, and regulation of survival through inactivation of pro-apoptotic proteins such as Caspase 9, BAD, and BAX (francavilla2022fibroblastgrowthfactor pages 2-3, liu2026fgffamilyin pages 3-4, ferguson2021fibroblastgrowthfactor pages 5-7). While both ERK and AKT can be activated by FGF10, the relative strength varies by context, with ERK activation typically more prominent in embryonic lung development (yin2020fgf9andfgf10 pages 9-9).

### PLCγ and STAT Pathways

Activated FGFR can recruit phospholipase C-gamma (PLCγ), which hydrolyzes PIP2 into IP3 and DAG, causing intracellular calcium release and protein kinase C (PKC) activation (bzdega2022phenotypicspectrumof pages 3-5, francavilla2022fibroblastgrowthfactor pages 2-3, ferguson2021fibroblastgrowthfactor pages 3-5, liu2026fgffamilyin pages 3-4, ferguson2021fibroblastgrowthfactor pages 5-7). Additionally, FGFR signaling can activate STAT proteins (STAT1, STAT3, STAT5), leading to their nuclear translocation and transcriptional regulation (francavilla2022fibroblastgrowthfactor pages 2-3, ferguson2021fibroblastgrowthfactor pages 3-5, liu2021fgfmechanismof pages 4-5, ferguson2021fibroblastgrowthfactor pages 5-7, wang2021fgffgfrsignalingin pages 2-4). These pathways collectively regulate diverse cellular responses including gene expression, proliferation, migration, and differentiation.

## Subcellular Localization and Sites of Action

FGF10 is a classically secreted protein that functions in the extracellular space and pericellular matrix (edirisinghe2024decodingfgffgfrsignaling pages 7-8, baran2025fibroblastgrowthfactors pages 7-8, bzdega2022phenotypicspectrumof pages 3-5). The N-terminal signal peptide (residues 1-37) directs nascent FGF10 into the endoplasmic reticulum, where it enters the secretory pathway through the ER-Golgi system (edirisinghe2024decodingfgffgfrsignaling pages 7-8). Following secretion, FGF10 does not act systemically but is retained locally through interactions with heparan sulfate proteoglycans in the extracellular matrix and on cell surfaces (chen2025fgfbaseddrugdiscovery pages 2-4, baran2025fibroblastgrowthfactors pages 7-8).

The primary site of FGF10 function is outside the producing cell, at the HS-rich surface and matrix surrounding target cells (edirisinghe2024decodingfgffgfrsignaling pages 7-8, baran2025fibroblastgrowthfactors pages 7-8). This represents classic mesenchyme-to-epithelium paracrine signaling: FGF10 is typically produced by mesenchymal or stromal fibroblasts and acts on adjacent epithelial cells expressing FGFR2b (baran2025fibroblastgrowthfactors pages 5-7, bzdega2022phenotypicspectrumof pages 3-5, ornitz2022newdevelopmentsin pages 59-60, yin2020fgf9andfgf10 pages 8-9). In developing lung, for example, FGF10 is expressed in the submesothelial mesenchyme surrounding distal lung buds and signals to epithelial FGFR2b-positive progenitor cells (baran2025fibroblastgrowthfactors pages 5-7, baran2025fibroblastgrowthfactors pages 7-8, yin2020fgf9andfgf10 pages 8-9).

## Primary Biological Function: Lung Branching Morphogenesis

The most comprehensively studied and best-characterized function of FGF10 is its essential role in lung development, particularly in directing branching morphogenesis of the respiratory epithelium.

### Essential Role in Lung Development

FGF10 is absolutely required for lung formation. Genetic deletion of Fgf10 or its receptor Fgfr2b in mice results in lung agenesis, with lung buds failing to develop beyond initial formation (sedighi2021theroleof pages 20-23, baran2025fibroblastgrowthfactors pages 7-8, yin2020fgf9andfgf10 pages 1-2). This demonstrates that FGF10-FGFR2b signaling is not merely contributory but essential for lung organogenesis. When FGF10 or FGFR2b is conditionally disrupted after initial bud formation, lung growth arrests, branching is severely impaired, and epithelial cell death increases (sedighi2021theroleof pages 20-23, baran2025fibroblastgrowthfactors pages 7-8, yin2020fgf9andfgf10 pages 1-2).

### Mechanism of Branching Morphogenesis

During the pseudoglandular stage of lung development, mesenchymal FGF10 signals to epithelial FGFR2b to coordinate multiple aspects of airway development (sedighi2021theroleof pages 20-23, baran2025fibroblastgrowthfactors pages 5-7, yin2020fgf9andfgf10 pages 1-2). FGF10 promotes epithelial chemotaxis toward the mesenchymal signal, helping direct the growth and positioning of distal buds (sedighi2021theroleof pages 20-23). It stimulates epithelial proliferation and outgrowth, drives branching morphogenesis, and regulates epithelial cell survival (baran2025fibroblastgrowthfactors pages 7-8, baran2025fibroblastgrowthfactors pages 5-7, yin2020fgf9andfgf10 pages 1-2, yin2020fgf9andfgf10 pages 7-8).

FGF10-FGFR2b signaling activates the WNT/β-catenin pathway, which increases expression of BMP4 and SOX9 in distal epithelial progenitors while activating SOX2 in proximal regions (baran2025fibroblastgrowthfactors pages 5-7). This establishes proximal-distal patterning: FGF10 helps maintain distal SOX9+/ID2+ epithelial progenitors in an undifferentiated state, supporting continued branching and preserving the progenitor pool, while more proximal cells express SOX2 and differentiate toward bronchial epithelium (baran2025fibroblastgrowthfactors pages 7-8, sedighi2021theroleof pages 20-23, baran2025fibroblastgrowthfactors pages 5-7).

Importantly, FGF10 activity must be precisely regulated. Transient inhibition of FGF10 signaling during embryogenesis causes lung growth arrest and severely impaired branching (baran2025fibroblastgrowthfactors pages 5-7, baran2025fibroblastgrowthfactors pages 7-8). Conversely, prolonged or excessive FGF10 activity paradoxically inhibits normal branching and drives distal epithelial cells toward cyst-like proliferation and differentiation (baran2025fibroblastgrowthfactors pages 7-8). Spatial restriction of FGF10 activity is achieved through negative feedback by Sonic Hedgehog (SHH) signaling from the distal mesenchyme (baran2025fibroblastgrowthfactors pages 5-7, baran2025fibroblastgrowthfactors pages 7-8).

### Alveolar Development and Postnatal Functions

Beyond airway branching, FGF10-FGFR2b signaling contributes to alveolar lineage formation, including development of alveolar myofibroblasts, secondary septa, and differentiation toward alveolar epithelial type II (AT2) cells (sedighi2021theroleof pages 20-23). FGF10 continues to function postnatally in alveolar development, epithelial regeneration, and protection from lung injury (sedighi2021theroleof pages 20-23, baran2025fibroblastgrowthfactors pages 18-19).

## Development of Other Organs

Beyond the lung, FGF10 plays critical roles in the development of multiple organs through similar mesenchymal-to-epithelial paracrine signaling mechanisms.

### Glandular Organs

FGF10 is essential for lacrimal and salivary gland development. High Fgf10 expression occurs in neural crest-derived mesenchyme of the forming salivary gland and in mesenchyme surrounding the developing lacrimal gland epithelial bud in mice (bzdega2022phenotypicspectrumof pages 3-5). Human genetic evidence strongly supports this developmental role: heterozygous pathogenic variants in FGF10 cause aplasia of the lacrimal and salivary glands (ALSG) and contribute to lacrimo-auriculo-dento-digital syndrome (LADD), a multisystem developmental disorder (edirisinghe2025pathologyandtherapeutic pages 8-10, edirisinghe2024decodingfgffgfrsignaling pages 7-8, sedighi2021theroleof pages 20-23). These syndromes involve malformations of multiple organs including ears, teeth, fingers, kidneys, respiratory system, and genitals, demonstrating FGF10's broad developmental importance and dosage sensitivity (sedighi2021theroleof pages 20-23).

### Gastrointestinal System

In the gastrointestinal tract, FGF10 is expressed in mesenchyme and signals to adjacent epithelium (ornitz2022newdevelopmentsin pages 59-60). Loss of Fgf10 or Fgfr2b disrupts development of the stomach, duodenum, cecum, and colon, and can produce duodenal or colonic atresia (ornitz2022newdevelopmentsin pages 59-60). In the colon, Fgfr2b deficiency is associated with increased epithelial apoptosis, abnormal epithelial architecture, and discontinuity of the basal lamina, demonstrating FGF10-FGFR2b's role in maintaining epithelial organization and survival (ornitz2022newdevelopmentsin pages 59-60). Postnatally, FGF10 overexpression increases small intestinal crypt depth and villus height and promotes goblet cell differentiation (ornitz2022newdevelopmentsin pages 59-60).

### Liver

During embryonic liver development, Fgf10 is expressed in mesoderm located near the hepatic endoderm (ornitz2022newdevelopmentsin pages 59-60). Fgf10-null embryos exhibit smaller livers with reduced hepatoblast proliferation and survival, indicating that FGF10 is required for embryonic liver growth and hepatoblast expansion (ornitz2022newdevelopmentsin pages 59-60).

### Other Organs

FGF10 also contributes to tooth development, facial formation during embryogenesis, prostatic growth, thalamocortical axon navigation, and ocular development (edirisinghe2025pathologyandtherapeutic pages 8-10, edirisinghe2024decodingfgffgfrsignaling pages 7-8). FGF10 expression has been documented in the sclera and in the second heart field, indicating roles in eye and cardiac development (bzdega2022phenotypicspectrumof pages 3-5).

## Tissue Repair and Regeneration

The paracrine signaling mechanisms employed during development are redeployed after injury, positioning FGF10 as a candidate regenerative factor. Experimental evidence demonstrates that FGF10 promotes epithelial survival and regeneration across multiple tissues.

### Lung Regeneration

In lung injury models, FGF10 supports epithelial regeneration by activating bronchioalveolar stem cells, promoting their expansion, and helping repair damaged airways (baran2025fibroblastgrowthfactors pages 10-12). It mediates stromal niche support for epithelial regeneration and may mobilize and proliferate mesenchymal stem cells (baran2025fibroblastgrowthfactors pages 10-12). After bleomycin-induced lung injury, FGF10/FGFR2b signaling can induce airway basal stem cells to differentiate into AT2 cells, with some subsequently becoming AT1 cells, suggesting a route toward alveolar repair (baran2025fibroblastgrowthfactors pages 10-12).

### Other Tissues

FGF10 treatment has shown protective effects in multiple injury models. In kidney ischemia/reperfusion injury, FGF10 improved renal function and histological integrity while suppressing excessive autophagy and endoplasmic reticulum stress (chen2022rolesofthe pages 8-10). In intestinal injury, FGF10 promoted repair of resected small bowel through activation of epithelial FGFR2IIIb (chen2022rolesofthe pages 8-10). For skin wounds, FGF10 enhanced healing in diabetic rats and stimulated keratinocyte proliferation while inhibiting endoplasmic reticulum stress, thereby accelerating wound healing and hair growth (chen2022rolesofthe pages 8-10, chen2022rolesofthe pages 10-11).

## Recent Therapeutic Applications: COPD and Emphysema (2023 Evidence)

Recent translational research has identified FGF10 as a promising therapeutic target for chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) and emphysema, supported by compelling preclinical evidence published in 2023.

### Loss of FGF10 in COPD

A landmark 2023 study published in the European Respiratory Journal demonstrated reduced FGF10 expression in alveolar walls and primary interstitial fibroblasts isolated from COPD patient lungs compared to healthy controls (hadzic2023fibroblastgrowthfactor pages 1-2, hadzic2023fibroblastgrowthfactor pages 3-4). Even after in vitro propagation, fibroblasts from end-stage emphysema/COPD lungs maintained persistently reduced FGF10 expression, and cigarette smoke extract further reduced FGF10 expression in both healthy and COPD fibroblasts (hadzic2023fibroblastgrowthfactor pages 3-4). These findings link impaired FGF10 signaling to COPD pathogenesis.

### Mouse Models Demonstrate Causal Role

Mice with genetically impaired FGF10/FGFR2b signaling (Fgf10+/− and Fgfr2b+/− heterozygotes) spontaneously developed emphysema, pulmonary hypertension, and other pathomechanistic features that typically arise from cigarette smoke exposure (hadzic2023fibroblastgrowthfactor pages 1-2, hadzic2023fibroblastgrowthfactor pages 3-4). This establishes that deficient alveolar FGF10 signaling is sufficient to produce disease-like phenotypes, supporting a causal rather than merely correlative relationship.

### Therapeutic Reversal of Established Disease

Most significantly, FGF10 overexpression successfully restored lung alveolar and vascular structure in mice with established cigarette smoke-induced and elastase-induced emphysema and pulmonary hypertension (hadzic2023fibroblastgrowthfactor pages 14-15, hadzic2023fibroblastgrowthfactor pages 1-2, hadzic2023fibroblastgrowthfactor pages 13-14, hadzic2023fibroblastgrowthfactor pages 3-4). This therapeutic intervention reversed rather than merely prevented disease progression. FGF10 treatment initially increased alveolar type 2 (AT2) cell numbers, which gradually returned toward baseline as the FGF10-mediated repair process progressed, suggesting engagement of epithelial regenerative mechanisms (hadzic2023fibroblastgrowthfactor pages 14-15, hadzic2023fibroblastgrowthfactor pages 13-14). The reversal of pulmonary hypertension may be linked to recovery of the pulmonary capillary network and reversal of vascular remodeling (hadzic2023fibroblastgrowthfactor pages 14-15, hadzic2023fibroblastgrowthfactor pages 13-14).

### Human Translational Evidence

The 2023 study validated findings in precision-cut lung slices (PCLS) obtained from COPD patients. Recombinant FGF10 treatment increased AT2 cell numbers in human COPD tissue and activated the canonical β-catenin pathway, identifying a downstream mechanism for epithelial regeneration (hadzic2023fibroblastgrowthfactor pages 14-15, hadzic2023fibroblastgrowthfactor pages 13-14). This human ex vivo evidence improves translational relevance, though clinical efficacy, optimal dosing, delivery route, and long-term safety in patients remain to be established.

### Endothelial Protection Mechanisms

FGF10 also protects pulmonary endothelium in COPD models. It reduces endothelial cell apoptosis and restores damaged glycocalyx components including heparan sulfate, chondroitin sulfate, and syndecan-1 (baran2025fibroblastgrowthfactors pages 13-14, jiang2022fibroblastgrowthfactor pages 1-2). The protective mechanism involves FGF10 activation of FGFR1 and downstream ERK/SOX9/HS6ST1 signaling, which promotes heparan sulfate biosynthesis (baran2025fibroblastgrowthfactors pages 13-14, jiang2022fibroblastgrowthfactor pages 1-2). This illustrates a context where FGFR1-associated activity complements the canonical epithelial FGFR2b axis and contributes to pulmonary vascular protection.

## Current Application Status and Translational Challenges

Based on the preclinical evidence, recombinant FGF10, gene delivery approaches, stabilized FGF10 variants, and downstream pathway stimulation are under investigation for emphysema, pulmonary hypertension, lung injury, wound repair, organoid culture, and regenerative medicine applications (hadzic2023fibroblastgrowthfactor pages 14-15, baran2025fibroblastgrowthfactors pages 10-12, baran2025fibroblastgrowthfactors pages 14-16). However, no approved FGF10-based medicine or proven patient benefit has been established in the cited literature.

Major translational barriers include FGF10's inherent protein instability at physiological temperature, heparan sulfate-dependent tissue distribution requirements, challenges in delivery and dosing, potential for unwanted ectopic growth, context-dependent receptor usage, and possible pro-tumorigenic signaling in certain settings (chen2025fgfbaseddrugdiscovery pages 2-4, baran2025fibroblastgrowthfactors pages 7-8). Engineered thermostable FGF10 variants with improved stability have been developed and show enhanced activity in experimental models, potentially addressing one barrier to therapeutic application (baran2025fibroblastgrowthfactors pages 13-14).

## Conclusions

FGF10 (UniProt O15520) is a secreted paracrine signaling molecule belonging to the FGF7 subfamily of heparin-binding growth factors. It functions primarily through binding to FGFR2b (and to a lesser extent FGFR1b) on epithelial cells, with heparan sulfate proteoglycans serving as essential cofactors. Upon receptor engagement, FGF10 activates multiple downstream pathways including MAPK/ERK, PI3K/AKT, PLCγ, and STAT signaling.

The protein's primary and best-characterized biological function is directing lung branching morphogenesis during development, where mesenchymal FGF10 coordinates epithelial growth, patterning, survival, and differentiation. Genetic loss of FGF10 or FGFR2b results in lung agenesis, establishing its essential developmental role. Beyond the lung, FGF10 is critical for development of lacrimal and salivary glands, gastrointestinal organs, liver, and other tissues, as evidenced by human genetic syndromes caused by FGF10 mutations.

Recent 2023 research has established FGF10 as a promising therapeutic target for COPD and emphysema, with preclinical studies demonstrating that FGF10 overexpression can reverse established disease in mouse models and activate regenerative responses in human lung tissue. These findings represent a significant advance in understanding FGF10's regenerative potential and suggest future clinical applications, though substantial translational challenges remain before therapeutic use in patients can be realized.
