| Category | FGF10 annotation and evidence | Functional interpretation | Key citations |
|---|---|---|---|
| Identity | Human **FGF10** encodes fibroblast growth factor 10, also called **keratinocyte growth factor 2 (KGF-2)**. It belongs to the heparin-binding FGF family and the **FGF7 subfamily** with FGF3, FGF7, and FGF22. | Confirms that UniProt **O15520** is the intended human protein and not a similarly named gene from another organism. | (pqac-00000000, pqac-00000001, pqac-00000007) |
| Protein architecture | FGF10 is synthesized as a **208-amino-acid precursor** of approximately **23.4 kDa**. Residues **1–37** form an N-terminal signal peptide; residues **38–208** encompass the mature FGF domain. Reported glycosylation sites occur at residues 51 and 196. | The signal peptide routes FGF10 through the classical ER–Golgi secretory pathway; the mature extracellular protein supplies the receptor- and heparan-sulfate-binding surfaces. | (pqac-00000000, pqac-00000001, pqac-00000024, pqac-00000027) |
| Fold and structural features | The mature protein adopts the characteristic FGF **β-trefoil fold**, containing 12 antiparallel β-strands. Relative to FGF1/FGF2, FGF10 has a longer β1 strand, less-organized β10–β11 regions, and a short N-terminal helix connected to β1. | These features support classification in the **Fibroblast_GF_fam/IL1–FGF structural superfamily** and help determine receptor selectivity. | (pqac-00000000) |
| Primary molecular function | FGF10 is **not an enzyme or transporter**. It is a locally acting extracellular growth-factor ligand that converts mesenchymal positional and regenerative cues into receptor-tyrosine-kinase signaling in nearby target cells. | Its most precise functional annotation is a **heparan-sulfate-dependent paracrine agonist of epithelial FGFR2b**, regulating proliferation, survival, migration, differentiation, and morphogenesis. | (pqac-00000002, pqac-00000012, pqac-00000018, pqac-00000024) |
| Principal receptor | The best-established receptor is **FGFR2b**, the epithelial IgIIIb splice isoform of FGFR2. FGF10 contacts receptor D2/D3 regions; implicated ligand surfaces include its N terminus, β1 and β4 strands, βB′–βC region, and β7–β8 loop. Asp76, Arg78, and Thr114 have experimentally supported functional importance. | Alternative splicing creates receptor specificity: FGF10 preferentially signals from mesenchyme to cells bearing epithelial **b** isoforms rather than mesenchymal **c** isoforms. | (pqac-00000008, pqac-00000012, pqac-00000014) |
| Additional receptor activity | Some receptor-specificity maps and functional studies support signaling through **FGFR1b**, although FGFR2b remains the characteristic and dominant developmental receptor. Evidence does not support treating FGFR1c, FGFR2c, FGFR3, or FGFR4 as equivalent physiological receptors. | Functional annotation should state **FGFR2b primarily; FGFR1b in selected contexts**, avoiding the overstatement that FGF10 is absolutely exclusive to FGFR2b in every tissue. | (pqac-00000009, pqac-00000011, pqac-00000015) |
| Heparan-sulfate cofactor | Cell-surface or extracellular-matrix **heparan sulfate proteoglycans (HSPGs)**—including syndecans, glypicans, and perlecan—bind FGF10 through sulfated HS chains. HS stabilizes the ligand–receptor complex, facilitates productive FGFR dimerization/activation, and retains FGF10 near its source. | HSPGs both enable signaling and restrict diffusion, thereby creating local morphogen fields and preventing endocrine-like systemic action. They are cofactors, not the signal-transducing receptor. | (pqac-00000016, pqac-00000018, pqac-00000019, pqac-00000021) |
| Receptor activation mechanism | FGF10–HS engagement promotes FGFR dimerization and trans-autophosphorylation of cytoplasmic tyrosines. Activated receptors recruit **FRS2α**, GRB2/SOS, GAB1, PI3K, and PLCγ; FGF10-induced FGFR2 phosphorylation at Y734 can recruit PI3K regulatory subunits and trafficking proteins. | The ligand has no catalytic activity itself; it allosterically assembles an extracellular signaling complex that activates the intracellular FGFR kinase domains. | (pqac-00000012, pqac-00000016, pqac-00000031, pqac-00000034) |
| MAPK pathway | FRS2α–GRB2–SOS activates **RAS–RAF–MEK–ERK**, with possible p38/JNK branches. In pseudoglandular lung epithelium, FGF10–FGFR2b preferentially produced strong ERK activation and promoted epithelial proliferation and bud outgrowth. | MAPK/ERK is the dominant experimentally demonstrated output in developing lung epithelium, though pathway weighting is tissue- and state-dependent. | (pqac-00000030, pqac-00000037, pqac-00000041, pqac-00000042) |
| PI3K pathway | FGFR2b can recruit GAB1/PI3K and activate **PI3K–AKT–mTOR**, supporting survival, metabolism, and growth. Direct FGF10-dependent recruitment of PI3K to FGFR2b has been described, although AKT activation is weaker than ERK in some embryonic-lung contexts. | PI3K/AKT is a genuine downstream branch but should not automatically be described as the dominant FGF10 output in every cell type. | (pqac-00000030, pqac-00000031, pqac-00000034, pqac-00000037) |
| PLCγ and STAT pathways | Activated FGFR can recruit **PLCγ**, which hydrolyzes PIP2 into IP3 and DAG, causing Ca²⁺ release and PKC activation. FGFR signaling can also activate **STAT1/3/5** and nuclear transcription. | These are recognized canonical FGFR outputs; evidence is less often resolved specifically for endogenous FGF10–FGFR2b than for ERK in lung development. | (pqac-00000001, pqac-00000030, pqac-00000031, pqac-00000032) |
| Localization and mode of action | FGF10 enters the ER–Golgi pathway, is secreted, and functions predominantly in the **extracellular/pericellular matrix**. It is commonly produced by mesenchymal or stromal cells and acts on adjacent FGFR2b-positive epithelial cells. | The principal site of function is **outside the producing cell**, at the HS-rich surface and matrix surrounding target cells; this is classic mesenchyme-to-epithelium paracrine signaling. | (pqac-00000024, pqac-00000025, pqac-00000026, pqac-00000028) |
| Lung branching morphogenesis | In developing lung, mesenchymal FGF10 signals to epithelial FGFR2b to direct epithelial growth, migration/outgrowth, survival, branching, and proximal–distal patterning. Genetic loss of Fgf10 or Fgfr2b causes severe growth failure or lung agenesis; conditional inhibition after bud initiation impairs branching. | Lung development supplies the strongest mechanistic model for FGF10: a spatially restricted mesenchymal cue organizes a branching epithelial organ. | (pqac-00000038, pqac-00000039, pqac-00000040, pqac-00000041) |
| Lung progenitor and lineage regulation | FGF10 helps maintain distal **SOX9⁺/ID2⁺** progenitors, activates β-catenin-associated programs, and influences BMP4, SOX9, SOX2, basal-cell fate, alveolar type 2-cell behavior, and later alveolar formation. SHH provides negative feedback that spatially restrains FGF10. | FGF10 is both mitogenic and instructive, but its effects depend on dose, location, and developmental stage; sustained excess can suppress normal branching and produce cyst-like epithelial expansion. | (pqac-00000005, pqac-00000038, pqac-00000039, pqac-00000040) |
| Other organogenesis | FGF10–FGFR2b signaling contributes to lacrimal and salivary gland formation, tooth and facial development, neural guidance, gastrointestinal morphogenesis, liver growth, and other epithelial organs. In the gut, loss disrupts stomach, duodenum, cecum, and colon development; in liver, null embryos show reduced hepatoblast proliferation/survival and smaller livers. | These phenotypes generalize the same core mechanism: mesenchymal FGF10 controls the survival, expansion, patterning, or branching of adjacent epithelia. | (pqac-00000059, pqac-00000060, pqac-00000061, pqac-00000062) |
| Human genetic evidence | Heterozygous pathogenic FGF10 variants cause **aplasia of the lacrimal and salivary glands (ALSG)** and contribute to **lacrimo-auriculo-dento-digital syndrome (LADD)**, demonstrating dosage sensitivity in human glandular development. | Human Mendelian disease strongly corroborates animal and tissue-model evidence that FGF10 is essential for epithelial gland morphogenesis. | (pqac-00000044, pqac-00000060, pqac-00000061, pqac-00000063) |
| Tissue repair and regeneration | Experimental FGF10 promotes epithelial survival and regeneration in lung, intestine, kidney, lacrimal gland, and skin models. Reported effects include intestinal epithelial repair through FGFR2b, reduced injury-associated stress in kidney, and enhanced keratinocyte proliferation and wound healing. | Developmental signaling is redeployed after injury, making FGF10 a candidate regenerative agonist; most evidence remains preclinical. | (pqac-00000046, pqac-00000047) |
| COPD and emphysema evidence | A 2023 study found reduced FGF10 in COPD alveolar walls and interstitial fibroblasts. Partial impairment of Fgf10/Fgfr2b signaling in mice produced emphysema and pulmonary-hypertension features, whereas FGF10 overexpression after cigarette-smoke- or elastase-induced disease restored alveolar and vascular structure. | This provides loss-of-function and therapeutic-rescue evidence that inadequate FGF10 signaling contributes to failed alveolar maintenance and that pathway restoration can be disease-modifying in models. | (pqac-00000051, pqac-00000052, pqac-00000054) |
| Human translational lung models | Recombinant FGF10 increased alveolar type 2-cell numbers in precision-cut lung slices from people with COPD; mouse and human ex-vivo responses implicated canonical β-catenin-associated epithelial repair. | Human tissue data improve translational relevance but do not establish clinical efficacy, optimal dose, delivery route, or long-term safety. | (pqac-00000051, pqac-00000053) |
| Endothelial and glycocalyx repair | In smoke-exposure models, FGF10 reduced endothelial apoptosis and glycocalyx shedding through an **FGFR1–ERK–SOX9–HS6ST1** mechanism, helping preserve heparan sulfate, chondroitin sulfate, and syndecan-1. | This illustrates a context in which FGFR1-associated activity complements the canonical epithelial FGFR2b axis and may contribute to pulmonary vascular protection. | (pqac-00000055, pqac-00000058) |
| Current application status | Recombinant FGF10, gene delivery, stabilized variants, and downstream-pathway stimulation are being investigated for emphysema, pulmonary hypertension, lung injury, wound repair, organoids, and regenerative medicine. No cited evidence establishes an approved FGF10 medicine or proven patient benefit. | Major translational barriers include short protein stability, HS-dependent tissue distribution, delivery and dosing, unwanted ectopic growth, context-dependent receptor use, and possible pro-tumorigenic signaling. | (pqac-00000018, pqac-00000051, pqac-00000056, pqac-00000057) |
