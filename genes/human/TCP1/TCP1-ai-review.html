Gene Description

TCP1 (CCT1, CCT-alpha) is the alpha subunit of the cytosolic chaperonin CCT/TRiC. This group II chaperonin is a hexadecamer of two stacked rings, each containing one copy of each of eight paralogous subunits (CCT1-CCT8), enclosing a folding chamber. Each subunit has an equatorial ATP-binding/ATPase domain, an intermediate hinge domain and an apical substrate-binding domain. CCT1 is one of the higher-affinity ATP-binding subunits. ATP binding and hydrolysis around the ring close the chamber and drive folding. TRiC folds newly synthesized cytoskeletal proteins (actin and alpha/beta-tubulin), G-protein beta subunits, WD40-repeat proteins such as the telomerase cofactor TCAB1, and other cytosolic clients. It cooperates with the co-chaperone prefoldin. A centrosomal pool of CCT, together with the chaperonin-like proteins BBS6, BBS10 and BBS12, assembles the BBSome.

Core Functions

As the alpha subunit of the CCT/TRiC hetero-oligomer, CCT1 binds and hydrolyzes ATP through its equatorial domain and contributes to the ATP-dependent folding of actin, tubulin, G-beta, TCAB1 and other cytosolic clients inside the closed chamber. Folding is a property of the assembled complex, to which CCT1 contributes.
