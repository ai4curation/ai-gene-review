Gene Description

DHX30 is an ATP-dependent RNA helicase with cytosolic and mitochondrial populations. It binds RNA and supports mitochondrial large ribosomal subunit assembly in association with mitochondrial RNA granules and nucleoids. Its endogenous RNA substrates and the remodeling step used during assembly remain unresolved. Pathogenic variants can impair RNA-dependent ATP hydrolysis or RNA unwinding, alter stress-granule behavior and disrupt neurodevelopment.

Core Functions

DHX30 uses ATP to unwind RNA and has cytosolic and mitochondrial populations. Its mitochondrial RNA-granule-associated activity supports large ribosomal subunit assembly. RNA binding and ATP hydrolysis are components of this helicase mechanism. The specific endogenous RNA substrates and the molecular remodeling step during assembly remain unresolved.

DHX30 biological and evidence notes

DHX30 is an ATP-dependent RNA helicase with mitochondrial and cytosolic populations. The 2017 human study demonstrates RNA-dependent ATP hydrolysis and RNA association; the later 2021 study directly demonstrates RNA duplex unwinding with purified full-length protein. These are complementary experiments, not interchangeable descriptions of one assay. The 2017 editorial correction changes an author's surname only. PMID:29100085(https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5673606/), PMID:34020708(https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8140440/), PMID:29304375(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29304375/)

Mitochondrial DHX30 associates with RNA granules and partly overlaps nucleoids. Depletion impairs large ribosomal subunit and monosome assembly. Its mitochondrial RNA immunoprecipitation is broadly distributed; the specific 16S-rRNA result belongs to other factors in that study. A direct endogenous remodeling substrate remains unresolved, and DHX30 is not established as a stable structural mitoribosomal subunit. RNA binding and ATP hydrolysis are components of the RNA-helicase mechanism, not separate physiological cores. PMID:25683715(https://doi.org/10.1016/j.celrep.2015.01.030)

The official products contain 1,194, 1,222 and 1,155 residues. Their distinct N termini were reconstructed from the official sequence features. Historical transcript-variant names and full-length assay constructs have not been mapped unambiguously to all three current products. Neither activity equivalence nor inactivity is inferred from length. MitoCoP distinguishes a mixed canonical/isoform-3 protein group from a mitochondrial-enriched isoform-2 group, which is operationally peripheral membrane-associated. This is not a measurement of membrane face or proof of freely soluble matrix residence. The separate curated matrix assertion is retained with its source limits: Figure 2C of the alternative-initiation paper tests PABPC5, whereas its Figure 3B includes DHX30 in nucleic-acid-associated mitochondrial fractions. UniProt Q7L2E3, PMID:34800366(https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8664129/), PMID:23275553(https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3575844/)

The nucleoid preparation in the 2008 study supports mitochondrial association and does not establish binding to the chromatin of the eukaryotic nucleus. Separately, nuclear crosslinking identifies an H1-5 association, with the preparation and post-lysis limits retained. The inherited nuclear location is kept without inventing a finer nuclear compartment. PMID:18063578(https://doi.org/10.1074/jbc.M708444200), PMID:30021884(https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6166682/)
