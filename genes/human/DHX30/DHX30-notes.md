# DHX30 biological and evidence notes

DHX30 is an ATP-dependent RNA helicase with mitochondrial and cytosolic populations. The 2017 human study demonstrates RNA-dependent ATP hydrolysis and RNA association; the later 2021 study directly demonstrates RNA duplex unwinding with purified full-length protein. These are complementary experiments, not interchangeable descriptions of one assay. The 2017 editorial correction changes an author's surname only. [PMID:29100085](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5673606/), [PMID:34020708](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8140440/), [PMID:29304375](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29304375/)

Mitochondrial DHX30 associates with RNA granules and partly overlaps nucleoids. Depletion impairs large ribosomal subunit and monosome assembly. Its mitochondrial RNA immunoprecipitation is broadly distributed; the specific 16S-rRNA result belongs to other factors in that study. A direct endogenous remodeling substrate remains unresolved, and DHX30 is not established as a stable structural mitoribosomal subunit. RNA binding and ATP hydrolysis are components of the RNA-helicase mechanism, not separate physiological cores. [PMID:25683715](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.celrep.2015.01.030)

The official products contain 1,194, 1,222 and 1,155 residues. Their distinct N termini were reconstructed from the official sequence features. Historical transcript-variant names and full-length assay constructs have not been mapped unambiguously to all three current products. Neither activity equivalence nor inactivity is inferred from length. MitoCoP distinguishes a mixed canonical/isoform-3 protein group from a mitochondrial-enriched isoform-2 group, which is operationally peripheral membrane-associated. This is not a measurement of membrane face or proof of freely soluble matrix residence. The separate curated matrix assertion is retained with its source limits: Figure 2C of the alternative-initiation paper tests PABPC5, whereas its Figure 3B includes DHX30 in nucleic-acid-associated mitochondrial fractions. [UniProt Q7L2E3](https://www.uniprot.org/uniprotkb/Q7L2E3/entry), [PMID:34800366](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8664129/), [PMID:23275553](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3575844/)

The nucleoid preparation in the 2008 study supports mitochondrial association and does not establish binding to the chromatin of the eukaryotic nucleus. Separately, nuclear crosslinking identifies an H1-5 association, with the preparation and post-lysis limits retained. The inherited nuclear location is kept without inventing a finer nuclear compartment. [PMID:18063578](https://doi.org/10.1074/jbc.M708444200), [PMID:30021884](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6166682/)

Ancestral DNA-helicase and G-quadruplex RNA-binding assertions are not rejected merely because the family includes DHX36. The target lies below the actual asserted nodes, and experimental descendant records ground them. A separate mouse DHX30 abstract reports purified-protein DNA untwisting and developmental phenotypes. Human physiological DNA and G-quadruplex substrates remain unresolved, so these are non-core activities. The current FlyBase query's lack of matching experimental rows does not establish absence of historical curator support. [GO_REF:0000033](https://geneontology.org/GO_REF/0000033.html), [PMID:25219788](https://doi.org/10.1089/scd.2014.0077)

AGO1/AGO2 association is RNA-sensitive, and the CFTR proximity-labeling observations do not by themselves establish a binary interface. Supported PKR, DHX58, OAS3, H1-5 and mammalian two-hybrid CFTR pairs permit partner-class refinements without transferring catalytic or signaling activities to DHX30. The generic positive associations retained as non-core follow the user's [scoped project instruction](https://github.com/ai4curation/ai-gene-review/blob/d77f71b566bd5ee0e87e410083f71e5b6f27036b/projects/CLINGEN_MENDELIAN.md#curation-instructions). The dsRNA capture assertion remains unresolved because the relevant target supplementary table could not be recovered; this is not a wrong-gene inference from the article title. ## Follow-up: RNA engagement and stress-dependent localization

The earlier unresolved dsRNA-screen assessment is now distinguished from the gene-level function. The target-specific PMID:21266579 supplementary hit remains unverified. Independently, DHX30 contains a DRBM-domain match (IPR056755/PF24995), its native 42–148 region forms the protein-only NMR construct in [PDB 2DB2](https://www.rcsb.org/structure/2DB2), and purified full-length human protein unwinds an RNA duplex. This supports non-core dsRNA binding without claiming that the NMR experiment contained RNA, measured an affinity or established the contact domain. [PMID:21266579](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3060521/), [PMID:34020708](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8140440/), [InterPro IPR056755](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/interpro/entry/InterPro/IPR056755/)

Wild-type stress-granule localization is distinct from pathological mutant aggregation. The original 2017 supplement, page 15 Figure S5B, shows endogenous DHX30 partially entering ATXN2- and DDX3X-positive granules after stress, with an unstressed comparison. Its cytoplasm annotation is refined to both the diffuse cytosolic pool and the stress-induced granule pool, without exclusive localization or a FMRP-specific identity. The source-specific refinement supplies component coverage without adding a redundant NEW row. [PMID:29100085](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ajhg.2017.09.014), [original Figure S5](https://ars.els-cdn.com/content/image/1-s2.0-S000292971730383X-mmc1.pdf)

The later human HEK293T depletion and zebrafish knockout experiments also show reduced granule formation. These positive results are retained, but granule counts, recruitment and mutant-driven foci do not identify a molecular step that wild-type DHX30 performs in assembling the granule. No NEW stress-granule assembly process or additional core is asserted until an RNA-remodeling, structural or cofactor role is distinguished from effects on translation or stress signaling. DDX3X and DDX6 provide positive annotation comparators, so this is a target-evidence limit rather than a general convention excluding helicases. The inspected GO-CAM index has no target match. [PMID:34020708](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8140440/), [GO:0034063](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/QuickGO/term/GO:0034063)

The inherited RNA-G4 and DNA-helicase assertions originate at PTN001053965 before the DHX30/DHX36 duplication. The narrower DNA-G4-binding assertion at PTN000433435 belongs to a sibling lineage and is not inherited by human DHX30; this distinction does not establish loss of the broader activities. The CFTR MaMTH pair remains a supported transporter-class association, while the separate BioID source retains its proximity-only interpretation. [GO_REF:0000033](https://geneontology.org/GO_REF/0000033), [PMID:35156780](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8842165/), [PMID:36012204](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9408702/)

The natural products differ in their N termini: canonical 1194 aa; a 1222-aa product replacing the first 41 residues with 69 residues; and a 1155-aa product lacking the first 39 residues with an SR-to-MA replacement at the next two positions. Historical transcript-variant labels and assay constructs are not independently equated to all current accessions, and these sequence differences do not establish functional equivalence or inactivity. [UniProt Q7L2E3](https://www.uniprot.org/uniprotkb/Q7L2E3/entry)
