# DNAL1 evidence notes

DNAL1 is a light-chain component of the outer dynein arm. Human binding and disease evidence supports heavy-chain association, alpha-tubulin association and a structural contribution to arm assembly or stability [PMID:15845866; PMID:21496787]. These are complementary features of one motor-complex role, without evidence that DNAL1 itself is an ATPase or autonomous motor.

## Primary source scope

[PMID:15845866, Identification and analysis of axonemal dynein light chain 1 in primary ciliary dyskinesia patients](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15845866/) was independently inspected in the [UNC-hosted original PDF](https://cdr.lib.unc.edu/downloads/dz010w61k), Methods (Expression analysis; Co-immunoprecipitation analysis), Results and Figure 2/3 text. Human 190-residue EGFP-DNAL1 expressed in HEK293T cells was mixed with pig tracheal high-salt axonemal extract, and DNAH5 was recovered relative to an EGFP control. This is positive mixed-species extract association, not purified human binary affinity. Human Northern expression in testis and mouse in-situ expression in node, airway and ependyma are separate observations. The negative screen in 86 PCD patients does not contradict the subsequently identified pathogenic DNAL1 allele.

[PMID:21496787, Primary ciliary dyskinesia caused by homozygous mutation in DNAL1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3146731/) was independently inspected in selected Methods, Results and Figure 1/3/4 text. Human wild-type/N150S DNAL1-Myc was expressed in HEK293T cells and combined with rat tracheal high-salt axonemal extract. Matching DNAL1 amounts required different cell-extract inputs. N150S reduced heavy-chain and alpha-tubulin co-recovery by approximately 80%; the separate cellular decay assay established reduced stability. Respiratory cilia from affected individuals lacked normal outer arms. The work supports the structural interaction role but does not directly measure a purified human ternary bridge, ATP-independent human tether mechanics or mechanosensing.
