# BLOC1S1 manual research and annotation review

## 2026-10-01: current biological synthesis

BLOS1/GCN5L1 contributes structural work to two distinct trafficking assemblies. BLOC-1 supports endosomal cargo sorting and lysosome-related organelle biogenesis. Its reconstituted pallidin-Cappuccino-BLOS1 trimer and the depletion-dependent loss of BLOC-1 components support an integral scaffold role, rather than identifying BLOS1 as a free-standing motor or enzyme [PMID:15102850; PMID:22203680; PMID:16837549; PMID:25898167]. BORC shares BLOS1, BLOS2 and SNAPIN with BLOC-1 but contains a different set of other subunits. Human HeLa BLOS1 depletion destabilizes BORC and disrupts lysosomal Arl8b and peripheral lysosome distribution [PMID:25898167]. The two complexes are represented separately in the core functions; complexes are not placed in the location slot.

GCN5L1 also has cytosolic and mitochondrial pools. Human HepG2 mitochondrial localization and endogenous mouse subcompartment studies are distinct experiments. Its association with ATP5F1A and NDUFA9, the reduction in acetylation after knockdown, and respiratory counter-regulation support an acetylation-regulatory role [PMID:22309213]. Selected Results show increased respiratory activity following depletion, despite a differently worded section heading. The aerobic-respiration assertion is refined to negative regulation of cellular respiration in the tested context. No intrinsic acetyltransferase MF is asserted in the core. Native mitochondrial extract dependence does not disprove catalysis; it leaves the catalytic-site versus cofactor/assembly distinction unresolved. Human AC16 knockdown/overexpression experiments support GCN5L1-dependent Rictor acetylation and stability, while the authors leave catalytic-domain/null-mutant work open [PMID:38281616]. Source row 50 remains UNDECIDED, preserving the positive cellular findings.
