# MAPK8/JNK1 (human; UniProt P45983): functional-annotation research report

## Executive summary

**Identity is verified.** The requested protein is human **mitogen-activated protein kinase 8 (MAPK8)**, conventionally called **c-Jun N-terminal kinase 1 (JNK1)**. It is not MAPK9/JNK2, MAPK10/JNK3, or a MAPK8-interacting scaffold such as MAPK8IP1. Independent literature consistently identifies MAPK8 as JNK1 and places it in the serine/threonine MAP-kinase family, matching the supplied UniProt accession P45983 and its CMGC protein-kinase/MAPK/JNK domain annotations. No gene-symbol or organism conflict was found. (koch2015inhibitorsofcjun pages 1-5, nadel2023jnkcascadeinducedapoptosis—a pages 2-4, nadel2023jnkcascadeinducedapoptosis—a pages 1-2)

Functionally, MAPK8 is an intracellular, ATP-dependent, proline-directed protein kinase. Its core reaction is:

**ATP + protein–Ser/Thr–OH → ADP + protein–Ser/Thr–O-phosphate.**

JNK1 preferentially phosphorylates serine or threonine followed immediately by proline, but physiological specificity also requires docking interactions and localization within signaling complexes. It is activated principally by dual phosphorylation of **Thr183 and Tyr185** in its Thr-Pro-Tyr activation loop. Activated JNK1 phosphorylates transcription factors—notably c-Jun—and numerous cytoplasmic substrates, converting stress, cytokine, immune-receptor, and growth-factor inputs into context-dependent changes in gene expression, survival, apoptosis, metabolism, differentiation, and cytoskeletal behavior. (zeke2016jnksignalingregulation pages 10-10, koch2015inhibitorsofcjun pages 1-5, gehi2022intrinsicdisorderin pages 17-18)

The most important translational development retrieved from 2023–2024 is a 2024 preclinical study identifying MAPK8/JNK1 as a distal B-cell-receptor survival node in chronic lymphocytic leukemia (CLL), including BTK-inhibitor-resistant disease. However, no evidence retrieved establishes an approved MAPK8-selective therapeutic application; commonly used JNK inhibitors have substantial isoform or kinome-selectivity limitations. (saleem2024jnk1inhibitorstarget pages 2-3, saleem2024jnk1inhibitorstarget pages 8-9, saleem2024jnk1inhibitorstarget pages 4-5)

## 1. Identity, nomenclature, family, and isoforms

The identity chain is **Homo sapiens MAPK8 → JNK1/SAPK1c → UniProt P45983**. The literature recognizes three separate JNK genes: MAPK8/JNK1, MAPK9/JNK2, and MAPK10/JNK3. JNK1 and JNK2 are broadly expressed, whereas JNK3 has a more restricted distribution, especially in nervous tissue. This distinction is important because many papers measure “JNK” collectively or use antibodies and inhibitors that do not separate the three gene products. (koch2015inhibitorsofcjun pages 1-5, gehi2022intrinsicdisorderin pages 17-18, nadel2023jnkcascadeinducedapoptosis—a pages 1-2)

The supplied UniProt annotations—protein-kinase superfamily, CMGC Ser/Thr kinase group, protein-kinase domain, MAPK signature, and JNK-family classification—are fully concordant with the literature. Alternative splicing produces approximately 46- and 54/55-kDa JNK1 forms. Sources differ in how they count transcripts or protein isoforms—one review describes four conventional JNK1 forms, whereas a 2023 review counts eight alternatively spliced forms—probably reflecting different transcript/protein annotation conventions. These counts should therefore not be treated as contradictory biochemical evidence. (koch2015inhibitorsofcjun pages 1-5, nadel2023jnkcascadeinducedapoptosis—a pages 2-4)

## 2. Primary molecular function and catalytic specificity

### Catalytic activity

MAPK8/JNK1 is a **protein serine/threonine kinase**. Its kinase domain binds ATP and transfers the terminal phosphate to hydroxyl groups on protein serine or threonine residues. JNKs are **proline directed**: the minimal phosphoacceptor preference is generally **Ser/Thr-Pro**. This local motif is insufficient by itself to define a physiological substrate because short S/T-P sequences are abundant. Productive phosphorylation depends heavily on docking motifs, accessibility, subcellular colocalization, scaffold assembly, and signal duration. (zeke2016jnksignalingregulation pages 10-10)

### Docking-dependent specificity

Many substrates, upstream MAP2Ks, phosphatases, and scaffolds recruit JNK through approximately 9–18-residue **D-motifs**. These bind a negatively charged common-docking region and adjacent hydrophobic groove on the kinase. JIP1-like and NFAT4-like docking motifs represent structurally distinct interaction classes. Because the docking surfaces of JNK1, JNK2, and JNK3 are nearly identical, a demonstrated JNK-binding motif does not by itself establish JNK1 selectivity. (zeke2016jnksignalingregulation pages 10-10)

Canonical substrates include **c-Jun** and **ATF2**. JNK was originally defined through phosphorylation of the c-Jun N-terminal sites **Ser63 and Ser73**, which enhances AP-1-associated transcriptional responses. Reviews report more than 100 JNK substrates and at least 30 transcription factors, but those totals describe the JNK family rather than a rigorously validated JNK1-only substrate set. (koch2015inhibitorsofcjun pages 1-5, gehi2022intrinsicdisorderin pages 17-18)

## 3. Activation and termination of signaling

### Activation loop

Full activation requires phosphorylation of the JNK1 activation-loop sequence **Thr183-Pro-Tyr185**. MKK7 preferentially phosphorylates the threonine, whereas MKK4 preferentially phosphorylates the tyrosine; their combined action can produce synergistic activation. This dual-input mechanism connects JNK1 to MAP3K-driven responses initiated by oxidative stress, inflammatory cytokines, ribotoxic or genotoxic stress, small GTPases, and receptor-associated signaling systems. (koch2015inhibitorsofcjun pages 1-5, nadel2023jnkcascadeinducedapoptosis—a pages 2-4, nadel2023jnkcascadeinducedapoptosis—a pages 1-2)

The pathway is therefore commonly represented as:

**stress/receptor → MAP3K → MKK4/MKK7 → MAPK8/JNK1 → substrates such as c-Jun/ATF2.**

This simple cascade is an abstraction. In cells, scaffold proteins—including JIP-family proteins, POSH and β-arrestin-associated complexes—organize selected upstream kinases, JNK, and substrates. AKT-mediated phosphorylation of cascade components or scaffolds can inhibit signaling. (nadel2023jnkcascadeinducedapoptosis—a pages 13-14, nadel2023jnkcascadeinducedapoptosis—a pages 2-4)

### Signal termination

JNK output is restrained by phosphatases, including dual-specificity MAPK phosphatases and serine/threonine phosphatases. Reported regulators include MKP1/DUSP1, MKP5, PPM1H, PPM1J, PP2A, and PP5. Dephosphorylation, protein degradation, feedback regulation, and relocalization collectively control the amplitude and duration of the response. Much of this evidence is pan-JNK; individual phosphatases should not automatically be assigned as uniquely regulating MAPK8. (nadel2023jnkcascadeinducedapoptosis—a pages 13-14, nadel2023jnkcascadeinducedapoptosis—a pages 2-4)

## 4. Cellular localization

MAPK8/JNK1 is an **intracellular, non-receptor kinase**, not a secreted or transmembrane protein. In unstimulated cells, JNK signaling is commonly organized in cytoplasmic complexes associated with scaffolds and regulatory proteins. Following activation, JNK can translocate into the nucleus through importin-dependent mechanisms involving Imp7, Imp9, and Imp3, where it phosphorylates c-Jun, ATF2, and other transcriptional regulators. (gehi2022intrinsicdisorderin pages 17-18, nadel2023jnkcascadeinducedapoptosis—a pages 2-4)

JNK activity is also reported in cytoskeletal and organelle-associated signaling contexts. These pools provide routes for regulating microtubules, actin-associated structures, trafficking, mitochondrial apoptosis, and localized stress responses. Nevertheless, the strongest retrieved localization evidence is family-level rather than MAPK8-exclusive. A conservative annotation is therefore: **predominantly cytoplasmic and nuclear, with stimulus-dependent nuclear accumulation and context-dependent association with scaffolds, cytoskeleton, and organelles.**

## 5. Core pathways and precise biological role

MAPK8’s primary role is to act as a regulated phosphorylation switch in the **JNK stress-activated MAPK pathway**. It integrates upstream kinase activity and redistributes that information to transcriptional and cytoplasmic substrates. Its output is determined by stimulus identity, signal strength and duration, cell type, scaffold composition, localization, splice isoform, and the concurrent activity of JNK2/JNK3 and other MAPKs. (zeke2016jnksignalingregulation pages 10-10, nadel2023jnkcascadeinducedapoptosis—a pages 2-4)

Principal pathway contexts include:

- **Stress and inflammatory signaling:** ROS, cytokines such as TNF-α and IL-1β, toxins, ribosomal stress, and other cellular injuries activate upstream MAP3Ks and MKK4/MKK7. (koch2015inhibitorsofcjun pages 1-5, nadel2023jnkcascadeinducedapoptosis—a pages 1-2)
- **AP-1 transcription:** phosphorylation of c-Jun and ATF2 alters transcriptional programs governing stress adaptation, inflammation, proliferation, differentiation, and death. (koch2015inhibitorsofcjun pages 1-5, gehi2022intrinsicdisorderin pages 17-18)
- **Apoptosis and survival:** prolonged or compartment-specific JNK activation can promote apoptosis, but JNK1 can also support survival depending on stimulus and cell state. Consequently, “JNK1 is pro-apoptotic” is not an adequate universal annotation. (yu2023componentsofthe pages 11-13, yu2023componentsofthe pages 6-7)
- **Metabolic regulation:** genetic evidence summarized in authoritative reviews indicates a more prominent role for JNK1 than JNK2 in metabolic homeostasis, although pathway compensation complicates knockout interpretation. (zeke2016jnksignalingregulation pages 10-10)
- **Immune-receptor signaling:** recent CLL work places JNK1 downstream of SYK, BTK, and PI3K in distal B-cell-receptor signaling. (saleem2024jnk1inhibitorstarget pages 6-7, saleem2024jnk1inhibitorstarget pages 9-10)

## 6. Recent research, 2023–2024

### Hepatocellular carcinoma: isoform- and stimulus-specific effects

Yu and colleagues, published online **30 October 2023**, used RNA interference in HepG2 and SMMC7721 hepatoma cells. JNK1 depletion sensitized cells to TRAIL-induced apoptosis, whereas depletion of either JNK1 or JNK2 did not materially alter etoposide-induced apoptosis. In contrast, JNK2 depletion reduced early apoptosis after TNF-α/cycloheximide or anisomycin in the reported experiments. Immunoblot quantification used three experiments and reported P<0.05 or P<0.01, but numerical effect sizes were not available in the extracted text. [DOI URL](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00432-023-05473-9). (yu2023componentsofthe pages 11-13, yu2023componentsofthe pages 6-7, yu2023componentsofthe pages 1-2)

The expert interpretation is not that JNK1 has a single oncogenic or tumor-suppressive role. Rather, the results demonstrate stimulus-specific division of labor between JNK1 and JNK2. The authors suggested that selective JNK1 targeting could have value in HCC, but this remains cell-line evidence; ATP-site inhibitor SP600125 cannot reliably distinguish JNK1 from JNK2. (yu2023componentsofthe pages 11-13)

### Chronic lymphocytic leukemia: strongest recent MAPK8-specific translational evidence

Saleem and colleagues, published **November 2024**, identified MAPK8/JNK1 as overexpressed and hyperactivated in CLL. JNK1 mRNA was **3.5-fold higher** in primary CLL samples (n=20) than in healthy CD19+ B cells (n=7), and increased total and phosphorylated JNK1 was detected in **20 of 21** CLL samples. The investigators also analyzed public datasets, lymph-node tissue, and IGHV-defined subgroups. [DOI URL](https://doi.org/10.1084/jem.20230681). (saleem2024jnk1inhibitorstarget pages 2-3, saleem2024jnk1inhibitorstarget pages 3-4)

Four MAPK8/JNK1-directed siRNAs reduced CLL-cell viability, supplying target-specific genetic evidence. Pharmacologically, SP600125, CC-930, and the substrate-binding inhibitory peptide L-JNKi produced more than 30% apoptosis after 24 hours in most of 43 primary samples. Under the study’s classification criteria, **49%** were responsive and **30%** intermediate. Normal B cells from five donors showed no survival effect at the highest tested concentrations. The pharmacology should nevertheless be interpreted more cautiously than the siRNA experiments because these compounds do not establish complete JNK1 or kinome selectivity. (saleem2024jnk1inhibitorstarget pages 2-3, saleem2024jnk1inhibitorstarget pages 4-5)

Mechanistically, SYK inhibition lowered phospho-JNK1 by **30–50%**, PI3K inhibition by **45–59%**, and SYK knockdown by **42–67%**. JNK1 inhibition decreased phosphorylation or expression of c-Jun and the survival proteins BCL2 and MCL1, supporting placement of JNK1 downstream of proximal B-cell-receptor kinases. (saleem2024jnk1inhibitorstarget pages 6-7)

Activity extended to three primary CLL samples resistant to BTK- or combined BTK/SYK-directed treatment, including cases with BTK or PLCγ2 alterations. In one resistant patient-derived NOG-mouse xenograft, two weeks of SP600125 reduced splenic CLL representation from **16.8% to 7.21%**, with n=6 per group. In TCL1-transgenic mice, treatment reduced leukemia-associated organ enlargement and signaling, lowered regulatory T cells from **1.38×10⁶ to 0.56×10⁶**, and increased CD4+ effector-cell degranulation/cytotoxic function without significantly reducing total CD4+ or CD8+ cell numbers. (saleem2024jnk1inhibitorstarget pages 12-13, saleem2024jnk1inhibitorstarget pages 8-9, saleem2024jnk1inhibitorstarget pages 13-13)

These results constitute a credible preclinical application—using JNK1-directed intervention to bypass proximal BCR-inhibitor resistance—but not a demonstrated clinical treatment. The model systems, small resistant-case series, and imperfect inhibitor selectivity are material limitations.

## 7. Applications and real-world implementation

### Research and assay use

MAPK8 activity is routinely used as a readout of cellular stress and MAPK signaling, generally through phospho-Thr183/Tyr185 immunoblotting, immunohistochemistry, flow-based assays, or measurement of phosphorylated c-Jun. A 2024 study demonstrated monitoring of endogenous MAPK8 phosphorylation using a CRISPR-inserted, 1.3-kDa HiBiT tag and a homogeneous BRET immunoassay, illustrating a practical platform for drug screening and stimulus-response profiling while preserving endogenous gene regulation. The retrieved text did not provide assay-performance statistics sufficient for quantitative comparison with conventional methods.

### Therapeutic development

JNK-directed medicinal chemistry has explored ATP-competitive, covalent, allosteric, and substrate-docking inhibitors. The central expert concern is selectivity: ATP-binding sites are highly conserved across JNK paralogs and the broader kinome, while JNK1–3 also have nearly identical docking surfaces. Consequently, apparent “JNK1 inhibitor” phenotypes require genetic corroboration, paralog profiling, and broad kinase-selectivity measurements. (zeke2016jnksignalingregulation pages 10-10, koch2015inhibitorsofcjun pages 1-5)

CC-90001, derived from CC-930, has undergone phase I/II testing in pulmonary fibrosis, demonstrating clinical implementation of JNK-pathway pharmacology rather than validated MAPK8-specific therapy. The cited discussion reported respiratory infections in 21% of participants, while erythrocytes, platelets, granulocytes, and T cells were reportedly unaffected. These data should not be interpreted as proof that selectively inhibiting MAPK8 is safe or effective because the compound and clinical context are not strictly JNK1-specific. (saleem2024jnk1inhibitorstarget pages 13-13)

Open Targets aggregates MAPK8 associations with cancer, neurodegenerative disease, xenobiotic responses, poisoning, and muscle cramps. These associations support biological and translational relevance but are heterogeneous database-level evidence, not proof that MAPK8 is causal or clinically actionable in every listed condition. (OpenTargets Search: -MAPK8)

## 8. Expert assessment and annotation confidence

**High-confidence annotation:** MAPK8/P45983 is human JNK1, an intracellular CMGC-family MAP kinase that catalyzes ATP-dependent phosphorylation of proline-directed serine/threonine sites. It is activated by MKK4/MKK7-mediated phosphorylation of Thr183/Tyr185 and transmits stress and receptor signals to c-Jun, ATF2, and other substrates. Its function is executed in scaffolded cytoplasmic complexes and, following activation, in the nucleus. (zeke2016jnksignalingregulation pages 10-10, koch2015inhibitorsofcjun pages 1-5, gehi2022intrinsicdisorderin pages 17-18)

**Moderate-confidence, context-dependent annotation:** JNK1 participates in apoptosis, survival, inflammation, metabolism, cytoskeletal regulation, and oncogenic signaling. The direction of effect cannot be inferred from activation alone; it depends on cell type, stimulus, localization, signal duration, and paralog compensation. (zeke2016jnksignalingregulation pages 10-10, yu2023componentsofthe pages 11-13, yu2023componentsofthe pages 6-7)

**Promising but preclinical application:** MAPK8/JNK1 inhibition in CLL, especially BTK-inhibitor-resistant disease, is supported by patient-sample, RNAi, and mouse-model evidence with substantial quantitative effects. Clinical efficacy and therapeutic index remain unproven. (saleem2024jnk1inhibitorstarget pages 2-3, saleem2024jnk1inhibitorstarget pages 12-13, saleem2024jnk1inhibitorstarget pages 8-9)

**Principal evidence gap:** Much of the established JNK literature is family-level. Claims about mitochondrial localization, individual substrates, disease causality, or inhibitor efficacy should be labeled pan-JNK unless MAPK8-specific genetics, isoform-resolved biochemistry, or selective pharmacology is available.
