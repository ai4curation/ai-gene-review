## Assembly, stress and compartment

The main human disease and cell studies establish an assembly factor rather than a respiratory-chain catalytic subunit. Patient complementation, native-complex analysis and mitochondrial pulse-chase experiments support complex IV biogenesis and stability of its newly synthesized subunits. The early study also reported surviving acute-knockdown cells without the patient-like COX decrease; that result is retained as context rather than omitted. Oxidant challenge raises mature COA8 and complementation protects complex IV subunit abundance, but these experiments do not establish ROS-scavenging chemistry. [PMID:25175347; PMID:30552096]

The 2014 mitochondrial targeting experiments did not resolve a membrane leaflet. Later fractionation found endogenous and tagged COA8 loosely associated with membranes. Digitonin protection alone was inconclusive; hypotonic-shock protection and super-resolution imaging support a matrix-facing C terminus. The same paper's Discussion contains an IMS-facing phrase that conflicts with its Results. The specific localization uses the experimental Results and records that internal discrepancy rather than inventing a correction notice. Broad location replacements explicitly cite the independent later evidence while preserving every original source. The MitoCoP table independently establishes positive mitochondrial detection, not precise topology. [PMID:30552096; PMID:34800366]

## COX10 association and molecular scope

A genuine public repository copy of the 2024 bioRxiv v2 preprint was recovered and its identity checked against official bioRxiv/Crossref metadata. It remains labelled as a preprint. Human 143B HA-SILAC immunopurification and GFP co-IP identify COX10 association; patient rescue and W174A experiments separate binding/COX10 abundance from successful heme A and complex IV restoration. The study measures mitochondrial heme pools and models a protein interface; it does not directly measure purified COX10 turnover. One enzyme-binding core therefore describes the observed association together with established assembly/stabilization processes. No intrinsic catalytic, chaperone, antioxidant or scaffold activity is assigned. The two CLUH-specific binding assertions are not retargeted to COX10, and an additional nested binding annotation is unnecessary. [DOI:10.1101/2024.04.02.587738]

## Historical apoptosis evidence

The older mouse paper was recovered as authentic author-uploaded article text. Mouse Apop-1 expression caused cytochrome c release and caspase activation; cyclosporin A and cyclophilin-D deficiency protected, and antisense expression reduced stimulus-induced death. These observations are positive evidence for the original pathway, not merely a name-based prediction. The paper distinguishes a 194-residue mouse construct and a different C-terminal splice form, so neither is automatically equated with present human products. Later human patient tissue, fibroblast and tagged-expression experiments did not reproduce a general proapoptotic phenotype. The electronic human inference is retained as non-core with these construct and context limits, without declaring the original experiments disproven. A later authentic abstract reports IGF-1 protection in the studied vascular smooth-muscle setting. [PMID:16782708; PMID:18977203; PMID:25175347]

An intake lead initially confused PMID:16712791 with the historical apoptosis study. Its genuine abstract is actually a human cDNA sequence resource. That error was corrected during discovery; it was never an original GOA citation or a basis for a scientific decision, and its unused cache is omitted from the publication proposal.
