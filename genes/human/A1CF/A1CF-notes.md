# A1CF review notes

## 2026-08-08 — initial review

A1CF is a three-RRM, nucleocytoplasmic-shuttling RNA-binding protein. Its best-established biochemical activity is recognition of the single-stranded APOB editing substrate and recruitment of the catalytic deaminase APOBEC1. A1CF binds the 11-nt mooring region rather than double-stranded APOB RNA [PMID:11871661, "ACF binds with high affinity to single-stranded but not double-stranded apo-B mRNA."]. Recombinant A1CF and APOBEC1 are sufficient for APOB C-to-U editing in vitro [PMID:10669759, "ACF and apobec-1 comprise the minimal protein requirements for apo-B mRNA editing in vitro."]. Human A1CF also supports APOBEC1-dependent APOB editing in a reconstituted HEK293T system, although less effectively than RBM47 [PMID:30844405, "human A1CF can mediate APO1 editing activity on APOB RNA in this cell-based assay system"].

The editosome annotation is therefore valid as biochemical capacity, but A1CF should not be described as the uniquely essential physiological APOBEC1 cofactor. A viable mouse null showed normal editing across tested adult tissues [PMID:28069890, "A1CF does not act as the APOBEC1 complementation factor in vivo under normal physiological conditions"]. A later combined-knockout study reconciled this with the biochemical work: A1CF and RBM47 contribute redundantly and in a tissue- and target-dependent manner [PMID:30309881, "A1CF and RBM47 each function independently, yet interact in a tissue-specific manner, to regulate the activity and site selection"].

The APOBEC1-A1CF complex remains associated with edited APOB RNA and protects it from nonsense-mediated decay [PMID:12881431, "The APOBEC1-ACF complex edits and remains associated with the edited RNA to protect it from NMD."]. Consequently, the legacy `protein stabilization` annotation from this paper is a term-selection error; the evidence supports edited-mRNA stabilization and negative regulation of NMD.

A separate physiological role is regulation of alternative splicing in hepatocytes. Hepatic loss of A1cf alters isoforms of ketohexokinase and glycerol kinase and thereby changes fructose/glycerol metabolism [PMID:31597092, "a previously unreported function of A1CF as a regulator of alternative splicing"]. This supports adding regulation of alternative mRNA splicing as a core process, while keeping the downstream metabolic phenotypes non-core.

The `chromosomal 5-methylcytosine DNA demethylation pathway` NAS annotation is not verifiable from the cached abstract. The paper describes TET1 and APOBEC1, not A1CF [PMID:21496894, "Tet1 and Apobec1 are involved in neuronal activity-induced, region-specific, active DNA demethylation"]. Because the full text is unavailable, the conservative action is `UNDECIDED`, not a claim of misattribution.

Current QuickGO checks confirmed the proposed terms and labels: GO:1990825 `sequence-specific mRNA binding`, GO:0000381 `regulation of alternative mRNA splicing, via spliceosome`, GO:0048255 `mRNA stabilization`, GO:2000623 `negative regulation of nuclear-transcribed mRNA catabolic process, nonsense-mediated decay`, and GO:0019899 `enzyme binding`.

Follow-up literature strengthens the splicing assignment in human cells. Homozygous A1CF deletion in human beta-cell models caused 1,907 differential-splicing events and altered SLC7A2 exon choice [PMID:40774250, "A1CFKO cells showed profound alternative splicing changes, with 1,907 differentially spliced events in 1,372 genes"]. Independent reanalysis of mouse A1cf knockout RNA-seq and PAR-CLIP data also supports direct, position-dependent regulation of liver cassette exons [PMID:32560708, "Here, we were able to apply EIG to identify A1CF as a regulator of the liver splicing program, then support this result using several lines of experimental and computational evidence based on motif maps, RNA-seq from a A1cf knockout in mouse, and PAR-CLIP binding assays."].

A1CF additionally has a transcript- and cell-type-specific stabilization role outside the APOB editosome: mouse A1cf binds an AU-rich IL6 3'-UTR region, and reduced A1cf dosage shortens IL6 mRNA half-life in Kupffer cells [PMID:20406809, "Recombinant ACF bound an AU-rich region in the IL-6 3′-untranslated region with high affinity and IL-6 mRNA half-life was significantly shorter in KC isolated from Acf +/− mice compared with wild-type controls."]. This is proposed for human A1CF by orthology and kept non-core.
