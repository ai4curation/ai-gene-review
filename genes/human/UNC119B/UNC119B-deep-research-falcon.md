# Human UNC119B (UniProt A6NIH7): functional annotation research report

**Identity verified.** The target is human **UNC119B**, protein unc-119 homolog B: a 2025 experimental study explicitly identifies its full-length human UNC119B reagent as **UniProt A6NIH7**. It is not UNC119A, the other human paralog, or PDE6D, a related lipid-cargo carrier. UNC119A and UNC119B share approximately 59% sequence identity. Their related immunoglobulin-like β-sandwich/PDE6D-like folds form hydrophobic cargo-binding pockets, consistent with the domain annotations supplied in the question. Critically, zebrafish *unc119a* has been described as closer to **human UNC119B**, whereas zebrafish *unc119b* is closer to **human UNC119A**; cross-species phenotypes must therefore be assigned by orthology, not symbol alone. (horne2025pskh1kinaseactivity pages 7-8, yelland2021thestructuraland pages 1-2)

## Primary molecular function

UNC119B is principally a **soluble carrier for proteins bearing an N-terminal myristoyl group**. It binds and shields the hydrophobic lipid—and contacts neighboring cargo residues—allowing membrane-associated proteins to move through aqueous cytosol. It is **not established as an enzyme**: its defining biochemical activity is cargo binding, solubilization and regulated release, rather than catalysis of myristoylation or a phosphodiesterase reaction. PDE6D has a related fold but is principally characterized as a carrier of *prenylated* cargo; that function should not be substituted for UNC119B’s experimentally demonstrated myristoyl-cargo specificity. (yelland2021thestructuraland pages 1-2, wright2011anarl3unc119rp2gtpase pages 1-2, jaiswal2016novelbiochemicaland pages 4-4)

Biochemical specificity is substantial, not merely recognition of any lipidated protein. In a 2016 study using purified proteins and **N-myristoylated N-terminal peptides**, UNC119B bound NPHP3 with a dissociation constant (*K*₍d₎) of **0.020 nM**, Cystin1 **0.059 nM**, GNAT1 **5.76 nM**, RP2 **47.2 nM**, and the non-ciliary comparator Src **145.12 nM**. A separate 2021 peptide-assay series measured **0.17 nM for NPHP3** and **72.59 nM for PCMTD1**, a **427-fold** span across its tested UNC119B ligands. These are results from different peptide assays, not interchangeable estimates for full-length cargo in cells. Small residues adjacent to the myristoylated glycine favor tight binding, but the 2021 comparison showed that a high-affinity motif defined for UNC119A does **not** predict identical affinities for UNC119B. Figure 1J of that study presents the side-by-side measured affinities. (jaiswal2016novelbiochemicaland pages 4-4, yelland2021thestructuraland pages 4-4, yelland2021thestructuraland media 0a236655, yelland2021thestructuraland pages 1-2)

**Cargo release is regulated by Arf-like GTPases.** ARL3 in its GTP-bound state releases high-affinity cargo from UNC119B; both ARL3 and ARL2 can release weaker-binding cargo, such as RP2 and Src, under the tested conditions. GNAT1 is a qualification to a strict two-class rule: ARL2 produced **partial** release from UNC119B. The 2021 structures capture UNC119B bound separately to a myristoylated LCK peptide and ARL3–GTP analogue. ARL3 binding rearranges the hydrophobic pocket, including residue Y194, in a manner that disfavors the bound lipid. An acidic segment near the UNC119B pocket may additionally influence release, but that **UNC119B-specific loop mechanism remains a structural proposal**, not an established cellular requirement. (jaiswal2016novelbiochemicaland pages 4-4, yelland2021thestructuraland pages 4-8, yelland2021thestructuraland pages 9-10)

## Cellular location and principal biological pathway

UNC119B acts **inside the cell**, particularly in the cytosol-to-primary-cilium delivery pathway; it is not characterized as a secreted protein or a transmembrane channel. In retinal pigment epithelial (RPE) cells, tagged UNC119B was enriched at the **ciliary transition zone and proximal cilium**, whereas tagged UNC119A was predominantly centrosomal. The evidence therefore supports a carrier that operates near the ciliary entry region and accompanies cargo into or toward the cilium, rather than a permanently resident ciliary scaffold. (wright2011anarl3unc119rp2gtpase pages 2-3, wright2011anarl3unc119rp2gtpase pages 7-8)

The clearest cellular cargo is **NPHP3**, a myristoylated nephrocystin. In RPE cells expressing a tagged **murine NPHP3 N-terminal 1–200-residue reporter**, siRNA against UNC119B, ARL3 or the ARL3 GTPase-activating protein **RP2** markedly reduced reporter localization to cilia; UNC119A or ARL2 depletion did not. Each tested siRNA reduced its target transcript to **less than 15% of control**. Wild-type UNC119B rescued the phenotype following UNC119B 3′-UTR-directed depletion, whereas a mutant defective in myristoyl-peptide binding failed to rescue. The mutant remained near the basal body/transition zone instead of extending into the proximal cilium. Depletion did not comparably disrupt the tested non-myristoylated ciliary G-protein-coupled receptors. These experiments give stronger UNC119B-specific causal evidence than cargo binding alone, while their reporter and cell-line design limits direct extrapolation to endogenous, full-length human NPHP3 in tissues. (wright2011anarl3unc119rp2gtpase pages 7-8, wright2011anarl3unc119rp2gtpase pages 1-2)

A coherent pathway model is **myristoylated cargo capture by UNC119B → soluble transport toward the cilium → cargo discharge promoted by ciliary ARL3–GTP → reset of the ARL3 cycle by RP2**. ARL13B is an upstream ciliary activator, or guanine-nucleotide exchange factor, for ARL3. A **2023** study showed that TULP3-dependent delivery of ARL13B to cilia affects ciliary enrichment of the downstream myristoylated proteins NPHP3 and CYS1. This adds pathway context, **not** a new UNC119B-specific perturbation: TULP3’s effects on those cargoes cannot all be ascribed directly to UNC119B. Cargo release and residence are also influenced by other ciliary localization and retention mechanisms. (wright2011anarl3unc119rp2gtpase pages 6-7, wright2011anarl3unc119rp2gtpase pages 8-10, palicharla2023interactionsbetweentulp3 pages 10-12)

## Recent development: an additional signaling role

The strongest newer **UNC119B-specific** finding is a **2025** study of protein serine kinase H1 (**PSKH1**). In human-cell interaction experiments, UNC119B associated with PSKH1; experiments with purified full-length human UNC119B/A6NIH7, cross-linking and radiometric kinase assays showed that UNC119B binds the PSKH1 kinase-domain region and **increases PSKH1 phosphorylation of an assay peptide**. Unexpectedly, PSKH1 mutants disrupting its N-terminal myristoylation-site Gly2, palmitoylation-site Cys3, or both still responded to UNC119B. Thus this interaction is **lipidation-independent allosteric regulation**, distinct from UNC119B’s established myristoyl-shuttle mechanism. The assays also found UNC119B-dependent activation without a Ca²⁺ requirement. Its precise endogenous signaling consequence, binding structure and site of action within living cells remain unresolved; proposed secretory-organelle context should not be presented as demonstrated UNC119B localization. (horne2025pskh1kinaseactivity pages 7-8, horne2025pskh1kinaseactivity pages 6-7, horne2025pskh1kinaseactivity pages 5-6)

A separate **2025** study found that inhibiting **UNC119** with squarunkin A displaced LCK from the immunological synapse, reduced downstream ZAP70-associated T-cell signaling and impaired proliferation of T-acute lymphoblastic leukemia cells; inducible genetic UNC119 depletion supported the proliferation result. LCK’s cellular distribution changed without a comparable loss of total or activating-Y394-phosphorylated LCK. These are potentially useful **preclinical** applications of the lipid-cargo trafficking concept, **not proof that UNC119B specifically mediates the T-cell effect**: the reported pharmacological and depletion experiments did not establish paralog-specific causality or UNC119B-selective inhibition. The 2021 UNC119B–myristoylated-LCK structure supports biochemical *capacity* to bind LCK, but does not resolve which paralog is necessary at the synapse. No clinical UNC119B-targeted implementation follows from these studies. (samarakoon2025unc119regulatestcell pages 8-10, samarakoon2025unc119regulatestcell pages 1-2, yelland2021thestructuraland pages 8-9, yelland2021thestructuraland pages 4-8)

## Functional-annotation assessment

**High-confidence primary annotation:** intracellular, N-myristoylated-protein-binding and solubilizing carrier involved in selective ciliary delivery of NPHP3; cargo release is coupled to ARL3 activity. **Additional experimentally demonstrated biochemical activity:** lipidation-independent positive regulation of PSKH1 kinase in the studied assay systems. **Important uncertainties:** physiological full-length cargo repertoires, the extent of human tissue-specific paralog redundancy, the proposed acidic-loop mechanism, and whether UNC119B rather than UNC119A mediates reported LCK/T-cell drug responses. Neither NPHP3-linked ciliopathy nor PSKH1-associated disease should automatically be described as a proven **UNC119B-caused human disease** on the evidence presented here. (wright2011anarl3unc119rp2gtpase pages 7-8, jaiswal2016novelbiochemicaland pages 4-4, yelland2021thestructuraland pages 9-10, horne2025pskh1kinaseactivity pages 7-8, samarakoon2025unc119regulatestcell pages 8-10)
