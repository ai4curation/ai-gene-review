## Completed source synthesis, 2026-10-13

The complete review retains the 67 genuine source assertions and both official product identities. Two compact core activities are supported: sequence-specific chromatin-loop anchoring and RNAPII DNA-binding transcription-factor activity. CTCF supplies a structural anchor and locus-specific regulatory contribution; cohesin supplies the extrusion machinery. Human MYC repression, APP activation and MHC-II/apolipoprotein promoter experiments establish context-dependent transcriptional effects, rather than an activator or repressor label inferred from zinc fingers alone. The older human experiments and the separate chicken-fragment/quail-host experiments remain distinct. [PMID:8649389](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8649389/), [PMID:9407128](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9407128/), [PMID:18347100](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2292219/), [PMID:19322193](https://doi.org/10.1038/emboj.2009.81).

The direct human fragment assays demonstrate motif-specific recognition and methylation-sensitive binding to mouse H19/Igf2 imprinting-control DNA and chicken insulator DNA. They do not establish all human germline imprinting steps. The independent neonatal mouse liver experiments connect inherited allele-specific CTCF occupancy, looping and enhancer restriction. The current 399-residue product is not any of these engineered finger fragments: it deletes the first 328 residues of the 727-residue sequence, including two complete fingers and part of a third. Sequence reconstruction is exact, whereas equivalent function remains unresolved. [PMID:17827499](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17827499/), [PMID:16815976](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1484419/).

Human contact assays and CTCF-site inversions support architectural participation. The CCAT1-L paper's correction changes a supplementary antisense sequence and does not withdraw its results. Its contact assays are distinct from the proposed RNA allostery explanation. The original PMID:18654629 annotation source studies nucleosome positioning and cites looping as prior work; its looping citation is marked MISCITED while the biological looping assertion is retained with direct independent support. [PMID:24662484](https://doi.org/10.1038/cr.2014.35), [corrigendum](https://doi.org/10.1038/cr.2014.117), [PMID:18654629](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2453330/), [PMID:26276636](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4642453/).

The mouse Jpx sources support conserved RNA binding and promoter-specific chromatin recognition, together with RNA-sensitive anchor occupancy. The early study directly measures RNA/protein binding and Xist promoter recognition; its proposed P1 loop is not itself measured. The later study measures motif-dependent P2 contacts and genome-wide contact changes. Its competition assay mixes RNA and DNA before CTCF, so it does not directly time eviction of protein already bound to DNA. RNA does not universally stabilize CTCF loops, and neither source establishes activity of the current short human product. [PMID:23791181](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3777401/), [PMID:34856126](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34856126/).

The BAG-1 source explicitly reports that “CTCF inhibited BAG-1 promoter activity”. Its BORIS-focused title is not a wrong-gene finding. The selected machine cache says full text, but actually contains Introduction/Discussion without Methods/Results; direct recovery returned failures or a challenge page. Positive promoter binding and repression are retained with curator deference, and complete assay verification remains UNVERIFIED. The nucleolar study and historical mapping study remain abstract-only at the reviewer access boundary, with their explicit positive results preserved. [PMID:18413740](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2733164/), [PMID:16595548](https://doi.org/10.1242/jcs.02890), [PMID:9591631](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9591631/).

Human centromeric occupancy and CENP-E recruitment are positive results at the tested conditions. The 2015 binding experiments compare bacterial MBP-CENP-E fragments with wheat-germ translated CTCF; they are not a homogeneous two-protein purification. The source reports that “CENP-E binding decreased” after CTCF depletion, with cell-cycle and region controls. The 2026 human HCT116-AID study instead finds unchanged CENP-E recruitment after three days and measures centromere spring/plate maintenance. This difference is preserved, not described as a retraction. Regulation of centromeric sister cohesion remains UNDECIDED because the original statement was prospective and the newer partial-RAD21 comparator preserves sister cohesion. [PMID:26321640](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4633288/), [PMID:18550811](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18550811/), [PMID:41486985](https://doi.org/10.1242/jcs.264181).

Endometrial-cell assays support contextual growth regulation. Wild-type expression can suppress MTT/colony endpoints, whereas partial KLE depletion does not significantly change proliferation; the authors state that “there is not a simple linear relationship”. This positive but context-dependent process is NON_CORE. Localization is also nonexclusive: human nucleus/nucleoplasm, mitotic chromosomes and specific nucleolar conditions can coexist. HPA independently reports supported nucleoplasmic staining in A-431, U-251MG and U2OS, without product-specific resolution. [PMID:28319062](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28319062/), [PMID:16107875](https://doi.org/10.1038/sj.emboj.7600793).
