Gene Description

KIF7 is a 1343-residue member of the kinesin-4 family and the vertebrate counterpart of Drosophila Costal-2, with which it shares PANTHER family PTHR24115 as a sister subfamily. It has an N-terminal kinesin motor domain, a coiled-coil stalk that supports homodimerisation, and a long C-terminal region carrying the GLI-binding and NPHP1-binding determinants. Despite the intact motor domain, KIF7 is not a transport motor: purified KIF7 binds microtubules with high affinity but shows no detectable movement, because it cannot release ADP in response to microtubule binding. Instead it binds the plus ends of growing microtubules, where it slows growth and promotes catastrophe in an ATP-hydrolysis-dependent manner. In cells this makes KIF7 the organiser of the distal tip of the primary cilium: it limits cilium length, keeps the axoneme correctly modified and stable, and creates a single tip compartment. KIF7 concentrates at the ciliary base when Hedgehog signalling is off and redistributes to the tip when the pathway is activated, and it tethers the GLI transcription factors together with their inhibitor SUFU at that tip; it also delivers the prolyl hydroxylase P4HA2 there. Through this one scaffolding activity KIF7 acts in both directions on the pathway downstream of Smoothened - restraining ligand-independent GLI2 activation and preventing ectopic activation of GLI-SUFU complexes scattered along the axoneme, while also promoting signalling by driving SUFU turnover and SUFU-GLI dissociation and by protecting full-length GLI3 from processing into its repressor form. It is not required for intraflagellar transport or for entry of pathway components into the cilium. In humans, loss of KIF7 causes a group of ciliopathies - acrocallosal syndrome, hydrolethalus syndrome 2, Joubert syndrome 12 and Al-Gazali-Bakalinova syndrome - with polydactyly, corpus callosum and other brain malformations, and skeletal defects, and KIF7 missense alleles act as modifiers across the wider ciliopathy spectrum.

Core Functions

Scaffolding of the GLI regulatory complex at the tip of the primary cilium. KIF7 brings the GLI transcription factors, their inhibitor SUFU and accessory enzymes such as the prolyl hydroxylase P4HA2 together in one place, using its C-terminal GLI-binding region while remaining anchored to axonemal microtubule plus ends. It performs no chemistry on these partners and does not carry them into the cilium - other motors do that - but without KIF7 the GLI-SUFU complex disperses into ectopic tip-like compartments along the axoneme and P4HA2 never reaches the cilium at all. This single adaptor activity accounts for both regulatory signs: it keeps GLI2 from being activated without ligand and full-length GLI3 from being processed into its repressor, and it permits SUFU turnover and SUFU-GLI dissociation when the pathway turns on.

Suggested Questions for Experts

Q: KIF7 hydrolyses ATP and needs that hydrolysis to promote microtubule catastrophe, yet it cannot step. Is there a GO molecular function that captures a nucleotide-driven, non-translocating plus-end regulator, or does the ontology force such proteins to choose between "microtubule motor activity", which is false, and "microtubule plus-end binding", which omits the catalysis? This affects every immotile kinesin, not only KIF7.

Q: Does the single adaptor activity proposed here really account for both regulatory signs, or are the negative and positive arms separable at the level of KIF7 itself? Halving Sufu rescues the Kif7-null chondrocyte phenotype, which argues that the positive arm is entirely SUFU-directed, while the negative arm persists when Sufu is absent. A KIF7 separation-of-function allele would settle whether these are one activity read in two contexts or two activities.

Q: KIF7 homodimerises and mouse Kif7 carries an experimental identical-protein-binding annotation, but human KIF7 has no such row. Is dimerisation required for plus-end tracking, as it is for other kinesin-4 proteins, and if so should it be annotated for human KIF7 despite being another uninformative child of protein binding?

Q: Zebrafish Kif7 accumulates in cytoplasmic puncta that disperse on pathway activation, and Drosophila Costal-2 can substitute for Kif7 outright. Do mammalian cells retain a functional cytoplasmic KIF7-GLI complex of the Drosophila kind alongside the ciliary one, or is the cytoplasmic pool purely a staging reservoir? The answer decides whether cytoplasm deserves more than a non-core location for vertebrate KIF7.

Q: P4HA2 reaches the ciliary tip only via KIF7 and hydroxylates SUFU there. Is P4HA2 one of a wider set of modifying enzymes that KIF7 delivers to the tip, which would make "enzyme-delivery adaptor" the general description of KIF7 function, or is it a lineage- or tissue-specific addition to the pathway?

  • Cilium-length / cilium-organization terms (GO:1902017, GO:0044782).
    He et al. 2014 shows KIF7 itself limits axonemal microtubule growth at the tip,
    so the participation test would pass. The comparator check argues against it:
    mouse Kif7 (B7ZNG0) carries no cilium-assembly or cilium-organization term
    anywhere in its GOA record, and the one annotation a curator did take from
    PMID:24952464 is GO:0042802 identical protein binding — i.e. a curator read

    structural effect is captured by the GO:0031115 replacement proposed on the
    GO:0007018 rows, which sits in the microtubule branch GOA already asserts.
  • GO:0042802 identical protein binding. KIF7 homodimerises (UniProt "Can

    than to add. Raised as a question instead.
  • GO:0008589 regulation of smoothened signaling pathway. Redundant: it is
    the parent of GO:0045879 and GO:0045880, both of which KIF7 already carries.
