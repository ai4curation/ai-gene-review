Gene Description

CTSF encodes cathepsin F, a papain-family cysteine endopeptidase that hydrolyzes proteins in acidic compartments. Its 484-residue precursor contains a signal peptide and an unusually long proregion with a cystatin-like segment; processing releases the 214-residue catalytic chain. Cathepsin F acts in lysosomes and can also be secreted by macrophages. It can cleave the MHC class II invariant chain and apolipoprotein B-containing lipoproteins in defined experimental settings. Biallelic CTSF defects cause adult-onset neuronal ceroid lipofuscinosis type B (CLN13).

Core Functions

Mature cathepsin F catalyzes cysteine-dependent internal peptide-bond hydrolysis in acidic lysosomal compartments. Activation removes its long proregion. Its catalytic role encompasses protein degradation and protein processing: measured substrates include blocked synthetic peptides, invariant chain and apoB-100. Invariant-chain cleavage and extracellular LDL degradation are context-dependent applications; the recombinant LDL assay does not establish an intact-cell turnover rate or a universal extracellular role.

Catalysis and processing

Human recombinant CTSF hydrolyzes blocked peptide substrates and protein substrates, supporting cysteine-type endopeptidase activity and proteolysis [PMID:9822672; PMID:10318784; PMID:10748235]. The long official precursor is 484 residues: predicted signal 1–19, activation propeptide 20–270, mature chain 271–484. The current record lists no alternative splice products. The original partial AF071749/AAC78839.1 is 302 residues and maps 183–484; AF071748/AAC78838.1 is 338 residues and maps 147–484. The COS-7 construct begins at the longer partial start; it is not the intact modern precursor. The yeast-processing experiments are kept separate from that imaging construct. Original AF088886/AAD26616.2 is the complete 484-residue sequence PMID:10198209.

The mature 1M6D deposition is 214 residues from human CTSF expressed in Pichia GS115, with N97Q/N108Q/N170Q in mature numbering (N367Q/N378Q/N440Q in the precursor). Its inhibitor-bound structure is not a wild-type kinetic assay or intact-precursor localization experiment PMID:12225749. The cystatin-like N-terminal fold is a structural prediction and regulatory hypothesis, not a separately demonstrated protease-inhibitor activity. No inhibitor MF is proposed for CTSF.

The catalytic/processing consultation by ROOT personally read the recovered 10318784 original PDF and indexed original 15752368 scientific text. Those reads are attributed to ROOT; the owner read the full report and read ledger, reproduced its verifier, and independently assessed the other original sources and all selected abstracts. In 15752368, human CTSF expressed in Pichia was assayed at pH 5.5 with Z-Phe-Arg-AMC. Cystatin F inhibits it with Ki 0.17 ± 0.05 nM. This supports the positive binding assertion, retained as non-core because no justified narrower binding class was established. The protease is the inhibited entity, not the inhibitor PMID:15752368.

Location and contextual activity

The original partial human protein in monkey COS-7 cells produces juxtanuclear vesicles resembling a separately imaged acidic-vesicle pattern. The original explicitly leaves lumen versus cytosolic membrane face unresolved. Its signal-independent trafficking interpretation is superseded by the later full precursor; its enzyme activity remains valid [PMID:9822672; PMID:10318784]. Lysosome is retained holistically with structural and curated mature-chain evidence. The broadly valid vacuole IBA is refined to the human lysosome. Strong LAMP2/CD68 colocalization in 15752368 is for cystatin F, not CTSF; the enzyme/protein antibody limitations are not concealed.

Recombinant human CTSF directly cleaves mouse MHC-II-associated invariant chain toward CLIP at acidic pH. Mouse cathepsin S/L-deficient macrophages and LHVS experiments support the contextual role, but LHVS is not selective CTSF genetics and the paper does not establish an exclusive human requirement [PMID:10748235, original Materials and Antibodies, in-vitro digestion Methods, Figure 6 Results and Discussion]. Reactome events 2130349 and 2130706 explicitly reach catalyst 2130638, physical-entity set 2130682, and hasMember CTSF entity 1181231/Q9UBX1 residues 271–484 in lysosomal lumen. Parent 2132295 contains those events. CTSF performs proteolysis here; it is not simply cargo. The MHC-II process remains non-core.

The complete 15184381 abstract reports secretion of active CTSF from human monocyte-derived macrophages and recombinant apoB-100/LDL cleavage. Full original Methods and Results were not recovered; preserve that access limit. This independent human evidence corroborates the extracellular IBA without treating secretion as a universal dominant location. Biallelic CTSF disease evidence identifies type B Kufs disease/CLN13 but does not alone specify a further performed biological process PMID:23297359.
