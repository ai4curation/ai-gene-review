## 2026-10-10: substantive annotation review

CEP85L links centrosomal organization to cortical neuronal migration. The five original GO assertions and all five UniProt products are preserved. The retained neuronal-migration role has mouse knockdown and partial rescue evidence; it is not described as a direct human-neuron perturbation. Tagged human protein localizes to the pericentriolar region, while independent endogenous human-cell experiments connect CEP85L to CDK5 recruitment at the proximal mother centriole.

[PMID:32097630](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7357395/) Figure 2 follows mouse cortical neurons after embryonic RNAi, including rescue and a persistent postnatal migration defect. Figure 3A–C maps human tagged CEP85L relative to centrin, SAS6 and pericentrin. Figure 3D separately uses mouse cortical expression. The construct Methods identify human NM_001042475 and mouse NM_001204983. Neither the human disease variants nor the mouse perturbations establish a uniform loss-of-function mechanism across all five current protein products. The start-codon variants affect a particular transcript, and the paper itself leaves their precise pathogenic mechanism open.

[PMID:32097629](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7255387/) Figure 4E reports CEP85L–CDK5 co-immunoprecipitation.
