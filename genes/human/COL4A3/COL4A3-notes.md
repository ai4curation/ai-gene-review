# COL4A3 review notes

## Two biological roles and their products

The alpha3 chain contributes the structure of alpha3/alpha4/alpha5 type-IV collagen protomers and terminal-domain networks. The current GO:0030020 comment explicitly permits extracellular-matrix collagens, so the structural class does not require a new isolated-chain tensile experiment. Actual assembly evidence in the complete available PMID:12193605 abstract and the official target record support the network role; the abstract does not resolve its original reagent species or full construct Methods. Broad structural and trimer terms are refined to existing specific terms. Network formation does not automatically resolve the distinct fibril-organization NAS assertion from the abstract-only PMID:29853175; that row remains UNDECIDED. The alpha1/alpha2 mapping title of PMID:3025878 is not used to remove an alpha3 assertion from incomplete evidence.

The released tumstatin fragment has a separable integrin-mediated antiangiogenic function. The official precursor is 1670 residues, mature chain 29–1670, tumstatin 1426–1670 and NC1 domain 1445–1669. These boundaries differ and are not blindly substituted for old fragment-relative coordinates. Human recombinant tumstatin rescue in mouse hosts and beta3-null endothelial/Matrigel comparisons in PMID:12842087 support performed receptor-dependent work. The study distinguishes pathological tumor angiogenesis from unaffected repair-associated skin/liver angiogenesis. Its bovine GBM/MMP9 generation assay does not demonstrate a COL4A3 protease or metalloendopeptidase inhibitor. The recovered PMID:10766752 Discussion proposes MMP inhibition as a hypothesis; the NAS inhibitor row is now marked over-annotated at that source-specific scope.

Six official FASTAs, including the canonical accession and five product accessions, reconstruct exactly. Alternative lengths are 1670, 1637, 1498, 1494 and 1424 amino acids. Splice changes modify or remove the NC1 region; the 1424-residue product lacks the annotated tumstatin segment. The original PMID:7758473 and PMID:8294492 abstracts support transcript architecture and antigen recognition, not equivalent network assembly or fragment function across all products. All original product objects remain unchanged.
