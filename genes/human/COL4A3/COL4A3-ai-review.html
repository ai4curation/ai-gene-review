Gene Description

COL4A3 encodes the alpha-3 chain of type-IV collagen, a secreted basement-membrane protein. It assembles with alpha-4 and alpha-5 chains into triple-helical protomers that associate through terminal domains to form a matrix network, particularly important in the glomerular basement membrane. Disruption of this architecture causes Alport-spectrum kidney disease and can affect hearing. Proteolytic release of its C-terminal NC1 region produces tumstatin, a fragment that binds integrins and inhibits endothelial proliferation and pathological angiogenesis in experimental systems. Alternative transcripts change or remove portions of the C-terminal region, so their assembly and fragment activities need not be equivalent.

Two biological roles and their products

The alpha3 chain contributes the structure of alpha3/alpha4/alpha5 type-IV collagen protomers and terminal-domain networks. The current GO:0030020 comment explicitly permits extracellular-matrix collagens, so the structural class does not require a new isolated-chain tensile experiment. Actual assembly evidence in the complete available PMID:12193605 abstract and the official target record support the network role; the abstract does not resolve its original reagent species or full construct Methods. The alpha1/alpha2 mapping title of PMID:3025878 is not used to remove an alpha3 assertion from incomplete evidence.

The released tumstatin fragment has a separable integrin-mediated antiangiogenic function. The official precursor is 1670 residues, mature chain 29–1670, tumstatin 1426–1670 and NC1 domain 1445–1669. These boundaries differ and are not blindly substituted for old fragment-relative coordinates.
