# DMRT1 curation notes

## 2026-10-17 — source and biological review

The review uses HGNC:2934 / UniProt Q9Y5R6 and preserves all 38 normal-fetch annotation source records, including their qualifiers and supporting entities. The historic alias DMT1 must not be confused with the unrelated metal transporter. Three official predicted products were reconstructed and independently matched to official FASTAs. No prior main review or history existed at the fixed intake boundary.

The 38 decisions comprise 20 ACCEPT, 9 MODIFY, 8 KEEP_AS_NON_CORE and 1 UNDECIDED. No NEW annotation is added. One nuclear transcription-factor core integrates activation and repression, male differentiation and the male germ-cell meiotic-entry gate. Short verbatim anchors occur once in the YAML core; repeated evidence support is reference-only. Detailed public source locators and access limits are in [the evidence guide](DMRT1-evidence-guide.md).

Human DNA recognition, full-length protein crosslinking and testis chromatin occupancy in [PMID:26005864](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4476070/) support the integrated mechanism. Full-length experiments are distinguished from the 67–136 structural fragment; mouse ChIP-exo is distinguished from human ChIP-seq. The earlier human DMRT1–VP16 reporter in [PMID:17605809](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17605809/) is not sufficient alone to establish native activation. Independent inducible human DMRT1 HEK293 DAZL cis-regulatory reporter experiments in [PMID:37709822](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37709822/) and mouse Sohlh1 regulation in [PMID:20951351](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2996490/) provide that support.

Developmental claims retain their exact contexts. Mouse fetal ovarian cytoplasmic staining in [PMID:21621532](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21621532/) is positive evidence for a transient pool, not an inference from the absence of a signal peptide. Mouse spermatogonial proliferation and induced human germ-cell mitotic arrest refer to different stages. High transgene expression and timing control may also accelerate the latter program. The DAZL experiments demonstrate activation of genomic-peak reporters, not endogenous human physiology or exclusively promoter activity. Motif-deletion constructs were tested, but no directional deletion effect is claimed without reading the figure/source data. The specific male-fate specification annotation is retained contextually; the summary does not call DMRT1 a mammalian SRY-like initiating switch. Human fetal-tissue depletion in [PMID:30272154](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30272154/) supports later testicular differentiation but does not prove first-trimester dispensability after partial depletion.

The sole unresolved annotation is the rat-derived intracellular signal-transduction claim. Exact donor Q925A6 carries IDA PMID:11181532. Available text establishes upstream regulation of Dmrt1 expression, while full-original access remains blocked; a performing signal-relay step could not be adjudicated. No experimental removal or wrong-gene allegation is made.

All five PAINT functions were traced to the actual ancestral node and target path. The negative branch in the same family does not contain human DMRT1. Target self-grounding for nucleus is legitimate. ARBA and InterPro mappings were checked against actual target features and current rule contents. The more specific replacements preserve the broad source identities and are not new annotations. The evidence-code difference between duplicate claims does not change their biological verdict.

Product uncertainty is retained explicitly: DMRT1c preserves the annotated feature through residue 118 but changes the segment containing the experimentally important R123. Neither equivalent activity nor inactivity follows from those sequence facts. DMRT1b retains the DNA-recognition region but loses a substantial C-terminal segment. The transcript paper [PMID:16617334](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16617334/) is abstract-only and is not treated as a biochemical comparison.
