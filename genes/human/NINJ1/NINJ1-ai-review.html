Gene Description

Ninjurin-1 is a small, widely expressed plasma-membrane protein of four alpha helices: two transmembrane helices (alpha3, alpha4) and two amphipathic helices (alpha1, alpha2) that are sequestered in the bilayer in the resting state. It is the executioner of plasma membrane rupture (PMR), the terminal, active step of lytic cell death. On a death signal, NINJ1 leaves its autoinhibited dimer, exposes the hydrophilic faces of alpha1 and alpha2 and polymerises laterally into branched filaments and rings in the plasma membrane; these amphipathic assemblies cap membrane edges and release membrane fragments, so the membrane is lost rather than merely perforated. PMR discharges damage-associated molecular patterns such as HMGB1 and LDH and thereby amplifies inflammation. NINJ1 acts downstream of gasdermins in pyroptosis and of MLKL in necroptosis, after apoptosis, during ferroptosis and after osmotic swelling, and it can also rupture membranes of glucose-starved macrophages independently of any known cell-death program; how its filaments finish the job - by excising membrane disks or by opening very large pores - is still argued. NINJ1 was originally characterised as a homophilic cell adhesion molecule induced by nerve injury, adhering through a short extracellular N-terminal adhesion motif, and it retains non-lytic activities: it promotes monocyte transendothelial migration, angiogenesis, and, together with ninjurin-2, haematopoietic stem and progenitor cell amplification via canonical WNT signalling, a function that persists in PMR-deficient mutants. A secreted form generated by cleavage has separate chemotactic and anti-inflammatory activity. Whether the adhesive and the membrane-destabilising activities are two uses of the same amphipathic helices, or genuinely separate functions, is unresolved.

Proposed New Ontology Terms

plasma membrane rupture

Definition: The active, protein-mediated disintegration of the plasma membrane that terminates lytic cell death, resulting in loss of membrane continuity and release of cytoplasmic contents including damage-associated molecular patterns. This is distinct from passive osmotic lysis and occurs downstream of, and separably from, pore formation by gasdermins or MLKL.

Justification: GO currently offers only GO:0019835 cytolysis, which conflates the active terminal step with lysis generally, and no term distinguishes membrane permeabilisation (gasdermin pores) from membrane rupture proper. The distinction is exactly what the NINJ1 literature established, and it is experimentally separable: Ninj1-null cells still die and still form gasdermin pores, but fail to rupture and persist as ballooned corpses.

Parent term: cytolysis

Supporting Evidence:

  • PMID:33472215 : Ninj1-/- macrophages died, but with a distinctive and persistent ballooned morphology, attributable to defective disintegration of bubble-like herniations.
  • PMID:38614101 : Therefore, NINJ1-mediated membrane disk formation is different from gasdermin-mediated pore formation, resulting in membrane loss and plasma membrane rupture.

Core Functions

NINJ1 is the executioner of plasma membrane rupture. Held as an autoinhibited dimer in which the amphipathic alpha1 and alpha2 helices are buried, it responds to a death signal by exposing their hydrophilic faces and polymerising laterally into branched filaments and rings within the plasma membrane. The resulting assemblies present a hydrophobic face to the bilayer and a hydrophilic face to the solvent, allowing them to cap membrane edges and shed membrane material, so the membrane is lost rather than simply perforated. The consequence is release of cytoplasmic DAMPs such as HMGB1 and LDH and amplification of inflammation. NINJ1 performs this same act downstream of gasdermins in pyroptosis, of MLKL in necroptosis, after apoptosis, in ferroptosis, after osmotic swelling, and in glucose-starved macrophages with no known death program engaged - it is a shared terminal effector, not a pathway-specific one. Whether the filaments finish by excising membrane disks or by opening very large pores is not settled.

Suggested Questions for Experts

Q: Do NINJ1 filaments rupture the membrane by excising and shedding membrane disks, or by enlarging into very large pores? Wu and colleagues and Dai and colleagues both resolve filaments that can cap a membrane edge, but the first describes released ring-encircled membrane disks and the second also observes less frequent pores. Are these two outcomes of one polymerisation reaction that differ by filament curvature and membrane tension, or genuinely different mechanisms?

Q: Is the homophilic adhesion activity of the N-terminal motif mechanistically the same event as the membrane-inserting polymerisation, using the same extracellular helices in trans rather than in cis - and if so, what switches between them?

Q: Which NINJ1 activity supports haematopoietic stem and progenitor cell amplification? PMR-deficient Ninj1 mutants retain the function and it requires canonical WNT signalling, but neither the adhesion motif nor a NINJ1-WNT physical link has been tested.

Q: What is the proximal trigger for NINJ1 activation? It fires downstream of gasdermins, MLKL, apoptosis, lipid peroxidation, osmotic swelling and glucose starvation alike, which suggests it reads a shared membrane property - tension, lipid packing or loss of a lipid asymmetry - rather than a pathway-specific signal.

Q: Is the secreted, cleaved form of NINJ1 a physiologically significant species in humans, which protease generates it, and does it act through the same adhesion motif?

Why this gene is in the contested-function batch

Two things are live for NINJ1 in 2025-2026:

  1. How it ruptures the plasma membrane - the "membrane-disk cutting" model versus the
    "large pore" model. This peaked in 2023-24 and 2025-26 is mostly consolidation.
  2. Whether membrane rupture is all it does. A 2026 paper shows PMR-deficient Ninj1
    mutants retain a haematopoietic function, i.e. a separable second role. That bears
    directly on the curation question of whether the 1990s cell-adhesion characterisation is
    core biology or history.

The core function: plasma membrane rupture

Founding forward-genetic identification, and the key phenotype - cells still die, but they
do not disintegrate:
PMID:33472215
and PMID:33472215.

Mechanism from the same paper: PMID:33472215.

Degen et al. resolved the filament and the amphipathic-helix insertion mechanism:

PMID:37198476.

PMID:38614101.

The Dai lab agrees on solubilisation but keeps pores on the table, and explains why the
paralog NINJ2 cannot do this at all:
PMID:39667936.

whether it exists. Every model is an instance of GO:0140912 membrane destabilizing activity
("Binding to a membrane and increasing its permeability"), so the dispute does not change the
action on any annotation. It is recorded in the reason and raised in suggested_questions.

PMID:9261151 and
PMID:9261151.

PMID:33028854.

core_functions:
- description: NINJ1 is the executioner of plasma membrane rupture. Held as an autoinhibited dimer
    in which the amphipathic alpha1 and alpha2 helices are buried, it responds to a death signal
    by exposing their hydrophilic faces and polymerising laterally into branched filaments and rings
    within the plasma membrane. The resulting assemblies present a hydrophobic face to the bilayer
    and a hydrophilic face to the solvent, allowing them to cap membrane edges and shed membrane
    material, so the membrane is lost rather than simply perforated. The consequence is release of
    cytoplasmic DAMPs such as HMGB1 and LDH and amplification of inflammation. NINJ1 performs this
    same act downstream of gasdermins in pyroptosis, of MLKL in necroptosis, after apoptosis, in
    ferroptosis, after osmotic swelling, and in glucose-starved macrophages with no known death program
    engaged - it is a shared terminal effector, not a pathway-specific one. Whether the filaments
    finish by excising membrane disks or by opening very large pores is not settled.

proposed_new_terms:
- proposed_name: plasma membrane rupture
  proposed_definition: The active, protein-mediated disintegration of the plasma membrane that terminates
    lytic cell death, resulting in loss of membrane continuity and release of cytoplasmic contents
    including damage-associated molecular patterns. This is distinct from passive osmotic lysis and
    occurs downstream of, and separably from, pore formation by gasdermins or MLKL.
  justification: 'GO currently offers only GO:0019835 cytolysis, which conflates the active terminal
    step with lysis generally, and no term distinguishes membrane permeabilisation (gasdermin pores)
    from membrane rupture proper. The distinction is exactly what the NINJ1 literature established,
    and it is experimentally separable: Ninj1-null cells still die and still form gasdermin pores,
    but fail to rupture and persist as ballooned corpses.'

suggested_questions:
- question: Do NINJ1 filaments rupture the membrane by excising and shedding membrane disks, or by
    enlarging into very large pores? Wu and colleagues and Dai and colleagues both resolve filaments
    that can cap a membrane edge, but the first describes released ring-encircled membrane disks
    and the second also observes less frequent pores. Are these two outcomes of one polymerisation
    reaction that differ by filament curvature and membrane tension, or genuinely different mechanisms?
- question: Is the homophilic adhesion activity of the N-terminal motif mechanistically the same
    event as the membrane-inserting polymerisation, using the same extracellular helices in trans
    rather than in cis - and if so, what switches between them?
- question: Which NINJ1 activity supports haematopoietic stem and progenitor cell amplification?
    PMR-deficient Ninj1 mutants retain the function and it requires canonical WNT signalling, but
    neither the adhesion motif nor a NINJ1-WNT physical link has been tested.
- question: What is the proximal trigger for NINJ1 activation? It fires downstream of gasdermins,
    MLKL, apoptosis, lipid peroxidation, osmotic swelling and glucose starvation alike, which suggests
    it reads a shared membrane property - tension, lipid packing or loss of a lipid asymmetry - rather
    than a pathway-specific signal.
- question: Is the secreted, cleaved form of NINJ1 a physiologically significant species in humans,
    which protease generates it, and does it act through the same adhesion motif?

- hypothesis: The membrane-disk and large-pore outcomes are the same polymerisation reaction resolved
    under different membrane tension.
  description: Reconstitute purified human NINJ1 into giant unilamellar vesicles held at defined,

    membrane fate by high-speed AFM and cryo-ET in parallel. Score the frequency of closed rings
