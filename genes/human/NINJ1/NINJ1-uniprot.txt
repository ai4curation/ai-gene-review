CC   -!- FUNCTION: [Ninjurin-1]: Effector of various programmed cell death, such
CC       as pyroptosis and necroptosis, which mediates plasma membrane rupture
CC       (cytolysis) (PubMed:33472215, PubMed:36468682, PubMed:37196676,
CC       PubMed:37198476, PubMed:38614101, PubMed:39667936). Oligomerizes in
CC       response to death stimuli and forms ring-like structures on the plasma
CC       membrane: acts by cutting and shedding membrane disks, like a cookie
CC       cutter, leading to membrane damage and loss that cannot be repaired by
CC       the cell (PubMed:38614101). Plasma membrane rupture leads to release
CC       intracellular molecules named damage-associated molecular patterns
CC       (DAMPs) that propagate the inflammatory response (PubMed:33472215,
CC       PubMed:36468682, PubMed:37196676, PubMed:37198476). Mechanistically,
CC       mediates plasma membrane rupture by introducing hydrophilic faces of 2
CC       alpha helices into the hydrophobic membrane (PubMed:37198476,
CC       PubMed:38614101). Induces plasma membrane rupture downstream of
CC       Gasdermin (GSDMA, GSDMB, GSDMC, GSDMD, or GSDME) or MLKL during
CC       pyroptosis or necroptosis, respectively (PubMed:33472215,
CC       PubMed:36468682, PubMed:37196676, PubMed:37198476). Acts as an effector
CC       of PANoptosis downstream of CASP1, CASP4, CASP8 and RIPK3 (By
CC       similarity). Also induces plasma membrane rupture in response to cell
CC       swelling caused by osmotic stress and ferroptosis downstream of lipid
CC       peroxidation (By similarity). Acts as a regulator of Toll-like receptor
CC       4 (TLR4) signaling triggered by lipopolysaccharide (LPS) during
CC       systemic inflammation; directly binds LPS (PubMed:26677008). Involved
CC       in leukocyte migration during inflammation by promoting
CC       transendothelial migration of macrophages via homotypic binding (By
CC       similarity). Promotes the migration of monocytes across the brain
CC       endothelium to central nervous system inflammatory lesions
CC       (PubMed:22162058). Also acts as a homophilic transmembrane adhesion
CC       molecule involved in various processes such as axonal growth, cell
CC       chemotaxis and angiogenesis (PubMed:33028854, PubMed:8780658,
CC       PubMed:9261151). Promotes cell adhesion by mediating homophilic
CC       interactions via its extracellular N-terminal adhesion motif (N-NAM)
CC       (PubMed:33028854, PubMed:8780658, PubMed:9261151). Involved in the
CC       progression of the inflammatory stress by promoting cell-to-cell
CC       interactions between immune cells and endothelial cells
CC       (PubMed:22162058, PubMed:26677008, PubMed:32147432). Plays a role in
CC       nerve regeneration by promoting maturation of Schwann cells
CC       (PubMed:8780658, PubMed:9261151). Acts as a regulator of angiogenesis
CC       (PubMed:33028854). Promotes the formation of new vessels by mediating
CC       the interaction between capillary pericyte cells and endothelial cells
CC       (By similarity). Promotes osteoclasts development by enhancing the
CC       survival of prefusion osteoclasts (By similarity). Also involved in
CC       striated muscle growth and differentiation (By similarity).
CC       {ECO:0000250|UniProtKB:O70131, ECO:0000269|PubMed:22162058,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:26677008, ECO:0000269|PubMed:32147432,

CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:36468682, ECO:0000269|PubMed:37196676,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:37198476, ECO:0000269|PubMed:38614101,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:39667936, ECO:0000269|PubMed:8780658,

CC   -!- ACTIVITY REGULATION: [Ninjurin-1]: In response to death stimuli,
CC       homooligomerizes and disrupts membrane integrity by introducing the
CC       hydrophilic faces of alpha1 and alpha2 helices into the hydrophobic
CC       membrane (PubMed:37198476). Homooligomerization and ability to mediate
CC       plasma membrane rupture is inhibited by glycine; it is unclear whether
CC       glycine directly or indirectly inhibits homooligomerization
CC       (PubMed:36468682). In normal conditions, NINJ1 is autoinhibited via
CC       formation of a homodimer: in the inactive homodimer, the alpha1 and
CC       alpha2 helices (residues 44-74) form a single transmembrane region
CC       without a kink, in which hydrophilic faces of alpha1 and alpha2 helices

CC   -!- DOMAIN: [Ninjurin-1]: Composed of 4 alpha helices: 2 hydrophobic
CC       transmembrane regions (alpha3 and alpha4) and 2 alpha helices (alpha1
CC       and alpha2) (PubMed:37198476). Alpha1 and alpha2 feature one
CC       hydrophobic side and a hydrophilic side (PubMed:37198476). Following
CC       NINJ1 activation, alpha1 and alpha2 helices insert into the membrane
CC       and drive NINJ1 oligomerization via interactions between alpha3 and
CC       alpha4 and the hydrophobic face of alpha1 from an adjacent subunit
CC       (PubMed:37198476). Such structures disrupt membrane integrity and form
CC       a lesion through the introduction of the hydrophilic faces of alpha1
CC       and alpha2 into the hydrophobic membrane (PubMed:37198476). In absence
CC       of death stimuli, NINJ1 is an inactive homodimer, where the alpha1 and
CC       alpha2 helices form a single transmembrane region without a kink: in
CC       the homodimer, hydrophilic faces of alpha1 and alpha2 helices are
CC       sequestered and the binding site for kinked alpha1 and alpha2 helices
CC       from neighboring activated NINJ1 molecules are occluded, thereby
CC       preventing membrane rupture (By similarity). The topology shown in the
CC       entry corresponds to the activated form (PubMed:37198476).
CC       {ECO:0000250|UniProtKB:O70131, ECO:0000269|PubMed:37198476}.
