Gene Description

Cas13a (originally C2c2) is the single-protein effector of the class 2, type VI-A CRISPR-Cas adaptive immune system of Leptotrichia shahii, and the only class of single-component CRISPR effector whose target is RNA rather than DNA. The 1389-residue protein is bilobed: a crRNA-recognition lobe makes most of the contacts with the guide, and a nuclease lobe carries two HEPN folds that come together to form a single composite ribonuclease active site. The protein matures its own guide, cleaving precursor CRISPR RNA one nucleotide upstream of the repeat stem-loop to yield a mature crRNA with an optimal 28-nucleotide spacer, using a catalytic pocket that is separate from the HEPN site and lies in the recognition lobe. Loaded with that crRNA it recognises a complementary single-stranded transcript, with efficacy set by a 3' protospacer flanking site rather than by a PAM, and cleaves it preferentially before uridines. Two features make it unlike DNA-targeting effectors. Cleavage falls outside the crRNA-pairing region, in exposed single-stranded loops, so the cut position depends on the secondary structure around the target. And engagement of a viable target converts the HEPN site into a substrate-non-specific ribonuclease that degrades unrelated bystander RNA; the same point mutations in either HEPN dyad abolish both specific and collateral cleavage, and either mutation leaves a catalytically inactive, RNA-programmable RNA-binding protein. The enzyme has no activity on single-stranded DNA, double-stranded DNA or partially double-stranded RNA. The biological output is spacer-programmed resistance to RNA invaders: a cell carrying the locus with a matching spacer shows a three- to four-log reduction in plaque formation by the single-stranded RNA phage MS2, and that protection is lost in each of four single catalytic-residue substitutions. Because indiscriminate degradation of cellular transcripts accompanies target destruction, type VI-A immunity is thought to act by arresting the infected cell rather than by cleanly clearing the invader from it.

Proposed New Ontology Terms

guide RNA-dependent nucleic acid endonuclease activity

Definition: Catalysis of the cleavage of ester linkages within a nucleic acid molecule by creating internal breaks, where the position of cleavage is determined by base pairing between the substrate and a separate guide RNA bound non-covalently by the enzyme. The guide RNA is neither substrate nor product; it programs the sequence specificity of an enzyme whose active site is otherwise sequence-non-specific, so that the specificity of the catalysis is a property of the bound guide rather than of the protein.

Justification: GO has no molecular-function term for guide-RNA-programmed target recognition or cleavage. Checked against the current GO release: no molecular-function label contains "RNA-guided", "guide RNA" or "CRISPR". The consequence is that every CRISPR effector, and every other guide-dependent nuclease, can only be annotated with a term that describes its chemistry (GO:0004520, GO:0004521, GO:0004530, GO:1990238) and is therefore indistinguishable in GO from an unguided nuclease of the same chemistry. Cas13a shows the cost acutely: the one GO term available, GO:0004521, has to carry both the guide-programmed target cleavage and the structure-directed processing of the guide itself, two activities at two distant sites in the same protein, and GO can say nothing about the difference. Two children are needed, guide RNA-dependent DNA endonuclease activity (Cas9 and Cas12 families) and guide RNA-dependent RNA endonuclease activity (Cas13 and type III effectors), and the parent would also accommodate class 1 multi-subunit effectors through contributes_to. This request is deliberately scoped to the nuclease function rather than to recognition in general, so that catalytically dead guide-loaded effectors - which Cas13a HEPN mutants are, and which are themselves used as programmable RNA-binding proteins - are handled by a binding term instead. This is the same request as in the reviews of U2UMQ6, A0Q7Q2 and C7NBY4 and should be merged with them.

defense response to virus by induction of host dormancy

Definition: A defense response to virus in which the infected cell halts its own growth and enters a reversible non-growing state, so that the viral replication cycle cannot be completed. The invader is not cleared from the infected cell and the cell does not necessarily lyse; the protective output is realised at the level of the population, because an arrested cell continues to adsorb virions while producing no progeny.

Justification: Type VI-A immunity does not work by destroying the invader. Target recognition licenses indiscriminate cleavage of cellular transcripts, and it is that degradation, not clearance of the phage, that halts the infection; trans-cleavage alone is sufficient to abort the infectious cycle. GO cannot express this. The nearest existing terms fail in different ways: GO:0051607 defense response to virus is correct but silent about the mechanism and about the fate of the infected cell; GO:0022611 dormancy process and its children GO:0097436 and GO:0097437 descend from GO:0032502 developmental process and describe a developmental programme, so applying them to phage-induced bacterial growth arrest would be a branch error rather than a stretch; and GO:0012501 programmed cell death is wrong because the arrest is reversible and the cell is not killed. On the question of which entity performs the step, the answer here favours the term: Cas13a itself degrades the host transcripts, and the arrest follows directly from that degradation rather than being executed by something else - which is exactly why the analogous request is not made for Cas12a trans-cleavage, where no cellular consequence has been demonstrated. The gap is not specific to CRISPR: abortive-infection defence by growth arrest is shared with toxin-antitoxin systems, retrons and Thoeris, none of which can be annotated for it either, so a term under GO:0051607 would serve a whole class of prokaryotic defence systems. No annotation to this term is asserted on P0DOC6, because the dormancy outcome was demonstrated on the Listeria seeligeri ortholog rather than on this protein.

Core Functions

Loaded with a mature crRNA, recognises a complementary single-stranded RNA transcript whose 3' protospacer flanking site is not G, and cleaves it preferentially before uridines at positions outside the crRNA-pairing region, in exposed single-stranded loops, so that the cut site depends on the secondary structure around the target. Engagement of a viable target then converts the same active site into a substrate-non-specific ribonuclease that degrades unrelated bystander RNA. Catalysis is by a single composite site formed where the two HEPN folds meet: substitution of either arginine or either histidine of the two R-H dyads abolishes both specific and collateral cleavage, and neither HEPN fold compensates for loss of the other, leaving a catalytically dead but RNA-programmable RNA-binding protein. Requires Mg2+. There is no activity on single-stranded DNA, double-stranded DNA, or partially double-stranded RNA.

Suggested Questions for Experts

Q: What do the native Leptotrichia shahii CRISPR spacers match? Type VI-A spacers surveyed elsewhere matched double-stranded DNA phages rather than RNA phages, which would make the MS2 experiment a proof of mechanism rather than a model of the natural threat, and would mean the physiological target is a phage transcript rather than a phage genome.

Q: Does LshCas13a induce reversible dormancy in a host, as the Listeria seeligeri ortholog does, and if so what fraction of arrested cells resumes growth once the infection has been aborted?

Q: What are the end-group products of LshCas13a target cleavage? Until these are known, no term below GO:0004521 can be justified for this activity, since both available children specify a hydrolytic mechanism and a particular phosphomonoester.

Q: What determines the functional split in the Cas13a family, which could not be predicted bioinformatically? Orthologs recognise orthogonal crRNA sets and differ in ssRNA cleavage specificity, so guide and substrate specificity should not be propagated across the family by sequence similarity.

Q: Should GO acquire a molecular-function branch for guide-RNA dependence, and a process term for defence by induced host dormancy? Both gaps are recorded as term requests here.

Suggested Experiments

Experiment: Sequence the native Leptotrichia shahii CRISPR array and search its spacers against phage and plasmid databases, to establish whether the system is directed at RNA phages, at transcripts of DNA phages, or at mobile elements, which the heterologous MS2 experiment cannot answer.

Experiment: Measure growth, viability and colony-forming ability of cells expressing the L. shahii locus before, during and after targeted phage infection, with and without the HEPN point mutants, to test directly whether this ortholog arrests the host as the L. seeligeri ortholog does and whether the arrest is reversible.

Experiment: Determine the chemical end groups of LshCas13a target-RNA cleavage products by treatment with alkaline phosphatase and T4 polynucleotide kinase, as was done for the L. buccalis pre-crRNA products, to establish whether the HEPN reaction is hydrolytic and which GO child term would apply.

Experiment: Quantify collateral RNA degradation in vivo by RNA sequencing of infected cells at intervals after targeting, to measure how much of the transcriptome is destroyed, whether phage and host transcripts are degraded with equal indiscrimination, and whether degradation is self-limiting.

1. Why this protein is a special case among Cas effectors

Cas13a is the only single-protein CRISPR effector class whose target is RNA. That is not a
side-activity; it is the defining property, and it was established by exclusion as well as by
demonstration. Abudayyeh et al. tested DNA explicitly and found nothing:
PMID:27256883. UniProt records the same negative result ("has no
activity on partially dsRNA, ssDNA or dsDNA").

Catalysis is by two HEPN domains that come together to form one composite active site, not by a
RuvC- or HNH-type nuclease centre:
PMID:27256883 All four single alanine substitutions of the two R-H dyads kill the activity,
and neither HEPN domain can compensate for loss of the other, which is why this counts as one site
rather than two.

Target recognition uses a 3' protospacer flanking site rather than a PAM, and the distinction is
substantive rather than terminological — in an RNA-targeting system there is no self/non-self
discrimination problem for a PAM to solve:
PMID:27256883.

2. The two RNase activities, at two separate sites

This is a real two-site enzyme, unlike Cas12a where cis and trans cleavage share the RuvC site.

2a. Target ssRNA cleavage + collateral cleavage — HEPN composite site

Specific, crRNA-directed cleavage of a complementary single-stranded transcript, preferentially
before U residues (hence "uridine-preferring"). Unusually, the cut falls outside the crRNA-pairing
region, in exposed single-stranded loops, so the cleavage position depends on target secondary
structure (UniProt FUNCTION, from PMID:27256883).

Binding a viable target then switches the same HEPN site into a substrate-non-specific RNase:
PMID:27256883. The HEPN mutants R597A and R1278A also lose collateral cleavage, which is the
evidence that it is the same catalytic centre. UniProt's ACTIVITY REGULATION block states it plainly:
"Target RNA acts as an activator for non-specific ssRNA degradation".

2b. pre-crRNA processing — a distinct, non-HEPN site

Cas13a matures its own guide (optimally a 28-nt spacer here), cleaving one nucleotide upstream of the
pre-crRNA stem-loop. The structure of this protein localises the two catalytic pockets to different
domains:
PMID:28086085 and
PMID:28086085

The mechanistic separation was shown on the L. buccalis ortholog, where HEPN mutants keep
processing and an R1079A mutant loses processing while keeping target cleavage
PMID:27669025.

Cross-ortholog note worth recording: this enzyme cleaves L. buccalis and L. wadei pre-crRNA at a
site 3 nt different from where those enzymes cut their own (UniProt FUNCTION, from PMID:28475872),
which is why the family splits into two functional groups
PMID:28475872. Those
groups "could not be bioinformatically predicted", which is a direct warning against propagating
substrate specificity across the family by similarity.

3. Native biological role: RNA phage immunity, and what follows the cleavage

The in vivo demonstration is for this ortholog and it is strong. A tiled spacer library against
the ssRNA phage MS2 was screened in E. coli carrying the reconstituted L. shahii locus
PMID:27256883, enriched
spacers were individually validated
PMID:27256883, and the requirement for catalysis was established by mutagenesis
PMID:27256883.

That is loss-of-function plus rescue-by-wild-type for a 3-4 log phenotype, with the catalytic residues
identified — about as good as IMP evidence gets for a heterologously reconstituted defence system.

The downstream outcome. Abudayyeh et al. already proposed that the collateral cleavage, rather
than clean clearance of the invader, is the protective mechanism:
PMID:27256883. UniProt carries this as a suggestion, correctly hedged.

It was later demonstrated, though on the Listeria seeligeri ortholog rather than this one:
PMID:31142834 and
PMID:31142834. That paper also showed type VI-A spacers in nature match
dsDNA phages, not RNA phages, which reframes the MS2 result as a proof of mechanism rather than a
picture of the natural invader.

Because the dormancy demonstration is on a different ortholog, it is cited here as MEDIUM-relevance
context and is not turned into an annotation on P0DOC6.

On the choice of GO:0004521 RNA endonuclease activity rather than a child: the children
GO:0016891 and GO:0016892 each stipulate a hydrolytic mechanism and specify the product
phosphomonoester. For LshCas13a the target-cleavage reaction is Mg2+-dependent (UniProt COFACTOR) but
the end-group chemistry is not established in the cached literature, and the pre-crRNA reaction is
metal-independent with a 2',3'-cyclic phosphate product in the L. buccalis ortholog. Asserting
either child would be asserting mechanism the data do not establish for this protein.
GO:0004521 is defined mechanism-agnostically and sits under GO:0004519/GO:0004540 rather than
under a hydrolase parent, so it is the correct stopping point.

One GO:0004521 row covers both activities, because GO cannot presently distinguish them: both are
RNA endonuclease activity, and the thing that separates them — guide dependence for one, structural
recognition of the repeat for the other — is exactly what the ontology lacks terms for. The
distinction is carried in two separate core_functions entries instead, which is where it belongs.

5. What is deliberately NOT asserted

  • Nucleic-acid diagnostics. Cas13a's collateral cleavage is the basis of a large family of
    detection methods. That is a reagent application, not a function of the gene in L. shahii, and it
    is excluded from description and core_functions entirely. UniProt's own BIOTECHNOLOGY block
    ("Can be reprogrammed to target specific mRNAs in an E.coli system") is likewise excluded.
  • Any DNA-directed activity. Explicitly tested and negative for this protein (§1). Note that a
    2025 paper reports DNA targeting by the L. buccalis ortholog (PMID:40542106, cited on that
    entry); nothing comparable is reported for LshCas13a, and the published negative result stands.
  • A dormancy / abortive-infection process annotation. The outcome is real but was demonstrated on
    the L. seeligeri ortholog, and GO has no term for it. Raised as a proposed_new_terms entry and a
    suggested_question, not asserted. See §6 for the reasoning.
  • GO:0003723 RNA binding. True — UniProt keywords it, and the HEPN mutants are specifically
    described as "catalytically inactive RNA-binding proteins" — but uninformative, since it does not
    distinguish a programmable guide-loaded surveillance complex from any RNA-binding protein. The
    informative version is the missing guide-dependence term.
  • GO:0022611 / GO:0097436 dormancy terms. These sit under GO:0032502 developmental process
    and describe a developmental programme; using them for phage-induced bacterial growth arrest would
    be a branch error, not merely a stretch.

6. The dormancy term request, and the NEW-bar reasoning behind it

Asking the right question — which entity performs the step? — gives a favourable answer here, unlike
the Cas12a trans-cleavage case. Cas13a itself degrades the host transcripts; the arrest is the direct
consequence of that degradation, not a separate process carried out by something else, and
PMID:31142834 shows trans-cleavage alone is sufficient to abort the infectious cycle.

Reading candidate parents: GO:0051607 defense response to virus is the right parent. It is already
asserted for this gene, so a child is additional specificity rather than redundancy. The existing
dormancy terms are in the wrong branch (§5).

Comparator check: GO annotates no gene product to any abortive-infection or
defence-by-growth-arrest term, because no such term exists — this is a genuine ontology gap rather
than a convention I have failed to identify, and the gap affects a whole class of bacterial defence
systems (toxin-antitoxin-based abortive infection, retrons, Thoeris) and not just Cas13a. That is an
argument for requesting the term, and simultaneously an argument for not asserting an annotation to
a nonexistent id on this entry, which is why only the term request appears.

7. Open questions recorded in the review

  • What do the native L. shahii spacers match? PMID:31142834 found type VI-A spacers matching dsDNA
    phages rather than RNA phages, which would make the MS2 experiment a proof of principle rather than
    a model of the natural threat.
  • Does LshCas13a induce dormancy in a host, as the L. seeligeri ortholog does, and is the arrest
    reversible?
  • What are the end-group products of LshCas13a target cleavage? Needed before any GO:0016891/
    GO:0016892-level term could be justified.
  • The functional split in the Cas13a family (PMID:28475872) could not be predicted
    bioinformatically. What determines which crRNAs and which ssRNA cleavage specificities an ortholog
    has?

  Cas13a (originally C2c2) is the single-protein effector of the class 2, type VI-A
  CRISPR-Cas adaptive immune system of Leptotrichia shahii, and the only class of
  single-component CRISPR effector whose target is RNA rather than DNA. The
  1389-residue protein is bilobed: a crRNA-recognition lobe makes most of the contacts
  with the guide, and a nuclease lobe carries two HEPN folds that come together to form
  a single composite ribonuclease active site. The protein matures its own guide,
  cleaving precursor CRISPR RNA one nucleotide upstream of the repeat stem-loop to
  yield a mature crRNA with an optimal 28-nucleotide spacer, using a catalytic pocket
  that is separate from the HEPN site and lies in the recognition lobe. Loaded with that
  crRNA it recognises a complementary single-stranded transcript, with efficacy set by a
  3' protospacer flanking site rather than by a PAM, and cleaves it preferentially before
  uridines. Two features make it unlike DNA-targeting effectors. Cleavage falls outside
  the crRNA-pairing region, in exposed single-stranded loops, so the cut position depends
  on the secondary structure around the target. And engagement of a viable target
  converts the HEPN site into a substrate-non-specific ribonuclease that degrades
  unrelated bystander RNA; the same point mutations in either HEPN dyad abolish both
  specific and collateral cleavage, and either mutation leaves a catalytically inactive,
  RNA-programmable RNA-binding protein. The enzyme has no activity on single-stranded
  DNA, double-stranded DNA or partially double-stranded RNA. The biological output is
  spacer-programmed resistance to RNA invaders: a cell carrying the locus with a matching
