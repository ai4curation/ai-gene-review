Gene Description

MnxG (locus PputGB1_2447) is a large (1,944 aa) multicopper oxidase of the manganese-oxidizing bacterium Pseudomonas putida GB-1, distantly related to the MnxG catalytic subunit of the Bacillus sp. PL-12 Mnx complex. It carries an N-terminal Sec signal peptide and multiple cupredoxin domains with conserved multicopper oxidase copper-binding motifs. Genetic analysis shows that MnxG is one of two multicopper oxidases (with McoA, PputGB1_2665) that are each sufficient for oxidation of soluble Mn(II), and of free or ligand-complexed Mn(III), to insoluble Mn(III/IV) oxides; only deletion of both genes abolishes oxidation under standard conditions, while a third, unrelated enzyme (the secreted animal heme peroxidase MopA) contributes only in particular genetic backgrounds. mnxG expression is positively controlled by the MnxS1/MnxS2-MnxR two-component system and is activated at low Mn(II) concentrations in a subset of cells; MnxG appears to be responsible for the initial precipitation of Mn oxides, which accumulate on the cell surface. The purified P. putida enzyme has not been characterized biochemically, and its cellular location has not been determined directly.

Proposed New Ontology Terms

manganese oxidase activity

Definition: Catalysis of the reaction: 2 Mn(2+) + O2 + 2 H2O = 2 MnO2 + 4 H(+), proceeding through Mn(III) intermediates, in which soluble Mn(II) is oxidized to insoluble manganese(III/IV) oxide with molecular oxygen as electron acceptor.

Justification: EC 1.16.3.3 (manganese oxidase), a multicopper-oxidase activity found in many Mn(II)-oxidizing bacteria, has no GO term; ec2go maps only EC 1.16.3.1 (ferroxidase) and the generic EC 1.16.3.- parent GO:0016724. Bacterial Mn(II)-oxidizing MCOs (Bacillus MnxG, P. putida GB-1 MnxG and McoA, Leptothrix MofA, Pedomicrobium MoxA) can currently only be annotated to the generic parent. Distinct from GO:0016689 manganese peroxidase activity, which uses H2O2.

Parent term: oxidoreductase activity, acting on metal ions, oxygen as acceptor

Core Functions

Copper-dependent multicopper oxidase that oxidizes Mn(II), and free or ligand-complexed Mn(III), to insoluble Mn(III/IV) oxide, presumably coupling this to O2 reduction; one of two MCOs that are each sufficient for Mn oxidation in P. putida GB-1, and the one responsible for initial oxide precipitation at low Mn(II). Activity is inferred from deletion genetics and family membership; the enzyme has not been purified.

(mnxG-notes.md)

mnxG (B0KQT1, PputGB1_2447) — Pseudomonas putida GB-1

Identity

  • TrEMBL B0KQT1, 1,944 aa, locus PputGB1_2447. UniProt has no gene name; the RecName
    "Manganese-oxidizing multicopper oxidase MnxG" is a ProtNLM machine name (ECO:0008006),
    which happens to agree with the literature name. Folder mnxG follows the literature
    symbol (fetched by accession with --alias mnxG).
  • Domains: SignalP signal peptide 1-26; PROSITE MULTICOPPER_OXIDASE2 (PS00080),
    IPR002355 Cu_oxidase_Cu_BS, NCBIfam NF041487 copper_ox_MnxG, IPR048215 MnxG-like.
    No PANTHER family assigned.

Function (genetic, not biochemical)

  • Identified by genome comparison and MnxR regulation; deletion of each MCO alone leaves
    oxidation, the double deletion abolishes it [PMID:23124227 "loss of oxidation was attained only upon deletion of
    both genes"; "PputGB1_2447 and PputGB1_2665 encode two
    MCOs that are independently capable of oxidizing both Mn(II) and Mn(III)"].
  • Strains retaining only MnxG oxidize Mn(III)-citrate and Mn(III)-DFOB
    PMID:29706936.
  • MnxG drives initial oxide precipitation, mnxG is induced at low Mn(II)
    PMID:40122939;
    PMID:40470839.
  • No biochemistry on the GB-1 enzyme PMID:42757987.
  • Localization not determined PMID:27084014;
    review infers Sec export from the signal peptide PMID:42757987.
    No CC term added (single line of evidence, no assay).

Relationship to McoA and MopA (paralogy)

  • MnxG and McoA are both MCOs but not recent paralogs: different copper-domain
    arrangement PMID:42757987.
  • MopA (PputGB1_3353) is an animal heme peroxidase with RTX Ca-binding domains,
    unrelated in fold to the MCOs: functional redundancy, not paralogy
    PMID:27084014.

GO

  • Only GOA row: copper ion binding (IEA InterPro) -> ACCEPT.
  • NEW: GO:0016724 (IMP, PMID:23124227). No GO term exists for EC 1.16.3.3 manganese
    oxidase (checked ec2go and QuickGO search, 2026-10-28); proposed as new term.
  • No BP added: there is no GO process for Mn(II) oxidation/biomineralization.

CMMMech record notes (data/records/manganese.yaml; not edited)

  • Protein example evidence says "genetic deletion and complementation in Wright et al.
    (2018)". Wright 2018 used existing mutants (the deletions were made in Geszvain et al.
    2013, PMID:23124227, and Geszvain et al. 2016, PMID:27084014, plus two strains built in
    Wright 2018); I found no complementation of mnxG in Wright 2018. The edge
    "Deletion and complementation of mnxG ... confirm its dominant role" is stronger than
    the cited source: no complementation is reported there, and "dominant" is supported
    by Gehin et al. 2025 (initial precipitation) rather than by Wright 2018.
  • functional_role says MnxG catalyzes "two-electron oxidation"; MCOs transfer electrons
    one at a time, Mn(II)->Mn(III)->Mn(IV) PMID:42757987.
    "Catalyzing" should be qualified as genetically inferred: the enzyme has not been
    purified (quote above).
  • "Primary multicopper oxidase" is reasonable only at low Mn(II); McoA dominates at higher
    Mn(II) (PMID:40470839).
