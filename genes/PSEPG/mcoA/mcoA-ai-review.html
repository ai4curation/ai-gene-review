Gene Description

McoA (locus PputGB1_2665) is a 1,131-aa multicopper oxidase of the manganese-oxidizing bacterium Pseudomonas putida GB-1. It has a hydrophobic N-terminal segment, described as a Sec signal peptide, a bacterial Ig-like (Big_9) region, and C-terminal cupredoxin domains with multicopper oxidase copper-binding motifs whose arrangement differs from that of the second GB-1 Mn-oxidizing multicopper oxidase, MnxG. Genetic analysis shows that McoA and MnxG are each sufficient for oxidation of soluble Mn(II), and of free or ligand-complexed Mn(III), to insoluble Mn(IV) oxides, and only deletion of both abolishes oxidation under standard conditions; the unrelated secreted heme peroxidase MopA is a third, conditional Mn oxidase. mcoA is positively regulated by MnxR and is activated at higher Mn(II) concentrations than mnxG, in a subset of cells, so that McoA contributes to Mn oxide biomineralization where MnxG activity is saturated or inhibited. The purified enzyme has not been characterized and its cellular location has not been determined directly.

Proposed New Ontology Terms

manganese oxidase activity

Definition: Catalysis of the reaction: 4 Mn(2+) + 2 O2 + 4 H2O = 4 Mn(IV)O2 + 8 H(+), in which soluble Mn(II) is oxidized to insoluble manganese(IV) oxide with molecular oxygen as electron acceptor.

Justification: EC 1.16.3.3 (manganese oxidase; systematic name manganese(II):oxygen oxidoreductase), a multicopper-oxidase activity found in many Mn(II)-oxidizing bacteria, has no GO term; ec2go maps only EC 1.16.3.1 (ferroxidase) and the generic EC 1.16.3.- parent GO:0016724. Bacterial Mn(II)-oxidizing MCOs (Bacillus MnxG, P. putida GB-1 MnxG and McoA, Leptothrix MofA, Pedomicrobium MoxA) can currently only be annotated to the generic parent; the EC entry lists mnxG, mofA, moxA and cotA among its gene names. The definition above states the reaction only, in the canonical overall form used by the ExplorEnz entry. Mechanism is stated separately - multicopper oxidases transfer electrons one at a time, so Mn(III) is an intermediate and not the product. ExplorEnz accordingly gives that overall reaction as the sum of a Mn(II) -> Mn(III) step (1a) and a Mn(III) -> Mn(IV) step (1b), and the Bacillus Mnx structure shows sequential one-electron oxidations ending in MnO2. Distinct from GO:0016689 manganese peroxidase activity, which uses H2O2.

Parent term: oxidoreductase activity, acting on metal ions, oxygen as acceptor

Core Functions

Copper-dependent multicopper oxidase that oxidizes Mn(II), and free or ligand-complexed Mn(III), to insoluble Mn(IV) oxide, presumably coupling this to O2 reduction; one of two MCOs each sufficient for Mn oxidation in P. putida GB-1, contributing mainly at higher Mn(II) concentrations or where MnxG is limited. Activity is inferred from deletion genetics and family membership; the enzyme has not been purified.

Suggested Questions for Experts

Q: Is the N-terminal hydrophobic segment of McoA (Phobius transmembrane helix 12-33) a cleaved Sec signal peptide or an uncleaved membrane anchor, and where does McoA act relative to the cell surface oxides?

Q: Do McoA and MnxG act cooperatively (as suggested by the higher Mn oxidation rate of the wild type than of either single mutant), or in different cells?

Suggested Experiments

Experiment: Heterologous expression and purification of McoA to determine copper content and Mn(II)/Mn(III) oxidation kinetics, and comparison of its Mn(II) affinity and substrate inhibition with MnxG.

Experiment: N-terminal sequencing or fractionation of epitope-tagged McoA to test signal-peptide cleavage and localization (periplasm, outer membrane, supernatant).

Knowledge Gaps

What is not known — curated, literature-grounded statements of the open unknowns (the inverse of core functions).

Gap: P. putida GB-1 McoA has never been purified, so its copper stoichiometry and the number and type of its copper centres are unmeasured, as are its Mn(II) and Mn(III) oxidation kinetics (kcat, Km, any substrate inhibition).

OPEN BIOLOGY MF_DARK

What is known: Deletion genetics establish that McoA alone is sufficient for Mn(II) and Mn(III) oxidation in vivo, and Pfam PF07731 plus the PTHR48267:SF1 assignment place it among three-domain multicopper oxidases; the GB-1 review counts four conserved copper-binding domains. No enzyme has been isolated from GB-1.

Significance: The activity asserted here (GO:0016724, and the proposed manganese oxidase activity) rests entirely on genetic sufficiency plus family membership; without enzyme data the copper dependence is an inference, and the claim that McoA relieves MnxG substrate inhibition at high Mn(II) cannot be grounded in measured affinities.

What would resolve it: Heterologous expression and purification of GB-1 McoA with copper and kinetic characterization, compared with MnxG (see suggested_experiments).

Provenance (the field's own admissions):

  • "While the biochemical characteristics of MnxG and McoA from P. putida GB-1 have not been investigated" — PMID:42757987

Gap: Where McoA resides and acts - and whether its hydrophobic N-terminal segment is a cleaved Sec signal peptide or an uncleaved membrane anchor - has not been determined experimentally.

OPEN BIOLOGY CC_DARK

What is known: Mn oxides accumulate extracellularly and the GB-1 review reports an N-terminal Sec signal peptide, while Phobius calls the same segment a transmembrane helix (residues 12-33); no fractionation, N-terminal sequencing or tagged-protein localization of McoA has been reported.

Significance: No cellular component annotation can be made for this protein until the compartment is established, and the topology decides whether Mn(II) is oxidized in the periplasm or outside the cell.

What would resolve it: N-terminal sequencing or fractionation of epitope-tagged McoA (see suggested_experiments).

Provenance (the field's own admissions):

  • "the cellular localization of MnxG and McoA is unknown, although the proteins are likely exported" — PMID:27084014

Gap: Whether P. putida GB-1 McoA needs accessory subunits for activity - analogous to the hexameric MnxE3F3 ring that caps Bacillus MnxG - or instead works cooperatively with MnxG in the same cells, is unknown.

OPEN BIOLOGY MF_DARK

What is known: The Bacillus Mnx complex is structurally defined as MnxG capped by alternating MnxE and MnxF subunits; in P. putida the wild type oxidizes Mn faster than either single deletion strain, and reporter fusions show mnxG and mcoA are induced in different cell subpopulations, so cooperation within a cell is consistent with the data but not established; the review that raises the question states it needs further investigation.

Significance: It decides whether the activity should be asserted with enables or contributes_to, and whether a protein complex term applies.

What would resolve it: Purification of McoA with and without candidate partners, and co-localization or interaction assays with MnxG (see suggested_experiments).

Provenance (the field's own admissions):

  • "The structure of the Bacillus Mnx complex raises the question of whether accessory proteins are needed for activity of the MCOs in pseudomonads or whether MnxG and McoA are colocalized and work cooperatively." — PMID:42757987

EC 1.16.3.3 (ExplorEnz, looked up 2026-10-28)

Recorded verbatim-ish so it need not be re-checked (see also mnxG notes):
- Accepted name: manganese oxidase
- Reaction: 4 Mn2+ + 2 O2 + 4 H2O = 4 Mn(IV)O2 + 8 H+ (overall reaction)
- (1a) 4 Mn2+ + O2 + 4 H+ = 4 Mn3+ + 2 H2O
- (1b) 4 Mn3+ + O2 + 6 H2O = 4 Mn(IV)O2 + 12 H+
- Other name(s): mnxG (gene name); mofA (gene name); moxA (gene name); cotA (gene name)
- Systematic name: manganese(II):oxygen oxidoreductase
- Comments: "The enzyme, which belongs to the multicopper oxidase family, is found in many
bacterial strains. It oxidizes soluble manganese(II) to insoluble manganese(IV) oxides."

The overall product is Mn(IV)O2; Mn(III) is an intermediate (step 1a), not the product, so
the proposed term's definition states MnO2 and the one-electron steps live in
justification as mechanism. The definition quotes the ExplorEnz overall reaction in that
entry's own canonical form (4 Mn2+ + 2 O2 + 4 H2O = 4 Mn(IV)O2 + 8 H+); an earlier draft
wrote the same reaction halved (2 Mn2+ + O2 + 2 H2O = 2 MnO2 + 4 H+), which balances but
does not match the form quoted two lines above it. Note that cotA, one of the EC entry's gene names, is also the
protein that dominates McoA's PANTHER subfamily (below).
