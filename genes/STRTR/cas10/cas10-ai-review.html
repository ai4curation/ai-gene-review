Gene Description

Cas10 (Csm1) is the large catalytic subunit of the type III-A Csm effector complex of Streptococcus thermophilus, which assembles one copy of Cas10 and one of Csm4 at one end of a filament of five Csm3 subunits, with three copies of Csm2 and a single Csm5, around a mature CRISPR RNA. The complex is licensed not by invader DNA but by base-pairing of its crRNA to a nascent transcript of the invading element, and that recognition event switches on two chemically unrelated activities that lie in separate domains of Cas10. The N-terminal HD domain is a single-strand-specific deoxyribonuclease, active with Mn2+, Co2+ or Ni2+ but not Mg2+, which degrades the single-stranded DNA exposed in the transcription bubble of the locus being transcribed; because recognition is transcription-dependent, the nuclease destroys actively expressed invader DNA while leaving silent and host DNA untouched. The C-terminal Palm domain, recognised as a GGDEF-like fold, polymerises and cyclises ATP into cyclic oligoadenylates ranging from cyclic triadenylate to cyclic hexaadenylate with release of diphosphate, a reaction that needs a free 3'-OH ribose. These cyclic oligoadenylates are diffusible second messengers that leave the complex and allosterically activate the standalone ribonuclease Csm6 through its CARF domain, amplifying a single recognition event into indiscriminate RNA degradation. The two catalytic centres are independent: substituting the HD-domain aspartate at position 16 abolishes DNA degradation while leaving cyclic oligoadenylate synthesis intact, and substituting the Palm-domain aspartate pair at 575-576 does the reverse. Both outputs are self-limiting, because cleavage of the bound target RNA by Csm3 and its subsequent dissociation shut the complex down, and pairing of the crRNA 5' handle with the target's 3' flank suppresses the nuclease on self transcripts.

Proposed New Ontology Terms

cyclic oligoadenylate synthase activity

Definition: Catalysis of the polymerisation and cyclisation of ATP into a cyclic oligoadenylate with release of diphosphate, for example 4 ATP = cyclic tetraadenylate + 4 diphosphate, or 6 ATP = cyclic hexaadenylate + 6 diphosphate. The product is a closed ring of three to six adenosine 5'-monophosphate units joined by 3'-5' phosphodiester bonds, which acts as a second messenger. The activity resides in the Palm (GGDEF-like) domains of the Cas10/Csm1 subunit of type III CRISPR-Cas effector complexes, where it is licensed by binding of a target RNA complementary to the complex's CRISPR RNA.

Justification: No GO molecular-function term describes the synthesis of cyclic oligoadenylate (cA3-cA6) second messengers, which is one of the two catalytic outputs of Cas10 and the step that makes type III CRISPR immunity a two-tier, amplifying response. GO:0001730 2'-5'-oligoadenylate synthetase activity must not be reused for it: that term is defined as "Catalysis of the reaction: ATP = pppA(2'p5'A)n oligomers. This reaction requires the binding of double-stranded RNA", which is the metazoan interferon-induced OAS enzyme. Its product is a linear 2'-5'-linked oligomer that retains a 5'-triphosphate, its trigger is free double-stranded RNA, and it sits under GO:0070566 adenylyltransferase activity. Cas10 instead makes a covalently closed ring of 3'-5'-linked AMP units, releases diphosphate at every step, and is triggered by crRNA-guided recognition of a target RNA inside a ribonucleoprotein complex; the requirement for a free 3'-OH ribose, measured on this very protein, is the direct chemical counter-indication to the 2'-5' term. The natural placement is alongside the bacterial cyclic-dinucleotide synthases, which GO already distinguishes at this granularity and places as direct children of GO:0016779 nucleotidyltransferase activity: GO:0106408 diadenylate cyclase activity ("Catalysis of the reaction: 2 ATP = 3',3'-c-di-AMP + 2 diphosphate") and GO:0052621 diguanylate cyclase activity. A cyclic oligoadenylate synthase term is the missing member of that series. UniProt already records the reaction for this entry with Rhea cross-references (RHEA:58276 for cA6, RHEA:58280 for cA4 in the Thermus orthologue) and ChEBI entities for the products, so the term can be given exact external mappings on creation. The module modules/crispr_cas_adaptive_immunity.yaml carries the Cas10 Palm-domain annoton with no GO id and records the gap, but a module cannot propose a term; this is that proposal. A matching biological-process term for cyclic oligoadenylate signalling, and a cyclic oligoadenylate ring nuclease activity term for the enzymes that destroy the signal, are the companion requests.

Core Functions

Metal-dependent, single-strand-specific deoxyribonuclease of the N-terminal HD domain, requiring Mn2+, Co2+ or Ni2+ and inactive with Mg2+. Base-pairing of the complex's crRNA to a nascent invader transcript activates the nuclease, which degrades the single-stranded DNA exposed in the transcription bubble, so that destruction is directed at invader loci that are actively transcribed and not at silent or host DNA. The D16A substitution abolishes this activity without affecting cyclic oligoadenylate synthesis. Activity is extinguished as Csm3 cleaves the bound target RNA and the fragments dissociate, and is suppressed on self transcripts when the crRNA 5' handle pairs with the target's 3' flanking sequence.

Substituting the Palm-domain aspartate pair at 575-576 abolishes this activity while leaving the HD nuclease intact, establishing the two centres as independent. GO has no term for this activity, so the proposed term is named here rather than forcing an ill-fitting id.

Suggested Questions for Experts

Q: Why does this enzyme produce a cA3 to cA6 mixture when its cognate receiver, Csm6, responds to one ring size? Is the distribution controlled by the complex, by ATP concentration, or trimmed afterwards by a ring nuclease?

Q: Does ablating Cas10 catalysis, as opposed to ablating Csm3, cause a measurable loss of phage immunity in Streptococcus thermophilus itself? Until that experiment exists, GO:0051607 for this subunit rests on curator inference rather than a phenotype.

Q: Should GO acquire a cellular-component term for the Csm/Cmr effector complex? Complex membership for every curated Cas subunit currently collapses onto the root term GO:0032991, which loses the distinction between Cascade, Csm and Cmr.

Q: Is a biological-process term for cyclic oligoadenylate signalling warranted, parallel to GO:0061939 c-di-GMP signaling, so that the producer, the CARF-domain receivers and the ring nucleases that terminate the signal can be linked in the ontology?

Suggested Experiments

Experiment: Determine the ring-size distribution of the products as a function of ATP concentration, metal identity and target-RNA length by LC-MS, to establish whether the cA3-cA6 spectrum is a property of the active site or of the assay conditions.

Experiment: Build a cryo-EM structure of the StCsm complex with a bound target RNA and a non-hydrolysable ATP analogue in the Palm domains, to show how target recognition at the Csm3 filament is transmitted to the cyclase active site tens of angstroms away.

Experiment: Challenge Streptococcus thermophilus carrying wild-type, D16A and D575/D576A cas10 alleles with a protospacer-bearing phage, separating the contribution of invader-DNA degradation from that of cyclic oligoadenylate signalling to immunity in the native host.

Experiment: Measure the kinetics of cOA accumulation and decay in vivo after phage challenge, using a quantitative assay for cA4 and cA6, to test whether the host encodes a ring nuclease that terminates the signal.

  • UniProt A0A0A7HFE1, reviewed (Swiss-Prot), 758 aa. RecName "CRISPR system
    single-strand-specific deoxyribonuclease Cas10/Csm1 (subtype III-A)", short name

The two activities are genetically separable

UniProt's MUTAGEN features (both ECO:0000269|PubMed:27105119 and
ECO:0000269|PubMed:28663439) are the cleanest evidence that one protein carries two
independent catalytic centres:

MUTAGEN 16: D->A: Dramatically decreased ssDNase activity. Wild-type synthesis of cOA.
MUTAGEN 575..576: DD->AA: Wild-type ssDNase activity. No synthesis of cOA.

That reciprocal pattern is why this review records two separate core functions rather
than one composite activity.

    (cA3-cA6) second messengers, which is one of the two catalytic outputs of Cas10 and
    the step that makes type III CRISPR immunity a two-tier, amplifying response.
