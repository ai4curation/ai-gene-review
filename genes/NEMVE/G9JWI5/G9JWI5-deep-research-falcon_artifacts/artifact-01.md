| **Overall receptor architecture** | Notch is a modular, single-pass transmembrane receptor with a large extracellular domain, one membrane-spanning segment, and an intracellular signaling domain. This architecture is conserved in cnidarians and bilaterians. (pqac-00000023, pqac-00000026) | Supports direct cell–cell signaling followed by proteolytic release of an intracellular transcriptional effector. | Strong comparative evidence; the supplied UniProt G9JWI5 record is explicitly a **fragment**, so it should not be treated as a complete receptor model. |
| **Extracellular domain** | The extracellular region contains EGF-like and calcium-binding EGF-like repeats; complete Notch receptors also generally contain LIN-12/Notch repeats and a negative regulatory region. EGF-repeat number and arrangement are more evolutionarily variable than the intracellular region. (pqac-00000022, pqac-00000023, pqac-00000029) | EGF-like repeats mediate ligand recognition and calcium-stabilized protein interactions, whereas the negative regulatory region keeps the cleavage site inaccessible until activation. | G9JWI5 directly carries EGF, calcium-binding EGF-like, and EGF-like conserved-site annotations; other extracellular elements are family-level inference unless confirmed in a complete *Nematostella* sequence. |
| **Intracellular domain** | The intracellular region contains ankyrin repeats and, in complete Notch proteins, typically includes RAM and nuclear-localization elements. Ankyrin repeats provide the conserved protein-interaction platform used by released NICD. (pqac-00000022, pqac-00000023, pqac-00000024) | After receptor cleavage, NICD can enter the nucleus and regulate transcription through canonical CSL-associated or alternative mechanisms. | Ankyrin repeats are directly annotated in G9JWI5; nuclear NICD has been observed experimentally in *Nematostella*. (pqac-00000021) |
| **Conservation across Metazoa** | LNR and intracellular/NICD architecture are strongly conserved across animal lineages, whereas the extracellular EGF-repeat region shows greater lineage-specific diversification. Core Notch components existed early in metazoan evolution. (pqac-00000022, pqac-00000025, pqac-00000026) | The receptor’s central signal-transduction mechanism is ancient, while extracellular diversification may alter ligand recognition, regulation, or tissue-specific deployment. | Supported by comparative domain and phylogenetic analyses. |
| **Receptor and ligand repertoire in Nematostella** | Current developmental-genomic analyses identify **one Notch receptor** and **four candidate ligands**: Delta, Delta-like, Jagged1-like, and Jagged1B-like. (pqac-00000001, pqac-00000005, pqac-00000049) | A single receptor can receive signals from a diversified DSL-ligand repertoire; Delta is currently the best-supported ligand in early germ-layer boundary signaling. | Repertoire supported by genomic and expression analyses; direct biochemical binding has not been demonstrated separately for all four ligands. |
| **Canonical signaling capacity** | The conserved model is ligand-dependent receptor activation, ADAM-family S2 cleavage, γ-secretase S3 cleavage, NICD release, nuclear entry, and transcriptional regulation through CSL/Su(H), often with MAML and Hes/Hey targets. *Nematostella* has NvSu(H) and NvHes-family genes, and its development is sensitive to γ-secretase inhibition. (pqac-00000000, pqac-00000004, pqac-00000006) | Demonstrates that *Nematostella* possesses the molecular toolkit and proteolytic logic required for canonical Notch signaling. | Strong evidence for cleavage-dependent signaling; direct use of every canonical component is context-dependent. |
| **Non-canonical or Hes-independent signaling** | Neurogenic Notch activity in *Nematostella* is often interpreted as predominantly non-canonical or Hes-independent. NvNotch knockdown reduces NvHes2/3 approximately three- to fourfold, but NICD/Delta gain-of-function and Hes-overexpression experiments do not establish a simple linear Notch→Hes mechanism. (pqac-00000018, pqac-00000026, pqac-00000036) | Signaling mode is likely context-specific rather than exclusively canonical or non-canonical; germ-layer induction and neural-progenitor regulation may use different nuclear effectors. | Mechanistically unresolved; “predominantly non-canonical” should be treated as an expert interpretation, not a universal property of all *Nematostella* tissues. |
| **Cnidarian lineage pattern** | Cnidarian Notch receptors form a lineage distinct from bilaterian receptors, with anthozoan and medusozoan subclades. Anthozoans retain a broadly conserved pathway, whereas some medusozoans show component losses or extracellular-domain reduction. (pqac-00000023, pqac-00000026, pqac-00000027) | *Nematostella*, an anthozoan, is especially informative for reconstructing the ancestral cnidarian–bilaterian Notch system because it retains a comparatively complete toolkit. | Supported by broad comparative genomic analyses. |
| **Key evolutionary implication** | Conserved receptor processing, NICD architecture, and cell-fate/boundary functions in cnidarians indicate that Notch-mediated juxtacrine fate regulation arose early in animal evolution. Recent *Nematostella* work suggests that Notch-dependent germ-layer boundary formation may predate the cnidarian–bilaterian split. (pqac-00000008, pqac-00000012, pqac-00000022) | Notch’s ancestral role likely included coordinating adjacent-cell fate decisions and tissue boundaries, with later lineage-specific expansion of ligands and extracellular receptor features. | Functional conservation is well supported; the precise ancestral developmental role remains an evolutionary inference. |
