|---|---|---|---|---|
| Germ-layer formation and boundary specification | Induces definitive endoderm at the interface between mesoderm and ectoderm; separates endodermal from mesodermal identity | Delta and Delta-like ligands in mesoderm activate Notch in adjacent ectodermal cells. Released NICD induces an endodermal transcriptional program, consistent with lateral induction | γ-Secretase inhibition reduces or eliminates endodermal markers, including *foxA*, *brachyury*, *wnt1*, and *wnt3*, while largely preserving mesodermal markers and gastrulation. Ectopic NICD induces *foxA* and *brachyury*, demonstrating necessity and sufficiency | (pqac-00000008, pqac-00000018, pqac-00000020, pqac-00000036) |
| Neural-progenitor regulation | Restricts the number of neural progenitors and prevents excess production of neurons and nematocytes | Contact-dependent Notch signaling operates through lateral-inhibition-like regulation and interacts with the NvSoxB(2)/NvAth-like neurogenic network | Notch inhibition increases NvSoxB(2)-expressing cells, neurons, and nematocytes, indicating expansion of the neural-progenitor pool | (pqac-00000012, pqac-00000029) |
| Neural differentiation | Broadly suppresses premature neuronal differentiation | Activated NvNotch or NvDelta represses *NvashA* and other differentiation-associated genes. Evidence in this context favors a non-canonical, Su(H)/Hes-independent mechanism, although canonical activity may occur in other tissues or stages | NvNotch morpholino or DAPT treatment increases *NvashA*, *Nvgcm*, *Nvsoxb2*, *Nvath-like*, and other neural markers; NICD overexpression suppresses these programs. Manipulating NvHes genes does not consistently reproduce the effect | (pqac-00000010, pqac-00000024, pqac-00000026, pqac-00000027, pqac-00000033) |
| Cnidocyte differentiation | Supports specification or differentiation of cnidocytes, the cnidarian-specific stinging sensory-effector cells | Notch cleavage and, in at least some experiments, Su(H)-dependent transcription regulate cnidocyte fate; neural and cnidocyte development share upstream progenitor regulators | DAPT treatment or Su(H) disruption causes loss or substantial reduction of cnidocytes. Notch inhibition can also expand early nematocyte/progenitor populations, suggesting stage-dependent control of progenitor production versus terminal differentiation | (pqac-00000009, pqac-00000011, pqac-00000012, pqac-00000022) |
| Endodermal tissue organization | Converts specified endodermal identity into correctly organized epithelial tissue | Canonical receptor cleavage and NICD–Su(H)-dependent transcription contribute to differentiation and maintenance of a normal endodermal monolayer | Notch or Su(H) inhibition produces stratified, disorganized endoderm even when gastrulation occurs | (pqac-00000011, pqac-00000019, pqac-00000043) |
| Pharynx and mesentery development | Promotes pharyngeal growth and mesentery formation after initial regional specification | Notch-dependent differentiation acts downstream of or alongside regional patterning genes; early endoderm induction also activates pharyngeal regulators such as *foxA* | DAPT-treated or Su(H)-depleted embryos show stunted pharynges and absent mesenteries. Earlier work found persistent *NvfoxA* despite defective pharyngeal differentiation, whereas newer work shows Notch-dependent induction of *foxA* during initial endoderm specification | (pqac-00000008, pqac-00000011, pqac-00000018, pqac-00000032) |
| Tentacle and polyp morphogenesis | Supports normal tentacle formation and later body-pattern development | Dynamic Notch/Delta activity persists in differentiating tentacle buds and coordinates cell-fate decisions during planula-to-polyp development | Pathway inhibition causes defective tentacle formation together with broader pharyngeal, mesenterial, and endodermal abnormalities | (pqac-00000019, pqac-00000043, pqac-00000047) |
| Oral–aboral and ectodermal patterning | Helps organize axial pattern indirectly through endoderm induction and downstream Wnt expression | Notch induces endodermal *wnt1* and *wnt3* and interacts functionally with β-catenin/Wnt- and MAPK-defined territories | Notch inhibition shifts *wnt2* expression orally, expands aboral markers *foxQ2a* and *six3/6*, and disrupts pharyngeal patterning | (pqac-00000034, pqac-00000036, pqac-00000037) |
| Head and whole-body regeneration | Is required for progression of the regenerative program after injury | The exact cellular targets remain unresolved; available evidence places Notch downstream of or separate from early wound closure and implicates it in regenerative patterning and differentiation | Pharmacological Notch inhibition blocks head regeneration. By contrast, MEK/ERK inhibition impairs both wound healing and regeneration, suggesting Notch is required more specifically for regeneration than for initial wound closure | (pqac-00000042, pqac-00000044) |
| Developmental self-organization | Coordinates restoration of germ-layer boundaries and axial polarity in reaggregated embryonic cells | A Wnt–Notch feedback loop polarizes endodermal cells and coordinates immigration of a mesodermal cluster with associated endoderm, establishing the inner layer and oral–aboral axis | Disruption of Notch after cell dissociation causes endodermal cells to transiently adopt an endomesodermal profile; restored signaling accompanies boundary re-establishment and organized gastruloid development | (pqac-00000023, pqac-00000025) |


*Table: Summary of experimentally supported roles for Notch signaling in *Nematostella vectensis*, spanning germ-layer specification, neural and cnidocyte development, tissue morphogenesis, and regeneration. The table distinguishes proposed mechanisms from observed loss- and gain-of-function phenotypes.*
