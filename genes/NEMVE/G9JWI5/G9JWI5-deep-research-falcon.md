# Comprehensive Research Report: Notch Gene Function in Nematostella vectensis

## Gene Identity and Protein Context

The Notch protein (UniProt ID: G9JWI5) from Nematostella vectensis (starlet sea anemone) is a fragment of the Notch family receptor containing characteristic ankyrin repeats and EGF-like domains (foster2025notchexpressionduring pages 8-10, lovy2025thenotchpathway pages 2-3). Nematostella vectensis, a cnidarian anthozoan, represents a phylogenetically informative position as the sister group to bilaterians, making it valuable for understanding the evolution and ancestral functions of Notch signaling (haillot2025segregationofendoderm pages 1-2, lovy2025thenotchpathway pages 1-2). Recent genomic analyses have identified one Notch receptor gene and four putative ligands (Delta, Delta-like, Jagged1-like, and Jagged1B-like) in the Nematostella genome (haillot2024notchβcateninand pages 16-20, haillot2025notchβcateninand pages 7-9).

## Molecular Mechanism and Protein Structure

### Structural Organization

Notch is a large, single-pass transmembrane receptor with a modular domain architecture that is evolutionarily conserved across metazoans (lovy2025thenotchpathway pages 2-3). The extracellular domain contains multiple EGF-like repeats and calcium-binding EGF-like repeats that mediate ligand recognition, along with LIN-12/Notch repeats (LNRs) and a negative regulatory region (foster2025notchexpressionduring pages 8-10, vlachakis2020anupdatedevolutionary pages 4-7, vlachakis2020anupdatedevolutionary pages 10-12). The intracellular domain contains ankyrin repeats, which provide a conserved protein-interaction platform, along with RAM and nuclear localization domains in complete receptors (foster2025notchexpressionduring pages 8-10, lovy2025thenotchpathway pages 2-3). Importantly, while the intracellular NICD architecture is highly conserved across metazoans, the extracellular EGF-repeat region shows greater evolutionary variability, reflecting lineage-specific diversification in ligand recognition and regulation (foster2025notchexpressionduring pages 8-10, lv2024evolutionandfunction pages 14-15).

### Molecular Mechanism of Action

Notch operates through a juxtacrine (cell-contact-dependent) signaling mechanism (lovy2025thenotchpathway pages 1-2, lv2024evolutionandfunction pages 1-2). The receptor is synthesized and glycosylated in the ER and Golgi, undergoes S1 cleavage by furin-like enzymes, and is displayed at the cell surface as a heterodimer (lv2024evolutionandfunction pages 1-2). Upon ligand binding to a neighboring cell, the receptor undergoes sequential proteolytic processing: S2 cleavage by ADAM metalloproteases exposes the transmembrane region, followed by S3 cleavage by the γ-secretase complex, which releases the Notch intracellular domain (NICD) (lv2024evolutionandfunction pages 15-16, lv2024evolutionandfunction pages 2-4). 

The released NICD translocates to the nucleus where it functions as a transcriptional regulator (lv2024evolutionandfunction pages 1-2, haillot2025notchβcateninand pages 9-12). In Nematostella, nuclear NICD has been directly observed at the endoderm-mesoderm boundary and in neural precursor regions during early gastrulation, forming an uninterrupted ring at the blastopore corresponding to germ-layer boundaries and smaller patches in the aboral half consistent with neuronal precursor specification (haillot2025notchβcateninand pages 9-12).

### Ligand Interactions and Substrate Specificity

The Nematostella genome encodes four putative Notch ligands: Delta, Delta-like, Jagged1-like, and Jagged1B-like, all belonging to the DSL (Delta/Serrate/LAG-2) ligand family (haillot2024notchβcateninand pages 16-20, haillot2025notchβcateninand pages 7-9). Delta is the best-supported functional ligand based on expression patterns and experimental evidence (haillot2024notchβcateninand pages 16-20, haillot2025notchβcateninand pages 9-12). Delta and Delta-like are zygotically expressed and predominantly localize to mesodermal cells, with Delta peaking at 14-16 hours post-fertilization (hpf) and Delta-like remaining abundant after gastrulation (haillot2024notchβcateninand pages 16-20). Jagged1-like shows later, weaker mesodermal expression beginning around 18 hpf, while Jagged1B-like is expressed at very low levels (haillot2024notchβcateninand pages 16-20). These ligands are membrane-bound proteins that interact with the Notch receptor on adjacent cells through their extracellular domains, though direct biochemical binding has not been demonstrated for each individual ligand (haillot2024notchβcateninand pages 16-20).

## Subcellular Localization

The Notch receptor is localized to the plasma membrane as a transmembrane protein in its intact form (lv2024evolutionandfunction pages 1-2, lovy2025thenotchpathway pages 2-3). Following ligand-induced activation and proteolytic processing, the Notch intracellular domain (NICD) translocates from the membrane to the nucleus, where it executes its transcriptional regulatory function (haillot2025notchβcateninand pages 9-12). Immunohistochemistry studies in Nematostella embryos have demonstrated nuclear NICD localization specifically at tissue boundaries and in differentiating cell populations (haillot2025notchβcateninand pages 9-12).

## Primary Biological Functions and Signaling Pathways

### 1. Definitive Endoderm Induction and Germ Layer Specification

One of the most precisely characterized functions of Notch in Nematostella is its essential role in definitive endoderm induction during early development (haillot2025segregationofendoderm pages 1-2, haillot2025notchβcateninand pages 12-14, haillot2025notchβcateninand pages 9-12). This represents a landmark finding published in 2025 demonstrating that cnidarians have three distinct germ-layer identities (haillot2025segregationofendoderm pages 1-2).

**Mechanism:** Following the initial specification of mesoderm (driven by MAPK signaling) and ectoderm (influenced by Wnt/β-catenin signaling), Delta-expressing mesodermal cells signal to adjacent Notch-expressing ectodermal cells at their interface (haillot2025notchβcateninand pages 9-12, haillot2025notchβcateninand pages 12-14). This Delta-Notch interaction induces a third cellular state—the definitive endoderm—at the boundary between mesoderm and ectoderm through a process of lateral induction (haillot2025notchβcateninand pages 12-14, haillot2024notchβcateninand pages 24-27). 

**Temporal dynamics:** Mesodermal markers appear at approximately 6-8 hours post-fertilization, while endodermal markers follow about four hours later at 10-12 hpf (haillot2025segregationofendoderm pages 1-2). Notch mRNA is maternally deposited and initially ubiquitous but becomes excluded from the mesoderm around 10 hpf, establishing complementary expression domains with Delta (haillot2024notchβcateninand pages 16-20).

**Experimental evidence:** Pharmacological inhibition of Notch with γ-secretase inhibitors (LY-411575) specifically suppresses endodermal gene expression including foxA, brachyury, wnt1, and wnt3, while largely sparing mesodermal markers and gastrulation (haillot2025notchβcateninand pages 9-12, haillot2024notchβcateninand pages 24-27). Conversely, ectopic expression of NICD is sufficient to induce endodermal markers (foxA and brachyury) in ectodermal cells without activating the mesodermal marker snailA, demonstrating that Notch signaling is both necessary and sufficient for endoderm induction and is selective for endodermal identity (haillot2025notchβcateninand pages 9-12, haillot2024notchβcateninand pages 24-27, haillot2025segregationofendoderm pages 8-11).

**Pathway interactions:** Notch functions within a coordinated network with Wnt/β-catenin and MAPK pathways (haillot2025notchβcateninand pages 12-14, haillot2025segregationofendoderm pages 11-12). β-catenin signaling helps establish the spatial domains by promoting ectodermal Notch expression and repressing mesodermal Delta expression (haillot2025notchβcateninand pages 9-12). MAPK/ERK signaling activates mesodermal genes and helps repress Notch in the mesoderm (haillot2025notchβcateninand pages 9-12). Notch inhibition also disrupts downstream Wnt-dependent ectodermal patterning, affecting the positioning of Wnt2, foxQ2a, and Six3/6 expression domains (haillot2025segregationofendoderm pages 8-11).

### 2. Neurogenesis Regulation and Neural Progenitor Control

Notch functions as a negative regulator of neurogenesis by restricting the generation and expansion of neural progenitor cells through lateral inhibition (lv2024evolutionandfunction pages 15-16, richards2015regulationofnematostella pages 1-2, rentzsch2017thecellularand pages 8-10). This represents a conserved neurogenic function shared with bilaterians.

**Mechanism:** Notch signaling limits the number of NvSoxB(2)-positive neural progenitor cells (NPCs), thereby restraining neuronal and nematocyte (cnidarian stinging cell) differentiation (richards2015regulationofnematostella pages 1-2, rentzsch2017thecellularand pages 8-10). The pathway interacts with key neurogenic transcription factors including NvSoxB(2), NvAth-like (Atonal/Neurogenin family), and NvAshA (Achaete-scute family) (lv2024evolutionandfunction pages 15-16, richards2015regulationofnematostella pages 4-5). NvSoxB(2) and NvAth-like are co-expressed in dividing neural progenitors from blastula stages, while NvAshA appears later in non-dividing differentiating cells (richards2015regulationofnematostella pages 4-5).

**Experimental evidence:** Treatment with the γ-secretase inhibitor DAPT, which blocks Notch receptor cleavage, increases neural marker expression and produces a pronounced "neurogenic" phenotype with elevated numbers of neurons, nematocytes, and NvSoxB(2)-expressing cells (richards2015regulationofnematostella pages 1-2, rentzsch2017thecellularand pages 8-10). NvNotch knockdown similarly enhances neurogenesis, while Notch hyperactivation suppresses neural differentiation (rentzsch2017thecellularand pages 8-10). Notch knockdown also reduces expression of NvHes2 and NvHes3 (Notch target genes) approximately three- to fourfold (richards2015regulationofnematostella pages 8-8).

**Signaling mode:** While NvHes2 and NvHes3 have been identified as candidate downstream targets, the precise transcriptional mechanism remains incompletely resolved (richards2015regulationofnematostella pages 8-8). Overexpression of NvHes2 or NvHes3 did not alter neural gene expression, and NICD/Delta gain-of-function did not affect NvHes expression, suggesting that Nematostella may utilize a predominantly non-canonical, Hes-independent signaling route for neurogenic regulation (rentzsch2017thecellularand pages 8-10, lovy2025thenotchpathway pages 1-2, richards2015regulationofnematostella pages 8-8).

**Spatial expression:** Delta shows an aboral salt-and-pepper expression pattern after 12 hpf, consistent with its role in lateral inhibition among neural precursors (haillot2024notchβcateninand pages 16-20). Nuclear NICD has been detected in small patches in the aboral half of early gastrulae, corresponding to neural precursor specification (haillot2025notchβcateninand pages 9-12).

### 3. Regeneration, Self-Organization, and Axial Polarity

Notch plays a critical role in whole-body regeneration and the self-organization of germ layers and body axes following tissue disruption (narayanaswamy2026notchcoordinatesselforganization pages 12-14, narayanaswamy2025notchcoordinatesselforganization pages 1-3, narayanaswamy2026notchcoordinatesselforganization pages 1-4, lv2024evolutionandfunction pages 15-16).

**Mechanism in gastruloids:** In dissociated and reaggregated early gastrula cells (gastruloids), Notch coordinates the restoration of germ-layer boundaries and endodermal identity through a feedback loop with Wnt/β-catenin signaling (narayanaswamy2026notchcoordinatesselforganization pages 12-14, narayanaswamy2025notchcoordinatesselforganization pages 1-3). Following dissociation, Notch signaling is transiently disrupted, causing endodermal cells to adopt an endomesodermal molecular state marked by expression of the mesodermal marker cadherin1 (narayanaswamy2025notchcoordinatesselforganization pages 1-3). As development proceeds, Notch activity is reestablished, which resolves this intermediate state and restores clear germ-layer boundaries (narayanaswamy2025notchcoordinatesselforganization pages 1-3, narayanaswamy2026notchcoordinatesselforganization pages 1-4).

**Morphogenetic organization:** Mesodermal and endodermal cells sort to the gastruloid surface, where mesodermal cells form clusters of approximately 30 cells with hitchhiking endodermal cells attached (narayanaswamy2026notchcoordinatesselforganization pages 1-4). One mesodermal cluster subsequently immigrates at a pole together with associated endodermal cells, establishing the future pharynx, inner layer, and oral-aboral axis in a coordinated process (narayanaswamy2026notchcoordinatesselforganization pages 12-14, narayanaswamy2026notchcoordinatesselforganization pages 1-4). This symmetry-breaking event depends on a Wnt-Notch feedback loop (narayanaswamy2026notchcoordinatesselforganization pages 1-4).

**Experimental evidence:** Continuous Notch inhibition prevents cell reclustering and blocks axial polarization in gastruloids (narayanaswamy2026notchcoordinatesselforganization pages 12-14). In intact regenerating animals, pharmacological Notch inhibition blocks whole-body and head regeneration without preventing wound healing, and disrupts pharyngeal growth, tentacle formation, mesentery formation, endodermal morphology, and cnidocyte differentiation (lv2024evolutionandfunction pages 15-16, rottinger2021nematostellavectensisan pages 9-11). This distinguishes Notch from MEK/ERK signaling, which blocks both wound healing and regeneration (rottinger2021nematostellavectensisan pages 9-11).

**Evolutionary significance:** The authors propose that Notch-mediated germ-layer boundary formation in Nematostella represents an evolutionarily conserved mechanism shared with bilaterians, suggesting that this developmental program may have predated the cnidarian-bilaterian split (haillot2025segregationofendoderm pages 1-2, haillot2025segregationofendoderm pages 11-12, narayanaswamy2026notchcoordinatesselforganization pages 1-4).

## Expression Patterns

Notch shows dynamic spatial and temporal expression during Nematostella development (lv2024evolutionandfunction pages 8-11, haillot2024notchβcateninand pages 16-20, lv2024evolutionandfunction pages 15-16). Notch transcripts are maternally deposited and initially expressed ubiquitously throughout the embryo (haillot2024notchβcateninand pages 16-20). Around 10 hpf, Notch expression is lost from the mesoderm while remaining in the ectoderm, and after gastrulation it is further downregulated in the midbody ectoderm (haillot2024notchβcateninand pages 16-20). During planula stages, NvNotch is associated with neural structures and pharyngeal endoderm, while NvDelta and NvJagged are found in oral ectoderm and endoderm (lv2024evolutionandfunction pages 8-11, lv2024evolutionandfunction pages 15-16). The pathway components are expressed during gastrulation, planula formation, and polyp formation in both ectodermal and endodermal tissues (lv2024evolutionandfunction pages 15-16).

## Evolutionary and Comparative Context

Notch signaling represents an ancient metazoan cell-communication system (foster2025notchexpressionduring pages 8-10, lv2024evolutionandfunction pages 14-15). Cnidarian Notch receptors form a distinct lineage from bilaterian receptors, with anthozoan and medusozoan subclades (lovy2025thenotchpathway pages 2-3, lovy2025thenotchpathway pages 12-13). Nematostella, as an anthozoan, retains a nearly complete Notch pathway toolkit, making it particularly informative for understanding the ancestral cnidarian-bilaterian signaling system (lovy2025thenotchpathway pages 12-13, lv2024evolutionandfunction pages 15-16).

The conserved NICD architecture and nuclear localization signals indicate that the core signal-transduction mechanism was established early in animal evolution, while extracellular domains have undergone greater lineage-specific diversification (foster2025notchexpressionduring pages 8-10, lovy2025thenotchpathway pages 1-2). The presence of Notch components in early-diverging metazoans, including cnidarians, supports the hypothesis that Notch-mediated juxtacrine fate regulation and boundary formation arose before the evolution of centralized nervous systems (lv2024evolutionandfunction pages 14-15, lv2024evolutionandfunction pages 15-16).

Nematostella predominantly utilizes a non-canonical, Hes-independent mode of Notch signaling in neural development, contrasting with the canonical CSL-dependent pathway more commonly described in bilaterians (lovy2025thenotchpathway pages 1-2). However, the pathway is sensitive to γ-secretase inhibition and possesses NvSu(H) (CSL homolog) and NvHes genes, demonstrating that it retains the molecular capacity for canonical signaling (lv2024evolutionandfunction pages 15-16, richards2015regulationofnematostella pages 8-8).

## Conclusions

The Notch protein (G9JWI5) in Nematostella vectensis is a transmembrane receptor that functions as a central regulator of cell-fate decisions and tissue organization through juxtacrine signaling. Its primary molecular function involves ligand-dependent proteolytic activation, nuclear translocation of the NICD, and transcriptional regulation. The protein localizes to the plasma membrane in its intact form, with activated NICD functioning in the nucleus.

Notch executes three major biological roles: (1) inducing definitive endoderm at the mesoderm-ectoderm boundary through Delta-Notch lateral induction, (2) restricting neurogenesis by limiting neural progenitor cell generation through lateral inhibition, and (3) coordinating germ-layer organization and axial polarity during regeneration and self-organization. These functions are mediated through interactions with Wnt/β-catenin and MAPK signaling pathways, with Notch acting as a key boundary-forming and fate-specifying signal.

Recent studies (2024-2025) have demonstrated that Nematostella Notch signaling contributes to the specification of three distinct germ-layer identities in this morphologically diploblastic cnidarian, challenging traditional views and suggesting that triploblasty may predate the cnidarian-bilaterian split (haillot2025segregationofendoderm pages 1-2). The conservation of Notch-mediated boundary formation and lateral signaling mechanisms across cnidarians and bilaterians supports an ancient evolutionary origin for these fundamental developmental processes.
