|---|---|---|---|---|
| **General Notch-pathway signaling** | NvDelta is a predicted type-I, single-pass membrane ligand that activates NvNotch on an adjacent cell. Its extracellular DSL and EGF-like domains support receptor recognition; ligand engagement is expected to promote ADAM- and γ-secretase-dependent Notch cleavage and NICD release. Canonical NICD–Su(H) transcription and Hes-independent outputs appear context-dependent in *Nematostella*. (pqac-00000016, pqac-00000051, pqac-00000052) | Cell-surface-associated rather than freely secreted, based on its predicted transmembrane domain. Tissue-restricted expression places signaling at contacts between Delta-producing and Notch-receiving cells. Direct protein immunolocalization remains limited. (pqac-00000012, pqac-00000013) | Comparative domain analysis and pathway perturbation identify NvDelta as a canonical DSL-family Notch ligand, although the physical NvDelta–NvNotch complex has not been structurally characterized. (pqac-00000006, pqac-00000051) | **Earlier foundational evidence:** Marlow et al., 2012; Layden and Martindale, 2014. **Recent synthesis:** Lv et al., 2024. |
| **Germ-layer specification and definitive endoderm formation** | Mesodermal Delta is proposed to activate Notch in immediately adjacent ectodermal cells, inducing definitive endoderm at the mesoderm–ectoderm interface. Notch inhibition reduces *foxA*, *brachyury*, *wnt1*, and *wnt3*, whereas ectopic NICD induces *foxA* and *brachyury* without inducing mesodermal *snailA*. The mechanism is interpreted as boundary-associated lateral induction. (pqac-00000021, pqac-00000022) | *delta* and *delta-like* transcripts are predominantly mesodermal from approximately 10 hours post-fertilization, peak around 14–16 hours, and form a complementary boundary with ectodermal *notch*. Endoderm emerges in interface cells around 12–14 hours post-fertilization. (pqac-00000025, pqac-00000027) | Haillot et al. combined spatial expression, pharmacological Notch inhibition, ectopic NICD activation, and single-cell analyses. The strongest functional tests manipulated Notch rather than G9JWI6 directly; Delta necessity is therefore strongly inferred but not yet established by a clean gene-specific knockout. (pqac-00000021, pqac-00000023, pqac-00000046) | **Recent major development:** 2024 bioRxiv study, followed by a 2025 peer-reviewed *Nature Communications* report. |
| **Neural differentiation and progenitor regulation** | NvDelta overexpression activates Notch and suppresses the proneural differentiation gene *NvashA*. Conversely, Notch knockdown or γ-secretase inhibition expands neural-marker expression. During embryonic neurogenesis this output was reported to be Su(H)- and Hes-independent, suggesting a non-canonical mechanism that limits or delays neural differentiation. (pqac-00000028, pqac-00000029) | Early *delta* expression has a scattered, salt-and-pepper distribution in ectoderm and later includes an aboral salt-and-pepper domain consistent with neurogenic territories. These patterns support short-range signaling among neighboring progenitors and differentiating cells. (pqac-00000010, pqac-00000027) | NvDelta–Venus mRNA overexpression suppressed neural differentiation markers; NvDelta splice-blocking morpholino and DAPT treatments were also developed. Independent Notch-inhibition experiments increased neurons, nematocytes, and NvSoxB(2)-positive progenitors, although not every phenotype was tested with Delta-specific loss of function. (pqac-00000028, pqac-00000030, pqac-00000044) | **Earlier mechanistic evidence:** Layden and Martindale, 2014; Richards and Rentzsch, 2015. **Recent interpretation:** Lv et al., 2024. |
| **Cnidocyte, nematocyte, and spirocyte development** | Notch-pathway activity is required for normal cnidocyte specification, while its inhibition can increase other neural outcomes. NvDelta is a likely local ligand, but available functional experiments chiefly inhibit Notch cleavage or Su(H), so a G9JWI6-specific requirement for cnidogenesis remains incompletely resolved. (pqac-00000005, pqac-00000031, pqac-00000039) | NvDelta transcripts occur in scattered ectodermal cells resembling developing spirocytes and later in spirocyte-producing tentacular ectoderm and developing mesenteries, within broader NvNotch-positive territories. (pqac-00000009, pqac-00000031) | DAPT-mediated Notch inhibition and Su(H) disruption caused marked reduction or loss of cnidocytes. The expression–phenotype correspondence supports Delta-mediated local signaling but does not distinguish NvDelta from other DSL-family ligands. (pqac-00000005, pqac-00000049) | **Earlier foundational evidence:** Marlow et al., 2012; Richards and Rentzsch, 2015. No comparably definitive 2023–2025 Delta-specific knockout study was identified. |
| **Embryonic and larval morphogenesis** | Delta-mediated Notch signaling is implicated in local fate allocation and epithelial organization during gastrulation, planula formation, metamorphosis, and polyp development. Pathway inhibition disrupts pharyngeal extension, mesentery formation, endoderm organization, and cnidocyte differentiation, but these broad phenotypes cannot all be assigned specifically to G9JWI6. (pqac-00000005, pqac-00000053) | Transcripts begin in a few blastula or early-gastrula cells, broaden into oral ectoderm and endoderm during gastrulation and planula development, and later become enriched in developing mesenteries and tentacle ectoderm. (pqac-00000009, pqac-00000011) | In situ hybridization defined dynamic expression, while DAPT and Su(H) perturbations linked the pathway to morphogenesis. These findings support a signaling role rather than an enzymatic, transporter, or structural function. (pqac-00000003, pqac-00000045, pqac-00000049) | **Earlier foundational evidence:** Marlow et al., 2012. Recent germ-layer studies refine the early embryonic interpretation. |
| **Gastruloid self-organization and boundary restoration** | Recent gastruloid work implicates Notch signaling in restoring germ-layer boundaries and axial organization after cell reaggregation. For G9JWI6 specifically, the most defensible model is that Delta supplies a contact-dependent boundary cue to neighboring Notch-positive cells; direct Delta-specific perturbation in gastruloids remains lacking. | Expected at contacts between Delta-positive mesodermal clusters and Notch-positive neighboring cells, extrapolating from complementary embryonic domains. This should be treated as a pathway-informed hypothesis rather than direct G9JWI6 protein imaging. (pqac-00000008, pqac-00000024) | The gene-level annotation is supported indirectly by the mesodermal-Delta/ectodermal-Notch boundary model. A ligand-specific knockout and rescue in gastruloids would be required to assign this process directly to G9JWI6. (pqac-00000023, pqac-00000041) | **Emerging post-2024 direction:** pathway-level evidence is recent, but direct G9JWI6-specific validation remains a research gap. |
| **Evolutionary conservation of developmental signaling** | The presence of a DSL/EGF-containing Delta ligand in an anthozoan supports an origin of ligand-dependent Notch signaling before the cnidarian–bilaterian split. Neural-differentiation restraint may represent an ancestral Hes-independent function, while endoderm induction and germ-layer-boundary formation may reflect an ancient deployment whose exact ancestry remains debated. (pqac-00000033, pqac-00000035) | Conservation concerns molecular topology and developmental deployment: a membrane-tethered ligand expressed in one cell population signals to neighboring Notch-positive cells. Delta ligands diversified early in metazoan evolution, although lineage-specific losses and functional divergence occurred. (pqac-00000036, pqac-00000038) | Comparative analyses and the 2024–2025 *Nematostella* germ-layer studies support conservation of pathway architecture but caution against assuming that every bilaterian Delta function applies to G9JWI6. (pqac-00000037, pqac-00000042) | **Recent synthesis:** Lv et al., 2024; Lövy et al., 2025. **Foundational interpretation:** Layden and Martindale, 2014. |
