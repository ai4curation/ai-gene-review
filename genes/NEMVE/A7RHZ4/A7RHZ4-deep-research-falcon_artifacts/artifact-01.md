| Biological Process/Pathway | Role of MyD88 | Evidence from *N. vectensis* or Cnidarians | Key References |
|---|---|---|---|
| Innate immunity | Acts as a conserved intracellular adaptor that converts receptor-based microbial sensing into host-defense gene expression; it is not an enzyme or direct pattern-recognition receptor. | *N. vectensis* MyD88 transcripts were detected in adult nematosomes, motile multicellular structures containing phagocytic cells and cnidocytes and proposed to have immune functions. Direct loss-of-function evidence for A7RHZ4 itself is unavailable. | Brennan et al. 2017 (pqac-00000003, pqac-00000047); Williams & Gilmore 2022 (pqac-00000032) |
| Toll-like receptor signaling | Uses its C-terminal TIR domain to associate with activated receptor or adaptor TIR domains and its N-terminal Death domain to recruit IRAK-family proteins, bridging receptors to cytosolic signaling machinery. | The single *N. vectensis* TLR can interact with human MYD88 in a heterologous system, demonstrating conserved receptor–adaptor compatibility. Endogenous binding of Nv-TLR to A7RHZ4 has not been shown directly. | Brennan et al. 2017 (pqac-00000004, pqac-00000049); Brennan & Gilmore 2018 (pqac-00000001) |
| Myddosome assembly | MyD88 oligomerization creates a supramolecular scaffold—the Myddosome—that recruits IRAK proteins through Death-domain interactions and amplifies receptor signals. | This mechanism is established for bilaterian MyD88 and is supported for A7RHZ4 by its conserved Death and TIR domains, but no *N. vectensis*-specific Myddosome has been isolated biochemically. | Nimma et al. 2021 (pqac-00000008); Pustelny et al. 2022 (pqac-00000009); Moncrieffe et al. 2020 (pqac-00000011) |
| NF-κB activation | Expected to relay signals through IRAK, TRAF6, TAK1, and IKK, causing IκB removal and NF-κB nuclear translocation; MAPK/AP-1 signaling may also be activated. | Transcripts encoding predicted TLR-to-NF-κB pathway components, including MyD88, occur in *N. vectensis* nematosomes. Nv-TLR activates NF-κB in reconstituted human cells, but endogenous A7RHZ4-dependent activation has not been demonstrated. | Brennan et al. 2017 (pqac-00000003, pqac-00000047); Brennan & Gilmore 2018 (pqac-00000006); Parisi et al. 2020 (pqac-00000036) |
| Pathogen-associated signal recognition | MyD88 does not bind microbial ligands itself; it transmits signals after a TLR or TLR-like receptor recognizes a microbial stimulus. | A reconstituted Nv-TLR pathway responded to heat-inactivated *Vibrio coralliilyticus* and *Salmonella* flagellin in human cells. These results support a MyD88-compatible pathogen-sensing pathway but do not establish A7RHZ4 ligand specificity. | Brennan et al. 2017 (pqac-00000033, pqac-00000049); Brennan & Gilmore 2018 (pqac-00000035) |
| Antibacterial defense | Couples bacterial sensing to transcriptional and cellular defenses, potentially including antimicrobial-effector production and phagocytic responses. | *N. vectensis* nematosomes engulf *V. coralliilyticus* and express predicted TLR–NF-κB pathway genes. In *Hydra*, MyD88 deficiency increases susceptibility to *Pseudomonas aeruginosa*, supplying cnidarian functional evidence but not evidence specific to A7RHZ4. | Brennan et al. 2017 (pqac-00000003, pqac-00000005); Brennan & Gilmore 2018 (pqac-00000035) |
| Host–microbiota interaction | May support constitutive or inducible sensing of resident microbes, helping manage commensal colonization as well as infection. | In *Hydra*, MyD88 reduction produces a host-expression profile resembling that of germ-free polyps, indicating that cnidarian MyD88 signaling responds to resident bacteria. Equivalent experiments have not been reported for A7RHZ4. | Williams & Gilmore 2022 (pqac-00000038, pqac-00000044) |
| Developmental signaling | A possible pathway-associated role remains unresolved; TLR signaling can regulate cnidarian development, but MyD88 dependence has not been established. | Nv-TLR knockdown disrupts embryonic development, yet the downstream route is unknown and may be NF-κB-independent. No experiment shows that A7RHZ4 mediates this phenotype. | Brennan & Gilmore 2018 (pqac-00000007, pqac-00000042); Brennan et al. 2017 (pqac-00000049) |
| Evolution of animal immunity | Represents an ancient modular adaptor linking TIR-domain receptors to downstream transcriptional control, likely predating the cnidarian–bilaterian split. | MyD88 homologs occur in sponges and cnidarians; cross-species interaction between cnidarian TLR TIR domains and human MYD88 demonstrates deep conservation of pathway architecture. | Williams & Gilmore 2022 (pqac-00000040); Brennan & Gilmore 2018 (pqac-00000042, pqac-00000043) |
