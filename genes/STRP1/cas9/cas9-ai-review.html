Gene Description

Cas9 (originally Csn1) is the single-protein effector of the type II-A CRISPR-Cas adaptive immune system of Streptococcus pyogenes. It is a 1368-residue, bilobed, magnesium-dependent nuclease that is catalytically inert on its own and becomes active only when loaded with a two-part guide: a mature CRISPR RNA (crRNA) base-paired to a trans-encoded tracrRNA. The loaded ribonucleoprotein interrogates double-stranded DNA for a 5'-NGG-3' protospacer adjacent motif (PAM); PAM recognition by the C-terminal PAM-interacting domain licenses local strand separation and invasion of the target strand by the crRNA spacer, and the resulting RNA:DNA heteroduplex positions the two nuclease centres on opposite strands. The HNH domain (catalytic His840) cleaves the crRNA-complementary target strand and the discontinuous RuvC-like domain (catalytic Asp10) cleaves the non-complementary strand three base pairs upstream of the PAM, generating an essentially blunt double-strand break that destroys the invading replicon; the non-complementary strand is additionally trimmed in the 3'-to-5' direction after the initial endonucleolytic cut. Because the sequence specificity resides in a dissociable RNA rather than in the polypeptide, one protein serves every spacer the array carries. Cas9 also acts at the two earlier stages of the pathway. It is required non-catalytically for tracrRNA-directed, RNase III-dependent maturation and accumulation of crRNA, acting as a molecular anchor that holds the tracrRNA:pre-crRNA duplex together. And it joins Cas1, Cas2 and Csn2 in the spacer-acquisition complex, where its PAM-binding motif selects which invader sequences are integrated into the CRISPR array, so that newly stored spacers correspond to targets the same enzyme will later be able to cut. In its native host the substrates are the genomes of infecting phages and other incoming mobile DNA, including plasmids and prophage-derived sequences. The enzyme is the target of the type II-A anti-CRISPR proteins AcrIIA2 and AcrIIA4, which block target DNA binding.

Proposed New Ontology Terms

guide RNA-directed nucleic acid target recognition activity

Definition: Binding to a nucleic acid target at a site whose sequence is specified by base-pairing with a separate, non-covalently bound guide RNA, such that altering the guide RNA sequence alters the target site that is recognised without any change to the polypeptide. The gene product supplies the guide-binding site and the machinery for unwinding the target and forming a guide:target heteroduplex; it does not itself encode the target sequence specificity.

Justification: Programmable, guide-dependent target recognition is the defining property of every CRISPR-Cas effector and the single most consequential thing about this protein, and GO cannot express it. A QuickGO search for "guide RNA binding", "crRNA", "CRISPR" and "RNA-guided" returns only process terms (GO:0099048, GO:0043571, GO:0098672 and two obsoletes) and generic binding terms. The nuclease chemistry is well covered (GO:0004520, GO:0004521, GO:0004530, GO:1990238), which is precisely why the gap matters: with only those terms, Cas9 is annotated indistinguishably from an unguided restriction endonuclease, and Cascade or Csy surveillance complexes are indistinguishable from generic double-stranded DNA binding proteins. The term is deliberately pitched above any one system so that it serves Cas9, Cas12a, Cas13a, Cascade/Csy subunits and Csm/Cmr complexes alike, with children for guide RNA-directed DNA endonuclease activity and guide RNA-directed RNA endonuclease activity layering the chemistry back on. Two things are deliberately excluded from the definition. PAM (or PFS) dependence, because it is protein-encoded and orthologue-specific - NGG for this enzyme, N4GAYW-class for Neisseria meningitidis Cas9, N4CC for Nme2Cas9, a protospacer flanking site for Cas13a - and so belongs to child terms or to the individual annotation, not to the general concept. And the fate of the target, because that is what the existing nuclease terms already say. The activity is separable from catalysis experimentally, not just conceptually: AcrIIC1 occupies the HNH domain and abolishes cleavage while leaving target binding intact, converting Cas9 into a recognition-only protein. One scope question is left for the ontology editors and is also raised in suggested_questions: as defined, the term would equally fit Argonaute-family slicers, which are guide-directed in exactly this sense. That seems right - the concept is specificity delegated to a dissociable nucleic-acid guide, and it is one concept - but it widens the term well beyond prokaryotic defence and is a decision GO should take explicitly.

Core Functions

crRNA:tracrRNA-programmed interrogation of double-stranded DNA and PAM-licensed recognition of a complementary protospacer. The protein supplies the guide-binding channel and the PAM-reading C-terminal domain; the sequence that is recognised is specified entirely by the spacer segment of the crRNA, so the same polypeptide serves every spacer the array holds. Recognition proceeds by PAM contact, local strand separation, and directional invasion of the target strand to form an RNA:DNA heteroduplex. This is a separable activity, not merely the first half of cleavage: binding the HNH domain with AcrIIC1 removes catalysis while leaving target binding intact.

Q: Is the 3'-to-5' trimming of the non-complementary strand a third activity of Cas9, or further RuvC action on the strand it has already nicked? No separate catalytic centre has been assigned, and the question decides whether GO:0008408 should remain on this protein at all.

Q: GO has no way to say that an inhibitor acts on one catalytic domain of a multidomain nuclease. Given that AcrIIC1 occludes HNH alone and that the HNH catalytic conformation activates RuvC, is domain-level occlusion a distinguishable mechanism GO should represent, or is the allosteric coupling enough to make protein-level inhibition the right granularity?

Q: Cas9's role in adaptation is PAM selection within a Cas1-Cas2-Csn2 complex. Should that be curated as sequence-specific DNA binding on Cas9, or as a has-input relationship in a GO-CAM of the acquisition complex?

Suggested Experiments

Experiment: Assay end-processing of a pre-nicked, PAM-adjacent duplex by wild-type Cas9, by the RuvC-dead D10A mutant and by the HNH-dead H840A mutant, reading products at single-nucleotide resolution. If trimming survives H840A but not D10A, it is RuvC chemistry and GO:0008408 is redundant with GO:0004520 on this protein.

Hypothesis: The 3'-to-5' trimming of the non-complementary strand is performed by the RuvC active site acting iteratively on the strand it has already cut, not by a distinct exonuclease centre.

Type: in vitro nuclease assay with catalytic point mutants

Experiment: Replace the S. pyogenes PI domain with that of an orthologue reading a different PAM in a strain whose interference guide is held constant, then sequence newly acquired spacers. The prediction is that the acquired PAM tracks the donor PI domain while cleavage of the pre-existing target is unchanged.

Hypothesis: Cas9's contribution to spacer acquisition is PAM reading by the PI domain and nothing else, so a PI-domain swap should retarget the memory without touching interference.

Type: domain-swap genetics with spacer-acquisition sequencing

Experiment: Challenge wild-type and in-frame cas9-deletion S. pyogenes with matched protospacer-bearing and protospacer-free plasmids delivered by conjugation and by transformation, alongside a phage challenge, and quantify interference for each delivery route in the same genetic background.

Hypothesis: Native S. pyogenes interference is directed at a substrate set wider than virus, including conjugative and transformable plasmid DNA, which would support GO:0099048 over GO:0051607 as the primary process term.

Type: comparative interference assay across delivery routes

Scope decision made at the outset

SpCas9 is the most heavily used genome-editing reagent in biology and the great
majority of the literature (and of the UniProt reference list — BIOTECHNOLOGY IN HUMAN CELLS, zebrafish, E. coli, PAM re-engineering) concerns that application.
None of it is a biological function of this gene. The review therefore confines
description and core_functions to what the protein does inside S. pyogenes:
the interference step of type II-A CRISPR-Cas immunity, plus its two further roles
in crRNA biogenesis and in spacer acquisition. Nothing requiring a heterologous host
is annotated.

2. The dual-nuclease architecture (HNH + RuvC) — one function or two?

The division of labour is unambiguous:

  • PMID:22745249
  • PMID:22745249

I considered modelling these as two core functions. Two lines of evidence argue
against splitting them, and for one core function plus prose:

  1. The two centres are allosterically coupled, not independent. In the
    meningococcal orthologue the catalytic HNH conformation is what switches RuvC
    on: PMID:31668930.
    A GO model with two independent DNA endonuclease activity nodes would assert
    something the mechanism denies.
  2. Occluding HNH alone abolishes cleavage outright rather than converting Cas9
    into a nickase: PMID:28844692,
    PMID:28844692.

That second result also cuts the other way and is the single most useful datum for
the module pair: AcrIIC1 separates recognition from cleavage, leaving DNA
binding intact while removing catalysis. Recognition and cleavage are therefore
genuinely two activities of this protein, even though the two nuclease domains are
one. Hence the review's two interference core functions: guide-directed recognition
(no GO term — see §5) and DNA endonuclease activity (GO:0004520).

GO has no vocabulary for "which domain of a multidomain nuclease", so the HNH/RuvC
distinction is recorded as prose in description and in the core-function
descriptions. It matters for the counterpart review (genes/NEIME/acrIIC1): the Acr's
target is a domain, not the protein, and the suppression module records that gap
explicitly.

3. GO:0008408 3'-5' exonuclease activity — adjudication

My prior was that this is a domain-based inference (HNH and RuvC are both
endonucleolytic and the canonical product is a blunt DSB), but that is wrong. It is a
direct observation in the cited paper:

  • PMID:22745249

UniProt stands behind it independently: the Q99ZW2 reference block carries
FUNCTION AS AN EXONUCLEASE against PMID:22745249, the FUNCTION comment states
"The target strand not complementary to crRNA is first cut endonucleolytically, then
trimmed 3'-5' exonucleolytically", and the entry carries the Exonuclease keyword.
The IDA is therefore correctly sourced and must not be removed.

It is nonetheless not a core function:

  • it acts on a product the enzyme has already cut, i.e. it is a secondary trimming
    step, not the activity that destroys the invader;
  • the decisive biological output is the near-blunt double-strand break
    ("Plasmid DNA cleavage produced blunt ends at a position three base pairs upstream of the PAM sequence");
  • no distinct exonuclease active site has been assigned — UniProt annotates exactly
    two catalytic residues (ACT_SITE 10, RuvC; ACT_SITE 840, HNH), so the trimming is
    most parsimoniously further RuvC action on the already-nicked non-target strand
    rather than a third activity;
  • it was observed in vitro on short duplexes (fig. S4B) and is not part of the
    cleavage signature used to define Cas9 chemistry anywhere since.

Action: KEEP_AS_NON_CORE. Not REMOVE (the evidence is real and curator-read),
not ACCEPT-as-core (it is a side reaction).

5. The ontology gap: no MF term for guide-RNA-directed recognition

Searched QuickGO for guide RNA binding, crRNA, CRISPR, RNA-guided. The only
CRISPR-related terms in GO are process terms (GO:0099048, GO:0043571, GO:0098672 and
two obsoletes). There is no molecular-function term for programmable,
guide-RNA-directed target recognition — the defining property of every Cas effector,
and the property that distinguishes Cas9 from any ordinary restriction endonuclease.
GO:0003676/GO:0003677/GO:0003690 describe the binding but say nothing about
where the specificity comes from.

The host module records this as an open ontology gap
(modules/crispr_cas_adaptive_immunity.yaml, knowledge_gaps[0]) and asserts no id.
This review goes one step further and proposes the term, since it would serve every
Cas effector (Cas9, Cas12a, Cas13a, Cascade, Csm/Cmr) rather than only this gene:

  • proposed_name: guide RNA-directed nucleic acid target recognition activity
  • proposed_parent: GO:0003676 nucleic acid binding
  • the asserted content is where the specificity lives: in a separate,
    non-covalently bound guide RNA, so that changing the guide changes the target with
    no change to the polypeptide.

Deliberately not in the definition: PAM dependence (protein-encoded, and specific
to Cas9/Cas12a; Cas13a has a PFS, Cascade a different motif) and the chemistry of what
happens next (covered by GO:0004520, GO:0004521, GO:0004530). Two children are
suggested in the justification rather than proposed here.

Scope caveat worth flagging to GO: as defined, the term would also fit Argonaute-family
slicers, which are guide-directed in exactly this sense. I think that is a feature —
the concept is "specificity by dissociable nucleic-acid guide" and it is one concept —
but it is a decision for the ontology editors, so it is raised in
suggested_questions rather than resolved here.

6. Other annotation calls

  • GO:0003676 nucleic acid binding (IEA, InterPro IPR036397 = RNase H-like
    superfamily). Root-level and uninformative. The DNA half of Cas9's nucleic-acid
    binding is already carried by the IDA GO:0003677 row (refined below), so this row
    is modified to the half that GOA is missing entirely: guide RNA binding
    (GO:0003723), which UniProt annotates experimentally from the ternary structures.
  • GO:0003677 DNA binding (IDA, PMID:22745249) → MODIFY to GO:0003690
    double-stranded DNA binding. The EMSA substrates were duplex DNA and plasmid, and
    PAM recognition requires the duplex: Cas9 does not recognise a PAM on single-stranded
    DNA.
  • GO:0004519 endonuclease activity (IEA) → MODIFY to GO:0004520 DNA endonuclease
    activity, the child already present with IDA. Too-general parent per the action enum.
  • GO:0004520 DNA endonuclease activity (IDA) → ACCEPT; core.
  • GO:0046872 metal ion binding (IEA, UniRule) → MODIFY to GO:0000287 magnesium
    ion binding. UniProt's COFACTOR block names only Mg(2+) (ChEBI:18420), cleavage
    requires magnesium PMID:22745249,
    and the orthologue structures place a Mg2+ ion in the HNH pocket
    PMID:31668930.
  • GO:0051607 defense response to virus (IEA, UniRule) → ACCEPT. Unlike the
    meningococcal orthologue, phage immunity is directly demonstrated for this locus:
    PMID:25707807,
    and the adaptation assays were phage-challenge assays with ϕNM4γ4 using the cloned
    S. pyogenes type II-A locus. The native substrate set is wider than virus
    (prophage-derived DNA and plasmids — PMID:21455174), which is why a NEW GO:0099048
    row is added rather than leaving GO:0051607 to carry the whole process.
  • NEW GO:0099048 CRISPR-cas system. The pathway term, absent from GOA for this
    protein. Covers the plasmid/prophage substrates that GO:0051607 does not, and sits
    under GO:0099046 clearance of foreign intracellular nucleic acids. Not an ancestor
    or descendant of GO:0051607 or GO:0043571 (checked: GO:0043571 is under
    GO:0090304/GO:0043570, a DNA-metabolism branch; GO:0099048 is under GO:0099046).

7. Things deliberately not annotated

  • Genome editing, base editing, dCas9 transcriptional control, PAM re-engineering,
    gene drives, diagnostics — all heterologous-host applications.
  • GO:0005515 protein binding for the Cas9–Cas1–Cas2–Csn2 association. The
    informative content is PAM selection, which is captured as sequence-specific DNA
    binding in the adaptation core function; a bare binding term would discard it.
  • Any involved_in for DNA repair or NHEJ. Those are the host responses to the
    break in editing applications, not Cas9's activity, and there is no bacterial
    counterpart in evidence.
  • Single-turnover vs multiple-turnover kinetics: PMID:22745249 reports multiple
    turnover, PMID:24476820 reports that Cas9:RNA stays bound to both cleaved ends and
    "acts as a single-turnover enzyme". Not an annotation-level question; left out.

  justification: >-
    Programmable, guide-dependent target recognition is the defining property of every
    CRISPR-Cas effector and the single most consequential thing about this protein, and
    GO cannot express it. A QuickGO search for "guide RNA binding", "crRNA", "CRISPR"
    and "RNA-guided" returns only process terms (GO:0099048, GO:0043571, GO:0098672 and
    two obsoletes) and generic binding terms. The nuclease chemistry is well covered
    (GO:0004520, GO:0004521, GO:0004530, GO:1990238), which is precisely why the gap

    indistinguishable from generic double-stranded DNA binding proteins. The term is
    deliberately pitched above any one system so that it serves Cas9, Cas12a, Cas13a,
