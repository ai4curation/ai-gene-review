Gene Description

FbpA is the periplasmic solute-binding protein of the FbpABC ABC importer of Neisseria gonorrhoeae and the major iron-regulated protein of the gonococcus. After TbpAB/LbpAB strip ferric iron from host transferrin or lactoferrin at the outer membrane, FbpA captures the released Fe(3+) in the periplasm and shuttles it to the FbpB permease for FbpC-powered, ATP-dependent transport into the cytosol. It is a structural and functional analogue of a single transferrin lobe: a bilobed protein that binds one ferric ion per molecule with high affinity through two tyrosines, one histidine and one glutamate together with an exchangeable synergistic anion, normally phosphate. The identity of that anion sets both the iron affinity and the reduction potential of the holo-protein, so anion exchange acts as a gate on iron release at the inner membrane. Beyond ferric iron, the exposed di-tyrosyl motif at the base of the open interdomain cleft nucleates multinuclear oxo-metal clusters of other hard, highly charged cations, including Hf(IV) and Cu(II), in vitro.

Suggested Questions for Experts

Q: Does the di-tyrosyl cluster-nucleation mode seen with Hf(IV), Zr(IV) and Cu(II) occur in a living gonococcus, and can FbpABC translocate anything that is not a mononuclear ferric ion into the cytosol?

Q: Which synergistic anion is actually loaded on FbpA in the gonococcal periplasm under iron limitation, and does the cell regulate that anion occupancy to control the rate of iron handover to FbpB?

Q: How does FbpA receive iron from the TonB-dependent outer-membrane receptors TbpA and LbpA, and is there a direct, transient FbpA-receptor contact or only free diffusion of released Fe(3+) in the periplasm?

Suggested Experiments

Experiment: Expose wild-type gonococci and a defined fbpA deletion (and a Tyr195/Tyr196 double mutant complemented in trans) to Hf(IV), Zr(IV) and Ti(IV) complexes, then fractionate periplasm and cytosol and quantify metal by ICP-MS. This is the missing test of whether the cluster binding seen on purified protein has any whole-cell counterpart, and whether FbpA and its di-tyrosyl motif are required for it.

Type: whole-cell metal uptake with inductively coupled plasma mass spectrometry

Experiment: Make Tyr195Phe, Tyr196Phe and the double mutant of mature FbpA and measure binding of Fe(3+) and of Hf(IV)/Zr(IV) cluster precursors separately, with structures where crystals form. Published mutant data address iron binding only, so necessity of these residues for cluster nucleation is currently asserted from the structure alone.

Type: site-directed mutagenesis with isothermal titration calorimetry and crystallography

Experiment: Reconstitute FbpB and FbpC into liposomes and supply holo-FbpA loaded with different synergistic anions, measuring ATP-dependent Fe(3+) translocation. This would test directly whether FbpA contributes to the complex-level transporter activity, and whether anion identity sets the handover rate as the chelator-competition kinetics predict.

Type: reconstituted transport assay in proteoliposomes

Non-ferric metals (the CMMMech angle)

The di-tyrosyl motif at the bottom of the open cleft nucleates polynuclear
oxo-metal clusters of hard, highly charged cations:

  • PMID:12598891
  • Cu(II) binds the same two tyrosines but as a dinuclear oxo-Cu2 centre:
    PMID:23884152

This is cell-free chemistry on purified protein. No whole-cell Hf (or Zr,
Ti) uptake, no FbpABC-dependent transport of those metals, and no mutagenesis
of Tyr195/Tyr196 for the non-ferric metals has been reported. Nothing here
supports a GO annotation beyond iron: GO has no hafnium- or zirconium-binding
term, and the only non-redundant grouping term (GO:0046914 transition metal
ion binding) is an ancestor of GO:0008199 ferric iron binding, so proposing
it would add nothing. The cluster chemistry is therefore recorded here and in
suggested_experiments, not as an annotation.

Notes on the CMMMech record (data/records/hafnium.yaml) — not edited

CMMMech is read-only for this work. Two things a future CMMMech curator may
want to check; neither is a GOA/UniProt/InterPro defect, so neither is recorded
as a source_defect:

  1. Resolution. The record's protein-example evidence says "a 1.8-A crystal
    structure of an oxo-bridged multinuclear Hf cluster". The paper's abstract
    says 1.7 Å and both UniProt's DR PDB line and the PDB entry give 1.65 Å
    for 1O7T. The 1.8 Å figure does not match any of the three.
  2. Residue numbering. Tyr195/Tyr196 is the mature-protein numbering used
    throughout the literature; readers going to UniProt Q50964 will find those
    residues at 217/218. Worth stating which sequence the numbers refer to.

The record's functional_role itself ("binds oxo-bridged polynuclear Hf(IV)
clusters (3-5 Hf atoms) within its interdomain metal-binding cleft in vitro")
is appropriately hedged and is supported by the cited structure; it does not
over-extrapolate to whole-cell hafnium handling, and the record's own evidence
notes say as much.
