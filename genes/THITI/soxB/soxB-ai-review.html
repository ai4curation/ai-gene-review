Gene Description

SoxB of the chemolithoautotrophic betaproteobacterium Thiobacillus thioparus, represented here by a 325-residue internal fragment translated from a soxB PCR amplicon (strain NZ). SoxB is the periplasmic thiosulfohydrolase of the Sox thiosulfate-oxidizing enzyme system. It is a dimanganese enzyme of the 5'-nucleotidase/metallophosphatase fold. It hydrolyses the terminal sulfone (sulfonate) group from the SoxY carrier-protein cysteine (S-sulfosulfanyl- or S-sulfodisulfanyl-cysteinyl-SoxY), releasing sulfate and regenerating the sulfane-bound or free carrier for the next round of thiosulfate oxidation (EC 3.1.6.20). The fragment covers about residues 150-470 of full-length SoxB. It is 50% identical to Paracoccus pantotrophus SoxB and keeps the four histidine Mn ligands that fall within it. Its function is inferred by homology: no biochemistry has been reported for the T. thioparus protein. T. thioparus carries soxXYZAB but lacks soxCD.

Proposed New Ontology Terms

S-sulfosulfanyl-L-cysteine sulfohydrolase activity

Definition: Catalysis of the reactions: S-sulfosulfanyl-L-cysteinyl-[SoxY protein] + H2O = S-sulfanyl-L-cysteinyl-[SoxY protein] + sulfate + H+, and S-(2-sulfodisulfanyl)-L-cysteinyl-[SoxY protein] + H2O = S-disulfanyl-L-cysteinyl-[SoxY protein] + sulfate + H+.

Justification: This is the SoxB reaction of the bacterial Sox thiosulfate-oxidation pathway (ENZYME EC 3.1.6.20). GO has no corresponding molecular function term. The only child of GO:0016828 is trithionate hydrolase activity. As a result, SoxB proteins, including the structurally characterised T. thermophilus SoxB, carry only generic InterPro2GO hydrolase rows plus a mis-propagated nucleotide catabolic process.

Parent term: hydrolase activity, acting on sulfur-sulfur bonds

soxB (U5TVQ7, Thiobacillus thioparus) review notes

Entry identity

  • UniProtKB U5TVQ7 (U5TVQ7_THITI) is an unreviewed TrEMBL entry, 325 aa, flagged
    Fragment with NON_TER at both ends. It comes from a soxB PCR amplicon of
    T. thioparus strain NZ (EMBL KC542803), deposited by a soil-microcosm study that
    designed soxB primers for nonfilamentous chemolithotrophic sulfur oxidisers
    PMID:24646185. That paper is an ecology study.
    It contains no biochemistry on this protein.
  • The UniProt submitter name "Sulfate thioesterase/sulfate thiohydrolase" is taken
    from the EMBL record (ECO:0000313), and so is the gene name. PANTHER places the
    fragment in PTHR11575:SF42 "SULFUR OXIDATION PROTEIN SOXB". This placement and the
    homology analysis below agree that it is a genuine SoxB.
  • No deep-research provider output exists (no API keys). These notes are manual
    literature research.

Bioinformatics (file:THITI/soxB/soxB-bioinformatics/RESULTS.md)

  • The fragment aligns to residues ~153-470 of Paracoccus pantotrophus SoxB
    (A0AAE6TS95; 50.3% identity) and ~148-465 of Thermus thermophilus HB27 SoxB
    (Q72IT0; 38.9% identity), whose crystal structures define the Mn site.
  • Of the seven PDB-derived Mn(2+) ligands of Q72IT0, the four that fall inside the
    fragment are conserved histidines (U5TVQ7 H27, H131, H154, H156). D47, H49 and D143
    lie upstream of the fragment and cannot be assessed.
  • The N-terminal Tat signal peptide is outside the fragment. Periplasmic location
    therefore rests on homology, not on this sequence.

SoxB biology (homologs)

  • SoxB is the Sox component that hydrolyses sulfate off the SoxY carrier cysteine.
    It has a dimanganese site PMID:19535341. The T. thermophilus structures include complexes with the
    substrate analogue thiosulfate and with product sulfate PMID:19535341.
  • The reaction is ENZYME EC 3.1.6.20, S-sulfosulfanyl-L-cysteine sulfohydrolase
    (S-sulfosulfanyl-L-cysteinyl-[SoxY protein] + H2O = S-sulfanyl-L-cysteinyl-[SoxY
    protein] + sulfate + H+; ExPASy ENZYME, checked 2026-10-28). The bond cleaved is
    the S-S bond between the sulfane and sulfone sulfur atoms. GO has no term for this
    reaction. The closest existing parent is GO:0016828 "hydrolase activity, acting on
    sulfur-sulfur bonds".
  • In P. pantotrophus, SoxB is required in the reconstituted system PMID:10940005. It has a Tat signal peptide PMID:10940005. It is homologous to 5'-nucleotidase PMID:10940005.
  • In Allochromatium vinosum, genetics showed SoxB is required in vivo PMID:16995898. Meyer et al. describe SoxB
    as a component of the periplasmic complex and found soxB in Thiobacillus
    PMID:17991026.
  • T. thioparus DSM 505T (= ATCC 8158T) has soxXYZAB but no soxCD PMID:28127420. The authors assign
    SoxB its canonical role PMID:28127420. Without SoxCD, the sulfane sulfur
    is not oxidised on SoxY, as in A. vinosum.

Annotation decisions (summary)

  • GO:0016787 hydrolase activity (InterPro2GO): correct but general. MODIFY to
    GO:0016828 hydrolase activity, acting on sulfur-sulfur bonds. A specific term
    (EC 3.1.6.20) is listed under proposed_new_terms.
  • GO:0009166 nucleotide catabolic process (InterPro2GO from IPR006179/IPR036907):
    REMOVE. The mapping is a family-level over-propagation from the 5'-nucleotidase
    members. SoxB's substrate is the SoxY-bound sulfonate, and no nucleotidase activity
    has been reported for any SoxB. Recorded as an InterPro2GO source defect (CHECK).
  • GO:0030288 outer membrane-bounded periplasmic space (TreeGrafter): ACCEPT. SoxB is a
    Tat-exported periplasmic Sox component in a Gram-negative bacterium. The PAINT IBD
    node PTN008520763 is seeded by E. coli UshA/CpdB, which are themselves periplasmic.
  • NEW GO:0030145 manganese ion binding (IC). Two lines of evidence: the dimanganese
    site of characterised SoxB, and conservation of all four Mn ligands covered by the
    fragment.
  • No NEW process term was added. GO:0019416 "thiosulfate oxidation" is defined as
    thiosulfate to tetrathionate, which is the Tsd/TQO reaction, not Sox. GO:0019414
    "aerobic respiration, using sulfur or sulfate as electron donor" has no annotations
    at all in QuickGO (checked 2026-10-28). Even the structurally characterised
    T. thermophilus SoxB (Q72IT0) carries only the same three IEA rows. This is raised
    as a suggested question rather than asserted.

CMMMech claim check (data/records/metacinnabar.yaml; not edited)

  • functional_role gives EC 3.12.1.1. That EC is trithionate hydrolase (GO:0047401),
    not the SoxB reaction. The correct EC is 3.1.6.20.
  • functional_role says SoxB catalyses "thiosulfate cleavage during sulfur-oxidizing
    mobilization of mercury from metacinnabar". This is stronger than the evidence:
  • The metacinnabar study (PMID:26157421) used T. thioparus ATCC 8158 and contains
    no mention of sox genes. The evidence block in CMMMech correctly says partial
    support.
  • U5TVQ7 is from a different strain (NZ, a soil isolate), and it is a fragment.
  • SoxB does not cleave free thiosulfate. It hydrolyses sulfate from the SoxY-bound
    S-thiosulfonate after SoxAX has attached thiosulfate to SoxY.
  • Any link from SoxB to HgS dissolution is an inference: thiosulfate/sulfide
    oxidation by the culture. It is not a measured SoxB function.
  • The node description "Periplasmic Sox-pathway thiosulfohydrolase driving
    chemolithoautotrophic sulfur/sulfide oxidation" is acceptable at the homology level.
    "sulfide oxidation" is not established for SoxB itself.

Interpretation

  • U5TVQ7 is an internal PCR-amplicon fragment (UniProt NON_TER at residues 1 and 325)
    covering roughly residues 150-470 of a ~565-575-residue SoxB. It lacks the N-terminal
    ~150 residues, which carry the twin-arginine signal peptide reported for
    P. pantotrophus SoxB (PMID:10940005), and the C-terminal ~100 residues.
  • The fragment is clearly SoxB-like (50% identical over 318 columns to P. pantotrophus
    SoxB, 39% to T. thermophilus SoxB), consistent with its PANTHER placement in
    PTHR11575:SF42 "SULFUR OXIDATION PROTEIN SOXB".
  • All four Mn(2+) ligands of the structural reference that fall inside the fragment
    are conserved histidines (U5TVQ7 H27, H131, H154, H156). The other three ligands
    (D47, H49, D143 in Q72IT0) are N-terminal to the fragment, so their conservation
    cannot be assessed from this sequence.
  • The sequence is therefore consistent with a dimanganese SoxB thiosulfohydrolase, but
    nothing here is experimental for the T. thioparus protein; the inference rests on
    homology to characterized SoxB proteins.
