# DdhC identity and assay scope

Audit 2026-11-06; primary PMID32631860 was recovered by normal publication fetch with full_text_available true. No manually rewritten publication cache is used.

The primary identifies WP_011506293. Native 694-residue WP_011506293.1 sequence equals the frozen UniProt Q1QYW1 sequence exactly (comparison provenance below). The assayed recombinant protein was N-terminal His6-tagged DdhC expressed in E. coli BL21(DE3), with the cloned insert sequenced to exclude PCR changes. Native genetics used ZW4-1, a spontaneous rifampin-resistant derivative of DSM3043, not an unrelated wild-type isolate.

Purified-enzyme dye-linked turnover was directly observed with both dimethylglycine and sarcosine. Apparent Km values were 8.61±1.43 and 14.85±1.64 mM; reported kcat values were0.312±0.026 and0.280±0.020 s−1. This supports dual-substrate catalytic capability. The report describes DMG-to-sarcosine/formaldehyde chemistry, but the sarcosine substrate kinetics do not independently resolve glycine product stoichiometry or establish a native two-step pathway carried entirely by DdhC. The mutants retain growth on sarcosine, consistent with additional enzymes.

NAD+ or NADP+ was required in the tested PMS/DCPIP system, with NAD+ preferred. The terminal electron acceptor was a dye (DCPIP or NBT), with PMS as mediator. The paper does not directly assay reduction of purified Csal_0991 or the ETF heterodimer, nor directly establish released NAD(P)H as terminal product. The bound-NAD(P) relay and DdhC→ferredoxin→ETF arrangement are the authors' Fig8 model, supported by genetics and domain information. Do not turn that model into a measured native electron-transfer reaction.

The PMS/NBT recipe omits added NAD(P)+ although the results describe dependence in PMS/DCPIP. This methods distinction should be retained rather than silently correcting the recipe or treating both assays as identical.

The FAD assignment derives from spectral/cofactor analysis; the primary explicitly discusses the absence of FMN despite the Old Yellow Enzyme-like fold. An inferred FMN site is therefore unsupported for this target, without extrapolating a target-negative conclusion to every family member.
