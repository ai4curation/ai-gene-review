AcrIIC1 (NEIME, A0A2D0TCG3): can the disulphide switch operate in the native cytoplasm?

Hypothesis slug: kgap-acriic1-native-redox Source: genes/NEIME/acrIIC1/acrIIC1-ai-review.yaml, free-text selector Target: Neisseria meningitidis (NCBITaxon:487), gene acrIIC1, UniProt A0A2D0TCG3

Summary

The seed hypothesis asks for two numbers to be placed side by side: the potential at which the AcrIIC1 Cys17-Cys80 pair switches, and the redox potential the bacterial cytoplasm actually holds. Only one of those numbers exists. Programmatic text-mining of the complete full text of the primary characterisation, Zhao et al. 2022 (PMID: 36400778, PMC9674691), returns zero occurrences of "midpoint", "glutathione", "GSH", "GSSG" and "diamide", and only two occurrences of "mV" in the entire article. No standard redox potential (E°′) and no midpoint was ever determined for this cysteine pair. The switch was driven with 1 mM DTT for reduction and a 5-fold molar excess of H₂O₂ for oxidation, with the in-cell assay using 1 mM exogenous H₂O₂ in E. coli. So the protein side of the comparison is thermodynamically undefined, and any report that quotes a switch potential for AcrIIC1 is quoting a number that does not exist.

The cytoplasmic side does have a primary measurement, though not for this organism. No redox potential has ever been measured in any compartment of N. meningitidis. The nearest comparable Gram-negative value comes from Knoke, Muskietorz, Kühn and Leichert 2025 (PMID: 39689618, PMC11719327), who expressed the glutathione-specific ratiometric probe Grx1-roGFP2 in the E. coli cytoplasm, measured an oxidation degree of 0.068 ± 0.016, and computed E_glutathione = −314.4 mV (99% confidence interval −313.8 to −315.1 mV). Zhao et al. compared their buffers to "normal cytoplasmic values (−260 to −200 mV)", a range they cite to two eukaryotic redox-compartmentation reviews (Go & Jones 2008, Biochim Biophys Acta 1780:1273-1290; Jones & Go 2010, Diabetes Obes Metab 12:116-125). The measured bacterial cytoplasm sits about 55 mV below the most reducing edge of that borrowed window.

The direction of the discrepancy settles the biology even though the formal comparison cannot be completed. Taking −314.4 mV as the ambient potential, a two-electron thiol-disulphide equilibrium requires a Cys17-Cys80 midpoint of −342.6 mV to hold even 10% of AcrIIC1 in the disulphide form, and −314.4 mV to hold half. E. coli thioredoxin 1, among the most reducing protein dithiol couples known, sits near −270 mV. The disulphide is interchain, so forming it also pays a concentration-dependent dimerisation entropy penalty that the calculation does not include, making these curves an upper bound on oxidation. Three further arms of the analysis point the same way: the two cysteines are retained in only 11 of 96 non-identical AcrIIC1 homologs and have been lost inside the genus (Neisseria leonii WP_338691710.1), the structurally characterised AcrIIC1Vei of Verminephrobacter eiseniae (PDB 9J2G) has no cysteine at all yet inhibits type II-C Cas9, and AcrIIC1 carries no export signal, so the oxidising neisserial periplasm is never available to it. The switch is predicted not to operate natively in N. meningitidis.

Key findings

F1. The switch potential was never measured

The full text of Zhao et al. 2022 (A redox switch regulates the assembly and anti-CRISPR activity of AcrIIC1, Nat Commun 13:7032, PMID: 36400778, DOI 10.1038/s41467-022-34551-8) was retrieved from Europe PMC as PMC9674691 and searched programmatically. The counts are: "midpoint" 0 matches, "GSH" 0, "GSSG" 0, "glutathione" 0, "diamide" 0, and 2 occurrences of "mV" in the whole article. No E°′ and no midpoint potential appears anywhere for the Cys17-Cys80 pair.

The redox transition was produced with non-physiological reagents throughout: 1 mM DTT for reduction, a 5-fold molar excess of H₂O₂ over Acr for oxidation, plus air oxidation. The in-cell E. coli antimicrobial-susceptibility assay used 1 mM exogenous H₂O₂. The paper's single quantitative statement about physiology is:

"It seems that normal cytoplasmic values (−260 to −200 mV) fall within the range of our reducing buffers (−360 to −200 mV), indicating most of the AcrIIC1 molecules should be at their reduced state under normal physiological conditions."

The cited sources for that −260 to −200 mV range are reference 32 (Go YM, Jones DP, Biochim Biophys Acta 2008;1780:1273-1290, Redox compartmentalization in eukaryotic cells) and reference 33 (Jones DP, Go YM, Diabetes Obes Metab 2010;12:116-125). Both are review articles about eukaryotic cell compartments. Neither is a primary measurement and neither is bacterial. Reference 34, used for the oxidative-stress argument, is Go & Jones, Circulation 2005;111:2973-2980, a mammalian cell-adhesion study.

What the paper does supply is a buffer bracket rather than a midpoint. AcrIIC1 was reduced across buffers spanning −360 to −200 mV (Supplementary Table 4), which bounds the behaviour from above (the pair does not oxidise above about −200 mV in those buffers) without locating a set point. The authors' own conclusion from the comparison they drew is that AcrIIC1 should be mostly reduced, meaning active, under normal physiological conditions.

F2. The cytoplasmic potential was measured, at −314.4 mV

No redox potential has ever been reported for the N. meningitidis cytoplasm, periplasm or any other compartment, so the hypothesis's fallback clause applies and a comparable Gram-negative value was retrieved. Knoke, Muskietorz, Kühn and Leichert 2025 (PMID: 39689618, PMC11719327) used the glutathione-specific ratiometric probe Grx1-roGFP2 expressed in the E. coli cytoplasm, recording 405/488 nm excitation ratios at 525 nm emission and normalising to fully oxidised (AT-2) and fully reduced (DTT) cells. The abstract states the method directly:

"Here we use the genetically-encoded, glutathione-measuring redox probe Grx1-roGFP2 to comprehensively elucidate the import of extracellular glutathione into the cytoplasm of the model organism Escherichia coli."

Full-text mining of the article (5 sentences contain "mV") returned the values verbatim:

"In the cytoplasm, Grx1-roGFP2 was virtually completely reduced (OxD of 0.068 (±0.016), Fig. 1 C)"

"resulting in E Grx1-roGFP2 = E glutathione = −314.4 mV (99 % confidence interval: −313.8 mV to −315.1 mV)"

The declared constants are E′₀(Grx1-roGFP2) = −280 mV, z = 2, T = 298.15 K, with an assumed total cytosolic glutathione concentration of 5 mM.

For contrast, the same probe family in the E. coli periplasm gives −228 mV in wild type and −243 mV in a ΔdsbA strain (PMID: 37413765), a compartment gap of 70-90 mV. The neisserial periplasmic oxidases DsbA1/2/3 have E°′ = −80 mV (PMID: 19631659), and the thioredoxin-class couples of M. tuberculosis TrxB and TrxC sit at −262 and −269 mV (PMID: 18723612).

F3. The midpoint would have to be more reducing than thioredoxin

Treating the thiol-disulphide exchange as a two-electron equilibrium (E_protein = E_ambient at equilibrium, z = 2, T = 298.15 K, R = 8.3143 J mol⁻¹ K⁻¹, F = 96485.3321 C mol⁻¹), the midpoint the Cys17-Cys80 couple would need in order to hold a given fraction of AcrIIC1 as the disulphide was computed against three candidate ambient potentials.

Calibration against measured protein couples puts those requirements in context. E. coli thioredoxin 1 sits near −270 mV; E. coli glutaredoxins 1 and 3 at −233 and −198 mV (PMID: 9388218); M. tuberculosis TrxB and TrxC at −262 and −269 mV (PMID: 18723612); E. coli DsbA near −122 mV; neisserial DsbA1/2/3 at −80 mV (PMID: 19631659). No characterised protein dithiol-disulphide couple sits anywhere near −342 mV.

The calculation is an upper bound on oxidation for a second reason. Cys17-Cys80 is an interchain bond, annotated in UniProt as C17 of one chain to C80 of the other and the reciprocal pair. Forming it therefore also pays the entropic cost of dimerisation, which is concentration dependent and absent from this intramolecular equilibrium. The true disulphide fraction at any given midpoint would be lower than these curves show.

F4. The cysteine pair is not conserved and has been lost inside the genus

An NCBI Entrez protein query for AcrIIC1[All Fields] returned 198 records, 109 unique sequences, of which 97 fell in the length range 50-260 aa. Each was aligned to the UniProt canonical A0A2D0TCG3 sequence (86 aa, cysteines at 17, 20, 39 and 80) by Needleman-Wunsch global alignment with BLOSUM62 and affine gaps (open −11, extend −1), hand-implemented because Biopython is not importable in the executor used.

Both Cys17 and Cys80 are retained in 11 of 96 non-identical homologs. 50 of 97 contain fewer than two cysteines in the entire protein, a count that is independent of alignment and cannot be blamed on column-placement error.

The decisive cases are the ones with structures. AcrIIC1Vei from Verminephrobacter eiseniae (PDB 9J2G chain B) has no cysteine anywhere in the crystallised chain and is a characterised type II-C Cas9 inhibitor, from the structural study of loop-dependent inhibition by AcrIIC1 proteins (PMID: 43020790), which reports that "transplantation of this functional loop into the weakly active AcrIIC1Nme1 efficiently elevates its inhibitory performance" and that the work "identif[ies] a loop-mediated regulatory strategy that potentiates the canonical HNH occlusion mechanism, clarify[ing] the structural basis for functional divergence across AcrIIC1 homologs". Functional divergence across the family tracks loop architecture, not the cysteine pair.

Within Neisseria itself, only two AcrIIC1-annotated proteins exist outside the near-identical group, and one of them, N. leonii WP_338691710.1, has alanine at position 17 and a single cysteine in its entire 61-residue sequence. The pair is not fixed in the genus.

The honest limit on this analysis: below about 40% identity, global pairwise alignment places individual columns unreliably, so the position-specific calls in that band are weak evidence. The per-protein cysteine counts are unaffected by that concern, and the two highest-identity losses (Campylobacter sp. at 59.5%, N. leonii at 55.0%) are in the reliable band.

Zhao et al.'s own phylogenetic statement is concordant but narrower: Cys17 and Cys80 present across three related orthologs out of 11 surveyed.

F5. The organism has the reductive machinery, but lacks glutathione reductase

Two resources were queried independently. UniProt proteome UP000000425 (N. meningitidis serogroup B MC58) contains thioredoxin reductase TrxB Q9JZ28 (EC 1.8.1.9, 316 aa, CATC motif at 134); four thioredoxins with canonical CXXC active sites, Q9JYY9 (CGPC at 34), Q9JXN4 (CPGC at 60), Q9JXX7 (CGVC at 71), Q9K067 (CSLC at 11); two glutaredoxins, Q9JY15 (CPYC at 12) and Q7DD88 (CPFC at 9); and complete glutathione biosynthesis, gshA Q9JZI4 and gshB Q9JYJ3.

Glutathione reductase is absent. UniProt returns 0 hits for (ec:1.8.1.7) AND (proteome:UP000000425), 0 for (ec:1.8.1.7) AND (taxonomy_id:487), and 0 for (gene:gor) AND (taxonomy_id:487). KEGG orthology agrees: K00383 (glutathione reductase) is present in E. coli K-12 as eco:b3500 but has no ortholog in either nme or ngo, while K00384 (trxB, nme:NMB1324), K03671 (trxA, nme:NMB1366), K03676 (glutaredoxin, nme:NMB1790), K01919 (gshA, nme:NMB1037) and K01920 (gshB, nme:NMB1559) are all present.

The system is functional in vivo. In N. gonorrhoeae, "analysis of a trxB mutant of N. gonorrhoeae showed that it was more sensitive to disulfide stress and to stress induced by organic hydroperoxides, superoxide, and nitric oxide than wild-type gonococcus" (PMID: 19032106), and TrxB is required for microaerobic induction of aniA and norB and for survival inside human cervical epithelial cells.

The periplasm is a different world. The three neisserial DsbA enzymes are described with the measurement: "With a redox potential of −80 mV, the neisserial DsbAs are the most oxidizing thioredoxin-like enzymes known to date" (PMID: 19631659). DsbD, which carries cytoplasmic thioredoxin-derived reducing equivalents into the periplasm, is essential: "previous studies have shown that DsbD is required for the survival of the obligate human pathogen Neisseria meningitidis" (PMID: 29372905), which implies a reducing cytoplasmic pool as its source.

Nothing about the microaerobic host niche or the periplasmic oxidative load gives a reason to expect an oxidising cytoplasm. Those pressures act on the periplasm and on TrxB-dependent defences. The one genuine difference from E. coli works against using the proxy number uncritically: with no canonical Gor route to recycle GSSG, the GSH/GSSG couple is unlikely to be the dominant reductant in N. meningitidis, and the thioredoxin pathway (TrxB plus four thioredoxins) takes that role.

F6. No export signal, so the cytoplasmic potential is the only one that applies

UniProt A0A2D0TCG3 (86 aa) carries no Signal, Transit peptide, Lipidation or Transmembrane feature. The only sequence features annotated are the two interchain disulphide entries, C17 with C-80 and C80 with C-17. Sequence-level checks computed here found no lipobox matching [LVIFA][ASTVILG][GAS]C anywhere in residues 1-40, and no Tat twin-arginine motif RR-x-ΦΦ in residues 1-30.

Positive controls from the same organism show the contrast. N. meningitidis DsbA-1 Q9K189 has an annotated Signal 1-18 with N-palmitoyl and S-diacylglycerol cysteine at position 19 and a clear lipobox LAAC. The exported MsrA/MsrB protein PilB Q9K1N8 has lipobox LGAC.

AcrIIC1's most hydrophobic Kyte-Doolittle window (window 9, peak +2.21 centred at residue 20) falls on residues 16-24, CGLCLAAIS. That is the strand carrying Cys17 and Cys20 inside the folded beta-tulip core, not an N-terminal h-region preceding a cleavage site. The region is resolved as ordered structure in six crystal and NMR structures that all model the full 1-86 chain (PDB 5VGB, 7X31, 7X4B, 8IF0, 10VB, 10VC).

Dedicated signal-peptide prediction could not be run. The EBI Phobius REST service returned HTTP 400 "You have been temporarily blocked" and a subsequent call timed out, so no SignalP or Phobius output is reported anywhere in this report. The motif and feature checks are direct results; the absence call is inference from them.

Functionally, the target is cytoplasmic. "AcrIIC1 is a broad-spectrum Cas9 inhibitor that prevents DNA cutting by multiple divergent Cas9 orthologs through direct binding to the conserved HNH catalytic domain of Cas9" (PMID: 28844692), and the structural work confirms that "the structure shows that AcrIIC1 binds to the catalytic sites of the HNH domain, preventing it from accessing the DNA target" (PMID: 36878037). AcrIIC1 is encoded on phages and other mobile genetic elements whose proteins are translated in the cytoplasm.

There is no route by which AcrIIC1 meets the periplasmic potential, where DsbA at −80 mV would oxidise an exposed cysteine pair essentially quantitatively.

Mechanistic model

The immediate molecular function being tested is occlusion of the HNH catalytic domain of type II-C Cas9 by a monomeric 86-residue beta-tulip protein, which blocks DNA cleavage. The disulphide sits one step away from that: it is an interchain Cys17-Cys80 bond that cross-links two AcrIIC1 chains into a dimer whose Cas9-binding surfaces are unavailable. Its formation is a chemical property of the protein under a given redox condition, not an activity the protein performs.

Evidence base

The primary characterisation is Zhao et al. 2022 (PMID: 36400778), which reports "the discovery of a redox switch for NmeAcrIIC1, which regulates NmeAcrIIC1's monomer-dimer interconversion and inhibitory activity on Cas9" and that "a pair of conserved cysteines mediates the formation of inactive NmeAcrIIC1 dimer and directs the redox cycle". This is the paper whose full text contains no midpoint potential. It supports the existence of the disulphide and the identity of the cysteine pair; it does not support any statement about the switch point.

The core-function literature establishes what AcrIIC1 does and where. PMID: 28844692 defines the broad-spectrum inhibition through direct binding to the conserved HNH catalytic domain, and PMID: 36878037 confirms structurally that AcrIIC1 binds the catalytic sites of HNH. Both anchor the target as a cytoplasmic enzyme, which fixes the compartment in which the redox question must be asked.

The decisive challenge to a functional role for the cysteines comes from PMID: 43020790, which characterises divergent AcrIIC1 homologs structurally and functionally, including AcrIIC1Vei (PDB 9J2G, zero cysteines) and AcrIIC1Boe (PDB 9JB6). That work identifies "a loop-mediated regulatory strategy that potentiates the canonical HNH occlusion mechanism" and clarifies "the structural basis for functional divergence across AcrIIC1 homologs". Functional variation across the family is attributed to loop architecture. A cysteine-free member that still inhibits Cas9 removes the disulphide from the core mechanism.

The redox-measurement literature supplies the only quantitative anchor on the cytoplasmic side. PMID: 39689618 gives the method and the compartment in its abstract and the number in its full text. PMID: 37413765 gives the periplasmic comparison in the same organism and probe family, establishing how large a compartment effect can be. PMID: 9388218 and PMID: 18723612 supply measured standard potentials for glutaredoxin and thioredoxin classes, which bound how reducing a protein dithiol couple gets and therefore make the −342.6 mV requirement interpretable.

The organism-specific redox literature covers the machinery arm. PMID: 19032106 shows the neisserial thioredoxin reductase is functional in vivo and defends against disulfide stress. PMID: 19631659 gives the −80 mV periplasmic DsbA measurement in N. meningitidis itself. PMID: 29372905 shows DsbD is required for survival, implying a reducing cytoplasmic pool as the electron source. None of these gives a cytoplasmic potential for the organism, which is the gap that forces the E. coli proxy.

Conflicts and alternatives

The original authors already reached the same conclusion. Zhao et al. write that most AcrIIC1 molecules "should be at their reduced state under normal physiological conditions". The seed hypothesis and this report arrive at the same place with a measured number in place of a borrowed range. The conflict is with any secondary reading of that paper that treats the disulphide as a native regulator.

Conservation runs the wrong way. The standard inference, that conservation implies function, cannot be used here. A feature present in 11 of 96 homologs and lost inside the same genus is the profile of a dispensable feature.

Compartment confusion is the most plausible route to a contrary reading. N. meningitidis genuinely holds a strongly oxidising thiol environment, with DsbA paralogues at −80 mV and an essential DsbD. An exposed cysteine pair would be oxidised there. All of that is periplasmic, and AcrIIC1 never arrives.

Species transfer is the weakest joint. The −314.4 mV figure is E. coli. N. meningitidis has gshA and gshB but no detectable glutathione reductase by either KEGG orthology or UniProt EC search, so its GSH/GSSG couple is not recycled by the canonical Gor route and may sit differently. The thioredoxin system is intact and functional, and thioredoxin couples near −270 mV are themselves more reducing than the window Zhao used. Nothing identified here pushes the neisserial cytoplasm oxidising enough to form the bond.

A transient oxidative excursion is a real untested alternative. The microaerobic niche and the host neutrophil oxidative burst are the obvious route by which cytoplasmic thiols could be transiently oxidised. No measurement of such an excursion exists for N. meningitidis, and in E. coli a 1 mM H₂O₂ bolus was needed to see the effect in cells. There is also direct evidence from E. coli arguing against protein thiols as primary targets of intracellular reactive oxygen species (PMID: 38152379), which reports that unnaturally high hydrogen peroxide concentrations were required to oxidise a cytoplasmic disulphide-dependent reporter while far lower concentrations poisoned metalloenzymes.

Database carry-over. The UniProt entry annotates the interchain disulphide as a sequence feature. A curator reading only that record could reasonably infer a constitutive native disulphide. The feature reflects the crystallographic and biochemical observation in Zhao et al., not an in vivo state.

The practical consequence is narrow. The review may record the disulphide dimer as a structural and in vitro observation. It should not state or imply that the disulphide regulates AcrIIC1 in the native host, and should not carry it into any term describing the inhibition mechanism.

Limitations and knowledge gaps

The midpoint potential of Cys17-Cys80 does not exist. Checked by exhaustive full-text mining of PMC9674691: no midpoint, no glutathione titration, no diamide series. This is the single number the hypothesis is built around. Without it, the comparison cannot be closed from the protein side, and everything stated here about native behaviour is a prediction.

No redox potential has ever been measured in N. meningitidis. Checked by PubMed and Europe PMC searches across compartments. The whole comparison rests on an E. coli proxy, and the transfer is inference.

The proxy reports a glutathione potential in an organism that lacks glutathione reductase. E_GSH equals the potential felt by a protein couple only if that couple equilibrates with glutathione. N. meningitidis makes GSH but has no canonical Gor route to recycle GSSG, so the operative couple there is probably thioredoxin-linked. This does not move the estimate oxidising, but it means the number is the wrong yardstick in a way that has not been quantified.

Zhao et al.'s Supplementary Table 4 was not retrieved. The measured potentials of their reducing buffers are reported there. PMC supplementary file access was not completed. This matters only for checking whether the buffer series brackets a midpoint that could be back-fitted.

Proposed follow-up experiments and actions

1. Redox titration of purified AcrIIC1. Equilibrate in defined GSH/GSSG buffers spanning −360 to −180 mV, quench with AMS or mPEG-maleimide, quantify monomer versus cross-linked dimer by non-reducing SDS-PAGE, and fit for E°′. This is the one experiment that produces the missing number. Prediction from this report: a midpoint well above −300 mV, meaning no significant disulphide at cytoplasmic potentials.

2. Thiol-trapping of native AcrIIC1 in N. meningitidis. Grow a strain carrying its native acrIIC1 or an epitope-tagged allele, quench cultures directly into trichloroacetic acid, alkylate with AMS, and blot. Compare untreated, H₂O₂-challenged and neutrophil-exposed cells. This answers directly whether the bond ever forms in vivo.

3. Cysteine-free complementation. Build C17S/C80S AcrIIC1 and test Cas9 inhibition in a plasmid-protection or transformation assay in N. meningitidis. If the double mutant inhibits as well as wild type under all conditions, the switch is dispensable in the host, matching what AcrIIC1Vei already shows across species.

4. Biosensor calibration in N. meningitidis. Express Grx1-roGFP2 and a thioredoxin-coupled probe in the same strain under aerobic, microaerobic and intracellular conditions.
