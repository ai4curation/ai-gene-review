Gene Description

AcrIIC1 is an 86-residue protein encoded by a prophage resident in the Neisseria meningitidis genome that disables the host type II-C CRISPR-Cas9 system, and it does so by a route distinct from the common anti-CRISPR strategy of blocking target recognition. Rather than preventing Cas9 from finding DNA, AcrIIC1 binds the active-site interface of the HNH catalytic domain through ionic and hydrogen-bonding contacts and leaves guide loading and target DNA binding entirely intact. Because HNH activation is the prerequisite for activating the second nuclease domain, RuvC, occluding HNH abolishes cleavage of both DNA strands, and the result is a Cas9 molecule trapped on its target in a catalytically dead state, functionally equivalent to the dead-Cas9 variants made by active-site mutation. The occluded surface is the most conserved region of Cas9, which is why inhibition generalises robustly across Cas9 orthologues from different species rather than being confined to the enzyme of the resident host. The protein interconverts between an active monomer and an inactive disulphide-linked dimer through a pair of conserved cysteines, making its inhibitory activity sensitive to the redox state of its surroundings.

Suggested Questions for Experts

Q: Does an AcrIIC1-trapped Cas9, which stays bound to its target but cannot cut, repress transcription at that locus in Neisseria, as the AcrF3-inhibited type I-F system was shown to do in Pseudomonas? If so, the inhibitor does not merely neutralise immunity but repurposes it.

Suggested experts: Lucas B. Harrington, Jennifer A. Doudna, Erik J. Sontheimer

Q: Is there a GO mechanism, perhaps an annotation extension on GO:0140721 naming the occluded domain, that could record single-domain occlusion and the survival of the target's other activities without creating a molecular-function class about protein architecture?

Q: Does the AcrIIC1 redox switch ever actually flip inside Neisseria meningitidis, or is the inactive disulphide dimer an artefact of the oxidising conditions in which the switch was characterised?

Suggested experts: Yanli Wang

Suggested Experiments

Experiment: In Neisseria meningitidis, retarget NmeCas9 with a guide against a reporter promoter in strains with and without induced AcrIIC1, and in a cas9 deletion, then measure reporter transcript by RT-qPCR and Cas9 occupancy by ChIP. The prediction specific to this mechanism is repression with occupancy retained, in contrast to a DNA mimic such as AcrIIA4, which should abolish both. Running both inhibitors side by side makes the comparison a direct test of the mechanism assignment rather than of AcrIIC1 alone.

Hypothesis: An AcrIIC1-trapped Cas9 acts as a sequence-specific transcriptional repressor at its target locus in the native host.

Type: paired ChIP and transcript quantification with two inhibitor classes

Experiment: Lyse Neisseria meningitidis under strictly anaerobic conditions with immediate thiol trapping by N-ethylmaleimide, then resolve AcrIIC1 monomer and dimer by non-reducing SDS-PAGE and immunoblot across aerobic, microaerobic and oxidative-stress growth. A constant, near-complete monomer fraction would argue the switch does not operate natively and that the cysteines are conserved for another reason.

Hypothesis: The redox switch is physiological, so the native cytoplasmic pool of AcrIIC1 contains a measurable fraction of inactive disulphide dimer that shifts with growth conditions.

Type: redox-state trapping and non-reducing immunoblot

Experiment: Map the minimal AcrIIC1 region that retains HNH affinity by isothermal titration calorimetry against the purified NmeCas9 HNH domain, then test each fragment for inhibition of full-length NmeCas9 plasmid cleavage. Divergence between affinity for the isolated domain and inhibition of the intact enzyme would indicate a second contact outside HNH, which neither the chimera experiments nor the HNH-only crystal structure could have detected.

Hypothesis: AcrIIC1 binding to HNH is sufficient for inhibition, so an isolated HNH-binding fragment would inhibit full-length Cas9 as well as the intact protein does.

Type: fragment mapping with paired affinity and inhibition readouts

Knowledge Gaps

What is not known — curated, literature-grounded statements of the open unknowns (the inverse of core functions).

Gap: GO can state that AcrIIC1 inhibits a nuclease but cannot state that it occludes one catalytic domain of a multidomain nuclease while the enzyme's other activities, including DNA binding, survive. The diagnostic feature of the mechanism is exactly the part the ontology cannot hold.

OPEN ONTOLOGY

What is known: GO:0140721 nuclease inhibitor activity is asserted on this entry and is correct, and GO:0098672 fits the process exactly. What is lost is the distinction between this inhibitor and one that disables the whole enzyme, which is the distinction that predicts the trapped-DNA-bound-inactive phenotype.

Significance: The residual activity is not a detail. A Cas9 trapped on target but unable to cut is a sequence-specific DNA-binding module, with consequences for host transcription that a fully inhibited Cas9 would not have, and the equivalent was observed in vivo for the type I-F system inhibited by the Cas3-sequestering AcrF3. Flattening both to nuclease inhibitor activity makes two mechanistically and phenotypically different outcomes indistinguishable.

What would resolve it: No new class is proposed here, because a term for occlusion of one catalytic domain within a multidomain enzyme would describe protein architecture rather than an activity, which is not what GO molecular-function terms are for. The better request is probably an annotation extension on GO:0140721 naming the occluded domain, together with a way to record that the target retains its other activities. That is a question for ontology editors about mechanism rather than a new class request, and is raised as such in suggested_questions.

Provenance (the field's own admissions):

  • "The ability of AcrIIC1 to inhibit multiple Cas9 orthologs without preventing DNA binding suggested that it targets a conserved region of Cas9 involved in DNA cleavage." — PMID:28844692

Gap: Whether the AcrIIC1 redox switch operates in the native Neisseria meningitidis cytoplasm is undetermined; the switch was characterised with reference to eukaryotic cellular environments and gene-editing applications.

OPEN BIOLOGY

What is known: That a pair of conserved cysteines forms an inactive disulphide-linked dimer, and that the monomer is the active species, are established structurally and biochemically. What is unknown is whether the native intracellular redox potential ever moves across the switch point.

Significance: If the switch is physiological, AcrIIC1 is a conditional inhibitor and the prophage can withhold counter-defence under some conditions, which would be a regulatory layer distinct from the transcriptional Aca switch that governs other acr loci. If it is an artefact of the oxidising extracellular or eukaryotic environments used to study it, the native protein is simply constitutively active and the cysteines are doing something else.

What would resolve it: Measure the AcrIIC1 monomer-to-dimer ratio in Neisseria meningitidis lysates prepared under strictly anaerobic and thiol-trapping conditions, across growth states, and test whether cysteine-free variants alter prophage retention in a cas9-competent host.

Provenance (the field's own admissions):

  • "we report the discovery of a redox switch for NmeAcrIIC1, which regulates NmeAcrIIC1's monomer-dimer interconversion and inhibitory activity on Cas9." — PMID:36400778

Gap: The protein is unreviewed in UniProt and named only "Anti-CRISPR protein", taken from a PDB deposition rather than asserted by a curator, despite four deposited structures of this sequence and a mechanism published in Cell.

OPEN CURATION

What is known: The mechanism is not in doubt. What is missing is a curated record of it on the accession, including the FUNCTION line that would let the keyword-to-GO pipeline produce the process term rather than leaving GOA with a single CACAO row.

Significance: This is one of the two accessions named explicitly in the curation gap recorded in modules/anti_crispr_suppression.yaml, which is why that module grounds the family in InterPro rather than in the member record. A reviewer who follows the accession reaches an entry that does not say what the protein does.

What would resolve it: Nominate A0A2D0TCG3 for Swiss-Prot review with a FUNCTION line citing PMID:27984730, PMID:28844692 and PMID:36400778, and the keywords that would map to GO:0098672.

Molecular function: GO:0140721 is adequate and is asserted

The module deliberately asserts no GO id here, saying "No GO id asserted: the activity is
occlusion of a catalytic domain within a multidomain nuclease, leaving DNA binding intact,
and no GO molecular-function term captures that."

I disagree in part. GO:0140721 nuclease inhibitor activity is defined as "Binds to
and modulates the activity of a nuclease", and that is exactly and literally what AcrIIC1
does — it binds Cas9, a nuclease, at the active-site interface of one of its two nuclease
domains, and abolishes cleavage. The term is less specific than the mechanism (it does
not say that one domain of a multidomain nuclease is occluded while the other activities,
including DNA binding, survive), but it is not wrong, and GO curation practice is to
annotate the nearest correct existing term and request the specific one, rather than to
assert nothing. Asserting nothing leaves this protein with no molecular function at all,
which is strictly less informative.

So: GO:0140721 is asserted as the core molecular function, and no new term is proposed
for it in this review. The gap — that GO cannot express single-domain occlusion within a
multidomain enzyme, and therefore cannot distinguish AcrIIC1 from an inhibitor that
disables the whole enzyme — is recorded as an ONTOLOGY knowledge gap with the specific
observation that the diagnostic feature (DNA binding survives) is the part GO loses.

I deliberately do not propose a term for it. Writing a term for "occlusion of one
catalytic domain within a multidomain nuclease" would be a term about protein architecture
rather than about activity, and GO molecular-function terms are about what the gene
product does; the better request is probably an extension on the existing term naming the
occluded domain, which is a curation-mechanism question rather than a new class. Recorded
as a question for ontology editors.

I also do not assert GO:0043021 ribonucleoprotein complex binding here, unlike the
other reviews in this round. AcrIIC1 binds the isolated HNH domain in solution — that is
how the chimera and size-exclusion experiments were done, and the crystal structure 5VGB
is of the free HNH domain, not of a Cas9-guide complex. So guide dependence, which is the
basis for the GO:0043021 assertions on AcrIIA4, AcrIIA2 and AcrF8, is not established for
AcrIIC1 and is in fact contraindicated.

GOA row and qualifier adjudication

One row: acts_upstream_of_or_within_positive_effect GO:0098672, IDA, PMID:27984730,
assigned by CACAO. Same adjudication as for AcrIIA4: the term is correct and maximally
specific, the evidence is on this sequence, so ACCEPT. The qualifier is poorly chosen —
AcrIIC1 does not act upstream of the suppression, its binding to the Cas9 HNH domain is
the suppression, so involved_in is better supported; and the positive_effect component
reads as though the protein up-regulated something. Flagged in review.reason for CACAO

  gap_kind:

  status: OPEN
