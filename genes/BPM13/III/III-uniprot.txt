CC   -!- FUNCTION: Plays essential roles both in the penetration of the viral
CC       genome into the bacterial host via pilus retraction and in the
CC       extrusion process. During the initial step of infection, G3P mediates
CC       adsorption of the phage to its primary receptor, the tip of host F-
CC       pilus. Attachment of the phage causes pilus retraction bringing the
CC       viral particle into close proximity of the host cell inner membrane.
CC       Binding to the host pilus initiates a change in the G3P conformation,
CC       allowing subsequent interaction with the host entry receptors TolA,
CC       TolQ and TolR and penetration of the viral DNA into the host cytoplasm.
CC       In the extrusion process, G3P mediates the release of the membrane-
CC       anchored virion from the cell via its C-terminal domain.
CC       {ECO:0000250|UniProtKB:P03661}.
CC   -!- SUBUNIT: Homopentamer (PubMed:37669979). Interacts with G6P; this
CC       interaction allows integration of G3P and G6P into the virion pointy
CC       tip (PubMed:7746960). Interacts with capsid protein G8P; this
CC       interaction forms the G3P/G6P pointy tip of the virion (By similarity).
CC       Interacts with the tip of the host pilus (By similarity). Interacts
CC       (via N-terminus) with host TolA (PubMed:10404600). Interacts (via
CC       transmembrane domain) with host TolQ (via 2nd and 3rd transmembrane
CC       domains); this interaction allows the phage translocation across the
CC       host inner membrane. Interacts (via transmembrane domain) with host
CC       TolR (via transmembrane domain); this interaction allows the phage
CC       translocation across the host inner membrane (By similarity).
CC       {ECO:0000250|UniProtKB:P03661, ECO:0000250|UniProtKB:P69169,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:10404600, ECO:0000269|PubMed:37669979,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:7746960}.
CC   -!- SUBCELLULAR LOCATION: Virion {ECO:0000269|PubMed:37669979}. Host
CC       membrane; Single-pass type I membrane protein {ECO:0000305}. Note=Prior
CC       to assembly, G3P is found associated with the bacterial host inner
CC       membrane. There are about five copies of this protein per mature phage
CC       that are located on the head side of the filamentous virion.
CC       {ECO:0000269|PubMed:37669979}.
CC   -!- DOMAIN: Consists of 3 domains (N1/D1, N2/D2, and CT) separated by
CC       glycine-rich repeats and a C-terminal transmembrane segment. The N2
CC       domain interacts with the F-pilus, whereas the N1 domain forms a
CC       complex with the C-terminal domain of TolA at later stages of the
CC       infection process. The C-terminal domain is required for release of
CC       viral particles from the host bacterial membrane and proper integration
CC       of G3P and G6P proteins in the virion. {ECO:0000250|UniProtKB:P03661}.
CC   -!- PTM: In one of the crystallographic structures Trp-39 is oxidized to
CC       1',2'-dihydro-2'-oxotryptophan. {ECO:0000269|PubMed:9461080}.
CC   -!- SIMILARITY: Belongs to the inovirus G3P protein family. {ECO:0000305}.
CC   -!- CAUTION: The oxidation form of Trp-39 is subject of controversy and
CC       could be the artifactual result of sample handling.
CC       {ECO:0000305|PubMed:9461080}.
