Gene Description

TRI5 (FGSG_03537) encodes trichodiene synthase (EC 4.2.3.6), a soluble class I sesquiterpene cyclase of the wheat head blight fungus Fusarium graminearum (teleomorph Gibberella zeae). Using Mg2+ bound by its conserved NSE/DTE motif, the enzyme ionizes and cyclizes (2E,6E)-farnesyl diphosphate to trichodiene plus diphosphate, the first committed step that diverts isoprenoid flux into trichothecene biosynthesis. Recombinant enzyme produces mainly trichodiene with minor related cyclization products. Trichodiene is subsequently oxygenated by the cytochrome P450 TRI4 and further modified by other TRI cluster enzymes to give the type B trichothecene deoxynivalenol (DON) and its acetylated derivatives in strain PH-1 (or nivalenol in other chemotypes). TRI5 is a cytosolic protein enriched next to the ER-derived toxisome where downstream, membrane-anchored TRI enzymes reside; independent of its catalytic activity, TRI5 protein is required for normal toxisome morphology and for production of several non-trichothecene sesquiterpenes such as culmorin. TRI5 expression is induced in infection structures on wheat, is controlled by the cluster regulators TRI6 and TRI10 and by a cis-acting promoter-derived long non-coding RNA. Because TRI5 deletion abolishes trichothecene production, tri5 mutants are widely used to show that DON acts as a virulence factor: they cause local lesions on inoculated wheat spikelets but fail to spread through the rachis, whereas virulence on barley, maize and Arabidopsis floral tissue is largely unaffected. The host-side effect is exerted by DON itself, an inhibitor of eukaryotic ribosomes, rather than by the TRI5 protein.

Functional annotation report: Fusarium graminearum TRI5 (UniProt Q00909)

Identity and scope

The supplied Q00909 record identifies TRI5 as trichodiene synthase (EC 4.2.3.6) from Gibberella zeae/Fusarium graminearum strain PH-1. Independent work on PH-1 explicitly identifies its TRI5 locus as FGSG_03537 and tests the encoded protein by expression, deletion, and complementation. Its experimentally examined Mg²⁺-binding terpene-synthase motif is consistent with the isoprenoid-synthase/TRI5 family and domains specified in the supplied record. The examined papers do not independently print the Q00909 accession or every supplied domain identifier; those particular identifiers remain record-based rather than independently sequence-verified here. TRI5 should not be confused with TRI4, a downstream oxygenase, or with similarly named TRI5 orthologs studied in other Fusarium species. (flynn2019expressionofthe pages 6-9, flynn2019expressionofthe pages 4-6, flynn2019expressionofthe pages 9-11, koizumi2024arolein pages 1-2)

Primary molecular function and substrate specificity

Tri5 is a sesquiterpene cyclase that converts farnesyl diphosphate (FPP) to trichodiene, releasing diphosphate. This commits FPP from the fungal isoprenoid pool to the trichothecene pathway; Tri5 does not itself synthesize deoxynivalenol (DON). Recombinant PH-1 Tri5 produced predominantly trichodiene in Escherichia coli, and purified enzyme was assayed with FPP. Substituting N225D/S229T in its conserved Mg²⁺-coordinating NSE/DTE motif abolished FPP-dependent sesquiterpene production while retaining the predominantly α-helical secondary structure measured by circular dichroism. These experiments provide substantially stronger functional evidence than domain annotation alone. (flynn2019expressionofthe pages 1-4, flynn2019expressionofthe pages 4-6, flynn2019expressionofthe pages 9-11)

Product specificity is substantial but not absolute. Recombinant Tri5 also yielded minor sesquiterpenes, including premnaspirodiene. Conversely, six sesquiterpenes detected in induced fungal cultures were absent from the recombinant Tri5 profile; examples include longifolene, germacrene D, β-copaene, sativene, cyclosativene, and ar-curcumene. Their occurrence in the fungus is therefore not evidence that Tri5 directly makes them. In particular, longifolene and the precursors of culmorin and culmorone are associated with the separate longiborneol synthase Clm1. The supported direct substrate assignment is FPP; these experiments do not establish comparable activity or quantitative kinetic preferences for other prenyl-diphosphate substrates. (flynn2019expressionofthe pages 9-11, flynn2019expressionofthe pages 11-14)

Pathway, cellular location, and biological role

Tri5 performs the first cyclization in trichothecene biosynthesis. Subsequently, Tri4 oxygenates trichodiene toward isotrichotriol; further transformations, including a nonenzymatic cyclization and reactions involving other TRI enzymes, generate downstream trichothecenes. A 2024 analysis of pathway-blocked F. graminearum mutants independently reaffirmed this ordering and distinguished Tri5’s initial reaction from Tri4’s oxidation. The identity of the final toxin depends on the isolate’s biosynthetic background, so TRI5 function should not be equated exclusively with production of one DON chemotype. (koizumi2024arolein pages 1-2, maier2006involvementoftrichothecenes pages 1-2)

Tri5 functions in the fungal cytosol, enriched near ER-derived toxisomes—not as an established integral ER-membrane enzyme. These induced structures organize downstream, ER-associated pathway proteins such as Tri1, Tri4, and Tri11. In PH-1-derived strains carrying an ER marker, deletion of TRI5 prevented normal toxisome morphology; reintroducing either functional Tri5–GFP or the catalytically inactive, folded N225D/S229T variant restored that morphology. The inactive variant also restored several sesquiterpenes made by other pathways, including Clm1-derived culmorin and culmorone, without restoring trichodiene-derived products. Thus, Tri5 protein has an experimentally supported noncatalytic organizational effect in addition to its indispensable cyclase activity. A proposed physical association with adjacent ER membranes or their anchored enzymes remains a model, not a demonstrated binding mechanism. Supplying trichodiene to a Δtri5 strain permitted formation of the downstream intermediate isotrichodermin, further showing that downstream enzymes can retain activity without Tri5 or normal toxisome morphology. (flynn2019expressionofthe pages 9-11, flynn2019expressionofthe pages 11-14)

A January 2024 study refined this cellular context: overproducing Tri1 or Tri4, both ER-localized proteins, generated ER-derived toxisome-shaped structures even under non-toxin-inducing conditions. Such structures did not themselves produce DON and should not automatically be called functional toxisomes. FgHog1/HOG-pathway activity contributed to their formation, whereas unfolded-protein-response activation was not required under the tested conditions. Crucially, this study does not establish ER-membrane localization of Tri5; its direct localization evidence concerns Tri1 and Tri4. (wang2024overproductionofmycotoxin pages 4-5, wang2024overproductionofmycotoxin pages 1-2, wang2024overproductionofmycotoxin pages 10-12)

During wheat infection, a TRI5-promoter GFP reporter was induced in specialized fungal infection structures, but the promoter reporter measures gene expression, not the subcellular location of Tri5 protein. TRI5 deletion did not abolish those structures or initial symptoms. In a separate study of three F. graminearum field isolates and nine independent TRI5 disruption mutants, mutants caused symptoms in inoculated wheat spikelets but failed to spread into neighboring spikelets. On barley they were not significantly less aggressive; on maize, reduced disease was observed for mutants of the nivalenol-producing isolate but not those of the two DON-producing isolates. This supports a context-dependent contribution of TRI5-dependent trichothecenes to virulence, especially wheat-head spread, rather than proving that the Tri5 protein directly attacks plant tissue. The field-isolate outcomes should not be presented as PH-1-specific measurements. (boenisch2011fusariumgraminearumforms pages 1-3, maier2006involvementoftrichothecenes pages 1-2)

Recent regulation research and quantitative findings

A February 2024 Nature Communications study identified RNA5P, a long noncoding RNA transcribed from the TRI5 promoter region, as a locus-dependent inhibitor of TRI5 expression and DON synthesis. Replacing an 834-bp upstream region containing RNA5P with the TRI12 promoter increased TRI5 sense transcripts 2.6-fold and increased DON production. Increasing RNA5P expression more than fivefold from an ectopic site did not significantly change TRI5 or DON, whereas approximately 15-fold overexpression at its native locus reduced TRI5 expression and made DON nearly undetectable. Together with perturbations of predicted Tri6-binding sites, these comparisons favor cis-linked regulation at the TRI5 locus, not a freely diffusible RNA inhibitor; Tri6 and Tri10 also support normal TRI5 expression and suppress antisense-TRI5 transcription. Promoter replacement changes local DNA regulatory context as well as RNA5P transcription, an important qualification when interpreting the exact molecular mechanism. (huang2024regulationoftri5 pages 1-2, huang2024regulationoftri5 pages 4-5, huang2024regulationoftri5 pages 5-7)

Research and practical implications

TRI5 is a useful experimental pathway marker and genetic intervention point: deletion separates trichothecene-deficient infection from initial fungal colonization, while its promoter reports when the pathway is induced in infected wheat. Its biochemically defined first reaction and noncatalytic influence on toxin-associated cellular organization also make it a plausible target for toxin-reduction research. These are research applications, not evidence that a TRI5-specific inhibitor or TRI5-targeted crop treatment has been validated for field use. Moreover, the different wheat, barley, and maize phenotypes caution against assuming that reducing TRI5-dependent trichothecenes will have identical disease-control effects across crops or chemotypes. (flynn2019expressionofthe pages 9-11, flynn2019expressionofthe pages 11-14, boenisch2011fusariumgraminearumforms pages 1-3, maier2006involvementoftrichothecenes pages 1-2)

Identity

  • Trichodiene synthase, EC 4.2.3.6, RHEA:12052; Pfam PF06330 (TRI5); UniProt: "catalyzes the isomerization and cyclization of farnesyl pyro-phosphate to form trichodiene, the first cyclic intermediate in the biosynthetic pathway for trichothecenes".

  • Recombinant enzyme: PMID:30664933
  • Non-catalytic role: PMID:30664933 The proposed physical interaction with ER-anchored TRI1/TRI4/TRI11 is a model only.

description: >-
  TRI5 (FGSG_03537) encodes trichodiene synthase (EC 4.2.3.6), a soluble
  class I sesquiterpene cyclase of the wheat head blight fungus Fusarium
  graminearum (teleomorph Gibberella zeae). Using Mg2+ bound by its conserved
  NSE/DTE motif, the enzyme ionizes and cyclizes (2E,6E)-farnesyl diphosphate
  to trichodiene plus diphosphate, the first committed step that diverts
  isoprenoid flux into trichothecene biosynthesis. Recombinant enzyme produces
  mainly trichodiene with minor related cyclization products. Trichodiene is
  subsequently oxygenated by the cytochrome P450 TRI4 and further modified by
  other TRI cluster enzymes to give the type B trichothecene deoxynivalenol
  (DON) and its acetylated derivatives in strain PH-1 (or nivalenol in other
  chemotypes). TRI5 is a cytosolic protein enriched next to the ER-derived
  toxisome where downstream, membrane-anchored TRI enzymes reside; independent
  of its catalytic activity, TRI5 protein is required for normal toxisome
  morphology and for production of several non-trichothecene sesquiterpenes
  such as culmorin. TRI5 expression is induced in infection structures on
  wheat, is controlled by the cluster regulators TRI6 and TRI10 and by a
  cis-acting promoter-derived long non-coding RNA. Because TRI5 deletion
  abolishes trichothecene production, tri5 mutants are widely used to show
  that DON acts as a virulence factor: they cause local lesions on inoculated
  wheat spikelets but fail to spread through the rachis, whereas virulence on
  barley, maize and Arabidopsis floral tissue is largely unaffected. The
  host-side effect is exerted by DON itself, an inhibitor of eukaryotic
  ribosomes, rather than by the TRI5 protein.
