# Functional annotation report: *Heterocephalus glaber* **Ntrk1** (UniProt A0AAX6QC09)

## Executive conclusion

The supplied UniProt identity is internally consistent: **A0AAX6QC09 is the naked mole-rat (*Heterocephalus glaber*) ortholog of Ntrk1, encoding tropomyosin receptor kinase A (TrkA), the high-affinity nerve-growth-factor receptor**. The description “tyrosine-protein kinase receptor,” EC 2.7.10.1, and the reported cysteine-rich and immunoglobulin-like extracellular domains all agree with canonical mammalian TrkA architecture. Literature on naked mole-rat sensory neurons independently refers to the relevant protein as TrkA/NGF receptor and therefore supports the gene assignment, although the papers retrieved do not explicitly cite accession A0AAX6QC09. No conflicting use of *Ntrk1* for a different naked mole-rat protein was found. (smith2020independentevolutionof pages 6-8, smith2020independentevolutionof pages 5-6, garciadominguez2025ngfinneuropathic pages 4-6)

The most defensible functional annotation is:

> **A single-pass cell-surface receptor tyrosine kinase that binds extracellular NGF and converts ligand binding into intracellular tyrosine phosphorylation, principally regulating sensory and sympathetic neuron development, survival, axonal physiology, and nociceptor sensitization. In the naked mole-rat, TrkA retains this identity but has evolutionarily reduced signaling efficacy, particularly in NGF-dependent inflammatory-pain sensitization.** (smith2020independentevolutionof pages 6-8, garciadominguez2025ngfinneuropathic pages 4-6, marlin2015biogenesisandfunction pages 4-8)

## 1. Identity verification and evidence boundaries

### Gene symbol and organism

*Ntrk1* is the established mammalian gene symbol for neurotrophic receptor tyrosine kinase 1, whose product is TrkA. The organism specified in the supplied record, *Heterocephalus glaber*, is the naked mole-rat. Naked mole-rat studies report TrkA expression and function in dorsal-root-ganglion sensory neurons, directly matching the stated receptor identity rather than a different similarly named protein. (smith2020independentevolutionof pages 6-8, smith2020independentevolutionof pages 5-6)

### Domain and family consistency

Canonical TrkA is a type-I, single-pass transmembrane glycoprotein with an N-terminal extracellular region, one membrane-spanning helix, and a C-terminal cytoplasmic tyrosine-kinase domain. Its extracellular region contains cysteine-rich and leucine-rich elements followed by immunoglobulin-like domains; the membrane-proximal fifth extracellular domain mediates NGF recognition. Mature mammalian TrkA is approximately 140 kDa after N-glycosylation. Thus, the supplied InterPro assignments—Cys-rich flank, Ig-like domain, Ig I-set, and Ig-like fold—fit TrkA, but they describe only part of the expected architecture; a complete sequence annotation should additionally recover a transmembrane segment and intracellular protein-tyrosine-kinase domain. (garciadominguez2025ngfinneuropathic pages 4-6, marlin2015biogenesisandfunction pages 4-8)

### Important limitation

No retrieved publication directly mapped experimental results to **A0AAX6QC09**. The accession-level identity is therefore based on the supplied UniProt annotation, while the strongest organism-specific experiments concern naked mole-rat TrkA/Ntrk1 generally. Functional extrapolation from canonical mammalian TrkA is identified below as orthology-based inference rather than direct biochemical characterization of the accession.

## 2. Primary molecular function

### Ligand and substrate specificity

TrkA is the principal high-affinity receptor for **mature nerve growth factor (NGF)**. NGF binds the extracellular immunoglobulin-like receptor region and stabilizes an active receptor dimer or dimeric conformation. The receptor is not a transporter and does not have a diffusible physiological small-molecule substrate. Rather, its intracellular kinase transfers the γ-phosphate of ATP to tyrosine residues on the receptor itself and on recruited protein substrates. In reaction terms:

**ATP + protein-L-tyrosine → ADP + protein-L-tyrosine phosphate.**

Ligand binding induces auto-/trans-phosphorylation of intracellular tyrosines. Canonical mammalian residues Y490 and Y785 form major signaling sites, recruiting adaptor proteins and PLCγ, respectively. The exact corresponding residue numbers should be confirmed in the A0AAX6QC09 sequence before accession-specific annotation because naked mole-rat numbering may differ. (peach2023neuropilin1isa pages 5-7, marlin2015biogenesisandfunction pages 4-8)

### Immediate biochemical outputs

Activated TrkA recruits SHC/FRS-family adaptors and signaling enzymes, engaging three core modules:

1. **Ras–RAF–MEK–ERK/MAPK**, supporting differentiation, neurite growth, transcription, and context-dependent proliferation.
2. **PI3K–AKT**, promoting neuronal survival and metabolic support.
3. **PLCγ–IP3/DAG–Ca²⁺/PKC**, controlling calcium-dependent and excitability-related responses.

Additional JAK/STAT and Rho-family cytoskeletal signaling has been reported in particular cell contexts, but these are less universal than the three core modules. (garciadominguez2025ngfinneuropathic pages 4-6, peach2023neuropilin1isa pages 5-7, marlin2015biogenesisandfunction pages 4-8, siddi2026epigeneticregulationof pages 2-4)

## 3. Cellular and subcellular localization

TrkA functions initially as an **integral plasma-membrane receptor**, with its NGF-binding region outside the cell and its kinase domain in the cytosol. In neurons, receptor is delivered to distal axons, growth cones, and target-contacting terminals. NGF binding at these sites activates TrkA and promotes internalization through clathrin-dependent endocytosis or macropinocytosis. (marlin2015biogenesisandfunction pages 4-8)

The internalized NGF–TrkA complex remains signaling competent in **endosomes** and can move retrogradely along the axon to the soma. This spatial arrangement permits target-derived NGF to regulate nuclear gene expression and long-term survival over substantial neuronal distances. Mammalian experiments also show that TrkA axonal delivery can depend on transcytosis; disruption of this trafficking lowers axonal receptor abundance, sympathetic-neuron survival, and target innervation. These detailed trafficking mechanisms are compelling mammalian orthology evidence but have not been demonstrated specifically for A0AAX6QC09. (garciadominguez2025ngfinneuropathic pages 4-6, marlin2015biogenesisandfunction pages 4-8)

For naked mole-rat, direct localization evidence retrieved here is narrower: TrkA is expressed in **dorsal-root-ganglion sensory neurons**, where NGF-dependent nociceptor signaling can be tested. (smith2020independentevolutionof pages 6-8, smith2020independentevolutionof pages 5-6)

## 4. Biological processes and pathway role

The conserved physiological role of NGF–TrkA signaling is to couple target-derived trophic support to the development and maintenance of peripheral sensory and sympathetic neurons. Its outputs include neuronal survival, differentiation, axonal growth, target innervation, and transcriptional adaptation. (garciadominguez2025ngfinneuropathic pages 4-6, marlin2015biogenesisandfunction pages 4-8)

In mature nociceptors, inflammatory tissues release NGF, which activates TrkA and sensitizes pain-transducing channels and excitability machinery. TRPV1 sensitization is a particularly relevant output: it lowers the threshold for heat-related nociception and contributes to thermal hyperalgesia. TrkA therefore acts as a signaling hub upstream of pain sensitization, rather than functioning as the ion channel that directly detects heat or capsaicin. (smith2020independentevolutionof pages 5-6, peach2023neuropilin1isa pages 5-7)

## 5. Naked mole-rat-specific functional evidence

The crucial species-specific result is not loss of TrkA expression or ligand recognition, but **hypofunctional intracellular signaling**. Cultured naked mole-rat DRG neurons express TrkA, yet NGF fails to produce the normal sensitization of TRPV1. Naked mole-rat TrkA is substantially less effective than rat TrkA in driving this response, and receptor-swapping experiments localized the deficit to the intracellular region. One to three lineage-specific amino-acid substitutions in the kinase domain were reported to account for the reduced signaling phenotype. (smith2020independentevolutionof pages 6-8, smith2020independentevolutionof pages 5-6)

At the organismal level, this hypofunction is associated with the absence of NGF-induced thermal hyperalgesia and with reduced maintenance of cutaneous unmyelinated C-fiber nociceptors into adulthood. Normal naked mole-rat TRPV1 responsiveness means the phenotype cannot be explained simply by an inactive capsaicin receptor; instead, it reflects weakened coupling between NGF/TrkA and nociceptor sensitization. (smith2020independentevolutionof pages 6-8, smith2020independentevolutionof pages 5-6)

Accordingly, A0AAX6QC09 should not be annotated as a pseudogene or inactive kinase solely because naked mole-rats resist NGF-evoked hyperalgesia. A better interpretation is **functional but signaling-attenuated TrkA**, with sufficient trophic activity for essential neural functions but reduced gain in inflammatory-pain pathways. This evolutionary “tuning” interpretation is supported by the receptor retaining expression and measurable signaling while showing a selective deficit in sensitization. (smith2020independentevolutionof pages 6-8)

## 6. Recent developments, 2023–2024

Recent work has refined how receptor availability and co-receptors determine TrkA output. A 2023 PNAS study showed in mice that PTP1B-dependent transcytosis is required to supply TrkA to sympathetic axons; a signaling-preserving trafficking mutation reduced axonal TrkA, caused developmental sympathetic-neuron loss, reduced target innervation, and produced autonomic signs. This establishes localization as a causal determinant of physiological TrkA function, not merely descriptive cell biology. Publication: February 2023, DOI URL: https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2205426120.

A December 2023 study proposed neuropilin-1 as an NGF co-receptor that supports optimal TrkA signaling and nuclear ERK activation at low NGF concentrations. It reported an approximately **1.4-fold** rise in TrkA phospho-Y785 immunofluorescence after NGF stimulation. Because the source was a bioRxiv preprint in the retrieved corpus, its conclusions warrant less weight than peer-reviewed evidence. DOI URL: https://doi.org/10.1101/2023.12.06.570398. (peach2023neuropilin1isa pages 5-7)

The recent naked mole-rat literature continues to treat hypofunctional TrkA as the established explanation for failed NGF-dependent neuronal sensitization. A 2025 comparative sensory-neuron study used this mechanism as the benchmark against which the relatively preserved artemin–GFRα3 sensitization pathway was assessed, supporting pathway-selective attenuation rather than globally defective neurotrophic signaling. Publication: July 2025, DOI URL: https://doi.org/10.1007/s00359-025-01752-7. (qiu2025arteminsensitisesmouse pages 1-2)

## 7. Applications and real-world implementation

### Pain biology

The naked mole-rat receptor is a natural experiment in reducing NGF–TrkA pain signaling without eliminating all neuronal function. It therefore provides a mechanistic model for designing analgesic strategies that attenuate sensitization while preserving trophic signaling. This remains a research application; it is not evidence that A0AAX6QC09 itself is used clinically. (smith2020independentevolutionof pages 6-8)

### Precision oncology—relevant but biologically distinct

In humans, chromosomal rearrangements can fuse the NTRK1 kinase domain to a partner that drives constitutive activation. Such **oncogenic NTRK1 fusions are fundamentally different from the normal full-length naked mole-rat receptor**. They nevertheless demonstrate the druggability of the conserved kinase domain.

Larotrectinib received FDA approval in November 2018 and entrectinib in August 2019 for eligible NTRK-fusion-positive solid tumors, independent of tissue of origin. Larotrectinib is administered at 100 mg twice daily in adults, with body-surface-area adjustment in children. Publication reviewing implementation: Bungaro and Garbo, September 2024, https://doi.org/10.21037/pcm-23-19. (bungaro2024ntrk123biologydetection pages 5-6, theik2024ntrktherapyamong pages 4-6, theik2024ntrktherapyamong pages 2-4)

Detection is implemented using pan-TRK immunohistochemistry, FISH, RT-PCR, and DNA/RNA next-generation sequencing. The 2024 reviews favor broad NGS panels—often with RNA confirmation—as the most comprehensive approach because fusion partners and breakpoints vary. Protein expression alone is less specific in neural tissues, where physiological TRK expression can produce positive staining. (bungaro2024ntrk123biologydetection pages 5-6, theik2024ntrktherapyamong pages 1-2, piekarz2026ntrkfusionas pages 2-4, theik2024ntrktherapyamong pages 2-4)

## 8. Quantitative clinical context

NTRK fusions are rare in common tumors: 2024 reviews estimate **<1%** overall in common solid cancers, approximately **0.5%** in NSCLC in one synthesis, and **0.1–0.2%** in NSCLC in another. Frequencies can be much higher in selected rare cancers. These estimates vary with cohort composition and assay, so they should not be combined as though they were a single prevalence measurement. (theik2024ntrktherapyamong pages 6-7, bungaro2024ntrk123biologydetection pages 1-2)

Across three early larotrectinib studies, 55 fusion-positive patients had an objective response rate of approximately **75–80%** at 9.4 months’ median follow-up. A later 218-patient analysis retained a **75% ORR**, comprising 22% complete and 53% partial responses. Entrectinib produced an approximately **57% ORR** among 54 patients across ten tumor types. (bungaro2024ntrk123biologydetection pages 5-6, theik2024ntrktherapyamong pages 4-6)

In reported NTRK-fusion-positive lung-cancer cohorts, larotrectinib achieved a **73% ORR** (95% CI 45–92), including 7% complete and 67% partial responses; reported median progression-free and overall survival were 35.4 and 40.7 months, respectively. Small samples and tumor-specific selection limit generalization. (theik2024ntrktherapyamong pages 6-7, theik2024ntrktherapyamong pages 7-9)

Most treatment-related adverse events were grade 1–2. Reported effects include dizziness, gastrointestinal symptoms, weight gain, anemia, neutropenia, and elevated transaminases. One synthesis reported grade 3–4 toxicity in 18% of larotrectinib recipients; another reported individual rates including anemia 11%, neutropenia 7%, transaminase elevation 7%, and weight gain 7%. On-target neurological effects are biologically plausible because normal TRK receptors function in the nervous system. (bungaro2024ntrk123biologydetection pages 5-6, theik2024ntrktherapyamong pages 6-7, theik2024ntrktherapyamong pages 4-6)

Acquired resistance includes NTRK1 solvent-front and kinase-domain substitutions such as **G595R** and **G667C**. This has motivated compact next-generation inhibitors including repotrectinib and selitrectinib. Again, these human tumor resistance mutations should not be attributed to normal naked mole-rat Ntrk1. (theik2024ntrktherapyamong pages 6-7, theik2024ntrktherapyamong pages 4-6)

## 9. Recommended database annotation

**Molecular function:** NGF-activated transmembrane receptor protein-tyrosine kinase; ATP-dependent phosphorylation of receptor and signaling proteins.

**Ligand specificity:** Primarily mature NGF; no evidence retrieved that naked mole-rat TrkA has switched to a different neurotrophin ligand.

**Cellular component:** Plasma membrane of responsive sensory/sympathetic neurons; distal axon/growth-cone membrane; ligand-induced intracellular signaling endosomes. The latter two are strongly supported in mammals but remain orthology-based for A0AAX6QC09. (garciadominguez2025ngfinneuropathic pages 4-6, marlin2015biogenesisandfunction pages 4-8)

**Biological processes:** NGF signaling, sensory and sympathetic neuron development/survival, axon growth and target innervation, nociceptor sensitization, and inflammatory thermal hyperalgesia.

**Species-specific qualifier:** Hypofunctional relative to conventional rodent TrkA in NGF-induced nociceptor sensitization, owing to intracellular/kinase-domain sequence changes; associated with diminished thermal hyperalgesia and altered adult C-fiber maintenance. (smith2020independentevolutionof pages 6-8, smith2020independentevolutionof pages 5-6)

**Confidence:** High for TrkA identity and receptor class; high for naked mole-rat pain-pathway hypofunction; moderate-to-high for detailed biochemical and trafficking annotation because much derives from conserved mammalian TrkA; low for any assertion that a particular experiment tested accession A0AAX6QC09 directly.
