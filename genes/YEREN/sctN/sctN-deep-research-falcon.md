# Functional annotation report: *Yersinia enterocolitica* **sctN/yscN** (UniProt P40290)

## Executive conclusion

The target is correctly identified as **SctN**, historically named **YscN**, of *Yersinia enterocolitica*. It is the approximately 48-kDa cytoplasmic ATPase associated with the plasmid-encoded Ysc–Yop type III secretion system (T3SS), not a membrane transporter and not a secreted protein. Its primary biochemical reaction is ATP hydrolysis, **ATP + H₂O → ADP + inorganic phosphate**, and its principal biological role is to process and deliver T3SS protein substrates at the cytoplasmic face of the injectisome. It operates within a dynamic sorting platform assembled with SctL/YscL, SctQ/YscQ, and SctK/YscK. Direct live-cell studies show that SctN occurs both in mobile cytosolic complexes and in injectisome-associated assemblies. The best current model is therefore not simply “ATPase motor,” but a regulated substrate-recruitment and substrate-processing hub that helps couple cytosolic cargo selection to export-gate engagement.

The literature is potentially confusing because *Y. enterocolitica* also encodes **YsaN**, the ATPase of a separate chromosomal Ysa–Ysp T3SS. YsaN is not P40290. Quantitative YsaN properties—such as its approximately 590–603-kDa dodecamer and ~35-nM YsaL affinity—must not be assigned to SctN/YscN (chatterjee2013identificationandmolecular pages 10-12, chatterjee2013identificationandmolecular pages 8-10, chatterjee2013identificationandmolecular pages 4-5).

## 1. Identity verification

### 1.1 Symbol, protein, and organism

The supplied UniProt record identifies P40290 as *Y. enterocolitica* **sctN**, synonym **yscN**, encoding the ATPase of the Ysc–Yop T3SS. This agrees with organism-specific literature describing YscN as the ATPase or “putative energizer” of the Yersinia Yop secretion apparatus. Older papers use **YscN**; **SctN** is the unified T3SS nomenclature used increasingly in recent comparative literature. The predicted molecular mass reported in the foundational literature is approximately **47.8 kDa** (cornelis1998thevirulenceplasmid pages 12-13, yoshida2014functionalcharacterizationof pages 11-11, chatterjee2013identificationandmolecular pages 12-13).

### 1.2 Family and domain agreement

The reported Walker A and Walker B nucleotide-binding motifs are consistent with the supplied InterPro assignments: AAA+-like ATPase, ATPase α/β-chain, and F₁/V₁/A₁ ATPase-related nucleotide-binding domains. This is also consistent with the established evolutionary and structural relationship between injectisome ATPases and flagellar FliI/F₁-like ATPase proteins. Thus, the supplied family/domain annotation and the literature describe the same protein class (cornelis1998thevirulenceplasmid pages 12-13, soto2023thesortingplatform pages 3-3).

### 1.3 Explicit exclusion of the similarly named YsaN

*Y. enterocolitica* possesses two distinct virulence-associated T3SS contexts. **YscN/SctN (P40290)** belongs to the plasmid-encoded Ysc–Yop injectisome. **YsaN** belongs to the chromosomal Ysa–Ysp system. Although both are homologous secretion ATPases, their measured oligomerization, regulator affinity, and substrate associations are not interchangeable. This report therefore excludes YsaN-specific biochemical values except where explicitly presented as comparative evidence (chatterjee2013identificationandmolecular pages 10-12, chatterjee2013identificationandmolecular pages 8-10, chatterjee2013identificationandmolecular pages 4-5).

## 2. Primary molecular function

### 2.1 Catalyzed reaction

SctN/YscN is an ATP phosphohydrolase associated with type III protein export. Its annotated reaction is:

**ATP + H₂O → ADP + Pi**.

ATP is therefore the small-molecule substrate. The exported proteins are better described as macromolecular clients or cargo rather than catalytic substrates. Conserved Walker motifs and the secretion defect caused by removal or disruption of Walker-box function directly connect nucleotide utilization to Yop export. Early literature appropriately described the protein as a putative ATPase because direct activity was initially established more firmly for homologs; subsequent organism-specific work characterized YscN as the Yersinia T3SS ATPase (cornelis1998thevirulenceplasmid pages 12-13, yoshida2014functionalcharacterizationof pages 11-11, chatterjee2013identificationandmolecular pages 12-13).

Exact P40290-specific values for *K*m, *k*cat, nucleotide selectivity, and divalent-ion dependence were not recoverable from the available primary-text passages. Values from YsaN, Salmonella InvC/SsaN, EPEC EscN, or other homologs should not be substituted as if they were direct measurements of P40290.

### 2.2 Protein-cargo specificity

SctN functions in the Ysc–Yop pathway, which exports sequential classes of proteins: early apparatus/needle substrates, translocators, and later Yop effectors. The sorting platform recognizes secretion-competent cargo through N-terminal secretion information and, for many substrates, associated cytosolic chaperones. General mechanistic models assign SctN roles in accepting cargo, promoting chaperone dissociation, and preparing or partially unfolding substrates for passage through the narrow export channel. However, direct live-cell evidence is strongest for effector association with SctQ and SctL-containing mobile complexes; precisely when SctN contacts cargo or removes its chaperone remains unresolved (carsten2023superresolvedandlive pages 12-15, soto2023thesortingplatform pages 3-3, pintor2024thepathand pages 87-91).

Accordingly, a conservative annotation is:

> **SctN hydrolyzes ATP within the cytoplasmic T3SS sorting platform and facilitates engagement and processing of secretion substrates for export through the Ysc injectisome.**

It should not be annotated as a substrate-specific transporter for one Yop. Its client range is pathway-level and includes multiple structural, translocator, and effector proteins.

## 3. Biological pathway and structural role

### 3.1 Position in the Ysc–Yop pathway

The Ysc–Yop T3SS is a multiprotein injectisome spanning the bacterial inner and outer membranes and extending a needle toward the host cell. SctN acts on the bacterial cytoplasmic side, upstream of translocation through the membrane export gate. Its immediate functional module is the sorting platform, containing SctK/YscK, SctQ/YscQ, SctL/YscL, and SctN/YscN (soto2023thesortingplatform pages 3-3, diepold2010decipheringtheassembly pages 1-1).

An interaction chain often represented as **SctK–SctQ–SctL–SctN** links the membrane-proximal/cytoplasmic ring region to the ATPase. This organization is analogous to related flagellar export components but has been tested directly in the Yersinia injectisome through fluorescent localization and genetic epistasis (soto2023thesortingplatform pages 3-3, diepold2010decipheringtheassembly pages 1-1).

### 3.2 Assembly evidence

Functional fluorescent fusions to YscC, YscD, YscQ, and YscN revealed peripheral injectisome foci and supported an outside-in assembly sequence. After membrane-ring formation, YscN and YscQ assemble synchronously. Their localization requires one another and also YscK and YscL, demonstrating that these four proteins form an interdependent cytoplasmic assembly. Importantly, formation of this complex does not require YscN ATPase activity, separating structural recruitment of the ATPase from its catalytic function in secretion (diepold2010decipheringtheassembly pages 1-1).

### 3.3 Energetics: ATP versus proton motive force

Current T3SS models do not treat SctN as the sole energetic source for moving an entire polypeptide through the channel. ATP hydrolysis is strongly implicated in substrate recognition, chaperone release, initiation, and conformational preparation, whereas the export gate and proton motive force contribute substantially to translocation. Thus, “energizer” is historically useful but mechanistically incomplete. The most defensible interpretation is that ATP hydrolysis licenses or prepares cargo for export and works in concert with membrane electrochemical energy rather than replacing it.

## 4. Cellular localization

SctN has no role outside the cell and is not an integral membrane protein. It is synthesized in the bacterial cytoplasm and dynamically partitions between two states:

1. **Mobile cytosolic complexes**, including SctN–SctL and SctN–SctL–SctQ assemblies.
2. **Peripheral, injectisome-associated complexes** at the cytoplasmic face of the inner membrane.

Fluorescent YscN formed peripheral foci at assembled injectisomes. Three-dimensional single-molecule tracking subsequently detected freely diffusing **SctNL** and **SctNLQ** complexes in wild-type cells and in Δ*sctD* cells that cannot assemble intact injectisomes. This shows that these complexes can form before membrane-apparatus docking (prindle2022distinctcytosoliccomplexes pages 18-21, diepold2010decipheringtheassembly pages 1-1).

External acidification from pH 7 to pH 4 was associated with dissociation of SctN, SctL, SctQ, and SctK from injectisomes and cessation of secretion. Returning cells to pH 7 restored injectisome association in approximately **10 minutes**, followed by renewed effector secretion. This supports reversible, physiological regulation rather than permanent incorporation of SctN into the basal body (prindle2022distinctcytosoliccomplexes pages 18-21).

## 5. Experimental evidence supporting function

### 5.1 Sequence and genetic evidence

YscN contains canonical Walker A and B motifs. A YscN variant lacking Walker-box-A function was impaired in Yop secretion, demonstrating that the nucleotide-binding machinery is essential for pathway output. The same literature places yscN in the virB/ysc operon encoding conserved T3SS components and reports its approximately 47.8-kDa predicted mass (cornelis1998thevirulenceplasmid pages 12-13).

### 5.2 Interaction and assembly evidence

YscN localization depends upon YscK, YscL, and YscQ, and YscN/YscQ appear synchronously during assembly. These results provide direct *Y. enterocolitica* evidence that SctN is not a free-standing enzyme but the catalytic hub of a larger cytoplasmic platform (diepold2010decipheringtheassembly pages 1-1).

YscL is generally interpreted as both an adaptor/stalk-associated component and a negative regulator of ATPase activity. Nevertheless, quantitative values from the YsaN–YsaL system—for example, the approximately 35-nM affinity and 2:1 regulator:ATPase complex—are not direct measurements of P40290 and should not be transferred to YscN (chatterjee2013identificationandmolecular pages 10-12, chatterjee2013identificationandmolecular pages 8-10).

### 5.3 Live-cell dynamics

The 2022 single-molecule study resolved distinct SctN-containing cytosolic complexes rather than one uniform freely diffusing population. Their persistence in Δ*sctD* cells demonstrates that preassembly does not require an intact membrane injectisome. The study did not establish exact complex stoichiometry or prove each complex’s individual function, so assignments such as “cargo-loaded intermediate” remain models rather than final conclusions (prindle2022distinctcytosoliccomplexes pages 18-21).

## 6. Recent developments, 2023–2024

### 6.1 Mobile sorting platforms shuttle cargo

The most important recent direct advance is Wimmi et al., published in **Nature Microbiology in January 2024**, “Cytosolic sorting platform complexes shuttle type III secretion system effectors to the injectisome in *Yersinia enterocolitica*,” DOI: https://doi.org/10.1038/s41564-023-01545-1. Combining photoactivated localization microscopy, single-particle tracking, proximity labeling, and interaction analyses, the study showed that sorting-platform proteins contact effectors in the bacterial cytosol and support their delivery to injectisomes. The mobile complexes include SctN, although the most direct effector-interaction evidence centered on SctQ and SctL (wimmi2024cytosolicsortingplatform pages 11-12, pintor2024thepathand pages 87-91).

Secretion activation increased the estimated number of injectisomes from approximately **5 to 18 per bacterium**, a roughly 3.6-fold change. This indicates that activation changes not only export activity but also the abundance and organization of secretion machinery (wimmi2024cytosolicsortingplatform pages 11-12).

### 6.2 Quantitative platform dynamics

Recent modeling supports pod-like units containing approximately one SctK, 2–4 SctQ, 4–8 SctQᶜ, two SctL, and one SctN, with six pods proposed around the ATPase-associated hub. Approximately **24 SctQ molecules per injectisome** were estimated. Fluorescence-recovery analyses yielded an approximately **68.2-second recovery half-time** and an exchange rate near **0.51 s⁻¹**, underscoring that the platform is dynamic rather than a static basal-body appendage (wimmi2024cytosolicsortingplatform pages 11-12, pintor2024thepathand pages 87-91).

These are imaging- and model-derived values, not a purified native SctN structure or directly counted SctN hexamer. Whether SctN is always hexameric in vivo, when it joins cargo-loaded pods, and exactly when ATP hydrolysis drives chaperone removal remain open questions (pintor2024thepathand pages 87-91).

### 6.3 Current expert interpretation

Recent reviews describe the sorting platform as a recruitment, hierarchy-control, and export-initiation machine rather than merely a fixed C ring. SctN is structurally related to F₁-like ATPases and is usually modeled as a central hexameric ATPase coupled to a stalk/adaptor architecture. The expert consensus is that ATPase activity is essential, but its mechanistic contribution must be integrated with dynamic substrate shuttling and proton-motive-force-driven export (soto2023thesortingplatform pages 3-3).

Relevant recent reviews include:

- Soto and Lara-Tejero, **June 2023**, “The sorting platform in the type III secretion pathway,” *BioEssays*: https://doi.org/10.1002/bies.202300078 (soto2023thesortingplatform pages 3-3).
- Pais, Kim, and Wagner, **June 2023**, “Virulence-associated type III secretion systems in Gram-negative bacteria,” *Microbiology*: https://doi.org/10.1099/mic.0.001328.
- Cabezón et al., **November 2023**, “Primary architecture and energy requirements of Type III and Type IV secretion systems,” *Frontiers in Cellular and Infection Microbiology*: https://doi.org/10.3389/fcimb.2023.1255852.

## 7. Applications and real-world implementation

### 7.1 Anti-virulence drug target

SctN/YscN is attractive as an anti-virulence target because it is conserved, required for functional effector secretion, and absent as a direct counterpart in human cells. Inhibiting it should disarm pathogenicity without necessarily killing the bacterium, an approach hypothesized to exert less selective pressure than conventional growth-inhibitory antibiotics.

Proof of principle is strongest for closely related **Y. pestis** YscN. Small-molecule studies reported YscN ATPase inhibitors with in-vitro IC₅₀ values below **20 µM**, accompanied by inhibition of YopE secretion. These findings validate the ATPase as chemically tractable but are not direct pharmacological measurements on *Y. enterocolitica* P40290. Likewise, inhibitor specificity remains a central issue because bacterial secretion ATPases and other ATP-utilizing proteins can share nucleotide-binding features.

No approved drug selectively targeting P40290 was identified. The application remains preclinical.

### 7.2 Attenuated vaccine engineering

Deletion of yscN has also been used to create attenuated *Y. pestis* vaccine strains. In mouse studies, Δ*yscN*-based strains have produced protection under selected vaccination regimens. This demonstrates the practical value of disabling the ATPase to abolish T3SS virulence, but it is an application in *Y. pestis*, not a deployed vaccine based on *Y. enterocolitica* P40290.

### 7.3 Experimental and biotechnology applications

SctN-associated proteins and their dynamic complexes provide tools for studying protein-export hierarchy, molecular-machine assembly, and cytoplasmic cargo trafficking. Live-cell PALM and three-dimensional single-molecule tracking in *Y. enterocolitica* now offer a real-world experimental implementation for resolving transient protein complexes that are difficult to isolate biochemically (prindle2022distinctcytosoliccomplexes pages 18-21, wimmi2024cytosolicsortingplatform pages 11-12).

## 8. Recommended functional annotation

**Primary function:** Cytoplasmic ATPase of the plasmid-encoded Ysc–Yop T3SS; hydrolyzes ATP and participates in recruitment, sorting, chaperone release and/or conformational preparation of protein substrates for export.

**Reaction:** ATP + H₂O → ADP + Pi.

**Substrate specificity:** Small-molecule substrate is ATP. Macromolecular clients comprise multiple Ysc–Yop T3SS secretion substrates, including apparatus/needle proteins, translocators, and Yop effectors, frequently presented with cognate chaperones. No evidence supports specificity for only one exported protein.

**Localization:** Bacterial cytoplasm and peripheral cytosolic face of the inner-membrane injectisome; dynamically exchanges between soluble SctN-containing complexes and membrane-associated sorting platforms.

**Pathway:** Ysc–Yop type III secretion/injectisome assembly and protein export.

**Key partners:** SctL/YscL, SctQ/YscQ, and SctK/YscK; indirectly the membrane export apparatus and cargo–chaperone complexes.

**Evidence level:** Strong direct genetic, localization, assembly, and live-cell interaction evidence in *Y. enterocolitica*. Exact kinetic constants, native oligomeric state under all physiological conditions, and the temporal sequence of cargo binding, chaperone release, unfolding, and ATP hydrolysis remain incompletely resolved.

## 9. Overall assessment and research gaps

P40290 is confidently assigned as the Ysc–Yop T3SS ATPase SctN/YscN. Its conserved domains, Walker motifs, secretion-defective mutants, sorting-platform dependencies, and dynamic localization all converge on the same functional interpretation. The major conceptual update from 2023–2024 is that SctN should be viewed within a **mobile and exchangeable sorting-platform network** that shuttles effectors to injectisomes, not simply as a permanently mounted export motor.

The highest-priority unresolved questions are: direct P40290 kinetic constants and nucleotide/metal specificity; the native oligomeric distribution of SctN in active and inactive cells; the order of SctN engagement relative to cargo capture by SctQ/SctL; whether ATP hydrolysis primarily drives chaperone release, partial unfolding, export-gate activation, or multiple steps; and the degree to which chemically identified Y. pestis YscN inhibitors act on *Y. enterocolitica* P40290 without off-target effects.
