| Identity and nomenclature | The supplied UniProt record assigns **P40290** to *Yersinia enterocolitica* **sctN**, synonym **yscN**. The literature consistently calls the plasmid-encoded Ysc–Yop T3SS ATPase **YscN**; standardized T3SS nomenclature calls it **SctN**. [Woestyn et al., 1994](https://doi.org/10.1128/jb.176.6.1561-1569.1994) and [Blaylock et al., 2006](https://doi.org/10.1128/JB.188.10.3525-3534.2006) are the foundational organism-specific studies (pqac-00000006, pqac-00000007). | **P40290 = Y. enterocolitica SctN/YscN**, the ATPase of the plasmid-encoded Ysc–Yop injectisome. | Accession mapping is based on the supplied UniProt identity; literature generally uses YscN rather than the newer unified name SctN. It must not be confused with chromosomal YsaN. |
| ATPase family, motifs, and size | Sequence analysis identified Walker A and Walker B nucleotide-binding motifs and a predicted mass of approximately **47.8 kDa**; a YscN construct lacking Walker box A was defective in Yop secretion. [Cornelis et al., 1998](https://doi.org/10.1128/MMBR.62.4.1315-1352.1998) (pqac-00000000). | The sequence and mutant phenotype place YscN in the conserved T3SS ATPase family, structurally related to F₁/V₁/A₁ ATPase catalytic subunits. | The family/domain assignment is strong. Early literature initially inferred ATPase activity from sequence and homologs; later organism-specific biochemical characterization is reported by Blaylock et al., but exact kinetic values were not recovered in the available evidence. |
| Catalytic reaction and substrate specificity | UniProt assigns EC 7.4.2.8; the chemically expected reaction is **ATP + H₂O → ADP + phosphate**, normally requiring a divalent cation such as Mg²⁺. Walker-motif disruption compromises secretion (pqac-00000000). | YscN hydrolyses ATP and couples nucleotide turnover to productive T3SS substrate handling/export. Its small-molecule substrate is ATP; its macromolecular clients are secretion substrates or substrate–chaperone assemblies. | Reaction chemistry is supported by database annotation and conserved ATPase mechanism. Exact P40290 values for *K*ₘ, *k*cat, nucleotide selectivity, and metal dependence were not established from the retrieved passages and should not be borrowed from YsaN or other homologs. |
| Requirement for Yop secretion | Walker-box deletion or disruption impaired Yop secretion, establishing that intact YscN nucleotide-binding machinery is required for the Ysc–Yop pathway (pqac-00000000). | YscN is an essential functional energizer or substrate-processing factor for secretion of Yops through the injectisome. | Strong direct genetic evidence. A secretion defect alone does not determine whether ATP hydrolysis powers bulk translocation, substrate engagement, chaperone release, unfolding, or several of these steps. |
| Sorting-platform partners | Fluorescent-focus epistasis showed that YscN and YscQ assemble synchronously and require each other plus **YscK and YscL**. [Diepold et al., 2010](https://doi.org/10.1038/emboj.2010.84) (pqac-00000011). General interaction architecture is represented as SctK–SctQ–SctL–SctN (pqac-00000005). | YscN forms the ATPase hub of a cytosolic sorting platform containing YscK/SctK, YscL/SctL, and YscQ/SctQ. | Strong direct assembly evidence in *Y. enterocolitica*. YscL is described as an ATPase regulator, but quantitative interaction or inhibition values should be taken from the YscN study itself, not from the distinct YsaN–YsaL system. |
| Cellular localization | Functional fluorescent YscN fusions formed peripheral foci after assembly of the membrane rings, placing YscN at the **cytosolic face of the inner-membrane injectisome** (pqac-00000011). Live-cell work also detected freely diffusing cytosolic SctN-containing complexes (pqac-00000008). | YscN is a soluble cytoplasmic protein that dynamically partitions between free cytosolic complexes and injectisome-associated sorting platforms at the inner membrane; it is not an integral membrane protein or a secreted effector. | Direct live-cell localization evidence. “Membrane-associated” here means peripheral attachment to the cytosolic apparatus, not membrane insertion. |
| Cytosolic SctNL and SctNLQ complexes | Three-dimensional single-molecule tracking resolved freely diffusing **SctN–SctL (NL)** and **SctN–SctL–SctQ (NLQ)** complexes in wild-type and Δ*sctD* cells unable to assemble injectisomes. [Prindle et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.1128/spectrum.01744-22); preprint DOI [10.1101/2022.04.25.488798](https://doi.org/10.1101/2022.04.25.488798) (pqac-00000008). | SctN can preassemble with SctL and SctQ in the cytosol before docking to an injectisome. | Direct live-cell evidence for distinct diffusive states and partner-dependent complexes; exact stoichiometries and biochemical functions were not resolved. |
| Dynamic association and secretion state | Acidification from extracellular pH 7 to 4 was associated with dissociation of SctN/L/Q/K and loss of secretion; restoration to pH 7 re-established injectisome binding in approximately **10 min**, followed by renewed effector secretion (pqac-00000008). | Sorting-platform association is reversible and coupled to the secretion-active physiological state. | Correlative live-cell evidence; it does not by itself prove that SctN dissociation is the primary pH sensor or cause of secretion arrest. |
| 2024 effector-shuttling model | sptPALM, proximity labelling, and interaction analyses showed that mobile sorting-platform assemblies containing SctN participate in cytosolic effector recruitment and delivery to membrane-bound injectisomes. Secretion activation increased injectisome abundance from approximately **5 to 18 per bacterium**. [Wimmi et al., published January 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41564-023-01545-1) (pqac-00000009). | Current evidence supports a **mobile cargo-shuttle model**: cytosolic sorting-platform complexes capture effectors and deliver them to the export gate, where SctN participates in substrate processing. | High-quality, direct *Y. enterocolitica* live-cell evidence. Direct binding of effectors was clearest for SctQ and SctL; SctN’s precise timing in chaperone removal or unfolding remains unresolved. |
| Quantitative platform architecture | Recent analyses support pod-like units containing approximately one SctK, 2–4 SctQ, 4–8 SctQᶜ, two SctL, and one SctN; approximately **24 SctQ molecules per injectisome**, a **68.2-s recovery half-time**, and an exchange rate near **0.51 s⁻¹** were estimated (pqac-00000009, pqac-00000010). | The SctN-containing sorting platform is dynamic rather than permanently fixed and may organize six pods around a central, potentially hexameric ATPase hub. | Quantitative imaging/model-based estimates; the exact oligomeric state of native YscN and the relationship between mobile pods and the proposed hexamer remain partly unresolved. |
| YsaN exclusion: avoid gene conflation | *Y. enterocolitica* also encodes **YsaN**, the ATPase of the distinct chromosomal Ysa–Ysp T3SS. YsaN is Mg²⁺ dependent, forms monomeric and approximately 590–603-kDa dodecameric populations, and binds YsaL with an estimated *K*D of approximately 35 nM (pqac-00000002, pqac-00000003, pqac-00000004). | **YsaN is not P40290 and is not a synonym of SctN/YscN.** Its oligomerization, kinetics, YsaL affinity, and substrate data cannot be reported as direct properties of P40290. | Critical limitation: both proteins are T3SS ATPases in the same organism, making literature conflation easy. YsaN findings may provide comparative context only when explicitly labelled as homolog-based inference. |
