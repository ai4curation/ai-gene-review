# pvdN (PA2394, G3XCY5) — review notes

Reviewed alongside `pvdO` and `pvdP` to fill the periplasmic-maturation segment. It carries the
batch's clearest wrong-activity finding.

## What is established

427 residues, a class-V pyridoxal-phosphate-dependent aminotransferase fold, and periplasmic by
direct evidence (`GO:0030288`, IDA, PMID:16621825). Four `GO:0002049` rows — one IDA and three
IMP from three separate papers — place it firmly in pyoverdine biosynthesis.

Its actual reaction is a **side-chain tailoring step**:

`[PMID:30386787 "PvdN generates the succinamide residue"]`
`[PMID:30386787 "The side chain modifications - surprising periplasmicreactions catalyzed by PvdN and PtaA"]`
`[PMID:30386787 "the lack of PtaA results in the succinamide and succinic acid forms generated by PvdN"]`
`[PMID:30386787 "demonstrating that PvdN may even accept ferribactin as substrate"]`

So PvdN converts the glutamate side chain of the pyoverdine precursor to a succinamide, using
the PLP cofactor its fold provides. PtaA, which some strains carry instead, makes the
α-ketoglutarate form from the same precursor.

## `GO:0031071` cysteine desulfurase activity is a fold-based misassignment

Two rows assert it: an IBA from `PTN008451775` and an IEA from `EC:2.8.1.7`. Tracing where that
EC came from is the point:

- `DE   RecName: Full=cysteine desulfurase {ECO:0000256|ARBA:ARBA00012239}` — the protein's
  **name** on the record is ARBA-derived.
- `EC=2.8.1.7 {ECO:0000256|ARBA:ARBA00012239}` — so is the EC number.
- The `CATALYTIC ACTIVITY` block (L-cysteine + sulfur carrier → L-alanine) is
  `{ECO:0000256|ARBA:ARBA00050776}` — ARBA again.
- `SIMILARITY: Belongs to the class-V pyridoxal-phosphate-dependent aminotransferase family.
  **Csd subfamily**. {ECO:0000256|ARBA:ARBA00010447}` — ARBA again.

Every statement placing this protein in the cysteine desulfurase family is an **ARBA
association rule**, which the project rules class as an unvetted prediction and not valid
support. The chain then runs ARBA → `DE`/`EC` → EC2GO → `GO:0031071`, and separately a PAINT
node seeded by real cysteine desulfurases (`P77444`, `Q46925`, `O32164`, an *Arabidopsis* and a
*Plasmodium* entry) picks up the same fold.

Against it: PvdN is a periplasmic pyoverdine tailoring enzyme by direct evidence, its reaction
is the succinamide conversion quoted above, and cysteine desulfuration — a cytoplasmic
sulfur-mobilisation step feeding Fe-S cluster and thiamine biosynthesis — has no place in the
periplasm or in this pathway. The class-V PLP fold is shared by many enzymes doing quite
different chemistry; that is exactly what a fold-level rule cannot distinguish.

`REMOVE` on both rows, with a `WRONG_TERM` source defect at `PROBABLE` against UniProt naming
the ARBA rule, since the protein name and EC are where the error originates and fixing it there
would clear the derived GO rows at once.

## An ontology gap, recorded rather than proposed

GO has no term for the succinamide-forming side-chain conversion PvdN performs; an OLS search
for pyoverdine side-chain or succinamide transaminase/decarboxylase activity returns nothing.
Recorded as a `knowledge_gaps` entry and a question rather than a `proposed_new_terms` item:
the review's own framing is "surprising periplasmic reactions", the precise chemistry
(transamination versus decarboxylation and the order of steps) is not pinned down in what I
read, and a term definition written from that would be guesswork. Two new terms are already
proposed in this campaign on firmer ground.

This leaves the gene with no molecular-function annotation at all once the cysteine desulfurase
rows go, which is the honest state: the activity is known in outline, named nowhere in GO, and
has never been assayed directly on the purified protein.
