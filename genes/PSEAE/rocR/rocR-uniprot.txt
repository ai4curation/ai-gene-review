CC   -!- FUNCTION: Phosphodiesterase (PDE) that catalyzes the hydrolysis of
CC       cyclic diguanylate (c-di-GMP) to 5'-pGpG (PubMed:18344366,
CC       PubMed:19376848). Cannot use cyclic AMP or cyclic GMP
CC       (PubMed:18344366). Part of the RocSAR two-component regulatory
CC       signaling system (also known as the SadARS system), which regulates
CC       biofilm maturation, type III secretion and expression of the cup
CC       fimbrial-gene cluster (PubMed:15659157, PubMed:15687209). Negatively
CC       regulates the expression of cup genes by antagonizing the activity of
CC       RocA1 (PubMed:15659157). {ECO:0000269|PubMed:15659157,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:15687209, ECO:0000269|PubMed:18344366,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:19376848}.
CC   -!- CATALYTIC ACTIVITY:
CC       Reaction=3',3'-c-di-GMP + H2O = 5'-phosphoguanylyl(3'->5')guanosine +
CC         H(+); Xref=Rhea:RHEA:24902, ChEBI:CHEBI:15377, ChEBI:CHEBI:15378,
CC         ChEBI:CHEBI:58754, ChEBI:CHEBI:58805; EC=3.1.4.52;
CC         Evidence={ECO:0000269|PubMed:18344366, ECO:0000269|PubMed:19376848};
CC       PhysiologicalDirection=left-to-right; Xref=Rhea:RHEA:24903;

CC       Name=Mg(2+); Xref=ChEBI:CHEBI:18420;
CC         Evidence={ECO:0000269|PubMed:18344366, ECO:0000269|PubMed:22753070};
CC       Note=Binds 1 Mg(2+) ion per subunit (PubMed:22753070). Can also use
CC       Mn(2+) (PubMed:18344366). {ECO:0000269|PubMed:18344366,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:22753070};
CC   -!- ACTIVITY REGULATION: Phosphorylation of Asp-56 probably induces local
CC       conformational changes in the response regulatory domain
CC       (PubMed:22753070). These structural changes are transmitted to the
CC       adjacent EAL domain, then the signal is further transmitted down to the
CC       active site (PubMed:22753070). The phosphodiesterase activity is
CC       inhibited by Ca(2+) and Zn(2+) (PubMed:18344366). Phosphodiesterase
CC       activity is inhibited by a benzoisothiazolinone derivative that
CC       specifically inhibited RocR, but not some other phosphodiesterases
CC       (PubMed:30034764). {ECO:0000269|PubMed:18344366,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:22753070, ECO:0000269|PubMed:30034764}.

CC       Kinetic parameters:
CC         KM=3.2 uM for c-di-GMP {ECO:0000269|PubMed:18344366};
CC         Note=kcat is 0.67 sec(-1). {ECO:0000269|PubMed:18344366};
CC       pH dependence:
CC         Optimum pH is 8.0-8.5. {ECO:0000269|PubMed:18344366};
CC   -!- SUBUNIT: Homotetramer (PubMed:19851016, PubMed:22753070). Exhibits a
CC       highly unusual tetrameric structure arranged around a single dyad, with
CC       the four subunits adopting two distinctly different conformations, with
CC       only two active sites accessible for substrate binding
CC       (PubMed:22753070). Interacts with RocS1 (PubMed:15659157).
CC       {ECO:0000269|PubMed:15659157, ECO:0000269|PubMed:19851016,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:22753070}.
CC   -!- DOMAIN: Contains an N-terminal phosphoreceiver (REC) domain and a C-
CC       terminal EAL domain that possesses c-di-GMP specific phosphodiesterase
CC       activity (PubMed:19376848, PubMed:22753070). The EAL domain contains a
CC       functional active site loop (loop 6), which plays crucial roles in
CC       stabilizing the overall protein structure and participating in
CC       catalysis, and also controls c-di-GMP and Mg(2+) ion binding
CC       (PubMed:19376848). The response regulatory domain is involved in
CC       interaction with the Hpt domain of RocS1 (PubMed:15659157).
CC       {ECO:0000269|PubMed:15659157, ECO:0000269|PubMed:19376848,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:22753070}.
CC   -!- DISRUPTION PHENOTYPE: Disruption of the gene results in biofilms with
CC       an altered mature structure but does not confer defects in growth or
CC       early biofilm formation, swimming or twitching motility.
CC       {ECO:0000269|PubMed:15687209}.
