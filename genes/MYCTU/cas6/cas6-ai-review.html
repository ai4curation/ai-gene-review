Gene Description

Cas6 (Rv2824c) is the pre-crRNA processing endoribonuclease of the type III-A CRISPR-Cas locus of Mycobacterium tuberculosis H37Rv, lying at one end of the cas6-to-cas2 gene cluster. The CRISPR array is transcribed as a single long precursor in which invader-derived spacers alternate with repeats, and Cas6 converts that precursor into the individual guide RNAs the rest of the system uses, cleaving within each repeat to release one crRNA per spacer. Recognition is structural rather than purely sequence-based: the enzyme requires a stem-loop in the 3' portion of the repeat and is sensitive to the identity of the bases at the base of that stem, so an intact hairpin rather than a bare consensus defines the cleavage site. Substrate binding and catalysis are separable functions of the protein, since substituting the catalytic histidine at position 99 leaves pre-crRNA binding intact while misdirecting cleavage, whereas substituting the glycine-methionine-glycine motif at 295-297 abolishes binding altogether. The products are atypical for a type III-A system. Elsewhere the Cas6 cut is followed by trimming of the crRNA 3' end to a ruler length set by the effector complex; here no such trimming occurs, and the mature species is a uniform molecule of about 71 nucleotides retaining an 8-nucleotide 5' handle and a 28-nucleotide 3' repeat tag with its 10-nucleotide stem-loop still in place, so the guides resemble those of type I systems more than those of their own subtype. Cas6 is a standalone enzyme rather than a retained structural subunit: it hands the mature crRNA on to the Csm effector complex instead of remaining within it, which distinguishes it from the type I-E and type I-F members of the same protein family. Loss of cas6 alone blocks crRNA maturation, and loss of the whole locus reduces resistance to incoming plasmids carrying sequences matching the array.

Core Functions

CRISPR RNA maturation endoribonuclease. Cas6 cleaves within each repeat of the long pre-crRNA transcribed from the CRISPR array, releasing one guide RNA per spacer, with the cut falling 8 nucleotides from the 3' end of the repeat. Specificity is structural: the enzyme requires a stem-loop in the 3' part of the repeat and is sensitive to the identity of the stem-base nucleotides, and substrate binding is a separable function from catalysis, since the H99A substitution misdirects cleavage while retaining pre-crRNA binding and the GMG295-297AMA substitution abolishes binding. Processing is stimulated by divalent cations and inhibited by EDTA, and the measured kinetics (KM 8.395 uM, kcat 12.156 min-1) are those of a characterised enzyme. The products are atypical for a type III-A system: they are not trimmed at the 3' end afterwards, so the mature guide is a uniform 71-nucleotide species retaining both a 5' handle and a full 3' repeat tag with its hairpin. Unlike the type I-E and type I-F members of the same family, Cas6 does not remain as a structural subunit of the complex it supplies; it hands the crRNA to the Csm effector complex and acts catalytically.

Suggested Questions for Experts

Q: Is the divalent-cation requirement a property of the enzyme or of the substrate? Cas6 family enzymes are classically metal independent, yet this one is stimulated by Ca2+ and Mn2+ and inhibited by EDTA, and accurate cleavage depends on a 3' hairpin whose folding those same cations would stabilise. Until a metal site is demonstrated on the protein, no metal-ion-binding annotation is justified.

Q: Should GO have a CRISPR RNA processing term as a child of GO:0006396 RNA processing, alongside the existing tRNA, rRNA, mRNA and snRNA processing children? At present crRNA-maturation enzymes land either on the generic parent or on GO:0043571 maintenance of CRISPR repeat elements, which sits in the DNA-repeat-maintenance branch and so says something different from what these enzymes do.

Q: What are the 5' and 3' end chemistries of the cleavage products? Establishing them would decide between the two mechanistic children of GO:0004521, GO:0016891 and GO:0016892, or show that neither applies because the 3' product carries a 2',3'-cyclic phosphate.

Q: Why is there no 3'-end trimming in this system? Elsewhere in type III-A the ruler mechanism of the effector complex sets a defined crRNA length, so either the H37Rv Csm complex lacks the trimming activity or the retained 3' hairpin blocks it.

Q: Does cas6 contribute to immunity on its own? The reported immunity phenotype is from a whole-locus deletion and is anti-plasmid, so the specific consequence of losing crRNA maturation for invader resistance in M. tuberculosis has not been measured.

Suggested Experiments

Experiment: Assay processing with chelated substrate refolded in monovalent salt and with cobalt-hexammine, which supports RNA folding but cannot act catalytically as a Lewis acid, to separate a metal requirement of the enzyme from one of the substrate hairpin.

Experiment: Map the 5' and 3' termini of the cleavage products by ligation-based sequencing or by testing susceptibility to T4 polynucleotide kinase and to 2',3'-cyclic phosphodiesterase, establishing the end chemistry the ontology children distinguish.

Experiment: Delete cas6 alone in M. tuberculosis or M. bovis BCG and measure resistance to spacer-matching plasmid transformation, separating the contribution of crRNA maturation from that of the rest of the locus.

Experiment: Determine a structure of Cas6 bound to an uncleaved repeat RNA to show how the 3' stem-loop is recognised and why the stem-base sequence matters, and to place the GMG motif at 295-297 relative to the substrate.

Experiment: Test whether Cas6 remains associated with the Csm effector complex by pulling down the complex from H37Rv lysate and assaying for Cas6, confirming that it acts catalytically rather than as a retained subunit as its type I relatives do.

What the protein does

A standalone pre-crRNA processing endoribonuclease. UniProt FUNCTION, ECO:0000269:

Processes pre-crRNA into individual crRNA units; accurate cleavage of pre-crRNA depends
on a 3' stem-loop and the sequence of the bases in the stem. The mature crRNA is unusual
for type III-A systems as it does not undergo 3' processing after Cas6 cleavage. Mature
crRNA is about 71 nucleotides (nt long) with an 8 nt 5' handle and 28 nt repeat with a 10
nt stem-loop at the 3' end.

PMID:29979631

PMID:29979631

The enzyme has measured steady-state kinetics (UniProt BIOPHYSICOCHEMICAL PROPERTIES,
ECO:0000269): KM 8.395 µM for repeat pre-crRNA in 0.125 mM CaCl2, kcat 12.156 min-1. That
is a characterised enzyme, not a putative one.

Standalone, not a retained subunit. This is the point of contrast with the type I
enzymes in the same family. In E. coli K-12 the Cas6e orthologue (casE) is a structural
subunit of the Cascade surveillance complex and stays bound to the crRNA it made, and in
P. aeruginosa PA14 Cas6f/Csy4 likewise remains in the Csy complex. Here the mature crRNA
is handed to the Csm effector complex and Cas6 is not a component of it; UniProt also notes
for the Streptococcus system that Cas6 is "found associated with a subset of the Csm
complex", i.e. transiently rather than stoichiometrically.

Phenotypes, UniProt DISRUPTION PHENOTYPE, ECO:0000269|PubMed:29979631:

Deletion of this gene alone blocks maturation of pre-crRNA. Deletion of the entire
CRISPR-Cas locus (cas6 to cas2, Rv2824c to Rv2816c) decreases resistance to plasmids
encoding spacer elements about 6-fold.

PMID:29979631

Note carefully what the immunity phenotype is: resistance to transformation with plasmids
bearing matching spacers. It is not a phage-restriction assay, and it is a whole-locus
deletion rather than a cas6 deletion. So GO:0051607 defense response to virus is not
annotated here, even though UniProt carries the Antiviral defense keyword; the cas6-specific
phenotype in this organism is loss of crRNA maturation, and the immunity phenotype is
anti-plasmid and locus-wide.

The existing annotation and why it is modified

GOA holds exactly one row for P9WPJ1:

GO:0016788  hydrolase activity, acting on ester bonds  IEA  GO_REF:0000002  InterPro:IPR010156

GO:0016788 is defined as "Catalysis of the hydrolysis of any ester bond" and its only
parent is GO:0016787 hydrolase activity. For a protein whose UniProt name is
"CRISPR-associated endoribonuclease Cas6", whose substrate, cleavage site, catalytic
histidine, kinetics and deletion phenotype are all known, that is several levels too
general — it does not even record that the substrate is a nucleic acid. The InterPro entry
that produced it, IPR010156, is itself named "CRISPR-associated endoribonuclease Cas6", so
the imprecision is in the InterPro2GO mapping rather than in the family assignment.

Replacement: GO:0004521 RNA endonuclease activity — "Catalysis of the cleavage of
ester linkages within ribonucleic acid by creating internal breaks", with parents
GO:0004519 endonuclease activity and GO:0004540 RNA nuclease activity. It records
substrate class (RNA), mode (endonucleolytic) and chemistry, all three of which are
established for this protein.

Comparator check, which confirms this is the conventional term for the family rather than
an invention: E. coli K-12 casE (Cas6e) carries exactly GO:0004521 by IDA
(PMID:18703739), alongside GO:0003723 RNA binding (IDA, PMID:25103409), GO:0099048
CRISPR-cas system (IDA) and GO:0006396 RNA processing (IMP). P. aeruginosa PA14 cas6f
carries GO:0004519 endonuclease activity by the same InterPro route, i.e. one step more
specific than what MYCTU cas6 currently has.

Two more specific children were considered and rejected. GO:0016891 (producing
5'-phosphomonoesters) and GO:0016892 (producing 3'-phosphomonoesters, hydrolytic
mechanism) each assert a product chemistry; the cleavage products of M. tuberculosis Cas6
have not been characterised for their 5'/3' end chemistry in anything available here, and
Cas6-family enzymes typically leave a 2',3'-cyclic phosphate, which matches neither child
cleanly. Asserting either would state a result that has not been reported.

Metal dependence: deliberately not annotated

Processing is ion dependent, Ca2+ and Mn2+ stimulate, and EDTA inhibits (UniProt COFACTOR
and ACTIVITY REGULATION, ECO:0000269). It is tempting to add GO:0046872 metal ion binding,
but there is a competing explanation that the available evidence does not exclude: accurate
cleavage depends on a 3' hairpin and on its stem-base sequence, and divalent cations
stabilise RNA secondary structure. The ion requirement may therefore be a property of the
substrate rather than of the enzyme — which would be notable, since Cas6-family enzymes
are classically metal independent. Without a direct demonstration of metal binding by the
protein, no metal-binding annotation is made, and the question is raised in
suggested_questions instead.

Ontology observation: no term for CRISPR RNA processing

The only available process term for what this enzyme does to RNA is the generic
GO:0006396 RNA processing. GO:0043571 maintenance of CRISPR repeat elements does mention
"transcription of the CRISPR repeat arrays into RNA and processing" in its definition, and
PA14 cas6f carries it, but it sits under GO:0043570 maintenance of DNA repeat elements,
which is the wrong branch for an RNA-maturation step; using it for a processing
endoribonuclease asserts involvement in repeat-array maintenance. A specific "CRISPR RNA
processing" term as a child of GO:0006396 would resolve this, and GO already has
tRNA/rRNA/mRNA/snRNA processing children at that level. Raised as a question rather than
proposed as a term here, because the gap is not specific to this gene.

Decisions

  • GO:0016788 → MODIFY to GO:0004521.
  • Three NEW rows: GO:0003723 RNA binding, GO:0006396 RNA processing, GO:0099048
    CRISPR-cas system.
  • No GO:0051607 and no GO:0046872, for the reasons above.

core_functions:
- description: >-
    CRISPR RNA maturation endoribonuclease. Cas6 cleaves within each repeat of the long
    pre-crRNA transcribed from the CRISPR array, releasing one guide RNA per spacer, with
    the cut falling 8 nucleotides from the 3' end of the repeat. Specificity is structural:
    the enzyme requires a stem-loop in the 3' part of the repeat and is sensitive to the
    identity of the stem-base nucleotides, and substrate binding is a separable function
    from catalysis, since the H99A substitution misdirects cleavage while retaining
    pre-crRNA binding and the GMG295-297AMA substitution abolishes binding. Processing is
    stimulated by divalent cations and inhibited by EDTA, and the measured kinetics
    (KM 8.395 uM, kcat 12.156 min-1) are those of a characterised enzyme. The products are
    atypical for a type III-A system: they are not trimmed at the 3' end afterwards, so the
    mature guide is a uniform 71-nucleotide species retaining both a 5' handle and a full 3'
    repeat tag with its hairpin. Unlike the type I-E and type I-F members of the same family,
    Cas6 does not remain as a structural subunit of the complex it supplies; it hands the
    crRNA to the Csm effector complex and acts catalytically.

suggested_questions:
- question: >-
    Is the divalent-cation requirement a property of the enzyme or of the substrate? Cas6
    family enzymes are classically metal independent, yet this one is stimulated by Ca2+ and
    Mn2+ and inhibited by EDTA, and accurate cleavage depends on a 3' hairpin whose folding
    those same cations would stabilise. Until a metal site is demonstrated on the protein, no
    metal-ion-binding annotation is justified.
- question: >-
    Should GO have a CRISPR RNA processing term as a child of GO:0006396 RNA processing,
    alongside the existing tRNA, rRNA, mRNA and snRNA processing children? At present
    crRNA-maturation enzymes land either on the generic parent or on GO:0043571 maintenance
    of CRISPR repeat elements, which sits in the DNA-repeat-maintenance branch and so says
    something different from what these enzymes do.
- question: >-
    What are the 5' and 3' end chemistries of the cleavage products? Establishing them would
    decide between the two mechanistic children of GO:0004521, GO:0016891 and GO:0016892, or
    show that neither applies because the 3' product carries a 2',3'-cyclic phosphate.
- question: >-
    Why is there no 3'-end trimming in this system? Elsewhere in type III-A the ruler
    mechanism of the effector complex sets a defined crRNA length, so either the H37Rv Csm
    complex lacks the trimming activity or the retained 3' hairpin blocks it.
- question: >-
    Does cas6 contribute to immunity on its own? The reported immunity phenotype is from a
    whole-locus deletion and is anti-plasmid, so the specific consequence of losing crRNA
    maturation for invader resistance in M. tuberculosis has not been measured.
