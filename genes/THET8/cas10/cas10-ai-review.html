Gene Description

Cas10 (Csm1) is the large catalytic subunit of the type III-A Csm effector complex encoded in the CRISPR-Cas locus on plasmid pTT27 of Thermus thermophilus HB8. The Csm complex assembles around a mature CRISPR RNA together with Csm2, Csm3, Csm4 and Csm5, and is licensed by base-pairing of its crRNA to a nascent transcript of the invading element rather than by the invader's DNA. Cas10 is bifunctional and its two activities sit in different domains. An N-terminal HD domain is a metal-dependent, single-strand-specific deoxyribonuclease with both endo- and exonuclease activity that degrades the single-stranded DNA exposed at the locus being transcribed, destroying the invading genome only where it is actively expressed and sparing silent and host DNA. A C-terminal Palm domain, recognised by InterPro and PROSITE as a GGDEF-like fold, polymerises and cyclises ATP into cyclic oligoadenylates, releasing diphosphate; cyclic tetraadenylate is the active species in this organism. The cyclic oligoadenylate is a diffusible second messenger that leaves the effector complex and allosterically switches on the standalone ribonuclease Csm6, so target detection by a small number of complexes is amplified into a cell-wide antiviral state. Both outputs decay as the bound target RNA is cleaved and dissociates, which limits the response in time; the signal itself is removed by cellular and viral ring nucleases.

Proposed New Ontology Terms

cyclic oligoadenylate synthase activity

Definition: Catalysis of the polymerisation and cyclisation of ATP into a cyclic oligoadenylate with release of diphosphate, for example 4 ATP = cyclic tetraadenylate + 4 diphosphate, or 6 ATP = cyclic hexaadenylate + 6 diphosphate. The product is a closed ring of three to six adenosine 5'-monophosphate units joined by 3'-5' phosphodiester bonds, which acts as a second messenger. The activity resides in the Palm (GGDEF-like) domains of the Cas10/Csm1 subunit of type III CRISPR-Cas effector complexes, where it is licensed by binding of a target RNA complementary to the complex's CRISPR RNA.

Justification: No GO molecular-function term describes the synthesis of cyclic oligoadenylate (cA3-cA6) second messengers, which is one of the two catalytic outputs of Cas10 and the step that makes type III CRISPR immunity a two-tier, amplifying response. GO:0001730 2'-5'-oligoadenylate synthetase activity must not be reused for it: that term is defined as "Catalysis of the reaction: ATP = pppA(2'p5'A)n oligomers. This reaction requires the binding of double-stranded RNA", which is the metazoan interferon-induced OAS enzyme. Its product is a linear 2'-5'-linked oligomer that retains a 5'-triphosphate, its trigger is free double-stranded RNA, and it sits under GO:0070566 adenylyltransferase activity. Cas10 instead makes a covalently closed ring of 3'-5'-linked AMP units, releases diphosphate at every step, and is triggered by crRNA-guided recognition of a target RNA inside a ribonucleoprotein complex; the chemistry, the product and the regulation all differ. The natural placement is alongside the bacterial cyclic-dinucleotide synthases, which GO already distinguishes at this granularity and places as direct children of GO:0016779 nucleotidyltransferase activity: GO:0106408 diadenylate cyclase activity ("Catalysis of the reaction: 2 ATP = 3',3'-c-di-AMP + 2 diphosphate") and GO:0052621 diguanylate cyclase activity. A cyclic oligoadenylate synthase term is the missing member of that series. UniProt already records the reaction for this family with Rhea cross-references (RHEA:58280 for cA4, RHEA:58276 for cA6) and ChEBI entities for the products, so the term can be given exact external mappings on creation. The module modules/crispr_cas_adaptive_immunity.yaml carries the Cas10 Palm-domain annoton with no GO id and records the gap, but a module cannot propose a term; this is that proposal. A matching biological-process term for cyclic oligoadenylate signalling, and a cyclic oligoadenylate ring nuclease activity term for the enzymes that destroy the signal, are the companion requests.

Core Functions

Metal-dependent, single-strand-specific deoxyribonuclease housed in the N-terminal HD domain of Cas10. Within the ternary Csm complex, base-pairing of the crRNA to a nascent invader transcript activates the nuclease, which then degrades the single-stranded DNA exposed in the transcription bubble of the locus being transcribed. Activity decays as the target RNA is cleaved and dissociates, and is suppressed when the crRNA 5' handle pairs with the target's 3' flank, so the nuclease acts on actively transcribed invader DNA and not on the host genome.

Suggested Questions for Experts

Q: Has cyclic oligoadenylate synthesis been measured directly on T. thermophilus HB8 Cas10, or is cA4 production in this organism still inferred from the activation of TtCsm6 by chemically supplied cA4? UniProt codes the reaction ECO:0000305 for this entry, and the distinction decides whether the annotation should remain ISS.

Q: Which cyclic oligoadenylate species does TtCas10 actually make? The S. thermophilus enzyme produces a cA3-cA6 mixture, whereas TtCsm6 responds specifically to cA4, so either the Thermus cyclase is more selective or the cell contains a ring nuclease that trims the mixture.

Q: Should GO acquire a cellular-component term for the Csm/Cmr effector complex? Complex membership for every curated Cas subunit currently collapses onto the root term GO:0032991, which loses the distinction between Cascade, Csm and Cmr.

Q: Is a biological-process term for cyclic oligoadenylate signalling warranted, parallel to GO:0061939 c-di-GMP signaling, so that the producer (Cas10), the receiver (Csm6) and the ring nucleases that terminate the signal can be linked in the ontology?

Suggested Experiments

Experiment: Reconstitute the T. thermophilus HB8 Csm complex with mature crRNA and assay cOA synthesis directly on TtCas10 by thin-layer chromatography or LC-MS of the ATP reaction products, in the presence and absence of a cognate target RNA, to replace the ECO:0000305 inference with a measurement in this organism.

Experiment: Mutate the Thermus GGDEF-domain catalytic aspartate pair, the residues equivalent to the S. thermophilus D575/D576 whose substitution abolishes cOA synthesis while leaving the ssDNase intact, and confirm the two activities are separable in TtCas10 as they are in the Streptococcus enzyme.

Experiment: Delete cas10 and the HD-domain catalytic residue separately in T. thermophilus HB8 and challenge with a phage carrying a protospacer, distinguishing the contribution of DNA degradation from that of cOA-driven Csm6 activation to the immunity phenotype.

Experiment: Determine the stoichiometry and structure of the Thermus Csm complex by cryo-EM with a bound target RNA, to establish whether the Palm domains adopt the same target-RNA-licensed conformation seen in other type III-A systems.

The two separable activities of Cas10

Type III effectors are licensed by target RNA, not by target DNA, and target-RNA binding
switches on two chemically unrelated activities that sit in different domains of the same
protein:

PMID:28663439

PMID:31942067

(a) HD-domain single-strand-specific DNase

UniProt assigns this to Q53W19 by similarity to UniProtKB:B6YWB8, and the activating
logic by similarity to the S. thermophilus orthologue UniProtKB:A0A0A7HFE1:

FUNCTION: This subunit is a single-strand-specific deoxyribonuclease (ssDNase) which
digests both linear and circular ssDNA; it has both exo- and endonuclease activity.

FUNCTION: ssDNase activity is stimulated in the ternary Csm effector complex; binding
of cognate target RNA activates the ssDNase, as the target RNA is degraded ssDNA
activity decreases. {ECO:0000250|UniProtKB:A0A0A7HFE1}

The experimental grounding for that behaviour is in the S. thermophilus system, not in
Thermus — see genes/STRTR/cas10/cas10-notes.md:

PMID:27105119

Because the protein has both endo- and exonuclease activity on single-stranded DNA, two
MF terms apply rather than one: GO:0000014 (single-stranded DNA endonuclease activity)
and GO:0008297 (single-stranded DNA exodeoxyribonuclease activity).

(b) Palm/GGDEF-domain cyclic oligoadenylate synthase

PMID:28663439

PMID:28722012

UniProt records the reaction for this entry with a Rhea cross-reference but an
inferential evidence code (ECO:0000305, i.e. curator inference from the two 2017 papers,
not a measurement on TtCas10):

CATALYTIC ACTIVITY: Reaction=4 ATP = cyclic tetraadenylate + 4 diphosphate;
Xref=Rhea:RHEA:58280 ... Evidence={ECO:0000305|PubMed:28663439,
ECO:0000305|PubMed:28722012}

FUNCTION: ... The active cOA in this bacteria is cyclic tetraadenylate (cA4),
presumably made by this enzyme. {ECO:0000305|PubMed:28663439,
ECO:0000305|PubMed:28722012}

Organism discipline. The cOA-synthesis assay in PMID:28663439 was done on the
Streptococcus thermophilus Csm complex, and the Thermus contribution of that paper is
the demonstration that cA4 activates T. thermophilus Csm6 (TtCsm6, Q53W17). What is
established in Thermus is therefore that cA4 is the active signal received by TtCsm6;
that TtCas10 is the enzyme that makes it is UniProt's inference (ECO:0000305), and the
annotations below are coded ISS accordingly. The demonstrated-synthesis exemplar is
A0A0A7HFE1 (STRTR), reviewed separately.

Ontology gap: no GO term for cyclic oligoadenylate synthase activity

This is the substantive curation problem for this gene.

  • GO:0001730 2'-5'-oligoadenylate synthetase activity is not reusable. Its
    definition is "Catalysis of the reaction: ATP = pppA(2'p5'A)n oligomers. This reaction
    requires the binding of double-stranded RNA." That is the metazoan interferon-induced
    OAS enzyme: the product is a linear 2'-5'-linked oligomer retaining a 5'-triphosphate,
    and the trigger is dsRNA. Cas10 makes a cyclic ring of 3'-5'-linked AMP units and is
    triggered by crRNA-guided target-RNA binding inside a ribonucleoprotein complex. The
    parentage differs too: GO:0001730 sits under GO:0070566 adenylyltransferase activity.
  • GO does have sibling terms for the analogous bacterial cyclic-dinucleotide synthases,
    and both are direct children of GO:0016779 nucleotidyltransferase activity:
    GO:0106408 diadenylate cyclase activity ("Catalysis of the reaction: 2 ATP =
    3',3'-c-di-AMP + 2 diphosphate") and GO:0052621 diguanylate cyclase activity. A
    cyclic oligoadenylate synthase term is the missing member of that series, not a
    variant of the OAS term.
  • modules/crispr_cas_adaptive_immunity.yaml (annoton cas10_coa_synthesis) deliberately
    carries no GO id for this function and records the gap under knowledge_gaps; a module
    cannot itself propose a term, so the proposal is made here.

Handled per CLAUDE.md "When a core activity has no GO term yet": a top-level
proposed_new_terms entry plus proposed_molecular_function on the core function, with
molecular_function left unset.

Comparator check for the process terms

GO:0099048 CRISPR-cas system is the established process term for Cas proteins that do
part of the work of the three CRISPR stages; its definition names "acquisition of foreign
DNA by integration into CRISPR loci in the host chromosome, CRISPR RNA (crRNA)
biogenesis, and target interference". Cas10 performs target interference, so it is a
participant, not merely a requirement. The comparator check confirms the convention:
every curated E. coli K-12 Cascade subunit and adaptation protein in this repository
carries GO:0099048 (genes/ECOLI/casA, casB, casC, casD, casE, ygbT/cas1,
ygbF/cas2 — IDA, PMID:18703739), and casA/casE additionally carry GO:0051607 defense
response to virus (IMP, same paper) and GO:0032991 protein-containing complex (IDA,
PMID:25103409). Cas10 is annotated on the same pattern here.

No GO cellular-component term exists for the Csm/Cmr effector complex, so complex
membership is recorded at GO:0032991, which is exactly what the Cascade subunits carry.

Decisions

  • No existing annotations to adjudicate (GOA empty); all rows are NEW, coded ISS with
    the orthologue carried in supporting_entities, matching UniProt's own ECO:0000250/0305
    evidence for this entry.
  • Two core functions: the HD ssDNase and the Palm cOA synthase, the latter via
    proposed_molecular_function.
  • Biotechnology applications of type III systems are deliberately absent; they are not
    functions of the gene in its native host.

proposed_new_terms:
- proposed_name: cyclic oligoadenylate synthase activity
  proposed_definition: >-
    Catalysis of the polymerisation and cyclisation of ATP into a cyclic oligoadenylate
    with release of diphosphate, for example 4 ATP = cyclic tetraadenylate + 4
    diphosphate, or 6 ATP = cyclic hexaadenylate + 6 diphosphate. The product is a closed
    ring of three to six adenosine 5'-monophosphate units joined by 3'-5' phosphodiester
    bonds, which acts as a second messenger. The activity resides in the Palm
    (GGDEF-like) domains of the Cas10/Csm1 subunit of type III CRISPR-Cas effector
    complexes, where it is licensed by binding of a target RNA complementary to the
    complex's CRISPR RNA.
  justification: >-
    No GO molecular-function term describes the synthesis of cyclic oligoadenylate
    (cA3-cA6) second messengers, which is one of the two catalytic outputs of Cas10 and
    the step that makes type III CRISPR immunity a two-tier, amplifying response.
    GO:0001730 2'-5'-oligoadenylate synthetase activity must not be reused for it: that
    term is defined as "Catalysis of the reaction: ATP = pppA(2'p5'A)n oligomers. This
    reaction requires the binding of double-stranded RNA", which is the metazoan
    interferon-induced OAS enzyme. Its product is a linear 2'-5'-linked oligomer that
    retains a 5'-triphosphate, its trigger is free double-stranded RNA, and it sits under
    GO:0070566 adenylyltransferase activity. Cas10 instead makes a covalently closed ring
    of 3'-5'-linked AMP units, releases diphosphate at every step, and is triggered by
    crRNA-guided recognition of a target RNA inside a ribonucleoprotein complex; the
    chemistry, the product and the regulation all differ. The natural placement is
    alongside the bacterial cyclic-dinucleotide synthases, which GO already distinguishes
    at this granularity and places as direct children of GO:0016779 nucleotidyltransferase
    activity: GO:0106408 diadenylate cyclase activity ("Catalysis of the reaction: 2 ATP =
    3',3'-c-di-AMP + 2 diphosphate") and GO:0052621 diguanylate cyclase activity. A cyclic
    oligoadenylate synthase term is the missing member of that series. UniProt already
    records the reaction for this family with Rhea cross-references (RHEA:58280 for cA4,
    RHEA:58276 for cA6) and ChEBI entities for the products, so the term can be given
    exact external mappings on creation. The module
    modules/crispr_cas_adaptive_immunity.yaml carries the Cas10 Palm-domain annoton with
    no GO id and records the gap, but a module cannot propose a term; this is that
    proposal. A matching biological-process term for cyclic oligoadenylate signalling,
    and a cyclic oligoadenylate ring nuclease activity term for the enzymes that destroy
    the signal, are the companion requests.
