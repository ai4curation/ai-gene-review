Gene Description

Csm6 is an accessory ribonuclease of the type III-A CRISPR-Cas locus carried on plasmid pTT27 of Thermus thermophilus HB8. Unlike the Cas proteins encoded alongside it, Csm6 is not a subunit of the Csm effector complex: it is a free, twisted head-to-head homodimer that is physically uncoupled from the machinery that recognises invading nucleic acid, and it learns that a target has been detected only through a diffusible chemical signal. The protein has two domains with distinct jobs. The C-terminal HEPN domain carries the catalysis; dimerisation juxtaposes the two HEPN domains and builds a single composite active site at their interface, whose floor is lined by the invariant arginine, asparagine and histidine of the R-X4-6-H motif. The enzyme is a single-strand-specific endoribonuclease that needs no metal cofactor and cleaves by transesterification, the ribose 2'-hydroxyl attacking the scissile phosphate to leave a free 5'-hydroxyl on one product and a 2',3'-cyclic phosphate on the other. The N-terminal CARF domain carries no catalysis at all and can be deleted entirely without loss of nuclease activity; instead the two CARF domains form a conserved electropositive pocket at their shared interface that binds the second messenger. Cyclic tetraadenylate, synthesised from ATP by the Cas10 subunit of the effector complex once that complex has engaged a target transcript, binds this pocket and allosterically stimulates the nuclease roughly a thousandfold; linear oligoadenylates of the same length do not, so recognition is of the closed ring rather than of adenylate as such. The consequence is an amplifier with a safety catch: on its own Csm6 is nearly inert, and only in the presence of the signal does it begin cleaving RNA indiscriminately, degrading invader transcripts and host RNA alike to impose a transient antiviral state. The response is self-terminating because ring nucleases, both cellular and virus-encoded, destroy the signal.

Proposed New Ontology Terms

cyclic oligoadenylate binding

Definition: Binding to a cyclic oligoadenylate, a closed ring of three to six adenosine 5'-monophosphate units joined by 3'-5' phosphodiester bonds, such as cyclic tetraadenylate or cyclic hexaadenylate. Cyclic oligoadenylates are second messengers synthesised from ATP by the Cas10 subunit of type III CRISPR-Cas effector complexes, and are bound by the CRISPR-associated Rossmann fold (CARF) domains of accessory effectors and of the ring nucleases that degrade them.

Justification: GO discriminates cyclic-nucleotide ligands at exactly this granularity and already has the two bacterial cyclic-dinucleotide cases, both as children of GO:0030551 cyclic nucleotide binding: GO:0035438 cyclic-di-GMP binding and GO:0180001 cyclic-di-AMP binding. There is no term for cyclic oligoadenylate, the ligand of every CARF-domain effector in type III CRISPR systems, so the series is simply missing its third member. The parent cannot be used in its place: GO:0030551 is defined as "Binding to a cyclic nucleotide, a nucleotide in which the phosphate group is in diester linkage to two positions on the sugar residue", which describes an intramolecular ring on a single ribose as in cAMP and cGMP. In a cyclic oligoadenylate each phosphate bridges two different riboses, so the molecule no more satisfies that definition than c-di-GMP does, which is precisely why GO:0035438 exists as a term rather than as an annotation at the parent. The function is the defining property of this gene product: the CARF domain performs no catalysis, can be deleted without loss of nuclease activity, and exists solely to recognise this ligand and transmit the signal to the HEPN active site, a roughly thousandfold stimulation that is specific for the cyclic and not the linear oligoadenylate. Note what is deliberately not proposed: the activated state itself needs no term, because the chemistry Csm6 performs once switched on is ordinary RNA endonuclease activity (GO:0004521) and allosteric activation is a property of the regulation rather than a distinct molecular function. The companion request is a cyclic oligoadenylate synthase activity term for the Cas10 Palm domain that makes the ligand (see genes/STRTR/cas10 and genes/THET8/cas10), and a cyclic oligoadenylate ring nuclease activity term for the enzymes that destroy it.

Core Functions

Metal-independent, single-strand-specific endoribonuclease. The protein is an obligate head-to-head homodimer and the active site is composite, assembled at the interface of the two C-terminal HEPN domains, with the invariant R-X4-6-H residues Arg415, Asn416 and His422 lining the cleft floor; substituting any of them, or the nearby Glu332, abolishes activity. Cleavage proceeds by transesterification, the ribose 2'-hydroxyl attacking the scissile phosphate to leave a free 5'-hydroxyl on one product and a 2',3'-cyclic phosphate on the other, which is why no divalent metal is needed and why EDTA does not inhibit. The activity is non-specific with respect to sequence and acts on host as well as invader RNA, and in the resting protein it is essentially held off: it is the cyclic-oligoadenylate-bound state, not the apo state, that does the work in vivo.

GO has no term for binding a cyclic oligoadenylate, so the proposed term is named here rather than forcing GO:0030551, whose definition covers only intramolecular cyclic mononucleotides.

Suggested Questions for Experts

Q: Is the cyclic tetraadenylate site of TtCsm6 the CARF dimer-interface pocket seen in the apo structure? UniProt codes the domain assignment for this protein ECO:0000305, and a co-crystal or cryo-EM structure with cA4 bound would convert it to direct evidence.

Q: Can the two reports be reconciled, one finding that only cyclic and not linear tetraadenylate activates TtCsm6 and the other finding stimulation by linear tetraadenylate at very high concentration without testing cA4? UniProt records both without choosing between them.

Q: Does GO need a mechanistic child of GO:0004521 for HEPN-type cleavage, which leaves a free 5'-hydroxyl and a 2',3'-cyclic phosphate by transesterification? Neither existing child fits: GO:0016891 names 5'-phosphomonoesters, which is excluded here, and GO:0016892 names 3'-phosphomonoesters and a hydrolytic mechanism, neither of which is what the paper reports.

Q: Does T. thermophilus encode a ring nuclease that terminates the cyclic oligoadenylate signal? Without one, an activated Csm6 would be expected to degrade host RNA indefinitely, and the self-limiting character of the response would be unexplained in this organism.

Suggested Experiments

Experiment: Determine the structure of TtCsm6 in complex with cyclic tetraadenylate, and compare it with the apo form, to establish the binding site and the conformational route from the CARF interface to the HEPN active site.

Experiment: Repeat the activation assay side by side with chemically defined cA3, cA4, cA5, cA6 and the corresponding linear oligoadenylates across a wide concentration range, resolving the discordance between PMID:28663439 and PMID:28722012 on linear ligands.

Experiment: Introduce CARF-pocket substitutions that abolish cA4 binding but leave the dimer intact, and test whether they uncouple nuclease activity from target recognition in vivo, as the equivalent mutations do in other type III-A systems.

Experiment: Measure host transcriptome decay by RNA-seq after triggering the Thermus Csm system with a cognate target, to determine how indiscriminate the activated Csm6 really is and whether and how quickly the cell recovers.

Ontology gap: no term for cyclic oligoadenylate binding

GO discriminates cyclic-nucleotide ligands at exactly this granularity and already has the
two bacterial dinucleotide cases, both placed under GO:0030551 cyclic nucleotide binding:
GO:0035438 cyclic-di-GMP binding and GO:0180001 cyclic-di-AMP binding. There is no
corresponding term for cyclic oligoadenylate (cA3-cA6), the ligand of every CARF-domain
effector in type III CRISPR systems. GO:0030551 itself cannot simply be used: its
definition is "Binding to a cyclic nucleotide, a nucleotide in which the phosphate group is
in diester linkage to two positions on the sugar residue", which describes cAMP and cGMP —
one sugar, an intramolecular ring. In cA4 each phosphate bridges two different riboses,
so the molecule does not satisfy that definition any more than c-di-GMP does, which is
precisely why GO:0035438 had to be created as a term in its own right rather than annotated
at the parent.

A cyclic oligoadenylate binding term is therefore the missing third member of an existing
series, and it is proposed here. The activated state itself does not need a term: the
chemistry Csm6 performs when switched on is ordinary RNA endonuclease activity, and
"allosterically activated" is a property of the regulation rather than a distinct molecular
function, so it belongs in prose and in ACTIVITY REGULATION-style commentary. What is
genuinely unrepresentable in GO today is the ligand-recognition function of the CARF domain.

modules/crispr_cas_adaptive_immunity.yaml (annoton csm6_coa_activated_rnase) assigns
GO:0004521 for the catalysis and records the cOA-activated state among its knowledge gaps.

Comparator check for the process terms

Csm6 performs chemistry that is part of target interference — it degrades invader RNA
transcripts — so it is a participant in the process and not merely required for it. Every
curated Cas protein of the E. coli K-12 type I-E system in this repository carries
GO:0099048 CRISPR-cas system (casA, casB, casC, casD, casE, cas1/ygbT, cas2/ygbF; IDA,
PMID:18703739), and casA/casE additionally carry GO:0051607 defense response to virus
(IMP). Csm6 is annotated on that pattern. Both are coded conservatively: the immunity
requirement has been demonstrated for Csm6 orthologues in other species (anti-plasmid
immunity in the type III-A systems of PMID:26763118's introduction, and the in vivo CARF
mutants of PMID:28722012), not in T. thermophilus HB8, so ISS and IC are used rather than
IMP.

Decisions

  • GOA empty, so every row is NEW. The catalytic rows are IDA (measured on this protein in
    PMID:26763118); the process rows are ISS/IC.
  • GO:0004521 RNA endonuclease activity, not GO:0016891 or GO:0016892, for the reasons
    above.
  • A second core function records the CARF domain's cOA-sensing role via
    proposed_molecular_function, because the activation dependency is the point of the

- description: >-
    Metal-independent, single-strand-specific endoribonuclease. The protein is an obligate
    head-to-head homodimer and the active site is composite, assembled at the interface of
    the two C-terminal HEPN domains, with the invariant R-X4-6-H residues Arg415, Asn416

proposed_new_terms:
- proposed_name: cyclic oligoadenylate binding
  proposed_definition: >-
    Binding to a cyclic oligoadenylate, a closed ring of three to six adenosine
    5'-monophosphate units joined by 3'-5' phosphodiester bonds, such as cyclic
    tetraadenylate or cyclic hexaadenylate. Cyclic oligoadenylates are second messengers
    synthesised from ATP by the Cas10 subunit of type III CRISPR-Cas effector complexes,
    and are bound by the CRISPR-associated Rossmann fold (CARF) domains of accessory
    effectors and of the ring nucleases that degrade them.
  justification: >-
    GO discriminates cyclic-nucleotide ligands at exactly this granularity and already has
    the two bacterial cyclic-dinucleotide cases, both as children of GO:0030551 cyclic
    nucleotide binding: GO:0035438 cyclic-di-GMP binding and GO:0180001 cyclic-di-AMP
    binding. There is no term for cyclic oligoadenylate, the ligand of every CARF-domain
    effector in type III CRISPR systems, so the series is simply missing its third member.
    The parent cannot be used in its place: GO:0030551 is defined as "Binding to a cyclic
    nucleotide, a nucleotide in which the phosphate group is in diester linkage to two
    positions on the sugar residue", which describes an intramolecular ring on a single
    ribose as in cAMP and cGMP. In a cyclic oligoadenylate each phosphate bridges two
    different riboses, so the molecule no more satisfies that definition than c-di-GMP
    does, which is precisely why GO:0035438 exists as a term rather than as an annotation
    at the parent. The function is the defining property of this gene product: the CARF
    domain performs no catalysis, can be deleted without loss of nuclease activity, and
    exists solely to recognise this ligand and transmit the signal to the HEPN active site,
    a roughly thousandfold stimulation that is specific for the cyclic and not the linear
    oligoadenylate. Note what is deliberately not proposed: the activated state itself

    regulation rather than a distinct molecular function. The companion request is a cyclic
    oligoadenylate synthase activity term for the Cas10 Palm domain that makes the ligand
    (see genes/STRTR/cas10 and genes/THET8/cas10), and a cyclic oligoadenylate ring
