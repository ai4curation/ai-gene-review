Gene Description

DafA is a small regulatory assembly protein of the Thermus DnaK-DnaJ2 chaperone system. It forms an inhibited complex by occupying the DnaK client-binding pocket and contacting the J domain in a way that prevents productive docking. The structurally characterized complex contains two copies each of DnaK, DnaJ2 and DafA. DafA regulates access to the folding machinery rather than supplying ATPase or nucleotide-exchange activity.

Proposed New Ontology Terms

DnaK-DnaJ chaperone inhibitor activity

Definition: Binding to the DnaK-DnaJ chaperone machinery and reducing its productive protein-folding activity by blocking client access and productive J-domain docking.

Justification: The proposed activity describes inhibition of productive chaperone function through both client-pocket occupancy and prevention of J-domain docking. GO:0042030 ATPase inhibitor activity describes only the nucleotide-hydrolysis consequence, not the full client-access mechanism. The primary article interprets docking blockade as suppressing ATPase activity; this is acknowledged, while a separate quantitative wild-type ATPase-inhibition assay was not identified in the inspected study. No direct inhibition is claimed absent, and no redundant ATPase-inhibitor proposal is added.

Parent term: molecular function inhibitor activity

Supporting Evidence:

  • PMID:41092901 : DafA inhibits the Hsp70-Hsp40 machinery through two distinct mechanisms: it blocks access of protein clients to Hsp70 and it prevents the docking of the J domain onto Hsp70, thereby suppressing its ATPase activity.
  • PMID:41092901 : We have used SEC-MALS to show that the complex has a molecular mass of 210 kDa consisting of a 2:2:2 stoichiometry.
