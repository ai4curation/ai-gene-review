Gene Description

Cas12a (Cpf1) is the single-protein effector of the class 2, type V-A CRISPR-Cas adaptive immune system of Acidaminococcus sp. BV3L6. The 1307-residue protein is bilobed: an alpha-helical recognition lobe and a nuclease lobe, joined by a wedge domain assembled from three dispersed segments, enclose a positively charged central channel that holds the CRISPR RNA (crRNA) and the crRNA-target DNA heteroduplex. Loaded with a single crRNA and needing no tracrRNA, it recognises a 5' T-rich protospacer adjacent motif (TTTN in this organism) by a combination of base and shape readout, unwinds the adjacent duplex, forms an R-loop in which the crRNA spacer base-pairs with the target strand, and cleaves both strands to leave a staggered double-strand break with a 4-base 5' overhang 19 and 22 bases downstream of the PAM on the non-target and target strands respectively. Cleavage of both strands occurs in a single RuvC-type active site, built from three dispersed RuvC motifs and requiring Mg2+; the protein has no HNH domain. Once the enzyme is engaged by a complementary target it also becomes a substrate-non-specific single-stranded DNase, degrading unrelated single-stranded DNA from the same catalytic site. Like other Cas12a enzymes it matures its own guide, cleaving precursor crRNA upstream of a hairpin in the CRISPR repeat without a dedicated Cas6-type ribonuclease. The biological output is spacer-programmed destruction of invading DNA: in a heterologous host the enzyme blocks transformation by matching plasmids and reduces plaque formation by several bacteriophages, with phages whose genomic cytosines are glycosylated escaping because their DNA is not recognised. Because specificity is set by the spacer content of the CRISPR array rather than by the protein sequence, it is heritable and can change without any change to the enzyme.

Proposed New Ontology Terms

guide RNA-dependent nucleic acid endonuclease activity

Definition: Catalysis of the cleavage of ester linkages within a nucleic acid molecule by creating internal breaks, where the position of cleavage is determined by base pairing between the substrate and a separate guide RNA bound non-covalently by the enzyme. The guide RNA is neither substrate nor product; it programs the sequence specificity of an enzyme whose active site is otherwise sequence-non-specific, so that the specificity of the catalysis is a property of the bound guide rather than of the protein.

Justification: GO has no molecular-function term for guide-RNA-programmed target recognition or cleavage. Checked against the current GO release: no molecular-function label contains "RNA-guided", "guide RNA" or "CRISPR". The consequence is that every CRISPR effector, and every other guide-dependent nuclease, can only be annotated with a term that describes its chemistry (GO:0004520, GO:0004521, GO:0004530, GO:1990238) and is therefore indistinguishable in GO from an unguided nuclease of the same chemistry. For Cas12a this is the single most important thing about the protein, since the sequence it cuts is set by the spacer content of the CRISPR array, is heritable, and changes without any change to the protein. Two children are needed, guide RNA-dependent DNA endonuclease activity (Cas9 and Cas12 families) and guide RNA-dependent RNA endonuclease activity (Cas13 and type III effectors), and the parent would also accommodate class 1 multi-subunit effectors through contributes_to. This request is deliberately scoped to the nuclease function rather than to recognition in general, so that catalytically dead guide-loaded effectors are handled by a binding term instead. This is the same request as in the reviews of A0Q7Q2, P0DOC6 and C7NBY4 and should be merged with them.

Core Functions

Loaded with a single mature crRNA and needing no tracrRNA, recognises a 5' TTTN protospacer adjacent motif in duplex DNA by combined base and shape readout, unwinds the adjacent duplex, forms an R-loop in which the crRNA spacer base-pairs with the target strand, and introduces internal breaks in both strands to leave a staggered double-strand break with a 4-base 5' overhang 19 and 22 bases downstream of the PAM. Both strands are cut in one RuvC-type active site assembled from three dispersed motifs, with Mg2+ as cofactor and no HNH domain. The sequence cut is set by the crRNA rather than by the protein, so GO's nuclease terms capture the chemistry but not the property that defines the enzyme.

Suggested Questions for Experts

Q: Is the Acidaminococcus sp. BV3L6 type V-A system active in its own host, and what are its native spacers? Every interference measurement on this protein has been made in a heterologous E. coli reconstitution with engineered spacers, so what this system defends against in Acidaminococcus is unknown.

Q: Does target-activated trans cleavage of single-stranded DNA contribute anything to immunity in vivo, or is it only a consequence of leaving an activated RuvC site exposed? Because cis and trans cleavage share the active site, a clean separation-of-function mutant may not exist, which is what makes the question hard rather than merely untested.

Q: Why do AcrVA4 and AcrVA5 fail against AsCas12a while inhibiting the Moraxella and Lachnospiraceae orthologs? The specificity difference is reported but its structural basis is unresolved, and it bears on which Cas12a-carrying hosts a given phage can invade.

Q: Should GO's molecular-function hierarchy acquire a guide-RNA-dependent branch, and should the cis and trans activities of one RuvC site be modelled as two functions or as one function with two substrate states?

Suggested Experiments

Experiment: Delete cas12a and the CRISPR array in Acidaminococcus sp. BV3L6 itself, or in a close relative that is genetically tractable, and challenge with phages and conjugative plasmids from the same habitat, to establish what the system defends against natively rather than what it can be programmed to cut.

Experiment: Measure crRNA maturation by purified AsCas12a directly, by incubating it with its own in vitro transcribed pre-crRNA and mapping the 5' and 3' ends of the products, to replace the current By-similarity assignment of EC 4.6.1.22 with direct evidence.

Experiment: Compare phage survival in hosts expressing wild-type AsCas12a against hosts expressing a variant with reduced trans-ssDNase but intact cis cleavage, if such a variant can be engineered, to test whether collateral cleavage contributes to immunity or is incidental.

Experiment: Systematically test AsCas12a interference against phages carrying hypermodified genomes beyond T4, to map how broadly DNA modification lets invaders escape type V-A immunity and whether the escape is at recognition or at cleavage.

2. Structure and the single-vs-two-active-site question

The 2.8 Å ternary structure is of this very protein:
PMID:27114038 and
PMID:27114038, with PAM readout by both base and shape:
PMID:27114038.

Yamano et al. proposed that RuvC and a second "Nuc" domain cut the two strands separately:
PMID:27114038. UniProt's DOMAIN block for U2UMQ6 still follows that reading
("One nuclease site is found in the discontinuous RuvC domain ... the other in the NUC domain").

The field has since converged on a single RuvC site that cuts both strands sequentially, on the
basis of FnCas12a mutagenesis PMID:28431230 and the E1006Q result
PMID:28431230, and this is how reviews
now state it PMID:30936531.

Curation consequence: one DNase molecular function, not two. The cis dsDNA break and the trans
ssDNA cleavage come from the same site and are distinguished by substrate and by activation state,
not by catalytic machinery — which is exactly why they are recorded as two core_functions entries
rather than two independent active sites.

4. Native biological role

Unlike the Francisella entry, this one has phage-challenge data, from a Cas12m paper that used
AsCas12a as its positive control:
PMID:38261981, with the T4 exception explained by DNA
modification PMID:38261981. UniProt records the same: "Protects E.coli against
plasmids and bacteriophage M13mp18, phage T4 with hydroxymethyl or unmodified (but not glycosylated)
cytosines and to a lesser extent against lambda and VpaE1 phage".

That supports both GO:0099048 CRISPR-cas system and GO:0051607 defense response to virus for
this entry. Both are NEW, since GOA holds no BP annotation at all.

The glycosylated-T4 escape is worth recording as biology rather than as a caveat: it shows that
interference depends on the chemical accessibility of the invader's DNA, which is a genuine limit on
the system and the reason phages carrying hypermodified genomes are resistant.

Both are MODIFY → GO:1990238 double-stranded DNA endonuclease activity, for the same reason as in
the Francisella review: GO:0004530 is the GO rendering of EC 3.1.21.1, deoxyribonuclease I, a
non-specific nuclease, and its definition carries DNase I's product specification
("5'-phosphodinucleotide and 5'-phosphooligonucleotide end products") rather than anything true of
Cas12a. GO:1990238 says what Cas12a does to a duplex target and says nothing false.

The ISS row is not challenged as a propagation — A0Q7Q2 is the right source and the orthology is not
in doubt, and the project rule is to keep a legitimate transfer. What is wrong is only the term, and
it is wrong at the source as well as at the target.

EC 4.6.1.22 has again produced no GOA row, so the crRNA-processing activity is missing entirely;
added as NEW with GO:0004521 RNA endonuclease activity. Rationale for that term rather than
GO:0016891/GO:0016892: those two stipulate a hydrolytic mechanism and this reaction is a
metal-independent transesterification leaving a 2',3'-cyclic phosphate
PMID:28431230. GO:0004521 is mechanism-agnostic
and sits under GO:0004519/GO:0004540, not under a hydrolase parent.

6. What is deliberately NOT asserted

  • Genome editing. UniProt's BIOTECHNOLOGY block is long and the engineered PAM variants
    (PMID:28595896) are a large part of this entry's literature. None of it is a function of the gene
    in its own organism.
  • A guide-RNA-recognition molecular function. No such GO term exists; verified against the
    current GO release (no MF label contains "RNA-guided", "guide RNA" or "CRISPR"). Raised in
    proposed_new_terms, with text identical to the Francisella review so the two requests can be
    merged rather than duplicated.
  • GO:0003677 DNA binding / GO:0003723 RNA binding. True, keyworded in UniProt, and
    uninformative: they do not separate Cas12a from any nucleic-acid-binding protein. The informative
    version of the claim is the missing guide-dependence term.
  • A trans-cleavage-specific process term. Considered and rejected. Asking which entity performs
    the step gives the right answer here — Cas12a does the cleaving — but the step is already named by
    the molecular function GO:0000014, and there is no demonstration that collateral ssDNA cleavage
    produces any distinct cellular outcome in a Cas12a host. (It does in Cas13a hosts, where dormancy
    is measurable; see the Cas13a notes.) Proposing a process term for an activity whose physiological
    consequence has not been observed would be asserting a pathway from an in vitro property.

7. Open questions recorded in the review

  • Is the Acidaminococcus BV3L6 type V-A system active in its own host, and what are its native
    spacers? All interference data are heterologous.
  • Does target-activated trans-ssDNase cleavage contribute to immunity in vivo, or is it only a
    consequence of an exposed RuvC site in vitro? The honest test is a separation-of-function mutant,
    which may not exist given that cis and trans share the site.
  • Why does AcrVA4/AcrVA5 inhibition spare AsCas12a while AcrVA1 does not? PMID:30936531 reports the
    specificity difference but the structural basis for the As resistance is unresolved.

description: >-
  Cas12a (Cpf1) is the single-protein effector of the class 2, type V-A CRISPR-Cas
  adaptive immune system of Acidaminococcus sp. BV3L6. The 1307-residue protein is
  bilobed: an alpha-helical recognition lobe and a nuclease lobe, joined by a wedge
  domain assembled from three dispersed segments, enclose a positively charged central
  channel that holds the CRISPR RNA (crRNA) and the crRNA-target DNA heteroduplex.
  Loaded with a single crRNA and needing no tracrRNA, it recognises a 5' T-rich
  protospacer adjacent motif (TTTN in this organism) by a combination of base and shape
  readout, unwinds the adjacent duplex, forms an R-loop in which the crRNA spacer
  base-pairs with the target strand, and cleaves both strands to leave a staggered
  double-strand break with a 4-base 5' overhang 19 and 22 bases downstream of the PAM on
  the non-target and target strands respectively. Cleavage of both strands occurs in a
  single RuvC-type active site, built from three dispersed RuvC motifs and requiring
  Mg2+; the protein has no HNH domain. Once the enzyme is engaged by a complementary
  target it also becomes a substrate-non-specific single-stranded DNase, degrading
  unrelated single-stranded DNA from the same catalytic site. Like other Cas12a enzymes
  it matures its own guide, cleaving precursor crRNA upstream of a hairpin in the CRISPR
  repeat without a dedicated Cas6-type ribonuclease. The biological output is
  spacer-programmed destruction of invading DNA: in a heterologous host the enzyme blocks
  transformation by matching plasmids and reduces plaque formation by several
  bacteriophages, with phages whose genomic cytosines are glycosylated escaping because
  their DNA is not recognised. Because specificity is set by the spacer content of the
  CRISPR array rather than by the protein sequence, it is heritable and can change without
  any change to the enzyme.

proposed_new_terms:
- proposed_name: guide RNA-dependent nucleic acid endonuclease activity
  proposed_definition: >-
    Catalysis of the cleavage of ester linkages within a nucleic acid molecule by creating
    internal breaks, where the position of cleavage is determined by base pairing between
    the substrate and a separate guide RNA bound non-covalently by the enzyme. The guide RNA
    is neither substrate nor product; it programs the sequence specificity of an enzyme whose
    active site is otherwise sequence-non-specific, so that the specificity of the catalysis
    is a property of the bound guide rather than of the protein.
  proposed_parent:
    id: GO:0004519
    label: endonuclease activity
  justification: >-
    GO has no molecular-function term for guide-RNA-programmed target recognition or
    cleavage. Checked against the current GO release: no molecular-function label contains
    "RNA-guided", "guide RNA" or "CRISPR". The consequence is that every CRISPR effector,
    and every other guide-dependent nuclease, can only be annotated with a term that
    describes its chemistry (GO:0004520, GO:0004521, GO:0004530, GO:1990238) and is therefore
    indistinguishable in GO from an unguided nuclease of the same chemistry. For Cas12a this
    is the single most important thing about the protein, since the sequence it cuts is set
    by the spacer content of the CRISPR array, is heritable, and changes without any change
    to the protein. Two children are needed, guide RNA-dependent DNA endonuclease activity
    (Cas9 and Cas12 families) and guide RNA-dependent RNA endonuclease activity (Cas13 and
    type III effectors), and the parent would also accommodate class 1 multi-subunit
    effectors through contributes_to. This request is deliberately scoped to the nuclease
    function rather than to recognition in general, so that catalytically dead guide-loaded
    effectors are handled by a binding term instead. This is the same request as in the
    reviews of A0Q7Q2, P0DOC6 and C7NBY4 and should be merged with them.

suggested_questions:
- question: >-
    Is the Acidaminococcus sp. BV3L6 type V-A system active in its own host, and what are
    its native spacers? Every interference measurement on this protein has been made in a
    heterologous E. coli reconstitution with engineered spacers, so what this system defends
    against in Acidaminococcus is unknown.
- question: >-
    Does target-activated trans cleavage of single-stranded DNA contribute anything to
    immunity in vivo, or is it only a consequence of leaving an activated RuvC site exposed?
    Because cis and trans cleavage share the active site, a clean separation-of-function
    mutant may not exist, which is what makes the question hard rather than merely untested.
- question: >-
    Why do AcrVA4 and AcrVA5 fail against AsCas12a while inhibiting the Moraxella and
    Lachnospiraceae orthologs? The specificity difference is reported but its structural
    basis is unresolved, and it bears on which Cas12a-carrying hosts a given phage can
    invade.
- question: >-
    Should GO's molecular-function hierarchy acquire a guide-RNA-dependent branch, and
    should the cis and trans activities of one RuvC site be modelled as two functions or as
    one function with two substrate states?
