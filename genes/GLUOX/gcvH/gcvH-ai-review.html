Gene Description

gcvH encodes the H protein of the glycine cleavage system of Gluconobacter oxydans 621H, a small (119 residue) soluble protein built from a single lipoyl-binding (biotinyl/lipoyl) domain. It carries one lipoyl cofactor amide-linked to Lys-59, and that lipoyl arm is the working part of the protein: it is not an enzyme but a swinging carrier that moves a reaction intermediate between the active sites of the other subunits. In the glycine cleavage reaction the P protein (glycine dehydrogenase, GOX1097) decarboxylates glycine and transfers the resulting aminomethyl group onto the distal thiol of the GcvH lipoyl arm; GcvH presents the S-aminomethyl-dihydrolipoyl intermediate to the T protein (aminomethyltransferase, GOX1095), which releases ammonia and transfers the remaining methylene unit to tetrahydrofolate to give 5,10-methylenetetrahydrofolate; the L protein (dihydrolipoyl dehydrogenase, GOX2292) then reoxidises the lipoyl arm with NAD+ so the cycle can repeat. The net reaction converts glycine to CO2, ammonia, a one-carbon unit on folate and NADH, linking amino-acid catabolism to the cellular one-carbon pool, and it is readily reversible, so the same machinery can run toward glycine synthesis. In this genome gcvH lies in the canonical gcvTHP cluster at consecutive loci GOX1095-GOX1096-GOX1097, immediately beside the two subunits its lipoyl arm shuttles between. GcvH is itself a target of the lipoylation machinery: the octanoyltransferase LipB (GOX0046) attaches an octanoyl group to Lys-59 from octanoyl-ACP and the radical-SAM lipoyl synthase LipA (GOX2293) inserts the two sulfur atoms that complete the cofactor.

Core Functions

Lipoyl-dependent carrier subunit of the glycine cleavage system. GcvH holds one lipoyl cofactor amide-linked to Lys-59 on a swinging arm, accepts the aminomethyl group generated by the P protein's decarboxylation of glycine as an S-aminomethyl-dihydrolipoyl adduct, and delivers it to the T protein, which releases ammonia and transfers the one-carbon unit to tetrahydrofolate. The L protein then reoxidises the arm with NAD+. GcvH contributes no catalysis; what it contributes is the cofactor and the geometry that let the intermediate pass between two active sites without leaving the system, which is why its loss breaks glycine cleavage. The system is reversible and in this organism sits in the canonical gcvTHP operon (GOX1095-GOX1096-GOX1097), linking glycine metabolism to the one-carbon folate pool.

Suggested Questions for Experts

Q: Does Gluconobacter oxydans run the glycine cleavage system in the degradative direction under acidogenic bioleaching conditions, or in the synthetic direction toward glycine? The system is reversible and the direction determines whether GcvH is a net source or a net sink of one-carbon units in this physiology.

Q: Do Acetobacteraceae possess any GcvH-dependent lipoyl relay, or is lipoylation of all acceptor domains in this lineage handled directly by LipB? A negative answer would settle whether GO:0009249 should ever propagate to GcvH outside the LipM/LipL clade, rather than being judged organism by organism.

Suggested Experiments

Experiment: Delete gcvH (GOX1096) in Gluconobacter oxydans 621H and assay the lipoylation state of the pyruvate and 2-oxoglutarate dehydrogenase E2 subunits by anti-lipoic acid immunoblot, alongside growth in minimal medium without lipoate. If the E2 subunits remain lipoylated and growth is unaffected, GcvH is not a relay intermediate here and the protein lipoylation annotation is an over-propagation; if E2 lipoylation is lost, the Bacillus-type donor role does operate in this lineage and the annotation should be restored.

Hypothesis: GcvH (GOX1096) is a lipoyl acceptor only, and is not required for lipoylation of the 2-oxoacid dehydrogenase E2 domains in Gluconobacter oxydans.

Experiment: Express wild-type and K59R GcvH in a gcvH deletion background and compare lipoylation by mass shift (intact-protein LC-MS, expecting the 188 Da lipoyl adduct) and glycine cleavage activity by 14C release from [1-14C]glycine in cell extracts. This tests a site that is currently only predicted from HAMAP and PIRSR and has never been shown experimentally in this organism.

2. Genomic context: the pathway is present, and that matters

I surveyed the 621H proteome for the glycine cleavage system and the lipoyl-attachment
machinery (gcvH-bioinformatics/survey_gcs_and_lipoylation.py, results in
gcvH-bioinformatics/RESULTS.md). Findings:

  • All four GCS subunits are encoded: P = Q5FRY0 (gcvP / GOX1097), H = Q5FRY1 (gcvH /
    GOX1096), T = Q5FRY2 (GOX1095), L = Q5FNM2 (GOX2292, dihydrolipoyl dehydrogenase).
  • GOX1095-GOX1096-GOX1097 are three consecutive locus tags, i.e. the canonical
    bacterial gcvTHP cluster. The H protein is in an operon with exactly the two
    subunits its lipoyl arm shuttles between.

This is what lifts the GO:0005960 and GO:0019464 annotations above bare family
membership: the partners each step needs are present, and the gene neighbourhood is
the one the pathway is normally found in.

  • Lipoylation route: LipB-type octanoyltransferase Q5FUX5 (lipB / GOX0046) plus LipA
    lipoyl synthase Q5FNM1 (lipA / GOX2293) are present. No LipM or LipL could be found.

3. The one call I make differently from the existing METEA/gcvH review

GO:0009249 protein lipoylation, propagated onto Q5FRY1 by TreeGrafter from
PANTHER:PTN000196926, is marked over-annotated here. The existing review at
genes/METEA/gcvH ACCEPTed the same term on the grounds that GcvH carries the
lipoylated lysine. I do not follow that, for three reasons.

First, being lipoylated is being the substrate of lipoylation. CLAUDE.md's
participation test is explicit that "being the thing it acts on is not participation";
the enzymes that do the work of protein lipoylation are the octanoyltransferase and
the lipoyl synthase, i.e. LipB (GOX0046) and LipA (GOX2293) in this organism.

Second, GcvH does genuinely participate in lipoylation in one clade, and that clade
is defined by enzymes G. oxydans does not have. In Bacillus subtilis, LipM
octanoylates GcvH and LipL then amidotransfers the group onto the E2 subunits, which
makes octanoyl-GcvH a mandatory waypoint: PMID:21338421, and the same paper puts it plainly that
PMID:21338421. That relay is absent where a LipB is present: the authors
showed that PMID:21338421 and concluded that PMID:21338421.
G. oxydans 621H has LipB and no detectable LipM/LipL, so the donor role does not
transfer to it.

Third, the comparator check agrees. B. subtilis GcvH (O32174), which runs the relay,
carries GO:0009249 by IMP from PMID:21338421 and also GO:0009107 lipoate biosynthetic
process. E. coli GcvH (P0A6T9), which does not, carries neither — its GOA has only
GO:0019464, GO:0005960, GO:0005829, GO:0005737 and GO:0006730 (QuickGO annotation
search for each accession, run 2026-10-28). The absence in E. coli is the convention,
not an oversight, and G. oxydans is on the E. coli side of the line.

The honest caveat, recorded in RESULTS.md too: my LipM/LipL search was by UniProt gene
and protein name within a TrEMBL proteome, not a profile-HMM search of the genome, so
"absent" means "not annotated as present". That is why the call is
MARK_AS_OVER_ANNOTATED and not REMOVE.

Follow-up: genes/METEA/gcvH needs the same correction

Recording this as an explicit follow-up rather than leaving the divergence noted and
unresolved. The argument above is not specific to G. oxydans: its first leg — that
carrying the lipoylated lysine makes GcvH the substrate of protein lipoylation,
not a participant in it — applies to every GcvH, and its second leg holds for
M. extorquens AM1 as well. That proteome carries the E. coli-type pair, LipB
(C5AQW0, lipB / MexAM1_META1p4604, Octanoyltransferase) and LipA (C5AVR2, lipA /
MexAM1_META1p3000, Lipoyl synthase), and no entry matches lipM or lipL (UniProtKB
REST, organism_id:272630, run 2026-10-28; the same name-search caveat applies as
above). So genes/METEA/gcvH (C5AUG1), which on main both ACCEPTs GO:0009249 on
the grounds that GcvH carries the lipoyl cofactor and lists GO:0009249 under
core_functions.directly_involved_in, asserts the donor role for an organism that
appears to be on the E. coli side of the line too, and asserts it as a core function
on reasoning that does not distinguish acceptor from donor. Both the annotation action
and the core-function entry should be revisited, and the UniProt quotes cited there
("Binds 1 lipoyl cofactor covalently"; "N6-lipoyllysine") re-read as establishing the
acceptor role only.

That is a separate gene and a separate change, deliberately not made in this PR: it is
work not yet done rather than something nobody knows, so it belongs here and in a
follow-up PR, not in knowledge_gaps.

4. The process term: does GcvH do any of the work of glycine decarboxylation?

This is the question CLAUDE.md's participation test is for, and the answer is yes, by a
route that needs stating rather than assuming. GcvH catalyses nothing. But the
aminomethyl group released by the P protein's decarboxylation of glycine is not free in
solution at any point: it is transferred onto the distal sulfur of GcvH's covalently
bound lipoyl arm, carried as S-aminomethyl-dihydrolipoyl-GcvH, and presented to the T
protein, which consumes it with tetrahydrofolate. UniProt's HAMAP text states exactly
this relationship — the H protein "shuttles the methylamine group of glycine from the P
protein to the T protein" — and the cofactor that does the shuttling is GcvH's own
[file:GLUOX/gcvH/gcvH-uniprot.txt "Binds 1 lipoyl cofactor covalently"].

So GcvH supplies the cofactor activity that two of the pathway's steps depend on, and
holds the intermediate in position between two active sites. In CLAUDE.md's own
taxonomy of substrate exceptions that is the thyroglobulin/fibrin shape — scaffold and
cofactor — not the angiotensinogen shape. The comparator check points the same way:
E. coli GcvH and B. subtilis GcvH both carry GO:0019464, so GO's convention plainly
admits the H protein to this process. ACCEPT.

Note that this reasoning is also what distinguishes GcvH from the genes/PSEPK/ureA
precedent for complex subunits. ureA is a non-catalytic chain whose contribution is
structural, so it takes structural molecule activity plus contributes_to the
catalytic activity that lives on another chain. GcvH is not in that position: carrying
a substrate between active sites on a covalently tethered cofactor is a molecular
function of its own, with its own GO term (GO:0140104 molecular carrier activity),
and no catalytic activity of the P, T or L proteins should be attributed to it even by
contributes_to.

5. CMMMech grounding: Q5FRY1 is linked to the wrong locus

This is the finding with consequences outside this review. CMMMech is read-only here
and I have not edited it; this section is the record.

CMMMech/data/records/neodymium.yaml grounds a causal-graph node
gcvh_glycine_cleavage_protein to UniProtKB:Q5FRY1 with

functional_role: Glycine cleavage system H protein GcvH (GOX1096 / GO_1096) targeted
by transposon disruption and clean deletion in Gluconobacter oxydans B58 Mon2
bioleaching assays.

and an annotation_note saying the 621H entry is "orthologous to the engineered B58
locus". The equation GO_1096 = GOX1096 is the whole basis of the grounding, and it
does not hold. Marecos et al. identify their own locus explicitly:

  • PMID:40301604
  • and in the abstract, PMID:40301604.

TypA/BipA in G. oxydans 621H is not GOX1096. It is GOX0636 (UniProtKB:Q5FT83,
"Large ribosomal subunit assembly factor BipA … (GTP-binding protein BipA)"; UniProt
REST query on organism_id 290633, run 2026-10-28). The GO_ tags are B58's numbering
and the GOX tags are 621H's; the two genomes differ in size and gene count (621H's
chromosome holds 2,432 ORFs PMID:15665824, while the B58 QC collection alone covers
PMID:40301604), so matching
the digits after the prefix is not an orthology assignment. B58 is not in UniProt, so
the mapping cannot be made that way either; it would need a reciprocal-best-hit or
synteny analysis against the B58 assembly.

What the paper actually reports for its GO_1096 also fits a stress-response GTPase
better than a GCS subunit: transposon disruption was the only hit that lowered
extraction — PMID:40301604 — overexpression raised it
(PMID:40301604), and the clean deletion did not reproduce the
disruption phenotype (PMID:40301604). The authors read it through stress adaptation, not one-carbon
metabolism.

Consequences for CMMMech, for whoever maintains that record:

  1. The protein example for the gcvh_glycine_cleavage_protein node should be Q5FT83
    (bipA / GOX0636), not Q5FRY1, and the node's label, gene_symbol and description
    should name TypA/BipA rather than GcvH — subject to the same orthology check,
    which still has to be done properly against the B58 assembly rather than by locus
    number.

7. Open questions I could not close

  • Whether G. oxydans actually runs glycine cleavage in the direction of degradation
    under acidogenic bioleaching conditions, or in the synthetic direction. The system is
    reversible and nothing measured here distinguishes them.
  • Whether a GcvH-dependent lipoyl relay exists in Acetobacteraceae at all. A profile-HMM
    search for LipL/LipM homologues over the 621H genome would settle the negative that my
    name-based probe can only suggest.
  • Lys-59 as the lipoylation site is HAMAP/PIRSR-predicted and has never been shown
    experimentally in this organism.

Probe Result
LipB-type octanoyltransferase (E. coli route) Q5FUX5, lipB / GOX0046, Octanoyltransferase
LipA lipoyl synthase Q5FNM1, lipA / GOX2293, Lipoyl synthase
LipM-type octanoyltransferase (B. subtilis route) no entry matched

G. oxydans 621H carries the E. coli-type de novo lipoylation pair LipB + LipA. In that
route LipB octanoylates each lipoyl-acceptor domain directly from octanoyl-ACP, and
GcvH is one acceptor among several. It does not carry the Bacillus subtilis-type
LipM/LipL pair, which is the route in which octanoyl-GcvH is an obligate relay
intermediate for lipoylating the E2 subunits of the 2-oxoacid dehydrogenases, and which
is why B. subtilis GcvH holds an experimental protein lipoylation annotation while
E. coli GcvH holds none.

Limitation, stated plainly. The LipM/LipL probes search UniProt gene names and
protein names in this proteome, not profile HMMs against the genome. G. oxydans 621H is
a TrEMBL proteome where many entries carry only a locus tag, so a negative probe is
suggestive, not proof of absence. The positive finding — that a LipB octanoyltransferase
is present and annotated — does not depend on the negative probes, and LipB alone is
sufficient for lipoyl-domain modification (Christensen et al. 2011, PMID:21338421,
showed that expressing E. coli lipB rescues a B. subtilis gcvH deletion). The
conservative reading is therefore that no GcvH-dependent lipoyl relay has been
established for this organism, not that one has been excluded.

What this analysis does not show

No measurement here speaks to glycine cleavage flux, lipoylation state, or any
phenotype in G. oxydans. These are annotation-level genomic-context observations drawn
from UniProtKB records, and none of the entries surveyed is manually reviewed
(Swiss-Prot) except where the table says so.
