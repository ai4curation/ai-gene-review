Gene Description

GOX1598 is an uncharacterised aldo-keto reductase (AKR) superfamily protein of Gluconobacter oxydans 621H. The 362-residue chain carries a single AKR catalytic domain spanning residues 29-353, recognised independently by Pfam PF00248 (Aldo_ket_red), SUPFAM SSF51430, CDD cd19094 (AKR_Tas-like) and Gene3D 3.20.20.100, and is placed by PANTHER in the mixed bacterial/eukaryotic subfamily PTHR43364:SF4 together with Escherichia coli Tas, E. coli YdjG, Bacillus subtilis IolS and the human AKR7 aflatoxin B1 aldehyde reductases. Enzymes of this superfamily adopt a (beta/alpha)8 barrel and reduce a carbonyl carbon by hydride transfer from NAD(P)H, using a catalytic Asp-Tyr-Lys-His tetrad in which the tyrosine donates the proton and the lysine lowers its pKa; all four positions are present and unmutated in GOX1598 (Asp62, Tyr67, Lys101, His147), as is the core of the dinucleotide pocket. The protein therefore has the equipment of a working NAD(P)(H)-dependent carbonyl reductase. Within the superfamily the CDD signature assigns it specifically to the Tas-like branch (cd19094), named for Escherichia coli Tas, a structurally characterised NADP(H)-dependent aldo-keto reductase that is 50% identical to it. Beyond that reaction class nothing is established: no substrate has been identified, the residues that discriminate NADP from NAD in characterised AKRs are not conserved, no subcellular location is recorded, and no study has assayed this locus or its product. G. oxydans is an obligately aerobic acetic acid bacterium whose hallmark is incomplete periplasmic oxidation of sugars and alcohols by membrane-bound dehydrogenases, but GOX1598 is a soluble cytoplasmic-type AKR with no signal peptide, so no connection to that periplasmic oxidative machinery is implied by its sequence.

Core Functions

NAD(P)(H)-dependent aldo-keto reductase: reduces a carbonyl carbon by hydride transfer from the reduced dinucleotide, using the superfamily's catalytic tetrad Asp62/Tyr67/Lys101/His147 within a single (beta/alpha)8 AKR barrel. Equivalently, in the oxidative direction, it oxidises a CH-OH group with NAD or NADP as acceptor. The substrate, the cofactor preference and the physiological role are unknown.

Suggested Questions for Experts

Q: Does GOX1598 have a physiological substrate at all in G. oxydans, or is it, like several PTHR43364:SF4 members, a broad-specificity carbonyl scavenger whose in vivo role is detoxification of reactive aldehydes generated by the organism's own incomplete oxidation of sugars and alcohols?

Q: The AKR superfamily catalytic domain IPR023210 has no interpro2go mapping, so a protein whose only InterPro matches are IPR023210, IPR036812 and IPR050523 receives no InterPro-derived GO annotation, even though relatives matching other AKR entries such as IPR020471 do. Should GO:0016491 be mapped to IPR023210, and if not, what is the reason for leaving the superfamily's catalytic domain unmapped?

Q: G. oxydans 621H encodes several aldo-keto reductase superfamily proteins. Which of them are paralogues with overlapping substrate ranges, and does that redundancy explain why no phenotype has ever been reported for this locus?

Suggested Experiments

Experiment: Express and purify the 362-residue protein and screen it against a carbonyl substrate panel chosen from what the organism actually makes and encounters - glyoxal, methylglyoxal, glyceraldehyde, 5-keto-D-gluconate, 2-keto-D-gluconate, D-glucono-1,5-lactone, furfural, 4-hydroxynonenal - in parallel with NADPH and NADH, following absorbance at 340 nm. This is the experiment that would turn the reaction-class annotation into a substrate-level one and simultaneously settle the cofactor question; a negative panel is itself informative and should be reported.

Experiment: Test catalytic competence directly rather than by inference: measure the activity of the wild-type protein against Y67F and K101M variants, which should abolish proton donation and raise the tyrosine pKa respectively if the tetrad identified here is the working active site.

Experiment: Establish the subcellular location, which this review deliberately leaves unannotated: fractionate G. oxydans 621H into periplasm (osmotic shock), cytoplasm and membrane with marker controls, and locate the protein by immunoblot or by targeted proteomics. For an acetic acid bacterium whose characteristic oxidations happen in the periplasm, whether this AKR is cytoplasmic is the difference between an intracellular detoxification role and a role in the organism's hallmark incomplete-oxidation chemistry.

Experiment: Make a clean deletion of GOX1598 in strain 621H and phenotype it for growth on and oxidation of the sugars and polyols G. oxydans is used for, and for sensitivity to exogenous reactive aldehydes. No mutant in this locus has ever been reported; note that the published bioleaching phenotype attributed to "GO_1598" is from strain NRRL B-58 and belongs to an orotate phosphoribosyltransferase, so it provides no prior expectation here.

I have recorded the missing IPR023210 → GO:0016491 mapping as a
source_defects entry (requested_change: ADD, resource: OTHER = InterPro /
GO external2go) on the one annotation row in this review. IPR023210 is the AKR
catalytic domain, so a safe oxidoreductase-activity mapping is defensible; I did
not propose anything more specific, because the oxidised donor group is not
uniform across the entry's matches. IPR023210 also matches human AKR1D1
(P51857), a Delta(4)-3-oxosteroid 5-beta-reductase of EC 1.3.1.3
(ECO:0000269), whose donor is a CH-CH group — GO:0016628 — while GO:0016616
and GO:0008106 both sit under GO:0016614 (CH-OH group of donors). QuickGO's
is_a ancestor sets confirm GO:0016616 and GO:0016628 share no ancestor below
GO:0016491, so GO:0016491 is the most specific term that holds for every
match.

An earlier draft of this section, and of the source_defects explanation that
ships to the InterPro/GO external2go maintainers, gave cofactor heterogeneity
("members with different cofactors (NAD and NADP)") as the reason to stop above
GO:0016616. That rationale is withdrawn: GO:0016616 is itself "...NAD or
NADP as acceptor", so it already accommodates both the NADP(+)- and the
NAD(+)-dependent members and cofactor heterogeneity is no obstacle to it. The
conclusion — GO:0016491 and nothing narrower — is unchanged; only its reason
is. A second, weaker point in the same direction: IPR023210 also matches the
voltage-gated potassium channel beta subunits (e.g. Q13303/KCNAB2), whose
experimentally supported function is Kv channel regulation and whose aldo-keto
reductase activity is annotated only by similarity, with no physiological
substrate identified. (Note that these subunits are not non-catalytic, as is
sometimes said: UniProt does credit them with NADPH-dependent aldoketoreductase
activity on an in-vitro substrate range, just without a physiological substrate
and without direct experimental support in the human entry.)

is kept in references with relevance: LOW and a reference_review recording
why, so the negative finding is not lost. No bioleaching, metal-binding or
nucleotide-metabolism annotation is made for this protein, and the +111%
phenotype is not cited as evidence for anything here.

Separately — and this is the deeper point the brief asked about — even if the
locus tags had matched, a disruption mutant extracting more neodymium would not
license a process annotation. That is necessity/perturbation evidence about a
downstream outcome, and GO's involved_in requires the gene product to perform
some of the work of the process. The paper's own proposed mechanism is that
losing the enzyme leaves more of its substrate (orotate) around to chelate
lanthanides
PMID:40301604 —
i.e. the metal binding is done by a small molecule, not by the protein, and the
protein's loss is what helps. Nothing there is participation by the gene product.

GOX1598 (Q5FQK7) — AKR catalytic tetrad and cofactor-site mapping

Question

UniProtKB Q5FQK7 (Q5FQK7_GLUOX, locus GOX1598 of Gluconobacter oxydans 621H)
is an unreviewed TrEMBL entry with no GO annotations at all and no
publication about the protein. Its only functional signal is signature-based:
Pfam PF00248 (Aldo_ket_red) over residues 29-353, SUPFAM SSF51430, CDD cd19094
(AKR_Tas-like), Gene3D 3.20.20.100 and PANTHER PTHR43364:SF4.

A fold assignment alone does not say whether the protein is a working enzyme.
The question this analysis answers is narrow and checkable: does Q5FQK7 retain
the catalytic residues that reviewed, experimentally characterised aldo-keto
reductases (AKRs) use?

Method

map_akr_catalytic_sites.py downloads UniProt flat files for Q5FQK7 and for five
reviewed AKRs that carry curated ACT_SITE, SITE and BINDING features,
aligns each reference pairwise to Q5FQK7 (Biopython PairwiseAligner, global,
BLOSUM62, gap open -11 / extend -1) and reports, for every curated feature
position, which Q5FQK7 residue it maps onto and whether the residue is identical.

No residue number is hard-coded: the positions come from the references' own
curated UniProt features, and the mapping comes from the alignment. Positions
that fall in a gap are reported as gaps.

References used (the first four are PANTHER PTHR43364:SF4, the same subfamily as
Q5FQK7; AKR1B1 is the superfamily structural archetype, included as an
out-of-subfamily control). These five are hand-picked for their curated
active-site features; the script runs no database search, so the identity
column below ranks these five proteins and says nothing about reviewed UniProt as
a whole:

Accession Protein Length % identity to Q5FQK7
P0A9T4 E. coli Tas, NADP(H)-dependent aldo-keto reductase 346 50.4
O43488 Human AKR7A2, aflatoxin B1 aldehyde reductase 359 38.4

Reproduce with uv run python map_akr_catalytic_sites.py; raw output is in
run.out, machine-readable mappings in results.json, alignments in
alignments.txt.

Result 1: the AKR catalytic tetrad is complete and unmutated

Every curated catalytic position of every reference maps onto an identical
residue in Q5FQK7, and all five references agree on the same four Q5FQK7
positions:

AKR catalytic role Curated reference positions Q5FQK7
Proton-donor Tyr (ACT_SITE) Tas Y53, AKR7A2 Y77, IolS Y58, YdjG Y59, AKR1B1 Y49 Tyr67
Lys that lowers the Tyr pKa (SITE) YdjG K85, IolS K84, AKR7A2 K105, AKR1B1 K78 Lys101
Asp of the catalytic tetrad (cofactor BINDING) AKR1B1 D44, AKR7A2 D72, YdjG D54 Asp62
His of the catalytic tetrad (substrate BINDING) AKR7A2 H141 (AKR1B1 W112 → Trp148 adjacent) His147

So the Asp-Tyr-Lys-His tetrad that performs hydride transfer and proton donation
in characterised AKRs is present at Asp62/Tyr67/Lys101/His147 of Q5FQK7, within a
Pfam-matched AKR domain (29-353). There is no evidence here of a fold-retaining
pseudoenzyme.

The cofactor pocket core is likewise conserved: the BINDING positions
AKR1B1 T20 → Thr34, AKR1B1/IolS S160+N161 → Ser196+Asn197, IolS/YdjG Q175/Q189 →
Gln221, and Tas/IolS K244/K214 → Lys260 are all identical.

Result 2: the analysis does not resolve cofactor preference or substrate

Two negative results matter as much as the positive one, and both bound what can
be annotated:

  • NAD vs NADP is undecided. The conserved positions listed above are
    conserved whether the reference binds NADP(+) (Tas, IolS, AKR7A2, AKR1B1) or
    NAD(+) (YdjG). The AKR1B1 C-terminal positions that discriminate the
    2'-phosphate (K263, S264, R269, E272, N273) map to non-identical residues
    in Q5FQK7 (Pro316, Phe317, Ile322, Ala325, Thr326), and AKR7A2 R250 maps to
    Thr273. The pocket is intact but its specificity cannot be read off this
    alignment.
  • No substrate can be assigned. The AKR7A2 substrate-binding residues other
    than His141 (Y260, R263, R359) map to non-identical residues or to a gap
    (Gly285, Asp288, gap), and AKR1B1's substrate residue C299 maps to Thr343.
    PTHR43364:SF4 is itself substrate-heterogeneous — its characterised members
    include aflatoxin B1 aldehyde reductases (AKR7A2/A3), a methylglyoxal
    reductase (YdjG), IolS and Tas, beside uncharacterised bacterial members
    (YccK, Rv2298/MT2355, Mb2320) — so subfamily membership
    transfers no substrate either. No member count is asserted: the only
    per-protein listing to hand, interpro/panther/PTHR43364/PTHR43364-entries.csv,
    is a 55-row sample of a family whose own metadata counters give 68362
    proteins in 11 subfamilies, so a row count in it is not a membership count and
    is not used as one here.

The protein that designates this branch of the superfamily independently of any
identity figure is E. coli Tas: Q5FQK7's CDD assignment is cd19094
(AKR_Tas-like). Tas is also the most similar of the five references aligned here,
at 50.4% identity. It has a solved structure and is described as an
NADP(H)-dependent aldo-keto reductase PMID:14517983, but its own physiological
substrate is likewise not established.

Conclusion

Q5FQK7 is, on sequence evidence, a complete and catalytically plausible
NAD(P)(H)-dependent aldo-keto reductase of the AKR superfamily. That supports an
annotation at the level of the reaction class (a CH-OH/NAD(P) oxidoreductase) and
no further: the cofactor, the substrate and the physiological role remain unknown
and need experiment, not more sequence analysis.
