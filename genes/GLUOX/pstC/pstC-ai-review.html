Gene Description

pstC (GOX0706) encodes one of the two transmembrane permease subunits of the high-affinity PstSCAB phosphate ABC importer of Gluconobacter oxydans. The protein has the MetI-like fold typical of type-I ABC-importer permeases, with six predicted membrane-spanning helices and an ABC transmembrane type-1 domain, and is assigned specifically to the PstC rather than the paralogous PstA subfamily by NCBIfam TIGR02138 and InterPro IPR011864. Together with PstA it forms the inner-membrane translocation pathway: the periplasmic binding protein PstS delivers inorganic phosphate to the PstC/PstA pair, and ATP hydrolysis by the PstB ATPase drives the substrate into the cytoplasm. PstC is not catalytic on its own; the transport reaction is a property of the assembled complex. In bacteria the intact Pst system is also the sensory input that reports phosphate sufficiency to the PhoR-PhoB two-component system, which in G. oxydans sits upstream of the organic-acid secretion that solubilizes mineral phosphate.

Core Functions

Inner-membrane permease subunit of the PstSCAB phosphate importer: with PstA it forms the translocation pathway that receives phosphate from the periplasmic binding protein PstS and passes it to the cytoplasm, driven by ATP hydrolysis at PstB.

Suggested Questions for Experts

Q: Does the acidification phenotype of the G. oxydans B58 pstC transposon disruption reproduce in a clean deletion in strain 621H, and does it require an intact PhoR? The published clean deletion of pstC was less acidic than the disruption, which the polar-effect hypothesis would explain but has not been tested.

Q: Does G. oxydans encode a PhoU orthologue, and is it the link between the Pst complex and PhoR, as in Escherichia coli? The Pho-regulon mechanism assumed throughout the bioleaching literature for this organism has not been demonstrated in it.

Q: Should the InterPro2GO mapping for the whole Pst family be moved off the secondary-active branch (GO:0005315) onto GO:0015415, and are other ABC-importer permease families affected by the same pattern?

Suggested Experiments

Experiment: Measure phosphate uptake (radiolabelled Pi, or Pi depletion from defined medium) in wild-type 621H and in a markerless pstC deletion complemented in trans, to show directly that GOX0706 is required for high-affinity phosphate import rather than inferring it from family membership.

Experiment: Distinguish polar from genuine loss-of-function effects behind the published disruption/deletion discrepancy: construct in-frame pstC deletions with and without a constitutive promoter driving the downstream pst genes, and measure biolixiviant pH, gluconic acid titre and Pho-regulon reporter output for each.

Experiment: Pull down the assembled complex from the inner-membrane fraction with a tagged PstC and identify partners by mass spectrometry, to confirm the PstSCAB stoichiometry assumed here from family rules and to test whether PhoU or PhoR co-purify.

PstC is therefore one of the two transmembrane subunits of the PstSCAB ABC importer: it

InterPro2GO defect: GO:0005315 is on the wrong branch

The current InterPro2GO mapping file maps the PstC family to a secondary active
transporter term:

InterPro:IPR011864 Phosphate ABC transporter, permease protein PstC > GO:phosphate transmembrane transporter activity ; GO:0005315

GO:0005315 "phosphate transmembrane transporter activity" is a direct child of GO:0015291
secondary active transmembrane transporter activity, and its definition says transport "is
driven by a chemiosmotic source of energy. Secondary active transporters include symporters
and antiporters" (verified via OLS). PstSCAB is an ABC importer: primary active, ATP-driven.
The correct term is GO:0015415 "ATPase-coupled phosphate ion transmembrane transporter
activity" (ATP + H2O + phosphate(out) = ADP + phosphate + phosphate(in)), which is under
GO:0042626 / GO:0015399, not under GO:0005315 — so this is a wrong-branch mapping, not an
over-general one. Recorded as a review.source_defects entry (resource OTHER = InterPro,
slot interpro2go, WRONG_TERM/REPOINT), and the row is MODIFY-ed to GO:0015415.

The same file maps IPR011864 to GO:0006817 phosphate ion transport (correct but more general
than GO:0035435, which the evidence supports) and to GO:0016020 membrane (true, but entailed
by the accepted GO:0005886 plasma membrane row).

Bioleaching context (not annotated)

whole knockout collection and improved REE extraction:

  • PMID:34795278
  • PMID:34795278
  • PMID:34795278
  • A clean deletion was less acidic than the transposon disruption
    PMID:40425722, so even the
    direction of the effect is not a simple loss-of-function readout.

No GO annotation is derived from this. The work is in strain B58 (locus tags GO_nnnn), not
in 621H whose GOX0706 product is reviewed here; and the phenotype is necessity/derepression
evidence — removing a transporter removes a repressive input to the Pho regulon. Under the
CLAUDE.md NEW bar, the entity that performs the regulatory step is PhoR/PhoB, and the
entity that performs acidification is the PQQ-dependent membrane glucose dehydrogenase, so
neither a regulation-of-acidification term nor a bioleaching term is proposed for PstC. It is
raised in suggested_questions/suggested_experiments instead.

Notes on the sources (not source defects)

  • PMID:40425722 calls PstB "the cytoplasmic signaling protein" and, in the introduction,
    says "pstS, which encodes a phosphate signaling protein; and pstC, pstA, and pstB, which
    encode an ABC-type phosphate transporter". PstB is the ABC ATPase and PstS the periplasmic
    substrate-binding protein; signalling is a property of the assembled PstSCAB–PhoU–PhoR
    system. Prose looseness in the publication, not a database defect.
  • CMMMech data/records/allanite-concentrate.yaml describes Q5FT13 as "Phosphate ABC
    transporter permease subunit PstC (GOX0706) disrupted in engineered Gluconobacter oxydans
    strains to derepress acidogenesis". The subunit identity is well supported; "to derepress
    acidogenesis" is a mechanistic inference from B58 disruption phenotypes that the engineered
    strains do not isolate (CMMMech's own supports: partial and its note on the Figure S2 /
    main-text pstS label discrepancy capture this). The strain used for the allanite work in
    Schmitz et al. (2025) was ΔpstS, not ΔpstC. No edit was made to CMMMech.

suggested_questions:
  - question: >-
      Does the acidification phenotype of the G. oxydans B58 pstC transposon disruption
      reproduce in a clean deletion in strain 621H, and does it require an intact PhoR?
      The published clean deletion of pstC was less acidic than the disruption, which the
      polar-effect hypothesis would explain but has not been tested.
  - question: >-
      Does G. oxydans encode a PhoU orthologue, and is it the link between the Pst complex
      and PhoR, as in Escherichia coli? The Pho-regulon mechanism assumed throughout the
      bioleaching literature for this organism has not been demonstrated in it.
  - question: >-
      Should the InterPro2GO mapping for the whole Pst family be moved off the
      secondary-active branch (GO:0005315) onto GO:0015415, and are other ABC-importer
      permease families affected by the same pattern?
