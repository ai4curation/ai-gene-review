Gene Description

pstS (GOX0537) encodes the substrate-binding protein of the high-affinity PstSCAB phosphate ABC importer of Gluconobacter oxydans. The protein belongs to the PstS family (PIRSF002756, TIGR00975, PANTHER PTHR42996:SF1) and consists of a single bilobed type-II periplasmic-binding-protein fold that closes around an inorganic phosphate ion. It is made with an N-terminal Sec signal sequence and, in this diderm alphaproteobacterium, works in the periplasm, where it scavenges free phosphate and delivers it to the PstC/PstA transmembrane subunits for ATP-driven translocation by PstB. In bacteria generally, the assembled Pst system also acts as the sensory input to the PhoR-PhoB two-component system, so phosphate capture by the binding protein is coupled to the cell's assessment of phosphate sufficiency; in G. oxydans that output includes the incomplete-glucose-oxidation pathway responsible for gluconic acid secretion and mineral phosphate solubilization. The protein itself has no catalytic activity.

Suggested Questions for Experts

Q: Does G. oxydans PstS, like its enterobacterial counterparts, participate in phosphate-sufficiency signalling through PhoU and PhoR, and if so is the signalling competence separable from phosphate transport?

Q: Is the phosphate affinity of GOX0537 in the range reported for enterobacterial PstS (sub-micromolar), and does it discriminate against arsenate, which matters for bioleaching feedstocks containing arsenate-bearing minerals?

Q: GO has no term for the sensory role of a substrate-binding protein that feeds a two-component system. Should that be requested, or is the signalling claim better represented as a GO-CAM with PhoR and PhoB performing the regulatory steps?

Suggested Experiments

Experiment: Construct the clean pstS deletion in strain 621H (the strain this accession comes from) and complement it with GOX0537 in trans, measuring phosphate uptake rate, alkaline-phosphatase (Pho regulon) induction and biolixiviant pH. This separates the transport contribution from the regulatory one and tests whether the B58 phenotype transfers to 621H.

Experiment: Purify the mature protein (from the predicted signal-peptide cleavage site, near residue 25-28, to residue 329) and measure phosphate binding by isothermal titration calorimetry or intrinsic tryptophan fluorescence, with arsenate and sulfate as counter-ligands, to put a number on the binding activity that is currently asserted only from family membership.

Experiment: Verify the predicted export and periplasmic location directly: signal-peptide cleavage by N-terminal sequencing of the mature protein, and periplasmic fractionation (osmotic shock) with an inner-membrane and a cytoplasmic marker.

Function

The record places the protein in the PstSCAB phosphate importer:

  • [file:GLUOX/pstS/pstS-uniprot.txt "Part of the ABC transporter complex PstSACB involved in
    phosphate import."] (CC FUNCTION, ECO:0000256|ARBA + ECO:0000256|PIRNR:PIRNR002756)
  • [file:GLUOX/pstS/pstS-uniprot.txt "The complex is composed of two ATP-binding proteins (PstB),
    two transmembrane proteins (PstC and PstA) and a solute-binding protein (PstS)."]
    (CC SUBUNIT)

That subunit composition is exactly the one GO:0055052 describes ("consisting of 5
subunits: two ATP-binding subunits, two membrane spanning subunits, and one
substrate-binding subunit"), so the GOA row on the parent term GO:0043190 is refined to
GO:0055052 in the review.

Localization: the entry carries no CC SUBCELLULAR LOCATION and no SignalP feature,
but (i) G. oxydans is a diderm alphaproteobacterium, so it has an outer
membrane-bounded periplasm; (ii) residues 1-27 (MFHRVGFIARGLAVALVVMVFGTAHSA) have the
canonical tripartite Sec signal architecture (basic n-region with H3/R4/R10, long apolar
h-region of about residues 11-21, small-residue c-region GTAHSA at residues 22-27) and no
lipobox cysteine. This is my own reading of the sequence, not a UniProt feature; the exact
cleavage position (somewhere in residues 24-27) is not determined, and the Pfam PBP_like_2
domain (FT DOMAIN 25..299) starts at or just inside that c-region, so the mature
N-terminus is expected near residue 25-28; and (iii) the primary literature on this very system calls
PstS periplasmic PMID:40425722. A NEW IC row for GO:0030288 outer membrane-bounded periplasmic space is therefore
added; the diderm-specific term is used rather than GO:0042597 periplasmic space, matching
genes/ECOLI/pstS. (Note the same
sentence mis-describes PstB, the ABC ATPase, as "the cytoplasmic signaling protein" — see
below.)

Why none of this becomes a GO annotation here

  1. Strain. All of the knockout work is in G. oxydans B58 (locus tags GO_nnnn), not in
    strain 621H, whose GOX0537 product is the protein reviewed here. The two are orthologous
    but the phenotype has not been demonstrated for this accession.
  2. Necessity, not participation. Deleting pstS raises acidification and leaching; that
    is loss of a repressive input, i.e. the gene is required for normal shutdown of
    acidogenesis. Under the CLAUDE.md NEW bar, being required for an outcome is not doing
    the work of it. The acid is made by the PQQ-dependent membrane glucose dehydrogenase, and
    the regulatory step (if it follows the E. coli paradigm) is executed by PhoR/PhoB with
    PstSCAB–PhoU as the input. No NEW regulatory process term is asserted; it is raised as
    a suggested question/experiment instead.
  3. Mineral dissolution and REE/Mg release are chemistry performed by the secreted organic
    acid on the ore, not by the protein, so no bioleaching-related process term is proposed.

Notes on the sources (not source defects)

  • PMID:40425722 describes "pstS, which encodes a phosphate signaling protein; and pstC,
    pstA, and pstB, which encode an ABC-type phosphate transporter", and elsewhere calls PstB
    "the cytoplasmic signaling protein". In the standard architecture (and in this entry's own
    CC SUBUNIT), PstS is the periplasmic substrate-binding protein and PstB is the ABC
    ATPase; signalling is a property of the assembled PstSCAB–PhoU–PhoR system, not of PstB
    alone. This is a looseness in the publication's prose, not a database record defect, so it
    is recorded here rather than in review.source_defects.
  • CMMMech data/records/dunite.yaml and data/records/allanite-concentrate.yaml give
    Q5FTI1 the functional_role "Phosphate-binding protein PstS (GOX0537) associated with
    phosphate-transport regulation of organic-acid biolixiviant production." The protein-level
    part (periplasmic phosphate binding) is supported; the regulatory role is an inference from
    B58 deletion phenotypes and is not established for this protein, and the leaching numbers
    come from a combined ΔpstS + P112:mgdh strain that does not isolate pstS. CMMMech already
    marks that evidence supports: partial, which is the right reading. No edit was made to
    CMMMech.
