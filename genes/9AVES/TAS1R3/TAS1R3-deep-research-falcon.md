This is not the mammalian sweet-receptor complex considered as a whole, nor is it TAS1R2 or a bitter TAS2R receptor. T1R3 is the common partnering subunit of vertebrate type-1 taste receptors. In mammals, T1R2/T1R3 is the principal sweet receptor and T1R1/T1R3 is the umami receptor; in hummingbirds, which lack the conventional avian T1R2-dependent sweet system, T1R1/T1R3 was evolutionarily repurposed to recognize carbohydrates (baldwin2014evolutionofsweet pages 1-1, ahmad2020gproteincoupledreceptors pages 3-4).

## 2. Molecular structure and domain interpretation

TAS1Rs are class-C GPCRs with three principal modules:

1. A large extracellular, bilobed **Venus-flytrap module**, corresponding to the supplied ANF_lig-bd_rcpt annotation. Its cleft and surrounding surfaces participate in ligand recognition.
2. An extracellular **cysteine-rich domain**, including the characteristic nine-cysteine region, which mechanically couples the ligand-binding module to the membrane domain.
3. A **seven-transmembrane GPCR_3 domain**, followed by an intracellular C-terminal region that participates in signaling and trafficking.

Authoritative reviews describe the same VFT–CRD–7TM organization and identify the VFT as a major orthosteric ligand-recognition region, while some ligands or modulators interact with the CRD or transmembrane domain (ahmad2020gproteincoupledreceptors pages 3-4, yoshida2024mechanismsandfunctions pages 1-2). Thus, the supplied InterPro annotations strongly support identification as T1R3 rather than an unrelated protein.

The fragment flag is important. Without the full sequence and residue coverage, one cannot assume that every module, trafficking determinant, disulfide-forming cysteine, or intracellular signaling region is present in A0A7L3N407. The record may represent an incomplete genome-derived model rather than a naturally truncated receptor.

## 3. Primary function

### Hummingbird-lineage function

Birds broadly lack T1R2, the partner used by most vertebrates for canonical sweet recognition. Hummingbird T1R1/T1R3 instead evolved from the ancestral amino-acid/umami receptor into a carbohydrate-responsive receptor. In heterologous assays, hummingbird T1R3 did not function as a sugar-responsive homodimer; coexpression with hummingbird T1R1 was required, making **heterodimeric receptor formation—not catalytic activity—the central molecular role of T1R3** (baldwin2015evolutionofsweet pages 76-82, baldwin2014evolutionofsweet pages 1-1).

Accordingly, A0A7L3N407 is not an enzyme and catalyzes no reaction. Its probable role is to:

- contribute extracellular ligand-recognition surfaces;
- form the T1R1/T1R3 receptor interface;
- couple extracellular nutrient binding to conformational rearrangement of the 7TM domains; and
- enable G-protein activation on the cytoplasmic side.

### Ligand spectrum and substrate specificity

Experiments with Anna’s hummingbird receptors found robust responses to increasing concentrations of **sucrose, fructose, and glucose**, as well as responses to **sucralose**. Tests generally used 1, 10, and 100 mM dose points; broad ligand screens commonly used 100 mM, with aspartame tested at 15 mM, six replicates, and statistical comparison against no-ligand controls. The receptor did not respond detectably to cyclamate, aspartame, acesulfame-K, or L-proline in the reported assays. Chicken and chimney-swift T1R1/T1R3 responded strongly to amino acids rather than carbohydrates, demonstrating a hummingbird-lineage shift in recognition rather than generic class-C GPCR activity (baldwin2015evolutionofsweet pages 71-76).

The receptor retains **bifunctionality** rather than becoming exclusively sugar-selective. Glycine and alanine enhanced sucrose responses dose-dependently; methionine, serine, and lysine also potentiated sucrose, and synergy was observed with glucose and fructose. Combined responses exceeded the arithmetic sum of the individual responses, including at 10 mM amino-acid concentrations that did not activate the receptor alone. This synergy was found in Anna’s hummingbird and saw-billed-hermit receptors but not in chimney-swift receptors (cockburn2022themolecularbasis pages 107-113).

For A0A7L3N407, the appropriate annotation is therefore **probable carbohydrate-responsive T1R1/T1R3 subunit, potentially retaining weak or synergistic amino-acid sensing**. Exact ligand potency, efficacy, and selectivity remain unknown for the hillstar sequence.

## 4. Structural and evolutionary basis of function

Chimera experiments localized much of hummingbird sugar responsiveness to T1R3’s extracellular N-terminal VFT region. A 109-residue segment was particularly important, and introducing 19 nonconsecutive hummingbird substitutions into chicken T1R3 restored sucrose and sucralose sensitivity. These residues formed three surface clusters: one near a putative ligand-binding pocket and two that may affect the T1R1–T1R3 interface (baldwin2015evolutionofsweet pages 76-82).

Comparative analysis found 14 sites differing between hummingbird and swift T1R3, 12 of which were unique to hummingbirds. A broader analysis included 68 T1R sequences from 26 vertebrates and identified six T1R3 codons with Bayes empirical Bayes posterior probabilities above 0.5 for lineage-specific selection. Nevertheless, the formal likelihood-ratio test was not significant, so relaxed selection could not be excluded. Functional chimeras therefore provide stronger evidence for adaptive receptor remodeling than the sequence-selection test alone (baldwin2015evolutionofsweet pages 76-82, baldwin2015evolutionofsweet pages 86-89).

These findings make the domain-level inference for A0A7L3N407 biologically credible, but they do not establish that this particular hillstar sequence contains all functionally critical states. Direct sequence alignment against experimentally tested hummingbird T1R3 proteins would be required.

## 5. Cellular and anatomical localization

The expected molecular localization is the **plasma membrane**, because T1R3 is a seven-transmembrane receptor that detects extracellular nutrients. In the canonical gustatory setting, TAS1Rs occur in specialized type-II taste receptor cells within taste buds (ahmad2020gproteincoupledreceptors pages 4-6, sukumaran2022sweettastesignaling pages 2-3).

For *O. melanogaster*, however, no retrieved study demonstrated:

- transcript expression in tongue or oral tissue;
- protein expression in taste buds;
- apical plasma-membrane localization;
- co-localization with T1R1;
- receptor surface trafficking; or
- expression in gastrointestinal or other extraoral organs.

Thus, “plasma membrane of gustatory receptor cells” is a **moderate-confidence localization prediction**, not a directly observed hillstar localization. Mammalian reports of TAS1R3 in gastrointestinal, pancreatic, neural, respiratory, and other extraoral tissues should not be transferred to this bird without avian expression data. A 2024 review confirms extensive oral and extraoral sweet-receptor biology in mammals but provides no evidence specific to *O. melanogaster* (published 5 July 2024; https://doi.org/10.3390/ijms25137398) (yoshida2024mechanismsandfunctions pages 1-2).

## 6. Signaling pathway

The canonical vertebrate TAS1R taste-cell cascade is:

**ligand binding to T1R heterodimer → heterotrimeric G-protein activation, commonly gustducin → PLCβ2 activation → PIP2 hydrolysis and IP3 production → IP3-receptor-dependent Ca²⁺ release from the endoplasmic reticulum → TRPM5/TRPM4 activation → membrane depolarization → ATP release through CALHM-associated pathways → P2X2/P2X3 activation on gustatory afferent nerves.** (ahmad2020gproteincoupledreceptors pages 4-6, ahmad2020gproteincoupledreceptors pages 3-4)

This framework is supported primarily by mammalian genetics, physiology, and cell biology. Hummingbird receptor assays demonstrated ligand-dependent intracellular calcium signaling in engineered human 293T cells using mouse Gα15 and a luminescence reporter, showing that the avian receptor can couple conformational activation to a G-protein/Ca²⁺ output. That engineered system does not establish which native Gα protein or downstream channels are used in hillstar taste cells (baldwin2015evolutionofsweet pages 71-76).

Therefore, the safest pathway annotation is **probable GPCR-mediated gustatory signal transduction**, with the gustducin–PLCβ2–IP3–Ca²⁺–TRPM5–ATP cascade as a testable homology-based model rather than demonstrated *O. melanogaster* biology.

## 7. Biological process and organismal relevance

The probable physiological process is rapid evaluation of nectar nutrient content and consequent control of feeding choice. Ruby-throated hummingbirds preferred sucrose over water and aspartame and responded within three to four licks—approximately 250 ms—consistent with immediate gustatory processing rather than delayed post-ingestive feedback. Molecular recognition measured in vitro corresponded with preferences in captive and wild hummingbirds (baldwin2014evolutionofsweet pages 2-4).

The evolutionary interpretation from the foundational Science study is that transformation of T1R1/T1R3 enabled hummingbirds to exploit sugar-rich nectar despite loss of T1R2, potentially contributing to nectar specialization and hummingbird diversification. This is an influential lineage-level hypothesis, not evidence that variation in A0A7L3N407 specifically determines black-breasted hillstar ecology (published August 2014; https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1255097) (baldwin2014evolutionofsweet pages 2-4, baldwin2014evolutionofsweet pages 1-1).

## 8. Recent research and current understanding

No 2023–2024 paper directly characterizing A0A7L3N407 or *O. melanogaster* Tas1r3 was retrieved. The most relevant current synthesis is Yoshida and Ninomiya’s 2024 review, which retains TAS1R2/TAS1R3 as the principal mammalian sweet receptor and the VFT–CRD–TMD architecture with gustducin, PLCβ2, and TRPM5 signaling. It also emphasizes that sweet reception includes receptor-independent glucose-transporter/KATP mechanisms, so not every physiological sugar response should automatically be assigned to TAS1R3 (yoshida2024mechanismsandfunctions pages 1-2).

Recent work therefore refines general receptor architecture, signaling, and extraoral physiology rather than changing the central hummingbird conclusion. For this target, the most precise experimental evidence remains the 2014 hummingbird receptor study and subsequent work demonstrating retained amino-acid function and sugar–amino-acid synergy (cockburn2022themolecularbasis pages 107-113, baldwin2014evolutionofsweet pages 2-4).

## 9. Applications and real-world implementations

There is no demonstrated biotechnology, veterinary, conservation, or commercial implementation involving A0A7L3N407 itself. Relevant research applications include:

- **Functional deorphanization:** expressing full-length hillstar T1R1 and T1R3 together and measuring concentration-response curves for nectar sugars and amino-acid mixtures.
- **Pollination ecology:** testing whether nectar amino-acid composition potentiates sugar perception and thereby alters hillstar plant choice, as suggested by receptor synergy in other hummingbirds (cockburn2022themolecularbasis pages 107-113).
- **Comparative sensory evolution:** mapping hillstar residues onto the 109-residue VFT segment and 19 functionally implicated substitutions to test whether sugar sensing is conserved or species-tuned (baldwin2015evolutionofsweet pages 76-82).
- **Food and sweetener science:** TAS1R structural and signaling knowledge is used broadly to understand sweetener selectivity and design sensory modulators, although mammalian sweet-receptor pharmacology cannot be assumed to apply to the hummingbird T1R1/T1R3 complex (ahmad2020gproteincoupledreceptors pages 4-6, yoshida2024mechanismsandfunctions pages 1-2).

**Recommended concise annotation:**

> *Tas1r3* encodes a probable class-C, seven-transmembrane taste-receptor subunit in *Oreotrochilus melanogaster*. By hummingbird-lineage homology, T1R3 likely heterodimerizes with T1R1 at the plasma membrane of gustatory receptor cells to detect nectar carbohydrates, with retained or synergistic amino-acid responsiveness, and initiates GPCR-dependent taste signaling. The record is fragmentary and lacks species-specific experimental validation.

- Full-length functional receptor status: **low to moderate**, because the entry is a fragment.

- Native gustducin–PLCβ2–TRPM5–ATP pathway: **low to moderate prediction**, because direct avian/hillstar pathway evidence is absent.
