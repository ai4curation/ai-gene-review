Gene Description

OmcA (SO_1779) is a 735-residue decaheme c-type cytochrome lipoprotein of Shewanella oneidensis MR-1 and the partner of MtrC on the cell surface. It is lipidated at Cys27, exported across the outer membrane by the type II secretion system, and exposed on the outer face of the outer membrane, where proteinase K added to intact cells degrades it. OmcA binds hematite with high affinity and transfers electrons directly to the mineral surface; its crystal structure places a hematite-binding motif next to heme 10, so that the electron-egress heme sits about 10 A from the mineral. Purified OmcA reduces soluble Fe(III)-NTA and is the faster of the two surface cytochromes on Cr(VI), and deleting omcA slows whole-cell reduction of Cr(VI), U(VI) and MnO2. OmcA forms a high-affinity complex with MtrC, which raises Fe(III)-NTA reductase activity above that of either protein alone; MtrC is charged by the MtrAB module of the outer-membrane conduit, so OmcA works as an accessory terminal reductase rather than as a subunit of MtrCAB itself. Like MtrC, OmcA binds flavin mononucleotide after reduction of a conserved disulfide and is found along the outer-membrane extensions called nanowires. Its expression falls in a fur deletion mutant, and a candidate Fur-binding site lies upstream of the gene.

Core Functions

Cell-surface terminal reductase for extracellular metals. OmcA, exposed on the outer face of the outer membrane, binds mineral surfaces through a hematite-binding motif adjacent to heme 10 and reduces Fe(III) in solution and in solid hematite, Cr(VI) (faster than MtrC in vitro), and contributes to whole-cell reduction of U(VI) and MnO2. It works alongside MtrC, with which it forms a high-affinity complex that is a better Fe(III) reductase than either protein alone.

Suggested Questions for Experts

Q: Does OmcA receive its electrons from MtrC, from MtrA or MtrB directly, or from another conduit, given that it is not a stoichiometric subunit of the MtrCAB complex yet depends on that pathway in vivo?

Q: Is OmcA's advantage on Cr(VI) in vitro reflected in vivo, and what structural feature of the heme chain or substrate-binding surface accounts for the ten-fold difference in kcat relative to MtrC?

Q: Is omcA genuinely part of an iron-responsive regulon, and does the predicted Fur site upstream of the gene bind Fur and drive the expression change seen in the fur mutant?

Suggested Experiments

Experiment: Test the predicted Fur site upstream of omcA directly: electrophoretic mobility shift or DNA footprinting with purified Fur, plus a reporter fusion measured across a range of iron concentrations in wild type and a fur mutant. This would settle whether the existing cellular response to iron ion annotation can be grounded in an iron stimulus rather than in a fur deletion.

Type: regulatory assay

Experiment: Compare Cr(VI), U(VI) and Fe(III)-oxide reduction by the single omcA and mtrC mutants and the double mutant under identical anaerobic conditions with the same acceptor loading, to separate the contributions of the two surface cytochromes with one method rather than across papers.

Type: single-cell mineral reduction

  • Nothing here supports an industrial-performance claim; all rates above are laboratory
