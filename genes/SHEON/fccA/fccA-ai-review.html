Gene Description

FccA (flavocytochrome c3, SO_0970) is a soluble periplasmic flavocytochrome of Shewanella oneidensis MR-1 and the organism's terminal fumarate reductase. It is exported with a cleavable signal peptide and matures into a monomer carrying four covalently bound heme c groups in an N-terminal tetraheme cytochrome domain and one non-covalently bound FAD in a C-terminal flavoprotein domain related to succinate dehydrogenase/fumarate reductase flavoproteins, with a mobile capping domain over the active site. All redox centres lie in van der Waals contact, so electrons delivered to the hemes pass through the FAD to fumarate bound at the flavin, reducing it to succinate. Electrons reach FccA from the inner-membrane tetraheme menaquinol dehydrogenase CymA, and complex formation with FccA is what allows CymA to run in the menaquinol-oxidising direction. Besides terminal fumarate reduction, FccA is one of the most abundant periplasmic cytochromes in anaerobic cells (alongside the small tetraheme cytochrome STC and the monoheme cytochrome c5 ScyA) and acts as a periplasmic electron-transfer hub: it exchanges electrons bidirectionally with the decaheme cytochrome MtrA of the MtrCAB outer-membrane module, providing a route to extracellular metal oxides, and its large heme pool appears to act as a transient electron store. FccA is also required for most of the early reduction of selenite to insoluble Se(0) by non-growing cells, a reaction that competes with fumarate for the enzyme.

Proposed New Ontology Terms

fumarate reductase (cytochrome) activity

Definition: Catalysis of the reaction: 2 Fe(II)-[cytochrome c] + fumarate + 2 H(+) = 2 Fe(III)-[cytochrome c] + succinate. Corresponds to EC 1.3.2.4 and is carried out by the periplasmic flavocytochrome c fumarate reductases of Shewanella and related bacteria, in which c-type hemes within the same polypeptide deliver the electrons to a flavin that reduces fumarate.

Justification: GO has terms for the NAD-linked (GO:0016156) and quinone-linked (GO:0008177) fumarate reductase/succinate dehydrogenase reactions, but none for the cytochrome-linked reaction of EC 1.3.2.4, so the best available term for the characterised activity of FccA and its orthologues is the unspecific GO:0016632. The reaction is standing EC chemistry with a RHEA entry (RHEA:77903) recorded by UniProt from experimental evidence, and it applies to a recognisable protein family (InterPro IPR010960, NCBIfam TIGR01813). A second parent is also requested, which the single-valued proposed_parent slot cannot hold: GO:0000104 succinate dehydrogenase activity ("succinate + acceptor = fumarate + reduced acceptor"). That is how GO places the analogous quinone-linked reaction, GO:0008177 succinate dehydrogenase (quinone) activity, which sits under both GO:0000104 and the acceptor-specific class GO:0016635; placing the new term under GO:0000104 as well as GO:0016632 would make it consistent with that sibling.

Core Functions

Terminal fumarate reductase of the periplasm: accepts electrons at its four covalently bound c-type hemes, relays them through the non-covalently bound FAD, and uses them to reduce fumarate bound at the flavin to succinate (EC 1.3.2.4). The capping domain closes over the substrate site, and because all redox centres are in van der Waals contact the enzyme needs no additional carrier between heme and substrate.

  • Abundant in the periplasm of anaerobic/ferric-citrate-reducing cells; reduced by CymA

  • In non-growing MR-1 suspensions, selenite reduction is strongly fccA-dependent

    selenite, consistent with reduction occurring at FccA itself.
  • The reduction product is cell-associated Se(0); the authors place the reduction in the
    periplasm PMID:24435070, and selenite reduction depends on CymA as the upstream
    electron donor.

Where CMMMech is stronger than the evidence

/home/user/CMMMech/data/records/selenium.yaml (node fcca_fumarate_reductase, and the
matching protein_examples entry) states that FccA is "the principal periplasmic
selenite Se(IV) reductase" and that it is a "fumarate/selenite reductase" catalysing
reduction of selenite to elemental selenium. The published evidence is a single
deletion-mutant rate experiment (PMID:24435070): ΔfccA retains ~40% of the wild-type
initial rate and substantial later reduction, there is no complementation, and no
purified-FccA selenite-reduction assay has been reported. So "FccA is required for most
of the early periplasmic selenite reduction under these conditions" is supported;
"FccA is a selenite reductase" (a direct catalytic claim) is an inference from genetics
plus the fumarate-competition experiment, not a demonstrated enzymatic activity. The
review therefore records no selenite-reductase molecular function and keeps the selenite
role as an involvement statement. (CMMMech was not edited.)
