Gene Description

CcmC (SO_0261) is a 248-residue polytopic inner-membrane protein of Shewanella oneidensis MR-1 and a member of the CcmC/CycZ/HelC family, one of the eight membrane components (CcmA-H) of System I cytochrome c biogenesis. It carries six transmembrane helices and the conserved tryptophan-rich WWD domain that defines the heme-handling superfamily it shares with CcmF and CcsA. The WWD domain lies in a periplasmic loop and forms the heme binding site; two external histidines provide the axial ligands to the heme iron. CcmC's step in the pathway is to bind heme at that external site and hand it to the membrane-tethered heme chaperone CcmE, which becomes covalently loaded to form holoCcmE; CcmC is therefore sometimes called the holoCcmE synthase. CcmC binds heme stably only when apoCcmE is present, and CcmE binds CcmC stably only when heme is present, so the three form a single complex. Release of holoCcmE from that complex requires ATP hydrolysis by CcmA together with CcmB, and in the absence of either subunit the CcmC:heme:CcmE complex accumulates as a dead end. HoloCcmE then delivers the heme to the CcmF/H cytochrome c synthetase for thioether attachment to the CXXCH motif of apocytochromes c. In S. oneidensis this pathway matures an unusually large complement of multiheme c-type cytochromes, and a strain disrupted in ccmC lacks all functionally active c-type cytochromes and cannot carry out c-type-cytochrome-dependent extracellular reduction of U(VI). Despite the legacy name "heme exporter protein C", the route by which heme reaches the periplasmic WWD domain has not been established, and no heme binding site within the membrane-spanning helices of CcmC has been found.

Core Functions

Heme-handling inner-membrane protein of System I cytochrome c biogenesis. CcmC binds heme at the conserved tryptophan-rich WWD domain in a periplasmic loop, with two external histidines as the axial ligands to the heme iron, and attaches that heme to the apo form of the membrane-tethered chaperone CcmE to make holoCcmE - the committed first step of c-type cytochrome maturation. Binding is mutual and heme-dependent: CcmC traps heme only when apoCcmE is present, and CcmE binds CcmC stably only when heme is present. Release of holoCcmE needs ATP hydrolysis by CcmA with CcmB, so in their absence the CcmC:heme:CcmE complex accumulates as a stable dead end. The mechanism is established in Escherichia coli, where the axial ligands are His60 and His184. The S. oneidensis protein carries that same heme-binding site in its own sequence: a pairwise alignment of Q8EK42 against E. coli P0ABM1 is gap-free from the N-terminus through residue 184 at 58.8% identity, and maps the two axial histidines onto His60 and His184 of Q8EK42 with the WWD motif intact as WWVWD at Q8EK42 122-126. The assignment therefore rests on conservation of the characterised site in this protein, not on family membership alone, and is corroborated by the mutant phenotype, where loss of ccmC leaves the cell with no functionally active c-type cytochromes. The heme-attachment chemistry itself has not been assayed for the S. oneidensis protein.

Q: GO has no molecular function term for the activity that distinguishes CcmC, namely attachment of heme to the conserved histidine of apoCcmE to form holoCcmE - the step for which the field uses the informal name holoCcmE synthase. What CcmC does (System I cytochrome c biogenesis)

CcmC is one of the eight integral membrane proteins of System I (CcmA–H). It works
at step 1: heme is handed to the periplasmic heme chaperone CcmE, which CcmC binds
in a heme-dependent way.

  • The conserved periplasmic tryptophan-rich WWD domain of CcmC is the heme
    binding site: alanine substitutions of its invariant tryptophans perturb the heme
    spectrum and abolish heme binding and function
    PMID:20599545.
  • Heme is liganded by two external histidines of CcmC (His60/His184 in E. coli
    numbering); the double mutant binds no heme, co-purifies no CcmE and is
    non-functional PMID:20599545.
  • CcmC is the protein that attaches heme to apoCcmE
    PMID:20599545.
  • Release of holoCcmE from CcmC requires the ATPase CcmA with CcmB, i.e. the
    CcmA2B1C1 complex; in the absence of CcmA or CcmB a stable CcmC:heme:CcmE dead-end
    complex accumulates PMID:16824107.
  • Cysteine/heme crosslinking maps endogenous heme to the WWD domain and supports
    trafficking of heme by CcmC PMID:29518410.

Is CcmC a heme transmembrane transporter?

The historical name "heme exporter protein C" and the InterPro2GO mapping to
GO:0015232 heme transmembrane transporter activity both assert an activity that
the primary literature still treats as unproven. The route by which heme reaches the
periplasmic WWD domain is explicitly open PMID:20599545, and no heme-binding
site within the membrane-spanning helices was found, in contrast to the System II
protein CcsBA. The CcmABCD ABC complex hydrolyses ATP to release holoCcmE, not to
pump heme [PMID:16824107 "This ABC transporter is composed of the ABC subunit CcmA,
and two membrane proteins, CcmB and CcmC."; the abstract frames the ABC function as
"required for release of the periplasmic haem chaperone CcmE"]. The 2018 review of

Shewanella-specific evidence

Marshall et al. 2006 used an MR-1 CcmC− mutant as the "no c-type cytochromes"
control in the U(VI)-reduction experiments
[PMID:16875436 "we evaluated a mutant lacking all functionally active c-type
cytochromes"; "we used an S. oneidensis MR-1 mutant lacking the ability to
covalently incorporate heme into nascent apocytochromes (CcmC"]. That mutant was
the only strain that abolished U(VI) reduction; single and double outer-membrane
cytochrome deletions only slowed it
PMID:16875436.

This is a maturation-pathway phenotype: the mutant lacks holo-cytochromes, so every
c-type-cytochrome-dependent activity in the cell fails. It establishes that CcmC is
required for c-type cytochrome maturation in MR-1, which is a genuine and
specific claim about this gene. It does not make CcmC a participant in metal
reduction: the electrons are moved by CymA, MtrA/MtrB/MtrC and OmcA, and CcmC does
none of that chemistry. No NEW metal-reduction or uranium-related process term was
proposed for this gene (CLAUDE.md participation test).

Bioinformatics Results

(RESULTS.md)

CcmC heme-binding site conservation: Q8EK42 (S. oneidensis MR-1) vs P0ABM1 (E. coli K-12)

Question

The heme-binding site of CcmC is defined experimentally in Escherichia coli: the
tryptophan-rich WWD domain in the second periplasmic loop, flanked by two external
histidines (His60 and His184 in the E. coli numbering) that serve as the axial
ligands to the heme iron (PMID:20599545, PMID:19721088). The gene review needs to
know whether that site is present in the sequence of the protein under review
(UniProtKB:Q8EK42), rather than only inferring it from family membership.

Method

align_wwd_domain.py fetches both sequences from the UniProt REST API, cross-checks
the fetched Q8EK42 sequence against the SQ block of the committed
../ccmC-uniprot.txt (aborting if they differ), performs a global Needleman-Wunsch
alignment (Biopython PairwiseAligner, BLOSUM62, gap open -11 / extend -1), and then
reads the Q8EK42 residue and position that each E. coli landmark aligns to directly
off the computed alignment. No position or residue identity is hardcoded; a landmark
that aligned to a gap or to a non-histidine would be reported as NOT CONSERVED.

uv run python align_wwd_domain.py

Raw output of the run behind this file is in align_wwd_domain.out.

Interpretation

The two proteins are 58.8% identical over the aligned length, and the alignment is
ungapped from the N-terminus through residue 184, so the E. coli numbering maps
one-to-one onto Q8EK42 across the whole heme-binding region:

  • Both axial histidines are conserved and fall at the same positions in Q8EK42,
    His60 and His184. (These are Q8EK42 positions established by this alignment, not
    the E. coli numbers carried over by assumption.)
  • The WWD motif is intact and identical in sequence, WWVWD at Q8EK42 122-126, in a
    17-residue window that is identical between the two proteins.

This is positional, sequence-level evidence that the characterised heme-binding site
of CcmC is present in the S. oneidensis MR-1 protein, which is a stronger basis for
the GO:0020037 heme binding assignment than family membership plus the maturation
mutant phenotype alone. It does not demonstrate heme binding by Q8EK42
experimentally, and it says nothing about the catalytic or transport questions the
review discusses separately; the heme-attachment chemistry itself has not been assayed
for this protein.

Caveats

  • Pairwise global alignment, not a curated family MSA. The conclusion rests on an
    unambiguous, gap-free, high-identity region, which is the favourable case for this
    method; a borderline alignment would need a profile approach.
  • The E. coli landmark positions are taken from the published E. coli work and are
    the input to the mapping, not a result of it.
  • Sequences are fetched live, so a future UniProt sequence revision would change the
    run; the flat-file cross-check against ../ccmC-uniprot.txt is what pins the
    reviewed sequence.

core_functions:
- description: >-
    Heme-handling inner-membrane protein of System I cytochrome c biogenesis. CcmC
    binds heme at the conserved tryptophan-rich WWD domain in a periplasmic loop, with
    two external histidines as the axial ligands to the heme iron, and attaches that
    heme to the apo form of the membrane-tethered chaperone CcmE to make holoCcmE -
    the committed first step of c-type cytochrome maturation. Binding is mutual and
    heme-dependent: CcmC traps heme only when apoCcmE is present, and CcmE binds CcmC
    stably only when heme is present. Release of holoCcmE needs ATP hydrolysis by CcmA
    with CcmB, so in their absence the CcmC:heme:CcmE complex accumulates as a stable
    dead end. The mechanism is established in Escherichia coli, where the axial ligands
    are His60 and His184. The S. oneidensis protein carries that same heme-binding site

    corroborated by the mutant phenotype, where loss of ccmC leaves the cell with no
    functionally active c-type cytochromes. The heme-attachment chemistry itself has
