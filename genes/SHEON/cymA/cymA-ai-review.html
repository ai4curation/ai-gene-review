Gene Description

CymA (SO_4591) is a 187-residue tetraheme c-type cytochrome of the NapC/NirT/NrfH family and the central quinol dehydrogenase of anaerobic respiration in Shewanella oneidensis MR-1. A single N-terminal transmembrane helix anchors it in the inner membrane with its four-heme catalytic domain in the periplasm, where three low-spin His/His hemes and one high-spin His/water heme lie next to the quinol-binding site. CymA oxidizes menaquinol and passes the electrons to periplasmic and outer-membrane acceptors: the flavocytochrome c3 fumarate reductase FccA, the small tetraheme cytochrome STC, the decaheme cytochrome MtrA of the MtrCAB conduit that reduces extracellular minerals, the periplasmic nitrate reductase NapA (through NapB), the nitrite reductase NrfA, and the DMSO reductase system. Menaquinone-7 is both its substrate and a required cofactor for turnover. Because one enzyme feeds all of these branches, deleting cymA abolishes reduction of Fe(III), Mn(IV), nitrate, nitrite, DMSO, V(V) and fumarate while leaving respiration on oxygen and TMAO intact, which makes CymA the shared entry point of the respiratory network that underlies Shewanella's dissimilatory metal reduction.

Summary: Inter-ontology inference from GO:0008121, which sits under proton transmembrane transporter activity because the archetypal quinol-cytochrome-c reductase is the proton-translocating cytochrome bc1 complex. CymA has neither a proton channel nor a Q-cycle, and its curated reaction is written without a translocation term.
Reason: The quinol-cytochrome-c reductase term carries proton-transporter parents from the bc1 Q-cycle, in which quinol oxidation at one side of the membrane is coupled to quinone reduction at the other through a dedicated proton path. CymA has neither a proton channel nor a Q-cycle: it is a single-pass protein with one N-terminal helix and its entire catalytic domain in the periplasm, and it oxidises menaquinol at a single periplasmic site. UniProt and Rhea model this entry's reaction accordingly: the curated CATALYTIC ACTIVITY statement (RHEA:11484) writes a quinol plus two Fe(III)-cytochrome c (out) going to a quinone, two Fe(II)-cytochrome c (out) and two H(+)(out), with H(+) appearing only as an (out) product and no H(+)(in) term at all, i.e. as a non-translocating reaction. That does not mean no proton crosses the membrane in this respiratory chain. Net H+ translocation in menaquinol-dependent respiration is a property of the redox loop as a whole: the cytoplasm-facing dehydrogenases (Nuo, Fdh, hydrogenase) take up protons from the cytoplasm to reduce menaquinone, those protons ride across on the menaquinol, and CymA performs the periplasmic release half-reaction. CymA therefore does share in the loop's vectorial chemistry rather than being irrelevant to it, but a protein-level involved_in proton transmembrane transport over-reads the bc1-derived parentage: it attributes to one half-reaction, catalysed entirely on the periplasmic face and modelled by UniProt/Rhea without a translocation term, a transport process that only the loop as a whole carries out. The row is marked over-annotated rather than removed because it is a mechanical consequence of a term the protein legitimately carries, and the fix belongs upstream in the inference rather than in this gene's annotation set.

Core Functions

Menaquinol dehydrogenase of the inner membrane. CymA oxidizes menaquinol at a site next to its high-spin heme and passes the electrons through its three low-spin hemes to periplasmic c-type cytochrome acceptors, measured directly with flavocytochrome c3 fumarate reductase and with the decaheme MtrA. Menaquinone-7 is both its substrate and a bound cofactor, and the catalytic domain faces the periplasm where its partners are. This single activity serves every anaerobic branch except TMAO: the electrons leaving CymA reach FccA, the small tetraheme shuttle STC, the MtrCAB conduit that reduces extracellular Fe(III) and Mn(IV) minerals, the periplasmic nitrate reductase through NapB, the nitrite reductase NrfA and the DMSO reductase system, which is why deleting cymA abolishes Fe(III), Mn(IV), nitrate, nitrite, DMSO, V(V) and fumarate respiration while respiration on oxygen and TMAO continues.

Suggested Questions for Experts

Q: Does CymA itself reduce metals at a physiologically meaningful rate, or only through its cytochrome partners? Heterologous expression makes E. coli reduce Fe(III)-NTA and the purified protein has ferric-reductase activity in vitro, yet in MR-1 the demonstrated acceptors are c-type cytochromes and the mineral chemistry happens at MtrC and OmcA.

Q: What is the non-heme cofactor that the spectroscopy implies is needed for quinol oxidation, and is it the same bound menaquinone-7 that the electrochemistry identifies, or a second species?

Q: How is the electron flux partitioned between the competing branches when several acceptors are present at once? CymA is the single entry point for fumarate, DMSO, nitrate, nitrite and the mineral-reducing conduit, and the partner-binding sites on CymA that set this are not mapped.

Q: Why does TMAO respiration not require CymA when the E. coli homologues that seed its PANTHER node, TorC and TorY, are exactly the TMAO-reductase quinol dehydrogenases? Does S. oneidensis use a distinct quinol dehydrogenase for its TorA-type system?

Suggested Experiments

Experiment: Reconstitute purified full-length CymA into proteoliposomes with defined menaquinone-7 content and measure proton uptake and release on both sides during turnover with a cytochrome acceptor, with and without a co-reconstituted menaquinone-reducing dehydrogenase. Identity

  • Swiss-Prot entry CYMA_SHEON, 187 aa, Shewanella oneidensis MR-1 (originally
    described as S. putrefaciens MR-1). NapC/NirT/NrfH family (IPR024717, PF03264,
    PIRSF000013), PANTHER PTHR30333:SF3.
  • N-terminal single transmembrane helix (13-33) with a periplasmic tetraheme
    domain (34-187); UniProt topology rests on the soluble-domain work of Schwalb et al.
    PMID:12899636

Localization

  • Cytoplasmic (inner) membrane, with a minor soluble pool after fractionation
    PMID:9023196;
    the full-text follow-up attributes the soluble pool to release during fractionation
    PMID:10613864.

Cofactors and catalysis

  • Four c-type hemes: three low-spin His/His and one high-spin His/H2O heme next to the
    quinol site PMID:22458729.
  • Quinol dehydrogenase using menaquinol; menaquinone-7 is both substrate and a required
    cofactor PMID:22393052.
  • Monotopic membrane protein, "the smallest example of a quinol dehydrogenase in nature"
    PMID:22393052.
    There is no evidence that CymA itself pumps protons: it has no proton channel and no
    Q-cycle, and UniProt/Rhea write the curated reaction (RHEA:11484) with H(+) only as an
    (out) product and no H(+)(in) term, i.e. as non-translocating
    [file:SHEON/cymA/cymA-uniprot.txt CC:CATALYTIC ACTIVITY]. This is not the same as saying
    no proton crosses the membrane. In a Mitchellian redox loop the cytoplasm-facing
    dehydrogenases (Nuo, Fdh, hydrogenase) take up 2 H+ from the cytoplasm to reduce
    menaquinone, those protons ride across the membrane on the menaquinol, and CymA releases
    them to the periplasm. CymA performs the release half of net H+ translocation, so the
    protonmotive contribution is shared across the loop rather than owned by the
    dehydrogenases; what CymA lacks is a translocating mechanism of its own.
  • Thermodynamics: reduction of CymA by menadiol needed the driving force from NADH
    oxidation PMID:22458729.

Redox partners (electron acceptors)

  • Flavocytochrome c3 fumarate reductase FccA: direct electron transfer from purified
    soluble CymA PMID:12899636;
    1:1 complex with reduced CymA PMID:22458729.
  • MtrA (periplasmic decaheme subunit of the MtrCAB conduit) and FccA reduced by CymA in vitro
    PMID:19837833.
  • Small tetraheme cytochrome (STC/CctA) as the main periplasmic shuttle
    PMID:26175726.
  • Nitrate/nitrite: CymA replaces both NapC and NrfH
    PMID:19387485.
  • Heterologous: CymA expressed in E. coli is sufficient to confer Fe(III)-NTA
    reduction, and spheroplasts reduce Fe(III) citrate and AQDS
    PMID:18394146.
    This is a ferric reductase activity of CymA in a heterologous membrane; in MR-1,
    electrons are routed to Fe(III) via MtrA/MtrCAB. I did not add a ferric-reductase MF
    row: the physiologically demonstrated acceptors are c-type cytochromes.

Genetics (necessity, not by itself participation)

  • A clean cymA replacement mutant with wild-type menaquinone levels cannot reduce
    Fe(III), Mn(IV) or nitrate, and cannot grow on fumarate; complementation restores all
    of it PMID:10613864.
    The earlier transposon mutant was in a rifampicin-resistant background that was itself
    menaquinone-depleted, which the 2000 paper corrects; this matters because it was the
    basis of the original claim PMID:9023196.
  • Also required for DMSO growth and nitrite reduction
    PMID:12899636.
  • Not required for O2 or TMAO respiration (UniProt DISRUPTION PHENOTYPE), which places
    CymA in the branch leading to periplasmic and surface acceptors rather than in a
    general respiratory role.
  • V(V): the cymA mutant shows essentially no reduction
    PMID:15006760.
  • Selenite: rate drops to about a tenth of wild type
    PMID:24435070.

Review decisions worth recording

  • GO:0019333 denitrification pathway (IEA from InterPro IPR024717) is wrong for this
    protein and, as a blanket family mapping, for the family. Denitrification is defined
    as reduction of nitrate all the way to dinitrogen; S. oneidensis performs
    respiratory ammonification (nitrate to nitrite to ammonium) and has no NO or N2O
    reductase in this pathway PMID:19387485.
    InterPro's own entry name is "Denitrification system component NapC/NirT/NrfH", but the
    family includes NrfH, a subunit of the ammonium-forming nitrite reductase complex.
    Recorded as a source_defects entry against the interpro2go mapping (DELETE_VALUE).
  • GO:1902600 proton transmembrane transport is an inter-ontology inference from
    GO:0008121, which carries proton-transporter parentage because of the cytochrome bc1
    Q-cycle. CymA has no Q-cycle: it is a monotopic single-TM protein with the catalytic
    domain wholly periplasmic, so the protons released on quinol oxidation are released on
    the side they are already on. Marked MARK_AS_OVER_ANNOTATED rather than REMOVE,
    since the row is a mechanical consequence of a term the gene legitimately carries.
  • GO:0016020 membrane is MODIFY to GO:0005886 plasma membrane, which the gene already
    carries by direct evidence.
