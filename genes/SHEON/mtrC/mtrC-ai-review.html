Gene Description

MtrC (SO_1778, called OmcB in the early Myers literature) is a 671-residue decaheme c-type cytochrome lipoprotein of Shewanella oneidensis MR-1. It carries a signal peptide and a lipobox cysteine, is lipidated, and is translocated across the outer membrane by the type II secretion system so that it faces the extracellular side of the cell. There it is the terminal, cell-surface subunit of the MtrCAB porin-cytochrome complex: the beta-barrel MtrB spans the outer membrane and holds the periplasmic decaheme cytochrome MtrA, which receives electrons from the inner-membrane quinol dehydrogenase CymA and passes them to MtrC. Reconstituted MtrCAB moves electrons across a lipid bilayer. Purified MtrC reduces soluble Fe(III)-NTA, U(VI) and Cr(VI), and cells lacking mtrC are slower at reducing Mn(IV) and Fe(III) oxides, U(VI) and Cr(VI). MtrC forms a high-affinity complex with the second surface decaheme cytochrome OmcA, which raises Fe(III)-NTA reductase activity. Its crystal structure shows ten hemes in a staggered-cross arrangement across four domains, with a conserved CX8C disulfide (Cys444-Cys453) whose reductive cleavage allows reversible binding of flavin mononucleotide and conversion to a more reactive flavocytochrome. MtrC and OmcA are also present along the outer-membrane extensions ("nanowires") that S. oneidensis produces under electron-acceptor limitation.

Core Functions

Terminal reductase of the Mtr extracellular electron-transfer pathway. Sitting on the outer face of the outer membrane as the exposed subunit of MtrCAB, MtrC transfers electrons from its ten hemes onto acceptors the cell cannot take in: soluble Fe(III) chelates, U(VI), Cr(VI), and solid Mn(IV)/Fe(III) oxides. Activity on each metal is measured with purified protein, and deletion of mtrC slows the corresponding whole-cell reduction.

Suggested Questions for Experts

Q: Is a single molecular-function term adequate for a cell-surface multiheme cytochrome that both carries electrons within a conduit and reduces several unrelated metals, or does GO need a term for terminal reduction of an extracellular or insoluble acceptor?

Q: How much of the in vivo reduction of U(VI) and Cr(VI) proceeds by direct contact with MtrC versus by flavin shuttling, and does the flavocytochrome form change that balance?

Q: What determines the division of labour between MtrC and OmcA, given that OmcA is the faster Cr(VI) reductase in vitro while mtrC deletion is the more damaging lesion for several other acceptors?

Suggested Experiments

Identity and nomenclature

  • UniProt entry is TrEMBL (Q8EG34_SHEON), name taken from the EMBL submission
    AAN54831.1: "Extracellular iron oxide respiratory system surface decaheme cytochrome
    c component MtrC", gene mtrC, locus SO_1778. Protein existence is "Evidence at
    protein level" and there are seven PDB entries (4LM8, 7O7G, 7QTH, 8QBQ, 8QBZ, 8QC9,
    9EOV).
  • Nomenclature trap. In the Myers & Myers literature SO_1778 is called OmcB, not
    MtrC PMID:11133454, and the Fur modulon paper uses the same naming
    PMID:15576789.
    Any claim quoted from those papers about "OmcB" is about this gene. Conversely,
    omcB in Geobacter is a different protein.
  • Features on the entry that the review relies on: SignalP signal peptide 1..21, lipobox
    with LIPID 25 (N-palmitoyl / S-diacylglycerol cysteine, PROSITE-ProRule PRU00303),
    ten heme c BINDING sites and several Ca(2+) sites from the crystal structures, and
    DISULFID 444..453 — which is the CX8C pair described in PMID:26126857.

What the evidence supports

  • Decaheme cytochrome: ten hemes counted in purified protein PMID:16788180 and resolved in the 1.8 Å structure PMID:26126857.
  • Metal reductase: Fe(III)-NTA (above), U(VI) PMID:16875436, Cr(VI) with kinetics
    PMID:21498755.
  • Conduit subunit: PMID:20018742; stoichiometry
    PMID:17675441.
  • Surface location: PMID:18502849.
  • Partner binding with OmcA: PMID:16788180. Not annotated here as protein binding
    (uninformative per the repository policy); it is used as evidence for the electron
    transfer role instead.

  • MODIFY the IBA to GO:0016722 oxidoreductase activity, acting on metal ions: the node
    placement is right, the term is simply generic because the family's characterized
    members reduce sulfite and nitrite rather than metals.
  • Five NEW rows, each from direct experiment on this protein: heme binding
    (GO:0020037), FMN binding (GO:0010181), electron transfer activity (GO:0009055),
    external side of cell outer membrane (GO:0031240), anaerobic electron transport
    chain (GO:0019645).
  • No NEW term was proposed for Cr/U/Mn reduction specifically: GO has no term for
    terminal reduction of an extracellular or insoluble acceptor, and inventing one was
    not warranted here. It is raised as a suggested_questions entry instead. This was
    checked rather than assumed: OLS/QuickGO searches for "extracellular electron
    transfer", "dissimilatory metal reduction", "metal oxide reduction" and "iron oxide"
    returned no matching molecular-function or process term (the nearest hits are the
    generic GO:0009055 electron transfer activity and GO:0016722 oxidoreductase activity
    acting on metal ions, both already used here; GO:0019420 is dissimilatory sulfate
    reduction). FMN binding (GO:0010181) does exist and is now a NEW row.
  • Flavin cofactor: PMID:26126857 shows stoichiometric, glutathione-dependent FMN
    binding by MtrC itself (0.93 FMN per MtrC), so GO:0010181 FMN binding is added as a
    NEW IDA row rather than left implicit in the flavocytochrome prose.

Where that is stronger than the evidence this review found (CMMMech was not edited):

  • Silver, vanadium, rhodochrosite, nontronite, montmorillonite, chlorite. The
    records mark supports: partial and say so explicitly ("individual cytochrome genes
    were not mutated"), which matches what is published: MtrC is a representative
    pathway component for those systems, with no MtrC-specific assay or mutant. The
    evidence found here supports Fe(III), Mn(IV), U(VI) and Cr(VI) only.
  • Selenium (supports: partial): the record is careful — MtrC is listed as
    "evaluated as an outer-membrane" component alongside FccA and CymA. Published
    selenite reduction in MR-1 is attributed mainly to the periplasmic FccA route, so an
    MtrC selenite-reductase claim would not be supported.
  • Chromium and uraninite/uranium (supports: support): this is the best-grounded
    of the set. Both the purified-protein kinetics and the deletion phenotypes exist
    (PMID:21498755, PMID:16875436), so the records' claims are proportionate, with the
    caveat that U(VI) evidence is a rate effect plus colocalization rather than
    Michaelis-Menten kinetics on purified MtrC.
  • None of the records implies industrial performance for this protein, and nothing in
    this review would support such a claim.
