Gene Description

Gene product 30 (gp30) of the Pseudomonas aeruginosa transposable phage D3112 is a 90-residue acidic protein and the founding member of the AcrF2 (AcrIF2) anti-CRISPR family. It allows the phage to replicate in hosts carrying an intact type I-F CRISPR-Cas system by disabling the crRNA-guided surveillance complex, the Csy complex, after that complex has assembled. The protein is a double-stranded DNA mimic. A pseudo-helical array of acidic residues on its surface reproduces the spacing and charge of the phosphate backbone of a DNA duplex, and the protein wedges into the lysine-rich pocket formed between the N-terminal hook of Cas8f and the thumb of Cas7f, which is the site at which incoming target DNA is captured. Occupying that pocket competitively excludes target DNA, wrenches the Cas8f hook away from its DNA-bound conformation, and so prevents target recognition without touching crRNA biogenesis, Cas protein levels or the nuclease chemistry of the pathway. Inhibition is stoichiometric and specific to type I-F systems, extending not even to the closely related type I-E. Convergence on this mechanism by sequence-unrelated inhibitors of other CRISPR classes makes the family a reference case for mimicry-based counter-defence.

Proposed New Ontology Terms

nucleic acid mimicry-based competitive inhibitor activity

Definition: A molecular function inhibitor activity in which a protein presents a surface whose shape and charge distribution imitate those of a nucleic acid, and thereby binds competitively to the nucleic-acid-binding site of a target protein or protein-containing complex, occluding that site so that the target cannot engage its genuine nucleic acid ligand. The inhibitor is not itself a nucleic acid, and because the mechanism is competitive occupancy rather than catalysis, inhibition is reversible and requires the inhibitor at or above the concentration of its target.

Justification: AcrF2 is the case that makes this term's scope clear, because its mimicked ligand is a whole double-stranded DNA duplex approaching a multi-subunit ribonucleoprotein, whereas the AcrIIA4 and AcrIIA2 cases mimic a short PAM duplex approaching a single-protein effector. One term covers both, which no existing term does. GO:0043021 ribonucleoprotein complex binding, asserted separately on this entry, records what is bound but not that the binding is inhibitory or competitive. GO:0140721 nuclease inhibitor activity is the wrong shape here, since the Csy complex itself is not the nuclease, Cas3 is, and AcrF2 never touches it. The generic GO:0140678 molecular function inhibitor activity cannot distinguish a mimic from a domain blocker or a catalytic inhibitor, which is the distinction that governs whether substrate excess defeats the inhibitor. Three sequence-unrelated families curated in this round would use the term at once, namely AcrF2 here, AcrIIA4 (UniProtKB:A0A247D711) and AcrIIA2 (UniProtKB:A0A059T5F6), and they attack two different CRISPR classes, which is the argument that the mechanism rather than the family is the reusable unit.

Parent term: molecular function inhibitor activity

Suggested Questions for Experts

Q: Is there any residual selective advantage to AcrF2 once the host Csy complex has been engaged, or does the type I-E result mean the family is locked to a single effector architecture and cannot be repurposed by point mutation?

Suggested experts: Blake Wiedenheft, Alan R. Davidson

Q: PDB 8DFS places an AcrIF2 in a type I-C Cascade, yet the original genetics found no inhibition of the related type I-E system. Does AcrF2 bind type I-C productively in vivo, and if so what makes I-E the exception?

Suggested experts: Blake Wiedenheft

Q: Does gp30 occupancy of the Cas8f hook pocket also block primed spacer acquisition, as was shown for the Cas3-binding inhibitor AcrF3, or only interference?

Suggested experts: Karen L. Maxwell

Suggested Experiments

Experiment: Measure Csy-complex target-DNA binding by electrophoretic mobility shift or fluorescence anisotropy across a matrix of gp30 and target duplex concentrations, and fit the surface to competitive and non-competitive models. A clean competitive fit, with apparent gp30 potency falling as duplex concentration rises, is the prediction; failure to see that would mean the structural mimicry does not translate into kinetic competition and the proposed molecular function would need rewording.

Hypothesis: gp30 inhibition is competitive with target DNA and can therefore be overcome by DNA excess, which is the operational signature that separates a mimic from a catalytic or allosteric inhibitor.

Type: competitive binding isotherm analysis

Experiment: Neutralise the surface acidic residues identified in the cryo-EM interface singly and in combination, verify folding by circular dichroism or by the 1.34 Å crystal form, then assay Csy binding and in vivo plaquing rescue. If charge rather than shape carries the function, potency should scale with the number of carboxylates removed irrespective of which ones.

Hypothesis: The pseudo-helical acidic array, and not the fold, is the inhibitory determinant.

Type: charge-neutralisation mutagenesis with paired in vitro and in vivo readouts

Experiment: Score new spacer acquisition in a Pseudomonas aeruginosa PA14 host challenged with a partially mismatched protospacer-bearing plasmid, with and without induced gp30, by deep sequencing of the CRISPR arrays. The Cas3-binding inhibitor AcrF3 was shown to block priming, so the comparison also tests whether the two mechanism classes differ in this respect.

Hypothesis: AcrF2 blocks primed spacer acquisition as well as interference, because the Cas8f hook pocket it occupies is required for the initial target capture that priming depends on.

Type: CRISPR array deep sequencing for primed acquisition

Knowledge Gaps

What is not known — curated, literature-grounded statements of the open unknowns (the inverse of core functions).

Gap: GO has no molecular-function term for mimicry-based competitive inhibition, so the activity that defines the AcrF2 family cannot be asserted with an existing identifier.

OPEN ONTOLOGY

What is known: The blocked surface is now established by two independent cryo-EM studies, and the process term GO:0098672 fits exactly. What cannot be expressed is that occupancy of that surface works by imitating double-stranded DNA and is therefore competitive with it.

Significance: Anti-CRISPR families share no sequence homology, so mechanism is the only axis on which they can be compared. Until the term exists, the module annoton for this family must either assert no molecular function, as it currently does, or flatten the mimicry into a binding term that loses it.

What would resolve it: Create the proposed term nucleic acid mimicry-based competitive inhibitor activity under GO:0140678, then move its identifier into the molecular_function slot here and in genes/LISMN/acrIIA4 and genes/9CAUD/acrIIA2.

Provenance (the field's own admissions):

  • "we did noticed a pseudo-helical display of acidic residues on the surface of AcrF2 mimics the negative charge distribution on the helical backbone of a DNA duplex" — PMID:28340349

Gap: It is undetermined whether a single copy of gp30 is sufficient to disable a Csy complex, or whether inhibition requires occupancy of more than one site, and consequently what intracellular concentration the phage must reach for the mechanism to work.

OPEN BIOLOGY

What is known: The structures establish the binding site and the competition. What they do not give is the stoichiometry of inhibition in solution or the inhibitory concentration relative to the host Csy pool.

Significance: A stoichiometric blocker, unlike a catalytic one, must be present in excess of its target. Without the stoichiometry, the kinetic race between phage gene expression and host interference cannot be modelled, and the distinction between the module's stoichiometric and enzymatic parts stays qualitative.

What would resolve it: Titrate purified gp30 against reconstituted Csy complex with a quantitative DNA-binding readout, and separately quantify gp30 and Csy subunit copies per cell through a D3112 infection cycle.

Provenance (the field's own admissions):

  • "AcrF2 is a small acidic protein wedged between positively charged residues in the N-terminal hook of Cas8 and the thumb of Cas7.6f" — PMID:28340349

Mechanism: the module's AcrF2 assignment holds, and can be sharpened

The module places gp30 in the csy_complex_blocker_variant and deliberately asserts no
molecular function, saying "no molecular function is asserted here because the specific
blocked surface is not established from the local evidence." That caution is no longer
necessary — the blocked surface is established, and it is the DNA-binding site.

Bondy-Denomy et al. showed by biochemistry that two of the three inhibitors they studied
block the DNA-binding activity of the Csy complex by contacting different subunits
PMID:26416740
(abstract-only in cache; which of the two is AcrF2 is not resolvable from the abstract
alone, so this is used as supporting rather than defining evidence).

The cryo-EM structure settles it. Chowdhury et al. have a results section titled
PMID:28340349,
locate the protein at the DNA-binding site
PMID:28340349,
describe the mimicry physically
PMID:28340349,
and conclude with a competition statement
PMID:28340349.
Their introduction gives the mechanism in one clause
PMID:28340349
(truncated in cache). The complementary Guo et al. structures add the conformational
consequence
PMID:28985564.
Yang & Patel's AcrIIA4 paper independently describes AcrF2 the same way
PMID:28602637,
and the AcrIIA4 NMR paper cites AcrF2 as the precedent for the acidic-patch mimicry
PMID:29497118.

Consequence for the module. The AcrF2 annoton should be moved from "mechanism not
established" to the same mimicry mechanism as AcrIIA4 and AcrIIA2, with the caveat that
the mimicked ligand is a dsDNA duplex approaching a multi-subunit surveillance complex
rather than a PAM duplex approaching a single effector. This is the strongest available
case that mechanism and not family is the right decomposition axis, since AcrF2 and
AcrIIA4 share no sequence relationship and attack different CRISPR classes, yet both work
by presenting acidic pseudo-DNA to a lysine-rich nucleic-acid-binding pocket.

the keyword-to-term mapping is a definitional mismatch rather than a biological

immunity by a symbiont) is sound and the correct term exists.

proposed_new_terms:
- proposed_name: nucleic acid mimicry-based competitive inhibitor activity
  proposed_definition: A molecular function inhibitor activity in which a protein
    presents a surface whose shape and charge distribution imitate those of a nucleic
    acid, and thereby binds competitively to the nucleic-acid-binding site of a target
    protein or protein-containing complex, occluding that site so that the target
    cannot engage its genuine nucleic acid ligand. The inhibitor is not itself a nucleic
    acid, and because the mechanism is competitive occupancy rather than catalysis,
    inhibition is reversible and requires the inhibitor at or above the concentration
    of its target.
  justification: AcrF2 is the case that makes this term's scope clear, because its
    mimicked ligand is a whole double-stranded DNA duplex approaching a multi-subunit
    ribonucleoprotein, whereas the AcrIIA4 and AcrIIA2 cases mimic a short PAM duplex
    approaching a single-protein effector. One term covers both, which no existing
    term does. GO:0043021 ribonucleoprotein complex binding, asserted separately on
    this entry, records what is bound but not that the binding is inhibitory or competitive.
    GO:0140721 nuclease inhibitor activity is the wrong shape here, since the Csy
    complex itself is not the nuclease, Cas3 is, and AcrF2 never touches it. The generic
    GO:0140678 molecular function inhibitor activity cannot distinguish a mimic from
    a domain blocker or a catalytic inhibitor, which is the distinction that governs
    whether substrate excess defeats the inhibitor. Three sequence-unrelated families
    curated in this round would use the term at once, namely AcrF2 here, AcrIIA4 (UniProtKB:A0A247D711)
    and AcrIIA2 (UniProtKB:A0A059T5F6), and they attack two different CRISPR classes,
    which is the argument that the mechanism rather than the family is the reusable
    unit.
