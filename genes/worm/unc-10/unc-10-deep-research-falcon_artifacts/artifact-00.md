| Identity and domains | **Direct:** *unc-10* encodes the *Caenorhabditis elegans* RIM/Rab3-interacting active-zone protein corresponding to UniProt Q22366. It contains a Rab3-binding zinc-finger region, a PDZ domain, and two C2 domains; alternative transcripts may omit C-terminal elements. | Positional cloning, complete coding-region deletion, loss of UNC-10 immunoreactivity, transgenic rescue, and comparison with vertebrate RIM1/2. | **High.** Koushika et al., 17 September 2001, *Nature Neuroscience*. [DOI 10.1038/nn732](https://doi.org/10.1038/nn732). The literature architecture agrees with the supplied UniProt Rab_BD, PDZ, and C2 annotations. (pqac-00000000, pqac-00000001) |
| Subcellular localization | **Direct:** UNC-10 is an intracellular presynaptic cytomatrix protein concentrated at active zones and dense projections. | Immunostaining detected discrete puncta in the nerve ring and dorsal and ventral nerve cords. Broader RAB-3-positive varicosities contained one or two tightly clustered UNC-10 puncta; ultrastructural studies localized UNC-10 to dense projections. | **High.** Koushika et al., 2001, [DOI 10.1038/nn732](https://doi.org/10.1038/nn732); Weimer et al., 2 August 2006, *Journal of Neuroscience*, [DOI 10.1523/JNEUROSCI.2350-06.2006](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.2350-06.2006). (pqac-00000000, pqac-00000006, pqac-00000010) |
| RAB-3 vesicle targeting | **Direct:** UNC-10 recognizes active RAB-3 and helps concentrate RAB-3-bearing synaptic vesicles near the presynaptic density. Genetic evidence places both proteins substantially in the same targeting pathway. | Yeast two-hybrid assays detected binding to GTP-locked RAB-3(Q81L), but not GDP-locked RAB-3(T36N). EM revealed similar vesicle mistargeting in *rab-3* and *unc-10* mutants, while the double mutant resembled the single-mutant targeting defects. | **High.** Gracheva et al., October 2008, *Neuroscience Letters*. [DOI 10.1016/j.neulet.2008.08.026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neulet.2008.08.026). (pqac-00000002) |
| UNC-13, syntaxin, priming, and release probability | **Direct:** UNC-10 acts mainly after conventional docking to promote fusion competence and neurotransmitter-release probability. **Inference:** this involves an UNC-13-associated mechanism that facilitates syntaxin opening. UNC-10 is therefore best classified as a scaffold or adaptor, not an enzyme. | Null mutants retained normal-looking presynaptic densities and total docked-vesicle numbers but had about fivefold fewer fusion-competent vesicles and a four- to fivefold reduction in miniature-event frequency. Miniature amplitudes and release calcium dependence were broadly preserved. Constitutively open UNC-64/syntaxin rescued the defect, whereas wild-type syntaxin did not. | **High for post-docking release function; moderate for the exact UNC-13 mechanism.** Koushika et al., 2001. [DOI 10.1038/nn732](https://doi.org/10.1038/nn732). (pqac-00000001, pqac-00000006) |
| UNC-2/CaV2 organization | **Direct:** UNC-10 recruits or stabilizes UNC-2/CaV2 channels at presynaptic active zones. It acts redundantly with RIMB-1 and partly in parallel with SYD-2-dependent assembly. | Endogenous fluorescence imaging showed marked reductions in UNC-2 punctum intensity and number in *unc-10* mutants. Combined *unc-10 syd-2* loss caused a particularly severe defect; *rimb-1; unc-10* mutants also had reduced channel abundance. Residual UNC-2 in higher-order mutants demonstrates additional localization mechanisms. | **High.** Kushibiki et al., 30 October 2019, *Journal of Neuroscience*, [DOI 10.1523/JNEUROSCI.0506-19.2019](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.0506-19.2019); Oh et al., 2 June 2021, *Journal of Neuroscience* 41:4782–4794, [preprint DOI 10.1101/2021.01.27.428454](https://doi.org/10.1101/2021.01.27.428454). (pqac-00000005, pqac-00000008, pqac-00000009) |
| SYD-2, RIMB-1, ELKS-1, and CLA-1 network | **Direct:** UNC-10 is part of a redundant active-zone scaffold in which SYD-2/Liprin-α is a major organizer and UNC-10, RIMB-1, and ELKS-1 make overlapping contributions to CaV2 clustering. **Inference:** CLA-1 intersects this network by recruiting RIMB-1 and UNC-13; direct CLA-1–UNC-10 binding was not established in the gathered evidence. | Endogenous tagging and combinatorial mutants defined SYD-2-dependent and independent routes. Relative punctum fluorescence was approximately 1× for UNC-2 and UNC-10, 2× for SYD-2 and ELKS-1, and 4× for RIMB-1; these are reporter-intensity comparisons, not absolute stoichiometry. | **High for network-level localization and genetic relationships; moderate for hierarchy and CLA-1 linkage.** Oh et al., 2021, [DOI 10.1101/2021.01.27.428454](https://doi.org/10.1101/2021.01.27.428454); Krout et al., 23 May 2023, *PNAS* 120:e2220856120, [DOI 10.1073/pnas.2220856120](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2220856120). (pqac-00000008, pqac-00000009, pqac-00000010) |
| Current limitations | No catalytic reaction or transported substrate applies. Isoform-specific functions, absolute molecular stoichiometry, several proposed binding interfaces, and the relative contributions of vesicle targeting, priming, and CaV2 anchoring remain unresolved. | Limitations include alternative transcripts, redundancy among active-zone proteins, residual UNC-2 localization in higher-order mutants, fluorescence intensity used as a proxy for abundance, and the absence of direct biochemical tests for every proposed interaction. | **High confidence in the core scaffold function; lower confidence in isoform- and partner-specific mechanisms.** Evidence spans 2001–2023, with some circuit-level RIMB-1/CaV2 evidence represented by a preprint in the gathered material. (pqac-00000000, pqac-00000003, pqac-00000005, pqac-00000009, pqac-00000010) |
