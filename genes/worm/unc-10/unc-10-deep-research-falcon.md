# Functional annotation report: *C. elegans unc-10* / UNC-10/RIM

## Executive conclusion

The identity check is positive. The target specified by UniProt accession **Q22366** is the *Caenorhabditis elegans* gene **unc-10** (ORF **T10A3.1**), encoding the conserved **Rab-3-interacting molecule UNC-10/RIM**. Positional cloning, deletion of the complete coding region, loss of UNC-10 immunoreactivity, and rescue with an UNC-10 coding construct establish this assignment. No literature for a similarly named protein from another organism was substituted. The experimentally defined zinc-finger/Rab3-binding, PDZ, C2A, and C2B architecture agrees with the supplied UniProt Rab_BD, PDZ, and C2 annotations. (koushika2001apostdockingrole pages 2-3, koushika2001apostdockingrole pages 1-2)

UNC-10 is **not an enzyme or transporter**; consequently, no catalytic reaction or transported substrate applies. Its primary molecular function is that of a multidomain **presynaptic active-zone scaffold/adaptor**. It spatially couples three components required for efficient neurotransmission: GTP-RAB-3-bearing synaptic vesicles, the UNC-13/syntaxin fusion-priming apparatus, and UNC-2/CaV2 voltage-gated calcium channels. The best-supported outcome is formation of a release-ready vesicle pool positioned close to calcium entry sites at presynaptic dense projections. (koushika2001apostdockingrole pages 1-2, gracheva2008directinteractionsbetween pages 1-2, oh2021unc2cav2channel pages 37-39, oh2021unc2cav2channel pages 8-11)

## 1. Identity, structure, and classification

Koushika and colleagues positionally cloned *unc-10* and identified it as the worm homolog of vertebrate RIM1/RIM2. The *md1117* deletion removes the entire RIM coding region, abolishes detectable UNC-10 immunoreactivity, and behaves as a null; an UNC-10 coding construct rescues behavioral and pharmacological defects. Their searches found no second RIM-like gene in the worm genome, strengthening the one-gene/one-target assignment. The largest protein contains an N-terminal Rab3-interacting zinc-finger region, a PDZ domain, and two C2 domains. Reported identities to the corresponding rat RIM1 regions were approximately **44%, 58%, 37%, and 53%**, respectively. Alternative transcripts include or exclude sequences near the PDZ and second C2 domains, and one predicted form terminates before C2B. (koushika2001apostdockingrole pages 2-3)

These modules explain UNC-10’s function better than an enzyme classification does. The zinc-finger/Rab-binding region recognizes activated RAB-3; the PDZ region is consistent with active-zone protein and CaV2-tail organization; and the C2 domains provide conserved interaction modules typical of RIM-family cytomatrix proteins. The presence of binding modules but no demonstrated catalytic center, combined with genetic, imaging, and ultrastructural evidence, supports annotation as an **intracellular protein-binding scaffold and vesicle-positioning adaptor**. (koushika2001apostdockingrole pages 2-3, gracheva2008directinteractionsbetween pages 1-2, krout2023c.elegansclarinetcla1 pages 11-12)

## 2. Cellular and subcellular localization

UNC-10 acts inside presynaptic terminals. Immunostaining showed discrete puncta in the nerve ring and dorsal and ventral nerve cords. UNC-10 was more tightly clustered than the synaptic-vesicle marker RAB-3: individual RAB-3-positive varicosities generally contained one or two UNC-10 puncta. Subsequent ultrastructural work localized UNC-10 to presynaptic dense projections, placing it at the active-zone membrane domain where vesicles are primed and fuse. (koushika2001apostdockingrole pages 2-3, krout2023c.elegansclarinetcla1 pages 11-12)

This localization is functionally important rather than merely correlative. UNC-10 loss does not erase synapses or grossly destroy presynaptic densities. Instead, it changes the distribution and fusion competence of vesicles adjacent to dense projections and reduces active-zone CaV2-channel clustering. UNC-10 is therefore a component of the **cytomatrix at the active zone**, not a secreted protein, postsynaptic receptor, or broadly distributed vesicle-membrane constituent. (koushika2001apostdockingrole pages 1-2, koushika2001apostdockingrole pages 6-7, oh2021unc2cav2channel pages 37-39)

## 3. Primary molecular function

### 3.1 Capturing RAB-3-positive vesicles near release sites

Direct yeast two-hybrid experiments showed that UNC-10 selectively interacts with GTP-locked RAB-3(Q81L), but not GDP-locked RAB-3(T36N). Binding did not require the RAB-3 C-terminal geranylgeranylation motif and was selective among the tested worm Rab proteins. This is direct evidence that UNC-10 recognizes the active form of RAB-3 rather than simply binding vesicle lipid. (gracheva2008directinteractionsbetween pages 1-2)

Electron microscopy provided the corresponding cellular evidence. *rab-3* and *unc-10* mutants showed similar loss or mistargeting of vesicles close to the presynaptic density, and *unc-10; rab-3* double mutants resembled the single mutants for this targeting phenotype. That non-additivity places RAB-3 and UNC-10 substantially in the same vesicle-positioning pathway. Release in the double mutant nevertheless resembled the more severe *unc-10* phenotype, indicating that UNC-10 also performs functions beyond RAB-3-dependent targeting. (gracheva2008directinteractionsbetween pages 1-2)

### 3.2 Post-docking acquisition of fusion competence

The foundational physiological result is that UNC-10 is not required simply to build a synapse or bring all vesicles into membrane contact. In *unc-10* null mutants, presynaptic-density ultrastructure and overall docked-vesicle numbers were broadly preserved, but the inferred pool of fusion-competent vesicles at release sites was reduced approximately **fivefold**. Miniature-event frequency fell **four- to fivefold**, including under low-calcium conditions, whereas miniature amplitudes were not comparably reduced—reported values were **23.6 ± 1.4 pA** in mutants and **32.3 ± 4.1 pA** in wild type. Evoked release was reduced at both 0.4 and 5 mM extracellular calcium without a major change in calcium dependence. These observations favor a defect in vesicle priming or release-site competence over altered postsynaptic responsiveness or calcium sensing by the final fusion reaction. (koushika2001apostdockingrole pages 1-2, koushika2001apostdockingrole pages 6-7)

Constitutively open UNC-64/syntaxin rescued the physiological defect, whereas wild-type syntaxin did not. The most parsimonious mechanistic interpretation is that UNC-10 promotes an UNC-13-associated transition in which syntaxin becomes available for productive SNARE-complex assembly. The rescue is strong causal evidence for action upstream of, or at, syntaxin opening; however, it does not by itself prove that UNC-10 directly opens syntaxin. The exact UNC-10–UNC-13 interface and isoform dependence should therefore be described as mechanistic inference rather than fully resolved biochemistry. (koushika2001apostdockingrole pages 1-2, koushika2001apostdockingrole pages 6-7)

### 3.3 Organizing calcium channels near primed vesicles

UNC-10 also organizes the calcium-entry side of the release apparatus. Endogenous fluorescent tagging and mutant analysis showed that the intensity and number of presynaptic UNC-2/CaV2 puncta are strongly reduced by *unc-10* loss. The defect is stronger in combined *unc-10 syd-2* mutants than in either relevant single-mutant context, revealing partially parallel localization pathways. RIMB-1/RIM-binding protein and ELKS-1 make additional, overlapping contributions; residual UNC-2 in higher-order mutants indicates that no single scaffold is solely responsible for channel delivery or retention. (oh2021unc2cav2channel pages 37-39, oh2021unc2cav2channel pages 8-11)

Quantitative reporter comparisons found UNC-10 active-zone fluorescence comparable to UNC-2, whereas SYD-2 and ELKS-1 puncta were approximately twice as bright and RIMB-1 approximately four times as bright. These measurements demonstrate unequal local abundance but should not be interpreted as absolute molecular stoichiometry because fluorophore behavior, tagging position, and protein accessibility can differ. (oh2021unc2cav2channel pages 8-11)

RIMB-1 and UNC-10 are partly redundant. Combined loss reduces synaptic UNC-2 abundance more strongly than loss of RIMB-1 alone, while UNC-10 deletion causes the larger transmission phenotype. Removing the UNC-2 C-terminal PDZ ligand shifts channels away from dense projections and delays evoked transmission, supporting a RIM-dependent channel-anchoring mechanism. Thus UNC-10 helps minimize the physical distance between CaV2-mediated calcium influx and primed vesicles, increasing the speed and probability of release. (janosi2021rimandrimbinding pages 1-3, kushibiki2019rimb1rimbindingproteinand pages 10-11, janosi2021rimandrimbinding pages 17-19)

## 4. Pathway and interaction model

A useful evidence-based sequence is:

1. **Active-zone assembly and delivery.** UNC-104/KIF1A-dependent transport is required for presynaptic accumulation of UNC-10 and other core active-zone proteins. SYD-2/Liprin-α is a major organizer, but residual UNC-10, RIMB-1, ELKS-1, and UNC-2 localization in relevant mutants demonstrates parallel and redundant assembly routes. (oh2021unc2cav2channel pages 37-39, oh2021unc2cav2channel pages 8-11)
2. **Vesicle recognition.** Vesicle-associated GTP-RAB-3 binds the UNC-10 Rab-binding region, concentrating vesicles near dense projections. (gracheva2008directinteractionsbetween pages 1-2)
3. **Release-site maturation.** UNC-10 and UNC-13 separately contribute to positioning membrane-contacting vesicles within the active-zone domain; UNC-10 promotes the post-docking transition to a fusion-competent state, probably by supporting UNC-13-dependent syntaxin opening. (koushika2001apostdockingrole pages 1-2, koushika2001apostdockingrole pages 6-7, krout2023c.elegansclarinetcla1 pages 11-12)
4. **Calcium-channel coupling.** UNC-10, together with RIMB-1, SYD-2, and ELKS-1, clusters UNC-2/CaV2 near the primed-vesicle pool. (kushibiki2019rimb1rimbindingproteinand pages 10-11, oh2021unc2cav2channel pages 37-39, oh2021unc2cav2channel pages 8-11)
5. **Fast exocytosis.** Action-potential-driven calcium influx through UNC-2 triggers fusion of the locally primed vesicles. UNC-10 therefore regulates release probability mainly by spatial and conformational organization, rather than by catalyzing a chemical reaction. (koushika2001apostdockingrole pages 1-2, janosi2021rimandrimbinding pages 1-3)

CLA-1/Clarinet adds another layer to this network. A 2023 PNAS study showed that CLA-1 recruits RIMB-1 and UNC-13 and helps orchestrate presynaptic release. These data place CLA-1 in the same functional active-zone system as UNC-10, but the gathered evidence does not establish a direct CLA-1–UNC-10 interaction. It is safer to describe their relationship as convergent or network-level rather than direct binding. (krout2023c.elegansclarinetcla1 pages 11-12)

## 5. Recent developments, 2023–2024

The most informative recent peer-reviewed UNC-10-adjacent advance is the **23 May 2023** PNAS analysis of CLA-1. Using endogenous tagging and genetic dissection, it refined the hierarchy of worm active-zone proteins: CLA-1 helps recruit RIMB-1 and UNC-13, while prior and accompanying analyses place UNC-10 in the overlapping channel-localization and vesicle-release machinery. This supports a modern view of the active zone as a resilient, distributed scaffold rather than a linear pathway controlled by one master protein. DOI: [10.1073/pnas.2220856120](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2220856120). (krout2023c.elegansclarinetcla1 pages 11-12)

Recent work has also shifted emphasis from static channel presence to channel positioning and synaptic homeostasis. The 2021 endogenous-tagging study established parallel SYD-2-dependent and independent pathways for UNC-2 localization, and later circuit-level work found that loss of RIMB-1 displaces channels and release sites away from dense projections and delays transmission. Together, these findings support the expert interpretation that UNC-10’s central role is nanoscale organization: it converts channel and vesicle abundance into efficient coupling. (oh2021unc2cav2channel pages 37-39, oh2021unc2cav2channel pages 8-11, janosi2021rimandrimbinding pages 17-19)

A 2024 PNAS study reported that presynaptic neurons inversely couple release efficiency to CaV2-channel abundance, revealing homeostatic feedback between exocytosis and channel levels. UNC-10/UNC-13 perturbation was used in that mechanistic framework. This result broadens the relevance of UNC-10 from a fixed scaffold to a node within adaptable release-site organization, although the evidence gathered here does not justify assigning the entire feedback mechanism specifically to UNC-10. The publication is Xiong, Richmond, and Kim, August 2024, DOI: [10.1073/pnas.2404969121](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2404969121).

## 6. Biological consequences and real-world use

At organismal level, *unc-10* null animals remain viable but exhibit severe locomotor, behavioral, pharmacological, and synaptic-transmission defects. These broad phenotypes are downstream consequences of defective release rather than separate primary molecular functions. The electrophysiological and ultrastructural phenotypes at the neuromuscular junction are the more precise evidence for annotation. (koushika2001apostdockingrole pages 2-3, koushika2001apostdockingrole pages 1-2, koushika2001apostdockingrole pages 6-7)

The principal current application is as an experimentally tractable model of conserved RIM biology. UNC-10 puncta are used as active-zone markers; *unc-10* mutants and combinations with *rimb-1*, *syd-2*, *elks-1*, and *cla-1* dissect redundancy in active-zone assembly; and endogenously tagged UNC-2 and scaffold proteins permit quantitative live-animal studies of channel localization. The worm system combines intact-circuit behavior, neuromuscular electrophysiology, electron microscopy, CRISPR tagging, and single-neuron imaging, making it valuable for studying mechanisms relevant to mammalian RIM/CaV2/Munc13 release complexes. (oh2021unc2cav2channel pages 37-39, oh2021unc2cav2channel pages 8-11, krout2023c.elegansclarinetcla1 pages 11-12)

## 7. Confidence assessment and unresolved questions

**High-confidence annotations** are: exact gene/protein identity; presynaptic active-zone and dense-projection localization; multidomain scaffold/adaptor classification; selective interaction with GTP-RAB-3; a post-docking role in generating fusion-competent vesicles; and contribution to UNC-2/CaV2 clustering. These conclusions are supported by convergent genetics, rescue, biochemistry, electrophysiology, EM, immunostaining, and endogenous fluorescence imaging. (koushika2001apostdockingrole pages 2-3, koushika2001apostdockingrole pages 1-2, gracheva2008directinteractionsbetween pages 1-2, oh2021unc2cav2channel pages 37-39)

**Moderate-confidence mechanistic details** include the exact molecular route from UNC-10 through UNC-13 to syntaxin opening, direct versus indirect binding to worm UNC-2, and the hierarchy among UNC-10, RIMB-1, ELKS-1, SYD-2, and CLA-1. Genetic and localization evidence is strong, but redundancy makes pairwise causality difficult to infer. (kushibiki2019rimb1rimbindingproteinand pages 10-11, oh2021unc2cav2channel pages 8-11, krout2023c.elegansclarinetcla1 pages 11-12)

Important open questions are the functions of individual UNC-10 splice isoforms, absolute active-zone stoichiometry, context dependence across cholinergic, GABAergic, peptidergic, and other synapses, and how much of the release defect derives from vesicle targeting versus priming versus channel coupling. Reporter intensity should not be mistaken for molecule counts, and conserved mammalian RIM interactions should not automatically be assigned to UNC-10 without worm-specific tests.
