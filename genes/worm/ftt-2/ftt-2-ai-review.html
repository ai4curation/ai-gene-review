Gene Description

FTT-2 is one of two C. elegans 14-3-3 proteins (with PAR-5/FTT-1), a small, dimeric phospho-serine/threonine-binding adaptor of the 14-3-3 family. Its best characterized role is in DAF-2 insulin/IGF-1-like signaling, where FTT-2 binds the FOXO transcription factor DAF-16 and retains it in the cytoplasm, restraining DAF-16 target-gene transcription; ftt-2 knockdown causes DAF-16 nuclear accumulation and enhances daf-2 dauer formation, whereas par-5 knockdown does not. FTT-2 also binds the sirtuin SIR-2.1 and is required, partly redundantly with PAR-5, for SIR-2.1- and HCF-1-dependent lifespan extension and stress-induced DAF-16 activation. It similarly restrains nuclear accumulation of the YAP homolog YAP-1. FTT-2 is found in the cytoplasm and nucleus.

Suggested Questions for Experts

Q: Does FTT-2 binding to DAF-16 require the AKT/SGK phospho-sites, as for mammalian 14-3-3/FOXO, and how does that reconcile with 14-3-3 being required for SIR-2.1-dependent DAF-16 activation after heat stress?

Q: Why does FTT-2, but not the ~80% identical PAR-5, regulate DAF-16 localization and dauer formation?

Key findings

  • FTT-2 binds DAF-16 and sequesters it in the cytoplasm. ftt-2 RNAi (but not par-5 RNAi) enhances daf-2 dauer formation and causes DAF-16 nuclear accumulation PMID:17098225.
  • 14-3-3 proteins bind SIR-2.1 and are required for sir-2.1-driven lifespan extension. This acts in parallel to insulin signaling PMID:16777605.
  • ftt-2 knockdown causes YAP-1 nuclear accumulation PMID:23396260.

Decisions

  • The DAF-16 protein binding row was changed (MODIFY) to DNA-binding transcription factor binding. The other protein binding rows were REMOVEd.
  • GO:0051457 maintenance of protein location in nucleus (NAS): UNDECIDED. The cached abstract gives no basis for it, and it runs opposite to the cytoplasmic sequestration role.
