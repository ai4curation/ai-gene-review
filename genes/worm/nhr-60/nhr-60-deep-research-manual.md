# nhr-60 (F57A10.5; UniProt O17898) - manual literature review

No deep-research API was available for this session; this summary was compiled by hand
from the cached publications, the UniProt record and a PubMed search
(`nhr-60 elegans`, 2026-10-28, which returns a single dedicated paper, PMID:17579999).

## Identity and family

- C. elegans nuclear hormone receptor family member NHR-60, ORF F57A10.5, WormBase
  WBGene00003650, GeneID 180059, 443 aa, chromosome V. UniProt: "Belongs to the nuclear
  hormone receptor family." (RuleBase:RU004334).
- Domain architecture (UniProt FT): nuclear-receptor DNA-binding domain 44..121 containing
  two NR C4-type zinc fingers (47..67, 83..104), and an NR ligand-binding domain 196..439
  (PROSITE PS51843). InterPro IPR049636 HNF4-like DBD; CDD cd06960 NR_DBD_HNF4A.
- NHR-60 is a supplementary nuclear receptor (supnr). [PMID:15983867 "We show that the
  supplementary nuclear receptors (supnrs) originated from an explosive burst of
  duplications of a unique orphan receptor, HNF4."] Supnrs on chromosome V are noted to
  evolve rapidly [PMID:15983867 "the supplementary nuclear receptors include a group of
  very rapidly evolving genes found primarily on chromosome V"].
- No ligand is known; UniProt: "Orphan nuclear receptor (Potential)."

## Expression and localization (PMID:17579999, abstract only in cache)

- [PMID:17579999 "NHR-60, a supnr expressed ubiquitously throughout development with a
  distinct pattern of localization on the nuclear periphery"].
- [PMID:17579999 "Both antibody staining and GFP reporter genes demonstrated high-level
  expression and accumulation of NHR-60 in seam cell nuclei that is dependent on NHR-23
  activity."]
- UniProt (curated from this paper): "Localized in the nucleus from the one-cell stage of
  development onwards. Localizes to the nuclear periphery of all cells." Also expressed in
  pharyngeal gland cells, VC4/VC5 neurons, uv1 cells and the L1 germ line.

## Loss-of-function

- [PMID:17579999 "Interference with NHR-60 activity, by either RNAi or overexpression of a
  putative dominant negative isoform, results in embryonic and early larval lethality,
  including defects in seam cell development."]
- UniProt disruption phenotype: arrest at the two-fold stage, incomplete ventral closure,
  elongation defects, absent or misplaced seam cells in head and tail.
- These are necessity phenotypes; no direct target genes are known, so they do not by
  themselves place NHR-60 in a specific developmental process.

## Physical interaction with the corepressor GEI-8 (PMID:23484030, full text)

- GEI-8 is the C. elegans NCoR/SMRT homologue. [PMID:23484030 "the C-terminal region I of
  GEI-8 that includes the predicted NR1 binding site showed affinity to GST-NHR-60 but not
  to the control protein expressing the GST anchor used for pull-down experiments
  (Figure S1)."]
- Rationale given by the authors: [PMID:23484030 "Mammalian HNF-4 alpha interacts both
  physically and functionally with SMRT [25] raising the possibility that NHR-60 may
  similarly interact with GEI-8."]
- Caveats: in vitro GST pull-down with in vitro translated GEI-8 fragments; supplementary
  figure; [PMID:23484030 "The figure presents one of two experiments that gave similar
  results."] No functional (repression) assay with NHR-60 was reported.

## Open questions

- Direct DNA-binding specificity (HNF4-like DR1 half-sites?) and target genes are not
  determined.
- Whether NHR-60 is ligand-regulated is unknown.
- Mechanism of nuclear-periphery localization is unknown.
