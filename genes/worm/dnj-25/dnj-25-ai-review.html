Gene Description

DNJ-25 is the single Caenorhabditis elegans auxilin, a J-domain co-chaperone of the auxilin/cyclin G-associated kinase family with a C-terminal DnaJ domain preceded by low-complexity clathrin-binding regions (the scaffold entry is the 786-residue isoform d). Like mammalian auxilin, it binds clathrin-coated vesicles and, through its J domain, recruits and stimulates the ATPase of the cytosolic Hsc70 chaperone HSP-1 to dismantle the clathrin lattice after membrane scission, releasing naked vesicles and free clathrin for reuse. The worm protein reproduces the in vitro uncoating co-chaperone activity of mammalian auxilin; its depletion by RNAi blocks receptor-mediated yolk endocytosis in oocytes, mislocalizes clathrin and freezes clathrin dynamics in many cell types, causes clathrin accumulation in the epidermis and molting defects, and arrests larval development. It supplies the auxilin step of clathrin-mediated endocytosis in all tissues, including the regeneration of synaptic vesicles at nerve terminals.

Functional annotation report: Caenorhabditis elegans dnj-25 (A0A486WXP9)

Executive conclusion

The identity check is consistent: A0A486WXP9 corresponds to the C. elegans locus W07A8.3/dnj-25, and published literature lists W07A8.3 as dnj-25, an Hsp40/DNAJ-family protein with the alternative name Auxilin. This matches the supplied UniProt description and its InterPro/Pfam DnaJ-domain annotations. No evidence from a different organism or similarly named gene was used. (frumkin2014challengingmusclehomeostasis pages 5-6)

The best-supported primary function is as an auxilin-like J-domain co-chaperone in clathrin-mediated membrane trafficking. DNJ-25 is not an enzyme with an independently catalyzed reaction, a transporter, or a structural coat subunit. Rather, it is predicted to recruit and activate an Hsp70-family chaperone—most plausibly Hsc70—at clathrin-coated vesicles, promoting coat disassembly and recycling after vesicle formation. Gene-specific perturbation evidence supports this assignment: inhibiting DNJ-25 causes clathrin accumulation in the hyp7 epidermis, reduced clathrin mobility in coelomocytes, and molting defects. Direct DNJ-25–Hsc70 binding, ATPase stimulation, and purified-vesicle uncoating have not, however, been demonstrated in the retrieved studies. (joseph2020controlofclathrinmediated pages 23-25)

An important nomenclature caveat is that mammalian auxilin 1 is encoded by DNAJC6. The available worm literature calls W07A8.3 the worm DNAJC6/auxilin homolog, but the retrieved evidence does not provide a formal phylogenetic analysis or a residue-level comparison. “Auxilin ortholog” is therefore a strong published assignment, while exact one-to-one orthology and complete domain equivalence were not independently re-established here. (joseph2020controlofclathrinmediated pages 23-25, ziv2014identificationofnovel pages 9-10)

2. Primary molecular function

2.1 Most likely biochemical role

The mechanistically coherent annotation is:

DNJ-25 is an auxilin-like co-chaperone that couples clathrin-coated-vesicle turnover to the ATPase cycle of Hsc70/Hsp70, thereby promoting clathrin-coat disassembly and coat-component recycling.

Auxilin-family proteins recruit Hsc70 to assembled clathrin coats. Their J-domain stimulates the otherwise relatively slow ATP hydrolysis of Hsp70-family chaperones, converting ATP-bound, lower-affinity Hsp70 into an ADP-bound state that tightly captures a client. Applied to auxilin biology, the relevant client system is the assembled clathrin coat rather than a small-molecule substrate. Thus, there is no DNJ-25-catalyzed reaction or substrate specificity in the conventional enzymatic sense; ATP hydrolysis occurs in the partner Hsc70, with DNJ-25 acting as its spatially targeted activator. (zhang2023jdomainproteinchaperone pages 3-4, zhang2023jdomainproteinchaperone pages 1-3, zhang2023jdomainproteinchaperone pages 24-28)

Recent structural synthesis describes the canonical J-domain as an approximately 70-residue, four-helix module. Helices II and III form the principal Hsp70-contact surface, and the conserved HPD region lies in their connecting loop. The J-domain engages Hsp70’s nucleotide-binding domain, interdomain linker, and substrate-binding domain, stabilizing an ATP-hydrolysis-competent conformation and coupling client loading to ATP cleavage. This is strong family-level evidence for how A0A486WXP9 could work, but it remains to be verified experimentally for DNJ-25 itself. (zhang2023jdomainproteinchaperone pages 3-4, zhang2023jdomainproteinchaperone pages 24-28)

2.2 Gene-specific functional evidence

The strongest direct evidence comes from Joseph and colleagues’ 2020 analysis of clathrin-mediated endocytosis. They identify DNJ-25 as the worm auxilin ortholog and report that its inhibition:

  1. increased clathrin accumulation in the hyp7 epidermis;
  2. reduced clathrin mobility in coelomocytes; and
  3. caused molting defects. (joseph2020controlofclathrinmediated pages 23-25)

These findings fit defective coat disassembly: if auxilin/Hsc70-mediated uncoating is impaired, clathrin should persist in relatively immobile assemblies rather than efficiently returning to the reusable cytosolic pool. The data therefore strongly support an endocytic/clathrin-turnover role. Nevertheless, this mechanistic interpretation is stronger than the observations alone; the retrieved passage did not report purified uncoating assays, DNJ-25–Hsc70 binding measurements, or rescue using an HPD-mutant versus wild-type DNJ-25. (joseph2020controlofclathrinmediated pages 23-25)

3. Cellular and tissue localization

No comprehensive endogenous-expression atlas or validated full-length DNJ-25 localization study was identified in the retrieved literature. Accordingly, a claim that the protein is exclusively neuronal, epidermal, cytosolic, or membrane-bound would be premature.

The most defensible localization statement is functional rather than anatomical: DNJ-25 acts at or near clathrin-coated membrane-trafficking intermediates. The perturbation evidence places relevant activity in at least two cellular contexts:

  • hyp7 epidermal syncytium, where inhibition produces excess clathrin accumulation; and
  • coelomocytes, professional endocytic cells in which clathrin mobility is reduced after inhibition. (joseph2020controlofclathrinmediated pages 23-25)

Because molting depends on intense membrane traffic and extracellular-matrix/cuticle remodeling in the epidermis, the hyp7 phenotype provides a plausible cellular connection to the organismal molting defect. It does not prove that DNJ-25 is restricted to hyp7 or that all molting effects originate there.

The predicted site of action is therefore the cytoplasmic face of clathrin-coated pits or newly formed coated vesicles, where an auxilin can engage both coat machinery and cytosolic Hsc70. This is an orthology- and phenotype-based model, not direct imaging evidence for A0A486WXP9.

4. Biological process and pathway placement

4.1 Clathrin-mediated endocytosis and vesicle recycling

DNJ-25 is best placed late in the clathrin-coated-vesicle cycle:

  1. cargo and adaptors assemble a clathrin coat at a donor membrane;
  2. membrane invagination and scission generate a coated vesicle;
  3. DNJ-25/auxilin recruits Hsc70 to the coat;
  4. J-domain-stimulated Hsc70 ATP hydrolysis drives destabilization and removal of clathrin; and
  5. clathrin and accessory factors are recycled for additional trafficking rounds.

The observed combination of excess clathrin and reduced mobility after DNJ-25 inhibition is more consistent with a defect in coat turnover/uncoating than with DNJ-25 being a cargo receptor or a clathrin structural subunit. (joseph2020controlofclathrinmediated pages 23-25)

4.2 Interaction with NEKL kinase-controlled endocytosis

The 2020 study found that DNJ-25 inhibition phenocopied aspects of defects caused by loss of NIMA-related NEKL kinases. The authors proposed that NEKL kinases might operate upstream of, or in parallel with, Hsc70–auxilin-mediated clathrin uncoating. This remains a pathway model: the retrieved evidence does not establish DNJ-25 as a NEKL phosphorylation substrate or demonstrate a direct physical or genetic interaction. (joseph2020controlofclathrinmediated pages 23-25)

4.3 Development and molting

A published table reports a WormBase-derived larval-arrest annotation, “Lva(3),” for dnj-25. In addition, the direct 2020 inhibition experiment found molting defects. These observations suggest that DNJ-25-dependent trafficking is developmentally important, plausibly because larval molts impose substantial demands on epidermal membrane traffic. The larval-arrest record lacks allele, penetrance, and assay details in the retrieved source and should therefore be treated as secondary annotation rather than a fully characterized phenotype. (frumkin2014challengingmusclehomeostasis pages 5-6, joseph2020controlofclathrinmediated pages 23-25)

A candidate RNAi screen for muscle proteostasis reported no phenotype for W07A8.3 under that screen’s conditions. This does not contradict the epidermal/endocytic phenotype; it indicates that DNJ-25 was not detectably required in that particular muscle-homeostasis assay or that knockdown/sensitization was insufficient. (frumkin2014challengingmusclehomeostasis pages 5-6)

4.4 Possible DNA-damage-tolerance association

A 2014 mammalian translesion-synthesis study noted that the C. elegans DNAJC6 homolog W07A8.3 had scored as a hit in an earlier mutagenesis screen and cited this as support for possible involvement in translesion synthesis. The retrieved text does not describe the worm screen’s design or phenotype and does not show that DNJ-25 acts at DNA lesions, binds a TLS polymerase, or regulates polymerase stability. This should be considered a low-confidence secondary association, not a coequal primary function. Indirect effects through trafficking, proteostasis, development, or stress tolerance remain possible. (ziv2014identificationofnovel pages 9-10)

5. Current understanding from 2023–2024 research

No 2023–2024 study specifically dissecting dnj-25 was found. Recent advances instead refine interpretation of its J-domain:

  • A 2023 review emphasizes that J-domain proteins recruit Hsp70 and couple client transfer to ATP hydrolysis; the J-domain is the conserved catalytic-regulatory interface, whereas regions outside it largely determine client choice and cellular deployment. Published January 2023; DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tcb.2022.05.004. (zhang2023jdomainproteinchaperone pages 3-4, zhang2023jdomainproteinchaperone pages 1-3)
  • The same review shows that J-domains are not universally interchangeable. Electrostatic differences around helices II–III and the HPD-proximal interface can tune pairing with particular Hsp70 paralogs. Thus, direct assays are needed before assuming that DNJ-25 interacts equivalently with every worm Hsp70. (zhang2023jdomainproteinchaperone pages 6-8)
  • A February 2024 expert review stresses that generic ATPase stimulation is insufficient to define a J-domain protein’s biological specificity. Non-J-domain architecture, topology, localization, and client context determine whether a JDP acts in the cytosol, ER, mitochondria, ribosome, or another specialized system. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cstres.2023.12.002. (marszalek2024jdomainproteinsfrom pages 3-4, marszalek2024jdomainproteinsfrom pages 4-5)

For DNJ-25, these developments support the Hsc70-co-chaperone mechanism but also caution against inferring exact localization or client range from PF00226 alone. The gene-specific clathrin phenotypes—not merely the presence of a J-domain—are what prioritize the auxilin/uncoating annotation.

6. Applications and real-world relevance

Functional-genomics application

dnj-25 offers a genetically tractable worm model for investigating how auxilin/Hsc70-driven coat turnover supports tissue physiology. Useful applications include live imaging of fluorescent clathrin in hyp7 or coelomocytes, analysis of molting-associated membrane traffic, and modifier screens involving NEKL kinases, AP2, clathrin, and Hsp70 machinery. The reported changes in clathrin accumulation and mobility establish practical cellular readouts for such studies. (joseph2020controlofclathrinmediated pages 23-25)

Comparative disease biology

Because the worm protein is identified as an ortholog of mammalian DNAJC6/auxilin, it may be useful for testing conserved variants or mechanisms relevant to disorders of synaptic vesicle recycling. Such translational use is presently prospective: the retrieved studies did not report human DNAJC6 variant complementation in dnj-25-deficient worms or demonstrate a neuron-specific DNJ-25 phenotype.

Assay development

The clearest mechanistic experiments would combine:

  • endogenous DNJ-25 fluorescent tagging and tissue-resolved imaging;
  • conditional or tissue-specific depletion to separate epidermal, coelomocyte, and neuronal requirements;
  • fluorescence-recovery or single-particle measurements of clathrin dynamics;
  • co-immunoprecipitation or proximity labeling for Hsc70 and coat partners;
  • reconstituted Hsc70 ATPase and clathrin-uncoating assays; and
  • rescue with wild-type versus HPD-disrupting J-domain mutants.

These experiments would distinguish a direct auxilin mechanism from indirect effects on trafficking or proteostasis.

7. Evidence strength, statistics, and limitations

The literature for this exact protein is limited. The strongest evidence is qualitative, gene-specific perturbation evidence from 2020 in two cellular settings and at the organismal level. The retrieved passage reports increased clathrin accumulation, reduced mobility, and molting defects but provides no extractable effect sizes, sample sizes, confidence intervals, or penetrance values. It would therefore be misleading to invent quantitative statistics. (joseph2020controlofclathrinmediated pages 23-25)

The available evidence can be graded as follows:

  • High confidence: W07A8.3 is dnj-25; it is annotated as an Hsp40/DNAJ protein and auxilin; its inhibition perturbs clathrin behavior and molting. (frumkin2014challengingmusclehomeostasis pages 5-6, joseph2020controlofclathrinmediated pages 23-25)
  • Moderate confidence: its principal role is Hsc70-dependent clathrin uncoating at cytoplasmic coated-vesicle intermediates. This is strongly supported by orthology and phenocopy but lacks direct worm biochemical reconstitution. (joseph2020controlofclathrinmediated pages 23-25)
  • Moderate family-level inference: its J-domain activates an Hsp70 ATPase through the canonical HPD-containing interface. This mechanism is current consensus for functional J-domains but remains untested for DNJ-25. (zhang2023jdomainproteinchaperone pages 3-4, zhang2023jdomainproteinchaperone pages 24-28)
  • Low-to-moderate confidence: participation in translesion synthesis or DNA-damage tolerance; current support is only a reported screen hit plus homology-based interpretation. (ziv2014identificationofnovel pages 9-10)
  • Unknown: exact endogenous subcellular distribution, tissue-expression breadth, direct protein partners, Hsp70-paralog preference, detailed domain architecture outside the supplied J-domain annotation, and whether multiple isoforms differ functionally.

Final annotation

Recommended concise functional annotation:

dnj-25 encodes a C. elegans auxilin-like J-domain co-chaperone. It most likely recruits and activates Hsc70 at clathrin-coated trafficking intermediates to promote coat disassembly and clathrin recycling. DNJ-25 activity is relevant in the hyp7 epidermis and coelomocytes and is required for normal molting. This assignment is supported by increased clathrin accumulation and reduced clathrin mobility following DNJ-25 inhibition; direct biochemical demonstration of DNJ-25-dependent Hsc70 activation and clathrin uncoating remains outstanding. (joseph2020controlofclathrinmediated pages 23-25)

The gene symbol is not being conflated with a different protein: all gene-specific conclusions above concern C. elegans W07A8.3/*dnj-25*, consistent with UniProt A0A486WXP9.
