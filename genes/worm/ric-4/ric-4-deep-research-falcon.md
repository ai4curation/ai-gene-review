# Functional annotation report: *C. elegans ric-4* (UniProt A5PEW5)

## Executive conclusion

The identity check is affirmative: the requested protein is the *Caenorhabditis elegans* gene product RIC-4, locus/ORF **Y22F5A.3**, and not an unrelated use of “RIC-4.” Organism-specific literature consistently identifies *ric-4* as the worm ortholog of neuronal **SNAP-25** and a member of the SNAP-25 family. This agrees with the supplied UniProt assignment and its SNAP-25/T-SNARE domains. RIC-4 is therefore neither an enzyme nor a transporter; it is a membrane-fusion factor whose principal structural role is to supply the two Q-SNARE helices of a neuronal SNARE bundle. Together with plasma-membrane UNC-64/syntaxin and vesicular SNB-1/synaptobrevin, it drives fusion of neurotransmitter-containing synaptic vesicles with the presynaptic plasma membrane. Genetic loss reduces acetylcholine release and produces aldicarb resistance. Recent work extends RIC-4 biology to presynaptic CaV2-channel homeostasis and to non-neuronal epidermal wound repair. (calahorro2018thepresynapticmachinery pages 6-7, kang2011essentialrolesof pages 1-2, xiong2024presynapticneuronsselftune pages 5-6)

## 1. Mandatory identity verification

### Gene, organism, and protein family

The supplied accession **A5PEW5** corresponds to *C. elegans ric-4*, with the locus names **Y22F5A.3/CELE_Y22F5A.3**. Independent worm literature describes RIC-4 as the synaptic SNAP-25 ortholog and one of three SNAP-25-family proteins reported in *C. elegans*, together with AEX-4 and K02D10.5/SNAP-29. This establishes agreement among gene symbol, organism, and family assignment. (kang2011essentialrolesof pages 1-2)

The supplied InterPro/Pfam annotations—SNAP-25 domain (IPR000928), t-SNARE domain (IPR000727), and PF00835—are mechanistically coherent with that identification. SNAP-25-family proteins are Qbc-SNAREs: their conserved helical SNARE motifs participate in assembly of the membrane-bridging SNARE bundle. Thus, “synaptosomal-associated protein” is a family designation, not evidence of enzymatic catalysis. The retrieved literature did not independently map A5PEW5’s precise domain boundaries or establish accession-specific lipid modifications; those details should remain database-level annotations rather than experimentally proven properties of this exact protein record.

### Ambiguity assessment

Searches retrieved many unrelated meanings of “RIC,” including remote ischemic conditioning. Those records were excluded. No evidence was found that the worm symbol in this report refers to a different protein. The literature’s use of “RIC-4/SNAP-25,” neuronal expression, and SNARE-dependent phenotypes matches the supplied UniProt description. (calahorro2018thepresynapticmachinery pages 6-7, kang2011essentialrolesof pages 1-2)

## 2. Primary molecular function

### Core SNARE role

RIC-4 is the SNAP-25/Qbc-SNARE component of the presynaptic fusion machinery. In the canonical worm neuronal complex, plasma-membrane **UNC-64/syntaxin** contributes a Qa-SNARE helix, vesicular **SNB-1/synaptobrevin** contributes the R-SNARE helix, and RIC-4 contributes the Qb and Qc elements. Assembly or “zippering” of these helices brings the synaptic-vesicle and plasma membranes into close apposition and supplies energy for membrane fusion. This is a structural/mechanical function, so there is no catalytic reaction or small-molecule substrate specificity to report. (calahorro2018thepresynapticmachinery pages 6-7, calahorro2018thepresynapticmachinery pages 2-4)

Functionally, RIC-4 acts after vesicles reach the active zone, participating in the priming/fusion stages that make neurotransmitter release possible. UNC-13 promotes productive SNARE assembly, UNC-18 regulates syntaxin and assembly, and synaptotagmin provides Ca²⁺-dependent triggering within the broader pathway. The retrieved passages establish this pathway architecture but do not constitute purified-protein fusion reconstitution using A5PEW5 itself; the finest structural assignment therefore combines worm genetics with conserved SNAP-25-family biochemistry. (calahorro2018thepresynapticmachinery pages 6-7, calahorro2018thepresynapticmachinery pages 2-4)

### Functional genetic evidence

*ric-4* mutants were identified through resistance to **aldicarb**, an acetylcholinesterase inhibitor. In a normal animal, blocking acetylcholine degradation causes excessive cholinergic stimulation and paralysis; resistance is a standard indication that less acetylcholine reaches the neuromuscular junction. Reviews of the primary genetic work attribute the *ric-4* phenotype to impaired synaptic-vesicle fusion and reduced acetylcholine release, rather than to acetylcholine synthesis or postsynaptic receptor failure. RNAi depletion similarly causes aldicarb resistance. (calahorro2018thepresynapticmachinery pages 6-7, kang2011essentialrolesof pages 1-2)

Exact aldicarb time courses, effect sizes, and rescue values were not available in the retrieved full-text excerpts, so they should not be reconstructed from secondary summaries. Nevertheless, the direction and mechanistic interpretation of the phenotype are consistent across authoritative sources.

## 3. Cellular and anatomical localization

### Tissue and cell-type expression

RIC-4 is expressed broadly in the nervous system. Reported anatomical expression includes the **nerve ring, commissures, ventral nerve cord, and dorsal nerve cord**. A genomic fosmid transcriptional reporter placed *ric-4* among broadly pan-neuronal genes, although expression intensity varied reproducibly between neuron classes. (calahorro2018thepresynapticmachinery pages 6-7, stefanakis2015regulatorylogicof pages 4-6, stefanakis2015regulatorylogicof pages 6-8)

Independent smFISH supported neuronally restricted *ric-4* transcription. Expression was first detected around the embryonic comma stage, after neurons had been generated, consistent with activation during postmitotic neuronal differentiation. The analyzed reporter included about **4.3 kb upstream sequence plus the first intron** and was active in essentially all neurons. (stefanakis2015regulatorylogicof pages 6-8)

A critical technical distinction is that the fosmid used an SL2-linked nuclear YFP-H2B reporter. It reports where *ric-4* is transcribed, not where native RIC-4 protein resides within a neuron. Earlier antibody evidence supports broad nervous-system protein expression, but the retrieved data are insufficient to map native A5PEW5 at nanometer-scale active-zone resolution. (stefanakis2015regulatorylogicof pages 4-6, stefanakis2015regulatorylogicof pages 6-8)

### Subcellular site of action

The best-supported site of RIC-4’s principal neuronal action is the **presynaptic plasma membrane/release apparatus**, where it acts with UNC-64 and vesicular SNB-1. This assignment follows from its SNAP-25-family role, the canonical SNARE complex, and the release phenotype. It is stronger as a functional localization than as direct imaging of endogenous RIC-4. (calahorro2018thepresynapticmachinery pages 6-7, calahorro2018thepresynapticmachinery pages 2-4)

Recent non-neuronal work places RIC-4-dependent fusion in the large adult epidermal syncytium **hyp7**, where a SYX-2/RIC-4/SEC-22 complex participates in membrane delivery during repair. Thus, neuronal presynapses are the principal established context, but they are not the only cellular context in which RIC-4 can operate. (wijaya2024reevaluatinggolgifragmentation pages 6-8)

## 4. Pathways and molecular regulation

### Synaptic-vesicle exocytosis pathway

The pathway can be summarized as follows:

1. Synaptic vesicles are targeted and docked near active zones.
2. UNC-13 and UNC-18 regulate syntaxin availability and productive SNARE assembly.
3. RIC-4 and UNC-64 on the target membrane assemble with SNB-1 on the vesicle.
4. Ca²⁺ entry and synaptotagmin-dependent triggering promote final SNARE zippering and bilayer fusion.
5. Neurotransmitter, including acetylcholine at cholinergic synapses, is released into the synaptic cleft. (calahorro2018thepresynapticmachinery pages 6-7, calahorro2018thepresynapticmachinery pages 2-4)

### TOM-1/tomosyn inhibition

TOM-1 is a negative regulator of vesicle priming. Its synaptobrevin-like R-SNARE motif can engage a syntaxin–SNAP-25 context and form an inhibitory complex, thereby competing with productive SNB-1-containing assembly. In GST pull-down experiments, syntaxin bound SNAP-25, whereas the tested TOM-1 C-terminal fragment did not form a stable syntaxin complex in the absence of SNAP-25. This supports regulation at the assembled syntaxin/SNAP-25 stage rather than a simple stable TOM-1–syntaxin binary interaction. (gracheva2006tomosyninhibitssynaptic pages 11-12)

The primary TOM-1 study also found increased release/priming phenotypes in *tom-1* mutants, but the retrieved quantitative physiology pertains primarily to TOM-1 rather than to *ric-4* mutation itself. It should therefore be used to define regulation of the RIC-4-containing pathway, not as a direct measurement of RIC-4 activity. (calahorro2018thepresynapticmachinery pages 6-7, gracheva2006tomosyninhibitssynaptic pages 11-12)

## 5. Recent developments, 2023–2024

### Epidermal wound repair—2023 primary study and 2024 synthesis

A 2023 *Cell Reports* study reported that a **RIC-4/SEC-22/SYX-2 SNARE complex** promotes *C. elegans* epidermal wound healing (Shao et al., published November 2023; DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.celrep.2023.113349). A 2024 review places this machinery in the adult hyp7 wound model, where Golgi material is recruited toward damaged plasma membrane and polarized secretion supplies membrane and phosphoinositide-related material required for repair. Blocking Golgi recruitment impaired repair and caused high post-injury lethality. (wijaya2024reevaluatinggolgifragmentation pages 6-8)

This work is important because it broadens RIC-4 annotation beyond neurotransmission: the same general SNARE principle is redeployed with different partners—SYX-2 and SEC-22—for somatic membrane repair. However, the retrieved primary article was not available in full text, and the accessible review excerpt did not provide the exact *ric-4*-specific effect size, sample number, or survival percentage. The pathway assignment is well supported, but quantitative details should be obtained directly from the Cell Reports figures before database deposition. (wijaya2024reevaluatinggolgifragmentation pages 6-8)

### Homeostatic control of presynaptic CaV2 abundance—2024

Xiong, Richmond, and Kim studied the loss-of-function allele **ric-4(md1088)** in a 2024 *PNAS* paper (published August 2024; DOI: https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2404969121). Loss of RIC-4 increased the intensity of GFP::UNC-2/CaV2 puncta in the dorsal nerve cord, indicating greater channel abundance at presynaptic sites when SNARE-dependent exocytosis is inefficient. The comparison used an unpaired Student’s *t* test, although the accessible excerpt did not supply the exact *P* value, sample size, or fold change. (xiong2024presynapticneuronsselftune pages 5-6)

The same study calibrated endogenous UNC-2 fluorescence against GFP-tagged yeast Cse4 standards and estimated approximately **100 UNC-2 channels per synapse**, with substantial synapse-to-synapse variability. This number describes baseline channel abundance in the experimental system; it is not the size of the *ric-4* effect. UNC-2 localized to active-zone-associated puncta and did not colocalize with the endocytic marker UNC-11, strengthening the presynaptic interpretation. (xiong2024presynapticneuronsselftune pages 2-3)

The authors’ model is a negative-feedback system: inefficient exocytosis increases presynaptic CaV2 abundance, while elevated channel function is associated with lower abundance. RIC-4 thus participates not only in the final fusion machinery but also indirectly in homeostatic tuning of release-site calcium-channel content. (xiong2024presynapticneuronsselftune pages 5-6, xiong2024presynapticneuronsselftune pages 4-4, xiong2024presynapticneuronsselftune pages 2-3)

## 6. Current applications and practical significance

RIC-4 has no direct clinical or industrial implementation; it is a basic-research protein in a model organism. Its principal applications are experimental:

- **Dissecting conserved SNARE mechanisms:** worm genetics permits in vivo testing of priming, fusion, and SNARE-regulator interactions in an intact nervous system.
- **Assaying presynaptic output:** aldicarb resistance provides a scalable functional readout for impaired cholinergic release, although it is not specific to RIC-4 or to a single exocytic step. (calahorro2018thepresynapticmachinery pages 6-7, kang2011essentialrolesof pages 1-2)
- **Mapping neuron-wide gene regulation:** *ric-4* reporters and smFISH serve as examples of broadly neuronal terminal-differentiation genes and reveal heterogeneous expression strength across neuron types. (stefanakis2015regulatorylogicof pages 4-6, stefanakis2015regulatorylogicof pages 6-8)
- **Studying synaptic homeostasis:** *ric-4(md1088)* is a perturbation for testing how release efficiency feeds back onto UNC-2/CaV2 channel abundance. (xiong2024presynapticneuronsselftune pages 5-6)
- **Investigating membrane repair:** the epidermal SYX-2/RIC-4/SEC-22 complex provides an in vivo system for studying polarized secretion and Golgi participation after plasma-membrane injury. (wijaya2024reevaluatinggolgifragmentation pages 6-8)

These uses make RIC-4 relevant to conserved principles of neurotransmission and membrane repair, but translation to human SNAP-25 biology should be framed as comparative-model inference rather than a worm-specific therapeutic claim.
