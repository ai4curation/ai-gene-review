Gene Description

UNC-47 is the vesicular GABA transporter of Caenorhabditis elegans and the founding member of the VGAT/VIAAT (SLC32A1) family. It is a ten-transmembrane protein of the synaptic vesicle membrane, expressed in GABAergic neurons, that concentrates cytosolic GABA into the vesicle lumen using the proton electrochemical gradient generated by the vesicular V-ATPase, so that GABAergic vesicles release transmitter on exocytosis. unc-47 mutants were isolated as uncoordinated animals defective in GABA release, and the gene's expression pattern is the standard marker of GABAergic identity in the worm. Its vertebrate orthologues load glycine in addition to GABA at inhibitory terminals.

Functional annotation report: C. elegans unc-47 / UNC-47

Executive conclusion

The requested identity is verified. In Caenorhabditis elegans, unc-47 (ORF T20G5.6; UniProt P34579) encodes UNC-47, the worm vesicular GABA transporter (VGAT), also called Uncoordinated protein 47. It is not UNC-49, the postsynaptic GABA_A receptor, or UNC-46, a trafficking factor for UNC-47. The literature describes unc-47 as the first gene identified in any species to encode VGAT, fully consistent with the supplied amino-acid/polyamine-transporter-2 family and AA_transpt_TM/Aa_trans domain annotations (McIntire et al., published 9 October 1997, DOI; Zhou and Bessereau, published 13 December 2019, DOI). (zhou2019moleculararchitectureof pages 1-2)

Its primary molecular function is to transport cytosolic GABA into presynaptic secretory vesicles. Loaded vesicles undergo regulated exocytosis, allowing GABA to activate postsynaptic receptors. Thus, UNC-47 acts at the vesicle membrane upstream of transmitter release—not in GABA synthesis, extracellular uptake, or receptor signaling itself. Direct worm evidence is strongest for GABA as the physiological substrate. Although VGAT/VIAAT-family proteins can transport glycine, physiologically relevant UNC-47-dependent glycinergic signaling in C. elegans remains unestablished. (accardi2010molecularcharacterizationof pages 31-36, serranosaiz2017aneurotransmitteratlas pages 16-17)

1. Identity, family, and molecular classification

The protein description, organism, gene symbol, and function are mutually consistent across the supplied UniProt record and the worm literature. UNC-47 is the C. elegans counterpart of vertebrate SLC32A1/VGAT/VIAAT and belongs to a membrane-transporter family distinct from the vesicular monoamine, acetylcholine, and glutamate transporter families. Historical VGAT models predicted approximately ten transmembrane segments, supporting its classification as an integral vesicle-membrane protein; however, an exact worm-specific topology and transport stoichiometry should not be asserted solely from comparative VGAT literature. Expert reviews describe VIAAT/SLC32 as comparatively less mechanistically characterized than other vesicular neurotransmitter transporters. (anne2014vesicularneurotransmittertransporters pages 16-19)

The supplied InterPro AA_transpt_TM (IPR013057) and Pfam Aa_trans (PF01490) assignments therefore align with the literature: UNC-47 is a multi-pass amino-acid transporter operating across an intracellular-organelle membrane. The family context also includes related vacuolar amino-acid transporters, reinforcing an evolutionary association with transport across acidic organelles rather than the plasma membrane.

2. Primary biochemical function and substrate specificity

UNC-47 loads GABA from neuronal cytosol into synaptic vesicles, creating the releasable transmitter pool. The core pathway is:

glutamate → UNC-25/glutamate decarboxylase → cytosolic GABA → UNC-47-dependent vesicle loading → Ca²⁺-triggered vesicle fusion → GABA-receptor activation.

At body-wall neuromuscular junctions, released GABA acts mainly through UNC-49 GABA_A receptors to inhibit or relax muscle. SNF-11 is instead a sodium-dependent plasma-membrane transporter involved in extracellular GABA uptake and clearance; it should not be conflated with the vesicular transporter UNC-47. (accardi2010molecularcharacterizationof pages 31-36, zhou2019moleculararchitectureof pages 2-4)

Vesicular neurotransmitter accumulation is ultimately energized by the electrochemical proton gradient generated by vesicular V-ATPase. That family-level principle is applicable to VGAT/VIAAT, but the retrieved evidence does not justify a precise UNC-47-specific H⁺:GABA coupling ratio, turnover rate, K_m, or detailed alternating-access mechanism. Those fields should therefore remain unannotated or explicitly marked as inferred from orthologs rather than experimentally measured in the worm protein. (anne2014vesicularneurotransmittertransporters pages 16-19)

GABA is the high-confidence physiological substrate. The broader transporter family can accommodate glycine, and worm atlas authors note this possibility, but C. elegans lacks obvious canonical glycine-receptor orthologs. Accordingly, glycine transport is best treated as a biochemical/family possibility, not an established physiological role of UNC-47 in the worm. (serranosaiz2017aneurotransmitteratlas pages 16-17)

3. Subcellular localization and trafficking

UNC-47 carries out its primary function in the membrane of presynaptic GABA-containing vesicles, concentrated at neuronal release sites. This location is functionally required: cytosolic GABA cannot support ordinary quantal signaling unless it is packaged into release-competent vesicles.

Correct vesicular targeting depends on UNC-46, a LAMP-family sorting/trafficking factor. UNC-46 is expressed in most GABA-positive, UNC-47-positive neurons and is required for appropriate vesicular localization of UNC-47. Consequently, UNC-46 loss compromises vesicular GABA transmission without making UNC-46 itself a GABA transporter. GABA-negative cells expressing an unc-47 reporter often lack unc-46, another reason not to infer conventional GABA-vesicle function from unc-47 transcription alone. (gendrel2016acellularand pages 37-38, gendrel2016acellularand pages 6-8)

A useful annotation distinction is therefore:

  • Molecular site of action: synaptic/secretory-vesicle membrane.
  • Cellular compartment: presynaptic terminals and vesicle pools of neurons capable of vesicular GABA release.
  • Not its primary site: plasma membrane, postsynaptic membrane, or extracellular space.

4. Cellular expression and circuit role

Early anti-GABA mapping identified 26 neurons: 19 D-class body motor neurons, four RME head motor neurons, AVL and DVB enteric motor neurons, and the RIS interneuron. A later map added 10 GABA-positive neurons, while a regulatory synthesis concluded that nine neuron classes use GABA for synaptic signaling. (gendrel2016acellularand pages 25-26, zhou2019moleculararchitectureof pages 2-4)

A more complete fosmid reporter detected unc-47 in canonical GABAergic neurons and in newly recognized GABA-positive ALA, RIB, SMDD, and SMDV neurons. AVF takes up GABA but lacks detectable unc-47, supporting a clearance role rather than vesicular rerelease. GABA-positive AVA, AVB, and AVJ also lacked detectable transporter in that study, leaving open alternative release mechanisms, expression below detection, or other transporters. (gendrel2016acellularand pages 6-8)

At body-wall neuromuscular junctions, cholinergic motor neurons contract one side of the animal while activating D-type GABAergic motor neurons that relax opposing muscle. This reciprocal excitation/inhibition generates out-of-phase dorsal and ventral contractions required for sinusoidal locomotion. Laser ablation or disruption of GABA signaling produces simultaneous contraction and the characteristic “shrinker” response. Each body-wall muscle extends approximately five arms toward motor axons and receives both cholinergic and GABAergic input. (zhou2019moleculararchitectureof pages 2-4)

UNC-47 also supports GABA output from neurons controlling head musculature and the enteric/defecation circuit. The physiological sign of GABA is receptor-dependent: although UNC-49 generally mediates anion conductance and muscle inhibition, specialized cation-selective GABA receptors can support excitatory GABA actions in other cells. UNC-47 should therefore be annotated as enabling vesicular GABA transmission rather than intrinsically “inhibitory” in every circuit.

5. Transcriptional and post-translational control

The PITX-family homeodomain transcription factor UNC-30 coordinates the GABAergic terminal program in D-type motor neurons by regulating both unc-25, which synthesizes GABA, and unc-47, which packages it. This couples transmitter production and release competence. Other neuron classes use modular regulatory mechanisms; for example, NHR-67 contributes to unc-47 regulation in selected GABAergic identities. (gendrel2016acellularand pages 25-26)

Later cis-regulatory analyses support a modular architecture for GABA-pathway genes: separate elements can direct expression in distinct neuron classes. Therefore, short unc-47 promoters are useful experimental tools but may not faithfully recapitulate the endogenous expression domain. This limitation motivated fosmid and CRISPR reporters containing more complete regulatory information.

At the protein-trafficking level, UNC-46 controls the delivery or retention of UNC-47 in synaptic vesicles. The regulatory hierarchy can thus be summarized as UNC-30/other terminal selectors → unc-47 transcription → UNC-46-dependent vesicle targeting → GABA loading and release.

6. Genetic and functional evidence

Loss-of-function unc-47 mutants can synthesize GABA but cannot package it normally for conventional synaptic release. They exhibit the shrinker phenotype and defective GABA-dependent enteric-muscle contractions. Their preserved response to the GABA receptor agonist muscimol distinguishes the lesion from postsynaptic unc-49 receptor defects and places UNC-47 presynaptically. (accardi2010molecularcharacterizationof pages 31-36, zhou2019moleculararchitectureof pages 1-2)

The worm remains viable and fertile under laboratory conditions after severe impairment of GABAergic transmission, making unc-47 mutants unusually useful for dissecting inhibitory circuits, synapse organization, behavioral output, and compensatory signaling without organismal lethality. (zhou2019moleculararchitectureof pages 1-2)

7. Recent developments, 2023–2024

7.1 Synaptic feedback and CaV2 organization—2023

Zhao et al. used vesicular GABA-release mutants, including unc-47, to examine feedback at the GABAergic neuromuscular junction. The study found that loss of postsynaptic UNC-49 reduced presynaptic UNC-2/CaV2 puncta intensity by 32% and reduced the UNC-2-dependent calcium current by the same 32%. Muscle-specific UNC-49B rescue restored the defect. Other active-zone proteins—including UNC-10/RIM, SYD-2/liprin-α, RAB-3, and NRX-1/neurexin—were unchanged, arguing for selective receptor-dependent regulation of presynaptic CaV2 rather than broad synapse disruption. The 32% result concerns the UNC-49→UNC-2 feedback pathway, not UNC-47 transport kinetics, but unc-47 is a key genetic tool for eliminating vesicular GABA release in this framework (published October 2023, DOI). (zhao2023postsynapticgabaareceptors pages 3-5)

7.2 Endogenous-style neurotransmitter atlas—2024

Wang et al. constructed the CRISPR reporter unc-47(syb7566[unc-47::SL2::GFP::H2B]) and compared it with a fosmid reporter and single-cell RNA-sequencing data. The systems agreed for most neurons, while identifying weak or variable signals in cells such as M1, I1, and AVJ. Dim expression occurred in SMD and ALA and variably in IL1; ADF expression was male-specific. Male-specific tail expression included PDB, AS11, PVN, and PHC, while PLM showed very dim expression in both sexes. The analysis supported expression in all GABA-synthesizing and most GABA-uptake neurons but also in many cells lacking evidence of GABA (published 15 October 2024, DOI). (wang2024aneurotransmitteratlas pages 11-12, wang2024aneurotransmitteratlas pages 10-11)

This finding refines, rather than overturns, UNC-47’s established GABA-transport function. It raises an unresolved question: do the additional neurons use low or transient GABA, another UNC-47 substrate, or noncanonical transporter functions? Reporter transcription alone cannot answer that question.

7.3 Systemic stress signaling—2024

Recent work has moved UNC-47 beyond locomotor-circuit analysis. Cornell et al. reported that loss of unc-47 activates the intestinal mitochondrial unfolded-protein-response reporter, supporting a neuro-intestinal circuit in which GABA restrains extrasynaptic acetylcholine signaling through GABA_B receptors and thereby suppresses intestinal UPR^mt. This uses UNC-47 deficiency as evidence that vesicular GABA output influences organism-wide proteostasis; it does not imply that UNC-47 itself is present in intestinal mitochondria (published 7 August 2024, DOI).

Related 2024 work found GABA to be a systemic suppressor in an ASI–RIM/TGF-β mitochondrial-stress circuit and used unc-47 mutants to test the contribution of vesicular GABA transmission. These studies broaden the biological consequences of UNC-47-dependent release while leaving its core biochemical annotation unchanged.

8. Current applications and real-world implementation

UNC-47 is used in four principal ways:

  1. Genetic elimination of vesicular GABA release. unc-47 mutants isolate presynaptic transmitter-packaging defects while leaving GABA synthesis and, often, receptor responsiveness intact.
  2. GABA-neuron targeting. unc-47 regulatory sequences drive fluorescent reporters, optogenetic proteins, calcium indicators, RNA-interference machinery, or rescue constructs in GABAergic neurons. Short promoters require caution because endogenous expression is more extensive and modular.
  3. Neurotransmitter-identity mapping. Fosmid and CRISPR unc-47 reporters complement anti-GABA staining and single-cell transcriptomics, helping distinguish synthesizing, uptake, and candidate releasing cells.
  4. Disease, toxicology, and systems-physiology models. UNC-47-positive neurons are used to study inhibitory synapse development, seizure-like behavior, neurodegeneration, pollutant toxicity, aging, immunity, mitochondrial stress, and organism-wide proteostasis.

These are research implementations rather than clinical applications; no direct therapeutic use of worm UNC-47 is established. Its broader relevance derives from conservation with mammalian SLC32A1/VGAT and the tractability of the C. elegans nervous system.

9. Expert interpretation and remaining uncertainties

The strongest annotation is narrow and mechanistically clear: UNC-47 is a presynaptic vesicle-membrane transporter required to package GABA for regulated secretion. Evidence from mutant phenotype, agonist responsiveness, transporter-family conservation, expression in GABA-releasing neurons, and dependence on the vesicle-sorting factor UNC-46 converges on this conclusion. (accardi2010molecularcharacterizationof pages 31-36, gendrel2016acellularand pages 6-8, zhou2019moleculararchitectureof pages 1-2)

Several annotations should remain qualified:

  • Exact topology: multi-pass membrane topology is secure, but a definitive UNC-47-specific structural model was not established by the retrieved worm studies.
  • Energetic stoichiometry and kinetics: proton-gradient dependence is supported by vesicular-transporter family biology, but precise H⁺ coupling, K_m, V_max, and turnover values should not be imported uncritically from vertebrate VGAT.
  • Glycine: transport competence is plausible at the family level; physiological glycinergic signaling in C. elegans is not demonstrated.
  • GABA-negative UNC-47-positive neurons: modern atlases establish the expression discrepancy, not its molecular explanation. The 2017 male atlas counted 76 non-GABAergic UNC-47-positive neurons, including 48 male-specific neurons and nine sex-shared neurons with male-specific GABA-negative expression. (serranosaiz2017aneurotransmitteratlas pages 16-17)
  • Reporter interpretation: SL2 nuclear CRISPR reporters measure transcription, whereas native UNC-47 activity requires protein translation, vesicle targeting, an electrochemical driving force, substrate availability, and exocytosis.

Final functional annotation

Recommended concise annotation:

UNC-47 (P34579; T20G5.6) is the Caenorhabditis elegans vesicular GABA transporter. It is a multi-pass amino-acid/polyamine-transporter-family protein localized to presynaptic secretory-vesicle membranes, where it loads cytosolic GABA into vesicles for regulated release. Its expression is coordinated with GABA synthesis by neuron-type-specific transcriptional programs including UNC-30, and its synaptic-vesicle localization depends on UNC-46. Loss of UNC-47 abolishes effective vesicular GABA signaling, causing shrinker locomotion and enteric-muscle defects while leaving postsynaptic receptor responsiveness intact.

unc-47 (P34579) — vesicular GABA transporter — curation notes

Identity

  • UniProt P34579 (UNC47_CAEEL, reviewed), WormBase T20G5.6, 486 aa, ten predicted
    transmembrane domains; amino acid/polyamine transporter 2 family. Orthologue of mammalian
    VGAT/VIAAT (SLC32A1).

Literature

McIntire et al. 1997 (PMID:9349821, abstract only)

  • unc-47 was the founding VGAT gene: "Studies in the nematode Caenorhabditis elegans have
    implicated the gene unc-47 in the release of GABA" PMID:9349821.
  • "Here we show that the sequence of unc-47 predicts a protein with ten transmembrane
    domains, that the gene is expressed by GABA neurons, and that the protein colocalizes with
    synaptic vesicles" PMID:9349821 — basis of the IDA synaptic vesicle annotation.
  • The transport assay was done on the rat homologue: "a rat homologue of unc-47 is expressed
    by central GABA neurons and confers vesicular GABA transport in transfected cells with
    kinetics and substrate specificity similar to those previously reported for synaptic
    vesicles from the brain" PMID:9349821.
  • Bioenergetics differ from VMAT: "Comparison of this vesicular GABA transporter (VGAT) with
    a vesicular transporter for monoamines shows that there are differences in the
    bioenergetic dependence of transport" PMID:9349821.
  • UniProt summarises the worm function as "Involved in the uptake of GABA into the synaptic
    vesicles" with ECO:0000269|PubMed:9349821.

Assessment

  • Core: GABA loading into synaptic vesicles at the synaptic vesicle membrane of GABAergic
    neurons. The GOA set has the process terms (GO:0015812 GABA transport, GO:0098700
    neurotransmitter loading into synaptic vesicle) and the location terms, but no GABA
    molecular-function term; the only MF row is GO:0015187 glycine transmembrane transporter
    activity (IBA). Mammalian VGAT carries GO:0015185 (GABA transmembrane transporter
    activity, IMP/ISS/IEA in QuickGO for SLC32A1 and rat Slc32a1), so the missing GABA MF is a
    gap rather than a convention.
  • Glycine: mammalian VGAT loads glycine as well as GABA, and the IBA node is seeded from
    mouse/rat VGAT where glycine transport is experimentally demonstrated (rat Slc32a1
    GO:0015187 IMP PMID:19843525). C. elegans has no glycinergic transmission, and no worm
    data address glycine; these rows are retained but not treated as core for the worm.
  • Dendrite terminus (IBA from mouse VGAT) has no worm support and does not match the
    presynaptic site of action; kept as non-core.

Deep research

  • unc-47-deep-research-falcon.md supports the review, including the glycine caveat: GABA is
    the physiological substrate, "Thus, UNC-47 acts at the vesicle membrane upstream of
    transmitter release", and while VGAT/VIAAT-family proteins can transport glycine,
    UNC-47-dependent glycinergic signalling in C. elegans is unestablished.
