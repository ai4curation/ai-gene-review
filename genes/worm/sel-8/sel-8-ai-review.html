Gene Description

sel-8 (also called lag-3) encodes the sole C. elegans member of the Mastermind/MAML family of Notch transcriptional coactivators. The glutamine-rich nuclear protein is recruited to DNA-bound LAG-1 (CSL) by the intracellular domains of the LIN-12 and GLP-1 Notch receptors, forming the CSL-Notch ICD-Mastermind ternary complex whose structure has been solved with the worm proteins; within this complex SEL-8/LAG-3 contacts the composite groove formed by the Notch ankyrin repeats and the CSL C-terminal domain and acts as the transcriptional activation module. It is required for essentially all LIN-12- and GLP-1-mediated cell fate decisions, including germline stem cell maintenance, anchor cell/ventral uterine specification, vulval and mesodermal (M lineage) patterning, and embryonic development. LAG-3 has also been reported to contribute to CEP-1/p53-dependent germ cell apoptosis after DNA damage.

Comprehensive Research Report: sel-8 (LAG-3) in Caenorhabditis elegans

Gene Identity and Nomenclature

The gene sel-8 (also known as lag-3; UniProt accession Q09260) encodes a nuclear transcriptional coactivator in Caenorhabditis elegans that is essential for Notch signaling (chen2020glp1notch—lag1csl pages 2-4, chen2004sel7apositive pages 1-2). The protein is named LAG-3 (for "Lin-12 And Glp-1 phenotype") based on its requirement for both major Notch-type signaling pathways in the worm, and SEL-8 (for "Suppressor/Enhancer of Lin-12") from genetic screens (chen2004sel7apositive pages 9-10). This gene was originally characterized around 2000 through combined genetic and biochemical approaches that identified it as a putative transcriptional activator required for LIN-12 and GLP-1 Notch signaling (chen2004sel7apositive pages 9-10).

Primary Molecular Function

Transcriptional Coactivator Role

SEL-8/LAG-3 functions as a Mastermind-like transcriptional coactivator in the canonical Notch signaling pathway (chen2020glp1notch—lag1csl pages 2-4). It is a glutamine-rich nuclear protein that does not bind DNA directly but instead serves as a critical bridging or scaffolding component within the Notch transcriptional activation complex (chen2004sel7apositive pages 1-2). Following ligand-dependent activation of Notch-type receptors (GLP-1 or LIN-12), proteolytic cleavage releases the receptor intracellular domain (ICD), which translocates to the nucleus (luo2020positiveautoregulationof pages 1-4, safdar2016ubr5aconserved pages 1-2). There, the Notch ICD associates with LAG-1, the C. elegans CSL-family DNA-binding transcription factor, and SEL-8/LAG-3 joins this complex to form a transcriptionally active ternary assembly (chen2020glp1notch—lag1csl pages 2-4, chen2004sel7apositive pages 1-2, safdar2016ubr5aconserved pages 1-2).

Mastermind-like identity: C. elegans SEL-8/LAG-3 is the functional counterpart of Mastermind-family Notch coactivators. This assignment rests primarily on its conserved role in the Notch transcription complex rather than strong primary-sequence similarity to non-nematode Mastermind proteins. (chen2004sel7apositive pages 9-10, chen2020glp1notch—lag1csl pages 2-4)

Mechanism and location: SEL-8/LAG-3 is a glutamine-rich nuclear protein. Following ligand-induced cleavage of GLP-1 or LIN-12, the receptor intracellular domain enters the nucleus and associates with LAG-1/CSL; SEL-8/LAG-3 joins this complex, bridging or stabilizing the receptor intracellular domain–LAG-1 assembly and converting it into a transcriptionally active complex. (chen2004sel7apositive pages 1-2, safdar2016ubr5aconserved pages 1-2)

Not a sequence-specific DNA-binding factor: Target-site recognition is supplied by LAG-1/CSL, which binds CSL motifs in regulatory DNA. SEL-8/LAG-3 instead provides coactivator or scaffolding activity; it has no known enzymatic substrate and is not established as independently recognizing DNA. The precise downstream biochemical step—such as direct recruitment of RNA polymerase or a particular chromatin modifier—remains unresolved. (chen2020glp1notch—lag1csl pages 2-4, luo2020positiveautoregulationof pages 1-4)

Context-dependent output: The same core coactivator supports different programs according to receptor, tissue, and cis-regulatory context. With GLP-1 in the germ line, it promotes transcription of lst-1 and sygl-1, maintaining stem-cell fate and preventing premature meiotic entry; with LIN-12 in somatic reproductive lineages, the complex participates in cell-fate specification and positive autoregulation of lag-1. Recent work also implicates SEL-8 in neuronal Notch transcription connecting hypodermal insulin signaling to associative memory. (chen2020glp1notch—lag1csl pages 2-4, luo2020positiveautoregulationof pages 12-15, murphy2024bodytobraininsulinand pages 9-11)

Blockquote: Mechanistic summary of SEL-8/LAG-3 as a nuclear, Mastermind-like component of the canonical Notch transcription complex, emphasizing its non-DNA-binding coactivator role and context-dependent outputs.

The specificity of target gene selection is determined by LAG-1/CSL, which recognizes CSL-binding motifs (such as TGGGAA or YRTGRGAA sequences) in regulatory DNA (luo2020positiveautoregulationof pages 1-4). SEL-8/LAG-3 provides coactivator function but does not independently recognize DNA sequences and has no known enzymatic activity (chen2020glp1notch—lag1csl pages 2-4). While the precise downstream biochemical mechanism—such as direct recruitment of RNA polymerase II or specific chromatin-modifying enzymes—remains to be fully elucidated, SEL-8/LAG-3 is essential for converting the LAG-1–Notch ICD interaction into productive transcriptional activation (chen2020glp1notch—lag1csl pages 2-4, luo2020positiveautoregulationof pages 1-4).

Evolutionary Relationship to Mastermind Proteins

SEL-8/LAG-3 is considered the functional counterpart of Mastermind-family proteins found in Drosophila and mammals (chen2004sel7apositive pages 9-10, chen2020glp1notch—lag1csl pages 2-4, zhang2021theroleof pages 162-166). This assignment is based primarily on its conserved role within the Notch transcriptional complex rather than strong primary-sequence similarity to Mastermind proteins from other organisms (chen2004sel7apositive pages 9-10). Like Mastermind in other species, SEL-8/LAG-3 occupies the coactivator position in the Notch–CSL–Mastermind transcriptional triad, linking activated Notch receptors to downstream gene activation (chen2020glp1notch—lag1csl pages 2-4).

Subcellular Localization

SEL-8/LAG-3 is a nuclear protein that carries out its function within the nucleus of C. elegans cells (chen2004sel7apositive pages 1-2, chen2004sel7apositive pages 7-9). It localizes to the nucleus where it participates in transcriptional complexes with LAG-1 and the intracellular domains of activated Notch receptors (chen2004sel7apositive pages 1-2, chen2004sel7apositive pages 7-9). This nuclear localization is consistent with its role as a transcriptional coactivator rather than a membrane-associated receptor or extracellular signaling molecule.

Biological Processes and Signaling Pathways

Germline Stem Cell Maintenance

One of the most extensively characterized functions of SEL-8/LAG-3 is its essential role in maintaining germline stem cells and regulating the proliferation-versus-differentiation decision in the C. elegans gonad (zhang2021theroleof pages 24-28, godard2026roleofrnainduced pages 34-39, chen2020glp1notch—lag1csl pages 2-4). In the distal germline, the somatic distal tip cell (DTC) expresses the Notch ligands LAG-2 and APX-1, which activate the GLP-1 Notch receptor on nearby germ cells (zhang2021theroleof pages 24-28, godard2026roleofrnainduced pages 34-39). Following GLP-1 activation, the cleaved GLP-1 intracellular domain enters germ cell nuclei and forms a transcriptional complex with LAG-1 and SEL-8/LAG-3 (zhang2021theroleof pages 24-28, chen2020glp1notch—lag1csl pages 2-4).

A landmark 2020 study by Chen et al. used comprehensive genome-wide approaches to identify the direct transcriptional targets of this GLP-1–LAG-1–SEL-8 complex (chen2020glp1notch—lag1csl pages 2-4, chen2020glp1notch—lag1csl pages 11-12). Through germline-specific ChIP-seq analysis, the researchers identified 137 genes with reproducible LAG-1 binding peaks in germ cells (chen2020glp1notch—lag1csl pages 9-11). However, when they intersected these ChIP-seq results with RNA-seq data identifying genes whose expression depends on GLP-1 signaling, only two protein-coding genes emerged as primary direct targets: lst-1 and sygl-1 (chen2020glp1notch—lag1csl pages 11-12, chen2020glp1notch—lag1csl pages 17-19, chen2020glp1notch—lag1csl pages 2-4). Time-course transcriptomics following auxin-induced LAG-1 degradation confirmed that lst-1 and sygl-1 mRNA levels declined approximately threefold within one hour and reached near-maximal reduction by two hours, supporting their classification as direct, primary transcriptional targets (chen2020glp1notch—lag1csl pages 12-14, chen2020glp1notch—lag1csl pages 14-16).

These two target genes—LST-1 and SYGL-1—function redundantly to maintain the germline stem cell fate (chen2020glp1notch—lag1csl pages 2-4, zhang2021theroleof pages 24-28, hubbard2019biologyofthe pages 11-13). They promote stem cell self-renewal by repressing three parallel pathways that drive meiotic entry and differentiation: the GLD-1 pathway, the GLD-2 pathway, and the SCF^PROM-1 pathway (chen2020glp1notch—lag1csl pages 2-4). LST-1 and SYGL-1 act in part through the FBF-1 and FBF-2 RNA-binding proteins to promote degradation or translational repression of mRNAs encoding meiotic-entry regulators (chen2020glp1notch—lag1csl pages 2-4, chen2020glp1notch—lag1csl pages 12-14). Loss of both lst-1 and sygl-1 phenocopies loss of GLP-1 signaling, causing premature entry into meiosis, while excessive expression of these genes maintains an expanded pool of stem-like cells and produces a tumorous germline phenotype (zhang2021theroleof pages 24-28, chen2020glp1notch—lag1csl pages 2-4).

Somatic Development and Cell Fate Specification

SEL-8/LAG-3 also functions with the LIN-12 Notch receptor in multiple somatic cell-fate decisions during C. elegans development (chen2004sel7apositive pages 1-2, luo2020positiveautoregulationof pages 15-18, luo2020positiveautoregulationof pages 10-12). In the developing reproductive system, LIN-12 activation regulates cell-fate choices in the vulval precursor cells (VPCs), the anchor cell/ventral uterine (AC/VU) decision, and the somatic gonad (luo2020positiveautoregulationof pages 15-18, luo2020positiveautoregulationof pages 10-12).

A 2020 study by Luo et al. demonstrated that LAG-1 expression is positively autoregulated in response to LIN-12 activation in somatic reproductive cells (luo2020positiveautoregulationof pages 4-7, luo2020positiveautoregulationof pages 1-4). This positive feedback depends on an enhancer region containing multiple LAG-1/CSL binding sites embedded within a conserved high-occupancy target (HOT) sequence (luo2020positiveautoregulationof pages 4-7). In vulval precursor cells receiving LIN-12 signals and adopting the secondary (2°) fate, LAG-1 protein levels are elevated compared to other VPCs (luo2020positiveautoregulationof pages 10-12, luo2020positiveautoregulationof pages 31-34). Deletion of the autoregulatory enhancer reduces LAG-1 expression and eliminates this positive feedback, resulting in defects in later execution of the 2° fate, abnormal vulval eversion, and strong egg-laying defects (luo2020positiveautoregulationof pages 15-18).

Importantly, this autoregulatory mechanism appears to be tissue-specific: while LAG-1 binding peaks were detected at the lag-1 locus in the germline, positive autoregulation was not functionally active there (luo2020positiveautoregulationof pages 4-7, luo2020positiveautoregulationof pages 1-4). This illustrates the context-dependent nature of SEL-8/LAG-3-mediated transcriptional regulation, where the same core molecular machinery produces different outputs depending on cellular environment, chromatin state, and cooperating factors (chen2020glp1notch—lag1csl pages 2-4, luo2020positiveautoregulationof pages 12-15, chen2020glp1notch—lag1csl pages 9-11).

Neuronal Function and Associative Memory

Recent discoveries in 2023-2025 have revealed a surprising role for SEL-8/LAG-3 in regulating neuronal function and associative memory in adult C. elegans (murphy2024bodytobraininsulinand pages 9-11, zhou2023signalingfromthe pages 6-7, zhou2025bodytobraininsulinand pages 7-8). Zhou et al. demonstrated that Notch signaling acts as a critical mediator of body-to-brain communication linking hypodermal insulin/IGF-1 signaling (IIS) to neuronal transcription and memory (zhou2025bodytobraininsulinand pages 7-8, zhou2025bodytobraininsulinand pages 6-7).

In this pathway, reduction of DAF-2 (the insulin/IGF-1 receptor) specifically in the hypodermis increases expression of the secreted Notch ligand OSM-11 (zhou2023signalingfromthe pages 6-7, murphy2024bodytobraininsulinand pages 6-9). OSM-11 then activates Notch receptors (primarily LIN-12) on neurons, particularly the RIG interneurons (zhou2023signalingfromthe pages 6-7). Following Notch activation, the intracellular domain enters neuronal nuclei and forms a transcriptional complex with LAG-1 and SEL-8/LAG-3, which activates downstream target genes including crh-1/CREB and potentially ins-19 (zhou2025bodytobraininsulinand pages 7-8, murphy2024bodytobraininsulinand pages 6-9, zhou2025bodytobraininsulinand pages 35-38). This Notch-CREB signaling axis is required for the memory enhancement produced by hypodermal DAF-2 reduction (murphy2024bodytobraininsulinand pages 9-11, zhou2025bodytobraininsulinand pages 7-8).

Critically, knockdown of sel-8 prevents the memory improvement caused by hypodermal DAF-2 degradation, demonstrating that SEL-8/LAG-3 is required for this non-autonomous, body-to-brain signaling pathway (murphy2024bodytobraininsulinand pages 9-11, murphy2024bodytobraininsulinand pages 6-9). However, sel-8 knockdown does not block memory enhancement caused by neuronal DAF-2 reduction, indicating that neuronal cell-autonomous IIS regulation of memory operates through a distinct mechanism independent of SEL-8-mediated Notch signaling (murphy2024bodytobraininsulinand pages 9-11, zhou2025bodytobraininsulinand pages 7-8, zhou2023signalingfromthe pages 13-13). This context-specificity further illustrates the nuanced roles of SEL-8/LAG-3 in different signaling contexts.

Experimental Evidence and Methodological Approaches

The functional characterization of SEL-8/LAG-3 has employed diverse experimental approaches across multiple studies:

Genetic analyses: Forward and reverse genetic screens identified sel-8/lag-3 mutations as suppressors or enhancers of Notch receptor phenotypes, establishing its requirement for GLP-1 and LIN-12 activity (chen2004sel7apositive pages 1-2, chen2004sel7apositive pages 9-10).

Biochemical complex studies: Protein interaction assays demonstrated that SEL-8 associates with LAG-1 and Notch receptor intracellular domains only when both components are present, supporting a model of ternary complex formation (chen2004sel7apositive pages 7-9).

Genome-wide chromatin immunoprecipitation: Sequential ChIP-seq targeting LAG-1 in purified germline tissue identified direct binding sites and distinguished true regulatory targets from non-functional binding events (chen2020glp1notch—lag1csl pages 9-11).

Transcriptomics: RNA-seq combined with auxin-inducible degron systems enabled time-resolved analysis of transcriptional responses following LAG-1 depletion, discriminating primary from secondary targets (chen2020glp1notch—lag1csl pages 12-14, chen2020glp1notch—lag1csl pages 14-16).

Tissue-specific protein degradation: The auxin-inducible degron (AID) system allowed conditional depletion of DAF-2 in specific tissues (hypodermis vs. neurons), revealing non-autonomous signaling mechanisms (zhou2025bodytobraininsulinand pages 7-8, zhou2025bodytobraininsulinand pages 6-7).

Behavioral assays: Associative memory tests using salt-food paired conditioning measured the functional impact of Notch pathway manipulation on learning and memory (zhou2025bodytobraininsulinand pages 7-8, zhou2025bodytobraininsulinand pages 6-7).

Context-Dependent Regulation and Tissue-Specific Functions

A recurring theme in SEL-8/LAG-3 biology is its context-dependent function. The same core transcriptional coactivator supports dramatically different biological outcomes depending on several factors:

Receptor context: SEL-8/LAG-3 functions with GLP-1 in the germline to maintain stem cells but with LIN-12 in somatic tissues to specify cell fates during development and in neurons to regulate memory (chen2020glp1notch—lag1csl pages 2-4).

Target gene selection: While LAG-1/CSL binding sites are present throughout the genome, only a small subset functions as bona fide Notch-responsive elements in any given cellular context, with lst-1 and sygl-1 being the primary germline targets (chen2020glp1notch—lag1csl pages 17-19, chen2020glp1notch—lag1csl pages 9-11).

Activation vs. repression: In most characterized contexts, the SEL-8-containing complex activates transcription (chen2020glp1notch—lag1csl pages 12-14). However, evidence suggests that in some somatic contexts (such as the AC/VU decision), LAG-1 may mediate default repression, though this appears minimal in the germline (luo2020positiveautoregulationof pages 12-15, chen2020glp1notch—lag1csl pages 12-14, chen2020glp1notch—lag1csl pages 17-19).

Positive feedback: LAG-1 positively autoregulates its own expression in LIN-12-active somatic reproductive cells but not in the GLP-1-active germline, despite LAG-1 binding peaks being present in both contexts (luo2020positiveautoregulationof pages 4-7, luo2020positiveautoregulationof pages 1-4).

Downstream Consequences of SEL-8/LAG-3-Mediated Transcription

The biological outcomes of SEL-8/LAG-3 transcriptional activity depend on the specific target genes activated:

Germline: Activation of lst-1 and sygl-1 leads to maintenance of the stem cell fate through repression of GLD-1, GLD-2, and SCF^PROM-1 meiotic-entry pathways, sustaining the proliferative germline progenitor pool and preventing premature differentiation (chen2020glp1notch—lag1csl pages 2-4, chen2020glp1notch—lag1csl pages 16-17, chen2020glp1notch—lag1csl pages 17-19).

Somatic development: Transcriptional reinforcement through lag-1 autoregulation supports robust cell-fate specification in vulval precursor cells and other somatic reproductive lineages, with downstream effects on terminal differentiation and organ morphogenesis (luo2020positiveautoregulationof pages 15-18, luo2020positiveautoregulationof pages 12-15).

Neurons and memory: Activation of CREB-associated memory genes promotes long-term associative memory formation, linking hypodermal metabolic state to neuronal plasticity through a non-autonomous signaling pathway (zhou2025bodytobraininsulinand pages 7-8, zhou2025bodytobraininsulinand pages 35-38).

In the germline, secondary targets such as epg-5, tbx-2, and others respond at later time points following LAG-1 depletion but lack LAG-1 binding peaks, suggesting they are indirect targets regulated downstream of LST-1 and SYGL-1 activity (chen2020glp1notch—lag1csl pages 14-16, chen2020glp1notch—lag1csl pages 16-17).

Current Understanding and Future Directions

SEL-8/LAG-3 represents a critical node in C. elegans Notch signaling, functioning as a transcriptional coactivator that is absolutely required for Notch-dependent gene activation across multiple developmental and physiological contexts. The protein operates as part of a conserved transcriptional mechanism—the Notch ICD–CSL–Mastermind ternary complex—that has been maintained across metazoan evolution.

Key insights from recent research (2020-2025) include:

  1. The identification of lst-1 and sygl-1 as the primary direct transcriptional targets in the germline, providing unprecedented molecular detail about how Notch signaling controls stem cell fate (chen2020glp1notch—lag1csl pages 11-12, chen2020glp1notch—lag1csl pages 17-19).

  2. The demonstration of positive autoregulation of lag-1 in somatic contexts, revealing an additional layer of transcriptional feedback that enhances robustness of developmental decisions (luo2020positiveautoregulationof pages 4-7, luo2020positiveautoregulationof pages 1-4).

  3. The discovery of SEL-8/LAG-3's role in non-autonomous body-to-brain signaling linking metabolism to memory, expanding the known functions of Notch signaling beyond development into adult neuronal plasticity (zhou2025bodytobraininsulinand pages 7-8, zhou2025bodytobraininsulinand pages 6-7).

While SEL-8/LAG-3 has been extensively characterized functionally, certain aspects remain incompletely understood. The precise structural domains within SEL-8/LAG-3 that mediate its coactivator function have not been fully mapped, and the downstream biochemical steps—such as recruitment of specific chromatin modifiers or transcriptional machinery components—await detailed characterization. Nevertheless, the available evidence clearly establishes SEL-8/LAG-3 as an essential, versatile transcriptional coactivator that enables context-appropriate Notch signaling outputs across the C. elegans lifecycle.

sel-8 / lag-3 (C. elegans) review notes

  • UniProt: Q09260 (LAG3_CAEEL), "Protein lag-3"; gene name sel-8, synonym lag-3; ORF C32A3.1; 490 aa, glutamine-rich.
  • PANTHER: none listed in the UniProt record (no DR PANTHER line). InterPro IPR021587 / Pfam PF11498 (Activator_LAG-3). Mastermind-family relationship is functional/structural (N-terminal helix binding the CSL-ANK groove) rather than detectable by a shared PANTHER family.
  • No IBA annotations in GOA.

Key findings

  • Two groups found it independently: SEL-8 (Greenwald; lin-12(gf) suppressor) and LAG-3 (Kimble; two-hybrid with LAG-1/GLP-1 ICD).
