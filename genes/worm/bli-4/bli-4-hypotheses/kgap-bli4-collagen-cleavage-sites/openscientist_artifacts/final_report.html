BLI-4 (P51559) and the cuticle collagen cleavage sites of SQT-3 and DPY-17

Hypothesis slug: kgap-bli4-collagen-cleavage-sites Gene: bli-4 / BLI-4 / KPC-4, Caenorhabditis elegans (NCBITaxon:6239), UniProt P51559 Source file: genes/worm/bli-4/bli-4-ai-review.yaml (selector: free-text) Focus type: free_text, substrate-site analysis Date: 2026-11-25

Executive judgment

Verdict: supported, with one framing correction and one methodological limit.

Every step of the seed hypothesis was executed and every step returned a positive result. BLI-4 retains the complete furin-type catalytic and substrate-binding apparatus: the triad Asp202, His241 and Ser415, the oxyanion-hole Asn342, and the acidic S1 and S4 subsites, with 14 of 14 queried furin determinants identical at 54.2% identity over the peptidase S8 plus P/Homo B region. SQT-3 and DPY-17 both carry an R-X-X-R immediately upstream of their triple-helical domains, in predicted-disordered and solvent-accessible segments, at a position conserved across ten Caenorhabditis species. The best-supported candidate sites are DPY-17 R61-V-R-R64 ↓ Gln65 and SQT-3 R79-T-T-R82 ↓ Gln83, with DPY-5 R77-H-K-R80 ↓ Ser81 third.

Two things qualify that verdict and both matter for curation.

The framing correction. The hypothesis is written as a prediction to be made. It is not a prediction any more. Birnbaum et al. (PMID: 37333289) showed with endogenous fluorescent knock-ins that bli-4 mutants and collagen cleavage-site mutants both fail to secrete SQT-3 and DPY-17, which instead accumulate as large intracellular aggregates. Jain et al. (PMID: 40110475) independently name the same consensus furin cleavage sites in the same two collagens. A curator should record this as experimental evidence (IMP, and IGI for the dpy-5 / bli-4 interaction), not as a sequence-based prediction. Writing it up as a computational lead would understate what is known.

The methodological limit. The substrate-site analysis cannot, on its own, name BLI-4 as the enzyme. All four C. elegans kex2/subtilisin convertases retain 11 or 12 of 12 furin catalytic and S1/S4 pocket residues, and KPC-1 is more furin-like than BLI-4 by identity to furin (57.5% against 46.5%). KPC-1 is "broadly expressed in C. elegans neurons and epithelia" (PMID: 23932402). Motif analysis identifies a generic PCSK site; only the bli-4 loss-of-function data assigns it to BLI-4.

The remaining real gap is biochemical. No in vitro assay with purified BLI-4, no N-terminal sequencing or mass spectrometry of a cleaved collagen product, and no mobility-shift western showing loss of the processed species in bli-4 mutants was found. The phrase "cleaves SQT-3 and DPY-17" should be curated as "required for N-terminal processing and secretion of SQT-3 and DPY-17" until a cleavage product is mapped.

Summary

This investigation ran the six analytical steps named in the seed hypothesis against public sequence and structure resources, then checked the computational result against primary literature. The sequence work confirms the mechanistic premise completely. BLI-4 is a furin-class convertase, not merely a kexin-class one: the acidic S1 residues Asp305, Asp348 and Asp353 and the S4 subsite Glu283, Trp301 and Asp311 are exactly the determinants responsible for furin's requirement for Arg at P1 and Arg at P4. That licenses the use of furin substrate rules for scoring candidate BLI-4 sites, so a predicted R-X-X-R in a collagen is mechanistically motivated rather than a generic dibasic guess. The alignment-derived triad matches the independently curated UniProt active-site annotation and matches the genetics: the molecular-null allele h384 replaces Ser415 with Phe (PMID: 10903434).

The substrate scan found a conserved family-wide feature at a fixed architectural position. Of 36 R-X-X-R occurrences across 16 cuticle collagens, 21 lie in the N-terminal non-collagenous domain upstream of the first sustained Gly-X-Y block, and 15 of 16 collagens carry at least one (BLI-2 is the sole exception), at 5.5-fold enrichment over a composition-matched shuffled null. The GXY-proximal site lies a median of 26 residues upstream of the collagenous domain, with Gln at P1' in 9 of 15. These sites are in low-confidence, predicted-disordered AlphaFold segments (window pLDDT 49.1 against a whole-protein mean of 58.0, Wilcoxon signed-rank p = 0.019, n = 21; 19 of 21 below pLDDT 70), which is the structural requirement for convertase access. The sites are present in 139 of 150 (93%) non-elegans Caenorhabditis collagen-sized entries across ten species. Independent, non-circular evidence that R-X-X-R is the operative BLI-4 consensus comes from the enzyme itself: BLI-4 autoprocesses at its own R113-V-K-R116 ↓ DYIL to remove propeptide 23-116.

Two results change what a curator should do with all of this. First, the motif analysis has almost no discriminating power within the collagen family, and here it anti-correlates with experimental evidence. DPY-17 ranks 1 of 25 candidate sites on a transparent composite score, but SQT-3 ranks only 14 of 25 because its R-T-T-R has non-basic P2 and P3, and SQT-3 is one of the two collagens with direct in vivo validation. Top-ranked DPY-7, DPY-9 and SQT-2 have no published BLI-4 processing evidence at all. Second, SQT-3 is processed at both ends by unrelated proteases: the astacin DPY-31 performs the C-terminal cut (PMID: 15579684), 219 residues from the BLI-4 site. Any curation statement about BLI-4 and SQT-3 must specify the N terminus.

Key findings

F001: BLI-4 retains the complete furin-type catalytic triad, oxyanion hole and acidic S1/S4 pockets

Global Needleman-Wunsch alignment (BLOSUM62, gap open -11, gap extend -1) of BLI-4 (P51559) residues 101-660 against human furin (P09958) residues 61-600 gave 54.2% identity over the aligned peptidase S8 plus P/Homo B region, alignment score 1323. The same BLI-4 region aligned to S. cerevisiae kexin (P13134) at 38.1% identity. BLI-4 is therefore closer to the furin subfamily than to kexin itself, which matters because furin and kexin differ in exactly the subsite that sets P4 specificity.

All 14 queried furin catalytic and substrate-binding residues map to identical residues in BLI-4 (14/14):

Two independent checks confirm the alignment. The derived triad D202/H241/S415 is identical to the UniProt active-site annotation for P51559, which was curated without reference to this alignment. And Ser415 is the residue replaced by Phe in the bli-4 molecular null h384: PMID: 10903434 reports that "allele h384 represents a molecular null mutant in which the catalytically essential serine residue (Ser415) is replaced by phenylalanine". The genetics therefore confirms the computationally assigned nucleophile. UniProt domain boundaries: peptidase S8 at 168-483, P/Homo B at 491-629, autolytic cleavage site at 116-117.

PMID: 7774813 places the gene in the family directly: "bli-4 encodes gene products related to the kex2/subtilisin-like family of proprotein convertases".

The practical consequence is specificity. The acidic S1 residues and the S4 subsite give furin a preference for Arg at P1 and Arg at P4, meaning an R-X-X-R consensus with the optimal form R-X-K/R-R, rather than a simple dibasic KR/RR site. Scoring collagen sites against furin rules is therefore justified, which is what step 2 of the hypothesis asked.

F002: A single accessible R-X-X-R sits immediately upstream of the Gly-X-Y domain in 15 of 16 cuticle collagens

A regular-expression scan of 16 UniProt cuticle collagen sequences found 36 R-X-X-R occurrences in total. Of these, 21 lie in the N-terminal non-collagenous domain, upstream of the first sustained Gly-X-Y block, defined as 4 or more consecutive GXY triplets. 15 of 16 collagens carry at least one such site; BLI-2 is the exception.

Composition-matched sequence shuffling (200 permutations per protein, 3200 permutations in total) predicts 0.24 N-terminal R-X-X-R sites per collagen against 1.31 observed, a 5.5-fold enrichment. The site is positionally fixed: the GXY-proximal site per collagen lies a median of 26 residues (IQR 20-32) upstream of the first Gly-X-Y block. P1' is Gln in 9 of 15 collagens, which is itself a signature, because Gln is a favourable P1' for convertases and its recurrence at a fixed distance from a structural boundary is hard to explain as chance.

AlphaFold DB v6 models give a mean pLDDT across the P5-P5' window at these sites of 49.1 against a whole-protein mean of 58.0 (Wilcoxon signed-rank, stat = 48.0, p = 0.019, n = 21 sites). 19 of 21 sites have window pLDDT below 70, placing them in low-confidence, predicted-disordered and therefore solvent-accessible segments rather than inside the triple-helical region. Convertases require an extended, accessible substrate, so this is the structural precondition the hypothesis asked about.

Across the genus, 139 of 150 (93%) of non-elegans collagen-sized UniProt entries from C. nigoni, remanei, briggsae, bovis, latens, auriculariae, tropicalis, brenneri, japonica and angaria carry an equivalent N-terminal R-X-X-R, with Gln the most common P1' residue (81 of 139).

Within the family, DPY-17 (R-V-R-R, basic at P2) matches the optimal furin consensus R-X-K/R-R, whereas SQT-3 (R-T-T-R) has only the minimal R-X-X-R with non-basic P2 and P3. On sequence alone SQT-3 is predicted to be the weaker, though still viable, substrate.

Method validation. The unbiased scan independently recovered the DPY-5 R77-H-K-R80 site that was previously identified and mutated. PMID: 16649143 states "The dpy-5 gene product contains an Arg-X-X-Arg cleavage motif that could be recognized by a proprotein convertase, such as BLI-4". The method recovers a known positive without being told where to look, which is the minimum test a de novo site scan should pass.

F003: Published in vivo genetics already establishes the BLI-4 dependence of SQT-3 and DPY-17 secretion

Birnbaum et al. (PMID: 37333289, peer-reviewed; preprint version PMID: 37721936) used endogenous SQT-3 and DPY-17 fluorescent-protein knock-ins and reported that "this early secretion depends on BLI-4/PCSK; in bli-4 and cleavage-site mutants, SQT-3 and DPY-17 are not efficiently secreted and instead form large intracellular aggregates", with later cuticle incorporation "reduced but not entirely blocked". The same paper states the premise of the seed hypothesis explicitly: "Some types of collagens, including transmembrane MACIT collagens and C. elegans cuticle collagens, are N-terminally cleaved at a dibasic site that resembles the consensus for furin or other proprotein convertases of the subtilisin/kexin (PCSK) family".

Jain, Gormly and Lundquist (PMID: 40110475) independently state that "apical secretion of DPY-17 and SQT-3 require the BLI-4 proprotein convertase of the subtilisin/kexin family and the consensus furin cleavage site (CFCS) in the N-terminus of DPY-17 and SQT-3", and that the CFCS sites are also required for Q neuroblast left-right asymmetric migration, while "bli-4 mutants had only weak effects on Q neuroblast migration, possibly due to redundancy among isoforms".

The computational sites identified here, SQT-3 R79-T-T-R82 and DPY-17 R61-V-R-R64, both with P1' Gln, correspond in position and motif class to the CFCS described in those two papers.

Two mutant classes phenocopy each other: bli-4 loss of function, and point substitution of the collagen cleavage site in cis. That is the standard genetic argument for a direct enzyme-substrate relationship, and it is stronger than either class alone. It does not exclude an indirect route in which BLI-4 matures another protease or a trafficking factor that in turn acts at the collagen site. Neither study reports an in vitro protease assay with purified BLI-4, N-terminal sequencing or mass-spectrometric mapping of the cleaved product, or a mobility-shift western showing loss of the processed SQT-3 or DPY-17 species in bli-4 mutants.

A third collagen is genetically implicated. PMID: 16649143 reports that "Genetic analysis of the nematode Caenorhabditis elegans reveals that all dpy-5 alleles are dominant suppressors of bli-4 blistering" and that "Mutation of this site cause a dominant dumpy phenotype suggesting Dpy-5 procollagen requires processing for normal cuticle production". This is an IGI-grade link between bli-4 and a procollagen carrying an R-X-X-R.

F004: SQT-3 is processed at two ends by two unrelated proteases

Novelli, Ahmed and Hodgkin (PMID: 15579684) recovered nine dominant extragenic suppressors of dpy-31 lethality, all carrying sqt-3 mutations affecting the sequence of the SQT-3 collagen C terminus, and concluded that "This suggests that DPY-31 is responsible for C-terminal proteolytic processing of collagen trimers and is therefore a structural and functional homolog of vertebrate BMP-1".

Novelli, Page and Hodgkin (PMID: 16452136) extended this with a SQT-3 antiserum. Most dpy-31 suppressors are allelic to sqt-3 and dpy-17, "the biochemical and genetic data indicate that the extracellular assemblies of DPY-17 and SQT-3 are interdependent", and "We find that absence of DPY-17 causes extensive intracellular retention of SQT-3".

The BLI-4-type R-X-X-R sits at SQT-3 residue 82 and DPY-17 residue 64, in the N-terminal non-collagenous domain, a median of 26 residues upstream of the collagenous domain and 219 residues upstream (in SQT-3) of the C terminus where DPY-31 acts. The two events are spatially distinct and non-competing: an N-terminal convertase cut attributable to BLI-4, and a C-terminal astacin/BMP-1-type cut attributable to DPY-31.

This creates two hazards for curation. First, a statement that BLI-4 "processes SQT-3" without the N-terminal qualifier will be read as competing with the well-supported DPY-31 claim. Second, because loss of DPY-17 alone causes extensive intracellular retention of SQT-3, a bli-4 phenotype on SQT-3 could in principle be partly indirect, mediated through failure to process DPY-17 within a shared trimer. The SQT-3 CFCS point mutant argues against a purely indirect route, because the SQT-3 site itself matters in cis, but it does not identify which enzyme uses that site.

F005: Sequence and structure cannot assign the collagen sites to BLI-4 rather than its three paralogues

This is a clean negative result for the purely computational arm of the hypothesis, and it is the single most important caveat in this report.

C. elegans has four reviewed kex2/subtilisin proprotein convertases besides TPP-2. Full-length global alignment of each to human furin shows that all four retain essentially the complete furin recognition apparatus:

Convertase UniProt Length Furin determinants conserved Identity to furin
BLI-4 / KPC-4 P51559 942 aa 12/12 46.5%
KPC-1 O17798 692 aa 12/12 57.5%
EGL-3 / KPC-2 G5ECN9 652 aa 11/12 (oxyanion Asn295 is Asp317) 47.0%
AEX-5 / KPC-3 P91863 537 aa 11/12 (S4 Asp264 is Glu247) 35.0%

KPC-1 is more furin-like overall than BLI-4. If an R-X-X-R site in a disordered propeptide junction were sufficient to name an enzyme, it would name KPC-1 ahead of BLI-4.

Discrimination among the four comes from expression and genetics, not from the catalytic pocket. AEX-5 "functions in the intestine to regulate the DMP" (PMID: 18852466). EGL-3 "is required in the cholinergic motor neurons to restrain convulsions" (PMID: 23658528). Those two are tissue-restricted away from hypodermis. KPC-1, however, is "broadly expressed in C. elegans neurons and epithelia" and "acts cell autonomously in IL2 neurons to regulate dauer-specific dendritic arborization and nictation" (PMID: 23932402). Hypodermis is an epithelium, so a KPC-1 contribution to cuticle collagen processing cannot be excluded by sequence or by the published expression data.

KPC-1 is the specific paralog-confusion risk a curator should record. It is untested for cuticle collagens.

F006: Motif quality anti-correlates with validated substrate status

A transparent composite score was applied to all 25 N-terminal R-X-X-R sites across 18 cuticle collagens. The components were fixed before the evidence ranking was examined: +2 for a basic P2 giving the optimal furin R-X-K/R-R, +1 for a basic P3, +1 for a favourable small or polar P1', -2 for Pro at P1', +1.5 for lying within 40 residues of the first Gly-X-Y block, +1.5 for predicted disorder with window pLDDT below 60, on a base of 3.0.

In a benchmark against validated convertase substrates on a P6-P1' basic-residue scale, SQT-3 scored 5.5, DPY-17 6.5, pro-TGF-beta1 6.5, pro-BMP1 6.5, anthrax protective antigen 7.5, the furin propeptide 6.0, and human Notch1 5.0. SQT-3 sits near the bottom of a range that includes several textbook furin substrates, yet it is one of the two collagens with direct in vivo BLI-4 dependence. DPY-5, whose site is also experimentally required, has an atypical P1' Ser rather than the family-typical Gln, and is still functionally required: "Mutation of this site cause a dominant dumpy phenotype suggesting Dpy-5 procollagen requires processing for normal cuticle production" (PMID: 16649143).

Curation consequence. Within this family the R-X-X-R motif is near-universal and positionally fixed, so motif quality has little discriminating power, and here it anti-correlates with the available experimental evidence. A curator should not treat SQT-3's weak R-T-T-R as an argument against BLI-4 processing, and should not treat the strong sites in DPY-7, DPY-9 or SQT-2 as evidence that those collagens are BLI-4 substrates. The best-supported candidate sites are DPY-17 R64 and SQT-3 R82 because of the genetic and imaging data, with DPY-5 R80 next. The motif analysis contributes structural plausibility, not ranking power.

F007: BLI-4 autoprocesses at its own furin-type site and its ten isoforms share an identical catalytic domain

UniProt P51559 annotates signal peptide 1-22, propeptide 23-116, mature chain 117-942, with a "Cleavage; by autolysis" site at 116-117. The sequence at that junction is R113-V-K-R116 ↓ DYIL, a canonical furin-type R-X-K/R-R with basic P2, confirmed by regular-expression match against the fetched sequence. Three further R-X-X-R sites lie in the BLI-4 propeptide itself (P1 at 87 RTRR, 90 RHKR, 146 RAQR), the same arrangement as the primary and secondary autoprocessing sites of furin (autolysis at 107-108 and 75-76 in P09958).

This is internal, non-circular evidence that R-X-X-R is the operative BLI-4 consensus. BLI-4 demonstrably cleaves a furin-type R-X-X-R in vivo, because it must do so on its own propeptide in order to mature. The argument holds independent of any collagen data and independent of the furin alignment.

Topology: lumenal 117-871, helical transmembrane 872-892, cytoplasmic 893-942. BLI-4 is a type I membrane protein like furin, with its catalytic domain in the lumen of the secretory pathway, the compartment through which newly synthesised procollagen transits. That matches the intracellular aggregation phenotype observed for SQT-3 and DPY-17 in bli-4 mutants. The UniProt subcellular location carries evidence code ECO:0000255 (sequence model), so it is a prediction, not an experimental localization.

UniProt lists ten alternative products (isoforms a-j, P51559-6 through P51559-15, isoform d displayed). The blisterases A-D of PMID: 7774813 correspond to the C-terminally distinct forms: "Analysis of bli-4 cDNAs has predicted four protein products, which we have designated blisterases A, B, C, and D. These protein products share a common amino terminus, but differ at the carboxyl termini". The peptidase S8 domain (168-483) and the P/Homo B domain (491-629) both lie upstream of residue 660, inside the exons shared by all isoforms. PMID: 10903434 confirms this from the allele side: "The lesions reside within the first 12 exons that are shared by all of the bli-4/kpc-4 gene products, with the majority of mutations clustered within the protease domain". The bli-4(e937) allele causing viable adult cuticle blistering deletes exon 13, unique to blisterase A.

Because all isoforms share an identical catalytic and P/Homo B region, the "isoform redundancy" invoked in PMID: 40110475 to explain the weak bli-4 Q neuroblast phenotype cannot be substrate-pocket redundancy. It must act through trafficking, retention or tissue-specific expression. A curator should not treat the isoforms as functionally distinct enzymes with different specificities.

Mechanistic model

The immediate molecular event under test is endoproteolysis of a procollagen at an R-X-X-R site in its N-terminal non-collagenous domain, by a lumenal, membrane-anchored subtilisin/kexin convertase, inside the secretory pathway, before the collagen reaches the extraembryonic space.

The dashed arrows are the uncertain links. BLI-4 acting directly on each collagen is inferred from genetics, not from a mapped product. The trimer-interdependence arrow is the competing explanation for part of the SQT-3 phenotype. The Q neuroblast link is dashed because the authors themselves describe the bli-4 effect as weak.

Conflicts and alternatives

  1. Two proteases act on SQT-3, at opposite ends. DPY-31, an astacin and BMP-1 homolog, performs C-terminal processing (PMID: 15579684), 219 residues from the BLI-4 site. An unqualified "BLI-4 processes SQT-3" reads as competing with a well-supported DPY-31 claim when the two are complementary.

  2. Indirect route through the trimer. Loss of DPY-17 alone causes extensive intracellular retention of SQT-3 (PMID: 16452136). If BLI-4 acted only on DPY-17, the SQT-3 phenotype would follow without BLI-4 ever touching SQT-3. The SQT-3 CFCS point mutant argues against a purely indirect route, because the site matters in cis, but it does not identify the enzyme that uses it.

  3. Paralog non-discrimination. All four worm PCSKs share the furin pocket, and KPC-1 is more furin-like than BLI-4 (57.5% against 46.5% identity to furin, both 12/12 determinants). KPC-1 is expressed in epithelia (PMID: 23932402), so a hypodermal contribution is not excluded by sequence. This is the strongest argument against treating the substrate-site analysis as self-sufficient.

  4. Motif score anti-correlates with evidence. DPY-17 ranks 1 of 25, the equally validated SQT-3 only 14 of 25, and the equally top-ranked DPY-7, DPY-9 and SQT-2 have no BLI-4 evidence. Site quality must not be used to grade substrate confidence in this family.

  5. Isoform redundancy is not specificity. PMID: 40110475 invokes isoform redundancy for the weak Q neuroblast phenotype. Because the peptidase S8 and P/Homo B domains lie in exons shared by all ten isoforms (PMID: 7774813, PMID: 10903434), that redundancy cannot be substrate-pocket redundancy and must be trafficking or expression.

  6. Database carry-over risk. The UniProt membrane CC assignment carries ECO:0000255, a sequence model. A curator propagating it should not upgrade it to experimental. The same applies to the topological domain boundaries used in the mechanistic model above.

  7. Residual assembly in bli-4 mutants. PMID: 37333289 reports cuticle incorporation "reduced but not entirely blocked". Either the alleles used retain partial activity, or a second route exists. Both readings weaken a strict obligate-substrate claim.

Method-level caveats, stated once each. pLDDT is a disorder proxy, not a measured solvent accessibility, and AlphaFold models of fibrous collagens are of limited reliability in the triple-helical region generally, which is a reason the comparison was made within each protein (paired Wilcoxon) rather than against an external baseline. The composition-matched shuffling null preserves amino acid composition but not local sequence correlations, so the 5.5-fold enrichment is a generous estimate of signal. The composite site score uses hand-specified weights, chosen before the evidence ranking was examined; its failure to rank SQT-3 highly is reported as a finding rather than tuned away. The genus conservation query returned collagen-sized entries by protein name, so it measures conservation of the family feature, not of the individual SQT-3 and DPY-17 sites.

Discriminating tests

Ranked by how efficiently each separates the live alternatives.

  1. N-terminal sequencing or LC-MS/MS of secreted SQT-3 and DPY-17, wild type against bli-4(h384). This is the single missing piece. It converts "required for processing" into "cleaved at R82" and would license an MF with a substrate qualifier.

  2. In vitro cleavage with a recombinant BLI-4 catalytic domain on synthetic SQT-3 and DPY-17 P6-P6' peptides, with the R79A/R82A double mutant as the negative control and furin as a positive control. Tests the chemistry directly and quantifies the predicted DPY-17 > SQT-3 preference that the benchmark scores imply (6.5 against 5.5).

  3. Hypodermis-specific kpc-1 knockdown in a bli-4 hypomorph, scoring endogenous SQT-3::FP secretion. Resolves the paralog ambiguity that the sequence analysis explicitly could not.

  4. DPY-17 CFCS point mutant, scoring SQT-3::FP separately from DPY-17::FP. Distinguishes a direct BLI-4-on-SQT-3 route from a trimer-mediated one, addressing the PMID: 16452136 confound.

  5. CFCS point mutants in the top-ranked but unvalidated collagens DPY-7, DPY-9 and SQT-2, which score 9.0 alongside DPY-17. If they are also BLI-4-dependent, the function generalises across the family; if not, the motif is confirmed to be non-predictive and the SQT-3/DPY-17 specificity has another source.

  6. Mobility-shift western with the existing SQT-3 antiserum from PMID: 16452136, comparing wild type, bli-4 and dpy-31 lysates. The cheapest experiment that would show an N-terminal processing defect distinct from the C-terminal one, using a reagent that already exists.

Possible action changes

  • Reframe the free-text hypothesis from a prediction to an experimentally established requirement, citing PMID: 37333289 and PMID: 40110475. Retain the computational analysis as support for structural plausibility, not as the basis of the claim.
  • Change "cleaves" to "required for N-terminal processing and apical secretion of", until a product is mapped.
  • Add the N-terminal qualifier to every SQT-3 statement, to keep it distinct from DPY-31 C-terminal processing.
  • Record that motif quality does not rank substrates within this family, so SQT-3's weak R-T-T-R is not evidence against it and the strong DPY-7, DPY-9 and SQT-2 sites are not evidence for them.
  • Add DPY-5 (PMID: 16649143) as a third genetically supported substrate candidate, with IGI evidence.
  • Flag the KPC-1 paralog risk explicitly, with its reason: epithelial expression plus a pocket that is more furin-like than BLI-4's.
  • Record the autolysis site R113-VKR116 as internal evidence that R-X-X-R is the operative BLI-4 consensus, which strengthens the MF independently of the collagen work.
  • Do not treat the ten isoforms as enzymes with different specificities; the catalytic and P/Homo B domains are identical across all of them.
