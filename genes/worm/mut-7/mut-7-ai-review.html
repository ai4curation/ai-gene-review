Gene Description

MUT-7 is a C. elegans 3'-5' exoribonuclease of the MUT-7/EXD3 family (RNase D-like DEDDy exonuclease domain plus a C-terminal MUT7-C domain), related to the exonuclease domain of the Werner syndrome protein and to Drosophila Nibbler. It is a core Mutator-class protein. Through its MUT7-C domain insertion it binds RDE-2/MUT-8, which links it to the scaffold MUT-16 and recruits it to perinuclear germline Mutator foci, where it supports amplification of secondary 22G siRNAs. Recombinant MUT-7 degrades single-stranded RNA 3'-to-5' in a metal-dependent way, and the MUT7-C domain contributes to RNA binding. MUT-7 is required for germline transposon silencing, exogenous RNAi and endogenous 22G and ERGO-1 class 26G siRNA pathways. Its precise substrate in the Mutator pathway is not known. MUT-7 is found in both cytosol and nucleus. In AWC olfactory neurons it acts with HPL-2 in the endogenous nuclear RNAi that mediates odor adaptation.

Core Functions

Metal-dependent 3'-5' exoribonuclease acting on single-stranded RNA. It is recruited to Mutator foci by RDE-2/MUT-8 and MUT-16, and its activity is required for 22G siRNA amplification. Its physiological substrate is not known.

Suggested Questions for Experts

Q: What does MUT-7 degrade in Mutator foci: the 3' ends of target RNA fragments before or after pUGylation, abortive RdRP products, or something else? Recombinant MUT-7 trims an A-tail but cannot degrade a folded pUG unit.

Q: What is the function of the nuclear MUT-7 complex, which lacks RDE-2?

Suggested Experiments

Experiment: Map MUT-7-dependent RNA 3' ends in vivo (e.g. 3'-end sequencing of pUG RNAs and RdRP products) in wild type, catalytic-dead mut-7 and mut-8 interface mutants.

Hypothesis: MUT-7 trims specific RNA intermediates in Mutator foci, and this trimming is needed for efficient RRF-1-dependent 22G-RNA synthesis.
