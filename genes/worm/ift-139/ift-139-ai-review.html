Gene Description

IFT-139 is the Caenorhabditis elegans ortholog of human TTC21B (IFT139), a subunit of intraflagellar transport complex A (IFT-A). It is a large (1324 aa) tetratricopeptide-repeat (TPR)/armadillo-like alpha-solenoid protein with no catalytic domain. ift-139 is expressed only in ciliated sensory neurons, and GFP-tagged IFT-139 concentrates at the ciliary base, is present at lower levels in dendrites, and moves bidirectionally along the axoneme at IFT rates. Genetic analysis places IFT-139 in IFT-A and in retrograde (tip-to-base) intraflagellar transport: ift-139 deletion mutants have short, irregular cilia that phenocopy dynein-2 (che-3) and ifta-1 mutants rather than kinesin-II mutants, and are defective in dye filling, dauer formation and male mating. IFT-139 acts partially redundantly with the small IFT-A subunit IFT-43 to promote dynein-2 motility, and with IFT-43 and IFTA-1 it is needed to remove periciliary membrane proteins from the cilium (transition-zone gating). A ciliopathy missense mutation modelled in the worm protein disrupts its function; human TTC21B mutations cause nephronophthisis and short-rib thoracic dysplasia.

Core Functions

Subunit of intraflagellar transport complex A (IFT-A). IFT-139 is a non-catalytic TPR/alpha-solenoid protein that rides IFT trains along the sensory cilium and, partly redundantly with IFT-43, promotes dynein-2-driven retrograde (tip-to-base) transport. Its specific molecular activity within IFT-A is unknown, so no MF is asserted.

Suggested Questions for Experts

Q: Does IFT-139 bind dynein-2 directly, or does it promote dynein-2 motility only by stabilizing the IFT-A2 lobe (with IFT-43 and IFTA-1)?

Suggested Experiments

Experiment: In ift-139 single and ift-139; ift-43 double mutants, image endogenously tagged dynein-2 (CHE-3) and IFT-A subunits at single-molecule resolution to measure train coupling at the tip and the frequency of retrograde starts.

Hypothesis: IFT-139 is needed for dynein-2 to couple to trains at the ciliary tip, and IFT-43 partly compensates for its loss.

Knowledge Gaps

What is not known — curated, literature-grounded statements of the open unknowns (the inverse of core functions).

Gap: How IFT-139 contributes to dynein-2 engagement is unknown. It acts redundantly with IFT-43 to promote dynein-2 motility, but which IFT-A interface it supplies, and whether it contacts dynein-2 directly, has not been shown in the worm.

OPEN BIOLOGY

What is known: Established: IFT-A membership, retrograde IFT role, and redundancy with IFT-43 for dynein-2 motility. Unknown: the direct binding partners of IFT-139 within IFT-A and on dynein-2, and why loss of IFT-139 alone is stronger than loss of IFT-43 alone.

Significance: Would explain how the peripheral IFT-A subunits (IFT-A2 lobe) help license retrograde transport and why TTC21B ciliopathy alleles are pathogenic.

What would resolve it: Structure-guided separation-of-function alleles of ift-139 in ift-43 and wild-type backgrounds, with single-molecule dynein-2 imaging.
