Gene Description

ANC-1 (nuclear anchorage defective 1) is the C. elegans ortholog of the giant nesprins nesprin-1 and nesprin-2. Its longest isoforms (up to 8,545 residues) have N-terminal tandem calponin-homology (CH) actin-binding domains, a very long central region of six nearly identical ~903-residue spectrin-like repeats, and a C-terminal transmembrane span with a KASH domain. ANC-1 is membrane-anchored in the endoplasmic reticulum and the continuous outer nuclear membrane, where its KASH domain binds the SUN protein UNC-84, and it extends through the cytoplasm of the hypodermal syncytium and other cells. ANC-1 holds nuclei, mitochondria, the ER and lipid droplets in place: in anc-1 null mutants nuclei float freely and cluster, and other organelles drift through the cytoplasm. The canonical LINC bridge (KASH-UNC-84 plus CH-actin) contributes only part of this anchorage; the central repeats together with the membrane anchor provide most of it, which supports a model in which ANC-1 maintains the mechanical integrity of the cytoplasm. Shorter isoforms lacking the CH domains are sufficient for hypodermal nuclear anchorage, whereas longer isoforms position mitochondria in touch neurons. ANC-1 also makes a minor contribution to male pronuclear migration in the zygote.

Functional annotation report: Caenorhabditis elegans anc-1 / ANC-1 (UniProt Q9N4M4)

Executive conclusion

The requested protein is correctly identified. The worm gene anc-1, also designated ZK973.6, encodes ANC-1, a giant C. elegans nesprin-family protein and ortholog of mammalian nesprin-1/2. The literature’s organism, gene synonym, protein family, exceptional size, N-terminal calponin-homology domains, extended spectrin-like region, and C-terminal membrane/KASH region all agree with the supplied UniProt Q9N4M4 record. This report excludes unrelated proteins with similar ANC/ANC1 names. (hao2021thenesprin12ortholog pages 1-2, hao2021thenesprin12ortholog pages 2-3)

ANC-1 is not an enzyme or transporter and therefore has no known catalytic reaction or transported substrate. Its best-supported primary function is that of a giant membrane-associated structural scaffold that maintains cytoplasmic integrity and spatially restrains nuclei, endoplasmic reticulum (ER), mitochondria, and lipid droplets. It acts from ER and nuclear-envelope-associated membranes through extensive cytoplasmic domains. The traditional model—an actin-binding nesprin joined through a KASH–SUN interaction to the nuclear interior—is only part of the story: in the hyp7 epidermal syncytium, neither the N-terminal actin-binding domains nor the KASH region is individually essential for most ANC-1-dependent nuclear positioning. (hao2021thenesprin12ortholog pages 1-2, hao2021thenesprin12ortholog pages 2-3, hao2021thenesprin12ortholog pages 3-6, hao2021thenesprin12ortholog pages 9-10)

More specialized functions include positioning mitochondria at the proximal axon in touch-receptor neurons, thereby supporting SLT-1-associated axon polarization, and participation in autophagy-associated nuclear and nucleolar quality control that supports stress resistance, longevity, fertility, and germline integrity. (papandreou2023nucleophagydelaysaging pages 3-4, papandreou2023nucleophagydelaysaging pages 1-2, fischer2022theanc1(nesprin12) pages 9-11, fischer2022theanc1(nesprin12) pages 6-7)

1. Identity, nomenclature, and domain organization

Hao and colleagues explicitly identify anc-1/ZK973.6 as encoding the C. elegans nesprin-1/2 ortholog. The longest reported protein is up to 8,545 amino acids and contains tandem N-terminal calponin-homology (CH) domains, an exceptionally long predicted helical/spectrin-like region with six approximately 903-residue repeats, and a C-terminal transmembrane/KASH region. These features align with the supplied InterPro annotations for CH/actinin-type actin-binding and ANC1 spectrin-like domains. The supplied BAG-domain annotation is compatible with the database record, but its biochemical contribution has not been resolved by the functional studies reviewed here. (hao2021thenesprin12ortholog pages 1-2, hao2021thenesprin12ortholog pages 2-3)

The CH domains bind actin in vitro and colocalize with actin in vivo. The terminal KASH region can engage the SUN-domain protein UNC-84 and promote outer nuclear-envelope localization. The long internal repeats are predicted by structural modeling to form spectrin-like helical bundles; that precise fold remains a bioinformatic/structural inference rather than a complete high-resolution structure. (hao2022nesprin12orthologanc1 pages 55-56, hao2021thenesprin12ortholog pages 1-2, hao2021thenesprin12ortholog pages 3-6)

At least three functionally distinct isoform classes matter experimentally. The shorter ANC-1B lacks the N-terminal CH-containing extension, is expressed in hyp7, and is sufficient for much nuclear anchoring there. Longer ANC-1A/C isoforms contain CH and additional N-terminal sequences, are enriched in ALM touch neurons, and mediate neuronal mitochondrial positioning and axon polarity; ANC-1B was not detected in ALM. Thus, apparent differences among studies reflect genuine tissue- and isoform-specific mechanisms rather than inconsistent gene identification. (fischer2022theanc1(nesprin12) pages 4-6, hao2021thenesprin12ortholog pages 2-3, fischer2022theanc1(nesprin12) pages 6-7)

2. Primary molecular function and cellular localization

ER-associated cytoplasmic scaffold

Endogenous or engineered GFP-tagged ANC-1B and C-terminal ANC-1 fragments colocalized with the ER marker TRAM-1 in hyp7 and accumulated at the nuclear envelope. ANC-1 did not itself colocalize detectably with mitochondria or lipid droplets, even though their positioning depends on it. The evidence therefore favors an ER-associated scaffold that indirectly constrains several organelle classes, rather than a resident component of every organelle surface. (hao2021thenesprin12ortholog pages 9-10)

The C-terminal transmembrane span, adjacent neck region, and internal repeats contribute to membrane localization and anchoring. Removing all six repeats redirected ANC-1 toward the nuclear envelope; this enrichment required UNC-84. Deleting the luminal KASH portion alone had relatively little effect on broader ER localization, whereas deleting the transmembrane span caused variable mislocalization. (hao2021thenesprin12ortholog pages 9-10)

Nuclear anchoring and the revised LINC-complex model

In the canonical model, ANC-1’s N-terminal CH domains bind cytoplasmic actin while its C-terminal KASH domain interacts with UNC-84/SUN across the nuclear envelope, forming a LINC complex. Both interactions are experimentally real, but domain-deletion analysis shows that this bridge is not the whole mechanism in hyp7. Deleting the CH domains did not disrupt hyp7 nuclear positioning; deleting KASH produced only a mild phenotype similar to an unc-84 null. By contrast, anc-1 null mutants were substantially more severe, with more than 50% of hyp7 nuclei contacting another nucleus. (hao2022nesprin12orthologanc1 pages 55-56, hao2021thenesprin12ortholog pages 2-3, hao2021thenesprin12ortholog pages 3-6)

Deleting internal cytoplasmic segments or the six-repeat region caused substantial nuclear-spacing defects. Full null-like disruption required combined impairment of the repeat region and transmembrane anchoring. These data support parallel contributions from canonical SUN–KASH coupling and a broader ER/cytoplasmic-scaffold mechanism. ANC-1 should consequently be annotated as a nesprin/LINC-associated protein whose dominant hyp7 role is substantially LINC-independent, not simply as an obligatory actin-to-nucleus tether. (hao2021thenesprin12ortholog pages 2-3, hao2021thenesprin12ortholog pages 3-6, hao2021thenesprin12ortholog pages 9-10)

Organelle positioning and cytoplasmic integrity

Live imaging showed that loss of ANC-1 causes ER fragmentation, aggregation, and drifting, together with abnormal movement of mitochondria and lipid droplets. ER landmarks in null mutants changed position by more than twice the wild-type amount. Because UNC-84 mutants retained nearly normal static ER organization, this broad organelle-anchoring function is separable from the canonical nuclear LINC connection. (hao2021thenesprin12ortholog pages 3-6, hao2021thenesprin12ortholog pages 9-10)

The current expert model is therefore a cytoplasmic-integrity model: membrane-anchored ANC-1 extends large helical domains into the cytoplasm, creating or stabilizing a mechanically coherent environment that restrains multiple organelles. This model is strongly supported by localization, deletion genetics, and live imaging, although the immediate binding partners and physical material properties of the scaffold remain unknown. Severe alleles also caused smaller, less-round nuclei, reduced body size, developmental abnormalities, and brood sizes below 25% of wild type, illustrating organismal consequences of impaired cellular organization. (hao2021thenesprin12ortholog pages 9-10, hao2021thenesprin12ortholog pages 14-17)

3. Pathway-specific and tissue-specific functions

SLT-1, mitochondria, and ALM axon polarization

In ALM touch-receptor neurons, ANC-1A/C localize to the cell body and base of the proximal axon. Nearly all wild-type ALM neurons contain a mitochondrial cluster at this site. Proximal mitochondrial localization failed in 42% of anc-1 null neurons and 38% of neurons carrying an ANC-1A/C-selective mutation; the latter phenotype was not significantly different from the null. Neurons lacking the cluster had approximately twice the penetrance of axon-polarity defects, with a significant association between mitochondrial-positioning and polarity phenotypes (P=0.017). (fischer2022theanc1(nesprin12) pages 6-7, fischer2022theanc1(nesprin12) pages 7-9)

Genetic evidence connects this function to the extracellular guidance cue SLT-1: slt-1 loss caused a posterior second process in 18% of animals, and anc-1 and slt-1 mutations did not enhance one another as expected for fully parallel pathways. Partial loss of mitochondrial genes spg-7, isp-1, or gas-1, as well as complex-I or complex-III inhibition with rotenone or antimycin, produced axon-polarity defects. Drug effects were dose-dependent, with more than 196 animals tested per concentration, and did not enhance the anc-1 null phenotype. (fischer2022theanc1(nesprin12) pages 9-11, fischer2022theanc1(nesprin12) pages 2-4)

The most defensible pathway model is nuanced: ANC-1A/C position mitochondria, while SLT-1 likely influences mitochondrial function or the cellular response to it. Loss of SLT-1 did not displace mitochondria, and rotenone did not alter their location, arguing against a simple linear “SLT-1 recruits ANC-1, which recruits mitochondria” pathway. CH-domain deletion caused strong polarity defects but relatively weak mitochondrial-positioning defects, further implying both mitochondria-dependent and additional CH-dependent outputs. (fischer2022theanc1(nesprin12) pages 11-13, fischer2022theanc1(nesprin12) pages 7-9)

Nucleophagy, nucleolar homeostasis, and aging

The newest direct functional study identified ANC-1 as part of autophagy-associated nuclear quality control. ANC-1 colocalized with LGG-1 under autophagy-inducing or flux-blocking conditions, while depletion of lgg-1 or the late-autophagy factor rab-7 caused ANC-1 accumulation. Loss of ANC-1 enlarged nuclei and nucleoli, altered nuclear shape, increased LMN-1 accumulation, and increased FIB-1-marked nucleolar material. In one nucleolar assay, the ANC-1-dependent difference was P<0.0001 with at least 12 nucleoli analyzed. (papandreou2023nucleophagydelaysaging pages 1-2, papandreou2023nucleophagydelaysaging pages 19-23)

ANC-1 and autophagy factors including BEC-1, LGG-1, LGG-2, ATG-18, and RAB-7 are required for elimination of nucleolar material from the most proximal oocyte. Combined anc-1 and bec-1 depletion did not further increase the nucleolar phenotype, supporting action in a shared process. ANC-1 or BEC-1 depletion was also epistatic to the reduced-nucleolus effect of daf-2 depletion. These results support an ANC-1-dependent nucleophagy pathway, although direct biochemical demonstration that ANC-1 is a selective cargo receptor has not been reported. (papandreou2023nucleophagydelaysaging pages 3-4, papandreou2023nucleophagydelaysaging pages 7-7)

Functionally, ANC-1 depletion suppressed longevity associated with reduced DAF-2 signaling and with eat-2-associated dietary restriction; lifespan assays included at least 131 animals. ANC-1 was also required for resistance to nutrient deprivation, heat, and DNA damage. Across generations at 25°C, ANC-1-deficient germ lines developed aberrant and multinucleate oocytes, impaired nucleolar elimination, tumor-like structures by approximately generations 4–5, and major loss of integrity by generation 7. This ties nuclear-component recycling to fertility and germline immortality, but the available evidence does not establish that every structural anchoring phenotype is mediated through nucleophagy. (papandreou2023nucleophagydelaysaging pages 3-4, papandreou2023nucleophagydelaysaging pages 7-8, papandreou2023nucleophagydelaysaging pages 1-2, papandreou2023nucleophagydelaysaging pages 8-9)

5. Current applications and biological significance

ANC-1 is principally a research model, not a clinical target with an established therapeutic implementation. Its major current applications are:

  1. Dissecting nesprin and LINC biology. Worm domain deletions reveal which nesprin functions truly require KASH–SUN or actin binding and which instead depend on large cytoplasmic regions and membrane association. This revises oversimplified models of giant nesprins as passive actin–nucleus cables. (hao2021thenesprin12ortholog pages 2-3, hao2021thenesprin12ortholog pages 3-6)
  2. In vivo organelle-mechanics studies. Transparent animals and hyp7 live imaging allow direct measurement of ER, mitochondrial, lipid-droplet, and nuclear movement in an intact tissue. (hao2021thenesprin12ortholog pages 3-6, hao2021thenesprin12ortholog pages 14-17)
  3. Neurodevelopmental pathway analysis. ANC-1A/C provide a genetically tractable link among extracellular guidance cues, local mitochondrial positioning/function, and axon-growth polarity. This is relevant to understanding mammalian nesprin biology, but worm findings should not be treated as evidence for a human therapy. (fischer2022theanc1(nesprin12) pages 9-11, fischer2022theanc1(nesprin12) pages 6-7)
  4. Aging and germline-quality models. ANC-1 reporters, FIB-1 nucleolar markers, and autophagy perturbations provide an in vivo system for studying nuclear-component turnover, nucleolar aging biomarkers, and multigenerational germline maintenance. (papandreou2023nucleophagydelaysaging pages 3-4, papandreou2023nucleophagydelaysaging pages 1-2, papandreou2023nucleophagydelaysaging pages 19-23)

6. Recent-research assessment

The most recent ANC-1-centered primary study retrieved was Papandreou et al., published in Nature Aging, volume 3, pages 34–46, with online/publication metadata spanning late 2022–2023; DOI 10.1038/s43587-022-00327-4. It substantially expanded ANC-1 annotation from structural anchoring to nucleophagy and nucleolar quality control. (papandreou2023nucleophagydelaysaging pages 3-4, papandreou2023nucleophagydelaysaging pages 1-2)

The key mechanistic precursors were Fischer et al., PLOS Genetics, published 21 November 2022, DOI 10.1371/journal.pgen.1010521, and Hao et al., eLife, published 16 April 2021, DOI 10.7554/eLife.61069. The literature search did not identify a 2024 primary article centered specifically on new ANC-1 molecular function; 2024 work retrieved concerned neighboring nuclear-envelope processes rather than ANC-1 itself. (hao2021thenesprin12ortholog pages 1-2, fischer2022theanc1(nesprin12) pages 2-4)

7. Recommended functional annotation and remaining uncertainties

Recommended concise annotation: “Giant nesprin-family, ER/nuclear-envelope-associated structural scaffold that maintains cytoplasmic integrity and anchors or positions nuclei, ER, mitochondria, and lipid droplets; acts through tissue-specific isoforms in mitochondrial positioning during axon polarization and participates in autophagy-associated nuclear/nucleolar quality control.”

Localization: principally ER-associated membranes and the outer nuclear-envelope/continuous ER system, with large domains extending into the cytoplasm; specialized enrichment occurs in neuronal cell bodies and at the proximal axon base. ANC-1 influences mitochondria and lipid droplets without being detectably resident on them in hyp7. (hao2021thenesprin12ortholog pages 9-10, fischer2022theanc1(nesprin12) pages 6-7)

Mechanistic confidence: direct evidence is strongest for membrane association, organelle-positioning phenotypes, CH-domain actin binding, UNC-84-dependent nuclear-envelope enrichment, isoform-specific mitochondrial positioning, and genetic/autophagic dependencies. The exact molecular partners of the internal repeats, the physical basis of “cytoplasmic integrity,” how ANC-1 controls mitochondrial activity downstream or alongside SLT-1, and whether ANC-1 directly recognizes nucleophagy cargo remain unresolved. (hao2021thenesprin12ortholog pages 3-6, hao2021thenesprin12ortholog pages 9-10, fischer2022theanc1(nesprin12) pages 11-13)

Accordingly, ANC-1 should not be annotated as an enzyme, transporter, exclusively canonical LINC component, or direct mitochondrial resident. Its broadest experimentally defensible designation is a giant, membrane-anchored cellular-architecture and organelle-positioning protein with context-dependent LINC, neuronal, and nuclear-quality-control functions.
