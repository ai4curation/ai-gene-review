CC   -!- FUNCTION: Plays a central role in nuclear and mitochondrial anchoring
CC       (PubMed:10375507, PubMed:12169658, PubMed:22298703, PubMed:6889924).
CC       Probably connects nuclei to the cytoskeleton by interacting with unc-84
CC       at the nuclear envelope and with F-actin in the cytoplasm, creating a
CC       bridge across the nuclear envelope between the cytoskeleton and the
CC       nucleus (PubMed:15773756). Has a role in positioning of the cell body
CC       of the PVQ lumbar interneuron (PubMed:22298703).
CC       {ECO:0000269|PubMed:10375507, ECO:0000269|PubMed:12169658,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:22298703, ECO:0000269|PubMed:6889924,
CC       ECO:0000303|PubMed:15773756}.
CC   -!- SUBUNIT: Interacts with F-actin via its N-terminal domain. Most likely
CC       interacts with unc-84; the interaction is probably required to recruit
CC       anc-1 to the nuclear envelope. {ECO:0000269|PubMed:12169658}.
CC   -!- SUBCELLULAR LOCATION: Nucleus outer membrane
CC       {ECO:0000269|PubMed:15773756, ECO:0000305|PubMed:12169658}; Single-pass
CC       type IV membrane protein {ECO:0000305}; Cytoplasmic side {ECO:0000305}.
CC       Cytoplasm, cytoskeleton {ECO:0000305|PubMed:15773756}. Note=The largest
CC       part of the protein is cytoplasmic, while its C-terminal part is
CC       associated either with the nuclear envelope, most probably the outer
CC       nuclear membrane, or with mitochondrial membrane.
CC       {ECO:0000305|PubMed:12169658}.
CC   -!- TISSUE SPECIFICITY: Ubiquitously expressed in all postembryonic cells.
CC       {ECO:0000269|PubMed:12169658}.
CC   -!- DEVELOPMENTAL STAGE: First expressed in L1 larvae, and thereafter
CC       throughout adulthood. {ECO:0000269|PubMed:12169658}.
CC   -!- DOMAIN: The large coiled coil domains are composed of 6 nearly exact
CC       repeats of 903 residues. The last repeat is partial. These repeats are
CC       conserved in Hawaii (CB4856), Australia (AB4) and Germany (RC301)
CC       strains. The length of the repeat may be maintained because of a
CC       selective advantage to keep the protein large and allow a single
CC       molecule to extend more than 0.5 micrometers.
CC       {ECO:0000269|PubMed:12169658}.
CC   -!- DOMAIN: The KASH domain, which contains a potential transmembrane
CC       domain, is essential for the nuclear envelope targeting.
CC   -!- DISRUPTION PHENOTYPE: Nuclear anchorage is lost resulting in free-
CC       floating nuclei that often cluster as the animal flexes. An increase in
CC       PVQ cell bodies mispositioned to the anterior is apparent between
CC       three-fold embryos and L1 larvae. Pharyngeal cells show defects
CC       including mispositioned organelles. {ECO:0000269|PubMed:22298703,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:6889924}.
CC   -!- SIMILARITY: Belongs to the nesprin family. {ECO:0000305}.
