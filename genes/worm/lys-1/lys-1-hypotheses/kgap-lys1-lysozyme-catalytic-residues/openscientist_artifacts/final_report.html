LYS-1 (C. elegans, UniProt O62415): does it retain the residues required for muramidase activity?

Hypothesis slug: kgap-lys1-lysozyme-catalytic-residues Source: genes/worm/lys-1/lys-1-ai-review.yaml, free-text focus Taxon: Caenorhabditis elegans, NCBITaxon:6239

Summary

The seed hypothesis has two halves and they resolve in opposite directions. The family assignment is correct. LYS-1 is a glycoside hydrolase family 25 (GH25) protein, the protist/Chalaropsis/Entamoeba type lysozyme family, confirmed independently at sequence level (PROSITE PS51904, CDD cd06416, Pfam PF31264, Gene3D 3.20.20.80, SUPFAM SSF51445) and at structure level, where a Foldseek search of the AlphaFold v6 model against PDB100 returns only GH25 structures in the top 12, led by Streptomyces globisporus Cellosyl 1JFX at E = 4.1e-9.

The catalytic half is wrong. The GH25 catalytic set is Asp1 followed by an Asp-x-Glu pair, curated as ACT_SITE in 22 reviewed UniProt GH25 entries spanning bacteria, phage, fungi, amoebae and nematodes. LYS-1 retains only the first of the three. Asp64 is the first catalytic aspartate and superimposes on the equivalent residue of the catalytically intact C. elegans paralog LYS-6 to within 0.17 Å. The catalytic Asp-x-Glu pair is replaced by Gln154 and Ser157. No other carboxylate in the protein occupies or can reach those positions: after a 150 CA-pair superposition at 1.20 Å RMSD, the nearest acidic side chain to the Asp2 site is Asp194 at 8.37 Å and to the Glu site Asp194 at 6.67 Å, and Asp194 sits 12.52 Å from Asp64, outside the carboxylate spacing used by either retaining or inverting glycosidases. LYS-1 is predicted catalytically incompetent as a muramidase.

The loss is ancient and clade-wide. Across 300 nematode PF31264 proteins the family splits into a LYS-6-like clade with an intact Asp-Asp-Glu triad in 118/138 proteins and a LYS-1-like clade with the triad intact in 1/162, where the dominant state is the exact Asp-Gln-Ser of C. elegans LYS-1 in 117/162 (Fisher exact OR = 950, p = 6.5e-61). The dead site is maintained by selection rather than decaying: Gln154 is fixed in 90.7% of the clade and Ser157 in 85.8%, at the 88.6th and 80.8th percentile of conservation among positions in their own GH25 domain, which matches where the genuine catalytic Asp112 and Glu114 sit in the active sister clade (95.1st and 90.2nd percentile). That makes a non-catalytic binding function the live hypothesis for this clade, and it stops short of proof because a burial control cannot separate ligand-site selection from fold packing in LYS-1.

Executive judgment

Verdict: partially supported in its family claim, refuted in its catalytic claim.

Supported: LYS-1 belongs to GH25 and adopts that fold with high confidence. Five independent domain models agree, and the structural search is unambiguous.

Refuted: LYS-1 does not retain the catalytic residues required for muramidase activity. Two of three are substituted, the substitution is clade-defining across nematodes, and no compensating carboxylate exists in the structure.

The most important caveats, in order of weight:

  1. Everything reported here is computational. No enzyme assay has been published on purified LYS-1 in either direction, so the correct statement is "predicted catalytically incompetent as a muramidase", and not "experimentally shown to be inactive".
  2. The only C. elegans lysozyme with measured peptidoglycan-degrading activity is ILYS-5, an invertebrate-type lysozyme from a different family (PMID: 38925432). The C. elegans GH25 proteins as a group are biochemically uncharacterised, so there is no within-organism positive control for any GH25 activity claim.
  3. Atypical GH25 proteins can turn out to be active. Dictyostelium CF45-1 and CF50 were assumed non-enzymatic and are not (PMID: 39909204). That paper does not rescue LYS-1, because the residue its mutagenesis identified as essential is the glutamate, and LYS-1 has serine there.
  4. All structural distances come from AlphaFold models. The backbone is high confidence (pLDDT 94-99 at every mapped position) and the side-chain rotamers are predictions.

Note on independence: UniProt already carries a CAUTION on O62415 ("Lacks conserved active site residues, suggesting it has no catalytic activity", ECO:0000305). That record was read during this analysis. The residue mapping, superposition, 300-protein clade scan and conservation statistics reported here were computed from primary sequence and structure data and reach the same conclusion by an independent route, with quantitative geometry and population genetics the database annotation does not provide.

Key findings

The GH25 fold assignment is solid

The AlphaFold v6 model AF-O62415-F1 (298 residues, mean pLDDT 82.9) was submitted to Foldseek in 3diaa mode against PDB100. The top 12 hits are all GH25 enzymes: 1jfx Cellosyl (E = 4.1e-9, 19.9% sequence identity), 6zmv muramidase (E = 9.9e-9), 2x8r, 2wag, 5jip Clostridium perfringens, 4krt and 4kru phage phiSM101, 4ff5, 5a6s and 1sfs. Superposing the LYS-1 model onto the model of the active C. elegans paralog LYS-6 (AF-Q20968, mean pLDDT 95.4) gives a 150 CA-pair core at 1.20 Å RMSD. The seed hypothesis is right about where this protein sits in sequence and structure space. That is the part of the question that was never really in doubt, and settling it is what makes the catalytic result interpretable: the active site is being read in a protein that genuinely has the fold the active site belongs to.

Two of three catalytic residues are gone and nothing replaces them

The GH25 catalytic set was taken from 22 reviewed UniProt entries with curated ACT_SITE features: Entamoeba histolytica LYS1 (Q27650) D18/D109/E111, Streptomyces globisporus Cellosyl (P25310) D86/D175/E177, phage Cp-1 CPL-1 (P15057) D10/D92/E94, Dictyostelium LYSG2 (Q86KC1) D24/D112/E114, and the C. elegans paralogs LYS-5 (Q20967) D23/D113/E115, LYS-4 (Q20964) D22/D112/E114, LYS-6 (Q20968) D22/D112/E114 and LYS-10 (O61518) D22/D128/E130. Global Needleman-Wunsch alignment of LYS-1 to three independent active references gives the same mapping every time, at pairwise identities of 33.8% (Entamoeba LYS1), 31.3% (LYS-6) and 27.1% (Cellosyl): Asp1 maps to LYS-1 Asp64, Asp2 maps to Gln154, and the catalytic Glu maps to Ser157/Pro158. AlphaFold pLDDT at those positions is 98.2, 98.8 and 94.9, so this is not a disordered or low-confidence stretch.

Geometry gives the same answer from a different direction. The catalytic Asp-Glu pair tips sit 3.53 Å apart in LYS-6, 3.62 Å in LYS-5 and 3.91 Å in Entamoeba LYS1, against 5.52 Å for Gln154-Ser157 in LYS-1. After superposition onto LYS-6, the LYS-6 Asp22 and LYS-1 Asp64 carboxylates coincide at 0.17 Å, but the residues physically occupying the Asp112 and Glu114 sites are Gln154 (1.73 Å and 1.99 Å) and Ser157 (3.59 Å). The nearest LYS-1 carboxylate to either site is Asp194, at 8.37 Å and 6.67 Å, and Asp194 is 12.52 Å from Asp64, outside the 3-10 Å range used by either mechanism class. The relocated-catalytic-residue escape route is closed.

The substitution defines a nematode-wide clade

Each of 300 nematode (taxon 6231) PF31264 proteins was globally aligned to both LYS-6 and LYS-1 and assigned to whichever reference scored higher. The LYS-6-like clade (n = 138) keeps the intact triad in 118/138 (85.5%). The LYS-1-like clade (n = 162) keeps it in 1/162 (0.6%), and the dominant motif is Asp-Gln-Ser in 117/162 (72%), with Asp-Gln-Thr in 8 and Asp-Lys-Thr in 4. Fisher exact test gives OR = 950, p = 6.5e-61. The dead clade spans Caenorhabditis, Diploscapter, Pristionchus, Acrobeloides, Plectus, Steinernema, Strongyloides, Meloidogyne, Oesophagostomum, Ancylostoma and Necator, so the loss predates the divergence of Rhabditida from Strongylida and Tylenchida.

The same split runs through C. elegans itself. lys-1, lys-2, lys-7 and lys-8 carry Asp-Gln-Ser or Asp-Gln-Thr, and lys-3 carries Ser/Asn-Gln-Ser, while lys-4, lys-5, lys-6 and lys-10 carry an intact Asp-Asp-Glu. C. elegans maintains two functionally distinct sets of GH25 proteins, and the gene with the largest phenotype literature in the dead set is lys-7.

They sit near the top of the distribution, in the same percentile band as the genuine catalytic residues of the active clade. The selection is confined to the GH25 domain, so this is not a whole-protein conservation artefact.

The whole groove is conserved, and the reason is not resolved

Cleft-lining residues were defined geometrically and identically in both clades as any residue with a side-chain heavy atom within 10 Å of the centroid of the four cleft positions (LYS-1 Asp64/Gln154/Ser157/Asp260, LYS-6 Asp22/Asp112/Glu114/Asp209). That gives 24 positions in LYS-1 and 23 in LYS-6 inside the respective domain windows.

The last row is the limit of this analysis. The groove of the catalytically dead clade is conserved slightly more above its own background than the groove of the enzymatically intact clade, which is the result a maintained ligand site would produce. It is also the result ordinary packing selection on the wall of a deep groove would produce, and in the LYS-1 clade the two cannot be separated because 22 of 24 cleft positions are buried by a neighbour-count proxy. The same control runs in LYS-6, where 7 exposed cleft positions give +33.7 pp (p = 0.0015), showing the metric detects surface selection when it is there.

Burial of the individual positions does weaken the pure-packing reading without defeating it. Gln154 and Ser157 sit at the 65.8th and 53.7th burial percentile of their structure, the same range as the genuine catalytic Asp112 (56.1st) and Asp22 (78.0th) of LYS-6, so they are no more buried than real catalytic residues are.

Net charge gives no discrimination either. The LYS-1 clade has median pI 7.79 and net charge +4.2 at pH 5.0; the LYS-6 clade has median pI 6.47 and +5.7. Mann-Whitney on charge at pH 5.0 gives p = 0.219. Both clades are cationic at the acidic pH where the one assayed C. elegans lysozyme, ILYS-5, degrades peptidoglycan, which fits a retained affinity for an anionic cell-wall polymer and is equally consistent with shared ancestry.

Mechanistic model

The immediate molecular activity under test is hydrolysis of the beta-1,4 bond between N-acetylmuramic acid and N-acetylglucosamine in peptidoglycan, carried out by the GH25 active site. That requires the Asp-x-Glu pair on the beta strand lining one wall of the cleft, with the glutamate acting as catalytic acid. Its removal abolishes activity in an experimentally tested GH25 protein (PMID: 39909204) and in the distantly related lysozyme-like Slt35, where Glu162 to Gln gave a completely inactive enzyme (PMID: 10545329). LYS-1 has Gln and Ser at the two positions.

What LYS-1 retains is the opposite wall of the cleft: Asp64 with its partner Asp260 at 3.25 Å, the same geometry as LYS-6 Asp22/Asp209 at 3.18 Å. That is a binding sub-site.

Separate from the direct activity, every published LYS-1 phenotype is organismal. Transcriptional induction by Serratia marcescens, increased resistance on lys-1 overexpression (PMID: 12176330), induction during Pseudomonas aeruginosa challenge (PMID: 22363222) and reduced survival on Staphylococcus aureus after RNAi are whole-animal readouts. They establish that LYS-1 contributes to defence. None distinguishes an enzyme from a binding protein, an opsonin, or a component acting alongside the catalytically intact paralogs LYS-4, LYS-5, LYS-6 and LYS-10 or the ILYS proteins. The defence phenotype must not be used as evidence for lysozyme activity.

Core function statement. The core function of LYS-1 is best described as a secreted or vesicular GH25-fold protein acting in intestinal antibacterial defence, with the muramidase activity of the family lost. It should not be described as a lysozyme enzyme. "Protein binding" is deliberately not recommended: the structural evidence points at a carbohydrate or cell-wall ligand, and GO:0042834 is the more informative term to test.

Conflicts and alternatives

  1. Atypical GH25 proteins can still be active. PMID: 39909204 showed that Dictyostelium CF45-1 and CF50, previously assumed non-enzymatic because of their unusual architecture, are active lysozymes. This is the strongest argument for caution. It does not rescue LYS-1: in the CF proteins the catalytic glutamate is present, and the same study identified that glutamate as essential by site-directed mutagenesis. LYS-1 has lost it.
  2. Relocated catalytic residue. Tested and rejected. Asp194 is the only candidate and sits 6.7-8.4 Å from the required positions and 12.52 Å from Asp64.
  3. Alternative GH25 sub-site geometry. The three reference enzymes used span bacteria, protists and nematodes and give the same mapping, so a lineage-specific repositioning of the active site is unlikely.
  4. Paralog confusion. Real and widespread in this family. C. elegans has at least nine GH25 proteins plus five ILYS proteins. Microarray and RNAi studies report "lysozymes" without distinguishing them (PMID: 16809667, PMID: 21853381, PMID: 22363222). Phenotypes attributed to "C. elegans lysozyme" should not be transferred to LYS-1, and activity measured on ILYS-5 (PMID: 38925432) belongs to a different family.
  5. AlphaFold limitation. All structural numbers come from predicted models. The backbone is high confidence (pLDDT 94-99 at every mapped position) and side-chain rotamers are predictions. This affects the precise distances, not the residue identities, which come from sequence.
  6. Database carry-over. GO:0003796 on LYS-1 is IEA from InterPro IPR002053, a domain-level signature carried equally by the catalytically intact paralogs, so the IEA pipeline cannot discriminate. This is a textbook case of domain-match overannotation.
  7. Conservation of the groove may be fold packing. The main objection to the binding lead, and it survives testing. Cleft-lining residues in the LYS-1 clade are conserved +36.0 pp over background, and 22 of the 24 positions are buried, so the exposed-only control is not computable for that clade. The control does run in LYS-6 (+33.7 pp on 7 exposed positions, p = 0.0015), showing the metric detects surface selection when present. Gln154 and Ser157 are no more buried than the genuine catalytic residues of LYS-6. Packing is an incomplete explanation and it is not excluded.
  8. Charge gives no discrimination. The two clades are statistically indistinguishable in net charge at pH 5.0 (p = 0.219), consistent with a retained affinity for an anionic cell-wall polymer and equally consistent with shared ancestry.

Knowledge gaps

  1. No enzyme assay on LYS-1. PubMed was searched for C. elegans lysozyme biochemistry; the only purified, assayed C. elegans lysozyme is ILYS-5 (PMID: 38925432). This matters because a negative result would convert the prediction into direct evidence and a positive one would overturn it. Resolved by: recombinant LYS-1 tested on Micrococcus luteus cells and fluorescent peptidoglycan across pH 3-7 at low ionic strength, using the ILYS-5 and CF50 conditions, with an active paralog as positive control.
  2. Does LYS-1 bind peptidoglycan without cleaving it? Not tested anywhere. The most informative open question, because the groove is under selection (Asp64, Gln154 and Ser157 all above the 80th percentile of their domain background) while the ligand is unidentified. Resolved by: pull-down or ITC of recombinant LYS-1 with insoluble peptidoglycan and with defined muropeptides. A negative result would redirect the search to another cell-wall or glycan ligand.
  3. Which GH25 residue is the general acid and which the base? The curated ACT_SITE records give positions without roles, and literature searches for the Cellosyl and CPL-1 mechanism papers returned nothing usable. This matters only for the precision of the mechanistic wording, not for the conclusion, since both roles are absent in LYS-1.
  4. Do the nine C. elegans GH25 proteins act together? Four keep an intact triad and five do not. Whether the dead ones work alongside the live ones is unknown and decides whether a shared non-catalytic BP term is appropriate.
  5. No experimental structure. There is no PDB entry for any nematode GH25 protein. This matters for confirming the cleft geometry and for measuring real side-chain positions rather than modelled ones.
  6. The exposure of the LYS-1 cleft is not properly measured. The neighbour-count proxy used here is weak inside a deep groove. Proper solvent-accessible surface area with a probe large enough to represent a glycan would give a sharper burial control and could rescue the exposed-only test.

Discriminating tests

  1. Express LYS-1 and LYS-6 in parallel and assay both on M. luteus and on peptidoglycan at pH 3.0-5.0. LYS-6 active and LYS-1 inactive confirms the prediction in one experiment.
  2. Gain-of-function reversion: LYS-1 Q154D/S157E double mutant. If muramidase activity appears, the two substitutions are the sole cause of inactivation and the structural mapping is correct.
  3. Loss-of-function control in the active paralog: LYS-6 D112Q/E114S. Should abolish activity.
  4. Peptidoglycan pull-down comparing wild-type LYS-1, a D64N mutant and a Q154A/S157A double mutant. The conservation result predicts Gln154 and Ser157 contribute to binding, so alanine substitution should reduce it if that prediction holds. This is the single test that separates the ligand-site and fold-packing explanations.
  5. Rescue specificity: test whether lys-1 overexpression still increases S. marcescens resistance when Asp64 is mutated. If the defence phenotype is Asp64-independent, it is not cleft-mediated.
  6. Extend the clade scan to a maximum-likelihood phylogeny of nematode PF31264 proteins to date the loss and confirm the two clades are monophyletic rather than score-defined.

Limitations

No wet-lab evidence exists on LYS-1 enzyme activity in either direction. The conclusion is a structural and evolutionary prediction.

All structures are AlphaFold models. No experimental structure exists for any nematode GH25 protein.

Clade assignment used best global-alignment score against two references, not a phylogeny. The two groups are strongly separated by this criterion, and monophyly is assumed rather than demonstrated.

The EBI Clustal Omega job dispatcher returned "You have been temporarily blocked from submitting new sequence analysis jobs", so no multiple-sequence alignment was run. All residue mapping uses pairwise global alignment against several independent references instead, which agree with each other.

Phenix could not be used for the superposition, because host-side network access is blocked and the structure files live in the code executor. The superposition was done with a Kabsch fit with iterative outlier trimming and is reported with its CA count (150) and RMSD (1.20 Å).

The burial control uses neighbour count as an exposure proxy, which performs poorly inside a deep groove, and this is why the LYS-1 exposed-only test could not be computed.
