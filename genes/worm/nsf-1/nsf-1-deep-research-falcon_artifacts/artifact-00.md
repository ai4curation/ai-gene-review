| Identity | **nsf-1** in *Caenorhabditis elegans* encodes the nematode N-ethylmaleimide-sensitive factor (NSF), corresponding to the target **H15N14.2 / UniProt Q94392**; recent literature describes it as the ortholog of human NSF. | Gene-to-accession mapping from the supplied UniProt record; worm identity and orthology supported directly by literature. | Gkikas et al., 20 Feb 2023 (pqac-00000009); Kinchen et al., Apr 2008 (pqac-00000015) |
| Primary biochemical function and reaction | NSF-1 is an ATP-dependent SNARE-disassembly factor. The inferred conserved reaction is **ATP + H₂O → ADP + phosphate**, with hydrolysis coupled to mechanical disassembly and recycling of assembled SNARE complexes rather than fusion of lipid bilayers directly. Direct ATPase kinetics have not been reported for purified worm NSF-1 in the retrieved literature. | Strong family/orthology-based mechanistic inference; SNARE-disassembly role used experimentally in worm studies, but the detailed catalytic cycle derives mainly from NSF work in other systems. | Gkikas et al., 2023 (pqac-00000002); Müller et al., Oct 1999 (pqac-00000003); Hay & Scheller, Aug 1997 (pqac-00000004) |
| Substrate specificity | The functional substrate is a **SNAP-bound assembled SNARE complex**—including binary or ternary cis-SNARE assemblies—not an individual lipid or transported solute. ATP hydrolysis promotes complex disassembly so SNARE proteins can be reused. The exact worm SNARE-complex preferences remain uncharacterized biochemically. | Conserved NSF mechanism; not established by purified *C. elegans* NSF-1 substrate-panel experiments. | Gkikas et al., 2023 (pqac-00000002); Müller et al., 1999 (pqac-00000003) |
| Protein family and domains | UniProt Q94392 assigns NSF-1 to the **AAA ATPase family** and annotates AAA+ ATPase, AAA lid, AAA core/conserved-site, and Asp-decarboxylase-like superfamily domains. This architecture agrees with an energy-dependent SNARE-remodeling enzyme. | **Database annotation from the UniProt information supplied in the research request**; consistent with literature identifying NSF-1 as an ATPase, but no worm protein structure or domain-mutagenesis study was found. | Supplied UniProt Q94392 annotation; Kinchen et al., 2008 (pqac-00000015) |
| Cellular and tissue localization | NSF-1 is expected to be a cytosolic, peripheral membrane-associated trafficking ATPase recruited transiently to SNAP–SNARE assemblies. Two isoforms, NSF-1a and NSF-1b, were reported; **NSF-1a was computationally predicted** to target mitochondria, with a predicted presequence cleavage between residues 23 and 24. Direct imaging of NSF-1 itself at mitochondria was not demonstrated in the cited passage. | General NSF localization is family-based inference; NSF-1a mitochondrial targeting is computational prediction, not direct localization evidence. | Gkikas et al., 2023 (pqac-00000009) |
| Uterine anchor-cell fusion | **nsf-1 is required cell-autonomously for uterine anchor-cell fusion.** Later identification of AFF-1 as the direct fusogen supports the interpretation that NSF-1 may enable trafficking or surface delivery of fusion machinery rather than directly merging the two plasma membranes. | Direct worm genetics reported by Choi et al.; mechanistic placement relative to AFF-1 remains interpretive. | Choi et al., Sep 2006, as summarized and refined by Sapir et al., May 2007 (pqac-00000014) |
| Mitochondrial mass and morphology | nsf-1 RNAi reduced mitochondrial mass and produced circular, swollen, disorganized muscle mitochondrial networks. Co-depletion of nsf-1 canceled mitochondrial-mass increases caused by loss of several mitochondrial-associated SNARE or tethering factors, placing NSF-1 functionally in the same maintenance system. | Direct *C. elegans* RNAi, reporter, immunoblot, and confocal evidence; pathway assignment is based on genetic interaction. | Gkikas et al., 2023 (pqac-00000007, pqac-00000009) |
| Basal autophagy | NSF-1 depletion reduced the lipidated/unlipidated LGG-1 ratio and decreased LGG-1-positive autophagosomes; it also abolished autophagosome accumulation caused by depletion of SYX-17, VAMP-7, or SNB-6. These results support a requirement for NSF-1 in basal autophagosome formation or maintenance, while not by themselves identifying its direct SNARE substrate in this pathway. | Direct worm RNAi, immunoblot, fluorescent-reporter, and epistasis evidence. | Gkikas et al., 2023 (pqac-00000008) |
| Aging and lifespan | Neuron-specific nsf-1 inhibition shortened lifespan, and broad non-neuronal inhibition also produced marked lifespan shortening, indicating that SNARE disassembly is required across tissues for normal longevity. This is a systems-level phenotype and does not establish a single causal organelle or substrate. | Direct tissue-restricted RNAi and survival assays; mechanism remains multifactorial. | Gkikas et al., 2023 (pqac-00000008, pqac-00000001) |
| α-Synuclein-associated neuronal phenotype | The **nsf-1(ty10)** mutant increased dopaminergic CEP dendrite blebbing in a neuronal human α-synuclein model. Insoluble α-synuclein was decreased rather than increased, arguing that the worsened morphology was not simply proportional to insoluble α-synuclein abundance. | Direct mutant validation, fluorescence microscopy, and biochemical fractionation in a transgenic disease model; modifier result does not prove a native Parkinson-disease pathway. | Vozdek et al., Apr 2022 (pqac-00000010, pqac-00000011) |
| Apoptotic-corpse phagosome maturation | nsf-1 RNAi did **not** increase persistent apoptotic-cell corpses despite substantial transcript depletion, indicating that NSF-1 was not detectably required for efficient corpse degradation under those assay conditions. This negative result is context-specific and does not exclude roles in other endosomal-fusion events. | Direct worm RNAi with RT-PCR depletion control and corpse-counting assay; informative negative evidence. | Kinchen et al., Apr 2008 (pqac-00000015) |
