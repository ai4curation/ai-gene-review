# Functional annotation report: *C. elegans nsf-1* (UniProt Q94392)

## Executive conclusion

The requested target is correctly identified: ***Caenorhabditis elegans nsf-1***, ORF **H15N14.2**, UniProt **Q94392**, encodes the nematode N-ethylmaleimide-sensitive factor—an evolutionarily conserved vesicle-fusing **AAA-family ATPase**. Recent literature explicitly calls worm NSF-1 the ortholog of human NSF, and older worm work independently identifies it as the nematode NSF homolog. No conflicting same-symbol protein from another organism was used in this report. The exact H15N14.2/Q94392 cross-reference comes primarily from the supplied UniProt record because most papers refer only to *nsf-1*. (gkikas2023mitosnareassemblyand pages 4-6, kinchen2008apathwayfor pages 13-16)

Its best-supported primary function is ATP-powered remodeling and disassembly of SNAP-bound SNARE complexes, thereby recycling SNAREs for subsequent rounds of membrane trafficking and fusion. In the worm, direct functional evidence links NSF-1 to uterine anchor-cell fusion, mitochondrial maintenance, basal autophagy, normal lifespan, and neuronal integrity under α-synuclein stress. However, purified *C. elegans* NSF-1 ATPase kinetics, a direct substrate panel, and a worm NSF-1 structure were not found in the retrieved literature.

## 1. Molecular identity, family, and domains

The supplied UniProt annotation names Q94392 as “vesicle-fusing ATPase,” with the alternatives N-ethylmaleimide-sensitive fusion protein and NSF. It assigns the protein to the **AAA ATPase family** and annotates an AAA+ ATPase module, AAA core and conserved-site features, an AAA lid, and an Asp-decarboxylase-like structural superfamily region. These annotations are internally consistent with the conserved architecture of NSF proteins, which use ATPase-driven conformational work to remodel protein complexes.

The literature supports the identity independently. Kinchen et al. described NSF-1 as the nematode homolog of NSF and as an ATPase component of vesicular-fusion machinery; Gkikas et al. described it as the *C. elegans* ortholog of human NSF and a SNARE-disassembly factor. Thus the gene symbol, species, protein description, and AAA-family assignment all align; there is no indication that the retrieved worm studies concern a different *nsf-1*. (gkikas2023mitosnareassemblyand pages 4-6, kinchen2008apathwayfor pages 13-16)

Gkikas et al. further reported two predicted protein isoforms, NSF-1a and NSF-1b. Bioinformatic analysis predicted a mitochondrial targeting presequence for NSF-1a, with cleavage between residues 23 and 24. This is a **prediction**, not direct imaging of endogenous NSF-1 at mitochondria. (gkikas2023mitosnareassemblyand pages 2-4, gkikas2023mitosnareassemblyand pages 4-6)

## 2. Primary biochemical function and substrate specificity

### Catalyzed reaction

As an ATPase (EC 3.6.4.6), the underlying chemical reaction is:

**ATP + H₂O → ADP + inorganic phosphate.**

The biologically important output is mechanical rather than transport of a small molecule: ATP hydrolysis powers conformational remodeling and disassembly of assembled SNARE complexes. NSF acts with soluble NSF-attachment proteins, or SNAPs, which couple NSF to the SNARE assembly. This releases individual SNARE proteins for reuse in later fusion cycles. Worm studies treat NSF-1 as this conserved SNARE-disassembly factor, although direct ATP-turnover or reconstitution measurements with purified worm NSF-1 were not identified. (gkikas2023mitosnareassemblyand pages 1-2, hay1997snaresandnsf pages 7-7)

### Substrate specificity

The relevant macromolecular substrate is best described as a **SNAP-bound assembled SNARE complex**, including assembled binary or ternary cis-SNARE complexes after membrane fusion—not ATP alone and not a transported solute. The retrieved evidence does not establish that worm NSF-1 selectively recognizes one particular SNARE combination. Accordingly, annotation as a general SNARE-complex recycling ATPase is better supported than assignment to a single vesicle class. (gkikas2023mitosnareassemblyand pages 1-2, hay1997snaresandnsf pages 7-7)

General mammalian work adds an important expert caveat: ATPase-defective NSF mutants could support approximately 80% of wild-type Golgi cisternal regrowth in a cell-free assay despite defective SNARE disassembly, suggesting that NSF may have priming or membrane-fusion functions beyond simple complex breakup. This result should be treated as conserved-family context, not direct evidence for an additional reaction by worm NSF-1. (muller1999annsffunction pages 4-5, muller1999annsffunction pages 5-6)

## 3. Where NSF-1 acts

NSF-family proteins are soluble cytosolic ATPases that associate transiently with membranes through SNAP–SNARE assemblies. NSF-1 should therefore be regarded primarily as a **cytosolic/peripheral membrane-trafficking enzyme**, not an integral membrane protein.

Direct worm genetics indicate action in multiple cellular contexts:

* **Uterine anchor cell:** *nsf-1* is required cell-autonomously for anchor-cell fusion. Subsequent discovery that AFF-1 is the direct fusogen makes it more likely that NSF-1 supports vesicular delivery, positioning, or recycling of fusion machinery rather than directly fusing the two plasma membranes. The original authors explicitly regarded direct action in cell fusion versus regulation of fusogen trafficking as unresolved. (sapir2007aff1afos1regulated pages 2-5)
* **Mitochondrial-associated trafficking:** NSF-1a has a predicted mitochondrial targeting sequence. In 2023 experiments, *nsf-1* knockdown altered mitochondrial abundance and morphology and genetically interacted with mitochondrial-associated SNAREs, but the cited localization experiments directly imaged SYX-17, VAMP-7, SNB-6, and USO-1—not NSF-1 itself. Mitochondrial localization of NSF-1 should therefore remain qualified as predicted/functional rather than definitively visualized. (gkikas2023mitosnareassemblyand pages 6-8, gkikas2023mitosnareassemblyand pages 4-6)
* **Neurons and non-neuronal tissues:** Tissue-restricted RNAi showed that neuronal NSF-1 is required for normal lifespan, while knockdown outside neurons also shortened lifespan substantially. These results imply broad physiological demand but do not define the protein’s subcellular location in each tissue. (gkikas2023mitosnareassemblyand pages 8-11, gkikas2023mitosnareassemblyand pages 11-13)

## 4. Biological processes and pathways

### 4.1 SNARE cycling and membrane trafficking

The central pathway assignment is the **SNARE-mediated membrane-fusion cycle**. SNAREs on opposing membranes assemble to drive bilayer fusion; NSF/SNAP then disassembles the post-fusion complex and regenerates fusion-competent components. NSF-1 is therefore a recycling/remodeling factor required to sustain repeated trafficking events rather than the principal fusogen itself. (gkikas2023mitosnareassemblyand pages 1-2, hay1997snaresandnsf pages 7-7)

### 4.2 Anchor-cell fusion during uterine development

Direct worm genetic evidence places *nsf-1* cell-autonomously in fusion of the uterine anchor cell. Later work established AFF-1, under FOS-1 regulation, as the direct anchor-cell fusogen and cautioned that NSF-1 might regulate AFF-1 or another fusion component’s trafficking and surface localization. This is the clearest developmental example of NSF-1 enabling a membrane-fusion-dependent event while probably acting upstream of the actual membrane fusogen. (sapir2007aff1afos1regulated pages 2-5)

### 4.3 Mitochondrial homeostasis—major 2023 development

Gkikas et al., published **20 February 2023**, substantially expanded the worm annotation. RNAi against *nsf-1* significantly reduced mitochondrial mass measured with mitochondrial protein reporters. In body-wall muscle, knockdown produced a circular, swollen, and disorganized mitochondrial network. Moreover, NSF-1 depletion canceled mitochondrial-mass increases caused by depletion of SYX-17, VAMP-7, SNB-6, or USO-1, providing genetic evidence that SNARE assembly and NSF-dependent disassembly cooperate in mitochondrial maintenance. COX-4::GFP analyses included **n=202** animals for the *nsf-1* experiment, with **p=0.03** reported; related interaction experiments included **n=225** or **n=116** and three biological replicates. [DOI: 10.3390/ijms24044230](https://doi.org/10.3390/ijms24044230). (gkikas2023mitosnareassemblyand pages 6-8, gkikas2023mitosnareassemblyand pages 4-6)

These findings do not prove that mitochondria are a direct NSF-1 membrane-fusion substrate. They support a functional mitochondrial-associated SNARE pathway, potentially involving organelle-contact sites, mitochondrial-derived vesicles, or autophagy-related membranes.

### 4.4 Basal autophagy

The same 2023 study found that *nsf-1* depletion reduced the lipidated/unlipidated LGG-1 ratio and decreased LGG-1-positive autophagosomes in seam cells, indicating suppressed basal autophagic activity or autophagosome formation/maintenance. Co-depletion of NSF-1 completely abolished the autophagosome accumulation produced by loss of SYX-17, VAMP-7, or SNB-6. The LGG-1 puncta analysis reported **p<0.001**, while immunoblot comparisons reached significance from **p<0.05 to p<0.0001**, depending on the condition. (gkikas2023mitosnareassemblyand pages 8-11)

The pathway interpretation requires nuance. Depletion of the SNARE assembly factors appeared to obstruct autophagic flux and cause autophagosome accumulation, whereas depletion of the disassembly factor NSF-1 reduced autophagosome abundance. These opposing phenotypes are consistent with the need for a balanced SNARE assembly–disassembly cycle; they do not show that every phenotype arises from one direct NSF-1/SNARE complex. (gkikas2023mitosnareassemblyand pages 8-11)

### 4.5 Aging and longevity

Neuron-specific inhibition of *nsf-1* significantly shortened lifespan. Broad inhibition in non-neuronal tissues also produced a striking lifespan reduction, unlike individual non-neuronal depletion of several mitoSNAREs. The authors interpreted this as evidence that SNARE tethering and disassembly are broadly required for normal aging. Exact lifespan values were assigned to supplementary data and were not available in the retrieved excerpt, so effect sizes should not be invented. (gkikas2023mitosnareassemblyand pages 11-13, gkikas2023mitosnareassemblyand pages 8-11)

### 4.6 Neuronal integrity and proteotoxic stress

A 2022 reverse-genetic study used a worm Parkinson-disease model expressing human α-synuclein–Venus in dopaminergic neurons. Among 98 tested knockdown/mutant populations, 10 gene inactivations reduced axonal inclusion penetrance and 18 increased CEP dendrite blebbing. The *nsf-1(ty10)* allele was independently validated as increasing dendrite blebbing. Intriguingly, insoluble α-synuclein was decreased in the mutant, so the worsened neuronal morphology did not simply track with insoluble α-synuclein abundance. [Published April 2022; DOI: 10.3389/fnagi.2022.806000](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnagi.2022.806000). (vozdek2022functionalscreeningof pages 6-8, vozdek2022functionalscreeningof pages 5-6)

This is a useful experimental application of *nsf-1* as a vesicular-trafficking modifier in a neurodegeneration model, but it does not demonstrate that endogenous worm NSF-1 is a Parkinson-disease gene or that α-synuclein is its biochemical substrate.

### 4.7 Context-specific negative evidence: corpse clearance

Kinchen et al. tested *nsf-1* during phagosome maturation and apoptotic-cell degradation. Despite approximately **86% reduction of gonadal nsf-1 mRNA**, RNAi did not increase persistent cell corpses. The authors concluded that the proposed NSF-1/EEA-1/RABS-5 vesicle-concentration step was dispensable for efficient corpse degradation under their assay conditions. [Published April 2008; DOI: 10.1038/ncb1718](https://doi.org/10.1038/ncb1718). This is informative negative evidence: NSF-1 is not detectably rate-limiting in every membrane-trafficking pathway. (kinchen2008apathwayfor pages 13-16)

## 5. Current applications and translational relevance

There is no direct clinical implementation of worm *nsf-1*. Its principal applications are experimental:

1. **Dissecting conserved SNARE recycling** in a genetically tractable animal.
2. **Developmental cell-fusion studies**, where *nsf-1* separates trafficking support from direct fusogen activity.
3. **Organelle-quality-control research**, particularly genetic interactions among mitochondrial-associated SNAREs, autophagy, and mitochondrial morphology.
4. **Aging studies** using tissue-selective RNAi and survival assays.
5. **Disease-model modifier screens**, exemplified by α-synuclein-associated dopaminergic-neuron damage.

The translational rationale is conservation: NSF-dependent SNARE recycling is fundamental to secretion, neurotransmission, endolysosomal traffic, and autophagy. Nevertheless, NSF is broadly required, and the worm lifespan data reinforce that systemic inhibition is likely to have extensive toxicity. Thus NSF-1 is currently more compelling as a pathway probe and model-system node than as a straightforward therapeutic target. (gkikas2023mitosnareassemblyand pages 8-11, vozdek2022functionalscreeningof pages 5-6)

## 6. Evidence-weighted expert assessment

**High-confidence annotation:** NSF-1 is a conserved AAA ATPase/SNARE-disassembly factor that uses ATP hydrolysis to recycle assembled SNARE machinery. It is required for normal membrane-trafficking capacity and supports specific fusion-dependent processes in *C. elegans*. (gkikas2023mitosnareassemblyand pages 1-2, gkikas2023mitosnareassemblyand pages 4-6, kinchen2008apathwayfor pages 13-16)

**Strong direct worm evidence:** cell-autonomous requirement in anchor-cell fusion; effects on mitochondrial mass and morphology; requirement for normal basal autophagy and lifespan; modification of α-synuclein-associated neuronal injury. (gkikas2023mitosnareassemblyand pages 6-8, gkikas2023mitosnareassemblyand pages 8-11, vozdek2022functionalscreeningof pages 5-6, sapir2007aff1afos1regulated pages 2-5)

**Moderate/inferential annotation:** direct mitochondrial targeting of NSF-1a, because this rests on sequence prediction and pathway genetics rather than direct endogenous-protein localization. Likewise, the precise SNARE substrates in anchor-cell fusion and autophagy remain unidentified. (gkikas2023mitosnareassemblyand pages 4-6, sapir2007aff1afos1regulated pages 2-5)

**Key knowledge gaps:** no retrieved purified-worm ATPase assay, catalytic constants, cryo-EM/X-ray structure, comprehensive worm SNARE substrate panel, or definitive endogenous NSF-1 localization map. The recent literature search identified the 2023 mitoSNARE/autophagy study as the newest directly relevant functional paper; no comparably direct 2024 *nsf-1* study was found.

## Overall functional annotation

The most defensible concise annotation is: **NSF-1 is a broadly acting, ATP-dependent SNARE-complex disassembly and recycling enzyme that operates on the cytosolic face of trafficking membranes. In *C. elegans*, this conserved activity supports developmental anchor-cell fusion, mitochondrial homeostasis, basal autophagy, neuronal robustness, and normal longevity. Its direct physiological substrate is assembled SNAP–SNARE machinery; individual pathway-specific SNARE preferences and direct mitochondrial localization remain unresolved.**
