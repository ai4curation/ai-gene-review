|---|---|---|---|
| **Somatic gonad: anchor cell/ventral uterine precursor cells** | Binary anchor-cell (AC) versus ventral-uterine (VU) fate decision between initially equivalent cells | Reciprocal ligand–receptor feedback produces unequal signaling: the cell with sustained LIN-12 activity adopts the VU fate, whereas the cell with low activity retains LAG-2 expression and becomes the AC. (pqac-00000023, pqac-00000024) | Loss or reduction of signaling can produce two AC-like cells; excessive LIN-12 activity can eliminate the AC, preventing AC-derived LIN-3/EGF signaling and causing a Vulvaless phenotype. (pqac-00000025, pqac-00000051) |
| **Vulval precursor cells (P3.p–P8.p)** | Patterning of primary (1°), secondary (2°), and tertiary (3°) vulval fates | LIN-3/EGFR signaling specifies central P6.p as 1° and induces LAG-2, APX-1, and DSL-1. These ligands activate LIN-12 in adjacent P5.p and P7.p, promoting 2° fate and suppressing the competing 1° program. (pqac-00000024, pqac-00000028) | The normal pattern is 3°–3°–2°–1°–2°–3°. Constitutive LIN-12 activity drives ectopic 2° fates and Multivulva phenotypes, whereas lin-12 null animals lack 2° fates. (pqac-00000028) |
| **Ventral uterus and uterine–vulval connection** | Specification of π-cell fates in ventral uterine descendants | After AC specification, AC-derived signaling activates LIN-12 unidirectionally in neighboring uterine cells, inducing specialized π fates rather than operating through lateral inhibition. (pqac-00000006) | π-cell descendants form specialized uterine structures required for a functional uterine–vulval connection and egg laying; defective signaling disrupts this connection. (pqac-00000006, pqac-00000029) |
| **Postembryonic M mesodermal lineage** | Dorsoventral diversification and sex-myoblast specification | LIN-12, together with TGF-β-related patterning inputs, promotes ventral sex-myoblast identity; it acts again within the sex-myoblast lineage to distinguish type-II (VM2) from type-I (VM1) vulval muscles. (pqac-00000023, pqac-00000027) | Reduced LIN-12 pathway activity dorsalizes the ventral M lineage, eliminates sex myoblasts, or transforms VM2 into VM1. Loss of VM2 identity and muscle-arm formation compromises innervation and egg laying. (pqac-00000027) |
| **Neurons and hypodermis-to-brain signaling** | Regulation of associative memory and age-dependent cognitive performance | Reduced hypodermal DAF-2 insulin signaling induces secreted OSM-11, which activates neuronal LIN-12 together with LAG-1 and SEL-8; downstream CRH-1/CREB activity promotes memory-gene expression. (pqac-00000057, pqac-00000059) | Hypodermal DAF-2 depletion extended associative memory for more than five hours in reported assays; neuronal LIN-12 knockdown or loss of LAG-1/SEL-8 blocked the improvement. OSM-11 overexpression improved memory and learning in older animals. (pqac-00000056, pqac-00000057) |
| **Epithelial-to-neuronal transdifferentiation** | Natural conversion of a differentiated epithelial cell into a PDA motoneuron | LIN-12 functions as a temporally controlled competence signal: appropriate early activation supports plasticity and neuronal reprogramming, whereas signaling outside the permissive window can inhibit the conversion. This illustrates that LIN-12 output depends on timing and cellular context. (pqac-00000029) | Reduced LIN-12 activity impairs the identity conversion, while appropriately timed or ectopic activation can promote precocious or ectopic transdifferentiation; prolonged or late activation can instead block completion of the process. (pqac-00000029) |
