| Feature | Evidence-based annotation | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Identity | **LIN-12**, encoded by *lin-12* (ORF R107.8), is one of the two Notch-family receptors in *Caenorhabditis elegans* and corresponds to UniProt **P14585**. It is primarily a somatic developmental receptor, whereas its paralog GLP-1 acts mainly in the early embryo and germ line. | (pqac-00000056, pqac-00000059, pqac-00000072) |
| Protein class and architecture | A precursor, single-pass transmembrane Notch receptor. Its extracellular region contains tandem EGF-like repeats for ligand binding and a negative regulatory region that prevents ligand-independent cleavage. The intracellular region contains a RAM/CSL-association module, ankyrin repeats, nuclear-localization information, a transcriptional activation region, and a C-terminal PEST/phosphodegron-containing region governing turnover. | (pqac-00000018, pqac-00000070, pqac-00000072) |
| Primary molecular function | LIN-12 is a **ligand-activated cell-surface signaling receptor**, not an enzyme or transporter. It converts short-range intercellular signals into changes in nuclear transcription, principally to distinguish alternative cell fates during lateral signaling. | (pqac-00000000, pqac-00000003, pqac-00000025) |
| Ligands | Experimentally supported activating ligands include the DSL-family proteins **LAG-2** and **APX-1**; **DSL-1** also participates in vulval lateral signaling. OSM-11 and other DOS proteins can act as noncanonical co-ligands or pathway modulators in physiological contexts. Effective canonical activation generally favors a membrane-associated ligand, although worm Notch receptors have unusually low mechanical activation thresholds. | (pqac-00000008, pqac-00000009, pqac-00000026, pqac-00000058) |
| Receptor activation | Ligand engagement changes the conformation of the autoinhibited negative regulatory region and exposes the extracellular **S2** site. SUP-17/ADAM, potentially with ADM-4, cleaves at S2 and sheds the ectodomain; the SEL-12/HOP-1 presenilin-containing γ-secretase complex then cleaves the membrane-tethered fragment at **S3**. | (pqac-00000012, pqac-00000013, pqac-00000027) |
| Distinctive mechanotransduction | The negative regulatory regions of LIN-12 and GLP-1 require less mechanical force for activation than *Drosophila* Notch. Consequently, LIN-12 signaling does not strictly require Epsin-dependent endocytosis of the ligand; adding the normally absent leucine “plug” raises the force requirement and restores Epsin dependence. | (pqac-00000008, pqac-00000009, pqac-00000016) |
| Nuclear signaling mechanism | S3 cleavage releases the LIN-12 intracellular domain, which enters the nucleus and binds **LAG-1/CSL** plus **SEL-8/LAG-3**, a Mastermind-family coactivator. This complex converts DNA-bound LAG-1 from a repressive state into a transcriptional activator and recruits additional coactivators. | (pqac-00000003, pqac-00000012, pqac-00000014) |
| Subcellular localization | Inactive full-length LIN-12 resides at the plasma membrane, prominently at the apical membrane of vulval precursor cells. Ligand activation causes the intracellular domain to accumulate in nuclei; endogenous LIN-12::mNeonGreen directly visualized nuclear signal in active P5.p/P7.p cells, ventral M-lineage progenitors, and somatic-gonad precursors, while inactive neighboring cells retained membrane/cytoplasmic signal. Receptor endocytosis can lead to recycling, cleavage, or lysosomal degradation. | (pqac-00000020, pqac-00000031, pqac-00000072) |
| Supported transcriptional outputs | Vulval LIN-12-responsive genes include **dpy-23, lst-1, lst-2, lst-3, lst-4, lst-5, lst-6, mir-61**, and candidate/associated targets such as **ark-1, lip-1, let-502**, and **tbc-17**. Several outputs antagonize LET-23/EGFR–RAS–MAPK signaling rather than acting as uniquely instructive 2°-fate determinants. LIN-12 activity also drives positive autoregulation of **lag-1** through LAG-1-binding sites in a conserved enhancer. | (pqac-00000038, pqac-00000040, pqac-00000041, pqac-00000042, pqac-00000043, pqac-00000044) |
| Vulval development | LIN-3/EGF–LET-23/EGFR signaling specifies P6.p as the primary (**1°**) vulval precursor and induces its DSL ligands. These ligands activate LIN-12 in adjacent P5.p and P7.p, specifying/stabilizing the secondary (**2°**) fates and producing the normal 3°–3°–2°–1°–2°–3° pattern. Loss of LIN-12 eliminates 2° fates; constitutive activation can convert all competent VPCs toward 2° fate and produce a multivulva phenotype. | (pqac-00000025, pqac-00000032, pqac-00000033) |
| Anchor-cell/ventral-uterine decision | Neighboring somatic-gonad precursors initially express LIN-12 and LAG-2. Reciprocal signaling is resolved so that the cell with higher LIN-12 activity adopts the **ventral uterine precursor (VU)** fate, whereas the low-LIN-12 cell becomes the single **anchor cell (AC)**. Constitutive LIN-12 activity suppresses AC formation; correct AC specification is subsequently essential because the AC supplies LIN-3/EGF for vulval induction. | (pqac-00000046, pqac-00000047, pqac-00000048, pqac-00000049) |
| Other developmental and physiological roles | LIN-12 participates in ventral M-lineage/sex-myoblast specification, somatic-gonad and spermathecal development, excretory-system decisions, and additional embryonic and larval somatic fate choices. Post-developmentally, LIN-12 modulates GABAergic signaling at the neuromuscular junction; emerging work also implicates neuronal LIN-12 signaling downstream of hypodermal OSM-11 in associative memory and cognitive aging. | (pqac-00000023, pqac-00000029, pqac-00000054, pqac-00000071, pqac-00000072) |
| Spatial regulation in vulval cells | LIN-12 is initially broadly present in VPCs. After induction, EGFR signaling promotes LIN-12 endocytosis and degradation in primary-fate P6.p, while LIN-12 persists and signals in P5.p/P7.p and their descendants. LIN-1/Elk1, SUR-2/Med23, and the CDK8 kinase module help integrate EGFR and LIN-12 outputs and prevent inappropriate Notch responses in P6.p. | (pqac-00000024, pqac-00000027, pqac-00000068) |
| Turnover and signal termination | **SEL-10/Fbw7** recognizes a phosphorylated Cdc4 phosphodegron in nuclear LIN-12 intracellular domain and promotes ubiquitination and degradation. A C-terminal “Y region,” association with the LAG-1 complex, and stage-specific mechanisms also control stability. **SEL-2** regulates receptor trafficking, and distinct degradation mechanisms operate before versus after VPC division. | (pqac-00000062, pqac-00000063, pqac-00000064, pqac-00000066) |
| Comparison with GLP-1 | LIN-12 and GLP-1 are paralogous Notch receptors with similar intracellular signaling machinery and partial interchangeability, but largely different expression and biological emphasis: LIN-12 controls multiple somatic fate decisions, especially gonadal and vulval patterning; GLP-1 dominates early embryonic signaling and germline stem-cell maintenance. GLP-1 appears even more mechanically sensitive to activation, and the receptors can have opposite effects on adult neuromuscular-junction physiology. | (pqac-00000054, pqac-00000055, pqac-00000056, pqac-00000057, pqac-00000059) |
| Functional interpretation | The best-supported annotation is **canonical Notch receptor and ligand-regulated transcriptional switch for local cell-fate specification**. LIN-12 does not catalyze a freely diffusible biochemical reaction; instead, its own regulated proteolysis physically carries information from the cell surface to the nucleus. | (pqac-00000000, pqac-00000014, pqac-00000072) |
