# Functional annotation report: *Caenorhabditis elegans rab-3* (UniProt Q94986)

## Executive conclusion

The supplied target is correctly identified: *C. elegans rab-3* (ORF **C18A3.6**, UniProt **Q94986**) encodes the worm’s single Rab3-family small GTPase, not a vertebrate RAB3 paralog or another similarly named Rab. Its Rab3, small-GTPase, P-loop NTPase, and small-GTPase-binding-domain annotations are mutually consistent with the organism-specific literature. The primary function of RAB-3 is to act as a **GTP-regulated, synaptic-vesicle-associated molecular switch** that organizes vesicles near presynaptic active zones and thereby supports efficient, spatially focused neurotransmitter release. It is not a metabolic enzyme, solute transporter, or static structural protein. (reiner2018smallgtpases. pages 30-32, nonet1997caenorhabditiselegansrab3 pages 1-2)

The strongest mechanistic model is:

**AEX-3-mediated nucleotide exchange → membrane-associated GTP–RAB-3 on synaptic vesicles → binding to active-zone UNC-10/RIM → vesicle recruitment/retention adjacent to the presynaptic density → efficient evoked and spontaneous transmitter release.** RAB-3 is important but not absolutely required for exocytosis; null mutants remain viable and retain regulated release, reflecting modulatory function and partial overlap with RAB-27. (mahoney2006regulationofsynaptic pages 5-6, mahoney2006regulationofsynaptic pages 6-7, gracheva2008directinteractionsbetween pages 1-2, nonet1997caenorhabditiselegansrab3 pages 1-2)

## 1. Mandatory identity verification

### Gene, organism, and family

Organism-specific genetic literature identifies *rab-3* as the single *C. elegans* Rab3 gene. This agrees with the supplied record—gene *rab-3*, ORF C18A3.6, UniProt Q94986, protein “Ras-related protein Rab-3”—and with assignment to the Rab branch of the small GTPase superfamily. No evidence was found that the symbol refers to a different worm protein. (nonet1997caenorhabditiselegansrab3 pages 1-2)

This distinction matters because mammals have RAB3A–RAB3D paralogs, whose functions and partners cannot automatically be assigned to Q94986. The present annotation therefore prioritizes direct worm genetics, microscopy, electron microscopy, electrophysiology, and biochemistry. General Rab-family biochemistry is labeled as conserved inference where it has not been demonstrated directly with purified Q94986.

## 2. Molecular function and biochemical mechanism

### A nucleotide-dependent molecular switch

Rab proteins alternate between an inactive GDP-bound state and an active GTP-bound state. Guanine-nucleotide exchange factors, or GEFs, promote GDP release and GTP loading; GAPs accelerate GTP hydrolysis. GDP-bound Rab is generally more cytosolic, whereas GTP-bound Rab associates with target membranes and recruits effectors. RAB-3’s P-loop NTPase/small-GTPase domains fit this mechanism. This cycle is authoritative Rab-family biochemistry, although every catalytic step has not been reconstructed with purified worm Q94986. (mahoney2006regulationofsynaptic pages 1-2, reiner2018smallgtpases. pages 30-32)

RAB-3 therefore has intrinsic **GTPase activity**: its biochemical reaction is hydrolysis of bound GTP to GDP and inorganic phosphate. It does not act on a free diffusible substrate in the manner of a conventional metabolic enzyme; specificity arises from guanine nucleotide binding, regulatory proteins, membrane association, and GTP-state-selective effectors.

### AEX-3 is the principal demonstrated upstream activator

AEX-3 is the worm Rab3-GEF homolog. Full-length AEX-3 strongly increased GTP-bound RAB-3 in biochemical pull-down experiments, whereas empty-vector and mutant AEX-3 controls did not. In *aex-3* mutants, endogenous and GFP-tagged RAB-3 were displaced from synapse-rich nerve-ring regions and accumulated in neuronal cell bodies. These results directly connect nucleotide activation to correct presynaptic localization. (mahoney2006regulationofsynaptic pages 5-6)

The more severe phenotype of *aex-3* than *rab-3* is not evidence that RAB-3 has an unrecognized essential catalytic function. AEX-3 also regulates RAB-27, creating partly overlapping exocytic pathways. (mahoney2006regulationofsynaptic pages 1-2, mahoney2006regulationofsynaptic pages 6-7)

### State-selective effector binding to UNC-10/RIM

The clearest direct effector mechanism is binding to UNC-10, the worm RIM active-zone protein. Yeast two-hybrid experiments found selective interaction of UNC-10 with activated GTP-state RAB-3, including RAB-3(Q81L), but not GDP-state RAB-3(T36N). The interaction did not require the C-terminal geranylgeranylation motif in that assay and was selective relative to other tested worm Rabs. (gracheva2008directinteractionsbetween pages 1-2)

This establishes a mechanistic bridge between a vesicle-associated Rab switch and the presynaptic active-zone scaffold. It supports a targeting/tethering role rather than RAB-3 being the terminal Ca²⁺ sensor or SNARE fusogen.

## 3. Cellular and subcellular localization

RAB-3 is principally neuronal and associated with **synaptic vesicles** in axons and presynaptic specializations. The functional site is therefore the cytoplasmic surface of synaptic vesicles near the presynaptic plasma membrane, especially around active zones/presynaptic dense projections. AEX-3 is required for enrichment in synapse-rich regions rather than neuronal somata. (mahoney2006regulationofsynaptic pages 5-6, reiner2018smallgtpases. pages 30-32)

Transport studies further identify RAB-3-positive vesicles as cargo of the kinesin-3 motor UNC-104. Co-immunoprecipitation, bimolecular fluorescence complementation, colocalization, and motility experiments implicate SYD-2 and UNC-10 as an additional linkage between UNC-104 and RAB-3-containing vesicles, strengthening motor–cargo association beyond the motor’s PH-domain interaction with vesicle lipid. This concerns long-range axonal delivery, whereas the RAB-3–UNC-10 interaction at the terminal concerns active-zone positioning. (bayansan2019unc10syd2linkskinesin3 pages 1-4)

## 4. Biological process and pathway

### Synaptic-vesicle organization near release sites

The foundational ultrastructural result is not loss of all vesicles, but their **redistribution**. In *rab-3* mutants, motor-neuron synapses contained approximately 40% of normal synaptic-vesicle levels, while intersynaptic axonal regions accumulated vesicles. Vesicles more than 400 nm from the presynaptic density increased by over fivefold. Yet total vesicle abundance per profile was not reduced: 9.7 ± 0.6 vesicles in *rab-3(js49)* versus 8.3 ± 0.6 in wild type. Synaptic-terminal density and overall vesicle populations were also broadly preserved. (nonet1997caenorhabditiselegansrab3 pages 1-2, nonet1997caenorhabditiselegansrab3 pages 10-11)

These observations argue against a primary defect in vesicle biogenesis or soma-to-axon transport. The more precise annotation is impaired **local recruitment, clustering, sequestration, or retention near release sites**. “Docking” alone is too narrow because the phenotype extends to vesicle spatial organization around the active zone.

### Active-zone targeting through UNC-10/RIM

Electron microscopy showed that *rab-3* and *unc-10* mutants share reduced positioning of docked vesicles immediately adjacent to the presynaptic density. The *unc-10; rab-3* double mutant did not worsen this targeting phenotype, supporting action in the same pathway. Release in the double mutant resembled the more severe *unc-10* phenotype, indicating that UNC-10 also has RAB-3-independent functions. Both mutants showed reduced calcium sensitivity of release, and UNC-10 colocalized with the UNC-2 calcium channel. (gracheva2008directinteractionsbetween pages 1-2)

Thus, the best-supported pathway-level role is that vesicular GTP–RAB-3 binds active-zone UNC-10/RIM to concentrate release-ready vesicles near the presynaptic density and calcium-entry machinery.

### Neurotransmitter-release consequences

At the neuromuscular junction, *rab-3* mutants had evoked postsynaptic currents equal to approximately **55% of wild type** and a **35% decrease in miniature-event frequency**, without a significant reduction in miniature-event amplitude. This combination indicates a presynaptic reduction in release probability or available release-ready vesicles rather than reduced postsynaptic responsiveness. (mahoney2006regulationofsynaptic pages 5-6)

Independent evidence includes impaired excitatory and inhibitory transmission in the pharyngeal nervous system and resistance to the acetylcholinesterase inhibitor aldicarb, consistent with depressed cholinergic secretion. Nevertheless, mutants survive and exhibit relatively mild overt behavioral abnormalities, showing that RAB-3 facilitates and organizes release rather than being absolutely required for vesicle fusion. (nonet1997caenorhabditiselegansrab3 pages 1-2, nonet1997caenorhabditiselegansrab3 pages 10-11)

### Relationship to RAB-27 and rabphilin

RAB-3 and RAB-27 have partially overlapping presynaptic functions and are both regulated by AEX-3. Double-mutant phenotypes are stronger than either single-mutant phenotype, explaining why loss of AEX-3 is more severe than loss of RAB-3 alone. Their localization overlaps only partly, consistent with distinct or partially overlapping exocytic-vesicle populations. (mahoney2006regulationofsynaptic pages 1-2, mahoney2006regulationofsynaptic pages 6-7)

A crucial species-specific caveat concerns RBF-1/rabphilin. In worms, RBF-1 behaves principally as a RAB-27 effector, remains localized in the absence of RAB-3, and can potentiate SNARE function independently of RAB-3. It is therefore inappropriate to annotate worm RBF-1 as a simple obligatory RAB-3 effector solely from vertebrate models. (mahoney2006regulationofsynaptic pages 6-7, staunton2001rabphilinpotentiatessoluble pages 9-9)

### Synaptotagmin and Rab3 cycling

A 2015 *C. elegans* study proposed that synaptotagmin-1 couples vesicle exocytosis to the Rab3 cycle: without Ca²⁺, synaptotagmin binds Rab3-GAP and inhibits Rab3 GTP hydrolysis; Ca²⁺ then releases the GAP and promotes RAB-3 membrane dissociation, facilitating repetitive release. This supplies a plausible activity-dependent reset mechanism, but the retrieved excerpt did not expose all primary numerical data, so it should be treated as a supported pathway model rather than the central basis of the Q94986 annotation. (cheng2015synaptotagmin1directs pages 18-19)

## 6. Recent developments and applications, 2023–2024

### Current mechanistic context

Recent work has emphasized the hierarchical and partly redundant organization of the *C. elegans* active zone. The key RAB-3 mechanism—vesicular GTP–RAB-3 engaging UNC-10/RIM and controlling vesicle positioning—remains based primarily on foundational experiments rather than having been overturned by 2023–2024 studies. The modern view places this module within a larger active-zone system containing UNC-10/RIM, UNC-13, CLA-1/Clarinet, RIM-binding protein, calcium channels, liprins, and motors.

### RAB-3 as a quantitative synapse reporter

The most important 2024 application is Majeed et al.’s WormPsyQi platform, published in *eLife* in January 2024 ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.91775)). The authors assembled **30 transgenic strains** labeling chemical or electrical synapses with CLA-1, RAB-3, GRASP, or innexin reporters and developed an automated pipeline for quantifying fluorescent synaptic puncta. Measured properties included punctum number, size/volume, intensity, density, and spatial distribution across nervous-system regions, developmental stages, and sexes. (majeed2024toolkitsfordetailed pages 1-2, majeed2024toolkitsfordetailed pages 3-5)

Specific RAB-3 reporters included srg-13p::RAB-3::BFP in PHA and srg-8p::RAB-3::GFP in ASK neurons. The software reproduced known anatomical distributions in PHA, AIB, and ASK and permitted probability-density estimates that separated stereotyped, high-probability synaptic positions from variable positions. In ASK, synapse volume scaled with synapse addition from the first larval stage to adulthood while spatial probability peaks remained stereotyped. (majeed2024toolkitsfordetailed pages 8-9, majeed2024toolkitsfordetailed pages 21-22)

Across five pharyngeal/enteric neuron classes—MC, M3, I2, M4, and NSM—CLA-1 or RAB-3 puncta and neurite length were quantified manually and automatically. MC and I2 maintained approximately constant punctum counts, M3 increased puncta modestly relative to pharyngeal elongation, and M4 and NSM added puncta approximately in proportion to neurite growth. Automated and manual analyses recovered similar trends. (majeed2024toolkitsfordetailed pages 19-21)

This is a real-world implementation of RAB-3 as a **presynaptic-specialization proxy** for developmental studies, genetic screens, synapse mapping, and high-throughput phenotyping. It does not change RAB-3’s molecular annotation.

### Methodological limitations

A fluorescent RAB-3 punctum reports one vesicle-associated component, not necessarily a complete or functional synapse. Multicopy overexpression can create trafficking or localization artifacts and selection bias. Dense early-larval synapses and weak or absent cytoplasmic markers can cause automated undercounting, and reporter-only measurements may not resolve neuron-to-neuron from neuromuscular contacts in dense regions. Accordingly, reporter punctum number should not automatically be equated with synapse number, endogenous RAB-3 abundance, or release competence. (majeed2024toolkitsfordetailed pages 21-22, majeed2024toolkitsfordetailed pages 19-21)

## 7. Expert interpretation and annotation recommendation

### Recommended primary-function annotation

> **RAB-3 is a prenylated Rab-family small GTPase associated with neuronal synaptic vesicles. In its GTP-bound state, it engages UNC-10/RIM and related presynaptic machinery to recruit, retain, and spatially organize vesicles near active zones, thereby promoting efficient neurotransmitter release. AEX-3 drives activation and correct presynaptic localization; GTP hydrolysis and membrane dissociation reset the cycle.**

### Recommended biological-process terms

The evidence supports annotation to:

- synaptic-vesicle trafficking and localization;
- synaptic-vesicle clustering/recruitment near active zones;
- regulation of synaptic-vesicle exocytosis;
- regulation of neurotransmitter secretion;
- presynaptic active-zone organization;
- small-GTPase-mediated signal transduction; and
- axonal transport of synaptic-vesicle precursors, with UNC-104/SYD-2/UNC-10.

### Recommended localization terms

The best-supported localizations are:

- synaptic-vesicle membrane/cytoplasmic vesicle surface;
- presynaptic terminal;
- synapse-rich axonal regions; and
- vesicles positioned near the active-zone/presynaptic density.

### Confidence and unresolved issues

Confidence is **high** for identity, Rab-family molecular-switch function, synaptic-vesicle localization, AEX-3 regulation, UNC-10/RIM binding, active-zone vesicle organization, and facilitation of transmitter release. The exact division among recruitment, tethering, docking, priming, and fusion remains less sharply resolved because RAB-3 loss redistributes vesicles broadly and produces partial rather than complete release failure. The specific worm GAP responsible for all phases of Q94986 cycling and the full activity-dependent recycling mechanism remain less firmly defined in the retrieved evidence. Finally, RAB-27 overlap means that mild *rab-3* phenotypes should not be interpreted as evidence of dispensability at the molecular level.
