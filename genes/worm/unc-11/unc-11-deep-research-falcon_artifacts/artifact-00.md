(pqac-00000001, pqac-00000009) | Direct worm molecular evidence; disorder supported by worm-protein biochemistry | Nonet et al., 1999; Wang et al., 2026 |
| Cellular and tissue localization | UNC-11 is predominantly neuronal and enriched at presynaptic regions, with additional signal in neuronal somata and lower expression outside the nervous system. | Antibody staining was punctate in the nerve ring and dorsal and ventral nerve cords, overlapping synaptotagmin-positive synaptic regions but appearing more diffuse; null-like alleles eliminated this staining. (pqac-00000001, pqac-00000004) | Direct worm immunolocalization | Nonet et al., 1999 |
| Clathrin-associated assembly | UNC-11 functions as a monomeric clathrin-associated assembly adaptor during synaptic membrane retrieval. | The UNC-11 C-terminal region promoted clathrin assembly in vitro. Endocytosis still occurred in null mutants, indicating that UNC-11 controls coat geometry and cargo composition rather than being absolutely required for membrane internalization. (pqac-00000001, pqac-00000003) | Biochemical assay with worm protein plus direct worm ultrastructure | Nonet et al., 1999 |
| Membrane and lipid recognition | The ANTH domain is expected to recruit UNC-11 to PI(4,5)P₂-rich membranes; full-length UNC-11 can couple such membranes to actin. | Full-length protein associated with 50-nm liposomes containing 2% PIP₂ and bridged them to F-actin, whereas phosphatidylcholine-only liposomes showed minimal association. Assignment of the canonical ANTH lipid-binding mechanism is also supported by AP180-family conservation. (pqac-00000006) | Biochemical evidence with worm protein; detailed binding mode partly family inference | Wang et al., 2026 |
| Synaptobrevin sorting | UNC-11 promotes incorporation and retention of synaptobrevin/VAMP on synaptic vesicles. | Null mutants had reduced vesicular synaptobrevin and diffuse or plasma-membrane-associated GFP–synaptobrevin, whereas localization of other tested vesicle proteins was comparatively preserved. (pqac-00000003, pqac-00000004) | Direct worm genetics, imaging, and vesicle analysis | Nonet et al., 1999 |
| Synaptic-vesicle size control | UNC-11 constrains vesicle diameter during recycling; it is not itself an enzyme or transporter. | Wild-type vesicles averaged **29.5 ± 5.4 nm**, versus **39.1 ± 7.3 nm** in *e47* and **40.4 ± 8.0 nm** in *q358*—about 33% greater diameter and 77% greater membrane area. A later study measured **32.1 ± 0.2 nm** in wild type and **40.2 ± 0.2 nm** in null mutants. (pqac-00000003, pqac-00000007) | Direct worm electron microscopy | Nonet et al., 1999; Wang et al., 2026 |
| Actin coupling and condensates | The disordered assembly domain concentrates actin monomers, promotes filament formation, and couples cortical actin to PIP₂-rich membranes; this activity helps set vesicle size. | Full-length UNC-11 and its isolated assembly domain formed salt-sensitive, fusing, FRAP-recovering condensates that recruited G-actin and generated F-actin within minutes. Replacing the domain with Lifeact restored vesicle diameter to **35.0 ± 0.3 nm**, miniature-event frequency to **37 ± 5 Hz**, and amplitude to **26 ± 2 pA**. (pqac-00000006, pqac-00000008) | Biochemical evidence with worm protein plus direct worm domain-rescue experiments | Wang et al., 2026 |
| Neurotransmission | UNC-11 is required for efficient evoked and spontaneous synaptic transmission; enlarged vesicles increase quantal amplitude even as null mutants release far less frequently. | Null alleles produced evoked EPSCs of **0.19–0.20 nA** versus **3.24 ± 0.20 nA** in wild type and miniature frequencies of **6–11 Hz** versus **53 ± 3 Hz**, while amplitudes rose to **30–31 pA** from **22 ± 2 pA**. Full-length neuronal UNC-11 rescued these defects. (pqac-00000002, pqac-00000009) | Direct worm electrophysiology and rescue | Wang et al., 2026 |
| Complexin relationship | UNC-11-dependent vesicle geometry enables curvature-sensitive complexin clamping of spontaneous fusion; complexin remains necessary for evoked release. | Removing complexin increased spontaneous frequency in wild type but not at UNC-11ΔAD synapses. Vesicle-tethered CPX-1::RAB-3 restored inhibition to **24 ± 6 Hz**, whereas untethered CPX-1 or a tether lacking its SNARE-binding helix did not. (pqac-00000007) | Direct worm genetics, electrophysiology, and engineered rescue | Wang et al., 2026 |
| ATG-9 sorting and presynaptic autophagy extension | UNC-11/AP180 also participates in sorting the autophagy membrane protein ATG-9 at synapses, linking endocytic organization to activity-dependent presynaptic autophagy. | *unc-11(e47)* caused abnormal subsynaptic ATG-9 foci that phenocopied *cla-1* defects; genetic interactions support an AP180/AP-2-dependent step in budding ATG-9 carriers from endocytic intermediates. Direct UNC-11 localization to a periactive zone or a direct autophagic-flux measurement in *unc-11* mutants was not established. (pqac-00000012, pqac-00000015, pqac-00000016) | Direct worm genetic and imaging evidence for ATG-9 sorting; pathway model partly inferential | Xuan et al., 2023 |
