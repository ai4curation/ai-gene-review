Gene Description

unc-11 encodes the Caenorhabditis elegans orthologue of AP180 (PICALM/SNAP91 family), a monomeric clathrin assembly protein expressed at high levels throughout the nervous system and at lower levels in coelomocytes, vulval uv1 cells and intestine. Its conserved N-terminal ANTH domain binds phosphatidylinositol-4,5-bisphosphate on the cytoplasmic face of the presynaptic membrane and engages the R-SNARE synaptobrevin, while the divergent, disordered C-terminal assembly domain binds clathrin heavy chain, promotes clathrin lattice assembly and couples the coat to actin. Through these activities UNC-11 sorts synaptobrevin into regenerating synaptic vesicles and constrains the size of the budded vesicle: null mutants are viable but uncoordinated, synaptobrevin is mislocalised away from synaptic vesicles, membrane retrieval continues but vesicles are about 33% larger in diameter, and neurotransmitter release is greatly reduced. UNC-11 also mediates clathrin-dependent endocytosis of the postsynaptic AMPA-type glutamate receptor GLR-1, coupling ubiquitin-dependent receptor removal to synaptic strength.

Functional annotation report: C. elegans unc-11 / UNC-11 (UniProt Q9XZI6)

Executive conclusion

The requested identity is verified. Caenorhabditis elegans unc-11—the locus historically assigned to ORF C32E8.10 and protein Q9XZI6—encodes the worm ortholog of neuronal AP180, a member of the PICALM/SNAP91 clathrin-adaptor family. Genetic rescue with an 8.8-kb genomic fragment and identification of five independent unc-11 mutations in its sole predicted ORF established the locus experimentally. UNC-11 shares 44.7% sequence identity with mouse AP180 and 41.4% with human CALM/PICALM, with strongest conservation in its N-terminal region. Thus, this report concerns the correct worm gene rather than a similarly named gene from another organism. (nonet1999unc11acaenorhabditis pages 3-6)

UNC-11 is not an enzyme or transporter. It is a predominantly neuronal, membrane-associated assembly adaptor/scaffold that operates at presynaptic membrane-retrieval sites. Its central functions are to organize clathrin-associated synaptic-vesicle formation, recruit selected cargo—most clearly synaptobrevin/VAMP—couple PI(4,5)P₂-rich membranes to actin, and constrain the diameter and molecular composition of recycled vesicles. Loss of UNC-11 does not abolish membrane endocytosis, but it produces abnormally large, compositionally altered vesicles and profoundly inefficient neurotransmission. (nonet1999unc11acaenorhabditis pages 12-14, nonet1999unc11acaenorhabditis pages 1-2, wang2026endocyticproteinap180 pages 12-15)

1. Identity, family, and domain architecture

The locus contains nine coding exons and generates six reported splice classes, UNC-11A–F. All share the conserved N-terminal region encoded by exons 1–4, while alternative use of downstream exons changes the C-terminal region and the number of NPF motifs. At least three products of approximately 60, 65, and 75 kDa were detected by immunoblotting. The largest originally predicted isoform was 588 amino acids. (nonet1999unc11acaenorhabditis pages 6-8, nonet1999unc11acaenorhabditis pages 3-6)

Modern functional mapping divides the experimentally studied protein into an N-terminal ANTH domain, residues 1–304, and a largely intrinsically disordered assembly domain (AD), residues 305–546. This agrees with the supplied InterPro assignments—ANTH, ENTH/VHS superfamily, and AP180-like—and with membership in the AP180/PICALM/SNAP91 family. The older sequence analysis identified a strongly conserved approximately 290-residue N-terminal region, 62.8% identical to mouse AP180, and a more divergent C terminus, 27.1% identical. (nonet1999unc11acaenorhabditis pages 3-6, wang2026endocyticproteinap180 pages 2-4)

The ANTH/ENTH terminology requires care: UNC-11 is functionally and evolutionarily an AP180-type ANTH-domain protein. Broader InterPro ENTH/VHS labels describe structural-superfamily relationships and should not be interpreted as evidence that UNC-11 is a canonical epsin.

2. Primary molecular function

2.1 Clathrin-associated vesicle assembly

UNC-11’s C-terminal region promotes clathrin assembly in vitro, directly supporting its classification as a clathrin assembly adaptor. Ultrastructurally, however, null mutants still contain synaptic vesicles and recognizable neuromuscular synapses. UNC-11 is therefore not absolutely required to internalize membrane; instead, it governs the geometry and cargo composition of vesicles generated during recycling. Alternative or partially redundant internalization routes likely remain active in the null background. (nonet1999unc11acaenorhabditis pages 6-8, nonet1999unc11acaenorhabditis pages 12-14)

The best-supported mechanistic model is that UNC-11 is recruited to phosphoinositide-rich presynaptic membrane through its ANTH domain, while its disordered assembly region organizes coat and cytoskeletal components. Direct biochemical work with worm protein showed that full-length UNC-11 associated with 50-nm liposomes containing 2% PI(4,5)P₂, 25% phosphatidylserine, and 71% phosphatidylcholine, whereas phosphatidylcholine-only liposomes showed little association. The canonical atomic details of ANTH–PI(4,5)P₂ recognition derive partly from AP180-family conservation, but membrane selectivity and membrane-to-actin bridging have now been demonstrated with UNC-11 itself. (wang2026endocyticproteinap180 pages 12-15)

2.2 Synaptic-vesicle cargo sorting

The strongest cargo-specific evidence concerns synaptobrevin, the vesicular R-SNARE. In unc-11 mutants, synaptobrevin was depleted from isolated synaptic vesicles and GFP–synaptobrevin became diffuse or plasma-membrane-associated instead of remaining concentrated in synaptic vesicles. Other vesicle proteins tested in the foundational study were much less affected. UNC-11 therefore performs selective cargo capture or retention, rather than indiscriminately controlling all vesicular proteins. (nonet1999unc11acaenorhabditis pages 12-14, nonet1999unc11acaenorhabditis pages 1-2)

Although AP180-family ANTH domains are broadly understood to interact with VAMP/synaptobrevin, the most secure worm-specific annotation is phenotypic and cell biological: UNC-11 is required for efficient synaptobrevin incorporation into recycled vesicles. A direct binding constant for purified UNC-11 and worm synaptobrevin was not established in the evidence reviewed here.

2.3 Vesicle-size control through actin

Electron microscopy originally showed average synaptic-vesicle diameters of 29.5 ± 5.4 nm in wild type, compared with 39.1 ± 7.3 nm in unc-11(e47) and 40.4 ± 8.0 nm in unc-11(q358). This is approximately a 33% increase in diameter and 77% increase in membrane area. The N-terminal in-frame-deletion allele n2954 produced both normal-sized and enlarged populations, consistent with partial domain function. (nonet1999unc11acaenorhabditis pages 12-14)

A recent mechanistic study independently measured 32.1 ± 0.2 nm in wild type and 40.2 ± 0.2 nm in null mutants; deletion of the assembly domain yielded 40.4 ± 0.2 nm vesicles. The difference in absolute wild-type values likely reflects experimental or analytical differences, while both studies agree on a robust enlargement phenotype. (wang2026endocyticproteinap180 pages 7-10)

The newer work identifies actin coupling as a major mechanism. Full-length UNC-11 and its isolated assembly domain formed salt-sensitive, fusing, FRAP-recovering condensates in vitro. These condensates enriched G-actin, supported appearance of F-actin within minutes, and associated with assembled filaments. Full-length UNC-11 additionally bridged F-actin to PI(4,5)P₂-containing liposomes. (wang2026endocyticproteinap180 pages 12-15, wang2026endocyticproteinap180 pages 10-12)

Domain replacement experiments provide causal support rather than merely correlation. Substitution with the actin-interacting HIPR-1 or epsin C-terminal regions reduced vesicle diameters to 35.6 ± 0.5 nm and 34.7 ± 0.4 nm, respectively. Removing HIPR-1’s THATCH actin-binding module impaired rescue, producing 39.8 ± 0.3 nm vesicles. Addition of the 17-residue Lifeact motif to UNC-11ΔAD restored diameter to 35.0 ± 0.3 nm and improved miniature-event frequency and amplitude to 37 ± 5 Hz and 26 ± 2 pA. These experiments indicate that actin interaction is a central and partly substitutable activity of the assembly domain. (wang2026endocyticproteinap180 pages 10-12)

3. Cellular and anatomical localization

UNC-11 is expressed most strongly in the nervous system, with lower expression outside it. Antibody staining showed punctate enrichment in the nerve ring and dorsal and ventral nerve cords, overlapping synaptotagmin-positive synaptic regions, although UNC-11 staining was more diffuse. Protein was also observed in neuronal cell bodies. Severe alleles eliminated or markedly reduced this staining, supporting antibody specificity. (nonet1999unc11acaenorhabditis pages 6-8, nonet1999unc11acaenorhabditis pages 1-2)

The functional site is therefore the cytoplasmic face of presynaptic membranes and associated endocytic/recycling structures, rather than the extracellular space or vesicle lumen. The available evidence supports presynaptic enrichment, but does not justify labeling UNC-11 as an exclusive active-zone protein. Recent work sometimes uses UNC-11 as an endocytic-zone reference, yet the 2023 ATG-9 study did not itself directly prove a precise periactive-zone localization for UNC-11. (xuan2023theactivezone pages 15-17, xuan2023theactivezone pages 8-10)

4. Biological pathways and physiological consequences

Synaptic-vesicle exo–endocytic cycle

UNC-11 acts after exocytosis during clathrin-associated membrane and cargo retrieval, helping to regenerate correctly sized, fusion-competent vesicles. Null mutants remain viable but display coiling, backward jerking, and uncoordinated locomotion, consistent with broad defects in neuronal transmission. Multiple neurotransmitter systems are impaired, although this is best interpreted as a consequence of defective vesicle recycling rather than direct action in transmitter synthesis. (nonet1999unc11acaenorhabditis pages 2-3)

Electrophysiology quantifies the severity. Two null alleles produced evoked EPSCs of only 0.20 ± 0.04 nA and 0.19 ± 0.04 nA, versus 3.24 ± 0.20 nA in wild type. Spontaneous frequencies fell to 6 ± 1 and 11 ± 3 Hz, versus 53 ± 3 Hz. In contrast, spontaneous amplitudes increased from 22 ± 2 pA to 31 ± 2 and 30 ± 2 pA, consistent with enlarged vesicles carrying larger quanta. Pan-neuronal full-length UNC-11 restored evoked current to 3.1 ± 0.2 nA, frequency to 51 ± 6 Hz, and amplitude to 23 ± 1 pA. (wang2026endocyticproteinap180 pages 2-4)

Assembly-domain deletion separated recycling efficiency from size control. UNC-11ΔAD increased evoked current to 4.3 ± 0.2 nA and spontaneous frequency to 75 ± 5 Hz, yet failed to normalize quantal amplitude (37 ± 2 pA) or vesicle diameter. Thus, the ANTH-containing fragment can support substantial release, whereas the assembly domain is specifically required to impose normal vesicle morphology and release regulation. (wang2026endocyticproteinap180 pages 2-4)

Vesicle curvature and complexin

The latest mechanistic interpretation connects UNC-11-dependent vesicle size to complexin-mediated fusion clamping. Complexin loss increased spontaneous release in wild type but had little additional effect at UNC-11ΔAD synapses, under either 1 or 0.25 mM extracellular Ca²⁺, while evoked responses still required complexin. Tethering CPX-1 directly to vesicles through RAB-3 restored spontaneous inhibition to 24 ± 6 Hz; untethered CPX-1 and a tether lacking the SNARE-binding helix did not. The authors infer that low curvature on enlarged vesicles reduces normal complexin recruitment or clamping, linking endocytic morphology to subsequent exocytic behavior. (wang2026endocyticproteinap180 pages 7-10)

This is an important conceptual advance: UNC-11 does not merely replenish vesicles. By setting their curvature, it determines how effectively curvature-sensitive release regulators control those vesicles in the next cycle.

ATG-9 sorting and presynaptic autophagy

A 2023 study broadened UNC-11’s cargo repertoire. unc-11(e47) animals developed abnormal subsynaptic ATG-9::GFP foci resembling those in cla-1 mutants, and genetic interactions placed AP180/UNC-11 and AP-2 in synaptic ATG-9 sorting. The proposed pathway has adaptor complexes helping bud ATG-9 carriers from endocytic intermediates near synapses. Because ATG-9 delivery supports activity-dependent presynaptic autophagosome formation, UNC-11-mediated sorting may couple endocytic organization to presynaptic proteostasis. (xuan2023theactivezone pages 11-13, xuan2023theactivezone pages 10-11, xuan2023theactivezone pages 17-18)

This extension should be stated conservatively. The study directly established an UNC-11-dependent ATG-9 localization phenotype, but did not directly quantify autophagic flux in unc-11 mutants or demonstrate that UNC-11 itself occupies a sharply defined periactive zone. CLA-1L’s role in activity-induced autophagy is stronger than the direct evidence for UNC-11 in that downstream process. (xuan2023theactivezone pages 15-17, xuan2023theactivezone pages 1-2)

5. Current applications and research relevance

UNC-11 is used primarily as an in vivo mechanistic model, not as a clinical target or industrial protein. Its principal applications are:

  1. Dissecting synaptic-vesicle biogenesis: viable null worms permit electron microscopy, cargo imaging, behavioral analysis, and electrophysiology in the same genetic system.
  2. Separating membrane retrieval from vesicle specification: continued endocytosis despite severe abnormalities shows that internalization and production of correctly sized, correctly composed vesicles are mechanistically separable. (nonet1999unc11acaenorhabditis pages 12-14)
  3. Domain engineering in vivo: single-copy neuronal rescue constructs, domain swaps, and artificial actin-binding motifs test which molecular activities are necessary and sufficient. (wang2026endocyticproteinap180 pages 10-12, wang2026endocyticproteinap180 pages 2-4)
  4. Connecting endocytosis to release regulation: the complexin experiments demonstrate that vesicle morphology generated during endocytosis affects the subsequent fusion cycle. (wang2026endocyticproteinap180 pages 7-10)
  5. Modeling conserved AP180/PICALM biology: sequence conservation makes UNC-11 useful for generating hypotheses about vertebrate AP180/SNAP91 and PICALM, but worm-specific results should not automatically be transferred to human proteins because neuronal AP180 and broadly expressed PICALM differ in expression, partners, and disease context. (nonet1999unc11acaenorhabditis pages 3-6)

6. Evidence assessment and expert interpretation

The functional annotation is unusually strong for a worm adaptor because it combines classical genetics, rescue, immunolocalization, ultrastructure, electrophysiology, and purified-protein reconstitution. The highest-confidence annotation is:

UNC-11 is a presynaptic AP180-family adaptor that organizes clathrin-associated recycling, sorts synaptobrevin and selected additional membrane cargo, and uses its ANTH and disordered assembly domains to couple phosphoinositide-rich membrane, coat assembly, and actin so that recycled synaptic vesicles acquire the correct size and release properties.

Several qualifications remain important. First, clathrin and AP-2 association is strongly supported by family biology and clathrin-assembly assays, but a complete quantitative interactome for native worm UNC-11 is unavailable. Second, altered cargo composition could contribute to the enlarged-vesicle and release phenotypes; the recent study explicitly notes that morphology-specific causality cannot be fully isolated without biochemical purification or proteomics of distinct vesicle populations. Third, condensate formation and actin nucleation were observed in reconstituted systems; their precise material state and concentration dependence in living presynaptic terminals remain to be established. (wang2026endocyticproteinap180 pages 12-15)

7. Recent-literature perspective

The targeted 2023–2024 literature is limited: UNC-11 was not the principal subject of a major dedicated paper in that interval. The most informative 2023 result extended UNC-11 function to synaptic ATG-9 sorting. The most detailed modern mechanistic study is instead the February 2026 PLOS Biology paper, which supplies direct evidence for assembly-domain condensates, actin recruitment, domain-swap rescue, and the curvature–complexin relationship. These newer findings refine rather than overturn the foundational 1999 model. (wang2026endocyticproteinap180 pages 12-15, wang2026endocyticproteinap180 pages 1-2, xuan2023theactivezone pages 1-2)

unc-11 — AP180 clathrin assembly protein (Q9XZI6) — curation notes

Identity and architecture

  • unc-11 (C32E8.10) is the sole C. elegans homologue of the neuronal clathrin assembly protein AP180 (PICALM/SNAP91 family), rescued by cosmid C32E8 PMID:10397769 PMID:10397769. The genome contains no second AP180 homologue PMID:10397769.
  • Multiple isoforms (a–h) share a conserved N-terminal ANTH domain (UniProt DOMAIN 29..161 ENTH-type) carrying the KKK phosphoinositide-binding motif, and diverge in a C-terminal low-complexity assembly domain PMID:10397769 PMID:10397769. The C-terminal region promotes clathrin assembly in vitro (cited as unpublished data) PMID:10397769.

Localisation

  • High in the nervous system, enriched at presynaptic sites and also in cell bodies; lower in coelomocytes, uv1 cells and intestine PMID:10397769 PMID:10397769. Axonal transport of UNC-11 is independent of UNC-104 PMID:10397769.
  • UniProt (curated from the same paper) records clathrin-coated pit, clathrin-coated vesicle and Golgi localisation [file:worm/unc-11/unc-11-uniprot.txt "Note=Colocalized with clathrin in the Golgi area."].

Function at the synapse

  • unc-11 null mutants are viable but uncoordinated (coiling, jerky backing) and defective in release of multiple neurotransmitters PMID:10397769.
  • Synaptobrevin is specifically mislocalised (diffuse, plasma-membrane-like) while other synaptic vesicle proteins are targeted normally PMID:10397769.
  • Synaptic membrane is still endocytosed, but vesicles are 33% larger in diameter (29.5 nm → 39–40 nm), i.e. AP180 sets vesicle size during clathrin coat assembly PMID:10397769 PMID:10397769 PMID:10397769.
  • unc-11; snb-1(hypomorph) double mutants arrest at L1, the only developmental-arrest phenotype reported PMID:10397769.
  • Deep research (file:worm/unc-11/unc-11-deep-research-falcon.md) adds a 2026 study showing the disordered assembly domain forms actin-recruiting condensates and bridges PI(4,5)P2 liposomes to F-actin, with actin coupling setting vesicle size [file:worm/unc-11/unc-11-deep-research-falcon.md "Full-length protein associated with 50-nm liposomes containing 2% PIP₂ and bridged them to F-actin, whereas phosphatidylcholine-only liposomes showed minimal association."], plus a role in sorting ATG-9 at synapses (Xuan et al. 2023).

Postsynaptic GLR-1 endocytosis

  • Ubiquitin overexpression lowers GLR-1 abundance at synapses and this requires unc-11 (abstract only) PMID:12123612.
  • UNC-11 is required for GLR-1 endocytosis; unc-11 mutations suppress the GLR-1 loss seen in rab-6.2 retromer-pathway mutants PMID:22213799 PMID:22213799.

Interactome rows

  • High-throughput Y2H/pooling interactome studies (PMID:14704431, 19123269, 19447967, 23549480) list EYA-1, ITSN-1, MAGU-1 and SORB-1 as binders; the ITSN-1 (intersectin SH3) and SORB-1 interactions are consistent with NPF/proline-motif accessory-protein contacts of AP180 family proteins, but none defines a distinct GO molecular function.

Curation decisions

  • Core: PI(4,5)P2 binding and clathrin heavy chain binding driving clathrin coat assembly at the presynaptic membrane, SNARE (synaptobrevin) cargo sorting into regenerated synaptic vesicles, and control of vesicle size; GLR-1 receptor endocytosis as a well-supported additional cargo context.
  • Non-core/downstream: locomotion, Golgi localisation, GLR-1 synaptic abundance regulation.
  • Over-annotated: post-embryonic development (only synthetic with snb-1); ubiquitin-dependent protein catabolic process (UNC-11 performs the internalisation step, degradation not shown).
  • Generic protein binding rows removed as uninformative; necroptotic process IGI left UNDECIDED (abstract-only, unc-11 not named).
