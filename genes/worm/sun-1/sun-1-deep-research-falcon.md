# Functional annotation report: *C. elegans sun-1* (UniProt Q20924)

## Executive conclusion

The target is correctly identified as ***Caenorhabditis elegans sun-1***, also called ***mtf-1/matefin*** and corresponding to ORF **F57B1.2**, not mammalian **SUN1** or a similarly named gene in another organism. Its product is a conserved **SUN-domain inner nuclear membrane protein** and the nuclear-side component of a **LINC (linker of nucleoskeleton and cytoskeleton) complex**. Its primary molecular function is therefore **structural and mechanotransductive, not enzymatic**: SUN-1 binds the outer-nuclear-membrane KASH protein ZYG-12 across the perinuclear space and couples nuclear structures—including meiotic chromosome pairing centers—to cytoplasmic dynein and microtubules. This bridge transmits force required for meiotic chromosome movement, homolog recognition and pairing, prevention of inappropriate nonhomologous synapsis, and embryonic centrosome–nucleus attachment. SUN-1 also serves as a regulated nuclear-envelope scaffold whose phosphorylation and oligomerization control the timing, distribution, and mechanical stability of these connections. (daryabeigi2016nuclearenveloperetention pages 1-7, minn2009sun1andzyg12 pages 8-9, penkner2009meioticchromosomehomology pages 1-2)

## 2. Molecular architecture and subcellular localization

SUN-1 is a **type-II integral inner nuclear membrane protein**. Its N-terminal region faces the nucleoplasm, a single functional transmembrane segment anchors it in the membrane, and the C-terminal region containing the SUN domain projects into the perinuclear lumen. This orientation allows the nucleoplasmic region to associate with lamin, chromatin-associated factors, and meiotic attachment machinery, while the luminal region contacts ZYG-12 in the outer nuclear membrane. (minn2009sun1andzyg12 pages 8-9)

SUN-1 is found at the nuclear envelope of germline nuclei and in early embryos. In mitotic or premeiotic germ cells it is comparatively rim-like; during the leptotene/zygotene “transition zone,” it concentrates into mobile patches at chromosome-attachment sites. SUN-1 and ZYG-12 colocalize and coprecipitate in vivo, and SUN-1 is required to recruit or retain ZYG-12 at the nuclear envelope. (minn2009sun1andzyg12 pages 8-9, penkner2009meioticchromosomehomology pages 1-2)

SUN-1 also interacts with the single worm lamin, LMN-1. Direct interaction has been detected in vitro, and lamin and SUN-1 show interdependent mitotic localization, although their interphase targeting is not absolutely codependent. This provides a nucleoskeletal anchor and helps distribute forces applied through the LINC bridge. (zuela2016matefinsun1phosphorylationon pages 12-13)

## 3. Primary function: a force-transmitting LINC-complex adaptor

SUN-1 is neither an enzyme nor a transporter and has no substrate or catalyzed reaction. Its primary function is to build and regulate a **trans-nuclear-envelope mechanical linkage**:

**Chromosome/pairing-center machinery → MJL-1 and SUN-1 in the inner membrane → ZYG-12 in the outer membrane → dynein/microtubules and centrosomes.**

SUN-1 and ZYG-12 form the core SUN/KASH bridge. ZYG-12’s cytoplasmic region associates with dynein and centrosomal isoforms of ZYG-12, while SUN-1 anchors the assembly to nucleoplasmic structures. This architecture enables cytoskeletal forces to move objects inside the nucleus or to anchor centrosomes outside it. (minn2009sun1andzyg12 pages 8-9, stewart2014themissinglinc pages 3-4)

The precise SUN–KASH interface in *C. elegans* may differ from a simplified generic model. Deletion mapping indicated that a luminal internal region of SUN-1 can bind the ZYG-12 tail and that the conserved SUN domain alone is not sufficient for this interaction. Thus, annotation should emphasize the experimentally established SUN-1–ZYG-12 LINC partnership rather than assume that all binding is mediated solely by the canonical SUN domain. (minn2009sun1andzyg12 pages 8-9)

## 4. Meiotic pathway and biological mechanism

### Pairing-center attachment and chromosome movement

Unlike many organisms that tether meiotic telomeres, *C. elegans* uses specialized subtelomeric **pairing centers**. HIM-8 binds the X-chromosome pairing center, whereas ZIM-1, ZIM-2, and ZIM-3 recognize autosomal pairing centers. These chromosome-bound proteins recruit CHK-2/PLK-2-regulated attachment machinery to SUN-1/ZYG-12 patches. Cytoplasmic dynein then moves the patches—and attached chromosome ends—along the nuclear envelope. (stewart2014themissinglinc pages 3-4, kim2023mjl1isa pages 1-2, kim2023mjl1isa pages 3-6)

Live imaging showed that SUN-1 aggregates move erratically, merge, and separate during leptotene/zygotene. Movement correlates with polarized chromatin organization and depends on an intact SUN/KASH bridge. In **sun-1(jf18)**, active aggregate movement is essentially absent; homolog alignment is lost, and synaptonemal complexes assemble prematurely between nonhomologous chromosomes. These findings establish force transmission, rather than simple static tethering, as a central SUN-1 function. (baudrimont2010leptotenezygotenechromosomemovement pages 1-2, penkner2007thenuclearenvelope pages 1-2)

A useful recent quantitative benchmark comes from 2023 measurements of GFP::HIM-8 pairing-center movement: average speed was **59.8 nm/s in wild type**, **22.7 nm/s in mjl-1 mutants**, and **18.7 nm/s in sun-1(jf18)**. The slow residual motion in the mutants was interpreted as diffusion rather than active motor-driven transport. (kim2023mjl1isa pages 2-3)

### Homolog pairing, synapsis, and recombination

SUN-1-dependent movement promotes the homology search and presynaptic alignment. It also helps enforce the rule that the synaptonemal complex assembles between homologs rather than unrelated chromosomes. The jf18 separation-of-function mutant retains SUN-1 at the envelope but fails to form normal ZYG-12 patches, lacks presynaptic homolog alignment, and undergoes precocious nonhomologous synapsis. By contrast, null/deletion alleles cause broader germline disorganization, demonstrating that SUN-1 also has functions beyond its meiosis-specific movement module. (penkner2007thenuclearenvelope pages 1-2)

Movement and recombination are reciprocally coordinated. Chromosome-axis integrity is needed to generate normal numbers of SUN-1 attachment plaques; abolishing synapsis slows SUN-1 aggregate motion; and double-strand-break or repair defects alter aggregate dynamics. SUN-1 should therefore be annotated as part of a regulatory-mechanical network coordinating pairing, synapsis, and interhomolog recombination—not as a recombination enzyme itself. (penkner2009meioticchromosomehomology pages 1-2, baudrimont2010leptotenezygotenechromosomemovement pages 1-2)

## 5. Regulation by phosphorylation and oligomerization

### Meiotic phosphorylation

Multiple residues in the nucleoplasmic N terminus are phosphorylated during early meiotic prophase. CHK-2-dependent phosphorylation appears during leptotene/zygotene, declines during pachytene, and is associated with SUN-1 patch formation and the movement-competent state. Phosphomimetic SUN-1 extends the transition-zone program and produces univalent chromosomes at diakinesis, showing that the modification state regulates both meiotic timing and productive pairing. (penkner2009meioticchromosomehomology pages 1-2)

SUN-1 phosphorylation also participates in a **meiotic surveillance system**. Incomplete synapsis or recombination prolongs phosphorylated SUN-1, chromosome movement, and the early-prophase state. This response is temporally restricted: zygotene and early-pachytene nuclei respond, whereas later nuclei do not readily reestablish SUN-1 and CHK-1 phosphorylation. S12 is one characterized regulatory site, and broader phosphosite-mutant analyses included T36 and other mapped residues. Phosphorylation correlates with patches and movement but is not simply a consequence of movement or successful pairing. (woglar2013matefinsun1phosphorylationis pages 13-14)

Because its phosphorylation is spatially restricted, **pSUN-1—especially pS8 or pS12—is widely used experimentally as a cytological marker of meiotic entry, transition-zone identity, and checkpoint extension**. This is an important research application, but marker status should not be confused with the protein’s primary mechanical function.

### Mitotic/embryonic phosphorylation

A separate CDK-1-dependent event at **Ser43** regulates SUN-1’s centrosomal localization and mitotic functions in embryos. Constitutive S43 phosphomimicry disrupts ZYG-12 and lamin organization, centrosome integrity, microtubule organization, and fertility. Reported quantitative phenotypes include **39.8% offspring viability** for S43E in the presence of endogenous SUN-1 but only **0.6% without endogenous SUN-1**, a **39% reduction in egg laying** in the null background, and **11–12 univalents rather than six bivalents** at diakinesis. Because these experiments used engineered phosphomutants, they strongly support regulatory importance but should not be interpreted as evidence that persistent physiological S43 phosphorylation normally occurs throughout these stages. (zuela2016matefinsun1phosphorylationon pages 8-12, zuela2016matefinsun1phosphorylationon pages 12-13)

### Oligomerization and envelope retention

SUN-1 forms dimers or higher-order assemblies in vivo. Its coiled-coil region promotes oligomerization and stable inner-membrane retention, particularly during late oogenesis when the bridge is exposed to asymmetric forces. Deletion of residues 158–235 makes SUN-1 force-sensitive and promotes premature loss from the nuclear envelope. Importantly, chromosome sorting and synapsis can still occur without this region, indicating that oligomerization is more important for **long-term retention and mechanical resilience** than for initial assembly of a functional meiotic LINC complex. (daryabeigi2016nuclearenveloperetention pages 1-7, daryabeigi2016nuclearenveloperetention pages 26-32)

## 6. Major 2023 development: MJL-1 defines the chromosome-to-SUN-1 connector

Kim and colleagues identified **MJL-1 (MAJIN-like-1)**, a small meiosis-specific inner-nuclear-membrane protein that bridges pairing centers to SUN-1. MJL-1 colocalizes with SUN-1 and pairing-center proteins, coimmunoprecipitates with SUN-1, and depends on SUN-1 for nuclear-envelope localization or stability. Loss of MJL-1 prevents SUN-1 from concentrating with pairing-center proteins, eliminates active chromosome motion, causes extensive nonhomologous synapsis, and severely impairs homolog pairing. (kim2023mjl1isa pages 2-3, kim2023mjl1isa pages 1-2, kim2023mjl1isa pages 3-6)

The study also identified **sun-1(ie143), Y128F**, a meiosis-selective allele that removes MJL-1 from the nuclear envelope while preserving essential mitotic SUN-1 functions. This places the SUN-1–MJL-1 interaction near their transmembrane or membrane-adjacent regions. CHK-2 and PLK-2 activity is required for pairing-center proteins to associate with the MJL-1/SUN-1 module, although MJL-1–SUN-1 association itself appears less dependent on PLK-2. (kim2023mjl1isa pages 2-3, kim2023mjl1isa pages 3-6)

MJL-1-null animals produced **<1% viable self-progeny**, with **29–33% males** among survivors, and typically showed **10–12 DAPI bodies** at diakinesis rather than six crossover-linked bivalents. These findings reinforce the biological consequence of disrupting the SUN-1-centered attachment module: nondisjunction, failed crossover formation, and embryonic inviability. (kim2023mjl1isa pages 2-3)

## 7. Embryonic centrosome attachment and pronuclear function

Maternally supplied SUN-1 has an essential postmeiotic role in attaching centrosomes to the nuclear envelope in early embryos. SUN-1 recruits ZYG-12 outer-membrane isoforms, which interact with centrosomal ZYG-12 and dynein-associated machinery. Loss or inadequate retention of SUN-1 causes centrosome detachment, defective nuclear/centrosomal organization, and embryonic lethality. (minn2009sun1andzyg12 pages 8-9, daryabeigi2016nuclearenveloperetention pages 41-52)

Photoconversion experiments showed that SUN-1 detected in four-cell embryos derived from protein already present in the −1 oocyte, demonstrating a maternal supply during this developmental interval. This explains why defects in late-oocyte SUN-1 retention have immediate embryonic effects. (daryabeigi2016nuclearenveloperetention pages 41-52)

The broader LINC machinery contributes to pronuclear migration, but the most direct SUN-1 evidence supports ZYG-12 recruitment and centrosome–nucleus attachment. Pronuclear-migration defects should therefore be described as a downstream consequence of impaired LINC/centrosome coupling rather than a separate biochemical activity of SUN-1. (stewart2014themissinglinc pages 3-4)

## 8. Recent mechanobiology: force transmission can become damaging

A 2023 Science Advances study extended SUN-1’s annotation from chromosome movement to **oocyte nuclear mechanoprotection and mechanical failure**. When LMN-1 was depleted, SUN-1 and ZYG-12 patches remained abnormally mobile into late meiotic prophase and polarized into an asymmetric cap before nuclear collapse. Nuclear-envelope deformation and shrinkage preceded chromosome-volume reduction, arguing that collapse arose from force-induced envelope destabilization rather than apoptosis or chromosome compaction. (liu2023acooperativenetwork pages 2-3, liu2023acooperativenetwork pages 3-6)

Depleting SUN-1 or ZYG-12 rescued collapse, as did depletion of dynein/dynactin components DLC-1, DNC-1, LIS-1, DHC-1, or DLI-1. Thus, SUN-1 is not merely associated with collapsed nuclei; it is required to transmit the damaging asymmetric force when the lamin network is compromised. Pairing-center proteins and MJL-1 were dispensable for this late-prophase collapse, indicating that LINC forces can act independently of the canonical meiotic pairing-center attachment. (liu2023acooperativenetwork pages 2-3, liu2023acooperativenetwork pages 3-6)

This result refines rather than replaces the primary annotation: SUN-1 is a **general force-bearing LINC element**, with outputs determined by developmental stage and its nuclear partner. During early meiosis, it moves chromosome ends; later, it contributes to nuclear positioning and can stress an inadequately supported envelope.

## 9. Current applications and real-world relevance

SUN-1 is chiefly used in basic and translational model-system research rather than in a clinical or industrial implementation:

- **Live reporter of chromosome-end mechanics:** SUN-1::GFP or SUN-1::mRuby patches permit quantitative tracking of meiotic chromosome attachment sites and LINC-complex mobility. (liu2023acooperativenetwork pages 3-6, baudrimont2010leptotenezygotenechromosomemovement pages 1-2)
- **Meiotic-stage/checkpoint marker:** phospho-SUN-1 staining is used to delineate leptotene/zygotene and detect prolonged early-prophase surveillance.
- **In vivo mechanotransduction model:** conditional depletion of SUN-1, ZYG-12, lamin, and dynein components provides a genetically tractable system for studying how cytoskeletal forces cross the nuclear envelope. (liu2023acooperativenetwork pages 2-3, liu2023acooperativenetwork pages 3-6)
- **Nuclear-envelope disease modeling:** the worm system separates force generation from nuclear resistance and has informed mechanistic models relevant to laminopathies, dystonia/torsin biology, infertility, and SUN/KASH-associated human disease. Direct therapeutic extrapolation remains premature because Q20924 is a worm protein and mammalian LINC complexes use additional paralogs and tissue-specific adaptors.
- **Reproductive toxicology and meiosis research:** pSUN-1 localization is used as a readout of meiotic progression in studies of germline stress. This is a research assay, not a validated clinical biomarker.

## 10. Evidence-weighted functional annotation

### Recommended primary annotation

> **SUN-1 is a type-II inner nuclear membrane SUN-domain protein that forms a LINC complex with the KASH protein ZYG-12. It anchors nuclear and meiotic chromosome-attachment machinery to cytoplasmic dynein/microtubules, transmitting force required for pairing-center-led chromosome movement, homolog pairing and accurate synapsis, and for centrosome–nucleus attachment in embryos. Its activity and mechanical stability are regulated by stage-specific phosphorylation, interaction with MJL-1 and lamin, and coiled-coil-dependent oligomerization.**

### Confidence by function

- **Very high confidence:** inner nuclear membrane localization; SUN-domain topology; ZYG-12 LINC partnership; meiotic pairing/movement function; embryonic centrosome attachment.
- **High confidence:** lamin association; phosphorylation-dependent regulation; oligomerization-dependent retention; MJL-1-mediated pairing-center linkage; dynein-dependent force transmission.
- **Moderate or context-dependent:** direct contacts between SUN-1 and individual HIM-8/ZIM proteins, because MJL-1 and kinase-regulated adaptors mediate at least part of this linkage; a generalized role in apoptosis, because SUN-1 requirements differ between damage-induced and physiological germ-cell death.
- **Not supported:** catalytic activity, transporter activity, or a defined chemical substrate.

## Overall assessment of recent literature

The most important recent advance is not a change in SUN-1’s core annotation but a sharper definition of its context-specific connections. The 2023 MJL-1 study supplied the missing inner-envelope adaptor between pairing-center proteins and SUN-1, while the 2023 nuclear-mechanics study showed that SUN-1/ZYG-12 continues to bear dynein-generated forces later in oogenesis and can damage lamin-deficient nuclei. The 2024 literature located in this search mainly used SUN-1 or phospho-SUN-1 as a meiotic-state readout or discussed it within conserved LINC mechanisms; it did not provide a comparably strong revision of Q20924’s direct molecular function. Consequently, the best current interpretation remains that SUN-1 is a regulated, force-transmitting nuclear-envelope scaffold whose principal precise roles are meiotic chromosome coupling and embryonic centrosome–nucleus attachment.
