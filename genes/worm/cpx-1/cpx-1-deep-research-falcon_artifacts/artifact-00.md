| Identity and family | *C. elegans* **cpx-1** encodes CPX-1, one of two predicted worm complexins and the closer ortholog of fly and mammalian complexins; its architecture matches the complexin/synaphin family. | Sequence comparison and mutant/rescue studies identified canonical N-terminal, accessory-helix, central-helix and C-terminal regions. | CPX-1 has 39% overall identity to mouse complexin-1; its central SNARE-binding helix is approximately 75% conserved. | High confidence for gene/protein identity and family. | Central helix and SNARE binding | The conserved central α-helix binds the assembled neuronal SNARE bundle between syntaxin and synaptobrevin and is required for essentially all CPX-1 functions; nevertheless, maximal-affinity binding is not strictly required for some evoked-release facilitation. | Domain deletions, K71A/Y72A SNARE-binding mutations and neuronal rescue at the worm neuromuscular junction. | Central-helix identity was reported as 78% to mouse Cplx1 and 91% to fly complexin; central-helix deletion failed to rescue evoked release. | High confidence for requirement of the helix; the exact fusion-control mechanism remains model-dependent. | Martin et al., 2011, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2010.12.014) (pqac-00000000, pqac-00000003, pqac-00000004) |

| Neuronal and subcellular localization | CPX-1 is a soluble cytoplasmic neuronal protein concentrated dynamically at presynaptic boutons and synaptic vesicles. | Spontaneous-release clamp | CPX-1 suppresses unregulated, largely calcium-independent synaptic-vesicle fusion. This inhibitory role requires the central helix plus C-terminal vesicle targeting. | Whole-cell recordings at cholinergic neuromuscular junctions, null mutants and domain-specific rescue. | Depending on assay, loss of CPX-1 increased spontaneous/tonic fusion about 2–20-fold; one study measured a 12-fold increase without external calcium, while another reported a threefold increase in tonic transmission. | High confidence for clamping; fold changes differ with preparation, calcium conditions and event definition. | Martin et al., 2011, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2010.12.014); Radoff et al., 2014, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.04553); Lottermoser & Dittman, 2023, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jmb.2022.167774) (pqac-00000002, pqac-00000004, pqac-00000005) |
| Evoked release and primed vesicles | CPX-1 also promotes stimulus-evoked release and maintains a docked/primed vesicle pool.
