# cpx-1 (UniProt Q9GUM7) — Complexin-1, C. elegans

## Identity
- Reviewed entry (Putative complexin-1), small cytosolic SNARE-binding protein with a central helix (coiled coil 40-71) and C-terminal domain; PANTHER PTHR16705 complexin family (from `cpx-1-uniprot.txt`). Of the two worm complexins, cpx-1 is closer to mouse/fly complexin and broadly expressed; cpx-2 is expressed in few neurons [PMID:21215634 "cpx-1 shares a higher degree of homology with the mouse (32% vs 19% identity) and fly (44% vs 28% identity) complexins (Figure 1A)."].

## Function: dual clamp/facilitator of synaptic vesicle fusion
- cpx-1 nulls are viable but slow (40% wild-type speed) and aldicarb-hypersensitive; neuronal CPX-1::GFP rescues [PMID:21215634 "cpx-1 mutants moved at significantly slower speeds (40 ± 2.2% of wild type), consistent with a nervous system defect (Figure 1D)."; "cpx-1 mutants paralyzed within 20 minutes of aldicarb exposure while wild-type animals required two hours to paralyze."].
- Electrophysiology: tonic release is 3-fold increased and evoked release 10-fold decreased without CPX-1 [PMID:21215634 "Loss of CPX-1 results in a 3-fold increase in the rate of tonic synaptic transmission at the neuromuscular junction, whereas stimulus-evoked SV fusion is decreased 10-fold."].
- The clamp requires SNARE binding and the C-terminal domain, whereas evoked release is partially rescued by both mutants [PMID:21215634 "Rescue of cpx-1 with the SNARE-binding mutant KY/AA failed to rescue the tonic rate of fusion (Figure 6A&C)."; "the eEPSC was partially restored in both ΔCTD and KY/AA rescue animals, indicating that these regions contribute to but are not essential to CPX-1 function during stimulus-evoked SV fusion"].
- Over-expression does not inhibit transmission; nervous-system development and synapse number are normal in the mutant [PMID:21215634 "The number of cholinergic motor neurons in the ventral cord was identical between wild type and cpx-1 mutant animals"].
- Conclusion: CPX-1 suppresses activity-independent (tonic) fusion and promotes synchronous evoked fusion [PMID:21215634 "These observations suggest a dual role for CPX-1: suppressing SV exocytosis, driven by low levels of endogenous neural activity, while promoting synchronous fusion of SVs driven by a depolarizing stimulus."].

## Localization and expression
- Expressed in many head, ventral-cord and tail neurons including all cholinergic and GABAergic motor neurons [PMID:21215634 "Expression was observed in a large number of neurons in the head, ventral cord, and tail, including all cholinergic and GABAergic motor neurons in the ventral cord (Figure 2A&B)."].
- CPX-1::GFP is diffuse in the axon with enrichment at presynaptic terminals (RAB-3 colocalisation) [PMID:21215634 "CPX-1::GFP was diffusely expressed throughout the axon with some enrichment at presynaptic terminals based on colocalization with an SV marker mCherry::Rab-3 (Figure 2C)."].

## Curation notes
- PAINT rows come from the complexin family node PTN001047612 (SNARE binding, SNARE complex) and the neuronal complexin node PTN000422952 (synaptic vesicle exocytosis, regulation of SV fusion at the active zone, terminal bouton, modulation of synaptic transmission).
- "SNARE complex" (part_of) overstates membership: complexin binds the assembled four-helix bundle but does not contribute a helix.
- "terminal bouton" is a mammalian-shaped location; worm motor neurons form en passant synapses along the axon, so presynapse is the fitting term.
