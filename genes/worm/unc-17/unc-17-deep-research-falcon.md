# Functional annotation report: *C. elegans unc-17* / UNC-17 (UniProt P34711)

In *Caenorhabditis elegans*, **unc-17 encodes UNC-17, the vesicular acetylcholine transporter (VAChT)**—an integral synaptic-vesicle membrane protein of cholinergic neurons. ## 1. Identity, family, and molecular function

UNC-17 is explicitly identified in worm studies as **UNC-17/VAChT** and as an integral membrane component of neurotransmitter vesicles. Its expression is tightly associated with cholinergic neurons, and disruption produces the expected reduction in cholinergic transmission. These independent molecular, anatomical, and genetic criteria make the identification secure. (lickteig2001regulationofneurotransmitter pages 3-5, mathews2012geneticinteractionsbetween pages 2-3)

### Transported substrate

The substrate is **acetylcholine**. In the cholinergic pathway, CHA-1/ChAT synthesizes ACh from choline and acetyl-CoA in the neuron; UNC-17 then loads cytosolic ACh into synaptic vesicles. Calcium-triggered vesicle fusion releases the stored ACh, after which extracellular acetylcholinesterases hydrolyze it and choline can be recovered. The broadly similar phenotypes of *cha-1* synthesis mutants and *unc-17* transport mutants support the sequential pathway—synthesis followed by vesicular packaging. (duerr2021analysisofcaenorhabditis pages 6-7)

### Transport mechanism and domain interpretation

The supplied InterPro assignments—MFS, MFS domain/superfamily, and vesicular-transporter family—fit a VAChT. The accepted family-level model is **H⁺/ACh exchange driven by the vesicular proton electrochemical gradient**, which is established by the vesicular V-type ATPase. Proton efflux down that gradient powers ACh accumulation into the vesicle lumen. The MFS fold supplies the alternating-access transmembrane pathway.

For evidence grading, this mechanistic description is strongly conserved across VAChTs but is partly **family-level inference for P34711**: the recovered worm literature directly establishes vesicular ACh transport and cites point-mutant mapping of regions involved in substrate translocation, but did not expose a purified-P34711 transport stoichiometry or kinetic constants. Zhu et al.’s 2001 point-mutant study is the key worm structure–function source: [published November 2001, DOI](https://doi.org/10.1074/jbc.M103550200). (duerr2021analysisofcaenorhabditis pages 13-13)

The domain label “Tet-R_TetA/multi-R_MdtG-like” should therefore not be interpreted as evidence that UNC-17 transports tetracycline or functions in multidrug resistance. It reflects distant MFS structural similarity; genetics, expression, localization, and pathway evidence identify ACh as the biologically relevant substrate.

## 2. Cellular and subcellular localization

UNC-17 carries out its transport reaction in the **membrane of synaptic vesicles at presynaptic terminals**. Endogenous antibody staining is punctate at presynaptic varicosities rather than diffusely distributed, and UNC-17 is enriched in synapse-rich structures such as the nerve ring and dorsal and ventral nerve cords. In VC motor neurons it occurs at varicosities associated with vulval muscles. (lickteig2001regulationofneurotransmitter pages 7-9, lickteig2001regulationofneurotransmitter pages 2-3, mathews2012geneticinteractionsbetween pages 6-7)

A 2012 study provided complementary trafficking evidence. Bimolecular fluorescence complementation placed UNC-17 in close proximity to the small transmembrane protein SUP-1 in cholinergic synaptic vesicles. SUP-1’s transmembrane segment mediated this proximity, whereas its lumenal region promoted proper vesicle trafficking. The assay supports close association, although the authors appropriately cautioned that BiFC does not by itself prove a native functional complex. [Published December 2012, DOI](https://doi.org/10.1534/genetics.112.145771). (mathews2012geneticinteractionsbetween pages 6-7)

UNC-17 should consequently be distinguished from:

- **CHO-1**, a plasma-membrane high-affinity choline transporter that supplies precursor;
- **CHA-1/ChAT**, the cytosolic ACh-synthetic enzyme;
- ACh receptors, which detect released transmitter on target cells; and
- vesicle-fusion or recycling proteins, which release or regenerate the UNC-17-containing vesicles.

## 3. Cellular expression and cholinergic identity

A systematic adult-hermaphrodite map identified **52 UNC-17-positive neuron classes comprising 159 neurons**. This included major locomotor motor-neuron classes such as VA and VB, VC egg-laying motor neurons, sensory/interneuronal classes, and pharyngeal neurons. The map combined *unc-17* reporters with endogenous UNC-17 staining and other cholinergic-pathway markers. [Published December 2015, DOI](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.12432). (pereira2015acellularand pages 3-6)

Expression of UNC-17 and CHA-1 overlaps because the two genes are organized together in the cholinergic locus/operon. Their co-expression is therefore a strong definition of transmitter identity: the same cell can synthesize ACh and package it for release. The 2024 atlas reconfirmed this organization using endogenous CRISPR reporters rather than relying solely on multicopy transgenes. (wang2024aneurotransmitteratlas pages 3-5)

Expression is nevertheless regulated in a neuron-class-specific manner. For example, UNC-4 and its Groucho-family corepressor UNC-37 are required for normal UNC-17 abundance in selected DA, VA, VC, and SAB motor neurons. In one quantitative analysis, wild-type UNC-17 staining occurred in 72% of scored DA/VA neurons, 100% of SAB processes, and 98% of VC varicosities; values fell to 10%, 56%, and 3% in *unc-4(wd1)*, and to 5%, 88%, and 2% in *unc-37(e262)*. Persistence of UNC-18 in processes that had lost UNC-17 indicated selective loss of the cholinergic vesicle component rather than simple disappearance of the axon. [Published March 2001, DOI](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.21-06-02001.2001). (lickteig2001regulationofneurotransmitter pages 5-7, lickteig2001regulationofneurotransmitter pages 2-3)

## 4. Biological pathway and physiological role

UNC-17 occupies a precise presynaptic step:

**choline uptake → ACh synthesis by CHA-1 → UNC-17-dependent vesicle filling → Ca²⁺-triggered vesicle fusion → ACh-receptor activation → acetylcholinesterase-mediated termination.**

Its direct output is therefore **vesicular ACh content**, which affects quantal cholinergic signaling. At body-wall neuromuscular junctions this supports excitatory drive for locomotion; in pharyngeal and egg-laying circuits it supports cholinergic control of those behaviors. These organismal effects are downstream consequences of the same vesicle-loading reaction, not evidence for additional UNC-17 substrates.

### Mutant evidence

The classic *unc-17(e245)* mutant grows slowly, is severely uncoordinated during both crawling and swimming, and resists acetylcholinesterase inhibitors—phenotypes expected when vesicular ACh output is low. A dominant *sup-1(e995)* suppressor restored approximately **70% of wild-type function** in swimming and aldicarb-response assays. Suppression was allele-specific for *unc-17* alleles encoding the same G347R substitution, providing strong residue-specific genetic evidence rather than a nonspecific improvement in sick animals. (mathews2012geneticinteractionsbetween pages 2-3)

The suppression and localization studies also show that UNC-17 performance depends on vesicle abundance, trafficking, and membrane-protein context. Those factors can alter cholinergic output without changing UNC-17’s substrate specificity.

## 5. Recent developments, 2023–2024

### Endogenous neurotransmitter atlas

Wang et al. generated a CRISPR *unc-17* knock-in with a **T2A–GFP–H2B** cassette immediately before the stop codon. Nuclear reporter localization allowed cells to be identified with NeuroPAL while retaining endogenous regulatory context, including the native 3′ region. The broader atlas examined **16 knock-in alleles spanning 15 neurotransmitter-related loci** and mapped transmitter identities in males and hermaphrodites. This is an important methodological improvement over older multicopy reporters, although reporter transcript detection still does not by itself establish transporter abundance or transport activity. [Published October 2024, DOI](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.95402.3). (wang2024aneurotransmitteratlas pages 5-6, wang2024aneurotransmitteratlas pages 3-5)

### Neuro-intestinal mitochondrial-stress signaling

Cornell et al. used *unc-17(e113)* as a genetic block of vesicular ACh output to demonstrate a systemic pathway in which neuronal ACh activates the intestinal α7-like nicotinic receptor ACR-11, elevates intestinal calcium, and induces the mitochondrial unfolded-protein response (UPRmt). Loss of *unc-17* reduced intestinal **hsp-6p::gfp** and suppressed reporter induction caused by loss of GABA synthesis in *unc-25(e156)* animals. Conversely, increased cholinergic signaling in *ace-2;ace-1* acetylcholinesterase mutants—reported to produce approximately twofold higher systemic ACh—required ACR-11 for UPRmt induction. (pocock2024neurointestinalacetylcholinesignalling pages 4-6, cornell2024neurointestinalacetylcholinesignalling pages 5-6)

The physiological endpoint was tested by exposing L4 animals to **200 mM paraquat** and scoring survival hourly for **15 hours**. *unc-17(e113)* increased paraquat resistance and rescued sensitivity associated with excess cholinergic signaling. Reported survival comparisons included *P* < 0.0001 and *P* = 0.0342; intestine-specific ACR-11 reporter experiments used **n = 30** and yielded *P* < 0.0001. [Published August 2024, DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-024-50973-y). (cornell2024neurointestinalacetylcholinesignalling pages 6-7, cornell2024neurointestinalacetylcholinesignalling pages 9-10, cornell2024neurointestinalacetylcholinesignalling pages 7-8)

This study expands the **physiological consequences** of UNC-17-dependent ACh release to extrasynaptic neuro-intestinal communication. It does not imply that UNC-17 is itself mitochondrial or intestinal: UNC-17 remains a neuronal vesicle loader, while intestinal ACR-11 is the downstream detector.

### Presynaptic homeostasis

A 2024 PNAS study found that prolonged optogenetic neuronal activation reduced synaptic abundance of the UNC-2/CaV2 channel. The decrease still occurred in *unc-17* mutants, indicating that this particular homeostatic response tracked neuronal activity/vesicle exocytosis rather than ACh quantal content itself. Stimulation longer than two hours reduced UNC-2 abundance without changing puncta number. [Published August 2024, DOI](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2404969121). (xiong2024presynapticneuronsselftune pages 4-5)

This is informative negative evidence: UNC-17 affects vesicle content, but not every presynaptic adaptation downstream of activity is caused by ACh loading.

## 6. Current experimental applications

The *unc-17* regulatory region is routinely used as a **cholinergic-neuron driver**. A 3,180-bp promoter fragment was reported to drive expression exclusively in cholinergic neurons, while the Punc-17b derivative targets AS, DA, DB, VA, and VB ventral-cord cholinergic motor neurons. It has consequently enabled cell-restricted fluorescent labeling, optogenetic activation, calcium imaging, RNA interference, rescue experiments, and expression of disease-associated proteins. (mathews2012geneticinteractionsbetween pages 2-3)

This use must be interpreted carefully. A construct described as “*unc-17p*::gene-X” generally means that gene-X was expressed in cholinergic neurons; it does **not** necessarily implicate the UNC-17 transporter mechanistically. By contrast, experiments using *unc-17* loss-of-function alleles, endogenous protein staining, or transporter mutations directly test UNC-17 biology.

UNC-17 is also an experimental benchmark for distinguishing stages of synaptic-vesicle function. In all-optical screens it represents the **vesicle-filling** step, whereas synaptotagmin, SNARE, endocytic, and recycling mutants represent exocytosis or vesicle-recovery steps. This enables circuit assays to separate reduced transmitter content from defective fusion or recycling.

## 7. Expert assessment and remaining uncertainties

The combined evidence supports a **high-confidence functional annotation**:

- **Molecular activity:** vesicular ACh transport.
- **Biological substrate:** acetylcholine; no persuasive evidence supports another physiological substrate.
- **Energy coupling:** proton-gradient-driven ACh accumulation, based on the conserved VAChT/MFS mechanism.
- **Functional compartment:** cholinergic presynaptic synaptic-vesicle membrane.
- **Pathway role:** connects ACh synthesis to quantal release.
- **Organismal consequences:** locomotor, feeding, egg-laying, circuit, and systemic stress phenotypes arise from altered cholinergic output.

The strongest direct worm evidence comes from genetics, antibody localization, neuron maps, and cell-specific epistasis. The main evidentiary limitation is that the retrieved literature did not provide purified P34711 transport kinetics, an experimentally measured coupling stoichiometry, or a high-resolution structure of the worm protein. Accordingly, detailed alternating-access and H⁺-exchange claims should be regarded as well-supported VAChT-family inference, whereas ACh substrate identity, synaptic-vesicle localization, and cholinergic function are directly demonstrated in *C. elegans*.
