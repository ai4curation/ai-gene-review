Gene Description

apa-2 (formerly apt-4) encodes the alpha-adaptin large subunit of the AP-2 clathrin adaptor complex of Caenorhabditis elegans. Alpha-adaptin forms the alpha/sigma2 half of the heterotetrameric adaptor: its trunk contributes phosphatidylinositol-4,5-bisphosphate binding that docks AP-2 on the plasma membrane and, with sigma2, the pocket that reads dileucine sorting signals, while its hinge and appendage ('ear') domain recruits accessory endocytic proteins such as the RAB-5 exchange factor RME-6, the RAB-35 regulator RME-4, the clathrin-associated sorting protein DAB-1 and the engulfment adaptor CED-6. APA-2 is expressed in most cells, is highest in neurons, and marks plasma-membrane clathrin-coated pits in coelomocytes, the presynaptic periactive zone of motor neurons and phagocytic cups around apoptotic corpses. It functions in clathrin-mediated endocytosis of yolk by oocytes, synaptic vesicle retrieval at neuromuscular junctions, and CED-1-dependent apoptotic-cell engulfment. Null mutants are viable but dumpy, egg-laying defective and mildly uncoordinated, with a 30% reduction in synaptic vesicles; combined loss of alpha and mu2 removes essentially all AP-2 function and is nearly lethal.

Core Functions

As the alpha subunit of the AP-2 adaptor complex, APA-2 docks the adaptor on PI(4,5)P2-containing plasma membrane, contributes with sigma2 to dileucine-signal recognition, and through its appendage recruits accessory proteins (RME-6, RME-4, DAB-1, CED-6) that couple selected cargo (yolk receptor/DAB-1, the CED-1 phagocytic receptor) to clathrin during clathrin-dependent endocytosis.

Functional annotation report: C. elegans apa-2 / UniProt Q22601

Executive conclusion

The identity is verified: Caenorhabditis elegans apa-2 encodes APA-2, the α-adaptin/large α subunit of the heterotetrameric AP-2 clathrin adaptor complex. This matches UniProt Q22601, ORF T20B5.1, and the supplied large-adaptin, AP2-α, ARM-like, and clathrin/coatomer-adaptin domain annotations. The literature examined does not indicate confusion with a different similarly named gene. Direct worm studies use multiple apa-2 null or severe-loss alleles and rescuing APA-2::GFP constructs, making the identification highly secure. (gu2013ap2hemicomplexescontribute pages 2-4, gu2013ap2hemicomplexescontribute pages 15-16)

APA-2 is not an enzyme or transporter. Its primary function is structural and organizational: as the large subunit of the α–σ2 half of AP-2, it helps couple membrane-associated cargo and accessory proteins to clathrin-mediated membrane-remodeling machinery. Its best-supported sites of action are the cytoplasmic face of the plasma membrane, synaptic endocytic zones, intestinal basolateral membrane, coelomocyte membrane, and corpse-containing phagosomes. Its most precisely demonstrated functions are cargo endocytosis, synaptic-vesicle recycling, and CED-1-dependent apoptotic-cell clearance. (gu2013ap2hemicomplexescontribute pages 8-10, gu2013ap2hemicomplexescontribute pages 6-8, chen2013clathrinandap2 pages 3-4)

1. Identity and domain-level interpretation

Primary genetic literature explicitly identifies apa-2 as the gene for AP-2 α-adaptin. Particularly strong alleles include b1044, a 925-bp deletion removing residues 93–318 of the trunk; ox422, a premature stop at Lys215; and ox421, which deletes the complete open reading frame. A functional APA-2::GFP transgene rescues mutant phenotypes, linking the cloned locus, tagged protein, and biological activity. (gu2013ap2hemicomplexescontribute pages 2-4, gu2013ap2hemicomplexescontribute pages 15-16)

The supplied UniProt architecture is fully consistent with this assignment. Large adaptin subunits have an extended α-solenoid/ARM-like trunk forming the AP-2 core, followed by flexible hinge and appendage or “ear” regions that organize coat-associated factors. The direct worm evidence establishes the trunk’s importance because both b1044 and ox422 disrupt it. Precise residue-level functions of all Q22601 domains have not, however, been determined experimentally in the worm; the detailed ARM-repeat and appendage interpretation is therefore family/structural inference rather than an APA-2-specific atomic structure. (gu2013ap2hemicomplexescontribute pages 2-4)

2. Molecular function and AP-2 organization

AP-2 contains α/APA-2, β, μ2/APM-2, and σ2/APS-2 subunits. Worm genetics revealed that it is not simply an indivisible heterotetramer: α–σ2 and μ2–β can retain partial function as “hemicomplexes.” In μ2-deficient animals, α-adaptin and σ2 remain membrane or synapse associated, although α abundance falls to approximately 60% by immunoblot or 40% by fluorescence. Conversely, loss of APA-2 destabilizes σ2 while μ2 and β remain comparatively stable. This provides strong in-vivo evidence that APA-2 is the principal large structural component of the α–σ2 hemicomplex. (gu2013ap2hemicomplexescontribute pages 6-8, gu2008μ2adaptinfacilitates pages 6-6)

The two halves are partially redundant but cargo-selective. Simultaneous α- and μ2-adaptin loss caused 95.70 ± 4.30% embryonic lethality and reduced brood size to 3.4 ± 3.1 progeny, versus 247.3 ± 8.8 in wild type. Thus, single-subunit viability should not be interpreted as AP-2 dispensability; residual hemicomplex activity masks the full requirement. (gu2013ap2hemicomplexescontribute pages 4-6)

APA-2’s clearest cargo-sorting role involves dileucine-signal-dependent internalization. An intestinal eGFP–CD4 reporter carrying an ExxxLL signal accumulated at the basolateral membrane in apa-2(ox422) animals, with fluorescence of 14,907 ± 990 versus 1,998 ± 275 in wild type (p<0.0001). The stronger α-subunit phenotype relative to μ2 loss supports preferential recognition or handling of this cargo class by the α–σ2 arm. By contrast, MIG-14/Wntless trafficking depends more strongly on μ2, demonstrating that the two halves are not functionally interchangeable. (gu2013ap2hemicomplexescontribute pages 4-6, gu2013ap2hemicomplexescontribute pages 6-8)

3. Cellular and tissue localization

A rescuing APA-2::GFP reporter is broadly expressed, with particularly evident expression in the nervous system, intestine, and hypodermis. In GABAergic neurons, APA-2 colocalizes with synaptobrevin at synaptic varicosities in the dorsal and ventral nerve cords. It is also found at the coelomocyte plasma membrane and at intestinal membranes, consistent with a cytoplasmic, membrane-recruited endocytic adaptor rather than a secreted, luminal, or integral-membrane protein. (gu2013ap2hemicomplexescontribute pages 8-10, gu2013ap2hemicomplexescontribute pages 2-4, gu2013ap2hemicomplexescontribute pages 6-8)

APA-2 additionally associates with germ-cell-corpse phagosomes. Phagosomal APA-2::GFP labeling is reduced after depletion of clathrin heavy chain CHC-1, while apa-2 or β-subunit depletion suppresses phagosomal CHC-1 labeling. This reciprocal dependence supports assembly or stabilization of an AP-2–clathrin coat on phagosomal membranes. (chen2013clathrinandap2 pages 3-4, chen2013clathrinandap2 pages 17-17)

Localization should be understood as dynamic. At the complex level, AP-2 cycles between a closed cytosolic state and an open membrane-associated state that exposes membrane- and cargo-binding surfaces. FCHO-1 promotes opening and stable clustering: in fcho-1 mutants, AP-2 remains membrane-associated but its membrane dwell time decreases from about 35 seconds to under 10 seconds. This is strong AP-2-complex evidence, although it does not establish an APA-2-only biochemical activity. (hollopeter2014themembraneassociatedproteins pages 11-12)

4. Biological pathways

4.1 Synaptic-vesicle endocytosis

APA-2 participates in recovery of synaptic-vesicle membrane and proteins after neurotransmitter release. Neuronal APA-2 loss reduced synaptic vesicle numbers to 71% of wild type in cholinergic neurons and 59% in GABAergic neurons; nervous-system expression of APA-2 restored the defect. When both α and μ2 activities were removed, vesicle abundance fell to 28% and 31%, respectively, and large vacuoles or abnormal vesicular profiles accumulated. These findings support AP-2-dependent retrieval at the plasma membrane and likely an additional role in regenerating synaptic vesicles from endosomal intermediates. (gu2013ap2hemicomplexescontribute pages 8-10, gu2013ap2hemicomplexescontribute pages 15-16)

Synaptotagmin, UNC-47, and synaptogyrin remain substantially synaptically localized even in α/μ2-deficient animals, implying that other adaptors or AP-2-independent routes can retrieve some vesicle proteins. Therefore, APA-2 is important but not the only synaptic endocytic adaptor in the worm. (gu2013ap2hemicomplexescontribute pages 8-10)

4.2 Receptor and cargo trafficking

Besides artificial dileucine cargo, AP-2 influences endogenous MIG-14/Wntless and GLR-1 glutamate-receptor trafficking. Mutations in APA-2/α, APM-2/μ2, or APS-2/σ2 reduce GLR-1 abundance at ventral-nerve-cord synapses. The GLR-1 study proposed an unexpected AP-2-dependent step early in the secretory pathway rather than a simple defect in surface internalization. Because the available experiments concern several AP-2 subunits and do not show direct APA-2–GLR-1 binding, this role should be assigned to the complex rather than uniquely to APA-2. (garafalo2015theap2clathrin pages 1-4)

4.3 CED-1-mediated apoptotic-cell clearance

APA-2 has a directly demonstrated role in phagocytic receptor signaling. The intracellular region of CED-1 binds APA-2 in vitro, whereas the other tested AP-2 subunits did not detectably bind CED-1. APA-2::GFP localizes to corpse-containing phagosomes, and the tagged transgene rescues corpse accumulation following apa-2 depletion. These results make APA-2 a physical bridge between CED-1 and the larger AP-2/clathrin machinery. (chen2013clathrinandap2 pages 3-4)

At the complex level, AP-2 and clathrin facilitate both corpse engulfment and phagosome maturation. They act with CED-6/Gulp in actin remodeling and promote recruitment of LST-4/Snx9-family and DYN-1/dynamin machinery. In clathrin or AP-2 β-subunit depletion experiments, 48–56% of corpses were not fully internalized, corpse persistence exceeded 120 minutes, and actin recruitment to CED-1- or CED-6-positive phagosomes fell from 77–85% to 35–41%. These numerical phenotypes demonstrate pathway importance, but they are whole-complex measurements and should not all be attributed solely to APA-2. (chen2013clathrinandap2 pages 3-4, chen2013clathrinandap2 pages 2-3, chen2013clathrinandap2 pages 1-2)

5. Regulation and interaction network

FCHO-1 is an upstream AP-2 activator. Its conserved approximately 90-residue activator segment binds the AP-2 core and stabilizes the open state, exposing membrane- and cargo-binding sites and enabling productive clathrin-coated-pit assembly. FCHO-1’s relevant contact was mapped to the AP-2 core rather than exclusively to the α appendage. (hollopeter2014themembraneassociatedproteins pages 11-12)

NCAP-1, the worm NECAP ortholog, acts oppositely. Loss of NCAP-1 bypasses the need for FCHO-1 and causes open, phosphorylated AP-2 to accumulate. NECAP preferentially recognizes open/phosphorylated AP-2 and is interpreted as promoting adaptor inactivation or recycling late in endocytosis. APA-2::GFP was used to monitor complex localization and dynamics, but the regulatory mechanism applies to AP-2 as a conformational unit rather than proving that APA-2 alone is the regulated substrate. (beacham2018necapsarenegative pages 11-15)

The highest-confidence APA-2-specific partners or associations are therefore σ2/APS-2 within the hemicomplex, CED-1 through direct intracellular-domain binding, and clathrin-associated membranes through reciprocal recruitment at phagosomes. Associations with FCHO-1, NCAP-1, phosphoinositides, and cargo-recognition machinery are best described at AP-2-complex level unless a direct APA-2 interaction was separately demonstrated. (gu2013ap2hemicomplexescontribute pages 6-8, chen2013clathrinandap2 pages 3-4, beacham2018necapsarenegative pages 11-15)

6. Organismal phenotypes and quantitative genetics

Single apa-2 mutants are viable but show jowls, variable dumpiness, egg-laying defects, mild uncoordination, and impaired thrashing. Tissue-specific rescue demonstrates that these are not merely nonspecific sickness phenotypes: neuronal expression rescues relevant body-length/locomotor effects, while hypodermal expression rescues cuticle-associated defects. Without compensating tissue rescue, synaptic-vesicle depletion can be more severe—56% of wild type in cholinergic neurons and 29% in GABAergic neurons. (gu2013ap2hemicomplexescontribute pages 8-10, gu2013ap2hemicomplexescontribute pages 2-4)

These pleiotropic effects are secondary to the core membrane-trafficking role. The mechanistically most informative phenotypes are cargo retention at the membrane, reduced synaptic-vesicle numbers, destabilization of σ2, synthetic lethality with μ2 loss, and impaired phagosome formation/maturation.

7. Recent developments and state of the literature

A targeted search prioritizing 2023–2024 did not identify a new peer-reviewed study that revises the molecular annotation of APA-2 itself. The decisive gene-specific mechanistic literature remains concentrated in 2013–2018. Recent endocytosis research continues to support the broader framework in which AP-2 is the major clathrin-associated cargo adaptor, but it does not supersede the worm-specific genetic, localization, and interaction evidence summarized here.

This absence of recent gene-specific papers is biologically informative rather than a reason to substitute literature on a different “APA-2.” The correct annotation should remain conservative: APA-2 is a validated AP-2 α-adaptin with direct roles in cargo endocytosis, synaptic-vesicle recycling, and CED-1-dependent corpse clearance; claims about atomic structure, lipid specificity, phosphorylation, or conformational cycling should generally be labeled as AP-2-family or complex-level inference.

8. Current applications and expert assessment

The most important real-world use of apa-2 is as an experimental genetic handle on conserved clathrin-mediated endocytosis. Viable α-subunit null animals permit separation of α–σ2 from μ2–β functions, tissue-specific rescue permits assignment of neuronal versus epithelial requirements, and APA-2::GFP enables live analysis of membrane recruitment and coat regulation. The system has been used to study synaptic-vesicle recycling, cargo-signal specificity, glutamate-receptor trafficking, FCHO/NECAP conformational control, and efferocytosis. (gu2013ap2hemicomplexescontribute pages 8-10, garafalo2015theap2clathrin pages 1-4, hollopeter2014themembraneassociatedproteins pages 11-12, chen2013clathrinandap2 pages 3-4, beacham2018necapsarenegative pages 11-15)

The strongest expert interpretation from the primary studies is that AP-2 is neither a rigid obligate tetramer nor the only endocytic route. Instead, APA-2 helps form a partially autonomous α–σ2 module with preferential cargo functions, while the complete tetramer provides maximal efficiency and organismal viability. This model explains why apa-2 single mutants survive but α/μ2 double loss is nearly lethal and produces severe synaptic defects. (gu2013ap2hemicomplexescontribute pages 4-6, gu2013ap2hemicomplexescontribute pages 6-8)

9. Final annotation

Recommended functional description: “AP-2 complex α subunit; large cytosolic adaptin that forms the α–σ2 hemicomplex and, within AP-2, recruits and organizes cargo- and clathrin-associated machinery at the cytoplasmic face of plasma and phagosomal membranes. Required for efficient dileucine-cargo internalization, synaptic-vesicle recycling, selected receptor trafficking, and CED-1-dependent apoptotic-cell engulfment/phagosome maturation.”

Primary location: cytoplasmic peripheral membrane protein recruited to plasma-membrane endocytic sites, neuronal synapses, intestinal basolateral membranes, coelomocyte membranes, and corpse-containing phagosomes.

Evidence confidence: high for identity, α–σ2 structural role, membrane/synaptic/phagosomal localization, dileucine-cargo endocytosis, synaptic-vesicle recycling, and CED-1 binding; moderate for APA-2-specific assignment of functions demonstrated only after perturbing AP-2 as a whole; inferential for detailed domain mechanics not solved directly for Q22601.

apa-2 (apt-4) — AP-2 complex subunit alpha (Q22601, TrEMBL) — curation notes

Identity

  • apa-2 (T20B5.1; older alias apt-4) encodes the alpha-adaptin large subunit of the AP-2 clathrin adaptor complex PMID:23482940. Two likely-null alleles exist: b1044 (925 bp deletion in the trunk) and ox422 (Lys215 premature stop); neither produces full-length protein PMID:23482940.
  • APA-2::GFP is expressed in most cells and is highest in the nervous system PMID:23482940.

Molecular role within AP-2

  • Alpha-adaptin forms a hemicomplex with sigma2; the alpha/sigma2 half remains localised and partially functional without mu2, and the alpha appendage ("ear") recruits accessory endocytic proteins such as dynamin, AP180, epsin and eps15 PMID:23482940 PMID:10588660.
  • Cargo specificity: recovery of di-leucine cargo depends on alpha more than mu2, whereas MIG-14/Wntless (tyrosine motif) depends on mu2 PMID:23482940 PMID:23482940.
  • Accessory-protein interactions documented in the worm (all via the alpha subunit): RME-6 (Vps9-domain RAB-5 GEF) co-immunoprecipitates with alpha-adaptin and carries a WxxF alpha-ear motif PMID:15895077; RME-4 (DENN protein, RAB-35 GEF) binds AP-2 (abstract) PMID:18354496; DAB-1 binds clathrin and APT-4 in vitro (abstract) PMID:16497226; CED-1 intracellular domain and CED-6 bind APA-2 directly and co-IP in vivo PMID:23696751.

Localisation

  • Coelomocytes: mRFP::alpha-adaptin marks plasma-membrane clathrin-coated pits together with CHC-1, unevenly distributed around the plasma membrane PMID:15895077 PMID:15895077.
  • Synapses: APT-4::GFP is predominantly at synaptic puncta and behaves as a periactive-zone / endocytic-intermediate marker whose abundance rises in synaptojanin and endophilin mutants PMID:19043554 PMID:22588719. Tagged alpha-adaptin remains at synapses and the coelomocyte plasma membrane in mu2 mutants PMID:23482940.
  • Phagosomes: APA-2::GFP surrounds apoptotic germ-cell corpses in a CED-1/CED-6- and clathrin-dependent manner PMID:23696751.

Functions and phenotypes

  • Yolk endocytosis: alpha-adaptin RNAi abolishes YP170::GFP uptake, and embryos from such mothers die PMID:10588660 PMID:10588660. However, apa-2 null mutants are viable with near-normal embryonic viability (2.4% vs 1.1% lethality) and still endocytose yolk, so AP-2 assists DAB-1 rather than being absolutely required PMID:23482940 PMID:23482940.
  • Synaptic vesicle endocytosis: loss of alpha alone reduces synaptic vesicle number ~30% and evoked amplitude ~25–42%; complete loss of AP-2 (alpha + mu2) reduces vesicles 70% and produces large vacuoles PMID:23482940 PMID:23482940.
  • Whole-animal phenotypes: variable dumpy (rescued by neuronal expression), jowls, Egl, mildly Unc; apa-2 apm-2 double mutants are almost fully embryonic lethal PMID:23482940 PMID:23482940. apa-2 mutants share the jowls phenotype with fcho-1, and fcho-1 bypass suppressors include mutations in the alpha solenoid PMID:25303366.
  • Apoptotic cell clearance: apa-2 RNAi increases germ-cell corpses; APA-2::GFP rescues PMID:23696751.
  • DAB-1 localisation in oocytes depends on clathrin and AP-2 (abstract only) PMID:17636000.

Curation decisions

  • GO:0032794 "GTPase activating protein binding" (IPI with RME-6): RME-6 is a Vps9-domain RAB-5 guanine-nucleotide exchange factor, not a GAP, and GO has no GEF-binding term; the row is removed as mislabelled (the interaction itself stands and is recorded above).
  • GO:0009792 embryo development (IMP, alpha RNAi): contradicted by viable apa-2 null strains with near wild-type embryonic viability; the RNAi lethality most likely reflects yolk deprivation of oocytes. Removed.
  • Four generic protein-binding rows (RME-6, DAB-1, RME-4, CED-6) are removed as uninformative per project policy; the interactions are genuine alpha-appendage/accessory-protein contacts.

Additional literature surfaced by deep research (not in GOA; see apa-2-deep-research-falcon.md)

  • Quantified dileucine-cargo defect: an intestinal eGFP-CD4 ExxxLL reporter accumulates ~7-fold at the basolateral membrane in apa-2(ox422) [file:worm/apa-2/apa-2-deep-research-falcon.md "An intestinal eGFP–CD4 reporter carrying an ExxxLL signal accumulated at the basolateral membrane in apa-2(ox422) animals, with fluorescence of 14,907 ± 990 versus 1,998 ± 275 in wild type (p<0.0001)."].
  • AP-2 subunit mutants (apa-2, apm-2, aps-2) reduce GLR-1 glutamate receptor abundance at ventral-cord synapses via an early secretory/endosomal step (Garafalo et al. 2015, DOI 10.1091/mbc.e14-06-1048).
  • NCAP-1/NECAP antagonises FCHO-1 by recognising open, phosphorylated AP-2 (Beacham et al. 2018); APA-2::GFP was the reporter for complex dynamics.
  • CED-1's intracellular domain binds APA-2 but not the other AP-2 subunits tested, making alpha the physical bridge to the phagocytic receptor [file:worm/apa-2/apa-2-deep-research-falcon.md "The intracellular region of CED-1 binds APA-2 in vitro, whereas the other tested AP-2 subunits did not detectably bind CED-1."].
