# snb-1 (UniProt O02495) — Synaptobrevin-1 (VAMP), C. elegans

## Identity and structure
- Reviewed entry SNB1_CAEEL, 109 aa, WormBase T10H9.4. N-terminal cytoplasmic v-SNARE coiled-coil (domain 23-83) and a single C-terminal transmembrane anchor (87-107); type IV membrane protein of synaptic vesicles (from `snb-1-uniprot.txt`). Orthologue of vertebrate VAMP1/2.

## Core function: vesicle R-SNARE for synaptic vesicle exocytosis
- First metazoan synaptobrevin mutants: nulls die just after hatching, hypomorphs are lethargic, jerky, aldicarb-resistant and have pharyngeal transmission defects [PMID:9412487 "Mutants lacking snb-1 die just after completing embryogenesis."; "The viable mutants are resistant to the acetylcholinesterase inhibitor aldicarb, indicating that cholinergic transmission is impaired."].
- Postsynaptic responses are intact (levamisole sensitivity normal), so the defect is presynaptic [PMID:9412487 "Thus, the assay indicates that postsynaptic responses are fundamentally intact, suggesting that cholinergic transmission is impaired presynaptically in snb-1 mutants."].
- Hypomorphic lesions lie on the hydrophobic face of the amphipathic helix that binds the t-SNAREs syntaxin and SNAP-25 [PMID:9412487 "The molecular lesions in the hypomorphic alleles reside on the hydrophobic face of a proposed amphipathic-helical region implicated biochemically in interacting with the t-SNAREs syntaxin and SNAP-25."]; unc-64 H3 alleles interact allele-specifically with these snb-1 lesions [PMID:9614171 "the unc-64 syntaxin mutants exhibit allele-specific genetic interactions with mutants carrying lesions in the coiled-coil domain of synaptobrevin"].
- Residual release persists in snb-1 nulls (some movement, 10-20 pumps/min), unlike unc-64 nulls, suggesting another R-SNARE can partially substitute [PMID:9412487 "Additionally, pharyngeal pumping (10–20 pumps/min) is observed in snb-1 null mutants."; "A more likely possibility is that the residual release events use another synaptobrevin-like molecule expressed in the nervous system."]. snt-1; snb-1 doubles resemble snb-1 singles and are less severe than unc-64 [PMID:9412487 "The double mutants were phenotypically very similar to snb-1(js124) animals."].
- Glutamatergic M3 pharyngeal motor-neuron transients are absent or tiny in snb-1(md247), lengthening pump duration [PMID:9412487 "M3 transients were usually completely absent or extremely small in recordings from snb-1(md247) mutants"]. Defecation cycle and enteric muscle contraction are also impaired [PMID:9412487 "the defecation cycle time was longer, and enteric muscle contraction step of the defecation cycle ( Avery and Thomas, 1997 ) failed more frequently than in the wild type"] — all read by the authors as manifestations of impaired synaptic transmission [PMID:9412487 "These behavioral defects presumably all represent manifestations of the underlying impairment of synaptic transmission."].

## Localization
- SNB-1 immunoreactivity is restricted to the synapse-rich process bundles, colocalises with RAB-3 and synaptotagmin, and is mislocalised to cell bodies in unc-104 kinesin mutants, i.e. it behaves as a synaptic vesicle protein [PMID:9412487 "Previously characterized C. elegans synaptic vesicle proteins RAB-3 (Fig. 2 D ) and synaptotagmin (data not shown) colocalized with synaptobrevin"; "synaptobrevin immunoreactivity was mislocalized in cell bodies in unc-104 , as would be expected of a synaptic vesicle-associated protein"].
- SNB-1::GFP marks presynaptic varicosities in living animals and is the standard C. elegans synaptic vesicle marker [PMID:10476681 "C. elegans synaptobrevin-GFP and synaptogyrin-GFP fusion proteins were observed to target to synaptic sites."]; used as such in PMID:17942601 ("SNB-1::GFP was accordingly found in clusters in the ventral and dorsal cords"), PMID:23527112 ("SNB-1-GFP localizes to punctate structures in the dorsal nerve cord that correspond to presynaptic sites in GABAergic motor neurons") and PMID:20573983 ("synaptic vesicle marker SNB-1-GFP (synaptobrevin), which labels synaptic terminals in the interneurons").
- A plasma-membrane pool: synaptopHluorin shows ~30% of synaptobrevin on the surface, supplied by vesicle fusion, equilibrating along the axon and enriched near active zones, with its extent set by AP180/UNC-11 [PMID:16844789 "We estimated that 30% of synaptobrevin was present in the plasma membrane."; "The surface synaptobrevin was enriched near active zones, and its spatial extent was regulated by the clathrin adaptin AP180."].
- Germline: SNB-1 is also expressed in the germline, where GFP-SNB-1 sits mostly on the plasma membrane of developing compartments (RAB-8/RAB-10-dependent) [PMID:20573983 "Although SNB-1 is best known as a neuronal protein found mainly found in synaptic vesicles, it is also normally expressed in the germline (Sato et al., 2008a)."]. The cortical-granule IDA row cites Sato et al. 2008 (PMID:18765566), whose cached abstract discusses SYN-4/RAB-11 and cortical granule exocytosis but does not mention SNB-1.
- Chemical-induced convulsions in snb-1 mutants parallel those of lis-1 and unc-104, linking vesicle supply to GABAergic signalling thresholds [PMID:15254012 "mutations in UNC-104, a neuronal-specific kinesin, and SNB-1, a synaptic vesicle-associated protein termed synaptobrevin, exhibit similar convulsion phenotypes following chemical induction"].

## Curation notes
- Larval development, defecation, pharyngeal pumping and hindgut contraction IMP rows all derive from the phenotypic survey of PMID:9412487 and are downstream consequences of impaired release.
- Several localisation rows (PMID:10476681, 15254012, 16466809, 17698012) cite papers where SNB-1::GFP is used as a synaptic marker; the abstracts do not always describe the localisation explicitly, but the locations are well established for SNB-1.
