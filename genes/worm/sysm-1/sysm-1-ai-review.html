Gene Description

sysm-1 (T24B8.5, systemic stress signaling mediator 1) encodes a small (99 aa) secreted, cysteine-rich protein with an N-terminal signal peptide and two ShKT (ShK toxin-like) domains, a member of a nematode-expanded family of ShKT peptides. Its transcription in the intestine is driven by the TIR-1/NSY-1/SEK-1/PMK-1 p38 MAPK pathway and the ATF-7 transcription factor, and it is strongly induced by Pseudomonas aeruginosa infection, which made the T24B8.5 promoter the standard in vivo reporter of PMK-1 pathway activity. Genetic analysis of a sysm-1 deletion showed that SYSM-1 is processed through the Golgi in anterior intestinal cells, secreted in a signal-peptide-dependent manner, and taken up by the germline, where it is required, and sufficient when expressed in either tissue, for DNA-damage-induced and heat-stress-enhanced apoptosis of pachytene germ cells. It acts in parallel to CEP-1/p53, since egl-1 and ced-13 induction is intact in sysm-1 mutants. A direct antimicrobial activity has not been demonstrated, and its molecular activity (receptor, binding partner or mechanism) is unknown.

Suggested Questions for Experts

Q: Does SYSM-1 contribute to pathogen resistance at all, given that it is the canonical PMK-1/ATF-7 reporter but has only been shown to act in germline DNA-damage apoptosis? Should modules treat it as an antibacterial effector or as a stress signal?

Q: What germline receptor or uptake route takes up intestinal SYSM-1, and do its ShKT domains bind a channel or receptor as ShK toxins do?

Suggested Experiments

Experiment: Compare survival and intestinal bacterial load of sysm-1(ok3236), wild type, and intestine-rescued sysm-1 animals on P. aeruginosa PA14, E. faecalis and S. aureus, alone and in a background lacking related ShKT paralogs.

Hypothesis: SYSM-1 contributes to survival during P. aeruginosa or other bacterial infection.

Experiment: Forward genetic suppressor screen for loss of the excess IR-induced apoptosis caused by germline SYSM-1 overexpression, plus proximity labeling of V5-tagged SYSM-1 in the germline to identify binding partners.

Hypothesis: SYSM-1 acts through a specific germline receptor or channel.

Key findings

  • The gene was named systemic stress signaling mediator in 2022 PMID:35121747.
  • Secreted intestinal ShKT peptide required for IR-induced germ cell apoptosis PMID:35121747.
  • Secretion depends on the signal sequence PMID:35121747.
  • It acts independently of CEP-1 output PMID:42173292.
  • It is the standard PMK-1 reporter PMID:20369020.
  • No antibacterial activity or infection phenotype has been reported. Single-gene RNAi of PMK-1 targets had no effect PMID:17096597.
