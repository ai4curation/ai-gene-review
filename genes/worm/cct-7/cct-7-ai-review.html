Gene Description

cct-7 encodes the eta (CCT7 / TCP-1-eta) subunit of the eukaryotic cytosolic chaperonin CCT/TRiC, an ATP-dependent double-ring chaperonin built from eight distinct paralogous subunits per ring that folds a subset of cytosolic proteins, notably actin and tubulin. The eta subunit lies in the low-ATP-affinity hemisphere of the ring and contributes structural and substrate-binding surfaces rather than serving as a major ATPase. In C. elegans cct-7 (T10B5.5) is expressed in somatic tissues with high levels in the pharynx and tail, and acts in the cytoplasm as an integral subunit of the chaperonin; gene-specific experimental data in the worm are sparse and its roles are inferred largely from the conserved complex.

Core Functions

cct-7 is the eta subunit of the cytosolic chaperonin CCT/TRiC, located in the low-ATP-affinity hemisphere of the ring. Its contribution to the ATP-dependent folding of actin, tubulin and other cytosolic clients is as a structural and substrate-contacting member of the folding chamber rather than as a major ATPase; the foldase activity belongs to the assembled complex.

Suggested Questions for Experts

Q: Which native C. elegans CCT clients depend specifically on contacts made by the eta subunit apical domain, as opposed to the complex as a whole?

Q: Is the low ATP affinity of the eta subunit measured for yeast and bovine TRiC conserved in the nematode complex, and should family-level ATP hydrolysis annotations be qualified for low-affinity CCT subunits?

Q: Is the shorter cct-7 product (Q86S29, 455 aa) a genuine isoform that incorporates into CCT, or an incomplete gene model?

Suggested Experiments

Experiment: Affinity purification and mass spectrometry of endogenously tagged cct-7 (or of assembled CCT) from staged C. elegans, combined with crosslinking, to map clients contacting the eta subunit.

Hypothesis: The eta subunit contributes a defined subset of client contacts, dominated by actin and tubulin but including WD40 and other proteins.

Experiment: Introduce nucleotide-binding (P-loop) or hydrolysis-dead mutations into cct-7 by CRISPR and test viability, actin/tubulin levels and fertility.

Hypothesis: As for yeast low-affinity subunits, ATP binding by the eta subunit is dispensable for CCT function in vivo.
