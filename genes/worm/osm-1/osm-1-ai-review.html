Gene Description

OSM-1 is the Caenorhabditis elegans ortholog of IFT172, the largest subunit of the intraflagellar transport complex B (IFT-B). It is a large protein (1737 aa) with an N-terminal array of WD40 repeats forming beta-propellers followed by an extended tetratricopeptide-like (TPR) alpha-solenoid with WAA repeats, a domain architecture shared with IFT-A subunits IFT140/WDR19 and with IFT80. OSM-1 is expressed in ciliated chemosensory neurons, concentrates at the base of the transition zone (basal body region) and moves bidirectionally along dendrites and along the ciliary axoneme as part of IFT particles, at the same rates as kinesin-II and the IFT-B subunit OSM-6; its retrograde movement within cilia depends on the dynein-2 heavy chain CHE-3. osm-1 mutants have normal transition zones but severely shortened axonemes, ectopic doublet microtubules proximal to the cilium, defective dye filling, osmotic avoidance and chemotaxis defects, and dauer-defective phenotypes, all secondary to loss of sensory cilium structure. By analogy to Chlamydomonas IFT172/FLA11, OSM-1 is expected to act at the ciliary tip in the conversion of anterograde to retrograde IFT trains.

Core Functions

OSM-1/IFT172 is a structural subunit of the IFT-B particle in ciliated sensory neurons. It assembles with IFT-B at the ciliary base, is carried anterogradely by kinesin-2 and returned by dynein-2, and is required to build the sensory ciliary axoneme. No discrete molecular activity has been assigned in the worm.
