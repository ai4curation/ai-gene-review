# sph-1 (UniProt O44511, TrEMBL) — synaptophysin (physin-family tetraspan vesicle protein), C. elegans

## Identity
- Unreviewed TrEMBL entry "MARVEL domain-containing protein"; four transmembrane helices (12-35, 94-116, 128-150, 190-212) within a MARVEL domain (12-216); PANTHER PTHR10306:SF17, synaptophysin/synaptobrevin family (from `sph-1-uniprot.txt`). SPH-1 is the single C. elegans physin, alongside the single gyrin SNG-1 and SCAMP SCM-1 [PMID:16698939 "Each of the three mammalian TVP families, SCAMPs, physins, and gyrins, are represented by a single family member in C. elegans : SCM-1, SPH-1, and SNG-1, respectively ( 2 )."]. SPH-1 has a predicted hydrophobic N-terminal extension absent from murine physins.

## Expression and localisation (the key worm-specific finding)
- Promoter-GFP: sph-1 is expressed mostly in pharyngeal and anal sphincter muscle, not pan-neuronally like sng-1 [PMID:16698939 "Synaptogyrin is expressed pan-neuronally ( 22 ). Synaptophysin is mostly localized to the muscles of the pharynx and anal sphincter."]. Translational fusions are punctate, consistent with vesicle association [PMID:16698939 "Translational fusions further revealed a punctate distribution, suggesting that these proteins are associated with vesicles (data not shown)."]. The authors caution that promoter fusions may be incomplete.
- In human epithelial cells the three worm TVPs colocalise in the same vesicular compartment [PMID:16698939 "Fluorescence microscopy showed a considerable degree of colocalization of all three TVPs ( Fig. 1 )."].

## Function
- Null alleles (ox277, ox278) and scm-1; sph-1; sng-1 triple mutants develop normally, have normal neuronal architecture and synapse density, normal pharmacology/chemotaxis/thermotaxis, and no electrophysiological phenotype at the NMJ [PMID:16698939 "The morphologies and gross behaviors of all mutant worms were remarkably unaffected."; "The gross density of synaptic varicosities in the dorsal nerve cord of wild-type and mutant worms was similar (35.5 ± 3.4 vs. 34.7 ± 2.2 per 100 μm)"]. Cholesterol depletion did not reveal a brood-size phenotype in the mutants [PMID:16698939 "we examined the influence of cholesterol depletion on brood size but could not detect any abnormalities (Fig. 9)."]. A small increase in clathrin-coated vesicles was noted [PMID:16698939 "we do find a small increase in clathrin-coated vesicles in the TVP mutants, suggesting that a compensatory nonclathrin pathway might be compromised in TVP mutants."].
- Conclusion of the primary study: TVPs are conserved but not needed for synaptogenesis or neuronal function in C. elegans.

## Curation notes
- The GOA "synaptic vesicle" (IEA, InterPro) and "synaptic vesicle membrane" (IBA, physin node PTN000033897) rows transfer the mammalian synaptophysin localisation; in worm, sph-1 expression is mainly non-neuronal (pharyngeal/anal sphincter muscle) and the protein is dispensable for synaptic function, so these are treated as over-annotations rather than removed (promoter fusions can miss neuronal expression).
- The synaptic-vesicle-cycle module lists sph-1 as the worm exemplar of the synaptophysin membrane-organiser annoton (cholesterol binding; synaptic vesicle maturation). The worm evidence does not support a synaptic role for SPH-1; the gyrin SNG-1 is the pan-neuronal TVP in C. elegans.

## Corroborating localisation detail (deep research, thesis-derived)
A translational SPH-1::YFP reporter was reported to be punctate and vesicular, "detected in pharyngeal epithelial cells and some pharyngeal neurons, the intestinal valve, intestine, excretory canal and duct cells, rectal gland and epithelial cells, anal sphincter, and weakly in coelomocytes and the nervous system" [file:worm/sph-1/sph-1-deep-research-falcon.md], with apical polarisation in pharynx and intestine from the twofold embryonic stage. The underlying source is a doctoral thesis (Abraham 2007) rather than a peer-reviewed paper, so it is treated as corroboration of PMID:16698939 rather than independent evidence — but it agrees with the promoter-GFP result that sph-1 is predominantly non-neuronal and epithelial/muscle, and it adds that some weak neuronal expression does exist. The same source reports that triple TVP mutants have normal synaptic vesicle diameter, density and positioning with a small increase in clathrin-coated vesicles, and normal evoked and spontaneous transmission (n=3).
