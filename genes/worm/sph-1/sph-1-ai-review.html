Gene Description

SPH-1 is the single Caenorhabditis elegans physin, a tetraspan (MARVEL-domain) vesicle membrane protein of the synaptophysin/synaptoporin family. Unlike mammalian synaptophysin, and unlike the worm synaptogyrin SNG-1 which is pan-neuronal, sph-1 is expressed mostly in the muscles of the pharynx and anal sphincter, where the protein shows a punctate, vesicle-associated distribution; when expressed in mammalian cells it shares a vesicular compartment with the other worm tetraspan vesicle proteins SNG-1 and SCM-1. Null mutants, and even scm-1 sph-1 sng-1 triple mutants, develop normally with normal neuronal architecture, synapse density, behaviour and neuromuscular electrophysiology, showing that tetraspan vesicle proteins are dispensable for synaptogenesis and neurotransmission in the worm. The molecular function of SPH-1 in muscle secretory vesicles is unknown.

Functional annotation report: Caenorhabditis elegans sph-1 (UniProt O44511)

Executive conclusion

The requested target is correctly identified as C. elegans sph-1, also designated F42G8.11, encoding the worm synaptophysin/Physin-family protein SPH-1. It is not sphk-1, the unrelated sphingosine-kinase gene that appears in some searches for similar symbols. The F42G8 genomic context, synaptophysin-family assignment, four-transmembrane topology, and characterized sph-1(ox277/ox278) alleles all agree with UniProt O44511 and the supplied MARVEL/synaptophysin-domain annotation (abraham2007expressionsundfunktionsanalysenvon" pages 88-91, abraham2007expressionsundfunktionsanalysenvon"b pages 110-113).

The most defensible current annotation is that SPH-1 is a non-enzymatic, four-pass vesicle/apical membrane protein that helps organize membrane microdomains or modulate vesicle trafficking and recycling. No catalytic reaction, transported substrate, or directly demonstrated ligand is known. Its precise molecular mechanism remains unresolved. Evidence for vesicular and apical localization is considerably stronger than evidence assigning it to a particular biochemical pathway (abraham2007expressionsundfunktionsanalysenvon" pages 75-81, abraham2007expressionsundfunktionsanalysenvon"b pages 110-113, abraham2007expressionsundfunktionsanalysenvon"b pages 119-122).

1. Identity verification and nomenclature

Exact-match literature calls the F42G8 locus sph-1 and its product the C. elegans synaptophysin. SPH-1 belongs to the Physin/synaptophysin class of tetraspan vesicle membrane proteins and has four predicted transmembrane segments, consistent with the supplied MARVEL (PF01284/IPR008253) and synaptophysin/porin (IPR001285) annotations (abraham2007expressionsundfunktionsanalysenvon" pages 88-91, abraham2007expressionsundfunktionsanalysenvon"b pages 110-113).

This identity should not be confused with:

  • C. elegans sphk-1, which encodes sphingosine kinase and participates in sphingolipid signaling; it is not O44511.
  • Mammalian Sph1/SYP, homologous synaptophysin genes whose phenotypes cannot automatically be assigned to the worm protein.
  • Unrelated SPH-1 symbols used in other organisms.

The database description “MARVEL domain-containing protein” and the literature description “synaptophysin” are therefore compatible rather than contradictory. “Synaptobrevin family,” as stated in the supplied metadata, should be interpreted cautiously: SPH-1 is structurally a synaptophysin/Physin-family tetraspan protein, not the v-SNARE synaptobrevin itself.

2. Protein architecture and likely molecular role

SPH-1 is a compact integral membrane protein with four membrane-spanning segments. This architecture is characteristic of MARVEL-domain proteins, a broad class associated with membrane apposition, membrane-domain organization, and trafficking. The sph-1(ox278) deletion removes 539 bp encompassing exons 3 and 4 and sequence encoding three of the four transmembrane segments; ox277 removes much of exon 3 and sequence encoding two transmembrane segments (abraham2007expressionsundfunktionsanalysenvon" pages 88-91).

These mutations strongly disrupt the predicted membrane topology, but ox278 was not proven to be a molecular null: residual transcript was detected, and inconsistent immunoblotting did not definitively exclude a truncated product. Consequently, weak phenotypes cannot be interpreted unambiguously as proof that SPH-1 has no function (abraham2007expressionsundfunktionsanalysenvon"b pages 122-126).

SPH-1 is neither an enzyme nor a conventional solute transporter. There is no established substrate specificity or catalytic reaction. Its likely role is structural or organizational: concentrating in small vesicles or specialized apical membranes and influencing membrane-protein organization, vesicle formation, retrieval, or recycling. Direct binding of worm SPH-1 to synaptobrevin/SNB-1 has been proposed by analogy with mammalian synaptophysin but was not demonstrated in the retrieved exact-gene studies (abraham2007expressionsundfunktionsanalysenvon"b pages 119-122).

3. Cellular and tissue localization

Native-worm reporter evidence

SPH-1::YFP had a punctate distribution characteristic of vesicular compartments. In adult worms, it was detected in pharyngeal epithelial cells and some pharyngeal neurons, the intestinal valve, intestine, excretory canal and duct cells, rectal gland and epithelial cells, anal sphincter, and weakly in coelomocytes and the nervous system (abraham2007expressionsundfunktionsanalysenvon" pages 75-81, abraham2007expressionsundfunktionsanalysenvon" pages 85-88).

The strongest spatial result was in intestinal cells: SPH-1::YFP was concentrated at the apical surface facing the lumen, apical to the IFB-2-positive terminal web, while lateral cell boundaries lacked comparable SPH-1 signal. Thus, SPH-1 is not simply a pan-neuronal synaptic-vesicle marker; it is prominently associated with polarized epithelial and secretory membranes (abraham2007expressionsundfunktionsanalysenvon" pages 75-81).

During embryogenesis, reporter expression began around gastrulation in the developing pharynx and intestine. By approximately the twofold stage, the signal became apically polarized in the pharynx, intestinal valve, and intestine. This supports a developmental role in establishing or maintaining specialized apical membrane compartments, although reporter localization alone does not establish necessity (abraham2007expressionsundfunktionsanalysenvon" pages 81-85, abraham2007expressionsundfunktionsanalysenvon" pages 85-88).

Heterologous-cell evidence

When expressed in human PLC epithelial cells, SPH-1::CFP entered intracellular vesicular compartments and partially colocalized with worm SNG-1 and SCM-1 reporters. Pearson correlations were approximately 0.67 with SNG-1 and 0.62 with SCM-1. This supports an intrinsic vesicular-sorting property, but heterologous overexpression is weaker evidence than endogenous localization (abraham2007expressionsundfunktionsanalysenvon" pages 73-75).

4. Biological processes and pathway placement

Vesicle trafficking and recycling

The best-supported process-level assignment is membrane-vesicle organization and recycling, including synaptic-vesicle trafficking in neurons and analogous vesicular/apical trafficking in epithelial cells. This conclusion rests on family architecture, punctate localization, and mutant ultrastructure rather than a defined linear signaling pathway (abraham2007expressionsundfunktionsanalysenvon"b pages 110-113, abraham2007expressionsundfunktionsanalysenvon"b pages 119-122).

In animals lacking SPH-1 together with the related tetraspan vesicle proteins SNG-1 and SCM-1, synaptic-vesicle size, density, and positioning remained largely normal. Mean vesicle diameter was 26.66 ± 0.19 nm in the triple mutant versus 27.82 ± 0.20 nm in wild type; vesicle density was 152 ± 8 versus 163 ± 13 vesicles/µm² (P=0.463). However, clathrin-coated vesicles increased from 0.74 ± 0.15% of vesicles in wild type to 1.93 ± 0.35% in the triple mutant (P=0.006) (abraham2007expressionsundfunktionsanalysenvon"b pages 101-106).

This increase is compatible with altered endocytic recycling or compensatory reliance on clathrin-mediated retrieval. It does not, by itself, prove that SPH-1 specifically operates in a clathrin-independent pathway, because the experiment removed three proteins and the accumulation of coated intermediates could reflect altered formation, maturation, or uncoating (abraham2007expressionsundfunktionsanalysenvon"b pages 101-106, abraham2007expressionsundfunktionsanalysenvon"b pages 119-122).

Neurotransmission and inhibitory-circuit robustness

Basal neuromuscular transmission was largely preserved in the triple mutant. No significant differences were detected in evoked responses (P=0.324), spontaneous fusion (P=0.321), or endogenous fusion-event amplitude (P=0.287), although the reported electrophysiological sample size was small (n=3) (abraham2007expressionsundfunktionsanalysenvon"b pages 101-106).

A conditional phenotype emerged with pentylenetetrazole, a GABA-receptor antagonist. At 8 mg/ml, fewer than 40% of sph-1(ox278) animals contracted, but the response differed significantly from control (P=0.005) and was weaker than that of other tetraspan-vesicle-protein mutants. This suggests a modest contribution to inhibitory-circuit robustness or vesicle cycling under pharmacological stress, not proof of a direct role in GABA release (abraham2007expressionsundfunktionsanalysenvon"b pages 101-106, abraham2007expressionsundfunktionsanalysenvon"b pages 106-110).

Reporter studies did not clearly establish SPH-1 expression in the relevant GABAergic neurons, and residual ox278 product could not be excluded. The PTZ result may therefore reflect low-level neuronal expression, non-cell-autonomous effects, or broader membrane-trafficking changes (abraham2007expressionsundfunktionsanalysenvon"b pages 122-126).

5. Organismal phenotype and redundancy

Even simultaneous loss of sph-1, sng-1, and scm-1 produced no large baseline defect in locomotion, mechanosensation, chemotaxis, adaptation, thermotaxis, osmotic avoidance, brood production, neuronal anatomy, or synaptobrevin-cluster distribution. Pharyngeal pumping was 152.8 ± 3.5 pumps/min in wild type and 156 ± 2.5 in the triple mutant (P=0.456); the defecation-cycle difference was also nonsignificant (P=0.134) (abraham2007expressionsundfunktionsanalysenvon" pages 91-95, abraham2007expressionsundfunktionsanalysenvon" pages 95-101).

These negative results argue that SPH-1 is not essential for basic synaptogenesis, vesicle production, or routine neurotransmission under laboratory conditions. They do not establish dispensability in every tissue or environmental condition. Functional compensation may involve proteins outside this three-member set, and the assays had limited ability to detect subtle kinetics, cell-specific defects, or responses to physiological stress (abraham2007expressionsundfunktionsanalysenvon"b pages 101-106, abraham2007expressionsundfunktionsanalysenvon"b pages 122-126).

7. Recent developments, 2023–2024

Ambiguity-controlled searches using “O44511,” “F42G8.11,” “sph-1,” “C. elegans synaptophysin,” and mutant nomenclature did not identify a 2023–2024 publication that experimentally resolved SPH-1’s molecular mechanism. The principal direct functional evidence remains the 2006–2008 experimental corpus. This is an important negative finding: recent papers concerning sphk-1, mammalian synaptophysin, or unrelated SPH-1 proteins should not be used to update this exact annotation.

Accordingly, no recent evidence establishes a catalytic activity, transported substrate, direct SPH-1–SNB-1 complex, cryo-EM structure, endogenous interactome, or cell-specific rescue mechanism for O44511. Modern single-cell or expression resources may contain sph-1 measurements, but expression alone would not resolve biochemical function.

8. Current applications and real-world relevance

SPH-1 presently has value primarily as a basic-research model rather than a translational target. Relevant applications include:

  1. Studying how MARVEL/synaptophysin-family proteins are sorted into vesicular and apical membranes.
  2. Testing redundancy among tetraspan vesicle proteins using sph-1, sng-1, and scm-1 mutants.
  3. Examining membrane retrieval under stress by electron microscopy, electrophysiology, or pharmacological perturbation.
  4. Comparing neuronal synaptic-vesicle organization with polarized epithelial trafficking in the genetically tractable worm.

No validated clinical, diagnostic, agricultural, or drug-development application for C. elegans SPH-1 was identified. Its chief broader relevance is evolutionary: the worm offers a reduced genetic system for investigating conserved synaptophysin-family membrane organization.

9. Expert assessment and recommended annotation

A calibrated database-style annotation would be:

SPH-1 is a four-pass MARVEL/synaptophysin-family integral membrane protein localized to intracellular vesicles and specialized apical membranes. It likely acts as a non-catalytic organizer or modulator of vesicle trafficking/recycling. It is not essential for gross synaptic-vesicle biogenesis or basal neurotransmission under standard conditions, but contributes modestly to endocytic balance or neural-circuit robustness under stress.

Confidence is high for gene identity and predicted four-pass membrane architecture, moderate to high for vesicular/apical localization, moderate for a broad trafficking/organizational role, and low for any specific direct partner or defined endocytic mechanism.

📚 Additional Documentation

Notes

(sph-1-notes.md)

sph-1 (UniProt O44511, TrEMBL) — synaptophysin (physin-family tetraspan vesicle protein), C. elegans

Identity

  • Unreviewed TrEMBL entry "MARVEL domain-containing protein"; four transmembrane helices (12-35, 94-116, 128-150, 190-212) within a MARVEL domain (12-216); PANTHER PTHR10306:SF17, synaptophysin/synaptobrevin family (from sph-1-uniprot.txt). SPH-1 is the single C. elegans physin, alongside the single gyrin SNG-1 and SCAMP SCM-1 PMID:16698939. SPH-1 has a predicted hydrophobic N-terminal extension absent from murine physins.

Expression and localisation (the key worm-specific finding)

  • Promoter-GFP: sph-1 is expressed mostly in pharyngeal and anal sphincter muscle, not pan-neuronally like sng-1 PMID:16698939. Translational fusions are punctate, consistent with vesicle association PMID:16698939. The authors caution that promoter fusions may be incomplete.
  • In human epithelial cells the three worm TVPs colocalise in the same vesicular compartment PMID:16698939.

Function

  • Null alleles (ox277, ox278) and scm-1; sph-1; sng-1 triple mutants develop normally, have normal neuronal architecture and synapse density, normal pharmacology/chemotaxis/thermotaxis, and no electrophysiological phenotype at the NMJ [PMID:16698939 "The morphologies and gross behaviors of all mutant worms were remarkably unaffected."; "The gross density of synaptic varicosities in the dorsal nerve cord of wild-type and mutant worms was similar (35.5 ± 3.4 vs. 34.7 ± 2.2 per 100 μm)"]. Cholesterol depletion did not reveal a brood-size phenotype in the mutants PMID:16698939. A small increase in clathrin-coated vesicles was noted PMID:16698939.
  • Conclusion of the primary study: TVPs are conserved but not needed for synaptogenesis or neuronal function in C. elegans.

Curation notes

  • The GOA "synaptic vesicle" (IEA, InterPro) and "synaptic vesicle membrane" (IBA, physin node PTN000033897) rows transfer the mammalian synaptophysin localisation; in worm, sph-1 expression is mainly non-neuronal (pharyngeal/anal sphincter muscle) and the protein is dispensable for synaptic function, so these are treated as over-annotations rather than removed (promoter fusions can miss neuronal expression).
  • The synaptic-vesicle-cycle module lists sph-1 as the worm exemplar of the synaptophysin membrane-organiser annoton (cholesterol binding; synaptic vesicle maturation). The worm evidence does not support a synaptic role for SPH-1; the gyrin SNG-1 is the pan-neuronal TVP in C. elegans.

Corroborating localisation detail (deep research, thesis-derived)

A translational SPH-1::YFP reporter was reported to be punctate and vesicular, "detected in pharyngeal epithelial cells and some pharyngeal neurons, the intestinal valve, intestine, excretory canal and duct cells, rectal gland and epithelial cells, anal sphincter, and weakly in coelomocytes and the nervous system" [file:worm/sph-1/sph-1-deep-research-falcon.md], with apical polarisation in pharynx and intestine from the twofold embryonic stage. The underlying source is a doctoral thesis (Abraham 2007) rather than a peer-reviewed paper, so it is treated as corroboration of PMID:16698939 rather than independent evidence — but it agrees with the promoter-GFP result that sph-1 is predominantly non-neuronal and epithelial/muscle, and it adds that some weak neuronal expression does exist. The same source reports that triple TVP mutants have normal synaptic vesicle diameter, density and positioning with a small increase in clathrin-coated vesicles, and normal evoked and spontaneous transmission (n=3).
