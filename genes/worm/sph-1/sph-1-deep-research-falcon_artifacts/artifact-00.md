| Identity and architecture | The *C. elegans* locus *sph-1* was mapped to cosmid F42G8 and characterized as the worm synaptophysin/Physin homolog. SPH-1 is a tetraspan membrane protein with four transmembrane segments, consistent with its MARVEL/synaptophysin-family annotation (pqac-00000011, pqac-00000014) | Sequence correction/comparison and locus-specific mutant characterization | Confirms that the relevant target is *sph-1/F42G8.11* (UniProt O44511), not the similarly named sphingosine-kinase gene *sphk-1* | **High** for identity and four-pass topology; MARVEL classification is principally sequence/domain based rather than demonstrated by a solved SPH-1 structure |
| SPH-1-specific localization | SPH-1::YFP showed punctate, vesicle-like labeling in the pharynx and strong apical enrichment in intestinal cells, lying apical to the IFB-2-positive terminal web. Signal also occurred in pharyngeal epithelium and neurons, the intestinal valve, excretory canal and duct cells, rectal gland and epithelial cells, anal sphincter, weakly in coelomocytes, and weakly in the nervous system (pqac-00000008, pqac-00000010) | Transcriptional and translational fluorescent reporters in living transgenic worms; confocal colocalization with IFB-2 | SPH-1 is a vesicular/apical membrane protein with substantial non-neuronal expression, especially in polarized secretory or epithelial tissues; it is not exclusively a neuronal synaptic-vesicle marker | **Moderate–high** for reporter localization; extrachromosomal transgenes and fluorescent tags can alter expression level or trafficking, and endogenous-protein confirmation was limited |
| Heterologous vesicular sorting | SPH-1::CFP displayed vesicular distribution in transfected human PLC cells and overlapped with worm SNG-1 and SCM-1 reporters; pairwise Pearson correlation with SNG-1 was 0.67 and with SCM-1 was 0.62 (pqac-00000007) | Ectopic expression in cultured human epithelial cells | Supports an intrinsic capacity for sorting into intracellular vesicular compartments and possible overlapping trafficking with other tetraspan vesicle proteins | **Moderate–low** for native function because the host cell and expression level were non-physiological |
| *sph-1(ox278)* allele architecture | *ox278* deletes 539 bp encompassing exons 3 and 4, including sequence encoding three of four transmembrane segments, plus adjacent noncoding regions; *ox277* removes much of exon 3 and sequence encoding two transmembrane segments (pqac-00000011) | PCR/Southern-supported deletion-allele mapping | *ox278* should severely disrupt normal SPH-1 membrane topology and is commonly treated as a strong loss-of-function allele | **Moderate**: residual *sph-1* transcript and inconsistent immunoblot results mean production of a residual product was not definitively excluded (pqac-00000017) |
| Baseline organismal phenotypes—combined TVP loss | Worms lacking SPH-1, SNG-1, and SCM-1 showed no significant defects in locomotion, mechanosensation, pharyngeal pumping (wild type 152.8 ± 3.5 versus mutant 156 ± 2.5 pumps/min; *P*=0.456), defecation, chemotaxis, adaptation, thermotaxis, osmotic avoidance, brood production, or neuronal anatomy under standard conditions (pqac-00000012, pqac-00000013) | Backcrossed tetraspan-vesicle-protein triple mutants | SPH-1 and related proteins are not individually or collectively indispensable for basic development, behavior, or gross neural function under routine laboratory conditions; redundancy outside this protein trio or conditional roles remain possible | **High** for lack of large baseline phenotypes in the tested assays; **low** for assigning any result specifically to *sph-1* because these are triple-mutant data |
| Synaptic-vesicle ultrastructure—triple mutant | Mean vesicle diameter was 26.66 ± 0.19 nm in the triple mutant versus 27.82 ± 0.20 nm in wild type; vesicle density was 152 ± 8 versus 163 ± 13 vesicles/µm² (*P*=0.463). Vesicle positioning was unchanged, but clathrin-coated vesicles increased from 0.74 ± 0.15% (16 terminals) to 1.93 ± 0.35% (21 terminals; *P*=0.006) (pqac-00000015) | Electron microscopy of neuromuscular synapses in *sph-1; sng-1; scm-1* mutants | The protein trio is dispensable for normal vesicle size, density, and active-zone distribution but may modulate endocytic recycling or the balance between clathrin-dependent and other retrieval routes | **Moderate** for a collective TVP recycling role; **low** for an SPH-1-specific mechanism. Increased coated vesicles do not by themselves establish a clathrin-independent pathway defect |
| Synaptic physiology—triple mutant | No significant differences were detected in evoked responses (*P*=0.324), spontaneous fusion (*P*=0.321), or endogenous fusion-event amplitude (*P*=0.287; reported *n*=3) (pqac-00000015) | Electrophysiological recordings from *sph-1; sng-1; scm-1* neuromuscular junctions | Basal neurotransmitter release remains largely intact without all three tetraspan vesicle proteins | **Moderate–low** because sample size was small and subtle, cell-type-specific, or stress-dependent effects could have been missed; not SPH-1-specific |
| SPH-1-specific PTZ phenotype | At 8 mg/ml pentylenetetrazole, fewer than 40% of *sph-1(ox278)* animals contracted, but this was significantly different from control (*P*=0.005) and weaker than phenotypes of the other TVP single mutants (pqac-00000015, pqac-00000018) | Pharmacological challenge with a GABA-receptor antagonist | Provides conditional evidence that SPH-1 contributes modestly to inhibitory/GABAergic circuit robustness or vesicle cycling under neural stress | **Low–moderate**: the assay is indirect, reporter studies did not clearly establish SPH-1 in GABAergic neurons, residual ox278 product cannot be excluded, and the molecular step is unknown (pqac-00000017) |
| Primary functional annotation | No enzymatic reaction, transported substrate, or direct ligand has been demonstrated. The strongest defensible annotation is a four-pass vesicle/apical membrane organizer or trafficking modulator, potentially affecting vesicle recycling rather than catalysis or solute transport (pqac-00000014, pqac-00000016) | Integration of family/domain similarity, reporter localization, mutant EM, electrophysiology, and pharmacology | SPH-1 most likely performs a structural/organizational role in membrane microdomains or vesicle trafficking, with nonessential or context-dependent activity | **Moderate** for broad structural/trafficking role; **low** for any precise molecular mechanism, direct binding partner, or specific endocytic pathway |
| 2023–2024 evidence status | Exact-target searches for O44511, F42G8.11, *sph-1*, and *C. elegans* synaptophysin yielded no 2023–2024 study that experimentally resolved this protein’s molecular mechanism | Ambiguity-controlled literature search excluding *sphk-1* and non-worm SPH-1 usages | Current annotation still depends mainly on the 2006–2008 experimental corpus; no recent exact-gene mechanistic advance or validated translational application was identified | **Moderate** as a search-based negative finding, not proof that no mention exists in every dataset or unindexed publication |
