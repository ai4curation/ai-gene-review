Gene Description

TMEM-107 (F39B2.9) is the Caenorhabditis elegans ortholog of human TMEM107, a small four-pass integral membrane protein of the ciliary transition zone (TZ). It is expressed only in ciliated sensory neurons, under control of the RFX transcription factor DAF-19, and is anchored, immobile, in the TZ membrane, where super-resolution imaging shows it in periodic ring-like subdomains. TMEM-107 is a component of the MKS module. Its own TZ localization depends on MKS-5 and the intermediate-layer MKS proteins, and it in turn organizes the recruitment of a submodule of MKS proteins (MKS-1, TMEM-231, JBTS-14/TMEM237 and TMEM-17) to the TZ. This recruitment depends on its transmembrane helices rather than its short cytosolic termini. tmem-107 single mutants have structurally normal cilia but a selectively leaky ciliary membrane diffusion barrier (TRAM-1 enters cilia). In combination with nphp-4 loss, the mutants lose Y-link connectors, TZs fail to dock at the membrane, cilia are truncated or missing, and phasmid dendrites are short. Human TMEM107 mutations cause Joubert and oral-facial-digital syndromes.

Core Functions

Integral membrane subunit of the MKS module at the ciliary transition zone. It is anchored in the TZ membrane, recruits a submodule of MKS proteins (MKS-1, TMEM-231, JBTS-14, TMEM-17), and acts redundantly with the NPHP module to build Y-link connectors, dock the TZ at the membrane and maintain the ciliary membrane diffusion barrier. Its molecular activity is unknown, so no MF is asserted.
