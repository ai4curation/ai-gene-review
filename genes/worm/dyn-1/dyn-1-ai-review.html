Gene Description

DYN-1 is the single classical dynamin of Caenorhabditis elegans, a large GTPase with an N-terminal GTPase domain, middle domain, pleckstrin-homology lipid-binding domain, GTPase effector domain and C-terminal proline-rich domain. Purified DYN-1 hydrolyses GTP, and negatively charged lipids stimulate its activity to the level of rat dynamin; self-assembly on membranes and GTP hydrolysis together set its residence on target membranes. Its core role is membrane fission: it pinches clathrin-coated vesicles from the plasma membrane, so that the temperature-sensitive GTPase-domain mutant dyn-1(ky51) is a shibire-like reversible paralytic with blocked synaptic vesicle recycling, yolk endocytosis by oocytes and coelomocyte uptake. DYN-1 is concentrated at synapses, on the apical membrane of intestinal cells and at the periciliary membrane of sensory neurons. In engulfing cells it is recruited to pseudopods and nascent phagosomes downstream of the CED-1/CED-6/CED-7 receptor pathway, where it recruits the PI3-kinase VPS-34 and the GTPases RAB-5 and RAB-7 to drive phagosome maturation and degradation of apoptotic corpses; its proline-rich domain binds SH3 proteins such as the sorting nexin LST-4 and syndapin SDPN-1. DYN-1 also localizes to cleavage furrows and the midbody and is needed for late cytokinesis, and its endocytic activity contributes to the execution of necrotic neurodegeneration.

Functional annotation report: C. elegans dyn-1 / DYN-1

Executive conclusion

The identity check is consistent: dyn-1 in the relevant literature is the Caenorhabditis elegans conventional dynamin, matching the user-supplied UniProt record P39055, ORF C02C6.1, and its classification as a TRAFAC-class dynamin-like GTPase. No results for a same-named gene in another organism were used as evidence for the worm protein. One provenance caveat is important: the exact P39055–C02C6.1 mapping and InterPro-domain list come from the supplied UniProt record; the retrieved primary-paper excerpts independently confirm C. elegans dyn-1 as dynamin but do not themselves display that accession mapping.

DYN-1 is best annotated as a membrane-remodeling mechanochemical GTPase. Its primary activity is hydrolysis of GTP—canonically, GTP + H₂O → GDP + inorganic phosphate, EC 3.6.5.5—coupled to dynamin assembly and membrane constriction/fission. In the worm, its best-supported functions are the severing of clathrin-coated plasma-membrane invaginations and recycling-endosome tubules, synaptic-membrane recycling, and recruitment of membrane-trafficking machinery during phagosome maturation. It is therefore not simply a signaling GTPase: it is an oligomerizing membrane-remodeling enzyme whose nucleotide cycle is converted into mechanical work. However, the retrieved evidence did not include a purified-C. elegans-DYN-1 kinetic assay, so the precise reaction and substrate specificity are strongly supported by family/UniProt annotation rather than newly measured worm-specific enzymology.

1. Identity, family, and domain interpretation

The user-provided annotation places DYN-1 in the dynamin-like TRAFAC GTPase family and identifies Dynamin, Dynamin_GTPase, Dynamin_GTPase_CS, Dynamin_N, and Dynamin_stalk signatures. This is fully consistent with primary literature describing dyn-1 as the C. elegans conventional dynamin and using loss-of-function alleles, RNAi, fluorescent DYN-1 fusions, and the temperature-sensitive dyn-1(ky51) allele. A genetic study recovered 14 loss-of-function alleles, many associated with embryonic lethality and persistent cell corpses, providing strong locus-specific evidence that the literature is studying the intended worm gene. (yu2006c.elegansdynamin pages 1-2)

Functionally, the N-terminal GTPase module supplies nucleotide-dependent activity, while the stalk/GED regions support oligomerization. Dynamin-family PH domains bind acidic phospholipid-containing membranes, and the proline-rich C-terminal region provides interaction sites for SH3-domain adaptors. Direct worm experiments specifically showed that a C-terminal DYN-1 region containing PH, GED, and PRD elements binds NCK-1 and is used to recruit DYN-1 to recycling endosomes. (gao2020anehbp1sid3dyn1axis pages 10-12)

Thus, DYN-1’s most defensible molecular description is: a cytosolic large GTPase that is transiently recruited to selected membranes, oligomerizes at constricted membrane domains, and promotes membrane fission while also organizing local trafficking complexes.

2. Primary biochemical and structural role

Catalytic reaction and specificity

DYN-1 is a guanosine-triphosphate hydrolase rather than a transporter or conventional metabolic enzyme. Its nucleotide substrate is GTP, not a broad range of cellular cargoes; GDP and inorganic phosphate are the expected products. Membrane lipid is therefore not a chemical substrate in the enzymatic sense. Instead, GTP binding, assembly, hydrolysis, and product release control conformational and oligomeric changes that remodel the associated membrane.

The most conservative interpretation is that DYN-1 has two coupled activities:

  1. Assembly/scaffolding: recruitment and helical or collar-like oligomerization at curved membrane necks or tubules.
  2. GTP-dependent mechanochemistry: constriction and/or destabilization of those necks, resulting in fission.

The worm literature directly supports membrane recruitment, oligomeric dynamin-family behavior, and fission phenotypes, but the retrieved excerpts do not provide worm-specific turnover numbers, nucleotide-binding constants, or a direct structural determination. Consequently, exact enzymatic kinetics should not be inferred from mammalian dynamin values.

Clathrin-coated-vesicle scission

In the C. elegans intestine, live imaging places DYN-1 at basolateral endocytic puncta together with WIP-1, DBN-1/Abp1, and actin machinery. DBN-1 interaction with F-actin is required for efficient DYN-1 recruitment. Loss or inactivation of DYN-1 leaves elongated endocytic intermediates, while RME-1 accumulates on and stabilizes the arrested tubules. The resulting model is that actin supplies force against the invaginated membrane and DYN-1 performs or completes neck fission to release a clathrin-coated vesicle. Experiments included at least 18 animals per group in three replicates, with major effects reaching P < 0.0001. (shi2019wip1anddbn1 pages 7-8)

Recycling-endosome tubule fission

DYN-1 also acts away from the plasma membrane. In intestinal epithelial cells, it is recruited to tubular recycling endosomes through an EHBP-1 → SID-3 → NCK-1 → DYN-1 cascade. DYN-1 labeling fell approximately 6.5-fold in sid-3 mutants, 5.1-fold in nck-1 mutants, and 2.8-fold after ehbp-1 RNAi. SID-3/DYN-1 colocalization decreased from about 0.74 to 0.39 without NCK-1. DYN-1 depletion caused approximately 11–15-fold cargo accumulation, whereas DYN-1 overexpression reduced the sid-3 cargo-accumulation phenotype about 10.5-fold. These data place DYN-1 downstream in the pathway and support a direct role in severing recycling tubules. Published 8 May 2020; DOI: https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pgen.1008763. (gao2020anehbp1sid3dyn1axis pages 10-12)

3. Cellular localization

DYN-1 is not best described as residing permanently in one organelle. It cycles between cytosolic and transient membrane-associated pools and is recruited to:

  • basolateral clathrin-coated endocytic sites in intestinal epithelia;
  • tubular recycling endosomes;
  • neuronal membrane-retrieval intermediates;
  • extending phagocytic pseudopods, cups, and nascent phagosomes;
  • mitotic spindle or midzone-associated structures during cytokinesis;
  • injured neurons in the functional context of regenerative axonal fusion.

Localization is determined by adaptors, phospholipid-binding domains, actin-associated factors, and pathway-specific receptors rather than by an integral membrane anchor. This transient localization explains why DYN-1 can execute the same core membrane-remodeling chemistry in multiple cellular processes.

4. Principal pathways and biological processes

4.1 Apoptotic-cell engulfment and phagosome maturation

This is one of the most experimentally developed non-neuronal functions. DYN-1 acts in the CED-7–CED-1–CED-6 phagocytic-receptor pathway. Following recognition of an apoptotic cell, activated CED-1 marks the engulfment site and, through CED-6, promotes transient DYN-1 recruitment to extending pseudopods and the nascent phagosome. DYN-1 arrives before HGRS-1-positive endosomal vesicles and is proposed to organize vesicle production or delivery to the phagocytic membrane. Loss of dyn-1 disrupts those vesicles and their recruitment, compromising corpse internalization and/or subsequent degradation. Published June 2006; DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.devcel.2006.04.007. (yu2006c.elegansdynamin pages 13-14, yu2006c.elegansdynamin pages 1-2, yu2006c.elegansdynamin pages 11-13)

The evidence distinguishes this role from generic endocytosis. DYN-1 is recruited in response to phagocytic-receptor signaling and functions in parallel to the CED-5/CED-10/CED-12 Rac pathway in the original genetic model. DYN-1-dependent focal membrane delivery may support pseudopod extension and later supply endosomal or lysosomal components to maturing phagosomes. (yu2006c.elegansdynamin pages 13-14)

There is, however, an important experimental disagreement. RNAi experiments reported that approximately 40% of corpses remained unengulfed, supporting roles in both engulfment and degradation. Acute analysis of dyn-1(ky51) instead found normal CED-1/CED-6 recruitment, apparently closed phagocytic cups, and accumulated receptor-positive phagosomes—evidence that the immediate defect was primarily post-internalization, at early phagosome maturation. Those experiments were performed 12 hours after transfer to 25°C because longer inactivation produced gross actin abnormalities that could cause secondary engulfment defects. Published April 2008; DOI: https://doi.org/10.1038/ncb1718. (kinchen2008apathwayfor pages 16-19, kinchen2008apathwayfor pages 19-22)

The expert synthesis is therefore that DYN-1 is securely required for early phagosome maturation and vesicle recruitment, while the magnitude of its direct contribution to cup extension and closure depends on allele, depletion method, and time after inactivation. In wild type, all 113 developmentally apoptotic somatic cells have been removed by the four-fold embryonic stage, emphasizing the efficiency of the pathway that dyn-1 helps sustain. (yu2006c.elegansdynamin pages 1-2)

4.2 Synaptic-vesicle membrane recycling

Dynamin’s canonical neuronal role is the fission of endocytic intermediates needed to replenish synaptic vesicles. In intact worms, optogenetic stimulation and rapid freezing revealed exceptionally fast membrane retrieval at neuromuscular junctions: invaginations occurred by 20 ms in about half of synapses, persisted at 30 ms, and disappeared by 50 ms. Large vesicles peaked near the dense projection at 50 ms, shifted toward the varicosity center at 100–300 ms, and returned to baseline by 3 s. Docked vesicles fell from 2.5 ± 0.1 to 0.9 ± 0.1 per profile, and vesicle replenishment had an approximately 2-second time constant. Published 3 September 2013; DOI: https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.00723. (watanabe2013ultrafastendocytosisat pages 7-8, watanabe2013ultrafastendocytosisat pages 1-2)

These kinetics define the physiological pathway in which DYN-1 operates, but an evidence boundary is necessary: the retrieved passages from that study did not themselves contain a DYN-1 perturbation or localization experiment. They therefore provide strong synaptic context rather than independent proof of every DYN-1-specific mechanistic step. The direct intestinal scission and genetic synaptic literature together support DYN-1 as the fission machinery, but the exact point at which it resolves ultrafast endosomes into synaptic vesicles remains pathway- and stimulation-dependent.

4.3 Cytokinesis

Older experiments implicated DYN-1 in animal-cell cytokinesis. Worm dynamin localized to mitotic spindle structures, and dyn-1 RNAi or temperature-sensitive inactivation caused division defects. The proposed role is membrane remodeling and trafficking at the cleavage furrow/midzone rather than microtubule motor activity. Published December 2002; DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/S0960-9822(02)01390-8. (thompson2002thelargegtpase pages 2-4)

Quantitative enrichment of dynamin by 30–50-fold in spindle/midbody extracts was measured in comparative mammalian experiments, not in worms, and should not be presented as a C. elegans value. (thompson2002thelargegtpase pages 2-4)

5. Recent developments, 2023–2024

Regenerative axonal fusion—2023

A 2023 study identified DYN-1 as a component of the axonal repair machinery. dyn-1(ky51) animals had normal fusion at the permissive temperature of 15°C but strongly reduced fusion and shorter regrowth at 25°C. Mechanosensory-neuron expression of wild-type DYN-1 rescued both phenotypes, demonstrating cell-autonomous action. Temperature-shift experiments placed the requirement after axotomy, and epistasis plus super-resolution imaging indicated that DYN-1 controls post-injury levels of the fusogen EFF-1. This extends DYN-1’s annotation from constitutive endocytosis to regulated membrane repair, although the likely proximate mechanism remains trafficking-dependent control of fusogen availability rather than DYN-1 directly fusing the axonal membranes. Published online 9 May 2023; DOI: https://doi.org/10.1093/pnasnexus/pgad114. (vijayaraghavan2023thedynamingtpase pages 1-2)

Epidermal endocytosis and dendrite patterning—2024

A November 2024 study showed that dyn-1 knockdown in the endocytic pathway causes ectopic epidermal distribution of the branching ligand SAX-7 and excessive sensory-dendrite branching. Non-additivity with eat-17 or bicd-1 defects supports placement in a common trafficking pathway. Analyses used 20 animals per condition, with reported comparisons as strong as P = 2.40 × 10⁻¹⁴, although the retrieved excerpt does not uniquely assign every P value to the dyn-1 comparison. The mechanistic conclusion is that DYN-1-dependent endocytosis restricts signaling spatially by removing misplaced surface ligand; it is not merely required for bulk membrane uptake. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-024-53970-3. (fang2024endocytosisrestrictsdendrite pages 6-7, fang2024endocytosisrestrictsdendrite pages 9-11)

6. Current applications and experimental implementations

There is no direct clinical implementation of worm DYN-1. Its practical value is as a genetically tractable model of conserved dynamin biology. Current applications include:

  • Conditional dissection of an essential trafficking enzyme: dyn-1(ky51) allows rapid temperature-dependent inactivation while avoiding constitutive embryonic lethality.
  • Cell-autonomy tests: tissue-specific rescue and RNAi separate neuronal, epidermal, intestinal, and phagocyte-associated functions.
  • Live trafficking assays: DYN-1::GFP, cargo reporters, colocalization measurements, and time-lapse imaging resolve recruitment order and organelle dynamics.
  • Neural physiology: channelrhodopsin stimulation combined with rapid-freeze electron microscopy measures endocytosis on millisecond timescales. (watanabe2013ultrafastendocytosisat pages 7-8, watanabe2013ultrafastendocytosisat pages 1-2)
  • Regeneration research: laser axotomy, temperature shifts, epistasis, and super-resolution EFF-1 imaging connect trafficking to membrane repair. (vijayaraghavan2023thedynamingtpase pages 1-2)
  • Spatial signaling studies: epidermal knockdown tests how receptor/ligand endocytosis constrains dendrite architecture. (fang2024endocytosisrestrictsdendrite pages 9-11)

The broader translational relevance is mechanistic conservation: dynamin-controlled fission, receptor trafficking, phagosome maturation, and synaptic recycling are central to human cell biology. Nevertheless, worm results should be treated as mechanistic models, not as evidence that modulating human dynamins will reproduce a worm phenotype without isoform- and tissue-specific validation.

8. Confidence assessment and remaining uncertainties

High-confidence annotation: DYN-1 is the conventional C. elegans dynamin and an essential, membrane-associated large GTPase whose primary molecular role is membrane fission. Direct worm evidence is especially strong for intestinal clathrin-coated-pit scission, recycling-endosome tubule fission, and CED-1/CED-6-linked phagosome maturation. (yu2006c.elegansdynamin pages 13-14, gao2020anehbp1sid3dyn1axis pages 10-12, shi2019wip1anddbn1 pages 7-8)

Moderate-to-high confidence: DYN-1 supports synaptic-vesicle recycling, cytokinesis, injury-induced axonal fusion, and spatial control of epidermal branching cues. The last two are strengthened by direct 2023–2024 genetics, rescue, and imaging. (thompson2002thelargegtpase pages 2-4, vijayaraghavan2023thedynamingtpase pages 1-2, fang2024endocytosisrestrictsdendrite pages 9-11)

Key limitations: no retrieved study supplied purified worm DYN-1 kinetics or a worm DYN-1 atomic structure; the exact EC reaction and complete domain assignment therefore depend substantially on family/database annotation. The synaptic ultrafast-endocytosis measurements retrieved here were physiological but not DYN-1-specific. Finally, studies disagree over whether the immediate apoptotic-cell phenotype is failed engulfment or primarily failed phagosome maturation; acute conditional experiments favor maturation, whereas prolonged RNAi can reveal both direct and secondary effects. (yu2006c.elegansdynamin pages 11-13, kinchen2008apathwayfor pages 16-19, watanabe2013ultrafastendocytosisat pages 7-8)
