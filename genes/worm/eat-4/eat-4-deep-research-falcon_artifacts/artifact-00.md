| Identity and family | **eat-4 = ZK512.6; EAT-4 = UniProt P34644**, the canonical *C. elegans* vesicular glutamate transporter and an ortholog of vertebrate SLC17A6/7/8 VGLUTs; it is an MFS-like sodium/anion transporter-family membrane protein. | Positional cloning and rescue (Lee et al., 1999); nematode SLC17 phylogeny (Serrano-Saiz et al., 2020) | Gene identity is experimentally secure. The original “Na⁺-dependent phosphate cotransporter” designation reflected similarity to BNPI before BNPI was recognized as VGLUT—not EAT-4’s accepted primary role today. |
| Transported substrate | **Glutamate** is loaded into synaptic vesicles, enabling quantal glutamatergic transmission. Phosphate is not the accepted physiological cargo annotation. | Loss of evoked iGluSnFR signal in *eat-4* mutant BAG neurons (Choi et al., 2021); conserved VGLUT homology and functional complementation | Strong in-vivo functional evidence, but purified EAT-4 transport kinetics, stoichiometry, and substrate selectivity have not been directly measured. |
| Cellular localization | Multipass integral membrane protein concentrated on **presynaptic/synaptic vesicles** and axonal presynaptic puncta. | EAT-4 and VST-1 double-label vesicle EM (Choi et al., 2021); endogenous EAT-4::GFP puncta aligned with ASE presynaptic sites | Tagged-protein localization is supported by functional validation; older transcriptional reporters mapped expressing cells but not protein localization. |
| M3 pharyngeal pathway | M3 packages and releases glutamate through EAT-4; glutamate activates AVR-15 channels in pharyngeal muscle, accelerating relaxation and shortening pumping contractions. | Mutant electropharyngeograms, normal response to applied glutamate, and genomic rescue (Lee et al., 1999) | Establishes a presynaptic defect and explains the original abnormal-pumping phenotype. |
| Thermotaxis | EAT-4-dependent AFD glutamate inhibits AIY through GLC-3, whereas AWC glutamate excites AIY; glutamate from RIA further shapes bidirectional thermotaxis. | Cell-specific rescue and calcium imaging (Ohnishi et al., 2011) | Circuit-specific rescue is strong, but EAT-4 marks vesicular release rather than determining whether a postsynaptic effect is excitatory or inhibitory. |
| BAG/VST-1 regulation | EAT-4 mediates BAG-neuron glutamate release; vesicular VST-1 opposes EAT-4-dependent vesicle filling and limits synaptic strength and CO₂-circuit recruitment. | iGluSnFR, synaptopHluorin, EM, genetics, and behavior (Choi et al., 2021) | VST-1 alters glutamate content rather than fusion rate. Competition for vesicular electrochemical energy versus glutamate efflux remains unresolved. |
| DVB cotransmission | The nominally GABAergic DVB motor neuron coexpresses EAT-4. Its glutamate activates inhibitory GLC-3 feedback in DVB and GLC-2/GLC-4 in enteric muscle; UBR-1 restrains this pathway. | Cell-specific knockdown/rescue, calcium imaging, and receptor genetics (Li et al., 2023) | Demonstrates GABA–glutamate cotransmission and that transmitter identity is not necessarily exclusive. |
| Rapid-cooling avoidance | ASH-derived EAT-4-dependent glutamate activates GLR-1 in AIB; AIZ-derived glutamate activates GLR-3/GLR-6 in RIA, forming parallel stimulatory and disinhibitory reversal circuits. | Neuron-specific RNAi/rescue and calcium imaging (Lin et al., 2024) | Recent mechanistic circuit evidence; broad *eat-4* null phenotypes were localized using cell-selective perturbations. |
| Social feeding | Glutamate packaged by EAT-4 in both ADL and ASH promotes aggregation; social animals show faster EAT-4::pHluorin recovery in ASH, independently of NRX-1-dependent changes in release-site number. | Cell-specific rescue, aggregation assays, and FRAP (Cowen et al., 2024) | ADL and ASH together were sufficient for rescue; pHluorin recovery is a release proxy rather than direct vesicular glutamate quantification. |
| Endogenous expression atlas | Endogenous *eat-4* is a broad marker of glutamatergic identity across sensory neurons, interneurons, motor neurons, and pharyngeal neurons; newer data support expression in substantially more classes than the original 15-class map. | CRISPR *eat-4* T2A–GFP–H2B reporter, NeuroPAL identification, and CeNGEN comparison (Wang et al., 2024) | CeNGEN detected transcripts in 37 of 38 previously mapped classes and suggested RIC, PVN, and DVA; RIC was independently confirmed. Detection thresholds and reporter sensitivity remain caveats. |
