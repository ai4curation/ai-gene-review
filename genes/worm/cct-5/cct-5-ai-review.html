Gene Description

cct-5 encodes the epsilon (CCT5 / TCP-1-epsilon) subunit of the eukaryotic cytosolic chaperonin CCT/TRiC, an ATP-dependent double-ring chaperonin built from eight distinct paralogous subunits per ring that folds a subset of cytosolic proteins, most notably actin and tubulin. CCT-5 is one of the four high-ATP-affinity subunits that power the folding cycle. In C. elegans CCT-5 is a biochemically identified component of the purified CCT complex and is expressed at all life stages. It is distributed diffusely in the cytoplasm of intestinal cells, where its depletion causes actin aggregation, reduced tubulin, microtubule disorganization and defective apical microvilli; cct-5 knockdown also causes larval arrest and embryonic lethality. In the germline CCT-5 co-purifies with CCT-4 and with the meiotic axis protein HIM-3.

Core Functions

cct-5 is the epsilon subunit of the cytosolic chaperonin CCT/TRiC and one of its four high-ATP-affinity subunits. Within the assembled double ring it binds ATP through its equatorial domain, driving the conformational cycle, and presents an apical substrate-binding surface, contributing to the ATP-dependent folding of actin, tubulin and other cytosolic clients.
