# Comprehensive Research Report: sel-12 Gene Function in *Caenorhabditis elegans*

## Gene Identity and Overview

The *sel-12* gene (suppressor/enhancer of lin-12) encodes presenilin SEL-12 (UniProt P52166) in *Caenorhabditis elegans* (ryan2024c.eleganspresenilin pages 4-6). SEL-12 is the *C. elegans* ortholog of human presenilins PSEN1 and PSEN2, with sel-12 showing higher sequence homology to human presenilins than the related *C. elegans* gene *hop-1* (chao2005lin12notchfunctions pages 8-9). The protein belongs to the peptidase A22A family and contains characteristic presenilin domains including the Pept_A22A and GxGD catalytic motifs (ryan2024c.eleganspresenilin pages 4-6, ashkavand2025identificationofpresenilin pages 15-17).

## Molecular Function and Enzymatic Activity

### Primary Function: Intramembrane Aspartyl Protease

SEL-12 serves as the catalytic subunit of the multi-protein gamma-secretase complex, functioning as an intramembrane aspartyl protease (ryan2024c.eleganspresenilin pages 4-6, ashkavand2025identificationofpresenilin pages 1-3). The protein contains a critical aspartate residue at position 226 (D226) that is essential for proteolytic activity (ryan2024c.eleganspresenilin pages 4-6, ashkavand2025identificationofpresenilin pages 3-4). Mutation of this catalytic aspartate to alanine (D226A or D257A) completely abolishes gamma-secretase proteolytic function, as demonstrated by loss of Notch signaling activity in egg-laying tissues (ashkavand2025identificationofpresenilin pages 3-4, ashkavand2025identificationofpresenilin pages 4-6).

### Substrate Specificity

SEL-12-containing gamma-secretase demonstrates specificity for type I single-pass transmembrane proteins (ryan2024c.eleganspresenilin pages 4-6, ashkavand2025identificationofpresenilin pages 1-3). In *C. elegans*, the best-characterized substrates are the Notch receptor family members:

1. **LIN-12/Notch**: The primary substrate for developmental Notch signaling. SEL-12 mediates transmembrane cleavage of LIN-12 following ligand-dependent ectodomain shedding, releasing the intracellular domain (NICD) for nuclear translocation and transcriptional activation (chen2004sel7apositive pages 1-2, chen2004sel7apositive pages 4-5).

2. **GLP-1**: The second *C. elegans* Notch receptor, particularly important for germline development. SEL-12 processes GLP-1 through the same gamma-secretase mechanism (ashkavand2025identificationofpresenilin pages 9-11, agarwal2018hop1presenilindeficiency pages 13-14).

The cleavage occurs within the transmembrane domain of these receptors, representing regulated intramembrane proteolysis (RIP) that is conserved from invertebrates to mammals (ashkavand2025identificationofpresenilin pages 7-9, ashkavand2025identificationofpresenilin pages 15-17).

## Subcellular Localization

SEL-12 localizes to endomembrane compartments, including the endoplasmic reticulum (ER), endosomes, and lysosomes (ashkavand2025identificationofpresenilin pages 7-9). This localization is conserved among presenilin proteins across species (ashkavand2025identificationofpresenilin pages 7-9). Importantly, SEL-12 functions at ER-mitochondrial contact sites, also known as mitochondria-associated ER membranes (MAMs), where it plays a critical role in regulating inter-organelle calcium communication (ryan2024c.eleganspresenilin pages 8-10, ryan2024c.eleganspresenilin pages 13-14, ryan2024c.eleganspresenilin pages 6-8). The protein is described as membrane-associated, consistent with its role in cleaving transmembrane substrates and regulating membrane contact sites (chen2004sel7apositive pages 1-2).

## Signaling Pathways and Biological Processes

### 1. LIN-12/Notch Signaling Pathway (Proteolytic Function)

SEL-12's canonical role is as an essential component of the LIN-12/GLP-1 Notch signaling pathway (chen2004sel7apositive pages 4-5, chen2004sel7apositive pages 1-2, deng2019ascreenof pages 7-8). Following ligand binding by DSL family proteins such as LAG-2, LIN-12 undergoes sequential proteolytic processing. SEL-12-containing gamma-secretase catalyzes the final transmembrane cleavage (S3 cleavage), releasing the LIN-12 intracellular domain from its membrane anchor (chen2004sel7apositive pages 1-2, chen2004sel7apositive pages 4-5).

The released intracellular domain translocates to the nucleus where it associates with the CSL transcription factor LAG-1 and other cofactors to activate Notch target genes (chen2004sel7apositive pages 1-2, chen2004sel7apositive pages 10-10). This proteolytic cascade is essential for multiple developmental processes including:

- Vulval-uterine connection development and egg-laying tissue formation (ashkavand2025identificationofpresenilin pages 3-4, chen2004sel7apositive pages 4-5)
- Anchor cell/ventral uterine precursor (AC/VU) cell fate decisions (chen2004sel7apositive pages 4-5, chen2004sel7apositive pages 3-4)
- Germline stem cell establishment and maintenance (agarwal2018hop1presenilindeficiency pages 13-14, agarwal2018hop1presenilindeficiency pages 1-2)
- Sex muscle morphology and attachment (eimer2003analysisandsuppression pages 40-44)

Loss of SEL-12 proteolytic activity causes defective egg-laying (Egl phenotype), abnormal vulval development, and when combined with loss of the partially redundant presenilin HOP-1, severe disruption of all Notch-dependent processes (ashkavand2025identificationofpresenilin pages 3-4, eimer2003analysisandsuppression pages 93-95, eimer2003analysisandsuppression pages 40-44).

### 2. ER-Mitochondrial Calcium Homeostasis (Non-Proteolytic Function)

Recent research from 2020-2025 has revealed that SEL-12 has critical gamma-secretase-independent functions in regulating calcium signaling between the ER and mitochondria (ryan2024c.eleganspresenilin pages 8-10, ryan2024c.eleganspresenilin pages 6-8, ryan2022deregulationofmitochondrial pages 4-5). This represents a fundamentally distinct role from its proteolytic activity and can be genetically separated using catalytic-dead mutants.

**Mechanism**: SEL-12 normally constrains or organizes ER-mitochondrial contact sites and prevents excessive calcium transfer from the ER to mitochondria (ryan2024c.eleganspresenilin pages 8-10, ryan2024c.eleganspresenilin pages 13-14). Loss of SEL-12 results in:

- Increased physical contact area between ER and mitochondria (ryan2024c.eleganspresenilin pages 8-10, ryan2024c.eleganspresenilin pages 10-13)
- Elevated ER calcium release through IP3 receptors (ITR-1) and ryanodine receptors (UNC-68) (ryan2024c.eleganspresenilin pages 8-10, ryan2024c.eleganspresenilin pages 6-8)
- Increased mitochondrial calcium uptake via the mitochondrial calcium uniporter MCU-1 (ryan2024c.eleganspresenilin pages 6-8, ashkavand2025identificationofpresenilin pages 22-22)
- Elevated basal mitochondrial calcium levels in neurons and hypodermis (ryan2024c.eleganspresenilin pages 6-8, ryan2022deregulationofmitochondrial pages 4-5, ryan2022deregulationofmitochondrial pages 5-7)

**Evidence for separation from proteolytic function**: The catalytically inactive SEL-12(D226A) mutant does NOT reproduce the elevated mitochondrial calcium or associated mitochondrial dysfunction seen in *sel-12* null mutants, demonstrating that this calcium-regulatory role is independent of gamma-secretase activity (ryan2022deregulationofmitochondrial pages 5-7, ashkavand2020corrupteder‐mitochondrialcalcium pages 3-4, ashkavand2025identificationofpresenilin pages 4-6).

**Functional consequences**: Genetic reduction of ER calcium release (by depleting ITR-1 or UNC-68) or blocking mitochondrial calcium uptake (by loss of MCU-1) prevents the mitochondrial calcium elevation and suppresses downstream phenotypes including lysosomal defects, neurodegeneration, and behavioral abnormalities (ryan2024c.eleganspresenilin pages 8-10, ryan2024c.eleganspresenilin pages 6-8, ashkavand2025identificationofpresenilin pages 22-22).

### 3. mTORC1 Signaling and Autophagy Regulation

Excess mitochondrial calcium and elevated mitochondrial metabolic activity resulting from SEL-12 loss drives hyperactivation of the mechanistic target of rapamycin complex 1 (mTORC1) pathway (ryan2021increasedmitochondrialcalcium pages 4-6, ryan2021increasedmitochondrialcalcium pages 7-10, ryan2021increasedmitochondrialcalcium pages 1-2). This is evidenced by:

- Significantly increased phosphorylation of RSKS-1/S6 kinase, a direct mTORC1 substrate (ryan2021increasedmitochondrialcalcium pages 4-6, ryan2022deregulationofmitochondrial pages 11-13)
- mTORC1 activation that is prevented by blocking mitochondrial calcium uptake (MCU-1 loss) or reducing mitochondrial activity (ryan2021increasedmitochondrialcalcium pages 4-6, ryan2021increasedmitochondrialcalcium pages 7-10)

**Impact on autophagy**: Because mTORC1 is a central negative regulator of autophagy, its hyperactivation in *sel-12* mutants suppresses autophagosome formation (ryan2021increasedmitochondrialcalcium pages 4-6, ryan2021increasedmitochondrialcalcium pages 10-11, ryan2021increasedmitochondrialcalcium pages 2-4). This is demonstrated by:

- Reduced GFP::LGG-1 puncta (autophagosome marker) in neurons and other tissues (ryan2021increasedmitochondrialcalcium pages 2-4)
- Decreased processed GFP::LGG-1-PE levels (ryan2021increasedmitochondrialcalcium pages 4-6, ryan2021increasedmitochondrialcalcium pages 2-4)
- Restoration of normal autophagy markers when mitochondrial calcium uptake is blocked or mTORC1 is inhibited genetically (RAGA-1, RSKS-1 loss) or pharmacologically (rapamycin) (ryan2021increasedmitochondrialcalcium pages 4-6, ryan2021increasedmitochondrialcalcium pages 7-10, ryan2021increasedmitochondrialcalcium pages 2-4)

**Downstream consequences**: The autophagy defect contributes to proteostasis collapse, as demonstrated by the requirement for intact autophagy machinery (LGG-1, BEC-1) for rescue of neuronal and proteostatic phenotypes by mTORC1 inhibition (ryan2021increasedmitochondrialcalcium pages 10-11, ryan2021increasedmitochondrialcalcium pages 7-10).

### 4. Lysosomal Function and Organelle Communication

SEL-12 plays a gamma-secretase-independent role in maintaining lysosomal structure, acidification, and degradative capacity (ryan2024c.eleganspresenilin pages 13-14, ryan2024c.eleganspresenilin pages 6-8, ryan2024c.eleganspresenilin pages 4-6). Loss of SEL-12 causes:

- Significantly enlarged lysosomes in touch receptor neurons and hypodermal cells (ryan2024c.eleganspresenilin pages 6-8, ryan2024c.eleganspresenilin pages 4-6)
- Reduced lysosome numbers in ALM neuronal soma and axons (ryan2024c.eleganspresenilin pages 4-6)
- Increased lysosomal pH (more alkaline), indicating impaired acidification (ryan2024c.eleganspresenilin pages 6-8, ryan2024c.eleganspresenilin pages 4-6)
- Defective lysosomal degradative activity (ryan2024c.eleganspresenilin pages 13-14)

These lysosomal abnormalities appear before major neurodegenerative phenotypes, are independent of SEL-12 proteolytic activity (absent in catalytic-dead D226A mutants), and are suppressed by limiting ER-to-mitochondrial calcium signaling (ryan2024c.eleganspresenilin pages 6-8, ryan2024c.eleganspresenilin pages 10-13, ryan2024c.eleganspresenilin pages 4-6). The mechanism involves dysregulated ER-mitochondria-lysosome contacts and mitochondrial ROS production downstream of calcium overload (ryan2024c.eleganspresenilin pages 13-14, ryan2024c.eleganspresenilin pages 10-13).

### 5. Redox Homeostasis and SKN-1/Nrf2 Pathway

SEL-12 loss disrupts cellular redox balance through multiple interconnected mechanisms (ryan2022deregulationofmitochondrial pages 13-14, ryan2022deregulationofmitochondrial pages 5-7, ryan2022deregulationofmitochondrial pages 9-11):

- Elevated mitochondrial calcium increases mitochondrial reactive oxygen species (ROS) generation (ryan2022deregulationofmitochondrial pages 13-14, ryan2022deregulationofmitochondrial pages 5-7)
- Mitochondrial oxidation is prevented by blocking mitochondrial calcium uptake, establishing the calcium-ROS connection (ryan2022deregulationofmitochondrial pages 5-7)
- Hyperactive mTORC1 suppresses the SKN-1/Nrf2 antioxidant transcription factor, preventing adequate induction of detoxification enzymes like GST-4 (ryan2022deregulationofmitochondrial pages 9-11)
- *sel-12* mutants show hypersensitivity to oxidative stressors like paraquat (ryan2022deregulationofmitochondrial pages 9-11)

Inhibiting mTORC1 or activating SKN-1 improves oxidative stress resistance and neuronal function, while antioxidant supplementation suppresses proteostasis defects (ashkavand2020corrupteder‐mitochondrialcalcium pages 3-4, ryan2022deregulationofmitochondrial pages 9-11). This pathway is independent of gamma-secretase activity, as catalytic-dead SEL-12(D226A) mutants do not exhibit the elevated mitochondrial ROS of null mutants (ryan2022deregulationofmitochondrial pages 13-14, ryan2022deregulationofmitochondrial pages 5-7).

## Functional Redundancy with HOP-1

*C. elegans* possesses two presenilin genes, *sel-12* and *hop-1*, that show partial functional redundancy but distinct expression patterns and developmental contributions (eimer2003analysisandsuppression pages 126-129, agarwal2018hop1presenilindeficiency pages 13-14, eimer2003analysisandsuppression pages 93-95, eimer2002lossofspr‐5 pages 5-6):

**Expression patterns**:
- *sel-12* is expressed ubiquitously and continuously throughout development (eimer2003analysisandsuppression pages 126-129, eimer2003analysisandsuppression pages 93-95, eimer2002lossofspr‐5 pages 5-6)
- *hop-1* expression is developmentally regulated: nearly absent in early larval stages (L1-L2), increases progressively, and peaks in adults (agarwal2018hop1presenilindeficiency pages 13-14, eimer2003analysisandsuppression pages 93-95, eimer2002lossofspr‐5 pages 5-6)

**Functional specialization**:
- SEL-12 provides the predominant presenilin activity during larval development for somatic Notch signaling (eimer2003analysisandsuppression pages 126-129, eimer2003analysisandsuppression pages 99-101)
- HOP-1 becomes essential in the adult germline where *sel-12* expression is low or absent; HOP-1 is required for maintaining the germline proliferative zone and fecundity (agarwal2018hop1presenilindeficiency pages 13-14, agarwal2018hop1presenilindeficiency pages 1-2)
- Either presenilin can support Notch signaling when adequately expressed, but *hop-1* is normally limiting during development (eimer2003analysisandsuppression pages 93-95, eimer2002lossofspr‐5 pages 5-6)

Derepression of *hop-1* (e.g., through loss of the transcriptional repressor SPR-5) can increase *hop-1* expression 20-30 fold and fully suppress *sel-12* mutant phenotypes, demonstrating biochemical equivalence despite different regulatory deployment (eimer2003analysisandsuppression pages 93-95, eimer2003analysisandsuppression pages 99-101, eimer2002lossofspr‐5 pages 5-6).

## Key Phenotypes and Their Mechanisms

The major phenotypes of *sel-12* mutants can be categorized by their dependence on proteolytic versus non-proteolytic functions:

### Proteolytic-Dependent Phenotypes:
- Egg-laying defects (Egl) due to impaired vulval-uterine development (ashkavand2025identificationofpresenilin pages 3-4, chen2004sel7apositive pages 4-5)
- Defective LIN-12-dependent cell fate decisions (chen2004sel7apositive pages 4-5, chen2004sel7apositive pages 3-4)
- Severe germline and embryonic defects in *sel-12; hop-1* double mutants (agarwal2018hop1presenilindeficiency pages 13-14, eimer2003analysisandsuppression pages 96-97)

### Non-Proteolytic-Dependent Phenotypes:
- Premature neurodegeneration with axonal blebs, breaks, and wave-like processes in mechanosensory neurons (ashkavand2025identificationofpresenilin pages 6-7, ryan2021increasedmitochondrialcalcium pages 4-6)
- Impaired gentle touch responses that worsen with age (ashkavand2025identificationofpresenilin pages 22-22, ashkavand2025identificationofpresenilin pages 4-6, ashkavand2025identificationofpresenilin pages 6-7)
- Progressive chemosensory deficits (parvand2024afamilialalzheimer’s pages 28-32, parvand2024afamilialalzheimer’s pages 23-28)
- Fragmented mitochondrial networks with altered morphology (ryan2024c.eleganspresenilin pages 6-8, ashkavand2025identificationofpresenilin pages 6-7)
- Proteostasis collapse with enhanced aggregation of disease proteins (ashkavand2020corrupteder‐mitochondrialcalcium pages 3-4, ryan2021increasedmitochondrialcalcium pages 10-11)

## Recent Developments (2023-2025)

### Separation of Proteolytic and Non-Proteolytic Functions

The most significant recent advance is the clear genetic and molecular separation of SEL-12's dual functions. A landmark 2025 study identified familial Alzheimer's disease-associated presenilin mutations in *sel-12* that preserve sufficient gamma-secretase activity to support Notch signaling and normal egg-laying but still cause mitochondrial calcium elevation, neuronal dysfunction, and premature neurodegeneration (ashkavand2025identificationofpresenilin pages 1-3, ashkavand2025identificationofpresenilin pages 6-7, ashkavand2025identificationofpresenilin pages 9-11, ashkavand2025identificationofpresenilin pages 7-9). This demonstrates that:

1. Notch signaling competence does not guarantee neuronal health
2. Non-proteolytic presenilin functions are conserved from *C. elegans* to humans
3. Familial Alzheimer's disease mutations may cause pathology through mechanisms independent of altered amyloid-β production

### Conservation to Human Disease

Studies published in 2024-2025 demonstrate remarkable conservation between *C. elegans* SEL-12 and human PSEN1/PSEN2 (ashkavand2025identificationofpresenilin pages 1-3, ryan2024c.eleganspresenilin pages 6-8, ashkavand2025identificationofpresenilin pages 7-9):

- Human PSEN1, PSEN2, and plant presenilins can rescue *sel-12* null phenotypes when expressed under the *sel-12* promoter (ashkavand2025identificationofpresenilin pages 1-3, ashkavand2025identificationofpresenilin pages 7-9)
- Mitochondrial calcium dysregulation and lysosomal defects in *sel-12* mutants are recapitulated in human familial Alzheimer's disease fibroblasts carrying PSEN1 mutations (ryan2024c.eleganspresenilin pages 6-8)
- Mitochondrial calcium uniporter inhibitors reduce lysosomal defects in both *C. elegans sel-12* mutants and human fAD cells, suggesting conserved therapeutic targets (ryan2024c.eleganspresenilin pages 6-8)

### Inter-Organelle Contact Regulation

A 2024 study established that SEL-12 mediates inter-organelle contacts and communication required for lysosomal activity (ryan2024c.eleganspresenilin pages 8-10, ryan2024c.eleganspresenilin pages 13-14, ryan2024c.eleganspresenilin pages 10-13). This work demonstrated that *sel-12* mutants have increased contact surface area between ER-mitochondria, ER-lysosomes, and mitochondria-lysosomes, and that disrupting specific calcium-signaling components prevents selective contact abnormalities, revealing a complex spatial organization role for presenilin.

## Conclusion

The *C. elegans sel-12* gene encodes a presenilin with dual, genetically separable functions. As a gamma-secretase catalytic subunit, SEL-12 cleaves LIN-12/GLP-1 Notch receptors within their transmembrane domains, releasing intracellular signaling fragments essential for developmental cell-fate decisions and tissue morphogenesis. Independent of this proteolytic activity, SEL-12 regulates ER-mitochondrial calcium homeostasis, inter-organelle contacts, lysosomal function, mTORC1 signaling, autophagy, and redox balance. These non-proteolytic roles are critical for neuronal maintenance, with their disruption causing mitochondrial dysfunction, proteostasis collapse, and age-progressive neurodegeneration. Recent work (2024-2025) demonstrates that these dual functions are conserved to human presenilins and that familial Alzheimer's disease mutations can selectively impair the non-proteolytic functions while preserving Notch signaling, suggesting that presenilin-dependent calcium dysregulation and organelle dysfunction contribute to neurodegenerative disease pathogenesis independently of altered amyloid processing. *C. elegans* thus provides a tractable genetic system for dissecting presenilin biology with direct relevance to human neurodegenerative disease.
