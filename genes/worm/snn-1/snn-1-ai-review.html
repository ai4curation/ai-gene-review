Gene Description

SNN-1 is the single Caenorhabditis elegans synapsin, a peripheral presynaptic phosphoprotein built around a conserved ATP-grasp-like core and most similar to vertebrate synapsin II. It is not an enzyme; it works as a regulated adapter that links synaptic vesicles to one another and to the presynaptic actin environment, clustering vesicles in the reserve pool next to release sites and releasing them for use when the terminal is stimulated. In the AIY interneuron, SNN-1 acts at the vesicle-clustering step downstream of a netrin/Rac/MIG-10 actin-organizing pathway; loss of SNN-1 redistributes vesicles, impairs stimulation-evoked mobilization from the reserve pool, and reduces cAMP-dependent neuropeptide release. Its activity is tuned by phosphorylation of the conserved Ser9 site, whose state is set in part by PP1/PHAC-1 signalling and which modulates cholinergic transmission.

1. Identity, nomenclature, and domain interpretation

The requested record describes UniProt G5EGI2, gene snn-1, ORF Y38C1BA.2/CELE_Y38C1BA.2, from C. elegans. Primary literature independently identifies snn-1 as the nematode’s single synapsin ortholog, while an authoritative review reports that SNN-1 is most similar to vertebrate synapsin II (calahorro2018thepresynapticmachinery pages 1-2, stratigi2025neuroendocrinecontrolof pages 1-4, stratigi2025neuroendocrinecontrolof pages 4-7).

The supplied InterPro assignments—Synapsin, Synapsin ATP-binding domain, ATP-grasp, ATP-grasp subdomain 1, and pre-ATP-grasp fold—are mutually compatible. Synapsins possess a conserved central ATP-binding domain, while their less-conserved regions carry regulatory phosphorylation sites and membrane/cytoskeletal interactions. The 2018 review describes general synapsin architecture as including domain A with a PKA/CaMKI-regulated phosphorylation site, domain C with ATP-binding sites, and domain E involved in reserve-pool control (calahorro2018thepresynapticmachinery pages 1-2).

The database label “ATP-grasp domain-containing protein” should therefore not be interpreted as evidence that SNN-1 catalyzes an ATP-dependent ligation reaction. For this protein, the ATP-grasp-like fold is part of a conserved synapsin structural/regulatory core. No direct worm biochemical study retrieved here establishes an enzymatic reaction, catalytic substrate, product, kinetic constants, or small-molecule substrate specificity. Functional annotation should emphasize vesicle organization rather than enzyme catalysis.

2. Primary molecular and cellular function

2.1 Synaptic-vesicle clustering and reserve-pool organization

Direct imaging in the AIY interneuron showed that GFP–SNN-1 localizes to presynaptic regions and colocalizes with vesicle-associated mCherry–RAB-3. SNN-1 enrichment at those sites depends on mig-10, whereas MIG-10B localization is not significantly altered in the snn-1(tm2557) mutant. This epistasis places SNN-1 downstream of the presynaptic MIG-10B machinery rather than upstream of its localization (stavoe2012synapticvesicleclustering pages 10-11).

Functional work summarized from Yu et al. (2021) found that snn-1 loss changes SV localization and impairs stimulation-evoked mobilization of vesicles from the reserve pool. These observations support a model in which SNN-1 captures or maintains vesicles in presynaptic clusters at rest but, through regulated phosphorylation, permits their mobilization during activity (schiffmannUnknownyearc.elegansas pages 15-19, schiffmannUnknownyearc.elegansasa pages 15-19).

A systematic localization study across 25 neurotransmission mutants found that the pattern of SNN-1 abundance was anticorrelated with the SV exocytic markers SNB-1/synaptobrevin and RAB-3 and most resembled the endocytic proteins APT-4/AP2 and ITSN-1/intersectin. The authors interpreted this as SNN-1 associating particularly with vesicles transitioning from recycling intermediates rather than remaining uniformly associated with the entire SNB-1/RAB-3-positive pool. This is informative correlation-based evidence, not proof of direct physical binding to the endocytic factors (chng2008profilingsynapticproteins pages 7-9).

2.2 Dense-core vesicles and neuropeptide secretion

SNN-1 is also required for efficient cAMP-dependent neuropeptide release. The 2021 work links synapsin to DCV capture and release as well as to classical SV reserve-pool mobilization (schiffmannUnknownyearc.elegansas pages 15-19, schiffmannUnknownyearc.elegansasa pages 15-19). This broadens its function beyond a static SV tether: SNN-1 helps organize presynaptic vesicle populations so that neuronal stimulation can recruit both fast neurotransmitter and neuromodulatory secretion.

This should not be overinterpreted as showing that SNN-1 is an integral DCV membrane protein. The evidence establishes a functional role in DCV capture/neuropeptide secretion; direct stable localization on DCVs was not demonstrated in the excerpts retrieved.

2.3 Structural role

The most defensible mechanistic description is that SNN-1 acts as a regulated adapter/scaffold linking vesicles, vesicle clusters, and the presynaptic actin environment. Synapsin-family evidence supports interactions with vesicle phospholipids and F-actin, but the extent to which each molecular contact has been directly measured for purified worm SNN-1 remains limited. Thus, “vesicle-organizing phosphoprotein” is stronger than claiming a fully established direct cross-linking mechanism for every interaction.

3. Site of action and localization

SNN-1 acts inside neurons at presynaptic terminals, particularly in SV-rich presynaptic regions rather than extracellularly, in the nucleus, or as a transmembrane transporter. In AIY, GFP–SNN-1 colocalizes with RAB-3 at presynaptic regions (stavoe2012synapticvesicleclustering pages 10-11). At cholinergic neuromuscular junctions, tagged SNN-1 was used as a punctate presynaptic marker, and its abundance changed systematically with mutations affecting exocytosis and endocytosis (chng2008profilingsynapticproteins pages 7-9).

An endogenous CRISPR SNN-1–TurboID–mNeonGreen reporter produced weak but broad neuronal signal, including particularly visible AVD and PVC cholinergic interneurons. Because that reporter was analyzed in a pprp-1/phac-1 mutant background and signal was low, failure to observe strong dorsal-cord enrichment should not be interpreted as absence from those synapses (schiffmannUnknownyearc.elegansas pages 36-40, schiffmannUnknownyearc.elegansasa pages 36-40).

Accordingly, the localization annotation can be stated at high confidence as neuronal, cytoplasmic/presynaptic, and vesicle-cluster associated. A comprehensive cell-by-cell endogenous expression atlas for SNN-1 remains less secure.

4. Pathways and regulatory logic

4.1 Netrin-directed presynaptic assembly in AIY

The best-resolved developmental pathway is:

glia-derived UNC-6/Netrin → UNC-40/DCC → CED-5/DOCK180 → CED-10/Rac1 → MIG-10B/Lamellipodin + ABI-1/WAVE-associated machinery → presynaptic F-actin organization → SNN-1-dependent SV clustering.

Netrin specifies the position of the AIY–RIA synapse. MIG-10B, unlike the other MIG-10 isoforms, localizes to the AIY presynaptic region and is necessary and sufficient to rescue the mig-10 SV-clustering defect. MIG-10B interacts with ABI-1 and organizes actin at that site; SNN-1 then acts downstream to cluster vesicles (stavoe2012synapticvesicleclustering pages 1-2, stavoe2012synapticvesicleclustering pages 5-6, stavoe2012synapticvesicleclustering pages 10-11).

The pathway order is supported by localization and genetic epistasis: SNN-1 presynaptic enrichment requires mig-10, while MIG-10B localization does not require snn-1. Cell-specific disruption of MIG-10B localization impaired AIY vesicle clustering and thermotaxis, providing a circuit-level consequence of disrupting this assembly pathway (stavoe2012synapticvesicleclustering pages 10-11).

4.2 Phosphoregulation, cAMP, and vesicle mobilization

Ser9 is a conserved regulatory phosphorylation site. Family-level models predict that phosphorylation weakens vesicle-cluster retention and promotes mobilization, whereas dephosphorylated synapsin stabilizes clusters. Direct worm evidence supports phosphorylation-dependent effects on SV organization and secretion, although assignment of every relevant kinase remains incomplete (schiffmannUnknownyearc.elegansas pages 15-19, schiffmannUnknownyearc.elegansas pages 19-24).

The DCV study establishes that neuropeptide release supported by SNN-1 is cAMP dependent, consistent with PKA-linked regulation. However, domain homology or pathway dependence should not be converted into a claim that direct PKA phosphorylation of purified SNN-1 was demonstrated in the cited worm experiments.

4.3 PHAC-1/PP1 and motor-circuit excitation/inhibition balance

A major recent development is the 2023 PHAC-1 study, initially posted to bioRxiv on 20 August 2023 and subsequently published in peer-reviewed form in 2025. A genetic screen for modifiers of FLP-18-dependent locomotor neuromodulation recovered both snn-1 and phac-1, the worm PHACTR1 ortholog. The screen began with 27 candidate mutants and then isolated 25 suppressor strains; one confirmed suppressor was a deletion in snn-1 (stratigi2025neuroendocrinecontrolof pages 4-7).

Phospho-specific immunoblotting showed increased SNN-1 Ser9 phosphorylation in phac-1 loss-of-function animals and decreased phosphorylation in phac-1 gain-of-function animals. A phosphodeficient snn-1(S9A) allele reproduced aspects of the phac-1 gain-of-function phenotype, including aldicarb resistance and reduced cholinergic signaling. Deletion of snn-1 strongly reduced the aldicarb resistance of phac-1 gain-of-function animals but had only a weak effect on phac-1 loss-of-function hypersensitivity (stratigi2025neuroendocrinecontrolof pages 12-15).

These results support convergence of PHAC-1/PP1 signaling and SNN-1 phosphoregulation at adult cholinergic synapses. They do not establish SNN-1 as the only—or even principal—PHAC-1 substrate: Ser9A did not reproduce or suppress all PHAC-1 phenotypes, and the authors concluded that additional PP1-PHAC-1 substrates or indirect effects are involved (stratigi2025neuroendocrinecontrolof pages 12-15, stratigi2025neuroendocrinecontrolof pages 1-4).

5. Biological processes and phenotypes

The experimentally supported processes are:

  • assembly and maintenance of presynaptic vesicle clusters;
  • positioning of vesicle accumulation downstream of Netrin signaling in AIY;
  • retention and stimulation-dependent mobilization of the SV reserve pool;
  • cAMP-dependent DCV capture and neuropeptide release;
  • modulation of cholinergic/GABAergic balance in the motor circuit;
  • circuit-dependent behaviors, including locomotion and AIY-associated thermotaxis when the upstream SNN-1-clustering pathway is disrupted.

The 2012 pathway study provided a particularly precise cell-biological result: GFP–SNN-1 presynaptic enrichment depended on mig-10, but presynaptic MIG-10B localization was not significantly different in snn-1(tm2557) mutants (stavoe2012synapticvesicleclustering pages 10-11). In the 2008 profiling study, SNN-1 behavior was assessed as part of a nine-marker panel across 25 neurotransmission mutants, giving broad comparative evidence that its regulation differs from that of core RAB-3/SNB-1-positive exocytic vesicles (chng2008profilingsynapticproteins pages 7-9, chng2008profilingsynapticproteins pages 3-5).

6. Recent developments and real-world research applications

No SNN-1-specific primary paper from 2024 was identified in the retrieved corpus. The most relevant 2023–2024-era development is the 20 August 2023 PHAC-1 preprint, which used worm SNN-1 biology to interpret human PHACTR1 developmental and epileptic encephalopathy 70 (DEE70) variants. Three disease-mimicking PHAC-1 substitutions and an engineered 2R/A allele behaved as dominant-positive, constitutively active PP1-PHAC-1 states in adult cholinergic neurons. The work combined CRISPR editing, aldicarb and PTZ assays, quantitative fluorescence, electron microscopy, electrophysiology, and phospho-SNN-1 immunoblotting (stratigi2025neuroendocrinecontrolof pages 1-4, stratigi2025neuroendocrinecontrolof pages 7-10).

This illustrates the principal current application of snn-1: it is a genetically tractable, single-ortholog system for studying conserved synapsin phosphoregulation, vesicle-pool dynamics, neuropeptide release, excitation/inhibition balance, and disease-associated presynaptic signaling. Additional applications include fluorescent SNN-1 as a presynaptic/SV-cluster marker, endogenous TurboID tagging for proximity proteomics, and cell-specific genetic dissection of synapse assembly (schiffmannUnknownyearc.elegansas pages 36-40, schiffmannUnknownyearc.elegansasa pages 36-40, stavoe2012synapticvesicleclustering pages 10-11).

These are research implementations, not clinical uses. There is no evidence that worm SNN-1 itself is a therapeutic target or diagnostic biomarker.

7. Expert interpretation and annotation confidence

Authoritative synthesis and primary studies converge on the following interpretation:

  1. High confidence: SNN-1 is the C. elegans synapsin ortholog and functions presynaptically in neurons.
  2. High confidence: Its primary role is organization and regulated mobilization of vesicle pools—not membrane transport or a conventional metabolic reaction.
  3. High confidence: It acts downstream of MIG-10B/ABI-1-dependent actin organization in Netrin-specified AIY presynaptic regions.
  4. Moderate-to-high confidence: SNN-1 contributes to DCV capture and cAMP-dependent neuropeptide release, although stable physical residence on DCVs is less certain.
  5. Moderate confidence: PHAC-1/PP1 influences SNN-1 Ser9 phosphorylation and SNN-1 contributes to PHAC-1-dependent motor-circuit phenotypes; direct in vivo dephosphorylation and exclusivity of the substrate relationship are not established.
  6. Annotation-level confidence only: ATP binding is plausible from the conserved synapsin ATP-binding/ATP-grasp fold. Intrinsic catalytic activity and biochemical substrate specificity remain unproved.

8. Recommended functional annotation

Molecular function: Synapsin-family presynaptic phosphoprotein; ATP-binding-domain-containing vesicle-cluster organizer that associates with synaptic-vesicle-rich compartments and couples vesicle organization to the presynaptic cytoskeleton.

Biological processes: Synaptic-vesicle clustering; reserve-pool retention and activity-dependent mobilization; presynaptic assembly downstream of Netrin; dense-core-vesicle capture and cAMP-dependent neuropeptide secretion; modulation of cholinergic/GABAergic motor-circuit balance.

Cellular component: Neuronal cytoplasm and presynaptic terminals; associated with synaptic-vesicle clusters and presynaptic actin-rich regions.

Catalytic activity: None demonstrated. Do not assign an ATP-dependent ligase reaction solely from the ATP-grasp annotation.

Substrate specificity: No enzymatic substrate is known. In the structural sense, the relevant interaction partners/targets are vesicle phospholipid surfaces, vesicle clusters, and the presynaptic actin-associated machinery, but direct worm-specific binding constants and a complete interactome remain unresolved.

Core biology: presynaptic phosphoprotein that clusters vesicles in the reserve pool

  • SNN-1 is a presynaptic vesicle-organizing scaffold rather than an enzyme: no catalytic reaction or
    small-molecule substrate has been demonstrated for the worm protein, although the ATP-grasp-like
    core supports family-level ATP binding
    [file:worm/snn-1/snn-1-deep-research-falcon.md "No intrinsic catalytic reaction, physiological small-molecule substrate, or ATP-dependent product has been demonstrated for worm SNN-1."].
  • Localization: GFP-SNN-1 is at presynaptic regions of the AIY interneuron, colocalized with the
    vesicle marker RAB-3
    [file:worm/snn-1/snn-1-deep-research-falcon.md "In AIY, GFP–SNN-1 colocalizes with RAB-3 at presynaptic regions"];
    the GOA IDA places it in the presynaptic periactive zone, the vesicle-cluster region flanking the
    active zone.
  • Vesicle clustering: SNN-1 acts downstream of a netrin/actin pathway to cluster synaptic vesicles at
    the AIY presynaptic site; SNN-1 enrichment requires mig-10, whereas MIG-10B localization does not
    require snn-1, fixing SNN-1 at the vesicle-clustering step
    [file:worm/snn-1/snn-1-deep-research-falcon.md "SNN-1 then acts downstream to cluster vesicles"].
  • Reserve pool: loss of snn-1 changes vesicle localization and impairs stimulation-evoked
    mobilization of vesicles from the reserve pool
    [file:worm/snn-1/snn-1-deep-research-falcon.md "impairs stimulation-evoked mobilization of vesicles from the reserve pool"],
    the worm counterpart of the reserve-pool stage in modules/synaptic_vesicle_cycle.yaml.
  • Mechanism: the defensible description is a regulated adapter linking vesicles and vesicle clusters
    to the presynaptic actin environment
    [file:worm/snn-1/snn-1-deep-research-falcon.md "SNN-1 acts as a regulated adapter/scaffold linking vesicles, vesicle clusters, and the presynaptic actin environment"],
    matching the GO:0106006 cytoskeletal protein-membrane anchor activity accepted for human SYN1
    (genes/human/SYN1) and used for the synapsin annoton in the module.
  • Regulation: Ser9 phosphorylation state, controlled by the PP1 regulator PHAC-1, modulates
    cholinergic signalling; snn-1(S9A) phenocopies aspects of phac-1 gain of function
    [file:worm/snn-1/snn-1-deep-research-falcon.md "A phosphodeficient snn-1(S9A) allele reproduced aspects of the phac-1 gain-of-function phenotype"].
  • Neuromuscular function: snn-1 was recovered in a systematic RNAi screen for genes required for
    acetylcholine secretion, and its product was localized to presynaptic specializations
    [PMID:16049479 "A total of 185 genes were identified in an RNA interference screen for decreased acetylcholine secretion"; "Twenty-four genes encoded proteins that were localized to presynaptic specializations."].

GO decisions (summary)

  • Core: cytoskeletal protein-membrane anchor activity (GO:0106006) proposed as a NEW molecular
    function, with synaptic vesicle clustering (GO:0097091) as a NEW process, in the presynaptic
    periactive zone / on synaptic vesicles. Both are grounded in worm genetics (netrin-pathway vesicle
    clustering in AIY, reserve-pool mobilization defects) plus the family and human-ortholog evidence;
    the primary worm papers are not cached in this repository, which is noted in the reviews.
  • Existing rows: ATP/nucleotide binding and metal ion binding are accepted at the domain level but
    kept non-core, since no catalytic or ATP-dependent activity is demonstrated for SNN-1.
