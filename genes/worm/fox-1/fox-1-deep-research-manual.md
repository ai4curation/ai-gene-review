# fox-1 (C. elegans, Q10572, T07D1.4, WBGene00001484) - manual literature synthesis

No deep-research API was available for this review; this summary was compiled by hand
from the cached publications in `publications/` and the UniProt record. Quotes are verbatim.

## Identity and family

- fox-1 ("Feminizing locus On X") was identified as an X-linked numerator element whose
  extra copies kill and feminize XO animals, and was found to encode a predicted RNA-binding
  protein [PMID:7821230 "One cosmid array was found to be viable in XX animals, but lethal and feminizing in XO animals, consistent with it containing a major numerator element."].
- FOX-1 is the founding member of the RBFOX family; it has a single FOX1-type RRM
  (UniProt FT DOMAIN 183..259; InterPro IPR034237 FOX1_RRM, IPR047131 RBFOX1-like). Its
  paralog in C. elegans is ASD-1.

## Molecular function

- Sequence-specific binding to GCAUG and GCACG motifs in xol-1 intron VI, shown by in vitro
  cross-linking/competition with purified FOX-1 [PMID:33372658 "FOX-1 RNA binding experiments performed in vitro demonstrate that FOX-1 binds to multiple GCAUG and GCACG motifs in intron VI."].
- Direct binding to the UGCAUG stretch in unc-32 intron 7b by EMSA [PMID:23468662 "demonstrating direct and specific binding of ASD-1 and FOX-1 to the UGCAUG stretch"].
- Binds RNA homopolymers in vitro (abstract-level evidence only) [PMID:9927456 "FOX-1 is an RRM-type RNA-binding protein, which can bind RNAs in vitro."].
- Forms a stable complex with SUP-12 on egl-15 intronic RNA [PMID:17923701 "The Fox-1 family and the SUP-12 proteins form a stable complex with egl-15 RNA, depending on the cis elements."].

## Biological roles

- X signal element (XSE) in primary sex determination: FOX-1 reduces XOL-1 post-transcriptionally
  [PMID:9217163 "The other uses the putative RNA-binding protein encoded by fox-1 to reduce the level of xol-1 protein."].
- Mechanism: non-productive alternative splicing of xol-1 (intron VI retention or alternative 3'
  splice site use deleting exon 7 coding sequence) [PMID:33372658 "By binding to multiple sites in intron VI using both GCAUG and GCACG motifs, FOX-1 causes either intron VI retention or directs the use of an alternative 3' splice site, causing deletion of exon 7 coding sequences."].
- Dose-dependence is the basis of X counting [PMID:33372658 "Splicing regulation is dose-dependent: mutating one copy of fox-1 or all binding motifs in one copy of xol-1 kills XX animals sensitized by reduced XSE activity."].
- Trans-splicing of xol-1 to unrelated genes was seen only under high (transgenic) FOX-1 levels
  [PMID:33372658 "Clones of cDNAs from high FOX-1 conditions revealed both expected and unexpected transcripts (Figure 3C)."].
- Dosage compensation is affected indirectly, because xol-1 repression is needed for SDC-2-triggered
  DCC loading on X; fox-1 sex-1 XX double mutants fail to load SDC-3/DPY-27 on X [PMID:16139225 "In fox-1 sex-1 double mutants, as in wild-type XO embryos, the dosage compensation protein SDC-3 is not present, and DPY-27 is diffusely distributed throughout the nucleus rather than localized to X"].
- Tissue-specific splicing outside sex determination: redundantly with ASD-1, egl-15 exon 5 choice
  in muscle [PMID:17060915 "an asd-1;fox-1 double mutant was defective in the expression of endogenous egl-15 (5A)"] and unc-32 exon 7a choice in neurons [PMID:23468662 "These results led to the conclusion that ASD-1 and FOX-1 regulate the selection of exon 7a from the unc-32 exon 7 reporter via the UGCAUG stretch in intron 7b in the nervous system."].
- Male mating: fox-1 deletion impairs mating efficiency (abstract only) [PMID:9927456 "Additionally, fox-1(Delta) impairs male mating efficiency, which, we propose, represents another function of fox-1, independent of xol-1 and its role in sex determination."].

## Localization

- Nuclear; C-terminal PY-NLS required [PMID:23468662 "indicating that the C-terminal portions of ASD-1 and FOX-1 are the sole NLSs"].
