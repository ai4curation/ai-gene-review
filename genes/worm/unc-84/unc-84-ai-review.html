Gene Description

UNC-84 is the founding SUN (Sad1/UNC-84) domain protein of C. elegans, a widely expressed type II integral protein of the inner nuclear membrane. Its large N-terminal nucleoplasmic domain binds the B-type lamin LMN-1, and its luminal C-terminal SUN domain binds the KASH domains of outer-nuclear-membrane KASH proteins, recruiting them to the nuclear envelope to form LINC complexes. With the KASH protein UNC-83, which recruits kinesin-1 and dynein, UNC-84 forms the bridge that moves nuclei along microtubules during nuclear migration in embryonic hyp7 precursors and larval P cells (P cells whose nuclei fail to migrate die, causing loss of vulval cells and ventral cord motor neurons). With the giant KASH protein ANC-1, UNC-84 anchors nuclei in the hypodermal syncytium through the actin cytoskeleton. Lamin binding by the nucleoplasmic domain transmits cytoplasmic force to the nucleoskeleton. UNC-84 is targeted to the inner nuclear membrane by redundant nuclear localization and inner-nuclear-membrane sorting signals and requires lamin for its nuclear-envelope retention. In the germline, UNC-84 also pairs with the KASH protein ZYG-12 and promotes repair of DNA interstrand cross-links, in part by limiting nonhomologous end joining and recruiting the FAN-1 nuclease.

Functional annotation report: C. elegans unc-84 (UniProt Q20745)

Executive conclusion

The requested identity is internally consistent: Q20745 is the Caenorhabditis elegans UNC-84 protein, encoded by unc-84/F54B11.3. The literature identifies it as the canonical somatic SUN-domain nuclear-envelope protein, not the distinct, more germline-associated protein SUN-1. Its C-terminal Sad1/UNC-84 homology domain agrees with the supplied InterPro entries IPR045119/IPR012919 and Pfam Sad1_UNC domain PF07738. No evidence was substituted from a similarly named protein in another organism. (starr2019anetworkof pages 2-4, lee2002lamindependentlocalizationof pages 1-2)

UNC-84 is not an enzyme or transporter. Its primary molecular function is structural and mechanical: it is the inner-nuclear-membrane SUN subunit of alternative LINC complexes—linkers of nucleoskeleton and cytoskeleton. Through UNC-83 it supports microtubule-motor-driven nuclear migration; through ANC-1 it supports actin-linked nuclear anchorage. Lamin-dependent retention on the nuclear side and SUN–KASH binding in the perinuclear space allow forces to be transmitted across the nuclear envelope. (starr2019anetworkof pages 2-4, lee2002lamindependentlocalizationof pages 5-7, starr2003anchorsawayan pages 2-4)

1. Identity and domain verification

Correct protein and organism

Foundational genetic, antibody-localization, and biochemical studies consistently concern unc-84 in C. elegans. Endogenous antibodies detected approximately 125- and 99-kDa forms that were absent from the predicted-null unc-84(n369) strain, strongly connecting the observed nuclear-envelope protein to the genetic locus. UNC-84 appears at approximately the 26-cell embryonic stage and is subsequently detected around most embryonic and adult somatic nuclei. (lee2002lamindependentlocalizationof pages 4-5, lee2002lamindependentlocalizationof pages 1-2)

UNC-84 must not be confused with SUN-1. Both names refer to SUN-domain proteins, but SUN-1 is a separate, divergent C. elegans protein with prominent germline and meiotic functions. The somatic migration and anchorage complexes discussed here use UNC-84. (starr2019anetworkof pages 2-4)

Architecture and topology

UNC-84 is predicted to contain one transmembrane segment and a C-terminal SUN domain. Current LINC topology places its N-terminal region on the nucleoplasmic side, associated with the lamina, and its C-terminal SUN domain in the perinuclear lumen, where it binds KASH domains protruding from outer-nuclear-membrane proteins. Early biochemical work established that UNC-84 is an integral membrane protein: it remained pellet-associated after 1 M NaCl or 8 M urea extraction, unlike lamin. The original study did not directly map membrane orientation, so the precise orientation rests on the combined biochemical, localization, interaction, and conserved-LINC evidence rather than an early protease-protection assay. (lee2002lamindependentlocalizationof pages 4-5, lee2002lamindependentlocalizationof pages 2-4, lee2002lamindependentlocalizationof pages 7-8)

2. Cellular localization

Endogenous UNC-84 colocalizes with the single worm lamin, LMN-1/Ce-lamin, at the nuclear rim. Lamin depletion abolishes stable UNC-84 envelope staining, whereas loss of UNC-84 does not displace lamin, emerin, MAN1, nucleoporins, or other envelope markers. UNC-84 is therefore best viewed as a lamin-retained inner-nuclear-membrane component rather than a general nuclear-envelope assembly factor. (lee2002lamindependentlocalizationof pages 1-2, lee2002lamindependentlocalizationof pages 5-7)

Its localization changes during mitosis: UNC-84 remains at the rim through early anaphase, is released during late anaphase, and begins reassociating in telophase. UNC-84 expression and localization do not require UNC-83 or ANC-1; the directionality is instead largely reversed, because UNC-84 and its SUN domain are required to recruit UNC-83 to the nuclear envelope. (lee2002lamindependentlocalizationof pages 4-5, lee2002lamindependentlocalizationof pages 2-4)

3. Primary molecular function: a force-transmitting LINC adaptor

UNC-84–UNC-83 complex: nuclear migration

For migrating somatic nuclei, UNC-84 binds the KASH protein UNC-83 across the perinuclear space. UNC-83 occupies the outer nuclear membrane and recruits kinesin-1 and dynein, thereby turning the nuclear surface into a microtubule-associated cargo platform. This machinery participates in nuclear movements in embryonic hyp7 precursors, larval P cells, intestinal cells, and body-wall muscle. (starr2019anetworkof pages 2-4, gregory2023theinterplayof pages 24-29)

The interface is unusually precise. Adding one alanine to the short C terminus of the UNC-83 KASH domain blocks nuclear migration, and replacement of its conserved −7 tyrosine can permit envelope localization while still eliminating productive migration. These observations show that simple colocalization is insufficient: a mechanically competent SUN–KASH interface is required. (hao2022nesprin12orthologanc1 pages 22-23, starr2019anetworkof pages 2-4)

UNC-84–ANC-1 complex: nuclear anchorage

For stable anchorage, UNC-84 pairs with the outer-membrane KASH protein ANC-1, the worm ortholog of giant nesprins. ANC-1 has cytoplasmic actin-binding elements and provides a route from UNC-84 to the actin cytoskeleton. In unc-84 or anc-1 mutants, nuclei in the hyp7 syncytium become mobile, lose even spacing, and form abnormal clusters. Thus, the same SUN protein supports two separable outputs by selecting different KASH partners: UNC-83 for movement and ANC-1 for anchorage. (starr2003anchorsawayan pages 1-2, starr2003anchorsawayan pages 2-4)

Structure–function genetics further separates the outputs. UNC-84 C953A retained envelope localization, UNC-83 recruitment, and substantial migration function but caused anchorage defects; two independent lines had approximately 16.2% ± 4.0% and 17.2% ± 2.6% clustered nuclei per lateral side. This supports a cysteine-dependent SUN–KASH linkage that is particularly important for ANC-1-mediated anchorage. (cain2018conservedsunkashinterfaces pages 6-8)

4. Biological processes and tissue-level functions

Developmental nuclear migration

Loss of UNC-84 causes failures of defined developmental nuclear migrations. In larval P cells, failed nuclear migration can lead to cell loss and contributes to the classic uncoordinated phenotype. Hyp7 precursors and intestinal cells likewise require the UNC-84/UNC-83 machinery for proper positioning. The broad expression but tissue-restricted severity indicates that UNC-84 is especially important where nuclei undergo directed movement or sustained mechanical loading; redundancy can mask its loss elsewhere. (lee2002lamindependentlocalizationof pages 7-8, starr2003anchorsawayan pages 1-2)

Body-wall muscle nuclei are also mispositioned after disruption of UNC-83/UNC-84. This is consistent with a cell-autonomous mechanical role, although migration and subsequent anchorage can be difficult to distinguish in mature muscle solely from endpoint position.

Nuclear-envelope mechanics

UNC-84 is not generally required to maintain nuclear-envelope spacing in every tissue. Its requirement becomes conspicuous in force-bearing muscle: unc-84-null muscle nuclei exhibit inner-to-outer membrane separations ranging from roughly 40 to 500 nm, whereas most other embryonic and larval tissues retain comparatively normal spacing. The most defensible interpretation is that UNC-84-containing bridges reinforce envelope architecture under load rather than act as universal molecular rulers of perinuclear width. (starr2019anetworkof pages 2-4)

DNA repair: secondary evidence, not the primary annotation

LINC complexes have also been implicated in double-strand-break repair and pathway choice, including promotion of homologous recombination and inhibition of error-prone nonhomologous end joining. UNC-84 has been linked genetically to this process. Nevertheless, the best-supported primary annotation of UNC-84 remains somatic nuclear positioning and mechanical coupling; DNA-repair effects are broader LINC-dependent functions and should not replace the core structural annotation.

5. Recent developments, 2023–2024

Recent work has refined UNC-84’s role less by discovering a new biochemical activity than by revealing parallel mechanisms that compensate for UNC-84/LINC loss during confined nuclear migration.

CDC-42-regulated actin pathway—published in 2023

Larval P-cell nuclei pass through a constriction reported to be approximately 200 nm wide. The unc-84 null phenotype is strongly temperature dependent: fewer than 40% of nuclei migrate at 25°C, whereas at least 90% migrate at 15°C. This residual migration enabled enhancer screens. The resulting work placed CGEF-1 upstream of CDC-42, branched actin/Arp2/3, and NMY-2 actomyosin in a pathway parallel to the UNC-84/UNC-83 microtubule-motor branch. Constitutively active CDC-42 partially rescued cgef-1; unc-84 animals, while CDC-42, Arp2/3, or NMY-2 depletion enhanced the unc-84 defect. (ho2023acdc42regulatedactin pages 4-6, ho2023acdc42regulatedactin pages 1-3, ho2023acdc42regulatedactin pages 33-33)

The peer-reviewed article is Ho et al., “Actin and CDC-42 contribute to nuclear migration through constricted spaces in C. elegans,” Development, October 2023, DOI/URL: https://doi.org/10.1242/dev.202115. Its annotation-level implication is that UNC-84 is one mechanically important branch of a redundant in-vivo migration system, not an upstream component of CDC-42 signaling.

FLN-2 pathway—2023 preprint evidence

A 2023 preprint identified FLN-2 as a third contribution to P-cell migration and envelope integrity. At 15°C, fln-2(tm4687) animals averaged 2.4 missing GABA neurons, compared with 6.6 in fln-2; unc-84 double mutants; fln-2 RNAi in unc-84 animals produced 4.6 missing neurons. Nuclear rupture was enhanced in the double mutant. These experiments predominantly used unc-84(n369) as a sensitized LINC-loss background and do not establish direct UNC-84–FLN-2 binding. (ma2023fln2functionsin pages 1-3, ma2023fln2functionsin pages 5-8)

Source: Ma et al., “FLN-2 functions in parallel to LINC complexes and Cdc42/actin pathways during P-cell nuclear migration…”, posted August 2023, bioRxiv, https://doi.org/10.1101/2023.08.04.552041.

Peripheral heterochromatin—2024 preprint evidence

A 2024 preprint found that CEC-4-mediated anchorage of H3K9-methylated heterochromatin, together with MET-2 and JMJD-1.2, helps P-cell nuclei negotiate constrictions when LINC function is compromised. Loss of met-2 and perturbation of jmjd-1.2 enhanced the unc-84 phenotype. Assays used 40 worms per condition at 15, 20, and 25°C, with 95% confidence intervals and Tukey-HSD correction. Again, UNC-84 served principally as the defined LINC-loss background; this is evidence for mechanical redundancy between envelope–cytoskeleton and chromatin–envelope systems, not a new direct binding partner. (gregory2024anchorageofh3k9methylated pages 8-12)

Source: Gregory, Luxton & Starr, “Anchorage of H3K9-methylated heterochromatin to the nuclear periphery helps mediate P-cell nuclear migration…”, posted May 2024, bioRxiv, https://doi.org/10.1101/2024.05.22.595380.

6. Applications and broader significance

UNC-84 provides a genetically tractable in-vivo model for:

  1. LINC-complex mechanotransduction—testing how defined SUN–KASH residues transmit force rather than merely recruit proteins.
  2. Nuclear migration through confined tissue spaces—a process relevant to development, immune-cell movement, and invasive cancer biology.
  3. Nuclear-envelope integrity under mechanical load—particularly in muscle and during constricted migration.
  4. Functional interpretation of human nuclear-envelope disease mechanisms—worm SUN/KASH and lamin experiments can separate migration, anchorage, and envelope-integrity functions that are difficult to isolate in mammalian tissues.

These are model-system applications rather than clinical implementations of UNC-84 itself. There is no basis to describe worm UNC-84 as a therapeutic target or a currently deployed biomarker.

7. Evidence-weighted annotation

Recommended primary annotation:

UNC-84 is a lamin-associated, inner-nuclear-membrane SUN-domain protein that forms force-transmitting LINC complexes. By binding the outer-nuclear-membrane KASH protein UNC-83, it couples nuclei to microtubule motors for developmental nuclear migration; by pairing with ANC-1, it couples nuclei to the actin cytoskeleton for nuclear anchorage. It also supports nuclear-envelope architecture in mechanically stressed cells.

Confidence: high for nuclear-envelope localization, lamin dependence, UNC-83 recruitment, migration, ANC-1-dependent anchorage, and force-sensitive envelope architecture; moderate for direct full-length UNC-84–ANC-1 biochemistry and secondary DNA-repair functions; emerging for the 2023–2024 compensatory pathway network.

  and requires lamin for its nuclear-envelope retention. In the germline, UNC-84 also pairs with the KASH
