| Identity and domain architecture | Q20745/UNC-84 is the canonical somatic SUN-family protein: a predicted single-pass nuclear-envelope membrane protein with a C-terminal SUN/Sad1–UNC-84 domain exposed to the perinuclear lumen. It is distinct from the divergent germline SUN protein SUN-1. | Broadly expressed in embryonic and adult somatic nuclei from approximately the 26-cell stage; limited or absent in some germ cells. | Endogenous UNC-84 colocalized with lamin at the nuclear rim; salt/urea extraction supported integral-membrane behavior. Antibodies detected approximately 125- and 99-kDa isoforms absent from the unc-84(n369) null mutant (pqac-00000000, pqac-00000001, pqac-00000002, pqac-00000003). | **Strong identity/localization evidence.** Inner-membrane assignment is supported by lamin dependence, membrane behavior, and LINC topology, although the early study did not directly determine membrane orientation. |
| Lamin-dependent nuclear-envelope localization | The nucleoplasmic region associates functionally with the single worm lamin, LMN-1/Ce-lamin, retaining UNC-84 at the inner nuclear membrane and coupling LINC forces to the lamina. | Embryonic and somatic nuclear envelopes. | Lamin depletion abolished UNC-84 nuclear-envelope staining, whereas loss of UNC-84 did not displace lamin, emerin, MAN1, or tested nucleoporins (pqac-00000003, pqac-00000006). | **Strong directional evidence:** lamin is required for stable UNC-84 localization, while UNC-84 is not a general nuclear-envelope assembly factor. |
| Nuclear-migration LINC complex | The luminal UNC-84 SUN domain binds and recruits the outer-nuclear-membrane KASH protein UNC-83. UNC-83 connects the nuclear surface to kinesin-1 and dynein, enabling microtubule-based force transmission. | Embryonic hyp7 precursors, larval P cells, intestinal cells, and body-wall muscle nuclei. | UNC-83 envelope localization requires UNC-84 and its SUN domain. Extending the short UNC-83 KASH terminus by one alanine or replacing its conserved −7 tyrosine disrupts migration despite envelope localization, demonstrating a precise SUN–KASH interface (pqac-00000002, pqac-00000009, pqac-00000012). | **Strong genetic, localization, and interface evidence.** UNC-84 is a structural force-transmission adaptor, not a motor or enzyme. |
| Nuclear-anchorage LINC complex | UNC-84 pairs with KASH protein ANC-1; ANC-1’s cytoplasmic actin-binding region links nuclei to actin. Migration and anchorage complexes use the same SUN protein but different KASH partners. | Hyp7 syncytium and intestine, where nuclei must remain evenly spaced. | unc-84 and anc-1 mutants have mobile, clustered nuclei. UNC-84 C953A retained localization and migration function but caused approximately 16.2% ± 4.0% and 17.2% ± 2.6% clustered nuclei in independent lines, supporting a cysteine-dependent SUN–KASH linkage specialized for anchorage (pqac-00000005, pqac-00000007, pqac-00000008). | **Strong genetic and structure–function evidence.** Direct UNC-84–UNC-83 binding is better established than direct full-length UNC-84–ANC-1 binding, but the ANC-1 LINC model is strongly supported in vivo. |
| Force-bearing nuclear-envelope architecture | UNC-84-containing LINC bridges help maintain membrane apposition when nuclei experience mechanical load; this is not a universal envelope-spacing mechanism. | Body-wall muscle nuclei versus most other embryonic and larval tissues. | In unc-84-null muscle nuclei, inner-to-outer nuclear-membrane separation varied from approximately 40 to 500 nm, whereas spacing remained largely normal in most tissues (pqac-00000000, pqac-00000009). | **Strong tissue-specific ultrastructural evidence.** UNC-84 is especially important for envelope architecture under force, not for basal spacing in every cell. |
| P-cell migration through constrictions | UNC-84–UNC-83 LINC complexes form the microtubule-motor branch that moves nuclei through a narrow in vivo passage; residual migration reveals parallel mechanisms. | Six bilateral pairs of larval hypodermal P cells migrating ventrally through an approximately 200-nm constriction. | unc-84-null animals showed marked temperature dependence: fewer than 40% of nuclei migrated at 25°C, whereas at least 90% migrated at 15°C (pqac-00000016). | **Strong loss-of-function evidence.** UNC-84 is important but not absolutely required under permissive conditions, demonstrating pathway redundancy. |
| Parallel CDC-42/actin pathway — 2023 | CGEF-1 activates CDC-42, which promotes Arp2/3-dependent branched actin; NMY-2-dependent actomyosin assists migration independently of the UNC-84 LINC branch. | Constrained larval P-cell nuclear migration. | CDC-42 depletion and loss of Arp2/3 or NMY-2 enhanced migration failure in unc-84-null animals; constitutively active CDC-42 partially rescued cgef-1; unc-84 mutants. The peer-reviewed Development study chiefly used unc-84 as a LINC-loss comparator rather than identifying a new UNC-84 biochemical activity (pqac-00000015, pqac-00000016, pqac-00000020). | **Strong genetic pathway evidence; indirect for UNC-84 mechanism.** It places the UNC-84 LINC complex parallel to, rather than within, the CDC-42/actin pathway. |
| Parallel FLN-2 pathway — 2023 preprint | FLN-2 supports nuclear-envelope integrity in a third pathway parallel to UNC-84-dependent LINC and CDC-42/actin mechanisms. | Constrained P-cell migration and nuclear rupture. | At 15°C, fln-2(tm4687) animals averaged 2.4 missing GABA neurons, versus 6.6 in fln-2; unc-84 double mutants; fln-2 RNAi in unc-84 animals yielded 4.6 missing neurons. Nuclear rupture increased in both single mutants and was enhanced in the double mutant (pqac-00000017, pqac-00000018). | **Moderate, preprint-stage genetic evidence.** unc-84 was primarily a sensitized LINC-loss background; the study adds network context rather than a new direct UNC-84 interaction. |
| Peripheral heterochromatin pathway — 2024 preprint | CEC-4-dependent anchorage of H3K9-methylated heterochromatin, with MET-2 and JMJD-1.2, supports nuclear mechanics and migration alongside LINC and actin pathways. | Constrained larval P-cell nuclear migration. | met-2 loss and jmjd-1.2 perturbation enhanced the unc-84 phenotype. Assays counted missing GABAergic neurons in 40 worms per condition at 15, 20, and 25°C, with 95% confidence intervals and Tukey-HSD correction (pqac-00000014). | **Moderate, preprint-stage genetic evidence; indirect for UNC-84.** The study used unc-84 null as a sensitized background, indicating that chromatin–envelope mechanics can compensate for loss of LINC force transmission. |
