# snt-1 (UniProt P34693) — Synaptotagmin-1, C. elegans

## Identity and structure
- Reviewed entry SYT1_CAEEL, 441 aa, WormBase F31E8.2. Short N-terminal lumenal segment, transmembrane anchor (70-96) and two cytoplasmic C2 domains (C2A 159-278, C2B 292-425) with the conserved aspartate Ca2+ ligands (from `snt-1-uniprot.txt`). Two splice isoforms (SNT-1A, SNT-1B) differ in three beta-strands of C2B [PMID:17321753 "SNT-1A is the predominant synaptic isoform; elimination of SNT-1A expression leads to severe growth and movement defects."].

## Core function: vesicle-tethered Ca2+ sensor for synchronous release
- First synaptotagmin mutant in any organism: snt-1 animals have severe locomotion, feeding and defecation defects, accumulate ACh and are aldicarb-resistant yet agonist-sensitive, but release is not abolished [PMID:8391930 "The mutants are defective in exocytosis, since they accumulate acetylcholine, and are resistant to cholinesterase inhibitors, but they nevertheless remain sensitive to cholinergic receptor agonists."].
- SNT-1 is the Ca2+ sensor for both tonic and evoked release at the NMJ; evoked release is triggered by Ca2+ binding to C2B, tonic release by either C2 domain, and SNT-1 also clamps spontaneous fusion [PMID:29760174 "functions as the Ca 2+ sensor for both tonic and evoked neurotransmitter release at the Caenorhabditis elegans neuromuscular junction"; "the largely decreased tonic release in snt-1 mutants argues SNT-1 has a clamping function"]. Both C2 domains are required for rescue [PMID:29760174 "Expression of the truncated SNT-1 failed to rescue the locomotion defect ( Fig. 1 A , B ), indicating that both C2 domains are indispensable for the normal function of S"].
- SNT-1 also contributes to priming: the sucrose-releasable pool is reduced and priming of loosely coupled vesicles is lost [PMID:29760174 "the sucrose-evoked quantal release in snt-1 mutants was significantly reduced compared to that in wild-type animals ( Fig. 3 D , E ), indicating a role of SNT-1 in SV priming"].
- The SNT-1 C2AB fragment binds liposomes only in the presence of Ca2+, and Ca2+-site mutations abolish this [PMID:33570571 "Our results showed that WT C2AB of either SNT-1 or SNT-3 cosedimented with liposomes (25%PS/75%PC) in the presence of 1 mM Ca2+; however, in the absence of Ca2+ (1 mM EGTA), no significant levels of C2AB membrane binding were detected"]. SNT-1 is the fast sensor and the vesicle-free SNT-3 the delayed sensor [PMID:33570571 "SNT-1, which is tethered to SVs, SNT-3 is not associated with SV"].

## Localization
- Synaptotagmin is concentrated in synapse-rich regions on synaptic vesicles [PMID:8391930 "In C. elegans neurons, synaptotagmin is localized to regions known to be rich in synapses and appears to be associated with synaptic vesicles."]; anti-SNT-1 staining is the reference synaptic-vesicle pattern colocalising with RAB-3 and SNB-1 [PMID:11717359 "This staining pattern is characteristic of and colocalized with the staining pattern of antibodies directed against other C. elegans synaptic vesicle proteins, including RAB-3, SNT-1, and SNB-1"].
- Either isoform fused to GFP traffics to synapses and is functional; the md220 (delta-VIL, C2B) protein is retained in cell bodies [PMID:17321753 "the protein is entirely retained within the cell bodies instead of being trafficked to synapses"].

## Other roles and phenotypes
- Synaptic vesicle recycling: snt-1 mutants show enlarged vesicles [PMID:33570571 "snt-1 mutants show a right shift in the frequency of SV diameter."]; synaptotagmin is treated as endocytic machinery whose loss suppresses necrotic neurodegeneration [PMID:22157748 "Depletion of the key endocytic machinery components dynamin, synaptotagmin and endophilin suppresses necrotic neurodegeneration induced by diverse genetic and environmental insults in C. elegans."]. The cited GO term is necroptosis (RIPK-dependent programmed necrosis), whereas the paper describes ion-channel-induced necrotic neurodegeneration.
- Behavioural read-outs (pumping, swimming, expulsion) are used to score snt-1 alleles; SNT-1B suffices for the expulsion step of defecation [PMID:17321753 "this isoform is sufficient in vivo to mediate at least one behavior, the expulsion step of the defecation motor program"].
- UNC-4/UNC-37 control synaptotagmin (and other SV protein) levels in specific motor neurons [PMID:11245684 "five different vesicular proteins (UNC-17, choline acetyltransferase, Synaptotagmin, Synaptobrevin, and RAB-3) are substantially reduced in unc-4 and unc-37 mutants"].
