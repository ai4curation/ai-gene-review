# Functional Annotation Report: *C. elegans rimb-1* / RIMB-1 (UniProt Q9N415)

## Executive conclusion

The identity is verified: the requested protein is the *Caenorhabditis elegans* gene product **RIMB-1**, formerly **TAG-168**, corresponding to **rimb-1 / Y39A3CL.2** and UniProt **Q9N415**. The worm literature independently identifies *rimb-1* as formerly *tag-168*, describes two splice isoforms, and assigns the protein to the evolutionarily conserved RIM-binding-protein family. No evidence was substituted from a similarly named gene in another organism. One source of search noise is that the historical *tag-168* promoter is widely used as a pan-neuronal expression driver; such studies do not necessarily investigate RIMB-1 function and were excluded. (kushibiki2019rimb1rimbindingproteinand pages 3-4)

RIMB-1 is **not an enzyme or transporter**. It is a multidomain, intracellular **presynaptic active-zone adaptor/scaffold**. Its best-supported primary function is to help retain and position the UNC-2/CaV2 voltage-gated calcium channel close to release-ready synaptic vesicles. It works in a redundant active-zone network with UNC-10/RIM and is recruited or stabilized by CLA-1/Clarinet, SYD-2/Liprin-α, and probably ELKS-1. This architecture ensures that local calcium influx rapidly triggers synaptic-vesicle fusion. Loss of RIMB-1 generally produces mild phenotypes because parallel scaffolds compensate, but it causes delayed and spatially less precise release and becomes severe when UNC-10/RIM or other channel-tethering mechanisms are also compromised. (kushibiki2019rimb1rimbindingproteinand pages 11-13, janosi2021rimandrimbinding pages 1-3, krout2023c.elegansclarinetcla1 pages 3-5, oh2021unc2cav2channel pages 1-5)

## 1. Identity, nomenclature, and domain architecture

The direct literature identifies *rimb-1* as the former gene **tag-168** and reports two alternatively spliced products, RIMB-1a and RIMB-1b. Both possess one N-terminal SH3 domain, two fibronectin type III-like domains, and two tandem C-terminal SH3 domains—three SH3 and two FN3 domains overall. Sequence similarity to mouse RIM-BP2 and Drosophila RIM-BP supports the RIM-binding-protein assignment. This agrees with the supplied UniProt annotation and InterPro calls for FN3, immunoglobulin-like fold, and RIM-BP SH3 modules. (kushibiki2019rimb1rimbindingproteinand pages 10-11, kushibiki2019rimb1rimbindingproteinand pages 3-4)

SH3 domains generally mediate protein–protein interactions with proline-rich motifs, while FN3 modules commonly provide interaction surfaces in scaffold proteins. In the worm, this structural interpretation has experimental support: rescue constructs lacking the relevant terminal region or third SH3 domain failed to restore normal UNC-2 localization and locomotion in the sensitized *rimb-1; unc-10* background, whereas full-length RIMB-1 and an appropriate N-terminal construct containing that functional module rescued the phenotypes. Thus, the terminal SH3 region is functionally important for calcium-channel organization, although a direct purified-protein binding assay was not reported in the retrieved evidence. (kushibiki2019rimb1rimbindingproteinand pages 10-11, kushibiki2019rimb1rimbindingproteinand pages 11-13)

## 2. Cellular and subcellular localization

RIMB-1 is broadly neuronal. Reporter and tagged-protein experiments detected expression in head and tail ganglia and the dorsal and ventral nerve cords. In GABAergic D-type motor neurons, tagged RIMB-1 forms discrete puncta along neurites and colocalizes with the synaptic-vesicle marker SNB-1/VAMP2 and UNC-10/RIM. RIMB-1 also overlaps with UNC-2/CaV2 in D-type motor neurons and AWC sensory neurons. (kushibiki2019rimb1rimbindingproteinand pages 4-7, kushibiki2019rimb1rimbindingproteinand pages 11-13, kushibiki2019rimb1rimbindingproteinand pages 4-4)

Later CRISPR tagging strengthened this conclusion by avoiding reliance on promoter reporters or overexpressed proteins. Endogenous mScarlet-tagged RIMB-1 and GFP-tagged UNC-2 colocalized at all examined synaptic puncta in the dorsal nerve cord. RIMB-1 should therefore be annotated as an **intracellular, cytoplasmic-face component of presynaptic active zones/dense projections**, rather than a membrane-spanning, extracellular, or secreted protein. (oh2021unc2cav2channel pages 5-8)

UNC-2 also feeds back on RIMB-1 organization. RIMB-1 puncta can still form without UNC-2, UNC-10, or SYD-2, but *unc-2* or *calf-1* loss causes irregular extrasynaptic RIMB-1 signal. UNC-2 is therefore more important for restricting or refining RIMB-1’s precise distribution than for initiating all RIMB-1 recruitment. (kushibiki2019rimb1rimbindingproteinand pages 10-11)

## 3. Primary molecular function

### Calcium-channel anchoring and nanoscale release coupling

The core mechanism is:

**CLA-1/SYD-2/other scaffolds → RIMB-1 plus UNC-10/RIM → positioning and stabilization of UNC-2/CaV2 → localized Ca²⁺ entry → rapid synaptic-vesicle fusion.**

An unbiased enhancer screen of approximately 5,000 haploid genomes identified *unc-10* as the major genetic enhancer of *rimb-1*. Either single mutant retained relatively normal UNC-2 clusters in several assays, but the double mutant showed a profound loss of presynaptic UNC-2. In an AWC-neuron analysis, relative UNC-2-positive area fell to approximately **0.07**, compared with **1.00** in wild type. Conversely, pan-neuronal RIMB-1 overexpression increased UNC-2 puncta. These results establish redundant control by RIMB-1 and UNC-10, rather than requiring RIMB-1 alone for every channel cluster. (kushibiki2019rimb1rimbindingproteinand pages 3-4, kushibiki2019rimb1rimbindingproteinand pages 11-13, kushibiki2019rimb1rimbindingproteinand pages 4-4)

Endogenous-protein imaging refined the model. A *rimb-1* single mutant had approximately wild-type UNC-2 punctum number and intensity, whereas UNC-10 or SYD-2 loss had stronger effects. However, combined loss of RIMB-1, ELKS-1, and/or UNC-10 produced further deficits, showing that the active zone is assembled through overlapping mechanisms. Quantitative fluorescence estimated that active zones contain approximately **four times as much RIMB-1 as UNC-2**; SYD-2 and ELKS-1 were each about twice as abundant as UNC-2, while UNC-10 abundance was comparable to UNC-2. (oh2021unc2cav2channel pages 5-8, oh2021unc2cav2channel pages 1-5)

The most defensible interpretation is that RIMB-1 is an abundant channel-associated scaffold that supplies robustness and spatial precision. It is not the sole determinant of channel delivery. SYD-2 acts further upstream as a major active-zone organizer, UNC-10 has particularly strong control over UNC-2 abundance, and RIMB-1 becomes essential when these parallel routes are weakened. (oh2021unc2cav2channel pages 5-8, oh2021unc2cav2channel pages 1-5)

## 4. Active-zone pathway and interaction hierarchy

### UNC-10/RIM

UNC-10 is RIMB-1’s principal genetically demonstrated redundant partner. The severe *rimb-1; unc-10* channel-localization and transmission phenotypes, compared with mild *rimb-1* phenotypes, are the clearest evidence. RIM links release-ready vesicles and active-zone machinery, while RIMB-1 reinforces channel positioning. Together they align UNC-2-generated calcium microdomains with vesicle-fusion sites. (kushibiki2019rimb1rimbindingproteinand pages 11-13, janosi2021rimandrimbinding pages 1-3)

### SYD-2/Liprin-α and ELKS-1

SYD-2 is upstream of multiple active-zone components. Its loss reduces, but does not abolish, RIMB-1, UNC-10, and ELKS-1 puncta, demonstrating both SYD-2-dependent and independent assembly. RIMB-1 and ELKS-1 make additional, partially redundant contributions to UNC-2 clustering. In a swimming assay, however, *rimb-1; elks-1* mutants did not show clear enhancement, illustrating that molecular redundancy may be assay- and synapse-dependent. (janosi2023characterizationoffactors pages 126-128, oh2021unc2cav2channel pages 5-8, oh2021unc2cav2channel pages 1-5)

### CLA-1/Clarinet: major 2023 development

Krout and colleagues, published **15 May 2023**, used endogenous GFP-RIMB-1, independent *cla-1* alleles, isoform-specific mutants, electrophysiology, and electron microscopy. Complete loss of CLA-1 drastically reduced both the intensity and number of RIMB-1 puncta, whereas deletion of only the long or short CLA-1 isoform did not. The result was reproduced with a second *cla-1* null allele and an independent RIMB-1 probe. CLA-1 is therefore a major upstream determinant of RIMB-1 recruitment or stability at active zones. The evidence demonstrates functional dependency but not necessarily direct physical binding. [DOI: 10.1073/pnas.2220856120](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2220856120). (krout2023c.elegansclarinetcla1 pages 1-2, krout2023c.elegansclarinetcla1 pages 5-7, krout2023c.elegansclarinetcla1 pages 3-5)

CLA-1 and UNC-10 act partly independently: removing both further reduced RIMB-1 intensity, while residual puncta suggested recruitment through SYD-2, ELKS-1, or other components. CLA-1 also has RIMB-1-independent functions, notably in UNC-13 localization and vesicle priming. Consequently, *cla-1; unc-10* animals are substantially more impaired than *rimb-1; unc-10* animals. (krout2023c.elegansclarinetcla1 pages 1-2, krout2023c.elegansclarinetcla1 pages 5-7)

## 5. Consequences for synaptic transmission

### Basal phenotypes and genetic redundancy

*rimb-1* loss causes modest reductions in swimming/body bending and resistance to aldicarb, consistent with reduced or altered cholinergic transmission. Gross neuronal morphology, synaptic-vesicle puncta, and active-zone structure remain largely intact, arguing against a general failure of neuronal development. Pan-neuronal RIMB-1 restores movement, while overexpression can increase aldicarb sensitivity. (kushibiki2019rimb1rimbindingproteinand pages 4-7, kushibiki2019rimb1rimbindingproteinand pages 4-4)

The mild single-mutant phenotype should not be interpreted as biological unimportance. In the 2023 study, *rimb-1; unc-10* evoked NMJ response amplitude was only **36% of wild type** and **56% of the unc-10 single-mutant value**. Nevertheless, this remained much less severe than *cla-1; unc-10*, whose evoked amplitude was approximately **3% of wild type**, confirming that CLA-1 performs additional RIMB-1-independent functions. (krout2023c.elegansclarinetcla1 pages 5-7)

### Release timing and excitation–inhibition balance

Detailed optogenetic and electrophysiological studies found delayed evoked release in both cholinergic and GABAergic synapses. At higher stimulation frequencies, cholinergic currents in *rimb-1* mutants were slightly increased, whereas GABAergic currents were reduced. Removing vesicular GABA release with *unc-47* abolished the apparent cholinergic enhancement and caused approximately an **80% reduction in spontaneous event frequency** in *rimb-1; unc-47* animals. The increased cholinergic current is therefore best interpreted as network-level homeostatic compensation for delayed transmission and disturbed excitation–inhibition balance, not as a direct gain-of-function effect of RIMB-1 loss on acetylcholine release. (janosi2023characterizationoffactors pages 103-107, janosi2021rimandrimbinding pages 1-3, janosi2021rimandrimbinding pages 9-12)

This work was subsequently published in peer-reviewed form in July 2024 as Jánosi et al., *Journal of Neuroscience*, DOI [10.1523/JNEUROSCI.0535-22.2024](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.0535-22.2024). The detailed retrieved text derives from the preceding version posted **7 March 2022**, DOI [10.1101/2021.02.01.429206](https://doi.org/10.1101/2021.02.01.429206), so fine numerical details should be checked against the final article when designing experiments. (janosi2021rimandrimbinding pages 1-3, janosi2021rimandrimbinding pages 9-12)

## 6. Ultrastructural mechanism

High-pressure freezing, serial-section transmission electron microscopy, and optogenetic stimulation showed that *rimb-1* mutants have fewer total and docked synaptic vesicles. The strongest spatial defect occurred within **33 nm of the dense projection**, where fewer vesicles were docked. After stimulation, wild-type fusion was concentrated near the dense projection, while fusion in *rimb-1* mutants shifted distally. Dense-projection size itself was not significantly changed. (janosi2021rimandrimbinding pages 12-14, janosi2021rimandrimbinding pages 9-12)

These observations distinguish two roles:

1. RIMB-1 is **not a primary builder of the dense projection**.
2. RIMB-1 organizes the nanoscale relationship among the dense projection, UNC-2 channels, and release-ready vesicles, thereby improving release speed and fidelity.

Deleting the final ten amino acids comprising the UNC-2 PDZ ligand weakened channel tethering through UNC-10, while combined loss of this ligand and RIMB-1 reduced synaptic UNC-2 fluorescence more strongly. Acute photodegradation of the CCB-1 CaV2 β-subunit reduced spontaneous release frequency by about **75%** within one hour and similarly destabilized channel function. These complementary perturbations support the conclusion that channel tethering and stabilization, rather than catalytic activity, explain RIMB-1 function. (janosi2021rimandrimbinding pages 14-17, janosi2021rimandrimbinding pages 12-14)

## 7. Recent developments, 2023–2024

The major **2023** advance was placement of CLA-1 upstream of RIMB-1 and UNC-2. It expanded the pathway from a simple RIMB-1–UNC-10 pair into a hierarchical but redundant network in which CLA-1 recruits RIMB-1, while CLA-1 and UNC-10 jointly regulate synaptic UNC-2 and neurotransmitter release. (krout2023c.elegansclarinetcla1 pages 1-2, krout2023c.elegansclarinetcla1 pages 3-5)

The **2024** peer-reviewed Jánosi study established the circuit-level consequence: RIMB-1 supports rapid, spatially precise CaV2-triggered release, and its loss produces transmitter-system-specific compensation at the mixed cholinergic/GABAergic NMJ. This connects molecular channel positioning to excitation–inhibition balance. (janosi2021rimandrimbinding pages 1-3, janosi2021rimandrimbinding pages 9-12)

A separate PNAS study published **22 August 2024** found that presynaptic UNC-2 abundance varies inversely with release efficiency, accompanies remodeling of selected active-zone proteins, and is controlled in part by the WWP-1 ubiquitin ligase. This provides important contemporary context: UNC-2 abundance is dynamically homeostatic rather than fixed. However, that study did not establish RIMB-1 as the mediator of WWP-1-dependent feedback, so assigning RIMB-1 a direct role in this pathway would be speculative. [DOI: 10.1073/pnas.2404969121](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2404969121). (xiong2024presynapticneuronsselftune pages 1-2)

## 8. Current applications and experimental utility

RIMB-1 currently has research rather than clinical applications. In *C. elegans*, it is useful for:

- dissecting conserved presynaptic CaV2 channel anchoring and active-zone assembly;
- testing genetic redundancy that can be obscured in single knockouts;
- linking nanometer-scale channel–vesicle organization to release timing;
- investigating excitation–inhibition balance at a mixed cholinergic/GABAergic NMJ;
- benchmarking endogenous CRISPR tagging, quantitative fluorescence, optogenetics, electrophysiology, and high-pressure-freeze EM;
- modeling conserved consequences of CaV2 dysregulation relevant to neurological channelopathies, while recognizing that RIMB-1 itself is not established as a therapeutic target.

Its historical *tag-168* regulatory sequence is also widely used as a pan-neuronal promoter, but this technical use must not be confused with experiments on endogenous RIMB-1 biology.

## 10. Recommended functional annotation

**Molecular function:** presynaptic active-zone protein–protein interaction adaptor; organizer and stabilizer of UNC-2/CaV2 channel localization. No catalytic reaction or transported substrate applies.

**Biological process:** active-zone organization; voltage-gated calcium-channel clustering/positioning; calcium-dependent synaptic-vesicle exocytosis; regulation of release timing and excitation–inhibition balance.

**Cellular component:** neuronal presynaptic active zone/dense projection, on the cytoplasmic side of the presynaptic membrane; punctate localization in nerve cords, ganglia, sensory-neuron synapses, and motor-neuron NMJs.

**Principal partners/pathway:** CLA-1 and SYD-2 help recruit the active-zone scaffold; RIMB-1 works redundantly with UNC-10/RIM and ELKS-1-associated routes to position UNC-2/CaV2 near docked, primed vesicles; calcium influx then activates synaptotagmin/SNARE-dependent fusion.

**Confidence:** high for neuronal/presynaptic localization and the UNC-2/UNC-10-dependent scaffold function; moderate for exact direct physical contacts in the worm, because much of the evidence is genetic, imaging-based, and structure–function based rather than purified biochemical binding; low for any proposed direct involvement in the 2024 WWP-1 homeostatic pathway until targeted *rimb-1* experiments are performed.
