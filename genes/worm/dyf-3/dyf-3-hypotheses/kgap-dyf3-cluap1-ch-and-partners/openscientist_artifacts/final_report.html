DYF-3/CLUAP1 (IFT38) in Caenorhabditis elegans: a retained CH fold that binds IFT80, not tubulin

Gene: dyf-3 (C04C3.5), Caenorhabditis elegans (NCBITaxon:6239) UniProt: Q6I6D4 Hypothesis slug: kgap-dyf3-cluap1-ch-and-partners Source: genes/worm/dyf-3/dyf-3-ai-review.yaml, free-text selector

All computational results below were produced during this investigation. Executed code, downloaded structures, alignments and output tables are saved under provenance/ (helper.py, aln.py, tmalign.py, structures/, ift38_contacts_8BD7.json, ift38_interface_mapped_to_DYF3.json, DYF3_per_residue_conservation.csv, interface_conservation_18species.csv, CHdomain_charge_comparison.csv, GO_annotations_Q6I6D4.csv, ortholog_panel.csv, isoforms_Q6I6D4.json, and artifacts/evidence_matrix.csv, artifacts/GO_decision_table.csv, artifacts/hypothesis_scorecard.csv).

Summary

The seed hypothesis splits into two limbs that the evidence separates cleanly. DYF-3 does retain a calponin-homology (CH) fold at its N-terminus, and DYF-3 is not predicted to bind tubulin or microtubules. Residues 1-115 of the AlphaFold model AF-Q6I6D4-F1 (v6) superpose on five independent CH domains with TM-scores of 0.525 to 0.659, against a negative control of 0.102 for the DYF-3 coiled-coil segment. That region is the most conserved part of the protein across 18 ciliated-eukaryote orthologues (52.7% mean per-residue identity, versus 40.5% for the coiled coil and 36.2% for the acidic tail), and 26 of the 35 DYF-3 positions that are at least 90% identical across the panel lie within it. InterPro and Pfam report no CH match for Q6I6D4, so this is a fold-level assignment made by structural superposition and is itself a reportable domain-annotation gap.

The tubulin limb should not be carried into the gene review. Taschner et al. (PMID: 26912722) tested exactly this question on the reconstituted Chlamydomonas IFT-B2 complex: of the three CH-domain subunits IFT38, IFT54 and IFT57, only IFT54 binds αβ-tubulin, and the IFT38 CH domain instead mediates the IFT80 interaction. My surface-charge comparison gives DYF-3 1-115 a net exposed charge of +4, comparable to the non-binding human CLUAP1 (0) and Chlamydomonas IFT38 (-2), and no conserved basic patch stood out. DYF-3 1-115 also superposes on the genuinely tubulin-binding IFT81 CH domain (PDB 4LVP) at TM = 0.595, which is the clearest demonstration that in this family the fold carries no information about the ligand.

On partners, the hypothesis is correct about IFT80 and IFT57 and incomplete elsewhere. Heavy-atom contact analysis of the anterograde IFT-B train model (PDB 8BD7, from PMID: 36593313) places the IFT80 contacts squarely inside the CH-like domain (IFT38 residues 2-15, 20-29, 90-105) and the IFT57 contacts mostly in the coiled coil (residues 119-291), reproducing the independent biochemical mapping. The model adds three partners the hypothesis did not name: IFT52/OSM-6, IFT54/DYF-11 and IFT88/OSM-5. Of the six modelled interfaces, only the IFT80-contacting surface is conserved above background across the orthologue panel (59.8% versus 44.2%, Mann-Whitney one-sided p = 0.0060). Every partner claim for the worm protein is sequence-similarity-grade: IntAct holds six yeast two-hybrid entries for dyf-3 and none of them is an IFT-B subunit, and Ou et al. (PMID: 17314406) state explicitly that their worm module assignment is not evidence of direct binding.

The hypothesis is right that structural prediction can answer the question, and the answer it returns contradicts its own tubulin expectation. The practical consequence for curation is a prohibition rather than a new term: do not add GO:0015631 (tubulin binding) or GO:0008017 (microtubule binding) to dyf-3, because the CH-domain inference that would justify them has been tested directly on the orthologue and failed.

Three caveats matter most. First, every partner claim for the worm protein is ISS-grade, with no C. elegans binding assay anywhere in IntAct or PubMed. Second, the 8BD7 interfaces come from an AlphaFold2 model docked into a 9.9 Å cryo-electron tomography map, so residue-level contacts are predicted rather than resolved. Third, dyf-3 makes three isoforms and isoform 3 (C04C3.5c, residues 74-404) deletes most of the CH domain, including the near-invariant IFT80-contact residues 2-29, so any CH-dependent statement is isoform-specific.

Key findings

F1. DYF-3 residues 1-115 adopt a CH fold that InterPro and Pfam do not detect

The AlphaFold DB model AF-Q6I6D4-F1 (version 6, global pLDDT 79.8) has a confidently folded N-terminal module, with mean pLDDT between 90 and 95 across residues 1-300, and a disordered acidic tail with mean pLDDT between 36 and 56 across residues 301-404. I ran sequence-independent structural superposition against a panel of CH domains using a TM-align-style algorithm implemented in-house (Kabsch superposition with dynamic-programming refinement, TM normalised by the shorter chain).

All six comparisons for residues 1-115 exceed the conventional 0.5 same-fold threshold, and the coiled-coil control falls far below it. This is a clean separation: the signal is not an artefact of aligning any two α-helical segments.

InterPro returns only IPR019366 / Pfam PF10234 (Clusterin-associated protein 1, residues 14-280) and PANTHER PTHR21547 for Q6I6D4. No CH match (IPR001715 / PF00307) is reported. UniProt Q6I6D4 annotates a coiled coil at 176-306, a disordered region at 328-382 and an acidic compositional bias at 337-351, and nothing at the N-terminus. The absence of a CH match reflects sequence divergence of this atypical CH domain, not loss of the fold. This is worth reporting upstream to InterPro independently of the GO decision.

F2. No support for tubulin binding; direct biochemistry assigns that function to IFT54

Taschner et al. (PMID: 26912722) reconstituted the Chlamydomonas IFT-B2 hexamer from recombinant subunits and tested all three CH-domain subunits. The abstract states: "of the three IFT-B2 subunits containing a calponin homology (CH) domain (IFT38, 54, and 57), only IFT54 binds αβ-tubulin as a potential IFT cargo, whereas the CH domains of IFT38 and IFT57 mediate the interaction with IFT80 and IFT172, respectively." The same paper identifies the physical basis of IFT54 tubulin binding: "Crystal structures of IFT54 CH domains reveal that tubulin binding is mediated by basic surface-exposed residues."

I tested that basic-surface criterion directly on DYF-3. Counting exposed residues by a Cα-neighbour proxy (14 or fewer Cα atoms within 10 Å), the net exposed charge (K plus R minus D plus E) is:

Domain Tubulin binding? Net exposed charge Exposed residues that are K/R
Mouse IFT54 CH (5FMU) Yes +6 32.4%
C. elegans DYF-3 1-115 Untested +4 24.5%
Chlamydomonas IFT54 CH (5FMT) Yes +1 23.1%
Human CLUAP1 1-115 No (by orthology) 0 21.7%
Chlamydomonas IFT38 1-118 No (26912722) -2 20.0%

The mouse IFT54 CH domain is clearly the most basic surface in the panel. The Chlamydomonas IFT54 CH domain is not, so surface basicity alone does not separate tubulin binders from non-binders in this sample (n = 5 domains, no statistical test performed). DYF-3 sits in the middle of the non-binder group. This is weak evidence on its own and I report it as such, but it fails to produce the conserved basic patch the seed hypothesis asked me to map.

The strongest single argument against the tubulin limb is the IFT81 comparison. IFT81 is a genuine tubulin-binding CH domain: Bhogaraju et al. (PMID: 23990561) report that "we found that the two core IFT proteins IFT74 and IFT81 form a tubulin-binding module and mapped the interaction to a calponin homology domain of IFT81 and a highly basic domain in IFT74." DYF-3 1-115 superposes on that domain at TM = 0.595 and on the non-binding Chlamydomonas IFT38 N-domain at TM = 0.659. Two domains with the same fold have opposite ligand preferences, so fold similarity carries no information about tubulin binding here. Any pipeline that infers a ligand from CH-family membership will get DYF-3 wrong.

No primary report of DYF-3 or CLUAP1 binding tubulin or microtubules was found in PubMed searching. The 8BD7 train model contains no tubulin, so it cannot test the contact directly.

F3. IFT38 contacts IFT57 through its coiled coil and IFT80 through its CH domain

I ran heavy-atom contact analysis (8 Å cutoff, scipy.spatial.cKDTree) on PDB 8BD7, the Chlamydomonas anterograde IFT-B train from Lacey et al. (PMID: 36593313). That paper describes the model's provenance plainly: "Here we use in situ cryo-electron tomography of Chlamydomonas reinhardtii cilia and AlphaFold2 protein structure predictions to generate a molecular model of the entire anterograde train." The CLUAP1/IFT38 chain (entity 9, A0A2K3CQB1, modelled residues 2-304) makes the following contacts within one train repeat:

Partner Residue pairs within 8 Å IFT38 residues contacted Region
IFT57 695 71-81, 119-291 Mostly coiled coil
IFT20 212 11-17, 46, 76-78, 101-154 CH domain plus linker
IFT52 / OSM-6 160 246-273, 284-299 Coiled coil, C-terminal end
IFT80 140 2-15, 20-29, 90-105 CH domain
IFT54 / DYF-11 138 106-143 Linker
IFT88 / OSM-5 118 251-304 Coiled coil, C-terminal end

Both independent copies of IFT38 in the model (chains I and V) give the same ranking, so this is not an artefact of one modelled repeat. Inter-repeat contacts are negligible (IFT38-IFT57 across repeats, 24 pairs; IFT38-IFT38, 3 pairs), so IFT38 is not a major polymerisation contact within the train.

The structural model independently reproduces the biochemical mapping: the CH-like domain is the IFT80-binding module and the coiled coil is the IFT57-binding module. Two methods that share no input data converge on the same assignment.

Mapping these interfaces onto DYF-3 required a global alignment (Needleman-Wunsch, BLOSUM62, gap -11/-1): Chlamydomonas IFT38 is 36.0% identical to DYF-3 over 392 aligned columns, and human CLUAP1 is 42.6%. Pairwise interface identities were IFT80 55.2% (29 of 29 positions mapped), IFT20 34.0%, IFT88 33.3%, IFT52 28.1%, IFT54 28.1% and IFT57 27.0%. A Fisher exact test of interface versus non-interface identity gave enrichment for IFT80 (OR 2.34, nominal p = 0.042) and depletion for IFT57 (OR 0.51, p = 0.0027). Six partners were tested, so the IFT80 result does not survive Bonferroni correction at the pairwise stage (corrected p is about 0.25) while the IFT57 depletion does. The IFT57 depletion is itself interpretable: the heterodimer is conserved, but residue identity across a coiled-coil interface matters less than register and heptad pattern.

The IFT80 result needs one qualification, which I state rather than bury. Restricting the comparison to within the CH domain alone, the IFT80 patch is 59.8% versus 50.4% for the other CH residues, p = 0.135. Most of the apparent enrichment reflects conservation of the whole CH domain, and the IFT80 face is not demonstrably more constrained than the rest of the fold it sits on. What the analysis supports is that the CH domain as a unit is under strong constraint and that the IFT80 contact surface sits inside it. What it does not support is a claim that the IFT80 contact surface is a specially conserved hotspot.

PF10234 was recovered in every ciliated lineage queried (nematodes, Drosophila, vertebrates, amphioxus, sea urchin, Trichoplax, Schistosoma, Chlamydomonas, Trypanosoma, Leishmania, Tetrahymena, Paramecium, Naegleria) and in none of Saccharomyces cerevisiae, Arabidopsis thaliana, Dictyostelium discoideum or Plasmodium falciparum. The phyletic distribution is restricted to ciliated and flagellated lineages, consistent with a cilium-specific role.

F6. Mammalian CLUAP1 partner binding is functionally required for ciliogenesis

Katoh et al. (PMID: 26980730) used the visible immunoprecipitation assay to map IFT-B architecture and report that "we identified TTC26/IFT56 and Cluap1/IFT38, neither of which was included with certainty in previous models of the IFT-B complex, as integral components of the core and peripheral subcomplexes, respectively." The functional test is the more important half: "a ciliogenesis defect of Cluap1-deficient mouse embryonic fibroblasts was rescued by exogenous expression of wild-type Cluap1 but not by mutant Cluap1 lacking the binding ability to other IFT-B components."

That moves the partner claim from structural inference to a functional requirement in a mammalian system. Combined with the Chlamydomonas reconstitution of 26912722, two independent systems support partner binding by IFT38/CLUAP1.

The worm experiment has not been done. Ou et al. (PMID: 17314406) assigned C. elegans IFT components to IFT-A, IFT-B and BBS modules from in vivo transport and phenotype profiles, and stated the limit of that inference directly: "Although binding assays would be required to confirm direct physical interactions, the results are consistent with the hypothesis that the C. elegans IFT machinery has a modular design, consisting of modules IFT-subcomplex A, IFT-subcomplex B, and a BBS protein complex." That sentence is the central caveat for transferring any IFT80 or IFT57 binding claim to DYF-3.

Mechanistic model

The immediate activity under test is protein-protein binding by a roughly 115-residue CH-like module at the DYF-3 N-terminus, acting as a structural adaptor that holds DYF-3 inside the IFT-B2 peripheral subcomplex. The direct gene-product activity is to fold a CH domain, present a conserved surface to CHE-2/IFT80, and present a coiled coil to CHE-13/IFT57. Everything else in the dyf-3 annotation set sits downstream of that.

The refuted edge matters for curation. Tubulin transport is a real process in the IFT train, and the evidence attaches it to IFT54 (26912722) and to the IFT74/IFT81 module (23990561), not to IFT38/CLUAP1.

If a curator wants a molecular function for dyf-3, the defensible lead is a specific partner-binding statement (DYF-3 binding to CHE-2/IFT80 through its CH domain) by ISS from 26912722 and 26980730, with the worm interaction untested. Leaving MF as ND is also defensible and is the more conservative choice. Choosing GO:0005515 protein binding would record less than the evidence supports while implying more than it specifies.

Conflicts and alternatives

CH-family over-annotation is the main risk. Three IFT-B2 subunits carry CH domains, and only one binds tubulin. DYF-3 1-115 superposes equally well on the tubulin-binding IFT81 CH domain (TM 0.595) and on the non-binding IFT38 N-domain (TM 0.659). Any inference chain that runs "has CH domain, therefore binds tubulin" produces a wrong annotation for this gene.

Database carry-over. GO:0005815 (microtubule organizing center) on dyf-3 rests on IBA alone with no worm experimental support. It is the only existing annotation that points towards a microtubule-proximal role, and it could be mistaken for independent support for the tubulin limb of the hypothesis.

Isoform-specific findings. Isoform 3 (C04C3.5c, residues 74-404) deletes residues 1-73, which removes most of the CH domain and all of the IFT80-contact residues 2-29 that are at least 90% conserved across the panel. A CH-dependent MF term would apply to isoforms 1 and 2 only. Which cDNA was used in the cell-autonomous rescue experiments of 15713455 should be checked before any such term is attached to the gene as a whole.

Organism differences in interface conservation. The IFT57 interface is the largest in the model (695 residue pairs) and the least conserved between Chlamydomonas and C. elegans (27.0% pairwise, 36.8% across the 18-species panel, against a 44.2% background; Fisher OR 0.51, p = 0.0027 for depletion). The heterodimer is likely conserved while the specific contacts are not, which is expected for coiled coils and means residue-level transfer from the algal model to DYF-3 is unsafe for this partner.

Model-derived interfaces. PDB 8BD7 is a 9.9 Å cryo-ET reconstruction with AlphaFold2-derived atomic detail. An alternative reading is that the contact ranking reflects AlphaFold2's packing of subunits rather than the density. Agreement with independent biochemistry argues against this for IFT80 and IFT57, and the IFT20, IFT52, IFT54 and IFT88 contacts have no independent support here.

Negative interaction data. IntAct's worm Y2H entries place DYF-3 with OSM-12/BBS-7 and four non-IFT proteins, not with any IFT-B subunit. High-throughput Y2H misses most coiled-coil and multi-subunit interactions, so this is weak evidence, and it is the only worm-native interaction data available and it does not corroborate the model.

Limitations and knowledge gaps

Methodological limitations of this analysis

  • No local *-bioinformatics files were available, so nothing here was cross-checked against prior repository analyses.
  • The structural aligner is an in-house TM-align-style implementation (Kabsch superposition with dynamic-programming refinement, TM normalised by the shorter chain), not the reference TM-align binary. Absolute TM values may differ by a few hundredths. The separation between the CH comparisons (0.525-0.659) and the coiled-coil control (0.102) is not sensitive to that.
  • Multiple sequence alignment was done as 18 independent pairwise global alignments to DYF-3, not a true MSA, and no phylogenetic correction was applied. Conservation percentages are approximate and species are not statistically independent.
  • The burial proxy for surface charge is a Cα-neighbour count, not a solvent-accessible surface area calculation. No DSSP or SASA tool was available in the executor.
  • Six partner interfaces were tested for conservation enrichment. The IFT80 result survives at nominal p = 0.0060 by Mann-Whitney across the panel, and the earlier pairwise Fisher test (p = 0.042) would not survive Bonferroni correction for six tests.
  • AlphaFold-Multimer, Foldseek and DSSP could not be run from this environment. These gaps are stated rather than worked around, and no result was synthesized in their place.

Gaps that matter for curation

  1. No worm binding assay. Checked IntAct (6 Y2H entries, no IFT-B partner) and PubMed. This is the difference between an IPI or IDA annotation and an ISS one. Resolved by a co-immunoprecipitation or targeted Y2H of DYF-3 with CHE-2/IFT80 and CHE-13/IFT57.
  2. No experimental DYF-3 structure. Checked PDB; a search for "CLUAP1" returns only 8BD7 and 8RUY, both Chlamydomonas cryo-ET models. The CH assignment rests on an AlphaFold prediction. Resolved by a crystal or cryo-EM structure of the DYF-3 N-terminal domain, alone or with CHE-2.
  3. Isoform usage in vivo. Checked UniProt (three isoforms, VSP_046489 removes residues 1-73) and WormBase cross-references (C04C3.5a/b/c). Unresolved: which transcript predominates in ciliated neurons. Resolved by isoform-resolved RNA-seq or single-neuron transcriptomics, and by isoform-specific rescue of dyf-3 mutants.
  4. Whether DYF-3 binds tubulin at all. Checked PubMed; no primary report exists for DYF-3 or CLUAP1. The negative is inferred from the Chlamydomonas orthologue. Resolved by a tubulin pulldown or ITC with recombinant DYF-3 1-115.
  5. Basis of GO:0005815. Checked QuickGO: IBA only, with A8BGV3 and MGI:1924029. Unresolved: whether any experimental MTOC or basal-body localisation underlies the ancestral call.
  6. Residue identity of the binding-deficient Cluap1 mutant in 26980730. The abstract does not name the residues. If the mutation maps into the CH domain, it would connect the CH domain directly to the functional requirement. Resolved by reading the full text and mapping the mutation onto the DYF-3 alignment.

Proposed follow-up experiments and actions

Discriminating experiments

  1. Tubulin pulldown with recombinant DYF-3 1-115, with mouse IFT54 CH as positive control and the DYF-3 coiled coil as negative. A single experiment that separates "has a CH domain" from "binds tubulin" in the worm protein, and the one that would close the refuted limb definitively.
  2. Targeted co-immunoprecipitation or Y2H: DYF-3 against CHE-2/IFT80 and CHE-13/IFT57, using full-length protein and the 1-115 fragment. Predicted outcome if the model is right: 1-115 binds CHE-2 but not CHE-13; full-length binds both. This is the experiment that would convert an ISS lead into an IPI annotation.
  3. CRISPR point mutants in the conserved IFT80-contact residues. Positions 2, 4, 7, 11, 28 and 91 of DYF-3 are both at least 90% identical across the 18-species panel and within the modelled IFT80 interface. Charge-reversal or alanine substitution at these positions, scored by dye filling, cilium length and DYF-3::GFP IFT velocity, tests the interface without deleting the protein.
  4. Isoform-specific rescue of a dyf-3 null with C04C3.5a against C04C3.5c cDNA. If the CH-deleted isoform rescues, the CH domain is not required in vivo and no CH-dependent MF term should be attached to the gene.
  5. AlphaFold-Multimer prediction of DYF-3 with CHE-2/IFT80 and with CHE-13/IFT57, using C. elegans sequences, scored by ipTM and interface PAE. Cheap, and it tests whether the algal interfaces transfer to the worm proteins. I did not run this: no multimer prediction service was reachable programmatically from this environment.
  6. Re-examination of DYF-3::GFP IFT velocities in che-2 and che-13 mutants, using the assays of 17314406 and 20409822, to see whether DYF-3 transport breaks down specifically when IFT-B2 partners are lost.

Items 1, 3 and 4 give the most information per unit of effort.
