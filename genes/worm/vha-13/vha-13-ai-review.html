Gene Description

VHA-13 is the catalytic subunit A of the cytosolic V1 sector of the vacuolar-type H+-ATPase (V-ATPase) of Caenorhabditis elegans, the orthologue of human ATP6V1A and yeast Vma1. Three A subunits alternate with three B subunits in the V1 hexamer, and ATP is hydrolysed at the A-B interface; the released energy drives rotation of the central stalk and proton translocation through the membrane-embedded V0 sector, so that the holoenzyme pumps protons into the lumen of endosomes, lysosomes, synaptic vesicles and other acidic compartments. In the germline, VHA-13 accumulates on lysosomes of maturing proximal oocytes in a sperm-dependent manner (its mRNA is translationally repressed distally by GLD-1), where the V-ATPase acidifies lysosomes that engulf and clear protein aggregates and thereby supports a metabolic and proteostatic reset before fertilization.

Research report: Caenorhabditis elegans vha-13 (UniProt Q9XW92)

Executive summary

The requested identity is internally consistent and supported by gene-specific worm literature. vha-13/Y49A3A.2 encodes the approximately 69-kDa A subunit of the peripheral V1 sector of vacuolar H+-ATPase (V-ATPase) and corresponds to the conserved ATP6V1A class. The UniProt description, ATPase alpha/beta-chain family assignment, and listed ATP-synthase/V1-A domains all agree with this function. No conflicting literature on a different same-symbol gene was used.

VHA-13 is the catalytic ATP-hydrolyzing component of a multisubunit rotary proton pump. Its best-supported biochemical annotation is MgATP + H2O → MgADP + inorganic phosphate, with hydrolysis-driven rotor motion coupled to H+ translocation through the membrane V0 sector. Thus, ATP/MgATP—not a transported metabolite—is its substrate, while protons are the substrate transported by the assembled holoenzyme. This precise catalytic mechanism is strongly inferred from conserved eukaryotic V-ATPases; purified Q9XW92 kinetics have not been reported in the literature recovered here.

Direct C. elegans evidence places VHA-13 in lysosomal V-ATPase assembly and acidification, particularly during sperm-triggered oocyte maturation. It is also implicated in germline proteostasis and fertility, miR-1-regulated muscle proteostasis during aging, and—at the whole-complex level—in asymmetric pH, NuRD segregation, and daughter-cell fate. The most important recent advance is a 2024 study connecting V-ATPase physically and functionally to asymmetric epigenetic inheritance, although asymmetric imaging in that study used VHA-17 rather than VHA-13 itself.

1. Identity and domain verification

The literature uses vha-13 for a C. elegans V-ATPase subunit and recognizes VHA-13/V1A with antibodies against the A subunit. Independent studies employ vha-13 RNAi, GFP::VHA-13 reporters, endogenous VHA-13 tagging, and anti-V1A/VHA-13 immunoblotting. This is consistent with the supplied identifiers UniProt Q9XW92, WormBase Y49A3A.2, and organism Caenorhabditis elegans (xie2024vacuolarh+atpasedetermines pages 9-11, samaddar2021ageneticscreen pages 5-8, schiffer2021mir1coordinatelyregulates pages 5-7).

The supplied domains—ATP-synt_VA_C, ATP-synth_a_Xtn, ATP_synth_asu-like, ATPase_a/bsu_AS, and ATPase_F1/V1/A1_a/bsu_N—are appropriate for an A/B-family rotary-ATPase subunit. They support a large soluble catalytic-head protein rather than an integral membrane transporter. The literature likewise assigns VHA-13 to the peripheral V1 domain, whereas the integral V0 domain forms the proton-translocation pathway (hahnwindgassen2009thecaenorhabditiselegans pages 11-12, samaddar2021ageneticscreen pages 5-8).

Identity confidence: high. No evidence was found that the studies cited here concern a different organism or an unrelated gene called “vha-13.”

2. Primary molecular function

2.1 Catalytic reaction and substrate specificity

The functional annotation is:

MgATP + H2O → MgADP + Pi, with the free energy of ATP hydrolysis converted into rotary mechanical work and ultimately into proton transport across an organelle membrane.

The physiological nucleotide substrate is therefore ATP coordinated by Mg2+. The products are MgADP and inorganic phosphate. VHA-13 does not by itself constitute the proton pore; proton translocation requires assembly with the membrane V0 sector. No evidence was recovered for an alternative small-molecule substrate or for VHA-13 functioning as an independent ATP transporter.

This reaction assignment is a conserved-mechanism inference, because no biochemical study was found that purified Q9XW92 and measured its Km, kcat, nucleotide selectivity, or inhibitor profile. In 2024 high-resolution single-molecule work on eukaryotic yeast V1, alternating A and B subunits formed an (AB)3 head containing three ATP-hydrolyzing sites; the proposed cycle placed MgATP binding near 112° and MgADP release near 45° of the rotary cycle (yanagisawa2024eukaryoticyeastv1atpase pages 1-2).

2.2 Structural and mechanical role

Three A and three B subunits form the A3B3 catalytic head. ATP hydrolysis at A/B interfaces drives rotation of the central D/F rotor in approximately 120° power strokes. Motion is transmitted through subunit d to the V0 c-ring, where rotation is coupled to proton translocation. Thus, VHA-13 has two inseparable roles: it contributes catalytic chemistry and forms part of the stationary catalytic chamber that converts nucleotide-state changes into rotor motion (yanagisawa2024eukaryoticyeastv1atpase pages 1-2).

The full eukaryotic pump consequently has a division of labor:

  • V1: soluble/cytosolic-facing ATP-hydrolytic motor containing VHA-13.
  • V0: membrane-embedded proton-conducting rotary sector.
  • Peripheral stalks and connecting subunits: prevent co-rotation of the catalytic head and couple V1 to V0.

For Q9XW92 specifically, this model has high confidence because the family and domain architecture are conserved, but worm-specific atomic structure and coupling stoichiometry remain unavailable.

3. Cellular and tissue localization

3.1 Subcellular site of action

VHA-13 is a peripheral V1 protein, so its functional location is the cytosolic face of membranes bearing assembled V-ATPase, particularly lysosomal/endolysosomal membranes. It is not predicted to span the membrane. Direct oocyte imaging showed that germline-expressed GFP::VHA-13 formed puncta that colocalized with LysoTracker-positive acidic lysosomes in sperm-containing hermaphrodites. In unmated females lacking sperm, the reporter was largely diffuse in the cytoplasm (samaddar2021ageneticscreen pages 17-19, samaddar2021ageneticscreen pages 5-8).

After mating, redistribution began in approximately 10 minutes, with distinct foci appearing within 20–30 minutes, coincident with oocyte maturation and release from arrest. RNAi against ER-related genes, ER-to-lysosome trafficking factors, V0 genes, or vha-12 prevented normal lysosomal GFP::VHA-13 puncta. These results support regulated recruitment or assembly of cytosolic V1 components on lysosomal V0 docking sites rather than constitutive lysosomal residence (samaddar2021ageneticscreen pages 17-19, samaddar2021ageneticscreen pages 10-12).

A significant caveat is that endogenous fluorescently tagged VHA-13 was not readily visible in mature oocytes, whereas overexpressed GFP::VHA-13 produced a clear lysosomal pattern. The investigators therefore raised the possibilities that excess protein changes subunit stoichiometry, occupies alternative docking sites, or promotes additional complexes. Localization is compelling but should not be interpreted as a quantitative map of endogenous protein abundance (samaddar2021ageneticscreen pages 19-20).

3.2 Tissue distribution

A C. elegans V-ATPase expression survey reported vha-13 expression in the intestine, body-wall muscle, and excretory cell. These sites fit its observed or inferred roles in lysosomal physiology, muscle proteostasis, and excretory-tube function (hahnwindgassen2009thecaenorhabditiselegans pages 6-8).

Evidence for an excretory role is stronger for the V-ATPase gene set than for vha-13 individually. NHR-31/HNF4 regulated 17 of 19 assessed vha genes, and knockdown of selected V-ATPase subunits produced excretory-canal defects resembling nhr-31 depletion. The study establishes coordinated V-ATPase control in the worm renal-like excretory cell, but its detailed perturbations involved other subunits rather than a definitive vha-13-specific phenotype (hahnwindgassen2009thecaenorhabditiselegans pages 11-12, hahnwindgassen2009thecaenorhabditiselegans pages 6-8).

4. Biological processes and pathways

4.1 Lysosomal acidification and germline proteostasis

The clearest pathway is:

sperm/oocyte-maturation signal → ER and vesicular reorganization → V0 delivery or availability at lysosomes → recruitment of V1/VHA-13 → lysosomal acidification → aggregate clearance.

In oocytes, germline-sensitive vha-13 RNAi caused sterility, whereas an RNAi-defective background did not show the same response, supporting a germline requirement. GFP::VHA-13 puncta coincided with acidic lysosomes; sperm-deficient females showed diffuse VHA-13 and weak LysoTracker staining. More broadly, V-ATPase inhibition promoted protein aggregation in hermaphrodite oocytes (samaddar2021ageneticscreen pages 5-8).

Most knockdowns in a subsequent genetic screen simultaneously reduced LysoTracker-detected acidification and eliminated punctate GFP::VHA-13 localization, supporting a close relationship between assembly/localization and proton-pump activity. ESCRT perturbations could preserve VHA-13 puncta and acidification yet impair aggregate removal, placing at least part of ESCRT-dependent microautophagy downstream of acidification (samaddar2021ageneticscreen pages 19-20, samaddar2021ageneticscreen pages 14-15).

The evidence supports VHA-13 as necessary for this system, but it does not establish that VHA-13 recruitment alone is sufficient to acidify lysosomes. Assembly requires multiple V1 and V0 subunits, membrane trafficking, and appropriate stoichiometry.

4.2 miR-1, muscle proteostasis, and aging

Two 2021 studies placed VHA-13 in a conserved miR-1–V-ATPase regulatory network in muscle. In a polyglutamine Q35 proteotoxicity model, vha-13 RNAi reduced or abolished the improved movement of mir-1 mutants. At day 4 of adulthood, vha-13 mRNA was approximately twofold higher in mir-1 mutants than in age-matched controls, consistent with de-repression when miR-1 is absent (schiffer2021mir1coordinatelyregulates pages 5-7).

Muscle-specific vha-13 overexpression reduced protein aggregates and improved locomotion and pharyngeal pumping under proteotoxic challenge; lifespan increased in two of four experiments. These data indicate that elevating VHA-13 can be beneficial in this specific stress model, but the inconsistent lifespan result and the stoichiometric nature of V-ATPase assembly argue against assuming that unrestricted overexpression will generally enhance pump activity (schiffer2021mir1coordinatelyregulatesa pages 15-17).

Indeed, V-ATPase is a multisubunit machine whose output depends on balanced assembly. The biological interpretation is therefore not simply “more VHA-13 equals more acidification.” Rather, miR-1 appears to coordinate multiple lysosomal and mitochondrial components; changing one subunit can be beneficial, neutral, or disruptive depending on tissue, age, proteotoxic load, and availability of partner subunits.

4.3 Asymmetric pH, epigenetic segregation, and cell fate: 2024 advance

A July 2024 eLife study identified 12 V-ATPase subunits in HDA-1/NuRD co-immunoprecipitation–mass spectrometry and validated the physical association by co-immunoprecipitation and immunoblotting with an anti-V1A/VHA-13 antibody. Pharmacological V-ATPase inhibition with bafilomycin A1 reduced the QR.ap:QR.aa pHluorin ratio from approximately 1.6-fold to 1.3-fold, disrupted asymmetric HDA-1/NuRD segregation, and allowed the normally apoptotic daughter to survive and extend a neurite-like process. Depleting HDA-1 restored apoptosis, linking altered NuRD inheritance to the fate change (xie2024vacuolarh+atpasedetermines pages 9-11).

The study proposed that asymmetric V-ATPase segregation creates unequal cytosolic acidification, helping direct NuRD preferentially into the surviving daughter. NuRD asymmetry was reported at approximately 1.5–1.8-fold between relevant nuclei and approximately 1.3-fold between daughter cells (xie2024vacuolarh+atpasedetermines pages 13-15).

This is important recent evidence but must be interpreted precisely. The asymmetric live imaging used VHA-17, a V0 e subunit, rather than VHA-13. Therefore, the study directly establishes VHA-13 as a biochemical marker/component of the NuRD-associated V-ATPase complex, but it does not prove that VHA-13 itself is asymmetrically segregated or independently controls the phenotype (xie2024vacuolarh+atpasedetermines pages 9-11, xie2024vacuolarh+atpasedetermines pages 11-13).

5. Current applications and real-world relevance

VHA-13 currently has its clearest value as a research handle, not a validated clinical or agricultural target:

  1. Reporter of V-ATPase assembly. GFP::VHA-13 redistribution provides a live readout of sperm-triggered V1 recruitment to acidic oocyte lysosomes and can identify ER, trafficking, and V0 factors required for pump assembly (samaddar2021ageneticscreen pages 17-19, samaddar2021ageneticscreen pages 10-12).
  2. Genetic probe of lysosomal proteostasis. vha-13 RNAi and overexpression are used to dissect aggregate clearance, germline rejuvenation, muscle proteotoxicity, and aging-associated movement phenotypes (schiffer2021mir1coordinatelyregulates pages 5-7, samaddar2021ageneticscreen pages 5-8, schiffer2021mir1coordinatelyregulatesa pages 15-17).
  3. Bridge to human ATP6V1A biology. Conservation of the A subunit and rotary catalytic mechanism makes the worm useful for studying consequences of altered V-ATPase dosage or assembly, although worm phenotypes should not be treated as direct disease models without human validation.
  4. Mechanistic marker in developmental cell biology. Anti-VHA-13 provides biochemical access to V-ATPase-associated protein complexes such as NuRD during asymmetric division (xie2024vacuolarh+atpasedetermines pages 9-11).

Because V-ATPase supports many essential compartments and tissues, systemic inhibition is likely to be pleiotropic. The worm data instead favor tissue-, developmental-stage-, assembly-, or regulator-specific interventions.

6. Evidence appraisal and expert interpretation

The strongest direct conclusions are that VHA-13 is a V1 V-ATPase component, is recruited to lysosomes during oocyte maturation, is required for germline and proteostasis-associated functions, and participates in a miR-1-regulated muscle program. The exact chemical mechanism is exceptionally well conserved but is inferred from homologous V-ATPases rather than measured with purified worm protein.

The literature also demonstrates why localization and expression should not be equated automatically with activity. Functional proton pumping requires complete V1–V0 assembly, correct subunit balance, and membrane targeting. The discrepancy between endogenous and overexpressed oocyte reporters reinforces this point, as does the context dependence of vha-13 overexpression (schiffer2021mir1coordinatelyregulatesa pages 15-17, samaddar2021ageneticscreen pages 19-20).

Recent research expanded the conceptual role of V-ATPase beyond bulk organelle acidification. The 2024 asymmetric-division study supports a model in which spatially restricted proton-pump activity contributes to cytosolic-pH differences, epigenetic-complex inheritance, and life-versus-death fate choice. Nevertheless, this remains a whole-complex conclusion rather than proof of a unique, separable signaling function for VHA-13 (xie2024vacuolarh+atpasedetermines pages 9-11, xie2024vacuolarh+atpasedetermines pages 13-15).

7. Knowledge gaps

No study recovered here reported purified Q9XW92 kinetics, nucleotide-selectivity panels, Km or kcat, proton-to-ATP coupling ratio, a worm VHA-13 structure, or catalytic-site mutagenesis. Gene-specific 2023 literature was not identified; the principal recent advance is the 2024 NuRD/pH study. Direct endogenous localization remains less certain than overexpression-reporter localization, and several excretory, developmental, and acidification phenotypes were measured after perturbing the complete V-ATPase or other subunits rather than vha-13 alone.

Conclusion

VHA-13 is best annotated as the catalytic A subunit of the cytosolic V1 motor of C. elegans V-ATPase. It hydrolyzes MgATP within an A3B3 catalytic head, and the resulting rotary motion powers V0-mediated proton transport. It acts principally on the cytosolic face of lysosomal/endolysosomal membranes after regulated assembly. Direct worm evidence connects it to sperm-triggered lysosomal acidification, germline fertility and aggregate clearance, and miR-1-dependent muscle proteostasis. Emerging 2024 work further places the VHA-13-containing complex in pH-dependent asymmetric epigenetic inheritance, while leaving open the extent to which this developmental function is uniquely attributable to VHA-13.

(vha-13-notes.md)

vha-13 (Q9XW92) — V-type proton ATPase catalytic subunit A — curation notes

Identity

  • UniProt Q9XW92 (VATA_CAEEL, reviewed), WormBase Y49A3A.2, 606 aa, EC 7.1.2.2.
  • Orthologue of human ATP6V1A / yeast Vma1. Belongs to the ATPase alpha/beta chains
    family; carries the F1/V1/A1 alpha/beta N-terminal domain (IPR004100), the
    nucleotide-binding domain (IPR000194) and the ATPase alpha/beta signature (IPR020003).
  • Subunit A is the catalytic nucleotide-binding subunit of the cytosolic V1 sector; three
    A/B heterodimers form the hexameric ring in which ATP is hydrolysed at the A–B interface
    (UniProt, by similarity to mammalian ATP6V1A).

Experimental literature

Bohnert & Kenyon 2017 (PMID:29168500, full text cached)

  • An RNAi screen of ~60 fertility/proteostasis genes identified vha-13 as required for the
    sperm-triggered clearance of protein aggregates in oocytes: "This gene, vha-13, encodes a
    catalytic subunit of the V-ATPase, a lysosomal proton pump" PMID:29168500.
  • The phenotype is shared by the holoenzyme: "Knockdown of other V-ATPase subunits produced
    similar protein-aggregation phenotypes" PMID:29168500.
  • vha-13 is required for lysosomal acidification in the proximal germline: "hermaphrodite,
    but not female, germlines exhibited acidic lysosomes (Fig. 2d, e; Extended Data Fig. 4a)
    that accumulated specifically in maturing, proximal oocytes in a V-ATPase-dependent
    manner" PMID:29168500.
  • Localization: "GFP-tagged VHA-13 was present in proximal, but not distal, germ cells" and
    "As V-ATPase puncta developed, they co-localized with LMP-1::GFP-positive vesicles"
    (LMP-1 = LAMP orthologue, i.e. lysosomes) PMID:29168500.
  • Conclusion: "we conclude that sperm trigger V-ATPase accumulation in proximal oocytes,
    thereby inducing lysosome acidification" PMID:29168500.
  • Translational control: vha-13 mRNA is repressed distally by GLD-1; a tbb-2 3'UTR reporter
    extends GFP::VHA-13 expression distally PMID:29168500.
  • Downstream effects (mitochondrial remodelling, drop in ATP:ADP ratio) are consequences of
    lysosomal activation, not additional molecular functions of vha-13.

Liegeois et al. 2006 (PMID:16785323, not cached)

  • UniProt cites this paper for a role of vha-13 in receptor-mediated endocytosis (the paper
    is primarily about the V0 sector, vha-5/vha-6, and apical exosome secretion in the
    epidermis). Not in the GOA rows for vha-13; not cached, so not used for review decisions.

Assessment

  • All V-ATPase functional annotations (GO:0046961 proton-transporting ATPase activity,
    rotational mechanism; GO:1902600 proton transmembrane transport; V1 domain membership;
    lysosomal membrane) are consistent with the identity of the protein and with the
    experimental lysosome-acidification phenotype.
  • The IMP annotation GO:0007042 lysosomal lumen acidification is directly supported by the
    full-text evidence above.
  • GO:0046034 ATP metabolic process (IEA from InterPro) is technically true but is a
    by-product of the coupled ATPase; over-annotation relative to the informative transport
    terms.
  • Synaptic-vesicle acidification: vha-13 is the sole catalytic A subunit gene in C. elegans,
    so the neuronal V-ATPase that acidifies synaptic vesicles necessarily uses VHA-13, but no
    vha-13-specific neuronal experiment is in the GOA set; no NEW annotation proposed.

Deep research

  • vha-13-deep-research-falcon.md (Edison Scientific Literature) corroborates the review:
    it assigns VHA-13 the catalytic A subunit role ("It hydrolyzes MgATP within an A3B3
    catalytic head, and the resulting rotary motion powers V0-mediated proton transport."),
    notes that no purified Q9XW92 kinetics exist, and adds later work not in the GOA set
    (miR-1 regulation of vha-13 in muscle proteostasis; V-ATPase association with HDA-1/NuRD
    during asymmetric division). None of it contradicts the annotation decisions taken here,
    and none of it is used as the sole basis for an action.
