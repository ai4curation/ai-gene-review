DYF-6 (Q0G838) and the IFT46-IFT52 interface: hypothesis evaluation

Gene: Caenorhabditis elegans dyf-6 / DYF-6, UniProt Q0G838, NCBITaxon:6239, organism code worm. Hypothesis slug: kgap-dyf6-ift52-interface-conservation. Source: genes/worm/dyf-6/dyf-6-ai-review.yaml, free-text selector. Focus type: free_text. No term context or reference context was supplied.

Summary

The seed hypothesis makes three claims and they do not stand or fall together. The first claim is supported: DYF-6 contacts the IFT-B1 core through its C-terminal IFT46_B_C domain and retains the IFT52-binding interface at residue resolution. Forty-four of the 45 IFT52-contacting residues resolved in the IFT46 C-terminal domain map onto DYF-6, with 23 identical and 9 conservatively substituted. Contact-weighted interface identity is 62.0 %, against a within-domain permutation null of 44.0 % from 20000 label shuffles (one-sided p = 0.0163). The high-contact anchors are retained without exception: W259 to W388 (48 heavy-atom contacts), I306 to I430 (33), F313 to F437 (29), E264 to E393 (28), L281 to L410 (23), H310 to H434 (20), F319 to F443 (20), K236 to K365 (18). The partner surface is conserved too: the 35 IFT46-contacting residues of IFT52 map onto C. elegans OSM-6 at 47.2 % contact-weighted identity, keeping the highest-contact position K430 as K426 (63 contacts). The AlphaFold DYF-6 model superposes on the crystallised epitope at 1.16 Å core Cα RMSD against a matched self control of 0.79 Å.

The second claim needs a factual correction. The hypothesis attributes the IFT52-IFT46 crystal structure to Chlamydomonas reinhardtii. PDB 4UZZ is the Tetrahymena thermophila IFT52-46 complex. PMID: 25349261 states that the authors "determined the crystal structures of C. reinhardtii IFT70/52 and Tetrahymena IFT52/46 subcomplexes". No Chlamydomonas IFT52-IFT46 crystal structure exists. The conclusion survives the correction, since the Tetrahymena structure supports it, but the review text cites a structure that does not exist as described.

The third claim is refuted at motif resolution. The N-terminal region of nematode IFT46 proteins is globally divergent, which the hypothesis gets right: the N-terminal to C-terminal Smith-Waterman score ratio is 0.32 in C. elegans against 0.56-0.65 in five motile-cilia species, with no overlap across five nematodes. An unbiased 15-residue sliding scan of the experimentally defined ODA16-binding fragment (Chlamydomonas IFT46 residues 1-147, PMID: 28298440) locates the single best-conserved block at residues 119-133, LFSYIGRYKPQTVEL. DYF-6 retains it at residues 254-268 as ILTYIDAYKTQEVEL: 8 of 15 identical, BLOSUM62 score 40 against a shuffled-sequence null of 10.2 ± 3.9 (empirical p < 0.0005), at the 43rd percentile of the motile-cilia set. C. briggsae retains it equally. Loss of the cargo-adaptor determinant cannot be asserted from sequence.

Executive judgment

Verdict: partially supported, with one factual correction and one refuted clause.

Direct answers to the two questions the hypothesis poses. DYF-6 is predicted to bind OSM-6/IFT52 directly through the IFT46_B_C domain, on the basis of structure-mapped conservation of both contact surfaces, the native anterograde train architecture, and the AlphaFold monomer geometry of DYF-6 itself. This remains a prediction, because no C. elegans DYF-6-OSM-6 binding assay was found in the literature. DYF-6 retains a candidate cargo-adaptor region, contrary to the hypothesis, although what such a region would adapt in an organism without outer dynein arms is unknown.

The most important caveats are three. AlphaFold-Multimer could not be run in this environment, so the requested DYF-6/OSM-6 complex prediction does not exist in this work; it was substituted with superposition of AlphaFold monomer models onto the crystallised epitope plus independent mapping of both contact surfaces, and every number reported comes from that substitute. The motif identification rests on inference: PMID: 39880089 reports "a conserved N-terminal motif in Chlamydomonas IFT46 that mediates its binding to one side of the ODA16 structure" without giving coordinates in its abstract, so equating window 119-133 with that motif comes from an independent conservation scan rather than a confirmed assignment. Per-residue Fisher tests at the interface are underpowered at n = 44 (OR 1.66, p = 0.238), which is why the permutation test on contact-weighted identity carries the statistical weight.

Key findings

F1. DYF-6 retains the IFT46 side of the IFT52 interface

PDB 4UZZ at 2.32 Å resolution contains chain A, the T. thermophila IFT46 C-terminal domain (UniProt Q23KH7 221-332, PDB numbering = UniProt + 15), and chain B, the T. thermophila IFT52 C-terminal fragment (I7LT74 371-434, PDB numbering = UniProt + 169). Computing all heavy-atom pairs at 4.5 Å gives 45 IFT46 residues in contact with IFT52. Smith-Waterman alignment of C. elegans DYF-6 to the 116-residue chain A fragment, using BLOSUM62 with affine gaps of -11 open and -1 extend, aligns 108 residues at 44.4 % identity and covers 44 of the 45 interface positions.

At those interface positions DYF-6 is 52.3 % identical (23 of 44) against 39.7 % (25 of 63) at non-interface positions of the same domain. The Fisher odds ratio is 1.66 with p = 0.238, two-sided, which is underpowered at this sample size. Weighting each position by its heavy-atom contact count raises interface identity to 62.0 %, and that is the statistic the permutation test addresses in F3.

The distribution of substitutions matters more than the aggregate. Every high-contact anchor is conserved. The divergent positions cluster in one peripheral loop, Tetrahymena 301-305 mapping to DYF-6 426-429, and at one aromatic position, Y316 to L440. The Chlamydomonas residues L285 and L286, defined by mutation as the IFT52-binding determinants in PMID: 28302912, map through the Tetrahymena reference onto DYF-6 I420 and V421, which are conservative branched-hydrophobic substitutions.

F2. The partner surface is conserved in OSM-6

A conserved interface needs both sides. The IFT52 side of 4UZZ contributes 35 residues within 4.5 Å of IFT46. Mapping these onto IFT52 orthologs gives interface identity and contact-weighted identity of 61.8 % / 70.4 % for human IFT52 (Q9Y366), 53.1 % / 63.5 % for Chlamydomonas IFT52 (Q946G4), and 30.3 % / 47.2 % for C. elegans OSM-6 (G5EDF6, 33 of 35 positions covered).

Unweighted identity is lowest in C. elegans, but the weighting changes the picture: the highest-contact positions are retained. TtIFT52 K430 with 63 heavy-atom contacts becomes OSM-6 K426; K416 (34) becomes K412; N385 (30) becomes N380; Y395 (28) becomes F390; L426 (25) becomes L422; L392 (24) becomes L387; F429 (22) becomes F425; F373 (20) becomes F368; K431 (20) becomes R427. The single largest change is TtIFT52 N389 with 49 contacts becoming OSM-6 E384, an asparagine-to-glutamate substitution at the second-highest contact position on that side.

The C. elegans pair therefore keeps the buried hydrophobic and lysine anchors on both surfaces. The faster drift on the IFT52 side means the contact is conserved in architecture and anchor chemistry, and compensatory coevolution at the N389/E384 position cannot be excluded without a direct assay.

F3. The conservation survives a burial control and a permutation test

The obvious competing explanation is that interface residues are conserved in DYF-6 only because they are buried hydrophobics, with no interface-specific selection. Testing this required a burial measure computed from chain A alone, so that the IFT52 partner does not contribute: heavy-atom neighbours within 8 Å per residue atom, median 55.7 across the 100 mapped residues.

Stratifying by burial gives the opposite of what the confound predicts. The interface-versus-non-interface identity gap is present in the exposed half (60.0 %, n = 20, against 36.7 %, n = 30; Fisher OR 2.59, p = 0.149) and nearly absent in the buried half (45.8 %, n = 24, against 38.5 %, n = 26; OR 1.35, p = 0.775). Logistic regression of DYF-6 identity on interface status and burial gives an interface coefficient of +0.618 (SE 0.412, z = 1.50, p = 0.134) with burial itself non-significant at -0.016 (p = 0.251).

The informative statistic is the permutation test. Shuffling identity labels across the whole domain 20000 times gives an observed contact-weighted interface identity of 62.0 % against a null mean of 44.0 %, one-sided p = 0.0163. The epitope is conserved above the background rate of its own domain, and the signal is weighted toward the high-contact positions.

F4. Three independent structural lines place the contact on the C-terminal domain

Kabsch superposition of AlphaFold model AF-Q0G838-F1-v6 onto 4UZZ chain A, using the 44 sequence-mapped interface Cα atoms, gives 2.91 Å over all pairs and 1.16 Å over the 37-atom core after removing pairs deviating more than 3 Å. Mean pLDDT over those residues is 85.4. The five largest deviations fall at DYF-6 365, 368 and the 426-427 loop, the same positions that are sequence-divergent.

The control matters here. An initial comparison of the AlphaFold Tetrahymena model against all 108 crystal Cα atoms gave 10.88 Å, which is misleading because it mixes a well-predicted epitope with poorly predicted flanking regions. Evaluating the same self control on the identical 44 interface positions gives 2.02 Å over all pairs and 0.79 Å over the 40-atom core. DYF-6 therefore reproduces the crystallographic epitope almost as well as the Tetrahymena protein's own AlphaFold model does.

In the cryo-EM anterograde train model 8BD7 (Chlamydomonas, 9.9 Å, fitted coordinates), IFT46 is modelled only over residues 212-344, which is the IFT46_B_C domain alone. Every modelled IFT46 residue within 12 Å of IFT52 lies in that domain, and the contacting IFT52 residues are 355-454, exclusively its C-terminal region. The resolution permits a domain-level statement, not a residue-level one.

F5. The ODA16-region motif is retained in DYF-6, refuting the loss claim

The N-terminal region of nematode IFT46 proteins is genuinely divergent. Smith-Waterman scores against Chlamydomonas IFT46 residues 1-147 give a mean of 161.7 with 57.3 % coverage in motile-cilia species (Danio 200, Xenopus 184, human 164, mouse 164, Trypanosoma 146) and 70.0 with 21.0 % coverage in nematodes (Brugia 88, Strongyloides 84, C. elegans 80, C. briggsae 73, Trichinella 25). Excluding a Drosophila outlier whose C-terminal alignment is itself poor (score 87), the N/C score ratios do not overlap: 0.56-0.65 against 0.11-0.40. Nematode-to-motile identity ratio is 0.67 over the N-terminal region against 1.16 over the C-terminal domain, confirming the divergence is region-specific. AlphaFold assigns DYF-6 residues 1-250 a mean pLDDT of 46.4 against 85.3 for 349-449, and UniProt annotates residues 1-202 as disordered with acidic compositional bias at 89-99 and 231-246.

That global picture does not survive contact with the motif-level test. An unbiased 15-residue sliding scan across CrIFT46 1-147 identifies residues 119-133, LFSYIGRYKPQTVEL, as the single best-conserved window in motile-cilia orthologs: mean per-position identity 0.444 against 0.138 across the whole fragment. A targeted ungapped BLOSUM62 search of that 15-mer in the N-terminal 60 % of each ortholog places the DYF-6 match at residues 254-268, ILTYIDAYKTQEVEL, with 8 of 15 identical, score 40, retaining the tyrosine pair, the lysine and the glutamine that anchor the block. Against 2000 shuffles of the same DYF-6 region the null best score is 10.2 ± 3.9, giving an empirical p < 0.0005. The spacing is positionally homologous: the motif sits 211 residues before the Chlamydomonas C-terminus and 203 before the DYF-6 C-terminus.

The motif does not discriminate ciliary motility. Trypanosoma brucei scores 3 and Danio rerio 12, both below the two nematodes that retain the block, and the motile-versus-nematode comparison is not significant (medians 52 against 28, Mann-Whitney one-sided p = 0.197).

Mechanistic model and interpretation

The supported mechanism is narrow and specific. DYF-6 residues 345-455 form the IFT46_B_C domain (Pfam PF12317, InterPro IPR022088) and present a contiguous surface to the C-terminal region of OSM-6/IFT52. The anchor is a tryptophan at DYF-6 W388, corresponding to the residue making 48 heavy-atom contacts in the crystal, packing against a lysine-rich IFT52 surface whose highest-contact residue at 63 contacts is retained as OSM-6 K426. This contact places DYF-6 in the IFT-B1 core, consistent with its movement along cilia by IFT (PMID: 16648645) and with the native train architecture where IFT46 engages IFT52 through this domain alone (PDB 8BD7).

The scope does not extend to the N-terminal 250 residues as a folded unit. AlphaFold assigns them a mean pLDDT of 46.4 against 85.3 over 349-449, so that region is predicted disordered or conditionally folded. The retained 254-268 motif sits at the boundary, just before the ordered domain, at the same distance from the C-terminus as its Chlamydomonas counterpart. That position is where a short linear motif that engages a partner on demand would be expected to sit, and short linear motifs inside disordered regions are exactly the features that whole-region conservation scores miss.

Two readings of the N-terminal data compete, and the data favour the second. The hypothesis reads the divergence as adaptive loss following the disappearance of motile cilia. Three observations resist that reading. The motif that the conservation scan singles out is retained in C. elegans and C. briggsae at human-comparable identity. Two motile-cilia organisms, Trypanosoma and Danio, score at or below the nematodes in the targeted search. Human ODA16 shows no detectable direct IFT46 interaction in vitro (PMID: 32239748), so the adaptor interaction is not uniformly present even where outer dynein arms are.

The simpler alternative fits the numbers: the IFT46 N-terminal region is poorly conserved everywhere, with mean per-position identity to CrIFT46 1-147 of only 0.138 even within the motile-cilia set, and nematodes sit at the low end of a broadly permissive distribution with the one constrained block preserved. Under this reading the low N/C ratio reflects relaxed constraint on a largely disordered linker, with ODA loss neither required nor excluded as the cause.

Evidence base

The structural backbone of this evaluation comes from three papers. PMID: 25349261 (Taschner 2014) supplies PDB 4UZZ and states its functional significance: "The structure of IFT52C/IFT46C was solved at 2.3 Å resolution, and we show that it is essential for IFT-B core integrity by mediating interaction between IFT88/70/52/46 and IFT81/74/27/25/22 subcomplexes." The same abstract carries the correction to the seed hypothesis, since the authors "determined the crystal structures of C. reinhardtii IFT70/52 and Tetrahymena IFT52/46 subcomplexes". PMID: 36354106 (Petriman 2022) places IFT46 and IFT52 in the IFT-B1 module: "The IFT-B complex organizes into IFT-B1 and IFT-B2 parts with binding sites for ciliary cargo and the inactive IFT dynein motor, respectively." PMID: 20435895 (Lucker 2010) provides the independent biochemistry: "three of the core B subunits, IFT88, IFT52, and IFT46, interact directly with each other and, together, are capable of forming a ternary complex", and shows the complex functions in the moving particle, since "electroporation of recombinant IFT46 was used to rescue flagellar assembly of a newly identified ift46 mutant and to monitor in vivo localization and movement of the IFT particles."

The residue-level and module-level genetics come from two Chlamydomonas papers. PMID: 28302912 (Lv 2017) names the determinants: "IFT52 interacts with IFT46 through residues L285 and L286 of IFT46 and recruits it to basal bodies", and shows the region suffices in vivo, since "The C-terminal sequence between residues 246-321, termed BBTS3, was sufficient to target YFP to basal bodies in the ift46-1 strain." PMID: 28701346 (Hou and Witman 2017) separates the two modules cleanly: "The IFT46 C-terminus can assemble into and stabilize IFT-B but does not support transport of outer arm dynein into flagella", concluding that "the IFT46 N-terminus, ODA16, and outer arm dynein interact for IFT of the latter."

The cargo-adaptor side rests on PMID: 28298440 (Taschner 2017), which defines the fragment that the motif scan used: the ODA16 domains "are both required for the association with the N-terminal 147 residues of IFT46". PMID: 39880089 (Wang 2025) narrows that fragment further, reporting that the authors "identify a conserved N-terminal motif in Chlamydomonas IFT46 that mediates its binding to one side of the ODA16 structure", which is the claim my independent conservation scan recovers a candidate for without being able to confirm the coordinates. PMID: 32239748 complicates the picture by reporting that "size exclusion chromatography and pull-down experiments failed to detect a direct interaction between human ODA16 and IFT46."

The only direct C. elegans evidence is PMID: 16648645 (Bell 2006), which establishes the gene identity and the cellular phenotype: "Phenotypic analysis of dyf-6 mutants expressing an OSM-6::GFP reporter indicates that the cilia of the amphid and phasmid dendritic endings are foreshortened", and "Movement of DYF-6::GFP within the ciliated endings of the neurons indicates that DYF-6 is involved in IFT." This paper supports every experimental annotation the gene currently carries and addresses no binding question.

Other IFT46 literature in the reviewed set bears on vertebrate and ciliopathy biology rather than on this hypothesis: zebrafish promoter-driven ciliopathy models (PMID: 37363725), neural crest conditional knockout and anterior segment dysgenesis (PMID: 38517430), Paramecium IFT46 depletion (PMID: 29915351), ODA transport kinetics in vivo (PMID: 30133350), and the SARS-CoV-2 ORF10/CUL2-ZYG11B degradation of IFT46 (PMID: 35674692, PMID: 39309499). None of these alters the interface or motif conclusions.

GO curation implications

All items below are leads requiring curator verification.

Add GO:0120170 intraciliary transport particle B binding (MF). Q0G838 has no molecular function annotation at all. This is the informative term the analysis supports, and it avoids recommending bare protein binding. Evidence code ISS with the Tetrahymena and Chlamydomonas data in the with field is defensible: the interface is structurally resolved in two organisms (PMID: 25349261, PDB 8BD7), mutationally validated in one (PMID: 28302912), and both contact surfaces are conserved in the C. elegans pair at above-background rates. An IPI annotation is not yet earned, because no C. elegans DYF-6-OSM-6 binding experiment exists.

Strengthen GO:0030992 intraciliary transport particle B (CC, part_of). The term is correct but under-evidenced: it rests only on IBA plus a ComplexPortal NAS (28479320), with no experimental or sequence-based line attached. The present analysis supplies the sequence-and-structure basis that was missing. Adding an ISS or ISO line would bring the evidence into line with the confidence.

Flag GO:0031514 motile cilium (CC, is_active_in, IBA, GO_REF:0000033) for removal. This is phylogenetic carry-over from vertebrate and algal orthologs onto a nematode gene. C. elegans has no motile cilia. The gene already carries same-organism GO:1905515 non-motile cilium assembly (IMP, PMID: 16648645), which contradicts it. GO:0097730 non-motile cilium exists and is the correct CC term. This is the clearest over-annotation in the record.

Scrutinise GO:0005815 microtubule organizing center (CC, is_active_in, IBA). It has no supporting evidence in this analysis and arrives by the same IBA route as GO:0031514. It may still be correct through basal-body localization, which PMID: 28302912 demonstrates for Chlamydomonas IFT46, but it deserves the same check.

What the analysis does not license: any annotation asserting loss of cargo-adaptor function, and any dynein complex binding or ODA-transport term. The motif result removes the sequence argument for loss, and no ODA machinery annotation is appropriate in an organism without outer dynein arms.

Limitations and knowledge gaps

No C. elegans binding experiment exists. The 4UZZ interface is Tetrahymena, the mutagenesis is Chlamydomonas, and the C. elegans evidence is a mutant cilium phenotype plus GFP transport (PMID: 16648645), neither of which reports a contact. This matters because the proposed MF annotation would be ISS rather than IPI until such an experiment exists.

AlphaFold-Multimer could not be run in this environment. The hypothesis asked for a predicted DYF-6/OSM-6 complex with interface comparison. No multimer prediction service was reachable programmatically from the execution environment. The substitute was superposition of the two AlphaFold monomer models onto the crystallised epitope plus independent mapping of both contact surfaces. All reported geometry comes from that substitute. No predicted complex interface exists in this work, and nothing in the report should be read as if one does.

The motif coordinates from PMID: 39880089 are not in its abstract. Window 119-133 was located by an independent conservation scan inside the fragment that PMID: 28298440 maps experimentally. If the published ODA16-binding motif lies elsewhere in 1-147, the refutation of clause 3 weakens and would need redoing against the correct coordinates. Reading the full text of that paper is the cheapest way to close this gap.

The ODA-absence premise was checked only superficially. Whether C. elegans truly lacks ODA16/DAW1 and axonemal dynein heavy chains was tested by UniProt name search and PANTHER family assignment, not by a sensitive HMM search of the proteome. Absence of a name-matched hit is weak evidence of absence for a divergent protein.

The OSM-6 side is the weakest conservation signal. At 30.3 % unweighted identity across the 35 contact positions it sits well below human (61.8 %) and Chlamydomonas (53.1 %), and the 49-contact N389 is replaced by glutamate. Contact weighting rescues the signal to 47.2 %, but a charge change at a high-contact position is the kind of substitution that compensatory coevolution would be invoked to explain, and that invocation is untested.

Statistical power. The interface comprises 44 mapped positions. No single Fisher test in this analysis reaches p < 0.05. The permutation test (p = 0.0163) and the shuffled-sequence null for the motif (p < 0.0005) are the two statistics that clear conventional thresholds, and both are single tests on derived quantities rather than independent replications.

Gene-model artefacts in the ortholog panel. Pristionchus pacificus A0A2A6CZI6 (1742 aa) is a fused or poor gene model and was excluded. C. briggsae A8XQI8 aligns poorly over the C-terminal domain (35.3 % identity) and may also be imperfect, which affects its weight in the nematode group. Drosophila melanogaster is an N/C ratio outlier at 1.29 driven by a poor C-terminal alignment (score 87) rather than by an unusually conserved N-terminus. Two candidate IFT52 orthologs (mouse Q9JIH7 at 2126 aa and zebrafish Q6NZZ8) were not genuine orthologs and were excluded from the IFT52-side table.

Tooling constraint. Biopython is not importable in the execution environment, so the pairwise aligner and the PDB parser were written directly in numpy. The alignment uses Smith-Waterman with BLOSUM62 and affine gaps (-11 open, -1 extend), which is standard, but no third-party implementation cross-checked the results.

Discriminating tests

Direct binding assay for the C. elegans pair. A yeast two-hybrid or recombinant pull-down of DYF-6 345-455 against OSM-6 355-472 would convert the ISS recommendation to IPI and settle whether the contact is retained in the nematode pair despite the lower IFT52-side identity. This is the single highest-value experiment for the curation decision.

Point mutation at the predicted anchors. A charge reversal or alanine substitution at DYF-6 W388, or at the I420/V421 pair corresponding to Chlamydomonas L285/L286 (PMID: 28302912), scored for IFT-B incorporation and amphid cilium length, would test whether this surface is required in vivo rather than merely conserved.

Test the retained motif. Pull down a DYF-6 254-268 peptide, or the DYF-6 200-300 fragment, against Chlamydomonas or human ODA16 in vitro. Retention of binding would show the motif is functional and that C. elegans keeps an orphan adaptor surface. Loss would show the sequence similarity is residual and would restore the hypothesis's third clause at the functional level even though it fails at the sequence level.

Domain deletion in vivo. Delete DYF-6 residues 1-250 while keeping the IFT46_B_C domain, then measure cilium length and IFT velocity in amphid neurons. This parallels the Chlamydomonas experiment of PMID: 28701346 and would say whether the N-terminal region retains any function in a sensory-only organism.

Sensitive homology search for ODA machinery. Run HMM profiles for DAW1/ODA16 and axonemal dynein heavy chains against the full C. elegans proteome. This would settle the ODA-absence premise that the "lost because no motile cilia" argument depends on, replacing a name-based search with a sequence-based one.

Read the full text of PMID: 39880089. Extract the published coordinates of the ODA16-binding motif and re-run the targeted search against them. This costs nothing and either confirms or overturns the refutation of clause 3.

Run AlphaFold-Multimer where it is available. Predict DYF-6/OSM-6 and compare the predicted interface residues against the 44 positions mapped here. Agreement would convert the current superposition-based inference into a direct prediction of the complex, which is what the hypothesis originally asked for.
