# Functional annotation report: *C. elegans unc-41* / UNC-41 (UniProt P90761)

## Executive conclusion

The requested identity is verified: *Caenorhabditis elegans unc-41* is the predicted locus **C27H6.1** and encodes the worm ortholog of **Stoned B/stonin 2**, not an enzyme or transporter. Its best-supported primary function is as a **specialized endocytic cargo-sorting adaptor** that recognizes the synaptic-vesicle Ca²⁺ sensor **SNT-1/synaptotagmin** and couples its retrieval to the **AP2-associated synaptic-vesicle endocytic machinery**. UNC-41 acts mainly at neuronal synapses, where it maintains synaptotagmin enrichment and supports regeneration of correctly composed synaptic vesicles after exocytosis. A lower-confidence, non-neuronal role has also been demonstrated genetically in semaphorin-regulated vesicle trafficking in larval male-tail epidermal cells. (mullen2012unc41a pages 1-2, mullen2012unc41stoninfunctionswith pages 1-2, jung2007molecularbasisof pages 7-11, tanaka2020involvementofthe pages 2-3)

The focused literature is limited and is dominated by mechanistic studies from 2007–2012. Targeted searches did not identify a substantive 2023–2024 primary study that materially revised UNC-41’s annotation; the newest focused extension retrieved was published in 2020. Thus, recent absence should be treated as a literature gap rather than substituted with findings about similarly named genes or unrelated stonins.

## 1. Mandatory identity verification

### Gene, organism, and protein family

Primary literature maps *unc-41* to **C27H6.1** in *C. elegans* and describes two stonin-family products. The longer UNC-41A transcript contains 12 exons and is approximately 5.3 kb, whereas UNC-41B begins at exon 3, contains 10 exons, and is approximately 4.5 kb; predicted protein sizes are approximately 188 and 160 kDa, respectively. This agrees with the supplied UniProt identity P90761 and the names *unc-41* and *apt-10*. (mullen2012unc41a pages 2-3, mullen2012unc41 pages 2-3)

Orthology is supported at three levels:

1. **Sequence and architecture:** UNC-41 contains the conserved stonin-homology domain and C-terminal μ-homology domain, corresponding to the supplied SHD/MHD, AP2_Mu_C-like and adaptor-complex domain annotations. Conserved regions are approximately 45% identical to *Drosophila* Stoned B and 31% identical to mouse stonin 2. (mullen2012unc41 pages 2-3, mullen2012unc41stoninfunctionswith pages 1-2)
2. **Cross-species complementation:** transgenic *Drosophila stonedB* rescues *unc-41* mutant phenotypes, demonstrating functional orthology rather than similarity based only on sequence. (mullen2012unc41a pages 1-2, mullen2012unc41stoninfunctionswith pages 1-2)
3. **Conserved cargo-recognition mechanism:** the μ-homology-domain KYE site used by stonins to recognize synaptotagmin is conserved and functionally required in UNC-41B. (jung2007molecularbasisof pages 7-11, jung2007molecularbasisof pages 4-5)

No conflicting literature about a different organism’s similarly named gene was used. The supplied family/domain description is therefore aligned with experimental literature.

## 2. Molecular function and mechanism

### UNC-41 is an adaptor, not a catalyst

UNC-41 has no established enzymatic reaction or transported substrate. Its functional “substrate” in an adaptor sense is principally the transmembrane synaptic-vesicle cargo **SNT-1/synaptotagmin**. UNC-41 selectively recognizes the cytoplasmic C2-domain region of synaptotagmin through its C-terminal μ-homology domain and helps connect that cargo to membrane-endocytic machinery. (jung2007molecularbasisof pages 7-11, jung2007molecularbasisof pages 2-4)

Biochemical evidence is unusually direct. GST-tagged rat or worm synaptotagmin C2 domains immobilized on beads pulled down HA-tagged UNC-41B from cell extracts. Mutation or deletion of UNC-41B’s conserved **KYE cargo-recognition site** strongly impaired or abolished binding. In vivo, wild-type GFP–UNC-41B accumulated in synaptic puncta and rescued the *unc-41* locomotor defect, whereas the synaptotagmin-binding-defective KYE mutant failed both to accumulate normally at synapses and to rescue, despite comparable or increased expression. These experiments link physical cargo binding to localization and organismal function. [Jung et al., published December 2007, DOI/URL: https://doi.org/10.1083/jcb.200708107] (jung2007molecularbasisof pages 7-11)

Structural and mutational analysis of mammalian stonin 2 further defines the conserved interface: a β strand containing K783–Y784–E785 in the μ-homology domain recognizes basic patches in synaptotagmin’s C2 domains. The strongest binding is associated with C2A/C2AB, while AP2 predominantly contacts C2B. These mammalian details provide a structural model for UNC-41, but only the conserved UNC-41 KYE mutagenesis/pulldown and rescue evidence should be considered direct worm evidence. (jung2007molecularbasisof pages 1-2, jung2007molecularbasisof pages 2-4, jung2007molecularbasisof pages 4-5)

### Coupling to AP2 and endocytosis

UNC-41 contains candidate AP2-ear-binding motifs—**DPF, FxDxF and WxxF**—as well as proline-rich regions that may recruit SH3-domain proteins and a nematode-specific C-terminal PDZ-binding motif. These features fit an adaptor that simultaneously recognizes cargo and engages coat/accessory proteins, although not every predicted motif has been functionally tested in worms. (mullen2012unc41a pages 2-3, mullen2012unc41stoninfunctionswith pages 5-7)

Genetic epistasis places UNC-41 in the same broad pathway as **APM-2/μ2 adaptin**, the medium subunit of AP2. The *unc-41; apm-2* double mutant showed essentially the same synaptic-vesicle deficit as *unc-41* alone rather than a further reduction. The favored interpretation is that UNC-41 and AP2 act at distinct but coupled steps in one endocytic pathway—UNC-41 providing synaptotagmin-selective cargo recognition and AP2 helping recruit membrane/coat machinery. Nonadditivity supports pathway membership but, by itself, does not prove a direct UNC-41–AP2 physical interaction in vivo. (mullen2012unc41 pages 5-7, mullen2012unc41a pages 5-7, mullen2012unc41stoninfunctionswith pages 5-7)

## 3. Biological process and pathway placement

The core pathway can be summarized as:

**Synaptic-vesicle exocytosis → vesicle proteins exposed in presynaptic plasma membrane → UNC-41 μ-homology domain recognizes SNT-1/synaptotagmin → UNC-41/AP2-associated machinery sorts cargo and retrieves membrane → synaptic-vesicle reformation and replenishment.**

Loss of UNC-41 disrupts SNT-1 much more strongly than other examined vesicle proteins. SNT-1::GFP puncta become fewer and smaller, with diffuse signal extending through axons and commissures; synaptobrevin and synaptogyrin display only minor abnormalities. This cargo selectivity argues that UNC-41 is not merely a general structural coat component. Conversely, UNC-41 remains synaptically localized in *snt-1* null mutants, showing that SNT-1 depends on UNC-41 for correct localization more than UNC-41 depends on SNT-1. (mullen2012unc41 pages 5-7, mullen2012unc41a pages 5-7, mullen2012unc41a pages 2-3)

Nevertheless, UNC-41 is probably not exclusively a synaptotagmin adaptor. The *snt-1 unc-41* double-mutant phenotype is more severe than that of *snt-1* alone, and overexpression of SNT-1 does not rescue *unc-41*. These observations suggest additional cargo, membrane-recruitment, or organizational functions that remain incompletely identified. (mullen2012unc41a pages 1-2, mullen2012unc41a pages 5-7)

## 4. Cellular and tissue localization

### Neurons and presynaptic regions

Reporter experiments found UNC-41A in most or all neurons and UNC-41B in a neuronal subset that includes GABAergic motor neurons. Both isoforms localize in synaptic puncta. UNC-41 signal overlaps SNT-1/synaptotagmin but does not substantially overlap the active-zone marker UNC-10/RIM, favoring a location in the perisynaptic vesicle-recycling domain rather than the release-site cytomatrix itself. (mullen2012unc41a pages 2-3, mullen2012unc41 pages 5-7)

Thus, the principal cellular site of function is the **cytoplasmic face of presynaptic membranes/endocytic intermediates at nerve terminals**. UNC-41 is a soluble peripheral adaptor rather than an extracellular or integral membrane protein.

### Epidermal cells

A 2020 study extended the pathway to larval male-tail ray epidermal cells. Loss or RNAi of *unc-41* suppressed cell-positioning and ray-1 abnormalities in *plx-1* and semaphorin mutants, and tail/ray-lineage expression of *unc-41* cDNA reversed the suppression, supporting a low-level, cell-autonomous epidermal function. However, direct endogenous UNC-41 protein localization in these cells was not convincingly documented; reporter observations were described as low-level or “data not shown.” [Tanaka et al., published April 2020, DOI/URL: https://doi.org/10.1111/gtc.12765] (tanaka2020involvementofthe pages 2-3)

Accordingly, epidermal function is supported more strongly than epidermal subcellular localization.

## 5. Quantitative experimental evidence

Electron microscopy provides the clearest quantitative phenotype. *unc-41* mutants contained approximately **50% fewer synaptic vesicles** in both cholinergic and GABAergic motor-neuron terminals. Docked vesicles fell proportionally, arguing against a selective docking defect. Mean vesicle diameter increased modestly from **28.5 nm to approximately 32 nm**, consistent with altered membrane retrieval or cargo composition. [Mullen et al., published 10 July 2012, DOI/URL: https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0040095] (mullen2012unc41 pages 5-7, mullen2012unc41a pages 5-7)

Behaviorally, null mutants exhibit severe uncoordinated movement and pharyngeal-pumping activity of roughly **20% of wild type**, along with slow growth, small adult size, cholinesterase-inhibitor resistance and GABA-associated defecation defects. These pleiotropic outputs are consistent with impaired neurotransmission but are less mechanistically specific than the cargo-localization and ultrastructural evidence. (mullen2012unc41a pages 2-3, mullen2012unc41 pages 2-3)

An all-optical assay stimulated cholinergic motor neurons for 120 seconds while measuring muscle RCaMP Ca²⁺ responses. *unc-41* perturbation produced the characteristic recycling-gene pattern—a faster initial rise followed by an earlier decline—interpreted as synaptic fatigue when recycled vesicles become limiting. The study used repeated measurements on approximately **1,000 worms per gene**, although the *unc-41(e1199)* effect was weak and no gene-specific numerical effect size was reported. [Wabnig et al., published 27 August 2015, DOI/URL: https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0135584] (wabnig2015highthroughputallopticalanalysis pages 12-15, wabnig2015highthroughputallopticalanalysis pages 1-2)

## 6. Semaphorin-regulated epidermal trafficking

In ray epidermal cells, semaphorins SMP-1/SMP-2 signal through PLX-1 and alter SNT-1-positive vesicle behavior. In *plx-1* mutants, SNT-1 vesicles show reduced motility and reduced colocalization with RAB-7-marked late endosomes. *unc-41* RNAi restored these measures toward wild-type levels. Imaging also observed SNT-1-positive vesicles undergoing fusion/fission with RAB-7- and LMP-1-positive compartments. (tanaka2020involvementofthe pages 7-8, tanaka2020involvementofthe pages 6-7)

The authors proposed that PLX-1 regulates an SNT-1/UNC-41 sorting system that can direct cargo away from RAB-5/RAB-11 recycling routes and toward RAB-7/LMP-1 late-endosome/lysosome routes, thereby influencing epidermal morphogenesis. This is an informative extension beyond synapses, but the precise UNC-41-containing compartment, cargo spectrum, and molecular connection to PLX-1 remain inferential. (tanaka2020involvementofthe pages 7-8, tanaka2020involvementofthe pages 1-2)

## 7. Current applications and model-system relevance

UNC-41 is principally used as:

- a **genetic model of stonin-dependent synaptic-vesicle cargo sorting**;
- a comparator for AP2, synaptotagmin, synaptojanin, endophilin and other endocytic mutants;
- a positive-control class for assays that distinguish defective vesicle recycling from exocytic or postsynaptic defects;
- a cross-species model for conserved stonin–synaptotagmin recognition; and
- a tool for testing whether vesicle-sorting machinery is redeployed outside neurons, as in semaphorin-regulated epidermal morphogenesis. (jung2007molecularbasisof pages 7-11, tanaka2020involvementofthe pages 2-3, wabnig2015highthroughputallopticalanalysis pages 1-2)

No therapeutic, diagnostic or industrial implementation specific to *unc-41* was identified. Its real-world value is foundational: it helps define conserved principles of presynaptic cargo retrieval that are relevant to neuronal maintenance and synaptic transmission.

## 9. Current expert interpretation and unresolved questions

The most defensible annotation is: **UNC-41 is a synaptically enriched stonin-family endocytic sorting adaptor whose μ-homology domain directly recognizes SNT-1/synaptotagmin and promotes its AP2-linked retrieval into newly formed synaptic vesicles.** This interpretation integrates direct binding, conserved-site mutagenesis, in-vivo rescue, selective cargo mislocalization, double-mutant epistasis and electron microscopy. (mullen2012unc41stoninfunctionswith pages 1-2, jung2007molecularbasisof pages 7-11, mullen2012unc41 pages 5-7)

Important uncertainties remain:

1. **Additional cargo:** stronger *snt-1 unc-41* phenotypes imply synaptotagmin-independent functions, but additional direct cargo has not been established. (mullen2012unc41a pages 1-2)
2. **Exact endocytic mode:** UNC-41 clearly supports vesicle recycling, but the retrieved studies do not prove that every UNC-41-dependent event is canonical clathrin-mediated budding rather than cargo sorting into intermediates generated by multiple endocytic modes.
3. **Isoform specialization:** UNC-41A and UNC-41B differ in expression breadth, yet UNC-41B can rescue when driven appropriately; unique endogenous functions of the long N terminus remain poorly resolved. (mullen2012unc41a pages 2-3, mullen2012unc41stoninfunctionswith pages 2-3)
4. **Epidermal localization:** genetic and trafficking evidence is credible, but direct endogenous protein localization and the mechanistic link to PLX-1 need stronger validation. (tanaka2020involvementofthe pages 2-3, tanaka2020involvementofthe pages 7-8)
5. **Recent research:** no retrieved 2023–2024 gene-specific study substantially advanced the annotation. The field’s primary mechanistic foundation remains the 2007 and 2012 work, supplemented by functional screening in 2015 and the epidermal extension in 2020.

## Bottom-line functional annotation

**Recommended concise annotation:** *unc-41* encodes the *C. elegans* stonin ortholog, a presynaptic endocytic cargo adaptor. Through its C-terminal μ-homology domain, UNC-41 binds SNT-1/synaptotagmin and works with AP2-associated machinery to sort synaptotagmin and membrane during synaptic-vesicle recycling. It localizes primarily to neuronal synapses; loss causes diffuse synaptotagmin, approximately 50% depletion of cholinergic and GABAergic synaptic vesicles, altered vesicle size and activity-dependent synaptic fatigue. UNC-41 additionally has a genetically supported, low-level role in semaphorin/PLX-1-regulated SNT-1 vesicle trafficking in larval epidermal cells, although that non-neuronal mechanism is less fully resolved. (mullen2012unc41a pages 5-7, jung2007molecularbasisof pages 7-11, tanaka2020involvementofthe pages 2-3, wabnig2015highthroughputallopticalanalysis pages 1-2)
