Gene Description

CED-1 is the C. elegans engulfment receptor for apoptotic cell corpses, a single-pass transmembrane protein of the CED-1/Draper/MEGF10 family with an EMI domain, extracellular EGF-like repeats and a cytoplasmic tail carrying NPXY and YXXL motifs. Expressed on engulfing cells, it clusters around neighbouring corpses and recognizes them through the secreted bridging protein TTR-52, which binds exposed phosphatidylserine. Its NPXY motif recruits the PTB-domain adaptor CED-6/GULP and, with the ABC transporter CED-7 and dynamin DYN-1, it drives pseudopod extension, actin reorganization and internalization, then initiates phagosome maturation through PI(3)P and RAB-7. After engulfment it is recycled to the plasma membrane by retromer. Engulfing cells also use it to promote the death of cells that receive weak death signals, and it contributes to defense against bacterial pathogens.
