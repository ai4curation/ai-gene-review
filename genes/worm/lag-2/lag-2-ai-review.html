Gene Description

LAG-2 is a transmembrane Delta/Serrate/LAG-2 (DSL) family ligand for the Caenorhabditis elegans Notch receptors LIN-12 and GLP-1. It is a single-pass type I membrane protein with an N-terminal region and DSL domain that engage the receptor EGF repeats; its own EGF-like repeats are dispensable for activity. LAG-2 lacks the DOS motif, which can be supplied in trans by secreted DOS-motif co-ligands such as OSM-11. LAG-2 is expressed by the distal tip cell and its processes. There it forms the germline stem-cell niche signal that activates GLP-1 to maintain germline proliferation. It is also the lateral signal in LIN-12-mediated decisions, including the anchor cell/ventral uterine decision, vulval precursor cell patterning, and ventral M-lineage fates. It acts partly redundantly with APX-1 and DSL-1, which can substitute for it. Secreted, truncated extracellular forms of LAG-2 retain signaling activity.

Comprehensive Research Report: lag-2 Gene in Caenorhabditis elegans

Gene Identity and Overview

The lag-2 gene (UniProt: P45442) in Caenorhabditis elegans encodes LAG-2 (also known as LET-461), a transmembrane ligand belonging to the evolutionarily conserved Delta/Serrate/LAG-2 (DSL) family of Notch signaling proteins (luo2020positiveautoregulationof pages 1-4, holt2016characterisationofthe pages 124-128). The gene name derives from the characteristic "Lag" (lineage abnormal) phenotype observed in loss-of-function mutants, which fail to form the rectum and anus, resulting in larval arrest (holt2016characterisationofthe pages 124-128). LAG-2 is a critical component of the Notch signaling pathway, functioning as an activating ligand for the LIN-12 and GLP-1 Notch receptors during multiple developmental processes throughout the C. elegans life cycle.

The MNNL/C2 domain at the N-terminus (approximately 105 amino acids) adopts a predicted C2-like fold and is essential for LAG-2 function, as deletion of this region abolishes rescue activity in genetic complementation experiments (holt2016characterisationofthe pages 50-55). This domain contributes to Notch receptor binding and likely helps orient the ligand at the membrane through phospholipid interactions (feng2024thestructuraland pages 44-49, feng2024thestructuraland pages 64-68). The DSL domain is the defining feature of canonical Notch ligands and contains six cysteine residues arranged in three disulfide bonds (holt2016characterisationofthe pages 45-50). Functional studies demonstrate that the DSL domain is absolutely required for LAG-2 activity: deletion mutants lacking this domain fail to rescue Notch-related phenotypes (holt2016characterisationofthe pages 45-50, holt2016characterisationofthe pages 180-186).

Molecular Mechanism of Notch Activation

LAG-2 functions as an activating ligand for Notch receptors through direct protein-protein interaction. When LAG-2 on a signal-sending cell binds to LIN-12 or GLP-1 on an adjacent cell, it triggers a series of proteolytic cleavages that release the Notch intracellular domain (NICD) (luo2020positiveautoregulationof pages 1-4, luo2020positiveautoregulationof pages 31-34). The released NICD translocates to the nucleus, where it associates with the DNA-binding protein LAG-1/CSL and co-activators to activate transcription of Notch target genes (luo2020positiveautoregulationof pages 1-4, chen2020glp1notch—lag1csl pages 2-4).

A distinctive feature of C. elegans LAG-2 compared to Drosophila and mammalian Notch ligands is its lower mechanical force requirement for receptor activation. Recent work by Langridge et al. (2021) demonstrated that LIN-12 and GLP-1 are tuned to lower force thresholds, allowing LAG-2 to activate these receptors without the strong Epsin-dependent endocytic pulling force required in other systems (langridge2021thec.elegans pages 8-12, langridge2021thec.elegans pages 5-8, langridge2021thec.elegans pages 1-5). Consequently, LAG-2 can signal effectively even when its intracellular domain is deleted or when ubiquitination sites are mutated, distinguishing it from canonical Drosophila Delta or mammalian Notch ligands (langridge2021thec.elegans pages 1-5, langridge2021thec.elegans pages 16-20). However, membrane tethering remains important: a secreted LAG-2 ectodomain alone does not rescue lag-2 null mutants, while GPI-anchored versions restore partial activity (langridge2021thec.elegans pages 5-8).

Binding Partners and Regulatory Interactions

LAG-2 interacts with multiple binding partners that modulate its activity:

  1. GLP-1 and LIN-12 Notch receptors: The primary substrates for LAG-2 signaling. LAG-2 binds the extracellular domain of these receptors to initiate signal transduction (post2025notchactivityis pages 2-3, post2025notchactivityis pages 5-7, luo2020positiveautoregulationof pages 1-4).

  2. LAT-1 (Latrophilin): A recent breakthrough discovery by Post et al. (2025) identified the adhesion GPCR LAT-1 as a direct binding partner of LAG-2 (post2025notchactivityis pages 5-7, post2025notchactivityis pages 8-9). This interaction occurs in cis (on the same cell) through LAT-1's RBL and GAIN domains and enhances Notch receptor activation through a G-protein-independent mechanism (post2025notchactivityis pages 8-9, post2025notchactivityis pages 7-8). LAT-1 appears to stabilize LAG-2 at the membrane or enhance its signaling capacity, thereby fine-tuning the strength of Notch pathway activation in the germline stem cell niche (post2025notchactivityis pages 8-9, post2025notchactivityis pages 2-3).

Subcellular Localization

LAG-2 is a membrane-bound protein localized to the plasma membrane of signal-sending cells, where it functions through direct cell-to-cell contact (juxtacrine signaling) (post2025notchactivityis pages 8-9, post2025notchactivityis pages 2-3, post2025notchactivityis pages 3-5). The protein is not secreted as a soluble ligand but remains anchored to the cell surface via its transmembrane domain (post2025notchactivityis pages 2-3). This membrane localization is functionally critical, as evidenced by the requirement for membrane association to achieve effective receptor activation (langridge2021thec.elegans pages 5-8).

LAG-2 is expressed in tissue- and context-specific patterns:

  • Distal tip cell (DTC): LAG-2 is prominently expressed on the surface of the DTC, the somatic niche cell that maintains the germline stem cell population (post2025notchactivityis pages 8-9, post2025notchactivityis pages 2-3, chen2020glp1notch—lag1csl pages 2-4, post2025notchactivityis pages 1-2).

  • Anchor cell precursors: During gonadal development, LAG-2 is initially expressed in equivalent AC/VU precursor cells and becomes enriched in the presumptive anchor cell through reciprocal regulation with LIN-12 (holt2016characterisationofthe pages 128-134, luo2020positiveautoregulationof pages 7-10).

  • Vulval precursor cells: LAG-2 is expressed in the primary-fated vulval precursor P6.p, from where it signals to neighboring P5.p and P7.p cells (luo2020positiveautoregulationof pages 31-34).

Germline Stem Cell Maintenance

The most extensively characterized role of LAG-2 is in maintaining the germline stem cell population. The distal tip cell (DTC) presents LAG-2 to adjacent germ cells, activating the GLP-1 Notch receptor (chen2020glp1notch—lag1csl pages 2-4, post2025notchactivityis pages 1-2). A landmark study by Chen et al. (2020) used integrated genomic approaches (LAG-1/CSL ChIP-seq, GLP-1-dependent RNA-seq, and temporal LAG-1 depletion) to identify lst-1 and sygl-1 as the only strongly supported direct transcriptional targets of GLP-1/Notch signaling in the germline (chen2020glp1notch—lag1csl pages 12-14, chen2020glp1notch—lag1csl pages 16-17, chen2020glp1notch—lag1csl pages 1-2, chen2020glp1notch—lag1csl pages 11-12). These two genes redundantly promote the stem cell fate by repressing meiotic entry pathways, thereby maintaining a proliferative progenitor pool (chen2020glp1notch—lag1csl pages 2-4).

Recent work by Koitz et al. (2025) revealed that LAG-2 protein levels are dynamically regulated by nutritional status during dauer formation (koitz2025predauerstarvationdecouples pages 8-11, koitz2025predauerstarvationdecouples pages 11-13). During starvation-induced dauer entry, LAG-2 protein decreases rapidly in the DTC, contributing to germline quiescence. Remarkably, upon dauer exit, LAG-2 protein levels recover within approximately two hours through post-transcriptional mechanisms, preceding the restoration of germline proliferation (koitz2025predauerstarvationdecouples pages 11-13). This finding links environmental sensing to stem cell niche signaling and demonstrates sophisticated regulation of LAG-2 beyond simple transcriptional control.

Anchor Cell and Ventral Uterine Cell Specification

During somatic gonadal development, LAG-2 participates in a lateral inhibition process that specifies one anchor cell (AC) and one ventral uterine (VU) cell from two initially equivalent precursors (holt2016characterisationofthe pages 128-134, luo2020positiveautoregulationof pages 7-10). Initially, both Z1.ppp and Z4.aaa cells express both lag-2 and lin-12. Through reciprocal signaling, one cell increases LAG-2 expression while reducing LIN-12, becoming the signal-sending anchor cell, while the neighboring cell maintains high LIN-12 activity and adopts the VU fate (holt2016characterisationofthe pages 128-134). Loss or reduction of LAG-2 function disrupts this balance, resulting in the specification of two anchor cells instead of the normal AC/VU pair (holt2016characterisationofthe pages 128-134).

Luo et al. (2020) discovered that this process involves positive autoregulation of lag-1: LIN-12 activation by LAG-2 induces transcriptional upregulation of lag-1 through direct LAG-1/CSL binding sites, creating a feedback mechanism that stabilizes cell fate decisions (luo2020positiveautoregulationof pages 1-4, luo2020positiveautoregulationof pages 7-10).

Vulval Development

LAG-2 plays a key role in vulval precursor cell (VPC) patterning through lateral inhibition. Following induction of P6.p by EGF/LET-23/Ras-MAPK signaling to adopt the primary (1°) vulval fate, P6.p expresses DSL ligands including LAG-2 (luo2020positiveautoregulationof pages 31-34). LAG-2 then activates LIN-12 in the neighboring P5.p and P7.p cells, promoting their adoption of secondary (2°) vulval fates while preventing them from inappropriately adopting primary fates (luo2020positiveautoregulationof pages 31-34). This establishes the characteristic 2°–1°–2° pattern essential for proper vulval morphogenesis.

Embryonic and Larval Development

Loss-of-function lag-2 mutations produce severe embryonic and early larval defects. The characteristic "Lag" phenotype includes failure to form the rectum and anus, resulting in developmental arrest during the L1 larval stage (holt2016characterisationofthe pages 124-128). Additional defects include abnormal nose morphology and absence of the excretory cell, demonstrating LAG-2's essential role in multiple tissue specifications during embryogenesis (holt2016characterisationofthe pages 124-128). Temperature-sensitive alleles such as lag-2(q420) allow conditional analysis and reveal additional post-embryonic developmental requirements when animals are shifted to restrictive temperatures after L1 (holt2016characterisationofthe pages 124-128).

Several important advances have enhanced our understanding of LAG-2 function in recent years:

Novel regulatory mechanisms: The discovery that LAT-1 (latrophilin) directly binds LAG-2 in cis and modulates Notch signaling represents a paradigm shift in understanding Notch pathway regulation (post2025notchactivityis pages 5-7, post2025notchactivityis pages 8-9). This G-protein-independent mechanism fine-tunes signaling strength and demonstrates that Notch ligand activity can be modulated by cell-surface interactions beyond the canonical receptor.

Direct target identification: The Chen et al. (2020) study definitively established lst-1 and sygl-1 as the sole primary protein-coding transcriptional targets of GLP-1/Notch signaling in the germline (chen2020glp1notch—lag1csl pages 12-14, chen2020glp1notch—lag1csl pages 11-12), providing a complete molecular pathway from LAG-2 ligand presentation through receptor activation to transcriptional output.

Mechanical activation insights: The work by Langridge et al. (2021) revealed fundamental differences in mechanotransduction between C. elegans and other organisms, showing that LAG-2-mediated activation requires substantially less mechanical force than Drosophila or mammalian systems (langridge2021thec.elegans pages 8-12, langridge2021thec.elegans pages 1-5, langridge2021thec.elegans pages 22-28). This finding explains why LAG-2 can function without its intracellular domain and without Epsin-dependent endocytosis.

Post-transcriptional regulation: The Koitz et al. (2025) study demonstrated rapid, post-transcriptional regulation of LAG-2 protein levels in response to nutritional status (koitz2025predauerstarvationdecouples pages 8-11, koitz2025predauerstarvationdecouples pages 11-13), revealing an additional layer of control that couples environmental conditions to stem cell niche signaling.

Tissue-specific trafficking regulation: Preliminary evidence from Kato et al. (2026) suggests that the AGEF-1/ARF-1/AP-1 membrane trafficking pathway differentially regulates LAG-2 and LIN-12/Notch signaling in a tissue-specific manner (kato2026theagef1arf1gtpaseap1 pages 9-13), though these findings await peer review.

Comparison with Related Ligands

C. elegans possesses a second DSL ligand, APX-1, which shares functional overlap with LAG-2 but is not fully redundant (holt2016characterisationofthe pages 153-157, holt2016characterisationofthe pages 124-128). Both ligands can activate LIN-12 and GLP-1 receptors, and APX-1 can partially rescue lag-2 mutant phenotypes (holt2016characterisationofthe pages 153-157). However, they differ in developmental contexts and expression patterns: APX-1 was initially identified through its maternal role in early embryonic ABp blastomere specification, whereas LAG-2 functions prominently in later developmental processes (holt2016characterisationofthe pages 124-128). The DSL domains of the two ligands share some residues but lack a strictly conserved motif, suggesting possible differences in receptor-binding affinities (holt2016characterisationofthe pages 153-157, holt2016characterisationofthe pages 98-102). APX-1 can also bind LAT-1, suggesting that latrophilin-mediated modulation may be a general mechanism for C. elegans Notch ligands (post2025notchactivityis pages 7-8).

Expert Perspectives and Current Understanding

LAG-2 exemplifies the versatility of Notch signaling: the same ligand-receptor pair is deployed in multiple cellular contexts to generate distinct developmental outcomes through combinatorial regulation, tissue-specific cofactors, and varying signaling thresholds. The discovery of LAT-1 as a LAG-2 modulator represents an emerging theme in the field—that canonical signaling pathways are fine-tuned by auxiliary cell-surface proteins that adjust signal strength without altering core pathway logic (post2025notchactivityis pages 8-9).

The finding that C. elegans Notch receptors function at lower force thresholds has important implications for understanding Notch evolution and mechanism. As reviewed in multiple recent articles, this demonstrates that the mechanical requirements for Notch activation are not universal but rather have been tuned during evolution to match organismal and developmental contexts (langridge2021thec.elegans pages 8-12, langridge2021thec.elegans pages 22-28). This evolutionary plasticity in force requirements while maintaining conserved core signaling logic highlights the adaptability of the Notch pathway.

Conclusions

LAG-2 is a membrane-bound DSL-family Notch ligand essential for multiple developmental processes in C. elegans. Its primary molecular function is to bind and activate LIN-12 and GLP-1 Notch receptors on neighboring cells, initiating proteolytic release of the Notch intracellular domain and subsequent transcriptional activation of context-specific target genes. The protein localizes to the plasma membrane of signal-sending cells and mediates juxtacrine signaling in the germline stem cell niche, during AC/VU specification, in vulval patterning, and throughout embryonic development.

Recent research (2020-2026) has revealed sophisticated regulatory mechanisms controlling LAG-2 activity, including direct modulation by the LAT-1 aGPCR, rapid post-transcriptional regulation in response to nutritional status, and mechanistic differences in force-dependent activation compared to other organisms. The identification of lst-1 and sygl-1 as the sole direct germline targets of GLP-1/Notch signaling provides a complete molecular pathway in this key developmental context. These advances establish LAG-2 as a paradigm for understanding how evolutionarily conserved signaling ligands achieve developmental specificity through multiple layers of regulation while maintaining core mechanistic principles.

lag-2 (C. elegans) curation notes

UniProt P45442 "Protein lag-2". PANTHER (verbatim from UniProt): PTHR22669 (DELTA/SERRATE/LAG-2 DOMAIN
PROTEIN), PTHR22669:SF8 (PROTEIN LAG-2). IBA rows: PANTHER:PTN000505678 for Notch binding (GO:0005112),
cell projection membrane (GO:0031253) and cell fate specification (GO:0001708). Donors are lag-2 itself
(WB:WBGene00002246) plus WB:WBGene00001103 for Notch binding.

Ligand function

  • Delta-like membrane protein expressed in the DTC PMID:7607081.
  • Required for both LIN-12 and GLP-1 signaling PMID:1769331.
  • ECD binds LIN-12 EGF repeats 1-6 (Y2H) PMID:18700817.
  • The N-terminal region and DSL domain are required; the EGF repeats are dispensable; the intracellular
    region down-regulates activity PMID:9307971.
  • APX-1 substitutes for LAG-2; truncated secreted ECDs activate LIN-12/GLP-1 ectopically PMID:8575327.

Variant biology

  • Lacks a DOS motif. The DOS motif is proposed to be supplied in trans by OSM-11-family co-ligands
    PMID:18700817
  • Germline stem-cell niche: LAG-2 on DTC processes PMID:16672375.
  • AC/VU decision: feedback restricts lag-2 transcription to the presumptive AC (HLH-2 directly activates
    lag-2) PMID:14701877.

Flags

  • GO:0032809 neuronal cell body membrane (IDA, PMID:16672375 and PMID:8575327): the cells assayed (DTC and
    gonadal cells) are not neurons, so I MODIFY this to plasma membrane.
