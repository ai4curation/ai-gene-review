TIN-44 (O02161, C. elegans) and the Tim44 features for mtHsp70 tethering and inner-membrane binding

Hypothesis slug: kgap-tin44-tim44-features Source file: genes/worm/tin-44/tin-44-ai-review.yaml Focus type: free_text Target: tin-44, UniProt O02161, NCBITaxon:6239

Summary

Verdict: partially supported, and the split runs along the domain boundary.

The seed hypothesis asks whether TIN-44 retains the structural features that let Tim44 bind the mitochondrial inner membrane and tether mtHsp70 to the TIM23 translocase, and proposes to answer this by AlphaFold modelling, crystal-structure comparison, residue mapping, presequence prediction and conservation scoring. Those analyses were run against public data. They answer the membrane and fold half of the question and they cannot answer the tethering half.

The C-terminal half is supported. InterPro member databases assign O02161 to the Tim44 family over residues 1-422 (PIRSF037871, PANTHER PTHR10721, NCBIfam TIGR00984, Pfam PF04280, SSF54427), so TIN-44 is a full-length orthologue and not a distant Tim44-like paralogue. The AlphaFold C-terminal domain superposes on the human TIMM44 crystal structure 2CW9 chain A at 1.13 Å over 157 of 179 Cα pairs and on the yeast 2FXT chain A at 1.46 Å over 133 of 186, cross-checked independently at 1.224 Å by phenix.superpose_pdbs. The hydrophobic cavity is present as a feature of that fold. The A1/A2 helix that Marom et al. 2009 identified as the acidic-lipid recognition site is the single most amphipathic 18-residue window in the TIN-44 C-terminal domain (Eisenberg µH 0.562, 99th percentile of its own domain), matching human (0.582) and exceeding yeast (0.495).

The N-terminal half is unresolved, and the stated method cannot resolve it. The segment of Tim44 that contacts mtHsp70 and the Tim23 matrix loop is predicted disordered in all three orthologues: 71.5% of TIN-44 residues 41-240 fall below pLDDT 70, with 89.3% in human and 58.5% in yeast. The residue-mapping route is also blocked. Identity at the 41 Tim44 residues that contact Tim17 or Tim23 in the yeast cryo-EM structure 8E1M is 22% in TIN-44 against a 27.4% background (Fisher exact OR 0.72, p = 0.465), and the control that matters gives 32% against 28.6% for human TIMM44 (OR 1.18, p = 0.716). Human TIMM44 is an established TIM23 component, so an interface this divergent is what yeast-to-animal distance looks like and carries no information about TIN-44 either way.

Two constraints govern the whole report. Everything computed here is prediction and geometry, not assay. And there is no direct experimental evidence of any kind for TIN-44 in C. elegans: PubMed searches returned no TIN-44 localization, no TIN-44/HSP-6 interaction, no membrane-association experiment and no tin-44 mutant or RNAi phenotype. Annotation therefore rests on orthology plus structure prediction, which caps the evidence code at ISS/ISO.

Key findings

F1. TIN-44 is a full-length Tim44 orthologue, closer to human than to yeast

The InterPro API returns a signature set for O02161 that covers the protein end to end: PIRSF037871 "Import inner membrane translocase, TIM44 subunit" at residues 1-422, PANTHER PTHR10721 at 25-421, the Tim44-like family IPR039544 at 25-421, NCBIfam TIGR00984 at 44-415, Pfam PF04280 Tim44-like domain at 269-417, SMART SM00978 at 268-418, SSF54427 NTF2-like superfamily at 232-418 and CATH-Gene3D G3DSA:3.10.450.240 at 236-422. The same set covers yeast Q01852 and human O43615. One difference is worth recording: yeast carries an additional Tim44 N-terminal domain model (PF43739) at 135-189 that is called on neither O02161 nor O43615, which is consistent with the N-terminal region diverging between fungi and animals.

Global Needleman-Wunsch alignment (BLOSUM62, affine gaps -11/-1, implemented from scratch and validated against an independent reference implementation to an identical score of 891) gives TIN-44 42.5% identity and 66.7% similarity to human TIMM44 over 424 aligned positions, against 27.4% and 49.9% to yeast Tim44 over 409. Resolved by domain, the C-terminal domain is 47.3% identical to human and 29.2% to yeast; the N-terminal region is 38.8% and 25.8%. The practical consequence is that human TIMM44 is the better structural template for TIN-44, and that any inference transferred from yeast Tim44 should be treated as a longer evolutionary jump.

Median C-terminal domain identity across 26 nematode genera (one PF04280 protein per genus, longest isoform) is 65.2%, so TIN-44 is not a fast-evolving outlier within the phylum.

F2. The C-terminal NTF2-like fold is retained at crystallographic accuracy

The AlphaFold DB model AF-O02161-F1 (v6) was superposed on both crystal structures by alignment-guided Kabsch fitting with iterative outlier rejection at a 4 Å cutoff. Against the human TIMM44 C-terminal domain 2CW9 chain A, 179 Cα pairs give a core RMSD of 1.13 Å over 157 pairs (88% retained), median deviation 0.94 Å, and 83% of pairs within 2 Å. Against the yeast 2FXT chain A, 186 pairs give 1.46 Å over 133 pairs (72%), median deviation 1.53 Å. An independent run of phenix.superpose_pdbs on trimmed models (worm 241-425 against 2CW9 266-452) returned 1.224 Å, confirming the result through a different algorithm and a different alignment.

One caveat sits exactly where it is least convenient. The only region of the C-terminal domain that both deviates beyond 4 Å from 2CW9 and is modelled at low confidence is TIN-44 245-264, the α1 segment (human 270-289). That is the membrane-binding element. The fold conclusion is therefore stronger than the membrane-helix-geometry conclusion, even though the amphipathicity calculation in F4 supports the latter on sequence grounds.

An earlier one-shot Kabsch fit with a single outlier trim produced an implausible 10.63 Å all-pair RMSD; this was an artefact of a few terminal outliers dominating the fit and was corrected by the iterative procedure. The corrected values agree with the independent Phenix number, so the 1.1-1.5 Å range is the result that stands.

F3. The cavity fold is retained; the cavity chemistry is not

Human TIMM44 crystallised with two pentaethylene glycol (1PE) molecules bound (2CW9, 1.9 Å). Thirteen residues lie within 4.5 Å of the 32 ligand atoms: M331, Q350, L351, P354, L397, M399, L417, M419, Y421, I443, S444, A445, S446. Mapped onto TIN-44 through the human-worm alignment, only 2 of 13 are identical (Q350/Q322, L351/L323), 4 are conservative substitutions (L397/M370, M419/I392, Y421/H394, I443/V416) and 7 differ outright. Three cavity methionines are replaced (M331 to Phe303, M399 to Asn372, M419 to Ile392) and the S444-A445-S446 patch becomes His417-Met418-Gln419.

Transforming the 1PE atoms into the TIN-44 model frame after superposition gives a median clearance to the nearest TIN-44 heavy atom of 3.13 Å (range 0.94-6.31), with 53% of ligand atoms at 3.0 Å or more and 31% under 2.5 Å. The cavity is open but partly narrowed relative to the human structure.

Human Pro308, mutated to Gln in familial oncocytic thyroid carcinoma and sitting in the α1-α2 loop that Handa et al. described as highly conserved (PMID: 18084070), aligns to Ala280 in TIN-44 with no gap in the surrounding 12 residues (human LRVDPAFDKDRF against worm HKIDANFDKQEW). Across the 26 nematode genera, the Pro308-equivalent position is identical in only 23% of orthologues, while the flanking D283 and K284 are 92% and 81% conserved. Cavity-equivalent positions average 49.7% per-site nematode identity against 53.8% for the rest of the C-terminal domain.

The reading for curation: a cavity-containing NTF2-like fold is predicted for TIN-44, and the specific lipid-contact chemistry characterised in human TIMM44 is substantially substituted. Do not transfer human lipid-binding or loop-conformation claims onto TIN-44 without experiment.

A planned cross-check could not be completed. The yeast structure 3QK9 is deposited as a Tim44 C-terminal domain "with Cymal-3", so it was downloaded to test whether a detergent occupies the equivalent cavity. The coordinate file contains only chloride and water, with no modelled detergent. That check is reported here as unavailable. No substitute result was generated.

F4. The A1/A2 amphipathic membrane-binding helix is retained

Marom et al. 2009 localised the acidic-phospholipid recognition site of Tim44 to the first α-helix of the C-terminal domain, helices A1 and A2 (PMID: 19863062). An 18-residue sliding-window Eisenberg hydrophobic moment was computed across the C-terminal domain of all three orthologues. In every species the single most amphipathic window falls in that A1 region:

Orthologue Most amphipathic window Sequence µH Domain mean ± sd Percentile in own domain
Yeast Tim44 240-257 NKLWDESENPLIVVMRKI 0.495 0.225 ± 0.126 99th
Human TIMM44 270-287 NAFIRASRALTDKVTDLL 0.582 0.248 ± 0.114 99th
C. elegans TIN-44 245-262 NMAVRMMRGVTEKIGSVF 0.562 0.261 ± 0.130 99th

The worm and human C-terminal domains begin with the identical five residues DESDN (worm 241-245, human 266-270), which anchors the alignment at the start of this element independently of the scoring matrix.

Charge composition differs by kingdom. Over the first 30 C-terminal-domain residues, the worm element is net -3 (3 basic, 6 acidic) and human is net -2 (4 basic, 6 acidic), while yeast is net +2 (5 basic, 3 acidic). Weiss et al. 1999 showed yeast Tim44 binds phospholipid monolayers "both by electrostatic interactions and by penetrating the polar head group region" (PMID: 10430866). The charge reversal between fungi and animals means the electrostatic component may not transfer residue-for-residue, even though the amphipathic moment does.

A hydrophobic moment is a prediction of amphipathicity. It is not evidence of membrane binding. This finding supports a membrane-association lead; it needs liposome flotation or carbonate extraction with the worm protein to become experimental.

F5. The TIM23 interface residues are not preferentially conserved, in worm or in human

The endogenous core TIM23 complex structure 8E1M (PMID: 37344598) resolves Tim44 as chain C, residues 107-431, alongside Tim17 (chain A) and Tim23 (chain B). Forty-one Tim44 residues lie within 4.5 Å of Tim17 or Tim23 (the Fab chains H and L were excluded as cryo-EM fiducials, not biological partners). They fall in five patches: an N-terminal segment 155-173 that contacts the Tim23 matrix loop, and C-terminal-domain patches 264-287, 342-347, 372-393 and 428-431 that contact Tim17 and Tim23.

Mapped through the yeast-worm alignment, 9 of 41 are identical in TIN-44 (22%) and 8 more are conservative, against a background of 112 of 409 identical over all aligned positions (27.4%). Fisher exact: OR 0.72, p = 0.465 for identity, OR 0.69, p = 0.323 for identity-or-similarity. Taken alone, that result invites the conclusion that the worm protein has lost the docking surface.

F6. The control overturns that reading

The yeast-to-human control was computed on exactly the same 41 positions. Human TIMM44 scores 13 of 41 identical (32%) against a background of 120 of 419 (28.6%): OR 1.18, p = 0.716. Human TIMM44 is an experimentally established component of the import motor, upregulated in angiogenesis-driven tumours and required for import capacity (PMID: 42696915), and its targeting is disrupted by UBQLN2 knockout (PMID: 33891006). Neither orthologue is enriched or depleted at the interface relative to its own background.

Six of the 41 positions are identical in all three species (yeast V159, F265, S271, F284, R342, D345). The five Tim44 residues that contact modelled lipid in 8E1M (V162 and I163 to phosphatidyl lipid; Y171, R347, F430 to cardiolipin) are conserved in neither orthologue: human has E189, I190, T198, D373, I451 and worm has T164, V165, A173, N346, A424.

The interface is maintained by fold and surface chemistry, not by individually conserved side chains. Residue-mapping arguments at this surface have no discriminating power for TIN-44 in either direction, and the yeast lipid contacts are best read as one cryo-EM snapshot rather than a transferable signature.

Weiss et al. 1999 established this architecture biochemically for yeast: "Limited proteolysis and analytical ultracentrifugation indicate that Tim44 is an elongated monomer with a stably folded C-terminal domain" (PMID: 10430866). The low pLDDT across the TIN-44 N-terminal domain is a correct prediction of native flexibility, not a model failure. It is why crystallography used C-terminal constructs only (2CW9 266-452, 2FXT 234-425) and why the 8E1M map resolves yeast Tim44 from residue 107.

The consequence for the seed hypothesis is asymmetric and is the central result of this investigation. Structural claims about the C-terminal domain can be tested by single-chain modelling and are supported. The claim about N-terminal mtHsp70 and Tim23 recruitment cannot be settled this way, because the relevant segment is disordered in isolation in every orthologue and its contacts form only inside the assembled motor.

F8. No direct experimental evidence exists for TIN-44

PubMed searches for "tin-44 Caenorhabditis elegans mitochondrial", "Tim44 N-terminal domain mtHsp70 binding Schiller" and "Tim44 Ssc1 import motor interaction mapping" returned no paper reporting TIN-44 localization, a TIN-44/HSP-6 interaction, TIN-44 membrane association, or a tin-44 mutant or RNAi phenotype.

The closest worm evidence is indirect. Yoneda et al. 2004 established that "the mitochondrial matrix HSP70 and HSP60 chaperones, encoded by the Caenorhabditis elegans hsp-6 and hsp-60 genes" are selectively activated by impaired mitochondrial protein handling (PMID: 15280428), which identifies HSP-6 as the proposed tethering partner. Rolland et al. 2019 screened the worm genome and found 171 mitochondrial genes whose knockdown induces the hsp-6 reporter, where "knockdown of these genes leads to reduced mitochondrial membrane potential and, hence, decreased protein import into mitochondria" (PMID: 31412237). That screen design predicts tin-44 RNAi should induce the reporter. Whether tin-44 appears among the 171 was not verifiable from the abstract and the supplementary list was not accessible in this run.

Every mechanistic fact cited in the seed hypothesis comes from yeast or human: peripheral cardiolipin-dependent membrane attachment through the C-terminal domain (PMID: 10430866, PMID: 23996176), the A1/A2 lipid site (PMID: 19863062), the cavity from the human crystal structure (PMID: 18084070), the N-terminal 167-residue mtHsp70-binding segment (PMID: 18426906) and the two-domain coupling mechanism (PMID: 26714107).

Mechanistic model and interpretation

The model that follows is the yeast and human mechanism, with each node labelled by how well the corresponding TIN-44 feature is supported.

Solid arrows are steps the computed evidence supports or that are established in the orthologues. Dashed arrows are links that remain predictions for TIN-44.

What the direct results show: a Tim44-family protein of 425 residues with a folded NTF2-like C-terminal domain that matches two crystal structures at 1.1-1.5 Å, an open cavity within that domain whose lining chemistry is substituted relative to human, the most amphipathic segment of the domain sitting exactly where the lipid-recognition helix sits in yeast and human, and a flexible N-terminal domain of the same character as in both reference organisms.

What is inference: that the amphipathic helix contacts cardiolipin in worm mitochondria, that the cavity accommodates a lipid tail, that the C-terminal domain sits on a worm Tim17 orthologue, and that the N-terminal domain holds HSP-6. Each of these is a reasonable transfer from Banerjee et al. 2015, who showed that the N-terminal domain interacts with import motor components while the C-terminal domain interacts with the channel (PMID: 26714107), and from Schiller et al. 2008, who narrowed the mtHsp70 tether to the N-terminal 167 residues of mature Tim44 (PMID: 18426906). None is tested in worm.

The structural asymmetry explains why the seed hypothesis, as designed, can only half-succeed. The tether lives in a region that has no crystal structure in any organism, is disordered in isolation, and forms its contacts only inside an assembled complex. Single-chain AlphaFold modelling returns low confidence there by construction. The reason this matters for curation is simple: the feature most relevant to a molecular-function annotation is the one the analysis cannot reach.

Mechanistic scope

The immediate molecular activity at issue is adaptor and membrane-anchoring activity. A peripheral inner-membrane protein contacts the Tim17/Tim23 channel on one face and holds mtHsp70 near the matrix exit of that channel, so that ATP-driven trapping can pull the incoming precursor inward. The structural correlates are a folded NTF2-like C-terminal domain with a hydrophobic cavity, an amphipathic helix for acidic-lipid contact, and a flexible N-terminal arm carrying the protein-protein contacts.

Downstream consequences that must not be annotated as if they were this activity: loss of mitochondrial membrane potential, failure to import matrix precursors, induction of the UPRmt through hsp-6 and hsp-60, and any developmental, locomotor or lifespan phenotype that follows. The hsp-6 reporter response in particular reads out general import competence across at least 171 genes (PMID: 31412237). A tin-44 hit in that assay would place the gene in the pathway and would say nothing about adaptor activity.

Conflicts and alternatives

The interface-conservation trap. The main conflict was interpretive, and it was resolved during the investigation. The first pass found the 8E1M Tim17/Tim23 interface poorly conserved in TIN-44 (22%) and this invites a conclusion of lost docking. The yeast-to-human control shows human TIMM44 scores 32% at the same positions against the same background, and human TIMM44 is a functional motor component (PMID: 42696915). If the withheld local *-bioinformatics analysis contains a claim based on low interface conservation, this control contradicts its interpretation.

Kingdom-specific electrostatics. The first 30 C-terminal-domain residues are net -3 in worm and -2 in human but net +2 in yeast. Weiss et al. 1999 reported an electrostatic contribution to yeast Tim44 membrane binding (PMID: 10430866). The amphipathic moment transfers; the charge does not. Worm membrane binding may use the same helix through a different balance of forces.

Cavity evidence is weaker than fold evidence. The cavity's lipid role comes from MD simulation (PMID: 19863062) and from a polyol cryoprotectant in a crystal (PMID: 18084070). The intended cross-check against the yeast 3QK9 structure, deposited as a Cymal-3 complex, could not be run because the coordinate file contains only chloride and water. That is reported as unavailable, not inferred.

Paralog confusion is not a live risk. InterPro, PIRSF, PANTHER and TIGRFAM signatures assign TIN-44 to the Tim44 family over its full length, and no second worm Tim44-family gene competes for the annotation.

Modelling artefacts were found and corrected. One-shot Kabsch fitting gave a spurious 10.63 Å RMSD before iterative outlier rejection. Selenomethionines in 2CW9 are HETATM MSE records and were silently dropped by a first parser until MSE was classified as protein. Nematode orthologue selection by shortest-per-genus gave implausible 18-27% identities until corrected to longest-per-genus. These are recorded because they show which numbers in this report depend on a methodological choice.

Limitations and knowledge gaps

Every result here is computational. No assay was performed and none of the numbers is evidence of binding, localization or function on its own.

Methodological limits. Biopython was not importable in the execution environment, so pairwise alignment was written from scratch (affine Needleman-Wunsch, BLOSUM62, -11/-1) and validated against an independent reference implementation. All conservation statistics derive from pairwise global alignments rather than a curated multiple alignment, so individual residue transfers in poorly aligned loops carry more uncertainty than the domain-level statistics. WormBase, STRING, IntAct and the supplementary files of 31412237 were not queried programmatically.

Discriminating tests

Ranked by information per unit effort.

  1. Read the supplementary gene list of PMID: 31412237 for tin-44. Zero cost. Converts a pathway inference into a worm-specific phenotype lead.
  2. AlphaFold-Multimer or AlphaFold 3 prediction of TIN-44 with HSP-6, and separately with the worm Tim17 and Tim23 orthologues, reporting interface PAE and ipTM. This is the only computational test that addresses the N-terminal claim, and it is the direct extension of the seed hypothesis beyond what single-chain modelling can do.
  3. Carbonate extraction of worm mitochondria probed for TIN-44. Peripheral release distinguishes the Tim44 architecture from an integral membrane protein and tests F4 directly.
  4. Co-immunoprecipitation of TIN-44 with HSP-6 under ATP against ADP. Tests tethering rather than mere association, matching the nucleotide dependence Schiller et al. described in yeast.
  5. tin-44 RNAi in an hsp-6p::GFP reporter strain with membrane-potential measurement, placing the gene in import rather than in a downstream stress response.
  6. Liposome flotation of the recombinant TIN-44 C-terminal domain against PC compared with PC/cardiolipin, repeating the yeast experiment of PMID: 10430866 in the worm protein. An A1/A2 charge-reversal mutant would test the electrostatic difference identified in F4.

Curation leads

Action on the structural hypothesis: record as partially supported. Accept the C-terminal conclusions (fold, cavity presence, amphipathic helix). Mark the N-terminal mtHsp70 and Tim23 conclusion as not testable by the stated method and leave it open.

Do not cite low interface conservation as evidence against TIN-44. The yeast-to-human control in F6 shows the comparison has no discriminating power.

Qualify the cavity claim. The correct statement is that TIN-44 is predicted to have a cavity-containing NTF2-like fold. The statement that it shares the Tim44 hydrophobic cavity as characterised in human overstates the evidence, since only 2 of 13 lining residues are identical and the Pro308-equivalent position is conserved in 23% of nematode orthologues.
