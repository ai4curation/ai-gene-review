Gene Description

SDPN-1 is the only Caenorhabditis elegans syndapin/PACSIN, an F-BAR domain protein with a C-terminal SH3 domain. Full-length SDPN-1 binds acidic phospholipids and tubulates membranes in vitro, and in the intestinal epithelium it resides on RAB-5 early endosomes and RME-1 basolateral recycling endosomes, recruited in a PI(4,5)P2-dependent manner. Loss of sdpn-1 traps recycling cargo (transferrin receptor, hTAC, DAF-4) in hybrid early/recycling endosomes and abolishes the F-actin enrichment normally found on early endosomes, indicating that SDPN-1 couples membrane curvature to actin assembly to drive endosomal fission and cargo exit toward the plasma membrane. Its SH3 domain binds the proline-rich domain of dynamin DYN-1 in the SH3 interactome, and SDPN-1 co-localizes with the amphiphysin AMPH-1 on recycling endosomes. A role for SDPN-1 in synaptic vesicle endocytosis, as for mammalian syndapin 1, has not been examined in the worm.

Research report: C. elegans sdpn-1 / SDPN-1 (UniProt Q6AHQ8)

Executive conclusion

The identity check is positive: the requested protein is SDPN-1 encoded by Caenorhabditis elegans sdpn-1/F45E1.7, not an unrelated similarly named gene. Peer-reviewed worm literature identifies SDPN-1 as the animal’s single syndapin/PACSIN-family protein and describes the same architecture supplied for UniProt Q6AHQ8: an N-terminal F-BAR/FCH–BAR membrane-remodeling region and a C-terminal SH3 interaction domain. Thus, the UniProt description and literature refer to the same protein. (gleason2016syndapinsdpn1isrequired pages 1-6, gleason2016syndapinsdpn1isrequired pages 6-9)

The best-supported primary function is that SDPN-1 is a nonenzymatic membrane-remodeling and signaling adaptor on early and basolateral recycling endosomes. It bends/tubulates acidic membranes, helps establish actin-rich endosomal domains, and enables selected internalized transmembrane cargo to leave early endosomes and return through the basolateral recycling pathway. It is therefore neither an enzyme nor a transporter and has no catalytic substrate; its functional “substrates” are curved endosomal membranes and associated trafficking/signaling complexes. Actual membrane fission in vivo remains a strong model rather than a directly visualized catalytic event. (gleason2016syndapinsdpn1isrequired pages 13-17, gleason2016endocyticrecyclingin pages 50-55, gleason2016syndapinsdpn1isrequired pages 6-9)

1. Identity, family, and domain interpretation

The 2016 definitive worm study described SDPN-1 as the only C. elegans syndapin/PACSIN-family member. The experimentally studied protein contains one conserved N-terminal F-BAR domain and a C-terminal SH3 domain, aligning with the supplied InterPro calls for FCH/F-BAR, AH/BAR superfamily, and SH3 domains. This concordance resolves the principal gene-symbol ambiguity. (gleason2016syndapinsdpn1isrequired pages 13-17, gleason2016syndapinsdpn1isrequired pages 6-9)

The domains imply complementary activities. F-BAR domains dimerize on negatively charged membranes and stabilize membrane curvature, whereas SH3 domains recruit proline-rich proteins. For worm SDPN-1, membrane remodeling is directly demonstrated: purified full-length SDPN-1A converted negatively charged liposomes into narrow tubules averaging approximately 40 nm in diameter. The adaptor role is also directly supported by SH3-dependent binding to PXF-1, although that interaction was reported in a 2023 preprint. (gleason2016syndapinsdpn1isrequired pages 6-9, rodriguezpolanco2023syndapinregulatesthe pages 9-13)

An evolutionary caveat is important. Worm SDPN-1 lacks the NPF motif used by mammalian syndapins to bind EHD proteins. Consequently, mammalian PACSIN–EHD interactions should not be automatically assigned to SDPN-1, even though SDPN-1 and the worm EHD protein RME-1 occupy related recycling compartments. (gleason2016endocyticrecyclingin pages 23-28)

2. Primary molecular and cellular function

2.1 Membrane remodeling and selective endocytic recycling

In intestinal epithelial cells, tagged SDPN-1 occupies cytoplasmic puncta near the basolateral cortex and, less prominently, the apical region. It overlaps most strongly with RAB-5-positive early endosomes and also with RME-1-positive basolateral recycling endosomes; overlap with RAB-7 late endosomes is sparse, and no overlap was detected with the tested Golgi marker. Expression was additionally observed in the pharynx and some head cells. (gleason2016endocyticrecyclingin pages 45-50, gleason2016syndapinsdpn1isrequired pages 6-9)

Loss of the predicted null allele sdpn-1(ok1667) caused the basolateral recycling reporters hTAC, hTfR, and DAF-4 to accumulate intracellularly in abnormal RAB-5- and RME-1-positive compartments. Intestine-specific SDPN-1 expression rescued hTfR accumulation, demonstrating a local, causal requirement. Early- and recycling-endosome markers became abnormally intermixed, supporting a defect in maturation, domain separation, carrier formation, or fission at the early-to-recycling-endosome transition. (gleason2016syndapinsdpn1isrequired pages 13-17, gleason2016syndapinsdpn1isrequired pages 6-9, gleason2016endocyticrecyclingin pages 40-45)

The defect is selective rather than a global collapse of membrane traffic. Retromer-dependent MIG-14 and SMA-6, degradative CD4-dileucine cargo, and apical PGP-1 were largely unaffected. These negative controls argue that SDPN-1 is not generally required for cargo uptake, lysosomal delivery, endosome-to-Golgi retrograde transport, or all apical recycling. (gleason2016syndapinsdpn1isrequired pages 6-9, gleason2016endocyticrecyclingin pages 40-45)

Taken together, experts interpreted these observations as indicating that SDPN-1 bends an endosomal membrane domain and cooperates with actin to produce or separate recycling carriers. However, the experiments demonstrate tubulation in vitro and trafficking dependence in vivo—not completed membrane scission directly. (gleason2016endocyticrecyclingin pages 50-55, gleason2016syndapinsdpn1isrequired pages 1-6)

2.2 Endosomal actin organization

SDPN-1-positive intestinal endosomes were enriched in LifeAct-labeled F-actin, with a reported SDPN-1–F-actin Pearson correlation of approximately 0.6. RNAi or ok1667 loss produced enlarged RAB-5-positive endosomes largely depleted of LifeAct. Comparable LifeAct enrichment was not detected on the tested RAB-7- or RME-1-positive compartments. Thus, SDPN-1 is required for normal F-actin association specifically at early-endosomal domains, but direct actin nucleation by SDPN-1 has not been established. (gleason2016endocyticrecyclingin pages 45-50, gleason2016syndapinsdpn1isrequired pages 13-17)

A 2021 study extended this model through RTKN-1/Rhotekin. Its data support SDPN-1-dependent targeting of RTKN-1 to recycling endosomes; RTKN-1, in turn, protects endosomal F-actin from UNC-60A/cofilin-mediated disassembly. The justified interpretation is therefore that SDPN-1 organizes the membrane platform on which an actin-protective pathway operates, not that SDPN-1 itself is a cofilin inhibitor. (rodriguezpolanco2023syndapinregulatesthe pages 16-20)

3. Signaling and trafficking pathways

3.1 RAB-10 pathway and aging

Earlier depletion experiments showed that RAB-10 is necessary for normal SDPN-1 organization: RAB-10 depletion made SDPN-1::GFP diffuse, reduced labeled structures, and disrupted its relationship with RAB-5-positive endosomes. This placed RAB-10 upstream of, or alongside, SDPN-1 in basolateral recycling. (gleason2016endocyticrecyclingin pages 45-50, gleason2016syndapinsdpn1isrequired pages 31-36)

Zhang et al., published in Nature Aging in August 2023, provided a more specific biochemical connection. SDPN-1 preferentially associated with GDP-bound RAB-10(T23N), and fragment pulldowns mapped this interaction to the F-BAR rather than SH3 domain. The result was reproduced in three experiments. Single-copy SDPN-1::GFP also colocalized with RAB-10 and RME-1 in intestinal cells; Manders analyses used nine animals, although the accessible evidence did not provide the coefficient values. DOI/URL: https://doi.org/10.1038/s43587-023-00475-1. (zhang2023ageassociateddeclinein pages 7-8)

In the aging model, SDPN-1 participates with LET-413 and the RAB-10 GEF DENN-4 in regulating RAB-10 functionality. SDPN-1 was detected in dimeric and hexameric states; KGB-1 promoted the dimeric state, and KGB-1 knockdown alleviated age-associated loss of RAB-10-labeled structures. KGB-1 loss did not substantially alter SDPN-1 transcript or protein abundance, and aged SDPN-1 was not detectably phosphorylated on serine, threonine, or tyrosine. The proposed link from oligomeric state to junctional trafficking and intestinal-barrier aging is biologically plausible but contains mechanistic steps that remain inferential. (zhang2023ageassociateddeclinein pages 15-16)

3.2 PXF-1–RAP-1–RHO-1–non-muscle myosin II

A detailed 2023 bioRxiv study proposed a second signaling axis. The SDPN-1 SH3 domain bound a proline-rich sequence at PXF-1 residues 191–198. Binding was lost either with SDPN-1 SH3 mutation P478L or when the PXF-1 sequence KGPAPLPP was changed to AGPAALPA, establishing a specific physical interface. PXF-1 is a RAP guanine-nucleotide exchange factor. Preprint posted February 2023; DOI/URL: https://doi.org/10.1101/2023.02.27.530328. (rodriguezpolanco2023syndapinregulatesthe pages 9-13)

Loss of SDPN-1 or RAP-1 elevated active RHO-1 on intestinal endosomes, while RHO-1 depletion suppressed the abnormal RME-1 structures and hTAC retention in sdpn-1 mutants. SDPN-1-positive endosomes also contained myosin regulatory light chain MLC-4. NMY-2 depletion phenocopied SDPN-1 loss, whereas NMY-1 depletion suppressed the mutant phenotype, suggesting opposing contributions of non-muscle myosin-II isoforms. The emerging pathway is SDPN-1–PXF-1→RAP-1 restraint of endosomal RHO-1→balanced actomyosin contractility→recycling. Because this evidence is from a preprint, it should be treated as compelling but less settled than the peer-reviewed 2016 core annotation. (rodriguezpolanco2023syndapinregulatesthe pages 16-20, rodriguezpolanco2023syndapinregulatesthe pages 9-13)

4. Additional biological processes

Basal autophagy and proteostasis

Oe et al., published in PLOS Genetics on June 30, 2022, found that sdpn-1 mutants accumulated LGG-1-positive autophagic structures and more muscle α-synuclein::YFP aggregates than wild type. Day-1 locomotion was similar, but day-5 mutants had significantly reduced bending frequency. These experiments support a conserved role in basal autophagy and aggregate clearance. DOI/URL: https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pgen.1010264. (oe2022pacsin1isindispensable pages 11-13)

The paper’s precise amphisome–lysosome-fusion mechanism was established mainly in mammalian PACSIN1 systems. The worm results demonstrate that SDPN-1 is required for normal basal autophagic flux/proteostasis, but do not by themselves prove that worm SDPN-1 directly executes the identical fusion step. (oe2022pacsin1isindispensable pages 11-13)

Neuronal membrane recycling

In developing GABAergic DD neurons, a 2023 Cell Reports study implicated syndapin in the UNC-8/Ca²⁺/calcineurin/dynamin/Arp2/3 pathway that dismantles old presynaptic boutons and recycles components to new ones through an activity-dependent-bulk-endocytosis-like mechanism. This supplies a physiologically distinct application of SDPN-1-mediated membrane remodeling, although the retrieved evidence did not expose sufficient SDPN-1-specific numerical data for a reliable effect-size statement. Publication: November 2023; DOI/URL: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.celrep.2023.113327.

By contrast, Xuan et al., published in PLOS Biology in April 2023, reported that sdpn-1(ok1667) alone did not detectably disrupt the assayed steady-state synaptic localization of ATG-9. SDPN-1 therefore should not be annotated as individually essential for all ATG-9 sorting at synapses; redundancy or activity-specific roles remain possible. DOI/URL: https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pbio.3002030.

5. Phenotypes and quantitative observations

Beyond endosomal morphology and cargo retention, reduced reproductive output has been reported. One reported comparison found 95 ± 10.5 eggs by day 2 in mutants versus 171 ± 21.5 in wild type (p<0.001); another reported total output of 141 ± 22.4 versus 187 ± 39.2. Gonad-arm morphology was normal in 30 examined mutants, arguing against abnormal gonad migration as the explanation. Differences in counting windows or assays likely account for the distinct fecundity values. (gleason2016endocyticrecyclingin pages 40-45, gleason2016syndapinsdpn1isrequired pages 17-21)

The most informative quantitative mechanistic results are the approximately 40-nm SDPN-1-induced liposome tubules, F-actin colocalization of approximately 0.6, three-replicate RAB-10 pulldowns, and nine-animal intestinal colocalization analyses. Many recent studies reported statistically significant differences without accessible exact worm effect sizes in the retrieved excerpts; those numbers should not be reconstructed or guessed. (zhang2023ageassociateddeclinein pages 7-8, gleason2016syndapinsdpn1isrequired pages 6-9, gleason2016syndapinsdpn1isrequired pages 13-17)

6. Current applications and research value

SDPN-1 is currently used as:

  1. A marker and perturbation point for basolateral recycling endosomes in the optically accessible worm intestine.
  2. An in-vivo model for PACSIN/syndapin membrane–actin coupling, complementing mammalian cell-culture studies.
  3. A mechanistic probe for epithelial aging and barrier maintenance, particularly RAB-10-dependent trafficking.
  4. A neuronal endocytosis model for presynaptic remodeling and bulk membrane retrieval.
  5. A conserved proteostasis model for testing how PACSIN-family dysfunction affects basal autophagy and aggregation.

These are research implementations rather than clinical applications. No evidence indicates that SDPN-1 itself is a therapeutic target in worms or that Q6AHQ8 has an enzyme reaction or transported substrate.

7. Evidence-weighted functional annotation

A defensible annotation is:

SDPN-1 is the sole C. elegans syndapin/PACSIN-family F-BAR–SH3 membrane-remodeling adaptor. It localizes principally to RAB-5-positive early endosomes and RME-1-associated basolateral recycling compartments, where it promotes F-actin association and selective recycling of internalized transmembrane cargo. Its F-BAR region tubulates acidic membranes and interacts preferentially with GDP-bound RAB-10, while its SH3 region recruits proline-rich signaling proteins, including PXF-1. SDPN-1 thereby couples membrane curvature to Rab/Rap/Rho–actomyosin regulation. It also contributes to basal autophagy/proteostasis and context-dependent neuronal membrane recycling.

The highest-confidence facts are identity, F-BAR/SH3 architecture, membrane tubulation, intestinal endosomal localization, selective basolateral cargo recycling, and endosomal F-actin dependence. The precise fission mechanism, full RAP/RHO/myosin hierarchy, aging-related oligomer regulation, and equivalence to mammalian PACSIN1 autophagic fusion mechanisms remain less completely established. No directly relevant 2024 primary paper was identified; the latest target-specific advances recovered were published or posted in 2023.
