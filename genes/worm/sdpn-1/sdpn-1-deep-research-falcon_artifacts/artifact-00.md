| **Identity and architecture** | Worm literature identifies **SDPN-1 as the sole *C. elegans* syndapin/PACSIN-family protein**, containing an N-terminal **F-BAR** domain and C-terminal **SH3** domain. This agrees with Q6AHQ8/F45E1.7 domain annotations. | Expressed in intestine, pharynx, and some head cells; intestinal protein occurs in puncta near basolateral and apical membranes. | **Strong, direct worm evidence.** The literature supports the family assignment, although the Q6AHQ8 accession itself was supplied by UniProt rather than independently tested. (pqac-00000004, pqac-00000007) | Gleason et al., 2016 |
| **Direct membrane remodeling** | Purified full-length SDPN-1A tubulated negatively charged liposomes, producing narrow membrane tubules averaging **~40 nm** in diameter. | Cell-free acidic-lipid membrane assay; relevant to curved endosomal tubules. | **Strong biochemical evidence** for membrane-bending/tubulating activity; actual membrane fission in vivo was not directly demonstrated. (pqac-00000003, pqac-00000007) | Gleason et al., 2016 |
| **Basolateral endocytic recycling** | In *sdpn-1(ok1667)*, basolateral recycling cargoes **hTAC, hTfR, and DAF-4** accumulated intracellularly in RAB-5- and RME-1-positive compartments; intestine-specific SDPN-1 rescued hTfR accumulation. | Early endosomes and basolateral recycling endosomes of intestinal epithelial cells. | **Strong genetic, localization, and rescue evidence.** Supports cargo exit/maturation at the early-to-recycling-endosome interface; fission is a mechanistic model rather than a directly observed event. (pqac-00000002, pqac-00000007, pqac-00000017) | Gleason et al., 2016 |
| **Cargo-pathway specificity and negative controls** | Retromer-dependent **MIG-14 and SMA-6**, degradative reporter **CD4-dileucine**, and apical **PGP-1** were largely unaffected, arguing against a universal uptake, degradation, retrograde-transport, or apical-recycling defect. | Intestinal endomembrane system. | **Strong negative-control evidence** for a selective role in basolateral recycling, although not every endogenous cargo class was examined. (pqac-00000007, pqac-00000017, pqac-00000019) | Gleason et al., 2016 |
| **Endosomal F-actin organization** | SDPN-1-positive endosomes were enriched in LifeAct-labeled F-actin, with **Pearson correlation ≈0.6**; SDPN-1 depletion caused enlarged RAB-5 endosomes depleted of LifeAct. | RAB-5-positive intestinal early endosomes; comparable enrichment was not detected on RAB-7- or RME-1-positive structures in the reported analysis. | **Strong imaging and loss-of-function evidence** that SDPN-1 supports endosomal actin association; direct actin nucleation by SDPN-1 was not shown. (pqac-00000000, pqac-00000002, pqac-00000016) | Gleason et al., 2016 |
| **RAB-10 interaction and recruitment** | SDPN-1 preferentially associated with GDP-bound **RAB-10(T23N)** in pulldowns; the interaction mapped to the **F-BAR**, not SH3, fragment and was validated in three replicates. SDPN-1::GFP also colocalized with RAB-10 and RME-1 in intestine (**n=9** animals for Manders analyses). | Intestinal sorting/recycling endosomes. | **Direct biochemical and imaging evidence.** Binding preference is established in vitro, but its complete in-vivo functional consequence and whether binding is direct under physiological nucleotide cycling remain incompletely resolved. (pqac-00000006, pqac-00000008, pqac-00000014) | Zhang et al., 2023 |
| **PXF-1–RAP-1–RHO-1–myosin pathway** | SDPN-1’s SH3 domain bound the proline-rich **PXF-1 residues 191–198**; SDPN-1 P478L or mutation of the PXF-1 site abolished binding. Loss of SDPN-1 or RAP-1 increased endosomal active RHO-1; RHO-1 depletion suppressed *sdpn-1* recycling defects. NMY-2 depletion phenocopied loss, whereas NMY-1 depletion suppressed it. | SDPN-1-positive intestinal endosomes containing RHO-1 and non-muscle myosin machinery. | **Mechanistically detailed but preprint-level evidence.** Direct binding and genetic epistasis support the pathway, but the source is a **2023 bioRxiv preprint**, and opposing NMY-1/NMY-2 roles require further validation. (pqac-00000001, pqac-00000015) | Rodriguez-Polanco et al., 2023, bioRxiv |
| **RTKN-1 and recycling-endosome actin** | The study reports that SDPN-1 directs proper recycling-endosomal residence of **RTKN-1/Rhotekin**, which protects endosomal F-actin from UNC-60A/cofilin-mediated disassembly. | Recycling endosomes in *C. elegans* intestinal cells. | **Direct worm study, but details are limited in the gathered excerpts.** The available evidence supports localization dependence; it does not establish that SDPN-1 itself inhibits cofilin. (pqac-00000001) | Yan et al., 2021 |
| **Basal autophagy and aggregate clearance** | *sdpn-1* mutants accumulated the autophagosome marker **LGG-1** and more muscle **α-synuclein::YFP aggregates** than wild type. Day-1 motility was similar to wild type, whereas day-5 mutants had significantly reduced bending frequency. | Whole-animal basal autophagy; α-synuclein expressed in muscle. | **Direct in-vivo evidence** for a conserved contribution to basal autophagy and aggregate clearance. Exact worm effect sizes, sample sizes, and whether SDPN-1 directly controls amphisome–lysosome fusion were unavailable in the gathered excerpt; the detailed fusion mechanism was established mainly for mammalian PACSIN1. (pqac-00000009) | Oe et al., 2022 |
| **Presynaptic remodeling / bulk endocytosis** | Reported *sdpn-1* knockdown was tested in the UNC-8/Ca²⁺-dependent dismantling and recycling of presynaptic material during DD-neuron remodeling, consistent with participation of syndapin and dynamin in an activity-dependent-bulk-endocytosis-like process. | Remodeling GABAergic DD-neuron presynaptic boutons. | **Relevant direct worm experiment, but quantitative SDPN-1-specific results were unavailable in the gathered evidence.** Therefore, SDPN-1’s exact necessity and effect size should not be overstated. | Cuentas-Condori et al., 2023 |
| **ATG-9 synaptic trafficking—negative result** | *sdpn-1(ok1667)* single mutants were tested for synaptic ATG-9 localization; the reported result was **no detectable disruption**, arguing that SDPN-1 is not individually required for the assayed steady-state ATG-9 localization phenotype. | Presynaptic ATG-9 trafficking and autophagy-related vesicles. | **Important negative genetic result.** It does not exclude redundancy, context-specific activity dependence, or genetic interaction with CLA-1; quantitative details were unavailable in the gathered evidence. | Xuan et al., 2023 |
