Gene Description

CCEP-290 (Y47G6A.17; also referred to as CEP-290) is the Caenorhabditis elegans ortholog of human CEP290 (NPHP6/MKS4), a large (1736 aa) protein made up largely of predicted coiled-coil segments. It is expressed only in ciliated sensory neurons and concentrates at the ciliary transition zone (TZ), where it occupies a central position inside the ring of axonemal doublet microtubules and is a core component of the central cylinder (apical ring), the inner scaffold to which the outer doublet and inner singlet microtubules attach. Loss of CCEP-290 fragments or abolishes the central cylinder and disrupts TZ ultrastructure, including Y-links, and compromises the ciliary gate, so that periciliary membrane proteins (TRAM-1a, RPI-2) leak into cilia and ARL-13 escapes its ciliary compartment. CCEP-290 acts in the TZ assembly hierarchy between MKS-5 and the MKS module, being required for TZ localisation of MKS-module proteins (MKSR-1, MKSR-2, MKS-2, TMEM-231, TMEM-17, TMEM-218) and of TMEM-138 and CDKL-1, but not of MKS-5 or the NPHP module. Genetically it behaves as an MKS-module gene: cep-290 single mutants form largely intact cilia, but combining cep-290 with nphp-4 causes synthetic cilium structure defects, and removing all three TZ modules abolishes TZ structure. Together with NPHP-4, CCEP-290 is also needed for dendrite anchoring and extension during embryogenesis.

Core Functions

Structural core component of the ciliary transition-zone central cylinder, the inner scaffold that holds the axonemal microtubules. It is needed for TZ ultrastructure, ciliary gate (diffusion barrier) function, and recruitment of MKS-module proteins and of TMEM-138/CDKL-1 to the TZ. Its specific molecular activity (e.g. which partners its coiled coils bind) is not established, so no MF is asserted.

Suggested Questions for Experts

Q: Does CCEP-290 need MKS-5 to reach the TZ (Li et al. 2016) or does it target independently (Schouteden et al. 2015)? Which alleles and imaging conditions explain the difference?

Q: Should PANTHER reclassify A2A266 from PTHR43941 (SMC2) into the CEP290 family PTHR18879? The SMC2 assignment propagates condensin terms to the worm CEP290 ortholog and keeps CEP290-family IBAs off it.

Q: Which CCEP-290 coiled-coil segments bind MKS-5 and the MKS-module proteins it recruits?

Suggested Experiments

Experiment: Map the CCEP-290 regions needed for central cylinder localisation and for MKSR-2/MKS-2 recruitment, using CRISPR domain deletions with endogenously tagged TZ markers and expansion or super-resolution microscopy.

Hypothesis: Distinct coiled-coil regions of CCEP-290 mediate central cylinder incorporation and tethering of MKS-module proteins.

Key findings

  • TZ protein, expressed only in ciliated neurons. It is needed for ciliary gate function and TZ ultrastructure PMID:26982032.
  • It is a core component of the central cylinder PMID:26124290.
  • It acts downstream of MKS-5 and upstream of the MKS module PMID:26982032. It is also needed at the TZ for TMEM-138 and CDKL-1.
  • Conflict: Schouteden et al. report that CCEP-290 targets the TZ independently of MKS-5 PMID:26124290. Li et al. 2016 report that it depends on MKS-5. This is recorded as a DISPUTED finding.
  • PANTHER misassignment: UniProt places A2A266 in PTHR43941 (SMC2). Because of that, five condensin/chromatin IBAs are wrong, and all five are REMOVEd. The CEP290 family is PTHR18879.
  • Wrong PMID: the central cylinder IDA cites PMID:25961505, a CLK-1 mitochondrial paper with no cilium content (full text checked). The intended paper is PMID:26124290. This is recorded as reference_review WRONG_IDENTIFIER with a replacement.
  • NEW GO:1905349 (ciliary transition zone assembly). CEP290-family orthologs carry this term by IBA (e.g. rat Cep290, checked in QuickGO). The worm protein misses it only because of the PANTHER misassignment.
