Gene Description

IFT-20 is the Caenorhabditis elegans ortholog of IFT20, a small (129 aa) coiled-coil subunit of the intraflagellar transport complex B (IFT-B, peripheral IFT-B2 subcomplex). Its gene carries a DAF-19-regulated X-box and is expressed in ciliated sensory neurons. IFT-20::GFP enters sensory cilia and undergoes IFT with the same kinetics as other IFT subunits, and is also seen in dendrites and neuronal cell bodies. Unlike mammalian IFT20, which is anchored at the Golgi by GMAP210, worm IFT-20 has not been detected at the Golgi. ift-20 null mutants position basal bodies and form transition zones but build only short, vestigial axonemes, recruit much less IFT-B (IFT-74) to the ciliary base and into cilia, fail to take up dye, and are defective in osmotic avoidance and male mating.

Core Functions

IFT-20 is a small coiled-coil subunit of the IFT-B particle in C. elegans ciliated sensory neurons. It moves by IFT, is needed for robust recruitment of IFT-B to the ciliary base and its entry into cilia, and is required to build a full-length sensory axoneme. No discrete molecular activity has been assigned in the worm, and the Golgi role of mammalian IFT20 does not appear to be conserved.

Suggested Questions for Experts

Q: Why can ift-20 null worms still assemble short vestigial cilia, when IFT20 loss blocks ciliogenesis in most mammalian cells? Is there partial redundancy within the worm IFT-B2 subcomplex?

Q: Does worm IFT-20 have any role in delivering ciliary membrane proteins (e.g. receptors or channels) given that it does not associate with the Golgi as in mammals?

Suggested Experiments

Experiment: Image endogenously tagged ciliary membrane proteins (e.g. ODR-10, OSM-9) in ift-20 null neurons to test whether IFT-20 is selectively required for membrane cargo delivery.

Experiment: Test direct binding of IFT-20 to other IFT-B2 subunits (IFT-54/DYF-11, IFT-57/CHE-13) with recombinant worm proteins to define its position in the worm IFT-B complex.
