# Functional annotation report: *C. elegans dpy-23* / DPY-23

## Executive conclusion

The target identity is verified. *Caenorhabditis elegans dpy-23* is the same locus as *apm-2* and encodes the worm’s sole μ2 medium subunit of the heterotetrameric AP-2 clathrin-adaptor complex. Genomic rescue, mutant mapping, and molecular characterization—not merely sequence similarity—established this assignment. This matches UniProt P35603 and the supplied names AP50/μ2-adaptin; no literature concerning a similarly named protein in another organism was used. (gu2008μ2adaptinfacilitates pages 2-3)

DPY-23 is **not an enzyme or transporter**. Its primary function is structural and selective: as the μ2 cargo-recognition subunit of AP-2, it helps couple plasma-membrane cargo and phosphatidylinositol-4,5-bisphosphate [PI(4,5)P2] to the clathrin-coated-pit machinery. Its best-supported worm-specific roles are in clathrin organization and synaptic-vesicle recycling, MIG-14/Wntless internalization, glutamate-receptor trafficking, and membrane/protein organization at sensory-cilium bases. Some signaling and developmental phenotypes are therefore downstream consequences of altered membrane trafficking rather than evidence that DPY-23 is itself a canonical signaling protein. (gu2008μ2adaptinfacilitates pages 1-2, gu2008μ2adaptinfacilitates pages 1-1, pan2008c.elegansap2 pages 4-5, ditirro2019thecaenorhabditiselegans pages 16-17)

## 1. Identity, nomenclature, family, and domains

The names *dpy-23*, *apm-2*, and protein APM-2/DPY-23 refer to the same *C. elegans* locus and product. The locus was initially defined by dumpy mutants; RNA interference phenocopied the morphology, overlapping genomic clones rescued it, and a 12-kb genomic fragment containing *apm-2* fully rescued dumpy, uncoordinated, and egg-laying defects. The study explicitly identified APM-2 as the only AP-2 μ2 subunit in the worm. (gu2008μ2adaptinfacilitates pages 2-3)

AP-2 comprises α and β2 large subunits, the μ2 medium subunit DPY-23, and a σ2 small subunit. The supplied InterPro assignments—Adaptor complex medium-subunit family, AP2_Mu_N, AP2_Mu_C superfamily, AP_mu_sigma, and clathrin-adaptor μ domains—are therefore internally consistent with the experimentally established identity. The characteristic architecture supports an N-terminal complex-assembly region and a C-terminal cargo-recognition region, although a purified DPY-23 domain structure was not identified in the retrieved worm literature. (garafalo2015theap2clathrin pages 1-4)

The mechanistic model is strongly conserved: μ2 recognizes cytoplasmic tyrosine-based YxxΦ sorting motifs, while μ2/AP-2 contacts PI(4,5)P2 and AP-2 recruits clathrin at plasma-membrane endocytic sites. These molecular interactions are established AP-2/μ2 principles cited by the worm studies, but should be distinguished from direct biochemical measurements using purified DPY-23. (gu2008μ2adaptinfacilitates pages 1-2, gu2008μ2adaptinfacilitates pages 1-1)

## 2. Primary molecular and structural function

DPY-23 performs three closely connected functions:

1. **Cargo selection:** it contributes to recognition of cytosolic sorting information on transmembrane cargo.
2. **Membrane engagement:** μ2/AP-2 binds the PI(4,5)P2-rich plasma-membrane environment.
3. **Coat organization:** assembled AP-2 links selected cargo to clathrin and other endocytic factors, promoting coated-pit formation and internalization. (gu2008μ2adaptinfacilitates pages 1-2, gu2008μ2adaptinfacilitates pages 1-1)

DPY-23 also stabilizes the AP-2 complex. In *apm-2(e840)* and *apm-2(gm17)* mutants, α-adaptin abundance fell to approximately 60% and 12% of wild type, respectively. Residual α-adaptin nevertheless remained detectable at synapses and coelomocyte plasma membranes. Thus, DPY-23 is important for complex integrity, but some partial AP-2 assemblies or alternative interactions persist in its absence. This residual activity is an important qualification when interpreting viable mutants and incomplete endocytic phenotypes. (gu2008μ2adaptinfacilitates pages 6-7, gu2008μ2adaptinfacilitates pages 8-8)

## 3. Cellular and subcellular localization

DPY-23 is highly expressed in the nervous system and localizes to synapses, consistent with a local role in synaptic-vesicle membrane retrieval. Neuron-specific restoration of APM-2 rescued synaptic-vesicle abundance, directly supporting a neuronal, presynaptic function. (gu2008μ2adaptinfacilitates pages 1-1, gu2008μ2adaptinfacilitates pages 5-6)

Its localization is not exclusively neuronal. Tissue-specific rescue places a critical pool in Wnt-producing hypodermal cells, where DPY-23 mediates MIG-14/Wntless internalization. At AWB olfactory sensory endings, DPY-23::GFP is concentrated at the ciliary base/periciliary membrane compartment, a specialized membrane-trafficking zone. Residual AP-2 components also occur at coelomocyte plasma membranes. Collectively, the protein acts principally on the cytoplasmic face of the plasma membrane and at specialized endocytic membrane domains, rather than extracellularly or as a stable resident of the ciliary axoneme. (gu2008μ2adaptinfacilitates pages 6-7, pan2008c.elegansap2 pages 4-5, ditirro2019thecaenorhabditiselegans pages 16-17)

## 4. Major biological processes and pathway-specific evidence

### 4.1 Synaptic-vesicle recycling

Loss of μ2 mislocalized synaptic clathrin and reduced synaptic-vesicle abundance to roughly 60% of control. Miniature-event frequency was 60% of wild type and evoked amplitude 82%; corresponding vesicle levels were 55% at GABAergic and 69% at cholinergic synapses. In broader summary measurements, evoked responses were approximately 65% of control. Neuronal rescue restored vesicle number. (gu2008μ2adaptinfacilitates pages 1-1, gu2008μ2adaptinfacilitates pages 5-6)

The defect was substantially milder than in synaptojanin or endophilin mutants, which retained only about 38% and 30% of normal vesicle numbers in one comparison, or approximately 35% in a broader comparison. Mutant animals retained neurotransmission, and synaptic-vesicle proteins remained localized. The correct annotation is therefore that DPY-23 **facilitates but is not absolutely essential for synaptic-vesicle recycling**. Residual AP-2 and AP-2-independent retrieval routes remain plausible explanations. (gu2008μ2adaptinfacilitates pages 5-6, gu2008μ2adaptinfacilitates pages 6-7, gu2008μ2adaptinfacilitates pages 8-8)

### 4.2 Wnt secretion: AP-2–retromer recycling of MIG-14/Wntless

This is the clearest cargo-specific developmental mechanism. In *dpy-23* mutants, MIG-14/Wntless::GFP accumulated close to the plasma membrane and in large membrane-associated vesicles, consistent with impaired internalization. By contrast, loss of retromer caused intracellular accumulation and reduced MIG-14 abundance, consistent with failure to retrieve internalized Wntless from a degradative route. (pan2008c.elegansap2 pages 4-5)

DPY-23 functions in Wnt-producing cells: hypodermal DPY-23::GFP completely rescued Q-cell migration defects and partly rescued HSN defects, whereas neuronal expression did not. Expression driven by the *egl-20/Wnt* promoter partly rescued both phenotypes. Extra MIG-14 rescued *dpy-23* defects, but extra DPY-23 did not bypass *mig-14*. The resulting mechanistic model is sequential: AP-2/DPY-23 first internalizes surface MIG-14; retromer then retrieves it for recycling, sustaining repeated Wnt secretion and long-range developmental signaling. (pan2008c.elegansap2 pages 4-5)

### 4.3 GLR-1 glutamate-receptor trafficking

Two independent *apm-2* alleles reduced GLR-1::GFP fluorescence in ventral-nerve-cord puncta by 26% and 30%, respectively, both with *p*<0.001. Rescue and temporal experiments placed the requirement in mature GLR-1-expressing interneurons. Rather than simply accumulating at the surface as expected from a pure endocytosis block, GLR-1 accumulated in neuronal cell bodies and Syntaxin-13- and RAB-14-positive intracellular compartments. (garafalo2015theap2clathrin pages 1-4)

This indicates that AP-2/DPY-23 also supports an early secretory or endosomal trafficking step needed to maintain receptor abundance in neurites. It is an important warning against annotating every DPY-23 phenotype as reduced plasma-membrane uptake alone. (garafalo2015theap2clathrin pages 1-4)

### 4.4 Sensory-cilium membrane organization

DPY-23 localizes to the AWB periciliary membrane compartment. *dpy-23* loss expands AWB ciliary membrane morphology, while loss of the Tubby-family protein TUB-1 completely suppresses this expansion; *tub-1; dpy-23* cilia resemble *tub-1* single mutants. DPY-23::GFP localization/intensity and PI(4,5)P2-reporter distribution were assessed directly by fluorescence imaging. (ditirro2019thecaenorhabditiselegans pages 16-17)

The favored model is that TUB-1 acts partly through DPY-23 to position PPK-1/PIP5 kinase and thereby regulate PI(4,5)P2 distribution, ciliary membrane shape, and ciliary signaling-protein traffic. The morphology, localization, and epistasis are direct observations; the precise linear TUB-1→DPY-23→PPK-1 mechanism remains an inference from those data. (ditirro2019thecaenorhabditiselegans pages 16-17)

### 4.5 BMP/TGF-β-associated extracellular-matrix regulation

In *dpy-23(e840)*, GFP::DBL-1 abundance was 39.87 ± 14.82 relative units versus 100 ± 15.57 in controls (*p*=0.0005), whereas the *spp-9p::GFP* pathway reporter rose to 120.47 ± 13.55 (*p*=0.0015). These results establish that DPY-23 loss changes DBL-1 abundance and BMP-associated transcriptional output. They do not by themselves demonstrate that DPY-23 directly binds or transports DBL-1 or its receptor; models involving ligand trafficking, receptor trafficking, or indirect extracellular-matrix feedback remain to be resolved. (lakdawala2022identifyingandcharacterizing pages 33-39)

### 4.6 Regulation by NEKL kinases

A forward suppressor study linked AP-2 activity to the NIMA-related kinases NEKL-2 and NEKL-3. The DPY-23 S434T allele *fd155* allowed 98% of otherwise severely affected *nekl-2; nekl-3* animals to reach adulthood, versus only 1–2% of the parental kinase-deficient animals. An independent Y275D allele, a predicted-null allele, and RNAi also suppressed, showing that the interaction was not unique to S434T. (joseph2020controlofclathrinmediated pages 3-5)

This is strong genetic evidence that lowering AP-2/DPY-23 activity counterbalances the NEKL-deficient state. It is not direct proof that NEKL proteins phosphorylate DPY-23; the mechanistic conclusion is pathway-level antagonism within clathrin-mediated endocytosis. (joseph2020controlofclathrinmediated pages 3-5)

## 5. Recent development: SEL-5/AAK1 and DPY-23 phosphorylation

The most directly relevant 2024 study examined SEL-5, the worm orthologue of AP2-associated kinase AAK1. Phospho-specific immunoblotting showed that SEL-5 loss reduced phosphorylation of DPY-23 at T160, corresponding to mammalian AP2M1 T156, but did not eliminate it. This implies at least one additional DPY-23 kinase. (knop2024caenorhabditiseleganssel5aak1 pages 6-7, knop2024caenorhabditiseleganssel5aak1 pages 7-8)

Crucially, animals expressing nonphosphorylatable DPY-23 did not reproduce the Wnt-dependent QL-descendant migration or excretory-canal shortening seen in *sel-5; vps-29* animals, and kinase-inactive SEL-5 rescued those defects. MIG-14 abundance and localization were also not significantly altered in *sel-5; vps-29*. The authors therefore concluded that SEL-5’s roles in these developmental processes are kinase-independent and are not explained by failure to phosphorylate DPY-23 or by a simple MIG-14-internalization defect. The paper was published 19 July 2024 in *eLife*, DOI [10.7554/eLife.91054](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.91054). (knop2024caenorhabditiseleganssel5aak1 pages 15-16, knop2024caenorhabditiseleganssel5aak1 pages 1-2, knop2024caenorhabditiseleganssel5aak1 pages 7-8)

No comparably substantial 2023 DPY-23-specific primary study was identified in the retrieved literature. Thus, the mechanistic foundation remains dominated by older, precise worm genetics and cell biology, with the 2024 work refining rather than replacing that model.

## 7. Phenotypes and interpretation

The historical “Dpy” phenotype reflects altered body morphology/cuticle biology, but it is not the protein’s primary biochemical activity. Depending on allele and tissue, mutants can also exhibit uncoordination, egg-laying defects, altered Wnt-dependent cell migration, perturbed synaptic physiology, receptor-trafficking defects, and abnormal ciliary membrane morphology. Full genomic rescue establishes that these phenotypes belong to the *dpy-23/apm-2* locus. (gu2008μ2adaptinfacilitates pages 2-3)

Phenotypic interpretation requires caution. Neuronal APM-2 rescued synaptic-vesicle number, whereas skin expression—not neuronal expression—rescued some whole-animal thrashing and aldicarb phenotypes. Therefore, locomotor or pharmacological readouts can include non-neuronal contributions and should not automatically be treated as direct measures of synaptic AP-2 function. (gu2008μ2adaptinfacilitates pages 5-6)

## 8. Current applications and real-world relevance

DPY-23 is primarily a **research model**, not a clinical target with a worm-specific therapeutic implementation. Its practical applications include:

- dissecting clathrin-mediated endocytosis in an intact animal;
- separating neuronal from epidermal or ligand-producing-cell functions by tissue-specific rescue;
- quantifying synaptic-vesicle recycling using electron microscopy and electrophysiology;
- studying how endocytosis and retromer cooperate to recycle Wntless and sustain Wnt secretion;
- testing receptor trafficking in mature neurons through GLR-1 reporters;
- analyzing membrane remodeling at sensory-cilium bases; and
- genetically probing conserved regulators such as AAK1/SEL-5 and NEK/NEKL kinases. (gu2008μ2adaptinfacilitates pages 5-6, garafalo2015theap2clathrin pages 1-4, pan2008c.elegansap2 pages 4-5, ditirro2019thecaenorhabditiselegans pages 16-17, joseph2020controlofclathrinmediated pages 3-5, knop2024caenorhabditiseleganssel5aak1 pages 15-16)

These uses have broader relevance because AP2M1/AP-2, Wntless-retromer recycling, synaptic-vesicle retrieval, and ciliary trafficking are conserved. Nevertheless, human disease or therapeutic claims should be made at the pathway level, not attributed directly to worm DPY-23 without cross-species validation.

## 9. Recommended functional annotation

**Primary function:** DPY-23 is the μ2 medium/cargo-recognition subunit of the *C. elegans* AP-2 clathrin-adaptor complex. It stabilizes AP-2 and helps select transmembrane cargo at PI(4,5)P2-rich plasma-membrane domains for clathrin-associated internalization.

**Principal location:** cytoplasmic face of the plasma membrane and specialized endocytic zones, including neuronal synapses, Wnt-producing hypodermal cells, coelomocyte membranes, and the periciliary membrane compartment of sensory neurons.

**Best-supported processes:** clathrin-mediated endocytosis; synaptic-vesicle recycling; MIG-14/Wntless internalization and retromer-dependent recycling; neuronal receptor trafficking; and ciliary membrane/phosphoinositide organization.

**Regulation:** DPY-23 is phosphorylated at T160 partly through SEL-5/AAK1, although this modification is dispensable for the specific Wnt-dependent migration and excretory-canal phenotypes tested in 2024. AP-2/DPY-23 activity also interacts genetically with NEKL kinase pathways. (joseph2020controlofclathrinmediated pages 3-5, knop2024caenorhabditiseleganssel5aak1 pages 15-16, knop2024caenorhabditiseleganssel5aak1 pages 6-7)

**Evidence confidence:** high for identity, AP-2 membership, synaptic localization/function, MIG-14 internalization, and GLR-1/ciliary trafficking phenotypes; moderate for exact cargo recognition by purified worm DPY-23 and for the proposed linear ciliary and BMP mechanisms, which rely partly on conserved AP-2 biology or genetic inference.
