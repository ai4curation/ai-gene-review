Gene Description

DAF-28 is a secreted insulin-like peptide of C. elegans and a major agonist ligand of the DAF-2 insulin/IGF-1-like receptor. It is expressed in the ASI and ASJ sensory neurons. Its expression in ASI and ASJ is down-regulated by dauer-inducing cues (pheromone, low food) and in daf-11 guanylyl cyclase mutants, which couples environmental sensing to DAF-2 signaling. DAF-28 acts with INS-6 and INS-4 to prevent dauer entry under favorable conditions; it is more important than INS-6 for inhibiting dauer entry. Neuron-derived DAF-28 keeps the intestinal FOXO factor DAF-16 out of the nucleus. The dominant-negative daf-28(sa191) allele disrupts a probable proteolytic processing site and causes dauer arrest; DAF-28 is processed by the proprotein convertases KPC-1 and/or EGL-3. DAF-28 localizes to synaptic regions of ASJ, and insulin released from ASJ also contributes to cold habituation and to insulin-dependent regulation of neuromuscular junction development.

Core Functions

DAF-28 is a secreted insulin-like peptide hormone released from ASI/ASJ sensory neurons in favorable conditions. It acts as a DAF-2 agonist, keeping intestinal DAF-16 inactive and preventing dauer entry; its expression falls under dauer-inducing cues, coupling sensory input to the dauer decision.
