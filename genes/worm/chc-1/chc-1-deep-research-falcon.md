# Functional annotation report: *C. elegans chc-1* / CHC-1

## Executive conclusion

The requested target is correctly identified: **UniProt P34574 corresponds to *Caenorhabditis elegans chc-1* (ORF T20G5.1), encoding the worm clathrin heavy chain**. The literature consistently uses *chc-1* for this protein, including precise CRISPR modeling of human **CLTC** variants at orthologous residues. No evidence reviewed here was taken from an unrelated gene with a similar symbol. The supplied ARM-type fold, CHCR, clathrin heavy-chain/VPS repeats, linker core, and N-terminal clathrin domains are coherent with a long, repeat-rich clathrin heavy chain rather than an enzyme or transporter. Direct worm structural determination was not found, so detailed triskelion/lattice architecture remains a strong inference from the conserved clathrin family rather than a P34574-specific atomic structure. (pannone2023therecurrentpathogenic pages 1-2, pannone2023therecurrentpathogenic pages 10-11)

CHC-1 is a **non-enzymatic membrane-coat scaffold**. Its primary function is to assemble clathrin coats that bend membranes and help form cargo-selective transport intermediates. It has no catalytic reaction or molecular “substrate” in the enzymatic sense. Cargo specificity is supplied mainly by adaptor complexes and accessory proteins, whereas CHC-1 provides the mechanically organizing cage. In worms it functions at plasma-membrane coated pits, post-Golgi/endosomal carriers, synaptic endosomes, ciliary sorting compartments, phagosomes, and apical epidermal structures. Genetic depletion and a temperature-sensitive allele show that CHC-1 is required for endocytosis and viability. (sato2014c.elegansas pages 4-6)

## 1. Identity, family, and structural interpretation

The identity assignment is supported at three levels. First, worm literature explicitly calls *chc-1* the clathrin heavy-chain gene. Second, functional CHC-1 fluorescent reporters rescue the viability and endocytosis defects of a temperature-sensitive *chc-1* allele, tying the named locus to clathrin-dependent membrane traffic. Third, human CLTC p.Pro890Leu and p.Leu1047Pro variants were modeled at the endogenous orthologous worm residues, CHC-1 p.Pro892Leu and p.Leu1049Pro, respectively. (pannone2023therecurrentpathogenic pages 1-2, pannone2023therecurrentpathogenic pages 10-11, sato2005caenorhabditiselegansrme6 pages 1-4)

The supplied domains also fit this assignment. Clathrin heavy chains contain an N-terminal adaptor/accessory-protein interaction region followed by extended repeat-rich segments that fold into the characteristic clathrin leg. Heavy chains oligomerize and associate with light chains to produce triskelion units, which polymerize into a curved lattice. In *C. elegans*, CHC-1 and the light chain CLIC-1 are principal cage components, although CHC-1 is genetically much more essential: *chc-1* RNAi or temperature-sensitive inactivation disrupts endocytosis and viability, whereas CLIC-1 loss alone is comparatively mild. Adaptors—not clathrin itself—provide most direct membrane- and cargo-binding specificity. (sato2014c.elegansas pages 4-6)

**Annotation confidence:** high for gene identity, clathrin-family membership, coat-scaffold function, and essentiality; moderate for residue-level structural interpretation because no CHC-1-specific high-resolution structure was identified.

## 2. Primary molecular function

### Canonical clathrin-mediated endocytosis

At the plasma membrane, CHC-1 assembles into clathrin-coated pits. The coat concentrates adaptor-selected cargo and forms a lattice that promotes membrane curvature and budding. Worm RNAi and temperature-sensitive genetics establish that CHC-1 is essential for receptor-mediated endocytosis and organismal viability. This is its best-supported primary function. (sato2014c.elegansas pages 4-6)

CHC-1-positive structures also connect coated-pit formation to early-endosome activation. In coelomocytes, tagged CHC-1 colocalized with α-adaptin and with RME-6, a regulator of RAB-5. These observations support a model in which RME-6 is recruited at clathrin-coated pits so that newly internalized cargo is coupled spatially to RAB-5 activation and early-endosome entry. The evidence directly establishes localization; the complete temporal coupling mechanism additionally depends on the RME-6 experiments in that study. [Sato et al., May 2005, DOI 10.1038/ncb1261](https://doi.org/10.1038/ncb1261). (sato2005caenorhabditiselegansrme6 pages 1-4)

### Post-Golgi and polarized sorting

CHC-1 is not restricted to plasma-membrane uptake. Together with AP-1, it supports post-Golgi/endosomal sorting needed for intestinal apical polarity and lumen formation. The favored model is that CHC-1/AP-1-coated carriers move newly synthesized membrane cargo through a Golgi-to-endosome-to-apical-surface route. Thus, CHC-1 contributes to epithelial morphogenesis by controlling where membrane proteins and lipids are delivered, not by acting as a polarity-signaling enzyme. [Zhang et al., June 2012, DOI 10.1242/dev.077347](https://doi.org/10.1242/dev.077347). (sato2014c.elegansas pages 33-35)

In sensory neurons, disruption of *chc-1* phenocopied loss of the AP-1 subunit UNC-101: ODR-10-containing carriers failed to form and ODR-10 was misrouted to inappropriate plasma-membrane domains rather than selectively delivered to the cilium. This places CHC-1 in an early AP-1/RAB-8-dependent ciliary membrane-sorting step. The direct CHC-1 result concerns cargo sorting; the more extensive defects in ciliary morphology reported for AP-1 mutants should not automatically be assigned to CHC-1 without equivalent experiments. [Kaplan et al., November 2010, DOI 10.1242/jcs.073908](https://doi.org/10.1242/jcs.073908). (kaplan2010theap1clathrin pages 1-2)

## 3. Cellular localization

CHC-1 is a cytosolic coat protein that is recruited transiently to membranes rather than an integral membrane protein. Directly observed worm locations include:

- **Plasma-membrane coated pits**, where CHC-1 forms the structural coat required for endocytosis. (sato2014c.elegansas pages 4-6)
- **Coelomocyte clathrin-coated structures**, colocalizing with α-adaptin and RME-6. (sato2005caenorhabditiselegansrme6 pages 1-4)
- **Synaptic endosomal membranes**, where clathrin buds new synaptic vesicles; a conditional plasma-membrane role becomes more evident when endophilin-dependent pathways are compromised. (yu2018endophilinaand pages 14-15, yu2018endophilinaand pages 1-2)
- **Post-Golgi/endosomal sorting compartments** serving polarized intestinal and ciliary membrane delivery. (sato2014c.elegansas pages 33-35, kaplan2010theap1clathrin pages 1-2)
- **Nascent and maturing phagosomes** around apoptotic germ cells, where CHC-1 associates with AP-2 and downstream engulfment machinery. (chen2013clathrinandap2 pages 3-4, chen2013clathrinandap2 pages 13-14)
- **Apical hyp7 epidermal puncta**: GFP-tagged CHC-1 and tagged CLIC-1 appeared as small, regularly dispersed structures at the apical epidermal surface in a 2024 preprint. [Milne et al., September 2024, DOI 10.1101/2024.09.15.613099](https://doi.org/10.1101/2024.09.15.613099). (milne2024tat1aphosphatidylserine pages 14-18)

Localization is therefore dynamic and compartment-dependent. “Cell membrane,” “Golgi,” or “endosome” alone would each be incomplete annotations.

## 4. Pathways and biological processes

### Synaptic-vesicle recycling

At the cholinergic neuromuscular junction, temperature-sensitive *chc-1* mutants accumulate numerous enlarged synaptic endosomes after stimulation. Electron microscopy and optogenetic experiments indicate that CHC-1 acts chiefly in budding new synaptic vesicles from synaptic endosomes rather than being obligatorily required for initial plasma-membrane retrieval under ordinary conditions. Endophilin A/UNC-57 and endophilin B/ERP-1 can compensate for clathrin loss at the plasma membrane; conversely, clathrin becomes more important there when endophilin function is compromised. The ultrastructural dataset included two animals, 9–13 synapses, and 67–88 profiles per condition, although the retrieved evidence did not provide exact CHC-1 vesicle counts. [Yu et al., June 2018, DOI 10.3389/fnmol.2018.00196](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnmol.2018.00196). (yu2018endophilinaand pages 14-15, yu2018endophilinaand pages 1-2)

This finding refines the broad statement that clathrin “recycles synaptic vesicles”: in this worm preparation its most clearly resolved site of action is the **synaptic endosome-to-vesicle step**, with context-dependent participation at the plasma membrane.

### Apoptotic-cell clearance

CHC-1 also has a noncanonical role in phagocytic receptor signaling. CHC-1 and AP-2 are recruited downstream of CED-1 and CED-6/Gulp to apoptotic germ-cell phagosomes. CED-6 associates with CHC-1/AP-2, and this complex promotes actin recruitment and rearrangement during engulfment. It subsequently facilitates recruitment of LST-4/Snx9-family protein and DYN-1/dynamin, phagosomal acidification, maturation, and corpse degradation. This is better interpreted as formation of a membrane-associated signaling/cytoskeletal hub than as simple production of a conventional small coated vesicle. (chen2013clathrinandap2 pages 1-2, chen2013clathrinandap2 pages 13-14)

Quantitatively, CHC-1 depletion left **56% of examined corpses incompletely engulfed**; most persisted for **more than 120 minutes**, compared with **29.3 ± 2.5 minutes** in controls. The fraction of CED-1-positive phagosomes labeled by actin fell from **77% to 35–40%**. These are among the most precise CHC-1-specific functional measurements available. [Chen et al., May 2013, DOI 10.1371/journal.pgen.1003517](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pgen.1003517). (chen2013clathrinandap2 pages 3-4)

### Epithelial polarity, tubulogenesis, and ciliary delivery

CHC-1/AP-1-dependent traffic is required to position apical components and form a normal intestinal lumen. In neurons, the same broad coat/adaptor logic selects membrane proteins for ciliary delivery. These roles illustrate a unifying principle: CHC-1 does not encode positional information itself; it converts adaptor-defined sorting information into physically segregated transport carriers. (sato2014c.elegansas pages 33-35, kaplan2010theap1clathrin pages 1-2)

## 5. Recent developments, 2023–2024

### Conserved disease-variant function—2023

The strongest recent CHC-1-centered study used CRISPR/Cas9 to introduce the worm p.P892L equivalent of recurrent human CLTC p.P890L. Homozygous animals were resistant to aldicarb and hypersensitive to pentylenetetrazole, indicating impaired acetylcholine and GABA release. They also showed synaptic-vesicle depletion, neurotransmitter accumulation at presynaptic membranes, slower locomotion, defective synaptic plasticity, and a mild dopamine-signaling defect. Heterozygous and overexpression experiments supported mild dominant-negative activity. The p.L1049P allele produced a more severe, near-null-like phenotype, giving an experimentally useful genotype–phenotype correlation. [Pannone et al., 31 May 2023, DOI 10.3389/fnmol.2023.1170061](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnmol.2023.1170061). (pannone2023therecurrentpathogenic pages 1-2, pannone2023therecurrentpathogenic pages 10-11)

Two independent p.P892L lines were established and each was outcrossed three times; editing injections used 20 animals for p.P892L and 60 for p.L1049P. The hypothesis that these substitutions alter triskelion assembly or coated-vesicle architecture is biologically plausible but was not directly demonstrated by structural analysis in the retrieved text. (pannone2023therecurrentpathogenic pages 10-11)

### Epidermal membrane organization—2024

A September 2024 preprint used endogenous fluorescent CHC-1 and CLIC-1 reporters to assess the consequences of disrupting the phosphatidylserine flippase TAT-1 in hyp7. In controls, CHC-1 formed small, regularly dispersed apical structures. This extends direct localization evidence into the epidermis and establishes CHC-1 patterning as an in-vivo membrane-trafficking readout. However, because the retrieved excerpt did not include quantitative mutant effects and the work was a preprint, it should be treated as emerging rather than definitive mechanistic evidence. [DOI 10.1101/2024.09.15.613099](https://doi.org/10.1101/2024.09.15.613099). (milne2024tat1aphosphatidylserine pages 14-18)

### State of the recent literature

The 2023–2024 literature contains several studies in which CHC-1 is used as an endocytic marker, sensitized allele, or pathway perturbation, but relatively few recent papers are devoted primarily to defining its core molecular mechanism. The most authoritative mechanistic conclusions still depend on foundational genetic, imaging, biochemical, and ultrastructural studies from 2005–2018. Recent work mainly extends CHC-1 into disease modeling and additional tissue-specific trafficking contexts.

## 6. Applications and real-world relevance

1. **Human CLTC disorder modeling.** Endogenous editing of orthologous residues provides an in-vivo system for testing pathogenicity, dominance, allele severity, synaptic mechanisms, and potentially therapeutic modifiers. The p.P892L and p.L1049P models recapitulate different severities of human CLTC-associated neurodevelopmental disease. (pannone2023therecurrentpathogenic pages 1-2, pannone2023therecurrentpathogenic pages 10-11)

2. **In-vivo membrane-trafficking assays.** Functional GFP/mRFP CHC-1 reporters permit visualization of coated structures in intact coelomocytes, phagosomes, neurons, intestine, and epidermis. Rescue of temperature-sensitive viability/endocytosis defects supports reporter functionality. (chen2013clathrinandap2 pages 3-4, sato2005caenorhabditiselegansrme6 pages 1-4)

3. **Synaptic-vesicle pathway dissection.** Temperature-sensitive *chc-1*, optogenetic stimulation, pharmacology, and electron microscopy distinguish plasma-membrane retrieval from endosomal vesicle reformation. This is useful for assigning where accessory proteins such as endophilins act in the vesicle cycle. (yu2018endophilinaand pages 14-15, yu2018endophilinaand pages 1-2)

4. **Cargo-sorting and polarity research.** CHC-1 perturbation is an established way to test whether localization of a receptor or membrane component requires clathrin/AP-1 or clathrin/AP-2 traffic, including ciliary and apical epithelial cargoes. (sato2014c.elegansas pages 33-35, kaplan2010theap1clathrin pages 1-2)

These are research implementations rather than clinical uses of worm CHC-1 itself.

## 7. Expert interpretation and annotation boundaries

The convergent expert view is that CHC-1 is an essential, conserved coat scaffold with context-specific adaptor partners. Its apparently diverse phenotypes—defective uptake, synaptic transmission, epithelial polarity, ciliary targeting, and corpse clearance—are unified by failure to organize membrane curvature, cargo segregation, or recruitment of accessory machinery. The most precise functional annotation is therefore **“clathrin heavy-chain scaffold for coated membrane carrier formation and membrane-associated trafficking complexes,”** not a generic label such as “signaling protein.” (sato2014c.elegansas pages 4-6, chen2013clathrinandap2 pages 13-14, yu2018endophilinaand pages 14-15)

Several boundaries are important:

- CHC-1 is **not an enzyme**, so no catalytic reaction or substrate specificity should be assigned.
- CHC-1 is **not a transporter**; it moves membrane and cargo indirectly by building coats.
- Cargo specificity belongs predominantly to adaptors such as AP-1 and AP-2 and to cargo receptors, although CHC-1 is indispensable for executing many of their sorting programs. (sato2014c.elegansas pages 4-6, kaplan2010theap1clathrin pages 1-2)
- Some phagosome functions are noncanonical and may involve scaffolding of actin/dynamin machinery rather than conventional coated-vesicle budding. (chen2013clathrinandap2 pages 13-14)
- Broad developmental or behavioral phenotypes should be interpreted as downstream consequences unless a trafficking intermediate has been directly demonstrated.

## Final functional annotation

**CHC-1/P34574 is the essential *C. elegans* clathrin heavy-chain coat scaffold.** It assembles with clathrin light chain into membrane-deforming coats and works through adaptor/accessory complexes to internalize plasma-membrane cargo, sort post-Golgi and endosomal cargo, regenerate synaptic vesicles from synaptic endosomes, target proteins to apical and ciliary membranes, and coordinate apoptotic-cell engulfment and phagosome maturation. Its localization is dynamic and includes coated pits, synaptic endosomes, post-Golgi/endosomal carriers, phagosomes, and apical epidermal puncta. The strongest direct evidence comes from RNAi and temperature-sensitive loss of function, rescuing fluorescent reporters, live-cell localization, electron microscopy, protein-interaction assays, and endogenous CRISPR disease modeling.
