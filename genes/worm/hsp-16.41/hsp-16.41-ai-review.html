Gene Description

HSP-16.41 is a heat-inducible small heat shock protein (sHSP) of the alpha-crystallin/HSP20 family, encoded by the single-copy gene Y46H3A.2 on chromosome V, which is transcribed divergently from hsp-16.2 off a shared intergenic promoter containing heat shock elements. It belongs to the same sHSP subclass as HSP-16.48. HSP-16.41 is not expressed under normal conditions and is strongly induced by heat in an HSF-1-dependent manner, with the hsp-16.2/hsp-16.41 promoter acting preferentially in intestine and pharynx; unlike hsp-16.2 it is not hypoxia-responsive. By homology with HSP-16.2, it functions as an ATP-independent holdase chaperone that binds unfolding proteins to prevent their aggregation. Its pharyngeal expression during Salmonella infection depends on the Toll-like receptor TOL-1.

Proposed New Ontology Terms

holdase chaperone activity

Definition: Binding to an unfolded or misfolded protein to prevent its aggregation without actively catalyzing refolding. The holdase maintains the client protein in a soluble, folding-competent state. This is mechanistically distinct from foldase activity (GO:0044183) and from carrier-holdase activity (GO:0140309).

Justification: hsp-16.41: an alpha-crystallin-domain small HSP of the C. elegans HSP-16 family; the paralogue HSP-16.2 suppresses thermal aggregation of citrate synthase ATP-independently. Obsolete GO:0051082 captured binding only; GO:0044183 requires assisting folding, and GO:0140309 (relabelled 'unfolded protein holdase activity') keeps a carrier-specific definition requiring escort to an acceptor molecule or location, which is not demonstrated here. See go-ontology#30552.

Suggested Questions for Experts

Q: Do HSP-16.1/16.2 (one subclass) and HSP-16.41/16.48 (the other subclass) form mixed oligomers in vivo, and do they differ in client range?

Q: Does the HSP-16 family hand clients to the inducible HSP70s (HSP-70, F44E5.4/5) for refolding, or primarily route them to sequestration/degradation?

Suggested Experiments

Experiment: Purify recombinant HSP-16.41 and test ATP-independent suppression of thermal aggregation of model clients (citrate synthase, luciferase), alone and with HSP-70 + DNJ-13 to measure hand-off refolding.

Hypothesis: HSP-16.41 is an ATP-independent holdase whose bound clients can be refolded by the worm HSP70/HSP40 system.
