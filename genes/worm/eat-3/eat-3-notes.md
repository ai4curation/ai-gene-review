## 2026-09-20 full annotation re-review

All 25 source rows and the old NEW proposal were assessed, together with the full primary studies PMID:18454199, PMID:18722182 and PMID:21248201. The Falcon report was read critically; its genetic-rescue description overstates measured GTPase restoration, and length-unit statements must be checked against the primary text. All source fields were preserved. The redundant old NEW GO:1990627 proposal was withdrawn because existing GO:0008053 annotations cover fusion; precise inner-membrane fusion remains in core_functions.

PTHR11566 IBDs are PTN000170013 for GTPase, cytoplasm and microtubule location/binding; PTN007514526 for mitochondrial fusion, mitochondrial membrane and IMS; and PTN008520527 for peroxisome fission. Worm fusion evidence is a legitimate descendant source for the IBD. Cytoplasm is restored to ACCEPT because mitochondria are cytoplasmic organelles; it does not mean free cytosol. Principal mitochondrial targeting and protected topology pose a real mechanistic question for microtubule/peroxisome access, but do not by themselves demonstrate loss across all precursor or processed contexts. Those three rows are UNDECIDED. No existing repository/global OpenScientist report matched eat-3. Exact neutral questions were sent to root; no independent report was launched.

The apoptosis rejection has direct experimental support. PMID:18454199 finds no increase in embryonic dying cells and no rescue of growth/brood-size defects by ced-3 or ced-4 loss. PMID:18722182 additionally assays developmental corpse profiles, inappropriate pharyngeal survival, and a sensitized ced-3 background: “eat-3(ad426); ced-3(n2438) animals did not reveal a significant decrease or increase in the number of extra cells compared with ced-3(n2438) animals”. This argues against transferring the canonical OPA1 death role; it does not prove absence of all effects in every untested stress condition.

PMID:21248201 directly uses EAT-3 as an inner-membrane/IMS marker despite foregrounding MOMA-1. It reports “88% of EAT-3, 12% of tubulin, and 98% of MOMA-1 is in the mitochondrial pellet” and protease protection in intact mitochondria. Exact quotes were repaired, including removal of invented ellipses and the correct F1β character. The cristae decrease in PMID:18454199 is 66.2% after normalization to inner-boundary length, not the unnormalized 1.21/7.34-µm ratio. Its intragenic suppressors restore biological function, with hydrolytic-cycle rescue proposed rather than directly measured.

The old lipid-binding support PMID:23226476 resolves to hCG-driven reproductive dysfunction and tumorigenesis in mice and is unrelated. This added reviewer citation was removed without changing the GOA source. Conserved family lipid interaction is instead supported by the full yeast Mgm1 lipid-binding/mutagenesis/complementation paper PMID:19703904 and human OPA1-cardiolipin reconstitution abstract PMID:28628083. Neither is presented as a direct EAT-3 biochemical assay. Growth/development and paraquat-response annotations remain non-core phenotypic process roles; fusion and membrane/cristae organization are retained as direct structural functions, without inventing respiratory-chain catalysis.

## Focused OpenScientist report incorporation (2026-09-20)

Read the full microtubule/peroxisome report. Its ancestry result places MT-binding PTN000170013 at the family root and peroxisome-fission PTN008520527 above OPA1 PTN007514526. Thus the latter is not an exclusively DRP1 clade from which a pairwise transfer crossed into OPA1: the PAINT curator asserted an ancestral capacity inherited by both. The report's exact caveat is: “this reasoning does not *prove* that EAT-3 can never, under any engineered or stress condition, contact a microtubule or a peroxisome.” The deposited artifacts are final HTML/PDF only; no executable domain/tree analysis was supplied.

IMS topology and the difference from conventional dynamin engagement modules are substantive mechanistic concerns. But missing PH/PRD/GED database calls do not establish a universally required binding apparatus, and an OPA1-specific stalk/assembly mechanism cannot be declared absent merely from conventional-domain labels. The report supplies no direct negative EAT-3 binding/fission assay and treats experimental-support asymmetry as decisive despite the IBD inheritance model. Keep microtubule location/binding and peroxisome fission UNDECIDED; mark the report DISPUTED and retain human follow-up. Fusion, cristae and protease-protected localization remain supported by the previously read worm primaries. Worm-specific negative apoptosis evidence is not a claim about other OPA1 orthologs. Shared these distinctions with the DANRE/opa1 reviewer; no duplicate request.

Independent OPA1 reviewer located full PMID:32228866; checked its Introduction, Figure 1A and Results/Figure 3. Exact excerpt: “a middle domain, and a C-terminal GTPase effector domain (GED).” The Results fit a middle/GED stalk into cryo-EM density. This directly contradicts the focused report's claim that the OPA1 family lacks GED; it is human ortholog structural evidence, not a worm microtubule/peroxisome assay. Added this distinction to the reviews and report assessment.
