MEC-14 (Q20127, C. elegans): AKR active site, cofactor site and subfamily placement

Hypothesis slug: kgap-mec14-akr-site-and-cofactor Source: genes/worm/mec-14/mec-14-ai-review.yaml, focus type free_text Target: UniProt Q20127, WormBase gene mec-14, NCBITaxon:6239 Iterations completed: 3. Findings recorded: 7. Papers retrieved: 11.

Every computational result below was produced in this run from public sequence and structure data. The executed code and its outputs are in provenance/, listed at the end. Local *-bioinformatics files in the repository were withheld from this run and were not used.

Summary

MEC-14 is not predicted to be a catalytically competent aldo-keto reductase, and it is not a Kv channel beta subunit homolog. Three of the four AKR catalytic tetrad positions are present and geometrically assembled in the AlphaFold model: Asp132, Tyr137 and Lys163 form the proton relay with side-chain tip distances of 2.70 Å (Asp-Lys) and 2.90 Å (Lys-Tyr), within 0.3 Å of the 2.66 and 3.11 Å measured in the 1US0 crystal structure of human aldose reductase. The fourth position is Gln204. Its amide sits 5.08 Å from the Tyr137 hydroxyl, too far for the hydrogen bond that tunes the catalytic tyrosine pKa in active AKRs (3.70 Å for AKR1B1 His110). That distance is within 0.24 Å of the equivalent broken contact in human Kvβ2, where Asn158 replaces the His (5.32 Å).

The cofactor pocket is open but its specificity is reversed. Transferring NADP+ from 1US0 and NADPH from 1QRQ into the MEC-14 frame by Kabsch superposition places the dinucleotide in a cleft lined by 25 and 22 MEC-14 residues within 4.0 Å, with only 3 and 1 contacts below 2.0 Å. At the 2'-phosphate subsite that makes AKR1B1 NADPH-specific (Lys262, Ser263, Arg268 in UniProt numbering), MEC-14 has Asp354, Ser355 and Gln358: both basic residues are gone and one is replaced by a charge-reversed aspartate. The same substitution pattern appears in NAD+-dependent L-galactose dehydrogenase (Gly270, Met271, Gln276) and in Kvβ2 (Gly323, Ala324, Gln329). If MEC-14 binds a cofactor, NAD(H) is the better prediction than NADPH.

The subfamily placement in the seed hypothesis is wrong in a specific way. Across a 41-protein AKR panel aligned over InterPro-defined PF00248 boundaries, MEC-14's closest characterised relative is Arabidopsis L-galactose dehydrogenase (O81884, EC 1.1.1.316) at 42.4% identity, 10.9 percentage points ahead of Drosophila Hyperkinetic (31.5%) and well ahead of human KCNAB2 (27.1%), KCNAB1 (26.7%) and KCNAB3 (26.3%). That margin is larger than the entire spread among the four AKR6 members. InterPro and CDD agree independently: Q20127 returns cd19163 (L-galactose dehydrogenase-like) and does not return cd19141 (Kvβ/AKR6A), IPR005983 or IPR005399. The substrate pocket matches nothing characterised: of 15 AKR1B1 residues contacting the IDD594 inhibitor in 1US0, 11 have a MEC-14 counterpart within 3 Å and only 2 keep the residue type, the aromatic lining falls from 8/15 to 3/11, and the entire C-terminal substrate-specificity loop (Cys298 to Tyr309) is absent.

Verdict: partially supported. The seed hypothesis is correct that the question can be addressed structurally and correct that the tetrad and cofactor site need testing. Its implicit framings, that MEC-14 might be a working AKR and that it resembles a Kvβ subunit, are both contradicted. The curation consequence is that AKR-derived molecular function terms and any potassium-channel-regulator term on mec-14 should be treated as over-annotations.

Executive judgment

Partially supported, with the catalytic claim refuted and the subfamily claim corrected.

The seed hypothesis bundles three separable questions. They resolve differently.

1. Is the AKR catalytic tetrad assembled? No, not completely. Three positions are present in near-crystal geometry. The fourth is a glutamine whose side chain cannot do what the canonical histidine does: it cannot act as a general acid-base partner for the catalytic tyrosine, and in the model it sits 1.4 Å too far away to hydrogen bond at all. MEC-14 is not predicted to be a catalytically competent aldo-keto reductase.

2. Is the NADPH-binding site assembled? Not as NADPH-specific. The nicotinamide half of the cleft is open and could in principle accept a dinucleotide. The 2'-phosphate clamp is absent and charge-reversed. The prediction is NAD(H) preference, shared with the NAD+-dependent L-GalDH branch.

3. Which subfamily does it resemble? L-galactose dehydrogenase-like, not AKR6. 42.4% identity over PF00248, a 10.9-point margin over the best Kvβ, corroborated by CDD model assignment. The widespread description of MEC-14 as a Kv channel beta subunit homolog is not supported by sequence distance.

Most important caveats

Every structural statement rests on an AlphaFold model. There is no experimental structure for Q20127. The backbone register is reliable, because it was fixed by superposition at 1.01 Å RMSD over 228 Cα pairs, and the local model confidence is high (PF00248 domain mean pLDDT 91.8, 81% of residues above 90; pLDDT 97.6 at Asp132, 89.8 at Tyr137, 96.2 at Lys163, 91.1 at Gln204). The specific 5.08 Å Tyr137-Gln204 distance depends on a predicted rotamer and should be read as "the canonical Tyr-to-fourth-residue hydrogen bond is not built in the model, and a Gln cannot substitute chemically for the His in any case", rather than as a measured distance in a real protein.

No biochemical assay of MEC-14 exists in the retrieved literature. PubMed searches across all three iterations returned exactly one paper on mec-14 itself (PMID: 8692859), which is a genetic interaction study with no protein biochemistry. Every molecular claim here is structural inference.

Pocket "openness" was assessed by transferring a cofactor from a holo crystal structure into a predicted apo model. This method cannot evaluate induced fit and gives no evidence that binding actually occurs.

The Penning AKR superfamily database was not accessible programmatically in this run and was not checked. Whether MEC-14 appears there as a pseudo-member is unknown.

Key findings

F1. The Asp-Lys-Tyr proton relay is present and assembled

The Pfam PF00248 domain of Q20127 spans residues 97 to 373 according to InterPro. Two independent methods place MEC-14 Asp132, Tyr137 and Lys163 at the AKR1B1 Asp43, Tyr48 and Lys77 positions: a PF00248-trimmed BLOSUM62 affine-gap global alignment, and a Kabsch superposition of the AlphaFold model of Q20127 onto 1US0 chain A (107 Cα pairs, RMSD 1.10 Å) and onto 1QRQ chain A (133 Cα pairs, RMSD 1.18 Å).

The geometry matches active enzymes. Measured side-chain tip distances in the AlphaFold model are 2.70 Å for Asp132-Lys163 and 2.90 Å for Lys163-Tyr137, against 2.72 and 3.01 Å in the AlphaFold model of AKR1B1, 2.67 and 3.05 Å in the AlphaFold model of L-galactose dehydrogenase, and 2.66 and 3.11 Å in the 1US0 crystal structure itself. The maximum deviation from the crystal reference is 0.21 Å.

Residue numbering was corrected during the run. The catalytic aspartate is Asp132, in the motif Ile131-Asp132-Thr133-Gly134-Tyr135-Trp136-Tyr137. Asp132 and Tyr137 are five residues apart, which is the canonical AKR D-x-x-x-Y spacing (AKR1B1 Asp43 and Tyr48). An earlier working number of Asp133 was off by one and does not appear in any conclusion here.

Across 20 nematode orthologs in UniRef50_Q20127, Asp132 is 20/20 identical, Tyr137 is 20/20, and Lys163 is 17/20 (serine in Haemonchus placei, arginine in Mesorhabditis belari). This part of the seed hypothesis, that the tetrad might be disassembled, is not supported for these three positions.

F2. The fourth tetrad position is Gln204, and the register question is settled

This was the single largest uncertainty after iteration 1 and it was resolved in iteration 2 by a method that does not depend on any sequence alignment.

The problem: direct alignment of MEC-14 to AKR1B1 (23.2% identity) put Gln204 at the His110 position, while alignment routed through the much closer homolog L-galactose dehydrogenase (42.4% identity) put His206 there instead, reflecting a 2-residue insertion in the MEC-14 β5 loop. His206 was an attractive candidate: it is 19/20 conserved in nematodes and it sits in a His-Asp dipeptide (His206-Asp207) shared with L-GalDH (His124-Asp125), while the Kvβ proteins have asparagine at this position.

The resolution: the AlphaFold model of MEC-14 was superposed directly onto the AlphaFold model of L-galactose dehydrogenase, converging on 228 Cα pairs at 1.01 Å RMSD. The residue whose position coincides with L-GalDH His124 is Gln204, at Cα-Cα 1.04 Å and side-chain tip to tip 0.93 Å, with pLDDT 91. The next-closest Cα belongs to Val203 at 4.12 Å. His206 sits 5.76 Å away with pLDDT 70. The register is fixed against His206.

The glutamine is a fixed feature of the nematode family across Caenorhabditis, Pristionchus, Haemonchus placei, Steinernema carpocapsae and Mesorhabditis belari, not a C. elegans-specific loss.

F3. The cofactor cleft is open but the NADPH 2'-phosphate clamp is charge-reversed

Contacts were computed directly from the crystal structures: 32 residues within 4.0 Å of NADP+ in 1US0 chain A, and 32 within 4.0 Å of NADPH in 1QRQ chain A. Transferring each cofactor into the MEC-14 frame by superposition lands it in an open cleft: 25 MEC-14 residues within 4.0 Å from the 1US0 frame and 22 from the 1QRQ frame, with only 3 and 1 contacts below 2.0 Å. A protein with a collapsed or occluded pocket would show many more severe clashes.

The cleft is lined by the glycine-rich β1 loop Gly101-Ala102-Ala103 (equivalent to AKR1B1 Gly19-Thr20-Trp21), the catalytic residues Asp132, Tyr137, Lys163, Gln204 and His206, then Thr240, Met262, Tyr264, Ser291-Gly292-Ala293-Leu294, Leu350-Val351-Gly352-Met353-Asp354 and Gln358.

The specificity determinants tell a different story from the pocket shape.

Both basic residues that clamp the 2'-phosphate of NADPH in AKR1B1 are lost in MEC-14, and one is replaced by a charge-reversed aspartate. Sequence alignment and the independent structural transfer agree on Asp354 and Gln358. The identical loss is present in NAD+-dependent L-GalDH and in Kvβ2, so this feature does not discriminate between those two, but it excludes the NADPH-specific AKR1 architecture.

Asp354 is conserved in 19/20 nematode orthologs. Gln358 is glutamine in 15/20 and arginine or histidine in 5/20 (Pristionchus spp. and S. carpocapsae), which would partly restore a basic 2'-phosphate contact in those species.

The practical consequence for curation: any statement that MEC-14 binds NADPH specifically is not supported. NAD(H) is the better structural prediction, and neither has been tested.

F4. The substrate pocket matches no characterised AKR

The AKR1B1 substrate pocket was defined empirically, not from annotation: the 15 residues within 4.5 Å of the IDD594 inhibitor (HET code LDT) in chain A of 1US0. They are Trp20, Val47, Tyr48, Trp79, His110, Trp111, Thr113, Phe115, Phe122, Trp219, Cys298, Ala299, Leu300, Cys303 and Tyr309.

Transferring them onto MEC-14 by both the PF00248-trimmed alignment and the 107-Cα superposition gives three results that point the same way.

Only 11 of 15 positions have a MEC-14 counterpart within 3.0 Å. Of those 11, only 2 keep the same residue type: Tyr137 at the Tyr48 site and Trp136 at the Val47 site. The aromatic lining falls from 8 of 15 residues in AKR1B1 (Trp20, Tyr48, Trp79, Trp111, Phe115, Phe122, Trp219, Tyr309) to 3 of the 11 matched positions in MEC-14. The aromatic cage that defines the AKR1B1 anion-binding site is gone.

The entire C-terminal substrate-specificity loop of AKR1B1 is absent. Cys298, Ala299, Leu300, Cys303 and Tyr309 all fall in alignment gaps. The nearest MEC-14 Cα after superposition is 5.99 Å (Cys298 to Tyr242), 4.37 Å (Ala299 to Glu396), 1.88 Å (Leu300 to Asp399), 2.21 Å (Cys303 to Ala208) and 3.49 Å (Tyr309 to Phe210): a scattered set with no loop-like correspondence. The two closest matches, Glu396 and Asp399, lie in the C-terminal extension outside PF00248 and are not the structural equivalent of the AKR safety belt.

Mechanistic model and interpretation

The three analyses combine into one coherent picture. MEC-14 is a nematode-specific protein built on an AKR (α/β)8 barrel that has kept the structural scaffold and part of the catalytic apparatus while losing the elements required to turn over a carbonyl substrate.

The alternative the data point toward

The 176 residues of terminal extension, not the barrel, are the distinctive feature of MEC-14 relative to every characterised AKR tested. Both extensions are predicted low-complexity. A scaffolding or protein-interaction role mediated by the extensions, with the AKR barrel acting as a folded platform, is consistent with everything measured here. That is a hypothesis, not a finding: no interaction data for MEC-14 were retrieved in this run.

Why this is probably not simple pseudogene decay

A degenerating pseudo-enzyme predicts drifting active-site residues. The ortholog data argue against drift. Asp132 and Tyr137 are invariant across 19 orthologs spanning Caenorhabditis, Pristionchus, Haemonchus, Steinernema and Mesorhabditis. Gln204 is fixed at 94.7% with no histidine reversion anywhere. The pocket residues Trp136 and Trp307 are 100% conserved and Arg166 and Phe175 are 94.7%. Selection is maintaining a specific non-canonical site across a long evolutionary span. That argues the protein does something, while saying nothing about what.

Mechanistic scope

What was directly tested

The immediate molecular question is whether the MEC-14 polypeptide can perform NAD(P)H-dependent carbonyl reduction. That reduces to three physical requirements, each tested here against cofactor-bound crystal structures.

Proton relay. Asp-Lys-Tyr must be hydrogen bonded so the lysine lowers the tyrosine pKa and the tyrosine can act as the proton donor. Present in MEC-14 at 2.70 and 2.90 Å.

Fourth position. A histidine must sit across the pocket to orient and polarise the substrate carbonyl and complete the pKa tuning. Absent. Gln204 occupies the site, 5.08 Å from Tyr137, and a glutamine amide cannot perform the general acid-base chemistry of the imidazole regardless of distance.

Cofactor clamp. The 2'-phosphate of NADPH must be held by basic residues. Absent and charge-reversed (Asp354, Gln358).

What is downstream and must not be conflated

The mec-14 touch-insensitivity phenotype (PMID: 8692859) is a whole-animal behavioural readout. That paper reports mec-14 among at least 13 genes needed for the gentle-touch response, and it states that the dominant enhancement and suppression patterns "suggest that the products of several touch genes interact" and that "almost all of the touch function genes contribute to the mechanosensory apparatus". This establishes that the gene is required for touch receptor neuron function and suggests the product participates in a protein assembly. It says nothing about whether the MEC-14 protein catalyses a reaction or binds a cofactor. Treating that phenotype as support for an AKR molecular function would be a category error, and the structural data argue in the opposite direction.

What the protein might actually be

MEC-14 carries 176 residues of terminal extension outside PF00248, 39% of the protein, the largest combined extension in the 8-protein panel. A scaffolding or protein-interaction role mediated by those extensions, with the barrel as a folded platform, is consistent with the genetic interaction data in PMID: 8692859 and with everything measured structurally. It has not been tested.

Conflicts and alternatives

Conflict 1: the Kvβ framing in the literature and databases. MEC-14 is widely described as related to Kv channel beta subunits, which is attractive because mec-14 acts in a sensory neuron and Kvβ regulates channels. The distance data do not support it: AKR6 members reach only 26.3 to 31.5% identity, below L-GalDH's 42.4%, and CDD assigns cd19163 rather than cd19141. The resemblance that does hold is narrow and specific: MEC-14 and Kvβ2 share a non-histidine fourth position with the same broken tyrosine contact (5.08 versus 5.32 Å). Shared losses are weak phylogenetic evidence because they arise independently.

Conflict 2: the Gln204 versus His206 register. His206 is 94.7% conserved across nematode orthologs and sits in a His-Asp dipeptide matching L-GalDH His124-Asp125, which made it a strong candidate for a catalytic histidine displaced by the 2-residue β5 insertion. Direct structural superposition settles it: His206 is 5.76 Å from the L-GalDH His124 position with pLDDT 70, while Gln204 is 0.93 Å away with pLDDT 91. The alternative is rejected, but the His206 conservation remains unexplained and may indicate a function unrelated to AKR catalysis.

Conflict 3: open pocket versus broken catalysis. The cofactor cleft is open, with only 3 severe clashes from a transferred NADP+. A protein that had lost all cofactor function would be expected to collapse or fill that pocket. The simplest reconciliation is that MEC-14 still binds a dinucleotide, probably NAD(H), without catalysing a conventional AKR reaction, which is the arrangement in Kvβ2. This is a prediction from an apo model and is the weakest claim in the report.

Conflict 4: degeneration versus maintained design. Covered in the mechanistic model above. Invariant Asp132 and Tyr137, fixed Gln204 and 100% conserved Trp136 and Trp307 across 19 orthologs argue for maintained selection on a non-canonical site. This is a reason to be cautious about declaring the protein non-functional, as opposed to non-AKR.

Possible database carry-over. Q20127 is named "NADP-dependent oxidoreductase domain-containing protein" in UniProt. That name is derived from the domain assignment, and this analysis argues the "NADP-dependent" element of it is wrong.

Paralog confusion checked and excluded. The nearest C. elegans AKR paralog, K07C5.2 (Q21274), is 30.9% identical to MEC-14 over PF00248, well below the L-GalDH match. The 13 nematode AKRs in the panel do not cluster with MEC-14 apart from the genuine orthologs from H. placei (67.4%) and P. pacificus (54.0%). An earlier error in this run, using Q24210 for Drosophila Hyperkinetic when that accession is CASK, was identified and corrected to Q9W2W9 before any conclusion was drawn.

Methodological limitations

Alignments used a custom BLOSUM62 affine-gap Needleman-Wunsch implementation over InterPro PF00248 boundaries, not a profile HMM. Profile methods would be more sensitive in the 20 to 30% identity range where most of the panel sits, and could change the ordering among the lower-ranked proteins. They would not plausibly close a 10.9-point gap.

Percent identity is used as the phylogenetic signal, supported by a neighbour-joining tree on Kimura-corrected distances. A maximum-likelihood tree with bootstrap support would be stronger evidence for the L-GalDH grouping as a clade, as opposed to a distance statistic.

The Arabidopsis L-galactose dehydrogenase literature retrieved in this run (PMID: 33793952, PMID: 25938231, PMID: 25724639 and others) covers the plant ascorbate pathway and the enzyme's role in it. None of it bears on MEC-14, and none of it is used as evidence for any claim here. It is listed in the evidence base below for completeness and to document that the L-GalDH connection was searched and found to have no nematode-relevant literature.

Evidence base

Primary and review literature used

PMID: 8692859 Genetic interactions affecting touch sensitivity in Caenorhabditis elegans. The only paper on mec-14 itself retrieved in this run. It reports that at least 13 genes including mec-14 are needed for the gentle-touch response of the 6 touch receptor neurons, and that dominant enhancement and suppression patterns "suggest that the products of several touch genes interact" and that "almost all of the touch function genes contribute to the mechanosensory apparatus". How it bears on the findings: it establishes the BP annotation and supports the scaffolding alternative, while providing no biochemistry. It is the competing account of MEC-14's function, not support for an AKR molecular function.

PMID: 12604248 The aldo-keto reductase superfamily homepage. Provides the definition that was tested: "Each structure has the characteristic (alpha/beta)(8)-barrel motif of the superfamily, a conserved cofactor binding site and a catalytic tetrad, and variable loop structures that define substrate specificity." It also establishes the comparison group: "Other families of interest include AKR6, which includes potassium channel beta-subunits, and AKR7 the aflatoxin aldehyde reductases." How it bears on the findings: it specifies the three features measured here (barrel, cofactor site, tetrad) plus the variable loops, which is the element MEC-14 most clearly lacks. It names AKR6 as the Kvβ family used in the distance analysis.

PMID: 38878851 Aldo-keto reductase (AKR) superfamily website and database: An update. States the membership criterion directly: "A criteria of family membership is that the protein must have a measured function, and thus, genomic sequences suggesting the transcription of potential AKR proteins are considered pseudo-members until evidence of a functionally expressed protein is available." How it bears on the findings: this is the principle that makes an AKR-derived molecular function term on MEC-14 inappropriate. MEC-14 has no measured function and would be a pseudo-member by this definition. The paper also notes the database contains "genetic, functional, and structural data drawn from various sources", which is the resource that could not be queried programmatically here.

Proposed follow-up experiments and actions

Ranked by how efficiently each separates the live hypotheses: active AKR, inactive cofactor-binding Kvβ-like protein, or non-cofactor scaffold.

1. Cofactor binding by DSF or ITC. Test recombinant MEC-14 PF00248 domain (residues 97-373) and full-length protein against NAD+, NADH, NADP+ and NADPH. A thermal shift with NAD(H) but not NADP(H) confirms the charge-reversal prediction in a single experiment. A null result across all four removes the cofactor annotation question entirely. Highest information per unit effort.

2. Gln204His and Gln204Asn point mutants assayed for carbonyl reductase activity. If Gln204His restores measurable activity, the glutamine is the single catalytic block and MEC-14 is a pseudo-enzyme by one substitution. If it does not, the pocket loss documented in F4 is the dominant defect, which would mean the protein diverged further than a single-residue reversion can undo.

3. In vivo structure-function rescue. Test whether mec-14(Q204H), mec-14(D132N) and mec-14(Y137F) transgenes rescue touch sensitivity in a mec-14 null background. If catalytic-site mutants rescue normally, the AKR site is irrelevant to the touch function and the GO molecular function question becomes moot for the core role. This is the single most decisive experiment for curation.

4. Interaction proteomics from touch receptor neurons using a tagged MEC-14 knock-in. Tests the scaffold alternative and tests the Kvβ functional analogy (does MEC-14 bind MEC-4 or a potassium channel subunit) without relying on sequence distance. PMID: 8692859 already provides genetic evidence that touch gene products interact, which makes the candidate list short.

5. Deletion of the terminal extensions. Express MEC-14 lacking residues 1-96 and 374-453 and test rescue. Separates "the barrel is the functional unit" from "the extensions are the functional unit". Given that the extensions are 39% of the protein and the most distinctive feature relative to every characterised AKR, this is a direct test of the main alternative hypothesis.

6. Crystal or cryo-EM structure with a dinucleotide soak. Replaces every model-dependent number in this report with a measurement, including the Tyr137-Gln204 geometry that currently rests on a predicted rotamer. Slowest, but the only definitive route.
