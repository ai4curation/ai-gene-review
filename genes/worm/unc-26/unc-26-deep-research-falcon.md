# Functional annotation report: *C. elegans unc-26* / UNC-26 synaptojanin

## Executive conclusion

The target identity is verified. In *Caenorhabditis elegans*, **unc-26 encodes the worm synaptojanin**, a cytosolic, membrane-associated polyphosphoinositide phosphatase required principally at presynaptic endocytic membranes. The original cloning study found mutations in the synaptojanin ORF in all 13 *unc-26* alleles examined and reported the predicted protein to be 43% identical to rat synaptojanin. This independently confirms the biological identity supplied for UniProt **G5ECL2** and ORF **JC8.10**, although those database identifiers were not printed in the retrieved primary-paper excerpts. No literature on a different gene with the same symbol was used. (harris2000mutationsinsynaptojanin pages 2-3, harris2000mutationsinsynaptojanin pages 3-5)

Its best-supported primary function is the hydrolysis of the 5-phosphate of phosphatidylinositol 4,5-bisphosphate:

**PI(4,5)P₂ + H₂O → PI(4)P + inorganic phosphate.**

This reaction changes the identity and charge of the cytosolic membrane surface, enabling endocytic proteins to disengage and synaptic-vesicle membranes to proceed through budding, post-fission uncoating, endosomal processing, and reuse. Direct worm domain-rescue experiments establish that the central 5-phosphatase activity is indispensable at synapses; a catalytic D716A substitution abolished rescue of evoked synaptic currents and locomotion. (dong2015synaptojanincooperatesin pages 8-10)

## 1. Identity and domain architecture

UNC-26 has the canonical synaptojanin organization:

1. **N-terminal Sac1 domain**—approximately residues 1–493 in the experimentally studied protein; corresponds to the supplied SAC/Endo-exonuclease-phosphatase-superfamily annotations.
2. **Central inositol-polyphosphate 5-phosphatase domain**—corresponds to IP5/IPPc and carries the synaptically essential PI(4,5)P₂ 5-phosphatase activity.
3. **C-terminal proline-rich domain (PRD)**—binds SH3-domain endocytic proteins such as endophilin and helps enrich UNC-26 at synapses, although it is not absolutely required under the tested experimental conditions. (dong2015synaptojanincooperatesin pages 8-10, harris2000mutationsinsynaptojanin pages 3-5, dong2015synaptojanincooperatesin pages 1-2)

The nucleotide-binding α/β-plait-superfamily annotation supplied by InterPro is compatible with this larger conserved catalytic architecture, but the retrieved functional studies did not assign it a separate biological activity. Thus, the experimentally defensible annotation is **tandem phosphoinositide-phosphatase enzyme with a protein-interaction-rich C terminus**, not a nucleotide-metabolizing enzyme.

## 2. Catalytic activity and substrate specificity

### Principal physiological substrate

The strongest direct in-vivo evidence identifies **PI(4,5)P₂** as UNC-26’s critical presynaptic substrate. Its 5-phosphatase domain removes the D5 phosphate to produce PI(4)P. PI(4,5)P₂ recruits or stabilizes numerous endocytic factors at the plasma membrane; its timely disappearance after vesicle fission is therefore an effective biochemical switch for coat and adaptor release. (dong2015synaptojanincooperatesin pages 8-10, dong2015synaptojanincooperatesin pages 14-16, harris2000mutationsinsynaptojanin pages 2-3)

The decisive experiment was domain-specific catalysis: **D716A**, which disables the 5-phosphatase, completely eliminated functional rescue, whereas Sac1 catalytic substitutions **C378S/D380N** had little effect on locomotion and synaptic physiology. The physical Sac1 domain nevertheless remained essential, showing that catalytic and targeting functions are separable. (dong2015synaptojanincooperatesin pages 8-10)

### Broader substrate range

Conserved synaptojanin enzymology assigns the Sac1 domain activity toward PI(3)P, PI(4)P, and PI(3,5)P₂ and assigns the central 5-phosphatase activity toward PI(4,5)P₂ and PI(3,4,5)P₃. However, this complete substrate panel was not established by kinetic assays using purified *C. elegans* G5ECL2 in the retrieved studies. It should therefore be recorded as **family-supported biochemical inference**, whereas PI(4,5)P₂ turnover and the requirement for the 5-phosphatase are supported directly by worm genetics and physiology. (harris2000mutationsinsynaptojanin pages 2-3)

The alternative UniProt name “synaptic inositol 1,4,5-trisphosphate 5-phosphatase” can be misleading if interpreted as evidence that soluble Ins(1,4,5)P₃ is the dominant physiological substrate. The worm literature instead places the principal function on **membrane phosphoinositides**, especially PI(4,5)P₂, during synaptic-vesicle recycling.

## 3. Cellular and subcellular localization

UNC-26 acts predominantly at **presynaptic terminals**, on the cytosolic surface of the plasma membrane, clathrin-coated endocytic intermediates, newly internalized vesicles, and synaptic endosomes. It is a peripheral membrane enzyme rather than an integral membrane protein. Synaptic enrichment positions its phosphatase activity next to the PI(4,5)P₂-rich endocytic membrane on which it must act. (harris2000mutationsinsynaptojanin pages 1-2, dong2015synaptojanincooperatesin pages 14-16, schuske2003endophilinisrequired pages 2-3)

The Sac1 domain has an important noncatalytic targeting function. Removing residues 1–493 severely compromised rescue, although catalytic inactivation of Sac1 did not. Physically reconnecting Sac1 to the rest of UNC-26 using split inteins restored substantial function, while unlinked fragments did not. Moreover, tethering the essential 5-phosphatase to endophilin’s membrane-binding BAR domain bypassed the requirement for Sac1. Collectively, these results support a model in which Sac1 helps position the 5-phosphatase at curved endocytic membranes rather than contributing the indispensable synaptic catalytic reaction itself. (dong2015synaptojanincooperatesin pages 8-10, dong2015synaptojanincooperatesin pages 14-16)

UNC-26 is not exclusively neuronal. Coated vesicles accumulated in mutant muscle, hypodermis, neuronal cell bodies, and near Golgi stacks, indicating broader membrane-trafficking functions. Nevertheless, the strongest defects occurred at synaptic termini, and relatively preserved body form, brood size, and growth argued against a nonspecific collapse of all cellular trafficking. (harris2000mutationsinsynaptojanin pages 7-9, harris2000mutationsinsynaptojanin pages 9-10)

## 4. Core pathway: synaptic-vesicle endocytosis and recycling

Loss of *unc-26* causes synaptic-vesicle depletion and accumulation of plasma-membrane pits, clathrin-coated vesicles, and endosome-like structures. Serial-section electron microscopy examined 433 sections from four mutant animals and 328 sections from two wild-type animals. The mutant phenotype implicated UNC-26 at several linked stages rather than at one isolated step: recruitment and progression of endocytic machinery, fission or resolution of deeply invaginated pits, post-fission uncoating, production of new vesicles from endosomes, and organization or tethering of reserve-pool vesicles. (harris2000mutationsinsynaptojanin pages 2-3, harris2000mutationsinsynaptojanin pages 1-2, harris2000mutationsinsynaptojanin pages 7-9)

Mutant synapses also contained membrane-bound endosome-like structures at least 50 nm across and abnormal linear “string-of-pearls” arrays of vesicles, in contrast to the normal concentration of vesicles around active zones. These observations suggest that phosphoinositide turnover also influences endosomal vesicle reformation and lipid-dependent coupling to the actin/cytoskeletal reserve pool. (harris2000mutationsinsynaptojanin pages 7-9, harris2000mutationsinsynaptojanin pages 9-10)

Mechanistically, hydrolysis of PI(4,5)P₂ both reduces negative surface charge and removes binding sites for PI(4,5)P₂-dependent proteins—including clathrin adaptors, dynamin, and synaptotagmin. UNC-26 therefore functions as a **membrane-identity timer**: PI(4,5)P₂ helps assemble endocytic machinery, and UNC-26-mediated dephosphorylation permits that machinery to disengage so the internalized membrane can mature into a reusable synaptic vesicle. (harris2000mutationsinsynaptojanin pages 9-10, harris2000mutationsinsynaptojanin pages 2-3)

## 5. Cooperation with endophilin/UNC-57

UNC-26 operates closely with **UNC-57/endophilin**. Endophilin’s BAR domain senses and bends curved membranes, while its SH3 domain can bind UNC-26’s PRD. Classical experiments concluded that endophilin stabilizes or localizes synaptojanin at synapses; *unc-26* and *unc-57* mutants exhibit similar uncoordinated behavior, aldicarb resistance, vesicle depletion, and accumulated endocytic intermediates, and the double-mutant phenotype is consistent with action in the same pathway. (schuske2003endophilinisrequired pages 2-3)

Later experiments refined this model. Deletion of the UNC-26 PRD and the endophilin SH3 domain did not abolish recycling: the truncated proteins restored locomotion, electrophysiology, vesicle abundance, FM4-64 recycling, and synaptopHluorin retrieval. This was not attributable to overexpression, because UNC-26ΔPRD and UNC-57ΔSH3 were present at approximately 32% and 75% of endogenous levels. Before rescue, the double mutant showed a 63% increase in axonal synaptopHluorin fluorescence, indicating defective retrieval of vesicle proteins from the surface. (dong2015synaptojanincooperatesin pages 5-8)

The current model is therefore not simply “SH3 binds PRD and recruits UNC-26.” Rather, **coincident membrane association** is central: endophilin’s BAR domain generates or recognizes curved, lipid-packing-defective membranes, while UNC-26’s Sac1 module positions the linked 5-phosphatase nearby. SH3–PRD binding can enhance synaptic enrichment and performance under demanding conditions but is not the sole targeting mechanism. (dong2015synaptojanincooperatesin pages 14-16, dong2015synaptojanincooperatesin pages 1-2)

## 6. Newer functions and 2022–2024 developments

### ATG-9 trafficking and presynaptic autophagy

Work published in 2022 and extended conceptually in 2023 connected UNC-26-dependent endocytosis to presynaptic autophagy. ATG-9 travels from the trans-Golgi network to synapses and undergoes activity-dependent exo-endocytosis there. In *unc-26* mutants, ATG-9 accumulated in clathrin-rich subsynaptic foci, and neuronal stimulation failed to elicit the normal increase in LGG-1-marked autophagosomes. A conserved early-onset-parkinsonism-associated synaptojanin lesion engineered into worm UNC-26 produced related ATG-9 trafficking, neurotransmission, and locomotor defects. Thus, UNC-26 helps couple synaptic activity and vesicle cycling to local autophagosome biogenesis. (yang2022transmembraneproteinatg9 pages 1-2, yang2022transmembraneproteinatg9 pages 2-2)

### Intact-animal measurement of recycling kinetics

A March 2023 study used pOpsicle—optogenetic stimulation combined with vesicle-targeted pH-sensitive fluorescence—to measure recycling in intact worms. In *unc-26(s1710)* animals, the stimulation-evoked fluorescence rise was slower (**3.6 s versus 2.3 s** in wild type), consistent with depletion or reduced availability of releasable vesicles. Post-stimulation decay was also delayed (**24.2 s versus 15.2 s**), directly quantifying impaired retrieval and/or reacidification. This contemporary assay independently validates the original ultrastructural model in living animals. (seidenthal2023popsicleanalloptical pages 7-9)

### Parkinson-related α-synuclein models

A 2023 *Acta Neuropathologica* study found that synaptojanin deficiency in nematodes accelerated α-synuclein aggregation and worsened locomotor dysfunction, supporting *unc-26* as an experimentally useful model of SYNJ1-linked neurodegenerative mechanisms. However, the reported elevation of PIP₃ and the demonstration that PIP₃ directly promotes α-synuclein fibrillation came from separate mammalian-cell, biochemical, and human-tissue experiments—not direct lipid measurements in *unc-26* worms. Those results are mechanistically suggestive but should not be entered as worm-specific biochemical proof. (choong2023phosphatidylinositol345trisphosphateinteractswith pages 1-2)

The 2023–2024 literature therefore broadens UNC-26 from a canonical synaptic-vesicle-recycling factor to a node connecting endocytic lipid remodeling, ATG-9 sorting, presynaptic autophagy, proteostasis, and neurodegenerative disease modeling. It does not materially overturn the established catalytic annotation: PI(4,5)P₂ 5-dephosphorylation remains the best-supported primary synaptic function.

## 7. Applications and real-world relevance

* **Functional genomics:** Strong and hypomorphic *unc-26* alleles, domain mutants, CRISPR disease variants, and rescue constructs permit dissection of catalytic versus targeting functions.
* **Live synaptic assays:** *unc-26* is a validated positive control for synaptopHluorin, FM-dye, electrophysiological, optogenetic-contraction, and pOpsicle measurements of vesicle recycling. The pOpsicle data provide defined kinetic benchmarks for impaired endocytosis. (dong2015synaptojanincooperatesin pages 5-8, seidenthal2023popsicleanalloptical pages 7-9)
* **Ultrastructural pathway assignment:** Its characteristic accumulation of pits, coated vesicles, and endosomal intermediates helps classify newly discovered trafficking genes by affected recycling stage. (harris2000mutationsinsynaptojanin pages 1-2, harris2000mutationsinsynaptojanin pages 7-9)
* **Disease modeling:** Worm UNC-26 provides a tractable in-vivo counterpart of human SYNJ1/PARK20 for studying early-onset parkinsonism, α-synuclein proteotoxicity, and activity-dependent presynaptic autophagy. Translation should remain cautious because worm phenotypes do not by themselves prove the same substrate imbalance or cell-selective pathology in humans. (yang2022transmembraneproteinatg9 pages 1-2, choong2023phosphatidylinositol345trisphosphateinteractswith pages 1-2)

## 8. Evidence limitations and recommended annotation

No retrieved study provided a complete kinetic substrate panel for purified G5ECL2, such as Km, kcat, or head-to-head activity against soluble Ins(1,4,5)P₃ versus membrane PI(4,5)P₂. The broad Sac1/5-phosphatase substrate range is therefore evolutionarily and biochemically well supported at the family level but incompletely measured for the worm protein itself. Protein localization is supported by synaptic enrichment, mutant ultrastructure, endophilin dependence, and targeting/rescue experiments; a modern endogenous knock-in map across all tissues would improve spatial resolution.

A defensible concise annotation is:

> **UNC-26 is the *C. elegans* synaptojanin, a presynaptically enriched polyphosphoinositide phosphatase whose essential in-vivo synaptic activity converts PI(4,5)P₂ to PI(4)P on endocytic membranes. Its Sac1 domain chiefly supports membrane/synaptic targeting, while its central 5-phosphatase drives coat/adaptor release and progression of synaptic-vesicle recycling. UNC-26 also supports endosomal vesicle reformation, ATG-9 sorting, and activity-induced presynaptic autophagy.**
