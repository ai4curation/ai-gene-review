Gene Description

ced-13 encodes a small BH3-only protein of the EGL-1 family (Pfam PF11430), the second BH3-only protein of Caenorhabditis elegans. Its transcription is induced by ionizing radiation and other genotoxic stress in a CEP-1/p53-dependent manner, together with egl-1. CED-13 binds the BCL2-family survival protein CED-9, and a CED-13 BH3 peptide, like that of EGL-1, displaces CED-4 from the CED-9/CED-4 complex in vitro. Overexpression in somatic cells kills cells that normally survive, through the core CED-9/CED-4/CED-3 pathway. Loss of ced-13 alone has little effect on developmental or DNA-damage-induced death; it contributes to physiological germ cell death that is increased when CISD (mitoNEET-like) proteins are lost. CED-13 also has a non-apoptotic role: it is required, upstream of mitochondrial ROS and CED-4, for the intrinsic-pathway-dependent pro-longevity and protective response of mitochondrial respiratory-chain mutants such as isp-1 and nuo-6.

Proposed New Ontology Terms

pro-survival BCL2 family protein inhibitor activity

Definition: Binding to a pro-survival BCL2-family protein through a BH3-mediated interaction to inhibit that protein from sequestering pro-apoptotic BCL2-family effectors or activators.

Justification: CED-13, like EGL-1 and the mammalian BH3-only proteins, donates a BH3 helix to a pro-survival BCL2-family protein (CED-9) and inhibits its sequestration of CED-4. GO:0051400 BH domain binding and GO:0051434 BH3 domain binding describe binding to a BH domain, which is the groove-side receptor's activity. The same term is proposed in the egl-1 review.

Parent term: molecular function inhibitor activity

Supporting Evidence:

  • PMID:16167070 : synthetic peptides corresponding to the BH3 domains of worm BH3-only proteins (EGL-1, CED-13) dissociate CED-4 from CED-9

Core Functions

CED-13 is a BH3-only protein that binds the pro-survival BCL2-family protein CED-9 and can release CED-4 from CED-9, promoting CED-3 activation. It is induced by CEP-1/p53 after DNA damage and feeds into the core apoptotic pathway, and it also activates the CED-9/CED-4 pathway non-apoptotically in the mitochondrial ROS longevity response.

Suggested Questions for Experts

Q: Should the CED-13-dependent, non-apoptotic activation of the CED-9/CED-4/CED-3 pathway by mitochondrial ROS (PMID:24813612) be annotated, and with which process term, given that it promotes longevity and stress protection rather than cell death?

Q: Why does CED-13 drive a protective, non-lethal output of the same CED-9/CED-4 pathway that EGL-1 uses to kill cells?

Suggested Experiments

Experiment: Compare CED-9 binding affinity and CED-4 release for full-length CED-13 and EGL-1 in vitro, and map endogenous tagged CED-13 expression in somatic tissues of isp-1 and nuo-6 mutants versus wild type.

Hypothesis: CED-13 and EGL-1 differ in where they are expressed or in how strongly they bind CED-9, which explains their different outputs.
