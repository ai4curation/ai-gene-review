# Comprehensive Research Report: osm-11 Gene in Caenorhabditis elegans

## Gene Identity and Overview

The gene osm-11 (UniProt accession O45346, gene locus F11C7.5) encodes a non-canonical Notch ligand in *Caenorhabditis elegans* that plays diverse roles in development, stress physiology, and cognitive function (komatsu2008osm11facilitateslin12 pages 1-2). The protein was originally identified through its osmotic avoidance phenotype, giving rise to its name "osmotic avoidance abnormal protein 11" (urso2021thec.elegans pages 7-9).

## Protein Structure and Molecular Characteristics

### Domain Architecture

OSM-11 is a small secreted protein with a predicted molecular mass of approximately 18.9 kDa, though it migrates at 20.7 kDa on gels, likely due to O-linked glycosylation (komatsu2008osm11facilitateslin12 pages 8-10, komatsu2008osm11facilitateslin12 pages 6-8). The protein architecture includes an N-terminal signal peptide that directs entry into the secretory pathway, followed by a conserved DOS (Delta and OSM-11) motif located within a putative calcium-binding epidermal growth factor-like (cEGF-1) domain (komatsu2008osm11facilitateslin12 pages 3-5, komatsu2008osm11facilitateslin12 pages 2-3).

The DOS motif is a cysteine-rich sequence with the conserved pattern C-X(3)-C-X(3,8)-C-X(2,5)-C-[KVER]-C-X(10,12)-C-X(1,3)-P-X(6,9)-C-X(1,4)-W-X(1,4)-C, which is shared with other Notch ligands across species (komatsu2008osm11facilitateslin12 pages 2-3, komatsu2008osm11facilitateslin12 pages 14-14). Critically, OSM-11 lacks the canonical DSL (Delta/Serrate/LAG-2) domain found in classical Notch ligands, classifying it as a DOS-motif-only protein similar to mammalian DLK1 and DLK2 (komatsu2008osm11facilitateslin12 pages 1-2, komatsu2008osm11facilitateslin12 pages 13-14).

Some evidence suggests OSM-11 may be synthesized as a transmembrane preproprotein with a consensus proprotein convertase cleavage site and C-terminal transmembrane domain, which would allow proteolytic release of the soluble DOS-containing extracellular region (komatsu2008osm11facilitateslin12 pages 2-3). However, the mature functional form is clearly secreted and diffusible rather than membrane-tethered (komatsu2008osm11facilitateslin12 pages 8-10, komatsu2008osm11facilitateslin12 pages 6-8).

## Subcellular Localization and Tissue Expression

### Cellular Localization

OSM-11 is a secreted extracellular protein. Experimental expression in Drosophila S2 cells demonstrated that OSM-11 accumulates in the culture medium rather than in cell lysates, confirming its secreted nature (komatsu2008osm11facilitateslin12 pages 8-10, komatsu2008osm11facilitateslin12 pages 6-8). During vulval development, OSM-11 localizes to the apical surfaces of vulval precursor cells (VPCs), positioning it appropriately for interaction with LIN-12 Notch receptors (komatsu2008osm11facilitateslin12 pages 6-8).

### Tissue Expression

OSM-11 is most prominently expressed in the adult hypodermis (skin), where it represents the most highly expressed hypodermal gene according to tissue-specific transcriptome analysis (kaletsky2018transcriptomeanalysisof pages 12-14, kaletsky2018transcriptomeanalysisof pages 10-12). Expression has also been reported in hypodermal seam cells and the spermatheca (zhou2023signalingfromthe pages 3-4). During vulval development, OSM-11 is detected in all six equivalent VPCs before cell-fate specification and becomes undetectable after fates are determined (komatsu2008osm11facilitateslin12 pages 6-8).

Importantly, OSM-11 is not expressed in the ciliated ASH sensory neurons despite its role in osmotic avoidance behavior, indicating that it functions non-autonomously through inter-tissue signaling rather than cell-autonomous mechanisms (urso2021thec.elegans pages 7-9).

## Primary Molecular Function

### Notch Receptor Binding and Activation

OSM-11 functions as a non-canonical Notch co-ligand that facilitates receptor activation. In yeast two-hybrid assays, OSM-11 interacted directly with the extracellular EGF repeats 1-6 of the LIN-12 Notch receptor (komatsu2008osm11facilitateslin12 pages 12-13, komatsu2008osm11facilitateslin12 pages 10-12). This interaction is specific, as established DSL ligands LAG-2 and DSL-1 also bound LIN-12 in the same assay, while unrelated signaling proteins did not (komatsu2008osm11facilitateslin12 pages 10-12).

The functional relationship between OSM-11 and Notch receptors is best characterized as cooperative activation: OSM-11 acts together with canonical DSL ligands to promote receptor signaling rather than functioning as an independent ligand (komatsu2008osm11facilitateslin12 pages 13-14, komatsu2008osm11facilitateslin12 pages 12-13). Loss-of-function osm-11 mutants show mild defects that are enhanced by simultaneous loss of DSL ligands or other DOS-motif proteins, supporting redundant or synergistic function (komatsu2008osm11facilitateslin12 pages 1-2, komatsu2008osm11facilitateslin12 pages 8-10).

### Receptor Specificity

OSM-11 primarily activates the LIN-12 Notch receptor in most characterized contexts (sorkac2018lin12notchregulatesgaba pages 7-7, sorkac2018lin12notchregulatesgaba pages 4-5). Evidence also suggests it can activate GLP-1, the other C. elegans Notch receptor, though GLP-1's role in OSM-11-mediated memory regulation could not be fully assessed due to broad learning defects in GLP-1-depleted animals (murphy2024bodytobraininsulinand pages 6-9). The functional relationship indicates LIN-12 as the primary receptor partner, with potential context-dependent GLP-1 activation (komatsu2008osm11facilitateslin12 pages 13-14).

### Body-to-Brain Insulin/Notch/CREB Signaling for Memory

The most thoroughly characterized recent function of OSM-11 is its role as a body-to-brain signal linking peripheral metabolism to cognitive function. When insulin/IGF-1 receptor (DAF-2) signaling is reduced in the hypodermis, DAF-16/FOXO transcription factor activity increases osm-11 expression (murphy2024bodytobraininsulinand pages 6-9, murphy2024bodytobraininsulinand pages 9-11). The secreted OSM-11 protein then acts on neurons, where it binds and activates the LIN-12 Notch receptor, particularly in RIG-associated interneurons (zhou2025bodytobraininsulinand pages 8-9, murphy2024bodytobraininsulinand pages 9-11).

Downstream of LIN-12 activation, the cleaved Notch intracellular domain (NICD) translocates to the nucleus and forms a transcriptional complex with the LAG-1 transcription factor and SEL-8/LAG-3 co-activator (murphy2024bodytobraininsulinand pages 6-9). This complex activates expression of crh-1, the C. elegans CREB (cAMP response element-binding protein) homolog, along with CREB-responsive memory genes including unc-43, unc-73, and casy-1 (zhou2025bodytobraininsulinand pages 8-9, murphy2024bodytobraininsulinand pages 9-11).

The functional significance is substantial: loss of osm-11 blocks the enhanced memory caused by hypodermal DAF-2 reduction, while hypodermal overexpression of OSM-11 is sufficient to improve both learning and long-term associative memory (murphy2024bodytobraininsulinand pages 9-11, murphy2024bodytobraininsulinand pages 6-9). Critically, OSM-11 overexpression can rescue memory deficits in aged animals, which normally show reduced osm-11 expression and cognitive decline (murphy2024bodytobraininsulinand pages 1-4, zhou2025bodytobraininsulinand pages 11-12). This pathway is CREB-dependent and represents a distinct mechanism from neuronal insulin signaling, which regulates memory independently of CREB (murphy2024bodytobraininsulinand pages 6-9, zhou2025bodytobraininsulinand pages 10-11).

### Osmotic and Salinity Stress Response

OSM-11 plays a negative regulatory role in osmotic stress physiology. In the hypodermis, OSM-11 normally restrains the expression of gpdh-1 (glycerol-3-phosphate dehydrogenase), thereby limiting glycerol accumulation under non-stress conditions (urso2021thec.elegans pages 7-9). Loss of osm-11 causes constitutive activation of the hypertonic stress response, including elevated gpdh-1 expression and glycerol accumulation, which paradoxically improves resistance to otherwise damaging hypertonic conditions (urso2021thec.elegans pages 7-9).

Despite this enhanced physiological stress resistance, osm-11 mutants exhibit defective osmotic avoidance behavior—they fail to move away from hypertonic stimuli as wild-type animals do (urso2021thec.elegans pages 7-9). Because OSM-11 is expressed in the hypodermis rather than the ASH sensory neurons that mediate avoidance, this behavioral defect likely involves paracrine signaling from the hypodermis to ASH neurons, though the specific intermediate signal remains unidentified (urso2021thec.elegans pages 7-9).

Recent preprint evidence (not yet peer-reviewed) suggests OSM-11 also modulates salinity stress tolerance through distinct metabolic pathways. Loss of osm-11 substantially improves survival at 30‰ salinity, correlating with increased expression of fatty acid metabolism and cytochrome P450 genes (including acdh-12, acs-17, and ugt-15), reduced mitochondrial fragmentation, and altered oxidative phosphorylation and TCA cycle activity (zhang2025osm11modulatessalinitystress pages 1-4, zhang2025osm11modulatessalinitystress pages 4-7). The enhanced salt tolerance requires glutathione peroxidase genes (gpx-1 and gpx-7) and involves modulation of ferroptosis and mitophagy pathways (zhang2025osm11modulatessalinitystress pages 4-7). Notably, this salinity phenotype appears independent of SKN-1/Nrf signaling, distinguishing it from OSM-11's previously reported roles in heat and oxidative stress responses (zhang2025osm11modulatessalinitystress pages 1-4).

### Vulval Development

OSM-11 was originally characterized for its role in vulval precursor cell (VPC) fate specification during larval development. OSM-11 acts as a secreted DOS-motif co-ligand that facilitates LIN-12-dependent specification of secondary (2°) vulval cell fates (komatsu2008osm11facilitateslin12 pages 1-2, komatsu2008osm11facilitateslin12 pages 5-6). Complete loss of OSM-11 causes defective or misshapen vulvae, with some VPCs inappropriately expressing primary fate markers and failing to express secondary fate markers such as lin-11 and lip-1, which are directly dependent on LIN-12 signaling (komatsu2008osm11facilitateslin12 pages 5-6).

Genetic epistasis analyses place osm-11 upstream of or at the level of LIN-12 receptor activation (komatsu2008osm11facilitateslin12 pages 8-10). Loss of osm-11 partially suppresses a temperature-sensitive lin-12 gain-of-function allele but does not suppress ligand-independent lin-12 hyperactivation, consistent with OSM-11 functioning before or during ligand-dependent receptor activation (komatsu2008osm11facilitateslin12 pages 8-10). The vulval defects are enhanced when other DOS-motif genes or DSL ligands are simultaneously lost, supporting cooperative function (komatsu2008osm11facilitateslin12 pages 10-12). Remarkably, mammalian DLK1, a DOS-motif protein, can partially substitute for OSM-11 in C. elegans vulval development, demonstrating functional conservation across species (komatsu2008osm11facilitateslin12 pages 1-2).

### Adult Neuromuscular Junction Signaling

OSM-11 also modulates synaptic function in adult animals through effects on GABA signaling at the neuromuscular junction (NMJ). This role is distinct from developmental cell-fate specification because manipulation of OSM-11 levels after neuronal and muscle cell fates are established still produces robust phenotypes (sorkac2018lin12notchregulatesgaba pages 4-5).

Loss of osm-11 or adult RNAi knockdown causes hypersensitivity to the acetylcholinesterase inhibitor aldicarb, resulting in faster paralysis, while OSM-11 overexpression causes aldicarb resistance and slower paralysis (sorkac2018lin12notchregulatesgaba pages 7-8, sorkac2018lin12notchregulatesgaba pages 4-5). These effects appear mediated through LIN-12/Notch-dependent regulation of GABAergic signaling, with the GABA-B receptor subunit gbb-2 proposed as a potential downstream transcriptional target of Notch signaling (sorkac2018lin12notchregulatesgaba pages 5-5, sorkac2018lin12notchregulatesgaba pages 2-3). OSM-11 thus positively modulates the excitation-inhibition balance at the NMJ independently of its developmental functions (sorkac2018lin12notchregulatesgaba pages 4-5).

## Broader Biological Processes

Beyond the specific pathways detailed above, OSM-11 has been implicated in several other processes:

**Embryonic development and longevity**: OSM-11 signaling has been linked to SKN-1/Nrf-dependent regulation during embryogenesis and adult lifespan determination (urso2021thec.elegans pages 15-16).

**Stress-responsive transcription**: OSM-11 contributes to organismal responses to heat and oxidative stress through SKN-1 pathway regulation, though the receptor-to-SKN-1 molecular mechanism remains incompletely characterized (urso2021thec.elegans pages 15-16).

**Inter-tissue coordination**: A common theme across OSM-11 functions is its role as a secreted, diffusible signal that coordinates responses across tissues. It links hypodermal metabolism to neuronal memory, peripheral stress sensing to behavioral avoidance, and local developmental signals to neighboring cells during patterning (zhou2023signalingfromthe pages 3-4, kaletsky2018transcriptomeanalysisof pages 12-14, murphy2024bodytobraininsulinand pages 9-11).

## Experimental Evidence Summary

The functional characterization of OSM-11 rests on multiple complementary experimental approaches:

**Molecular evidence**: Yeast two-hybrid assays demonstrated direct interaction between OSM-11 and LIN-12 extracellular EGF repeats (komatsu2008osm11facilitateslin12 pages 12-13, komatsu2008osm11facilitateslin12 pages 10-12). Secretion assays in Drosophila S2 cells confirmed extracellular localization (komatsu2008osm11facilitateslin12 pages 8-10).

**Genetic evidence**: Loss-of-function analyses across multiple null and strong alleles established phenotypes in vulval development, osmotic physiology, and behavior (sorkac2018lin12notchregulatesgaba pages 4-5, komatsu2008osm11facilitateslin12 pages 5-6). Epistasis experiments positioned osm-11 upstream of Notch receptors (komatsu2008osm11facilitateslin12 pages 8-10). Tissue-specific rescue and RNAi experiments demonstrated cell non-autonomous function (komatsu2008osm11facilitateslin12 pages 8-10, komatsu2008osm11facilitateslin12 pages 6-8).

**Transcriptomic evidence**: Single-nucleus RNA sequencing of neurons after hypodermal DAF-2 reduction revealed OSM-11-dependent upregulation of CREB and memory-associated genes (zhou2025bodytobraininsulinand pages 8-9, zhou2025bodytobraininsulinand pages 10-10). Bulk transcriptomics of osm-11 mutants identified changes in fatty acid metabolism, cytochrome P450, and calcium signaling pathways (zhang2025osm11modulatessalinitystress pages 1-4).

**Functional behavioral studies**: Associative memory assays demonstrated OSM-11's role in learning and memory extension, including rescue of age-related cognitive decline (murphy2024bodytobraininsulinand pages 1-4, zhou2025bodytobraininsulinand pages 11-12). Osmotic avoidance assays established the osmotic avoidance abnormal phenotype (urso2021thec.elegans pages 7-9). Aldicarb sensitivity tests revealed neuromuscular junction function (sorkac2018lin12notchregulatesgaba pages 7-8, sorkac2018lin12notchregulatesgaba pages 4-5).

## Current Understanding and Recent Developments (2023-2025)

The most significant recent advance is the 2025 publication in *Nature Aging* by Zhou et al. establishing OSM-11 as a critical mediator of body-to-brain signaling for memory regulation (zhou2025bodytobraininsulinand pages 8-9, zhou2025bodytobraininsulinand pages 10-10, zhou2025bodytobraininsulinand pages 11-12). This work demonstrated that peripheral metabolic state, communicated through hypodermal OSM-11 secretion, can modulate cognitive function through neuronal Notch and CREB activation. The finding that OSM-11 overexpression rescues age-related memory deficits suggests potential therapeutic relevance for understanding metabolic influences on cognitive aging.

A 2025 bioRxiv preprint (Zhang et al.) reported a new role for OSM-11 in salinity stress tolerance involving mitochondrial function, fatty acid metabolism, and ferroptosis pathways (zhang2025osm11modulatessalinitystress pages 1-4, zhang2025osm11modulatessalinitystress pages 4-7). While these findings await peer review, they expand OSM-11's repertoire beyond canonical Notch-mediated developmental and behavioral functions to include metabolic stress adaptation.

Earlier work (2018-2023) established OSM-11's roles in neuromuscular junction signaling (sorkac2018lin12notchregulatesgaba pages 7-8), osmotic stress (urso2021thec.elegans pages 7-9), and the initial characterization of its memory function (zhou2023signalingfromthe pages 3-4). The 2008 discovery paper by Komatsu et al. remains the foundational reference for OSM-11's molecular characterization and vulval development function (komatsu2008osm11facilitateslin12 pages 1-2, komatsu2008osm11facilitateslin12 pages 12-13).

## Conclusion

OSM-11 represents a functionally versatile non-canonical Notch ligand that integrates developmental, physiological, and cognitive processes in C. elegans. As a secreted DOS-motif protein lacking a DSL domain, it acts cooperatively with canonical Notch ligands to modulate LIN-12 and possibly GLP-1 receptor activation. The protein serves as a key mediator of inter-tissue communication, linking peripheral metabolic state to neuronal function, hypodermal stress sensing to behavioral responses, and local developmental cues to cell fate decisions. Recent work highlighting its role in the body-to-brain axis regulating memory and cognitive aging suggests that DOS-motif Notch ligands may have conserved functions in metabolic-neural integration across species, given the functional conservation demonstrated by mammalian DLK1 rescue experiments.
