Gene Description

CED-7 is a C. elegans ABCA-family ATP-binding cassette transporter, the orthologue of mammalian ABCA1. It has two homologous halves, each with a membrane-spanning domain and an ATP-binding cassette, and it localizes to the plasma membrane. It is broadly expressed in embryos, and mosaic analysis shows it acts in both dying and engulfing cells during corpse engulfment. CED-7 belongs to the CED-1/CED-6/CED-7 engulfment pathway. It is required for the CED-1 receptor to cluster around cell corpses and for actin reorganization around them. With the secreted phosphatidylserine (PS)-binding bridging protein TTR-52, it promotes the release of PS-containing extracellular vesicles from apoptotic cells. Those vesicles lead to PS exposure on neighbouring engulfing cells, which depends on CED-1 and helps corpse clearance. Like other engulfment genes, ced-7 helps engulfing cells promote the death of cells that receive weak death signals.

Core Functions

ABCA-family ATP-binding cassette transporter at the plasma membrane of dying and engulfing cells. With TTR-52 it promotes phosphatidylserine efflux from apoptotic cells into extracellular vesicles, and it is needed for CED-1 to recognize and cluster around corpses.

Suggested Questions for Experts

Q: Does CED-7 transport phosphatidylserine itself, or does it move another lipid that drives PS-vesicle release? No reconstituted transport assay exists for CED-7.

Q: What does CED-7 do in the dying cell versus the engulfing cell, given that mosaic analysis shows a requirement in both?

Suggested Experiments

Experiment: Express Walker A mutant CED-7 in ced-7 null embryos, in dying cells or engulfing cells only, and score sAnxV::GFP loss from corpses, extracellular PS vesicles by immuno-EM, and CED-1::GFP clustering.

Hypothesis: CED-7 ATPase activity is required for PS-vesicle release from apoptotic cells and for CED-1 clustering.
