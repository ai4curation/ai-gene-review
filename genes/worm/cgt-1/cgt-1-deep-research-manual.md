## Identity and family
- C. elegans CGT-1, 466 aa, one of three ceramide glucosyltransferase (UGCG/GCS) paralogs
  (cgt-1, cgt-2, cgt-3). GT2 family (Pfam PF13506 Glyco_transf_21; CDD cd02520
  Glucosylceramide_synthase; PANTHER PTHR12726:SF1). Three predicted TM helices
  (70-90, 354-374, 403-423); D1/D2/D3 and (Q/R)XXRW catalytic motifs conserved, D294
  proposed catalytic base by similarity to rat Ugcg (UniProt O18037).

## Enzymatic activity
- Nomura et al. 2011 [PMID:21325339 "Each gene product showed active enzymatic activity when
  expressed in GM95 cells deficient in glycosphingolipids (GSLs)."] CGT-3 had the highest
  activity [PMID:21325339 "The CGT-3 protein had the highest Ugcg activity"].
- C. elegans glucosylceramides contain only an iso-branched C17 sphingoid base and glucose
  [PMID:7651085 "Glucose was the only sugar moiety detected."].

## Physiological role
- Loss of CGT (cgt-1;cgt-3 or triple) eliminates GSLs and causes L1 arrest from intestinal /
  digestive-tract defects [PMID:19240113 "Animals lacking CGT do not synthesize GSLs, arrest
  growth at the first larval stage"]; rescue in digestive-tract cells suffices (abstract;
  full text not available to us).
- Germline: single knockouts reduce brood size, abnormal oocytes, multinucleate embryos
  [PMID:21325339 "When each gene function was disrupted, the brood size of the animal markedly
  decreased"].
- Zhu et al. 2013 eLife: d17iso-GlcCer produced by CGT-1/CGT-3 acts upstream of TORC1 in the
  intestine to promote postembryonic development; cgt-1(tm1027);cgt-3 L1 arrest is suppressed
  by nprl-3(ku540) [PMID:23705068 "cgt-1 and cgt-3 encode two ceramide glucosyltransferases that
  glucosylate ceramides to GlcCer"; "TORC1 acts downstream of d17iso-GlcCer to promote L1 growth
  and development"].
- Zhu et al. 2015: cgt-1/3 double RNAi suppresses sgk-1 null trafficking defects of hTAC::GFP and
  MIG-14::GFP in intestine; slows development [PMID:26115433 "sgk-1 null nearly abolished
  MIG-14::GFP on the basolateral membrane, but this was restored by cgt-1/3 double RNAi"].
- Arvin et al. 2023 microPublication: cgt-1 mutants have shortened lifespan
  [PMID:37090153 "We found that loss of cgt-1 or cgt-3 reduces lifespan compared to wildtype
  worms"].

## Localization
- No experimental subcellular localization for CGT-1 in the material available. Mammalian
  UGCG is a Golgi (cis/medial) membrane protein with its active site on the cytosolic face;
  CGT-1 location is inferred by homology only.
