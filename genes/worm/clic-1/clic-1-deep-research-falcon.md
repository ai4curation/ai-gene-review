# Functional annotation report: *C. elegans clic-1* (UniProt P90961)

## Executive conclusion

The supplied identity is correct: *Caenorhabditis elegans clic-1* (T05B11.3; UniProt P90961) encodes a **clathrin light chain**, not a chloride intracellular channel. Worm literature explicitly identifies CLIC-1 and clathrin heavy chain CHC-1 as the principal structural subunits of the clathrin coat, matching the supplied Clathrin_L-chain/PF01086 domain assignment. No evidence was found that P90961 has chloride-channel activity. Papers about vertebrate CLIC1—the chloride intracellular channel protein—therefore concern a different protein and were excluded. (kaplan2012endocytosisgenesfacilitate pages 1-2, sato2014c.elegansas pages 4-6)

The best-supported primary function is as a **nonenzymatic, cytosolic coat component associated with clathrin-coated membranes**. CLIC-1 supports clathrin-dependent membrane remodeling and cargo traffic rather than catalyzing a reaction or transporting a substrate. In worms it is individually nonessential under standard assays, but becomes essential when CHC-1 activity is compromised, indicating an accessory or stabilizing role in clathrin function. Its experimentally observed locations include plasma-membrane-associated puncta, the sensory-neuron periciliary membrane compartment, the neuronal axon initial segment, and neuronal lysosome/autolysosome membranes and tubules. (sato2014c.elegansas pages 4-6, swords2023aconservedrequirement pages 47-50, eichel2022endocytosisinthe pages 4-4)

## 1. Identity verification and nomenclature

The gene symbol is potentially misleading because mammalian **CLIC1** commonly denotes “chloride intracellular channel 1.” In *C. elegans*, however, **CLIC-1** is explicitly described as the clathrin light chain and is experimentally visualized with GFP, mNeonGreen, mScarlet, or tagRFP fusions as a marker of clathrin-associated structures. This agrees with UniProt P90961, gene/ORF names *clic-1*, T05B11.3 and CELE_T05B11.3, and the supplied clathrin-light-chain family/domain annotations. (kaplan2012endocytosisgenesfacilitate pages 1-2, reimann2025piki1aclass pages 10-15, milne2024tat1aphosphatidylserine pages 14-18)

Accordingly, the defensible naming convention is **CLIC-1 (clathrin light chain)**, ideally accompanied by “*C. elegans*” or P90961 to prevent confusion. There is no basis for annotating this protein as an ion channel, chloride transporter, or enzyme.

## 2. Molecular function and structural role

CLIC-1 is one of the structural components of the clathrin coat, partnered functionally with CHC-1. Clathrin coats assemble on cytosolic membrane surfaces and participate in membrane curvature, coated-bud formation, cargo internalization, and subsequent membrane-trafficking steps. For CLIC-1 specifically, the strongest worm evidence is genetic: RNAi or deletion of *clic-1* alone did not detectably abolish viability or endocytosis, whereas *clic-1* RNAi in a temperature-sensitive *chc-1* background caused embryonic lethality even at the normally permissive temperature. This synthetic interaction shows that CLIC-1 contributes materially to clathrin function when heavy-chain capacity is limiting. (sato2014c.elegansas pages 4-6)

This evidence supports a structural/regulatory annotation rather than an indispensable catalytic role. It does **not**, by itself, establish direct CLIC-1–CHC-1 binding, define a cargo-binding site, or show which step of coat assembly is most dependent on the light chain. The conclusion that light chains regulate cage mechanics or interactions with actin-associated machinery is biologically plausible from the conserved clathrin family, but remains family-level inference unless tested directly in this worm protein.

## 3. Cellular localization and site of action

### 3.1 Plasma-membrane and epidermal clathrin structures

In the polarized hyp7 epidermal syncytium, mScarlet::CLIC-1 appears as small, regularly dispersed puncta, including in the apical region, and has been imaged alongside GFP::CHC-1. These puncta are consistent with clathrin-coated membrane structures and demonstrate that CLIC-1 functions on the cytoplasmic face of intracellular or plasma membranes rather than outside the cell. The 2024 experiment perturbed the phosphatidylserine flippase TAT-1, not CLIC-1 itself, so associated membrane-bending and scission conclusions are pathway context rather than proof of a CLIC-1-specific biochemical mechanism. (milne2024tat1aphosphatidylserine pages 14-18)

### 3.2 Sensory-cilium base

A che-12-driven CLIC-1::GFP reporter accumulated immediately proximal to amphid and phasmid sensory cilia in the **periciliary membrane compartment (PCMC)** and partially overlapped the CHE-13 intraflagellar-transport marker. This places clathrin machinery at the ciliary base, where endocytic retrieval can balance membrane delivery and regulate ciliary/periciliary protein composition. However, functional rescue by this reporter could not be assessed, and the broad membrane-expansion phenotypes observed after disruption of endocytic genes cannot be assigned specifically to loss of CLIC-1. Kaplan et al., published March 20, 2012: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2012.01.060. (kaplan2012endocytosisgenesfacilitate pages 1-2)

### 3.3 Neuronal axon initial segment

A cell-specific endogenous GFP–FLPon–CLIC-1 reporter marked clathrin structures in the PVD-neuron axon initial segment (AIS). The broader study showed that AIS endocytosis removes inappropriately localized membrane receptors and thereby reinforces neuronal polarity. Nevertheless, the quantitative receptor-internalization experiments centered on DMA-1 and AP-2, not on CLIC-1 loss of function. Thus, CLIC-1 is securely localized to the relevant endocytic compartment, but an individually required role in AIS clearance was not demonstrated. Eichel et al., published August 2022: https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-022-05074-5. (eichel2022endocytosisinthe pages 4-4)

### 3.4 Neuronal lysosomes and autolysosome-reformation tubules

Recent work found CLIC-1::mNeonGreen on LMP-1-positive lysosomal/autolysosomal vesicles and tubules in touch neurons. Recruitment was reduced in *rme-8*, *snx-1*, *bec-1*, and *vps-15* mutants; the abnormally elongated autolysosomal tubules in *rme-8* mutants lacked detectable CLIC-1 labeling. By contrast, *dyn-1* mutants retained recruitment, and the *dyn-1;rme-8* combination resembled *rme-8*, consistent with RME-8-dependent clathrin recruitment preceding dynamin-mediated tubule severing. (swords2023aconservedrequirement pages 77-93, swords2023aconservedrequirement pages 47-50)

This is an important 2023–2024 development connecting clathrin recruitment with neuronal autolysosome reformation. The evidence remains localization/genetic-dependency evidence: CLIC-1 itself was not depleted and rescued to establish causality, and the investigators noted that nonspecific association of a tagged light chain was an alternative requiring validation. The peer-reviewed study appeared in *Autophagy* in 2024: https://doi.org/10.1080/15548627.2023.2269028. (swords2023aconservedrequirement pages 93-97, swords2023aconservedrequirement pages 47-50)

## 4. Biological processes and pathways

### Clathrin-mediated endocytosis

The primary pathway assignment is clathrin-mediated membrane traffic. CLIC-1 is used as the light-chain component/reporting subunit of coated structures in the epidermis, sensory neurons, intestine, germline/embryonic imaging systems, and neurons. Its conditional interaction with *chc-1* provides the clearest causal evidence that it supports at least one clathrin-mediated process in vivo. The precise cargo spectrum and tissue-specific requirement remain incompletely defined. (sato2014c.elegansas pages 4-6, milne2024tat1aphosphatidylserine pages 14-18)

### Ciliary membrane homeostasis

PCMC localization supports participation in membrane retrieval at the sensory-cilium base. The appropriate mechanistic model is that clathrin-associated endocytosis counterbalances delivery pathways to preserve ciliary and periciliary membrane composition. This is stronger as a localization/pathway assignment than as a CLIC-1-specific phenotype because the reporter was not accompanied by a definitive loss-and-rescue analysis. (kaplan2012endocytosisgenesfacilitate pages 1-2)

### Maintenance of neuronal polarity

AIS-localized CLIC-1 is part of an endocytic compartment that removes receptors which diffuse into an inappropriate neuronal domain. The study supports a conserved membrane-clearance mechanism across worms and vertebrate neurons, but direct CLIC-1-specific necessity was not measured. (eichel2022endocytosisinthe pages 4-4)

### Lysosome homeostasis and autolysosome reformation

CLIC-1 reporter recruitment to autolysosomes is regulated by RME-8/DNAJC13, SNX-1, and the BEC-1–VPS-15 phosphatidylinositol-3-kinase machinery and occurs upstream of, or independently from, DYN-1 recruitment. This positions clathrin at membrane budding/tubulation or scission-associated stages of autolysosome reformation. It is a plausible extension of CLIC-1’s coat function, but current experiments establish regulated recruitment rather than a CLIC-1-specific knockout phenotype. (swords2023aconservedrequirement pages 93-97, swords2023aconservedrequirement pages 47-50)

### Apoptotic-cell clearance: negative evidence

RNAi against *clic-1* did not produce germ-cell-corpse accumulation comparable to depletion of CHC-1. Consequently, the substantial roles of clathrin/AP-2 in corpse engulfment and phagosome maturation should not be attributed directly to CLIC-1. This may indicate redundancy or a lower light-chain requirement in that assay, not universal dispensability. Chen et al., May 2013: https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pgen.1003517. (chen2013clathrinandap2 pages 13-14)

## 5. Recent research, applications, and quantitative data

The 2023–2024 literature did not reveal a new biochemical assay, structure, substrate, or CLIC-1-specific conditional knockout analysis. Instead, recent progress largely uses fluorescent CLIC-1 fusions as **in vivo spatial reporters of clathrin recruitment**:

* In 2023–2024 neuronal work, CLIC-1::mNeonGreen was quantified as the percentage of LMP-1-positive area containing clathrin signal across trafficking-mutant backgrounds. The retrieved report confirms statistical analysis by one-way ANOVA with Tukey correction, but did not expose exact numerical values or sample sizes; these should not be invented. (swords2023aconservedrequirement pages 77-93)
* The September 15, 2024 TAT-1 preprint used mScarlet::CLIC-1 and GFP::CHC-1 to assess apical epidermal clathrin organization: https://doi.org/10.1101/2024.09.15.613099. Its value for CLIC-1 annotation is chiefly localization and reporter application, because TAT-1—not *clic-1*—was perturbed. (milne2024tat1aphosphatidylserine pages 14-18)
* As a post-window update, a May 2025 study found that approximately **50% of mScarlet::CLIC-1 signal overlapped PIKI-1::mNeonGreen**, approximately **25% of PIKI-1 overlapped CLIC-1**, and the Pearson correlation was approximately **0.23**; rotated-channel controls reduced the association. This demonstrates partial, nonrandom colocalization of clathrin structures with the class-II PI3K system, but does not establish CLIC-1 causality. https://doi.org/10.1101/2025.05.22.655458. (reimann2025piki1aclass pages 10-15)

In practical terms, the major current application of CLIC-1 is as a genetically encoded marker for clathrin-coated structures and recruitment dynamics in intact animals. This enables tissue-resolved analysis of ciliary trafficking, epidermal endocytosis, neuronal polarity, and lysosome homeostasis. It is a research implementation rather than a clinical or industrial application; no therapeutic or diagnostic use of worm CLIC-1 was identified.

## 6. Expert assessment and recommended annotation

A conservative, evidence-matched annotation is:

> **CLIC-1 is the *C. elegans* clathrin light chain, a nonenzymatic component of cytosolic clathrin coats that supports clathrin-dependent membrane budding and trafficking. It localizes to clathrin-associated puncta at the plasma membrane and endomembranes, including the sensory-neuron periciliary compartment, neuronal axon initial segment, and lysosomal/autolysosomal membranes. Its individual loss is tolerated in standard viability/endocytosis assays, but it becomes essential when clathrin heavy-chain function is compromised.**

Confidence is **high** for identity, clathrin-family membership, nonenzymatic coat function, and the conditional *chc-1* genetic interaction; **moderate** for participation in ciliary-base and AIS endocytosis because evidence is mainly reporter localization; and **moderate-to-low for CLIC-1-specific causality** in autolysosome reformation, where regulated recruitment is compelling but direct depletion/rescue remains lacking. (kaplan2012endocytosisgenesfacilitate pages 1-2, sato2014c.elegansas pages 4-6, swords2023aconservedrequirement pages 93-97, eichel2022endocytosisinthe pages 4-4)

The most important knowledge gaps are a validated endogenous loss-and-rescue analysis in individual tissues, direct biochemical characterization of worm CLIC-1–CHC-1 association, quantitative coat-assembly dynamics, and tests separating a structural cage role from regulation of actin or cargo adaptors. Until those are available, broad phenotypes caused by CHC-1, AP-2, RME-8, TAT-1, PIKI-1, or other trafficking factors should not be recoded as direct CLIC-1 functions.
