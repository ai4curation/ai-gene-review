Gene Description

INS-6 is a neuronally expressed, secreted beta-type insulin-like peptide of C. elegans, one of about 40 insulin-like peptides (ILPs). Its structure recapitulates the insulin fold, and it binds and activates the human insulin receptor in vitro. In vivo INS-6 acts as an agonist of the DAF-2 insulin/IGF-1-like receptor. Released from sensory neurons such as ASI and ASJ, it acts redundantly with DAF-28 and INS-4 to prevent dauer entry under favorable conditions, and has a more prominent role than DAF-28 in promoting dauer exit. INS-6 is also a neuromodulator. ASE salt-sensing neurons release it after BLI-4-dependent processing, switching AWC into the salt circuit and enabling high-salt chemotaxis. From ASI it enables aversive olfactory learning by repressing ins-7 expression in URX. It also contributes to insulin/IGF signaling-dependent regulation of neuromuscular junction development.

Core Functions

INS-6 is a secreted insulin-like peptide hormone that binds and activates insulin receptors (DAF-2 in vivo), acting from sensory neurons on distant tissues such as the intestine to keep DAF-16 inactive and prevent dauer entry under favorable conditions.

Key findings

  • INS-6 adopts an insulin fold and binds and activates the human insulin receptor PMID:12654724.
  • Removing INS-4, INS-6 and DAF-28 together causes fully penetrant Daf-c PMID:24671950. daf-28 and ins-6 are non-redundant: ins-6 matters more for dauer exit PMID:21343369.
  • INS-6 is a neuromodulator in salt chemotaxis, released from ASE in a BLI-4-dependent way PMID:24013594, and in olfactory learning, acting from ASI to repress ins-7 in URX PMID:23395381.

Decisions

  • dauer larval development (IGI) was changed (MODIFY) to negative regulation of dauer entry.
  • Behavioral and NMJ annotations: KEEP_AS_NON_CORE.
