# Functional annotation report: *Caenorhabditis elegans nud-2* (UniProt O45717)

## Executive conclusion

The target is correctly identified as ***C. elegans nud-2***, ORF **R11A5.2**, encoding protein NUD-2 (UniProt **O45717**). The experimental literature identifies it as the worm’s sole NudE/NudEL-family homolog, consistent with the supplied NUDE-family/domain annotation. It should not be confused with similarly named genes in other organisms. The extensively studied allele **nud-2(ok949)** removes 87% of the open reading frame and is treated as a null/deletion allele. (simoes2018nuderegulatesdynein pages 2-3, rocha2018agenomescalernai pages 1-2)

NUD-2 is **not an enzyme or transporter**. Its primary function is that of a non-catalytic cytoplasmic-dynein regulatory cofactor. The most precisely established role is at mitotic kinetochores and the spindle region, where NUD-2 supports the local accumulation or retention of dynein, dynactin, and LIS-1, thereby promoting timely formation of load-bearing kinetochore–microtubule attachments and faithful chromosome segregation. Under ordinary conditions it is not universally required for every dynein-dependent process in the one-cell embryo. (torisawa2022sequentialaccumulationof pages 4-5, simoes2018nuderegulatesdynein pages 3-4, simoes2018nuderegulatesdynein pages 1-2)

## Identity verification

1. **Gene-symbol match:** The primary literature explicitly names **NUD-2** as the *C. elegans* NudE homolog and, more specifically, the sole worm homolog corresponding to vertebrate NDE1/NDEL1. This agrees with the supplied description “Protein nud-2.” (simoes2018nuderegulatesdynein pages 2-3)
2. **Organism:** All direct functional conclusions below concern ***Caenorhabditis elegans*** embryos or worms, not proteins with similar names in other taxa.
3. **Family/domain agreement:** The reported architecture—a long N-terminal coiled-coil region followed by a less structured C-terminal region—is consistent with the NudE/NudEL family and the supplied NUDE-domain annotation. Worm fragment experiments mapped distinct localization behavior to residues 2–165 and 166–293. (torisawa2022sequentialaccumulationof pages 5-7)
4. **Ambiguity control:** Evidence from mammalian NDE1/NDEL1 or other NudE-family proteins is used only to explain family-level mechanistic models and is explicitly distinguished from direct worm evidence.

## Primary molecular function

NUD-2 acts as a **spatial and temporal regulator of cytoplasmic dynein**, rather than supplying motor activity itself. Dynein’s ATPase and minus-end-directed motility reside in the dynein heavy chain; NUD-2 helps organize or stabilize the dynein regulatory machinery at specific cellular sites. In the best-supported worm pathway, HCP-1/HCP-2 recruit NUD-2 to kinetochores, after which NUD-2 promotes normal local levels and function of dynein–dynactin–LIS-1. (simoes2018nuderegulatesdynein pages 3-4, simoes2018nuderegulatesdynein pages 9-11)

Loss of NUD-2 reduced kinetochore-associated dynein heavy chain DHC-1 to **52±8% of control**, LIS-1 to **65±13%**, and dynactin p50/DNC-2 to **62±8%**, without reducing total cellular abundance. These measurements strongly support a local recruitment or stabilization function rather than control of protein expression. (simoes2018nuderegulatesdynein pages 3-4)

The exact biochemical interface remains less certain. Family studies indicate that NudE/NudEL N-terminal coiled coils bind dynein intermediate chain and LIS1, whereas their C-terminal regions interact with CENP-F-family receptors. A 2023 authoritative review proposed that Nde1/Ndel1 may assist LIS1-dependent dynein–dynactin assembly and optimize force output under load, but emphasized that aspects of this recruitment model remain to be tested. Thus, this is a compelling mechanism consistent with worm genetics, not a complete structural demonstration for the *C. elegans* complex. (gassmann2023dyneinatthe pages 8-9, gassmann2023dyneinatthe pages 5-6)

## Cellular localization and targeting

### Kinetochores

NUD-2::mCherry is enriched at mitotic kinetochores and is also prominent across the spindle. Its kinetochore recruitment requires the CENP-F-like proteins **HCP-1 and HCP-2**: their co-depletion abolishes NUD-2 recruitment. Targeting is independent of dynein, LIS-1, and the RZZ–Spindly component SPDL-1, placing NUD-2 in a kinetochore-recruitment branch distinct from RZZ–Spindly. (simoes2018nuderegulatesdynein pages 9-11, simoes2018nuderegulatesdynein pages 1-2)

Although the functional pathway is often summarized as HCP-1/HCP-2–NUD-2–dynein/LIS-1, the worm work establishes localization dependence more securely than a direct, purified HCP–NUD-2 binding interface. The latter remains partly supported by conservation with the CENP-F–Nde1/Ndel1 system in other animals.

### Nucleus and spindle region

Live imaging showed that NUD-2 begins accumulating in the prospective spindle/nuclear region **before nuclear-envelope breakdown (NEBD)**, earlier than LIS-1/LIN-5, dynactin, and DHC-1. It also exhibited the strongest normalized enrichment among the examined dynein-associated regulators. Pre-NEBD accumulation required **RAN-1**, whereas post-NEBD accumulation did not. (torisawa2022sequentialaccumulationof pages 4-5, torisawa2022sequentialaccumulationof pages 2-3)

NUD-2 depletion did not block the initial arrival of DHC-1, dynactin, or LIS-1 in the spindle region, but impaired their subsequent maintenance. LIN-5 and tubulin accumulation were not detectably reduced. These observations refine the annotation from generic “dynein recruitment factor” to a factor that is particularly important for **retention or stable enrichment** of selected dynein machinery in the spindle region. (torisawa2022sequentialaccumulationof pages 4-5)

## Domain and structural interpretation

Recombinant-fragment injection provided worm-specific localization mapping. The N-terminal coiled-coil fragment, **residues 2–165**, did not reproduce pre-NEBD accumulation. The C-terminal fragment, **residues 166–293**, retained both pre- and post-NEBD accumulation, and constructs containing the C-terminal helix supported early accumulation. The C-terminal helix is therefore an experimentally supported determinant of NUD-2’s unusual pre-NEBD localization. (torisawa2022sequentialaccumulationof pages 5-7)

This does not mean the C terminus is the only functional region. Based on conserved NudE/NudEL architecture, the N-terminal coiled coil is expected to participate in dynein/LIS-1 regulation. However, a complete structure of worm NUD-2 in complex with DHC-1, LIS-1, dynactin, or HCP-1/HCP-2 has not been reported in the retrieved literature. Family-level binding assignments should therefore not be presented as fully resolved worm-specific biochemistry. (gassmann2023dyneinatthe pages 8-9, gassmann2023dyneinatthe pages 5-6)

## Biological processes and pathway placement

### Kinetochore–microtubule attachment and chromosome segregation

The clearest biological role is in **mitotic chromosome segregation**. NUD-2 loss delays the establishment of load-bearing kinetochore–microtubule attachments and can produce lagging or bridged chromatin during anaphase. In one experiment, lagging anaphase chromatin occurred in **4/20 Δnud-2 embryos**, compared with **0/18 controls**. (simoes2018nuderegulatesdynein pages 3-4)

The phenotype resembles inhibition of the parallel RZZ–Spindly kinetochore-dynein pathway. Combining Δnud-2 with *rod-1* RNAi did not markedly enhance the attachment phenotype, consistent with the pathways ultimately supporting a common kinetochore dynein machinery rather than wholly independent motor pools. (simoes2018nuderegulatesdynein pages 11-11, simoes2018nuderegulatesdynein pages 3-4)

### Spindle-assembly checkpoint

Reduced kinetochore dynein after NUD-2 loss increases recruitment of spindle-assembly-checkpoint proteins. The SAC consequently becomes important for mutant survival by delaying cell-cycle progression while attachment errors are corrected. At the 16–32-cell stage, Δnud-2 mitosis was approximately **20% longer** than control, whereas no comparable delay was evident in the one-cell embryo. This developmental-stage dependence illustrates that a strengthening SAC buffers a relatively mild dynein defect and suppresses chromosome mis-segregation. (simoes2018nuderegulatesdynein pages 11-11, simoes2018nuderegulatesdynein pages 8-9)

### Other dynein-dependent processes

NUD-2 is not an obligatory component of all embryonic dynein functions. In otherwise unperturbed Δnud-2 one-cell embryos, centrosome separation and positioning, male-pronucleus migration, pronuclear centration, spindle assembly, and asymmetric spindle positioning were essentially normal. A small nuclear–centrosome-orientation delay was corrected by anaphase. NUD-2 is therefore best described as a context-selective dynein cofactor whose strongest demonstrated requirement is at kinetochores, not as an essential core motor subunit. (simoes2018nuderegulatesdynein pages 3-4, simoes2018nuderegulatesdynein pages 2-3, simoes2018nuderegulatesdynein pages 9-11)

The apparent dispensability is conditional. Partial *lis-1* RNAi produced **46±7% survival** in a NUD-2-positive background but only **0±1% viability** in Δnud-2 animals and impaired pronuclear meeting. This strong synthetic interaction indicates that abundant LIS-1 or other regulatory routes normally compensate for NUD-2 loss in several dynein-dependent processes. Genetic synergy supports functional cooperation but does not, by itself, prove direct physical binding. (simoes2018nuderegulatesdynein pages 2-3)

## Genetic interaction landscape and experimental applications

The viable and fertile **nud-2(ok949)** mutant has been used as a sensitized background to discover factors that support dynein function. A genome-scale feeding-RNAi screen examined **19,762 bacterial clones**, representing approximately **87% of annotated worm ORFs**. Of 1,077 initial candidates with at least a twofold relative reproductive-fitness defect, secondary screening yielded **38 validated enhancers** of nud-2-associated lethality. These included **13 microtubule-cytoskeleton genes**, **four known dynein-binding partners**, and **19 genes implicated in embryonic cell division**. (rocha2018agenomescalernai pages 1-2)

This represents the principal real-world research application of the gene: NUD-2 is a tractable model for dissecting conserved dynein regulation, kinetochore attachment, chromosome segregation, and genetic buffering. The screen’s hits are genetic interactors, not necessarily direct NUD-2-binding proteins; biochemical and imaging validation is required before assigning physical interaction. (rocha2018agenomescalernai pages 1-2)

## Recent developments and current expert interpretation

The most recent major direct mechanistic advance identified was the 2022 live-imaging and fragment-injection study, which established sequential recruitment of dynein regulators, RAN-1-dependent pre-NEBD enrichment of NUD-2, a retention role for spindle-associated dynein machinery, and involvement of the C-terminal helix. Published July 2022: [Torisawa and Kimura, *Scientific Reports* 12:11740](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-022-15042-8). (torisawa2022sequentialaccumulationof pages 4-5, torisawa2022sequentialaccumulationof pages 5-7)

A March 2023 review integrated worm findings into the broader CENP-F–Nde1/Ndel1–Lis1 pathway. Its expert interpretation is that NudE-family proteins may tether or position LIS1 near dynein, assist assembly of active dynein–dynactin complexes, and optimize force under load. Importantly, it identifies unresolved questions, including the exact contribution of the Lis1–Nde1/Ndel1 interaction to kinetochore recruitment. [Gassmann, *Journal of Cell Science* 136, published March 2023](https://doi.org/10.1242/jcs.220269). (gassmann2023dyneinatthe pages 8-9, gassmann2023dyneinatthe pages 5-6)

The 2023 oocyte-meiosis literature mentions nud-2 in testing kinetochore mechanisms, but the retrieved evidence does not support replacing the established embryonic-mitosis annotation with a major meiosis-specific role. No 2024 publication directly advancing the molecular annotation of worm NUD-2 was identified in the targeted search. The absence of a 2024-specific advance should be interpreted as a sparse direct literature base, not evidence that the protein lacks additional functions.

## Recommended functional annotation

**Short annotation:** “NUD-2 is the sole *C. elegans* NudE/NudEL-family, non-enzymatic cytoplasmic-dynein cofactor. It is recruited to mitotic kinetochores through HCP-1/HCP-2 and accumulates in the spindle region before and after nuclear-envelope breakdown. NUD-2 promotes stable local enrichment of dynein, dynactin, and LIS-1, facilitating kinetochore–microtubule attachment and chromosome-segregation fidelity; its loss is partly buffered by LIS-1, parallel dynein-recruitment mechanisms, and the spindle-assembly checkpoint.”

**Primary cellular sites:** mitotic kinetochores; nucleus/prospective spindle region before NEBD; spindle region after NEBD. (torisawa2022sequentialaccumulationof pages 4-5, simoes2018nuderegulatesdynein pages 3-4, simoes2018nuderegulatesdynein pages 2-3)

**Primary pathway:** HCP-1/HCP-2/CENP-F-like kinetochore branch → NUD-2 → local dynein–dynactin–LIS-1 regulation → kinetochore–microtubule attachment and chromosome segregation, with functional interplay with RZZ–Spindly and the SAC. (simoes2018nuderegulatesdynein pages 11-11, simoes2018nuderegulatesdynein pages 9-11, simoes2018nuderegulatesdynein pages 1-2)

**Catalysis or substrate specificity:** none known or expected; NUD-2 is a structural/regulatory cofactor rather than an enzyme or transporter.

**Confidence:** high for identity, kinetochore/spindle localization, HCP-1/HCP-2-dependent targeting, effects on local dynein machinery, and chromosome-fidelity function; moderate for the detailed molecular assembly mechanism because several binding and force-generation models derive from conserved-family studies rather than complete biochemical reconstitution of worm NUD-2.
