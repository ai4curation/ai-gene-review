# Functional annotation report: *C. elegans unc-32* / UniProt P30628

## Executive summary

The requested identity is verified. In *Caenorhabditis elegans*, **unc-32 is ZK637.8 and encodes a large, multipass membrane subunit-a protein of the V0 sector of vacuolar H+-ATPase (V-ATPase)**. Positional mapping, transformation rescue, and mutations within ZK637.8 established the assignment; phylogenetic analysis places UNC-32 closest to vertebrate a1/ATP6V0A1. This agrees with UniProt P30628, the supplied V-ATPase 116-kDa-subunit family assignment, and the V-ATPase_116kDa/PF01496 domains. No literature concerning a different “UNC-32” gene was used. (pujol2001thecaenorhabditiselegans pages 7-7, pujol2001thecaenorhabditiselegans pages 3-3, pujol2001thecaenorhabditiselegans pages 3-4)

UNC-32 is not itself the ATP-hydrolyzing enzyme. Its primary function is to form the **subunit-a component of the membrane V0 proton-translocation apparatus**. In the assembled V-ATPase, ATP hydrolysis by cytosolic V1 drives rotation of the V0 c-ring, while subunit a supplies offset aqueous hemichannels through which **H+—the transported substrate—moves from cytosol to organelle lumen**. The resulting acidification supports neurotransmitter loading into synaptic vesicles and degradative activity in endosomes, lysosomes, and autolysosomes. The biochemical mechanism is strongly supported by conserved V-ATPase structures, whereas UNC-32-specific transport kinetics have not been measured. (indrawinata2023structuralandfunctional pages 5-6, indrawinata2023structuralandfunctional pages 2-4)

Direct worm evidence indicates substantial isoform specialization. At least six splice products are generated; exon-4b-containing forms are preferentially neuronal and are selectively disrupted by the locomotion allele *e189*. Other isoforms are broadly expressed and essential for embryogenesis and viability. Direct RNAi evidence additionally links UNC-32 to lysosomal hydrolysis and autophagic clearance. Recent 2024 work detected UNC-32 in lysosome-associated proteomic data and showed that *unc-32(e189)* confers strong resistance to nematicidal Cry14A-family proteins. (pujol2001thecaenorhabditiselegans pages 7-7, pujol2001thecaenorhabditiselegans pages 4-5, bott2021variantsinatp6v0a1 pages 9-10, yu2024organelleproteomicprofiling pages 11-11, kim2024resistancetocry14a pages 8-9)

## 1. Identity, family, and gene architecture

### 1.1 Mandatory identity verification

The original molecular characterization mapped *unc-32* to a roughly 9-kb interval containing ZK637.8. Transformation rescue and sequencing of four independent mutant alleles—revealing splice-site or coding lesions—confirmed that ZK637.8 is the *unc-32* locus. The gene is the first member of an operon with ZK637.9 and ZK637.10 and is SL1 trans-spliced. (pujol2001thecaenorhabditiselegans pages 3-3, pujol2001thecaenorhabditiselegans pages 3-4)

UNC-32 is one of four *C. elegans* V-ATPase subunit-a proteins, alongside VHA-5, VHA-6, and VHA-7. UNC-32A showed 55% amino-acid identity and 69% similarity to murine a1, compared with lower similarity to murine a2 and a3, supporting its annotation as the worm a1-like protein. Thus, the correct conceptual ortholog is mammalian **ATP6V0A1**, although paralog expansion and isoform specialization preclude assuming that every mammalian a1 property has been demonstrated in UNC-32. (pujol2001thecaenorhabditiselegans pages 5-7)

### 1.2 Alternative splicing

Every transcript analyzed in the foundational study contained ten exons, but mutually alternative exons 4a/4b/4c and 7a/7b generated at least six proteins:

- A: 4a/7b
- B: 4b/7a
- C: 4c/7b
- D: 4a/7a
- E: 4c/7a
- F: 4b/7b

Their predicted masses range from approximately 100.7 to 103.4 kDa. The discrepancy between these values and the historical family designation “116 kDa” reflects nomenclature rather than conflicting identity. The 2001 sequence analysis predicted nine membrane-spanning regions. Modern mammalian structures instead describe a cytosolic N-terminal domain and a C-terminal membrane domain containing eight helices; therefore, UNC-32 should confidently be called a multipass V0 subunit, but its exact topology has not been experimentally resolved in the worm. (pujol2001thecaenorhabditiselegans pages 4-5, pujol2001thecaenorhabditiselegans pages 3-4, indrawinata2023structuralandfunctional pages 5-6)

## 2. Primary molecular function

### 2.1 Reaction and substrate

The complete V-ATPase catalyzes the coupled process:

**ATP + H2O + H+cytosol → ADP + Pi + H+organelle lumen**

UNC-32 does **not** contain the catalytic ATP-hydrolysis site; those sites lie at V1 A/B-subunit interfaces. UNC-32’s role is transporter-associated: its membrane C-terminal region forms the stationary subunit-a interface and two offset hemichannels that allow proton access to and exit from the rotating c-ring. Accordingly, the transported substrate is **the proton, H+**, and the physiological output is electrochemical acidification of the vesicle or organelle lumen. (indrawinata2023structuralandfunctional pages 5-6, indrawinata2023structuralandfunctional pages 2-4)

### 2.2 Mechanism inferred from conserved structures

Current structural understanding is as follows:

1. A proton enters the cytosolic subunit-a hemichannel and protonates a conserved glutamate on a c-ring proteolipid.
2. ATP hydrolysis in V1 rotates the central stalk and c-ring.
3. Rotation moves the protonated glutamate through the membrane to the luminal subunit-a hemichannel.
4. A conserved subunit-a arginine forms an electrostatic interaction with the c-ring glutamate, promoting deprotonation.
5. The proton exits through a polar residue network into the lumen.

For mammalian V-ATPase, the reported stoichiometry is about **10 H+ translocated per three ATP hydrolyzed**. This value is an authoritative structural/mechanistic estimate for the conserved complex, not a direct measurement for purified UNC-32. Likewise, the importance of human a1 R740 and other hemichannel residues is highly informative for UNC-32 annotation but has not been tested residue-by-residue in the worm protein. (indrawinata2023structuralandfunctional pages 5-6, indrawinata2023structuralandfunctional pages 2-4)

## 3. Cellular and tissue localization

### 3.1 Neurons and synaptic vesicles

The strongest isoform-specific evidence concerns exon-4b forms. The *e189* mutation disrupts the exon-4b splice acceptor, activates a cryptic acceptor 22 nucleotides upstream, and introduces a premature stop, while exon-4a and exon-4c transcripts remain available. An exon-4b reporter was preferentially expressed in the nervous system, including motor neurons. *e189* animals have uncoordinated movement, abnormal synaptic-vesicle morphology, and low but reproducible resistance to acetylcholinesterase inhibitors. Their motor-circuit defect was not accompanied by an obvious wiring defect. (pujol2001thecaenorhabditiselegans pages 7-7, pujol2001thecaenorhabditiselegans pages 4-5, pujol2001thecaenorhabditiselegans pages 5-7)

These results support a model in which UNC-32B and/or UNC-32F targets V-ATPase to neuronal secretory vesicles. Acidification supplies the proton electrochemical gradient used by vesicular neurotransmitter transporters; in cholinergic motor neurons, defective acidification would reduce acetylcholine accumulation and consequently synaptic output. This interpretation fits the phenotype, but the original study did not directly image endogenous UNC-32 on single synaptic vesicles or measure vesicular pH or acetylcholine uptake. Thus, “synaptic-vesicle acidification” is a strong mechanistic model rather than a completed biochemical demonstration. (pujol2001thecaenorhabditiselegans pages 7-7, pujol2001thecaenorhabditiselegans pages 1-1)

### 3.2 Endolysosomal compartments

Direct functional evidence supports an endolysosomal/autophagic role. *unc-32* RNAi increased LGG-1::mCherry reporter accumulation without raising *lgg-1* transcription, consistent with reduced autophagic processing or clearance rather than simply increased autophagosome production. Knockdown also reduced *atg-7* and *atg-16.2*, lowered transcripts encoding lysosomal hydrolytic enzymes (*asp-1, cpr-4, cpr-5,* and *imp-2*), and increased the stress-responsive autophagy receptor *sqst-1*. These findings connect UNC-32-dependent V-ATPase function to lysosomal hydrolysis and autophagic flux. (bott2021variantsinatp6v0a1 pages 9-10)

The 2024 *C. elegans* lysosome-proteomics study also included UNC-32 among lysosome-associated V-ATPase components. This strengthens the localization assignment using an orthogonal method. However, the available evidence does not show UNC-32-specific abundance statistics or establish that UNC-32 itself causes the reported tissue or positional heterogeneity of lysosomes. (yu2024organelleproteomicprofiling pages 11-11)

### 3.3 Broader expression

Expression begins at the one-cell embryo and remains high through gastrulation. Reporters and transcript analyses identify expression in head neurons, ventral-cord motor neurons, intestine, gonad, excretory cell and canals, and probable somatic-gonad cells. This distribution is consistent with use in several acidic organelle classes rather than a neuron-exclusive role. It also suggests that alternative splicing helps determine tissue or membrane targeting. (pujol2001thecaenorhabditiselegans pages 7-7, pujol2001thecaenorhabditiselegans pages 5-7)

## 4. Biological pathways

### 4.1 Vesicular neurotransmission

UNC-32 acts upstream of neurotransmitter loading by establishing vesicular proton motive force. In cholinergic neurons, that gradient is expected to energize vesicular acetylcholine uptake. The abnormal-vesicle and locomotion phenotypes of *e189* support this pathway assignment. A proposed additional V0 role in membrane fusion or exocytosis is plausible from broader V-ATPase literature, but it has not been cleanly separated from loss of proton pumping for UNC-32 itself. (pujol2001thecaenorhabditiselegans pages 7-7, indrawinata2023structuralandfunctional pages 5-6)

### 4.2 Lysosome–autophagy pathway

By acidifying endosomes, lysosomes, and autolysosomes, UNC-32-containing V-ATPase promotes activation of acid hydrolases and degradation of autophagic cargo. The worm RNAi phenotype—LGG-1 accumulation, increased *sqst-1*, and reduced lysosomal-hydrolase expression—is consistent with impaired degradative flux. The 2023 expert review emphasizes that a1-subunit defects compromise lysosomal acidification and autophagy and can cause neuronal protein accumulation and degeneration. (bott2021variantsinatp6v0a1 pages 9-10, indrawinata2023structuralandfunctional pages 14-15, indrawinata2023structuralandfunctional pages 2-4)

### 4.3 Development and membrane homeostasis

Strong alleles demonstrate that non-neuronal isoforms are essential. The null-like *f123* allele causes late-embryonic arrest, intestinal vacuolization, maternal-effect lethality, and early embryonic adhesion defects; *f121* causes larval lethality, while *f131* can produce severe paralysis and abnormal body morphology. These outcomes likely reflect failure of acidification-dependent trafficking and degradation across several tissues, although individual defects have not all been assigned to a particular organelle or splice isoform. (pujol2001thecaenorhabditiselegans pages 3-3, pujol2001thecaenorhabditiselegans pages 3-4)

## 5. Recent developments and applications

### 5.1 Lysosome profiling and aging research—2024

Yu et al. developed deep organelle proteomic profiling to analyze lysosome composition across tissues and longevity paradigms in living adult worms. Detection of UNC-32 as a lysosome-associated V-ATPase component provides recent systems-level confirmation that it participates in the lysosomal apparatus. The larger study establishes that lysosome protein composition is heterogeneous and dynamically linked to signaling and longevity, but no UNC-32-specific longevity requirement was demonstrated in the extracted evidence. Publication: January 2024; DOI: https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.85214. (yu2024organelleproteomicprofiling pages 11-11, yu2024organelleproteomicprofiling pages 24-25)

### 5.2 Nematode biocontrol and Cry14A intoxication—2024

Kim et al. tested V-ATPase involvement in susceptibility to *Bacillus thuringiensis* Cry14A nematicidal proteins. The partial-loss *unc-32(e189)* mutant showed high resistance to both Cry14Aa and Cry14Ab in viability-based dose-response assays; each graph summarized three independent experiments. This establishes a real-world application of UNC-32 biology to the durability and resistance mechanisms of nematicidal Bt proteins used in crop protection. Publication: October 18, 2024; DOI: https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.ppat.1012611. (kim2024resistancetocry14a pages 8-9)

The same study found that 18 V-ATPase-subunit transcripts were downregulated in *nhr-31(ye123)* animals. However, *unc-32* and *vha-7* showed comparatively weak dependence on NHR-31, cautioning against describing UNC-32 as a strongly or directly controlled NHR-31 target. The Cry-resistance mechanism remains unresolved: altered receptor production or reduced proton-dependent cytotoxic responses were proposed, not demonstrated. (kim2024resistancetocry14a pages 8-9)

### 5.3 Translational disease modeling

Human ATP6V0A1 variants cause developmental and epileptic encephalopathy or progressive myoclonus epilepsy. Worm *unc-32* knockdown and variant modeling have been used to connect a1 dysfunction to failed lysosomal hydrolysis, defective autophagy, and developmental abnormalities. In the reported human cohort, 17 affected individuals from 14 families were described; recurrent R740Q directly impaired endolysosomal acidification. These findings do not redefine native UNC-32 function, but they validate *C. elegans* as a functional model for conserved a1-dependent lysosomal biology. (bott2021variantsinatp6v0a1 pages 9-10, indrawinata2023structuralandfunctional pages 14-15, indrawinata2023structuralandfunctional pages 2-4)

## 6. Expert interpretation and confidence assessment

The most defensible annotation is:

> **UNC-32 is the a1-like membrane subunit of the *C. elegans* V-ATPase V0 sector. It enables ATP-coupled H+ translocation into synaptic vesicles and endolysosomal organelles, thereby supporting vesicular neurotransmitter loading, lysosomal hydrolysis, autophagic clearance, membrane trafficking, and development. Alternative splicing generates neuronal and broadly expressed forms with partially distinct functions.**

Confidence is **high** for gene identity, V0-subunit family membership, alternative splicing, multipass membrane architecture, broad expression, and essentiality. Confidence is also high that H+ is the transported substrate of the assembled complex. Confidence is **moderate-to-high** for synaptic-vesicle and lysosomal localization because it is supported by allele-specific neuronal phenotypes, autophagy perturbation, and lysosome proteomics, but endogenous isoform-resolved localization remains incomplete. Exact proton/ATP stoichiometry, hemichannel residues, and rotary mechanism are **strong conserved inferences**, not UNC-32-specific biochemical measurements. (pujol2001thecaenorhabditiselegans pages 7-7, bott2021variantsinatp6v0a1 pages 9-10, yu2024organelleproteomicprofiling pages 11-11, indrawinata2023structuralandfunctional pages 5-6, indrawinata2023structuralandfunctional pages 2-4)

## 7. Major unresolved questions

1. **Isoform-resolved localization:** endogenous tagging is needed to determine which of UNC-32A–F occupies synaptic vesicles, endosomes, lysosomes, or other membranes.
2. **Direct transport measurements:** no purified UNC-32 isoform has been assayed for H+ flux, turnover, coupling efficiency, or inhibitor sensitivity.
3. **Organelle physiology:** direct measurements of synaptic-vesicle and lysosomal pH in isoform-specific mutants would test the central acidification model.
4. **Transport versus trafficking:** experiments that uncouple proton conductance from proposed V0-dependent fusion functions are needed.
5. **Regulation:** the targeting motifs, assembly factors, and splice regulators that allocate UNC-32 isoforms among tissues and organelles remain incompletely defined.
6. **Cry14A mechanism:** although *unc-32(e189)* produces strong resistance, whether this reflects receptor trafficking, luminal pH, membrane repair, or a proton-dependent death pathway is unknown. (pujol2001thecaenorhabditiselegans pages 4-5, bott2021variantsinatp6v0a1 pages 9-10, kim2024resistancetocry14a pages 8-9)
