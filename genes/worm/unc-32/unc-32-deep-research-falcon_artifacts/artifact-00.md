| Identity | *C. elegans* **unc-32** is **ZK637.8**, encoding a V-ATPase membrane-sector V0 subunit-a protein most similar to vertebrate a1/ATP6V0A1; this matches UniProt **P30628** and the V-ATPase 116-kDa subunit family. | **Direct:** positional mapping, transformation rescue, and mutations in ZK637.8; **comparative:** phylogeny and sequence similarity. (pqac-00000000, pqac-00000006, pqac-00000007) | Pujol et al., **April 2001**, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1074/jbc.m009451200) | *C. elegans* also has *vha-5*, *vha-6*, and *vha-7* subunit-a genes; general subunit-a findings are not automatically UNC-32-specific. |
| Isoforms and topology | Alternative use of exons **4a/4b/4c** and **7a/7b** produces at least **six isoforms (A-F)** with predicted masses of **100.7-103.4 kDa**; the original worm analysis predicted **nine membrane-spanning regions**. | **Direct transcript evidence** plus **bioinformatic topology prediction**. (pqac-00000001, pqac-00000007) | Pujol et al., **April 2001**, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1074/jbc.m009451200) | The family name “116 kDa” differs from the predicted worm masses. Modern a1 structures resolve eight helices in the membrane-embedded C-terminal domain, so the historical nine-pass prediction is not experimentally resolved topology. |
| Proton substrate and rotary mechanism | The transported substrate is **H+**. UNC-32 is not the ATP-hydrolyzing catalytic subunit; as V0 subunit a, it is inferred to form cytosolic and luminal hemichannels coupling V1-driven c-ring rotation to proton movement from cytosol into an organelle lumen. Conserved stoichiometry is approximately **10 H+ per 3 ATP**. | **Conserved mechanistic inference** from mammalian and yeast structural and biochemical studies, not direct UNC-32 kinetics. (pqac-00000010, pqac-00000012) | Indrawinata et al., **July 2023**, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnmol.2023.1135015) | No purified UNC-32 assay has established turnover, H+/ATP ratio, coupling efficiency, or isoform-specific kinetics. |
| Neuronal and synaptic-vesicle role | Exon-4b-containing transcripts, including **UNC-32B**, are preferentially neuronal. The *e189* splice-site allele disrupts these transcripts and causes uncoordinated movement, abnormal synaptic-vesicle morphology, and mild acetylcholinesterase-inhibitor resistance, supporting a role in cholinergic vesicle acidification and acetylcholine loading or release. | **Direct:** isoform reporter, allele-specific genetics, phenotype, and vesicle morphology. Vesicle targeting and impaired acetylcholine loading are interpretations. (pqac-00000000, pqac-00000001, pqac-00000002) | Pujol et al., **April 2001**, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1074/jbc.m009451200) | Localization to individual synaptic vesicles, vesicular pH, and acetylcholine uptake were not measured directly. |
| Broader expression and essentiality | *unc-32* is expressed from the **one-cell embryo through gastrulation** and in head neurons, ventral-cord motor neurons, intestine, gonad, excretory cell and canals, and probable somatic-gonad cells. Severe alleles cause embryonic or larval lethality, intestinal vacuolization, adhesion defects, and maternal-effect lethality. | **Direct:** transcript and reporter expression, mutant analysis, and rescue. (pqac-00000000, pqac-00000002, pqac-00000006, pqac-00000007) | Pujol et al., **April 2001**, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1074/jbc.m009451200) | Reporter expression does not identify the organelle containing each isoform; severe phenotypes may include secondary consequences. |
| Lysosomes and autophagy | *unc-32* RNAi increased LGG-1::mCherry accumulation without increasing *lgg-1* transcription, reduced *atg-7* and *atg-16.2*, lowered lysosomal-hydrolase transcripts *asp-1*, *cpr-4*, *cpr-5*, and *imp-2*, and increased *sqst-1*, supporting impaired lysosomal and autophagic processing. | **Direct worm perturbation and reporter/transcript evidence**; acidification is supported by conserved V-ATPase biology. (pqac-00000003) | Bott et al., **October 2021**, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/braincomms/fcab245) | Increased LGG-1 signal can reflect blocked clearance rather than increased autophagy; isoform-specific lysosomal pH and proton flux were not reported. |
| 2024 lysosome proteomics | UNC-32 appeared among V-ATPase components in a deep *C. elegans* lysosome-associated proteomic framework, supporting association with lysosomal membranes. | **Direct proteomic association**, but not an UNC-32-focused functional perturbation. (pqac-00000008) | Yu et al., **January 2024**, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.85214) | The available evidence does not provide UNC-32-specific abundance, microscopy, pH effects, or proof that it determines lysosomal heterogeneity or longevity. |
| 2024 Cry14A resistance | The partial-loss allele **unc-32(e189)** produced high resistance to nematicidal **Cry14Aa and Cry14Ab** in viability-based dose-response assays averaged across **three independent experiments**, indicating that intact V-ATPase function contributes to Cry14A intoxication. | **Direct mutant evidence** in a biocontrol-resistance model. (pqac-00000015, pqac-00000016) | Kim et al., **October 18, 2024**, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.ppat.1012611) | Exact resistance values were unavailable in the extracted text, and the mechanism remains unresolved; altered receptor production and proton-dependent toxicity were proposed rather than demonstrated. |
| Regulatory context | NHR-31 regulates much of the V-ATPase transcriptional program; **18 V-ATPase-subunit transcripts** were reduced in *nhr-31(ye123)* animals. *unc-32* and *vha-7* showed comparatively weak dependence on NHR-31, yet mutations in either conferred Cry14A resistance. | **Direct RNA-seq and genetics**, with pathway-level interpretation. (pqac-00000015, pqac-00000016) | Kim et al., **October 18, 2024**, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.ppat.1012611) | The data do not establish direct NHR-31 binding to the *unc-32* promoter or strong transcriptional control of *unc-32*. |
| Major knowledge gaps | Priorities include endogenous isoform-resolved localization, direct vesicular and lysosomal pH measurements, purified UNC-32 kinetics, targeting motifs and binding partners, and separation of proton-pump activity from proposed V0 roles in fusion or trafficking. | **Evidence-gap assessment** contrasting worm genetics with conserved structural literature. (pqac-00000000, pqac-00000003, pqac-00000010, pqac-00000012) | Evidence reviewed through **2024** | The primary annotation is high confidence, but many compartment- and isoform-specific assignments remain inferential. |
