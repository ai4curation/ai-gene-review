Gene Description

TTR-52 is a secreted transthyretin-like protein of C. elegans that bridges apoptotic cells to engulfing cells. It is made in and secreted from the endoderm and clusters around apoptotic cells. There it binds surface-exposed phosphatidylserine (PS) on the dying cell and the N-terminal EMI domain of the engulfment receptor CED-1 on the engulfing cell. Its crystal structure shows an open beta-barrel resembling a C2 domain, and it forms dimers that are needed for function in vivo. TTR-52 acts in the CED-1/CED-6/CED-7 pathway and is needed for CED-1 to cluster around corpses. With CED-7 and the lipid-binding protein NRF-5, it also takes part in the release of PS-containing extracellular vesicles from apoptotic cells and in PS appearance on engulfing cells.

Suggested Questions for Experts

Q: Does TTR-52 carry PS between membranes (as an acceptor or carrier, possibly with NRF-5), or does it only bind PS-bearing vesicles?

Q: Does TTR-52, like CED-1, CED-6 and CED-7, contribute to engulfing cells promoting the death of weakly signalled cells?

Suggested Experiments

Experiment: In a weak ced-3 or ced-4 partial loss-of-function background, compare survival of specific cells such as the extra pharyngeal cells in ttr-52 mutants versus ced-1 mutants, scored with cell-type reporters.

Hypothesis: TTR-52 promotes the death of cells receiving weak death signals, as other CED-1 pathway genes do.
