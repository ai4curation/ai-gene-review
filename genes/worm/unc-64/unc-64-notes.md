# unc-64 (UniProt O16000) — Syntaxin-1A homolog, C. elegans

## Identity and structure
- Reviewed Swiss-Prot entry STX1A_CAEEL (O16000), 291 aa, WormBase F56A8.7. Cytoplasmic N-terminal Habc/coiled-coil region, SNARE (H3/t-SNARE) domain at 193-255 and a single C-terminal transmembrane anchor at 267-287 (from `unc-64-uniprot.txt`).
- Three transcripts / two protein isoforms differing only in the C-terminal membrane anchor [PMID:9614171 "unc-64 expresses three transcripts, each encoding a molecule with 63–64% identity to human syntaxin 1A, a membrane- anchored protein involved in synaptic vesicle fusion"]. Isoforms A and C are neuronal plus secretory tissues; B is non-neuronal only [PMID:9614171 "Syntaxin A and C were expressed in neurons and in five nonneuronal tissues. By contrast, expression of syntaxin B was limited to the five nonneuronal cell types."].

## Core function: plasma-membrane Qa-SNARE for Ca2+-triggered neurotransmitter release
- Genetic identity: unc-64 is one of the ACh-accumulating, aldicarb-resistant "unc" genes together with unc-17, unc-18, unc-13 and unc-11 [PMID:1945043 "We have now identified 3 more such genes (unc-63, unc-11 and unc-64)"]; the mutants share locomotion, AChE-inhibitor-resistance and post-embryonic development phenotypes.
- Null (js115) animals complete embryogenesis but arrest as paralysed L1 larvae [PMID:9614171 "A complete loss-of-function mutation in unc-64 results in a worm that completes embryogenesis, but arrests development shortly thereafter as a paralyzed L1 larva, presumably as a consequence of neuronal dysfunction."]. Cuticle secretion and muscle function do not require unc-64, in contrast to Drosophila syntaxin [PMID:9614171 "Most notably, secretion of first larval cuticle from the hypodermis is normal in the syntaxin null mutant."].
- Hypomorphic lesions map to the hydrophobic face of the H3 SNARE helix and show allele-specific genetic interactions with snb-1 synaptobrevin SNARE-domain mutants, in vivo evidence for the SNARE four-helix-bundle mechanism [PMID:9614171 "the unc-64 syntaxin mutants exhibit allele-specific genetic interactions with mutants carrying lesions in the coiled-coil domain of synaptobrevin, providing in vivo evidence for the significance of these domains in regulating synaptic vesicle fusion"].
- Constitutively open UNC-64 (L166A/E167A) supports wild-type release and bypasses the priming factors UNC-13 and UNC-10/RIM, but still needs the other SNAREs and Ca2+ [PMID:12973353 "Neurotransmitter release via open syntaxin requires the normal presynaptic machinery, such as other SNARE proteins and calcium, but open syntaxin bypasses the requirement of proteins that function in vesicle priming, such as UNC-13 and Rim"].
- Binds the SM protein UNC-18 with high affinity; the e246 (A->V) lesion in the C-terminal region reduces binding [PMID:9442061 "C. elegans syntaxin binds to UNC-18 with high affinity."]. UNC-18 binds both closed syntaxin and the syntaxin N-peptide; N-terminal-mode binding is what matters for release [PMID:18596236 "UNC-18 binding to the N terminus of Syntaxin was required for normal locomotion behavior and neurotransmitter release."]. PKC-2 phosphorylation of UNC-18 Ser322 reduces closed-conformation binding to the UNC-64 cytoplasmic domain [PMID:22593072 "In a binding assay with the full cytoplasmic domain of C. elegans syntaxin (UNC-64), we found that UNC-18 S322E indeed had a specific reduction in binding as predicted"].

## Localization
- Immunogold: syntaxin is on the axonal plasma membrane [PMID:12973353 "immunogold labeling of syntaxin on ultra-thin sections showed that syntaxin is associated with axonal plasma membranes in both wild-type and unc-18 mutant"].
- Uniform distribution along axons, dendrites and cell bodies, not concentrated at release sites, unlike the vesicle markers SNB-1, SNT-1 and RAB-3 [PMID:9614171 "In neurons, syntaxin A and C were found ubiquitously on neuronal cell bodies, axons, and dendrites."; "C. elegans syntaxin does not appear to be concentrated at release sites, as detected by either antibodies or a GFP tag."].
- Non-neuronal expression in secretory tissues: intestine, spermatheca, uv1 vulval cells, excretory gland, distal tip cells [PMID:9614171 "Immunoreactivity was strong in the uv1 cells of the vulva, in the intestine (Figure 2 C), and in the spermatheca (Figure 2 D)."]. The paper does not itself image a basolateral pool; it only infers a secretory role from yolk being released across the intestinal basolateral surface [PMID:9614171 "yolk in C. elegans is synthesized in the intestine and secreted from the basolateral surface into the pseudocoelom"].
- ER exit / anterograde transport depends on UNC-18 (chaperone-like role) and on the UNC-76/FEZ1-UNC-116/kinesin-1 transport complex: in unc-18 mutants UNC-64 accumulates in the ER of cell bodies [PMID:18596236 "Together, these studies indicate that UNC-64 accumulates in the ER in unc-18 mutants, resulting in decreased anterograde trafficking of UNC-64 to axons."]; in unc-76 mutants GFP-UNC-64 clusters in axons [PMID:22451907 "Together, these results demonstrate that UNC-76 functions as an adaptor that links UNC-64 to UNC-116 in anterograde axonal trafficking."]. Cell-body (perikaryon) signal is dim in wild type [PMID:18596236 "In wild-type animals, bright uniform anti-UNC-64 immunostaining was observed in all neuronal processes, with a small number of dimly stained neuronal cell bodies."].

## Non-synaptic / organismal roles
- Dauer formation and lifespan: unc-64(e246) is dauer-constitutive at 27 C and long-lived, both suppressed by daf-16, placing unc-64 in the daf-2/age-1 insulin pathway; the effect is neuronal and is interpreted as Ca2+-regulated secretion of an insulin-like DAF-2 ligand rather than general synaptic dysfunction (snt-1, snb-1, unc-13 mutants are not Daf-c) [PMID:10377425 "These data place unc-64 and unc-31 in the genetic pathway of the daf-2 and age-1 genes."; "The simplest model for unc-64 and unc-31 function in regulating life span and dauer formation is that they are involved in the Ca 2+ -regulated secretion of an insulin-like ligand for the DAF-2 receptor."].
- Axon guidance: unc-64 single mutants have no AVG pioneer-axon defects but enhance nid-1 defects in the same pathway as aex-3/unc-31, probably via release of precursor vesicles in the growth cone [PMID:27116976 "Neither unc-31 nor unc-64 single mutants have AVG axon cross-over defects; however, both mutants enhance AVG defects of nid-1 mutants but do not enhance aex-3 mutant defects"].
- Locomotion and growth: all unc-64 alleles are uncoordinated; these are downstream consequences of impaired neurotransmission [PMID:9614171 "All six unc-64 mutants exhibit locomotory abnormalities."].

## Curation notes
- GO:0005484 is labelled "helical-bundle zippering fusogenic activity" in the current GOA export (formerly "SNAP receptor activity"); the module YAMLs use the older label. Same id.
- The two `protein binding` IPI rows (UNC-18, UNC-76) report real, well-characterised interactions but the term is uninformative; the interactions are captured in the description/core functions.
- Basolateral plasma membrane (IDA, PMID:9614171): the full text shows intestinal expression but not a basolateral-restricted pool; treated as over-annotation.
- Insulin receptor signaling pathway (IMP, PMID:10377425): unc-64 acts upstream of DAF-2 (ligand secretion), so a regulation term fits better than pathway membership.
