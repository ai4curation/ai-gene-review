| Identity and domain architecture | *C. elegans* **unc-64** encodes the worm syntaxin-1A homolog, a membrane-anchored syntaxin-family t-/Qa-SNARE. Three products share a cytoplasmic region containing regulatory syntaxin/Habc and H3 SNARE-coil elements but have alternative C-terminal hydrophobic anchors. This matches UniProt O16000/F56A8.7 and the listed Syntaxin_N, syntaxin, and T_SNARE domains. | Molecular cloning, cDNA/genomic sequencing, transcript analysis, mutant mapping, and protein-sequence comparison. | Three transcripts; encoded proteins are **63–64% identical to human syntaxin-1A**. | 1998; [10.1091/mbc.9.6.1235](https://doi.org/10.1091/mbc.9.6.1235) (pqac-00000014, pqac-00000015) |
| Cellular and subcellular localization | UNC-64 is expressed broadly in neurons and selected secretory tissues. Neuronal isoforms occur throughout cell bodies, axons, and dendrites and are not detectably restricted to active zones; as a syntaxin, the functional pool is principally associated with the plasma membrane through its C-terminal anchor. | UNC-64 immunostaining, promoter–lacZ reporters, and isoform-specific GFP-tagged constructs. | Isoforms A and C were neuronal and secretory; isoform B was restricted to five examined non-neuronal cell types. | 1998; [10.1091/mbc.9.6.1235](https://doi.org/10.1091/mbc.9.6.1235) (pqac-00000002, pqac-00000016) |
| Core SNARE function | UNC-64 contributes one helix to the neuronal four-helix SNARE bundle, together with one SNB-1/synaptobrevin helix and two RIC-4/SNAP-25 helices. Assembly bridges synaptic-vesicle and presynaptic membranes and supplies energy for docking/priming and fusion; UNC-64 is not an enzyme or transporter. | Allele-specific genetic interactions between lesions in the UNC-64 and synaptobrevin coiled-coil domains; null and hypomorphic phenotypes; conserved SNARE-complex architecture. | Complete loss causes near-total paralysis and postembryonic arrest as an **L1 larva**; hypomorphs are strongly aldicarb-resistant. | 1998; [10.1091/mbc.9.6.1235](https://doi.org/10.1091/mbc.9.6.1235) (pqac-00000000, pqac-00000014) |
| UNC-13-dependent opening and docking | UNC-13 promotes conversion of UNC-64 from a closed state to an open, SNARE-accessible state. Open UNC-64 is directly required for morphological synaptic-vesicle docking and can bypass the docking requirement for UNC-13. | Genetic mosaics and electron microscopy of cholinergic and GABAergic synapses; rescue with constitutively open versus wild-type syntaxin. | Constitutively open UNC-64 **fully rescued the vesicle-docking defect** of *unc-13* mutants, whereas overexpressed wild-type UNC-64 did not. | 2007; [10.1371/journal.pbio.0050198](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pbio.0050198) (pqac-00000004, pqac-00000017) |
| UNC-18 interaction | UNC-18 binds closed UNC-64, supports its trafficking/chaperoning to the plasma membrane, and subsequently templates productive SNARE assembly. The UNC-64 Habc domain is functionally required for the interaction and for synaptic vesicle docking. | Domain replacement and interspecies-complementation genetics, locomotion and aldicarb assays, electrophysiology, and electron microscopy. | A yeast-Habc UNC-64 chimera reduced docking by **91% at acetylcholine synapses and 95% at GABA synapses**; opening UNC-18 bypassed the defective Habc–UNC-18 interface. | 2022 preprint; [10.1101/2022.05.02.490315](https://doi.org/10.1101/2022.05.02.490315) (pqac-00000001) |
| Open-syntaxin physiology | Endogenous L166A/E167A “open” UNC-64 accelerates SNARE assembly and increases spontaneous and evoked acetylcholine release without increasing quantal amplitude or disrupting baseline motility. It can partially bypass several defects upstream of fusion but cannot replace UNC-18. | CRISPR knock-in, aldicarb and thrashing assays, neuromuscular-junction patch clamp, optogenetic stimulation, and SNARE-complex immunoblotting. | Baseline motility was **122 versus 125 thrashes/min**; miniature-current frequency increased (*p*=0.02) while amplitude did not (*p*=0.44). In *snt-1* mutants, open UNC-64 increased thrashing from **20.1 to 48.9/min**. | 2020; [10.1038/s41467-020-19178-x](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-020-19178-x) (pqac-00000009, pqac-00000010) |
| DAT-1 regulation | Beyond fusion, UNC-64 physically associates with the dopamine transporter DAT-1 and suppresses its channel-like activity. Disrupting this complex enhances channel openings and dopaminergic signaling, showing that UNC-64 can allosterically regulate a transporter as a secondary function. | Native-neuron coassociation studies, single-channel electrophysiology, dopamine-uptake assays, and swimming-induced-paralysis behavior. | Disrupted association increased channel events by **40%**; **28%** of channels had prolonged openings averaging **19 ± 3 ms**, versus **6.9 ± 3 ms** in controls, without changing dopamine uptake. | 2008; [10.1073/pnas.0802214105](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.0802214105) (pqac-00000011) |
| 2023 development: combined UNC-64/UNC-18 gain of function | Open UNC-64 and UNC-18(P334A) are not simply additive: their combination biases transmission toward excitation, impairs inhibitory release, and worsens several whole-animal traits. This cautions against treating maximal SNARE activation as uniformly beneficial. **Preprint—not peer reviewed in the retrieved record.** | Double-mutant genetics, cholinergic and GABAergic electrophysiology, aldicarb sensitivity, motility, growth, body-size, and brood assays; complementary liposome-fusion experiments. | The double mutant increased excitatory evoked charge transfer but reduced spontaneous and evoked inhibitory release and did not improve motility, growth, body size, or brood size. | **2023 preprint**; [10.1101/2023.08.18.553709](https://doi.org/10.1101/2023.08.18.553709) (pqac-00000007, pqac-00000019) |
| 2024 development: presynaptic homeostasis | UNC-64-mediated release is embedded in a homeostatic pathway that inversely couples vesicle-exocytosis efficiency to synaptic UNC-2/CaV2-channel abundance and active-zone remodeling. UNC-64 is the fusion-output component used to perturb release, not the channel-removal enzyme. | Endogenous channel tagging and copy-number calibration, release-enhancing/reducing mutations, active-zone imaging, forward genetics, and identification of the WWP-1 E3 ligase. | Reduced release increased UNC-2 abundance, whereas enhanced release reversed that increase; WWP-1 was identified as a mediator of UNC-2 removal from synapses. | 2024; [10.1073/pnas.2404969121](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2404969121) (pqac-00000013) |
| 2024 development: neuronal identity context | Current developmental synthesis classifies *unc-64* as a panneuronal effector gene used broadly by differentiated neurons for synaptic-vesicle biology, consistent with its original widespread neuronal expression rather than a neuron-class-specific signaling identity. | Integration of connectomics, neuronal transcriptomics, and developmental gene-regulatory literature in a WormBook review. | Adult *C. elegans* has **302 hermaphrodite neurons, 387 male neurons, and 118 recognized hermaphrodite neuronal classes**, providing the anatomical context for broad neuronal expression. | 2024; [10.1093/genetics/iyae116](https://doi.org/10.1093/genetics/iyae116) (pqac-00000018) |
