# Functional annotation report: *C. elegans unc-64* / UNC-64

## Executive conclusion

The requested identity is verified: **unc-64** in *Caenorhabditis elegans* encodes the neuronal syntaxin-1 homolog represented by **UniProt O16000** and locus/ORF **F56A8.7**. Molecular cloning identified three alternatively terminated transcripts encoding proteins that are 63–64% identical to human syntaxin-1A. They share the same cytoplasmic syntaxin/SNARE machinery but have different C-terminal hydrophobic membrane anchors. This agrees with the supplied Syntaxin_N, syntaxin, T_SNARE, SNARE, and syntaxin/epimorphin annotations. No evidence retrieved referred to a conflicting same-symbol protein in another organism. (saifee1998thecaenorhabditiselegans pages 1-2, saifee1998thecaenorhabditiselegans pages 2-3)

**Primary annotation:** UNC-64 is a membrane-anchored **Qa-/target-SNARE**, not an enzyme or solute transporter. At presynaptic plasma membranes, it acts as a core structural and mechanochemical component of synaptic-vesicle docking, priming, and fusion. Its SNARE helix assembles with SNB-1/synaptobrevin and RIC-4/SNAP-25 into the four-helix neuronal SNARE bundle. UNC-13 promotes opening of UNC-64, while UNC-18 chaperones syntaxin trafficking and templates productive SNARE assembly. (hammarlund2007opensyntaxindocks pages 1-2, huang2023doublemutationof pages 1-4)

## 1. Identity, architecture, and molecular classification

The foundational cloning study, published in June 1998, established that the *C. elegans unc-64* locus encodes syntaxin proteins with 63–64% sequence identity to human syntaxin-1A. Three transcripts encode products with an identical cytoplasmic region but alternative C-terminal membrane anchors. Missense lesions in viable hypomorphs map to the hydrophobic face of the H3/SNARE coiled coil, and allele-specific genetic interactions with coiled-coil mutations in synaptobrevin demonstrate that this conserved region is functionally important in vivo. [Saifee et al., 1998, DOI 10.1091/mbc.9.6.1235](https://doi.org/10.1091/mbc.9.6.1235). (saifee1998thecaenorhabditiselegans pages 1-2, saifee1998thecaenorhabditiselegans pages 2-3)

Functionally, the protein can be divided into three elements:

1. **N-terminal peptide and Syntaxin_N/Habc region:** a regulatory helical bundle that supports UNC-18 binding, folding, trafficking, and conformational control.
2. **C-terminal H3/SNARE motif:** the helix contributed by UNC-64 to the four-helix neuronal SNARE bundle.
3. **C-terminal hydrophobic anchor:** a tail anchor that inserts UNC-64 into the target, principally plasma, membrane; alternative terminal exons diversify this anchor without changing the cytoplasmic fusion apparatus. (saifee1998thecaenorhabditiselegans pages 1-2, parrarivas2022syntaxinhabcis pages 4-7)

The supplied UniProt domain calls therefore align closely with experimental literature. “Syntaxin_N” corresponds principally to the Habc regulatory region, while the T_SNARE/SNARE annotation corresponds to the H3 helix that associates with SNAP-25 and synaptobrevin. The protein is best called a **syntaxin-1-like Qa-SNARE**, although older papers commonly use “t-SNARE.”

## 2. Primary molecular function

### 2.1 Core membrane-fusion role

UNC-64 supplies one helix to the neuronal SNARE complex; vesicular SNB-1/synaptobrevin supplies one, and plasma-membrane RIC-4/SNAP-25 supplies two. Their progressive assembly into a tightly wound four-helix bundle brings the vesicle and plasma membranes together and provides mechanical energy for membrane fusion. Thus, there is no catalytic reaction or transported substrate to annotate. The relevant “substrates” in a structural sense are the opposing vesicle and target membranes and the cognate SNARE partners. (saifee1998thecaenorhabditiselegans pages 1-2, hammarlund2007opensyntaxindocks pages 1-2, huang2023doublemutationof pages 1-4)

Several lines of direct evidence establish this function:

- Hypomorphic *unc-64* lesions alter transmitter-release kinetics and confer strong resistance to aldicarb, which indicates reduced cholinergic output.
- Coiled-coil mutations in UNC-64 interact allele-specifically with mutations in the corresponding synaptobrevin coiled coil.
- Complete loss causes near-total paralysis and postembryonic arrest as an L1 larva, despite grossly normal embryogenesis and tissue morphology. (saifee1998thecaenorhabditiselegans pages 1-2, saifee1998thecaenorhabditiselegans pages 10-11, saifee1998thecaenorhabditiselegans pages 7-8)

### 2.2 Docking and UNC-13-dependent opening

Syntaxin adopts closed and open conformations. In the closed state, Habc folds back onto the SNARE motif, limiting its availability for SNARE assembly. Electron microscopy and genetic mosaic analysis showed that UNC-64 is required for docking synaptic vesicles to the plasma membrane. Constitutively open syntaxin fully rescued the docking defect of *unc-13* mutants, whereas excess wild-type syntaxin did not. The resulting model is that active-zone UNC-13 opens UNC-64, and open UNC-64 directly participates in vesicle attachment before subsequent priming and fusion. [Hammarlund et al., published July 17, 2007, DOI 10.1371/journal.pbio.0050198](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pbio.0050198). (hammarlund2007opensyntaxindocks pages 1-2)

Open UNC-64 does not, however, make UNC-13 wholly dispensable for efficient physiological release. In an endogenous open-syntaxin knock-in, *unc-13* mutant miniature-current frequency rose from below 0.5 Hz to approximately 2 Hz, still far below the 30–40 Hz wild-type range; evoked release remained below 10% of wild type. This suggests that UNC-13 has functions beyond simply shifting syntaxin’s conformational equilibrium or that physiological concentrations of open syntaxin cannot bypass all UNC-13-dependent steps. [Tien et al., published November 2020, DOI 10.1038/s41467-020-19178-x](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-020-19178-x). (tien2020opensyntaxinovercomesa pages 6-7)

### 2.3 UNC-18-dependent trafficking and SNARE templating

UNC-18 is the cognate Sec1/Munc18-family regulator. Closed UNC-64 binds within the central cavity of UNC-18; this association stabilizes/chaperones syntaxin and supports its delivery to the plasma membrane. UNC-18 also participates later as a template that aligns syntaxin with synaptobrevin for productive complex assembly. (parrarivas2022syntaxinhabcis pages 4-7, huang2023doublemutationof pages 1-4)

Domain-replacement experiments reinforce the specificity of this relationship. Substitution with yeast Habc caused severe release phenotypes and reduced morphologically docked vesicles by 91% at acetylcholine synapses and 95% at GABA synapses. Pairing the yeast Habc chimera with yeast Sec1 modestly improved locomotion and miniature currents but did not restore docking, showing that compatible interfaces within the complete machinery matter. These results were reported in a 2022 preprint and should be weighted below peer-reviewed primary studies until formally published. [Parra-Rivas et al., posted May 2, 2022, DOI 10.1101/2022.05.02.490315](https://doi.org/10.1101/2022.05.02.490315). (parrarivas2022syntaxinhabcis pages 4-7)

## 3. Cellular and anatomical localization

UNC-64 is broadly neuronal rather than limited to one neurotransmitter class. Antibody staining and reporter studies detected it in neuronal cell bodies, axons, and dendrites. It was not detectably concentrated only at synaptic release sites, consistent with a broadly distributed axonal/plasma-membrane pool from which active-zone machinery recruits or activates release-competent syntaxin. Isoforms A and C were observed in neurons and selected secretory tissues; isoform B was limited to the examined non-neuronal secretory cell types. (saifee1998thecaenorhabditiselegans pages 10-11, saifee1998thecaenorhabditiselegans pages 7-8)

Non-neuronal expression was reported in the intestine, spermatheca, excretory gland, distal-tip cells, uv1 vulval secretory cells, and intestinal muscle. Nevertheless, mosaic experiments supported the nervous system as the principal site responsible for the severe locomotor phenotype: animals lacking muscle expression could move normally, whereas neuronal loss was associated with severe uncoordination. (saifee1998thecaenorhabditiselegans pages 10-11, saifee1998thecaenorhabditiselegans pages 7-8)

The most defensible subcellular annotation is therefore **cytoplasmic face of the presynaptic/neuronal plasma membrane, extending through neuronal processes, with additional expression in secretory tissues**. Low-level syntaxin on vesicles has been described for neuronal syntaxins generally, but the core UNC-64 function addressed by worm experiments is at the target plasma membrane. (saifee1998thecaenorhabditiselegans pages 1-2, hammarlund2007opensyntaxindocks pages 1-2)

A 2024 WormBook synthesis classifies *unc-64* among panneuronal effector genes involved in synaptic-vesicle biology. That interpretation fits the original broad neuronal expression data rather than a neuron-class-specific developmental or signaling identity. [Poole et al., advance publication August 21, 2024, DOI 10.1093/genetics/iyae116](https://doi.org/10.1093/genetics/iyae116). (poole2024neurogenesisincaenorhabditis pages 1-2)

## 4. Pathways and interaction network

The principal pathway can be summarized as:

**UNC-2/CaV2-mediated Ca²⁺ entry → Ca²⁺ sensor and active-zone regulators → UNC-13-dependent opening of UNC-64 → UNC-18-templated assembly of UNC-64–RIC-4–SNB-1 SNARE complex → vesicle fusion and neurotransmitter release.**

Important functional partners include:

- **UNC-13/Munc13:** opens UNC-64 and promotes docking/priming.
- **UNC-18/Munc18:** chaperones/traffics UNC-64 and templates SNARE assembly.
- **SNB-1/synaptobrevin/VAMP:** vesicular R-SNARE with direct genetic evidence of functional interaction.
- **RIC-4/SNAP-25:** plasma-membrane Qbc-SNARE completing the four-helix bundle.
- **SNT-1/synaptotagmin:** Ca²⁺ sensor for evoked fusion.
- **UNC-2/CaV2:** presynaptic calcium channel upstream of Ca²⁺-triggered release.
- **TOM-1/tomosyn:** inhibitory SNARE regulator that competes with productive assembly.
- **NSF/α-SNAP machinery:** disassembles post-fusion SNARE complexes for recycling. (saifee1998thecaenorhabditiselegans pages 1-2, hammarlund2007opensyntaxindocks pages 1-2, huang2023doublemutationof pages 1-4)

## 5. Quantitative functional evidence

An endogenous L166A/E167A open-syntaxin knock-in left baseline swimming essentially unchanged—122 versus 125 thrashes/min, with 51 animals per genotype and *p*=0.46—but accelerated aldicarb paralysis. Miniature postsynaptic-current frequency increased significantly (*p*=0.02), while amplitude did not (*p*=0.44), indicating more frequent vesicle fusion rather than larger quantal events. Biochemically, the mutation promoted an approximately 80-kDa heat-sensitive SNARE-complex species. (tien2020opensyntaxinovercomesa pages 2-3)

Open syntaxin also partially suppressed several release-defective genotypes. In *snt-1* mutants, thrashing increased from 20.1 to 48.9/min. In *unc-2* null animals, which showed 5.43 thrashes/min, open syntaxin restored movement to a level not significantly different from wild type and normalized aldicarb sensitivity. By contrast, rescue of *unc-13* was weak, demonstrating that increased syntaxin accessibility is powerful but not universally sufficient. (tien2020opensyntaxinovercomesa pages 6-7, tien2020opensyntaxinovercomesa pages 3-4)

These results support a mechanistic interpretation in which opening UNC-64 increases the probability or speed of productive SNARE assembly. Unchanged miniature-current amplitude argues against altered postsynaptic responsiveness or vesicle neurotransmitter content as the primary explanation. (tien2020opensyntaxinovercomesa pages 3-4, tien2020opensyntaxinovercomesa pages 2-3)

## 6. Secondary function in dopaminergic neurons

UNC-64 also regulates DAT-1, the worm dopamine transporter. Native-neuron experiments found that UNC-64 associates with DAT-1 and suppresses its transporter-associated channel activity. Disrupting the stable complex with an N-terminal GFP tag increased channel events by 40% without changing unitary conductance or bulk dopamine uptake. Prolonged openings occurred in 28% of channels and averaged 19 ± 3 ms, compared with 6.9 ± 3 ms in wild-type neurons. The animals displayed swimming-induced paralysis consistent with excessive dopaminergic signaling. [Carvelli et al., published September 16, 2008, DOI 10.1073/pnas.0802214105](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.0802214105). (carvelli2008dopaminetransportersyntaxin1a pages 1-2)

Cell-specific *unc-64* knockdown later reduced dopamine uptake in cultured neurons by approximately 30%, compared with 40% after *dat-1* silencing, reduced amphetamine-evoked dopamine release, and abolished amphetamine-induced locomotor behavior. [Lanzo et al., published May 2018, DOI 10.3389/fphys.2018.00576](https://doi.org/10.3389/fphys.2018.00576). These observations extend UNC-64 biology beyond vesicle fusion to allosteric regulation of a neurotransmitter transporter, but this is best regarded as a specialized secondary role rather than its primary annotation.

## 7. Developments in 2023–2024

### 7.1 Excitation–inhibition balance

A 2023 preprint combined open UNC-64 with gain-of-function UNC-18(P334A). Contrary to a simple additive model, the double mutant increased excitatory drive while reducing spontaneous and evoked inhibitory release. It failed to improve—or worsened—motility, growth, body size, brood output, and other traits in several mutant backgrounds. This suggests that globally accelerating SNARE assembly can differentially affect cholinergic and GABAergic terminals and that “more fusion” is not necessarily physiologically beneficial. Because the retrieved record is a preprint, these conclusions remain provisional. [Huang et al., posted August 2023, DOI 10.1101/2023.08.18.553709](https://doi.org/10.1101/2023.08.18.553709). (huang2023doublemutationof pages 13-17, huang2023doublemutationof pages 1-4)

### 7.2 Release-dependent presynaptic homeostasis

A peer-reviewed 2024 PNAS study showed that vesicle-release efficiency is inversely coupled to presynaptic UNC-2/CaV2 abundance. Reduced release increased channel abundance and remodeled active-zone composition; enhanced release reversed the increase. A forward genetic screen implicated the WWP-1 E3 ubiquitin ligase in removing UNC-2 from synapses. UNC-64 is important here as part of the manipulated exocytotic output rather than as the ubiquitin ligase or channel-removal factor. [Xiong et al., published August 22, 2024, DOI 10.1073/pnas.2404969121](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2404969121). (xiong2024presynapticneuronsselftune pages 1-2)

The conceptual advance is that UNC-64-mediated fusion participates in a feedback system: release efficiency is sensed and translated into altered calcium-channel abundance and active-zone structure, stabilizing synaptic output. This places the core SNARE machinery within homeostatic regulation rather than treating it only as a terminal fusion apparatus. (xiong2024presynapticneuronsselftune pages 1-2)

## 8. Applications and real-world relevance

UNC-64 itself has no established clinical or industrial implementation. Its practical value is as an experimentally tractable ortholog of mammalian syntaxin-1 in a genetically accessible intact nervous system.

Current applications include:

1. **Mechanistic dissection of conserved exocytosis.** CRISPR knock-ins, electrophysiology, electron microscopy, and genetic epistasis can distinguish docking, priming, and fusion functions in vivo.
2. **Disease-model modifier studies.** Open syntaxin can suppress defects caused by impaired synaptotagmin, CaV2 channels, CAPS, RIM, or Munc13, motivating investigation of syntaxin conformation as a potential modifier of hyposecretory synaptopathies. The failure of combined UNC-64/UNC-18 gain of function cautions that therapeutic enhancement would need cell-type and dose specificity. (tien2020opensyntaxinovercomesa pages 6-7, tien2020opensyntaxinovercomesa pages 3-4, huang2023doublemutationof pages 13-17)
3. **Drug and toxicology models.** UNC-64-dependent assays have informed studies of aldicarb, volatile anesthetics, amphetamine, and dopamine-transporter regulation. Aldicarb sensitivity provides an organism-level proxy for cholinergic output, while dopaminergic behaviors connect molecular interaction to circuit function. (saifee1998thecaenorhabditiselegans pages 1-2, carvelli2008dopaminetransportersyntaxin1a pages 1-2)
4. **Presynaptic homeostasis research.** Manipulating syntaxin-mediated release allows researchers to test how active zones compensate through CaV2 abundance and remodeling. (xiong2024presynapticneuronsselftune pages 1-2)

## 9. Evidence assessment and unresolved questions

The **highest-confidence annotation** is UNC-64 as the broadly neuronal, plasma-membrane syntaxin required for synaptic-vesicle docking and SNARE-mediated fusion. It is supported by molecular cloning, loss- and gain-of-function genetics, allele-specific interaction, electron microscopy, electrophysiology, and behavior. (saifee1998thecaenorhabditiselegans pages 1-2, hammarlund2007opensyntaxindocks pages 1-2, tien2020opensyntaxinovercomesa pages 3-4)

The UNC-13/open-syntaxin model is also strongly supported, but endogenous knock-in results show that “UNC-13 only opens syntaxin” is incomplete. Likewise, UNC-18 has both chaperoning and templating functions that cannot be bypassed simply by holding syntaxin open. (tien2020opensyntaxinovercomesa pages 6-7, huang2023doublemutationof pages 1-4)

Important remaining questions include how the alternative membrane anchors affect tissue-specific trafficking, why cholinergic and GABAergic synapses respond differently to simultaneous UNC-64/UNC-18 activation, and precisely how release efficiency is sensed in the 2024 CaV2-homeostasis pathway. The domain-swap mechanism and 2023 excitation–inhibition study should be interpreted cautiously because the retrieved versions were preprints. (parrarivas2022syntaxinhabcis pages 4-7, huang2023doublemutationof pages 13-17)

## Final functional annotation

**UNC-64/O16000 is the *C. elegans* syntaxin-1-like, membrane-anchored Qa-SNARE. It operates mainly on the cytoplasmic face of presynaptic plasma membranes. UNC-18 stabilizes and templates UNC-64, UNC-13 converts it toward an open state, and open UNC-64 engages RIC-4/SNAP-25 and vesicular SNB-1/synaptobrevin to dock, prime, and fuse neurotransmitter vesicles. Its best-established biological process is chemical synaptic transmission, with a secondary, experimentally supported role in regulating DAT-1 channel/transporter behavior in dopaminergic neurons.**
