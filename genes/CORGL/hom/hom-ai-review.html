Gene Description

Hom is the standalone homoserine dehydrogenase of Corynebacterium glutamicum ATCC 13032. It reduces L-aspartate 4-semialdehyde to L-homoserine, directing the shared aspartate-derived precursor into threonine and methionine biosynthesis. NADPH-dependent chemistry is supported by the conserved Hom family and biochemical work on related corynebacterial enzymes. Wild-type C. glutamicum Hom is feedback inhibited by threonine; mutations affecting its C-terminal regulatory region can release this inhibition and alter flux between the homoserine and lysine branches.

2026-11-01 — NADPH chemistry, feedback and strain boundaries

P08499 is the reviewed ATCC13032 standalone homoserine dehydrogenase (taxon196627; PTHR43331 HOMOSERINE DEHYDROGENASE, SF1 same). The sequence record integrates historical corynebacterial work from other strains. In particular, PMID:2835591 hom-thrB operon sequencing is assigned by UniProt to ATCC13059. Its abstract says the genes encode two separate polypeptides, supporting standalone architecture rather than an aspartokinase/Hom fusion.

PMID:1743520 abstract reports wild-type C.glutamicum threonine inhibition (Ki0.16mM) and an engineered frameshift/deletion mutant with markedly reduced inhibition. The full ScienceDirect endpoint returned403. The abstract neither identifies ATCC13032 nor resolves cofactor preference. PMID:1902466, also abstract-only despite the PMC landing URL, documents a different feedback-resistant mutation G378E. Neither mutant is the unmodified native enzyme. The recovered PMID:4394351 paper is B.flavum No2247, not ATCC13032. Its full primary J-STAGE PDF establishes NADPH chemistry, substrate/product inhibition and threonine feedback using a partially purified preparation; it explicitly notes small amounts of other enzyme activities remaining in FractionIII. It does not demonstrate NADH use. See hom-source-analysis.md for canonical paper/manifestation mapping and exact boundary evidence.

QuickGO GO:0004412 currently defines L-homoserine + NADP+ = L-aspartate4-semialdehyde + NADPH +H+. GO:0004022 explicitly uses NAD+, and GO:0070403 is NAD+ binding. The raw UniProt record asserts both NAD- and NADP-linked reactions while citing1743520/4394351. The latter recovered paper supports NADPH only; the former abstract cannot settle NAD specificity. Therefore GO:0004022 and GO:0070403 remain UNDECIDED. They are not removed based on inaccessible text and are not mechanically changed to GO:0004412, which would silently change the cofactor. A purified wild-type ATCC13032 NADPH/NADH comparison is the outstanding question.

The four GO:0004412 rows, including the two experimental rows, are consistently accepted: family identity, corynebacterial enzyme activity/regulation and comparative primary chemistry establish the function. The absence of a full1743520 paper does not erase a clearly corroborated Hom function. Strain-specific source limitations are explicit, not an allegation of wrong-gene experimental curation. NADP binding and threonine/methionine biosynthesis are supported. Broad redox and amino-acid metabolism terms are refined. The conservative native module uses NADPH for Hom and should not present an equally established NADH alternative. All12 seeded rows adjudicated; no NEW.

Hom primary-source identity and evidence scope

One paper, two cited source manifestations

Canonical PMID:4394351 / DOI:10.1093/oxfordjournals.jbchem.a129361 is Miyajima and Shiio, Journal of Biochemistry68:311–319 (1970), “Regulation of aspartate family amino acid biosynthesis in Brevibacterium flavum.3. Its machine-fetched title contains “Properites”; caches are unedited. The separately fetched _article URL is the same paper's abstract landing page and is not an independent study.

The review's two reference entries (PMID and full-PDF URL) represent one unique paper, not independent replications. Verbatim fulltext quotations remain attributed to the URL that actually contains them. The optional replacement schema is for identifier remapping, not alternate manifestations, so no misleading replacement reason is invented. Both reference_review notes link this mapping.

Direct observations and limits

The PDF methods specify B.flavum No2247; it is not the reviewed ATCC13032 strain. The preparation is partial purification by ammonium sulfate and gel filtration; FractionIII contains residual Asd, dihydrodipicolinate synthetase and NADPH oxidase. The standard reaction mixture contains ASA and NADPH. The paper reports NADPH as the first binding substrate and NADP+ as the last releasing product. It establishes NADPH reaction chemistry and threonine feedback in the characterized strain, not target-specific NADH catalytic efficiency.

PMID:1743520 is a distinct1991 study of C.glutamicum Hom regulation. Its available abstract states wild-type threonine inhibition and a C-terminal mutant phenotype but does not identify the strain or compare NAD/NADP. The full publisher endpoint returned403. PMID:2835591 is the hom-thrB operon paper; the reviewed record assigns that sequence source to ATCC13059. The current target record is ATCC13032 and also includes complete-genome sources. These identities are not collapsed into one native purified-enzyme experiment.

The conservative supported target function is homoserine dehydrogenase with NADPH chemistry, supported by family plus corynebacterial biochemical evidence. NAD-linked activity/binding remains unresolved. No source record was edited, and no native NADH assay or lack of NADH activity was invented.
