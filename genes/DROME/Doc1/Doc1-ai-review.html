Gene Description

Dorsocross1 (Doc1) encodes a T-box transcription factor of the Tbx6-related subfamily, one of three closely related, clustered and functionally redundant paralogs (Doc1, Doc2, Doc3) with identical embryonic expression. Doc proteins bind T-box (GTG-containing) sites in enhancers via the T-box domain and act in the nucleus as activators or repressors of RNA polymerase II target genes. Doc genes are direct targets of high-level Dpp/Screw (BMP) signaling. In the early embryo they are required for differentiation and survival of the extraembryonic amnioserosa and for patterning of the dorsolateral ectoderm. In the dorsal mesoderm they are induced at the intersection of Dpp and Wingless signals and act with Tinman to activate pannier; mutual cross-regulation among Doc, Tinman and Pannier specifies cardiac progenitors, and Doc is required for all myocardial and most pericardial cells. Later Doc is re-expressed in a segmental subset of cardioblasts (restricted by Tinman) that form the ostia (inflow valves), where Doc activates targets such as Toll. Doc genes also function in wing disc primordium formation, anterior Malpighian tubule budding, and as Dpp mediators in leading-edge cells during pupal thorax closure.

Suggested Questions for Experts

Q: Given the complete redundancy of Doc1, Doc2 and Doc3, which loss-of-function annotations derived from deficiencies removing all three genes should be propagated equally to each paralog?

Q: Does Doc physically interact with Tinman or Pannier on shared cardiac enhancers, as TBX5 does with NKX2-5 and GATA4 in vertebrates?

Q: Is there a GO term suitable for specification of the ostial (inflow valve) cardioblast fate in arthropod hearts?

Suggested Experiments

Experiment: Co-immunoprecipitation and ChIP/CUT&RUN of Doc with Tinman and Pannier in embryonic cardiac mesoderm to test for a physical cardiogenic complex.
