| Feature | Functional annotation for *Drosophila melanogaster* Tube (tub; UniProt P22812) | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Protein identity | Tube is the *D. melanogaster* **tub/tube** gene product and a core intracellular component of canonical Toll signaling; this identity is distinct from unrelated genes or anatomical “tubes.” | The accession and death-domain annotation match the experimentally established Toll-pathway adaptor Tube (pqac-00000007, pqac-00000010). |
| Domains | Contains an **N-terminal death domain** (InterPro IPR000488) within the **death-like domain superfamily** (IPR011029); related annotations include Tube-specific death-domain families. These are protein-interaction modules, not catalytic domains. | Comparative domain analysis identifies the conserved death/death-like domain and the structurally characterized Pelle–Tube death-domain interface (pqac-00000007, pqac-00000009). |
| Primary molecular function | **Non-enzymatic adaptor/scaffold protein.** Tube assembles the receptor-proximal signaling machinery and bridges Toll-associated MyD88 to the kinase Pelle; no catalytic reaction or substrate specificity is established for *D. melanogaster* Tube. | Tube is characterized as the scaffold connecting MyD88 and Pelle through death-domain interactions (pqac-00000003, pqac-00000008, pqac-00000039). |
| Direct/complex partners | **MyD88** and **Pelle** are the principal partners in the MyD88–Tube–Pelle complex. The Tube–Pelle association is directly supported structurally by their death-domain complex; MyD88 recruits Tube through corresponding death-domain interactions. | Structural modeling and pathway studies support Tube–Pelle binding and MyD88–Tube–Pelle assembly (pqac-00000000, pqac-00000014, pqac-00000043). |
| Subcellular localization | Functions on the **cytoplasmic face of the plasma membrane** after recruitment to ligand-activated Toll. Forced membrane targeting of Tube can activate ventralizing Toll output, demonstrating that membrane-proximal localization is functionally important. It is not secreted or transmembrane. | Membrane-associated complex formation and membrane-targeting experiments are summarized in the developmental Toll literature (pqac-00000042). |
| Core signaling pathway | Acts in the canonical **Spätzle–Toll–MyD88–Tube–Pelle–Cactus–NF-κB pathway**. Tube is not assigned a primary role in the distinct Imd pathway. | Multiple pathway syntheses place Tube specifically in the Toll signaling module (pqac-00000014, pqac-00000015, pqac-00000020). |
| Proximal pathway mechanism | Processed Spätzle activates Toll; Toll recruits MyD88, which recruits Tube and Pelle. Tube-mediated assembly positions Pelle for signaling, leading to phosphorylation-dependent loss of Cactus and activation of NF-κB-family transcription factors. | The receptor-to-NF-κB sequence and Tube’s bridging role are described in recent pathway reviews and comparative work (pqac-00000014, pqac-00000016, pqac-00000017). |
| Downstream regulator: Cactus | **Cactus**, the fly IκB-like inhibitor, is the proximal inhibitory target downstream of the Tube-containing complex. Pelle-dependent phosphorylation promotes Cactus degradation, although Tube itself is not the kinase. | Cactus phosphorylation/degradation is linked to assembly of the MyD88–Tube–Pelle complex (pqac-00000014, pqac-00000015, pqac-00000018). |
| Downstream transcription factors | Cactus removal releases **Dorsal** and **Dif**, permitting their nuclear translocation. Dorsal is especially central to embryonic patterning, whereas Dif is prominent in systemic immune transcription; both can participate in immune Toll output. | Nuclear activation of Dorsal/Dif downstream of Tube-containing signaling is consistently reported (pqac-00000013, pqac-00000014, pqac-00000016). |
| Biological process: innate immunity | Required for Toll-dependent defense, principally against **fungi/yeasts and Gram-positive bacteria** recognized through fungal β-glucans or lysine-type peptidoglycan; antiviral Toll functions have also been reported, but they are less directly attributable to Tube-specific experiments. | Pathogen recognition and downstream Tube signaling are supported by recent immunity reviews (pqac-00000029, pqac-00000031, pqac-00000032). |
| Immune tissues and output location | The intracellular Tube complex functions within Toll-responsive cells, notably **fat-body cells** during systemic immunity. The resulting antimicrobial products—not Tube—are secreted into the hemolymph. | Tube-containing signaling induces immune genes in the fat body following infection (pqac-00000016, pqac-00000021, pqac-00000023). |
| Antimicrobial effectors | **Drosomycin** is the best-established Toll-responsive antifungal readout downstream of Tube-associated signaling. **Defensin** has been listed among Toll-associated fly antimicrobial peptides in comparative summaries, but its regulation is less specific than that of Drosomycin and should not be treated as a uniquely Tube-dependent output. | Drosomycin is repeatedly identified as a canonical output (pqac-00000021, pqac-00000022, pqac-00000024); Defensin appears in a comparative Toll-associated effector table (pqac-00000025). |
| Biological process: embryonic dorsoventral patterning | Tube is a maternally supplied **dorsal-group gene product** required to convert spatially restricted ventral Toll activation into the ventral-to-dorsal nuclear Dorsal gradient that specifies embryonic cell fates. | Genetic epistasis places maternal Tube downstream of active Spätzle/Toll and upstream of Pelle, Cactus, and Dorsal (pqac-00000046, pqac-00000047, pqac-00000048). |
| Loss-of-function phenotype | Embryos from mothers lacking functional **tube** fail to establish normal dorsoventral polarity and become strongly **dorsalized**, with dorsal characteristics around the circumference and loss of normal ventral and lateral structures. | Classical maternal-effect phenotypes of the dorsal group, including tube, are summarized in developmental reviews (pqac-00000046, pqac-00000049). |
| Evolutionary interpretation | The death-domain-mediated MyD88–Tube–Pelle assembly is organizationally analogous to mammalian MyD88–IRAK complexes. Tube is the scaffold and Pelle is the principal kinase; therefore, Tube should not be annotated as an active IRAK-like enzyme solely from distant homology or kinase-like predictions in other insects. | Evolutionary and structural comparisons distinguish the adaptor role of *D. melanogaster* Tube from the kinase role of Pelle/IRAK proteins (pqac-00000036, pqac-00000039, pqac-00000040). |
