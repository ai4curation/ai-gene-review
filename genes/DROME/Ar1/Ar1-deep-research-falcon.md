# Functional annotation report: *Drosophila melanogaster* Ar1 (CG6084; UniProt M9PF61)

## Identity and principal function

**Identity verified.** The requested Ar1 is the fruit-fly gene **CG6084**, also called **dAR1** or **Akr1B**, not a similarly named gene from another organism. Yang and colleagues explicitly identify CG6084 as dAR1; an independent sequence analysis identifies CG6084 as FlyBase **FBgn0086254** and an aldo-keto reductase (AKR). These identifiers agree with the supplied UniProt record. **CG10638 is a separate, putative aldose-reductase gene** and must not be treated as a synonym of Ar1. (yang2019sugaralcoholsof pages 5-6, scarpati2019acombinedcomputational pages 4-5, sano2022thepolyolpathway pages 4-6)

The best-supported primary function is **aldose reduction in the polyol pathway**, particularly the conversion of **glucose to sorbitol**. The proposed reductive reaction is glucose + NADPH + H⁺ → sorbitol + NADP⁺. Galactose-to-galactitol reduction is also consistent with the fly immune-pathway results. NADPH preference and hydride transfer are well-established properties of the AKR family, but the reviewed studies do **not** establish CG6084-specific cofactor preference, purified-enzyme glucose-versus-galactose kinetics, or a ranked substrate-specificity profile. Thus, the reaction assignment has stronger *in-vivo functional* support than *purified-protein biochemical* support. (yang2019sugaralcoholsof pages 5-6, sano2022thepolyolpathway pages 4-6, barski2008thealdoketoreductase pages 5-6)

Sequence and structure support this assignment: CG6084 is a **316-residue, single-AKR-domain** protein with approximately **49% identity and 68% similarity** to human AKR1B1 in one analysis. Its modeled protein preserves candidate catalytic residues **Y50, K79 and H112** corresponding to residues in the human enzyme. Domain similarity supports an oxidoreductase mechanism; it does not independently establish which physiological carbonyl substrates Ar1 uses. (scarpati2019acombinedcomputational pages 7-8, barski2008thealdoketoreductase pages 4-5)

### Established Ar1-specific role: hemocyte-to-fat-body immune signaling

The strongest gene-specific physiological evidence is **Yang et al., published August 2019, *Cell Host & Microbe*** ([DOI: 10.1016/j.chom.2019.07.001](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.chom.2019.07.001)). In larvae challenged orally with *Erwinia carotovora* Ecc15, intestinal immune-deficiency (**IMD**) signaling preceded increased Ar1 expression in **hemocytes**, increased aldose-reductase activity measured in **hemolymph**, and accumulation of circulating polyols. Two *dAR1* mutant alleles impaired the ensuing **fat-body** IMD response without eliminating the intestinal response. Hemocyte-specific Ar1 RNAi reproduced this defect; knockdown in the gut or fat body did not. Expressing human **AKR1B10 in mutant hemocytes** restored the response, providing functional complementation rather than merely a sequence-based assignment. (yang2019sugaralcoholsof pages 5-6, yang2019sugaralcoholsof pages 6-7)

The mechanistic model is **intestinal IMD activation → hemocyte-associated Ar1-dependent polyol production → circulating sorbitol/galactitol → fat-body Mmp2-dependent PGRP-LC cleavage → IMD/Relish-dependent antimicrobial transcription**. Sorbitol or galactitol stimulated the fat-body response in tissue assays, whereas mannitol did not; sorbitol injected into hemolymph also induced fat-body *Diptericin* expression. These are evidence that the **small metabolites**, rather than Ar1 itself, can carry the interorgan signal. The exact receptor or initial means by which sorbitol activates Mmp2 remains unresolved: the authors specifically did not establish PGRPs as sorbitol receptors or transporters. (yang2019sugaralcoholsof pages 6-7, yang2019sugaralcoholsof pages 7-10, yang2019sugaralcoholsof pages 1-2)

### Polyol-mediated nutrient sensing: strong pathway evidence, limited Ar1 attribution

**Sano et al., published June 10, 2022, *PLOS Biology*** ([DOI: 10.1371/journal.pbio.3001678](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pbio.3001678)) used labeled ingested glucose to show formation of **sorbitol and fructose**. Flies jointly mutant for **CG6084 and CG10638** had reduced hemolymph sorbitol; flies mutant for sorbitol dehydrogenases accumulated it. These findings support a two-step pathway, **glucose → sorbitol → fructose**, in which aldose reductases supply the first step and sorbitol dehydrogenases supply the second. Because the AR mutant disrupts **both genes**, its metabolic and developmental phenotypes cannot be assigned specifically to Ar1. (sano2022thepolyolpathway pages 4-6)

A principal output is regulation of **Mondo/Mlx**, the fly glucose-responsive transcriptional system. In cultured fat bodies from normally fed larvae, the proportion of nuclear **Mondo::Venus** signal rose from **5.2%** in basal medium to **14.6% with added glucose**, **12.7% with sorbitol**, and **16.7% with fructose**. Glucose failed to induce comparable nuclear accumulation in AR-double-mutant or sorbitol-dehydrogenase-mutant fat bodies, whereas supplying downstream polyol-pathway metabolites bypassed the appropriate metabolic block. The requirement is **conditional**, however: glucose refeeding after starvation could still promote Mondo nuclear localization in these mutants. These measurements concern **Mondo**, not Ar1 protein localization. (sano2022thepolyolpathway pages 8-11, sano2022thepolyolpathway pages 6-8)

**Hoshino et al., published February 24, 2023, *Science Advances*** ([DOI: 10.1126/sciadv.add5551](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.add5551)) extended the pathway to an endocrine circuit in mated females. Circulating fructose generated through the polyol pathway activates **Gr43a** in **NPF-positive midgut enteroendocrine cells**; NPF release supports an increase in ovarian germline stem cells. A **CG6084/CG10638 double mutant** impaired the postmating stem-cell increase. Sorbitol-dehydrogenase mutants showed defective fructose-associated responses, and dietary **fructose—but not sorbitol—**bypassed their downstream conversion defect. These results identify a real physiological application of polyol-pathway metabolism, **not an independently proven Ar1-only role** in the adult endocrine response. The study found no direct stem-cell response to sugars added to cultured ovaries, supporting an indirect hormonal mechanism. (hoshino2023circulatingfructoseregulates pages 9-11, hoshino2023circulatingfructoseregulates pages 7-9, hoshino2023circulatingfructoseregulates pages 6-7)

### Recent, provisional gene-specific finding: glial glucose sensing

A **July 2024 bioRxiv preprint** by **Kulshrestha et al.** ([DOI: 10.1101/2024.07.26.605306](https://doi.org/10.1101/2024.07.26.605306)) explicitly identifies **CG6084 as Akr1B** and tests its knockdown individually in larval **glia**. Panglial Akr1B knockdown impaired postingestive **glucose preference** but not **sorbitol preference**; panneuronal knockdown did not reproduce the glucose defect. Knockdown of another reductase, **CG10863/Akr2E3**, also impaired glucose preference, and combined glial knockdown abolished it. Glial sorbitol-dehydrogenase knockdown affected both glucose and sorbitol preference. The proposed mechanism is glial conversion of glucose through sorbitol to fructose for detection by **Gr43a-positive neurons**; the reported choice assays used **four replicates and 80 larvae per condition**. This is valuable **Ar1-specific RNAi evidence**, but it remains a **preprint**, and a behavioral phenotype does not itself measure Ar1-catalyzed metabolite flux or exclude all indirect RNAi effects. (kulshrestha2024anessentialrole pages 4-9, kulshrestha2024anessentialrole pages 1-4)

## Where the protein acts—and what remains uncertain

The most firmly established **cellular site of Ar1 requirement is the larval hemocyte** during the infection-induced immune relay. The 2024 preprint additionally implicates **larval central-nervous-system glia**, with combined glial-subtype experiments pointing toward cortex and astrocyte-like glia. In contrast, the fat body is demonstrably a site of **downstream response** in the immune and Mondo studies, not necessarily the essential site of Ar1 catalysis in those experiments. Circulating sorbitol and galactitol act in the **hemolymph**. (yang2019sugaralcoholsof pages 6-7, kulshrestha2024anessentialrole pages 4-9, sano2022thepolyolpathway pages 8-11)

**Subcellular localization is unresolved.** Although Yang et al. refer to aldose reductase as secreted and detect increased reductase activity in hemolymph, the reviewed evidence does not directly show where **CG6084 protein** resides within hemocytes or glia, or demonstrate secretion of intact Ar1 by protein imaging or secretion-specific assays. Consequently, a confident annotation is **“hemocyte-associated—and provisionally glial—aldo-keto reductase supporting production of circulating metabolites,”** not “experimentally proven extracellular Ar1.” (yang2019sugaralcoholsof pages 5-6, yang2019sugaralcoholsof pages 11-12)

## Scope of substrate annotation and research gaps

The source UniProt record lists several EC assignments, but the studies examined here most clearly connect Ar1 to **polyol-pathway aldose reduction**. In particular, neither the in-vivo metabolite changes nor complementation by human AKR1B10 supplies an Ar1-specific **Km**, **kcat**, glucose/galactose preference ratio, or a validated enzyme-by-enzyme accounting of every annotated EC activity. The 2019 proposal that CG6084 might also perform a **prostaglandin/eicosanoid reductase** reaction is based on computational similarity to mammalian AKRs; it is **not an experimentally demonstrated physiological function** of fly Ar1. A defensible functional annotation therefore prioritizes glucose-to-sorbitol reduction and polyol-dependent signaling while marking additional substrates and exact protein localization as unresolved. (yang2019sugaralcoholsof pages 5-6, scarpati2019acombinedcomputational pages 16-17, scarpati2019acombinedcomputational pages 7-8)

**Overall assessment:** CG6084/Ar1 is a genuine *D. melanogaster* AKR with particularly strong **gene-specific genetic evidence in hemocytes**, broader **double-mutant evidence for glucose-derived polyol signaling**, and newer **preprint-level evidence in glia**. The clearest remaining experiments would assay purified fly Ar1 against defined substrates and cofactors, measure Ar1-specific metabolic flux separately from CG10638, and image or biochemically localize the native protein. (yang2019sugaralcoholsof pages 6-7, sano2022thepolyolpathway pages 4-6, kulshrestha2024anessentialrole pages 4-9, yang2019sugaralcoholsof pages 5-6)
