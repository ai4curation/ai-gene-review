CC   -!- FUNCTION: Stores iron in a soluble, non-toxic, readily available form
CC       (By similarity) (PubMed:23771129). Important for iron homeostasis (By
CC       similarity) (PubMed:23064556, PubMed:23771129). Iron is taken up in the
CC       ferrous form and deposited as ferric hydroxides after oxidation (By
CC       similarity) (PubMed:17603097, PubMed:23771129). Ferritin is composed of
CC       a heavy (H) chain which is responsible for the oxidation and uptake of
CC       ferrous iron, and a light (L) chain which facilitates the nucleation of
CC       the ferrihydrite iron core (PubMed:23771129). Required for dietary iron
CC       absorption in the midgut (PubMed:23064556). Involved in tissue iron
CC       detoxification by exporting excess iron (PubMed:23064556). Functions as
CC       an antioxidant and protects the developing organs from cell-mediated
CC       ferroptosis (PubMed:31568497). Required for embryo and larval
CC       development (PubMed:26192321). Plays a role in blood cell (haemocyte)
CC       differentiation in the lymph gland at the larval stage
CC       (PubMed:29237257). May also store Zn(2+) and Mn(2+) and thus may play a
CC       role in zinc and manganese homeostasis (PubMed:23771129).
CC       {ECO:0000255|RuleBase:RU361145, ECO:0000269|PubMed:17603097,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:23064556, ECO:0000269|PubMed:23771129,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:26192321, ECO:0000269|PubMed:29237257,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:31568497}.

CC       12 nm and contains a central cavity into which the insoluble ferric
CC       iron core is deposited (PubMed:23771129). {ECO:0000269|PubMed:23771129,

CC   -!- DEVELOPMENTAL STAGE: Expressed in embryos, first, second, and third
CC       instar larvae, pupae and adults (at protein level) (PubMed:11804801,
CC       PubMed:23064556, PubMed:9801172). Expressed in the gut and hemolymph of
CC       second instar larvae, wandering larvae and early pupae (at protein
CC       level) (PubMed:11804801, PubMed:17603097). In embryos, expressed in the
CC       neuroectoderm (PubMed:26192321). Expressed in the middle part of the
CC       gut in third instar larvae (PubMed:17603097). During embryogenesis,
CC       expressed in blastoderm, in mesoderm cells during germ-band elongation
CC       that give rise to fat bodies and amnioserosa and in cells that give
CC       rise macrophages in the anterior head region (PubMed:17603097). During
CC       germ-band retraction and dorsal closure, expressed in amnioserosa
CC       (PubMed:17603097). At late stages of embryogenesis, expressed in cells
CC       in the developing midgut (PubMed:17603097). At the wandering third
CC       instar, expressed in brain hemispheres (but not in optic lobes),
CC       ventral nerve cord, fat body, pericardial cells, Garland cells,
CC       intestine, Malpighian tubules, lymph gland blood cells and circulating
CC       blood cells (PubMed:29237257). {ECO:0000269|PubMed:11804801,

CC   -!- DISRUPTION PHENOTYPE: Embryonic lethal with some embryos displaying
CC       cuticle and nervous system defects (PubMed:17603097, PubMed:26192321).
CC       RNAi-mediated knockdown is lethal at second-instar stage at 25 degrees
CC       Celsius and first-instar stage at 29 degrees Celsius (PubMed:23064556).
CC       Reduces Fer2LCH protein levels (PubMed:23064556). RNAi-mediated
CC       knockdown in the midgut causes a growth delay and most flies die at the
CC       larval or pupal stage; lethality is partially reduced on an iron
CC       supplemented diet and enhanced in presence of the iron chelator BPS
CC       (PubMed:23064556). Few surviving adults have a smaller and shorter
CC       intestine (PubMed:23064556). Also, results in gut iron accumulation and
CC       systemic iron deficiency, reduces Mvl transcription and aconitase
CC       activity (PubMed:23064556). Also causes an increase in crystal cell
CC       differentiation in the lymph gland (PubMed:29237257). RNAi-mediated
CC       knockdown in progenitor blood cells reduces crystal cell
CC       differentiation (PubMed:29237257). RNAi-mediated knockdown in mature
CC       blood cells causes an increase in the number of crystal cells and in
CC       the lymph gland cortical zone (PubMed:29237257). RNAi-mediated
CC       knockdown in the eye results in rough eyes, necrotic black patches on
CC       eyes during aging of the flies, enlarged and malformed rhabdomere in
CC       the retina and iron accumulation in the head (PubMed:23064556). RNAi-
CC       mediated knockdown in the nervous system results in growth delay,
CC       reduced survival, non-fully developed retina and neurodegenerative
CC       vacuoles in brain (PubMed:23064556). RNAi-mediated knockdown in the
CC       wings results in small and crinkled wings (PubMed:23064556). RNAi-
CC       mediated knockdown in the wing pouch results in small adult wings or no
CC       wings and in some cases in lethality (PubMed:31568497). During larval
CC       disk development, cell death in the pouch is increased and cell
CC       proliferation rate is reduced, mitochondria are round and fragmented
CC       and levels of reactive oxygen species (ROS) are increased
CC       (PubMed:31568497). RNAi-mediated knockdown in the posterior is lethal
CC       before the third instar stage; the few survivors have small larval
CC       disks and increased number of apoptotic cells (PubMed:31568497). RNAi-
CC       mediated knockdown in embryonic epidermis and imaginal disks results is
CC       lethal at the larval stage (PubMed:23064556). RNAi-mediated knockdown
CC       in endoderm or mesoderm results in growth delay and death at the pupal
CC       stage (PubMed:23064556). RNAi-mediated knockdown in fat body or somatic
CC       muscles causes no defect (PubMed:23064556). RNAi-mediated knockdown in
CC       cry+ neurons (LNds and s-LNvs) has no effect on circadian activity in
CC       the absence of external cues (PubMed:22885802).
