# Functional annotation of *Drosophila melanogaster* Wdr20

**Identity and principal finding.** The specified UniProt accession **Q9VBC4** is supplied for the fruit-fly gene **Wdr20**, also called **CG6420**. This identification is independently corroborated by a 2023 primary study that names CG6420 as the fly Wdr20 ortholog and reports conservation of its WD40 repeats with human WDR20. Thus, the relevant fly protein is a **WD40-repeat regulatory protein**, not the mammalian protein studied under the same symbol. Its best-supported function is to assist the **Usp46–Uaf1–Wdr20 deubiquitylase complex** in maintaining the Wingless/Wnt receptor **Arrow** (the fly LRP6 homolog), thereby strengthening signaling in Wingless-responsive cells. (spencer2023theusp46deubiquitylase pages 2-4, spencer2023theusp46deubiquitylase pages 1-2)

## Molecular function and pathway

Wdr20 is a **noncatalytic cofactor**, not an enzyme with an independently established reaction or substrate specificity. The complex’s catalytic component is **Usp46**, which removes ubiquitin from protein substrates; Uaf1 and Wdr20 are WD40-repeat regulatory partners. In the fly system, the experimentally supported substrate of the *complex* is Arrow: complex expression reduces ubiquitylated Arrow in cultured cells and increases Arrow abundance at the cell surface. These findings support a model in which Wdr20 helps the complex counteract Arrow ubiquitylation and turnover. They do **not** establish that Wdr20 itself hydrolyzes ubiquitin or that it directly recognizes Arrow independently of its partners. (spencer2023theusp46deubiquitylase pages 1-2, spencer2023theusp46deubiquitylase pages 11-12, spencer2023theusp46deubiquitylase pages 10-11)

The pathway consequence is specific. More available Arrow increases cellular responsiveness to the Wingless morphogen; downstream, cytoplasmic **Armadillo/β-catenin** accumulates and Wingless-responsive genes are activated. Genetic epistasis places the complex upstream of the β-catenin destruction complex, consistent with action at the receptor level. Depleting Wdr20, Usp46, or Uaf1 did **not** reduce *wingless* expression, arguing against impaired ligand production as the explanation for reduced signaling. (spencer2023theusp46deubiquitylase pages 2-4, spencer2023theusp46deubiquitylase pages 9-10, spencer2023theusp46deubiquitylase pages 4-7)

## Evidence in the target organism

The central fly study is **Spencer et al., *Nature Communications*, October 2023**, [DOI: 10.1038/s41467-023-41843-0](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-023-41843-0). Its conclusions rest on complementary genetic and biochemical tests rather than annotation by homology alone. (spencer2023theusp46deubiquitylase pages 7-9, spencer2023theusp46deubiquitylase pages 2-4)

* **Developing wing:** Wdr20 RNAi reduced expression of the Wingless target *senseless*. CRISPR targeting of Wdr20 also reduced Senseless in the targeted compartment, and independent guide RNAs reproduced the effect. Arrow overexpression rescued the reduced Senseless readout caused by Wdr20 depletion, supporting Arrow as a consequential downstream target of the complex. (spencer2023theusp46deubiquitylase pages 2-4, spencer2023theusp46deubiquitylase pages 9-10)
* **Adult intestinal epithelium:** Independent Wdr20 mutant clones reduced the Wingless reporters *fz3-GFP* and *nkd-lacZ*. The *fz3-GFP* defect was prominent approximately **8–20 cell lengths** from the midgut–hindgut boundary, consistent with a role in maintaining signaling farther from the ligand source. Wdr20 loss also increased stem/progenitor proliferation in this tissue; this is a physiological consequence of altered signaling, not a separate established biochemical activity of Wdr20. (spencer2023theusp46deubiquitylase pages 4-4)
* **Receptor and downstream protein:** Wdr20-deficient fly clones showed cell-autonomous reductions in Arrow and cytoplasmic Armadillo. In fly S2R+ cells, Wdr20 knockdown reduced steady-state Arrow; the reported Arrow reduction across **three experiments** was significant at **p < 0.0001**. Co-immunoprecipitation, Arrow ubiquitylation assays, and surface-biotinylation experiments further support physical association of complex components with Arrow and complex-dependent receptor stabilization and surface accumulation. Association of the *complex* should not be mistaken for proof that purified fly Wdr20 alone binds or deubiquitylates Arrow. (spencer2023theusp46deubiquitylase pages 9-10, spencer2023theusp46deubiquitylase pages 11-12, spencer2023theusp46deubiquitylase pages 10-11)

## Where does Wdr20 act?

**The defensible localization assignment is functional, not a directly imaged Wdr20 compartment.** Its demonstrated effects occur within Wingless-responding fly cells, where the Usp46 complex controls the abundance of a membrane receptor. The cell-surface measurements concern **Arrow**, not Wdr20; the available fly experiments do not establish an endogenous Wdr20 localization by imaging or fractionation. Consequently, a precise assignment of fly Wdr20 to the plasma membrane, a particular endosome, or the nucleus would overstate the evidence. The receptor-regulatory model implies access to the intracellular side of the Arrow trafficking pathway, but does not pinpoint where Wdr20 resides or where Arrow’s ubiquitin is removed. (spencer2023theusp46deubiquitylase pages 12-13, spencer2023theusp46deubiquitylase pages 1-2, spencer2023theusp46deubiquitylase pages 11-12)

## Structural interpretation and 2024 developments

Structural evidence strengthens the cofactor interpretation without replacing fly-specific experiments. **Zhu et al., *Cell Discovery*, July 2019**, [DOI: 10.1038/s41421-019-0102-1](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41421-019-0102-1), determined a **3.1 Å** structure of a USP46–WDR48–WDR20 complex. In that system, WDR20 binds near the base of the USP46 palm, away from its catalytic center; WDR48 binds the fingers region. Purified USP46 had no measurable activity in the reported ubiquitin–AMC assay without partners, while the two WD40 proteins stimulated activity independently and more strongly together. This explains why WD40 conservation supports a regulatory scaffold/allosteric role for fly Wdr20, but the structure does not determine a fly-specific interface or reaction rate. (zhu2019structuralinsightsinto pages 1-3, spencer2023theusp46deubiquitylase pages 2-4)

Two **2024 mammalian** studies broaden what the complex can do, but **their substrates must not be assigned to fly CG6420 without fly experiments**. **Yu et al., *EMBO Reports*, November 2024**, [DOI: 10.1038/s44319-024-00300-9](https://doi.org/10.1038/s44319-024-00300-9), found that USP12 *or* USP46 with WDR48 and WDR20 removes ubiquitin from internalized **integrin β1** in early endosomes, limiting ESCRT-mediated lysosomal sorting and preserving receptor abundance. The study supports an endosomal site for that mammalian complex’s activity, not direct endosomal localization of fly Wdr20. (yu2024theusp1246deubiquitinases pages 11-13, yu2024theusp1246deubiquitinases pages 1-2) **Jiang et al., *PNAS*, October 2024**, [DOI: 10.1073/pnas.2407904121](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2407904121), described **human** WDR20 as a shared adaptor coordinating USP12/USP46-dependent c-Myc stabilization in hepatocellular-carcinoma models; evidence included **three patient-derived organoids** and an **88-sample** tumor tissue microarray. These are human disease findings, not demonstrated fly Wdr20 functions or clinical applications of the fly gene. (jiang2024wdr20preventshepatocellular pages 1-2)

For comparison, **Hodul et al., *Journal of Neuroscience*, February 2021**, [DOI: 10.1523/JNEUROSCI.1074-20.2021](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.1074-20.2021), showed that the *C. elegans* WDR-20/USP-46 system increases neuronal GLR-1 glutamate-receptor surface levels; combined cofactor expression increased the measured surface signal by **nearly twofold**. This provides an independent example of WDR20-family involvement in receptor trafficking, **not** evidence that fly Wdr20 regulates glutamate receptors. (hodul2021thewd40repeatprotein pages 4-5, hodul2021thewd40repeatprotein pages 5-6)

**Annotation conclusion.** The most precise current functional description is: *Drosophila* Wdr20/CG6420 is a conserved WD40-repeat, noncatalytic regulatory component of the Usp46–Uaf1 deubiquitylase complex that promotes Arrow receptor stability and cell-surface abundance, thereby increasing Wingless/Wnt signaling strength and spatial reach.
