Gene Description

eyes absent (eya) encodes the founding member of the EYA family, a conserved nuclear transcriptional coactivator that also carries a C-terminal Eya domain (ED) belonging to the haloacid dehalogenase (HAD) phosphatase superfamily. Eya lacks DNA-binding activity and is recruited to target enhancers by the SIX-family homeodomain factor Sine oculis (So); in the So/Eya complex So supplies DNA binding and Eya supplies transactivation through an internal proline/serine/threonine-rich region, and Eya also binds Dachshund. The ED is a metal (Mg2+)-dependent protein tyrosine phosphatase that uses an aspartate nucleophile and can dephosphorylate itself; Abelson kinase phosphorylation shifts a pool of Eya to the cytoplasm, where its phosphatase activity contributes to axon pathfinding, although genomic rescue shows that tyrosine phosphatase activity is dispensable for normal eye development and viability. Within the retinal determination network eya is induced by Eyeless, acts in positive feedback with so, eyeless and dachshund, is required for survival and differentiation of eye disc progenitors, and can induce ectopic eyes. Outside the eye eya is required for somatic gonadal precursor identity and gonad formation, somatic cyst cell support of spermatocyte development, main-body follicle cell fate (repressing polar cell fate), Bolwig's organ formation, somatic muscle development downstream of Tinman, and specification of Apterous cluster neurons in the ventral nerve cord.

Core Functions

Eya is a non-DNA-binding nuclear transcriptional coactivator recruited to enhancers by the SIX-family factor So; within the So/Eya complex its P/S/T-rich transactivation region (substitutable by VP16) activates RNA polymerase II target genes such as dac, atonal and string, driving eye-disc progenitor survival and compound eye development.
