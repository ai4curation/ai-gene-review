Gene Description

NudE is the single Drosophila member of the NudE/NDE1-NDEL1 family, a coiled-coil protein that binds Lis1 and cytoplasmic dynein and tethers Lis1 to the motor to promote dynein function. In dividing larval neuroblasts NudE localizes to kinetochores (dependent on Lis1 and Cenp-meta) and streams onto kinetochore microtubules; it is needed for focused spindle poles, attachment of centrosomes to the spindle, chromosome congression and dynein-dependent stripping of checkpoint proteins, so null mutant neuroblasts arrest in metaphase. In male meiosis it associates with the nuclear envelope and spindle envelope and is required for centrosome migration from the cortex to the nucleus and accurate chromosome segregation. In the ovary NudE is required in the germline for oocyte specification, and in border cells for collective migration. In sensory neurons it co-moves with Golgi outposts and other cargoes and is required, with Lis1, for dendrite arborization, uniform axonal microtubule polarity and positioning of the nucleus relative to the primary dendrites.

Functional annotation report: Drosophila melanogaster nudE (UniProt Q9VT70)

Executive conclusion

The requested protein is correctly identified as the Drosophila melanogaster Nuclear distribution protein NudE, encoded by nudE (CG8104; FBgn0036059), not a mammalian NDE1/NDEL1 protein or another similarly named gene. The fly has a single experimentally studied NudE-family protein, whereas mammals have the related paralogs NDE1 and NDEL1; conclusions below therefore concern the fly protein unless explicitly identified as inference. Direct fly experiments describe an N-terminal coiled-coil/Lis1-interaction region and a poorly conserved C-terminal region, consistent with the supplied NUDE, NUDE_dom and NUDE_C annotations (arthur2015dendritearborizationrequires pages 1-2, arthur2015dendritearborizationrequires pages 6-7).

Primary functional annotation: NudE is a non-enzymatic cytoplasmic-dynein regulatory cofactor/scaffold. Its central role is to couple or stabilize Lis1-dependent dynein activity at selected cellular structures—not to catalyze a chemical reaction or transport a substrate itself. The strongest direct evidence supports functions at kinetochores and spindle-associated structures during cell division, in centrosome migration and spindle-pole coupling, and in neuronal Golgi-outpost behavior and microtubule organization. Lis1 is the best-supported physical partner: tandem-affinity purification recovered a strong NudE–Lis1 association, and genetic/rescue experiments place NudE, Lis1 and dynein in the same functional pathway (jacklyn2021rolesofthe pages 62-81, jacklyn2021rolesofthe pages 30-38, arthur2015dendritearborizationrequires pages 1-2).

Identity and ambiguity control

The target identity is supported by studies using the Drosophila protein-null allele nudE39A, which deletes the first three exons and part of the fourth and eliminates detectable NudE protein. This allele causes larval/pupal lethality and the cellular phenotypes summarized below (jacklyn2021rolesofthe pages 13-20). A genomic construct containing the full nudE transcript, approximately 650 bp upstream sequence and 420 bp downstream of the 3′ UTR rescued lethality and neuronal morphology, providing particularly strong gene-to-phenotype attribution (arthur2015dendritearborizationrequires pages 2-3, arthur2015dendritearborizationrequires pages 9-10).

No literature on mammalian NDE1, NDEL1/NUDEL, fungal NudE homologs, or the unrelated dynein-associated protein NudC was used as direct evidence for Q9VT70. Conserved-family models are mentioned only where they agree with fly genetics and biochemistry.

Molecular function and structural logic

NudE-family proteins contain an N-terminal coiled-coil that engages Lis1 and a less-conserved, largely unstructured C-terminal region. In fly sensory neurons, increasing Lis1 dosage rescued nudE-null dendrite length, branch number and branch distribution, while reducing dynein light-intermediate-chain function enhanced the nudE phenotype. These observations support the expert model that fly NudE facilitates a productive Lis1–dynein interaction rather than acting as a motor itself (arthur2015dendritearborizationrequires pages 1-2, arthur2015dendritearborizationrequires pages 6-7).

Domain-rescue experiments refine this model. A truncation after residue 196, NudEΔC, fully rescued dendrite-arborization defects when supplied either neuronally or genomically. By contrast, substitutions in conserved N-terminal Lis1-interaction residues produced incomplete rescue. Thus, the N-terminal interaction module is functionally important, whereas the C-terminal region is dispensable for dendrite morphogenesis and may inhibit, tune, or switch NudE activity in that particular context (arthur2015dendritearborizationrequires pages 7-8, arthur2015dendritearborizationrequires pages 6-7, arthur2015dendritearborizationrequires pages 9-10). This conclusion should not be generalized to every tissue: dispensability was established for neuronal arborization, not for all mitotic or developmental functions.

There is no enzymatic reaction, catalytic substrate, or transporter substrate to assign. NudE is best classified as a dynein-regulatory adapter/cofactor whose relevant “cargoes” or sites vary by context, including kinetochores, centrosome/spindle structures and neuronal Golgi outposts.

Cellular localization and site of action

During larval neuroblast mitosis, NudE occurs at discrete chromosome-associated sites adjacent to the centromere marker CID, identifying them as kinetochores. At metaphase, it can extend or “stream” from kinetochores along kinetochore microtubules; this was observed in 22% of 50 metaphase cells, compared with 54% for the RZZ component Rod. NudE disappears from discrete structures during anaphase/telophase (jacklyn2021rolesofthe pages 13-20).

In male meiosis, NudE is prominent at the nuclear envelope during prophase, at kinetochores and kinetochore microtubules during prometaphase/metaphase, and in the spindle-envelope region during anaphase I. In lis1 or cenp-meta mutants it fails to localize normally to kinetochores and can instead accumulate at spindle poles or in spindle-envelope patches, showing that localization is dynamically regulated rather than constitutively centrosomal (jacklyn2021rolesofthe pages 7-13, jacklyn2021rolesofthe pages 62-81).

In class-IV dendritic-arborization neurons, fluorescent NudE associates with vesicular or organellar structures and colocalizes with ManII-positive dendritic Golgi outposts. Nearly two-thirds of the colocalized particles were stationary. This places neuronal NudE at or near a dynein-regulated organelle population, although direct membrane binding or physical tethering was not demonstrated (arthur2015dendritearborizationrequires pages 8-9, arthur2015dendritearborizationrequires pages 2-3).

Biological pathways and processes

1. Kinetochore dynein, chromosome congression and checkpoint silencing

NudE is needed for normal chromosome alignment, spindle organization and progression out of prometaphase/metaphase. Protein-null neuroblasts had chromosome overcondensation in 89.4% of cells and a relative mitotic index of 3.00; 6,055 prometaphase/metaphase cells versus only 53 anaphase/telophase cells were scored. Removing the RZZ/checkpoint component Zwilch largely restored mitotic progression, indicating that much of the arrest is imposed by an active spindle-assembly checkpoint rather than an inability to execute anaphase (jacklyn2021rolesofthe pages 13-20, jacklyn2021rolesofthe pages 20-30).

NudE is not simply a universal kinetochore-targeting factor. Dynein heavy chain, Bub3, Rod, ZW10 and Zwilch can remain at nudE mutant kinetochores, but poleward streaming of Rod/ZW10/Zwilch is impaired. Conversely, NudE kinetochore recruitment requires Lis1 and Cenp-meta and is perturbed in bub3 mutants, but does not require the RZZ components ZW10, Rod or Zwilch. The evidence therefore favors a role in activating or organizing kinetochore dynein and removing checkpoint proteins rather than recruiting every component (jacklyn2021rolesofthe pages 30-38, jacklyn2021rolesofthe pages 46-50).

Meiotic segregation is correspondingly severe: only 9 of 26 meiosis-I divisions and 1 of 9 meiosis-II divisions in the reported nudE39A sample segregated DNA normally. Mutants displayed bridges or lagging DNA, unequal segregation and abnormal postmeiotic nuclei (jacklyn2021rolesofthe pages 62-81, jacklyn2021rolesofthe pages 30-38).

2. Centrosome migration and spindle-pole attachment

NudE also acts in centrosome positioning and mechanical integration with the spindle. Mutant neuroblasts exhibit broad or unfocused poles, curved or non-bipolar spindles, centrosomes detached from spindle poles and occasional supernumerary centrosomes. Unaligned chromosomes occurred in 61% of mutant versus 42% of control prometaphase/metaphase cells (n=189 and 176; P<0.0001) (jacklyn2021rolesofthe pages 20-30).

The spermatocyte phenotype is more striking: centrosome–spindle detachment occurred in 88% of 77 mutant meiosis-I cells and in none of 46 controls. Live imaging showed failure of centrosomes to migrate from the cell cortex to the nuclear envelope, leaving spindle-like microtubule arrays disconnected from membrane-associated asters. Together with the Lis1 association, these observations support a dynein-regulatory role in force generation or force transmission during centrosome movement and spindle-pole coupling (jacklyn2021rolesofthe pages 20-30).

3. Neuronal morphogenesis, organelle behavior and microtubule organization

In intact third-instar larvae, nudE39A homozygous and nudE39A/deficiency class-IV ddaC neurons had significantly reduced dendrite length and branch number, especially in distal arbor regions. Their normally unbranched axons formed multiple fine branches near the soma, sometimes extending into the dendritic field. A wild-type genomic transgene fully restored dendrite growth and branching; neuron-specific UAS-nudE gave partial rescue, plausibly because of timing or expression-level differences (arthur2015dendritearborizationrequires pages 2-3).

EB1–GFP imaging showed that dendritic microtubules retained their predominantly cell-body-directed orientation, but microtubule dynamics increased. Axons lost their normal uniformly plus-end-distal organization and acquired retrograde EB1 comets, indicating mixed polarity. The analysis included 156 comets in mutant axons and 540 in mutant dendrites, with the polarity difference reported at P<0.0001. Reducing γ-tubulin activity did not remove the retrograde axonal comets, arguing against excess γ-tubulin-dependent nucleation as the simple cause (arthur2015dendritearborizationrequires pages 4-6).

The colocalization of NudE with Golgi outposts, appearance of ectopic axonal Golgi outposts in mutants, and altered microtubule organization suggest two potentially connected roles: regulation of dynein-driven outpost transport or capture, and maintenance of the microtubule tracks that support polarized neuronal trafficking. The authors specifically proposed action at transport initiation/termination or tethering of stationary outposts, but this remains a mechanistic interpretation because NudE lacks a demonstrated intrinsic microtubule-binding or membrane-tethering activity in these experiments (arthur2015dendritearborizationrequires pages 8-9).

4. Collective border-cell migration

A screen of 67 microtubule-interacting genes identified a cell-autonomous requirement for NudE in ovarian border-cell migration. NudE perturbation reduced net cluster migration, with particularly strong effects on migration initiation and formation of the dominant front-cell protrusion; cluster organization and adhesion-molecule localization were also abnormal. These results place NudE with Lis1 and dynein in the spatial control of protrusive polarity and cell–cell adhesion during invasive migration over other cells (yang2012microtubulesandlis1nudedynein pages 1-2, yang2012microtubulesandlis1nudedynein pages 5-7). The retrieved text did not provide a NudE-specific numerical effect size, so the migration evidence is less quantitatively resolved than the mitotic and neuronal evidence.

Current applications and real-world implementation

There is no established clinical, agricultural or biotechnological application directed specifically at fly NudE/Q9VT70. Its practical use is as a genetic and cell-biological model for conserved Lis1–dynein regulation. The available toolkit includes a protein-null allele, genomic and neuron-specific rescue transgenes, fluorescent Cherry–NudE, EB1–GFP microtubule imaging, Golgi-outpost markers and tissue-specific RNAi. These permit mechanistic dissection of mitosis, neuronal polarity, organelle positioning and collective migration (arthur2015dendritearborizationrequires pages 2-3, arthur2015dendritearborizationrequires pages 9-10, yang2012microtubulesandlis1nudedynein pages 5-7).

This model is relevant to understanding conserved dynein/Lis1 biology, but human disease claims should be made through direct studies of human NDE1 or NDEL1 rather than by treating fly NudE as molecularly identical. The single fly gene can be experimentally advantageous because it avoids the NDE1/NDEL1 paralog redundancy present in mammals (arthur2015dendritearborizationrequires pages 1-2).

Recent articles concerning mammalian NDE1/NDEL1, other organisms’ NudE homologs, or general dynein mechanisms should not be represented as new evidence for Q9VT70.

Confidence assessment and unresolved questions

High-confidence conclusions are that Q9VT70 is a non-enzymatic NudE-family dynein cofactor; it forms a strong association with Lis1; it is required for normal kinetochore/spindle behavior, checkpoint-protein streaming, centrosome migration, neuronal arborization and axonal microtubule polarity; and its N-terminal Lis1-interaction region is functionally important (arthur2015dendritearborizationrequires pages 6-7, jacklyn2021rolesofthe pages 62-81, jacklyn2021rolesofthe pages 30-38).

Moderate-confidence mechanistic conclusions are that NudE promotes or stabilizes Lis1–dynein engagement and that this explains most phenotypes. Genetic interaction and rescue data strongly support this model, but stable soluble association with dynein itself was not convincingly detected in the reported TAP experiment, implying that the interaction may be transient, structure-specific or indirect (jacklyn2021rolesofthe pages 30-38).

Important unresolved issues include how NudE is targeted to Golgi outposts and the nuclear envelope; whether it directly links dynein to particular cargoes; how its C-terminus regulates activity outside dendrites; and how its kinetochore, centrosome and neuronal functions are coordinated. The precise centrosome-positioning mechanism remains unresolved, and the Golgi-outpost tethering model has not been directly demonstrated (arthur2015dendritearborizationrequires pages 8-9, jacklyn2021rolesofthe pages 46-50).

Recommended concise annotation

NudE (Q9VT70) is a Drosophila NudE-family, non-catalytic cytoplasmic-dynein cofactor that binds Lis1 through its conserved N-terminal coiled-coil and promotes context-dependent dynein function at kinetochores, spindle/centrosome-associated structures and neuronal organelles. It is required for chromosome congression and checkpoint-protein removal, centrosome migration and spindle-pole coupling, normal dendrite arborization, Golgi-outpost organization, axonal microtubule polarity and efficient collective border-cell migration.

2026-09-27 session (claude-code)

Sources: UniProt Q9VT70, GOA seed (36 rows), cached publications (all GOA PMIDs have full text); Falcon deep research not yet available at time of writing.

Biology summary

  • Single fly NudE/NDE1-NDEL1 family member PMID:19417004.
  • Localization: kinetochores, spindles, nuclear envelope PMID:19417004; spindle envelope in male meiosis; NOT at centrosomes PMID:19417004.
  • Phenotypes: centrosome detachment, unfocused spindle poles, congression failure with SAC arrest, failed centrosome migration to spermatocyte NE, male meiotic missegregation PMID:19417004.
  • Lis1 partner: TAP-tag association PMID:19417004; dendrite defects rescued by Lis1, Lis1-binding mutant only partially rescues PMID:25908857.
  • Neuronal cargo transport: co-moves with retrograde Golgi outposts, colocalizes with SV/mito markers PMID:25908857.
  • Nuclear positioning in da neurons PMID:26490864.
  • Oocyte specification PMID:24531791; border cell migration PMID:22808215.

Decisions

  • Centrosome IBA/IEA and spindle pole centrosome ISS -> MARK_AS_OVER_ANNOTATED (fly-specific immunostaining negative).
  • Centrosome duplication ISS -> MARK_AS_OVER_ANNOTATED (fly phenotype is detachment/migration).
  • Kinesin complex IBA -> REMOVE (consistent with human NDE1/NDEL1 reviews).
  • Microtubule binding (IBA/IEA/ISS) -> KEEP_AS_NON_CORE (authors note no known MT-binding domain).
  • NEW GO:0140659 cytoskeletal motor regulator activity (ISS from human Nde1 reconstitution, PMID:37940657), matching the module's NudE-unit term and human NDEL1.

Module relevance (nucleokinesis)

  • Fly NudE supports the module's NudE-unit role (Lis1-dynein regulator; GO:0140659) and a nucleus-positioning role in postmitotic neurons, but there is no fly evidence for centrosome residency, differing from mammalian NDE1/NDEL1.

Deep research (Falcon) incorporated

  • Consistent with review; supports non-centrosomal localization [file:DROME/nudE/nudE-deep-research-falcon.md "showing that localization is dynamically regulated rather than constitutively centrosomal"] and notes no stable NudE-dynein association in fly TAP ("stable soluble association with dynein itself was not convincingly detected in the reported TAP experiment"), so GO:0140659 kept as ISS.
