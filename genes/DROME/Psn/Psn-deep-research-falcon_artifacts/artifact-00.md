| Functional Aspect | Details |
|---|---|
| Molecular function | **Psn (DmPS)** is a polytopic intramembrane aspartyl protease and the catalytic core of the *Drosophila* γ-secretase complex. (pqac-00000002, pqac-00000017) |
| Catalytic mechanism | Two conserved aspartates in transmembrane domains 6 and 7 form the active site; mutation of either abolishes γ-secretase activity. Psn hydrolyzes peptide bonds within substrate transmembrane helices in a water-accessible intramembrane cavity. (pqac-00000003, pqac-00000007) |
| Protein complex and maturation | Active γ-secretase contains Psn, Nicastrin (Nct), Aph-1, and Pen-2, approximately one copy of each. Psn is endoproteolyzed into associated N- and C-terminal fragments during complex maturation; Pen-2 promotes this activation step. (pqac-00000012, pqac-00000017, pqac-00000018) |
| Substrate specificity | The best-supported fly substrate is **Notch**; APP-family processing is studied using endogenous Appl and human APP-derived reporters. γ-Secretase generally accepts ectodomain-shed type-I membrane-protein stubs rather than recognizing one strict sequence. Delta and numerous adhesion/signaling receptors are broader metazoan substrates, but not all have been demonstrated directly for fly Psn. (pqac-00000007, pqac-00000020, pqac-00000046, pqac-00000054) |
| Cleavage products | Notch cleavage releases **NICD** for nuclear signaling. Cleavage of APP-derived C83 or C99 produces **AICD** plus p3 or Aβ peptides, respectively; human C99 reporters confirm Psn-dependent intracellular-fragment release in flies. Endogenous fly Appl products are less completely mapped, and flies lack the normal mammalian BACE-driven amyloidogenic context. (pqac-00000020, pqac-00000045, pqac-00000047, pqac-00000050) |
| Subcellular localization | Psn is embedded in endomembranes. γ-Secretase assembles in the ER, matures during Golgi transit, and reaches the plasma membrane and endosomes; activity or complex-associated protein is also reported in late endosomal/lysosomal compartments. A proposed mitochondrial Psn pool in Debcl-dependent apoptosis remains less firmly established. (pqac-00000009, pqac-00000011, pqac-00000043) |
| Primary pathway | In canonical Notch signaling, ligand engagement and ADAM-family ectodomain cleavage leave a membrane-tethered Notch stub. Psn/γ-secretase then releases NICD, which enters the nucleus and cooperates with Suppressor of Hairless and Mastermind to activate target genes. (pqac-00000020, pqac-00000024) |
| Secondary functions | Psn participates in APP/Appl processing and is linked to endolysosomal function, calcium and mitochondrial homeostasis, and neuronal maintenance. A genetically supported but mechanistically unresolved γ-secretase-independent interaction with the pro-apoptotic protein Debcl has also been proposed. (pqac-00000033, pqac-00000039, pqac-00000041) |
| Biological roles | Psn-dependent Notch activation controls developmental cell-fate and tissue-patterning decisions. In adults, neuron-selective Psn reduction causes progressive vacuolization, apoptosis, locomotor impairment, and shortened lifespan, supporting an evolutionarily conserved neuroprotective role during aging. (pqac-00000029, pqac-00000036, pqac-00000038) |
| Recent findings, 2023–2024 | Recent work strengthens links among APP-family biology, proteostasis, autophagy, and aging, but these findings concern Appl and do not by themselves prove direct Psn catalysis in those pathways. A 2024 *Genetics* study showed that loss of lipophorin receptors **LpR1/LpR2**, especially LpR2, aggravates apoptosis and age-dependent neurodegeneration after neuronal Psn reduction, implicating lipid/lipoprotein homeostasis as a modifier rather than a defined Psn substrate pathway. (pqac-00000034, pqac-00000037, pqac-00000040) |
