Automatically Generated Summary (FlyBase)

Gene Group (FlyBase)
BETA IMPORTINS -
Importin-β family proteins are nucleocytoplasmic transport receptors that import and export proteins and RNAs through the nuclear pores. In some import/export pathways, importin-β directly interacts with the cargo. For cargo containing a classical nuclear localization (cNLS) signal, importin-α acts as an adaptor linking the cargo to importin-β. (Adapted from FBrf0235243, FBrf0131350 and PMID:21029754).
Summary (Interactive Fly)

Importin 7 - functions in nuclear transport of MAPK - surface receptor signaling regulates MAPK localization through the adaptor protein Corkscrew

              Other Comments

              In an EMS screen for dominant modifiers of the eye phenotype caused by expression of sensScer\UAS.cNa under the control of Scer\GAL4lz-gal4. 15 alleles of msk have been isolated in the screen.

              (Pepple et al., 2007)

              msk is involved in the nuclear transportation of rho.

              (Marenda et al., 2006)

              msk is required for cell proliferation and survival in phase 0 of eye development, as well as for correct ommatidial rotation (and not chirality) in phase 2.

              (Vrailas et al., 2006)

              msk is required for cell proliferation in the larval stage, and later for the survival of postmitotic cells.

              (Vrailas et al., 2006)

              msk is genetically downstream of the hh and dpp receptors in the eye furrow.

              (Vrailas et al., 2006)

              Identification: in a yeast two hybrid screen for interactors with csw protein.

              (Lorenzen et al., 2001)

              The msk protein interacts with the C-terminal domain of csw.

              (Lorenzen et al., 2001)

              Identification: Screen for proteins that bind csw protein.

              (Lorenzen et al., 1999)

              Identification: Yeast two hybrid screen for proteins that interact specifically with the carboxyl terminal region of the csw protein.

              (Lorenzen et al., 1998)

              msk encodes a phosphoprotein that interacts with both the csw and drk proteins.

              (Lorenzen et al., 1998)
