Gene Description

patched (ptc) is a 1286-residue, twelve-pass integral membrane protein of the RND permease superfamily and the cell-surface receptor for the Hedgehog (Hh) morphogen in Drosophila. It carries a sterol-sensing domain spanning transmembrane helices 2-6, two large glycosylated extracellular loops that form the ligand-binding face, and a long disordered cytoplasmic C-terminal tail. Patched has two linked activities. First, in the absence of ligand it is the pathway's brake: it holds the class-F receptor Smoothened inactive and destabilised, so that Cubitus interruptus is processed into its transcriptional repressor form. Hedgehog binding abolishes this restraint and triggers ubiquitin-dependent internalisation and degradation of Patched, allowing Smoothened to accumulate in a phosphorylated, active state at the plasma membrane. The restraint appears to be indirect and catalytic rather than a stoichiometric Patched-Smoothened association, and in Drosophila it is associated with Patched control of membrane lipid pools: Patched binds phosphatidylinositol 4-phosphate through its sterol-sensing domain and phosphatidic acid through its C-terminal tail, and mutating the phosphatidic acid sites abolishes Smoothened inhibition. Second, because patched is itself a Hedgehog target gene, ligand-induced Patched acts as a sink that binds and internalises Hedgehog and thereby shortens and steepens the morphogen gradient. High-affinity ligand binding and transduction additionally require the Ig/fibronectin co-receptors Interference hedgehog and Brother of ihog, which bind Patched directly and promote its surface presentation, although Patched alone is sufficient to sequester ligand. Patched functions at the plasma membrane, concentrated on the basal surface and on signalling filopodia where Hedgehog-sending and Hedgehog-receiving cells contact, and cycles through endocytic vesicles; in Drosophila olfactory sensory neurons, one of the few ciliated cell types in the animal, it also localises to the ciliary membrane. Independently of Hedgehog signalling, Patched internalises lipophorin particles and lowers intracellular lipid droplet content. Through its control of Hedgehog reception, patched is required for embryonic segment polarity and for anterior/posterior patterning and growth of the imaginal discs, and its loss causes ligand-independent pathway activation, overgrowth and mis-specification in many tissues.

Core Functions

Cell-surface receptor for the Hedgehog morphogen. Patched binds the lipid-modified Hedgehog ligand at the basal plasma membrane and on cytoneme contact sites and transmits the occupancy state across the membrane, switching from the restraining, pathway-off configuration to the relieved, pathway-on configuration. High-affinity binding and transduction are potentiated by the Ig/fibronectin co-receptors Ihog and Boi, which bind Patched directly, but Patched alone is sufficient to retain and sequester ligand.

Suggested Questions for Experts

Q: Does Drosophila Patched move any lipid across or within the membrane, as vertebrate PTCH1 is proposed to do for cholesterol, or is its effect on Smoothened entirely a matter of binding and sequestering PI(4)P and phosphatidic acid in place? No Drosophila transport assay has been reported, and GO:0140303 intramembrane lipid carrier activity is currently asserted for the fly protein only by similarity to human PTCH1.

Q: Are the PI(4)P and phosphatidic acid interactions two readouts of one mechanism or two independent ones? They are localised to different parts of the protein - the sterol-sensing domain and the cytoplasmic tail - and both are reported to be reduced by Hedgehog, so an epistasis or double-mutant analysis would say whether either alone is sufficient.

Q: Does Patched ever contact Smoothened directly in vivo? The stoichiometric argument says the restraint is catalytic and indirect, but Patched and Smoothened do co-fractionate in a large complex; proximity labelling or crosslinking in wing discs would distinguish an incidental co-residence from a functional interaction.

Q: Is the lipophorin receptor activity of Patched ancestral to the family or a Drosophila elaboration, and does it contribute anything to Hedgehog gradient formation beyond the effect on lipid droplets? Its independence from signalling was shown with two separation-of-function alleles, but its normal physiological purpose is unknown.

Q: How much of Drosophila Hedgehog signalling runs through the ciliary route? Patched and Smoothened localise to the ciliary membrane in olfactory sensory neurons, but nearly all other fly cells are non-ciliated; whether the ciliary and cytoplasmic Costal-2 routes use Patched in the same way is untested.

Suggested Experiments

Experiment: Reconstitute purified full-length Patched into proteoliposomes of defined composition and assay for transbilayer or intramembrane movement of cholesterol, PI(4)P and phosphatidic acid, comparing wild type with the sterol-sensing-domain mutant and the PA-motif mutant PtcM124. A binding-only protein will show saturable association with no flux; a carrier will show flux that the separation-of-function mutants lose.

Hypothesis: Drosophila Patched restrains Smoothened by sequestering phosphatidic acid and PI(4)P rather than by catalysing net lipid transport.

Experiment: Build wing-disc rescue lines carrying the sterol-sensing-domain mutation, the PA-motif mutations, and both together in a ptc null background, and score Smoothened accumulation, Ci processing and dpp and ptc reporter expression. Additivity would indicate two mechanisms; epistasis would indicate one.

Hypothesis: The PI(4)P and phosphatidic acid interactions are separable contributions to Smoothened restraint.

Experiment: Apply proximity-dependent biotinylation from tagged Patched in wing discs with and without Hedgehog and compare the labelling of Smoothened with that of known direct partners such as Ihog. A catalytic, indirect mechanism predicts that Smoothened is not preferentially labelled even though it co-fractionates.

Hypothesis: Patched and Smoothened do not contact each other directly in the pathway-off state.

1. What the protein is

Twelve-pass integral membrane protein of the RND (resistance-nodulation-division) permease
superfamily, with a sterol-sensing domain (SSD) spanning TM2–TM6 (UniProt features: DOMAIN 428..583 /note="SSD"),
two large extracellular loops, and a ~180-residue disordered cytoplasmic C-terminal tail
(REGION 1116..1237 /note="Disordered"). TCDB classifies it under the RND superfamily
(DR TCDB; 2.A.6.6.2). It is the founding member of the Patched family; human PTCH1 (Q13635)
and PTCH2 (Q9Y6C5) sit in the same PANTHER family PTHR46022, which is the structural evidence
that the Hedgehog reception tier is conserved from insects to vertebrates
(see modules/hedgehog_signaling.yaml, annoton patched_receptor).

Two things are true simultaneously and are the frame for everything below:

  • ptc is the receptor for the Hedgehog (Hh) morphogen, and
  • ptc is the pathway's brake: in the unliganded state it keeps Smoothened (Smo) off, and it is
    itself a Hh target gene, so Hh reception raises ptc levels and thereby sharpens and shortens the
    gradient.

5. The mechanism question: how does Ptc restrain Smo?

This is an open knowledge gap, and modules/hedgehog_signaling.yaml records it as such
("The biochemical mechanism by which ligand-free Patched restricts Smoothened remains undetermined").
The important point for this review is that the Drosophila record does not support a cholesterol-transport
molecular function for ptc. What the fly record does contain:

  1. Arguments against direct Ptc–Smo inhibition. PMID:26863604,
    with the stoichiometric argument spelled out in the same sentence — inhibition persists with Smo in
    50-fold molar excess, and substoichiometric Ptc represses Smo, i.e. the inhibition behaves catalytically.
    Denef et al. reached the same conclusion from trafficking PMID:10966113.
    The only fly physical evidence is complex-level co-fractionation
    PMID:17284519,
    which does not distinguish direct from indirect association.

  2. Ptc binds PI(4)P through its SSD. PMID:26863604,
    and Hh shifts the pool PMID:26863604.
    Loss of Ptc raises PI(4)P PMID:26863604.

  3. Ptc binds phosphatidic acid through its cytoplasmic tail, and this is required for Smo inhibition.
    PMID:37847757,
    PMID:37847757,
    PMID:37847757

  4. Ptc is a lipoprotein receptor and moves lipid in bulk, independently of Hh signalling.
    PMID:18198278,
    PMID:18198278,
    and the loss-of-function control PMID:18198278,
    with the authors' own separation of the two jobs PMID:18198278.

Decision taken in this review. The human PTCH1 framing — molecular function GO:0140303 intramembrane lipid carrier activity, cholesterol as the transported species — is grounded in
vertebrate structures and transport assays and in the curated human GO-CAMs. Drosophila UniProt
carries the same claim but flags it By similarity from Q13635, and the fly GOA contains no
GO:0140303 annotation and no cholesterol-transport annotation of any kind. The three fly lipid
observations that do exist point at three different lipids (PI(4)P, phosphatidic acid, lipophorin
cargo), two of which (PI(4)P, PA) are read as Ptc sequestering a Smo activator rather than
exporting a Smo inhibitor, and one of which (lipophorin) the authors explicitly uncouple from
signalling. On that record it would be an unsupported import to assert an intramembrane cholesterol
carrier activity for fly ptc. The review therefore grounds ptc's molecular function on what the fly
data show — GO:0008158 hedgehog receptor activity, GO:0097108 hedgehog family protein binding,
and the two lipid-binding activities — and raises the transport question in suggested_questions
rather than asserting it. No NEW molecular-function term is proposed.

This is also why GO:0005119 smoothened binding (IBA) is marked as an over-annotation rather than
accepted: the PAINT node placement is reasonable at family level, but in the fly the best evidence
argues the restraint is not by direct association, and the term invites reading a binding claim as
the mechanism.

suggested_questions:
- question: >-
    Does Drosophila Patched move any lipid across or within the membrane, as vertebrate PTCH1 is
    proposed to do for cholesterol, or is its effect on Smoothened entirely a matter of binding and
    sequestering PI(4)P and phosphatidic acid in place? No Drosophila transport assay has been
    reported, and GO:0140303 intramembrane lipid carrier activity is currently asserted for the fly
    protein only by similarity to human PTCH1.
- question: >-
    Are the PI(4)P and phosphatidic acid interactions two readouts of one mechanism or two independent
    ones? They are localised to different parts of the protein - the sterol-sensing domain and the
    cytoplasmic tail - and both are reported to be reduced by Hedgehog, so an epistasis or
    double-mutant analysis would say whether either alone is sufficient.
- question: >-
    Does Patched ever contact Smoothened directly in vivo? The stoichiometric argument says the
    restraint is catalytic and indirect, but Patched and Smoothened do co-fractionate in a large
    complex; proximity labelling or crosslinking in wing discs would distinguish an incidental
    co-residence from a functional interaction.
- question: >-
    Is the lipophorin receptor activity of Patched ancestral to the family or a Drosophila
    elaboration, and does it contribute anything to Hedgehog gradient formation beyond the effect on
    lipid droplets? Its independence from signalling was shown with two separation-of-function
    alleles, but its normal physiological purpose is unknown.
- question: >-
    How much of Drosophila Hedgehog signalling runs through the ciliary route? Patched and Smoothened
    localise to the ciliary membrane in olfactory sensory neurons, but nearly all other fly cells are
    non-ciliated; whether the ciliary and cytoplasmic Costal-2 routes use Patched in the same way is
    untested.
