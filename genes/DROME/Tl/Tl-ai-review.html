Gene Description

Toll (Tl) is a type I transmembrane receptor with an extracellular leucine-rich-repeat ectodomain and a cytoplasmic Toll/interleukin-1 receptor (TIR) domain; it is the founding member of the Toll/Toll-like receptor family. Its ligand is the endogenous cystine-knot cytokine Spätzle, which is cleaved by an extracellular serine protease cascade and binds the concave face of the membrane-distal LRR solenoid; Toll does not bind microbial molecules directly. Ligand binding promotes receptor dimerisation and endocytosis, and the TIR domain recruits MyD88 (with Weckle), which assembles Tube and the kinase Pelle, leading to degradation of the IkappaB homolog Cactus and nuclear import of the NF-kappaB proteins Dorsal and Dif. Maternal Toll, uniformly distributed on the plasma membrane of the syncytial embryo and activated ventrally, generates the nuclear Dorsal gradient that specifies the dorsoventral axis. In larvae and adults the same Spätzle-Toll pathway, triggered by circulating recognition proteins (PGRP-SA, GNBP1, GNBP3) and the protease Persephone, induces antifungal and antibacterial peptides such as drosomycin in the fat body and is required for defence against fungi and Gram-positive bacteria. Toll signalling is also reused in muscle and heart development, as a repulsive synaptic target cue, in cell competition, wound-induced gene expression and in fat-body control of growth and insulin signalling.

Comprehensive Research Report: Drosophila melanogaster Toll (Tl) Gene

Gene Identity Verification

The research targets the correct gene: Toll (gene symbol Tl, also known as CG5490), UniProt accession P08953, from Drosophila melanogaster. Toll is the founding member of the Toll-like receptor (TLR) family and belongs to the leucine-rich repeat (LRR) protein superfamily (umetsu2022cellmechanicsand pages 1-3, orusalcalde2021theevolutionof pages 1-2). This protein was initially identified for its essential role in embryonic dorsal-ventral patterning and subsequently recognized as a central innate immune receptor, making it one of the most well-characterized developmental and immune signaling molecules in insects (umetsu2022cellmechanicsand pages 1-3).

Protein Structure and Molecular Organization

Toll is a type I single-pass transmembrane receptor with a modular three-domain architecture (umetsu2022cellmechanicsand pages 1-3, kato2023analysisofthe pages 1-2). The extracellular region contains leucine-rich repeat (LRR) domains flanked by cysteine-rich motifs that form a characteristic horseshoe-shaped solenoid structure. These LRR motifs, typically 20–40 amino acids in length and enriched in leucine residues, assemble into a concave surface involved in ligand recognition and a convex surface containing variable structural elements (behzadi2021tolllikereceptorsgeneral pages 5-6, behzadi2021tolllikereceptorsgeneral pages 6-8). A single transmembrane domain anchors the receptor in the plasma membrane (umetsu2022cellmechanicsand pages 1-3, kato2023analysisofthe pages 1-2). The intracellular region contains a Toll/Interleukin-1 receptor (TIR) domain, which mediates downstream signaling through recruitment of adaptor proteins (umetsu2022cellmechanicsand pages 1-3). Drosophila Toll belongs to the P-type/mcc class of TLRs, characterized by multiple cysteine-rich C-terminal LRR capping regions (orusalcalde2021theevolutionof pages 1-2).

Primary Molecular Function: Receptor Activity and Ligand Specificity

Direct Ligand: Spätzle

The primary molecular function of Toll is to serve as a transmembrane signal-transducing receptor for the cytokine-like ligand Spätzle (Spz) (fitzgerald2020tolllikereceptorsand pages 2-3, umetsu2022cellmechanicsand pages 1-3, kato2023analysisofthe pages 1-2). Critically, unlike mammalian Toll-like receptors, Drosophila Toll does not directly recognize pathogen-associated molecular patterns (PAMPs) such as bacterial peptidoglycan or fungal β-glucans. Instead, Toll binds the processed, mature form of Spätzle, which is generated through upstream proteolytic cascades (fitzgerald2020tolllikereceptorsand pages 2-3, wang2023signalingeventsinduced pages 1-2). This represents a fundamental mechanistic difference: Toll functions as a downstream receptor that receives signals from soluble pattern-recognition pathways rather than directly detecting microbial components (fitzgerald2020tolllikereceptorsand pages 2-3).

Intracellular Binding Partners

Upon Spätzle binding, Toll undergoes a conformational change that enables its intracellular TIR domain to recruit the adaptor protein MyD88 through TIR–TIR domain interactions (umetsu2022cellmechanicsand pages 1-3, umetsu2022cellmechanicsand pages 3-5). This receptor-adaptor interaction is the critical first step in transducing the extracellular signal into intracellular signaling cascades (umetsu2022cellmechanicsand pages 3-5).

Subcellular Localization

Toll is localized to the plasma membrane where it functions as a cell-surface receptor (umetsu2022cellmechanicsand pages 1-3, li2020tollsignalingpromotes pages 1-2, kato2023analysisofthe pages 1-2). The receptor's topology positions the LRR-containing ectodomain in the extracellular space or hemolymph, the transmembrane segment within the lipid bilayer, and the TIR signaling domain in the cytoplasm (umetsu2022cellmechanicsand pages 1-3, kato2023analysisofthe pages 1-2). Toll can also function at cell-cell junctions where it participates in cell recognition and adhesion processes during tissue morphogenesis (umetsu2022cellmechanicsand pages 7-8).

Canonical Toll Signaling Pathway

The canonical Toll signaling pathway proceeds through a well-defined molecular cascade (umetsu2022cellmechanicsand pages 3-5, kietz2023drosophilacaspasesas pages 3-4, umetsu2022cellmechanicsand pages 1-3):

  1. Ligand binding: Activated Spätzle binds to the extracellular LRR domain of Toll
  2. Receptor activation: Conformational change enables TIR domain function
  3. Adaptor recruitment: MyD88 binds to Toll via TIR–TIR interactions
  4. Signaling complex assembly: MyD88 organizes a complex with Tube and the serine/threonine kinase Pelle
  5. Kinase activation: Pelle undergoes autophosphorylation and activation
  6. Inhibitor degradation: Activated Pelle promotes phosphorylation and proteasomal degradation of Cactus, the Drosophila IκB-like inhibitor
  7. Transcription factor release: Cactus degradation releases the NF-κB family transcription factors Dorsal (Dl) and Dorsal-related immunity factor (Dif) from cytoplasmic sequestration
  8. Nuclear translocation: Dorsal and/or Dif translocate to the nucleus
  9. Target gene activation: Nuclear Dorsal/Dif activate transcription of downstream target genes

This pathway is evolutionarily conserved and shares architectural similarities with mammalian TLR/IL-1R signaling (umetsu2022cellmechanicsand pages 3-5, li2020tollsignalingpromotes pages 1-2).

Biological Functions

Embryonic Dorsal-Ventral Patterning

Toll was first identified for its essential role in establishing the dorsal-ventral (DV) body axis during early Drosophila embryogenesis (umetsu2022cellmechanicsand pages 1-3, lusk2022anoncanonicalraf pages 1-2). In this developmental context, Toll functions as a spatially informative signaling system that converts localized maternal cues into embryonic positional information (pechmann2021strikingparallelsbetween pages 26-27, pechmann2021strikingparallelsbetween pages 24-26).

Mechanism of DV patterning:
- The sulfotransferase Pipe is expressed asymmetrically in the ventral follicular epithelium during oogenesis, where it sulfates vitelline membrane glycoproteins (pechmann2021strikingparallelsbetween pages 24-26, pechmann2021strikingparallelsbetween pages 18-20)
- These modified eggshell proteins initiate an extracellular proteolytic cascade that activates Spätzle specifically on the ventral side of the embryo (pechmann2021strikingparallelsbetween pages 24-26, pechmann2021strikingparallelsbetween pages 18-20)
- Ventral Spätzle activation creates a graded Toll signaling activity along the DV axis
- This Toll gradient establishes a corresponding gradient of nuclear Dorsal/NF-κB, which acts as a morphogen to specify distinct DV cell fates (pechmann2021strikingparallelsbetween pages 26-27, pechmann2021strikingparallelsbetween pages 16-18)
- High ventral Toll activity induces expression of mesodermal determinants such as Twist and Snail, leading to ventral furrow formation and mesoderm specification (lusk2022anoncanonicalraf pages 1-2, pechmann2021strikingparallelsbetween pages 22-24)
- Toll also patterns mesectodermal and ventral neuroectodermal territories and helps establish the dorsal BMP/Dpp signaling domain (pechmann2021strikingparallelsbetween pages 22-24, pechmann2021strikingparallelsbetween pages 26-27)

Loss-of-function phenotype: Toll mutant embryos fail to form ventral structures, do not establish a ventral furrow, lack mesodermal specification, and exhibit dorsalized phenotypes with expanded ectodermal fates (lusk2022anoncanonicalraf pages 1-2, pechmann2021strikingparallelsbetween pages 16-18). This demonstrates that Toll is absolutely required for breaking radial symmetry and organizing distinct DV territories (pechmann2021strikingparallelsbetween pages 20-22).

Innate Immunity

Toll plays a central role in systemic innate immune defense, particularly against fungi and Gram-positive bacteria (mpamhanga2024theutilityof pages 2-3, kojour2023innateimmuneresponse pages 1-2, umetsu2022cellmechanicsand pages 1-3, kietz2023drosophilacaspasesas pages 8-8). This immune function was discovered after the developmental role and represents a major evolutionary insight into the conservation of pattern-recognition receptor pathways from insects to mammals.

Pathogen recognition and Toll activation:
The immune activation of Toll differs mechanistically from its developmental role in that it responds to microbial infection rather than maternal spatial cues. Toll does not directly bind microbial components; instead, upstream pattern-recognition proteins detect PAMPs and initiate proteolytic cascades that generate active Spätzle (fitzgerald2020tolllikereceptorsand pages 2-3, sakakibara2023drosophilatoll9is pages 1-2):

  • Fungal detection: Gram-negative binding protein 3 (GNBP3) recognizes fungal β-1,3-glucans in the fungal cell wall (kato2023analysisofthe pages 1-2, miao2024identificationofimmunityrelated pages 13-14, kietz2023drosophilacaspasesas pages 3-4)
  • Gram-positive bacterial detection: Peptidoglycan recognition protein-SA (PGRP-SA), together with GNBP1, recognizes Lys-type peptidoglycan from Gram-positive bacteria (kato2023analysisofthe pages 1-2, miao2024identificationofimmunityrelated pages 13-14, kietz2023drosophilacaspasesas pages 3-4)
  • Protease sensing: The serine protease Persephone can be activated by pathogen-derived proteases, providing an alternative activation route (miao2024identificationofimmunityrelated pages 13-14)

These recognition events initiate extracellular serine protease cascades involving modular serine proteases (ModSP, Grass, Spirit, SPE) that culminate in cleavage of pro-Spätzle by Spätzle-processing enzyme (SPE) to generate mature, active Spätzle (kato2023analysisofthe pages 1-2, kietz2023drosophilacaspasesas pages 3-4, liu2023serpin1aandserpin6 pages 27-28).

Immune effector mechanisms:
Toll activation induces production of antimicrobial peptides (AMPs) through NF-κB-dependent transcription (mpamhanga2024theutilityof pages 2-3, zhu2023endoplasmicreticulumassociatedprotein pages 2-4):
- Drosomycin (Drs): A major antifungal peptide
- Metchnikowin (Mtk): An antimicrobial peptide with antibacterial activity
- Defensin: Contributes to antibacterial defense
- Daisho: Another immune effector peptide

The pathway also promotes melanization cascades and other cellular immune responses (mpamhanga2024theutilityof pages 2-3).

Experimental evidence: Genetic disruption of Toll pathway components leads to increased susceptibility to microbial infection. For example, loss of function in ER-associated degradation component Sip3/Hrd1, which positively regulates Toll signaling, results in reduced Drosomycin and Metchnikowin expression after infection, higher bacterial burdens, and increased mortality following challenge with Enterococcus faecalis and Staphylococcus aureus (zhu2023endoplasmicreticulumassociatedprotein pages 2-4).

In adult immunity, the transcription factor Dif is particularly important for Toll-mediated antimicrobial responses, whereas Dorsal plays the primary role in embryonic patterning (li2020tollsignalingpromotes pages 1-2, kietz2023drosophilacaspasesas pages 8-8).

Upstream Activation Mechanisms

The activation of Toll depends critically on proteolytic processing of pro-Spätzle to generate the active ligand (kato2023analysisofthe pages 1-2, kietz2023drosophilacaspasesas pages 3-4). This processing represents a regulated amplification step that integrates diverse microbial recognition signals:

  1. Pattern recognition: PGRP-SA/GNBP1 (for Lys-type PGN) or GNBP3 (for β-1,3-glucan) bind their respective microbial ligands
  2. Protease cascade initiation: Recognition triggers activation of an extracellular serine protease cascade
  3. Signal amplification: Sequential proteolytic activation steps propagate and amplify the signal
  4. Spätzle processing: The terminal protease SPE cleaves pro-Spätzle to release the active C-terminal C-106 domain
  5. Toll activation: Mature Spätzle binds Toll and initiates signaling

This indirect activation mechanism allows Toll to respond to multiple pathogen types through distinct upstream sensors while maintaining a unified downstream signaling output (fitzgerald2020tolllikereceptorsand pages 2-3, kojour2023innateimmuneresponse pages 2-4).

Additional Biological Roles

Beyond its canonical developmental and immune functions, recent research has revealed additional roles for Toll:

  • Cell competition and tissue homeostasis: Toll participates in elimination of suboptimal "loser" cells during epithelial competition (umetsu2022cellmechanicsand pages 3-5)
  • Cell mechanics and morphogenesis: Toll receptors can function in cell-cell adhesion through homophilic binding and regulate actomyosin organization at cell junctions (umetsu2022cellmechanicsand pages 10-12, umetsu2022cellmechanicsand pages 7-8)
  • Stress signaling: Toll can activate JNK and p38/SAPK pathways in addition to NF-κB, contributing to stress responses and apoptosis (li2020tollsignalingpromotes pages 1-2, li2020tollsignalingpromotes pages 4-6)
  • Neurodegeneration: Toll-9, a related family member, modulates stress signaling in aging and neurodegenerative conditions (sakakibara2023drosophilatoll9is pages 1-2)

The Toll Receptor Family in Drosophila

Drosophila possesses nine Toll-like receptors (Toll-1 through Toll-9), with distinct and sometimes overlapping functions (kojour2023innateimmuneresponse pages 1-2, umetsu2022cellmechanicsand pages 1-3):

  • Toll-1: Canonical receptor for DV patterning and immunity
  • Toll-2 (18-Wheeler): Involved in compartment boundary adhesion, salivary gland morphogenesis, and antibacterial activity
  • Toll-3, Toll-4: Functions less well characterized
  • Toll-5: Promotes antimicrobial peptide expression
  • Toll-6, Toll-7: Neuronal functions including circuit wiring, axon/dendrite targeting, and neuronal survival; Toll-7 also implicated in antiviral defense
  • Toll-8 (Tollo): Contributes to embryonic elongation and cell competition
  • Toll-9: Immune signaling, stress responses, and neurodegeneration

Evolutionary and Biomedical Significance

The discovery that Drosophila Toll is homologous to mammalian Toll-like receptors represented a landmark finding in immunology, establishing the deep evolutionary conservation of innate immune signaling from insects to vertebrates. The Drosophila Toll pathway served as a paradigm for understanding mammalian TLR signaling and NF-κB activation, with direct parallels including the TIR domain-mediated recruitment of adaptor proteins and IκB degradation leading to NF-κB nuclear translocation (umetsu2022cellmechanicsand pages 3-5, umetsu2022cellmechanicsand pages 1-3).

Toll (Tl/P08953) is a plasma membrane-localized, type I transmembrane receptor belonging to the Toll-like receptor family. Its primary molecular function is to bind the processed cytokine-like ligand Spätzle and transduce this extracellular signal through recruitment of the adaptor MyD88 and assembly of a downstream signaling complex containing Tube and Pelle. This canonical pathway leads to degradation of the inhibitor Cactus and nuclear translocation of NF-κB transcription factors Dorsal and Dif. Toll plays dual essential roles in Drosophila biology: it establishes the embryonic dorsal-ventral axis through morphogen-like activity of graded Spätzle signaling, and it mediates systemic innate immune defense against fungi and Gram-positive bacteria through induction of antimicrobial peptides. Unlike mammalian TLRs, Toll does not directly recognize PAMPs but instead responds to Spätzle that is generated by upstream pattern-recognition proteins (PGRP-SA, GNBP1, GNBP3) and proteolytic cascades. This elegant system allows integration of diverse microbial and developmental signals through a conserved receptor-mediated signaling pathway that has been central to our understanding of both development and immunity.

Tl (Toll, P08953) — review notes

Batch 1 (Toll/TLR axis) of the INNATE_IMMUNITY project. Falcon deep research had
not started for DROME genes when this review was drafted; notes below are built from
the cached GOA publications and the UniProt record.

Identity and architecture

  • Type I transmembrane receptor, 1097 aa; extracellular LRR ectodomain with
    cysteine-rich caps, single TM, cytoplasmic TIR domain (UniProt P08953; InterPro
    IPR000157 TIR).
  • Cloned as the maternal dorsal-group gene PMID:2449285

Ligand: the endogenous cytokine Spätzle, not a microbial molecule

  • Mature Spätzle (C-106) binds Toll directly PMID:12872120.
  • Crystal structures: PMID:24282309; PMID:24733933.
  • Microbial sensing is done upstream by circulating PRRs (PGRP-SA, GNBP1, GNBP3) and the Persephone protease, which converge on Spätzle processing PMID:17190605.
  • Toll pathway does not mediate LPS responses in S2 cells PMID:10973475.
  • Conclusion for project question 1: Tl is a cytokine receptor; no GO:0038187 (PRR activity) or GO:0002224 (toll-like receptor signaling pathway) annotation is present, and none should be added. The pathway term used throughout is GO:0008063 Toll signaling pathway, which is correct.

Signalling

  • TIR-mediated recruitment of MyD88 PMID:11743586; PMID:11606776.
  • Weckle adaptor PMID:16782008.
  • Multimerization PMID:15197269.
  • Endocytosis to Rab5 early endosomes required for signalling PMID:20921412; PMID:20404143.

Two core roles

  1. Embryonic dorsoventral patterning (maternal) PMID:3931919; PMID:3931918.
  2. Systemic humoral immunity against fungi and Gram-positive bacteria PMID:8808632; PMID:10369678.

Both are the same molecular function (Spätzle receptor signalling via MyD88/Tube/Pelle
to Cactus degradation and Dorsal/Dif nuclear import). Neither is "non-core": the
developmental patterning role is the historically defining one and the immune role is
the conserved defence role. Other developmental roles (heart, muscle, synaptic target
choice, cell competition, wound gene induction, fat-body growth control) are
context-specific reuse and are kept as non-core.

Other roles (non-core)

  • Synaptic target repulsion PMID:20504957.
  • Heart PMID:15870289.
  • Muscle patterning PMID:9676200.
  • Cell competition PMID:30146479.
  • Wound-induced barrier-repair genes PMID:28289197.
  • Fat body growth/insulin crosstalk PMID:29514084.
  • Tumour response via fat body PMID:24582964.

Virus binding: conflicting data

  • Chowdhury et al. 2019 report Toll-1 ectodomain co-precipitates VSV virions PMID:31088910.
  • Nakamoto et al. 2012 used Toll as the negative control PMID:22464169; and "flies lacking core Toll pathway components did not demonstrate increased susceptibility to VSV".
  • Moy et al. 2014 likewise PMID:24374193.
  • Decision: GO:0046790 virion binding and GO:0009597 detection of virus on Tl -> UNDECIDED.

  • Accept core MF: cytokine receptor activity (GO:0004896), cytokine binding (GO:0019955), transmembrane signaling receptor activity, TIR domain binding.
  • protein binding rows: MyD88 rows -> MODIFY to GO:0070976 TIR domain binding; Spätzle row -> MODIFY to GO:0019955 cytokine binding; Tehao and Weckle rows -> REMOVE (uninformative).
  • positive regulation of transcription by RNA polymerase II (Akirin paper, IMP) -> MARK_AS_OVER_ANNOTATED (the receptor is several steps upstream of transcription).
