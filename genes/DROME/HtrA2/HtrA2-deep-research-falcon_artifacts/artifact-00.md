| Identity and architecture | *D. melanogaster* CG8464 encodes the single 422-aa Omi/HtrA2 homolog, with an N-terminal mitochondrial-targeting/transmembrane region, trypsin-like serine-protease domain, catalytic Ser266, and C-terminal PDZ domain. | Confirms that the evidence concerns Q9VFJ3/CG8464—not human HTRA2. | (pqac-00000003, pqac-00000013, pqac-00000021) |
| Mitochondrial localization and processing | Tagged dOmi colocalized with mitochondrial markers; fractionation detected mature endogenous protein in mitochondrial fractions. Import generated two processed products of approximately 37 and 35 kDa, consistent with intermembrane-space maturation. | The products are proteolytically processed forms (“long” and “short”), not necessarily products of separate transcripts. | (pqac-00000000, pqac-00000003, pqac-00000013, pqac-00000014) |
| Stress-dependent cytosolic release | UV treatment of Drosophila S2 cells shifted endogenous dOmi from mitochondria into the cytosol. | Supports release during experimentally induced cell death; it does not establish that release is required for every physiological HtrA2 function. | (pqac-00000000, pqac-00000009, pqac-00000013) |
| Broad protease activity | Purified recombinant dOmi and immunoprecipitated endogenous insect-cell protein cleaved β-casein. Recombinant fly HtrA2 cleaved the HtrA2-optimized fluorescent peptide H2-Opt but not a control peptide. | Establishes serine-endopeptidase activity and sequence discrimination, but casein and H2-Opt are assay substrates rather than demonstrated endogenous fly targets. | (pqac-00000000, pqac-00000012, pqac-00000013, pqac-00000015) |
| Direct DIAP1 proteolysis | Wild-type mature dOmi-L cleaved DIAP1 between Ile165 and Gly166 in the BIR1–BIR2 linker; I165D reduced cleavage and shifted it to a nearby site. Catalytically inactive S266A failed to cleave DIAP1. | DIAP1 is a direct fly substrate. The P1-site experiment indicates preference for a nonpolar aliphatic residue, but does not define complete proteome-wide specificity. | (pqac-00000009, pqac-00000011) |
| Direct DIAP2 proteolysis | Wild-type dOmi-L cleaved DIAP2 dose-dependently, whereas S266A did not; cleavage disrupted DIAP2 BIR domains. | Establishes DIAP2 as another direct substrate under the biochemical conditions tested. Its physiological importance is less clearly established than catalytic HtrA2 activity in germ cells. | (pqac-00000009, pqac-00000011) |
| IAP binding by mature forms | Processing exposed different N termini in dOmi-L and dOmi-S; both bound DIAP1, while mutation of the first residue of the canonical long-form IBM weakened binding. | IBM-mediated binding and proteolytic cleavage are separable functions. Reported mature N-terminal sequences vary somewhat among constructs and studies because different processing sites and nomenclature were used. | (pqac-00000002, pqac-00000009, pqac-00000010, pqac-00000014) |
| Proapoptotic capacity versus physiological requirement | Overexpression induced cell death in cultured cells and severe eye phenotypes, and DIAP1 expression suppressed eye-disc ablation. In contrast, an HtrA2-null mutant showed normal development and normal apoptosis after 4-Gy irradiation, 4 mM staurosporine, or 2.5 kJ/m² UV. | HtrA2 is sufficient to promote death when overexpressed or released, but is dispensable for broad developmental and experimentally induced canonical apoptosis under the tested conditions. | (pqac-00000004, pqac-00000015, pqac-00000029) |
| Pink1/Parkin and mitochondrial maintenance: Tain model | HtrA2-null flies had mild, age-progressive mitochondrial abnormalities, locomotor decline, stress sensitivity, shortened lifespan, and male sterility. Epistasis placed HtrA2 downstream of Pink1 in a branch parallel to Parkin; HtrA2 overexpression partially rescued Pink1 phenotypes. | Supports a subsidiary Pink1–HtrA2 stress pathway, not HtrA2 as the principal Pink1 effector or as part of the canonical Pink1/Parkin branch. | (pqac-00000028, pqac-00000029, pqac-00000031, pqac-00000051) |
| Pink1/Parkin and mitochondrial maintenance: Yun model | Independent omi-null analysis found normal mitochondrial morphology in testes and flight muscle, no muscle degeneration after 30 days, no dopaminergic-neuron loss, and no loss-of-function interaction supporting placement in the Pink1/Parkin mitochondrial-integrity pathway. | This conflicts with the mild mitochondrial phenotype reported by Tain; allele, environment, assay sensitivity, and the subtle or age-dependent nature of the phenotype may contribute. The safest conclusion is that HtrA2 is not an essential canonical Pink1/Parkin effector. | (pqac-00000022, pqac-00000030, pqac-00000038) |
| Spermatogonial programmed cell death | Normally about 20%–30% of newly emerging spermatogonial cysts die. HtrA2 deficiencies or protease-domain mutation reduced this death by roughly 50%–60%; genomic rescue restored it to about 80% of wild type. | This is HtrA2’s strongest physiological death-pathway annotation. The process is effector-caspase/apoptosome independent, lysosome- and ROS-associated, and was subsequently characterized as programmed necrosis. | (pqac-00000008, pqac-00000023, pqac-00000024, pqac-00000036, pqac-00000039) |
| Catalysis versus IAP antagonism in germ-cell death | A rescue transgene carrying S266A was strongly impaired, whereas mutation of the proposed IBMs retained near-wild-type rescue of germ-cell death. | Demonstrates that protease activity—not IBM-dependent IAP antagonism—is the critical HtrA2 function in this physiological context. The relevant endogenous germ-cell substrate remains unidentified. | (pqac-00000019, pqac-00000023, pqac-00000024, pqac-00000040) |
| Male fertility | omiDf1/IFD males produced approximately 2% as many progeny as wild type. Wild-type or IBM-deficient rescue restored fertility to about 75% of wild type, whereas S266A provided little rescue. | Male fertility requires HtrA2 catalytic function, but sterility cannot be attributed solely to reduced germ-cell death because mutants can still produce mature, motile sperm and most cysts normally survive. | (pqac-00000019, pqac-00000024, pqac-00000040) |
| Pink1, Parkin, and lysosomes in germ-cell death | pink1 loss reduced germ-cell death by about 40%, whereas parkin loss had no significant effect. HtrA2 mutants had about 50% fewer LysoTracker-positive spermatogonia; combining HtrA2 and cathepsin-D defects reduced death to about 20% of wild type. | Places Pink1 and HtrA2 in a Parkin-independent mitochondrial branch, with lysosomes acting partly downstream and partly in parallel. Genetic interaction does not demonstrate that Pink1 is cleaved by HtrA2 or vice versa. | (pqac-00000017, pqac-00000018, pqac-00000025) |
