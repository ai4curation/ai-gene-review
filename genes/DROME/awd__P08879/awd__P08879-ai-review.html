Gene Description

abnormal wing discs (awd; also Killer of prune, Nm23) encodes the major nucleoside diphosphate kinase (NDPK, EC 2.7.4.6) of Drosophila, a 153-residue NME/Nm23-type enzyme about 78% identical to human NME1 and NME2. Purified Awd is a homohexamer that transfers the gamma-phosphate of ATP to ribo- and deoxyribonucleoside diphosphates by a ping-pong mechanism through an N-delta phosphohistidine on His119, with Mg2+ as cofactor; crystal structures of the free and phosphorylated enzyme have been solved. Awd is a soluble cytoplasmic protein that co-purifies with microtubules. Null mutants die around pupariation with small imaginal discs, lymph glands and brain lobes, and their neuroblasts arrest in metaphase. An H119A transgene that lacks NDPK activity does not rescue the null, so the catalytic activity is required in vivo; human NME2, but not NME1, can substitute. Genetic studies place Awd in dynamin (shibire)-dependent endocytosis: awd alleles enhance shibire synaptic-vesicle recycling defects, and loss of awd causes Breathless/FGFR to accumulate in tracheal cells, adherens-junction components to accumulate in follicle cells, and internalized Notch to stall in enlarged Rab5-positive early endosomes. The favoured explanation is that Awd supplies GTP to dynamin and Rab5, but local GTP delivery has not been demonstrated directly.

Core Functions

Nucleoside diphosphate kinase. The cytoplasmic Awd homohexamer transfers the gamma-phosphate of ATP to ribo- and deoxyribonucleoside diphosphates through an N-delta phosphohistidine on His119, regenerating GTP, UTP, CTP and dNTPs. Experimentally established in Drosophila by enzyme purification, crystal structures and active-site mutagenesis, and inherited from the PAINT NDPK node.
