# Functional annotation report: *Drosophila melanogaster* Mtap (CG4802; UniProt Q9V813)

## Identity and evidence boundary

**The gene symbol “Mtap” is shared across organisms, and literature on this specific fly protein is limited.** Here, the target is **CG4802 in *D. melanogaster***, not human or mouse MTAP. The supplied UniProt record identifies Q9V813 as S-methyl-5′-thioadenosine phosphorylase (EC 2.4.2.28), and an independent fly study explicitly identifies CG4802 as the *Drosophila* MTAP ortholog. The supplied PNP/MTAP-family and nucleoside-phosphorylase domain assignments are consistent with that identity; they do not, by themselves, establish the enzyme’s measured activity or location in flies. [UniProt Q9V813](https://www.uniprot.org/uniprotkb/Q9V813/entry); Williamson et al., **2007**, [doi:10.1002/ajmg.a.31724](https://doi.org/10.1002/ajmg.a.31724). (williamson2007methylthioadenosinephosphorylase(mtap) pages 1-2, williamson2007methylthioadenosinephosphorylase(mtap) pages 2-3)

## Primary molecular function and pathway

**The strongest functional annotation is MTA phosphorolysis, inferred for CG4802 from orthology and its annotated protein family:**

**5′-methylthioadenosine (MTA) + inorganic phosphate ⇌ adenine + 5-methylthioribose-1-phosphate.**

This is the established reaction of MTAP enzymes. The sugar-phosphate product is processed further in **methionine salvage**; the released adenine provides a potential input to **purine salvage**. The reaction therefore recovers useful components of MTA rather than directly converting MTA into methionine in a single step. The available fly study discusses this chemistry but **does not assay purified CG4802 or measure CG4802-dependent metabolic flux**. Williamson et al., **2007**, [doi:10.1002/ajmg.a.31724](https://doi.org/10.1002/ajmg.a.31724); Bernasocchi and Mostoslavsky, **July 2024**, [doi:10.3389/freae.2024.1451971](https://doi.org/10.3389/freae.2024.1451971). (williamson2007methylthioadenosinephosphorylase(mtap) pages 1-2, bernasocchi2024subcellularonecarbon pages 4-6)

The physiological rationale is especially clear at the junction with **polyamine biosynthesis**. Production of spermidine and spermine from decarboxylated S-adenosylmethionine generates MTA; accumulated MTA can inhibit the polyamine-synthesis reactions that produce it. An MTAP-like activity would remove this by-product while supplying methionine-salvage and adenine-salvage pathways. This is a **biochemical pathway assignment for fly Mtap**, not evidence that changing CG4802 has been shown to alter fly polyamine concentrations. Williamson et al., **2007**, [doi:10.1002/ajmg.a.31724](https://doi.org/10.1002/ajmg.a.31724); Bernasocchi and Mostoslavsky, **2024**, [doi:10.3389/freae.2024.1451971](https://doi.org/10.3389/freae.2024.1451971). (williamson2007methylthioadenosinephosphorylase(mtap) pages 1-2, bernasocchi2024subcellularonecarbon pages 3-4, bernasocchi2024subcellularonecarbon pages 4-6)

**Substrate specificity remains unresolved for Q9V813.** MTA is the canonical assigned substrate, but the located fly-specific work provides no comparative assays against other nucleosides, phosphate-dependence measurements, affinity constants, or turnover rates. Activities reported for an MTAP from another organism should not be used as measured CG4802 substrate preferences. (williamson2007methylthioadenosinephosphorylase(mtap) pages 5-7, williamson2007methylthioadenosinephosphorylase(mtap) pages 1-2)

## Where the gene product acts

Whole-mount *in situ* hybridization detected **CG4802 transcript in the fat body and visceral mesoderm of stage 13–14 embryos**. This is direct evidence of **embryonic tissue expression**, not proof of protein abundance, activity, or exclusive expression in those tissues. **Subcellular localization is not established** by this experiment: the retrieved fly-specific study did not localize endogenous Mtap protein within cells or demonstrate secretion. Thus, a precise cytosolic, organellar, or extracellular site of catalysis cannot be assigned experimentally for Q9V813 from the located evidence. Williamson et al., **2007**, [doi:10.1002/ajmg.a.31724](https://doi.org/10.1002/ajmg.a.31724). (williamson2007methylthioadenosinephosphorylase(mtap) pages 5-7)

## Direct genetic evidence and interpretation

Williamson and colleagues generated **three independently PCR-verified excision lines** lacking CG4802 genomic sequence. **One line was homozygous viable; two were homozygous lethal**, but the investigators considered loss of surrounding sequence a likely explanation for the latter result. Consequently, those two deletions **do not establish that Mtap alone is essential**. In an auditory assay using sound-evoked antennal-nerve potentials in **0–2-day-old flies, with 10 flies per genotype**, the authors reported **no significant difference** between the tested mutant and control groups. This argues against a detectable early-adult hearing defect under their assay conditions; it neither disproves the predicted metabolic function nor excludes phenotypes under other conditions. Williamson et al., **2007**, [doi:10.1002/ajmg.a.31724](https://doi.org/10.1002/ajmg.a.31724). (williamson2007methylthioadenosinephosphorylase(mtap) pages 2-3, williamson2007methylthioadenosinephosphorylase(mtap) pages 5-7)

## Recent research and applications

A **2024 review** places MTAP at the entry to the MTA/methionine-salvage route and discusses its importance in one-carbon metabolism; its discussion of **MTAP-deleted cancers and therapeutic dependencies concerns human disease models, not experiments on fly CG4802**. The targeted literature retrieved here did **not** establish a 2023–2024 advance measuring Q9V813 enzymology, localization, substrate selectivity, or a CG4802-specific intervention. There is accordingly **no supported fly-specific clinical or industrial implementation** to report. The most defensible current use of this annotation is as a **testable metabolic hypothesis**, anchored by fly gene identity and embryonic expression rather than by a completed fly biochemical characterization. Bernasocchi and Mostoslavsky, **July 2024**, [doi:10.3389/freae.2024.1451971](https://doi.org/10.3389/freae.2024.1451971); Williamson et al., **2007**, [doi:10.1002/ajmg.a.31724](https://doi.org/10.1002/ajmg.a.31724). (williamson2007methylthioadenosinephosphorylase(mtap) pages 5-7, bernasocchi2024subcellularonecarbon pages 6-7, bernasocchi2024subcellularonecarbon pages 4-6)

**Bottom line:** Annotate fly Mtap/CG4802 primarily as a **predicted MTA phosphorylase linking polyamine by-product removal to methionine and adenine salvage**. Treat its precise substrate profile and intracellular site of action as **unverified in *Drosophila***, while retaining the direct findings of stage 13–14 embryonic fat-body/visceral-mesoderm transcript expression and a negative early-adult auditory assay. (williamson2007methylthioadenosinephosphorylase(mtap) pages 1-2, williamson2007methylthioadenosinephosphorylase(mtap) pages 5-7, bernasocchi2024subcellularonecarbon pages 4-6)
