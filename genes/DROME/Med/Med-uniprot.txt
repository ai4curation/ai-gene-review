CC   -!- FUNCTION: Component of the Mad-Med transcription factor complex
CC       involved in the dpp/BMP TGF-beta signal transduction pathway; not
CC       essential but enhances or modifies the signal (PubMed:15296719,
CC       PubMed:16109720, PubMed:7705627, PubMed:8702167, PubMed:9502724,
CC       PubMed:9694800). Functions downstream of dpp and its receptors
CC       (PubMed:9502722, PubMed:9694800). Associates with the R-SMAD Mad upon
CC       dpp-signaling and translocates to the nucleus (PubMed:9502724,
CC       PubMed:9502733). Can also associate with the activin-like pathway R-
CC       SMAD Smox (PubMed:16951053). Binds GC-rich enhancer sites with
CC       consensus sequence 5'-GTCTG-3' in the enhancer region of dpp-responsive
CC       genes (PubMed:15296719, PubMed:16109720, PubMed:9694800). Mad-Med acts
CC       as a transcriptional activator but recruits shn/Shnurri to enhance its
CC       activity or switch to transcriptional repression (PubMed:16109720).
CC       Regulates dpp-responsive genes by directly activating their expression
CC       or by repressing the transcriptional repressor brk/brinker
CC       (PubMed:12705870). Also involved in repression of other key
CC       developmental genes including bam and gsb (PubMed:15296719). Required
CC       for dpp-induced expression of tin/tinman during embryogenesis
CC       (PubMed:9694800). Required for dpp-signaling during dorsoventral
CC       patterning of the embryo (PubMed:9502724, PubMed:9502733). Maternal
CC       contribution is essential for dorsoventral patterning in embryos
CC       (PubMed:9502722). Involved in dpp/BMP signaling during development of
CC       imaginal discs (PubMed:9502733). {ECO:0000269|PubMed:12705870,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:15296719, ECO:0000269|PubMed:16109720,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:16951053, ECO:0000269|PubMed:7705627,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:8702167, ECO:0000269|PubMed:9502722,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:9502724, ECO:0000269|PubMed:9502733,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:9694800}.
CC   -!- ACTIVITY REGULATION: DNA binding of the Mad-Med complex, specifically
CC       of the enhancer region of the brk gene, is activated by dpp-signaling
CC       (PubMed:15296719). Dpp-signaling induces phosphorylation of Mad by dpp-
CC       specific receptor serine/threonine kinase complexes, which in turn
CC       induces interaction of Mad with Med and nuclear localization of the
CC       Mad-Med complex, where it can bind the enhancer regions of dpp-
CC       responsive genes (PubMed:9502724). {ECO:0000269|PubMed:15296719,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:9502724}.
CC   -!- SUBUNIT: Component of a DNA binding Mad-Med complex composed of two
CC       copies of Mad and one copy of Med (PubMed:16109720, PubMed:9502724).
CC       May form heterodimers composed of one copy of Mad and one copy of Med
CC       when associated with certain gene enhancer regions such as the enhancer
CC       for brk (PubMed:15296719). Interacts (via C-terminus) with Mad (when
CC       phosphorylated at the C-terminus) (PubMed:16109720, PubMed:16951053,
CC       PubMed:9502724). The Mad-Med complex associates with shn/Shnurri;
CC       recruitment of shn to DNA enhancer elements results in transcriptional
CC       repression (PubMed:15296719, PubMed:16109720). Interacts with
CC       Smox/SMAD2 (when phosphorylated on the C-terminal SSXS motif); the
CC       interaction is dependent on a receptor serine/threonine kinase complex,
CC       due to phosphorylation of Smox by the receptor complex
CC       (PubMed:10320478, PubMed:16951053). Heterodimerizes with the R-SMAD
CC       Smox/SMAD2 upon activation of the activin pathway and translocates to
CC       the nucleus, where it transcriptionally activates activin-responsive
CC       genes (PubMed:10320478, PubMed:9887103). Interacts with Snoo; the
CC       interaction reduces binding of Med to the dpp/BMP-like pathway R-SMAD
CC       Mad and promotes binding to the activin-like pathway R-SMAD Smox acting
CC       like a pathway switch (PubMed:16951053). Interacts (via N-terminus)
CC       with lwr; the interaction is direct (PubMed:18794353).
CC       {ECO:0000269|PubMed:10320478, ECO:0000269|PubMed:15296719,
