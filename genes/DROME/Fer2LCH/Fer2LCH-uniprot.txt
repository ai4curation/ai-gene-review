CC   -!- FUNCTION: Stores iron in a soluble, non-toxic, readily available form
CC       (By similarity) (PubMed:23771129). Important for iron homeostasis (By
CC       similarity) (PubMed:23064556, PubMed:23771129). Iron is taken up in the
CC       ferrous form and deposited as ferric hydroxides after oxidation (By
CC       similarity) (PubMed:17603097, PubMed:23771129). Ferritin is composed of
CC       a heavy (H) chain which is responsible for the oxidation and uptake of
CC       ferrous iron, and a light (L) chain which facilitates the nucleation of
CC       the ferrihydrite iron core (PubMed:23771129). Required for dietary iron
CC       absorption in the midgut (PubMed:23064556). Involved in tissue iron
CC       detoxification by exporting excess iron (PubMed:23064556). Plays a role
CC       in the maintenance of circadian rhythms (PubMed:22885802). Required for
CC       embryo and larval development (PubMed:26192321).
CC       {ECO:0000255|RuleBase:RU361145, ECO:0000269|PubMed:17603097,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:22885802, ECO:0000269|PubMed:23064556,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:23771129, ECO:0000269|PubMed:26192321}.
CC   -!- SUBUNIT: Oligomer of 12 light (L) chains and 12 heavy (H) chains; L and
CC       H chains are disulfide-linked (PubMed:9266686). The functional molecule
CC       forms a roughly spherical shell with a diameter of 12 nm and contains a
CC       central cavity into which the insoluble ferric iron core is deposited
CC       (PubMed:23771129). {ECO:0000269|PubMed:23771129,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:9266686}.
CC   -!- SUBCELLULAR LOCATION: Golgi apparatus {ECO:0000269|PubMed:17603097}.
CC       Secreted {ECO:0000269|PubMed:26192321}.
CC   -!- TISSUE SPECIFICITY: Expressed in hemolymph, gut, ovaries and to a
CC       lesser extent in testes (at protein level) (PubMed:11804801). Expressed
CC       in the head (at protein level) (PubMed:23064556).
CC       {ECO:0000269|PubMed:11804801, ECO:0000269|PubMed:23064556}.
CC   -!- DEVELOPMENTAL STAGE: Expressed in the gut and hemolymph of second
CC       instar larvae, wandering larvae and early pupae (at protein level)
CC       (PubMed:11804801, PubMed:23064556). Expressed in the middle part of the
CC       gut in third instar larvae (PubMed:17603097). Expressed in embryos,
CC       third instar larvae, light pupae, dark pupae and adults
CC       (PubMed:11804801, PubMed:17603097). During embryogenesis, expressed in
CC       blastoderm, in mesoderm cells during germ-band elongation that give
CC       rise to fat bodies and amnioserosa and in cells that give rise
CC       macrophages in the anterior head region (PubMed:17603097). During germ-
CC       band retraction and dorsal closure, expressed in amnioserosa
CC       (PubMed:17603097). At late stages of embryogenesis, expressed in cells
CC       in the developing midgut (PubMed:17603097).
CC       {ECO:0000269|PubMed:11804801, ECO:0000269|PubMed:17603097,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:23064556}.
CC   -!- INDUCTION: Up-regulated by iron-supplemented diet in the anterior part
CC       of the gut of third instar larvae and adults (at protein level).
CC       {ECO:0000269|PubMed:11804801, ECO:0000269|PubMed:17603097,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:26192321}.
CC   -!- DISRUPTION PHENOTYPE: Embryonic lethal with some embryos displaying
CC       cuticle and nervous system defects (PubMed:17603097, PubMed:26192321).
CC       RNAi-mediated knockdown is lethal at the late pupal stage
CC       (PubMed:23064556). Also the levels of ferritin heavy chain Fer1HCH are
CC       reduced (PubMed:23064556). RNAi-mediated knockdown in the midgut causes
CC       growth delays and reduces survival rate only in the presence of the
CC       iron chelator BPS. Also, results in gut iron accumulation and systemic
CC       iron deficiency, reduces Mvl transcription and aconitase activity
CC       (PubMed:23064556). RNAi-mediated knockdown in the eye does not affect
CC       eye morphology; however, the eyes develop degenerative vacuoles
CC       (PubMed:23064556). RNAi-mediated knockdown in the wing pouch results in
CC       slightly smaller wings with no defect in cell proliferation and cell
CC       death patterns (PubMed:26192321, PubMed:31568497). RNAi-mediated
CC       knockdown in cry+ neurons (LNds and s-LNvs) disrupts circadian
CC       oscillations of PER and TIM leading to the disruption of circadian
CC       activity in the absence of external cues (PubMed:22885802). RNAi-
CC       mediated knockdown in the nervous system, embryonic epidermis and
CC       imaginal disks, endoderm, mesoderm, fat body or somatic muscles causes
CC       no defect (PubMed:23064556). {ECO:0000269|PubMed:17603097,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:22885802, ECO:0000269|PubMed:23064556,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:26192321, ECO:0000269|PubMed:31568497}.
CC   -!- SIMILARITY: Belongs to the ferritin family.
