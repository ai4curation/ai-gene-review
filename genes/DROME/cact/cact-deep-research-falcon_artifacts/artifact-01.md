|---|---|---|---|
| Embryonic dorsal–ventral patterning | Cytoplasmic Cactus binds Dorsal and limits its nuclear entry. Spatially restricted Toll signaling promotes Cactus phosphorylation, ubiquitination, and degradation, generating the nuclear Dorsal gradient. Cactus can also facilitate Toll-dependent Dorsal import rather than acting solely as an inhibitor. (pqac-00000027, pqac-00000030, pqac-00000034) | Concentration-dependent Dorsal targets include **snail** and **sog**; altered Cactus dosage changes the position and precision of their expression domains. (pqac-00000029, pqac-00000043) | Maternal and early syncytial embryo during establishment of the dorsal–ventral axis. (pqac-00000031, pqac-00000034) |
| Morphogen shuttling and gradient robustness | Mobile Dorsal–Cactus complexes undergo facilitated diffusion toward the ventral Toll-activation domain. Local Cactus degradation releases Dorsal for nuclear capture, making Cactus both an inhibitor and a carrier that concentrates Dorsal ventrally. (pqac-00000052, pqac-00000053, pqac-00000054) | Ventral enrichment of total and nuclear Dorsal and sharpening of the Dorsal gradient. A reaction–diffusion model estimated a Dorsal–Cactus diffusion coefficient of approximately **0.2–0.5 μm²/s**. (pqac-00000055, pqac-00000056, pqac-00000059) | Shared cytoplasm of the early syncytial embryo before cellularization. (pqac-00000052, pqac-00000054) |
| Innate humoral immunity | Cactus restrains the Toll-responsive NF-κB factors **Dif** and **Dorsal** in the cytoplasm. Infection-induced Cactus degradation permits their nuclear entry; loss or silencing of Cactus increases and prolongs immune activation. (pqac-00000002, pqac-00000024) | Antimicrobial-peptide expression, hemolymph antibacterial activity, and immune-response magnitude and resolution; in *D. melanogaster*, Cactus loss is associated with increased AMP production and hemocyte proliferation. (pqac-00000024) | Primarily larval and adult immune tissues, including fat body and hemocytes, during microbial challenge. (pqac-00000002, pqac-00000024) |
| Immune homeostasis and fitness trade-offs | By limiting and resolving Toll signaling, Cactus protects against excessive immune investment and immunopathology. Quantitative evidence from *Tribolium castaneum*—not *D. melanogaster*—shows that graded Cactus depletion can improve infection resistance while reducing lifespan, fecundity, body mass, and gut integrity. (pqac-00000024, pqac-00000025, pqac-00000026) | AMP induction and decay, circulating hemocytes, infection survival, reproductive output, longevity, and gut homeostasis. (pqac-00000025, pqac-00000026) | Comparative whole-organism insect model following microbial challenge. (pqac-00000025) |
| Ras/polarity-deficient carcinoma models | Toll–Dorsal signaling and Cactus expression are heterogeneous within tumors, including Dorsal-high/Cactus-high populations. Cactus is a pathway component and feedback-associated output, whereas Dorsal is the better-established causal driver of survival, impaired differentiation, and invasion. (pqac-00000049) | Elevated **cact**, **PGRP-SA**, and **wntD**; reduced retinal-determination gene **eya**; Dorsal-dependent invasion involving **snail** and **twist**. (pqac-00000042, pqac-00000049) | Ras^V12^; *scribble*-deficient epithelial and imaginal-disc tumor models. (pqac-00000042, pqac-00000049) |
| Yorkie/Hippo–JNK tumor signaling | In polarity-deficient tumors, Yorkie/Scalloped-dependent Cactus accumulation acts upstream of JNK. Cactus depletion reduces phosphorylated JNK, MMP1, clone growth, and invasion, revealing a context-specific tumor-promoting function distinct from canonical NF-κB inhibition. (pqac-00000044, pqac-00000045, pqac-00000048) | Increased phospho-JNK and **MMP1**, basement-membrane remodeling, tumor growth, and invasive behavior. (pqac-00000045, pqac-00000046) | Larval imaginal-disc tumors driven by activated Yorkie or Ras together with impaired epithelial polarity. (pqac-00000046, pqac-00000047) |
| Toll-associated apoptosis and tissue homeostasis | Experimental Toll activation can promote reactive-oxygen-species production, JNK activity, and caspase-dependent death. Cactus regulates access to the activated Toll state through its inhibition of Dorsal/Dif, although a direct Cactus-specific apoptotic mechanism remains unestablished. (pqac-00000018, pqac-00000039) | ROS, activated JNK, caspase-dependent apoptosis, and altered tissue growth. (pqac-00000039) | Developing *Drosophila* tissues under experimental or physiological Toll activation. (pqac-00000018) |
