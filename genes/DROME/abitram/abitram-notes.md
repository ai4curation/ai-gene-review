## 2. Literature Search & Comparative Insect Evidence
Queries across PubMed and EuropePMC (`"CG9288"`, `"FBgn0260464"`, `"abitram" AND "Drosophila"`, `"Simiate" AND ("Drosophila" OR "insect")`, `"Q9VFR1"`) revealed no targeted biochemical or cell-biological characterization of *D. melanogaster* `abitram` (`CG9288`), alongside three relevant genomic/comparative publications:
1. **Hayden & Bosco 2008 (`PMID:18237443`, *BMC Genomics*)**: Identified `CG9288` (`FBtr0082829`) among 44 *D. melanogaster* transcripts carrying an evolutionarily conserved peptide upstream open reading frame (`CPuORF`, `Ka/Ks = 0.16`, conserved to *Aedes aegypti*) containing a `CX9C` motif, suggesting translational regulation of `abitram` expression in Diptera.
2. **Izutsu et al. 2016 (`PMID:26637434`, *G3*)**: Listed `CG9288` among 12 genes within a 37 kb chromosome `3R` quantitative trait locus (`LOD Peak 8`) showing allele-frequency divergence in long-term dark-adapted *D. melanogaster* (`Dark-fly`) populations, without single-gene causal validation.
3. **chettouh/Khila group 2022 (`PMID:35193982`, *PNAS*)**: In the hemipteran water strider *Gerris buenoi*, embryonic RNAi knockdown of the insect `Simiate` (`abitram`) ortholog caused disorganized, size-variable sensory bristles (whose inner shaft scaffold consists of parallel actin filament bundles) as well as shortened embryonic legs, explicitly noting that `abitram`/`CG9288` had not previously been functionally characterized in *D. melanogaster*. This provides independent insect genetic support for conserved actin-filament regulation while highlighting non-neuronal epidermal/appendage contexts in insects.
