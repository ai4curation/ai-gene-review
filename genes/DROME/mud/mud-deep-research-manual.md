# mud (mushroom body defect) - manual literature synthesis

No deep-research API was available for this review; this file is a manual synthesis
from cached publications (publications/PMID_*.md) and PubMed searches.

## Identity

- Drosophila melanogaster *mud* (FlyBase FBgn0002873, CG12047, GeneID 44839); reviewed
  entry here is the TrEMBL record M9NEG4 (2165 aa).
- Large coiled-coil protein; the fly orthologue/functional counterpart of vertebrate NuMA
  and C. elegans LIN-5. UniProt M9NEG4 carries nine predicted coiled-coil segments
  (Coils) spanning most of the protein and PANTHER family PTHR18902 (NUCLEAR MITOTIC
  APPARATUS PROTEIN 1-RELATED).
- Originally cloned as a gene controlling neuroblast proliferation and mushroom body
  development [PMID:10884435 "The gene is predicted to encode a polypeptide without obvious
  homologs but with two prominent structural features, a long coiled coil that constitutes
  the central core of the protein and a carboxyl-terminal transmembrane domain."]

## Core molecular role: cortical Pins-binding, microtubule-binding spindle orientation factor

- Pins binds Mud directly; Mud binds microtubules [PMID:16740476 "we show that Pins binds
  directly to the microtubule binding protein Mud, the Drosophila homolog of NuMA. Like
  NuMA, Mud can bind to microtubules and enhance microtubule polymerization."]
- Pins recruits Mud to the apical neuroblast cortex; Mud also at centrosomes/spindle poles
  [PMID:16648843 "Pins recruits Mud to the neuroblast apical cortex, and Mud is also
  strongly localized to centrosome/spindle poles, in a similar way to NuMA."]
- Mud is a downstream effector of Pins in neuroblasts and epithelia [PMID:16648846 "Mud
  directly associates and colocalizes with Pins at the cell cortex overlying the spindle
  pole(s) in both neuroblasts and epithelial cells."]
- Gαi/Pins regulation: Pins TPR domain binds Mud; Gαi opens autoinhibited Pins
  [PMID:17726110 "Galphai and Mud bind cooperatively to the Pins GoLocos 2/3 and
  tetratricopeptide repeat domains, respectively"]. Quantitative reconstitution with a
  Mud peptide (residues 1936-1951, isoform B) shows ultrasensitive activation
  [PMID:21855794 "A tetramethylrhodamine labeled Mud peptide (TMR-Mud residues 1936–1951,
  isoform B) containing the region that binds the Pins TPRs allows detection of Pins-Mud
  association"]. No DNA-binding experiment is present in this paper (full text read).
- Canoe/RanGTP promote Mud recruitment to cortical Pins in induced-polarity S2 cells
  [PMID:22024168 "Canoe/RanGTP acts by promoting Mud recruitment to the cortical Pins
  domain"]; importin-alpha/beta binds the Mud TIP-NLS fragment.
- Mud functions with dynein: "Pins/Mud/dynein spindle orientation pathway"
  [PMID:22024168].
- Ana2/Ctp (dynein light chain) and Mud form a complex that localizes Mud to centrosomes and
  cortex [PMID:21920316 "Ana2 and Ctp localize Mud to the centrosomes and cell cortex and
  facilitate/maintain the association of Mud with Pins at the apical cortex."] (abstract only).

## Other polarity inputs

- Dishevelled (planar cell polarity) recruits Mud to the posterior cortex of the sensory
  organ precursor pI cell [PMID:21074723 "We conclude that Dsh recruits Mud to the apical
  posterior cortex in interphase/prophase pI cells."]; Mud C-terminus (1825-2475)
  co-immunoprecipitates Dsh.
- Epithelial tricellular junctions: Mud localizes to TCJs (with Gliotactin) independently of
  Pins and orients division along interphase long axis [PMID:26886796 "The TCJ localization
  of Mud was independent of Pins and Gαi in both interphase and mitotic cells"].

## Biological consequences

- Asymmetric neuroblast division / spindle orientation [PMID:19619498].
- Female meiosis II: Mud on the central spindle pole body between the tandem MII spindles
  [PMID:16510791 "In wild-type eggs, Mud is found associated with the central spindle pole
  body that lies between the two spindles of meiosis II."]
- Midline axon guidance in parallel with Netrin, independent of neuroblast role
  [PMID:26893348 "mutation of mushroom body defect (mud) dramatically enhances the phenotype
  of Netrin or frazzled mutants"].
- Mushroom body defects (the eponymous phenotype) arise from neuroblast over-proliferation
  [PMID:10884435].
- Temperature preference behaviour screen [PMID:18594510] — abstract does not mention mud;
  likely a downstream consequence of mushroom body defects.
