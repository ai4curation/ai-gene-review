CC   -!- FUNCTION: Required for the Mettl1-dependent formation of N(7)-
CC       methylguanine at position 46 (m7G46) in tRNA (By similarity)
CC       (PubMed:39317727). In the Mettl1-wuho methyltransferase complex, it is
CC       required to stabilize and induce conformational changes of the
CC       catalytic subunit (By similarity). Required for binding of nanos mRNA
CC       and repression of translation by the mei-P26-bgcn-bam-sxl complex
CC       (PubMed:31941704). May cooperate with mei-P26 and nanos to derepress
CC       the BMP signaling pathway (PubMed:31941704). May cooperate with mei-P26
CC       to suppress expression of a subset of microRNAs (PubMed:31941704). May
CC       cooperate with mei-P26 to regulate bam expression levels in germline
CC       cells during gametogenesis (PubMed:31941704). Required to promote
CC       mitosis to meiosis transition during gametogenesis (PubMed:31941704).
CC       May regulate germline cell division in part by regulating ribosome
CC       biogenesis (PubMed:31941704). {ECO:0000255|HAMAP-Rule:MF_03056,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:31941704, ECO:0000269|PubMed:39317727}.
CC   -!- PATHWAY: tRNA modification; N(7)-methylguanine-tRNA biosynthesis.
CC       {ECO:0000255|HAMAP-Rule:MF_03056}.
CC   -!- SUBUNIT: Forms a heterodimer with the catalytic subunit Mettl1 (By
CC       similarity) (PubMed:39317727). Interacts with mei-P26 and weakly
CC       interacts with bgcn; required for the function or formation of the mei-
CC       P26-bgcn-bam-sxl complex (PubMed:31941704). Interacts with nanos; may
CC       be involved in mei-P26-dependent derepression of the BMP signaling
CC       pathway (PubMed:31941704). Interacts with Myc; the interaction may be
CC       mediated by mei-P26 and may be involved in the regulation of ribosome
CC       biogenesis (PubMed:31941704). {ECO:0000255|HAMAP-Rule:MF_03056,

CC   -!- TISSUE SPECIFICITY: In testis, it is present at high level in hub
CC       cells, a niche for germline stem cells of testis (PubMed:16762337).
CC       Ubiquitously expressed in all testicular cells throughout
CC       spermatogenesis (PubMed:39317727). Ubiquitously expressed in all
CC       germline and somatic cells of the ovary (PubMed:31941704).
CC       {ECO:0000269|PubMed:16762337, ECO:0000269|PubMed:31941704,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:39317727}.
CC   -!- DISRUPTION PHENOTYPE: Male sterile and female semi-sterile phenotypes
CC       (PubMed:16762337, PubMed:31941704). In males, spermatogenesis is
CC       arrested at the elongating stage of the developing spermatids,
CC       resulting in an absence of mature sperms in the seminal vesicles
CC       (PubMed:16762337). The decreased fertility in females is mostly due to
CC       defects in oogenesis (PubMed:16762337, PubMed:31941704). There are
CC       abnormal egg chambers, in which cystocytes fail to arrest cell division
CC       at the fourth mitotic cycle, resulting in overprolliferation
CC       (PubMed:16762337, PubMed:31941704). Cystocytes fail to differentiate
CC       and do not make the transition from mitosis to meiosis
CC       (PubMed:16762337, PubMed:31941704). Maintenance of ovarian germline
CC       stem cell (GSC) numbers is disrupted, reducing further with age
CC       (PubMed:31941704). Abscission of ovarian GSCs from daughter cystoblasts
CC       is disrupted with some GSCs remaining connected with several daughter
CC       cells via long branched fusomes and open ring canals and ending up in
CC       the germarium (PubMed:31941704). Tissue specific RNAi-mediated
CC       knockdown in ovarian germline cells results in failed abscission of
CC       GSCs from daughter cystoblasts (PubMed:31941704). Ovarian cysts may
CC       become tumorous (PubMed:31941704). Germline cells display enlarged
CC       nucleoli indicating enhanced ribosomal biogenesis (PubMed:31941704).
CC       {ECO:0000269|PubMed:16762337, ECO:0000269|PubMed:31941704}.
