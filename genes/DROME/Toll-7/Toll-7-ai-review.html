Gene Description

Toll-7 is a Toll-family type I transmembrane receptor with an extracellular leucine-rich-repeat ectodomain and a cytoplasmic TIR domain, expressed at the plasma membrane and prominently in the central nervous system. It is a receptor for the Spätzle-family neurotrophins DNT1 (Spz2) and DNT2 (Spz5), and its ectodomain also binds Spätzle-1. In the nervous system Toll-7, together with its paralog Toll-6, regulates motor axon targeting, neuronal survival and locomotion, and cell-autonomously instructs olfactory receptor neuron axon targeting and synaptic partner matching. Toll-7 also contributes to antiviral defence: it associates with vesicular stomatitis virus and Rift Valley fever virus particles at the cell surface and is required for virus-induced autophagy that restricts these viruses, through a MyD88- and NF-kappaB-independent mechanism. Whether it also drives canonical Toll pathway antimicrobial peptide induction is disputed.

Comprehensive Research Report: Toll-7 (CG8595, UniProt Q7KIN0) in Drosophila melanogaster

Gene Identity Verification

This report addresses Toll-7, encoded by gene CG8595 (FlyBase: FBgn0034476, UniProt: Q7KIN0) in Drosophila melanogaster (fruit fly). Toll-7 belongs to the Toll-like receptor family and contains leucine-rich repeat domains, consistent with the UniProt annotation (kojour2023innateimmuneresponse pages 1-2, umetsu2022cellmechanicsand pages 1-3). The gene identity has been confirmed across multiple recent authoritative studies published in 2022-2024.

Protein Structure and Molecular Architecture

Toll-7 is a type I transmembrane receptor with a modular architecture consisting of three principal domains: an extracellular leucine-rich repeat (LRR) region flanked by cysteine-rich modules, a single-pass transmembrane segment, and an intracellular Toll/Interleukin-1 receptor (TIR) domain (kojour2023innateimmuneresponse pages 1-2, umetsu2022cellmechanicsand pages 1-3, lima2021evolutionoftoll pages 1-2). This architecture positions the LRR ectodomain on the cell surface for ligand recognition and cell-cell interactions, while the cytoplasmic TIR domain enables intracellular signal transduction. The receptor is localized to the plasma membrane and functions at the cell surface for ligand binding and recognition, with likely endocytosis for signaling as typical of Toll receptors (zhang2024maintainingtollsignaling pages 2-3).

Primary Molecular Functions and Ligand Specificity

Ligand Binding and Receptor Activation

Toll-7 functions as a receptor for multiple Spätzle-family cytokines, displaying promiscuous ligand binding. The receptor can bind Spätzle-1 (Spz-1), Spätzle-2/Drosophila neurotrophin-1 (Spz-2/DNT-1), and Spätzle-5/Drosophila neurotrophin-2 (Spz-5/DNT-2) (zhang2024maintainingtollsignaling pages 2-3). These ligands function as neurotrophin-like molecules in Drosophila, mediating neuronal survival, circuit connectivity, and structural synaptic plasticity (zhang2024maintainingtollsignaling pages 2-3). However, ligand-receptor relationships are context-dependent; while DNT-2 can bind Toll-7, recent studies show that specific DNT-2 developmental functions are mediated primarily through Toll-2 in the visual system and Toll-6 in dopaminergic circuits (alshamsi2025theneurotrophindnt2 pages 1-4, alshamsi2025theneurotrophindnt2 pages 14-17, alshamsi2025theneurotrophindnt2 pages 8-11).

Dual Signaling Mechanisms

Toll-7 employs distinct signaling pathways depending on cellular context, representing a critical difference from canonical Toll-1 signaling:

MyD88-Independent Autophagy Pathway: For autophagy regulation, Toll-7 signals through a MyD88-independent mechanism that nevertheless requires Tube, Pelle, and protein phosphatase 2A (PP2A). This pathway regulates the Akt-Tor-S6K axis, decreasing Akt phosphorylation and promoting autophagy through increased Atg8a/LC3 lipidation and reduced P62/Ref(2)P accumulation (zhang2024maintainingtollsignaling pages 3-6, zhang2024maintainingtollsignaling pages 2-3, zhang2024maintainingtollsignaling pages 6-8). Importantly, RNAi knockdown of dMyd88 suppressed Toll-1-mediated but not Toll-7-mediated autophagy-associated gene expression, demonstrating pathway divergence (zhang2024maintainingtollsignaling pages 3-6, zhang2024maintainingtollsignaling pages 1-2).

MyD88-Dependent Immune Transcription: For antimicrobial peptide production, Toll-7 engages a MyD88-dependent pathway. Overexpression of Toll-7's intracellular TIR domain (TIR-7) activated the drosomycin (Drs) antimicrobial peptide promoter, and this activation was blocked by dMyd88 knockdown (zhang2024maintainingtollsignaling pages 3-6). This likely involves the canonical Toll pathway components including Tube, Pelle, Cactus degradation, and NF-κB transcription factors Dif/Dorsal (lima2021evolutionoftoll pages 1-2, zhang2024maintainingtollsignaling pages 2-3).

TIR-Independent Cell Recognition: In olfactory system development, Toll-7 functions primarily through its extracellular domain for cell-cell recognition, independently of intracellular TIR domain signaling. The extracellular domain is sufficient for neuronal wiring specificity, suggesting Toll-7 acts as a cell-surface recognition molecule or adhesion receptor in this context (umetsu2022cellmechanicsand pages 10-12).

Biological Processes and Pathways

Autophagy Regulation and Neuronal Homeostasis

The most robustly characterized function of Toll-7 is the maintenance of basal autophagy in the adult Drosophila brain. Recent experimental evidence from Zhang et al. (2024) demonstrates this role through complementary approaches (zhang2024maintainingtollsignaling pages 10-12, zhang2024maintainingtollsignaling pages 8-10, zhang2024maintainingtollsignaling pages 6-8, zhang2024maintainingtollsignaling pages 2-3, zhang2024maintainingtollsignaling pages 1-2, zhang2024maintainingtollsignaling pages 3-6):

Loss-of-function studies: Toll-7 mutant flies (Toll-7^g1-5/Df(2R)) showed markedly reduced or nearly absent mCherry-Atg8a puncta and lacked detectable endogenous Atg8a-II compared with wild-type controls, indicating severely impaired autophagy (zhang2024maintainingtollsignaling pages 3-6). These mutants also exhibited altered phosphorylation of Akt, Tor, and S6K, increased accumulation of P62/Ref(2)P aggregates and ubiquitinated proteins, demonstrating defective autophagic clearance and proteostasis (zhang2024maintainingtollsignaling pages 10-12, zhang2024maintainingtollsignaling pages 3-6).

Gain-of-function studies: In Drosophila S2 cells, overexpression of Toll-7, its intracellular TIR-7 domain, or activation with Spätzle proteins increased LC3-II/LC3-PE levels (a marker of autophagosome formation), reduced P62 levels (indicating enhanced autophagic flux), and produced autophagic puncta (zhang2024maintainingtollsignaling pages 2-3, zhang2024maintainingtollsignaling pages 6-8).

Genetic epistasis: The autophagy response required Tube, Pelle, and PP2A activity; RNAi knockdown of these genes suppressed Toll-7-induced autophagy (zhang2024maintainingtollsignaling pages 10-12, zhang2024maintainingtollsignaling pages 6-8). PP2A activity was specifically increased by Toll-7 signaling (zhang2024maintainingtollsignaling pages 10-12).

Dopaminergic Neuron Survival and Locomotor Function

Toll-7-mediated autophagy is functionally critical for dopaminergic (DA) neuron survival in the adult brain. Toll-7 loss-of-function flies showed significantly fewer tyrosine hydroxylase (TH)-positive dopaminergic neurons compared with controls and exhibited increased TUNEL staining, indicating elevated apoptosis (zhang2024maintainingtollsignaling pages 10-12, zhang2024maintainingtollsignaling pages 8-10). This neuronal loss had behavioral consequences: only approximately 70% of Toll-7 mutant flies crossed the climbing assay threshold within two minutes compared with nearly 100% of control flies, demonstrating impaired locomotor function (zhang2024maintainingtollsignaling pages 8-10). Atg8a mutants, defective in autophagy, showed comparable reductions in DA neurons and climbing ability, supporting the conclusion that autophagy is the critical mechanism linking Toll-7 to neuronal maintenance (zhang2024maintainingtollsignaling pages 10-12, zhang2024maintainingtollsignaling pages 8-10).

Spätzle-5 (Spz-5) is a candidate physiological ligand for this function, as Spz-5 mutants—but not Spz-1 mutants—showed significant dopamine neuron loss (zhang2024maintainingtollsignaling pages 10-12).

Olfactory System Development and Neural Circuit Wiring

Toll-7 plays a specialized role in establishing precise connectivity in the olfactory system. It is expressed in a subset of olfactory receptor neurons (ORNs) in the antenna, where it promotes accurate axon targeting to ensure proper matching with corresponding projection neurons (PNs) in antennal-lobe glomeruli (umetsu2022cellmechanicsand pages 10-12). This function is mechanistically distinct from other Toll-7 activities: the extracellular LRR domain is sufficient for wiring specificity, while the intracellular TIR domain is dispensable (umetsu2022cellmechanicsand pages 10-12). This indicates that Toll-7 acts primarily as a cell-surface recognition tag or adhesion molecule rather than through canonical intracellular signal transduction in this developmental context.

Toll-7 and Toll-6 have complementary but distinct roles in olfactory circuit assembly, with Toll-6 acting mainly in PN dendrite targeting while Toll-7 functions in ORN axon targeting (umetsu2022cellmechanicsand pages 10-12). Importantly, the known neurotrophin ligands DNT-1 and DNT-2 are dispensable for this ORN-PN matching function, and Toll-6 and Toll-7 do not bind each other, suggesting an unidentified heterophilic cell-surface partner mediates the wiring specificity (umetsu2022cellmechanicsand pages 10-12).

Innate Immunity Functions

Antimicrobial peptide production: Toll-7 can activate transcription of antimicrobial peptide genes, particularly drosomycin (Drs), an antifungal peptide. In S2 cells, Toll-7 and Toll-7-Spätzle complexes stimulated drosomycin promoter activity through a MyD88-dependent mechanism (zhang2024maintainingtollsignaling pages 3-6, zhang2024maintainingtollsignaling pages 2-3). This immune transcription response is mechanistically separable from Toll-7's autophagy function: dMyd88 knockdown suppressed Drs induction but did not block Toll-7-mediated Atg8a and InR expression (zhang2024maintainingtollsignaling pages 3-6).

Antiviral defense: Toll-7's role in antiviral immunity remains controversial. Earlier work proposed that Toll-7 recognizes viruses and activates antiviral autophagy (prince2023elucidatingtherole pages 15-15), but multiple recent authoritative reviews explicitly note that Toll-7's involvement in the response to viral infection remains unresolved and should not be treated as established functional annotation (umetsu2022cellmechanicsand pages 1-3). Further experimental validation is needed to clarify this potential function.

Subcellular Localization and Tissue Expression

Toll-7 is a plasma membrane protein with its extracellular LRR domain exposed on the cell surface and its intracellular TIR domain in the cytoplasm (kojour2023innateimmuneresponse pages 1-2, umetsu2022cellmechanicsand pages 1-3). The receptor is highly expressed in the adult Drosophila brain, particularly in distinct anatomical domains and neuronal populations (zhang2024maintainingtollsignaling pages 2-3). Specific expression sites include:

  • Dopaminergic neurons in the adult brain (zhang2024maintainingtollsignaling pages 2-3, zhang2024maintainingtollsignaling pages 10-12)
  • Olfactory receptor neurons (ORNs) in the antenna (umetsu2022cellmechanicsand pages 10-12)
  • Additional neuronal populations involved in circuit connectivity and synaptic plasticity (zhang2024maintainingtollsignaling pages 2-3)

The receptor functions at the plasma membrane for ligand binding and cell-cell recognition, and likely undergoes endocytosis for intracellular signaling, as is typical for Toll family receptors.

Evolutionary Context and Functional Diversification

Toll-7 belongs to the TOLL2_7 subfamily along with Toll-2/18-wheeler, representing a lineage that arose through duplication in ancestral Diptera or Schizophora (lima2021evolutionoftoll pages 14-15, lima2021evolutionoftoll pages 8-10). This subfamily shows substantial lineage-specific expansion and gene gain/loss events, particularly in Diptera and Lepidoptera, contrasting with the more evolutionarily conserved Toll-6 and Toll-8 subfamilies (lima2021evolutionoftoll pages 14-15).

Phylogenomic analyses reveal that TOLL2_7 genes have undergone repeated duplication, neofunctionalization, and subfunctionalization, explaining their participation in diverse biological responses (lima2021evolutionoftoll pages 14-15, lima2021evolutionoftoll pages 8-10, lima2021evolutionoftoll pages 13-14). Importantly, comparative studies emphasize that homology-based functional inference may be unreliable for Toll-7; its functions cannot be safely extrapolated from Toll-1, other Drosophila Toll receptors, or vertebrate TLRs despite structural similarities (lima2021evolutionoftoll pages 8-10).

This evolutionary context helps explain Toll-7's functional diversity: the receptor combines ancestral immune-related functions with derived developmental and neuronal roles that emerged through gene duplication and divergence (lima2021evolutionoftoll pages 14-15, lima2021evolutionoftoll pages 13-14).

Toll-7 in Drosophila melanogaster is a multifunctional receptor that coordinates neuronal homeostasis, development, and immunity through context-dependent signaling mechanisms. Its primary experimentally validated functions include:

  1. Maintenance of basal autophagy in adult brain neurons through a MyD88-independent, Tube-Pelle-PP2A-Akt/Tor pathway
  2. Dopaminergic neuron survival and locomotor function via autophagy-dependent proteostasis
  3. Olfactory circuit wiring through TIR-independent, extracellular domain-mediated cell recognition
  4. Antimicrobial peptide transcription through MyD88-dependent canonical Toll signaling

The receptor's ability to employ distinct signaling mechanisms—MyD88-dependent versus MyD88-independent pathways, and TIR-dependent versus TIR-independent functions—represents a sophisticated regulatory strategy that allows a single receptor to coordinate multiple aspects of neuronal and immune physiology. Recent high-quality studies (2022-2024) have substantially advanced our understanding of Toll-7, particularly its critical role in autophagy and neuronal maintenance, though some proposed functions (notably antiviral defense) require further experimental validation.
