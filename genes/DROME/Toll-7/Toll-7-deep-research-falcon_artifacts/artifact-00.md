| **Basal autophagy and proteostasis** | Cell-surface Toll-7 activates a **MyD88-independent but Tube–Pelle–PP2A-dependent** pathway. Signaling lowers Akt activity and modulates the Akt–Tor–S6K axis, increases Atg8a-associated autophagy, and promotes clearance of Ref(2)P/P62 and ubiquitinated proteins. The adaptor connecting Toll-7 to Tube/Pelle remains unresolved. | Active Spätzle proteins; biochemical interaction or activity has been reported with Spz-1, Spz-2/DNT-1, and Spz-5/DNT-2. Tube, Pelle, PP2A, Akt, Tor, S6K, Atg8a, and Ref(2)P/P62 are downstream components or readouts. | Adult brain and cultured Drosophila S2 cells; receptor signaling begins at the plasma membrane, with downstream effects on cytoplasmic autophagy machinery. | Toll-7/TIR-7 gain-of-function increased LC3-II/Atg8a-II and autolysosomal puncta and reduced P62 in S2 cells. Toll-7 mutants had nearly absent mCherry-Atg8a puncta and Atg8a-II, altered Akt/Tor/S6K phosphorylation, and accumulated P62 and ubiquitinated proteins. RNAi epistasis showed dependence on Tube, Pelle, and PP2A but not MyD88 for autophagy-associated outputs (pqac-00000019, pqac-00000025, pqac-00000026, pqac-00000028). |
| **Dopaminergic-neuron survival and locomotor maintenance** | Toll-7-dependent basal autophagy maintains neuronal proteostasis; impaired signaling is associated with aggregate accumulation, apoptosis, dopaminergic-neuron loss, and motor dysfunction. The data strongly associate autophagy failure with neuron loss, although rescue by Atg1 or rapamycin was not tested, limiting proof of direct causality. | Spz-5 is a candidate physiological ligand because Spz-5 mutants—but not Spz-1 mutants—showed dopaminergic-neuron loss. Atg8a provides a genetic/phenotypic comparator. | Adult brain, including TH-positive dopaminergic neurons. | Toll-7 loss-of-function and brain-specific RNAi reduced TH-positive neuron number and increased TUNEL staining. About **70%** of Toll-7 mutants crossed the climbing threshold within two minutes versus nearly **100%** of controls; Atg8a mutants showed comparable neuronal and behavioral defects (pqac-00000023, pqac-00000024, pqac-00000025). |
| **Olfactory-circuit wiring and partner matching** | Toll-7 acts principally as an extracellular **cell-recognition/tagging receptor** rather than through canonical TIR-domain signaling. Its extracellular domain is sufficient for wiring specificity; the intracellular domain is dispensable. | Unknown heterophilic cell-surface partner. Toll-6 and Toll-7 do not bind each other; DNT-1 and DNT-2 are dispensable for this particular matching function. | Cell surface of a subset of olfactory receptor neurons; Toll-7 guides ORN axons toward appropriate projection-neuron partners in antennal-lobe glomeruli. | Genetic/domain-manipulation studies showed defects in ORN axon targeting and ORN–PN matching and demonstrated sufficiency of the Toll-7 extracellular region. Toll-6 has a complementary role, acting mainly in PN dendrite targeting (pqac-00000017, pqac-00000018, pqac-00000033). |
| **Neurotrophin-like signaling, neuronal survival, and connectivity** | Toll-7 can serve as a receptor for Spätzle-family neurotrophin-like ligands and has been associated with neuronal survival, connectivity, and structural synaptic plasticity. Ligand–receptor usage is promiscuous and context dependent; not every DNT-2 phenotype is mediated by Toll-7. | Strongest prior assignment: Spz-2/DNT-1–Toll-7. Spz-5/DNT-2 can also bind Toll-7, but DNT-2 prominently signals through Toll-6/Kek-6 in dopaminergic circuits and through Toll-2 in the developing visual system. | Distinct neuronal domains in the developing and adult brain; exact Toll-7-positive populations vary by circuit. | Binding and receptor-activation studies support interactions with Spz-2 and Spz-5. Recent work confirms DNT receptor promiscuity but attributes specific dopaminergic and visual-system DNT-2 phenotypes chiefly to Toll-6 or Toll-2, so Toll-7-specific in-vivo causality remains less secure (pqac-00000000, pqac-00000008, pqac-00000012, pqac-00000013, pqac-00000014). |
| **Antimicrobial transcription** | Toll-7 can engage a **MyD88-dependent immune-transcription branch**: TIR-7 activates the drosomycin promoter, and MyD88 knockdown suppresses this response. This differs from Toll-7-induced autophagy, which is MyD88 independent. Canonical downstream NF-κB factors are plausible, but their Toll-7-specific requirement was not established in the cited experiments. | MyD88; likely canonical Toll-pathway components Tube, Pelle, Cactus, Dif/Dorsal. Tested extracellular partners include Spz-1, Spz-2, and Spz-5. | Demonstrated primarily in Drosophila S2 cells; systemic physiological relevance and tissue of action remain incompletely defined. | TIR-7 overexpression and Toll-7–Spätzle combinations activated **drosomycin (Drs)** reporter/expression. MyD88 RNAi inhibited Drs induction but did not inhibit Toll-7-mediated Atg8a/InR responses, experimentally separating immune transcription from autophagy signaling (pqac-00000038, pqac-00000041, pqac-00000042). |
| **Antiviral recognition/autophagy—contested assignment** | A 2012 study proposed direct viral recognition by Toll-7 followed by antiviral autophagy. Subsequent reviews emphasize that Toll-7’s antiviral role remains controversial, so it should not be treated as settled functional annotation. | Vesicular stomatitis virus was reported as the viral stimulus or binding target; the downstream mechanism was described as noncanonical antiviral autophagy. | Proposed at the surface and intracellular signaling machinery of virus-exposed Drosophila cells and tissues. | Earlier infection experiments linked Toll-7 to virus recognition and antiviral autophagy, but later expert reviews explicitly characterize the assignment as unresolved. Evidence for generalized antiviral activity or broad antimicrobial-peptide control is insufficient (pqac-00000004, pqac-00000010, pqac-00000037, pqac-00000040). |
| **Receptor architecture enabling signaling and recognition** | Predicted/annotated **type-I single-pass transmembrane receptor**: N-terminal extracellular leucine-rich repeats with cysteine-rich flanking modules, one transmembrane helix, and a cytoplasmic TIR domain. This topology supports extracellular ligand/cell recognition and intracellular signal initiation; no Toll-7-specific experimental atomic structure was identified. | Extracellular Spätzle-family ligands or unidentified cell-surface partners; cytoplasmic signaling factors interact functionally downstream of the TIR region. | Plasma membrane, with the LRR ectodomain outside the cell and TIR domain in the cytoplasm. | Conserved domain architecture agrees with UniProt Q7KIN0/InterPro annotations and comparative analyses of insect Toll receptors. Toll-7-specific domain boundaries and high-resolution structure remain unavailable (pqac-00000030, pqac-00000031, pqac-00000032). |
| **Evolutionary context and functional diversification** | Toll-7 belongs to the **TOLL2_7** subfamily with Toll-2/18-wheeler. Lineage-specific duplication, gain/loss, and possible neofunctionalization caution against assigning Toll-7 functions solely from other insect Tolls or mammalian TLRs. | Evolutionary relationship to Toll-2; function cannot safely be extrapolated from Toll-1, Toll-6, Toll-8, vertebrate TLR7, or similarly named receptors in other organisms. | Drosophila and related dipteran lineages; this is phylogenetic rather than subcellular localization evidence. | Comparative phylogenomic analysis found substantial lineage-specific expansion and placed Toll-7 in a distinct Toll2_7 evolutionary group. Authors warn that homology-based annotation is unreliable for this subfamily (pqac-00000034, pqac-00000036). |
