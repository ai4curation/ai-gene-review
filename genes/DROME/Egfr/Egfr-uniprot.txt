CC   -!- FUNCTION: Receptor tyrosine kinase, binding ligands of the EGF family
CC       and activating several signaling cascades to convert extracellular cues
CC       into appropriate cellular responses (PubMed:22140578, PubMed:23579691,
CC       PubMed:8070664, PubMed:9882502). Known ligands include spitz, gurken,
CC       vein and giant-lens (PubMed:19718021, PubMed:20723758, PubMed:22140578,
CC       PubMed:9882502). Transduces the signal through the ras-raf-MAPK pathway
CC       (PubMed:9094709). Critical for the proliferation of imaginal tissues,
CC       and for the determination of both the antero-posterior and dorso-
CC       ventral polarities of the oocyte (PubMed:23579691, PubMed:34411095,
CC       PubMed:9882502). In the embryo, plays a role in the establishment of
CC       ventral cell fates, maintenance of amnioserosa and ventral
CC       neuroectodermal cells, germ band retraction, cell fate specification in
CC       the central nervous system, and production and repair of the cuticle
CC       (PubMed:22140578, PubMed:23029159, PubMed:23579691, PubMed:9094709).
CC       During dorsal closure (DC) functions with the dpp- and ACK-signaling
CC       pathways to regulate expression of the myosin zip in the embryonic
CC       epidermis and amnioserosa (AS), and thus coordinate the progression of
CC       epidermal cell shape changes required for correct DC (PubMed:23579691).
CC       In the embryonic epidermis, functions by negatively regulating dpp and
CC       consequently the dpp-dependent expression of the myosin zip
CC       (PubMed:23579691). In the AS, negatively regulates the production/ and
CC       or secretion of a diffusible signal which, is produced by the ACK-
CC       signaling pathway, and acts in the AS and epidermal cells to promote
CC       zip expression (PubMed:23579691). Also required in the AS to inhibit or
CC       delay apoptosis, and consequently slow the rate of DC
CC       (PubMed:23579691). Therefore functions at multiple levels to negatively
CC       regulate morphogenesis during DC, suggesting that it acts as a general
CC       brake mechanism for adjusting the rate of dorsal closure to ensure that
CC       closure proceeds smoothly and without loss of epidermal integrity
CC       (PubMed:23579691). During oogenesis, one of two tyrosine kinase
CC       chemoattractant receptors (Egfr and Pvr), that function in the border
CC       cells (BC) to detect guidance cues from the oocyte and transduce this
CC       information to the guidance pathway that regulate the collective
CC       migration of the BC cluster through the nurse cells to the oocyte
CC       (PubMed:24855950). {ECO:0000269|PubMed:19718021,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:20723758, ECO:0000269|PubMed:22140578,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:23029159, ECO:0000269|PubMed:23579691,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:24855950, ECO:0000269|PubMed:34411095,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:8070664, ECO:0000269|PubMed:9882502,
CC       ECO:0000303|PubMed:9094709}.
