# Lam (Lamin Dm0, LMNB_DROME, P08928) - manual literature synthesis

No deep-research API was available for this review. This file was written by hand from the
cached publications in `publications/` and the UniProt record `Lam-uniprot.txt`. Every
statement carries its provenance.

## Identity and family

- Lam (synonyms Lamin Dm0, egk1, misg, CG6944) encodes the single B-type nuclear lamin of
  *Drosophila melanogaster*, 622 aa, UniProt P08928 [file:DROME/Lam/Lam-uniprot.txt].
- Member of the intermediate filament family, with a central coiled-coil rod and a C-terminal
  CaaX (CxxM) box [file:DROME/Lam/Lam-uniprot.txt "Belongs to the intermediate filament family."].
- Drosophila has two lamins: Lam (B-type, CaaX, constitutive) and LamC (A-type-like, late,
  differentiated tissues) [PMID:7593280 "Thus, Drosophila has two lamins: lamin Dm0,
  containing a CaaX motif, is expressed throughout, while lamin C, lacking a CaaX motif, is
  expressed only later in development."].

## Biochemistry: dimerization and polymerization

- Rod-domain coiled-coil homodimers polymerize head-to-tail through the conserved ends of the
  rod [PMID:8776884 "Our data indicate that one type of interaction in intermediate filament
  protein polymerization is the longitudinal binding of dimers via the conserved end segments
  of the coiled-coil rod domain."].
- Head-to-tail polymerization is inhibited by phosphorylation at Ser42 (cdc2) or Ser50 (PKA)
  [PMID:9013881 "Phosphorylation of serine-50 by cAMP-dependent kinase inhibited head-to-tail
  binding in the same manner as phosphorylation of serine-42 by cdc2 kinase."].
- Lam Dm0 assembles into fibrillar aggregates and associates with the lamina when expressed
  in Sf9 cells [PMID:9372441 "only lamin Dmo was found in the nuclear compartment of Sf9 cells
  in association with the lamina"].
- Interphase isoforms Dm1/Dm2 and a mitotic soluble form Dm(mit) differ by phosphorylation
  [PMID:2492999; PMID:8537453 "Immunoprecipitation with ADL84 demonstrated that purified
  Drosophila nuclear lamins Dm1 and Dm2 are present as a random mixture of homo- and
  heterodimers."].

## Chromatin binding

- The tail domain binds chromosomes (Kd ~1 uM), competed by H2A/H2B [PMID:10077600 "these
  results suggest that the interaction between the tail domain of lamin Dm0 and histones H2A
  and H2B may mediate the attachment of the nuclear lamina to chromosomes in vivo"].
- In vivo photo-crosslinking: interphase (not mitotic) lamins contact nucleic acid
  [PMID:9394018 "it was found that interphase lamins were associated with nucleic acid"].
- Single phosphomimetic mutations do not change chromatin binding [PMID:22393432 "We also
  confirmed that none of the single phosphorylation site modifications affected the chromatin
  binding of lamin Dm"].

## Nuclear envelope assembly and architecture

- Required in embryo extracts for vesicle attachment to chromatin and nuclear envelope
  formation [PMID:1527167 "Upon incubation of the embryonic assembly extract with
  anti-Drosophila lamin antibodies, the attachment of nuclear membrane vesicles to chromatin
  surface and nuclear envelope formation did not occur."].
- Insertional mutant: NPC clustering, annulate lamellae, defective envelopes, rescued by a
  transgene [PMID:9166402 "reduced lamin Dm0 expression caused an enrichment of nuclear pore
  complexes in cytoplasmic annulate lamellae and in nuclear envelope clusters"].
- Null mutants: late pupal lethality, abnormal NPC distribution, organ-specific defects
  [PMID:15996653 "obviously abnormal nuclear pore complex distribution was observed concomitant
  with the loss of lamin Dm(0) protein"].
- RNAi in embryos and cells: nuclear fragility, arrest [PMID:15054108 "the depletion of
  Drosophila lamin Dm0 in cultured cells and embryos caused morphological alterations of
  nuclei, nuclear fragility and the arrest of embryonic development"].
- The CxxM motif targets lamins to the inner nuclear membrane and drives membrane growth when
  overexpressed (Xenopus A6 cells) [PMID:15546914 "Our results indicate that the CxxM motif of
  B-type lamins has a dual function: it mediates lamin targeting to the inner nuclear membrane
  thereby promoting nuclear membrane growth."].

## Anchoring nuclear envelope proteins

- Required for nuclear envelope localization of LEM-domain proteins Bocksbeutel, Otefin
  [PMID:15035436 "when lamin DmO was reduced by RNAi the distribution of Bocksbeutel and otefin
  in the nuclear envelope of Kc167 cells was significantly altered"] and MAN1 [PMID:16439308
  "the localization of dMAN1, Bocksbeutel and otefin at the INM is supported by lamin Dm0"].
- Binds Otefin and YA (two-hybrid, co-IP) [PMID:9632815], LBR [PMID:15054108], Wash
  [PMID:25754639].
- Required for perinuclear Klarsicht and for MTOC-nucleus attachment in photoreceptor nuclear
  migration [PMID:14617811 "We conclude that like Klar, lamin is required for nuclear migration
  and to link the MTOC to the nucleus."; "We conclude that localization of Klar to the nuclear
  envelope requires nuclear lamin."].

## Chromatin organization and gene repression

- LamDm0 is required for somatic repression and peripheral positioning of testis-specific gene
  clusters [PMID:19218438 "Ablation of LamDm(o) by using mutants and RNAi led to detachment of
  testis-specific clusters from nuclear envelope and to their selective transcriptional
  up-regulation in somatic cells"]. The authors argue lamins act as docking sites rather than
  direct regulators [PMID:19218438 "Although lamins themselves probably do not regulate gene
  expression, they probably contribute to gene silencing at nuclear envelope by providing
  docking sites for diverse repressors"].
- Lam knockdown changes heterochromatin organization and accessibility [PMID:25754639].
- Age-associated loss of lamin-B in fat body causes heterochromatin loss and derepression of
  immune genes, with systemic inflammation and non-autonomous gut hyperplasia [PMID:25417159
  "fat body immunosenecence is caused by age-associated lamin-B reduction specifically in fat
  body cells, which then contributes to heterochromatin loss and derepression of genes
  involved in immune responses"].

## Mitosis and meiosis

- Lam persists in a "spindle envelope" around the mitotic apparatus in early embryos
  [PMID:2517292] and around the spindle in S2 cells [PMID:19273613 "In this study, we used
  immunofluorescence to show that lamin B is not fully disintegrated at this stage"], and in
  male meiosis I, where Lam depletion causes cytokinesis failure [PMID:27402967 "Lam depletion
  resulted in a higher frequency of cytokinesis failure in male meiosis"].

## Developmental requirements (phenotypes)

- Tracheal terminal-branch outgrowth and oocyte D/V polarity (gurken mRNA localization)
  [PMID:11533666].
- Cyst stem cell niche organization in testis via nuclear ERK retention [PMID:23827710].
- Eye, gonad, CNS and ventriculus defects in nulls [PMID:15996653].
