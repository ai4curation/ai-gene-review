# Functional annotation of *Drosophila melanogaster* Snx1 (Q9VQQ6)

## Identity and principal function

**Identity verified.** The requested protein is the fruit-fly gene product **Snx1**, also called **Dsnx1, CG2774 and Vps5**, rather than a human protein bearing the SNX1 symbol. A fly primary study identifies CG2774 as the homolog of yeast Vps5 and mammalian SNX1/2. This agrees with the supplied UniProt Q9VQQ6 annotation of a sorting nexin containing a phox-homology (**PX**) domain and BAR-related/Vps5_C regions. [UniProt Q9VQQ6](https://www.uniprot.org/uniprotkb/Q9VQQ6/entry); Zhang et al., *Cell Research*, November 2011, [doi:10.1038/cr.2011.167](https://doi.org/10.1038/cr.2011.167). (zhang2011snx3controlswinglesswnt pages 1-2)

**Best-supported functional annotation:** Snx1 is a **putative endosomal membrane-sorting and membrane-remodeling scaffold**, not an enzyme or a solute transporter. Its predicted role is to help assemble curved, cargo-exporting endosomal membranes as an SNX1/2-type PX–BAR protein. This molecular assignment rests substantially on conserved architecture and experiments on related proteins; the clearest direct evidence for *fly* Snx1 is genetic involvement in embryonic axon guidance, not identification of a transported cargo. Simonetti et al., *Nature Cell Biology*, October 2019, [doi:10.1038/s41556-019-0393-3](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41556-019-0393-3). (simonetti2019molecularidentificationof pages 8-9, simonetti2019molecularidentificationof pages 4-6, simonetti2019molecularidentificationof pages 28-29)

## Molecular mechanism and site of action

In characterized SNX-BAR systems, a PX domain helps associate the protein with phosphoinositide-containing endosomal membranes, while BAR-domain dimerization, curvature sensing and membrane deformation support formation of tubular export carriers. In mammalian cells, SNX1 or SNX2 pairs with SNX5 or SNX6 in the **endosomal SNX–BAR sorting complex for promoting exit 1 (ESCPE-1)**. Structural and cellular experiments assign recognition of a cytosolic cargo β-hairpin motif chiefly to a groove in the **SNX5/6 PX domain**; they do **not** establish that fly Snx1 itself recognizes that motif or carries the human cargoes tested. Simonetti et al., October 2019, [doi:10.1038/s41556-019-0393-3](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41556-019-0393-3). (simonetti2019molecularidentificationof pages 4-6, simonetti2019molecularidentificationof pages 28-29)

**Likely working location:** the **cytosolic face of intracellular endosomal membranes**, potentially including emerging endosomal tubules. This is a localization *inferred* for Q9VQQ6 from its domains and conserved SNX1/2-type machinery, not a verified microscopy result for the fly protein: the retrieved primary studies did not establish colocalization of fly Snx1 with Rab5, Rab7 or a trans-Golgi marker. Nor did they directly measure Q9VQQ6 phosphoinositide-binding specificity or demonstrate a physical fly Snx1–Snx6 heterodimer. Rowlands and Moore, *Philosophical Transactions B*, February 2024, [doi:10.1098/rstb.2022.0384](https://doi.org/10.1098/rstb.2022.0384); Simonetti et al., October 2019. (simonetti2019molecularidentificationof pages 28-29, rowlands2024vps35andretromer pages 2-3)

## Biological processes and pathway specificity

**Axon guidance: direct fly evidence.** Simonetti and colleagues examined *snx1* and *snx6* in embryonic nervous-system development. Individual zygotic mutants had no dramatic defect in the initial coarse axon analyses, which the authors cautioned could reflect maternal protein supply. In sensitized *slit/robo* backgrounds, reducing either gene's dosage suppressed abnormal ectopic midline crossing; reducing both produced a stronger effect. In a separate truncated-Frazzled assay, combined *snx1/snx6* dosage reduction increased failure of commissural axons to cross. The direction of the phenotype depends on the sensitized background: together these genetic results support a contribution to normal axonal responses, but neither identifies the receptor cargo nor proves that the two fly proteins act in one physical complex. Simonetti et al., October 2019, [doi:10.1038/s41556-019-0393-3](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41556-019-0393-3). (simonetti2019molecularidentificationof pages 8-9, simonetti2019molecularidentificationof pages 6-8, simonetti2019molecularidentificationof pages 28-29)

**Wingless/Wnt secretion: an important negative result.** Zhang and colleagues generated a probable *Dsnx1* null allele by deleting **1,566 base pairs**, from upstream of the start codon into the third exon. Mutants were **viable and fertile** and showed no detected Wingless-signaling or Wingless-secretion defect in the tested wing-disc assays. Increasing DSNX1 expression also failed to rescue the secretion defect caused by loss of *Dsnx3*. These findings argue against assigning **Wntless recycling as an established primary cargo function of fly Snx1** under those conditions. Rather, the experimentally supported fly pathway uses **Snx3 with retromer**; absence of a phenotype in one assay does not exclude a context-dependent Snx1 contribution. Zhang et al., November 2011, [doi:10.1038/cr.2011.167](https://doi.org/10.1038/cr.2011.167). (zhang2011snx3controlswinglesswnt pages 11-12, zhang2011snx3controlswinglesswnt pages 1-2)

**Retromer is related, but not synonymous with Snx1.** Current mechanistic interpretation distinguishes the **VPS26–VPS29–VPS35 retromer core** from SNX1/2–SNX5/6-type membrane-remodeling assemblies. A sorting nexin such as **Snx3** can instead cooperate with the core on a distinct, cargo-specific route. Thus, a phenotype caused by depletion of VPS35 or Snx3 cannot automatically be attributed to Snx1. This distinction is relevant to proposed links between endosomal recycling, receptor signaling and lysosome function. Carosi et al., *Molecular and Cellular Biology*, June 2023, [doi:10.1080/10985549.2023.2222053](https://doi.org/10.1080/10985549.2023.2222053); McNally and Cullen, *Trends in Cell Biology*, October 2018, [doi:10.1016/j.tcb.2018.06.005](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tcb.2018.06.005). (mcnally2018endosomalretrievalof pages 4-6, mcnally2018endosomalretrievalof pages 6-9, carosi2023receptorrecyclingby pages 1-3)

In particular, fly larval-fat-body experiments showed impaired late endocytic/autolysosomal degradation and defective cathepsin-L delivery after disruption of retromer components, and implicated **Snx3 and Snx6** in proper autolysosomal degradation. They did **not** establish the same phenotype for Snx1. Likewise, the 2023 expert review of Evi/Wntless export emphasizes an Snx3–retromer retrieval route while discussing potentially context-dependent contributions from SNX-BAR machinery; it does not turn Wntless into a demonstrated cargo of fly Snx1. Maruzs et al., *Traffic*, published online August 2015, [doi:10.1111/tra.12309](https://doi.org/10.1111/tra.12309); Wolf and Boutros, *Development*, February 2023, [doi:10.1242/dev.201352](https://doi.org/10.1242/dev.201352). (maruzs2015retromerensuresthe pages 1-2, wolf2023theroleof pages 6-7, wolf2023theroleof pages 7-8)

## Current use, recent research and evidence limits

*Snx1* is used experimentally as a **genetic perturbation of conserved endosomal sorting**, including fly wing-disc comparisons with *Snx3* and embryonic axon-guidance interaction assays with *Snx6*. The newer 2023–2024 literature sharpens interpretation of these experiments by distinguishing retromer-core, SNX3-dependent and SNX-BAR sorting routes; the retrieved recent work did **not** establish a new Q9VQQ6-specific cargo, drug target or clinical application. Human SNX1 studies—including disease-model studies—are mechanistic context, **not** evidence of the same phenotype in this fly gene. Wolf and Boutros, February 2023; Carosi et al., June 2023; Rowlands and Moore, February 2024. (carosi2023receptorrecyclingby pages 1-3, rowlands2024vps35andretromer pages 2-3, wolf2023theroleof pages 6-7)

The principal numerical fly-specific result recoverable from the axon-guidance study is its **sampling**, not an effect-size estimate: one assay lists groups of **23–26 embryos**, with **11 segments per embryo** scored; another lists **16–26 embryos per group**, with **eight abdominal segments per embryo**. The accessible caption did not yield reliable plotted percentages for the individual genotype comparisons, and the Wingless study's retrieved passages report its Snx1-negative result qualitatively. Accordingly, no precise percentage change in axon crossing or Wnt secretion is assigned here. Simonetti et al., October 2019; Zhang et al., November 2011. (simonetti2019molecularidentificationof pages 28-29, zhang2011snx3controlswinglesswnt pages 11-12)

**Annotation conclusion:** classify *D. melanogaster* Snx1/Q9VQQ6 as a **PX–BAR-family, probably endosome-associated membrane-remodeling sorting nexin with genetic evidence for a role in axon guidance**. Annotate direct cargo, biochemical lipid specificity, physical partners and exact fly subcellular distribution as **unresolved**; do not annotate Snx3-mediated Wntless recycling or other species’ SNX1 cargoes as established functions of Q9VQQ6. (zhang2011snx3controlswinglesswnt pages 1-2, simonetti2019molecularidentificationof pages 8-9, simonetti2019molecularidentificationof pages 4-6, simonetti2019molecularidentificationof pages 28-29)
