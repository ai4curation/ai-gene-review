| Pathway component | Type/category | Specific role | Mechanism |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pipe-dependent ventral cue | Maternal spatial determinant | Defines where the extracellular Toll-activating cascade can operate | Ventral follicle-cell expression of **pipe** establishes spatial asymmetry in the eggshell/perivitelline environment, restricting downstream protease activity to the future ventral side (pqac-00000032, pqac-00000037) |
| Gastrulation defective, Snake, and Easter | Extracellular serine-protease cascade | Converts maternal positional information into localized activation of Spätzle | Sequential proteolysis culminates in cleavage of the Spätzle precursor; active ligand is produced most strongly near the ventral midline and declines laterally (pqac-00000032, pqac-00000034) |
| Spätzle (Spz) | Secreted Toll ligand | Provides the extracellular ventralizing signal | Proteolytically activated Spätzle binds Toll, producing spatially graded receptor activation across the embryonic dorsoventral circumference (pqac-00000032, pqac-00000038) |
| Toll | Transmembrane receptor with cytoplasmic TIR domain | Transduces extracellular Spätzle activity into intracellular signaling | Ligand binding promotes receptor dimerization or multimerization and recruitment of the cytoplasmic signaling complex; activation is strongest ventrally and weak or absent dorsally (pqac-00000009, pqac-00000034) |
| MyD88 and Tube | Cytoplasmic adaptor proteins | Couple activated Toll to Pelle kinase | Their death-domain interactions assemble a membrane-associated signaling complex that recruits and activates Pelle; Weckle also supports complex assembly (pqac-00000010, pqac-00000039) |
| Pelle | IRAK-related serine/threonine kinase | Relays the Toll signal to the Dorsal–Cactus regulatory complex | Pelle-dependent signaling promotes modification of Cactus and Dorsal; the evidence favors indirect or complex-mediated Cactus phosphorylation rather than simple direct phosphorylation by Pelle (pqac-00000016, pqac-00000050) |
| Cactus | IκB-family cytoplasmic inhibitor | Sequesters Dorsal and limits its DNA-binding transcriptional activity | Ankyrin-repeat-mediated binding retains Dorsal in the cytoplasm, probably through NLS masking and/or cytoplasmic tethering; Cactus can also inhibit Dorsal–DNA binding in vitro (pqac-00000019, pqac-00000023) |
| Cactus phosphorylation, ubiquitination, and degradation | Signal-dependent proteolytic switch | Releases Dorsal from cytoplasmic inhibition | Toll signaling targets N-terminal serine-containing motifs in Cactus, followed by ubiquitination and 26S-proteasome degradation; removal is strongest ventrally (pqac-00000010, pqac-00000016, pqac-00000039) |
| Dorsal phosphorylation | Post-translational activation step | Promotes efficient signal-dependent nuclear accumulation independently of Cactus removal alone | Toll-responsive phosphorylation of conserved residues in the Rel-homology region enhances nuclear uptake; mutations at relevant serines can impair import even when Cactus is absent or Toll is activated (pqac-00000011, pqac-00000040) |
| Dorsal nuclear localization signal | Nuclear-targeting sequence | Directs liberated Dorsal to nuclear pores | The NLS lies near the C-terminal edge of the Rel-homology domain; Cactus association restricts its availability, whereas signaling and nearby phosphorylation may improve recognition by import receptors (pqac-00000041, pqac-00000043) |
| Importin and nuclear-pore machinery | Nucleocytoplasmic transport system | Transports activated Dorsal into nuclei and contributes to subsequent shuttling | Importin α/β-related transport, NTF-2, Nup88/Mbo, Nup214, and CRM1-associated machinery participate in Dorsal import/export; functional nuclear-pore assembly is required for normal Dorsal dynamics (pqac-00000009, pqac-00000013) |
| Dorsal–Cactus facilitated diffusion | Intracellular shuttling mechanism | Redistributes Dorsal toward Toll-active ventral regions and sharpens or stabilizes the gradient | Mobile Dorsal–Cactus complexes can facilitate Dorsal movement; their recruitment to ventral Toll signaling complexes allows Cactus to act not only as an inhibitor but also as a context-dependent promoter of ventral Dorsal accumulation (pqac-00000017, pqac-00000020) |
| Nuclear Dorsal gradient | Spatial transcription-factor output | Converts graded Toll activity into positional information | Nuclear Dorsal is highest at the ventral midline, intermediate laterally, and low or absent dorsally; different concentrations activate or repress distinct enhancer programs specifying mesoderm, neurogenic ectoderm, dorsal ectoderm, and amnioserosa (pqac-00000014, pqac-00000035, pqac-00000036) |


*Table: This table traces the signaling sequence from ventrally restricted Spätzle activation to regulated Dorsal nuclear import. It also distinguishes the canonical Cactus-degradation mechanism from newer gradient-refining mechanisms such as phosphorylation and facilitated diffusion.*
