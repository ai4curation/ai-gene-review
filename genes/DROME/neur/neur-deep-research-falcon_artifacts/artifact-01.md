| Aspect | Role of *Drosophila melanogaster* Neuralized (Neur) | Evidence and significance |
|---|---|---|
| Primary pathway | Neur is a positive regulator of canonical Delta/Serrate–Notch signaling. It acts chiefly on the ligand side of the pathway rather than by ubiquitinating the Notch receptor. (pqac-00000028, pqac-00000029, pqac-00000075) | Genetic disruption produces a neurogenic phenotype resembling loss of core Notch signaling, supporting placement of Neur in the Notch pathway. |
| Cellular context | Neur functions intracellularly in the **signal-sending cell**, where it associates with the cytoplasmic domain of Delta at the plasma membrane and in Delta-containing endocytic structures. Its action activates Notch **in trans** in an adjacent signal-receiving cell. (pqac-00000016, pqac-00000017, pqac-00000019) | Neur is not a diffusible signal or receptor component; it controls the signaling competence of membrane-bound ligand in the sender. |
| Mechanism of Notch activation | Neur binds Delta, promotes its ubiquitination and endocytosis, and helps generate a force-producing Delta–Neur internalization complex. Pulling on ligand-bound Notch exposes the receptor’s S2 protease site; subsequent S2 and γ-secretase cleavages release NICD, which enters the nucleus and activates Notch-responsive transcription. (pqac-00000025, pqac-00000028, pqac-00000031) | This connects Neur-mediated ligand trafficking directly to the mechanotransduction step that initiates canonical Notch signaling. |
| Embryonic neurogenesis and lateral inhibition | Neur is a predominant ligand-activating factor during selection of embryonic neuroblasts. Neur-dependent Delta signaling suppresses neural fate in neighboring cells, preserving the neural–epidermal balance. (pqac-00000030, pqac-00000058, pqac-00000060) | Loss of *neur* causes excess neural tissue at the expense of ventral epidermis, the classic embryonic neurogenic phenotype, and can cause embryonic lethality. |
| CNS neuronal lineages | In larval brain and ventral-nerve-cord lineages, Neur is the major activator of Delta during ganglion mother-cell sibling specification. Delta–Notch signaling induces different fates in the two newborn neurons. (pqac-00000056, pqac-00000057) | In one assay, *neur* loss abolished the Notch target **Hey** in about **80%** of mutant GMC lineages; combined loss of *neur* and *mib1* increased the defect to **100%**, whereas *mib1* alone had an accessory role. (pqac-00000056) |
| Sensory-organ precursor lineage | During sensory-organ precursor mitosis, Neur accumulates at the antero-basal cortex and is inherited preferentially by the pIIb daughter. In pIIb it promotes Delta uptake and thereby activates Notch in the adjacent pIIa daughter, establishing distinct pIIb and pIIa fates. (pqac-00000016, pqac-00000025, pqac-00000059) | Asymmetric Neur inheritance helps impose signaling directionality after cytokinesis rather than merely increasing pathway activity globally. |
| Relationship with Mindbomb1 | Neur and Mib1 are distinct RING E3 ligases with partly overlapping ligand-activation functions. Neur is enriched in mesoderm, neuroectoderm, and neural precursors, whereas Mib1 is more broadly expressed. Neur binds Delta chiefly through the icd1/NxxN site and forms a relatively stable complex; Mib1 uses other intracellular motifs and depends more strongly on Delta ubiquitination. (pqac-00000070, pqac-00000071, pqac-00000072, pqac-00000073) | They can compensate in some tissues but are not interchangeable everywhere: Neur predominates in embryonic neurogenesis and CNS lineages, while Delta signaling in the wing pouch—where Neur is not expressed—depends on Mib1. (pqac-00000070, pqac-00000076) |
| Ubiquitin-dependent function | The C-terminal RING domain promotes ubiquitin transfer to lysines in DSL-ligand intracellular domains. Ubiquitination enhances endocytic-adaptor recruitment, ligand internalization, pulling-force generation and/or ligand recycling, producing robust Notch activation. (pqac-00000010, pqac-00000036, pqac-00000041, pqac-00000047) | This is the canonical E3-ligase function and is important for maximal signaling, although neither the exact ubiquitin topology nor a complete in-vivo substrate inventory is established. |
| Ubiquitin-independent function | Neur can remain bound to Delta and act as an endocytic co-adaptor even when its RING domain is inactive or Delta intracellular lysines cannot accept ubiquitin. This Delta–Neur complex supports weak-to-moderate, dynamin-dependent signaling; ubiquitin then amplifies its efficiency. (pqac-00000039, pqac-00000040, pqac-00000041, pqac-00000044) | Recent work revises the older model that Delta ubiquitination is absolutely required: Neur-controlled neural development can tolerate lysine-less Delta better than Mib1-dependent processes can. |
| Context dependence of endocytic machinery | Endocytosis remains essential, but its adaptors vary by tissue. Many Delta-signaling settings use Epsin, whereas GMC and sensory-lineage signaling can show substantial Epsin independence while remaining Neur- and dynamin-dependent. (pqac-00000031, pqac-00000032, pqac-00000033) | Neur is therefore a context-sensitive organizer of signaling-competent ligand uptake, not simply a universal ubiquitin tagger. |
| Key loss-of-function phenotypes | Reported consequences include embryonic neurogenic transformation, loss of Notch-target expression and sibling-fate asymmetry in CNS lineages, and defective sensory-organ precursor selection or pIIa/pIIb specification. Severity can increase when Mib1-dependent backup is also removed. (pqac-00000056, pqac-00000058, pqac-00000059, pqac-00000060) | These phenotypes consistently reflect insufficient ligand-mediated Notch activation rather than a generalized failure of Notch receptor synthesis. |


*Table: This table summarizes where Neuralized acts in the Drosophila Notch pathway, how its catalytic and adaptor functions activate ligands, and the developmental consequences of losing neur. It also distinguishes Neuralized from the partially redundant E3 ligase Mindbomb1.*
