CC   -!- FUNCTION: Part of the Mon1-Ccz1 guanyl-nucleotide exchange factor
CC       complex specific for Rab7 that promotes the exchange of GDP to GTP,
CC       converting Rab7 from an inactive GDP-bound form into an active GTP-
CC       bound form (Probable). Plays an important role in membrane trafficking
CC       through the secretory apparatus (By similarity). Required for
CC       recruitment of Rab7 to endosomal and autophagosomal membranes to
CC       mediate endolysosomal and autolysosomal vesicle maturation
CC       (PubMed:23418349, PubMed:27559127, PubMed:37463208). Required for
CC       fusion of multivesicular bodies and lysosomes but not their formation
CC       or trafficking (PubMed:23418349). Involved in the replacement of Rab5
CC       (and possibly Rab4) with Rab7, also known as Rab conversion or the Rab
CC       cascade, during endosomal maturation (PubMed:23418349). The Mon1-Ccz1
CC       complex is recruited to phosphatidylinositol 3-phosphate (PtdIns[3]P)
CC       enriched membranes by Rab5, which stimulates recruitment and guanyl-
CC       nucleotide exchange of Rab7 (PubMed:32391792). Together with Rab7
CC       required for autolysosome formation in fat cells and autophagic
CC       degradation during starvation-induced basal and developmental autophagy
CC       (PubMed:27559127). Involved in neuromuscular junction (NMJ) presynaptic
CC       bouton function and morphogenesis (PubMed:32658990). Together with
CC       Rab7, regulates levels of postsynaptic glutamate receptor GluRIIA in
CC       the NMJ presynapse (PubMed:32658990). {ECO:0000255|RuleBase:RU367048,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:23418349, ECO:0000269|PubMed:27559127,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:32391792, ECO:0000269|PubMed:32658990,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:37463208, ECO:0000305|PubMed:23418349}.
CC   -!- ACTIVITY REGULATION: The Rab7 guanyl-nucleotide exchange factor (GEF)
CC       activity of the Mon1-Ccz1 complex is autoinhibited by the N-terminal
CC       disordered region of Mon1 (PubMed:37463208). GEF activity is stimulated
CC       by Rab5-mediated recruitment to membranes (PubMed:32391792).
CC       {ECO:0000269|PubMed:32391792, ECO:0000269|PubMed:37463208}.
CC   -!- SUBUNIT: Component of the Mon1-Ccz1 guanyl-nucleotide exchange factor
CC       complex made up of Mon1, Ccz1 and Bulli; the interaction of Bulli with
CC       the Mon1-Ccz1 heterodimer is mediated via the C-terminal Mic1 domain of
CC       Bulli (PubMed:27559127, PubMed:32499409, PubMed:37155863,
CC       PubMed:37463208). Mon1 and Ccz1 form a stable complex which displays
CC       Rab7 GEF activity with or without Bulli; GEF activity is enhanced by
CC       Bulli possibly by improving membrane association of the complex
CC       (PubMed:32499409, PubMed:37155863). Interacts with Rab5 and Rab7;
CC       preferentially binds GTP-bound Rab5 and GDP-bound Rab7
CC       (PubMed:27559127). {ECO:0000269|PubMed:27559127,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:32499409, ECO:0000269|PubMed:37155863,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:37463208}.
CC   -!- SUBCELLULAR LOCATION: Cytoplasm, cytosol {ECO:0000269|PubMed:23418349}.
CC       Note=There is no evidence of association with endosomal membranes,
CC       however this association may be transient (Probable). Binds
CC       phospholipids with a preference for phosphatidylinositol
CC       monophosphates, including phosphatidylinositol 3-phosphate (PtdIns[3]P)
CC       that is enriched in early endosomal and autophagosomal membranes
CC       (PubMed:27559127). {ECO:0000269|PubMed:27559127,
CC       ECO:0000305|PubMed:23418349}.
CC   -!- DOMAIN: The disordered N-terminal is involved in autoinhibition of the
CC       Mon1-Ccz1 complex guanyl-nucleotide exchange factor activity.
CC       {ECO:0000269|PubMed:37463208}.
CC   -!- DISRUPTION PHENOTYPE: Accumulation of autophagosomes and reduced number
CC       of autolysosomes in fat cells caused by disruption of Rab7 recruitment
CC       to autophagosomes leading to a defect in autophagosome-autolysosome
CC       fusion (PubMed:27559127). Impaired lysosomal degradation of endocytosed
CC       Notch and Boss receptors in the developing eye (PubMed:27559127).
CC       {ECO:0000269|PubMed:27559127}.
CC   -!- SIMILARITY: Belongs to the MON1/SAND family.
CC       {ECO:0000255|RuleBase:RU367048}.
