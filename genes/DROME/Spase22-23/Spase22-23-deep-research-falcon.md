# Functional annotation of *Drosophila melanogaster* Spase22-23

## Identity and scope

**Spase22-23 is the *D. melanogaster* gene CG5677**, annotated here under the supplied UniProt accession **Q9VCA9**. Primary fly literature explicitly identifies CG5677 as the signal-peptidase-complex **Spase22/23 subunit** and distinguishes it from Spase12 (CG11500), Spase18/21 (CG2358), and Spase25 (CG1751). A comparative study places fly Spase22/23 with yeast Spc3p and human SPC22/23, also called **SPCS3**. Thus, the literature supports the intended gene identification; the exact Q9VCA9 accession and supplied SPC3/SPC22 domain identifiers were not independently established by the papers examined. Spase22-23 must not be confused with the separately studied fly gene *spase12* or human **SPCS1**. (abrams2005crebaregulatessecretory pages 4-5, abrams2005crebaregulatessecretory pages 5-7, gilbert2013drosophilasignalpeptidase pages 2-3, liaci2021structureofthe pages 1-3)

**Bottom line.** Spase22-23 is best annotated as a conserved, **non-proteolytic but functionally important structural partner of the endoplasmic-reticulum (ER) signal peptidase complex (SPC)**. Its principal inferred role is to support removal of cleavable signal peptides from proteins entering the ER, rather than to catalyze peptide-bond hydrolysis by itself. Fly-specific studies support its identity, transcriptional regulation, and importance during West Nile virus infection; the detailed molecular mechanism comes predominantly from human SPC structures and yeast homolog studies. (gilbert2013drosophilasignalpeptidase pages 2-3, zhang2016acrisprscreen pages 1-2, liaci2021structureofthe pages 8-10, liaci2021structureofthe pages 3-4)

## Primary biochemical function and substrate specificity

The **reaction of the assembled SPC** is hydrolysis of the peptide bond at a cleavable signal-sequence junction, releasing the signal peptide from a newly synthesized secretory or membrane-protein precursor after targeting to the ER. In flies, the proposed four-member complex contains Spase18/21, Spase22/23, Spase25, and Spase12. Fly embryonic microsomes can cleave an added murine immunoglobulin light-chain precursor in vitro, supporting conservation of the *complex’s* activity, but that assay neither purifies Spase22-23 nor measures a reaction catalyzed by CG5677 alone. The Spase12 knockout phenotypes in the same publication are **not** Spase22-23 knockout phenotypes. [Gilbert et al., published April 2013](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0060908). (gilbert2013drosophilasignalpeptidase pages 1-2, gilbert2013drosophilasignalpeptidase pages 2-3)

**Which subunit is the enzyme?** Human reconstitution and approximately **4.9-Å** cryo-electron-microscopy structures identify **SEC11A or SEC11C** as the alternative proteolytic subunit in two four-member SPC forms; human SPC22/23/SPCS3 is their interacting accessory subunit. The catalytic Ser–His–Asp residues are in SEC11, **not** in SPC22/23. The human SPCS3 luminal β-sandwich embraces the SEC11 catalytic domain and helps position or stabilize its active center against the luminal face of the ER membrane. This explains why an accessory subunit can be necessary for robust proteolysis without itself being the protease. Because flies have one identified Spase18/21 homolog rather than the two alternative human SEC11 subunits, the two-human-complex distinction should not be projected onto the fly as a demonstrated two-isoform system. [Liaci et al., published October 2021](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.molcel.2021.07.031). (liaci2021structureofthe pages 1-3, liaci2021structureofthe pages 4-5, liaci2021structureofthe pages 8-10, liaci2021structureofthe pages 3-4, gilbert2013drosophilasignalpeptidase pages 2-3)

**Specificity belongs to the assembled complex, not to Spase22-23 alone.** Cleavable signal peptides typically have an N-terminal **n-region**, a relatively short hydrophobic **h-region**, and a **c-region** containing the cleavage junction; small, uncharged residues at positions −1 and −3 favor processing. The human SEC11 active-site groove accommodates the c-region, while a lipid-filled window assembled by the SPC subunits locally thins the ER membrane and favors short signal-peptide hydrophobic segments over longer, stable transmembrane helices. A 2021 analysis of **921** experimentally verified eukaryotic signal peptides reported a mean and median h-region length of **11 amino acids**; modeling and earlier biochemical assays indicate poor engagement or cleavage when the hydrophobic region extends to approximately **18–20 residues**. These are cross-species substrate rules, **not measured substrate preferences of isolated fly Spase22-23**. [Liaci et al., October 2021](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.molcel.2021.07.031). (liaci2021structureofthe pages 1-3, liaci2021structureofthe pages 4-5, liaci2021structureofthe pages 5-7, liaci2021structureofthe pages 7-8)

## Cellular location and pathway

The supported **site of action is the ER membrane**, with the conserved SPC22/23-family protein membrane-anchored and most of its structured mass exposed to the **ER lumen**, where it contacts the SEC11 proteolytic domain. In the human complex, SPC22/23 also helps form one side of the transmembrane window; its luminal location is supported by structural observations and N-linked glycosylation. Yeast Spc3 topogenesis experiments independently characterize the homolog as an ER signal-anchored subunit whose final insertion depends on ER-targeting and membrane-topogenesis machinery. These observations support ER-membrane localization for fly Spase22-23 by **homology**; they are not a direct imaging measurement of CG5677 in fly cells. [Liaci et al., October 2021](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.molcel.2021.07.031); [Jung et al., September 2021](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jmb.2021.167109). (liaci2021structureofthe pages 3-4, jung2021cotranslationaltargetingand pages 1-2)

Functionally, Spase22-23 belongs to the **early secretory-protein-biogenesis pathway**: signal recognition particle-mediated targeting and Sec61-mediated ER entry bring appropriate nascent chains into proximity with the membrane-associated SPC; signal-peptide removal precedes maturation and subsequent trafficking of many secretory proteins. It is **not itself a signal-transduction receptor or a vesicular cargo transporter**. The fly secretory-gene study places CG5677 alongside genes encoding ER targeting, translocation, and trafficking machinery, rather than assigning it a separate pathway-specific signaling activity. [Abrams and Andrew, published June 2005](https://doi.org/10.1242/dev.01863). (abrams2005crebaregulatessecretory pages 4-5, abrams2005crebaregulatessecretory pages 5-7)

## Direct observations in *Drosophila* and real-world relevance

During embryonic salivary-gland development, CG5677 was identified among highly expressed secretory-pathway-component genes. The SPC gene group showed markedly reduced salivary-gland expression in **CrebA** mutants and loss of sustained high expression in later **fork head (*fkh*)** mutants. A later review’s gene-level table lists **Spase22-23/CG5677** as CrebA-dependent by in-situ analysis and reports a **−1.81** CrebA-null expression-change entry; that is **transcriptional evidence**, not a measure of protein abundance or protease activity. The asterisk attached to that table entry merits caution in interpreting the numerical estimate. [Abrams and Andrew, June 2005](https://doi.org/10.1242/dev.01863); [Fox and Andrew, published 2015](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11515-014-1338-7). (abrams2005crebaregulatessecretory pages 5-7, fox2015transcriptionalregulationof pages 11-13)

In an experimental infection model, inducible **Spase22-23 RNA interference in adult flies decreased West Nile virus Kunjin titres** relative to controls. The study also tested SPC-pathway genes in *Drosophila* cells and human cells, connecting ER signal processing to flavivirus propagation. Its fly experiment establishes a **Spase22-23-dependent infection phenotype**, but does **not** identify a viral polypeptide directly cleaved by purified fly Spase22-23 or establish an antiviral treatment in practice. Crucially, the paper’s separate **Spase12** heterozygous-fly experiment concerns the homolog of human **SPCS1**, not Spase22-23/SPCS3. [Zhang et al., published June 2016](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature18625). (zhang2016acrisprscreen pages 1-2, zhang2016acrisprscreen pages 5-6, zhang2016acrisprscreen pages 2-2)

## Recent research and limits of transfer to CG5677

Recent work expands what the **SPC as a whole** can process, without establishing these particular reactions for the fly subunit. In a **2023 human-cell study**, SPC cleaved the lumen-facing C-terminal segment of the tail-anchored protein **Jaw1** after membrane insertion; the **SEC11A-containing** complex was implicated, and cleavage enhanced Jaw1-associated IP3-receptor-dependent ER Ca²⁺ release. This connects an SPC-dependent cleavage event to signaling, but does **not** show that fly Spase22-23 independently regulates calcium signaling. [Kozono et al., published February 2023](https://doi.org/10.1242/jcs.260439). (kozono2023cleavageofthe pages 1-4)

Human-cell quality-control studies also indicate that SPC can cleave **cryptic sites in misfolded or poorly assembled membrane proteins**, facilitating ER-associated degradation. A **2023 doctoral thesis** describes experimental examples including connexins and iRhom2 and identifies **SPCS1**, rather than SPCS3, as particularly important for recruiting these noncanonical substrates; its candidate-site counts are computational predictions, not validated substrate totals. These findings broaden the possible complex-level biology but cannot be assigned as a proven substrate-recognition function of fly CG5677. [Zanotti, 2023 doctoral thesis](https://doi.org/10.11588/heidok.00033417). (zanotti2023characterisationofthe pages 53-57, zanotti2023characterisationofthe pages 26-30, zanotti2023characterisationofthe pages 34-38)

A **2026** human SPC-A structure bound to a model signal peptide reaches **2.6 Å** resolution. It directly visualizes the c-region in a hydrophobic active-site groove and the h-region in the membrane window, sharpening the 2021 architectural model. It is a substantial advance in **human complex-level** substrate recognition, not an experimental structure of *Drosophila* Spase22-23. [Liaci et al., published May 2026](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-73423-3). (liaci2026structuralbasisof pages 1-2, liaci2026structuralbasisof pages 2-3)

**Annotation confidence.** Membership in the fly SPC and participation in ER signal-peptide processing are well supported by identity, comparative biology, and whole-complex studies. An **accessory, catalytic-support role** and **ER-luminal membrane topology** are strong homolog-based mechanistic inferences. Directly demonstrated CG5677-specific findings are narrower: developmental expression/regulation and reduced West Nile virus titres after RNAi. The available studies do **not** establish an intrinsic catalytic reaction, a fly-specific substrate catalogue, direct fly protein localization, or a Spase22-23-specific null-mutant phenotype. (abrams2005crebaregulatessecretory pages 5-7, fox2015transcriptionalregulationof pages 11-13, zhang2016acrisprscreen pages 1-2, gilbert2013drosophilasignalpeptidase pages 2-3, liaci2021structureofthe pages 3-4)
