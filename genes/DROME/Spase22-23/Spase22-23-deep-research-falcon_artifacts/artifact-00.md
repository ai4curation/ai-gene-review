| **Direct fly identity mapping** | *D. melanogaster* **CG5677** was assigned as the **Spase22/23 subunit**; the other fly SPC genes were listed separately. (pqac-00000018, pqac-00000019) | Strong verification of the gene/protein identity. The paper does not independently provide the UniProt accession **Q9VCA9** or domain identifiers. | [Abrams & Andrew, 2005](https://doi.org/10.1242/dev.01863) |
| **Direct fly expression/regulation** | The review table reports **Spase22-23/CG5677** expression as **−1.81-fold change in CrebA-null embryos**, marked with an asterisk, and identifies CrebA dependence by in-situ analysis. (pqac-00000017, pqac-00000002) | Supports regulation as part of the CrebA-controlled secretory machinery; the asterisk is retained because its table-specific qualification is not reproduced in the available excerpt. It is not a biochemical activity assay. | [Fox & Andrew, 2015](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11515-014-1338-7) |
| **Direct fly perturbation** | Heat-shock-inducible **Spase22-23 inverted-repeat RNAi** in adult flies reduced West Nile virus Kunjin titres seven days after challenge; the experiment used pools of ten flies across three experiments. (pqac-00000025, pqac-00000028) | Demonstrates that fly Spase22-23 supports efficient viral infection, consistent with an SPC-dependent viral-protein-processing requirement. It does not identify the direct viral substrate in flies and must not be confused with the separate **Spase12** heterozygous-mutant experiment. | [Zhang et al., 2016](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature18625) |
| **Fly-complex biochemical evidence** | *Drosophila* embryonic microsomes cleaved murine myeloma light-chain IgG in vitro, demonstrating conserved fly SPC activity; the fly complex contains Spase18/21, Spase22/23, Spase25 and Spase12. (pqac-00000011, pqac-00000012) | Establishes activity of the **whole fly SPC**, not a purified Spase22-23 reaction or a Spase22-23-specific substrate assay. Spase12 loss-of-function phenotypes reported in this study cannot be assigned to Spase22-23. | [Gilbert et al., 2013](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0060908) |
| **Ortholog-based structural mechanism** | At approximately **4.9 Å**, human SPC structures show accessory **SPC22/23/SPCS3** contacting SEC11 and forming a luminal β-sandwich that embraces the catalytic core; all subunits contribute to a lipid-filled transmembrane window. (pqac-00000015, pqac-00000040) | Strong conserved-family evidence that fly Spase22-23 is an ER-membrane structural/catalytic-support subunit. Proteolytic residues belong to **SEC11**, not SPCS3; conservation in the fly protein remains an inference rather than a fly structure. | [Liaci et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.molcel.2021.07.031) |
| **Recent ortholog-based application** | Human SPC containing SPCS3 cleaves the lumen-facing C-terminal region of the tail-anchored protein Jaw1 after membrane insertion; the **SEC11A-containing**, rather than SEC11C-containing, complex was implicated, and cleavage enhanced Jaw1-dependent IP3-receptor Ca²⁺ release. (pqac-00000032) | Expands known SPC substrate classes and physiological outputs, but does not show that fly Spase22-23 or a fly Jaw1 ortholog performs this reaction. It does not assign substrate recognition or catalysis specifically to SPCS3. | [Kozono et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1242/jcs.260439) |
| **Latest ortholog-based structural context** | A **2.6 Å** human SPC-A structure bound to a model signal peptide shows its c-region in the SEC11A hydrophobic groove and its h-region in the thinned transmembrane window; peptide binding orders SEC11A helices that frame the substrate. (pqac-00000042, pqac-00000043) | High-resolution support for the conserved complex-level recognition model, not a fruit-fly experiment. SPCS3 is part of the complex architecture, whereas SEC11A supplies the catalytic binding groove and active site. | [Liaci et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-73423-3) |


*Table: Evidence is ordered from direct Drosophila identity and perturbation results to mechanistic findings inferred from human orthologs. The table explicitly separates complex-level activity from Spase22-23-specific evidence and distinguishes accessory SPCS3 from catalytic SEC11.*
