Summary

Verdict: partially supported, with one branch of the hypothesis negative by proxy and the central molecular-function annotation over-annotated.

The pseudoenzyme call is now auditable at residue level. CG14717 belongs to the ABHD11 family (Pfam PF00561 Abhydrolase_1, InterPro IPR000073/IPR029058, PANTHER PTHR46118:SF4), and its closest characterised catalytic relatives are the Drosophila paralog CG2059 (Q9W3R8, 33.7% identity), human ABHD11 (Q8NFV4, 31.4%) and E. coli YbfF (P75736, PDB 3BF7/3BF8). The nucleophile elbow reads GHSMGG in human ABHD11 and GHGMGG in CG14717: the catalytic serine, human Ser132, is Gly120. The oxyanion-hole glycine, human Gly65, is Asp53, and human Phe67 is Asn55. The charge-relay pair is untouched: human Asp156-His287 maps to fly Asp144-His282 and the fly pair hydrogen bonds at 2.72 A, the same distance measured in the human model. Serine hydrolase chemistry needs the nucleophile, so hydrolase activity is excluded on structural grounds. Resolving the triad required correcting a database error: UniProt lists Asp228 as a human ABHD11 active site, but Asp228 OD1 sits 20.73 A from Ser132 OG, while the experimental YbfF structures put the acid 24 residues after the nucleophile, which is Asp156.

Of the two non-catalytic roles the seed hypothesis offers, the architecture supports ligand binding and gives no positive support for complex scaffolding. The pocket has not collapsed. After superposing the two AlphaFold models (241 CA pairs, RMSD 1.31 A, every seeded catalytic anchor landing within 0.16-0.46 A of its sequence-predicted partner), a LIGSITE-style grid finds 155 A³ of enclosed cavity in CG14717 against 56 A³ in human ABHD11 on a common grid, with 75% voxel overlap and 0.77 A mean displacement. The scaffolding test is negative on both proxies available. Conserved surface residues across 16 Drosophilid orthologs show no spatial clustering (mean pairwise CB distance 31.21 A versus 29.47 ± 1.04 A for 5000 random same-size surface sets, z = +1.67, empirical p = 0.96), while the same test applied to cavity-lining residues returns p < 0.0005. The exposed face of the cap insertion, which is where this fold normally makes partner contacts, is the least conserved part of the protein at 10.3% identity to human ABHD11 against 20.0% for the exposed core.

The curation consequence is firm and independent of the structural argument. CG14717's carboxylesterase annotation is paralog carry-over. GO:0106435 on Q9VGJ4 is ISS under GO_REF:0000024 with UniProtKB:Q9W3R8 in the withFrom field, copied from CG2059, the second Drosophila ABHD11 gene, which retains Ser127 and holds the only measured activity evidence (GO:0106435 IDA PMID: 22198472, GO:0017171 HDA PMID: 33827210). Neither activity-based proteomics experiment produced a CG14717 record. One limit has to be stated plainly: no experimental structure of ABHD11 exists in any species, and the human ABHD11-OGDHc association has no interface map, so the assembly surfaces the hypothesis asks about have never been defined in the catalytic relative and could only be tested by proxy.

Key findings

F1. Family assignment and the three characterised catalytic relatives

CG14717 is not an orphan. UniProt cross-references for Q9VGJ4 give Pfam PF00561 (Abhydrolase_1), InterPro IPR000073 and IPR029058, Gene3D 3.40.50.1820, SUPFAM SSF53474 and PANTHER PTHR46118:SF4 "PROTEIN ABHD11", with a single AB-hydrolase-1 domain spanning residues 46-288 of 306. Global Needleman-Wunsch alignment with BLOSUM62 and affine gaps (-11/-1) gives pairwise identities of 31.4% to human ABHD11 over 293 aligned columns, 33.7% to the Drosophila paralog CG2059, and 36.6% between CG2059 and human ABHD11. Both fly proteins carry the same PANTHER subfamily assignment.

E. coli YbfF (P75736, 254 aa) shares the family's diagnostic N-terminal motifs (YbfF IVLVHGLFGSLDNL...IQVDMRNHGLSPRD against CG14717 IVVMHDLNLSLESW...VITVDARNHGLSPY) and is the only member of this immediate group with an experimental structure. That matters for the audit: with no ABHD11 crystal structure anywhere, YbfF is the only place where the family's triad geometry has been measured rather than predicted.

The ordering is itself informative. CG2059, not CG14717, is the closer Drosophila match to human ABHD11, yet both fly genes receive the same automatic UniProt name "sn-1-specific diacylglycerol lipase ABHD11" with EC 3.1.1.116 from ARBA rule inference.

The retained Asp-His dyad in native geometry means the loss is a point change on an otherwise intact catalytic scaffold.

What survives the caveats is the position and the overlap: the pocket sits where the ABHD11 active site sits, lined by the equivalent positions, and 75% of the human cavity volume is reproduced in the fly protein.

F4. No conserved surface patch: the structural test of scaffolding is negative

Star alignment of 16 Drosophilid CG14717-orthogroup sequences onto Q9VGJ4 covers a wide range of divergence: D. sechellia 98.0%, D. simulans 97.7%, D. mauritiana 97.4%, D. erecta 94.8%, D. yakuba 94.1%, D. rhopaloa 88.2%, D. suzukii 84.9%, then 45.9-52.8% for D. ananassae, D. virilis, D. lebanonensis, D. navojoa, D. mojavensis, D. arizonae, D. grimshawi, D. albomicans and D. hydei. Per-residue identity across the orthogroup averages 0.68.

Shrake-Rupley solvent accessibility on the AlphaFold v6 model (120 sphere points, 1.4 A probe) partitions the fold as a folded protein should: 120 buried residues (RSA < 0.1) have mean conservation 0.83 while 147 exposed residues (RSA > 0.25) have mean 0.54. The core is constrained and the surface is not.

The largest connected conserved-surface patch (CB-CB < 8 A) contains only 7 residues (Pro172, Ser176, Leu177, Glu179, Phe183, Arg206, Lys207) and its centroid sits 28.0 A from His282 NE2; the next two patches have 4 and 3 residues. Power is the caveat. Seven of the 16 orthologs are more than 84% identical to CG14717, so the effective sample size is closer to 9 independent lineages, and an interface conserved in physicochemical character rather than exact residue identity would be missed. The result argues against a conserved scaffolding surface without excluding a lineage-restricted or chemically degenerate one.

F5. The assembly surface of the catalytic relative has never been defined

UniProt PDB cross-references are empty for Q8NFV4 (human ABHD11), Q9VGJ4 (CG14717) and Q9W3R8 (CG2059). Only the bacterial relative P75736 (YbfF) has experimental structures, 3BF7 and 3BF8. RCSB full-text search for "ABHD11" and for "CG14717" returned HTTP 204, no content, for both. The ABHD11-OGDHc association reported in PMID: 32792488 rests on coimmunoprecipitation and functional rescue, with no structure, no crosslinking map and no interface mutant.

The hypothesis asks which surfaces mediate assembly into the complex in the catalytic relative and whether CG14717 retains them. Those surfaces cannot be enumerated from any public structure. Any claim either way would be an assertion. The structural test was therefore run by proxy, in two forms: the orthogroup surface-conservation test above, and a region-by-region human-to-fly divergence comparison on the AlphaFold model.

F6. Reported interaction partners: chaperones and one two-hybrid hit, no OGDHc subunit

The seed hypothesis asks for any reported interaction partner, stating plainly if none exists. Partners exist, and they are weak.

IntAct returns 10 interaction records for Q9VGJ4. Six are "association" by anti-tag coimmunoprecipitation in Drosophila Schneider 2 late embryonic cells from the DPIM2 affinity purification-mass spectrometry map (PMID: 38944040): Hsp22 (P02515), Hsp23 (P02516), Hsp26 (P02517), Hsp27 (P02518), Hsp60A (O02649) and Hsp60C (Q9VMN5), all at IntAct MI score 0.35. Four are "physical association" by yeast two-hybrid with Fnta (Q9VR31, farnesyltransferase alpha subunit) at MI score 0.67, from PMID: 37061542 plus one unassigned record. No OGDH, DLST, DLD or any other 2-oxoglutarate dehydrogenase complex subunit appears.

Human ABHD11 is a different picture. UniProt SUBUNIT records "Interacts with OGDH and DLST; this interaction maintains the functional lipoylation of the 2-oxoglutarate dehydrogenase complex" with ECO:0000269 from PMID: 32792488, and GO carries GO:0045252 oxoglutarate dehydrogenase complex, part_of, IDA from the same paper.

Reading: Hsp22 and the Hsp60 chaperonins are mitochondrial, so the simplest interpretation of the AP-MS set is that tagged CG14717 is a chaperone client in the matrix. Chaperone recovery in tagged AP-MS is also a frequent background pattern and can indicate overexpression-driven folding stress. The Fnta two-hybrid hit is cytosolic and topologically inconsistent with a matrix protein. The one interaction that would support a conserved scaffolding role has not been reported for CG14717 or for any invertebrate ortholog.

Mechanistic model and interpretation

The architecture of CG14717 separates cleanly into three parts with three different histories. The import signal is intact and more canonical than the human ortholog's. The hydrophobic core and the substrate cavity are conserved: buried residue conservation is 0.83 and 75% of the human cavity volume is reproduced. The catalytic chemistry is gone at one position, and it has gone independently in at least three ways across the Drosophilidae. The exposed surface, which is what a scaffold would need, is free to drift, and in the cap insertion where this fold makes its partner contacts it has drifted furthest.

Three readings are consistent with this pattern, and the data rank them.

Ligand binding without turnover is the best-supported non-catalytic role. The pocket sits in the native position, lined by the equivalent residues, and the protein is still imported. The limit is that the structure does not name a ligand, and an open alpha/beta hydrolase cleft is the default outcome of losing an active-site side chain. "Can bind" is supported; "binds X" is not.

Complex scaffolding has no positive structural support. Conserved surface residues are not clustered (p = 0.96 against a positive control at p < 0.0005), the largest conserved patch has 7 residues and sits 28.0 A from the catalytic histidine, the cap surface is the most divergent part of the protein, and no OGDHc subunit appears in any of the 10 CG14717 interaction records. The branch cannot be closed, because the human interface has never been mapped and an interface can be maintained by shape and charge while individual residues turn over.

Decaying duplicate. The uneven decay argues against it: the targeting signal and the cavity are intact while the chemistry was lost repeatedly over tens of millions of years. The argument is suggestive rather than decisive, since no fly phenotype exists to anchor it.

The curation implication does not depend on which of these is right. The conflict between the pseudoenzyme call and the annotation record is traceable to a single transfer step: 31-34% identity between two fly paralogs is high enough to trigger ISS and far too low to license transfer across a lost catalytic nucleophile.

Mechanistic scope

The immediate molecular question is whether the CG14717 polypeptide performs ester hydrolysis, binds a small molecule, or binds a protein partner. The layers separate as follows.

Direct gene-product chemistry. Ester hydrolysis is excluded on structural grounds. A serine hydrolase without the nucleophile serine cannot form the acyl-enzyme intermediate, and no exception to the two-step mechanism has been reported in this superfamily (PMID: 33827210). Mutation of the conserved GXSXG residues abolishes ABHD11 activity in heterologous assay (PMID: 28465236). No assay has ever been run on CG14717 protein.

Direct gene-product binding. A cavity of 155-178 A³ lined by the same positions as the human pocket is retained and open to solvent. This is a capability and the structure does not name a ligand. The one reported binary partner, Fnta, comes from a Y2H screen performed outside the mitochondrion.

Localization. Inferred, not measured, in the fly. The presequence is intact and more canonical than that of human ABHD11 (amphipathic moment 1.68 against 0.90), which is a sequence observation rather than an import assay.

Downstream phenotype. None exists. FlyBase records 2 alleles of CG14717 with no phenotypic data and a viable phenotypic class, and 4 alleles of CG2059 with no phenotypic data. Every statement about a cellular or organismal role for CG14717 is inference.

This separation matters because the human work that motivates the scaffolding hypothesis (PMID: 32792488) is a protein-level association plus a metabolic rescue in a catalytically intact protein, and the downstream immunometabolic work (PMID: 41184294, PMID: 40166327) describes what happens when that active enzyme is pharmacologically inhibited. Neither addresses what a nucleophile-less homolog does.

Conflicts and alternatives

Paralog confusion is the source of the conflict, and it is documented in the record. Drosophila has two ABHD11 genes. CG2059 (Q9W3R8, X:7286358..7288117) retains Ser127 and is detected as an active serine hydrolase by two independent chemoproteomic experiments. CG14717 (FBgn0265271, 3R:11696509..11698327) does not and is not. Across the Drosophilidae, 41 of 42 CG2059-orthogroup proteins retain the serine while 16 of 19 CG14717-orthogroup proteins have lost it. The loss is old and maintained, which argues against a sequencing or annotation artefact in a single species.

Database carry-over compounds it. Both substantive annotations on CG14717 are similarity transfers, one from the catalytic paralog and one from the human ortholog. The UniProt protein name for all three accessions is "sn-1-specific diacylglycerol lipase ABHD11" with EC 3.1.1.116 from ARBA rule inference, so the name itself propagates an activity CG14717 cannot perform. The substrate claim embedded in that name is also doubtful for the human enzyme: competitive ABPP found no activity of hABHD11 towards mono- or diacylglycerols (PMID: 27544863).

A competing reading of the interaction data. The six chaperones recovered with tagged CG14717 in S2 cells (PMID: 38944040) are consistent with a client relationship or with overexpression-driven folding stress. They do not establish a complex and they are not the OGDHc.

The organism-specific alternative. If ABHD11's conserved function is OGDHc association rather than catalysis, a fly paralog could keep the association while shedding the chemistry. The surface data do not support that: no conserved exposed patch, and the cap insertion is the most divergent exposed region. This remains the strongest competing hypothesis because the human interface has never been mapped.

An alternative this report cannot exclude. CG14717 may be a decaying duplicate whose retained cavity and presequence reflect recent relaxation rather than a maintained function. The strongest argument against is the uneven decay, which is suggestive and not decisive.

Limitations and knowledge gaps

The structural work uses AlphaFold predictions, not experimental structures, for all three ABHD11 proteins. Mean pLDDT is high (93.6 in the core, 96.4 in the cap) but a prediction does not report alternative conformations or a bound state. Cavity volumes depend on the probe radius (1.4 A), grid spacing (0.7 A) and PSP threshold, and the human-to-fly ratio reverses at the stricter threshold (27 A³ human against 17 A³ fly at PSP ≥ 7), so the correct statement is that a pocket is retained. The surface-conservation test assumes an interface would be conserved within the Drosophilidae, which is reasonable and untested at 46-98% ortholog identity, and the effective sample size is about 9 independent lineages. No experiment has ever been performed on CG14717 protein other than two interaction screens.

Proposed follow-up experiments and discriminating tests

Ordered by how much each separates the three candidate answers (ligand binding, complex scaffolding, neither).

  1. Matrix proximity labelling from CG14717. TurboID fused to endogenously tagged CG14717 in adult flies, with CG2059-TurboID and a matrix-targeted TurboID as controls. Specific recovery of Ogdh, kdn and Dld supports the scaffolding branch despite the surface result. Recovery limited to Hsp60A/Hsp60C and other chaperones marks the AP-MS pattern as background or a client relationship.
  2. Serine restoration. Build CG14717 G120S, and the double G120S/D53G that also restores the oxyanion glycine, and test for fluorophosphonate probe labelling. Gain of labelling confirms the fold is otherwise intact and that a single substitution removed the activity, which makes the pseudoenzyme call experimental rather than inferential.
  3. Ligand capture against the retained cavity. Thermal proteome profiling or native MS of purified CG14717 against a mitochondrial metabolite panel, with human ABHD11 as a positive-control comparator. A reproducible binder converts "retained cavity" into a molecular function.
  4. Genetic interaction with 2-oxoglutarate dehydrogenase. Test CG14717 null flies for sensitivity to OGDHc perturbation and for 2-oxoglutarate and succinate levels. Human ABHD11 loss destabilizes OGDHc function (PMID: 32792488); if the fly protein scaffolds the same complex, the null should move in that direction.
  5. Double mutant with CG2059. At 33.7% identity with the catalytic paralog annotated from direct assays, single-mutant redundancy is a real possibility. The double null tests it directly.
  6. Comparative check in the three species that kept the serine. Three of 19 CG14717-orthogroup proteins still have the nucleophile. Comparing those against the 16 that lost it, for cap-region substitution and expression, shows whether the loss is accompanied by any other coordinated change.

Direct answer to the hypothesis as posed

  • Family and closest characterised catalytic relative. ABHD11 family, alpha/beta hydrolase fold (PF00561, PANTHER PTHR46118:SF4). Closest characterised catalytic relatives: the Drosophila paralog CG2059 (Q9W3R8, 33.7% identity, active by two chemoproteomic assays), human ABHD11 (Q8NFV4, 31.4%, lipase IDA and OGDHc association) and E. coli YbfF (P75736), the only member of the group with an experimental structure.
  • Residues lost, with positions in both proteins. Ser132 to Gly120 (nucleophile), Gly65 to Asp53 (oxyanion hole), Phe67 to Asn55. Retained: His64/His52, Leu66/Leu54, Met133/Met121, Asp156/Asp144, Gly286-His287/Gly281-His282.
  • Pocket. Present, not collapsed. 155 A³ on a common grid with human ABHD11 (56 A³), 178 A³ in its own frame, 75% voxel overlap after superposition at 0.77 A mean displacement.
  • Assembly surfaces of the catalytic relative. Not defined by any experiment or structure, so retention cannot be assessed directly. Stated plainly rather than inferred.
  • Reported interaction partners. They exist. CG14717 has 10 IntAct records: six chaperones by AP-MS (PMID: 38944040) and Fnta by Y2H (PMID: 37061542). Human ABHD11 interacts with OGDH and DLST (PMID: 32792488). No OGDHc subunit appears in any CG14717 record.
  • Which non-catalytic role the architecture supports. Ligand binding. The pocket is retained and enlarged, the import signal is intact, and the interior is conserved (buried residue conservation 0.83 against 0.54 at the surface). Complex scaffolding has no positive structural support: conserved surface residues are not clustered (p = 0.96) and the cap insertion is the most divergent exposed surface. The structure does distinguish the two options here, and the distinction rests on proxies, because the interface of the catalytic relative has never been mapped. The structure cannot name the ligand.
