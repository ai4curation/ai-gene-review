Gene Description

Fringe (Fng) is a Golgi-resident type II transmembrane glycosyltransferase (GT31 family) with O-fucosylpeptide 3-beta-N-acetylglucosaminyltransferase activity: it adds GlcNAc in beta1,3 linkage to O-fucose that protein O-fucosyltransferase 1 (O-fut1) attaches to consensus sites in EGF-like repeats of Notch (and, in vitro, of Delta and Serrate). This single-sugar extension, installed site-selectively on Notch EGF repeats, is sufficient to increase Notch binding to Delta and decrease binding to Serrate, so Fng acts cell-autonomously in the signal-receiving cell to potentiate Delta-Notch and inhibit Serrate-Notch signalling (both in trans and in cis). Fng is expressed in dorsal cells of the wing disc and in other restricted domains; its expression boundaries position Notch activation to organise the wing D/V boundary and margin, leg joints, the eye equator, and ovarian polar cell/follicle formation.

Comprehensive Research Report: Drosophila melanogaster fringe (fng) Gene

Gene Identity and Verification

The gene fng (fringe, ORF name CG10580) from Drosophila melanogaster encodes a Fringe glycosyltransferase (UniProt Q24342, EC 2.4.1.222) (pandey2019glycosylationofspecific pages 1-3). This protein belongs to the glycosyltransferase family 31 (GT31) and is correctly identified as the β1,3-N-acetylglucosaminyltransferase described in the provided UniProt annotation (pandey2019glycosylationofspecific pages 1-3). Unlike vertebrates, which possess three Fringe paralogs (Lunatic, Manic, and Radical Fringe), Drosophila has a single Fringe protein encoded by the fng gene (pandey2019glycosylationofspecific pages 1-3).

Primary Enzymatic Function and Catalytic Activity

The Catalyzed Reaction

Drosophila Fringe (Fng) catalyzes the transfer of N-acetylglucosamine (GlcNAc) from the nucleotide-sugar donor UDP-GlcNAc to pre-existing O-linked fucose residues on specific epidermal growth factor-like (EGF) repeats within the Notch receptor extracellular domain (gomezUnknownyearmasterproject pages 6-10, gomezUnknownyearmasterprojecta pages 6-10, pandey2019glycosylationofspecific pages 1-3). The reaction produces a GlcNAc-β1,3-fucose-O-serine/threonine disaccharide structure, releasing UDP as a byproduct (gomezUnknownyearmasterprojecta pages 6-10, zhong2011golgiduringdevelopment. pages 6-7).

Critically, the acceptor substrate is not free fucose or an unglycosylated protein site; rather, Fringe recognizes O-fucose that has already been attached to specific serine or threonine residues within properly folded Notch EGF repeats (gomezUnknownyearmasterprojecta pages 40-43, gomezUnknownyearmasterproject pages 40-43, yamamoto2020makingsenseout pages 3-4). This O-fucose is initially added by the enzyme O-fucosyltransferase 1 (O-fut1) in the endoplasmic reticulum; Fringe then extends this modification in the Golgi apparatus (yamamoto2020makingsenseout pages 3-4).

Substrate Specificity

Fringe exhibits remarkable substrate specificity at multiple levels:

  1. Donor specificity: Fringe uses UDP-GlcNAc as its activated sugar donor (gomezUnknownyearmasterproject pages 6-10, gomezUnknownyearmasterprojecta pages 6-10).

  2. Acceptor specificity: The enzyme selectively modifies O-fucosylated EGF-like repeats of Notch receptors rather than acting on other glycoproteins or glycan structures (pandey2019glycosylationofspecific pages 10-11, zhong2011golgiduringdevelopment. pages 6-7).

  3. Site selectivity within Notch: Mass spectrometric analyses reveal that Fringe does not uniformly modify all potential O-fucose sites on Notch. Instead, it shows site-selective elongation (harvey2016mappingsitesof pages 1-2, harvey2016mappingsitesof pages 3-4). The functionally most important target sites are EGF8, EGF9, and EGF12, with EGF8 and EGF12 making particularly strong contributions to wing development phenotypes (pandey2019glycosylationofspecific pages 1-3, pandey2019glycosylationofspecific pages 23-28). Additional sites such as EGF1 and EGF7 have been experimentally shown to undergo Fringe-dependent elongation, while other sites (e.g., EGF31) remain predominantly in the monosaccharide O-fucose form (harvey2016mappingsitesof pages 3-4).

Catalytic Mechanism

Fringe is classified as an inverting Leloir glycosyltransferase that employs a single-displacement (SN2-like) mechanism, wherein the acceptor hydroxyl group attacks the anomeric carbon of the donor sugar from the opposite face while the UDP leaving group departs, resulting in inversion of the anomeric configuration (gomezUnknownyearmasterproject pages 6-10, gomezUnknownyearmasterprojecta pages 6-10). Structural and mutagenesis studies on mammalian Fringe homologs suggest that a conserved aspartate residue (D232 in mouse Manic Fringe) functions as a catalytic base to activate the acceptor hydroxyl group (gomezUnknownyearmasterprojecta pages 40-43, gomezUnknownyearmasterproject pages 23-30). However, the precise transition state and detailed chemical mechanism have not been directly demonstrated experimentally for Drosophila Fringe and remain inferred from structural modeling and molecular dynamics simulations (gomezUnknownyearmasterprojecta pages 40-43).

Structural Features

Fringe adopts a GT-A fold, a characteristic structural architecture of many glycosyltransferases comprising two tightly associated β/α/β domains arranged around a continuous central β-sheet (gomezUnknownyearmasterproject pages 1-6, moremen2019emergingstructuralinsights pages 3-4). A key feature of this fold is the conserved DXD motif, whose two carboxylate residues coordinate a divalent metal ion (typically Mn²⁺) and interact with the ribose and phosphate moieties of the nucleotide-sugar donor (gomezUnknownyearmasterproject pages 1-6, gomezUnknownyearmasterprojecta pages 1-6, moremen2019emergingstructuralinsights pages 3-4). Mutation of this motif in Drosophila Fringe abolishes enzymatic activity, confirming its functional importance (pandey2019glycosylationofspecific pages 3-5). Structural insights derive primarily from crystal structures of vertebrate Manic Fringe and related GT31 enzymes, complemented by molecular docking and molecular dynamics simulations (gomezUnknownyearmasterproject pages 23-30, gomezUnknownyearmasterprojecta pages 40-43, gomezUnknownyearmasterproject pages 6-10).

Subcellular Localization

Drosophila Fringe is localized to the Golgi apparatus, where it modifies Notch receptors as they transit through the secretory pathway (zhong2011golgiduringdevelopment. pages 6-7, holdener2019proteinofucosylationstructure pages 10-12, yamamoto2020makingsenseout pages 3-4). This Golgi localization is consistent with Fringe's role as a glycan-processing enzyme that acts on substrates already modified by upstream enzymes in the endoplasmic reticulum (yamamoto2020makingsenseout pages 3-4). The sequential glycosylation pathway involves initial O-fucosylation by O-fut1 in the ER, followed by Fringe-mediated GlcNAc addition in the Golgi, before the mature, glycosylated Notch receptor reaches the cell surface (zhong2011golgiduringdevelopment. pages 6-7, yamamoto2020makingsenseout pages 3-4).

Role in Notch Signaling Pathway

Modulation of Ligand-Receptor Interactions

Fringe's primary biochemical function in the Notch pathway is to modulate the receptor's differential responsiveness to its two classes of ligands: Delta and Serrate (the Drosophila ortholog of mammalian Jagged) (zhong2011golgiduringdevelopment. pages 6-7).

Enhancement of Delta-Notch Signaling: Fringe modification enhances Notch binding to and activation by Delta ligands (kakuda2020canonicalnotchligands pages 2-3, zhong2011golgiduringdevelopment. pages 6-7). This enhancement has been demonstrated through cell-based ligand-binding assays and developmental rescue experiments showing that Fringe-dependent glycosylation of EGF8 and EGF12 is particularly important for Delta-mediated Notch activation (pandey2019glycosylationofspecific pages 23-28, kakuda2020canonicalnotchligands pages 6-8).

Inhibition of Serrate-Notch Signaling: Conversely, Fringe modification reduces Notch responsiveness to Serrate, creating ligand discrimination (kakuda2020canonicalnotchligands pages 2-3, zhong2011golgiduringdevelopment. pages 6-7, kakuda2020canonicalnotchligands pages 6-8). This inhibitory effect involves both reduced ligand binding and altered signaling output (pandey2019glycosylationofspecific pages 23-28, pandey2019glycosylationofspecific pages 1-3).

Regulation of cis-Inhibition: Beyond modulating trans-activation (receptor-ligand interactions between neighboring cells), Fringe also regulates cis-inhibition, wherein Notch and ligands in the same cell interact to suppress signaling. Glycosylation of EGF8 and EGF12 prevents excessive Notch-Serrate cis-inhibition, allowing appropriate Serrate-mediated signaling to occur (pandey2019glycosylationofspecific pages 23-28, pandey2019glycosylationofspecific pages 1-3).

Developmental Functions: Wing Development and Boundary Formation

The most thoroughly characterized developmental role of Fringe in Drosophila is in wing imaginal disc patterning and wing margin formation (chen2023notchsignalingin pages 5-6, zhong2011golgiduringdevelopment. pages 6-7, zhong2011golgiduringdevelopment. pages 4-6).

Dorsal-Ventral Boundary Establishment: During wing development, the wing imaginal disc comprises distinct dorsal and ventral cell populations that must be maintained as separate compartments (zhong2011golgiduringdevelopment. pages 6-7, zhong2011golgiduringdevelopment. pages 4-6). Fringe is expressed specifically in dorsal cells, where it modifies Notch to enhance Delta responsiveness and reduce Serrate responsiveness (chen2023notchsignalingin pages 5-6, zhong2011golgiduringdevelopment. pages 6-7). This asymmetric distribution creates a stripe of Notch activation at the boundary between Fringe-expressing dorsal cells and Fringe-negative ventral cells (zhong2011golgiduringdevelopment. pages 6-7). This boundary region subsequently becomes the adult wing margin (chen2023notchsignalingin pages 5-6, zhong2011golgiduringdevelopment. pages 6-7).

Wing Vein Development: Fringe-dependent glycosylation of Notch EGF repeats—particularly EGF8, EGF9, and EGF12—is also important for proper wing vein formation (pandey2019glycosylationofspecific pages 8-10, pandey2019glycosylationofspecific pages 1-3). Loss of Fringe or mutation of these glycosylation sites produces wing vein defects (pandey2019glycosylationofspecific pages 8-10).

Context-Dependent Activity: Notably, Fringe function shows developmental context specificity. While loss of fng severely disrupts wing boundary formation and margin development, it has relatively little effect on other Notch-dependent processes such as lateral inhibition during neurogenesis or asymmetric cell division (zhong2011golgiduringdevelopment. pages 6-7). This context dependence reflects the differential importance of ligand discrimination in different developmental settings.

Signaling Mechanism

Fringe does not simply increase or decrease total Notch signaling; rather, it remodels the receptor's ligand preference, creating spatial patterns of differential ligand responsiveness (zhong2011golgiduringdevelopment. pages 6-7, chen2023notchsignalingin pages 5-6). By making dorsal cells more responsive to Delta and less responsive to Serrate, Fringe helps organize asymmetric signaling at compartment boundaries (chen2023notchsignalingin pages 17-18, zhong2011golgiduringdevelopment. pages 4-6). This enables neighboring dorsal and ventral cells to communicate through specific ligand-receptor pairs, establishing distinct signaling domains that pattern the developing wing (zhong2011golgiduringdevelopment. pages 4-6, zhong2011golgiduringdevelopment. pages 15-16).

Experimental and Structural Evidence

The understanding of Fringe function derives from diverse experimental and computational approaches:

Genetic and Developmental Studies: Classical genetic analyses, including loss-of-function mutations, dosage studies, and developmental rescue experiments, established Fringe's role in wing development and boundary formation (pandey2019glycosylationofspecific pages 8-10, pandey2019glycosylationofspecific pages 1-3).

Mutagenesis Studies: Site-directed mutagenesis of both the Fringe enzyme (particularly the catalytic DXD motif) and Notch O-fucosylation sites identified critical residues and target sites (pandey2019glycosylationofspecific pages 3-5, pandey2019glycosylationofspecific pages 23-28, gomezUnknownyearmasterproject pages 23-30, gomezUnknownyearmasterprojecta pages 23-30).

Mass Spectrometry: Comprehensive mass spectrometric mapping of Notch EGF repeats 1-36, comparing samples with and without Fringe expression, revealed site-specific glycosylation patterns and identified which O-fucose sites are efficiently elongated by Fringe (harvey2016mappingsitesof pages 1-2, harvey2016mappingsitesof pages 3-4, harvey2016mappingsitesof pages 13-15).

Cell-Based Functional Assays: Ligand-binding assays, cell aggregation assays, and signaling reporter assays demonstrated Fringe's effects on Notch-Delta and Notch-Serrate interactions (pandey2019glycosylationofspecific pages 23-28, harvey2016mappingsitesof pages 1-2).

Structural Biology: X-ray crystallography of mammalian Manic Fringe provided structural insights into the GT-A fold, donor and acceptor binding sites, and metal coordination (gomezUnknownyearmasterproject pages 23-30, gomezUnknownyearmasterprojecta pages 23-30, gomezUnknownyearmasterproject pages 6-10).

Evolutionary Conservation and Functional Diversification

Fringe is evolutionarily conserved across metazoans (gomezUnknownyearmasterproject pages 6-10, zhong2011golgiduringdevelopment. pages 6-7). While Drosophila possesses a single Fringe gene, vertebrates have expanded to three paralogs—Lunatic Fringe (LFNG), Manic Fringe (MFNG), and Radical Fringe (RFNG)—that show both conserved substrate recognition and functional specialization (holdener2019proteinofucosylationstructure pages 10-12, pandey2019glycosylationofspecific pages 1-3). All Fringe proteins recognize similar O-fucosylated EGF-repeat determinants, but they differ in expression patterns and biological contexts, contributing to non-redundant developmental functions (holdener2019proteinofucosylationstructure pages 10-12). The conservation of the Fringe-Notch glycosylation module across diverse organisms underscores its fundamental importance in regulating cell-cell communication during development (chen2023notchsignalingin pages 17-18).

Summary

The Drosophila melanogaster gene fng encodes Fringe, a Golgi-localized β1,3-N-acetylglucosaminyltransferase (EC 2.4.1.222) that catalyzes the transfer of GlcNAc from UDP-GlcNAc to O-fucosylated EGF-like repeats on the Notch receptor (gomezUnknownyearmasterproject pages 6-10, gomezUnknownyearmasterprojecta pages 6-10, pandey2019glycosylationofspecific pages 1-3). This modification, occurring primarily at EGF8, EGF9, and EGF12, remodels Notch's ligand responsiveness by enhancing Delta-mediated activation while inhibiting Serrate-mediated activation (pandey2019glycosylationofspecific pages 23-28, pandey2019glycosylationofspecific pages 1-3, kakuda2020canonicalnotchligands pages 2-3, zhong2011golgiduringdevelopment. pages 6-7). Fringe's primary developmental role in Drosophila is establishing the dorsal-ventral boundary in the wing imaginal disc through spatially restricted expression that creates asymmetric Notch signaling, ultimately giving rise to the adult wing margin (chen2023notchsignalingin pages 5-6, zhong2011golgiduringdevelopment. pages 6-7, zhong2011golgiduringdevelopment. pages 4-6). The enzyme adopts a GT-A fold with a conserved DXD metal-binding motif essential for catalysis (gomezUnknownyearmasterproject pages 1-6, pandey2019glycosylationofspecific pages 3-5). Understanding of Fringe derives from integrated genetic, biochemical, mass spectrometric, structural, and computational studies (pandey2019glycosylationofspecific pages 23-28, harvey2016mappingsitesof pages 1-2, harvey2016mappingsitesof pages 3-4, gomezUnknownyearmasterproject pages 23-30, gomezUnknownyearmasterprojecta pages 23-30), revealing a precisely regulated glycosyltransferase that fine-tunes receptor-ligand interactions to control developmental patterning.

fng (Fringe, Q24342) — curation notes

Drosophila melanogaster, FBgn0011591, CG10580. 412 aa, type II Golgi membrane protein,
GT31 family. PANTHER PTHR10811 (FRINGE-RELATED); the UniProt record lists no PANTHER
subfamily line.

Notch role: Fringe O-fucose beta1,3-GlcNAc-transferase (signal-receiving cell)

  • Activity PMID:10935626;
    PMID:10935637.
  • Acts in Golgi, DxD motif required; secreted form inactive PMID:10899003.
  • Sufficiency of GlcNAc addition: PMID:17923477
  • Site selectivity on Notch EGF repeats PMID:27268051; also modifies Dl/Ser in vitro
    PMID:12036964; in vivo Fng product extended with GlcA PMID:18725413.
  • Genetic: cell-autonomously inhibits Ser response and potentiates Dl response
    PMID:9202123; boundary of fng expression positions Notch activation at wing D/V boundary
    [PMID:7954826, PMID:7671307], eye equator PMID:9834035, leg joints [PMID:9806911,
    PMID:10357895], ovarian polar cells PMID:11493544. Modulates cis interactions too
    PMID:25255098. Can also positively affect Ser signalling PMID:23152840.

Pathway variant notes

  • Drosophila has a single Fringe; mammals have LFNG, MFNG, RFNG with differing effects
    (e.g. RFNG/LFNG enhance DLL1 and inhibit/enhance JAG1 differently). C. elegans lacks
    Fringe. Requires prior O-fucosylation by O-fut1 (Pofut1).

description: >-
  Fringe (Fng) is a Golgi-resident type II transmembrane glycosyltransferase (GT31 family)
  with O-fucosylpeptide 3-beta-N-acetylglucosaminyltransferase activity: it adds GlcNAc in
  beta1,3 linkage to O-fucose that protein O-fucosyltransferase 1 (O-fut1) attaches to consensus
  sites in EGF-like repeats of Notch (and, in vitro, of Delta and Serrate). This single-sugar
  extension, installed site-selectively on Notch EGF repeats, is sufficient to increase Notch
  binding to Delta and decrease binding to Serrate, so Fng acts cell-autonomously in the signal-receiving
  cell to potentiate Delta-Notch and inhibit Serrate-Notch signalling (both in trans and in
  cis). Fng is expressed in dorsal cells of the wing disc and in other restricted domains;
  its expression boundaries position Notch activation to organise the wing D/V boundary and
  margin, leg joints, the eye equator, and ovarian polar cell/follicle formation.
