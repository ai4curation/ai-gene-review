| Property | Description |
|---|---|
| Gene name | **fng** (*fringe*; ORF **CG10580**) from *Drosophila melanogaster*; protein accession **UniProt Q24342**. Drosophila has one Fringe protein, unlike the three vertebrate paralogs LFNG, MFNG, and RFNG (pqac-00000007, pqac-00000057). |
| EC number | **EC 2.4.1.222**, O-fucosylpeptide 3-β-N-acetylglucosaminyltransferase. |
| Protein family | A Fringe-family, glycosyltransferase-family-31 (**GT31**) enzyme specialized for elongating protein-bound O-fucose glycans on Notch EGF-like repeats (pqac-00000007, pqac-00000057). |
| Enzymatic function | Catalyzes β1,3 transfer of N-acetylglucosamine (GlcNAc) to an existing O-linked fucose on selected epidermal-growth-factor-like repeats of the Notch extracellular domain, producing a **GlcNAc-β1,3-Fuc-O-Ser/Thr** disaccharide and UDP (pqac-00000003, pqac-00000006). |
| Donor substrate | **UDP-GlcNAc** is the activated sugar donor; nucleotide-sugar binding and catalysis are associated with a divalent metal ion, generally Mn²⁺ in structural and biochemical models (pqac-00000001, pqac-00000032). |
| Acceptor substrate | The immediate acceptor is not free fucose or an unglycosylated peptide: it is **O-fucose attached to Ser/Thr within a properly folded Notch EGF-like repeat**. Recognition depends on both the glycan and the surrounding EGF-domain context (pqac-00000001, pqac-00000004, pqac-00000017). |
| Key target sites | Mass spectrometry shows site-selective rather than uniform elongation across Notch. Functionally important Fringe-responsive repeats include **EGF8, EGF9, and EGF12**, with EGF8 and EGF12 making the strongest demonstrated contributions to wing-vein and wing-margin phenotypes; EGF1 and EGF7 are additional experimentally observed elongated sites, whereas several repeats are inefficiently modified (pqac-00000019, pqac-00000020, pqac-00000022). |
| Catalytic mechanism | Classified as an **inverting Leloir glycosyltransferase**, consistent with a direct-displacement, SN2-like transfer that inverts the donor sugar’s anomeric configuration. The precise transition state and proposed catalytic-base assignment have not been directly demonstrated for Drosophila Fng and should be treated as structure-supported inference rather than established chemistry (pqac-00000000, pqac-00000001, pqac-00000048). |
| Structural fold | Fringe proteins are assigned a **GT-A-like fold** with a central β-sheet surrounded by α-helices and a metal-dependent nucleotide-sugar-binding site. Detailed structural conclusions derive principally from vertebrate Manic Fringe and homologous GT31 enzymes rather than an experimentally solved full-length Drosophila Fng structure (pqac-00000032, pqac-00000038, pqac-00000053). |
| Conserved motifs | A conserved **DXD motif** characteristic of many GT-A glycosyltransferases is required for activity and supports divalent-metal and nucleotide-sugar coordination. Mutation of the Drosophila Fng catalytic motif abolishes glycosyltransferase-dependent effects, linking signaling activity to catalysis (pqac-00000005, pqac-00000032, pqac-00000038). |
| Subcellular localization | Fng functions in the **Golgi apparatus**, where it encounters and elongates O-fucosylated Notch during receptor transit through the secretory pathway. Initial O-fucosylation by O-fut1 occurs upstream in the ER; Fringe-dependent extension follows in the Golgi lumen (pqac-00000008, pqac-00000009, pqac-00000017). |
| Effect on Notch–Delta signaling | Fringe modification generally **enhances Notch responsiveness to Delta**, affecting ligand binding and trans-activation rather than merely increasing receptor abundance. Glycans on EGF8 and EGF12 are especially important for Delta-dependent signaling, with EGF9 contributing more modestly in tested wing contexts (pqac-00000005, pqac-00000016, pqac-00000041). |
| Effect on Notch–Serrate signaling | Fng generally **reduces Serrate-dependent activation of Fringe-modified Notch**, generating ligand discrimination between Delta and Serrate. Site-specific work also shows that glycans on EGF8 and EGF12 regulate same-cell Notch–Serrate interactions and prevent excessive Serrate cis-inhibition, illustrating that the outcome depends on whether receptor and ligand interact in cis or trans (pqac-00000011, pqac-00000012, pqac-00000039). |
| Primary developmental role | Its best-defined role is spatial control of Notch at the wing imaginal disc’s **dorsal–ventral boundary**. Dorsally restricted fng expression biases cells toward Delta and away from Serrate responses, producing a stripe of Notch activity that organizes wing growth and gives rise to the adult wing margin; loss or ectopic expression disrupts compartment-boundary behavior (pqac-00000024, pqac-00000025). |
| Experimental evidence | Evidence includes genetic loss-of-function and dosage studies; catalytically inactive Fng mutants; Notch O-fucosylation-site mutants; ligand-binding, cell-aggregation, and signaling assays; developmental rescue and wing-phenotype analysis; and mass-spectrometric mapping of glycoforms across Notch EGF1–36. Crystallography of mammalian Fringe homologs plus docking and molecular-dynamics studies provide structural inference for donor, acceptor, metal, and catalytic-residue recognition (pqac-00000011, pqac-00000014, pqac-00000020, pqac-00000047, pqac-00000049). |
