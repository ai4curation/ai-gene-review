CC   -!- FUNCTION: Low-capacity facilitative transporter for trehalose
CC       (PubMed:20035867, PubMed:34032568). Can also transport glucose
CC       (PubMed:34032568). Does not transport maltose, sucrose, lactose or
CC       fructose (PubMed:20035867, PubMed:34032568). Mediates the bidirectional
CC       transfer of trehalose (PubMed:20035867). Responsible for the transport
CC       of trehalose synthesized in the fat body and the incorporation of
CC       trehalose into other tissues that require a carbon source, thereby
CC       regulating trehalose levels in the hemolymph (PubMed:20035867).
CC       Required in glial cells of the blood brain barrier to fuel glycolysis
CC       but not required in neurons (PubMed:26235423). Neurons rely on the
CC       citric acid cycle for their energy needs and utilise alanine and
CC       lactate, by-products of glial cell glycolysis released into the
CC       hemolymph, as fuel (PubMed:26235423). Increased expression in glial
CC       cells of the blood brain barrier during starvation and increased cell
CC       surface localization enhances carbohydrate uptake to protect the
CC       central nervous system from restricted nutrient availability
CC       (PubMed:34032568). {ECO:0000269|PubMed:20035867,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:26235423, ECO:0000269|PubMed:34032568}.
