| **Pnut–Sep2–Sep1 septin complex; guanine-nucleotide binding, GTP hydrolysis, and filament assembly** | Immunoaffinity-purified embryonic complex contained Pnut, Sep2, and Sep1 and was inferred to be a heterohexamer with two copies of each subunit. It carried approximately one guanine nucleotide per septin polypeptide, bound and hydrolyzed added GTP, and polymerized into filaments. Pnut therefore functions primarily as a polymerizing septin subunit rather than as a soluble metabolic enzyme. (pqac-00000017, pqac-00000036) | Field et al. (1 May 1996), [doi:10.1083/jcb.133.3.605](https://doi.org/10.1083/jcb.133.3.605) | GTPase activity was measured for the purified **complex**, not isolated Pnut; a Pnut-specific turnover rate or substrate specificity beyond guanine nucleotide was not established. The P40797–CG8705 cross-reference derives from the supplied UniProt identity and was not independently demonstrated in this paper. |
| **Pnut C-terminal coiled coil: septin-complex assembly, Orc6 interaction, and cortical organization** | Recombinant pull-downs showed that deleting Pnut’s coiled-coil region prevented assembly with Sep1/Sep2; leucine substitutions could preserve complex incorporation while strongly reducing Orc6 binding. Orc6 bound the Pnut-containing complex, increased complex GTPase activity, and promoted filament formation. Pnut RNAi in L2 cells caused multinucleation and Orc6 mislocalization. (pqac-00000039, pqac-00000040) | Huijbregts et al. (1 January 2009), [doi:10.1091/mbc.e08-07-0754](https://doi.org/10.1091/mbc.e08-07-0754) | Orc6 stimulation applies to the assembled septin complex, not demonstrably to isolated Pnut catalysis. Overexpressed Pnut formed aggregates, and deletion mutants may perturb folding as well as individual interaction surfaces. |
| **Cortical furrows and Ran–importin–Anillin-dependent Pnut recruitment** | In syncytial embryos, inhibiting Ran impaired pseudocleavage-furrow formation, depth, and persistence and specifically prevented Pnut—but not actin or Anillin—from localizing to furrows. Importin-α/β binding to Anillin blocked the Anillin–Pnut interaction in biochemical assays; RanGTP reversed that inhibition. An Anillin mutant lacking the importin-binding site bypassed the effect of Ran inhibition on furrow ingression. (pqac-00000021, pqac-00000022) | Silverman-Gavrila et al. (September 2008; online 25 June 2008), [doi:10.1091/mbc.e08-01-0049](https://doi.org/10.1091/mbc.e08-01-0049) | Establishes regulated recruitment and furrow function, not direct membrane binding by isolated Pnut. Pseudocleavage furrows are transient embryonic structures and do not complete cytokinesis. |
| **Maintenance of embryonic cellularization architecture** | Maternal Pnut depletion did not prevent initial furrow ingression or initial actin-network assembly, but during fast cellularization F-actin became patchy/punctate, Anillin progressively declined at the front, and gastrulation subsequently failed. Sep1 disappeared from the front whereas Sep2 remained, indicating Pnut-dependent complex organization plus partial septin redundancy. (pqac-00000001, pqac-00000003, pqac-00000004) | Adam et al. (September 2000), [doi:10.1091/mbc.11.9.3123](https://doi.org/10.1091/mbc.11.9.3123) | Residual Sep2 antibody signal may include cross-reactivity with Sep5. The results argue that Pnut maintains later cortical organization, not that it is universally indispensable for every division. |
| **Planar epithelial cytokinesis and adherens-junction remodeling** | In *pnut* mutant epidermal cells, the Anillin-positive actomyosin ring contracted slowly, remained anchored at the adherens-junction belt, became oblong, and failed to disengage E-cadherin contacts efficiently. Laser ablation of neighboring junctions relieved tension and rescued junction disengagement/new daughter junction formation in **10/22 cases (45%)**; E-cadherin overexpression delayed contraction by an additional **3 min ± 30 s**. Pnut was required for planar but not orthogonal epithelial divisions. (pqac-00000029, pqac-00000042, pqac-00000043) | Founounou et al. (11 February 2013), [doi:10.1016/j.devcel.2013.01.008](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.devcel.2013.01.008) | The phenotype reflects tissue mechanics and the assembled septin system; it does not prove that Pnut alone generates contractile force. Orthogonal divisions can finish without Pnut, demonstrating strong context dependence. |
| **C-terminal phosphorylation controls membrane association, complex stability, and viability** | Mass spectrometry identified phosphorylation at **T509 and S517**; phosphorylated Pnut reached up to **40%** of total Pnut in the first 2 h of embryogenesis. In mitotic S2 cells, membrane localization occurred in **9.5% of WT (n=74)**, **69.6–69.7% of non-phosphorylatable T509A/S517A (n=69)**, and **17.5% of phosphomimetic T509E/S517E cells (n=63)**. At 29°C, adult survival was **21.7±4.8%** for the alanine mutant versus **57.5±9.7%** for WT. The phosphomimetic complex was unstable at low salt and formed aggregates/few short filaments. (pqac-00000008, pqac-00000010, pqac-00000011, pqac-00000016) | Akhmetova et al. (January 2018), [doi:10.1534/g3.117.300186](https://doi.org/10.1534/g3.117.300186) | Glutamate and alanine substitutions only approximate constitutive phosphorylation and dephosphorylation. The responsible kinase/phosphatase was not established, and in-vitro salt conditions do not fully reproduce the embryonic cortex. |
| **Rho-regulated membrane recruitment, border-cell surface geometry, and collective migration** | Pnut occupied cytoplasmic and membrane pools and was enriched at border-cell–nurse-cell interfaces. Dominant-negative RhoN19 decreased membrane-associated Pnut and increased cytoplasmic puncta; active RhoV14 increased cortical Pnut. Pnut, Sep1, or Sep2 RNAi impaired detachment and migration, while excess Sep1/Sep2 also impaired migration; among clusters that detached after septin perturbation, **30–70%** migrated abnormally. Knockdown of one complex member reduced abundance of the others, supporting mutual stabilization. (pqac-00000023, pqac-00000025, pqac-00000035, pqac-00000037, pqac-00000038) | Gabbert et al. (August 2023), [doi:10.1016/j.devcel.2023.05.017](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.devcel.2023.05.017) | Rho manipulation establishes upstream control of localization but not direct Rho–Pnut binding. Myosin and septins colocalized dynamically yet did not measurably recruit one another, so their mechanistic coupling remains unresolved. |
| **Anillin-organized anillo-septin pool during contractile-ring closure (recent contextual evidence)** | In Drosophila S2-cell deplete-and-rescue experiments, an Anillin allele defective in Rho1-GTP binding and anillo-septin assembly complemented an allele defective in Sticky/Citron-kinase interaction. The results support physically distinct Anillin pools: a Rho1/septin-associated pool involved in contractile-ring closure/shedding and a Sticky-associated pool involved in midbody-ring formation. (pqac-00000019, pqac-00000041) | Chambaud, Carim & Hickson (preprint, 22 April 2024), [doi:10.1101/2024.04.21.590302](https://doi.org/10.1101/2024.04.21.590302) | **Contextual, not standalone biochemical proof about Pnut.** The experiments manipulate Anillin domains and infer septin-pool behavior from prior Pnut-dependent work; they do not purify Pnut, measure its GTPase activity, or demonstrate a new direct Pnut interaction. It is also a preprint rather than a peer-reviewed 2024 paper in the retrieved evidence set. |
