# Functional annotation report: *Drosophila melanogaster* peanut (*pnut*)

## Identity and principal function

**Identity check.** This report concerns **Peanut (Pnut)**, the *D. melanogaster* protein specified by the supplied UniProt accession **P40797**, gene **pnut**, and ORF **CG8705**: [UniProt P40797](https://www.uniprot.org/uniprotkb/P40797/entry). Fly-specific biochemical, genetic, and immunolocalization studies independently identify Pnut as a **septin**, rather than a similarly named protein from another organism. Its experimentally characterized guanine-nucleotide-binding properties agree with the supplied septin, G_SEPTIN and P-loop NTPase domain annotations. The precise P40797–CG8705 cross-reference comes from the supplied UniProt identification; the papers examined primarily use the names *pnut* and Peanut. (field1996apurifieddrosophila pages 1-2, huijbregts2009drosophilaorc6facilitates pages 1-2, founounou2013septinsregulatethe pages 2-3)

**Primary molecular role.** Pnut is a **membrane-associated, polymerizing cytoskeletal scaffold**, not a transporter or a conventional metabolic enzyme. Together with Sep2 and Sep1, it forms a six-subunit septin complex, commonly represented **Pnut–Sep2–Sep1–Sep1–Sep2–Pnut**, that can assemble into higher-order filaments. The purified embryonic complex carries approximately **one bound guanine nucleotide per septin polypeptide** and binds and hydrolyzes added **GTP**. Thus, GTP is the relevant biochemical substrate for the *complex*, while its principal established cellular output is spatial organization of the cortex, cleavage furrows and associated actomyosin machinery. The cited experiments do **not** establish an isolated-Pnut catalytic rate or demonstrate that Pnut is the sole hydrolyzing subunit. (field1996apurifieddrosophila pages 1-2, akhmetova2018phosphorylationofpnut pages 1-7, huijbregts2009drosophilaorc6facilitates pages 1-2)

Pnut’s C-terminal coiled-coil region helps assemble the Sep1/Sep2 complex and engage **Orc6**, the origin-recognition-complex subunit with an additional cytokinetic role. Pnut coiled-coil deletions disrupt septin-complex incorporation; particular leucine substitutions retain incorporation but markedly weaken Orc6 binding. Orc6 increases the **assembled complex’s** GTPase activity and promotes filament formation under the tested conditions. These findings support a structural and regulatory function for Pnut within a multiprotein assembly, rather than an independently established Orc6-controlled reaction catalyzed by purified Pnut alone. (huijbregts2009drosophilaorc6facilitates pages 1-2, huijbregts2009drosophilaorc6facilitates pages 4-5)

## Where Pnut acts

Pnut acts principally **inside cells at the plasma-membrane-associated cortex**: at dividing-cell cleavage furrows and intercellular bridges, transient embryonic pseudocleavage furrows, and the leading-edge/furrow-canal network of embryonic cellularization. In the developing ovary it also occurs in the cytoplasm and is enriched at the outer interfaces of migrating border cells with nurse cells. These are context-dependent distributions, not evidence that Pnut is secreted or a transmembrane transporter. Antibody staining, tagged-protein imaging, protein depletion and rescue collectively support these sites of action. (adam2000evidenceforfunctional pages 2-3, adam2000evidenceforfunctional pages 4-5, silvermangavrila2008anillinmediatedtargetingof pages 1-2, gabbert2023septinsregulateborder pages 37-42, huijbregts2009drosophilaorc6facilitates pages 1-2)

Pnut is **not constitutively membrane-bound**. Mass spectrometry identified phosphorylation of its C-terminal **T509 and S517** in early embryos; the phosphorylated form reached up to **40% of total Pnut during the first two hours**. In mitotic S2 cells, membrane-associated staining occurred in **9.5% of wild-type cells (n = 74)**, versus **69.6–69.7% with nonphosphorylatable T509A/S517A (n = 69)** and **17.5% with phosphomimetic T509E/S517E (n = 63)**. The alanine mutant also compromised development: survival to adulthood at **29°C was 21.7 ± 4.8%**, compared with **57.5 ± 9.7%** for the wild-type transgene. Recombinant phosphomimetic complexes were less stable and assembled poorly into long filaments under low-salt conditions. The data indicate that regulated Pnut modification helps control cortical association and septin-assembly dynamics; the responsible kinase was not established, and amino-acid substitutions are imperfect substitutes for native phosphorylation. [Akhmetova *et al.*, January 2018](https://doi.org/10.1534/g3.117.300186). (akhmetova2018phosphorylationofpnut pages 11-16, akhmetova2018phosphorylationofpnut pages 16-20, akhmetova2018phosphorylationofpnut pages 20-24)

## Biological processes and mechanistic pathways

**Embryonic furrows and Anillin/Ran regulation.** Pnut participates in the cortical network that organizes embryonic cellularization. In embryos deprived of maternal Pnut, initial cellularization and early actin assembly still occur, but F-actin at cell bases becomes progressively disorganized during the fast phase; Anillin at the front decreases and subsequent morphogenesis is abnormal. Importantly, **Sep1 no longer concentrates at the front, whereas a Sep2-reactive signal remains**, demonstrating differential septin dependence rather than a requirement for Pnut in every early furrow event. The Sep2 antibody could also detect Sep5, limiting assignment of the residual signal solely to Sep2. [Adam *et al.*, September 2000](https://doi.org/10.1091/mbc.11.9.3123). (adam2000evidenceforfunctional pages 1-2, adam2000evidenceforfunctional pages 7-10, adam2000evidenceforfunctional pages 11-12)

An experimentally defined recruitment pathway places Pnut downstream of **Ran–importin–Anillin** in syncytial embryos. Importin-α/β binding to Anillin inhibits Anillin’s interaction with Pnut; **RanGTP relieves this inhibition**. Perturbing Ran prevents Pnut—but not Anillin or actin—from localizing normally to pseudocleavage furrows and impairs their ingression. An Anillin variant lacking the importin-binding site bypasses the measured effect of Ran inhibition on furrow depth and persistence. This is evidence for regulated **Pnut targeting and cortical-furrow organization**, not evidence that Pnut itself is an effector GTPase of Ran. [Silverman-Gavrila *et al.*, September 2008; published online June 2008](https://doi.org/10.1091/mbc.e08-01-0049). (silvermangavrila2008anillinmediatedtargetingof pages 1-2)

**Cytokinesis under epithelial mechanical load.** In the fly dorsal-thorax epithelium, Pnut-dependent septin function supports contractile-ring constriction where a *planar* cleavage furrow must remodel **E-cadherin-based adherens junctions**. *pnut* mutant rings constrict more slowly, remain abnormally shaped and attached at the junctional belt, and often fail to disengage neighboring adhesive contacts. Reducing mechanical opposition by laser-ablation of nearby junctions restored disengagement and new daughter-cell junction formation in **10 of 22 trials (45%)**; E-cadherin overexpression added **3 ± 0.5 minutes** of contraction delay. By contrast, *orthogonal* epithelial divisions can finish without Pnut, although more slowly. The investigators’ force-threshold interpretation is that septin-supported contraction must overcome junctional resistance; the experiments do not show that Pnut itself is a motor or generates force independently of actomyosin. [Founounou *et al.*, February 2013](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.devcel.2013.01.008). (founounou2013septinsregulatethe pages 1-2, founounou2013septinsregulatethe pages 11-12, founounou2013septinsregulatethe pages 2-3)

**Rho signaling and collective migration—recent primary evidence.** The **2023** border-cell study identifies a second, non-cytokinetic setting for the Pnut-containing complex. Pnut, Sep1 and Sep2 occur in membrane-associated and cytoplasmic pools, and reducing Sep1 or Sep2 reduces Pnut abundance, consistent with mutually stabilized assemblies. **Dominant-negative RhoN19 decreases cortical Pnut**, especially at border-cell–nurse-cell contacts, whereas **active RhoV14 increases cortical Pnut**; opposite changes occur in cytoplasmic septin puncta. RNAi against *pnut*, *Sep1* or *Sep2* impairs border-cell detachment and migration. Among perturbed clusters that did detach, **30–70% failed to migrate normally**; altered septin abundance also changed cell-surface geometry. Although septins and myosin dynamically colocalize, their knockdowns did not demonstrate that one directly recruits the other. Accordingly, the supported pathway is **Rho activity → septin cortical recruitment → appropriate cell-surface organization and collective motility**, *not* proven direct Rho–Pnut binding. [Gabbert *et al.*, August 2023](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.devcel.2023.05.017). (gabbert2023septinsregulateborder pages 28-37, gabbert2023septinsregulateborder pages 37-42)

**2024 mechanistic context.** A **2024 preprint** using *Drosophila* S2-cell Anillin separation-of-function alleles reports that Anillin molecules supporting Rho1-dependent septin assembly and those engaging Citron kinase/Sticky can constitute separable functional pools during the transition from contractile ring to midbody ring. Earlier Pnut-depletion experiments cited in that work implicated Pnut in shedding Anillin from the nascent midbody ring. This refines the proposed **Rho1–Anillin–septin cytokinesis pathway**, but the 2024 experiments principally manipulate **Anillin**, rather than biochemically assigning a new catalytic or binding activity to Pnut; the source is a preprint. [Chambaud, Carim & Hickson, April 2024](https://doi.org/10.1101/2024.04.21.590302). (chambaud2024heteroalleliccombinationof pages 1-6, chambaud2024heteroalleliccombinationof pages 15-19)

## Interpretation and research utility

The most defensible functional annotation is **“GTP-binding septin subunit that assembles with other septins into a regulated cortical scaffold supporting furrow architecture, actomyosin-associated cytokinesis and, in border cells, Rho-dependent surface organization and motility.”** This integrates purified-complex biochemistry with *pnut*-specific genetic, imaging and rescue results while retaining an important qualification: **Pnut dependence is cell-type and mechanical-context dependent**, and complex-level GTP hydrolysis must not be mistaken for a measured Pnut-only enzyme reaction. The fly knockout, RNAi, transgenic-mutant, microscopy and in-vitro reconstitution systems constitute current research implementations; the retrieved evidence establishes experimental model-organism use, not a clinical application. [Field *et al.*, May 1996](https://doi.org/10.1083/jcb.133.3.605); [Adam *et al.*, September 2000](https://doi.org/10.1091/mbc.11.9.3123); [Gabbert *et al.*, August 2023](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.devcel.2023.05.017). (field1996apurifieddrosophila pages 1-2, adam2000evidenceforfunctional pages 10-11, gabbert2023septinsregulateborder pages 28-37)
