# Functional annotation of *Drosophila melanogaster* Uaf1 (CG9062; UniProt Q1LZ08)

## Identity and principal conclusion

**The target is the fruit-fly gene *Uaf1*, also called CG9062—not the human gene WDR48, although the proteins are homologs.** The supplied UniProt accession Q1LZ08 identifies a WD-repeat protein in the WDR48 family; a species-specific experimental study independently identifies fly Uaf1/CG9062 as the UAF1/WDR48 component of a complex with fly Usp46 and Wdr20. The WD40-repeat annotation is consistent with its experimentally supported role as a protein-interaction and regulatory subunit. (spencer2023theusp46deubiquitylase pages 2-4, spencer2023theusp46deubiquitylase pages 1-2)

**Best-supported primary function:** Uaf1 is a **noncatalytic cofactor of the Usp46–Uaf1–Wdr20 deubiquitylase complex** that sustains the abundance of Arrow, the fly Wingless/Wnt coreceptor corresponding to vertebrate LRP5/6. By limiting Arrow ubiquitylation and turnover, the complex increases the receptor available at the cell surface and strengthens the response to Wingless. **Usp46—not Uaf1—provides deubiquitylase catalysis**; Uaf1 and Wdr20 regulate the enzyme and help maintain its abundance. Accordingly, there is no separate reaction or catalytic substrate specificity to assign to Uaf1 itself. Arrow is the experimentally supported substrate of the *complex*, whereas its exact ubiquitin-chain linkage preference on fly Arrow has not been established by the cited work. (spencer2023theusp46deubiquitylase pages 9-10, spencer2023theusp46deubiquitylase pages 7-9, spencer2023theusp46deubiquitylase pages 1-2, villamil2013thewd40repeatproteincontaining pages 7-9)

The following evidence summary separates experiments **on the fly protein** from mechanistic corroboration in other organisms.

## Mechanism and biological process

The most informative fly study is **Spencer and colleagues, published October 2023**. Tagged Usp46-complex components associated with tagged Arrow in *Drosophila* S2R+ cells. Depleting any of Usp46, Uaf1 or Wdr20 lowered steady-state Arrow; for **Uaf1 RNAi, the reported comparison was *p* = 0.0008**, compared with *p* = 0.00006 for Usp46 and *p* = 0.000078 for Wdr20. These significance values establish evidence for an effect but are **not** measurements of its magnitude. Uaf1 depletion also reduced Usp46 protein, whereas Uaf1 overexpression increased it; Wdr20 had a stronger effect on Usp46 abundance. These results support both a regulatory-complex role and a contribution to stability of its catalytic partner. Co-immunoprecipitation establishes complex association, not necessarily direct contact between isolated Uaf1 and Arrow. (spencer2023theusp46deubiquitylase pages 9-10, spencer2023theusp46deubiquitylase pages 7-9, spencer2023theusp46deubiquitylase pages 10-11)

At the complex level, experiments found reduced Arrow ubiquitylation and increased cell-surface Arrow when Usp46-complex activity was increased. Conversely, **Uaf1-null clones in the adult posterior midgut had less membrane-associated and cytoplasmic Arrow than adjacent wild-type cells**. The Arrow-reduction comparisons for the tested complex-component mutants were reported as *p* < 0.0001; an Armadillo comparison for Uaf1 mutants was reported as *p* = 0.043. These observations place the functional defect at least partly at the receptor-abundance step, upstream of the signaling pool of Armadillo/β-catenin. They do **not** establish that Uaf1 alone catalyzes Arrow deubiquitylation. The identity of the opposing fly Arrow ubiquitin ligase was not established in this study. (spencer2023theusp46deubiquitylase pages 7-9, spencer2023theusp46deubiquitylase pages 12-13, spencer2023theusp46deubiquitylase pages 11-12)

This molecular role is supported by independent **in vivo** pathway readouts. RNAi against fly Uaf1, and independent CRISPR guides targeting different portions of its coding sequence, reduced the Wingless target **Senseless** in the posterior wing-disc compartment. Wingless-ligand expression at the dorsal–ventral boundary was not reduced, arguing against impaired ligand production as the explanation. In the posterior midgut, *Uaf1*-null mosaic clones reduced the **fz3-GFP** and **nkd-lacZ** Wingless-response reporters cell-autonomously; fz3-GFP could be almost absent in clones approximately **8–20 cells from the midgut–hindgut boundary**, with a less pronounced effect nearer the Wingless source. The reporter comparisons included **91 Uaf1 mutant clones** in one quantified experiment. This spatial pattern supports the authors’ interpretation that the complex maintains the **amplitude and range** of responses to a Wingless gradient. An Axin epistasis experiment with **Usp46**, rather than specifically Uaf1, further places complex action upstream of the β-catenin destruction machinery. (spencer2023theusp46deubiquitylase pages 4-7, spencer2023theusp46deubiquitylase pages 2-4, spencer2023theusp46deubiquitylase pages 4-4)

A physiological consequence is disturbed **Wingless-dependent intestinal homeostasis**: Uaf1-mutant midgut clones increased enteroblasts and were associated with more neighboring stem/progenitor cells and mitoses. This is relevant as an outcome of impaired receptor-level signaling, rather than evidence that Uaf1’s primary molecular activity is cell-cycle control. (spencer2023theusp46deubiquitylase pages 4-4)

## Where the protein acts—and what has not been localized

The demonstrated functional setting is **inside Wingless-responsive fly cells**, including developing wing-disc cells, cultured S2R+ cells and adult posterior-midgut epithelium. The complex regulates a receptor whose functional pool is at the **plasma membrane**; its effects on membrane-associated Arrow, and the complex’s association with Arrow, are consistent with action on receptor trafficking or stability. **They do not directly locate Uaf1 itself at the plasma membrane.** The available fly evidence does not establish a definitive Uaf1-specific cytoplasmic, endosomal or nuclear distribution by localization imaging or fractionation. Similarly, changes in membrane Arrow and cytoplasmic Armadillo must not be presented as measurements of Uaf1 localization. The most precise defensible annotation is an **intracellular regulatory protein acting in a pathway controlling cell-surface Arrow**, with its exact subcellular site of action unresolved. (spencer2023theusp46deubiquitylase pages 7-9, spencer2023theusp46deubiquitylase pages 12-13, spencer2023theusp46deubiquitylase pages 1-2)

## Structural interpretation and cross-species evidence

Uaf1’s WD40-repeat architecture fits a binding/regulatory function, not a USP-family catalytic domain. Mechanistic work on homologous complexes describes UAF1/WDR48 and WDR20 as **allosteric activators** of USP12/USP46: they enhance catalytic turnover without a corresponding increase in substrate-binding affinity. Structural analyses of the homologous USP12 complex describe UAF1 binding away from the catalytic cleft and transmitting conformational changes toward the ubiquitin-binding/catalytic region. This explains *how a WD-repeat cofactor could potentiate a protease*, but the detailed structural mechanism is **inferred for Q1LZ08**, not directly resolved in fly Uaf1. (hodul2017functionofthe pages 2-3, villamil2013thewd40repeatproteincontaining pages 7-9, li2021molecularmechanismsof pages 12-15)

A companion **October 2023** study found a concordant mechanism in **human cells**, with developmental support from *Xenopus* and zebrafish: human USP46–UAF1–WDR20 promoted Wnt/β-catenin signaling, Wnt stimulation increased association with LRP6, and the complex opposed LRP6 ubiquitylation associated with **RNF43 and ZNRF3**. Experiments also found reduced Wnt-dependent intestinal-organoid viability after USP46/UAF1 perturbation. This is strong evidence that receptor protection is evolutionarily conserved, but human organoid, ligase and localization results should **not** be attributed as direct experiments on fly Q1LZ08. The human study proposed that removing ubiquitin chains may facilitate assembly of receptor signaling complexes; that more detailed proposal remains distinct from the directly measured fly Uaf1 phenotypes. (ng2023theusp46complex pages 5-6, ng2023theusp46complex pages 1-2, ng2023theusp46complex pages 6-8, ng2023theusp46complex pages 2-3)

## Limits of annotation: Notch and DNA repair

The historical name **“USP1-associated factor 1” does not establish that fly Uaf1’s principal demonstrated pathway is USP1-mediated DNA repair.** In mammals, USP1–UAF1 deubiquitylates FANCD2/FANCI in the Fanconi-anemia pathway and PCNA in DNA-damage tolerance; reconstituted experiments also show a role for UAF1 DNA binding in FANCD2 deubiquitylation. These are well-supported **mammalian** functions, but the literature examined here does not demonstrate a fly Uaf1–Usp1 interaction, a fly Uaf1-dependent FANCD2/PCNA reaction, a fly Uaf1-specific DNA-repair phenotype or nuclear localization. Such functions are plausible hypotheses from homology, **not established annotations for Q1LZ08**. (villamil2013thewd40repeatproteincontaining pages 6-7, liang2019dnarequirementin pages 1-2, cukras2016theusp1uaf1complex pages 1-5)

Likewise, a 2012 study genetically implicated the distinct fly protein **CG7023**, a USP12/USP46-related deubiquitylase, in *negative* regulation of Notch; its direct USP12–UAF1 biochemical Notch assays used **mammalian proteins**. It did **not** test fly Uaf1/CG9062 as the Notch regulator. In the 2023 fly wing-disc study, Uaf1 depletion did not reduce **wingless** expression, a readout dependent on Notch at the dorsal–ventral boundary, arguing against a general Notch defect **in that setting**. These observations do not exclude untested context-specific Uaf1 functions. (moretti2012theubiquitinspecificprotease pages 5-6, moretti2012theubiquitinspecificprotease pages 11-12, spencer2023theusp46deubiquitylase pages 1-2)

**Annotation recommendation:** describe Q1LZ08 as a **WD40-repeat-containing, nonenzymatic Usp46-complex cofactor that promotes Wingless/Wnt receptor Arrow stability and signal reception in *D. melanogaster***. Record Arrow deubiquitylation as an activity of the **Usp46–Uaf1–Wdr20 complex**, rather than of Uaf1 alone; mark precise Uaf1 subcellular localization, a particular Arrow ubiquitin-linkage preference, and proposed USP1/DNA-repair roles as unresolved or inferred. (spencer2023theusp46deubiquitylase pages 9-10, spencer2023theusp46deubiquitylase pages 12-13, spencer2023theusp46deubiquitylase pages 1-2, liang2019dnarequirementin pages 1-2)
