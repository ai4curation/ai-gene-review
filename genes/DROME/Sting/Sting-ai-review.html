Gene Description

Drosophila Sting (dSTING, CG1667) is the single fly ortholog of the STING family and a multi-pass endoplasmic reticulum membrane protein that acts as an innate-immune signaling adapter. It directly binds cyclic dinucleotides - bacterial cyclic di-GMP and the virus-induced cyclic GMP-AMP isomers 3'2'-cGAMP (its preferred ligand) and 2'3'-cGAMP that are synthesized by the cGAS-like receptors cGLR1 and cGLR2 in response to RNA/DNA virus infection. Ligand binding drives dSTING oligomerization (via a cryptic RHIM motif) and activation of the NF-kappaB transcription factor Relish through the IMD pathway kinase dIKKbeta. Because insects lack type I interferons and the TBK1-IRF3-IFN axis, dSTING signals to NF-kappaB rather than to interferon, inducing antimicrobial peptides and antiviral genes. It mediates defense against bacteria (Listeria monocytogenes, Coxiella burnetii) and a broad range of viruses, and in the adult brain restricts Zika virus by inducing autophagy.

Summary of gene function

dSTING is the single Drosophila ortholog of the STING family (STING_LBD + STING_TM domains;
IPR029158). It is a multi-pass endoplasmic reticulum membrane protein (343 aa) that functions as an
innate-immune signaling adapter. Unlike vertebrate STING, the fly (and insects generally) have no
type I interferon system and no TBK1->IRF3->IFN axis; dSTING instead signals to the NF-kappaB
transcription factor Relish through the IMD pathway (via the kinase dIKKbeta), driving
antimicrobial peptide (AMP) and antiviral gene expression.

Ligand recognition (molecular function)

  • Binds bacterial cyclic di-GMP (c-di-GMP): PMID:29924997
  • Binds the virus-induced CDNs 3'2'-cGAMP (strongly preferred) and 2'3'-cGAMP, produced by the cGAS-like receptors cGLR1/cGLR2 in response to (ds)RNA virus: PMID:34261127 and PMID:34261128
  • A crystal structure of dSTING bound to 3'2'-cGAMP explains isomer selectivity: PMID:34261127
  • Also responds to the virus-induced CDN 2'3'-c-di-GMP, a more potent dSTING agonist than cGAMP: PMID:37659413

Antibacterial function

  • Activates AMP production and defense against Listeria monocytogenes via Relish/IMD: PMID:29924997 and PMID:29924997
  • Protects against the Gram-negative bacterium Coxiella burnetii by limiting reactive oxygen species: PMID:38363133

Antiviral function (NF-kappaB / Relish, interferon-independent)

  • Controls picorna-like viruses downstream of the cGLR sensors, acting upstream of dIKKbeta to induce the antiviral factor Nazo: PMID:30119996
  • 2'3'-cGAMP triggers a Relish (NF-kB)-dependent, interferon-independent antiviral program: PMID:33262294
  • Orally acquired bacterial CDNs drive dSTING/dTBK1-dependent, NF-kB-dependent antiviral immunity in gut enterocytes: PMID:34965418
  • A cryptic RHIM (cRHIM) motif drives dSTING oligomerization and antiviral signaling and its interaction with IMD: PMID:38969190

Autophagy (interferon-independent, context-specific)

  • In the adult brain, Zika-virus-induced NF-kB signaling induces dSTING, which restricts infection by inducing autophagy: PMID:29934091

Localization

  • Endoplasmic reticulum membrane, multi-pass membrane protein: PMID:30119996 (UniProt SUBCELLULAR LOCATION: "Endoplasmic reticulum membrane {ECO:0000269|PubMed:30119996}").

Curation implications

  • The IEA GO:0032481 positive regulation of type I interferon production (InterPro2GO from IPR029158)
    is taxonomically inappropriate for Drosophila, which has no type I interferons. dSTING signals to
    NF-kB/Relish, not IFN. This is a demonstrably wrong electronic mapping and is removed.
  • Autophagy / autophagosome / reticulophagy terms reflect the ancestral STING autophagy function
    (mammalian Q86WV6) transferred by IBA; the fly has a real but context-specific (Zika brain) autophagy
    role — retained as non-core where they are inherited/component terms.
  • Core functions are cyclic-dinucleotide binding (c-di-GMP, 3'2'-cGAMP, 2'3'-cGAMP) driving the
    cGAS/STING signaling pathway to NF-kB (Relish), producing antibacterial and antiviral innate immune

  adapter. It directly binds cyclic dinucleotides - bacterial cyclic di-GMP and the
