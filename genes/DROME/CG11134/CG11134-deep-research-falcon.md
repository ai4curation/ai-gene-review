# CG11134 (UniProt Q9VY93): functional-annotation research report

## Identity and strength of evidence

**The gene symbol ‘CG11134’ is ambiguous or literature is limited for this specific protein.** The identity used here is the one supplied in the question: *Drosophila melanogaster* CG11134, UniProt [Q9VY93](https://www.uniprot.org/uniprotkb/Q9VY93/entry), annotated as a *probable* methylthioribulose-1-phosphate dehydratase (EC 4.2.1.109) in the class-II-aldolase/MtnB family. The supplied HAMAP and domain assignments support that interpretation, but they are computational annotations, not a reported enzyme assay. I could not independently establish the CG11134–Q9VY93 identifier mapping from a retrieved primary paper. Published MtnB comparisons include a *Drosophila* sequence, but do not identify that sequence as CG11134/Q9VY93 or experimentally test this fly protein. Accordingly, results for human APIP, yeast Mde1p, bacterial MtnB, and *Tetrahymena* MtnBD below are **homolog evidence, not direct findings for CG11134**. (ashida2008enzymaticcharacterizationof pages 6-7, nakano2013mtnbdisa pages 5-6, pirkov2008acompleteinventory pages 1-2)

## Probable molecular function and substrate

The most defensible primary-function assignment is **5-methylthioribulose-1-phosphate (MTRu-1-P) dehydratase**. The predicted reaction is:

**MTRu-1-P → 2,3-diketo-5-methylthiopentyl-1-phosphate (DK-MTP-1-P) + H₂O.**

This is the experimentally characterized MtnB reaction in *Bacillus subtilis* and the reaction assigned to human APIP/MtnB; for fly CG11134, both catalysis and physiological substrate remain predictions based on the supplied family annotation. DK-MTP-1-P is unstable: the *Bacillus* study reported a 14.4-second half-life under its assay conditions. No alternative-substrate panel or substrate-preference measurement for Q9VY93 was identified, so MTRu-1-P should be described as the **predicted pathway substrate**, not as a specificity established in flies. (ashida2008enzymaticcharacterizationof pages 6-7, kang2014structuralandbiochemical pages 1-2, ashida2008enzymaticcharacterizationof pages 2-3)

The MtnB-family assignment is mechanistically coherent with the supplied aldolase-II-related domains, but an aldolase-like fold does **not** mean this protein should be annotated as catalyzing a generic aldol cleavage. In human APIP, a 2.0-Å crystal structure showed an aldolase-II-like, zinc-containing active site; human active-site substitutions C97A, E139A, and H115A each left less than 6% of wild-type MtnB activity. EDTA treatment reduced purified *Bacillus* MtnB activity to 16.5% of untreated activity. These findings make divalent-metal-assisted catalysis plausible for CG11134, but do not establish its metal identity, metal dependence, catalytic residues, or oligomeric state experimentally. (kang2014structuralandbiochemical pages 3-4, kang2014structuralandbiochemical pages 4-6, kang2014structuralandbiochemical pages 2-3, ashida2008enzymaticcharacterizationof pages 2-3)

For scale, purified human APIP had a reported *K*ₘ of **9.32 µM** and *V*ₘₐₓ of **1.39 µmol min⁻¹ mg⁻¹** in a coupled MTRu-1-P assay. *Bacillus* MtnB had a reported *K*ₘ of **8.9 ± 0.6 µM** and *k*cat of **16.7 ± 0.5 s⁻¹** under different conditions. **Neither set of values measures fly CG11134**, and they should not be substituted for fly kinetic parameters. (kang2014structuralandbiochemical pages 2-3, ashida2008enzymaticcharacterizationof pages 2-3)

The distinction between the principal dehydratase assignment and possible additional reactions matters. An isolated MtnB domain from the *Tetrahymena thermophila* MtnBD fusion also catalyzed subsequent enolization; purified-protein and NMR experiments did **not** support assigning it an intrinsic phosphatase reaction. Those results neither demonstrate enolase activity nor establish an MtnBD fusion in *Drosophila* CG11134. (nakano2013mtnbdisa pages 1-2, nakano2013mtnbdisa pages 5-6)

## Biological pathway and cellular location

CG11134 is **predicted to act in methionine salvage**, the biochemical route that recycles 5′-methylthioadenosine (MTA), produced during polyamine synthesis, toward methionine. In the eukaryotic pathway characterized in yeast and human cells, MTA is converted to methylthioribose-1-phosphate and then MTRu-1-P; the proposed CG11134/MtnB step dehydrates MTRu-1-P. Subsequent reactions process its product toward 4-methylthio-2-oxobutanoate, which is transaminated to methionine. Thus, CG11134’s specific proposed contribution is **metabolic conversion of a phosphorylated MTA-derived intermediate**, not direct SAM-dependent methylation, polyamine synthesis, or a demonstrated signaling reaction. Yeast deletion experiments established the importance of its MtnB counterpart, Mde1p, for use of MTA in methionine salvage; human APIP knockdown impaired growth when MTA replaced methionine. Neither experiment tested the fly gene. (camille2012functionalidentificationof pages 1-3, pirkov2008acompleteinventory pages 1-2, camille2012functionalidentificationof pages 5-5)

A **cytosolic site of action is plausible but unverified for CG11134**. The characterized eukaryotic salvage pathway is described as cytosolic, and immunofluorescence showed predominantly cytoplasmic human APIP in HeLa cells. I found no direct subcellular-localization experiment for Q9VY93; the available evidence does not warrant a more precise fly compartment or an extracellular assignment. (camille2012functionalidentificationof pages 1-3)

## Recent work, applications, and unresolved questions

A [2024 review of compartmentalized one-carbon metabolism](https://doi.org/10.3389/freae.2024.1451971) discusses MTA production during polyamine synthesis and its connection to methionine salvage; it does **not** supply CG11134-specific biochemical or localization data. No retrieved 2023–2024 study established a revised function, a measured phenotype, or a real-world implementation for this particular fly gene. The most relevant precise experimental foundation therefore remains older primary work in yeast, human cells, *Bacillus*, and *Tetrahymena*. Human APIP has additional experimentally studied associations with cell-death responses, but those must **not** be attributed to fly CG11134 without fly-specific evidence. (kang2014structuralandbiochemical pages 4-6, kang2014structuralandbiochemical pages 1-2, bernasocchi2024subcellularonecarbon pages 3-4)

**Functional-annotation conclusion:** retain **“probable MTRu-1-P dehydratase; predicted methionine-salvage enzyme; likely cytosolic”**, with explicit uncertainty on the fly protein’s substrate selectivity, cofactors, localization, additional activities, and biological phenotype. The most decisive missing evidence would be purified CG11134 product identification and substrate testing, followed by fly loss-of-function metabolite tracing and protein-localization experiments. (nakano2013mtnbdisa pages 5-6, pirkov2008acompleteinventory pages 1-2, camille2012functionalidentificationof pages 1-3, ashida2008enzymaticcharacterizationof pages 2-3)
