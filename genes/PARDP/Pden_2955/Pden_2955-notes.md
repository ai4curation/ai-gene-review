The same paper finds much less N1-aminopropylagmatine production by the Agrobacterium/Paracoccus pairs than by the dedicated CAPA comparators. This does not support replacing the native putrescine route with an agmatine route, or transferring CAPA specificity from any family label.

## Ontology and annotation decisions

QuickGO was queried on 2026-11-05. GO:0102143 is active and its definition explicitly says "Also converts carboxyspermidine to putrescine." GO:0102144 is obsolete as the same activity. No specific carboxyspermidine decarboxylase term was found by name; the supported NTR is used instead. Separate QuickGO name searches for carboxyspermine and carboxythermospermine each returned zero hits. Additional tetraamine chemistry is recorded with proposed MF terms and the distinct GO:0006597/GO:1903603 process terms. The added spermidine, spermine, thermospermine and conditional norspermidine BP terms are siblings, not ancestor/descendant duplicates.

The participation test is satisfied because CASDH catalyzes the condensation and CASDC the decarboxylation. This is not an argument from an annotation-count gap. A QuickGO comparator check nevertheless found GO:0008295 on E. coli SpeE (P09158), GO:0006597 on human SMS (P52788), and GO:0008295/GO:0045312 on Vibrio NspC (Q9KRL4), all catalytic participants. No match for these four target accessions or locus names was found in `gocams/index.tsv`. Existing row payloads are preserved, with only reviews changed; new proposals are appended and marked NEW.

The generic source protein name is not functional evidence. In particular, the ProtNLM saccharopine-dehydrogenase name on A9CG66 conflicts with locus-specific pathway characterization; domain homology alone does not supply that reaction. No new PAINT node placement or family-wide specificity is inferred here.

## 2026-11-05 independent pre-integration re-audit

The initial secondary tetraamine core entry was too strong. PMID:40074085 establishes accession-matched biochemical capabilities by heterologous reconstruction, with final isomers separated by LC-MS/MS, but does not establish native locus-dependent spermine/thermospermine flux or centrality. Native species-level tetraamine observations make the route plausible but do not resolve that gap for each target. The secondary core entry is removed; the experimentally supported NEW BP proposals, proposed MF terms, description and reference findings remain. Spermidine synthesis remains the primary core: it has native C58 genetic support and, for PD1222, a consistent pathway assignment backed by accession-matched reconstruction.

Each NEW reason now states its actual assay and the catalytic step performed by the target, explains the ontology hierarchy and lack of ancestor/descendant duplication, and reports comparator results. QuickGO exact-term queries on 2026-11-05 found: CANSDHs Q9KRL3 and P0DPE4 carry GO:0102143 but no exact GO:0008295/GO:0045312; product-forming catalysts Q9KRL4, P09158 and P19623 carry GO:0008295; NspC proteins Q9KRL4 and Q88IS0 carry GO:0045312; SMS P52788 and SPE4 Q12455 carry GO:0006597. These annotation states check usage and are not cited as biological support. Absences on the same-step CASDH comparators are acknowledged explicitly; the direct experimental pathway evidence, not an absence-based completeness argument, motivates the proposals.

Thermospermine deserves a separate caveat: GO:1903603 has zero exact QuickGO annotations globally. Three same-role catalysts, ACL5 Q9S7X6, H. butylicus SpeE1 A2BIX4 and E. coli SpeE P09158, carry GO:0010487 thermospermine synthase activity but not that BP. This cannot establish that curators overlooked the process. The BP remains a proposal because its active, unrestricted definition is specifically formation of thermospermine under polyamine biosynthesis, and the experiments identify that product plus a direct catalytic route. A suggested question asks curators about use of the currently unpopulated BP for reconstructed pathways. The dcAdoMet-dependent comparator MF is not transferred to the ASA-dependent enzymes.
