PMID33850044 (https://doi.org/10.1128/mSystems.01349-20) was acquired by the normal fetch helper with full text. It assays EGDe metabolite production, proteomics and electron microscopy, but uses 10403S for the eutB deletion and ndh2 comparison in Fig5. The 10403S eutB deletion impairs ethanolamine utilization under anaerobic conditions in modified Welshimer broth with15mM ethanolamine and20nM B12. Native carbon-source use is supported in this experimental system; the study does not separately demonstrate sole-nitrogen-source growth of10403S. EGDe acetate:ethanol ratios must not be represented as measurements on the10403S mutant or purified subunit. The eutB deletion removes catalytic cargo; it is not a shell-specific perturbation proving that a BMC shell is essential.

PMID20519496 (https://doi.org/10.1074/jbc.M110.125112) provides structural evidence from E.coli: the large subunit supplies the catalytic barrel and the small subunit contacts the interfacial cobalamin. This is conserved-role inference for10403S, not native Listeria structural enzymology. EutB is described as catalytic within the holoenzyme, not independently active in isolation. EutC is represented as contributing to the complex activity; its original enables payload remains intact and a PROBABLE qualifier source defect records the recommendation.

GO0006520 was checked against current QuickGO: its amino-acid scope requires carboxylic acids containing amino groups. Ethanolamine is an amino alcohol, so the InterPro amino-acid-process row is removed without changing its original ID. Both the generic and specific BMC assignments concern the ethanolamine compartment; the broad one is refined. Compartment localization is conditional inference from the conserved family/rule and EGDe pathway architecture, not direct imaging of10403S EutB/C. Neither shell assembly nor extracellular electron transfer is added as a gene process merely because pathway disruption changes those outcomes.
