CC   -!- FUNCTION: [Capsid protein]: Forms an icosahedral capsid with a T=4
CC       symmetry composed of 240 copies of the capsid protein surrounded by a
CC       lipid membrane through which penetrate 80 spikes composed of trimers of
CC       E1-E2 heterodimers (PubMed:12388725, PubMed:16407066, PubMed:22001018,
CC       PubMed:8415660). The capsid protein binds to the viral RNA genome at a
CC       site adjacent to a ribosome binding site for viral genome translation
CC       following genome release (By similarity). Possesses a protease activity
CC       that results in its autocatalytic cleavage from the nascent structural
CC       protein (PubMed:1944569). Following its self-cleavage, the capsid
CC       protein transiently associates with ribosomes, and within several
CC       minutes the protein binds to viral RNA and rapidly assembles into
CC       icosahedric core particles (By similarity). The resulting nucleocapsid
CC       eventually associates with the cytoplasmic domain of the spike
CC       glycoprotein E2 at the cell membrane, leading to budding and formation
CC       of mature virions (PubMed:9143274). In case of infection, new virions
CC       attach to target cells and after clathrin-mediated endocytosis their
CC       membrane fuses with the host endosomal membrane (PubMed:9707418). This
CC       leads to the release of the nucleocapsid into the cytoplasm, followed
CC       by an uncoating event necessary for the genomic RNA to become
CC       accessible (By similarity). The uncoating might be triggered by the
CC       interaction of capsid proteins with ribosomes (PubMed:3656418). Binding
CC       of ribosomes would release the genomic RNA since the same region is
CC       genomic RNA-binding and ribosome-binding (By similarity). Specifically
CC       inhibits interleukin-1 receptor-associated kinase 1/IRAK1-dependent
CC       signaling during viral entry, representing a means by which the
CC       alphaviruses may evade innate immune detection and activation prior to
CC       viral gene expression (PubMed:33673546). {ECO:0000250|UniProtKB:P03315,
CC       ECO:0000250|UniProtKB:P27284, ECO:0000269|PubMed:12388725,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:16407066, ECO:0000269|PubMed:1944569,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:22001018, ECO:0000269|PubMed:33673546,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:3656418, ECO:0000269|PubMed:8415660,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:9143274, ECO:0000269|PubMed:9707418}.
CC   -!- FUNCTION: [Assembly protein E3]: Provides the signal sequence for the
CC       translocation of the precursor of protein E3/E2 to the host endoplasmic
CC       reticulum. Furin-cleaved E3 remains associated with spike glycoprotein
CC       E1 and mediates pH protection of the latter during the transport via
CC       the secretory pathway. After virion release from the host cell, the
CC       assembly protein E3 is gradually released in the extracellular space.
CC       {ECO:0000250|UniProtKB:P03315}.
CC   -!- FUNCTION: [Spike glycoprotein E2]: Plays a role in viral attachment to
CC       target host cell, by binding to the cell receptors VLDLR or LRP8/APOER2
CC       (PubMed:34929721). Synthesized as a pE2 precursor which is processed by
CC       furin at the cell membrane just before virion budding, giving rise to
CC       E2-E1 heterodimer. The pE2-E1 heterodimer is stable, whereas E2-E1 is
CC       unstable and dissociate at low pH. pE2 is processed at the last step,
CC       presumably to avoid E1 fusion activation before its final export to
CC       cell surface. E2 C-terminus contains a transitory transmembrane that
CC       would be disrupted by palmitoylation, resulting in reorientation of the
CC       C-terminal tail from lumenal to cytoplasmic side. This step is critical
CC       since E2 C-terminus is involved in budding by interacting with capsid
CC       proteins. This release of E2 C-terminus in cytoplasm occurs lately in
CC       protein export, and precludes premature assembly of particles at the
CC       endoplasmic reticulum membrane. {ECO:0000250|UniProtKB:P03315,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:8995682}.
CC   -!- FUNCTION: [6K protein]: Acts as a viroporin that participates in virus
CC       glycoprotein processing and transport to the plasma membrane, cell
CC       permeabilization and budding of viral particles (PubMed:12424249,
CC       PubMed:36191050, PubMed:8892914). Disrupts the calcium homeostasis of
CC       the cell, probably at the endoplasmic reticulum level resulting in the
CC       increased levels of cytoplasmic calcium (PubMed:17483865). Because of
CC       its lipophilic properties, the 6K protein is postulated to influence
CC       the selection of lipids that interact with the transmembrane domains of
CC       the glycoproteins, which, in turn, affects the deformability of the
CC       bilayer required for the extreme curvature that occurs as budding
CC       proceeds. Present in low amount in virions, about 3% compared to viral
CC       glycoproteins (By similarity). {ECO:0000250|UniProtKB:P03315,
