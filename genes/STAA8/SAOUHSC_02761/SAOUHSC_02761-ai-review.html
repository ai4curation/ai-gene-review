Gene Description

LnsB is the integral membrane partner of the LnsA lipoprotein N-acyltransferase in Staphylococcus aureus. Both proteins are required for terminal N-acylation of diacylglyceryl lipoproteins, and the reconstituted complex preferentially uses the sn-1 acyl chain of phosphatidylglycerol. LnsB associates with LnsA at the external membrane surface and contributes to the active complex, although its precise role in substrate presentation or catalysis remains unresolved. It belongs to a divergent CPBP family; the tested canonical CPBP motif residues are dispensable for N-acylation, and protease activity has not been established. Partner recognition is selective, so activity cannot be inferred for every cross-species LnsA-LnsB combination.

Proposed New Ontology Terms

apolipoprotein N-acyltransferase activity

Definition: Catalysis of the phospholipid-dependent N-acylation of the N-terminal S-diacylglyceryl-L-cysteine of a bacterial lipoprotein.

Justification: Reconstitution in PMID:42580408 establishes phospholipid-dependent terminal N-acylation by the compatible LnsA/LnsB complex, with preference for the sn-1 acyl chain of phosphatidylglycerol under the tested conditions. This substrate-specific proposal matches the Lnt activity proposal without asserting a shared mechanism or family. LnsB contributes to the assembled activity; this does not assign autonomous acyltransferase or protease chemistry to LnsB.

Parent term: acyltransferase activity, transferring groups other than amino-acyl groups
