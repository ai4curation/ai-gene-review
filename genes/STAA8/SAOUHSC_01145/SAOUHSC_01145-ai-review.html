Gene Description

SAOUHSC_01145 encodes the membrane-anchored class-B penicillin-binding protein PBP1 of Staphylococcus aureus NCTC8325. PBP1 is a serine transpeptidase associated with septal peptidoglycan construction and a physical activating partner of the FtsW glycan polymerase. Its PASTA domains and protein interactions contribute to divisome function independently of the transpeptidase active site. Dependence on PBP1 catalysis varies with strain background and the presence of resistance-associated PBP2A.

Proposed New Ontology Terms

serine-type peptidoglycan D,D-transpeptidase activity

Definition: Catalysis of peptide crosslink formation in peptidoglycan by a serine-dependent D,D-transpeptidase: transfer of the donor D-Ala4 acyl group from a pentapeptide stem to an amino acceptor on another stem or its peptide bridge, with release of terminal D-Ala5. This forms a D,D (4-to-3) crosslink rather than an L,D (3-to-3) crosslink; the acceptor amino group and bridge depend on the organism.

Justification: The reviewed enzyme performs the class-B PBP crosslink reaction; neither a carboxypeptidase hydrolysis term nor an L,D-transpeptidase term accurately represents this endpoint. Reaction and physiological-scope limits are stated in the core description. Round-1 QuickGO search found no exact D,D-specific peptidoglycan transpeptidase term; GO:0016755 is the verified aminoacyl-transfer parent. The shared definition does not standardize the organism-specific acceptor chemistry or upgrade inferential linkage evidence.

Parent term: aminoacyltransferase activity

Core Functions

Catalyzes class-B-PBP D,D peptide crosslinking in S. aureus peptidoglycan, whose acceptor includes the lysine-associated pentaglycine bridge, and physically activates FtsW. NCTC8325 constructs directly establish activation even with PBP1 S314A; the physiological contribution of the catalytic site is independently supported by SH1000/MRSA comparisons and transferred explicitly to this reference protein, not inferred solely from activation or antibiotic binding.
