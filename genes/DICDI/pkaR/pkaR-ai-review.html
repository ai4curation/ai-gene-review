Gene Description

pkaR encodes the single regulatory (R) subunit of cAMP-dependent protein kinase (PKA) in Dictyostelium discoideum. It binds the catalytic subunit pkaC to form the inactive PKA holoenzyme, which in this organism is an unusual R-C dimer rather than the R2C2 tetramer of most eukaryotes, because the R subunit lacks the N-terminal dimerization domain. The R subunit carries a single high-affinity cAMP-binding site (Kd in the low nanomolar range); binding of cAMP releases the active catalytic subunit. Through this cAMP-gated inhibition of pkaC, pkaR is the intracellular cAMP sensor that gates PKA activity throughout the Dictyostelium developmental program, including aggregation and cAMP relay, prespore and prestalk/stalk cell differentiation, culmination, and sporulation. Loss-of-function or inhibitor-defective mutations in the R subunit, such as the rdeC allele, cause premature heterochronic terminal differentiation by unleashing constitutive catalytic-subunit activity.

Core Functions

pkaR is the cAMP-dependent protein kinase inhibitor/regulatory subunit. It binds the PKA catalytic subunit (pkaC) to form the inactive holoenzyme and inhibits catalytic activity in a manner that is reversed by cAMP, making it the switch that keeps PKA off until cAMP levels rise.
