Gene Description

Phg2 is a large (1387 aa) TKL-family serine/threonine protein kinase of Dictyostelium discoideum. An N-terminal domain binds PI(4,5)P2 and PI(4)P and recruits the protein to the plasma membrane, a central Ras-association (RA) domain binds active Rap1-GTP, an adjacent region binds the Adrm1 homolog, and a C-terminal kinase domain lies at residues 807-1072. Phg2 acts as a Rap1 effector at the cell cortex and leading edge, where it is needed for cell-substrate adhesion, particle binding during phagocytosis, actin organization at the substrate interface, cell motility and chemotaxis; it is required for chemoattractant-induced phosphorylation and disassembly of cortical myosin II at the leading edge, apparently acting upstream of myosin heavy chain kinase. Phg2 also acts cell-autonomously as a negative regulator of nutrient-controlled initiation of multicellular development, and promotes STATc activation during hyperosmotic stress, apparently by phosphorylating and inhibiting the tyrosine phosphatase PTP3 on Ser747.

Suggested Questions for Experts

Q: Does Phg2 directly phosphorylate PTP3 Ser747 and/or myosin II heavy chain (or MHCK-A), or does it act through an intermediate kinase? Recombinant Phg2 has so far been inactive on exogenous substrates.

Q: Why do phg2-null strains in DH1 and KAx-3 backgrounds show opposite adhesion phenotypes (reduced vs increased attachment)?

  C-terminal kinase domain lies at residues 807-1072. Phg2 acts as a Rap1 effector at the cell cortex
  and leading edge, where it is needed for cell-substrate adhesion, particle binding during phagocytosis,
  actin organization at the substrate interface, cell motility and chemotaxis; it is required for chemoattractant-induced
  phosphorylation and disassembly of cortical myosin II at the leading edge, apparently acting upstream
  of myosin heavy chain kinase. Phg2 also acts cell-autonomously as a negative regulator of nutrient-controlled
  initiation of multicellular development, and promotes STATc activation during hyperosmotic stress, apparently
  by phosphorylating and inhibiting the tyrosine phosphatase PTP3 on Ser747.
