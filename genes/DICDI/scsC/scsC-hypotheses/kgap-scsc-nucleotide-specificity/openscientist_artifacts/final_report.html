scsC (Q55AI5) nucleotide specificity: can it be predicted from the established determinants?

Gene: scsC / DDB_G0271842 / Q55AI5, Dictyostelium discoideum (NCBITaxon:44689) Paralogue examined alongside: scsB / DDB_G0274449 / Q869S7 Shared alpha subunit: scsA / p36 / P36967 Hypothesis slug: kgap-scsc-nucleotide-specificity · Focus type: free text · Source: genes/DICDI/scsC/scsC-ai-review.yaml

Summary

The primary structural literature assigns nucleotide specificity in succinyl-CoA synthetase to exactly one residue, Gln20β. Fraser et al. (PMID: 16481318), working on the pig GTP-specific enzyme crystallised with GTP and magnesium (PDB 2FP4, 2.1 Å), state that "GTP is bound in the ATP-grasp domain, where interactions of the guanine base with a glutamine residue (Gln-20beta) and with backbone atoms provide the specificity." Recomputing contacts from the deposited coordinates confirms Gln20β is the only side chain in the base pocket able to hydrogen bond the guanine O6/N1/N2 edge (2.58 Å). The six other residues within 4.0 Å of the base (Val44β, Leu69β, Ala108β, Leu109β, Ile111β, Phe219β) make hydrophobic packing contacts. Arg54β, the other named residue in that paper, is assigned to the gamma-phosphate, not to base discrimination.

Reading both Dictyostelium beta subunits at those seven positions gives a split answer. scsB (Q869S7) has the guanine-reading glutamine (Gln46 in full-length numbering), and an AlphaFold model superposed on the GTP complex places its side chain 2.59 Å from the guanine edge, against 2.58 Å for pig Gln20β. scsC (Q55AI5) has proline (Pro54), which sits 5.68 Å back from the base, matching the geometry of the Thermus aquaticus Pro20β. scsC also carries Arg142 and Met253 where mammalian ATP-specific subunits differ from the GTP-specific ones. So scsB is predicted GTP-specific with moderate confidence, resting on the one residue with a mechanistic assignment, and scsC is predicted to use ATP with low-to-moderate confidence, resting on the absence of that residue plus co-varying substitutions that cannot be separated from lineage signal.

The Pro at mature position 20 is a lineage marker and a permissive state, not an established determinant. The gap treats it as informative in the direction of ATP specificity, and that inference fails on the structural evidence. Joyce et al. (PMID: 22751660) characterised T. aquaticus SCS, which carries Pro20β and prefers GDP/GTP: "A water molecule and Pro20β in T. aquaticus take the place of Gln20β in pig GTP-specific SCS, interacting well with the guanine base and other residues of the nucleotide-binding site. This leads to the preference for GDP/GTP, but does not hinder the binding of ADP/ATP." E. coli SucC has the same proline and prefers ADP/ATP. Two Pro20β enzymes, opposite preferences. Pro20β marks the absence of the guanine-reading amide, which is not the same as the presence of an adenine-reading mechanism. Scoring nine beta subunits at the seven candidate positions, six of seven sites separate the strictly specific enzymes, but none of the seven also places the two enzymes with measured preference on the side they prefer. The determinants are not established well enough to predict specificity from sequence with confidence. A mixed outcome, one ATP-type and one GTP-type beta subunit in one mitochondrion, is fully consistent with the purified enzyme using GTP.

Executive judgment

Verdict: partially supported.

The seed hypothesis has two halves and they do not fare equally.

Supported. scsB is predicted GTP-specific on the strength of Gln46, the one position with a direct structural assignment to guanine recognition, verified in three dimensions by superposition. The mixed-outcome scenario is internally consistent and matches the mammalian arrangement, where SUCLG1 alpha pairs with either SUCLA2 (ATP-specific) or SUCLG2 (GTP-specific).

Weakly supported. scsC as the ATP-specific subunit. The inference rests on the absence of Gln20β plus two second-shell substitutions (Arg142 and Met253) whose discriminating power comes from a single characterised clade.

The gap's stated reasoning is wrong and should be corrected. The Pro at mature position 20 is cited as informative. It is not a determinant. The structure that describes Pro20β with a bound nucleotide describes it in a GTP-preferring enzyme.

Most important caveats.

No crystal or cryo-EM structure of any ATP-specific succinyl-CoA synthetase with a bound adenine nucleotide exists. The RCSB entries mapping to human SUCLA2 are 6G4Q (2.59 Å, apo SUCLG1-SUCLA2) and 9SWJ (3.7 Å cryo-EM, CoA only). None map to pig SUCLA2. The "ATP-specific pattern" has therefore never been observed reading an adenine; it is defined by difference from the GTP-specific enzymes.

Lineage and residue pattern are not independent lines of evidence. Both reduce to similarity against the same small set of mammalian A-SCS sequences.

The numbering in the seed is alignment-derived. Placing scsC Pro54 at mature position 20 implies a cleaved presequence of 34 residues. UniProt Q55AI5 predicts a 17-residue transit peptide (HAMAP MF_03220, ECO:0000255), and Q869S7 has no transit peptide annotated at all. The structural equivalence is unambiguous regardless of the numbering.

Predicted specificity. scsB: GTP-specific, moderate confidence. scsC: ATP-using and probably ADP-forming, low-to-moderate confidence. Neither should be stated as fact in the review without an experimental qualifier.

Key findings

F1. Gln20β is the only residue the primary literature assigns to nucleotide specificity

Fraser et al. (PMID: 16481318) determined structures of pig GTP-specific succinyl-CoA synthetase with GTP and with GDP bound in the ATP-grasp domain of the beta subunit (PDB 2FP4, 2FPI, 2FPP, 2FPG; the GTP-Mg complex at 2.08 Å). Their assignment is explicit: specificity comes from "interactions of the guanine base with a glutamine residue (Gln-20beta) and with backbone atoms." The same paper separates the role of the other named side chain: "The gamma-phosphate interacts with the side chain of an arginine residue (Arg-54beta) and with backbone amide nitrogen atoms, leading to tight interactions between the gamma-phosphate and the protein." Arg54β binds phosphate, not the base, so it is not a discrimination determinant.

Recomputing residue-to-base distances directly from the deposited 2FP4 coordinates (chain B, GTP 403; base atoms N1, C2, N2, N3, C4, C5, C6, O6, N7, C8, N9) returns seven residues within 4.0 Å:

Contacts to the whole GTP additionally include the P-loop Gly52β-Gly53β-Arg54β-Gly55β, Val67β, Glu114β, Asp220β and Lys222β. Gln20β is the only one of the seven base-contacting residues whose side chain can read the hydrogen-bonding pattern that distinguishes guanine from adenine. The rest define a pocket shape.

The asymmetry of the evidence matters for curation. The determinant is assigned from the GTP-bound side only. Searching RCSB for structures mapped to human SUCLA2 (Q9P2R7) returns 6G4Q, an apo SUCLG1-SUCLA2 heterodimer at 2.59 Å, and 9SWJ, a 3.7 Å cryo-EM structure with CoA only. No structure maps to pig SUCLA2 (O97580). The only nucleotide-bound structure from an ADP/ATP-preferring enzyme is E. coli 1CQI (3.3 Å, ADP-Mg), and that enzyme is dual-specificity.

F2. Pro20β is permissive, not an ATP-specificity determinant

This is the finding that directly answers the question the seed asks to be stated explicitly.

Joyce et al. (PMID: 22751660) characterised T. aquaticus SCS biochemically and structurally. Their activity assays show that "the enzyme uses either ADP/ATP or GDP/GTP, but prefers GDP/GTP. This contrasts with Escherichia coli SCS, which uses GDP/GTP but prefers ADP/ATP." Their structure explains how: "A water molecule and Pro20β in T. aquaticus take the place of Gln20β in pig GTP-specific SCS, interacting well with the guanine base and other residues of the nucleotide-binding site. This leads to the preference for GDP/GTP, but does not hinder the binding of ADP/ATP."

Recomputing from the deposited coordinates of PDB 3UFX (GDP-Mn, four copies in the asymmetric unit, chains B/E/G/I) gives Pro20β minimum distances to the guanine base of 6.15, 5.56, 5.77 and 5.59 Å, with no direct contact, and ordered water molecules hydrogen bonding the base at 2.63/2.74, 2.93/3.18/2.63, 2.61/2.31 and 2.45/3.01 Å. The direct guanine contacts in 3UFX are Val43β, Ala93β, Val94β and Ile96β, equivalent to pig Val44β, Ala108β, Leu109β and Ile111β. The pocket is intact; the glutamine is replaced by a water.

For the opposite preference, recomputation of PDB 1CQI (E. coli SCS with ADP-Mg, two copies, PMID: 9917402) shows the adenine contacted by Val44β, Lys46β, Glu99β, Ala100β, Ala101β, Thr102β, Ile104β and Leu212β, with Pro20β not within 4.0 Å (4.22 ± 0.15 Å across the two copies).

Residue 20β is Pro in every measured ADP/ATP user examined (pig and human SUCLA2, mouse Sucla2, S. cerevisiae Lsc2, E. coli SucC) and also in the GTP-preferring T. aquaticus enzyme. It is Gln in every strictly GTP-specific one (pig, human, mouse SUCLG2). The residue is informative in one direction only: Gln20β predicts guanine recognition, Pro20β predicts the loss of that particular contact and nothing more.

The scsB Gln46 side chain sits 2.59 Å from the polar guanine edge atoms O6/N1/N2/N7, within hydrogen-bonding range and within 0.01 Å of the pig measurement. The scsC Pro54 sits 5.68 Å back, in the same position as the water-substituted T. aquaticus Pro20β (5.56-6.15 Å).

One substitution in scsC deserves attention. Arg142, equivalent to pig Ala108β, projects to 3.82 Å from the base. Arginine at 108β is present in pig, human and mouse A-SCS, in yeast Lsc2 and in scsC, and absent from every GTP-specific or dual-specificity enzyme examined. It is the only candidate that could make the scsC prediction mechanistic. No primary paper assigns specificity to it.

F5. A mixed outcome is consistent with the GTP-utilising purified enzyme

The arrangement the seed proposes for Dictyostelium, a single alpha subunit partnering two beta subunits of different specificity, is the arrangement established in mammals. The Dictyostelium biochemistry matches a two-beta system. In crude preparations the alpha subunit p36 is phosphorylated by both nucleotides, and the ATP activity is the one lost on purification (PMID: 8448193): "In crude sample preparations, p36 could be phosphorylated by both ATP or GTP and in either case, its phosphorylation was stimulated by low concentrations of GDP. Partially purified p36 retained its ability to be phosphorylated with GTP while exhibiting little or no phosphorylation with ATP." An independent study of aggregation-stage mitochondrial fractions reports the same dual donor use (PMID: 8384004): "p38 can be phosphorylated in vitro using either ATP or GTP as phosphoryl donors."

The recorded purified-enzyme evidence (PMID: 7771809) is a statement about a preparation: "We have previously shown that the enzyme purified from growing D. discoideum is a GTP-utilizing enzyme." It constrains which holoenzyme survives purification from growing cells, not which beta subunits the genome encodes. Both loci exist as separate genes, Q55AI5/DDB_G0271842 and Q869S7/DDB_G0274449.

F6. Both beta paralogues are abundant in growing cells, so expression does not explain the GTP-only result

EBI Expression Atlas baseline experiment E-MTAB-7215 (RNA-seq, 13,731 genes, four cell types, 3 replicates per group) gives median TPM in vegetative (growing) cells of 590 for scsA, 313 for scsB and 236 for scsC. All three rank in the top 4% of expressed genes (percentile ranks 98.2, 97.1, 96.1), and the scsB/scsC ratio is 1.33. The alpha subunit TPM (590) is close to the sum of the two betas (549). The ratio shifts through development: 7.75 in spore cells, 2.67 in cup cells, 0.64 in stalk cells.

Growth-stage silencing of the ATP-type subunit does not explain the GTP-utilising purified enzyme. The remaining explanations are selective loss of the second holoenzyme during purification, which is exactly the behaviour reported in PMID: 8448193, or scsC not being ATP-specific. The developmental divergence of the two paralogues indicates the duplication is functionally maintained rather than a redundant copy. mRNA abundance is not protein abundance, and this is a single dataset. dictyExpress could not be reached (DNS resolution failure for dictyexpress.research.bcm.edu) and dictyBase serves a JavaScript application rather than a queryable REST endpoint, so no second expression dataset was obtained.

F7. No candidate position separates the beta subunits once measured preference is scored

Scoring the seven guanine-contacting positions against the nine beta subunits with measured or orthology-assigned nucleotide use gives a specific negative result. Six of seven sites (20β, 69β, 108β, 109β, 111β, 219β) separate the strictly GTP-specific set from the strictly ATP/ADP-using set. None of the seven also places the two dual-specificity enzymes on the side of their measured preference. At 20β the GTP-specific set is {Q} and the ATP/ADP set is {P}, yet T. aquaticus, which prefers GDP/GTP, has P. At 108β the sets are {A} and {R}, yet both dual enzymes have A regardless of their preference.

Counting independent evolutionary origins in the evidence set: the strictly GTP-specific enzymes come from one origin (animal G-SCS, three near-identical sequences) and the ATP/ADP users from two (animal A-SCS and fungal single-copy Lsc2). The apparent discriminating power of the residue set comes almost entirely from one contrast, three near-identical animal G-SCS sequences against three near-identical animal A-SCS sequences.

Applying the per-site rule to Dictyostelium gives mixed scores for both subunits. scsC scores ATP-side at 20β, 108β and 219β, GTP-specific at 44β and 69β, and neither at 109β and 111β. scsB scores GTP-specific at 20β, 44β and 69β, ATP-side at 109β, 111β and 219β, and neither at 108β.

This is the direct answer to the last clause of the seed. The determinants are not established well enough to predict nucleotide specificity from sequence with confidence. Only Gln20β carries a mechanistic assignment from a structure, and it predicts in one direction.

F8. The A-beta/G-beta duplication is placed in animals, so applying those labels to Dictyostelium is an extrapolation

Johnson et al. (PMID: 9765291) determined the mature pigeon ATP-specific and GTP-specific beta subunit sequences and completed the mouse, human and pig counterparts. They report that "the sequences of the mature ATP- and GTP-specific beta-subunits (A-beta and G-beta, respectively) in pigeon are 54% identical" and conclude that "alignment with two sequences of the beta-subunit in Caenorhabditis elegans suggests that the A-beta and G-beta genes arose by duplication early in the evolution of multicellular eucaryotes."

Dictyostelium discoideum is an amoebozoan outside that clade, and S. cerevisiae carries a single beta subunit (Lsc2) rather than an A/G pair. The 54% figure for the pigeon A-beta/G-beta pair is the same range as scsC (54.5%) and pig SUCLA2 (54.7%) against the pig G-SCS structure, so percent identity alone does not place the Dictyostelium paralogues in either family. The seed instructs that the phylogeny should not be re-derived, so this was not tested here. The point for curation is that lineage membership and residue pattern are not independent evidence, because both trace back to similarity with the same mammalian A-SCS sequences.

Mechanistic model

The activity under test is the immediate molecular function of a single subunit. The beta subunit supplies the ATP-grasp nucleotide site that binds the NDP/NTP and is phosphorylated in the second half-reaction. The catalytic histidine then shuttles the phosphoryl group roughly 29 Å between the two active sites (PMID: 33645539). Nucleotide specificity is a property of the base pocket in that site.

The dashed arrows mark the uncertain links. scsB's path to a GDP-forming assignment runs through a residue with a direct structural assignment and a verified 3D position. scsC's path forks, because the Pro20β state is compatible with both outcomes in the characterised enzymes.

The biochemical observations fit the model without strain. Both beta transcripts are abundant in growing cells at a 1.33:1 ratio, so both holoenzymes should be present. Crude extracts phosphorylate the alpha subunit with either ATP or GTP (PMID: 8448193, PMID: 8384004). Partial purification removes the ATP-dependent activity and retains the GTP-dependent one, which is what a labile or lower-abundance second holoenzyme would produce. The purified GTP-utilising enzyme (PMID: 7771809) is then a property of the surviving species.

Separate from that, and not evidence for it: the developmental regulation of SCS transcripts and enzyme activity (PMID: 7771809), the GDP-stimulated allosteric behaviour of the phosphoenzyme (PMID: 8240297, PMID: 8060491), the chemoattractant-induced change in alpha-subunit phosphorylation (PMID: 8384004), and the interaction of mammalian SCS-betaA with ALAS-E (PMID: 10727444). None of these bear on which nucleotide scsC uses.

Conflicts and alternatives

The gap's own reasoning conflicts with the structural literature. Pro20β is treated as informative, but the paper that describes Pro20β with a bound nucleotide describes it in a GTP-preferring enzyme (PMID: 22751660). The conclusion about scsC can survive, the premise cannot.

Lineage and residue pattern are not independent. PMID: 9765291 places the A-β/G-β duplication within multicellular eukaryotes, and the Dictyostelium duplication may be a separate event. Citing both lines overstates the support.

Differential expression does not rescue the purified-enzyme result. Both beta transcripts are abundant in vegetative cells at a 1.33:1 ratio, so the GTP-utilising enzyme from growing cells is not explained by scsC being switched off. The remaining explanations are selective loss of the scsC holoenzyme during purification, exactly the behaviour reported in PMID: 8448193, or scsC not being ATP-specific.

"Specific" may be the wrong frame outside animals. Both characterised bacterial enzymes use both nucleotides and differ only in preference. A dual-specificity Dictyostelium enzyme with a preference would satisfy every observation reported so far, and would make both the GO:0004775 on scsC and the NOT GO:0004775 on scsB partly wrong.

Database carry-over. The scsC mitochondrion annotation is ISS against human SUCLA2 and the scsB one is ISS against mouse Suclg2, so the A/G assignment is encoded in the CC evidence as well as the MF evidence. These are not independent.

Alpha-subunit evidence cannot be transferred. The Pseudomonas work (PMID: 8016083) reports that the 33 kDa alpha subunit "appears to undergo autophosphorylation in the presence of either ATP or GTP, although the presence of small amounts of Ndk activity may influence the level of such phosphorylation." Contaminating nucleoside-diphosphate kinase is a known confounder for phosphoenzyme assays, which bears on how much weight the Dictyostelium dual-donor observations can carry.

Limitations and knowledge gaps

No ATP-bound structure of an ATP-specific SCS. Checked: RCSB UniProt-mapped searches for Q9P2R7 and O97580. 6G4Q is apo and 9SWJ has only CoA. This matters because the ATP-specific pattern is defined entirely by difference and has never been observed reading an adenine. Resolved by a SUCLG1-SUCLA2 structure with ADP or ATP bound.

No kinetics for either Dictyostelium beta subunit. Checked: PubMed for Dictyostelium SCS returns only phosphoenzyme assays on the alpha subunit. Every annotation on both beta subunits is IBA or IEA. Resolved by recombinant co-expression and kcat/Km measurement.

The mature N-terminus of scsC is not experimentally mapped. UniProt predicts a 17-residue transit peptide for Q55AI5 (HAMAP MF_03220, ECO:0000255) and none for Q869S7, but placing Pro54 at mature position 20 requires a 34-residue presequence. This matters because the gap quotes a mature-numbering position. The structural equivalence is unambiguous regardless. Resolved by N-terminal sequencing or mitochondrial N-terminome data.

Whether Arg108β is a real second determinant. Checked: present in pig, human and mouse A-SCS, in yeast Lsc2 and in scsC; absent from every GTP-specific or dual enzyme. Not assigned by any primary paper. It is the only candidate that could make the scsC prediction mechanistic rather than correlative. Resolved by an A108R mutation in the pig GTP-specific enzyme.

Whether the Dictyostelium paralogues descend from the animal duplication. Not tested here, because the seed forbids re-deriving the phylogeny. It matters because the lineage claim is used as independent support. Resolved by a beta-subunit tree with broad amoebozoan and opisthokont sampling, with branch support values.

Protein-level abundance in growing cells is unknown. Only mRNA was obtained (E-MTAB-7215). dictyExpress was unreachable (DNS resolution failure) and dictyBase serves no queryable REST endpoint. Resolved by quantitative proteomics of vegetative-cell mitochondria.

The two Dictyostelium structural predictions rest on AlphaFold models, not experimental structures, and no nucleotide was docked. The superposition measures where a residue sits, not whether it binds. The comparative analysis covers nine beta subunits, of which five have directly measured nucleotide use, spanning three independent evolutionary origins.

Proposed follow-up experiments and actions

Co-express scsA with scsB and with scsC separately in E. coli and measure ADP and GDP kinetics. This single experiment settles the hypothesis and converts every IBA on both beta subunits into an IDA. It also tests whether either enzyme is strictly specific or merely preferential, which is the outcome the current GO terms cannot express.

Swap residue 20β in both directions. scsB Q46P and scsC P54Q in the recombinant holoenzymes. If Gln20β is the determinant in this organism, the swap should move the preference. This is the only test that makes the sequence-based prediction falsifiable.

Mutate Ala108β to Arg in pig GTP-specific SCS and measure the change in GDP/ADP preference. This would promote or eliminate Arg108β as a second determinant and decide how much weight scsC's Arg142 can carry.

Knock out scsB and scsC separately and assay nucleotide-dependent phosphoenzyme formation in mitochondrial fractions. The prediction is that the scsB knockout loses GTP-dependent and retains ATP-dependent phosphorylation. Dictyostelium gene disruption is routine, and this uses the existing assay from PMID: 8448193 unchanged.

Quantitative proteomics of vegetative-cell mitochondria to test whether scsC protein is present at the level its mRNA implies, which would locate the purification artefact.

Crystallise or cryo-image human SUCLG1-SUCLA2 with ADP or ATP bound. This is the missing reciprocal structure, and it would turn the ATP-specific pattern from a sequence difference into a recognition mechanism.
