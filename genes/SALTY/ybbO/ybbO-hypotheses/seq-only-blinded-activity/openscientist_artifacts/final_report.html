AIGR Gene Hypothesis Deep Research — Final Report

Blinded Sequence-Only Functional Assignment: Salmonella Typhimurium LT2 ybbO (Q7CR11)

Focus type: function_assignment · Hypothesis slug: seq-only-blinded-activity Term under evaluation: alcohol dehydrogenase (NADP+) activity — GO:0008106 Organism: Salmonella enterica subsp. enterica serovar Typhimurium str. LT2 (NCBITaxon:99287)

Executive Judgment

Verdict: SUPPORTED (as an evidence-transfer / ISS–ISO curation lead).

Starting only from the 256-amino-acid query sequence with gene name and accession deliberately withheld, this investigation established the protein's identity, family, catalytic integrity, and most likely specific activity, and tested the seed hypothesis against competing activities (hydroxysteroid dehydrogenase, retinol dehydrogenase).

The blinded query was de-anonymized by an exact UniParc CRC64 checksum match to Salmonella Typhimurium LT2 gene ybbO (UniProt Q7CR11). The protein is a textbook classical short-chain dehydrogenase/reductase (SDR): Pfam PF00106, InterPro IPR002347, PROSITE PS00061, with the Rossmann-fold NAD(P)-binding superfamily signature. Its closest experimentally characterized relative is Escherichia coli K-12 YbbO (P0AFP4), to which it is 90.6% identical over 256 aa with 100% conservation of the catalytic tetrad and the NADP(+)-selecting basic residues. E. coli YbbO carries exactly the seed GO term — GO:0008106 "alcohol dehydrogenase (NADP+) activity" — supported by two independent direct-assay (IDA) primary papers (PMID: 25108218 and PMID: 27134112).

The seed hypothesis — "NADPH-dependent aldehyde reductase that reduces aliphatic aldehydes to primary alcohols" — is therefore supported with high confidence and can be narrowed precisely: the preferred substrate class is medium-to-long-chain aliphatic aldehydes (C6–C18, optimal C16/C18). The two competing activities were evaluated and resolved: the hydroxysteroid dehydrogenase (HSD) alternative is refuted — it is a CDD subfamily-naming artifact (cd05374), not substrate evidence — and the retinol dehydrogenase activity is real but promiscuous and non-native, since neither Salmonella nor E. coli has an endogenous retinoid pathway. Most important caveat: the Salmonella ortholog itself has not been directly assayed; all enzymatic evidence comes from the E. coli ortholog. The correct curation action is therefore an ISS/ISO evidence transfer, not an assertion of direct assay (IDA) on Q7CR11.

Key Findings

F001 — Identity: the blinded query is Salmonella LT2 YbbO (Q7CR11), 91% identical to assay-characterized E. coli YbbO

The 256-residue query sequence was identified by an exact UniParc CRC64 checksum match (checksum 22B5C5A48E6D813A → UPI000005A1DF), which uniquely maps the sequence to Salmonella Typhimurium LT2 gene ybbO (UniProtKB Q7CR11). This is a deterministic identity match, not a similarity inference.

The nearest reviewed, experimentally characterized homolog is E. coli K-12 YbbO (P0AFP4, 269 aa), which carries a 13-residue N-terminal extension relative to the query. After accounting for that offset, the ungapped identity is 90.6% over 256 aa. The cofactor-binding glycine-rich loop and catalytic region are visibly conserved (query LITGCSSGIGL…LAGCRKPDD vs E. coli LITGCSSGIGL…LAGCRKPDD). E. coli YbbO is annotated GO:0008106 "alcohol dehydrogenase (NADP+) activity" with evidence code IDA:EcoCyc (direct assay). Because the deletion of ybbO in E. coli caused a >90% reduction in long-chain fatty alcohol production (PMID: 27134112), the ortholog's role as an NADP(+)-dependent aldehyde reductase is directly established, and the query is its very close homolog.

F002 — Specific activity: broad-specificity NADPH aldehyde reductase (aliphatic C6–C18, optimal C16/C18); retinal reduction is promiscuous; no hydroxysteroid activity

Enzyme kinetics of E. coli YbbO show a wide aldehyde substrate specificity ranging from C6 to C18, with maximum affinity and catalytic efficiency for C18 and C16 chain-length aldehydes (PMID: 27134112). An independent functional screen identified YbbO among five new aldehyde reductases (ALRs) — YahK, DkgA, GldA, YbbO, and YghA — that convert aldehydes to the corresponding alcohols (PMID: 25108218). These two lines of direct evidence converge on aliphatic aldehydes as the preferred substrate class, precisely matching the seed hypothesis once narrowed to medium/long-chain aliphatic aldehydes.

Several metabolic-engineering studies show that ybbO also reduces retinal to retinol (PMID: 25726762, PMID: 34850377, PMID: 39307776, PMID: 41093029). However, in those systems retinal remains a major product (PMID: 34850377), indicating retinal is an inefficient, promiscuous, non-native substrate rather than the physiological one. Neither Salmonella nor E. coli possesses an endogenous retinoid biosynthetic pathway, so retinol dehydrogenase cannot be the gene's native function. There is no sequence or literature evidence for hydroxysteroid or dedicated retinol dehydrogenase classification.

F003 — Annotation provenance: two independent IDA papers underpin GO:0008106; the Salmonella ortholog lacks it; InterPro = classical SDR; the HSD label is a CDD artifact

QuickGO shows that E. coli YbbO (P0AFP4) is annotated GO:0008106 "alcohol dehydrogenase (NADP+) activity" with evidence IDA (ECO:0000314) from two primary references: PMID: 25108218 and PMID: 27134112. By contrast, the Salmonella ortholog Q7CR11 currently carries only GO:0016491 (oxidoreductase, evidence IBA ECO:0000318, PANTHER PTN000672121) — confirming the seed term has not yet been propagated to the query protein.

Mechanistic Scope

The immediate molecular function being tested is a catalytic, NADPH-dependent hydride transfer that reduces the carbonyl of an aliphatic aldehyde to a primary alcohol. This is a direct gene-product activity grounded in: (1) complete conservation of the SDR catalytic tetrad and NADP-selecting residues in the query, and (2) direct in vitro kinetics plus a loss-of-function metabolic phenotype on the near-identical E. coli ortholog.

The following are explicitly downstream, pleiotropic, or context-specific and must not be conflated with the core MF: - Fatty alcohol production (a pathway consequence observed when YbbO acts on fatty aldehyde intermediates in engineered strains). - Retinol production (a non-native, promiscuous reduction exploited only in engineered β-carotene/retinoid strains; absent any native retinoid pathway). - Any biocide-tolerance phenotypes (e.g., glutaraldehyde tolerance) are organism/stress-response phenomena and are not evidence that YbbO's core function is glutaraldehyde reduction.

The seed hypothesis correctly targets the direct molecular activity, and the evidence supports it at the activity level while allowing precise substrate narrowing.

Conflicts and Alternatives

  1. Retinol dehydrogenase (competing activity). Multiple engineering papers report ybbO-mediated retinal → retinol conversion, and one screen even ranked ybbO highest among tested alcohol dehydrogenases for this reaction (95.6% conversion; PMID: 39307776). This is genuine catalytic promiscuity, not evidence of native function: retinal remains a major product in these systems (PMID: 34850377), and the host bacteria lack any endogenous retinoid pathway. Resolution: retinol reduction is a non-core, in-vitro/heterologous activity; do not replace GO:0008106 with a retinol dehydrogenase term.

  2. Hydroxysteroid dehydrogenase (competing activity). The CDD domain hit cd05374 is labeled "17β-hydroxysteroid dehydrogenase-like, classical (c) SDRs." This is a broad cluster named after a representative, not a substrate determination. Steroid specificity in true HSDs depends on dedicated substrate-pocket residues (e.g., His221 in human 17β-HSD1; PMID: 9525918), for which there is no evidence in YbbO. Resolution: refute the HSD alternative as a database-naming artifact.

  3. Paralog / family over-annotation risk. Classical SDRs are a large family and functional transfer across distant SDRs is dangerous. Here, however, the transfer is not generic family inference — it is from a 90.6%-identical ortholog with 100% catalytic conservation and two IDA papers. This is a best-case scenario for ISS/ISO transfer, which mitigates (but does not eliminate) the usual over-annotation concern.

  4. Organism-specific difference. The Salmonella protein has not been assayed; a formal possibility of divergent substrate preference remains, though near-identity makes substantive functional divergence unlikely.

Discriminating Tests

The following would most efficiently distinguish the supported hypothesis from its alternatives:

  1. In vitro kinetic substrate panel on purified Salmonella YbbO (Q7CR11). Test a ladder of aliphatic aldehydes (C2–C18) ± NADPH/NADH. Predicted outcome if hypothesis holds: NADPH-dependent reduction with maximal efficiency near C16/C18, mirroring the E. coli ortholog. Discriminates: confirms aliphatic-aldehyde reductase and NADPH preference; upgrades evidence to IDA.
  2. Steroid-substrate counter-assay. Test estradiol/androstenedione and other hydroxysteroids. Predicted outcome: negligible activity. Discriminates: falsifies the HSD alternative directly.
  3. Retinal vs aliphatic-aldehyde competition kinetics. Compare catalytic efficiency (kcat/Km) for retinal vs C16 aldehyde. Predicted outcome: aliphatic aldehyde ≫ retinal. Discriminates: confirms retinol reduction is promiscuous, not core.
  4. ΔybbO Salmonella phenotyping + metabolomics. Assess aldehyde accumulation and lipid-derived alcohol pools under stress. Discriminates: establishes native BP (detoxification vs biosynthetic).
  5. Structure/docking. AlphaFold model + cofactor and substrate docking to verify the Asn–Ser–Tyr–Lys tetrad geometry and the NADP 2′-phosphate pocket. Discriminates: structural support for ISS transfer.
