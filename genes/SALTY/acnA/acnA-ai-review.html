Gene Description

Aconitate hydratase A (aconitase A, AcnA) of Salmonella enterica serovar Typhimurium, a cytoplasmic [4Fe-4S] cluster-containing member of the aconitase/IRP family. AcnA catalyses the reversible isomerisation of citrate to isocitrate via cis-aconitate in the tricarboxylic acid cycle, and also hydrates 2-methyl-cis-aconitate to (2R,3S)-2-methylisocitrate, the step of the 2-methylcitric acid cycle of propionate catabolism that the operon-encoded PrpD dehydratase cannot perform. Purified AcnA, together with PrpE, PrpC, PrpD and PrpB, reconstitutes conversion of propionate to pyruvate in vitro. AcnA is functionally redundant with aconitase B (AcnB): an acnA mutant grows normally on propionate and other carbon sources, whereas an acnA acnB double mutant cannot grow on propionate. By analogy with Escherichia coli AcnA, the cluster-free apo-enzyme is expected to act as a post-transcriptional regulator that binds the 3' untranslated regions of aconitase mRNAs.

Suggested Questions for Experts

Q: Does Salmonella apo-AcnA bind the 3'UTRs of acnA/acnB mRNAs and regulate their stability, as shown for E. coli AcnA, and does this matter under oxidative or iron stress during infection?

Q: What is the relative contribution of AcnA versus AcnB to 2-methylisocitrate formation in vivo, given that AcnA is stress-induced and AcnB is the main housekeeping aconitase?

Suggested Experiments

Experiment: Measure kinetic parameters of purified AcnA and AcnB for citrate, cis-aconitate and 2-methyl-cis-aconitate, and quantify methylcitrate-cycle flux in acnA and acnB single mutants grown on propionate.

Experiment: Test RNA binding of Salmonella apo-AcnA to acnA/acnB 3'UTRs by gel retardation and assess mRNA stability in cluster-ligand cysteine mutants.
