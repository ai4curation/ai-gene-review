Gene Description

maiA encodes maleylacetoacetate isomerase (MaiA, EC 5.2.1.2) of Aspergillus nidulans, a 230-residue zeta-class glutathione S-transferase family enzyme that uses glutathione as a cofactor to isomerize 4-maleylacetoacetate (cis) to 4-fumarylacetoacetate (trans). It catalyses the middle step of the three-enzyme homogentisate branch of phenylalanine and phenylacetate catabolism, between homogentisate dioxygenase (HmgA) and fumarylacetoacetase (FahA), whose genes are clustered with maiA. maiA is induced by phenylalanine or phenylacetate; disruption abolishes MAAI activity, prevents growth on these carbon sources, causes phenylalanine toxicity and leads to secretion of 2,4-dioxohept-2-enoic acid isomers rather than succinylacetone. MaiA was the first MAAI gene characterized in any organism and led to identification of human GSTZ1 as the MAAI. It is predicted to be cytoplasmic.

GO_REF:0000033 		MARK AS OVER ANNOTATED
Summary: Mitochondrion (is_active_in) inferred at PAINT node PTN000137159 from mammalian GSTZ1 (human, mouse, rat) data.
Reason: Mitochondrial localization of zeta-class GST has been reported only for mammalian GSTZ1, and these mammalian proteins are the only sources of the IBD. In mammals the mitochondrial matrix pool is a minor fraction alongside the predominant cytosolic enzyme, and it is the cytosolic pool that carries out homogentisate-pathway isomerization. I found no localization data for A. nidulans MaiA or other fungal MAAIs, UniProt places it in the cytoplasm by similarity, and the protein has no predicted targeting features. Extending a minor, mammal-documented mitochondrial pool to the fungal enzyme is therefore more than the evidence supports; the row is kept as a likely over-annotation rather than removed, because a mitochondrial fraction has not been tested and excluded.

Q: Does MaiA have measurable glutathione-conjugating or dichloroacetate-dehalogenating activity like animal zeta-class GSTs, or is it a dedicated isomerase?
