# Functional annotation report: *Aspergillus nidulans nudE* (UniProt O74689)

## Executive conclusion

The requested target is correctly identified as **nudE/AN6125 from *Emericella nidulans* (= *Aspergillus nidulans*) strain FGSC A4**, encoding the fungal **nuclear-distribution protein NUDE**. The gene-specific literature describes a 586-residue protein homologous to *Neurospora crassa* RO11, with a conserved N-terminal coiled-coil corresponding to the supplied NUDE-family/domain annotation. This is not *A. nidulans nudF* (the LIS1 homolog), *nudC*, or the mammalian paralogs NDE1/NDEL1. Those proteins are discussed only where they illuminate NUDE’s pathway position. (efimov2000thelis1relatednudf pages 6-6, efimov2000thelis1relatednudf pages 5-6, efimov2000thelis1relatednudf pages 2-2)

The best-supported primary annotation is: **a nonenzymatic, coiled-coil dynein-pathway regulator that binds NUDF/LIS1 and facilitates its function in microtubule-dependent nuclear migration and distribution**. NUDE is therefore an accessory scaffold/regulatory factor, not a motor, enzyme, or transporter. Its deletion is viable but disrupts nuclear positioning, colony growth, and conidiation. Full-length NUDE occurs at motile, microtubule-end-associated “comets,” although localization experiments show that these comets are not themselves required for all NUDE activity. (efimov2000thelis1relatednudf pages 4-5, efimov2003rolesofnude pages 13-14, efimov2003rolesofnude pages 1-2, efimov2000thelis1relatednudf pages 6-6)

## 1. Identity verification and ambiguity control

### Verified match

The literature’s *A. nidulans nudE* matches the supplied protein description in all critical respects:

- organism: filamentous ascomycete *Aspergillus nidulans*;
- product: nuclear-distribution protein NUDE;
- family relationship: homolog of the fungal nuclear-migration protein RO11;
- architecture: a conserved N-terminal NUDE/coiled-coil region followed by a less-conserved C-terminal region;
- pathway: NUDF/LIS1–cytoplasmic-dynein nuclear-distribution pathway. (efimov2000thelis1relatednudf pages 6-6, efimov2000thelis1relatednudf pages 1-2, efimov2000thelis1relatednudf pages 2-2)

The cloned protein was reported as **586 amino acids**. Its N-terminal approximately 190 residues are strongly conserved and predicted to form a continuous coiled coil; one estimate placed the coiled coil at about 170 residues or 25 nm. NUDF binding was mapped more narrowly to NUDE residues **25–183**. These findings align with the supplied InterPro NUDE/NUDE_dom and Pfam NUDE_C assignments, although exact database-domain boundaries need not coincide with the experimental truncations. (efimov2000thelis1relatednudf pages 6-6, efimov2000thelis1relatednudf pages 4-5, efimov2000thelis1relatednudf pages 5-6, efimov2000thelis1relatednudf pages 2-2)

### Symbols that must not be conflated

- **nudE/NUDE/O74689/AN6125:** the protein evaluated here.
- **nudF/NUDF:** the *A. nidulans* LIS1 homolog and direct NUDE-binding partner.
- **nudA/NUDA:** cytoplasmic-dynein heavy chain.
- **nudC/NUDC:** a separate nuclear-distribution protein.
- **NDE1/NDEL1:** animal homologs/paralogs that provide evolutionary context but are not O74689.

## 2. Primary molecular function

### NUDE is a scaffold/regulator, not an enzyme

No catalytic activity, chemical reaction, or small-molecule substrate has been demonstrated or is expected from its coiled-coil architecture. The relevant “substrate specificity” question is therefore inapplicable. NUDE’s primary molecular function is protein–protein interaction within the cytoplasmic-dynein regulatory apparatus.

The strongest direct biochemical result is binding to **NUDF**, the fungal LIS1 homolog. NUDE–NUDF interaction was detected by yeast two-hybrid analysis and independently confirmed by coprecipitation of NUDF with epitope-tagged NUDE from *A. nidulans* extracts. The two-hybrid interaction was weak and detected with the more sensitive reporter, but the fungal co-immunoprecipitation provides orthogonal evidence for association in vivo. (efimov2000thelis1relatednudf pages 6-6, efimov2000thelis1relatednudf pages 5-6, efimov2000thelis1relatednudf pages 1-2)

The interaction surface lies in the N-terminal coiled coil. A construct containing the coiled-coil region could complement Δ*nudE* and suppress *nudF7* when overexpressed, whereas an expressed internal deletion removing residues 45–214 was nonfunctional. Conversely, the C-terminal region was dispensable in the overexpression assay. Thus, the N-terminal NUDE domain is both the experimentally mapped NUDF-binding site and the core functional module. (efimov2000thelis1relatednudf pages 4-5, efimov2000thelis1relatednudf pages 5-6)

### Position in the dynein pathway

NUDF binds the *A. nidulans* dynein heavy chain, placing the direct NUDE–NUDF interaction upstream of, or alongside, dynein regulation. Genetic pathway ordering is especially informative: increased NUDF production completely suppresses Δ*nudE*, leading the original investigators to conclude that NUDE has a supportive or secondary role relative to NUDF. NUDE likely raises the effective local concentration, stability, localization, or productive engagement of NUDF/LIS1 with the dynein machinery. However, the experiments do **not** resolve which of these mechanisms is primary. (efimov2003rolesofnude pages 13-14, efimov2003rolesofnude pages 1-2)

A subsequent cited biochemical study reported binding of NUDE and NUDF to specific dynein/dynactin subunits and tubulins. Nevertheless, the clearest directly inspected evidence remains the NUDE–NUDF interaction, and neither affinity, stoichiometry, nor a complete native complex structure has been established for O74689. (efimov2003rolesofnude pages 17-18)

## 3. Cellular localization and site of action

Full-length NUDE-GFP forms **comet-like structures moving toward hyphal tips**, similar to NUDF-GFP and the plus-end accumulations of dynein-pathway proteins. This supports a cytoplasmic site of action associated with dynamic microtubule ends in elongating hyphae, rather than localization inside the nucleus or secretion outside the cell. (efimov2000thelis1relatednudf pages 6-6)

Important qualifications follow from domain and deletion experiments:

1. The isolated, functional N-terminal coiled-coil did **not** form visible comets.
2. The nonfunctional C-terminal region could form comets.
3. Deleting *nudE* did not abolish NUDF-GFP or dynein-heavy-chain GFP comets, although it changed dynein behavior.

Therefore, full-length NUDE can occupy microtubule-end-associated structures, but comet localization is neither sufficient nor strictly required for the experimentally demonstrated activity of its N-terminal module. NUDE should not be annotated simply as a constitutive microtubule-plus-end anchor. (efimov2003rolesofnude pages 13-14, efimov2003rolesofnude pages 1-2)

Other work has proposed that NUDE and the CLIP-170 homolog CLIPA have overlapping roles in NUDF localization, including at spindle-pole-associated sites. This supports a broader localization/scaffolding function, but evidence that fungal NUDE specifically recruits NUDF onto dynein or spindle pole bodies is less direct than the evidence for physical NUDE–NUDF binding. (zhang2018theactincapping pages 13-13, helmstaedt2008thenuclearmigration pages 9-10)

## 4. Biological process and phenotype

### Nuclear migration and distribution

The defining biological role is microtubule/dynein-dependent **nuclear migration and spacing in multinucleate hyphae**. Δ*nudE* is viable, showing that NUDE is not essential under the tested laboratory conditions, but deletion impairs nuclear distribution, colony growth, and conidiation. Its phenotype is milder than loss of NUDF or dynein heavy chain, consistent with NUDE being an important facilitator rather than the force-producing motor. (efimov2000thelis1relatednudf pages 4-5)

In germlings scored by DAPI staining:

- Δ*nudE*: **21% failed migration, 67% intermediate, 12% wild type**;
- Δ*nudF*: **64% failed, 36% intermediate, 0% wild type**.

These data show both substantial penetrance of the Δ*nudE* defect and residual pathway activity without NUDE. (efimov2000thelis1relatednudf pages 4-5)

### Genetic interaction with NUDF/LIS1

*nudE* was discovered as a multicopy suppressor of temperature-sensitive *nudF7*. Extra *nudE* copies changed the *nudF7* nuclear-distribution categories from **52% to 16% failed migration** and from **5% to 28% wild type**, without increasing the amount of NUDF protein. The result therefore favors improved NUDF function or complex formation rather than transcriptional/protein-abundance rescue. (efimov2000thelis1relatednudf pages 4-5)

The initial suppressor screen examined more than **3 × 10⁴ transformants**, identified 79 putative suppressors, and ultimately recovered two overlapping plasmids containing *nudE*. Extra *nudE* also partially suppressed a *nudA1* dynein-heavy-chain phenotype under mildly restrictive conditions, reinforcing pathway membership. (efimov2000thelis1relatednudf pages 2-2, efimov2000thelis1relatednudf pages 2-4)

### Growth and development

Reduced colony growth and conidiation are best interpreted as downstream consequences of defective nuclear distribution in a polarized multinucleate fungus, rather than evidence that NUDE directly regulates developmental signaling. In one double-mutant analysis at 37°C, *apsA5 nudE* grew at **6.7 mm/day**, compared with **3.2 mm/day** for *nudF* and approximately **13.1–15.7 mm/day** for wild type. (efimov2003rolesofnude pages 13-14)

## 5. Pathway model

A conservative evidence-based model is:

1. NUDE oligomerizes through or uses its conserved N-terminal coiled coil as a protein-interaction scaffold.
2. This region binds NUDF/LIS1.
3. NUDF engages cytoplasmic dynein and helps regulate productive motor activity.
4. The dynein–dynactin system generates minus-end-directed force along microtubules, allowing nuclei to migrate and become properly distributed through the hypha.
5. NUDE enhances the efficiency and spatial organization of this pathway but is not absolutely required for all NUDF/dynein localization or activity. (efimov2000thelis1relatednudf pages 6-6, efimov2003rolesofnude pages 13-14, efimov2003rolesofnude pages 1-2)

This can be summarized as **NUDE → supports NUDF/LIS1 → regulates dynein/dynactin → microtubule-based nuclear positioning**. The arrow denotes functional support, not a demonstrated linear enzymatic reaction.

## 6. Early-endosome transport and cargo specificity

Cytoplasmic dynein in *A. nidulans* also drives retrograde early-endosome transport, and modern work frequently uses endosome distribution as a sensitive dynein-function assay. However, the retrieved gene-specific experiments do not establish a direct, quantitative requirement for O74689/NUDE in early-endosome transport. The fungal literature supports a general principle that NudE helps recruit or organize LIS1-family regulation of dynein, but a dedicated O74689 endosome-transport phenotype should be treated as unproven rather than inferred automatically from dynein-pathway membership. (efimov2003rolesofnude pages 1-2, zhang2018theactincapping pages 13-13)

Accordingly, the most defensible primary biological-process annotation is **nuclear migration/distribution**, with broader dynein-dependent cargo transport listed only as a plausible or family-level function pending direct testing.

## 7. Recent developments, 2023–2024

No direct 2023–2024 functional study of *A. nidulans* O74689 was identified in the retrieved literature. The foundational gene-specific evidence remains the primary work published in 2000 and 2003. Recent studies nevertheless sharpen interpretation of the conserved LIS1–dynein module and explain why a LIS1-binding scaffold such as NUDE can strongly influence motor function.

A 2024 *Science* study used cryo-electron microscopy and motility experiments to show in a purified metazoan system that LIS1 can contact both a dynein motor and dynactin p150 during dynein–dynactin–adaptor assembly. The authors proposed that LIS1 constrains and orients the components to favor productive adaptor binding before being released during movement initiation. This is compelling contemporary context for NUDE’s genetic support of NUDF, but it is not direct evidence that fungal NUDE employs precisely the same assembly intermediate. Publication: Singh et al., March 2024, DOI/URL: https://doi.org/10.1126/science.adk8544. (singh2024molecularmechanismof pages 28-29)

A 2024 single-molecule/optical-trapping study using yeast dynein found that Lis1 slowed force-induced detachment from microtubules and altered dynein’s mechanical response without simply acting as a roadblock or tether. The experiments included measurements at 2 mM ATP and support a “clutch”-like coupling of dynein’s ATPase and microtubule-binding domains. Again, this informs the conserved LIS1 axis, not O74689’s exact mechanism. Publication: Kusakci et al., *Nature Chemical Biology* 20:521–529, 2024; DOI/URL: https://doi.org/10.1038/s41589-023-01464-6. (kusakci2024lis1slowsforceinduced pages 22-23)

Recent human genetics also reinforces the importance of the homologous NDE1/NDEL1–LIS1 interaction in nucleokinesis, but animal NDE1/NDEL1 proteins have lineage-specific functions and regulation. They should not be used to assign centrosomal, neuronal, kinase-substrate, or disease functions directly to fungal O74689.

## 8. Current applications and real-world relevance

There is no established industrial, diagnostic, or therapeutic application targeting *A. nidulans nudE* itself. Its principal current applications are as a **research tool and model-system component**:

- dissecting how LIS1-family regulators control cytoplasmic dynein;
- studying nuclear migration and spacing in polarized, multinucleate cells;
- testing conserved coiled-coil interaction mechanisms through fungal genetics;
- using multicopy suppression and domain replacement to order components in a motor-regulatory pathway;
- providing evolutionary context for animal NDE1/NDEL1–LIS1 biology and nucleokinesis.

The utility of *A. nidulans* lies in combining live imaging of long hyphae with strong genetics. Nevertheless, applications to human disease or antifungal development remain indirect; Δ*nudE* viability and its relatively mild phenotype compared with core dynein components also argue against assuming that NUDE is an immediately attractive fungicidal target.

## 9. Expert assessment and confidence

**High-confidence conclusions** are that O74689 is the *A. nidulans* NUDE-family protein; its N-terminal coiled coil binds NUDF/LIS1; it functions in the cytoplasmic-dynein nuclear-distribution pathway; and deletion causes a viable but penetrant nuclear-positioning defect. These conclusions are supported by gene cloning, targeted deletion, quantitative phenotyping, suppression genetics, two-hybrid assays, fungal co-immunoprecipitation, domain dissection, and live-cell localization. (efimov2000thelis1relatednudf pages 6-6, efimov2000thelis1relatednudf pages 4-5, efimov2000thelis1relatednudf pages 5-6, efimov2003rolesofnude pages 1-2)

**Moderate-confidence interpretation** is that NUDE acts as an assembly/localization scaffold that increases the productive engagement of NUDF with dynein. The genetics strongly support this model, but the native stoichiometry, affinity, structural arrangement, and timing of complex assembly have not been determined for the fungal protein.

**Unresolved questions** include whether NUDE directly activates dynein, exactly how its C-terminal region controls localization, whether it is required for early-endosome transport, which post-translational modifications produce the observed multiple electrophoretic species, and how its role is divided among microtubule ends, spindle pole bodies, and cargo-initiation sites. Epitope-tagged NUDE often migrated as a close doublet with at least four separable bands, suggesting modification, but the responsible modifications and functions were not identified. (efimov2000thelis1relatednudf pages 5-6)

## Final annotation

**Recommended functional description:** “NUDF/LIS1-binding coiled-coil regulator of cytoplasmic dynein; promotes microtubule-dependent nuclear migration and distribution in *Aspergillus nidulans*.”

**Recommended localization:** “Cytoplasmic; enriched in motile comet-like structures at dynamic microtubule ends, with possible additional spindle-pole-associated action. Visible comet localization is not required for all activity.”

**Recommended process/pathway terms:** cytoplasmic-dynein regulation; microtubule-based movement; nuclear migration; nuclear positioning/distribution; hyphal growth and conidiation as downstream phenotypes.

**Do not annotate without qualification:** enzyme activity, transporter substrate, secretion, direct early-endosome cargo-adaptor activity, or mammalian NDE1/NDEL1-specific neuronal and centrosomal functions.
