Gene Description

LCIB (Low-CO2-inducible protein B) is a nuclear-encoded, chloroplast-targeted soluble protein of about 48 kDa in the green alga Chlamydomonas reinhardtii, strongly induced when CO2 is limiting. It is the gene product affected in the classical air-dier mutants pmp1 and ad1, which cannot grow at air levels of CO2 but grow at high or very low CO2 and fail to accumulate inorganic carbon at air CO2. LCIB belongs to a small algal and bacterial family (with LCIC, LCID and LCIE in Chlamydomonas) whose crystal structures reveal a beta-carbonic anhydrase fold with a zinc ion bound by two cysteines and a histidine. Some family members from diatoms and bacteria are active carbonic anhydrases. Purified Chlamydomonas LCIB, and the native LCIB-LCIC complex, showed no activity in vitro, but LCIB restores growth of yeast and Arabidopsis carbonic anhydrase knockout mutants and raises carbonic anhydrase activity when expressed in the tobacco chloroplast stroma, indicating that it can hydrate CO2 to bicarbonate and that its activity may be tightly regulated. In vivo LCIB forms a large hetero-oligomeric complex (about 350-440 kDa) with its paralog LCIC. The complex is dispersed in the chloroplast stroma at high CO2 and moves to form a ring around the pyrenoid, outside the starch sheath, when CO2 falls; this relocation needs LCIC and an intact starch sheath. Genetic suppressor analysis places LCIB downstream of the thylakoid lumen carbonic anhydrase CAH3, and the favoured model is that the stromal LCIB-LCIC complex converts CO2 that escapes the pyrenoid, or enters the chloroplast, into bicarbonate, keeping inorganic carbon trapped in the stroma for the CO2-concentrating mechanism.

Core Functions

Stromal beta-type carbonic anhydrase subunit that, as part of a hetero-oligomeric complex with its paralog LCIC, hydrates CO2 to bicarbonate in the chloroplast stroma. Under limiting CO2 the complex gathers around the pyrenoid outside the starch sheath, where it is proposed to recapture CO2 released by CAH3 and escaping the Rubisco matrix, trapping it as bicarbonate in the stromal pool of the CO2-concentrating mechanism.

Q: Why are purified Chlamydomonas LCIB and the native LCIB-LCIC complex inactive in vitro while LCIB complements yeast and Arabidopsis CA knockouts? Is activity switched on by a post-translational modification, a partner such as LCIC in a defined stoichiometry, redox state, or the C-terminal region absent from the crystallized construct?

Suggested Experiments

Experiment: Measure 18O-exchange (MIMS) activity of full-length LCIB, LCIB with LCIC co-expressed at native stoichiometry, and complex purified from very-low-CO2 cells under varied redox, pH and Zn conditions; test zinc-ligand mutants (C120A, H180A, C204A) for loss of yeast nce103 complementation.

Hypothesis: Chlamydomonas LCIB has intrinsic CO2 hydration activity that is masked under standard in vitro conditions.

Experiment: Complement ad1 or pmp1 with wild-type LCIB versus zinc-site mutants, and with a heterologous active beta-CA (for example PtLCIB4) targeted to the stroma and to the pyrenoid periphery; score growth at air CO2 and Ci accumulation.

Hypothesis: CA activity, rather than a structural role, explains the lcib air-dier phenotype.
