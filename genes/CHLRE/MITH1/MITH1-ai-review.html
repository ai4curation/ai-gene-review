Gene Description

MITH1 (MIssing THylakoids 1; also called SAGA3; locus Cre06.g259100) is a large (1,304 aa, ~138 kDa) nuclear-encoded pyrenoid protein of Chlamydomonas reinhardtii. It is a distant homologue of the pyrenoid starch-sheath and tubule proteins SAGA1 and SAGA2, but it has no CBM20 starch-binding domain and no detectable Rubisco-binding motif. Its sequence is dominated by long predicted coiled-coil segments flanked by disordered, low-complexity N- and C-terminal regions. MITH1 localizes to the thylakoid membrane tubules that traverse the pyrenoid Rubisco matrix. It is most concentrated on the peripheral, cylindrical tubules, sits on the stromal (matrix-facing) side of the membrane, and co-pellets with membranes in a detergent-sensitive way. Under high CO2 it spreads through the whole tubule network, and at limiting CO2 it retreats to the peripheral tubules. MITH1 binds SAGA1, its most abundant specific interactor, and depends on SAGA1 to reach the pyrenoid. Without SAGA1 it is dispersed through the chloroplast. MITH1 is required to extend the membranes through the matrix after SAGA1 has started them. In mith1 mutants, thylakoids cross the starch sheath and stop at the matrix surface, so pyrenoids lack matrix-traversing tubules. The mutants form some extra small Rubisco condensates and grow poorly at limiting CO2, a milder defect than that of saga1. Expressed together with SAGA1 in Arabidopsis plants carrying an EPYC1-Rubisco condensate, MITH1 makes thylakoid membrane sheets traverse the condensate. Neither protein does this alone. MITH1 is proposed to strengthen the adhesive contact between the membrane and the Rubisco matrix that SAGA1 makes.

Proposed New Ontology Terms

pyrenoid tubule assembly

Definition: The formation of pyrenoid tubules, the thylakoid membranes that enter and traverse the Rubisco matrix of a pyrenoid.

Justification: SAGA1 and MITH1 are necessary for pyrenoid tubules and together sufficient to make thylakoids traverse a Rubisco condensate in Arabidopsis. GO has a pyrenoid tubule cellular component term but no process term for its formation, so the role can only be captured with the generic thylakoid membrane organization term.

Core Functions

Extends pyrenoid tubules through the Rubisco matrix. Recruited by SAGA1 to the sites where thylakoids enter the matrix, MITH1 associates with the membrane surface, binds SAGA1 and projects a long coiled-coil region into the matrix. It is proposed to strengthen the adhesive membrane-matrix contact so that the membrane can push past EPYC1-Rubisco bonds and continue through the matrix. Its molecular activity is not established; a protein-membrane adaptor role is a hypothesis, so no molecular function term is asserted.

Suggested Questions for Experts

Q: Does MITH1 bind Rubisco directly? Hennacy et al. found no yeast two-hybrid interaction and no recruitment to the condensate without SAGA1, but a 2026 preprint cites structural work reporting MITH1 binding to membranes and Rubisco. The answer decides whether MITH1 is a membrane-matrix adaptor in its own right or an amplifier of SAGA1.

Suggested experts: Jonikas MC

Q: How does MITH1 attach to the membrane? It has no predicted transmembrane segment, yet it co-pellets with membranes and is released by detergent. Is the contact through lipids, a thylakoid membrane protein, or SAGA1?

Q: Should GO add a "pyrenoid tubule assembly" process term so that tubule biogenesis factors (SAGA1, MITH1) can be annotated more specifically than GO:0010027 thylakoid membrane organization?

Suggested Experiments

Experiment: Purify MITH1 and fragments (N-terminal disordered region, coiled-coil core, C-terminal region) and test binding to thylakoid-lipid liposomes (flotation), to SAGA1 and to Rubisco (pull-down, SPR). Test which fragments complement the mith1 tubule-extension defect.

Hypothesis: MITH1 bridges membranes and the Rubisco matrix through separable regions.

Type: biochemical reconstitution and genetic complementation

Experiment: Image MITH1 by expansion microscopy across high-to-low CO2 shifts in strains with altered matrix size (for example EPYC1 dosage mutants) and compare its radial distribution with Rubisco.

Hypothesis: The CO2-dependent relocalization of MITH1 tracks Rubisco availability in the matrix.

Type: quantitative imaging

Decisions

  1. Location, GO:0160223 pyrenoid tubule (NEW, IDA). Strongly supported by a
    functional fusion, membrane fractionation and antibody-based expansion
    microscopy. No separate GO:1990732 pyrenoid row, because the tubule term is
    part_of pyrenoid.
  2. Process, GO:0010027 thylakoid membrane organization (NEW, IMP).
  3. Participation test. MITH1 is physically on the membranes being
    reorganised, binds SAGA1, and is proposed to supply part of the adhesive
    force that moves the membrane into the matrix. That is a structural
    contribution to the step, not substrate-like necessity.
  4. The evidence is complemented loss of function plus heterologous
    sufficiency with SAGA1.
  5. Comparator check. VIPP1 and FZL (Arabidopsis thylakoid shapers) carry
    GO:0010027, and SAGA1 was given the same term in its sibling review.
  6. There is no "pyrenoid tubule assembly" term. Requesting one is raised as a
    suggested question and an ontology gap.
  7. Molecular function, GO:0043495 protein-membrane adaptor activity (core
    function only, tentative).     The term matches the SAGA1 annoton. MITH1
    associates with membranes and binds SAGA1 (a Rubisco binder), bridging SAGA1
    and the matrix to the membrane surface.
  8. Caveats: there is no membrane-binding domain, lipid binding has not been
    measured, and direct Rubisco binding is disputed.
  9. It is not added as a NEW existing-annotation row, because no direct assay
    of the activity exists. The validator warns about this, and the warning is
    accepted.
  10. "Protein binding" was avoided.
  11. Not proposed:
  12. Starch binding: MITH1 has no CBM20.
  13. Rubisco binding: the yeast two-hybrid result is negative, and the only
    positive report is an uncached, unverifiable citation.
  14. Any CCM or CO2-response process term: the low-CO2 growth defect is a
    downstream consequence of missing tubules.

core_functions:
  - description: Extends pyrenoid tubules through the Rubisco matrix. Recruited
      by SAGA1 to the sites where thylakoids enter the matrix, MITH1 associates
      with the membrane surface, binds SAGA1 and projects a long coiled-coil
      region into the matrix. It is proposed to strengthen the adhesive
      membrane-matrix contact so that the membrane can push past EPYC1-Rubisco
      bonds and continue through the matrix. Its molecular activity is not
      established; a protein-membrane adaptor role is a hypothesis, so no
      molecular function term is asserted.
