Gene Description

LCIA (Low-CO2-inducible protein A, also called NAR1.2; locus Cre06.g309000) is a nuclear-encoded member of the formate/nitrite transporter (FNT) family of anion channels in the green alga Chlamydomonas reinhardtii. The 336-residue protein carries an N-terminal chloroplast transit peptide followed by an FNT-type transmembrane domain, and it resides in the chloroplast envelope, as shown by immunofluorescence, envelope membrane fractionation and fluorescent-protein fusions. Its cryo-EM structure shows the pentameric FNT channel fold with a selectivity filter in which Lys220, together with Ala117 and Val267, sets preference for bicarbonate. LCIA moves bicarbonate across membranes: it increases bicarbonate uptake and gives HCO3- and NO2- currents in Xenopus oocytes, and it restores growth in air of an Escherichia coli mutant lacking carbonic anhydrases and of an Arabidopsis mutant lacking the plastid carbonic anhydrase betaCA5. Unlike its nitrogen-regulated NAR1 paralogs, LCIA is induced strongly at low CO2 under the control of the regulator CIA5/CCM1, and its accumulation also depends on the chloroplast Ca2+-binding protein CAS. In the algal CO2-concentrating mechanism it imports cytosolic bicarbonate into the chloroplast stroma, acting together with the plasma membrane ABC transporter HLA3. Insertion mutants have reduced inorganic carbon affinity, especially at alkaline pH where bicarbonate dominates; loss of both LCIA and LCIB prevents survival at very low CO2; and co-overexpression of LCIA with HLA3 raises inorganic carbon affinity. Loss of LCIA also lowers HLA3 mRNA levels.

Core Functions

Bicarbonate-selective FNT-family anion channel in the chloroplast envelope that moves cytosolic HCO3- into the chloroplast stroma, supplying inorganic carbon to the CO2-concentrating mechanism at low and very low CO2, in cooperation with the plasma membrane HCO3- transporter HLA3.

Suggested Questions for Experts

Q: Does LCIA conduct bicarbonate by passive channel-type permeation (GO:0160133 bicarbonate channel activity), as the FNT fold and the 2026 structure suggest, and if so how is net import into the stroma driven against a stromal pool that is kept alkaline in the light?

Q: Which envelope membrane does native LCIA occupy in Chlamydomonas? The inner envelope is expected and was reported for transgenic tobacco, but has not been shown in the alga.

Q: Is nitrite transport by LCIA (seen in oocytes) physiologically relevant, given that LCIA is regulated by CO2 and not by nitrogen?

Q: How does loss of LCIA lower HLA3 mRNA, and is this retrograde signal linked to the CAS-dependent Ca2+ signal from the pyrenoid?

Suggested Experiments

Experiment: Reconstitute purified LciA in proteoliposomes or planar bilayers and measure HCO3- flux and single-channel currents, testing dependence on membrane potential and pH gradient, and the effect of the selectivity-filter mutants (K220, A117, V267) and the activity-enhancing K136A/A114F variant.

Hypothesis: LCIA is a passive bicarbonate channel rather than an active transporter.

Type: biophysics / electrophysiology

Experiment: Express K136A/A114F LciA in an lcia insertion mutant (and in an lcia lcib background) and measure Ci affinity at pH 9, internal Ci pool size and growth at very low CO2.

Hypothesis: The activity-enhanced LciA variants improve the CO2-concentrating mechanism in vivo.

Type: genetic complementation and physiology

Experiment: Separate inner and outer envelope membranes from low-CO2 Chlamydomonas and immunoblot for LCIA alongside inner (e.g. TIC) and outer (e.g. TOC) envelope markers, or use immunogold electron microscopy.

Hypothesis: Native LCIA resides in the inner chloroplast envelope.

Type: cell fractionation and imaging

Identity

  • UniProt Q75NZ3 (TrEMBL, unreviewed, PE 1), 336 aa, gene LciA, also NAR1.2;
    KEGG cre:CHLRE_06g309000v5. FNT transporter family (TC 1.A.16), Pfam PF01226,
    PANTHER PTHR30520:SF6. Six predicted TM helices (106-322). Cryo-EM structure
    PDB 9V2A (residues 39-336, five chains A-E, i.e. an FNT-type pentamer).
  • Same protein as NAR1.2: PMID:25336519.
  • Has an N-terminal chloroplast transit peptide: PMID:16905358. Removing the predicted 73-aa
    transit peptide sends LCIA:GFP to the cytosol in tobacco
    PMID:26538195.

Expression

  • Identified as a CCM1(CIA5)-dependent low-CO2-inducible gene
    PMID:15235119
  • Unlike other NAR1 paralogs it is carbon-regulated, not nitrogen/Nit2-regulated
    PMID:16905358.
  • Expression depends on the Ca2+-binding protein CAS (retrograde signal from the
    pyrenoid) PMID:27791081.

Location: chloroplast envelope

  • Immunofluorescence (cup-shaped chloroplast signal) and envelope membrane fractionation
    [PMID:26015566 "LCIA was highly enriched in the CE fraction, where CE protein CCP1 (30)
    was also enriched."; "we concluded that HLA3 and LCIA were localized to the PM and CE,
    respectively."]
  • Venus fusion in Chlamydomonas and GFP in tobacco/Arabidopsis
    PMID:26538195.
  • Which envelope membrane: Nolke et al. place transgenic LCIA in the inner envelope of
    tobacco PMID:29888874. Not resolved for the native protein in Chlamydomonas, so chloroplast
    envelope (GO:0009941) is the safe level; inner membrane (GO:0009706) is plausible
    because the outer envelope is generally porous to small anions.
  • Plasma membrane location of bacterial FNT members (IBA donors) does not transfer: LCIA
    carries a transit peptide and is not at the plasma membrane in Chlamydomonas.

Activity: bicarbonate (and nitrite) transport

  • Xenopus oocytes, electrophysiology (Mariscal 2006, abstract only):
    PMID:16905358
  • Xenopus oocytes, H14CO3- uptake with mature LCIA (no transit peptide)
    PMID:26538195.
  • Complementation of E. coli lacking carbonic anhydrases and Arabidopsis beta-ca5
    (Forster 2023, abstract only) PMID:36987927
  • Cryo-EM structure (Guo 2026, abstract only): FNT-type channel with a bicarbonate
    selectivity filter (Lys220; Ala117/Val267); K136A/A114F increase activity; bacterial
    nitrite channel NirC could be engineered to conduct bicarbonate, and Chlamydomonas
    NAR1.1/NAR1.5 have some bicarbonate transport capacity PMID:41507353.
  • Mechanism: FNT family proteins are pentameric anion channels; the 2026 abstract calls
    LciA "a chloroplast envelope bicarbonate channel". GO has GO:0160133 bicarbonate
    channel activity (is_a GO:0015106). I used the parent GO:0015106 in core_functions
    because the channel mechanism for LCIA rests on an abstract-only structural paper and
    family inference; flagged as a question.
  • Formate: no report that LCIA transports formate. Its bicarbonate selectivity is set by
    a specific filter, and the IBA formate terms come from bacterial FocA-type members
    (P0AC23 FocA, P77733 FocB). Formate IBA rows modified to bicarbonate.
  • Nitrite: oocyte currents with NO2- (PMID:16905358); physiological relevance unclear
    because LCIA is not nitrogen-regulated. Not proposed as a NEW annotation.

In vivo role in the CCM

  • Off-target LCIA knockdown together with HLA3 RNAi strongly reduced Ci uptake at pH 9
    PMID:19321421.
  • lcia/lcib double mutant cannot survive very low CO2; LCIA associated with HCO3-
    uptake, LCIB with CO2 uptake PMID:25336519
  • Insertion mutants and overexpression: cooperative HLA3/LCIA HCO3- uptake; LCIA loss
    also lowers HLA3 mRNA (retrograde) PMID:26015566.
  • Heterologous expression in Arabidopsis gave no growth advantage (PMID:26538195);
    Nolke 2019 reported increased CO2 uptake and biomass in tobacco (PMID:29888874).
