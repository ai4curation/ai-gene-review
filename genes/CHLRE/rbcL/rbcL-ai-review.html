Gene Description

rbcL is the chloroplast-genome-encoded large subunit (about 55 kDa, 475 residues) of form I ribulose-1,5-bisphosphate carboxylase/oxygenase (Rubisco, EC 4.1.1.39) in Chlamydomonas reinhardtii. Eight large subunits, arranged as four head-to-tail homodimers, form the catalytic core of the L8S8 holoenzyme and are capped at each end by four nuclear-encoded small subunits. Each large-subunit dimer contributes the active sites, which bind Mg2+ and are activated by carbamylation of Lys-201. The enzyme catalyses the carboxylation of D-ribulose 1,5-bisphosphate to two molecules of 3-phosphoglycerate, the CO2-fixing step of the Calvin-Benson-Bassham (reductive pentose-phosphate) cycle, and, in competition at the same active site, the oxygenation of ribulose 1,5-bisphosphate that starts photorespiration. Crystal structures of the activated Chlamydomonas enzyme with the transition-state analogue 2-CABP show the conserved form I architecture and post-translational modifications including 4-hydroxyproline and S-methylcysteine. In the chloroplast the holoenzyme is distributed between the stroma and the pyrenoid, a liquid-like, non-membranous Rubisco condensate in which most of the enzyme is concentrated when cells grow at limiting CO2; recruitment to the pyrenoid is determined by the small subunit, which carries the binding sites for the linker protein EPYC1, not by the large subunit. Because rbcL is encoded in the chloroplast and is accessible by chloroplast transformation, Chlamydomonas rbcL has been a central system for directed mutagenesis of Rubisco catalysis and CO2/O2 specificity.

Core Functions

Catalytic large subunit of the chloroplast L8S8 Rubisco holoenzyme. Its Mg2+-dependent, Lys-201-carbamylated active site, formed at the large-subunit dimer interface, carboxylates ribulose 1,5-bisphosphate to two 3-phosphoglycerate (and competitively oxygenates it). This is the CO2-fixing step of the Calvin-Benson-Bassham cycle. The holoenzyme acts in the chloroplast stroma and, mostly under CO2 limitation, inside the pyrenoid matrix, where small-subunit/EPYC1 interactions concentrate it.

Suggested Questions for Experts

Q: Rubisco oxygenation of ribulose 1,5-bisphosphate is the first step of photorespiration and is catalysed by the same large-subunit active site. GO has no separate oxygenase activity term (the oxygenase is an exact synonym of GO:0016984). Should the large subunit carry GO:0009853 photorespiration, and how do Arabidopsis and other plant RBCL annotations handle this?

Q: Does the large subunit make any contribution to Rubisco-EPYC1 binding? Cryo-EM suggests a possible contact between EPYC1 R56 and large-subunit E433. Or is pyrenoid packing set entirely by the small-subunit alpha-helices?

Q: Is the stromal (non-pyrenoid) pool of Rubisco, which is larger at high CO2, catalytically different from the pyrenoid pool, or does only the CO2 supply differ?

Suggested Experiments

Experiment: Express plant-like or carboxysome-linker-binding large-subunit variants by chloroplast transformation of the rbcL deletion strain MX3312, and measure pyrenoid formation and EPYC1 co-condensation. This would test directly whether the large-subunit surface affects pyrenoid partitioning.

Type: chloroplast transformation and imaging

Experiment: Compare in situ Rubisco activation state and kinetics in isolated pyrenoids and in stroma fractions from low- and high-CO2-grown cells.

Type: subcellular fractionation and enzyme kinetics

Biology summary

  • Chlamydomonas Rubisco is L8S8. The ~55 kDa large subunits are encoded by the
    chloroplast rbcL gene and the ~15 kDa small subunits by the nuclear rbcS family
    PMID:18664299.
  • The large subunit contains the active site, and the small subunit modulates
    catalysis PMID:20424165.
  • Crystal structures: 1.4 A structure with L8S8 architecture PMID:11641402. Activated enzyme with 2-CABP, where the
    large-subunit fold and active site are like spinach Rubisco PMID:11866526. PTMs: Hyp104/Hyp151 and
    S-methyl-Cys256/Cys369 PMID:11866526.
  • Mg2+ cofactor, one per subunit, and Lys-201 carbamylation (UniProt, from the
    crystal structures).
  • Genetics: the rbcL deletion strain MX3312 (rbcL replaced by aadA) is the host
    for chloroplast transformation with mutant rbcL genes PMID:18664299. Rubisco-null mutants are acetate-requiring
    PMID:18664299.
  • Localization: quantitative immunogold shows Rubisco in both the pyrenoid and the
    stroma, about 90% in the pyrenoid at ambient CO2 and about 40% at high CO2
    PMID:9536077. Pyrenoidal Rubisco is active
    PMID:9536077.
  • Pyrenoid packing is a property of the small subunit:
  • Plant small subunits on algal large subunits give catalytically proficient
    Rubisco but no pyrenoid PMID:20424165.
  • Two surface alpha-helices of the SSU determine pyrenoid formation
    PMID:23112177.
  • EPYC1 binds the small subunit, unlike carboxysomal linkers, which bind
    between large subunits PMID:33230314. One tentative contact with large-subunit E433 is mentioned.
  • Condensed Rubisco remains active PMID:30498228.

Curation decisions

  • All five IEA rows are accepted: Mg2+ binding, chloroplast, carbon fixation,
    RuBP carboxylase activity, and reductive pentose-phosphate cycle.
  • NEW annotations:
  • GO:0009573 chloroplast Rubisco complex (IDA, crystal structures).
  • GO:1990732 pyrenoid (IDA, immunolocalization, PMID:9536077).
  • GO:0009570 chloroplast stroma (IDA, same paper).
  • No pyrenoid assembly or condensate scaffold term is given to rbcL. The large
    subunit is part of the condensed holoenzyme but does not mediate condensation,
    which depends on the SSU helices and EPYC1.
  • Photorespiration is not added. The oxygenase reaction takes place at the
    large-subunit active site, so participation is plausible. A comparator check
    against plant RBCL annotations has not been done, so this is raised as a
    question rather than asserted.
