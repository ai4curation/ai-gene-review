Gene Description

RBCS1 encodes one of the two nearly identical small subunits (SSU) of ribulose-1,5-bisphosphate carboxylase/oxygenase (Rubisco) in Chlamydomonas reinhardtii; RBCS1 and RBCS2 differ by only four residues and either one alone supports holoenzyme assembly and photosynthesis. The 185-residue nuclear-encoded precursor carries a 45-residue N-terminal chloroplast transit peptide; the mature 140-residue subunit (N-terminally methylated at Met-46) assembles with the chloroplast-encoded large subunit (rbcL) into the hexadecameric L8S8 holoenzyme, in which four small subunits cap each end of the core of large-subunit dimers. The small subunit carries no catalytic residues, since the active site for RuBP carboxylation and oxygenation is formed by the large subunits. The SSU is nonetheless required for holoenzyme accumulation (without small subunits, large-subunit synthesis is inhibited at translation) and it changes the carboxylation rate and the CO2/O2 specificity of the enzyme. In Chlamydomonas the SSU also determines where Rubisco is in the chloroplast. Two solvent-exposed alpha-helices (helix A and helix B) on each SSU form the binding site for the Rubisco-binding regions of the intrinsically disordered linker EPYC1, through salt bridges (for example D23/E24 of helix A) and a hydrophobic patch (M87, V94 of helix B), giving each holoenzyme eight EPYC1 sites. This multivalent, low-affinity Rubisco-EPYC1 network demixes into the liquid-like pyrenoid matrix in the chloroplast stroma, the site of CO2 fixation in the algal CO2-concentrating mechanism. Replacing the algal SSU helices with higher-plant helices, or point mutations at the EPYC1 interface, abolish the pyrenoid, disperse Rubisco through the stroma and impair growth that depends on the CCM. A Chlamydomonas SSU also allows Arabidopsis-Chlamydomonas hybrid Rubisco to phase separate with EPYC1 in vitro.

Core Functions

Non-catalytic small subunit of the chloroplast L8S8 Rubisco holoenzyme. It is required for holoenzyme accumulation and tunes the carboxylation rate and CO2/O2 specificity of the large-subunit active sites, so it contributes to RuBP carboxylation in the Calvin-Benson cycle. Its two surface-exposed alpha-helices form the binding site for the EPYC1 linker, which condenses the holoenzyme into the pyrenoid matrix.

Suggested Questions for Experts

Q: Should the UniRule/ARBA rule that assigns "enables ribulose-bisphosphate carboxylase activity" to every RbcS be changed to contributes_to, since the small subunit has no active-site residues?

Q: Should the Rubisco small subunit carry a process term for pyrenoid (membraneless organelle) assembly? Its helices supply the binding sites of the two-component Rubisco-EPYC1 condensate, which is a structural contribution rather than only a requirement. GO has no "pyrenoid assembly" term, and no MF term covers being the multivalent client or co-scaffold bound by a condensate linker.

Suggested experts: Jonikas MC, Griffiths H

Q: Do RBCS1 and RBCS2, which differ by four residues and are expressed differently with growth conditions, form holoenzymes with different catalytic or pyrenoid-forming properties?

Q: Which other pyrenoid proteins (for example RBMP1/2, SAGA1) use the same SSU helix motif-binding site, as proposed from the stronger phenotype of the SSU interface mutants compared with epyc1?

Suggested Experiments

Experiment: Complement the T60 rbcS deletion with RBCS1 alone or RBCS2 alone, and compare purified holoenzyme kinetics (kcat, Kc, specificity factor), EPYC1 binding affinity and pyrenoid morphology at low and high CO2.

Hypothesis: RBCS1 and RBCS2 differ in their contributions to Rubisco kinetics or pyrenoid condensation.

Type: genetic complementation and enzyme kinetics

Experiment: Test binding of RBM peptides from RBMP1, RBMP2, SAGA1 and SAGA2 to wild-type and D23A/E24A or M87D/V94D RBCS1 holoenzymes by SPR or peptide pull-down, and compare their localization in the mutant strains.

Hypothesis: The SSU helix interface is a shared docking site for several RBM-containing pyrenoid proteins in addition to EPYC1.

Type: biochemistry and imaging

Identity

  • Nuclear gene CHLRE_02g120100v5; 185-aa precursor with a 45-aa chloroplast transit
    peptide (UniProt TRANSIT 1..45, ECO:0000269 PMID:11641402); the mature chain
    (46..185) is N-methylated at Met-46.
  • Two small-subunit genes in Chlamydomonas PMID:3820291.

Complex and catalysis

  • L8S8 holoenzyme PMID:11641402.
  • Active site is in the large subunit; the SSU modulates kinetics PMID:20424165.
  • Deletion of both RBCS genes: photosynthesis-deficient and rescued by either gene
    PMID:8942995; the large subunit is not made without the SSU PMID:8942995.
  • Curation consequence: GO:0016984 should be contributes_to for the SSU. The UniRule
    IEA ("enables") is applied to all RbcS, for example Arabidopsis P10795 and spinach
    P00870 (checked in QuickGO). No other RbcS review exists in this repository to follow
    (grep of genes/ for Rubisco small subunit found none), so I followed the
    non-catalytic-subunit precedent in genes/PSEAE/pqsB (MARK_AS_OVER_ANNOTATED, then
    contributes_to in the core function).

Pyrenoid role (SSU helices)

  • The helices control pyrenoid formation PMID:23112177. The
    reciprocal mutant was built in native RBCS1 PMID:23112177.
  • Plant-SSU hybrids are catalytically proficient but lack pyrenoids PMID:20424165.
  • Cryo-EM: 8 EPYC1 sites per holoenzyme, one per SSU PMID:33230314.
    The RBCS1 point mutants D23A/E24A and M87D/V94D lack the pyrenoid matrix.
  • Y2H interaction depends on the SSU helices PMID:31504763.
  • Rubisco plus EPYC1 are necessary and sufficient for LLPS PMID:30498228.
  • RbcS1-Venus in the pyrenoid matrix PMID:28938114.

Decisions

  • chloroplast IEA: ACCEPT. pyrenoid: NEW (IDA, PMID:28938114).
  • carbon fixation, reductive pentose-phosphate cycle IEA: ACCEPT. The SSU is a
    structural subunit of the enzyme that catalyses the step, so it contributes
    structure to it.
  • ribulose-bisphosphate carboxylase activity IEA (enables): MARK_AS_OVER_ANNOTATED, to
    be stated as contributes_to.
  • chloroplast Rubisco complex GO:0009573: NEW.
  • I did not add a NEW BP for pyrenoid or membraneless organelle assembly. The SSU helices
    are a structural part of the condensate, so a case can be made, but GO has no
    pyrenoid-assembly term and adding a process to the client of the condensate was
    judged too far without curator input. Raised as a suggested question and knowledge gap.
