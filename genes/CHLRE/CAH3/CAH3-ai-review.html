Gene Description

CAH3 (locus Cre09.g415700) is a nuclear-encoded alpha-type carbonic anhydrase of the green alga Chlamydomonas reinhardtii and the only carbonic anhydrase known in its thylakoid lumen. The 310-residue precursor carries a bipartite transit peptide (stromal targeting followed by a twin-arginine lumen-targeting domain); the mature ~29.5 kDa enzyme is a peripheral, lumenal protein of the thylakoid membranes, protected from protease in intact thylakoids. Crystal structures show a canonical alpha-CA fold with a catalytic zinc held by three histidines, a disulfide bond, and an unusual dimer formed by swapped N-terminal arms. The enzyme interconverts bicarbonate and CO2 and is most active at the mildly acidic pH of the illuminated lumen, so in vivo it is thought to work mainly in the dehydration direction. Its main physiological role is in the CO2-concentrating mechanism: bicarbonate pumped into the chloroplast and across the thylakoid membrane is converted to CO2 in the lumen of the thylakoid tubules that traverse the pyrenoid, and the CO2 diffuses into the surrounding Rubisco matrix. Mutants lacking active CAH3 (cia3, ca-1) over-accumulate inorganic carbon yet cannot supply Rubisco with enough CO2 and fail to grow at air levels of CO2; cah3 mutations also suppress the air-dier phenotype of lcib mutants. Immunolocalization places much of CAH3 in the pyrenoid-traversing thylakoids, an enrichment reported to increase when cells are moved to limiting CO2, when CAH3 is also phosphorylated and its activity rises five- to six-fold; a recent expansion-microscopy study instead finds CAH3 concentrated in the central reticulated tubules at both high and low CO2. A second proposed role is at the donor side of photosystem II, where CAH3 co-purifies with PSII-enriched membranes and is suggested to consume protons released by water oxidation by dehydrating bicarbonate; PSII preparations from cia3 have impaired oxygen evolution, but in intact cells at low inorganic carbon photosynthesis in cia3 is limited by CO2 supply to Rubisco rather than by PSII.

Core Functions

Lumenal alpha-carbonic anhydrase that dehydrates bicarbonate to CO2 in the acidic lumen of the thylakoid tubules traversing the pyrenoid, releasing CO2 that diffuses into the Rubisco matrix as the final step of the CO2-concentrating mechanism. Activity rises five- to six-fold and CAH3 is phosphorylated after transfer to limiting CO2.

Suggested Questions for Experts

Q: Is the PSII donor-side role of CAH3 physiologically significant in intact cells, or a property of isolated PSII membranes? Most PSII evidence uses the cia3 allele (transit-peptide lesion) and in vitro preparations, while in vivo gas exchange at low Ci shows no PSII limitation.

Q: Does CAH3 really redistribute into the pyrenoid tubules at low CO2 (immunogold, 19% to 37% of total), or is it constitutively enriched in the reticulated tubules (expansion microscopy)? Is any CAH3 left on PSII-containing stromal thylakoids?

Suggested experts: Jonikas MC, Moroney JV

Q: GO has no process term for a CO2-concentrating mechanism and the water-oxidation process term is obsolete. How should the role of a carbonic anhydrase that delivers CO2 to Rubisco (or protons away from PSII) be captured in GO beyond its molecular function and location?

Q: Which residues are phosphorylated, which kinase acts in the lumen, and does phosphorylation change catalytic rate, dimerization or membrane association?

Suggested Experiments

Experiment: Express CAH3 variants targeted or retained in stromal thylakoids versus tubules (e.g. fused to tubule-specific membrane proteins) in a cah3 null background (e.g. a CLiP insertion or the ca-1 nonsense allele), and measure growth, Ci affinity and PSII oxygen evolution in vivo.

Hypothesis: CAH3 activity in pyrenoid tubules, not near PSII, is what is needed for growth at low CO2.

Type: genetic complementation and physiology

Experiment: Map phosphosites by mass spectrometry of low-CO2 thylakoids, then test phospho-null and phosphomimetic CAH3 for activity and localization by expansion microscopy at high and low CO2.

Hypothesis: Low-CO2 phosphorylation of CAH3 controls its activity and/or tubule enrichment.

Type: proteomics, mutagenesis and imaging

Experiment: Compare PSII oxygen evolution and Mn content in cia3, the ca-1 nonsense allele and a catalytically dead lumen-targeted CAH3 (zinc-ligand histidine mutant).

Hypothesis: The PSII phenotypes of cia3 are partly caused by mistargeted CAH3 precursor rather than loss of lumenal CA activity.

Identity and biochemistry

  • Alpha-type CA, first intracellular alpha-CA found in a photosynthetic eukaryote; lumenal
    PMID:9482718.
  • Bipartite transit peptide with twin-arginine lumen-targeting domain; the cia3 substitutions
    lie next to it PMID:38247801.
  • Crystal structures 4XIW/4XIX; zinc site, disulfide, N-terminal arm-swapped dimer; most
    active at mildly acidic pH PMID:25617045.
  • Recombinant CAH3 highly active PMID:39795314.

CCM role (main function)

  • ca-1 nonsense allele complemented by CAH3 PMID:9159949.
    Important because cia3 (the other allele) is a transit-peptide lesion and might have
    mistargeting side effects; ca-1 is a null-type allele.
  • In vivo, cia3 is limited by CO2 supply to Rubisco, not PSII PMID:12913181.
  • Epistasis: cah3 suppresses lcib air-dier; internal Ci >20-fold up PMID:19074623.
  • BST1-3 supply lumenal bicarbonate; cia3 used as CCM-deficient control PMID:31391312.

Localization: pyrenoid tubules and CO2 dependence (conflicting)

  • Immunogold: mostly in thylakoids through the pyrenoid PMID:22709623.
  • Blanco-Rivero 2012: phosphorylated after low CO2, activity up 5-6 fold, pyrenoid fraction
    19% -> 37% PMID:23139834. So even at low CO2 most CAH3 is outside the pyrenoid in this study.
  • Garde et al. 2026 bioRxiv (preprint): CAH3 in central reticulated tubules, membrane
    associated, and unchanged between high and low CO2 PMID:42465278. Conflicts with the
    relocalization claim; not yet peer reviewed.

PSII donor-side hypothesis (critical evaluation)

  • Villarejo 2002: cia3 PSII particles have impaired water splitting, ~2 Mn per RC, rescued
    by bicarbonate PMID:11953312.
  • Shutova 2008: O2 evolution in cia3 PSII preps raised by HCO3- and further by CAH3; model of
    bicarbonate as proton carrier PMID:18239688.
  • Terentyev 2019, 2026 [PMID:31226314; PMID:42511718] further in vitro and cia3
    photoinhibition data.
  • Assessment: supporting data are mainly from isolated PSII membranes and the single cia3
    allele (a targeting mutant). Hanson et al. 2003 found no PSII limitation in vivo at low Ci.
    Pyrenoid tubules lack PSII (D1 absent from isolated pyrenoids, PMID:23139834), and the
    expansion-microscopy preprint finds CAH3 concentrated in tubules even at high CO2, which
    sits awkwardly with a large PSII-associated pool. The PSII effect is plausible as a
    secondary, local proton-consuming effect of CA activity but is not established as a
    physiological function. GO:0009781 photosynthetic water oxidation is obsolete; no
    annotation proposed. Recorded as a suggested question/experiment instead.
