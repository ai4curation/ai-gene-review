Gene Description

CIA5, also named CCM1 (locus Cre02.g096300), is a 699-residue nuclear zinc-binding regulatory protein of Chlamydomonas reinhardtii that acts as the master regulator of acclimation to limiting inorganic carbon. Its N-terminal region carries two zinc-binding sites, a C2H2-type zinc finger (around residues 35-70) and a second Cys/His site (around residues 72-101), each of which binds one zinc ion; the rest of the protein is largely disordered, with glutamine-, alanine- and glycine-rich low-complexity tracts reminiscent of transcriptional activation domains. CIA5 mRNA and protein are present at constant levels in high and low CO2, and the protein resides in the nucleus under both conditions, so its activity is thought to be switched post-translationally when CO2 becomes limiting. Loss of CIA5 (the cia5 point mutant, His54Tyr in the first zinc site, or the ccm1 insertion mutant C16) abolishes active inorganic-carbon uptake, pyrenoid development and induction of essentially all low-CO2-inducible genes, including the bicarbonate transporters HLA3 and LCIA, the bestrophins, LCIB/LCIC, carbonic anhydrases CAH1 and CAH3, the Myb transcription factor LCR1 and the photoprotective LHCSR3, and RNA-seq shows that CIA5 affects the expression of about a quarter of all genes; the cells grow only with elevated CO2. Removing the 54 C-terminal residues yields constitutive expression of CO2-responsive genes, indicating that the C-terminus restrains its activity under high CO2. CIA5 forms a 290-580 kDa complex and binds constitutively to the nuclear CobW/WW-domain metallochaperone-like GTPase CBP1 (ZNG3), which represses a subset of CIA5-dependent genes under high CO2; under zinc deficiency CIA5 and CBP1/ZNG3 are degraded by the proteasome, preventing induction of the zinc-demanding carbon-concentrating mechanism. Whether CIA5 binds DNA itself in vivo is unresolved: it was long thought not to, but recent in vitro work reports specific binding of purified CIA5 to a GC-rich promoter motif. CIA5 appears restricted to some chlorophyte lineages, with an ortholog in Volvox carteri.

Core Functions

Nuclear zinc-finger master regulator that, when inorganic carbon becomes limiting, switches on transcription of the carbon-concentrating mechanism genes (Ci transporters, carbonic anhydrases, pyrenoid and photoprotection components). The protein is constitutively present and nuclear and is thought to be activated post-translationally; its two zinc sites are essential, it partners constitutively with the CobW/WW-domain protein CBP1/ZNG3, and it is degraded by the proteasome under zinc deficiency. No molecular function term is asserted because direct DNA binding in vivo is not established.

Suggested Experiments

Experiment: Perform ChIP-seq on endogenous CIA5-HA knock-in lines under high and very low CO2, test motif enrichment, and use promoter reporters with the GC-rich motif mutated in a cia5 complemented background.

Hypothesis: CIA5 is a sequence-specific DNA-binding transcriptional activator of CCM genes.

Experiment: Measure zinc stoichiometry of CIA5 purified from wild type and cbp1/zng3 mutants under high and low CO2, and test whether zinc-site mutants that retain zinc constitutively bypass CBP1-mediated repression.

Hypothesis: Zinc delivery or removal by CBP1/ZNG3 toggles CIA5 activity.

  • PMID:42488443 (2026 review): cites Chen and Spalding 2026 (Plant Mol Biol Rep,

    promoters of three CIA5-dependent genes. Not indexed in PubMed; publisher page not
    retrievable here, so not cached and not used as direct evidence.
  • Decision: do NOT assert GO:0003700 DNA-binding transcription factor activity or
    GO:0043565 sequence-specific DNA binding. A single in vitro study, unavailable for
    verification, with no in vivo occupancy data. Recorded as a suggested question.
    Positive regulation of DNA-templated transcription (GO:0045893, IMP) is proposed,
    because the effect is transcriptional and CIA5 is a nuclear regulator whose own
    C-terminal state determines target transcription PMID:11309511.

Cellular response to carbon dioxide (GO:0071244)

  • The stimulus is CO2 limitation. GO has no "low CO2" term; GO:0071244 is the
    closest. Comparator check (QuickGO, exact, experimental): Candida RCA1, UME6, FLO8
    and yeast CST6 (transcription regulators mediating CO2 responses) carry GO:0071244 or
    GO:0010037 by IMP, so annotating a CO2-responsive transcription regulator to this
    term follows existing practice. CIA5 passes the participation test as the regulator
    that converts CO2 status into transcriptional output.
