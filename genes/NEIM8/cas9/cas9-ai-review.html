Gene Description

Cas9 of Neisseria meningitidis strain 8013 (Nme1Cas9) is the single-protein effector of a compact type II-C CRISPR-Cas adaptive immune system. The locus is minimal, carrying only cas9, cas1 and cas2 and lacking the csn2 and cas4 genes found in type II-A and II-B loci, and the protein itself is substantially smaller than its type II-A relatives at 1082 residues. Like all Cas9 enzymes it is catalytically inert alone and is activated by loading a crRNA:tracrRNA guide, which both licenses target engagement and reorganises the bilobed protein around a central nucleic-acid channel. The loaded ribonucleoprotein recognises a long, purine-rich protospacer adjacent motif of the N4GAYW/N4GYTT/N4GTCT class through a PAM-interacting domain that is highly diverged even from the closely related Nme2Cas9, then unwinds the duplex and forms an RNA:DNA heteroduplex nucleated at the PAM-proximal seed. Two magnesium-dependent nuclease centres then cut opposite strands: the HNH domain, whose active site is built around Asp587, His588 and Asn611 with a single Mg2+ ion coordinated to the scissile phosphate, cleaves the crRNA-complementary target strand, while the discontinuous RuvC-like domain with catalytic Asp16 cleaves the non-target strand. The two centres are allosterically coupled rather than independent: adoption of the catalytic HNH conformation is what activates RuvC. Cas9 is the only Cas protein this system needs for interference - cas1 and cas2 deletions retain full activity while cas9 deletion abolishes it, and alanine substitution of either catalytic residue is as damaging as deleting the gene. It also governs guide availability: mature crRNA and tracrRNA are strongly depleted in cas9 mutants without accumulation of precursors, indicating that Cas9 stabilises the guides rather than processing them. Unusually, crRNAs in this species can also be transcribed directly from promoters embedded in each CRISPR repeat, so RNase III-dependent processing is dispensable for immunity. The substrate the system is known to clear is DNA entering the cell by natural transformation: plasmid and neisserial genomic DNA carrying a spacer-matched protospacer with a correct PAM cannot transform the strain unless cas9 is disrupted. The enzyme is the target of the type II-C anti-CRISPR proteins AcrIIC1, which binds the conserved HNH domain and traps it on DNA in a catalytically inactive state, and AcrIIC3, which blocks target binding and dimerises the enzyme.

Proposed New Ontology Terms

guide RNA-directed nucleic acid target recognition activity

Definition: Binding to a nucleic acid target at a site whose sequence is specified by base-pairing with a separate, non-covalently bound guide RNA, such that altering the guide RNA sequence alters the target site that is recognised without any change to the polypeptide. The gene product supplies the guide-binding site and the machinery for unwinding the target and forming a guide:target heteroduplex; it does not itself encode the target sequence specificity.

Justification: Programmable, guide-dependent target recognition is the defining property of every CRISPR-Cas effector, and GO cannot express it. A QuickGO search for "guide RNA binding", "crRNA", "CRISPR" and "RNA-guided" returns only process terms (GO:0099048, GO:0043571, GO:0098672 and two obsoletes) and generic binding terms. The nuclease chemistry is well covered (GO:0004520, GO:0004521, GO:0004530, GO:1990238), which is exactly why the gap matters: with only those terms this protein is annotated indistinguishably from an unguided restriction endonuclease. The term is pitched above any single system so that it serves Cas9, Cas12a, Cas13a, Cascade/Csy subunits and Csm/Cmr complexes alike, with children for guide RNA-directed DNA endonuclease activity and guide RNA-directed RNA endonuclease activity layering the chemistry back on. PAM (or PFS) dependence is deliberately excluded from the definition because it is protein-encoded and orthologue-specific - this enzyme reads an N4GAYW-class motif, the nearly identical Nme2Cas9 reads N4CC, SpCas9 reads NGG, and Cas13a uses a protospacer flanking site - so it belongs on child terms or on individual annotations, not on the general concept. The fate of the target is excluded too, being what the existing nuclease terms already say. That recognition is genuinely separable from catalysis is an experimental result in this subtype, not a conceptual convenience: AcrIIC1 occupies the HNH domain and abolishes cleavage while leaving target binding intact, converting the enzyme into a recognition-only protein. One scope question is left to the ontology editors and is also raised in suggested_questions: as defined the term would equally fit Argonaute-family slicers, which delegate specificity to a dissociable guide in the same way. That is arguably correct, since the concept is specificity delegated to a dissociable nucleic-acid guide, but it widens the term well beyond prokaryotic defence and should be decided explicitly. The same proposal is carried in the review of the type II-A exemplar (UniProtKB:Q99ZW2) so that the two can be compared.

Core Functions

crRNA:tracrRNA-programmed interrogation of double-stranded DNA and PAM-licensed recognition of a complementary protospacer. The protein supplies the guide-binding channel and a PAM-interacting domain that reads a long, purine-rich motif of the N4GAYW/N4GYTT/N4GTCT class; the sequence actually recognised is specified entirely by the spacer segment of the guide, so one polypeptide serves every spacer the array holds. Recognition proceeds through PAM contact, duplex unwinding and nucleation of an RNA:DNA heteroduplex at a PAM-proximal seed, and is captured structurally as an early target-recognition state preceding the catalytic ones. It is a separable activity rather than the first half of cleavage: AcrIIC1 occupies the HNH domain and removes catalysis while leaving target binding intact.

Suggested Questions for Experts

Q: Does the meningococcal type II-C system restrict any phage or prophage? Every published interference assay in strain 8013 used plasmid or genomic DNA delivered by natural transformation, which is why GO:0051607 defense response to virus is refined away here; a single phage-challenge result would reverse that call.

Q: Is Cas9 involved in spacer acquisition in a type II-C system that lacks csn2 and cas4? In Streptococcus pyogenes Cas9 supplies PAM selection within a Cas1-Cas2-Csn2 complex, but that accessory is absent here and adaptation has never been assayed in this strain, so the spacer-capture limb of GO:0043571 is left unclaimed.

Q: Should a GO molecular-function term for guide RNA-directed target recognition be scoped to CRISPR-Cas effectors, or pitched broadly enough to include Argonaute-family slicers, which delegate specificity to a dissociable guide in the same way?

Q: GO has no way to state that an inhibitor occludes one catalytic domain of a multidomain nuclease. Given that AcrIIC1 binds HNH alone and that the catalytic HNH conformation is what activates RuvC, is domain-level occlusion a mechanism GO should represent, or does the allosteric coupling make protein-level inhibition the right granularity?

Scope decision

Like SpCas9, this protein is used as a genome-editing reagent (it is the compact
"Nme1Cas9" platform, and its close relative Nme2Cas9 differs only in the PI domain).
That is not a biological function of the gene, so description and core_functions
describe only what the enzyme does in N. meningitidis: the interference step of a
compact type II-C CRISPR-Cas system. The UniProt entry's own
BIOTECHNOLOGY: Coexpression with both gRNAs or a synthetic gRNA in human embryonic or pluripotent stem cells... comment is deliberately not reflected in any annotation.

1. What makes this a type II-C enzyme, and why it must be kept separate from SpCas9

Locus architecture. The meningococcal locus is minimal — three cas genes, with
neither of the type II-A/II-B adaptation accessories:

  • PMID:23706818

PAM. Long and purine-rich, not SpCas9's NGG:

  • PMID:31668930

Size. 1082 aa versus SpCas9's 1368 aa; HNH domain at 512–667 with catalytic
His588 (SpCas9: 770–921, His840), RuvC catalytic Asp16 (SpCas9: Asp10).

Anti-CRISPR target set. Nme1Cas9 is the AcrIIC1/AcrIIC2/AcrIIC3 target; SpCas9 is
the AcrIIA2/AcrIIA4 target. These are unrelated protein families with different

guide RNA, so changing the guide changes the target with no change to the polypeptide.
PAM dependence is excluded from the definition on purpose — it is protein-encoded and

covered by GO:0004520 / GO:0004521 / GO:0004530, which is exactly why a term is needed
for the part that is not.

proposed_new_terms:
- proposed_name: guide RNA-directed nucleic acid target recognition activity
  proposed_definition: >-
    Binding to a nucleic acid target at a site whose sequence is specified by
    base-pairing with a separate, non-covalently bound guide RNA, such that altering the
    guide RNA sequence alters the target site that is recognised without any change to
    the polypeptide. The gene product supplies the guide-binding site and the machinery
    for unwinding the target and forming a guide:target heteroduplex; it does not itself
    encode the target sequence specificity.
  proposed_parent:
    id: GO:0003676
    label: nucleic acid binding
  justification: >-
    Programmable, guide-dependent target recognition is the defining property of every
    CRISPR-Cas effector, and GO cannot express it. A QuickGO search for "guide RNA
    binding", "crRNA", "CRISPR" and "RNA-guided" returns only process terms (GO:0099048,
    GO:0043571, GO:0098672 and two obsoletes) and generic binding terms. The nuclease
    chemistry is well covered (GO:0004520, GO:0004521, GO:0004530, GO:1990238), which is
    exactly why the gap matters: with only those terms this protein is annotated
    indistinguishably from an unguided restriction endonuclease. The term is pitched above
    any single system so that it serves Cas9, Cas12a, Cas13a, Cascade/Csy subunits and
    Csm/Cmr complexes alike, with children for guide RNA-directed DNA endonuclease
    activity and guide RNA-directed RNA endonuclease activity layering the chemistry back
    on. PAM (or PFS) dependence is deliberately excluded from the definition because it is
    protein-encoded and orthologue-specific - this enzyme reads an N4GAYW-class motif, the
    nearly identical Nme2Cas9 reads N4CC, SpCas9 reads NGG, and Cas13a uses a protospacer
    flanking site - so it belongs on child terms or on individual annotations, not on the
    general concept. The fate of the target is excluded too, being what the existing
    nuclease terms already say. That recognition is genuinely separable from catalysis is
    an experimental result in this subtype, not a conceptual convenience: AcrIIC1 occupies
    the HNH domain and abolishes cleavage while leaving target binding intact, converting
    the enzyme into a recognition-only protein. One scope question is left to the ontology
    editors and is also raised in suggested_questions: as defined the term would equally
    fit Argonaute-family slicers, which delegate specificity to a dissociable guide in the
    same way. That is arguably correct, since the concept is specificity delegated to a
    dissociable nucleic-acid guide, but it widens the term well beyond prokaryotic defence
    and should be decided explicitly. The same proposal is carried in the review of the
    type II-A exemplar (UniProtKB:Q99ZW2) so that the two can be compared.
