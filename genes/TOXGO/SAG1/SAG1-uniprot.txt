CC   -!- FUNCTION: Mediates binding to host cells and is especially important
CC       for parasite attachment and fitness in IFN-gamma/IFNG-stimulated host
CC       cells (PubMed:15151144, PubMed:40607979). May regulate binding between
CC       human S100A6 and vimentin (VIM) for cytoskeleton organization during
CC       the early stages of invasion into host cells (PubMed:34950858).
CC       Promotes TNF secretion in host cells probably by enhancing binding
CC       between human VIM and PRKCQ and activating NF-kappa-B pathway
CC       (PubMed:34950858). Promotes viability of host cells to satisfy the
CC       survival needs of parasites through the interaction with human RACK1
CC       (PubMed:38157925). {ECO:0000269|PubMed:15151144,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:34950858, ECO:0000269|PubMed:38157925,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:40607979}.
CC   -!- SUBUNIT: Monomer (PubMed:12091874). Forms homodimers (PubMed:12091874).
CC       Interacts with human lysine-rich coiled-coil protein 1 (KRCC1)
CC       (PubMed:29351065, PubMed:31165984). Interacts with human S100A6
CC       (PubMed:34950858). Interacts with human RACK1; the interaction promotes
CC       viability of host cells (PubMed:38157925).
CC       {ECO:0000269|PubMed:12091874, ECO:0000269|PubMed:29351065,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:31165984, ECO:0000269|PubMed:34950858,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:38157925}.
CC   -!- SUBCELLULAR LOCATION: Parasitophorous vacuole. Note=Surface of
CC       extra- and intracellular tachyzoites and also component of the
CC       vesicular network within the parasitophorous vacuole.
CC   -!- DEVELOPMENTAL STAGE: Detected only in rapidly dividing and invasive
CC       tachyzoites.
CC   -!- DISRUPTION PHENOTYPE: Gene knockout results in higher levels of S100A6-
CC       VIM interaction at 30 min post infection compared to the wild-type
CC       parasites, with similar S100A6-VIM binding levels after 3 hours
CC       (PubMed:34950858). Gene disruption results in increased susceptibility
CC       to IFN-gamma/IFNG (PubMed:40607979). Attachment defect, which is
CC       significantly exacerbated in the presence of IFNG (PubMed:40607979).
CC       {ECO:0000269|PubMed:34950858, ECO:0000269|PubMed:40607979}.
