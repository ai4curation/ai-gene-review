|---|---|---|
| Gene nomenclature | **rbpjb** encodes zebrafish Su(H)B (UniProt **A4FTT4**) in *Danio rerio*. It is one of two zebrafish RBPJ orthologs/paralogs, **rbpja** and **rbpjb**. The available literature confirms this paralog pair but rarely distinguishes their individual biochemical or developmental contributions; therefore, most functional annotation below is inferred from conserved RBPJ/CSL-family evidence rather than direct experiments on A4FTT4. | A zebrafish retinal review explicitly identifies Rbpja and Rbpjb as the two RBP-Jκ orthologs (pqac-00000018). |
| Protein family | Member of the **Su(H)/CSL DNA-binding transcription-factor family**. “CSL” refers to mammalian CBF1/RBPJ, *Drosophila* Suppressor of Hairless [Su(H)], and *C. elegans* LAG-1. This classification agrees with the supplied UniProt description and its beta-trefoil DNA-binding, Ig-like/E-set and CSL-family domain annotations. | RBPJ is identified as the mammalian CSL-family factor related to Su(H) and LAG-1 (pqac-00000008). Conserved RBPJ structure comprises N-terminal, beta-trefoil and C-terminal modules (pqac-00000033, pqac-00000035). |
| DNA-binding specificity | RBPJ recognizes canonical **RBPJ-binding sites (RBSs)** centered on **TGGGAA**. Reported motifs include **5′-CGTGGGAA-3′** and **GTGGGAA**; a shorter core, **TGGGAA**, is commonly used in binding assays. For Rbpjb, recognition of this motif is a strong family-based inference, but a purified A4FTT4-specific binding assay was not located. | The 5′-CGTGGGAA-3′ RBPJ site is reported in a focused RBPJ review (pqac-00000008); canonical GTGGGAA/TGGGAA elements were used in DNA-binding studies (pqac-00000009, pqac-00000010). |
| Structural domains: NTD, BTD and CTD | RBPJ has three principal domains: an **N-terminal domain (NTD)**, central **beta-trefoil domain (BTD)** and **C-terminal domain (CTD)**. The NTD and BTD contribute to DNA recognition; the BTD binds NICD and several alternative coregulators; the CTD also contacts NICD and MAML. Thus, the supplied A4FTT4 Beta-trefoil_DNA-bd_dom and Ig-like/E-set annotations are structurally consistent with the canonical CSL scaffold. | Structural analyses define the NTD–BTD–CTD architecture and identify the BTD/CTD as NICD-contacting surfaces (pqac-00000033, pqac-00000034). The NTD contacts DNA and MAML, the BTD contacts DNA and NICD, and the CTD contacts NICD and MAML (pqac-00000035). |
| Subcellular localization | Functional localization is **nuclear and chromatin associated**. RBPJ occupies regulatory DNA in the Notch-OFF state; following receptor activation, NICD enters the nucleus and engages DNA-bound RBPJ. Direct zebrafish Rbpjb localization imaging was not identified, so nuclear localization is inferred from conserved mechanism. | Live-cell tracking detected nuclear RBPJ binding events and a longest binding-time component of approximately **910 seconds**, reduced when DNA binding was disrupted (pqac-00000011). RBPJ remains at target genes while NICD translocates into the nucleus (pqac-00000012, pqac-00000013). |
| Molecular function | **Sequence-specific DNA-binding transcriptional regulator and context-dependent molecular switch; not an enzyme or transporter.** In the absence of activated Notch, DNA-bound RBPJ supports repression or dampening of target transcription. NICD binding converts it into the DNA-anchoring component of a transcriptional activation complex. Its relevant molecular “substrate” is therefore regulatory DNA containing RBPJ sites, while its activity depends on mutually alternative protein cofactors. | RBPJ is described as the central Notch DNA-binding hub that alternates between corepressor- and coactivator-associated states (pqac-00000026, pqac-00000028). Loss of RBPJ reduces Notch-associated transcription and chromatin accessibility while enabling neurogenic programs in retinal glia (pqac-00000001). |
| Cofactors in the repression state | In the Notch-OFF state, RBPJ associates with corepressors including **SHARP/SPEN**, **KyoT2/FHL1C** and **L3MBTL3**. These complexes can recruit chromatin-regulatory machinery such as **NCoR/HDAC**, **KDM5A** and **KDM1A/LSD1**, promoting reduced transcriptional output at Notch-responsive loci. The exact complement used by zebrafish Rbpjb has not been directly mapped. | SHARP binds RBPJ and connects it to HDAC-containing NCoR and ETO/MTG complexes (pqac-00000026). KyoT2/FHL1C, L3MBTL3 and histone-demethylase recruitment are described as alternative OFF-state mechanisms (pqac-00000027). |
| Cofactors in the activation state | Ligand-induced Notch cleavage releases **NICD**, which enters the nucleus and binds RBPJ. RBPJ, NICD and **Mastermind-like (MAML)** form the core activation complex, which recruits coactivators and chromatin modifiers including **EP300/KAT3B**, **PCAF/KAT2B**, **GCN5/KAT2A**, and, in some contexts, NACK, cyclin C–CDK8, DDX5/SRA, CARM1 and EP400/TIP60. Direct interaction mapping for zebrafish Rbpjb remains to be done. | The RBPJ–NICD–MAML ternary complex and recruitment of EP300, PCAF and GCN5 are established in mechanistic studies and reviews (pqac-00000025, pqac-00000028). Additional activation-associated cofactors are summarized in (pqac-00000027). |
| Primary role in pathway | Rbpjb is best annotated as a **nuclear transcriptional effector of canonical Notch signaling**, converting cell–cell ligand/receptor activation into target-gene regulation. In zebrafish, canonical outputs include **her** and **hey** genes; Notch-dependent neural genes include her2, her4.1–her4.5, her12, her15.1 and her15.2. Because most zebrafish experiments perturb the pathway rather than rbpjb alone, these outputs should be assigned to the Rbpja/Rbpjb-mediated pathway collectively, not exclusively to Rbpjb. | Zebrafish Rbpja/Rbpjb act with NICD and Mastermind-like cofactors, and her/hey families are principal outputs (pqac-00000018). Nine Notch-dependent her genes were identified in the zebrafish thalamic proliferation zone, although direct Rbpjb occupancy was not tested (pqac-00000017, pqac-00000019). |


*Table: This table consolidates the identity, structure, DNA specificity, localization, cofactors and canonical Notch-pathway role of zebrafish Rbpjb. It distinguishes direct zebrafish evidence from conserved RBPJ/CSL-family inference, an important limitation because A4FTT4-specific functional studies are sparse.*
