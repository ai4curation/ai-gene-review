| Biological process/tissue | Role of Rbpjb/RBPJ–Notch signaling | Notch target genes involved | Supporting evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Neurogenesis and neural progenitors | Canonical Notch–RBPJ signaling restrains premature neuronal differentiation and preserves progenitor states. In zebrafish, pathway disruption produces excessive neurogenesis, but available experiments generally do not distinguish Rbpjb from Rbpja. | Context-dependent **her/hey** genes; **her4.1** is a well-supported Notch-dependent neural target. | Zebrafish Notch loss causes excessive neuronal differentiation, while Su(H)/RBPJ mediators connect receptor activation to transcription (pqac-00000023, pqac-00000024). Her4.1 is specifically identified as Notch dependent (pqac-00000021). |
| Spinal-cord morphogenesis | RBPJ-mediated Notch output suppresses neuronal differentiation long enough for neuroepithelial epithelialization, apicobasal polarity and normal midline-crossing movements. This is pathway-level evidence rather than an Rbpjb-specific knockout result. | Not specified directly; neural **her** genes are likely downstream effectors. | Notch-deficient zebrafish show reduced neuroepithelial programs, abnormal polarity and movement, and disproportionate spinal cords (pqac-00000023). |
| Neural stem-cell maintenance in the thalamic proliferation zone | Notch-responsive Her factors help define neural stem and progenitor populations. Extensive redundancy makes the system robust: deleting nine Notch-dependent Hes5-like genes alone produced little disruption, whereas combined loss with Notch-independent Her factors caused major stem/progenitor-cell depletion. | **her2**, **her4.1–her4.5**, **her12**, **her15.1**, **her15.2**; **her8a** is also Notch responsive. **her6** and **her9** are largely Notch independent in this context. | Nine Notch-dependent genes define distinct zebrafish thalamic stem/progenitor populations (pqac-00000017, pqac-00000019). Genetic analyses demonstrate redundancy between Notch-dependent and independent Her branches (pqac-00000022). |
| Retinal homeostasis and regeneration | In adult zebrafish retina, Notch signaling maintains Müller glia in a quiescent state; transient pathway suppression permits injury-induced reprogramming, while later dynamic signaling helps regulate progenitor proliferation and neurogenesis. Rbpja and Rbpjb are both candidate nuclear mediators, but paralog-specific contributions remain unresolved. | **her** and **hey** families; reported retinal pathway readouts include **her6** and **id1**. | Zebrafish possess Rbpja and Rbpjb, and the conserved pathway operates through NICD–RBPJ–Mastermind complexes (pqac-00000018). Zebrafish Müller glia express Notch components and targets including her6 and id1 (pqac-00000004). |
| Somitogenesis and segmentation clock | RBPJ/CSL converts intercellular Notch activity into oscillatory transcription that coordinates neighboring presomitic-mesoderm cells and segment formation. The zebrafish clock cycles approximately every **30 minutes**, but direct attribution to Rbpjb rather than combined Rbpja/Rbpjb activity is lacking. | **her1** and other oscillatory Her-family genes; vertebrate Notch–RBPJ regulation of **Hes7** provides a conserved mechanistic analogue. | Su(H)-dependent regulation and her1 function are linked to zebrafish somitogenesis (pqac-00000024). Comparative work reports an approximately 30-minute zebrafish segmentation period and describes CSL repression without NICD (Ramesh and Chu, 2024; DOI: https://doi.org/10.3389/fcell.2023.1327227). |
| Oligodendrocyte specification and gliogenesis | Notch signaling maintains selected spinal neural progenitors and promotes their transition toward oligodendrocyte/glial fates, including through interaction with Sonic hedgehog programs. Rbpjb participation is inferred from its conserved position as a canonical Notch effector. | Specific zebrafish Rbpjb-bound targets were not identified in the retrieved evidence. | Zebrafish Notch signaling is required for spinal-cord oligodendrocyte specification and progenitor maintenance (pqac-00000039, pqac-00000040). |
| Broader brain neurodevelopment | Notch-dependent RBPJ transcription creates spatially and temporally heterogeneous neural outputs; therefore, individual Her genes are not universal proxies for Rbpjb activity. | Responses vary among **her** genes by neural region, cell type and developmental stage. | Zebrafish studies identify distinct Notch-dependent and independent Her programs (pqac-00000017, pqac-00000019, pqac-00000020). |
| Vascular and hematopoietic development | Canonical RBPJ is broadly implicated in endothelial identity and emergence of hematopoietic stem cells, but the retrieved evidence does not establish an Rbpjb-specific zebrafish phenotype. This should be treated as a plausible conserved context, not a confirmed paralog-specific annotation. | Context-dependent **hes/her** and **hey** outputs; no Rbpjb-specific targets established. | Reviews identify RBPJ as the nuclear Notch effector in vascular transcription and place canonical Notch centrally in definitive hematopoiesis, including zebrafish models (pqac-00000025, pqac-00000042). |
