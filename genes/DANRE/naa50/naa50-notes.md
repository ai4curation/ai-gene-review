GO:0004596 protein-N-terminal amino-acid acetyltransferase activity -> ACCEPT (core; the family's defining activity, [PMID:19744929 "As the preferred substrate this activity acetylates oligopeptides with N termini Met-Leu-Xxx-Pro."]). - GO:0061733 ISS: KEEP_AS_NON_CORE -> MARK_AS_OVER_ANNOTATED, to match the IBA row and the histone H4 row.
- GO:0034087 establishment of mitotic sister chromatid cohesion (ISS): KEEP_AS_NON_CORE -> MODIFY to GO:0071962 (already annotated by ISS). The ortholog evidence is about mitotic centromeric maintenance, not S-phase establishment [PMID:17502424 "San may be specifically required for the maintenance of the centromeric cohesion in mitosis."]. propagation_review added (TERM_SCOPING_PROBLEM / GRANULARITY_MISMATCH on UniProtKB:Q9GZZ1).
- GO:0005634 nucleus (IEA and ISS): ACCEPT -> KEEP_AS_NON_CORE. The ortholog is cytoplasmic in interphase [PMID:17502424 "San localizes to the cytoplasm in interphase cells"] and the nuclear pool is a UniProt by-similarity statement; the core activity is cytoplasmic and co-translational.
- GO:0001756 somitogenesis (IMP): UNDECIDED -> KEEP_AS_NON_CORE. The cache is abstract-only, so the ZFIN curator's reading is not second-guessed; on the visible evidence the phenotype is global early developmental failure [PMID:16484612 "We demonstrate EGAP- and Mak3-deficient zebrafish fail to develop because of, in part, decreased cell proliferation, increased apoptosis, and poor blood vessel formation contributing to embryonic lethality."], so segmentation is a downstream consequence rather than work the enzyme does in somite formation. UniProt cites this same paper for the naa50-naa35 interaction, so the paper does concern this gene product despite the abstract naming EGAP/naa35.
- GO:0006338 chromatin remodeling (IEA, GO_REF:0000108) REMOVE retained but re-argued: the inference chains off the in vitro histone H4 activity, and acetylating H4 in vitro is not participation in chromatin remodeling.
- GO:0031415 NatA complex kept non-core, now argued from structure [PMID:31155310 "Naa50 makes evolutionarily conserved contacts to both the Naa10 and Naa15 subunits of NatA"] and from the independence of the cohesion role [PMID:17502424 "although a small fraction of San interacts with the NatA complex, San appears to mediate cohesion independently"].
