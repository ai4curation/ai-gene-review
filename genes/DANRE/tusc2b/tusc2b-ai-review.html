Gene Description

tusc2b is one of two zebrafish co-orthologs of human TUSC2 (FUS1), a small (111-residue) N-myristoylated mitochondrial protein. It keeps the N-terminal myristoylation glycine and the acidic calcium-binding motif (DEDGDLAHEFYEE) that define the TUSC2 family. In mammals TUSC2 localizes to mitochondria and regulates mitochondrial calcium uptake and calcium-coupled mitochondrial parameters (membrane potential, reactive oxygen species, permeability transition), and Tusc2-deficient mice show chronic inflammation, autoimmunity and premature age-related pathologies. Nothing has been measured for the zebrafish protein. tusc2b is the main zygotic TUSC2 transcript, expressed from the blastula through larval stages, and is expressed more highly than tusc2a in most adult tissues, including muscle, liver and brain.

Core Functions

Myristoylated mitochondrial protein that, by analogy with mammalian TUSC2, regulates mitochondrial calcium uptake and calcium-coupled mitochondrial parameters such as membrane potential; its molecular activity (proposed calcium sensing through the conserved acidic motif) is not established. Both zebrafish copies keep the myristoylation site and the calcium-binding motif.
