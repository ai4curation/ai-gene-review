Gene Description

col1a1a (collagen type I alpha 1a; the chihuahua locus) encodes the pro-alpha1(I) chain of zebrafish type I collagen, the main fibrillar collagen of bone, skin, scales, tendons and ligaments. It is one of two zebrafish co-orthologs of human COL1A1 that arose in the teleost-specific genome duplication; its paralog col1a1b encodes the teleost alpha3(I) chain. The 1447-aa secreted procollagen has the fibrillar collagen architecture of a signal peptide, an N-propeptide with a von Willebrand factor type C domain, an uninterrupted Gly-X-Y triple-helical domain and a C-terminal fibrillar collagen NC1 (C-propeptide) domain that drives chain recognition and trimer assembly. Its product associates with alpha2(I) (col1a2) and alpha3(I) (col1a1b) chains into type I collagen trimers that self-assemble into banded fibrils of the extracellular matrix; in adult bone, skin and scales the three chains occur at about equal amounts. col1a1a is co-expressed with col1a1b and col1a2 in osteoblasts, tendon and ligament fibroblasts, myosepta and basal epidermis, but it is the only type I collagen gene expressed in the median fin fold and pectoral fin margin, where alpha1(I) homotrimers contribute to actinotrichia. Loss of col1a1a is lethal in larvae (uninflated swim bladder, absent actinotrichia), and dominant glycine substitutions (chihuahua, dmh13, dmh14) cause osteogenesis imperfecta-like short stature, fractures, vertebral deformities and abnormal mineralization, and disrupt fin development and regeneration.

Core Functions

Col1a1a (alpha1(I)) is a structural chain of zebrafish type I collagen. It assembles with alpha2(I) (col1a2) and the teleost alpha3(I) (col1a1b) into triple-helical trimers that are secreted and self-assemble into fibrils. These fibrils give tensile strength to the matrix of bone, skin, scales, tendons and ligaments, and serve as the template for bone mineralization. alpha1(I) is the dose-limiting chain: its complete loss is lethal, whereas loss of alpha3(I) is tolerated.

  col1a1a (collagen type I alpha 1a; the chihuahua locus) encodes the
  pro-alpha1(I) chain of zebrafish type I collagen, the main fibrillar
  collagen of bone, skin, scales, tendons and ligaments. It is one of two
  zebrafish co-orthologs of human COL1A1 that arose in the teleost-specific
  genome duplication; its paralog col1a1b encodes the teleost alpha3(I)
  chain. The 1447-aa secreted procollagen has the fibrillar collagen
  architecture of a signal peptide, an N-propeptide with a von Willebrand
  factor type C domain, an uninterrupted Gly-X-Y triple-helical domain and a
  C-terminal fibrillar collagen NC1 (C-propeptide) domain that drives chain
  recognition and trimer assembly. Its product associates with alpha2(I)
  (col1a2) and alpha3(I) (col1a1b) chains into type I collagen trimers that
  self-assemble into banded fibrils of the extracellular matrix; in adult
  bone, skin and scales the three chains occur at about equal amounts. col1a1a
  is co-expressed with col1a1b and col1a2 in osteoblasts, tendon and ligament
  fibroblasts, myosepta and basal epidermis, but it is the only type I
  collagen gene expressed in the median fin fold and pectoral fin margin,
  where alpha1(I) homotrimers contribute to actinotrichia. Loss of col1a1a is
  lethal in larvae (uninflated swim bladder, absent actinotrichia), and
  dominant glycine substitutions (chihuahua, dmh13, dmh14) cause
  osteogenesis imperfecta-like short stature, fractures, vertebral
  deformities and abnormal mineralization, and disrupt fin development and
  regeneration.
