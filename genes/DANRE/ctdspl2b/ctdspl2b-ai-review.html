Gene Description

ctdspl2b (CTD small phosphatase-like 2b) is one of two zebrafish co-orthologs of vertebrate CTDSPL2 (SCP4), a nuclear protein phosphatase of the haloacid dehalogenase (HAD) superfamily; its paralog is ctdspl2a. The protein has a disordered N-terminal regulatory region, which in the human protein carries nuclear localization sequences, and a C-terminal FCP1-homology phosphatase domain with the magnesium-dependent DxDx(T/V) catalytic motif. Human and mouse CTDSPL2 dephosphorylate serine and threonine residues on several nuclear substrates, including the RNA polymerase II C-terminal domain (Ser5-preferential), FoxO1/FoxO3a (promoting their nuclear retention and target-gene transcription, including gluconeogenic genes), Smad1/5/8 (attenuating BMP signalling) and mitotic histone H3 Thr3. The zebrafish protein keeps the catalytic motif (DLDET) and a phosphatase domain 96.2% identical to human CTDSPL2, and is 70.9% identical to ctdspl2a overall. ctdspl2b is maternally supplied and expressed throughout development and in all adult tissues sampled, in the same pattern as ctdspl2a but at two- to three-fold lower levels. No functional study of the zebrafish gene has been published.

Core Functions

Nuclear HAD-family serine/threonine protein phosphatase (CTDSPL2/SCP4 ortholog) with a conserved DxDx(T/V) catalytic motif; by homology it dephosphorylates nuclear substrates such as the RNA polymerase II CTD, FoxO transcription factors and Smad1/5/8. Enzyme activity has not been measured for the zebrafish protein; the catalytic motif and phosphatase domain are shared with ctdspl2a.
