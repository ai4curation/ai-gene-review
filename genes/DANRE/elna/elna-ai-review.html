Gene Description

elna (elastin a, formerly eln1) is one of two zebrafish tropoelastin genes, co-orthologous with the single tetrapod ELN gene, that arose in the teleost-specific whole-genome duplication together with its paralog elnb. It encodes a secreted, non-enzymatic tropoelastin precursor (about 101 kDa, 57 exons) with the alternating hydrophobic and lysine-containing crosslinking domain arrangement characteristic of all tropoelastins; it is the zebrafish copy closest in sequence to human ELN. Elna is expressed broadly in elastic tissues, including head skeletal cartilage, the cardiac outflow tract (bulbus arteriosus and ventral aorta), heart valves and the swim bladder wall, a breadth that resembles the single elastin gene of the non-teleost Polypterus. Its product is deposited as elastin in the extracellular matrix, where it is the predominant elastin of the atrioventricular and ventriculo-bulbar valves. A germline nonsense allele (sa12235) is viable through embryogenesis but reduces valve elastin and causes adult valve disorganization, cardiac enlargement and shortened lifespan; morphants show a swim bladder defect and a mild reduction in bulbus arteriosus deformation, without the bulbus hypoplasia or cell-fate change seen for elnb.

Core Functions

Elna is a secreted tropoelastin that is deposited and crosslinked into elastic fibers of the extracellular matrix, where it provides elasticity to a broad set of tissues (head cartilage, outflow tract, ventral aorta, swim bladder). It is the predominant elastin of the adult heart valves, and its loss causes progressive valve disorganization and reduced survival. This broad role resembles that of the single ancestral elastin gene.

Functional annotation report: zebrafish elna (UniProt A0A8N1TQS2)

Executive conclusion

The requested identity is verified. elna—also historically called eln1—is a Danio rerio gene encoding Elastin a, a secreted tropoelastin precursor in the elastin family. It is not the distinct zebrafish paralog elnb/eln2. The strongest functional interpretation is that Elna is a non-enzymatic extracellular-matrix structural protein whose mature, cross-linked product supplies reversible extensibility and elastic recoil, particularly to cardiac valves and cardiovascular elastic fibers. Direct mutant evidence indicates that Elna is dispensable for gross embryonic viability but important for long-term valve architecture, cardiac integrity, and survival.

The zebrafish literature must be interpreted carefully because many histochemical and antibody assays detect total elastin without distinguishing Elna from Elnb. Elnb is the dominant elastin paralog in the bulbus arteriosus and has a specialized role in its smooth-muscle fate and elasticity; those Elnb-specific findings should not be assigned to Elna. (hoareau2022zebrafishasa pages 8-9, moriyama2016evolutionofthe pages 4-4)

1. Identity verification and nomenclature

Correct gene and organism

The literature confirms that zebrafish possess two teleost-duplication-derived elastin genes, elna/eln1 and elnb/eln2, encoding distinct tropoelastins. Thus, the supplied annotation—A0A8N1TQS2, Elastin/Tropoelastin precursor, gene elna, organism Danio rerio—is internally consistent. Elna lies on chromosome 15, whereas elnb lies on chromosome 21. Predicted Elna is approximately 101 kDa and encoded by 57 exons; Elnb is approximately 173 kDa and encoded by 59 exons. Elna is the more human-ELN-like paralog, with approximately 37% amino-acid identity and 41% similarity to human tropoelastin, compared with about 18% identity and 21% similarity for Elnb. (hoareau2022zebrafishasa pages 2-4)

This assignment also agrees with the supplied InterPro designation Tropoelastin, IPR003979. No evidence encountered indicated that zebrafish elna denotes an unrelated protein. Results concerning similarly named genes in other organisms were therefore excluded.

2. Molecular nature and primary function

Elna is not an enzyme, transporter, receptor, or conventional soluble signal. Its primary function is mechanical and structural. Like other vertebrate tropoelastins, it contains alternating hydrophobic and lysine-rich cross-linking regions. Hydrophobic regions support association and elastomeric behavior; lysine-containing regions enable covalent cross-link formation. Its conserved C terminus includes a tetrabasic interaction motif and paired cysteines that form a disulfide bond important for elastic-fiber assembly. (hoareau2022zebrafishasa pages 2-4)

The accepted elastogenesis model is that soluble tropoelastin is secreted, associates with a fibrillin-rich microfibrillar scaffold and accessory proteins such as fibulins and EMILINs, and is covalently cross-linked by lysyl oxidases to form insoluble elastin. The resulting elastic fiber has an elastin-rich core surrounded by fibrillin-rich microfibrils. For zebrafish Elna, the family membership and sequence architecture strongly support this mechanism, although direct paralog-specific secretion and biochemical cross-linking experiments remain limited. (bhanji2007elastininzebrafisha pages 23-29, hoareau2022zebrafishasa pages 1-2)

Functionally, the cross-linked polymer stores energy during tissue deformation and releases it during recoil. In the cardiovascular system this helps valves and vessel walls endure repetitive loading, maintain structural integrity, and smooth pulsatile flow. Collagen provides complementary resistance to overextension and rupture. Elna should therefore be annotated as an extracellular elastic-fiber structural constituent contributing to tissue resilience and recoil. (hoareau2022zebrafishasa pages 9-11, hoareau2022zebrafishasa pages 1-2)

3. Cellular and anatomical localization

Cellular compartment

The functional product acts outside the cell, in the extracellular matrix. Tropoelastin is the soluble precursor; mature elastin is an insoluble, covalently cross-linked elastic-fiber component. It is therefore inappropriate to describe Elna's principal action as cytoplasmic or nuclear.

Cardiovascular sites

Zebrafish elastin is detected histologically in the bulbus arteriosus, atrioventricular valve, ventriculo-bulbar valve, and arterial elastic lamellae. Elastic lamellae alternate with vascular smooth-muscle layers. Their abundance decreases with distance from the heart: approximately 8–10 lamellae occur near the bulbus exit, 4–5 in the ventral aorta, and 1–2 in the dorsal aorta. (hoareau2022zebrafishasa pages 9-11)

These measurements establish total elastin distribution but are not wholly paralog-specific. Antibodies raised against mammalian tropoelastin may preferentially recognize one zebrafish paralog, while orcein stains mature elastic material rather than a specific gene product. The strongest Elna-specific localization inference comes from the elna mutant: elastin is markedly depleted and valve architecture disrupted, whereas residual elastin in vessels and the bulbus arteriosus is plausibly supplied by Elnb. The 2023 authors consequently proposed that cardiac valves preferentially depend on Elna, while Elnb provides partial compensation elsewhere. (hoareau2023characterizationofthe pages 12-13, hoareau2023characterizationofthe pages 13-14)

4. Developmental expression and paralog specialization

Whole-mount in situ hybridization and quantitative RT-PCR detected eln1/elna expression near the cardiac outflow tract and bulbus arteriosus at approximately 3 days post-fertilization. At 3 dpf, both elna and elnb are expressed in the outflow tract, but elnb expression is stronger; by 4 dpf, elnb also exceeds elna in the bulbus arteriosus and swim bladder. (bhanji2007elastininzebrafish pages 23-29, bhanji2007elastininzebrafisha pages 23-29, faucherre2020piezo1isrequired pages 6-9)

The paralogs are therefore not simply redundant copies. Elna retains greater sequence resemblance to tetrapod ELN and is especially important for adult valve integrity. Elnb has undergone stronger teleost-specific specialization: it is the major bulbar elastin and helps direct cardiac precursor cells toward smooth-muscle rather than cardiomyocyte fate. Elna knockdown can reduce bulbar deformation, indicating a contribution to mechanics, but elnb disruption has much larger effects on contraction duration, bulbar morphogenesis, and cell identity. Elna RNA also fails to rescue the elnb cell-fate phenotype, demonstrating functional nonequivalence. (hoareau2022zebrafishasa pages 8-9, moriyama2016evolutionofthe pages 4-4)

5. Direct loss-of-function evidence

The most informative recent study is Hoareau et al., published 23 May 2023 in Cells (DOI/URL). The elna^sa12235 allele is a T→A point mutation that replaces Tyr88 with a stop codon, predicting an 87-amino-acid truncation likely to be rapidly degraded. Genotypes followed Mendelian segregation, supporting viability through early development. (hoareau2023characterizationofthe pages 2-4, hoareau2023characterizationofthe pages 4-6)

Early phenotype

Embryos showed normal gross development and survival through 7 dpf, and heart rate at 7 dpf was not detectably altered. This argues against an indispensable early contractile or cardiogenic role and is consistent with compensation by Elnb and the delayed consequences of defective extracellular-matrix maintenance. (hoareau2023characterizationofthe pages 6-12, hoareau2023characterizationofthe pages 14-16)

Adult cardiovascular phenotype

Pathology emerged principally in adults and was heterogeneous and incompletely penetrant:

  • At 20 months, mortality was 56% in heterozygotes and 53% in homozygotes, versus 12% in wild-type siblings; survival cohorts comprised 27 wild types, 78 heterozygotes, and 41 homozygotes, and the survival difference was significant by log-rank testing. Body mass index did not differ significantly. (hoareau2023characterizationofthe pages 4-6)
  • Some mutant hearts at 22 months were four- to fivefold larger than normal, and homozygous ventricles were significantly enlarged. (hoareau2023characterizationofthe pages 6-12)
  • At 12 months, atrioventricular and ventriculo-bulbar valves were enlarged, poorly defined, and disorganized. Honeycomb-like areas approached 60% in mutants but did not exceed 20% in wild type. More than 50% of mutants had abnormal valve organization, compared with none of the wild types examined. (hoareau2023characterizationofthe pages 6-12)
  • Severe animals developed swelling/dropsy, protruding scales, ulcers, impaired swimming, cardiomegaly, and pathological tissue around the bulbus arteriosus. Lipid-containing nodules and CD3e-positive inflammatory cells were observed, although causality and lesion origin remain uncertain. (hoareau2023characterizationofthe pages 12-13, hoareau2023characterizationofthe pages 6-12)

Valve histology used four fish per genotype and age, and not every valve was evaluable. Some genotype-dependent reductions in elastin staining consequently failed to reach statistical significance. The robust interpretation is therefore that elna deficiency causes age-progressive, variably penetrant valve and cardiac pathology, while exact gene-dosage relationships require larger cohorts. (hoareau2023characterizationofthe pages 12-13, hoareau2023characterizationofthe pages 2-4)

6. Pathways and physiological regulation

Elastogenesis pathway

Elna participates in the extracellular elastogenesis program: tropoelastin deposition on fibrillin-rich microfibrils, organization by elastic-fiber-associated proteins, and lysyl-oxidase-mediated cross-linking into mature elastin. Associated matrix proteins include fibrillins, fibulins, EMILINs, MAGPs, latent TGF-β-binding proteins, and ADAMTS-family components. This is a matrix-assembly pathway rather than a metabolic reaction catalyzed by Elna itself. (bhanji2007elastininzebrafisha pages 23-29, hoareau2022zebrafishasa pages 9-11)

Hemodynamic mechanotransduction

Faucherre et al., published June 2020 in the Journal of Molecular and Cellular Cardiology (DOI/URL), place outflow-tract elastin expression downstream of hemodynamic sensing. Endothelial Piezo1b responds to mechanical force; its loss or inhibition abolishes mechanically induced endothelial currents, disrupts outflow-tract maturation, and reduces expression of both elna and elnb at 3 dpf. Mouse PIEZO1 RNA rescues the zebrafish outflow-tract defect, supporting pathway conservation. (faucherre2020piezo1isrequired pages 6-9, faucherre2020piezo1isrequired pages 20-23)

The mechanical stimulus is substantial: outflow-tract diameter changed by about 16.87 ± 0.24 μm during systole, compared with only a 0.72 ± 0.20 μm systolic increase in the dorsal aorta. The supported pathway is therefore:

cardiac contraction/blood flow → endothelial Piezo1b activation → downstream mechanotransductive signaling → elna/elnb expression and elastic outflow-tract maturation.

The intermediate transcriptional mechanism remains unresolved, and this pathway is not Elna-exclusive. (faucherre2020piezo1isrequired pages 6-9)

TGF-β-related context

Latent TGF-β-binding protein 3 is required for proper Elnb-associated bulbar development but does not alter elnb transcript abundance, suggesting a role in extracellular maturation rather than gene production. Valve regeneration also involves TGF-β signaling. These observations establish relevant elastic-matrix context but should not be represented as direct proof of Elna-specific TGF-β regulation. (hoareau2022zebrafishasa pages 8-9, moriyama2016evolutionofthe pages 4-4)

7. Current applications and expert assessment

The principal real-world application is as a zebrafish model of elastin insufficiency and age-progressive cardiac-valve disease. Human ELN insufficiency causes disorders including supravalvular aortic stenosis and Williams–Beuren syndrome, while other ELN variants cause cutis laxa. Because elna^sa12235 fish survive embryogenesis yet develop premature adult pathology, the line is suitable for longitudinal imaging, genetic-modifier studies, extracellular-matrix research, and relatively scalable testing of candidate interventions. The transparent embryo, high fecundity, and availability of CRISPR and fluorescent reporters strengthen this use case. (hoareau2022zebrafishasa pages 1-2, hoareau2023characterizationofthe pages 1-2)

The 2023 investigators argue that zebrafish can accelerate therapeutic screening and reduce mammalian-animal use, rather than replace mammalian models. That caution is warranted: zebrafish retain the compensating elnb paralog, their bulbus arteriosus has no exact adult mammalian counterpart, and the mutant phenotype is heterogeneous. (hoareau2023characterizationofthe pages 13-14, hoareau2023characterizationofthe pages 14-16)

8. Recent developments and evidence gaps

The most recent gene-specific functional advance in the retrieved 2023–2024 literature is the 2023 characterization of elna^sa12235, which shifted the annotation from sequence/expression inference toward direct evidence for adult valve maintenance and survival. A 2024 review of duplicated zebrafish genes emphasized that teleost paralogs commonly develop distinct functions and should be studied together, an interpretive principle strongly applicable to elna/elnb; however, it added little direct Elna-specific experimentation.

Important unresolved questions are:

  1. Which cell populations synthesize Elna at each life stage, at single-cell resolution?
  2. What fraction of elastin in each valve, artery, skin, or swim-bladder compartment derives from Elna rather than Elnb?
  3. Does the sa12235 transcript undergo nonsense-mediated decay, and is any truncated peptide produced?
  4. What transcription factors connect Piezo1-dependent mechanosensation to elna expression?
  5. Why are heterozygous and homozygous survival effects similar, and what controls incomplete penetrance?

Final functional annotation

Danio rerio elna encodes a secreted tropoelastin precursor that is assembled and cross-linked in the extracellular matrix into elastic fibers. Its principal molecular role is to provide reversible extensibility, recoil, and long-term mechanical integrity, particularly in cardiac valves and cardiovascular tissues. Expression in the developing outflow tract is responsive to Piezo1-dependent hemodynamic mechanotransduction. Direct loss-of-function evidence shows that Elna is not essential for gross embryonic development but is required for normal adult valve organization, cardiac homeostasis, and lifespan. Functional attribution must remain distinct from elnb, the more specialized and predominant bulbus-arteriosus paralog.

description: >-
  elna (elastin a, formerly eln1) is one of two zebrafish tropoelastin genes,
  co-orthologous with the single tetrapod ELN gene, that arose in the
  teleost-specific whole-genome duplication together with its paralog elnb. It
  encodes a secreted, non-enzymatic tropoelastin precursor (about 101 kDa, 57
  exons) with the alternating hydrophobic and lysine-containing crosslinking
  domain arrangement characteristic of all tropoelastins; it is the zebrafish
  copy closest in sequence to human ELN. Elna is expressed broadly in elastic
  tissues, including head skeletal cartilage, the cardiac outflow tract
  (bulbus arteriosus and ventral aorta), heart valves and the swim bladder
  wall, a breadth that resembles the single elastin gene of the non-teleost
  Polypterus. Its product is deposited as elastin in the extracellular matrix,
  where it is the predominant elastin of the atrioventricular and
  ventriculo-bulbar valves. A germline nonsense allele (sa12235) is viable
  through embryogenesis but reduces valve elastin and causes adult valve
  disorganization, cardiac enlargement and shortened lifespan; morphants show
  a swim bladder defect and a mild reduction in bulbus arteriosus
  deformation, without the bulbus hypoplasia or cell-fate change seen for
  elnb.
