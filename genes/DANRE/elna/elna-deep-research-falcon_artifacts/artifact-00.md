| Identity | **Danio rerio elna**, also called **eln1**, encodes Elastin a/tropoelastin and corresponds to UniProt **A0A8N1TQS2**. It is distinct from the teleost paralog **elnb/eln2**. Elna is on chromosome 15 and is more similar to human ELN than Elnb: approximately 37% identity and 41% similarity versus 18% and 21% for Elnb. (pqac-00000003) | Zebrafish comparative genomics, synteny, and sequence analysis | **High.** Multiple zebrafish-specific sources agree; findings for elnb must not be assigned to elna. |
| Protein class and extracellular maturation | Elna is a secreted tropoelastin precursor in the elastin family, with alternating hydrophobic and lysine-rich cross-linking regions and a conserved cysteine-containing C terminus. By established elastin biology, tropoelastin is deposited on fibrillin-rich microfibrils and covalently cross-linked by lysyl oxidases into insoluble elastic fibers. (pqac-00000003, pqac-00000004) | Elna sequence/domain comparison plus established elastin-family biochemistry | **High** for protein-family architecture; **moderate** for the detailed zebrafish-Elna maturation mechanism because direct paralog-specific secretion and cross-linking assays are limited. |
| Principal localization | The mature product functions **outside cells in the extracellular matrix**, principally within elastic fibers of cardiovascular structures. Zebrafish elastin occurs in the bulbus arteriosus, atrioventricular and ventriculo-bulbar valves, and arterial elastic lamellae. Mutant evidence indicates that valves preferentially depend on Elna, whereas Elnb remains prominent in vessels and the bulbus arteriosus. (pqac-00000005, pqac-00000011) | Histological orcein staining, elastin immunohistochemistry, and mutant comparison | **Moderate–high.** Histological reagents generally detect elastin and may not distinguish Elna from Elnb; valve depletion in elna mutants provides stronger Elna-specific localization evidence. |
| Primary mechanical function | Elna contributes to elastic-fiber resilience, reversible extension, and recoil during repetitive cardiovascular loading. Together with collagen and microfibrils, elastin helps smooth pulsatile flow and maintain valve and vessel-wall integrity; zebrafish vascular elastic lamellae decline from about 8–10 near the bulbus exit to 4–5 in the ventral aorta and 1–2 in the dorsal aorta. (pqac-00000005) | Histology, comparative vascular mechanics, and elastin-family functional inference | **High** for elastin’s structural role; **moderate** for Elna’s precise share because both paralogs contribute in some cardiovascular sites. Elna is structural, not an enzyme or transporter. |
| Developmental expression | elna/eln1 expression is detected near the cardiac outflow tract and bulbus arteriosus at approximately 3 days post-fertilization. At 3 dpf, both elna and elnb are expressed in the outflow tract, but elnb is stronger; by 4 dpf, elnb also exceeds elna in the bulbus and swim bladder. (pqac-00000002, pqac-00000016) | Whole-mount in situ hybridization and quantitative RT-PCR | **High** for spatial and temporal expression; reported comparisons are largely relative rather than absolute. |
| Direct loss-of-function evidence | The **elnasa12235** T→A nonsense allele converts Tyr88 to a stop codon, predicting an 87-residue product likely subject to degradation. Embryonic development and 7-dpf heart rate were grossly normal, but adult mutants developed premature, incompletely penetrant valve disease and reduced survival. By 20 months, mortality was 56% in heterozygotes and 53% in homozygotes versus 12% in wild type; some hearts were 4–5 times normal size. At 12 months, abnormal valve organization affected more than 50% of mutants versus none of the wild types, and honeycomb-like valve areas approached 60% versus no more than 20% in wild type. (pqac-00000009, pqac-00000014) | Direct germline mutant, longitudinal survival analysis, cardiac histology, and heart-rate measurement | **High** for delayed adult pathology and survival effect. Valve histology used only four fish per genotype and some measurements lacked statistical power; heterogeneity and residual Elnb complicate dosage interpretation. |
| Piezo1/hemodynamic regulation | Cyclic blood-flow forces are sensed by endothelial Piezo1b in the outflow tract. Loss of Piezo1b disrupts outflow-tract development and reduces expression of both elna and elnb at 3 dpf, placing Elna-associated matrix production downstream of mechanosensation. The outflow tract undergoes much greater cyclic deformation than the dorsal aorta—approximately 16.87 ± 0.24 μm at systole versus a 0.72 ± 0.20 μm systolic increase, respectively. (pqac-00000016, pqac-00000017) | Knockdown, channel inhibition, rescue, endothelial electrophysiology, in situ hybridization, and live morphometry | **Moderate–high.** Direct evidence links Piezo1b to elna expression, but the complete intermediates between mechanosensation and elna transcription remain unresolved and effects are not Elna-exclusive. |
| Translational application | The elnasa12235 line is a zebrafish model for elastin insufficiency and age-progressive cardiac-valve pathology. Its delayed, viable phenotype supports longitudinal imaging, modifier studies, and comparatively scalable testing of candidate therapies for elastinopathy and valve degeneration. (pqac-00000010, pqac-00000015) | Disease-model characterization and expert interpretation | **Moderate.** The model is preclinical, exhibits incomplete penetrance, and does not fully reproduce single-copy human ELN biology because zebrafish retain elnb. |
