Gene Description

Toll-like receptor of the fish-specific TLR22 group, found in aquatic vertebrates and absent from tetrapods. The protein has the canonical architecture of the family: a signal peptide, a leucine-rich-repeat ectodomain with a cysteine-rich flanking region, a single transmembrane segment and a cytoplasmic TIR domain, and it belongs phylogenetically to the branch containing TLR21 and mouse TLR13 rather than to any mammalian receptor's orthology group. In zebrafish its transcript is found mainly in spleen and kidney and is raised by exposure to unrelated microbial products including lipopolysaccharide, peptidoglycan and double-stranded RNA, and its leucine-rich repeats carry signatures of positive selection at several sites, consistent with ongoing diversification of ligand recognition. The function of the receptor is best characterised in another teleost, fugu, where TLR22 detects long double-stranded RNA at the cell surface and signals through the TICAM-1 adaptor to induce interferon and confer resistance to aquatic RNA viruses, a role proposed to substitute for cell-surface TLR3 in fish. Whether the zebrafish protein has the same ligand and adaptor has not been tested.

Comprehensive Research Report: Zebrafish TLR22 (UniProt A0A2R8RTN4)

Gene Identity Verification

The gene tlr22 (UniProt accession A0A2R8RTN4) in zebrafish (Danio rerio) encodes Toll-like receptor 22, a pattern recognition receptor belonging to the Toll-like receptor family (davis2026tolllikereceptorsin pages 14-15, tanekhy2016theroleof pages 1-3). The protein is annotated as a precursor containing characteristic TLR domains including leucine-rich repeats (LRR), cysteine-rich flanking regions, and a Toll/interleukin-1 receptor (TIR) domain, consistent with the UniProt annotation provided (ghani2024unveilingthemultifaceted pages 2-4, ji2018characterizationofthe pages 1-2).

Structural Organization and Molecular Architecture

TLR22 exhibits the canonical type-I transmembrane protein architecture characteristic of Toll-like receptors. The protein consists of three major structural regions: an N-terminal extracellular leucine-rich repeat (LRR) ectodomain that mediates pathogen recognition, a single-pass transmembrane (TM) domain, and a C-terminal intracellular Toll/interleukin-1 receptor (TIR) domain responsible for downstream signaling (ji2018characterizationofthe pages 11-12, davis2026tolllikereceptorsin pages 7-8, ji2018characterizationofthe pages 2-3).

The LRR ectodomain forms a horseshoe-shaped structure that serves as the ligand-binding surface for pathogen-associated molecular patterns (PAMPs) (ji2018characterizationofthe pages 11-12, ghani2024unveilingthemultifaceted pages 2-4). Reports differ on the exact number of LRR motifs in zebrafish TLR22: one recent analysis reported 26 LRRs (ghani2024unveilingthemultifaceted pages 2-4), while another comprehensive review reported 15 LRR motifs (davis2026tolllikereceptorsin pages 14-15). This discrepancy likely reflects differences in prediction algorithms and repeat-definition criteria. Comparative structural modeling suggests that zebrafish TLR22 has undergone positive selection in its LRR motifs, resulting in a flattened horseshoe-shaped ectodomain associated with functional adaptation toward sensing long double-stranded RNA (sousa2022tolllikereceptorevolution pages 11-12).

The leucine-rich repeats form tandem arrays, typically 20-29 amino acids long with conserved hydrophobic residues, providing the structural basis for pathogen recognition (davis2026tolllikereceptorsin pages 7-8, ji2018characterizationofthe pages 2-3). The C-terminal LRR region (LRRCT) is particularly important as it mediates receptor dimerization required for complete ligand binding (ji2018characterizationofthe pages 11-12).

Primary Molecular Function: Ligand Recognition and Substrate Specificity

TLR22 functions as a pattern recognition receptor specialized for detecting viral double-stranded RNA (dsRNA). This ligand specificity has been established through multiple experimental approaches, though most direct functional evidence comes from studies of TLR22 orthologs in other teleost species rather than zebrafish specifically.

The seminal functional study by Matsuo et al. (2008) demonstrated that teleost TLR22 recognizes RNA duplexes to induce interferon (IFN) and protect cells from birnavirus infection (shan2021identificationandfunctional pages 15-15, poynter2015sensorsofinfection pages 21-24). When Japanese pufferfish (Takifugu rubripes) TLR22 was expressed in RTG-2 cells and exposed to the dsRNA genome of infectious pancreatic necrosis virus (IPNV), type I interferon production was significantly increased, and cells acquired antiviral resistance (aoki2015dnavaccinemediatedinnate pages 5-7).

Crucially, TLR22-mediated interferon activity exhibits length-dependent recognition: experimental studies with Fugu TLR22 expressed in human cells showed dose-dependent IFN activity after polyinosinic-polycytidylic acid (poly I:C) stimulation, with maximal response observed at approximately 1,000-bp dsRNA (poynter2015sensorsofinfection pages 8-10, aoki2015dnavaccinemediatedinnate pages 5-7). This demonstrates preferential recognition of long dsRNA molecules, distinguishing TLR22 from other nucleic acid sensors that recognize shorter RNA species (sousa2022tolllikereceptorevolution pages 11-12, tanekhy2016theroleof pages 3-5).

While TLR22 is primarily characterized as a dsRNA receptor, some reports suggest it may respond to bacterial ligands in addition to viral RNA (davis2026tolllikereceptorsin pages 5-7). However, direct binding studies have not established whether these bacterial responses reflect direct recognition or indirect activation through other pattern recognition pathways.

It is important to emphasize that the zebrafish TLR22 protein itself (A0A2R8RTN4) has not been directly tested for ligand binding in the identified literature. The functional annotation is based on orthology to pufferfish TLR22 and evolutionary inference rather than direct biochemical or cell-based assays with the zebrafish protein (ghani2024unveilingthemultifaceted pages 13-14, davis2026tolllikereceptorsin pages 5-7).

Subcellular Localization

The subcellular localization of TLR22 appears to vary among teleost species, and the precise localization of zebrafish TLR22 has not been definitively established experimentally. Recent comprehensive reviews describe TLR22 as predominantly localized to intracellular endosomal and lysosomal membranes in immune cells (davis2026tolllikereceptorsin pages 14-15). However, earlier functional work characterized Japanese pufferfish TLR22 as a cell-surface or plasma membrane receptor for dsRNA (poynter2015sensorsofinfection pages 4-6, aoki2015dnavaccinemediatedinnate pages 5-7).

This apparent discrepancy may reflect genuine species-specific differences in TLR22 localization, with some fish species exhibiting cell-surface expression while others show primarily endosomal localization (davis2026tolllikereceptorsin pages 14-15, shan2021identificationandfunctional pages 15-15). For zebrafish A0A2R8RTN4, the most defensible annotation is that it is a membrane-associated receptor, with the precise plasma membrane versus endolysosomal distribution requiring experimental validation in zebrafish cells.

Signaling Mechanisms and Adaptor Proteins

TLR22 initiates innate immune signaling following recognition of viral dsRNA, though the precise adaptor molecules and signaling pathways show some variation in the literature. Most recent comparative analyses associate fish TLR22 with MyD88 (myeloid differentiation primary response 88) as the primary adaptor protein (davis2026tolllikereceptorsin pages 5-7, davis2026tolllikereceptorsin pages 14-15, davis2026tolllikereceptorsin pages 4-5). MyD88-dependent signaling leads to activation of downstream kinase cascades and transcription factors.

However, earlier functional studies reported that teleost TLR22 can signal through TICAM-1 (TRIF-related adaptor molecule) or TRIF-like pathways leading to type I interferon induction (poynter2015sensorsofinfection pages 8-10). This apparent conflict may reflect species-specific differences, experimental system variations, or the possibility that TLR22 can utilize multiple adaptor pathways depending on cellular context.

Downstream of adaptor recruitment, TLR22 activates multiple signaling cascades:

  1. NF-κB pathway: Activation of nuclear factor kappa B leads to transcription of pro-inflammatory cytokines including IL-1, IL-6, and TNF-α (davis2026tolllikereceptorsin pages 14-15, ghani2024unveilingthemultifaceted pages 4-5, ghani2024unveilingthemultifaceted pages 8-9).

  2. Interferon regulatory pathways: TLR22 signaling induces type I interferon production, establishing an antiviral state in cells (davis2026tolllikereceptorsin pages 14-15, davis2026tolllikereceptorsin pages 5-7, shan2021identificationandfunctional pages 15-15).

  3. MAPK pathways: Studies in orange-spotted grouper demonstrated that TLR22 upregulates extracellular signal-regulated kinase (ERK) signaling while negatively regulating c-Jun N-terminal kinase (JNK) and p38 mitogen-activated protein kinase pathways (davis2026tolllikereceptorsin pages 14-15).

The magnitude of NF-κB pathway activation appears to depend on the length of the poly I:C ligand, suggesting that ligand characteristics influence the downstream signaling output (davis2026tolllikereceptorsin pages 14-15).

It should be noted that adaptor usage and downstream signaling pathways have not been experimentally established for zebrafish A0A2R8RTN4 specifically through receptor-specific interaction studies, knockdown experiments, or loss-of-function analyses (davis2026tolllikereceptorsin pages 5-7).

Biological Processes and Immune Functions

TLR22 participates primarily in antiviral innate immunity through detection of viral dsRNA and initiation of interferon-dependent antiviral defenses (davis2026tolllikereceptorsin pages 14-15, davis2026tolllikereceptorsin pages 5-7, ghani2024unveilingthemultifaceted pages 13-14, shan2021identificationandfunctional pages 15-15). The principal biological role is to serve as an early-warning receptor: by identifying viral dsRNA, TLR22 activates downstream innate immune defenses that limit viral infection and support pathogen clearance (ghani2024unveilingthemultifaceted pages 8-9, ghani2024unveilingthemultifaceted pages 13-14).

Key biological processes involving TLR22 include:

  1. Viral dsRNA recognition: TLR22 detects double-stranded RNA produced during viral replication, serving as a molecular signature of viral infection (shan2021identificationandfunctional pages 15-15, davis2026tolllikereceptorsin pages 5-7, ghani2024unveilingthemultifaceted pages 8-9).

  2. Type I interferon induction: Upon dsRNA recognition, TLR22 triggers interferon production, leading to expression of interferon-stimulated genes (ISGs) and establishment of an antiviral cellular state (shan2021identificationandfunctional pages 15-15).

  3. Protection from viral infection: Experimental studies demonstrated that TLR22 expression protects cells from birnavirus infection, establishing a direct link between receptor activation and antiviral protection (shan2021identificationandfunctional pages 15-15, aoki2015dnavaccinemediatedinnate pages 5-7).

  4. Pro-inflammatory cytokine production: TLR22 signaling induces production of inflammatory mediators including IL-1, IL-6, and TNF-α, contributing to the broader inflammatory response (ghani2024unveilingthemultifaceted pages 4-5, ghani2024unveilingthemultifaceted pages 8-9).

  5. Regulation of inflammation: Beyond pathogen detection, TLR22 helps control the magnitude of inflammation by limiting excessive reactive oxygen species production, thereby reducing the risk of host tissue damage (davis2026tolllikereceptorsin pages 14-15).

  6. Dendritic cell functions: In rainbow trout, TLR22 is expressed on skin CD8+ dendritic cells where it participates in cross-presentation to activate CD8+ T cells, linking innate dsRNA sensing with adaptive cytotoxic antiviral responses (ghani2024unveilingthemultifaceted pages 8-9). This connection to dendritic cell function has not been established in zebrafish.

Expression Pattern in Zebrafish

Limited data exist on the tissue-specific and developmental expression of zebrafish tlr22. Developmental studies indicate that tlr22 transcript levels are relatively low before approximately 1 day post-fertilization (dpf), then stabilize with expression from 2-7 dpf no longer statistically different from the 1 dpf level (gurupalli2020identificationandmolecular pages 96-100). This developmental timing coincides with the emergence of macrophages (appearing at 1 dpf) and neutrophils (after 33 hours post-fertilization), supporting a relationship between TLR22 expression and the developing innate immune system (gurupalli2020identificationandmolecular pages 96-100).

In response to infection, zebrafish tlr22 gene expression is upregulated during Mycobacterium infection (ghani2024unveilingthemultifaceted pages 8-9). This infection-responsive transcription demonstrates that the gene participates in immune responses to infectious or inflammatory conditions, though upregulation alone does not prove that TLR22 directly recognizes mycobacterial molecules.

Comprehensive adult tissue distribution data and protein-level localization studies for zebrafish TLR22 are lacking in the identified literature. In rainbow trout, TLR22 has been detected in skin CD8+ dendritic cells (ghani2024unveilingthemultifaceted pages 8-9), but equivalent cell-type-specific expression data are not available for zebrafish.

Evolutionary Context and Phylogenetic Distribution

TLR22 belongs to the TLR11 superfamily, which has undergone extensive expansion and diversification in teleost fishes (sousa2022tolllikereceptorevolution pages 11-12, davis2026tolllikereceptorsin pages 14-15, davis2026tolllikereceptorsin pages 13-14). While often described as teleost-specific or fish-specific, recent evidence suggests a more nuanced evolutionary history. TLR22 is more accurately classified as fish/amphibian-specific (davis2026tolllikereceptorsin pages 14-15), and the discovery of a functionally similar TLR22-like receptor in amphioxus (Branchiostoma lanceolatum) suggests that teleost and amphioxus TLR22-like genes may share a common ancestral gene rather than TLR22 arising exclusively within teleosts (ji2018characterizationofthe pages 1-2).

The TLR22 lineage has experienced extensive evolutionary diversification through gene duplication, positive selection, and lineage-specific adaptation:

  1. Gene duplications: Some teleost lineages possess multiple TLR22 paralogs. For example, Atlantic cod shows extreme TLR22 expansion with copies distributed across multiple genomic regions (solbakken2016evolutionaryredesignof pages 10-11, solbakken2016evolutionaryredesignof pages 3-5), while mudskipper (Boleophthalmus pectinirostris) has four tlr22 genes (sousa2022tolllikereceptorevolution pages 11-12).

  2. Positive selection: Diversifying selection has been detected in TLR22, particularly in sites located in loops between leucine-rich repeat elements in the extracellular ligand-binding domain (sousa2022tolllikereceptorevolution pages 11-12, solbakken2016evolutionaryredesignof pages 10-11, solbakken2016evolutionaryredesignof pages 3-5). These positively selected changes in LRR motifs are associated with altered ectodomain structure and functional shifts toward recognizing specific pathogen-associated patterns, such as long-sized dsRNA (sousa2022tolllikereceptorevolution pages 11-12).

  3. Lineage-specific variation: TLR22 has been lost in some teleost lineages; for example, one comparative genomic study reported absence of TLR22 from zebrafish genome assemblies (solbakken2016evolutionaryredesignof pages 7-10), though this conflicts with the existence of the annotated tlr22 gene (A0A2R8RTN4). Such discrepancies may reflect assembly quality, annotation differences, or genuine copy number variation.

The evolutionary pattern of TLR22 reflects adaptation to species-specific pathogen pressures, particularly viral and bacterial challenges encountered in aquatic environments (sousa2022tolllikereceptorevolution pages 11-12, tanekhy2016theroleof pages 1-3). This lineage-specific evolution and adaptive diversification suggest that TLR22 orthologs across different fish species may exhibit variation in ligand specificity, binding affinity, and downstream signaling, emphasizing the importance of species-specific functional validation.

The functional annotation of zebrafish TLR22 (A0A2R8RTN4) currently relies heavily on comparative teleost evidence, structural inference, and orthology-based predictions rather than direct experimental characterization of the zebrafish protein itself. While the broader teleost TLR22 family is reasonably well-characterized, several important gaps remain for zebrafish specifically:

Summary

Zebrafish TLR22 (tlr22, UniProt A0A2R8RTN4) is a pattern recognition receptor belonging to the Toll-like receptor family, specifically the fish/amphibian-specific TLR11 superfamily branch. The protein exhibits canonical TLR architecture with an N-terminal leucine-rich repeat ectodomain for ligand recognition, a transmembrane domain, and a C-terminal TIR signaling domain.

Primary molecular function: TLR22 functions as a receptor for long double-stranded RNA (dsRNA), particularly viral dsRNA and the synthetic analog poly I:C. Recognition is length-dependent, with maximal activity observed for approximately 1,000-bp dsRNA. This function positions TLR22 as a specialized viral nucleic acid sensor.

Subcellular localization: Evidence suggests membrane association, with localization varying by species between cell-surface and intracellular endosomal/lysosomal compartments. Zebrafish-specific localization requires experimental determination.

Signaling and pathways: TLR22 initiates innate immune signaling primarily through MyD88-dependent pathways (though some evidence suggests TRIF involvement), activating NF-κB, interferon regulatory factors, and MAPK cascades. This leads to production of type I interferons and pro-inflammatory cytokines (IL-1, IL-6, TNF-α).

Biological role: The primary function is antiviral innate immunity—detecting viral dsRNA to trigger interferon-dependent antiviral defenses and establish cellular antiviral states. TLR22 expression is developmentally regulated (stabilizing after 1 dpf) and upregulated during infection.

Critical caveat: Most direct functional evidence derives from Japanese pufferfish and other teleost TLR22 orthologs rather than zebrafish A0A2R8RTN4 specifically. Functional annotation is based on orthology inference and requires validation through direct experimental characterization of the zebrafish protein.

(davis2026tolllikereceptorsin pages 5-7, poynter2015sensorsofinfection pages 8-10) Adaptor usage is conflicting and may be species- or assay-dependent. Neither MyD88 nor TICAM-1 is experimentally established for zebrafish A0A2R8RTN4 through receptor-specific interaction or loss-of-function experiments.
Downstream pathways Reported fish-family outputs include type-I interferon and antiviral-gene induction, NF-κB regulation, and MAPK signaling. In orange-spotted grouper, TLR22 was associated with ERK enhancement and modulation of JNK and p38. (davis2026tolllikereceptorsin pages 14-15, poynter2015sensorsofinfection pages 8-10) These findings cannot automatically be assigned to zebrafish. Zebrafish-specific pathway wiring, transcription factors, and cytokine outputs have not been resolved.
Biological processes and immune role The principal inferred role is antiviral innate immunity through detection of viral dsRNA, induction of an interferon-dependent antiviral state, and restriction of dsRNA viruses. Teleost TLR22 expression produced increased interferon activity and protection against birnavirus infection in cell-based experiments. (shan2021identificationandfunctional pages 15-15, aoki2015dnavaccinemediatedinnate pages 5-7) Antiviral function is experimentally strong at the teleost-family level. Evidence connecting TLR22 with dendritic-cell cross-presentation comes from rainbow trout, not zebrafish. (ghani2024unveilingthemultifaceted pages 8-9)
Zebrafish expression pattern Available summaries report low expression before approximately 1 day post-fertilization, followed by comparatively stable expression through 2–7 dpf, coinciding with development of innate immune-cell populations. Zebrafish tlr22 transcription has also been reported to increase after Mycobacterium infection. (gurupalli2020identificationandmolecular pages 96-100, ghani2024unveilingthemultifaceted pages 8-9) Evidence is transcript-level and does not provide a reliable adult tissue atlas, cell-type map, or protein-level localization. Infection-induced transcription does not prove direct recognition of the pathogen.
Evolutionary and taxonomic distribution TLR22 belongs to a lineage-rich aquatic-vertebrate TLR clade that underwent duplications, losses, and positive selection in ligand-facing LRR regions. It is often described as teleost- or fish/amphibian-associated, although a functionally similar TLR22-like receptor in amphioxus suggests deeper ancestry or a related ancestral receptor. (sousa2022tolllikereceptorevolution pages 11-12, ji2018characterizationofthe pages 1-2, davis2026tolllikereceptorsin pages 14-15) Orthology and nomenclature are difficult in this rapidly evolving family. Some comparative genomic studies reported absence of TLR22 from older zebrafish assemblies, conflicting with the supplied gene and accession; accession- and sequence-level identity should therefore take precedence over symbol-only searches. (solbakken2016evolutionaryredesignof pages 7-10, solbakken2016evolutionaryredesignof pages 3-5)
Overall functional annotation Zebrafish TLR22 is most defensibly annotated as a canonical LRR–TIR membrane pattern-recognition receptor predicted by teleost orthology to sense long viral dsRNA and initiate interferon-centered antiviral signaling. Confidence is high for receptor class and domain architecture, moderate for long-dsRNA sensing by orthology, and low to moderate for zebrafish-specific localization, adaptor usage, ligand breadth, and downstream pathway details.

These notes rest on the UniProt record, the GOA rows and the cached publications;
the Falcon deep-research report arrived after the review was written and is
cross-checked at the end of this file. This is the thinnest evidence base of the

The project's question: which pathway term fits TLR22?

GO has numbered pathway terms for TLR1-13, 15 and 21 but none for TLR22. The answer
this review reaches is that GO:0002224 toll-like receptor signaling pathway is
sufficient and no new numbered term should be requested yet. The reasoning:

  1. The numbered terms exist where a receptor's ligand and signalling route are
    established, which is what makes the term more informative than its parent. For
    zebrafish tlr22 neither is established: the ligand is inferred from fugu, and no
    signalling assay has been reported for the zebrafish protein.
  2. If a term were created now it would be defined by receptor name alone, and the
    name is unstable: this gene's own synonym is TLR21.2, the teleost tlr21/tlr22/tlr23
    genes have been renamed and re-partitioned between species, and the PANTHER
    subfamily groups it with TLR13.
  3. A term would be worth requesting once the zebrafish receptor's ligand and adaptor
    are demonstrated — and at that point the useful shape would be a term modelled on
    GO:0035682, with the fugu route (TICAM-1-dependent interferon induction in response
    to long dsRNA) as the template if it proves conserved.

Recorded in suggested_questions rather than in proposed_new_terms, deliberately:
proposing a term now would assert a receptor-specific pathway that nobody has
demonstrated for this species.

Annotation decisions (summary)

  • GO:0002221 pattern recognition receptor signaling pathway (IDA, PMID:22729906)
    modified to GO:0002224 toll-like receptor signaling pathway. The gene is an
    unambiguous member of the family by domain architecture and phylogeny, so the
    TLR-specific child of the annotated term is the right level; this is also the
    concrete answer to the project's "which pathway term" question.
  • GO:0009617 response to bacterium (IDA, PMID:17804254) left UNDECIDED. The cached
    record is abstract-only; the abstract lists tlr22 among genes measured but does not
    report how it behaved, and the genes it singles out as induced are others. The
    ZFIN curator may well have read a figure showing induction, so the row is not
    removed; but the assertion cannot be verified from what is available, which is the
    circumstance the UNDECIDED action exists for.
  • GO:0007165 kept as non-core; GO:0016020 membrane accepted (signal peptide plus a
    predicted transmembrane segment) but deliberately not refined to a specific
    compartment.
  • No NEW annotations. In particular no dsRNA-binding, interferon-pathway or
    cell-surface annotation: all three would rest on the fugu orthologue, and
    cross-species transfer of a ligand specificity is exactly what this project set out
    to scrutinise. They appear instead as the leading suggested experiments.
