| Protein identity | Zebrafish (*Danio rerio*) Delta-like protein A (DeltaA), encoded by *dla*; UniProt Q6DI48. It is a precursor and canonical Delta/Serrate/Lag-2-family Notch ligand. | Confirms that *dla* refers to zebrafish DeltaA and places it in the contact-dependent Notch-ligand family. (pqac-00000009, pqac-00000015) |
| Signal peptide and secretory-pathway targeting | As a precursor membrane protein, DeltaA is synthesized with an N-terminal signal sequence that targets its extracellular region through the secretory pathway. | Enables maturation and delivery to the plasma membrane; it does not imply that mature DeltaA is a freely diffusible ligand. |
| Extracellular N-terminal region | The exposed N terminus precedes the DSL domain and forms part of the receptor-facing extracellular region characteristic of canonical Delta ligands. | Organizes the extracellular ligand-binding surface and positions the DSL and EGF-like modules for interaction with Notch on an adjacent cell. (pqac-00000016) |
| DSL domain (InterPro IPR001774) | Conserved Delta/Serrate/Lag-2 domain in the extracellular portion of DeltaA. | Principal defining module for Notch recognition and activation; identifies DeltaA as a canonical signaling ligand rather than an enzyme or transporter. (pqac-00000015, pqac-00000016) |
| EGF-like repeats (IPR000742; IPR013032) | Multiple extracellular epidermal growth factor-like modules form an elongated, disulfide-stabilized interaction scaffold. Some carry the conserved EGF-like signature. | Support folding and presentation of the receptor-binding region and can modulate ligand–receptor recognition. Delta-family ligands generally possess approximately 6–9 repeats, but the precise Q6DI48 repeat count should be obtained from curated sequence annotation. (pqac-00000015, pqac-00000016) |
| Calcium-binding EGF-like domain (IPR001881) | At least one EGF-like module has sequence features associated with calcium binding. | Calcium binding can stabilize EGF-domain conformation and the extracellular architecture needed for productive receptor engagement. |
| Asp/Asn hydroxylation-site motif (IPR000152) | An EGF-type motif predicted to permit post-translational hydroxylation of an Asp or Asn residue. | Consistent with extracellular EGF-domain maturation, although its modification status and functional effect have not been demonstrated specifically for zebrafish DeltaA. |
| Single-pass transmembrane segment | A hydrophobic membrane-spanning segment anchors DeltaA in the signal-sending cell, with the DSL/EGF region outside the cell and the regulatory tail in the cytoplasm. | Makes signaling contact-dependent and mechanically couples ligand endocytosis in one cell to Notch activation in an adjacent cell. DeltaA is a membrane-tethered ligand, not a catalyst with a biochemical substrate. (pqac-00000002, pqac-00000013) |
| Intracellular cytoplasmic domain | The cytosolic tail is accessible to ubiquitin ligases and trafficking machinery. Delta-family tails generally contain lysines and ligase-recognition elements, but DeltaA-specific residue-level interfaces remain insufficiently mapped. | Controls ubiquitination, endocytosis, recycling, and signaling competence. Residue-level mechanisms established in other Delta proteins should not automatically be assigned to zebrafish DeltaA. (pqac-00000017, pqac-00000043) |
| Plasma-membrane and cortical localization | DeltaA occurs in puncta or short lines at the plasma membrane and cell cortex, often at apparent interfaces between neighboring neural cells; it can overlap with cortical beta-catenin labeling. | Places the functional ligand at cell–cell contacts where it can activate Notch in trans and argues against DeltaA acting primarily as a soluble secreted protein. (pqac-00000008, pqac-00000009, pqac-00000011) |
| Endosomal trafficking | DeltaA is internalized from the cell surface and can occur in intracellular puncta; disruption of trafficking or relevant receptor interactions produces plasma-membrane accumulation. | Regulates surface availability and provides the pulling force that exposes activating cleavage sites in receptor-bound Notch. (pqac-00000003, pqac-00000006, pqac-00000046) |
| Receptor partners | Trafficking evidence implicates Notch1a and Notch3 in DeltaA interactions. Genetic evidence functionally associates Notch1a, Notch1b, and Notch3 with DeltaA-dependent V2 progenitor maintenance, with Notch1a dominant in the V2a/V2b decision. | Supports context-dependent, combinatorial receptor use rather than one proven exclusive receptor or a defined biochemical affinity hierarchy. (pqac-00000051, pqac-00000052, pqac-00000053) |
| Mind bomb 1 (Mib1) regulation | Mib1 is a RING-family E3 ubiquitin ligase that promotes DeltaA ubiquitination-dependent internalization; loss of Mib1 causes excessive DeltaA accumulation at the cell surface. | Produces signaling-competent ligand trafficking. Internalization of receptor-bound ligand exerts force on Notch, enabling S2 cleavage, gamma-secretase-mediated S3 cleavage, and release of the Notch intracellular domain. (pqac-00000043, pqac-00000044, pqac-00000046) |
| Canonical signaling output | Following trans interaction with DeltaA, the neighboring cell releases the Notch intracellular domain, which enters the nucleus and activates CSL/RBPJ-associated transcription, including HES/Her repressors of proneural programs. | Connects DeltaA receptor binding and trafficking to lateral inhibition, precursor maintenance, and context-specific cell-fate decisions. (pqac-00000018, pqac-00000045) |
| Cis versus trans behavior | Productive activation normally occurs in trans between adjacent cells; ligand and receptor coexpressed in one cell may interact in cis and undergo co-internalization. | Trans binding activates neighboring-cell Notch, whereas cis interaction can reduce surface availability and sharpen sender–receiver asymmetry. DeltaA-specific cis mechanisms remain less firmly resolved than its trans-ligand role. (pqac-00000002, pqac-00000049) |
