# Comprehensive Research Report: Zebrafish Toll-like Receptor 5b (tlr5b)

## Gene Identity and Overview

The *tlr5b* gene (UniProt: A0ACM8R384) encodes Toll-like receptor 5b precursor in zebrafish (*Danio rerio*), a member of the Toll-like receptor family of pattern recognition receptors (PRRs) (vaart2012pathogenrecognitionand pages 1-2, meijer2004expressionanalysisof pages 1-2). The protein contains characteristic structural features including leucine-rich repeats (LRRs) in the extracellular domain, a single transmembrane segment, and a cytoplasmic Toll/interleukin-1 receptor (TIR) signaling domain, consistent with the InterPro domains reported in the UniProt entry (meijer2004expressionanalysisof pages 1-2). Zebrafish possess two TLR5 paralogs, TLR5a and TLR5b, which arose through gene duplication during teleost evolution (voogdt2018duplicatedtlr5of pages 1-1, voogdt2018duplicatedtlr5of pages 6-7, meijer2004expressionanalysisof pages 9-10).

## Primary Molecular Function and Ligand Specificity

### Bacterial Flagellin Recognition

TLR5b functions as a non-enzymatic pattern recognition receptor whose primary substrate specificity is bacterial flagellin, the major structural protein of bacterial flagella (voogdt2018duplicatedtlr5of pages 1-1, yang2002tolllikereceptorsignaling pages 12-14, voogdt2018duplicatedtlr5of pages 1-2). Rather than catalyzing a biochemical reaction, TLR5b acts as a molecular sensor that recognizes this conserved pathogen-associated molecular pattern (PAMP). The receptor has been experimentally demonstrated to respond to flagellins from multiple bacterial species, including *Salmonella enterica* serovar Enteritidis and *Pseudomonas aeruginosa* (voogdt2018duplicatedtlr5of pages 1-1, voogdt2018duplicatedtlr5of pages 1-2).

The C-terminal region of flagellin directly contacts TLR5, and sequence variations in this region can influence the strength of receptor activation (yang2002tolllikereceptorsignaling pages 12-14). Different flagellar serotypes activate zebrafish TLR5 to varying degrees, indicating that ligand recognition involves specific structural requirements within the flagellin molecule (yang2002tolllikereceptorsignaling pages 12-14).

### Unique Heterodimeric Receptor Configuration

A defining characteristic that distinguishes zebrafish TLR5b from mammalian TLR5 is its requirement for heterodimerization with TLR5a to achieve functional flagellin signaling (voogdt2018duplicatedtlr5of pages 6-7, voogdt2018duplicatedtlr5of pages 5-5, voogdt2018duplicatedtlr5of pages 1-1). This represents a fundamental departure from the canonical TLR5 paradigm observed in mammals, birds, and reptiles, where TLR5 functions as a homodimer (voogdt2018duplicatedtlr5of pages 1-1, voogdt2018duplicatedtlr5of pages 6-7).

Extensive experimental evidence demonstrates that full-length TLR5b alone cannot activate NF-κB signaling in response to flagellin stimulation, even when expressed with the trafficking chaperone UNC93B1 (voogdt2018duplicatedtlr5of pages 1-2, voogdt2018duplicatedtlr5of pages 5-5). Instead, productive receptor activation requires the complementary pairing of TLR5a and TLR5b ectodomains and intracellular domains (voogdt2018duplicatedtlr5of pages 5-6, voogdt2018duplicatedtlr5of pages 6-7). Domain-swapping experiments revealed that while the N-terminal ectodomain of TLR5b can bind flagellin in a homodimeric configuration, the cytoplasmic domains must be heterodimeric for signal transduction (voogdt2018duplicatedtlr5of pages 6-7).

This obligate heterodimerization reflects subfunctionalization following gene duplication, where the two paralogs have undergone concerted coevolution to create complementary, non-interchangeable roles in a unique flagellin-sensing receptor complex (voogdt2018duplicatedtlr5of pages 1-1, voogdt2018duplicatedtlr5of pages 6-7). Morpholino-mediated knockdown of either *tlr5a* or *tlr5b* individually blocks flagellin-induced inflammatory gene activation, confirming their functional interdependence in vivo (yang2015commonandspecific pages 2-4, yang2015commonandspecific pages 1-2).

### Emerging Evidence for Expanded Ligand Recognition

Recent studies in related cyprinid fish have revealed unexpected ligand versatility in TLR5 paralogs. In grass carp (*Ctenopharyngodon idella*), CiTLR5b homodimers were shown to recognize double-stranded RNA (dsRNA), including the synthetic analog poly(I:C), and to activate interferon-responsive elements during viral infection (weber2022fishtlr5develops pages 1-2, weber2022fishtlr5develops pages 2-3). This dsRNA recognition occurred independently of the flagellin-sensing TLR5a/TLR5b heterodimeric configuration and involved distinct binding sites from those used for flagellin recognition (weber2022fishtlr5develops pages 1-2).

However, this dual-ligand capability has not been directly demonstrated for zebrafish TLR5b, and functional differences between fish species necessitate caution in extrapolating these findings (weber2022fishtlr5develops pages 1-2, davis2026tolllikereceptorsin pages 12-13, voogdt2018duplicatedtlr5of pages 1-1). The canonical and experimentally validated function of zebrafish TLR5b remains bacterial flagellin recognition through heterodimerization with TLR5a (voogdt2018duplicatedtlr5of pages 1-1).

## Cellular Localization and Trafficking

### Intracellular Vesicular Localization

Subcellular localization studies using heterologous expression systems revealed that zebrafish TLR5b predominantly accumulates in LAMP-1-positive vesicular compartments, corresponding to late endosomes or lysosomes (voogdt2018duplicatedtlr5of pages 3-4). This intracellular localization contrasts with mammalian TLR5, which is typically found at the plasma membrane with tissue-specific polarization—apical in respiratory epithelium and basolateral in intestinal epithelium (yang2017tlr5beyondthe pages 1-2).

The intracellular localization of TLR5b was not altered by flagellin stimulation, suggesting a constitutive vesicular residence rather than ligand-induced trafficking (voogdt2018duplicatedtlr5of pages 3-4). This localization pattern is consistent with TLR5b being a transmembrane protein that requires proper cellular trafficking for function but raises questions about how extracellular bacterial flagellin accesses the receptor in these compartments.

### Role of UNC93B1 Chaperone

The trafficking chaperone UNC93B1 plays a critical role in TLR5b function by facilitating receptor redistribution and enabling productive heterodimerization with TLR5a (voogdt2018duplicatedtlr5of pages 1-1, voogdt2018duplicatedtlr5of pages 3-4). UNC93B1 expression enhances flagellin-induced NF-κB activation in cell-based assays, indicating that proper receptor trafficking is essential for signal transduction (voogdt2018duplicatedtlr5of pages 1-1). This chaperone-dependent mechanism suggests that TLR5b subcellular localization is actively regulated and contributes to the specificity of immune responses.

## Signaling Pathways and Downstream Responses

### MyD88-Dependent Canonical Pathway

TLR5b activates innate immune signaling primarily through the myeloid differentiation primary response protein MyD88 (li2017patternrecognitionreceptors pages 2-3, li2017patternrecognitionreceptors pages 1-2, stockhammer2009transcriptomeprofilingand pages 2-3). Following ligand-induced conformational changes in the TLR5a–TLR5b heterodimer, the cytoplasmic TIR domains recruit MyD88, which in turn engages IRAK family kinases (li2017patternrecognitionreceptors pages 2-3, yang2002tolllikereceptorsignaling pages 1-6). This initiates a signaling cascade involving TRAF6, the TAK1/TAB complex, and ultimately the IKK complex (li2017patternrecognitionreceptors pages 2-3, yang2002tolllikereceptorsignaling pages 1-6).

The signaling pathway bifurcates into two major branches:

**NF-κB Activation:** The IKK complex phosphorylates IκB proteins, leading to their degradation and allowing NF-κB family members (Rel, RelA, RelB, NF-κB2) to translocate to the nucleus (stockhammer2009transcriptomeprofilingand pages 11-12, voogdt2018duplicatedtlr5of pages 5-5). Reconstitution experiments with TLR5a and TLR5b in reporter cell lines directly demonstrated NF-κB luciferase activity in response to flagellin (voogdt2018duplicatedtlr5of pages 5-5, voogdt2018duplicatedtlr5of pages 6-7).

**MAPK/AP-1 Activation:** The TAK1 complex also activates mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK) cascades, including ERK, JNK, and p38, which converge on AP-1 transcription factors (Fos, Jun, ATF family members) (stockhammer2009transcriptomeprofilingand pages 11-12, yang2002tolllikereceptorsignaling pages 1-6). Infection and flagellin stimulation induce expression of *fos*, *jun*, *atf3*, *atf4*, and *atf5* in zebrafish embryos (stockhammer2009transcriptomeprofilingand pages 11-12).

### Downstream Target Genes and Biological Processes

Functional genomic studies using morpholino-mediated TLR5a/b knockdown combined with flagellin challenge have identified specific downstream target genes:

**Pro-inflammatory Cytokines:** *il1b* (interleukin-1β) and *il8* (CXCL8/interleukin-8) are rapidly induced by flagellin in a TLR5-dependent manner (stockhammer2009transcriptomeprofilingand pages 11-12, yang2015commonandspecific pages 2-4). TLR5a/b depletion significantly reduced but did not completely abolish these responses, indicating both TLR5-dependent and TLR5-independent recognition of flagellated bacteria (stockhammer2009transcriptomeprofilingand pages 11-12, stockhammer2009transcriptomeprofilingand pages 11-11).

**Chemokines:** *cxcl-C1c* is strongly induced by flagellin, and its activation is almost completely dependent on TLR5a/b, as demonstrated by morpholino knockdown experiments (stockhammer2009transcriptomeprofilingand pages 11-12).

**Matrix Metalloproteinases:** *mmp9* (matrix metalloproteinase 9) is among the most consistently induced genes across multiple infection models and flagellin stimulations (stockhammer2009transcriptomeprofilingand pages 11-12, stockhammer2009transcriptomeprofilingand pages 12-13). TLR5a/b knockdown nearly abolished *mmp9* induction, establishing it as a bona fide TLR5 target gene (stockhammer2009transcriptomeprofilingand pages 11-12). Mmp9 likely contributes to inflammatory cell recruitment and tissue remodeling during bacterial infection.

**Negative Feedback Regulators:** *irak3*, encoding a putative negative regulator of TLR signaling, is induced in a TLR5a/b-dependent manner, suggesting an intrinsic feedback mechanism to limit inflammatory responses (stockhammer2009transcriptomeprofilingand pages 11-12, stockhammer2009transcriptomeprofilingand pages 10-11).

Additional genes induced by flagellin or bacterial infection include *tnfa* (tumor necrosis factor α), NF-κB pathway components (*nfkbiaa*, *nfkbiab*), and signaling intermediates (*traf1*, *pik3cg*, *socs* family members) (stockhammer2009transcriptomeprofilingand pages 11-12, yang2015commonandspecific pages 2-4).

### MyD88-Dependent and -Independent Gene Regulation

Morpholino experiments targeting MyD88 revealed pathway-specific gene regulation. Genes such as *il1b*, *mmp9*, and *irak3* require MyD88 for induction, confirming their position in the canonical TLR5–MyD88 pathway (stockhammer2009transcriptomeprofilingand pages 2-3). However, *ifn1* (type I interferon) and, to some extent, *il8* can be induced during *Salmonella* infection independently of MyD88, suggesting the existence of MyD88-independent signaling branches or compensatory pathways during whole-bacterium infection (stockhammer2009transcriptomeprofilingand pages 2-3).

## Role in Bacterial Infection and Host Defense

### Response to Salmonella and Other Pathogens

Both *tlr5a* and *tlr5b* are upregulated during *Salmonella typhimurium* infection in zebrafish embryos (stockhammer2009transcriptomeprofilingand pages 12-13, stockhammer2009transcriptomeprofilingand pages 11-12, yang2015commonandspecific pages 2-4). Interestingly, this upregulation occurs with both wild-type flagellated strains and nonflagellated LPS mutants, indicating that TLR5 gene expression during infection is induced by broader infection-associated signals beyond direct flagellin recognition (stockhammer2009transcriptomeprofilingand pages 12-13).

Functional studies using morpholino-mediated TLR5a/b knockdown followed by bacterial challenge or purified flagellin injection demonstrated that these receptors are required for robust inflammatory responses to flagellated bacteria (stockhammer2009transcriptomeprofilingand pages 10-11, stockhammer2009transcriptomeprofilingand pages 11-12). However, during intact bacterial infection, TLR5a/b knockdown did not completely prevent inflammatory gene induction, likely because multiple PRRs (TLR2, TLR4, and others) can recognize different PAMPs from Gram-negative bacteria (stockhammer2009transcriptomeprofilingand pages 11-12, stockhammer2009transcriptomeprofilingand pages 11-11).

TLR5 expression has been documented in zebrafish during mycobacterial infection and is induced in response to *Vibrio anguillarum* challenge, suggesting a broad role in recognition of diverse flagellated bacterial pathogens (tanekhy2016theroleof pages 8-10, tanekhy2016theroleof pages 13-14, meijer2004expressionanalysisof pages 1-2). The receptor likely contributes to early antibacterial innate immunity, particularly in mucosal tissues where flagellated bacteria are commonly encountered (yang2002tolllikereceptorsignaling pages 12-14).

## Evolutionary Origin and Comparative Biology

### Teleost-Specific Gene Duplication

Zebrafish TLR5a and TLR5b arose through gene duplication during teleost fish evolution (voogdt2018duplicatedtlr5of pages 1-1, meijer2004expressionanalysisof pages 9-10). Phylogenetic and synteny analyses support a tandem duplication model, where an ancestral *TLR5* gene was duplicated to produce two closely linked paralogs (voogdt2018duplicatedtlr5of pages 6-7, zhang2023genomewideidentificationand pages 5-8). This duplication is distinct from the broader teleost-specific whole-genome duplication (3R) but may have been facilitated by the genomic plasticity characteristic of teleost genomes (zhang2023genomewideidentificationand pages 5-8, zhang2023genomewideidentificationand pages 1-2).

Non-teleost vertebrates, including mammals, birds, reptiles, and the spotted gar (a basal ray-finned fish), retain only a single *TLR5* ortholog that functions as a homodimeric receptor (voogdt2018duplicatedtlr5of pages 1-1, voogdt2018duplicatedtlr5of pages 6-7). The retention of both TLR5 duplicates in zebrafish, rather than loss of one copy, resulted from subfunctionalization through the duplication-degeneration-complementation (DDC) process (voogdt2018duplicatedtlr5of pages 6-7). Each paralog acquired complementary, non-redundant functions, such that both are now required for effective flagellin sensing.

### Functional Divergence in Fish TLR5 Paralogs

Genome-wide surveys of fish TLR5 genes reveal extensive diversity across teleost lineages, reflecting different evolutionary trajectories following whole-genome duplications and lineage-specific gene losses (zhang2023genomewideidentificationand pages 5-8, zhang2023genomewideidentificationand pages 1-2, zhang2023genomewideidentificationand pages 8-10). Some fish species possess membrane-bound TLR5 (TLR5M) and soluble TLR5 (TLR5S) forms, while others retain multiple membrane-bound paralogs designated TLR5a, TLR5b, and additional copies (TLR5Ma, TLR5Mb1, TLR5Mb2) (zhang2023genomewideidentificationand pages 5-8).

Differences at flagellin-interaction sites and potential viral-binding sites among TLR5 isoforms suggest lineage-specific functional divergence and adaptation to distinct pathogen pressures (zhang2023genomewideidentificationand pages 5-8, zhang2023genomewideidentificationand pages 2-3). The discovery that grass carp TLR5b can recognize both flagellin (as a heterodimer) and dsRNA (as a homodimer) exemplifies this functional versatility, though whether such dual ligand recognition represents an ancestral feature or a derived innovation remains unclear (weber2022fishtlr5develops pages 1-2, weber2022fishtlr5develops pages 2-3).

## Experimental Approaches and Current Limitations

### Loss-of-Function Studies

The primary experimental approach for investigating TLR5b function in zebrafish has been transient morpholino-mediated knockdown (stockhammer2009transcriptomeprofilingand pages 3-4, yang2015commonandspecific pages 2-4, stockhammer2009transcriptomeprofilingand pages 4-4). Translation-blocking morpholinos targeting the 5' untranslated region of *tlr5a* and *tlr5b* mRNA have been injected into zebrafish embryos, either separately or in combination, followed by assessment of inflammatory gene expression after flagellin challenge or bacterial infection (stockhammer2009transcriptomeprofilingand pages 3-4, stockhammer2009transcriptomeprofilingand pages 11-12, yang2015commonandspecific pages 1-2).

These studies consistently demonstrate that knockdown of either *tlr5a* or *tlr5b* prevents flagellin-induced activation of target genes such as *il1b*, *mmp9*, and *cxcl-C1c*, supporting the functional interdependence of the two paralogs (yang2015commonandspecific pages 2-4, stockhammer2009transcriptomeprofilingand pages 11-12, stockhammer2009transcriptomeprofilingand pages 10-11). Standard control morpholinos have been used to control for nonspecific effects (stockhammer2009transcriptomeprofilingand pages 3-4, stockhammer2009transcriptomeprofilingand pages 4-4).

However, morpholino approaches have inherent limitations, including potential off-target effects, variable knockdown efficiency, and inability to assess long-term developmental compensation. To date, stable CRISPR/Cas9-mediated *tlr5b* knockout lines have not been reported in the literature examined, representing an important gap in the functional characterization (stockhammer2009transcriptomeprofilingand pages 4-4, voogdt2018duplicatedtlr5of pages 6-7).

### Gain-of-Function and Structure–Function Studies

Complementary gain-of-function approaches have employed overexpression of wild-type, chimeric, and mutant TLR5 constructs in heterologous cell lines (typically HeLa or HEK293 cells) coupled with NF-κB luciferase reporter assays (voogdt2018duplicatedtlr5of pages 5-6, voogdt2018duplicatedtlr5of pages 5-5, voogdt2018duplicatedtlr5of pages 6-7). These studies have been instrumental in defining the heterodimeric architecture of zebrafish TLR5, revealing that productive signaling requires specific combinations of TLR5a and TLR5b extracellular and intracellular regions (voogdt2018duplicatedtlr5of pages 5-6, voogdt2018duplicatedtlr5of pages 6-7).

Domain-swapping experiments demonstrated that the N-terminal ectodomain of TLR5b can function in homodimeric configurations within chimeric receptors, whereas full-length native TLR5b requires heterodimerization (voogdt2018duplicatedtlr5of pages 3-4, voogdt2018duplicatedtlr5of pages 6-7). These structure–function studies have provided mechanistic insights complementary to in vivo knockdown experiments.

## Recent Developments and Current Understanding (2022-2026)

### Genome-Wide Characterization

A comprehensive genome-wide analysis by Zhang et al. (2023) characterized TLR5 diversity across fish species, identifying three TLR5M isotypes (TLR5Ma, TLR5Mb1, TLR5Mb2) and TLR5S forms resulting from various combinations of whole-genome duplications and gene losses (zhang2023genomewideidentificationand pages 5-8, zhang2023genomewideidentificationand pages 1-2). This work established that TLR5 copy-number variation in fishes reflects both WGD events and lineage-specific adaptations, with functional divergence at pathogen-recognition sites suggesting subfunctionalization or neofunctionalization (zhang2023genomewideidentificationand pages 5-8, zhang2023genomewideidentificationand pages 8-10).

### Expanded Ligand Recognition

Weber (2022) highlighted the discovery that grass carp TLR5b can sense viral dsRNA in addition to bacterial flagellin, representing a significant expansion of TLR5 function beyond its traditional role (weber2022fishtlr5develops pages 1-2, weber2022fishtlr5develops pages 2-3). This finding challenges the established paradigm that TLR5 exclusively recognizes flagellin and raises questions about evolutionary constraints on ligand specificity. Whether zebrafish TLR5b possesses similar dual recognition capacity awaits experimental validation (weber2022fishtlr5develops pages 1-2, davis2026tolllikereceptorsin pages 12-13).

### Applications in Aquaculture and Vaccine Development

Davis et al. (2026) reviewed the translational potential of fish TLRs, including TLR5, for adjuvant-based vaccine design in aquaculture (davis2026tolllikereceptorsin pages 12-13). Flagellin's potent immunostimulatory properties through TLR5 activation make it an attractive vaccine adjuvant candidate for enhancing protective immunity against bacterial and viral pathogens in farmed fish (davis2026tolllikereceptorsin pages 12-13). The heterodimeric requirement for TLR5a/TLR5b in zebrafish suggests that both receptor components must be considered in designing TLR5-targeted immunotherapies.

Productive flagellin recognition and signaling require a complementary TLR5a–TLR5b heterodimer; isolated TLR5b N-terminal ectodomain regions can support a homodimeric arrangement in engineered constructs, but the complete intracellular signaling configuration must include contributions from both paralogs (voogdt2018duplicatedtlr5of pages 1-1, voogdt2018duplicatedtlr5of pages 5-6, voogdt2018duplicatedtlr5of pages 6-7). |
| Cellular localization | An integral membrane protein. In heterologous-cell imaging experiments, zebrafish TLR5b accumulated principally in LAMP-1-positive late-endosomal/lysosomal vesicles rather than predominantly at the plasma membrane. The trafficking chaperone UNC93B1 promoted receptor redistribution and functional association with TLR5a; this localization should be interpreted as direct cell-model evidence, not yet a complete in-vivo zebrafish tissue map (voogdt2018duplicatedtlr5of pages 3-4, voogdt2018duplicatedtlr5of pages 1-1). |
| Key structural domains | N-terminal signal peptide/precursor organization; extracellular leucine-rich repeats and LRR C-terminal cysteine-rich flanking region for ligand recognition; one transmembrane helix; and a cytosolic Toll/interleukin-1 receptor (TIR) domain for adaptor recruitment and signaling. This architecture is characteristic of TLR proteins and agrees with the supplied InterPro assignments IPR001611, IPR003591, IPR032675, IPR000483, and IPR000157 (vaart2012pathogenrecognitionand pages 1-2, meijer2004expressionanalysisof pages 1-2). |
| Evolutionary origin | TLR5a and TLR5b are distinct paralogs rather than splice isoforms. Recent comparative phylogenetic and synteny analysis favors a tandem duplication of an ancestral membrane-form *TLR5* gene for the zebrafish M/M pair, followed by subfunctionalization and concerted coevolution into a complementary heterodimer; broader teleost TLR5 diversity also reflects whole-genome duplication and gene loss (voogdt2018duplicatedtlr5of pages 6-7, zhang2023genomewideidentificationand pages 5-8, zhang2023genomewideidentificationand pages 8-10). |

TLR5b represents a specialized innate immune receptor in zebrafish that has evolved a unique heterodimeric configuration with TLR5a for bacterial flagellin recognition. This obligate heterodimerization, intracellular vesicular localization, and dependence on UNC93B1 trafficking distinguish zebrafish TLR5b from its mammalian TLR5 counterpart. The receptor activates MyD88-dependent signaling cascades culminating in NF-κB and AP-1 transcription factors, which drive expression of pro-inflammatory cytokines, chemokines, and antimicrobial effectors critical for host defense against flagellated bacteria.

Recent discoveries of potential dual-ligand recognition capabilities in related fish species, combined with comprehensive evolutionary analyses revealing extensive TLR5 diversification across teleosts, suggest that fish TLR5 paralogs may possess broader functional versatility than previously appreciated. Future studies employing stable genetic knockouts, tissue-specific expression mapping, and direct ligand-binding assays will be essential for fully elucidating TLR5b's role in zebrafish immunity and its potential applications in aquaculture disease management.
