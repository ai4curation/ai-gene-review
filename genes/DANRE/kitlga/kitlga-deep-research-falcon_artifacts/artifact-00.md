| Annotation aspect | Best-supported conclusion | Direct evidence/method | Evidence strength/caveat |
|---|---|---|---|
| Identity and paralogy | **kitlga**, historically **kitla**, is the *Danio rerio* Kit-ligand-a co-ortholog of mammalian **KITLG/SCF** and is distinct from zebrafish **kitlgb**. The two paralogs arose through teleost duplication; reported sequence identity is approximately 30%. | Cloning from 24-hpf cDNA, 5′/3′ RACE, phylogenetic analysis, chromosome mapping, and paralog-specific expression and loss-of-function studies. (pqac-00000001, pqac-00000002) | **Strong.** Exact-organism molecular and functional evidence supports the identification. The literature does not independently map every experiment to UniProt Q56JH6, but the gene name and aliases match that record. |
| Protein class and location | Kitlga is an **SCF-family cytokine ligand**, not an enzyme or transporter. It is synthesized as a membrane-associated ligand and can be released extracellularly; alternative transcripts differ around exon 6 and cleavage-associated sequence. Its operative compartment is therefore the producing-cell surface and extracellular or paracrine space. | Sequence and splice-form analysis found exon-6-containing Kitlga transcripts and predicted membrane-cleavage sites; recombinant soluble extracellular Kitlga was biologically active in cell and marrow assays. (pqac-00000002, pqac-00000005, pqac-00000017) | **Moderate to strong.** Cytokine identity and soluble activity are experimentally supported, but direct zebrafish protein-topology imaging, cleavage-site mutagenesis, and endogenous protein-localization data are limited. The four-helix cytokine and SCF-domain assignment is chiefly sequence-based. |
| Receptor specificity and pathway | Kitlga preferentially activates the receptor tyrosine kinase **Kita**; Kitlgb preferentially activates **Kitb**. Kitlga–Kita signaling can activate **MAPK/ERK**, although weak Kitb cross-activation is possible in ovarian assays. | Recombinant ligands, Kita or Kitb expression in COS cells, receptor-phosphorylation Western blots, MAPK assays, and denuded-oocyte or follicle-cell assays established preferential Kitlga-to-Kita and Kitlgb-to-Kitb signaling. (pqac-00000003, pqac-00000004) | **Strong for preferential, not absolute, specificity.** Genetic Kita dependence in pigmentation and erythropoiesis independently supports the biochemical result. |
| Embryonic melanocytes | The principal established developmental function is **Kita-dependent control of melanocyte number, migration, growth, and survival**. Signaling is required before 2 dpf mainly for migration and after 2 dpf for survival. | kitlga, but not kitlgb, morphants phenocopied kita loss; low-dose kitlga knockdown genetically enhanced a sensitized kita allele; kitlga overexpression caused Kita-dependent hyperpigmentation through increased melanocyte number and size. In situ hybridization placed kitlga along the medial trunk migration route at 18–30 hpf and in skin at 4–5 dpf. (pqac-00000000, pqac-00000002, pqac-00000005) | **Strong.** Convergent expression, loss-of-function, genetic-interaction, overexpression, and receptor-dependence evidence assign this role directly to kitlga rather than kitlgb. |
| Adult melanocyte regeneration and RAS–MAPK | Injury-induced kitlga–Kita signaling drives melanocyte-progenitor differentiation, especially direct differentiation, and increases progenitor ERK activity. Kita loss caused **more than an eightfold reduction in direct differentiation** and an approximately **twofold reduction in asymmetric divisions**; kitlga mutants showed similar defects. | scRNA-seq and NicheNetR, three-replicate qRT-PCR, mutant analysis, longitudinal single-cell imaging, melanocyte-lineage ERKKTR imaging, and downstream BRAFV600E rescue were used. After ablation, kitlga rose transiently in skin; ERK activation failed in kita mutants, while BRAFV600E restored regeneration toward wild-type levels. Imaging cohorts were WT n=10, kita(lf) n=9, and kitlga(lf) n=11. (pqac-00000008, pqac-00000009, pqac-00000010, pqac-00000012) | **Strong and recent (2023).** Wild type restored the full stripe complement within two weeks, whereas kita mutants regenerated a mean 51.4%; an earlier report gave 65.2% for kitlga mutants. Exact kitlga transcript fold change and ERK-sensor magnitude were unavailable in the extracted evidence. (pqac-00000011, pqac-00000012) |
| Ovarian localization and function | In preovulatory follicles, **kitlga is produced by somatic follicle cells**, whereas **Kita is oocyte-localized**, establishing a follicle-cell-to-oocyte paracrine axis. Kitlga activates oocyte MAPK and suppresses spontaneous oocyte maturation in vitro. | RT-PCR and qPCR across follicular stages, spatial localization, recombinant-ligand assays, receptor phosphorylation, MAPK measurement, and germinal-vesicle-breakdown assays were used. Expression rose from primary to secondary growth, peaked in early vitellogenesis, then declined and was marginal in ovulated oocytes lacking follicle cells. (pqac-00000001, pqac-00000003, pqac-00000004) | **Strong for cell-type localization and in vitro signaling; moderate for whole-animal physiological necessity.** Kitlga did not significantly enhance DHP-induced maturation, unlike Kitlgb, demonstrating paralog specialization. |
| Erythroid progenitors and Epo | Kitlga cooperates with **erythropoietin (Epo)** to expand erythroid progenitors and requires **Kita** for this response. It acts mainly as a growth or survival co-signal rather than as an enzyme or transporter. | Adult whole-kidney-marrow cultures started at 3×10⁶ cells/ml and were maintained at 2×10⁶ cells/ml; Epo and Kitlga were tested at 100 ng/ml. Kitlga alone had a mild nonsignificant effect, whereas Kitlga plus Epo significantly increased day-7 cell numbers over Epo alone; dexamethasone further enhanced expansion. Embryonic mRNA injection, lcr:EGFP FACS, benzidine staining, gata1a and hbbe1 qPCR, and sparse or kita mutants showed loss of Epo–Kit-ligand cooperation without Kita. (pqac-00000006, pqac-00000007, pqac-00000016, pqac-00000017, pqac-00000018) | **Moderate.** Results combine ex vivo and in vivo evidence but derive from a 2020 bioRxiv preprint; exact effect sizes and P values were unavailable in the extracted text. Soluble and full-length ligand forms differ, and Kitlgb also contributes in vivo. |
| HSC signaling: distinction from Kitlgb and Kitb | kitlga should **not** be annotated as the principal embryonic HSC-specification ligand. Evidence instead assigns that role mainly to **Kitlgb–Kitb**, while kitlga–Kita is dominant in pigmentation and contributes to erythropoiesis. | Injected kitlga mRNA did not alter cmyb at 4 dpf; kitb knockdown reduced aortic runx1 and cmyb and caudal-hematopoietic-tissue cmyb. Combined subthreshold kitlgb and osm increased cmyb (p=0.0001), and combined kitb and osmr inhibition reduced HSC markers (p=0.0000009). (pqac-00000019, pqac-00000020) | **Strong caution against paralog conflation.** Low Kita expression in HSCs and possible receptor cross-activation mean a minor or context-dependent kitlga contribution cannot be excluded, but direct evidence favors Kitlgb–Kitb for embryonic HSC control. |
