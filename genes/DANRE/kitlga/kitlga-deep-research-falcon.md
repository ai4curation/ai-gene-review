# Functional annotation report: *Danio rerio kitlga* (UniProt Q56JH6)

## Executive conclusion

The requested identity is verified: **Q56JH6 corresponds to zebrafish Kit ligand a, encoded by *kitlga* (historical alias *kitla*)**, not the paralog *kitlgb* and not a similarly named gene from another organism. It is a co-ortholog of mammalian **KITLG/stem cell factor (SCF)** generated by teleost gene duplication. Cloning, phylogenetic analysis, chromosome mapping, expression, and paralog-specific genetic experiments all support this assignment. The literature therefore agrees with the supplied UniProt description and with assignment to the SCF family and four-helix-cytokine-like structural class. (yao2010kitsystemin pages 2-3, hultman2007geneduplicationof pages 2-3)

Functionally, Kitlga is a **signaling cytokine rather than an enzyme, transporter, or structural protein**. Its best-established role is to activate the receptor tyrosine kinase **Kita** on neighboring target cells. This promotes embryonic melanocyte migration and survival and, after pigment-cell injury, activates RAS–MAPK/ERK signaling in melanocyte progenitors to drive regeneration. Additional context-dependent functions include a follicle-cell-to-oocyte signal in the ovary and cooperation with erythropoietin in erythroid progenitor expansion. (hultman2007geneduplicationof pages 1-2, yao2013spatialdistributionand pages 1-2, frantz2023pigmentcellprogenitor pages 11-13, oltova2020zebrafishkitligands pages 4-7)

## 1. Identity, nomenclature, and evolutionary context

Zebrafish retained two Kit-ligand genes following teleost duplication: ***kitlga/kitla*** and ***kitlgb/kitlb***. The corresponding receptors are **Kita** and **Kitb**. The two ligand paralogs have only approximately 30% sequence identity and have undergone substantial functional partitioning. *kitlga* was cloned from 24-hours-post-fertilization zebrafish cDNA using RACE and exon-spanning PCR; phylogenetic analysis identified both zebrafish proteins as Kit-ligand co-orthologs, with *kitlga* and *kitlgb* located on chromosomes 25 and 4, respectively. (yao2010kitsystemin pages 2-3, hultman2007geneduplicationof pages 2-3)

This distinction is critical. Much of the pigmentation evidence belongs specifically to **Kitlga–Kita**, whereas embryonic hematopoietic-stem-cell regulation has been assigned predominantly to **Kitlgb–Kitb**. Results for mammalian KITLG or zebrafish Kitlgb should not automatically be transferred to Q56JH6. (hultman2007geneduplicationof pages 1-2, mahony2018oncostatinmand pages 1-2, mahony2018oncostatinmand pages 8-11)

## 2. Molecular function and cellular location

### Ligand activity

Kitlga is an SCF-family cytokine that binds and activates a Kit-family receptor tyrosine kinase. Recombinant-ligand experiments, receptor-expression assays in COS cells, receptor-phosphorylation Western blots, and MAPK assays demonstrate a preferred pairing of **Kitlga with Kita**. Kitlgb preferentially activates Kitb. The specificity is not necessarily absolute: weak Kitlga activation of Kitb was observed in ovarian follicle-cell assays, but the dominant biochemical and genetic relationship is Kitlga→Kita. (yao2013spatialdistributionand pages 1-2, yao2013spatialdistributionand pages 13-14)

Upon Kita activation, the receptor initiates intracellular signaling that includes **RAS–RAF–MEK–ERK/MAPK**. In adult melanocyte regeneration, live ERK-reporter imaging showed that injury normally increases ERK activity in progenitors; this response failed in *kita* loss-of-function animals. Expression of constitutively active BRAFV600E downstream of Kita restored regeneration toward wild-type levels, supplying both necessity and downstream-sufficiency evidence for the KIT–RAS/MAPK model. (frantz2023pigmentcellprogenitor pages 11-13)

### Membrane-associated and extracellular operation

Sequence and splice-form evidence indicates that Kitlga is initially a **membrane-associated cytokine** and can be proteolytically released as a soluble extracellular ligand. *kitlga* transcripts include forms differing around exon 6 and predicted cleavage-associated sequences. Recombinant soluble extracellular Kitlga is biologically active in receptor and marrow-culture assays. Thus, its functional compartment is the producing-cell surface and adjacent extracellular space, where it acts through juxtacrine or paracrine signaling. Direct endogenous-protein imaging and zebrafish cleavage-site mutagenesis remain limited, so exact topology and the relative physiological contributions of soluble versus membrane-bound forms are less firmly established than receptor activity. (hultman2007geneduplicationof pages 10-11, oltova2020zebrafishkitligands pages 9-12, oltova2020zebrafishkitligands pages 4-7)

The supplied InterPro/Pfam annotations—4_helix_cytokine-like_core, SCF, and PF02404—are therefore coherent with the experimentally established signaling role. The domain assignment itself is principally sequence/structure inference; the ligand function is supported directly by genetics and receptor-activation assays.

## 3. Primary biological role: pigment-cell development

The strongest classical evidence assigns Kitlga a central role in **melanocyte/melanophore development**. During embryogenesis, *kitlga* RNA is present along the medial trunk melanocyte-migration route at approximately 18–30 hpf and later in skin at 4–5 dpf. Other complementary Kitlga/Kita expression domains include the tail bud, ear, and pineal region. (hultman2007geneduplicationof pages 1-2, hultman2007geneduplicationof pages 2-3)

Paralog-specific morpholino knockdown showed that loss of *kitlga*, but not *kitlgb*, phenocopied *kita* deficiency, causing defects in melanocyte migration and later survival. Low-dose *kitlga* knockdown genetically enhanced a sensitized *kita* allele, whereas *kitlga* overexpression caused Kita-dependent hyperpigmentation by increasing melanocyte number and size. Temperature-shift experiments placed the principal Kita requirement before 2 dpf for migration and after 2 dpf for survival. These complementary loss-of-function, interaction, expression, and gain-of-function results make melanocyte regulation the most secure primary annotation for Q56JH6. (hultman2007geneduplicationof pages 1-2, hultman2007geneduplicationof pages 2-3, hultman2007geneduplicationof pages 10-11)

## 4. Recent development: melanocyte regeneration and progenitor fate

The most important recent exact-gene study located was Frantz et al., published in **eLife in April 2023** ([DOI and article URL](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.78942)). It combined single-cell RNA sequencing, ligand–receptor inference, qRT-PCR, longitudinal single-cell imaging, mutants, an in-vivo ERK activity reporter, and downstream BRAF activation. No comparably substantive 2024 exact-*kitlga* study was identified in the retrieved literature. (frantz2023pigmentcellprogenitor pages 11-13, frantz2023pigmentcellprogenitor pages 9-11, frantz2023pigmentcellprogenitor pages 7-9)

Following neocuproine-mediated melanocyte destruction, skin *kitlga* expression rose transiently and then declined as regeneration progressed. Single-cell data localized ligand expression mainly to **fibroblasts and keratinocytes**, while *kita* was expressed in melanocyte progenitors and differentiating intermediates. Wild-type fish restored their full stripe melanocyte complement within two weeks, whereas *kita* mutants regenerated a mean of only **51.4%**; the preceding report gave **65.2%** for *kitlga* mutants. (frantz2022stemcellheterogeneity pages 14-20, frantz2023pigmentcellprogenitor pages 7-9)

Serial imaging found that Kita loss produced **more than an eightfold reduction in direct progenitor differentiation** and approximately a **twofold reduction in asymmetric divisions**; *kitlga* loss caused similar defects. Cohorts comprised wild type n=10, *kita*(lf) n=9, and *kitlga*(lf) n=11, analyzed by ANOVA with Dunnett correction. The strongest dependence was in the direct-differentiation route; some cycling progenitor populations were less affected, demonstrating progenitor heterogeneity rather than a universal requirement at every melanocyte-lineage stage. (frantz2023pigmentcellprogenitor pages 17-19, frantz2023pigmentcellprogenitor pages 9-11)

Mechanistically, progenitors had relatively low basal ERK activity, but injury induced Kita-dependent ERK activation. BRAFV600E bypassed receptor loss and increased melanocyte density toward wild-type levels. The current model is therefore that injury induces Kitlga in the skin niche, Kitlga activates Kita on melanocyte progenitors, and Kita–RAS/MAPK signaling biases these cells toward direct differentiation and contributes to asymmetric regenerative divisions. (frantz2023pigmentcellprogenitor pages 11-13, frantz2023pigmentcellprogenitor pages 17-19)

## 5. Ovarian follicle signaling

In the zebrafish ovary, *kitlga* is expressed by **somatic follicle cells**, while **Kita is localized to the oocyte**, creating a paracrine follicle-cell-to-oocyte pathway. Expression rises markedly during the transition from primary to rapid secondary growth, peaks in early vitellogenesis, then declines; it is marginal in ovulated oocytes from which follicle cells are absent. Broader RT-PCR detected transcripts in brain, gill, kidney, liver, muscle, ovary, and testis, although transcript detection does not by itself demonstrate active protein signaling in every tissue. (yao2010kitsystemin pages 2-3, yao2013spatialdistributionand pages 13-14)

Recombinant Kitlga preferentially activated Kita and MAPK in denuded oocytes. In vitro, it suppressed spontaneous germinal-vesicle breakdown, but unlike Kitlgb it did not significantly enhance DHP-induced maturation. These findings support a role in folliculogenesis and restraint of premature spontaneous maturation while also demonstrating specialization between the duplicated ligand systems. The evidence for localization and acute in-vitro signaling is strong; whole-animal evidence defining its indispensability at each ovarian stage is less complete. (yao2013spatialdistributionand pages 1-2, yao2013spatialdistributionand pages 13-14)

## 6. Erythroid and hematopoietic functions

A 2020 study tested recombinant Kitlga in adult zebrafish whole-kidney-marrow cultures ([bioRxiv DOI](https://doi.org/10.1101/2020.02.03.931634)). Cultures were initiated at **3×10⁶ cells/ml**, maintained at **2×10⁶ cells/ml**, and treated with Epo and/or Kitlga at **100 ng/ml**. Kitlga alone produced a mild, reproducible but nonsignificant effect. Kitlga plus Epo significantly increased day-7 cell numbers over Epo alone, while addition of dexamethasone further promoted erythroid-progenitor expansion and colony formation. RNA sequencing associated the response with erythroid, translation, and cell-cycle programs. (oltova2020zebrafishkitligands pages 7-9, oltova2020zebrafishkitligands pages 4-7, oltova2020zebrafishkitligands pages 9-12)

In embryos, full-length *kitlga* mRNA cooperated with *epo* to increase erythroid readouts at 72 hpf, including lcr:EGFP-positive cells, hemoglobinization, and erythroid-gene expression. This cooperation disappeared in *kita/sparse* mutants, as did induction of *gata1a* and β-globin *hbbe1*, indicating receptor dependence. Soluble and full-length ligand forms were not equivalent, and Kitlgb also contributed in vivo. These results support annotation as an erythroid growth/survival co-signal, especially under expansion or stress conditions, rather than as a uniquely lineage-determining factor. Because this source is a preprint and exact effect sizes were unavailable in the retrieved text, confidence is moderate rather than equivalent to the pigmentation evidence. (oltova2020zebrafishkitligands pages 12-15, oltova2020zebrafishkitligands pages 9-12)

A crucial qualification is that Kitlga is **not the principal demonstrated embryonic HSC-specification ligand**. Injected *kitlga* did not alter *cmyb* expression at 4 dpf, whereas Kitlgb–Kitb controlled HSC/HSPC markers. Combined subthreshold *kitlgb* and *osm* increased *cmyb* with p=0.0001, and combined *kitb/osmr* inhibition reduced HSC markers with p=9×10⁻⁷. Low Kita expression and possible receptor cross-activation leave room for minor context-dependent effects, but the authoritative interpretation is functional partitioning: Kitlga–Kita dominates pigmentation and contributes to erythropoiesis; Kitlgb–Kitb is more central to embryonic HSC control. (mahony2018oncostatinmand pages 1-2, mahony2018oncostatinmand pages 8-11)

## 7. Research applications and real-world implementation

Kitlga currently has value primarily as a **research tool and model-system pathway**, rather than as a deployed clinical or aquaculture product:

1. **Regenerative biology:** *kitlga* and *kita* mutants, inducible expression, pigment-cell ablation, and ERK reporters provide an accessible vertebrate system for studying how a tissue niche activates resident progenitors after injury. The visible pigment phenotype allows direct longitudinal quantification in living animals. (frantz2023pigmentcellprogenitor pages 11-13, frantz2023pigmentcellprogenitor pages 9-11)
2. **Pigmentation and neural-crest biology:** The pathway permits dissection of distinct requirements for melanocyte migration, survival, stem/progenitor recruitment, and differentiation. Its duplicated paralogs also provide a natural experiment in post-genome-duplication subfunctionalization. (hultman2007geneduplicationof pages 1-2, hultman2007geneduplicationof pages 2-3)
3. **Hematopoietic culture:** Recombinant Kitlga, Epo, and dexamethasone have been used to establish zebrafish kidney-marrow erythroid expansion and colony assays, facilitating studies of normal and abnormal erythropoiesis. (oltova2020zebrafishkitligands pages 9-12, oltova2020zebrafishkitligands pages 4-7)
4. **Reproductive biology:** Recombinant ligand and isolated-follicle assays model bidirectional soma–germ-cell communication and receptor specialization during folliculogenesis. (yao2013spatialdistributionand pages 1-2, yao2013spatialdistributionand pages 13-14)
5. **Pathway and drug screening:** The visible Kita-dependent pigment phenotype and genetically defined RAS/MAPK output make the system suitable for modifier screens, although translation to human therapy requires caution because zebrafish split ancestral KITLG functions between paralogs.

## 8. Evidence-weighted annotation

A defensible concise annotation is:

> **Kit ligand a is a membrane-associated and releasable extracellular SCF-family cytokine that preferentially activates the Kita receptor tyrosine kinase. In zebrafish, Kitlga–Kita signaling controls embryonic melanocyte migration and survival and is reactivated after pigment-cell injury to stimulate RAS/MAPK-dependent melanocyte-progenitor differentiation. It also functions as a somatic-follicle-cell signal to the oocyte and cooperates with erythropoietin in erythroid-progenitor expansion.**

The pigmentation and regeneration assignments have the strongest direct support. Ovarian receptor specificity is also well demonstrated biochemically. Erythroid activity is supported by ex-vivo and embryonic experiments but rests partly on preprint evidence. Membrane attachment, proteolytic release, and the four-helix cytokine fold are consistent with sequence and soluble-protein activity, but endogenous zebrafish protein-topology data remain comparatively limited. Finally, embryonic HSC findings must not be imported from the separate Kitlgb–Kitb pathway.
