Gene Description

lhfpl5a (LHFPL tetraspan subfamily member 5a) is one of two zebrafish co-orthologs of vertebrate LHFPL5 (TMHS), retained from the teleost-specific genome duplication; its paralog is lhfpl5b. It encodes a small four-pass membrane protein of the tetraspan junctional protein superfamily that acts as an accessory subunit of the hair-cell mechanoelectrical transduction (MET) complex. GFP-Lhfpl5a sits at the tips of stereocilia and is enriched at the tallest stereocilia next to the kinocilium; its bundle localization depends on Pcdh15a, Cdh23 and Myo7aa, and Lhfpl5a in turn is needed to bring the tip-link protein Pcdh15a to the hair bundle. It is not needed for bundle targeting of Tmc1 or Tmc2b. lhfpl5a is expressed only in hair cells of the inner ear (maculae and cristae), from 1 dpf into adulthood, and not in the lateral line. The astronaut (tm290d) nonsense mutant has no transduction in any inner-ear sensory patch and is deaf and balance-defective, with splayed hair bundles, while lateral-line hair cells, which use lhfpl5b, work normally.

Core Functions

Accessory transmembrane subunit of the inner-ear hair-cell MET complex at stereocilia tips: transmits tip-link tension to the channel (mouse LHFPL5) and is needed to bring Pcdh15a to the hair bundle; required for transduction in all inner-ear sensory patches. Recorded as a contribution to the channel complex's activity.

Suggested Questions for Experts

Q: Does Lhfpl5b rescue lhfpl5a mutant inner-ear hair cells when expressed there? The reverse rescue (Lhfpl5a in lhfpl5b mutant neuromasts) works; the reciprocal would complete the evidence that the two proteins are interchangeable.

Q: Is lhfpl5b ever expressed in adult inner-ear hair cells, or is the ear/lateral-line split maintained for life?

Q: What protein links Lhfpl5a to Cdh23 and Myo7aa for its localization at kinocilial links, given that no direct LHFPL5-CDH23 interaction has been detected?

Suggested Experiments

Experiment: Express GFP-Lhfpl5b from a pan-hair-cell (myo6b) or lhfpl5a promoter in lhfpl5a tm290d mutants and score acoustic startle, inner-ear microphonics and FM dye uptake.

Hypothesis: The lhfpl5a/lhfpl5b split is purely regulatory.

Type: cross-rescue

Experiment: Test gar lhfpl5 upstream sequences in zebrafish reporter assays for activity in both inner-ear and lateral-line hair cells; compare with the lhfpl5b 2 kb promoter that already drives lateral-line-specific expression.

Hypothesis: The cis-regulatory elements that restrict lhfpl5a to the ear and lhfpl5b to the lateral line are ancestral elements partitioned between the copies.

Type: enhancer reporter assay

Conserved synteny

Of 30 neighbours of each copy, those whose gar orthologue lies within 5 Mb of the gar lhfpl5 anchor: lhfpl5a 5,
lhfpl5b 9. Those whose human orthologue lies within 5 Mb of human LHFPL5 (chr6:35.8 Mb): lhfpl5a 3, lhfpl5b 5.
The key observation is that the same gene families flank both copies: mapk14b/mapk14a, srpk1b/srpk1a and
cpne5b/cpne5a sit next to lhfpl5a and lhfpl5b respectively, and their orthologues, where Compara reports one (gar LG3:32.2-32.4 Mb for
mapk14b, srpk1b, cpne5b and cpne5a; human 6p21, 35.8-36.7 Mb for all but srpk1a), are next to gar lhfpl5 and human
LHFPL5. Two zebrafish segments, each holding one copy of a
block that is single in gar and human, is the double-conserved-synteny signature of the teleost genome duplication.

Public expression records

  • ZFIN (curated; all records cite one publication, ZDB-PUB-200207-5, dated to early 2020 like Erickson et al., PMID:32009898 — the mapping was not checked): lhfpl5a anterior/posterior macula and their hair
    cells (Prim-5 to Day 5); lhfpl5b neuromast and neuromast hair cell. No shared terms.
  • Bgee: lhfpl5a in maculae/hair cells (in situ) plus bulk testis and larva; lhfpl5b only bulk RNA-seq calls in many
    adult tissues and early embryos (score 36-82). The bulk calls are not hair-cell resolved and are not interpreted.

Interpretation

Synteny and Compara support a TGD origin (two chromosomes, flanking paralog pairs, one gar gene). The public curated
expression records reproduce the published ear/lateral-line split; they are not independent of it.
