| Historical model: Pi-ta (Q84UB1; LOC_Os12g18360) directly recognizes AVR-Pita | Jia et al. (2000) mapped interaction with mature AVR-Pita176 to the C-terminal leucine-rich domain of Pi-ta using yeast two-hybrid assays; far-western analysis supported direct in-vitro binding. Mutations altered interaction specificity. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/emboj/19.15.4004) (pqac-00000025, pqac-00000039, pqac-00000040) | Supports physical association under yeast and in-vitro assay conditions, but not endogenous Pi-ta necessity during infection. Direct interaction in infected rice was not established, and Xiao et al. later failed to reproduce the yeast interaction. No Pi-ta cellular localization or ATPase activity was measured by these experiments. |
| Pi-ta can be required for isolate-specific resistance in Fuhui 2663 | He et al. (2022) mapped resistance to an approximately 80-kb chromosome-12 interval containing LOC_Os12g18360. Two CRISPR knockout lines of the Fuhui 2663 Pi-ta allele became susceptible to isolate KJ201; measured agronomic traits were not significantly altered. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-022-19004-y) (pqac-00000023, pqac-00000024, pqac-00000034) | Provides genotype- and isolate-specific loss-of-function evidence for Pi-ta or a Pi-ta-dependent mechanism. Because Pi-ta lies in a tightly linked resistance region containing Ptr, the result should be reconciled with later locus-resolved studies and independently replicated. It does not establish AVR-Pita as the relevant KJ201 determinant or prove direct recognition. |
| PTR is a distinct resistance protein and must not be annotated as Pi-ta | Zhao et al. (2018) identified Ptr as LOC_Os12g18729, an atypical ARM-repeat protein near Pi-ta. A 2-bp deletion and 34 independent CRISPR Ptr knockouts caused susceptibility; mutant M2354 was susceptible to more than 300 isolates. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-018-04369-4) (pqac-00000018, pqac-00000019) | Demonstrates a major resistance function for PTR, not for Q84UB1/LOC_Os12g18360. Earlier interpretation placed PTR downstream of or alongside Pi-ta; the 2024 evidence instead assigns AVR-Pita recognition directly to PTR alleles. |
| Cytoplasmic localization evidence applies to PTR, not Pi-ta | Zhao et al. (2018) reported that two PTR isoforms were mainly cytoplasmic using PTR:GFP-related experiments; altered PTR products showed differing cytoplasmic/nuclear distributions. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-018-04369-4) (pqac-00000019, pqac-00000020) | This result cannot be transferred to Pi-ta. A direct, experimentally established subcellular localization for Q84UB1/LOC_Os12g18360 was not identified in the assessed literature; “intracellular/cytoplasmic receptor” remains an architectural inference or historical description. |
| In IR64 and K1, Pi-ta is dispensable whereas the corresponding Ptr allele is required | Xiao et al. (2024) tested four independent nlr pi-ta and four ptr mutant lines in IR64: nlr pi-ta mutants retained resistance to AVR-PitaO-137, whereas ptr mutants became susceptible. In K1, Pi-ta RNAi or knockout did not remove resistance, but PtrB disruption did. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41477-024-01694-z) (pqac-00000010, pqac-00000011, pqac-00000014, pqac-00000031) | Strong locus-resolved evidence that LOC_Os12g18360 is neither necessary nor sufficient for the tested historical Pi-ta/Pi-ta2 phenotypes. It assigns those phenotypes to PtrB and PtrA, respectively, but does not show that Pi-ta lacks every immune or physiological function. |
| PtrA is sufficient for AVR-Pita-dependent resistance without Pi-ta | Xiao et al. (2024) introduced PtrA into Nipponbare, which lacks functional Pi-ta/Pi-ta2 resistance; four independent transgenic lines became resistant specifically to an AVR-PitaO-137-expressing strain. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41477-024-01694-z) (pqac-00000010, pqac-00000014, pqac-00000031) | Establishes sufficiency of PtrA in the tested background and supports a Pi-ta-independent mechanism. It does not establish direct AVR-Pita–PTR binding; the study did not detect such binding and allowed for additional host factors. |
| The 2022 and 2024 Pi-ta knockout results conflict | He et al. (2022) found that Pi-ta knockout abolished Fuhui 2663 resistance to KJ201, whereas Xiao et al. (2024) found that Pi-ta knockout did not affect AVR-Pita-triggered resistance in IR64 or K1 and that Ptr loss was causal. [He DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-022-19004-y); [Xiao DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41477-024-01694-z) (pqac-00000023, pqac-00000024, pqac-00000010, pqac-00000011) | The safest accession-level annotation is “NB-ARC/LRR immune-receptor-like protein of presently unresolved physiological specificity,” not a confirmed AVR-Pita receptor. Possible explanations include genetic-background or isolate specificity, dependence on linked genes, editing effects, or an uncharacterized Pi-ta role; these alternatives remain to be tested. |


*Table: Primary evidence distinguishes the Pi-ta NLR from the linked PTR resistance protein and highlights the unresolved conflict between 2022 and 2024 knockout studies. It also prevents PTR localization and AVR-Pita-recognition findings from being incorrectly assigned to Q84UB1.*
