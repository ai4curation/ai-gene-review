Summary

The TreeGrafter/PANTHER-propagated annotation that squid ADAR2 (UniProt C1JAR3) has double-stranded RNA adenosine deaminase activity (GO:0003726) is correct and should be retained. This is one of the cleaner outcomes a function-inference stress test can produce, because the propagated term happens to be directly, experimentally validated for the exact protein in question — a rare case where the automated pipeline reached the biochemically correct answer and independent primary-evidence review confirms it.

We actively tested each of the three characteristic phylogenetic-propagation failure modes and found that none applies. The protein has canonical ADAR2 architecture — three dsRNA-binding domains (dsRBDs) followed by a PF02137 deaminase domain — with a fully intact, correctly-spaced active site (His458, Glu460, Cys516, Cys580; NDCHAE/APCG/GTGTKC signature motifs), which excludes the pseudo-enzyme mode. A global alignment of the deaminase domain gives 69.4% identity to human ADAR2 versus only 37.3% to the ADAT1 tRNA-deaminase sibling, with the three core catalytic residues mapping one-to-one — excluding both the granularity and within-superfamily-misplacement modes.

Most decisively, the identical recombinant squid enzyme was shown by direct in-vitro assay to be "active on duplex RNA" (PMID: 19390115), and this was independently reconfirmed under varied ionic conditions (PMID: 22457361). The recommended curation action is to retain GO:0003726 and upgrade its evidence code from IEA to experimental (IDA), while flagging any co-propagated tRNA-specific deaminase term (GO:0008251) as a likely over-propagation error for removal.

Key Findings

Finding 1 — Intact ADAR deaminase active site and canonical ADAR2 domain architecture

UniProt C1JAR3 is a 786-residue protein encoding a textbook ADAR2 layout: three double-stranded RNA-binding domains (DRBM) at approximately residues 57–106, 164–230, and 315–364, followed by a single A-to-I editase/deaminase domain spanning residues 434–761 (Pfam PF02137 A_deamin; InterPro IPR002466).

Critically, all catalytic and metal-coordinating residues of the ADAR active site are present and correctly positioned relative to characterized ADARs: His458 and the catalytic proton-donor Glu460, embedded in the conserved NDCHAE context; the zinc-coordinating Cys516 within the ADAR-signature APCG motif (beginning at residue 514); a second active-site cysteine Cys580 within the SCSDK context; and the conserved ADAR GTGTKC fingerprint at residue 436.

This constellation is the defining active-site chemistry of a catalytically competent ADAR. The presence of multiple dsRBDs upstream of the deaminase domain is the architectural hallmark of ADAR2 (ADARB family) and distinguishes it decisively from the dsRBD-less ADAT tRNA deaminases, which lack dsRNA-binding modules. There is no sign of active-site degeneration — the pseudo-enzyme failure mode is firmly excluded.

Finding 2 — Direct biochemical assay confirms editing of duplex RNA

The squid ADAR2 captured by C1JAR3 is not merely a computational prediction — it has been cloned and assayed. PMID: 19390115 (Palavicini, O'Connell & Rosenthal, RNA 2009) cloned squid ADAR2 from the Doryteuthis opalescens giant axon and expressed recombinant protein in Pichia pastoris. The paper states directly that "Recombinant sqADAR2a and sqADAR2b, produced in Pichia pastoris, are both active on duplex RNA," and that the enzyme edited two K⁺-channel mRNA substrates in vitro, with the extra-dsRBD variant (sqADAR2a) editing more sites. The same work describes the protein as "a conventional ADAR2 family member with an evolutionarily conserved deaminase domain and two double-stranded RNA binding domains (dsRBD)."

Independent corroboration comes from PMID: 22457361, which studied adenosine deamination/editing activity of the same squid enzyme under varying salt and chloride conditions — further in-vitro confirmation of catalytic activity on dsRNA. This is the gold-standard evidence a curator would want: a direct activity assay on the actual protein, matching GO:0003726.

Moreover, the three core ADAR catalytic residues map one-to-one across the alignment to their human ADAR2 counterparts: human His394 → squid His458, catalytic Glu396 → squid Glu460, zinc Cys451 → squid Cys516. This places the protein in the dsRNA-acting ADAR2 subfamily with high confidence and rules out accidental grafting onto the functionally distinct ADAT (tRNA-specific) branch of the deaminase superfamily. It simultaneously resolves failure mode 1 (granularity: correct) and failure mode 3 (no mis-placement).

Finding 4 — Review-level corroboration from cephalopod RNA-editing biology

PMID: 37981860 (Voss & Rosenthal, 2023) reviews the cephalopod ADAR complement and states that "The deamination of adenosine by adenosine deaminase acting on RNA (ADAR) enzymes produces inosine, which is interpreted as guanosine during translation." Coleoid cephalopods, including squid, recode the majority of expressed proteins in the nervous system through tens of thousands of A-to-I editing sites — a biology that depends on exactly the catalytic ADAR activity attributed to C1JAR3. This situates squid ADAR2 as a functional member of a catalytically active, biologically central ADAR enzyme set, consistent at the organismal level with the molecular assignment.

Conflicts, Knowledge Gaps, and Discriminating Tests

Conflicting evidence: None substantive. All architectural, phylogenetic, and biochemical evidence converges on the same conclusion. The only nuance is ionic-context dependence of activity (PMID: 22457361): conventional ADAR2 editing is partially inhibited by the high chloride of squid neurons, but this is compensated by the squid-specific extra dsRBD in the sqADAR2a splice variant. This qualifies the efficiency of editing under physiological conditions, not the existence of the catalytic function.

Knowledge gaps / uncertainties for curation: - Paralog/isoform note: two splice variants exist — sqADAR2a (extra dsRBD) and sqADAR2b (conventional). Both are active; the extra dsRBD modulates activity/affinity, not the catalytic verdict. C1JAR3 (786 aa, 3 dsRBDs) corresponds to the a-type variant. - Accession-to-assay linkage: the exact 1-to-1 identity between C1JAR3 and the assayed clone rests on same-species + same-architecture + same-lab provenance rather than an accession stated explicitly in the 2009 abstract.
