CnoX is a constitutive holdase. It suppresses citrate-synthase aggregation before bleach exposure; HOCl enhances activity in vitro but the primary authors explicitly leave its physiological relevance unclear. CnoX alone does not refold citrate synthase. Its general holdase core therefore uses the supported holdase chaperone activity NTR, without a new GO row. The reductase core is directly supported by insulin reduction, TrxR recycling, MsrA/DsbDalpha reduction and in-vivo catalytic cysteine redox status [PMID:30482828, “When recycled by TrxR, CcCnoX also catalyzes the reduction of MsrA and DsbDα, two classical Trx substrates”].

The primary study identifies 50 aggregation-associated proteins and 90 **putative** redox substrates by catalytic-mutant trapping; 10 overlap. Those are different experiments and neither count establishes that every candidate is a directly characterized physiological substrate. PMID:33996917 describes approximately90 aggregation-protected proteins, apparently conflating the sets; that secondary claim is not adopted. Trapping uses a resolving-cysteine mutant, and stable mutant complexes do not imply the stable protective mixed-disulfide mechanism of E. coli CnoX in wild-type Caulobacter CnoX [PMID:30482828, “CcCnoX does not protect its substrates from overoxidation by forming stable mixed-disulfide complexes with them”].
