| Identity and architecture | Q40235 corresponds to the wild currant-tomato *Cf-9* product from *Lycopersicon pimpinellifolium* (now *Solanum pimpinellifolium*): an 863-aa extracellular LRR receptor-like protein with 27 LRRs, a single transmembrane segment and a short cytoplasmic tail. Cf-9 has two more LRRs than Cf-4. (pqac-00000006, pqac-00000011, pqac-00000015) | This is a non-enzymatic immune receptor without an intracellular kinase domain; it must signal through associated kinases. | [10.1098/rstb.1998.0296](https://doi.org/10.1098/rstb.1998.0296), 1998; [10.1105/tpc.104.028118](https://doi.org/10.1105/tpc.104.028118), 2005 |
| Avr9 specificity | Genetic and transient-expression evidence establishes that Cf-9—designated Cf-9C in the five-gene locus—specifically recognizes apoplastic Avr9 from *Fulvia fulva* (syn. *Cladosporium fulvum*) and initiates hypersensitive cell death and resistance. (pqac-00000000, pqac-00000018, pqac-00000020, pqac-00000021) | Direct physical Cf-9–Avr9 binding has not been demonstrated. Radiolabeled Avr9 binds a high-affinity plasma-membrane site even in plants lacking Cf-9, indicating that recognition may be indirect or require another host component. (pqac-00000008, pqac-00000014) | [10.1098/rstb.1998.0296](https://doi.org/10.1098/rstb.1998.0296), 1998; [10.1111/nph.19925](https://doi.org/10.1111/nph.19925), 2024 |
| Structure–function and glycosylation | A study of 66 site-directed Cf-9 mutants tested Avr9 recognition and protein stability. Of 22 predicted N-glycosylation sites, all but one were experimentally glycosylated; many supported activity, and sites in predicted LRR α-helices were essential. Added glycans on the predicted concave β-sheet recognition surface blocked activity. (pqac-00000009, pqac-00000015) | These results map surfaces necessary for receptor folding or recognition but do not identify a direct Avr9-binding interface. | [10.1105/tpc.104.028118](https://doi.org/10.1105/tpc.104.028118), 2005 |
| Cellular localization | Functional Cf-9 was detected at the plasma membrane, consistent with perception of an effector in the leaf apoplast. Mutation of its putative C-terminal dilysine ER-retrieval motif did not abolish receptor function. (pqac-00000004, pqac-00000030) | Overexpressed Cf-9 has also accumulated in the ER; this likely reflects unmasked retrieval motifs or secretory-pathway overload. The functional signaling pool is therefore best assigned to the plasma membrane, while ER localization reflects biogenesis or overexpression. | [10.1111/j.1364-3703.2004.00264.x](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1364-3703.2004.00264.x), 2005 |
| SOBIR1 coreceptor | Co-immunopurification showed that tomato SOBIR1 and SOBIR1-like associate with Cf-family RLPs, including Cf-9. The association is constitutive and provides the intracellular kinase function absent from Cf-9; SOBIR1 is also required for Cf-9C/Avr9-triggered cell death. (pqac-00000017, pqac-00000020, pqac-00000024) | The most detailed stability, kinase-dependence and resistance experiments in the 2013 study used Cf-4 or other RLPs; these should not all be attributed directly to Cf-9. | [10.1073/pnas.1220015110](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1220015110), 2013; [10.1111/nph.19925](https://doi.org/10.1111/nph.19925), 2024 |
| SERK1 and SERK3/BAK1 recruitment | After Avr9 elicitation, both tomato SERK1 and SERK3a/BAK1 strongly co-immunoprecipitated with Cf-9–GFP; the nonmatching Avr4 control did not produce significant recruitment. This supports an activated Cf-9–SOBIR1–SERK signaling complex. (pqac-00000022, pqac-00000032) | Endocytosis, kinase-silencing and fungal-colonization experiments in the same publication were primarily performed with Cf-4/Avr4; only ligand-dependent SERK recruitment was directly confirmed for Cf-9/Avr9. | [10.1111/nph.13802](https://doi.org/10.1111/nph.13802), 2016 |
| CITRX regulation | The thioredoxin CITRX interacts with the final 33 residues of Cf-9, encompassing its cytoplasmic tail and part of the transmembrane region. CITRX silencing accelerated Cf-9/Avr9 hypersensitive cell death and increased ROS and CDPK activity, whereas overexpression delayed cell death, supporting receptor-proximal negative regulation. (pqac-00000038, pqac-00000039, pqac-00000042) | CITRX silencing reduced rather than increased MAPK activity, indicating differential regulation of CDPK and MAPK branches. Comparable negative regulation was not observed for Cf-2. | [10.1038/sj.emboj.7600224](https://doi.org/10.1038/sj.emboj.7600224), 2004 |
| Exact locus assignment | The introgressed Cf-9 locus contains five paralogous RLP genes, Cf-9A–Cf-9E. Q40235 corresponds to the historically named Cf-9 receptor, now termed Cf-9C, which recognizes Avr9 and protects throughout plant development. Cf-9B is a distinct paralog recognizing Avr9B and principally conferring mature-plant resistance. (pqac-00000018, pqac-00000020, pqac-00000050) | Avr9B recognition and Cf-9B phenotypes must not be assigned to Q40235/Cf-9C. Specificity assays showed matched Cf-9C–Avr9 and Cf-9B–Avr9B responses but not the mismatched combinations. (pqac-00000020, pqac-00000021) | [10.1111/nph.19925](https://doi.org/10.1111/nph.19925), 2024 |
| Resistance durability and isolate statistics | Among 190 geographically diverse *F. fulva* isolates, 93 lacked *Avr9*, including every isolate obtained from Cf-9/Ff:A–E cultivars. Of 76 race-9 isolates collected during or after 2005, 75 also carried resistance-breaking *Avr9B* mutations; by contrast, 9 of 11 pre-1990 race-9 isolates retained an unmutated *Avr9B* coding sequence. (pqac-00000035) | These population data demonstrate sequential breakdown of the two-gene locus: loss of Avr9 first defeats Q40235/Cf-9C, followed by mutation of Avr9B to defeat the separate Cf-9B specificity. They do not alter Q40235’s molecular annotation. | [10.1111/nph.19925](https://doi.org/10.1111/nph.19925), 2024 |
