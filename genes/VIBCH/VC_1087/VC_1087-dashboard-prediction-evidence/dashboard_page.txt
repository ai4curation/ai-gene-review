why
The 88.6%-identical V. cholerae HnoD/VC1087 homolog experimentally receives phosphotransfer from HnoK and HnoS and lacks detectable c-di-GMP hydrolytic activity. Query InterProScan identifies receiver and HD-GYP domains; STRING kinase associations corroborate signaling function. Conflicting colonization phenotypes do not support a specific colonization role. Fitness Browser entries provide annotations but no condition-specific fitness measurements.
Why this tier
Tier A is supported by an experimentally characterized homolog at 88.6% identity with demonstrated phosphotransfer, corroborated by query receiver/HD-GYP domains and STRING signaling associations. An active c-di-GMP phosphodiesterase annotation is rejected because direct homolog experiments show absent hydrolytic activity and loss of conserved catalytic residues, outweighing generic phosphodiesterase family labels.

2 terms
why these terms
The protein is identified as a 'phosphoacceptor response regulator' that receives phosphotransfer from HnoK and HnoS. This activity is explicitly supported by a high-identity homolog and the presence of a receiver domain. The most specific molecular function term is 'phosphorelay response regulator activity', which describes a regulator that receives a phosphate from a sensor/kinase. This process belongs to the broader biological process 'phosphorelay signal transduction system'. The trace explicitly rejects c-di-GMP phosphodiesterase activity due to a lack of catalytic residues and experimental evidence of no hydrolytic activity, so that term is not assigned.
