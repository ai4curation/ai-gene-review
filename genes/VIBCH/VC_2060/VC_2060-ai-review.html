Gene Description

parP (VC_2060; UniProt Q9KQE0, unreviewed and named only "CheW-like domain-containing protein") encodes ParP, a 338-residue cytoplasmic protein from chemotaxis cluster II of Vibrio cholerae. It tethers the cluster II chemoreceptor arrays to the polar ParA-like ATPase ParC (VC_2061). The gene lies directly downstream of parC. ParP has three interaction surfaces. A conserved extreme N-terminal motif binds ParC. A long, proline-rich, variable linker follows. The C-terminal CheW-like (SH3-like) "array integration and formation" (AIF) domain binds the protein-interaction tip of methyl-accepting chemotaxis proteins (MCPs), through L209, and the localization-and-inheritance domain (LID) of CheA, through W305. Through these contacts ParP becomes part of the core signaling unit and the array baseplate, at about one ParP per 35 CheW1 and 5 CheA. It stimulates array formation, and either ParP or CheA alone is sufficient for arrays to form. Once ParC has recruited ParP to the pole, ParP holds arrays there and prevents CheA from dissociating. It also promotes retention of ParC and of itself at the pole. Cells lacking ParP have mislocalized or absent arrays, so daughter cells do not reliably inherit an array, and they show reduced chemotaxis and fewer swimming reversals. The motility phenotypes were measured mainly in V. parahaemolyticus. ParP is a localization and assembly adaptor of the chemosensory machinery. It is not a CheW-type coupling protein that is required for kinase activation, and it has no enzymatic activity.

Core Functions

Polar tethering and assembly adaptor of the cluster II chemoreceptor arrays. The C-terminal CheW-like AIF domain inserts into the array core unit by binding the MCP protein-interaction tip (through L209) and the CheA LID (through W305), which stimulates array formation. The N-terminal motif binds the polar ATPase ParC, which recruits and retains the arrays, and ParP itself, at the cell pole. This ensures that each daughter cell inherits an array and that chemotaxis works properly.

ParP (VC_2060, UniProt Q9KQE0) of Vibrio cholerae O1 El Tor N16961: research report

Summary

Q9KQE0 is ParP, an adaptor that holds the chemotaxis signalling arrays at the cell pole. It does not catalyse any reaction or transport anything. ParP has two binding modules joined by a linker. The N-terminal domain binds the ParA-like ATPase ParC, which sits at the pole. The C-terminal CheW-like domain (SH3-like) inserts into the core signalling unit of the array, where it binds the receptors (MCPs) and the histidine kinase CheA. Because one protein carries both modules, building the array is coupled to placing it at the pole. ParP also keeps CheA from leaving the array. Loss of ParP leaves arrays mislocalised and less stable. Earlier work on the operon reported reduced chemotaxis in strains lacking ParC, attributed mainly to ParC's role in positioning ParP (Ringgaard et al. 2011; Ringgaard et al. 2014).

2. Domain architecture

UniProt residue ranges are given for the protein's 338 residues. Functional assignments come from Alvarado et al. 2017.

  1. N-terminal ParC-interaction domain. This domain is needed for ParP's own polar localisation and for recruiting arrays to the pole. It "enables polar recruitment of arrays and facilitates its own polar localization." Ringgaard et al. (2014) also report that ParP can dimerise through its N-terminus. The specific ParC-contacting residues have not been mapped.
  2. Long linker. This region is rich in proline and acidic residues; the sequence around residues ~40–190 is low-complexity and Glu/Pro-rich. The paper describes "an N-terminal domain that is separated from AIF by a long proline-rich linker."
  3. C-terminal AIF domain ("array integration and formation" domain), residues ~192–329. This is a predicted SH3-like fold "similar to CheW and the P5 domain of CheA." It has two separate binding surfaces:
  4. MCP-binding groove. Residues L196, L209 and L212 are "almost 100% conserved amongst ParP proteins." The substitution L209A abolishes binding to MCPs but leaves CheA binding intact. I215A has no effect.
  5. CheA-binding surface. The substitution W305A abolishes binding to CheA ("V. cholerae ParPW305A did not interact with CheA") and has little effect on MCP binding. L209 and W305 are "predicted to be positioned on opposite sides of the AIF domain." This was modelled on Thermotoga maritima CheW (PDB 3UR1).
  6. The double mutant ParP2PM (L209A + W305A) binds neither partner. Neither substitution, alone or combined, prevents binding to ParC.

ParP is therefore a natural fusion of a polar-anchor-binding module with a CheW-like coupling module. UniProt's IPR014506/PIRSF020479 ("UCP020479_CheW") family captures this architecture, and it differs from the canonical standalone CheW.

3. Molecular function

3.1 Coupling to ParC, the polar anchor

  • ParC (VC_2061) is a ParA/MinD-family ATPase in the same operon. It moves to the pole and actively promotes polar localisation of the chemotaxis proteins encoded with it. In newborn cells it sits at the flagellated old pole. As the cell matures it relocates to the new pole by a "diffusion and capture" mechanism (Ringgaard et al. 2011).
  • ParP binds ParC. Ringgaard et al. (2014) conclude that "ParC's principal contribution to chemotaxis appears to be via positioning of ParP" (PMC3896188). ParC is itself held at the pole by the landmark protein HubP (Yamaichi et al. 2012, as cited in the 2018 J. Bacteriol. review). The resulting chain is HubP → ParC → ParP → array.

3.2 Integration into the core signalling unit of the array

  • ParP's C-terminus "integrates into the core-unit of signaling arrays through interactions with MCP-proteins and CheA" (Alvarado et al. 2017).
  • MCPs. A two-hybrid screen found 15 of the roughly 45 predicted V. cholerae MCPs as ParP partners; VC1898 gave the strongest signal. The interaction depends on conserved residues at the MCP signalling tip: VC1898 L518R, L521R, N522R and A524R disrupt it. These are the same tip residues that CheW and CheA-P5 contact. Eleven peer reviewers' comments (eLife) noted that the data suggest ParP, CheA and CheW compete for the same MCP tip binding site. The authors themselves "cannot rule out the possibility that ParP also is able to compete with CheW for integration into the core unit."
  • CheA. ParP binds CheA (CheA1, VC_2063) through W305. Ringgaard et al. (2014), working mainly in V. parahaemolyticus, mapped the interaction to a CheA region called the "CheA-LID". This region occurs only in CheA proteins from organisms that have the ParC/ParP system, and a single ParP residue was critical for the contact. This is as cited secondhand in Alvarado et al. 2017; the PNAS full text could not be retrieved.

3.3 Holding CheA in the array

  • After ParC recruits it to the pole, ParP "captures" CheA and prevents its dissociation from arrays. ParP also stabilises these complexes without ParC, so part of its activity does not depend on ParC (Ringgaard et al. 2014).
  • The authors propose a tripartite ParC–ParP–CheA interaction network that localises arrays to the pole and keeps them there in bacteria with a polar flagellum.

3.4 Promoting array formation (scaffold-like role)

  • In ΔcheA1 cells, arrays still form at the poles, mostly as in wild type. In ΔparP ΔcheA1 cells, or with ParP lacking its AIF domain, chemotaxis clusters do not form: "formation of signaling arrays is severely compromised in the absence of both ParP and CheA." ParP's insertion into core units "stimulates array formation," which partly overlaps with CheA's role in building the array (Alvarado et al. 2017).
  • Swapping ParP's AIF domain for the P5 domain of CheA restored polar clusters in 65% of ΔparP/ΔcheA1 cells, compared with 0% without the swap. This shows the AIF domain works as a CheW/P5-type coupling module.

What kind of function this is (for GO). ParP is a non-enzymatic adaptor/scaffold. Its activities are protein–protein binding to ParC, to the MCP cytoplasmic tip and to CheA, and its role is structural: it organises the chemosensory array and anchors it to the pole. There is no evidence that it modulates CheA kinase activity directly, the way CheW couples receptors to the kinase. Alvarado et al. did not test kinase output.

4. Localisation

  • Cytoplasmic face of the cell pole. YFP-ParP is polar in about 97% of wild-type cells. The array-interaction mutant (ParP2PM) is diffuse in about 40% of cells, so the array itself helps keep ParP at the pole (Alvarado et al. 2017).
  • Exchange at the pole (FRAP). ParP exchanges with the polar pool continuously and reaches steady state in about 5 minutes. ParP2PM is released faster.
  • Effect on ParC. In the parP2PM background, about 20% of cells show diffuse YFP-ParC, compared with about 4% in wild type. Integration into the array therefore stabilises the polar retention of both ParP and ParC. In the authors' words, ParP is "a critical nexus that regulates the localization dynamics of its network constituents."
  • ParP has no transmembrane segment or signal peptide. It reaches the polar membrane region only through protein–protein contacts with ParC, the MCPs and the array.

5. Pathway context and phenotypes

Pathway. ParP belongs to the V. cholerae chemosensory system encoded in chemotaxis cluster II, the only one of the three clusters needed for chemotaxis toward chemoattractants. The pathway runs MCPs → CheW1/CheA1 → CheY3 → flagellar motor. ParP does not relay signals itself. It is an accessory organiser that sets where the array is assembled and how stably it holds together (Ringgaard et al. 2011; Alvarado et al. 2017).

Inference about inheritance. Because arrays are not anchored to the poles in ΔparP cells, the authors expect that each daughter cell will not reliably inherit an array at division. They describe this as an inference, not a measured result.

6. Evolution and conservation

  • ParP orthologs are encoded in chemotaxis operons, directly downstream of parC. The ParC/ParP pair is found in polarly flagellated γ-proteobacteria, including V. cholerae, V. parahaemolyticus and other Vibrionaceae (Ringgaard et al. 2011; Alvarado et al. 2017).
  • The MCP-binding residues of the AIF domain are almost invariant across ParP proteins. The CheA-LID region of CheA appears only in CheA proteins from genomes that have ParC/ParP, which points to co-evolution of the ParP–CheA interface (Ringgaard 2014, cited in Alvarado 2017).
  • The 2018 J. Bacteriol. review places ParP among the "CheW-like" array proteins. These include CheW, CheV and the CheA P5 domain, all of which bind MCP tips through the same SH3-like fold.

7. Notes for GO annotation

Existing annotations (GOA) are two IEA rows from InterPro2GO: GO:0006935 chemotaxis and GO:0007165 signal transduction.

  • Chemotaxis (GO:0006935). Supported. ParP is a dedicated part of the cluster II chemotaxis machinery, and parP/parC mutants have altered chemotaxis and array organisation. This is the core process.
  • Signal transduction (GO:0007165). Broad and only partly apt. ParP organises the signalling complex rather than relaying a signal. It is best treated as a non-core or general term.
  • Possible additions. These should be grounded in Alvarado 2017 and Ringgaard 2014, and the term IDs should be checked in OLS before use:
  • A process term for organising or localising chemosensory arrays at the pole: protein localisation to the cell pole, or array assembly.
  • A cellular-component term for the cell pole (believed to be GO:0060187; not checked in OLS).
  • Molecular-function terms for adaptor or scaffold activity, rather than bare "protein binding". The binding partners are ParC, the MCP tip and CheA.
  • Evidence caveats.
  • The CheA-retention data in Ringgaard 2014 come largely from V. parahaemolyticus ParP, which would support ISS-type transfer to this protein.
  • The array-formation and localisation data in Alvarado 2017 are direct V. cholerae experiments (VC_2060).
  • The ParP–MCP and ParP–CheA interaction data come mainly from bacterial two-hybrid assays.
  • Open questions.
  • Which ParC residues and which N-terminal ParP residues form the ParC–ParP interface?
  • Does ParP displace CheW from some core units, and does that change kinase activation or receptor specificity?
  • How much of the ParC and ParP function is relevant during host colonisation?
