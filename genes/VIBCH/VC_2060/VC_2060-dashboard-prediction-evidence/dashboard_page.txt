why
The 97.9%-identical V. cholerae homolog is experimentally characterized as ParP: its C-terminal AIF domain binds MCPs and CheA, and parP deletion disrupts chemotaxis-array formation and localization. Query CheW-like domain annotations and high-confidence STRING associations with MCPs and a ParA-family protein corroborate this assignment.
Why this tier
Tier A is supported by a 97.9%-identical experimentally characterized homolog with explicit protein-interaction mechanisms and array-localization phenotypes, corroborated by query InterProScan and STRING evidence. No fitness, cofitness, or dedicated gene-neighborhood tables are supplied. Generic CheW annotations in more distant homologs were rejected as the primary assignment because the closest characterized homolog specifically identifies ParP and its array-assembly role.
GO terms
4 terms
why these terms
The annotation describes ParP as a 'chemotaxis-array assembly and polar-localization protein'. This supports biological processes related to protein complex assembly (specifically the chemotaxis array) and intracellular protein localization. The phrase 'interacting with methyl-accepting chemotaxis proteins, CheA, and ParC' explicitly supports 'protein binding'. The phrase 'polar-localization protein' supports the cellular component 'cell pole'. I checked for more specific chemotaxis array assembly terms, but none were found in the search results, so I use the more general 'protein-containing complex assembly'. I also considered 'chemotaxis' (BP), but that describes the cellular movement process, whereas ParP is involved in the structural assembly of the machinery enabling it; thus, 'protein-containing complex assembly' is a more accurate description of ParP's direct activity.
