Gene Description

VC_A0119 (VCA0119, historically named vasJ) encodes TssA of the Vibrio cholerae type VI secretion system (T6SS), a 469-residue cytoplasmic, non-catalytic protein with an N-terminal ImpA_N domain (PF06812) and a C-terminal VasJ domain (PF16989). That architecture places it in Class A of the TssA-like proteins (Schneider et al. 2019) and in clade TssA2, sub-clade A of Dix et al. 2018; it is not a TssA1-class (baseplate) protein. In V. cholerae strain 2740-80 a functional chromosomal TssA-mNeonGreen fusion appears at T6SS assembly initiation sites and then tracks the membrane-distal end of the polymerizing VipA/VipB sheath and Hcp tube as a stable, non-exchanging dodecamer. The purified protein is a dodecameric six-pointed star by cryo-EM (PDB 6RIU) whose C-terminal ring fits the cap density at the distal end of isolated V. cholerae sheaths. Loss of tssA slows sheath polymerization roughly eight-fold and reduces, without abolishing, T6SS-dependent killing of E. coli, so the protein is best described as a sheath-tube assembly chaperone or processivity cap rather than a strictly essential structural subunit. In the O37 strain V52, deletion of VCA0119 abolished Hcp secretion and E. coli killing (Zheng et al. 2011). Its ImpA_N paralog in the same gene cluster, VCA0121 (TagA, Class B), performs the opposite role of stopping sheath polymerization at the far membrane, so ImpA_N presence alone does not determine function. The dashboard protein NZ_CWQN01000007.1_75 is 98.7% identical to this N16961 entry.

(2019, EMBO J), which states that Class A of TssA-like proteins "is represented by E. coli TssAEC (EC042_4540) and A. hydrophila TssAAH (AHA1844), as well as V. cholerae TssAVC (VCA0119)", and whose Methods record that "tssAVC (amino acids 1–469, Gene number: VC_A0119) of V. cholerae VC2740-80 was cloned into a modified pACEBACI expression [vector]". The 469-aa length, the ImpA_N + VasJ architecture, the chromosome-II vas locus position, and PDB 6RIU all agree. There is no gene-symbol ambiguity problem here: the protein is the V. cholerae TssA, the only Class A TssA-like protein of this organism's single T6SS, and it is distinct from the paralogous ImpA_N protein TagAVC discussed below.

One important caveat for annotation: the local VC_A0119-goa.tsv is empty — this protein currently carries zero GO annotations despite having a published cryo-EM structure, a defined mutant phenotype, and a named mechanistic role. That is a substantial curation gap, not a knowledge gap.

Note also the naming churn in the literature: the same protein appears as VCA0119, VC_A0119, TssAVC, historically VasJ (the C-terminal Pfam domain PF16989 is named for the vas gene nomenclature), and Schneider et al. propose renaming its whole class to TsaC (T6SS sheath assembly chaperone). All refer to Q9KN46.

2. Biological context: the V. cholerae type VI secretion system

The T6SS is a membrane-anchored contractile nanomachine, structurally and functionally homologous to the contractile tail of bacteriophages and R-type pyocins, that injects proteins from the bacterial cytosol directly into an extracellular space or across a target-cell membrane (Schneider et al. 2019, EMBO J 38:e100825, PMID:31403721, Introduction). Its biogenesis proceeds in a strictly hierarchical order: a trans-envelope membrane complex (TssJLM) forms first; the baseplate (VgrG, TssE, TssF, TssG, TssK) assembles on it; then the sheath–tube copolymer (TssB/VipA + TssC/VipB sheath around an Hcp tube) polymerizes from the baseplate across the cytoplasm. Once fully extended, the sheath contracts rapidly, propelling the inner tube and its associated effectors out of the cell; the contracted sheath is then disassembled by the AAA+ unfoldase ClpV and its subunits recycled (same reference, with the primary citations therein: Basler et al. 2012; Bönemann et al. 2009; Brunet et al. 2015; Nguyen et al. 2017; Nazarov et al. 2018).

In V. cholerae, this machine is both anti-eukaryotic (virulence toward Dictyostelium discoideum amoebae) and anti-bacterial (contact-dependent killing of competitor cells such as E. coli). The systematic single-gene mutagenesis of the V. cholerae V52 T6SS locus by Zheng, Ho & Mekalanos (2011) PLoS ONE 6:e23876 scored every locus gene for Hcp expression, Hcp secretion, amoeba virulence, and E. coli killing. Deletion of 15 individual genes abolished amoeba virulence; twelve of these — including VCA0119 — were required for secretion of Hcp but not for its expression, the signature of a structural/assembly component rather than a regulator or effector. (By contrast, VasH and VCA0122 behaved as expression regulators, and VCA0118 as a candidate effector.) This is the earliest genetic placement of VC_A0119 inside the secretion apparatus itself.

3. Primary molecular function: a sheath-assembly chaperone / processivity cap at the distal end of the growing sheath

VC_A0119 is not an enzyme, not a transporter, and not a secreted effector. It is a cytoplasmic, self-assembling oligomeric scaffold protein that caps the growing tip of the T6SS sheath–tube polymer and catalyses its elongation. The mechanistic case rests on four independent lines of evidence in Schneider et al. (2019), all obtained in V. cholerae itself.

3.1 Loss-of-function phenotype: sheath elongation collapses

Live-cell fluorescence imaging of VipA-mCherry2-labelled sheaths showed that the parental strain forms ~5 T6SS structures per cell during logarithmic growth, whereas "the tssAVC-negative strain formed mostly dynamic sheath spots and only few structures that fully extended and contracted." Quantitatively, sheath polymerization speed dropped from 23 nm/s in the parent to 3 nm/s in the ΔtssAVC strain — an ~8-fold loss of elongation rate. In bacterial competition assays the ΔtssAVC knockout "was able to kill E. coli prey cells only at a reduced rate", a defect the authors note "resembled the reduced prey cell killing by a strain lacking the baseplate component tssE."

Crucially, the defect is kinetic and processivity-related, not all-or-none: "TssAVC is not strictly required for sheath assembly as tssAVC-negative strain still kills prey cells, assembles short dynamic sheaths and occasionally also full-length sheath with significantly reduced assembly speed." This distinguishes V. cholerae TssA from the orthologues in E. coli and P. aeruginosa H1-T6SS, where Hcp/effector secretion was reported to be undetectable in the knockouts (Zoued et al. 2016; Planamente et al. 2016, as reviewed in Schneider et al. 2019). The V. cholerae genetic result of Zheng et al. (2011) — VCA0119 required for Hcp secretion — and the imaging result are reconcilable: a system reduced to short, infrequent sheaths loses bulk Hcp secretion in a population assay while retaining residual single-cell firing.

3.2 Localization: the distal (membrane-distal) end of the polymerizing sheath

A chromosomal TssAVC-mNeonGreen fusion (functional, though it slowed polymerization to 11 nm/s) was imaged in a VipA-mCherry2 sheath background. "In most cases, TssAVC first localized to T6SS assembly initiation site before sheath signal appeared and then colocalized with a distal end of a polymerizing sheath" — i.e. it is recruited at the baseplate/membrane-proximal initiation site, then travels at the advancing tip, remaining at the end farthest from the membrane anchor. Kymograph analysis of 300 cells partitioned the behaviour: in ~two-thirds of events TssAVC stayed attached until contraction (<5 s after full assembly); in ~27% it dissociated after the sheath reached the opposite membrane, which was followed by a prolonged stably extended sheath; in ~3% it remained attached even after contraction. Dissociation and re-association events were also seen, with the authors describing re-association as allowing TssAVC "to continue catalysis of polymerization."

Subcellular location is therefore cytoplasmic, at a dynamic, sub-diffraction-sized site on the inner face of the cell envelope and then along the cytoplasmic path of the extending sheath. It is not an integral membrane protein (no transmembrane segments in the UniProt record), and it is not itself secreted — unlike Hcp or VgrG it is a machine component that stays inside.

3.3 The cap is a stable, non-exchanging dodecamer

Two orthogonal measurements establish the oligomeric state in vivo:

  • Photobleaching. Cytosolic TssAVC-mNeonGreen was photobleached during sheath polymerization; "TssAVC-mNeonGreen signal of the complex at the distal end of the polymerizing sheath remained constant after photobleaching of the cytosol, suggesting that the TssAVC subunits are not exchanged with the cytosolic TssAVC pool during sheath polymerization." The cap is thus assembled once and rides the tip as a unit — a processive cap, not a transiently sampling factor.
  • Single-molecule step calibration. Spot intensities were compared against a LacI-mNeonGreen/lacO-array standard (0, 3, 6, 12 operators). "Signals observed for TssAVC-mNeonGreen fusions were most similar to the signal produced by LacI-mNeonGreen molecules binding to 6 copies of lacO… This suggests that TssAVC forms a dodecamer in vivo."

3.4 Cryo-EM structure (PDB 6RIU / this study): a 12-subunit two-ring star that matches the sheath tip

Recombinant full-length TssAVC (1–469, expressed in insect cells via MultiBac) was purified and solved by cryo-EM. Key structural findings:

  • Symmetry and dimensions. Unbiased C1 refinement "resulted in clear six-pointed star reconstruction"; refinement then used C6. "The outer and lumenal diameters are 132 and 53 Å, and the height of the assembly is 38 Å."
  • Stoichiometry confirmed biochemically. "Molecular weight estimation from the size-exclusion chromatography (SEC) profile is ~600 kDa, which corresponds to twelve copies of TssAVC in one oligomer (634 kDa from sequence)."
  • The ordered core is the C-terminal VasJ domain. "The model built in cryo-EM density of TssAVC is composed of twelve C-terminal subunits (G376–T466, helices α8–α12) tightly packed as two interpenetrating rings", resolved to a global resolution of 3.9 Å (consistent with PDB 6RIU's deposited 3.90 Å). This parallels the E. coli TssAEC C-terminal VasJ-domain crystal structure, which forms "two stacked hexameric rings" (Zoued et al. 2016).
  • A flexible middle domain decorates the ring. "Well-resolved TssAVC star-like CTD is surrounded by disordered density… which most likely represents the middle N-terminal domain (Nt2)", resolved as Nt2 dimers (six per particle) after symmetry expansion and focused classification to 6.6–10 Å, with a modelled range of motion of roughly ±60° relative to the CTD ring plane. The MobiDB-lite disordered region at 185–214 in the UniProt record corresponds to the kind of flexible linker that permits this.
  • Direct structural identification of the sheath-tip cap. "Our reconstruction of TssAVC CTD fits as a rigid body with CC = 0.87 into the distal end of VipA-N3 sheath mutant reconstruction (EMD-3878)." This is the decisive structural link: the sixfold "flattened star-like complex" at the distal end of isolated non-contractile V. cholerae sheaths — which Nazarov et al. 2018, EMBO J reported at 7.5 Å (EMD-3878) but could not assign compositionally, noting only that it "could be either VipA/VipB sheath subunits without the Hcp ring or a cap" — is TssAVC.

The dodecameric ring's dimensions are, as noted for the homologous TssA1PA, "similar to sheath-tube ring" with "a central hole that could accommodate Hcp" — geometrically consistent with a template/jig that holds the leading sheath–tube ring in register.

3.5 Mechanistic interpretation: a chaperone for sheath–tube copolymerization

Schneider et al. reason by comparison with phage T4, whose sheath elongation requires no chaperone (Arisaka et al. 1979): "while potentially not being strictly essential, Class A TssAs act as chaperones assisting sheath-tube copolymerization, which likely requires assistance in proper formation of the sheath hand-shake domain" — the interdigitating TssB/TssC extension that locks successive sheath rings together (Ge et al. 2015; Kudryashev et al. 2015; Wang et al. 2017). On this basis they propose the class name TsaC (T6SS sheath assembly chaperone). The functional model (their Fig 6A) is: after the membrane complex forms, TsaC assists in recruitment of baseplate components and sheath–tube copolymerization; once the extending sheath–tube reaches the opposing cell membrane, TsmA (TagA) replaces TsaC at the distal end and holds it in place; shortly after TsmA disappearance the sheath contracts and releases the spike/tube to the environment; sheath components are then recycled by ClpV.

Domain division of labour is inferred by homology to E. coli, where "the N-terminal part of TssAEC containing ImpA_N domain might be responsible for interaction with sheath and to a lesser extent with baseplate components, while the C-terminus preferably interacted with Hcp" (Zoued et al. 2016, as summarized in Schneider et al. 2019). The V. cholerae protein's architecture (ImpA_N 12–125, flexible Nt2, VasJ CTD ~376–466) is consistent with this partition, but the per-domain binding assignment for Q9KN46 specifically has not been separately mapped — a genuine open question.

4. Protein–protein interactions and the TagA antagonism

The interaction data reported for V. cholerae come from HA-tag co-immunoprecipitation followed by mass spectrometry, plus bacterial two-hybrid assays (Schneider et al. 2019, Methods and author contributions). The published summary statement is about the paralogue: "We show that TagAVC interacts with TssAVC, TssK, ClpV, VipA, and VipB" — placing TssAVC in a physical network with the baseplate (TssK), the sheath (VipA/TssB, VipB/TssC), the recycling ATPase (ClpV), and TagA. For the orthologues, the reported partner sets are TssK, TssE, VgrG, Hcp, TssB, TssC (TssAEC; Zoued et al. 2016) and TssK1, TssF1, ClpV1, Hcp1, TssB1 (TssA1PA; Planamente et al. 2016).

TagAVC is the functional antagonist of VC_A0119, and the two are the reason V. cholerae is the organism where the TssA/TagA relationship was dissected. TagAVC (Class B: ImpA_N followed by a hydrophobic domain) "localizes to cell membrane and prevents sheath assembly likely by competing with TssAVC", forming hexameric spots at the distal ends of fully assembled sheaths. Three observations tie the two together:

  1. TssAVC spots normally disappear from fully extended sheaths (their Fig 2C).
  2. In the ΔtagAVC mutant, "the fraction of sheaths with TssAVC-mNeonGreen spots still attached to sheath distal end after contraction increased from 3% in the parental strain to 30%."
  3. Overproducing TagAVC blocked sheath assembly entirely and froze TssAVC-mNeonGreen spots at the membrane, colocalized with sheath foci: "High concentration of TagAVC thus results in retention of TssAVC at sheath assembly initiation sites and inhibition of sheath assembly." Overproducing TssAVC itself had no such effect (killing and sheath assembly remained like the parent).

Interpretation: "TagAVC blocks function of TssAVC potentially by removing TssAVC from the fully assembled sheath. Removal of TssAVC stops or slows down further sheath polymerization and thus stabilizes the extended sheath." Phenotypically, ΔtagAVC gives ~5-fold fewer sheath assemblies, ~3-fold fewer contractions, reduced polymerization speed (13 vs 23 nm/s), and sheaths that are "mostly very long, stretching from one pole of a cell to another", often bent and sometimes detached from the membrane anchor — yet with negligible effect on killing. Schneider et al. propose renaming this class TsmA (T6SS sheath membrane anchor).

So the elongation switch in V. cholerae is a two-protein, two-ImpA_N-protein module: TssAVC (VC_A0119) licenses and accelerates elongation from the tip; TagAVC terminates it at the far membrane.

5. Evolutionary and bioinformatic placement

  • PF06812 (ImpA_N) is a shared N-terminal module across functionally divergent proteins. Schneider et al. conclude that "proteins with a conserved N-terminal domain PF06812 cluster into three main classes and mainly differ in their C-terminal sequence", and that "while various proteins share a conserved N-terminal domain, their roles in T6SS biogenesis are fundamentally different." This is a direct caution against transferring function on ImpA_N presence alone, and it means UniProt's placeholder name "ImpA N-terminal domain-containing protein" is uninformative about what Q9KN46 does.
  • Class A (TssA / proposed TsaC) — ImpA_N + C-terminal VasJ (PF16989): TssAVC (VC_A0119), TssAEC (EC042_4540), TssAAH (AHA1844), TssA2PA (PA1656). Distal-end dodecameric cap; accelerates initiation and elongation.
  • Class B (TagA / proposed TsmA) — ImpA_N + hydrophobic domain: TagAVC, TagAEC. Membrane-localized elongation stop/anchor.
  • Class C (proposed TsaB) — e.g. TssA1PA of the P. aeruginosa H1-T6SS: baseplate-localized, required for initiation of sheath assembly.
  • Q9KN46's two-domain architecture (PF06812 + PF16989) places it squarely in Class A, matching both its Pfam complement in UniProt and its PANTHER subfamily assignment PTHR37024:SF3 "Type VI secretion system protein TssA". The NCBIfam model TIGR03362 (VI_chp_7) and the dedicated InterPro entry IPR017739 "T6SS-associated VCA0119" are both built around this protein lineage, i.e. the family signatures are effectively defined by it.
  • Orthologue identity is low but the function is conserved: TssAVC shares 19.9% identity with TssAEC, 32.8% with TssAAH, and 21.8% with TssA2PA. Despite that divergence, "TssA2PA displays almost identical dynamics to TssAVC", leading to the generalization that "it is very likely that all TssA proteins of the Class A perform the same function in T6SS biogenesis and are required for fast and efficient polymerization of T6SS sheath." Function in this family is thus carried by the domain architecture and the oligomeric geometry, not by sequence conservation — an important point for homology-based annotation of other Vibrionaceae TssA proteins.
  • Fold/assembly relationships. The Class A CTD two-interpenetrating-ring architecture is shared with TssAEC ("two stacked hexameric rings") and TssAAH, whose crystal packing also showed the Nt2-dimer behaviour reproduced in the V. cholerae cryo-EM map (Dix et al. 2018). TssA1PA (Class C) was noted to resemble phage baseplate component gp6 in its C-terminal part (Planamente et al. 2016) — consistent with the T6SS's overall phage-tail ancestry.
  • TCDB 3.A.23.1.1 assigns the protein to the "Type VI symbiosis/virulence secretory system (T6SS)" family — correct at the system level, though the TC framing as a transport system should not be read as implying VC_A0119 is itself a transporter.

6. Summary of function, location and pathway

Primary function. VC_A0119 / Q9KN46 is TssA of the Vibrio cholerae type VI secretion system — a non-catalytic, self-assembling dodecameric sheath-assembly chaperone and processivity cap. It is recruited to the T6SS assembly initiation site at the inner membrane before sheath signal is detectable, then rides the membrane-distal tip of the elongating TssB/TssC(VipA/VipB) sheath–Hcp tube copolymer as a stable, non-exchanging 12-subunit star-shaped ring (outer Ø 132 Å, lumen Ø 53 Å, height 38 Å; the ordered core being twelve copies of the C-terminal VasJ domain, residues ~376–466, in two interpenetrating hexameric rings), where it templates and accelerates addition of successive sheath–tube rings — most plausibly by assisting correct formation of the sheath hand-shake interface.

Location of action. Cytoplasm, at a dynamic membrane-proximal initiation site and then at the advancing cytoplasmic tip of the sheath; a component of the T6SS sheath–tube complex / distal-end cap, physically continuous with the baseplate at initiation. It is a machine subunit, not a secreted substrate, and has no transmembrane or signal features.

Pathway. The hierarchical T6SS biogenesis/firing cycle: membrane complex (TssJLM) → baseplate (TssE/F/G/K, VgrG) → TssA-capped sheath–tube elongation (this protein) → TagA-mediated elongation arrest and distal membrane anchoring → contraction and effector/Hcp expulsion → ClpV-dependent sheath disassembly and subunit recycling. Downstream biological outputs are contact-dependent interbacterial antagonism (killing of E. coli and other competitors) and anti-eukaryotic virulence (toward D. discoideum). Loss of VC_A0119 reduces, but does not abolish, both: elongation rate falls from 23 to 3 nm/s, full-length sheaths become rare, and prey killing is slowed to roughly the level of a baseplate (tssE) mutant, while bulk Hcp secretion is scored as lost in population assays.

Evidence quality. Unusually strong and multi-modal for a 469-aa protein of an unreviewed TrEMBL entry: single-cell live imaging of a functional chromosomal fluorescent fusion (localization, dynamics, polymerization kinetics), FRAP/photobleaching (subunit exchange), single-molecule intensity calibration (stoichiometry in vivo), a 3.9 Å cryo-EM structure of the purified complex with SEC mass corroboration (PDB 6RIU), rigid-body fitting into an independently determined in-situ sheath-end map (EMD-3878, CC = 0.87), co-IP/MS and bacterial two-hybrid interaction mapping, deletion phenotyping, and competition assays — plus independent prior genetic evidence that VCA0119 is required for Hcp secretion but not Hcp expression. The main residual uncertainties are (i) which domain of this protein contacts sheath versus Hcp (assigned only by E. coli homology), (ii) whether the Nt2 flexibility has a defined mechanical role in ring addition, and (iii) the molecular nature of the TssA→TagA handover at the far membrane.

What the protein is

Class A TssA (= TssA2A in the Dix et al. 2018 clade scheme) of the V. cholerae T6SS:
ImpA_N (PF06812, residues 12–125) plus a C-terminal VasJ domain (PF16989). Direct
V. cholerae evidence:

  • Hcp secretion / killing. In the O37 strain V52, a VCA0119 deletion removed Hcp from
    supernatants without changing cellular Hcp, and abolished E. coli killing
    (PMID:21909372).
  • Cap on the growing sheath. In the O1 El Tor strain 2740-80, a functional chromosomal
    TssA-mNeonGreen fusion appears at T6SS initiation sites and then at the distal end of the
    polymerizing sheath; photobleaching shows the cap does not exchange with the cytosolic
    pool; brightness calibration gives 12 subunits (PMID:31403721).
  • Structure. Cryo-EM of the purified protein gives a dodecameric six-pointed star
    (PDB 6RIU, 3.9 Å; ordered core = the C-terminal VasJ domains) that fits the cap density at
    the distal end of isolated V. cholerae sheaths (CC = 0.87).
  • Phenotype in O1 El Tor is partial. ΔtssA polymerizes sheath at 3 nm/s instead of
    23 nm/s and kills prey more slowly, but is not null for T6SS function. Schneider et al.
    therefore call Class A TssAs sheath-tube assembly chaperones and propose the class name
    TsaC.

The V. cholerae protein did not reproduce the Hcp/baseplate interactions reported for
the E. coli orthologue in pulldown or two-hybrid assays, so its contact map is an open
question (recorded as a knowledge gap).

Paralog signal flagged by the dashboard

The dashboard's paralog axis paired this protein with NZ_WYBZ01000044.1_3
("VasL/ImpA-family inner membrane protein associated with the type VI secretion system",
tier B) in PANTHER family PTHR37024. That is the second ImpA_N protein of the same cluster,
VCA0121 / TagA. It is a genuine paralog with an opposite role: a Class B, membrane-
localized hexamer that stops sheath polymerization at the far membrane and antagonizes TssA
(PMID:31403721). Zheng et al. (2011) already noted that both VCA0119 and VCA0121 carry
ImpA_N domains but that only VCA0119 is required for secretion, and speculated VCA0121 was a
degenerate duplicate. So ImpA_N alone cannot carry the TssA call; the dashboard call used
the C-terminal VasJ domain and the VCA0119-specific family signatures (IPR017739,
TIGR03362) and so landed on the right class.
