why
The 97.5%-identical V. cholerae homolog VCA0119/VasJ is experimentally required for Hcp secretion and bacterial killing. Query domains identify the VCA0119 T6SS family and ImpA N-terminus; comparative annotations and STRING COG3515 identify TssA/VasJ. Recurring proximity to T6SS genes supports apparatus membership. Query topology predicts a cytoplasmic globular protein with no membrane-spanning segments.
Why this tier
Tier A is supported by convergence of experimental loss-of-function evidence for a 97.5%-identical homolog, query-specific T6SS family domains, and recurring T6SS genomic context. Additional evidence includes comparative homolog annotations, STRING associations, distant-homolog plant fitness, and topology prediction; no cofitness data are supplied. The computational inner-membrane trafficking annotation is rejected because query topology lacks membrane-spanning segments and the direct experimental evidence identifies a T6SS structural component; the plant phenotype does not establish a separate molecular activity.

2 terms
why these terms
The protein is identified as TssA (VasJ), a structural component of the Type VI secretion system (T6SS), and is explicitly stated to be required for Hcp secretion. This supports a biological process role in T6SS protein secretion. Since it is a 'structural component', it is part of the T6SS protein secretion system complex. I searched for a specific molecular function for 'type VI secretion system structural component', but no such specific MF term was found in the search results. While it is a 'component', this is usually captured by the CC term for the complex. The reasoning trace explicitly rejects a 'membrane-spanning' or 'inner-membrane trafficking' role, so no membrane-specific CC terms are assigned.
