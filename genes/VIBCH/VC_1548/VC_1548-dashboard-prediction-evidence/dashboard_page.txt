why
A 55.2%-identical Vibrio vulnificus TtpB2 homolog supports siderophore uptake in deletion experiments, although species-specific complementation limits transfer of its exact substrate range. Repeated association with ExbB/ExbD/TonB genes and STRING partners scoring 946–956 supports a TonB2 accessory role. Query InterProScan identifies UCP028069; its cleavable signal peptide and absence of membrane-spanning segments support a soluble exported protein.
Why this tier
Tier A is supported by convergence of homolog functional genetics, conserved TonB-system neighborhood associations, and the 100%-identity STRING hit's TonB2/ExbB/ExbD partners. Experimental homolog summaries support iron acquisition, but no direct biochemical mechanism is established; distant Shewanella fitness phenotypes do not identify a transported substrate, and no cofitness data are provided. The isolated zot assignment and STRING Smc orthogroup conflict with the convergent transport context and query topology, so toxin and chromosome-segregation annotations are rejected; species-specific complementation also argues against transferring individual siderophore specificities.

why these terms
The protein is described as a 'Putative exported TonB2-associated accessory protein supporting ferric siderophore uptake'. The annotation and reasoning trace explicitly link it to the process of ferric siderophore uptake, which is a specific form of iron ion transport across the outer membrane in bacteria. I will assign 'siderophore transport' (BP) and 'iron ion transport' (BP). Because it is an 'exported' protein supporting 'uptake' and associated with TonB2 (a system known to operate at the outer membrane), I will assign 'cell outer membrane' (CC). The text specifies it is an 'accessory protein' rather than the transporter itself, so I avoid assigning specific 'transporter activity' or 'receptor activity'. I also avoid 'iron ion binding' as the protein 'supports' uptake but is not stated to bind the iron itself.
