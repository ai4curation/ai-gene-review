enzyme

why
The 98.8%-identity Vibrio cholerae homolog is identified as ComEC. Query NCBIFAM and PANTHER annotations identify ComEC/Rec2 and competence-related DNA uptake, while predicted topology shows 13 transmembrane segments. A 31.1%-identity Haemophilus influenzae Rec2 homolog is described as required for ssDNA translocation across the inner membrane. STRING competence-associated partners corroborate this assignment. No fitness measurements or cofitness tables are supplied; Fitness Browser hits provide annotations only.
Why this tier
Tier A is supported by convergence of homolog literature identifying ComEC and its DNA internalization role, query-specific ComEC/Rec2 family signatures, and integral membrane topology. The close homolog's regulatory experiments do not directly establish transport biochemistry, but the combined evidence supports the channel assignment. A beta-lactam antibiotic hydrolase annotation is unsupported: the metallo-beta-lactamase-like domain occurs within a specifically identified ComEC architecture and does not establish antibiotic hydrolysis or a separate catalytic activity.

5 terms
why these terms
The protein is identified as ComEC/Rec2, which is described as an 'inner membrane DNA uptake channel'. This directly supports a cellular component (CC) of the plasma membrane (the bacterial inner membrane) and a molecular function (MF) of channel activity and transporter activity. The process it mediates is the translocation of single-stranded DNA during 'natural transformation', which is explicitly captured by the biological process (BP) terms 'DNA import into cell involved in transformation' and 'DNA-mediated transformation'. The trace explicitly rejects a beta-lactam antibiotic hydrolase activity, so no terms related to that are assigned.
