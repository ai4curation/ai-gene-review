Gene Description

VC_1669 is a 448-residue inner-membrane protein of the neurotransmitter:sodium symporter (NSS/SNF, PF00209) family, in the prokaryotic YocR/YhdH-like subgroup (cd10336 SLC6sbd_Tyt1-like). It is a paralog of the tryptophan transporter AatX (VC2283), 55.5% identical to it. Every characterized bacterial NSS member is a Na+-coupled amino acid symporter: TnaT for tryptophan, Tyt1 for tyrosine, MhsT for hydrophobic amino acids including tryptophan, and LeuT for leucine and alanine. VC_1669 is only 32-41% identical to each of them and no closer to any one, so its substrate cannot be assigned. The only experimental report naming VC1669 is a CarR ChIP-seq study that lists it as a direct CarR target, relayed here through the dashboard's PaperBLAST summary. The tryptophan data for AatX do not transfer to VC_1669. AatX is TrpR-repressed and has a near-consensus TrpR operator, whereas VC_1669 has no TrpR-like site (9 of 20 mismatches, the genome median) and no tryptophan-related neighbouring genes. Its neighbours are a pseudouridine synthase and cardiolipin synthase (cls). VC_1669 is best described as a putative Na+-coupled NSS-family symporter of unknown substrate.

VC_1669 (UniProt Q9KRH3), Vibrio cholerae O1 El Tor N16961: research report

Summary

VC_1669 encodes a 448-residue integral membrane protein of unknown function. It belongs to the bacterial neurotransmitter:sodium symporter (NSS/SNF, SLC6-homolog, TC 2.A.22) family. No published study characterizes VC_1669 itself. A search for "VC_1669"/"VC1669" found no literature that mentions the gene (web search, Oct 2026). Its function therefore has to be inferred from its family and domain signatures, from sequence comparison with experimentally characterized bacterial NSS transporters, and from structure-based mapping of conserved sites.

Best-supported inference: VC_1669 is probably a Na⁺-coupled symporter of neutral, hydrophobic amino acids in the cytoplasmic (inner) membrane. It is most closely related to MhsT from Bacillus halodurans and to the uncharacterized B. subtilis paralogs YocR and YhdH. The exact substrate has not been determined experimentally, and the transport activity itself has never been tested.

2. Family context: bacterial NSS (SLC6) transporters

The NSS/SLC6 family includes the mammalian Na⁺(/Cl⁻)-dependent transporters for GABA, glycine, serotonin, dopamine and norepinephrine. Its bacterial members are Na⁺-coupled amino acid symporters (Singh & Pal review, PMC4818570). Bacterial NSS homologs were first recognized in 1998, and the first to be functionally characterized was TnaT, a Na⁺-dependent, tryptophan-selective transporter from Symbiobacterium thermophilum (Androutsellis-Theotokis et al. 2003, as summarized in PMC4818570).

Three bacterial members have been studied experimentally and serve as reference points for VC_1669:

  1. LeuT (Aquifex aeolicus, O67854). This Na⁺-coupled symporter of small nonpolar amino acids is the structural paradigm for the family (LeuT fold; PDB 2A65 and later structures). Its transport is chloride-independent. A single mutation, I359Q, converts tryptophan from an inhibitor into a transported substrate, which shows how a few binding-pocket residues set substrate selectivity (Piscitelli & Gouaux, EMBO J 2012; OSTI 1033019; PDB 3QS6).
  2. Tyt1 (Fusobacterium nucleatum). Expressed in E. coli, Tyt1 "catalyzes robust Na⁺-dependent, highly selective L-tyrosine transport". It was described as an NSS member with only 11 TMs, and Na⁺ binding shifts it toward an outward-facing conformation (Quick et al., J Biol Chem 2006, PMID:16798738; VIVO record). Later work found that transport by Tyt1 is chloride-independent and coupled to an H⁺ flux (PMC2808765; PMC2826427). The CDD model that VC_1669 hits, cd10336 "SLC6sbd_Tyt1-Like", is named after this subgroup.
  3. MhsT (B. halodurans BH1128, Q9KDT3). This hydrophobic amino acid transporter has broad specificity for hydrophobic amino acids, including tryptophan, other aromatic amino acids and aliphatic ones. Malinauskaite et al. (Nat Struct Mol Biol 21:1006–1012, 2014) solved its structure in an occluded inward-facing state with Na⁺ and L-Trp bound (PDB 4US4, 2.6 Å; PDBsum 4US4; PDBj 4US4). A conserved GlyX₉Pro motif lets TM5 partly unwind, which opens a water pathway to the Na2 site and allows Na⁺ release to the cytoplasm. Focht et al. (EMBO J 40:e105164, 2021; PMC7780149) solved six substrate-bound structures. They found that a flexible, non-helical Gly-Met-Gly segment in TM6 reshapes the binding pocket to fit substrates of different size, and that a general (G/A/C)ΦG motif is conserved across SLC6 transporters (PDB 6YU7).

The YocR/YhdH subfamily (IPR047218). B. subtilis YocR (O34383, 445 aa) and YhdH (O07577, 451 aa) are SNF-family paralogs with no experimentally established function. SubtiWiki lists their function as unknown and maps both to the MhsT structure 4US4 at about 44–45% identity (SubtiWiki YocR; SubtiWiki YhdH). VC_1669 falls in the same InterPro subfamily.

Two other features stand out:

  • The TM6 segment of VC_1669, LSLGVCVMTTYGSY, lines up with MhsT LSLGMGALITYGSY. VC_1669 has G-V-C where MhsT has the flexible G-M-G loop. This still fits the (G/A/C)ΦG pattern that Focht et al. found conserved across SLC6 transporters, but the substitution may change how the pocket adapts to substrates of different size.
  • The TM1–TM2 region, including the GxxGLGNxW(R)F signature, is strongly conserved with MhsT. VC_1669 has GSAVGLGNVWGF and MhsT has GSAVGLGNIWRF.

Interpretation. Both Na⁺ sites are almost entirely conserved, which supports Na⁺-coupled symport. The aromatic and aliphatic residues of the substrate pocket are conserved with MhsT, which points to a neutral, probably hydrophobic or aromatic, L-amino acid substrate. VC_1669's substrate cannot be predicted reliably beyond that, because single changes at positions like LeuT I359 or the TM6 GΦG loop can switch selectivity in this family.

The analysis was run in a throwaway /tmp directory and is not saved in the repository. A reproducible version belongs in VC_1669-bioinformatics/ if it is used in the review.

4. Localization

  • Inferred location: cytoplasmic (inner) membrane. Phobius predicts 10 TM helices, and the UniProt keywords Membrane and Transmembrane are backed by SAM:Phobius. Proteins of the LeuT fold have 12 TM helices (Tyt1 is an 11-TM exception), so Phobius has probably merged or missed some. VC_1669 is about the same length as MhsT (448 vs 453 aa), which suggests the full LeuT-type core.
  • It has no predicted signal peptide or outer-membrane β-barrel features. NSS transporters use the inward-directed Na⁺ gradient across the cytoplasmic membrane.
  • This is therefore an inference: no fractionation or localization experiment exists for VC_1669. The current GO annotation, GO:0016020 membrane (IEA), is correct but general. GO:0005886 plasma membrane is the defensible refinement for a Gram-negative secondary active transporter, but it would rest on inference.

5. Pathways and biological role

  • Likely process: uptake of amino acids across the cytoplasmic membrane, driven by the Na⁺ electrochemical gradient. This fits V. cholerae's marine/estuarine lifestyle. Vibrio species generate a strong Na⁺-motive force through the Na⁺-pumping NADH:quinone oxidoreductase (Na⁺-NQR) and depend on it for many Na⁺-coupled processes. That link is general physiology, not a VC_1669-specific result.
  • Genomic neighborhood (KEGG): VC_1666, an ABC transporter ATP-binding protein (putative thiamine); VC_1667, hypothetical; VC_1668, Rlu-family pseudouridine synthase; VC_1670, cardiolipin synthase; VC_1671, cystathionine β-lyase; VC_1672, DNA-3-methyladenine glycosylase I. None of these forms an operon with VC_1669 that would point to a specific substrate. VC_1669 is on the opposite strand from VC_1670 onward.
  • Virulence / colonization: I found no literature linking VC_1669 to virulence, colonization, the regulons of ToxR, quorum sensing or ppGpp, or to fitness phenotypes.

7. Open questions and suggested experiments

  1. Substrate identification. Express VC_1669 in an E. coli strain lacking aromatic and branched-chain amino acid uptake, as done for Tyt1 (Quick et al. 2006). Screen radiolabelled L-amino acids (Trp, Tyr, Phe, Leu, Ile, Val, Met, Ala) in the presence and absence of Na⁺.
  2. Coupling ions. Test whether transport depends on Na⁺ and is independent of Cl⁻, and whether it is coupled to H⁺ as in Tyt1. Mutate the Na2 site (A328, T331, S332) and the Na1 site (N32, S228).
  3. Selectivity determinants. Swap VC_1669's TM6 GVC segment for MhsT's GMG and test the M336 position, which corresponds to LeuT I359.
  4. Physiology. Measure ΔVC_1669 growth with single amino acids as nitrogen or carbon sources, and check fitness in published V. cholerae Tn-seq datasets (not examined here).

Question

The dashboard called both V. cholerae NSS (PF00209) paralogs tryptophan transporters.
Only VC2283 is named in the literature (aatX, PMID:36130487). Which paralog does the

  • VC1669 (minus strand): VC1668 TIGR01621 pseudouridine synthase (-), VC1670 cls

Conclusion

The tryptophan evidence belongs to VC2283. It carries a near-consensus TrpR operator,
which fits the RegPrecise TrpR-regulon prediction and the TrpR repression measured in
PMID:36130487. VC1669 has no TrpR site, no tryptophan-related neighbours and no
experimental data beyond being a CarR ChIP-seq target (PMID:35676416). Its substrate is
unknown. Calling VC1669 a tryptophan transporter transfers the AatX annotation to a
55%-identical paralog in the same genome. Two paralogs in one genome usually differ in
substrate or regulation, and here they demonstrably differ in regulation.

Caveats:
- The operator scan uses the E. coli operator, not a Vibrio-trained TrpR matrix.
- Neither protein has been assayed for transport. AatX's tryptophan role rests on
regulation plus a double-mutant consumption phenotype.
