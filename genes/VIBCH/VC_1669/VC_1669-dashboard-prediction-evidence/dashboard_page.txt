why
The 54.9%-identical Vibrio cholerae AatX homolog is experimentally identified as a tryptophan transporter; deletion reduces environmental tryptophan consumption. Query InterProScan annotations and the 98.2%-identical STRING homolog independently support the sodium:neurotransmitter symporter family. Eleven predicted transmembrane helices support an integral inner-membrane location. Hypotaurine transport at 45.1% identity and tyrosine-associated fitness at 35% identity indicate substrate variation among homologs, warranting a putative qualifier.
Why this tier
Tier A is supported by an experimentally characterized homolog at 54.9% identity with an explicit tryptophan transport assignment and a substrate-consumption phenotype. Homolog summaries, fitness, query domains, neighborhoods, STRING orthogroup evidence, and topology predictions are available; no cofitness data are supplied. The closer 98.2%-identical homolog establishes regulation and a predicted transporter role without identifying a substrate; hypotaurine and tyrosine assignments from more distant homologs provide weaker substrate support than the same-species AatX evidence.

why these terms
The protein is annotated as a putative sodium-dependent tryptophan transporter and a member of the sodium:neurotransmitter symporter family. The reasoning trace provides strong support for tryptophan transport based on an experimentally characterized homolog (54.9% identity) with a substrate-consumption phenotype. Consequently, 'tryptophan transport' (BP) and 'L-tryptophan transmembrane transporter activity' (MF) are appropriate. Additionally, the 'sodium-dependent' and 'symporter' descriptions justify 'symporter activity' (MF) and 'sodium ion binding' (MF). I stop at 'L-tryptophan transmembrane transporter activity' as there is no evidence for high- or low-affinity specifics.
