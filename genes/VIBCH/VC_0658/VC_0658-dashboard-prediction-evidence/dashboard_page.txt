why
A 99.1%-identical Vibrio cholerae homolog binds c-di-GMP experimentally in a DRaCALA screen. Query InterProScan identifies GGDEF and EAL domains, and topology prediction identifies two transmembrane helices. The phosphodiesterase-like family assignment and distant BifA-related phenotypes support c-di-GMP signaling but do not establish catalytic activity or bifunctionality.
Why this tier
Tier A is supported by experimentally demonstrated binding of the explicit ligand c-di-GMP by a 99.1%-identical homolog. Available evidence includes homolog summaries, query domains and topology, distant-homolog fitness, neighborhoods, and STRING associations; no cofitness or direct catalytic assays are supplied. Diguanylate cyclase and phosphodiesterase activities are not assigned because domain membership and distant-homolog phenotypes do not demonstrate either reaction in this protein.

2 terms
why these terms
The annotation explicitly states the protein is an 'Integral inner-membrane' protein and a 'c-di-GMP-binding protein'. In bacteria, the 'inner membrane' is synonymous with the 'plasma membrane' or 'cytoplasmic membrane'. The reasoning trace confirms that binding of c-di-GMP was experimentally demonstrated in a nearly identical homolog. The trace specifically rejects assigning diguanylate cyclase or phosphodiesterase activities (catalytic functions associated with GGDEF and EAL domains) because they were not demonstrated for this specific protein. Therefore, only the binding function and the cellular location are assigned.
