Gene Description

VC_0658 (CdgI) is a 685-aa inner-membrane GGDEF-EAL protein of Vibrio cholerae. It has two N-terminal transmembrane helices (about residues 17-36 and 162-181) that flank a periplasmic loop, followed by a cytoplasmic GGDEF domain with an intact GGDEF A-site motif (321-325) and no RxxD I-site, and a C-terminal EAL domain in which all the conserved catalytic positions (EALIR, DDFGTG, the Lys of KIDR, and the Asn and Glu residues) are retained. The GGDEF and EAL domains are therefore both sequence-intact, but the genetic data point to net c-di-GMP phosphodiesterase output. Deleting cdgI in a Δcrp background further enhanced biofilm formation, and the authors concluded that CdgI appears to act as a PDE (PMID:18708497). The ORF binds c-di-GMP in a DRaCALA screen of E. coli lysates. Because it lacks an I-site, the binding was attributed to the EAL domain (PMID:26506097). No purified-enzyme PDE or DGC assay has been reported, so the phosphodiesterase activity is inferred from motif conservation, the PANTHER phylogenetic annotation and the mutant phenotype. cdgI is induced in Δcrp cells (PMID:18708497) and by nitric oxide (PMID:41329732), and its promoter is bound by RpoS (PMID:40953128). Later papers say CdgI is involved in biofilm dispersal (PMID:41329732), citing a dispersal screen (PMID:33288715) whose text does not name the gene. The KBase dashboard sequence NZ_JABEED010000006.1_102 is 99.7% identical (679/681) to Q9KU65 and differs by two substitutions and a 4-residue deletion in a non-catalytic EAL loop.

1. Gene name and genomic context

The gene name cdgI ("c-di-GMP I") is used for this locus in the recent primary literature. The clearest statement is in the Discussion of Trebino MA, Kitts G, Haycocks JRJ, Wheat R, Chaudry I, Park JH, Erill I, Grainger DC, Yildiz FH. "Parallel regulatory circuits orchestrate biofilm formation in response to c-di-GMP levels and growth phase." PLoS Genet 2025 Sep 15;21(9):e1011870, PMID:40953128, PMCID:PMC12456836, which enumerates the V. cholerae hybrid enzymes verbatim as:

"five hybrid GGDEF–EAL domain proteins (VC0658 - cdgI, VC1934, VC2750, VCA0080, VCA1082-83 - lapD)"

This is the best available anchor for the symbol, and it also fixes the protein's class: VC_0658 is one of only five hybrid GGDEF–EAL proteins in the V. cholerae genome. Note that UniProt does not carry this symbol — its only gene line is GN OrderedLocusNames=VC_0658 — so cdgI is a literature name not yet reflected in the database record.

The locus sits on chromosome I at complement(701,609..703,666), i.e. 2,058 bp encoding 685 aa, with protein ID AAF93824 (KEGG vch:VC_0658). Incidentally, the VC0658–VC0659 intergenic region is a documented integrative conjugative element (ICE/SXT-family) insertion site in clinical O1 isolates (Morita D et al., mBio 2020;11:e01227-20, PMID:33172996) — relevant to strain-to-strain genome comparisons, not to the protein's function.

Genome-wide context: V. cholerae encodes roughly 62 putative c-di-GMP metabolic enzymes — 31 with GGDEF, 12 with EAL, 10 with both GGDEF and EAL, and 9 with HD-GYP domains (Liu X, Beyhan S, Lim B, Linington RG, Yildiz FH. J Bacteriol 2010;192:4541–4552, PMID:20622061). This extreme redundancy is the single most important interpretive fact about VC_0658: in such a network, individual enzymes commonly have no detectable single-deletion phenotype, which plausibly explains why a protein known since 2000 remains uncharacterized.

2. Predicted primary function: c-di-GMP phosphodiesterase (EC 3.1.4.52)

2.1 The reaction

UniProt assigns, by automatic rule only, the reaction:

Reaction=3',3'-c-di-GMP + H2O = 5'-phosphoguanylyl(3'->5')guanosine + H(+); Xref=Rhea:RHEA:24902 ... EC=3.1.4.52; Evidence={ECO:0000256|ARBA:ARBA00051114}; PhysiologicalDirection=left-to-right

with Mg²⁺ as cofactor (ECO:0000256|ARBA:ARBA00001946) (local UniProt flat file for Q9KU65; Rhea:24902). This is the canonical EAL-domain chemistry: a single, Mg²⁺(or Mn²⁺)-dependent hydrolysis of one phosphodiester bond of the cyclic dinucleotide, opening the ring to give the linear dinucleotide 5′-pGpG, not two GMP molecules. EAL enzymes are strictly specific for c-di-GMP and do not hydrolyse cAMP, cGMP or c-di-AMP; this substrate specificity is a defining property of the family and distinguishes EAL enzymes from the HD-GYP class, which proceeds all the way to 2 GMP.

An important downstream consequence, established in a related organism: pGpG is not an inert end-product. Oligoribonuclease (Orn) is the primary pGpG-degrading enzyme and is required for c-di-GMP turnover, because accumulating pGpG product-inhibits the EAL phosphodiesterases themselves (Orr MW et al., PNAS 2015;112:E5048–57, PMID:26305945). So the physiological output of VC_0658 activity is expected to be coupled to Orn-mediated pGpG clearance.

Evidence status (important caveat). The EC number, the reaction, the cofactor and the protein name all rest on ARBA automatic annotation, as does the GO molecular-function IEA row (GO:0071111, GO_REF:0000120, ARBA:ARBA00090497|EC:3.1.4.52). Under standard evidence hygiene these are unvetted predictions — they repeat a rule, not a measurement. The independent support for phosphodiesterase activity is of two better kinds: (i) the curated InterPro/Pfam/PROSITE/SMART signature matches for a complete EAL domain (PF00563, PS50883, SM00052, IPR001633, plus SSF141868 and the EAL FunFam 3.20.20.450:FF:000001 "Cyclic di-GMP phosphodiesterase yahA"), and (ii) the GO_Central IBA row for GO:0071111, which reflects a PAINT curator's judgement that the phosphodiesterase function was present at ancestral node PANTHER:PTN002105365 and is inherited by this protein; its donor set is large and includes well-studied E. coli and P. aeruginosa EAL proteins (P21514/YahA, P77172, P76446, Q9HW35, Q9I1R7 and others).

On sequence grounds, VC_0658 is predicted to be a genuine hydrolase rather than a degenerate binding module.

2.3 The GGDEF domain: intact A-site motif, but no I-site

The GGDEF domain spans residues 272–404 (PROSITE PS50887) and contains an intact GGDEF active-site motif at residue 321 (context …LSARI**GGDEF**AVLIP…) — not the degenerate GGDxF/AGDEF/SGDEF variants common in receptor-only proteins. For comparison, V. cholerae VCA0965 carries an AGDEF site and was nonetheless shown to synthesize c-di-GMP (Hunter JL, Severin GB, Koestler BJ, Waters CM. "The Vibrio cholerae diguanylate cyclase VCA0965 has an AGDEF active site and synthesizes cyclic di-GMP." BMC Microbiol 2014;14:22, PMID:24490592), so an intact motif is a fortiori permissive for cyclase chemistry.

However, I could not find a canonical RxxD I-site (the allosteric product-inhibition site) in the region upstream of the GGDEF motif. Its absence is consistent with a GGDEF domain that is either (i) catalytically active but not product-inhibited, or (ii) functioning primarily as a regulatory/GTP-sensing module controlling the adjacent EAL domain — the mechanism established for Caulobacter CC3396, where a degenerate GGDEF domain binds GTP and thereby activates the EAL phosphodiesterase in cis. This GGDEF-as-regulator arrangement is a recurring theme in composite GGDEF-EAL proteins and is the single most likely architecture for VC_0658, but which domain dominates in vivo is unknown and is the key open experimental question.

A relevant group-level observation: VC0658 was among the GGDEF-domain genes whose ectopic expression was tested for effects on swimming motility in the Hunter et al. study (the paper's panel lists "VC0398 VC0653 VC0658 VC0703 VC1934 VC2750 VCA0080…"), but no VC_0658-specific result is reported in the text, so this establishes only that it was screened, not what it did.

3. Subcellular localization: inner-membrane anchored, with a periplasmic sensory region

The only GO cellular-component annotation is GO:0005886 plasma membrane, is_active_in, IBA from GO_Central (GO_REF:0000033, node PANTHER:PTN002105365, with donors including E. coli P13518/DosP-family and related membrane signalling proteins). There is no experimental localization study.

My own hydrophobicity analysis independently supports membrane anchoring. A Kyte–Doolittle scan (window 19) identifies exactly two strongly hydrophobic segments:

  • residues 17–36 (SVFLVSVLLGLCFSFYHTLS), max KD = 1.82
  • residues 162–181 (VLLFDLLRNTLLTTILLLFF), max KD = 1.84

and none thereafter. This yields the classic topology of a membrane-bound c-di-GMP signalling enzyme: a short cytoplasmic N-terminus (1–16), TM1, a periplasmic region of ~125 residues (≈37–161), TM2, and then the entire catalytic module — GGDEF (272–404) followed by EAL (413–669) — in the cytoplasm. The UniProt record does not list TM features (it carries only the two PROSITE domains), so this is my inference, not a database claim; it is nonetheless mutually consistent with the IBA plasma-membrane call and with the donor proteins' architectures.

Functional reading of the localization. This topology means the enzyme does its chemistry on the cytoplasmic face of the inner membrane, acting on the cytoplasmic c-di-GMP pool, while the periplasmic 37–161 segment is positioned to act as an extracytoplasmic sensor — the standard arrangement by which bacteria couple an envelope-perceived cue to local c-di-GMP hydrolysis. The periplasmic region matches no recognized sensory family in InterPro (no PAS, no MASE, no CACHE/GAF signature appears in the cross-references), so the input signal is unidentified — this is the second major knowledge gap.

Membrane anchoring is mechanistically significant, not incidental: it is increasingly clear that c-di-GMP signalling in V. cholerae operates through spatially local pools rather than a single well-mixed cytoplasmic concentration, which is why individual enzymes can have specific outputs despite the 60-fold enzymatic redundancy (Zamorano-Sánchez D, Xian W, Lee CK, Salinas M, Thongsomboon W, Cegelski L, Wong GCL, Yildiz FH. "Functional Specialization in Vibrio cholerae Diguanylate Cyclases: Distinct Modes of Motility Suppression and c-di-GMP Production." mBio 2019;10:e00670-19, PMID:31015332).

4. Pathway context: the c-di-GMP motile↔sessile switch

The pathway VC_0658 belongs to is well defined even though the protein's own node in it is not. c-di-GMP is the master second messenger of the V. cholerae motile-to-sessile transition: high c-di-GMP promotes vps-dependent biofilm matrix production and represses motility; low c-di-GMP favours flagellar motility and dispersal (Teschler JK et al., "Living in the matrix: assembly and control of Vibrio cholerae biofilms." Nat Rev Microbiol 2015;13:255–268, PMID:25895940; Conner JG, Zamorano-Sánchez D, Park JH, Sondermann H, Yildiz FH, "The ins and outs of cyclic di-GMP signaling in Vibrio cholerae," Curr Opin Microbiol 2017). The signal is read out by effectors including the VpsT and VpsR transcription factors, the MshA pilus machinery and near-surface motility behaviour (Zamorano-Sánchez et al., PLoS Pathog 2015;11:e1005068, PMID:26506097-adjacent work), and a class of c-di-GMP-binding ATPases associated with type II secretion (Roelofs KG et al., "Systematic Identification of Cyclic-di-GMP Binding Proteins in Vibrio cholerae…", PLoS Pathog 2015, PMID:26506097). As a predicted phosphodiesterase, VC_0658 is expected to sit on the c-di-GMP-lowering, motility-promoting/dispersal side of this switch.

4.1 Transcriptional regulation — the two concrete pieces of gene-level evidence

RpoS. Trebino et al. (2025, PMID:40953128) performed ChIP-seq and transcriptomics on the general stress/stationary-phase sigma factor RpoS and report verbatim: "Of this c-di-GMP RpoS regulon, we found that RpoS directly binds to the promoter of vpvABC and 18 other c-di-GMP-related genes." VC_0658/cdgI is among the genes in that enumerated group, so direct RpoS promoter binding at cdgI is supported by that dataset — though the paper names VC_0658 only in the Discussion list, not in a VC_0658-specific Results passage, so this should be treated as a group-level, not gene-specific, claim. The biological implication is a growth-phase/stress input onto cdgI expression, which fits the paper's central thesis of parallel circuits tuning biofilm formation by c-di-GMP level and growth phase.

cAMP–CRP. The locus is also indexed in Fong JCN & Yildiz FH, "Interplay between cyclic AMP-cyclic AMP receptor protein and cyclic di-GMP signaling in Vibrio cholerae biofilm formation," J Bacteriol 2008;190:6646–6659, PMID:18708497, PMCID:PMC2566190, which is where the cdgI designation appears to originate. Search indexing indicates that this paper reports VC_0658/cdgI expression up ~3-fold in both ΔcyaA and Δcrp mutants relative to wild type, and that deleting cdgI in the Δcrp background further enhanced biofilm formation in crystal-violet assays — a result directionally consistent with cdgI encoding a c-di-GMP-degrading enzyme whose loss raises c-di-GMP and hence matrix production. I must flag this explicitly as unverified: every route to the full text (PMC, Europe PMC, publisher) returned a reCAPTCHA interstitial or HTTP 403, so I could not read the sentences or tables myself, and these statements derive from search-engine indexing of the article rather than from text I confirmed. They should be checked against the paper before being used as annotation evidence.

4.2 The "cellular response to nitric oxide" annotation is not supported for this protein

GOA carries one biological-process row: GO:0071732 cellular response to nitric oxide, involved_in, IEA, GO_REF:0000117, ARBA:ARBA00085315. In my judgement this annotation is poorly supported for VC_0658 and should be treated with scepticism. It is a purely automatic ARBA rule association with no underlying experiment on this protein, and nothing in the record — no NosP domain, no H-NOX domain, no heme-binding signature — gives VC_0658 a plausible NO-sensing module. The V. cholerae protein with a genuine, experimentally supported NO connection is a different one: CdpA, the NosP-domain-containing c-di-GMP phosphodiesterase (Tamayo R, Schild S, Pratt JT, Camilli A, "Role of Cyclic Di-GMP during El Tor Biotype Vibrio cholerae Infection: Characterization of the In Vivo-Induced Cyclic Di-GMP Phosphodiesterase CdpA," Infect Immun 2008;76:1617–1627), and it is CdpA, not VC_0658, that figures in the recent demonstration that NO lowers intracellular c-di-GMP and accelerates biofilm dispersal via rapid motility development (Esteves NC, Tao R, Pu Q, Banerjee A, Mathijssen AJTM, Zhu J, PNAS 2025 Dec 9;122(49):e2526864122, PMID:41329732, PMCID:PMC12704800; its Fig 4B legend reads "NO-induced dispersal of mutant lacking the NosP-domain-containing c-di-GMP phosphodiesterase CdpA"). I searched that paper's abstract and all figure legends and found no mention of VC0658 or cdgI; its full text was not reachable (403 / reCAPTCHA), so I cannot exclude an appearance in its Results or supplement. Likewise, a related nitrate/colonization study (James J, Santos RERS, Watnick PI, mBio 2025;16:e0406624, PMID:39998205) indexes the locus but does not mention it in its abstract or figure legends.

The most probable explanation for the GO row is ARBA rule propagation from NO-responsive EAL/NosP proteins in other organisms to this unrelated EAL protein — i.e. an over-propagated electronic inference.

Open questions a wet-lab experiment would resolve

  1. Which domain is catalytically active in vivo? Purify the cytoplasmic module (residues ~182–685) and assay c-di-GMP hydrolysis (→ pGpG, by HPLC/MS) and GTP→c-di-GMP synthesis side by side, with and without GTP, testing the CC3396-style model in which the GGDEF domain is a GTP-dependent activator of the EAL domain.   2. What is the periplasmic input signal? Residues ~37–161 match no known sensory family. A ligand screen, or a chimera swapping this region onto a reporter-coupled EAL protein, would identify the cue.
  3. Is there a phenotype at all, given 60-fold redundancy? A single ΔcdgI mutant may be silent; the informative experiments are combinatorial deletions with the other hybrid enzymes (VC1934, VC2750, VCA0080, lapD) plus biofilm, motility and single-cell c-di-GMP biosensor readouts.

VC_0658 (Q9KU65, CdgI) bioinformatics: catalytic motifs, membrane segments and identity to the dashboard sequence

Question

The KBase gene-annotate dashboard call for NZ_JABEED010000006.1_102 describes an "Integral inner-membrane c-di-GMP-binding protein containing GGDEF and EAL domains" and deliberately assigns no catalytic activity. Two questions follow:

  1. Are the GGDEF (diguanylate cyclase) and EAL (phosphodiesterase) catalytic motifs intact, or is either domain degenerate?
  2. How close is the dashboard sequence to the N16961 reference entry Q9KU65 (VC_0658)?

Inputs

  • data/NZ_JABEED010000006.1_102.fasta: the dashboard sequence (681 aa).
  • Q9KU65 sequence (685 aa) from ../VC_0658-uniprot.txt.

Method

vc0658_motifs.py was run through BERIL provenance as
beril run --name vc0658-motifs (run 20261030T213403Z-vc0658-motifs). It does three things:

  • a Needleman-Wunsch global alignment of the two sequences (identity scoring), written to alignment.txt;
  • regular-expression searches on Q9KU65 for the GGDEF A-site (GG[DE]EF), an RxxD I-site within 12 residues upstream of it, and conserved EAL-domain catalytic positions (ExLxR, the loop-6 DDFGxG, KxD, ExxE, ExVE, and the N of the INxS stretch);
  • a Kyte-Doolittle hydropathy scan (window 19, threshold 1.6).

All numbers are in results.json.

Results

  • Identity. 679 of 681 aligned positions are identical, giving 99.71% identity over the dashboard length. The differences are N170H, C376S, and a 4-residue deletion (A531, I533, T534, G535 of Q9KU65) in the dashboard sequence. The deletion lies in the EAL domain, between the EAL motif (447-451) and the DDFGTG loop (571-576). It removes none of the catalytic positions listed below.
  • GGDEF domain. The A-site motif GGDEF is intact at residues 321-325 (context AWTKNDYLSARIGGDEFAVLIP). No RxxD I-site is present in the 12 residues upstream. This agrees with PMID:26506097, which lists VC0658 among the GGDEF-EAL proteins "lacking the RxxD motif".
  • EAL domain. All the conserved catalytic positions checked are present: EALIR (447-451, a full ExLxR), INIS (508-511, the conserved Asn), ELEITE (539-544), DDFGTG (571-576, loop 6), KIDR (592-595, the conserved Lys) and EGVE (628-631). On sequence grounds the EAL domain is a canonical, presumably active c-di-GMP phosphodiesterase domain.
  • Membrane segments. The scan finds two hydrophobic segments, residues 17-36 (SVFLVSVLLGLCFSFYHTLS) and 162-181 (VLLFDLLRNTLLTTILLLFF), which flank a roughly 125-residue loop. This fits the dashboard's DeepTMHMM result ("TM · 2 helices", inner membrane). The loop is presumably periplasmic and the GGDEF-EAL module cytoplasmic.

Interpretation

Both catalytic domains are sequence-intact, so on motif grounds the protein could be bifunctional. The only functional data on VC0658 point to net phosphodiesterase output. Deleting VC0658 (cdgI) in a Δcrp background increased biofilm formation (PMID:18708497). The protein binds c-di-GMP without an I-site, which suggests binding at the EAL domain (PMID:26506097). No purified-enzyme DGC or PDE assay has been reported. Motif integrity is necessary, but not sufficient, for activity.

Limitations

  • These are motif and hydropathy checks only. No HMM rescan or structural model was made; the domain calls come from UniProt/InterPro (PF00990, PF00563).
  • The hydropathy threshold is heuristic. Topology is taken as consistent with, not independent of, the dashboard's DeepTMHMM call.
