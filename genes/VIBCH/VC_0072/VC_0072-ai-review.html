Gene Description

VC0072 is a 768-aa uncharacterized PAS-GGDEF-EAL protein of V. cholerae N16961, anchored in the inner membrane by an N-terminal hydrophobic segment and a single transmembrane helix (57-79), with its PAS, GGDEF and EAL domains in the cytoplasm. Both output domains keep canonical catalytic motifs: GGDEF has the GGDEF A-site, the DLD metal-binding motif and the HDAGD loop, and EAL has EVLVR, the N-loop Asn, DDFGSG, the catalytic Lys and the conserved Glu residues. The GGDEF domain lacks an RxxD I-site. In a DRaCALA ORFeome screen, lysates of E. coli expressing VC0072 bound 32P-c-di-GMP. Unlabeled c-di-GMP competed this binding but GTP and cGMP did not, and the authors attributed the binding to the EAL domain (PMID:26506097). Neither diguanylate cyclase nor phosphodiesterase activity has been measured, and no deletion phenotype has been reported. The gene is repressed during infant-mouse colonization (an ivr gene), and constitutive expression did not reduce colonization fitness (PMID:29463743). The protein is best regarded as a c-di-GMP-metabolizing enzyme, most likely a PDE and possibly bifunctional, with no assigned physiological role. The KBase dashboard sequence NZ_JAACMT010000013.1_13 is 99.7% identical to Q9KVR7 over 768 aligned residues. It is 17 aa longer because its gene model starts at an upstream in-frame start codon.

Core Functions

Inner-membrane-anchored PAS-GGDEF-EAL c-di-GMP-metabolizing protein. Its EAL domain is catalytically intact and binds c-di-GMP specifically, so it is predicted to hydrolyze c-di-GMP. The GGDEF domain also has an intact catalytic signature, so the protein may be bifunctional. Neither activity has been measured, and its physiological role in V. cholerae is unknown.

2. Summary

VC_0072 is one of the roughly ten V. cholerae proteins that carry both a GGDEF (diguanylate cyclase, DGC) domain and an EAL (c-di-GMP phosphodiesterase, PDE) domain.

  • Location (inferred): it is predicted to sit in the inner membrane, with its PAS sensory domain and both catalytic domains on the cytoplasmic side.
  • Catalytic sites (inferred): both domains keep their catalytic motifs, so the protein may make c-di-GMP from two GTP (DGC, EC 2.7.7.65) and/or break it down to pApG (PDE-A, EC 3.1.4.52).
  • Binding (shown experimentally): a genome-wide screen found that the purified protein binds c-di-GMP specifically. Because it lacks the RxxD inhibitory site (I-site), the authors proposed that the binding happens at the EAL domain (Roelofs et al., 2015, PLoS Pathog 11:e1005232; PMID 26506097).
  • Untested: whether it actually makes or degrades c-di-GMP, and what it does to biofilm or motility, has not been reported in any source I could read.

3. Sequence and domain analysis (my own analysis of the UniProt sequence)

3.1 N-terminal membrane anchor and likely topology

  • Lipobox (predicted): PROSITE PS51257 predicts a cleavable lipoprotein signal peptide (residues 1–24) with N-palmitoyl/S-diacylglycerol Cys25 (motif "LGC↓"). This is a rule-based prediction only (PRU00303).
  • Transmembrane helix (predicted): Phobius predicts a helix at residues 57–79 (ECO:0000256|SAM:Phobius).
  • Most likely topology: two N-terminal hydrophobic segments (the 1–24 segment acting as a signal or transmembrane anchor, plus TM 57–79). That gives a short periplasmic loop of about 30 residues and a large cytoplasmic part (residues ~80–768) holding the PAS, GGDEF and EAL domains. This is the usual layout of membrane-bound c-di-GMP enzymes such as E. coli DgcE and V. cholerae MbaA.
  • Lipoprotein call is doubtful: a periplasmic lipoprotein with a downstream TM helix and cytoplasmic catalytic domains would be topologically incoherent, and the catalytic domains need cytoplasmic GTP.
  • GOA: the plasma-membrane location is consistent with the existing IBA annotation, GO:0005886.

3.2 PAS sensor domain(s)

  • Domain calls: PAS_9 (Pfam PF13426), PROSITE PS50112 at 214–269, and SMART SM00091 ×2, which indicates a second, less canonical PAS repeat around 100–210.
  • Likely role: in GGDEF/EAL proteins, PAS domains usually sense signals such as O₂, redox state or small ligands, or mediate dimerization. They control which catalytic output dominates.
  • Unknown: no ligand has been identified for VC_0072's PAS domain.

3.3 GGDEF domain (DGC): catalytic motifs intact

Feature VC_0072 Canonical Interpretation
Metal-binding D "LYLDLD" (~D388) DxLD intact
GTP-binding loop "NDTAGHDAGD" NDxxGHxxGD intact
A-site GGDEF at 417–421 GG(D/E)EF intact
I-site (RxxD) absent RxxD ~5–10 aa from A-site no allosteric product inhibition
  • Active site: the A-site and the conserved β-hairpin and metal-binding residues are intact, so a functional DGC is predicted.
  • Caveat: many GGDEF–EAL proteins with intact GGDEF motifs work mainly as PDEs, or use the GGDEF domain to regulate the EAL domain. Examples are Caulobacter CC3396 and Pseudomonas RbdA and MorA.
  • Missing I-site: Roelofs et al. independently noted this: "Four ORFs (VC0072, VC0658, VC0703 (MbaA), VC1934) were identified with both GGDEF and EAL domains, but lacking the RxxD motif, suggesting that c-di-GMP binding occurs at the EAL domain" (PMID 26506097).

3.4 EAL domain (PDE-A): one conservative substitution

  • Signature motif: "LHFEVLVR" (E547-V548-L549) replaces the canonical EAL. EVL is a conservative change that is often found in active EAL PDEs.
  • Other catalytic residues match the canonical EAL PDE set (as defined for TBD1265, RocR, BlrP1 and others):
  • "INLSGN": the catalytic N/Q loop
  • "EITE": the metal-coordinating E
  • DDFGSG (668–673): the DDFGTG loop, with a conservative S for T
  • "VKIDGIF": the catalytic K and metal-binding D (loop 6 region)
  • "VAEFVEN": the general base E in the "AEGVE" motif
  • "QGYLFSRP": the QGYxxxRP dimerization/loop motif
  • Overall: the EAL domain looks fully competent to hydrolyze c-di-GMP to 5′-pGpG in a Mg²⁺/Mn²⁺-dependent reaction (my sequence inference; not tested biochemically).
  • Context: CdgC (VC0658), the best-characterized close relative in the same genome with a GGDEF–EAL layout, has an active EAL domain and a predicted inactive GGDEF domain (Lim et al., 2007, J Bacteriol 189:717).

4. Experimental evidence specific to VC_0072

4.1 c-di-GMP binding (DRaCALA): positive

Roelofs et al. (2015; Lee and Yildiz labs) expressed nearly all V. cholerae ORFs as His and His-MBP fusions and screened them for ³²P-c-di-GMP binding by DRaCALA (PLoS Pathog 11:e1005232, Oct 2015; PMID 26506097).

Table 1 results for VC0072 (fraction of ³²P-c-di-GMP bound, by competitor):

Construct No competitor Unlabeled c-di-GMP cGMP GTP
His-ORF 0.36 0.00* 0.49 0.48
His-MBP-ORF 0.47 0.02* 0.51 0.55

* p < 0.001 (two-way ANOVA against no competitor).

  • Specificity: only unlabeled c-di-GMP competed binding away; cGMP and GTP did not. So the binding is specific for c-di-GMP.
  • Classification: Table 2 lists VC0072 under "EAL" as "Predicted | Positive", meaning a predicted binder that was experimentally confirmed.
  • Interpretation: binding without an I-site fits substrate binding at the EAL active site. That supports, but does not prove, PDE activity. A substrate-binding EAL domain can be either catalytic or a degenerate receptor; in VC_0072 the catalytic residues are intact (section 3.4).

4.2 Systematic phenotypic screens

  • Hunter et al. 2014: this study of the degenerate DGC VCA0965 tested all predicted V. cholerae DGCs for whether overexpressing them inhibits swimming motility. VC0072 appears among the tested GGDEF proteins in a figure (Hunter et al., 2014, BMC Microbiol 14:22; PMC version). I could not get the full text, so I cannot report VC0072's specific result.
  • Biofilm deletion screen: a 2010 Yildiz-lab screen deleted every GGDEF/EAL gene and looked for effects on biofilm and vps expression. It identified CdgJ (VC0137) as the gene whose loss affected these; VC0072 was not singled out (Liu et al., 2010, J Bacteriol).
  • Larger profiling efforts: later studies of all ~53 c-di-GMP modulators (including a 2024 bioRxiv preprint, doi 10.1101/2024.09.01.610008) list VC0072. I could not get its specific data (the server rate-limited my access).
  • Bottom line: no published experiment I could verify assigns VC_0072 a measured DGC or PDE activity, a change in cellular c-di-GMP, or a biofilm or motility phenotype.

5. Pathway context: the V. cholerae c-di-GMP network

  • Network size: the genome encodes roughly 31 GGDEF-only, 12 EAL-only, 10 GGDEF–EAL and 9 HD-GYP proteins. This is one of the largest c-di-GMP networks studied, as summarized in Hunter et al. 2014 and Tischler & Camilli, 2005, Infect Immun.
  • High c-di-GMP effects:
  • It activates vps (Vibrio polysaccharide) gene expression through VpsR and VpsT, causing rugose colonies and biofilm (Beyhan et al., 2006, Mol Microbiol).
  • It promotes MSHA pilus extension and surface attachment through the MshE ATPase receptor (Floyd et al., 2020, Nat Commun; Jones et al., 2015, PLoS Pathog).
  • It induces type II secretion (Sloup et al., 2017, J Bacteriol).
  • It represses FlrA-dependent flagellar gene transcription and motility (Srivastava et al., 2013, Mol Microbiol; PMID 24134710).
  • It positively regulates DNA repair (Fernandez et al., 2018, J Bacteriol).
  • Low c-di-GMP effects: motility and virulence gene expression (toxT, ctx, tcp) are favoured (Tischler & Camilli 2005).
  • Where VC_0072 fits: as a membrane-anchored, PAS-regulated GGDEF–EAL protein, it most likely acts as a signal-responsive local source or sink of c-di-GMP. In that role it would feed into one or more of these outputs, probably locally ("high-specificity" signalling, as described for V. cholerae DGCs). Which output it controls is unknown.

7. Open questions and suggested experiments

  1. Which activity dominates? Purify the cytoplasmic region (~80–768) and assay it with GTP (DGC) and with c-di-GMP (PDE). Test the GGDEF→GGAAF and EVL→AAA (or E547A) mutants.
  2. What does the PAS domain sense? Screen for heme or flavin cofactors and test effects of O₂ or redox state. Ask whether the periplasmic loop senses an external signal.
  3. What does it do in vivo? Examine a ΔVC_0072 mutant and an overexpression strain for c-di-GMP levels (by LC-MS/MS), vps reporter activity, biofilm, motility and MSHA pilus dynamics. Do this in both the high-c-di-GMP (rugose) and the wild-type backgrounds.
  4. Topology: test the lipoprotein vs. two-TM model with PhoA/LacZ fusions.

VC_0072 (Q9KVR7) bioinformatics: GGDEF/EAL motif check and dashboard gene model

Question

The dashboard call ("c-di-GMP-binding protein containing PAS, GGDEF, and EAL domains")
declines to name a catalytic activity. Are the GGDEF (diguanylate cyclase) and EAL
(c-di-GMP phosphodiesterase) catalytic motifs intact, is there a GGDEF I-site (RxxD), and
how does the 785-aa dashboard sequence relate to the 768-aa UniProt entry?

Method

analyze.py (stdlib Python, regex motif search on the UniProt Q9KVR7 sequence within the
PROSITE domain boundaries GGDEF 368-501 and EAL 512-767; difflib comparison of the
dashboard sequence with UniProt). Run through BERIL:
beril run --name vc0072-motifs -> run 20261030T213409Z-vc0072-motifs
(projects/hackathon/runs/20261030T213409Z-vc0072-motifs/out/results.json, copied here
as results.json). An earlier run 20261030T213353Z-vc0072-motifs wrote nothing because
the output-directory variable was not expanded; it should be ignored.

Motif positions are given in UniProt Q9KVR7 numbering. Motif expectations follow the
canonical GGDEF (PleD/WspR) and EAL (RocR/YkuI/FimX) literature; no structural
alignment to a characterized enzyme was done.

Results

Dashboard vs UniProt gene model

  • The dashboard sequence carries an 18-residue N-terminal segment MKLKCAYFTTIKTIGLTL
    before the residue that aligns with UniProt position 2 (E). UniProt Met1 therefore
    corresponds to dashboard Leu18, i.e. the N16961 gene model starts at an alternative
    (non-ATG, likely TTG/CTG) codon, and the dashboard (draft genome NZ_JAACMT010000013.1)
    gene caller chose an in-frame upstream start. Net length difference 17 aa (785 vs 768).
  • Only two substitutions in the aligned region: dashboard V84 / UniProt A67 (inside the
    predicted TM helix) and dashboard E381 / UniProt D364 (just before the GGDEF domain).
    99.7% identity over 768 aligned residues.
  • Neither difference touches any catalytic motif. Which start codon is used in vivo is
    unknown; both models keep the hydrophobic N-terminal region and the single TM helix
    (UniProt 57-79; DeepTMHMM on the dashboard sequence: TM 78-96, cytoplasmic 97-785).

GGDEF domain (UniProt 368-501)

  • A-site: GGDEF at 417-421 (intact).
  • Beta-1 metal-binding D[LI]D: DLD at 376-378 (context YLDLDQLK).
  • HxxGD (metal/GTP-binding loop): HDAGD at 389-393 (context TAGHDAGDGA).
  • I-site: the 9 residues preceding GGDEF are PFKATLARM; no RxxD in the canonical
    I-site position (RxxD-x5-GGDEF). The only RxxD in the domain (RMID, 465) is
    downstream of the GGDEF motif and not at the I-site position. This agrees with Roelofs
    et al. 2015 (PMID:26506097).
  • Conclusion: the GGDEF domain has the canonical catalytic signature and could be an
    active diguanylate cyclase, but activity has not been measured.

EAL domain (UniProt 512-767)

  • ExLxR motif: EVLVR at 548-552 (EVL variant of EAL; Glu and Arg retained).
  • Asn of the N-loop region: INLS at 606 (SINLSGNS).
  • Glu of ExxE-type motif: EITE at 639 (CFEITETAAM).
  • Loop 6: DDFGSG at 669-674 (ALDDFGSGLSSF; the conserved DDFGTG with T->S).
  • Catalytic Lys: KID at 690 (VDIVKIDGIF).
  • Glu in E[GF]VE: EFVE at 726 (TVAEFVENQQ).
  • Conclusion: all EAL residues normally needed for Mg2+-dependent c-di-GMP hydrolysis are
    present; the domain is predicted to be catalytically competent. Activity has not been
    measured for VC0072.

Interpretation

Both output domains retain canonical catalytic motifs, so VC0072 cannot be classed as a
degenerate (receptor-only) GGDEF-EAL protein on sequence grounds. Its experimentally shown
c-di-GMP binding (lysate DRaCALA, PMID:26506097) is most simply explained by substrate
binding at the intact EAL site. The protein is best described as a membrane-anchored
PAS-GGDEF-EAL c-di-GMP-metabolizing protein, probably a phosphodiesterase and possibly
bifunctional; which activity dominates in vivo is untested.
