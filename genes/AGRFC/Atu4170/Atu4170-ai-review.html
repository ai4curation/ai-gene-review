Gene Description

Atu4170 is a carboxyspermidine dehydrogenase that reductively condenses putrescine with L-aspartate 4-semialdehyde to form carboxyspermidine, the first intermediate in an alternative spermidine biosynthetic pathway. The adjacent NspC-family decarboxylase converts this intermediate to spermidine. Native C58 deletion and metabolite analysis establish the spermidine pathway. The enzyme pair also produces spermine and thermospermine through carboxylated intermediates in heterologous E. coli reconstruction. In C58, replacing putrescine supply with 1,3-diaminopropane or cadaverine permits synthesis of norspermidine or aminopropylcadaverine. Additional tetraamine products are detected when homospermidine or norspermidine is supplied to the reconstructed pathway; these activities do not establish the predominant substrates in the native bacterium.

Proposed New Ontology Terms

carboxyspermine/carboxythermospermine dehydrogenase activity

Definition: Catalysis of the reductive condensation of spermidine with L-aspartate 4-semialdehyde to form carboxyspermine or carboxythermospermine.

Justification: The 2025 reconstruction establishes the two-step aspartate semialdehyde-dependent route and separates the final products by LC-MS/MS. It must not be represented by the chemically different decarboxylated-S-adenosylmethionine-dependent spermine/thermospermine synthase activity terms. The intermediate isomers were not resolved and individual purified-enzyme kinetic constants were not measured. This is an assayed catalytic capability retained outside core_functions: native locus-dependent tetraamine flux and physiological centrality remain unestablished. Heterologous expression of a functional route does not by itself demonstrate conservation of that role.

Ontology and annotation decisions

QuickGO was queried on 2026-11-05. GO:0102143 is active and its definition explicitly says "Also converts carboxyspermidine to putrescine." GO:0102144 is obsolete as the same activity. No specific carboxyspermidine decarboxylase term was found by name; the supported NTR is used instead. Separate QuickGO name searches for carboxyspermine and carboxythermospermine each returned zero hits. Additional tetraamine chemistry is recorded with proposed MF terms and the distinct GO:0006597/GO:1903603 process terms. The added spermidine, spermine, thermospermine and conditional norspermidine BP terms are siblings, not ancestor/descendant duplicates.

The participation test is satisfied because CASDH catalyzes the condensation and CASDC the decarboxylation. This is not an argument from an annotation-count gap. A QuickGO comparator check nevertheless found GO:0008295 on E. coli SpeE (P09158), GO:0006597 on human SMS (P52788), and GO:0008295/GO:0045312 on Vibrio NspC (Q9KRL4), all catalytic participants. No match for these four target accessions or locus names was found in gocams/index.tsv. Existing row payloads are preserved, with only reviews changed; new proposals are appended and marked NEW.

The generic source protein name is not functional evidence. In particular, the ProtNLM saccharopine-dehydrogenase name on A9CG66 conflicts with locus-specific pathway characterization; domain homology alone does not supply that reaction. No new PAINT node placement or family-wide specificity is inferred here.

PR6089 item 3: native tetraamine context and BP proposals (2026-11-05)

Re-read the primary full text of PMID:40074085 and the C58 experiments in PMID:26682642. The previous annotation audit correctly recognized direct catalytic work in the reconstructed route, but the separate NEW assertions GO:0006597 (spermine biosynthetic process) and GO:1903603 (thermospermine biosynthetic process) went beyond the native product resolution. Both authored NEW BP rows are withdrawn, not recoded as REMOVE: they were proposals, not seeded GOA annotations. All other annotation rows, including earlier supported NEW rows, remain unchanged.

Atu4170 is the CASDH performing reductive condensation: expression alone generated unresolved carboxyspermine/carboxythermospermine intermediates (Table 3). Table 1 resolves both final tetraamine products after expression of the accession-matched pair in E. coli. This supports the retained carboxyspermine/carboxythermospermine MF proposal, with the original assay limits; it does not independently show each native isomer-specific process.

PMID:26682642 Figure 4C measures C58 wild type treated with DFMO, an inhibitor of ODC, rather than testing tetraamine flux after deletion of Atu4170 or nspC/Atu4169. The native LC-MS peak is consistent with either tetraamine isomer. The 2025 paper explicitly states, "These studies did not resolve Spm from the same mass isomer Tspm." [PMID:40074085, New routes for spermine biosynthesis.] The absence of AdoMetDC and APT supports a plausible native alternative route and means the physiological context must not be dismissed as absent. However, exclusion of the canonical route cannot resolve the native product identity or prove the individual reviewed locus carries that flux. The inference is therefore retained as biological context rather than strengthened into two gene-specific BP assertions.

The prior comparator/parent checks remain documented above; human SMS and yeast SPE4 show use of the spermine BP for catalysts, while thermospermine BP lacked exact QuickGO annotations at the audit date. Those comparisons establish neither this target's native substrate usage nor its product identity. No redundant broad polyamine BP is substituted. Existing MF NTRs and core functions are unchanged. Native isomer-resolved metabolomics with CASDH/CASDC perturbation and complementation would distinguish the remaining hypotheses. The earlier default Falcon attempt/failure provenance remains unchanged; this is a focused primary-literature revision, not a new provider report.
