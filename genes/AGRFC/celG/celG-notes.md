# celG evidence and identity review

Q8U4V1 / Atu8186 is748 aa, crossreferenced to AAK90086.2. Preserved GenBankAAK90086.1 and UniSavev1 are751 aa, whereas currentAAK90086.2 is748 aa. The2018 review's Table2 instead lists Atu3306 and781 aa. Neither sequence version supports781 residues. Exact mapping of the historical Atu3306 designation and mutant insertion to current Atu8186 remains unresolved; they are not asserted as synonymous loci. Current and first protein versions lack an obvious hydrophobic N-terminal signal, and the frozen UniProt has no signal peptide, transmembrane helix or porin feature. These discrepancies preclude assigning current protein localization from a schematic.

Primary2005 abstract [PMID:16167770] reports celG mutations causing cellulose overproduction by chemical fractionation/methylation. A primary-author DOE report (OSTI840242;2000–2004) independently reports celG insertion overproduction and explicitly says celG/celD were not demonstrated required for synthesis. The official PDF and unmodified pdftotext extraction are retained with provenance and visually checked. This phenotype cannot establish an essential export pore, and does not by itself establish a direct negative-regulatory molecular activity. Polarity, insertion position, gene model and native complementation remain material questions.

The2015 full review [PMID:26077867] calls typeIII CelG/BcsK a TPR-containing relative of the BcsC N-terminal region that lacks its C-terminal porin domain. Its scaffolding/peptidoglycan role is explicitly conjectural. The2018 full review [PMID:29998422] calls it an outer-membrane secretin while also acknowledging that the overproduction phenotype is unexplained. That secretin claim exceeds the cited2015 evidence and is flagged as disputed. This is not proof that CelG can never influence export, but no known pore or established export MF should be asserted.

There are zero original annotation rows. No artificial function or ancestor term is added to fill the empty review. The review records a literature-supported biology gap, retains the primary phenotype, and leaves core_functions empty pending evidence for an actual activity. This is a completed evidence review with unresolved biology, not an unperformed review.

Identity and strain scope

The current organism is Agrobacterium fabrum C58 (NCBITaxon176299), historically called A. tumefaciens C58. Indexed primary text of PMID:7860586 identifies A1045 as a chvB mutant of C58, corroborated by Table1 of primary PMC2293172. The available primary-paper copy is https://www.academia.edu/23372784/Mechanism_of_cellulose_synthesis_in_Agrobacterium_tumefaciens ; the independent strain table is https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2293172/ . Thus A1045 is a C58 lineage strain, not evidence for another wild-type accession. This does not establish exact identity of every historical engineered protein construct to the current UniProt sequence.
