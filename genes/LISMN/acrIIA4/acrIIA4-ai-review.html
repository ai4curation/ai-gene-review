Gene Description

AcrIIA4 is an 87-residue acidic protein encoded by prophages integrated in the genomes of Listeria monocytogenes, where it neutralises the host type II-A CRISPR-Cas9 adaptive immune system and so protects the resident prophage and any co-infecting phage from Cas9-mediated destruction. It acts as a nucleic acid mimic. A densely negatively charged surface imitates the phosphate backbone of a DNA duplex and competitively occupies the protospacer-adjacent-motif (PAM) recognition surface of guide-RNA-loaded Cas9, so the enzyme can no longer license a double-stranded DNA target. A protruding loop simultaneously reaches into the RuvC catalytic pocket and blocks entry of the non-target DNA strand, so both target licensing and non-target-strand cleavage are shut down. Binding is guide-dependent, because the surface AcrIIA4 docks onto only forms once Cas9 has loaded its guide RNA, which means the inhibitor engages a ribonucleoprotein rather than apo Cas9. Inhibition is stoichiometric and reversible, requiring the inhibitor in excess of its target, and it extends to Cas9 orthologues from other species because the mimicked surface is conserved. Prophage-encoded AcrIIA proteins are carried by more than half of cas9-bearing L. monocytogenes strains, making this counter-defence a common feature of the species rather than an isolated case.

Proposed New Ontology Terms

nucleic acid mimicry-based competitive inhibitor activity

Definition: A molecular function inhibitor activity in which a protein presents a surface whose shape and charge distribution imitate those of a nucleic acid, and thereby binds competitively to the nucleic-acid-binding site of a target protein or protein-containing complex, occluding that site so that the target cannot engage its genuine nucleic acid ligand. The inhibitor is not itself a nucleic acid, and because the mechanism is competitive occupancy rather than catalysis, inhibition is reversible and requires the inhibitor at or above the concentration of its target.

Justification: Mimicry-based competitive inhibition is the most common anti-CRISPR mechanism and is also used outside CRISPR biology, yet GO has no term for it, and every alternative loses the mechanism. A binding term such as GO:0043021 ribonucleoprotein complex binding records what is bound but not that binding is inhibitory, and GO:0005515 protein binding is uninformative. GO:0140721 nuclease inhibitor activity and the generic GO:0140678 molecular function inhibitor activity record that the target is inhibited but cannot distinguish a mimic from a domain blocker or from a catalytic inhibitor, which is precisely the distinction that matters, because a mimic competes with the substrate for one site and is therefore defeated by substrate excess whereas a catalytic inhibitor is not. GO:0140489 molecular template activity concerns templating polymer synthesis and is unrelated. The term is needed because anti-CRISPR proteins share no sequence homology, so mechanism is the only axis on which they can be compared, and without an MF term for mimicry that axis is invisible to GO-based reasoning. At least three proteins in this curation round would use it immediately, namely AcrIIA4 (this entry), the sequence-unrelated AcrIIA2 (UniProtKB:A0A059T5F6) which converges on the same Cas9 surface, and the unrelated type I-F inhibitor AcrF2/gp30 (UniProtKB:Q6TM72) which mimics a DNA duplex against a completely different effector. That three unrelated families converge on one mechanism is the argument that the mechanism, not the family, is the reusable unit.

Suggested Questions for Experts

Q: Does AcrIIA4 occupancy of the PAM-recognition surface displace an already bound target duplex, or can it only act on Cas9 that has not yet engaged DNA? The buried-area comparison implies the former is thermodynamically possible, but it has not been tested directly.

Suggested experts: Hongtu Zhao, Dinshaw J. Patel

Q: Is the acidic surface of AcrIIA4 under selection to track variation in the PAM-interacting domain across Cas9 orthologues, or is it conserved because it mimics a chemically invariant feature, the phosphate backbone, that no Cas9 can abandon?

Suggested experts: Joseph Bondy-Denomy, Jeong-Yong Suh

Q: Is there an Aca-like repressor in the acrIIA4 operon, and if not, what limits the cost of expressing a stoichiometric inhibitor in a lysogen?

Suggested experts: Joseph Bondy-Denomy

Suggested Experiments

Experiment: Form a dCas9-sgRNA-dsDNA ternary complex with radiolabelled target DNA, then titrate AcrIIA4 and follow DNA release by electrophoretic mobility shift assay, with unlabelled duplex as the competitor control. This is the experiment that distinguished a dislodging from a merely occluding inhibitor for AcrVA4 against Cas12a and it transfers directly.

Hypothesis: AcrIIA4 can displace a bound PAM duplex from Cas9, because it buries roughly three times the surface area that the duplex does.

Type: electrophoretic mobility shift competition assay

Experiment: Build a charge-neutralisation series across the beta3-alpha2 and alpha2-alpha3 loops, with single, double and triple Asp or Glu to Asn or Gln substitutions, confirm each variant is folded by NMR or circular dichroism, then measure Cas9 affinity by isothermal titration calorimetry alongside inhibition of DNA cleavage. A quantitative correlation between net surface charge and inhibitory potency, with the fold intact, would convert mimicry from a structural interpretation into a measured structure-activity relationship.

Hypothesis: The acidic patch is the inhibitory determinant, so charge rather than fold carries the function.

Type: charge-reversal mutagenesis with paired binding and activity readout

Experiment: Epitope-tag the chromosomal acrIIA4 in a natural L. monocytogenes lysogen, then quantify AcrIIA4 and Cas9 copies per cell by selected-reaction-monitoring mass spectrometry through exponential growth, stationary phase and mitomycin-C-induced prophage induction. The prediction specific to a stoichiometric inhibitor is that AcrIIA4 is in molar excess of Cas9 at every point where the prophage is at risk.

Hypothesis: AcrIIA4 is expressed at inhibitory levels from the resident prophage during natural lysogeny.

Type: targeted quantitative proteomics in a natural lysogen

Knowledge Gaps

What is not known — curated, literature-grounded statements of the open unknowns (the inverse of core functions).

Gap: GO has no molecular-function term for mimicry-based competitive inhibition, so the activity that is the whole point of AcrIIA4 cannot be asserted with an existing identifier.

OPEN ONTOLOGY

What is known: The process is well served by GO:0098672, and what AcrIIA4 binds is capturable with GO:0043021. What cannot be expressed is that the binding works by imitating the substrate and is therefore competitive and substrate-displaceable.

Significance: Anti-CRISPR families share no sequence homology, so mechanism is the only axis on which they can be compared. Without an MF term for mimicry, a DNA mimic, a catalytic-domain blocker and a guide-destroying enzyme all flatten to the same process term.

What would resolve it: Create the proposed term nucleic acid mimicry-based competitive inhibitor activity under GO:0140678, then move its identifier into the molecular_function slot here and in genes/9CAUD/acrIIA2 and genes/BPD31/orf30.

Provenance (the field's own admissions):

  • "It has been suggested that the negatively charged patches mimic the phosphate groups of nucleic acids that associate with the PAM interaction site of SpyCas9" — PMID:29497118

Gap: Whether AcrIIA4 is expressed from the resident prophage at a level and time that matters during a natural Listeria infection is undetermined; all inhibition data come from induced heterologous expression or from purified protein.

OPEN BIOLOGY

What is known: That AcrIIA4 inhibits Lmo Cas9 and SpyCas9, and how it does so structurally, are both firmly established. What is missing is the native expression regime.

Significance: Stoichiometric inhibitors must be present in excess of their target, so the native expression level is not a detail but a precondition for the mechanism to work in vivo. For the related AcrF8 locus the answer turned out to be a promoter burst followed by Aca-mediated repression, and no aca gene has been assigned to this locus.

What would resolve it: Measure AcrIIA4 protein abundance relative to Cas9 across lysogeny and prophage induction in a natural L. monocytogenes lysogen, and test for an aca-like repressor in the same operon.

Provenance (the field's own admissions):

  • "More than half of L. monocytogenes strains with cas9 contain at least one prophage-encoded inhibitor, suggesting widespread CRISPR-Cas9 inactivation." — PMID:28041849

Discovery and in vivo inhibition

AcrIIA4 was one of four type II-A Cas9 inhibitors found by searching cas9-bearing
genomes for a CRISPR spacer coexisting with its own target
PMID:28041849.
Two of the four, AcrIIA2 and AcrIIA4, also inhibit the heterologous S. pyogenes Cas9
PMID:28041849,
and the authors attribute the breadth to a conserved target surface
PMID:28041849.
Prophage carriage is common, so this is a widespread counter-defence and not a curiosity
PMID:28041849.

Mechanism: DNA mimicry at the PAM-recognition site, plus RuvC occlusion

The module (modules/anti_crispr_suppression.yaml, annoton acriia4_dna_mimic) assigns
AcrIIA4 to the "Type II Cas9 PAM-site occlusion and DNA mimicry" variant. The
assignment holds, and is if anything understated: two surfaces are occluded, not one.

Yang & Patel solved the AcrIIA4–SpyCas9–sgRNA crystal structure and found competitive
occupancy of both the PAM-reading surface and the RuvC pocket
PMID:28602637,
PMID:28602637.
The buried-area comparison is the quantitative statement of the competition
PMID:28602637,
and the β1–β2 loop reaches into the RuvC active site itself
PMID:28602637.

Dong et al., independently, state the mimicry explicitly
PMID:28448066
(abstract only in cache; no PMC record).

The physical basis of the mimicry is an acidic surface patch, characterised by NMR
PMID:29497118,
PMID:29497118.
The same paper notes convergence with the unrelated type I-F inhibitor AcrF2
PMID:29497118,
which is exactly the mechanism-not-family convergence the module is built around.

Binding is guide-dependent: the binding site on Cas9 only exists once sgRNA is loaded
PMID:28448066,
PMID:28448066,
and Yang & Patel devote a results section to it
PMID:28602637
— so AcrIIA4 engages a ribonucleoprotein, not apo Cas9.

Contrast with AcrIIC1, which also inhibits Cas9 but leaves DNA binding intact

(truncated in cache at this point) — the two mechanism classes in the module (part 1

Ontology gap

There is no GO molecular-function term for mimicry-based competitive inhibition. QuickGO
searches for "molecular mimicry" return only GO:0140489 molecular template activity and
the generic regulator terms; nothing for a protein that imitates a nucleic acid to occupy
its binding site. GO:0043021 ribonucleoprotein complex binding is true of AcrIIA4
(binding requires sgRNA-loaded Cas9) but says nothing about mimicry, and
GO:0140721 nuclease inhibitor activity is true but flattens the mechanism to generic
inhibition. A new term nucleic acid mimicry-based competitive inhibitor activity is
proposed under GO:0140678 molecular function inhibitor activity; it would serve
AcrIIA4, AcrIIA2 (genes/9CAUD/acrIIA2) and AcrF2/gp30 (genes/BPD31/orf30) at minimum.

GOA qualifier adjudication

GOA (CACAO) holds one row: acts_upstream_of_or_within_positive_effect GO:0098672, IDA,
PMID:28041849. The term is right. The qualifier is not well chosen: AcrIIA4 does not
act upstream of the suppression process, it is the executing step — it binds Cas9
directly and the binding is the suppression. involved_in is the better-supported
qualifier, and acts_upstream_of_or_within_positive_effect additionally reads as though
AcrIIA4 positively regulated something, which inverts the sense for a reader skimming the
GAF. Same issue on A0A2D0TCG3 (genes/NEIME/acrIIC1). Flagged in review.reason;
the annotation itself is accepted.

Scope note

AcrIIA4 is heavily used as a Cas9 "off-switch" reagent in human cells. That is a
laboratory application, not a biological function of the prophage gene, and is excluded
from the review's description and from all annotations.

proposed_new_terms:
- proposed_name: nucleic acid mimicry-based competitive inhibitor activity
  proposed_definition: A molecular function inhibitor activity in which a protein
    presents a surface whose shape and charge distribution imitate those of a nucleic
    acid, and thereby binds competitively to the nucleic-acid-binding site of a target
    protein or protein-containing complex, occluding that site so that the target
    cannot engage its genuine nucleic acid ligand. The inhibitor is not itself a nucleic
    acid, and because the mechanism is competitive occupancy rather than catalysis,
    inhibition is reversible and requires the inhibitor at or above the concentration
    of its target.
  justification: Mimicry-based competitive inhibition is the most common anti-CRISPR
    mechanism and is also used outside CRISPR biology, yet GO has no term for it,
    and every alternative loses the mechanism. A binding term such as GO:0043021 ribonucleoprotein
    complex binding records what is bound but not that binding is inhibitory, and
    GO:0005515 protein binding is uninformative. GO:0140721 nuclease inhibitor activity
    and the generic GO:0140678 molecular function inhibitor activity record that the
    target is inhibited but cannot distinguish a mimic from a domain blocker or from
    a catalytic inhibitor, which is precisely the distinction that matters, because
    a mimic competes with the substrate for one site and is therefore defeated by
    substrate excess whereas a catalytic inhibitor is not. GO:0140489 molecular template
    activity concerns templating polymer synthesis and is unrelated. The term is needed
    because anti-CRISPR proteins share no sequence homology, so mechanism is the only
    axis on which they can be compared, and without an MF term for mimicry that axis
    is invisible to GO-based reasoning. At least three proteins in this curation round
    would use it immediately, namely AcrIIA4 (this entry), the sequence-unrelated
    AcrIIA2 (UniProtKB:A0A059T5F6) which converges on the same Cas9 surface, and the
    unrelated type I-F inhibitor AcrF2/gp30 (UniProtKB:Q6TM72) which mimics a DNA
    duplex against a completely different effector. That three unrelated families
    converge on one mechanism is the argument that the mechanism, not the family,
    is the reusable unit.
