# Functional annotation of *snai2* in *Xenopus laevis*

## Identity and principal function

**Identity verified.** The supplied UniProt accession [Q91924](https://www.uniprot.org/uniprotkb/Q91924/entry) identifies *Xenopus laevis* Snai2, also called Slug, XSlug or slug-alpha. Primary *X. laevis* studies explicitly identify Slug as Snai2 and distinguish it from the related transcription factor Snail/Snai1. The experimentally studied XSlug protein contains a zinc-finger DNA-binding region, consistent with the supplied C2H2 zinc-finger annotations. These are not interchangeable gene names for Snai1 and Snai2. (zhang2006annfκband pages 1-2, aybar2003snailprecedesslug pages 2-3)

**Primary annotation:** Snai2 is a **DNA-binding transcriptional repressor** that helps establish and maintain neural-crest cell identity, promotes neural-crest epithelial-to-mesenchymal transition (EMT) and migration, and supports survival of embryonic cells. It is neither an enzyme nor a transporter: its zinc fingers recognize regulatory DNA, while associated corepressors provide chromatin-modifying activity. The strongest demonstrated *X. laevis* molecular mechanism is Snai2-dependent repression of **E-cadherin** expression through recruitment of the EZH2-containing Polycomb repressive complex 2 (PRC2) to its promoter. (langer2008ajubalimproteins pages 1-2, tien2015snail2slugcooperateswith pages 54-58, tien2015snail2slugcooperateswith pages 58-62)

## Molecular mechanism and site of action

Snail-family zinc fingers recognize E-box regulatory sequences; a vertebrate Snai2/Snail1 biochemical study identifies the preferred E2-box motifs as **CAGGTG/CACCTG**. Motif preference measured for vertebrate proteins should not be mistaken for a complete, experimentally established binding-specificity profile of Q91924. In *X. laevis* embryos, chromatin immunoprecipitation of **epitope-tagged Snai2** detected association with E-box-containing regions near the E-cadherin transcription start site and approximately 2.2 kb upstream, but not with several other candidate E-boxes within that gene. Thus promoter occupancy is demonstrated, although that experiment did not map every base required for binding by endogenous Snai2. (tien2015snail2slugcooperateswith pages 90-100, tien2015snail2slugcooperateswith pages 58-62)

Snai2 acts at **nuclear chromatin**, rather than as a secreted factor, cell-surface structural protein or cytoplasmic signaling receptor. In *X. laevis*, reciprocal co-immunoprecipitation and purified-protein pull-down showed interaction of Snai2 with **EZH2**, PRC2’s histone-methyltransferase subunit. At the late-neurula E-cadherin promoter, Snai2 depletion decreased both EZH2 occupancy and the repressive histone mark **H3K27me3**. This supports a model in which DNA-associated Snai2 recruits PRC2, which then modifies nearby histones to help repress E-cadherin transcription. Snai2 is **not** itself the enzyme depositing H3K27me3; purified PRC2 did not methylate Snai2 in the reported assay. Nuclear action is strongly supported by promoter occupancy and this chromatin mechanism, but the cited *X. laevis* studies do not establish an endogenous Snai2 nuclear-versus-cytoplasmic distribution by direct protein imaging. (tien2015snail2slugcooperateswith pages 62-66, tien2015snail2slugcooperateswith pages 54-58, tien2015snail2slugcooperateswith pages 58-62)

A complementary corepression mechanism involves Snai2’s N-terminal **SNAG repression region** and Ajuba-family LIM proteins. In *X. laevis* embryos, depleting XLIMD1 or XWTIP blocked neural-crest development; a Slug zinc-finger fusion supplied with an **Engrailed repression domain** rescued this defect, whereas the zinc-finger fragment alone did not. This genetic result supports the requirement for transcriptional repression. The particularly direct observations of Ajuba/Snail nuclear interaction and endogenous E-cadherin-promoter occupancy in that paper were obtained in **mammalian cells**, however, and should not be presented as endogenous-Q91924 localization assays in frog embryos. (langer2008ajubalimproteins pages 1-2, langer2008ajubalimproteins pages 2-4, langer2008ajubalimproteins pages 6-9)

## Biological process, regulatory pathways and tissue distribution

In *X. laevis*, Snai2/Slug is expressed in early mesendoderm and subsequently in the neural-crest-forming territory at the neural plate border. An embryo-stage comparison detected Snail/Snai1 at the border from approximately **stage 11**, followed by Slug/Snai2 from approximately **stage 12.5**. Conditional perturbations place Snail upstream of Slug in the tested neural-crest genetic cascade: Slug could rescue an inhibitory Snail construct, whereas Snail could not rescue an inhibitory Slug construct. This describes **genetic order**, not proof that Snail directly binds the *snai2* promoter. Later, Snai2 acts as neural-crest cells leave the dorsal neural tube and migrate; its developmental expression domain is a tissue location, distinct from its nuclear site of biochemical action. (aybar2003snailprecedesslug pages 2-3, aybar2003snailprecedesslug pages 7-8, montequin2025dynamicandnonuniform pages 1-5)

A mechanistically resolved output is **adhesion remodeling during neural-crest EMT**. In dissected neural-crest tissue, Snai2 or EZH2 depletion had no significant effect on E-cadherin RNA at early neurula stages **13–14**. At stages **19–20**, when crest cells undergo EMT, E-cadherin RNA increased **1.5-fold after Snai2 depletion** and **2.4-fold after EZH2 depletion** relative to controls. Together with promoter ChIP, these observations connect Snai2-dependent chromatin repression to the reduction of an epithelial adhesion gene at the appropriate developmental time. The larger EZH2 effect also cautions against treating all PRC2 activity as exclusively Snai2-dependent. **The paper’s Figure 8** presents the stage-specific expression and promoter-chromatin evidence. (tien2015snail2slugcooperateswith pages 58-62, tien2015snail2slugcooperateswith media d0451033)

Snai2 also participates in a **cell-survival/NF-κB regulatory circuit** during early *X. laevis* development. Slug morpholino treatment reduced mesodermal markers, including Xbra, and neural-crest marker Sox9, while increasing apoptosis. Bcl-xL rescued marker defects through IκB-kinase-dependent NF-κB activity; NF-κB activity increased Slug and Snail RNA levels. Slug increased several NF-κB-subunit RNAs indirectly and reduced pro-apoptotic **caspase-9 RNA** in a translation-independent assay. In the latter study, “direct” means that the measured RNA response did not require newly synthesized intermediary protein; **physical Snai2 binding to a caspase-9 promoter was not demonstrated**. The circuit should therefore not be interpreted as proof that each component is a direct DNA target of Snai2. (zhang2006annfκband pages 1-2, zhang2006annfκband pages 9-10, zhang2006annfκband pages 4-5, zhang2006annfκband pages 7-9)

Upstream of *snai2*, **canonical Wnt/β-catenin–LEF/TCF** is implicated in activation of Xenopus slug transcription: promoter analyses identified a required LEF-responsive element. BMP and FGF signals contribute to the broader neural-plate-border network. These are signals regulating expression of Snai2, **not evidence that Snai2 itself is a Wnt ligand, receptor or kinase**. PRC2/EZH2 and Ajuba-family proteins are better-characterized molecular partners of the Snai2 protein. (heeg‐truesdell2004asluga pages 7-8, tien2015snail2slugcooperateswith pages 54-58)

## Recent research and applications

A [2024 comparative transcriptomic study](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41559-024-02476-8) involving *X. laevis* followed neural-crest and blastula regulatory programs, including *snai2* expression. Its reported cross-species expression correlations are **global comparisons**, not Snai2-specific effect sizes; the study did not directly perturb Snai2 or revise the demonstrated E-cadherin/PRC2 mechanism. A separate [April 2024 single-cell neural-crest roadmap](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2311685121) places *snail2* among early neural-crest program genes, but its analyzed single-cell embryo series is ***X. tropicalis***; it must not be cited as a Q91924-specific functional experiment. A [2019 snai2:eGFP reporter](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-019-47665-9) likewise uses ***X. tropicalis*** and illustrates an experimental neural-crest reporter application, not a validated *X. laevis* Q91924 reporter line. (york2024sharedfeaturesof pages 4-6, kotov2024atimeresolvedsinglecell pages 1-2, li2019anewtransgenic pages 1-2)

More recent, but **post-2024 and preprint-level**, [October 2025 HCR-FISH work in *X. laevis*](https://doi.org/10.1101/2025.10.03.680132) reports broad, partly overlapping onset of *snai2*, *sox8* and *foxd3* expression at late gastrulation, followed by more coordinated yet spatially non-uniform neural-crest expression. It refines **where and when *snai2* RNA is detected**, not Snai2 protein localization or its direct target repertoire. In practice, *snai2* is used as a developmental neural-crest expression marker and as a perturbation target in *Xenopus* embryo experiments; neither use alone establishes that every *snai2*-positive cell has completed EMT or that Snai2 expression is sufficient for neural-crest specification. (montequin2025dynamicandnonuniform pages 1-5, aybar2003snailprecedesslug pages 1-2, zhang2006annfκband pages 5-7)

**Assessment.** The highest-confidence Q91924 annotation is a **nuclear, C2H2 zinc-finger transcriptional repressor that cooperates with corepressors, especially EZH2/PRC2, to suppress E-cadherin expression during *X. laevis* neural-crest EMT**, while contributing to earlier crest specification, migration and survival. Directly mapped frog targets beyond E-cadherin, precise endogenous-protein subcellular imaging, and the extent of Snai2-specific versus Snai1/other-factor redundancy remain less well resolved than the core developmental phenotype and promoter-chromatin mechanism. (zhang2006annfκband pages 1-2, aybar2003snailprecedesslug pages 7-8, langer2008ajubalimproteins pages 6-9, tien2015snail2slugcooperateswith pages 58-62)
