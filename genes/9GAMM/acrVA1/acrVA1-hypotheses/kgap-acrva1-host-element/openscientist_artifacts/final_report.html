Summary

Verdict: partially supported. The seed asks two questions and they resolve differently. The mobile-element question is answered with direct sequence evidence. The native-pairing question remains inferred, though the inference is considerably tighter than the seed assumed.

acrVA1 is locus tag AAX09_07405, protein AKG19227.1 (170 aa), at CP011374.1 (= NZ_CP011374.1): 1,623,942-1,624,454, plus strand, 513 bp, in Moraxella bovoculi strain 58069, assembly GCA_001017275.1 / GCF_000988605.1 (complete chromosome, 2,193,308 bp, 45.52 % GC). The protein matches UniProt A0A5H1ZR47 (ORF name AAX09_07405, PDB 6NMD chain B, InterPro IPR064663). It sits inside a 55.8 kb prophage spanning roughly 1,616,725-1,672,475. The call rests on what is actually there: a tyrosine-type DNA recombinase 453 bp upstream of acrVA1, a second integrase at the element's far edge 147 bp from tRNA-Val, a siphovirus structural module (tail proteins, phage tail tube, tail terminator, minor tail protein L, a 1,364 aa tape measure protein, terminase), 55 of 60 CDS annotated as hypothetical protein, GC of 43.26 % against a 45.52 % genomic mean (Mann-Whitney U p = 1.3e-16 over 2 kb windows), and codon usage further from the genome mean inside the element than outside (p = 1.9e-4), with acrVA1 itself at the 94.8th percentile of codon deviation. No transposase lies within 92 kb. No relaxase, mob or type IV secretion gene occurs anywhere in the region, which rules out an ICE. The element is chromosomal, which rules out a plasmid. The one missing piece is the attachment site: the longest exact direct repeat between the left-boundary and tRNA-Val windows is 14 bp (AATATTCAACAAAA), within chance expectation, so attL/attR were not recovered and the boundaries are inferred from gene content rather than delimited by sequence.

The same chromosome carries a type V-A CRISPR-Cas locus at 45.8-53.7 kb, about 1.57 Mb from acrVA1. Its Cas12a, AAX09_00210 / AKG18099.1, is 1,251 aa and was deposited in 2015 as a hypothetical protein, which is why the strain looks CRISPR-free on a keyword search. Downstream lie a Cas4-size endonuclease, a 1,039 bp unannotated gap where cas1 is expected, cas2, and a 2,062 bp array with 32 copies of a 36 nt repeat carrying the type V-A AATTTCTACT…AGAT handle and 31 spacers. Scanning all 31 spacers against the whole chromosome found exactly four extra-array hits, and all four fall inside the acrVA1 prophage. Spacer 16 matches perfectly at 1,629,057 on the plus strand behind a canonical 5'-TTTA PAM, inside prophage CDS AAX09_07440, 5.1 kb from acrVA1. That is a self-targeting, PAM-licensed protospacer in the very element that encodes the anti-CRISPR.

The number the seed asked for is this: the Cas12a in the anti-CRISPR's own cell is 95.4 % identical to MbCas12a (PDB 6IV6 chain A) and only 40.0 % and 32.2 % identical to the LbCas12a and AsCas12a editing reagents. The seed's framing is therefore accurate for the detailed biochemistry and the structures, which used Lb and As enzymes, but incomplete for the discovery paper: PMID: 30190308 reports AcrVA1 inhibition of MbCas12a and Mb3Cas12a, Moraxella bovoculi orthologues, alongside the reagents. The candidate natural target is nearly the same protein as one already shown to be inhibited. The pairing is not demonstrated, because AcrVA1 has not been assayed against AKG18099.1 itself, and per the seed's own instruction no GO annotation is proposed or changed on the strength of genomic context.

Element type call: prophage, that is, a genomic island of phage type. Not a plasmid (the sequence is the chromosome). Not an ICE (no conjugation machinery of any kind). Not undetermined (the structural module and the tRNA-adjacent integrase are diagnostic). The honest qualifier is that no att site was recovered, so the left boundary near 1,616,700-1,618,200 is set by where housekeeping annotation stops (lpxD, AAX09_07350, ends at 1,616,724) rather than by a repeat, and no portal, capsid, holin or endolysin was identified, so the element may be a decaying prophage rather than an inducible phage.

A correction worth recording, because it affects any repeat of this analysis. Iteration 1 stated that AAX09_07730 was the chromosome's only integrase, placing the nearest recombinase 48 kb from acrVA1. That was an artefact of searching product names in the 2015 GenBank annotation, where AAX09_07400 is labelled hypothetical protein. Transferring annotation from the modern assembly of strain SL1 (GCF_056856035.1) by direct protein alignment identified AAX09_07400 as a tyrosine-type DNA recombinase 453 bp from acrVA1, along with twelve further informative phage assignments. The prophage call is strengthened by the correction: acrVA1 sits at the integration module, which is where anti-CRISPR cassettes in temperate phages normally sit.

F5. The AcrVA1 family is narrow, and a text-search survey over-counted it

Across all 27 RefSeq-annotated M. bovoculi assemblies, cas12a is present in 19 (70.4 %). An NCBI Datasets annotation search for "AcrVA1" returned 7 assemblies, but retrieving the translated CDS at the reported coordinates showed four were false positives: strains 28389 (AAX07_RS12520), 22581 (AAX06_RS12160) and 33362 (AAX11_RS11890) each return a 54 aa hypothetical protein at 5.6 % identity to AcrVA1, and each also returns a 319 aa AcrVA2 family anti-CRISPR protein at 5.7 % identity, which is what the free-text query actually matched on substring. Sequence-verified AcrVA1 is present in 3 of 27 assemblies.

Two of those three also carry Cas12a; the third is a draft assembly where absence is uninformative. A Fisher exact test of AcrVA1 against Cas12a presence gives OR = 3.23, p = 0.633. With n = 3 carriers this has no power, so the non-significant result is uninformative rather than evidence against co-occurrence. The family is equally narrow outside this survey: InterPro IPR064663 has 3 UniProt members, and only 3 RefSeq proteins carry the name AcrVA1, all from M. bovoculi.

Mechanistic model and interpretation

The findings assemble into one account with a single untested link.

Solid arrows are established by data in this run or by published assay. Dashed arrows are the inferred step: that AcrVA1 inhibits the particular Cas12a encoded in strain 58069, and that this is why a self-targeted prophage survives. Nothing here tests that step.

The identity numbers are what make the inference reasonable rather than speculative. If the only inhibition data were LbCas12a and AsCas12a at 40.0 % and 32.2 % identity to the native enzyme, the published biochemistry would speak to breadth of the inhibitor and say little about the native partner. PMID: 30190308 changes that: MbCas12a and Mb3Cas12a, M. bovoculi orthologues, were among the enzymes inhibited, and MbCas12a is 95.4 % identical to AKG18099.1. Roughly 58 substitutions separate them across 1,251 residues. Anti-CRISPR specificity can turn on a handful of residues, so this is a strong prior and not a proof.

There is a competing reading of the self-targeting that cannot be excluded from sequence. A perfect spacer against one's own chromosome behind a TTTV PAM should be lethal; the system being non-functional would explain survival just as well as the anti-CRISPR explaining it. The cas1 gene is unannotated in a 1,039 bp gap, and the entire locus was deposited as hypothetical protein, so the state of the system is not established. A third reading is that the array is transcribed but the crRNA is not processed. Distinguishing these needs expression or interference data, not more sequence.

Mechanistic scope

Direct gene-product activity: AcrVA1 binds a Cas12a-crRNA complex, occupies the PAM-recognition pocket, and promotes Cas12a-dependent cleavage within the guide spacer, releasing the 3' portion while the 5' handle stays with Cas12a. One molecule disables several guide-loaded complexes, so substoichiometric amounts suffice. These points were supplied as already recorded and are not re-derived here; the structural and biochemical citations above are given for the curator's convenience.

Downstream of that activity: loss of interference against whatever DNA the crRNA targets. In strain 58069 the array's spacers 16 and 19 point into the acrVA1-bearing prophage, so the downstream consequence of AcrVA1 in that genome would be survival of that prophage. That is a consequence of the activity, inferred from sequence rather than observed.

Not supported at any level: any claim about subcellular localization, about a host process other than CRISPR interference, or about activity against type I, II, III or VI systems.

Conflicts and alternatives

The seed's premise is partly contradicted by the primary literature. The statement that inhibition was shown only with orthologues chosen for divergence, from Lachnospiraceae and Acidaminococcus, holds for the kinetics (30936531) and the structures (31155345) but not for the discovery paper, which reports MbCas12a and Mb3Cas12a inhibition. A curator should not carry the stronger version of that claim forward.

Perfect self-targeting admits more than one reading, as set out in the mechanistic model: the resident anti-CRISPRs neutralize the system, the cas locus is non-functional, or the array is transcribed but unprocessed. Sequence alone cannot separate these.

Database carry-over produced two errors in this run, both caught and corrected, and both relevant to anyone repeating the analysis. A product-name search of the stale 2015 annotation made it appear that the nearest integrase lay 48 kb from acrVA1; annotation transfer from the SL1 assembly placed a tyrosine recombinase 453 bp away. And the NCBI Datasets free-text search for "AcrVA1" matched "AcrVA2 family anti-CRISPR protein" records, inflating the homologue count from 3 to 7; direct CDS retrieval showed the extra hits are a 54 aa hypothetical protein (5.6 % identity) and a 319 aa AcrVA2 (5.7 %).

A competing account of the gene's origin is possible but unsupported: the cassette could have been acquired by the phage elsewhere and imported, rather than having evolved against the resident system. Nothing in the data distinguishes acquisition history from local coevolution.

Limitations and knowledge gaps

No attachment site. The longest exact repeat flanking the element is 14 bp, within chance for a 55.8 kb window, so prophage boundaries come from gene content and GC skew rather than from sequence delimitation. This matters because the extent of the element determines whether acrVA1 and the self-targeted protospacer are certainly on the same mobile unit. Read-level evidence of a circular excision intermediate, or comparison with a prophage-free genome at the same locus, would resolve it.

Is the strain-58069 type V-A system active? cas12a, a Cas4-like gene, cas2 and a 31-spacer array were found; cas1 was not annotated and falls in a 1,039 bp gap. Activity matters because an inactive system removes the selective pressure the whole natural-pairing argument assumes.

Does AcrVA1 inhibit AKG18099.1? Identity to MbCas12a is 95.4 %, which makes inhibition likely, but about 58 substitutions separate the two proteins and anti-CRISPR specificity can turn on few residues. Only a direct assay resolves this.

The family is too small to generalize. Three sequence-verified AcrVA1 proteins, all M. bovoculi, give Fisher OR = 3.23, p = 0.633 against Cas12a presence, which is uninformative rather than negative. Profile searches (HMM, or structure-based with Foldseek against the 6NMD chain B fold) across Moraxellaceae and their phages would say whether the family is genuinely narrow or merely under-named.

Expression is unknown. Whether acrVA1 is transcribed in the lysogen, and whether it belongs to the early or late phage program, was not checked and bears on whether the protein acts at injection or during lysogeny.

Tooling constraints, stated plainly. CRISPRCasFinder, PHASTER and IslandViewer were not reachable programmatically from this environment, so the prophage and CRISPR calls were made from gene content, composition and manual array parsing rather than from those tools, and should be confirmed against them. The EBI EMBOSS Needle REST service timed out and Biopython was unavailable in the executor, so alignments used a locally implemented global Needleman-Wunsch with an inline BLOSUM62 matrix, validated by a self-alignment returning exactly 100.0 %. No other resource failure occurred; no result here is extrapolated from a tool that did not run.

Proposed follow-up experiments and actions

Assay AcrVA1 against the strain-58069 Cas12a (AKG18099.1) directly, in vitro and by plasmid interference, using the array's own spacer 16 as the guide and the matching TTTA protospacer at 1,629,057 as the target. This single experiment converts the inferred pairing into a demonstrated one, and the reagents are already defined by this analysis.

Test whether the resident system is functional. Express the native cas12a locus with spacer 16 in a background lacking the prophage and score survival, with and without acrVA1 supplied in trans. Survival only in the presence of acrVA1 would tie the anti-CRISPR to prophage maintenance.

Induce the prophage with mitomycin C and test for excision by PCR across the predicted junction. A product would confirm mobility and define the att site that sequence analysis failed to recover.

Run a profile or structure-based homology search for AcrVA1 beyond named RefSeq records, then check whether each hit sits in a phage context. Association across more than three genomes would move the mobile-element claim from replicated to general.

Sequence crRNA from a lysogen to confirm array processing and measure spacer-16 abundance.
