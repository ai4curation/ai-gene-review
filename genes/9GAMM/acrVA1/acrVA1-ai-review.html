Gene Description

AcrVA1 is a 174-residue protein of Moraxella bovoculi that inactivates type V-A CRISPR-Cas12a systems, and it is the one characterised anti-CRISPR mechanism that destroys a CRISPR component rather than occupying one. It binds the assembled Cas12a-crRNA ribonucleoprotein, wedging between the recognition and nuclease lobes of the enzyme and inserting into the pocket that reads the protospacer-adjacent motif, and this positioning is followed by endonucleolytic cleavage of the bound guide RNA within its spacer, between the fifth and eighth nucleotides. The truncated guide dissociates and leaves behind an effector that can no longer be programmed to any target, so inhibition is irreversible rather than reversible. Because the reaction turns over, a single inhibitor molecule disarms many effectors and inhibition is achieved at sub-stoichiometric concentrations, which distinguishes it sharply from the stoichiometric blockers that must be present in excess of their target. Inhibition extends across phylogenetically divergent Cas12a orthologues. The guide-cleaving activity appears only when inhibitor and effector complex are both present, and the protein shows no ribonuclease activity on its own.

Suggested Questions for Experts

Q: Is AcrVA1 itself the nuclease, or does it license a cryptic ribonuclease activity of Cas12a? Every published experiment is consistent with both readings, and the two put the molecular function on different gene products.

Suggested experts: Gavin J. Knott, Jennifer A. Doudna, Leifu Chang

Q: AcrVA1 inserts into the PAM-binding pocket, which is where the DNA mimics AcrIIA4 and AcrIIA2 also act. Is the PAM pocket simply the most accessible route to the guide, or is there an element of nucleic acid mimicry in AcrVA1 that has not been looked for?

Suggested experts: Leifu Chang

Q: Guide destruction makes inhibition irreversible and catalytic. Does that predict that AcrVA1 is expressed at much lower levels than stoichiometric anti-CRISPRs in its native element, and is there any evidence of that?

Suggested experts: Joseph Bondy-Denomy

Suggested Experiments

Experiment: Mutate every conserved surface residue of AcrVA1 individually and in small combinations, screen each variant for Cas12a-crRNA binding by size-exclusion chromatography or mass photometry and for crRNA truncation by denaturing PAGE, and look for the separation-of-function class. Recovering a bind-but-not-cleave variant would establish AcrVA1 as the nuclease and justify GO:0004521 on this gene product without caveat; failing to recover one after saturating the surface, while recovering such a variant from a Cas12a mutagenesis done in parallel, would move the term to Cas12a and reclassify AcrVA1 as an activator.

Hypothesis: AcrVA1 bears the catalytic centre, so a variant exists that binds Cas12a-crRNA normally but cannot cleave the guide.

Type: separation-of-function mutagenesis

Experiment: Determine the end chemistry of both cleavage products precisely by mass spectrometry, test for the 2-prime-3-prime cyclic phosphate signature of general acid-base RNases, and attempt chemical rescue of candidate active-site mutants with imidazole, the approach that confirmed the catalytic histidine of the AcrIII-1 ring nuclease. Rescue by a small-molecule surrogate of a specific side chain would identify that side chain's owner.

Hypothesis: The cleavage chemistry is that of a protein ribonuclease active site rather than a metal-independent in-line attack templated by the complex.

Type: product end-chemistry and chemical rescue

Experiment: Locate acrVA1 in its genomic context in Moraxella bovoculi, construct an element lacking it, and compare retention and transfer frequency between hosts with an intact type V-A system and an isogenic cas12a deletion, with complementation in trans. This is the only experiment here that tests the biological role rather than the mechanism, and it is what would ground the symbiont-mediated process term in the organism's own ecology.

Hypothesis: acrVA1 is required for its native mobile element to persist in a Cas12a-competent host.

Type: element deletion and host-range retention assay

Knowledge Gaps

What is not known — curated, literature-grounded statements of the open unknowns (the inverse of core functions).

Gap: Which entity bears the catalytic centre for the crRNA cleavage that AcrVA1 causes is unestablished. AcrVA1 alone has no detectable ribonuclease activity, the primary work states that its data do not identify the component carrying the catalytic centre, and no catalytic residues have been reported in any study.

OPEN BIOLOGY MF_DARK

What is known: That the crRNA is cleaved, with multiple turnover and sub-stoichiometric inhibitor, is established and is why GO:0004521 is asserted at all; both known Cas12a nuclease centres were excluded by mutagenesis, so the obvious alternatives are gone without the AcrVA1 attribution being confirmed. The unexcluded alternative is that AcrVA1 allosterically licenses a latent Cas12a ribonuclease, in which case GO:0004521 belongs on Cas12a and AcrVA1 is an enzyme activator instead.

Significance: The two readings put the same molecular function on different proteins and make AcrVA1 a different kind of anti-CRISPR, catalytic rather than activating. modules/anti_crispr_suppression.yaml carries no activator mechanism class, so the alternative reading would also add one.

What would resolve it: Identify catalytic residues in AcrVA1 by structure-guided mutagenesis and test whether a residue substitution abolishes cleavage without abolishing Cas12a binding; conversely test a catalytically dead Cas12a for loss of AcrVA1-dependent crRNA cleavage.

Gap: The protein is unreviewed in UniProt, named only "AcrVA1" from a PDB deposition, and carries no GO annotations at all, despite a published mechanism the discovering authors called a mode of inhibition not previously observed for any anti-CRISPR protein.

OPEN CURATION

What is known: The mechanism class and the structural basis of binding are established by two independent studies. What is missing is any curated record of them on the accession.

Significance: This is the same curation gap recorded in modules/anti_crispr_suppression.yaml for the AcrF3 and AcrIIC1 exemplars, and AcrVA1 is the worst instance of it in this round, since it is the only one of the three with zero GOA rows. The seeded review also inherited a wrong taxon identifier, NCBITaxon:9 for Buchnera aphidicola instead of NCBITaxon:386891 for Moraxella bovoculi, which has been corrected here.

What would resolve it: Nominate A0A5H1ZR47 for Swiss-Prot review with a FUNCTION line citing PMID:30936531 and PMID:31155345, and record the catalyst ambiguity in that line rather than asserting ribonuclease activity flatly.

Provenance (the field's own admissions):

  • "Thus, AcrVA1 activity is multiple-turnover where cleavage of a crRNA will permanently inactivate Cas12a-crRNA complexes through a mode of inhibition not previously observed for any anti-CRISPR protein." — PMID:30936531

Gap: The natural host-element pairing is unestablished. Which mobile element carries acrVA1, and which Cas12a system it evolved against, are not determined, so the biological setting in which this counter-defence operates is unknown.

OPEN BIOLOGY

What is known: Both the biochemistry and the structure were done with Cas12a orthologues chosen for their divergence and their use as genome-editing reagents, including enzymes from Lachnospiraceae and Acidaminococcus, not with a demonstrated natural host-element pair.

Significance: Broad-spectrum inhibition across engineered orthologues is a reagent property. Whether AcrVA1 confers a fitness advantage on a mobile element challenged by a Cas12a system in its own ecological setting is a separate question, and it is the question that justifies the symbiont-mediated process term biologically rather than only mechanistically.

What would resolve it: Identify the element context of acrVA1 in the Moraxella bovoculi genome, then test whether deleting it costs that element persistence in a Cas12a-competent host, which is the necessity test that grounded the original anti-CRISPR assignments.

Provenance (the field's own admissions):

  • "AcrVA1 blocked dsDNA cleavage by all three Cas12a orthologs" — PMID:30936531

Mechanism: the module's assignment is right about the outcome and over-asserts the catalyst

The module (annoton acrva1_crrna_nuclease) places AcrVA1 in the
crrna_cleavage_variant of the enzymatic-subversion part, and asserts
required_function and function = GO:0004521 RNA endonuclease activity, with substrate
"Cas12a-bound crRNA guide". The mechanism class is correct. The attribution of the
catalytic centre to AcrVA1 is not established, and the module asserts it without
qualification.

What is established:

  • AcrVA1 is a broad-spectrum inhibitor of Cas12a cis-cleavage
    PMID:30936531.
  • It blocks dsDNA binding
    PMID:30936531.
  • Its distinctive mechanism is guide destruction, not occupancy
    PMID:30936531,
    PMID:30936531.
  • The chemistry is endonucleolytic and the cut positions are mapped
    PMID:30936531,
    PMID:30936531.
  • Catalysis is sub-stoichiometric, which is what makes it catalytic rather than
    stoichiometric and is the basis of the module's part-3 boundary
    PMID:30936531.
  • Structurally, AcrVA1 occupies the PAM-binding pocket
    PMID:31155345.

What is not established — the catalyst. The activity requires both AcrVA1 and
Cas12a-crRNA together; AcrVA1 alone does nothing to RNA
PMID:30936531,
and Knott et al. say explicitly that they could not assign the catalytic centre
PMID:30936531.
They also report that mutating either known Cas12a nuclease centre (RuvC, and the
pre-crRNA processing nuclease) does not abolish the truncation, so the obvious alternative
candidates are excluded without the AcrVA1 option being confirmed. The independent
structural paper states the active reading without qualification
PMID:31155345
but does not identify catalytic residues either.

Curation decision. GO:0004521 RNA endonuclease activity is asserted, because two
independent groups report Cas12a-dependent endoribonucleolytic cleavage with multiple
turnover and the balance of the published interpretation is that AcrVA1 is the nuclease.
But it is asserted with the caveat in review.reason and with an explicit BIOLOGY
knowledge gap, because the alternative — that AcrVA1 allosterically licenses a latent
Cas12a ribonuclease activity — has not been excluded, and if it were true the term would
belong on Cas12a and AcrVA1 would instead be an enzyme activator. This is the point at
which the module should be softened: required_function: GO:0004521 makes the
unestablished attribution a membership criterion for the family, which is stronger than
the evidence.

GO:0043021 ribonucleoprotein complex binding is also asserted, and is on firmer ground
than the catalytic term: the cryo-EM structure is of AcrVA1 bound to the Cas12a-crRNA
ribonucleoprotein, and every activity AcrVA1 has is conditional on that complex existing.

Note on "DNA mimicry"

AcrVA1 inserts into the PAM-binding pocket, which is superficially the AcrIIA4/AcrIIA2
mechanism. It is not annotated as a nucleic acid mimic here, because neither paper
describes an acidic pseudo-DNA surface or competition with the duplex; the PAM-pocket
insertion is reported as the positioning that puts AcrVA1 next to the crRNA spacer it then
cleaves. Assigning mimicry would be reading the AcrIIA4 story onto a different protein.
The module's placement of AcrVA1 in part 3 (enzymatic) rather than part 1 (recognition
blockade) is the right call, even though AcrVA1 does also block dsDNA binding.

Curation gap

Unreviewed entry, PDB-derived name, zero GOA annotations, for a protein whose mechanism
was called "a mode of inhibition not previously observed for any anti-CRISPR protein".
Same curation gap class as L7P7R7 and A0A2D0TCG3.
