PMID:30936526 is the direct structural/biochemical primary paper attached to PDB 6IUF. The accessible abstract states that AcrVA5 modifies MbCas12a Lys635 and that Lys635Arg is resistant. Thus the active entity performs covalent protein-lysine acetylation; generic nuclease binding would obscure the mechanism. Live QuickGO verified GO:0061733 "protein-lysine-acetyltransferase activity", defined by L-lysyl-protein plus acetyl-CoA conversion to N6-acetyl-L-lysyl-protein plus CoA. The publication cache is abstract-only; no full-text assay detail is invented. The archived record's structure-derived acetyl-CoA binding sites independently support cofactor association, but the experimental publication supports the chemistry.

PMID:35581175 is a research letter with original data, despite machine publication-type metadata `COMMENT_EDITORIAL`. Full text describes LbCas12a acetylation, CobB-dependent reactivation, and engineered plasmid-transformation tests in E. coli. It demonstrates reversibility and expression-context dependence, not a native Moraxella infection outcome. The reported 1,101 potential acetylation targets in heterologous E. coli are not automatically physiological substrates or core processes.

PMID:38627399 full text shows inhibition of protospacer integration, Cas2 Lys55 acetylation and an AcrVA5 N-terminal peptide–Cas2 complex. The N-terminal peptide sequence matches the archived protein. The study explicitly notes Cas1 is catalytic for integration while Cas2 provides a structural platform; Cas2 nuclease inhibition must not be substituted for the integration mechanism. No universal native adaptation phenotype is inferred. Its biochemical extension is included in the standalone description and the open biological question; it does not create a redundant second acetyltransferase core.

The description calls the protein mobile-element-associated and does not infer that all bacterial genomic Acrs are viral. The module adds covalent modification as an alternative route, not a compulsory step after recognition blockade.
