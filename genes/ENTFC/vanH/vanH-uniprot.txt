CC   -!- FUNCTION: Required for high-level resistance to glycopeptide
CC       antibiotics (PubMed:1503450, PubMed:1522072). Catalyzes the reduction
CC       of 2-keto acids to 2-D-hydroxy acids, exhibiting highest catalytic
CC       efficiency with pyruvate and 2-oxobutanoate/alpha-ketobutyrate as
CC       substrates, producing D-lactate and (2R)-hydroxybutanoate, respectively
CC       (PubMed:1503450, PubMed:1931965, PubMed:9605319). Together with D-
CC       alanine--D-lactate ligase VanA, gives rise to peptidoglycan precursors
CC       that terminate in the depsipeptide D-alanine-D-lactate rather than the
CC       dipeptide D-alanine-D-alanine thus preventing vancomycin binding
CC       (PubMed:1522072, PubMed:1931965, PubMed:9605319). Shows a slight
CC       preference for NADPH over NADH as the electron donor (PubMed:1931965,
CC       PubMed:9605319). {ECO:0000269|PubMed:1503450,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:1522072, ECO:0000269|PubMed:1931965,
