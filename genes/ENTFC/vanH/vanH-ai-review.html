Gene Description

VanH is the NAD(P)H-dependent dehydrogenase that supplies the D-hydroxy acid used to build glycopeptide-resistant peptidoglycan precursors in enterococci. It is encoded in the Tn1546-type van cluster and cotranscribed with vanA and vanX from a promoter switched on by the VanS-VanR two-component system when the antibiotic is present. The protein is a member of the D-isomer-specific 2-hydroxyacid dehydrogenase family, with a Rossmann dinucleotide-binding fold and a catalytic His-Arg-Glu triad, and it is a D-specific 2-keto acid reductase: it reduces 2-oxocarboxylates to the corresponding (2R)-2-hydroxy acids, with the highest catalytic efficiency on pyruvate and on 2-oxobutanoate, giving D-lactate and (2R)-hydroxybutanoate. Either dinucleotide serves as electron donor, with a slight preference for NADPH over NADH, and full activity requires a reducing environment and an activating anion such as phosphate or acetate. The physiological product is D-lactate, which the normal cytoplasm does not supply at useful concentration: it is the acyl acceptor that the partner ligase VanA esterifies to D-alanine, giving the depsipeptide D-Ala-D-Lac that replaces D-Ala-D-Ala at the end of the pentapeptide side chain. Because glycopeptides bind the depsipeptide terminus far more weakly, VanH is one of the three enzymes required for high-level, inducible vancomycin and teicoplanin resistance, and feeding D-lactate to a vanH-deficient strain restores that resistance.

Core Functions

D-specific 2-keto acid reductase. Using NADPH or NADH, VanH reduces a 2-oxocarboxylate to the corresponding (2R)-2-hydroxy acid, most efficiently pyruvate to D-lactate and 2-oxobutanoate to (2R)-hydroxybutanoate. Full activity needs a reducing environment and an activating anion. The physiological product is D-lactate, which the cell does not otherwise make in useful amounts, and which the partner ligase VanA esterifies to D-alanine to give the depsipeptide terminus that glycopeptide antibiotics bind poorly. Supplying D-lactate exogenously substitutes for the enzyme, which is what identifies this as its role in resistance.

Suggested Questions for Experts

Suggested Experiments

Experiment: Determine a structure of VanH with NAD(P)H and pyruvate bound, following up the tetragonal crystals that diffracted to 3 angstroms in PMID:9605319, and look for an anion site. Comparing it with a housekeeping D-lactate dehydrogenase would show whether the resistance enzyme has a distinguishable active site.

Experiment: Test in vivo which product matters. Replace vanH with variants shifted toward 2-oxobutanoate or toward pyruvate, measure the D-lactate and (2R)-hydroxybutanoate pools and the vancomycin and teicoplanin minimum inhibitory concentrations, and ask whether the depsipeptide made from the longer hydroxy acid supports resistance as well as D-Ala-D-Lac does.

Knowledge Gaps

What is not known — curated, literature-grounded statements of the open unknowns (the inverse of core functions).

Gap: Why VanH requires an activating anion and a strongly reducing environment for full activity is not understood, and neither is what sets its position on the NADPH/NADH boundary. Both papers report the anion effect, in the native enzyme and in the fusion, without identifying a site or a mechanism, and no structure of VanH has been reported despite crystals that diffracted to 3 angstroms in 1998. Whether the anion occupies the substrate carboxylate site, stabilises an oligomeric state, or acts on the catalytic triad is open.

BIOLOGY MF_DARK

What is known: Firmly established: the reactions and their three EC numbers, K_m values for pyruvate, NADH and NADPH, competitive inhibition by NAD+, ADP-ribose and oxamate, the requirement for anion and reducing conditions, and the position of the catalytic residues by homology to the D-isomer-specific 2-hydroxyacid dehydrogenase family.

Significance: The anion and redox dependence may be how the enzyme is tuned to run as a reductase in the cytoplasm rather than oxidising the D-lactate it has just made, and a structure would show whether the resistance enzyme differs from housekeeping D-lactate dehydrogenases in a way that could be targeted.

Why several molecular-function terms are all correct here

This is a genuinely broad-specificity enzyme, and the annotation set reflects
that rather than duplicating one claim. UniProt gives it three EC numbers, each
with experimental evidence from both papers, and five Rhea reactions:

  • GO:0008720 D-lactate dehydrogenase (NAD+) activity — the physiological
    reaction, RHEA:16369, EC 1.1.1.28.
  • GO:0140175 (2R)-2-hydroxyacid dehydrogenase (NAD+) activity — the generic
    NAD-dependent reaction, RHEA:35643, EC 1.1.1.345. This is an ancestor of the
    lactate-specific term, but it is not merely the uninformative parent: the
    enzyme really does act on 2-oxocarboxylates generally, with the highest
    catalytic efficiency on pyruvate and 2-oxobutanoate, and that broader
    claim is not entailed by the lactate term.
  • GO:0050578 (2R)-2-hydroxyacid dehydrogenase (NADP+) activity — the NADP
    counterpart, RHEA:35735, EC 1.1.1.272. The cofactor preference is slightly
    toward NADPH (K_m 3.5 µM versus 19 µM), so this is not a secondary activity.
  • GO:0047809 D-lactate dehydrogenase activity — the cofactor-agnostic parent.
    GOA derives it from RHEA:62968, the NADP+-dependent lactate reaction, for
    which GO has no cofactor-specific child. So although it is formally an
    ancestor of GO:0008720, the row is carrying the NADP-lactate activity and is
    accepted rather than marked too general. The missing child is raised as a
    question.
  • GO:0016616 is the EC-class-level parent of all of these and says nothing
    they do not, so that row is marked TOO_GENERAL.

Note the direction: every UniProt reaction is written in the oxidation
direction with PhysiologicalDirection=right-to-left, i.e. the enzyme runs as a
reductase in the cell, consuming NAD(P)H and pyruvate to make D-lactate. The GO
terms are direction-neutral, so this does not affect the actions, but it is why
the core function is described as a reduction.
