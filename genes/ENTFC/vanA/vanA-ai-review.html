Gene Description

VanA is the ATP-dependent ligase that gives enterococci high-level, inducible resistance to the glycopeptide antibiotics vancomycin and teicoplanin. It is encoded on the Tn1546-type van gene cluster carried by plasmids such as pIP816 and is transcribed, together with vanH and vanX, from a promoter switched on by the VanS-VanR two-component system in response to the drug. VanA belongs to the D-alanine--D-alanine ligase (Ddl) family and shares its ATP-grasp architecture and two divalent-metal site, but its substrate specificity has shifted: instead of joining two D-alanines through an amide bond, it condenses D-alanine with a D-2-hydroxy acid through an ester bond, giving the depsipeptide D-alanyl-(R)-lactate. D-lactate is supplied by the partner dehydrogenase VanH, and the depsipeptide is accepted by the normal D-Ala-D-Ala-adding enzyme, so the cell builds a UDP-MurNAc-pentadepsipeptide that ends in D-Ala-D-Lac rather than D-Ala-D-Ala. Glycopeptides bind this terminus with more than a thousand-fold lower affinity, because one of the five hydrogen bonds of the drug-target complex is lost, and the cell is resistant. The enzyme is a soluble precursor-biosynthetic protein that associates peripherally with the cytoplasmic face of the plasma membrane; it retains enough D-Ala-D-Ala ligase activity to complement a temperature-sensitive ddl mutant, but its evolved activity, and the one its kinetic constants describe, is the ester-forming reaction.

Core Functions

ATP-dependent D-alanine-(R)-lactate ligase. A Ddl-family ATP-grasp enzyme whose specificity has shifted from amide to ester bond formation: it condenses D-alanine with a D-2-hydroxy acid, physiologically the D-lactate made by VanH, to give the depsipeptide D-alanyl-(R)-lactate. The product is accepted by the normal D-Ala-D-Ala-adding enzyme, so the cell assembles a UDP-MurNAc-pentadepsipeptide ending in D-Ala-D-Lac. Glycopeptides bind that terminus far more weakly than D-Ala-D-Ala, which is the molecular basis of high-level, inducible vancomycin and teicoplanin resistance.

Suggested Questions for Experts

Q: GOA has no antibiotic-response annotation for any gene of the van cluster, although the operon is defined by inducible glycopeptide resistance and UniProt records the vancomycin induction on each entry. Should GO:0046677 response to antibiotic be annotated across vanR, vanS, vanH, vanA, vanX and vanY, and is a more specific child for glycopeptide resistance worth creating?

Q: VanA carries both GO:0160220 D-alanine-(R)-lactate ligase activity and GO:0008716 D-alanine-D-alanine ligase activity. The second is real, since vanA complements a temperature-sensitive ddl mutant, but it is the ancestral activity rather than the resistance one. Is there a convention for marking which of two genuine activities of an enzyme of altered specificity is the physiological one?

Suggested Experiments

Experiment: Measure the acceptor specificity of VanA against Ddl side by side under one set of conditions, with D-alanine, D-lactate, D-2-hydroxybutyrate and D-serine as acceptors, and compare the catalytic efficiencies. This would quantify how far the specificity has shifted and whether the residual dipeptide-ligase activity is enough to matter in a cell that has lost ddl.

Experiment: Test whether VanA's membrane association is functional. Fractionate induced E. faecium under conditions that preserve peripheral interactions, ask whether VanA co-fractionates with the MurF or MraY steps, and compare a soluble VanA variant for its ability to confer resistance. This would settle whether the membrane association reported in PMID:2266943 reflects channelling to the precursor pathway or is an artefact of the fractionation.

Knowledge Gaps

What is not known — curated, literature-grounded statements of the open unknowns (the inverse of core functions).

Gap: What makes VanA prefer an ester to an amide acceptor is not established at the level of the enzyme. VanA is a Ddl homolog that still complements a ddl mutant, yet its measured specificity is for D-2-hydroxy acids, with D-2-hydroxybutyrate the best substrate in vitro and D-lactate the one used in vivo. Which residues reposition the acceptor site, and why the best in vitro substrate is not the physiological one, are open; so is how the enzyme avoids being outcompeted by the large intracellular D-alanine pool.

BIOLOGY MF_DARK

What is known: Firmly established: the reaction and its EC number, K_m values for D-lactate, D-alanine and three longer D-2-hydroxy acids, the ATP-grasp domain and its ATP and divalent-metal sites, incorporation of the product into UDP-MurNAc-pentapeptide, and in vivo use of D-lactate.

Significance: The acceptor-site determinants are what distinguish a resistance ligase from a housekeeping one, so they decide whether a ligase found in a new genome confers resistance, and they are the target surface for an inhibitor that would spare Ddl.

What VanA does

VanA is the ligase that re-terminates the peptidoglycan precursor. It is a
D-Ala-D-Ala ligase homolog (36% identity to the Salmonella Ddl, 28% to the E.
coli enzyme) whose specificity has shifted from amide to ester bond formation:

PMID:1931965
PMID:1931965

The product is incorporated into the normal precursor pathway:
PMID:1931965

In vivo the physiological acyl acceptor is D-lactate, supplied by VanH:
PMID:1503450
PMID:8852545

and the point of the substitution is the loss of a hydrogen bond to the drug:
PMID:8852545

The residual D-Ala-D-Ala ligase activity is real, but secondary

VanA is annotated both to GO:0160220 (the depsipeptide ligase reaction) and to
GO:0008716 (the dipeptide ligase reaction). The latter is not an artefact:
PMID:2266943
So VanA can make D-Ala-D-Ala well enough to substitute for Ddl in vivo. But it is
the ester-forming activity that the enzyme exists for and that UniProt's
RecName, EC number and reaction statement record, and the measured K_m values
are for D-hydroxy acids. Following the enzyme-specificity tiers, the dipeptide
ligase activity is treated as a genuine secondary activity
(KEEP_AS_NON_CORE), not as an over-annotation and not as the core function.

Location: peripheral, on the cytoplasmic face

The paper that first identified VanA found it in membrane fractions
PMID:2266943
but concluded that the protein belongs with the soluble precursor-synthesising
enzymes
PMID:2266943.
UniProt reconciles the two as a peripheral membrane protein on the cytoplasmic
side, and the substrates (free D-alanine, D-lactate, ATP) and the downstream
MurF step are cytoplasmic. The review therefore accepts the cytoplasm/cytosol
rows as the site of catalysis and keeps the plasma-membrane rows as a real but
non-core observation; neither is removed.

Process annotations

GO:0009252 peptidoglycan biosynthetic process is right and VanA does the work
of a step in it: it supplies the side chain terminus that MurF adds to the
precursor. What is missing from GOA is any antibiotic-response term, even though
the whole point of the gene is inducible glycopeptide resistance and UniProt
records INDUCTION: By vancomycin, mediated by VanS/VanR. GO:0046677 response to antibiotic is added as a NEW row: it is the most specific available term,
since GO has no glycopeptide-resistance-specific child, and functionally
equivalent resistance enzymes already carry it in this repository.
