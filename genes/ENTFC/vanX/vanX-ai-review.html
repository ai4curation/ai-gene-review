Gene Description

VanX is the zinc dipeptidase that clears D-alanyl-D-alanine from the cytoplasm of glycopeptide-resistant enterococci. It is encoded in the Tn1546-type van cluster and cotranscribed with vanH and vanA from a promoter switched on by the VanS-VanR two-component system when the antibiotic is present. A homodimer of the peptidase M15D family, each subunit binds one zinc ion through two histidines and an aspartate and uses an aspartate as the catalytic proton donor; divalent cations raise the turnover number more than a hundredfold, to as much as 788 per second. Its reaction is the hydrolysis of the free dipeptide D-Ala-D-Ala to two D-alanines, and its defining property is what it does not do: it is more than a hundred thousand times less efficient on D-alanyl-D-lactate, the depsipeptide that the partner ligase VanA makes. That asymmetry is the reason the gene is in the cluster. By destroying the normal dipeptide while sparing the depsipeptide, VanX ensures that the D-Ala-D-Ala-adding enzyme is offered the resistant terminus rather than the drug-binding one, so the cell wall is built almost entirely from precursors that glycopeptides cannot grip. VanX is indispensable for resistance, unlike the downstream carboxypeptidase VanY, which only raises the level a little; it acts on the free dipeptide pool, whereas VanY trims precursors that were already assembled with D-Ala-D-Ala.

Core Functions

Zinc-dependent D-Ala-D-Ala dipeptidase. VanX hydrolyses the free dipeptide D-alanyl-D-alanine to two D-alanines, with a turnover that rises more than a hundredfold in the presence of divalent cations, and is more than a hundred thousand times less efficient on the depsipeptide D-alanyl-D-lactate. That discrimination is the function: by emptying the normal dipeptide pool while leaving VanA's product untouched, it forces the D-Ala-D-Ala-adding enzyme to use the resistant terminus, so the wall is built from precursors that glycopeptides bind weakly. It acts on the free dipeptide, upstream of the carboxypeptidase VanY, which trims precursors already carrying D-Ala-D-Ala.

Suggested Questions for Experts

Q: All five GOA rows for vanX are electronic and none cites a publication, even though the enzyme was purified and kinetically characterised in 1995 and UniProt cites that work with experimental evidence for the reaction, the cofactor, the kinetics and the subunit structure. Could experimental annotations be made from PMID:7873524 and PMID:7854121, in particular for GO:0160237?

Q: The InterPro peptidase-domain signature produces GO:0006508 proteolysis for both vanX and vanY, but neither enzyme hydrolyses a protein: VanX cleaves a free dipeptide and VanY trims a UDP-MurNAc-peptide precursor. Should the InterPro2GO mapping for the M15 families point somewhere other than proteolysis?

Suggested Experiments

Experiment: Map the amide-versus-ester discrimination structurally. Solve VanX with a non-hydrolysable D-Ala-D-Ala analogue and with D-Ala-D-lactate bound, and test the predicted contacts by mutagenesis, measuring the catalytic efficiency on both substrates for each variant. A variant that lost the discrimination would be expected to abolish resistance in vivo, which is a direct test of the model for why the gene is in the cluster.

Experiment: Determine which metal VanX uses in the cell. Measure the metal content of VanX purified from induced E. faecium rather than from an E. coli overproducer, and compare activity across zinc, iron, cobalt and nickel at intracellular concentrations, to find out whether the broad metal activation seen in vitro reflects anything physiological.

Knowledge Gaps

What is not known — curated, literature-grounded statements of the open unknowns (the inverse of core functions).

Gap: How VanX achieves its hundred-thousand-fold discrimination between an amide and an ester substrate that differ by a single atom is not explained. The kinetics decompose the effect into both a binding and a turnover component, with a K_i for D-Ala-D-lactate estimated at 242 mM and a k_cat below 0.005 per second, but which active-site contacts read the difference, and whether the enzyme could be engineered or evolved to lose the discrimination, is unknown. The related question of why a zinc enzyme is also activated by iron, cobalt and nickel, and which metal it uses in the cell, is likewise open.

BIOLOGY MF_DARK

What is known: Firmly established: the reaction and its EC number, kinetic constants for D-Ala-D-Ala, D-Ala-D-Ser, D-Ser-D-Ala and D-Ala-D-lactate, the pH optimum, the homodimer, one zinc per subunit with three identified ligands, the catalytic proton donor, inhibition by aminoalkyl phosphinates, and the requirement for the gene in resistance.

Significance: An inhibitor that blocked VanX would restore the D-Ala-D-Ala pool and so restore glycopeptide susceptibility without needing to inhibit VanA or VanH, which makes the determinants of its substrate discrimination directly relevant to resensitising resistant enterococci.

What VanX does

VanX is the enzyme that makes the resistance switch clean. VanA's depsipeptide
has to compete with the cell's own D-Ala-D-Ala for the D-Ala-D-Ala-adding
enzyme, and VanX removes the competitor:

Annotation issues

All five GOA rows are electronic, with no publication cited, so each was judged
against the primary literature and the curated record rather than against the
row's own evidence.

  • GO:0160237 D-Ala-D-Ala dipeptidase activity is the core function and matches
    the curated reaction and EC number exactly: accepted.
  • GO:0016805 dipeptidase activity is its is_a parent, so the row is modified
    to the specific child the gene already carries.
  • GO:0008237 metallopeptidase activity is not an ancestor of GO:0160237, so
    it is an independent and true claim: VanX is a zinc enzyme of the peptidase
    M15D family whose k_cat rises more than a hundredfold in the presence of
    divalent cations. Accepted.
  • GO:0008270 zinc ion binding is supported by three curated zinc-binding
    residues and by the cofactor statement, which carries experimental evidence.
    Accepted.
  • GO:0006508 proteolysis is the problem row. It comes from the peptidase-domain
    signature, but VanX hydrolyses a free dipeptide, not a protein, and the GO
    definition of proteolysis is restricted to the hydrolysis of proteins. The
    aspect and the region of the ontology are right and the essence of the claim is
    sound, so the row is modified to GO:0043171 peptide catabolic process rather
    than removed. The same issue arises on vanY, where the substrate is a
    UDP-MurNAc-peptide precursor and the replacement chosen there is a
    peptidoglycan term instead.

Nothing in GOA connects vanX to antibiotic resistance, and the record has no
cellular-component annotation at all. GO:0046677 response to antibiotic and
GO:0005829 cytosol are added as NEW rows; the cytosol inference rests on the
absence of any membrane-targeting feature together with the compartment of the
substrate pool, and GO:0046677 is the most specific resistance-response term GO
offers.

core_functions:
- description: >-
    Zinc-dependent D-Ala-D-Ala dipeptidase. VanX hydrolyses the free dipeptide
    D-alanyl-D-alanine to two D-alanines, with a turnover that rises more than a
    hundredfold in the presence of divalent cations, and is more than a hundred
    thousand times less efficient on the depsipeptide D-alanyl-D-lactate. That
    discrimination is the function: by emptying the normal dipeptide pool while
    leaving VanA's product untouched, it forces the D-Ala-D-Ala-adding enzyme to
    use the resistant terminus, so the wall is built from precursors that
    glycopeptides bind weakly. It acts on the free dipeptide, upstream of the
    carboxypeptidase VanY, which trims precursors already carrying D-Ala-D-Ala.

knowledge_gaps:
- gap_statement: >-
    How VanX achieves its hundred-thousand-fold discrimination between an amide
    and an ester substrate that differ by a single atom is not explained. The
    kinetics decompose the effect into both a binding and a turnover component,
    with a K_i for D-Ala-D-lactate estimated at 242 mM and a k_cat below 0.005 per
    second, but which active-site contacts read the difference, and whether the
    enzyme could be engineered or evolved to lose the discrimination, is unknown.
    The related question of why a zinc enzyme is also activated by iron, cobalt and
    nickel, and which metal it uses in the cell, is likewise open.
  boundary: >-
    Firmly established: the reaction and its EC number, kinetic constants for
    D-Ala-D-Ala, D-Ala-D-Ser, D-Ser-D-Ala and D-Ala-D-lactate, the pH optimum, the
    homodimer, one zinc per subunit with three identified ligands, the catalytic
    proton donor, inhibition by aminoalkyl phosphinates, and the requirement for
    the gene in resistance.

    An inhibitor that blocked VanX would restore the D-Ala-D-Ala pool and so

    proteolysis?
suggested_experiments:
- description: >-
    Map the amide-versus-ester discrimination structurally. Solve VanX with a
    non-hydrolysable D-Ala-D-Ala analogue and with D-Ala-D-lactate bound, and test
    the predicted contacts by mutagenesis, measuring the catalytic efficiency on
    both substrates for each variant. A variant that lost the discrimination would
    be expected to abolish resistance in vivo, which is a direct test of the model
    for why the gene is in the cluster.
- description: >-
    Determine which metal VanX uses in the cell. Measure the metal content of VanX
    purified from induced E. faecium rather than from an E. coli overproducer, and
    compare activity across zinc, iron, cobalt and nickel at intracellular
    concentrations, to find out whether the broad metal activation seen in vitro
    reflects anything physiological.
