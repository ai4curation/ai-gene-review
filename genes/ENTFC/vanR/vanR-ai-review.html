Gene Description

VanR is the response regulator that turns the glycopeptide-resistance operon on. It is a cytoplasmic, monomeric, two-domain protein of the OmpR/PhoB subclass: an N-terminal receiver domain that is phosphorylated on Asp53, and a C-terminal winged-helix DNA-binding domain. Its partner, the membrane sensor kinase VanS, controls that phosphorylation in both directions, transferring a phosphoryl group from its own phosphohistidine to Asp53 and also removing it again; the relay is highly specific, with a ten-thousand-fold kinetic preference over a heterologous response regulator. Phospho-VanR binds the promoter regions of vanR and of vanH with much higher affinity than the unphosphorylated protein, and from the vanH promoter the resistance genes vanH, vanA and vanX are cotranscribed, so the cell acquires the enzymes that build peptidoglycan precursors ending in D-Ala-D-Lac instead of D-Ala-D-Ala. The aspartyl phosphate is chemically stable, with a half-life of ten to twelve hours in vitro, which is why the regulator does not simply relax to the off state: the phosphatase arm of VanS has to remove the mark actively, and that is what keeps the operon silent in the absence of the inducing signal. VanR can also be phosphorylated non-enzymatically by acetyl phosphate, and its DNA binding has been reported to be inhibited by VanS itself.

Core Functions

Phosphorylation-controlled transcriptional activator of the van resistance operon. VanR's receiver domain accepts a phosphoryl group on Asp53 from the phosphohistidine of the sensor kinase VanS, with a K_M of 3 micromolar and a ten-thousand-fold kinetic preference over a heterologous regulator; the resulting phosphoaspartate is stable for ten to twelve hours in vitro. Phosphorylation greatly raises the affinity of its OmpR/PhoB-type winged-helix domain for the vanR and vanH promoters, and from the vanH promoter vanH, vanA and vanX are cotranscribed. Because the mark is chemically long-lived, the off state depends on VanS actively removing it rather than on spontaneous decay, which makes VanR a latch rather than a timer.

Receiver half of the VanS-VanR phosphorelay. VanR is the dedicated phosphoryl-group acceptor of VanS, and the specificity runs both ways: VanS transfers to VanR ten thousand times more efficiently than to the heterologous regulator PhoB and not at all to OmpR, ArcB or CreB. It is also the substrate of the VanS phosphatase arm, so its phosphorylation state, and hence the state of the operon, is set entirely by that one sensor. Acetyl phosphate can phosphorylate it non-enzymatically in vitro.

Suggested Questions for Experts

Q: All eight GOA rows for vanR are electronic and none cites a publication, although the protein was purified and characterised in 1993, its phosphotransfer kinetics measured in 1996, and its promoter binding and phosphorylation-dependent affinity reported in the work UniProt cites with experimental evidence. Could experimental annotations be made from PMID:8494882, PMID:8664263 and PMID:8161518?

Q: UniProt entry Q06239 carries both the Activator and the Repressor keywords, while its FUNCTION text describes only activation of vanH, vanA and vanX. Is there evidence for repression by VanR, for example autoregulation of the vanR promoter in the unphosphorylated state, or should the Repressor keyword be reviewed? The answer decides whether the direction-specific process term for this gene should be positive regulation alone.

Q: GO-CAM model gomodel:671ae02600002832 represents VanR twice with the same gene product, function and process, splitting the VanS kinase and phosphatase edges between the two copies so that the inhibitory branch points at a node with no outgoing edges, and it gives the VanR activity has_input UniProtKB:Q05709, the VanH protein, rather than the operon's genes as targets. Both points are recorded as source_defects entries on the GO:0001216 row of this review; should the two nodes be merged?

Suggested Experiments

Experiment: Define the operator and the activation mechanism. Map the VanR binding site on the vanH and vanR promoters by footprinting with phosphorylated and unphosphorylated protein, measure the stoichiometry on DNA, and test whether a phosphomimetic substitution at Asp53 gives constitutive promoter occupancy and constitutive resistance in vivo.

Experiment: Test the reported inhibition of VanR DNA binding by VanS. Reconstitute full-length VanS in nanodiscs with VanR and the vanH promoter, and ask whether the sensor sequesters the regulator independently of its phosphatase activity, using a phosphatase-dead VanS variant to separate the two effects.

Knowledge Gaps

What is not known — curated, literature-grounded statements of the open unknowns (the inverse of core functions).

Gap: How phosphorylation of Asp53 is communicated to the DNA-binding domain, and what the activated VanR-promoter complex looks like, are unknown for this system. There is no structure of VanR in either state and none of it bound to the vanH promoter, the operator sequence it recognises has not been defined at base-pair resolution in the published record used here, and whether activation works by relieving an autoinhibitory contact, by promoting oligomerisation on the promoter, or by both is undetermined. The reported inhibition of VanR's DNA binding by VanS itself is a further unexplained layer.

BIOLOGY MF_DARK

What is known: Firmly established: the domain organisation, the phosphorylation site, the kinetics and specificity of phosphotransfer from VanS, the stability of the phosphoaspartate, dephosphorylation by VanS, the increase in promoter affinity on phosphorylation, the monomeric state of the purified protein, and the identity of the cotranscribed target genes.

Significance: An activated-state structure would show whether the latch can be jammed pharmacologically, which is an attractive way to block inducible resistance without having to inhibit any of the three remodelling enzymes; it would also settle whether the reported VanS inhibition of DNA binding is a real third layer of control.

core_functions:
- description: >-
    Phosphorylation-controlled transcriptional activator of the van resistance
    operon. VanR's receiver domain accepts a phosphoryl group on Asp53 from the
    phosphohistidine of the sensor kinase VanS, with a K_M of 3 micromolar and a
    ten-thousand-fold kinetic preference over a heterologous regulator; the
    resulting phosphoaspartate is stable for ten to twelve hours in vitro.
    Phosphorylation greatly raises the affinity of its OmpR/PhoB-type winged-helix
    domain for the vanR and vanH promoters, and from the vanH promoter vanH, vanA
    and vanX are cotranscribed. Because the mark is chemically long-lived, the off
    state depends on VanS actively removing it rather than on spontaneous decay,
    which makes VanR a latch rather than a timer.
