Gene Description

VanY is a membrane-anchored, zinc-dependent D,D-carboxypeptidase encoded in the Tn1546-type van gene cluster of glycopeptide-resistant enterococci and induced by vancomycin along with the rest of the cluster. A single N-terminal transmembrane helix tethers it to the cytoplasmic membrane; the catalytic region belongs to the peptidase M15B family and carries a zinc site formed by two histidines and an aspartate, with an aspartate as the catalytic proton donor. The enzyme removes the C-terminal residue from assembled peptidoglycan precursors: it cleaves the terminal D-alanine from UDP-MurNAc-pentapeptide, and it also releases the C-terminal D-hydroxy acid from the depsipeptide precursor that the resistance ligase VanA generates. It has no transpeptidase or beta-lactamase activity, and, unlike the penicillin-binding serine D,D-carboxypeptidases, it is not inhibited by beta-lactam antibiotics, which is what marks it as a metalloenzyme. Functionally VanY is the second and weaker half of a clearance pair: VanX destroys the free D-Ala-D-Ala dipeptide before it can be incorporated, while VanY trims precursors that were assembled with D-Ala-D-Ala before VanX could act. Because it is not selective for the dipeptide, and because the depsipeptide precursor is a substrate too, it is dispensable: a vanY-deficient strain remains vancomycin-resistant, and the gene only slightly raises the resistance level conferred by VanH, VanA and VanX.

Core Functions

Membrane-anchored zinc D,D-carboxypeptidase that trims assembled peptidoglycan precursors. VanY removes the C-terminal D-alanine from UDP-MurNAc-pentapeptide and the C-terminal D-hydroxy acid from the depsipeptide precursor made by VanA, without transpeptidase or beta-lactamase activity and without being inhibited by beta-lactams. In the resistance cluster it works downstream of VanX: VanX removes the free D-Ala-D-Ala dipeptide before incorporation, VanY removes the terminus from precursors that carry it already. Because it cleaves the depsipeptide too, it is a blunt instrument, which is why it only raises the level of resistance rather than being required for it.

Suggested Questions for Experts

Q: The InterPro M15 signature produces GO:0006508 proteolysis for both vanY and vanX, but neither enzyme hydrolyses a protein: VanY trims a UDP-MurNAc-peptide precursor and VanX cleaves a free dipeptide. Should the InterPro2GO mapping for these families point to peptidoglycan or peptide terms rather than to proteolysis?

Q: No gene of the van cluster carries an antibiotic-response annotation in GOA, although the whole cluster is vancomycin-inducible. For vanY the claim is weaker than for the others, since UniProt states it is not necessary for resistance. Is GO:0046677 the right way to record a dispensable accessory contribution, or would an annotation extension or a regulatory term be preferable?

Suggested Experiments

Experiment: Determine which substrate pool VanY trims in vivo. Compare the muropeptide composition of wild-type and vanY-deleted E. faecium by HPLC after vancomycin induction, looking separately at the soluble UDP-MurNAc-peptide pool and at the polymerised sacculus, to see whether the terminal residue disappears from one, the other, or both.

Experiment: Establish the topology. Use cysteine-accessibility or reporter-fusion mapping on the single N-terminal helix to place the catalytic domain inside or outside the membrane, which decides whether the soluble nucleotide precursor can be a physiological substrate at all.

Experiment: Test the zinc assignment directly. Measure the metal content of purified VanY and the effect of chelators and of zinc, together with substitutions of the three predicted zinc ligands, on the carboxypeptidase activity. The present assignment rests on homology plus the negative beta-lactam result.

Knowledge Gaps

What is not known — curated, literature-grounded statements of the open unknowns (the inverse of core functions).

Gap: What VanY's real substrate is in a growing enterococcus is not settled. The assays were done on UDP-MurNAc-pentapeptide and on a diacetyl-L-Lys-D-Ala-D-Ala model peptide with an mDAP-containing precursor, whereas UniProt notes that the physiological precursor of this organism probably carries L-lysine at the third position; and because the enzyme is membrane-anchored it could equally act on lipid-linked or already-polymerised peptidoglycan rather than on the soluble nucleotide precursor. Which pool it actually trims, and on which side of the membrane its catalytic domain sits, are open.

BIOLOGY BP_DARK

What is known: Firmly established: the reaction on UDP-MurNAc-pentapeptide and on the VanA depsipeptide, the absence of transpeptidase and beta-lactamase activity, the insensitivity to beta-lactams, the membrane association, the single transmembrane helix and the M15B zinc site, and the dispensability of the gene for resistance.

Significance: If VanY acts on polymerised peptidoglycan rather than on the nucleotide precursor, it is a cell-wall-remodelling enzyme that happens to help resistance, not a precursor-editing one, and that changes what its small contribution to the resistance level is actually doing.

What VanY does

VanY is the accessory, dispensable member of the cluster: a membrane-anchored
D,D-carboxypeptidase that finishes what VanX starts, by trimming precursors that
were already assembled with D-Ala-D-Ala.

PMID:1510448
PMID:1510448
PMID:1510448

So its specificity is the mirror image of VanX's: VanX spares the depsipeptide,
while VanY cleaves both the dipeptide- and the depsipeptide-terminated
precursor. That is also why VanY is not required for resistance and only raises
its level a little:
PMID:8852545
PMID:8852545
UniProt states the dispensability directly, from two papers.

It is a zinc, not a serine, carboxypeptidase. The functional evidence for that is
the inhibitor profile:
PMID:1510448
which is what distinguishes the metallo D,D-carboxypeptidases from the
penicillin-binding serine enzymes; UniProt adds a zinc cofactor and three
zinc-coordinating residues, and places the protein in the peptidase M15B family.

Annotation issues

Only four rows, two of them location.

  • GO:0005886 plasma membrane is directly observed and is where the enzyme acts,
    on membrane-associated precursors; UniProt adds a single-pass transmembrane
    helix. Both the EXP and the mapped row are accepted.
  • GO:0008233 peptidase activity is the InterPro signature's generic term and is
    far less specific than the evidence supports. GO:0009046 zinc D-Ala-D-Ala carboxypeptidase activity is an is_a descendant and states the measured
    reaction, the metal class and the substrate, so the row is refined to it. This
    is also the typing used in the module.
  • GO:0006508 proteolysis has the same problem here as on vanX, but the
    replacement differs because the substrate differs: VanY does not act on a free
    peptide but on UDP-MurNAc-pentapeptide and on the corresponding depsipeptide,
    i.e. on peptidoglycan precursors. GO:0000270 peptidoglycan metabolic process
    is the term that fits, and UniProt's keywords for this entry already include
    peptidoglycan synthesis and cell wall biogenesis/degradation.

Nothing in GOA connects vanY to antibiotic resistance, so GO:0046677 response to antibiotic is proposed as a NEW row. The reason given is deliberately weaker
than for the other van genes, because VanY is dispensable; what justifies it is
that VanY is vancomycin-inducible, is part of the resistance cluster, and does
perform a step that raises the resistance level rather than merely accompanying
it.

    and the C-terminal D-hydroxy acid from the depsipeptide precursor made by
    VanA, without transpeptidase or beta-lactamase activity and without being
    inhibited by beta-lactams. In the resistance cluster it works downstream of
    VanX: VanX removes the free D-Ala-D-Ala dipeptide before incorporation, VanY
    removes the terminus from precursors that carry it already. Because it cleaves
    the depsipeptide too, it is a blunt instrument, which is why it only raises the
    level of resistance rather than being required for it.

- gap_statement: >-
    What VanY's real substrate is in a growing enterococcus is not settled. The
    assays were done on UDP-MurNAc-pentapeptide and on a diacetyl-L-Lys-D-Ala-D-Ala
    model peptide with an mDAP-containing precursor, whereas UniProt notes that the
    physiological precursor of this organism probably carries L-lysine at the third
    position; and because the enzyme is membrane-anchored it could equally act on
    lipid-linked or already-polymerised peptidoglycan rather than on the soluble
    nucleotide precursor. Which pool it actually trims, and on which side of the
    membrane its catalytic domain sits, are open.
