Gene Description

A0A0K2YWP2 is a 54-residue partial sequence of a PgeF/LACC1-family (YfiH, DUF152) protein from Mesorhizobium australicum isolate T-G1. It was obtained as an RT-PCR amplicon from primers designed on the WSM2073 locus Mesau_02205. The fragment matches the C-terminal region of WSM2073 Mesau_02205 (UniProtKB:L0KKS2, residues 207-260) at about 89% identity, and does not include the catalytic cysteine of the family. The full-length T-G1 protein is expected to be, like characterized bacterial PgeF, a cytoplasmic hydrolase. Such enzymes proofread peptidoglycan precursor synthesis by removing misincorporated L-serine or glycine from UDP-N-acetylmuramoyl-peptides. Its transcript is more abundant in acidic (pH 5.5) Mn(II)-oxidizing T-G1 cultures than at neutral pH. The gene was first described as a putative laccase-like multicopper oxidase, a label that comes from the family's historical "Cu-oxidase_4" name. No function of the T-G1 protein, including any role in Mn(II) oxidation, has been tested.

Proposed New Ontology Terms

UDP-N-acetylmuramoyl-peptide editing amidase activity

Definition: Catalysis of the hydrolysis of the amide bond between the lactyl group of UDP-N-acetylmuramic acid and the first amino acid of a UDP-N-acetylmuramoyl-peptide peptidoglycan precursor, preferentially when that residue is a non-canonical L-serine or glycine instead of L-alanine. The products are UDP-N-acetylmuramate and the free peptide. The activity proofreads cytoplasmic peptidoglycan precursor synthesis.

Justification: Same proposal as in the L0KKS2 (Mesau_02205) review. PgeF activity has been shown biochemically (PMID:40632566), but GO has no term for it. GO:0008745 N-acetylmuramoyl-L-alanine amidase activity is defined on cell-wall glycopeptides, under peptidoglycan muralytic activity.

Parent term: hydrolase activity, acting on carbon-nitrogen (but not peptide) bonds, in linear amides

Supporting Evidence:

  • PMID:40632566 : confirming that PgeF cleaves the amide bond between UDP-MurNAc and the peptide

A0A0K2YWP2 — Mesorhizobium australicum T-G1 PgeF fragment — review notes

Reviewed 2026-10-28. The family literature, the PgeF biochemistry and the critique of the CMMMech
bixbyite-mn.yaml claims are in genes/MESAW/Mesau_02205/Mesau_02205-notes.md (UniProtKB:L0KKS2).
They are not repeated here.

Identity

  • A0A0K2YWP2_9HYPH: 54-aa TrEMBL fragment (NON_TER at both ends), PE 3, taxon 536018
    (species level), strain T-G1. Translated from EMBL LN864498 / CRL80388.1, the RT-PCR amplicon
    deposited by Bohu et al. 2015
    PMID:26236307.
    No gene name in UniProt. Not in any reference proteome.
  • Same protein as L0KKS2? No. The UniParc ids differ (UPI00067A44EE vs UPI00022DFB2D) and the sequences
    differ. The fragment aligns to L0KKS2 residues 207-260 at 48/54 identity (88.9%), without gaps
    (A0A0K2YWP2-bioinformatics/RESULTS.md). It is the T-G1 counterpart of the WSM2073 locus from a
    different strain: probably orthologous, as Bohu et al. call it, but a 54-residue fragment
    cannot establish locus identity. Following repo conventions this gives one review per accession, with
    L0KKS2 recorded as an alternative_records REFERENCE_PROTEOME_CANDIDATE (UP000010998). Equivalence
    is not asserted and GO annotations are not transferred.
  • The fragment covers the C-terminal quarter of the PgeF domain. The catalytic cysteine (L0KKS2
    Cys117, aligned with E. coli PgeF Cys107) lies outside it. Any functional statement about this
    record is a family-level inference about the full-length T-G1 protein whose partial sequence it is.

CMMMech

  • CMMMech gives this record gene_symbol: Mesau_02205. That is the WSM2073 locus tag, not a T-G1
    gene name.
  • Its functional_role repeats the "putative multicopper polyphenol oxidase (Cu-oxidase_4)"
    label. The protein belongs to the PgeF/LACC1 family and is not a multicopper oxidase; see the
    MESAW notes.
  • The only experimental data on this sequence are transcript levels (qRT-PCR) in pH 5.5 versus
    pH 7.2 T-G1 cultures. They show expression correlated with acidic Mn(II) oxidation. They do not
    show function.

Decisions

  • cytoplasm (IEA SubCell, ARBA-derived) -> ACCEPT as the location of the full-length T-G1 PgeF,
    the cytoplasmic PG-precursor editing enzyme.
  • No NEW rows on the fragment record. Process and function proposals are made on the
    full-length reference-proteome record L0KKS2.

Findings

    (UPI00067A44EE vs UPI00022DFB2D) and the sequences are not identical.
  • Local alignment (BLOSUM62, gap open -10, extend -0.5) places the whole 54-residue fragment on
    L0KKS2 residues 207-260 with 48/54 identical residues (88.9% identity, no gaps).
  • The fragment therefore covers only the C-terminal quarter of the PgeF domain. The catalytic
    cysteine of E. coli PgeF (Cys107, PMID:40632566) aligns to L0KKS2 Cys117, which lies outside
    the fragment.
  • L0KKS2 vs E. coli PgeF (global alignment): 101/236 identical aligned residues (42.8%).

Interpretation

A0A0K2YWP2 is a partial sequence of the T-G1 ortholog of WSM2073 Mesau_02205, from a different
strain, and is not interchangeable with L0KKS2 at the sequence level. The 11% divergence over the
amplified region is consistent with strain-level divergence of an orthologous locus, but a 54-residue
fragment cannot by itself establish orthology versus paralogy; the WSM2073 genome contains a single
PgeF-family gene (Bohu et al. 2015 report "only one" such gene from a keyword search; not
independently re-verified here).
