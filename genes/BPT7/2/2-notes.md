# Escherichia phage T7 gene 2 (Bacterial RNA polymerase inhibitor) Notes

## Overview
- **Gene symbol**: `2` (Bacterial RNA polymerase inhibitor; gp2)
- **UniProt ID**: `P03704`
- **Taxon**: Escherichia phage T7 (`NCBITaxon:10760`)
- **Length**: 64 AA (~7.1 kDa)

## Biological Function and Mechanism
- **Essential inhibitor of host $\text{E}\sigma^{70}$ RNA polymerase**: Gene `2` is a Class II middle gene located between `1.8` and `2.5` [PMID:6864790]. It encodes a 64-AA protein (`gp2`) that binds a 53-residue segment in the jaw domain of the host *Escherichia coli* RNA polymerase $\beta'$ subunit (`RpoC`) and inhibits transcription initiation by the $\sigma^{70}$ holoenzyme ($\text{E}\sigma^{70}$) at `-10/-35` consensus promoters by blocking $\sigma^{70}$ region 4 interaction with the `-35` element and preventing open promoter complex formation [PMID:10369763].
- **Essential role in T7 DNA packaging and concatemer processing**: Unlike most host-shutoff factors, gene `2` is strictly essential for T7 infection: if host RNA polymerase is not inhibited by gp2 during late infection, transcription elongation by the slow host RNA polymerase along T7 DNA concatemers interferes with T7 DNA packaging and leads to aberrant degradation of viral concatemers [PMID:6864790, PMID:24218612].
