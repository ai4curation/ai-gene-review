  - Contains a catalytic zinc-binding cleft (His-17, Tyr-46 via water, His-122, Cys-130).

## Functional Evidence
- **Bifunctional zinc amidase & T7 RNA polymerase inhibitor**:
  - Crystal structure at 2.2 Å resolution (`PMID:8171031`) shows that T7 lysozyme (gp3.5) is a bifunctional enzyme with a catalytic zinc atom bound in a cleft (coordinated by His-17, His-122, Cys-130, and via a water molecule to Tyr-46). Zinc is required for its N-acetylmuramoyl-L-alanine amidase activity (which cuts amide bonds between N-acetylmuramic acid and L-alanine in the bacterial peptidoglycan wall, rather than glycosidic bonds like true lysozymes) but is not required for its binding to and inhibition of T7 RNA polymerase [PMID:8171031, "The lysozyme of bacteriophage T7 is a bifunctional protein that cuts amide bonds in the bacterial cell wall and binds to and inhibits transcription by T7 RNA polymerase. A zinc atom is located in the cleft, bound directly to three amino acids and, through a water molecule, to a fourth. Zinc is required for amidase activity but not for inhibition of T7 RNA polymerase."].
- **Allosteric negative regulation of T7 RNA polymerase transcription**:
  - Biochemical studies (`PMID:9192997`) show that T7 lysozyme forms a reversible 1:1 complex with T7 RNA polymerase (gp1) at a site remote from the catalytic cleft, locking the polymerase in an initiation/abortive conformation and inhibiting the transition from initiation to processive elongation, particularly at Class II promoters [PMID:9192997, "Bacteriophage T7 lysozyme is known to inhibit transcription by T7 RNA polymerase. Lysozyme present before initiation inhibited the synthesis of long RNA chains but did not inhibit elongation when added shortly after chains were initiated."].
  - During T7 infection (`PMID:15223315`), gp3.5 amidase activity is required for abrupt host cell lysis and release of progeny phage particles, while the gp1-gp3.5 interaction shuts down Class II transcription and stimulates DNA replication and maturation [PMID:15223315, "The amidase activity of lysozyme is needed for abrupt lysis and release of phage particles but appears to have no role in replication and packaging."].
