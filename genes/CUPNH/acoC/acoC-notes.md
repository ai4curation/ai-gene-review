# Cupriavidus necator H16 (historically Alcaligenes eutrophus H16) acoC: source-grounded review

## Identity and native context

Current CUPNH records use H16 strain tax381666. Each links the original M66060 clone and later AM260480 genome; all three are sequence version3, so locus identity is secure without claiming every current residue was assayed in 1991.

AcoC is the lipoyl-bearing E2 acetyltransferase of the acetoin catabolic system in Cupriavidus necator H16 (historically Alcaligenes eutrophus H16). Its lipoyl arm accepts acetyl equivalents generated by AcoA/AcoB, and its acetyltransferase activity transfers acetyl to coenzyme A, producing acetyl-CoA and a reduced lipoyl arm for E3-dependent reoxidation. The native protein was purified as the fast-migrating protein, linked to acoC by N-terminal sequencing and assigned an E2 role by comparative sequence evidence and curated functional assignment.

## Assays and limits

[PMID:2061286] "whereas those deduced from acoC
for FMP (Mr, 38,941) revealed homology to the dihydrolipoamide acetyltransferase
of Escherichia coli."

DCPIP is an artificial electron acceptor. Its oxidative-hydrolytic cleavage assay does not by itself establish physiological lipoyl acetylation or autonomous activity of either isolated E1 subunit. PMID1898934 explicitly reports native Pelobacter E1/E2/E3 reconstitution with CoA-dependent NAD reduction. The 1994 gene paper maps purified components to acoABCL; heterologous expression is a distinct observation. H16 lacks an identified E3 in acoXABC; a Pelobacter AcoL must not be silently imported as its native partner. Bacillus physiological evidence is strongest at the native aco system and E1 activity; E2/E3 assignments retain their sequence-inference status.

## All-row decisions and ontology

- GO:0004742 (IEA): ACCEPT. The native-system assignment, lipoyl-bearing architecture and curated E2 chemistry support this reaction as a functional inference. GO:0004742 is written reversibly; acetoin catabolism uses the acetyl-CoA-forming direction. Its acceptance does not imply membership in a pyruvate-specific complex.
- GO:0045150 (IEA): ACCEPT. AcoC performs part of the acetoin catabolic enzyme system: E2 acetyl transfer to CoA. Native protein identification and comparative biochemical evidence support this participation, rather than a downstream phenotype alone.

GO:0004739 specifically uses pyruvate and releases CO2. GO:0019152 is acetoin/diacetyl NAD-dependent interconversion, not the acetoin-cleaving E1 reaction. GO:0016624 is therefore the compatible broad E1 chemistry. E2 GO:0004742 is reversible and used toward acetyl-CoA formation here. E3 GO:0004148 explicitly uses a protein-bound lipoyl substrate and NAD+. No new process annotation is proposed merely for pathway completeness.

## Distinct AcoC architecture

The current H16 record annotates a lipoyl domain (9–84) and an alpha/beta-hydrolase domain (137–360), unlike canonical SYNCB/BACSU E2 acetyltransferase folds. The 1991 abstract reports native FMP purification, N-terminal mapping and sequence similarity, not a standalone E2 transfer assay. Targeted literature searching did not identify a primary functional revision; current PROSITE PDOC00168 still calls FMP “most probably a dihydrolipamide acyltransferase.” Retain the curated E2 functional inference with that limit rather than claiming one conserved catalytic family or discarding it solely from a different fold. Original H16 AcoC sequence discrepancies at positions 95 and 121 are documented by UniProt against AAA21950; the current reference sequence is not presented as the exact purified 1991 sequence.
