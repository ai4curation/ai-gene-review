Gene Description

NAD(+)-dependent glycerol-3-phosphate dehydrogenase (EC 1.1.1.8) of the acid- and osmotolerant yeast Pichia kudriavzevii (Issatchenkia orientalis), a 388-residue cytosolic enzyme of the NAD-dependent glycerol-3-phosphate dehydrogenase family (PANTHER PTHR11728, NCBIfam TIGR03376, PIRSF000114) with an N-terminal Rossmann-fold NAD(+)-binding domain and a C-terminal substrate-binding domain carrying the conserved proton-acceptor lysine. Although the EC reaction is written as the oxidation of sn-glycerol 3-phosphate, the physiological direction in yeast is reductive: the enzyme reduces dihydroxyacetone phosphate from glycolysis to glycerol 3-phosphate using NADH, which glycerol-3-phosphate phosphatase then dephosphorylates to glycerol. It is therefore the committed and rate-controlling step of glycerol synthesis, which serves two purposes in yeast: accumulating glycerol as a compatible solute during osmotic stress, and reoxidising surplus cytosolic NADH. The protein complements Saccharomyces cerevisiae gpd1 and gpd1 gpd2 mutants, restoring osmotic tolerance and intracellular glycerol accumulation, and deleting the gene in I. orientalis abolishes glycerol as a fermentation byproduct.

Core Functions

NAD(+)-dependent reduction of dihydroxyacetone phosphate to sn-glycerol 3-phosphate in the cytosol, the committed step of glycerol biosynthesis. The reaction consumes cytosolic NADH, so it serves both as the route to glycerol as a compatible solute under osmotic stress and as a redox sink for surplus NADH during fermentative growth. Direct evidence for this protein comes from complementation of S. cerevisiae gpd1 and gpd1 gpd2 mutants with restored osmotolerance, glycerol accumulation and NaCl-inducible GPD activity; in P. kudriavzevii itself, deleting the gene removes glycerol from the fermentation broth.

Suggested Questions for Experts

Q: Which initiator codon does this gene use in vivo? The reference-proteome model A0A2U9RB90 adds 27 N-terminal residues to the 388-residue product cloned and shown to be functional, and the extension is basic, Ser/Thr-rich and free of acidic residues, the composition of a mitochondrial targeting presequence, so it would be worth testing for mitochondrial import rather than assuming it is a gene-model artefact.

Q: Does P. kudriavzevii use a single NAD(+)-dependent glycerol-3-phosphate dehydrogenase for both the osmotic and the redox role that S. cerevisiae splits between GPD1 and GPD2? Only one family member is present in the reference proteome, and the regulation of that single gene has not been described.

Suggested Experiments

Experiment: Purify the recombinant enzyme and measure steady-state kinetics in both directions with NAD(+)/NADH, to establish the catalytic preference directly rather than inferring it from genetics, and test whether NADP(H) is used at all (which would decide between GO:0141152 and the broader GO:0047952).

Experiment: Determine the subcellular distribution of a tagged GPD in P. kudriavzevii under normal and hyperosmotic conditions, to test whether the stress-induced nuclear redistribution seen for S. cerevisiae Gpd1p, which is the basis of the transferred nucleus annotation, occurs in this species. Score the peroxisomal pool in the same experiment: Gpd1p is imported into peroxisomes through an N-terminal PTS2 read by Pex7p, so co-localisation with a peroxisomal marker, and the behaviour of the protein in a pex7 background, would say whether that route is also used here. The N-terminus of A8W1U0 lies in the region where Gpd1p's PTS2 sits, and the 27-residue extension of the reference-proteome model falls in the same window, so the two gene models should be tested separately.

Experiment: Compare the two gene models by 5' RACE or ribosome profiling, and express both the 388- and 415-residue forms in a gpd-null background, to establish which product is made and whether the N-terminal extension changes localisation or activity.

Why GO:0006973 intracellular accumulation of glycerol was not added

GO:0006973 intracellular accumulation of glycerol was considered as a NEW row and
declined. The participation test is not the obstacle: an enzyme that makes the glycerol
that accumulates does part of the work of accumulating it, and genes/yeast/GPD1
carries the term as a core process. The obstacle is whose phenotype was measured. The
only accumulation data for this protein come from expressing it in a heterologous
S. cerevisiae host, where the osmotic response machinery, the glycerol channel Fps1
and the transcriptional regulation are all S. cerevisiae's, not
P. kudriavzevii's PMID:18452539. No osmotic phenotype, and no intracellular glycerol measurement, has been
reported for P. kudriavzevii itself: the only native-host data are the fermentation
knockout, which measures glycerol in the broth, not retained inside the cell

process of the cell that retains the solute, so transferring it on the strength of a
heterologous host's regulation would be asserting more than was measured. If an osmotic
or intracellular-glycerol phenotype is reported for P. kudriavzevii, the term should
be added; suggested_experiments item 2, which now scores localisation under
hyperosmotic conditions, is the natural place for that measurement.
