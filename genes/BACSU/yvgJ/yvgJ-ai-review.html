Gene Description

YvgJ (locus BSU33360) is one of four LtaS-type enzymes of Bacillus subtilis. It is a 617-residue polytopic membrane protein with five N-terminal transmembrane helices and a large extracytoplasmic catalytic domain of the alkaline phosphatase/sulfatase fold (LTA synthase family, PANTHER PTHR47371), with the conserved Mn2+-binding and active-site residues. Unlike the three polymer-forming synthases (LtaS2, LtaS1, YqgS), YvgJ functions as a lipoteichoic acid (LTA) primase: using phosphatidylglycerol as the glycerolphosphate donor, it transfers a single glycerolphosphate unit onto the diglucosyl-diacylglycerol glycolipid anchor, producing the GroP-Glc2-DAG glycolipid intermediate rather than a polyglycerolphosphate polymer. It hydrolyses phosphatidylglycerol in a Mn2+-dependent manner in vitro, but when expressed alone (in B. subtilis or in an S. aureus ltaS depletion strain) it does not produce detectable LTA polymer. The full-length protein is predicted to be cleaved after an AXA motif and the catalytic domain secreted. YvgJ is the B. subtilis counterpart of the Listeria monocytogenes LTA primase Lmo0644.

Proposed New Ontology Terms

lipoteichoic acid primase activity

Definition: Catalysis of the transfer of the sn-glycerol-1-phosphate head group of phosphatidylglycerol onto the diglucosyl-diacylglycerol glycolipid anchor, producing 1,2-diacyl-sn-glycerol and the glycerophosphate-glycolipid primer (GroP-Glc2-DAG) that initiates polyglycerophosphate lipoteichoic acid synthesis. The term covers only the transfer onto the unsubstituted glycolipid anchor; extension of an existing polyglycerophosphate chain is the separate polyglycerophosphate lipoteichoic acid synthase activity.

Justification: GO has no molecular function term for the LTA priming reaction (EC 2.7.8.-); an OLS search for lipoteichoic acid synthase or primase activity returns nothing. The nearest term, GO:0016780, is a genus several levels too general, and its one relevant existing child, GO:0008960 phosphatidylglycerol-membrane-oligosaccharide glycerophosphotransferase activity, is the Gram-negative OpgB reaction. This term and the proposed polyglycerophosphate lipoteichoic acid synthase activity are defined to be disjoint by acceptor (the glycolipid anchor versus the terminal glycerophosphate of a growing chain), so neither entails the other and an enzyme shown to do both would carry both. The distinction is not predictable from sequence - Wörmann et al. found no motif separating synthases from primases - so it should be asserted only from product analysis, as it is here for YvgJ (GroP-Glc2-DAG accumulation, no polymer).

Parent term: phosphotransferase activity, for other substituted phosphate groups

Summary: Mn2+ is the catalytic metal of the LtaS-type active site. The purified YvgJ catalytic domain gives its highest phosphatidylglycerol-hydrolysing activity with MnCl2 and only weak activity with Mg2+ or Ca2+, and UniProt records four Mn(2+)-coordinating residues (Glu251, Thr293, Asp467, His468) in the sulfatase-fold active site, Thr293 also being the catalytic nucleophile.
Reason: The protein itself binds the metal, so there is no participation question to answer: the metal is a cofactor of the reaction YvgJ catalyses, not something another gene product supplies to it. Wörmann et al. tested Mg2+, Mn2+, Ca2+ and Zn2+ side by side on the purified enzymatic domains of all four B. subtilis LtaS-type proteins and found Mn2+ dependence in every case; the priming reaction uses the same chemistry as elongation, so the metal requirement is not specific to the polymer-forming paralogues. The metal-binding residues are curated on the UniProt entry (ECO:0000250, from the S. aureus LtaS Mn(2+) cocrystal). GOA has no metal-binding row for any of the four paralogues, so this is added as NEW rather than refined from an existing annotation.

Core Functions

Lipoteichoic acid primase. Using phosphatidylglycerol as the glycerolphosphate donor, it transfers a single glycerolphosphate unit onto the diglucosyl-diacylglycerol glycolipid anchor at the outer face of the cytoplasmic membrane in a Mn2+-dependent manner, producing the GroP-Glc2-DAG primer. It does not elongate the polyglycerolphosphate backbone.

Suggested Questions for Experts

Q: What is the physiological role of the YvgJ-primed GroP-Glc2-DAG intermediate in B. subtilis, and do the polymer-forming synthases LtaS2/LtaS1/YqgS depend on YvgJ priming, or can they both prime and elongate?

Suggested Experiments

Experiment: Reconstitute the priming and elongation steps in vitro with purified YvgJ and a polymer-forming synthase (e.g. LtaS2) on defined glycolipid substrates, to test whether YvgJ-made GroP-Glc2-DAG is the preferred substrate for elongation.

Identity

  • UniProt O32206 = YVGJ_BACSU, gene yvgJ, locus BSU33360 (note: the CMMMech
    thulium record and the Moriwaki manuscript use BSU33400, a locus-tag
    discrepancy worth checking; UniProt current = BSU33360). PE 3 (inferred from
    homology).
  • Functionally characterised as an LTA primase (not a polymer synthase):
    it transfers the first glycerolphosphate onto the glycolipid anchor to make
    GroP-Glc2-DAG PMID:21255105. A strain expressing YvgJ only makes no LTA polymer.
    It is the B. subtilis counterpart of L. monocytogenes primase Lmo0644.
  • Mn2+-dependent PG hydrolysis in vitro like the other three; does not restore
    S. aureus LTA synthesis PMID:17483484.

CMMMech cross-reference (data/records/thulium.yaml) — do NOT edit CMMMech

  • Identity O32206 = YvgJ: correct. CMMMech calls it
    "Lipoteichoic acid primase/synthase YvgJ", consistent with its primase role.
  • Locus tag discrepancy. CMMMech uses yvgJ / BSU33400; current UniProt
    O32206 lists BSU33360. (BSU33400 appears to be an outdated/renumbered tag.)
  • EC number wrong. CMMMech asserts EC 2.7.8.40 (an unrelated Aeromonas
    O-antigen enzyme). YvgJ has no specific EC in UniProt; its reaction is a
    glycerophosphate priming transfer (EC 2.7.8.-).
  • The CMMMech causal graph treats all four paralogues as "LTA synthases" that
    "produce" the LTA layer. YvgJ is strictly a primase and cannot elongate
    the polymer; the shared GENE_OR_PROTEIN node label slightly over-states YvgJ's
    role, though it does contribute the priming step.

proposed_new_terms:
- proposed_name: lipoteichoic acid primase activity
  proposed_definition: >-
    Catalysis of the transfer of the sn-glycerol-1-phosphate head group of
    phosphatidylglycerol onto the diglucosyl-diacylglycerol glycolipid anchor,
    producing 1,2-diacyl-sn-glycerol and the glycerophosphate-glycolipid primer
    (GroP-Glc2-DAG) that initiates polyglycerophosphate lipoteichoic acid
    synthesis. The term covers only the transfer onto the unsubstituted glycolipid
    anchor; extension of an existing polyglycerophosphate chain is the separate
    polyglycerophosphate lipoteichoic acid synthase activity.
  justification: >-
    GO has no molecular function term for the LTA priming reaction (EC 2.7.8.-);
    an OLS search for lipoteichoic acid synthase or primase activity returns
    nothing. The nearest term, GO:0016780, is a genus several levels too general,
    and its one relevant existing child, GO:0008960
    phosphatidylglycerol-membrane-oligosaccharide glycerophosphotransferase
    activity, is the Gram-negative OpgB reaction. This term and the proposed
    polyglycerophosphate lipoteichoic acid synthase activity are defined to be
    disjoint by acceptor (the glycolipid anchor versus the terminal
    glycerophosphate of a growing chain), so neither entails the other and an
    enzyme shown to do both would carry both. The distinction is not predictable
    from sequence - Wörmann et al. found no motif separating synthases from
    primases - so it should be asserted only from product analysis, as it is
    here for YvgJ (GroP-Glc2-DAG accumulation, no polymer).

    label: phosphotransferase activity, for other substituted phosphate groups
