# Native Bacillus Nas evidence audit, 2026-10-29

## Primary genetics and sequence analysis

[Ogawa et al., 1995, PMID:7868621](https://journals.asm.org/doi/pdf/10.1128/jb.177.5.1409-1413.1995) was read in the publisher's five-page PDF through browser extraction. The normal publisher fetch returned 403 and the canonical cache remains abstract-only; no cache was rewritten. Cite cached abstract text only under its PMID. Detailed conclusions below are this source analysis.

JH642 derivatives separated nitrate from nitrite utilization. NasA loss abolished nitrate growth despite retained nitrate reductase activity, supporting uptake. NasB and NasC were independently required for nitrate utilization after downstream expression was restored. NasD and NasE were required for nitrite utilization with analogous polarity controls. NasB resembles nitrite reductases but lacks the conserved siroheme-associated cysteine motif present in NasD. Thus the family-level nitrite-reductase and heme mappings on NasB conflict with its specialized electron-transfer role. NasC carries the molybdenum-cofactor domain; NasB supplies the dinucleotide-binding region. The nitrate assay used reduced methyl viologen in permeabilized cells, so it did not measure physiological NADH specificity. Nitrite activity was not reproducibly measured in this study. These are genetic and sequence-based subunit assignments, not a purified native stoichiometric structure. NasA mutants retained nitrite utilization; this does not exclude redundant or incidental nitrite transport.

## Native activity, donor and localization

[PMID:9765565](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9765565/) was obtained through the normal PMID fetcher as a genuine full article. JH642 and its derivatives were tested with downstream expression controls. Loss of NasD or NasE eliminated the NADH-dependent activity under aerobic and anaerobic conditions. Most activity partitioned into cytosol. NADPH activity was measurable but below 10% of NADH activity; neither absolute NADH exclusivity nor equal donor efficiency is supported. NasDE contributes to nitrite assimilation and anaerobic nitrite reduction. NasBC is the assimilatory upstream enzyme; respiratory NarGHJI can instead supply nitrite anaerobically. These assignments are clear in the results and discussion. The abstract's sentence pairing narGHI/nasBC with anabolic/catabolic metabolism in that order is reversed relative to its own body and should not be propagated.

## Scope and source identity

The reviewed accessions P42432-P42436 are B. subtilis 168 proteins. The experiments name JH642 and its derivatives, a laboratory lineage of 168; no claim that the assay strain was the exact current reference isolate is needed. The 1999 review [PMID:10542156](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10542156/), available as `url:https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC94119/`, explicitly assigns NasB to electron transfer and NasC to nitrate reduction. It supplies the NADH donor interpretation for NasBC; the primary methyl-viologen experiment alone does not. PMID and PMC URL denote one paper, not independent biological evidence. The URL manifestation retains fulltext quote provenance. PMID:24467397 section 5.5.3 corroborates cross-taxon nomenclature but its cyanobacterial donor kinetics are not native Bacillus measurements.

FAD and iron-sulfur assignments combine native sequence analysis with curated target-domain/ligand annotations. NasB's exact cluster stoichiometry is not established here. NasE's Rieske-like domain and predicted ligands support a 2Fe-2S cofactor, but do not prove a particular electron-transfer step or isolated-subunit nitrite catalysis. NasDE assembly requirements are experimentally supported; precise oligomeric stoichiometry is not inferred from a homology-only NasD homodimer note.
