Gene Description

lspA encodes type II signal peptidase (SPase II, lipoprotein signal peptidase) of Bacillus subtilis, a polytopic cytoplasmic-membrane aspartic protease of the peptidase A8 family that removes the signal peptide from diacylglyceryl-modified prolipoproteins immediately upstream of the lipidated lipobox cysteine, the second step of lipoprotein maturation after Lgt. Asp-102 and Asp-129 are the proposed catalytic residues. The B. subtilis enzyme complements an E. coli lsp mutant, but unlike its E. coli counterpart it is not essential for viability; lspA-null cells grow, although they are impaired at low and high temperatures, accumulate lipid-modified PrsA precursor and secrete alpha-amylase poorly. B. subtilis lacks the canonical Lnt route but can further N-acetylate lipoproteins through LhaT/YpjA; the LspA-generated product is therefore an intermediate rather than proof of a universally unmodified mature amino terminus.

Proposed New Ontology Terms

lipoprotein signal peptidase activity

Definition: Catalysis of the hydrolysis of the signal peptide from a bacterial membrane diacylglyceryl-prolipoprotein, cleaving immediately N-terminal to the lipid-modified lipobox cysteine.

Justification: GO:0009003 is an active signal-peptidase genus, and GO:0004190 captures the aspartic mechanism. Neither alone captures the diacylglyceryl-prolipoprotein substrate and cleavage immediately before lipidated cysteine. The proposal concerns this residual type-II/lipoprotein specificity, not absence of any signal-peptidase term.

Parent term: aspartic-type endopeptidase activity

Supporting Evidence:

  • file:BACSU/lspA/lspA-uniprot.txt : This protein specifically catalyzes the removal of signal peptides from prolipoproteins.
  • PMID:10497172 : Type II signal peptidases (SPase II) remove signal peptides from lipid-modified preproteins of eubacteria.
