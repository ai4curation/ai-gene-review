# Bacillus GlpD primary-source reading

Accessed 2026-11-07. Primary full PDF PMID4368746:
https://journals.asm.org/doi/pdf/10.1128/jb.119.2.431-442.1974
The shared publication cache is abstract-only and was not rewritten.

Short verbatim excerpt, p.433:
> Enzymatic activities in the supernatant were measured.

Paraphrase of pp.432–435 and Tables 1–4: W168 and BR95-derived strains were grown with shaking. Extracts were sonicated and centrifuged at 17,000 × g for 15 minutes. The NAD-independent glycerophosphate dehydrogenase assay measured tetrazolium reduction to formazan, not physiological quinone reduction. GlpD mutants lost this activity and failed growth on glycerol or glycerophosphate; GlpK mutants retained growth on glycerophosphate. The low-speed supernatant does not distinguish soluble enzyme from membrane vesicles. The experiments establish native NAD-independent catabolic activity, not purified acceptor specificity or membrane topology.

Short verbatim excerpt:
> Whole cell membranes appeared to contain more penicillin binding protein (PBP) 2a, glycerol-3-phosphate dehydrogenase

The abstract describes strain BD474 and compares whole-cell and minicell membranes. Full-text/PDF requests failed. This is activity in a membrane preparation of another strain, not molecular localization of strain 168 P18158; it does not establish integral insertion or a GlpABC complex.

Additional primary full text inspected:
https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-020-79811-z
Short verbatim excerpt:
> in B. subtilis GlpD could play a similar role.

The 2021 paper compares E. coli membrane quinone reduction with a proposed analogous role in Bacillus. Its Bacillus deletion and menaquinone measurements do not directly assay purified GlpD electron-acceptor specificity. This caveat is consistent with retaining reviewed UniProt/PAINT quinone activity as synthesis rather than calling it a direct Bacillus quinone assay.
