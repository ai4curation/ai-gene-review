PMID:8752328 — differential precursor rescue and radiolabeled conversion
Mutations
in the gbsAB and gbsB genes confer different growth pheno-
types in high-osmolarity medium in the presence of glycine
betaine aldehyde. The D(gbsAB::neo)2 mutant strain JBB5
could not use this compound for osmoprotection, whereas the
gbsA1
gbsB strain JBB4 was able to grow in high-osmolarity
medium containing 1 mM glycine betaine aldehyde (Fig. 5A).
The inability of strains JBB5 and JBB4 to use choline as an
osmoprotectant is a reflection of a defect in the glycine betaine
synthesis pathway in these strains (Fig. 5B). The gbsAB1
strain
JH642 accumulated choline and converted it quantitatively
into glycine betaine (Fig. 5B, lanes 1 and 2), whereas choline
was taken up by strains JBB5 and JBB4 but no glycine betaine
was synthesized (Fig. 5B, lanes 3 to 6). Thus, the gbsAB gene
cluster is essential for the synthesis of glycine betaine in B.
subtilis.


PMID:8752328 — metal uncertainty
The GbsB
protein of B. subtilis lacks one of the three histidine residues
implicated in metal binding, thus leaving the nature of the
metal cofactor for this enzyme unclear.


PMID:8752328 — unresolved second GbsB oxidation
Our mutant analysis of the
gbsAB genes shows that the GbsB protein is absolutely re-
quired for the use of choline as a precursor for glycine betaine
synthesis, but it is unclear whether GbsB can also oxidize
glycine betaine aldehyde to glycine betaine.
