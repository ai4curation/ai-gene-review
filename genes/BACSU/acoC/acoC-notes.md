# Bacillus subtilis strain 168 acoC: source-grounded review

## Identity and native context

The accession is the strain168 reference protein (tax224308), with aco symbol and BSU locus verified before normal seeding. PMID10368162 studied168 (DSMZ402), with SMY as a growth comparator. AF006075 is the experimental clone set; AcoC has minor C-terminal differences from the reference sequence, explicitly reported by the authors.

AcoC is the lipoyl-bearing E2 acetyltransferase of the acetoin catabolic system in Bacillus subtilis strain 168. Its lipoyl arm accepts acetyl equivalents generated by AcoA/AcoB, and its acetyltransferase activity transfers acetyl to coenzyme A, producing acetyl-CoA and a reduced lipoyl arm for E3-dependent reoxidation. The E2 assignment rests on the native aco pathway and conserved lipoyl and catalytic features; the published acoC clone differs slightly at its C terminus from the reference sequence.

## Assays and limits

[PMID:10368162] "The N-terminal
region of B. subtilis AcoC shares most characteristics with
the dihydrolipoamide acyltransferases of various origins—such as
Lys 43 , which is presumably lipoylated, and
Gly 33 and Gly 54 , which flank this lysine
residue—and with the corresponding C-terminal catalytic domains of
various dihydrolipoamide acyltransferases, including the conserved
putative active-site histidine-aspartate couple ( 3 , 16 , 22 )."

DCPIP is an artificial electron acceptor. Its oxidative-hydrolytic cleavage assay does not by itself establish physiological lipoyl acetylation or autonomous activity of either isolated E1 subunit. PMID1898934 explicitly reports native Pelobacter E1/E2/E3 reconstitution with CoA-dependent NAD reduction. The 1994 gene paper maps purified components to acoABCL; heterologous expression is a distinct observation. H16 lacks an identified E3 in acoXABC; a Pelobacter AcoL must not be silently imported as its native partner. Bacillus physiological evidence is strongest at the native aco system and E1 activity; E2/E3 assignments retain their sequence-inference status.

## All-row decisions and ontology

- GO:0004742 (IEA): ACCEPT. The native-system assignment, lipoyl-bearing architecture and curated E2 chemistry support this reaction as a functional inference. GO:0004742 is written reversibly; acetoin catabolism uses the acetyl-CoA-forming direction. Its acceptance does not imply membership in a pyruvate-specific complex. The AF006075 clone differs slightly from reference O31550, so the reference protein is not presented as an independently assayed identical construct.
- GO:0016746 (IEA): MODIFY. The acetyl transfer between lipoyl-lysine and coenzyme A is more precise than generic acyl transfer. GO:0004742 captures the supported chemistry and is already represented by another original row. The inference is specific to the characterized aco-system protein, not to every member of the broad domain family.
- GO:0045150 (IEA): ACCEPT. AcoC performs part of the acetoin catabolic enzyme system: E2 acetyl transfer to CoA. Native Bacillus genetics establish the system-level role; the individual E2/E3 chemistry is supported by sequence and comparative biochemistry, not a separate subunit knockout.

GO:0004739 specifically uses pyruvate and releases CO2. GO:0019152 is acetoin/diacetyl NAD-dependent interconversion, not the acetoin-cleaving E1 reaction. GO:0016624 is therefore the compatible broad E1 chemistry. E2 GO:0004742 is reversible and used toward acetyl-CoA formation here. E3 GO:0004148 explicitly uses a protein-bound lipoyl substrate and NAD+. No new process annotation is proposed merely for pathway completeness.
