Gene Description

EgsA (AraM) is a soluble, homodimeric, nickel-dependent glycerol-1-phosphate dehydrogenase of Bacillus subtilis. The purified enzyme reversibly interconverts glycerone phosphate and sn-glycerol 1-phosphate, preferentially using NAD(H), with lower catalytic efficiency using NADP(H). Its stereospecific reaction produces the glycerophosphate enantiomer used by the PcrB ether-lipid synthase. EgsA is therefore a candidate source of that precursor, but its contribution to native heptaprenylglyceryl-lipid or phosphatidylglycerol synthesis has not been established. Kinetic differences between reaction directions do not determine the net cellular flux. The gene lies in the L-arabinose-inducible ara operon and is regulated by AraR and glucose, yet it is dispensable for L-arabinose utilization and no chemical role in arabinose catabolism has been demonstrated.

Proposed New Ontology Terms

sn-glycerol 1-phosphate biosynthetic process

Definition: The chemical reactions and pathways resulting in the formation of sn-glycerol 1-phosphate, the S-configured enantiomer of glycerol phosphate.

Justification: A stereospecific G1P biosynthesis process would describe precursor formation separately from downstream lipid synthesis. Free G1P is not itself a glycerophospholipid, so absence of the specific process term does not justify assigning lipid participation without evidence. This retained proposal is a terminology suggestion, not a new annotation or a claim of measured native flux.

Nickel-dependent, homodimeric reversible conversion between glycerone phosphate and sn-glycerol 1-phosphate, preferentially coupled to NAD(H). The enzyme also has lower-efficiency NADP(H) activity. Its in-vitro production of G1P is established, whereas net cellular direction and contribution to the native PcrB precursor pool remain unresolved.

Suggested Experiments

Experiment: Construct a clean markerless egsA deletion and a complemented derivative, and quantify heptaprenylglycerol and acetyl-heptaprenylglycerol, free glycerophosphate enantiomer pools and the backbone chirality of phosphatidylglycerol by chiral chromatography coupled to mass spectrometry. Loss of the S-backbone species and of the ether lipid in the deletion, with restoration on complementation, would establish the flux; their persistence would show the second G1P route operates in B. subtilis as well.

Hypothesis: EgsA is the source of the sn-glycerol 1-phosphate used by PcrB and of the glycerol-1-phosphate backbone found in B. subtilis phosphatidylglycerol.

Type: targeted deletion with chiral lipidomics

Experiment: Titrate egsA expression from an inducible promoter across a range spanning the native level, and measure free G1P, G1P-backbone phosphatidylglycerol and heptaprenylglycerol at each level. A monotonic response of the lipid pools to egsA dosage would identify the dehydrogenase as the controlling step; a flat response would place control downstream.

Hypothesis: Flux through EgsA, rather than the specificity of the downstream enzymes, sets how much glycerol-1-phosphate-backbone lipid the cell makes.

Type: expression titration with metabolite and lipid quantification

Experiment: Determine the full set of steady-state kinetic constants and the apparent equilibrium position for both directions at physiological pH and nickel occupancy, and measure the intracellular glycerone phosphate, G1P, NADH and NAD+ concentrations in growing cells, so that the thermodynamic driving force in vivo can be computed rather than inferred from KM values alone.

Hypothesis: The reductive direction observed in vitro is the direction the enzyme runs in the cell.

Type: enzyme kinetics combined with intracellular metabolite quantification

Experiment: Compare wild type, an egsA deletion and an araR mutant for growth, lipoprotein N-acetylation state and envelope stress sensitivity on L-arabinose and on plant arabinan-containing substrates versus glucose, and measure ether-lipid levels in each condition. A deletion phenotype confined to arabinose growth would give the enzyme a physiological setting; its absence would indicate the operon placement is not functionally informative.

Hypothesis: Arabinose induction of egsA is physiologically meaningful, and the archaeal-type ether lipid matters under a condition the operon reports.

Type: conditional phenotyping with lipid and lipoprotein analysis

Knowledge Gaps

What is not known — curated, literature-grounded statements of the open unknowns (the inverse of core functions).

Gap: Whether EgsA supplies the sn-glycerol 1-phosphate used natively by PcrB or in G1P-backbone phosphatidylglycerol remains unresolved in the evidence inspected. EgsA is a characterized candidate, but precursor synthesis in vitro does not establish those fluxes.

OPEN BIOLOGY BP_DARK

What is known: Recombinant AraM reduces glycerone phosphate to G1P and catalyzes the reverse reaction. B. subtilis contains G1P-derived lipids and PcrB uses G1P, but precursor identity alone does not establish enzyme-to-lipid flux. The author thesis reports an araM-disruption LTA ELISA and later G1P-fed lipid labeling; the latter bypasses the step in question. The 2026 PlsY/PlsC paper cites prior engineered E. coli lipid synthesis without AraM, suggesting an unidentified alternative G1P source in that host, not proving an alternative mechanism in B. subtilis.

Significance: This is the join between the archaeal-type backbone chemistry and the lipids that use it, and it is the point at which any process annotation on a bacterial G1P dehydrogenase stops being a statement about a measured reaction and becomes an inference from pathway logic. It also bears on whether G1P supply or acyltransferase specificity is what limits heterochiral lipid production in bacteria.

What would resolve it: Chiral lipidomics of an egsA deletion and of a complemented strain, measuring heptaprenylglycerol, its acetylated form and the backbone chirality of phosphatidylglycerol; isotopic tracing from glycerone phosphate into those pools; and a search for the second G1P route, for example by selecting for restored archaeal-lipid synthesis in an AraM-free host.

Gap: Why a glycerophosphate dehydrogenase that produces a candidate lipid precursor is transcribed as part of an L-arabinose catabolic operon, and under what physiological condition that coupling matters, is unknown. No metabolic connection between the enzyme's reaction and arabinose catabolism has been identified.

OPEN BIOLOGY BP_DARK

What is known: What is established is that egsA (araM) is the fifth cistron of the araABDLMNPQ-abfA operon, the operon is induced by L-arabinose and repressed by glucose through the repressor AraR, and araM is not required for L-arabinose utilisation. The enzyme's substrates are glycerone phosphate and NAD(H), neither of which is specific to pentose catabolism. What is not established is any condition under which arabinose-induced G1P production is advantageous, and whether the arrangement is functional at all rather than a relic of genome organisation.

Significance: Regulation is often the only clue to the physiological setting of an enzyme whose product has no obvious bulk role, and this enzyme's product is a minor lipid backbone and a carrier cofactor rather than a bulk membrane component. If arabinose-induced G1P synthesis is functional, it would point to the condition in which the archaeal-type lipid matters; if it is not, it is a caution against reading the operon's name into the gene's role, which the gene symbol araM has already encouraged.

What would resolve it: Measure heptaprenylglycerol, its acetylated form and glycerophosphate enantiomer pools in wild type, an egsA deletion and an araR mutant, with and without arabinose, on plant polysaccharide and on glucose; and test whether an egsA deletion has a growth, lipoprotein acetylation or envelope-stress phenotype specifically under arabinose induction.

  • Guldan 2008 propose a lipid role from the enzymology plus an araM knockout

  • Guldan 2011 PMID:21761520 show PcrB makes heptaprenylglyceryl phosphate

  • Rieche 2026 PMID:42286214 note that in engineered E. coli "other
