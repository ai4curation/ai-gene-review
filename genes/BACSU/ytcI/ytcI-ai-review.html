Gene Description

ytcI encodes an uncharacterised AMP-forming acyl-CoA synthetase (acyl-CoA ligase) of Bacillus subtilis. Its AMP-binding and AMP-binding C-terminal domains and the conserved AMP-binding motif place it in the medium-chain acyl-CoA synthetase (ACSM) family of the ANL adenylate-forming superfamily, which activates carboxylic acids to their CoA thioesters through an acyl-adenylate intermediate. It has no fatty acid synthase domains. YtcI is the only member of this family in B. subtilis and is 58% identical to Staphylococcus aureus MbcS, an acyl-CoA synthetase that activates 2-methylbutyrate and isobutyrate to the acyl-CoA primers of branched-chain fatty acid synthesis. That makes YtcI a candidate short-chain, branched-chain acyl-CoA synthetase, but its substrate and physiological role have not been determined.

Core Functions

ATP-dependent fatty acid ligase (acyl-CoA synthetase) activity, activating a carboxylic acid to its CoA thioester. The substrate is unknown; homology to S. aureus MbcS suggests short branched-chain acids such as 2-methylbutyrate and isobutyrate.

Suggested Questions for Experts

Q: Is YtcI the B. subtilis counterpart of S. aureus MbcS, responsible for incorporating exogenous 2-methylbutyrate and isobutyrate into branched-chain fatty acids?

Q: Should the PAINT IBD for GO:0004321 fatty-acyl-CoA synthase activity on PTHR43605 root node PTN001944787 be retracted, and should GO:0006633 be moved from the root to a node covering MbcS-like bacterial members if MbcS orthologs are confirmed?

Suggested Experiments

Experiment: Purify recombinant YtcI and assay ATP- and CoA-dependent activation of short straight- and branched-chain acids (acetate, propionate, butyrate, isobutyrate, 2-methylbutyrate, isovalerate). In parallel, test whether a ytcI deletion in a bkd-deficient B. subtilis strain loses the ability to grow on, and incorporate into membrane lipids, supplemented 2-methylbutyrate or isobutyrate.

Knowledge Gaps

Gap: The physiological substrate and role of YtcI are unknown. Homology to S. aureus MbcS predicts activation of 2-methylbutyrate and isobutyrate for branched-chain fatty acid priming, but this has not been tested in B. subtilis.

Key homology finding

  • S. aureus MbcS (SAUSA300_2542, A0A0H2XHR6) is in PTHR43605:SF10 and NF047394 too, and is 58%
    identical to YtcI (ytcI-bioinformatics/RESULTS.md). YtcI is the only PTHR43605 / NF047394 member
    in B. subtilis 168.
  • MbcS: PMID:36806679; PMID:36806679.
  • The MbcS paper notes that B. subtilis also incorporates such acids: PMID:36806679. This makes YtcI a candidate for that activation step in
    B. subtilis, untested.
  • Other SF10 relatives: M. sedula Msed_0406 (4-hydroxybutyrate-CoA ligase, broad C2-C5) and
    P. putida PP_2213 (uncharacterised). Both characterised relatives use short-chain (<C6) acids.

    oxidation PMID:11470804, so the node's basis does not support the term; but an independent line (MbcS
    orthology) makes a primer-supplying role in branched-chain fatty acid synthesis plausible for
    YtcI. Needs experiment. (PP_2213: MARK_AS_OVER_ANNOTATED, where no such line exists.)
  • GO:0006637 acyl-CoA metabolic process (IBA): ACCEPT.
  • GO:0015645 fatty acid ligase activity (IBA): ACCEPT; as precise as family evidence allows.
  • GO:0031956 medium-chain fatty acid-CoA ligase (ARBA IEA): MARK_AS_OVER_ANNOTATED; ARBA
    prediction, and the characterised relatives (MbcS, Msed_0406) act on short-chain acids.
  • No NEW annotations: short-chain/branched-chain activity is a homology hypothesis only.

homology inference only; no reciprocal genome-wide search or phylogeny was run, and YtcI itself
has not been assayed.
