# Spo0J reaction and phylogenetic scope check (2026-09-20)

The actual PTHR33375 tree places Bacillus subtilis P26497 at leaf PTN002121226, below chromosome/segregation IBD PTN002121184 and positive-sporulation IBD PTN002121186. Exact lineage, source rows, ancestral assertions and raw-response hash are in spo0J-P26497-paint-lineage.json. Target self-inclusion in source evidence is expected because Spo0J itself supplies experimental grounding.

PMID:31649139 directly assays purified B. subtilis ParB/Spo0J, rather than only a homolog: radiolabeled nucleotide binding and ITC establish CTP preference; R80A loses CTP binding, and phosphate production establishes parS-stimulated CTP hydrolysis. The C-terminal region also supports nonspecific DNA binding, while the central HTH recognizes parS. An ATP-binding-like GxxRxxA motif and structural similarity to sulfiredoxin did not predict the actual substrate correctly; the direct nucleotide assay is decisive. Soj/ParA and Smc are separate ATPases.

CTP binding supports clamp engagement, DNA loading and spreading. CTPγS supports loading too, so hydrolysis is dispensable for the loading steps assayed. The 2019 paper proposes unloading/recycling as a possible hydrolysis role; it does not demonstrate hydrolysis as the motor for DNA loading. This corrects the previous coupled-loading narrative and the Falcon report's generalization.

PMID:19450517 (full primary) establishes Spo0J/parS-dependent SMC recruitment and ectopic parS-dependent relocation of SMC, showing work by a loader rather than mere necessity. PMID:36044845 (full primary) combines Bacillus/Streptococcus chimeras with in vivo crosslinking to map direct ParB–Smc binding at the CTP-binding domain/Smc joint. The crosslink mapping uses engineered cysteine substitutions and ATPase-trapped/modified Smc constructs; these resolve interface proximity, not a new wild-type affinity measurement. PMID:19450516 explicitly distinguishes Spo0J regulation of initiation via Soj from its Smc recruitment role. The abstract-only PMID:18854156 explains how Soj-DnaA control can affect sporulation through the Sda checkpoint; the title naming Soj does not invalidate the Spo0J-related regulatory context.

PMID:8071208 directly links Spo0J loss to anucleate cells and shows that a soj deletion suppresses the sporulation defect. This supports an active positive regulatory contribution to sporulation, even though the route to Spo0A is indirect. PMID:9114058 establishes Spo0J's own nucleoid/origin-proximal location. That localization observation alone does not demonstrate a protein-localization process performed by Spo0J. Later SMC recruitment does demonstrate loader work, but the precise GO:0071459 centromeric-region scope for bacterial parS warrants ontology review before retaining that old authored proposal.

The old authored centromeric-DNA-binding proposal was withdrawn as redundant with an existing DNA-binding assertion; the GO definition's mitotic/meiosis spindle wording is an additional scope question, not evidence that Spo0J cannot bind parS. Core uses sequence-specific DNA binding and keeps the parS mechanism explicit. The old CTPase, CTP-binding and SMC-binding proposals are retained with direct target primary references and experimental evidence codes; their earlier authored IBA/GO_REF metadata was not an actual inherited GOA source assertion. All ten genuine source rows are unchanged outside their review blocks.
