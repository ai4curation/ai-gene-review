Gene Description

PcrB is the heptaprenylglyceryl phosphate (HepGP) synthase of Bacillus subtilis, a homodimeric Mg2+-dependent TIM-barrel prenyltransferase of the GGGPS family (group I subfamily). It transfers the C35 heptaprenyl moiety of all-trans-heptaprenyl diphosphate to the C3 hydroxyl of sn-glycerol 1-phosphate, forming an ether bond and releasing diphosphate. This chemistry resembles archaeal ether-lipid synthesis despite being experimentally established in bacteria. The acceptor is the sn-1 glycerophosphate enantiomer used by archaeal membrane lipids, and the product carries an isoprenoid chain in ether rather than ester linkage. Published acceptor assays support sn-glycerol 1-phosphate specificity, while shorter C10 and C20 prenyl donors are accepted in vitro with much lower efficiency, and dimerization is what allows the long native donor to be bound. Unlike the archaeal pathway, the Bacillales product is not elaborated into a bulk membrane lipid by addition of a second prenyl chain. HepGP is instead dephosphorylated by a non-specific phosphatase and O-acetylated by YvoF, giving acetylated heptaprenylglycerol (Ac-HepG), a low-abundance lipophilic acetyl carrier. Ac-HepG supplies acetyl groups at the extracytoplasmic face of the membrane through an unresolved translocation route, where LhaT (YpjA) transfers them to the N-terminal cysteine of mature diacylglyceryl lipoproteins, and loss of pcrB abolishes that lipoprotein N-acetylation. PcrB is therefore the committed, ether-bond-forming step of a prenyl-lipid acetyl-carrier pathway, and has a broader distribution than LhaT; the lipoprotein-acetylation role cannot therefore be assumed for every PcrB homolog.

Core Functions

Forms the committed ether linkage of the Bacillales prenyl-glycerol pathway, transferring the C35 heptaprenyl group of all-trans-heptaprenyl diphosphate to the C3 hydroxyl of sn-glycerol 1-phosphate to give 3-heptaprenyl-sn-glycero-1-phosphate and diphosphate. Published assays establish the sn-glycerol 1-phosphate acceptor, the reaction requires Mg2+, and the homodimer is what permits the long native donor to be bound. This is the only step of the pathway that creates the ether bond, and the lipid it makes is the precursor of heptaprenylglycerol and its acetylated form, the lipophilic acetyl carrier that supplies lipoprotein N-acetylation; the acetyl group itself comes from acetyl-CoA via YvoF, and the transfer onto protein is carried out by LhaT, not by PcrB.

Suggested Questions for Experts

Q: What is the appropriate process boundary for enzymes that synthesize carrier backbones subsequently used in protein modification? PcrB directly synthesizes the lipid precursor; YvoF loads the acetyl group and LhaT transfers it to protein. No additional lipoprotein-process annotation is proposed from the loss phenotype alone. Carrier recycling in vivo also remains unresolved.

Suggested experts: GO Consortium biocurators, GO biological process ontology editors

Q: Should the ether-lipid biosynthesis ontology or guidance explicitly discuss isoprenoid ethers on sn-glycerol 1-phosphate? The current definition accommodates the chemistry, but sparse prokaryotic annotation patterns merit checking without assuming they represent either deliberate exclusion or curator oversight.

Suggested experts: GO biological process ontology editors, archaeal and bacterial lipid biochemists

Q: Does GO want a way to describe a lipophilic group-carrier cycle, in which a lipid is built, charged, discharged and reused? The heptaprenylglycerol pathway makes a carrier rather than a membrane component, and nothing in the lipid biosynthesis branch distinguishes the two, which is why the purpose of this pathway is invisible in the gene's annotations.

Suggested experts: GO Consortium biocurators, lipid metabolism curators

Q: Is the in vitro use of C10 and C20 prenyl donors by PcrB of any physiological consequence in Bacillales, or purely substrate range for an enzyme built around the C35 donor? The 2025 reconstitution used the shorter geranylgeranyl donor for convenience, so the shorter products are known to be usable by the downstream enzymes.

Suggested experts: Bacterial prenyltransferase biochemists

Suggested Experiments

Experiment: Delete or conditionally deplete araM and measure heptaprenylglycerol, acetylated heptaprenylglycerol and acetylated lipoprotein by mass spectrometry, alongside chiral analysis of the glycerol-phosphate pool. Loss of the lipid would establish the flux from EgsA to PcrB; its persistence would show that a second, unidentified route to the sn-1 enantiomer operates in B. subtilis.

Hypothesis: EgsA (AraM) is the physiological source of the sn-glycerol 1-phosphate that PcrB uses.

Type: targeted deletion with lipidomics and chiral metabolite analysis

Experiment: Feed cells or proteoliposomes isotopically labelled heptaprenylglycerol and follow the label through the acetylated and free forms while lipoprotein acetylation proceeds, then compare the number of acetyl groups transferred per molecule of carrier. A ratio well above one would establish a catalytic cycle and place a regeneration step in the pathway.

Hypothesis: The ether lipid is a genuine carrier that is regenerated and reused, rather than a lipid consumed once per acetyl group delivered.

Type: isotope tracing with in vitro reconstitution

Experiment: In a Bacillus species that encodes PcrB but a pseudogenized LhaT, compare wild type with pcrB and yvoF deletions by untargeted lipidomics and by acetyl-proteomics of the extracytoplasmic and membrane fractions, and test the deletions for membrane-stress phenotypes. Any acetylated species or lipid lost with the carrier would identify the alternative use.

Hypothesis: PcrB is retained in strains lacking a functional LhaT because its product has another acceptor or another membrane role.

Type: comparative lipidomics and acetyl-proteomics in a non-model Bacillus

Experiment: Compare lipoprotein N-acetylation efficiency and carrier partitioning in reconstituted membranes using heptaprenyl (C35) and geranylgeranyl (C20) versions of the carrier, since the shorter form is known to be accepted by PcrB, YvoF and LhaT. A strong chain-length dependence in the transfer or flipping step would show that the C35 preference of PcrB is selected by the carrier cycle rather than by catalysis alone.

Hypothesis: The chain length of the prenyl donor matters for the carrier function, not just for the enzyme active site.

Type: in vitro reconstitution with defined carrier chain lengths

What is known: What is established is that PcrB and YvoF build Ac-HepG on the cytoplasmic side from cytoplasmic substrates, that LhaT is an integral membrane protein acting on an acceptor on the outer leaflet, and that the whole pathway can be reconstituted in vitro from four purified activities with no transporter. What is unknown is whether a dedicated transporter exists, whether spontaneous flipping of the neutral lipid suffices, and whether HepG is recycled rather than discarded after the acetyl group is handed over.

Significance: Whether the pathway is a true carrier cycle or a one-way lipid flux decides how the PcrB reaction should be modelled, and in particular whether the product is consumed stoichiometrically or regenerated. It also determines whether a transport step belongs in the pathway at all.

What would resolve it: Identify the translocation step, by screening for mutants that accumulate cytoplasmic Ac-HepG while losing acetylated lipoprotein, and by measuring flipping of Ac-HepG and HepG in proteoliposomes with and without candidate transporters; then test recycling with isotopically labelled HepG.

Gap: Whether the sn-glycerol 1-phosphate that PcrB uses in the living cell is supplied by the glycerol-1-phosphate dehydrogenase EgsA (AraM) is not established, and whether any other route to that enantiomer operates in B. subtilis is unknown.

2. GO:0008611 ether lipid biosynthetic process: USED, but as an added row, not a replacement

Definition (OLS, 2026-11-06): "lipids that contain (normally) one lipid alcohol
in ether linkage to one of the carbon atoms (normally C-1) of glycerol."

The C-3 objection dissolves on inspection. In HepGP the ether is on the
glycerol carbon distal to the phosphate. That is the same physical position
as C-1 in the mirror-image sn-glycerol-3-phosphate numbering used for
plasmanyl/plasmenyl lipids; the number differs only because sn numbering is
fixed by backbone stereochemistry. The two hedged "normally" clauses cover
exactly this. UniProt independently calls the reaction an ether-bond-formation
step, and the family survey states the family forms "an ether bond between
glycerol-1-phosphate and polyprenyl diphosphates" PMID:24684232. No new term
is needed for ether-bond formation as such.

But the planned MODIFY was wrong on a different ground, which the plan had not
checked: GO:0008611 is not a descendant of GO:0046474. Its parents are
GO:0008610 lipid biosynthetic process, GO:0046485 ether lipid metabolic
process and GO:0046504 glycerol ether biosynthetic process (OLS). Replacing
GO:0046474 with it would therefore discard a true claim (HepGP is a
glycerophospholipid; UniProt's PATHWAY line says so) rather than refine it.
