# YpjA / LhaT evidence and curation notes

## 2026-11-07 primary review

The original symbol and folder remain `ypjA`; PMID:40626731 identifies this protein as lipoprotein heptaprenylglyceryl N-acetyltransferase, LhaT. Figure5 explicitly identifies B. subtilis168 and the locus corresponding to BSU22530. Native deletion and complementation show loss and restoration of the N-acetylated lipoprotein chemotype. The functional assignment does not rest on the still-uncharacterized UniProt name or on family presence.

Reconstitution used recombinant B. subtilis YpjA in E. coli membranes lacking endogenous Lnt, a synthetic fluorescent diacyl lipopeptide, and an acetylated C20 geranylgeranyl donor mixture generated by PcrB/phosphatase/YvoF. TLC and mass spectrometry detect an acetylated product (+42Da). The C35 native carrier, donor regioisomer preference, and spontaneous versus protein-mediated membrane flipping are not separately demonstrated by that assay. [PMID:40626731]

GO:0008080 N-acetyltransferase is donor-agnostic and accurately states the chemistry. GO:0004596 is excluded because its official reaction explicitly consumes acetyl-CoA; LhaT uses an acetylated lipid instead. Acetyl-CoA is present upstream in the coupled preparation for YvoF, which does not make it LhaT's direct donor. One NEW molecular-function annotation supplies the experimentally established activity missing from the location-only seed. No redundant generic acetyltransferase row or NEW process row is added.

Plasma membrane is ACCEPT. Native UniProt curation, predicted helices, membrane recovery of recombinant protein and active membrane preparations converge. UniProt predicts three segments whereas the paper's DeepTMHMM prediction gives six; neither is a direct topology experiment. The extracytoplasmic reaction face follows exposure of the Lsp-cleaved lipoprotein amino terminus and the pathway model. No flippase activity is assigned to LhaT.

The function must not be propagated across all DUF1405/PTHR40042 proteins. B. anthracis Sterne wild-type LhaT was inactive; replacing its poly-Tyr region by the B. subtilis YFL sequence restored activity, whereas shortening to one Tyr did not. The paper's machine-readable figure alternative text oversimplifies this result; the detailed Results explicitly distinguish these constructs. Functional Bacillus subtilis evidence does not imply universal family activity or that every other Bacillus loss uses the same mutation. [PMID:40626731]

No TLR2 signaling annotation is proposed for LhaT itself. Modified lipoproteins are the ligands; LhaT performs bacterial protein modification. The new MF is direct biochemical evidence, not a response-to-reporter inference.
