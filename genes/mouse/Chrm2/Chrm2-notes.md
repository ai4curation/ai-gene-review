## GO:0005886 plasma membrane — ACCEPT
M2 is a functional cell-surface muscarinic receptor, with receptor-mediated acetylcholine responses and membrane receptor localization. Native cardiac and brain knockout experiments independently support the membrane signaling role.
Sources: PMID:9990086, PMID:11880500, PMID:20884879

## GO:0006940 regulation of smooth muscle contraction — KEEP_AS_NON_CORE
M2 contributes to muscarinic control of smooth-muscle contraction as a Gi/o-coupled receptor; the effect is context-dependent and need not be the primary contractile M3 pathway. Retain the conserved regulatory role rather than describe M2 as a contractile structural protein. Native tissue specificity must be distinguished from a universal receptor hierarchy.
Sources: PMID:9990086

## GO:0007186 G protein-coupled receptor signaling pathway — ACCEPT
M2 directly receives cholinergic signals and activates G proteins. Native knockout evidence establishes cardiac and brain functions, with hippocampal/cortical autoreceptor use distinct from the predominantly M4 striatal implementation.
Sources: PMID:9990086, PMID:11880500

## GO:0007197 adenylate cyclase-inhibiting G protein-coupled acetylcholine receptor signaling pathway — ACCEPT
M2 couples to inhibitory Gi/o signaling and participates in the adenylyl-cyclase-inhibiting muscarinic pathway. Cardiac GIRK regulation is a parallel G protein output, not evidence that all M2 effects require direct G alpha inhibition of one adenylyl cyclase isoform.
Sources: PMID:9990086, PMID:20884879

## GO:0007268 chemical synaptic transmission — ACCEPT
Native M2 regulates acetylcholine release as a presynaptic autoreceptor in hippocampus and cerebral cortex. This is direct receptor participation in synaptic transmission, with region-specific division of labor from M4.
Sources: PMID:11880500

## GO:0008016 regulation of heart contraction — KEEP_AS_NON_CORE
M2 receptor deletion alters cholinergic control of cardiac responses; Rgs6/Gnb5 experiments further establish the regulated M2–GIRK branch in pacemaker cells. Retain the organ-level physiological role, distinct from the receptor molecular activity.
Sources: PMID:9990086, PMID:20884879, PMID:24318880

## GO:0098978 glutamatergic synapse — KEEP_AS_NON_CORE
The rat source establishes M2-positive asymmetric contacts, while PMID:8389473 explicitly places primate M2 at pre- and postsynaptic compartments of asymmetric pathways using excitatory amino acids. Together these support the conserved glutamatergic-synapse assignment as an anatomical and orthology inference, not direct glutamate colabeling of mouse M2 in the present sources. A muscarinic receptor can modulate a glutamatergic synapse without becoming a glutamate receptor; not every asymmetric synapse is being equated with a chemically verified glutamatergic contact.
Sources: PMID:8389473

Sources: PMID:11880500
