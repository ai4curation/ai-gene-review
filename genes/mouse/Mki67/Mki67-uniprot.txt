CC   -!- FUNCTION: Protein that associates with the surface of mitotic
CC       chromosomes and acts both as a chromosome repellent during early
CC       mitosis and chromosome attractant during late mitosis
CC       (PubMed:27362226). Required to maintain individual mitotic chromosomes
CC       dispersed in the cytoplasm following nuclear envelope disassembly
CC       (PubMed:27362226). During early mitosis, relocalizes from nucleoli to
CC       the chromosome surface where it forms extended brush structures that
CC       cover a substantial fraction of the chromosome surface
CC       (PubMed:27362226). The MKI67 brush structure prevents chromosomes from
CC       collapsing into a single chromatin mass by forming a steric and
CC       electrostatic charge barrier: the protein has a high net electrical
CC       charge and acts as a surfactant, dispersing chromosomes and enabling
CC       independent chromosome motility (PubMed:27362226). During mitotic
CC       anaphase, the MKI67 brush structure collapses and MKI67 switches from a
CC       chromosome repellent to a chromosome attractant to promote chromosome
CC       clustering and facilitate the exclusion of large cytoplasmic particles
CC       from the future nuclear space (By similarity). Mechanistically,
CC       dephosphorylation during mitotic exit and simultaneous exposure of a
CC       conserved basic patch induce the RNA-dependent formation of a liquid-
CC       like condensed phase on the chromosome surface, promoting coalescence
CC       of neighboring chromosome surfaces and clustering of chromosomes (By
CC       similarity). Binds premature ribosomal RNAs during anaphase; promoting
CC       liquid-liquid phase separation. Binds DNA, with a preference for
CC       supercoiled DNA and AT-rich DNA (By similarity). Does not contribute to
CC       the internal structure of mitotic chromosomes (PubMed:26949251). May
CC       play a role in chromatin organization; it is however unclear whether it
CC       plays a direct role in chromatin organization or whether it is an
CC       indirect consequence of its function in mitotic chromosome
CC       (PubMed:26949251). {ECO:0000250|UniProtKB:P46013,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:26949251, ECO:0000269|PubMed:27362226}.
CC   -!- SUBUNIT: Interacts with KIF15. Interacts (via the FHA domain) with
CC       NIFK. Interacts with PPP1CC. Component of a complex at least composed
CC       of ZNF335, HCFC1, CCAR2, EMSY, MKI67, RBBP5, ASH2L and WDR5; the
CC       complex is formed as a result of interactions between components of a
CC       nuclear receptor-mediated transcription complex and a histone
CC       methylation complex. Interacts with ZNF335.
CC       {ECO:0000250|UniProtKB:P46013}.
CC   -!- SUBCELLULAR LOCATION: Chromosome {ECO:0000269|PubMed:12355204,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:8834799}. Nucleus {ECO:0000269|PubMed:12355204,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:8834799}. Nucleus, nucleolus
CC       {ECO:0000269|PubMed:12355204, ECO:0000269|PubMed:8834799}. Note=During
CC       early mitosis, relocalizes from nucleoli to the surface of the mitotic
CC       chromosome, the perichromosomal layer, and covers a substantial
CC       fraction of the mitotic chromosome surface (PubMed:12355204,
CC       PubMed:8834799). Associates with satellite DNA in G1 phase (By
CC       similarity). Binds tightly to chromatin in interphase, chromatin-
CC       binding decreases in mitosis when it associates with the surface of the
CC       condensed chromosomes (By similarity). Predominantly localized in the
CC       G1 phase in the perinucleolar region, in the later phases it is also
CC       detected throughout the nuclear interior, being predominantly localized
CC       in the nuclear matrix (By similarity). {ECO:0000250|UniProtKB:P46013,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:12355204, ECO:0000269|PubMed:8834799}.

CC   -!- DEVELOPMENTAL STAGE: Accumulates during the late G1 stage in the
CC       nucleus and maximum expression is found during G2 phase and mitosis
CC       (PubMed:8834799). During male meiosis, present in nuclei of all stages
CC       from the spermatogonium through spermatocytes I and II up to the
CC       earliest spermatid stage (early round spermatids) and then fades out
CC       (PubMed:12355204). Not detected in later spermatid stages or sperm
CC       (PubMed:12355204). During female meiosis, present in prophase I oocytes
CC       of fetal ovaries, while it is absent in resting oocytes. Reappears in
CC       oocytes of growing follicles and is continuously present up to
CC       metaphase II (at protein level) (PubMed:12355204).
CC       {ECO:0000269|PubMed:12355204, ECO:0000269|PubMed:8834799}.
CC   -!- DOMAIN: The positively charged patch (CP) region mediates liquid-liquid
CC       phase separation. {ECO:0000250|UniProtKB:P46013}.
CC   -!- PTM: Hyperphosphorylated by CDK1 in mitosis; hyperphosphorylatiom
CC       prevents undergoing liquid-liquid phase separation (By similarity).
CC       Dephosphorylated by PPP1CC at the onset of anaphase (By similarity).
CC       Dephosphorylation by protein phosphatase 2A (PP2A) and simultaneous
CC       exposure of the positively charged patch (CP) during mitotic exit
CC       induce the RNA-dependent formation of a liquid-like condensed phase on
CC       the chromosome surface (PubMed:39003739).
CC       {ECO:0000250|UniProtKB:P46013, ECO:0000269|PubMed:39003739}.
CC   -!- PTM: Ubiquitinated by the APC/C complex after neuronal progenitors exit
CC       mitosis during brain development, leading to clearance from
CC       constitutive heterochromatin. {ECO:0000250|UniProtKB:P46013}.
CC   -!- DISRUPTION PHENOTYPE: No visible phenotype (PubMed:26949251). Mice were
CC       born at the expected Mendelian ratio and show no overt phenotype
CC       (PubMed:26949251). Cells do not show proliferation defects, but
CC       chromatin organization is impaired, with defects in heterochromatin
CC       compaction and long-range genomic interactions (PubMed:26949251).
CC       {ECO:0000269|PubMed:26949251}.
CC   -!- CAUTION: Was thought to play a key role in cell proliferation, and is
CC       commonly used as a marker of cell proliferation. However, its primary
CC       function is uncoupled from cell proliferation (PubMed:26949251).
CC       Required to maintain mitotic chromosomes dispersed by forming a steric
CC       and electrostatic charge barrier (PubMed:27362226).
CC       {ECO:0000269|PubMed:26949251, ECO:0000269|PubMed:27362226}.
