Gene Description

Nkx2-5 (Csx) is an NK-2 class homeodomain transcription factor, the vertebrate ortholog of Drosophila tinman, and a central regulator of heart development. It binds NK2 elements in RNA polymerase II promoters and enhancers as a monomer or homodimer and activates cardiac genes such as Nppa, cardiac alpha-actin, myocardin, Mef2c and Id2 in combination with partner transcription factors (GATA4, TBX5, TBX20, SRF, MEF2C, HAND2, FOXH1) and coactivators; it also represses targets in a context-dependent manner, for example Nppa in the atrioventricular canal with TBX2, the pacemaker genes Hcn4 and Tbx3 at the atrial/sinoatrial border, and Bmp2/Smad1 signalling in second heart field progenitors. Loss of Nkx2-5 in mice arrests heart looping at the linear tube stage with loss of ventricular markers such as Mlc2v and embryonic death around E9.5-10. Nkx2-5 controls cardiomyocyte differentiation and proliferation, ventricle formation, trabeculation, outflow tract development through the second heart field, and dose-dependent development and postnatal maintenance of the cardiac conduction system (atrioventricular node, His-Purkinje system). Outside the heart it is required for spleen development (with Pbx1, repressing p15Ink4b) and contributes to pharyngeal endoderm and thyroid bud development. Human NKX2-5 mutations cause congenital heart disease with atrioventricular conduction block and, rarely, asplenia.
