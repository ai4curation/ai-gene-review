| Property | Mouse RBPJ summary | Functional significance | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Protein identity | RBPJ, also called RBP-Jκ or CSL, is the mammalian Su(H)-family DNA-binding effector of canonical Notch signaling. This identity matches mouse **Rbpj** rather than the pancreas-enriched paralog **Rbpjl**. | Confirms the target as mouse RBPJ, UniProt P31266. | (pqac-00000000, pqac-00000016) |
| Primary molecular function | Sequence-specific DNA-binding transcriptional regulator and protein-interaction scaffold. It is not an enzyme and catalyzes no chemical reaction. | Switches Notch target loci between transcriptional repression and activation according to the cofactors assembled on RBPJ. | (pqac-00000000, pqac-00000013, pqac-00000014) |
| DNA-binding specificity | Recognizes canonical CSL response elements centered on **5′-GTGGGAA-3′**; **5′-CGTGGGAA-3′** is a representative extended motif. Its functional substrate is regulatory DNA rather than a small molecule. | Positions RBPJ at Notch-responsive promoters and enhancers. | (pqac-00000000, pqac-00000002, pqac-00000017) |
| Domain architecture | Contains an N-terminal domain or Rel-homology domain, a central β-trefoil domain, and a C-terminal domain or Rel-homology domain. A β-strand extending across the domains helps organize the compact fold. | Integrates DNA recognition with recruitment of activating or repressive partners. | (pqac-00000026, pqac-00000029) |
| DNA-binding surfaces | The N-terminal and β-trefoil domains form the principal DNA-recognition surfaces. Conserved Arg218 is critical; R218H disrupts canonical-site binding and reduces long-lived nuclear binding. | Shows that DNA occupancy is essential and functionally separable from cofactor recruitment. | (pqac-00000026, pqac-00000027, pqac-00000033) |
| β-trefoil interaction hub | A hydrophobic groove in the β-trefoil domain binds the NICD RAM region and RAM-like motifs in FHL1 or KyoT2, RITA1, and L3MBTL3. Structural analysis shows L3MBTL3 bound across this surface in an RBPJ–L3MBTL3–DNA complex. | Competition at this shared surface facilitates exchange between repressors and NICD. | (pqac-00000026, pqac-00000030, pqac-00000031) |
| Notch-OFF cofactors | Without nuclear NICD, RBPJ associates with corepressors including SHARP or SPEN, FHL1 or KyoT2, and L3MBTL3. Associated machinery includes NCoR–HDAC complexes and KDM1A or LSD1. | Maintains or poises Notch-responsive chromatin in a transcriptionally repressed state. | (pqac-00000013, pqac-00000047, pqac-00000053) |
| Notch-ON cofactors | Notch cleavage releases NICD, whose RAM region binds the RBPJ β-trefoil domain while its ankyrin repeats contact RBPJ. MAML1–3 stabilizes the DNA-bound RBPJ–NICD–MAML complex. | Converts RBPJ into the canonical Notch transcription-activation platform. | (pqac-00000014, pqac-00000018, pqac-00000047) |
| Downstream coactivators | The active complex recruits Mediator and chromatin-activating factors including p300 or CBP, PCAF, GCN5, and EP400 or TIP60-associated activity. | Promotes histone acetylation, enhancer activation, and target-gene transcription. | (pqac-00000014, pqac-00000048, pqac-00000049) |
| Primary localization | RBPJ performs its principal function in the nucleus on regulatory DNA. Live-cell tracking measured a longest nuclear binding time of approximately 910 seconds for wild-type RBPJ versus 194 seconds for DNA-binding-defective R218H. | Links long nuclear residence events to productive DNA binding. | (pqac-00000008, pqac-00000010) |
| Localization dynamics | RBPJ is capable of regulated nucleocytoplasmic shuttling. Murine RBPJ followed cofactor-dependent transport in a genetic fly model, while RITA can promote nuclear export in mammalian contexts. | Nuclear availability may provide an additional level of Notch-pathway regulation. | (pqac-00000009, pqac-00000012, pqac-00000018) |
| Representative target genes | Canonical targets include **Hes** and **Hey** family genes, **Myc**, **Dtx1 or Deltex**, and **Nrarp**. Dtx1 and Nrarp also contribute to pathway feedback. | Translates Notch input into programs controlling cell fate, proliferation, and differentiation. | (pqac-00000042, pqac-00000043, pqac-00000045) |
| Biological interpretation | RBPJ is the terminal nuclear hub of canonical Notch signaling and contributes to cell-fate choice, differentiation, stem-cell maintenance, embryonic development, and adult tissue homeostasis. | Its pleiotropic phenotypes arise from a precise core activity: DNA-bound, cofactor-dependent transcriptional switching. | (pqac-00000021, pqac-00000023, pqac-00000024) |
