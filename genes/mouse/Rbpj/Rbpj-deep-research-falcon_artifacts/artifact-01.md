| Whole embryo | Germline disruption/null allele | Early embryonic lethality with severe growth and developmental defects, establishing that Rbpj is essential for embryogenesis. | Loss of the principal nuclear effector of canonical Notch signaling prevents normal developmental transcriptional programs. | (pqac-00000036, pqac-00000041) |
| Vascular endothelium and cardiovascular development | Endothelial expression of the Adams–Oliver syndrome-associated **E89G** allele | Increased embryonic lethality and cardiovascular abnormalities; endothelial expression was both necessary and sufficient for the major disease phenotypes. | E89G impairs DNA binding while retaining cofactor binding, supporting dominant-negative cofactor sequestration and reduced endothelial Notch1 output rather than simple haploinsufficiency. | (pqac-00000024, pqac-00000035, pqac-00000038) |
| Notch1-sensitized embryo | **Rbpj E89G/+** combined with **Notch1** heterozygosity | Severe under-representation, embryonic lethality, failure to thrive, cardiovascular defects, and occasional scalp or skin abnormalities. Equivalent compound heterozygous null alleles were substantially better tolerated. | Demonstrates a strong, allele-specific genetic interaction with Notch1 and supports dominant-negative activity of E89G. | (pqac-00000034, pqac-00000035, pqac-00000038) |
| Whole embryo—severe disease-associated allele | Homozygous **Rbpj E89G** | Embryos were markedly smaller and displayed hemorrhage, pallor, pericardial edema, incomplete axial rotation, and embryonic lethality. | Severe impairment of RBPJ–DNA binding disrupts developmental Notch transcription, although the phenotype is somewhat less severe than complete Rbpj loss. | (pqac-00000035, pqac-00000036, pqac-00000039) |
| Whole animal—mild disease-associated allele | Homozygous **Rbpj S358R** | Mice were generally viable without major gross abnormalities; homozygotes were initially smaller but achieved normal size by about five weeks. | S358R behaves as a weak hypomorphic allele whose residual activity is sufficient in an otherwise unsensitized background. | (pqac-00000035, pqac-00000039) |
| Whole animal—reduced Rbpj dosage | **Rbpj S358R/null** | Significantly reduced viability and marked growth impairment among survivors. | Pairing the weak S358R allele with a null allele exposes its partial loss of function. | (pqac-00000036, pqac-00000039) |
| Myeloid lineage | Conditional deletion in Lyz2-expressing cells | Accumulation of circulating **Ly6CloCCR2hi** monocytes and expansion of Ly6Clo monocytes and CD16.2-positive interstitial macrophages in lung; additional Ccr2 deletion genetically corrected the phenotype. | RBPJ cell-autonomously constrains nonclassical-monocyte homeostasis and tissue distribution, at least partly through CCR2 regulation. | (pqac-00000022) |
| Myeloid lineage—cell kinetics | Conditional deletion in Lyz2-expressing cells | No major change in monocyte apoptosis, proliferation, labeling kinetics, or Ly6Chi-to-Ly6Clo conversion under the tested conditions. | The accumulation phenotype is unlikely to result simply from increased proliferation, reduced death, or accelerated subset conversion. | (pqac-00000025, pqac-00000040) |
| Embryonic ectoderm | Conditional ectodermal Rbpj deletion beginning around E8.5 | Neonatal death, complete secondary-palate cleft, extensive oral epithelial adhesions, disrupted periderm, thin epidermis, malformed cyst-like vibrissa follicles, absent visible whiskers, and ectopic mineralization at vibrissa sites. | Canonical RBPJ-dependent Notch signaling promotes periderm and epidermal cell fate while preventing inappropriate adhesion and mineralization programs. | (pqac-00000037) |


*Table: Mouse genetic evidence shows that Rbpj is essential for embryogenesis and has tissue-specific functions in vascular, myeloid, and ectodermal biology. Disease-associated missense alleles can behave as hypomorphic or dominant-negative variants rather than simple nulls.*
