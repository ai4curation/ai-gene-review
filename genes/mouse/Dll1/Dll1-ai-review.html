Gene Description

Dll1 (Delta-like 1) is a type I transmembrane DSL-family ligand of Notch receptors, with an N-terminal MNNL domain, a DSL domain and eight EGF-like repeats in its ectodomain and a cytoplasmic tail ending in a PDZ-binding motif. Presented at the plasma membrane of signal-sending cells, Dll1 binds Notch1 and Notch2 in trans on neighbouring cells; its ubiquitination by the E3 ligases Mind bomb 1 and Neuralized drives ligand endocytosis and recycling and generates the pulling force that exposes Notch to S2 cleavage, thereby activating canonical Notch-RBP-J signalling. Fringe glycosylation of Notch enhances Dll1 binding. Dll1 itself undergoes ADAM10 shedding and gamma-secretase cleavage, and is recruited to adherens junctions by the PDZ scaffold MAGI1. As the principal activating Delta ligand in many mouse tissues, Dll1 mediates lateral inhibition in neurogenesis, inner-ear hair cell patterning and intestinal secretory lineage selection, and is required for somitogenesis and somite polarity, left-right asymmetry, arterial endothelial identity, marginal zone B cell development and adult neural and muscle stem cell maintenance.

Delta-like protein 1 (Dll1), encoded by the Dll1 gene (UniProt accession Q61483), is a canonical transmembrane Notch ligand in Mus musculus (mouse). The protein is also known as Drosophila Delta homolog 1 or Delta1, confirming alignment with the specified UniProt entry. Dll1 belongs to the Delta/Serrate/LAG-2 (DSL) family of Notch ligands and contains the defining DSL domain along with eight epidermal growth factor (EGF)-like repeats in its extracellular region (tveriakhina2018theectodomainsdetermine pages 2-5, wang2011notchsignalingand pages 3-5, ladi2005thedivergentdsl pages 6-7).

Dll1 is synthesized as a type I single-pass transmembrane protein comprising an N-terminal extracellular region, a transmembrane helix, and a short cytoplasmic tail (tveriakhina2018theectodomainsdetermine pages 1-2, wang2011notchsignalingand pages 3-5). The extracellular domain begins with an N-terminal C2 domain (also termed the MNNL domain), which participates in receptor recognition and contacts Notch EGF repeat 12 (tveriakhina2018theectodomainsdetermine pages 2-5, handford2018structuralinsightsinto pages 6-9). Unlike Jagged-family ligands, the Delta-family C2 domains do not bind calcium (handford2018structuralinsightsinto pages 6-9, cordle2008aconservedface pages 17-21). Immediately following the C2/MNNL region is the DSL domain, the defining structural signature of Notch ligands, which contacts Notch EGF repeat 11 and provides the principal binding interface (tveriakhina2018theectodomainsdetermine pages 1-2, cordle2008aconservedface pages 17-21). Within the DSL domain, conserved tyrosine (YY) and GWxG motifs are critical for structural integrity: disrupting the GWxG motif abolishes Notch transactivation, while tyrosine mutations severely impair both activation and cell-surface delivery (geffers2008biochemicalnonequivalenceof pages 87-90).

The eight EGF-like repeats form an extended, nearly linear extracellular scaffold (handford2018structuralinsightsinto pages 6-9). The first two EGF repeats, together with the DSL domain, constitute the minimal functional unit for Notch activation; their correct sequence and spacing are essential, as disruption abolishes signaling (hirano2020deltalike1and pages 8-9, geffers2007divergentfunctionsand pages 4-5). Dll1 also contains a Delta-and-OSM-11-like (DOS) motif within the EGF1–2 region, which is present in Dll1 but absent from Dll4, and contributes to enhanced ligand activity (hirano2020deltalike1and pages 1-2, hirano2020deltalike1and pages 8-9). Domain-swap experiments demonstrate that the N-terminal C2/MNNL–DSL–EGF1–3 region largely determines ligand-specific signaling behavior: Dll1 activates NOTCH1 and NOTCH2 approximately equally, whereas Dll4 preferentially activates NOTCH1 (tveriakhina2018theectodomainsdetermine pages 1-2, tveriakhina2018theectodomainsdetermine pages 2-5).

The short intracellular domain contains numerous lysine residues (17 in mouse Dll1) that serve as ubiquitination sites and a conserved C-terminal PDZ-binding motif (geffers2008biochemicalnonequivalenceof pages 90-93, ladi2005thedivergentdsl pages 6-7). Ubiquitination by E3 ligases such as Mindbomb and Neuralized promotes ligand endocytosis, which is mechanistically required for productive Notch activation (geffers2008biochemicalnonequivalenceof pages 90-93, geffers2008biochemicalnonequivalenceof pages 14-17).

Primary Molecular Function and Mechanism of Action

Dll1 functions as a membrane-bound Notch ligand that activates Notch receptors on adjacent cells through contact-dependent, trans-cellular signaling (hirano2020deltalike1and pages 1-2, tveriakhina2018theectodomainsdetermine pages 1-2). The molecular mechanism proceeds through several coordinated steps:

Receptor Binding and Engagement

Dll1 on the signal-sending cell binds Notch receptors on a neighboring signal-receiving cell via its extracellular DSL and EGF-repeat regions (tveriakhina2018theectodomainsdetermine pages 1-2, wang2011notchsignalingand pages 3-5). The MNNL/C2 and DSL domains provide high-affinity contacts with Notch EGF repeats 12 and 11, respectively, while adjacent EGF repeats contribute to productive interaction (tveriakhina2018theectodomainsdetermine pages 2-5). This trans-interaction occurs in an antiparallel orientation that positions the two membranes in close apposition (vazquezulloa2022reversibleandbidirectional pages 7-8).

Endocytosis-Dependent Mechanical Activation

Following ligand–receptor binding, ubiquitination of the Dll1 intracellular domain by E3 ligases promotes endocytosis of the ligand-Notch complex by the signal-sending cell (geffers2008biochemicalnonequivalenceof pages 90-93, geffers2008biochemicalnonequivalenceof pages 14-17). This endocytic internalization generates a mechanical pulling force that physically dissociates the Notch heterodimer, exposing the receptor to proteolytic cleavage (geffers2008biochemicalnonequivalenceof pages 14-17, wang2011notchsignalingand pages 3-5). The requirement for ligand endocytosis distinguishes productive trans-activation from nonproductive cis-inhibition and explains why soluble or intracellular-domain-deficient ligands cannot fully activate Notch (geffers2008biochemicalnonequivalenceof pages 90-93, zolkiewska2008adamproteasesligand pages 6-8).

Sequential Proteolytic Processing

After ligand-induced conformational change, an ADAM metalloprotease—primarily ADAM10 under physiological conditions—cleaves Notch at the S2 site, releasing the Notch extracellular domain (geffers2008biochemicalnonequivalenceof pages 14-17, wang2011notchsignalingand pages 3-5). Subsequent γ-secretase-dependent intramembrane proteolysis at the S3 site releases the Notch intracellular domain (NICD) from the membrane (geffers2008biochemicalnonequivalenceof pages 14-17). NICD then translocates to the nucleus, where it associates with the CSL/RBPJ transcription factor and Mastermind-like coactivators to activate Notch target-gene expression (hirano2020deltalike1and pages 1-2, tveriakhina2018theectodomainsdetermine pages 1-2, geffers2008biochemicalnonequivalenceof pages 14-17).

Cis-Inhibition versus Trans-Activation

Dll1 also mediates cis-inhibition when present in the same cell as Notch (geffers2008biochemicalnonequivalenceof pages 87-90, vazquezulloa2022reversibleandbidirectional pages 7-8, vazquezulloa2022reversibleandbidirectional pages 5-7). In this cell-autonomous interaction, Dll1 binds and sequesters Notch receptors, preventing them from responding to activating ligands on neighboring cells (vazquezulloa2022reversibleandbidirectional pages 7-8, vazquezulloa2022reversibleandbidirectional pages 5-7). Cis-inhibition can occur intracellularly and does not require a functional DSL domain or cell-surface presentation, distinguishing it mechanistically from trans-activation (geffers2008biochemicalnonequivalenceof pages 87-90, geffers2008biochemicalnonequivalenceof pages 75-78). This cis/trans regulatory balance helps establish directionality in Notch signaling and prevents autocrine activation (vazquezulloa2022reversibleandbidirectional pages 7-8, vazquezulloa2022reversibleandbidirectional pages 5-7).

Proteolytic Processing and Protein Forms

As noted in the UniProt annotation, Dll1 exists in multiple processed forms:

Full-Length Dll1

The approximately 90-kDa full-length, membrane-anchored form represents the canonical ligand capable of trans-activating Notch (dyczynska2007proteolyticprocessingof pages 1-2, dyczynska2007proteolyticprocessingof pages 3-4).

Dll1-Soluble Form (Dll1-EC)

ADAM metalloproteases, particularly ADAM9, ADAM10, ADAM12, and ADAM17, cleave Dll1 in its juxtamembrane extracellular region, approximately 10 amino acids from the transmembrane domain (dyczynska2007proteolyticprocessingof pages 7-8, zolkiewska2008adamproteasesligand pages 4-6). The principal cleavage site in mouse Dll1 has been mapped between His535 and Met536 (zolkiewska2008adamproteasesligand pages 4-6). This cleavage releases a soluble N-terminal extracellular fragment (Dll1-EC or NTF, approximately 60–65 kDa) into the extracellular medium (dyczynska2007proteolyticprocessingof pages 1-2, zolkiewska2008adamproteasesligand pages 2-4, dyczynska2007proteolyticprocessingof pages 4-5). ADAM10 is the primary physiological sheddase, though ADAM12-mediated processing has been extensively characterized in vitro (dyczynska2007proteolyticprocessingof pages 7-8, dyczynska2007proteolyticprocessingof pages 1-2, zolkiewska2008adamproteasesligand pages 4-6). Soluble Dll1 fragments do not activate Notch and can act as competitive inhibitors (klose2015solublenotchligand pages 5-7, zolkiewska2008adamproteasesligand pages 6-8).

Dll1-Derived Cell-Associated Form (Dll1-TMIC)

ADAM cleavage leaves a membrane-associated C-terminal fragment (Dll1-TMIC, approximately 29 kDa) containing the transmembrane and intracellular domains (dyczynska2007proteolyticprocessingof pages 1-2, zolkiewska2008adamproteasesligand pages 8-9, zolkiewska2008adamproteasesligand pages 2-4). This intermediate fragment is a substrate for subsequent γ-secretase processing (zolkiewska2008adamproteasesligand pages 8-9, dyczynska2007proteolyticprocessingof pages 4-5).

Dll1-Intracellular Form (Dll1-IC)

Following ADAM cleavage, presenilin-dependent γ-secretase cleaves Dll1-TMIC, releasing an intracellular domain (Dll1-IC) of approximately 3 kDa smaller than the TMIC (zolkiewska2008adamproteasesligand pages 8-9, dyczynska2007proteolyticprocessingof pages 4-5). This fragment is rapidly degraded by the proteasome and is difficult to detect without proteasome inhibitors (dyczynska2007proteolyticprocessingof pages 4-5). The physiological significance of Dll1-IC remains less well understood than the canonical forward signaling mechanism, though it may participate in reverse or bidirectional signaling (zolkiewska2008adamproteasesligand pages 8-9, zolkiewska2008adamproteasesligand pages 2-4).

ADAM-mediated Dll1 processing is regulated: cleavage by ADAM12 is constitutive, cell-density-dependent, and occurs in cis orientation (dyczynska2007proteolyticprocessingof pages 1-2). Importantly, ADAM cleavage of Dll1 relieves cis-inhibition of Notch, thereby restoring Notch responsiveness in ligand-expressing cells (zolkiewska2008adamproteasesligand pages 8-9, zolkiewska2008adamproteasesligand pages 6-8).

Cellular and Subcellular Localization

Dll1 protein is predominantly localized at the plasma membrane or cell surface, where it functions as a contact-dependent signaling ligand (geffers2007divergentfunctionsand pages 6-8, geffers2008biochemicalnonequivalenceof pages 59-62, geffers2007divergentfunctionsand pages 5-6). Immunofluorescence and surface-biotinylation experiments demonstrate efficient cell-surface presentation in both cultured cells and embryonic tissues (geffers2008biochemicalnonequivalenceof pages 59-62, geffers2007divergentfunctionsand pages 5-6). In presomitic mesoderm cells and wing-disc epithelia, endogenous Dll1 colocalizes with apical membrane markers and outlines cell–cell contacts (geffers2007divergentfunctionsand pages 6-8, geffers2007divergentfunctionsand pages 5-6).

In addition to its primary membrane localization, Dll1 is also present in intracellular vesicular compartments, including the cis-Golgi network (marked by GM130 colocalization) and clathrin-coated vesicles/early endosomes (geffers2007divergentfunctionsand pages 6-8, geffers2007divergentfunctionsand pages 5-6, serth2015ofucosylationofdll3 pages 5-8). This intracellular pool reflects the protein's trafficking through the secretory pathway and its endocytosis following receptor engagement (geffers2007divergentfunctionsand pages 5-6, serth2015ofucosylationofdll3 pages 5-8). The distribution between membrane and intracellular pools can be influenced by coexpression with other Notch-pathway components such as Dll3 (serth2015ofucosylationofdll3 pages 5-8).

Functionally, Dll1's membrane localization is essential for trans-activation: disruption of the DSL domain or intracellular-domain-dependent trafficking impairs surface presentation and abolishes signaling (geffers2008biochemicalnonequivalenceof pages 59-62, geffers2008biochemicalnonequivalenceof pages 90-93, geffers2008biochemicalnonequivalenceof pages 81-84). This contrasts with the divergent ligand Dll3, which is predominantly intracellular and largely absent from the cell surface (geffers2007divergentfunctionsand pages 6-8, geffers2007divergentfunctionsand pages 5-6).

Somitogenesis and Segmentation Clock

Dll1 is a central component of the vertebrate segmentation clock, which generates periodic somites along the embryonic anterior–posterior axis (shimojo2016oscillatorycontrolof pages 2-3, bone2012investigatingtheregulation pages 170-175). Dll1 mRNA and protein expression oscillate dynamically in the presomitic mesoderm (PSM) with a period matching somite formation (approximately 2 hours in mouse) (shimojo2016oscillatorycontrolof pages 2-3, bone2012investigatingtheregulation pages 114-122). Live imaging revealed that Dll1 protein waves propagate rostrally through the PSM and regress caudally as each new somite forms (shimojo2016oscillatorycontrolof pages 2-3). These oscillations are part of a negative-feedback network involving Hes7, which represses Dll1 transcription, and Lunatic fringe (Lfng), which modulates Notch–Dll1 interaction kinetics (bone2012investigatingtheregulation pages 170-175, bone2012investigatingtheregulation pages 46-51). Nascent Dll1 transcription cycles in phase with Wnt targets such as Snail1 but out of phase with canonical Notch targets (bone2012investigatingtheregulation pages 114-122).

Functionally, Dll1-mediated Notch signaling is required for somite epithelialization, boundary maintenance, and rostrocaudal polarity (geffers2008biochemicalnonequivalenceof pages 26-28, teppner2007compartmentalisedexpressionof pages 11-12). Loss of Dll1 disrupts somite organization, impairs myotome and sclerotome patterning, and deranges polarity markers such as Uncx4.1 and Tbx18 (geffers2008biochemicalnonequivalenceof pages 26-28). Compartmentalized Dll1 expression in caudal somite halves is also necessary for proper intervertebral-joint formation (teppner2007compartmentalisedexpressionof pages 11-12).

Skeletal Muscle Development and Stem-Cell Maintenance

Dll1 maintains embryonic myogenic progenitor populations and regulates the timing of differentiation (tveriakhina2018theectodomainsdetermine pages 26-27, serth2015ofucosylationofdll3 pages 20-21). Delta1-deficient embryos exhibit premature myogenic differentiation, depletion of muscle progenitors, and severe muscle hypotrophy (tveriakhina2018theectodomainsdetermine pages 26-27, serth2015ofucosylationofdll3 pages 20-21). More recent work (2021–2025) demonstrated that oscillatory Dll1 expression—rather than simply its steady-state level—is critical for balancing self-renewal and differentiation of muscle stem cells during postnatal growth and regeneration (peng2025stagespecificandcellautonomous pages 1-2). Stage-specific deletion studies revealed both neighboring-cell and cell-autonomous Dll1 functions in myogenic regulation (peng2025stagespecificandcellautonomous pages 1-2).

Nervous System and Neurogenesis

In the developing nervous system, Dll1 activates Notch to mediate lateral inhibition, thereby restraining premature neurogenesis and maintaining neural progenitor pools (geffers2008biochemicalnonequivalenceof pages 26-28). Dll1-deficient embryos develop a hyperplastic central nervous system due to excessive neuronal differentiation (geffers2008biochemicalnonequivalenceof pages 26-28). This lateral-inhibition mechanism ensures coordinated neuronal production and proper nervous-system patterning.

Immune System and Hematopoiesis

Dll1 plays context-dependent roles in lymphoid development. Genetic studies indicate that Dll1 is specifically required for marginal-zone B-cell generation but is not the indispensable thymic ligand for T-cell production; Dll4 fulfills that essential role (tveriakhina2018theectodomainsdetermine pages 26-27, hirano2020deltalike1and pages 17-17). Nevertheless, Dll1 can support T-lineage differentiation when appropriately expressed: ectopic Dll1 in stromal cells induces T-cell development from hematopoietic progenitors in vitro, and Dll1-bearing cells promoted differentiation of CD19− Thy1+ CD25+ T-lineage cells from fetal liver progenitors (hirano2020deltalike1and pages 4-6, hirano2020deltalike1and pages 17-18).

In mature immune function, dendritic-cell-expressed Dll1 regulates T-cell effector and memory responses (tchekneva2019determinantrolesof pages 1-2). CD11c-lineage-specific deletion of Dll1 impaired antigen-specific CD8+ T-cell activity, reduced effector-memory differentiation, decreased IFN-γ production, increased IL-4 and immunosuppressive regulatory T cells, and accelerated tumor growth in experimental models (tchekneva2019determinantrolesof pages 1-2). Multivalent clustered DLL1 restored anti-tumor T-cell responses, whereas monomeric soluble DLL1 suppressed tumor and allograft rejection (tchekneva2019determinantrolesof pages 1-2). These findings position Dll1 as an important regulator of adaptive immunity and anti-tumor immune responses.

Vascular Development and Arterial Specification

Dll1 is essential for maintaining fetal arterial endothelial identity (sorensen2009dll1mediatednotchactivation pages 7-8, sorensen2009dll1mediatednotchactivation pages 6-7, sorensen2009dll1mediatednotchactivation pages 4-5). Dll1 expression is selectively upregulated in arterial endothelial cells beginning around embryonic day 13.5 and is required for sustained Notch1 activation in fetal arteries (sorensen2009dll1mediatednotchactivation pages 6-7, sorensen2009dll1mediatednotchactivation pages 3-4). Endothelial-specific deletion of Dll1 eliminates detectable NICD and downstream Notch signaling in arterial endothelium despite continued expression of Notch1 and other ligands such as Dll4, demonstrating Dll1's nonredundant function in this context (sorensen2009dll1mediatednotchactivation pages 6-7, sorensen2009dll1mediatednotchactivation pages 4-5).

Loss of Dll1-mediated Notch signaling causes loss of arterial markers (ephrinB2, VEGFR2, neuropilin-1) and ectopic expression of the venous marker COUP-TFII (sorensen2009dll1mediatednotchactivation pages 4-5, sorensen2009dll1mediatednotchactivation pages 5-6). Dll1/Notch activity also directly promotes neuropilin-1 (Nrp1) transcription through RBPjκ sites in the Nrp1 promoter, thereby preserving VEGFR2/NRP1-mediated VEGF responsiveness (sorensen2009dll1mediatednotchactivation pages 1-2, sorensen2009dll1mediatednotchactivation pages 5-6, sorensen2009dll1mediatednotchactivation pages 2-3). Dll1 mutants exhibit reduced arterial lumen size, altered capillary branching, and impaired vascular structure (sorensen2009dll1mediatednotchactivation pages 4-5, sorensen2009dll1mediatednotchactivation pages 5-6), although gross arteriovenous shunts are not observed (sorensen2009dll1mediatednotchactivation pages 5-6). These findings establish Dll1 as a critical regulator of arterial endothelial differentiation, VEGF signaling competence, and coordinated vessel-wall development.

Recent Developments (2023–2026)

Recent studies continue to expand understanding of Dll1 function:

Muscle Stem Cells and Myogenesis (2025)

A 2025 Nature Communications study demonstrated stage-specific and cell-autonomous functions of Dll1 in skeletal muscle stem cells and myogenesis (peng2025stagespecificandcellautonomous pages 1-2). Dll1 conditional knockout in muscle stem cells disrupts homeostasis in adult mice and impairs regeneration, effects that are intrinsic and cannot be rescued by coculture with wild-type cells. The study also identified a cell-autonomous role for a Dll1-derived intracellular domain in promoting myoblast differentiation and fusion (peng2025stagespecificandcellautonomous pages 1-2).

Atherosclerosis and Cardiovascular Disease (2026)

Research published in 2026 in the Journal of Atherosclerosis and Thrombosis examined the role of Dll1 blockade in atherosclerotic lesion formation (Orkhonselenge et al., 2026). Because genetic deletion of Dll1 is embryonic lethal, the investigators used Dll1-blocking antibodies in mouse atherosclerosis models. Dll1 inhibition reduced lesion formation and attenuated plaque vulnerability, suggesting Dll1 contributes to atherosclerotic pathogenesis and may represent a therapeutic target for cardiovascular disease.

Comparative Ligand Function and Structural Determinants (2023–2024)

Recent structural and functional studies have continued to elucidate how Dll1 differs from closely related ligands such as Dll4. Work published in 2023 (Nature Chemical Biology) developed affinity-matured Dll4 variants as broad-spectrum Notch modulators, providing tools to dissect ligand-specific versus receptor-specific contributions to signaling. Comparative domain-swap and mutagenesis studies published through 2024 reinforced that the Dll1 extracellular domain—particularly the MNNL/C2, DSL, and proximal EGF repeats—determines ligand-selective receptor activation and developmental outcomes (hirano2020deltalike1and pages 1-2, tveriakhina2018theectodomainsdetermine pages 1-2).

Clinical and Translational Contexts (2024–2026)

Although Dll1 itself has not been the primary target of recent clinical trials, investigations into Notch ligand biology and Delta-family proteins continue. Studies on Dll3-targeted therapies in neuroendocrine cancers have highlighted general principles of DSL ligand function and provided comparative context for Dll1 research. Additionally, a 2026 study examined DLL-1 as a biomarker for sepsis prediction in emergency-department patients (Hübner et al., 2026), demonstrating elevated serum Dll1 concentrations in sepsis patients and suggesting that Dll1 may reflect disease severity and immune/endothelial activation.

Current Understanding and Expert Consensus

Dll1 is now recognized as a canonical, indispensable Notch ligand with context-dependent, nonredundant functions across multiple developmental and physiological systems. Expert consensus, based on extensive genetic, biochemical, and cell-biological evidence, establishes several key principles:

  1. Mechanistic Model: Dll1-mediated Notch activation requires membrane anchorage, ligand endocytosis to generate mechanical force, sequential ADAM and γ-secretase proteolysis of Notch, and nuclear translocation of NICD for transcriptional regulation (hirano2020deltalike1and pages 1-2, tveriakhina2018theectodomainsdetermine pages 1-2, geffers2008biochemicalnonequivalenceof pages 14-17, wang2011notchsignalingand pages 3-5).

  2. Ligand Specificity: Dll1 and Dll4 are structurally similar but functionally distinct. Dll1 activates Notch1 and Notch2 approximately equally, whereas Dll4 preferentially activates Notch1. These differences map primarily to the extracellular C2/MNNL, DSL, and proximal EGF regions (tveriakhina2018theectodomainsdetermine pages 1-2, tveriakhina2018theectodomainsdetermine pages 2-5).

  3. Developmental Essentiality: Complete loss of Dll1 causes embryonic lethality due to severe defects in somitogenesis, neurogenesis, vascular development, and other processes (geffers2008biochemicalnonequivalenceof pages 26-28). Tissue-specific deletions reveal stage- and context-dependent requirements (peng2025stagespecificandcellautonomous pages 1-2, sorensen2009dll1mediatednotchactivation pages 4-5).

  4. Dynamic Regulation: Dll1 expression is not static; oscillatory dynamics in the presomitic mesoderm and muscle stem cells are functionally important and cannot be replaced by steady-state expression (shimojo2016oscillatorycontrolof pages 2-3, bone2012investigatingtheregulation pages 114-122, peng2025stagespecificandcellautonomous pages 1-2).

  5. Proteolytic Regulation: ADAM-mediated Dll1 shedding modulates signaling by producing inactive soluble fragments and relieving cis-inhibition (dyczynska2007proteolyticprocessingof pages 7-8, dyczynska2007proteolyticprocessingof pages 1-2, zolkiewska2008adamproteasesligand pages 8-9).

Summary

Delta-like protein 1 (Dll1; UniProt Q61483) is a type I transmembrane Notch ligand in mouse, characterized by an N-terminal C2/MNNL domain, a DSL domain, eight EGF-like repeats including a DOS motif, and a short intracellular tail. Dll1 activates Notch receptors on neighboring cells through a multi-step process involving receptor binding, endocytosis-dependent mechanical force generation, sequential proteolytic cleavage, and nuclear translocation of the Notch intracellular domain. The protein is predominantly localized at the plasma membrane, with secondary trafficking through Golgi and endosomal compartments. Dll1 undergoes proteolytic processing by ADAM metalloproteases and γ-secretase, producing soluble, membrane-associated, and intracellular forms.

Biologically, Dll1 is essential for somitogenesis and the segmentation clock, skeletal muscle development and stem-cell maintenance, neurogenesis, immune-cell differentiation (particularly marginal-zone B cells and T-cell effector responses), and fetal arterial endothelial specification. Recent research (2023–2026) continues to expand understanding of Dll1 in muscle regeneration, atherosclerosis, and comparative ligand biology. Dll1 functions through context-dependent Notch signaling to regulate cell-fate decisions, maintain progenitor pools, and coordinate developmental patterning across multiple organ systems.

Dll1 (mouse) review notes

UniProt Q61483 (DLL1_MOUSE), Delta-like protein 1, 722 aa, type I transmembrane DSL ligand.
PANTHER PTHR24049 (CRUMBS FAMILY MEMBER) per UniProt DR line. MGI:104659.
Notch-module role: canonical activating Delta-class (DSL) Notch ligand (signal-sending cell).

Molecular function

  • Binds Notch1/Notch2 in trans and activates cleavage and RBP-J-dependent transcription
    PMID:10958687
    PMID:10958687
  • Fringe increases Delta1 binding and Notch1 activation PMID:15574878
  • Ubiquitinated by E3 ligases Mib1 and Neurl1/Neurl1b; ubiquitination needed for recycling and Notch-binding
    competence and to drive Notch1 transendocytosis
    PMID:16000382
    PMID:17003037
    PMID:18676613
    PMID:21985982
  • Itself a substrate of regulated intramembrane proteolysis (ADAM10 shedding then gamma-secretase); ICD partly nuclear
    PMID:12794186
  • C-terminal PDZ-binding motif binds MAGI1/MAGI2 (Acvrinp1) PDZ domains; MAGI1 recruits Dll1 to adherens junctions
    PMID:14529612
    PMID:15908431
  • MT1-MMP (MMP14) cleaves Dll1 on stromal cells to dampen Notch signalling PMID:21572390.

Localisation

  • Plasma membrane; Mib1-dependent endocytosis to cytoplasmic vesicles
    PMID:16000382
  • Adherens junctions/apical endfeet in neural tube PMID:24715457
  • Lipid rafts PMID:21985982.

Biological processes

  • Lateral inhibition in neurogenesis, inner ear hair cells, intestinal secretory cells
    PMID:16495313
    PMID:18997111
  • Somitogenesis (segmentation clock, somite polarity), left-right asymmetry via Nodal
    PMID:12730124
  • Arterial identity (DLL1 activates Notch1 in fetal arteries) PMID:19144989
  • Marginal zone B cells, adult neural stem cell quiescence, satellite cells, epidermis: pleiotropic.

Pathway-variant relevance

  • Canonical activating Delta with MNNL/C2, DSL (InterPro IPR001774) and eight EGF repeats. Falcon deep
    research reports a DOS motif in the EGF1-2 region of Dll1 (absent from Dll4), citing Hirano et al. 2020
    [file:mouse/Dll1/Dll1-deep-research-falcon.md "Dll1 also contains a Delta-and-OSM-11-like (DOS) motif within the EGF1–2 region, which is present in Dll1 but absent from Dll4"];
    primary source not independently checked here. Requires Mib1 (and Neurl) ubiquitination to signal. Dll4 paralog partially redundant but
    functionally divergent PMID:26114479. Dll3 is the divergent non-activating paralog.

  Dll1 (Delta-like 1) is a type I transmembrane DSL-family ligand of Notch receptors, with an N-terminal
  MNNL domain, a DSL domain and eight EGF-like repeats in its ectodomain and a cytoplasmic tail ending
  in a PDZ-binding motif. Presented at the plasma membrane of signal-sending cells, Dll1 binds Notch1
  and Notch2 in trans on neighbouring cells; its ubiquitination by the E3 ligases Mind bomb 1 and Neuralized
  drives ligand endocytosis and recycling and generates the pulling force that exposes Notch to S2 cleavage,
  thereby activating canonical Notch-RBP-J signalling. Fringe glycosylation of Notch enhances Dll1 binding.
  Dll1 itself undergoes ADAM10 shedding and gamma-secretase cleavage, and is recruited to adherens junctions
  by the PDZ scaffold MAGI1. As the principal activating Delta ligand in many mouse tissues, Dll1 mediates
  lateral inhibition in neurogenesis, inner-ear hair cell patterning and intestinal secretory lineage
  selection, and is required for somitogenesis and somite polarity, left-right asymmetry, arterial endothelial
  identity, marginal zone B cell development and adult neural and muscle stem cell maintenance.
