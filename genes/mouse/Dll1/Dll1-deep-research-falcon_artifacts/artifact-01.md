| Tissue/system | Specific Dll1 function | Key evidence | Outcome when Dll1 signaling is disrupted |
|---|---|---|---|
| Somitogenesis and axial skeleton | Provides contact-dependent Notch signaling in the presomitic mesoderm; coordinates segmentation-clock activity, somite epithelialization, boundary maintenance, and rostrocaudal identity. Dll1 RNA and protein exhibit rostrally propagating oscillations coupled to somite formation. | Live imaging detected oscillatory Dll1 protein waves in mouse presomitic mesoderm; genetic studies showed that Dll1 is required for organized somites and appropriate expression of polarity markers. Restricted expression in caudal somite compartments is also important for intervertebral-joint morphogenesis. (pqac-00000032, pqac-00000036, pqac-00000046) | Loss disrupts somite epithelialization and boundaries, deranges rostrocaudal polarity, and disorganizes myotome and sclerotome patterning. Inappropriate spatial expression causes vertebral and intervertebral-joint abnormalities. (pqac-00000032, pqac-00000036) |
| Skeletal muscle | Maintains embryonic myogenic progenitors and regulates the timing of differentiation; oscillatory Dll1 input helps balance muscle-stem-cell self-renewal against differentiation during growth and regeneration. | Delta1-mutant studies associate reduced signaling with premature myogenic differentiation and progenitor depletion. Later mouse work showed that Dll1 dynamics—not merely its mean abundance—are important for muscle stem-cell maintenance. (pqac-00000033, pqac-00000035) | Disruption causes premature differentiation, loss of the progenitor pool, severe muscle hypotrophy, and impaired regenerative self-renewal. (pqac-00000033, pqac-00000035) |
| Nervous system | Activates Notch in neighboring neural progenitors to mediate lateral inhibition, restrain premature neurogenesis, and preserve undifferentiated progenitor populations. | Dll1-deficient embryos display neural overproduction and central nervous-system hyperplasia, consistent with failure of Notch-dependent control of neuronal differentiation. (pqac-00000032) | Premature or excessive neuronal differentiation depletes progenitors and produces a hyperplastic embryonic central nervous system. (pqac-00000032) |
| Immune and hematopoietic systems | Regulates lineage decisions through Notch, including marginal-zone B-cell generation and context-dependent T-lineage induction. Dendritic-cell Dll1 also supports antigen-specific CD8 T-cell effector and memory responses. | Mouse genetics indicate that Dll1 is required for marginal-zone B cells but is not the indispensable physiological thymic ligand for T-cell generation; Dll4 has that dominant role. In tumor models, CD11c-lineage Dll1 deletion reduced CD8 effector-memory differentiation and IFN-γ production, whereas clustered DLL1 restored antitumor responses. (pqac-00000039, pqac-00000041, pqac-00000042, pqac-00000045) | Depending on context, loss impairs marginal-zone B-cell formation, weakens antigen-specific CD8 activity and effector-memory differentiation, lowers IFN-γ, increases IL-4 and immunosuppressive cell populations, and accelerates experimental tumor growth. (pqac-00000041, pqac-00000042) |
| Vascular system | Maintains fetal arterial endothelial identity by activating Notch1 and sustaining ephrin-B2, VEGFR2, and neuropilin-1; thereby preserves VEGF responsiveness and coordinated vessel-wall differentiation. | Endothelial Dll1 becomes artery-selective from about embryonic day 13.5. Conditional deletion eliminates later arterial NICD despite continued Notch1 and DLL4 expression; constitutively active Notch rescues arterial markers, placing Dll1 upstream of Notch. (pqac-00000052, pqac-00000053, pqac-00000054, pqac-00000055) | Loss causes reduced arterial-marker expression, ectopic venous COUP-TFII, reduced arterial lumen size, altered capillary branching, impaired VEGF responsiveness, and defective arterial endothelial identity. (pqac-00000054, pqac-00000056, pqac-00000057) |
