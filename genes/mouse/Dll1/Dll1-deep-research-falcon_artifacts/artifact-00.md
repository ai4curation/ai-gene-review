| Structural feature | Position / organization | Principal function | Key structural and conservation features | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Signal peptide / precursor organization | N terminus; directs the nascent type-I membrane protein into the secretory pathway | Enables membrane insertion, extracellular-domain maturation, and delivery toward the cell surface | Consistent with DLL1 being synthesized as a precursor and subsequently displayed with its N terminus extracellular and short C terminus cytoplasmic | (pqac-00000012, pqac-00000024) |
| C2 domain, also termed MNNL | N-terminal extracellular module preceding the DSL domain | Contributes to high-affinity Notch recognition, ligand orientation, and possibly membrane association | Forms part of an extended C2–DSL–EGF architecture; contacts the receptor near Notch EGF12. Unlike Jagged-family C2 domains, Delta-family C2 domains do not bind calcium. “MNNL” and “C2” are overlapping nomenclatures used for this N-terminal module | (pqac-00000007, pqac-00000010, pqac-00000014) |
| DSL domain | Extracellular; immediately follows C2/MNNL | Provides the defining canonical Notch-ligand interaction surface and is essential for productive trans-activation | Delta/Serrate/LAG-2-family signature domain; contacts Notch near EGF11. Conserved YY and GWxG features help stabilize its fold: disrupting GWxG abolishes activation, while tyrosine mutations strongly reduce activation and can impair surface delivery | (pqac-00000005, pqac-00000009, pqac-00000015) |
| EGF-like repeat array | Eight extracellular EGF-like repeats follow the DSL domain | Creates an elongated extracellular scaffold, supports receptor binding and productive signaling, and helps determine ligand-specific activity | Each canonical EGF-like module is about 40 residues and generally contains six cysteines forming three disulfide bonds. DLL1 has eight repeats, compared with six in divergent DLL3; some repeats contain calcium-binding or Asp/Asn-hydroxylation-associated motifs | (pqac-00000008, pqac-00000012, pqac-00000013) |
| EGF repeats 1–2 and DOS motif | First two EGF repeats immediately C-terminal to DSL; DOS motif spans the EGF1–2 region | Acts with DSL as an additional Notch-binding module and enhances signal transmission | The Delta-and-OSM-11-like proteins (DOS) motif is present in DLL1 but absent from DLL4. Both DLL1-specific sequence and correct spacing of EGF1–2 are important: substitution or spacer disruption can abolish trans-activation even when membrane localization is retained | (pqac-00000006, pqac-00000011, pqac-00000039) |
| N terminus through EGF3 | Composite C2/MNNL–DSL–EGF1–3 receptor-interaction region | Major determinant of receptor selectivity and ligand-specific signaling behavior | Domain-swap experiments localize much of the difference between DLL1 and DLL4 to this region. DLL1 activates NOTCH1 and NOTCH2 approximately equally in tested cell assays, whereas DLL4 preferentially activates NOTCH1 | (pqac-00000001, pqac-00000007, pqac-00000008) |
| Distal EGF repeats 4–8 | Membrane-proximal portion of the extracellular EGF array | Extends and positions the receptor-binding head and can contribute context-dependent structural support | DLL1 is nearly linear through its N-terminal domains but exhibits an approximately 90° bend between EGF4 and EGF5; interfaces among repeats can be rigid, flexible, or bent. Some distal repeats are more interchangeable with DLL3 than EGF1–2 | (pqac-00000010, pqac-00000011) |
| Juxtamembrane ectodomain cleavage region | Extracellular, approximately 10 residues before the transmembrane segment; principal mouse site mapped near His535–Met536 | Substrate region for ADAM-mediated ectodomain shedding, producing soluble DLL1-EC and membrane-retained DLL1-TMIC | Cleavage depends on the local region rather than one invariant short motif. ADAM10 contributes physiologically, while ADAM9, ADAM12, and ADAM17 can also process DLL1 in cellular systems | (pqac-00000016, pqac-00000021) |
| Transmembrane helix | Single membrane-spanning segment near the C terminus | Anchors full-length DLL1 at the signal-sending cell surface, permitting mechanically coupled, contact-dependent signaling | Establishes DLL1 as a type-I single-pass membrane protein; after ectodomain shedding, it remains within DLL1-TMIC and becomes a substrate for presenilin-dependent γ-secretase | (pqac-00000018, pqac-00000019) |
| Intracellular domain | Short cytoplasmic C-terminal tail | Controls ubiquitination, endocytosis, trafficking, clustering, and signaling competence of membrane-bound DLL1 | Contains numerous lysines—17 reported in mouse DLL1—as candidate ubiquitination sites and a conserved C-terminal PDZ-binding motif. Mindbomb/Neuralized-dependent ubiquitination and endocytosis help generate the pulling force required for Notch activation | (pqac-00000002, pqac-00000009, pqac-00000013) |
| Proteolytically derived intracellular domain, DLL1-IC | Released from DLL1-TMIC by γ-secretase after ADAM cleavage | Proposed reverse-signaling or cell-autonomous regulatory fragment; its physiological importance remains less certain than canonical forward signaling | Short-lived and rapidly degraded by the proteasome; γ-secretase inhibition causes DLL1-TMIC accumulation, demonstrating sequential ADAM-then-γ-secretase processing | (pqac-00000018, pqac-00000020, pqac-00000022) |


*Table: Domain-by-domain summary of mouse DLL1 (UniProt Q61483), linking its C2/MNNL, DSL, eight EGF repeats, DOS motif, membrane anchor, and cytoplasmic tail to receptor binding, trafficking, and proteolytic processing.*
