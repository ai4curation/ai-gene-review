CC   -!- FUNCTION: Mitochondrial malate dehydrogenase that catalyzes the
CC       reversible conversion of malate to oxaloacetate using NAD(+) as a
CC       cofactor. Plays a central role in the tricarboxylic acid (TCA) cycle
CC       contributing to cellular respiration and energy production via the
CC       mitochondrial electron transport chain (By similarity). Also
CC       participates in the malate-aspartate NADH shuttle, facilitating
CC       transfer of reducing equivalents between cytosol and mitochondria to
CC       maintain redox balance (NAD(+)/NADH homeostasis) and support metabolic
CC       flux between compartments (PubMed:35063410). In presence of an elevated
CC       NADH/NAD(+) ratio in the mitochondrial matrix, can also use 2-
CC       oxoglutarate as substrate and catalyze the reduction of 2-oxoglutarate
CC       to L-2-hydroxyglutarate. L-2-hydroxyglutarate is a physiological
CC       metabolite that competes with 2-oxoglutarate: it inhibits the activity
CC       of some 2-oxoglutarate-dependent dioxygenases, such as KDM4A, KDM4B and
CC       KDM4C histone demethylases, thereby repressing nascent transcription
CC       (By similarity). In addition to its metabolic role, binds to RNA in
CC       cells, associating with cytosolic RNAs including tRNAs and mRNAs (By
CC       similarity). {ECO:0000250|UniProtKB:P40926,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:35063410, ECO:0000269|PubMed:36815302}.

CC   -!- ACTIVITY REGULATION: Malate dehydrogenase activity is enhanced by
CC       acetylation. Acts as a 2-oxoglutarate reductase in presence of an
CC       elevated NADH/NAD(+) ratio. {ECO:0000250|UniProtKB:P40926}.
CC   -!- SUBUNIT: Homodimer. Interacts with GATD3.
CC       {ECO:0000250|UniProtKB:P40926}.
CC   -!- SUBCELLULAR LOCATION: Mitochondrion matrix
CC       {ECO:0000250|UniProtKB:P40926}.
CC   -!- PTM: Acetylation is enhanced after treatment either with trichostin A
CC       (TCA) or with nicotinamide (NAM) with the appearance of tri- and
CC       tetraacetylations. Glucose also increases acetylation (By similarity).
CC       Acetylation of Lys-239 and Lys-314 is observed in liver mitochondria
CC       from fasted mice but not from fed mice. {ECO:0000250|UniProtKB:P40926}.
CC   -!- PTM: Deacetylation by SIRT3 leads to decreased enzymatic activity and
CC       promotes mitochondrial fusion. {ECO:0000269|PubMed:36815302}.

CC       {ECO:0000305}.
