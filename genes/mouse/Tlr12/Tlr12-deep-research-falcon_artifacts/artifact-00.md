| Functional characteristic | Evidence-based annotation | Key evidence / qualification |
|---|---|---|
| Target identity | Mouse **Tlr12**, encoding Toll-like receptor 12; an innate immune pattern-recognition receptor of the TLR family rather than an enzyme or transporter. | The experimentally studied receptor is murine TLR12, consistent with the supplied UniProt accession **Q6QNU9** and its leucine-rich-repeat/TIR-domain architecture. Mouse has 12 recognized TLRs, whereas humans have 10 and no functional TLR12 ortholog (pqac-00000001, pqac-00000051). |
| Primary ligand (“substrate”) | **Profilin-like protein from *Toxoplasma gondii*** (TgPRF/PFTg). This is a binding ligand, not an enzymatic substrate; no catalytic reaction is attributed to TLR12. | TLR12’s ectodomain directly binds parasite profilin, and TLR12 loss selectively impairs responses to TgPRF without broadly disabling responses to other tested TLR ligands (pqac-00000017, pqac-00000023, pqac-00000045). |
| Receptor configuration | Forms **TLR11–TLR12 heterodimers** and **TLR12 homodimers**. The dominant functional configuration is cell-type dependent. | Co-immunoprecipitation and intracellular colocalization support physical TLR11–TLR12 association. Both receptors are required in macrophages and conventional dendritic cells, whereas TLR12 can signal without TLR11—probably as a homodimer—in plasmacytoid dendritic cells (pqac-00000009, pqac-00000013, pqac-00000014). |
| Subcellular localization | An intracellular transmembrane receptor concentrated in **endosomal/endolysosomal compartments**, rather than primarily at the plasma membrane. | TLR12 colocalizes with TLR11 and the intracellular-TLR trafficking protein **UNC93B1**. Profilin binding is favored under acidic conditions compatible with the endolysosomal lumen (pqac-00000004, pqac-00000017, pqac-00000039). |
| Trafficking requirement | **UNC93B1-dependent** trafficking from the endoplasmic reticulum toward endolysosomal signaling compartments. | Both TLR11 and TLR12 interact with UNC93B1; this dependence supports their classification as intracellular TLRs (pqac-00000009, pqac-00000011, pqac-00000016). |
| Principal expressing/responding cells | Most firmly established in **plasmacytoid dendritic cells (pDCs), conventional dendritic cells (cDCs), and macrophages**. Transcripts have also been detected in mouse T and B cells, but their TLR12-specific function is less defined. | Expression is enriched in hematopoietic cells and strongest at the tissue level in spleen. NK cells are activated indirectly and are not established as direct TgPRF-sensing TLR12-positive responders (pqac-00000033, pqac-00000035, pqac-00000037). |
| Proximal signaling adaptor | The response is **MyD88 dependent**. In the heterodimer model, TLR11 directly recruits MyD88, whereas TLR12 contributes ligand recognition and receptor cooperation; direct TLR12–MyD88 binding was not detected in one study. | TLR12 or MyD88 knockdown markedly reduces profilin-induced IL-12. Co-immunoprecipitation showed TLR11, but not TLR12, interacting directly with MyD88, cautioning against describing MyD88 recruitment as a proven direct TLR12 interaction (pqac-00000022, pqac-00000027, pqac-00000028). |
| Transcriptional pathway | In dendritic cells, the best-defined output is a **MyD88-dependent, IRF8-governed IL-12 program**. Canonical MyD88–IRAK–TRAF6 signaling provides the general TLR framework, but the exact molecular bridge from MyD88 to IRF8 remains incompletely resolved. | IRF8 restoration rescues profilin-induced IL-12 in IRF8-deficient cells but not in MyD88-deficient cells, placing MyD88 upstream or as an indispensable parallel requirement (pqac-00000025, pqac-00000026). |
| NF-κB qualification | Evidence is **cell/context dependent**: TLR12-dependent IκBα degradation and NF-κB activity were observed in some macrophage/pDC experiments, whereas the IRF8-dependent dendritic-cell IL-12 response did not require NF-κB1 or NF-κB2 and resisted broad NF-κB inhibition. | TLR12 should not be annotated simply as either universally NF-κB dependent or independent; the strongest precise annotation is an IRF8-dominant IL-12 program with NF-κB involvement varying by cell type and assay (pqac-00000017, pqac-00000019, pqac-00000025). |
| Direct cytokine outputs | **IL-12/IL-12p40** is the most reproducible direct output. TLR12-mediated TgPRF sensing by pDCs can additionally induce **IFN-α/type I interferon**. | TLR12 silencing or deletion markedly diminishes profilin-induced IL-12/23p40. pDC experiments identify both IL-12 and IFN-α downstream of TLR12 (pqac-00000017, pqac-00000018, pqac-00000021). |
| Downstream immune circuit | IL-12 and type I interferon activate **NK cells**, inducing **IFN-γ**; this supports Th1 polarization and IFN-γ-dependent anti-*Toxoplasma* effector mechanisms. IFN-γ is therefore an indirect downstream output, not necessarily produced by the TLR12-expressing cell. | TgPRF-induced NK-cell IFN-γ is nearly absent in Tlr12-deficient mice, connecting receptor activation to the protective IL-12/type-I-IFN → NK-cell → IFN-γ axis (pqac-00000018, pqac-00000021, pqac-00000044). |
| Primary biological function | Endolysosomal detection of apicomplexan profilin, especially from ***T. gondii***, initiating early innate cytokine responses and protective cell-mediated immunity. | TLR12 is nonredundant with TLR11: it cooperates with TLR11 in macrophages/cDCs but also sustains TLR11-independent pDC sensing (pqac-00000010, pqac-00000013, pqac-00000040). |
| Tlr12-knockout phenotype | **Tlr12−/− mice show strongly reduced serum IL-12, defective NK-cell IFN-γ responses, high susceptibility, and rapid mortality after *T. gondii* challenge**; reported mortality approached that of Myd88-deficient mice in the foundational study. | Tlr12−/− animals lose both heterodimer-dependent recognition and the TLR11-independent pDC pathway, explaining why isolated TLR12 loss can be more severe than isolated TLR11 loss (pqac-00000040, pqac-00000041, pqac-00000042). |
| Human relevance | Humans do **not** possess a functional TLR12 counterpart; mouse TLR12 biology cannot be directly assigned to human cells. Humans detect *T. gondii* through alternative innate pathways. | This species difference is central when interpreting mouse toxoplasmosis models or considering translation of profilin/TLR12 agonism (pqac-00000003, pqac-00000012, pqac-00000051). |
| Evolutionary distribution | TLR12 has a **patchy, lineage-specific distribution**: present in mice and other rodents, lagomorphs, several bats, elephants, equids, rhinoceroses, and tapirs; pseudogenized in many cetaceans and absent from humans and several other mammalian lineages. | A 2024 comparative study found functional TLR12 genes and strong sequence conservation across equids/perissodactyls, but extensive lineage-specific loss or pseudogenization elsewhere (pqac-00000048, pqac-00000050, pqac-00000052). |


*Table: Summary of the experimentally supported molecular function, localization, signaling, cellular expression, knockout phenotype, and evolutionary distribution of mouse TLR12. Qualifications distinguish direct TLR12 evidence from pathway-level inference and cell-type-specific findings.*
