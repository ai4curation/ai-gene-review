Gene Description

Toll-like receptor of the TLR11/TLR12 pair, found in rodents and in some other mammals including horses, where both paralogues are transcribed, but with no human counterpart. It has a leucine-rich-repeat ectodomain, one transmembrane segment and a cytoplasmic TIR domain, and is expressed in macrophages and in liver, kidney and bladder epithelium. Unlike the surface TLRs it is an intracellular receptor: it resides in the endoplasmic reticulum, binds the trafficking chaperone UNC93B1 directly and engages ligand in endolysosomal compartments. Two chemically defined ligands are established, bacterial flagellin and the profilin protein of apicomplexan parasites such as Toxoplasma gondii, and the receptor does not respond to the standard panel of agonists for other TLRs. On ligand binding it recruits MyD88 through its TIR domain and drives IL-12 and IL-6 production and Th1 polarisation; the IL-12 response of dendritic cells to profilin was shown to require MyD88 and IRF8 rather than NF-kappa-B1 or NF-kappa-B2, so the transcriptional route downstream of the receptor is cell-type dependent; it can act as a homodimer and also as a heterodimer with its close paralogue, to which it supplies the MyD88-recruiting function. Animals lacking the receptor are highly susceptible to kidney infection by uropathogenic bacteria and mount impaired IL-12 responses to Toxoplasma. The literature names of the two paralogues are interchanged relative to the MGI and UniProt gene symbols, so published work on "TLR11" refers to this protein.

Comprehensive Research Report: Mouse TLR12 (Toll-like Receptor 12)

Gene Identity Confirmation

The target gene Tlr12 (UniProt: Q6QNU9) in Mus musculus encodes Toll-like receptor 12, a member of the Toll-like receptor family of pattern recognition receptors. This protein contains characteristic leucine-rich repeat (LRR) domains in its extracellular region and a Toll/interleukin-1 receptor (TIR) domain in its cytoplasmic tail, consistent with the canonical TLR architecture (rodrigues2024tlr10anintriguing pages 1-2, koblansky2013recognitionofprofilin pages 1-2). The gene was historically also referred to as TLR11 in some early publications, reflecting the close evolutionary and functional relationship between these two receptors (zou2026innateimmunitycurrent pages 4-5).

Primary Function and Ligand Specificity

Molecular Recognition

TLR12 functions as an innate immune pattern recognition receptor specialized for detecting profilin-like proteins from the apicomplexan parasite Toxoplasma gondii (TgPRF) (rodrigues2024tlr10anintriguing pages 1-2, koblansky2013recognitionofprofilin pages 9-10, koblansky2013recognitionofprofilin pages 1-2). Unlike enzymes or transporters, TLR12 does not catalyze biochemical reactions or transport substrates; instead, its primary function is ligand binding and signal initiation. The extracellular domain of TLR12 directly binds to T. gondii profilin under acidic conditions consistent with the endolysosomal pH, where ligand recognition occurs (raetz2013cooperationoftlr12 pages 6-7, raetz2013cooperationoftlr12 pages 4-6).

Receptor Configuration

TLR12 exhibits cell-type-dependent receptor configurations (koblansky2013recognitionofprofilin pages 9-10, koblansky2013recognitionofprofilin pages 1-2):

  1. TLR11-TLR12 heterodimers: In conventional dendritic cells (cDCs) and macrophages, TLR12 forms obligate heterodimers with TLR11. Both receptors directly bind profilin, and both are required for optimal ligand recognition and downstream signaling in these cell types (raetz2013cooperationoftlr12 pages 4-6, koblansky2013recognitionofprofilin pages 2-4, broz2013newlydescribedpattern pages 3-4).

  2. TLR12 homodimers: In plasmacytoid dendritic cells (pDCs), TLR12 can function independently of TLR11, likely as homodimers, to recognize profilin and initiate immune responses (koblansky2013recognitionofprofilin pages 1-2, broz2013newlydescribedpattern pages 3-4).

The physical interaction between TLR11 and TLR12 has been demonstrated by co-immunoprecipitation experiments, and both receptors exhibit extensive intracellular co-localization (raetz2013cooperationoftlr12 pages 4-6, koblansky2013recognitionofprofilin pages 2-4).

Subcellular Localization

TLR12 is an intracellular, endosomal/endolysosomal receptor rather than a cell-surface TLR (zou2026innateimmunitycurrent pages 4-5, broz2013newlydescribedpattern pages 3-4). Its trafficking and localization depend on the chaperone protein UNC93B1, which facilitates the transport of intracellular TLRs from the endoplasmic reticulum to endolysosomal compartments (raetz2013cooperationoftlr12 pages 4-6, raetz2013cooperationoftlr12 pages 1-3, broz2013newlydescribedpattern pages 3-4). This localization is functionally significant because it positions TLR12 to encounter profilin released from parasites during phagolysosomal processing of T. gondii (raetz2013cooperationoftlr12 pages 6-7). The pH-dependent binding characteristics of TLR12-profilin interactions are consistent with recognition occurring within the acidic environment of endolysosomes (raetz2013cooperationoftlr12 pages 6-7, raetz2013cooperationoftlr12 pages 4-6).

Cellular Expression Profile

TLR12 expression is primarily restricted to hematopoietic immune cells (koblansky2013recognitionofprofilin pages 2-4, koblansky2013recognitionofprofilin pages 1-2):

  • Plasmacytoid dendritic cells (pDCs): These are major TLR12-expressing cells where the receptor can function independently of TLR11 (koblansky2013recognitionofprofilin pages 9-10, koblansky2013recognitionofprofilin pages 10-10).
  • Conventional dendritic cells (cDCs): TLR12 works in cooperation with TLR11 in these cells (koblansky2013recognitionofprofilin pages 2-4, koblansky2013recognitionofprofilin pages 4-5).
  • Macrophages: Both peritoneal and tissue macrophages express functional TLR12 (koblansky2013recognitionofprofilin pages 2-4, koblansky2013recognitionofprofilin pages 4-5).
  • Lymphocytes: TLR12 transcripts have been detected in T cells and B cells, though the functional significance in these populations is less well characterized (koblansky2013recognitionofprofilin pages 2-4).

At the tissue level, TLR12 expression is strongest in the spleen, with expression also detected in the small intestine (broz2013newlydescribedpattern pages 3-4, koblansky2013recognitionofprofilin pages 2-4). Notably, TLR12 is not expressed in epithelial tissues and has a more restricted distribution than TLR11 (koblansky2013recognitionofprofilin pages 4-5, koblansky2013recognitionofprofilin pages 1-2).

Signaling Pathways and Mechanisms

Adaptor Molecules

TLR12 signaling is MyD88-dependent (raetz2013cooperationoftlr12 pages 7-9, raetz2013cooperationoftlr12 pages 6-7, pandey2015microbialsensingby pages 7-8). However, the molecular organization differs between TLR11 and TLR12: TLR11 directly recruits MyD88, while TLR12 does not show direct MyD88 binding in co-immunoprecipitation experiments (raetz2013cooperationoftlr12 pages 4-6). This suggests that in TLR11-TLR12 heterodimers, TLR11 provides the MyD88 recruitment interface while TLR12 contributes to ligand recognition and receptor cooperation (raetz2013cooperationoftlr12 pages 4-6).

Transcriptional Regulation

A distinctive feature of TLR12/TLR11 signaling in dendritic cells is the IRF8-dependent pathway for IL-12 production (raetz2013cooperationoftlr12 pages 7-9, raetz2013cooperationoftlr12 pages 18-22, raetz2013cooperationoftlr12 pages 6-7). This pathway is notable for being largely NF-κB-independent, distinguishing it from most other TLR responses:

  • Restoration of IRF8 expression in IRF8-deficient dendritic cells rescued profilin-induced IL-12 production (raetz2013cooperationoftlr12 pages 7-9).
  • The response did not require NF-κB1 or NF-κB2, and broad NF-κB inhibition did not block profilin-induced IL-12 (raetz2013cooperationoftlr12 pages 6-7, raetz2013cooperationoftlr12 pages 7-9).
  • MyD88 functions upstream of IRF8, as IRF8 restoration could not rescue IL-12 production in MyD88-deficient cells (raetz2013cooperationoftlr12 pages 7-9, raetz2013cooperationoftlr12 pages 18-22).

It should be noted that some NF-κB activation has been observed in macrophages and pDCs in response to profilin stimulation, indicating cell-type-specific variations in signaling pathway utilization (koblansky2013recognitionofprofilin pages 7-8, koblansky2013recognitionofprofilin pages 8-9).

Cytokine Production

The primary direct cytokine outputs of TLR12 activation include (raetz2013cooperationoftlr12 pages 6-7, koblansky2013recognitionofprofilin pages 1-2, koblansky2013recognitionofprofilin pages 9-10):

  1. Interleukin-12 (IL-12/IL-12p40): The most consistent and well-documented cytokine produced downstream of TLR12 activation in dendritic cells and macrophages (raetz2013cooperationoftlr12 pages 6-7, koblansky2013recognitionofprofilin pages 2-4).

  2. Type I interferons (IFN-α): Particularly in plasmacytoid dendritic cells, TLR12 activation induces IFN-α production alongside IL-12 (koblansky2013recognitionofprofilin pages 1-2, koblansky2013recognitionofprofilin pages 8-9).

  3. Interferon-gamma (IFN-γ): This is an indirect downstream output, produced primarily by natural killer (NK) cells that are activated by TLR12-induced IL-12 and type I interferons (koblansky2013recognitionofprofilin pages 1-2, koblansky2013recognitionofprofilin pages 9-10, koblansky2013recognitionofprofilin pages 8-9).

Biological Function and Pathway Integration

TLR12 serves as a critical component of the innate immune response to Toxoplasma gondii infection by initiating a protective cytokine cascade (koblansky2013recognitionofprofilin pages 9-10, koblansky2013recognitionofprofilin pages 1-2):

TLR12-mediated pathway: Profilin recognition → IL-12 and IFN-α production (by DCs/pDCs) → NK cell activation → IFN-γ production → Th1 polarization and antiparasitic immunity

This pathway bridges innate and adaptive immunity, with the TLR12-dependent IL-12 production being essential for establishing protective Th1 responses and IFN-γ-mediated parasite control (koblansky2013recognitionofprofilin pages 1-2, koblansky2013recognitionofprofilin pages 7-8, koblansky2013recognitionofprofilin pages 9-10).

In Vivo Role and Knockout Phenotype

The critical importance of TLR12 in host defense has been demonstrated through studies of Tlr12-deficient mice (koblansky2013recognitionofprofilin pages 9-10, koblansky2013recognitionofprofilin pages 5-7, koblansky2013recognitionofprofilin pages 1-2):

Susceptibility to Infection

  • TLR12-deficient mice show markedly increased susceptibility to T. gondii infection, with rapid mortality comparable to that observed in MyD88-deficient mice (koblansky2013recognitionofprofilin pages 9-10, koblansky2013recognitionofprofilin pages 5-7).
  • This high susceptibility exceeds that of TLR11-deficient mice, indicating that TLR12 is not merely redundant with TLR11 but provides additional, essential protective functions (koblansky2013recognitionofprofilin pages 9-10, koblansky2013recognitionofprofilin pages 1-2).

Impaired Cytokine Responses

  • Tlr12−/− mice exhibit significantly reduced serum IL-12 both after profilin injection and during T. gondii infection (koblansky2013recognitionofprofilin pages 5-7).
  • NK cell IFN-γ production is nearly absent in Tlr12-deficient mice following profilin stimulation, demonstrating the dependence of this downstream response on TLR12 (koblansky2013recognitionofprofilin pages 8-9).

Mechanism of Susceptibility

The increased susceptibility of TLR12-deficient mice reflects the loss of both the TLR11-TLR12 heterodimer-dependent pathway in conventional dendritic cells and macrophages, as well as the TLR11-independent, TLR12-dependent pathway in plasmacytoid dendritic cells (koblansky2013recognitionofprofilin pages 9-10, koblansky2013recognitionofprofilin pages 10-10). Depletion experiments have shown that both pDCs and NK cells are required for the residual resistance observed in TLR11-deficient mice, underscoring the importance of the TLR12-pDC-NK cell axis (koblansky2013recognitionofprofilin pages 1-2, koblansky2013recognitionofprofilin pages 10-10).

Experimental Evidence

The functional characterization of TLR12 is supported by multiple complementary experimental approaches:

Direct Binding Studies

  • Recombinant TLR12 ectodomain directly binds T. gondii profilin in a pH-dependent manner (raetz2013cooperationoftlr12 pages 6-7, raetz2013cooperationoftlr12 pages 4-6).

Genetic Approaches

  • TLR12 knockout mice demonstrate receptor-specific defects in profilin responses while maintaining responses to other TLR ligands (koblansky2013recognitionofprofilin pages 2-4).
  • Knockdown of TLR12 in dendritic cells specifically impairs profilin-induced IL-12 production (raetz2013cooperationoftlr12 pages 6-7).

Protein-Protein Interactions

  • Co-immunoprecipitation demonstrates TLR11-TLR12 heterodimer formation (raetz2013cooperationoftlr12 pages 4-6, koblansky2013recognitionofprofilin pages 2-4).
  • Co-localization studies show TLR12 association with UNC93B1 in endolysosomal compartments (raetz2013cooperationoftlr12 pages 4-6, raetz2013cooperationoftlr12 pages 1-3).

Functional Rescue Experiments

  • IRF8 reconstitution restores IL-12 responses in IRF8-deficient cells, establishing the IRF8-dependent pathway (raetz2013cooperationoftlr12 pages 7-9, raetz2013cooperationoftlr12 pages 18-22).

In Vivo Infection Studies

  • TLR12-deficient mice show impaired cytokine production, defective NK cell activation, and rapid mortality during T. gondii challenge (koblansky2013recognitionofprofilin pages 9-10, koblansky2013recognitionofprofilin pages 5-7, koblansky2013recognitionofprofilin pages 1-2).

Evolutionary and Species Distribution

Absence in Humans

A critical consideration for translational relevance is that humans do not possess a functional TLR12 ortholog (rodrigues2024tlr10anintriguing pages 1-2, koblansky2013recognitionofprofilin pages 1-2). Humans have 10 TLRs (TLR1-TLR10), while mice have 12 (TLR1-TLR9, TLR11-TLR13) (koblansky2013recognitionofprofilin pages 1-2). The absence of TLR11 and TLR12 in humans means that human innate immune recognition of T. gondii relies on alternative pathways, which has important implications for understanding human toxoplasmosis and for developing therapeutics based on mouse models (ihara2024theroleof pages 1-2).

Phylogenetic Distribution

TLR12 exhibits a patchy, lineage-specific distribution across mammals (stejskalova2024twelvetolllikereceptor pages 8-10, stejskalova2024twelvetolllikereceptor pages 10-12):

Present in: Mice and other rodents, lagomorphs, several bat species, elephants, equids (horses, donkeys, zebras), rhinoceroses, and tapirs (stejskalova2024twelvetolllikereceptor pages 8-10, stejskalova2024twelvetolllikereceptor pages 10-12, stejskalova2024twelvetolllikereceptor pages 4-5).

Absent or pseudogenized in: Humans, cetaceans (where TLR12 exists predominantly as a pseudogene), and several other mammalian lineages (stejskalova2024twelvetolllikereceptor pages 8-10).

This distribution suggests lineage-specific evolution rather than deep conservation across all mammals. A 2024 comparative genomics study of equids found that TLR12 is expressed and highly conserved across horse family members, with evidence of purifying selection maintaining its sequence, indicating ongoing functional importance in these species (stejskalova2024twelvetolllikereceptor pages 8-10, stejskalova2024twelvetolllikereceptor pages 10-12, stejskalova2024twelvetolllikereceptor pages 4-5).

Structural and Domain Organization

Based on the UniProt annotation and published literature, TLR12 contains:

  • Extracellular leucine-rich repeat (LRR) domains: These mediate ligand recognition and are characteristic of TLR family members (rodrigues2024tlr10anintriguing pages 1-2, li2025genomewideidentificationand pages 9-11).
  • Transmembrane domain: Anchors the receptor in endolysosomal membranes.
  • Cytoplasmic TIR (Toll/IL-1 receptor) domain: Mediates protein-protein interactions with signaling adaptors, though TLR12 itself does not directly bind MyD88 (raetz2013cooperationoftlr12 pages 4-6).

The LRR domains are responsible for the direct binding to T. gondii profilin, and this interaction is enhanced under acidic pH conditions (raetz2013cooperationoftlr12 pages 6-7).

Recent literature continues to explore TLR12 biology and its evolutionary context:

  • A 2024 review on innate immunity confirms TLR12's function as similar to TLR11 in mice, with profilin-like molecule recognition and IL-12 production (zou2026innateimmunitycurrent pages 4-5).
  • A 2024 study on IFN-γ-mediated responses to T. gondii emphasizes the TLR11/TLR12 heterodimer's role in IL-12 induction and notes the absence of these receptors in humans (ihara2024theroleof pages 1-2).
  • Comparative genomic studies published in 2024-2026 have mapped TLR12 distribution across diverse mammalian lineages, revealing its presence in equids with strong evolutionary conservation and its absence in humans (stejskalova2024twelvetolllikereceptor pages 8-10, neves2026cartilaginousfishinform pages 18-21, stejskalova2024twelvetolllikereceptor pages 10-12).

Summary

Functional characteristic Evidence-based annotation Key evidence / qualification
Target identity Mouse Tlr12, encoding Toll-like receptor 12; an innate immune pattern-recognition receptor of the TLR family rather than an enzyme or transporter. The experimentally studied receptor is murine TLR12, consistent with the supplied UniProt accession Q6QNU9 and its leucine-rich-repeat/TIR-domain architecture. Mouse has 12 recognized TLRs, whereas humans have 10 and no functional TLR12 ortholog (rodrigues2024tlr10anintriguing pages 1-2, koblansky2013recognitionofprofilin pages 1-2).
Primary ligand (“substrate”) Profilin-like protein from Toxoplasma gondii (TgPRF/PFTg). This is a binding ligand, not an enzymatic substrate; no catalytic reaction is attributed to TLR12. TLR12’s ectodomain directly binds parasite profilin, and TLR12 loss selectively impairs responses to TgPRF without broadly disabling responses to other tested TLR ligands (raetz2013cooperationoftlr12 pages 6-7, koblansky2013recognitionofprofilin pages 2-4).
Receptor configuration Forms TLR11–TLR12 heterodimers and TLR12 homodimers. The dominant functional configuration is cell-type dependent. Co-immunoprecipitation and intracellular colocalization support physical TLR11–TLR12 association. Both receptors are required in macrophages and conventional dendritic cells, whereas TLR12 can signal without TLR11—probably as a homodimer—in plasmacytoid dendritic cells (raetz2013cooperationoftlr12 pages 4-6, koblansky2013recognitionofprofilin pages 1-2, koblansky2013recognitionofprofilin pages 2-4).
Subcellular localization An intracellular transmembrane receptor concentrated in endosomal/endolysosomal compartments, rather than primarily at the plasma membrane. TLR12 colocalizes with TLR11 and the intracellular-TLR trafficking protein UNC93B1. Profilin binding is favored under acidic conditions compatible with the endolysosomal lumen (zou2026innateimmunitycurrent pages 4-5, raetz2013cooperationoftlr12 pages 6-7, raetz2013cooperationoftlr12 pages 4-6).
Trafficking requirement UNC93B1-dependent trafficking from the endoplasmic reticulum toward endolysosomal signaling compartments. Both TLR11 and TLR12 interact with UNC93B1; this dependence supports their classification as intracellular TLRs (raetz2013cooperationoftlr12 pages 4-6, raetz2013cooperationoftlr12 pages 1-3, broz2013newlydescribedpattern pages 3-4).
Principal expressing/responding cells Most firmly established in plasmacytoid dendritic cells (pDCs), conventional dendritic cells (cDCs), and macrophages. Transcripts have also been detected in mouse T and B cells, but their TLR12-specific function is less defined. Expression is enriched in hematopoietic cells and strongest at the tissue level in spleen. NK cells are activated indirectly and are not established as direct TgPRF-sensing TLR12-positive responders (koblansky2013recognitionofprofilin pages 9-10, koblansky2013recognitionofprofilin pages 2-4, koblansky2013recognitionofprofilin pages 1-2).
Proximal signaling adaptor The response is MyD88 dependent. In the heterodimer model, TLR11 directly recruits MyD88, whereas TLR12 contributes ligand recognition and receptor cooperation; direct TLR12–MyD88 binding was not detected in one study. TLR12 or MyD88 knockdown markedly reduces profilin-induced IL-12. Co-immunoprecipitation showed TLR11, but not TLR12, interacting directly with MyD88, cautioning against describing MyD88 recruitment as a proven direct TLR12 interaction (raetz2013cooperationoftlr12 pages 4-6, raetz2013cooperationoftlr12 pages 6-7).
Transcriptional pathway In dendritic cells, the best-defined output is a MyD88-dependent, IRF8-governed IL-12 program. Canonical MyD88–IRAK–TRAF6 signaling provides the general TLR framework, but the exact molecular bridge from MyD88 to IRF8 remains incompletely resolved. IRF8 restoration rescues profilin-induced IL-12 in IRF8-deficient cells but not in MyD88-deficient cells, placing MyD88 upstream or as an indispensable parallel requirement (raetz2013cooperationoftlr12 pages 7-9, raetz2013cooperationoftlr12 pages 18-22).
NF-κB qualification Evidence is cell/context dependent: TLR12-dependent IκBα degradation and NF-κB activity were observed in some macrophage/pDC experiments, whereas the IRF8-dependent dendritic-cell IL-12 response did not require NF-κB1 or NF-κB2 and resisted broad NF-κB inhibition. TLR12 should not be annotated simply as either universally NF-κB dependent or independent; the strongest precise annotation is an IRF8-dominant IL-12 program with NF-κB involvement varying by cell type and assay (raetz2013cooperationoftlr12 pages 6-7, koblansky2013recognitionofprofilin pages 7-8, raetz2013cooperationoftlr12 pages 7-9).
Direct cytokine outputs IL-12/IL-12p40 is the most reproducible direct output. TLR12-mediated TgPRF sensing by pDCs can additionally induce IFN-α/type I interferon. TLR12 silencing or deletion markedly diminishes profilin-induced IL-12/23p40. pDC experiments identify both IL-12 and IFN-α downstream of TLR12 (raetz2013cooperationoftlr12 pages 6-7, koblansky2013recognitionofprofilin pages 1-2, koblansky2013recognitionofprofilin pages 8-9).
Downstream immune circuit IL-12 and type I interferon activate NK cells, inducing IFN-γ; this supports Th1 polarization and IFN-γ-dependent anti-Toxoplasma effector mechanisms. IFN-γ is therefore an indirect downstream output, not necessarily produced by the TLR12-expressing cell. TgPRF-induced NK-cell IFN-γ is nearly absent in Tlr12-deficient mice, connecting receptor activation to the protective IL-12/type-I-IFN → NK-cell → IFN-γ axis (koblansky2013recognitionofprofilin pages 1-2, koblansky2013recognitionofprofilin pages 8-9).
Primary biological function Endolysosomal detection of apicomplexan profilin, especially from T. gondii, initiating early innate cytokine responses and protective cell-mediated immunity. TLR12 is nonredundant with TLR11: it cooperates with TLR11 in macrophages/cDCs but also sustains TLR11-independent pDC sensing (koblansky2013recognitionofprofilin pages 9-10, koblansky2013recognitionofprofilin pages 1-2).
Tlr12-knockout phenotype Tlr12−/− mice show strongly reduced serum IL-12, defective NK-cell IFN-γ responses, high susceptibility, and rapid mortality after T. gondii challenge; reported mortality approached that of Myd88-deficient mice in the foundational study. Tlr12−/− animals lose both heterodimer-dependent recognition and the TLR11-independent pDC pathway, explaining why isolated TLR12 loss can be more severe than isolated TLR11 loss (koblansky2013recognitionofprofilin pages 9-10, koblansky2013recognitionofprofilin pages 5-7, koblansky2013recognitionofprofilin pages 1-2).
Human relevance Humans do not possess a functional TLR12 counterpart; mouse TLR12 biology cannot be directly assigned to human cells. Humans detect T. gondii through alternative innate pathways. This species difference is central when interpreting mouse toxoplasmosis models or considering translation of profilin/TLR12 agonism (ihara2024theroleof pages 1-2, koblansky2013recognitionofprofilin pages 1-2).
Evolutionary distribution TLR12 has a patchy, lineage-specific distribution: present in mice and other rodents, lagomorphs, several bats, elephants, equids, rhinoceroses, and tapirs; pseudogenized in many cetaceans and absent from humans and several other mammalian lineages. A 2024 comparative study found functional TLR12 genes and strong sequence conservation across equids/perissodactyls, but extensive lineage-specific loss or pseudogenization elsewhere (stejskalova2024twelvetolllikereceptor pages 8-10, stejskalova2024twelvetolllikereceptor pages 10-12, stejskalova2024twelvetolllikereceptor pages 4-5).

Table: Summary of the experimentally supported molecular function, localization, signaling, cellular expression, knockout phenotype, and evolutionary distribution of mouse TLR12. Qualifications distinguish direct TLR12 evidence from pathway-level inference and cell-type-specific findings.

TLR12 is an essential endolysosomal pattern recognition receptor in mice that detects Toxoplasma gondii profilin, initiating MyD88- and IRF8-dependent IL-12 production. Operating as both TLR11-TLR12 heterodimers and TLR12 homodimers depending on cell type, it plays a nonredundant role in protective immunity against toxoplasmosis. The absence of functional TLR12 in humans highlights species-specific differences in innate immunity and underscores the importance of considering these differences when translating findings from mouse models to human disease.
