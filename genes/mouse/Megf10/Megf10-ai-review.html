Gene Description

Megf10 is the mouse ortholog of the C. elegans engulfment receptor CED-1 and Drosophila Draper. It is a single-pass type I transmembrane class F scavenger receptor with an EMI domain, EGF-like repeats and an NPxY/YxxL cytoplasmic tail. Glial cells, including astrocytes and satellite glial precursors of the dorsal root ganglia, use it to recognize and engulf apoptotic neurons, in part through the eat-me signal C1q. Astrocytes also use it to engulf synapses during synapse elimination. In the retina, homotypic Megf10 signalling repels neighbouring cells of the same type and so spaces starburst amacrine and horizontal cells into mosaics. In skeletal muscle it marks quiescent satellite cells and regulates the balance between satellite-cell proliferation and differentiation, in part through Notch signalling.

Megf10 Is a Receptor for C1Q That Mediates Clearance of Apop... 		REMOVE
Summary: Apoptotic process involved in development (IMP, Iram et al. 2016).
Reason: The phenotype behind this IMP is more TUNEL- and cleaved-caspase-3-positive cells in the P7 cerebellum of Megf10-/- and Megf10+/- mice. The authors interpret this as a clearance defect, not a change in apoptosis. The Figure 1 title in the full text (PMC4863057, read for this review; not in the local cache) says that Megf10 "is necessary for apoptotic cell uptake by astrocytes, and its deficiency results in accumulation of apoptotic cells in the developing CB". Uncleared apoptotic cells accumulate whether or not more cells die. The same paper shows impaired astrocyte phagocytosis ex vivo and gain of phagocytosis on Megf10 expression. That role is captured by engulfment of apoptotic cell (GO:0043652, rows 31-32), which GO's own usage note separates from processes inside the dying cell. The phenotype is more TUNEL- and cleaved-caspase-3-positive cells in the P7 cerebellum. The full text was read at PMC4863057; the local cache has only the abstract.
