Gene Description

Pck1 encodes the cytosolic, GTP-dependent phosphoenolpyruvate carboxykinase (PEPCK-C), a Mn2+-requiring enzyme that converts oxaloacetate and GTP to phosphoenolpyruvate, GDP and CO2. This is the committed step that removes carbon from the tricarboxylic acid cycle (cataplerosis) for glucose and glycerol synthesis. In liver and kidney proximal tubule it drives gluconeogenesis from lactate, amino acids and TCA-cycle intermediates and supports renal ammoniagenesis and acid-base balance. In white and brown adipose tissue it drives glyceroneogenesis, which supplies glycerol 3-phosphate for triglyceride re-esterification. Its expression is under strong hormonal control (induced by glucagon/cAMP and glucocorticoids, repressed by insulin), and acetylation of Lys91 under high glucose shifts it toward the reverse, anaplerotic reaction. Complete loss is lethal shortly after birth, liver-specific loss abolishes hepatic gluconeogenesis from TCA intermediates and causes fasting steatosis, and the kidney compensates. Pck1 is also upregulated in memory CD8+ T cells, where its gluconeogenic flux feeds glycogen and the pentose phosphate pathway. Work in human cancer cells has proposed that the AKT-phosphorylated enzyme acts as a GTP-dependent protein kinase on INSIG1/2, but this has not been examined in mouse.
