Gene Description

PDS1 encodes securin, a largely intrinsically disordered nuclear protein that is the stoichiometric binding partner and inhibitor of the separase Esp1, a cysteine endopeptidase. From S phase until metaphase Pds1 holds Esp1 in a separase-securin complex: its C-terminal separase-interaction segment traverses all Esp1 domains and residues 258-269 occupy the separase active site as an uncleavable pseudosubstrate, blocking access of the cohesin kleisin Scc1/Mcd1. The same interaction is an activating, chaperone-like one, since Pds1 is needed for efficient nuclear accumulation of Esp1, for its delivery to the spindle pole bodies and midzone, and for separase to attain full proteolytic activity after Pds1 is gone. At the metaphase-to-anaphase transition APC/C-Cdc20 ubiquitinates Pds1 through its N-terminal destruction box and the proteasome destroys it, liberating Esp1 to cleave Scc1/Mcd1 and trigger sister-chromatid separation; residual Pds1 is cleared by APC/C-Cdh1 in telophase and its persistence delays mitotic exit. Pds1 is the effector on which the spindle-assembly and DNA-damage checkpoints converge, because Mad2-dependent inhibition of APC/C-Cdc20 and Mec1/Chk1-dependent phosphorylation both stabilise it and thereby arrest cells before anaphase. Cdc28 phosphorylation of Pds1 strengthens Esp1 binding, and the protein shuttles between nucleus and cytoplasm through an NLS and Msn5-dependent export. Pds1 is destroyed in two waves during meiosis, in anaphase I and anaphase II, and pds1 null cells additionally show defects in meiotic recombination and prophase I progression, mild DNA-damage sensitivity and a defect in single-strand-annealing repair. Pds1 shares no recognisable sequence similarity with metazoan securin (PTTG1) or fission-yeast Cut2, but is functionally equivalent to them.

Core Functions

Pseudosubstrate inhibition of the separase Esp1: Pds1 residues 258-269 occupy the cysteine-endopeptidase active site, with Pro263 at the P1 position preventing cleavage, and the extended separase-interaction segment holds Esp1 inactive in the nucleus until APC/C-Cdc20-mediated destruction of Pds1 at anaphase onset; checkpoint-dependent stabilisation of Pds1 prolongs this restraint after DNA damage or spindle defects.

Functional annotation report: Saccharomyces cerevisiae PDS1 / Pds1 securin

Executive summary

The target is correctly identified: PDS1 (ordered locus YDR113C, UniProt P40316) in Saccharomyces cerevisiae S288c encodes Pds1, the budding-yeast securin. The literature consistently uses Pds1 for the stable binding partner and inhibitor of the separase Esp1; no evidence concerning an unrelated same-symbol gene was incorporated. Its experimentally mapped separase-binding region is consistent with the supplied Securin/PF04856 and securin-separation-inhibitor annotations. (ciosk1998anesp1pds1complex pages 1-2, luo2017molecularmechanismfor pages 1-2)

Pds1 is not an enzyme, transporter, or structural subunit of cohesin. Its primary function is that of a regulated, stoichiometric inhibitor and stabilizing/chaperone-like partner of Esp1. Before anaphase, Pds1 binds Esp1, occludes its protease active site, helps maintain the separase complex, and promotes efficient Esp1 accumulation in the nucleus. At anaphase onset, APC/C–Cdc20-dependent ubiquitination and proteasomal destruction of Pds1 release Esp1. Active Esp1 then cleaves the Mcd1/Scc1 cohesin subunit, permitting sister-chromatid separation. (agarwal2002phosphorylationofthe pages 1-2, ciosk1998anesp1pds1complex pages 1-2, luo2017molecularmechanismfor pages 1-2, luo2017molecularmechanismfor pages 2-2)

The dominant functional location is therefore the nucleus, particularly the pre-anaphase nucleus where the Pds1–Esp1 complex controls chromosome cohesion. Pds1 also integrates spindle-assembly and DNA-damage checkpoint signals. A proposed DNA-damage-dependent vacuolar pool has experimental support only from a 2019 bioRxiv preprint and should not replace the nucleus as the established functional localization. (agarwal2002phosphorylationofthe pages 7-8, lemos2019yeastsecurinis pages 14-20, cohenfix1996anaphaseinitiationin pages 5-6)

1. Identity and annotation verification

The supplied identifiers—PDS1, YDR113C, P40316, S288c, and “securin”—are mutually consistent with the protein characterized in direct budding-yeast studies. Ciosk and colleagues isolated Pds1 in a stable complex with a 180-kDa protein identified as Esp1, establishing the defining yeast securin–separase relationship. Later structural work explicitly analyzed the S. cerevisiae Esp1–Pds1 complex. (ciosk1998anesp1pds1complex pages 1-2, luo2017molecularmechanismfor pages 1-2)

The family/domain annotation is also mechanistically appropriate. Rather than forming a compact catalytic domain, the experimentally resolved Pds1 separase-interaction segment (SIS), residues 258–373, is mostly extended, contains only short helical elements, and traverses the length of Esp1. Thus, the InterPro “securin separation inhibitor” designation describes a protein–protein interaction/inhibitory module, not an enzymatic active domain. (luo2017molecularmechanismfor pages 1-2, luo2017molecularmechanismfor pages 2-2, luo2017molecularmechanismfor pages 3-4)

2. Primary molecular function

2.1 Direct inhibition of Esp1 separase

Pds1 binds Esp1 and prevents premature cohesin cleavage. Classical genetic and biochemical work showed that Pds1 destruction is the APC/C’s critical contribution to initiating sister separation: deleting PDS1 bypassed the requirement for APC activity for sister separation, whereas destruction-box-stabilized Pds1 prevented anaphase. Conversely, Pds1 disappeared normally in an esp1-1 mutant, but Scc1 remained chromosome-associated and sisters remained paired, placing functional Esp1 downstream of Pds1 destruction. (cohenfix1996anaphaseinitiationin pages 1-2, ciosk1998anesp1pds1complex pages 3-4, ciosk1998anesp1pds1complex pages 1-2)

This is best interpreted as stoichiometric inhibition, not catalysis by Pds1. Pds1 and Esp1 were reported to be present at broadly similar total abundance before anaphase, with nuclear Pds1 inferred to exceed nuclear Esp1 because Esp1 also occupies the cytoplasm. Increasing ESP1 dosage could overcome Pds1-mediated inhibition: at least 20% of cdc20-3 GAL-ESP1 cells separated sisters within three hours despite high Pds1 and inactive Cdc20. (ciosk1998anesp1pds1complex pages 5-7)

2.2 Atomic mechanism and substrate specificity

Structures of the yeast Esp1–Pds1 complex were refined to 3.0 Å and 2.6 Å. Pds1 residues 258–373 contact all Esp1 domains and bury approximately 3,900 Å² of solvent-accessible surface. Residues 258–269 occupy the separase substrate-binding/active-site region; downstream segments provide extensive stabilizing contacts across Esp1. (luo2017molecularmechanismfor pages 1-2, luo2017molecularmechanismfor pages 2-2, luo2017molecularmechanismfor pages 5-6)

Pds1 behaves as a non-cleaved, substrate-like inhibitor. Its Ile259–Glu260–Ile261 segment mimics substrate-side interactions, but Pro263 replaces the arginine expected at the substrate P1 position. The Pro263 carbonyl lies about 6 Å from catalytic Cys1531 and helps impose an inactive active-site geometry. Accordingly, Pds1 blocks access without undergoing normal Esp1 cleavage. Mutations at residues corresponding to substrate P2, P1, and P1′ positions can convert securin into a separase substrate, experimentally supporting this model. (luo2017molecularmechanismfor pages 2-3, luo2017molecularmechanismfor pages 2-2)

The relevant “substrate specificity” therefore belongs to Esp1, not Pds1: Pds1 inhibits Esp1’s cleavage of cohesin, especially Mcd1/Scc1, by occupying its substrate-recognition site. Pds1 itself catalyzes no reaction. (lianga2018cdk1phosphorylationof pages 1-2, ciosk1998anesp1pds1complex pages 3-4)

2.3 Stabilizing and chaperone-like activity

Pds1 is not merely a removable plug. Contacts outside the catalytic pocket are necessary for stable complex formation and soluble Esp1 production. Pds1 residues 258–316 were required for robust interaction, while active-site binding alone was insufficient. The extended SIS also stabilizes interdomain features of separase, supporting the expert interpretation that securin acts as an inhibitory chaperone or folding/stability partner. This terminology should remain “chaperone-like,” because the strongest direct evidence concerns complex stabilization rather than a separately demonstrated catalytic chaperone cycle. (luo2017molecularmechanismfor pages 1-2, luo2017molecularmechanismfor pages 3-4)

3. Cell-cycle pathway and regulation

3.1 APC/C–Cdc20–Pds1–Esp1–cohesin axis

The central pathway is:

SAC satisfaction → APC/C–Cdc20 activation → Pds1 polyubiquitination → proteasomal Pds1 destruction → Esp1 activation → Mcd1/Scc1 cleavage → sister-chromatid separation and anaphase.

Pds1 contains APC/C-recognition elements in its N-terminal region. Classic experiments identified the destruction-box residues Arg85 and Leu88: R85A/L88A substitutions reduced APC-dependent ubiquitination and stabilized Pds1. Wild-type Pds1 had an approximate half-life of 12 minutes in mitotic extract, compared with >60 minutes following APC depletion or for destruction-box mutants, and >90 minutes in interphase extract. Wild-type protein was ubiquitinated approximately fivefold more efficiently than the mutant with Ubc4 and threefold more efficiently with another tested E2. (cohenfix1996anaphaseinitiationin pages 9-10, cohenfix1996anaphaseinitiationin pages 5-6, cohenfix1996anaphaseinitiationin pages 3-5)

In cells, nuclear Pds1 is detectable from late G1/pre-S through pre-anaphase and disappears at anaphase onset. A nondegradable Pds1 derivative blocks anaphase even while endogenous Pds1 is degraded, demonstrating that the phenotype results from persistent Esp1 inhibition rather than generalized APC/C poisoning. (cohenfix1996anaphaseinitiationin pages 9-10, cohenfix1996anaphaseinitiationin pages 5-6, cohenfix1996anaphaseinitiationin pages 7-9)

3.2 Pds1 is central but not the only Esp1 control

A 2018 analysis refined the simple on/off model. Pds1 degradation cooperates with Cdk1-dependent Esp1 phosphorylation, while PP2A–Cdc55 and Slk19 oppose Esp1 activation and act redundantly with Pds1 around pericentric chromatin. Combining inducible Pds1 depletion with phosphomimetic ESP1-3D, cdc55Δ, or slk19Δ caused premature Mcd1 degradation, spindle elongation, disrupted pericentric cohesin, and chromosome mis-segregation. More than half of the tested mutant cells entered anaphase soon after mitotic entry, and chromosome V segregation approached random behavior in some backgrounds. Pds1 is therefore the principal securin brake, but the commitment to anaphase is produced by a regulatory network rather than by Pds1 abundance alone. (lianga2018cdk1phosphorylationof pages 17-18, lianga2018cdk1phosphorylationof pages 1-2)

4. Localization

4.1 Established localization: pre-anaphase nucleus

The well-supported functional localization is nuclear. Immunofluorescence detected Pds1 in nuclei from late G1 through pre-anaphase and not during anaphase or telophase, consistent with timed destruction. Meiotic studies have likewise quantified myc-tagged Pds1 in nuclei, using 50–100 cells per meiotic stage in one analysis. (jonak2017apcccdc20mediatesdeprotection pages 12-18, cohenfix1996anaphaseinitiationin pages 5-6)

Pds1 also controls where Esp1 acts. Cdc28/CDK phosphorylation of Pds1 promotes efficient Pds1–Esp1 association and Esp1 nuclear accumulation. In the temperature-sensitive pds1-38 mutant, mutant Pds1 remained nuclear at 37°C, but Esp1 failed to concentrate efficiently in the nucleus. Thus, Pds1 enters or remains in the nucleus independently of this defect, while its productive association with Esp1 is needed to deliver or retain separase there. This localization-promoting activity coexists with catalytic inhibition. (agarwal2002phosphorylationofthe pages 2-3, agarwal2002phosphorylationofthe pages 7-8)

4.2 Conditional vacuolar localization: provisional evidence

A 2019 bioRxiv preprint reported partial vacuolar localization of Pds1-GFP and Esp1-mCherry after an HO-induced double-strand break. Genetic experiments implicated the alkaline-phosphatase endosomal pathway and Crm1-dependent export rather than core autophagy; COPI/COPII perturbations and ER stress altered Pds1 abundance and checkpoint behavior. Adaptation assays used 50 G1 cells and at least three replicates. Because this work is a preprint and describes a stress-dependent subpopulation, vacuolar trafficking should be annotated as conditional/proposed, not as Pds1’s principal localization. (lemos2019yeastsecurinis pages 14-20)

5. Checkpoint functions

5.1 Spindle-assembly checkpoint

Unattached or improperly tensioned kinetochores activate the spindle-assembly checkpoint (SAC), which restrains APC/C–Cdc20. Pds1 consequently remains stable, Esp1 stays inhibited, and metaphase is prolonged. Pds1 abundance is therefore both a mechanistic effector and a practical biochemical readout of SAC activity. (agarwal2002phosphorylationofthe pages 7-8, helsen2024spindlearchitectureconstrainsa pages 7-8)

A strong recent application appeared in August 2024. Helsen et al. compared engineered S. cerevisiae strains with three fused chromosomes to the native 16-chromosome karyotype. Western blotting after G1 release showed a 5–10-minute delay and approximately 15% higher Pds1 at the end of the cell cycle in the three-chromosome strain. The phenotype was rescued by MAD2 deletion, supporting the conclusion that too few kinetochore–microtubule attachments alter spindle-force balance, activate the SAC, and delay Pds1 destruction. This work did not redefine Pds1’s molecular function; it used established Pds1 biology to reveal how spindle architecture constrains karyotype evolution. Publication: Nature Cell Biology 26, 1496–1503, August 2024, https://doi.org/10.1038/s41556-024-01485-w. (helsen2024spindlearchitectureconstrainsa pages 7-8, helsen2024spindlearchitectureconstrainsa pages 6-7, helsen2024spindlearchitectureconstrains pages 7-11)

5.2 DNA-damage checkpoint

DNA damage stabilizes Pds1 and prevents premature nuclear division. This is distinct from Cdc28-dependent phosphorylation that promotes Esp1 binding/localization: damage-induced Pds1 phosphorylation depends on the Mec1/Chk1 checkpoint branch and is required for effective damage-induced arrest. Pds1 thereby converts DNA-damage signaling into direct inhibition of the chromosome-separation machinery. (agarwal2002phosphorylationofthe pages 1-2, agarwal2002phosphorylationofthe pages 2-3, agarwal2002phosphorylationofthe pages 7-8)

Recent 2024 work on prolonged arrest showed that one persistent double-strand break can arrest budding yeast for approximately 12–15 hours before adaptation, whereas two persistent breaks produce a sustained arrest. Chk1 was particularly important for prolonged maintenance, and after approximately 15 hours arrest from two breaks became dependent on selected SAC factors. Although that study addresses the checkpoint network more broadly rather than discovering a new Pds1 biochemical activity, its results reinforce the current view that damage-induced mitotic arrest is dynamically maintained and eventually handed between checkpoint systems upstream of the Pds1–Esp1 transition. Publication: Zhou et al., eLife 13, December 2024, https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.94334.

6. Mitotic and meiotic biological processes

In mitosis, Pds1 prevents sister-chromatid separation until all replicated chromosomes are properly attached and the genome is ready for segregation. Its immediate processes are negative regulation of Esp1 protease activity, control of cohesin cleavage, metaphase-to-anaphase transition, spindle elongation timing, and maintenance of chromosome segregation fidelity. (lianga2018cdk1phosphorylationof pages 1-2, ciosk1998anesp1pds1complex pages 3-4, ciosk1998anesp1pds1complex pages 1-2)

In meiosis, the same core principle is reused: APC/C–Cdc20-dependent securin destruction coordinates separase activation with the two successive segregation divisions. Nuclear Pds1 has been measured across meiotic stages, and APC/C–Cdc20 regulation is coupled to stepwise cohesin removal and centromeric protection/deprotection. The present evidence supports a conserved regulatory role but does not justify assigning Pds1 a separate meiosis-specific biochemical activity. (jonak2017apcccdc20mediatesdeprotection pages 12-18)

7. Mutant phenotypes and functional evidence

The most informative perturbations are mechanistically coherent:

  • Nondegradable/destruction-box Pds1: persistent inhibition of Esp1 and failure to initiate anaphase. (cohenfix1996anaphaseinitiationin pages 9-10, cohenfix1996anaphaseinitiationin pages 7-9)
  • pds1Δ: can bypass APC defects for sister separation, showing that Pds1 is the relevant inhibitory APC substrate, but it does not eliminate the requirement for functional Esp1; esp1-1 pds1Δ is inviable. (cohenfix1996anaphaseinitiationin pages 1-2, ciosk1998anesp1pds1complex pages 3-4)
  • pds1-38: weakens Esp1 association and prevents efficient Esp1 nuclear concentration at restrictive temperature despite continued nuclear localization of Pds1 itself. (agarwal2002phosphorylationofthe pages 7-8)
  • Pds1 depletion plus loss of parallel Esp1 restraints: causes premature cohesin destruction, spindle elongation, and chromosome mis-segregation. (lianga2018cdk1phosphorylationof pages 17-18, lianga2018cdk1phosphorylationof pages 1-2)
  • Persistent SAC activity: stabilizes Pds1 and delays anaphase; engineered low-chromosome strains provide a 2024 real-world experimental example. (helsen2024spindlearchitectureconstrainsa pages 7-8, helsen2024spindlearchitectureconstrains pages 7-11)

8. Current applications and translational relevance

Pds1 is widely useful as an experimental metaphase/SAC reporter: its disappearance reports APC/C–Cdc20 activation and imminent separase activation. It is also manipulated through destruction-box mutants, auxin-inducible depletion, epitope tagging, and fluorescent fusion proteins to dissect chromosome segregation, cohesin regulation, checkpoint adaptation, and spindle mechanics. The 2024 karyotype-engineering study demonstrates a current implementation in synthetic-genome and experimental-evolution research. (lianga2018cdk1phosphorylationof pages 17-18, helsen2024spindlearchitectureconstrainsa pages 7-8, helsen2024spindlearchitectureconstrainsa pages 6-7)

The yeast Pds1–Esp1 system additionally provides a high-resolution model for eukaryotic securin–separase regulation. Separase misregulation is relevant to chromosome instability and cancer, but yeast Pds1 itself is not a human therapeutic target. Its principal value is mechanistic: the yeast complex revealed how securin can simultaneously block the active site, stabilize separase, and be removed abruptly by regulated proteolysis. (luo2017molecularmechanismfor pages 1-2, luo2017molecularmechanismfor pages 2-2, luo2017molecularmechanismfor pages 3-4)

9. Evidence-weighted annotation

Recommended primary annotation: Pds1 is the nuclear securin of budding yeast, a regulated stoichiometric inhibitor and stabilizing partner of Esp1/separase. It prevents premature Mcd1/Scc1 cohesin cleavage and sister-chromatid separation. APC/C–Cdc20-dependent destruction of Pds1 at anaphase onset releases Esp1, while spindle-assembly and DNA-damage checkpoints delay this destruction or stabilize Pds1. Cdc28-dependent Pds1 phosphorylation also promotes productive Pds1–Esp1 association and efficient Esp1 nuclear localization. (agarwal2002phosphorylationofthe pages 1-2, cohenfix1996anaphaseinitiationin pages 9-10, ciosk1998anesp1pds1complex pages 1-2, luo2017molecularmechanismfor pages 1-2)

Localization annotation: nucleus from late G1/S through metaphase/pre-anaphase; lost at anaphase through proteolysis. Conditional endomembrane/vacuolar trafficking after DNA damage is provisional, preprint-level evidence. (lemos2019yeastsecurinis pages 14-20, cohenfix1996anaphaseinitiationin pages 5-6)

Confidence: high for identity, Esp1 binding/inhibition, APC-dependent degradation, nuclear function, and control of cohesin cleavage; moderate for a generalized chaperone label; low-to-moderate for vacuolar trafficking until peer-reviewed replication. The 2023–2024 literature largely extends the application of established Pds1 mechanisms rather than changing this core functional annotation.

PDS1 (securin, P40316) - curation notes

Identity

  • YDR113C, 373 aa, largely disordered (MobiDB regions 1-27, 177-278); N-terminal D-box
    RLPL 85-88; C-terminal separase-interaction segment 258-373 resolved in PDB 5U1S/5U1T.
  • No PANTHER family (PTTG1 and Cut2 sit in PTHR10418; Pds1 is unclassified) - so no IBA
    rows and no PAINT node to argue with. Functional equivalence to PTTG1/Cut2 is by
    mechanism, not sequence.
  • Not in gocams/index.tsv; cited as a securin representative member in
    modules/metaphase_anaphase_transition_and_mitotic_exit.yaml with MF GO:0004869.

Mechanism synthesis

  • Founding complex paper: "Pds1p forms a stable complex with a 180 kDa protein called
    Esp1p, which is essential for the dissociation of Scc1p from sister chromatids and for
    their separation" and the APC acts "by liberating the sister-separating Esp1 protein
    from its inhibitor Pds1p" PMID:9635435.
  • Dual mechanism: "securin, while present, directly inhibits the proteolytic activity of
    separase. Securin prevents the binding of separase to its substrates." and "securin is
    required and sufficient to cause accumulation of separase in the nucleus"; "Securin
    also ensures that separase gains full proteolytic activity in anaphase." PMID:12123570.
  • Structure: "residues 258-269 of securin are located in the separase active site,
    illuminating the mechanism of inhibition"; Pro263 replaces the P1 Arg so securin is not
    cleaved; "contacts outside the separase active site are crucial for stabilizing the
    complex"; "securin likely helps to stabilize the overall structure of separase"
    PMID:28146474.
  • Localisation: "As cells progressed through S-phase, Pds1GFP protein accumulated almost
    exclusively in the nucleus."; a fraction "remains at the spindle pole bodies and the
    spindle midzone in anaphase cells"; "Association with Pds1 occurs during S phase and is
    required for efficient nuclear targeting of Esp1." PMID:11149918.
  • Shuttling: Pds1 among proteins "displaying CDK1- and cell cycle-regulated nuclear
    transport"; Msn5 mediates export PMID:19520826.
  • Cdc28 phosphorylation (S277, S292) "is important for efficient binding of Pds1 to Esp1
    and for promoting the nuclear localization of Esp1" PMID:12050115.
  • Late mitosis: "securin still inhibits separase to repress mitotic exit in anaphase";
    APC/C-Cdh1 clears it at telophase PMID:27418100.
  • DNA damage: "the Saccharomyces cerevisiae securin Pds1 blocks anaphase promotion by
    inhibiting ESP1-dependent degradation of cohesins"; pds1 is epistatic with rad51,
    synergistic with rad9, "defective in SSA" PMID:15533838; "scc1-15A pds1Δ double
    mutant, however, exhibits marked sensitivity to the DNA-damaging agent phleomycin"
    PMID:27325700.
  • Meiosis: "Pds1p destruction is required for metaphase I-anaphase I transition";
    "Pds1p is required for recombination in both double-strand-break formation and
    synaptonemal complex assembly"; Mcd1 "precociously destroyed" in meiotic pds1 cells
    PMID:19001291; "Two waves of securin (Pds1) destruction were detected in anaphase I
    and II" PMID:38413836.
  • Deep research (falcon) agrees: stoichiometric inhibitor plus chaperone-like partner,
    nucleus as functional site; the vacuolar-trafficking claim is preprint-only and was not
    used.

  its delivery to the spindle pole bodies and midzone, and for separase to

  N-terminal destruction box and the proteasome destroys it, liberating Esp1 to
  cleave Scc1/Mcd1 and trigger sister-chromatid separation; residual Pds1 is
  cleared by APC/C-Cdh1 in telophase and its persistence delays mitotic exit.
  Pds1 is the effector on which the spindle-assembly and DNA-damage checkpoints
  converge, because Mad2-dependent inhibition of APC/C-Cdc20 and
  Mec1/Chk1-dependent phosphorylation both stabilise it and thereby arrest
  cells before anaphase. Cdc28 phosphorylation of Pds1 strengthens Esp1
  binding, and the protein shuttles between nucleus and cytoplasm through an
  NLS and Msn5-dependent export. Pds1 is destroyed in two waves during meiosis, in anaphase

  recombination and prophase I progression, mild DNA-damage sensitivity and
  a defect in single-strand-annealing repair. Pds1 shares no recognisable
