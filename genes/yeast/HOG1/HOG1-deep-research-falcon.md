# HOG1 (P32485/YLR113W) functional-annotation report

## Executive conclusion

The requested protein is correctly identified as **Hog1**, the stress-activated mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK) at the output of the **high-osmolarity glycerol (HOG) pathway** in *Saccharomyces cerevisiae* S288c. The supplied identifiers—**HOG1**, synonym **SSK3**, locus **YLR113W**, and UniProt **P32485**—are consistent with literature describing the budding-yeast TGY-family MAPK and functional homolog of mammalian p38. No conclusions below were transferred from a same-named protein in another organism.

Hog1 is a **Ser/Thr protein kinase and signaling effector**, not a transporter or structural protein. Its primary biochemical reaction is ATP-dependent phosphorylation of protein substrates:

**ATP + protein Ser/Thr–OH → ADP + protein Ser/Thr–OPO₃²⁻.**

Its defining physiological role is to convert an increase in extracellular osmolarity into coordinated cytoplasmic and nuclear responses that restore water balance and turgor. These include glycerol synthesis and retention, transcriptional reprogramming, translational and metabolic control, transient cell-cycle delay, and insulation of the HOG module from other yeast MAPK pathways. Pbs2 activates Hog1 by dual phosphorylation of **Thr174 and Tyr176** in its TGY activation loop. This identity and mechanism align with the supplied MAPK, MAPK-HOG-like, and Sty1/Hog1 domain-family annotations. (westfall2006analysisofmitogenactivated pages 1-1, warmka2001ptc1atype pages 1-2)

## 1. Identity, family, and substrate specificity

Hog1 belongs to the conserved stress-activated MAPK/p38 class. Like other MAPKs, it has a protein-kinase catalytic domain and requires phosphorylation of conserved threonine and tyrosine residues in the activation loop for full activity. The relevant sequence in Hog1 is **TGY**, distinguishing this stress-activated MAPK group from TEY-type ERKs and TPY-type JNKs. Pbs2 is the immediate upstream MAPK kinase and phosphorylates Hog1 at Thr174 and Tyr176. (warmka2001ptc1atype pages 1-2, westfall2006analysisofmitogenactivated pages 1-1)

Hog1’s physiological specificity is best understood as **docking- and localization-assisted Ser/Thr phosphorylation**, rather than unrestricted phosphorylation of soluble proteins. Recruitment to scaffolds, promoters, channels, and nuclear targets places the kinase near appropriate substrates. A simple universal sequence motif is therefore insufficient to predict genuine substrates; direct interaction, stress-dependent phosphorylation, and Hog1-dependence are stronger evidence. The retrieved sources establish multiple direct targets but do not provide enough evidence to assign a more restrictive, experimentally universal consensus beyond its MAPK-like Ser/Thr specificity.

Well-supported downstream targets and mechanisms include:

* **Rgc2**, a positive regulator of the plasma-membrane glycerol channel Fps1. Fps1 recruits Hog1 to its N-terminal cytoplasmic region, allowing Hog1 to multiply phosphorylate Rgc2. Rgc2 is then displaced from Fps1, causing channel closure and glycerol retention. (lee2013mapkhog1closes pages 1-2)
* **Net1**, a nucleolar Cdc14-binding protein. Hog1 phosphorylation of Net1 changes Net1–Cdc14 regulation and delays mitotic exit during osmostress, protecting telomere and rDNA segregation. (tognetti2020hog1activationdelays pages 1-2)
* Transcription-associated targets and partners, including Sko1, Hot1, Msn2/Msn4-associated machinery, and chromatin/transcription complexes. Hog1 thereby acts not merely upstream of transcription factors but at stress-responsive chromatin and transcriptional machinery. (du2025therelationshipmammalian pages 6-7, saito2004regulationofthe pages 5-6)
* Cell-cycle regulators and replication machinery. Established Hog1-dependent controls involve Sic1, Whi5, Msa1, Clb5/6, Mrc1, Swe1, and Net1, allowing phase-specific slowing in G1, S, G2, and mitosis. The Net1 study provides especially direct evidence for substrate phosphorylation. (tognetti2020hog1activationdelays pages 1-2)

## 2. Pathway architecture and activation

Two canonical osmosensing routes converge on Pbs2 and Hog1.

### Sln1 branch

Under relatively low osmotic stress, the membrane histidine kinase Sln1 participates in an Sln1–Ypd1–Ssk1 phosphorelay that restrains signaling. Increased osmolarity reduces this inhibitory phosphorelay; unphosphorylated Ssk1 activates the related MAPKKKs Ssk2 and Ssk22, which activate Pbs2, followed by Hog1. (saito2004regulationofthe pages 1-2, warmka2001ptc1atype pages 1-2)

### Sho1 branch

The second route is assembled around the membrane protein Sho1 and mucin-like osmosensors such as Msb2. Cdc42-associated Ste20, adaptor Ste50, and MAPKKK Ste11 transmit the signal to Pbs2. Pbs2 therefore serves both as the common MAPKK and an organizing component that helps preserve signaling specificity. (du2025therelationshipmammalian pages 6-7, westfall2006analysisofmitogenactivated pages 1-1)

Experimental ranges reported in one study were approximately **0.1–0.4 M NaCl** for prominent Sln1-route activation and **0.5–1.0 M NaCl** where Sho1 also contributes; these should not be treated as absolute physiological thresholds because activation depends on strain, solute, dose, and experimental design. Very severe stress—**1.4 M NaCl**—produced delayed, sustained Hog1 phosphorylation and nuclear accumulation and suggested an additional Pbs2-directed input independent of the canonical Sho1 and Sln1 sensors. (wuytswinkel2000responseofsaccharomyces pages 1-2, westfall2006analysisofmitogenactivated pages 1-1)

## 3. Cellular localization

Hog1 functions in both the **cytoplasm/plasma-membrane signaling environment** and the **nucleus**.

In unstressed cells, Hog1 continuously cycles between cytoplasmic and nuclear compartments. Hyperosmotic challenge causes rapid, phosphorylation-associated nuclear accumulation; the importin homolog Nmd5 contributes to nuclear import. Nuclear residence is reinforced by interactions with targets such as Msn2/Msn4-associated factors, whereas return to the cytoplasm after adaptation depends on Hog1 kinase activity. Thus, its distribution reflects at least three processes: activation-dependent accumulation, retention by nuclear targets, and kinase-dependent export. (saito2004regulationofthe pages 4-4, reiser1999kinaseactivitydependentnuclear pages 1-2, westfall2006analysisofmitogenactivated pages 1-1)

The literature contains some assay-dependent disagreement over whether catalytic activity itself is required for import. K52M experiments indicate that activation-loop phosphorylation can permit import despite catalytic inactivity, whereas acute inhibition of an analog-sensitive allele indicated that ongoing catalytic activity is necessary. The robust consensus is that activation/phosphorylation is closely coupled to nuclear enrichment and that catalysis is required for productive signaling and timely export. (saito2004regulationofthe pages 4-4, westfall2006analysisofmitogenactivated pages 1-1)

Outside the nucleus, Hog1 acts near the plasma membrane on the Fps1–Rgc system and controls metabolism, ion/glycerol transport, and translation. In the nucleus and nucleolus it regulates transcription, chromatin, replication/cell-cycle targets, and Net1–Cdc14 signaling. It is not secreted and has no extracellular catalytic role supported by the reviewed evidence. (tognetti2020hog1activationdelays pages 1-2, lee2013mapkhog1closes pages 1-2)

## 4. Core biological output: osmoadaptation

The immediate consequence of hyperosmotic shock is water loss, reduced cell volume, and impaired growth. Hog1 coordinates recovery by increasing synthesis and retention of the compatible osmolyte **glycerol**. Transcriptional targets include **GPD1/GPD2** and **GPP1/GPP2**, which support glycerol biosynthesis, as well as **STL1**, **CTT1**, and **HSP12** and other stress-response genes. One widely cited estimate is that activated Hog1 affects transcription of approximately **600 genes**, although this number includes indirect outputs and varies with condition and analysis method. (westfall2006analysisofmitogenactivated pages 1-1, du2025therelationshipmammalian pages 6-7)

Retention is as important as synthesis. Fps1 is an aquaglyceroporin that opens or closes in response to osmolarity. Following **1.8 M sorbitol**, most Rgc2 dissociated from Fps1 within **10 seconds** and remained dissociated for at least **20 minutes**; this displacement required Hog1 and led to channel closure. This is strong mechanistic evidence that Hog1 rapidly prevents glycerol loss before slower transcriptional responses fully develop. (lee2013mapkhog1closes pages 1-2)

Hog1 also maintains pathway fidelity. Acute analog-sensitive inhibition showed that catalytic activity is continuously required to prevent osmostress-activated Ste11 from inappropriately engaging the pheromone-response and invasive-growth MAPK pathways. In the absence of HOG1, osmotic stress can produce pheromone-like “shmoo” morphology, illustrating the biological cost of failed pathway insulation. (galello2024thecamppkasignalling pages 4-6, westfall2006analysisofmitogenactivated pages 1-1)

## 5. Cell-cycle and genome-protection functions

Transient growth arrest is an adaptive component rather than a nonspecific consequence. Hog1 adjusts multiple cell-cycle phases so that biosynthesis and chromosome duplication/segregation do not proceed while volume and metabolism are perturbed. During early mitosis, Hog1 directly phosphorylates Net1 and delays Cdc14-dependent mitotic exit. A Net1 variant that could not be phosphorylated by Hog1 showed reduced viability under osmostress, connecting the biochemical event to fitness. (tognetti2020hog1activationdelays pages 1-2)

This pathway also delays G1/S progression through Sic1 stabilization and regulation of Whi5/Msa1 and G1/S cyclins, acts on replication factor Mrc1 during S phase, and influences Swe1 and cyclin regulation at G2/M. These are broader outputs, but they reinforce the precise interpretation that Hog1 is a central coordinator that temporarily couples cell-cycle progression to completion of osmoadaptation. (tognetti2020hog1activationdelays pages 1-2)

## 6. Signal termination and adaptation

Hog1 activation must be transient. Tyrosine phosphatases **Ptp2/Ptp3** and PP2C-family phosphatases **Ptc1/Ptc2/Ptc3** contribute to shutoff, with compartmental localization and docking affecting their roles. Ptp2 is predominantly associated with nuclear regulation and Ptp3 with cytoplasmic control. (saito2004regulationofthe pages 4-4, saito2004regulationofthe pages 1-2)

Ptc1 provides direct biochemical evidence: it dephosphorylates Hog1’s activation-loop phosphothreonine but not phosphotyrosine and acts on Hog1 rather than Pbs2. Osmotic stress increased Hog1 kinase activity approximately **12-fold** in wild type, whereas PTC1 overexpression restricted the increase to approximately **3-fold**. Deleting PTC1 elevated basal activity and prolonged the stress response. These findings establish phosphatase action as a feedback/adaptation mechanism rather than merely constitutive suppression. (warmka2001ptc1atype pages 1-2)

## 7. Recent developments and current expert interpretation

The most directly relevant recent authoritative source retrieved was the review by **Galello et al., published 15 March 2024**. It emphasizes that HOG-MAPK should no longer be viewed as an isolated linear osmosensing pathway. Instead, it operates in a coordinated network with **cAMP–PKA** and the **cell-wall-integrity pathway**, integrating transcription, translation, protein activity, metabolic flux, and transient growth arrest. Hog1 also inhibits inappropriate Ste11-dependent crosstalk into pheromone and filamentous-growth MAPK pathways. [DOI URL: https://doi.org/10.15698/mic2024.03.818] (galello2024thecamppkasignalling pages 4-6, galello2024thecamppkasignalling pages 1-2)

An important expert-level qualification is that recent work mostly refines network integration and context dependence; it does not overturn the established core mechanism of Sln1/Sho1 → Pbs2 → Hog1 → glycerol-centered adaptation. The 2024 review also stresses that a complete description of signal integration remains unfinished. Consequently, inferred phosphoproteomic targets should not be treated as equivalent to substrates validated by direct phosphorylation and functional rescue. (galello2024thecamppkasignalling pages 1-2, lee2013mapkhog1closes pages 1-2)

## 8. Applications and real-world implementation

### Industrial fermentation

*S. cerevisiae* is used in bread, alcoholic beverages, bioethanol, and other food and non-food fermentations. Industrial cells encounter changing sugar/osmotic pressure, oxygen, pH, ethanol, nutrient availability, and temperature. The 2024 review argues that fermentation performance depends on coordinated stress adaptation through HOG-MAPK, cAMP–PKA, and cell-wall-integrity signaling; understanding this crosstalk is therefore relevant to selecting or engineering robust production strains. (galello2024thecamppkasignalling pages 1-2)

HOG1 is consequently useful as:

1. **A strain-phenotyping marker:** Hog1 phosphorylation and nuclear translocation report acute osmotic signaling.
2. **An engineering node:** tuning activation, glycerol metabolism, or Fps1 regulation can alter osmotic tolerance and carbon partitioning. Such interventions require care because excess glycerol can divert carbon away from ethanol or product formation, while insufficient glycerol compromises viability.
3. **A systems-biology model:** fluorescent Hog1 reporters and analog-sensitive alleles enable quantitative study of signaling dynamics, feedback, localization, and pathway insulation. (reiser1999kinaseactivitydependentnuclear pages 1-2, westfall2006analysisofmitogenactivated pages 1-1, lee2013mapkhog1closes pages 1-2)
4. **A comparative stress-kinase model:** Hog1 is a functional homolog of mammalian p38 and has become a tractable model for conserved stress-activated MAPK principles. This does not make yeast Hog1 a human therapeutic target, but it makes its mechanism useful for fundamental kinase research. (du2025therelationshipmammalian pages 6-7, tognetti2020hog1activationdelays pages 1-2)

No recent source retrieved supplied a single validated percentage improvement in fermentation productivity attributable solely to manipulating native S288c HOG1. Claims of industrial benefit should therefore be framed as mechanistically well motivated but dependent on strain, substrate, product, and process conditions.

## 9. Evidence-strength assessment

**High-confidence annotation:** terminal HOG-pathway MAPK; Pbs2-dependent Thr174/Tyr176 activation; cytoplasmic and nuclear functions; glycerol synthesis/retention; stress-induced nuclear accumulation; Rgc2 phosphorylation/Fps1 closure; transcriptional regulation; pathway insulation; phosphatase-mediated shutoff. These conclusions are supported by direct genetics, kinase assays, microscopy, analog-sensitive inhibition, and substrate-focused experiments. (reiser1999kinaseactivitydependentnuclear pages 1-2, warmka2001ptc1atype pages 1-2, westfall2006analysisofmitogenactivated pages 1-1, lee2013mapkhog1closes pages 1-2)

**Context-dependent conclusions:** exact branch thresholds, the requirement for catalytic activity during nuclear import, and responses to extreme versus moderate osmotic stress vary by assay and condition. (wuytswinkel2000responseofsaccharomyces pages 1-2, saito2004regulationofthe pages 4-4, westfall2006analysisofmitogenactivated pages 1-1)

**Inference requiring further validation:** many candidate phosphoproteomic substrates and broad non-osmotic phenotypes may be real but should not be assigned as primary molecular functions without direct phosphorylation and functional evidence.
