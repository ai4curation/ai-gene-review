MAL33 (P38157): is S288C Mal33p disabled by a specific sequence change relative to Mal63p?

Gene: MAL33 / YBR297W, Saccharomyces cerevisiae S288C (NCBITaxon:559292), UniProt P38157. Hypothesis slug: kgap-mal33-disabling-changes. Source: genes/yeast/MAL33/MAL33-ai-review.yaml, free-text focus. Three rounds of analysis, 13 recorded findings, 17 papers reviewed.

Summary

The seed hypothesis asks for a specific disabling sequence change in S288C Mal33p relative to the functional activator Mal63p, named from among truncations, frameshift-derived termini, or substitutions in the Zn(2)Cys(6) domain, the activation domain, or the maltose-responsive regulatory region. No such change exists in the sequence. Mal33p is a full-length 468-residue Zn(2)Cys(6) activator, colinear with the 470-residue Mal63p, with all six zinc ligands and all four Gal4-equivalent base-contacting positions intact. Its single indel, a two-residue deletion of Mal63p Met350-Asn351, is also carried by Mal23-Cp, the constitutive and transcriptionally hyperactive MAL23-C allele, so that change is compatible with elevated activator function. Mal13p (P53338) is 473 residues and equally full length. Every lesion class the hypothesis names is excluded by direct sequence comparison.

Three further results close the remaining room for a coding explanation. Residues 317-468 of Mal33p are 100% identical to Mal23-Cp, and all 20 differences between the inducible Mal23p and its constitutive derivative lie between residues 325 and 378, so the Mal33p-type maltose-responsive domain confers gain of function when placed in a working activator backbone. Three S. cerevisiae strains (CEN.PK113-7D, PE-2, P301) encode a Mal33p with zero amino-acid differences from the S288C reference, and all nine natural Mal33p-class proteins examined are 468 residues, so the S288C protein carries no strain-specific change. Using the two genuinely non-functional alleles in the family as positive controls, the only residue-level correlate of loss of function is a four-residue block at positions 355, 357, 358 and 364, Ser-Ser-Ser-Lys in both dead alleles and Asn-Asn-Asn-Glu in all three functional ones. Mal33p and Mal13p carry the functional residues at all four.

The verdict is partially supported, with the mechanistic core refuted. The premise that S288C does not supply MAL-activator function from MAL33 or MAL13 holds as a strain phenotype, but round three traced SGD's "nonfunctional in genomic reference strain S288C" to three locus-structure and genome-sequencing papers with no activity assay on the S288C ORF, and found that SGD separately records a carbon-source utilization phenotype for the S288C mal33 deletant. Round three also corrected an error in this report's own first two rounds: UniProt E9P8G3, used as the wild-type Mal63p reference, is the mal63-13 allele that its GenBank record annotates as non-functional. The correction moved every identity figure by under one percentage point and changed no conclusion, and it supplied the positive control that makes the 355-364 result interpretable.

Executive judgment

Partially supported, with the mechanistic core refuted.

The premise, that S288C MAL33 and MAL13 do not supply MAL-activator function in that strain, holds at the level of strain phenotype and database annotation, though round three found that the annotation itself traces to three locus-structure and genome-sequencing papers (PMID: 2548922, PMID: 2549370, PMID: 7483839) with no activity assay on the S288C ORF, and that SGD records a carbon-source utilization phenotype for the S288C mal33 deletant (PMID: 24721214). The mechanism proposed in the seed hypothesis does not hold. Three of the four candidate lesions named in the hypothesis are directly excluded by sequence, and the fourth cannot be resolved to a residue.

  1. Truncation: absent. Mal33p is a full-length 468-residue protein colinear with the 470-residue Mal63p. Its last twelve residues (NDDSKDEDDIIP) are identical to those of the active Mal23p and of the constitutive Mal23-Cp allele. Mal13p is 473 residues and equally full length.

  2. Frameshift-derived terminus: absent. Both S288C proteins terminate in the aligned position with no frame-shifted tail. The alignment covers 468/470 and 473/470 positions with no internal gap larger than five residues.

  3. Zn(2)Cys(6) lesion: absent. All six zinc ligands (Mal63p C8, C11, C18, C24, C27, C34) are present with identical spacing in Mal33p, Mal13p, and in every comparator, including the non-functional mal64p. The Gal4-equivalent base-contacting residues (Mal63p R12, R14, R15, K17) are unchanged in both S288C proteins.

  4. A named disabling substitution: not identifiable. Mal33p differs from Mal63p at 136 of 470 aligned positions, distributed across every mapped functional domain. The DNA-binding domain (residues 1-100) is the most diverged region, modestly enriched for differences (39/100 versus 97/370; Fisher odds ratio 1.80, p = 0.018), which is the opposite polarity to the active alleles. That is a regional signal, not a residue-level one. All 39 differences in 1-100 fall at positions invariant among the three active alleles, so conservation gives no discrimination among them.

  5. The maltose-responsive C-terminus of Mal33p is a gain-of-function segment. All 20 differences between inducible Mal23p and constitutive Mal23-Cp lie in residues 325-378. Over residues 300-468, Mal23-Cp matches Mal33p at 165/169 positions (97.6%) and its own Mal23p parent at only 150/169 (88.8%); Mal33p residues 317-468 are 100% identical to Mal23-Cp. The MAL23-C allele is a recombinant carrying a MAL33-type regulatory domain, which matches the ectopic-recombination mechanism reported for this family. A segment that makes an activator hyperactive cannot be the disabling change. Any coding lesion would have to lie in residues 1-316.

  6. The S288C Mal33p sequence is the ordinary species-wide allele. Across nine natural Mal33p-class proteins, three strains encode a protein with zero differences from the S288C reference: CEN.PK113-7D (EIW12238.1), PE-2 (CAF1547419.1) and P301 (EWG92613.1). RM11-1a, EC1118, P283 and R103 differ by 4 to 8 residues; the S. paradoxus ortholog by 57. All nine are 468 residues, so the two-residue shortening relative to Mal63p is the MAL3-type length, not an S288C event. CEN.PK113-7D grows on maltose. The S288C protein therefore carries no change specific to S288C or to the maltose-negative phenotype, and the 136 differences from Mal63p are paralog divergence between the MAL3-locus and MAL6-locus activators.

A seventh line of evidence is negative and is reported as such. AlphaFold models add nothing: the Zn(2)Cys(6) motif superposes on Mal63p at 0.09-0.30 Å in all six alleles, and the large RMSD over residues 1-100 (Mal33p 15.87 Å) is matched by the functional constitutive Mal23-Cp (11.09 Å, 98% sequence identity over that region), so the spread tracks a low-confidence hinge angle rather than function. Mean pLDDT differs by at most 4 points between functional and non-functional alleles.

  1. At the positions where a MAL activator is actually known to break, Mal33p carries the functional residue. Round three identified a genuinely disabling allele with its changes mapped: mal63-13 (GenBank AF003003, UniProt E9P8G3), annotated in its own source record as a non-functional mutant of MAL63. It differs from wild-type Mal63p (GenBank M36537, 470 aa) at only 7 positions, 98.5% identity, and 6 of the 7 lie in the maltose-responsive domain 283-470. Four of them, 355, 357, 358 and 364, separate the panel perfectly: wild-type Mal63p, inducible Mal23p and constitutive Mal23-Cp all carry Asn-Asn-Asn-Glu, while both non-functional alleles, mal63-13p and mal64p, carry Ser-Ser-Ser-Lys. Mal33p and Mal13p carry Asn-Asn-Asn-Glu, the functional pattern. The block sits inside residues 325-378, the same window that holds all 20 inducible-to-constitutive differences in MAL23. Two cautions apply: 3 functional versus 2 non-functional alleles cannot reach statistical significance, and mal63-13p and mal64p may share the block by descent or by ectopic recombination rather than by independent mutation. Taken with points 1 to 6, the direction is consistent. The one place in this family where loss of function has a residue-level signature is the place where Mal33p looks like the working alleles.

  2. Percent identity does not predict function here. Against wild-type Mal63p, the dead mal63-13p is 98.5% identical, the dead mal64p 85.5%, and the hyperactive constitutive Mal23-Cp 91.2%. The ~71% identity of Mal33p and Mal13p therefore supports no inference about activity in either direction. It is the expected divergence between the MAL3-locus and MAL6-locus paralogs.

The single indel in Mal33p is a two-residue deletion of Mal63p Met350-Asn351. The identical deletion is present in Mal23-Cp, the constitutive, transcriptionally hyperactive MAL23-C allele sequenced by Gibson et al. (PMID: 9258674). A change compatible with elevated activator function cannot be the disabling change.

Most important caveat. The absence of a nameable lesion does not demonstrate that Mal33p is an active transcription factor. It shows that the protein sequence carries no information identifying a disabling change, so the S288C maltose-negative phenotype should not be attributed to a specific residue in the gene review. The strongest competing explanations are locus-level regulation (promoter or chromatin context at the MAL3 locus), expression dosage, and functional divergence of the MAL3-locus activator toward a different target set.

Key findings

Mal33p and Mal13p are full-length activators with intact zinc clusters

Banded Needleman-Wunsch global alignment (BLOSUM62, gap open -11, extend -1) of all alleles projected onto Mal63p coordinates gives these lengths: Mal63p 470 aa, Mal23p 470 aa, Mal23-Cp 468 aa, mal64p 470 aa, Mal33p 468 aa, Mal13p 473 aa. The Mal33p C-terminal twelve residues NDDSKDEDDIIP are identical to those of Mal23p and Mal23-Cp. Mal13p ends DGYPKDNEDIDP, aligned colinearly to the Mal63p terminus NDNSKDEDDIIP. Mal13p carries a five-residue N-terminal extension (TKQTC after Mal63p Ile3) and a two-residue deletion at Mal63p Met348-Arg349.

The Gal4-type cluster is intact in every allele tested. All six zinc-coordinating cysteines occupy identical register with the spacing C-X2-C-X6-C-X5-C-X2-C-X6-C. Mapping Gal4 base-contacting residues Arg15, Lys17, Lys18 and Lys20 onto Mal63p Arg12, Arg14, Arg15 and Lys17 shows all four unchanged in Mal33p and Mal13p. Mal33p carries 8 substitutions within residues 8-34 (70.4% identity, versus 96.3% for the active Mal23p allele), the most radical being Cys10Tyr (Grantham 194), at a position equivalent to Gal4 Ile13 that is not a zinc ligand. Mal13p, mal64p, Mal23p and Mal23-Cp all retain Cys10.

The divergence is distributed, and no single substitution can be nominated

Per-domain identity to Mal63p for Mal33p, using the Hu et al. 1999 boundaries: Zn(2)Cys(6) 8-34, 70.4%; DNA binding 1-100, 61.0%; transactivation core 60-283, 69.6%; inhibitory region 251-299, 75.5%; maltose-responsive 300-470, 77.2%. Equivalent values for active Mal23p are 96.3 / 98.0 / 96.4 / 98.0 / 90.1%. A Fisher exact test on differences in residues 1-100 versus 101-470 gives Mal33p 39/100 versus 97/370 (odds ratio 1.80, p = 0.018), Mal13p 30/100 versus 104/370 (OR 1.10, p = 0.71), non-functional mal64p 20/100 versus 41/370 (OR 2.01, p = 0.028), and active Mal23-Cp the opposite polarity, 2/100 versus 42/370 (OR 0.16, p = 0.0030). Radical substitutions (Grantham ≥ 100) in Mal33p number 10 in residues 1-100, 20 in 60-283, 2 in 251-299 and 2 in 300-470; Mal23p has 0, 2, 0 and 1.

All 39 Mal33p changes in residues 1-100 fall at positions invariant across Mal63p, Mal23p and Mal23-Cp. Conservation within the active set therefore gives no discrimination among them. The conclusion the alignment supports is regional and weak: the DNA-binding domain is the most diverged part of Mal33p and is modestly enriched for substitutions relative to the rest of the protein, the reverse of the pattern in active alleles. It does not support naming any one substitution.

The Mal33p C-terminus is the segment that makes MAL23-C constitutive

Aligning Mal23p (inducible, functional) against Mal23-Cp (constitutive) gives exactly 20 amino-acid differences (95.7% identity), every one of them between residues 325 and 378, with zero differences in residues 1-299. Over residues 300-468, Mal23-Cp is identical to Mal33p at 165/169 positions (97.6%) and to its own Mal23p parent at 150/169 (88.8%). Residues 317-468 of Mal33p, 152 amino acids including the Met350-Asn351 deletion, are 100% identical to Mal23-Cp; the same block is 88.2% identical to Mal23p and 78.3% to the mal63-13 reference. The alignment was confirmed independently by phenix.superpose_pdbs, which reports 71.1% identity for Mal33p/Mal63p and 90.6% for Mal23-Cp/Mal63p with the same C-terminal mismatch pattern.

The constitutive MAL23-C allele is a recombinant in which the maltose-responsive negative regulatory domain of MAL23 has been replaced by a MAL33-type (MAL3-locus) segment. Gibson et al. mapped constitutivity to extensive alteration of this domain and named ectopic recombination among subtelomeric MAL loci as the mechanism. The Mal33p regulatory region, placed in a functional activator backbone, yields a hyperactive factor. If S288C Mal33p is non-functional for a protein-coding reason, the lesion must lie in residues 1-316, where Mal33p differs from Mal23-Cp at 101 positions.

No S288C-specific change exists in the Mal33p protein

Nine full-length Mal33p-class proteins were compared to the S288C reference P38157 (468 aa). Three strains encode a protein with zero differences: CEN.PK113-7D (EIW12238.1), PE-2 (CAF1547419.1) and P301 (EWG92613.1). Four differ by 4 to 8 residues: RM11-1a (EDV11819.1, 4), EC1118 (CBK39375.1, 6), P283 (EWH19664.1, 8), R103 (EWG97481.1, 8). The S. paradoxus ortholog (XP_033764884.1) differs by 57 residues. All nine are 468 aa.

CEN.PK113-7D ferments maltose, carried by its constitutive MAL2-8c locus, and encodes a Mal33p identical to the S288C protein. The comparison shows absence of an S288C-specific change, not that Mal33p is active, because maltose growth in those strains can come from other MAL loci.

Structure prediction is non-discriminating

AlphaFold DB v6 models for six alleles were superposed on the Mal63p-class model using alignment-mapped CA pairs and Kabsch fitting. Over residues 8-34 all alleles superpose at 0.09 to 0.30 Å (Mal23-Cp 0.09, Mal23p 0.11, Mal33p 0.20, mal64p 0.22, Mal13p 0.30). Over residues 1-100 the RMSD is 15.87 Å for Mal33p, 10.68 Å for Mal13p, 10.07 Å for mal64p, and 11.09 Å for the functional constitutive Mal23-Cp, which is 98% identical over that region. Inducible Mal23p gives 1.76 Å. The 1-100 spread tracks the modelled hinge angle between the zinc cluster and the downstream linker, a low-confidence region (mean pLDDT 68.2 to 76.4). Per-domain mean pLDDT differs by at most 4 points between functional and non-functional alleles. Whole-chain phenix.superpose_pdbs gives 10.8 Å for Mal33p and 12.1 Å for the functional Mal23-Cp.

The result is negative and is reported as such. Including the functional constitutive allele as a control is what makes the negative interpretable: without it, the 15.87 Å RMSD over residues 1-100 would have read as a structural defect in Mal33p.

The reference allele was wrong, and the correction supplied a positive control

GenBank AF003003, the source of UniProt E9P8G3, carries /allele="mal63-13", /note="nonfunctional mutant allele of MAL63" and /product="Mal63-13p"; UniProt's submitted name for the entry is "Mal63-13p". Rounds one and two of this report used E9P8G3 as wild-type Mal63p. The wild-type reference is GenBank M36537, whose CDS translates to exactly the 470 residues reported in PMID: 3145816. X12576 translates to 473 residues and is the source of UniProt P10508, carrying three extra residues (Leu and Arg after position 86, Ile after position 202).

The impact was measured, not assumed. mal63-13 differs from M36537 at 7 positions (98.5% identity): 280 V/I, 307 W/R, 355 S/N, 356 A/T, 357 S/N, 358 S/N, 364 K/E, with six of seven inside the maltose-responsive domain 283-470. Recomputing every domain identity against M36537 changes Mal33p whole-protein identity from 71.1% to 71.6% and Mal13p from 71.5% to 72.0%; no per-domain value moves by more than 2.1 points. The two load-bearing results of this review, the MAL23-C breakpoint and the nine-strain panel, never used E9P8G3.

At positions 355, 357, 358 and 364 the panel separates perfectly. Wild-type Mal63p, Mal23p and Mal23-Cp carry Asn-Asn-Asn-Glu; mal63-13p and mal64p carry Ser-Ser-Ser-Lys; Mal33p and Mal13p carry Asn-Asn-Asn-Glu. Position 356 does not discriminate, since the functional Mal23-Cp carries the same Ala as both dead alleles. Positions 280 and 307 are wild-type-Mal63p-specific, with all five other alleles carrying Val280 and Trp307.

The "nonfunctional" label traces to locus-structure work, and an S288C deletion phenotype exists

SGD lists exactly three primary references for YBR297W: PMID: 2548922 (molecular evolution of the telomere-associated MAL loci), PMID: 2549370 (structure of the multigene family) and PMID: 7483839 (the chromosome II sequence). None reports a complementation, reporter or transactivation assay on the S288C ORF. Feuermann et al. report the three MAL3 ORFs present as non-overlapping ORFs and state that the Mal- phenotype of S288C cannot be explained by telomeric silencing. SGD phenotype records for YBR297W include "utilization of carbon source: decreased rate" in strain S288C from PMID: 24721214, recorded twice. YGR288W (MAL13) has "utilization of carbon source: absent" from PMID: 6387396, a classical-genetics reduction-of-function study in a strain recorded as "Other". Genomic coordinates confirm the intact three-gene locus: YBR297W chrII 800523-801929 (+), YBR298C 802631-804475 (-), YBR299W 805351-807105 (+).

Every manual GO annotation on Mal33p is transferred from MAL63

QuickGO returned 42 annotations across P38157, P53338, P10508 and Q06595. All three non-IEA annotations on P38157 are ISS with withFrom SGD:S000029659 and reference PMID: 10447589: GO:0003700 (enables), GO:0006355 (involved_in), GO:0005634 (is_active_in). SGD:S000029659 resolves to MAL63, and 10447589 is the Mal63p deletion-mapping paper that supplies Mal63p's own IMP and IDA annotations. Mal33p also carries GO:0008270 by RCA and seven IEA annotations. GO:0000978 and GO:0045944 are not present on the entry, correcting an earlier draft of this report. Mal13p (P53338) has no ISS annotations but carries GO:0005975 by IMP from PMID: 3025617, a study of a functional MAL1 locus in a strain other than S288C.

Mechanistic model and interpretation

The direct molecular activity under test is sequence-specific DNA binding by a Gal4-type Zn(2)Cys(6) cluster followed by maltose-dependent transcriptional activation of the linked MALx1 permease and MALx2 maltase genes. Everything else in the seed context is downstream: maltose fermentation, the Mal- phenotype of S288C, and complementation by plasmid MAL63. Complementation establishes that the endogenous MAL3 and MAL1 loci of S288C do not supply activator function in that strain. It does not localise the defect to the MAL33 coding sequence, because a plasmid-borne MAL63 supplies promoter, coding sequence, expression level and chromosomal context together.

The regulatory architecture reported for Mal63p explains why the seed hypothesis points in the wrong direction. Residues 1-100 bind DNA, 60-283 form the functional core including the transactivation domain, 251-299 inhibit activation, and the remaining C-terminus is the maltose-responsive domain that relieves that inhibition (PMID: 10447589). Removing the C-terminus removes a brake. Gibson et al. report that the two mutations converting the non-functional mal64 allele into an active constitutive activator are nonsense codons producing 306- and 282-residue proteins, and that extensive mutation or deletion of 283-470 abolishes negative regulation (PMID: 9258674). Danzi et al. independently find constitutive alleles are either truncations deleting 283-470 or carry many scattered C-terminal alterations (PMID: 11191206). A truncation model for MAL33 predicts constitutive maltose fermentation, which is the opposite of the observed phenotype.

These are leads requiring curator verification, not finished decisions.

The ISS chain is circular and should be labelled as such. SGD:S000029659 is MAL63, and 10447589 is the Mal63p deletion-mapping paper that supplies Mal63p's own experimental annotations. Three annotation notes follow from the analysis. First, "nonfunctional in genomic reference strain S288C" should be curated as a strain-level phenotype with its three locus-structure references, since three other strains encode an identical Mal33p and all nine natural alleles are 468 aa, so the statement carries no residue-level content. Second, no specific disabling change should be recorded: every named lesion class is excluded, residues 317-468 are identical to a hyperactive allele, and no residue-level candidate survives. Third, the reference sequence matters, because "Mal63p" resolves to three distinct public sequences and only M36537 is wild type.

Mechanistic scope

Direct gene-product activity in scope: Zn(2)Cys(6)-mediated sequence-specific DNA binding, maltose-responsive relief of an internal inhibitory element, and transcriptional activation of linked MAL structural genes. The sequence and structural evidence here bears on the first and, through the MAL23-C recombinant, on the second. No assay of Mal33p activation activity exists in the literature searched.

Downstream and out of scope for a direct function call: maltose fermentation by S288C, growth on maltose in strain panels, the carbon-source utilization phenotype of the mal33 deletant, and complementation by plasmid MAL63. Each of these is a locus-level or strain-level readout that integrates promoter strength, chromatin context at the subtelomere, inducer availability, glucose repression through Mig1p (PMID: 8529272), and the state of the linked MAL31 and MAL32 genes. Inferring a disabled protein from any of them is the inference this report argues against.

Conflicts and alternatives

  1. Truncation produces the opposite phenotype. Gibson et al. (PMID: 9258674) and Danzi et al. (PMID: 11191206) both show that deleting the C-terminal 283-470 region makes the activator constitutive. The seed hypothesis's first two lesion classes predict maltose-constitutive growth.

  2. mal64p is a counterexample within the family. It is non-functional, 85.5% identical to wild-type Mal63p, full length, with all six zinc ligands intact and no indel. Non-function in the dissected cases is substitution-driven and involves none of the lesion classes the seed hypothesis names.

  3. The Mal33p C-terminus is a gain-of-function segment. The MAL23-C recombinant carries a MAL33-type maltose-responsive domain and is hyperactive. Any model placing the disabling change in residues 300-468 of Mal33p conflicts with this directly.

  4. A MAL33 allele with frameshift and premature-stop mutations still retains activity when overexpressed. In S. eubayanus, overexpression of the QC18 MAL33 allele improved maltose metabolism, which the authors read as dosage-dependent transcriptional limitation (PMID: 41642680). Different species and allele, but it undercuts inferring a dead protein from a Mal- phenotype.

  5. Full-length MAL activators can be non-inducible without truncation. Non-inducible mal63 proteins bind Ssa1 alone and do not stably associate with Hsp82 or Sti1, while inducible Mal63p binds all three and is released by maltose (PMID: 18458105). This supplies a chaperone-dependent maturation mechanism and a concrete assay for Mal33p.

  6. Functional divergence rather than breakage. MAL loci are subtelomeric and diversify rapidly, generating alleles that metabolise different carbohydrates (PMID: 20471265, PMID: 2548922, PMID: 8005435). Mal33p and Mal13p are only 69.2% identical to each other, less than either is to Mal63p, so they are independently diverged lineages and a single shared disabling change in both is evolutionarily implausible.

  7. Database carry-over risk, including an error in the first two rounds of this report. There is no single clean UniProt entry for wild-type Mal63p. P10508 is 473 residues while its source paper states 470; the extra residues trace to GenBank X12576. E9P8G3 is 470 residues and was used as the functional reference in rounds one and two. That was wrong: its source record, GenBank AF003003, is annotated /allele="mal63-13", /note="nonfunctional mutant allele of MAL63", /product="Mal63-13p", and UniProt's own submitted name is "Mal63-13p". The wild-type reference is M36537, whose CDS translates to exactly the 470 residues the paper states. Round three repeated the domain analysis against M36537. mal63-13 is 98.5% identical to wild type, so every identity figure moves by less than one percentage point: Mal33p is 71.6% against wild type versus 71.1% against mal63-13, Mal13p 72.0% versus 71.5%. No conclusion changes, and the two load-bearing results never used E9P8G3. The episode is itself the warning: three plausible "Mal63p" records exist in public databases and two of them are not wild type.

  8. The MAL3 locus is repeated. Chow et al. report that the MAL3 locus carries a repeat of more than 10 kb giving four copies of MAL3R-homologous sequence (PMID: 2549370). Which copy YBR297W corresponds to was not resolved here.

Discriminating tests and proposed follow-up

  1. Swap the 355-364 block. Convert wild-type Mal63p to Asn355Ser, Asn357Ser, Asn358Ser, Glu364Lys and score maltose-inducible activation; make the reciprocal repair in mal63-13p. This is the cheapest test in the list and it decides whether the family's one residue-level correlate of loss of function is causal. Mal33p already carries the functional residues, so a positive result would close the seed hypothesis against itself.

  2. LexA-Mal33p fusion reporter assay, using the design applied to Mal63p in 10447589. This directly measures whether the Mal33p activation function exists, separating a dead protein from a locus-level regulatory defect.

  3. Promoter swap at the native locus. Place the MAL63 promoter upstream of the chromosomal MAL33 ORF in S288C and test maltose growth. A rescued phenotype localises the defect outside the coding sequence.

  4. Reciprocal domain swaps between Mal33p and Mal63p at the 1-100 / 101-470 boundary. The alignment points at 1-100 as the most diverged region; this is the direct test of whether that regional signal is causal.

  5. Chaperone-complex profiling of Mal33p against inducible, constitutive and non-inducible Mal63p alleles, following 18458105. If Mal33p binds Ssa1 alone without stable Hsp82 and Sti1 association, it falls into the non-inducible class without any truncation.

  6. Overexpression of the native S288C MAL33 allele, following the design that rescued maltose metabolism in S. eubayanus QC18 (41642680). Rescue would show dosage limitation rather than a dead protein.

  7. Carbon-source panel on the S288C mal33 deletant against the isogenic wild type, with MAL31 and MAL32 deletants as controls, to resolve the 24721214 phenotype.

  8. Long-read mapping of the S288C chrII right arm to establish which MAL3R-homologous copy YBR297W is.

Evidence base

Seventeen papers were reviewed. The ones that carry weight here fall into three groups.

Domain mapping and allele dissection. Hu et al. (PMID: 10447589) supply the domain boundaries used throughout. Gibson et al. (PMID: 9258674) supply the MAL63, MAL23, MAL23-C and mal64 allele sequences, the constitutivity mapping to the C-terminal domain, and the ectopic-recombination mechanism. Danzi et al. (PMID: 11191206) independently confirm that C-terminal truncation gives constitutivity. Kim and Michels (PMID: 3145816) fix the 470-residue reference length and identify the cysteine-zinc finger. The Leu343Phe constitutivity study (PMID: 10369955) places further function between residues 343 and 375, a window that overlaps the 355-364 block found here. These papers refute the mechanistic core of the seed hypothesis by inverting the predicted direction of truncation effects.

Locus structure and evolution. Charron et al. (PMID: 2548922) and Chow et al. (PMID: 2549370) describe the five telomere-proximal complexes and report that genuinely inactive MAL1 alleles show deletion or divergence of their MALR sequences, the pattern absent from S288C MAL33. The natural-population survey (PMID: 8005435) shows partial-function MAL alleles are common in natural isolates and arise by mutation and/or rearrangement. Brown et al. (PMID: 20471265) show duplication followed by functional divergence yields alleles metabolising different carbohydrates, supporting a diverged-regulator reading of a 71%-identical paralog. Feuermann et al. (PMID: 7483839) report the three MAL3 ORFs intact in S288C and exclude telomeric silencing as the explanation for the Mal- phenotype.

Competing mechanisms. Ran et al. (PMID: 18458105) show full-length non-inducible mal63 proteins exist and are distinguished by their chaperone complexes. The S. eubayanus QTL study (PMID: 41642680) shows a MAL33 allele with frameshift and premature-stop mutations still improves maltose metabolism when overexpressed. Charron et al. (PMID: 3025617) establish MAL13 as the MAL1 counterpart of MAL63 in a strain with a functional MAL1 locus, which is the provenance problem behind Mal13p's GO:0005975 IMP. The Mig1p study (PMID: 8529272) adds the glucose-repression layer that sits between any activator genotype and a maltose-growth phenotype, including Mig1p repression of MAL63 itself.
