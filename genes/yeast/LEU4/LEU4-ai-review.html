Gene Description

Major 2-isopropylmalate synthase (alpha-isopropylmalate synthase, alpha-IPMS, EC 2.3.3.13) of Saccharomyces cerevisiae, catalysing the first committed step of leucine biosynthesis: condensation of acetyl-CoA with 3-methyl-2-oxobutanoate (alpha-ketoisovalerate) to give 2-isopropylmalate. LEU4 encodes two forms by alternative translation initiation: a long form imported into mitochondria, which provides most cellular alpha-IPMS activity, and a short cytosolic form that is also functional. 2-Isopropylmalate made in mitochondria is exported to the cytosol by the Oac1 carrier, where it is converted by Leu1 and Leu2 and also acts as the coactivator of the transcription factor Leu3. Leu4 homodimers are feedback-inhibited by leucine, whereas its paralog Leu9 is leucine-resistant; Leu4-Leu9 heterodimers form in vivo with intermediate regulation. leu4 deletion leaves ~20% residual activity from Leu9, and leu4 leu9 double mutants are leucine auxotrophs.

Core Functions

Major alpha-isopropylmalate synthase: condenses acetyl-CoA with alpha-ketoisovalerate to 2-isopropylmalate (EC 2.3.3.13), the first committed step of leucine biosynthesis, chiefly in mitochondria (long form) with a minor cytosolic short form; leucine-feedback-sensitive.
