Gene Description

MON1 encodes the Mon1 subunit of the peripheral Mon1-Ccz1 guanine nucleotide exchange factor complex that activates the Rab7-family GTPase Ypt7 on late endosomal, multivesicular body, and fungal vacuole membranes. Mon1 and Ccz1 together stimulate nucleotide exchange on Ypt7 through their longin-domain catalytic core; acidic phospholipids and Rab5-family GTPases help recruit the complex to the correct membranes, while the disordered Mon1 N-terminal region and Yck3-dependent phosphorylation tune its Rab5-dependent activity. Loss of MON1 disrupts multiple trafficking routes that fuse with the vacuole, including CPY, ALP and multivesicular body cargo delivery, the cytoplasm-to-vacuole targeting pathway, autophagy, pexophagy, and homotypic vacuole fusion.

Core Functions

Mon1, together with Ccz1, forms the longin-domain Rab7/Ypt7 guanine nucleotide exchange factor that activates Ypt7 on late endosomal, MVB and vacuolar membranes, driving Rab5-to-Ypt7/Rab7 maturation and fusion of MVB, Cvt, and autophagic vesicles with the fungal vacuole.

Suggested Questions for Experts

Q: Should the yeast Mon1-Ccz1 module be annotated to a more specific Rab5-to-Ypt7/Rab7 transition or vacuolar vesicle fusion process term that separates the complex's exchange step from generic protein targeting to vacuole?

Suggested Experiments

Experiment: Compare Ypt7 activation and endosomal/vacuolar recruitment in cells carrying Mon1 and Ccz1 basic-patch mutants, Rab5-binding mutants, and Atg8-binding mutants, using allele combinations that separate endosomal maturation from Cvt/autophagosomal delivery.

Hypothesis: The acidic-lipid binding detected for isolated Mon1 primarily provides nonspecific electrostatic membrane recruitment, while Rab5-family GTPases and Atg8-family proteins determine the physiologic membrane on which Mon1-Ccz1 activates Ypt7.

MON1 review notes

  • MON1/YGL124C encodes the S. cerevisiae Mon1 subunit of the Mon1-Ccz1 longin-domain Rab7/Ypt7 GEF. The PAINT rows for PANTHER:PTN000315021, PANTHER:PTN000315023, and fungal PANTHER:PTN000989606 all line up with the direct yeast literature rather than exposing a PAINT misplacement.
  • The early vacuole-delivery paper shows the broad trafficking phenotype and the physical complex: PMID:12364329, "Subcellular fractionation and co-immunoprecipitation experiments confirm that Mon1 and Ccz1 physically interact as a stable protein complex termed the Ccz1-Mon1 complex."
  • The same paper places Mon1-Ccz1 after vesicle formation: PMID:12364329, "Taken together, our data indicate that Ccz1 and Mon1 function at the stage of fusion of autophagosomes/Cvt vesicles with the vacuole."
  • Homotypic vacuole fusion also depends on Mon1-Ccz1 at tethering/docking: PMID:14662743, "Together, we propose that the Ccz1-Mon1 complex is required for vacuole fusion by regulating the coordinated priming and tethering/docking stages."
  • Direct biochemical work identifies the heterodimer as the GEF: PMID:20797862, "The Mon1-Ccz1 complex, but neither protein alone, counteracts GAP function in vivo, rescues in vitro fusion of vacuoles carrying Ypt7-GDP, and promotes nucleotide exchange on Ypt7 independently of Vps39/HOPS."
  • Mon1 and Ccz1 each contribute to the longin-domain catalytic surface, and membrane association strongly enhances the reaction: PMID:24413168, "Mon1-Ccz1 activity requires residues within the predicted longin domains of both subunits."
  • The PI3P/PI5P/phosphatidylserine IDA rows are direct lipid-strip observations, but the best synthesis is acidic-membrane recruitment of the GEF rather than three separate core activities: PMID:24413168, "When blots were probed for Mon1 or Ccz1, we observed strong interactions with PI3P, PI5P and phosphatidylserine (PS)."
  • Rab5-family GTPases directly recruit and stimulate Mon1-Ccz1. The 2020 reconstitution paper found a dual lipid/Rab5 targeting mechanism and the 2023 paper mapped a Rab5-binding site in Mon1; PMID:37463208, "Mutations impairing Rab5 interaction result in poor GEF activity on membranes and growth defects in vivo."
  • The 2023 GO:1990624 guanyl nucleotide exchange factor inhibitor activity row is an overextension. The underlying observation is real: PMID:37463208, "We show that the intrinsically disordered N-terminal domain of Mon1 autoinhibits Rab5-dependent GEF activity on membranes." That is intramolecular autoinhibition inside the GEF that Mon1 belongs to, not a separate activity in which Mon1 inhibits another GEF.
  • A 2025 structural TLD-RabGEF paper used fungal Mon1-Ccz1 and Ypt7 from Chaetomium thermophilum and supports conservation of the catalytic mechanism at family level, but it does not change S. cerevisiae GO decisions here: PMID:40864718, "We here report the cryo-EM structures of the Mon1-Ccz1 and Inturned-Fuzzy core complexes bound to their cognate GTPases Ypt7 and Rab23 in the nucleotide exchange transition states, revealing a conserved catalytic mechanism with distinct modes of substrate recognition."

  endosomal, multivesicular body, and fungal vacuole membranes. Mon1 and Ccz1
  together stimulate nucleotide exchange on Ypt7 through their longin-domain
  catalytic core; acidic phospholipids and Rab5-family GTPases help recruit the
  complex to the correct membranes, while the disordered Mon1 N-terminal region
  and Yck3-dependent phosphorylation tune its Rab5-dependent activity. Loss of
  MON1 disrupts multiple trafficking routes that fuse with the vacuole,
  including CPY, ALP and multivesicular body cargo delivery, the
  cytoplasm-to-vacuole targeting pathway, autophagy, pexophagy, and homotypic
  vacuole fusion.
