| Feature | Best-supported annotation | Key quantitative or direct evidence | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Identity and domains | TEM1/YML064C encodes Tem1, UniProt P38987, the *Saccharomyces cerevisiae* S288c Ras-like small GTPase that initiates the mitotic exit network. The supplied P-loop NTPase, small-GTPase, Tem1/Spg1 and Ras-family annotations agree with the literature. | Tem1 is experimentally characterized as a Ras-like or Rab-like guanine-nucleotide-binding protein. Switch I and II correspond approximately to residues 50–55 and 77–84 (pqac-00000000, pqac-00000019). | Identity, organism and molecular class are verified. Results concerning Tem1 homologs in other fungi are not treated as direct evidence about P38987. |
| Biochemical activity | Tem1 binds GDP and GTP and catalyzes GTP hydrolysis. It is a regulatory GTPase rather than an enzyme acting on a macromolecular substrate. | Purified Tem1 had a MANT-GDP dissociation constant of 1.2 ± 0.1 µM, intrinsic nucleotide-exchange rate of 0.005 ± 0.0006 s⁻¹ and intrinsic hydrolysis rate of 0.011 ± 0.001 min⁻¹, equivalent to 1.8 × 10⁻⁴ s⁻¹ (pqac-00000016). | Low nucleotide affinity and relatively fast spontaneous exchange make GDP-bound Tem1 unstable under cellular GTP-to-GDP conditions and explain why a conventional GEF is unnecessary for nucleotide loading. |
| Bub2–Bfa1 regulation | Bub2–Bfa1 is Tem1’s bipartite GAP and an SPB receptor. Bfa1 binds Tem1, whereas Bub2 supplies catalytic GAP activity. | GAP affinity for Tem1GTPγS was estimated at less than 0.4 ± 0.2 nM. FRAP gave a GAP-stimulated hydrolysis rate of approximately 0.07 s⁻¹, about 400-fold above intrinsic hydrolysis. Tem1 SPB residence increased from 6 ± 2 s to 16 ± 7 s for hydrolysis-deficient Q79L (pqac-00000016, pqac-00000020). | Bub2–Bfa1 both recruits Tem1GTP to SPBs and accelerates conversion to Tem1GDP, driving rapid SPB turnover. Contrary to an older model, GAP activity was not detectably reduced at anaphase onset. |
| Cdc15 effector interaction | Cdc15 is Tem1’s downstream kinase effector but discriminates only modestly between nucleotide states. | Cdc15 affinity was 3.9 ± 0.8 nM for Tem1GTPγS and 16 ± 3 nM for Tem1GDP, representing only about fourfold selectivity. GDP-biased or apo-biased Tem1 mutants retained measurable Cdc15 binding (pqac-00000013, pqac-00000018). | Tem1–Cdc15 signaling cannot be explained by a strict GTP-on and GDP-off interaction. Effective Tem1 concentration and localization are major determinants of complex formation. |
| Localization | Tem1 functions principally at spindle pole bodies, especially the daughter-directed SPB, while smaller cytoplasmic pools are present. SPBs are signaling scaffolds rather than Tem1 substrates. | Wild-type Tem1 localizes mainly to the daughter SPB in metaphase, increases there at anaphase onset and declines during mitotic exit. Q79L accumulates strongly at both SPBs; T34A and T34N abolish SPB localization, while T52A reduces it. Recruitment requires Bub2–Bfa1 or, in its absence, Cdc15 (pqac-00000018, pqac-00000020). | Nucleotide state primarily controls access to localization partners. Preferential recruitment of Tem1GTP produces a high local concentration that promotes Cdc15 engagement. |
| MEN pathway output | Tem1 is the upstream spatial sensor and activator of the MEN pathway: Tem1 to Cdc15 to Dbf2–Mob1 to Cdc14. Cdc14 reverses mitotic-CDK phosphorylation and promotes mitotic exit and cytokinesis. | Genetic and cell-biological studies place Tem1 upstream of Cdc15 and Dbf2–Mob1. Tem1-dependent signaling releases or activates Cdc14, while the Nud1 scaffold organizes SPB association of MEN components (pqac-00000003, pqac-00000022). | Tem1’s primary biological role is to license late-mitotic MEN signaling after spindle-position and cell-cycle requirements are satisfied. Cytokinesis is a downstream consequence, not a direct Tem1-catalyzed reaction. |
| Essential phenotype | TEM1 is essential under standard vegetative growth conditions; loss of function prevents completion of mitosis. | *tem1Δ* is lethal, and temperature-sensitive mutants arrest in telophase. Hyperactive Q79L compromises the spindle-position checkpoint and shortened average actomyosin-ring contraction from about 8 min to 6 min (pqac-00000021, pqac-00000002). | Insufficient Tem1 signaling causes late-anaphase or telophase arrest. Excessive or mislocalized signaling permits inappropriate mitotic exit and can endanger genome partitioning. |
| Concentration and tethering tests | Raising Tem1’s local or global effective concentration can activate MEN independently of normal nucleotide-state and spindle-position requirements. | SPB tethering rescued lethal T34A, T34N and T52A mutants. A tetramer failed, a hexamer partially rescued and a 120-mer nanoparticle fully rescued T34A; cytoplasmic nanoparticles also rescued it in *bfa1Δ*. Doubling TEM1 caused about 40% checkpoint failure, fourfold dosage caused 95% failure and lowering Q79L reduced failure from 100% to about 15% (pqac-00000014, pqac-00000017). | These causal perturbations strongly support localization-dependent concentration, rather than a simple binary nucleotide switch, as the principal controlled variable. |
| Revised nucleotide-switch model | Tem1 retains a functional GTP/GDP cycle, but MEN activation does not appear to involve a metaphase-to-anaphase increase in the cellular Tem1GTP-to-Tem1GDP ratio. | Modeling constrained by measured rates predicted less than 10% Tem1GTP with active GAP and 97% without GAP. The estimated fraction of GAP occupied by Tem1 remained steady across metaphase and anaphase (pqac-00000017, pqac-00000020). | The current model holds that the nucleotide cycle enables efficient spatial concentration control, while spindle position regulates Tem1 localization. The less-than-10% and 97% values are model-based estimates rather than direct nucleotide measurements. |
| Lte1 and remaining uncertainty | Lte1 is a positive spatial regulator of mitotic exit, but direct Tem1 GEF activity has not been established biochemically. Evidence instead supports effects on Bfa1 and Kin4 spatial regulation. | Earlier genetic results suggested that Lte1 was a Tem1 GEF, whereas biochemical work failed to confirm exchange stimulation and implicated Lte1 in Bfa1 localization and restriction of Kin4 activity (pqac-00000000, pqac-00000007, pqac-00000021). | Lte1 should not be annotated as a proven Tem1 GEF. It also remains unresolved exactly how Tem1–Cdc15 association produces active SPB-localized Cdc15 and what prevents premature interaction during metaphase; Bfa1-mediated inhibition is a current hypothesis (pqac-00000015). |


*Table: Evidence-based annotation of budding-yeast Tem1/P38987, integrating biochemical measurements, spatial regulation, pathway position and essential phenotypes. The table highlights the revised model of Tem1 as a localization- and concentration-controlled signaling GTPase.*
