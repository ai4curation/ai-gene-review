Gene Description

Tem1 is the essential Ras/Rab-like small GTPase of Saccharomyces cerevisiae that sits at the head of the mitotic exit network (MEN), the SPB-scaffolded, Hippo-like kinase cascade that ends mitosis. It binds GTP and GDP with unusually low (micromolar) affinity, exchanges nucleotide spontaneously, and hydrolyses GTP slowly on its own but some 400-fold faster when stimulated by the two-component GTPase-activating protein Bub2-Bfa1, in which Bfa1 binds Tem1 and Bub2 supplies the catalytic TBC domain. Bub2-Bfa1 is also Tem1's receptor at the cytoplasmic face of the spindle pole body: Tem1 accumulates preferentially on the SPB destined for the bud, and when that pole enters the daughter cell in anaphase, away from the mother-confined Kin4 kinase that keeps the GAP active, Tem1 builds up on the daughter SPB and engages its effector, the protein kinase Cdc15. Tem1 recruits Cdc15 to the SPB scaffold Nud1, where Cdc15 phosphorylates Nud1 to dock and activate the Dbf2-Mob1 kinase; Dbf2-Mob1 then phosphorylates Cdc14 so that the phosphatase, released from its nucleolar Net1/RENT anchor, spreads through the cell, reverses mitotic CDK phosphorylation and drives cyclin destruction, mitotic exit and cytokinesis. The GTP-bound form is preferentially recruited to SPBs and binds Cdc15 more tightly, but Cdc15 discriminates only modestly between nucleotide states, and the current view is that spindle position controls Tem1 chiefly by setting its local concentration at the SPB rather than by switching its GTP:GDP ratio; concentrating Tem1 artificially at SPBs or in cytoplasmic assemblies bypasses the need for nucleotide binding. The same GAP-controlled hydrolysis makes Tem1 the target of the spindle position checkpoint: on mispositioned spindles Tem1 remains GDP-bound and symmetrically distributed, mitotic exit is delayed, and GAP-refractory mutants such as Tem1-Q79L exit mitosis regardless of spindle position and yield aneuploid cells. In metaphase, MEN activity emanating from asymmetric Bub2-Bfa1-Tem1 also promotes asymmetric loading of Kar9 on spindle poles and thus correct spindle orientation toward the bud. After exit, the daughter-specific Amn1 protein binds Tem1 and displaces Cdc15 to reset the pathway for G1. Tem1 is orthologous to the fission-yeast Spg1 GTPase that initiates the septation initiation network.

Functional annotation report: Saccharomyces cerevisiae TEM1 (UniProt P38987)

Executive summary

Identity is verified. The requested protein is Tem1 encoded by TEM1/YML064C in Saccharomyces cerevisiae S288c, not a similarly named protein from another organism. Its experimentally established identity as a Ras/Rab-like small GTPase agrees with the supplied P-loop NTPase, small-GTPase, Tem1/Spg1-family and Ras-domain annotations. Studies of Tem1 homologs in Fusarium, Magnaporthe and other fungi were therefore not used as direct evidence about P38987.

Tem1 is an essential, regulatory guanine-nucleotide-binding protein at the apex of the mitotic exit network (MEN). Its primary function is not transport or structural support and it does not modify a protein substrate directly. Rather, Tem1 binds GDP/GTP, hydrolyzes GTP, and—when concentrated at spindle pole bodies (SPBs)—promotes engagement and SPB recruitment of the protein kinase Cdc15. This initiates the Cdc15–Dbf2/Mob1–Cdc14 signaling cascade, which reverses mitotic-CDK phosphorylation, completes mitotic exit and enables cytokinesis. Loss of TEM1 is lethal, while conditional loss-of-function mutants arrest in telophase. (scarfone2015couplingspindleposition pages 1-2, scarfone2015asymmetryofthe pages 2-4)

The principal recent advance is a 2024 mechanistic revision: Tem1 is not well described as a conventional Ras-like binary switch whose GTP:GDP ratio rises at pathway activation. Instead, spindle position regulates Tem1 localization and effective concentration. Its nucleotide cycle chiefly determines recruitment and turnover at SPBs, while local concentration controls productive interaction with Cdc15. This conclusion is supported by purified-protein biochemistry, live-cell imaging, FRAP, artificial SPB tethering, multimeric nanoparticles and gene-dosage experiments. (zhou2024anoncanonicalgtpase pages 8-9, zhou2024anoncanonicalgtpase pages 6-8, zhou2024anoncanonicalgtpase pages 1-2)

1. Identification, protein class and domain agreement

TEM1 was originally identified as a gene encoding a GTP-binding protein required for termination of M phase. The literature consistently identifies budding-yeast Tem1 as a Ras-like or Rab-like small GTPase controlling the MEN and spindle-position checkpoint (SPoC). This agrees with UniProt accession P38987, systematic locus YML064C, organism S. cerevisiae S288c, and the supplied InterPro/Pfam assignments P-loop NTPase, small GTP-binding protein, small GTPase Tem1/Spg1, and Ras family. Switch I and Switch II have been mapped approximately to residues 50–55 and 77–84. (scarfone2015asymmetryofthe pages 2-4, scarfone2015couplingspindleposition pages 5-5, zhou2024anoncanonicalgtpase pages 1-2)

The symbol is potentially confusing across fungi: recent papers describe proteins named FgTem1 and MoTem1 in fungal pathogens. Those are homologs, not the target protein. No evidence about their organism-specific roles in virulence was transferred to the S288c annotation.

2. Primary molecular and biochemical function

2.1 Catalytic reaction and specificity

Tem1 is a regulatory GTPase that catalyzes:

Tem1–GTP + H₂O → Tem1–GDP + inorganic phosphate.

Its nucleotide substrates are guanine nucleotides, principally GTP and GDP; it is not a kinase, transporter or enzyme acting on a macromolecular substrate. Purified Tem1 exhibited an intrinsic GTP-hydrolysis rate of 0.011 ± 0.001 min⁻¹ (1.8 × 10⁻⁴ s⁻¹), a spontaneous nucleotide-exchange rate of 0.005 ± 0.0006 s⁻¹, and a MANT-GDP dissociation constant of 1.2 ± 0.1 μM. This micromolar nucleotide affinity is unusually weak compared with the picomolar-to-nanomolar affinities typical of canonical small GTPases. Rapid spontaneous exchange and the high cellular GTP:GDP ratio make Tem1 effectively self-loading with GTP when its GAP is absent; a dedicated GEF is therefore not required by the current biochemical model. (zhou2024anoncanonicalgtpase pages 2-3)

2.2 Interaction with Cdc15

Cdc15 is the immediate downstream kinase effector. It binds Tem1GTPγS with K_D = 3.9 ± 0.8 nM and Tem1GDP with K_D = 16 ± 3 nM—only about fourfold selectivity. GDP/apo-biased Tem1 mutants retained appreciable Cdc15 binding, and an AlphaFold2 model places the proposed interface away from the nucleotide pocket. Thus, Cdc15 differs from canonical effectors that bind almost exclusively to a GTP-state conformation. (zhou2024anoncanonicalgtpase pages 3-5)

This weak discrimination is central to current interpretation: the GTP state favors signaling, but productive Tem1–Cdc15 association is regulated more strongly by effective concentration and cellular location than by a strict GDP-off/GTP-on transition.

3. Cellular pathway and biological process

Tem1 lies at the apex of the budding-yeast MEN. The best-supported pathway order is:

spindle-position inputs → Bub2–Bfa1/Tem1 → Cdc15 → Dbf2–Mob1 → Cdc14 → mitotic-CDK reversal, mitotic exit and cytokinesis.

SPBs function as MEN signaling scaffolds, with Nud1 organizing downstream components. Tem1 promotes Cdc15 access to the SPB/Nud1 platform; Cdc15 then activates the Dbf2–Mob1 kinase module. MEN activity causes sustained release/activation of the Cdc14 phosphatase, which dephosphorylates mitotic-CDK substrates, promotes mitotic-cyclin inactivation, spindle disassembly, nuclear division and cytokinesis. Tem1 therefore acts as a signaling initiator or spatial licensing factor—not as the enzyme directly executing cytokinesis. (scarfone2015couplingspindleposition pages 5-5, scarfone2015asymmetryofthe pages 2-4)

TEM1 is essential in standard vegetative conditions: deletion is lethal, and temperature-sensitive alleles arrest in telophase. Conversely, hyperactive or improperly concentrated Tem1 can allow exit when spindle position is defective, risking failed genome partitioning. (scarfone2015couplingspindleposition pages 1-2)

MEN also contributes before telophase to spindle orientation. Dbf2–Mob1-dependent regulation of Kar9 helps establish asymmetric Kar9 distribution and orient the spindle toward the bud. Accordingly, altered Tem1/Bub2–Bfa1 organization reduces Kar9 asymmetry and causes spindle-position defects, although this earlier role is distinct from Tem1’s essential late-mitotic function. (scarfone2015asymmetryofthe pages 15-19, scarfone2015asymmetryofthe pages 2-4)

4. Subcellular localization and site of action

Tem1 acts principally at the cytoplasmic face of the spindle pole bodies, the yeast functional equivalents of centrosomes. Wild-type Tem1 is enriched at the daughter-directed SPB in metaphase, increases there around anaphase onset as that pole enters the bud, appears at both SPBs later in anaphase, and declines as mitotic exit is completed. A cytoplasmic pool remains important because Tem1 can associate with Cdc15 before SPB recruitment under some genetic conditions. After exit, Tem1 is reported to enter the nucleus for degradation, helping reset MEN signaling for the next cycle. (zhou2024anoncanonicalgtpase pages 9-10, zhou2024anoncanonicalgtpase pages 3-5)

Localization is highly dynamic. FRAP gave an SPB residence half-time of 6 ± 2 s for wild-type Tem1 and 16 ± 7 s for hydrolysis-defective Tem1Q79L. Tem1 cannot stably associate with SPBs by itself: recruitment occurs predominantly through Bub2–Bfa1 and, when that complex is absent, through Cdc15. Depleting Cdc15 had little effect on Tem1 localization in BFA1 cells but abolished the remaining localization in bfa1Δ cells. (zhou2024anoncanonicalgtpase pages 5-6)

The nucleotide state controls access to these localization partners. GTP-locked Q79L accumulates strongly at both SPBs; T34A/T34N nucleotide-binding mutants lose SPB localization, while the switch-I/effector mutant T52A strongly reduces it. These findings indicate that Tem1GTP is preferentially recruited, even though GTP occupancy is not itself the principal cell-cycle-varying signal. (zhou2024anoncanonicalgtpase pages 3-5)

5. Upstream regulation and the spindle-position checkpoint

Bub2–Bfa1

Bub2–Bfa1 is a bipartite Tem1 GAP and localization receptor. Bfa1 binds Tem1 and supports nucleotide retention/recruitment; Bub2 supplies the catalytic TBC-domain GAP activity. The complex binds Tem1GTPγS with estimated affinity below 0.4 ± 0.2 nM, whereas interaction with Tem1GDP is several orders of magnitude weaker. Bub2 alone does not detectably bind Tem1 without Bfa1. (zhou2024anoncanonicalgtpase pages 2-3)

FRAP-derived in-vivo GAP-stimulated hydrolysis was approximately 0.07 s⁻¹, about 400-fold above intrinsic hydrolysis. This reaction converts recruited Tem1GTP to Tem1GDP and drives its rapid departure from the SPB. Importantly, the 2024 study found no significant metaphase-to-anaphase reduction in this GAP activity, contradicting the older model that pathway activation is caused chiefly by GAP shutdown. (zhou2024anoncanonicalgtpase pages 5-6)

Kin4, Cdc5 and spatial checkpoint logic

When both SPBs remain in the mother cell because the spindle is mispositioned, the mother-cell inhibitory-zone kinase Kin4 phosphorylates Bfa1 and promotes removal/maintenance of checkpoint-competent Bub2–Bfa1 organization. Kin4 thereby prevents inappropriate Cdc5-dependent inhibition of Bfa1 and keeps MEN signaling below the activation threshold. When the daughter SPB enters the bud, it escapes the Kin4-rich mother compartment; Bub2–Bfa1 and Tem1 can then accumulate at that SPB and license MEN activation. (zhou2024anoncanonicalgtpase pages 3-5, scarfone2015couplingspindleposition pages 1-2)

Cdc5/Polo has temporal roles in MEN and FEAR signaling and phosphorylates Bfa1. Older models treated this phosphorylation as the principal means of inactivating the GAP at anaphase. Current data instead indicate that GAP-stimulated hydrolysis persists during anaphase, so Cdc5 regulation is better regarded as one component of a broader spatial and temporal network rather than a simple Tem1 nucleotide switch. (zhou2024anoncanonicalgtpase pages 2-3, zhou2024anoncanonicalgtpase pages 5-6)

Lte1

Lte1 was historically designated a putative Tem1 GEF because genetic evidence made it a positive regulator of mitotic exit. Direct stimulation of Tem1 nucleotide exchange has not been established biochemically. Later work instead implicated Lte1 in Bfa1 localization and in restricting Kin4 activity to the mother cell. Lte1 should therefore not be annotated as a proven Tem1 GEF. (scarfone2015asymmetryofthe pages 2-4, scarfone2015asymmetryofthe pages 26-27, scarfone2015couplingspindleposition pages 1-2)

6. Major 2024 development: a noncanonical localization/concentration mechanism

Zhou and colleagues, published 30 October 2024 in PNAS, concluded that Tem1 does not operate primarily as a canonical binary molecular switch. Measured rates and cellular concentrations predicted that <10% of Tem1 is GTP-bound with active Bub2–Bfa1, versus 97% without the GAP. The estimated fraction of GAP occupied by Tem1 did not change appreciably from metaphase to anaphase, indicating that most cellular Tem1 remains GDP-bound in both MEN-off and MEN-on states. These percentages are model-based estimates constrained by measurements, not direct mass-spectrometric nucleotide-occupancy measurements. (zhou2024anoncanonicalgtpase pages 6-8, zhou2024anoncanonicalgtpase pages 5-6)

The decisive experiments manipulated localization or concentration directly:

  • Tethering Tem1 to the SPB rescued lethal T34A, T34N and T52A mutants, and tethered proteins supported similar anaphase kinetics despite differing nucleotide states.
  • A tetrameric clustering module did not rescue T34A, a hexamer partially rescued it, and a 120-mer genetically encoded nanoparticle fully rescued it.
  • Concentrated T34A also rescued growth in bfa1Δ, where nanoparticles remained predominantly cytoplasmic, showing that sufficiently concentrated Tem1 can engage Cdc15 away from the SPB and subsequently promote MEN signaling.
  • Doubling TEM1 dosage caused approximately 40% checkpoint failure during spindle mispositioning; fourfold dosage caused 95% failure. Conversely, lowering GTP-locked Q79L reduced failure from 100% to about 15%. (zhou2024anoncanonicalgtpase pages 8-9, zhou2024anoncanonicalgtpase pages 6-8)

These interventions support a causal model: spindle position changes Tem1’s localization and hence its local effective concentration; high local Tem1 promotes Tem1–Cdc15 complex formation and Cdc15 access to the SPB signaling scaffold. The GTP/GDP cycle remains useful because preferential recruitment of Tem1GTP and GAP-catalyzed turnover sharpen spatial concentration differences, balancing rapid mitotic exit against checkpoint fidelity. (zhou2024anoncanonicalgtpase pages 8-9, zhou2024anoncanonicalgtpase pages 9-10)

7. Experimental phenotypes and quantitative biological evidence

Constitutively active TEM1-Q79L is refractory to Bub2–Bfa1-stimulated hydrolysis. Under unperturbed conditions it causes little gross growth defect, illustrating that downstream temporal controls still restrain MEN. Nevertheless, it accelerates actomyosin-ring contraction from an average of approximately 8 min in wild type to 6 min, impairs the spindle-position checkpoint, worsens spindle-positioning defects and permits genome re-replication after nocodazole treatment. (scarfone2015asymmetryofthe pages 9-11)

Spatially altered Tem1 signaling affects Kar9 asymmetry. Strong Kar9 asymmetry was observed in 84.4% of wild-type metaphase cells but only 47.6% of TEM1-Q79L cells. GAP-defective Bub2 mutants reduced complete Kar9 asymmetry from 80.3% in wild type to 33.9% and 27.8%, supporting a direct relationship between Tem1/GAP spatial organization and spindle orientation. (scarfone2015asymmetryofthe pages 15-19)

Tem1 activation alone is not always sufficient for premature mitotic exit. Forced Bub2–Bfa1 localization and constitutive Tem1 activity depended on FEAR-pathway support for unscheduled Cdc14 release. This illustrates that Tem1 is the essential upstream license, while successful execution depends on the state of downstream cell-cycle circuitry. (scarfone2015asymmetryofthe pages 9-11)

8. Current applications and real-world relevance

Tem1 is primarily a fundamental cell-biology model, not an established clinical or industrial target. Its chief applications are:

  1. Spatial signaling research: Tem1 provides a rigorous example of a GTPase controlled through localization and effective concentration rather than a simple nucleotide-occupancy switch.
  2. Checkpoint and genome-stability studies: Manipulating Tem1, Bub2–Bfa1 or SPB targeting allows researchers to uncouple spindle position from mitotic exit and measure consequences for ploidy and chromosome segregation.
  3. Hippo/MEN pathway evolution: The MEN is a fungal Hippo-like kinase network, making Tem1 useful for studying the evolutionary logic of centrosome/SPB-organized signaling.
  4. Synthetic cell biology: SPB nanobody tethering, oligomeric tags and 120-mer nanoparticles demonstrate experimentally how local concentration can rewire an essential signaling pathway. (zhou2024anoncanonicalgtpase pages 8-9, zhou2024anoncanonicalgtpase pages 6-8, zhou2024anoncanonicalgtpase pages 1-2)

Because Tem1 homologs occur in pathogenic fungi, the pathway has comparative antifungal relevance, but organism-specific phenotypes should not be attributed to S288c Tem1 without direct evidence.

9. Evidence-weighted annotation and unresolved questions

The strongest functional annotation is:

Tem1 is an essential Ras-like small GTPase and spatial sensor at budding-yeast spindle pole bodies. Its GTP/GDP cycle governs recruitment and turnover, while spindle-position-dependent localization raises its effective concentration sufficiently to engage Cdc15 and initiate MEN signaling, culminating in Cdc14 activation, mitotic exit and cytokinesis.

Several points remain unresolved. It is not yet known exactly how Tem1 binding converts Cdc15 into the SPB-competent signaling form. Tem1 is already present at the daughter SPB in metaphase, while Cdc15 does not accumulate there until anaphase; Bfa1-mediated inhibition of Tem1–Cdc15 interaction is a current hypothesis requiring direct testing. Likewise, the 2024 nucleotide-state conclusion is supported by kinetic modeling, FRAP and GAP occupancy rather than direct measurement of endogenous Tem1-bound GDP and GTP. TEM1 (YML064C, UniProt P38987) – curation notes

Working journal for the GO review of S. cerevisiae Tem1, the Ras/Rab-like GTPase at the
head of the mitotic exit network (MEN). Sources: TEM1-uniprot.txt, TEM1-goa.tsv,
TEM1-deep-research-falcon.md (Edison/Falcon synthesis, largely built on Zhou et al. 2024
PNAS, PMID:39475649, and Scarfone & Piatti 2015 Small GTPases), and the cached publications
for every original_reference_id. Abstract-only caches: PMID:10219244, 11805837, 12048186,
12628189, 21937712. Full text cached: PMID:14734533, 19154724, 21321099, 25658911, 32074005.
The founding paper (Shirayama et al. 1994, PMID:7935462) and Zhou et al. 2024 are not in the
local cache and are therefore not cited in supported_by.

Identity and domain architecture

  • 245 aa, single Ras-like small-GTPase domain (UniProt REGION 16-244), three GTP-binding
    motifs (27-34, 75-79, 132-135), disordered C-terminal tail (210-245). InterPro
    IPR017231 "Small_GTPase_Tem1/Spg1", CDD cd04128 Spg1, Pfam PF00071 Ras, PANTHER PTHR47978.
  • Orthologue of S. pombe Spg1 (SIN GTPase). PAINT nodes used for the IBAs: PTN004662974
    (Ras-superfamily-level node for GTPase activity / GTP binding) and PTN000634389
    (Tem1/Spg1 clade node for SPB localisation and septum-assembly regulation).
  • Cloned as a GTP-binding protein required for termination of M phase; UniProt FUNCTION:
    "GTP-binding protein involved in termination of M phase ... Acts upstream of CDC15 kinase".
    573 molecules/cell (PMID:14562106, per UniProt).

Biochemistry

  • Intrinsic GTPase: PMID:12048186 and the two-component GAP:
    PMID:12048186. Bfa1 alone inhibits both hydrolysis and exchange;
    Bfa1 is proposed to act as adaptor connecting Bub2 and Tem1.
  • Quantitative parameters (deep research, from Zhou et al. 2024): MANT-GDP Kd 1.2 uM,
    spontaneous exchange 0.005 /s, intrinsic hydrolysis 0.011 /min; GAP-stimulated hydrolysis
    ~0.07 /s (~400-fold); Bub2-Bfa1 binds Tem1-GTPgS at <0.4 nM; Cdc15 binds Tem1-GTPgS at
    3.9 nM vs Tem1-GDP at 16 nM (only ~4-fold selectivity). [file:yeast/TEM1/TEM1-deep-research-falcon.md
    "Cdc15 is the immediate downstream kinase effector."]
  • Q79L (switch II glutamine) is GAP-refractory: PMID:25658911
  • Lte1 as GEF is not established: [file:yeast/TEM1/TEM1-deep-research-falcon.md "Direct
    stimulation of Tem1 nucleotide exchange has not been established biochemically."] Given the
    fast spontaneous exchange, a GEF may not be needed at all. Do not annotate Lte1 as a Tem1 GEF
    on the strength of the older genetics.

Localisation

  • SPB, asymmetric, daughter-bound pole: PMID:19154724; PMID:19154724.
  • Residence is dynamic (FRAP t1/2 ~6 s WT, ~16 s Q79L); recruitment via Bub2-Bfa1, or via Cdc15
    when Bfa1 is absent [file:yeast/TEM1/TEM1-deep-research-falcon.md "Tem1 cannot stably
    associate with SPBs by itself: recruitment occurs predominantly through Bub2–Bfa1 and, when
    that complex is absent, through Cdc15."]
  • SPB localisation is required for function: PMID:21321099; the
    Cnm67-Tem1 fusion (constitutive SPB loading) keeps tem1-null cells alive.
  • Anaphase accumulation on the dSPB initiates signalling: PMID:32074005

Pathway position and outputs

  • MEN order: Tem1 -> Cdc15 -> Dbf2-Mob1 -> Cdc14. PMID:12628189
  • Tem1 recruits Cdc15 to SPBs: PMID:32074005; PMID:21937712 Cdc15 is a coincidence detector of Tem1 (spatial) and Cdc5
    (temporal) inputs; MEN restriction to anaphase can occur Tem1-independently in unperturbed
    cycles PMID:21937712.
  • Cdc14 release from the nucleolar RENT complex: PMID:10219244; net1-1 bypasses tem1-null lethality.
  • Reset after exit: Amn1 PMID:12628189.
  • Revised model (Zhou 2024 via deep research): Tem1 is not a classic binary switch whose
    GTP:GDP ratio rises at anaphase; the nucleotide cycle sets SPB recruitment/turnover, and
    spindle position controls MEN activation by setting the local concentration of Tem1.
    SPB-tethering or 120-mer nanoparticle clustering rescues nucleotide-binding-dead T34A/T34N.
    Model estimates (<10 % Tem1-GTP with active GAP) are not direct measurements – flagged in
    suggested_experiments.

Spindle position checkpoint (SPOC) and spindle orientation

  • Constitutively active Tem1 mutants are SPOC-defective and symmetric: PMID:25658911; removal of
    Tem1 from SPBs is needed for the SPOC PMID:21321099
  • Kar9 asymmetry / spindle orientation: PMID:25658911
    but PMID:25658911. This role is exerted via MEN kinase activity on Kar9 – indirect for Tem1.
