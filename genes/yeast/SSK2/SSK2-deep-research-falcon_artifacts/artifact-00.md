| Identity and domain fit | The target is budding-yeast **Ssk2**, encoded by **SSK2/YNR031C** and corresponding to UniProt **P53599**. Its experimentally demonstrated N-terminal regulatory region and C-terminal Ser/Thr kinase region agree with classification as a STE-family MAP kinase kinase kinase in the HOG pathway. Studies of identically named proteins in *Candida*, *Cryptococcus*, and other fungi are not evidence for this protein. | **Ssk2-specific** biochemical/domain evidence; accession and locus identity supplied by UniProt context | Posas & Saito, March 1998, [10.1093/emboj/17.5.1385](https://doi.org/10.1093/emboj/17.5.1385) (pqac-00000003, pqac-00000004) |
| Catalytic reaction and substrate | Ssk2 is an ATP-dependent protein Ser/Thr kinase whose primary established downstream substrate is the MAP2K **Pbs2**: purified Ssk2 phosphorylated kinase-dead Pbs2 in vitro. Ssk2 **K1295N** lacked detectable activity, and activation-loop **Thr1460** was required for robust kinase activity and osmotic-stress complementation. | **Direct, Ssk2-specific** biochemical and genetic evidence | Posas & Saito, March 1998, [10.1093/emboj/17.5.1385](https://doi.org/10.1093/emboj/17.5.1385) (pqac-00000003) |
| Activation by Ssk1 and autophosphorylation | Hyperosmotic stress generates unphosphorylated Ssk1, which binds the Ssk2 N-terminal regulatory region, relieves autoinhibition, and permits predominantly intramolecular Ssk2 autophosphorylation. Osmotic shock increased Ssk2 phosphorylation about **tenfold**; this induction was lost in *ssk1Δ* and became constitutive in *sln1Δ*. | **Direct, Ssk2-specific** interaction, phosphorylation, biochemical, and genetic evidence | Posas & Saito, March 1998, [10.1093/emboj/17.5.1385](https://doi.org/10.1093/emboj/17.5.1385) (pqac-00000000, pqac-00000003) |
| Pbs2 docking and substrate specificity | A docking determinant in Pbs2 binds the kinase domains of Ssk2/Ssk22 and promotes selective phosphorylation and HOG-pathway activation; transferring this determinant to another MAP2K conferred recognition by Ssk2/Ssk22. | **Ssk2/Ssk22-combined**, not uniquely attributable to Ssk2 | Tatebayashi, Takekawa & Saito, July 2003, [10.1093/emboj/cdg353](https://doi.org/10.1093/emboj/cdg353) (pqac-00000001) |
| Pbs2 Ser514/Thr518 sites | Under appropriate osmostress conditions, **Ssk2/Ssk22 can phosphorylate both Pbs2 Ser514 and Thr518**, whereas Ste11 primarily phosphorylates Thr518. Diphosphorylated Pbs2 can activate Hog1 without an additional osmotic enhancement, unlike Thr518-only Pbs2. These data establish branch-level site specificity but do not prove that Ssk2 alone phosphorylates both sites. | **Ssk2/Ssk22-combined** biochemical/genetic evidence | Tatebayashi et al., February 2020, [10.15252/embj.2019103444](https://doi.org/10.15252/embj.2019103444); Tatebayashi & Saito, April 2023, [10.1016/j.jbc.2023.104569](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jbc.2023.104569) (pqac-00000008, pqac-00000009) |
| Localization and actin recovery | Following osmotic stress, Ssk2 was reported to concentrate at the **bud neck** and subsequently redistribute through the cytosol; associations with the polarity scaffold Spa2 and septin Shs1 support a localized role in actin repolarization and bud emergence. Localization is therefore dynamic rather than evidence for residence in a membrane-bound organelle. | **Ssk2-specific** cell-biological evidence, summarized from the actin-recovery literature | Yuzyuk & Amberg, July 2003, [10.1091/mbc.e02-11-0747](https://doi.org/10.1091/mbc.e02-11-0747) (pqac-00000007) |
| Redundancy and alternative activation | Ssk2 and Ssk22 are substantially redundant in canonical Sln1-branch osmotic signaling, so many deletion phenotypes require loss of both. However, Ssk2 can also undergo a strong, short-lived **Ssk1-independent** activation involving residues **177–240** and contributing to salt resistance; Ssk22 was reported to remain Ssk1-dependent. | Redundancy: **Ssk2/Ssk22-combined**; alternative activation: **Ssk2-specific** genetic/functional evidence | Zhi et al., February 2013, [10.1371/journal.pone.0054867](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0054867) (pqac-00000002, pqac-00000011) |
| ER-stress role | During tunicamycin-induced ER stress, Ssk2 contributes to resistance whereas Ssk22 does not. Simultaneously disrupting the RDS-I and kinase-domain docking determinants that support Ssk2–Pbs2 binding caused strong tunicamycin sensitivity, suggesting a noncanonical requirement for an assembled signaling complex rather than ordinary osmotic output alone. | **Ssk2-specific** genetic/docking evidence for ER stress; broader complex includes SLN1-branch proteins | Hernández-Elvira et al., May 2022, [10.1007/s10123-022-00250-z](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10123-022-00250-z) (pqac-00000010) |
| 2023 development: signal termination | Four PP2C phosphatases reverse Pbs2 activation: Ptc1 preferentially dephosphorylates Thr518 with Nbp2 assistance, whereas Ser514 can be dephosphorylated appreciably by Ptc1–Ptc4. This refines how output from the Ssk2/Ssk22 branch is terminated but does not identify direct phosphatase control of Ssk2 itself. | **Downstream/branch-level** evidence | Tatebayashi & Saito, April 2023, [10.1016/j.jbc.2023.104569](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jbc.2023.104569) (pqac-00000008) |
| 2024 development: branch dynamics and crosstalk | Experiments across **0–550 mM KCl** showed that the Sln1 and Sho1 branches are not merely interchangeable: Sln1-branch perturbation altered Hog1 response amplitude, transcription, and nuclear-entry/exit kinetics, while the Sho1 branch strongly influenced duration and MAPK-pathway crosstalk. Because these experiments manipulated *SSK1* or *SHO1*, they cannot be assigned specifically to Ssk2. | **Branch-level**, not Ssk2-specific | Zhang et al., October 2024, [10.1091/mbc.e24-06-0270](https://doi.org/10.1091/mbc.e24-06-0270) (pqac-00000012) |
