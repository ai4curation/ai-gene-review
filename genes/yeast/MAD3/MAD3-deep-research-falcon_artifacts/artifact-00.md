| Identity | **MAD3/YJL013C (UniProt P47074)** is the *S. cerevisiae* spindle-assembly-checkpoint protein Mad3, a Mad3/BubR1-family protein related to the non-catalytic N-terminal region of Bub1. | Budding-yeast genetic and biochemical studies identify MAD3 as a spindle-checkpoint gene and its product as an MCC component (pqac-00000000, pqac-00000011). | Direct budding-yeast evidence; **high confidence**. Accession and locus mapping derive from the specified UniProt record. |
| Primary biochemical role | Mad3 is a **non-enzymatic checkpoint effector and adaptor**, not a kinase or metabolic enzyme. It helps form the mitotic checkpoint complex (MCC), which inhibits Cdc20–APC/C and thereby delays securin destruction, sister-chromatid separation, and anaphase. | Complex purification, immunoprecipitation, abundance analysis, and checkpoint-establishment and maintenance experiments (pqac-00000000, pqac-00000002). | Direct budding-yeast evidence; **high confidence**. |
| Molecular partners | The budding-yeast MCC contains **Mad3, Mad2, Bub3, and Cdc20**; Mad3 associates functionally with APC/C through Cdc20. Mad3 and Mad2 are needed to establish and maintain checkpoint arrest. | Co-complex and conditional-mutant analyses; mitotic extracts contained a smaller Mad2–Mad3–Bub3–Cdc20 pool and a larger Mad2–Cdc20 pool (pqac-00000000). | Direct budding-yeast evidence; **high confidence** for complex membership. Co-complex membership does not prove that every pairwise contact is direct. |
| KEN motifs | Mad3 contains conserved **KEN30 and KEN296** motifs. KEN30 mutation abolishes Cdc20 binding and MCC formation and produces a checkpoint-null phenotype; both motifs are required for full checkpoint function. KEN30 also contributes to mitotic Cdc20 turnover and Cdh1–APC/C-dependent Mad3 turnover in G1. | Site-directed KEN-to-AAA mutants, genomic integration, co-immunoprecipitation, checkpoint assays, and cycloheximide-chase immunoblotting (pqac-00000005, pqac-00000007). | Direct budding-yeast evidence; **high confidence**. Detailed two-Cdc20 models depend partly on other species and should not automatically be assigned to budding yeast. |
| Phosphoregulation | Mad3 becomes hyperphosphorylated during mitosis and SAC activation. Modification depends principally on Polo kinase **Cdc5** and to a lesser extent on Aurora B **Ipl1**; alanine substitution of five candidate Cdc5-dependent serines markedly reduces phosphorylation. | Cell-cycle and nocodazole time courses, electrophoretic mobility-shift and phosphatase analysis, kinase perturbation, and phosphosite-mutant analysis (pqac-00000002, pqac-00000004). | Direct budding-yeast evidence; **moderate-to-high confidence**. Kinase dependence and candidate sites are better supported than direct phosphorylation of every site by purified kinase. |
| Mitotic phenotype and dosage | MAD3 loss or functional disruption prevents robust arrest after spindle damage and causes sensitivity to antimicrotubule conditions. Conversely, approximately **fourfold overproduction** disrupts chromosome biorientation and increases chromosome loss during recovery from nocodazole arrest. | Deletion and mutant checkpoint assays, benomyl or nocodazole treatment, fluorescent chromosome and spindle-pole markers, and overexpression experiments (pqac-00000005, pqac-00000006, pqac-00000011). | Direct budding-yeast evidence; **high qualitative confidence**. A numerical chromosome-loss percentage was not available in the extracted evidence. |
| Cellular localization | Mad3 acts in the **nuclear mitotic checkpoint system** as part of an MCC output that inhibits APC/C–Cdc20. Mad2-containing complexes can assemble during mitosis independently of intact kinetochores, so Mad3 should not be annotated as a constitutive structural kinetochore component solely from pathway membership. | Biochemical experiments tested the kinetochore dependence of complex assembly (pqac-00000000). Recent meiotic work localized Stu1, rather than Mad3 itself, to kinetochores, spindles, and the spindle midzone (pqac-00000012, pqac-00000017). | Nuclear and mitotic function is supported directly in budding yeast, but **specific steady-state Mad3 localization remains incompletely established**; moderate confidence with an explicit annotation caveat. |
| 2024 meiosis-I role | Mad3 has a **checkpoint-independent chromosome-capture role** in meiosis I through association with the TOGL1 domain of Stu1/CLASP. mad3Δ causes same-pole homolog segregation in about **5%** of cells, stu1ΔTOGL1 in about **11%**, and the double mutant in about **13%**. Achiasmate mini-chromosome segregation approaches random, at approximately **50%**, in these mutants. | Mad3-FLAG immunoprecipitation and mass spectrometry, TOGL1 separation-of-function mutants, live imaging of fluorescent centromeres and GFP-Tub1, and genetic epistasis (pqac-00000012, pqac-00000014, pqac-00000016). | Direct *S. cerevisiae* evidence from a **peer-reviewed August 2024 Current Biology article**; **high confidence**. The January 2024 version was a bioRxiv preprint (pqac-00000013, pqac-00000015). |
