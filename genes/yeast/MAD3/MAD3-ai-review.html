Gene Description

MAD3 encodes the budding-yeast orthologue of BubR1, a 515-residue, non-catalytic spindle assembly checkpoint protein. Unlike vertebrate BubR1 it has no C-terminal kinase-like domain at all: it consists of an N-terminal Bub1/Mad3 TPR-like region (homology region I, containing the KEN30 motif and the GIGS156-159 residues needed for Cdc20 binding), a central GLEBS motif (residues 354-401, homology region II, Glu382) that forms a constitutive 1:1 heterodimer with the WD40 protein Bub3, and a disordered C-terminus. Mad3 is a nuclear protein present throughout the cell cycle. When kinetochores are unattached or lack tension, Mad3-Bub3 combines with Mad2 and the APC/C activator Cdc20 to form the mitotic checkpoint complex (MCC; Mad2-Mad3-Bub3-Cdc20), which assembles during mitosis even without intact kinetochores. Within the MCC the Mad3 KEN boxes act as pseudosubstrate motifs that occupy the degron-binding sites of Cdc20, so that APC/C-Cdc20 cannot ubiquitinate securin (Pds1) and cyclins; reconstitution with purified proteins shows that Mad3-Bub3 inhibits Cdc20-dependent securin ubiquitination and, synergistically with Mad2 (IC50 ~6 nM), locks Cdc20 on the APC/C while stimulating Cdc20 autoubiquitination, which keeps Cdc20 levels low during a checkpoint arrest. Loss of Mad3 abolishes the checkpoint: mad3 cells are benomyl-sensitive, keep dividing when microtubules are depolymerised, and cannot maintain a nocodazole arrest (Pds1 is destroyed as soon as Mad3 is depleted). Mad3 is hyperphosphorylated on checkpoint activation in a Cdc5 (Polo)- and Ipl1 (Aurora B)-dependent manner, and its KEN boxes also mediate Cdc20 turnover in mitosis and Cdh1-APC/C-dependent Mad3 turnover in G1. Mad3 is additionally required for the Topoisomerase II-dependent G2/M checkpoint, and in meiosis it acts as a prophase timer and is essential for the segregation of nonexchange (achiasmate) chromosomes, a checkpoint-independent role mediated through association with the TOGL1 domain of the CLASP orthologue Stu1.

1. Identity, family, and domain interpretation

The supplied identifiers are mutually consistent with the budding-yeast spindle-checkpoint protein: gene MAD3, systematic locus YJL013C, accession P47074, in S. cerevisiae S288c. Independent yeast genetic work treats MAD3 as one of the canonical spindle-checkpoint genes, while biochemical work identifies Mad3 as a component of the budding-yeast MCC. (poddar2005twocomplexesof pages 1-2, daniel2006diversefunctionsof pages 5-7)

Mad3 belongs to the Mad3/BubR1 branch of the broader Bub1-like checkpoint family. The supplied InterPro/Pfam assignments—Bub1/Mad3, Mad3/Bub1_I and _II—are therefore functionally coherent. Evolutionarily, Bub1 and Mad3/BubR1 share conserved N-terminal checkpoint-interaction architecture, but budding-yeast Mad3 lacks the catalytic kinase output of Bub1. Accordingly, no catalyzed reaction or small-molecule substrate should be assigned to P47074. Its relevant “substrates” are interaction partners, chiefly Cdc20 and Bub3, and its pathway target is APC/C–Cdc20.

Two conserved sequence elements have especially strong direct evidence in budding yeast: KEN30 and KEN296. KEN30-to-AAA mutation eliminates detectable Cdc20 binding, prevents MCC assembly, and yields a checkpoint-null phenotype. Both KEN motifs contribute to full checkpoint function. KEN30 also participates in mitotic Cdc20 turnover and acts as a Cdh1–APC/C-dependent Mad3 degradation signal in G1. These conclusions derive from integrated genomic mutants, co-immunoprecipitation, checkpoint assays, and cycloheximide-chase experiments. (king2007mad3kenboxes pages 1-2, king2007mad3kenboxes pages 10-11)

Bub3 binding is consistent with the supplied Mad3/Bub1 family domains and with recovery of Mad3 and Bub3 in the same budding-yeast MCC. However, co-complex membership does not by itself prove that every MCC pair contacts directly. The safest annotation is that Mad3 is a Bub3-associated Mad3/BubR1-family checkpoint adaptor. (poddar2005twocomplexesof pages 1-2)

2. Core biochemical and signaling mechanism

Checkpoint input

The spindle assembly checkpoint responds to unattached or improperly attached kinetochores and inadequate tension. Kinetochore-associated signaling machinery generates active Mad2–Cdc20 species and promotes production of MCC. Mad3 functions principally in the downstream inhibitory complex rather than as the initiating kinase or attachment sensor.

MCC formation and APC/C inhibition

Budding-yeast biochemical analyses identify an MCC containing Mad3–Mad2–Bub3–Cdc20. In nocodazole-arrested mitotic cells, Mad2 was found in at least two populations: a smaller Mad2–Mad3–Bub3–Cdc20 complex and a larger Mad2–Cdc20 complex. Conditional-mutant experiments showed that Mad3 and Mad2 are required not only to initiate but also to maintain checkpoint arrest. The complexes formed during mitosis and did not require intact kinetochores for their biochemical assembly, indicating that kinetochores catalyze checkpoint signaling but are not necessarily the final residence of all active MCC. (poddar2005twocomplexesof pages 1-2)

Functionally, the MCC sequesters and inhibits Cdc20, the mitotic APC/C co-activator. APC/C is an E3 ubiquitin ligase whose activation normally drives securin and cyclin destruction. Mad3-mediated inhibition therefore preserves sister-chromatid cohesion and prevents premature anaphase until spindle defects are corrected. Direct budding-yeast evidence supports this pathway-level conclusion; detailed atomic models involving two Cdc20 molecules come substantially from fission yeast and metazoan systems and should not be transferred uncritically to P47074. (rancati2005mad3bubr1phosphorylationduring pages 1-2, sewart2017differentfunctionalityof pages 3-3)

3. Regulation of Mad3

Cell-cycle phosphorylation

Mad3 abundance is comparatively stable through much of the cell cycle, but slower-migrating modified forms appear near S phase, increase through G2/mitosis, and decline near mitotic completion. Mad3 becomes hyperphosphorylated following checkpoint activation by microtubule depolymerization, kinetochore defects, or insufficient tension. This phosphorylation depends mainly on the Polo-like kinase Cdc5, with a smaller contribution from Aurora B/Ipl1. Replacing five candidate Cdc5-dependent serines with alanine substantially reduced the modification, supporting Mad3 as an in-vivo Cdc5-regulated target. (rancati2005mad3bubr1phosphorylationduring pages 1-2, rancati2005mad3bubr1phosphorylationduring pages 3-3)

The evidence is strongest for kinase dependence and phosphoregulation; it is less definitive that every candidate serine is directly phosphorylated by purified Cdc5. Mad3 should therefore be annotated as a cell-cycle-regulated phosphoprotein downstream of Cdc5 and Ipl1, rather than as a kinase.

Proteolytic control and dosage

Mad3 KEN30 helps couple checkpoint signaling to proteolysis. During mitosis, Mad3, Mad2, and KEN30 promote Cdc20 turnover; during G1, Mad3 degradation depends on KEN30, Cdh1, and APC/C. Thus, the same interaction motif contributes to checkpoint-complex formation and temporally restricted protein turnover. (king2007mad3kenboxes pages 1-2, king2007mad3kenboxes pages 8-10)

Protein dosage is functionally important. Approximately fourfold Mad3 overproduction caused chromosome-biorientation defects and elevated chromosome loss during recovery from anti-microtubule-drug arrest. Both insufficient and excessive Mad3 activity can therefore compromise segregation fidelity. (king2007mad3kenboxes pages 1-2, king2007mad3kenboxes pages 8-10)

4. Cellular localization and site of action

Mad3 acts in the nuclear chromosome-segregation machinery during mitosis and meiosis, but the available direct evidence does not justify annotating it as a permanently kinetochore-bound or structural spindle protein. Mad2-containing checkpoint complexes can assemble independently of intact kinetochores, supporting a model in which unattached kinetochores generate the checkpoint signal and a soluble nuclear MCC inhibits APC/C–Cdc20 more broadly. (poddar2005twocomplexesof pages 1-2)

Accordingly, the most defensible localization statement is:

  • Compartment: nucleus during chromosome division.
  • Functional site: MCC/APC/C–Cdc20 signaling system downstream of kinetochore-generated SAC signals.
  • Caveat: a precise steady-state localization pattern for budding-yeast Mad3 itself is less well established than its biochemical complex membership; localization reported for mammalian BUBR1 or fission-yeast Mad3 should not be assigned automatically to P47074.

Recent meiotic work showed that Mad3 interacts with Stu1/CLASP, a microtubule-regulatory protein found at meiotic kinetochores, spindles, and the spindle midzone. However, Stu1 retained those localizations after MAD3 deletion, so these data do not establish that Mad3 is the localization receptor for Stu1 or that all Mad3 resides at those structures. (mukherjee2024distinctrolesof pages 5-6, mukherjee2024checkpointindependentassociationof pages 25-28)

5. Genetic and cellular phenotypes

MAD3 deletion or disruption of its Cdc20-binding KEN motif compromises arrest after spindle damage and causes sensitivity to benomyl/nocodazole-like microtubule perturbations. The phenotype is failure to restrain cell-cycle progression despite defective spindle attachment, leading to chromosome mis-segregation. Conversely, overexpression also impairs biorientation, showing that checkpoint stoichiometry must be controlled. (king2007mad3kenboxes pages 1-2, king2007mad3kenboxes pages 10-11, daniel2006diversefunctionsof pages 5-7)

A genome-scale interaction study identified broad synthetic interactions for spindle-checkpoint genes but found that Mad3 shared relatively few interactions with the other checkpoint mutants. This supports functional specialization within the pathway, although high-throughput interaction profiles are less mechanistically decisive than the direct MCC and KEN-motif experiments. (daniel2006diversefunctionsof pages 5-7)

6. Major 2024 development: a checkpoint-independent role in meiosis I

The most important recent advance is the demonstration that Mad3 also promotes meiotic chromosome capture independently of its canonical MCC function. A peer-reviewed Current Biology study published in August 2024 found that Mad3 associates with the N-terminal TOGL1 domain of Stu1/CLASP, a conserved microtubule plus-end regulator. Mad3-FLAG immunoprecipitation/mass spectrometry, live-cell imaging, and separation-of-function genetics showed that this pathway helps homologous chromosomes attach to and align on the meiosis-I spindle. [Mukherjee, Spanos & Marston, 2024; DOI URL: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2024.07.025]. (mukherjee2024distinctrolesof pages 5-6, mukherjee2024distinctrolesof pages 18-24, mukherjee2024distinctrolesof pages 24-27)

Deleting MAD2 or MAD3 shortened meiotic metaphase through the expected checkpoint defect, whereas removing Stu1 TOGL1 did not reproduce that timing phenotype. This separates Mad3’s canonical checkpoint-delay function from its Stu1-dependent chromosome-capture function. (mukherjee2024distinctrolesof pages 5-6)

Quantitatively, same-pole homolog segregation occurred in approximately 5% of mad3Δ, 11% of stu1ΔTOGL1, and 13% of double-mutant cells. The absence of strong additivity places Mad3 and Stu1 TOGL1 in the same pathway. More strikingly, achiasmate mini-chromosomes segregated nearly randomly—about 50% mis-segregation—in mad3Δ, stu1ΔTOGL1, and double mutants. Stu1ΔTOGL1 nevertheless remained at kinetochores, spindles, and the spindle midzone; its meiotic kinetochore intensity was not significantly altered in the tested comparison (p=0.709; 50 cells per condition). Thus, TOGL1 is a separation-of-function interaction module rather than a general Stu1 localization or spindle-assembly determinant. (mukherjee2024distinctrolesof pages 5-6, mukherjee2024checkpointindependentassociationof pages 4-6, mukherjee2024checkpointindependentassociationof pages 25-28)

The January 2024 bioRxiv report (https://doi.org/10.1101/2024.01.04.574163) was a preprint; the principal conclusions were subsequently represented in the peer-reviewed August 2024 Current Biology article. The final peer-reviewed paper should be preferred for annotation. (mukherjee2024checkpointindependentassociationof pages 4-6, mukherjee2024distinctrolesof pages 24-27)

7. Current applications and broader significance

Mad3 is primarily a research model, not a clinical or industrial intervention target in yeast. Its established applications include:

  1. Dissecting spindle-checkpoint signal propagation: KEN mutants separate Cdc20 binding, MCC assembly, and protein-turnover functions.
  2. Testing chromosome-segregation fidelity: mad3Δ and controlled overexpression provide complementary loss- and gain-of-function perturbations for benomyl/nocodazole recovery and chromosome-loss assays.
  3. Studying checkpoint stoichiometry and adaptation: Mad3 illustrates how abundance and degradation of an inhibitor determine checkpoint strength.
  4. Separating canonical and noncanonical meiotic functions: the Mad3–Stu1/CLASP interaction now provides a tractable system for distinguishing cell-cycle delay from active chromosome capture and alignment.
  5. Evolutionary comparison: budding-yeast Mad3 is useful for identifying the conserved non-catalytic core of vertebrate BUBR1 while avoiding assumptions based on BUBR1’s lineage-specific C-terminal kinase/pseudokinase architecture.

The broader relevance is genome stability. SAC failure permits premature anaphase and aneuploidy, whereas excessive checkpoint output can delay division or perturb biorientation. Nevertheless, mechanistic or therapeutic claims about human BUBR1 must be validated separately rather than inferred directly from yeast Mad3.

8. Recommended functional annotation

MAD3/YJL013C encodes a non-enzymatic Mad3/BubR1-family spindle-assembly-checkpoint effector. Mad3 forms the mitotic checkpoint complex with Mad2, Bub3, and Cdc20 and inhibits APC/C–Cdc20, delaying anaphase until kinetochore–microtubule attachment defects are corrected. Its conserved KEN motifs regulate Cdc20 binding, MCC formation, Cdc20 turnover, and Mad3 degradation; Mad3 is also regulated by Cdc5- and Ipl1-dependent mitotic phosphorylation. In meiosis I, Mad3 additionally associates with the Stu1/CLASP TOGL1 domain to promote spindle capture and segregation of difficult-to-biorient or achiasmate chromosomes independently of checkpoint timing.

MAD3 (P47074, YJL013C) - curation notes

Working notes for the GO annotation review of Saccharomyces cerevisiae MAD3, the
budding-yeast orthologue of BubR1. Inline citations use the repository convention
[PMID:NNN "verbatim text"]; claims from papers that are not in the publication cache are
attributed to the deep-research file (MAD3-deep-research-falcon.md) with their DOI.

1. Identity and architecture

  • MAD3 = YJL013C = UniProt P47074, 515 aa, 58 kDa. Nuclear. Not essential.
    PMID:10704439
  • Two regions of homology to the N-terminus of Bub1 (46-47 % identity); homology region I
    (Bub1 N-terminal/TPR-like, PROSITE BUB1_N 67-228) and homology region II (GLEBS motif,
    354-401). No kinase domain of any kind, unlike vertebrate BubR1's pseudokinase.
    PMID:17227844
  • Mad3 arose from a fungal duplication of Bub1 and retained only the non-catalytic part.
    PMID:17227844
  • Extended (~200 A) molecule; 1:1 heterodimer with the Bub3 beta-propeller via the GLEBS
    motif; crystal structure PDB 2I3T (Mad3 354-400 + Bub3).
    PMID:17227844
    PMID:17227844
  • Key residues: GIGS156-159 (region I) needed for Cdc20 binding; E382 (region II, buried salt
    bridge with Bub3 R197) needed for Bub3 binding; both mutants benomyl-sensitive
    (UniProt MUTAGEN features from PMID:10704439).
    PMID:17227844
  • KEN boxes KEN30 and KEN296 (from King et al. 2007 PLoS ONE, doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0000342,
    not cached): KEN30 is essential for Cdc20 binding and MCC assembly.
    [file:yeast/MAD3/MAD3-deep-research-falcon.md "KEN30-to-AAA mutation eliminates detectable Cdc20 binding, prevents MCC assembly, and yields a checkpoint-null phenotype."]

2. Interactions and the mitotic checkpoint complex

  • Two-hybrid: Mad3 interacts with Bub3 and Cdc20 but not Mad1, Mad2, Bub1 or Bub2.
    PMID:10704439
  • Co-IP: Bub3 binding is constitutive and independent of other checkpoint proteins; Cdc20 and
    Mad2 co-associate with Mad3 in mitotically arrested cells.
    PMID:10704439
    PMID:10704439
  • Fraschini 2001: Mad2, Mad3, Bub3 co-fractionate with Cdc20; complex formation needs all
    checkpoint proteins but not intact kinetochores; Bub3 WD40 mutants lose Mad3/Mad2/Cdc20
    binding and the checkpoint.
    PMID:11726501
    PMID:11726501
  • Poddar 2005: Mad2/Mad3/Bub3 are in excess over Cdc20 and Cdc27; MCC (Mad2-Mad3-Bub3-Cdc20)
    is a minor Mad2 pool distinct from the larger Mad2-Cdc20 pool; MCC forms in ndc10-1 cells
    without kinetochores; Mad3 degron shows Mad3 is needed to maintain an arrest.
    PMID:15879521
    PMID:15879521
    PMID:15879521
  • SGD identifiers in the IPI/IGI rows: S000003084 = CDC20, S000003567 = MAD2, S000005552 =
    BUB3, S000003420 = BUB1, S000005032 = TOP2 (checked against the sibling UniProt records and
    UniProt cross-references).

3. Molecular function: APC/C-Cdc20 inhibition (Foster and Morgan 2012)

  • Purified Mad3-Bub3 (co-expressed in E. coli) inhibits Cdc20-dependent securin
    ubiquitination by purified yeast APC/C.
    PMID:22940250
  • Synergy with Mad2 brings the IC50 to 6 nM.
    PMID:22940250
  • Mad3-Bub3 stimulates Cdc20 binding to the APC/C and Cdc20 autoubiquitination (opposite to
    Mad2); the MCC is not a global APC/C inhibitor but blocks substrate targeting.
    PMID:22940250
    PMID:22940250
  • Pseudosubstrate mechanism via KEN boxes.
    PMID:22940250
  • This is the basis for resolving the ND molecular_function row and the mechanistic
    Cdc20 protein-binding rows to GO:1990948 ubiquitin ligase inhibitor activity (the term
    used for human BUB1B in this repository), and for the GO:1902499 IDA row.

4. Checkpoint phenotypes

  • mad3 disruptions: benomyl sensitive, continue dividing in microcolony assays under
    microtubule perturbation.
    PMID:10704439
    PMID:10704439
  • mad3 delta fails to establish a benomyl arrest (securin destroyed with normal timing).
    PMID:22940250
  • Cdc20 instability during a SAC arrest requires Mad3 (King 2007; Pan and Chen 2004).
    PMID:22940250
  • Phosphorylation on checkpoint activation depends on Cdc5 (Polo) and Ipl1 (Aurora B)
    (Rancati et al. 2005 Cell Cycle, doi:10.4161/cc.4.7.1829, not cached).
    [file:yeast/MAD3/MAD3-deep-research-falcon.md "Mad3 becomes hyperphosphorylated following checkpoint activation by microtubule depolymerization, kinetochore defects, or insufficient tension."]
  • Ser268 is a Cdk1-site phosphosite from a global study (UniProt MOD_RES, PMID:19779198).

5. Topoisomerase II checkpoint (GO:0044774 IGI with TOP2)

  • top2-B44 cells show a G2/M delay independent of DNA-damage checkpoints and of Pds1; deletion
    of MAD1, MAD2, MAD3 or IPL1 abolishes it, BUB3 is partially required.
    PMID:16651657
    PMID:16651657
  • Kept as non-core: genuine genetic requirement, mechanism not via securin, so not the core
    MCC output.

6. Meiosis

  • Cheslock 2005 (abstract only): Mad3 dispensable for exchange chromosomes but essential for
    nonexchange chromosome segregation; acts as a prophase timer in every meiosis.
    PMID:15951820
    PMID:15951820
  • Mukherjee, Spanos and Marston 2024:
    Mad3 associates with the TOGL1 domain of Stu1/CLASP and promotes homolog capture/alignment in
    meiosis I independently of checkpoint timing; achiasmate mini-chromosomes segregate randomly
    in mad3 delta.
    PMID:39079532
    PMID:39079532
  • GO:0032837 distributive segregation IMP therefore kept as non-core. No NEW term proposed for
    the Stu1-dependent role because the appropriate MF is unclear; raised in suggested_questions.

7. The GO:0051754 IBA (meiotic sister chromatid cohesion, centromeric)

  • Donors: PomBase SPCC1322.12c (bub1 kinase) and FB FBgn0263855 (Drosophila BubR1), node
    PTN000361607 (pre-duplication Bub1/Mad3 ancestor). QuickGO shows yeast BUB1 carries the same
    IBA from the same node; eight S. cerevisiae products carry GO:0051754, all cohesion/shugoshin
    machinery plus the two Bub1-family IBAs.
  • In yeasts the centromeric-cohesion function is the Bub1 kinase (H2A phosphorylation ->
    shugoshin). Mad3 has no kinase domain and no yeast study links it to cohesion; its direct
    meiotic phenotypes are timing and nonexchange segregation.
  • Graded MARK_AS_OVER_ANNOTATED with a propagation_review (PROPAGATION_BAD;
    WRONG_ORTHOLOG_OR_PARALOG + PSEUDO_OR_SUBACTIVITY_LOSS). Not REMOVE because there is no
    direct contradicting experiment (suggested in suggested_experiments). One step below the
    BUB1B row (KEEP_AS_NON_CORE), where mouse oocyte data provide some support.

8. Protein-binding IPI rows (13 rows) - policy applied

  • Cdc20 rows from mechanistic papers (PMID:10704439, PMID:11726501, PMID:15879521) ->
    MODIFY to GO:1990948 ubiquitin ligase inhibitor activity (as for BUB1B-CDC20).
  • Bub3 and Mad2 rows from mechanistic/structural papers (PMID:10704439, PMID:11726501,
    PMID:15879521, PMID:17227844) -> REMOVE. They support Mad3's MCC membership, but that is a CC
    assertion and is already present in existing GO:0033597 rows; a protein-binding MF row should not be
    converted into a cellular-component replacement.
  • High-throughput rows (PMID:10688190 Uetz Y2H, PMID:11805837 Ho HMS-PCI, PMID:14660704
    Graumann TAP-MudPIT, PMID:18719252 Yu Y2H) -> REMOVE as uninformative; interactions not
    disputed.

  MAD3 encodes the budding-yeast orthologue of BubR1, a 515-residue, non-catalytic spindle assembly
  checkpoint protein. Unlike vertebrate BubR1 it has no C-terminal kinase-like domain at all: it
  consists of an N-terminal Bub1/Mad3 TPR-like region (homology region I, containing the KEN30 motif
  and the GIGS156-159 residues needed for Cdc20 binding), a central GLEBS motif (residues 354-401,
  homology region II, Glu382) that forms a constitutive 1:1 heterodimer with the WD40 protein Bub3,
  and a disordered C-terminus. Mad3 is a nuclear protein present throughout the cell cycle. When
  kinetochores are unattached or lack tension, Mad3-Bub3 combines with Mad2 and the APC/C activator
  Cdc20 to form the mitotic checkpoint complex (MCC; Mad2-Mad3-Bub3-Cdc20), which assembles during
  mitosis even without intact kinetochores. Within the MCC the Mad3 KEN boxes act as pseudosubstrate
  motifs that occupy the degron-binding sites of Cdc20, so that APC/C-Cdc20 cannot ubiquitinate
  securin (Pds1) and cyclins; reconstitution with purified proteins shows that Mad3-Bub3 inhibits
  Cdc20-dependent securin ubiquitination and, synergistically with Mad2 (IC50 ~6 nM), locks Cdc20 on
  the APC/C while stimulating Cdc20 autoubiquitination, which keeps Cdc20 levels low during a
  checkpoint arrest. Loss of Mad3 abolishes the checkpoint: mad3 cells are benomyl-sensitive, keep
  dividing when microtubules are depolymerised, and cannot maintain a nocodazole arrest (Pds1 is
  destroyed as soon as Mad3 is depleted). Mad3 is hyperphosphorylated on checkpoint activation in a
  Cdc5 (Polo)- and Ipl1 (Aurora B)-dependent manner, and its KEN boxes also mediate Cdc20 turnover in
  mitosis and Cdh1-APC/C-dependent Mad3 turnover in G1. Mad3 is additionally required for the
  Topoisomerase II-dependent G2/M checkpoint, and in meiosis it acts as a prophase timer and is
  essential for the segregation of nonexchange (achiasmate) chromosomes, a checkpoint-independent
  role mediated through association with the TOGL1 domain of the CLASP orthologue Stu1.
