# HSC82 notes

## 2026-05-13 Falcon incorporation

Falcon supports HSC82 as the constitutive cytosolic Hsp90 isoform, distinct from the more heat-inducible HSP82 paralog [file:yeast/HSC82/HSC82-deep-research-falcon.md "HSC82 encodes the constitutive cytosolic Hsp90 isoform (Hsc82), whereas HSP82 encodes a strongly heat‑inducible isoform (Hsp82); both are ~97% identical but differ measurably in ATPase/conformational properties and stress robustness."]. Core function should therefore emphasize ATP-dependent Hsp90 chaperone cycling and client maturation, not only generic protein binding or generic complex membership [file:yeast/HSC82/HSC82-deep-research-falcon.md "Hsp90/Hsc82 functions as an ATP-dependent folding chaperone for late intermediates/clients, and specific Hsp90 mutants that disrupt client loading/closing/reopening cause client-specific maturation defects and temperature-sensitive growth."].

For MF curation, ATP binding and ATP hydrolysis are central and should be preferred over generic nucleotide binding [file:yeast/HSC82/HSC82-deep-research-falcon.md "N-terminal ATP-binding domain; ATP binding drives lid closure and N-terminal association; Hsc82 shows higher ATPase activity than Hsp82 at 30–37°C"] [file:yeast/HSC82/HSC82-deep-research-falcon.md "True but much less informative than ATP binding/ATPase activity; GO should prefer the specific ATP-related terms"]. Likewise, `protein-containing complex` is too broad because Hsc82 forms many specific Hsp90/co-chaperone/client assemblies [file:yeast/HSC82/HSC82-deep-research-falcon.md "Hsc82 forms many complexes, but specific complexes (homodimer; Sti1/Cpr6/Sba1/Cdc37/Sgt1 complexes) are more biologically useful than a generic complex term"].

Falcon supports cytosol as the core localization and treats mitochondrial, plasma membrane, and perinuclear localizations cautiously unless backed by direct HSC82-specific localization evidence [file:yeast/HSC82/HSC82-deep-research-falcon.md "Hsc82 is described as the constitutive cytosolic Hsp90 isoform; many experimental Hsc82 interactome/purification studies use cytosolic lysates, consistent with CC:cytosol annotations for both isoforms, with expression-level differences for HSC82 vs HSP82."] [file:yeast/HSC82/HSC82-deep-research-falcon.md "No direct Hsc82 localization evidence in the retrieved sources"].

Client-dependent processes such as calcineurin regulation, box C/D snoRNP/R2TP, proteasome assembly, telomere maintenance, and SGT1/kinetochore biology should be kept separate from HSC82's core Hsp90 foldase activity unless the annotation is directly tied to the relevant client evidence [file:yeast/HSC82/HSC82-deep-research-falcon.md "Direct Hsc82 binding to CNB1 and effects on CNA2/CNB1 complex state/activity support calcineurin as an Hsp90 client/regulatory target"] [file:yeast/HSC82/HSC82-deep-research-falcon.md "Hsp90 network reviews mention Tah1/Pih1 (R2TP-like) functions, but direct Hsc82-specific experimental support in the retrieved set is weak"].

## 2026-08-28 iterative re-review

Fresh Falcon research reconfirmed the identity and principal role of P15108 while adding recent mechanistic evidence. HSC82 is the abundant constitutive cytosolic Hsp90 paralog; HSP82 is stress inducible, and the two proteins are highly similar but measurably specialized [file:yeast/HSC82/HSC82-deep-research-falcon.md "The paralogs are 97% identical and differ at only 16 amino-acid positions, but these substitutions produce measurable specialization."]. Hsc82's core function is an ATP-regulated dimeric chaperone cycle that loads, stabilizes, remodels, and activates selected conformationally dynamic clients, rather than generic protein binding or generic complex membership [file:yeast/HSC82/HSC82-deep-research-falcon.md "Hsc82 is the central ATP-regulated scaffold of a multicomponent folding pathway rather than an isolated chaperone."].

The ATPase annotation remains directly supported by biochemical measurements on purified yeast Hsc82 (PMID:18492664), but its biological interpretation needs precision. Recent yeast complementation work found that nucleotide binding is indispensable while hydrolysis-defective Hsp90 can retain limited viability-supporting activity; ATP hydrolysis controls cycle timing and efficiency rather than serving as an absolutely required power stroke [file:yeast/HSC82/HSC82-deep-research-falcon.md "An important 2023 revision to the classical mechanism is that **ATP binding and nucleotide exchange appear more fundamental than ATP hydrolysis itself**."] [file:yeast/HSC82/HSC82-deep-research-falcon.md "The authors concluded that passage between ATP- and ADP-associated conformations through nucleotide exchange can sustain essential Hsp90 function, while hydrolysis normally controls cycle timing and responsiveness to clients and cochaperones."]. Because the decisive 2023 complementation experiments used Hsp82 alleles, this is conserved-mechanism/paralog evidence rather than a direct P15108 mutant result.

Recent client-mapping work also supports selective recognition of exposed intrinsically disordered regions rather than a single sequence motif, while emphasizing that cochaperones and different Hsp90 surfaces refine client selection [file:yeast/HSC82/HSC82-deep-research-falcon.md "Hsp90 preferentially contacted **intrinsically disordered regions**, largely independently of their precise sequence or charge."] This strengthens GO:0140662 (ATP-dependent protein folding chaperone) as the informative replacement for obsolete GO:0051082 and argues against treating high-throughput interaction hits or downstream client pathways as additional core molecular functions.

Localization remains centered on the soluble cytosolic Hsp90 system. Proteomic detections at the plasma membrane or mitochondrion do not establish stable resident pools, and perinuclear localization is plausible but not demonstrated by retrieved HSC82-specific imaging [file:yeast/HSC82/HSC82-deep-research-falcon.md "The retrieved literature did not include a dedicated quantitative microscopy study establishing a fixed cytosol-to-nucleus distribution for P15108."]. These locations should therefore remain non-core or unresolved rather than being elevated to defining HSC82 biology.

The two initially ambiguous client-pathway annotations are retained cautiously at family level. The cached HAP1/heme abstract identifies Hsp82 rather than Hsc82 in the purified complex, so response to oxygen remains an unverified paralog extrapolation and a non-core role [PMID:9632766 "Our data suggest that this complex contains HAP1 and four other cellular proteins including Hsp82 and Ydj1."]. The telomerase abstract reports yeast-Hsp90-dependent telomere length but names Hsp82p in its mechanistic assays; deference to the experimental curator supports retaining telomere maintenance as non-core without claiming direct HSC82 evidence [PMID:17954556 "Importantly, telomere length and telomerase telomere occupancy was yeast Hsp90 dependent."].
