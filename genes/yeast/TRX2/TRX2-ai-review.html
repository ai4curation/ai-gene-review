Gene Description

TRX2 (YGR209C) encodes thioredoxin-2, one of the two cytoplasmic thioredoxins of S. cerevisiae (78% identical to Trx1; Trx3 is mitochondrial). It is a CGPC-active-site disulfide oxidoreductase reduced by NADPH-thioredoxin reductase Trr1. Reduced Trx2 donates electrons to PAPS reductase (Met16) in sulfate assimilation, to ribonucleotide reductase and to thiol peroxidases (Tsa1, Tsa2, Ahp1, Dot5), reduces protein disulfides and glutathione disulfide (backing up glutathione reductase) and deglutathionylates proteins such as the vacuolar TRP channel Yvc1. TRX2 is strongly induced by H2O2 and diamide through Yap1 and Skn7, and Trx2 feeds back to inactivate Yap1. Under standard tested growth conditions, trx1 trx2 double mutants are methionine auxotrophs. Like Trx1, Trx2 also forms the redox-independent LMA1 complex with Pbi2 that promotes Sec18-dependent homotypic vacuole fusion and vacuole inheritance. It is found in the cytosol and nucleus. Sulfate assimilation can recover without both cytoplasmic thioredoxins under conditions that minimize reactive oxygen species; the alternative electron donor is unresolved.

Core Functions

Cytosolic/nuclear thioredoxin reducing protein disulfides and glutathione disulfide with electrons from NADPH via Trr1; the oxidant-inducible paralogue that supplies peroxiredoxins and other thiol enzymes during oxidative stress and maintains thiol redox homeostasis (redundantly with TRX1).
