CC   -!- FUNCTION: Lipoamide dehydrogenase is a component of the alpha-ketoacid
CC       dehydrogenase complexes. This includes the pyruvate dehydrogenase
CC       complex, which catalyzes the overall conversion of pyruvate to acetyl-
CC       CoA and CO(2), and the 2-oxoglutarate dehydrogenase complex, which
CC       catalyzes the overall conversion of 2-oxoglutarate to succinyl-CoA and
CC       CO(2) (PubMed:2821168). Also acts as a component of the glycine
CC       cleavage system (glycine decarboxylase complex), which catalyzes the
CC       degradation of glycine (PubMed:7498764). {ECO:0000269|PubMed:2821168,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:7498764}.

CC   -!- SUBUNIT: LPD1 is a homodimer. Eukaryotic pyruvate dehydrogenase (PDH)
CC       complexes are organized as a core consisting of the oligomeric
CC       dihydrolipoamide acetyl-transferase (E2), around which are arranged
CC       multiple copies of pyruvate dehydrogenase (E1), dihydrolipoamide
CC       dehydrogenase (E3) and protein X (E3BP) bound by non-covalent bonds
CC       (PubMed:12756305, PubMed:9538259, Ref.13). Component of the 2-
CC       oxoglutarate dehydrogenase complex (OGDC), also called alpha-
CC       ketoglutarate dehydrogenase (KGDH) complex. The complex is composed of
CC       the catalytic subunits OGDH (2-oxoglutarate dehydrogenase KGD1; also
CC       called E1 subunit), DLST (dihydrolipoamide succinyltransferase KGD2;
CC       also called E2 subunit) and DLD (dihydrolipoamide dehydrogenase LPD1;
CC       also called E3 subunit), and the assembly factor KGD4 (PubMed:2072900,
CC       PubMed:25165143). LPD1 is a component of the glycine decarboxylase
CC       complex (GDC), which is composed of four proteins: P, T, L and H
CC       (PubMed:7498764). {ECO:0000269|PubMed:12756305,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:2072900, ECO:0000269|PubMed:25165143,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:7498764, ECO:0000269|PubMed:9538259,
CC       ECO:0000269|Ref.13}.
