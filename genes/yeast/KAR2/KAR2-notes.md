# KAR2 review notes

## 2026-08-28 annotation audit

Kar2p is the essential yeast ER-lumen BiP/Hsp70. The original characterization identifies a functional secretory signal and terminal HDEL ER-retention signal and reports that deletion is lethal [PMID:2661018, "a functional secretory signal sequence, a yeast endoplasmic reticulum retention signal (HDEL) at the carboxyl terminus"; "Deletion of the KAR2 gene generated a recessive lethal mutation"]. Purified Kar2p has direct ATPase activity [PMID:1325440, "It is active as a homodimer and exhibits ATPase activity with a specific activity of 2 pmol/min/micrograms of protein."].

The mechanistically best-supported core roles are ATP-dependent ER protein folding and protein import. Depletion of BiP causes secretory-protein precursors to accumulate and supports a requirement after nascent-chain membrane association [PMID:1996357, "BiP function is required for translocation of soluble proteins into the endoplasmic reticulum at a stage beyond the initial nascent chain-membrane association."]. Reconstitution establishes the post-translational molecular-ratchet mechanism [PMID:10367885, "BiP (Kar2p), a member of the Hsp70 family resident in the ER lumen, acts as a molecular ratchet during translocation of the secretory protein prepro-alpha factor through the channel formed by the Sec complex."], while mutant microsomes establish requirements in both co- and post-translational import [PMID:7568189, "Sec63p and Kar2p are necessary for both co- and posttranslational translocation in yeast."].

Kar2p also has direct ER quality-control roles. ERAD substrates aggregate and are stabilized in a kar2 mutant [PMID:11381090, "these ERAD substrates are stabilized and aggregate in the ER at elevated temperatures when BiP, the lumenal Hsp70 molecular chaperone, is mutated"], and Kar2p participates with Yos9p and Hrd3p in the luminal surveillance complex [PMID:16873065, "Yos9p, together with Kar2p and Hrd3p, forms a luminal surveillance complex that both recruits nonnative proteins to the core ERAD machinery"]. Kar2-dependent Ire1 regulation is supported by mutant phenotypes, but the authors explicitly present unfolded-client competition for Kar2 as speculation rather than an established titration mechanism [PMID:12808051, "We speculate that recognition of unfolded proteins is based on their competition with Ire1 for binding with BiP/Kar2."].

All 41 distinct GOA signatures were reconciled with their explicit relation qualifiers. The 11 IBA rows trace to three cached PTHR19375 PAINT nodes: PTN000452648 (conserved Hsp70 ATPase/chaperone functions), PTN001834223 (ER-BiP localization, complex, ERAD and UPR functions), and PTN002321897 (broad cytoplasm). No IBA was treated as pairwise similarity transfer. ATP hydrolysis, folding, refolding, ER-lumen localization, ERAD, ER-chaperone-complex membership, and UPR rows are retained as core. Heat-shock-protein binding and broad membrane, nucleus, and cytoplasm calls are defensible but non-core. In particular, cytoplasm is broad enough to include the ER and was not treated as a mutually exclusive cytosolic localization.

The obsolete GO:0051082 `unfolded protein binding` rows are modified to chaperone activity. A NEW GO:0140662 `ATP-dependent protein folding chaperone` row captures the ATP-coupled function more precisely. Direct Kar2/Lhs1 evidence establishes nucleotide-exchange stimulation and coupled ATPase regulation [PMID:14704430, "Lhs1p stimulated Kar2p by providing a specific nucleotide exchange activity, whereas Kar2p reciprocally activated the Lhs1p adenosine triphosphatase (ATPase). The two ATPase activities are coupled, and their coordinated regulation is essential for normal function in vivo."], while PMID:23704572 separately supports Kar2-client engagement. Generic GO:0005515 `protein binding` interaction-survey rows remain marked over-annotated because they do not convey Kar2p's client-folding or cochaperone mechanism.
