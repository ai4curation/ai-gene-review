# Functional annotation report: *Saccharomyces cerevisiae* YPD1 (Q07688)

## Executive summary

The requested protein is unambiguously the *S. cerevisiae* S288c phosphorelay intermediate **Ypd1**, encoded by **YPD1/YDL235C** (UniProt **Q07688**), not a similarly named protein from another organism. Direct yeast literature describes a 167-residue, approximately 19–20-kDa, soluble histidine-containing phosphotransfer (**HPt**) protein whose experimentally determined all-helical four-helix-bundle structure and exposed phosphoacceptor **His64** agree with the supplied YPD1-family, Hpt/PF01627, IPR008207, IPR036641, and IPR045871 annotations. Thus, the database identity, organism, family, and domains are mutually consistent. (xu2001thestructureand pages 38-41, xu2003theyeastypd1sln1 pages 1-2)

Ypd1 is not an ATP-dependent kinase or phosphatase. Its primary function is to act as a mobile phosphate-transfer intermediate and branch point in the Sln1 multistep phosphorelay. It accepts phosphate from the receiver domain of Sln1 and transfers it to either Ssk1 or Skn7. The chemically defined sequence is **Sln1-His576 → Sln1-Asp1144 → Ypd1-His64 → response-regulator Asp**, a His–Asp–His–Asp relay. Mutation of Ypd1 His64 prevents its phosphorylation and disrupts phosphate transfer, establishing His64 as the essential phosphoacceptor/donor site. (xu2001thestructureand pages 38-41, kennedy2016thebiochemicalcharacterization pages 36-41, kennedy2016thebiochemicalcharacterizationa pages 36-41)

## 1. Identity and terminology

**Verified identifiers:** gene **YPD1**, ordered locus **YDL235C**, organism *Saccharomyces cerevisiae* strain S288c, UniProt **Q07688**. The literature’s “Ypd1,” “Ypd1p,” and “YPD1” refer to this HPt protein when the organism is *S. cerevisiae*. “Tyrosine phosphatase-dependent protein 1” is a historical alternative name and should not be interpreted as evidence that Ypd1 is itself a tyrosine phosphatase.

The protein is a **stand-alone HPt domain**, rather than the kinase or output response regulator. HPt proteins transiently carry a high-energy phosphohistidine and mediate phosphotransfer between receiver domains. Ypd1’s four-helix-bundle fold, exposed His64, and response-regulator-binding surface directly support the supplied domain annotation. (kennedy2019roleofthe pages 1-2, xu2003theyeastypd1sln1 pages 1-2)

Reports about Ypd1 homologs in *Candida albicans*, *Cryptococcus neoformans*, *Aspergillus fumigatus*, or other fungi are not direct evidence for S288c Ypd1. For example, *C. albicans* YPD1 is dispensable and has distinct mutant phenotypes, whereas deletion of *S. cerevisiae* YPD1 is lethal under ordinary genetic backgrounds. (mavrianos2014twocomponenthistidinephosphotransfer pages 1-2, mavrianos2014twocomponenthistidinephosphotransfer pages 7-7)

## 2. Primary molecular function and substrate specificity

Ypd1 catalyzes reversible phosphotransfer involving its **N-phosphorylated His64**. Its physiological upstream phosphodonor is the phosphorylated receiver domain of **Sln1**, and its physiological downstream phosphate acceptors are the receiver domains of **Ssk1** and **Skn7**. ATP is used by Sln1 for initial autophosphorylation, not by Ypd1; transfer from phospho-Sln1 to Ypd1 and onward is ATP-independent. (kennedy2019roleofthe pages 1-2, mavrianos2014twocomponenthistidinephosphotransfer pages 1-2)

The direct relay is:

1. Sln1 autophosphorylates at **His576** using ATP.
2. Phosphate transfers intramolecularly to **Asp1144** in Sln1’s receiver domain.
3. Sln1-Asp1144 transfers phosphate to **Ypd1-His64**.
4. Phospho-Ypd1 transfers phosphate to a receiver-domain aspartate in Ssk1 or Skn7. (kennedy2016thebiochemicalcharacterization pages 36-41, kennedy2016thebiochemicalcharacterizationa pages 36-41)

Ypd1 is therefore best classified as a **signaling phosphotransfer protein**, not an enzyme with a freely diffusible small-molecule substrate. Its effective substrate specificity is protein-partner specificity: Sln1-R1 as donor/acceptor upstream and Ssk1-R2 or Skn7-R3 downstream. In vitro transfer involving bacterial CheY demonstrates some biochemical promiscuity, but this is not known to be physiologically relevant. (xu2001thestructureand pages 38-41, xu2003theyeastypd1sln1 pages 9-11)

## 3. Pathway roles

### 3.1 Sln1–Ypd1–Ssk1 control of the HOG pathway

Under normal osmolarity, active plasma-membrane Sln1 continuously feeds phosphate through Ypd1 to Ssk1. Phosphorylated Ssk1 does not activate the redundant MAP3Ks Ssk2/Ssk22; consequently Pbs2 and Hog1 remain inactive. Ypd1 thus acts functionally as an upstream **negative regulator of basal HOG signaling**, although its immediate biochemical action is positive phosphate delivery to Ssk1. (lu2003saccharomycescerevisiaehistidine pages 1-3, dexter2015robustnetworkstructure pages 1-2)

Hyperosmotic stress reduces Sln1-relay output. Ssk1 becomes dephosphorylated, binds/activates Ssk2 and Ssk22, and triggers **Ssk2/Ssk22 → Pbs2 → Hog1** signaling. Hog1 then drives transcriptional and metabolic osmoadaptation, including increased glycerol accumulation. HOG activation can occur within approximately one minute of osmotic shock, illustrating the relay’s rapid response. (porter2005acommondocking pages 1-2, dexter2015robustnetworkstructure pages 1-2)

YPD1 deletion or severe depletion removes phosphate delivery to Ssk1 and causes inappropriate HOG activation. The resulting lethality is pathway-based rather than evidence for a constitutive housekeeping reaction: comparative and genetic evidence associates loss of YPD1 or SLN1 with constitutive HOG signaling, and Ypd1 underexpression produces a clear growth defect. (mavrianos2014twocomponenthistidinephosphotransfer pages 7-7, dexter2015robustnetworkstructure pages 7-8)

### 3.2 Sln1–Ypd1–Skn7 branch

Ypd1 also transfers phosphate to **Skn7**, a predominantly nuclear response regulator and transcription factor. This branch contributes to cell-wall biosynthesis/stress responses and cell-cycle-associated regulation. It is mechanistically distinct from the Ssk1-HOG branch: Ypd1 is the common bifurcation component, but Skn7 acts primarily through transcriptional regulation rather than initiating the Ssk2/Ssk22–Pbs2–Hog1 cascade. (lu2003saccharomycescerevisiaehistidine pages 1-3, porter2005acommondocking pages 1-2)

Oxidative-stress regulation involving Skn7 is more nuanced: Skn7 participates in antioxidant responses, but not every oxidative-stress output necessarily requires its Sln1-dependent receiver phosphorylation. Accordingly, Ypd1 should be annotated confidently for Sln1-dependent Skn7 phosphotransfer, but not as the sole conduit for all Skn7 oxidative-stress functions. (kennedy2019roleofthe pages 1-2, mavrianos2014twocomponenthistidinephosphotransfer pages 1-2)

## 4. Cellular localization

Ypd1 is a soluble intracellular protein detected in **both cytosol and nucleus**. GFP and tandem-GFP fusion microscopy, including confocal imaging with nuclear DNA staining in a *ypd1Δ* replacement background, supported nucleocytoplasmic distribution. This localization is mechanistically appropriate: Sln1 is membrane-associated, Ssk1 is cytosolic, and Skn7 is predominantly nuclear. (lu2003saccharomycescerevisiaehistidine pages 1-3)

The influential 2003 interpretation is that Ypd1 shuttles between nucleus and cytoplasm to phosphorylate both Ssk1 and Skn7. No obvious stress-dependent relocalization of Ssk1 or Skn7 was observed. A later engineering study did not detect marked nuclear accumulation of GFP-Ypd1 and questioned whether active shuttling was required. The conservative current annotation is therefore **nuclear and cytosolic localization permitting compartment-spanning phosphotransfer**; regulated or directional shuttling is plausible but less firmly established than dual-compartment presence. (lu2003saccharomycescerevisiaehistidine pages 1-3, bury2018designcharacterizationand pages 7-8)

## 5. Structural and mechanistic evidence

X-ray crystallography shows an all-helical HPt fold centered on a **four-helix bundle**, with His64 solvent-exposed. Structures of the Ypd1–Sln1 receiver-domain complex were solved in two crystal forms at **2.1 and 2.3 Å** resolution. The complex positions Ypd1 His64 near Sln1 Asp1144; modeling His64 into its conserved rotamer and adding the required Mg²⁺ yields geometry compatible with direct His–Asp transfer without large backbone rearrangements. (xu2003theyeastypd1sln1 pages 1-2, xu2003theyeastypd1sln1 pages 9-11)

The principal partner-binding region is a largely hydrophobic patch between His64 and helix A, estimated at approximately **690 Å²**, surrounded by polar and charged residues. Alanine scanning and yeast two-hybrid experiments identified a common docking region used by Sln1-R1, Ssk1-R2, and Skn7-R3: ten Ypd1 surface residues contributed to interaction with all three domains, while peripheral residues appeared to tune partner specificity. Eight of the ten core residues also contacted Sln1-R1 in the crystal structure. (xu2003theyeastypd1sln1 pages 1-2, porter2005acommondocking pages 6-7)

His64-adjacent residues are important for catalytic geometry. N61 and K67 help stabilize/position the active-site region; K67 and R90 contact Sln1-R1 in the phosphoryl-analog complex. Substitution of conserved **Gly68**, four residues after His64, with bulky, hydrophobic, or charged residues nearly abolished phosphate acceptance from Sln1-R1 while causing only modest affinity changes. This indicates failure to form a productive transition-state geometry rather than simple loss of binding. (kennedy2019roleofthe pages 1-2)

The common hydrophobic docking site explains how one small HPt protein recognizes three homologous receiver domains, while less-conserved peripheral electrostatic and loop contacts provide discrimination. Only about **11.5%** of Ypd1’s surface was buried in the Sln1-R1 complex, consistent with a transient signaling complex, yet the interface showed high shape complementarity. (porter2005acommondocking pages 6-7)

## 6. Network robustness and quantitative observations

Ypd1 is not merely a passive wire. Mathematical modeling and controlled expression experiments indicate that a relatively large cellular pool of Ypd1 buffers fluctuations in Sln1, Ssk1, and relay flux, preventing accidental HOG activation under normal conditions. Moderate expression perturbations were tolerated, whereas Ypd1 depletion caused inappropriate pathway activation and toxicity. (dexter2015robustnetworkstructure pages 1-2, dexter2015robustnetworkstructure pages 7-8)

The 2015 experiments used inducible single-copy alleles and high-copy 2μ plasmids estimated at **15–50 copies per cell**. Massive Ypd1 overexpression did not produce the growth defect seen with strong Sln1 or Ssk1 overexpression; conversely, low Ypd1 expression caused growth defects at 0–5 nM inducer. These findings support an expert interpretation of Ypd1 as an **excess intermediate/buffer** whose abundance makes HOG repression robust until its free pool is depleted. (dexter2015robustnetworkstructure pages 7-8)

This architecture provides a potential evolutionary advantage over a simple two-component circuit: an intermediate present in excess can absorb component-level fluctuations while still transmitting a rapid environmental signal. That conclusion combines experimental depletion/overexpression data with a biochemically constrained mathematical model and should be regarded as strongly supported systems-level interpretation rather than a separate catalytic function. (dexter2015robustnetworkstructure pages 1-2, dexter2015robustnetworkstructure pages 7-8)

## 7. Applications and real-world relevance

### Experimental and synthetic-biology use

Ypd1 has been exploited as the endogenous relay component in an optogenetic proof of concept. A blue-light-responsive LOV–Sln1 hybrid kinase altered ATP-dependent autophosphorylation and phosphate transfer to Ypd1. In yeast lacking native Sln1, illumination propagated through Ypd1 to alter downstream signaling, decreasing steady-state OCH1 reporter expression by about **40%** and producing persistent Hog1 nuclear accumulation. This demonstrates that the Sln1–Ypd1 relay can couple engineered sensory inputs to native transcriptional and MAPK outputs. (bury2018designcharacterizationand pages 1-2)

This remains a laboratory implementation rather than an established industrial Ypd1 product. Its practical value lies in programmable stress signaling, studies of intracellular signal diffusion, and potential control of yeast gene expression. The study also reported up to approximately threefold light-induced Hog1 nuclear enrichment in selected cells, compared with up to fourfold following salt stress. (bury2018designcharacterizationand pages 7-8)

### Antifungal relevance

Fungal histidine-kinase/HPt systems are attractive antifungal targets because canonical His–Asp relays are absent from animals. However, most drug-target evidence concerns Ypd1 homologs in pathogenic fungi, not S288c Ypd1 itself. Differences in essentiality—including dispensability of YPD1 in *C. albicans*—show why results cannot be transferred uncritically across species. (mavrianos2014twocomponenthistidinephosphotransfer pages 1-2, mavrianos2014twocomponenthistidinephosphotransfer pages 7-7)

## 8. Recent research, 2023–2024

The search found no 2023–2024 primary study that overturns or substantially revises the direct mechanistic annotation of S288c Ypd1. Recent work has instead extended fungal HPt structural principles and examined broader HOG-network coordination. A July 2024 *Communications Biology* study analyzed recognition of fungal HPt proteins using receiver domains and HPts from other fungi; its findings support the continuing relevance of low-affinity, geometry-dependent receiver–HPt recognition, but they are **comparative evidence**, not direct experiments on Q07688. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1038/s42003-024-06459-0.

A 2024 study of yeast MAPK cross-talk confirms the contemporary importance of the Sln1 and Sho1 branches in coordinating osmotic responses, but focuses on downstream MAPK sharing rather than revising Ypd1’s biochemical role. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1091/mbc.e24-06-0270. Consequently, precise foundational studies from 1999–2019 remain the authoritative sources for Ypd1’s catalytic residue, structure, localization, and partner specificity.

## 9. Evidence-weighted annotation

**High-confidence molecular function:** histidine-containing phosphotransfer intermediate in the Sln1 phosphorelay; accepts phosphate on His64 from Sln1-Asp1144 and donates it to Ssk1 or Skn7 receiver domains. This is supported by genetics, mutagenesis, in-vitro transfer, and structural complexes. (kennedy2019roleofthe pages 1-2, kennedy2016thebiochemicalcharacterization pages 36-41, xu2003theyeastypd1sln1 pages 1-2)

**High-confidence biological processes:** negative control of basal HOG signaling through Ssk1 phosphorylation; activation of osmoadaptation when relay flux falls; Sln1-dependent Skn7 regulation associated with cell-wall and cell-cycle outputs. (lu2003saccharomycescerevisiaehistidine pages 1-3, porter2005acommondocking pages 1-2, dexter2015robustnetworkstructure pages 1-2)

**High-confidence localization:** cytosol and nucleus. **Moderate-confidence refinement:** dynamic nucleocytoplasmic shuttling, because dual localization is direct but later imaging did not show conspicuous nuclear accumulation. (lu2003saccharomycescerevisiaehistidine pages 1-3, bury2018designcharacterizationand pages 7-8)

**High-confidence structural annotation:** soluble four-helix HPt fold with exposed His64 and a shared hydrophobic response-regulator docking surface. (xu2003theyeastypd1sln1 pages 1-2, porter2005acommondocking pages 6-7)

**Overall conclusion:** Ypd1 is the central mobile phosphohistidine branch point connecting the membrane Sln1 sensor to cytosolic Ssk1/HOG control and nuclear Skn7 regulation. Its essentiality arises primarily from preventing constitutive, toxic HOG activation under non-stress conditions, while its excess cellular pool makes that repression robust to fluctuations. Its dual-compartment distribution and reusable receiver-domain interface are integral to its signaling role rather than secondary pleiotropic features. (mavrianos2014twocomponenthistidinephosphotransfer pages 7-7, lu2003saccharomycescerevisiaehistidine pages 1-3, dexter2015robustnetworkstructure pages 1-2)
