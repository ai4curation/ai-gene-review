PTM1 (P32857) GOST cavity and lipid binding: hypothesis evaluation

Hypothesis slug: kgap-ptm1-gost-cavity-lipid-binding Target: Saccharomyces cerevisiae S288C PTM1 / YKL039W, UniProt P32857, SGD:S000001522, NCBITaxon:559292 Source: genes/yeast/PTM1/PTM1-ai-review.yaml, focus type free_text Iterations completed: 3. Findings recorded: 8. Papers reviewed: 16.

Summary

Verdict: partially supported. Three of the four limbs of the seed hypothesis hold under direct computational test, and one fails.

PTM1 adopts the GOST fold. A sequence-independent structural alignment of the AlphaFold model AF-P32857-F1 against the TMEM87A cryo-EM structure (PDB 8CTJ chain A) gives a TM-score of 0.657 with 243 core residue pairs below 5 Å at 17.3 percent sequence identity, and the alignment covers the N-terminal extramembrane region that GPCRs do not have. Wntless aligns at 0.409 over 111 core residues once the search is seeded transitively through TMEM87A. PTM1 contains a large buried cavity inside the seven-helix bundle, 1884 Å³ in the mature protein under a single consistent protocol, with a lining that is 60.8 percent hydrophobic and a mean Kyte-Doolittle value of +0.69. That cavity sits at the same place as the TMEM87A cavity: after superposition the two centroids are 5.5 Å apart, 89 percent of PTM1 cavity voxels fall within 5 Å of TMEM87A cavity voxels, and 56 of 75 mappable lining residues (75 percent) map onto TMEM87A lining residues against a permutation expectation of 26 ± 5 percent (2000 permutations, empirical p < 0.0005).

The two alternatives named in the hypothesis are both poorly supported. PTM1 lacks all three class A GPCR activation motifs (DRY/ERY, NPxxY, CWxP), as do YHL017W, TMEM87A and WLS, while bovine rhodopsin and the M1 muscarinic receptor carry them. Its cavity is 3.7 to 4.6 times larger than the orthosteric pockets of those two receptors and has the opposite sign of mean lining hydropathy. A ray-casting test found a 3.39 Å lateral portal connecting the PTM1 cavity to the bilayer, lined by F280, I347, Y350 and F387, while the dark-state rhodopsin pocket (0.67 Å) and the KcsA ion pore (0.21 Å) are sealed from lipid by the same measure. An acyl chain needs roughly 2.0 Å of clearance, and 21 of 180 rays from the PTM1 cavity admit a 1.8 Å probe.

The conservation limb fails. Across 45 fungal orders, cavity-lining transmembrane residues (n=60, median identity 0.273) are statistically indistinguishable from other transmembrane residues (n=181, median identity 0.159): one-sided Mann-Whitney U=5658, p=0.314, rank-biserial r=0.042; Fisher exact on counts at identity ≥ 0.80, p=0.355, odds ratio 1.42. Nine near-invariant cavity residues do exist and eight of them are polar or aromatic, but mapped onto TMEM87A through the 0.657 superposition only E241 keeps its identity (as E272). The conserved polar sub-site is fungal-specific, which blocks transfer of a TMEM87A or WLS ligand assignment to PTM1. The most defensible molecular activity class is binding of a lipid or lipid-anchored moiety inside the transmembrane bundle, and that should enter curation as a family-level lead rather than an assertion, because no binding assay exists for PTM1 or for any fungal GOST protein and the paralog YHL017W is indistinguishable from PTM1 on every measurement made here.

Key findings

F1. PTM1 has the GOST fold, with TMEM87A as its closest experimental template

The structural comparison used a TM-align-style algorithm written for this job in numpy: gapless-threading seeds, Kabsch superposition, iterative Needleman-Wunsch on the 1/(1+(d/d0)²) score with d0 = 1.24·(L−15)^(1/3) − 1.8, and TM-score normalised by target length. Against the AlphaFold v6 model of P32857 (523 residues) the scores are 0.657 for TMEM87A 8CTJ chain A (397 aligned Cα, 243 core pairs below 5 Å, core RMSD 3.29 Å, 17.3 percent identity over the core), 0.589 for the yeast paralog YHL017W AF-P38745 (199 core pairs, RMSD 3.49 Å, 49.2 percent identity), 0.409 for WLS 7DRT chain B after transitive seeding through TMEM87A (111 core pairs, RMSD 3.68 Å, 8.1 percent identity), 0.530 for bovine rhodopsin 1F88 chain A, and 0.301 for the M1 muscarinic receptor 6OIJ chain R. As an internal control, TMEM87A against WLS scored 0.411 to 0.515, reproducing the homology these two proteins are published to have.

The rhodopsin number is the important caveat and it is reported here rather than buried. Any seven-helix bundle scores near 0.5 against a short class A GPCR, because TM-score normalised by a 348-residue target rewards the generic fold. TM-score alone therefore does not separate GOST from GPCR. The discriminators are the aligned length (243 core residues against a 401-residue GOST target versus 146 against the 348-residue rhodopsin) and the fact that the PTM1-to-TMEM87A alignment extends into the N-terminal extramembrane region. Transitive seeding also mattered: naive gapless seeding of PTM1 against WLS fell into a local optimum at TM-score 0.233 with only 29 core residues, and seeding from the PTM1→TMEM87A and TMEM87A→WLS alignments (384 seed pairs) recovered 0.409 with 111 core residues.

F2. The cavity is large, hydrophobic, and positioned where the TMEM87A and WLS cavities sit

Cavity detection used a LIGSITE-type algorithm: 1.0 Å grid, 1.4 Å probe added to van der Waals radii, a 13-direction protein-solvent-protein buriedness scan over 14 Å, free voxels retained at buriedness ≥ 10/13, connected-component labelling, and lining defined as any residue with a heavy atom within 5 Å of a pocket voxel. An earlier large-probe exclusion method was discarded during iteration 1 because it merged surface grooves into a 5892 Å³ object spanning the whole 61 × 47 × 60 Å protein envelope, which is not a cavity.

All four GOST proteins have cavities 2.3 to 5.4 times larger than the two GPCR pockets, and all four have positive mean lining hydropathy while both GPCR pockets are negative. The separation holds on two axes at once, size and chemistry.

The iteration-1 run, which used different structure choices (WLS 7DRT chain B, rhodopsin 1F88, M1R 6OIJ) and different residue trimming, gave PTM1 860 Å³ for the full-length model and 2159 Å³ once the N-terminal segment (residues 1-30) was removed. The signal peptide is the reason for the spread: residues 1-26 are cleaved in the mature protein but are modelled packed into the bundle by AlphaFold, and they occlude part of the cavity. Measuring from residue 27 gives 1884 Å³ and from residue 31 gives 2159 Å³. Cavity volumes are protocol-dependent and model-trim-dependent, so only numbers from one protocol should be compared against each other.

Spatial correspondence was tested directly rather than assumed. After superposing PTM1 onto 8CTJ, the two cavity centroids lie 5.5 Å apart, 72 percent of PTM1 cavity voxels fall within 3 Å of a TMEM87A cavity voxel and 89 percent within 5 Å. At residue level, 75 of 85 PTM1 lining residues have a structural equivalent in TMEM87A, and 56 of those 75 map onto TMEM87A lining residues. A permutation test drawing matched residue sets 2000 times gives an expectation of 26 ± 5 percent, so the observed 75 percent has empirical p < 0.0005. The cavity is the same cavity, not a coincidental void.

F3. The cavity opens laterally to the bilayer, and the axial pore test is uninformative

Two independent geometric tests were run on the membrane-embedded region. The first was a HOLE-style axial radius profile along the mean transmembrane helix axis, with candidate points required to be enclosed in all 12 azimuthal sectors within 20 Å. Minimum enclosed radii across the core of the membrane span were 1.45 Å (PTM1), 1.99 Å (TMEM87A), 1.36 Å (rhodopsin) and 1.19 Å (KcsA). This test does not separate a genuine ion channel from two proteins that are definitely not channels, because inter-helical packing defects register as free radius. It is reported as uninformative and must not be cited in either direction.

The second test was informative. From the widest enclosed mid-membrane cavity point, 180 rays were cast outward in the membrane plane, and the portal radius recorded as the largest probe that can travel from the cavity to bulk lipid. PTM1 gives an enclosed interior radius of 3.79 Å and a portal radius of 3.39 Å, with 21 of 180 rays admitting a 1.8 Å probe and 13 admitting 2.5 Å. TMEM87A gives 3.59 Å interior and 2.13 Å portal (2 rays at 1.8 Å, none at 2.5 Å). Rhodopsin gives 2.84 Å interior and a 0.67 Å portal, and KcsA gives a 4.60 Å interior (its water-filled central vestibule) with a 0.21 Å portal. Both negative controls are sealed from lipid. The PTM1 residues within 6 Å of the widest lateral path are F280, I347, Y350 and F387, on TM3, TM5 and TM6, all hydrophobic or aromatic.

The first version of this test was wrong and is reported here for provenance rather than suppressed. Without the enclosure constraint, the seed point search for PTM1 and rhodopsin selected points outside the helical bundle, giving a spurious PTM1 interior radius of 7.08 Å, a 7.26 Å portal, and 73 of 180 open rays for rhodopsin. Those numbers are invalid and were superseded by the corrected run above.

F4. The cavity lining is not more conserved than the rest of the transmembrane domain

Forty-five fungal GOST orthologs were taken from the UniProt PF06814 stream, one representative per fungal order, and aligned to PTM1 by pairwise Needleman-Wunsch with BLOSUM62 and free end gaps, with alignment columns indexed on PTM1 positions. Conservation at a position is the fraction of orthologs carrying the identical residue.

Within the annotated transmembrane domain (residues 198-438), the 60 cavity-lining residues have median identity 0.273 and the 181 other transmembrane residues 0.159. One-sided Mann-Whitney gives U=5658, p=0.314, with a rank-biserial effect size of 0.042, which is negligible. A Fisher exact test on the count of positions at identity ≥ 0.80 (9 of 60 lining, 20 of 181 other) gives one-sided p=0.355 and an odds ratio of 1.42. Both tests are negative, so the "conservation of any internal transmembrane cavity across fungal PTM1 orthologs" limb of the seed hypothesis is not supported as stated.

What the alignment does show is a small conserved sub-site. Nine cavity-lining transmembrane residues reach identity ≥ 0.80: Y199, E241, Y249, S279, L283, W414, R417, D422 and Y429. Eight of the nine are polar or aromatic, with L283 the only aliphatic one. Cβ-Cβ distances place K195, Y199, Y249 and D422 within 4.8 to 7.6 Å of each other, R417 7.9 Å from D422, and S279 and Y429 6.1 Å apart as a separate pair. The overall lining remains hydrophobic (fraction AVLIMFWCY 0.62, mean Kyte-Doolittle +1.14 for the transmembrane-restricted lining set), so these polar residues are a minority sub-site inside a hydrophobic pocket, clustered at the lumenal end.

Identity is preserved at 1 of 9 conserved cavity positions. Every hydroxyl- and charge-bearing position that is near-invariant across 45 fungal orders is replaced by an apolar residue in TMEM87A. PTM1 and TMEM87A share the fold and the existence of a large internal cavity, and the chemistry of the cavity surface has diverged. At 17 percent identity with Cα deviations of 1.0 to 3.6 Å, single-residue equivalences can be off by one position, so this mapping is a statement about local chemistry rather than about exact pairings. The practical consequence for curation is firm either way: a ligand assignment from TMEM87A or WLS cannot be carried across to PTM1.

F6. No class A GPCR activation motif is present anywhere in the GOST family

A regular-expression scan of full-length UniProt sequences for [DE]R[YFWH] (DRY/ERY, TM3), NP..Y (NPxxY, TM7) and CW.P (CWxP, TM6) found all three absent from PTM1 P32857 (523 aa), YHL017W P38745 (532 aa), TMEM87A Q8NBN3 (555 aa), WLS Q5T9L3 (541 aa) and GPR108 Q9NPR9 (543 aa). GPR107 Q5VW38 has a single NP..Y match at position 250, inside the lumenal domain rather than TM7. All three are present in the controls: bovine rhodopsin P02699 (ERY at 134, NPVIY at 302, CWLP at 264), human ADRB2 P07550 (130, 322, 285) and human CHRM1 P11229 (DRY at 122, NPMCY at 414). The cavity lining of PTM1 is also poorer in polar and charged residues than a GPCR orthosteric pocket: fraction DEKRHNQST is 0.34 in PTM1 and 0.30 to 0.37 across TMEM87A and WLS, against 0.54 in rhodopsin.

Mechanistic model and interpretation

The direct molecular event the hypothesis proposes is accommodation of a hydrophobic moiety inside the PTM1 transmembrane bundle. Every measurement made here speaks to that event: the geometry of the site, the chemistry of its surface, and whether it can be reached from the bilayer. The proposed path is entry of an acyl chain through the lateral portal at F280 / I347 / Y350 / F387 into a 1884 Å³ hydrophobic cavity, with a head group or ester linkage contacting the lumenal-end polar cluster at K195, Y199, Y249 and D422, with R417 nearby and S279 and Y429 forming a second pair.

Solid arrows are measurements made in this work. Dashed arrows are inference, and the chain from "lipid or lipidated cargo binding" to the retrograde transport process is the weakest link of all: nothing measured here says anything about process, and GO:0042147 came from the database rather than from this analysis.

Three limbs of the chain are secure. The fold assignment rests on a 243-residue core alignment to an experimental structure, validated by an internal control that reproduces the published TMEM87A-to-WLS homology. The cavity assignment rests on a permutation-tested spatial correspondence with the TMEM87A cavity at p < 0.0005. The exclusion of GPCR and channel alternatives rests on two orthogonal negatives, the absent activation motifs and the sealed-versus-open portal comparison against rhodopsin and KcsA.

One limb is broken, and the way it broke is informative. A cavity whose surface drifts as fast as the rest of the bundle is what a site with loose chemical requirements looks like: any acyl chain will do. That reading is compatible with lipid binding and argues for a general term rather than a specific lipid class. It does not exclude the alternative that the cavity is a packing feature of the fold with no physiological ligand, and no measurement here can separate those two.

The fungal-specific polar sub-site is the most interesting unexplained observation. It is conserved across 45 fungal orders, it clusters in space at the lumenal end of the cavity, and it is absent from the human protein that otherwise matches PTM1 best. If PTM1 binds something, the fungal lineage has conserved residues that a human GOST protein does not need. E241, equivalent to TMEM87A E272, is the one position conserved in both.

Evidence base

Six papers carry the argument, and the way each one is used is stated so that a curator can check the reasoning rather than the conclusion.

Structure of the GOLD-domain seven-transmembrane helix protein family member TMEM87A (PMID: 36373655) is the anchor. It supplies the structure (8CTJ) that PTM1 was aligned to, it defines the GOST architecture of a GOLD domain atop a seven-helix bundle with "a large cavity open to solution and the membrane outer leaflet", and it reaches the same negative conclusion for the human protein that this analysis reaches for PTM1: TMEM87A "may not function as an ion channel or G-protein coupled receptor". It also groups WLS with TMEM87A, which is what licenses the comparison across both.

Cryo-EM structure of human Wntless in complex with Wnt3a (PMID: 34315898) supplies the functional content. It reports that the WLS transmembrane domain has "a conserved core cavity and a lateral opening", and that "the lipid moiety on Wnt3a traversing a hydrophobic tunnel of WLS transmembrane domain and inserting into membrane". The lateral opening in that sentence is the same geometric feature measured in PTM1 at 3.39 Å. This is the strongest literature support for reading a lateral portal plus hydrophobic cavity as a lipid-handling site.

Molecular basis of Wnt biogenesis, secretion, and Wnt7-specific signaling (PMID: 37852257) shows that the GOST core is occupied by lipid even when not holding cargo: "a phospholipid in the core of Wnt-bound WLS regulates the association and dissociation between Wnt and WLS". A cavity of this type is therefore expected to hold a lipid in the resting state, which is directly relevant to interpreting an empty AlphaFold cavity.

Conformational cycling of the Wntless transporter drives trafficking and secretion of Wnt morphogens (PMID: 42321207) qualifies the static-model reasoning. WLS cycles between states, and "Wnt release from cells is driven by dramatic conformational changes in the WLS transmembrane domain, reminiscent of GPCR activation". A single AlphaFold model shows one state. The GPCR comparison in that sentence is about helix motion and must not be read as support for G protein coupling.

GPR107, a G-protein-coupled receptor essential for intoxication by Pseudomonas aeruginosa exotoxin A, localizes to the Golgi and is cleaved by furin (PMID: 25031321) is the human-side experimental link to the process term already on PTM1: "GPR107 localizes to the trans-Golgi network and is essential for retrograde transport". The same paper also illustrates the carry-over problem, since it calls GPR107 an orphan GPCR and speculates that it "might be one of the long-sought receptors that associates with G-proteins to regulate intracellular vesicular transport".

Human GPR107 and murine Gpr108 are members of the LUSTR family (PMID: 17454009) is used as orientation only. It confirms that the family has Saccharomyces members ("LUSTR proteins are also found in Drosophila, Saccharomyces and Arabidopsis") and it documents the architecture UniProt assigns to PTM1 ("an amino-terminal hydrophobic signal peptide sequence, a long extracellular domain and a carboxy-terminal seven transmembrane domain (LUSTR domain) similar to GPCRs"). That last phrase, written in 2007 and before any GOST structure existed, is where the receptor naming on P32857 comes from.

No primary literature specific to yeast PTM1 was retrieved. Searches for PTM1 with Saccharomyces cerevisiae, for YKL039W, and for PTM1 yeast vacuolar membrane protein returned nothing. The search tool is strictly conjunctive and short queries work better than long ones, so this is a weak negative, and a curator should check SGD directly.

Limitations and knowledge gaps

No direct binding data for PTM1 or for any fungal GOST protein. Checked by PubMed searches for PTM1 with S. cerevisiae, for YKL039W, and for PTM1 yeast vacuolar membrane protein, all returning nothing. This matters because every MF lead in this report rests on shape and chemistry alone. Lipidomics or native mass spectrometry on purified PTM1, or a photocrosslinkable lipid capture, would resolve it.

The PTM1 geometry is a model. There is no experimental structure of any fungal GOST protein; RCSB holds 8CTJ for TMEM87A and 7KC4 and 7DRT for WLS. AlphaFold side-chain packing can be looser than cryo-EM, which would inflate both the 1884 Å³ cavity and the 3.39 Å portal relative to the TMEM87A values measured on a real structure. The signal peptide makes this worse: residues 1-26 are cleaved in vivo but modelled inside the bundle, which is why the volume ranges from 860 Å³ (untrimmed) to 2159 Å³ (trimmed at residue 31).

The evidence basis of GO:0042147 was not retrieved. It appears as a GO cross-reference on the UniProt entry, but its evidence code was not checked. A term transferred from GPR107 and a term from a yeast experiment carry different weight for a core-function decision. Reading the SGD annotation record resolves this.

Nothing distinguishes PTM1 from YHL017W. Cavity volume (1884 against 1194 Å³), lining hydrophobicity (0.608 against 0.658) and family membership are equivalent, and the two align to each other at TM-score 0.589 with 49.2 percent identity. Any MF lead from this analysis applies to both genes, and applying it to one would be an error that propagates.

The polar sub-site has no assigned role. Its conservation across 45 fungal orders and its absence from TMEM87A are established; what it binds is not.

Tools that could not be used, stated plainly. Foldseek was not run. The structural comparison used a TM-align reimplementation written for this job, validated by reproducing the published TMEM87A-to-WLS homology at 0.411 to 0.515. The Phenix tools (phenix.superpose_pdbs, phenix.molprobity) were not usable because the Phenix binaries were not on PATH in this environment and the host shell had no network access to fetch comparison structures, so all structural work was done in Python inside the code executor. Biopython was not available, so the PDB parser, the aligners and the cavity detector were written from scratch in numpy. No number in this report comes from a tool that did not run, and the one analysis that produced invalid output (the first lateral portal run) is shown and labelled rather than removed.

A methodological caveat specific to the conservation test. Conservation was scored as exact residue identity. A test on physicochemical class, or a rate-based test such as a per-site dN/dS or a Rate4Site analysis, could give a different answer, and the negative result should be read as "no identity-level enrichment" rather than "no selection".

Proposed follow-up experiments and actions

Lipidomics of purified PTM1. Affinity-purify PTM1 from yeast in mild detergent or in nanodiscs and run lipid mass spectrometry against a matched purification of a non-GOST membrane protein. A specific enriched species separates "binds a lipid" from "has a hydrophobic cavity", which is the single distinction this report cannot make. First priority.

Portal mutagenesis. Introduce bulk at the lateral opening (F280W, Y350W, F387W, singly and combined) and test whether lipid co-purification or any PTM1-dependent phenotype is lost. This tests the portal specifically, independent of the fold-similarity argument.

Polar sub-site mutagenesis. K195A, Y199F, Y249F, D422A and R417A for the fungal-specific cluster, plus E241A for the one cross-kingdom conserved position (TMEM87A E272). If only E241 matters the site is a family feature; if the fungal cluster matters it is a lineage-specific determinant.

Ortholog portal survey. Run the cavity and portal measurement on AlphaFold models of a dozen fungal orthologs. If the portal is present across the family and not only in the S. cerevisiae model, the model-packing caveat weakens considerably. This is cheap, needs no new data, and should be done before any experiment.

Double-mutant phenotyping. ptm1Δ yhl017wΔ against each single deletion, scored for GPI-anchored protein trafficking, Chs3 transport and general retrograde cargo. Redundancy between the two paralogs is the most likely reason this gene is unstudied, and it is the only route to separating them.

Cross-species complementation. Test whether PTM1 or YHL017W rescues retrograde transport defects in GPR107-null human cells, and whether GPR107 rescues any ptm1Δ phenotype. A positive result would license transferring the GPR107 experimental process annotation (PMID: 25031321).
