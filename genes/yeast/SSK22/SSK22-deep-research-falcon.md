# Functional annotation report: yeast **SSK22** (UniProt P25390)

## Executive conclusion

**SSK22/YCR073C encodes Ssk22, an intracellular STE-family serine/threonine MAP kinase kinase kinase (MAP3K/MAPKKK) in the Sln1 branch of the *Saccharomyces cerevisiae* high-osmolarity glycerol (HOG) pathway.** Its primary biochemical role is to transfer phosphate from ATP to the activation loop of the MAP2K Pbs2. Activated Pbs2 then activates Hog1, initiating osmoadaptation. Ssk22 is substantially redundant with its paralog Ssk2, which explains why many experiments and annotations report the pair as “Ssk2/Ssk22” rather than resolving Ssk22 alone. The strongest substrate assignment is Pbs2; no comparably well-established alternative direct substrate emerged from the literature reviewed here. (tatebayashi2003adockingsite pages 2-4, tatebayashi2003adockingsite pages 1-2, saito2012responsetohyperosmotic pages 4-5)

## 2. Primary molecular function and reaction

Ssk22 is a **protein serine/threonine kinase**. Its generic reaction is:

**ATP + Pbs2 → ADP + phospho-Pbs2**

The relevant biological substrate is the MAP2K **Pbs2**. Pair-level biochemical evidence indicates that Ssk2/Ssk22 can phosphorylate both conserved Pbs2 activation-loop residues, **Ser514 and Thr518**, under appropriate osmostress conditions. These modifications activate Pbs2, which subsequently phosphorylates Hog1. Importantly, much of the residue-level literature assays Ssk2 and Ssk22 together; therefore, phosphorylation of both sites should be considered securely established for the redundant MAPKKK tier but not necessarily measured as purified Ssk22-only enzyme kinetics. Recent experiments directly monitored Pbs2 Thr518 phosphorylation by phosphosite-specific methods and Phos-tag electrophoresis. (tatebayashi2003adockingsite pages 2-4, tatebayashi2023twoactivatingphosphorylation pages 8-9)

Substrate specificity is not based only on a generic kinase consensus. Pbs2 contains an **Ssk2/Ssk22-selective docking region in its N-terminal regulatory portion**. Mutating this region reduces Pbs2–Ssk2/Ssk22 interaction and impairs signaling through the Sln1 branch. Conversely, grafting the docking element onto Ste7 can make Ste7 susceptible to Ssk2/Ssk22-dependent activation. These experiments show that docking contributes causally to both catalytic efficiency and pathway insulation. (tatebayashi2003adockingsite pages 2-4, tatebayashi2003adockingsite pages 1-2)

This specificity distinguishes Ssk2/Ssk22 from the Sho1-branch MAPKKK Ste11. Ssk2/Ssk22 normally activate Pbs2 rather than Ste7, whereas Ste11 participates in several yeast MAPK systems. The Ssk2/Ssk22–Pbs2 docking interface is therefore a major mechanism preventing inappropriate signaling to mating or filamentation pathways. (tatebayashi2003adockingsite pages 2-4)

## 3. Upstream regulation

Under nonstress conditions, the membrane-associated sensor histidine kinase Sln1 drives a phosphorelay through Ypd1 to Ssk1. Phosphorylated Ssk1 is inactive toward the MAPKKKs. Increased extracellular osmolarity reduces turgor and Sln1 signaling, allowing **unphosphorylated Ssk1** to accumulate. Unphosphorylated Ssk1 binds the long N-terminal regulatory region of Ssk2/Ssk22 and promotes MAPKKK activation, including autophosphorylation. Ssk22 has the expected MAPKKK architecture: an N-terminal regulatory/autoinhibitory region and a C-terminal catalytic kinase domain. (horie2008phosphorylatedssk1prevents pages 2-2, horie2008phosphorylatedssk1prevents pages 1-2, saito2012responsetohyperosmotic pages 4-5)

Interaction evidence includes yeast two-hybrid and coprecipitation results supporting binding between Ssk1 and the MAPKKK regulatory region. However, the most detailed mechanistic experiments were conducted on Ssk2. Applying the complete molecular model to Ssk22 is supported by paralogy, shared pathway behavior, and genetic redundancy, but should be recognized partly as pair-level inference. (horie2008phosphorylatedssk1prevents pages 2-2, horie2008phosphorylatedssk1prevents pages 11-12)

A reported distinction is that **Ssk22 activation appears more strictly dependent on Ssk1**, whereas Ssk2 can retain an alternative Ssk1-independent mode of osmotic activation. Thus, the two proteins are redundant at the Pbs2/Hog1 output tier but not necessarily identical in upstream regulation. (zhi2013ssk1pindependentactivationof pages 1-2)

## 4. Pathway role and biological process

The principal pathway sequence is:

**hyperosmotic stress → reduced Sln1–Ypd1 phosphorelay → unphosphorylated Ssk1 → Ssk22/Ssk2 → Pbs2 → Hog1 → osmoadaptation**.

Hog1 controls a broad adaptive program that includes transcriptional remodeling, temporary changes in growth and cell-cycle progression, and increased synthesis and retention of glycerol. Glycerol functions as a compatible intracellular osmolyte that helps restore water balance and turgor. Ssk22 is therefore best annotated as an upstream signal-transduction enzyme rather than as a glycerol-metabolic enzyme itself. (horie2008phosphorylatedssk1prevents pages 1-2, saito2012responsetohyperosmotic pages 4-5)

The Sln1–Ssk1–Ssk2/Ssk22 arm runs in parallel with the Sho1–Ste11 arm. Both converge on Pbs2 and Hog1. Consequently, loss of *SSK22* alone can produce little phenotype because Ssk2 and the parallel Sho1 branch compensate. Strong osmosensitivity generally emerges when redundant MAPKKKs or branches are removed. This is a critical interpretive point: a weak single-deletion phenotype does not imply that Ssk22 lacks kinase activity or pathway function. (tatebayashi2003adockingsite pages 1-2, horie2008phosphorylatedssk1prevents pages 2-2, horie2008phosphorylatedssk1prevents pages 1-2)

Ssk22 should not be assigned all broader Hog1-associated stress phenotypes indiscriminately. For example, available evidence indicates that Ssk2 and Ssk22 can differ outside canonical osmostress signaling. The precise, high-confidence role of Ssk22 remains activation of the **Pbs2–Hog1 module in the Sln1 osmosensing branch**.

## 5. Cellular localization

Ssk22 operates in an **intracellular signaling module**, interacting with soluble Ssk1 and Pbs2. The pathway topology is most consistent with a predominantly cytoplasmic site of action upstream of Hog1, after which activated Hog1 can redistribute to the nucleus and regulate nuclear responses. Nevertheless, no definitive Ssk22-specific imaging or fractionation study was identified in the retrieved literature. The 2003 localization-related discussion concerned Pbs2 localization signals, not direct localization of Ssk22. Thus, “intracellular/cytoplasmic signaling kinase” is a reasonable functional annotation, but **exclusive cytosolic localization should not be stated as experimentally established**. (tatebayashi2003adockingsite pages 1-2)

## 6. Experimental evidence and confidence assessment

### High-confidence conclusions

1. **MAPKKK identity and pathway placement:** Multiple primary studies and an authoritative review place Ssk22 with Ssk2 upstream of Pbs2 and Hog1. (tatebayashi2003adockingsite pages 1-2, horie2008phosphorylatedssk1prevents pages 1-2, saito2012responsetohyperosmotic pages 4-5)
2. **Pbs2 as the principal substrate:** Genetic signaling assays, physical interaction mapping, docking-site mutation, and domain-transfer experiments support a direct and selective Ssk2/Ssk22–Pbs2 relationship. (tatebayashi2003adockingsite pages 2-4, tatebayashi2003adockingsite pages 1-2)
3. **Activation by the Sln1–Ypd1–Ssk1 phosphorelay:** Unphosphorylated Ssk1 is the active upstream form and interacts with the MAPKKK regulatory region. (horie2008phosphorylatedssk1prevents pages 2-2)
4. **Functional redundancy:** Ssk22 and Ssk2 compensate strongly for one another in canonical HOG signaling. (horie2008phosphorylatedssk1prevents pages 11-12, saito2012responsetohyperosmotic pages 4-5)

### Moderate-confidence or pair-level conclusions

- Activation-loop phosphorylation at Pbs2 Ser514 and Thr518 is well supported for the Ssk2/Ssk22 MAPKKK tier, but the retrieved evidence does not provide separate catalytic constants for purified Ssk22.
- Ssk22 autophosphorylation is consistent with the accepted activation mechanism but is less extensively characterized than Ssk2 autophosphorylation.
- Cytoplasmic operation is strongly supported by pathway topology and interaction partners, but a definitive Ssk22 localization experiment was not identified.

### Important missing data

No robust Ssk22-specific values for **Km, Vmax, kcat**, ATP affinity, or Pbs2 peptide preference were found. Likewise, no high-resolution experimental Ssk22 structure or well-established non-Pbs2 physiological substrate was identified. These are genuine annotation gaps rather than grounds to extrapolate from unrelated kinases.

## 7. Recent developments, 2023–2024

The most directly relevant recent primary study was published in **April 2023**. Tatebayashi and Saito showed that the two activating Pbs2 phosphosites are differentially dephosphorylated by the PP2C phosphatases Ptc1–Ptc4. This work refines the current model from a simple linear cascade to a dynamically opposed phosphorylation cycle: Ssk2/Ssk22 generate active Pbs2, while multiple phosphatases tune the magnitude and duration of the signal. The experiments used immunoprecipitation, phosphosite detection, and Phos-tag electrophoresis. It did not identify a new Ssk22 substrate or Ssk22-specific localization mechanism. Publication: *Journal of Biological Chemistry* 299, 104569; DOI/URL: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jbc.2023.104569. (tatebayashi2023twoactivatingphosphorylation pages 8-9)

Recent 2024 work largely places this established HOG architecture into broader contexts—MAPK cross-talk, natural stress variation, chemogenomic screening, and fermentation stress—rather than redefining Ssk22’s catalytic function. Accordingly, foundational mechanistic studies remain the strongest evidence for the individual protein. The scarcity of 2023–2024 Ssk22-specific enzymology should be reported transparently rather than replaced by results from Ssk2 proteins in other fungal species.

## 8. Applications and real-world relevance

The HOG pathway is a major model for quantitative eukaryotic signal transduction and for understanding how cells convert osmotic input into transient kinase activity and adaptive output. Ssk22 is useful experimentally as one of the redundant MAPKKKs whose deletion, usually in combination with *SSK2* or manipulation of the Sho1 branch, isolates pathway architecture and signaling specificity.

The pathway also has industrial relevance. During baking, brewing, winemaking, and other fermentations, yeast encounters high sugar, salts, ethanol, heat, nutrient changes, and fluctuating oxygen. HOG-dependent osmoadaptation influences stress tolerance and fermentation performance. However, direct industrial engineering specifically of *SSK22* is less established than manipulation or selection at the level of Hog1 signaling, glycerol metabolism, or global stress tolerance. Any proposal to overexpress or delete *SSK22* should account for redundancy with *SSK2*, pathway cross-talk, and possible fitness costs from constitutive Hog1 signaling.

Comparative fungal HOG systems are also important in antifungal and fungicide research. Yet Ssk22 homologs in pathogenic fungi may differ in copy number and wiring, so results from *Candida*, *Botrytis*, or other fungi should not be transferred directly to S288c Ssk22/P25390.

## 9. Authoritative interpretation

The consensus interpretation of the foundational literature is that Ssk22 is not a general stress kinase with an unrestricted substrate repertoire. It is a **specialized MAPKKK whose central purpose is to couple the Sln1/Ssk1 osmosensor branch to Pbs2**. The Pbs2 docking interaction provides biochemical specificity, redundancy with Ssk2 provides robustness, and phosphatase opposition at Pbs2 shapes signal dynamics. The strongest defensible localization statement is that Ssk22 acts intracellularly, principally in the cytoplasmic upstream HOG module; more precise spatial claims remain under-supported. (tatebayashi2003adockingsite pages 2-4, saito2012responsetohyperosmotic pages 4-5, tatebayashi2023twoactivatingphosphorylation pages 8-9)
