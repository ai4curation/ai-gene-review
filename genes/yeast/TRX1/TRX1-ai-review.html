Gene Description

TRX1 (YLR043C) encodes thioredoxin-1, one of the two cytoplasmic thioredoxins of S. cerevisiae (with the paralogue TRX2, 78% identical in amino-acid sequence; TRX3 is mitochondrial). It is a small CGPC-active-site disulfide oxidoreductase kept reduced by NADPH-thioredoxin reductase Trr1. Reduced Trx1 donates electrons to thioredoxin-dependent enzymes, including PAPS reductase (Met16) in sulfate assimilation, ribonucleotide reductase and thiol peroxidases (Tsa1, Tsa2, Ahp1, Dot5), reduces protein disulfides and glutathione disulfide, and deglutathionylates proteins, maintaining cytosolic and nuclear redox homeostasis. Under standard tested growth conditions, trx1 trx2 double mutants are methionine auxotrophs with a prolonged S phase, and loss of both thioredoxins and both dithiol glutaredoxins is lethal. Independently of its redox activity, Trx1 forms LMA1, a heterodimer with the proteinase B inhibitor Pbi2, which cooperates with Sec18/NSF in SNARE priming for homotypic vacuole fusion (vacuole inheritance) and ER-Golgi vesicle fusion. Trx1 is located in the cytosol and nucleus, with a minor pool in the mitochondrial intermembrane space. Sulfate assimilation can recover without both cytoplasmic thioredoxins under conditions that minimize reactive oxygen species; the alternative electron donor is unresolved.
