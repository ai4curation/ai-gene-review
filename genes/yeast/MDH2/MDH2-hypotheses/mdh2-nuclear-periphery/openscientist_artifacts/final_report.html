Mdh2 nuclear periphery: hypothesis-level curation report

Gene: MDH2 / YOL126C, Saccharomyces cerevisiae S288C (NCBITaxon:559292) UniProt: P22133 | SGD: S000005486 Hypothesis slug: mdh2-nuclear-periphery Seed hypothesis: Yeast Mdh2 (cytosolic malate dehydrogenase) localizes to the nuclear periphery in addition to the cytosol. Date of database queries: 2026-11-18

Summary

Verdict: partially supported, and over-annotated as currently recorded in GO. The observation behind the seed hypothesis is real and is in the database, but it is a single-condition observation that the GO record presents without its condition.

The hypothesis traces to exactly one annotation: GO:0034399 nuclear periphery, evidence code IDA, qualifier located_in, reference PMID: 25817432, assigned by SGD on 2016-01-08. A QuickGO audit of all 27 GO annotations on P22133 found no second source for any nuclear location. That single reference is Gallina et al. 2015, the paper that defines the intranuclear quality control compartment (INQ), which its own abstract describes as a structure "that sequesters misfolded, ubiquitylated and sumoylated proteins in response to genotoxic stress". The Mdh2 call came from a screen of 4800 GFP-tagged strains for perinuclear foci induced by the proteasome inhibitor MG132 and co-localizing with Cmr1-yEmRFP. The same paper states that only 18 of its 27 hits are nuclear in the absence of stress, and Mdh2 sits in the remaining group, since its unstressed localization is cytosolic.

Three independent lines of analysis converge on the same reading. First, a provenance audit shows that all 28 gene products given GO:0034399 on the authority of 25817432 are the INQ hit list, a set that also contains Qcr6 (a mitochondrial inner-membrane cytochrome b-c1 subunit) and Pir1 (a cell wall mannoprotein). Neither can constitutively reside in the nuclear lumen, which marks the whole batch as a deposit proteome. Second, comparative sequence analysis of the three yeast MDH isozymes shows Mdh2 has no PTS1, no PTS2, no mitochondrial presequence and no classical NLS, while Mdh1 and Mdh3 carry their expected signals, so Mdh2 has no autonomous route into the nucleus; its GFP fusion is also above the passive nuclear pore limit. Third, Mdh2's recorded physical interactome in IntAct is weighted towards Hsp70 and Hsp90 chaperones and contains no nuclear envelope or nuclear pore component.

A mechanism explains the observation without a native nuclear function. Mdh2 carries a Pro/N-degron at residues 2-5 and is targeted for proteasomal degradation on shift to glucose, so MG132 is precisely the treatment that stabilizes a pool of Mdh2 headed for destruction. Sequestration of that stabilized pool into a quality-control deposit is expected behaviour for a degradation substrate. A second problem runs opposite to the seed hypothesis: GO holds the stress-conditional location for Mdh2 while the mechanistically explained peroxisomal location is missing entirely, and PMID: 33177075 is not cited anywhere in Mdh2's GO record.

Key findings

F001. The annotation rests on a single IDA from a stress-deposit screen

A provenance audit through the QuickGO REST API (geneProductId=UniProtKB:P22133, 27 annotations retrieved) and the SGD backend (locus S000005486, go_details) establishes that GO:0034399 "nuclear periphery" appears exactly once for Mdh2: evidence IDA, qualifier located_in, assigned by SGD, reference 25817432. No other reference in GO supports any nuclear location for this protein. UniProt P22133 lists Subcellular location: Cytoplasm only.

This is the INQ hit list, reproduced wholesale as nuclear periphery annotations. The internal control for whether that set can be read as a list of nuclear-periphery residents is the presence of Qcr6 and Pir1 in it. Qcr6 is a subunit of the mitochondrial inner-membrane cytochrome b-c1 complex and Pir1 is a cell wall mannoprotein. A set containing both is a sequestration proteome.

Steady-state localization evidence for Mdh2 points elsewhere: GO:0005829 cytosol, IDA, from PMID: 1986231 (Minard and McAlister-Henn 1991), and GO:0005737 cytoplasm, HDA, from PMID: 11914276 (Kumar et al. 2002).

F002. The focus appears only after proteasome inhibition, and the source paper says Mdh2 is not nuclear at baseline

The full text of Gallina et al. 2015 (PMC4389229) was retrieved through Europe PMC and text-mined. The method that generated the Mdh2 call reads: "we screened a collection of 4800 green fluorescent protein (GFP)-tagged proteins for co-localization with Cmr1 ... We took advantage of the observation that Cmr1 perinuclear foci were also induced by proteasome inhibition (MG132 ...), to avoid induction of DNA repair foci ... yielding a list of 27 proteins that form Cmr1-co-localizing foci in response to MG132 (Fig. 3a). Eighty-one per cent (22/27) of these proteins also co-localized with Cmr1 after MMS treatment."

The discussion of that paper then supplies the qualifier a curator needs: "the majority of the INQ-localized proteins are exclusively nuclear (67%, 18/27) in the absence of stress. We propose that the remaining proteins that do not generally localize to the nucleus in the absence of stress might be translocated to the nucleus specifically during stress, or that a minor nuclear pool of these proteins could become detectable on relocalization to INQ due to the increased local concentration." Mdh2 falls in that remaining 33% (9 of 27), because its unstressed localization is cytosolic by two independent references.

The strings "Mdh2", "MDH2" and "malate" occur zero times in the main text, confirmed both by regex over the retrieved full text and by Europe PMC full-text queries (PMCID:"PMC4389229" AND "MDH2", hitCount 0). The Mdh2 call therefore sits in Supplementary Fig. 3 or Supplementary Tables 1-7, which I could not download; the Europe PMC supplementaryFiles endpoint timed out.

The inducing condition and Mdh2's own biology fit together. UniProt P22133 annotates a Pro/N-degron motif at residues 2-5 and the PTM "Targeted for proteasomal degradation when cells are shifted to glucose-containing growth medium" (ECO:0000269, PMID: 28126757). MG132 blocks the proteasome, so it stabilizes exactly the Mdh2 pool that the GID complex would otherwise destroy. Capture of that stabilized pool in a quality-control deposit is what a degradation substrate is expected to do.

F003. Mdh2 carries no organelle targeting signal of any kind

Mdh2 fails every classical targeting test. Its C-terminus is ASS, not a PTS1. Its N-terminal 25 residues carry a net negative charge with two acidic residues and no arginine, all incompatible with a mitochondrial matrix presequence; the positive control Mdh1 has a UniProt-annotated 17-residue transit peptide with net charge +4 and no acidic residues. UniProt lists no Signal and no Transit peptide feature for Mdh2, only the Pro/N-degron motif. The NLS scan returned zero hits, though it returned zero for both paralogs as well, so on its own it is uninformative; the targeting-signal contrast is what carries weight.

A size argument points the same way. Mdh2 is 40.7 kDa as a monomer and roughly 81.5 kDa as its functional homodimer. The GFP fusion used in the screen is about 67.6 kDa as a monomer and 135 kDa as a tagged dimer, well above the approximately 40 kDa passive diffusion limit of the nuclear pore for globular proteins. Any nuclear-periphery signal therefore has to arise from piggybacking, co-aggregation with a chaperone or aggregase, or active recruitment by another factor.

F004. The well-supported secondary location is the peroxisome, reached by piggybacking

Gabay-Maskit et al. 2020 (PMID: 33177075, A piggybacking mechanism enables peroxisomal localization of the glyoxylate cycle enzyme Mdh2 in yeast, doi 10.1242/jcs.244376) report that "the yeast malate dehydrogenase 2 (Mdh2) is dually localized to the cytosol and to peroxisomes and is targeted to peroxisomes via association with Mdh3 and a Pex5-dependent piggybacking mechanism."

That mechanism is exactly what the sequence analysis predicts would be required. Mdh2 ends in ASS and has no PTS1 of its own, so it has to ride on a PTS1-bearing partner, and Mdh3 ends in SKL. The dependence of this isozyme family on PTS1 was established directly by McAlister-Henn et al. 1995 (PMID: 7673155), who removed the Ser-Lys-Leu tripeptide from Mdh3 and reported that "This resulted in cytosolic localization of MDH3 in yeast transformants." The same paper showed that the 12-residue N-terminal extension of Mdh2 is a catabolite-inactivation determinant and failed to confer that property on Mdh3, which removes the only candidate N-terminal targeting element in Mdh2 from consideration.

A supporting phenotype is already in GO for Mdh2: GO:0016558 protein import into peroxisome matrix, IMP, qualifier involved_in, from PMID: 19118449 (Wolinski et al. 2009). The authors of 33177075 present the peroxisomal pool as functionally relevant: "This dual localization of Mdh2 contributes to our understanding of the glyoxylate cycle and provides a new perspective on compartmentalization of cellular metabolism." No primary paper proposes a nuclear or nuclear-periphery function for Mdh2.

F005. The annotation carries no condition qualifier and has already propagated

A GAF-level audit through QuickGO downloadSearch (text/gaf) for UniProtKB:P22133 returned 10 cellular-component lines. The GO:0034399 line reads: qualifier located_in, reference 25817432, evidence IDA, With/From empty, Annotation_Extension empty, date 20160108, assigned by SGD. Nothing in the annotation records that the observation required MG132 or MMS.

A second line, GO:0070013 intracellular organelle lumen, is an IEA from UniProt GO_REF:0000117 with With/From ARBA:ARBA00027917, dated 20260727. A QuickGO ontology query confirms GO:0070013 is an ancestor of GO:0034399 (the ancestor set runs GO:0031981 nuclear lumen, GO:0070013 intracellular organelle lumen, GO:0043233, GO:0031974, GO:0005634 nucleus and higher). That IEA is therefore a propagation of the same single IDA, not independent support. One unqualified, single-condition observation now generates two cellular component terms for Mdh2.

F007. The recorded interactome is chaperone-weighted with no nuclear envelope partner

An IntAct query through PSICQUIC (P22133, tab25 format, retrieved 2026-11) returned 8 interaction lines covering 7 distinct partners: GET3, GTT1, HSP82, MGA2, PYK2, SSA1 and SSB1. Three are canonical chaperones (Hsp82 is Hsp90; Ssa1 and Ssb1 are Hsp70 family), and Get3 has a documented ATP-independent holdase activity under oxidative stress, so 4 of 7 partners are chaperone-class. Zero partners matched a curated list of 34 nuclear envelope, nuclear pore and nucleocytoplasmic transport proteins (NUP49, NUP57, NUP100, NUP116, NUP133, NUP145, NUP159, NUP170, NUP188, NUP192, NSP1, POM152, POM34, NDC1, MLP1, MLP2, HEH1, HEH2, SRC1, ESC1, NIC96, SEH1, SEC13, KAP95, KAP60, SRP1, CSE1, MSN5, NTF2, HMG1, HMG2, MPS3, NDJ1).

With only 7 recorded partners, the negative has low power and corroborates rather than demonstrates. The direction of the positive signal carries information on its own: an enzyme whose recorded partners are Hsp70 and Hsp90 is a chaperone client, which is what a protein sequestered into a quality-control deposit after proteasome inhibition should look like.

Mechanistic model and interpretation

The immediate molecular activity of Mdh2 is L-malate dehydrogenase (GO:0030060, IDA and IMP from 1986231), acting in gluconeogenesis (GO:0006094) and the glyoxylate cycle (GO:0006097). Nothing in any evidence item connects that catalysis to the nuclear periphery. No paper proposes a nuclear substrate, a nuclear redox role, or a moonlighting nuclear function for Mdh2.

What is being tested is a location, and the location has two readings. The first treats the perinuclear focus as a property Mdh2 brings with it. The second treats it as a destination imposed on Mdh2 by the cell's sequestration machinery. The evidence favours the second, and the chain is specific:

The dashed link from chaperone binding to INQ sequestration is the uncertain step: Gallina et al. establish Btn2 and Hsp42 dependence for Cmr1 foci and for misfolded VHL, but the main text gives no per-hit breakdown, so Mdh2's dependence on an aggregase is inferred rather than shown.

The alternative route into the nucleus is closed off by the sequence data. Mdh2 has no NLS and its GFP fusion exceeds the passive pore limit, so it cannot simply diffuse in and accumulate. The paper's own second explanation, that a minor pre-existing nuclear pool becomes detectable when local concentration rises, remains open but still describes a pool made visible by a deposit, which is equally non-core.

By contrast, the peroxisomal route has a named mechanism that is consistent with the same sequence facts: Mdh2 lacks a PTS1, Mdh3 has one (SKL), and Pex5-dependent piggybacking carries Mdh2 in as a passenger. That is the one non-cytosolic pool with a plausible catalytic role, in the glyoxylate cycle.

Mechanistic scope

Direct gene-product activity. Mdh2 catalyses the interconversion of malate and oxaloacetate with NAD+ (GO:0030060). It is expressed under non-fermentative growth through Cat8- and Sip4-dependent CSRE elements (PMID: 11169757), and functions in gluconeogenesis and the glyoxylate cycle. Nothing about this activity is nuclear.

Direct gene-product property relevant to the hypothesis. Mdh2 is an aggregation-prone, constitutively degraded enzyme. It carries a Pro/N-degron at residues 2-5, is a GID complex substrate, and its recorded physical partners are weighted towards Hsp70 and Hsp90. These are intrinsic properties of the protein.

Downstream, condition-dependent phenotype. The appearance of an Mdh2-GFP focus at the nuclear periphery after MG132 is a consequence of that client status combined with the cell's sequestration machinery (Btn2, Hsp42, Cmr1). It is a destination imposed on Mdh2 by other proteins, not an activity of Mdh2 and not a place where Mdh2 does chemistry. Gallina et al. offer the alternative reading themselves, that a minor pre-existing nuclear pool becomes detectable only when local concentration rises on sequestration.

Effect inferred only from perturbation. The entire nuclear-periphery observation exists only under proteasome inhibition or MMS. No unperturbed observation of nuclear Mdh2 is on record. Either reading of the data makes the term non-core.

Conflicts and alternatives

The co-annotated batch is incoherent as a localization set. All 28 gene products given GO:0034399 on the authority of 25817432 are the INQ hit list. Qcr6 is a mitochondrial inner-membrane cytochrome b-c1 subunit and Pir1 is a cell wall mannoprotein. Neither can constitutively reside in the nuclear lumen. A set containing both is a deposit proteome, and Mdh2's membership should be read the same way as theirs.

Not paralog confusion. This was the most likely database artifact and it is ruled out. Experimental CC annotations across the isozyme family are cleanly specific: Mdh1 has six experimental mitochondrial references and Mdh3 has three peroxisomal ones, and neither paralog carries GO:0034399. The term is genuinely Mdh2-specific.

The stronger competing location is the peroxisome. 33177075 reports Mdh2 dually localized to cytosol and peroxisome through Mdh3 association and Pex5-dependent piggybacking. This fits the sequence data exactly: Mdh2 ends in ASS with no PTS1, so it must ride on a PTS1-bearing partner, and Mdh3 ends in SKL. Counting independent experimental support gives cytosol 2 references with no stress requirement, peroxisome 2 references with no stress requirement and a stated mechanism, nuclear periphery 1 reference requiring MG132 or MMS.

Tagging artifact cannot be excluded. The screen used C-terminally GFP-tagged Mdh2 expressed from its native locus. A 27 kDa tag on a 40.7 kDa homodimerizing enzyme is a plausible source of folding stress, which is the exact property an INQ screen selects for. The same caution applies to every hit in that screen.

Database carry-over is already under way. UniProt's ARBA rule has turned the single SGD IDA into an additional GO:0070013 IEA. Left unqualified, one condition-restricted observation keeps generating terms, and a downstream consumer reading only the GO record sees two cellular component terms with no indication of their shared single-condition origin.

Isozyme non-equivalence is real but does not help the hypothesis. Small and McAlister-Henn 1997 (PMID: 9244381) found that overexpressed MDH2 failed to restore growth of a deltaMDH1 strain on ethanol or acetate, "suggesting that MDH2 may also have unique metabolic functions". Those unique functions are metabolic and compartment-related, with no nuclear component proposed.

Limitations and knowledge gaps

The Mdh2 panel in Gallina et al. is unseen. Checked: the main text of PMC4389229 by regex over the retrieved full text and by Europe PMC full-text query (PMCID:"PMC4389229" AND "MDH2", hitCount 0); the strings Mdh2, MDH2 and malate occur zero times. The call therefore sits in Supplementary Fig. 3a or Supplementary Tables 1-7. The Europe PMC supplementaryFiles endpoint timed out and I did not retrieve it. Why it matters: the penetrance (what fraction of cells showed the focus), the brightness relative to the cytosolic signal, and whether a diffuse nucleoplasmic pool was visible all change how a curator should word the annotation. Resolution: open the supplementary PDF and Excel file directly.

Unstressed high-resolution imaging of Mdh2-GFP. Checked: GO carries only HDA cytoplasm from 11914276 for Mdh2, and SGD records no HDA from the Huh et al. 2003 GFP survey (PMID: 14562095) for this gene. I could not query the GFP collection image databases (yeastgfp, CYCLoPs, LoQAtE) programmatically. Why it matters: the seed context asks specifically about large-scale localization data, and the single most relevant large-scale resource is the one I could not reach. Resolution: pull the Mdh2-GFP images from those resources.

Whether the focus requires Btn2 or Hsp42. Checked: Gallina et al. report this genetic requirement for Cmr1 foci and for misfolded VHL, but no per-hit breakdown appears in the main text. Why it matters: dependence on an aggregase would settle the quality-control reading and justify the condition qualifier without further argument. Resolution: image Mdh2-GFP plus MG132 in btn2Δ and hsp42Δ.

Whether the untagged protein does this. Checked: the only evidence comes from a C-terminal GFP fusion. Why it matters: a tag-induced folding defect would make the focus an artifact of the screen rather than a property of Mdh2. Resolution: immunofluorescence or nuclear fractionation of untagged Mdh2 after MG132.

Quantitative penetrance under normal growth. Checked: no source reports an unstressed nuclear fraction for Mdh2. Why it matters: a GO located_in term without a qualifier implies a normal-condition location, so the defensibility of the bare term depends on this number. Resolution: quantitative imaging of untreated cells at high sensitivity with Nup49 as the envelope reference used in the original paper.

Statistical limits of the interaction result. IntAct records only 7 partners for Mdh2, so the absence of nuclear envelope and nucleoporin partners has low power. It corroborates the other two lines and does not stand alone.

Limits of motif scanning. The sequence analysis establishes the absence of classical targeting signals. It cannot exclude a non-canonical import route, and the NLS scan returned zero hits for all three isozymes, so that particular scan is uninformative in isolation.

Proposed follow-up experiments and actions

Discriminating tests, in order of cost-effectiveness

  1. Aggregase dependence. Image Mdh2-GFP after MG132 in wild type, btn2Δ, hsp42Δ and cmr1Δ. Loss of the focus in btn2Δ or hsp42Δ identifies it as quality-control sequestration and removes any reading as a native location. This is the cheapest decisive test.
  2. Degron dependence. Compare Mdh2-GFP with an N-terminal Pro/N-degron mutant (residues 2-5) and in gid2Δ. If stabilizing Mdh2 by removing its degron abolishes the MG132-induced focus, the focus is a consequence of blocked turnover rather than of a targeting event.
  3. Unstressed quantitation. Score the nuclear-to-cytosolic ratio of Mdh2-GFP in untreated cells at high sensitivity, with Nup49 as the envelope reference. This directly tests whether the bare located_in term is defensible without a condition.
  4. Untagged protein. Fractionate nuclei after MG132 and immunoblot for untagged Mdh2. Removes the GFP tag as an explanation.
  5. Catalytic and folding state. Test whether a catalytically dead or destabilized Mdh2 point mutant enters the focus more readily under mild stress. Preferential capture of the destabilized form marks the deposit as a misfolding sink.
  6. Side-by-side comparison with the peroxisomal claim. Run the 33177075 reporters and the MG132 condition in the same cells under gluconeogenic growth, the condition where Mdh2 is actually expressed and active, and measure which pool dominates.
