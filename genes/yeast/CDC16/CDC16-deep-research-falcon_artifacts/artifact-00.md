| Identity and ambiguity | Target is *Saccharomyces cerevisiae* S288c **Cdc16/YKL022C/P09798**, the APC/C subunit APC6. It must not be confused with the unrelated *Schizosaccharomyces pombe* **cdc16** septation-initiation-network protein or with human CDC16 literature unless explicitly used for comparison. | Strong: organism-specific genetics, purification, and yeast APC/C structural work agree with the supplied UniProt identity. | Lamb et al., September 1994; Schwickart et al., April 2004; Vazquez-Fernandez et al., October 2024 (pqac-00000004, pqac-00000006, pqac-00000009) |
| Primary molecular role | Cdc16 is a **nonenzymatic structural/scaffolding subunit** of the anaphase-promoting complex/cyclosome (APC/C), an E3 ubiquitin ligase. Ubiquitin-transfer catalysis resides in the APC2–APC11 catalytic module, not in Cdc16. | Strong: APC/C composition, biochemical purification, and structural placement; absence of a catalytic domain is consistent with the TPR architecture. | Schwickart et al., April 2004; Vazquez-Fernandez et al., October 2024 (pqac-00000003, pqac-00000004) |
| Structure and domains | Cdc16/APC6 is a canonical **tetratricopeptide-repeat (TPR) protein** in the APC/C TPR lobe. Its repeated ∼34-residue TPR motifs form an elongated α-helical interaction scaffold, aligning with the supplied TPR-like, TPR_2, TPR-repeat, ANAPC3-family, and TPR_12 annotations. | Strong for family-level/complex architecture; direct high-resolution Cdc16/Cut9 crystallography includes fission-yeast material, so residue-level transfer to budding yeast is partly comparative. | Zhang et al., November 2010; Vazquez-Fernandez et al., October 2024 (pqac-00000004, pqac-00000005) |
| Interactions and assembly | Cdc16 associates with the related TPR subunits Cdc23 and Cdc27. Swm1/Apc13 stabilizes high-affinity incorporation of Cdc16 and Cdc27 into budding-yeast APC/C; CDC16-TAP purification and mass spectrometry directly recover APC/C components. | Strong, direct *S. cerevisiae* biochemical evidence. | Lamb et al., September 1994; Schwickart et al., April 2004 (pqac-00000000, pqac-00000006, pqac-00000009) |
| Pathway and substrates | Through APC/C, Cdc16 supports ubiquitin-dependent destruction of securin/Pds1, mitotic cyclins, and other cell-cycle regulators, enabling sister-chromatid separation, mitotic exit, and maintenance of low cyclin-CDK activity in G1. Substrate specificity is supplied chiefly by Cdc20 or Cdh1/Hct1—not by a demonstrated Cdc16 catalytic or substrate-binding site. | Strong at APC/C-complex level; indirect for Cdc16-specific substrate contact. | Schwickart et al., April 2004; Vazquez-Fernandez et al., October 2024 (pqac-00000003, pqac-00000004) |
| Regulation | Cdc16 is among the APC/C TPR subunits phosphorylated in mitosis by Cdc28/Cdk1. Simultaneously replacing 12 candidate Cdk1 sites across Cdc16, Cdc23, and Cdc27 compromises mitotic APC/C–Cdc20 activity while preserving G1 APC/C–Cdh1/Hct1 activity; this is a collective-subunit experiment, not proof that every effect is Cdc16-specific. | Strong direct yeast evidence for collective TPR-subunit phosphorylation; moderate for the isolated contribution of Cdc16. | Rudner and Murray, June 2000; Lianga et al., June 2013 (pqac-00000001, pqac-00000006) |
| Localization caveat | APC/C is predominantly nuclear in budding yeast, with punctate nuclear and late-anaphase spindle-associated signals reported for **Cdc23-GFP**. These observations support the likely compartment of Cdc16-containing APC/C but are **not direct imaging evidence for Cdc16 itself**; the puncta did not correspond to spindle pole bodies. | Moderate for APC/C localization; insufficient for a definitive Cdc16-specific localization assignment. | Melloy and Holloway, July 2004 (pqac-00000008) |
| Phenotype and essentiality | **CDC16 is essential.** Classical temperature-sensitive or loss-of-function APC/C studies place Cdc16 in a mitosis-essential Cdc16–Cdc23–Cdc27 complex; impaired function blocks sister-chromatid separation/B-type cyclin destruction and causes mitotic arrest. | Strong direct yeast genetic and biochemical evidence, although some phenotypes reflect failure of the entire APC/C. | Lamb et al., September 1994; Irniger et al., April 1995; Schwickart et al., April 2004 (pqac-00000003, pqac-00000006, pqac-00000009) |
| 2024 structural update | Cryo-EM of unphosphorylated apo-APC/C, phosphorylated apo-APC/C, and APC/C–Cdh1–substrate confirms conserved yeast TPR-lobe architecture but reveals species-specific regulation: yeast’s APC2–APC11 module is already positioned for E2 binding in apo-APC/C, and yeast lacks evidence for the human-like APC1 autoinhibitory mechanism controlling the APC8 C-box site. | Strong recent structural evidence for purified *S. cerevisiae* APC/C; conclusions concern the complex rather than a unique enzymatic action of Cdc16. | Vazquez-Fernandez et al., version of record October 14, 2024 (pqac-00000004, pqac-00000007) |
