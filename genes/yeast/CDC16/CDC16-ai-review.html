Gene Description

Cdc16 (Apc6) is an essential tetratricopeptide-repeat (TPR) subunit of the Saccharomyces cerevisiae anaphase-promoting complex/cyclosome (APC/C), the ~1.5 MDa, 13-subunit cullin-RING E3 ubiquitin ligase that triggers the metaphase-to-anaphase transition and exit from mitosis. The 840-residue protein is built from 14 tandem TPR motifs that form an alpha-helical superhelix; together with Cdc23 (Apc8) and Cdc27 (Apc3) it forms the TPR lobe ('arc lamp') of the complex, assembled in the order Cdc23 (innermost, contacting Apc1, Apc4 and Apc5), Cdc16 and Cdc27 (outermost), with the small subunits Cdc26, Swm1/Apc13 and Apc9 stabilising the Cdc16-Cdc27 pair; the TPR subcomplex stays assembled even without Apc1. Cdc16 has no catalytic activity: ubiquitin transfer is performed by the Apc2 cullin-Apc11 RING module with the E2 enzymes Ubc4 and Ubc1, and substrate D-box and KEN-box degrons are read by the coactivators Cdc20 and Cdh1/Hct1 (or Ama1 in meiosis) together with Doc1/Apc10. Cdc16 instead scaffolds the ligase: it holds the TPR lobe together, is contacted directly by Doc1, and is required for coactivator engagement, so that APC/C lacking Cdc16 retains neither Cdh1 binding nor ubiquitin-ligase activity. Cdc16, Cdc23 and Cdc27 are phosphorylated by Cdc28/Cdk1 in mitosis, and this phosphorylation of the TPR module is needed for the Cdc20-dependent activity of the APC/C. Conditional cdc16 mutants arrest as large-budded cells with a short spindle, high Clb2 and stabilised securin Pds1, fail to execute the first meiotic division, and have been reported to re-replicate chromosomal DNA during the arrest. Cdc16 is nuclear in vegetatively growing cells, consistent with the closed mitosis of budding yeast, and relocalises to the cytosol under hypoxia.

Executive summary

The requested target is correctly identified as CDC16/YKL022C of Saccharomyces cerevisiae S288c, encoding Cdc16 (APC6), an essential tetratricopeptide-repeat subunit of the anaphase-promoting complex/cyclosome (APC/C). This assignment agrees with organism-specific genetic, biochemical, and structural literature and with the supplied UniProt family/domain annotations. It is important not to confuse this protein with the unrelated Schizosaccharomyces pombe gene named cdc16, which functions in the septation-initiation network; human CDC16/APC6 is homologous but is not the organism studied here. Cdc16 is not an enzyme by itself and has no known substrate-specific catalytic reaction. Its primary role is architectural: it is an α-helical TPR scaffold within the APC/C TPR lobe, helping assemble and organize the ubiquitin-ligase complex. APC/C catalysis is carried out by the APC2–APC11 catalytic module together with E2 ubiquitin-conjugating enzymes, whereas substrate timing and specificity are supplied principally by the coactivators Cdc20, Cdh1/Hct1, and, during meiosis, Ama1. Through this complex, Cdc16 is required for destruction of securin/Pds1, mitotic cyclins, and additional cell-cycle regulators, thereby enabling anaphase, mitotic exit, and stable G1. (schwickart2004swm1apc13isan pages 1-2, vazquezfernandez2024acomparativestudy pages 1-2)

1. Identity verification and nomenclature

The evidence supports the following identity:

  • Gene: CDC16
  • Systematic locus: YKL022C
  • UniProt accession: P09798
  • Organism: Saccharomyces cerevisiae strain S288c
  • Protein: anaphase-promoting complex subunit Cdc16/APC6
  • Functional class: essential, noncatalytic TPR scaffold of APC/C

Early budding-yeast work identified Cdc16, Cdc23, and Cdc27 as a physical complex required for mitosis. Later TAP purification and mass spectrometry recovered Cdc16 in native APC/C preparations, including experiments employing chromosomally tagged CDC16-TAP. The 2024 yeast APC/C cryo-EM study identifies CDC16/APC6 as a canonical TPR-lobe component. These mutually reinforcing data establish that the supplied UniProt record and the literature target are the same protein. (schwickart2004swm1apc13isan pages 2-3, vazquezfernandez2024acomparativestudy pages 1-2, vazquezfernandez2024acomparativestudy pages 25-26, schwickart2004swm1apc13isan pages 1-2)

A particularly serious nomenclature trap is S. pombe cdc16, a different protein involved in cytokinesis signaling. Structural papers may also refer to Cdc16/Cut9: Cut9 is the fission-yeast APC6 ortholog and is evolutionarily related to budding-yeast Cdc16, but results obtained from Cut9 are comparative evidence rather than direct characterization of P09798. (zhang2010theapccsubunit pages 11-12)

2. Molecular structure and primary function

2.1 TPR scaffold rather than catalytic enzyme

Cdc16 belongs to the APC6/CDC16 family and contains tandem tetratricopeptide repeats, compact helix-turn-helix motifs of approximately 34 residues. Tandem TPRs generate an elongated α-helical superstructure that is well suited to protein–protein interactions. This agrees with the supplied InterPro/Pfam annotations—TPR-like helical superfamily, TPR_2, TPR repeats, TPR_12, and ANAPC3-family-related annotation—and provides the structural basis for assigning Cdc16 an assembly/scaffolding role. (vazquezfernandez2024acomparativestudy pages 1-2, zhang2010theapccsubunit pages 11-12)

In current APC/C models, Cdc16/APC6 lies in the TPR lobe, alongside related TPR subunits including Cdc23/APC8 and Cdc27/APC3. These α-solenoids form much of the complex’s outer structural frame and organize interaction surfaces around the central cavity. They do not contain the APC/C’s RING/cullin catalytic center. Ubiquitin transfer instead depends on APC2 and the RING protein APC11, which recruit and activate E2–ubiquitin conjugates. Consequently, asking for a reaction or small-molecule substrate specific to Cdc16 is not applicable: its functional “substrates” are interacting APC/C proteins, and its contribution is structural and regulatory rather than catalytic. (schwickart2004swm1apc13isan pages 1-2, vazquezfernandez2024acomparativestudy pages 1-2)

2.2 Assembly interactions

Direct yeast biochemistry places Cdc16 in a mitosis-essential complex with Cdc23 and Cdc27. Swm1/Apc13 stabilizes high-affinity incorporation of Cdc16 and Cdc27 into APC/C, demonstrating that Cdc16 is part of an interdependent structural assembly rather than an autonomous enzyme. CDC16-TAP purification followed by SDS-PAGE and mass spectrometry supplied direct native-complex evidence. (schwickart2004swm1apc13isan pages 2-3, schwickart2004swm1apc13isan pages 1-2)

Structural work supports a dimeric organization among canonical TPR subunits and describes Cdc16/Cut9 as a contiguous TPR superhelix with an interface related to that of Cdc27. However, the isolated high-resolution Cdc16/Cut9 crystallographic evidence includes non-S. cerevisiae material; its detailed residue-level conclusions should therefore be treated as evolutionarily informed structural inference for P09798, not direct budding-yeast proof. (zhang2010theapccsubunit pages 11-12)

3. Biochemical pathway and substrate specificity

APC/C is a large E3 ubiquitin ligase—historically described in budding yeast as an approximately megadalton, 11-subunit complex, with later inventories expanding as smaller subunits were identified. Cdc16 contributes to this complex’s structural integrity. It does not independently recognize degradation motifs or transfer ubiquitin. (schwickart2004swm1apc13isan pages 1-2, vazquezfernandez2024acomparativestudy pages 1-2)

Substrate specificity is generated cooperatively:

  1. Cdc20 activates APC/C around the metaphase-to-anaphase transition.
  2. Cdh1/Hct1 sustains APC/C activity during late mitosis and G1.
  3. Coactivators and APC10/Doc1 recognize degrons such as destruction boxes and KEN boxes.
  4. In budding yeast, Ubc4 can prime substrates and Ubc1 can extend ubiquitin chains.
  5. The polyubiquitylated substrate is subsequently degraded, generally by the 26S proteasome. (schwickart2004swm1apc13isan pages 1-2, vazquezfernandez2024acomparativestudy pages 1-2)

Major physiological outputs include destruction of Pds1/securin, which permits sister-chromatid separation, and degradation of mitotic cyclins, which reduces Cdc28/Cdk1 activity and permits mitotic exit. APC/C also targets additional regulators, but those broader substrate effects should be attributed to the holoenzyme and its coactivators—not uniquely to Cdc16. (schwickart2004swm1apc13isan pages 1-2)

4. Cell-cycle regulation

Cdc16 participates in a regulatory feedback circuit in which mitotic Cdc28/Cdk1 helps activate APC/C–Cdc20. Cdc16, Cdc23, and Cdc27 become phosphorylated during mitosis, and purified Cdc28 can phosphorylate these subunits in vitro. Simultaneous substitution of candidate Cdk1 sites across these three TPR subunits makes APC/C resistant to normal phosphorylation and compromises its mitotic Cdc20-dependent activity while leaving G1 Hct1/Cdh1-dependent activity comparatively intact. This is strong evidence that phosphorylation of the TPR module promotes mitotic APC/C activation, but because the mutations were distributed among three proteins, it does not isolate the quantitative contribution of Cdc16 alone. (williams2015cdk1dependentphosphorylationof pages 104-109, vazquezfernandez2024acomparativestudy pages 25-26, jaspersen2000themitoticexit pages 29-32)

A later morphogenesis-checkpoint study connected this mechanism to Swe1/Wee1 and PP2A-Cdc55: Swe1-mediated inhibition of Cdk1 reduces APC/C phosphorylation and delays anaphase, while collective mutation of 12 Cdk1 sites on Cdc16, Cdc23, and Cdc27 alters checkpoint behavior. Thus Cdc16 is best regarded as part of a phosphoregulated TPR platform through which cell morphology and mitotic state are coupled to anaphase timing. (williams2015cdk1dependentphosphorylationof pages 104-109)

The 2024 cryo-EM comparison refines this model. Yeast and human APC/C share an overall conserved architecture, but budding-yeast apo-APC/C already positions its APC2–APC11 catalytic module for E2 binding. The study found no evidence for the same APC1 autoinhibitory segment used in the proposed human APC/C activation mechanism. Therefore, conservation of Cdc16’s structural role does not imply that every regulatory mechanism described in human cells applies unchanged to yeast. (vazquezfernandez2024acomparativestudy pages 1-2, vazquezfernandez2024acomparativestudy pages 16-17)

5. Biological processes and mutant evidence

CDC16 is essential for normal mitosis. Classical genetics and physical-association experiments established Cdc16–Cdc23–Cdc27 as a complex required for cell division. Failure of this module impairs APC/C-mediated destruction of B-type cyclins and blocks proper sister-chromatid separation and mitotic progression. The phenotype is fundamentally attributable to loss of holo-APC/C function rather than to loss of an independent Cdc16 enzymatic reaction. (schwickart2004swm1apc13isan pages 1-2, vazquezfernandez2024acomparativestudy pages 25-26)

The mechanistic sequence most strongly supported by the literature is:

Cdc28-dependent APC/C phosphorylation → APC/C–Cdc20 activation → Pds1 and cyclin ubiquitylation → anaphase onset and declining mitotic CDK activity → Cdh1/Hct1-dependent late-mitotic/G1 substrate destruction.

Cdc16 contributes by maintaining and regulating the TPR scaffold needed for this sequence. APC/C activity in G1 also prevents inappropriate accumulation of mitotic cyclins, helping restrict DNA replication and preserve orderly cell-cycle alternation. (williams2015cdk1dependentphosphorylationof pages 104-109, schwickart2004swm1apc13isan pages 1-2)

6. Cellular localization

The safest conclusion is that Cdc16 functions as part of the predominantly nuclear APC/C, where it acts on nuclear cell-cycle regulators during closed mitosis. However, the best retrieved budding-yeast imaging study visualized Cdc23-GFP, not Cdc16 directly. Cdc23-GFP showed diffuse nuclear signal plus punctate nuclear foci throughout the cell cycle and late-anaphase association along the spindle. The foci colocalized with kinetochore-associated proteins but not with the spindle-pole-body marker Spc42. These observations identify the compartment and dynamic behavior of a Cdc16-containing APC/C assembly, but they should not be misreported as direct Cdc16 microscopy. (melloy2004changesinthe pages 1-2)

Accordingly, the evidence-weighted annotation is nuclear APC/C component, with APC/C-level kinetochore- and spindle-associated behavior during mitosis; a Cdc16-specific localization pattern remains less directly established in the retrieved literature.

7. Recent developments, applications, and expert interpretation

Recent research

The principal 2023–2024 development directly relevant to this target is the October 14, 2024 eLife cryo-EM analysis of three S. cerevisiae APC/C states: unphosphorylated apo-APC/C, phosphorylated apo-APC/C, and an APC/C–Cdh1–substrate complex. It confirms the conserved TPR-lobe placement of Cdc16/APC6 while showing that yeast and human APC/C use different molecular details for E2 engagement and phosphorylation-dependent activation. This is currently the strongest recent structural framework for interpreting P09798. DOI: https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.100821. (vazquezfernandez2024acomparativestudy pages 1-2, vazquezfernandez2024acomparativestudy pages 16-17)

Research applications

Cdc16 is used experimentally in several ways:

  • Conditional APC/C inactivation: temperature-sensitive cdc16 alleles permit synchronization or arrest near the metaphase/anaphase transition and help test whether candidate proteins require APC/C for degradation.
  • Native-complex purification: CDC16-TAP provides an affinity handle for isolating APC/C and testing subunit composition or assembly defects.
  • Phosphoregulation studies: nonphosphorylatable Cdc16/Cdc23/Cdc27 combinations reveal how Cdk1, Swe1, and PP2A control APC/C–Cdc20.
  • Substrate-validation assays: APC/C-defective backgrounds can stabilize candidate substrates, although direct ubiquitylation assays are needed to separate direct substrates from secondary effects.
  • Comparative structural biology: budding yeast offers a genetically tractable system for testing whether mechanisms inferred from mammalian APC/C are truly conserved. (schwickart2004swm1apc13isan pages 2-3, williams2015cdk1dependentphosphorylationof pages 104-109, vazquezfernandez2024acomparativestudy pages 1-2, schwickart2004swm1apc13isan pages 1-2)

These are research implementations rather than established industrial or clinical uses of yeast Cdc16. Therapeutic interest in human APC/C does not constitute a direct application of P09798.

Expert interpretation

The convergent expert view is that Cdc16 should be annotated as an essential structural and regulatory APC/C subunit, not as a ubiquitin ligase operating independently. Its TPR architecture explains its protein-interaction role; genetics establishes its requirement for mitosis; purification places it in the APC/C; phosphorylation experiments connect it to APC/C–Cdc20 activation; and modern cryo-EM places it within a conserved but species-specifically regulated TPR lobe. (schwickart2004swm1apc13isan pages 2-3, williams2015cdk1dependentphosphorylationof pages 104-109, vazquezfernandez2024acomparativestudy pages 1-2)

8. Evidence limitations

Several qualifications are important:

  1. Many substrate and catalytic claims are demonstrated for APC/C as a complex, not for isolated Cdc16.
  2. The strongest localization experiment retrieved used Cdc23-GFP, so Cdc16-specific localization should remain qualified.
  3. Collective phosphorylation mutants alter Cdc16, Cdc23, and Cdc27 simultaneously; they establish TPR-module regulation but not the isolated effect size of each subunit.
  4. Fine structural conclusions from Cdc16/Cut9 homologs are useful evolutionary evidence but cannot automatically be assigned residue-for-residue to P09798.
  5. Recent literature is dominated by holo-APC/C structural studies; relatively little 2023–2024 work focuses on budding-yeast Cdc16 alone.

  anaphase-promoting complex/cyclosome (APC/C), the ~1.5 MDa, 13-subunit cullin-RING E3 ubiquitin ligase
  that triggers the metaphase-to-anaphase transition and exit from mitosis. The 840-residue protein is
  built from 14 tandem TPR motifs that form an alpha-helical superhelix; together with Cdc23 (Apc8) and
  Cdc27 (Apc3) it forms the TPR lobe ('arc lamp') of the complex, assembled in the order Cdc23 (innermost,
  contacting Apc1, Apc4 and Apc5), Cdc16 and Cdc27 (outermost), with the small subunits Cdc26, Swm1/Apc13
  and Apc9 stabilising the Cdc16-Cdc27 pair; the TPR subcomplex stays assembled even without Apc1. Cdc16
  has no catalytic activity: ubiquitin transfer is performed by the Apc2 cullin-Apc11 RING module with
  the E2 enzymes Ubc4 and Ubc1, and substrate D-box and KEN-box degrons are read by the coactivators Cdc20
  and Cdh1/Hct1 (or Ama1 in meiosis) together with Doc1/Apc10. Cdc16 instead scaffolds the ligase: it
  holds the TPR lobe together, is contacted directly by Doc1, and is required for coactivator engagement,
  so that APC/C lacking Cdc16 retains neither Cdh1 binding nor ubiquitin-ligase activity. Cdc16, Cdc23
  and Cdc27 are phosphorylated by Cdc28/Cdk1 in mitosis, and this phosphorylation of the TPR module is
  needed for the Cdc20-dependent activity of the APC/C. Conditional cdc16 mutants arrest as large-budded
  cells with a short spindle, high Clb2 and stabilised securin Pds1, fail to execute the first meiotic
  division, and have been reported to re-replicate chromosomal DNA during the arrest. Cdc16 is nuclear
  in vegetatively growing cells, consistent with the closed mitosis of budding yeast, and relocalises
  to the cytosol under hypoxia.
