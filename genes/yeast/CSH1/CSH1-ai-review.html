Gene Description

CSH1 (YBR161W) encodes a GT32-family multi-pass membrane glycosyltransferase that mannosylates inositol phosphorylceramide (IPC) using GDP-mannose to form mannosyl-inositol phosphorylceramide (MIPC), the precursor of M(IP)2C. It is a paralog of Sur1/Csg1 and acts redundantly with it: csg1 cells only reduce mannosylated sphingolipid synthesis, while csg1 csh1 double mutants make none. Csh1 forms a separate complex with the regulatory subunit Csg2, which it absolutely requires for activity, and it differs from Csg1 in ceramide-substrate specificity (revealed in ipt1, sur2 and scs7 backgrounds). Its biosynthetic activity is assigned to the Golgi, although high-throughput GFP screens detect the tagged protein at the vacuolar membrane.

Core Functions

GDP-mannose-dependent IPC mannosyltransferase acting with Csg2 in the Golgi to make MIPC, redundant with the Sur1/Csg1-Csg2 enzyme but with distinct ceramide specificity.

Evidence journal

  • Redundancy PMID:12954640
  • Distinct specificity PMID:12954640
  • Csg2 dependence PMID:12954640
  • Vacuole membrane GFP (Huh), heterodimer with Csg2 [UniProt:P38287]
  • 2022 N-glycan follow-up: Sur1 and Csh1 N-glycans tune synthesis of different MIPC subtypes rather than changing the core IPC-to-MIPC reaction PMID:35713525
  • 2026 ethanol-stress follow-up: csg1D csh1D MIPC-deficient cells are ethanol-hypersensitive and lose viability, but this is downstream of MIPC loss and does not make either IPC mannosyltransferase a direct ethanol-response or cell-wall-integrity-pathway component PMID:42169399
