Gene Description

MBP1 encodes the 833-residue sequence-specific DNA-binding subunit of MBF (MluI cell-cycle box binding factor, also called DSC1), the Mbp1-Swi6 heterodimer that, together with the paralogous SBF (Swi4-Swi6), drives the G1/S (Start) transcriptional program of budding yeast and is a functional analog of the metazoan E2F-DP activators. Its N-terminal APSES/KilA-N helix-turn-helix domain (residues 5-111; crystal structure of residues 4-102) recognises the MCB element (consensus ACGCGTNA) in target promoters, a central block of ankyrin repeats is shared with Swi4 and Swi6, and the C-terminal region binds Swi6, which supplies the transcriptional activation function of the complex. Mbp1 is imported by Srp1-Kap95 through two redundant basic nuclear localization signals and remains nuclear throughout the cell cycle, unlike Swi6, which is exported in S/G2/M. MBF occupies MCB-containing promoters and activates, in late G1, the S-phase cyclin genes CLB5 and CLB6, the SWI4 gene and a large set of DNA-replication and repair genes such as POL1, CDC9, CDC21 and RNR1; genome-wide, the MBF and SBF target sets overlap extensively and the two complexes are largely redundant, so mbp1 null cells are viable with deregulated DNA-synthesis gene expression whereas mbp1 swi4 double mutants arrest before S phase and die. Outside G1 the complex is held off by the MBF-specific corepressor Nrm1 (itself an MBF target, closing a negative feedback loop); Whi5, the SBF corepressor, does not bind MBF. Under DNA replication stress the checkpoint kinase Rad53 phosphorylates and removes Nrm1 from MBF-bound promoters, so Mbp1-dependent genes such as RNR1 and TOS4 stay on, linking the DNA replication checkpoint to the G1/S transcriptional program. Nuclear Mbp1 copy number scales with nuclear volume in G1 at near-constant concentration, which has been proposed to contribute to the size-dependent timing of Start.

Core Functions

Mbp1 is the sequence-specific DNA-binding subunit of the MBF transcription factor. Its N-terminal APSES helix-turn-helix domain recognises MCB elements (ACGCGTNA) in the promoters of the S-phase cyclin genes CLB5 and CLB6, of SWI4 and of DNA-replication and repair genes such as POL1, CDC9, CDC21 and RNR1, and its C-terminal region binds Swi6, forming the constitutively nuclear Mbp1-Swi6 heterodimer that occupies MCB-containing promoter chromatin. In late G1 MBF activates Pol II transcription of this regulon, which together with the SBF-driven G1 cyclin genes constitutes the Start transcriptional switch; after G1 the MBF-bound corepressor Nrm1 (an MBF target) switches the complex off, and under replication stress Rad53-dependent removal of Nrm1 keeps the Mbp1-dependent genes on. MBF is largely redundant with SBF, so mbp1 null cells are viable but mbp1 swi4 double mutants cannot pass Start.

1. Identity verification and nomenclature

The symbol MBP1 in this report means S. cerevisiae strain-background S288c gene YDL056W, encoding transcription factor Mbp1, UniProt P39678. Primary structural literature describes the corresponding protein as an 833-amino-acid component of MBF and specifically as its sequence-selective DNA-binding subunit; Swi6 is the associated regulatory subunit and does not itself provide MBF’s principal sequence recognition. This exactly matches the supplied description “Transcription factor MBP1; MBF subunit p120.” (xu1997crystalstructureof pages 1-2)

The domain evidence is also concordant. Mbp1 has an N-terminal DNA-binding region within approximately residues 1–124, a central ankyrin-repeat-containing region, and a C-terminal region implicated in association with Swi6. The crystallized N-terminal region adopts a helix-turn-helix-related fold corresponding to the APSES/KilA-type HTH annotations supplied by UniProt/InterPro. Thus, the HTH_APSES/KilA_APSES, ankyrin-repeat, and SWI6-like annotations collectively fit a fungal cell-cycle transcription factor rather than an enzyme. (xu1997crystalstructureof pages 6-7, ho1999proteinsphysicallyinteracting pages 49-57)

2. Primary molecular function

2.1 MBF assembly and division of labor

Mbp1 combines with Swi6 to form MBF, the MluI cell-cycle box binding factor. Mbp1 supplies promoter recognition, whereas Swi6 supplies much of the regulated transcriptional activation and coregulatory interface. MBF is related to SBF, in which Swi4 replaces Mbp1 while Swi6 remains the common partner. Consequently, Mbp1 and Swi4 have partially overlapping—but not identical—promoter repertoires and biological functions. (xu1997crystalstructureof pages 1-2, costanzo2003g1transcriptionfactors pages 2-2, costanzo2003g1transcriptionfactors pages 1-2)

Mbp1 has no known catalytic reaction or small-molecule substrate specificity. Its relevant “substrate” in a binding sense is promoter DNA containing an MCB element, together with the surrounding promoter/chromatin and transcriptional regulatory machinery.

2.2 DNA recognition and structural mechanism

MBF recognizes MCB sites with the consensus ACGCGTNA, derived in the structural study from comparison of 32 sites. MBF footprinting protected approximately 10 nucleotides. The N-terminal 124 residues of Mbp1 are sufficient for DNA binding; the 1.71-Å crystal structure modeled residues 4–102 and revealed a six-stranded β-sheet, two pairs of α-helices, a basic DNA-contacting face, an HTH-related recognition helix, and a potentially adaptable β-hairpin. These observations provide direct structural support for sequence-specific major-groove recognition rather than catalytic activity. (xu1997crystalstructureof pages 6-7, xu1997crystalstructureof pages 8-9)

The supplied ankyrin-repeat annotations are likewise plausible for the experimentally described central repeat region. The C-terminal region participates in association with Swi6, helping assemble the functional heteromeric transcription factor. The exact contributions of individual repeats to activation, repression, or partner recruitment are less completely resolved than the N-terminal DNA-binding mechanism. (ho1999proteinsphysicallyinteracting pages 49-57)

3. Biological process and pathway placement

3.1 Start and the G1/S transcriptional program

MBF functions at Start, the commitment transition from G1 into a new division cycle. As cells approach the G1/S boundary, cyclin-CDK-dependent regulatory pathways activate SBF and MBF. Mbp1-bound MBF then induces genes required for DNA synthesis and associated S-phase functions. Experimentally supported or canonical targets include CLB5, CLB6, CDC9, POL1, RNR1, CDC21, and TMP1. These encode or support S-phase cyclins, DNA polymerase/ligation, nucleotide production, and thymidylate metabolism. (xu1997crystalstructureof pages 1-2, costanzo2003g1transcriptionfactors pages 1-2)

SBF and MBF together regulate a broad Start regulon: one synthesis cited more than 120 coordinately induced late-G1 genes and binding at 235 promoters. The latter represented only 21% of genes then classified as G1/S-induced, illustrating that occupancy datasets, expression datasets, and functional target definitions are not interchangeable. Target specificity is also not absolute because Mbp1 and Swi4 can occupy overlapping promoters. (costanzo2003g1transcriptionfactors pages 1-2)

3.2 Functional redundancy with SBF

The Mbp1 and Swi4 DNA-binding regions are approximately 50% identical, isolated regions can bind both MCB and SCB sequences in vitro, and one genome-wide comparison found that they shared about 34% of target genes. This explains why mbp1Δ is viable and relatively mild: SBF can compensate for a substantial part of the Start program. In contrast, loss of both MBP1 and SWI4 causes G1 arrest/inviability, indicating that at least one of the two DNA-binding arms is required for adequate Start transcription. (costanzo2003g1transcriptionfactors pages 2-2)

The redundancy is incomplete. Deleting SWI4 produces much stronger growth, morphology, bud-emergence, and temperature phenotypes than deleting MBP1 in commonly used laboratory conditions. An independent summary associates mbp1 deletion with an approximately 20% increase in cell size, consistent with delayed or altered Start rather than complete failure. (costanzo2003g1transcriptionfactors pages 2-2, busnelli2013proteinkinasesnf1ampk pages 38-42)

3.3 Termination and replication-stress checkpoint control

MBF activation is normally curtailed after G1/S through negative feedback involving the MBF corepressor Nrm1, with Yox1 contributing at a subset of promoters. During DNA-replication stress, the checkpoint kinase Rad53 sustains expression of an MBF-associated subset by promoting Nrm1 inactivation or dissociation from promoter-bound MBF. TOS4 illustrates this regulatory switch: SBF leaves its promoter in S phase, MBF–Nrm1 occupies it, and replication stress induces Nrm1 dissociation and TOS4 transcription. (oliveira2012linkingdnareplication pages 9-10)

This distinction is mechanistically important: the evidence supports checkpoint regulation of the Mbp1-containing MBF complex, but does not establish direct Rad53 phosphorylation of Mbp1. Mbp1 should therefore be annotated as a DNA-binding platform embedded in checkpoint-responsive transcription, not as a demonstrated direct Rad53 substrate. (oliveira2012linkingdnareplication pages 9-10)

4. Subcellular localization

Mbp1 carries out its principal function in the nucleus at promoter chromatin. Endogenously expressed HA-tagged Mbp1 colocalized with DAPI throughout the cell-cycle stages examined. A Mbp1 fragment containing its candidate nuclear-localization sequences was sufficient to direct a four-GFP reporter to the nucleus. Simultaneously disrupting both basic NLS elements shifted the reporter mainly to the cytoplasm, whereas individual mutations had little effect, demonstrating functional redundancy. (taberner2010yeastkaryopherinkap95 pages 6-8)

The candidate signals include KRKK and RKR-X10-RRK motifs. Import depends strongly on the classical importin-α/β pathway, involving Srp1 and Kap95. Kap95 inactivation or an srp1 defect abolished nuclear accumulation of the tested Mbp1 targeting fragment. Residual nuclear accumulation of full-length Mbp1 under some perturbations suggests that an additional import route or partner-assisted import may operate. (taberner2010yeastkaryopherinkap95 pages 6-8, taberner2010yeastkaryopherinkap95 pages 11-12)

Live-cell fluorescence measurements independently showed strong nuclear enrichment: Mbp1 and related G1/S factors had cytosolic concentrations approximately 10–75-fold below their nuclear concentrations. Thus, “nuclear” is strongly supported experimentally and is not merely inferred from transcription-factor annotation. (dorsey2018g1stranscriptionfactor pages 7-8)

5. Quantitative cellular evidence

Scanning number-and-brightness microscopy quantified Mbp1-GFP in living G1 cells. In glucose, small and large G1 cells contained approximately 79 and 248 nuclear Mbp1 molecules, respectively, at nearly constant concentrations of 105 and 110 nM. In glycerol, corresponding estimates were 109 and 339 molecules at 145 and 150 nM. The glucose Mbp1 dataset included 194 cells across at least three experiments. Thus, nuclear Mbp1 concentration remains broadly stable as cells grow, while total nuclear copy number rises approximately threefold because nuclear volume increases. (dorsey2018g1stranscriptionfactor pages 7-8, dorsey2018g1stranscriptionfactor pages 6-7)

The authors interpreted SBF/MBF abundance as limiting relative to roughly 200 G1/S promoters in small cells and becoming sufficient for broader promoter occupancy near Start. This promoter-titration interpretation is a mechanistic model supported by abundance measurements, rather than proof that Mbp1 copy number alone determines commitment. Nutrient-dependent elevation of MBF components in poorer carbon conditions may help explain how Start size is adjusted to growth conditions. (dorsey2018g1stranscriptionfactor pages 1-2, dorsey2018g1stranscriptionfactor pages 8-8, dorsey2018g1stranscriptionfactor pages 2-5)

6. Recent developments, emphasizing 2023–2024

6.1 Direct 2024 experimental update: WHI7 regulation

Ros-Carrero and colleagues reported that WHI7, encoding a Start repressor, is a direct overlapping target of MBF and SBF. Mbp1-HA ChIP detected Mbp1 at the WHI7 promoter, with untagged cells and the established RNR1 promoter used as controls. A promoter–lacZ reporter decreased in mbp1 and swi4 mutants and showed an additive decrease in the double mutant, supporting positive regulation by both complexes. (roscarrero2024cellcycleregulated pages 6-8)

The WHI7 promoter contains an MCB-like ACGCGT sequence at position −217. In synchronized α-factor-release experiments, WHI7 RNA varied approximately threefold and peaked in G1, between the SIC1-associated mitotic-exit/G1 peak and the CLN2 Start peak. Mutation studies showed that its MCB and Swi5-responsive elements can partly compensate for one another, whereas combined disruption abolished maximal periodic expression. These results add WHI7 to the experimentally supported MBF/SBF-overlap regulon and suggest a feedback architecture in which the Start program induces one of its repressors. The feedback interpretation is biologically plausible, but its precise dynamical role remains less directly demonstrated than promoter binding and reporter regulation. The paper was published in September 2024; DOI/URL: https://doi.org/10.1080/15384101.2024.2402192. (roscarrero2024cellcycleregulated pages 4-6, roscarrero2024cellcycleregulated pages 2-4, roscarrero2024cellcycleregulated pages 8-11)

6.2 2024 mechanistic application: START-BYCC

Ravi, Samart, and Zwolak incorporated Mbp1/MBF into the START-BYCC mathematical model, which explicitly represents promoter-bound and free complexes, phosphorylation states, cellular localization, nutrient effects, and size control. Its Start subsystem contains 51 species and 56 parameters. Analyses covered more than 120 Start-mutant phenotypes and, in a broader simulation set, more than 200 relevant mutants; approximately 95% of tested mutant phenotypes were reproduced. The model recapitulates established findings such as inviability of swi4Δ mbp1Δ and predicts the behavior of compound Start mutants. Published August 2, 2024; DOI/URL: https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1012048. (ravi2024modelingthestart pages 9-10, ravi2024modelingthestart pages 6-9, ravi2024modelingthestart pages 23-25)

These are computational results calibrated against published experiments, not new biochemical proof about Mbp1. Their practical value is integration: they make the Mbp1–Swi6 branch available for executable hypothesis testing, mutant simulation, and comparison of competing Start mechanisms.

6.3 State of the recent literature

The 2023–2024 search found relatively little new work devoted specifically to the biochemical mechanism of S. cerevisiae Mbp1. Recent research instead uses MBF as an established module in promoter-regulation studies and systems-level Start models. Consequently, the most reliable annotation still depends on foundational crystallographic, biochemical, localization, and genetic studies, complemented by the 2024 WHI7 experiments and START-BYCC modeling.

7. Current applications and real-world relevance

Mbp1 is principally a research and systems-biology component, not a clinically deployed target. Current uses include:

  1. Mechanistic analysis of eukaryotic cell-cycle commitment. The SBF/MBF network is a tractable model for understanding how cell growth, cyclin-CDK activity, transcriptional positive feedback, and repression produce commitment at Start. The 2024 START-BYCC implementation permits simulation of compound mutants and spatial regulatory states. (ravi2024modelingthestart pages 6-9, ravi2024modelingthestart pages 23-25)
  2. Promoter and regulatory-network annotation. MCB motifs, Mbp1 ChIP, and perturbation of MBP1 are used to classify G1/S promoters and distinguish MBF-biased, SBF-biased, and overlapping targets, as illustrated by WHI7. (roscarrero2024cellcycleregulated pages 6-8, roscarrero2024cellcycleregulated pages 2-4)
  3. Replication-stress research. The Mbp1-containing MBF complex links normal cell-cycle transcription to Rad53-dependent checkpoint responses through Nrm1, making it useful for studying how cells sustain nucleotide metabolism and repair-associated expression during stalled DNA synthesis. (oliveira2012linkingdnareplication pages 9-10)
  4. Quantitative cell-size control. Absolute Mbp1 abundance measurements support testable promoter-titration models connecting nuclear growth to Start commitment. (dorsey2018g1stranscriptionfactor pages 7-8, dorsey2018g1stranscriptionfactor pages 2-5)

Any industrial interpretation should remain cautious. Altering MBP1 can change proliferation and stress-responsive transcription in laboratory yeast, but the reviewed evidence does not establish MBP1 engineering as a validated general strategy for improving fermentation yield or heterologous protein production.

8. Evidence-weighted functional annotation

Recommended concise annotation:

Mbp1 is the nuclear, sequence-specific DNA-binding subunit of the Swi6-containing MBF transcription factor. It recognizes MCB promoter elements, principally ACGCGTNA-like sequences, and activates a late-G1/S regulon enriched for DNA-replication, nucleotide-metabolism, and S-phase genes. MBF activity is integrated with Start cyclin-CDK control and is subsequently repressed by Nrm1-associated negative feedback; replication stress relieves this repression through Rad53-dependent checkpoint signaling.

Evidence strength:

  • High confidence: identity; MBF composition; MCB binding; N-terminal HTH/APSES-like structure; nuclear localization/import; participation in G1/S transcription; partial redundancy with Swi4.
  • Moderate-to-high confidence: specific target membership where supported by promoter occupancy plus expression/genetics; Nrm1-mediated checkpoint regulation of Mbp1-containing MBF.
  • Moderate confidence/model-dependent: promoter titration as the causal size sensor and detailed START-BYCC species-state dynamics.
  • Not established: catalytic activity, transporter function, direct Rad53 modification of Mbp1, or a unique essential phenotype for MBP1 under standard growth conditions.

Core biology (with provenance)

  • Discovery: MBF (also DSC1) was purified from yeast as Swi6 plus a 120 kDa protein and the
    p120 gene cloned as MBP1
    PMID:8372350. The complex binds MCB elements
    PMID:8372350.
  • Mbp1 is the DNA-binding subunit; Swi6 is the shared regulatory/activation subunit of both
    MBF and SBF PMID:10409718
    PMID:10490612. The N-terminal ~124 residues suffice for DNA binding and the 1.71 A
    crystal structure of the domain shows an HTH-related fold with a six-stranded beta-sheet
    (PMID:9083114, not cached) PMID:10490612.
  • MCB consensus ACGCGTNA; MBF targets include the S-phase cyclins CLB5/CLB6, SWI4 and
    DNA-synthesis genes such as CDC9, POL1 and RNR1 PMID:10409718 PMID:10409718 PMID:10409718.

    PMID:8372350
    PMID:8372350 PMID:10409718. Genome-wide, MBF and SBF have
    highly overlapping target sets and largely redundant roles (PMID:15965243, not cached;

    via Nrm1, not direct Rad53 phosphorylation of Mbp1.

    stages; two redundant NLSs; Srp1/Kap95 import; PMID:20587033, not cached), and the HDA

    PMID:11914276. The nucleocytoplasmic

  MBP1 encodes the 833-residue sequence-specific DNA-binding subunit of MBF (MluI cell-cycle
  box binding factor, also called DSC1), the Mbp1-Swi6 heterodimer that, together with the
  paralogous SBF (Swi4-Swi6), drives the G1/S (Start) transcriptional program of budding yeast
  and is a functional analog of the metazoan E2F-DP activators. Its N-terminal APSES/KilA-N
  helix-turn-helix domain (residues 5-111; crystal structure of residues 4-102) recognises the
  MCB element (consensus ACGCGTNA) in target promoters, a central block of ankyrin repeats is
  shared with Swi4 and Swi6, and the C-terminal region binds Swi6, which supplies the
  transcriptional activation function of the complex. Mbp1 is imported by Srp1-Kap95 through
  two redundant basic nuclear localization signals and remains nuclear throughout the cell
  cycle, unlike Swi6, which is exported in S/G2/M. MBF occupies MCB-containing promoters and
  activates, in late G1, the S-phase cyclin genes CLB5 and CLB6, the SWI4 gene and a large
  set of DNA-replication and repair genes such as POL1, CDC9, CDC21 and RNR1; genome-wide,
  the MBF and SBF target sets overlap extensively and the two complexes are largely
  redundant, so mbp1 null cells are viable with deregulated DNA-synthesis gene expression
  whereas mbp1 swi4 double mutants arrest before S phase and die. Outside G1 the complex is
  held off by the MBF-specific corepressor Nrm1 (itself an MBF target, closing a negative
  feedback loop); Whi5, the SBF corepressor, does not bind MBF. Under DNA replication stress
  the checkpoint kinase Rad53 phosphorylates and removes Nrm1 from MBF-bound promoters, so
  Mbp1-dependent genes such as RNR1 and TOS4 stay on, linking the DNA replication checkpoint
  to the G1/S transcriptional program. Nuclear Mbp1 copy number scales with nuclear volume
  in G1 at near-constant concentration, which has been proposed to contribute to the
  size-dependent timing of Start.
