Gene Description

N-acetylglutamate synthase (NAGS; amino-acid acetyltransferase, EC 2.3.1.1) of Saccharomyces cerevisiae, a mitochondrial matrix enzyme that acetylates L-glutamate with acetyl-CoA to form N-acetyl-L-glutamate, the first step of the acetylated (ornithine-producing) branch of arginine biosynthesis. Like other fungal NAGS it is unrelated in sequence to bacterial ArgA. Arg2 is active only in a stoichiometric complex (metabolon) with the acetylglutamate kinase domain of Arg5,6; complex formation is required for synthase activity and stability and couples arginine feedback inhibition of NAGS and NAGK, the two flux-controlling steps of the pathway. Arg2 provides the anaplerotic supply of N-acetylglutamate, which is otherwise recycled by ornithine acetyltransferase (Arg7). The kinase-associated domain is required for synthase activity, while the separate reductase product is dispensable for this support.

Core Functions

Acetyl-CoA-dependent acetylation of L-glutamate to N-acetyl-L-glutamate (EC 2.3.1.1), the first, arginine-feedback-inhibited step of arginine biosynthesis, carried out in the mitochondrial matrix as part of the NAGS/NAGK complex with Arg5,6.
