Gene Description

RAD27 (also known as RTH1 and YKL510; the Saccharomyces cerevisiae flap endonuclease 1) encodes the budding-yeast orthologue of FEN1, a magnesium-dependent structure-specific nuclease of the XPG/RAD2 family. Rad27 cleaves 5' single-stranded flaps, strongly preferring a double-flap substrate in which the upstream primer carries a one-nucleotide 3' tail; the tail positions cleavage exactly one nucleotide into the annealed region, so that every product is a nick that DNA ligase I (Cdc9) can seal. The same active site supports a processive 5'-to-3' exonuclease activity on duplex DNA and cleaves RNA flaps within RNA:DNA hybrid intermediates. Its central cellular role is Okazaki fragment maturation on the lagging strand, acting on the flaps produced by DNA polymerase delta strand-displacement synthesis together with PCNA (Pol30), which binds the Rad27 PIP box and both targets and stimulates the enzyme; a second, minor route uses Dna2 to trim long RPA-coated flaps before Rad27 makes the final cut, and DNA2 and RAD27 interact physically and are synthetically lethal. Rad27 also removes abasic-site flaps in long-patch base excision repair and trims incompatible 5' ends during nonhomologous end joining with Pol4 and Dnl4/Lif1; its human orthologue additionally incises the RNA strand of R-loops, an activity the yeast enzyme is expected to share on the basis of its RNA-flap cleavage but which has not been tested directly. Loss of RAD27 leaves unligated Okazaki fragments and causes pronounced genome instability, including expansion and contraction of CAG trinucleotide repeats and severe ribosomal DNA copy-number changes. Rad27 is predominantly nuclear and enriched in the nucleolus, and a functional pool is present in mitochondria, where it contributes to mitochondrial DNA integrity.

Function summary as used for the review

Rad27 is the budding-yeast FEN1 orthologue: a Mg2+-dependent, structure-specific XPG/RAD2-family nuclease.

Substrate specificity is the most precisely characterized aspect. Rad27p prefers a double-flap
substrate and the 3' tail of the upstream primer, not the 5' flap, sets the cut site:
PMID:11825897
PMID:11825897
It discriminates among branch-migration isomers of the same substrate:
PMID:11825897
This is why every Rad27 product is directly ligatable and why Rad27 and Cdc9 must act in an ordered way.

Exonuclease activity. Purified as the pol alpha-associated 5'→3' exonuclease before it was recognized
as the RAD27 product:
PMID:9166764

Two primer-removal routes. Reconstitution shows the short-flap/Rad27-only route dominates, with a
minor Dna2-dependent long-flap route:
PMID:16837458
PMID:16837458
Genetically, DNA2 and RAD27 are synthetically lethal and their products co-purify:
PMID:9121462

PCNA ordering. PIP-box alleles establish that PCNA sequences Rad27 and Cdc9 entry into fragment joining:
PMID:16079237

Repeat instability — the strongest in vivo phenotypes.
CAG tracts: PMID:16079237

  • §3.3–§3.5 (the R-loop sections) are human-only: the reconstituted BER reactions use

  • GO:0004523 RNA-DNA hybrid ribonuclease activity — IDA. Directly assayed on purified Rad27.
    PMID:36672839
  • GO:0062176 R-loop processing — ISS from human FEN1 (UniProtKB:P39748), not IDA. Rad27 has the

- question: The IBA 'is_active_in cytoplasm' annotation has no identifiable cytosolic substrate for Rad27.
    Should the PANTHER family annotation be re-scoped to the mitochondrion, the only extranuclear compartment
    where Rad27 has a demonstrated DNA substrate?
- question: How much of the rad27 delta genome-instability phenotype is attributable to unremoved flaps
    versus to the secondary recombination and checkpoint responses they trigger?
- question: Exo1 and PCNA partially compensate for loss of Rad27 in rDNA stabilization. Which step of
    Okazaki fragment maturation does Exo1 actually substitute for, and is the compensation nucleolytic
    or structural?
- question: Does the cytosolic Rad27 signal reported under hypoxia represent regulated relocalization
    with a functional consequence, or redistribution of a nuclear pool with no substrate outside the nucleus
    and mitochondria?

- description: Okazaki fragment sequencing (OK-seq or equivalent) in rad27 delta, rad27-p (PIP-box) and
    nuclease-dead RAD27 strains, to separate the contributions of PCNA targeting and of catalysis to fragment
    maturation genome-wide and at the rDNA specifically.

    RPA, Pol30 and Cdc9 to quantify the flux through the short-flap versus long-flap routes under near-physiological
    conditions.
