Gene Description

ERG1 (YGR175C) encodes squalene epoxidase (squalene monooxygenase, EC 1.14.14.17), which converts squalene to (3S)-2,3-epoxysqualene (2,3-oxidosqualene), the first oxygen-dependent step of the sterol branch and the substrate for lanosterol synthase Erg7. The enzyme is a membrane-bound flavoprotein of the squalene monooxygenase family that draws electrons from NADPH via NADPH-cytochrome P450 reductase; it is a rate-limiting step of ergosterol synthesis and the target of allylamine antifungals such as terbinafine. Erg1p is distributed between the ER membrane and lipid droplets, but catalytic activity is detected only in the ER, the LD pool requiring an ER factor for activity. erg1 mutants are ergosterol auxotrophs. Erg1p is anchored in the ER membrane by a hairpin that is recognised by Get3, so its targeting, especially its rapid upregulation under low-sterol conditions, depends on the GET pathway. Its abundance is also controlled by ER-associated degradation through the Doa10-Ubc7 ubiquitin ligase, which is triggered when membrane lipids become saturated.
