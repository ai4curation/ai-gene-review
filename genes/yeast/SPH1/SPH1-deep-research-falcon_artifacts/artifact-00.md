| SPH1-specific finding | Evidence type and strength | Evidence and methodological qualification |
|---|---|---|
| **Identity and domains** | **Curated identity; homology-supported domain annotation** | Target is *S. cerevisiae* Sph1p, UniProt **Q06160**, gene **SPH1**, locus **YLR313C** (identifiers supplied in the research brief). Spa2 contains five regions conserved with Sph1p, designated SHD-I–V; the supplied InterPro/Pfam annotations place Sph1p in the Spa2/Sph1 family and identify a GIT_SHD-related region. These establish homology, not identical partner specificity or function (pqac-00000019, pqac-00000020). |
| **Polarized localization** | **Moderate: later primary-study summary plus review; foundational direct imaging not retrieved** | Li *et al.* state that the seven recognized polarisome proteins, including Sph1p, localize to small-bud tips and large-bud necks. A later review likewise describes the conserved region as important for targeting Spa2 and Sph1 to polarized-growth sites. Because the original Sph1 imaging study was unavailable, precise stage-specific localization should be treated as literature-supported rather than independently re-evaluated here (pqac-00000017, pqac-00000001). |
| **Polarized mating and bipolar budding** | **Moderate: primary article reporting earlier findings; foundational paper unavailable** | Fujiwara *et al.* report that **SPH1** had been shown to be required for bipolar budding and mating-projection (“shmoo”) formation, citing the foundational Arkowitz–Lowe and Roemer *et al.* studies. The original Roemer study is identifiable as *The Spa2-related protein, Sph1p, is important for polarized growth in yeast*, but its full experimental details and numerical phenotype penetrance were not available for verification (pqac-00000015, pqac-00000000). |
| **Cortical-ER inheritance in a `ptc1Δ` background** | **Strong direct genetic and imaging evidence** | `sph1Δ` significantly suppressed the `ptc1Δ` cortical-ER inheritance defect. Across the rescued polarisome double mutants, including `sph1Δ ptc1Δ`, approximately **70–90%** of small buds contained a distributed cortical-ER network, versus approximately **80%** in wild type; in `ptc1Δ`, **>80%** instead contained mother–bud-axis ER tubules without detectable cortical ER. The 70–90% interval is pooled across several mutant classes and is not a uniquely reported Sph1 value (pqac-00000012). |
| **Slt2/Mpk1 activation versus localization** | **Strong direct biochemical and fluorescence evidence** | In `sph1Δ ptc1Δ`, dually phosphorylated Slt2 was significantly reduced relative to `ptc1Δ`, based on immunoblots quantified over **four independent experiments**. By contrast, deleting **SPH1** did not significantly alter Slt2-GFP localization or Mkk1-GFP localization at bud tips/necks. Thus Sph1 supports full CWI-MAPK activation in this context rather than recruiting the kinase module to the polarized site (pqac-00000006, pqac-00000013, pqac-00000018, pqac-00000030). |
| **No detected Bni1 interaction or tested genetic coupling** | **Limited negative evidence reported as unpublished observations within a primary paper** | Fujiwara *et al.* reported that `sph1` was not synthetically lethal with `rho1::RhoA` or `pkc1` and that Sph1p did not interact with Bni1p in their yeast two-hybrid assay. These results distinguish Sph1 from the experimentally demonstrated Spa2–Bni1 axis, but negative two-hybrid and genetic tests do **not** exclude condition-dependent, indirect, or assay-inaccessible interactions (pqac-00000015). |


*Table: Evidence specific to *Saccharomyces cerevisiae* Sph1p/Q06160, separated by direct experiment, secondary reporting, and homology-based inference. The table explicitly avoids transferring Spa2-specific binding results to Sph1p.*
