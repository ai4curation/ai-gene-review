# Functional annotation report: *S. cerevisiae* **NDL1** (YLR254C; UniProt Q06568)

## Executive conclusion

The requested identity is verified: **NDL1 is YLR254C in *Saccharomyces cerevisiae* S288c and encodes the budding-yeast homolog of NudE/NudEL-family dynein regulators**. The original study identified YLR254C as Ndl1 and found 24% amino-acid identity and 46% similarity to zebrafish NudEL, with coiled-coil and NUDE_C-family architecture. Tagged endogenous YLR254C constructs were explicitly designated NDL1 in the experiments. Thus, this report does not concern plant NDL proteins, mammalian NDE1/NDEL1 themselves, or the similarly named yeast meiotic protein Ndj1. (li2005nudeltargetsdynein pages 1-1, li2005nudeltargetsdynein pages 1-3)

**Primary functional annotation:** Ndl1 is not an enzyme or transporter. It is a **nonenzymatic, coiled-coil dynein-regulatory assembly/adaptor protein**. Its best-supported role is to coordinate the Pac1/LIS1 regulator with cytoplasmic dynein during dynein recruitment and activation, particularly at astral-microtubule plus ends. This contributes to dynein-dependent mitotic-spindle positioning and faithful nuclear segregation.

## 1. Molecular identity and structural features

Li, Lee and Cooper first characterized yeast YLR254C as the NudEL homolog and named it **Ndl1**. Their sequence comparison identified coiled-coil regions and a C-terminal NUDE_C-family motif, consistent with UniProt’s assignment to the NudE family. The relatively modest primary-sequence conservation—24% identity and 46% similarity to zebrafish NudEL—helps explain why family recognition depends partly on conserved architecture rather than gene symbol alone. (li2005nudeltargetsdynein pages 1-1)

No catalytic reaction, substrate specificity, membrane-transport substrate, or enzymatic active site has been demonstrated. The protein is better classified as a **protein-interaction scaffold/regulator**. Its coiled-coil supports interactions with Pac1 and with the N-terminal region of the dynein intermediate chain; mutagenesis indicates that these contacts can be assigned to distinguishable interaction surfaces. In particular, substitution of Ndl1 E64 and R78 disrupted Pac1 binding without disrupting dynein binding, supporting separable interfaces rather than nonspecific association. (okada2023conservedrolesfor pages 6-6)

## 2. Primary function and molecular partners

### Pac1/LIS1 interaction

Pac1 is the budding-yeast homolog of LIS1. Reciprocal epitope-tag immunoprecipitation, immunoblotting and sedimentation analyses support physical association between Ndl1 and Pac1 in yeast extracts. Pac1 overexpression can suppress an *ndl1* defect, whereas reciprocal suppression was not observed, placing Ndl1 functionally upstream of or alongside Pac1 in the dynein-recruitment pathway. (li2005nudeltargetsdynein pages 1-1, ranran2007identificationandcharacterization pages 36-39)

### Dynein interaction

Recent biochemical work shows that Ndl1 directly contacts the **N-terminal region of the dynein intermediate chain**, while also binding Pac1 through its coiled-coil. This makes Ndl1 an assembly/regulatory factor connecting Pac1 biology to the dynein complex, rather than a dynein motor subunit that generates force itself. (okada2023conservedrolesfor pages 7-8, okada2023conservedrolesfor pages 7-7, okada2023conservedrolesfor pages 6-6)

### Updated handoff mechanism

The important 2023 revision is that Ndl1, Pac1 and dynein should not simply be represented as one stable ternary complex. Ndl1 and the dynein motor use overlapping or competing surfaces on Pac1/LIS1. Consequently, Pac1 is proposed to bind Ndl1 and then be **handed off** to dynein in temporally separate steps. Mass photometry found 1:1 dimeric Pac1/LIS1–Ndel1-family complexes, while biochemical competition and yeast-cell experiments supported mutually regulated engagement with dynein. (okada2023conservedrolesfor pages 6-7)

This reconciles apparently paradoxical cellular data: deleting **NDL1** increased detectable dynein–Pac1 association at plus-end foci, whereas Ndl1 overexpression reduced the frequency and intensity of those foci. Ndl1 therefore regulates the timing and availability of Pac1–dynein binding rather than merely maximizing stable association. The relevant imaging analyses included 105/102 wild-type versus 104/101 *ndl1Δ* cells in independent replicates and 113/100 uninduced versus 102/100 Ndl1-overexpressing cells; reported comparisons included *P* < 0.0001 and *P* = 0.0199, although the available text did not expose every plotted mean or effect size. (okada2023conservedrolesfor pages 7-8, okada2023conservedrolesfor pages 7-7)

## 3. Cellular localization and site of action

Live two-color microscopy directly observed Ndl1 transiently associating with **cytoplasmic microtubule plus ends**. Ndl1 and Pac1 colocalize there, and both contribute to concentrating Dyn1/dynein at plus ends. This is the strongest localization assignment for the protein. (li2005nudeltargetsdynein pages 1-3, ranran2007identificationandcharacterization pages 36-39)

The pathway proceeds spatially as follows:

1. Dynein is recruited to the plus ends of astral cytoplasmic microtubules through cooperating regulators, including Ndl1–Pac1 and the Kip2–Bik1 machinery.
2. Plus-end-associated dynein encounters the bud cortex.
3. Dynein is transferred to cortical Num1 anchoring sites and activated with dynactin.
4. Cortex-anchored dynein pulls on astral microtubules toward their minus ends, repositioning the spindle across the mother–bud neck.

Ndl1 acts predominantly in the **plus-end recruitment/assembly stage**. It is not established as a stable cortical component, and Pac1/Ndl1 are not prominent at the final cortical force-generating site. The two recruitment branches are cooperative: disruption of either Ndl1–Pac1 or Bik1–Kip2 can cause almost complete loss of dynein plus-end localization. (ranran2007identificationandcharacterization pages 36-39, moore2009functionofdynein pages 1-3)

## 4. Biological process and pathway placement

In budding yeast, cytoplasmic dynein’s principal physiological role is mitotic-spindle positioning rather than the broad vesicular-cargo transport typical of animal cells. Dynein moves toward microtubule minus ends using its AAA+ motor, while Ndl1 regulates recruitment and assembly rather than ATP hydrolysis. The dynein pathway helps pull an astral-microtubule-associated spindle pole toward and through the bud neck. (moore2009functionofdynein pages 1-3)

This pathway operates partly redundantly with the **Kar9 pathway**. Loss of NDL1 causes binucleate/chromosome-segregation defects, and the phenotype becomes stronger when **KAR9** or **KIP3** is also deleted. This genetic interaction is consistent with defective spindle positioning: if both the Ndl1-dependent dynein route and the compensating Kar9 route are compromised, nuclear segregation fails more severely. It should not be interpreted as evidence that Ndl1 is a kinetochore protein or chromosome-segregation enzyme. (li2005nudeltargetsdynein pages 1-1)

## 5. Evidence hierarchy

**Direct biochemical evidence:** Ndl1–Pac1 association was detected by co-immunoprecipitation and sedimentation; purified-protein assays and interface mutations support Ndl1 binding to the dynein intermediate-chain N terminus and Pac1 through separable surfaces. (li2005nudeltargetsdynein pages 1-1, okada2023conservedrolesfor pages 6-6)

**Direct cell-biological evidence:** fluorescent Ndl1 transiently occupies microtubule plus ends; NDL1 deletion and overexpression alter dynein–Pac1 plus-end foci. (okada2023conservedrolesfor pages 7-8, li2005nudeltargetsdynein pages 1-3)

**Direct genetic evidence:** *ndl1Δ* produces segregation/binucleate phenotypes that are enhanced in Kar9-pathway mutant backgrounds; Pac1 overexpression suppresses an Ndl1 defect. (ranran2007identificationandcharacterization pages 36-39, li2005nudeltargetsdynein pages 1-1)

**Evolutionary/structural inference:** conserved coiled-coil architecture, NUDE_C assignment, analogous residues and cross-species interaction organization support orthology to NudE/NDEL proteins. Nevertheless, mammalian NDE1/NDEL1 phenotypes must not automatically be assigned to yeast Ndl1. A 2022 expert review emphasized that yeast and mammalian proteins can have different measured effects on dynein–microtubule affinity and that the family’s complete regulatory mechanism remained unresolved. (okada2023conservedrolesfor pages 6-6, garrott2022nde1andndel1 pages 2-4)

## 6. Recent developments, 2023–2024

### 2023: conserved assembly and sequential Pac1/LIS1 transfer

Okada *et al.* provided the most important recent Ndl1-specific advance. Their combined yeast/human biochemical, structural and cellular experiments showed that the dynein intermediate-chain N terminus has conserved roles in Ndl1/Ndel1-dependent dynein assembly. The study replaced the simple stable-bridge model with a sequential mechanism in which Ndl1 recruits or organizes Pac1, after which Pac1 must disengage from Ndl1 before binding the dynein motor. Published September 2023: https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-023-41466-5. (okada2023conservedrolesfor pages 7-8, okada2023conservedrolesfor pages 6-6, okada2023conservedrolesfor pages 6-7)

### 2024: Pac1/LIS1 mechanochemistry adjacent to Ndl1 function

A 2024 single-molecule and optical-trapping study used yeast dynein to show that Pac1/LIS1 slows motor movement through contacts with the AAA+ ring and stalk, prolongs stalls under load and reduces the normal asymmetry of force-induced microtubule detachment without reducing force generation. Stalk-site mutations partly restored detachment asymmetry but did not restore velocity. This sharpens understanding of what Pac1 does after recruitment, but **Ndl1 was not directly tested**, so these findings should be treated as pathway context rather than an additional Ndl1 function. Published online under DOI https://doi.org/10.1038/s41589-023-01464-6 and appearing in *Nature Chemical Biology* volume 20 (2024), pp. 521–529. (kusakci2024lis1slowsforceinduced pages 1-3)

No 2024 paper directly revising the functional annotation of yeast Ndl1 was identified in the retrieved literature. Thus, the 2023 handoff study remains the latest direct mechanistic source found for this protein.

## 7. Applications and real-world relevance

There is no established industrial, diagnostic or therapeutic application of yeast Ndl1 itself. Its practical importance is as an **experimental model component**:

- Budding yeast allows viable deletion of dynein-pathway components, quantitative live-cell imaging and synthetic-interaction analysis with the Kar9 pathway.
- Purified yeast proteins permit single-molecule dissection of dynein recruitment, motility and force response.
- The single-copy, genetically tractable system helps separate conserved dynein-regulatory principles from vertebrate-specific NDE1/NDEL1 paralog functions.
- Because human LIS1/NDE1/NDEL1 pathways are implicated in neurodevelopment and intracellular transport, yeast Ndl1 supplies mechanistic hypotheses, but not a direct disease model unless conservation is experimentally demonstrated. (kusakci2024lis1slowsforceinduced pages 1-3, garrott2022nde1andndel1 pages 2-4, ranran2007identificationandcharacterization pages 54-56)

## 8. Expert interpretation and remaining uncertainties

The strongest current interpretation is that Ndl1 is a **transient dynein-assembly regulator**, not a constitutive structural subunit or motor activator acting at all stages. It helps organize Pac1 and dynein before cortical activation, while controlling when Pac1 transfers onto the motor. This interpretation accounts for its plus-end localization, physical interactions, deletion phenotype and concentration-dependent effects. (okada2023conservedrolesfor pages 7-8, okada2023conservedrolesfor pages 6-7)

Important unresolved questions include the exact timing and cellular trigger for Pac1 handoff, whether Ndl1 participates in every dynein-delivery event, and how its concentration is regulated. The detailed effects of mammalian NDE1/NDEL1 cannot be assumed for yeast because species-specific mechanistic differences have been reported. (kusakci2024lis1slowsforceinduced pages 1-3, garrott2022nde1andndel1 pages 2-4)

## Bottom-line annotation

**NDL1/YLR254C/Q06568 encodes a cytoplasmic, nonenzymatic NudE-family coiled-coil regulator of the budding-yeast dynein pathway. Ndl1 transiently functions at astral-microtubule plus ends, binding Pac1/LIS1 and the dynein intermediate-chain N terminus to regulate Pac1 transfer to dynein. This enables proper dynein localization and subsequent cortex-dependent spindle positioning and nuclear segregation, especially when the parallel Kar9 pathway cannot compensate.**
