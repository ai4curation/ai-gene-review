| Question | Best-supported conclusion | Evidence type | Confidence or limitation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Identity | Ndl1 is encoded by the *Saccharomyces cerevisiae* locus **YLR254C** and corresponds to UniProt **Q06568**. It is a NudE-family homolog with coiled-coil and NUDE_C-like architecture. | Sequence comparison, tagged endogenous constructs, and comparative structural analysis | **High.** The original comparison found 24% identity and 46% similarity to zebrafish NudEL. It must not be confused with similarly named proteins in other organisms. |
| Molecular function | Ndl1 is a **nonenzymatic dynein-regulatory adaptor or assembly factor**, not an enzyme or transporter. It regulates recruitment and transfer of Pac1/LIS1 to cytoplasmic dynein. | Biochemical binding, mutagenesis, and live-cell imaging | **High** for dynein regulation; **moderate** for the complete temporal sequence in vivo. |
| Physical partners | Directly supported partners are **Pac1**, the yeast LIS1 homolog, and the N-terminal region of the dynein intermediate chain. Distinguishable coiled-coil surfaces mediate these interactions. | Reciprocal co-immunoprecipitation, sedimentation, purified-protein binding, and interface mutagenesis | **High.** Association and separable interfaces are supported, although the complexes may be transient in cells. |
| Localization | Ndl1 transiently associates with **cytoplasmic astral-microtubule plus ends**, where Pac1 and dynein are recruited before dynein is transferred to cortical Num1 sites. | Two-color live-cell fluorescence microscopy and dynein-focus quantification | **High** for transient plus-end localization; **moderate** for whether Ndl1 participates in every transfer event. Ndl1 is not established as a stable cortical component. |
| Pathway and phenotype | Ndl1 acts in the **dynein-dependent spindle-positioning and nuclear-segregation pathway**. Loss of NDL1 produces binucleate or segregation defects that become stronger when the partially redundant Kar9 pathway is impaired. | Deletion genetics, synthetic-interaction tests, and cell-phenotype scoring | **High** for pathway placement. The phenotype is best interpreted as defective spindle positioning rather than a direct kinetochore defect. |
| 2023 revised mechanism | The current model favors a **sequential Pac1 handoff** rather than a stable Ndl1–Pac1–dynein ternary complex. Ndl1 binds the dynein intermediate-chain N terminus, but Ndl1 and the dynein motor compete for an overlapping Pac1 surface. | Purified yeast and human proteins, structural modeling, interface mutations, deletion, overexpression, and cellular imaging | **High** for competition and interface organization in the tested systems; **moderate** for the exact timing of handoff in living yeast. |
| 2024 adjacent Pac1 evidence | Single-molecule experiments showed that Pac1/LIS1 slows yeast-dynein movement through contacts with the AAA+ ring and stalk, prolongs load-induced stalls, and slows force-induced detachment without reducing force generation. | Single-molecule fluorescence, optical trapping, and motor-site mutagenesis | **High** for Pac1 effects on purified yeast dynein but **indirect for Ndl1**, because Ndl1 was not tested. |
