| Aspect | Best-supported annotation | Key evidence/qualification |
|---|---|---|
| Identity | **Ste11**, encoded by **STE11/YLR362W** in *Saccharomyces cerevisiae* S288c; UniProt **P23561**; a MEKK-family serine/threonine MAP kinase kinase kinase (MAP3K). | Identity matches the supplied accession, locus, organism, kinase family, and literature. It must not be confused with the unrelated *Schizosaccharomyces pombe* transcription factor Ste11 or Ste11 homologs from other fungi. |
| Molecular function/reaction | ATP-dependent protein serine/threonine kinase: **ATP + protein-OH → ADP + phosphoprotein**. Its primary signaling function is phosphorylation and activation of pathway-specific MAPK kinases. | Catalytic activity resides in the C-terminal protein-kinase domain. Physiological specificity depends strongly on scaffolds, adaptors, recruitment, and stimulus context rather than on an unrestricted set of cellular substrates. |
| Direct canonical substrates | **Ste7** is the canonical MAPKK substrate in mating and filamentous/invasive-growth signaling; **Pbs2** is the canonical MAPKK substrate in the Sho1 branch of the HOG pathway. | Ste7 activation has biochemical and genetic support; Ste11-to-Pbs2 signaling is supported by pathway reconstitution, epistasis, interaction, and recruitment studies. Ste50 can also be phosphorylated by Ste11, but it is principally an adaptor rather than a canonical MAPKK output. Reported Cmk2 and Ptk2 connections are putative/context-dependent, and Mpk1/Slt2 activation is indirect; these should not be annotated as equally established direct substrates. |
| Upstream activation | Ste11 is activated principally by the Cdc42-associated PAK-family kinase **Ste20** after assembly at the plasma membrane; the adaptor **Ste50** promotes recruitment and relieves N-terminal autoinhibition. | Cdc42 mainly organizes and activates Ste20 and recruits Ste50-containing complexes; it is not itself a kinase. Ste50–Ste11 association uses their SAM-containing regions, while pathway-specific membrane anchors and scaffolds bring the module to the appropriate cortical site. |
| Mating pathway | Transmits pheromone-receptor signaling through **Ste20 → Ste11 → Ste7 → Fus3/Kss1**, producing mating-gene expression, cell-cycle arrest, polarized growth, and cell fusion. | The **Ste5** scaffold assembles Ste11, Ste7, and Fus3 and recruits the module to Gβγ-associated plasma-membrane sites. Ste5-dependent organization favors the mating output and helps prevent inappropriate signaling through other Ste11-dependent pathways. |
| Filamentous/invasive growth pathway | Functions in the filamentous-growth MAPK cascade, principally **Cdc42/Ste20 → Ste11 → Ste7 → Kss1**, which promotes invasive growth in haploids and pseudohyphal development in appropriate diploids. | Surface and nutrient inputs converge through proteins including Msb2, Sho1, Cdc42, Ste50, and Ste20. Ste50’s RA domain is important for delivering the Ste11 complex to the plasma membrane. Phenotypic strength is strain- and condition-dependent, so S288c results should not automatically be generalized to strongly filamentous backgrounds. |
| Sho1–HOG pathway | Serves as the MAP3K of the **Sho1 branch**: osmosensing complexes activate **Ste20/Cla4 → Ste11 → Pbs2 → Hog1**, enabling adaptation to hyperosmotic stress. | This role is distinct from the parallel Sln1 branch, which uses Ssk2/Ssk22 rather than Ste11. Sho1–Pbs2 and Opy2–Ste50 assemblies position Pbs2 and Ste11 near one another at the plasma membrane, promoting selective Pbs2 phosphorylation. |
| Localization | Ste11 is primarily a soluble intracellular signaling protein that is **transiently recruited to cortical/plasma-membrane complexes** where pathway activation occurs; constitutive residence in an organelle is not its defining localization. | In mating, membrane-localized Ste5 positions Ste11 near Ste20. In filamentous growth and Sho1–HOG signaling, Ste50 links Ste11 to Cdc42 or the Opy2-containing membrane complex, while Pbs2 and Sho1 organize the HOG module. Localization is dynamic and pathway dependent. |
| Domain/regulatory architecture | The N terminus is a regulatory and interaction region containing a **SAM domain**; the C terminus contains the conserved **serine/threonine protein-kinase domain and ATP-binding site**. | The SAM-containing region binds Ste50. A neighboring N-terminal segment binds Ste5 and participates in inhibitory contacts with the catalytic region. Deletion or activating mutations in the regulatory region can generate constitutive Ste11 activity. The supplied Ras-binding-like annotation should not be interpreted as proving that Ste11 itself is a conventional Ras effector; the best-established RA-domain recruitment function belongs to Ste50. |
| Signaling specificity/crosstalk | One shared MAP3K is routed into different outputs by stimulus-specific complexes: **Ste5–Ste7** for mating, **Ste7/Kss1 assemblies** for filamentation, and **Sho1/Pbs2** for HOG. Hog1-dependent feedback and phosphatases further suppress inappropriate cross-activation. | Loss of Hog1 or Pbs2 permits osmotic stress to activate mating-pathway outputs through Sho1, Ste20, Ste50, Ste11, and Ste7. Ste11 has also been linked experimentally to FKS2 and cell-wall-integrity regulation, but Cmk2, Ptk2, and Mpk1/Slt2 effects reflect broader crosstalk and should remain secondary annotations. |
| 2024 update | Concurrent salt and pheromone stimulation revealed that the two HOG branches are not functionally interchangeable: Sho1 sustains Hog1 activation and can promote **Kss1-dependent cross-pathway transcription**, whereas the Sln1 branch contributes strongly to Hog1 activation amplitude. | The study identified **108 Kss1-responsive genes** and strong salt-dependent induction of mating genes under Sho1-dominant conditions, including FUS1, PRM1, and BAR1. It treated Ste11 as shared upstream pathway architecture but **did not directly perturb Ste11**, so its findings refine the context of Ste11 signaling rather than its catalytic mechanism. |
| Applications/limitations | Ste11-dependent cascades are widely used as model systems for MAPK scaffolding, signal insulation, concurrent-stimulus processing, quantitative signaling, and engineered yeast biosensors; homologous modules also inform fungal stress and virulence research. | Ste11 itself has no established clinical application. Results from engineered constitutively active alleles, other yeast strains, or Ste11 homologs in pathogenic fungi cannot be assigned directly to P23561 without validation. Direct kinetic constants, high-resolution full-length structure, and a comprehensive physiological substrate map remain limited. |


*Table: Concise evidence-based annotation of *S. cerevisiae* S288c Ste11, distinguishing its canonical MAPKK substrates and pathway-specific recruitment from secondary crosstalk. It also summarizes the qualified relevance of recent 2024 signaling results.*
