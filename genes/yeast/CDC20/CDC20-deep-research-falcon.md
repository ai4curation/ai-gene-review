# Functional annotation report: *Saccharomyces cerevisiae* CDC20 (P26309/YGL116W)

## Executive conclusion

The requested protein is correctly identified. **CDC20/YGL116W from *Saccharomyces cerevisiae* S288c (UniProt P26309)** encodes the conserved **Cdc20/Fizzy-family WD-repeat activator of the anaphase-promoting complex/cyclosome (APC/C)**. It is not an enzyme with a small-molecule reaction and should not be annotated as a quinoprotein dehydrogenase despite the generic “quinoprotein_ADH-like” structural-superfamily assignment. Its primary function is to act as a **noncatalytic APC/C coactivator and substrate receptor**, selecting cell-cycle proteins for polyubiquitylation and proteasomal destruction. The best-established physiological outputs are degradation of **Pds1/securin** and the S-phase cyclin **Clb5**, thereby initiating sister-chromatid separation and helping drive mitotic exit. Cdc20 is also the principal downstream target of the spindle-assembly checkpoint (SAC). (melloy2004changesinthe pages 15-16, wang2017transcriptionalandposttranscriptional pages 1-6, ostapenko2012identificationofanaphase pages 1-2)

No conflicting same-symbol protein was used in this report. Human CDC20, fission-yeast Slp1, and plant CDC20 findings are mentioned only where they illuminate a conserved mechanism and are not treated as direct evidence for P26309.

## 1. Identity, family, and molecular architecture

The supplied identity—CDC20, ordered locus **YGL116W**, accession **P26309**, in *S. cerevisiae* S288c—is fully consistent with the budding-yeast literature. Yeast Cdc20 was originally recognized as a β-transducin-like, WD-repeat protein and was subsequently placed in the Cdc20/Fizzy family of APC/C regulators. Direct yeast work and later annotation identify three mechanistically important architectural elements:

1. A large **C-terminal WD40-repeat domain**, expected to form a β-propeller and provide protein-interaction surfaces for APC/C substrates and checkpoint factors.
2. An **N-terminal C-box**, important for productive association with and activation of APC/C.
3. A C-terminal **Ile–Arg (IR) motif**, contributing to APC/C association and regulated turnover. (cosma2022biochemicalbiophysicaland pages 6-7, wang2017transcriptionalandposttranscriptional pages 1-6, wang2017transcriptionalandposttranscriptional pages 15-19)

The InterPro terms “Cdc20/Fizzy,” “WD40_CDC20-Fz,” “WD40 repeat,” and “WD40/YVTN-repeat-like” therefore fit the experimentally established protein family. The “quinoprotein_ADH-like superfamily” label reflects a broad β-propeller fold relationship; there is no evidence that Cdc20 binds PQQ or catalyzes alcohol oxidation.

## 2. Primary biochemical function

### 2.1 APC/C coactivator and substrate receptor

APC/C is a multisubunit cullin–RING E3 ubiquitin ligase. Cdc20 is a transient regulatory subunit rather than a stable stoichiometric core component. Cell-cycle-dependent association of Cdc20 with APC/C correlates with APC/C activation; addition of Cdc20 to APC/C stimulates cyclin ubiquitin-ligase activity in vitro, and Cdc20 overproduction can induce APC/C-dependent proteolysis in yeast. Thus, Cdc20 both helps activate the catalytic core and recruits selected substrates. (melloy2004changesinthe pages 16-16, jaspersen2000themitoticexit pages 29-32)

Cdc20 itself does **not** catalyze ubiquitin transfer. APC/C’s catalytic module and associated E2 enzymes perform ubiquitin-chain assembly; Cdc20 confers timing and specificity. This distinction is central to functional annotation: Cdc20 is best classified as an **adapter/coactivator/substrate receptor**.

### 2.2 Recognition specificity

The WD40 domain recognizes short APC/C degrons, particularly **destruction boxes (D-boxes)** and **KEN boxes**. Mutation of these motifs can prevent recognition by Cdc20/Cdh1 and stabilize substrates. Degrons are necessary recognition signals but are not, by themselves, complete predictors of degradation: phosphorylation, APC/C conformation, co-receptor engagement, accessibility, localization, and checkpoint state also determine when a protein is destroyed. (wang2017transcriptionalandposttranscriptional pages 1-6, ostapenko2012identificationofanaphase pages 1-2)

Early yeast genetics established distinct but overlapping substrate preferences for Cdc20 and Cdh1/Hct1. Cdc20 is required for Pds1 destruction, whereas Cdh1 is particularly important for later destruction of substrates such as Clb2 and Ase1. Subsequent work showed that Cdc20-dependent destruction of **both Pds1 and Clb5** accounts for its core role in anaphase and mitotic exit. Claims that every mitotic cyclin is equivalently dependent on Cdc20 would therefore be an oversimplification. (melloy2004changesinthe pages 16-16, melloy2004changesinthe pages 15-16, ostapenko2012identificationofanaphase pages 1-2)

## 3. Mechanism in the cell cycle

### 3.1 Pds1/securin destruction and anaphase onset

Pds1 inhibits the separase Esp1. Once all chromosomes are appropriately attached to the spindle, APC/C–Cdc20 ubiquitylates Pds1, leading to proteasomal destruction. Released Esp1 cleaves the cohesin subunit that holds sister chromatids together, making sister-chromatid segregation effectively irreversible. Failure to activate Cdc20 therefore causes a metaphase-like arrest with stabilized securin and persistent cohesion. Cdc20 is essential in budding yeast, and Pds1 degradation is one of its essential outputs. (melloy2004changesinthe pages 16-16, ostapenko2012identificationofanaphase pages 1-2)

### 3.2 Clb5 degradation and mitotic exit

APC/C–Cdc20 also destroys the B-type/S-phase cyclin Clb5. This lowers cyclin-dependent kinase activity and facilitates later mitotic-exit events, including full APC/C–Cdh1 activation. The mechanistic division of labor is therefore sequential: APC/C–Cdc20 triggers anaphase and begins reducing cyclin-CDK activity; mitotic-exit pathways and APC/C–Cdh1 then complete broader substrate clearance and establish G1. (melloy2004changesinthe pages 15-16, ostapenko2012identificationofanaphase pages 1-2)

### 3.3 Ordered substrate destruction

The pathway is not a simultaneous bulk-degradation event. Budding-yeast APC/C–Cdc20 substrates are destroyed in an ordered sequence shaped by degron affinity, phosphorylation, substrate competition, and SAC status. This temporal ordering explains how cells can lower selected cyclin activities while preserving other mitotic functions long enough to complete chromosome segregation.

## 4. Spindle-assembly checkpoint pathway

Cdc20 is both the activator that initiates anaphase and the checkpoint’s principal inhibited target. Unattached or improperly attached kinetochores activate an Mps1-dependent signaling network that promotes assembly of Cdc20 with **Mad2, Mad3, and Bub3** into inhibitory checkpoint complexes. The resulting mitotic checkpoint complex sequesters and inhibits Cdc20 and suppresses APC/C substrate recognition, thereby stabilizing Pds1 until chromosome attachment is satisfactory. (wang2017transcriptionalandposttranscriptional pages 1-6, wang2017transcriptionalandposttranscriptional pages 15-19)

This creates a switch with two alternative Cdc20 states:

- **Checkpoint satisfied:** Cdc20 engages APC/C productively, Pds1 and Clb5 are ubiquitylated, and anaphase/mitotic exit begin.
- **Checkpoint active:** Cdc20 is incorporated into Mad2–Mad3–Bub3-containing inhibitory complexes, APC/C–Cdc20 activity is restrained, and Pds1 remains stable.

Current expert understanding emphasizes that kinetochores act catalytically to generate a diffusible inhibitory signal rather than merely retaining all checkpoint components at chromosomes. However, high-resolution atomic descriptions of MCC–APC/C inhibition come substantially from metazoan and other model systems; the conserved framework is strongly applicable, but residue-level assignments should not be transferred to yeast P26309 without direct testing. (sun2024negativeregulationof pages 29-30, cosma2022biochemicalbiophysicaland pages 8-10)

## 5. Localization

Cdc20 accumulates in the **nucleus**, placing it with chromosomes, kinetochores, spindle-associated structures, APC/C, and major substrates. Nuclear accumulation was directly observed in budding yeast. APC/C localization is itself dynamic and responds to microtubule depolymerization and checkpoint activation, supporting spatial regulation of the Cdc20–APC/C system. The available evidence does not justify describing Cdc20 as exclusively nuclear; a more defensible annotation is **nuclear-enriched, cell-cycle-regulated, and associated transiently with APC/C/checkpoint assemblies**. (melloy2004changesinthe pages 16-16, melloy2004changesinthe pages 15-16)

Accordingly, the principal site of action is the **nuclear mitotic compartment**, particularly the APC/C–kinetochore–spindle regulatory axis, rather than the plasma membrane, secretory pathway, or extracellular space.

## 6. Regulation of CDC20 abundance and activity

CDC20 is regulated at several levels:

- **Cell-cycle transcription:** abundance oscillates, with expression coupled to mitotic progression.
- **Complex assembly:** C-box and IR-motif interactions regulate APC/C engagement.
- **Checkpoint sequestration:** Mad2/Mad3/Bub3 shift Cdc20 into an inhibitory state.
- **Proteolysis:** Cdc20 is itself an APC/C substrate and undergoes APC-dependent turnover during normal cycling and checkpoint arrest.
- **Feedback:** APC/C-associated Cdc20 can be ubiquitylated in cis; during checkpoint arrest, increased degradation is counterbalanced in part by increased CDC20 transcription. (wang2017transcriptionalandposttranscriptional pages 1-6, wang2017transcriptionalandposttranscriptional pages 15-19)

The evidence supports regulated instability but does not provide a single universally applicable half-life or copy number. Such values depend strongly on cell-cycle stage, growth conditions, tagging, checkpoint activation, and strain background; consequently, no unsupported quantitative estimate is assigned here.

## 7. Mitosis, meiosis, and broader pathways

### Mitosis

The highest-confidence annotation is the metaphase–anaphase transition and early mitotic exit: Cdc20 activates APC/C toward Pds1 and Clb5, couples chromosome attachment to sister separation, and initiates reduction of cyclin-CDK activity. Its essentiality follows from this tightly defined role rather than from a general structural function in microtubules. (ostapenko2012identificationofanaphase pages 1-2, jaspersen2000themitoticexit pages 29-32)

### Meiosis

Cdc20 also operates during the meiotic nuclear divisions, where APC/C-dependent securin and cyclin destruction must occur twice while preserving the specialized order of homolog and sister-chromatid segregation. In budding yeast, Cdc20’s meiotic activity is coordinated with **Ama1**, a meiosis-specific APC/C activator. Cdc20 should therefore be annotated as participating in meiotic chromosome segregation, but not as the sole meiotic APC/C activator. The complete meiosis-specific substrate repertoire is less precisely resolved than its mitotic Pds1/Clb5 function. (melloy2004changesinthe pages 16-16)

### Broader checkpoint connections

Genetic studies have linked Cdc20-dependent APC/C activity to DNA-damage responses, spindle defects, nutrient signaling, and cellular stress. These effects are best interpreted as modulation of the core APC/C/SAC transition unless a direct Cdc20–substrate relationship has been established. They should not displace the primary annotation with broad pleiotropic labels.

## 8. Recent developments, 2023–2024

Recent work has refined the conserved structural and systems-level picture rather than overturning the yeast annotation. Modern studies emphasize that Cdc20 is a two-sided signaling hub: it activates APC/C when attachment is complete but is converted into the inhibitory checkpoint effector when attachment is defective. Recent structural work in other eukaryotes further resolves APC/C regulation and coactivator-dependent substrate recognition, while 2023 reviews synthesize kinetochore-catalyzed MCC formation and checkpoint dynamics. These advances support the yeast model but remain **comparative evidence**, not direct demonstrations of every molecular contact in P26309. (sun2024negativeregulationof pages 29-30, sun2024negativeregulationof pages 19-21, cosma2022biochemicalbiophysicaland pages 8-10)

A 2024 fission-yeast study identified stress-responsive MAPK phosphorylation and degradation of Slp1/Cdc20. This is an important indication that environmental signaling can regulate Cdc20-family abundance, but it concerns *Schizosaccharomyces pombe*, not *S. cerevisiae*, and should not be incorporated as a verified P26309 mechanism without direct budding-yeast evidence. (sun2024negativeregulationof pages 19-21)

The relative scarcity of 2023–2024 papers specifically re-annotating S288c Cdc20 reflects a mature mechanistic consensus: the decisive yeast discoveries—APC/C coactivation, substrate specificity, securin/cyclin destruction, and SAC inhibition—were established in foundational primary studies and continue to underpin current models.

## 9. Applications and real-world implementation

Cdc20’s principal applications are in experimental and systems biology rather than biotechnology as an isolated reagent or clinical target:

1. **Cell-cycle synchronization and conditional arrest:** temperature-sensitive alleles or regulated CDC20 expression permit reversible metaphase arrest.
2. **APC/C substrate discovery:** Cdc20 dependence, degron mutation, protein-stability assays, and APC/C-binding experiments distinguish direct substrates from secondary effects.
3. **Checkpoint analysis:** Cdc20 provides the central readout for testing kinetochore attachment, Mad2/Mad3/Bub3 function, checkpoint establishment, maintenance, and silencing.
4. **Synthetic and quantitative biology:** the Cdc20–Pds1–Esp1 and Cdc20–Clb5 modules are used to model irreversible transitions, feedback, robustness, and ordered proteolysis.
5. **Comparative disease biology:** yeast establishes conserved APC/C/SAC principles relevant to chromosome-instability research, but human cancer claims concern human CDC20 and should not be presented as direct applications of yeast P26309. (wang2017transcriptionalandposttranscriptional pages 1-6, ostapenko2012identificationofanaphase pages 1-2, jaspersen2000themitoticexit pages 29-32)

## 10. Evidence-weighted annotation

**Recommended primary annotation:**

> Cdc20 is an essential, nuclear-enriched WD40/Fizzy-family coactivator and substrate receptor of the anaphase-promoting complex/cyclosome. APC/C–Cdc20 recognizes degron-containing cell-cycle substrates, especially Pds1/securin and Clb5, promoting their ubiquitin-dependent proteasomal destruction to trigger sister-chromatid separation and initiate mitotic exit. Unattached or improperly attached kinetochores inhibit this activity by incorporating Cdc20 into Mad2–Mad3–Bub3-containing spindle-checkpoint complexes. Cdc20 abundance is regulated by cell-cycle transcription and APC/C-dependent turnover.

**Confidence:** high for identity, family, APC/C coactivation, Pds1/Clb5 degradation, SAC regulation, and essential mitotic function; moderate for precise localization fractions, the complete substrate catalogue, and detailed meiosis-specific timing.
