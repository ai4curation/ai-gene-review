| Identity and domain architecture | **CDC20/YGL116W (UniProt P26309)** encodes the budding-yeast Cdc20/Fizzy-family protein. It is an essential WD-repeat protein with a C-terminal WD40 substrate-recognition domain, an N-terminal C-box important for APC/C association and activation, and a C-terminal Ile–Arg motif (pqac-00000008, pqac-00000009) | Direct *S. cerevisiae* evidence, consistent with conserved comparative structural evidence (pqac-00000005) | **High.** Literature identity, organism, protein family, and supplied UniProt annotation agree. WD40 superfamily labels do not imply quinoprotein-alcohol-dehydrogenase enzymatic activity. |
| Primary molecular function | Cdc20 is a **nonenzymatic, limiting coactivator and substrate receptor** for the APC/C E3 ubiquitin ligase. APC/C-bound Cdc20 stimulates ubiquitination of selected cell-cycle regulators; it does not itself catalyze a small-molecule reaction (pqac-00000001, pqac-00000008, pqac-00000011) | Direct yeast genetics and biochemical activation evidence | **High.** Association with APC/C correlates with activity, addition of Cdc20 stimulates cyclin ubiquitin-ligase activity, and Cdc20 overproduction drives APC/C-dependent proteolysis. |
| Substrate specificity | The WD40 domain recognizes APC/C degrons, especially D-box and KEN-box motifs. Best-established budding-yeast physiological targets include **Pds1/securin and Clb5**; Clb2 and other targets can be regulated in context-dependent or partly Cdh1-overlapping ways (pqac-00000003, pqac-00000008, pqac-00000009) | Direct yeast genetics, degradation assays, and degron-binding studies; structural interpretation partly conserved | **High** for Pds1 and Clb5; **moderate** for the full substrate catalogue. Degrons are necessary recognition elements but cellular timing also depends on phosphorylation, APC/C state, localization, and checkpoint status. |
| Spindle-assembly checkpoint regulation | Unattached or incorrectly attached kinetochores channel Cdc20 into inhibitory complexes with **Mad2, Mad3, and Bub3**. This mitotic checkpoint complex suppresses APC/C–Cdc20 and prevents premature Pds1 destruction and anaphase (pqac-00000008, pqac-00000010) | Direct budding-yeast biochemical/genetic evidence; current structural framework is conserved comparative evidence (pqac-00000002, pqac-00000007) | **High** for checkpoint inhibition and complex membership. Atomic details of MCC–APC/C inhibition derive substantially from non-*S. cerevisiae* systems and should not automatically be assigned residue-for-residue to P26309. |
| Cellular localization | Cdc20 accumulates predominantly in the **nucleus**, the compartment containing chromosomes, kinetochores, and much of the relevant APC/C substrate-processing machinery; localization and abundance vary with cell-cycle state (pqac-00000003) | Direct yeast imaging/localization evidence | **Moderate to high.** Nuclear accumulation is supported, but the retrieved evidence does not justify a precise compartmental fraction or a definitive claim of exclusive localization. APC/C localization itself is dynamic during checkpoint activation (pqac-00000001). |
| Turnover and expression | Cdc20 abundance oscillates during the cell cycle. It is itself an APC/C substrate, undergoing APC-dependent degradation during normal cycling and enhanced turnover during checkpoint arrest; a major route is intramolecular or **cis autoubiquitination**. Checkpoint inhibition also induces compensatory CDC20 transcription (pqac-00000008, pqac-00000010) | Direct budding-yeast transcriptional, mutational, and degradation evidence | **High** for regulated instability and cis turnover; **moderate** for their quantitative contribution under every growth condition. No unsupported half-life or copy-number estimate is assigned. |
| Mitosis | APC/C–Cdc20 initiates the metaphase-to-anaphase transition by destroying Pds1, thereby permitting separase-dependent sister-chromatid separation, and contributes to mitotic exit by removing Clb5 and lowering cyclin-CDK activity (pqac-00000001, pqac-00000003, pqac-00000009) | Direct budding-yeast genetics and substrate-degradation evidence | **High.** CDC20 is essential, and combined control of securin and B-type cyclins explains its core viability requirement better than broad pleiotropic phenotypes. |
| Meiosis | Cdc20 also activates APC/C during the meiotic nuclear divisions, including securin/cyclin turnover and meiosis-II events; its activity is coordinated with the meiosis-specific APC/C activator Ama1 rather than replacing Ama1 (pqac-00000001) | Direct yeast evidence supplemented by yeast-focused meiotic reviews | **Moderate.** The mitotic mechanism is better resolved than the complete meiosis-specific substrate repertoire and regulatory timing. |
| Research applications | *S. cerevisiae* Cdc20 is used experimentally to dissect APC/C substrate selection, degron function, spindle-checkpoint establishment and silencing, ordered substrate destruction, protein-turnover feedback, and mathematical models of irreversible cell-cycle transitions (pqac-00000008, pqac-00000009, pqac-00000011) | Direct experimental-model and mechanistic application | **High** as a basic-research model. These are laboratory and systems-biology implementations, not direct clinical applications of yeast Cdc20. |
