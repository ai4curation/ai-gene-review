# Functional annotation report: *Saccharomyces cerevisiae* **MPS3** (UniProt P47069)

## Executive conclusion

The requested target is correctly identified as **MPS3/YJL019W (NEP98)** from *Saccharomyces cerevisiae* S288c—not the similarly named Mps1 kinase or Mps2 membrane protein. Mps3 is an essential, 682-residue, single-pass **inner nuclear membrane (INM) SUN-domain protein** enriched at the nuclear side of the spindle pole body (SPB) half-bridge. It is not an enzyme or transporter and therefore has no catalytic reaction or transported substrate. Its primary function is structural: it organizes membrane-spanning protein assemblies that couple the SPB or chromosome ends to the nuclear envelope and, in meiosis, to the cytoskeleton. Its best-supported roles are SPB duplication and nuclear-envelope insertion, telomere anchoring, meiotic chromosome movement, and maintenance of nuclear-envelope organization. (jaspersen2006thesad1unc84homology pages 1-2, conrad2007mps3mediatesmeiotic pages 1-1, jaspersen2002mps3pisa pages 1-2)

## 1. Identity and domain verification

The literature target matches the supplied UniProt record. The original characterization named **MPS3** for “monopolar spindle,” identified the product as an essential integral membrane component of the budding-yeast SPB half-bridge, and showed that loss of function produces monopolar spindles. Later sequence analysis established Mps3 as the budding-yeast member of the Sad1–UNC-84, or **SUN**, protein family. The historical alias **NEP98** is also reported in the Mps3 literature. (conrad2007mps3mediatesmeiotic pages 1-1, jaspersen2006thesad1unc84homology pages 2-3, jaspersen2002mps3pisa pages 1-2)

The accepted topology is a nucleoplasmic N terminus, one transmembrane segment, and a C-terminal region exposed to the nuclear-envelope lumen/perinuclear space. Published maps place the transmembrane segment approximately at residues 154–181 and the SUN region approximately at residues 427–616, although boundaries vary with the prediction method. Coiled-coil/helical segments occur in the central and C-terminal portions. This architecture agrees with the supplied InterPro/Pfam annotations **SUN_dom/SUN1-5** and **MPS3_helical**: the latter is consistent with the extended helical/coiled-coil scaffold rather than a catalytic domain. (jaspersen2006thesad1unc84homology pages 1-2, ponce2020sunnywaysthe pages 1-2, jaspersen2006thesad1unc84homology pages 2-3)

**Ambiguity control:** Mps3 must not be confused with **Mps1**, a protein kinase involved in the spindle checkpoint, or **Mps2**, an SPB/nuclear-envelope membrane protein that physically partners with Mps3. Literature centered on those proteins was not used as evidence for Mps3 identity or enzymatic activity. (ponce2020sunnywaysthe pages 1-2, jaspersen2006thesad1unc84homology pages 1-2)

## 2. Primary molecular function and cellular location

Mps3 is principally a **membrane-embedded scaffold and force-transmission component**. It is concentrated at the SPB half-bridge and SPB nuclear-envelope insertion site, with a lower-abundance pool distributed around the INM. Its nucleoplasmic N terminus can bind chromosome- and SPB-associated factors, whereas its luminal SUN domain engages membrane partners such as Mps2. This topology allows one polypeptide to couple nuclear structures to assemblies within or across the nuclear envelope. (ponce2020sunnywaysthe pages 1-2, jaspersen2002mps3pisa pages 6-7, jaspersen2006thesad1unc84homology pages 1-2)

Localization is supported quantitatively by immuno-electron microscopy: Mps3 was detected at 37 of 40 examined SPBs; 96% of SPB-associated gold particles lay at the half-bridge or half-bridge/central-plaque interface, usually on the nuclear side. Approximately 13% of particles occurred in nuclear envelope away from the SPB. Membrane fractionation independently supported integral-membrane status. (jaspersen2002mps3pisa pages 6-7)

## 3. SPB duplication and insertion into the nuclear envelope

### 3.1 Early half-bridge assembly

The SPB is the budding-yeast microtubule-organizing center and remains embedded in the nuclear envelope during closed mitosis. Mps3 acts at the first stages of SPB duplication, when the half-bridge elongates and a satellite/duplication plaque forms. Temperature-sensitive *mps3-1* cells arrest with a single unduplicated SPB and monopolar spindle, often lacking a normal associated half-bridge. Execution-point experiments placed Mps3 activity in G1 before satellite deposition. (jaspersen2002mps3pisa pages 1-2, jaspersen2002mps3pisa pages 5-6)

Mps3 works with the centrin Cdc31 and Kar1. Genetic interaction, in-vitro binding, and Cdc31-localization experiments support a role for Mps3 in recruiting or retaining Cdc31 at the half-bridge, although “recruitment” remains a mechanistic interpretation rather than a fully reconstructed reaction. At restrictive temperature, SPB-associated Cdc31 remained detectable in 54% of *mps3-1* cells and 17% of *kar1-17* cells, but in none of the double-mutant cells, supporting partially redundant Cdc31-binding inputs from Mps3 and Kar1. (jaspersen2002mps3pisa pages 1-2, jaspersen2002mps3pisa pages 8-9)

### 3.2 Mps2 binding, SPIN, and membrane insertion

Mps3’s luminal SUN domain binds the C terminus of Mps2. Mutations in either interface cause abnormal half-bridges, SPB-duplication defects, and karyogamy defects, establishing a biochemical connection between the half-bridge and SPB core/nuclear-envelope anchor. (jaspersen2006thesad1unc84homology pages 1-2)

Structured-illumination microscopy subsequently showed that Mps3 forms a ring-like distribution around the SPB fenestra. Together with Mps2 and other insertion factors, it constitutes part of the **spindle-pole-body insertion network (SPIN)**. Mps3–Mps2 forms a noncanonical LINC-like assembly with both luminal and extraluminal connections and controls the distribution of Bbp1, thereby linking the SPB core to membrane remodeling at the insertion site. This is the current structural explanation for why Mps3 mutants can fail both SPB assembly and insertion into the nuclear envelope. Exact Mps3 stoichiometry and ring dimensions remain unresolved. (chen2019yeastcentrosomecomponents pages 1-3)

A dominant *MPS3-G186K* allele disrupts multiple duplication stages, causes insertion defects and severe INM proliferation, and genetically interacts with sterol-biosynthetic pathways. These observations connect SPB insertion to the physical and lipid environment of the nuclear envelope, although they do not establish Mps3 as a lipid enzyme. (friederichs2011thesunprotein pages 2-4, friederichs2011thesunprotein pages 1-2)

## 4. Chromosome and telomere organization

### 4.1 Mitotic telomere anchoring

Outside the SPB, the nucleoplasmic N-terminal acidic region of Mps3 acts as a receptor for peripheral telomere positioning. Deletion analysis showed that this region is dispensable for viability but required for S-phase telomere tethering and for silencing telomeric reporter constructs. Mps3 binds or recruits Sir4 at the nuclear periphery, connecting it to a telomeric silencing pathway. Thus, telomere anchoring is a separable function of the nonessential N-terminal module, whereas the essential SUN/SPB function resides elsewhere in the protein. (bupp2007telomereanchoringat pages 1-2)

A second route links Mps3 to Yku80 and telomerase-associated telomere anchoring. Dominant-interfering Mps3-N9 did not substantially change telomeric repeat length or telomerase elongation but displaced telomeres and increased subtelomeric recombination in sensitized *tel1* cells. Approximately 60% of *tel1* cells carrying empty vector were unbudded/G1, compared with only about 20% expressing Mps3-N9; the latter accumulated large-budded, poorly proliferating cells and amplified subtelomeric Y′ repeats. These data argue that peripheral anchoring contributes to chromosome-end stability independently of simply changing telomere length. (schober2009yeasttelomeraseand pages 6-7)

### 4.2 Meiotic telomere attachment and chromosome motion

In meiotic prophase, Mps3 is redeployed into multiple nuclear-envelope foci. Its N-terminal residues 2–64 bind the meiosis-specific telomere factor Ndj1; deleting this segment produces an *ndj1*-like separation-of-function phenotype with delayed prophase, impaired homolog pairing, defective ascus formation, and reduced spore viability. Ndj1 and Mps3 are codependent for stable telomere association, establishing Mps3 as the INM receptor for meiotic chromosome ends and bouquet formation. (conrad2007mps3mediatesmeiotic pages 1-1)

The updated model is a heterotrimeric telomere-associated LINC complex: **Ndj1–telomere → Mps3(INM) → Mps2/Csm4(outer nuclear membrane) → Myo2/actin cytoskeleton**. Mps2 binds both Mps3 and Csm4; direct Csm4–Mps3 binding was not detected, indicating that Mps2 is the bridge. Csm4 purification recovered Mps2 with 99 peptide spectra and 30% sequence coverage, and reciprocal immunoprecipitation supported both Mps2–Csm4 and Mps2–Mps3 associations. (fan2020mps2linkscsm4 pages 1-3, lee2020extranuclearstructuralcomponents pages 1-3, fan2020mps2linkscsm4 pages 3-3)

This t-LINC complex transmits cytoskeletal force to chromosome ends, driving rapid telomere-led motion, bouquet organization, homolog pairing, and normal recombination. Meiotic Mps2 depletion illustrates the severity of disrupting the assembly: more than 60% of cells entered meiosis I, but fewer than 5% developed three or four SPB foci, whereas more than 80% of wild-type cells completed meiosis. Ectopic expression experiments in vegetative cells reconstituted an Mps3/Mps2/Csm4-dependent telomere-tethering activity, providing a useful synthetic test of complex sufficiency. (fan2020mps2linkscsm4 pages 5-7, fan2020mps2linkscsm4 pages 3-3)

Expert reviews interpret this machinery as a variant, noncanonical LINC system rather than a simple canonical SUN–KASH pair. Its heterotrimeric organization explains how Csm4, which has only a very short luminal tail, can participate without directly engaging Mps3. (fan2020mps2linkscsm4 pages 1-3, lee2020extranuclearstructuralcomponents pages 1-3)

## 5. Regulation and nuclear-envelope homeostasis

### Phosphorylation and irreversible cleavage

Mps3 is phosphorylated at **Ser70** in meiosis. Mass spectrometry detected four S70-containing phosphopeptide spectra, and a phosphospecific antibody signal was removed by phosphatase. Phosphorylation peaked approximately six hours after meiotic induction, near the prophase-I/metaphase-I transition and SPB separation. (li2017cleavageofthe pages 6-7)

S70 phosphorylation promotes N-terminal cleavage and SPB disjunction. The nonphosphorylatable S70A allele severely inhibited separation, whereas phosphomimetic S70D behaved approximately like wild type. A noncleavable allele also inhibited separation, while engineered TEV cleavage restored it, supporting causality. Conversely, deleting residues 1–93 caused premature separation in 95% of cells; over 66% underwent another SPB duplication/separation cycle in a sensitized background. As the sole allele, noncleavable Mps3 caused 35% of cells to abort meiosis without SPB separation or spores, and 46% of cells completing a second separation had unequal SPBs. Cleavage depends on proteasome activity, but the responsible protease and exact physiological cleavage chemistry remain unresolved. (li2017cleavageofthe pages 10-12, li2017cleavageofthe pages 6-7)

### Ubiquitin-dependent turnover

Mps3 abundance is also controlled through APC/C–Cdh1-linked INM proteolysis. Its nucleoplasmic N terminus contains candidate KEN- and D-box destruction motifs, while Ubc6/Ubc7, Cdc48, and nuclear proteasomes are required for efficient turnover. Mps3’s half-life is approximately 45 minutes in rich medium, about 150 minutes after Cdc48 inactivation or displacement of proteasomes from the nucleus, and more than 180 minutes after MG132 treatment; *ubc7Δ* raises it above 150 minutes. Stabilized Mps3 accumulates at the INM, causes nuclear-envelope expansion, and impairs nuclear morphogenesis and cell division. (ponce2020sunnywaysthe pages 1-2, koch2018theanaphasepromotingcomplex pages 1-5, koch2018theanaphasepromotingcomplex pages 5-9)

These results support an expert model in which Mps3 must be assembled and removed with cell-cycle precision: insufficient Mps3 prevents SPB duplication or chromosome attachment, whereas excess or uncleaved Mps3 creates persistent membrane/half-bridge assemblies that obstruct SPB disjunction and distort the nuclear envelope. (ponce2020sunnywaysthe pages 1-2, li2017cleavageofthe pages 10-12, koch2018theanaphasepromotingcomplex pages 5-9)

## 6. Recent developments and current applications

A focused search found **no major peer-reviewed 2023–2024 primary paper centered specifically on S288c Mps3/P47069**. Recent search results were predominantly theses, broader nuclear-envelope work, or papers about the distinct Mps1 kinase. Accordingly, the latest robust Mps3-specific mechanistic advances remain the 2020 heterotrimeric t-LINC study and 2020–2022 reviews integrating Mps3 with meiotic force transmission, lipid homeostasis, and INM protein quality control. This recency gap should not be filled by misattributing Mps1 or Mps2 findings to Mps3. (fan2020mps2linkscsm4 pages 1-3, lee2020extranuclearstructuralcomponents pages 1-3, ponce2020sunnywaysthe pages 1-2)

Mps3 currently has research rather than clinical applications:

1. **SPB/centrosome-biogenesis model:** conditional alleles and fluorescent Mps3 reporters dissect how a large microtubule-organizing center is assembled and inserted into an intact nuclear envelope.
2. **LINC mechanobiology:** the Ndj1–Mps3–Mps2–Csm4 system is a genetically tractable model for force transmission across the nuclear envelope and telomere-led chromosome motion.
3. **Nuclear-envelope quality control:** Mps3 turnover provides a defined endogenous substrate for APC/C-, Cdc48-, and proteasome-dependent INM degradation.
4. **Membrane–organelle coupling:** dominant alleles such as G186K are used to study how sterol/lipid state, membrane remodeling, and SPB insertion influence one another.
5. **Synthetic reconstitution:** ectopic Csm4/Ndj1 expression can reconstitute aspects of t-LINC-dependent telomere tethering in vegetative cells. (friederichs2011thesunprotein pages 2-4, chen2019yeastcentrosomecomponents pages 1-3, fan2020mps2linkscsm4 pages 5-7, koch2018theanaphasepromotingcomplex pages 5-9)

There is no evidence in the reviewed literature that Mps3 is an approved therapeutic target or that it has a direct industrial implementation. Its value is as a conserved SUN-protein model whose principles inform centrosome attachment, nuclear mechanotransduction, chromosome organization, and nuclear-envelope proteostasis.

## 7. Evidence gaps and final annotation

Several mechanistic questions remain open: the atomic structure and stoichiometry of full-length Mps3 assemblies; exact architecture of the SPB-associated ring; the identity of the physiological N-terminal protease; how S70 phosphorylation is coordinated with cleavage and APC/C-mediated turnover; and whether Mps3 affects lipids directly or mainly responds to—and organizes proteins within—the membrane environment. The broad term “lipid homeostasis” should therefore be interpreted as a genetically and morphologically supported systems role, not as evidence that Mps3 catalyzes lipid metabolism. (friederichs2011thesunprotein pages 2-4, friederichs2011thesunprotein pages 1-2, li2017cleavageofthe pages 10-12)

**Recommended functional annotation:** *Essential inner nuclear membrane SUN-domain scaffold of the spindle pole body half-bridge and insertion network; recruits/organizes centrin-associated duplication factors, binds Mps2 to connect the SPB with the nuclear envelope, anchors mitotic and meiotic telomeres, and forms a meiosis-specific Mps3–Mps2–Csm4 LINC complex that transmits actin-dependent forces for chromosome movement. Regulated by phosphorylation, N-terminal cleavage, ubiquitination, and proteasomal turnover.*
