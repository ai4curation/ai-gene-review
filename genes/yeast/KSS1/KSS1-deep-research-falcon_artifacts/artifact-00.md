| Identity | Kss1 is the *Saccharomyces cerevisiae* MAP kinase encoded by **KSS1**; the supplied UniProt mapping is **P14681 / YGR040W** in strain S288c. Literature places Kss1 downstream of Ste11 and Ste7 and distinguishes it from Fus3, Hog1, and Mpk1/Slt2. | Ste7 selectively associated with Kss1 and Fus3 but not Hog1, Mpk1/Slt2, or mammalian ERK2 (pqac-00000000). | The organism, pathway position, and MAPK/protein-kinase classification align with the supplied UniProt identity; no conflicting same-symbol protein was used. |
| Catalytic role | Activated Kss1 is a protein kinase whose positive signaling function depends on Ste7-mediated activation-loop phosphorylation and Kss1 catalytic activity. | Catalytically inactive or unactivatable Kss1 variants did not efficiently support filamentation; activated Kss1-dependent assays phosphorylated Ste12- and Dig1-related substrates (pqac-00000008, pqac-00000010). | Kss1 converts upstream MAPK-cascade activity into phosphorylation-dependent transcriptional responses, although exact physiological substrate-site assignments remain incomplete. |
| Ste7 docking and activation | Ste7 is both the upstream MAPK kinase and a high-affinity docking partner of Kss1; docking determinants lie outside the catalytic and phosphoacceptor regions. | Kss1–Ste7 binding had an approximate dissociation constant of **5 nM**. Ste7 phosphorylated Kss1 in complexes isolated from yeast, and docking-defective Ste7 variants substantially reduced signaling (pqac-00000000). | High-affinity docking increases activation efficiency and pathway specificity rather than merely reflecting transient enzyme–substrate collision. |
| Inactive Kss1–Ste12 binding | Unphosphorylated Kss1 directly binds and represses Ste12; Ste7-dependent Kss1 phosphorylation switches off this noncatalytic repression while activating kinase function. | Approximately **25%** of input unphosphorylated Kss1 bound GST–Ste12, compared with only **1–2%** of phosphorylated Kss1—a greater than tenfold reduction. Both states retained roughly **25%** binding to GST–Ste7 (pqac-00000010). | Kss1 is a bifunctional signaling switch: inactive Kss1 restrains transcription, whereas phosphorylated Kss1 dissociates from Ste12 and promotes pathway output. |
| Dig1/Dig2 repression | The nuclear repressors Dig1 and Dig2 are required cofactors for Kss1-imposed inhibition of Ste12 at filamentation-response elements and also contribute at pheromone-response elements. | Loss-of-function and transcriptional assays showed that inactive-Kss1 repression requires Dig1 and Dig2; phosphorylation weakens Kss1–Ste12 association and may additionally regulate Dig proteins catalytically (pqac-00000008, pqac-00000011). | Kss1 regulates transcription through both physical assembly of a repressive Ste12 complex and phosphorylation-dependent relief or reinforcement of repression. |
| Filamentous and invasive growth | Kss1 is the principal MAPK output of the Ste20–Ste11–Ste7 filamentous-growth cascade, promoting Ste12–Tec1-dependent differentiation under appropriate nutrient and surface cues. | Kss1 catalytic activity was required for optimal haploid invasion and efficient diploid filamentation; constitutively active Ste7 preferentially activated Kss1 and induced invasion and filamentation-gene expression (pqac-00000008, pqac-00000012). | Kss1 coordinates transcription with elongated morphology, polarized budding, adhesion, and substrate invasion; both insufficient activation and persistent inactive-Kss1 repression can limit the response. |
| Mating and Fus3 redundancy | Kss1 can support pheromone-induced Ste12 transcription and partly substitute for Fus3, but Fus3 is normally the dominant mating MAPK and uniquely controls key outputs such as Far1-dependent G1 arrest. | Deleting either **KSS1** or **FUS3** alone does not cause sterility, whereas the double mutant fails to mate or induce pheromone-responsive genes (pqac-00000009, pqac-00000013). | Kss1 is not exclusively a filamentation kinase: it is a shared MAPK whose output is constrained by scaffolding, substrate selection, feedback, and Fus3. |
| Cellular localization | Kss1 acts at nuclear Ste12–Dig1/2–Tec1 transcriptional complexes after activation through an upstream cascade assembled partly at the cell cortex or scaffold, but direct quantitative evidence for Kss1’s own distribution or stimulus-induced translocation is limited. | Direct binding to Ste12 and dependence on nuclear Dig1/Dig2 establish a nuclear functional pool; available evidence broadly indicates that activated yeast MAPKs reach the nucleus but does not precisely quantify Kss1 localization (pqac-00000008, pqac-00000009, pqac-00000011). | Nuclear transcriptional regulation is secure, whereas claims that Kss1 is exclusively nuclear or undergoes a defined translocation magnitude should be avoided without Kss1-specific imaging data. |
| 2024 HOG–mating cross-talk | Recent work identifies Kss1 as a shared signal coordinator through which the Sho1 HOG branch can produce a transcriptional program distinct from canonical Hog1- or Fus3-driven responses. | After **350 mM KCl**, Kss1-selective induction included **FUS1 +2.56 log₂-fold** (adjusted *p*=1.48×10⁻²⁷), **PRM1 +2.18** (1.78×10⁻²¹), **BAR1 +1.14** (4.95×10⁻²⁰), and **KAR4 +1.88** (2.38×10⁻³⁶). Responses occurred in *ssk1Δ* cells but not *ssk1Δ kss1Δ* or Sho1-branch-deficient cells (pqac-00000007). | Kss1 integrates osmotic and pheromone-related inputs rather than functioning in a strictly insulated pathway; sexual-reproduction GO enrichment did not remain significant after correction (**14/108 genes; corrected p=0.07116; FDR 46%**) (pqac-00000007). |
