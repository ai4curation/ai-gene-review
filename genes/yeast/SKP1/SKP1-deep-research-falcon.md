# Functional annotation report: *Saccharomyces cerevisiae* **SKP1** (UniProt P52286)

## 1. Identity verification

The requested target is the budding-yeast gene **SKP1**, also called **CBF3D**, ordered locus **YDR328C**, from *Saccharomyces cerevisiae* S288c. The literature identifies budding-yeast Skp1p as an approximately 29–32-kDa protein that is both a subunit of the CBF3 kinetochore complex and the conserved adaptor in Skp1–Cullin–F-box (SCF) ubiquitin ligases. This agrees with the supplied UniProt description and with assignment to the Skp1/Skp1-like and BTB/POZ-related structural families. (kitagawa1999sgt1encodesan pages 1-2, willhoft2017thecrystalstructure pages 2-3, galan2001skp1pandthe pages 10-11)

No contradictory yeast identity was found. Literature on human **SKP1A**, *Caenorhabditis elegans* SKR-1/SKR-2, *Schizosaccharomyces pombe* Skp1, or parasite Skp1 homologues concerns different proteins and was excluded from the functional annotation. The conclusions below therefore concern P52286/YDR328C unless explicitly identified as broader mechanistic context.

## 2. Executive functional annotation

**Skp1 is an essential, nonenzymatic protein-interaction adaptor.** It does not catalyze ubiquitin transfer, bind centromeric DNA sequence-specifically, or itself recognize most SCF substrates. Its primary molecular activity is to bind proteins carrying an F-box and incorporate them into functional assemblies. In canonical SCF ligases, Skp1 connects an F-box substrate receptor to the Cdc53/Cul1–Rbx1/Hrt1 catalytic scaffold. In the CBF3 kinetochore, it binds the F-box protein Ctf13 and contacts Cep3, stabilizing the centromere-recognition complex. It also participates in an apparently non-SCF complex with Rcy1 during endosomal recycling. (kitagawa1999sgt1encodesan pages 1-2, willhoft2017thecrystalstructure pages 1-2, leber2018structuralbasisfor pages 5-6, galan2001skp1pandthe pages 1-1)

Consequently, Skp1 has no enzyme reaction or intrinsic substrate specificity analogous to an enzyme or transporter. **The relevant “substrate specificity” is delegated to its F-box partner:** Cdc4 recognizes phosphorylated cell-cycle substrates such as Sic1; Grr1 regulates G1 cyclins; Met30 controls Met4/Met32 and sulfur-stress signaling; Ctf13 incorporates Skp1 into CBF3 rather than a canonical degradation complex. (kitagawa1999sgt1encodesan pages 1-2, harris2024molecularinsightsintoa pages 24-27, lauinger2024cadmiumbindingby pages 1-2)

## 3. Canonical function in SCF ubiquitin ligases

### Architecture and mechanism

A canonical yeast SCF consists of Skp1, the cullin scaffold Cdc53, Rbx1/Hrt1, and an interchangeable F-box protein. Skp1 binds the F-box receptor and the cullin, positioning the receptor-bound substrate near the RING-associated E2 ubiquitin-conjugating enzyme. Thus, the assembled complex is an E3 ubiquitin ligase, but Skp1 itself is a **noncatalytic bridge** rather than the catalytic ubiquitin-transfer subunit. (harris2024molecularinsightsintoa pages 24-27, willhoft2017thecrystalstructure pages 2-3)

Structurally, Skp1 has an N-terminal BTB/POZ-like region associated with cullin and Sgt1 interactions and a C-terminal helical region that embraces F-box motifs. The roughly 35-residue C-terminal extension involved in F-box binding and the overall Skp1–F-box interaction mode are conserved among eukaryotes. (willhoft2017thecrystalstructure pages 2-3)

### Cell-cycle pathway

Direct yeast genetics established that Skp1 inactivation can arrest cells in both G1 and G2 and disrupt ubiquitin-dependent turnover of CDK regulators. SCF^Cdc4 ubiquitylates Sic1, an inhibitor of S-phase CDK activity; its degradation permits cell-cycle progression. SCF complexes using other receptors extend Skp1-dependent regulation to G1 cyclins and additional phosphorylated regulators. Temperature-sensitive *skp1* mutants exhibit chromosome-missegregation and cell-cycle-arrest phenotypes, while all four CBF3 components—including Skp1—are essential for viability. (kitagawa1999sgt1encodesan pages 1-2, harris2024molecularinsightsintoa pages 24-27)

The strongest interpretation is therefore not that Skp1 is a pathway-specific enzyme, but that it is a reusable molecular coupling module. Mutating or depleting it simultaneously compromises multiple F-box assemblies, explaining its essentiality and pleiotropic cell-cycle phenotypes.

## 4. CBF3 and kinetochore assembly

### Structural role

Budding yeast uses short “point centromeres.” CBF3 recognizes the CDEIII element and initiates assembly of the inner kinetochore, which in turn supports deposition and positioning of the centromeric Cse4 nucleosome. Skp1 forms a tight heterodimer with the F-box/leucine-rich-repeat protein Ctf13 and attaches that module to a Cep3 dimer. Cep3—not Skp1—provides the principal sequence-specific CDEIII recognition through its Gal4-type Zn₂Cys₆ DNA-binding domain. (leber2018structuralbasisfor pages 1-2, leber2018structuralbasisfor pages 5-6)

Cryo-EM and solution analysis support an intact CBF3 stoichiometry of **Ndc10₂:Cep3₂:Ctf13₁:Skp1₁**, with an estimated mass near **445 kDa**. The resolved Cep3₂–Ctf13–Skp1 core is approximately **220 kDa** and was reconstructed at **3.6 Å** resolution. Steric exclusion prevents a second Ctf13–Skp1 heterodimer from occupying the symmetric Cep3 dimer, explaining the asymmetric 2:1:1 core stoichiometry. (leber2018structuralbasisfor pages 2-3, leber2018structuralbasisfor pages 1-2, leber2018structuralbasisfor pages 3-5)

Skp1’s C-terminal helices α6–α8 bind the Ctf13 F-box, while Skp1 residues 105–112 form an additional loop contacting both the Ctf13 F-box and leucine-rich repeats. Skp1 also contacts Cep3 and stabilizes one Cep3 Gal4 DNA-binding domain. Mutating Skp1 Y139 and N140 prevented complex formation in pull-down assays despite comparable protein expression, providing direct functional validation of the structural interface. (leber2018structuralbasisfor pages 2-3, leber2018structuralbasisfor pages 3-5, leber2018structuralbasisfor pages 5-6)

### Functional interpretation

The Skp1–Ctf13 interaction is best viewed as an evolutionary reuse of the Skp1–F-box interface for kinetochore construction. In CBF3, Skp1’s primary role is structural and assembly-promoting rather than ubiquitin transfer. Biochemical reconstitution further showed that coexpression of Ctf13 with Skp1 restored CBF3 activity, whereas either protein with Sgt1 alone did not; Ctf13–Skp1 also rescued defective CBF3 activity in *sgt1-3* extracts. (kitagawa1999sgt1encodesan pages 4-5)

## 5. Sgt1–Hsp90-dependent assembly axis

Sgt1 is a cochaperone-associated assembly factor connecting Hsp90 to both SCF-associated proteins and the kinetochore. A **2.8-Å** crystal structure showed the concave surface of the Sgt1 TPR domain binding the Skp1 BTB/POZ region. Purified full-length Sgt1 and an isolated Sgt1 TPR construct, residues 1–150, each bound Skp1 with a dissociation constant of approximately **0.6–0.7 μM**. Alteration of Skp1 residues 65–74 abolished a detectable ITC binding isotherm, and yeast genetics indicated that Sgt1–Skp1 binding is an essential Sgt1 function. (willhoft2017thecrystalstructure pages 2-3, willhoft2017thecrystalstructure pages 6-7, willhoft2017thecrystalstructure pages 1-2)

Only about **2% of total Skp1** was estimated to associate with Sgt1 in yeast extracts. Sgt1–Skp1-containing material migrated at an apparent **200 ± 50 kDa** and overlapped SCF ubiquitin-ligase activity. This low steady-state fraction is consistent with Sgt1 acting transiently in assembly or quality control rather than as a stoichiometric component of every mature Skp1 complex. (kitagawa1999sgt1encodesan pages 5-6)

## 6. Noncanonical role in membrane-protein recycling

Skp1 also binds the F-box protein Rcy1 in a complex lacking detectable Cdc53 and Hrt1. Two-hybrid reporter activity was **1,818 ± 134** or **2,187 ± 156 Miller units**, depending on fusion orientation; mutation of three conserved Rcy1 F-box residues reduced activity to **631 ± 73**, and F-box deletion reduced it to **91 ± 12**. Co-immunoprecipitation recovered approximately 32-kDa Skp1 with Rcy1 but not canonical cullin/RING components, supporting classification as a non-SCF complex. (galan2001skp1pandthe pages 9-10, galan2001skp1pandthe pages 10-11)

Functionally, the Rcy1–Skp1 assembly contributes to post-endocytic recycling of the exocytic v-SNARE Snc1. In *skp1-12* cells, about **40%** displayed clearly intracellular GFP-Snc1, and only **49%** of GFP-Snc1 was hyperphosphorylated, compared with approximately **70%** in wild-type or canonical SCF-mutant cells. Mutants in Cdc34, Cdc53, or Hrt1 did not reproduce the recycling defect, separating this activity from conventional SCF-dependent ubiquitylation. (galan2001skp1pandthe pages 4-5, galan2001skp1pandthe pages 5-7)

Rcy1 localizes to cytoplasmic patches enriched at buds, bud necks, and mating projections; its CAAX motif, polarized secretion, and an intact actin cytoskeleton are required for this localization. These data locate the **Rcy1-associated Skp1 function** to polarized endosomal/Golgi recycling sites, although they do not demonstrate that the entire cellular pool of Skp1 shares Rcy1’s visible localization. (galan2001skp1pandthe pages 2-3, galan2001skp1pandthe pages 10-11)

## 7. Cellular localization

Skp1 should be annotated as a **nucleocytoplasmic, complex-dependent protein**, rather than as a resident of one organelle.

* **Nucleus/centromere:** its CBF3 function is executed at centromeric CDEIII DNA in the inner kinetochore. The structural and genetic evidence for this location is direct and strong. (leber2018structuralbasisfor pages 1-2, leber2018structuralbasisfor pages 5-6)
* **Nuclear and cytosolic SCF pools:** different F-box receptors recruit Skp1 to substrates controlling the cell cycle, transcription, nutrient responses, and protein turnover. Localization is consequently receptor- and substrate-dependent rather than specified by an intrinsic membrane anchor. (harris2024molecularinsightsintoa pages 24-27, lauinger2024cadmiumbindingby pages 1-2)
* **Endosomal/polarized-growth pathway:** the Rcy1-bound fraction acts in Snc1 recycling associated with polarized cytoplasmic trafficking structures. This assignment is based primarily on Rcy1 and cargo localization plus Skp1-dependent trafficking phenotypes. (galan2001skp1pandthe pages 2-3, galan2001skp1pandthe pages 10-11)

Skp1 is not a transporter, secreted protein, integral membrane protein, or sequence-specific DNA-binding protein.

## 8. Recent developments, 2023–2024

### Cadmium sensing by SCF^Met30

The most important recent Skp1-relevant advance is a 2024 mechanistic study of SCF^Met30. Met30’s F-box cysteines C201, C205, and C211 bind cadmium with a reported **K_D of approximately 8 μM**. Cadmium induces a conformational change and Met30 autoubiquitylation, recruiting Shp1 and the Cdc48/p97 segregase. Cdc48 then extracts Met30 from the Cdc53–Skp1 core, disassembling SCF^Met30 and relieving ubiquitylation-dependent repression of Met4/Met32-controlled stress programs. (lauinger2024cadmiumbindingby pages 8-9, lauinger2024cadmiumbindingby pages 1-2, lauinger2024cadmiumbindingby pages 2-3)

This result refines the annotation of Skp1: the Skp1–F-box interface is not merely a static bridge but a regulated disassembly point. Nevertheless, **Met30, not Skp1, is the demonstrated cadmium sensor**. Inactivation of SCF^Met30 supports sulfur-metabolite restoration and glutathione production during cadmium stress. (lauinger2024cadmiumbindingby pages 1-2)

### Hydrogen-sulfide and sulfur-flux sensing

A separate 2024 study found that hydrogen sulfide is sufficient to induce Met4 ubiquitylation in vivo and implicated cysteines in Met30’s WD40 region in sulfur sensing. Met30 contains **23 cysteines**, nearly all of which remained reduced under the tested sulfur-replete and sulfur-deficient conditions. The proposed persulfidation or metal-dependent mechanisms therefore remain hypotheses rather than established chemistry. Again, the sensing function belongs to Met30 within a Skp1-containing SCF—not to Skp1 itself. (johnson2024evidencefora pages 1-2, johnson2024evidencefora pages 6-8)

These 2024 results illustrate a current expert view of SCF biology: F-box proteins can be regulated environmental sensors as well as substrate receptors, while Skp1 supplies the conserved interface through which their association with the catalytic core is assembled or dismantled.

## 9. Research applications and real-world relevance

1. **Cell-cycle and targeted-proteolysis research.** Yeast Skp1 is a foundational model for understanding modular cullin–RING ligases, phosphodegron-dependent substrate selection, and proteasomal control of CDK transitions. The direct value is mechanistic rather than clinical: manipulating Skp1 generally perturbs many SCFs and is less selective than manipulating a particular F-box receptor. (kitagawa1999sgt1encodesan pages 1-2, harris2024molecularinsightsintoa pages 24-27)

2. **Kinetochore assembly and chromosome segregation.** The genetically compact budding-yeast point centromere makes Skp1–Ctf13–Cep3 an experimentally tractable model for coupling sequence-specific centromere recognition to kinetochore assembly. The 3.6-Å CBF3 structure provides a framework for testing interface mutations and chromosome-segregation defects. (leber2018structuralbasisfor pages 1-2, leber2018structuralbasisfor pages 5-6)

3. **Chaperone-assisted complex biogenesis.** Quantitative Sgt1–Skp1 binding and structural data make this system useful for studying how Hsp90/cochaperones assemble multiprotein machines without remaining permanent subunits. (willhoft2017thecrystalstructure pages 2-3, kitagawa1999sgt1encodesan pages 5-6)

4. **Environmental and nutrient sensing.** The 2024 SCF^Met30 studies connect a Skp1-based ligase to cadmium detoxification, glutathione homeostasis, sulfur flux, and H₂S signaling. Potential biotechnology relevance includes understanding yeast robustness under metal or nutrient stress, but no direct industrial SKP1 engineering implementation was established by the retrieved evidence. (lauinger2024cadmiumbindingby pages 8-9, johnson2024evidencefora pages 1-2)

5. **Membrane-trafficking models.** The Rcy1–Skp1 complex demonstrates that Skp1–F-box modules can be repurposed outside canonical cullin E3 ligases and supports mechanistic dissection of post-endocytic SNARE recycling. (galan2001skp1pandthe pages 5-7, galan2001skp1pandthe pages 10-11)

## 10. Evidence-weighted conclusion

The most defensible primary annotation is: **Skp1 is an essential, conserved F-box-binding adaptor that organizes multiple protein complexes.** Its two best-established essential contexts are canonical SCF ubiquitin ligases and the CBF3 inner kinetochore. In SCFs it couples receptor-mediated substrate recognition to Cdc53–Rbx1/E2-dependent ubiquitylation; in CBF3 it binds Ctf13 and Cep3 to assemble the centromere-recognition apparatus. Additional direct evidence supports transient Sgt1 association and a noncanonical Rcy1-dependent function in Snc1 recycling. (kitagawa1999sgt1encodesan pages 1-2, kitagawa1999sgt1encodesan pages 5-6, leber2018structuralbasisfor pages 5-6, galan2001skp1pandthe pages 1-1)

The annotation should avoid three common errors: calling Skp1 the catalytic E3 enzyme, assigning it direct substrate specificity, or describing it as the direct cadmium/H₂S sensor. Catalysis resides in the assembled RING/E2 machinery, substrate recognition ordinarily resides in the F-box receptor, and the recent stress-sensing chemistry maps to Met30. (harris2024molecularinsightsintoa pages 24-27, lauinger2024cadmiumbindingby pages 1-2, johnson2024evidencefora pages 6-8)
