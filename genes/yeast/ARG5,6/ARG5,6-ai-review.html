Gene Description

ARG5,6 of Saccharomyces cerevisiae encodes an 863-residue mitochondrially targeted precursor that is proteolytically processed in the mitochondrial matrix into two enzymes of the acetylated branch of arginine biosynthesis: N-acetylglutamate kinase (NAGK, EC 2.7.2.8; N-terminal part, ARG6), which phosphorylates N-acetyl-L-glutamate, and N-acetyl-gamma-glutamyl-phosphate reductase (EC 1.2.1.38; C-terminal part, ARG5), which reduces N-acetylglutamyl phosphate to N-acetylglutamate semialdehyde with NADPH. The kinase is feedback-inhibited by arginine and, via an ascomycete-specific C-terminal domain, forms a complex with N-acetylglutamate synthase (Arg2) that is required for synthase activity and coordinates feedback regulation of the first two steps. Arg5,6 has also been reported to bind specific nuclear and mitochondrial DNA loci and to influence transcript levels of target genes.
