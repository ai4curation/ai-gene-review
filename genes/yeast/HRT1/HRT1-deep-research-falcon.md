# Functional annotation report: *Saccharomyces cerevisiae* HRT1 (Q08273)

## Executive conclusion

The requested protein is correctly identified. **HRT1 (systematic locus YOL133W; aliases RBX1 and ROC1; UniProt Q08273)** encodes the approximately 17-kDa RING-H2/RING-box subunit of budding-yeast cullin–RING ubiquitin ligases. This assignment agrees with the supplied RING-box and zinc-finger domain annotations and with primary biochemical identification of YOL133W among Cdc53-associated proteins. It must not be confused with yeast **HRD1**, an ER-membrane ubiquitin ligase, or with metazoan **HRT2/RBX2**. (seol1999cdc53cullinandthe pages 2-4)

Hrt1’s primary function is **not substrate recognition and not proteolysis itself**. It binds a cullin scaffold—most prominently Cdc53 in SCF complexes—and recruits/activates a ubiquitin-charged E2, principally Cdc34. The resulting cullin–Hrt1 catalytic module enables transfer of ubiquitin directly from E2 to substrates selected by an interchangeable F-box receptor. Hrt1 also works with Ubc12 and Dcn1 in the distinct Rub1/NEDD8-like pathway to modify cullins. (seol1999cdc53cullinandthe pages 10-11, scott2010aduale3 pages 1-2, sengupta2023theubiquitinlikeproteins pages 4-5)

## 1. Identity, family, and domain interpretation

Seol and colleagues identified Hrt1 by affinity purification of Cdc53 complexes followed by mass spectrometry. The protein was assigned to **HRT1/YOL133W**, had an apparent molecular mass of about 17 kDa, and contained a RING-H2 domain. Genetic complementation established that lethality associated with disruption of the overlapping locus was attributable to HRT1 rather than neighboring YOL134C. These observations strongly validate the UniProt Q08273 identity in the S288c background. (seol1999cdc53cullinandthe pages 2-4, seol1999cdc53cullinandthe pages 4-6)

The supplied InterPro/Pfam calls—RING-box E3 ubiquitin ligase, zinc-finger RING, RING-H2, and zf-rbx1—are therefore mutually consistent. Functionally, the zinc-coordinating RING fold is an E2-binding catalytic platform rather than a DNA-binding zinc finger. Hrt1 is the budding-yeast counterpart of RBX1/ROC1 and is evolutionarily related to the APC/C RING protein Apc11. Human HRT1 could complement yeast HRT1 deletion, providing unusually strong cross-species evidence for conserved function. (seol1999cdc53cullinandthe pages 4-6, seol1999cdc53cullinandthe pages 9-10)

## 2. Primary molecular function

### 2.1 Core subunit of SCF cullin–RING ligases

An SCF complex is modular:

- **Cdc53/cullin:** elongated scaffold;
- **Hrt1:** cullin-bound RING subunit at the catalytic end;
- **Skp1:** adaptor;
- **F-box protein:** substrate receptor, such as Cdc4, Grr1, Met30, or Dia2;
- **Cdc34:** ubiquitin-conjugating E2.

Tagged Hrt1 co-immunoprecipitated with Cdc53, Cdc4, Skp1, and Cdc34. In recombinant binding assays, Hrt1 associated with Cdc53, Cdc4, and Cdc34, but not detectably with Skp1 in the isolated assay. Approximately 20% of tagged Cdc53 and Cdc4 was recovered in the reported Hrt1-binding experiments. Together, Cdc53 and Hrt1 constituted a minimal ubiquitin-ligase module that strongly stimulated complete SCF complexes. (seol1999cdc53cullinandthe pages 2-4, seol1999cdc53cullinandthe pages 4-6)

### 2.2 Reaction and catalytic mechanism

For canonical SCF ubiquitination, the overall reaction is:

**E2–S–ubiquitin + substrate lysine → E2-SH + substrate–NH–CO–ubiquitin**, followed by repeated transfer to form a polyubiquitin chain.

Hrt1 is a **RING E3**, not a HECT-type ligase. It does not form an obligatory Hrt1–ubiquitin thioester. Instead, its RING domain binds the charged E2 and promotes a transfer-competent state, while the cullin–adaptor–receptor arm presents substrate. The original yeast work showed that Cdc53–Hrt1 activated Cdc34 autoubiquitination and multiubiquitin-chain formation; resistance to N-ethylmaleimide argued against a required catalytic-cysteine intermediate in Hrt1. The authors proposed stabilization of ubiquitin-transfer chemistry or a productive Cdc34 conformation, an interpretation consistent with the modern RING-E3 mechanism. (seol1999cdc53cullinandthe pages 10-11, seol1999cdc53cullinandthe pages 9-10)

In vitro, adding Hrt1 increased SCF-Cdc4-dependent ubiquitination of phosphorylated Sic1 by **more than fivefold**, affecting both rate and extent. Hrt1-containing SCF-Grr1 supported Cdc34-dependent Cln2 ubiquitination, whereas complexes lacking Hrt1 were essentially inactive. (seol1999cdc53cullinandthe pages 6-7, seol1999cdc53cullinandthe pages 4-6)

### 2.3 Substrate specificity

Hrt1 itself should **not** be annotated as specifically recognizing Sic1, Cln2, or another individual substrate. Specificity is supplied chiefly by the F-box receptor and often by a regulated degron, such as a phosphorylation-dependent recognition sequence. The common Cdc53–Hrt1 catalytic module can therefore serve numerous SCFs:

- **SCF-Cdc4:** phosphorylated Sic1 and other Cdc4 substrates;
- **SCF-Grr1:** Cln2 and additional Grr1 substrates;
- **SCF-Met30:** regulators of sulfur-metabolism transcription;
- **SCF-Dia2:** replication-associated substrates.

The 2023 yeast ubiquitin-like-protein review lists Cdc4 targets including Sic1, Cln1, Cln2, Far1, and Clb6, but these are receptor/complex-level substrate assignments, not direct binding specificities of Hrt1. (sengupta2023theubiquitinlikeproteins pages 4-5)

## 3. Second biochemical role: Rub1 modification of cullins

Hrt1 participates in a second conjugation reaction using **Rub1**, the budding-yeast NEDD8 ortholog. Rub1 is activated by its E1, transferred by transthiolation to Ubc12, and ultimately attached through an isopeptide bond to a cullin lysine. Hrt1 and Dcn1 act as complementary E3 components in this process. A 2023 review summarizes this dual role: Hrt1 functions with Cdc34 in ubiquitination and with Ubc12 in Rub1 modification of yeast cullins. (sengupta2023theubiquitinlikeproteins pages 4-5)

Scott et al. resolved the mechanism in budding yeast. Hrt1’s N-terminal region associates with Cdc53, while its C-terminal RING activates Ubc12~Rub1. The Hrt1 RING stimulated substrate-independent discharge of Ubc12~Rub1 and was necessary for Rub1 transfer to **Cdc53 Lys760**; Cdc53 K760R prevented modification. Dcn1’s PONY domain bound Cdc53 and Ubc12, suppressed nonspecific discharge, and positioned the Ubc12 active site near the cullin acceptor lysine. Thus, Hrt1 supplies conventional RING-E3 activation while Dcn1 supplies substrate positioning—a “dual-E3” mechanism. (scott2010aduale3 pages 1-2, scott2010aduale3 pages 2-3)

This mechanism regulates rather than simply destroys the cullin. Rub1 modification promotes active CRL conformations and counteracts inhibition by the yeast CAND1-like factor Lag2. Rub1 can subsequently be removed, allowing dynamic CRL regulation. (scott2010aduale3 pages 1-2, sengupta2023theubiquitinlikeproteins pages 4-5)

## 4. Biological processes and pathways

### Cell-cycle entry and protein turnover

HRT1 is essential for vegetative growth under standard laboratory conditions. Conditional Hrt1 impairment stabilized Sic1 and Cln2 and disrupted progression from G1 into S phase. Sic1 is a cyclin-dependent-kinase inhibitor whose timely SCF-Cdc4-dependent elimination permits S-phase CDK activation; failure to remove it explains much of the observed G1/S defect. (seol1999cdc53cullinandthe pages 1-2, seol1999cdc53cullinandthe pages 6-7)

A useful quantitative result is the increase in Cln2 half-life from approximately **8 minutes to 19 minutes** in a temperature-sensitive C-terminally tagged hrt1-myc9 background. Because this result came from a conditional tagged allele, it is compelling functional evidence but should not be interpreted as the native half-life under every growth condition. (seol1999cdc53cullinandthe pages 4-6)

### Nutrient-responsive transcription

Hrt1 impairment also compromised SCF-Met30 output, including normal MET25 repression. This shows that the Cdc53–Hrt1 module supports regulated transcriptional responses through proteolytic or ubiquitin-dependent control of pathway regulators; Hrt1 is not itself a sequence-specific transcription factor. (seol1999cdc53cullinandthe pages 6-7, seol1999cdc53cullinandthe pages 4-6)

### Replication and other SCF programs

Recent syntheses retain Dia2 among the established budding-yeast F-box proteins and place Hrt1 in the invariant SCF catalytic core. SCF-Dia2 is implicated in replication-fork functions and replisome/CMG processing. However, this is best regarded as a function of an **Hrt1-containing SCF-Dia2 complex**, not evidence that Hrt1 alone recognizes the replisome. The strongest direct Hrt1 experiments remain the Cdc4, Grr1, and Met30 studies. (sengupta2023theubiquitinlikeproteins pages 4-5)

### Ty3 transposition phenotype

The historical name “high-level expression reduces Ty3 transposition” derives from an HRT1 overexpression observation. The foundational paper attributed it to database information and unpublished work and supplied neither a quantitative effect nor a defined mechanism. It is therefore a secondary phenotype, potentially arising through altered ubiquitination, and should not replace “cullin–RING E3 subunit” as the primary annotation. (seol1999cdc53cullinandthe pages 2-4)

## 5. Cellular localization

Hrt1 is an **intracellular, cullin-associated component of soluble ubiquitin-ligase complexes**. Its demonstrated substrates and pathways encompass nuclear cell-cycle regulation and other intracellular programs. Nevertheless, the retrieved primary evidence does not justify assigning Q08273 an exclusively nuclear, exclusively cytoplasmic, membrane, or organellar localization. As an invariant catalytic subunit used by different CRLs, its effective localization is expected to depend on the associated cullin, F-box receptor, substrate, and cell state. This compartment-flexible interpretation is more defensible than assigning localization from one substrate alone. (seol1999cdc53cullinandthe pages 2-4, seol1999cdc53cullinandthe pages 1-2)

## 6. Recent developments, 2023–2024

The most relevant 2023 source is Sengupta and Pick’s review, **“The Ubiquitin-like Proteins of Saccharomyces cerevisiae,” published April 2023**, which integrates Hrt1 into both the Cdc34–ubiquitin and Ubc12–Rub1 systems and emphasizes reversible cullin modification. DOI/URL: https://doi.org/10.3390/biom13050734. (sengupta2023theubiquitinlikeproteins pages 4-5)

The recent literature largely refines the wider CRL/neddylation framework rather than overturning Hrt1’s established annotation. No 2023–2024 Hrt1-specific budding-yeast primary study retrieved here provided a replacement for the foundational reconstitution, genetics, or dual-E3 work. Accordingly, the current consensus remains grounded in Seol et al. (1999) for ubiquitin-ligase function and Scott et al. (2010) for Rub1 ligation, with newer reviews integrating those mechanisms into contemporary CRL biology. (seol1999cdc53cullinandthe pages 2-4, scott2010aduale3 pages 1-2, sengupta2023theubiquitinlikeproteins pages 4-5)

## 7. Applications and broader relevance

Hrt1 is valuable experimentally as a conserved model for:

1. **Reconstituting SCF catalysis** and separating substrate recruitment from E2 activation;
2. **Studying essential-protein turnover** through conditional alleles;
3. **Dissecting cullin neddylation/Rub1 modification**, including cooperation between RING and Dcn1-type E3 activities;
4. **Understanding human RBX1-containing CRLs**, which are central to targeted-protein-degradation and neddylation-inhibitor research.

The translational connection requires care: human RBX1/CRL pharmacology is supported by evolutionary conservation and by human HRT1 rescuing yeast lethality, but disease associations or drug responses of human RBX1 are not direct evidence about yeast Hrt1 physiology. Yeast Hrt1 is primarily a mechanistic model and experimental platform, not itself a clinical target. (seol1999cdc53cullinandthe pages 4-6, scott2010aduale3 pages 2-3)

## 8. Evidence-weighted annotation

**Recommended primary annotation:** “Essential RING-box subunit of cullin–RING E3 ligases; binds cullins such as Cdc53 and recruits/activates E2 enzymes to catalyze substrate ubiquitination. Also participates with Ubc12 and Dcn1 in Rub1 modification of cullins.”

**Recommended process terms:** ubiquitin-dependent protein catabolism; SCF ubiquitin-ligase activity; cell-cycle regulation, especially G1/S; regulated transcription through SCF-Met30; cullin Rub1/NEDD8-like modification; and additional receptor-defined SCF pathways.

**Key caveat:** Hrt1 provides the conserved catalytic/E2-facing module; substrate selectivity belongs chiefly to the associated F-box receptor. Its localization and biological outputs are consequently complex-dependent rather than defined by a single substrate or compartment.
