| Identity and kleisin architecture | The user-supplied UniProt record identifies Q12158 as S288c **MCD1**, with aliases **SCC1/PDS3/RHC21**. Literature independently identifies budding-yeast Mcd1/Scc1 as the Rad21-family kleisin: its N- and C-terminal domains bind the Smc3 and Smc1 ATPase heads, respectively, closing the cohesin ring; the central region contains separase-cleavage sites. | Electron microscopy showed ring-like cohesin; live-cell FRET placed Scc1 near both SMC heads; genetic and biochemical studies define an Mcd1–Smc1–Smc3 core trimer. The exact accession-to-gene mapping is supported here only by the supplied UniProt record, not independently asserted from these papers. (pqac-00000000, pqac-00000007) | Established; foundational evidence summarized in 2012 and directly supported in 2015. |
| Chromosome localization | Mcd1-containing cohesin acts in the nucleus on chromatin, accumulating from S phase through metaphase and disappearing from chromosomes in early anaphase. Cohesin is enriched around centromeres, at chromosome-arm sites roughly every **10–15 kb**, frequently in convergent-gene intergenic regions, and at rDNA, telomeres, and silent HM loci. | Cell-cycle localization studies showed chromatin-associated Scc1/Mcd1 from S phase to early anaphase. Genome-scale mapping and transcription-perturbation studies distinguish cohesin-loading sites from accumulation sites and show transcription-dependent redistribution. (pqac-00000000, pqac-00000014) | Established distribution model; foundational mapping with later mechanistic refinement. |
| S-phase cohesion establishment | Scc2–Scc4 loads cohesin, but stable sister-DNA cohesion is established during replication. Eco1/Ctf7 acetylates Smc3 K112/K113 during S phase, antagonizing Wpl1-mediated release and stabilizing cohesin around newly replicated sister chromatids. Mcd1 is structural rather than catalytic: it supplies the cleavable ring closure and interaction platform. | Genetics, replication-coupling studies, and acetylation experiments show that loading and cohesion establishment are distinct and that Eco1-dependent Smc3 acetylation is required for S-phase cohesion; non-acetylated cohesin remains susceptible to Wpl1-dependent release. (pqac-00000002, pqac-00000003, pqac-00000014) | Conserved, well-supported mechanism; synthesis through 2025. |
| Anaphase cleavage | APC/C-mediated securin destruction activates Esp1/separase, which cleaves Mcd1/Scc1, opens cohesin, and irreversibly permits sister-chromatid segregation. Mcd1 therefore functions as both the physical ring closure and the regulated “break point” for anaphase. | Engineered TEV cleavage of Scc1 released cohesin from chromatin and triggered sister separation, demonstrating cleavage sufficiency; normal Mcd1 cleavage depends on APC/C–Esp1. (pqac-00000000, pqac-00000006) | Canonical direct experimental evidence; established before 2012 and reaffirmed in 2025. |
| Accessory-protein scaffold | Mcd1 recruits Scc3, Pds5, and Wpl1/Rad61, integrating cohesin loading, residence, release, and chromosome-organization functions. Thus, it is not merely a passive linker but an interaction scaffold within the complex. | A five-residue insertion after Scc3 I358 or Scc3 D373 mutation abolished Scc3–Mcd1 binding, chromosome association, cohesion, and viability while preserving Scc3 contacts with Pds5, Wpl1, and the loader; Y371A/K372A separated condensation from cohesion without disrupting Mcd1 binding. (pqac-00000007) | Peer-reviewed primary study, March 2015. |
| DNA repair and genome organization | Mcd1-containing cohesin promotes genome stability by maintaining sister proximity for homologous repair and by organizing chromosome structure. Direct protein-specific attribution is difficult because most perturbations assay the cohesin complex; genome-organization evidence is stronger than the retrieved direct Mcd1-specific DNA-repair evidence. | Cohesin literature links the complex to DNA repair, replication-fork progression, condensation, and genome organization; recent experiments use auxin-degradable Scc1/Mcd1 and chromosome-contact mapping to test architectural function directly. (pqac-00000001, pqac-00000008, pqac-00000013) | Cohesin-level role established; Mcd1-specific repair mechanism less directly resolved in the retrieved evidence. |
| 2024 transcription-driven chromatin-loop model | Guérin and colleagues concluded that cohesin is required for detectable budding-yeast loop signals, but intrinsic cohesin loop extrusion is not necessarily their in-vivo motor. Their model proposes that cohesin captures two DNA segments while transcription supplies an **extrinsic motor** that expands and positions loops. This challenges—not yet universally replaces—the conventional intrinsic-extrusion interpretation. | Scc3 and Smc1 variants lost measurable in-vitro extrusion while retaining topological DNA entrapment and substantial in-vivo loop formation. Auxin-mediated Scc1 depletion eliminated loop signals, whereas TAD boundaries and genome-wide insulation persisted. Increasing positive helical tension reduced mean loop score from **0.31 to 0.18**. Analyses included **1,059/1,447** G1/G2-M anchor ChIP measurements, **788/1,060** corresponding loop sets, **822** TAD boundaries, and **1,300** loops. (pqac-00000008, pqac-00000009, pqac-00000011, pqac-00000013) | Peer-reviewed *EMBO Journal*, August 2024; mechanistically important but interpretively debated. |
