# MCD1/SCC1 (UniProt Q12158) in *Saccharomyces cerevisiae*: functional-annotation report

## Executive summary

**MCD1** encodes the mitotic kleisin subunit of cohesin in budding yeast. It is also called **SCC1**, **PDS3**, or **RHC21** and is the fungal ortholog of Rad21. It is **not an enzyme or transporter**. Its primary function is structural and regulatory: Mcd1 connects the ATPase heads of Smc1 and Smc3, closes the cohesin ring around DNA, and provides a binding platform for Scc3, Pds5, and Wpl1/Rad61. Through this complex, Mcd1 enables sister-chromatid cohesion, accurate chromosome segregation, chromosome folding, and genome-repair functions. At anaphase, cleavage of Mcd1 by Esp1/separase opens cohesin and releases sister chromatids. (kulemzina2012theroleof pages 25-28, orgil2015aconserveddomain pages 1-2)

## 1. Identity verification

The supplied UniProt record assigns **Q12158** to *S. cerevisiae* strain S288c, locus **YDL003W**, gene **MCD1**, and the aliases **SCC1/PDS3/RHC21**. The literature independently and consistently identifies budding-yeast Mcd1/Scc1 as the Rad21-family cohesin kleisin; no evidence retrieved concerned a conflicting protein with the same symbol. The papers generally identify the protein by gene aliases rather than by UniProt accession, so the exact Q12158–S288c mapping is based on the supplied UniProt record, while the functional identity is independently supported by the literature. (kulemzina2012theroleof pages 25-28, orgil2015aconserveddomain pages 1-2)

The reported Rad21/Rec8-like N- and C-terminal domains are mechanistically appropriate. Mcd1’s N-terminal globular domain binds the Smc3 head and its C-terminal domain binds the Smc1 head, bridging the long Smc1–Smc3 dimer into a closed ring. Its less structured central region contains separase-cleavage sites and binding surfaces for cohesin-associated proteins. This agrees with the reported Rad21/Rec8_N, Rad21/Rec8_C, ScpA-like_C, and winged-helix-superfamily annotations. (kulemzina2012theroleof pages 25-28)

## 2. Primary molecular function

Mcd1 is the **cleavable ring-closure and interaction scaffold of cohesin**. Smc1 and Smc3 supply ATP binding and hydrolysis; Mcd1 does not catalyze a reaction. Its functional “substrates” are therefore not small molecules but the Smc1/Smc3 heads and associated chromosomal DNA. By linking the two SMC heads, Mcd1 permits topological DNA entrapment and enables cohesin to tether replicated sister DNA molecules or organize contacts within one chromosome. Electron microscopy, live-cell FRET, genetics, and biochemical interaction studies support proximity of Scc1/Mcd1 to both SMC heads and the ring architecture. (kulemzina2012theroleof pages 25-28)

Mcd1 is also an organizational hub. Scc3, Pds5, and Wpl1/Rad61 associate through the kleisin and regulate loading, chromosomal residence, cohesion maintenance, and release. Direct evidence comes from Scc3 mutants: a five-residue insertion after I358 or substitution of D373 abolished Scc3–Mcd1 binding, chromosome association, cohesion, and viability, while preserving Scc3 interactions with Pds5, Wpl1, and the loader. Conversely, Scc3 Y371A and K372A retained Mcd1 binding and cohesion but impaired chromosome condensation, separating cohesin’s cohesion and condensation outputs. (orgil2015aconserveddomain pages 1-2)

## 3. Biological pathway and cell-cycle mechanism

### Loading and establishment

The Scc2–Scc4 loader promotes ATP-dependent loading of the Mcd1–Smc1–Smc3 complex onto chromosomes. Loading alone does not establish sister cohesion. During S phase, replication-associated Eco1/Ctf7 acetylates Smc3, particularly K112 and K113, opposing Wpl1-dependent release and converting chromatin-bound cohesin into a stable cohesive state. Thus, Mcd1 supplies the ring closure, while Smc ATPase cycling and Smc3 acetylation control loading and stabilization. (singh2025aberrantcohesinfunction pages 22-24, martin2025theroleanda pages 35-39)

This mechanism couples cohesion to DNA replication: cohesin loaded before or during S phase becomes competent to tether newly produced sisters as the replication fork passes. Loss of Mcd1 or disruption of its cohesin interfaces therefore compromises cohesion even though Mcd1 itself is not the acetyltransferase or ATPase. (singh2025aberrantcohesinfunction pages 22-24, martin2025theroleanda pages 35-39)

### Maintenance and chromosome mechanics

From S phase through metaphase, Mcd1-containing cohesin resists spindle-generated forces and maintains sister proximity. Pericentromeric cohesion is particularly important for sister-kinetochore biorientation, whereas chromosome-arm cohesion constrains sister movement and favors use of the sister chromatid as a homologous-recombination template. Cohesin also contributes to chromosome condensation and three-dimensional genome organization; these are complex-level properties for which Mcd1 is indispensable as the ring-closing subunit. (singh2026breakingupwithout pages 35-41, orgil2015aconserveddomain pages 1-2)

### Anaphase release

At the metaphase–anaphase transition, APC/C-dependent destruction of securin activates Esp1/separase. Esp1 cleaves Mcd1, opening the cohesin ring and permitting sister-chromatid segregation. Engineered TEV-protease cleavage of Scc1/Mcd1 releases cohesin from chromosomes and triggers sister separation, demonstrating that kleisin cleavage is sufficient rather than merely correlated with anaphase. Mcd1 is subsequently degraded, and Hos1-dependent Smc3 deacetylation helps reset cohesin for another cell cycle. (kulemzina2012theroleof pages 25-28, martin2025theroleanda pages 35-39)

## 4. Localization

Mcd1 acts predominantly **in the nucleus on chromatin**. It accumulates from S phase through metaphase, colocalizes with chromosomes, and is lost from chromatin in early anaphase following cleavage. There is no evidence that its primary function occurs extracellularly, at membranes, or in an organelle outside the nucleus. (kulemzina2012theroleof pages 25-28)

Genome-mapping studies place cohesin strongly in pericentromeric regions and at chromosome-arm sites spaced approximately **10–15 kb** apart. Arm peaks frequently occur in intergenic regions between convergently transcribed genes; additional enrichment occurs at rDNA, telomeres, and silent mating-type loci. Loading and accumulation sites need not coincide because transcription and other DNA-based motors can redistribute cohesin after loading. (kraycer2025determinantsofcohesin pages 13-19)

## 5. DNA repair and genome organization

Cohesin promotes homologous-recombination repair by retaining or re-establishing proximity between sister chromatids. However, much of this evidence pertains to the cohesin complex rather than a biochemical repair activity intrinsic to Mcd1. The defensible annotation is therefore that **Mcd1 is an essential structural component enabling cohesin-dependent repair**, not a nuclease, ligase, helicase, or recombinase. (singh2026breakingupwithout pages 35-41, singh2025aberrantcohesinfunction pages 22-24)

The same distinction applies to chromosome looping. Mcd1 is required because it completes the cohesin architecture, but the physical motor or capture mechanism that generates loops remains actively debated. Auxin-induced Scc1/Mcd1 depletion provides a useful acute perturbation for distinguishing cohesin-dependent contacts from other chromosome domains. (guerin2024anextrinsicmotor pages 8-9, guerin2024anextrinsicmotor pages 22-24)

## 6. Recent research, especially 2023–2024

The most consequential recent direct study was Guérin and colleagues, published in the *EMBO Journal* in August 2024, DOI: https://doi.org/10.1038/s44318-024-00202-5. The investigators produced budding-yeast cohesin variants defective in measured in-vitro loop extrusion but retaining topological DNA entrapment. Considerable in-vivo loop formation persisted, leading them to argue that intrinsic cohesin extrusion is not necessarily the operative motor in yeast. They proposed instead that cohesin captures two DNA segments and that transcription supplies an **extrinsic motor** that expands and positions the loop. (guerin2024anextrinsicmotor pages 1-2)

Acute Scc1/Mcd1 depletion eliminated detectable loop signals, confirming that the contacts remained cohesin dependent. In contrast, prominent transcription-associated domain boundaries—including at the induced GAL7–GAL10–GAL1 region—and genome-wide insulation could persist without cohesin, separating cohesin-dependent loops from cohesin-independent transcriptional domains. (guerin2024anextrinsicmotor pages 8-9)

The study included **1,059 G1 and 1,447 G2/M loop-anchor ChIP measurements**, corresponding loop sets of **788 and 1,060**, and additional analyses of **822 domain boundaries and 1,300 loops**. Experimentally increasing positive DNA helical tension reduced the displayed mean loop score from **0.31 to 0.18**, consistent with—but not proving—a preference for transcriptionally unwound DNA during loop capture. (guerin2024anextrinsicmotor pages 7-8, guerin2024anextrinsicmotor pages 22-24)

This 2024 model should be treated as an important mechanistic challenge, not settled consensus. In-vitro extrusion by cohesin and extrusion-based chromosome models remain influential. Guérin et al. argue that their yeast results favor transcription-driven DNA–DNA capture in vivo; the distinction may depend on organism, assay, chromosomal context, or cohesin state. The paper is peer reviewed, whereas some related 2023 observations cited in this area were initially bioRxiv preprints and should not be assigned equal evidentiary weight. (guerin2024anextrinsicmotor pages 8-9, guerin2024anextrinsicmotor pages 13-14)

## 7. Applications and real-world implementation

MCD1 has no direct therapeutic or industrial application established in the retrieved evidence. Its practical importance is as an experimentally tractable **model-system handle on conserved cohesin biology**. Temperature-sensitive alleles, tagged Mcd1, separase- or TEV-cleavable constructs, and auxin-inducible Scc1 degradation permit temporal dissection of cohesion, anaphase, DNA repair, and chromosome folding. Chromatin immunoprecipitation and Micro-C/contact mapping using these strains connect molecular perturbations to genome-wide binding and three-dimensional chromosome structure. (kulemzina2012theroleof pages 25-28, guerin2024anextrinsicmotor pages 8-9, guerin2024anextrinsicmotor pages 22-24)

These yeast implementations inform conserved mechanisms relevant to aneuploidy, developmental cohesin disorders, and cohesin-mutant cancers, but extrapolation must be cautious: budding yeast lacks some metazoan cohesin regulatory features, and Mcd1 itself is not a validated human drug target. The appropriate translational value is mechanistic conservation and assay development, not direct clinical deployment.

## 8. Functional annotation conclusion

The strongest annotation is:

> **Mcd1/Scc1 is the essential mitotic Rad21-family kleisin of nuclear cohesin. It binds the Smc3 and Smc1 ATPase heads to close the cohesin ring, recruits cohesin-associated regulators, and enables topological DNA entrapment underlying sister-chromatid cohesion and chromosome organization. Cohesion is established with DNA replication through Eco1-dependent Smc3 acetylation and terminated at anaphase by Esp1/separase cleavage of Mcd1.** (kulemzina2012theroleof pages 25-28, martin2025theroleanda pages 35-39, orgil2015aconserveddomain pages 1-2)

The evidence supports direct roles in cohesin architecture, chromosome association, cohesion, and regulated release. DNA repair, condensation, transcriptional regulation, and chromatin looping are best annotated as **cohesin-complex functions enabled by Mcd1**, rather than independent enzymatic activities of Mcd1.
