# STE7 (P06784/YDL159W) in *Saccharomyces cerevisiae*: functional-annotation report

## Executive summary

The requested protein is correctly identified as **Ste7**, encoded by **STE7/YDL159W** in *Saccharomyces cerevisiae* S288c, UniProt **P06784**. The literature consistently identifies this protein as the central **MAP kinase kinase (MAP2K/MEK)** shared by the mating and filamentous/invasive-growth signaling pathways. This agrees with the supplied protein-kinase, ATP-binding, serine/threonine-kinase active-site, and Byr1-like catalytic-domain annotations. Papers about Ste7-like proteins in other fungi were excluded from the functional annotation.

Ste7 is best described as an ATP-dependent, dual-specificity MAPKK that is activated by the MAPKKK Ste11 and activates the MAPKs Fus3 and Kss1 through phosphorylation of their activation-loop threonine and tyrosine. In mating signaling, Ste7 functions in a Ste5-organized complex recruited to the plasma membrane and preferentially activates Fus3. In filamentous/invasive growth, it preferentially activates Kss1 and is much less dependent on Ste5. Thus, Ste7 is not simply a generic kinase relay: docking, scaffold-dependent localization, and allosteric substrate control determine which developmental program it activates. (maleri2004persistentactivationby pages 1-2, bardwell1996signalinginthe pages 1-2, good2009theste5scaffold pages 1-2)

## 1. Identity verification and nomenclature

The supplied record—STE7, ordered locus **YDL159W**, UniProt **P06784**, organism *S. cerevisiae* strain S288c—is internally consistent with the retrieved literature. An independent *S. cerevisiae* study explicitly lists **STE7/YDL159W** as a kinase in pheromone-induced signaling, while mechanistic studies identify yeast Ste7 as the MAPKK upstream of Fus3 and Kss1. Accordingly, the target is not the unrelated STE7/Ste7-like proteins described in *Schizosaccharomyces*, *Candida*, or filamentous fungi.

The UniProt description “serine/threonine-protein kinase” reflects Ste7’s membership in the protein-kinase superfamily, but its experimentally important biochemical classification is more specific: it is a **dual-specificity MAPK kinase**, because it installs both phosphothreonine and phosphotyrosine on MAPK activation loops. The catalytic-domain annotations supplied by the user—Byr1-like catalytic domain, kinase-like fold, protein-kinase ATP-binding region, and serine/threonine-kinase active site—are therefore fully compatible with its literature-defined MAP2K function. (bardwell1996signalinginthe pages 1-2, wang2002pheromonedependentubiquitinationof pages 1-2, gartner1992signaltransductionin pages 1-2)

## 2. Primary molecular function and catalytic reaction

Ste7 catalyzes phosphotransfer from ATP to protein hydroxyl groups:

**ATP + MAPK → ADP + doubly phosphorylated MAPK.**

Its validated physiological MAPK substrates are **Fus3** and **Kss1**. Pheromone treatment rapidly induces threonine and tyrosine phosphorylation of both MAPKs. For Fus3, the experimentally defined activation sites are **Thr180 and Tyr182**; substitution of either residue caused complete loss of function in vivo. Catalytically inactive Fus3 was still phosphorylated, excluding MAPK autophosphorylation as the principal activation mechanism. Fus3 phosphorylation required STE7 and STE11 genetically, and subsequent biochemical work placed Ste7 directly between Ste11 and Fus3/Kss1. (bardwell1996signalinginthe pages 1-2, gartner1992signaltransductionin pages 1-2, gartner1992signaltransductionin pages 8-10)

Ste7 is therefore a dual-specificity kinase in terms of its substrate reaction, notwithstanding its sequence-level grouping with serine/threonine kinases. This is characteristic of MAP2Ks: Ste7 recognizes folded MAPKs and modifies the two activation-loop residues rather than functioning as a broadly promiscuous peptide kinase.

### Substrate recognition

Ste7 binds Fus3 and Kss1 specifically and with high affinity—approximately **5 nM dissociation constant** for either MAPK. The interaction occurs outside the catalytic active site and persists in extracts lacking Ste5 and Ste11, showing that direct MAPKK–MAPK docking does not require the scaffold or upstream kinase. Other yeast MAPKs, including Hog1 and Mpk1, did not detectably bind under the reported conditions. Mutations disrupting the Ste7 docking region reduced signaling, and biochemical experiments indicate that complex dissociation is needed to permit productive reciprocal phosphorylation. (bardwell1996signalinginthe pages 1-2)

This evidence supports a restricted primary substrate set: **Fus3 and Kss1 are the established direct physiological substrates**. Broader phenotypes should not be interpreted as evidence that Ste7 directly phosphorylates every downstream effector; most transcriptional and morphological effects are mediated through these MAPKs.

## 3. Position in the signaling cascade

The core kinase relationship is:

**Ste20 → Ste11 (MAPKKK) → Ste7 (MAPKK) → Fus3 and/or Kss1 (MAPKs).**

Reconstitution and pathway studies establish Ste11 as the kinase immediately upstream of Ste7. Ste11 phosphorylation activates Ste7; active Ste7 then phosphorylates Fus3 or Kss1. Fus3 and Kss1 can subsequently phosphorylate Ste7 at regulatory sites, creating feedback rather than a strictly one-way linear relay. (maleri2004persistentactivationby pages 1-2, peter2007ste7variantsthat pages 120-125, bardwell1996signalinginthe pages 1-2)

### Mating pathway

Pheromone binding to Ste2 or Ste3 activates the heterotrimeric G-protein system, releasing Ste4–Ste18 (Gβγ). Gβγ recruits Ste5 and associated signaling components to the plasma membrane, where membrane-associated Ste20 activates Ste11. Ste5 binds Ste11, Ste7, and Fus3, organizing the Ste11→Ste7→Fus3 module. Activated Fus3 then controls mating transcription, G1 arrest, polarized “shmoo” growth, and cell fusion through downstream regulators such as Ste12 and Far1. (good2009theste5scaffold pages 1-2, wang2002pheromonedependentubiquitinationof pages 1-2, farley2023effectsofhsp70 pages 1-2)

### Filamentous/invasive-growth pathway

Ste7 and Ste11 are also shared with the filamentous/invasive-growth pathway. Under poor nutrient conditions and other appropriate inputs, Ste7 principally activates **Kss1**, leading through Ste12/Tec1-regulated transcription to invasive growth in haploids and pseudohyphal growth in diploids. Unlike efficient Fus3 activation, Kss1 activation is substantially less dependent on Ste5. Constitutively active Ste7 preferentially produced Kss1 phosphorylation and invasive-growth transcription but failed to reproduce Fus3-dependent mating, experimentally demonstrating that Ste7 activity alone is insufficient to specify the mating fate. (maleri2004persistentactivationby pages 1-2, peter2007ste7variantsthat pages 82-86, good2009theste5scaffold pages 1-2)

## 4. Localization and spatial mechanism

Ste7 is not a membrane-spanning or secreted protein. Its most functionally important localization is **transient recruitment to the cytoplasmic face of the plasma membrane as part of the Ste5 signaling complex** during pheromone stimulation. Ste5 binds Ste7 and the other mating-cascade kinases and is recruited to membrane-associated Gβγ/Ste4. This colocalizes Ste7 with its upstream activator Ste11 and downstream substrate Fus3 at the site of receptor activation. (good2010theroleof pages 184-189, good2009theste5scaffold pages 1-2)

Available evidence supports a dynamic cytosolic/scaffold-associated kinase rather than an enzyme resident in one organelle. In the mating pathway, the plasma membrane is the key activation site; downstream Fus3 and Kss1 transmit the signal to nuclear and cytoplasmic effectors. For invasive-growth signaling, Ste7→Kss1 transmission can occur without the same strict Ste5-dependent membrane organization. Therefore, database labels such as “cytoplasmic” are not wrong but are mechanistically incomplete: **stimulus-dependent scaffold recruitment is the important localization principle**.

## 5. How Ste5 determines Ste7 substrate specificity

A major mechanistic insight is that Ste5 is more than a passive tether. Purified Fus3 is intrinsically a poor Ste7 substrate, whereas Kss1 is intrinsically efficient. A roughly 200-residue Ste5 minimal-scaffold domain, residues 593–786, binds Ste7 with a reported **KD of approximately 75 nM** and increases the **kcat of Ste7-mediated Fus3 phosphorylation by about 5,000-fold**, with little effect on KM. It has essentially no effect on Ste7-mediated Kss1 phosphorylation. (good2009theste5scaffold pages 1-2, good2009theste5scaffold pages 4-6)

The Ste5 domain consequently acts as a **substrate-specific cocatalyst**: it allosterically “unlocks” Fus3 so Ste7 can phosphorylate it efficiently. The structure of this Ste5 domain was solved at **1.6 Å resolution** and displays a VWA-like fold. Direct Ste7–MAPK docking remains necessary, but simple physical proximity is insufficient to explain Fus3 activation. This is the strongest mechanistic explanation for why pheromone-activated, scaffold-bound Ste7 selects Fus3, whereas Ste7 used in the filamentation circuit readily activates Kss1. (good2009theste5scaffold pages 4-6)

## 6. Feedback and post-translational regulation

### MAPK feedback

Activated Fus3 and Kss1 phosphorylate Ste7 at regulatory sites, including multiple N-terminal sites and a site C-terminal to the catalytic domain. These events produce the characteristic hyperphosphorylated Ste7 forms seen after pathway activation and contribute to feedback control and pathway-specific behavior. Constitutively active Ste7 experiments distinguish this feedback phosphorylation from the Ste11-dependent activation-loop phosphorylation needed to turn on Ste7’s catalytic domain. (peter2007ste7variantsthat pages 95-101, maleri2004persistentactivationby pages 1-2)

### Ubiquitination

Pheromone stimulation induces Ste7 polyubiquitination. A screen of **15 ubiquitin-specific protease disruption mutants** identified **Ubp3** as a selective regulator of the pheromone pathway. In ubp3Δ cells, polyubiquitinated Ste7 and free polyubiquitin chains accumulated, while MAPK activity, pheromone-induced transcription, and cell-cycle arrest were enhanced. Epistasis placed the effect downstream of Ste4 and Ste11 but upstream of Ste12, consistent with direct regulation at the Ste7/MAPK tier. (wang2002pheromonedependentubiquitinationof pages 1-2, wang2002pheromonedependentubiquitinationof pages 5-6)

Later work implicated SCF/Cdc4-mediated ubiquitination and a feedback circuit involving Fus3 and Ubp3 in maintaining signal strength and preventing inappropriate Kss1 activation. Thus ubiquitination is not merely bulk protein disposal: it helps determine mating-versus-invasive-growth specificity. (maleri2004persistentactivationby pages 1-2)

A useful abundance estimate from quantitative immunoblotting is approximately **2,000 Ste7 molecules per cell**, compared with approximately **5,000 molecules each of Fus3 and Kss1**. This lower abundance makes Ste7 a potentially limiting and sensitive regulatory node. (wang2002pheromonedependentubiquitinationof pages 5-6)

## 7. Biological consequences of STE7 perturbation

Loss of STE7 interrupts signal flow to both Fus3 and Kss1. In the mating program, this prevents normal pheromone-induced MAPK activation and consequently disrupts transcription, G1 arrest, polarized morphogenesis, and efficient mating. In the filamentation program, loss of Ste7 prevents normal Kss1 activation and invasive/pseudohyphal differentiation. These are pathway-proximal consequences and are more informative for annotation than broad secondary phenotypes.

Conversely, constitutively active Ste7 does not simply activate both outputs equally. Persistent Ste7 activity preferentially supports Kss1-mediated invasive growth and fails to support normal Fus3-dependent mating, emphasizing that signal duration, scaffolding, and substrate conformation—not kinase activity alone—determine fate. (maleri2004persistentactivationby pages 1-2, peter2007ste7variantsthat pages 82-86)

## 8. Recent developments, 2023–2024

Direct molecular characterization of Ste7 itself is mature; the decisive catalytic, docking, and scaffold-mechanism experiments are foundational studies from 1992–2009. Recent work has instead examined how the **Ste7-containing circuit** is maintained, coordinated with other pathways, and engineered. These studies should not be misrepresented as discovering new direct Ste7 substrates.

### Hsp70 control of the Ste5–Ste7 signaling module, 2023

Farley and colleagues reported that the Hsp70 chaperones Ssa1 and Ssa2 support Ste5 abundance, integrity, and localization. Loss of Ssa1/Ssa2 reduced Fus3 and Kss1 activation, FUS1 expression, and shmoo morphogenesis. Ste7 was part of the Ste5-bound kinase module rather than the directly manipulated protein. The work extends the model by showing that scaffold proteostasis controls efficient transmission through the Ste11–Ste7–MAPK cascade. Interestingly, various SSA single, double, and triple mutants retained some mating capability, indicating compensation within the chaperone network. Published **18 October 2023** in *PLOS ONE*; DOI/URL: https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0289339. (farley2023effectsofhsp70 pages 1-2, farley2023effectsofhsp70 pages 7-8)

### Concurrent-signal coordination, 2024

Zhang and colleagues examined mating–HOG pathway cross-talk under pheromone and osmotic stress. The work confirmed the Ste5-directed **Ste11–Ste7–Fus3** circuit as the mating module but principally manipulated HOG branches and Kss1. Sho1-mediated cross-talk depended on Kss1, and Kss1 initiated a transcriptional program distinct from those of Fus3 and Hog1. RNA-seq significance criteria included |log2 fold change| ≥1 and adjusted P≤0.05, and osmotic-response experiments used KCl concentrations up to 550 mM. Published **1 October 2024** in *Molecular Biology of the Cell*; DOI/URL: https://doi.org/10.1091/mbc.e24-06-0270. (zhang2024sharedandredundant pages 4-5, zhang2024sharedandredundant pages 1-2)

These results reinforce the current expert view that shared enzymes do not inevitably cause indiscriminate cross-talk: scaffolds, branch architecture, MAPK identity, localization, and transcriptional decoding jointly insulate outputs.

## 9. Applications and real-world implementation

Ste7 itself is not a clinical target or commercial enzyme. Its principal application is as a mechanistically defined component of a model eukaryotic signaling system.

### Synthetic biology and biosensing

A 2024 engineered-yeast molecular-communication testbed used α-factor-secreting sender cells and mating-responsive receiver cells. Signal transmission necessarily traverses the native mating circuit containing Ste11, Ste7, and Fus3/Kss1, although STE7 was not directly engineered in the reported implementation. The system demonstrated repeated output events at approximately **one event per two hours**, while RNA fluctuations indicated responses in **less than three minutes**. Experiments included repeated 10 μM α-factor pulses and 1:1 sender-to-receiver cocultures. The authors propose this platform for yeast biosensors and interfaces to bio-nano communication networks. Published January 2024; DOI/URL: https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2401.13712. (ntetsikas2024engineeringyeastcells pages 1-2, ntetsikas2024engineeringyeastcells pages 13-15)

### Signaling-circuit engineering

Because Ste7 is a limiting, shared MAPKK with well-defined docking and scaffold interactions, it is a useful control point for engineering pathway gain, specificity, or cross-talk. In practice, however, altering Ste5 recruitment, MAPK docking, pheromone degradation, or transcriptional output may offer more selective control than simply overexpressing or constitutively activating STE7; persistent active Ste7 biases cells toward Kss1/invasive-growth signaling rather than faithfully reproducing mating. (maleri2004persistentactivationby pages 1-2, good2009theste5scaffold pages 4-6)

### Broader translational relevance

The Ste5–Ste11–Ste7–Fus3 system is an authoritative model for understanding mammalian scaffold-regulated RAF–MEK–ERK signaling. The most transferable principle is that scaffolds can control enzyme specificity allosterically, not only through colocalization. Extrapolation to human kinases should nevertheless remain conceptual: Ste7/P06784 is a yeast protein, and the cited experiments do not establish it as a human disease target.

## 10. Evidence assessment and remaining uncertainties

The highest-confidence annotations are:

1. **Molecular function:** dual-specificity MAPKK using ATP to activate Fus3 and Kss1.
2. **Direct substrates:** Fus3 and Kss1, with activation-loop threonine/tyrosine phosphorylation.
3. **Upstream activator:** Ste11.
4. **Mating localization:** Ste5-dependent recruitment of the kinase module to the plasma membrane.
5. **Pathway roles:** Fus3-centered mating and Kss1-centered invasive/filamentous growth.

The principal uncertainty is not Ste7’s core function, which is well established, but the complete state-resolved map of its regulatory modifications and complexes in living cells. Recent studies increasingly treat Ste7 as part of an integrated signaling network rather than revisiting its basic enzymology. Consequently, older primary biochemical studies remain the authoritative sources for catalytic mechanism, while 2023–2024 publications chiefly update network context and applications.
