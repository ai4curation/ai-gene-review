Gene Description

Met10 is the alpha (flavoprotein) subunit of the assimilatory NADPH-sulfite reductase of Saccharomyces cerevisiae (EC 1.8.1.2), a cytoplasmic alpha2beta2 hemoflavoprotein in which Met10 pairs with the siroheme/[4Fe-4S] beta subunit Met5. The enzyme carries out the six-electron reduction of sulfite to hydrogen sulfide, the last reductive step of sulfate assimilation, which supplies the sulfide that is built into homocysteine and from there into methionine, cysteine and glutathione. Met10 has the C-terminal FAD- and NADPH-binding ferredoxin-NADP reductase-like module that takes electrons from NADPH. Its N-terminal region resembles pyruvate-flavodoxin oxidoreductase, and it lacks the flavodoxin-like FMN domain of bacterial CysJ (that domain is found on Met5 in yeast). met10 mutants are methionine auxotrophs, and partial loss-of-function alleles strongly reduce H2S release by wine yeasts during fermentation. Assignment of FMN to the beta subunit is supported by flavodoxin-domain architecture and holoenzyme cofactor content, rather than a direct isolated-subunit FMN assay.

MET10 (YFR030W, P39692) notes

Evidence journal (UniProt and cached publications).

  • NADPH-sulfite reductase is an alpha2beta2 hemoflavoprotein PMID:6751400; it contains 2 FAD, 2 FMN and 2 hemes per molecule PMID:6751400.
  • MET10 encodes the alpha (flavoprotein) subunit, with FAD/NADPH motifs but no obvious FMN motif PMID:7928966.
  • Domain check (UniProt DR lines): Met10 has Pfam FAD_binding_1 and NAD_binding_1 plus a PFOR-like N-terminal region (IPR002869) and no flavodoxin domain. Met5 has Pfam Flavodoxin_1 (PF00258) [UniProt:P47169]. The FMN domain sits on the beta subunit in yeast, unlike E. coli CysJ. The IBA for FMN binding on Met10 was therefore REMOVED.
  • In vivo: MET10/MET5 point mutants cut wine H2S by 50-99% PMID:19236486.
  • Mms19 (CIA targeting) co-purification PMID:22678362: probably maturation of the SiR complex. Protein-binding rows REMOVED as uninformative.
