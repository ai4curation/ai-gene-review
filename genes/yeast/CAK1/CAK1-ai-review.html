Gene Description

CAK1 (also CIV1) encodes the CDK-activating kinase of Saccharomyces cerevisiae, an essential 368-residue serine/threonine protein kinase that phosphorylates the activation-loop (T-loop) threonine of cyclin-dependent kinases. Unlike the metazoan and fission-yeast CAK (CDK7-cyclin H-MAT1; Mcs6-Mcs2), Cak1 is only distantly related to CDKs, lacks the canonical glycine-rich ATP-binding loop, is active as a monomer without a cyclin or activating phosphorylation, has full activity when expressed in bacteria and is not a subunit of TFIIH; it represents a fungal-specific solution to CDK activation. Its principal substrate is Cdc28 (Cdk1), which it phosphorylates on Thr169, preferentially as monomeric CDK before cyclin binding; this modification is required for Cdc28 kinase activity, so loss of Cak1 lowers Cdc28 activity and delays cells in G2 (with a substantial G1 fraction), and CAK1 is essential unless CDC28 carries T-loop-bypassing hyperactive substitutions. Cak1 also activates the transcriptional CDKs by the same mechanism: Kin28 (Thr162, the TFIIH kinase that phosphorylates the RNA polymerase II CTD on Ser5), Bur1 (Thr240) and Ctk1 (Thr338, CTD Ser2 kinase), and through Ctk1 it feeds the Set2/Rpd3C(S) pathway that suppresses cryptic intragenic transcription initiation. It additionally has Cdc28-independent, catalysis-dependent roles in sporulation, being required for timely premeiotic DNA replication and sporulation-gene expression and for activation of the Smk1 MAP kinase that drives spore wall assembly, although its direct meiotic targets are not identified. Cak1 abundance and activity are constant through the mitotic cell cycle, decline in stationary phase and oscillate during meiosis; the protein is dispersed throughout the cell with the largest pool in the cytoplasm.

1. Identity verification and molecular classification

Two independent discovery studies in 1996 converged on the same protein. Purified yeast CAK activity contained a 43–44-kDa polypeptide, peptide sequences matched YFL029C, and the predicted molecular mass was approximately 42.2 kDa. Recombinant protein expressed in bacteria was catalytically active, while immunodepletion removed more than 97% of detectable CAK activity from yeast lysate. These observations establish YFL029C/CAK1/CIV1—not a similarly named gene from another species—as the principal budding-yeast CAK. [Kaldis, Sutton & Solomon, August 1996, Cell, DOI/URL: https://doi.org/10.1016/S0092-8674(00)80129-4; Espinoza et al., 20 September 1996, Science, DOI/URL: https://doi.org/10.1126/science.273.5282.1714]. (kaldis1996thecdkactivatingkinase pages 2-4, espinoza1996acyclindependentkinaseactivating pages 3-3)

Cak1 belongs to the protein-kinase superfamily but is exceptionally divergent. It has only approximately 20–25% sequence identity to conventional Cdk7-type CAKs, lacks the canonical subdomain-I GxGxxG glycine-rich nucleotide-binding motif, and substitutes several otherwise highly conserved kinase residues. Nevertheless, ATP-dependent phosphorylation assays and recombinant activity establish it as a genuine protein kinase. Its activity as an apparently monomeric single polypeptide agrees with the supplied kinase-like and protein-kinase domain calls, while distinguishing it from metazoan Cdk7–cyclin H–MAT1 CAK. (kaldis1996thecdkactivatingkinase pages 2-4, kaldis1999thecdkactivatingkinase pages 1-2, espinoza1996acyclindependentkinaseactivating pages 3-3)

2. Primary catalytic function and reaction

The reaction is the standard protein-kinase phosphotransfer:

ATP + CDK T-loop threonine → ADP + phospho-CDK T-loop threonine.

Radiolabeled γ-³²P-ATP experiments showed direct incorporation of phosphate into CDK substrates. Cak1 phosphorylated kinase-dead CDK2, excluding substrate autophosphorylation, but failed to phosphorylate CDK2-T160A or T160E activation-site mutants. Equivalent genetic and biochemical evidence identifies Cdc28 Thr169 as the physiological budding-yeast site. Phosphorylation rearranges/stabilizes the CDK activation segment and permits high catalytic activity after cyclin association. (kaldis1996thecdkactivatingkinase pages 2-4, kaldis1999thecdkactivatingkinase pages 1-2, espinoza1996acyclindependentkinaseactivating pages 3-3, kaldis1998humanandyeast pages 1-2)

Substrate specificity

Cak1 is not a broadly indiscriminate kinase. Its defining specificity is for activation-loop threonines in selected CDKs, with an experimentally demonstrated preference for free or monomeric CDKs. In comparative assays, yeast Cak1 preferentially phosphorylated monomeric CDKs, whereas human Cdk7-containing CAK favored cyclin-bound complexes. Cak1 can phosphorylate Cdc28 without cyclin, although robust downstream Cdc28 activity requires cyclin such as Clb2. This supports a maturation pathway in which Cak1 phosphorylation commonly precedes cyclin binding. [Kaldis et al., September 1998, Molecular Biology of the Cell, DOI/URL: https://doi.org/10.1091/mbc.9.9.2545]. (kaldis1998humanandyeast pages 1-2, espinoza1998theactivationof pages 72-76)

Cak1 is itself active without an associated cyclin. Gel filtration, biochemical purification, immunoprecipitation, and bacterial expression all support an active monomer rather than a conventional CDK–cyclin complex. This is a central functional distinction: Cak1 activates CDKs but is not regulated like an ordinary cyclin-dependent kinase. (kaldis1996thecdkactivatingkinase pages 2-4, espinoza1996acyclindependentkinaseactivating pages 3-3)

3. Direct physiological substrates and pathways

3.1 Cdc28/Cdk1: core cell-cycle pathway

The dominant cell-cycle substrate is Cdc28, the principal CDK that drives budding-yeast cell-cycle transitions. Cak1 directly phosphorylates Thr169 in the Cdc28 T-loop. Conditional cak1 mutants lose Thr169 phosphorylation, CAK activity, and Cdc28 kinase activity, while purified or recombinant Cak1 phosphorylates and activates Cdc28 in vitro. This activating modification is required for normal Cdc28 activity and viability. (kaldis1999thecdkactivatingkinase pages 1-2, kaldis1998humanandyeast pages 1-2, kaldis1996thecdkactivatingkinase pages 8-9)

Because Cdc28 acts with different cyclins across the cycle, Cak1 lies upstream of both G1/S and G2/M progression rather than acting as a phase-specific cyclin signal. Conditional mutants arrest heterogeneously, including predominant G2/M arrest and a substantial G1 fraction; in one analysis approximately 35% arrested in G1. The delayed arrest—about 5–7 hours after temperature shift despite faster Cak1 inactivation—is consistent with persistence of previously phosphorylated Cdc28 and dilution or turnover over subsequent divisions. (kaldis1996thecdkactivatingkinase pages 8-9)

A later study also reported a phosphorylation-independent genetic role: elevated Cak1 could suppress phenotypes of certain Cdc28 C-terminal mutants even when Cak1 catalytic activity was compromised. This suggests a possible physical or chaperone-like contribution to Cdc28 maturation, but it is subsidiary to—and less firmly established than—the essential Thr169-phosphorylation mechanism. [Kim et al., 15 November 2009, Gene, DOI/URL: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.gene.2009.07.016]. (kim2009aphosphorylationindependentrole pages 1-3)

3.2 Kin28: transcription initiation/TFIIH pathway

Cak1 also activates Kin28, the budding-yeast Cdk7-related kinase associated with TFIIH. Conditional cak1 mutations reduce Kin28 phosphorylation and activity in vivo; coexpression of Cak1 with Kin28 and its regulatory partners Ccl1 and Tfb3 produces active, phosphorylated Kin28. The relevant T-loop context has been tested through a Kin28-T162A allele. Genetic interactions between CAK1 and KIN28 further support physiological coupling. In contrast, Pho85 and Srb10 activities were not detectably dependent on Cak1, demonstrating selectivity among yeast CDKs. [Espinoza et al., November 1998, Molecular and Cellular Biology, DOI/URL: https://doi.org/10.1128/MCB.18.11.6365]. (espinoza1998cak1isrequired pages 1-1, yao2002activationofthe pages 1-2)

Through Kin28, Cak1 contributes indirectly to RNA polymerase II transcription initiation and promoter-associated CTD phosphorylation. Cak1 itself is not a TFIIH subunit and lacks the direct RNA-polymerase-II CTD kinase activity characteristic of Cdk7-containing metazoan CAK. (kaldis1998humanandyeast pages 1-2)

3.3 Bur1–Bur2: transcription elongation pathway

Cak1 phosphorylates the activation-loop residue Thr240 of Bur1, the catalytic component of the Bur1–Bur2 transcriptional CDK complex. Recombinant Cak1 stimulates Bur1–Bur2-associated CTD kinase activity. Bur1-T240 substitution and isolation of Bur1 from a cak1 mutant each cause a comparable loss of detectable in-vitro activity, supporting a direct activation mechanism. [Yao & Prelich, October 2002, Molecular and Cellular Biology, DOI/URL: https://doi.org/10.1128/MCB.22.19.6750-6758.2002]. (yao2002activationofthe pages 1-1, yao2002activationofthe pages 7-8)

The phenotype is nuanced: Bur1-T240A and T240E strains remain viable and grow relatively well, whereas deletion of BUR1 or mutation of its catalytic Asp213 is lethal. T-loop phosphorylation is therefore important for maximal Bur1 catalytic output but is not absolutely required for every essential in-vivo Bur1 function; residual activity, cellular cofactors, or pathway compensation may explain this difference. (yao2002activationofthe pages 7-8)

3.4 Ctk1/CTDK-I: elongation, CTD Ser2 phosphorylation, and nutrient adaptation

Cak1 directly phosphorylates Ctk1 Thr338, the conserved T-loop residue of the CTDK-I catalytic subunit. Wild-type Ctk1 efficiently phosphorylates an RNA-polymerase-II CTD peptide, whereas Ctk1-T338A has near-background activity comparable to a catalytic mutant. Ctk1 from cak1 cells is unphosphorylated and inactive in vitro; reintroduction of CAK1 restores activity, and recombinant Cak1 directly phosphorylates wild-type Ctk1 but not Ctk1-T338A. Kin28 and Bur1 mutants do not abolish Ctk1 activity, arguing against their serving as obligatory intermediates. [Ostapenko & Solomon, May 2005, Molecular and Cellular Biology, DOI/URL: https://doi.org/10.1128/MCB.25.10.3906-3913.2005]. (ostapenko2005phosphorylationbycak1 pages 3-4, ostapenko2005phosphorylationbycak1 pages 1-2, ostapenko2005phosphorylationbycak1 pages 4-5)

Ctk1-T338A still assembles with Ctk2/Ctk3, so its catalytic defect is not simply loss of the kinase complex. Functionally, Cak1-dependent Ctk1 activation supports RNA polymerase II CTD Ser2 phosphorylation, transcriptional elongation and 3′-end processing. The defect is condition-sensitive: T338A cells grow nearly normally at 30°C but poorly at 17°C, fail to produce the normal rise in CTD Ser2 phosphorylation during the diauxic shift, enter stationary phase inefficiently, and show impaired activation of genes including CTT1 and SPI1. (ostapenko2005phosphorylationbycak1 pages 1-2, ostapenko2005phosphorylationbycak1 pages 4-5, ostapenko2005phosphorylationbycak1 pages 2-3)

4. Cellular localization and temporal regulation

Older localization work and subsequent authoritative synthesis describe Cak1 as predominantly cytoplasmic. This fits its preference for phosphorylation of monomeric Cdc28 before cyclin assembly. However, the retrieved localization evidence is less detailed than the substrate biochemistry, and cytoplasmic enrichment should not be interpreted as strict nuclear exclusion: Cak1 functionally activates Kin28, Bur1, and Ctk1, whose principal actions occur in nuclear transcription. Transient substrate encounters, shuttling, or phosphorylation before assembly into nuclear complexes remain compatible with the evidence. (kaldis1999thecdkactivatingkinase pages 1-2)

Cak1 abundance and measured CAK activity are broadly constant through G1, S phase, and mitosis. Thus, Cak1 is best understood as a constitutive CDK-maturation enzyme rather than an oscillatory cell-cycle switch. Cell-cycle timing is generated mainly downstream through cyclin availability, inhibitory phosphorylation, phosphatases, localization, and substrate access. (espinoza1998theactivationof pages 72-76)

5. Essentiality and quantitative evidence

  • CAK1 deletion is lethal. Null spores terminate as heterogeneous microcolonies of approximately 8–12 cells, rather than arresting uniformly at one morphology. One analysis found 21% unbudded, 5% small-budded, and 33% large-budded cells, with the remainder abnormal. This heterogeneity is expected because Cak1 supports both the central Cdc28 engine and transcriptional CDKs. (espinoza1998theactivationof pages 72-76, espinoza1996acyclindependentkinaseactivating pages 3-3)
  • Immunodepletion removes >97% of lysate CAK activity, demonstrating that Cak1 accounts for essentially all readily detectable budding-yeast CAK activity. (espinoza1998theactivationof pages 72-76, espinoza1996acyclindependentkinaseactivating pages 3-3)
  • The original purification achieved >300,000-fold enrichment at approximately 4% yield, with the principal active species migrating at about 43 kDa by SDS-PAGE. (kaldis1996thecdkactivatingkinase pages 2-4)
  • Conditional loss causes severe depletion of Cdc28 Thr169 phosphorylation and activity, with G2/M and G1 arrest populations rather than a single terminal state. (kaldis1998humanandyeast pages 1-2, kaldis1996thecdkactivatingkinase pages 8-9)

6. Structural and evolutionary interpretation

Cak1 represents a distinct monomeric CAK solution found principally among fungi and some plant lineages. Its lack of the conventional glycine-rich loop and unusual conserved-residue substitutions show that canonical motif inspection alone can underestimate kinase function. Active bacterial recombinant protein demonstrates that neither a cyclin nor a eukaryote-specific activating modification is obligatorily required for basal catalysis. The strongest structural-functional inference is therefore that Cak1 has evolved a noncanonical ATP-binding pocket while retaining the bilobal protein-kinase fold represented by the supplied PF00069/IPR000719 annotations. (kaldis1996thecdkactivatingkinase pages 2-4, kaldis1999thecdkactivatingkinase pages 1-2)

Its preference for unbound CDKs is biologically coherent: Cak1 acts as a CDK-maturation kinase, installing the activating phosphate before or during assembly of cell-cycle and transcription complexes. This division of labor contrasts with metazoan Cdk7, which is itself a cyclin-associated CDK and participates directly in TFIIH. (kaldis1998humanandyeast pages 1-2)

7. Current applications and recent research status

Experimental applications

Cak1 is used principally as a mechanistic tool rather than as an industrial enzyme or approved therapeutic target. Conditional cak1 alleles, activation-loop mutants, and recombinant Cak1 assays allow investigators to separate CDK activation from cyclin binding and to perturb several CDK modules from one upstream node. This has been especially useful for distinguishing direct T-loop regulation from secondary effects on cell-cycle progression or transcription. (espinoza1998cak1isrequired pages 1-1, yao2002activationofthe pages 7-8, ostapenko2005phosphorylationbycak1 pages 4-5)

The unusual fungal-type ATP pocket and absence of a close human equivalent have also motivated the expert proposal that Cak1-like kinases could provide fungal-selective inhibitor targets. This remains a target-discovery concept, not a demonstrated clinical or agricultural implementation for S. cerevisiae Cak1. Any inhibitor program would need to establish conservation in the pathogen of interest, cellular target engagement, selectivity, and whether the pathogen’s CAK1 ortholog is essential under infection conditions.

2023–2024 literature assessment

A targeted search of 2023–2024 literature did not identify a primary study that materially revises the core functional annotation of S288c Cak1/P43568. Recent papers mentioning “Cak1” frequently concern orthologs in other fungi, broad kinase screens, cell-cycle reviews, or high-throughput yeast interaction resources. Those sources were not used to transfer organism-specific phenotypes onto P43568. Accordingly, the most precise evidence remains the foundational 1996–2005 biochemical and genetic literature rather than a newer omics association.

This absence of recent revision is itself informative: the direct assignments—Cdc28 Thr169, Kin28 activation, Bur1 Thr240, and Ctk1 Thr338—are mature mechanistic conclusions. The major remaining uncertainties concern quantitative in-vivo flux through each substrate, dynamic compartmentalization, complete structural characterization of substrate recognition, and whether non-CDK substrates exist.

8. Confidence-ranked functional annotation

High-confidence primary annotation: Cak1 is an essential, monomeric CDK-activating serine/threonine kinase that transfers phosphate from ATP to activation-loop threonines of selected CDKs, most importantly Cdc28 Thr169. (kaldis1996thecdkactivatingkinase pages 2-4, kaldis1999thecdkactivatingkinase pages 1-2, kaldis1998humanandyeast pages 1-2)

High-confidence pathway annotation: It activates the core Cdc28 cell-cycle engine and the transcriptional CDKs Kin28, Bur1, and Ctk1, thereby influencing cell-cycle transitions, RNA polymerase II initiation/elongation, CTD Ser2 phosphorylation, and transcriptional adaptation to nutrient depletion. (espinoza1998cak1isrequired pages 1-1, yao2002activationofthe pages 7-8, ostapenko2005phosphorylationbycak1 pages 1-2, ostapenko2005phosphorylationbycak1 pages 4-5)

Moderate-confidence localization annotation: A major Cak1 pool is cytoplasmic, but this should not be represented as exclusive localization because its direct physiological substrate set includes nuclear transcriptional kinases. (kaldis1999thecdkactivatingkinase pages 1-2)

Not supported: Cak1 should not be annotated as a TFIIH CTD kinase, a transporter, a structural protein, or a conventional cyclin-dependent kinase requiring its own cyclin. It is upstream of those CDK complexes and has no demonstrated direct RNA-polymerase-II CTD kinase activity. (espinoza1996acyclindependentkinaseactivating pages 3-3, kaldis1998humanandyeast pages 1-2)

What Cak1 is

  • Three groups independently purified/cloned the budding-yeast CAK in 1996. It is a
    monomer, active without a cyclin, active in E. coli, and not part of TFIIH
    PMID:8752210; it is "most similar in sequence to the Cdks, but
    unlike them is active as a monomer" PMID:8752211; and it is "an unusual
    44-kilodalton protein kinase, Cak1, that is only distantly related to CDKs"
    PMID:8781234.
  • Essential: "The CIV1 gene is essential for yeast cell viability" PMID:8752211;
    "The CAK1 gene was essential for cell viability." PMID:8781234.
  • Activity is not cell-cycle regulated: "Cak1 accounted for most CAK activity in yeast
    cell lysates, and its activity was constant throughout the cell cycle." PMID:8781234.
    So Cak1 is a constitutive CDK-maturation step, not a timed switch; the timing of
    Cdc28 activity comes from cyclins, Swe1/Mih1, Sic1 etc.
  • Localisation: "Cak1p is dispersed throughout the cell as shown by
    immunofluorescence; biochemical subcellular fractionation confirmed that most of the
    Cak1p is found in the cytoplasm." PMID:9819350. Stable in exponential growth,
    drops in stationary phase, oscillates in meiosis PMID:9819350.
  • Substrate preference (deep research, from Kaldis et al. 1998 MBoC, not cached):
    "yeast Cak1 preferentially phosphorylated monomeric CDKs, whereas human
    Cdk7-containing CAK favored cyclin-bound complexes" - consistent with a cytoplasmic
    pool acting on Cdc28 before cyclin binding and nuclear import.
