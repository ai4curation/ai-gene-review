Gene Description

Aminomethyltransferase (glycine cleavage system T-protein, EC 2.1.2.10) of Saccharomyces cerevisiae. Gcv1 is the T subunit of the mitochondrial glycine decarboxylase complex (P, H, T and L proteins: Gcv2, Gcv3, Gcv1 and Lpd1). It accepts the aminomethyl group carried on the lipoyl arm of the H-protein and transfers the methylene unit to tetrahydrofolate, releasing ammonia and forming 5,10-methylenetetrahydrofolate. Glycine cleavage lets yeast use glycine as sole nitrogen source and supplies mitochondrial one-carbon units; GCV1 expression is induced by glycine and responds to the cytoplasmic level of 5,10-methylenetetrahydrofolate.

Core Functions

T-protein of the mitochondrial glycine cleavage system: transfers the aminomethyl group carried on the lipoyl arm of the H-protein (Gcv3) to tetrahydrofolate, releasing NH3 and forming 5,10-methylene-THF (EC 2.1.2.10), a major source of mitochondrial one-carbon units.
