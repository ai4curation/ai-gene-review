Gene Description

Vma1p is the catalytic A subunit of the Saccharomyces cerevisiae V-type H+-ATPase. The precursor is translated as a 1071-amino-acid protein in which the PI-SceI/VDE intein interrupts the A subunit; protein splicing excises the homing endonuclease and ligates the N- and C-terminal exteins to produce the mature V1 catalytic subunit. Mature Vma1p hydrolyzes ATP in the peripheral V1 sector of vacuolar and Golgi/endosomal V-ATPase complexes, driving rotary proton translocation through the membrane V0 sector to acidify the fungal vacuole and other endomembrane compartments.

VMA1 review notes

Scope

VMA1 / TFP1 encodes the catalytic A subunit of the S. cerevisiae
V-type H+-ATPase. The P17255 primary translation product is unusual: a
PI-SceI/VDE homing-endonuclease intein interrupts the V-ATPase A subunit, and
protein splicing joins the N- and C-terminal exteins to produce the mature
69-kDa V1 catalytic subunit [PMID:2139027, PMID:2146742].

The review therefore separates two real activities that are present in the same
translated precursor:

  • mature Vma1p: ATP hydrolysis in the peripheral V1 motor, coupled to VO proton
    translocation in vacuolar and Golgi/endosomal V-ATPase holoenzymes;
  • PI-SceI/VDE: the excised intein endonuclease, which accounts for the
    InterPro-derived DNA-binding, endonuclease, and intein-protein-splicing rows.
    This is now recorded explicitly as the UniProt chain PRO_0000002459.

PAINT/IBA rows

The PTHR43607 PAINT rows fit VMA1 and do not need pruning.

  • PTN000389800 is the PTHR43607 root call for GO:0046961 proton-transporting ATPase activity, rotational mechanism and GO:1902600 proton transmembrane transport. Yeast Vma1p is the catalytic A subunit of a
    rotary V-ATPase, and there is no evidence of subactivity loss.
  • PTN000389802 is the eukaryotic node for GO:0000221 vacuolar proton-transporting V-type ATPase, V1 domain. This is directly supported by
    yeast V1/V1VO structural and biochemical work.
  • PTN001032540 is the fungal node for GO:0000329 fungal-type vacuole membrane, seeded by Candida albicans TFP1 and S. cerevisiae VMA1; this
    is correct for the Vph1-containing yeast V-ATPase.
