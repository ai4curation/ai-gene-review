Gene Description

Gcd6 is the epsilon subunit of eukaryotic translation initiation factor 2B (eIF2B), the guanine nucleotide exchange factor that converts eIF2-GDP into eIF2-GTP so that eIF2 can bind initiator Met-tRNAi and support another round of translation initiation. eIF2B is a cytosolic decamer of two copies each of Gcn3 (alpha), Gcd7 (beta), Gcd1 (gamma), Gcd2 (delta) and Gcd6 (epsilon); Gcd1 and Gcd6 form the catalytic subcomplex and Gcd6 carries the exchange activity in its C-terminal region (residues 518-712, including the W2 domain that also binds eIF2), while its N-terminal region couples catalysis to assembly of the full complex. GCD6 is essential. When Gcn2 phosphorylates eIF2alpha, phosphorylated eIF2 binds the regulatory subcomplex and blocks productive engagement with the catalytic subunits, lowering ternary complex. This reduces general translation and lets ribosomes bypass the inhibitory uORFs of GCN4 mRNA; reduced-function gcd6 alleles mimic this and derepress GCN4, which is how the gene was first identified. The human ortholog EIF2B5 is mutated in leukoencephalopathy with vanishing white matter.

Core Functions

Catalytic subunit of the eIF2B guanine nucleotide exchange factor. Gcd6 binds eIF2 and catalyzes GDP-GTP exchange on it, regenerating eIF2-GTP for ternary complex formation in each round of cytoplasmic translation initiation.
