# Functional-annotation report: *Saccharomyces cerevisiae* **SLN1** (UniProt P39928)

## Executive summary

The supplied identity is internally consistent and matches the literature: **SLN1**, also called **YPD2** and ordered locus **YIL147C**, encodes the S288c budding-yeast protein Sln1p, a plasma-membrane **hybrid sensor histidine kinase**. Its architecture—an extracellular region between two transmembrane segments, a cytosolic HAMP/coiled-coil signaling region, a HisKA/HATPase transmitter, and a C-terminal CheY-like receiver—agrees with the domains listed for UniProt P39928. No evidence from a different same-symbol gene was used. Proteins called Sln1 in *Candida*, *Aspergillus*, and plant-pathogenic fungi are homologs, not P39928, and their phenotypes cannot automatically be assigned to budding-yeast Sln1.

Sln1’s primary biochemical function is ATP-dependent protein histidine kinase signaling. In a membrane dimer, Sln1 uses ATP to autophosphorylate **His576**, apparently in trans, and transfers the phosphoryl group to its own receiver **Asp1144**. Phosphate then passes to **Ypd1-His64** and from Ypd1 to either **Ssk1-Asp554** or **Skn7-Asp427**. Thus, Sln1 does not phosphorylate a conventional soluble metabolite; its physiological substrates are residues within an ordered protein phosphorelay. Direct transfer from Sln1-His576 to Ypd1 is not supported—the intervening Sln1-Asp1144 step is required (posas1996yeasthog1map pages 4-6, posas1996yeasthog1map pages 1-2).

Under ordinary osmolarity, active Sln1 keeps Ssk1 phosphorylated and thereby represses the Ssk2/Ssk22–Pbs2–Hog1 MAPK cascade. Hyperosmotic shock inhibits Sln1 activity, allowing dephosphorylated Ssk1 to activate that cascade and promote glycerol-centered osmoadaptation. A parallel Sln1–Ypd1–Skn7 branch controls hypo-osmotic/cell-wall transcription, including **OCH1**. The strongest mechanistic evidence remains the foundational biochemical and genetic literature; 2023–2024 work chiefly refines the evolutionary classification and broader significance of fungal histidine kinases rather than changing this core model.

## 1. Identity verification and molecular architecture

### Required verification

1. **Symbol and description:** The literature’s *S. cerevisiae* Sln1p is the osmosensing, transmembrane hybrid histidine kinase specified in the prompt. It is not a conventional Ser/Thr or Tyr kinase.
2. **Organism:** The findings summarized as direct evidence concern budding yeast, *Saccharomyces cerevisiae*, consistent with strain S288c and locus YIL147C. Results from other fungi are explicitly labeled contextual.
3. **Domain agreement:** The literature describes an N-terminal extracellular sensor region bounded by two predicted transmembrane helices, followed by cytoplasmic signaling/transmitter and receiver modules. This matches the supplied HAMP, His-kinase, HATPase, and CheY-like annotations (xu2001thestructureand pages 35-38, posas1996yeasthog1map pages 1-2).
4. **No symbol substitution:** Recent papers use “Sln1” for homologs in other fungi. Those proteins differ in sequence, physiological context, and sometimes pathway organization; they are not UniProt P39928.

Sln1 is a **hybrid** histidine kinase because one polypeptide contains both the transmitter and first response-regulator receiver. Functional dissection showed that a construct ending near residue 1070 retained the transmitter but lacked the C-terminal receiver, while signaling required receiver Asp1144. Ypd1 interacts with the Sln1 receiver region, consistent with a modular transmitter→receiver→HPt relay rather than direct kinase-to-Ypd1 transfer (posas1996yeasthog1map pages 3-4).

The HAMP/coiled-coil region couples the transmembrane input to the cytosolic kinase. The HATPase module binds ATP and supports phosphoryl transfer to the conserved histidine in the dimerization/histidine-phosphotransfer region. The C-terminal CheY-like domain contains the conserved receiver aspartate. This architecture is characteristic of fungal hybrid histidine kinases, whose added receiver domain distinguishes their multistep relays from many canonical bacterial two-component systems (mina2024structureanddistribution pages 1-2).

## 2. Cellular localization and spatial signaling

Sln1 is an integral **plasma-membrane protein** functioning as a membrane-bound dimer. Its extracellular N-terminal region lies between two transmembrane helices, whereas its HAMP/transmitter and receiver machinery is cytoplasmic. Consequently, ATP utilization and all phosphotransfer reactions occur on the cytoplasmic side of the membrane (lu2003saccharomycescerevisiaehistidine pages 1-3, xu2001thestructureand pages 35-38, dexter2015robustnetworkstructure pages 1-2).

The downstream topology is compartmentalized. Ssk1 acts in the cytoplasmic HOG cascade, whereas Skn7 is a nuclear transcription factor. Ypd1 is found in both cytosol and nucleus and shuttles between them, providing a physical means to distribute the membrane-generated Sln1 signal to both response regulators (lu2003saccharomycescerevisiaehistidine pages 1-3). Sln1 itself should therefore be annotated primarily to the **plasma membrane**, not to the nucleus; nuclear signaling is mediated by mobile Ypd1 and nuclear Skn7.

## 3. Catalytic reaction and substrate specificity

A useful reaction scheme is:

**ATP + Sln1-His576 → ADP + Sln1-His576~P**  
**Sln1-His576~P + Sln1-Asp1144 → Sln1-His576 + Sln1-Asp1144~P**  
**Sln1-Asp1144~P + Ypd1-His64 → Sln1-Asp1144 + Ypd1-His64~P**

Ypd1 subsequently donates phosphate to Ssk1-Asp554 or Skn7-Asp427. The first step is reported to occur in trans within the Sln1 dimer; the His→Asp step is intramolecular in the standard model (kaserer2009effectofosmolytes pages 43-49).

Direct biochemical support includes incorporation of phosphate from [γ-³²P]ATP by a purified Sln1 transmitter construct. H576Q abolished this autophosphorylation. Approximately half of the radiolabel transferred to the receiver construct within 15 minutes, and transfer required Asp1144. Differential acid/base sensitivity identified phosphohistidine on the transmitter and phosphoaspartate on the receiver. H576Q or D1144N also abolished Ypd1 phosphorylation in vivo, and no direct H576→Ypd1-H64 shortcut was detected (posas1996yeasthog1map pages 4-6).

Accordingly, substrate specificity should be described at two levels:

- **Nucleotide donor:** ATP; the available studies establish ATP-dependent autophosphorylation but do not provide a modern, comprehensive nucleotide-selectivity or Michaelis–Menten analysis.
- **Protein phosphoacceptors:** Sln1 His576 first, then its cognate receiver Asp1144, followed specifically by Ypd1 His64. Physiological output specificity is subsequently conferred by Ypd1 interactions with Ssk1 and Skn7.

EC **2.7.13.3**, protein histidine kinase, is therefore appropriate. Sln1 should not be annotated as a tyrosine phosphatase despite the historical synonym “tyrosine phosphatase-dependent protein 2”; that name reflects genetic context rather than Sln1’s catalytic chemistry.

## 4. Biological pathway and signaling logic

### 4.1 Sln1–Ypd1–Ssk1 branch: negative control of HOG signaling

At normal osmolarity, Sln1 is active and maintains the relay:

**Sln1-H576 → Sln1-D1144 → Ypd1-H64 → Ssk1-D554.**

Phosphorylated Ssk1 does not activate the functionally overlapping MAPKKKs Ssk2 and Ssk22. Pbs2 and Hog1 therefore remain relatively inactive. Hyperosmotic stress suppresses Sln1 kinase activity, the supply of phosphoryl groups declines, and unphosphorylated Ssk1 binds and activates Ssk2/Ssk22. These activate Pbs2 and then Hog1, leading to transcriptional and metabolic adaptation, notably glycerol production and accumulation (posas1996yeasthog1map pages 1-2, dexter2015robustnetworkstructure pages 1-2).

The model is supported by several convergent experiments:

- Sln1-dependent phosphorylation was strongly reduced after exposure to **0.4 M NaCl** (posas1996yeasthog1map pages 4-6).
- Loss of SLN1 or YPD1 causes lethal or severely toxic constitutive HOG signaling; deletion of downstream **SSK1, SSK2, PBS2, or HOG1**, or increased tyrosine-phosphatase activity, suppresses that phenotype (posas1996yeasthog1map pages 3-4).
- Sln1 H576 and D1144 substitutions interrupt the ordered relay (posas1996yeasthog1map pages 4-6).
- Dephosphorylated, rather than phosphorylated, Ssk1 is the active MAPKKK regulator.

Sln1 is thus an unusual stress sensor whose kinase is relatively active in the **absence** of hyperosmotic stress. Stress is represented as loss of inhibitory phosphorelay output—a release-of-inhibition circuit.

### 4.2 Sln1–Ypd1–Skn7 branch

Ypd1 also transfers phosphate to nuclear Skn7 at Asp427. Increased Sln1 activity, associated especially with hypo-osmotic signaling or activating *sln1* alleles, promotes Skn7-dependent transcription. Skn7 contains a receiver domain and an HSF-like DNA-binding region, making it a response regulator directly coupled to transcription (li1998theyeasthistidine pages 1-2, li2002theeukaryotictwocomponent pages 1-2).

A particularly well-defined target is **OCH1**, encoding an α-1,6-mannosyltransferase involved in N-linked glycoprotein maturation and cell-wall integrity. Skn7 binds a repeated response element in the OCH1 promoter. Activating *sln1* alleles produced approximately 3–5-fold reporter induction; an isolated OCH1 promoter region gave 3.2-fold activation, and a duplicated response element gave 5.2-fold activation. Substitution of two conserved guanines abolished the response (li1998theyeasthistidine pages 1-2, li2002theeukaryotictwocomponent pages 7-8).

This branch must be distinguished from Skn7’s oxidative-stress role. OCH1/hypo-osmotic signaling requires the phosphoacceptor Asp427, whereas important oxidative-stress functions of Skn7 can be Asp427-independent. Therefore, not every Skn7-regulated oxidative response should be attributed directly to Sln1 phosphotransfer (li2002theeukaryotictwocomponent pages 1-2).

## 5. What Sln1 senses

The physiological input is best described as **osmotic/turgor or membrane–cell-wall mechanical state**, not a chemically defined extracellular ligand. Hyperosmotic shock causes water loss and reduced turgor and inhibits Sln1 kinase activity; hypotonic conditions favor increased pathway phosphorylation. The extracellular domain is functionally important and has been implicated in dimerization and communication with the cell-wall environment, but the precise molecular stimulus and conformational sequence remain incompletely resolved (xu2001thestructureand pages 35-38, kaserer2009effectofosmolytes pages 43-49).

Consequently, “osmosensor” is experimentally justified at the systems level, while statements that Sln1 directly binds a particular osmolyte would be unsupported. The most cautious current model is that extracellular-domain/transmembrane/HAMP conformational coupling reports physical changes associated with turgor, membrane tension, or cell-wall state.

## 6. Quantitative behavior and systems-level interpretation

The HOG pathway can activate within roughly **one minute** of osmotic shock and can follow periodic changes in external osmolyte concentration up to approximately **0.0046 Hz**. Such dynamics require both rapid phosphorelay shutdown and robust suppression during unstressed growth (dexter2015robustnetworkstructure pages 1-2).

A combined perturbation/modeling study concluded that the three-component architecture is robust because Ypd1 forms an excess buffering pool. Ypd1 was estimated to be at least **fivefold more abundant than Ssk1**. Moderate changes in Sln1, Ypd1, or Ssk1 generally did not spuriously activate Hog1, whereas depletion of Ypd1 caused inappropriate HOG activation. Massive Ssk1 overexpression increased phospho-Hog1 (*p*=0.0224), while Ypd1 overexpression reduced it (*p*=0.0246) (dexter2015robustnetworkstructure pages 8-11).

This supports an expert interpretation of Ypd1 as more than a passive wire: its abundance buffers fluctuations in sensor and response-regulator levels. The multicomponent relay thereby combines sensitivity to osmotic shutdown with resistance to molecular-expression noise—an advantage not readily available to a direct two-component architecture (dexter2015robustnetworkstructure pages 1-2, dexter2015robustnetworkstructure pages 8-11).

## 7. Recent research, 2023–2024

Direct 2023–2024 mechanistic studies of *S. cerevisiae* P39928 are limited; the core phosphorelay model is mature and continues to rest on high-quality biochemical and genetic work from the 1990s–2010s. The most relevant recent authoritative development is the **Mina et al. 2024** fungal-histidine-kinase phylogenomics study, received May 24, revised July 2, accepted August 17, and published online **September 14, 2024**: [https://doi.org/10.1007/s00294-024-01301-w](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00294-024-01301-w) (mina2024structureanddistribution pages 1-2).

That study analyzed **726 predicted histidine kinases from 91 fungal species** and classified **42 group-VI transmembrane osmosensors**. It places *S. cerevisiae* Sln1 as the well-studied, unique histidine kinase of budding yeast and a representative of group VI. The work reinforces the view that fungal hybrid kinases combine variable sensing regions with conserved HisKA, HATPase, and receiver machinery (mina2024structureanddistribution pages 1-2, mina2024structureanddistribution pages 5-8).

The same survey emphasizes that humans lack two-component histidine-kinase systems, making fungal histidine kinases conceptually attractive antifungal targets. This is an **expert translational inference**, not evidence that P39928 itself is a clinical drug target: *S. cerevisiae* is principally a model and industrial organism, and group-VI homolog phenotypes in *Candida*, *Aspergillus*, or phytopathogens do not establish identical essentiality or inhibitor sensitivity for Sln1/P39928 (mina2024structureanddistribution pages 2-5, mina2024structureanddistribution pages 5-8).

A 2024 structural paper examined receiver–HPt recognition in several fungal hybrid kinases, including *Candida albicans* Sln1-related material, but it did not directly determine the structure or mechanism of *S. cerevisiae* P39928. It is therefore useful as broader family context, not as direct annotation evidence.

## 8. Applications and real-world relevance

### Synthetic biology and optogenetics

Sln1’s modular transmitter and Ypd1 interface have been repurposed in a LOV–Sln1 light-regulated kinase. Blue light inhibited ATP-dependent autophosphorylation and transfer to Ypd1. In cells lacking endogenous Sln1, the construct enabled optical control of downstream signaling, reduced steady-state OCH1 expression by about **40%** under saturating illumination, and produced persistent Hog1 nuclear accumulation rather than the transient response characteristic of salt shock. This demonstrates an experimentally implemented route for controlling eukaryotic gene expression through a histidine phosphorelay: Bury and Hellingwerf, published April 2018, [https://doi.org/10.1186/s13568-018-0582-7](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13568-018-0582-7) (bury2018designcharacterizationand pages 1-2).

### Yeast stress and biotechnology

The HOG system is central to yeast survival under high sugar, salt, desiccation, and related osmotic conditions relevant to fermentation and industrial cultivation. Sln1 is the membrane input governing one major HOG branch. Nevertheless, direct industrial implementation of SLN1 engineering is less established than general HOG-pathway engineering; constitutive manipulation is potentially hazardous because excessive HOG activity impairs growth, while excessive Sln1/Ssk1 expression can also produce fitness defects (dexter2015robustnetworkstructure pages 1-2, dexter2015robustnetworkstructure pages 8-11).

### Antifungal-target discovery

Fungal histidine kinases are absent from humans and regulate stress adaptation and, in pathogenic species, sometimes virulence and antifungal susceptibility. Sln1 supplies a mechanistic model for this target class. However, drug-development conclusions require pathogen-specific validation because fungal kinase repertoires and redundancy differ substantially. The 2024 survey found marked diversity across fungal clades, including 19-class classification schemes and newly recognized groups, underscoring that an inhibitor designed against one group may not generalize (mina2024structureanddistribution pages 1-2, mina2024structureanddistribution pages 2-5).

## 9. Evidence quality, uncertainties, and recommended annotation

### High-confidence conclusions

- Sln1/P39928 is a plasma-membrane, dimeric hybrid histidine kinase.
- Its catalytic pathway is ATP→His576→Asp1144→Ypd1-His64.
- Ypd1 distributes phosphate to Ssk1-Asp554 and Skn7-Asp427.
- The Ssk1 branch negatively regulates Ssk2/Ssk22–Pbs2–Hog1 under normal osmolarity.
- Hyperosmotic stress decreases Sln1 pathway activity and releases HOG signaling.
- The Skn7 branch controls OCH1/cell-wall-associated transcription and differs from Asp427-independent oxidative-stress functions.

### Remaining uncertainties

- The exact physical variable directly detected by Sln1—turgor, membrane tension, cell-wall strain, or a coupled combination—has not been molecularly resolved.
- A full-length high-resolution structure of *S. cerevisiae* Sln1 and direct visualization of its stimulus-induced conformational change remain lacking in the evidence reviewed.
- Detailed nucleotide selectivity and full kinetic constants for intact membrane Sln1 are insufficiently characterized.
- A possible additional phosphatase role has been considered in systems models but is not established sufficiently to replace the kinase-centered annotation (dexter2015robustnetworkstructure pages 8-11).

### Recommended concise functional annotation

> **Plasma-membrane hybrid osmosensing histidine kinase that uses ATP to autophosphorylate His576 and transfers phosphate through its receiver Asp1144 to Ypd1. The Ypd1–Ssk1 branch represses the Ssk2/Ssk22–Pbs2–Hog1 high-osmolarity pathway in unstressed cells; hyperosmotic inhibition of Sln1 releases HOG signaling. A parallel Ypd1–Skn7 branch regulates hypo-osmotic and cell-wall transcription, including OCH1.**

This wording captures the experimentally established primary function, reaction, protein-substrate specificity, localization, and pathway role without overassigning broader phenotypes or findings from non-*cerevisiae* Sln1 homologs.
