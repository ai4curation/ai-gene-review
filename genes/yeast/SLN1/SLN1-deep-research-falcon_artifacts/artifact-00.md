| Identity and domain architecture | The target is *S. cerevisiae* Sln1p, a hybrid sensor histidine kinase containing an extracellular sensor region, two membrane-spanning segments, a cytosolic HAMP/coiled-coil region, histidine-kinase transmitter/HATPase module, and C-terminal CheY-like receiver domain. This agrees with P39928, **SLN1/YPD2/YIL147C**, rather than a similarly named protein from another organism. | Deletion/mutation and domain-complementation experiments showed that the transmitter and receiver are distinct required modules; residues 1–1070 retain the kinase module but lack the receiver, and receiver Asp1144 is required for signaling. | Direct genetics and domain dissection, supported by curated domain annotation | Posas et al., September 1996, [https://doi.org/10.1016/S0092-8674(00)80162-2](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0092-8674(00)80162-2) (pqac-00000000, pqac-00000005) |
| Localization and topology | Sln1p is an integral **plasma-membrane**, dimeric sensor. Its N-terminal extracellular region lies between two predicted transmembrane helices; the catalytic transmitter and receiver regions face the cytoplasm. Ypd1 subsequently carries the signal through cytoplasm and nucleus. | Sln1p was characterized as a membrane-bound dimer and plasma-membrane kinase; localization studies showed Ypd1 in both cytosol and nucleus, consistent with signaling from membrane Sln1 to cytoplasmic Ssk1 and nuclear Skn7. | Topology prediction, biochemical/genetic characterization, and fluorescence localization | Lu et al., December 2003, [https://doi.org/10.1128/EC.2.6.1304-1314.2003](https://doi.org/10.1128/EC.2.6.1304-1314.2003) (pqac-00000006, pqac-00000008, pqac-00000020) |
| Catalytic reaction and specificity | Sln1p uses **ATP** to autophosphorylate His576, apparently in trans within the dimer, and then transfers the phosphoryl group intramolecularly to Asp1144. The receiver transfers it specifically to Ypd1-His64; there is no supported direct His576→Ypd1 shortcut. | A purified GST–Sln1 transmitter incorporated phosphate from [γ-³²P]ATP; H576Q abolished autophosphorylation. About half of the label transferred to the receiver within 15 minutes, dependent on Asp1144. Acid/base sensitivity distinguished phosphohistidine from phosphoaspartate. | Direct in-vitro radiochemical biochemistry and site-directed mutagenesis | Posas et al., September 1996, [https://doi.org/10.1016/S0092-8674(00)80162-2](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0092-8674(00)80162-2) (pqac-00000004) |
| Ssk1–HOG branch | In normal osmolarity, active Sln1 drives **His576→Asp1144→Ypd1-His64→Ssk1-Asp554** phosphotransfer; phosphorylated Ssk1 cannot activate Ssk2/Ssk22, keeping Pbs2–Hog1 off. Hyperosmotic stress inhibits Sln1, allowing dephosphorylated Ssk1 to activate the HOG MAPK cascade and osmoadaptation. | Sln1-dependent phosphorylation fell strongly after **0.4 M NaCl**. Mutating Sln1 His576 or Asp1144 abolished Ypd1 phosphorylation. *sln1* lethality was suppressed by deleting **SSK1, SSK2, PBS2, or HOG1**, linking loss of Sln1 to toxic constitutive HOG activation. | Direct biochemical and genetic epistasis evidence | Posas et al., September 1996, [https://doi.org/10.1016/S0092-8674(00)80162-2](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0092-8674(00)80162-2) (pqac-00000000, pqac-00000004, pqac-00000005) |
| Skn7–OCH1 branch | The same Sln1–Ypd1 relay also phosphorylates nuclear Skn7 at Asp427. Phospho-Skn7 activates hypo-osmotic/cell-wall transcription, including **OCH1**, which encodes an α-1,6-mannosyltransferase required for glycoprotein maturation and cell-wall integrity. This branch is mechanistically distinct from Skn7’s Asp427-independent oxidative-stress function. | Activating *sln1* alleles increased Skn7-dependent reporters roughly **3–5-fold**; loss of SLN1 reduced one reporter about **10-fold**. An OCH1 promoter fragment showed 3.2-fold activation and a duplicated response element 5.2-fold activation; mutation of two conserved guanines abolished the response. | Genetics, reporter assays, EMSA, promoter mutagenesis, and phosphoacceptor mutation | Li et al., December 1998, [https://doi.org/10.1093/emboj/17.23.6952](https://doi.org/10.1093/emboj/17.23.6952); Li et al., February 2002, [https://doi.org/10.1091/mbc.01-09-0434](https://doi.org/10.1091/mbc.01-09-0434) (pqac-00000001, pqac-00000018, pqac-00000019) |
| Systems robustness | The three-component relay is robust to moderate variation in Sln1, Ypd1, and Ssk1 abundance because a large excess pool of Ypd1 buffers fluctuations; depletion of Ypd1 causes inappropriate HOG activation. | Ypd1 was estimated to be at least **fivefold more abundant than Ssk1**. HOG signaling can activate within **1 minute** and track osmolyte oscillations up to **0.0046 Hz**. Massive Ssk1 overexpression increased phospho-Hog1 (*p*=0.0224), whereas Ypd1 overexpression lowered it (*p*=0.0246). | Quantitative perturbation experiments and biochemical mathematical modeling | Dexter et al., March 2015, [https://doi.org/10.1186/s12918-015-0158-y](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12918-015-0158-y) (pqac-00000020, pqac-00000021) |
| 2024 phylogenomics | **Directly relevant to classification, not new mechanistic evidence for P39928:** Sln1 is the unique *S. cerevisiae* representative of fungal group-VI transmembrane osmosensors.
