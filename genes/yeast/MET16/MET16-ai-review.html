Gene Description

Met16 is the thioredoxin-dependent 3'-phosphoadenylyl-sulfate (PAPS) reductase of Saccharomyces cerevisiae (EC 1.8.4.8). It reduces PAPS, made by Met3 and Met14, to free sulfite and adenosine 3',5'-bisphosphate (PAP), with reduced thioredoxin as the electron donor and cosubstrate. This is the reductive step that turns activated sulfate into sulfite, which the Met5/Met10 sulfite reductase then reduces to sulfide for homocysteine, methionine, cysteine and glutathione synthesis. The enzyme is a homodimer. Product-bound crystal structures identify the PAPS-specific phosphate pocket and catalytic C-terminal Cys245. Thioredoxin provides reducing equivalents for sulfite release; early steady-state kinetic interpretations do not establish an obligatory thioredoxin-first molecular sequence. Met16 belongs to the CysH subfamily of PAPS reductases and is a soluble cytoplasmic protein. MET16 is transcriptionally controlled by Met4. Met16 also reduces the selenium analogue 3'-phosphoadenylyl selenate to selenite, so it takes part in selenate assimilation. MET16 overexpression raises cellular glutathione.
