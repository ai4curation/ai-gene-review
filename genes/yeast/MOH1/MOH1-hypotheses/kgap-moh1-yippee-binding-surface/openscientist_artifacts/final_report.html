MOH1 (P38191) Yippee domain: zinc-binding module, binding surface, and nucleic-acid exclusion

Gene: MOH1 / YBL049W, Saccharomyces cerevisiae S288C (NCBITaxon:559292) UniProt: P38191 (MOH1_YEAST), 138 aa (UniProt and SGD agree; MW 16094.7, pI 9.4), Yippee domain 41-138 Hypothesis slug: kgap-moh1-yippee-binding-surface Focus type: free_text Verdict: Partially supported

Summary

The seed hypothesis makes three claims. Two hold, one is not yet demonstrated for Moh1 itself.

The zinc-binding Yippee/CULT assignment is correct and now calibrated. In the AlphaFold model AF-P38191-F1 v6, cysteines C45, C48, C101 and C104 sit at a mean pairwise SG-SG distance of 3.70 Å against the 3.76 Å expected for four thiolates tetrahedrally coordinating one Zn at 2.3 Å. AlphaFold places no metals, so this geometry is an independent prediction and not a copied ligand. The domain superposes on five experimentally determined Yippee/CULT structures at TM 0.763 to 0.947, while rubredoxin, a RING domain, C2H2 zinc fingers and ubiquitin score 0.19 to 0.29. The 0.475 TM gap between the lowest in-family score and the highest true negative shows the assignment is not an artifact of comparing small zinc-binding domains to one another. Human MsrB1, the "MsrB-like fold" the hypothesis asked about, scores 0.688: a real β-tent relative, sitting below every Yippee/CULT structure. Moh1 is specifically a YPEL protein, at 38-46 % domain identity to human YPEL1-5 against 18-26 % for MIS18A, MIS18B and cereblon, with no overlap between the two groups.

The exclusion of nucleic-acid binding holds, for two reasons the hypothesis does not itself give. Across 600 fungal PF03226 homologs, no charged surface of any kind is conserved: exposed Lys and Arg positions average 0.218 identity, the largest contiguous exposed basic patch is 5 residues at mean Lys/Arg frequency 0.354, and the four well-conserved basic residues (R87, K111, K123, K126) are all buried below RSA 0.19. The conserved surface positions are the zinc ligands, glycines, and the aromatics W110, Y112 and Y122. Independently, Moh1 is 138 residues and stops at the last residue of its folded domain. The element through which S. pombe Mis18 binds nucleosomal DNA, the extreme C terminus, does not exist in Moh1. Moh1 sits at the 1.8th percentile for C-terminal extension length among 2618 fungal PF03226 proteins.

The phrase "protein-interaction module" is supported for the family and undemonstrated for Moh1. Every family member whose binding surface has been mapped uses a different, lineage-specific face: Mis18 an acidic patch that is charge-reversed in Moh1 (D34→S54, D52→K75, net −3 becomes +1), YPEL5 an N-terminal degron mimic plus a C-terminal segment that leaves the cradle untouched (W82 ΔRSA 0.000, Y84 0.012, Y94 0.000), cereblon a ligand pocket at the cradle. Moh1 retains none of these surfaces. Its entire experimental interaction record is five two-hybrid entries from a single publication, naming Rim20 and Cap2. The authors of the Mis18 structure explicitly keep nucleic-acid binding open for the same cradle pocket, so the exclusionary wording "rather than a nucleic-acid-binding domain" overstates what is settled for the fold as a category, even where it is right for Moh1.

Executive judgment

Partially supported. Three components, graded separately.

1. Zinc-binding Yippee-family module: supported. Cys4 geometry at 3.70 Å mean SG-SG against 3.76 Å ideal, local pLDDT 97.4 over residues 41-138, TM 0.763-0.947 against five in-family structures by two independent superposition methods (zinc-anchored and unseeded gapless threading, agreeing to within 0.005 TM units), and separation of 0.475 TM units from true negatives. Family placement is quantitative: YPEL subfamily at 38-46 % identity, not Mis18 at 21-26 % or cereblon at 18 %. Domain architecture agrees: Moh1 is 138 aa like YPEL1-5 at 119-127 aa, against MIS18A 233, MIS18B 229 and CRBN 442.

2. Not a nucleic-acid-binding domain: supported by two independent arguments. No conserved charged surface exists across 600 fungal homologs, basic or acidic. And Moh1 lacks the only element in the family ever shown to contact DNA, the Mis18 basic C-terminal tail (net +6 over 64 aa, extreme 30 residues net +8 at 40 % Lys plus Arg). Moh1's C-extension is 0 residues.

3. Protein-interaction module, for Moh1 specifically: undemonstrated. The family-level claim is well supported by direct assay in four organisms. For Moh1 the supporting evidence is five two-hybrid records from one source (41935050), and the structural analysis runs against partner inference, because Moh1 shares no mapped binding surface with any relative.

Most important caveats. No experiment has measured zinc content, nucleic-acid binding, or any binding partner of purified Moh1 protein. All structural work here uses an AlphaFold model, not an experimental Moh1 structure. PMID: 26921242 describes the Mis18 cradle as "implicated in protein/nucleic acid binding", which is a competing reading of the same pocket from the authors who solved it.

Key findings

1. A tetrahedral Cys4 zinc site at C45, C48, C101, C104

The four cysteines UniProt P38191 annotates as binding sites are arranged in the AlphaFold model exactly as a structural Cys4-Zn site. The six pairwise SG-SG distances are 3.84, 3.95, 3.53, 3.49, 3.44 and 3.92 Å, mean 3.70 Å. Four thiolates tetrahedrally coordinating one Zn at 2.3 Å Zn-S and 109.5° give 2 × 2.3 × sin(54.75°) = 3.76 Å. The deviation is 0.06 Å on the mean and at most 0.32 Å on any single pair.

The same measurement on experimentally determined zinc-bound family structures returns the same architecture, CxxC...CxxC in all five: Mis18 (S. pombe) 5HJ0 C25/C28/C79/C82 with Zn-S 2.29-2.36 Å; human cereblon 4TZ4 C323/C326/C391/C394; mouse cereblon 3WX1 C326/C329/C394/C397; human YPEL5 8QBN chain Y C17/C20/C73/C76.

AlphaFold does not model metal ligands, so the geometry is an independent prediction rather than a transferred ligand. Model confidence over the domain is mean pLDDT 97.4 (global 83.19); residues 1-40 average 48.4 and are predicted disordered.

This is a structural prediction. No metal-content measurement for Moh1 protein was found.

2. Fold assignment, calibrated against a null distribution

Two superposition methods were run. The first seeded the alignment on the four zinc-coordinating cysteines; the second used gapless-threading initialization with no anchors at all. The two agree to within 0.005 TM units on every target, which rules out the concern that anchoring on the zinc site manufactures the result.

The seed hypothesis asked specifically about MsrB-like folds. The answer: MsrB1 is a genuine structural relative in the same β-tent superfamily, sitting below every Yippee/CULT structure and far above the true negatives. That matches the published grouping of CULT, Yippee, Mis18 and MsrB-like domains as one fold class (PMID: 25569776). Moh1 is closer to all four Yippee/CULT structures than to MsrB1.

A second identifier tried as a bacterial MsrB (3E0M) returned TM 0.286 over a 200-residue chain slice. I did not verify that this entry is in fact an MsrB, so that number is excluded from every conclusion here.

The five YPEL proteins occupy 38-46 % and the three non-YPEL members occupy 18-26 %, with no overlap. Structure agrees: the one available YPEL representative, YPEL5, is by far the closest by TM-score (0.947 against 0.765 for Mis18 and 0.799 for cereblon). SGD describes MOH1 as a "Yippee-like (YPEL) family member", and human YPEL5 functionally complements a yeast moh1Δ mutation (PMID: 28173693).

This bounds annotation transfer. Any centromere, CENP-A, kinetochore or E3-ligase-substrate-receptor term reaching P38191 through family-level inference is a cross-family transfer at 18-26 % identity, into a protein that also lacks the defining accessory regions of those relatives: the Mis18 basic C-terminal tail and the cereblon 317-residue N-terminal DDB1-binding region.

A search of S. cerevisiae S288C for Pfam PF03226 returns MOH1/P38191 as the only member. No within-yeast paralog can carry a conflicting annotation, so paralog-overannotation risk runs across species rather than within the genome.

The extreme 30 residues of the Mis18 tail, ETISSLKKPKIEGKEGKKEKARTYSKRTRK, are net +8 at 40 % Lys plus Arg. That is the element shown to bind nucleosomal DNA (PMID: 32318758). It has no Moh1 equivalent.

Across 2618 fungal PF03226 proteins measured from the zinc motif, Moh1 has 34 residues past the motif, the 1.8th percentile, against a median of 39 (IQR 34-101). SpMis18 has 112 and human YPEL5 has 45. Among the 1301 homologs with at least 40 residues past the motif, mean net charge is −4.50 and only 22 % are net positive. A basic C-terminal tail is not a family feature; Mis18's is the exception. Among the 1058 homologs with at least 30 residues before the motif, mean net charge is +2.35 and 75 % are net positive, which matches Moh1's mildly basic N-terminal extension.

First correction made within this run. An earlier step used a 211-residue length for P38191. That was wrong, and the error surfaced as a division by zero when a C-terminal region of length 0 was sliced. Both UniProt and SGD give 138. The corrected length changes the conclusion in the hypothesis's favour: the absent C-terminal tail becomes an architectural argument against nucleic-acid binding that is independent of the surface-conservation argument.

The one region of Moh1 outside the folded domain is the 40-residue disordered N terminus: net +2, 25 % Ser plus Thr, sequence MGLRYSIYIENPLSSPSSSYKSINDPLFHSQHRSQKNVSF, with 60 % of residues below pLDDT 50.

7. Each family member binds through a different surface, and Moh1 retains none of them

Three mapping tests were run. All three are negative for surface conservation.

Mis18's acidic histone-binding face is charge-reversed in Moh1. Largest contiguous exposed acidic patch (single-linkage, 10 Å, RSA > 0.25): Moh1 D118/D119/E124 (n = 3); Mis18 D34/D52/E55 (n = 3); cereblon E328 (n = 1); YPEL5 E108/E110/E113/E114 (n = 4). Largest exposed basic patch: Moh1 n = 5 (R46, K49, K105, R106, K132); Mis18 n = 3 (K26, K27, K110); cereblon n = 2 (K324, R419); YPEL5 n = 6 (R13, R27, R34, R41, R103, R107).

Mapping the Mis18 acidic patch onto Moh1 through the zinc-anchored superposition gives D34→S54, D52→K75, E55→no equivalent: net −3 becomes net +1. The reverse test is equally negative: Moh1 D118/D119/E124 map onto Mis18 I95, Y96, R101.

The lenalidomide analogue LVY in 4TZ4 chain C contacts 11 residues within 4.5 Å, including W400 at 3.43 Å and F402 at 3.27 Å, whose apo RSA values are 0.088 and 0.013. The fuller contact set is V350 4.10, N351 3.19, P352 3.53, H353 3.73, H357 4.06, H378 2.74, S379 3.23, W380 2.96, W386 3.29 Å.

Low solvent accessibility therefore does not argue against a binding function for Moh1 W110 and Y112. The cereblon pocket is formed by residues at the same burial, and the drug reaches them because the pocket opens between the two β-meanders. Moh1 keeps a tryptophan and a tyrosine at the structurally equivalent positions, at the same depth, at 0.946 and 0.938 identity across 600 fungal homologs. That is the clearest conserved surface feature of the fold apart from the zinc ligands, and it defines a candidate ligand or peptide pocket worth testing directly.

Three caveats on this finding. The W110/Y112/Y122 set is not one contiguous cluster: side-chain centroid distances are W110-Y112 5.72 Å, Y112-Y122 8.36 Å, W110-Y122 13.57 Å, so W110 and Y112 form the buried pair and Y122 sits at the rim. A relaxed-burial cleft-volume measure returned 127, 127, 127 and 85 Å³ for Moh1, YPEL5, Mis18 and cereblon; three identical values indicate the measure was dominated by the sampling box rather than by structure, so it is reported for completeness and given no weight. A block-level conservation test on the mapped binding sites was negative: residues in the union of the two mapped sites are not preferentially conserved (n = 19, mean identity 0.348) against the rest of the domain (n = 79, mean 0.412), Mann-Whitney p = 0.8777. The claim rests on the individual conservation of W110, Y112 and Y122 and on the positional correspondence to the cereblon pocket, so it is a lead for experiment rather than an established Moh1 binding site.

The abstract for 41935050 could not be retrieved through the literature search tool available in this run. Nothing is claimed here about that study's design or scale; the IntAct record is reported exactly as retrieved.

Rim20 is independently characterized as a Bro1-domain protein of the Rim101 alkaline pH-response pathway that associates with endosomes and interacts with ESCRT-III Snf7 (PMID: 16407402, PMID: 15371534, PMID: 20190076). Cap2 is the beta subunit of the F-actin capping protein. The SGD description of MOH1 reads "Protein of unknown function, essential for stationary phase survival; influences metabolic and physiological processes important for selective stress adaptation and survival; possibly linked with vacuolar transport; Yippee-like (YPEL) family member".

Five two-hybrid records from a single publication make GO:0005515 technically assertable with IPI evidence and do not make it recommendable. One method and one source cannot distinguish a real complex from a two-hybrid artifact. The Rim20 convergence with the SGD vacuolar-transport note is a lead for a curator to follow. Neither partner is predicted by anything in the structural analysis, which is consistent with finding 7: Moh1 shares no binding surface with other family members, so its partners cannot be guessed from the fold.

Mechanistic model and interpretation

The hypothesis concerns the direct molecular activity of the Moh1 gene product: what its folded domain binds. Three levels must be separated.

Direct gene-product activity, addressed here. The Yippee domain folds as a β-tent and staples itself with a structural Cys4-Zn site. The zinc is structural rather than catalytic: no catalytic residue set is apparent, and the family has no assigned enzymatic activity. The conserved surface feature is a buried aromatic pair (W110, Y112) at the apex of the tent, at the position where cereblon binds thalidomide-class ligands. Whether Moh1 uses that position to bind anything has not been tested.

Downstream phenotypes, not direct activity. The moh1Δ phenotypes reported by PMID: 28173693 (altered viability after UV, 1.8 mM MMS, 100 µM CPT, 50 °C and 1.2 M KCl) and the SGD description of stationary-phase survival and stress adaptation are loss-of-function readouts several steps from any molecular activity. They constrain the biological process and say nothing about what the domain binds.

Pathway context, inferred only. The Rim20 two-hybrid hit places Moh1 adjacent to the Rim101 alkaline pH-response pathway at endosomes, which converges with the SGD vacuolar-transport note. Rim20 itself has no role in endosome function or MVB formation (PMID: 16407402, PMID: 15371534), so the convergence is between two weak signals and is a lead only.

The pattern across the family explains why partner inference fails. The zinc-stapled core and the internal aromatic pocket are conserved, while every peripheral charged surface is lineage-specific. Mis18 built an acidic face for H2A-H2B and a basic tail for DNA. YPEL5 built an N-terminal degron mimic and a C-terminal segment for WDR26. Cereblon built a ligand pocket at the cradle behind a 317-residue DDB1-binding region. Moh1 kept the core and built none of those extensions. If Moh1 binds a partner, the two candidate elements are the cradle (conserved, buried, equivalent to the cereblon pocket) and the 40-residue disordered N terminus (lineage-specific, the YPEL5 analogy), and neither has been tested.

Evidence base

Structure and fold family

PMID: 25569776 defines the β-tent fold and groups CULT, Yippee, Mis18 and MsrB-like domains: "Its nearest relatives are yippee, a highly conserved eukaryotic protein of unknown function, and Mis18, a protein involved in the priming of centromeres for recruitment of CENP-A." The TM-score matrix computed here is a direct quantitative test of that grouping, and it passes, with MsrB1 at 0.688 sitting between the Yippee/CULT members and the true negatives.

PMID: 26921242 solved the S. pombe Mis18 Yippee domain (PDB 5HJ0): "The crystal structure of the N-terminal Yippee-like domain reveals a fold containing a cradle-shaped pocket that is implicated in protein/nucleic acid binding, which we show is required for Mis18 function." This paper both supplies the reference structure used in every superposition here and supplies the main competing reading of the hypothesis: the authors keep nucleic-acid binding on the table for the same pocket.

PMID: 38759627 provides PDB 8QBN, the cryo-EM structure of YPEL5 bound to the WDR26-CTLH E3 complex, and states the mechanism measured independently here by ΔRSA: "degron mimicry by YPEL5's N terminus antagonizing substrate binding". PMID: 38575527 identifies WDR26 as the CTLH subunit that mediates YPEL5 binding.

The discriminating experiment

PMID: 32318758 is the single paper in the family that separates DNA binding from Yippee-domain binding: "We found that spMis18 directly binds to nucleosomal DNA through its extreme C-terminus and interacts with H2A-H2B dimer via the acidic region on the surface of its Yippee-like domain." Two measurements here follow from it. The acidic surface it names is charge-reversed in Moh1 (net −3 becomes +1). The extreme C terminus it names has no Moh1 equivalent at all, since Moh1 ends at residue 138 with the domain.

Yippee domains as protein-interaction modules

PMID: 27239045 gives the strongest family-level evidence: "we find that their conserved YIPPEE domains mediate distinct interactions that are essential to link new CENP-A deposition to existing centromeres", with Mis18α binding Mis18BP1 and Mis18β binding CENP-C. The word "distinct" matters for this review: two closely related domains use the fold for two different partners, which is the same pattern seen in the surface mapping here.

PMID: 11240639 founded the family through a protein interaction: "An intracellular Drosophila protein, Yippee, was identified in a yeast interaction trap screen as physically interacting with Hyalophora cecropia Hemolin", and assigned the metal site: "Yippee contains a putative zinc-finger-like metal binding domain." PMID: 27261060 supports the Yippee:Hemolin interaction in Antheraea pernyi by Far-Western blotting. PMID: 20555386 gives a validated partner for the subfamily closest to Moh1: "The major vault protein (MVP), a lung resistance associated protein, was identified as a binding partner of YPEL4", with the same paper restating the metal assignment, "It contains a Yippee domain, which is a putative zinc-finger-like, metal-binding domain."

Every Yippee-domain function demonstrated by direct assay in these papers is a protein interaction. No direct nucleic-acid-binding assay on Moh1, or on any YPEL protein, was found in this search.

Moh1 in yeast

PMID: 28173693 connects Moh1 to the YPEL subfamily functionally: human YPEL5 complements a yeast moh1Δ mutation. The complementation is the functional counterpart to the structural near-identity measured here (TM 0.947 between Moh1 41-138 and YPEL5 in 8QBN) and to the 38 % sequence identity.

The three Rim20 papers give pathway context for the one Moh1 partner with multiple IntAct records. PMID: 16407402: "Yeast Bro1-domain protein Rim20 has no role in endosome function, but is required for alkaline pH-stimulated cleavage of transcription factor Rim101." PMID: 15371534: "Rim20p has no role in MVB formation, but functions in the Rim101p pH-response pathway". PMID: 20190076 describes pH-dependent endosomal localization of Rim20 under Bro1 control. These constrain rather than support the SGD note: the Moh1 partner with the best two-hybrid support has no MVB or endosome-trafficking role.

Conflicts and alternatives

The authors of the Mis18 structure keep nucleic-acid binding open. PMID: 26921242 describes the cradle as implicated in protein or nucleic acid binding. The seed hypothesis's exclusionary wording runs against the published reading of the same pocket. The resolution offered here: the exclusion is right for Moh1 on architectural and conservation grounds, and is not established as a property of the fold in general.

Cross-species annotation transfer is the real paralog risk. MOH1 is the only PF03226 protein in S. cerevisiae S288C, so no within-genome paralog can carry a conflicting annotation. The risk runs from human MIS18A and MIS18B (21-26 % identity) and cereblon (18 %), both of which carry accessory regions Moh1 lacks. Transfer within the YPEL subfamily (38-46 %) is better supported by identity and by the complementation result, and still cannot deliver a specific partner, because the YPEL5 interface is built from an N-terminal degron mimic for which Moh1 has no equivalent inside the aligned domain.

Moh1's surface charge is the one measure that cuts against the hypothesis. At net +7, basic fraction 0.307 and +5.30 kT/e, Moh1 is the most basic of the four family domains measured. Nucleic-acid binding cannot be excluded on charge grounds alone for this specific protein. The counterargument is that the charge is lineage-specific: the positions carrying it average 0.218 identity across 600 homologs, so it is not a maintained functional surface. An opportunistic polyanion interaction by S. cerevisiae Moh1 specifically remains possible and untested.

Database carry-over and the SGD note. SGD's "possibly linked with vacuolar transport" is hedged and its basis is not stated in the description. Rim20, the partner that converges with it, explicitly has no MVB role (PMID: 15371534). A curator should trace the SGD statement to its source before using it.

Two-hybrid as the sole method. All five IntAct records use a two-hybrid variant, from one publication. Two-hybrid is prone to false positives through self-activation and through forced nuclear co-localization. Without an orthogonal method, neither Rim20 nor Cap2 should be treated as a confirmed Moh1 partner.

Model-based structural evidence. Every structural statement about Moh1 rests on AlphaFold AF-P38191-F1 v6. Local confidence is high (pLDDT 97.4 over residues 41-138), which supports the fold and side-chain packing, and does not substitute for an experimental structure or for a measured metal.

Proposed follow-up experiments and actions

Ordered by how efficiently each separates the hypothesis from its alternatives.

1. Nucleic-acid binding by purified Moh1 41-138. EMSA and fluorescence polarization against dsDNA, ssDNA, total RNA and reconstituted nucleosomes, with full-length SpMis18 as the positive control and the Mis18 C-terminal deletion as the critical comparator. A negative result on Moh1 converts the architectural argument into a direct one. A positive result would show that Moh1's lineage-specific basic surface does what no conserved family surface does. Either outcome is informative.

2. Zinc stoichiometry. ICP-MS on recombinant Moh1 41-138, with a C45S/C48S double mutant as the control. One measurement moves GO:0008270 from ISM to IDA.

3. Cradle mutagenesis against the stress phenotype. W110A and Y112A singly and together, assayed against the moh1Δ readouts of PMID: 28173693 (UV, 1.8 mM MMS, 100 µM CPT, 50 °C, 1.2 M KCl) and against stationary-phase survival. The cereblon control in finding 8 shows that burial does not preclude a pocket, so loss of function from these substitutions would make the cradle a real binding site in Moh1 rather than one inferred by position.

4. AP-MS of full-length Moh1 against the 1-40 truncation. Separates partners that need the disordered N terminus from partners that need the folded domain. By YPEL5 analogy the N terminus is the likelier interaction element, and that prediction has never been tested in Moh1.

5. Orthogonal test of the Rim20 interaction. Co-IP or split-luciferase from yeast lysate under acidic and alkaline conditions, paired with a moh1Δ growth test at pH 8 and a Rim101 cleavage assay. This decides whether any Rim101-pathway BP term is defensible.

6. Cross-complementation panel. Test which of human YPEL1-5 complement moh1Δ, and whether MIS18A, MIS18B or the cereblon CULT domain alone do. PMID: 28173693 establishes YPEL5 complementation; extending the panel would calibrate the 38-46 % against 18-26 % identity boundary functionally and tell a curator how far annotation transfer reaches.

7. Complete the interaction picture. Query BioGRID for YBL049W and read 41935050. The current record is five entries from one publication by one method, and the whole GO:0005515 question turns on it.
