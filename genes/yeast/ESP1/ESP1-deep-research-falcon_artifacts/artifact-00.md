| Identity/domain architecture | **ESP1/YGR098C (UniProt Q03018)** encodes the **1,630-aa separin/separase**, a large cysteine endopeptidase with an N-terminal regulatory/substrate-recruitment region and conserved C-terminal **SEPARIN core, Peptidase_C50, and Separin_C** catalytic architecture. | Strong: concordant curated annotation, sequence conservation, mutational analysis, and yeast genetics. | The N-terminal region is essential but structurally and mechanistically less well defined than the catalytic C terminus. |
| Primary enzymatic reaction | Esp1 hydrolyzes peptide bonds in the kleisin **Mcd1/Scc1** subunit of chromosomal cohesin. Cleavage opens/disassembles functional cohesin, irreversibly releasing sister-chromatid cohesion at anaphase onset. | Very strong: sequence-specific cleavage demonstrated in vivo and in vitro; catalytic mutants block cohesin removal and chromosome separation. | Esp1 is required for additional anaphase functions, so Mcd1 cleavage does not explain every esp1 phenotype. |
| Substrate specificity | Esp1 cleaves after a conserved P1 arginine. The experimentally derived motif is **[not FKRWY]-[ACFHILMPVWY]-[DE]-X-[AGSV]-R↓X**, with **(D/E)XXR** as the minimal core. Recognition also depends on substrate features outside the short motif. | Strong: systematic Scc1-site mutagenesis, inhibitor-binding experiments, and candidate testing. The motif occurs in **1,139 of 5,889** predicted yeast proteins; screening **28** full-motif plus **35** core-motif candidates confirmed no new substrates. | Motif occurrence alone has poor predictive value and should not be used as proof of cleavage. Exact efficiency is context dependent, and the two Scc1 sites are not equally cleaved. |
| Physiological output | The principal output is timely sister-chromatid separation and faithful chromosome segregation. Esp1 activity also coordinates anaphase spindle elongation and pericentric-cohesin reorganization. Under combined Pds1 depletion and **cdc55Δ**, chromosome V markers separated at **6.71 min** after spindle-pole-body separation versus **13.85 min** in controls. | Very strong for chromosome segregation; strong genetic and live-cell evidence for timing and spindle coordination. | Premature activity causes chromosome mis-segregation, but some spindle effects may involve unidentified substrates or indirect network consequences. |
| Regulation | **Pds1/securin** binds and inhibits Esp1 while promoting its nuclear accumulation. Once the spindle checkpoint is satisfied, **APC/C–Cdc20** targets Pds1 for destruction. **Cdk1** phosphorylation promotes Esp1 activation, whereas **PP2A–Cdc55** and **Slk19** provide parallel inhibitory restraints, particularly around pericentric chromatin. | Strong: biochemistry, phosphosite mutants, inducible Pds1 degradation, epistasis, checkpoint perturbation, and live-cell imaging. | Regulation differs among eukaryotes; vertebrate cyclin-B–CDK1 pseudosubstrate inhibition and autocleavage should not automatically be assigned to budding-yeast Esp1. |
| Localization | Esp1 is broadly nuclear and cytoplasmic before anaphase, then becomes enriched in the nucleus and associates with the anaphase spindle and spindle poles. Efficient nuclear/spindle localization depends on prior Pds1 binding and Pds1 phosphorylation. Cohesin removal occurs broadly on nuclear chromosomes rather than only at kinetochores. | Strong: immunofluorescence, chromosome spreads, tubulin colocalization, and PDS1 mutant analysis. | Only a fraction of Esp1 is spindle associated; direct evidence for stable, discrete kinetochore or pericentromere localization is limited. |
| Additional substrates/pathways | Experimentally supported substrates include mitotic **Mcd1/Scc1**, meiotic **Rec8**, and spindle/kinetochore protein **Slk19**. Esp1 also participates in the **FEAR–Cdc14** early-anaphase network, cohesin removal during DNA repair, spindle elongation, and **Ty1 retrotransposition**, where it associates with Ty1 integrase. | Strong for cleavage of Scc1, Rec8, and Slk19; moderate-to-strong genetic/biochemical support for FEAR and Ty1 roles. | Slk19 cleavage is not required for normal spindle elongation; the relevant additional catalytic target remains unknown. Some FEAR and Ty1 effects may be non-proteolytic or secondary to cohesin removal. |
| Current/recent applications | Esp1 is used as a model for irreversible cell-cycle transitions, cohesin-ring opening, checkpoint–APC/C coupling, protease specificity, chromosome-instability mechanisms, and retrotransposon integration. Recent separase work includes cleavage reporters and live-cell activity/localization sensors that could be adapted to yeast. | Established yeast genetic and biochemical platform; active 2023–2024 methodological development across separase systems. | Recent FRET sensors and spatiotemporal studies were developed mainly in mammalian cells or *C. elegans*, not validated directly for *S. cerevisiae* Esp1; translational cancer relevance concerns human ESPL1 rather than yeast ESP1. |
