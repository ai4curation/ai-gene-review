# ESP1 (YGR098C; UniProt Q03018) in *Saccharomyces cerevisiae*: functional-annotation report

## Executive conclusion

The requested identity is verified. **ESP1/YGR098C in *Saccharomyces cerevisiae* S288c encodes Esp1, the 1,630-amino-acid separin/separase**, not a similarly named protein from another organism. The literature describes budding-yeast Esp1 as a large, caspase-related cysteine endopeptidase with a conserved C-terminal catalytic region and a large, functionally essential N-terminal region. This architecture agrees with the supplied UniProt Q03018 annotations—Peptidase_C50, SEPARIN core, and Separin_C. No conflicting ESP1 identity was used in this report. (baskerville2008theproteaseactivity pages 3-4, baskerville2008theproteaseactivity pages 1-2)

Esp1’s primary function is the sequence-specific endoproteolytic cleavage of **Mcd1/Scc1**, the kleisin subunit that closes the cohesin ring. Cleavage releases chromosomal cohesin and irreversibly initiates sister-chromatid separation at anaphase. Esp1 is therefore the proteolytic trigger coupling spindle-checkpoint satisfaction to chromosome segregation. Its activity is controlled principally by Pds1/securin and APC/C–Cdc20, with additional regulation by Cdk1 phosphorylation, PP2A–Cdc55, Slk19, and localization. (baskerville2008theproteaseactivity pages 1-2, lianga2018cdk1phosphorylationof pages 2-3, norell2022theregulationof pages 21-24)

## 1. Molecular identity and architecture

Esp1 is a **separase-family cysteine endopeptidase**. The reported 1,630-aa protein contains a poorly conserved, approximately 850-aa N-terminal region and a more conserved C-terminal protease region related to CD-clan/caspase-like proteases. Mutations distributed across the protein produce stringent loss-of-function phenotypes, indicating that the N terminus is not dispensable packaging around an autonomous catalytic domain; it contributes to substrate recruitment, regulation, or productive assembly of the active enzyme. (baskerville2008theproteaseactivity pages 10-11, baskerville2008theproteaseactivity pages 3-4)

The domain interpretation is consequently:

- **Peptidase_C50/Separin_C:** catalytic C-terminal separase module.
- **SEPARIN core and extensive N-terminal helical region:** regulatory and substrate-recognition scaffold that cooperates with the catalytic region.
- **Functional unit:** the full-length protein, rather than an isolated catalytic fragment. Active-site blockade does not abolish association with substrate or regulatory partners, supporting recognition surfaces outside the catalytic cleft. (baskerville2008theproteaseactivity pages 10-11, sullivan2004studiesonsubstrate pages 4-5)

The gene is functionally essential for normal nuclear division. Restrictive esp1 mutants fail to remove cohesion, fail to elongate the anaphase spindle normally, and undergo abortive nuclear division. These phenotypes are consistent with Esp1 being an indispensable mitotic regulator rather than a redundant protease. (baskerville2008theproteaseactivity pages 10-11, baskerville2008theproteaseactivity pages 1-2)

## 2. Primary biochemical function

### Reaction and physiological substrate

Esp1 catalyzes hydrolysis of peptide bonds in **Mcd1/Scc1**, the kleisin subunit of cohesin. Cleaving kleisin opens or functionally dismantles the cohesin ring that physically links replicated sister chromatids. The immediate products are cleaved Mcd1 fragments and cohesin release from chromosomes; the cellular consequence is irreversible loss of sister-chromatid cohesion and poleward segregation at anaphase onset. Esp1-dependent Mcd1 cleavage has been demonstrated both **in vivo and in vitro**, and is sequence specific. (baskerville2008theproteaseactivity pages 1-2)

Mcd1/Scc1 contains two separase-cleavage sites. The C-terminal site is cleaved more efficiently; cleavage at either site can be sufficient for chromosome segregation. Substrate phosphorylation, including regulation associated with Polo kinase Cdc5, can make cohesin more permissive to cleavage, but Esp1 performs the proteolytic reaction. (sullivan2004studiesonsubstrate pages 2-3, norell2022theregulationof pages 21-24)

### Substrate specificity

Systematic mutagenesis of an Scc1 cleavage site yielded the consensus:

**[not F/K/R/W/Y]-[A/C/F/H/I/L/M/P/V/W/Y]-[D/E]-X-[A/G/S/V]-R↓X**

The most informative core is **(D/E)XXR↓**, with cleavage after the P1 arginine. A hydrophobic P5 residue and small P2 residue are generally favored; P3 is permissive, while P4 favors an acidic residue. Spacing is important: inserting a residue between P4 and P3 strongly impaired cleavage. (sullivan2004studiesonsubstrate pages 2-3, sullivan2004studiesonsubstrate pages 3-4)

This short motif is necessary context but not a sufficient substrate definition. It occurs in **1,139 of 5,889** predicted yeast proteins. Testing 28 proteins bearing the full motif and 35 bearing the core motif failed to confirm additional substrates. Moreover, an active-site-directed peptide inhibitor blocked catalysis without preventing Scc1 association, and an Scc1 fragment lacking the cleavage motif retained separase interaction. Esp1 therefore recognizes both the scissile sequence and extended docking or structural features in the substrate. (sullivan2004studiesonsubstrate pages 1-1, sullivan2004studiesonsubstrate pages 4-5, sullivan2004studiesonsubstrate pages 3-4)

## 3. Biological process: the metaphase–anaphase switch

Before anaphase, chromosomal cohesin opposes spindle-generated pulling forces. Once all kinetochores are appropriately attached, APC/C–Cdc20 becomes active and promotes destruction of Pds1/securin. Released and properly activated Esp1 cleaves Mcd1/Scc1, cohesin is removed, and sister chromatids separate. Because kleisin cleavage is irreversible, layered control of Esp1 is central to genome stability. (lianga2018cdk1phosphorylationof pages 2-3, norell2022theregulationof pages 21-24)

Genetic replacement or depletion experiments show that Mcd1 cleavage is the core chromosome-separation reaction but does not explain every Esp1-dependent event. Depleting Mcd1 or replacing an Esp1 site with an ectopic TEV-protease site did not fully restore anaphase when Esp1 was inactive. Esp1 catalytic activity is also required for normal spindle elongation through an additional, still-unidentified substrate or mechanism. Slk19 is cleaved by Esp1, but a non-cleavable Slk19 allele does not reproduce the spindle-elongation defect, arguing that Slk19 is not that missing substrate. (baskerville2008theproteaseactivity pages 10-11, baskerville2008theproteaseactivity pages 1-2)

## 4. Regulation of Esp1 activity

### Pds1/securin and APC/C

Pds1 has a dual role. It directly inhibits Esp1 before anaphase, but also acts as a chaperone that promotes Esp1 stability, nuclear accumulation, and later spindle loading. APC/C-dependent Pds1 destruction releases the protease at the correct cell-cycle stage. The spindle-assembly checkpoint delays this event by restraining APC/C when kinetochore attachment or spindle assembly is defective. (lianga2018cdk1phosphorylationof pages 2-3, norell2022theregulationof pages 21-24, agarwal2002phosphorylationofthe pages 7-8)

This dual function explains why complete PDS1 deletion is not equivalent to an ideally timed burst of separase activation: pds1Δ cells have impaired Esp1 nuclear localization and altered timing, despite losing the inhibitor. Pds1 phosphorylation by Cdc28/Cdk1 promotes efficient Esp1 nuclear localization. (agarwal2002phosphorylationofthe pages 7-8, lianga2018cdk1phosphorylationof pages 17-18)

### Cdk1, PP2A–Cdc55, and Slk19

Budding-yeast Esp1 is also regulated through phosphorylation. Cdk1–Clb2 and Cdk1–Clb5 phosphorylate Esp1 in vitro; three central sites—T1014, S1027, and T1034—account for detectable phosphorylation in vivo. Esp1 phosphorylation rises during cell-cycle progression and peaks around the decline of Pds1 and Mcd1. PP2A–Cdc55 reverses this phosphorylation and may additionally inhibit Esp1 through binding. (lianga2018cdk1phosphorylationof pages 18-20, lianga2018cdk1phosphorylationof pages 3-6)

Slk19 and PP2A–Cdc55 provide restraints that operate partly in parallel with Pds1, especially around pericentric cohesin. Combining Pds1 depletion with phosphomimetic ESP1, cdc55Δ, or slk19Δ causes premature spindle elongation, advanced Mcd1 proteolysis, disorganization of pericentric cohesin, and chromosome mis-segregation. In one quantitative experiment, chromosome V markers separated **6.71 minutes** after spindle-pole-body separation in cdc55Δ PDS1-AID cells, versus **13.85 minutes** in controls; 88% of the mutant cells displayed immediate spindle elongation. (lianga2018cdk1phosphorylationof pages 1-2, lianga2018cdk1phosphorylationof pages 7-9)

The best current model is therefore an AND-gate-like transition: APC/C-mediated removal of Pds1 combines with positive Cdk1 input and relief of PP2A–Cdc55/Slk19 inhibition to produce a sharp, spatially controlled wave of Esp1 activity. Phosphorylation did not simply accelerate Mcd1 cleavage in a purified assay, suggesting that its major effect is on in-cell access, conformation, localization, or regulatory-complex assembly. (lianga2018cdk1phosphorylationof pages 18-20)

## 5. Cellular localization

Esp1 abundance changes relatively little through the cell cycle, but its distribution changes markedly. From late G1 through metaphase, Esp1 is broadly distributed in the cytoplasm and nucleus without strong chromatin enrichment. At the metaphase–anaphase transition it concentrates in the nucleus, and a subset localizes to the mitotic spindle and spindle poles. Tubulin colocalization and chromosome-spread experiments directly support the spindle/pole pattern. (ciosk1998anesp1pds1complex pages 8-9, ciosk1998anesp1pds1complex pages 5-7, ciosk1998anesp1pds1complex pages 4-5)

Efficient nuclear and spindle localization requires prior Pds1 binding. In pds1 mutants, Esp1 fails to accumulate efficiently in the nucleus and displays little spindle or spindle-pole association despite similar protein abundance. Pds1 therefore delivers or retains Esp1 at its nuclear site of action before Pds1 itself is destroyed. (ciosk1998anesp1pds1complex pages 8-9, agarwal2002phosphorylationofthe pages 7-8, ciosk1998anesp1pds1complex pages 4-5)

Esp1 is not established as a constitutively concentrated kinetochore protein. Some anaphase Esp1 dots coincide with spindle poles or spindle structures near separated kinetochores, but bulk chromatin association was not detected. Functionally, Esp1-dependent cohesin removal occurs broadly along chromosomes and does not require kinetochore–microtubule attachment; thus its primary reaction should be annotated as occurring on **nuclear chromosome-associated cohesin**, with an additional anaphase-spindle pool, rather than as a kinetochore-restricted reaction. (ciosk1998anesp1pds1complex pages 5-7, ciosk1998anesp1pds1complex pages 7-8)

## 6. Pathways and additional functions

### FEAR network and Cdc14 release

Esp1 participates in the **FEAR (Cdc Fourteen Early Anaphase Release)** network with Slk19, Spo12, Cdc5, PP2A–Cdc55, and related regulators. This pathway transiently releases Cdc14 phosphatase from the nucleolus in early anaphase, coordinating spindle behavior and early mitotic-exit events. Esp1 is therefore both the trigger of chromosome disjunction and a regulator that helps coordinate disjunction with later anaphase processes. Evidence does not establish that Esp1 directly cleaves Cdc14, Net1, or PP2A; FEAR involvement should be treated as a pathway-level function, with both catalytic and non-proteolytic contributions still incompletely resolved. (norell2022theregulationof pages 21-24, konecna2023separaseandroads pages 6-7, schafer2006roleofame1 pages 28-31)

### Other substrates

Beyond mitotic Mcd1/Scc1, established budding-yeast separase cleavage sites occur in meiotic **Rec8** and in **Slk19**. Five known sites were catalogued across Scc1, Rec8, and Slk19—two in Scc1, two in Rec8, and one in Slk19. Slk19 cleavage is real but is not essential for viability and does not explain Esp1’s spindle-elongation requirement. (sullivan2004studiesonsubstrate pages 1-1, sullivan2004studiesonsubstrate pages 1-2, baskerville2008theproteaseactivity pages 10-11)

### Ty1 retrotransposition

A genome-wide esp1-1 interaction study found 161 aggravating loss-of-function interactions and 44 detrimental increased-dosage interactions; 24% of the identified genes were linked to Ty1 retrotransposition. Esp1 co-immunoprecipitated with Ty1 integrase, esp1-1 reduced Ty1 mobility and integration near the SUF16 tRNA gene, and cohesin mutations increased these outcomes. The proposed model gives Esp1 two roles: removing cohesin barriers and helping target Ty1 integrase to chromatin. This is a supported real-world use of Esp1 biology in understanding retroelement–chromosome interactions, although direct proteolysis of Ty1 integrase has not been established. (ho2015arolefor pages 6-8, ho2015arolefor pages 1-2)

## 7. Recent developments, 2023–2024

Direct 2023–2024 work specifically redefining *S. cerevisiae* Esp1 is limited; the core yeast mechanism remains grounded in precise foundational biochemical, genetic, and microscopy studies. A 2023 review consolidates separase as an irreversible chromosome-disjunction switch and emphasizes layered regulation, localization, and functions beyond cohesin cleavage. For budding yeast specifically, it reiterates Pds1-dependent nuclear delivery and Esp1’s FEAR-network role. (konecna2023separaseandroads pages 6-7)

Recent work in other organisms should be viewed as comparative rather than direct yeast annotation. A 2023/2024 *C. elegans* preprint showed APC/C- and securin-dependent redistribution of separase among kinetochores, spindle, chromosomes, cortex, and vesicles. This supports the general principle that securin controls not only catalytic inhibition but also local substrate access; it does not demonstrate vesicular or exocytic functions for yeast Esp1. (turpin2024securinregulatesthe pages 1-4, turpin2024securinregulatesthe pages 34-39)

Likewise, contemporary live-cell cleavage reporters and FRET-based separase sensors offer potential methods for resolving the timing and subcellular distribution of protease activity. However, the retrieved recent implementations were not validated directly for *S. cerevisiae* Esp1. Adapting an Scc1-site FRET reporter to yeast would be a plausible research application, but it remains a prospective implementation rather than an established Esp1 assay in this evidence set.

Recent structural syntheses also stress that vertebrate cyclin-B–CDK1 pseudosubstrate inhibition, Pin1 regulation, and separase autocleavage should not be automatically transferred to budding yeast. The conserved conclusion is securin-mediated inhibition and regulated substrate access; the detailed inhibitory architecture differs across eukaryotes. (konecna2023separaseandroads pages 4-6)

## 8. Applications and expert assessment

Esp1 is currently most useful as an experimental model for:

1. **Irreversible cell-cycle transitions:** manipulating Pds1, APC/C, Esp1 phosphosites, or PP2A–Cdc55 dissects how checkpoints generate an abrupt anaphase switch.
2. **Cohesin-ring opening:** engineered Scc1 cleavage sites allow separation of cohesin proteolysis from other Esp1 functions.
3. **Protease-substrate discovery:** Esp1 demonstrates why short cleavage motifs require extended docking and structural context.
4. **Chromosome-instability mechanisms:** premature Esp1 activity produces disrupted pericentric cohesion and mis-segregation, whereas insufficient activity blocks nuclear division.
5. **Mitotic-spindle and exit pathways:** Esp1 provides an entry point into spindle elongation and FEAR/Cdc14 regulation.
6. **Retrotransposon integration:** Esp1–Ty1 studies connect chromosome architecture to integration-site selection. (lianga2018cdk1phosphorylationof pages 18-20, baskerville2008theproteaseactivity pages 1-2, norell2022theregulationof pages 21-24, ho2015arolefor pages 1-2)

The most defensible expert interpretation is that Esp1 is **not merely a pair of catalytic residues attached to a cleavage motif reader**. It is a large, spatially regulated protease whose N-terminal scaffold, securin chaperoning, phosphorylation state, and substrate-docking interactions jointly determine when and where cleavage occurs. Nevertheless, Mcd1/Scc1 cleavage remains its defining primary function; spindle elongation, FEAR signaling, and Ty1 effects should be annotated as additional supported functions with varying mechanistic certainty. (baskerville2008theproteaseactivity pages 10-11)

## 9. Confidence-ranked annotation

- **Very high confidence:** ESP1/YGR098C encodes the Q03018 separase; Esp1 cleaves Mcd1/Scc1 to release cohesion and initiate anaphase.
- **High confidence:** Esp1 is inhibited and chaperoned by Pds1, activated following APC/C-dependent Pds1 destruction, regulated by Cdk1 and PP2A–Cdc55/Slk19, and enriched in the nucleus and anaphase spindle.
- **High confidence:** recognition requires a (D/E)XXR-centered motif plus extended substrate determinants.
- **Moderate-to-high confidence:** Esp1 has additional catalytic or pathway functions in spindle elongation and FEAR/Cdc14 regulation.
- **Moderate confidence:** Esp1 promotes Ty1 integration through cohesin removal and integrase/chromatin targeting.
- **Unresolved:** the additional proteolytic substrate required for spindle elongation, the exact division between catalytic and scaffolding functions in FEAR, and whether recent live-cell separase sensors can be transferred quantitatively to budding yeast.
